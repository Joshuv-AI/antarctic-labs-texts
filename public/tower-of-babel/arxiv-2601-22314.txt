Geometric configuration of integrally closed Noetherian domains
Gyu Whan Chang, Giulio Peruginelli

Abstract
In this paper, we completely describe the family of integrally closed Noetherian domains between ℤ ⁡ [ X ] \mathbb{Z}[X] and ℚ ⁡ [ X ] \mathbb{Q}[X] . We accomplish this result by classifying the Krull domains between these two polynomial rings. To this end, we first describe the DVRs of ℚ ⁡ ( X ) \mathbb{Q}(X) lying over ℤ ( p ) \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} for some prime p ∈ ℤ p\in\mathbb{Z} , by distinguishing them according to whether the extension of the residue fields is algebraic or transcendental. We unify the known descriptions of such valuations by considering ultrametric balls in ℂ p \mathbb{C}_{p} , the completion of the algebraic closure of the field ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} of p p -adic numbers. We then study when the intersection R R of such DVRs with ℚ ⁡ [ X ] \mathbb{Q}[X] is of finite character, so that R R is a Krull domain, and we finally compute the divisor class group of R R . It turns out that such a ring is formed by those polynomials which simultaneously map a finite union of ultrametric balls of ℂ p \mathbb{C}_{p} to its valuation domain 𝕆 p \mathbb{O}_{p} , as p ∈ ℤ p\in\mathbb{Z} ranges through the set of primes. By a result of Heinzer, the Krull domains of this class are precisely the integrally closed Noetherian domains between ℤ ⁡ [ X ] \mathbb{Z}[X] and ℚ ⁡ [ X ] \mathbb{Q}[X] . This novel approach provides a geometric understanding of this class of integrally closed domains. Furthermore, we also describe the UFDs between ℤ ⁡ [ X ] \mathbb{Z}[X] and ℚ ⁡ [ X ] \mathbb{Q}[X] .



Keywords: Noetherian domain, integrally closed domain, DVR, Krull domain, UFD.

Let
\bf{X}

\mathbb{C}

The class of Noetherian domains is of the utmost importance in all areas of mathematics and especially in commutative algebra
and algebraic geometry.

An integral domain is said to be a discrete valuation ring (DVR) if it is a principal ideal domain with exactly one nonzero prime ideal; equivalently, it is a Noetherian valuation domain that is not a field. Let

The class of Krull domains includes Dedekind domains, unique factorization domains (UFDs),

[
13

[

Let

For each

. It is knonw tht

1.1.1
Ideal factorization property of Krull domains

A Dedekind domain
D
is an integral domain in which each nonzero ideal can be written as a finite product of prime ideals. Then

D
is a Dedekind domain if and only if
D
is an integrally closed one-dimensional Noetherian domain, if and only if each nonzero ideal of
D
is invertible. It is known that
D
is a Dedekind domain if and only if

D
is a Krull domain of (Krull) dimension at most one. Furthermore,
the integral closure of a Noetherian domain is a Krull domain

[
23
, Theorem 2]
, so
an overring of
2
-dimensional Noetherian domain is a Krull domain if and only if it is an integrally closed Noetherian domain
[
18
, Theorem 9]
. Hence, a Krull domain can be considered as a higher dimensional generalization of Dedekind domains, so it is natural to ask whether a Krull domain can be characterized by an ideal factorization property as in Dedekind domains.

For
I\in F(D)
, let
I_{t}=\bigcup\{J_{v}\mid J\in F(D),J\subseteq I
and

is finitely generated
\}
. Then

and the map

The

, (ii)
(aI)_{t}=aI_{t}
, (iii)

and if

, then
I_{t}\subseteq J_{t}
and (iv)
(I_{t})_{t}=I
. An
I\in F(D)

is said to be
t
-invertible if
(II^{-1})_{t}=D
.
We then use the
t
-operation to characterize Krull domains as follows:
D
is a Krull domain if and only if, given a nonzero ideal
I
of
D
,

I_{t}=(P_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots P_{n}^{e_{n}})_{t}
for some
P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\in X^{1}(D)
and positive integers
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
,
if and only if, given a nonzero element
a
of
D
,

aD=(P_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots P_{n}^{e_{n}})_{t}
for some
P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\in X^{1}(D)
and positive integers
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
,
if and only if every nonzero (prime) ideal of
D
is
t
-invertible,
if and only if every nonzero prime ideal of
D
contains a
t
-invertible prime ideal;
in this case,
I_{v}=I_{t}
for all

(see, for example,
[
21
]
).

1.1.2
Divisor class group of a Krull domain

Let
\mathcal{D}(D)=\{I_{v}\mid I\in F(D)\}
. Then

\mathcal{C}(D)
is a commutative semigroup under
I*J=(IJ)_{v}
,
and
\mathcal{D}(D)
is a group, i.e.,
(II^{-1})_{v}=D
for all

, if and only if

is completely integrally closed. Next let Inv
(D)
be the group of invertible factional ideals of

and Prin
(D)=\{xD\mid x\in K,x\neq 0\}
.
Then Inv
(D)
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{D}(D)
and Prin
(D)
is a subgroup of Inv
(D)
, so Prin
(D)\subseteq

(D)\subseteq\mathcal{D}(D)
.
The divisor class group of a completely integrally closed domain

is defined by the factor group Cl

Prin
(D)
of
\mathcal{C}(D)
modulo Prin
(D)
and the ideal class group or Picard group of an integral domain

is defined by the factor group Inv
(D)/
Prin
(D)
of Inv
(D)
modulo Prin
(D)
. Hence, if

is completely integrally closed, then Pic

is a subgroup of Cl
(D)
.

It is known that

is almost integral over

if and only if

for some nonzero ideal

of

is completely integrally closed if and only if the (Krull) dimension of

is at most one; in particular, a DVR is completely integrally closed. A Krull domain

is definited by an intersection of DVRs, so

is completely integrally closed and its divisor class group is also well defined.

A
\pi
-domain

is a Krull domain with Pic
(D)=

(D)

equivalently, every nonzero principal ideal of

can be written as a finite product of prime ideals
[

\pi
-domains]
. Hence, a Dedekind domain

is a

-domain and Pic
(D)=

(D)
.
A UFD is a Krull domain, and Krull domain

is a UFD if and only if Cl
(D)=\{0\}
. A Krull domain

is called an almost factorial domain if Cl

is torsion; equivalently, given

there is a positive integer

Therefore, the divisor class group of a Krull domain

Let

It is well known that Int

Let

Residually transcendental extensions

Let

1.3.1
Monomial valuations

In this subsection we review the notion of monomial valuations, which is a particular class of extensions of

Definition 1.1
.

Let

Clearly,

Note that

Let

. Given

(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{K}\times\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
, we denote by
v_{\alpha,\delta}
the restriction of
u_{\alpha,\delta}
to
K(X)
; note that this valuation depends on the chosen extension
u
of
v
to
\overline{K}
, see
[
26
, Remark 3.9]
. If
V_{\alpha,\delta}
(
U_{\alpha,\delta}
, resp.) is the valuation domain associated to
v_{\alpha,\delta}
(
u_{\alpha,\delta}
, resp.) then clearly we have
V_{\alpha,\delta}=U_{\alpha,\delta}\cap K(X)
.
We also say that
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{K}\times\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
is a
minimal pair of definition of
V_{\alpha,\delta}
(with respect to
K
) if for each
\beta\in\overline{K}
such that
u(\beta-\alpha)\geq\delta
, we have
[K(\beta):K]\geq[K(\alpha):K]
. Equivalently, among the set of pairs which define the valuation
u_{\alpha,\delta}
, the degree
[K(\alpha):K]
is minimal (see
[
1
, Proposition 3]
and
[
2
]
). We stress that when
\alpha\in\overline{K}
, the coefficients
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}
of the
(X-\alpha)
-adic expansion of
f\in K[X]
lie in
\overline{K}
and may not be in
K
.

Remark 1.2
.

Suppose that
\delta>v(a)
for every
a\in K
. We denote such
\delta
by
\infty
. Given
\alpha\in\overline{K}
, for every
f\in K[X]
with
f(X)=a_{0}+a_{1}(X-\alpha)+\ldots+a_{n}(X-\alpha)^{n}
where
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\in\overline{K}
we have

v_{\alpha,\delta}(f)=u(a_{0})=u(f(\alpha))

so in this case
V_{\alpha,\infty}=V_{\alpha}=\{\phi\in K(X)\mid\phi(\alpha)\in U\}
.

Remark 1.3
.

(1) Let
U
be a fixed extension of
V
to
\overline{K}
.
Given
\alpha\in\overline{K}
and an element
\delta
in a totally ordered group containing
\Gamma_{v}
,
it is well-known that
V_{\alpha,\delta}
is a residually transcendental extension of
V

if and only if
\delta
belongs to
\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
(see for example
[
2
]
or
[
26
, Lemma 3.5]
).
In this case, by
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
, the value group of
V_{\alpha,\delta}
has finite index over the value group
\Gamma_{v}
of
V
.
In particular, if
V
is a DVR, then a residually transcendental extension of
V
to
K(X)
is a DVR, also.

(2) Let
V
be a DVR and
U
a fixed extension of
V
to
\overline{K}
. Given a (minimal) pair
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{K}\times\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
,
the intersection
V_{\alpha,\delta}\cap K[X]
is clearly a Krull domain which is not Prüfer, since the valuation overring
V_{\alpha,\delta}
is residually transcendental over
V
, see
[
26
, Theorem 3.15]
.

It is known that if we restrict to the family of Krull domains
\{V_{\alpha,\delta}\cap K[X]\mid(\alpha,\delta)\in K\times\Gamma_{\overline{v}}\}
, this family has a natural ordering, namely:

V_{\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}\cap K[X]\subseteq V_{\alpha_{2},\delta_{2}}\cap K[X]\Leftrightarrow\delta_{1}\leq\delta_{2}\text{ and }v(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\geq\delta_{1}

(1.4)

(see
[
4
, Proposition 1.1]
; see also
[
26
, Remark 3.10]
), so

V_{\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}\cap K[X]=V_{\alpha_{2},\delta_{2}}\cap K[X]\Leftrightarrow\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}\text{ and }v(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\geq\delta_{1}

(1.5)

We will see in Lemma
2.1
that the previous conditions are indeed equivalent to
V_{\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}=V_{\alpha_{2},\delta_{2}}
.

If
(\alpha,\delta)\in K\times\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
and we consider the ball
B(\alpha,\delta)=\{x\in K\mid v(x-\alpha)\geq\delta\}
, then condition (
1.4
) holds if and only if
B(\alpha_{2},\delta_{2})\subseteq B(\alpha_{1},\delta_{1})
.
In the case
V=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for some prime
p
, we generalize this ordering to the case of pairs
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}
in Section
3.2
.

The main result of Alexandru, Popescu and Zaharescu is the following characterization of the residually transcendental extensions of
V
to
K(X)
(see
[
1
,
2
,
4
]
).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
V
be a valuation domain with quotient field
K
and let
U
be a fixed extension of
V
to
\overline{K}
.
Let
W
be a residually transcendental extension of
V
to
K(X)
. Then there exists a minimal pair

(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{K}\times\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
such that
W
is the restriction to
K(X)

of the monomial valuation
U_{\alpha,\delta}
of the field
\overline{K}(X)
.

Note that the valuation domain
U_{\alpha,\delta}
of
\overline{K}(X)
in Theorem
1.6
is a residually transcendental extension of
U
to
\overline{K}(X)
.
The next lemma is a generalization of
[
26
, Lemma 3.13]
to valuation domains of arbitrary rank.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
U
be an extension of
V
to
\overline{K}
and let
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{K}\times\Gamma_{v}
. Then

\displaystyle V[X]\subset U_{\alpha,\delta}\cap K(X)\Leftrightarrow u(\alpha)\geq 0\;\text{ and }\delta\geq 0.

Proof.

Note that the containment is equivalent to
u_{\alpha,\delta}(X)\geq 0
(and, in particular, equivalent to
U[X]\subset U_{\alpha,\delta}
). We have
u_{\alpha,\delta}(X)=\min\{\delta,u(\alpha)\}
so that the claim is true.
∎

1.3.2
Parametrization of residually transcendental extensions

In this subsection, we show how to parametrize
\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}
by means of ultrametric balls contained in
\overline{K}

with respect to some fixed extension
U
of
V
to
\overline{K}
.

Let
G_{K}
be the absolute Galois group
\text{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
. Note that
G_{K}
is also equal to the Galois group of
\overline{K}(X)/K(X)
.
Given an extension
U
of
V
to
\overline{K}
, we denote by
\mathcal{D}(U)
the decomposition group of
U
,
namely:
\mathcal{D}(U)=\{\sigma\in G_{K}\mid\sigma(U)=U\}
.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
U
be a fixed extension of
V
to
\overline{K}
.
Let
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{K}\times\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
,
with
\delta=u(c)
for some
c\in\overline{K}
, and
\sigma\in G_{K}
. Then we have

\sigma(U_{\alpha,\delta})=\sigma(U)_{\sigma(\alpha),\delta}.

In particular, if
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(U)
, then
\sigma(U_{\alpha,\delta})=U_{\sigma(\alpha),\delta}
.

Proof.

Since
\frac{X-\alpha}{c}
is a unit in
U_{\alpha,\delta}
and
U_{\alpha,\delta}
is residually transcendental over the residue field
k_{U}
of
U
,
it follows that
\sigma\left(\frac{X-\alpha}{c}\right)=\frac{X-\sigma(\alpha)}{\sigma(c)}
is a unit in
\sigma(U_{\alpha,\delta})

and
\sigma(U_{\alpha,\delta})
is residually transcendental over
k_{\sigma(U)}
(note that
\sigma(U_{\alpha,\delta})
is an extension of
\sigma(U)
to
\overline{K}(X)
).
Recall that the valuation associated to
\sigma(U)=U^{\prime}
is equal to
u^{\prime}=u\circ\sigma^{-1}
(
[
12
, Proposition 3.2.16, (1)]
), so
u^{\prime}(\sigma(c))=\delta
.
Then the claim follows by
[
7
, Chapt. VI, §10, Proposition 2]
.

If
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(U)
, then the last claim follows at once.
∎

We give next a criterion for when two residually transcendental extensions of
U
to
\overline{K}(X)
contracts down to the same valuation domain of
K(X)

(which is a residually transcendental extension of
V
to
K(X)
).
This has been done in a similar way in
[
24
]
.
In particular, given a residually transcendental extension
V_{\alpha,\delta}
of
V
to
K(X)
, where
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{K}\times\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
,
we describe the set of extensions of
V_{\alpha,\delta}
to
\overline{K}(X)
lying above
U
(see also
[
3
, Theorem 2.2]
).

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
U
be a fixed extension of
V
to
\overline{K}
and
let
(\alpha,\delta),(\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})\in\overline{K}\times\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

U_{\alpha,\delta}\cap K(X)=U_{\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}\cap K(X)
.

(2)

\sigma(U_{\alpha,\delta})=U_{\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}
for some
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(U)
.

(3)

\delta=\delta^{\prime}
and
u(\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha^{\prime})\geq\delta
, for some
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(U)
.

In particular, the set of extensions of a residually transcendental extension
W=U_{\alpha,\delta}\cap K(X)
to
\overline{K}(X)
lying above
U
is equal to
\{U_{\sigma(\alpha),\delta}\mid\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(U)\}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) By
[
12
, Conjugation Theorem 3.2.15]
, there exists
\sigma\in G_{K}
such that
\sigma(U_{\alpha,\delta})=U_{\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}
. We claim that
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(U)
. In fact, let
x\in U\subset U_{\alpha,\delta}
. Then
\sigma(x)\in\sigma(U_{\alpha,\delta})\cap\overline{K}=U_{\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}\cap\overline{K}=U
. Thus, (2) holds.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3) By Lemma
1.8
,
U_{\sigma(\alpha),\delta}=\sigma(U_{\alpha,\delta})=U_{\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}
.
By
[
4
, Proposition 1.1]
,
\delta^{\prime}=\delta
and
u(\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha^{\prime})\geq\delta
, which is condition (3).

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1) The inequality
u(\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha^{\prime})\geq\delta
is equivalent to
\alpha^{\prime}\in B_{u}(\sigma(\alpha),\delta)=\{x\in\overline{K}\mid u(\sigma(\alpha)-x)\geq\delta\}
, so that
(\alpha^{\prime},\delta)
is another pair of definition for
U_{\sigma(\alpha),\delta}
by
[
1
, Proposition 3]
and thus
U_{\alpha^{\prime},\delta}=U_{\sigma(\alpha),\delta}
. By Lemma
1.8
, we also have
U_{\sigma(\alpha),\delta}=\sigma(U_{\alpha,\delta})
, so
U_{\alpha,\delta}

and
U_{\alpha^{\prime},\delta}
contracts down to
K(X)
to the same valuation domain. Thus, (1) holds.

The last claim follows immediately from the previous part and Lemma
1.8
.
∎

For the next theorem, we fix the following notation: given an extension
U
of
V
to
\overline{K}
, let
\mathcal{B}_{u}
be the set of ultrametric balls
B_{u}(\alpha,\delta)=\{x\in\overline{K}\mid u(x-\alpha)\geq\delta\}
in
\overline{K}
with respect to
U
, where
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{K}\times\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
.
It is worthwhile to note that condition (3) of Theorem
1.9
is equivalent to the following:

\sigma(B_{u}(\alpha,\delta))=B_{u}(\sigma(\alpha),\delta)=B_{u}(\alpha^{\prime},\delta)
for some
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(U)
.

By Lemma
1.8
,
\mathcal{D}(U)
acts on
\mathcal{B}_{u}
, since for
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(U)
we have
\sigma(B_{u}(\alpha,\delta))=B_{u}(\sigma(\alpha),\delta)
.
In particular,
\mathcal{D}(U)
does not change the radius of an ultrametric ball.
We denote by
\mathcal{B}_{u}/\mathcal{D}(U)
the set of equivalence classes of
\mathcal{B}_{u}
under the action of the decomposition group
\mathcal{D}(U)
.
By Lemma
1.8
, an element of
\mathcal{B}_{u}/\mathcal{D}(U)
is equal to
[B_{u}(\alpha,\delta)]=\{B_{u}(\sigma(\alpha),\delta)\mid\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(U)\}
;
in particular, note that such an equivalence class is formed by finitely many closed balls (i.e., at most as many as the number of conjugates of
\alpha
over
K
).

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
U
be a fixed extension of
V
to
\overline{K}
. Then the map

\Theta:\mathcal{B}_{u}/\mathcal{D}(U)\to\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}
given by

[B_{u}(\alpha,\delta)]\mapsto U_{\alpha,\delta}\cap K(X)
is bijective.

Proof.

In order to show that the map
\Theta
is well-defined, we observe first that given
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{K}\times\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
, we have
U_{\alpha,\delta}\cap K(X)\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}
by Remark
1.3
. Moreover, if
\sigma(B_{u}(\alpha,\delta))=B_{u}(\sigma(\alpha),\delta)\in[B_{u}(\alpha,\delta)]
for some
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(U)
, then
U_{\alpha,\delta}\cap K(X)=U_{\sigma(\alpha),\delta}\cap K(X)
by Theorem
1.9
.

The surjectivity of
\Theta
follows by Theorem
1.6
.
Finally, the injectivity follows again by Theorem
1.9
. In fact, suppose that
\Theta([B_{u}(\alpha,\delta)])=\Theta([B_{u}(\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})])
, that is,
U_{\alpha,\delta}\cap K(X)=U_{\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}\cap K(X)
. Then, by Theorem
1.9
,
\delta=\delta^{\prime}
and
u(\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha^{\prime})\geq\delta
, so that
B_{u}(\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})=B_{u}(\sigma(\alpha),\delta)
. Hence,
[B_{u}(\alpha,\delta)]=[B_{u}(\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})]
.
∎

2
Polynomial ordering of torsion extensions

We say that an extension
W
of
V
to
K(X)
is torsion if
\Gamma_{w}\subseteq\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
; we denote by
\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
the set of all torsion extensions of
V
to
K(X)
. Note that a residually transcendental extension of
V
to
K(X)
is torsion by Remark
1.3
,
so
\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
. We stress that not all the residually algebraic extensions of
V
to
K(X)
are torsion. For example,
\{\phi\in K(X)\mid\phi(0)\in V\}
is a residually algebraic extension of
V
(its residue field is the same as the residue field of
V
) which is not torsion (its one-dimensional valuation overring is
K[X]_{(X)}
; see Proposition
4.17
and also Remark
3.3
).

2.1
A partial order on the set of torsion extensions of
V
to
K(X)

Let
W_{1},W_{2}
be in
\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
. In
[
4
, page 282]
, the following definition is given only for residually transcendental extensions (which are torsion):

w_{1}\leq w_{2}\Leftrightarrow w_{1}(f)\leq w_{2}(f),\forall f\in K[X]

where the last inequality is considered in

\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
. More generally, we give the same definition for torsion extensions.
We remark that since any
f\in K[X]
can be written as
\frac{g(X)}{c}
for some
g\in V[X]
and
c\in V
,
c\not=0
,
and the valuations
w_{1},w_{2}
coincide on
K
, the previous condition is equivalent to:
w_{1}(g)\leq w_{2}(g)
for each
g\in V[X]
.
Note that clearly we have

w_{1}=w_{2}\Leftrightarrow w_{1}(f)=w_{2}(f),\;\forall f\in K[X]\Leftrightarrow W_{1}=W_{2}.

In particular, we also say that
w_{1}<w_{2}
if and only if
w_{1}\leq w_{2}
and
w_{1}\not=w_{2}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
W_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
and
M_{i}
be the maximal ideal of
W_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

(1)

The following statements are equivalent.

(a)

w_{1}\leq w_{2}
.

(b)

W_{1}\cap K[X]\subseteq W_{2}\cap K[X].

(c)

M_{1}\cap K[X]\subseteq M_{2}\cap K[X]
.

(2)

W_{1}=W_{2}
if and only if
W_{1}\cap K[X]=W_{2}\cap K[X]
, if and only if
M_{1}\cap K[X]=M_{2}\cap K[X]
.

(3)

(\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}},\leq)
is a partially ordered set.

Proof.

(1) (a)
\Rightarrow
(c). Let
f\in M_{1}\cap K[X]
be nonzero. Then
0<w_{1}(f)\leq w_{2}(f)
, so
f\in M_{2}\cap K[X]
.
Thus
M_{1}\cap K[X]\subseteq M_{2}\cap K[X].

(c)
\Rightarrow
(b). Let
g\in W_{1}\cap K[X]
be nonzero. If
g\in M_{1}
, then
g\in M_{2}\cap K[X]\subseteq W_{2}\cap K[X]
.
Next assume that
g\not\in M_{1}
and
g\not\in W_{2}\cap K[X]
. Since
W_{2}
is a torsion extension of
V
,
there are
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
b\in V
such that
w_{2}(g^{n})=nw_{2}(g)=-v(b)<0
or
w_{2}(g^{n}b)=0
.
Note that
w_{1}(g^{n}b)=nw_{1}(g)+w_{1}(b)=v(b)>0
, so
g^{n}b\in M_{1}\cap K[X]\subseteq M_{2}\cap K[X]

by assumption. Hence
w_{2}(g^{n}b)>0
, a contradiction, which implies
g\in W_{2}\cap K[X]
.
Thus
W_{1}\cap K[X]\subseteq W_{2}\cap K[X].

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a). Assume that
f\in K[X]
is nonzero. Then either
f\in W_{1}
or
\frac{1}{f}\in W_{1}
, and since

W_{1}
is torsion over
V
, there exist a positive integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\neq c\in K
such that
nw_{1}(f)=v(c)
.
Hence,
w_{1}(\frac{f^{n}}{c})=0
.
In particular,
\frac{f^{n}}{c}\in W_{1}\cap K[X]\subseteq W_{2}\cap K[X]
, so that we also have

0\leq w_{2}\left(\frac{f^{n}}{c}\right)\Leftrightarrow w_{2}(f)\geq\frac{v(c)}{n}=w_{1}(f),

so the inequality holds true.

(2) It is clear that
W_{1}=W_{2}
implies both of
W_{1}\cap K[X]=W_{2}\cap K[X]

and
M_{1}\cap K[X]=M_{2}\cap K[X]
. Conversely, if
W_{1}\cap K[X]=W_{2}\cap K[X]
or
M_{1}\cap K[X]=M_{2}\cap K[X]
,
then
w_{1}(f)=w_{2}(f)
for every
f\in K[X]
by (1), which implies that
w_{1}=w_{2}
on
K(X)
. Thus,
W_{1}=W_{2}
.

(3) This follows directly from (1) and (2) above.
∎

Let the notation be as in Lemma
2.1
.
Then
w_{1}<w_{2}
if and only if
W_{1}\cap K[X]\subset W_{2}\cap K[X]
,
if and only if
M_{1}\cap K[X]\subset M_{2}\cap K[X]
. The next corollary gives an alternative characterization of this strict inequality.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
W_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
and
M_{i}
be the maximal ideal of
W_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Then
the following statements are satisfied.

(1)

w_{1}<w_{2}
if and only if
M_{1}\cap K[X]\subset M_{2}\cap(W_{1}\cap K[X])
, and in this case,

M_{1}\cap K[X]
is a prime non-maximal ideal of
W_{1}\cap K[X]
.

(2)

W\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
is a maximal element of
(\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}},\leq)
if and only if
W
is residually algebraic.

Proof.

(1) Assume that
w_{1}<w_{2}
. Then, by Lemma
2.1
, there exists
f\in(W_{2}\cap K[X])\setminus(W_{1}\cap K[X])
, so

w_{1}(f)<0\leq w_{2}(f)
. Since
W_{1}
is a torsion extension of
V
,
there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\neq a\in K
such that
w_{1}(f^{n})=v(a)
. Then, we have

0=w_{1}\left(\frac{f^{n}}{a}\right)<w_{2}\left(\frac{f^{n}}{a}\right),

so
\frac{f^{n}}{a}\in M_{2}\cap(W_{1}\cap K[X])
but
\frac{f^{n}}{a}\notin M_{1}\cap K[X]
. Conversely, suppose that

M_{1}\cap K[X]\subset M_{2}\cap(W_{1}\cap K[X])
. Then
M_{2}\cap(W_{1}\cap K[X])\subseteq M_{2}\cap K[X]

implies
M_{1}\cap K[X]\subset M_{2}\cap K[X]
. Thus
w_{1}<w_{2}
by Lemma
2.1
.

(2)
(\Rightarrow)
Suppose that
W
is residually transcendental. By Theorem
1.6
,
there exists
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{K}\times\Gamma_{\overline{v}}
such that
W=U_{\alpha,\delta}\cap K(X)

(where
U
is a fixed extension of
V
to
\overline{K}
). If
\delta^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{v}

is strictly greater than
\delta
, we have

W\cap K[X]\subsetneq W^{\prime}\cap K[X]
, where
W^{\prime}=U_{\alpha,\delta^{\prime}}\cap K(X)
. In fact,
U_{\alpha,\delta}\cap\overline{K}[X]\subsetneq U_{\alpha,\delta^{\prime}}\cap\overline{K}[X]
because
\frac{X-\alpha}{c^{\prime}}
is in the latter ring but not in the former (where
c^{\prime}\in K
has value
\delta^{\prime}
) and by Theorem
1.9
,
U_{\alpha,\delta}\cap K[X]\subsetneq U_{\alpha,\delta^{\prime}}\cap K[X]
.

(\Leftarrow)
Suppose that
W\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
is residually algebraic. We have the following containments,
where
M
is the maximal ideal of
W
:

\frac{V}{M\cap K}\subseteq\frac{W\cap K[X]}{M\cap K[X]}\subseteq\frac{W}{M}.

It follows that
(W\cap K[X])/(M\cap K[X])
is a field and thus
M\cap K[X]
is a maximal ideal of
W\cap K[X]
.
By the first part of the Corollary,
W
is a maximal element of
(\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}},\leq)
.
∎

It is worthwhile to note that if
K
is algebraically closed and maximally complete (i.e., it does not admit any proper immediate extension), then it is straightforward to see that there are no residually algebraic torsion extensions of
V
to
K(X)
.
Hence, in this case,
\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
has no maximal element by Corollary
2.2
.

2.2
Residually transcendental extensions over the completion

Let
\widehat{V}
(resp.
\widehat{K}
) denote the
v
-adic completion of
V
(resp.
K
),

\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\text{t}}
be the set of all torsion extensions of
\widehat{V}
to
\widehat{K}(X)
,
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\text{rt}}
be the set of all residually transcendental
extensions of
\widehat{V}
to
\widehat{K}(X)
. In this subsection,
we show that the map of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\text{t}}
(resp.
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\text{rt}}
)
into
\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
(resp.
\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}
) given by
W\mapsto W\cap K(X)
is bijective.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\widetilde{W}
be an extension of
\widehat{V}
to
\widehat{K}(X)
such that
\tilde{w}(X)>v(a)
, for some
a\in K
. If
W=\widetilde{W}\cap K(X)
, then we have

\frac{\widetilde{W}\cap\widehat{K}[X]}{\widetilde{M}\cap\widehat{K}[X]}=\frac{W\cap K[X]}{M\cap K[X]},

where
\widetilde{M},M
are the maximal ideals of
\widetilde{W},W
, respectively.

Proof.

Clearly, we have the containment
(\supseteq)
. For the other containment,
we need to show that for every
f\in\widetilde{W}\cap\widehat{K}[X]
there exists
g\in W\cap K[X]
such that
\tilde{w}(f-g)>0
.
Suppose that
f(X)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{i}X^{i}
. We choose
g\in K[X]
,
g(X)=\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}^{d}a_{i}X^{i}
,
such that
v(\alpha_{i}-a_{i})+iv(a)>0
, for each
i=0,\ldots,d
. In particular,

\tilde{w}(f-g)\geq\min\{v(\alpha_{i}-a_{i})+i\tilde{w}(X)\}>\min\{v(\alpha_{i}-a_{i})+iv(a)\}>0

(2.4)

so that
f-g\in\widetilde{M}
. Note that
g\in W
, so we get the claim.
∎

Let
\widehat{V}_{\infty}=\{\psi\in\widehat{K}(X)\mid\psi(\infty)\in\widehat{V}\}
, where
\psi(\infty)
is defined as
\varphi(0)
with
\varphi(X)=\psi(1/X)
(see
[
25
]
). Then
the assumption on
\widetilde{W}
of Lemma
2.3
is equivalent to say
\widetilde{W}\not=\widehat{V}_{\infty}
.
Note that every
W\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
satisfies the assumption of Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\widetilde{R}
be an integral domain such that
\widehat{V}[X/a]\subseteq\widetilde{R}\subseteq\widehat{K}[X]

for some nonzero
a\in K
and let
R=\widetilde{R}\cap K[X]
. Let
\widetilde{W}
be a valuation domain
of
\widehat{K}(X)
extending
\widehat{V}
and let
W=\widetilde{W}\cap K(X)
. Then

R\subset W
if and only if
\widetilde{R}\subset\widetilde{W}
.

Proof.

The implication
(\Leftarrow)
is clear. Conversely, suppose that
R\subset W
and let
f\in\widetilde{R}
. In particular,
f\in\widehat{K}[X]=\widehat{K}[X/a]
, so
f(X)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{i}(X/a)^{i}
, for some
\alpha_{i}\in\widehat{K}
. Let
g(X)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}(X/a)^{i}\in K[X]
be such that
v(\alpha_{i}-a_{i})\geq 0
for each
i=0,\ldots,d
. Then
h:=f-g\in\widehat{V}[X/a]\subseteq\widetilde{R}
so that
g=f-h\in\widetilde{R}\cap K[X]=R
. By assumption,
g\in W
and note that
h\in\widetilde{W}
, because
V[X/a]\subset W
implies that
\widehat{V}[X/a]\subset\widetilde{W}
. Hence,
f=h+g\in\widetilde{W}
, as claimed.
∎

The following corollary follows at once.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\widetilde{W}_{i}
be an extension of
\widehat{V}
to
\widehat{K}(X)
such that
\tilde{w}_{i}(X)>v(a)
for some
a\in K
, for
i=1,2
. Let
W_{i}=\widetilde{W}_{i}\cap K(X)
, for
i=1,2
. Then we have

W_{1}\cap K[X]\subseteq W_{2}\cap K[X]\Leftrightarrow\widetilde{W}_{1}\cap\widehat{K}[X]\subseteq\widetilde{W}_{2}\cap\widehat{K}[X].

(2.7)

Proof.

It is sufficient to notice that the assumption of
\tilde{w}_{1}
implies that
\widehat{V}[X/a]\subseteq\widetilde{W}_{1}\cap\widehat{K}[X]
and then apply Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
W
be a residually transcendental extension of
V
to
K(X)

and
\widehat{K(X)}

(
resp.
\widehat{W})
be the completion of
K(X)

(
resp.
W)
with respect to
W
.
Then the following statements hold true:

(1)

\widehat{K}(X)
is a subfield of
\widehat{K(X)}
.

(2)

\widehat{W}
is immediate over
\widehat{W}\cap\widehat{K}(X)
.

(3)

\widehat{W}\cap\widehat{K}(X)
is a residually transcendental extension of
\widehat{V}
.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma_{w}/\Gamma_{v}
is torsion, it follows that
\widehat{K}
is equal to the topological closure of
K
in
\widehat{K(X)}
.
Hence, we may embed
\widehat{K}(X)
into
\widehat{K(X)}
.

Let
\widetilde{W}=\widehat{W}\cap\widehat{K}(X)
.
Since
V\subseteq\widehat{V}
and
W\subseteq\widehat{W}
are immediate extensions, it follows that
\widehat{W}
is immediate over
\widetilde{W}
and
\widehat{W}
is residually transcendental over
\widehat{V}
.
In particular, since
K(X)\subseteq\widehat{K}(X)\subseteq\widehat{K(X)}
, it follows that also
W\subseteq\widetilde{W}
is
an immediate extension and so
\widetilde{W}
is also a residually transcendental extension of
\widehat{V}
. If
X
were algebraic over
\widehat{K}
,
then
\widehat{K}\subseteq\widehat{K}(X)
would be a finite extension and so
\widetilde{W}
could not be residually transcendental over
\widehat{V}
.
Then
X
is transcendental over
\widehat{K}
.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\widetilde{W}
be a residually transcendental extension of
\widehat{V}
to
\widehat{K}(X)
. Then
\widetilde{W}\cap K(X)
is a residually transcendental extension of
V
.

Proof.

By
[
1
, Proposition 1]
, there exists
f\in\widehat{K}[X]\cap\widetilde{W}
such that its residue modulo
\widetilde{M}
is transcendental over
\widehat{V}/\widehat{M}=V/M
. By Lemma
2.3
,
if
g\in K[X]
is a polynomial of the same degree as
f
sufficiently
v
-adically close to
f
,
then
g\in\widetilde{W}\cap K(X)
and
f
and
g
have the same residue modulo
\widetilde{M}
so
g
is residually transcendental over
V/M
.
Thus,
\widetilde{W}\cap K(X)
is a residually transcendental extension of
V
.
∎

Theorem 2.10
.

If
\widetilde{W}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\text{t}}
then
\widetilde{W}\cap K(X)\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
. Moreover, if
\widetilde{W}_{i}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\text{t}}
and
W_{i}=\widetilde{W}_{i}\cap K(X)
for
i=1,2
, then
w_{1}\leq w_{2}
if and only if
\tilde{w}_{1}\leq\tilde{w}_{2}
. In particular,
W_{1}=W_{2}
if and only if
\widetilde{W}_{1}=\widetilde{W}_{2}
.

Proof.

It is clear that if
\widetilde{W}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\text{t}}
, then
\widetilde{W}\cap K(X)\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
,
because
\Gamma_{v}=\Gamma_{\hat{v}}
.
Hence, by Lemma
2.1
, the claim
w_{1}\leq w_{2}\Leftrightarrow\tilde{w}_{1}\leq\tilde{w}_{2}

is equivalent to (
2.7
), which holds by Corollary
2.6
.

For the final claim,
(\Leftarrow)
is straightforward. Conversely, suppose
W_{1}=W_{2}
.
Then
W_{1}\cap K[X]=W_{2}\cap K[X]
, so by (
2.7
),
\widetilde{W}_{1}\cap\widehat{K}[X]=\widetilde{W}_{2}\cap\widehat{K}[X]
.
Thus, by Lemma
2.1
,
\widetilde{W}_{1}=\widetilde{W}_{2}
.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

The map
\Phi:\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\text{rt}}\to\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}
given by

\widetilde{W}\mapsto\widetilde{W}\cap K(X)
is bijective.

Proof.

Note that if
\widetilde{W}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\text{rt}}
, then
W=\widetilde{W}\cap K(X)\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}
by Lemma
2.9
.
Hence,
\Phi
is well-defined. Then
\Phi
is surjective by Lemma
2.8

and
\Phi
is injective by Theorem
2.10
.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

Let
\Psi
be the map from
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\text{t}}
to
\mathcal{W}_{\text{t}}
which sends
\widetilde{W}
to
\widetilde{W}\cap K(X)
. Clearly, the restriction of
\Psi
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\text{rt}}
is equal to
\Phi
. As in the proof of Corollary
2.11
,
\Psi
is well-defined and injective by Theorem
2.10
. However,
\Psi
is not surjective: if
\alpha\in\widehat{K}
is transcendental over
K
, then
V_{\alpha}=\{\phi\in K(X)\mid\phi(\alpha)\in\widehat{V}\}
is a torsion extension of
V
, while
\widehat{V}_{\alpha}=\{\psi\in\widehat{K}(X)\mid\psi(\alpha)\in\widehat{V}\}
is not, since
\widehat{V}_{\alpha}
is contained in the DVR
\widehat{K}[X]_{(X-\alpha)}
.

3
Complete list of DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)

In this section we give a complete description of the family of DVRs with quotient field
\mathbb{Q}(X)
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be the set of prime numbers in
\mathbb{Z}
, so
\{p\mathbb{Z}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
is the set of nonzero (principal)
prime ideals of the ring
\mathbb{Z}
of integers.
Let
W\subset\mathbb{Q}(X)
be a DVR with maximal ideal
M
. We have one of the following two cases:

•

W\cap\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}
.

•

W\cap\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
.

In the first case we say that
W
is a
non-unitary
valuation domain, and in the second case, that
W
is a
unitary
valuation domain. For each
p\in\mathbb{P}
, let
\mathcal{W}_{p}
be the set of DVRs
W
of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
such that
W\cap\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.

It is a classical result (see for example
[
11
, p. 3]
) that if
W
is a non-unitary valuation domain,
then
W
is equal to one of the following DVRs, according to whether
X\in W
or
X\not\in W
:

-

W=\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(g)}
, for some irreducible
g\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
.

-

W=\mathbb{Q}[1/X]_{(1/X)}.

If
W\in\mathcal{W}_{p}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
,
we distinguish between these two subcases:

1.

W
is residually algebraic over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.

2.

W
is residually transcendental over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.

Throughout this paper, for each
p\in\mathbb{P}
we set:

-

\mathcal{W}_{p,\text{ra}}=\{W\in\mathcal{W}_{p}\mid W
is residually algebraic over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\}
;

-

\mathcal{W}_{p,\text{rt}}=\{W\in\mathcal{W}_{p}\mid W
is residually transcendental over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\}
.

In particular,
\mathcal{W}_{p}=\mathcal{W}_{p,\text{ra}}\cup\mathcal{W}_{p,\text{rt}}
and
\mathcal{W}_{p,\text{ra}}\cap\mathcal{W}_{p,\text{rt}}=\emptyset
.
Moreover, let

\mathcal{W}=\bigcup\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{W}_{p},\;\;\mathcal{W}_{\text{ra}}=\bigcup\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{W}_{p,\text{ra}},\;\;\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}=\bigcup\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{W}_{p,\text{rt}},

so

\mathcal{W}
is the set of DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
,

\mathcal{W}_{\text{ra}}
is the set of DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
that are residually algebraic extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
, and

\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}
is the set of DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
that are residually transcendental extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
.

3.1
Extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
to
\mathbb{Q}(X)

Let
\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be the field of
p
-adic numbers and the ring of
p
-adic integers, respectively, which are the completion of
\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
with respect to the topology induced by the
p
-adic valuation. Let
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
be a fixed algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\overline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
be the integral closure of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
; since
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is Henselian,
\overline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
is a one-dimensional non-discrete valuation domain. We denote by
v_{p}
the unique extension of the
p
-adic valuation to
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. The ring
\overline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
is the valuation domain of
v_{p}
(i.e.,
\overline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}=\{x\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mid v_{p}(x)\geq 0\}
). Let
\mathbb{C}_{p}
be the completion of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
with respect to the topology induced by the valuation
v_{p}
and
\mathbb{O}_{p}
the completion of
\overline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
. It is well-known that
\mathbb{C}_{p}
is algebraically closed (
[
17
, Proposition 5.7.8]
).
Clearly, each element
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and so, in particular, such an
\alpha
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
; on the contrary, an element
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
can be either algebraic or transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
. We denote by
\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}
the subset of those
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
such that the ramification index of the extension
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is finite. We say that an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}
is unramified over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
if the ramification index of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is
1
(we stress that such an element may not be algebraic over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
). Note that
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\subset\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}\subset\mathbb{C}_{p}
. We also set
\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}=\mathbb{O}_{p}\cap\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}
.

3.1.1
Residually algebraic extensions

The following theorem characterizes the DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
which are residually algebraic extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
(see
[
28
, Corollary 2.28]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
W
be a DVR of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
which is a residually algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
.
Then there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}
, transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
, such that

W=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha}=\{\phi\in\mathbb{Q}(X)\mid\phi(\alpha)\in\mathbb{O}_{p}\}.

(3.2)

In particular,
\mathcal{W}_{p,\text{ra}}=\{\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}
,
\alpha
transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}\}
.
Furthermore,
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
if and only if the extension of the residue fields
W/M\supseteq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is finite.

Remark 3.3
.

We remark that if
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, we may define in general the following valuation domain as above:

\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha}=\{\phi\in\mathbb{Q}(X)\mid\phi(\alpha)\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\}

By
[
25
, Proposition 2.2]
,
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha}
is a DVR precisely if
\alpha
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
, it has rank
2
otherwise. In this last case, if
q\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
is the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
, we have
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha}\subset\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}
.

3.1.2
Residually transcendental extensions

Let
W
be a DVR of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
which is residually transcendental over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}

for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
.
The next important theorem gives an alternative characterization of these valuations than Theorem
1.6
,
namely, we show that we can parametrize them by means of pairs of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\times\mathbb{Q}
rather than pairs of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\times\mathbb{Q}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
and let
W
be a residually transcendental extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
to
\mathbb{Q}(X)
.
Then
W
is a DVR such that
W=\mathbb{Z}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{Q}(X)
for some
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\times\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

We have already said that
W
is a DVR in Remark
1.3
. By Corollary
2.11
,
W
is equal to the restriction to
\mathbb{Q}(X)
of a (unique) residually transcendental extension
\widetilde{W}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(X)
. By Theorem
1.6
,
\widetilde{W}=\mathbb{Z}_{p,\alpha,\delta}
for some
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\times\mathbb{Q}
. Hence,
W=\mathbb{Z}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{Q}(X)
.
∎

The advantage of working over the completion of
\mathbb{Q}
with respect to the
p
-adic valuation
v_{p}
is that there exists a unique extension of
v_{p}
to
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
since
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is Henselian. Therefore we don’t have to worry about fixing an extension of
v_{p}
to
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
in advance (as in Theorem
1.6
).

3.1.3
Unitary DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)

In order to unify the aforementioned two cases, namely, residually algebraic
and transcendental extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
to
\mathbb{Q}(X)
, we consider pairs
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
. In the following we will always assume the following conditions for such a pair:

-

if
\delta=\infty
, then
\alpha
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
.

-

if
\delta\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
.

The first assumption is due to Theorem
3.1
. The second assumption is due to the following fact : in the case of a monomial valuation
v_{p,\alpha,\delta}
as introduced in section
1.3.1
, this valuation can also be defined when
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, but by a density argument we may take
\alpha^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
such that
v_{p,\alpha,\delta}=v_{p,\alpha^{\prime},\delta}
(see Remark
3.18
(2) below).
Consequently, given a pair
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, automatically
\delta=\infty
. The converse is not true: we can consider pairs
(\alpha,\infty)
, where
\alpha
is either in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
or in
\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
with the above assumptions. We recall that the value group of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
is of finite index over the value group of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
(by Remark
1.3
(1) if
\delta\not=\infty
and by
[
25
, Proposition 2.2]
if
\delta=\infty
). In particular, if
\delta\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
is a DVR. Furthermore, if
\delta=\infty
, by
[
28
, Proposition 2.14, Remark 2.18, Corollary 2.28]
,
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
is a DVR precisely when
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}
(we are assuming that
\alpha
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
). If
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}
, then
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\infty}
is a one-dimensional non-discrete valuation domain (by the same references).

Given
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
, we denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
the valuation domain
\mathbb{Z}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{Q}(X)
. In particular,
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\infty}
by Remark
1.2
.
The next theorem summarizes the results discussed so far and give a complete description of the unitary DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
W
be a DVR of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
lying above
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Then
the following statements are satisfied.

(1)

W=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
for some
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
. In this case,
X\in W
if and only if
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})
.

(2)

W
is residually transcendental (algebraic, respectively) over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}

if and only if
\delta<\infty
(
\alpha
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\delta=\infty
, respectively).

In particular, we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{p,\text{ra}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}\text{ and }\alpha\text{ is transcendental over }\mathbb{Q}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{p,\text{rt}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}\mid(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\times\mathbb{Q}\}

Proof.

If
W
is residually transcendental over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, then there exists
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\times\mathbb{Q}

such that
W=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
(Theorem
3.4
).
Next if
W
is residually algebraic over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, then there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}

such that
\alpha
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
and
W=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha}
(Theorem
3.1
).

The second part of item (1) follows by Lemma
1.7
. The final claim follows immediately.
∎

3.2
Polynomial ordering of unitary valuation domains and ultrametric balls in
\mathbb{C}_{p}

For
p\in\mathbb{P}
, we set

\mathcal{S}_{p}=\{\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}\mid(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})\}.

In this subsection, we study under which conditions we have
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
for

(\alpha,\delta),(\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
, and we show that
\mathcal{S}_{p}
is a
partially ordered set.

Let

\mathcal{S}=(\bigcup\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{S}_{p})\cup\{\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}\mid q\in\mathbb{Q}[X]\text{ is irreducible }\}.

It is noteworthy that if
W
is a two-dimensional valuation overring of
\mathbb{Z}[X]
with
W\subset\mathbb{Z}_{(p),0,0}
, then
W\neq\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
for all
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
. Therefore
\mathcal{S}
is not the set of all valuation rings of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
, while
\mathcal{S}
contains the set of all DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
by Theorem
3.6
.

Now, in order to show the correspondence between the valuation domains of
\mathcal{S}_{p}
and ultrametric balls in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
, we need to consider ultrametric balls possibly having infinite radius (thus, a singleton). Let
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
. The ball in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
of center
\alpha
and radius
\delta
is
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)=\{x\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\mid v_{p}(x-\alpha)\geq\delta\}
. Note that
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)=\{\alpha\}
precisely when
\delta=\infty
. If
\delta\in\mathbb{Q}
,
then
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
has a non-empty intersection with
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, because
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
lies dense in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
, and so we may find an element in
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)\cap\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
which can be considered as the center of the ball.

We will show that the ordering on
\mathcal{S}_{p}
reflects the containment among ultrametric balls in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
under the action of the Galois group
G_{p}
(Proposition

and Remarks

).
We first need a couple of lemmas. The first lemma uses the notion of pseudo-stationary sequence introduced in
[
9
]
, see also
[
26
]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
and suppose that
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{j=1,\ldots,n}B_{j}
,
where the
B_{j}
’s are polynomially closed subsets of
\mathbb{C}_{p}
. Then
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)\subseteq B_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

If
\delta=\infty
, then
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)=\{\alpha\}
and the claim clearly is true.

Suppose that
\delta\in\mathbb{Q}
. Let
E=\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
be a pseudo-stationary sequence with set of pseudo-limits
\mathcal{L}_{E}=\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
.
By the pigeonhole principle, a subsequence of
E
, say
E^{\prime}=\{s_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, is contained in
B_{i}
, for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
;
note that
E^{\prime}
is still pseudo-stationary with
\mathcal{L}_{E^{\prime}}=\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
. By
[
9
, Proposition 4.8]
,

\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)\subseteq B_{i}
, as claimed.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

More generally, Lemma
3.7
holds for any valuation domain
V
having infinite residue field;
we don’t need that the polynomial closure is a topological closure,
which in general is not true for valuation domains of arbitrary rank (
[
30
]
).

We denote by
G_{p}
the Galois group of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. We recall that
G_{p}
is identified with
the group of all continuous
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-automorphism of
\mathbb{C}_{p}
(see
[
5
, §3, 1.]
) and so
G_{p}
acts on
\mathbb{C}_{p}
.
Moreover, for each
\sigma\in G_{p}
, we have
v_{p}\circ\sigma=v_{p}
, because
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is Henselian.

The following remark contains important informations about the set of conjugates of an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\mathbb{Q}_{p}
under the action of the group
G_{p}
.

Remark 3.9
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
. By
[
5
, Remark 3.2]
,
G_{p}(\{\alpha\})=\{\sigma(\alpha)\mid\sigma\in G_{p}\}
is
an infinite subset of
\mathbb{C}_{p}
precisely when
\alpha\notin\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. In this case, by
[
5
, Theorem 3.5]
,
G_{p}(\{\alpha\})

is totally disconnected, closed and compact (note that this is true also when
G_{p}(\{\alpha\})
is a finite set).
In particular, by
[
9
, Proposition 2.4]
,
G_{p}(\{\alpha\})
is polynomially closed.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then,
G_{p}(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}))
is polynomially closed.

Proof.

We remark that

G_{p}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}))=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}G_{p}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}))=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\bigcup_{\sigma\in G_{p}}\bar{B}_{p}(\sigma(\alpha_{i}),\delta_{i}).

(3.11)

If
\alpha_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, then
G_{p}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}))
is equal to a finite union of balls
\bar{B}_{p}(\sigma(\alpha_{i}),\delta_{i})
,
for
\sigma\in G_{p}
(which may not be necessarily disjoint);
therefore, by
[
9
, Proposition 3.4]
,
G_{p}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}))
is polynomially closed.
If
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
(so
\delta_{i}
is necessarily equal to
\infty
by our convention),
then
G_{p}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}))=G_{p}(\{\alpha_{i}\})
is polynomially closed by Remark
3.9
.
Hence,
G_{p}(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}))
is polynomially closed, being a finite union of polynomially closed sets.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this subsection by which we
can show that
\mathcal{S}_{p}
is a partially ordered set. We need first the following remark, which gives the connection with rings of integer-valued polynomials.

Remark 3.12
.

Let
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
. If
\delta\not=\infty
, given
c\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
such that
v_{p}(c)=\delta
we have the following equalities:

\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{C}_{p}[X]=\mathbb{O}_{p}\left[\frac{X-\alpha}{c}\right]=\textnormal{Int}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{O}_{p}).

The first equality follows from
[
26
, Remark 3.4]
and the second one
from
[
26
, Lemma 4.1]
, since
\mathbb{O}_{p}
is a rank one non-discrete valuation domain with infinite residue field. More generally, it is clear that the equality

\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{C}_{p}[X]=\textnormal{Int}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{O}_{p})

(3.13)

is true also when
\delta=\infty
.
In particular, contracting down the previous equality to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[X]
(and similarly to
\mathbb{Q}[X]
), we have

\mathbb{Z}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{Q}_{p}[X]=\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{O}_{p})

(3.14)

Clearly, we have

\textnormal{Int}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{O}_{p})=\bigcap_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)}\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha^{\prime}}\cap\mathbb{C}_{p}[X]

(3.15)

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
and
(\alpha,\delta),(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]\subset\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

(2)

\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]\subset\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
.

(3)

\delta\geq\delta_{i}
and
v_{p}(\alpha-\sigma(\alpha_{i}))\geq\delta_{i}
for some
\sigma\in G_{p}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) Clear.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3) By Lemma
2.5
, (2) is equivalent to the analogous containment in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(X)
:

\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p,\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}\cap\mathbb{Q}_{p}[X])\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p,\alpha,\delta}.

(3.17)

By (
3.14
), the left-hand side of (
3.17
) is equal to

\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}),\mathbb{O}_{p})=\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(B,\mathbb{O}_{p}),

where
B=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})
.

A straightforward adaptation of
[
27
, Lemma 2.20]
shows that
the integral closure of
\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(B,\mathbb{O}_{p})
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(X)
is equal to
\textnormal{Int}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(G_{p}(B),\mathbb{O}_{p})
. Hence, (
3.17
) is actually equivalent to

\textnormal{Int}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(G_{p}(B),\mathbb{O}_{p})\subset(\overline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})_{\alpha,\delta}.

By applying Lemma
2.5
again, we can transfer the previous containment over
\mathbb{C}_{p}
:

\textnormal{Int}(G_{p}(B),\mathbb{O}_{p})\subset\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{C}_{p}[X]=\textnormal{Int}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{O}_{p})

which amounts to say that
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
is contained in the polynomial closure of
G_{p}(B)
. By Lemma
3.10
,
G_{p}(B)
is polynomially closed, so
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)\subseteq G_{p}(B)
. By Lemma
3.10
again,
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
G_{p}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}))
is polynomially closed,
so by Lemma
3.7
,
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)\subseteq G_{p}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}))
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
If
\delta_{i}=\infty
, then necessarily
\delta=\infty
and
\alpha=\sigma(\alpha_{i})
for some
\sigma\in G_{p}
,
because otherwise a similar argument to the proof of Lemma
3.7
shows
that
G_{p}(\{\alpha_{i}\})
would contain a pseudo-stationary sequence which is not possible by compactness.
If
\delta_{i}<\infty
, again Lemma
3.7
implies that
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
is contained
in
\sigma(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}))=\bar{B}_{p}(\sigma(\alpha_{i}),\delta_{i})
for some
\sigma\in G_{p}
,
which amounts to say that
\delta\geq\delta_{i}
and
v_{p}(\alpha-\sigma(\alpha_{i}))\geq\delta_{i}
, which is precisely condition (3).

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1) Note that
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\sigma(\alpha_{i}),\delta_{i}}
for each
\sigma\in G_{p}

by Theorem
1.9
. Note also that the inequality
v_{p}(\alpha-\sigma(\alpha_{i}))\geq\delta_{i}

by (
1.5
) is equivalent to
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\sigma(\alpha_{i}),\delta_{i}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta_{i}}
. Therefore,

\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta_{i}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]\subset\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}

where the last containment follows from the inequality
\delta\geq\delta_{i}
.
∎

Remarks 3.18
.

(1) Let
(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
, for
i=1,2
. By Proposition
3.16
, we have the following equivalence:

\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]\subset\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{2},\delta_{2}}\Leftrightarrow\delta_{2}\geq\delta_{1}\;\text{ and }v_{p}(\alpha_{2}-\sigma(\alpha_{1}))\geq\delta_{1}\;\text{ for some }\sigma\in G_{p}.

This last condition is equivalent to saying that

\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{2},\delta_{2})\subseteq\sigma(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}))=\bar{B}_{p}(\sigma(\alpha_{1}),\delta_{1}).

In particular, the residually algebraic DVRs
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\infty}
of
\mathcal{S}_{p}
are precisely the maximal elements of
\mathcal{S}_{p}
under this ordering (Corollary
2.2
(2)) and each residually transcendental
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
, with
\delta\in\mathbb{Q}
, is strictly less than a maximal element of
\mathcal{S}_{p}
, namely,
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha^{\prime},\infty}
for
\alpha^{\prime}\in\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
.

(2) Let
(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
for
i=1,2
be such that
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{2},\delta_{2}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
. By Proposition
3.16
, we have

\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}
,
v_{p}(\alpha_{2}-\sigma_{1}(\alpha_{1}))\geq\delta_{1}
, and

v_{p}(\alpha_{1}-\sigma_{2}(\alpha_{2}))\geq\delta_{2}
for some
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in G_{p}
.
If
\delta_{1}=\infty
, then
\alpha_{1}=\sigma(\alpha_{2})
for some
\sigma\in G_{p}
(that is,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are conjugate over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
),
so, in particular,
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{2},\delta_{2}}
. If instead we have
\delta_{i}<\infty

for
i=1,2
, then
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\sigma(\alpha_{1}),\delta_{1}}
by Theorem
1.9

and
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\sigma_{1}(\alpha_{1}),\delta_{1}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{2},\delta_{1}}
by (
1.5
),
and hence again we get
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{2},\delta_{2}}
.
Thus,
\mathcal{S}_{p}
is a partially ordered set under
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{2},\delta_{2}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]

for
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{p}
be the set of ultrametric balls in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
with center in
\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}
and possibly infinite radius, namely:

\mathcal{B}_{p}=\{\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)\mid(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})\}

with the conventions that

-

if
\delta=\infty
, then
\alpha
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
.

-

if
\delta<\infty
, then
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
.

Note that the Galois group
G_{p}=\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts on
\mathcal{B}_{p}
(Lemma
1.8
and Theorem
1.9
), we denote by
\mathcal{B}_{p}/G_{p}
the set of equivalence classes of
\mathcal{B}_{p}
under this action. Note that, by Remark
3.9
, the cardinality of an equivalence class of a ball
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
under the action of
G_{p}
is infinite precisely when
\delta=\infty
and
\alpha\not\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
.

Theorem 3.19
.

The map

\begin{array}[]{lcc}\Theta_{p}:&\mathcal{B}_{p}/G_{p}\to&\mathcal{W}_{p}\\
&{[}\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta){]}\mapsto&\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}\end{array}

is a bijection.

Proof.

We show first that
\Theta_{p}
is well-defined. If
[\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)]=[\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})]
then there exists
\sigma\in G_{p}
such that
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)=\sigma(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}))=\bar{B}_{p}(\sigma(\alpha^{\prime}),\delta^{\prime})
, so in particular we get
\delta=\delta^{\prime}
and
v_{p}(\alpha-\sigma(\alpha^{\prime}))\geq\delta
. Hence, by Proposition
3.16
, we have
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
and thus by Remark
3.18
(2) we get
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}
.

For the injectivity of
\Theta_{p}
, if the equality
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}
holds, then in particular
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
and the same arguments as above show that
[\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)]=[\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})]
.

The surjectivity of
\Theta_{p}
follows from Theorem
3.6
.
∎

4
Polynomial Krull domains

We are interested in characterizing polynomial Krull Domains, i.e., Krull domains
R
such that
\mathbb{Z}[X]\subseteq R\subseteq\mathbb{Q}[X]
.
Note that since
\mathbb{Z}[X]
is a
2
-dimensional Noetherian domain, by
[
18
, Theorem 9]
such a Krull domain
R
is an integrally closed Noetherian domain of Krull dimension at most
2
. Conversely,
it is well-known that every integrally closed Noetherian domain is a Krull domain (for example, see
[
32
, Chapter VI, §13, (b), p. 82]
). Hence, we are describing the family of integrally closed Noetherian domains between
\mathbb{Z}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
. Given a prime ideal
Q
of an integral domain
R
as above, we say that
Q
is
unitary
if
Q\cap\mathbb{Z}=p\mathbb{Z}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
and that
Q
is
non-unitary
if
Q\cap\mathbb{Z}=(0)
.

4.1
Representations of polynomial Krull domains

We recall that we have the following representation of the PID
\mathbb{Q}[X]
as an intersection of DVRs:

\mathbb{Q}[X]=\bigcap_{q\in\mathcal{P}^{\text{irr}}}\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}

where
\mathcal{P}^{\text{irr}}
is the set of irreducible polynomials of
\mathbb{Q}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}
is the localization of
\mathbb{Q}[X]
at the prime ideal generated by
q(X)
.

In general, let
R
be a polynomial Krull domain,

\Lambda=\{p\in\mathbb{P}\mid pR\subset R\}
,
X^{1}(R)
be the set
of height one prime ideals of
R
, and
X^{1}_{p}(R)=\{Q\in X^{1}(R)\mid p\in Q\}
for each
p\in\Lambda
.
Then
X^{1}_{p}(R)
is finite,
R_{Q}
is a DVR for all
Q\in X^{1}_{p}(R)
, and

R=\bigcap_{p\in\Lambda}(\bigcap_{Q\in X^{1}_{p}(R)}R_{Q})\cap\mathbb{Q}[X].

Hence, for each
p\in\Lambda
, there exists a finite set
\{W_{p,1},\dots,W_{p,m_{p}}\}

of DVRs with quotient field
\mathbb{Q}(X)
, so that
W_{p,j}\cap\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for
j=1,\dots,m_{p}
, and

R=\bigcap_{p\in\Lambda}(\bigcap_{j=1}^{m_{p}}W_{p,j})\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\bigcap_{p\in\Lambda}(\bigcap_{j=1}^{m_{p}}(W_{p,j}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X])).

(4.1)

For each
p\in\Lambda
, let

R_{p}=\bigcap_{j=1}^{m_{p}}(W_{p,j}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X])

(4.2)

It is clear that

R_{p}
is a Krull domain between
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
and
R=\bigcap\limits_{p\in\Lambda}R_{p}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
and
R
be a ring between
\mathbb{Z}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
such that

R=\bigcap\limits_{Q\in X^{1}(R)}R_{Q}
and
R_{Q}
is a DVR for all
Q\in X^{1}(R)
,
X^{1}_{p}(R)=\{Q\in X^{1}(R)\mid p\in Q\}

and
R_{p}=(\bigcap\limits_{Q\in X^{1}_{p}(R)}R_{Q})\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
.
Then the following statements hold true.

(1)

R=\bigcap\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}R_{p}
.

(2)

If
R_{(p)}=(\mathbb{Z}\setminus p\mathbb{Z})^{-1}R
, then
R_{(p)}=R_{p}
.

(3)

R_{(p)}\cap\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.

Proof.

(1) If
Q
is a prime ideal of
R
, either
Q\cap\mathbb{Z}=(0)
or
Q\cap\mathbb{Z}=p\mathbb{Z}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
.
Moreover, if
Q\cap\mathbb{Z}=(0)
, then
\mathbb{Q}[X]\subseteq R_{Q}
.
Hence,
R=R\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\bigcap\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}((\bigcap\limits_{Q\in X^{1}_{p}(R)}R_{Q})\cap\mathbb{Q}[X])=\bigcap\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}R_{p}
.

(2)
(\subseteq)
Let
\frac{f}{n}\in R_{(p)}
, where
f\in R
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is not divisible by
p
. Then clearly
\frac{f}{n}\in R_{Q}
,
for every
Q\in X^{1}_{p}(R)
since the value of each of the corresponding valuation of
n
is zero and
R\subseteq R_{p}
.
Thus,
R_{(p)}\subseteq R_{p}
.

(\supseteq)
Conversely, let
h\in R_{p}
. Then we may write
h=\frac{g}{mp^{k}}
,
for some
g\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
not divisible by
p
and
k\geq 0
.
Then
mh=\frac{g}{p^{k}}
is clearly in
R
(because for each
q\in\mathbb{P}
,
q\not=p
,
p^{k}
is a unit of
R_{Q}
for all
Q\in X^{1}_{q}(R)
).
Thus, we also have
R_{(p)}\supseteq R_{p}
.

(3) The result follows from the following two observations: (i)
R_{Q}\cap\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for all
Q\in X^{1}_{p}(R)

and (ii)
R_{(p)}=R_{p}
by (2).
∎

Let
R
be as in Lemma
4.3
and
R_{p}^{\prime}=\bigcap\limits_{Q\in X^{1}_{p}(R)}R_{Q}
for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Then
R
is a Krull domain
if and only if
R_{p}
is a Krull domain for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
and, for any
0\neq f\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
,

fR_{p}^{\prime}=R_{p}^{\prime}
for all but a finitely many
p
’s in
\mathbb{P}
.
Hence, in the remaining part of this paper, we first study when
R_{p}
is a Krull domain and we then study the Krull domain
R
by investigating the property that if
f\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
is nonzero, then
fR_{p}^{\prime}=R_{p}^{\prime}
for all but a finitely many
p
’s in
\mathbb{P}
. The first case will be called “Local case” and the second one “Global case”.

4.2
Local case

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
be fixed. In this section we consider a Krull domain
R
between
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
; we show that
R
can be represented as a ring of integer-valued polynomials over a finite union of ultrametric balls in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
. Clearly, for every such
R
we have

R=\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}W_{j}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]

(4.4)

where
\{W_{i}\mid j=1,\ldots,m\}
is a finite family (possibly empty) of DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
lying above
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.

4.2.1
Irredundant representation

We first investigate when a representation of a Krull domain between
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
as in (
4.4
) is irredundant. Let
D
be an integral domain with quotient field
K
and suppose that
D=\bigcap\limits_{i\in I}V_{i}
, where
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a family of valuation domains of
K
(by Krull’s Theorem this is equivalent to say that
D
is integrally closed in
K
). As in
[
15
]
,
we say that an element
V=V_{i_{0}}
of this family is superfluous if
D=\bigcap\limits_{i\in I,i\not=i_{0}}V_{i}
and that the representation
D=\bigcap\limits_{i\in I}V_{i}
is irredundant if no element
V_{i}
of the family
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is superfluous.

The following result is classical, see for example
[
23
, Remark on page 296]
or
[
14
, Corollary 43.9]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
D
be a Krull domain. Then
\{D_{P}\mid P\in X^{1}(D)\}
is
the unique defining family for
D
, that is,
if
\{V_{\alpha}\}
is a defining family for
D
, then

\{D_{P}\mid P\in X^{1}(D)\}\subseteq\{V_{\alpha}\}
.

Let
D
be a Krull domain.
Theorem
4.5
also says that if
\Lambda
is a subset of
X^{1}(D)
,
then
D\subsetneq\bigcap\limits_{P\in\Lambda}D_{P}
if and only if
\Lambda\subsetneq X^{1}(D)
.
In particular, for every
P\in X^{1}(D)
,
D_{P}
is not superfluous for the defining family
\{D_{P}\mid P\in X^{1}(D)\}
,
which therefore realizes the unique irredundant representation of
D
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
D
be a Krull domain,
\{V_{\alpha}\}
be a defining family for
D

and
V\in\{V_{\alpha}\}
with maximal ideal
M
.

(1)

V
is superfluous in
D=\bigcap\limits_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}

if and only if ht
(M\cap D)\geq 2
.

(2)

The intersection
D=\bigcap\limits_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}
is irredundant if and only if ht
(M_{\alpha}\cap D)=1
for all
\alpha
.

Proof.

(1) Suppose that
V
is not superfluous in
D=\bigcap\limits_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}

and let
R=\bigcap\limits_{V_{\alpha}\neq V}V_{\alpha}
. Then
D=R\cap V
and
R\nsubseteq V
.
Hence
V=D_{M\cap D}

[
19
, Lemma 1.3]
, and since
V
is a DVR,
ht
(M\cap D)=
ht
((M\cap D)D_{M\cap D})=1
. Conversely, assume that ht
(M\cap D)<2
.
Then ht
(M\cap D)=1
, and hence
D_{M\cap D}
is a DVR.
Note that
D_{M\cap D}\subseteq V
, so
D_{M\cap D}=V
.
Thus
V
is not superfluous in
D=\bigcap\limits_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}
.

(2) This follows directly from (1).
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
R
be as in (
4.4
) and let
W=W_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. If
W^{\prime}
is a DVR extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
such that
w<w^{\prime}
, then
R\subset W^{\prime}
and the center of
W^{\prime}
on
R
has height two.

Moreover, if
W^{\prime\prime}
is another DVR extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
such that
w<w^{\prime}\leq w^{\prime\prime}
, then
M\cap R\subset M^{\prime}\cap R=M^{\prime\prime}\cap R
.

Proof.

By assumption that
w<w^{\prime}
, we have
R\subseteq W\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]\subseteq W^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
.
Hence
W^{\prime}
is superfluous in
R\cap W^{\prime}
, and since
W\neq W^{\prime}
, we have ht
(M^{\prime}\cap R)\geq 2
by Corollary
4.6
. Note that dim
(R)\leq 2
.
Thus ht
(M^{\prime}\cap R)=2
.

For the last statement, we have
R\subset W^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]\subseteq W^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
, so
M^{\prime}\cap R\subseteq M^{\prime\prime}\cap R
. Since dim
(R)\leq 2
, equality holds in the previous containment.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
R
be as in (
4.4
). Then, for every
q\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
irreducible,
\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}
is not superfluous in that representation of
R
.

Proof.

Let
Q
be the center of
\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}
on
R
.
Then
Q\cap\mathbb{Z}=(0)
, and hence if
S=\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, then
R_{S}=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}(W_{i})_{S}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\mathbb{Q}[X]

[
14
, Proposition 43.5]
and
QR_{S}\subsetneq R_{S}
. Thus ht
Q=
ht
(QR_{S})=1
,
so, by Corollary
4.6
, 2.,
\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}
is not superfluous.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

(1) By Proposition
3.16
, geometrically the representation of
R
in (
4.4
) is irredundant if and only if the balls
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})
,
(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
, are disjoint under the action of the Galois group
G_{p}
in the following sense: for each pair of balls
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i_{1}},\delta_{i_{1}}),\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i_{2}},\delta_{i_{2}})
, there is no
\sigma\in G_{p}
such that
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i_{1}},\delta_{i_{1}})
is contained in
\sigma(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i_{2}},\delta_{i_{2}}))
(see Remarks
3.18
). Equivalently, the balls
\bar{B}_{p}(\sigma(\alpha_{i}),\delta_{i})
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
\sigma\in G_{p}
, are disjoint. We stress that the number of such balls is finite if and only if there is no
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
(see Remark
3.9
).

(2) By Corollary
4.6
and Corollary
4.8
,
the representation of
R
in (
4.4
) is irredundant
if and only if
M_{j}\cap R
has height one for
j=1,\ldots,m
, where
M_{j}
is the maximal ideal of
W_{j}
.

Now we study some basic ring-theoretic properties of
R=\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{m}W_{j}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
,
which are very useful for the study of polynomial Krull domains. In particular, we characterize when the prime unitary ideals of
R
are maximal. If
W
is a DVR of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
, we denote by
e(W\mid\mathbb{Z}_{(p)})
the ramification index of the extension
W\supset\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
. If
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}
, we denote by
e(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)\mid\mathbb{Q}_{p})
the ramification index of the extension
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)\supseteq\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(we stress that this extension is algebraic precisely when
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
).

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Suppose that
W_{j}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}
, for some
(\alpha_{j},\delta_{j})\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
,
is a DVR of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
and
X\in W_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,m
. Now, let

M_{j}=M_{(p),\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}
for each
j=1,\ldots,m
, and

R=\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}W_{j}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X].

(4.11)

If the representation of
R
in (
4.11
) is irredundant (namely, ht
(M_{j}\cap R)=1
for
j=1,\dots,m
), then the following statements hold:

(1)

R
is a Krull domain such that
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\subseteq R\subseteq\mathbb{Q}[X]
.

(2)

X^{1}(R)=\{M_{j}\cap R\mid j=1,\dots,m\}\cup\{f\mathbb{Q}[X]\cap R\mid f\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
is irreducible
\}
.

(3)

M_{j}\cap R
is a maximal ideal of
R
if and only if
W_{j}

is residually algebraic over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.

(4)

Suppose that
(\alpha_{j},\delta_{j})
is a minimal pair if
\delta_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then we have

e(W_{j}|\mathbb{Z}_{(p)})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}e(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha_{j})|\mathbb{Q}_{p}),&\text{ if }\delta_{j}=\infty\\
e(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha_{j})|\mathbb{Q}_{p})e(\gamma_{j}|\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha_{j})),&\text{ if }\delta_{j}<\infty\end{array}\right.

where
\gamma_{j}=w_{j}(f_{\alpha_{j}})
,
f_{\alpha_{j}}
is the minimal polynomial of
\alpha_{j}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
e(\gamma_{j}|\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha_{j}))

is the smallest natural number
n
such that
n\gamma_{j}
is in the value group of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha_{j})
.

(5)

If
e_{j}=e(W_{j}|\mathbb{Z}_{(p)})
for
j=1,\dots,m
and
d=gcd(e_{1},\dots,e_{m})
, then
Cl
(R)=\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{m-1}
.

(6)

R
is Dedekind if and only if
\delta_{j}=\infty
and
\alpha_{i}
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
for each
j=1,\ldots,m
.

Proof.

(1) Clear, since
R
is a finite intersection of Krull domains with the same quotient field (
[
7
, Chapter VII, §1, 3. Exemples, 3)]
).

(2) The containment
(\supseteq)
follows by
[
10
, Corollary 2.3(3)]

and the assumption that
M_{j}\cap R\in X^{1}(R)
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
The other containment follows easily by Theorem
4.5
.

(3) If
W_{j}
is residually transcendental over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, that is,
\delta_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}
(Theorem
3.6
), then the claim follows by Corollary
4.7
. Suppose instead that
W_{j}
is residually algebraic over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, that is,
\delta=\infty
(Theorem
3.6
). We have the following containments:
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\subseteq R/(M_{j}\cap R)\subseteq W_{j}/M_{j}
; since
W_{j}/M_{j}
is algebraic over
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, it follows that
M_{j}\cap R
is a maximal ideal of
R
.

(4) If
\delta_{j}<\infty
, see
[
2
, Theorem 2.1, c)]
, while if
\delta_{j}=\infty
, see
[
25
, Proposition 2.2]
.

(5) Let
G
be a free abelian group of rank
m
on the generators
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}

and
H
be the subgroup of
G
generated by
r=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}e_{i}g_{i}
. Then Cl
(R)=G/H

[
10
, Theorem 2.4]
,
and since
d=gcd(e_{1},\dots,e_{m})
, Cl
(R)=\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{m-1}

[
10
, Remark 1.6]
.

(6) A Krull domain is Dedekind if and only if it has (Krull) dimension one (
[
32
, Theorem 28, Chapt. VI, §13]
). The proof then follows from items (2), (3) and Theorem
3.6
.
∎

Definition 4.12
.

An infinite family
\{W_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
will be said to be

polynomially finite
if there exists a finite set
\{V_{1},\dots,V_{n}\}
of DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
such that

\bigcap\limits_{i\in I}W_{i}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{n}V_{j}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
.

For example, putting together (
3.13
) and (
3.15
), we have

\bigcap_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)}\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha^{\prime},\infty}\cap\mathbb{C}_{p}[X]=\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{C}_{p}[X].

Note that an element of the family
\{\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha^{\prime},\infty}\mid\alpha^{\prime}\in\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)\}
is a DVR
if and only if
\alpha^{\prime}
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
(
[
25
, Proposition 2.2]
), so in particular, not every of the valuation overring
\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha_{p},\infty}
above is a DVR.

The next theorem establishes when an infinite family
\{W_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
is polynomially finite.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Suppose that
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}
, for some
(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
,
is an infinite family of DVRs of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, for
i\in I
,
and let
R=\bigcap\limits_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

R
is a Krull domain.

(2)

The set
\{\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}\mid i\in I\}
is polynomially finite.

(3)

R=\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
for some
\{(\alpha_{j},\delta_{j})\mid j=1,\dots,n\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
.

(4)

There exists a finite subset
\{(\alpha_{j},\delta_{j})\mid j=1,\dots,n\}
of
\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
,
so that
R\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}

and for each
i\in I
, there exists
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
\delta_{i}\geq\delta_{j}
and
v(\alpha_{i}-\sigma(\alpha_{j}))\geq\delta_{j}
,
for some
\sigma\in G_{p}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) Let
\mathcal{A}=X^{1}_{p}(R)
. Then
\mathcal{A}
is finite,

R=\bigcap\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{A}}R_{Q}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
,
R_{Q}
is a DVR of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.
Thus
\{\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}\mid i\in I\}
is polynomially finite.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3) This follows from the fact that each DVR
of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
is of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
for some
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})

by Theorem
3.6
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1) Clear, since
R
is a finite intersection of Krull domains with the same quotient field (
[
7
, Chapter VII, §1, 3. Exemples, 3)]
).

(3)
\Rightarrow
(4) Each
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}
is an overring of
\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
,
so by Proposition
3.16
,
\delta_{i}\geq\delta_{j}
and
v(\alpha_{i}-\sigma(\alpha_{j}))\geq\delta_{j}
,
for some

and

.

(4)
\Rightarrow
(3) Suppose that for each

, there exists

and

,
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}

Remark 4.14
.

Geometrically, condition 4. of Theorem
4.13

means that the union of the balls
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, for

(each of them corresponds to a valuation domain
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}
), is contained in finitely many of them up to the action of the Galois group

, namely:

\bigcup_{i\in I}\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1,\ldots,m}\bigcup_{\sigma\in G_{p}}\bar{B}_{p}(\sigma(\alpha_{j}),\delta_{j}).

We can now characterize Krull Domains
R
between
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
. By Theorem
3.6
, we may restrict to consider in the defining family of DVRs of

only those DVRs extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
defined by pairs
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})
.
We recall that
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
is a Krull domain (for example see
[
13
, Proposition 1.6]
).
If
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),0,0}
is the valuation domain of the trivial Gaussian extension, then
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),0,0}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{O}_{p})
.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
and
R
be a Krull domain between
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
. Then there exist finitely many pairs
(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})
,

, with
\alpha_{i}
transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
if

, such that

R=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left(E_{p},\mathbb{O}_{p}\right),

(4.16)

where
E_{p}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})
. Moreover,
if the representation in (
4.16
) is irredundant (see Remark
4.9
),
then
R
is Dedekind if and only if either
n=0
(and then
R=\mathbb{Q}[X]
) or

and
\delta_{i}=\infty
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
; thus, in either case,
R
is Dedekind if and only if

contains finitely many elements.

Proof.

We remarked at the beginning of this section in (
4.4
) that
R=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
, where
W_{i}
is a DVR of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
containing
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
, for each
i=1,\ldots,n
. By Theorem
3.6
, there exist
(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})
such that
W_{i}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}
, for each
i=1,\ldots,n
. Note that, if
\delta_{i}=\infty
, then necessarily
\alpha
must be transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
(§
3.1.3
). The representation of
R
as a ring of integer-valued polynomials follows by (

).

Clearly,

is equal either to
\mathbb{Q}
or to
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
according to whether the family of DVRs

is empty or not. In the former case,

which is a PID (thus, Dedekind and a UFD). In the latter case, by
[

, Theorem 3.14]
, the Krull domain

is Dedekind if and only if there is no
W_{i}
which is residually transcendental over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
. This corresponds to saying that
\delta_{i}=\infty
for each

, by Theorem
3.6
.

∎

4.2.2
Non-unitary prime ideals

Let
R
be a polynomial Krull domain between
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
as in (
4.4
).
Let
q\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
be irreducible and let
Q=q(X)\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}\cap R
. By Theorem
4.10
(2),
Q
is an height 1 prime ideal of
R
and
R_{Q}=\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}
. In this section we characterize when
Q
is a maximal ideal of
R
.

In general, we remark that if
W
is a valuation domain of the field of rational functions
K(X)
over a field
K
and
W
has dimension at least
2
, then
W\cap K=V
is a valuation domain of
K
which is not
K
itself. In fact,
W\cap K=K\Leftrightarrow K\subset W
and in this case
W
would be a DVR (
[
11
, p. 3]
).

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
W
be a valuation domain of
K(X)
such that
W\subset K[X]_{(q)}
for some irreducible
q\in K[X]
. Let
V=W\cap K
. Then, there exist a root
\alpha\in\overline{K}
of
q(X)
and an extension
U
of
V
to
K(\alpha)
such that
W=U_{\alpha}=\{\phi\in K(X)\mid\phi(\alpha)\in U\}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:K[X]_{(q)}\to K[X]/(q)\cong K(\alpha)
be the canonical residue map, where
\alpha
is the residue class of
X
and can be identified to a root of
q
. Note that for
\phi\in K[X]_{(q)}
,
\pi(\phi)=\phi(\alpha)
because
\pi
is an homomorphism.

It is well-known that every valuation domain
W
strictly contained in
K[X]_{(q)}
is of the form
\pi^{-1}(U)
, where
U
is a valuation domain of
K(\alpha)
; moreover,
U=\pi(W)=W/Q
, where
Q
is the maximal ideal of
K[X]_{(q)}
. Hence,

U=W/Q=\{\phi(\alpha)\mid\phi\in W\}=\{\phi(\alpha)\in K(\alpha)\mid\phi(\alpha)\in U\}.

(4.18)

(Actually, every element of
K(\alpha)
is of the form
g(\alpha)
for some
g\in K[X]
). Moreover, the maximal ideal of
U
is equal to
M_{W}/Q
, where
M_{W}
is the maximal ideal of
W
. In particular,
U\cap K=V
. In fact, if
\beta\in V=W\cap K
, then
\pi(\beta)=\beta
because
\pi_{\mid K}
is the identity. Hence,
\beta=\pi(\beta)\in U=W/Q
. Similary, one can show that
M_{V}\subseteq M_{U}=M_{W}/Q
, the maximal ideal of
U
. Therefore,
V=U\cap K
.

Finally, by (
4.18
) and the above considerations,

W=\pi^{-1}(U)=\{\phi\in K(X)\mid\phi(\alpha)\in U\}=U_{\alpha}.

∎

We remark that if we consider the completion of
K(\alpha)
with respect to the valuation domain
U
, we may consider
\alpha
as an element of
\overline{\widehat{K}}
, algebraic over
K
, so that we have the equality:

W=\{\phi\in K(X)\mid\phi(\alpha)\in\widehat{U}\}

where
\widehat{U}
is the completion of
U
.

Theorem 4.19
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
and let
R=\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
be a Krull domain, where
(\alpha_{j},\delta_{j})\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
for
j=1,\ldots,m
. Let
Q=q(X)\mathbb{Q}[X]\cap R
be a non-unitary prime ideal of
R
, for some irreducible
q\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
. Then
Q
is a maximal ideal of
R
if and only if
q
has no root in
\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{m}\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{j},\delta_{j})
.

Proof.

Note that when
R
is Dedekind (that is,
\delta_{j}=\infty
for
j=1,\ldots,m
by Theorem
4.10
, (7)), then
Q
is automatically maximal. In this case each ball
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{j},\delta_{j})
is the singleton
\{\alpha_{j}\}
which is necessarily transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
because otherwise
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{j},\infty}
would not be a DVR.

In general, suppose that
q(\alpha^{\prime})=0
for some
\alpha^{\prime}\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{j},\delta_{j})
. Then, we have these containments:

R\subset\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha^{\prime}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha^{\prime},\infty}\subset\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}

where the first containment follows by Proposition
3.16
and the second one from the fact that
\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}=\{\phi\in K(X)\mid\phi(\alpha^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\}
. In particular,
Q\subset M_{(p),\alpha^{\prime}}\cap R
, so
Q
is not a maximal ideal.

Conversely, suppose that
Q\subset M\subset R
for some height
2
maximal ideal
M
of
R
, which necessarily contains
p
. By
[
14
, Corollary 19.7]
, there exists a
2
-dimensional valuation overring
W
of
R
with non-zero prime ideals
Q^{\prime}\subset M^{\prime}
such that
Q^{\prime}\cap R=Q
and
M^{\prime}\cap R=M
. Note that
W\cap\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
and
W_{Q^{\prime}}=R_{Q}=\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}
. By Proposition
4.17
, there exist an extension
U
of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
to
\mathbb{Q}[X]/(q)
and a root
\alpha^{\prime}
of
q(X)
such that
W=\{\phi\in\mathbb{Q}(X)\mid\phi(\alpha^{\prime})\in U\}
. By the remark after that Proposition, we may assume that
\alpha^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, necessarily algebraic over
\mathbb{Q}
, so that
W=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha^{\prime}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha^{\prime},\infty}
. Since
R\subset\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha^{\prime}}
, it follows by Proposition
3.16
that
\alpha^{\prime}
(or possibly some of its conjugate) is in
\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{j},\delta_{j})
, as claimed.
∎

Corollary 4.20
.

Let
R
and
Q
be as in Theorem
4.19
and suppose that
Q
is not a maximal ideal. Then the maximal ideals of
R
containing
Q
are unitary and they are finitely many.

Proof.

If
Q\subset M\subset R
where
M
is a maximal ideal, the above proof of Theorem
4.19
shows that
M=M_{(p),\alpha^{\prime}}\cap R
, where
\alpha^{\prime}
is a root of
q(X)
. Hence, such
M
’s are finitely many.
∎

4.2.3
Unique factorization domains

In this section we completely describe the UFDs between
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
, where
p\in\mathbb{Z}
is a fixed prime.
It is known that an integral domain is a UFD if and only if it is a Krull domain with trivial divisor class group
[
13
, Proposition 6.1]
.

The following lemma follows from
[
29
, Proposition 3.2 & Proposition 3.7]
, but for the sake of the reader we give a self-contained proof.

Lemma 4.21
.

Suppose
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is unramified over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and let
q_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}[X]
be its minimal polynomial. Then,
v_{p,\alpha,\delta}(q_{\alpha})=k\delta+\gamma
, for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
and
k=\#(\Omega_{\alpha}\cap\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta))
, where
\Omega_{\alpha}
is the set of conjugates of
\alpha
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Proof.

We remark that
\Omega_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)
because
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a Galois extension. Note also that
q_{\alpha}(X)=\prod\limits_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\alpha}}(X-\alpha^{\prime})
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)
.
For each
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\alpha}
, we have

v_{p,\alpha,\delta}(X-\alpha^{\prime})=\min\{\delta,v_{p}(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})\}

so that

v_{p,\alpha,\delta}(q_{\alpha})=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\alpha}\cap\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)}\delta+\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\alpha}\setminus\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)}v_{p}(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})=k\delta+\gamma

where
\gamma=\sum\limits_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\alpha}\setminus\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)}v_{p}(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})
. Let
O_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)}
be
the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)
, so
O_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)}
is a valuation domain.
Then, as
\alpha
is unramified,

O_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
have the same value group, and thus we have
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
. More precisely,
\gamma=v_{p}(h(\alpha))
, where
h(X)=\prod\limits_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\alpha}\setminus\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)}(X-\alpha^{\prime})
.
∎

Next, we show that the cardinality of the set of conjugates of
\alpha
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
in
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
, namely, the quantity
\#(\Omega_{\alpha}\cap\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta))
is 1 when
\alpha
is unramified over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
(\alpha,\delta)
is a minimal pair.

Proposition 4.22
.

Let
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\times\mathbb{Q}
be a minimal pair such that
\alpha
is unramified over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Then
\#(\Omega_{\alpha}\cap\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta))=1
. In particular,
\gamma=v_{p,\alpha,\delta}(q_{\alpha})=\delta+\lambda
, for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
and we therefore have:

e(\gamma\mid\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha))=e(\delta\mid\mathbb{Q}_{p})

Proof.

Suppose first that
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, so that
q_{\alpha}(X)=X-\alpha
and
\Omega_{\alpha}=\{\alpha\}
. Then
v_{p,\alpha,\delta}(q_{\alpha})=\delta
and the statement is true in this case.

Suppose now that
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. We consider the following constants:

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{v_{p}(\alpha-c)\mid c\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}},\ [\mathbb{Q}_{p}(c):\mathbb{Q}_{p}]<[\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\}

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{v_{p}(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})\mid\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\alpha}\setminus\{\alpha\}\}

which are both well-defined because
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. It is known that
\delta_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\alpha)
is a maximum (see for example
[
28
, Lemma 1.2]
) and so in general we have the inequality
\delta_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\alpha)\leq\omega_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\alpha)
(which follows by the classical Krasner’s lemma). Since
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is unramified, by
[
22
, Theorem 1.3]
we have the equality
\delta_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\alpha)=\omega_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\alpha)
. Moreover, from the definitions it follows that
(\alpha,\delta)
is a minimal pair if and only if
\delta>\delta_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)}
. Now, if
\beta\in\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
, we have

v_{p}(\alpha-\beta)\geq\delta>\delta_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)}=\omega_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\alpha)

so, the only conjugate of
\alpha
in
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
is
\alpha
itself, that is,
\#(\Omega_{\alpha}\cap\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta))=1
.

The last claim now follows by Lemma
4.21
and the displayed equation is clear from the fact that
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

The next theorem characterizes when a DVR of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
is unramified over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.

Theorem 4.23
.

Let
W
be a DVR of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
containing
X
. Then
W
is unramified over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
if and only if there exists a minimal pair
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})
such that
W=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is unramified.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.6
, there exists
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
such that
W=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
.

Suppose first that
W
is residually algebraic over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, so, by the same Theorem
\delta=\infty
and
\alpha
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
. In this case, if
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, then by
[
25
, Proposition 2.2]
we have
e(W\mid\mathbb{Z}_{(p)})=e(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)\mid\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. If instead
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, then
e(W\mid\mathbb{Z}_{(p)})=e(\mathbb{Z}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\mid\mathbb{Z}_{p})=e(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)\mid\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, where the first equality follows from
[
28
, Proposition 2.24]
and the second equality from
[
28
, Proposition 2.14]
. In either case, the claim follows.

Suppose now that
W
is residually transcendental over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, so in particular,
\delta\in\mathbb{Q}
by the aforementioned Theorem, and we may assume that
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. By
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
,
e(W\mid\mathbb{Z}_{(p)})=1
if and only if the following condition holds:

there exists a minimal pair
(\alpha,\delta)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\times\mathbb{Q}
of definition of
W
such that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is unramified and
w(q_{\alpha})\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)}
where
q_{\alpha}
is the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

By Lemma
4.21
, we have
w(q_{\alpha})=v_{p,\alpha,\delta}(q_{\alpha})=k\delta+\lambda
, where
k=\#(\Omega_{\alpha}\cap\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta))
and
\lambda\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)}=\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}=\mathbb{Z}
; in particular, we have
w(q_{\alpha})\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)}\Leftrightarrow k\delta\in\mathbb{Z}
.

If
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, then
q_{\alpha}(X)=X-\alpha
, so
v_{p,\alpha,\delta}(q_{\alpha})=\delta
which is therefore an integer.
Suppose that
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Since
(\alpha,\delta)
is a minimal pair,
\delta>0
. By Proposition
4.22
we have
k=1
, so that above displayed condition is equivalent to the condition of the main statement of the theorem.
∎

Theorem 4.24
.

Let
R
be a ring between
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
. Then
R
is a UFD if and only if either
R=\mathbb{Q}[X]
or
R=W\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
where
W
is a DVR of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
unramified over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
. More precisely, the last condition holds if and only if there exists
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})
such that
\alpha
is unramified over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and in this case
R=\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{O}_{p})
.

Proof.

A UFD is just a Krull domain with trivial divisor class group
[
13
, Proposition 6.1]
. So, if
R
is a UFD, then, by Theorem
4.15
we have
R=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
, for some
(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
. By items (4) and (5) of Theorem
4.10
, either
R=\mathbb{Q}[X]
or
R=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
and
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
is unramified over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
. Conversely, if
R
has the stated form, then by Theorem
4.10
,
R
is a Krull domain with trivial class group, hence, a Krull domain.

The proof of the last claim follows from Theorem
4.23
.
∎

Example 4.25
.

Let
R
be a UFD between

and

. We remark that
R
is equal to
\mathbb{Q}[X]
precisely when
p
is a unit of

, otherwise

for some DVR

of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
extending

, such that

4.3
Global case

In this section, we globalize the results of Section
4.2
. Namely, we consider a subset
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{P}
,
W_{p,j}
a DVR
of
\mathbb{Q}(X)
with
X\in W_{p,j}
and
W_{p,j}\cap\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
,
and
R=\bigcap\limits_{p\in\Lambda}(\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{m_{p}}(W_{p,j}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]))
.

4.3.1
When a polynomial Krull domain is Dedekind

Let
R
be a polynomial Krull domain. The next proposition shows that if all the unitary DVRs of the defining family of
R
are residually algebraic extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, for
p\in\mathbb{P}
, then
R
is a Dedekind domain. We first recall the following definition given in
[
27
,
28
]
.

Definition 4.26
.

Let
\underline{E}=\prod\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}E_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{O}=\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\mathbb{O}_{p}
be a subset.
We say that
\underline{E}
is
polynomially factorizable
if, for each
g\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{p})\in\underline{E}
, there exist
n,d\in\mathbb{Z}
,
n,d\geq 1
such that
\frac{g(\alpha)^{n}}{d}
is a unit of
\overline{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}
, that is,
v_{p}(\frac{g(\alpha_{p})^{n}}{d})=0
for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
.

If every
\alpha_{p}\in E_{p}
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
for every
p\in\mathbb{P}
,
[
27
, Lemma 2.12]
shows that a set
\underline{E}
as in the above definition is polynomially factorizable if and only if, for each
g\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
, the following set is finite:

\mathbb{P}_{g,\underline{E}}=\{p\in\mathbb{P}\mid\exists\alpha_{p}\in E_{p}\text{ such that }v_{p}(g(\alpha_{p}))>0\}.

Clearly, we have

\mathbb{P}_{g,\underline{E}}=\{p\in\mathbb{P}\mid\exists\alpha_{p}\in E_{p}\text{ such that }g\in\mathbb{Z}[X]\cap M_{(p),\alpha_{p}}\},

where
M_{(p),\alpha_{p}}=M_{(p),\alpha_{p},\infty}
is the maximal ideal of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p},\infty}
.

Proposition 4.27
.

Let
R
be a Krull domain between
\mathbb{Z}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
. Then the following statements
are equivalent.

(1)

R
is a Dedekind domain.

(2)

Each unitary DVR
W
of the defining family of
R
is a residually algebraic extension of
W\cap\mathbb{Q}
.

(3)

R=\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\underline{E},\mathbb{O})
, for some subset
\underline{E}=\prod\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}E_{p}\subset\mathbb{O}=\prod\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\mathbb{O}_{p}
such that
E_{p}\subset\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}
is a finite set of transcendental elements over
\mathbb{Q}
for each
p\in\mathbb{P}
.

In this case,
\underline{E}
is polynomially factorizable.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) This is an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.10
, (3).

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3) Let
\{W_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the proper subset of the defining family of DVRs of
R
which are extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
. By Theorem
3.1
, if
W_{i}
is a residually algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, then there exists
\alpha_{p}\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}
, transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
, such that
W_{i}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p},\infty}
. For each
p\in\mathbb{P}
, we denote by
E_{p}
the subset formed by these
\alpha_{p}\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}
. Then the defining family of DVRs of
R
is equal to:

\{\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p}}\mid p\in\mathbb{P},\alpha_{p}\in E_{p}\}\cup\{\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(g)}\mid g\in\mathbb{Q}[X],\text{ irreducible}\}

In particular, we have

R=\bigcap_{i\in I}W_{i}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\bigcap_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\bigcap_{\alpha_{p}\in E_{p}}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\bigcap_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E_{p},\mathbb{O}_{p})

where the last equality follows from Remark
3.12
. Note that we also have

\bigcap_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E_{p},\mathbb{O}_{p})=\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\underline{E},\mathbb{O})=\{f\in\mathbb{Q}[X]\mid f(\alpha)\in\mathbb{O},\forall\alpha\in\underline{E}\}

where
\underline{E}=\prod\limits_{p}E_{p}
,
\mathbb{O}=\prod\limits_{p}\mathbb{O}_{p}
and
f(\alpha)=(f(\alpha_{p}))_{p}
if
\alpha=(\alpha_{p})_{p}\in\underline{E}
.
Finally, let
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Then, since
p\in R
,
p
is contained in finitely many centers of the defining family of DVRs of
R
. This implies that
E_{p}
is finite.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1) Let
M
be a maximal ideal of
R
.
If
M\cap\mathbb{Z}=(0)
, then ht
M=1
, so assume that
M\cap\mathbb{Z}=p\mathbb{Z}

for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Note that

R=\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\underline{E},\mathbb{O})=\bigcap\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\bigcap\limits_{\alpha_{p}\in E_{p}}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
, so if
S=\mathbb{Z}\setminus p\mathbb{Z}
, then

R_{S}=\bigcap\limits_{\alpha_{p}\in E_{p}}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]

by Lemma
4.3
and
\bigcap\limits_{\alpha_{p}\in E_{p}}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
is a Dedekind domain by Theorem
4.15
. Hence, ht
M=
ht
(MR_{S})=1
.
Thus,
R
is a one-dimensional Krull domain, therefore
R
is a Dedekind domain.

Now for the proof of “In this case”, let
f\in\mathbb{Z}[X]\subset R
. Since
R
is a Krull domain,
f
is contained in only finitely many centers of the valuation domains of the defining family for
R
(clearly,
f
is contained in finitely many centers of the non-unitary valuation domains
\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(g)}
, which are the non-unitary prime ideals of
R
). This precisely means that the set
\mathbb{P}_{g,\underline{E}}=\{p\in\mathbb{P}\mid\exists\alpha_{p}\in E_{p},v_{p}(f(\alpha_{p}))>0\}
is finite. Hence,
\underline{E}
is polynomially factorizable by
[
27
, Lemma 2.12]
.
∎

In particular, a polynomial Krull domain which is not Dedekind must have an essential valuation
overring which is a residually transcendental extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
.

Proposition 4.28
.

Let
R
be a polynomial Krull domain. Then

R=R_{1}\cap R_{2}
, where
R_{1}
is a Dedekind domain such that
\mathbb{Z}[X]\subset R_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}[X]
, and
R_{2}
is either
a non-Prüfer Krull domain such that
\mathbb{Z}[X]\subseteq R_{2}\subset\mathbb{Q}[X]

and
Q
is not maximal for all
Q\in X^{1}(R_{2})
with
Q\cap\mathbb{Z}\neq(0)

or
R_{2}=\mathbb{Q}[X]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{W}(R)=\{W_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the subset of the defining family of DVRs for
R
which are unitary valuation domains.
Note that
\mathcal{W}(R)=\emptyset
is equivalent to saying that
R=\mathbb{Q}[X]
; in this case we have
R_{1}=R_{2}=\mathbb{Q}[X]
.
Suppose now that
\mathcal{W}(R)\not=\emptyset
.
We partition
\mathcal{W}(R)
into the subset
\mathcal{W}_{\text{ra}}(R)
of residually algebraic extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}

and the subset
\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}(R)
of residually transcendental extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
.

We set:

\displaystyle R_{1}=\bigcap_{W_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{ra}}(R)}W_{i}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]\ \ \text{ and }\ \ R_{2}=\bigcap_{W_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}(R)}W_{i}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X].

Then
R=R_{1}\cap R_{2}
. Clearly,
R_{1}=\mathbb{Q}[X]
if and only if
\mathcal{W}_{\text{ra}}(R)=\emptyset
and similarly
R_{2}=\mathbb{Q}[X]
if and only if
\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}(R)=\emptyset
.
Note that since
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are both subintersections of the Krull domain
R
, they both are Krull domains.
Moreover, by Proposition
4.27
,

R_{1}
is Dedekind, while if
\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}(R)\not=\emptyset
, then
R_{2}
is not Prüfer,
since every
W_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}(R)
is residually transcendental over
W_{i}\cap\mathbb{Q}
(see
[
26
, Theorem 3.14]
).

Now, suppose that
R_{2}\subset\mathbb{Q}[X]
. Let
Q\in X^{1}(R_{2})
be such that
Q\cap\mathbb{Z}\neq(0)
,
so
Q\cap\mathbb{Q}=p\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
.
Then
{(R_{2})}_{Q}=W_{k}
for some
W_{k}\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}(R)
by
[
10
, Corollary 2.3(i)]
.
It is clear that if
S
is the multiplicative subset of
\mathbb{Z}
generated by
\mathbb{P}-\{p\}
, then

{(R_{2})}_{S}
is a Krull domain between
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
.
Note that
(R_{2})_{S}=\bigcap\limits_{W_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{rt}}(R)}(W_{i})_{S}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]

and
{(W_{k})}_{S}=W_{k}
, so
Q_{S}
is not a maximal ideal of
{(R_{2})}_{S}
by Theorem
4.10
(3).
Thus
Q
is not a maximal ideal of
R_{2}
.
∎

In Proposition
4.28
,

R_{1}=\mathbb{Q}[X]
if
R
has no valuation overrings which are residually algebraic extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
,
and
R_{2}=\mathbb{Q}[X]
if
R
is a Dedekind domain (or, equivalently,
R
has no valuation overrings
which are residually transcendental extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
).
Therefore, the objective is now to characterize when, for a given family
\{W_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of residually transcendental extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
,
p\in\mathbb{P}
, the ring
\bigcap\limits_{i\in I}W_{i}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
has finite character, and thus is a Krull domain.

4.3.2
Centers of monomial valuations on
\mathbb{Z}[X]

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
be fixed. Given
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
, we denote the maximal ideal of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
by
M_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
. By Theorem
3.6
,
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
is an overring of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
if and only if
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{O}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})
. In this section, we restrict to this case and describe the center of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
, namely,
M_{(p),\alpha,\delta}\cap(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X])
.

Let
\mathbb{M}_{p}
be the maximal ideal of
\mathbb{O}_{p}
. We set
\textnormal{Int}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{M}_{p})=\{f\in\mathbb{C}_{p}[X]\mid f(\bar{B}(\alpha,\delta))\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{p}\}
. It is easy to see that
\textnormal{Int}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{M}_{p})
is an ideal of
\textnormal{Int}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{O}_{p})
. The following lemma shows that this ideal is precisely the center of
\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha,\delta}
on
\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{C}_{p}[X]=\textnormal{Int}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{O}_{p})
(see (
3.13
)).

Lemma 4.29
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
,
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\})
and
\mathbb{M}_{p,\alpha,\delta}
be
the maximal ideal of
\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha,\delta}
. Then, we have

\mathbb{M}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap(\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{C}_{p}[X])=\textnormal{Int}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{M}_{p}).

Proof.

The statement clearly holds if
\delta=\infty
. Suppose then that
\delta<\infty
.

(\subseteq)
Let
f\in\mathbb{M}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap(\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{C}_{p}[X])
. Then, if
f(X)=\sum\limits_{k}a_{k}(X-\alpha)^{k}
, we have
v_{p}(a_{k})+k\delta>0
for each
k
. In particular, if
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
is such that
v_{p}(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})\geq\delta
, we have
f(\alpha^{\prime})=\sum\limits_{k}a_{k}(\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha)^{k}
, so that
v_{p}(f(\alpha^{\prime}))\geq\min_{k}\{v_{p}(a_{k})+k\delta\}>0
. Hence,
f(\bar{B}(\alpha,\delta))\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{p}
.

(\supseteq)
Let
f\in\textnormal{Int}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{M}_{p})\subset\textnormal{Int}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{O}_{p})
. Let
E=\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
be a pseudo-stationary sequence such that
\delta_{E}=\delta
and let
v_{p,E}
be the associated valuation (see
[
29
]
). By
[
29
, Proposition 3.7]
,
v_{p,\alpha,\delta}=v_{p,E}
and by
[
29
, Theorem 3.4]
,
f(X)
is in the maximal ideal of
\mathbb{O}_{p,E}=\mathbb{O}_{p,\alpha,\delta}
, because by assumption
v_{p}(f(\alpha_{n}))>0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
; thus,
f\in\mathbb{M}_{p,\alpha,\delta}
.
∎

For each
p\in\mathbb{P}
, let
\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the finite field of
p
elements, i.e.,

\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, and
\overline{f}
be the image of
f\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
.
Let Spec
(\mathbb{Z}[X])
(resp., Max
(\mathbb{Z}[X])
) be the set of prime ideals (resp., maximal ideals) of
\mathbb{Z}[X]
.
Note that dim
(\mathbb{Z}[X])=2
and each maximal ideal of
\mathbb{Z}[X]
has height two,
so

Spec
(\mathbb{Z}[X])=\{0\}\cup X^{1}(\mathbb{Z}[X])\cup
Max
(\mathbb{Z}[X])
and
X^{1}(\mathbb{Z}[X])\cap
Max
(\mathbb{Z}[X])=\emptyset
.

It is well known and easy to see that

(a)

X^{1}(\mathbb{Z}[X])=\{p\mathbb{Z}[X]\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}\cup\{f\mathbb{Z}[X]\mid f\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
and
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[X]\}

(b)

Max
(\mathbb{Z}[X])=\{(p,f)\mid p\in\mathbb{P},f\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
and
\overline{f}\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
is a prime ideal
\}
.

In particular, for each
p\in\mathbb{P}
,

\displaystyle\text{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{0\}\cup\{p\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\{f\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\mid f\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\text{ and }f\text{ is irrducible in }\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\{(p,f)\mid\overline{f}\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]\text{ is a prime ideal }\}.

Recall that if
\alpha\in\mathbb{O}_{p}
, then
\overline{\alpha}
is the image of
\alpha

modulo
\mathbb{M}_{p}
and
g_{\alpha}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
denotes a monic polynomial
such that
\overline{g_{\alpha}(X)}
is the minimal polynomial over
\overline{\alpha}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Proposition 4.30
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
and
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{O}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})
. Then

\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\cap M_{(p),\alpha,\delta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}(p),&\text{ if }\delta=0\\
(p,g_{\alpha}(X)),&\text{ if }\delta>0\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\delta=0
. Then
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,0}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),0,0}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]_{p\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]}
(i.e., the trivial Gaussian extension).
In particular,
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),0,0}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]
, so, the center of
M_{(p),\alpha,0}=M_{(p),0,0}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
is equal to
p\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
.

Suppose now that
\delta=\infty
. Since
(p,g_{\alpha}(X))
is a maximal ideal of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
and
g_{\alpha}\in M_{(p),\alpha,\infty}
,
(p,g_{\alpha}(X))
is the center of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\infty}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
.

Finally, suppose that
0<\delta<\infty
. By Lemma
4.29
,

M_{(p),\alpha,\delta}\cap(\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X])=\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta),\mathbb{M}_{p}),

so
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\cap M_{(p),\alpha,\delta}=\{f\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\mid f(\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta))\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{p}\}
.
Hence, in order to show that this last intersection is equal to
(p,g_{\alpha}(X))
, it is sufficient to show that
g_{\alpha}
maps
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
into
\mathbb{M}_{p}
. If
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
is such that
v(\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha)\geq\delta>0
, then
g_{\alpha}(\alpha^{\prime})\equiv g_{\alpha}(\alpha)\equiv 0\pmod{\mathbb{M}_{p}}
, which is what we wanted to prove.
∎

Remark 4.31
.

Clearly, if
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)\subset\mathbb{O}_{p}
with
\delta>0
, then for all
\alpha^{\prime}\in\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
,
we have
\overline{\alpha}=\overline{\alpha^{\prime}}
, so
\overline{g_{\alpha}}=\overline{g_{\alpha^{\prime}}}
.
Hence, for
\delta>0
, the center of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
does not depend on the particular center of the ball
\bar{B}(\alpha,\delta)
.
Most importantly, by Lemma
4.30
, given
\alpha\in\mathbb{O}_{p}
,
the center of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha,\delta}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
is the same maximal ideal for all
\delta\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}
.

Putting these two remarks together, for each
\delta\in\mathbb{Q},\delta>0
, we have that

M_{(p),\alpha,\delta}\cap\mathbb{Z}[X]=M_{(p),\alpha^{\prime},\infty}\cap\mathbb{Z}[X],\;\;\forall\alpha^{\prime}\in\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta).

In particular, for
\delta>0
and
g\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
, we have that
g\in M_{(p),\alpha,\delta}

if and only if
g\in M_{(p),\alpha^{\prime},\infty}
for some (hence, all)
\alpha^{\prime}\in\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)
.

Corollary 4.32
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
and

(\alpha_{i},\delta_{i})\in\mathbb{O}_{p}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})
, for
i=1,2
.

Then
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\cap M_{(p),\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\cap M_{(p),\alpha_{2},\delta_{2}}
if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(a)

\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=0
;

(b)

\delta_{i}\not=0
, for
i=1,2
, and
\overline{\alpha_{1}},\overline{\alpha_{2}}
are conjugate over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Equivalently,
v_{p}(\sigma(\alpha_{1})-\alpha_{2})>0
for some
\sigma\in G_{p}=\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
Suppose first that
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}},\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}
have the same center on
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
.
By Proposition
4.30
, we have either
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=0
or
\delta_{i}\not=0
for
i=1,2
. In the last case, we have
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\cap M_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}=(p,g_{\alpha_{i}}(X))
, where
g_{\alpha_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
is a monic polynomial such that
\overline{g_{\alpha_{i}}}
is the minimal polynomial of
\overline{\alpha_{i}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, for
i=1,2
. Then, these prime ideals are the same if and only if
\overline{g_{\alpha_{1}}}=\overline{g_{\alpha_{2}}}
, that is,
\overline{\alpha_{1}},\overline{\alpha_{2}}
are conjugate over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Note that this last condition amounts
to saying that there exists some
\overline{\sigma}\in\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
such that
\overline{\sigma}(\overline{\alpha_{1}})=\overline{\alpha_{2}}
. Since
\overline{\sigma}(\overline{\alpha_{1}})=\overline{\sigma(\alpha_{1})}
for some
\sigma\in G_{p}

[
7
, Chapt. V, §2.2, Proposition 6(ii)]
), it follows that
v_{p}(\sigma(\alpha_{1})-\alpha_{2})>0
, as claimed.

(\Leftarrow)
If
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=0
, then
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\cap M_{(p),\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}=(p)
for
i=1,2
.
If instead
\delta_{i}\not=0
for
i=1,2
and
\overline{\alpha_{1}},\overline{\alpha_{2}}
are conjugate over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
then by the above arguments we have
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\cap M_{(p),\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\cap M_{(p),\alpha_{2},\delta_{2}}
.
∎

In Corollary
4.32
, if
\delta_{i}\not=0
for
i=1,2
, we have
the following three possibilities: (i)
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are conjugate by the action of
G_{p}
and
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}

and so
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{2},\delta_{2}}
(and so in particular they have the same center on
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
),
(ii)
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are not conjugate over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
or (iii)

\delta_{1}\not=\delta_{2}
so that
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{1},\delta_{1}}\not=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{2},\delta_{2}}
but

\overline{\alpha_{1}},\overline{\alpha_{2}}
are conjugate over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
so the corresponding valuation domains have the same center on
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
.
For example, if
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{O}_{p}
are such that
v_{p}(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})>0
(that is, they belong to the same ball contained in
\mathbb{O}_{p}
of strictly positive radius), then
\overline{\alpha_{1}}=\overline{\alpha_{2}}
.

4.3.3
Finite character

A Polynomial Krull domain
R
must be of the form
R=\bigcap\limits_{p\in\Lambda}(\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{m_{p}}(W_{p,j}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X])),
for some
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{P}
and DVRs
W_{p,j}
, for each
p\in\Lambda
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,m_{p}\}
. But, in general, a ring of this form need not be a polynomial Krull domain (see, for example,
[
10
, Theorem 3.5]

or Example
4.33
).

Example 4.33
.

For each prime
p\in\mathbb{P}
, let
\delta_{p}\in\mathbb{Q}
,
\delta_{p}>0
, and let

R=\bigcap_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),0,\delta_{p}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X].

Then
R
is not Krull, because
X
is contained in each center of
the residually transcendental extensions
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),0,\delta_{p}}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, for
p\in\mathbb{P}
.

In this section, we determine when such an intersection is a Krull domain.

We consider the case of a subset
E=\prod\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}}E_{p}
of
\mathbb{O}
such that, for each
p\in\mathbb{P}
,
E_{p}
is a finite union of balls in
\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}
:

E_{p}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m_{p}}\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j}),\;\;(\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j})\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})\;\text{ for }j=1,\ldots,m_{p},

with the assumption that
\alpha_{p,j}
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
for each
p\in\mathbb{P}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,m_{p}\}
. If
\delta_{p,j}\not=\infty
this can always be assumed by a density argument and if
\delta_{p,j}=\infty
this condition has to be imposed. For each
p\in\mathbb{P}
, we consider the set of centers of the balls in
E_{p}
of strictly positive radius given by
C(E_{p})=\{\alpha_{p,1},\ldots,\alpha_{p,m_{p}}\mid\delta_{p,j}>0,\forall j=1,\ldots,m_{p}\}
and set

C(\underline{E})=\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}}C(E_{p})

Note that, for each
p\in\mathbb{P}
, the set of the centers of
\{\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j}}\mid j=1,\ldots,m_{p}\}
on
\mathbb{Z}[X]
, namely

\{\mathbb{Z}[X]\cap M_{(p),\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j}}\mid j=1,\ldots,m_{p}\}

is finite (possibly, by Corollary
4.32
its cardinality can be strictly smaller than
m_{p}
).

We remark that the choice of a center of an ultrametric ball
\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha,\delta)\subset\mathbb{O}_{p}
is completely arbitrary (every element of the ball is a center of the ball itself). However, for our scopes, by Remark
4.31

a possible different choice does not affect the next Theorem
4.34
,
provided
the center of each ball is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}

(because, in this case,
[
27
, Lemma 2.12]
can be applied for the proof of Theorem
4.34
). This property can always be assumed if
\delta_{p,j}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
and it has to be imposed when
\delta_{p,j}=\infty
.

Theorem 4.34
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{P}
be a subset,
E=\prod\limits_{p\in\Lambda}E_{p}\subset\mathbb{O}
where for each
p\in\Lambda
,
E_{p}=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{m_{p}}\bar{B}_{p}(\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j})
with
(\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j})\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})
,
\alpha_{p,j}
transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
, for each

j\in\{1,\ldots,m_{p}\}
and let
R=\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\underline{E},\mathbb{O})
, where
\underline{E}=\prod\limits_{p\in\Lambda}E_{p}
, namely:

R=\bigcap_{p\in\Lambda}\bigcap_{j=1}^{m_{p}}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X].

Assuming that the above representation of
R
is irredundant,
then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R
is a Krull domain.

(2)

For each (nonconstant and irreducible)
g\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
,
g
is contained in finitely many heigth one unitary prime ideals of
R
.

(3)

For each
g\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
, the following set is finite:

\mathbb{D}_{g,\underline{E}}=\{p\in\Lambda\mid\exists j\in\{1,\ldots,m_{p}\}\text{ such that }g\in\mathbb{Z}[X]\cap\ M_{(p),\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j}}\}.

(4)

C(\underline{E})=\prod\limits_{p\in\Lambda}\{\alpha_{p,1},\ldots,\alpha_{p,m_{p}}\mid\forall j=1,\ldots,m_{p},\delta_{p,j}>0\}
is polynomially factorizable.

We remark that if for some
p\in\Lambda
we have
\delta_{p,\alpha_{j}}=0
for
j=1,\ldots,m_{p}
, then
m_{p}=1
by the assumption that the above representation of
R
is irredundant. For such a prime
p
, we have

R_{(p)}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p},0}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{O}_{p})=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]

(see for example (
3.14
)).

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) Note that
R
is a Krull domain if and only if, for every
f\in R
,
f\not=0
,

f
is contained in finitely many centers of the defining family of DVR overrings of
R
,
namely,
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j}}
, for
p\in\Lambda
and
j=1,\ldots,m_{p}

and
\mathbb{Q}[X]_{(q)}
, for
q\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
irreducible.

Since every
f\in R
can be written as
\frac{g}{d}
, for some
g\in\mathbb{Z}[X]

and
d\in\mathbb{Z}
,
d\not=0
, it is clear that
f
is contained in finitely many centers of the defining family of DVR overrings of
R
if and only if the same holds for
g
,
since the integer
d
is divisible by finitely many
p\in\Lambda
and for each
p\in\Lambda
there are only finitely many DVRs overring of
R
extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.
Moreover,
g
is clearly contained in finitely many nonunitary prime ideals of
R
(whose height is one by an argument similar
to the one of Corollary
4.8
), so
R
is Krull if and only if condition (2) holds (by assumption and by Corollary
4.6
, the centers on
R
of the DVRs
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j}}
are of height one).

(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3)
Since for each
p\in\Lambda
, we only have finitely many unitary DVR overrings of
R
extending
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}

(namely,
\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j}}
, for
j=1,\ldots,m_{p}
) and their centers on
R
are of height one by Corollary
4.6
, it follows that (2) is equivalent to (3).

(3)
\Leftrightarrow
(4)
Let
\mathbb{P}_{0}
be the set of primes in
\Lambda
for which there exists
j\in\{1,\ldots,m_{p}\}
such that
\delta_{p,j}=0
. If
p\in\mathbb{P}_{0}
, then
m_{p}=1

by Remark
3.18
and the assumption that the above representation of
R
is irredundant, and
M_{(p),\alpha_{p,1},\delta_{p,1}}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]=(p)
by Proposition
4.30
. Hence, for each nonzero
g\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
,
\mathbb{D}_{g,\underline{E}}\cap\mathbb{P}_{0}
is a finite set. By the same aforementioned Proposition, if

, we have
M_{(p),\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j}}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]=M_{(p),\alpha_{p,j},\infty}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]
, so
\mathbb{D}_{g,\underline{E}}\setminus\mathbb{P}_{0}=\mathbb{P}_{g,C(\underline{E})}
. Therefore,
\mathbb{D}_{g,\underline{E}}
is finite if and only if
\mathbb{P}_{g,C(\underline{E})}
is finite. Since each
\alpha_{p,j}
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
by assumption,
[

, Lemma 2.12]
shows that

is polynomially factorizable if and only if for every
g\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
,
\mathbb{P}_{g,C(\underline{E})}
is finite. Hence, (3) and (4) are equivalent. ∎

Let
\Lambda
and

be as in Theorem
4.34
. It is helpful to note that

if and only if

, and, if

is always a Krull domain.
The next theorem is the global version of Theorem

and describes the class of polynomial Krull Domains.
By a result of Heinzer, this is precisely the class of integrally closed Noetherian domains between
\mathbb{Z}[X]
and

Theorem 4.35
.

Let

be a polynomial Krull domain. Then

R=\bigcap_{p\in\Lambda}\bigcap_{j=1}^{m_{p}}\mathbb{Z}_{(p),\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j}}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X]=\textnormal{Int}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\underline{E},\mathbb{O})

(4.36)

for some subset
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{P}
, where
(\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j})\in\mathbb{O}_{p}^{\text{br}}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\})

\alpha_{p,j}
transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
if
\delta_{p,j}=\infty

and

;

\underline{E}=\prod\limits_{p\in\Lambda}E_{p}

Moreover, if ht
(M_{(p),\alpha_{p,j},\delta_{p,j}}\cap R)=1

As we remarked in (

for some subset

\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
for each

.

By Theorem

, for each

, for each

Finally,

is polynomially factorizable by Theorem

and Cl

by the proof of
[

, Theorem 2.4(4)]
.
∎

(2) Let the notation be as in Theorems

4.3.4
Construction of Krull domains with prescribed class group

For convenience, we say that a polynomial Krull domain
R
is a
pure
Krull domain if
Q
is not maximal for all
Q\in X^{1}(R)
with
Q\cap\mathbb{Z}\neq(0)
. By Proposition
4.28
, if
R
is a polynomial Krull domain that is not Dedekind, then
R
can be represented as an intersection of a Dedekind domain
R_{1}
and a pure Krull domain
R_{2}
, i.e.,
R=R_{1}\cap R_{2}
.
The divisor class group of a polynomial Krull domain is a direct sum of a countable family of finitely generated abelian groups by Theorem
4.35
. Conversely, if
G
is a direct sum of a countable family of finitely generated abelian groups, then there is a Dedekind domain between
\mathbb{Z}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
with ideal class group
G

[
27
, Theorem 3.1]
.

We next construct a pure Krull domain with prescribed divisor class group.

Proposition 4.41
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated abelian group. Then there exists a pure Krull domain
R
between
\mathbb{Z}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
such that Cl
(R)=G
.

Proof.

Suppose
G=\mathbb{Z}/e_{1}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}/e_{n}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{m}

for some integers
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\geq 0
.
Let
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\in\mathbb{P}
be distinct.

We pick
m+1
distinct elements
\alpha_{p_{n},1},\ldots,\alpha_{p_{n},m+1}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{n}}
which are transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
N=\max\{v_{p_{n}}(\alpha_{p_{n},i}-\alpha_{p_{n},j})\mid i\not=j\in\{1,\ldots,m+1\}\}
.
Let
W_{p_{n},j}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p_{n}),\alpha_{p_{n},j},N+1/e_{n}}
, for
j\in\{1,\ldots,m+1\}
. Note that the
W_{p_{n},j}
’s are distinct (because
v_{p_{n}}(\alpha_{p_{n},i}-\alpha_{p_{n},j})<N+1/e_{n}
for each

i\not=j
) and
(\alpha_{p_{n},j},N+1/e_{n})
is a minimal pair for each

j\in\{1,\ldots,m+1\}
. Then by Theorem
4.10
, (4) and Proposition
4.22
we have

e_{p_{n},j}=e(W_{p_{n},j}\mid\mathbb{Z}_{(p_{n})})=e(N+1/e_{n}\mid\mathbb{Q}_{p})=e_{n}.

By the same argument, we can also choose
\alpha_{p_{i}}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}}

which is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
, so that
(\alpha_{p_{i}},1/e_{i})
is minimal pair for
i=1,\dots,n-1
. Let
W_{p_{i}}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p_{i}),\alpha_{p_{i}},1/e_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
so that, if we set
R=(\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}W_{p_{i}})\cap(\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{m+1}W_{p_{n},j}\cap\mathbb{Q}[X])
,
we have that
R
is a Krull domain such that Cl
(R)=G

by Theorem
4.35

and
Q
is not maximal for all
Q\in X^{1}(R)
with
Q\cap\mathbb{Z}\neq(0)

by Theorem
4.10
(3).
∎

Theorem 4.42
.

Let
G
be a direct sum of a countable family
\{G_{i}\mid i\in I\}
of finitely generated abelian groups (which are not necessarily distinct).
Then there exists a pure Krull domain
R
between
\mathbb{Z}[X]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X]
with Cl
(R)=G
. Moreover, for each
i\in I
, there is a multiplicative subset
S_{i}
of
\mathbb{Z}
such that
R_{S_{i}}
is a pure Krull domain with Cl
(R_{S_{i}})=G_{i}
.

Proof.

For each
i\in I
, let
G_{i}=\mathbb{Z}/e_{i1}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}/e_{in_{i}}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{m_{i}}
for some
e_{i1},\dots,e_{in_{i}}\geq 1
,
n_{i}\geq 1
and
m_{i}\geq 0
. Without loss of generality, we may suppose that we have a partition of
\mathbb{P}
into a family of finite subsets which is indexed by
I
, namely,
\{\mathbb{P}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a partition of
\mathbb{P}
with
|\mathbb{P}_{i}|=n_{i}
for each
i\in I
. By Proposition
4.41
, there exists a pure Krull domain
R_{i}
,
\mathbb{Z}[X]\subseteq R_{i}\subset\mathbb{Q}[X]
, such that Cl
(R_{i})=G_{i}
.

Now set
R=\bigcap\limits_{i\in I}R_{i}
. Note that if
S_{i}=\mathbb{Z}\setminus\bigcup\limits_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{i}}p\mathbb{Z}
for each

, then
R_{S_{i}}=R_{i}
by Lemma
4.3
, thus

R_{S_{i}}
is a pure Krull domain with Cl
(R_{S_{i}})=G_{i}
.
However, it may be the case that
R
is not a Krull domain, that is, it has not the finite character property.

Let
i\in I
be such that
G_{i}
is not trivial, and let
p
be a fixed prime in the finite set
\mathbb{P}_{i}
. Suppose that for the construction of
R_{i}
we chose in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

k_{p}\geq 1
distinct elements, say for example
E_{p}=\{\alpha_{p,1},\ldots,\alpha_{p,k_{p}}\}
; let also
\delta_{p,j}
be the radius of the ultrametric ball of center
\alpha_{p,j}
, for
j=1,\ldots,k_{p}
. We then replace the elements in
E_{p}
by possibly other
k_{p}
distinct
\alpha_{p}
’s in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
which lie in the same residue class

, such that these
\alpha_{p}
’s are transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}
. We also modify the corresponding
\delta_{p,j}
’s accordingly so that the previous facts about the corresponding ring

still hold. If

is trivial, then we simply choose an element

. We claim that the set

is polynomially factorizable, so by Theorems
4.34
and
4.10
(3),

is a pure Krull domain. Let

and let

Since for

∎

4.3.5

(3)

Let

Hence, in general, Pic

Corollary 4.47
.

Let the notation be as in Corollary

.
If

the following statements are satisfied.

(1)

(1) By Corollary

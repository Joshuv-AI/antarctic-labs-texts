Quantum sensing of arbitrary magnetic signals with molecular spins
Matteo Lanza, Claudio Bonizzoni, Olga Mironova, Fabio Santanni, Alessio Nicolini, Alberto Ghirri, Andrea Cornia, Marco Affronte

Abstract
Molecular spins offer a promising platform for quantum sensing, particularly in organic, supramolecular or biological environments. Detection of periodic magnetic fields has been demonstrated with molecular spins ( S S = 1/2) using manipulation protocols synchronized with the probed field. In this work, we develop two quantum sensing protocols that enable discrimination between different time-dependent magnetic field signals, without synchronization. These are based on the Hahn echo sequence and have been tested on VO(TPP) and VOPt(SCOPh) 4 molecular spins embedded in a superconducting YBCO microwave resonator. We report a magnetic field sensitivity up to a few 10 − 7 10^{-7} T Hz − 1 2 -\frac{1}{2} (with lower bounds approaching 10 − 8 10^{-8} T Hz − 1 2 -\frac{1}{2} ) for signals with duration of a few microseconds. Under the given conditions, the minimum signal area that can be measured is in the 10 − 10 10^{-10} T s range, suggesting a potential trade-off between minimum measurable field and the required signal duration and memory time.



I
Introduction

Quantum sensing exploits the quantum properties of a system for detecting a physical quantity
Degen et al. 2017
. Spin systems with sufficiently long coherence times have the potential to be used for this purpose
Bar-Gill et al. 2013
;
Herbschleb et al. 2019
.
Ensembles of Nitrogen-Vacancy (NV) centers in diamond, have shown remarkable performances as quantum sensors
Zhou et al. 2020
;
Schirhagl et al. 2014
. A large collection of spins acts as an average of the single spin responses
Zhou et al. 2020
and gives a larger fluorescence signal measured through Optically-Detected-Magnetic-Resonance (ODMR)
Taylor et al. 2008
;
Zhou et al. 2020
based on microwave (MW) Dynamical Decoupling schemes
Pham et al. 2012
;
Rondin et al. 2014
;
Taylor et al. 2008
;
Fang et al. 2013
;
Zhou et al. 2020
;
Farfurnik et al. 2018
.
This allows to reach typical sensitivities in the order of few
10^{-9}
T Hz
-\frac{1}{2}
.

Molecular spins, in addition to displaying microsecond coherence times
Atzori et al. 2016
, greatly benefit from chemical design , a powerful tool to control their composition and local environment, tune their energy levels, incorporate them in supramolecular structures or crystalline lattices, and deposit them on surfaces
Timco et al. 2009
;
Santanni and Privitera 2024
;
Yamabayashi et al. 2018
. Thus, they are promising candidates as spin quantum sensors
Yu et al. 2021
;
Latawiec et al. 2025
;
Dzuba 2007
. In this perspective, the development of advanced sensing protocols can further expand their potential in this field
Troiani et al. 2019
;
Sun et al. 2022a
;
Singh et al. 2025
;
Singh et al. 2024
;
Privitera et al. 2025
;
Grzegorzek et al. 2020
;
Bonizzoni et al. 2024
.
We have shown the possibility of using molecular spins as quantum memories
Bonizzoni et al. 2020
and, more recently, for quantum sensing of AC magnetic fields
Bonizzoni et al. 2024
. A more complex task is the detection of a non-periodic time-dependent magnetic field signal. So far, this has been demonstrated in state-of-the-art experiments on NV centers in diamond through ODMR protocols
Cooper et al. 2014
. Yet, the sequences used in these works employ optical initialization and detection
Maze et al. 2008
, several MW pulses
Magesan et al. 2013
, multiple triggering of the external signal
Zopes and Degen 2019
or a combination of these elements.
In this work, we propose two spin echo sequences that consist of only two MW pulses
Hahn 1950
and do not require optical readout.
Furthermore, the external magnetic signal has to be triggered only once per sequence repetition. In both sequences the sensing principle relies on the difference in the phase accumulation obtained during the two free precession times of the sequence.

The Hahn echo sequence consists of two MW pulses: an initial

\phi\textsubscript{echo}(T\textsubscript{seq},s)=\int_{0}^{T\textsubscript{seq}}\gamma B\textsubscript{1}(t,s)dt,

(1)

where

\frac{\pi}{2}

\pi

Eq.

\frac{\pi}{2}

\pi

\pi

\pi

1

\pi

The equation for the phase accumulation can then be written as:

\begin{split}&\phi\textsubscript{echo}(T\textsubscript{seq},s)\\
&=\int_{t\textsubscript{$\frac{\pi}{2}$,end}}^{t\textsubscript{$\pi,start$}}\gamma B\textsubscript{1}(t,s)dt\\
&-\int_{t\textsubscript{$\pi$,start}}^{t\textsubscript{$\pi$,end}}\gamma B\textsubscript{1}(t,s)\sin\left(\frac{t-t\textsubscript{$\pi$,start}-\frac{T\textsubscript{$\pi$}}{2}}{T\textsubscript{$\pi$}/\pi}\right)dt\\
&-\int_{t\textsubscript{$\pi$,end}}^{t\textsubscript{echo}}\gamma B\textsubscript{1}(t,s)dt,\end{split}

(2)

where
t\textsubscript{$\frac{\pi}{2}$,end}
is the time at which the
\frac{\pi}{2}
pulse ends,
t\textsubscript{$\pi$,start}
and
t\textsubscript{$\pi$,end}

\pi

\pi

\pi

is the time at which the echo is observed. Hereafter, we will fix

\frac{\pi}{2}
,end

V
Conclusions and outlooks

We developed two quantum sensing protocols for the detection of magnetic fields with arbitrary time dependence. Through these protocols we also demonstrated the possibility of using molecular spins as detectors for such signals. This further expands the sensing capabilities of molecular spins beyond quantum sensing of AC magnetic fields, since no synchronization with the free precession time is needed. Furthermore, the two sequences shown in this work result in sensitivities on the order of few
S\approx 10^{-7}
T Hz
-\frac{1}{2}
with lower bounds approaching few
S\approx 10^{-8}
T Hz
-\frac{1}{2}
.
These values are in the same range as the ones obtained with similar sequences performed on NV centers
Zopes and Degen 2019
;
Balasubramanian et al. 2009
and as those we previously obtained in AC synchronized quantum sensing experiments
Bonizzoni et al. 2024
.

In both sequences the only requirement is the possibility to trigger the external signal once for each sequence repetition. Sequence 1 is further constrained by the phase memory time, which acts as an upper bound for the duration of the signal to be measured.

A common feature of the two sequences is the minimum area of the signal which can be measured. The values are
A
min

\approx
1.6
\cdot 10^{-10}
T s for VO(TPP) and
A
min

\approx
1.1
\cdot 10^{-10}
T s for VOPt(SCOPh)
4
. These values allow for a good trade-off between the required signal duration and the available sensitivity.

Sequence 2, in principle, would allow for the reconstruction of the RF signal by fitting the data with Eq.
2
(see Supplementary Information
sup
) or by performing finite differences on the phase accumulation curves as in similar methods
Zopes and Degen 2019
. However, under our experimental conditions this possibility is currently limited by phase noise. Yet, margins for improvement can be foreseen by optimizing the setup. Another possibility of improvement relies on the integration of machine learning protocols into the recognition of the signal shape from a given phase accumulation
Bonizzoni et al. 2022
.

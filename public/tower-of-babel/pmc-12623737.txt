Integrating Reinforcement Learning with Dynamic Knowledge Tracing for personalized learning path optimization
Authors: Zhiming Fu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPersonalized learning systems aim to improve student engagement and outcomes by adapting to individual learning needs. Traditional models, however, struggle to handle the dynamic nature of student learning and task sequencing. Knowledge Tracing (KT) is foundational for predicting student performance, but existing approaches lack the flexibility to account for evolving student knowledge. We propose RL-DKT, a novel framework that integrates Dynamic Knowledge Tracing (DKT) with Reinforcement Learning (RL). While DKT tracks the temporal evolution of a student’s knowledge state, RL dynamically selects tasks based on individual performance, optimizing the learning path. The RL agent adapts task difficulty in real-time to ensure maximum retention and engagement. We evaluate the RL-DKT framework using three real-world educational datasets: ASSISTments, KDD Cup 2010, and Cognitive Tutor. These datasets represent diverse learning environments and provide insights into student performance and task complexity. Our experiments involve comparing RL-DKT to traditional KT models, including Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) and DKT. The results show that RL-DKT outperforms conventional KT models across several metrics, including prediction accuracy, task completion time, student engagement, and learning path optimization. Introduction

The rapid advancements in machine learning and artificial intelligence have led to the development of personalized learning systems that aim to optimize educational experiences by adapting to the unique needs of each student. These systems have the potential to improve student engagement, knowledge retention, and learning outcomes by tailoring instructional content based on the student’s current knowledge state1,2. One of the key challenges in personalized learning is accurately tracing and predicting a student’s knowledge state over time, especially as the student interacts with various learning activities. Knowledge Tracing (KT) is a well-established method for modeling and predicting student performance based on their prior responses to educational tasks3–5. However, traditional Knowledge Tracing models have limitations in adapting to the dynamic nature of student learning, which often requires real-time adjustments to the learning path based on evolving student needs.

Dynamic Knowledge Tracing (DKT) is an advanced KT version that incorporates an understanding of temporal patterns to improve student knowledge state modeling6. Despite its advancements over conventional models, DKT needs improvement because it fails to adapt learning sequence and complexity at the same time students develop their understanding7. A new framework unites DKT with Reinforcement Learning methods to establish adaptable knowledge-tracing abilities and resource sequencing algorithms.

An agent learns to decide through Reinforcement Learning by connecting to an environment that gives them rewards and penalties feedback6,7. RL enables the modeling of student learning trajectories through an environment consisting of sequential decision points for personalized learning applications. At different stages of the educational journey, the RL agent determines suitable learning assignments that deliver both peak interest levels and best student memory retention. DKT enhanced with RL produces a system that reacts automatically to student development patterns while delivering instant feedback together with individualized learning sessions that evolve according to student progress8.

To demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, we conduct extensive experiments using several real-world educational datasets, including the ASSISTments9, KDD Cup 201010, and Cognitive Tutor dataset11. These datasets feature diverse educational content which makes them suitable for testing our RL-based DKT framework. The research indicates that the constructed framework achieves higher performance than conventional knowledge tracing systems when measuring student achievement and steering education progression. Aachieves three benefits for educational assessments it raises prediction accuracy rates while strengthening learner participation in classwork and provides learners with appropriate difficulty levels for their assignments at all times.

In this paper, we present RL-DKT, a novel framework that integrates Dynamic Knowledge Tracing (DKT) with Reinforcement Learning (RL) for personalized learning path optimization. We identify a gap in existing knowledge tracing models that fail to adapt learning sequences dynamically based on student progress. Our framework addresses this limitation by using RL to select tasks based on real-time performance predictions from DKT, ensuring that the learning path is tailored to each student’s evolving needs. Related work

In the past few decades, significant advanced progress has been made in the development of educational systems that aim to personalize learning and predict student performance12–15. Knowledge Tracing (KT) is a widely used technique for modeling student knowledge. The basic premise of KT is to predict the probability of a student’s particular skill based on their past responses. Traditional KT models, such as the Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT)16,17, have been extensively used in intelligent tutoring systems13. These models often struggle to account for the complex, dynamic nature of student learning, where the rate of knowledge acquisition may change over time, and where students’ learning patterns can vary based on individual abilities and other contextual factors.

Despite their usefulness challenges, KT models have several limitations. They generally rely on a static set of parameters for each student, which does not reflect the evolving nature of a student’s learning process13,18,19. Second, KT models often assume that students learn at a constant rate, which does not hold in real-world learning environments. In reality, the rate at which a student learns is influenced by factors such as task difficulty, student motivation, and prior knowledge. Finally, existing methods tend to treat learning as a sequence of independent tasks, ignoring the long-term dependencies between tasks that can significantly affect learning outcomes18,20,21.

To address these challenges, Dynamic Knowledge Tracing (DKT)22,23 has been proposed as an extension of traditional KT. DKT leverages deep learning techniques, particularly Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), to model temporal dependencies and the dynamic nature of student knowledge. Unlike BKT, DKT does not require a predefined model of student learning and instead learns directly from data24,25. While DKT significantly improves over static KT models, it still faces limitations. One key challenge is that DKT only models the student’s knowledge state and does not consider the adaptability of the learning path itself. In other words, while DKT can predict a student’s mastery of a skill, it does not optimize the learning path for individual students based on their evolving needs26–28.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has recently gained attention as a potential solution to the adaptive learning problem6,15,16,29,30. RL provides a framework for learning an optimal policy through sequential decision-making, where an agent interacts with an environment and learns by receiving feedback in the form of rewards or penalties31 . In the context of education, RL has been used to develop intelligent tutoring systems that can adapt the sequence of tasks based on a student’s performance and knowledge state32,33.

While RL-based systems offer an effective mechanism for task sequencing, the main challenges in integrating RL with KT methods. Many existing RL-based approaches ignore the complex dependencies between tasks, leading to suboptimal learning paths15,29,33. Most of RL models used in educational contexts are relatively simple and fail to capture the dynamic, nonlinear aspects of student learning. Therefore combination of RL with DKT offers a promising solution for improving learning path optimization by continuously adapting to students’ needs.

Knowledge Tracing particularly Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT), has been widely used in personalized learning but lacks adaptability in dynamic task sequencing. Dynamic Knowledge Tracing improves on this by using recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to model student knowledge evolution. However, DKT still struggles with optimizing learning paths. Reinforcement Learning has been introduced to address this by optimizing task sequences based on real-time performance feedback. Methodology

Dynamic Knowledge Tracing (DKT) alongside Reinforcement Learning (RL) renders the optimized framework method through which personal learning trajectories are optimized. The method contains two core components and elements. The DKT model built using RNNs tracks changes in student knowledge states. The model identifies chronological student achievement patterns since it tracks student knowledge bases using multiple task attributes such as competency levels and content dimensions. The DKT model enables dynamic estimation of student responses to maintain ongoing alterations which align with shifting student performance. The Reinforcement Learning (RL) agent chooses the subsequent learning tasks by harnessing the information from DKT about knowledge states. The RL agent optimizes task progression continuously while its learning performance and student engagement improve when focused on results from student performance. Subsequent adaptations of effective educational offerings happen in real-time through the synchronized system framework when student learning needs arise. Implementation details

We implemented the system using the PyTorch framework for training the RNN-based DKT model and the RL agent. PyTorch was chosen due to its flexibility and ease of integration with reinforcement learning algorithms. We used PyTorch’s built-in libraries for both model training and optimization. The Gradient-based optimization processes for both DKT loss and RL reward functions were handled using PyTorch’s automatic differentiation. Although TensorFlow is another popular deep learning framework, we opted for PyTorch because of its seamless compatibility with dynamic graph structures required for our RL agent’s learning process. The assessment of the system occurred through multiple educational dataset evaluations involving ASSISTments, KDD Cup 2010 and Cognitive Tutor for its performance analysis within actual learning settings.

Discussion

The results of our experiments highlight the effectiveness of the RL-DKT framework in optimizing personalized learning paths. RL-DKT achieved a 7.6% AUC improvement (0.874) over DKT, showcasing enhanced student performance prediction through real- time task difficulty adjustments. Task completion time was reduced by 12.5%, thanks to the framework’s dynamic task sequencing, which ensures tasks align with students’ evolving knowledge. Additionally, RL-DKT reduced the dropout rate by 50%, maintaining student engagement by preventing task overload or underload. Compared to recent RL-based methods like DyGKT, DASKT, and PAKES, RL-DKT consistently outperformed them in AUC, learning efficiency (with an 18% improvement over DASKT and 20% over PAKES), and dropout rate. The synergy between Dynamic Knowledge Tracing and Reinforcement Learning enables RL-DKT to adapt tasks in real-time, offering a more effective and engaging personalized learning experience, with broad implications for intelligent tutoring systems.

Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced RL-DKT, a novel framework that integrates Dynamic Knowledge Tracing (DKT) with Reinforcement Learning (RL) to optimize personalized learning paths. Our results demonstrate that RL-DKT outperforms traditional models, improving AUC, reducing task completion time, and enhancing student engagement. These findings indicate the potential of RL-DKT to significantly improve the efficiency and effectiveness of intelligent tutoring systems by providing personalized, real-time adaptations to learning paths. While our results are promising, there are some limitations to consider. First, our evaluation was conducted on a limited set of educational datasets, and further research is needed to test the generalizability of RL-DKT across different domains and subject areas. Additionally, our model currently uses a simplistic reward function, and exploring more complex reward structures may yield further improvements in performance. Future research should focus on several key areas: (1) expanding the range of datasets used to evaluate RL-DKT, particularly in non-mathematical domains; (2) refining the reward function to better capture student motivation and engagement; and (3) exploring transfer learning to adapt RL-DKT to new students with minimal data. By addressing these limitations and building on the strengths of RL-DKT, we believe that personalized learning systems can be further optimized to meet the diverse needs of learners.

Identify novel molecular subtypes of lung adenocarcinoma to predict treatment response and prognosis
Authors: Yuan-Xiang Shi, Tao Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to establish a novel quantification system of anoikis and angiogenesis related genes (AAGs) and comprehensively analyze the relationship between AAG signature score (AAGscore) and the prognosis, tumor immune microenvironment, and therapeutic response in LUAD. Univariate Cox regression analysis was used to screen prognosis-related AAGs. A consensus clustering algorithm was applied for AAG subtypes identification on 750 LUAD samples from TCGA and GEO databases. The differences in prognosis, immune infiltration, and therapeutic response were evaluated among the subtypes. The AAG signature scoring system was constructed by a principal component analysis algorithm. Cluster-A demonstrated a high gene expression and stromal-score with a poor prognosis. Our results showed that there were significant differences in survival time, mutation frequency, expression of chemokines and receptors, expression of immune checkpoint related genes, immunotherapy efficacy and antitumor drug sensitivity between high- and low-AAGscore groups. Survival analysis revealed that patients in the high-AAGscore group had better prognosis. We also discovered that the AAGcluster-B and geneCluster-2 subtype showed higher AAGscores. In addition, a number of conventional antitumor drugs were selected to test the sensitivity of high- and low-AAGscore groups to drug therapy. In summary, we constructed molecular subtypes and AAGscores of LUAD based on AAGs. The risk score model can be used to predict the prognosis of LUAD patients and the efficacy and sensitivity of antitumor drugs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-32262-w.Keywords: Anoikis, Angiogenesis, Immunotherapy, Drug sensitivity, Lung cancerSubject terms: Cancer, Biomarkers, Oncology

Introduction

Lung cancer is one of the deadliest tumors, posing a serious health threat1,2. In cancer patients, tumor metastasis is the biggest challenge and the leading cause of death. It occurs in a series of discrete steps, which have been modeled into a metastatic cascade. Among them, anoikis and angiogenesis are important links of the metastasis cascade and new targets for tumor therapy3. In tumor angiogenesis and metastasis, anoikis is a mode of programmed cell death that results from insufficient cell-matrix interactions4. In hematogenous, lymphatic, and peritoneal metastases, anoikis resistance is a prerequisite5. Du et al. demonstrated that anoikis-resistant gastric cancer cells accelerate angiogenesis and metastasis via C/EBPβ-PDGFB-PDGFRβ-MAPK pathway6. Blocking angiogenesis is a key therapeutic strategy for inhibiting tumor growth and metastasis7.

In this study, anoikis and angiogenesis related genes (AAGs) were integrated into lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), and two different anoikis-angiogenesis clusters (AAGclusters) were obtained. Prognostic analysis and immunoinfiltration analysis were performed for these two subtypes. Interestingly, the results showed that different AAGclusters had unique immune cell infiltration characteristics. Subsequently, differential expression analysis was performed for these two subtypes, and consensus clustering was performed again based on the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) associated with prognosis. In addition, we established a risk score model to quantify the characteristics of AAGs, and analyzed the relationship between AAG signature score (AAGscore) and prognosis, immune infiltration, and therapeutic response. Our findings may provide new insights into prognosis prediction and treatment of LUAD.

Materials and methods

Data source and preprocessing

The transcriptome expression data and clinical data of LUAD patients were collected from the TCGA (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/) and GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) databases. The genes related to anoikis and angiogenesis were obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/index.jsp)8. After merging and removing duplicates, 90 genes related to anoikis and angiogenesis were used for further studies.

Anoikis-angiogenesis based consensus clustering analysis

Using univariate Cox regression analysis, prognosis-related genes involved in anoikis-angiogenesis were identified. Subsequently, R packages “survival” and “survminer” were used for survival analysis of the above genes9,10. Importantly, we carried out unsupervised clustering of all samples (n = 750) using the expression values of 28 AAGs via the R package “ConsensusClusterPlus”11. Two anoikis-angiogenesis clusters (AAGclusters) were obtained, named “Cluster-A” (n = 437) and “Cluster-B” (n = 313).

Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) and estimation of immune cell infiltration

We downloaded the “h.all.v7.5.1.symbols” (HALLMARK), “c2.cp.kegg.v7.5.1.symbols” (KEGG), and “c2.cp.reactome.v7.5.1.symbols” (Reactome) files from the MSigDB database12,13. Pathway scoring was performed using the R package “GSVA” to compare biological differences between different subtypes14. The R package “ESTIMATE” is used to evaluate immune scores and stromal scores15. Immune cell infiltration was calculated using the single-sample gene-set enrichment analysis algorithm of the R package “GSVA”.

Identification of DEGs and construction of geneclusters

R package “limma” was used to screen differentially expressed genes (DEGs) of the two AAGclusters, | logFC | > 1 and P < 0.05 as threshold16. We identified survival-related DEGs using univariate Cox regression analysis, and defined distinct geneClusters based on selected DEGs using the R package “ConsensuClusterPlus”. Two geneClusters were obtained, named “Cluster 1 (C1)” and “Cluster 2 (C2)”.

Construction of the AAG signature score

Risk scores were calculated based on the survival-related DEGs using principal component analysis (PCA)17–19. We examined the distribution differences in LUAD phenotypes and explored the tumor immune microenvironment, clinicopathologic features, mutation characteristics, immunotherapy, and drug sensitivity to validate the effectiveness of the AAGscore.

Chemotherapy sensitivity and immunotherapy response predictions

Moreover, two immunotherapeutic cohorts were employed in this study: IMvigor210 (R package “IMvigor210CoreBiologies”) and GSE7822020,21. A patient with a complete remission (CR) or partial response (PR) was categorized as a responder, while a patient with stable disease (SD) or progressive disease (PD) was classified as a nonresponder. With the R package “pRRophetic”, we calculated semi-inhibitory concentration (IC50) of common antitumor drugs in patients22.

Statistical analysis

R software (v4.1.1) and its related packages were utilized for processing, analyzing, and visualizing the data (https://www.r-project.org/). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Construction of distinct AAGclusters

Based on survival-related AAGs, 750 LUAD patients were stratified into two distinct clusters (cluster-A and cluster-B), which were defined as AAGclusters (Fig. 4A, Supplementary Fig. 1). Survival analysis revealed that the survival rate of cluster-A and cluster-B was significantly different (P < 0.001), and the survival time of cluster-A was significantly shorter than that of cluster-B (Fig. 4B). The expression of most genes in cluster-A was significantly upregulated, except for DAPK2, HLA-DMA, LPL, CX3CL1, NOTCH1, ZNF304, ZTRK2, and PTK2 (Fig. 4C, D). Then, we downloaded the “h.all.v7.5.1.symbols” (HALLMARK), “c2.cp.kegg.v7.5.1.symbols” (KEGG), and “c2.cp.reactome.v7.5.1.symbols” (Reactome) files from the MSigDB database. GSVA enrichment analysis was used to identify differences in biological pathway activity between cluster-A and cluster-B (Supplementary Table 4). Compared with cluster-B, hallmark activities of epithelial-mesenchymal transition, angiogenesis, glycolysis, mTORC1 signaling, hypoxia, G2M checkpoint, DNA repair, and inflammatory response were higher in cluster-A (Fig. 4E). As shown in Fig. 4F, compared with cluster-A, cluster-B was markedly enriched in fatty acid metabolism, histidine metabolism, tyrosine metabolism, and drug metabolism cytochrome p450. A significant enrichment in glycolysis, MET activation of PTK2 signaling, and cell cycle checkpoint was shown in cluster-A based on the reactome-based pathway (Fig. 4G). Principal component analysis of AAGcluster showed segregation among different AAGclusters (Fig. 4H). We then calculated the stromal, immune, and ESTIMATE scores using the ESTIMATE algorithm (Fig. 4I). As for the differences between the two clusters, we found cluster-A to be remarkably abundant in immune cell infiltrates, including CD4+ T cell, dendritic cell, myeloid-derived suppressor cell (MDSC), macrophage, neutrophil, regulatory T cell, and others (Fig. 4J). In conclusion, there were significant differences in survival time, expression level, pathway enrichment and immune infiltration level between cluster-A and cluster-B subgroups.

Functional distinctions between the two AAGclusters

A differential expression analysis was conducted to determine the functional differences between the two AAGclusters (Supplementary Table 5). The volcano plot showed the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the two AAGclusters (Fig. 5A). Subsequently, GO and KEGG functional enrichment analysis was performed for these 172 DEGs. Genes were enriched in extracellular matrix organization, ossification, extracellular structure organization, external encapsulating structure organization, and connective tissue development, as indicated by GO analysis (BP); collagen-containing extracellular matrix, apical plasma membrane, endoplasmic reticulum lumen, collagen trimer, and complex of collagen trimers (CC); extracellular matrix structural constituent, endopeptidase activity, serine hydrolase activity, serine-type endopeptidase activity, and serine-type peptidase activity (MF) (Fig. 5B). KEGG enrichment analysis showed that 172 DEGs were mostly enriched in the protein digestion and absorption, ECM-receptor interaction, IL-17 signaling pathway, focal adhesion, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, relaxin signaling pathway, and transcriptional misregulation in cancer (Fig. 5C, D).

Next, 172 DEGs were analyzed by univariate Cox regression, and 28 survival-related DEGs (KRT6A, FAM83A, ANLN, SLC2A1, DLGAP5, FOXM1, RRM2, LYPD3, EXO1, SFTA3, CCNB1, TPX2, CHRDL1, CDKN3, KIF4A, CDC20, LOXL2, NEK2, CYP4B1, GPR116, PLOD2, CEP55, TMPRSS2, EGLN3, CACNA2D2, BIRC5, KIF2C, and MELK) with P < 0.00001 were selected for further analysis (Fig. 5E, Supplementary Table 6). Finally, we used the GSCA database to comprehensively analyze the expression levels, SNV, CNV, and DNA methylation of these 28 DEGs in LUAD (Fig. 5F–L). Figure 5G shows SNV mutations in these genes in LUAD, with mutation frequencies from highest to lowest being KIF4A (17%), EXO1 (16%), CHRDL1 (16%), TPX2 (14%), CYP4B1 (14%), ANLN (13%), PLOD2 (12%), KRT6A (12%), KIF2C (12%), ADGRF5 (10%), and FOXM1 (9%). Figure 5H demonstrates the presence of multiple mutation types in these genes, dominated by missense mutations. Figure 5I-J demonstrates the CNV mutations in these genes in LUAD and the correlation between CNV mutations and their mRNA expression levels (Supplementary Table 7). FAM83A, CEP55, and KRT6A showed significant hypomethylation in LUAD (Fig. 5K). FAM83A, CEP55, KRT6A, EGLN3, SLC2A1, TPX2, SFTA3, CACNA2D2, LYPD3, LOXL2, PLOD2, TMPRSS2, and CYP4B1 showed significant negative correlation between DNA methylation and mRNA expression in LUAD (Fig. 5L, Supplementary Table 8).

Construction of geneclusters based on survival-related DEGs

Based on the 28 survival-related DEGs, 750 patients were divided into two geneClusters (Cluster-1 and Cluster-2) (Fig. 6A). Compared with the Cluster-1 (C1), the Cluster-2 (C2) had a better prognosis (Fig. 6B). There were significant differences in DEGs between the two geneClusters, and most DEGs were upregulated in Cluster-1, except CYP4B1, CACNA2D2, GPR116, TMPRSS2, SFTA3, and CHRDL1 (Fig. 6C, D).

Construction of AAGscore and exploration of its clinical significance

To quantify the AAG signature of a single LUAD sample, a scoring system called AAGscore was developed based on PCA algorithm. Survival analysis showed that patients in the high-AAGscore group had better prognosis (Fig. 7A). We discovered that AAGcluster-B and geneCluster-2 subtype had higher AAGscores (Fig. 7B, C). Using the alluvial diagram, we found that AAGcluster-B subtypes were highly overlapping with geneCluster-2, and this overlapping population exhibited high AAGscores and a favorable outcome (Fig. 7D). To illustrate the relationship between AAGscore and immune cell infiltration, correlation analysis heatmaps showed that AAGscore were significantly positively correlated with the infiltration of activated B cells, MDSCs, macrophages, monocytes, natural killer T cells, type-1 T helper cells, and regulatory T cells (Fig. 7E). In addition, the correlation between clinical characteristics (survival status, gender, recurrence/metastasis, and stage) and risk score was examined. Notably, there was an association between a high-score and favorable survival outcomes in patients with LUAD (Fig. 7F–G). There were significant differences in AAGscore by gender, with a higher percentage of females in the high-score group (Fig. 7H–I). Using recurrence/metastasis as an indicator, the recurrence/metastasis rate was lower in the high-score group (Fig. 7J–K). There were also significant differences in AAGscore across clinical stages, with a higher proportion of patients in clinical stage I in the high-score group (Fig. 7L–M). Finally, we investigated the distribution differences of mutation in high- and low-score groups of LUAD cohort with “maftools” package (Fig. 7N, O). According to the mutation landscape of the waterfall plot, the low-score group (87.92%) exhibited a higher mutation burden than the high-score group (73.26%).

Correlation of AAGscore with immunotherapy response and drug sensitivity

In cancer, chemokines play a key role in mediating the migration of immune cells to the tumor, thus forming the immune signature of the tumor microenvironment, usually a tumor-promoting state. We further investigated the association between AAGscore and chemokines and receptor expression (Fig. 8A). As shown in Fig. 8B, risk scores were positively correlated with bile acid metabolism, hedgehog signaling, P53 pathway, heme metabolism, NOTCH signaling, coagulation, early estrogen response, TGF-β signaling and other hallmark pathways; and negatively correlated with glycolysis, PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling, unfolded protein reaction, mitotic spindle, DNA repair, mTORC1 signaling. To predict the LUAD patients’ response to immunotherapy, we further examined the difference in the expression of immune-checkpoint related genes (CD274, CTLA4, LAG3, PDCD1, and TIGIT) between patients with low and high AAGscore, and found that the expression of these genes was upregulated in the low-score group (Fig. 8C). Furthermore, two immunotherapy cohorts (IMvigor210, and GSE78220) were used to investigate whether AAGscore could predict patients’ responses to anti-PD-L1 therapy (Fig. 9). In the IMvigor210 cohort, patients with a low score responded better to immunotherapy. However, in the GSE78220 cohort, there was no significant difference in immunotherapy sensitivity between the high and low score groups. Next, to determine the efficacy of AAGscore as a biomarker for predicting chemotherapeutic response, we estimated the IC50 values of commonly used antitumor agents in the high- and low-score groups (Fig. 10). We could observe significant differences in IC50 estimates in cisplatin (P = 1.4e−12) (Fig. 10A), paclitaxel (P < 2.22e-16) (Fig. 10B), docetaxel (P < 2.22e−16) (Fig. 10C), camptothecin (P = 1.2−e11) (Fig. 10D), doxorubicin (P = 1.8e−05) (Fig. 10E), erlotinib (P = 2.1e−11) (Fig. 10F), etoposide (P = 6.5e−05) (Fig. 10G), gemcitabine (P = 2.8e−07) (Fig. 10H), imatinib (P < 2.22e−16) (Fig. 10I), rapamycin (P < 2.22e−16) (Fig. 10J), tipifarnib (P = 3.8e−09) (Fig. 10K), and vinblastine (P = 4e−14) (Fig. 10L) between the high- and low-score groups. Taken together, these findings indicate that AAGscores are correlated with drug sensitivity.

Discussion

Malignant tumors are a major threat to human health. In recent years, the incidence of cancer has been on the rise, the mortality rate has been high, and the patient population tends to be younger. Consequently, accurate cancer diagnosis and treatment are now key research focuses23–27. As malignant tumors are a complex disease with high molecular heterogeneity, accurate tumor classification plays a crucial role in clinical diagnosis, clinical medication guidance and prognosis prediction28–30.

A comprehensive and accurate understanding of the molecular mechanism of cancer development can promote the precise treatment of tumors31. Wang et al. explored the molecular mechanism of anoikis regulation of tumor metastasis, and discussed the therapeutic potential of targeting anoikis in the treatment of lung cancer metastasis32. The clinical behavior and molecular landscape of LUAD are highly heterogeneous33–35. In the present study, anoikis and angiogenesis related genes (AAGs) were integrated into LUAD, and two different AAGclusters were obtained. Interestingly, the results showed that different AAGclusters had unique immune cell infiltration characteristics. In addition, in order to quantify the AAG signature of a single LUAD sample, a scoring system called AAGscore was developed based on PCA algorithm. Our results showed that there were significant differences in survival time, mutation frequency, expression of chemokines and receptors, expression of immune checkpoint related genes, immunotherapy efficacy and antitumor drug sensitivity between high- and low-AAGscore groups of LUAD patients. Patients with high-AAGscores had a better prognosis according to survival analysis. We also discovered that AAGcluster-B and geneCluster-2 subtype showed the higher AAGscore. These results suggest that AAGscore is a positive prognostic indicator in LUAD. IMvigor210 and GSE78220 are datasets often used as cross-cancer validation in lung cancer immunotherapy studies36–38. Cross-cancer validation also has limitations and the influence of tumor microenvironment heterogeneity needs to be considered. Importantly, a number of conventional antitumor drugs were selected to test the sensitivity (IC50) of high- and low-AAGscore groups to drug therapy. Cisplatin, paclitaxel, docetaxel, camptothecin, doxorubicin, etoposide, gemcitabine, rapamycin, and vinblastine have higher drug sensitivity in the low-score group; erlotinib, imatinib, and tipifarnib are more sensitive in the high-score group. Taken together, these findings indicated that AAGscores are correlated with drug sensitivity.

Functional enrichment analysis was performed on the 172 DEGs. GO analysis indicated significant enrichments in biological processes related to extracellular matrix organization and connective tissue development. Cellular components were primarily associated with the collagen-containing extracellular matrix and apical plasma membrane, while molecular functions involved extracellular matrix structural constituents and serine hydrolase activities. Additionally, KEGG pathway analysis showed these genes were predominantly enriched in ECM-receptor interaction, focal adhesion, the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, and protein digestion and absorption. These findings suggest that DEGs influence cancer initiation, progression, and immune evasion not independently, but by acting collaboratively with other genes, a discovery which deepens our understanding of their biological functions in cancer.

The immune microenvironment is an important part of the tumor microenvironment, which contains the comprehensive combination of immune cells and immune-checkpoint related genes such as programmed cell death-1 (PD-1), and programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1)39–43. Many of these genes are considered putative biomarkers in clinical practice. Immuno-checkpoint related genes CD274, CTLA4, LAG3, PDCD1, and TIGIT were highly expressed in the low-AAGscore group. Previous studies have shown that chemokines can directly target nonimmune cells in the tumor microenvironment, including tumor cells, stroma, and vascular endothelial cells44,45. Therefore, chemokines are involved in the development of a variety of tumors, such as angiogenesis, metastasis, tumor cell proliferation, stemness and aggressiveness, and are key determinants of disease progression, with a significant impact on patient prognosis and treatment response. We initially believe that AAGscore can serve as a predictive biomarker for the benefits of immunotherapy for LUAD. However, our research results are based solely on 750 samples from the public database, with a very small sample size. There is a lack of multi-center and large-sample clinical studies to verify these findings. In the future, longitudinal study designs can be implemented to track the disease progression and treatment response of patients. Such a design can provide more abundant data to better study the relationship between AAGscore and disease progression.

Conclusions

In summary, we constructed molecular subtypes and AAGscores of LUAD based on AAG signatures. Risk score model is used to predict the prognosis of LUAD patients and the efficacy and sensitivity of antitumor drugs. By systematically evaluating risk scores, we can gain a deeper understanding of tumor heterogeneity and mechanisms of invasion and metastasis, as well as improve the precision and personalization of treatment strategies.

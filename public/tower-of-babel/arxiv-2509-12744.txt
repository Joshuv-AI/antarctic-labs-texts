Introduction

Consider the following stochastic partial differential equation(SPDE):

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}u(t,x)+b(u(t,x))+\sigma(u(t,x))W(dt,dx).\\
u(t,0)=u(t,1)=0.\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
t\geq 0
,
x\in[0,1]
and
W
is the space-time white noise on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,1]
defined on some filtrated probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
with
\mathcal{F}_{t}
satisfying the usual conditions.

In this paper, assuming
u_{0}\in L^{2}([0,1])
we are concerned with the
L^{2}
-well-posedness of the SPDE (
1.1
) with superlinear coefficient
b
which are generally not dissipative and have growth like
|z|log|z|
at infinity.

It is well known that equation (
1.1
) admits a unique global solution when the coefficients
b
and
\sigma
meet the usual Lipschitz condition. There are many papers in the literature that discuss stochastic partial differential equations with locally Lipschitz coefficients that have polynomial growth and satisfy certain monotonicity (or dissipative) conditions, see e.g.
[
5
,
16
,
25
]
. However, to be able to use certain localization procedure, one has to consider the solution in a
L^{\infty}
-space with an initial value that is continuous and bounded.

If the drift
b(z)
in (
1.1
) grows faster than
|z|\log|z|
as
z\to\infty
, then a blow-up can occur. In particular, if
b(z)=z^{1+\epsilon}
, and
\sigma=0
(the deterministic setting),
then equation (1.1) has blow-up solutions, but nontrivial global stationary solutions may also exist. There are many articles on this topic, see e.g.
[
20
,
23
,
11
]
. In
[
17
]
, Bonder and Groisman studied the stochastic setting and proved that for (
1.1
) if
u_{0}\geq 0
is continuous,
b\geq 0
is convex, and
\sigma
is constant, then the Osgood condition
\int^{\infty}\frac{1}{b(z)}dz<\infty
results in a blow-up in finite time with probability 1.
In particular, if
b(z)
grows faster than
C|z|(\log|z|)^{1+\epsilon}
for some
\epsilon>0
as
|z|\to\infty
,
then the introduction of any amount of additive space–time white noise to equation (1.1) results in a blow-up.
So, the critical case is that the drift function
b
grows as fast as
|u|\log|u|
when
|u|\to\infty
. A typical example of the critical case is
b(u)=u\log|u|
.
Several papers also investigated the connections between the Osgood condition and the finite time blow-up,
see e.g.
[
19
,
18
,
8
,
9
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
. In particular, in
[
19
]
, the authors proved that if
b
is nonnegative, increasing and locally Lipschitz,
\sigma
is bounded and locally Lipschitz, the initial value
u_{0}
is nonnegative and continuous, then the solutions to equation (
1.1
) blow up in finite time with positive probability implies that
b
satisfies the Osgood condition. However, in all these papers, the initial value
u_{0}
is assumed to be continuous or bounded, in contrast to the case
u_{0}\in L^{2}([0,1])
that we study in this paper.

In addition, logarithmic nonlinearity
b(z)=z\log|z|
also appears widely in other fields. It has been introduced in the study of nonlinear wave mechanics and a relativistic field in physics,
and represents an important type of nonlinearity with a distinctive physical background.
The logarithmic wave mechanics and the logarithmic Schrödinger equations have been studied by many authors. We refer the reader to
[
3
,
4
,
28
]
and the references therein for more details. The logarithmic parabolic equations have also been widely studied, see
[
34
,
1
,
6
,
7
]
and references therein. In particular, in
[
34
]
(see also
[
26
]
for related works), the logarithmic parabolic (elliptic) equations were studied to understand the Ricci flow.

For the well-posedness of the stochastic reaction-diffusion equation (
1.1
) with logarithmic superlinear drift, in
[
13
]
the authors obtained two main results. One is that when
|b(z)|{=}O(|z|\log|z|)
,
\sigma
is bounded and
u_{0}\in L^{2}([0,1])
, if
L^{2}
-valued solutions exist, then the solutions will not blow up, but the well-posedness of the solutions in
L^{2}([0,1])
is left open. The other one is that if
b
and
\sigma
are locally Lipschitz,
|b(z)|{=}O(|z|\log|z|)
and
|\sigma(z)|{=}o(|z|(\log|z|)^{\frac{1}{4}})
, then the solution is globally well-posed in
C(\mathbb{R}_{+}{\times}[0,1],\mathbb{R})
. In
[
32
]
, the authors considered the equation (
1.1
) driven by a Brownian motion on a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, they established the global well-posedness of
L^{2}
-valued solutions when
b(z){=}z\log|z|
,
\sigma
is locally-log-Lipschitz and
|\sigma(z)|{=}O(|z|(\log|z|)^{\frac{1}{2}})
. In
[
33
]
, the authors considered equation (
1.1
) on the whole line, they established the global well-posedness of the solutions in
C(\mathbb{R}_{+},C_{tem})
with initial data in
C_{tem}
(tempered continuous functions) when
b
is locally-log-Lipshitz and
|b(z)|{=}O(|z|\log|z|)
,
\sigma
is Lipshitz and bounded. In
[
30
]
, the authors studied the equation (
1.1
) on a bounded domain with bounded initial values.
In
[
8
]
, the authors also considered equation (
1.1
) on the whole line, they proved that if
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for
p\geq 6
,
b
and
\sigma
are locally Lipschitz with
b(0){=}\sigma(0){=}0
, and
|b(z)|\leq|z|\log|z|
,
|\sigma(z)|\leq|z|(\log|z|)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\log\log|z|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for big

, then there exists a unique global solution. In
[
31
]
, the author considered equation (
1.1
) on a bounded domain, they established the existence and uniqueness of global solutions under two sets of assumptions on the coefficients
b
and
\sigma
and assume that the initial value

is continuous and periodic. In particular, they allowed

to be polynomially growing up to

[

is Hölder continuous and uniformly bounded away from below and above,
|b(z)|\leq|z|(\log(1/z))^{A_{1}}
and
|\sigma(z)|\leq|z|(\log(1/z))^{A_{2}}
with

and

,

and

are Lipschitz continuous away from

[
9
,

,

,
27
,

]
and references therein.

In this article, we consider the SPDE (
1.1
) in the
L^{2}
setting with the initial value
u_{0}\in L^{2}([0,1])
and a driving space-time white noise. We assume that the coefficient
b
is locally log-Lipschitz (see (
2.4
)) and
|b(z)|=O(|z|\log|z|)
at infinity, the coefficient
\sigma
is Lipschitz and of sublinear growth. Under these conditions, we obtain the global well-posedness of solutions to equation (
1.1
) in
L^{2}([0,1])
, thereby resolve the problem left open in
[
13
]
. Moreover, our proof provides an alternative approach to demonstrate the non-blowup of solutions to (
1.1
) obtained in
[
13
]
. Since equation (
1.1
) is driven by multiplicative space-time white noise, the Itô’s formula is not valid and therefore we can not apply the techniques used in
[
32
]
. Also in the
L^{2}
-setting, the usual localization techniques of truncating the coefficients are not applicable. Furthermore, due to the nature of logarithmic nonlinearity, to establish uniqueness, we are forced to work with the first order moment estimates for the solutions, which is harder to handle. To address the aftermetioned problems, we derive some new types of Gronwall’s inequalities and carry out delicate analysis for the logarithmic nonlinear terms, thereby successfully establishing a priori estimates for the solutions. Meanwhile, to prove the pathwise uniquenss, we establish precise lower order moment estimates for the stochastic convolution with respect to space-time white noise.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we present the framework for equation (
1.1
), give the hypothesis and state the main results. In Section 3, we will give several Gronwall-type inequalities and prove some estimates associated with the heat kernel of the Laplacian operator. In Section 4, we establish lower order moment estimates of the stochastic convolution with respect to space-time white noise and establish a priori estimates of the solutions to equation (
1.1
). In Section 5, we prove the existence of probabilistically weak solutions to equation (
1.1
). Section 6 is devoted to the proof of the pathwise uniqueness of solutions to (
1.1
). In Appendix, we prove a convergence theorem for stochastic integrals with respect to space-time white noise.

Convention on constants. Throughout the paper,
C
denotes a generic positive constant whose value may change from line to line. Other constants will be denoted by
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
, etc. They are all positive and their precise values are not important. The dependence of constants on parameters if needed will be indicated, e.g.

,
C_{p}
.

2
Statement of main results

Recall the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

An
L^{2}([0,1])
valued random field solution to equation (
1.1
) is a jointly measurable and adapted space-time process
u:=
{
u(t,x):(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,1]
}
such that
u(t)\in L^{2}([0,1])

\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\forall\ t\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u(t,x)&=P_{t}u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)b(u(s,y))dyds\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(u(s,y))W(ds,dy),\quad\text{a.s.}\ x\in[0,1],\end{aligned}

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{2}n^{2}\pi^{2}t}e_{n}(x)e_{n}(y),

(2.2)

with
e_{n}(x)=\sqrt{2}\sin(n\pi x)
, which constitutes a set of orthonormal basis in
L^{2}([0,1])
, and {
P_{t}
}
t≥0
is the corresponding heat semigroup in
[0,1]
with the Dirichlet boundary condition defined by
P_{t}f(x)=\int_{0}^{1}p_{t}(x,y)f(y)dy
.

Remark 2.2.
The above mild formulation is equivalent to the weak (in the sense of partial differential equations) formulation of the stochastic reaction-diffusion equations.

Next, we introduce the following conditions for the nonlinear term
b
and
\sigma
. Set
\log_{+}(u):=\log(1\vee u)
for any
u\geq 0.

(H1).
b
is continuous, and there exist two nonnegative constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that for any
u\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle|b(u)|\leq c_{1}|u|\log_{+}|u|+c_{2}.

(2.3)

(H2). There exist nonnegative constants
c_{3}
,
c_{4}
,
c_{5}
such that for any
u,v\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle|b(u)-b(v)|\leq c_{3}|u-v|\log_{+}\left(\frac{1}{|u-v|}\right)+c_{4}\log_{+}(|u|\vee|v|)|u-v|+c_{5}|u-v|.

(2.4)

(H3).
\sigma
is continuous, and there exist two nonnegative constants
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
and
\theta\in[0,1)
such that for any
u\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle|\sigma(u)|\leq d_{1}|u|^{\theta}+d_{2}.

(2.5)

(H4). There exists a nonnegative constant
d_{3}
such that for any
u,v\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle|\sigma(u)-\sigma(v)|\leq d_{3}|u-v|.

(2.6)

Remark 2.3.
Note that condition (H2) implies condition (H1). A typical example that satisfies (H2) is
b(z)=z\log|z|
. For the global existence of the solution, only the condition (H1) is needed. The condition (H2) is used for the uniqueness of the solution.

Now we can state the main results of this paper.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that
u_{0}\in L^{2}([0,1])
and (H1) and (H3) are satisfied. Then for any
T>0
there exists a probabilistically weak solution to (
1.1
) with sample paths a.s. in
C([0,T],L^{2}([0,1]))
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume that
u_{0}\in L^{2}([0,1])
and (H2) and (H4) are satisfied. Then for any
T>0
, the pathwise uniqueness holds for solutions of (
1.1
) in
C([0,T],L^{2}([0,1]))
.

Preliminaries

3.1
Gronwall-type inequalities

In this subsection, we will provide three Gronwall-type inequalities, which play an important role in this paper.

The next result can be found in
[
33
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
,
a
,
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
be nonnegative functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and let
M
be an increasing function with
M(0)\geq 1
. Moreover, suppose that
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
are integrable in finite time intervals. Assume that
\forall\ t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle X(t)+a(t)\leq M(t)+\int_{0}^{t}c_{1}(s)X(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}c_{2}(s)X(s)\log_{+}X(s)ds,

and the above integral is finite. Then for any
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle X(t)+a(t)\leq M(t)^{\exp(C_{2}(t))}\exp(\exp(C_{2}(t))\int_{0}^{t}c_{1}(s)\exp(-C_{2}(s))ds),

where
C_{2}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}c_{2}(s)ds
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
f\geq 0
is an integrable function, and
M
is a nonnegative increasing function,
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
,
c_{3}
are nonnegative constant numbers. And
f
satisfies the following inequality:

\displaystyle f(t)\leq M(t)+c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}f(s)ds+c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}f(s)ds+c_{3}\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\log_{+}\frac{1}{f(s)}ds,\quad\forall\ t\geq 0.

Then there exists an increasing function
C(t)
such that for any
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle f(t)\leq C(t)M(t)+C(t)\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\log_{+}\frac{1}{f(s)}ds.

(3.1)

Furthermore, if
M\equiv 0
, then
f\equiv 0
.

Proof.

By carrying out one iteration on the aforementioned inequality, we get

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle\leq M(t)+c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}f(s)ds+c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}M(s)ds

\displaystyle+c_{1}c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}(t-s)^{-\alpha}f(u)duds+c_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}(t-s)^{-\alpha}(s-u)^{-\alpha}f(u)duds

\displaystyle+c_{2}c_{3}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}(t-s)^{-\alpha}f(u)\log_{+}\frac{1}{f(u)}duds+c_{3}\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\log_{+}\frac{1}{f(s)}ds.

Using the monotonicity of
M
and changing the order of integration gives

\displaystyle f(t)\leq C_{1}(t)M(t)+C_{2}(t)\int_{0}^{t}f(s)ds+C_{3}(t)\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\log_{+}\frac{1}{f(s)}ds,

where
C_{1}(t)
,
C_{2}(t)
and
C_{3}(t)
are all positive increasing functions. By the usual Gronwall inequality,

\displaystyle f(t)\leq C_{1}(t)e^{C_{2}(t)t}M(t)+C_{3}(t)e^{C_{2}(t)t}\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\log_{+}\frac{1}{f(s)}ds.

Let
C(t)=\max\left\{C_{1}(t)e^{C_{2}(t)t},C_{3}(t)e^{C_{2}(t)t}\right\}
, this proves (
3.1
). In particular, when
M\equiv 0
, then we have

\displaystyle f(t)\leq C(t)\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\log_{+}\frac{1}{f(s)}ds.

By Lemma 3.2 in
[
33
]
, we get
f\equiv 0
.
∎

, and any

3.2
Estimates associated with heat kernels

In this subsection, we present some estimates associated with the heat kernel of the Laplacian operator, which will be used in our analysis later.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p_{t}(x,y)
be the heat kernel of Laplacian operator defined by (
2.2
), then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
h>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}(p_{r+h}(x,z)-p_{r}(x,z))^{2}dzdr\leq Ch^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall\ x\in[0,1].

(3.3)

The above lemma can be found in the Appendix of
[
14
]
(Lemma B.2.1). In fact, we can prove the following stronger estimate.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
p_{t}(x,y)
be defined by (
2.2
), then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
h>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{0}^{1}(p_{r+h}(x,z)-p_{r}(x,z))^{2}dzdr\leq Ch^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.4)

Proof.

By the expression of the heat kernel and the orthonormal property of
e_{n}
in
L^{2}([0,1])
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{0}^{1}(p_{r+h}(x,z)-p_{r}(x,z))^{2}dzdr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}n^{2}(r+h)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}n^{2}r})e_{n}(x)e_{n}(z)\right]^{2}dzdr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(e_{n}(x))^{2}(e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}n^{2}(r+h)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}n^{2}r})^{2}\right]dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}n^{2}(r+h)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}n^{2}r})^{2}\right)dr.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}n^{2}(r+h)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}n^{2}r})^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}r}(1-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}n^{2}h})^{2}.

By virtue of the following inequality:

\displaystyle 1-e^{-x}\leq\min\{1,x\},\quad\forall\ x\geq 0,

we get

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}r}(1-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}n^{2}h})^{2}dr=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}n^{2}h})^{2}}{n^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\min\left\{n^{-2},\frac{1}{4}\pi^{4}n^{2}h^{2}\right\}.

Next, we will show that there exists a constant
C>0
, such that for any
h>0
the right-hand side of the above inequality can be bounded by
Ch^{\frac{1}{2}}
. The proof will be divided into two cases according to the value of
h
.

Case 1
:
h\geq\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}
. In this case, for any
n\geq 1
,
\min\{n^{-2},\frac{1}{4}\pi^{4}n^{2}h^{2}\}=n^{-2}
. So

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{3}\leq\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{2}}h^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Case 2
:
0<h<\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}
. In this case, the sum can be further divided into two parts. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\min\{n^{-2},\frac{1}{4}\pi^{4}n^{2}h^{2}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{1}{\pi}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}]}\frac{1}{4}\pi^{4}n^{2}h^{2}+\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=[\frac{1}{\pi}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}]+1}^{\infty}n^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\left[\sqrt{2}\pi h^{\frac{1}{2}}+2\int_{\frac{1}{\pi}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\infty}z^{-2}dz\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}[\sqrt{2}\pi h^{\frac{1}{2}}+2\pi h^{\frac{1}{2}}]\leq\frac{4}{\pi}h^{\frac{1}{2}}.

So we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
p_{t}(x,y)
be the same as before. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
x
,
y\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sup_{z\in[0,1]}|p_{t}(x,z)-p_{t}(y,z)|\right)dt\leq C|x-y|+C|x-y|\log\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right).

(3.5)

Proof.

By (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in[0,1]}|p_{t}(x,z)-p_{t}(y,z)|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\sin(n\pi x)-\sin(n\pi y)|e^{-\frac{1}{2}n^{2}\pi^{2}t}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\min\{1,n|x-y|\}e^{-\frac{1}{2}n^{2}\pi^{2}t}.

So

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sup_{z\in[0,1]}|p_{t}(x,z)-p_{t}(y,z)|\right)dt&\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\min\{1,n|x-y|\}}{n^{2}}\\
&\leq C|x-y|\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{1}{|x-y|}]}\frac{1}{n}+C\sum_{n=1+[\frac{1}{|x-y|}]}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}.\end{aligned}

(3.6)

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{1}{|x-y|}]}\frac{1}{n}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{1}{|x-y|}]-1}\frac{1}{n+1}\leq 1+\int_{1}^{[\frac{1}{|x-y|}]}\frac{1}{z}dz\leq 1+\log\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right).

Thus, the first term of (
3.6
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle C|x-y|\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{1}{|x-y|}]}\frac{1}{n}\leq C|x-y|+C|x-y|\log\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right).

(3.7)

For the second term of (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle C\sum_{n=1+[\frac{1}{|x-y|}]}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\leq C\int_{[\frac{1}{|x-y|}]}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z^{2}}dz\leq C|x-y|.

(3.8)

Combining (
3.6
)-(
3.8
) together yields (
3.5
).
∎

4
Moment estimates

In this section, we establish a priori estimates for solutions to equation (
1.1
). To this end, we first establish the
L^{2}([0,1])
-norm moment estimate of stochastic convolution with respect to space-time white noise. In the sequel, for simplicity, we use the symbol
L^{2}
to represent
L^{2}([0,1])
, and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
. For clarity, sometimes we will use
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{x}}
to emphasize that the integral is with respect to the variable
x
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{\sigma(s,y):(s,y)\in\mathbb{R_{+}}\times[0,1]\}
be a random
field such that the following stochastic convolution with respect to space-time white noise is well defined. Let
\tau
be a stopping time. Then for
p>8
and
T>0
, there exits a constant
C_{p,T}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T\land\tau}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,y)W(ds,dy)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{p}\right]\leq C_{p,T}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T\land\tau}\|\sigma(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}ds.

(4.1)

Proof.

Let
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}([0,1])
. Set

\displaystyle W_{t}^{j}:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}e_{j}(y)W(ds,dy).

Then
\{W^{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
is a family of independent standard Brownian motions. Moreover, the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,y)W(ds,dy)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,y)e_{j}(y)dy\right)dW_{s}^{j}.

(4.2)

Set
W_{s}:=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}W_{s}^{j}e_{j}
. Then
W
is the corresponding cylindrical Wiener process on
L^{2}([0,1])
. And (
4.2
) can be written as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,y)W(ds,dy)=\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}\sigma(s)dW_{s},

where
P_{t-s}\sigma(s)
is an Hilbert-Schmidt operator from
L^{2}([0,1])
to
L^{2}([0,1])
defined by

\displaystyle\left(P_{t-s}\sigma(s)h\right)(x):=\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,y)h(y)dy,\quad\forall\ h\in L^{2}([0,1]),

and its Hilbert-Schmidt norm can be computed as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|P_{s-r}\sigma(r)\|_{HS(L^{2},L^{2})}^{2}&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{s-r}\sigma(r)e_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}p_{s-r}(x,y)\sigma(r,y)e_{n}(y)dy\right)^{2}dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle p_{s-r}(x,\cdot)\sigma(r,\cdot),e_{n}(\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}^{2}dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|p_{s-r}(x,y)\sigma(r,y)|^{2}dydx.\end{aligned}

(4.3)

The proof is based on the factorization method introduced in
[
12
]
and is inspired by
[
33
]
. Set

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}\sigma(s)=\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\alpha}P_{s-r}\sigma(r)dW_{r},

\displaystyle J^{\alpha-1}f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}P_{t-s}f(s)ds.

Then by the stochastic Fibini theorem we obtain,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}\sigma(s)dW_{s}=\frac{\sin\alpha\pi}{\pi}J^{\alpha-1}(J_{\alpha}\sigma).

(4.4)

Take
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha<\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{p}
, use Hölder’s inequality and the BDG inequality to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T\land\tau}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,x)W(ds,dy)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T\land\tau}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}P_{t-s}J_{\alpha}\sigma(s)ds\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T\land\tau}\left\{\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\|P_{t-s}J_{\alpha}\sigma\|_{L^{2}}ds\right\}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T\land\tau}\left\{\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\|J_{\alpha}\sigma\|_{L^{2}}ds\right\}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T\land\tau}\left\{\left[\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{(\alpha-1)\frac{p}{p-1}}ds\right]^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|J_{\alpha}\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{p}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}s^{(\alpha-1)\frac{p}{p-1}}ds\right)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\|J_{\alpha}\sigma(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}1_{[0,\tau]}(s)ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\alpha}P_{s-r}\sigma(r)\mathbf{1}_{[0,\tau]}(r)dW_{r}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\tau]}(s)\right]ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\alpha}\|P_{t-r}\sigma(r)\|_{HS(L^{2},L^{2})}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\tau]}(r)dr\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}ds.

(4.5)

By the equality (
4.3
) and the following inequality,

\displaystyle p_{s-r}(x,y)\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{s-r}},\quad\quad\forall\ x,y\in[0,1],

we have

\displaystyle\left\|P_{t-r}\sigma(r)\right\|_{HS}^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|p_{s-r}(x,y)\sigma(r,y)|^{2}dydx\leq C(s-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\sigma(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.6)

Substituting (
4.6
) into (
4
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T\land\tau}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(u(t,x))W(ds,dy)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\|\sigma(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\tau]}(r)dr\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left\{\left[\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{(-2\alpha-\frac{1}{2})\frac{p}{p-2}}dr\right]^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left[\int_{0}^{s}\|\sigma(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\tau]}(r)dr\right]^{\frac{2}{p}}\right\}^{\frac{p}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{s}r^{-(2\alpha+\frac{1}{2})\frac{p}{p-2}}dr\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{s}\|\sigma(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\tau]}(r)dr\right]ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,T}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T\land\tau}\|\sigma(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}dr.

∎

Having Lemma 4.1, the proof of the following result is almost identical to that of Proposition 4.2 in
[
33
]
, hence we omit its proof.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\{\sigma(s,y):(s,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,1]\}
be a random field such that the following stochastic convolution with respect to the space-time white noise is well defined. Let
\tau
be a stopping time. Then for any
\epsilon
,
T>0
, and
0<p\leq 8
, there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon,p,T}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T\land\tau}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(s,y)W(ds,dy)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T\land\tau}\|\sigma(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+C_{\epsilon,p,T}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T\land\tau}\|\sigma(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}ds\right].

Using the above moment estimates, we can prove the following a prior estimate of solutions.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that (H1) and (H3) are satisfied and let

\displaystyle T_{0}=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{\widetilde{C}}\log\frac{1}{\theta}\right\}

(4.7)

where
\widetilde{C}
is a positive constant which appears in (
4.14
). Assume
u
is a solution to equation (
1.1
), then for any
p\geq 1
we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}.

(4.8)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
p>8
. It follows from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{t}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)b(u(s,y))dsdy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(u(s,y))W(ds,dy)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

We denote

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)b(u(t,x))dsdy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

\displaystyle I_{2}(t)=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(u(t,x))W(ds,dy)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

For
I_{1}(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{1}(t)&\leq\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)(c_{1}|u(s,y)|\log_{+}(u(s,y))+c_{2})dsdy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\\
&\leq c_{1}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)|u(s,y)|\log_{+}(u(s,y))dsdy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+c_{2}t,\end{aligned}

(4.9)

where we have used

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}p_{t}(x,y)dy\leq 1,\quad\forall\ t>0,\ x\in[0,1].

(4.10)

By Hölder’s inequality and (
4.10
), the first term on the right hand side of (
4.9
) can be estimated as follows,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)|u(s,y)|\log_{+}(u(s,y))dsdy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\\
\leq&\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)|u(s,y)|\log_{+}(u(s,y))dy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}ds\\
\leq&\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\log_{+}^{2}(|u(s,y)|dy\right)\left(\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)|u(s,y)|^{2}dy\right)dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds\\
\leq&\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\log_{+}^{2}(|u(s,y)|)dy\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds.\end{aligned}

(4.11)

We now estimate the term

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{0}^{1}\log_{+}^{2}(|u(s,y)|)p_{t-s}(x,y)dy.

Let
g(z)=\log_{+}^{2}z
. Then we know
g^{\prime}(z)=2\frac{1}{z}\log_{+}z\geq 0
. So
g(z)
is increasing.
And
g^{\prime\prime}(z)=\frac{2}{z^{2}}(1-\log_{+}z)<0
, provided
z>e
. So
g(z)
is concave when
z>e
. Next, we fix any
s>0
. Let
\Gamma:=\{y\in[0,1],|u(s,y)|>e\}
and
\mu_{x}(dy):=p_{t-s}(x,y)dy
. Obviously,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{0}^{1}\log_{+}^{2}(|u(s,y)|)p_{t-s}(x,y)dy\\
=&\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{[0,1]\setminus\Gamma}g(|u(s,y)|)\mu_{x}(dy)+\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{\Gamma}g(|u(s,y)|)\mu_{x}(dy).\end{aligned}

(4.12)

The first term on the right hand side of (
4.12
) can be bounded as follows,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{[0,1]\setminus\Gamma}g(|u(s,y)|)\mu_{x}(dy)\leq\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)dy\leq 1.

Thus, by Jensen’s inequality and the fact that
g
is inceasing, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{0}^{1}\log_{+}^{2}(|u(s,y)|)p_{t-s}(x,y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left\{1+\mu_{x}(\Gamma)\int_{\Gamma}g(|u(s,y)|)\frac{\mu_{x}(dy)}{\mu_{x}(\Gamma)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left\{1+\mu_{x}(\Gamma)g\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{x}(\Gamma)}\int_{\Gamma}|u(s,y)|\mu_{x}(dy)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left\{1+\mu_{x}(\Gamma)\log_{+}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{x}(\Gamma)}\int_{0}^{1}|u(s,y)|p_{t-s}(x,y)dy\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left\{1+\sqrt{\mu_{x}(\Gamma)}\log_{+}\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{x}(\Gamma)}\int_{0}^{1}|u(s,y)|p_{t-s}(x,y)dy\right)\right\}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left\{2+\log_{+}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}|u(s,y)|^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\right\}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{2+\log_{+}\left[(4\pi)^{-\frac{1}{4}}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{4}}||u(s)||_{L^{2}}\right]\right\}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{2+\frac{1}{4}\log_{+}\left(\frac{1}{t-s}\right)+\log_{+}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}\right\}^{2},

(4.13)

where we have used the facts that
\log_{+}(ab)\leq\log_{+}(a)+\log_{+}(b)
for any
a
,
b\geq 0
,
\sqrt{\mu_{x}(\Gamma)}\log_{+}\frac{1}{\mu_{x}(\Gamma)}\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}p_{t}(x,y)^{2}dy\leq(4\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}t^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall\ t>0.

Combining (
4.9
), (
4.11
) and (
4
) together gives

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)b(u(t,x))dsdy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}c_{1}\left[2+\frac{1}{4}\log_{+}\left(\frac{1}{t-s}\right)+\log_{+}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}\right]\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds+c_{2}t.

Hence for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
C_{c_{1},\alpha}
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+I_{2}(t)+c_{2}t+2c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}C_{c_{1},\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds+c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}\log_{+}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds.

In the sequel, we take
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
. By Lemma 3.3 and noting that
T_{0}\leq 1
, we have for any
t\leq T_{0}
,

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+c_{2}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+I_{2}(t))^{e^{\widetilde{C}t}},

(4.14)

Thus

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(1+c_{2}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\sup_{t\leq T_{0}}I_{2}(t)\right)^{e^{\widetilde{C}T_{0}}}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3^{pe^{\widetilde{C}T_{0}}-1}C^{p}\left[(1+c_{2})^{pe^{\widetilde{C}T_{0}}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{pe^{\widetilde{C}T_{0}}}+\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\leq T_{0}}I_{2}(t)^{pe^{\widetilde{C}T_{0}}}\right)\right].

(4.15)

By the Lemma 4.1, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T_{0}}I_{2}(t)^{pe^{\widetilde{C}T_{0}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T_{0}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(s,y)\sigma(u(s,y))W(ds,dy)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{pe^{\widetilde{C}T_{0}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{T_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\sigma(u(s))\|_{L^{2}}^{pe^{\widetilde{C}T_{0}}}ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{T_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}c(1+|u(s,y)|^{2\theta})dy\right)^{\frac{p}{2}e^{\widetilde{C}T_{0}}}ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T_{0}}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}ds\right]\right),

where we have used the fact that
\theta e^{\widetilde{C}T_{0}}\leq 1
and Hölder’s inequality. From (
4
) we deduce that for all
t\leq T_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\leq t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right)\leq C_{p,T_{0}}+3^{pe^{\widetilde{C}T_{0}}-1}C^{p}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{pe^{\widetilde{C}T_{0}}}+C_{p,T_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}ds\right].

By the Gronwall inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\leq T_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right)\leq C_{p,T_{0}}\left(1+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{pe^{\widetilde{C}}}\right).

∎

5
Existence of weak solutions

In this section, we fix
T>0
and prove the existence of probabilistically weak solutions of equation (
1.1
). We will approximate the coefficients
b
and
\sigma
by Lipschitz functions.

Let
\varphi
be a nonnegative smooth function on
\mathbb{R}
such that the support of
\varphi
is contained in
(-1,1)
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)dx=1
. Let
\{\eta_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of symmetric smooth functions such that
0\leq\eta_{n}\leq 1
,
\eta_{n}(x)=1
if
|x|\leq n
, and
\eta_{n}=0
if
|x|\geq n+2.

Define

\displaystyle b_{n}(x):=n\int_{\mathbb{R}}b(y)\varphi(n(x-y))dy\times\eta_{n}(x).

Then it is easy to check that there exist constants
L_{n}
and
L_{b}^{\prime}
such that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle|b_{n}(x)-b_{n}(y)|\leq L_{n}|x-y|,

\displaystyle|b_{n}(x)|\leq c_{1}|x|\log_{+}|x|+L_{b}^{\prime}(|x|+1),

where the constant
L_{b}^{\prime}
is independent of
n
. Moreover, if
x_{n}\xrightarrow{}x
in
\mathbb{R}
, then

\displaystyle b_{n}(x_{n})\xrightarrow{}b(x).

(5.1)

For
n\geq 1
, consider the following stochastic partial differential equation on the interval
[0,1]
,

\displaystyle u_{n}(t,x)

\displaystyle=P_{t}u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)b_{n}(u(s,y))dyds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(u_{n}(s,y))W(ds,dy).

It is known that for each
n\geq 1
, there exists a unique solution
u_{n}
to the above equation. Moreover, the sample paths of
u_{n}
are
a.s. in
C(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}([0,1])).

Following the proof of Theorem 4.3, we see that the following uniform estimate holds,

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T_{0}}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]\leq C_{p,T_{0}}

(5.2)

for any
p\geq 1
, where
T_{0}
is defined by (
4.7
).

We now want to show that the above estimate holds for any
T>0
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that (H1) and (H3) are satisfied then for any
T>0
and
\forall\ p\geq 1
, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]\leq C_{p,T}.

Proof.

\forall\ T\geq 0
, there is a nonnegative number
m
such that
T\in[mT_{0},(m+1)T_{0}]
, so we only need to show
\forall\ m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[mT_{0},(m+1)T_{0}]}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]\leq C_{m,p}.

As an example, we prove the case
m=1
, i.e.

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[T_{0},2T_{0}]}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}.

(5.3)

The same method can be applied to any
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By the uniqueness of
u_{n}(s,y)
,
\forall\ n\geq 0
, we have for
t\in[T_{0},2T_{0}]
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{n}(t,x)&=P_{t-T_{0}}u_{n}(T_{0})+\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)b_{n}(u_{n}(s,y))dyds\\
&+\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(u_{n}(s,y))W(ds,dy).\end{aligned}

(5.4)

Let
W^{T_{0}}(t,x):=W(t+T_{0},x)-W(T_{0},x)
,
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{T_{0}}:=\mathcal{F}_{t+T_{0}}
, and
u_{n}^{T_{0}}(t,x):=u_{n}(t+T_{0},x)
,
\forall\ t\geq 0
.
Then
W^{T_{0}}(t,x)
is a Brownian sheet with respect to the filtration
(\mathcal{F}^{T_{0}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Then by changing of variables, (
5.4
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle u_{n}^{T_{0}}(t,x)

\displaystyle=P_{t}u_{n}^{T_{0}}(0,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)b_{n}(u^{T_{0}}_{n}(s,y))dsdy

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(u_{n}^{T_{0}}(s,y))W^{T_{0}}(ds,dy),

where
t\in[0,T_{0}]
. By the contractivity of the heat kernel and the similar argument as in the proof of Theorem 4.3, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[T_{0},2T_{0}]}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]=\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T_{0}}\|u_{n}^{T_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}\left(1+\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\|u_{n}(T_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{pe^{C_{c_{1}}}}\right]\right)

for any
p\geq 1
. Since (
5.2
) holds for any
p\geq 1
, we see that

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[T_{0},2T_{0}]}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]\leq C_{p},

which proves (
5.3
).
∎

We let
L_{b}
be a constant independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle|b_{n}(x)|\leq L_{b}(|x|\log_{+}|x|+1).

Such a constant always exists because of the assumption on the coefficient
b
.

Next, we will establish the tightness of the approximating solutions
u_{n}
in
C([0,T],L^{2}([0,1]))
.
Set

\displaystyle I_{n}(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)b_{n}(u_{n}(s,y))dyds

and

\displaystyle J_{n}(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(u_{n}(s,y))W(ds,dy).

To prove the tightness of
u_{n}
in
C([0,T],L^{2}([0,1]))
, it suffices to prove that both
I_{n}(t,x)
and
J_{n}(t,x)
are tight in
C([0,T],L^{2}([0,1]))
. This will be done in Lemma 5.2 and Lemma 5.3 below.

Lemma 5.2
.

The sequence
I_{n}(t,x)
is tight in
C([0,T],L^{2}([0,1]))
.

Proof.

Since
C^{\alpha}([0,1])
is compactly embedded into
L^{2}([0,1])
, to establish the tightness in
C([0,T],L^{2}([0,1]))
, it suffices to show that:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{M\to\infty}\sup_{n}\mathbb{P}(\|I_{n}(t)\|_{C^{\alpha}([0,1])}\geq M)=0,\quad\quad\forall\ t\in(0,T],

where
\|\cdot\|_{C^{\alpha}([0,1])}
is the norm of the
\alpha
-Hölder space, and

\displaystyle\forall\ \epsilon>0,\quad\quad\lim\limits_{h\to 0}\sup_{n}\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{t,s\in[0,T],|t-s|\leq h}\|I_{n}(t,\cdot)-I_{n}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}>\epsilon\right)=0.

Noticing that
I_{n}(t,0)=0
, to verify these two conditions, it is sufficient to prove the following claims:

Claim 1
:
\forall\ t\in[0,T],

\exists\ p,q>0,

C_{p,q,T}>0
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}[|I_{n}(t,x)-I_{n}(t,y)|^{p}]\leq C_{p,q,T}|x-y|^{1+q},\quad\forall\ x,y\in[0,1].

Claim 2
:
\exists\ p,q>0,
and
C_{T}>0,
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}[\|I_{n}(t,\cdot)-I_{n}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}]\leq C_{T}|t-s|^{1+q},\quad\forall\ s,t\in[0,T].

We now prove
Claim 1
. Without loss of generality, we assume that

\displaystyle|b_{n}(z)|\leq L_{b}(1+|z|^{2}).

We have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |I_{n}(t,x)-I_{n}(t,y)|&=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}(p_{t-s}(x,z)-p_{t-s}(y,z))b_{n}(u_{n}(s,z))dsdz\right|\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}|p_{t-s}(x,z)-p_{t-s}(y,z)||b_{n}(u_{n}(s,z))|dsdz\\
&\leq L_{b}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}|p_{t-s}(x,z)-p_{t-s}(y,z)||u_{n}(s,z)|^{2}dsdz\right)\\
&+L_{b}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}|p_{t-s}(x,z)-p_{t-s}(y,z)|dsdz.\end{aligned}

(5.5)

The first term on the right hand side of above can be bounded as follows,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}|p_{t-s}(x,z)-p_{t-s}(y,z)||u_{n}(s,z)|^{2}dsdz\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\max_{s\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}|u_{n}(s,z)|^{2}dz\right)\int_{0}^{t}\max_{z\in[0,1]}\left|p_{t-s}(x,z)-p_{t-s}(y,z)\right|ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\max_{s\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}|u_{n}(s,z)|^{2}dz\right)|x-y|\left[\log\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right)+1\right],

where we have used Lemma 3.6. Note that
\forall\ \gamma\in(0,1)
, there exist nonnegative constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right)\leq c_{1}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}+c_{2}.

So we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}|p_{t-s}(x,z)-p_{t-s}(y,z)||u_{n}(s,z)|^{2}dsdz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\max_{s\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}|u_{n}(s,z)|^{2}dz\right)(c_{1}|x-y|^{1-\gamma}+c_{2}|x-y|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\max_{s\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}|u_{n}(s,z)|^{2}dz+1\right)|x-y|^{1-\gamma}.

The second term on the right hand side of (
5.5
) can be bounded similarly, but much simpler. Hence

\displaystyle|I_{n}(t,x)-I_{n}(t,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\max_{s\leq T}\|u_{n}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right)|x-y|^{1-\gamma}+L_{b}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}|p_{t-s}(x,z)-p_{t-s}(y,z)|dsdz

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\max_{s\leq T}\|u_{n}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\right)|x-y|^{1-\gamma}.

So

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[|I_{n}(t,x)-I_{n}(t,y)|^{p}\right]\leq C\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{s\leq T}\|u_{n}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\right]^{p}|x-y|^{(1-\gamma)p}.

By choosing
p
such that
(1-\gamma)p>1
and noting Lemma 5.1,
Claim 1
is proved. Next we prove
Claim 2
. Without loss of generality, we assume
s<t
. Obviously

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|I_{n}(t)-I_{n}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}&\leq c_{p}\left\|\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{1}(p_{t-r}(x,y)-p_{s-r}(x,y))b_{n}(u_{n}(r,y))drdy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{p}\\
&+c_{p}\left\|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-r}(x,y)b_{n}(u_{n}(r,y))drdy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{p}\\
&:=c_{p}(A_{1}^{p}+A_{2}^{p}).\end{aligned}

(5.6)

Now we estimate the terms
A_{1}^{p}
and
A_{2}^{p}
. For
A_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle\leq L_{b}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}\|p_{t-r}(x,y)-p_{s-r}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}|u_{n}(r,y)|^{2}drdy\right)

\displaystyle+L_{b}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}\|p_{t-r}(x,y)-p_{s-r}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}drdy.

Now

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}\|p_{t-r}(x,y)-p_{s-r}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}|u_{n}(r,y)|^{2}drdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{s}\sup_{y}\|p_{t-r}(x,y)-p_{s-r}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}dr\right)\times\left(\max_{r\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}|u_{n}(r,y)|^{2}dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{s}\sup_{y}\|p_{t-r}(x,y)-p_{s-r}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\times\left(\max_{r\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}|u_{n}(r,y)|^{2}dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t-s)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\max_{r\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}|u_{n}(r,y)|^{2}dy\right),

where Lemma 3.5 is used in the last line. Therefore,

\displaystyle A_{1}^{p}\leq C_{T}\left(\max_{r\leq T}\|u_{n}(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{2p}+1\right)(t-s)^{\frac{p}{4}}.

(5.7)

For
A_{2}^{p},
we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left\|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-r}(x,y)b_{n}(u_{n}(r,y))drdy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{p}\\
\leq&\left(\int_{s}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\|p_{t-r}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}}|b_{n}(u_{n}(r,y))|drdy\right)^{p}\\
\leq&\left(L_{b}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}(t-r)^{-\frac{1}{4}}(|u_{n}(r,y)|^{2}+1)drdy\right)^{p}\\
\leq&C_{p}\left(\sup_{r\leq T}\|u_{n}(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{2p}+1\right)(t-s)^{\frac{3}{4}p}.\end{aligned}

(5.8)

Combining (
5.6
)-(
5.8
) together and then taking expectations gives

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}[\|I_{n}(t)-I_{n}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}]\leq C_{T}\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{r\leq T}\|u_{n}(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{2p}+1\right](t-s)^{\frac{p}{4}},\quad\forall\ s,t\in[0,T].

We choose
p>4
, then the
Claim 2
is proved by Lemma 5.1.
∎

Next, we will prove the tightness of
\{J_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
with a different method.

Lemma 5.3
.

The sequence
J_{n}(t,x)
is tight in
C([0,T],L^{2}([0,1]))
.

Proof.

As in (
4.4
), we use the factorization method to write

\displaystyle J_{n}(t)=\frac{\sin\alpha\pi}{\pi}J^{\alpha-1}(J_{\alpha}\sigma(u_{n}))(t).

According to the fact (see Proposition 8.4 in
[
12
]
) that the embedding

\displaystyle J^{\alpha-1}:L^{p}([0,T],L^{2}([0,1]))\to C([0,T],L^{2}([0,1]))

is compact provided
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha
. Thus to prove the tightness of
J_{n}
in
C([0,T],L^{2}([0,1]))
, it suffices to prove that for some
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha
,
\forall\ \epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
M>0
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{P}(\|J_{\alpha}\sigma\|_{L^{p}([0,T],L^{2})}>M)<\epsilon.

By the Chebyshev inequality, it is sufficient to show

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\|J_{\alpha}\sigma(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}]ds=\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\alpha}P_{s-r}\sigma(u_{n}(r))dW_{r}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]ds<\infty.

We use the BDG inequality and (
4.3
) to deduce

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\alpha}P_{s-r}\sigma(u_{n}(r))dW_{r}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\alpha}\|P_{t-r}\sigma(u_{n}(r))\|_{HS(L^{2},L^{2})}^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\|\sigma(u_{n}(r))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{p}\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\|\sigma(u_{n}(r))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dr\right\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{E}\|\sigma(u_{n}(r))\|_{L^{2}}^{p})^{\frac{2}{p}}dr\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\|\sigma(u_{n}(r))\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}dr\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\|u_{n}(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+1\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}dr\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}ds.

If we take
p>4
so that
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha<\frac{1}{4}
, then the right-hand side of the above inequality is finite by Theorem 5.1. So we complete the proof.
∎

Now we prove the existence of probabilistically weak solutions to equation (
1.1
).

Proof of Theorem 2.4.

Let
C([0,T]{\times}[0,1],\mathbb{R})
be the space of real-valued continuous functions on
[0,T]\times[0,1]
. Set
\Upsilon:=C([0,T],L^{2}([0,1])){\times}C([0,T]{\times}[0,1],\mathbb{R})
. By Lemma 5.2 and Lemma 5.3, the family of laws
\mathcal{L}(u_{n},W)
of the random vectors
(u_{n},W)
is tight in
\Upsilon
, where
W
(with a slight abuse of notations) is the Brownian sheet corresponding to the space-time noise. By Prokhorov’s theorem and Skorokhod’s representation theorem, there exist a new probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
, a sequence of of
\Upsilon
-valued random vectors
\{(\tilde{u}_{n},\widetilde{W}_{n})\}
and a
\Upsilon
-valued random vector
(\tilde{u},\widetilde{W})
such that
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{u}_{n},\widetilde{W}_{n})=\mathcal{L}(u_{n},W)
,
\mathcal{L}(\widetilde{W})=\mathcal{L}(W)
and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\|\tilde{u}_{n}-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}+\sup_{t\geq 0,x\in[0,1]}|\widetilde{W}_{n}(t,x)-\widetilde{W}(t,x)|\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

(5.9)

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
be the filtration satisfying the usual conditions and generated by

\displaystyle\{\tilde{u}(s),\widetilde{W}(s,x):s\leq t,x\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Then
\widetilde{W}
is an
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\}
-Brownian sheet. And let
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{n}
be the filtration satisfying the usual conditions and generated by

\displaystyle\{\tilde{u}_{n}(s),\widetilde{W}_{n}(s,x):s\leq t,x\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Then
\widetilde{W}_{n}
is an
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{n}\}
-Brownian sheet.
From the equation satisfied by the random vector
(u_{n},W)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{u}_{n}(t,x)&=P_{t}\tilde{u}_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)b_{n}(\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y))dsdy\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y))\widetilde{W}_{n}(ds,dy),\end{aligned}

(5.10)

where for each
n\geq 1
,
\widetilde{W}_{n}(ds,dy)
is the space-time white noise corresponding to the Brownian sheet
\widetilde{W}_{n}
. We will let
n
tend to
\infty
in (
5.10
) to show that
(\tilde{u},\widetilde{W})
is a solution to equation (
1.1
). First, for any
t\leq T
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\|p_{t-s}(x,y)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}|b_{n}(\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y))|^{\frac{3}{2}}dyds\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\|p_{t-s}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{3}{2}}|\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y)|^{\frac{3}{2}}\log_{+}^{\frac{3}{2}}|\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y)|dyds\right]+C_{T,c_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2c_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\|p_{t-s}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{3}{2}}|\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y)|^{2}dyds\right]+C_{T,c_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T,c_{1}}\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\leq T}\|\tilde{u}_{n}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+C_{T,c_{2}}<\infty,

where we have used the inequality

\displaystyle\log_{+}|u|\leq 2|u|^{\frac{1}{3}},\quad\forall\ u\in\mathbb{R}.

So the sequence

\{\|p_{t-s}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}|b_{n}(\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y))|\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

is uniformly integrable on
\Omega\times[0,t]\times[0,1]
. Hence by (
5.1
), (
5.9
) and Vitali’s convergence theorem, we have for any
t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)b_{n}(\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y))dyds-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)b(\tilde{u}(s,y))dyds\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\|p_{t-s}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}|b_{n}(\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y))-b(\tilde{u}(s,y))|dyds\right]\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

To prove the convergence of the stochastic integral term in (
5.10
), we will show that
f_{n}(s,y):=p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y))
,
f(s,y):=p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(\tilde{u}(s,y))
satisfy the conditions in Theorem 7.1 in the appendix. First it is easy to see that
f_{n}
,
f
satisfy (
7.1
) and (
7.2
) respectively. And for any
1<\eta<\min\left\{\frac{1}{\theta},\frac{3}{2}\right\}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y))|^{2\eta}dxdyds\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{n}2^{2\eta-1}d_{1}^{2\eta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|p_{t-s}(x,y)|^{2\eta}|\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y)|^{2\theta\eta}dxdyds\right]

\displaystyle+2^{2\eta-1}d_{2}^{2\eta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|p_{t-s}(x,y)|^{2\eta}dxdyds\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{n}C_{T,d_{1},\eta}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}(|\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y)|^{2}+1)dy\right]+C_{T,d_{2},\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{n}C_{T,d_{1},\eta}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\leq T}\|\tilde{u}_{n}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+C_{T,d_{1},d_{2},\eta}<\infty,

where we have used (H3) and the following fact,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)^{2\eta}dxds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-(\eta-\frac{1}{2})}ds<\infty.

So the sequence
\{|p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y))|^{2}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly integrable on
\Omega\times[0,t]\times[0,1]\times[0,1]
. Hence by the continuity of
\sigma
and Vitali’s convergence theorem, we have for any
t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\|p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y))-p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(\tilde{u}(s,y))\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}dyds\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)^{2}|\sigma(\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y))-\sigma(\tilde{u}(s,y))|^{2}dxdyds\right]\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

This implies that (
7.4
) holds. So we can apply Theorem 7.1 to conclude that for any
t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(\tilde{u}_{n}(s,y))\widetilde{W}_{n}(ds,dy)\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\sigma(\tilde{u}(s,y))\widetilde{W}(ds,dy)

in
L^{2}([0,1])
as
n\to\infty
. Taking
n\to\infty
in (
5.10
), we see that for any
t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\tilde{u}(t)=P_{t}\tilde{u}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(\cdot,y)b(\tilde{u}(s,y))dsdy+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(\cdot,y)\sigma(\tilde{u}(s,y))\widetilde{W}(ds,dy).

And by (
5.9
) we have
\tilde{u}\in C([0,T],L^{2}([0,1]))
. So
\tilde{u}
is a probabilistically weak solution of (
1.1
).
∎

6
Pathwise uniqueness

In this section, we prove the pathwise uniqueness of solutions to (
1.1
) and hence obtain a unique probabilistically strong solution.

Proof of Theorem 2.5.

Let
u
,
v
be two solutions of equation (
1.1
). Then we have

\displaystyle u(t,x)-v(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)(b(u(s,y))-b(v(s,y)))dyds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)(\sigma(u(s,y))-\sigma(v(s,y)))W(ds,dy).

Set
z(t,x):=u(t,x)-v(t,x)
. Then

\displaystyle\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)|b(u(s,y))-b(v(s,y))|dyds\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)(\sigma(u(s,y))-\sigma(v(s,y)))W(ds,dy)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle:=I(t)+J(t).

By (H2),

\displaystyle I(t)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|\log_{+}\frac{1}{|u(s,y-v(s,y))|}dyds\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\|c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\log_{+}(|u(s,y)|\vee|v(s,y)|)|u(s,y)-v(s,y))|dyds\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\|c_{3}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|dyds\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle:=I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t)+I_{3}(t).

We first estimate
I_{3}(t)
as follows. By the contractivity of the heat semigroup
P_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{3}(t)

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|P_{t-s}(|u(s)-v(s)|)\right\|_{L^{2}}ds\leq c_{3}\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds.

(6.1)

Next, we consider
I_{2}(t)
. By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\log_{+}(|u(s,y)|\vee|v(s,y)|)|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|dy\right]^{2}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)|z(s,y)|^{2}dy\right]\left[\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\log_{+}^{2}(|u(s,y)|\vee|v(s,y)|)dy\right]dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}\left\{\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\log_{+}^{2}(|u(s,y)|\vee|v(s,y)|)dy\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds.

According to (
4
), we see that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)\log_{+}^{2}(|u(s,y)|\vee|v(s,y)|)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{\log_{+}C+\frac{1}{4}\log_{+}\left(\frac{1}{t-s}\right)+\log_{+}(\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}+\|v(s)\|_{L^{2}})\right\}^{2}.

Let
\tau_{M}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq M\}\land\inf\{t\geq 0:\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq M\}
. For
t<\tau_{M}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{2}(t)&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds+C\log_{+}(M)\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+\frac{c_{2}}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}\log_{+}\left(\frac{1}{t-s}\right)ds.\end{aligned}

(6.2)

Next, for any
\delta\in(0,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}e^{-1})
, we let
\tau^{\delta}=\inf\{t>0:\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\delta\}
. We now estimate
I_{1}(t)
for
t\leq\tau^{\delta}
,

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=c_{1}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|\log_{+}\left(\frac{1}{|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|}\right)dyds\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|\log_{+}\left(\frac{1}{|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|}\right)dy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}ds

\displaystyle=c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|\log_{+}\left(\frac{1}{|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|}\right)dy\right]^{2}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|^{2}\log_{+}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|}\right)dydx\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|^{2}\log_{+}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|}\right)dy\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds.

Let
f(z)=z(\log\frac{1}{z})^{2},
for
z\in(0,1]
.
Then
f^{\prime}(z)=(2+\log z)\log z>0
when
z\in(0,e^{-2})
, so
f(z)
increases for
z\in(0,e^{-2})
.
We know
f^{\prime\prime}(z)=\frac{2}{z}(1+\log z)<0
when
z<e^{-1}
. So
f
is concave in
(0,e^{-1})
. Moreover,

\displaystyle|f(z)|\leq 4|z|,\quad\text{for}\ z\in[e^{-2},1].

(6.3)

Next, we fix
s>0
and set
\Gamma:=\{y\in[0,1]:|z(s,y)|^{2}<e^{-2}\}
. In view of
\log_{+}\frac{1}{z}=0
for
z>1
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{1}(t)&\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|^{2}\log_{+}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{|u(s,y)-v(s,y)|}\right)dy\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds\\
&\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\int_{\{y\in[0,1]:e^{-2}\leq|z(s,y)|^{2}<1\}}f(z^{2}(s,y))dy+\int_{\Gamma}f(z^{2}(s,y))dy\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds.\end{aligned}

(6.4)

Then by the Chebyshev inequality, we know that the Lebesgue measure
|\Gamma|
satisfies

\displaystyle|\Gamma|\geq 1-e^{2}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This implies that
\forall\ t\leq\tau^{\delta}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{1-e^{2}\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{-2}-e^{2}}\leq e^{-2}.

By the Jensen inequality, (
6.3
) and the fact that
f
is concave and increasing in
(0,e^{-2})
, (
6.4
) becomes

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\int_{\{y\in[0,1]:\frac{2}{e}\leq|z(s,y)|^{2}<1\}}4|z(s,y)|^{2}dy+|\Gamma|\int_{\Gamma}f(z^{2}(s,y))\frac{dy}{|\Gamma|}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{4\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\Gamma|f(\int_{\Gamma}z^{2}(s,y)\frac{dy}{|\Gamma|})\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{4\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\Gamma|f(\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds+\frac{c_{1}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{|\Gamma|}\frac{\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}}{\sqrt{|\Gamma|}}\log_{+}\left(\frac{|\Gamma|}{\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds+c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}\log_{+}{\frac{1}{\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}}}ds.

(6.5)

Combining (
6.1
), (
6.2
) and (
6
) together yields that for any
t\leq\tau^{\delta}_{M}:=\tau_{M}\wedge\tau^{\delta}
,

\displaystyle I(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds+c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(C_{M}+\frac{1}{4}\log_{+}\left(\frac{1}{t-s}\right)\right)\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle+c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds+c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}\log_{+}\frac{1}{\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}}ds.

Hence there exists a constant
c>0
such that for
t\leq\tau^{\delta}_{M}
,

\displaystyle\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{s\leq t}J(s)+C_{M}\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}\log_{+}\frac{1}{\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}}ds.

Then, by Lemma 3.2, we have

\displaystyle\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{T}\sup_{s\leq t}J(s)+C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}\log_{+}\frac{1}{\|z(s)\|_{L^{2}}}ds.

This implies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq r\land\tau_{M}^{\delta}}\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,T}\sup_{t\leq r\land\tau_{M}^{\delta}}J(t)+C_{M,T}\int_{0}^{r}\sup_{\rho\leq s\land\tau_{M}^{\delta}}\left(\|z(\rho)\|_{L^{2}}\log_{+}\frac{1}{\|z(\rho)\|_{L^{2}}}\right)ds.

Denote

\displaystyle F(r)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq r\land\tau_{M}^{\delta}}\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right].

Let
h(x)=x\log_{+}\frac{1}{x}
for
x\in(0,e^{-1})
. Then
h^{\prime}(x)=\log\frac{1}{x}-1\geq 0
, this implies that
h(x)
increases in
(0,e^{-1})
. And
h^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\frac{1}{x}\leq 0
, so
h(x)
is concave in
(0,e^{-1})
.
Then we have

\displaystyle F(r)\leq C_{M,T}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq r\land\tau_{M}^{\delta}}J(t)\right]+C_{M,T}\int_{0}^{r}F(s)\log_{+}\frac{1}{F(s)}ds.

According to Lemma 4.2, for any
\ \epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq r\land\tau_{M}^{\delta}}J(t)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq r\land\tau_{M}^{\delta}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}p_{t-s}(x,y)[\sigma(u(s,y))-\sigma(v(s,y))]W(ds,dy)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon F(r)+C_{\epsilon,T}\int_{0}^{r}F(s)ds.

So we get that

\displaystyle F(r)\leq\epsilon F(r)+C_{\epsilon,M,T}\int_{0}^{r}F(s)ds+C_{M,T}\int_{0}^{r}F(s)\log_{+}\frac{1}{F(s)}ds.

According to Theorem 3.2, we conclude that
F\equiv 0
on
[0,T]
. This implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T\land\tau_{M}^{\delta}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right]=0.

Let
M\to\infty
, then
\tau_{M}\to\infty
. By Fatou’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T\land\tau^{\delta}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right]=0.

So
\sup_{t\leq T\land\tau^{\delta}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0
,
\mathbb{P}-a.s
. This implies
\tau^{\delta}\geq T

a.s.
and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0.

The proof of pathwise uniqueness is completed.
∎

Appendix

In this section, we will prove a result on the convergence of stochastic integral with respect to space-time white noise, which will be used in the proof of existence of probabilistically weak solutions. The result is similar to Lemma 4.3 in
[
2
]
and Lemma 2.1 in
[
15
]
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume that
H
is a separable Hilbert space. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F,\mathbb{P}})
be a probability space with a sequence of filtrations
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{n}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfying the usual conditions. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
W_{n}
be a Brownian sheet with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{n}
and let
W
be a Brownian sheet with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}
. Assume that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{n}
is
H
-valued
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{n}
-adapted measurable processes such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sup_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\|f_{n}(s,y)\|_{H}^{2}dyds<\infty,\quad a.s.\end{aligned}

(7.1)

And
f
is a
H
-valued
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted measurable process such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\|f(s,y)\|_{H}^{2}dyds<\infty,\quad a.s.\end{aligned}

(7.2)

If

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T,x\in[0,1]}|W_{n}(t,x)-W(t,x)|\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0,\quad

(7.3)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\|f_{n}(s,y)-f(s,y)\|_{H}^{2}dyds\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0.

(7.4)

Then we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(s,y)W_{n}(ds,dy)-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}f(s,y)W(ds,dy)\right\|_{H}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0,

(7.5)

where
W_{n}(ds,dy)
and
W(ds,dy)
are the space-time white noise corresponding to the Brownian sheet
W_{n}
and
W
respectively.

Proof.

Let
e_{k}(x)=\sqrt{2}\sin(k\pi x)
, which constitutes a set of orthonormal basis of
L^{2}([0,1])
. Then for each
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle W_{n}^{k}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}e_{k}(y)W_{n}(ds,dy)=-\int_{0}^{1}W_{n}(t,x)e^{\prime}_{k}(x)dx

is a one-dimensional standard Brownian motion. And similarly,

\displaystyle W^{k}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}e_{k}(y)W(ds,dy)=-\int_{0}^{1}W(t,x)e^{\prime}_{k}(x)dx

is also a one-dimensional standard Brownian motion for each
k\geq 1
. From (
7.3
), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}|W^{k}_{n}(t)-W^{k}(t)|\leq C\sup_{t\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}|W_{n}(t,x)-W(t,x)|dx\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0.

(7.6)

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(s,y)W_{n}(ds,dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(s,y)e_{k}(y)dy\right)dW_{n}^{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}F_{n}(s)dW_{n}(s),

where
W_{n}(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}W_{n}^{k}(s)e_{k}
is the corresponding cylindrical Wiener process on
L^{2}([0,1])
, and
F_{n}(s)
is an Hilbert-Schmidt operator from
L^{2}([0,1])
to
H
and defined by

\displaystyle F_{n}(s)(g):=\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(s,y)g(y)dy,\quad\forall\ g\in L^{2}([0,1]).

Let
\{h_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis of
H
and define
h_{j}\otimes e_{k}:=h_{j}\langle e_{k},\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}
for any
k,j\geq 1
, then
h_{j}\otimes e_{k}
is an orthonormal basis of
HS(L^{2},H)
(see Proposition B.0.7 in
[
25
]
). Then the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
F_{n}(s)
can be computed as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|F_{n}(s)\|_{HS(L^{2},H)}^{2}&=\sum_{k,j=1}^{\infty}\langle F_{n}(s),h_{j}\otimes e_{k}\rangle^{2}_{HS(L^{2},H)}\\
&=\sum_{k,j=1}^{\infty}\langle h_{j},F_{n}(s)(e_{k})\rangle_{H}^{2}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\langle f_{n}(s,y),h_{j}\rangle_{H}e_{k}(y)dy\right)^{2}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\langle f_{n}(s,y),h_{j}\rangle_{H}^{2}dy\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\|f_{n}(s,y)\|_{H}^{2}dy,\end{aligned}

(7.7)

Similarly, we can define
F(s)
and
W(s)
and have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}f(s,y)W(ds,dy)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{1}f(s,y)e_{k}(y)dy\right)dW^{k}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}F(s)dW(s).

By (
7.4
) and (
7.7
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|F_{n}(s)-F(s)\|_{HS(L^{2},H)}^{2}ds=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\|f_{n}(s,y)-f(s,y)\|_{H}^{2}dyds\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0.

(7.8)

As the conditions of Lemma 4.3 in
[
2
]
are fulfilled by (
7.6
) and (
7.8
), it follows from Lemma 4.3 in
[
2
]
that (
7.5
) holds.
∎

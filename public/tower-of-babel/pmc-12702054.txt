Protocol for quantifying hepatitis B virus transcript abundance and genomic segment distribution from single-cell RNA-seq
Authors: Lina Liu, Liyu Chen, Lingyun Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryHepatitis B virus (HBV) drives liver disease progression. Here, we present a protocol for sequencing single cells from HBV-infected liver tissue, producing two complementary outputs: quantitative per-cell HBV transcript abundance and a genome-wide map of read distribution. We describe steps for tissue dissociation and purification, single-cell RNA sequencing (RNA-seq), library construction, sequencing, and data processing. We then detail procedures for HBV expression and genome distribution analysis. This protocol enables detailed analysis of viral expression patterns and HBV-host interactions at single-cell resolution.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Zhou et al.1Subject areas: Bioinformatics, Sequence analysis, Cell biology, Single cell, Health sciences, Molecular biology, Gene expression

Before you begin

Innovation

We present a single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) protocol optimized for analyzing hepatitis B virus (HBV) expression and genome-wide fragment distribution. Unlike traditional approaches that focus only on host transcriptomes or bulk viral RNA, this workflow enables precise detection and quantification of HBV transcripts at single-cell resolution. Raw scRNA-seq reads are first aligned to a customized reference combining human and HBV transcriptomes to accurately distinguish host from viral sequences. Human-derived reads are then removed, and the remaining viral reads are mapped to the HBV genome using a transcriptome-to-genome coordinate conversion strategy. This generates a base-resolution depth profile, revealing both viral expression levels and fragmentation patterns across the genome. The workflow produces two key outputs: (1) quantitative, cell-resolved measurements of HBV transcription and (2) a genome-wide visualization of read distribution. By simultaneously characterizing both virus and host, this protocol addresses a critical gap in current HBV research and provides a powerful tool for dissecting virus–host interactions.

The central component of this workflow is the HBV_parsing.py script, which automates read parsing and quantification. We further validated its reliability using publicly available data from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO: SRP136347). Analysis of four spatially distinct tumor samples (Pt14a–d) from a single patient revealed consistent HBV integration patterns across all regions, demonstrating the robustness and reproducibility of the workflow and suggesting that HBV exhibits individualized integration patterns across patients.

Institutional permissions

All procedures with human material complied with applicable ethical regulations. Informed consent was obtained from every participant prior to tissue collection. Samples were derived solely from clinically indicated surgeries; no research-specific interventions were performed. Approval was granted by the institutional review board (IRB), and handling followed national and institutional guidelines for human subject research.

Identification of a candidate patient

Fresh hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) tissues were obtained from 10 patients who underwent surgical resection at West China Hospital between July and December 2020. None had received anti-tumor therapy prior to surgery. Tissue was transported on ice and processed immediately upon arrival.

BSL2 workspace

Work with human tissues was conducted in BSL-2 facilities. Areas dedicated to human tissue processing were clearly labeled with biohazard signage, and all procedures followed institutional biosafety policies.

Timing: 2–3 h (excluding transport)

3.Remove red blood cells (Miltenyi 130-094-183).

4.Assess total cell count and viability on a Countstar Rigel S2 using AO/PI staining.

5.If needed, clear debris (Miltenyi 130-109-398) and deplete dead cells (Miltenyi 130-090-101).

6.Wash cells twice in RPMI 1640 and resuspend at 1×106 cells/mL in 1× PBS with 0.04% BSA.

CRITICAL: Minimize cold ischemia time. Aim to initiate processing within 1 hour of resection to preserve viability and RNA integrity.

Timing: ∼48 h (sequencing run may extend ≥1 day)

7.Prepare single-cell suspensions and load onto a Chromium Next GEM Chip G with reagents from the v.3.1 3′ kit (10× Genomics).

Expected outcomes

The pipeline produces circular plots summarizing HBV read coverage across the viral genome. Each concentric ring represents one sample, and radial black bars indicate the positions of mapped HBV reads, enabling intuitive comparison of expression levels and segment representation among samples (Figure 1). When applied to external datasets, such as SRP136347,7 the workflow reproduced consistent integration patterns, demonstrating its robustness and broad applicability (Figure 2).

Circular display of HBV transcript coverage across samples

Radial black bars denote HBV-mapped reads positioned along the viral genome, reflecting transcript abundance at those loci. The outermost colored arcs indicate annotated HBV genes (X = blue; P = red; C = purple; S = green). Concentric rings, arranged from outer to inner, represent individual tumor samples (HCC1–3, HCC5–10). Samples without detectable HBV transcripts (HCC4; PT13A–C) are not shown.

Circular plots showing HBV read coverage across the viral genome for four spatially distinct tumor samples (Pt14a–d) from a single HCC patient. From outer to inner rings, the samples are arranged as Pt14a, Pt14b, Pt14c, and Pt14d. Radial black bars indicate the positions of HBV-mapped reads along the genome, while the outermost colored arcs mark annotated HBV genes (X = blue; P = red; C = purple; S = green). Consistent integration patterns were observed across all four samples, demonstrating the reproducibility of the workflow and suggesting that HBV exhibits individualized integration patterns within each patient.

In addition to circular coverage visualization, the workflow generates tabular summaries and simple bar charts of per-cell or per-sample HBV transcript abundance. Comprehensive lists of HBV integration breakpoints are provided in Data S1 and S2.

Limitations

Standard 3′ end scRNA-seq captures short transcript fragments and is not designed to robustly detect integration breakpoints or full-length integration sequences, requiring additional targeted methods for comprehensive integration mapping. Moreover, single-cell RNA-seq data are inherently affected by low signal-to-noise ratios and cell-to-cell variability in library preparation, which may influence the sensitivity and accuracy of HBV transcript quantification and read alignment.

Lyapunov spectrum via boundary theory I - framework
Uri Bader, Alex Furman

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the Lyapunov spectrum for measurable cocycles over an ergodic pmp system taking values in semi-simple real Lie groups. We prove simplicity of the Lyapunov spectrum and its continuity under certain perturbations for a class systems that includes many familiar examples. Our framework uses some soft qualitative assumptions, and does not rely on symbolic dynamics. We use ideas from boundary theory that appear in the study of super-rigidity to deduce our results. This gives a new perspective even on the most studied case of random matrix products. The current paper introduces the general framework and contains the proofs of the main results and some basic examples. In a follow up paper we discuss further examples.

1.
Introduction

Let
(X,\mathcal{X},m)
be a standard probability space,
T:X\to X

an ergodic measure preserving transformation,
and
F:X\to\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})

a measurable map satisfying the integrability condition

\int_{X}\log^{+}\|F(x)\|\,{\rm d}m(x)<+\infty,\qquad\int_{X}\log^{+}\|F(x)^{-1}\|\,{\rm d}m(x)<+\infty,

where
\log^{+}(t)=\max(\log(t),0)

and
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator norm.
Let
F_{n}:X\to\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
be the cocycle generated by
F
, namely

F_{n}(x)=F(T^{n-1}x)\cdots F(Tx)F(x),

and define the corresponding
Lyapunov exponents

\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{d}
by

\begin{split}&\lambda_{1}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\ \frac{1}{n}\int_{X}\log\|F_{n}(x)\|\,{\rm d}m(x),\\
&\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{k}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\ \frac{1}{n}\int_{X}\log\|\wedge^{k}F_{n}(x)\|\,{\rm d}m(x)\qquad(1\leq k\leq d).\end{split}

These Lyapunov exponents can be recorded as an element
\Lambda_{F}
in the positive Weyl chamber
\mathfrak{a}^{+}

of the Cartan sub-algebra
\mathfrak{a}
, consisting of diagonal elements
in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{d}
of the reductive group
G=\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
:

\Lambda_{F}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d})\ \in\mathfrak{a}^{+}.

Oseledts’ Multiplicative Ergodic Theorem describes asymptotic behavior of
F_{n}(x)

and its action on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
in terms of
\exp(n\Lambda_{F}+o_{x}(n))
.

More generally, given a reductive real Lie group
G
, one considers the asymptotic
behavior of the cocycle
\{F_{n}(-)\}
generated by a measurable map
F:X\to G
.
Since the effect of the center can be easily analyzed separately, we will hereafter focus
on the case were

G
is a
connected semisimple real Lie group with a finite center
.
Fix a real representation with finite kernel
\tau:G\to\operatorname{GL}(V)

and an inner product on
V
to obtain an operator norm,
which we denote
\|\cdot\|
, on
\operatorname{GL}(V)
.
We define a (pseudo-)length function on
G
by

|\cdot|:G\to[0,\infty),\quad|g|=\log(\max\{\|\tau(g)\|,\|\tau(g^{-1})\|\}).

The integrability condition can now be stated as:

(1.1)

\int_{X}|F(x)|\,{\rm d}m(x)<+\infty.

This condition is independent of the choices made for the representation

\tau
and the inner product on
V
,
see Lemma
5.13
below.

Fix a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}<\mathfrak{g}
in the Lie algebra of
G
,
a Weyl chamber
\mathfrak{a}^{+}\subset\mathfrak{a}
,
and a maximal compact subgroup
K<G
. We let

\kappa:G\to\mathfrak{a}^{+}
be the corresponding Cartan projection; so
g\in K\exp(\kappa(g))K
for every
g\in G
.
The theorem of Oseledets describes the
asymptotic behavior of
F_{n}
; in particular (see Corollary
5.18
) it asserts
an almost everywhere and an
L^{1}
-convergence of the normalized Cartan projections

(1.2)

\Lambda_{F}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\kappa(F_{n}(x))\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}.

The element
\Lambda_{F}\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
is said to be the
Lyapunov spectrum

associated with
F
.
If
\Lambda_{F}=0
we say that the Lyapunov spectrum is
trivial
;
this case corresponds
to sub-linear growth:
|F_{n}(x)|=o_{x}(n)
.
Otherwise, if
\Lambda_{F}\neq 0
, we say that the Lyapunov spectrum is
non-trivial
or that
F
has a
positive drift
.
We are particularly interested in the situations where the spectrum is not merely non-trivial, but is
simple in the following sense.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
F
has a
simple Lyapunov spectrum
if
\Lambda_{F}
given by
(
1.2
) is in the interior
\mathfrak{a}^{++}

of the positive Weyl chamber
\mathfrak{a}^{+}\subset\mathfrak{a}
.

If
G
is a simple real Lie group of rank one then
\dim\mathfrak{a}=1
, and the simplicity of the spectrum is equivalent to positivity of the drift.
However, if
G
is of higher rank the simplicity of the spectrum is a much more subtle condition than positivity of the the drift.
For example:

•

In case of
G=\operatorname{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
we have
\Lambda_{F}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d})

where
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{d}
and
\sum\lambda_{i}=0
.
The condition
\Lambda_{F}\neq 0
is equivalent to
\lambda_{1}>0
,
while simplicity of
\Lambda_{F}
refers to the strict inequalities
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\dots>\lambda_{d}
.

•

In case of
G=\operatorname{Sp}_{2d}({\mathbb{R}})
realized as a subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2d}({\mathbb{R}})
,
the Lyapunov spectrum has the form

\Lambda_{F}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d},-\lambda_{d},\dots,-\lambda_{1})

with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{d}\geq 0
.
The condition
\Lambda_{F}\neq 0
is still equivalent to
\lambda_{1}>0
,
while simplicity of
\Lambda_{F}
refers to the strict inequalities

\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\dots>\lambda_{d}>0
.

The purpose of this paper is to describe a rather general
setting in which the Lyapunov spectrum can
be proved to be simple, and to depend continuously on certain deformations of
F
.
Our main theorem is Theorem
A
.
In order to state it, we need to introduce our general framework
which includes an auxiliary group
\Gamma
.

1.1.
Statement of the main results

Definition 1.2
.

By a
Group Random Element Generator
, or
Greg
for short,
we mean a tuple
(X,\mathcal{X},m,T,w,\Gamma)
,
where
(X,\mathcal{X},m)
is a standard probability space,

T:X\to X
is an invertible measure preserving transformation,

\Gamma
is a locally compact second countable group
and
w:X\to\Gamma
is a measurable map.

A
\mu
-random sequence
\{w(T^{n}x)\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
can be viewed as a
(bi-infinite) sequence of random, not necessarily independent,

\Gamma
-valued elements.
Taking consecutive products of these sequences,
w_{n}(x)=w(T^{n-1}x)\cdots w(Tx)w(x)
,
we obtain a cocycle
w:{\mathbb{Z}}\times X\to\Gamma
defined by

w_{1}(x)=w(x),\qquad w_{n+k}(x)=w_{k}(T^{n}x)w_{n}(x)\qquad(n,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

(see Equation (
2.4
)).
Consider the map

j:X\times\Gamma\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,\Gamma^{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad j(x,g)=(w_{n}(x)g^{-1})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}

and let
\widetilde{m}
denote the measure on
\Gamma^{\mathbb{Z}}
obtained
by the push-forward of
m\times m_{\Gamma}
,

\widetilde{m}=j_{*}(m\times m_{\Gamma})
, where
m_{\Gamma}
is
a Haar measure on
\Gamma
. It will be more convenient to work
with a probability measure
\widetilde{m}^{1}=j_{*}(m\times m_{\Gamma}^{1})

where
m_{\Gamma}^{1}
is some choice of a probability measure
in the Haar measure class on
\Gamma
.
The measure
\widetilde{m}^{1}
provides a notion of a random sequence of group elements in
\Gamma
.
We think of
j
as an
(X,m,T)
-
driven random walk

with
j(x,g)
passing through
g^{-1}
at time
n=0
.
We denote by
\widetilde{m}^{1}_{+}
and
\widetilde{m}^{1}_{-}

the marginal distributions of
\widetilde{m}^{1}
on the space of futures
\Gamma^{{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}

and the space of pasts
\Gamma^{{\mathbb{Z}}_{-}}
, respectively.
Taking the ergodic components for the natural non-singular actions
of the semi-groups
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{-}
on these spaces
we obtain the spaces
(E_{+},\eta_{+})
and
(E_{-},\eta_{-})

of
ideal futures
and
ideal pasts
as measurable quotients

\beta_{+}:(\Gamma^{\mathbb{Z}},\widetilde{m})\ \to\ (E_{+},\eta_{+}),\qquad\beta_{-}:(\Gamma^{\mathbb{Z}},\widetilde{m})\ \to\ (E_{-},\eta_{-}).

Definition 1.3
.

The Greg system
(X,\mathcal{X},m,T,\Gamma,w)
is said to
satisfy the
asymptotic past and future independence condition

if on the product space
E_{+}\times E_{-}
,
the pushed measure
(\beta_{+}\times\beta_{-})_{*}\widetilde{m}^{1}
is equivalent
to the product measure
\eta_{+}\times\eta_{-}
.
In this case we will say that the Greg satisfies the
Apafi condition
,
or that it is an
Apafic Greg
.

Hereafter we will assume that
(X,\mathcal{X},m,T,w,\Gamma)
is an Apafic Greg.
This assumption has several consequences, including ergodicity of the
system
(X,\mathcal{X},m,T)
.

Consider a representation
\rho:\Gamma\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,G
into a connected
semi-simple real Lie group
G
with finite center, and form the
G
-valued
measurable map

F=\rho\circ w\ :X\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{w}}}\,\Gamma\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\rho}}}\,G.

Assuming
F=\rho\circ w
satisfies integrability condition (
1.1
),
we can associate the Lyapunov spectrum
\Lambda_{\rho\circ w}\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}

to
\rho\in\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
.

Connected semi-simple real Lie group
G
with finite center can be equipped
with Zariski topology, when viewed as real points of a real algebraic group.
A representation
\rho\in\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
is called
Zariski dense

if
\rho(\Gamma)
is Zariski dense in
G
,
and it is called
integrable
if
F=\rho\circ w
satisfies (
1.1
).
We endow the space
\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
with the compact-open topology.
A subset
\Sigma\subset\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
is said to be
uniformly integrable

if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
\rho\in\Sigma
and measurable
E\subset X
:

m(E)<\delta\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\int_{E}|\rho\circ w(x)|\,{\rm d}m(x)<\epsilon.

For example, for any
h\in L^{1}(X,\mathcal{X},m)
the set of representations

(1.3)

\Sigma_{h}=\left\{\left.{\rho\in\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,G)}\ \right|\ {|\rho\circ w(x)|\leq h(x)}\right\}

is uniformly integrable.

Theorem A
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X},m,T,\Gamma,w)
be an Apafic Greg as in
Definitions
1.2
and
1.3
.
Let
G
be a connected semisimple real Lie group with a finite center.
Then

(1)

For every integrable Zariski dense representation
\rho:\Gamma\to G
,
the map
F=\rho\circ w:X\to G
has a simple spectrum,
i.e.
\Lambda_{F}\in\mathfrak{a}^{++}
.

(2)

For every uniformly integrable subset
\Sigma\subset\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
,
the Lyapunov spectrum map
\Lambda:\Sigma\to\mathfrak{a}^{+}
is continuous
at every Zariski dense representation
\rho\in\Sigma
.

(3)

For every integrable representation
\rho\in\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,G)

such that
\rho(\Gamma)
is not contained in a compact extension
of a solvable subgroup,
the map
F=\rho\circ w:X\to G
has positive drift:
\Lambda_{F}\neq 0
.

Random walks with i.i.d. increments

Geodesic flow in negative curvature

Let

Among the

Recall that there is a canonical cohomology class of measurable cocycles

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a negatively curved compact manifold,

\phi^{\mathbb{R}}
the geodesic flow,
\mathfrak{m}
a Gibbs measure on

Let

Let

be a semi-simple real Lie group and

\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,G

Then the
{\mathbb{R}}
-cocycle

,
has a simple Lyapunov spectrum

and the latter depends continuously

\rho

Here we used the

-flow version of the Oseledets theorem

-a.e. and

Since in this case the flow is mixing, the spectrum could also be defined

1.4.
Some Background and Context

The study of Lyapunov spectrum has a long history and has been most
successful in the context of random walks (products of i.i.d. elements)
on semi-simple real Lie groups.
The pioneering work of Furstenberg
[
21
]

gave, among other things, the condition for positivity of the drift.
The concept of stationary measure on the flag variety
G/P

played an important role in this analysis.
Guivarc’h–Raugi
[
24
,
25
]
developed a sufficient condition
for simplicity of the Lyapunov spectrum
using, what they called,
contracting property

for the closed
semi-group
generated by
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
.
Further work of Gol’dsheid–Margulis
[
23
]
showed that
the contraction property holds if
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
generates
a
Zariski dense subgroup
in
G
.
Hence, if
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
generates a Zariski dense subgroup in
G
,
and
\mu
is integrable, then the Lyapunov spectrum is simple.
In a different direction, LePage
[
30
]
studied the regularity of
the Lyapunov spectrum under perturbations of the law
\mu

(see also Furstenberg–Kifer
[
22
]
,
and the recent Avila–Eskin–Viana
[
3
]
).
For more background and references see
[
14
]
,
[
20
]
,

[
13
]
,
[
33
]
.

The breakthrough work of Avila–Viana
[
4
]
(see also
[
5
,
6
]
),
gave a sufficient condition for simplicity of the Lyapunov spectrum
for matrix cocycles over dynamical systems under certain conditions.
This criterion in particular allowed to prove the conjectured simplicity
of the Lyapunov spectrum for Kontsevich–Zorich cocycle (
[
5
]
).

Our proof follows a different approach and gives a different
framework while covering some of the same examples.
It should be pointed out that our approach passes through
some ideas of Boundary Theory, that were inspired (in part)
by the study of random walks.
While our approach allows to recover Furstenberg, Guivarc’h–Raugi,
Gol’dsheid–Margulis results for random walks with
Zariski-dense distribution, we follow a different path
while using similar objects and constructions
such as stationary measures.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Lebesgue spaces and non-singular actions

A topological space is said to be Polish if it admits a countable dense subspace and the topology is generated by a complete metric.
A Borel space (or a measurable space) is a set endowed with a
\sigma
-algebra.
A standard Borel space is a Borel space which is isomorphic as such to a Polish space.
A Lebesgue space is a standard Borel space which is endowed with a measure class.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X},m)
be a Lebesgue space, and
(V,\mathcal{V})
be a standard Borel space.
We use the term
map
for an equivalence class, up to null sets,
of measurable functions
f:X\to V
, and
denote by
\operatorname{Map}(X,Y)
the space of such maps:

\operatorname{Map}(X,V)=\left\{\left.{f:X\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,V}\ \right|\ {\forall\ E\in\mathcal{V},\ f^{-1}(E)\in\mathcal{X}}\right\}/\sim

where
f_{1}\sim f_{2}
if
m(\left\{\left.{x\in X}\ \right|\ {f_{1}(x)\neq f_{2}(x)}\right\})=0
.

If
V
is a Polish space, then
\operatorname{Map}(X,V)
is endowed with a natural Polish structure as well.
For Lebesgue spaces
X,Y
we have, by Fubini argument, the standard identification of Polish spaces

(2.1)

\operatorname{Map}(X\times Y,V)\simeq\operatorname{Map}\left(X,\operatorname{Map}(Y,V)\right).

Let
p:(X,\mathcal{X},m)\to(Y,\mathcal{Y},n)
be a map of probability spaces with
p_{*}m=n
.
The
disintegration
of
m
with respect to
n
, is the unique map

Y\to\operatorname{Prob}(X,\mathcal{X})
,
y\mapsto\mu_{y}
,
so that

m=\int_{Y}\mu_{y}\,{\rm d}n(y)\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\forall^{\textrm{ae}}\,y\in Y:\quad\mu_{y}\left(p^{-1}(\{y\})\right)=1.

Let
\Gamma
be a locally compact secondly countable group and
(X,\mathcal{X},m)
be a Lebesgue space.
A
non-singular action

\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mathcal{X},m)
is a measurable map
a:\Gamma\times X\to X
satisfying a.e

a(1,x)=x
and
a(\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2},x)=a(\gamma_{1},a(\gamma_{2},x))
and such that for every
\gamma\in\Gamma

the measures
\gamma_{*}m
and
m
are equivalent, i.e. have the same
\sigma
-ideal of null sets.
A non-singular action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mathcal{X},m)
is
ergodic
if
E\in\mathcal{X}
with
m(\gamma E\triangle E)=0

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, satisfies
m(E)=0
or
m(X\setminus E)=0
.

An action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mathcal{X},m)
is
measure-preserving
if
\gamma_{*}m=m
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
We say that the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mathcal{X},m)
is
probability measure preserving

(abbreviated
p.m.p
) if in addition
m(X)=1
.

A p.m.p action of
\mathbf{Z}
is given by an invertible measure preserving map
T
of a probability space
(X,\mathcal{X},m)
,
with
T
corresponding to the generator
1\in\mathbf{Z}
.
We shall sometimes denote the system
(X,\mathcal{X},m,T)
by
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathcal{X},m,T)
.
We shall also consider not necessarily invertible measure-preserving transformation
S
of a probability space

(Y,\mathcal{Y},n)
, meaning
n(S^{-1}E)=n(E)
for all
E\in\mathcal{Y}
.
It can be viewed as the action of the semi-group
\mathbf{N}
.

Let
T
be a probability measure-preserving transformation of a Lebesgue space
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathcal{X},m)

and
S
of
\mathbf{Y}=(Y,\mathcal{Y},n)
.
An
equivariant quotient

p:\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Y}
, is a measurable map
p:X\to Y
with
p_{*}m=n
and
p\circ T=S\circ p
a.e.
In this case the disintegration
y\mapsto\mu_{y}
is equivariant, i.e.

\forall^{\textrm{ae}}y\in Y:\qquad\mu_{Sy}=T_{*}\mu_{y}.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{X}
be an
m
-complete sub-
\sigma

2.4.
Compact convex spaces

Hereafter we use the term
compact convex space
as a shorthand for non-empty, convex, compact space
that has enough continuous affine functionals to separate its points.
Such a space can be viewed as a non-empty closed convex subset
Q\subset[0,1]^{A}
,
where
A
is a subset of the space
Q^{*}
of affine functionals

Q^{*}:=\left\{\left.{\lambda:Q\to[0,1]}\ \right|\ {\lambda\ \textrm{is\ continuous\ and\ affine}}\right\}

and
A
separates points of
Q
.
The class of convex compact spaces is closed under products, and under taking closed convex subsets.
A version of Hahn-Banach theorem shows that continuous affine functionals on a closed convex subset can be extended to an ambient space,
and that disjoint closed convex subsets can be separated by continuous affine functionals.

Denote by
\operatorname{Aff}(Q)
the group of all continuous affine bijections
T:Q\to Q
.
It is a complete topological group with respect to the compact-open topology, i.e. the topology defined by

g\mapsto\langle\lambda,g.q\rangle
,
\lambda\in Q^{*}
,
q\in Q
.

In what follows we will focus on metrizable convex compact spaces, i.e. spaces which are isomorphic to non-empty closed convex subsets of
[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}
.
For such a space
Q
, the group
\operatorname{Aff}(Q)
is a Polish group, and a homomorphism

\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aff}(Q)
from a lcsc group
\Gamma
is continuous iff the action map

\Gamma\times Q\to Q
,
(\gamma,q)\mapsto\gamma.q
, is continuous.

Example 2.2
.

Let
V
be a separable Banach space, and
Q\subset V^{*}
be a non-empty closed convex
subset of a ball in
V^{*}
.
Then
Q
is a convex compact space with respect to the weak* topology.
In all of our examples

is separable, hence
Q
embeds in
[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}

and is therefore metrizable.
There is a continuous homomorphism of Polish groups

\left\{\left.{g\in\operatorname{GL}(V)}\ \right|\ {g(Q)=Q}\right\}\ \,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,\ \operatorname{Aff}(Q)

where
\operatorname{GL}(V)
denotes the group of all bounded invertible linear automorphisms
of the Banach space

,
equipped with the weak topology.
The following is a particular example of this general setting,
that we shall use below.
Let
M
be a compact metrizable space and

the space of all Borel probability

equipped with the weak* topology,

\mu_{n}\to\mu

Then every

Let

be a metrizable convex compact space. The

is defined using integration of affine functionals as follows: for

define

It follows from the definitions that the affine continuous map

If

.
One can denote the barycenter

\begin{split}(X,\mathcal{F},m|_{\mathcal{F}})&\to\operatorname{Prob}(X,\mathcal{X})\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\nu_{*}}}}\,\operatorname{Prob}(Q)\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\operatorname{bar}}}}\,Q,\\

In case

and

Proposition 2.15
.

The natural extension

is relatively metrically ergodic.

In particular,
X

Let us introduce some notations and preliminary lemmas before proving this proposition.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X},m)
be a standard probability space,
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{X}
a complete sub-
\sigma
-algebra.
It corresponds to a measurable quotient
p:(X,\mathcal{X},m)\to(Y,\mathcal{Y},n)
so that
\mathcal{F}=p^{-1}(\mathcal{Y})
,
and let
m=\int_{Y}\mu_{y}\,{\rm d}n(y)
be the disintegration of measures.
We shall denote by

the fiber product probability space

\begin{split}X\times_{Y}X&:=\left\{\left.{(x,x^{\prime})\in X\times X}\ \right|\ {p(x)=p(x^{\prime})}\right\},\\
m\times_{Y}m&:=\int_{Y}\mu_{y}\times\mu_{y}\,{\rm d}n(y).\end{split}

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X},m)
be a standard probability space,
\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\mathcal{F}_{2}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{X}

a sequence of complete
\sigma
-algebras with
\mathcal{F}_{n}\nearrow\mathcal{X}
,
Let
X\to\dots\to Y_{2}\to Y_{1}
be the corresponding quotients,
and let
F:X\to W
be a measurable map into a Polish metric space
(W,d)
with
\operatorname{diam}(W,d)\leq 1
.
Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\ \int_{X\times_{Y_{n}}X}d(F(x),F(x^{\prime}))\,{\rm d}(m\times_{Y_{n}}m)(x,x^{\prime})=0.

Proof.

Take an arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
.
Since
(W,d)
is separable, we can cover
W
by a countable union of
\epsilon
-balls

W=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}B(w_{i},\epsilon)
for some
\left\{\left.{w_{i}}\ \right|\ {i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\right\}
in
W
.
Let
W_{1}=B(w_{1},\epsilon)
and

W_{j+1}=B(w_{j+1},\epsilon)\setminus\ \bigcup_{i=1}^{j}B(w_{i},\epsilon)\qquad(j\in{\mathbb{N}}).

Then
W=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\infty}W_{i}
where
\operatorname{diam}(W_{i})\leq 2\epsilon
.
The sets
A_{i}=F^{-1}(W_{i})
form a countable
measurable partition of
X
, and so there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
so that

m(A_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup A_{k})>1-\epsilon.

Let
\delta=(\epsilon/2k)^{2}
.
Since
\mathcal{F}_{n}\nearrow\mathcal{X}
, there is
N
and
\mathcal{F}_{N}
-measurable sets
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}

so that
m(E_{i}\triangle A_{i})<\delta
.
Denoting
E_{0}=X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}E_{i}
we note that
m(E_{0})<\epsilon+k\delta<2\epsilon
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we denote
p_{n}:(X,\mathcal{X},m)\to(Y_{n},\mathcal{Y}_{n},m_{n})
the quotient of measure spaces
so that
\mathcal{F}_{n}=p_{n}^{-1}(\mathcal{Y}_{n})
, and let

m=\int_{Y_{n}}\mu_{n,y}\,{\rm d}m_{n}(y)

the corresponding disintegration. Fix any
n\geq N
. Since
\mathcal{F}_{N}\subset\mathcal{F}_{n}

the sets
E_{0},E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
are
\mathcal{F}_{n}
-measurable,
and we denote by
\bar{E}_{0},\bar{E}_{1},\dots,\bar{E}_{k}\in\mathcal{Y}_{n}

the corresponding sets in
Y_{n}
.
Note that for
y\in\bar{E}_{i}
, we have
\mu_{n,y}(X\setminus E_{i})=0
.

Define
\operatorname{Var}_{n}:Y_{n}\to[0,1]
by

\operatorname{Var}_{n}(y):=\int_{X}\int_{X}d(F(x),F(x^{\prime}))\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,y}(x)\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,y}(x^{\prime}).

Then we have

\begin{split}\int_{X\times_{Y_{n}}X}&d(F(x),F(x^{\prime}))\,{\rm d}m\times_{Y_{n}}m(x,x^{\prime})=\int_{Y_{n}}\operatorname{Var}_{n}(y)\,{\rm d}m_{n}(y)\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\bar{E}_{i}}\operatorname{Var}_{n}\,{\rm d}m_{n}+\int_{\bar{E}_{0}}\operatorname{Var}_{n}\,{\rm d}m_{n}\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\bar{E}_{i}}\operatorname{Var}_{n}\,{\rm d}m_{n}+m_{n}(\bar{E}_{0}).\end{split}

Let
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Since

\int_{\bar{E}_{i}}\mu_{n,y}(E_{i}\setminus A_{i})\,{\rm d}m_{n}(y)=m(E_{i}\setminus A_{i})<\delta

the set
\bar{E}_{i}^{*}:=\left\{\left.{y\in\bar{E}_{i}}\ \right|\ {\mu_{n,y}(E_{i}\setminus A_{i})<\sqrt{\delta}}\right\}

satisfies
m_{n}(\bar{E}_{i}\setminus\bar{E}_{i}^{*})<\sqrt{\delta}
by Chebyshev’s inequality.
Since for
y\in\bar{E}_{i}
, we have
\mu_{n,y}(X\setminus E_{i})=0
,
we get that for
y\in\bar{E}_{i}^{*}
,
\mu_{n,y}(X\setminus A_{i})<\sqrt{\delta}
.
For
y\in\bar{E}_{i}^{*}
we have
d(F(x),F(x^{\prime}))\leq 2\epsilon
for those pairs
(x,x^{\prime})\in X\times_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}X

and
x,x^{\prime}\in A_{i}
, therefore

\begin{split}\operatorname{Var}_{n}(y)&\leq 2\int_{X\setminus A_{i}}\int_{X}d(F(x),F(x^{\prime}))\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,y}(x)\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,y}(x^{\prime})\\
&+\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{i}}d(F(x),F(x^{\prime}))\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,y}(x)\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,y}(x^{\prime})\\
&\leq 2\sqrt{\delta}+2\epsilon\mu_{n,y}(A_{i})^{2}\\
&\leq 2\sqrt{\delta}+2\epsilon\cdot\mu_{n,y}(A_{i}).\end{split}

We thus get

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\bar{E}_{i}}\operatorname{Var}_{n}\,{\rm d}m_{n}&\leq 2k\sqrt{\delta}+2\epsilon\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{Y_{n}}\mu_{n,y}(A_{i})\,{\rm d}m_{n}(y)\\
&=2k\sqrt{\delta}+2\epsilon\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}m(A_{i})\leq 2k\sqrt{\delta}+2\epsilon<4\epsilon.\end{split}

We just proved that given
\epsilon>0
there is
N
so that for
n\geq N

\begin{split}\int_{X\times_{Y_{n}}X}&d(F(x),F(x^{\prime}))\,{\rm d}(m\times_{Y_{n}}m)(x,x^{\prime})=\int_{Y_{n}}\operatorname{Var}_{n}\,{\rm d}m_{n}\\
&<\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\bar{E}_{i}}\operatorname{Var}_{n}\,{\rm d}m_{n}+m_{n}(\bar{E}_{0})<6\epsilon.\end{split}

This proves the lemma.
∎

Proof of Proposition

.

2.9.
A useful Lemma

Let
p:(X,m,T)\to(Y,n,S)
be an equivariant map between ergodic p.m.p systems, where
T
is invertible and
S
is not necessarily.
Let

y\mapsto\operatorname{Prob}(X),\qquad m=\int_{Y}\mu_{y}\,{\rm d}n(y)

be the corresponding disintegration of measures.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(Y,n)
be such that

\ f(y)\leq\int f(p(T^{-1}x))\,{\rm d}\mu_{y}(x)\

for
n
-a.e
y\in Y
.

Then
f
is a.e constant.

Proof.

The quotient map
p
defines an isometric embedding
p:L^{2}(Y,n)\to L^{2}(X,m)
;
the dual operator
p^{*}:L^{2}(X,m)\to L^{2}(Y,n)

is an orthogonal projection, explicitly defined by

p^{*}F(y)=\int_{X}F(x)\,{\rm d}\mu_{y}(x)\qquad(F\in L^{2}(X,m)).

Let
V_{S}
on
L^{2}(Y,n)
and
V_{T}
on
L^{2}(X,m)
be the isometries defined by
S
and
T
.
Then
V_{S}=p^{*}V_{T}p
and therefore the dual operator
U=V_{S}^{*}
is given by

pV_{T^{-1}}p^{*}
, meaning

Uf(y)=\int f(p(T^{-1}x))\,{\rm d}\mu_{y}(x)\qquad(f\in L^{2}(Y,n)).

The assumption of a.e inequality
f\leq Uf
implies that for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}

f\leq Uf\leq U^{2}\leq\dots\leq U^{k}f.

Ergodicity of
(Y,n,S)
implies by von Neumann’s mean ergodic theorem an
L^{2}
-convergence

\frac{1}{k}(f+Uf+\dots U^{k-1}f)\ \,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{L^{2}}}}\,\ \int_{Y}f.

Since
f
is a.e dominated by the averages on the LHS for any
\epsilon>0
one has

n\left\{\left.{y\in Y}\ \right|\ {f(y)\geq\int f\,+\epsilon}\right\}\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\cdot\left\|\frac{1}{k}(f+Uf+\dots U^{k-1}f)-\int f\right\|_{2}^{2}\to 0.

Therefore we get a.e inequality

f(y)\leq\int_{Y}f

which implies that
f
is a.e constant, as claimed.
∎

2.10.
Some lemmas regarding spaces of measures

Let
M
be a compact metrizable space
and denote by

on

Lemma 2.18
.

The map
\operatorname{ev}
is upper semi-continuous (usc).

Proof.

We fix
(\nu,\xi)\in Q\times M
.
Next we fix

such that

\operatorname{ev}(\nu,\xi)<t
.
we find an open neighborhood

such that

such that

and

.
The integration of

is a continuous function on

,
we thus find a neighborhood of

consisting of measures

.
Then for every

,
\operatorname{ev}(\nu^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})<t
.
As

is arbitrary, this shows that
\operatorname{ev}(-,-)
is usc at
(\nu,\xi)
.
As
(\nu,\xi)
is arbitrary, this show that
\operatorname{ev}
is usc.
∎

We recall the following standard result.

We define the map

\operatorname{max-ev}:Q\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,[0,1],\quad\operatorname{max-ev}(\nu)=\max\left\{\left.{\nu(\{\xi\})}\ \right|\ {\xi\in M}\right\}.

The map

3.7.

In this subsection we define the notion of stationary maps and prove their basic properties.
We consider the disintegration of the measure

(Stationary maps)
.

Let

a continuous homomorphism.
A map

We denote by

The space

Therefore it is convex compact.
The following Theorem identifies it with a familiar object.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
Q
be a metrizable convex compact space and
\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aff}(Q)
a continuous homomorphism.
Then there is a continuous affine bijection

\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X,Q)\cong\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}(X_{+},Q),\qquad\phi\ \longleftrightarrow\ \psi

that in one direction is given by the formula

\psi(y)=\int_{X}\phi(x)\,{\rm d}\mu_{y}(x)\qquad(y\in X_{+}),

and in the opposite direction by a.e weak-* convergence:

\begin{split}\phi(x)&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ w_{-n}(x)^{-1}.\psi(p_{+}(T^{-n}x))\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ w(T^{-1}x)w(T^{-2}x)\cdots w(T^{-n}x).\psi(p_{+}(T^{-n}x)).\end{split}

Proof.

Given
\phi\in\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X,Q)
we define
\psi\in\operatorname{Map}(X_{+},Q)
using the formula

\psi(y)=\int_{X}\phi(x)\,{\rm d}\mu_{y}(x)\qquad(y\in X_{+}).

Maps

can be identified with
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\infty}
-measurable maps

, and in this proof

to simplify notations.
Thus
\psi
is given by the conditional expectation:
\psi=\mathbb{E}(\phi\mid\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\infty})

Using equivariance of

and the fact

is

-measurable we have the relation

\begin{split}\psi&=\mathbb{E}(\phi\mid\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\infty})=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(\phi\mid\mathcal{F}_{-1}^{\infty})\mid\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\infty})=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(w_{-1}^{-1}.\phi\circ T^{-1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{-1}^{\infty})\mid\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\infty})\\

\psi(y)=\int_{X}w(T^{-1}x).\psi(p_{+}(T^{-1}x))\,{\rm d}\mu_{y}(x)\qquad(y\in X_{+}).

Conversely, we consider

The fact that it is stationary is expressed by the relation

for

Note that since

so we have

\Gamma=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
and
w:X\to\Gamma
be the identity map.
Check that
X,X_{\pm},E
and
E_{\pm}
are all isomorphic to the action of
\Gamma
on itself and the maps
p_{\pm}
and
\pi_{\pm}
are isomorphisms.
Consider
\psi:X_{+}\to\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
to be the map taking a point to the corresponding Dirac measure.
This is a determinstic stationary map.

We will see in Lemma
4.3
that under the Apafi condition, every deterministic stationary map is trivial.
Below we give a criterion for a stationary map to be deterministic.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
Q
be a non-empty metrizable convex compact space and
\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aff}(Q)
a continuous homomorphism.
Fix a stationary map
\psi\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}(X_{+},Q)
.
If the image of
\psi
is a.e an extreme point of
Q
then
\psi
is determinstic,
that is
\psi\in\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X_{+},Q)
.

More generally,
let
Q_{0}\subset Q
be a measurable
\Gamma
-invariant convex subset
such that image of
\psi
is a.e in
Q_{0}
.
If the image of
\psi
is a.e an extreme point of
Q_{0}
then
\psi
is determinstic.
Assume, moreover, that the Greg
(X,\mathcal{X},m,T,\Gamma,w)
is ergodic.
If
m_{+}(\psi^{-1}(\operatorname{ext}(Q_{0})))>0
then
\psi
is determinstic.

Proof.

We prove the “more general” and “moreover” case.
We assume as we may that
\psi(X_{+})\subset Q_{0}

and conclude from the
statoinarity equation for
y\in X_{+}

\psi(y)=\int_{X}w(T^{-1}x).\psi(p_{+}(T^{-1}x))\,{\rm d}\mu_{y}(x)

that if
\psi(y)\in\operatorname{ext}(Q_{0})
then for
\mu_{y}
-a.e
x\in X
,
\psi\circ p_{+}(x)=w(T^{-1}x).\psi\circ p_{+}(T^{-1}x)
.
Applying Lemma
3.7
to the characteristic function of
\operatorname{ext}(Q_{0})\subset Q_{0}
,
h=\chi_{\operatorname{ext}(Q_{0})}:Q_{0}\to[0,1]
,
we get that
m_{+}(\psi^{-1}(\operatorname{ext}(Q_{0})))=1
.
Thus
m((\psi\circ p_{+})^{-1}(\operatorname{ext}(Q_{0})))=1
and we cocnlude that
\psi\circ p_{+}
is in fact a
w
-map.
It follows that indeed,
\psi
is a
w
-map.
∎

3.9.
Stationary measures

The following example illustrates the concept of stationarity and will be used later
to determine the Lyapunov spectra in certain situations.

Example 3.10

(Stationary measures)
.

Let
M
be a compact metrizable space,
\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Homeo}(M)
a continuous homomorphism and consider
the space
X\times M
with the transformation

S:X\times M\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,X\times M,\qquad S:(x,\xi)\mapsto(Tx,\rho\circ w(x).\xi).

Let
\operatorname{Prob}_{m}(X\times M)
denote all probability measures on

that project to

Using disintegration, such measures are in an affine bijection with
\operatorname{Map}(X,\operatorname{Prob}(M))
,

\operatorname{Prob}_{m}(X\times M)^{S}

\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X,\operatorname{Prob}(M))

Let

\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X,\operatorname{Prob}(M))\ni\phi\longleftrightarrow\psi\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))

\bar{m}:=\int_{X}\delta_{x}\times\psi(x)\,{\rm d}m(x),\qquad\bar{m}_{+}:=\int_{X_{+}}\delta_{y}\times\psi(y)\,{\rm d}m_{+}(y).

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X\times M,\bar{m},S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X_{+}\times M,\bar{m}_{+},S_{+})}

Apafic Gregs

In this section we work with a fixed Greg

as in Definition

and retain the notations introduced

In particular we have the past and future factors

,

,

.
Throughout this section we assume further that

4.1.
From Gregs to boundary systems

The main significance of the Apafi assumption is the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

The triple of
\Gamma
-Lebesgue spaces
E,E_{+},E_{-}
, together with the maps
p_{\pm}:E\to E_{\pm}
forms a boundary system,
see Definition
2.11
.

Proof.

The amenability of the
\Gamma
-actions on

and

is given in Lemma
3.4

and the relative metric ergodicity of

is given in Theorem
3.3
.
By the Apafi condition we have that, indeed,

is measure class preserving.
∎

Some ergodicity results are implied automatically.

Lemma 4.2
.

The
\Gamma
spaces
E_{+},E_{-}
and

are all metrically ergodic.
If
(V,d)
is a separable metric space on which
\Gamma
acts isometrically
then every

is essentially constant.
In particular,

, hence also

and

, are ergodic systems.

Proof.

The first claim follows from Lemma

and the second now follows by Lemma
3.2
.
The last statement follows by Remark

.
∎

4.2.
The atomic part of stationary measures

We keep the assumption that our Greg

(X,\mathcal{X},m,T,w,\Gamma)
is Apafic.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
Q
be a non-empty metrizable convex compact space
and let
\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aff}(Q)
a continuous homomorphism.
The following are criteria for triviality of
Q
-valued maps.

(1)

A map
f\in\operatorname{Map}_{w}(E,Q)
is essentially constant with a
\Gamma
-invariant essential image if and only if
there exist
f_{\pm}\in\operatorname{Map}_{w}(E_{\pm},Q)
such that
f=f_{\pm}\circ\pi_{\pm}
.

(2)

A map
\phi\in\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X,Q)
is essentially constant with a
\Gamma
-invariant
essential image if and only if
there exist
\phi_{\pm}\in\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X_{\pm},Q)
such that
\phi=\phi_{\pm}\circ p_{\pm}
.

(3)

A map
\phi_{+}\in\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X_{+},Q)
is essentially constant with a
\Gamma
-invariant
essential image if and only if

\phi_{+}\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}_{-}(X_{+},Q)
.

(4)

A map
\psi\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}_{-}(X_{+},Q)
is essentially constant with a
\Gamma
-invariant
essential image if and only if
there exists a measurable
\Gamma
-invariant convex subset
Q_{0}\subset Q
such that

m_{+}(\psi^{-1}(Q_{0}))=1,\qquad m_{+}(\psi^{-1}(\operatorname{ext}(Q_{0})))>0.

In particular, this is the case if
m_{+}(\psi^{-1}(\operatorname{ext}(Q)))>0
.

Proof.

In all statements the “only if” part is trivial. We argue below to show the “if” parts.

In the first statement,
f=f_{-}\circ\pi_{-}=f_{+}\circ\pi_{+}
,
thus
f
factors via
E_{+}\times E_{-}
, by the Apafi assumption,
that is
f=\bar{f}\circ(\pi_{+}\times\pi_{-})

for some
\bar{f}\in\operatorname{Map}_{\Gamma}(E_{+}\times E_{-},Q)
.
We get that
\bar{f}=f_{\pm}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\pm}
, where
\operatorname{pr}_{\pm}:E_{+}\times E_{-}\to E_{\pm}
are the coordinate projections,
which implies that
\bar{f}
is essentially constant with a
\Gamma
-invariant essential image, thus so is
f
as well.

In the second statement, we consider the corresponding maps
f\in\operatorname{Map}_{w}(E,Q)
and
f_{\pm}\in\operatorname{Map}_{w}(E_{\pm},Q)

given by Lemma
3.2
and observe that
f=f_{\pm}\circ\pi_{\pm}
.
We apply the first statement to
f
and conclude that the result holds also for
\phi
.

In the third statement we define
\phi=\phi_{+}\circ p_{+}\in\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X,Q)
and let

\phi_{-}\in\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X_{-},Q)
be the map given in Theorem
3.6
,
thus for a.e
x\in X
,

\phi_{-}(p_{-}(x))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ w_{-n}(x)^{-1}.\phi(T^{-n}x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ \phi(x)=\phi(x).

We apply the second statement to
\phi
and conclude that the result holds also for
\phi_{+}
.

The fourth statement follows from the third by Lemma
3.9
.
∎

Specializing to
Q=\operatorname{Prob}(M)
where
M
is a compact
\Gamma
-space,
we get from the special case discussed in Lemma
4.3
(4)
that if the values of
\psi\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}_{-}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))

are Dirac measures on a non-null subset of
X_{+}
then
\psi
is essentially constant
and its image is the Dirac measure at a
\Gamma
-invariant point of
M
.
In fact, more is true as we will see in first in
Remark
4.6
and then in Proposition
4.7
below.

Definition 4.4
.

For a measure space
\Omega
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, a map
f\in\operatorname{Map}(\Omega,\operatorname{Prob}(M))

is said to be
n
-uniform
if for a.e
z\in\Omega
,
f(z)
is a uniform measure on
n
-points.
It is said to be
finite-uniform
if it is
n
-uniform for some
n
.

Example 4.5
.

If
M
is a
\Gamma
-space and
\Omega
is an ergodic
\Gamma
-space
then every map in
\operatorname{Map}_{\Gamma}(\Omega,\operatorname{Prob}(M))
which values have a non-tirvial atomic part on a non-null subset of
\Omega
is
finite-uniform.
indeed, this follows easily from the measurability of
\operatorname{max-ev}
and
\operatorname{max-part}
,
see Lemma
2.20
and Lemma
2.22
.

Remark 4.6
.

If
\psi\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}_{-}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))
is finite-uniform
then it is essentially constant
and its image is a finite
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
M
.
This follows from Lemma
4.3
(4),
as the
n
-uniform measures on
M
are the extreme points of
Q_{0}=\operatorname{max-ev}^{-1}([0,1/n])
,
see Lemma
2.20
and the discussion before.

The following result is a generalization of this remark.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
M
be a compact metrizable
\Gamma
-space
which admits no finite invariant subset
and fix a map
\psi\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}_{-}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))
.
Then for a.e
x\in X_{+}
, the measure
\psi(x)

has no atomic part.

For the proof of Proposition
4.7

we will need the following.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
M
be a compact metrizable
\Gamma
-space
and fix a stationary map
\psi\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))
.
If the values of
\psi

have non-trivial atomic part on a non-null subset of
X_{+}
then

\psi
could be represented as a convex combination
\psi=t\psi_{0}+(1-t)\psi_{1}
,
where
\psi_{0}\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))
is a deterministic finite-uniform map and
t\in(0,1]
.

In particular, if
\psi
is extremal in
\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))
, then
\psi
is
a deterministic finite-uniform map.
Furthermore, if
\psi\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}_{-}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))
,
then
\psi
is essentially constant,
and its image is a finite
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
M
.

Proof.

We will use the notation and results of §
2.10
.
In particular, we let
Q
be the compact convex
\Gamma
-space
consisting of positive measures on
M

with total mass in
[0,1]
and view
\operatorname{Prob}(M)
as a
\Gamma
-invariant
convex compact subset of
Q
,
thus
\psi\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}(X_{+},Q)
.

By Lemma
2.20
, the map

\operatorname{max-ev}:Q\to[0,1],\quad\operatorname{max-ev}(\nu)=\max\left\{\left.{\nu(\{\xi\})}\ \right|\ {\xi\in M}\right\}

is convex and measurable, thus by Lemma
3.7
it is essentially constant.
We denote this constant by
s

and note that by the assumption on
\psi
,
s>0
.
We denote
Q_{s}=\operatorname{max-ev}^{-1}([0,s])
.
By Lemma
2.21
it is a
\Gamma
-invariant measurable convex subset of
Q

such that
m_{+}(\psi^{-1}(Q_{s}))=1
.

We consider the map

\operatorname{max-part}:Q_{s}\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,\operatorname{ext}(Q_{s})

taking a measure
\nu\in Q_{s}
to the maximal measure in
\operatorname{ext}(Q_{s})

which is dominated by
\nu
.
This map is measurable by Lemma
2.22
.
Precomposing this map with
\psi
and extending its codomain to
Q
we get the map

\operatorname{max-part}\circ\psi:X_{+}\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,Q.

The stationarity equation for
\psi
, together with an obvious extremality argument,
gives that this map is stationary.
By Lemma
3.9
, this map is deterministic.
Applying Lemma
3.7
to
\operatorname{max-part}\circ\psi
,
taking
h
to be the total mass, we get that the total
mass of the values of
\operatorname{max-part}\circ\psi

are essentially a non-zero constant which we denote by
t
.
If
t=1
then
\psi=\operatorname{max-part}\circ\psi
is a deterministic finite-uniform map
and there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise, we denote

\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{t}\cdot\operatorname{max-part}\circ\psi,\qquad\psi_{1}=\frac{1}{1-t}\cdot(\psi-\operatorname{max-part}\circ\psi)

and get the decomposition
\psi=t\psi_{0}+(1-t)\psi_{1}
.
We note that
\psi_{0}
is deterministic, as
\operatorname{max-part}\circ\psi
is,
and it is
n
-uniform for
n=t/s
.
This proves the first part of the lemma.
The last part of the lemma follows from Remark
4.6
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.7
.

We will prove the contrapositive: we will assume that the values of
\psi

have a non-trivial atomic parts on a non-null subset of
X_{+}
,
and argue to show
M
admits a finite invariant subset.

As the evaluation maps
\operatorname{ev}_{m}
are convex (in fact, affine),
there must be also an extreme point of
\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}_{-}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))

such that its values
have non-trivial atomic parts on a non-null subset of
X_{+}
.
We thus assume as we may that
\psi
is extremal in
\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}_{-}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))
.
We note that if we knew that
\psi
is extremal also in
\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))

then we would be done by the last part of Lemma
4.8
,
as the essential image of
\psi
is a finite
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
M
.
However, this lemma gives only that
\psi=t\psi_{0}+(1-t)\psi_{1}

for some
t>0
, where
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))
and
\psi_{0}
is
a deterministic finite-uniform map.
We will show that this is enough for our proof.

Using Theorem
3.12
, we find maps corresponding to
\psi
and
\psi_{i}
,

\begin{split}&\phi\in\operatorname{ext}(\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X_{-},\operatorname{Prob}(M)))\quad\mbox{and}\quad f\in\operatorname{ext}(\operatorname{Map}_{\Gamma}(E_{-},\operatorname{Prob}(M))),\\
&\phi_{i}\in\operatorname{ext}(\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X,\operatorname{Prob}(M)))\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{i}\in\operatorname{ext}(\operatorname{Map}_{\Gamma}(E,\operatorname{Prob}(M))).\end{split}

which satisfy similar relations:

\phi=t\phi_{0}+(1-t)\phi_{1}
and
f=tf_{0}+(1-t)f_{1}
.
Since
\psi_{0}
is determinstic we get
\phi_{0}=\psi_{0}

and in particular
\phi_{0}
is finite-uniform.
It follows from Lemma
3.2
that
f_{0}
is finite-uniform as well.
We conclude in particular that
f
has a non-trivial atomic part.
By the ergodicity of
E_{-}
, we get that also
f
is
n
-uniform for some natural
n
, see Example
4.5
.

If
n=1
then
f
takes values in the Dirac measures on
M
, that is
\operatorname{ext}(\operatorname{Prob}(M))
,
and the same goes to the map
f\circ\pi_{-}:E\to\operatorname{Prob}(M)
.
thus
f\circ\pi_{-}
is extremal in the space
\operatorname{Map}(E,\operatorname{Prob}(M))
.
In particular
f\circ\pi_{-}
is extremal in its subspace
\operatorname{Map}_{\Gamma}(E,\operatorname{Prob}(M))

and by Theorem
3.12
,
\psi
is extremal in the space

\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M))
.
As mentioned above, in this case we are done by the last part of Lemma
4.8
.
Below we will exaplain how to treat the general case,
essentially by reducing it to the case
n=1
.

We consider the space of unordered
n
-tuples in
M
(possibly with non-trivial multiplicities),

M_{n}=M^{n}/S_{n}
and the map
\delta_{n}:M_{n}\to\operatorname{Prob}(M)
taking a tuple to the uniform measure it supports
(taking multiplicities into account, on the diagonals). Note that this map is an isomorphism on its (closed) image.
As the image of
f
is in
\delta(M_{n})
a.e, we have
\delta^{-1}\circ f:E_{-}\to M_{n}
and identifying points of
M_{n}
with Dirac
measures on
M_{n}
, we get a map
f^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Map}_{\Gamma}(E_{-},\operatorname{Prob}(M_{n}))
.
Note that
f^{\prime}\circ\pi_{-}
is extremal in
\operatorname{Map}_{\Gamma}(E,\operatorname{Prob}(M_{n}))
, as it takes values in Dirac measures.
Using Theorem
3.12
, we find corresponding maps

\phi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Map}_{w}(X_{-},\operatorname{Prob}(M_{n}))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}_{-}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M_{n}))

and we note that
\psi^{\prime}
is extremal in
\operatorname{Map}^{\operatorname{stat}}(X_{+},\operatorname{Prob}(M_{n}))
,
by Theorem
3.12
.
We extend the map
\delta_{n}
to
(\delta_{n})_{*}:\operatorname{Prob}(M_{n})\to\operatorname{Prob}(M)

by pushing measures and taking barycenters.
By construction we have
f=(\delta_{n})_{*}\circ f^{\prime}
, thus
\phi=(\delta_{n})_{*}\circ\phi^{\prime}

and
\psi=(\delta_{n})_{*}\circ\psi^{\prime}
.
Since the values of
\psi

have a non-trivial atomic parts on a non-null subset of
X_{+}
, we deduce that the same holds for
\psi^{\prime}
.
We are done by the last part of Lemma
4.8
,
as the essential image of
\psi^{\prime}
is a finite
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
M
.
∎

5.
Semi-simple Lie groups

In this section we fix a connected semisimple real algebraic group
\mathbf{G}

and consider its group of real points
G=\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{R})
.
We let
K<G
be a fixed maximal compact subgroup and
P<G
be a minimal parabolic subgroup.
We fix a maximal split torus
A<P
and let
Z
denote its centralizer in
G
.
Note that
Z<P
.

5.1.
The flag variety
G/P
and the space
G/Z
.

The minimal parabolic subgroup
P<G
is solvable by compact, hence amenable,
and it is cocompact. In fact,
G/P
is (the real points of) a projective variety,
called the
flag variety
or the
Furstenberg boundary
of
G
.
It will play an important role in our discussion.

The inclusion
Z<P
gives rise to a natural map
\operatorname{pr}_{+}:G/Z\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,G/P
.
We let
W=N_{G}(A)/C_{G}(A)=N_{G}(A)/Z
be the
Weyl group
associated with
A

and note that it acts on
G/Z
by
G
-automorphisms:

w\in W
gives the
G
-map
G/Z\to G/Z
,
gZ\mapsto g\tilde{w}Z
,
where
\tilde{w}\in N_{G}(A)
is a lift of
w
.
By a standard abuse of notation, we denote this automorphism of
G/Z
by
w
as well.

For every
w\in W
, we get a
G
-map

\Phi_{w}=(\operatorname{pr}_{+},\operatorname{pr}_{+}\circ w):G/Z\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,G/P\times G/P.

We denote the corresponding image by
B_{w}\subset G/P\times G/P
.
These subsets are called the
Bruhat cells
.
Note that
B_{e}
is the diagonal in
G/P\times G/P
.
The following lemma is well known.

Lemma 5.1

5.4.
Length functions

Definition 5.7
.

Let
H
be a compactly generated locally compact group.
A
length function
on
H
is a map
|\cdot|:H\to[0,\infty)

which is bounded on compact subsets,
satisfying for every

h,h^{\prime}\in G
,
|hh^{\prime}|\leq|h|+|h^{\prime}|
.
It is said to be
symmetric

if for every

h\in G
,
|h|=|h^{-1}|
.

For two functions,
|\cdot|,|\cdot|^{\prime}:H\to[0,\infty)
,
we say that
|\cdot|
dominates
|\cdot|^{\prime}

if there exist
\alpha\geq 1
and
\beta\geq 0
such that for every
h\in H
,

|h|^{\prime}\leq\alpha|h|+\beta
.
We say that
|\cdot|
and
|\cdot|^{\prime}
are
equivalent

if both dominate each other.

Note that by our convention, a length function
need not be continuous and it might have
|e|>0
and
|h|=0
for
h\neq e
.
The word distance with respect to a non-open precompact generating set is an example of a discontinuous length function.
Clearly, the word distance dominates every other length function.
A length function is said to be in the
word class
if it is equivalent to the word distance.

Example 5.8
.

Let
H
be the additive group of a finite dimensional real vector space.
An
asymmetric norm
on
H

is a length function
\|\cdot\|
which is homogeneous with respect to positive scalars
such that for
v\in H
,
\|v\|=0
iff
v=0
.
Note that such an asymmetric norm is necessarily symmetric.
Every asymmetric norm on
H
gives a word class length function.
In this case we use the term
norm class
as a synonymous to word class.

We are now back to consider the semsimple group
G
.
A prominent example of a length function
|\cdot|
on
G

is given by
|g|=d(gx,x)
, where
d
is the Reiamannian metric on the symmetric space

and

. Note that for
v\in\mathfrak{a}
, the function
v\mapsto|\exp(v)|
gives the
Hilbertian norm on
\mathfrak{a}
associated with the restriction of the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{a}
.
In particular, this is a norm class length function on
\mathfrak{a}
.

Lemma 5.9
.

A length function
|\cdot|
on
G
is in the word class iff
the length function
v\mapsto|\exp(v)|
on
\mathfrak{a}
is in the norm class.

Proof.

By the Cartan decomposition, a length function
|\cdot|
on
G
is equivalent to the
bi-
K
-invariant function
kak^{\prime}\mapsto|a|
.
Thus,
|\cdot|
is determined by its restriction to

, which could be identified with
\mathfrak{a}
via
\exp
.
The corresponding length function on
\mathfrak{a}
is dominated by the norm class.
The symmetric space example shows that it could actually be a norm,
so the class of length functions on
G
which corresponds to the norm class on
\mathfrak{a}
is not empty,
thus it must coinside with the word class on
G
, by maximality.
∎

5.5.
Subadditive Ergodic Theorems

A translation invariant partial order on a real vector space
V
is determined by
the positive cone
V_{+}=\{v\in V\mid v\geq 0\}
.
A partial order on
V
is said to be
compatible
if it is translation invariant,
its positive cone
V_{+}
is closed
and it satisfies
V_{+}\cap(-V_{+})=\{0\}
.
Kingman’s Subadditive Ergodic Theorem stated below is usually stated for real valued functions.
However, the version we give here follows at once, upon applying positive functionals.

Theorem 5.17

(Kingman)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X},m,T)
be an ergodic p.m.p system
and let
V
be a finite dimensional real vector space endowed with a compatible partial order.
Let
f_{n}\in L^{1}(X,V)
be a sequence satisfying
for every
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, for a.e
x\in X
,

f_{n+k}(x)\leq f_{n}(x)+f_{k}(T^{n}x).

Then there exists a vector
v\in V
so that there is an
m
-a.e and
L^{1}
-convergence

v=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot f_{n}.

Corollary 5.18
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X},m,T)
be an ergodic p.m.p system,
let
G
be a connected semisimple real Lie group with a finite center
and
F:X\to G
be a measurable map so that
\log\|\operatorname{Ad}\circ F\|\in L^{1}(X,m)
.
Then there exists
\Lambda_{F}\in\mathfrak{a}
so that there is an
m
-a.e and
L^{1}
-convergence

\Lambda_{F}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\kappa\circ F_{n}.

This corollary is part of the Multiplicative Ergodic Theorem of Oseledets,
that also produces a measurable
F
-equivariant map
\xi_{+}:X_{+}\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,G/Q_{+}
,
and
\xi_{-}:X_{-}\to G/Q_{-}
,
where
P<Q_{-},Q_{+}<G
are some parabolic subgroups determined by
\Lambda_{F}
.
In the case of simple Lyapunov spectrum, i.e. when
\Lambda_{F}\in\mathfrak{a}^{++}
,
one has
Q_{-}=Q_{+}=P
is the minimal parabolic.
Oseledets theorem asserts
m
-a.e. and in
L^{1}
-convergence of the Iwasawa cocycle

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\sigma(F_{n}(x),\xi)=\Lambda_{F}

for any
\xi
that is in general position with respect to
\xi_{+}(x)
.
Similarly, denoting by
\iota:\mathfrak{a}^{+}\to\mathfrak{a}^{+}
Cartan involution,
we have

\lim_{n\to-\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\sigma(F_{n}(x),\xi)=\iota(\Lambda_{F})

for any
\xi\in G/P
in general position with respect to
\xi_{-}(x)
.
This is a paraphrasing of Oseledets theorem; we refer to
Kaimanovich
[
26
]
, Karlsson–Margulis
[
28
]
,
Filip
[
19
]
for the full treatment.

Here let us point out the following consequence of Oseledets theorem,
and the dynamics of
regular
elements

on
G/P
.
Such an element has
|W|
-many fixed points, where

is the Weyl group of

.

Let

In this subsection we fix a locally compact group
\Gamma

\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,G)

Note that the group

We refer to

We endow the space

A representation

The subset of

Lemma 5.22
.

Every proper algebraic subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
is contained in a maximal proper algebraic subgroup and there is a finite collection of conjugacy classes of maximal proper algebraic subgroups.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{M}

the collection of maximal proper subgroups of
\mathbf{G}
which contain a non-trivial normal subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
.
If
\mathbf{G}
is simple and adjoint than this collection is empty.
Since
\mathbf{G}
has a finite number of normal subgroups, it follows by an induction argument that
\mathcal{M}
has a finite number of conjugacy classes.

Denote by
\mathcal{S}
the collection of all connected semisimple proper subgroups which are not normal in
\mathbf{G}

and by
\mathcal{R}
the collection of normalizers of elements in
\mathcal{S}
.
The collection
\mathcal{S}
has a finite number of conjugacy classes.
Indeed, in every dimension

the number of conjugacy classes of

-dimensional elements of
\mathcal{S}
is bounded by the number of
irreducible components of the variety of
d
-dimensional subalgebras of the Lie algebra of
\mathbf{G}
, as every conjugation orbit is Zariski open by

[

, Proposition 12.1]
.
Thus also the collection
\mathcal{R}
has a finite number of conjugacy classes.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}
the collection of all maximal tori in
\mathbf{G}

and by
\mathcal{N}
the collection of normalizers of elements in
\mathcal{A}
.
It is a standard fact that the collection
\mathcal{A}
consists of a single conjugacy class and thus also the collection
\mathcal{N}
consists of a single conjugacy class.

Denote by
\mathcal{P}
the collection of all proper parabolic subgroups in
\mathbf{G}
. It is a standard fact that the collection
\mathcal{P}
has a finite number of conjugacy classes.

By Jordan Theorem, there exists

such that every finite subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
of order greater than
n

has a non-central normal abelian subgroup.
Denote by
\mathcal{F}
the collection of all finite subgroups of
\mathbf{G}
of order bounded by
n
.
The collection
\mathcal{F}
has a finite number of conjugacy classes.
Indeed, there is a finite number of isomorphism classes of groups of order bounded by
n
and for every such group

, every homomorphism into
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{C})
could be conjugated into
K
, a maximal compact subgroup, and the compact set
\operatorname{Hom}(L,K)
has finitely many

.

The proof now follows as every
proper algebraic subgroup
\mathbf{H}<\mathbf{G}
is contained in an element of either

\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{N}
,
\mathcal{P}
or
\mathcal{F}

and all of these have finitely many conjugacy classes, as explained above.
We will explain this.
Fix
\mathbf{H}<\mathbf{G}
and
denote by
\mathbf{H}^{o}
its identity component.
Assume
\mathbf{H}
is not contained in an element of
\mathcal{M}
.
If
\mathbf{H}

has a non-trivial unipotent radical then
\mathbf{H}
is contained in a parabolic subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
.
Assume this is not the case, thus
\mathbf{H}
is reductive.
If
\mathbf{H}
normalizes an element of
\mathcal{S}
then it is contained in an element of
\mathcal{R}
.
Assume this is not the case, thus
\mathbf{H}^{o}
is a torus.
If
\mathbf{H}^{o}
is contained in a unique maximal torus then
\mathbf{H}
is contain in an element of
\mathcal{N}
.
Assume this is not the case.
If
\dim(\mathbf{H})>0
then
\mathbf{H}
is contained in the normalizer of the derived subgroup of the identity component of the centralizer of
\mathbf{H}^{0}
, which is an element
\mathcal{S}
, thus
\mathbf{H}
is contained in an element of
\mathcal{R}
.
Thus we assume as we may that
\mathbf{H}
is finite.
If the order of
\mathbf{H}
is greater than

then
\mathbf{H}
contains a normal non-central abelian subgroup and it is contained in its normalizer which is of positive dimension, and we are reduced to the above cases.
Assuming this is not the case, we get that
\mathbf{H}
is an element of

.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.20
.

Clearly, the second statement follows from the first, as
\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
could be naturally identified with a closed subset
of
\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{C}))
,
so we focus on proving the first statement.

By Lemma

there exists a finite list

of maximal proper algebraic subgroups of
\mathbf{G}
representing every conjugacy class of such.
Using Chevalley’s Theorem, we fix for every

an irreducible projective representation

with

such that

fixes a point in

and we note that

5.7.
AREA – Algebraic Representations of Ergodic Actions

In this subsection we define the notion of AREA, relate it to the relative metric ergodicity, and will
use these results in the proof of Theorem
2.14
in the following subsection.
The idea of AREA was developed in
[
8
]
, and used in
[
10
]
and
[
12
]
for
the proofs of super-rigidity for lattice representations.
Here we will focus on the case of algebraic groups and varieties defined over
{\mathbb{R}}
rather than more general fields.
Let
G
be a real algebraic Lie group. We shall use the following facts that are mostly due to Zimmer:

(1)

Let
G\curvearrowright V
be an algebraic action of an algebraic group on an algebraic variety.
Then the orbits of the corresponding
G
-action on
\operatorname{Prob}(V)
are locally closed,
and as a consequence the space
\operatorname{Prob}(V)/G
of

-orbits on
\operatorname{Prob}(V)
is standard Borel
(see
[
34
]
*Corollary 3.2.17).

(2)

For any
\mu\in\operatorname{Prob}(V)
the stabilizer
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(\mu)=\left\{\left.{g\in G}\ \right|\ {g_{*}\mu=\mu}\right\}

is a real algebraic subgroup of
G
(see
[
34
]
*Corollary 3.2.18).

(3)

For the
G
-action on
V=G/P
for any
\mu\in\operatorname{Prob}(G/P)
the stabilizer
\operatorname{Stab}(\mu)

is a compact extension of solvable subgroup (see
[
34
]
*Theorem 3.2.22).

We note that working over
{\mathbb{R}}
is essential for (

).
However, in other respects the scope can be significantly broadened to Polish fields –
see
[
7
]
.

Let us fix a countable group
\Gamma
and a homomorphism

\rho:\Gamma\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,G

with Zariski dense image, taking values in a real semisimple Lie group
G
.
We shall also consider measure class preserving ergodic actions
\Gamma\curvearrowright(A,\alpha)
.
In the proof of Theorem

the role of the Lebesgue
\Gamma
-space

will be played by

,

, and

A

-

is an algebraic variety over

with an algebraic action
of

-variety

, and a measure class preserving

-action on

a

We denote by

•

-equivariant map

6.3.

6.4.
Proof of Theorem
A
.(3) – positivity of the drift

Note that for
G=\operatorname{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
for

g\in G
one has
\log\|g\|=\|\kappa(g)\|_{\mathfrak{sl}_{d}}
.
For a general semi-simple
G
and a choice of length function
|-|
on it,
there are constants
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}<\infty
so that

c_{1}|g|\leq\|\kappa(g)\|_{\mathfrak{a}}\leq c_{2}|g|
.
Given an integrable
F:X\to G
over an ergodic system
(X,m,T)
,
Kingman’s theorem ensures that there is an a.e. (and
L^{1}
)
convergence to a constant

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}|F_{n}(x)|=\lambda.

Positivity of the drift, i.e.
\Lambda_{F}\neq 0
, is equivalent to
\lambda>0
.

Our goal is to show that if
\rho:\Gamma\to G
is a homomorphism
whose image
\rho(\Gamma)
is not contained in a solvable by compact extension
in
G
, then the map
F=\rho\circ w:X\to G

has positive drift.

Let
H
denote the Zariski closure of
\rho(\Gamma)
in
G
.
Then
H
contains a connected normal subgroup of finite index
H_{0}\triangleleft H

that is a semi-direct product

H_{0}=L\ltimes S
of a semisimple Lie group
L
and a solvable group
S
.
Note that
L
cannot be compact, for then
\rho(\Gamma)

would be contained in a compact extension

H
of a solvable group
S
, contradicting our assumption.
The non-compact connected semisimple Lie group
L
admits an epimorphism
onto a non-compact simple Lie group
G_{0}
with trivial center, so we have
an epimorphism

\pi:H_{0}\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,L\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,G_{0}.

We denote by
|-|
a length function on
G
, and by
|-|_{0}

a choice of a length function on
G_{0}
.
Then there exists some
c_{0}>0
so that for
h\in H_{0}
we have

(6.12)

|h|\geq c_{0}\cdot|\pi(h)|_{0}

In the situation where
H=H_{0}
, the representation

\pi\circ\rho:\Gamma\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,H_{0}\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,G_{0}

is Zariski dense, and simplicity of the spectrum
(see §
6.2
above)
for the map
F_{0}:X\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{w}}}\,\Gamma\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\rho}}}\,H_{0}\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\pi}}}\,G_{0}

implies positivity of its drift and therefore
implies positivity of the drift for

F=\rho\circ w
using the inequality (
6.12
).

To deal with the case where
H
is not connected, and therefore

H_{0}
is a proper subgroup of finite index in
H
,
we will use a cocycle trick.
Let
\Lambda=\rho^{-1}(H_{0})
. This is a normal subgroup of finite
index in
\Gamma
.
Consider the cocycle

c:\Gamma\times\Gamma/\Lambda\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,\Lambda,\qquad c(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma\Lambda)=s(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma\Lambda)^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}s(\gamma\Lambda),

is a cross-section,

Then the cocycle

\rho_{0}:\Gamma\times\Gamma/\Lambda\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{c}}}\,\Lambda\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\rho}}}\,H_{0}\to L\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\pi}}}\,G_{0}

is Zariski dense. Thus [to be justified in the updated version with cocycles]
implies that the
G_{0}
-valued sequence :

\rho_{0}(w_{n}(x),\gamma\Lambda)=\pi\circ\rho\left(s(w_{n}(x)\gamma\Lambda)^{-1}w_{n}(x)s(\gamma\Lambda)\right)

has simple spectrum. In particular, it has positive drift:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left|\rho_{0}(w_{n}(x),\gamma\Lambda)\right|_{0}=\lambda_{0}>0.

For
m
-a.e.
x\in X
and
\gamma\Lambda\in\Gamma/\Lambda
we have the same limit

\lambda=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left|\rho\left(s(w_{n}(x)\gamma\Lambda)^{-1}w_{n}(x)s(\gamma\Lambda)\right)\right|

It follows from (
6.12

\lambda\geq c_{0}\lambda_{0}>0
.
In view of the ”triangle inequality”
|abc|\geq|b|-|a|-|c|
,
and denoting
C=\max_{\gamma\Lambda}|s(\gamma\Lambda)|
(recall
\Gamma/\Lambda

is finite), we have

|F_{n}(x)|=|\rho(w_{n}(x)|\geq\left|\rho\left(s(w_{n}(x)\gamma\Lambda)^{-1}w_{n}(x)s(\gamma\Lambda)\right)\right|-2C.

Therefore

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left|F_{n}(x)\right|=\lambda\geq c_{0}\lambda_{0}>0.

This proves positivity of the drift for the original system.

The proof of Theorem

is completed.

7.
Real Gregs

In this paper we study thoroughly the notion of Greg, which is built up over a
\mathbb{Z}
-action.
In this section we discuss the analogous notion of a
real Greg
, which is built up over an
\mathbb{R}
-action, and we develop a machinery of passing between these two notions.
At the moment, we hold the temptation to develop the theory of real Gregs further in an independent manner.
Rather, we will associate with any given real Greg some standard (integer) Gregs.
We will do it in two ways. One is simply by restricting the
\mathbb{R}
-action to
a
\mathbb{Z}
-action.
For the other way we will use the theory of
cross sections
.

A real Greg is a tuple

(Y,\mathcal{Y},\mu,\phi,c,\Gamma)
,
where
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\mu)
is a probability measure space,

\phi:\mathbb{R}\times Y\to Y,\quad(t,y)\mapsto\phi^{t}(y)

is a measure preserving
\mathbb{R}
-action,
\Gamma
is a locally compact second countable group and

c:\mathbb{R}\times Y\to\Gamma,\quad(t,y)\mapsto c_{t}(y)

is a measurable cocycle,
that is

c_{t+s}(y)=c_{s}(\phi^{t}(y))c_{t}(y)
for all
t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}

and
\mu
-a.e.
y\in Y
.

Next, we consider a representation
\rho:\Gamma\to G
into a connected semi-simple real Lie group G with finite center.
We form the measurable
G
-valued cocycle

\Phi=\rho\circ c:\mathbb{R}\times Y\to G,\quad(t,y)\mapsto\Phi_{t}(y).

Assuming ergodicity of the system
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\mu,\phi)
and the integrability condition

(7.1)

\int_{Y}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\Phi_{t}(y)|\,{\rm d}\mu(y)<\infty,

the flow version of Oseledets theorem implies that the limit

(7.2)

\Lambda_{Y}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\cdot\kappa(\Phi_{t}(y))

exists and is
\mu
-a.e. constant element of
\mathfrak{a}^{+}
.

Assume given a real Greg
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\mu,\phi,c,\Gamma)
.
For any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
, we consider the transformation
\phi^{t}:Y\to Y

and the map
c_{t}:Y\to\Gamma
, thus forming an integer Greg
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\mu,\phi^{t},c_{t},\Gamma)
.
We regard it as the
t
-discretization Greg
.
If
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\mu,\phi)
is weakly mixing then the
t
-discretization is ergodic.
Note that the condition (
7.1
) implies the integrability of
|\Phi_{t}|
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
We thus may consider the associated Lyapunov spectrum of

Lemma 7.2
.

Assume the real Greg
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\mu,\phi,c,\Gamma)
is weakly mixing and

\rho:\Gamma\to G
be a representation into a connected semi-simple real Lie group G with finite center which satisfies the integrability condition (

Let

, let

be the associated first return time function and let
(X,\mathcal{X},m,T,w,\Gamma)

Then
F=\rho\circ w
is integrable, so the associated Lyapunov spectrum
\Lambda_{F}
is defined, and we have

\Lambda_{F}=\left(\int_{X}r\,{\rm d}m\right)\cdot\Lambda_{Y}.

Proof.

Let us first make a simplifying assumption that

is bounded away from
0

\Phi_{t}(x)
is bounded for small

.
More precisely, assume there is
\epsilon_{0}>0

Fix some

and consider the
\epsilon

(Y,\mathcal{Y},\mu,\phi^{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon},\Gamma)
.
By Lemma
7.1
, we have
Then
\Lambda_{\epsilon}=\epsilon\cdot\Lambda_{Y}
.
In the sequel we denote
S=\phi^{\epsilon}
.

As above, we identify
Y
with the flow under the graph of
r
associated with
X
.
Consider the thickening

Y^{\prime}=X_{[0,\epsilon)}=\left\{\left.{\phi^{t}(x)}\ \right|\ {x\in X,\ t\in[0,\epsilon)}\right\}\simeq X\times[0,\epsilon)

of
X
. Setting
R=\int_{X}r\,{\rm d}m
, we have
\mu(Y^{\prime})=\epsilon/R
.
Let

n:Y\to\mathbb{N},\quad n(y)=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid S^{n}(y)\in Y^{\prime}\}

be the first return time to
Y^{\prime}
and let

be the transformation on
Y^{\prime}
induced by

Denoting

we get by Lemma

is integrable and

Note that

\begin{split}S^{\prime}(y)&=S^{n(y)}(y)=\phi^{n(y)\epsilon}(y)=\phi^{n(y)\epsilon+s_{0}}(x)\\
&=\phi^{s_{1}+r(x)}(x)=\phi^{s_{1}}\phi^{r(x)}(x)=\phi^{s_{1}}(T(x)).\end{split}

Inductively, we find for every

\begin{split}&F^{\prime}((S^{\prime})^{m}(y))\Phi_{s_{m}}(T^{m}(x))=F^{\prime}(\phi^{s_{m}}(T^{m}(x)))\Phi_{s_{m}}(T^{m}(x))\\
&=\Phi_{n(y)\epsilon}(\phi^{s_{m}}(T^{m}(x)))\Phi_{s_{m}}(T^{m}(x))=\Phi_{n(y)\epsilon+s_{m}}(T^{m}(x))\\

For the last equation we used the equation

as

This finishes the proof under the assumption that

is bounded away from 0.

We will now treat the general case by using an induced system.

Then we have the obvious relations

by Kac lemma we have for

∎

8.
Negatively curved manifolds – Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section we prove Theorem
1.4

by deducing it from the already proven Corollary
7.3
of Theorem
A
and from Theorem

below, which is the main result of this section.
Theorem
8.1
says essentially that the canonical cohomology class associated with a Gibbs measure on a negatively curved manifold could be encoded into an Apafic Greg.
In order to formulate it properly, we will introduce some terminology.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed negatively curved Riemannian manifold.
Denote by
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
the fundamental group,
acting isometrically via deck transformations on the
the universal cover
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g})
.
This action is free and cocompact, with

being the quotient.
The unit tangent bundle

is equipped
with the commuting actions of
\Gamma
and of the

\widetilde{\phi}^{\mathbb{R}}

is the quotient

by
\Gamma
.

The action of
\Gamma
and
\mathbb{R}
on

could be encoded into a cocycle as follows.
For a choice of a fundamental domain

for the

we define the Borel cocycle

(8.1)

c=c_{\mathscr{F}}:\mathbb{R}\times T^{1}M\to\Gamma

for which

, where
\widehat{u}\in\mathscr{F}
is lift of

.
Different choices of fundamental domains give cohomological cocycles
and the corresponding cohomological class is called

.

Given a fundamental domain
\mathscr{F}\subset T^{1}\widetilde{M}
and a
\phi
-invariant probability measure

on

we get the real Greg,

(T^{1}M,\mathcal{B},\mu,\phi,c_{\mathscr{F}},\Gamma)
,
where

is the Borel

-algenbra on

.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a negatively curved compact Riemannian manifold,

\mathscr{F}
be a fundamental domain for the action of
\pi_{1}(M)
on

which has a non-empty interior and let

Remark 8.2

In fact, we will see that the Gibbs assumption on the invariant measure on

.

8.1.
Setting up

We keep the setup discussed above and provide more.

We endow the spaces
T^{1}\widetilde{M}
and
T^{1}M
with the
Sasaki
Riemannian structures are derived from the Riemannian structures on
\widetilde{M}
and
M
correspondingly.
We thus regard the spaces
M
,
\widetilde{M}
,
T^{1}M
and
T^{1}\widetilde{M}
as metric spaces with corresponding distance functions
d_{M}
,
d_{\widetilde{M}}
,
d_{T^{1}M}
and
d_{T^{1}\widetilde{M}}

when there is no chance of confusion.

We denote by
\partial\widetilde{M}
the
ideal boundary

of the universal cover
\widetilde{M}
of

, which is an example
of a proper
{\rm CAT}(-\kappa)
space.
We identify it with the Gromov boundary of the hyperbolic group
\Gamma
,
\partial\widetilde{M}\simeq\partial\Gamma
.
We denote by
\partial^{2}\widetilde{M}=\left\{\left.{(\xi,\eta)\in\partial\widetilde{M}^{2}}\ \right|\ {\xi\neq\eta}\right\}
the space distinct pairs of ideal points.

For

we denote by
L_{u}:{\mathbb{R}}\to\widetilde{M}
the geodesic satisfying
\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}L_{u}(0)=u

and we let
u_{\pm}\in\partial\widetilde{M}
be the ideal end-points of this geodesic,
namely
u_{\pm}=\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}L_{u}(t)
.
We obtain maps

p_{\pm}:T^{1}\widetilde{M}\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,\partial\widetilde{M},\quad u\mapsto u_{\pm}

and

p_{\bowtie}:T^{1}\widetilde{M}\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,\partial^{2}\widetilde{M},\quad u\mapsto(u_{-},u_{+}).

Noticing that the fibers of
p_{\bowtie}
are exactly the geodesics in
\widetilde{M}
,
we identify
\partial^{2}\widetilde{M}
with the set of oriented, unparametrized geodesics in
\widetilde{M}
.

The fibers of
p_{+}
are called the
stable submanifolds
of
T^{1}\widetilde{M}
.
Elements in the same fiber are said to be
stably equivalent
.
Elements

such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}d(\widetilde{\phi}^{t}(u),\widetilde{\phi}^{t}(v))=0
are said to be

, and this forms a finer equivalence relation. The corresponding equivalence classes are the

of

.
For every pair of stably equivalent

there exists a unique

such that

is strongly stably equivalent to
\widetilde{\phi}^{t}(v)
.
The contraction of the strongly stable submanifolds is uniformly exponentially fast in the following sense.
There exists
c_{0}>0
such that for every strongly equivalent
u,v\in T^{1}\widetilde{M}
there exists
C_{u,v}>0
such that for every
t\geq 0
,

(8.2)

d(\widetilde{\phi}^{t}(u),\widetilde{\phi}^{t}(v))<C_{u,v}e^{-c_{0}t}.

We also recall (cf. Kaimanovich
[
27
]
)
that there is a natural correspondence between
finite
\phi^{\mathbb{R}}
-invariant measures on
T^{1}M
,
Radon measures on
T^{1}\widetilde{M}
that are invariant under
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mathbb{R}}\times\Gamma
,
and Radon
\Gamma
-invariant measures on
\partial^{2}\widetilde{M}
.
Given a
\phi^{\mathbb{R}}
-invariant measure
\mu
on
T^{1}M
, we denote by

\widetilde{\mu}
its
\Gamma\times\widetilde{\phi}^{\mathbb{R}}
-invariant

T^{1}\widetilde{M}
, and by
\overline{\mu}
the
\Gamma
-invariant Radon

such that
\widetilde{\mu}
can be identified
with
\overline{\mu}\times{\rm Leb}_{\mathbb{R}}
on

\partial^{2}\widetilde{M}\times{\mathbb{R}}\cong T^{1}\widetilde{M}
(aka Hopf coordinates).
Since we are mostly interested in measure classes,
it is convenient to replace infinite measures
by equivalent probability measures.
Let
\widetilde{\mu}^{1}
be a probability measure on
T^{1}\widetilde{M}

in the measure class of
\widetilde{\mu}
, coming from
\mu
on
T^{1}M
.
Let

\overline{\mu}^{1}\in\operatorname{Prob}(\partial^{2}\widetilde{M}),\qquad\overline{\mu}^{1}_{-}\in\operatorname{Prob}(\partial\widetilde{M}),\qquad\overline{\mu}_{+}^{1}\in\operatorname{Prob}(\partial\widetilde{M})

be the push-forwards of the probability measure
\widetilde{\mu}^{1}
on

under the maps
p_{\bowtie}:u\mapsto(u_{-},u_{+})
,

p_{-}:u\mapsto u_{-}
,
p_{+}:u\mapsto u_{+}
, respectively.

Definition 8.3
.

We say that
\mu
has the

if
there is equivalence of measure classes

\overline{\mu}^{1}\sim\overline{\mu}^{1}_{-}\times\overline{\mu}^{1}_{+}.

Observe that this condition is independent of the specific
choice of
\widetilde{\mu}^{1}
in the measure class of
\widetilde{\mu}
,
and therefore it is a condition on the
\phi^{\mathbb{R}}
-invariant
measure
\mu
on
T^{1}M
.

Lemma 8.4
.

If
\mu
is a Gibbs measure then it satisfies the local product property.

Proof.

Geodesic flow on a negatively curved manifolds, and more generally Anosov flows,
admit Markov partitions that relate the flow to the flow under a Hólder
continuous function over a topologically mixing subshift of finite type
(see Bowen
[
16
]
,
[
15
]
, and more recent survey Chernov
[
17
]
).
Gibbs measures for the flow correspond to Gibbs measures on the subshift of finite type,
and the local product structure for the latter is well known.
∎

and set
\epsilon_{0}=|I|/N^{n_{2}}
.
We argue to find an
\alpha\in I
satisfying the claim.

We will consider a nested sequence of intervals

I=J_{0}=I_{0}\supset J_{1}\supset I_{1}\supset J_{2}\supset I_{2}\supset\dots

satisfying

|I_{n}|=\frac{|I|}{N^{n}},\quad|J_{n}|=\frac{3|I|}{N^{n}},

where

and the sequence is constructed inductively as follows.
Fix

and assume that

and

were already constructed.
Divide the interval
I_{n}
into
N
equal
length subintervals

,
I_{n,2}
,…,
I_{n,N}
with indices
corresponding to the order. Consider blocks of consecutive triples

J_{n,i}=I_{n,i-1}\cup I_{n,i}\cup I_{n,i+1}
and
consider an index
2\leq i_{0}\leq N-1
such that
\nu(J_{n,i_{0}})
is minimal among the
\nu(J_{n,i})
. Now set
J_{n+1}=J_{n,i_{0}}
and
I_{n+1}=I_{n,i_{0}}
.
Finally, we let
\alpha\in I
be the intersection point:
\{\alpha\}=\bigcap I_{n}
.

We now fix
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
. All things are in place for proving the claim, we are just left to show (
8.3
).
Fix

.
Running over

, the union of the
N-2
intervals
J_{n,i}
covers

with multiplicity
at most
3
, thus the average measure of the intervals
J_{n,i}
is bounded from above by
\frac{3}{N-2}\cdot\nu(I_{n})
and in particular,

\nu(J_{n+1})=\nu(J_{n,i_{0}})\leq\frac{3}{N-2}\cdot\nu(I_{n})\leq\frac{3}{N}\cdot\nu(J_{n}).

By induction we get that

\nu(J_{n})\leq\left(\frac{3}{N}\right)^{n}\cdot\nu(I).

We let
n_{3}
to be the unique integer satisfying

|I|/N^{n_{3}+1}<\epsilon\leq|I|/N^{n_{3}}
.
Note that
for
n\leq n_{2}
,
|I|/N^{n}\geq|I|/N^{n_{2}}=\epsilon_{0}\geq\epsilon
, thus

and we get

\left(\frac{N}{3}\right)^{n_{3}}\cdot\frac{1}{(N^{n_{3}+1})^{c}}=\left(\frac{N^{1-c(1+1/n_{3})}}{3}\right)^{n_{3}}\geq\left(\frac{N^{1-c(1+1/n_{1})}}{3}\right)^{n_{2}}>\frac{\nu(I)}{|I|^{c}}.

By
\epsilon\leq|I|/N^{n_{3}}=|I_{n_{3}}|
we get that
(\alpha-\epsilon,\alpha+\epsilon)\subset J_{n_{3}}
, therefore

\displaystyle\nu\left((\alpha-\epsilon,\alpha+\epsilon)\right)\leq\nu(J_{n_{3}})\leq\left(\frac{3}{N}\right)^{n_{3}}\cdot\nu(I)\leq\frac{|I|^{c}}{(N^{n_{3}+1})^{c}}<\epsilon^{c},

showing (
8.3
), thus proving the claim.

Now let
\nu
be a finite Borel measure on a metric space
(S,d)
,
point
p\in S
, and
0<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
two radii.
Choose a closed subinterval
I\subset(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
and apply
the claim to the pushforward of
\nu

to
[0,\infty)
by the map
x\mapsto d(p,x)
to deduce Lemma.
∎

Remark 8.11
.

In the proof below, the most subtle part is showing that the maps
\tau_{+}
and
\tau_{-}
, which are easily constructed (Lemma
8.9
), are actually isomorphisms.
This is where using the Greg
(X^{\prime},\mathcal{X}^{\prime},m^{\prime},T^{\prime},w^{\prime},\Gamma)
from §
8.3
is insufficient and the reason for us to use Lemma
8.5
from §
8.2
and construct
(X,\mathcal{X},m,T,w,\Gamma)
.
The technicalities are hidden in the proof of Lemma
8.8
and the lemmas preceding it.

Proof.

We identify both
(\Gamma^{\mathbb{Z}},\widetilde{m}^{1})
and
(T^{1}\widetilde{M},\bar{m}_{1})
with
(X\times\Gamma,m\times m^{1}_{\Gamma})
.
This provides the isomorphism
\sigma
.
The map

X\times\Gamma\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,\partial^{2}\widetilde{M},\quad(x,g)\mapsto(g\widehat{x}_{-},g\widehat{x}_{+})

coincides with the maps
\operatorname{pr}_{\bowtie}
and
\operatorname{pr}_{\bowtie}\circ\sigma
under these identifications of.
Considered as a map from
\Gamma^{\mathbb{Z}}
, by Lemma
8.9
, it is given explicitly by

(w_{n}(x)g^{-1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mapsto g.\left(\lim_{n\to-\infty}w_{n}(x)^{-1},\lim_{n\to+\infty}w_{n}(x)^{-1}\right).

Since the limits are shift invariant, the map
\operatorname{pr}_{\bowtie}\circ\sigma
factors through
the space
(E,\eta)
of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-ergodic components of
(\Gamma^{\mathbb{Z}},\widetilde{m}^{1})
, giving rise to the map
\tau
.

We claim that
\tau
is a measure space isomorphism.
Indeed, assume

\tau\left((w_{n}(x)g^{-1})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)=\tau\left((w_{n}(y)h^{-1})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right).

Then
g\widehat{x}_{\pm}=h\widehat{y}_{\pm}
, and therefore
\widehat{y}_{\pm}=(h^{-1}g)\widehat{x}_{\pm}
.
Thus
h^{-1}g
maps the unparametrized oriented geodesic line
(\widehat{x}_{-},\widehat{x}_{+})
to the unparametrized oriented geodesic line
(\widehat{y}_{-},\widehat{y}_{+})
.
It follows that there exists a time shift
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that for every
t

(h^{-1}g)\widetilde{\phi}^{t+s}(\widehat{x})=\widetilde{\phi}^{t}(\widehat{y})
.
In particular,
\widehat{y}=(h^{-1}g)\widetilde{\phi}^{s}(\widehat{x})
.
This implies that there exists
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
so that

s=r_{k}(x),\qquad h^{-1}g=w_{k}(x),\qquad y=T^{k}x.

Therefore

w_{n+k}(x)g^{-1}=w_{n}(T^{k}x)w_{k}(x)g^{-1}=w_{n}(y)h^{-1}\qquad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}})

which means that such two points are related by a

{\mathbb{Z}}
-shift.
Hence the measure space map
\tau:(E,\eta)\to(\partial\widetilde{M},\overline{m}^{1})

is an isomorphism as claimed.

In a similar fashion to the construction of
\tau
, Lemma
8.9
is used to construct
\tau_{+}
and
\tau_{-}
.
Indeed, the maps

(\Gamma^{{\mathbb{Z}}_{\pm}},\widetilde{m}_{\pm})\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\,\partial\widetilde{M}
:

\left(w_{n}(x)^{-1}g^{-1}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\pm}}\mapsto g.\lim_{n\to\pm\infty}w_{n}(x)^{-1}=g\widehat{x}_{\pm}

are shift invariant, thus factor via
(E_{\pm},\eta_{\pm})
.
We are left to show that
\tau_{\pm}
are measure space isomorphisms.
We will do this for
\tau_{+}
, the other case being similar.

Suppose

\tau_{+}\left((w_{n}(x)g^{-1})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\right)=\tau_{+}\left((w_{n}(y)h^{-1})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\right).

That is, assume
g\widehat{x}_{+}=h\widehat{y}_{+}
for
x,y\in X
,
g,h\in\Gamma
.
Then
\widehat{x}_{+}=g^{-1}h\widehat{y}_{+}
, thus by Lemma
8.8
there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that for every
n
big enough,
w_{n+k}(y)=w_{n}(x)g^{-1}h
.
It follows that the two sequences
(w_{n}(x)g^{-1})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}

and
(w_{n}(y)h^{-1})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}
are equivalent
by the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
-action on
\Gamma^{{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}
, and so define
the same point in
E_{+}
. This proves that
\tau_{+}
is an isomorphism
and completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 8.12
.

We have equivalence of measures

\overline{m}^{1}_{\bowtie}\sim\overline{\mu}^{1}_{\bowtie}\quad\textrm{on}\quad\partial^{2}\widetilde{M},\qquad\overline{m}^{1}_{\pm}\sim\overline{\mu}^{1}_{\pm}\quad\textrm{on}\quad\partial\widetilde{M}.

Proof.

Recall that
\overline{m}^{1}_{\bowtie}
and
\overline{\mu}^{1}_{\bowtie}

are obtained from
\widetilde{m}^{1}
and
\widetilde{\mu}^{1}
, respectively,
via the pushforward by the map
p_{\bowtie}:T^{1}\widetilde{M}\to\partial^{2}\widetilde{M}
.
Consider the convolution
\widetilde{m}^{1}_{[0,\epsilon_{0}]}
of
the measure
\widetilde{m}^{1}
on
\widetilde{X}\subset T^{1}\widetilde{M}

with the normalized Lebesgue measure on
[0,\epsilon_{0}]
via
\widetilde{\phi}^{t}
, defined by

\int_{T^{1}\widetilde{M}}f\,{\rm d}\widetilde{m}^{1}_{[0,\epsilon_{0}]}=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{0}}\int_{\widetilde{X}}f(\widetilde{\phi}^{t}(u))\,{\rm d}\widetilde{m}^{1}(u)\,{\rm d}t\qquad(f\in C_{c}(T^{1}\widetilde{M}).

Then
\widetilde{m}^{1}_{[0,\epsilon_{0}]}
is absolutely continuous with respect to

\widetilde{\mu}^{1}
, yet its pushforward via
p_{\bowtie}
coincides with that of

\widetilde{m}^{1}
. This shows

\overline{m}^{1}_{\bowtie}=(p_{\bowtie})_{*}\widetilde{m}^{1}=(p_{\bowtie})_{*}\widetilde{m}^{1}_{[0,\epsilon_{0}]}\preceq(p_{\bowtie})_{*}\widetilde{\mu}^{1}=\overline{\mu}^{1}_{\bowtie}.

Now take some probability distribution
\theta
on
[0,\infty)

with everywhere positive density and define the convolution

\widetilde{m}^{1}_{\theta}
in a similar fashion.
Then we get
\widetilde{\mu}^{1}\preceq\widetilde{m}^{1}_{\theta}
and comparing the pushforward
to
\partial^{2}\widetilde{M}
using
p_{\bowtie}
,
we get
\overline{\mu}^{1}_{\bowtie}\preceq\overline{m}^{1}_{\bowtie}
.
Therefore
\overline{m}^{1}_{\bowtie}\sim\overline{\mu}^{1}_{\bowtie}
.
The other cases follow similarly.
∎

We have now established measure space isomorphisms and equality of measure classes

\begin{split}&(E,\eta)\cong(\partial^{2}\widetilde{M},\overline{m}^{1}_{\bowtie})\sim(\partial^{2}\widetilde{M},\overline{\mu}^{1}_{\bowtie}),\\
&(E_{\pm},\eta_{\pm})\cong(\partial\widetilde{M},\overline{m}^{1}_{\pm})\sim(\partial\widetilde{M},\overline{\mu}^{1}_{\pm}).\end{split}

Therefore the local product property of
\mu
,

\overline{\mu}^{1}_{\bowtie}\sim\overline{\mu}^{1}_{-}\times\overline{\mu}^{1}_{+}
,
becomes the Apafic condition

\eta\sim\eta_{-}\times\eta_{+}
on the Greg
(X,\mathcal{X},m,T,w,\Gamma)
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
8.1
.

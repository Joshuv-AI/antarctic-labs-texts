Crystalloid vs colloid for Hemodynamics during Anesthesia INduction in TAVR patients (CHAIN-T): study protocol for a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Xiaoshuang Tuo, Fahan Wang, Youjun Chen, Min Chen, Zhihong Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo assess whether preload with colloids could reduce hypotension during induction in patients undergoing transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) compared to crystalloids.MethodsThis study is a single-center, prospective, randomized, double-blind, parallel-controlled trial conducted at Xijing Hospital. A total of 116 patients scheduled for elective TAVR under general anesthesia will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the colloid group or the crystalloid group, receiving either 5 ml/kg of colloid or crystalloid solution before anesthesia induction infused over 15 min. The primary outcome is the area under curve (AUC) of the change in mean arterial pressure (MAP) from baseline during the induction period (until 15 min after induction). Secondary outcomes include the incidence of intraoperative hemodynamic instability, the use of intraoperative vasoactive drugs, the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score on the first postoperative day, postoperative hospital length of stay, the rate of intensive care unit (ICU) admission postoperatively, all-cause mortality during hospitalization and by 30 days after surgery, and the incidence of renal replacement therapy by 30 days after surgery.DiscussionThe results of this study will provide evidence on optimizing fluid preload to prevent hypotension during induction, reduce postoperative complications, and enhance recovery for patients undergoing TAVR. The findings are expected to promote anesthesia protocols in this patient population.Trial registrationThe study protocol and statistical analysis plan were approved by the institutional Ethics Committee of Xijing Hospital, Fourth Military Medical University (XJLL-KY-20252560). The study was registered in ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT07329660) on December 29, 2025.

Introduction

Background and rationale {9a}

Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) has now become the standard treatment for patients with severe aortic stenosis or regurgitation who are at high surgical risk or ineligible for surgery [1, 2]. Since the first successful procedure in 2002 [3], TAVR has evolved rapidly. Initially reserved for high-risk patients, it is now increasingly performed in intermediate- and low-risk populations [4]. Early landmark trials demonstrated that TAVR was non-inferior to surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) in high-risk patients. Subsequently, evidence has shown that TAVR is non-inferior across all risk strata and, in some cases, superior [5, 6].

Patients with severe aortic stenosis or regurgitation are often elderly and have multiple comorbidities, such as hypertension, coronary artery disease, heart failure, and renal insufficiency, leading to poor cardiovascular reserve [7]. During the anesthesia induction for TAVR, hypotension is common due to vasodilation and myocardial depression caused by anesthetic agents [8]. Severe hypotension can lead to insufficient coronary artery perfusion, potentially triggering myocardial ischemia [9, 10], malignant arrhythmias [11, 12], or cardiac arrest. It may also impair perfusion to vital organs such as the brain or kidneys and is strongly associated with increased postoperative complications and mortality [13].

Volume status is crucial for maintaining hemodynamic stability [14]. Preloading fluid prior to anesthesia induction can effectively expand blood volume, increase cardiac preload, and thereby attenuate anesthesia-induced hemodynamic changes [15, 16]. Pre-induction fluid infusion can reduce the incidence of post-induction hypotension by more than 50%, decrease the need for vasoactive drugs, and improve cardiac index (CI) and stroke volume variation (SVV) in the early intraoperative period [17]. Colloids provide a nearly 1:1 intravascular volume expansion effect, whereas crystalloids expand plasma volume by only 20% to 25% due to their distribution into the extracellular space [18]. During acute severe hypovolemic shock [19], both crystalloids and colloids help stabilize hemodynamics, but colloids require significantly lower volumes. Patients receiving colloids also tend to have lower heart rates, which is more favorable for myocardial oxygen balance. One study [20] confirmed that rapid plasma volume expansion with colloids before anesthesia induction can effectively prevent hemodynamic fluctuations and maintain the balance between oxygen delivery and consumption during gastrointestinal surgery.

Explanation for the choice of comparator {9b}

During crystalloid fluid infusion, approximately 50% to 60% of the volume is retained within the plasma compartment [21]. Upon cessation of crystalloid infusion, the plasma concentration peaks immediately and then rapidly distributes in proportion to the extracellular fluid space, declining to 15% to 20% at 30 min after finishing infusion [18]. In contrast, colloids exhibit no distribution phase and possess an intravascular half-life of 2 to 3 h, conferring a more sustained and stable effect [22]. The molecular diameter of the principal components of colloid solutions approximates or exceeds capillary pore size, enabling their retention by the vascular endothelial glycocalyx and basement membrane [23]. This generates effective colloid osmotic pressure, which retains water molecules within the intravascular space. Consequently, during preloading prior to anesthesia induction, colloid fluids provide stable and sustained preload support because of their superior volume-expanding capacity, prolonged intravascular persistence, and non-attenuating profile, thereby substantially enhancing hemodynamic stability during the induction phase. In summary, we contend that colloid preloading confers significantly superior hemodynamic stability compared with crystalloid preloading of TAVR patients. Existing clinical evidence is predominantly derived from non-cardiac surgical populations [24, 25] and lacks direct comparisons specifically targeting TAVR patients. It remains unclear whether the choice of fluid types for preloading affects hemodynamic stability during induction in this specific population. Therefore, a dedicated clinical study is needed to address this gap.

Objectives {10}

The objective of this study is to compare the effects of preload with 5 ml/kg of colloid versus crystalloid before anesthesia induction on hemodynamics during the induction period in TAVR patients, as well as to compare intraoperative hemodynamic stability, the requirement for vasoactive drugs, and postoperative outcomes.

Methods: participants, interventions, and outcomes

Eligibility criteria for participants {14a}

Inclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria include patients aged 18 years or older and scheduled for elective TAVR under general anesthesia.

Exclusion criteria

Patients with any of the following conditions will be excluded: allergy to hydroxyethyl starch and/or its components and/or sodium lactate Ringer’s solution, or a history of related allergies; severe cardiac dysfunction (ejection fraction < 35%); severe renal dysfunction (creatinine > 132 μmol/L and/or receiving renal replacement therapy); morbid obesity (body mass index (BMI) > 37.5 kg/m2, or > 32.5 kg/m2 with concomitant metabolic disease); hepatic dysfunction (AST and/or ALT > 80 IU/L); severe electrolyte disturbance (Na⁺ ≥ 160 mmol/L or ≤ 120 mmol/L); intracranial hypertension requiring dehydration therapy; expected postoperative hospital stay < 24 h; and scheduled for multiple surgeries during this hospitalization.

Intervention and comparator

Intervention and comparator description {15a}

Colloid group

Patients will receive 5 mL/kg of hydroxyethyl starch 130/0.4 sodium chloride injection (Voluven@, Beijing Fresenius Kabi Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) before anesthesia induction. The solution will be infused via an infusion pump over 15 min, after which routine anesthesia induction will be performed.

Crystalloid group

Patients will receive 5 mL/kg of sodium lactate Ringer’s injection (Sichuan Meida Kangjiale Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) before anesthesia induction. The solution will be infused via an infusion pump over 15 min, after which routine anesthesia induction will be performed.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated intervention/comparator {15b}

If any serious adverse event is identified during the study, the investigator or relevant site staff must report it to the clinical monitor and the ethics committee within 24 h and follow up until the adverse event is resolved.

Concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {15d}

During the anesthesia evaluation, TAVR patients who meet the inclusion criteria are screened, and the investigator introduces the study details and invites the patients to participate in the study. Routine monitoring and intravenous access are established prior to anesthesia. The corresponding fluid intervention is implemented followed by anesthesia induction. Intraoperatively, anesthetic maintenance drugs are continuously infused via pump, with the infusion rate adjusted according to the bispectral index (BIS). Hemodynamic measurements will be recorded during anesthesia, and vasoactive drugs are judiciously administered based on the patient’s specific vital signs and the surgeon’s requests to maintain hemodynamic stability as stable as possible. At the end of surgery, anesthetics are discontinued, and muscle relaxant reversal is administered as appropriate. Endotracheal extubation is performed when indicated. Then the patient is transferred to the ward or intensive care unit (ICU) as needed.

Ancillary and post-trial care {34}

Compensation

The interventions in this study are all routine clinical treatments. For participants who suffer harm resulting from their participation in the trial, necessary medical care and compensation will be provided in accordance with the relevant governing laws and regulations of China.

Primary outcome

The area under the curve (AUC) of the change in mean arterial pressure from baseline during the induction period.

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes include (1) the incidence of hemodynamic instability after induction, defined as MAP < 65 mmHg or a decrease of more than 20% from baseline within 15 min after induction, (2) intraoperative use of vasoactive agents, (3) Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score on postoperative day 1, (4) postoperative hospital length of stay, (5) rate of postoperative ICU admission, (6) in-hospital all-cause mortality, (7) all-cause mortality by 30 days postoperatively, (8) proportion of patients requiring renal replacement therapy by 30 days postoperatively.

Participant timeline {18}

Sample size {19}

Based on previous study [27], assuming that AUC of MAP relative to baseline within 15 min after induction is − 8505 (7272.8) mmHg s in the control group, the intervention group is expected to achieve a 15% improvement in AUC compared with the control group. With two-sided α of 0.05 and β of 0.20 (80% power), the sample size is calculated to be 50 patients per group. Accounting for a 10% safety margin and an anticipated dropout rate of 5%, the final sample size is 58 patients per group, for a total of 116 patients.

Recruitment {20}

During the anesthesia evaluation, TAVR patients who meet the inclusion criteria are screened, and the investigator introduces the study details and invites the patients to participate in the study.

Assignment of interventions: randomization

Sequence generation: who will generate the sequence {21a}

Participants will be randomly allocated to two groups in a 1:1 ratio using computer-generated randomization sequences created by PASS software.

Sequence generation: type of randomization {21b}

Block randomization with block sizes of 4 or 6 will be performed.

Allocation concealment mechanism {22}

To ensure allocation concealment, the randomization sequence will be sealed in opaque envelopes. The fluid packages will be put in opaque bags.

Implementation {23}

At admission in the operating room, an independent researcher otherwise not involved in the study will open the envelope and administer the corresponding intervention.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {24a}

The patients, anesthesiologists, data recorders, statistical analysts, and follow-up personnel will be blinded to group allocation.

How will be blinding be achieved {24b}

To ensure allocation concealment, the randomization sequence will be sealed in opaque envelopes. The fluid packages will be put in opaque bags.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {24c}

Only in an emergency (e.g., the subject experiences a serious adverse event or a drug allergy) and when immediate knowledge of the subject’s group is necessary, will an emergency unblinding be permitted by a pre-authorized researcher in this study.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {25a}

During the postoperative hospital stay, follow-up is conducted through postoperative visits, collection of the patient’s various laboratory test results, and gathering of the surgeon’s medical orders. After discharge, patients are followed up by phone.

The mean arterial pressure (MAP) is continuously recorded by the DoCare Information System (Medicalsystem Co.) and averaged every 30 s.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {25b}

During the postoperative hospital stay, follow-up is conducted through postoperative visits, collection of the patient’s various laboratory test results, and gathering of the surgeon’s medical orders. After discharge, patients are followed up by phone.

Data management {26}

Data entry is performed using dual entry with both electronic case report forms (CRFs) and paper CRFs, carried out by two individuals. For any discrepancies in the entered results, verification and corrections are made item by item against the electronic medical record system.

Confidentiality {33}

All participant data will be de-identified after collection and identified using unique participant codes. Data containing personal identification information will be stored separately and access will be strictly controlled.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {27a}

The primary endpoint is compared with Mann–Whitney U test. Secondary outcomes are considered as exploratory and thus do not include any multiplicity adjustment. The multiple imputation method will be used to account for missing data on the primary outcome.

Who will be included in each analysis {27b}

Data analyses are primarily performed in a modified intention-to-treat (mITT) population, which includes all randomly assigned participants who initiate the study intervention and do not withdraw consent.

How missing data will be handled in the analysis {27c}

The multiple imputation method will be used to account for missing data on the primary outcome.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {27d}

Sensitivity analysis will include analysis of the primary outcome in the per-protocol (PP) set (those who finished the study). Subgroup analysis of the primary outcome based on type of surgery, gender, age, and presence of comorbid coronary artery disease will be done.

Interim analyses {28b}

Interim analysis is not designed.

Discussion

This study will be the first prospective, randomized, double-blind, parallel-controlled trial to investigate the impact of different preload strategies on hemodynamics specifically in TAVR patients. Our findings will provide evidence on whether colloid or crystalloid preloading should be used before anesthesia induction in TAVR patients.

This study hypothesizes that in patients undergoing TAVR, preloading with colloid solution, compared with an equivalent volume of crystalloid solution, can significantly reduce the AUC for the change in MAP from baseline during anesthesia induction, thereby attenuating hemodynamic fluctuations during the induction period. Furthermore, preloading with colloid solution is expected to lower the incidence of secondary outcomes, including dose of vasoactive agents and postoperative complications.

A previous study has confirmed that the infusion of 4 ml/kg of crystalloid solution within 5 min can reliably increase the mean systemic filling pressure, thereby effectively influencing the cardiovascular system [28]. Another study on endotracheal intubation in the ICU recommended 500 ml of balanced crystalloid solution infused over 15 min for preloading which is applicable to patients without contraindications who require semi-elective or delayed emergency procedures [29]. Based on these findings, we select a dose of 5 ml/kg for preloading, as it significantly improves hemodynamic stability without causing cardiac overload, making it both an effective and safe dose.

During the preloading phase, a resolute researcher is responsible for implementing the intervention, while the anesthesiologists are blinded to the intervention to prevent any influence on subsequent anesthesia induction and intraoperative vital signs management, thereby ensuring the objectivity and reliability of the study results. The primary endpoint of this study is the AUC of the change in MAP from baseline during the induction period, which can comprehensively reflect the overall blood pressure fluctuation during induction and offer advantages over single timepoint measurements.

To address potential issues with missing data in this trial, we prespecified the use of multiple imputation to minimize bias caused by missing data. Additionally, by comparing the results with those from complete case analysis in sensitivity analyses, we can further assess the potential impact of missing data on the conclusions. Furthermore, this study is conducted in the mITT population, excluding cases that are found not meeting the inclusion or exclusion criteria after randomization, thereby making the results more targeted for interpretation in the target population.

This study has several limitations. First, as a single-center study, it may not represent the characteristics and clinical practices of a broader population, thus limiting the generalizability of the findings. Second, the follow-up period is limited to 30 days after surgery, while sufficient to observe short-term efficacy and immediate complications, it is inadequate to assess the impact of the intervention on patients’ long-term outcomes. Finally, the study population is racially homogeneous, which may restrict the applicability of the conclusions to other racial or ethnic groups. Multi-center studies with long-term follow-up and diverse populations are needed to further validate our findings in the future.

In conclusion, the results of this trial are expected to clarify whether colloid or crystalloid solutions are more effective in maintaining hemodynamic stability during anesthesia induction in TAVR patients.

Dichotomy in the small-time asymptotics of spectral heat content for Lévy processes
Jaehun Lee, Hyunchul Park

Abstract
We establish a dichotomy in the small-time asymptotic behavior of the spectral heat content (SHC) for symmetric, but not necessarily isotropic, Lévy processes whose Lévy density satisfies a weak lower scaling condition near zero. This dichotomy is governed by whether the process has unbounded or bounded variation. In the unbounded variation case, the leading asymptotic behavior of the SHC is determined by the expected supremum of the process projected in the normal direction near the boundary. In contrast, for processes with bounded variation, the SHC decays linearly in time. Our main result, Theorem 2.1 , extends and unifies key results from [ 8 ] , [ 10 ] , and [ 14 ] , covering a broader class of non-isotropic Lévy processes and offering a streamlined proof.

Introduction

The spectral heat content (SHC) represents the total heat that remains in a domain
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
at time
t>0
with the Dirichlet boundary condition and a unit initial condition.
The heat equation involves the Laplacian, which is the infinitesimal generator of Brownian motions.
If the Brownian motions are replaced by other Lévy processes, one obtains the spectral heat content for those Lévy processes.
In the past decade, there were extensive interests on SHC for jump processes (see
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
and references therein).
The most well-known example was investigated in
[
14
]
, where the authors studied the small-time asymptotic behavior of rotationally invariant (isotropic) stable processes (SSP) on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
In their main result, they proved that there are three different regimes for the small-time asymptotic behavior of SHC for SSP, where the three regimes are determined by the stability index of SSP.

The purpose of this paper is to prove that there is a dichotomy in the small-time asymptotic behavior of SHC for a large class of Lévy processes. We consider Lévy processes with Lévy density
J(x)
that is comparable to
\frac{1}{|x|^{d}\psi(|x|)}
, where
\psi(x)
satisfies so-called weak lower scaling condition near zero.
Within this class, we demonstrate that the small-time asymptotics of SHC exhibit two distinct regimes, determined by whether the underlying process X has unbounded or bounded variation.
The condition that
X
has unbounded variation is that either it has a non-trivial diffusion part (
A\neq 0
), or there are enough small jumps (
\int_{0+}\frac{dr}{\psi(r)}=\infty
).
In the unbounded variation case, the leading contribution to the heat loss arises from particles near the boundary exiting the domain in the direction of the outward normal—this being the most efficient escape route. The asymptotic behavior of the SHC in this regime is given by an integral over the boundary involving the expected minimum of the supremum of the one-dimensional projection of the process in the normal direction and a fixed constant (e.g., 1), with respect to the surface measure.
In contrast, when
X
has bounded variation (
A=0
and
\int_{0+}\frac{dr}{\psi(r)}<\infty
), particles located throughout the domain can still exit by time
t
with linear probability in
t
. As a result, the SHC decays at rate
t
, and all regions of the domain contribute to the leading term. In this case, the leading coefficient is given by the so-called perimeter of
D
with respect to the process
X
.
To illustrate the usefulness of the main theorem, we consider the following example (see Theorem
2.1
, Remark
2.2
, and Section
4
for proof).

Example 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a
C^{1,1}
open set.
Let
\Psi(\xi)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\log^{\beta}(1+|\xi|^{\gamma})
be a characteristic exponent of a Lévy process

in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
\alpha,\alpha+2\beta,\gamma\in(0,2)
. If
\alpha\in[1,2)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{|D|-Q_{D}^{X}(t)}{{\mathbb{E}}[\overline{Y}_{t}\wedge 1]}=|\partial D|,

where
Y
is (any) one-dimensional projection of X onto a one-dimensional subspace,
\overline{Y}_{t}:=\sup_{0<s\leq t}(Y_{s}-Y_{0})
is its supremum process, and

is the perimeter of
D
.
If
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{|D|-Q_{D}^{X}(t)}{t}=\text{Per}_{X}(D),

where
\text{Per}_{X}(D)=\int_{D}\int_{D^{c}}J(y-x)dydx
is the perimeter of

with respect to

Let us highlight the contribution of this paper into the existing literature.
Firstly, as mentioned above, we show that the dichotomy exists in the asymptotic behavior of SHC for Lévy processes.
In particular, Theorem
2.1
shows that the three different regimes in the main result of
[
14
]
is in fact two regimes (see Remark
2.2
).
Secondly, our main theorem covers SHC for non-isotropic processes, whereas most known results for SHC were done for isotropic processes.
Thirdly, when the process
X
has unbounded variation, the main theorem, Theorem
2.1
, can cover the critical case when the one-dimensional projection is not integrable.
To the best of authors’ knowledge, the only known example where the small-time asymptotic behavior of SHC is known for this class of Lévy process is the Cauchy process (1-stable process) in
[
14
]
.
However, the proof in
[
14
]
relied on a highly sophisticated argument involving comparisons between subordinate killed and killed subordinate stable processes and required an upper bound for the transition density of the supremum process of the Cauchy process.
These detailed information for other processes is not available until now and there seems to be little hope that the argument in
[
14
]
can be applied to obtain the asymptotic behavior of the SHC for the critical case.
A generalization of
[
14
]
into more general processes than SSP was done in
[
10
]
, but processes there have a regularly varying exponent with index in
(1,2]
which does not cover the critical case mentioned above.
Finally, the proof of Theorem
2.1
is more robust than those in
[
10
]
or
[
14
]
, and it applies to a broader class of processes, including those with jump densities of variable order (see Remark
2.2
and Section
4

We outline the main ideas behind the proof of our main result. The first part of Theorem
2.1
is when the process has unbounded variation, and it follows a similar path as
[
10
]
, where the authors studied SHC for Lévy processes with the regularly varying characteristic exponent with an index in
(1,2]
, corresponding to the case where projections are integrable.
However, since we aim to provide a robust proof that is applicable for all processes that have unbounded variation, the main techniques employed differ significantly from those in
[
10
]
.
The crucial ingredients for proofs are the near-diagonal lower bound for the heat kernel (Proposition
5.1
), the lower and upper bounds for the exit probabilities from balls (Lemma
5.2
), and corresponding results for the projections onto subspaces (Corollary
5.5
).
While these estimates and bounds are similar to those in
[
2
,
4
]
, we only assume the
lower weak scaling condition near zero
on the jump density, whereas two-sided weak scaling condition was imposed in
[
2
,
4
]
. Additionally, the underlying processes may include a diffusion component, requiring a tailored derivation of the results.
Using these heat kernel estimates and upper and lower bounds for the exit probabilities from balls, we prove three key lemmas, Lemmas
3.2
,
3.3
, and
3.4
, where we prove the small-time asymptotics of events of exiting from half-spaces, inner balls, and complement of outer balls of the underlying

The event of exiting from inner balls or complement of outer balls could arise in two different scenarios.
The main contribution occurs when the projected process with respect to the normal vector exits the domain, which is the most efficient way of exiting, and this contributes the main term.
On the other hand, there is an alternative way of exiting, which happens when the projection onto the orthogonal complement exits the domain. Since
C^{1,1}
open set satisfies the
R
-ball condition in (
2.5
), the event can be controlled by those associated with the inner ball and the complement of outer ball.
Along the subspace orthogonal to the normal direction, the projected process has to move much longer distance, at least the square root of the distance of the most efficient path, and from estimates for the exit probabilities from balls in Corollary
5.5
, this is at most in
O(t)
, which is negligible compared to the main contribution from the most efficient exit.
Finally, these estimates from Lemmas
3.2

3.3
, and
3.4
are combined to prove Theorem
2.1
using Lemma
3.5
, which is an integration-based version of the pointwise estimate of
[
18
, Lemma 5]
, specifically tailored to suit our needs.

For the second part of Theorem
2.1
, we assume that
X
has bounded variation, in which case a related result is already available in
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
. We show that under this assumption, the process
X
does not hit the boundary of
D
upon exiting the domain. This property allows us to apply
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
to establish the desired asymptotic behavior.

Preliminaries

Let
X=\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a Lévy process in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
.
It follows from the Lévy-Khintchine theorem (
[
16
, Theorem 8.1]
)

{\mathbb{E}}[e^{i\langle\xi,X_{t}\rangle}]=e^{-t\Psi(\xi)},\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

with

\Psi(\xi)=-i\langle\xi,\gamma\rangle+\langle\xi,A\xi\rangle-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(e^{i\langle\xi,x\rangle}-1-i\langle\xi,x\rangle 1_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}\right)\nu(\mathrm{d}x),

where
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
A
is a symmetric, nonnegative-definite matrix, and
\nu
is a measure satisfying
\nu(\{0\})=0
and
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(1\wedge|x|^{2})\nu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty
.
The law of
X
is uniquely characterized by the characteristic exponent
\Psi(\xi)
, and the triplet
(A,\gamma,\nu)
is called the Lévy triplet of
X
.
Throughout this paper, we assume that
X=\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a symmetric Lévy process corresponding to the triplet
(A,0,J(x)\mathrm{d}x)
, where
A
is non-degenerate or
A=0
. The key assumptions of this paper are as follows.

(A)

Lower weak scaling condition near zero

There exist a constant
R_{0}\in(0,\infty]
, an increasing function
\psi:(0,R_{0}]\to(0,\infty)
and constants
0<C_{1}\leq C_{2}
such that

\frac{C_{1}}{|x|^{d}\psi(|x|)}\leq J(x)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{|x|^{d}\psi(|x|)},\qquad|x|\leq R_{0}.

(2.1)

Moreover, the following lower weak scaling condition near zero on
\psi
holds: there exist constants
\alpha\in(0,2]
and
C_{\psi}\in(0,1]
such that for any
0<r\leq R<R_{0}
,

\frac{\psi(R)}{\psi(r)}\geq C_{\psi}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\alpha}.

(2.2)

We impose one of the following assumptions.

(B1)

Processes with unbounded variation

The process
X
has unbounded variation. It follows from
[
16
, Theorem 21.9]
and the assumption (
2.1
) that the process has unbounded variation if and only if the following conditions hold.

\int_{0}^{R_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\psi(r)}=\infty\quad\mbox{or}\quad A\mbox{ is non-degenerate,}

(2.3)

(B2)

Processes with bounded variation

The process
X
has unbounded variation. Again, it follows from
[
16
, Theorem 21.9]
and the assumption (
2.1
) that this is equivalent to the following.

\int_{0}^{R_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\psi(r)}<\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad A=0.

(2.4)

We note that once (
2.2
) holds for some
\alpha\in(0,2]
, it holds for any
\beta<\alpha
with the same constant
C_{\psi}
.
Also, we remark that when
\alpha>1
, the first part of (

) is a consequence of (

) since
\frac{C_{\psi}R_{0}^{\alpha}}{\psi(R_{0})}r^{-\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{\psi(r)}
for

.
However, the maximal value

\overline{\alpha}=\overline{\alpha}(\psi):=\sup\{\alpha\in(0,2]:\exists\,C_{\psi}\in(0,1]\,\,\mbox{such that}\,\,\eqref{e:psi}\,\,\mbox{holds with some }C_{\psi}\mbox{ and }\psi\}.

can be less or equal to

(see Example

(v)), even though

An open set
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is said to be
C^{1,1}
if there exist
R_{1}>0
and
\Lambda>0
such that for every
z\in\partial D
, there exist a
C^{1,1}
function
\phi=\phi_{z}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
\phi(0)=0
,
\nabla\phi(0)=(0,\dots,0)

. It is well known that any bounded

with characteristics

satisfies the following uniform interior and exterior ball-condition: There exists
R=R(d,R_{1},\Lambda)>0
such that for any

, there exist open balls
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
of radius
R
such that

B_{1}\subset D,\,\,B_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{D}\mbox{ and }\partial B_{1}\cap\partial B_{2}=\{z\}.

(2.5)

We say that
D
satisfies the
R
-ball condition
if (

) holds with

.

Let
D
be an open set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and let
\tau^{X}_{D}=\inf\{t>0:X_{t}\notin D\}
be the first exit time of the Lévy process

The spectral heat content

Q^{X}_{D}(t)=\int_{D}{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\tau_{D}^{X}>t)\mathrm{d}x.

As the survival probability

is the unique solution for the (non-local) heat equation with the Dirichlet

and unit initial condition, the spectral heat content

represents the total heat that remains in
D
at time
t>0
when the initial temperature of
D
is one and the temperature of
D^{c}
is kept identically zero.

A projected process
X^{\nu}=\{X^{\nu}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
onto a line generated by

is a one-dimensional Lévy process defined by

, where
\nu\in{\bf S}^{d-1}:=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:|y|=1\}
.
For a one-dimensional stochastic process

, we define the supremum process
\overline{Y}=\{\overline{Y}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
by
\overline{Y}_{t}:=\sup_{0<s\leq t}(Y_{s}-Y_{0})
.

.

Moreover, when

t=o({\mathbb{E}}[\overline{X^{\nu(y)}}_{t}\land 1])

1.

Since

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
D
be a bounded

open set satisfying the
R
-ball condition.
Let
a\in(0,R/2)
. Then, for any bounded function

,

\left(\frac{R-a}{R}\right)^{d-1}\leq\frac{\int_{D\setminus D_{a}}f(x)\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\partial D}f(y-s\nu(y))S(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}s}\leq\left(\frac{R}{R-a}\right)^{d-1},

where
S(\mathrm{d}y)
is the surface measure of
\partial D
, and
\nu(y)
is the outer unit normal vector at
y\in\partial D
such that
x=y-r\nu\in D\setminus D_{a}
.

Proof.
Let
d(x)=dist(x,\partial D)
. It follows from the coarea formula that for any
f\in L^{1}(\overline{D\setminus D_{a}},\mathrm{d}x)

\int_{D\setminus D_{a}}f(x)|\nabla d(x)|\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\partial D_{q}}f(y)S_{q}(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}q,

where
S_{q}(\mathrm{d}y)
is surface measure of
\partial D_{q}
.
Since
\nabla d(x)=\nu(y)
, we have
|\nabla d(x)|=1
for all
x\in D\setminus D_{a}
. Thus, for
f\in L^{1}(\overline{D\setminus D_{a}},\mathrm{d}x)
,

\int_{D\setminus D_{a}}f(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\partial D_{q}}f(y)S_{q}(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}q.

(3.19)

Since
D_{s}
satisfies
(R-s)
inner and outer ball conditions, by
[
18
, Equations (33) and (34)]
we have

|{\rm div}\,\nu(x)|\leq\frac{d-1}{R-s},\qquad x\in\partial D_{s}.

(3.20)

From now on, we follow the proof of
[
18
, Lemma 5]
.
Fix
0<q\leq a
,
u:\partial D_{q}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a bounded function defined on
\partial D_{q}
, and let
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of
C^{1}
functions on
\partial D_{q}
converging to
u
.
Define a sequence of
C^{1}
-function
g_{n}:D\setminus D_{q}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by
g_{n}(y-s\nu):=u_{n}(y-q\nu)
,
y\in\partial D
and
s\in(0,q)
.
For any
0\leq q_{1}<q_{2}\leq q
, using (
3.19
) and the divergence theorem for
g_{n}
on
D_{q_{1}}\setminus D_{q_{2}}
, we obtain

\int_{D_{q_{1}}\setminus D_{q_{2}}}\langle\nabla g_{n}(x),\nu(x)\rangle\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{D_{q_{1}}\setminus D_{q_{2}}}g_{n}(x){\rm div}\,\nu(x)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial D_{q_{2}}}g_{n}(y)S_{q_{2}}(\mathrm{d}y)-\int_{\partial D_{q_{1}}}g_{n}(y)S_{q_{1}}(\mathrm{d}y).

(3.21)

Since
\langle\nabla g_{n},\nu\rangle\equiv 0
by the definition of
g_{n}
, we obtain from (
3.19
), (
3.20
), and (
3.21
)

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{\partial D_{q_{1}}}g_{n}(y)S_{q_{1}}(\mathrm{d}y)-\int_{\partial D_{q_{2}}}g_{n}(y)S_{q_{2}}(\mathrm{d}y)\Bigg|=\left|\int_{D_{q_{1}}\setminus D_{q_{2}}}g_{n}(x){\rm div}\,\nu(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}}\int_{\partial D_{s}}g_{n}(y){\rm div}\,(y)S_{s}(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq\int_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}}\frac{d-1}{R-s}\Big(\int_{\partial D_{s}}g_{n}(y)S_{s}(\mathrm{d}y)\Big)\mathrm{d}s.

(3.22)

For
s\in[0,q]
, let
h_{n}(s):=\int_{\partial D_{s}}g_{n}(y)S_{s}(\mathrm{d}y)
. Then, by (
3.22
),

\left|h_{n}(q_{1})-h_{n}(q_{2})\right|\leq\left|\int_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}}\frac{d-1}{R-s}h_{n}(s)\mathrm{d}s\right|.

Therefore,
|\frac{d}{\mathrm{d}s}\log h_{n}(s)|\leq\frac{d-1}{R-s}
, which implies

\big(\frac{R-q}{R}\big)^{d-1}h_{n}(0)\leq h_{n}(q)\leq\big(\frac{R}{R-q}\big)^{d-1}h_{n}(0).

Since
h_{n}(0)=\int_{\partial D}g_{n}(y)S(dy)=\int_{\partial D}u_{n}(y+q\nu)S(dy)
and
h_{n}(q)=\int_{\partial D_{q}}u_{n}(y)S_{q}(dy)
,
it follows from the bounded convergence theorem that for any

,

\big(\frac{R-q}{R}\big)^{d-1}\int_{\partial D}u(y+q\nu)S(\mathrm{d}y)\leq\int_{\partial D_{q}}u(y)S_{q}(\mathrm{d}y)\leq\big(\frac{R}{R-q}\big)^{d-1}\int_{\partial D}u(y+q\nu)S(\mathrm{d}y).

(3.23)

Therefore, for any bounded function

, by integrating both sides of (
3.23
) and using (
3.19
) we have

\int_{0}^{a}\big(\frac{R-a}{R}\big)^{d-1}\int_{\partial D}f(y+s\nu)S(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{D\setminus D_{a}}f(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\partial D_{q}}f(y)S_{q}(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}s,

and

\int_{D\setminus D_{a}}f(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{\partial D_{q}}f(y)S_{q}(\mathrm{d}y)ds\leq\int_{0}^{a}\big(\frac{R}{R-a}\big)^{d-1}\int_{\partial D}f(y+s\nu)S(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}s.

This establishes the conclusion of this lemma.

Proposition 5.1

r^{\alpha},&\qquad r>R_{0},\end{cases}

3.3
). Consider a symmetric pure-jump Lévy process

whose Lévy measure

) with the function

) with the constants

. Also, from

[

and

(5.3)

Moreover, the constants

and
\delta

and

.

Step 2.
Let

, where

is a Brownian motion in

which is independent of

. We claim that (
5.2
) holds for
X^{2}
. The case

By

, there is

and

(5.4)

Recall that by (
3.2
) we have
2\int_{0}^{r}\frac{sds}{\psi(s)}\leq 2\int_{0}^{R_{0}}\frac{sds}{\psi(s)}\leq c_{3}
for

is a constant depends on

,

,

and

(5.5)

Moreover, taking

(5.6)

Denote

) for
X^{1}
we have for any
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

p_{X^{1}}^{B(x_{0},r)}(t,x,y)\geq c_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1}(t)^{-d},\qquad r>0,\,\,0<t\leq\phi_{1}(\delta_{1}r),\,\,x,y\in B(x_{0},\delta_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1}(t)).

(5.7)

Since

and
\phi_{1}^{-1}(t)\leq\phi^{-1}(t)

5.7
) holds for

and

.
It follows from (
5.4
) and (
5.6
), there is
\delta_{2}\in(0,1)
such that

p^{B(x_{0},r)}_{\widetilde{B}}(t,x,y)\geq c_{4}\phi^{-1}(t)^{-d},\qquad 0<r\leq R_{0},\,\,0<t\leq\phi(\delta_{2}r),\,\,x,y\in B(x_{0},\delta_{2}\phi_{1}^{-1}(t)).

(5.8)

Let
\delta_{3}=\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}\land\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}
. Then, for any
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
r\leq R_{0}
,
0<t\leq\phi(\delta_{3}r)
and
x,y\in B(x_{0},\delta_{3}\phi^{-1}(t))
, it follows from (
5.7
) and (
5.8
), together with the fact that

\{\tau^{X^{1}}_{B(X^{1}_{0},r/2)}>t\}\cap\{\tau^{\widetilde{B}}_{B(\widetilde{B}_{0},r/2)}>t\}\subset\{\tau^{X^{2}}_{B(X^{2}_{0},r)}>t\},

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(x_{0},\delta_{2}\phi_{1}^{-1}(t))}p^{B(x_{0},r/2)}_{X^{1}}(t,x,z)p^{B(x_{0},r/2)}_{\widetilde{B}}(t,z,y)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(x_{0},\delta_{2}\phi_{1}^{-1}(t))}c_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1}(t)^{-d}\cdot c_{4}\phi^{-1}(t)^{-d}\mathrm{d}z\geq c_{5}\phi^{-1}(t)^{-d}.

Step 3.
Let
X^{3}
be the
R_{0}
-truncated process of
X^{2}
, i.e., the Lévy triplet of

is given by
(A,0,J_{1}(\mathrm{d}x){\bf 1}_{|x|\leq R_{0}})
.

to decompose

into the truncated process

and a compound Poisson process whose jump sizes are larger than

Define

Then,

is a Poisson random measure with the intensity measure

\nu((0,t]\times E)):=tJ_{1}(E\cap B(x,R_{0})^{c})
for

. Hence,

X^{2}_{t}=Y_{t}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}xj(\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}x):=Y_{t}+Z_{t},

where

and

are independent, and

and

are identical in law.

For any
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
r\leq R_{0}/2
,

, and a Borel set

, we have

{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\{Y^{B(x_{0},r)}_{t}\in F\}\cap\{Z_{t}=0\})={\mathbb{P}}_{x}(X^{2,B(x_{0},r)}_{t}\in F),

where

is the killed process of

upon

.
Indeed, since

, we observe that

if there exists a jump whose size is larger than

. Therefore, since

and

are identical in law, for any

,

, and

{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(X^{3,B(x_{0},r)}_{t}\in F)={\mathbb{P}}_{x}(Y^{B(x_{0},r)}_{t}\in F)\geq{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(X^{2,B(x_{0},r)}_{t}\in F),\quad x\in B(x_{0},r),\,\,F\subset B(x_{0},r).

Thus, for any
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
r\leq r_{0}
, and
0<t\leq\phi(\delta_{3}r)
,

p_{X^{3}}^{B(x_{0},r)}(t,x,y)\geq p_{X^{2}}^{B(x_{0},r)}(t,x,y)\geq c_{6}\phi^{-1}(t)^{-d},\quad x,y\in B(x_{0},\delta_{3}\phi^{-1}(t)).

(5.9)

Step 4.
Since
X^{3}
is also
R_{0}
-truncated process of

, by a similar way as before, we obtain

{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\{X^{3,B(x_{0},r)}_{t}\in F\}\cap\{\widetilde{Z}_{t}=0\})={\mathbb{P}}_{x}(X^{B(x_{0},r)}_{t}\in F),

where
\widetilde{Z}
corresponds to a compound Poisson process independent of

with the intensity measure
\widetilde{\nu}
with
\widetilde{\nu}((0,t]\times E)=tJ(E\cap B(x,R_{0})^{c})
for

.
In particular, by (
3.1
) we have
{\mathbb{P}}(\widetilde{Z}_{t}=0)\geq 1-tJ(B(0,R_{0})^{c})\geq 1-c_{7}t
. Since

and
\widetilde{Z}
are independent, for any
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
t>0
,
r\leq R_{0}/2
,

, and a Borel set

{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(X^{B(x_{0},r)}_{t}\in F)\geq{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\{X^{3,B(x_{0},r)}_{t}\in F\}){\mathbb{P}}(\widetilde{Z}_{t}=0)\geq(1-c_{7}t){\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\{X^{3,B(x_{0},r)}_{t}\in F\}).

Now we choose

.
Then, using (

5.2

Let

) and (

(ii)

where

Proof.
First, we establish
\rm{(i)}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that

since

\displaystyle J_{H}(B_{H}(0,r)^{c})=J(\pi_{H}^{-1}(B_{H}(0,r)^{c}))\leq J(B(0,r)^{c}).

Thus, by Lemma
3.1
we have
J_{H}(B_{H}(0,r)^{c})\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\psi(r)}
for any

.

For the lower bound, consider

C:=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}):r\leq x_{i}\leq 2r,\,\,1\leq i\leq d\}\subset\pi_{H}^{-1}(B_{H}(0,r)^{c}).

Note that since

\pi_{H}(C)=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{d_{1}},0,0,\dots,0):r\leq x_{i}\leq 2r,\,\,1\leq i\leq d_{1}\}\subset B_{H}(0,r)^{c},

We have

\displaystyle J_{H}(B_{H}(0,r)^{c})

\displaystyle\geq J(C)=\int_{r}^{2r}\dots\int_{r}^{2r}\int_{r}^{2r}\frac{C_{1}}{|x|^{d}\psi(|x|)}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}x_{d}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{1}r^{d}}{(2\sqrt{d}r)^{d}\psi(2\sqrt{d}r)}\geq\frac{c_{2}}{\psi(2\sqrt{d}r)}.

Now we prove
\rm{(ii)}
. Let
r_{0}
and
\delta
be the constants in (
5.2
). For any
r\leq r_{0}
,
0<t\leq\phi(\delta r)
,
x^{0}=(x^{0}_{1},\dots,x^{0}_{d})\in H
,

and any Borel set

\displaystyle\int_{A}p^{B_{H}(x^{0},r)}_{X^{H}}(t,x,y)\mathrm{d}y={\mathbb{P}}_{x}(X^{H,B_{H}(x^{0},r)}_{t}\in A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(X_{t}^{\pi_{H}^{-1}(B_{H}(x^{0},r))}\in\pi_{H}^{-1}(A))\geq{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(X_{t}^{B(x^{0},r)}\in\pi_{H}^{-1}(A)),

where
X^{H,B_{H}(x^{0},r)}
is the killed process of
X^{H}
upon
B_{H}(x^{0},r)
.
Let
F:=\prod_{i=d_{1}+1}^{d}B_{{\mathbb{R}}}(x^{0}_{i},\delta\phi^{-1}(t)/\sqrt{d})
be a box centered in
(x_{d_{1}+1}^{0},\cdots,x_{d}^{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-d_{1}}
. Then, for any
A\subset H
we have
A\times F\subset\pi_{H}^{-1}(A)\cap B(x^{0},\delta\phi^{-1}(t))
. Thus by (
5.2
) we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(X_{t}^{B(x^{0},r)}\in\pi_{H}^{-1}(A))

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(X_{t}^{B(x^{0},r)}\in A\times F)=\int_{A\times F}p_{X}^{B(x^{0},r)}(t,x,y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq c_{3}\phi^{-1}(t)^{-d}|A\times F|=c_{4}\phi^{-1}(t)^{-d_{1}}|A|.

Define
\delta_{1}=\delta/\sqrt{d}
. Then, since
\delta_{1}<\delta
, (
5.15
) follows from the above estimate, the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, and the joint continuity of the Dirichlet heat kernel.

Note that every constant in this proof is independent of nontrivial subspace
H
.

\Box

5.2
and
5.4
, we conclude that

.

be a symmetric Lévy process satisfying (
2.1
) and (

). Also, assume that
A=0
or

is non-degenerate. Then, there exist constants

(5.16)

Linear Perturbations and Stability Analysis in f ⁡ ( T ) f(T) Braneworld Scenario
Ju-Ying Zhao, Mao-Jiang Liu, Ke Yang

Abstract
We conduct a detailed analysis of the full linear perturbations in the braneworld scenario within f ⁡ ( T ) f(T) gravity. By decomposing the perturbations of the theory into the scalar, transverse vector, antisymmetric pseudotensor, and symmetric transverse-traceless tensor modes, we derive the quadratic action for each mode. The results indicate that there is a total of one scalar, one massless vector, and one tensor propagating degrees of freedom. Consequently, in comparison to general relativity, no additional degrees of freedom appear under the flat braneworld background in the linearized theory. For a thick brane model with f ⁡ ( T ) = T + α ​ T 2 f(T)=T+\alpha T^{2} , we find that it exhibits stability against linear perturbations.

In

\displaystyle{S_{P}}^{MN}=\frac{1}{2}\left(K^{MN}{}_{P}+\delta^{N}_{P}T_{Q}{}^{QM}-\delta^{M}_{P}T_{Q}{}^{QN}\right).

(2)

\displaystyle T={S_{P}}^{MN}{T^{P}}_{MN}.

3
Linear perturbations in
f(T)
braneworlds

The local Lorentz violation in
f(T)
gravity becomes more evident when the field equation (
6a
) is reformulated as a modified Einstein equation
[
54
]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle f_{T}G_{MN}+\frac{1}{2}g_{MN}\left(f-f_{T}T\right)+f_{TT}S_{MNK}\nabla^{K}T=\mathcal{T}_{MN},

(12)

where
G_{MN}
is the Einstein tensor. When
f(T)=T
, the equation recovers the Einstein equation, which is locally Lorentz invariant. However, for
f(T)\neq T
, the nonvanishing last two terms are not locally Lorentz invariant. Consequently, the broken gauge freedom in the tangent frame introduces additional DOFs in the vielbein.

For the 4D GR, there are a total of 10 DOFs in the metric. In a spatially flat FLRW cosmology background, they can be decomposed into 4 scalars (4 DOFs), 2 transverse vectors (4 DOFs), and a symmetric transverse-traceless (TT) tensor (2 DOFs). Since the scalar, vector, and tensor modes correspond to irreducible representations of the SO(3) group of spatial rotations, this decomposition guarantees that they do not mix in linearized theory
[
55
]
.

However, for the 4D
f(T)
gravity, there are additional 6 DOFs, corresponding to a vector (3 DOFs) and an antisymmetric tensor (3 DOFs). In 3D space, the antisymmetric tensor is dual to a pseudovector, i.e.,
\mathcal{B}_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}B^{k}
with
i,j,k=1,2,3
corresponding to the indices of spatial coordinates. Therefore, as pointed out in Ref.
[
56
]
, the vector and pseudovector can ultimately be decomposed into a scalar (1 DOF), a transverse vector (2 DOFs), a pseudoscalar (1 DOF), and a transverse pseudovector (2 DOFs).

For the 5D
f(T)
gravity, there are 10 additional DOFs in the vielbein. Correspondingly, they can be decomposed into a 4D vector (4 DOFs) and a 4D antisymmetric tensor (6 DOFs). Due to
\mathcal{B}_{\mu\nu}=\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}B^{\alpha\beta}
in 4D spacetime, the antisymmetric tensor is self-dual and can be equivalently expressed as an antisymmetric pseudotensor. Thus, the 10 additional DOFs can ultimately be decomposed into a scalar (
\alpha
, 1 DOF), a transverse vector (
\beta_{\mu}
, 3 DOFs), and an antisymmetric pseudotensor (
B_{\mu\nu}
, 6 DOFs). Consequently, the full perturbed vielbein can be expressed as

\displaystyle e^{a}{}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=a\left[\delta^{a}{}_{\mu}(1-\varphi)+\partial^{a}\partial_{\mu}E+\partial_{\mu}C^{a}+h^{a}{}_{\mu}+\epsilon^{a}{}_{\mu\alpha\beta}B^{\alpha\beta}\right],

(13a)

\displaystyle e^{5}{}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=a\left({\partial_{\mu}F}+G_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\alpha+\beta_{\mu}\right),

(13b)

\displaystyle e^{a}{}_{5}

\displaystyle=-\left(\partial^{a}\alpha+\beta^{a}\right),

(13c)

\displaystyle e^{5}{}_{5}

\displaystyle=1+\psi,

(13d)

where the transverse vectors satisfy
\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}C_{\mu}=\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}G_{\mu}=\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\beta_{\mu}=0
and the transverse-traceless tensor satisfies
\eta^{\mu\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}h_{\mu\nu}=\eta^{\mu\lambda}h_{\mu\nu}=0
.

With the full perturbed vielbein, the perturbed metric at linear order is expressed as

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=a^{2}\left[(1-2\varphi)\eta_{\mu\nu}+2\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}E+\partial_{(\mu}C_{\nu)}+2h_{\mu\nu}\right],

(14a)

\displaystyle g_{\mu 5}

\displaystyle=a\left(\partial_{\mu}F+G_{\mu}\right),

(14b)

\displaystyle g_{55}

\displaystyle=1+2\psi.

(14c)

This is the standard perturbed metric in GR without additional DOFs (
\alpha
,
\beta_{\mu}
, and
B_{\mu\nu}
) associated with Lorentz breaking. Moreover, the corresponding perturbed scalar field is expressed as
\Phi=\bar{\Phi}+\phi
, where
\bar{\Phi}
represents the background scalar.

Although the
f(T)
gravity theory is formulated using orthogonal vielbein fields, as shown in the field equation (
12
), it still utilizes covariant tensors to describe the dynamics of spacetime. Consequently, similar to GR, the action of the
f(T)
gravity remains invariant under the general coordinate transformations
x^{M}\to{x^{\prime}}^{M}=x^{M}+\xi^{M}(x)
. Under the transformations, the vielbein transforms as

\displaystyle\delta e^{A}{}_{M}=-\bar{e}^{A}{}_{N}{\partial_{M}}{\xi^{N}}-\xi^{N}\partial_{N}\bar{e}^{A}{}_{M},

(15)

where
\bar{e}^{A}{}_{M}
denotes the background vielbein. Furthermore, by decomposing the 4D component of
\xi^{M}
as
\xi_{\mu}=a^{2}(\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\|}+\xi^{\perp}_{\mu})
with
\xi^{\perp}_{\mu}
a transverse vector and
\xi^{\|}
a longitudinal scalar, the gauge transformations of the perturbed quantities can be derived as

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=-\bar{\Phi}^{\prime}\xi^{5},~\Delta\psi=-\xi^{5\prime},~\Delta\varphi=H\xi^{5},~\Delta E=-\xi^{\|},~\Delta\alpha=a\left({\xi^{\|}}^{\prime}+2H\xi^{\|}\right),~\Delta F=-a\left({\xi^{\|}}^{\prime}+2H\xi^{\|}\right)-{a^{-1}}{\xi^{5}},~

\displaystyle\Delta\beta_{\mu}=a\left({\xi^{\perp}_{\mu}}^{\prime}+2H\xi^{\perp}_{\mu}\right),~\Delta G_{\mu}=-a\left({\xi^{\perp}_{\mu}}^{\prime}+2H\xi^{\perp}_{\mu}\right),~\Delta C_{\mu}=-\xi^{\perp}_{\mu},~\Delta h_{\mu\nu}=0,~\Delta B_{\mu\nu}=0.

(16)

It is clear that the tensor mode
h_{\mu\nu}
and the pseudotensor mode
B_{\mu\nu}
are gauge invariant. However, 2 scalar modes and 1 vector mode can be directly gauged out by appropriately choosing
\xi^{5}
,
\xi^{\|}
and
\xi^{\perp}_{\mu}
.

The scalar, vector, antisymmetric pseudotensor, and symmetric tensor modes do not mix with each other under the 4D Lorentz transformation, allowing us to treat them separately in linearized theory.

\displaystyle=\frac{2a}{3H^{2}f_{T}^{2}}\Big[f_{T}HV_{\Phi}\phi-f_{T}\bar{\Phi}^{\prime}\left(H^{\prime}\phi+H\phi^{\prime}\right)+24H^{2}f_{TT}H^{\prime}\bar{\Phi}^{\prime}\phi\Big].

(33)

Further, by substituting the results into the quadratic action (
24

\displaystyle\!S^{(2)}_{\text{S}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{M_{*}^{3}}{2}\int{d^{4}xdy}a^{2}\bigg[\partial^{\lambda}\phi{\partial_{\lambda}}{\phi}+a^{2}{\phi^{\prime}}^{2}+a^{2}\bigg(V_{\Phi\Phi}+\frac{16\bar{\Phi}^{{}^{\prime}2}}{3f_{T}}+\frac{8{\bar{\Phi}}^{{}^{\prime}4}}{9H^{2}f_{T}^{2}}+\frac{8\bar{\Phi}^{\prime}\bar{\Phi}^{\prime\prime}}{3Hf_{T}}\bigg)\phi^{2}\bigg].

(34)

Thus, it is evident that there is only one scalar propagating DOF. Specifically, the correct sign of the kinetic term ensures that there is no ghost scalar mode in the linearized theory.

In order to study the mass spectrum of the scalar mode, it is convenient to rewrite the action in the conformal extra dimension coordinate
z
, which relates to the physical extra dimension coordinate as
dy=adz
, yielding

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{\text{S}}=-\frac{M_{*}^{3}}{2}\int{d^{4}xdz}a^{3}\left[\partial^{\lambda}\phi{\partial_{\lambda}}{\phi}+\dot{\phi}^{2}+K(z)\phi^{2}\right],

(35)

where the dot denotes the derivative with respect to the conformal coordinate
z
, and

\displaystyle K(z)=a^{2}V_{\Phi\Phi}+\frac{8\dot{\bar{\Phi}}^{2}}{3f_{T}}+\frac{8\dot{\bar{\Phi}}^{4}}{9H^{2}f_{T}^{2}}+\frac{8\dot{\bar{\Phi}}\ddot{\bar{\Phi}}}{3Hf_{T}},

(36)

with
H\equiv{\dot{a}(z)}/{a(z)}
in
z
coordinate.

By varying the action (
35
) with respect to
\phi
, we obtain the equation of motion

\displaystyle\Box^{(4)}{\phi}+\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}-K\phi=0,

(37)

where
\Box^{(4)}\equiv\eta^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}{\partial_{\beta}}
is the 4D d’Alembert operator.

With a KK decomposition in the form
\phi(x,z)=\hat{\phi}(x)\chi_{\text{S}}(z)a^{-\frac{3}{2}}(z)
, the equation of motion (
37
) can be further decomposed into a Klein-Gordon equation
\Box^{(4)}\hat{\phi}=m^{2}\hat{\phi}
and a Schrödinger-like equation

\displaystyle-\ddot{\chi}_{\text{S}}(z)+U_{\text{S}}(z)\chi_{\text{S}}(z)={m^{2}}\chi_{\text{S}}(z),

(38)

where
m
is the mass of scalar KK modes, and the effective potential reads

\displaystyle U_{\text{S}}(z)=\frac{9}{4}H^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\dot{H}+K.

(39)

Since the mass spectrum is entirely governed by the effective potential, it is essential to analyze the behavior of this potential. 3.2
Vector perturbations

For the vector perturbations, we have the gauge freedom
\xi^{\perp}_{\mu}
to eliminate the mode
C_{\mu}
in the perturbed vielbein, yielding

\displaystyle e^{A}{}_{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a\delta^{a}{}_{\mu}&-\beta^{a}\\
a\left(G_{\mu}+\beta_{\mu}\right)&1\\
\end{array}\right).

Using the perturbed vielbein, we can derive the nonvanishing components of the torsion tensor up to second order in perturbations:

\displaystyle T^{5}{}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=a\left({\partial_{[\mu}}{G_{\nu]}}+{\partial_{[\mu}}{\beta_{\nu]}}\right),

(42)

\displaystyle T^{5}{}_{5\mu}

\displaystyle=-T^{5}{}_{\mu 5}=a\left(G_{\mu}^{\prime}+\beta_{\mu}^{\prime}\right)-\left(G^{\lambda}+\beta^{\lambda}\right){\partial_{\mu}}{\beta_{\lambda}},

(43)

\displaystyle T^{\rho}{}_{5\mu}

\displaystyle=-T^{\rho}{}_{\mu 5}=H\delta^{\rho}{}_{\mu}+{a^{-1}}{\partial_{\mu}}{{\alpha^{\rho}}}+\alpha^{\rho}\left(G_{\mu}^{\prime}+\alpha_{\mu}^{\prime}\right),

(44)

\displaystyle T^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\rho}\left({\partial_{[\mu}}{G_{\nu]}}+{\partial_{[\mu}}{\alpha_{\nu]}}\right).

(45)

Then, the perturbed torsion scalar is given by

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=-12H^{2}-6{a^{-1}}H\partial^{\lambda}\beta_{\lambda}-12H^{2}\left(G^{\lambda}G_{\lambda}-\beta^{\lambda}\beta_{\lambda}\right)+\frac{1}{2a^{2}}\left(\partial^{\lambda}G^{\rho}{\partial_{\lambda}}{G_{\rho}}-\partial^{\lambda}G_{\rho}\partial^{\rho}G_{\lambda}-2\partial^{\lambda}\beta_{\lambda}\partial^{\rho}\beta_{\rho}+2\partial^{\lambda}\beta_{\rho}\partial^{\rho}\beta_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle-6H\left(G^{\lambda}G_{\lambda}^{\prime}+G^{\lambda}\beta_{\lambda}^{\prime}+G_{\lambda}^{\prime}\beta^{\lambda}+\beta^{\lambda}\beta_{\lambda}^{\prime}\right).

(46)

From the action (
4
), after integrating by parts and dropping the boundary terms, the quadratic action for vector perturbations is given by

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{\text{V}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{M_{*}^{3}}{4}\int{d^{4}xdy}a^{4}\bigg[\frac{1}{2}a^{-2}f_{T}\partial^{\lambda}G^{\rho}{\partial_{\lambda}}{G_{\rho}}+\Big(3H^{\prime}f_{T}-72H^{2}H^{\prime}f_{TT}+2{\Phi^{\prime}}^{2}\Big)G^{\lambda}G_{\lambda}+\big(f+4V+3H^{\prime}f_{T}

\displaystyle+24H^{2}f_{T}-72H^{2}f_{\text{TT}}H^{\prime}\big)\alpha^{\lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}+\Big(f+4V+2{\Phi^{\prime}}^{2}+6H^{\prime}f_{T}+24H^{2}f_{T}-144H^{2}f_{TT}H^{\prime}\Big)G^{\lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}\bigg].

(47)

By using the background equations (
9
), the action can be further simplified as

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{\text{V}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{M_{*}^{3}}{8}\int{d^{4}xdy}a^{2}f_{T}\partial^{\lambda}G^{\rho}{\partial_{\lambda}}{G_{\rho}}.

(48)

It is clear that the sign of the kinetic term is entirely determined by
f_{T}
. When
f_{T}
is positive, the vector mode is ghost-free. Conversely, when
f_{T}
is negative, it corresponds to a ghost mode.

For the solution (
10
), the function
f_{T}
is given by

\displaystyle f_{T}(y)=1+2\alpha T=1-24\alpha b^{2}k^{2}\tanh^{2}(ky),

(49)

which is always positive. We can define
\mathcal{G}={\sqrt{f_{T}}}G/{2}
to achieve a canonically normalized form in the conformal coordinate
z
:

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{\text{V}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{M_{*}^{3}}{2}\int{d^{4}xdz}a^{3}\partial^{\lambda}\mathcal{G}^{\rho}{\partial_{\lambda}}{\mathcal{G}_{\rho}}.

(50)

In this case, the vector mode is massless and robust.

3.3
Pseudotensor perturbation

For the pseudotensor perturbation, the perturbed vielbein reads

\displaystyle e^{A}{}_{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a\left(\delta^{a}{}_{\mu}+\epsilon^{a}{}_{\mu\alpha\beta}B^{\alpha\beta}\right)&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right).

After some straightforward algebra, the nonvanishing components of the perturbed torsion tensor are expressed as

\displaystyle T^{\rho}{}_{5\mu}

\displaystyle=H\delta^{\rho}{}_{\mu}+{\epsilon^{\rho}}_{\mu\tau\kappa}{B^{\tau\kappa}}^{\prime}+{{\epsilon}^{\alpha\rho}}_{\sigma\omega}{\epsilon_{\alpha\mu\tau\kappa}}B^{\sigma\omega}{B^{\tau\kappa}}^{\prime},

(53)

\displaystyle T^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}\!

\displaystyle=\!\epsilon^{\rho}{}_{\nu\tau\kappa}{\partial_{\mu}}{B^{\tau\kappa}}-\epsilon^{\rho}{}_{\mu\tau\kappa}{\partial_{\nu}}{B^{\tau\kappa}}+{{\epsilon}^{\alpha\rho}}_{\sigma\omega}\epsilon_{\alpha\nu\tau\kappa}B^{\sigma\omega}{\partial_{\mu}}{B^{\tau\kappa}}-{{\epsilon}^{\alpha\rho}}_{\sigma\omega}\epsilon_{\alpha\mu\tau\kappa}B^{\sigma\omega}{\partial_{\nu}}{B^{\tau\kappa}}.

(54)

Then, the perturbed torsion scalar reads

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=-12H^{2}-6H\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\sigma\omega}{{\epsilon}_{\alpha\beta\tau\kappa}}B^{\sigma\omega}{{B^{\tau\kappa}}}^{\prime}+a^{-2}\epsilon^{\rho}{}_{\beta\sigma\omega}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\tau\kappa}\left({\partial_{\alpha}B^{\sigma\omega}}{\partial_{\rho}B^{\tau\kappa}}-{\partial_{\alpha}B^{\tau\kappa}}{\partial_{\rho}B^{\sigma\omega}}\right).

(55)

From the action (
4
), after integrating by parts and dropping the boundary terms, the quadratic action for pseudotensor perturbation is given by

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{\text{PT}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{M_{*}^{3}}{2}\int{d^{4}xdy}a^{4}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\sigma\omega}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\tau\kappa}\bigg[\left(\frac{f}{4}+\frac{{\Phi^{\prime}}^{2}}{2}+V\right)B^{\sigma\omega}B^{\tau\kappa}-3Hf_{T}B^{\sigma\omega}{B^{\tau\kappa}}^{\prime}\bigg].

(56)

By using the background equation (
9b
) and the identity
\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\sigma\omega}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\tau\kappa}=2\left(\eta_{\kappa\sigma}\eta_{\tau\omega}-\eta_{\tau\sigma}\eta_{\kappa\omega}\right)
, the action can be further simplified as

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{\text{PT}}

\displaystyle=2{M_{*}^{3}}\int\!\!{d^{4}xdy}a^{4}\bigg[\bigg(36H^{2}H^{\prime}f_{TT}-\frac{3}{2}H^{\prime}f_{T}-6H^{2}f_{T}\bigg)B_{\alpha\beta}B^{\alpha\beta}-3Hf_{T}B_{\alpha\beta}{B^{\alpha\beta}}^{\prime}\bigg].

(57)

After integrating the last term by parts and neglecting the boundary terms, it exactly cancels the first term in the action. Therefore, the vanishing Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{\text{PT}}=0
implies that, up to second order in perturbations, there is no propagating pseudotensor mode in the theory.

3.4
Tensor perturbation

For the tensor perturbation, the perturbed vielbein reads

\displaystyle e^{A}{}_{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a\left(\delta^{a}{}_{\mu}+h^{a}{}_{\mu}\right)&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right).

Consequently, the nonvanishing components of the torsion tensor, up to second order in perturbations, are calculated as follows:

\displaystyle T^{\rho}{}_{5\mu}

\displaystyle=-T^{\rho}{}_{\mu 5}=H\delta^{\rho}{}_{\mu}+h^{\rho}{}_{\mu}^{\prime}-h^{\rho}{}_{\lambda}h^{\lambda}{}_{\mu}^{\prime},

(60)

\displaystyle T^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle={\partial_{[\mu}}{h^{\rho}{}_{\nu]}}-h^{\rho}{}_{\lambda}{\partial_{[\mu}}{h^{\lambda}{}_{\nu]}}.

(61)

Moreover, the perturbed torsion scalar is obtained as

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=-12H^{2}+{a^{-2}}(\partial^{\lambda}h^{\alpha\beta}{\partial_{\lambda}}{h_{\alpha\beta}}-{\partial^{\lambda}}{h^{\alpha\beta}}{\partial_{\alpha}}{h_{\lambda\beta}})+{h^{\alpha\beta}}^{\prime}h_{\alpha\beta}^{\prime}+6Hh^{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}^{\prime}.

(62)

With the action (
4
) and the background equation (
9b
), after integrating by parts and neglecting the boundary terms, the quadratic action for tensor perturbations is obtained as

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{\text{T}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{M_{*}^{3}}{4}\int{dx}^{4}dya^{2}f_{T}\left(\partial^{\lambda}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\lambda}h_{\alpha\beta}+a^{2}{h^{\alpha\beta}}^{\prime}h^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}\right).

(63)

Similar to the vector perturbations, the sign of the kinetic term is determined by
f_{T}
. Therefore, the tensor perturbation is ghost-free for the solution (
10
).

By varying the action with respect to
h^{\alpha\beta}
, one obtains the equation of motion for the tensor mode as

\displaystyle f_{T}\left(\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\prime\prime}+4H\gamma_{\alpha\beta}+a^{-2}\Box^{(4)}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\right)-24HH^{\prime}f_{{TT}}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\prime}=0,

(64)

which was first presented in Ref.
[
42
]
. In the conformal coordinate
z
, the equation can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{z}^{2}+\mathcal{K}\partial_{z}+\Box^{(4)}\right)\gamma_{\mu\nu}=0,

(65)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\equiv 3H+\frac{24f_{TT}}{a^{2}f_{T}}\left(H^{3}-H\dot{H}\right).

(66)

With a KK decomposition
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}=\hat{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}(x)\chi_{\text{T}}(z)e^{-\int{}\frac{\mathcal{K}}{2}}dz
, the equation of motion (
64
) can be decomposed into a Klein-Gordon equation
\Box^{(4)}\hat{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}=m^{2}\hat{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}
and a Schrödinger-like equation

\displaystyle-\ddot{\chi}_{\text{T}}(z)+U_{\text{T}}(z)\chi_{\text{T}}(z)={m^{2}}\chi_{\text{T}}(z),

(67)

where
m
is the mass of KK gravitons, and the effective potential is given by

\displaystyle U_{\text{T}}(z)=\frac{\dot{\mathcal{K}}}{2}+\frac{\mathcal{K}^{2}}{4}.

(68)

The Hamiltonian can be factorized as a supersymmetric quantum mechanics form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=-\partial_{z}^{2}+U_{\text{T}}=A^{\dagger}A=\left(\partial_{z}+\frac{\mathcal{K}}{2}\right)\left(-\partial_{z}+\frac{\mathcal{K}}{2}\right).

(69)

Given a Neumann boundary condition
\partial_{z}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}|_{z\to\pm\infty}=0
, it can be shown that the eigenvalues
m^{2}
are non-negative
[
59
]
. Consequently, there are no tachyonic modes in the tensor perturbation, indicating that the theory is stable against the tensor perturbation.

For the tensor KK modes, it is easy to check that there is a localized massless graviton, whose wave function reads

\displaystyle\chi_{\text{T}}=N_{0}e^{\int{}\frac{\mathcal{K}}{2}dz},

(70)

where
N_{0}
is the normalization factor. The massless mode refers to the 4D graviton on the brane. As shown in Refs.
[
42
,
39
]
, the massless mode is localized on the brane, and all the massive KK modes are continuous free states.

4
Conclusions

In this work, we investigated the full linear perturbations of the thick brane scenario in
f(T)
gravity. Due to the local Lorentz violation in
f(T)
gravity, the theory possesses more DOFs compared to GR. In 5D spacetime, there are a total of 25 DOFs in the perturbed vielbein, which can be decomposed into 5 scalars, 3 transverse vectors, 1 antisymmetric pseudotensor, and 1 symmetric transverse-traceless tensor. By appropriately choosing the gauge, we calculated the quadratic action for each mode and analyzed their stability.

Working in the gauge
E=\delta^{(1)}T=0
, we found that there is only one propagating DOF among the scalar perturbations. For the vector perturbations, our research indicates that there is only one massless propagating DOF, and its stability is entirely determined by the sign of
f_{T}
. When
f_{T}
is positive, the vector mode is stable. In contrast, when
f_{T}
is negative, the vector mode suffers from the ghost instability. Interestingly, we found that there is no propagating DOF in the pseudotensor perturbation. For the tensor perturbation, it represents a propagating DOF, and its stability is also determined by the sign of
f_{T}
, similar to the case of the vector mode. For the thick brane solution (
10
) with
f(T)=T+\alpha T^{2}
, all the scalar, vector, pseudotensor, and tensor perturbations are stable.

As is well known, due to the relation
R=-T+2\nabla_{P}T_{Q}{}^{QP}

[
31
]
, teleparallel gravity is dynamically equivalent to GR. When
f(T)=T
,
f(T)
gravity reduces to teleparallel gravity, and consequently, to GR. In this case, it can be directly verified that for the flat brane solutions in GR, such as the sine-Gordon solution
[
13
]
, our results indicate the presence of continuous massive scalar modes, a massless vector mode, and both massless and continuous massive tensor modes, with all perturbations being stable. These findings are consistent with the analysis of thick brane stability in GR
[
12
,
60
,
61
,
62
]
. Interestingly, for flat braneworlds in
f(R)
gravity, the scalar perturbations are stable, and the stability of the vector and tensor perturbations is similarly determined by the sign of
f_{R}
. When
f_{R}
is positive, the vector and tensor perturbations are also stable
[
57
,
58
]
.

The propagating DOFs in the
f(T)
braneworld consist of one scalar mode, one massless vector mode, and one tensor mode. Therefore, under the flat braneworld background (
8
), no additional DOFs appear in the linearized theory compared to the case of GR. This conclusion is consistent with the findings from the study of linear perturbations in
f(T)
gravity cosmology
[
56
]
, which also observed that no additional DOFs emerge at the linear level. In general, there are four additional DOFs for the 5D
f(T)
gravity, as noted by Ref.
[
63
]
. These additional DOFs may emerge at the nonlinear level or in less symmetric braneworld backgrounds. However, this is beyond the scope of the current paper and will be left for future investigation.

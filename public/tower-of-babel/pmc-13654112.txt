Utilization of 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT improves the gross tumor volume delineation in nasopharyngeal carcinoma within a safe radiation dose to organs at risk
Authors: Yiqiao Luo, Xiao Zhong, Huaju Yang, Jiayi Lv, Jia Luo, Lijiao Pei, Xin Zhang, Huijun Zhou, Minggang Su, Xingchen Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAccurate delineation of the nasopharynx gross tumor volume (GTVnx) of nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is crucial for patients requiring radiotherapy, as it correlates with prognosis. Current GTVnx delineation methods have significant shortcomings, often resulting in insufficient coverage of the radiotherapy target area. Gallium-68-labeled fibroblast activation protein inhibitor (68Ga-FAPI) is an emerging and promising tumor imaging agent. This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of combining 68Ga-FAPI positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) for delineating GTVnx in NPC.MethodsThis prospective study enrolled 19 patients with NPC who underwent 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT from August 2022 to August 2023. GTVnx were delineated, quantified, and compared based on enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT, and the fusion of both imaging modalities, and were respectively named and abbreviated as GTVnx-MRI, GTVnx-FAPI, and GTVnx-MRI + FAPI. GTVnx-FAPI was automatically delineated using maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) thresholds, with the optimal threshold determined by the Jaccard index and intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). Radiotherapy plans were developed based on GTVnx-MRI and GTVnx-MRI + FAPI to assess radiation doses to organs at risk (OARs). A paired t-test was employed to compare variables among groups.ResultsNineteen pathologically confirmed NPC patients [mean age, 48.84±12.12 years (range, 24–70 years); 12 men (63.16%)] were enrolled. All included NPC patients demonstrated high 68Ga-FAPI uptake in their primary tumor lesions. The 20% SUVmax on 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT of the primary tumor (GTVnx-FAPI20%) was determined to be the most suitable threshold for guiding GTVnx-FAPI with an ICC of 0.891. The GTVnx-MRI + FAPI20% was significant lager than both GTVnx-MRI and GTVnx-FAPI20%, with average measurements of 24.92±17.31, 17.44±13.12, and 16.29±11.15 cm3, respectively (P<0.001). In the radiotherapy plan based on GTVnx-MRI + FAPI20%, radiation dose to all OARs was not increased significantly compared to that based on GTVnx-MRI.ConclusionsThe combined use of 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT shows potential to enhance the delineation of GTVnx in NPC patients while maintaining OAR doses within acceptable limits.Keywords: Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC), nasopharynx gross tumor volume (GTVnx), organs at risk protection (OAR protection), gallium-68-labeled fibroblast activation protein inhibitor (68Ga-FAPI), positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT)

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is prevalent in southern China and Southeast Asia, with an annual incidence of approximately 9 per 100,000 individuals compared to 1 per 100,000 in the US (1,2). Radiotherapy, particularly intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT), is a primary treatment for NPC and can significantly improve prognosis when applied appropriately (3,4).

Precise delineation of the gross tumor volume (GTV) is essential for radiotherapy planning, ensuring treatment accuracy and effectiveness (5). It is a complex process influenced by multiple variables and heavily dependent on the radiation oncologist’s expertise (6,7). To enhance GTV delineation, medical image processing software is routinely used in clinical practice to register multiple imaging modalities. By accurately aligning these image datasets within the same spatial context, clinicians can delineate GTV on a comprehensive image, optimising each modality’s strengths while minimising its limitations.

Currently, enhanced computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) are the primary imaging modalities for delineating nasopharynx gross tumor volume (GTVnx) for NPC, with 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) used occasionally (5). Although IMRT effectively treats NPC and significantly improves prognosis, the 5-year recurrence rate remains high, ranging from 5% to 30%, depending on tumor staging and accompanying treatments (8). Recurrences typically occur within the radiation field and tumor margins, particularly near normal tissues (9). This may result from insufficient radiation dose and concealed edge lesions, indicating inadequate dose and coverage in the local area. Therefore, developing complementary imaging approaches to enhance the definition of GTVnx is crucial.

Previous studies indicate that combining MRI with 18F-FDG PET/CT for GTV delineation can improve local control and disease-free survival in certain patients with malignant tumors (10-12). However, in the head and neck, 18F-FDG exhibits higher background uptake and a greater false-positive rate (13-15), compromising the accuracy of GTVnx determination with 18F-FDG PET/CT. This is particularly problematic in NPC, where elevated 18F-FDG uptake in the brain impairs the effectiveness of GTVnx delineation in patients with skull base and intracranial brain invasion.

Gallium-68-labeled fibroblast activation protein inhibitor (68Ga-FAPI) is a promising imaging agent that targets fibroblast activation protein (FAP) overexpressed in cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) (16,17). It offers a higher target-to-background ratio and low brain uptake (15,18). Previous studies demonstrate that 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT offers distinct advantages over 18F-FDG PET/CT in visualising primary tumors, metastatic lymph nodes and distant metastases in NPC patients (13,14). Preliminary studies indicate that 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT can enhance the delineation of GTVnx in head and neck cancer (5,19). But in the radiotherapy plan, it is essential not only to effectively increase GTVnx but also to control the radiation dose to organs at risk (OARs).

Therefore, this study aims to investigate whether the combined use of 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT can enhance GTVnx delineation in NPC while still protecting OARs. While state-of-the-art radiotherapy techniques have significantly improved local control, the integration of advanced imaging modalities like PET/CT into routine clinical workflows remains challenging due to logistical complexity, cost, and varying institutional protocols (20,21). We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2026-0194/rc).

Methods

Patients’ enrollment

Between August 2022 and August 2023, patients with a pathological diagnosis of NPC who required radiotherapy at West China Hospital were considered potential candidates for this study. Those who voluntarily underwent 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT examination prior to radiotherapy were included. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) patients under 18 years of age; (II) patients with other concurrent malignant tumors or a history of other malignancies; (III) previous neck surgery; (IV) patients who received other anti-cancer treatments within the past 4 weeks; (V) patients unwilling or unable to undergo 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT, enhanced CT, or enhanced MRI due to any reasons, such as contrast agent allergy or severe underlying conditions that preclude examination completion; (VI) 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT images of unsatisfactory quality cannot guide the delineation of GTVnx.

Prior to participation, each patient provided informed consent, having fully comprehended the document’s details. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Biomedical Ethics Review Committee of West China Hospital and was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry under the registration number ChiCTR2300074538.

68Ga-FAPI PET/CT imaging and image analysis

PET/CT images were acquired in accordance with established standard imaging protocols. PET images were acquired 50 to 70 minutes after the intravenous injection of 68Ga-FAPI-04. The administered dose was 2 MBq/kg (0.0541 mCi/kg). The CT scans were performed with a tube voltage of 120 kV, an effective tube current of 70–200 mAs using dose modulation (uMI780, United-Imaging Healthcare), and a slice thickness of 3 mm. PET scans were performed immediately after the CT scan in 3D acquisition mode (matrix: 192×192) with 3 min per bed position. PET data were iteratively reconstructed (2 iterations and 20 subsets) with CT data for attenuation correction. Finally, the PET images were fused with a low-dose CT to improve anatomical location. The PET/CT images were coregistered and displayed using dedicated software (Image Fusion software, United Imaging Healthcare).

The PET/CT images were analyzed using visual and semiquantitative methods by two experienced nuclear medicine physicians. For patients who also underwent 18F-FDG PET/CT for various reasons, we conducted a comparative analysis of the imaging features of 18F-FDG and 68Ga FAPI PET/CT. An area of focal uptake visually higher than that of the surrounding background was considered positive. The maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) of the primary tumor and metastatic lymph nodes for each patient was automatically calculated.

The delineation of GTVnx and radiotherapy planning

An experienced radiation oncologist used the RayStation Doctor 9A system to delineate tumor contours for the included NPC patients based on enhanced MRI in each frame, quantitatively calculating their GTVnx (GTVnx-MRI). Subsequently, the 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT images were integrated into the MIM system (version 7.2.8), utilizing various SUVmax thresholds (15%, 17.5%, 20%, 22.5%, 25%, 27.5%, 30%, 32.5%, 35%, 37.5%, 40%, and 50% SUVmax) to automatically delineate GTVnx (GTVnx-FAPI). The auto-contoured GTVnx-FAPI volumes were used without further manual adjustment. Contrast-enhanced MRI-defined GTVnx (GTVnx-MRI) served as the reference standard for determining the optimal threshold, in accordance with the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines, which recommend contrast-enhanced MRI as the primary imaging modality for primary tumor delineation in NPC. GTVnx-MRI was manually delineated by a radiation oncologist with over 10 years of experience in NPC radiotherapy planning and independently reviewed by a second senior radiation oncologist; any discrepancies were resolved by consensus. Consistency between GTVnx-FAPI and GTVnx-MRI was evaluated using the Jaccard index and intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), and the threshold yielding the highest consistency was selected as optimal. Through consistency analysis with GTVnx-MRI, the optimal SUVmax threshold for GTVnx-FAPI delineation was determined. The 20% SUVmax threshold was chosen based on the highest Jaccard index and ICC (0.891) compared with GTVnx delineated on contrast‑enhanced MRI, the current clinical reference standard for NPC primary tumor evaluation. The 20% SUVmax is deemed the most appropriate threshold for GTVnx-FAPI (GTVnx-FAPI20%). The detailed analysis process is outlined in the “Results” section. Finally, GTVnx-MRI and GTVnx-GTV-FAPI20% were registered and fused onto the localization CT, resulting in the automatic generation of GTV-MRI + FAPI20%. Planning target volume (PTV) margins were 3–5 mm from GTVnx to planning gross tumor volume (PGTVnx) and 5 mm from clinical target volume (CTV) to PTV. All plans were required to meet the dose constraints specified in the RTOG 0615 guidelines.

Radiotherapy plans were developed using the RayStation Doctor 9A System. Each patient’s plan was developed based on their GTVnx-MRI and GTVnx-MRI + FAPI20%, referred to as PLAN-MRI and PLAN-MRI + FAPI20%. In their plans, radiation dose to OARs was calculated using multiple dosimetric parameters, including D99 (minimum dose received by 99% of the OAR volume), D95 (minimum dose received by 95%), D50 (median dose for 50%), and D1 (maximum dose received by 1%).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics software, version 24.0. Data with a normal distribution are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Consistency analysis was conducted using the ICC and Jaccard index. The Jaccard index between GTVnx-MRI and GTVnx-FAPI was calculated as follows: Jaccard index (GTVnx-MRI, GTVnx-FAPI) = | GTVnx-MRI ∩ GTVnx-FAPI | / | GTVnx-MRI ∪ GTVnx-FAPI |. The minimum sample size was calculated using PASS software (https://www.ncss.com/software/pass/) based on the incidence rate of NPC. A paired t-test was utilized to assess differences among groups, with P<0.05 indicating statistical significance.

Comparison of imaging results between 68Ga-FAPI and 18F-FDG PET/CT

The 19 included patients also underwent 18F-FDG PET/CT examinations prior to radiotherapy for various reasons, with a maximum time interval of 7 days from the 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT. All primary NPC lesions demonstrated focal, well‑circumscribed 68Ga‑FAPI uptake, with no diffuse or non‑specific uptake patterns observed in the nasopharynx or surrounding soft tissues. All primary tumors displayed increased uptake on both 68Ga-FAPI and 18F-FDG PET/CT. The average SUVmax of primary tumors was 17.01±5.87 (range, 7.45–29.30) for 68Ga-FAPI and 17.92±5.01 (range, 9.24–30.34) for 18F-FDG (P=0.90). No significant difference was observed in the average SUVmax of regional metastatic lymph nodes, which were 13.65±6.46 (range, 4.03–27.57) for 68Ga-FAPI and 15.88±6.67 (range, 6.56–29.12) for 18F-FDG (P=0.16). 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT and 18F-FDG PET/CT detected 66 and 89 lymph nodes in the neck, respectively, but they could not validate the false-positive and false-negative results of lymph node detection.

When using 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT alone for tumor staging compared to 18F-FDG PET/CT, only one patient had a reduction in T stage from T3 to T2, and three patients showed a decrease in N stage. Among these four, only two ultimately demonstrated a decrease in clinical stage. The use of 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT provided a distinct advantage in evaluating primary tumors invading the skull base and intracranial structures. The lesion borders were clearly delineated with 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT, as the brain does not uptake this tracer (Figure 2). The optimal SUVmax threshold for delineating GTVnx on 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT

Due to limited experience with 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT, there are insufficient references to establish an optimal threshold for automatically delineating GTVnx-FAPI. Therefore, we conducted a comparative analysis to evaluate the consistency of GTVnx-FAPI using various SUVmax thresholds (15%, 17.5%, 20%, 22.5%, 25%, 27.5%, 30%, 32.5%, 35%, 37.5%, 40%, and 50% SUVmax) compared to GTVnx-MRI, as enhanced MRI is recognized by the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines as the gold standard for primary tumor assessment (22). Given the absence of a pathological gold standard in non-surgical NPC patients, contrast-enhanced MRI was utilized as the reference for threshold optimization to ensure spatial coherence, rather than to define absolute tumor extent.

Firstly, the Jaccard index was utilized to assess the similarity between GTVnx-MRI and GTV-FAPI (23). The 17.5%, 20%, and 22.5% SUVmax thresholds produced the highest Jaccard index, with no significant differences among them (Figure 3), indicating that any of these thresholds may be suitable for GTVnx delineation. To identify the most suitable threshold, we analyzed the ICC. The 20% SUVmax threshold exhibited the highest ICC of 0.891 among all evaluated thresholds (Table 2), indicating that it is the optimal choice in our cohort for precise GTVnx delineation in radiotherapy planning.

The differences in Jaccard coefficients between GTVnx-MRI and GTVnx-FAPI at 15%, 17.5%, and 20% SUVmax thresholds were not statistically significant; however, they were significantly higher than the Jaccard coefficients of GTV-MRI and GTVnx-FAPI at other thresholds. Comparison of GTVnx and radiation dose to OARs in radiotherapy plan

We conducted a comparative analysis of GTVnx-MRI, GTVnx-FAPI20%, and the fusion of GTVnx-MRI + FAPI20%. The average GTVnx of each group were 17.44±13.12 (range, 3.44–57.39), 16.29±11.15 (range, 5.05–51.16) and 24.92±17.31 (range, 5.93–80.24), respectively, indicating that GTVnx-MRI + FAPI20% was significantly larger than both GTVnx-MRI and GTVnx-FAPI (P<0.001). Spatial analysis revealed that the additional volume detected by FAPI was primarily distributed in high‑risk anatomical regions for NPC, including the skull base and intracranial structures (such as the clivus, petrous apex, and cavernous sinus), the deep parapharyngeal space along neurovascular bundles, and retropharyngeal lymph nodes. These regions are often challenging to delineate on MRI due to low soft‑tissue contrast or small lesion size. In our study, there were no instances where GTVnx-FAPI20% fully encompassed the corresponding GTVnx-MRI or vice versa. Rather, the delineation ranges of the two methods complemented each other.

To compare the radiation dose to OARs, radiotherapy plans were developed for all 19 enrolled patients based on GTV-MRI and GTV-MRI + FAPI20%, referred to as PLAN-MRI and PLAN-MRI + FAPI20% (Figure 4). The average radiation doses to OARs for each group are summarized in Table 3. The results indicated that the radiation doses to all OARs in PLAN-MRI + FAPI20% were not significantly higher than those in PLAN-MRI and remained within the safe dose range (all P>0.05; Table 3). Notably, the mean dose to the right ear was significantly lower in PLAN-MRI + FAPI20% (33.15 vs. 27.42 Gy, P=0.038), reflecting individualized beam angle adjustments to spare normal tissues adjacent to FAPI-detected skull base extensions rather than a systematic effect of target volume expansion. Additionally, the average radiation doses of D99, D95, and D50 to the right ear were significantly reduced in PLAN-MRI + FAPI20% (P<0.05). Discussion

Accurately delineating GTVnx is a key challenge in effectively implementing radiotherapy for NPC (24). Despite the guidance from enhanced CT and MRI, current radiotherapy plans have limitations. Therefore, supplementary imaging techniques are essential for improving GTVnx delineation. This study demonstrates that the combined use of 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT effectively enhances GTVnx delineation in NPC patients. It increases the GTVnx without significantly raising the radiation dose to OARs, suggesting that 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT is a valuable supplementary imaging tool for guiding GTVnx delineation in NPC.

Although a larger GTV does not inherently indicate improved accuracy, several lines of evidence support that the additional FAPI‑positive regions represent true tumor extension rather than non‑specific uptake. First, FAP is physiologically expressed at very low levels in normal tissues, while being markedly upregulated in CAFs within the NPC stroma. Diffuse inflammatory or reparative fibrosis typically shows far lower FAP expression than malignant lesions, and all primary tumors in our cohort presented as focal (15), well‑defined uptake without diffuse patterns suggestive of benign inflammation. Second, the 20% SUVmax threshold was prospectively selected based on maximal consistency with contrast‑enhanced MRI, the NCCN‑recommended reference standard for primary tumor delineation. Third, the radiotherapy planning results demonstrated no significant increase in doses to any organ at risk, indirectly supporting that the expanded volume corresponds to target tissue rather than normal structures.

Consistent with previous reports, the additional FAPI‑positive volume in our cohort was mainly located in areas where MRI has known limitations, such as the skull base, deep parapharyngeal space, and retropharyngeal lymph nodes. The high tumor‑to‑background ratio of 68Ga‑FAPI, particularly in the skull base where physiological uptake is negligible, facilitates the identification of subtle tumor extension that may be underestimated on MRI.

In line with these findings, 68Ga‑FAPI PET/CT offers particular advantages over 18F‑FDG PET/CT in NPC, especially in regions adjacent to the brain where physiological glucose metabolism hampers tumor delineation. Previous studies have demonstrated that 18F-FDG PET/CT accurately delineates the boundaries of primary lesions in NPC (25) and that 18F-FDG PET/CT-guided radiotherapy results in higher survival rates and lower recurrence rates compared to conventional imaging methods (26-28). However, high background uptake along with the false-positive rates of 18F-FDG in the head and neck can result in inaccuracies in outlining GTVnx and may reduce the efficacy of radiotherapy (15,24). In contrast, 68Ga-FAPI reportedly demonstrates higher uptake in primary tumors and lower uptake in surrounding normal tissue (24,29). Zhao et al. reported that 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT provides clearer tumor margins in both primary and metastatic NPC (30). This advantageous biodistribution allows 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT to better delineate GTVnx. Additionally, Zhao et al. also found that 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT detects over half of occult NPC lesions, which represent approximately 30% of all NPC cases, whereas 18F-FDG PET/CT fails to do so (30). This highlights the advantages of 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT in depicting tumor lesions compared to 18F-FDG PET/CT. Among the head and neck cancers, NPC often invades the skull base and intracranial brain tissue. The high biological uptake of 18F-FDG in brain tissue provides 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT with a unique advantage.

The potential of 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT to guide GTV delineation has been evaluated in multiple malignant tumors. These studies consistently show that 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT provides valuable information for target volume assessment, helps prevent geographical tumor omission, and enhances the accuracy of radiotherapy planning (31-33). Zhao et al. evaluated the ability of 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT to delineate GTVnx in head and neck cancers without specifying the primary tumor site. Their results indicated that GTVnx-FAPI was larger than GTVnx based on enhanced CT (GTV-CT), and some patients exhibited FAPI-positive primary tumor areas not included in GTV-CT (19). In another study involving three NPC patients, researchers compared GTVnx delineation in head and neck cancers guided by 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT, 18F-FDG PET/CT (GTVnx-FDG), and conventional imaging (GTVnx-CONV). They found no significant difference between GTVnx-FDG and GTVnx-CONV, while GTVnx-FAPI was significantly larger than both (5). Since different imaging modalities yield varying information, combining multiple imaging techniques may better leverage their respective strengths for GTVnx delineation. Thus, our study assessed the effectiveness of enhanced MRI combined with 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT in guiding GTVnx delineation for NPC, confirming the added value of 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT in this context. Furthermore, our research evaluated the safety of OARs under this GTVnx delineation approach.

This study established 20% SUVmax as the optimal threshold for guiding GTVnx-FAPI delineation, using consistency with GTVnx-MRI as the evaluation criterion. Similar methods were employed by Zhao et al. to determine the optimal threshold (32). However, further studies are needed to identify the optimal threshold for maximizing its benefits. Other thresholds, such as 17.5%, 15%, or 25% SUVmax, while showing relatively low consistency with GTVnx-MRI in this study, may still enhance GTVnx delineation without significantly increasing radiation doses to OARs.

While our study highlighted several advantages of 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT in guiding delineation of GTVnx for NPC, it is important to acknowledge its limitations. Firstly, this study focused on method exploration rather than assessing true efficacy through comparative implementation. The treatment outcomes for PLAN-MRI + FAPI require longitudinal follow-up. Secondly, pathological validation of the additional FAPI‑positive volume was not routinely performed due to the prospective design and the fact that most patients received definitive chemoradiotherapy without surgery. Thirdly, the sample size remains relatively small. The relatively small sample size (n=19) may limit the generalizability of the 20% SUVmax threshold identified in this exploratory analysis. This threshold should currently be regarded as a preliminary finding pending larger-scale multicenter validation. A larger cohort is needed to confirm our findings.

Conclusions

68Ga-FAPI PET/CT demonstrates exploratory value in delineating GTVnx in NPC patients. Its combined use expanded target coverage without exceeding established OAR dose constraints. Prospective studies with larger sample sizes and long-term clinical endpoints are required to confirm its impact on recurrence and survival.

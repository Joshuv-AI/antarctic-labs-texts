On the Various Translations between Classical, Intuitionistic and Linear Logic
Gilda Ferreira, Paulo Oliva, Clarence Lewis Protin

Abstract
Several different proof translations exist between classical and intuitionistic logic (negative translations), and intuitionistic and linear logic (Girard translations). Our aims in this paper are (1) to consider extensions of intuitionistic linear logic which correspond to each of these systems, and (2) with this common logical basis, to develop a uniform approach to devising and simplifying proof translations. As we shall see, through this process of “simplification” we obtain most of the well-known translations in the literature.

Introduction

Classical logic
, the most widely known and studied form of mathematical reasoning, is based on the paradigm of
truth
. During the 20th century some mathematicians started focusing on
justification
rather than truth.
Intuitionistic logic

[
19
,
31
]
captures this emphasis on justification, dealing with the constructive nature of proofs. The intuitionistic connectives, instead of being seen as truth functions propagating the logical values of
true
and
false
, are seen as generating new proofs from previously constructed proofs.

In the Gentzen sequent calculus
[
14
,
35
]
, intuitionistic logic can be obtained from classical logic by restricting the number of formulas on the right side of sequents to at most one formula. In this way, one can view intuitionistic reasoning as a restricted form or classical reasoning.

Linear logic
[
15
,
38
]
can also be seen as trying to formalise “constructivism”, by imposing explicit control over the uses of structural rules such as weakening and contraction. One could say that instead of focusing on truth or proofs, linear logic places its emphasis on the proof
resources
, and the manner in which these resources are “used” during an inference.

In the literature we find several translations between
classical and intuitionistic logic, and various translations involving linear logic. The translations from classical to intuitionistic logic are often called
negative translations
or
double-negation translations
, the most well-known being translations due to Glivenko
[
16
]
, Gödel and Gentzen
[
14
,
17
]
, Kuroda
[
24
]
, and Krivine
[
23
]
. For comprehensive surveys on negative tranlsations see
[
12
]
,
[
18
, Chapter 6]
,
[
37
, Chapter 2.3]
and
[
36
]
. For further developments and applications of negative translations in different contexts see
[
4
,
28
]
(modal logic),
[
11
]
(substructural logics),
[
2
,
21
]
(extraction of computational content from classical proofs),
[
32
]
(ecumenical systems), and
[
3
,
26
]
(polarization).

The translations involving linear logic are often called
Girard translations
. They can be classified into three categories: (1) translations of classical linear logic into intuitionistic linear logic, which can be seen as substructural variants of the negative translations

27

30
]
, (2) translations of intuitionistic logic into linear logic

The first contribution of the present paper is an analysis and comparison of these different logical systems via
Intuitionistic Linear Logic

{\sf ILL}
. Reformulating Classical Logic

, Intuitionistic Logic

{\sf IL}

1.2
Intuitionistic Logic
{\sf IL}
as
{\sf ILL}+{\sf PRO}

Let us write
{\mathcal{L}}({\sf IL})
and
{\mathcal{L}}({\sf ILL})
, for the languages of
{\sf IL}
and
{\sf ILL}
respectively. Consider the following translation of formulas of
{\sf IL}

{\sf ILL}

Definition 1

(Translating
{\mathcal{L}}({\sf IL})
into
{\mathcal{L}}({\sf ILL})
)
.

For each formula
A

{\mathcal{L}}({\sf IL})
define a formula
{A}^{\dagger}
, in the language of
{\sf ILL}
, as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}{(A\wedge B)}^{\dagger}&:\equiv&{A}^{\dagger}\,\&\,{B}^{\dagger}&{P}^{\dagger}&:\equiv&P,\textup{~for $P$ atomic except $\bot$}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\vee B)}^{\dagger}&:\equiv&{A}^{\dagger}\oplus{B}^{\dagger}&{\bot}^{\dagger}&:\equiv&0\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\rightarrow B)}^{\dagger}&:\equiv&{A}^{\dagger}\multimap{B}^{\dagger}&{(\forall xA)}^{\dagger}&:\equiv&\forall x{A}^{\dagger}\\[2.84526pt]
&&&{(\exists xA)}^{\dagger}&:\equiv&\exists x{A}^{\dagger}.\end{array}

It is easy to see that this translation of formulas extends to a translation of proofs, from proofs in
{\sf IL}
to proofs in
{\sf ILL}+{\sf PRO}
. First, we need a simple lemma:

Lemma 1
.

Let
{\sf IL}_{\rm b}:={\sf ILL}+{\sf PRO}
. The following are provable in
{\sf IL}_{\rm b}
:

(i)

A\leftrightsquigarrow\,!A

(ii)

A\,\&\,B\leftrightsquigarrow A\otimes B

(iii)

\top\leftrightsquigarrow 1
.

Proof.

(i)
In
{\sf ILL}
we have
!A\vdash A
, a consequence of
(!\textup{L})
.
{\sf PRO}
gives us the converse
A\vdash\,!A
.
(ii)
Since we have
!(A\,\&\,B)\leftrightsquigarrow\;!A\otimes!B
the result follows from
(i)
.
(iii)
One possible instance for the axiom schema
(\top\textup{R})
is
1\vdash\top
. On the other hand, we have
\vdash 1

(1\textup{R})
and hence
!\top\vdash 1

(i)

\top\vdash 1
. ∎

Then it follows that
{\sf IL}
can be seen as an extension of
{\sf ILL}
with the promotion axiom
{\sf PRO}
.

Proposition 1

(
{\sf IL}
as an extension of
{\sf ILL}
)
.

\vdash_{{\sf IL}}A

\vdash_{{\sf IL}_{\rm b}}{A}^{\dagger}

Proof.

From left to right, we can use Girard’s translation (see

1.3
Classical Linear Logic
{\sf CLL}
as
{\sf ILL}_{\bot}+{\sf DNE}

As in the previous section, we can also translate the language of
{\sf CLL}

{\sf ILL}_{\bot}

Definition 2

(Translating
{\mathcal{L}}({\sf CLL})
into
{\mathcal{L}}({\sf ILL}_{\bot})
)
.

The translation
A\mapsto{A}^{\ddagger}
is defined inductively as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}{(A\otimes B)}^{\ddagger}&:\equiv&{A}^{\ddagger}\otimes{B}^{\ddagger}&{P}^{\ddagger}&:\equiv&P\quad(\textup{$P$ atomic})\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\,\&\,B)}^{\ddagger}&:\equiv&{A}^{\ddagger}\,\&\,{B}^{\ddagger}&{(\forall xA)}^{\ddagger}&:\equiv&\forall x{A}^{\ddagger}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\oplus B)}^{\ddagger}&:\equiv&{A}^{\ddagger}\oplus{B}^{\ddagger}&{(\exists xA)}^{\ddagger}&:\equiv&\exists x{A}^{\ddagger}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\multimap B)}^{\ddagger}&:\equiv&{A}^{\ddagger}\multimap{B}^{\ddagger}&{(!A)}^{\ddagger}&:\equiv&!{A}^{\ddagger}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\operatorname{\bindnasrepma}B)}^{\ddagger}&:\equiv&\neg(\neg{A}^{\ddagger}\otimes\neg{B}^{\ddagger})&{(?A)}^{\ddagger}&:\equiv&\neg!\neg{A}^{\ddagger}\\[2.84526pt]
\end{array}

As in the case of Intuitionistic Logic (Proposition
1

{\sf CLL}

{\sf ILL}_{\bot}+{\sf DNE}

Proposition 2

(
{\sf CLL}
as an extension of
{\sf ILL}_{\bot}
)
.

Let
{\sf CLL}_{\rm b}:={\sf ILL}_{\bot}+{\sf DNE}
. Then
\vdash_{{\sf CLL}}A
iff
\vdash_{{\sf CLL}_{\rm b}}{A}^{\ddagger}
.

Proof.

From left-to-right the result follows by induction on the derivation of
A
in
{\sf CLL}
. For the converse, note that
{\sf CLL}
proves the equivalences
\neg(?A)\leftrightsquigarrow~!\neg A
and
\neg(A\operatorname{\bindnasrepma}B)\leftrightsquigarrow\neg A\otimes\neg B
.
∎

Proposition 3

(
{\sf CL}
as an extension of
{\sf ILL}
)
.

Let
{\sf CL}_{\rm b}:={\sf ILL}_{\bot}+{\sf PRO}+{\sf DNE}

\vdash_{{\sf CL}}A

{\sf IL}

\neg\neg B\to B

\neg B

\vdash_{{\sf CL}}A

\vdash_{{\sf IL}+(\neg\neg B\rightarrow B)}A

\vdash_{{\sf CL}}A

Most formula/proof translations are

{\sf CL}_{\rm b}
to
{\sf IL}_{\bot}

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}{(A\otimes B)}_{\textup{Ku}}&:\equiv&{A}_{\textup{Ku}}\otimes{B}_{\textup{Ku}}&{(P)}_{\textup{Ku}}&:\equiv&P,\textup{~for $P$ atomic}\\[2.84526pt]

{(A\multimap B)}_{\textup{Ku}}&:\equiv&{A}_{\textup{Ku}}\multimap{B}_{\textup{Ku}}&{(!A)}_{\textup{Ku}}&:\equiv&!{A}_{\textup{Ku}}\end{array}

and
{A}^{\textup{Ku}}:\equiv\neg\neg{A}_{\textup{Ku}}

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}{(A\otimes B)}^{\textup{G}}&:\equiv&{A}^{\textup{G}}\otimes{B}^{\textup{G}}&{(P)}^{\textup{G}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg P,\textup{~for $P$ atomic}\\[2.84526pt]

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}{(A\otimes B)}^{{\rm Tr}}&:\equiv&{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\otimes}({A}^{{\rm Tr}},{B}^{{\rm Tr}})&{P}^{{\rm Tr}}&:\equiv&T(P),\textup{~for $P$ atomic}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\,\&\,B)}^{{\rm Tr}}&:\equiv&{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\,\&\,}({A}^{{\rm Tr}},{B}^{{\rm Tr}})&{(\forall xA)}^{{\rm Tr}}&:\equiv&{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\forall x}({A}^{{\rm Tr}})\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\oplus B)}^{{\rm Tr}}&:\equiv&{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\oplus}({A}^{{\rm Tr}},{B}^{{\rm Tr}})&{(\exists xA)}^{{\rm Tr}}&:\equiv&{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\exists x}({A}^{{\rm Tr}})\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\multimap B)}^{{\rm Tr}}&:\equiv&{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\multimap}({A}^{{\rm Tr}},{B}^{{\rm Tr}})&{(!A)}^{{\rm Tr}}&:\equiv&{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{!}({A}^{{\rm Tr}})\end{array}

where
{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\forall x},{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\exists x},{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{!},{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\otimes},{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\,\&\,},{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\oplus},{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\multimap}
are
T
-based outer-transforms.

Remark 2
.

As we will see, some formula translations can be presented both as modular inner
T
-translations or modular outer
T
-translations. So, one should think of the formulation of the translation via the inner and outer transforms as possible presentations (or computations) of the formula translation. Notable cases are the Kolmogorov translation (see Def.
11
) and the full Girard translation (see Def.
14
).

Obviously, we want translations from a logic
S_{1}
into another logic
S_{2}
that are “sound”, in the sense that they preserve provability in a non-trivial way:

Definition 6

(Sound translations)
.

Given two
{\sf ILL}
theories
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. We say that
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
is a sound translation from
S_{1}
to
S_{2}
if for all formulas
A
we have that

(i)

If
\vdash_{S_{1}}A
then
\vdash_{S_{2}}{A}^{{\rm Tr}}
, and

(ii)

A
and
{A}^{{\rm Tr}}
are equivalent over
S_{1}
.

All the translations we are going to present in this paper are sound modular
T
-translations for some extensions of
{\sf ILL}
and some
T(\cdot)
.

Definition 7

(Equivalent translations)
.

Two sound translations
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
and
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
from
S_{1}
to
S_{2}
are
equivalent
if
{A}^{{\rm Tr}}
and
{A}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
are equivalent over
S_{2}
, for all
S_{1}
formulas
A
.

2.2
Simplifying translations (from inside and outside)

Although two different translations might be equivalent, it can still be that one is “simpler” than the other. The following definition formalises this notion.

Definition 8

(Simplified translation)
.

Let
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
and

{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
be two equivalent translations. We say that
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
is a
simplification of

{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
if the transforms of
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
are either identical to those of
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
or simpler – in the sense of Definition
3
– than the corresponding transforms of
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
(and must be simpler in at least one case).

Our motivation for the present work is twofold. As we already mentioned above, expressing the logical systems considered in this paper as extensions of Intuitionistic Linear Logic (
{\sf ILL}
) allows us to identify the specific axioms that distinguish them, enabling the construction of straightforward canonical modular translations between these systems. Secondly, we aim to show that these canonical formula translations (being modular) can be systematically transformed into simpler (also modular) ones. We refer to this process, applied to a modular translation, as a “simplification”. Through this approach, as we will see, many of the well-known translations in the literature can be obtained, thus offering an explanation for how seemingly non-trivial modular translations arise. The “simplification” of a modular translation can happen in two ways:
from outside
or
from inside
.

Definition 9

(Simplification from outside)
.

Given two equivalent modular outer
T
-translations
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
and
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
, we say that
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
is a
simplification from outside of

{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
if
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
is a
simplification of

{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
, and, when we consider the set of formula reductions

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\Box}(T(A),T(B))&\mapsto&{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}_{\Box}(T(A),T(B))\\[2.84526pt]
{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{Q}(T(A))&\mapsto&{\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}_{Q}(T(A))\end{array}

for
\Box\in\{\oplus,\otimes,\,\&\,,\multimap\}
and
Q\in\{\forall x,\exists x,!\}
, we have that
{A}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
can be obtained from
{A}^{{\rm Tr}}
by applying these reductions recursively, starting from whole formula and working towards the atomic formulas.

Therefore, if
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
is a simplification from outside of
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
, the
T
-based inner-transforms of
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
and
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
will tell us how to systematically simplify
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
to obtain
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
. For instance, we will see in the next section that the Gödel-Gentzen negative translation is a simplification from outside of (an outer-presentation of) the Kolmogorov translation, whereas the Kuroda translation will be shown to be a simplification from inside of (an inner-presentation of) the Kolmogorov translation.

Definition 10

(Simplification from inside)
.

Given two equivalent modular inner
T
-translations
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
and
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
, we say that
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
is a
simplification from inside of

{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
if
{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
is a
simplification of

{(\cdot)}^{{\rm Tr}}
, and, when we consider the set of formula reductions

\begin{array}[]{rcl}T({\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{\Box}(A,B))&\mapsto&T({\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}_{\Box}(A,B))\\[2.84526pt]
T({\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}}_{Q}(A))&\mapsto&T({\cal T}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}_{Q}(A))\end{array}

for
\Box\in\{\oplus,\otimes,\,\&\,,\multimap\}
and
Q\in\{\forall x,\exists x,!\}
, we find that
{A}^{{\rm Tr}^{\prime}}
can be obtained from
{A}^{{\rm Tr}}
by applying these reductions inductively, starting from the atomic formulas and working toward the whole formula.

3
Translations from
{\sf CL}_{\rm b}
to
{\sf IL}_{\bot}

Let us now see how the general definitions of a translation simplification of the previous section apply to the concrete case of
negative translations
, such as the Kolmogorov, Gödel-Gentzen and Kuroda translations. In the following sections, we will carry out a similar study of the Girard translations of standard logic into linear logic.

3.1
Kolmogorov, Gödel-Gentzen and Kuroda translations

Kolmogorov’s negative translation
[
22
]
of
{\sf CL}
to
{\sf IL}
works by placing double negations in front of each subformula inductively. The same can be done for translating
{\sf CL}_{\rm b}
to
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
– in this case we are working with
T(A)=\neg\neg A
. In fact, there are two ways to formally present this inductively, which we will call
{\textup{K}}_{o}
and
{\textup{K}}_{i}
.

Definition 11

(Kolmogorov translation
[
22
]
)
.

The
outer
presentation of Kolmogorov’s translation, which we will denote by
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}
, uses of outer transforms

1.

{\cal T}_{Q}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}(A)=\neg\neg QA
, for
Q\in\{\forall x,\exists x,!\}
, and

2.

{\cal T}_{\Box}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}(A,B)=\neg\neg(A\operatorname{\Box}B)
, for
\Box\in\{\otimes,\,\&\,,\oplus,\multimap\}
,

giving rise to a modular outer
\neg\neg
-translation
{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}
, which can also be inductively defined as:

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}{(A\otimes B)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg({A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\otimes{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}})&{P}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg P,\textup{~for $P$ atomic}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\,\&\,B)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg({A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\,\&\,{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}})&{(\forall xA)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg\forall x{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\oplus B)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg({A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\oplus{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}})&{(\exists xA)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg\exists x{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\multimap B)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg({A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\multimap{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}})&{(!A)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg!{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}.\end{array}

Alternatively, we can also make use of inner transforms

1.

{\cal T}_{Q}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}(A)=Q\neg\neg A
, for
Q\in\{\forall x,\exists x,!\}
, and

2.

{\cal T}_{\Box}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}(A,B)=\neg\neg A\operatorname{\Box}\neg\neg B
, for
\Box\in\{\otimes,\,\&\,,\oplus,\multimap\}
,

giving rise to a modular inner
\neg\neg
-translation

{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}:\equiv\neg\neg{A}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}

where
{A}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
is defined inductively as:

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}{(A\otimes B)}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg{A}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}\otimes\neg\neg{B}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}&{P}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}&:\equiv&P,\textup{~for $P$ atomic}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\,\&\,B)}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg{A}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}\,\&\,\neg\neg{B}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}&{(\forall xA)}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}&:\equiv&\forall x\neg\neg{A}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\oplus B)}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg{A}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}\oplus\neg\neg{B}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}&{(\exists xA)}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}&:\equiv&\exists x\neg\neg{A}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\multimap B)}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg{A}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}\multimap\neg\neg{B}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}&{(!A)}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}&:\equiv&!\neg\neg{A}_{{\textup{K}}_{i}}.\end{array}

It is easy to check, by induction on the structure of
A
, that
{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\equiv{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
, for all formulas
A
. Intuitively, we can think of
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}
as working bottom-up: start by placing double negations in front of all atomic formulas, and then work your way up the syntax tree placing double negations in front of all connectives, quantifiers and modality.
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
, on the other hand, produces the same result, but we are working top-down: placing a double negation in front of the whole formula, and then recursively going down the syntax tree placing double negations in front of all subformulas.

The Gödel-Gentzen translation, on the other hand, only has a modular outer
\neg\neg
-translation presentation:

Definition 12

(Gödel-Gentzen translation
[
17
]
)
.

The Gödel-Gentzen negative translation is a modular outer
\neg\neg
-translation defined by
\neg\neg
-based outer transforms:

\begin{array}[]{cclccl}{\cal T}^{\textup{G}}_{\otimes}(A,B)&:\equiv&A\otimes B&&&\\[2.84526pt]
{\cal T}^{\textup{G}}_{\,\&\,}(A,B)&:\equiv&A\,\&\,B&{\cal T}^{\textup{G}}_{\forall x}(A)&:\equiv&\forall xA\\[2.84526pt]
{\cal T}^{\textup{G}}_{\oplus}(A,B)&:\equiv&\neg\neg(A\oplus B)&{\cal T}^{\textup{G}}_{\exists x}(A)&:\equiv&\neg\neg\exists xA\\[2.84526pt]
{\cal T}^{\textup{G}}_{\multimap}(A,B)&:\equiv&A\multimap B&{\cal T}^{\textup{G}}_{!}(A)&:\equiv&!A.\end{array}

Hence,
{A}^{\textup{G}}
is defined inductively as:

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}{(A\otimes B)}^{\textup{G}}&:\equiv&{A}^{\textup{G}}\otimes{B}^{\textup{G}}&{P}^{\textup{G}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg P,\textup{~for $P$ atomic}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\,\&\,B)}^{\textup{G}}&:\equiv&{A}^{\textup{G}}\,\&\,{B}^{\textup{G}}&{(\forall xA)}^{\textup{G}}&:\equiv&\forall x{A}^{\textup{G}}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\oplus B)}^{\textup{G}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg({A}^{\textup{G}}\oplus{B}^{\textup{G}})&{(\exists xA)}^{\textup{G}}&:\equiv&\neg\neg\exists x{A}^{\textup{G}}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\multimap B)}^{\textup{G}}&:\equiv&{A}^{\textup{G}}\multimap{B}^{\textup{G}}&{(!A)}^{\textup{G}}&:\equiv&!{A}^{\textup{G}}.\end{array}

The Kuroda negative translation, on the other hand, is a modular inner
\neg\neg
-translation:

Definition 13

(Kuroda translation
[
24
]
)
.

The Kuroda negation translation is a modular inner
\neg\neg
-translation defined by
\neg\neg
-based inner transforms:

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}{\cal T}^{\textup{Ku}}_{\otimes}(A,B)&:\equiv&A\otimes B&&&\\[2.84526pt]
{\cal T}^{\textup{Ku}}_{\,\&\,}(A,B)&:\equiv&A\,\&\,B&{\cal T}^{\textup{Ku}}_{\forall x}(A)&:\equiv&\forall x\neg\neg A\\[2.84526pt]
{\cal T}^{\textup{Ku}}_{\oplus}(A,B)&:\equiv&A\oplus B&{\cal T}^{\textup{Ku}}_{\exists x}(A)&:\equiv&\exists xA\\[2.84526pt]
{\cal T}^{\textup{Ku}}_{\multimap}(A,B)&:\equiv&A\multimap B&{\cal T}^{\textup{Ku}}_{!}(A)&:\equiv&!A.\end{array}

Hence,

{A}^{\textup{Ku}}:\equiv\neg\neg{A}_{\textup{Ku}}

where
{A}_{\textup{Ku}}
is defined inductively as:

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}{(A\otimes B)}_{\textup{Ku}}&:\equiv&{A}_{\textup{Ku}}\otimes{B}_{\textup{Ku}}&{(P)}_{\textup{Ku}}&:\equiv&P,\textup{~for $P$ atomic}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\,\&\,B)}_{\textup{Ku}}&:\equiv&{A}_{\textup{Ku}}\,\&\,{B}_{\textup{Ku}}&{(\forall xA)}_{\textup{Ku}}&:\equiv&\forall x\neg\neg{A}_{\textup{Ku}}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\oplus B)}_{\textup{Ku}}&:\equiv&{A}_{\textup{Ku}}\oplus{B}_{\textup{Ku}}&{(\exists xA)}_{\textup{Ku}}&:\equiv&\exists x{A}_{\textup{Ku}}\\[2.84526pt]
{(A\multimap B)}_{\textup{Ku}}&:\equiv&{A}_{\textup{Ku}}\multimap{B}_{\textup{Ku}}&{(!A)}_{\textup{Ku}}&:\equiv&!{A}_{\textup{Ku}}.\end{array}

Proposition 6
.

The above four modular
\neg\neg
-translations (inner Kolmogorov, outer Kolmogorov, Gödel-Gentzen and Kuroda) are all sound translations of
{\sf CL}_{\rm b}
to
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
, and equivalent to each other.

Proof.

Consider, for example, the inner Kolmogorov translation of
{\sf CL}_{\rm b}
into
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
. Clearly,
{\sf CL}_{\rm b}
proves the equivalence between
A
and
{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
. Now, we can show that
{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
is derivable in
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
whenever
A
is derivable in
{\sf CL}_{\rm b}
, by induction on the
{\sf CL}_{\rm b}
-derivation of
A
. The main challenge is to prove that the translations of the axioms of
{\sf CL}_{\rm b}
are derivable in
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
(it is easy to check that the translations respect logical rules). But, except for
{\sf DNE}
, the translation of each axiom of
{\sf CL}_{\rm b}
is easily derivable in
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
. Finally, one can also show that
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
proves
{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
, for any instance
B
of
{\sf DNE}
. The equivalence between the translations (over
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
) can be shown using the
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
-equivalences of Proposition
4
.
∎

Although all four translations above are equivalent, one can observe that the Gödel-Gentzen translation follows the same pattern as the outer Kolmogorov
\neg\neg
-translation, but has fewer double negation in certain places (namely,
\otimes,\,\&\,,\multimap
,
\forall
and
!
),
whereas the Kuroda translation is similar to the inner Kolmorov
\neg\neg
-translation, but again with fewer double negations in certain places (namely
\otimes
,
\,\&\,
,
\oplus
,
\multimap
,
\exists
and
!
).

3.2
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{G}}
is a simplification from outside of
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}

One might ask, what is it that allows us to simplify the outer Kolmogorov translation
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}
into the Gödel-Gentzen translation
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{G}}
, omitting double negations in additive and multiplicative conjunctions (
\,\&\,
,
\otimes
), implications (
\multimap
), universal quantifiers (
\forall
) and
(!)
? Similarly, why can we simplify the inner Kolmogorov translation
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
into the Kuroda translation
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{Ku}}
, omitting double negations in the logical connectives, in the existential quantifiers (
\exists
) and in the modalities
(!)
? We observe the following:

Proposition 7

(
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{G}}
is a simplification from outside of
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}
)
.

For any formula
A
, one can obtain
{A}^{\textup{G}}
from
{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}
by systematically applying the following formula reductions starting from the whole formula and inductively applying these to the subformulas:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\underbrace{\neg\neg(\neg\neg A\,\&\,\neg\neg B)}_{{\cal T}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}_{\,\&\,}(\neg\neg A,\neg\neg B)}&\quad\mapsto&\underbrace{\neg\neg A\,\&\,\neg\neg B}_{{\cal T}^{\textup{G}}_{\,\&\,}(\neg\neg A,\neg\neg B)}\\[19.91692pt]
\underbrace{\neg\neg(\neg\neg A\otimes\neg\neg B)}_{{\cal T}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}_{\otimes}(\neg\neg A,\neg\neg B)}&\quad\mapsto&\underbrace{\neg\neg A\otimes\neg\neg B}_{{\cal T}^{\textup{G}}_{\otimes}(\neg\neg A,\neg\neg B)}\\[19.91692pt]
\underbrace{\neg\neg(\neg\neg A\multimap\neg\neg B)}_{{\cal T}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}_{\multimap}(\neg\neg A,\neg\neg B)}&\quad\mapsto&\underbrace{\neg\neg A\multimap\neg\neg B}_{{\cal T}^{\textup{G}}_{\multimap}(\neg\neg A,\neg\neg B)}\\[19.91692pt]
\underbrace{\neg\neg\forall x\neg\neg A}_{{\cal T}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}_{\forall x}(\neg\neg A)}&\quad\mapsto&\underbrace{\forall x\neg\neg A}_{{\cal T}^{\textup{G}}_{\forall x}(\neg\neg A)}\\[19.91692pt]
\underbrace{\neg\neg!\neg\neg A}_{{\cal T}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}_{!}(\neg\neg A)}&\quad\mapsto&\underbrace{!\neg\neg A}_{{\cal T}^{\textup{G}}_{!}(\neg\neg A)}.\end{array}

Hence,
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{G}}
is a simplification from outside of
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}
– in the sense of Definition
9
. Moreover, by Proposition
4
, the reductions above are reversible in
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
, so
{A}^{\textup{G}}
and
{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}
are
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
-equivalent.

Proof.

Let us write
A\mapsto^{o}B
when
B
is obtained from
A
by systematically applying the reductions
\mapsto
“from the outside”. We are going to prove by induction on the logical structure of
A
that
A^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\mapsto^{o}{A}^{\textup{G}}
. We will make use of the fact that for any formula
A
there is a formula
A^{\prime}
such that
{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}=\neg\neg A^{\prime}
.

If
A=P
then
{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}=\neg\neg P={A}^{\textup{G}}
, and no reduction needs to be applied.

If
A=B\oplus C
then
{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}=\neg\neg({B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\oplus{C}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}})
. In this case, no reduction is applicable to the whole formula. By induction hypothesis we have that
{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\mapsto^{o}{B}^{\textup{G}}
and
{C}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\mapsto^{o}{C}^{\textup{G}}
. Hence,

{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}=\neg\neg({B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\oplus{C}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}})\mapsto^{o}\neg\neg({B}^{\textup{G}}\oplus{C}^{\textup{G}})=(B\oplus C)^{\textup{G}}={A}^{\textup{G}}.

If
A=B\operatorname{\Box}C
, with
\operatorname{\Box}\in\{\otimes,\,\&\,,\multimap\}
then

{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}=\neg\neg({B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\operatorname{\Box}{C}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}})=\neg\neg(\neg\neg B^{\prime}\operatorname{\Box}\neg\neg C^{\prime})

for some formulas
B^{\prime}
and
C^{\prime}
. Hence, we can apply one of the reductions to the whole formula so that

{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}=\neg\neg(\neg\neg B^{\prime}\operatorname{\Box}\neg\neg C^{\prime})\mapsto\neg\neg B^{\prime}\operatorname{\Box}\neg\neg C^{\prime}={B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\operatorname{\Box}{C}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}.

By induction hypothesis we have that
{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\mapsto^{o}{B}^{\textup{G}}
and
{C}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\mapsto^{o}{C}^{\textup{G}}
. Hence,

{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\mapsto{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\operatorname{\Box}{C}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\mapsto^{o}{B}^{\textup{G}}\operatorname{\Box}{C}^{\textup{G}}=(B\operatorname{\Box}C)^{\textup{G}}={A}^{\textup{G}}.

If
A=\exists xB
then
{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}=\neg\neg\exists x{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}
. In this case, no reduction is applicable to the whole formula. By induction hypothesis,
{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\mapsto^{o}{B}^{\textup{G}}
. Hence

{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}=\neg\neg\exists x{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\mapsto^{o}\neg\neg\exists x{B}^{\textup{G}}=(\exists xB)^{\textup{G}}={A}^{\textup{G}}.

If
A=QB
, with
Q\in\{\forall x,!\}
, then
{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}={(QB)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}=\neg\neg Q{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}=\neg\neg Q\neg\neg B^{\prime}
, for some formula
B^{\prime}
. Hence, we can apply one of the reductions to the whole formula so that

{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}=\neg\neg Q\neg\neg B^{\prime}\mapsto Q\neg\neg B^{\prime}=Q{B}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}\mapsto^{o}Q{B}^{\textup{G}}={A}^{\textup{G}}.

That concludes the proof.
∎

Observation 1
.

Note that the order of in which the reductions are applied is important. Consider

\neg\neg(\neg\neg(\neg\neg P\,\&\,\neg\neg Q)\otimes\neg\neg R).

(3)

If we applied reductions to the whole formula (from inside) we would get the following (and the process stops):

\neg\neg((\neg\neg P\,\&\,\neg\neg Q)\otimes\neg\neg R).

(4)

But if we applied the reductions to
(\ref{ex3-formula})
from outside we get

\neg\neg(\neg\neg P\,\&\,\neg\neg Q)\otimes\neg\neg R

(5)

and then

(\neg\neg P\,\&\,\neg\neg Q)\otimes\neg\neg R.

(6)

Although (
4
) and (
6
) are equivalent in
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
, (
6
) is certainly simpler than (
4
). The formula (
3
) is the
{\textup{K}}_{o}
-translation of
(P\,\&\,Q)\otimes R
, and (
6
) is the Gödel-Gentzen translation of
(P\,\&\,Q)\otimes R
obtained by applying the reductions from the outside.

Observation 2

(Maximality of simplifications)
.

As in
[
12
,
13
]
, we could also argue that
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{G}}
is not just a simplification from outside of
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}
, but it is actually a
maximal simplification
, in the sense that simplifying the clauses for additive disjunction and existential quantifiers would not be possible (see Proposition

). In order to keep this paper as concise as possible we will not discuss the issue of maximality of the simplifications any further, but the reader will be able to verify, using Propositions

3.3
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{Ku}}
is a simplification from inside of
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}

A similar phenomena holds for the inner presentation of the Kolmogorov translation
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
and Kuroda’s translation
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{Ku}}
.

Proposition 8

(
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{Ku}}
is a simplification from inside of
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
)
.

For any formula
A
, one can obtain
{A}^{\textup{Ku}}
from

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\underbrace{\neg\neg(\neg\neg A\,\&\,\neg\neg B)}_{{\cal T}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}_{\,\&\,}(\neg\neg A,\neg\neg B)}&\quad\mapsto&\underbrace{\neg\neg A\,\&\,\neg\neg B}_{{\cal T}^{\textup{lG}}_{\,\&\,}(\neg\neg A,\neg\neg B)}\\[8.53581pt]
\underbrace{\neg\neg(\neg\neg A\multimap\neg\neg B)}_{{\cal T}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}_{\multimap}(\neg\neg A,\neg\neg B)}&\quad\mapsto&\underbrace{\neg\neg A\multimap\neg\neg B}_{{\cal T}^{\textup{lG}}_{\multimap}(\neg\neg A,\neg\neg B)}\\[8.53581pt]
\underbrace{\neg\neg\forall x\neg\neg A}_{{\cal T}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}_{\forall x}(\neg\neg A)}&\quad\mapsto&\underbrace{\forall x\neg\neg A}_{{\cal T}^{\textup{lG}}_{\forall x}(\neg\neg A)}.\end{array}

Hence,
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{lG}}
is a simplification from outside of
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{o}}
, in the sense of Definition
9
. Moreover, by Proposition
4
, the reductions above are reversible in
{\sf ILL}_{\bot}
.

The proof of the above proposition would be similar to that of Proposition
7
, except that in here we are working in
{\sf ILL}_{\bot}
, instead of
{\sf IL}_{\bot}
, so fewer equivalences (or simplifications) would be available.

5.3
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{lKu}}
is a simplification from inside of
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}

A similar phenomenon holds for the inner presentation of the Kolmogorov translation
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
and Kuroda’s linear translation
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
, where we get a linear version of Proposition
8
:

Proposition 13

(
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{lKu}}
is a simplification from inside of
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
)
.

For any formula
A
, one can obtain
{A}^{\textup{lKu}}
from
{A}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
by systematically applying the following formula reductions starting from the atomic formulas and inductively applying these to composite formulas:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\underbrace{\neg\neg(\neg\neg A\otimes\neg\neg B)}_{\neg\neg{\cal T}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}_{\otimes}(A,B)}&\quad\mapsto&\underbrace{\neg\neg(A\otimes B)}_{\neg\neg{\cal T}^{\textup{lKu}}_{\otimes}(A,B)}\\[8.53581pt]
\underbrace{\neg\neg(\neg\neg A\oplus\neg\neg B)}_{\neg\neg{\cal T}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}_{\oplus}(A,B)}&\quad\mapsto&\underbrace{\neg\neg(A\oplus B)}_{\neg\neg{\cal T}^{\textup{lKu}}_{\oplus}(A,B)}\\[8.53581pt]
\underbrace{\neg\neg(\neg\neg A\multimap\neg\neg B)}_{\neg\neg{\cal T}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}_{\multimap}(A,B)}&\quad\mapsto&\underbrace{\neg\neg(A\multimap\neg\neg B)}_{\neg\neg{\cal T}^{\textup{lKu}}_{\multimap}(A,B)}\\[8.53581pt]
\underbrace{\neg\neg\exists x\neg\neg A}_{\neg\neg{\cal T}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}_{\exists x}(A)}&\quad\mapsto&\underbrace{\neg\neg\exists xA}_{\neg\neg{\cal T}^{\textup{lKu}}_{\exists x}(A)}.\end{array}

Hence,
{(\cdot)}^{\textup{lKu}}
is a simplification from inside of
{(\cdot)}^{{\textup{K}}_{i}}
, in the sense of Definition
10
. Moreover, by Proposition
4
, the reductions above are reversible in
{\sf ILL}_{\bot}
.

Let

\neg!\neg A

(\cdot)^{*}

(\cdot)^{\circ}

Lemma 2
.

Let
(\cdot)^{*}
and
(\cdot)^{\circ}
be the Girard translations presented in Definitions
15
and
16
. For any formula
A
the following are provable in
{\sf CLL}_{\rm b}

(i)

(\neg\neg A)_{\circ}\leftrightsquigarrow\;?!A_{\circ}
, and hence
(\neg\neg A)^{\circ}\leftrightsquigarrow\;!?!A_{\circ}
, and

(ii)

(\neg\neg A)^{*}\leftrightsquigarrow\;!?A^{*}
.

Proof.

(i)
A direct computation yields:

(\neg\neg A)_{\circ}=\;!(!A_{\circ}\multimap\bot)\multimap\bot=\neg!\neg!A_{\circ}=\;?!A_{\circ}

which implies
(\neg\neg A)^{\circ}\leftrightsquigarrow\;!?!A_{\circ}
.
(ii)
We have:

(\neg\neg A)^{*}=\,!(!(A^{*}\multimap\bot^{*})\multimap\bot^{*})=\,!(!(A^{*}\multimap~!\bot)\multimap~!\bot).

Using
(x)
of Proposition 5 we get that

!(!(A^{*}\multimap~!\bot)\multimap~!\bot)\leftrightsquigarrow\,!(!(A^{*}\multimap\bot)\multimap\bot)=\,!\neg!\neg A^{*}=\,!?A^{*}

which concludes the proof.
∎

Before proceeding we state two more lemmas about

Lemma 3
.

The following are derivable rules in
{\sf CLL}_{\rm b}
:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\vbox{\hbox spread0.0pt{\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{\kern 2.36111pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\penalty!\Gamma,A\vdash?B$}}\hskip 0.0pt}\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to50.17003pt{$\mathord{=}\mkern-6.0mu\leaders{\hbox{$\mkern-2.0mu=\mkern-2.0mu$}}{\hfill}\mkern-6.0mu\mathord{=}$}\hbox{}}}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle!\Gamma,?A\vdash?B$}}}}&&{\vbox{\hbox spread0.0pt{\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{\kern 2.36111pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\penalty\Gamma\vdash B$}}\hskip 0.0pt}\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to30.72559pt{$\mathord{=}\mkern-6.0mu\leaders{\hbox{$\mkern-2.0mu=\mkern-2.0mu$}}{\hfill}\mkern-6.0mu\mathord{=}$}\hbox{}}}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\Gamma\vdash?B$}}}}\end{array}

We will use “double lines” for one or more steps in a proof.

Proof.

These are straightforward to derive, for instance, the first rule can be derived as:

\displaystyle\penalty!\Gamma,A\vdash?B

(def)

\displaystyle!\Gamma,A\vdash\neg!\neg B

\displaystyle\penalty!\neg B\vdash!\neg B\quad\bot\vdash\bot

{(\multimap\mbox{L})}

\displaystyle!\neg B,\neg!\neg B\vdash\bot

{(\textup{cut})}

\displaystyle!\Gamma,!\neg B,A\vdash\bot

{(\multimap\textup{R})}

\displaystyle!\Gamma,!\neg B\vdash\neg A

{(!\textup{R})}

\displaystyle!\Gamma,!\neg B\vdash!\neg A

\mathord{=}\mkern-6.0mu\leaders{\hbox{$\mkern-2.0mu=\mkern-2.0mu$}}{\hfill}\mkern-6.0mu\mathord{=}

\displaystyle!\Gamma,\neg!\neg A\vdash\neg!\neg B

(def)

\displaystyle!\Gamma,?A\vdash?B

where on the double-line step (derivable rule) we are again using (cut) and the axiom
\bot\vdash\bot
to go from
!\Gamma,!\neg B\vdash!\neg A
to
!\Gamma,\neg!\neg A\vdash\neg!\neg B
.
The second rule can also be easily derived in
{\sf ILL}+{\sf DNE}
.
∎

Let us consider first the combination of Gödel’s negative translation with the Girard
(\cdot)^{\circ}

Theorem 1

(
(\cdot)^{\textup{G}\circ}
translation)
.

Consider the following translation of
{\sf CL}_{\rm b}
to
{\sf CLL}_{\rm b}
obtained by composing
(\cdot)^{\textup{G}}
and
(\cdot)^{\circ}
:

A^{\textup{G}\circ}:\equiv(A^{\textup{G}})^{\circ}.

This composition can be presented in a modular (and simpler) way as follows:

A^{\textup{G}\circ}:\equiv~!A_{\textup{G}\circ}

where
A_{\textup{G}\circ}
is defined inductively as:

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}(A\otimes B)_{\textup{G}\circ}&:\equiv&!A_{\textup{G}\circ}\otimes!B_{\textup{G}\circ}&P_{\textup{G}\circ}&:\equiv&?!P,\textup{~for $P$ atomic}\\[2.84526pt]
(A\,\&\,B)_{\textup{G}\circ}&:\equiv&A_{\textup{G}\circ}\,\&\,B_{\textup{G}\circ}&(\forall xA)_{\textup{G}\circ}&:\equiv&\forall xA_{\textup{G}\circ}\\[2.84526pt]
(A\oplus B)_{\textup{G}\circ}&:\equiv&?(!A_{\textup{G}\circ}\oplus!B_{\textup{G}\circ})&(\exists xA)_{\textup{G}\circ}&:\equiv&?\exists x!A_{\textup{G}\circ}\\[2.84526pt]
(A\multimap B)_{\textup{G}\circ}&:\equiv&!A_{\textup{G}\circ}\multimap B_{\textup{G}\circ}&{(!A)}_{\textup{G}\circ}&:\equiv&!{A}_{\textup{G}\circ}.\end{array}

Proof.

We can show by induction on
A
that
!(A^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}
is equivalent to the simpler formula
!A_{\textup{G}\circ}
, proving this way that the composed translation
(A^{\textup{G}})^{\circ}
is equivalent to
!A_{\textup{G}\circ}
. For
P
atomic we have:

!(P^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\equiv\;!(\neg\neg P)_{\circ}\equiv\;!?!P\equiv\;!P_{\textup{G}\circ}.

In the case of implications
A\multimap B
we have:

!((A\multimap B)^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\equiv\;!(A^{\textup{G}}\multimap B^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\equiv\;!(!(A^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\multimap(B^{\textup{G}})_{\circ})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(IH)}}}{{\leftrightsquigarrow}}\;!(!A_{\textup{G}\circ}\multimap B_{\textup{G}\circ})

using
!(!A\multimap B)\leftrightsquigarrow\;!(!A\multimap\,!B)
, cf. Prop.
5

(xi)
.

Additive conjunction:

!((A\,\&\,B)^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\equiv\;!((A^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\,\&\,(B^{\textup{G}})_{\circ})\leftrightsquigarrow\;!(!(A^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\,\&\,!(B^{\textup{G}})_{\circ})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(IH)}}}{{\leftrightsquigarrow}}\;!(A_{\textup{G}\circ}\,\&\,B_{\textup{G}\circ})

using
!(!A\,\&\,!B)\leftrightsquigarrow\;!(A\,\&\,B)
, cf. Prop.
5

(ix)
.

For the additive disjunction we have:

!((A\oplus B)^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\equiv\;!(\neg\neg(A^{\textup{G}}\oplus B^{\textup{G}}))_{\circ}\equiv\;!?!(!(A^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\oplus!(B^{\textup{G}})_{\circ})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(IH)}}}{{\leftrightsquigarrow}}\;!?(!A_{\textup{G}\circ}\oplus!B_{\textup{G}\circ})

using
!(!A\oplus!B)\leftrightsquigarrow\;!A\oplus!B
, cf. Prop.
5

(iii)
.

Multiplicative conjunction:

!((A\otimes B)_{\textup{G}})^{\circ}\equiv\;!(!(A^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\otimes!(B^{\textup{G}})_{\circ})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(IH)}}}{{\leftrightsquigarrow}}\;!(!A_{\textup{G}\circ}\otimes!B_{\textup{G}\circ}).

Universal quantifier:

!((\forall xA)^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\equiv\;!\forall x(A^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(IH)}}}{{\leftrightsquigarrow}}\;!\forall xA_{\textup{G}\circ}

using
!\forall x!A\leftrightsquigarrow\;!\forall xA
, cf. Prop.
5

(xii)
.

Existential quantifier:

!((\exists xA)^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\equiv\;!(\neg\neg\exists xA^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\equiv\;!?!\exists x!(A^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(IH)}}}{{\leftrightsquigarrow}}\;!?\exists x!A_{\textup{G}\circ}

using
!\exists x!A\leftrightsquigarrow\exists x!A
, cf. Prop.
5

(vi)
.

Modality
!

!((!A)^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\equiv\;!(!A^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\equiv\;!!(A^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(L.\ref{bang ?})}}}{{\leftrightsquigarrow}}\;!(A^{\textup{G}})_{\circ}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(IH)}}}{{\leftrightsquigarrow}}\;!A_{\textup{G}\circ}

which concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2

(
(\cdot)^{\textup{G}*}
translation)
.

Consider the following translation of
{\sf CL}_{\rm b}
to
{\sf CLL}_{\rm b}
obtained by composing
(\cdot)^{\textup{G}}
and
(\cdot)^{*}
:

This composition can be presented directly in a modular (and simpler) way as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}(A\otimes B)^{\textup{G}*}&:\equiv&A^{\textup{G}*}\otimes B^{\textup{G}*}&P^{\textup{G}*}&:\equiv&!?!P,\textup{~for $P$ atomic}\\[2.84526pt]

will show that the direct modular translation

is equivalent to the composition
((\cdot)^{\textup{G}})^{*}

.

Observation 5
.

{\sf CL}
into

see

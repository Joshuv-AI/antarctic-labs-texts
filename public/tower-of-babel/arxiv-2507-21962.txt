Pohozaev-like identity for the regional fractional laplacian
Sidy M. Djitte

Abstract
. We establish a new integration by parts formula for the regional fractional laplacian ( − Δ ) Ω s (-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega} in bounded open sets of class C 2 C^{2} . As a direct application, we prove that weak solutions to the corresponding Dirichlet problem satisfy a Pohozaev-like identity with an explicit remainder term. We apply the later to eigenvalue problems in the unit ball and discuss its potential use in establishing boundary-type unique continuation properties.



Keywords.
Regional fractional laplacian, integration by parts, Pohozaev-like identities.

1.
Introduction

The present paper is devoted to nonlocal operators of fractional order the prototype of which is the so-called fractional laplacian defined for sufficiently regular functions, by the principal value integral

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=c_{N,s}p.v\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy,

where
s\in(0,1)
and
c_{N,s}=s4^{s}\pi^{-N/2}\frac{\Gamma(N/2+s)}{\Gamma(1-s)}
. In the above, p.v stands for the Cauchy principal value. The fractional Laplacian has attracted extensive attention due to its appearance in mathematical model describing phenomenon in quantum mechanics, biology, finance and probability
[
16
,
20
,
18
]
. In Probability point of view, the fractional Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
arises as the infinitesimal generators of symmetric stable processes. Elliptic problems involving such operators have been extensively studied over the last few decays and many issues related to regularity, existence/non-existence and symmetry properties of solutions are now relatively well understood. In particular, boundary regularity has been addressed in
[
22
]
, and extensions of these results to more general operators have been considered in
[
7
,
8
,
23
]
. We also highlight the works
[
21
,
13
,
5
,
24
]
, where integration by parts formulas were derived. These results have proven to be highly useful in the analysis of elliptic problems involving such operators. Another important nonlocal operator that has attracted increasing interest in recent years is the so-called regional fractional Laplacian, denoted by
(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}
. It is defined by restricting the integration domain of the fractional Laplacian to the subregion
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u(x)=c_{N,s}p.v\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy.

The operators
(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}
are the infinitesimal generators of symmetric stable processes describing the motions of random particles in
\Omega
which are allowed to jump only in the region, and are killed or reflected when they reach the boundary. While
(-\Delta)^{s}
and
(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}
appear similar in form, they turn out to be different in many aspects. For instance, solutions to Dirichlet problem involving
(-\Delta)^{s}
are globally Hölder continuous with exponent
s
, whereas for the regional operator, solutions are generally only
(2s-1)
-Hölder continuous when
s\in(1/2,1)
, see e.g
[
9
,
11
]
. Another difference that is worth to mention is the lack of explicit solutions: up to date, no explicit solution is known for the simple equation
(-\Delta)^{s}_{(0,1)}u=1
in
(0,1)
. Despite some known results, elliptic problems involving the regional fractional Laplacian remain much less understood compared to those involving the standard fractional Laplacian. In this work, we are interested in deriving integration by parts formulas for solutions to the Dirichlet problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u&=f(u)\quad\text{in}\quad{\Omega},\\
u&=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial{\Omega},\end{aligned}\right.

with
s>1/2
. We note that some integration by parts formulas for
(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}
are already present in the literature. For instance, in
[
15
]
(see also
[
28
]
), the authors establish the counterpart of the classical Green’s identity for
(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}
. Specifically, it was proved that if
\Omega
is a bounded open set of class
C^{1,1}
;
v\in C^{2}_{2s-1}(\overline{\Omega}):=\{u=g\delta^{2s-1}+h:\quad g,h\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\}
and
w\in H^{s}(\Omega)
, then there holds:

\int_{\Omega}v(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}w\,dx=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{(v(x)-v(y))(w(x)-w(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy-b_{N}(s)\int_{\partial\Omega}(w/\delta^{2s-1})vd\sigma,

for some explicit constant
b_{N}(s)
. A similar identity is shown in
[
2
]
.
However, although these formulas are interesting in their own, they are of limited practical use for solving the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
). In particular, the requirement
v\in C^{2}_{2s-1}(\overline{\Omega})
is rather restrictive since the quotient
u/\delta^{2s-1}
is not in general
C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
for solutions to (
1.1
); see e.g.,
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
.

A much more useful identity is the so-called Rellich-Nečas-type identities sometimes called Pohozaev identities in specific setting. In this context of fractional operators, such identities were first derived by Ros-Oton and Serra for the fractional laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
in
[
21
, Proposition 1.6]
. It reads

(1.2)

-\int_{\Omega}x\cdot\nabla u(-\Delta)^{s}u\,dx=\frac{N-2s}{2}[u]^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\Gamma^{2}(1+s)\int_{\partial\Omega}(u/\delta^{s})^{2}x\cdot\nu d\sigma

for sufficiently regular functions
u
, where
\nu
denotes the outer unit normal to the boundary and
\Gamma
stands for the classical Gamma function. A generalization of (
1.2
) with an arbitrary globally Lipschitz vector field can be found in
[
4
]
.

In this work, we are interested to the analogue of (
1.2
) for the regional fractional laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}
. We observe that it is not even clear what the appropriate analogue of identity (
1.2
) should be in the case of the regional operator
(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}
. In particular, one cannot obtain such an analogue merely by replacing the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
with
(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}
, the Gagliardo semi-norm
[u]^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
with its regional counterpart
[u]^{2}_{H^{s}(\Omega)}
and the ratio
u/\delta^{s}
with
u/\delta^{2s-1}
as this would contradict the existence result established in
[
12
, Theorem 1.1 ]
. To the best of our knowledge, no analogue identities have been previously established for the regional fractional Laplacian, even in dimension one. In this work, we find and establish such identity. To state the result, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 1
be a bounded open set of class
C^{2}
. Throughout the paper, we fix a function
\delta:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
to be a
C^{2}
function that coincides with the signed distance function near the boundary. Moreover, we assume
\delta
is positive in
\Omega
. For notational convenience, we define the weighted semi-norm

[v]^{2}_{H^{s}(\Omega;\;\delta^{2-2s})}:=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{(v(x)-v(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\delta^{2-2s}(x)dxdy,

for all
v\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
, where
H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
is the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
with respect to the semi-norm

[v]^{2}_{H^{s}(\Omega)}=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy.

We also introduce the weight function
\omega_{s}:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
define as:

(1.3)

\omega_{s}(x,y)=x\cdot\nabla\delta^{2-2s}(x)+y\cdot\nabla\delta^{2-2s}(y)-(N+2s)\frac{\big[\delta^{2-2s}(x)-\delta^{2-2s}(y)\big]y\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}.

Then we have the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s\in(1/2,1)
, and let
\omega
be defined by (
1.3
). Let
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfies

(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u\in C^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega),

and set

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}[u]

\displaystyle:=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\omega_{s}(x,y)dx\,dy.

Then, for some constant
c>0
that depends on
N
,
s
and
\Omega
, we have

(1.5)

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{R}[u]\big|\leq c\Bigg(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\big\|u/\delta^{2s-1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big(\|(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)\Bigg).

Moreover, there exists
d_{s}(N)\in\mathbb{R}
such that the following identity holds true:

(1.6)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\delta^{2-2s}x\cdot\nabla u(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u\,dx=\frac{N-2s}{2}[u]^{2}_{H^{s}(\Omega;\;\delta^{2-2s})}-d_{s}\int_{\partial\Omega}(u/\delta^{2s-1})^{2}x\cdot\nu d\sigma+\mathcal{R}[u].

In the above,
\nu
denotes outer unit normal to the boundary
\partial\Omega
, and the remainder term
\mathcal{R}[u]
is given by (
1.4
).

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(2NF(u)+(2s-N)

Remark 1.4
.

In the particular case where
\Omega
is the centered unit ball

and

, identity (

(1.11)

\displaystyle 2s\lambda_{s}\int_{B}u_{s}^{2}dx=-d_{s}(N)\int_{\partial B}(u_{s}/\rho^{2s-1})^{2}+\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}[u_{s}].

where
\rho=1-|\cdot|
is the distance function to the boundary and the remainder term is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}[u_{s}]

\displaystyle:=(2-2s)\lambda_{s}\int_{B}\frac{u_{s}^{2}(x)}{\rho^{2s-1}}|x|\,dx-2N\lambda_{s}\int_{\Omega}\big[\rho^{2-2s}(x)-1\big]u_{s}^{2}dx+\mathcal{R}[u_{s}],

(1.12)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{N-2s}{2}[u_{s}]^{2}_{H^{s}\big(\Omega;\;\rho^{2-2s}-1\big)}.

An eventual application of the identity (
1.11
) concerns boundary unique continuation properties of eigenfunctions: if the fractional normal derivative
u/\rho^{2s-1}
vanishes at the boundary, then the corresponding eigenfunction should identically vanish for all
s\in[s_{0},1)
for some
s_{0}>1/2
.
Indeed, observe that since
0<\rho<1
, the last integral in (
1.12
) is non-positive and can therefore be dropped. If
u_{s}
is not identically zero, then without loss of generality
we may assume
\int_{B}u_{s}^{2}dx=1
, so that the identity (
1.11
) becomes

(1.13)

s\lambda_{s}\leq(1-s)\lambda_{s}\int_{B}\frac{u_{s}^{2}(x)}{\rho^{2s}}dx-N\lambda_{s}\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho^{2-2s}(x)-1\right)u_{s}^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}[u_{s}].

By
[

, Theorem 1.4]
, we know that there exists a constant
c_{3}=c_{3}(N)>0
such that

\|u_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq c_{3}\lambda_{s}\|u_{s}\|_{L^{2}(B)}\leq c_{3}\lambda_{s}.

Taking this into account and using the classical Hardy inequality (see e.g
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
), it is not difficult to see that

(1.14)

\limsup_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\lambda_{s}\int_{B}\frac{u_{s}^{2}(x)}{\rho^{2s}}\,dx-N\lambda_{s}\int_{\Omega}\big[\rho^{2-2s}(x)-1\big]u_{s}^{2}dx=0.

If we know that

(1.15)

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\to 1^{-}}\mathcal{R}[u_{s}]\leq 0,

then substituting (
1.15
) and (
1.14
) into (
1.13
) would gives

\limsup_{s\to 1^{-}}\lambda_{s}=0,

which is a contradiction since by the classical Hardy inequality, see e.g
[

, Theorem 1.1]
, we know that

\limsup_{s\to 1^{-}}\lambda_{s}\geq 1/4.

We note that he hypothesis (
1.15
) is not unreasonable since
\mathcal{R}[u_{s}]
does converge to zero formally.
However, this convergence does not follow directly from the estimate in Lemma
3.3
, even though one can, in principle, track the dependence of the constant on

. The argument only ensures that
\mathcal{R}[u_{s}]
remains uniformly bounded with respect to

, but does not guarantee its vanishing in the limit. We leave the validity of (

) as an open question.

\big|(-\Delta)^{s}\beta(r)\big|\leq\frac{C}{1+r^{1+2s}}
. Next, using that
(-\Delta)^{s}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
can be written as the sum of the fractional laplacian and a killing term, we derive the estimate:

(3.6)

\big|(-\Delta)^{s}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\beta(r)\big|\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{1+r^{1+2s}}+\frac{\beta(r)}{r^{2s}}\Big)\quad\textrm{for $r>1$}.

It follows that

\big|(D1)\big|\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi(r)\Big[(2s-1)\xi(r)-r\beta^{\prime}(r)\Big]\Big(\frac{1}{1+r^{1+2s}}+\frac{\beta(r)}{r^{2s}}\Big)dr<\infty.

To estimate
(D2)
, we write

(3.7)

\displaystyle(D2)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}\cdots drd\tilde{r}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}.

Since
(2s-1)\xi^{2}(r)+r\xi(r)\xi^{\prime}(r)
is bounded, it follows from (
3.4
) that

(3.8)

\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}<\infty.

It remains to estimate the contribution from
\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}
. For this, we write

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}=\int_{1}^{4}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}+\int_{4}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}.

The first contribution from (
3.9
) can be estimate as

(3.10)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{1}^{4}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}\Big|\leq c(\beta)\int_{1}^{4}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{drd\tilde{r}}{\tilde{r}^{2-2s}|r-\tilde{r}|^{2s-1}}<\infty.

The second contribution is controlled by

(3.11)

\Big|\int_{4}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}\Big|\leq C\int_{4}^{\infty}r^{2s-1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\tilde{r}}{|r-\tilde{r}|^{1+2s}}<\infty.

Combining (
3.7
), (
3.8
), (
3.9
), (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) show that
(D2)<\infty
and hence also
e_{s}(\xi)<\infty
.

Next, we estimate
f_{s}(\xi)
. For this, we write:

(3.12)

\displaystyle f_{s}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{1}^{\infty}\cdots drd\tilde{r}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}\cdots drd\tilde{r},

(3.13)

\displaystyle:=(A)+(B)+(C).

To estimate
(C)
, we split it into three different pieces:

\displaystyle(C)=

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{2}\int_{1}^{2}\cdots drd\tilde{r}+\int_{1}^{2}\int_{2}^{\infty}\cdots drd\tilde{r}+\int_{2}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}\cdots drd\tilde{r},

(3.14)

\displaystyle:=(C1)+(C2)+(C3).

It is clear that the quantity
(C1)
is finite. To estimate
(C2)
, we note since
\beta
in supported in
(-2,+2)
, then we have

\displaystyle(C2)

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{2}r^{2-2s}\beta(r)\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\big(r^{2s-1}\xi(r)-\tilde{r}^{2s-1}\xi(\tilde{r})\big)^{2}}{|r-\tilde{r}|^{1+2s}}\frac{(r-\tilde{r})}{|r-\tilde{r}|^{2}}d\tilde{r},

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{2}r^{2s}\beta(r)\xi(r)\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{r}^{2(2s-1)}}{(\tilde{r}-r)^{2+2s}}d\tilde{r},

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\beta(r)}{r^{3-4s}}dr\Big)\Big(\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{h^{2(2s-1)}}{(h-1)^{2+2s}}dh\Big)<\infty.

Similarly, because
\beta(r)=0
for
r>2
, we have

\displaystyle(C3)

\displaystyle=\int_{2}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}\cdots drd\tilde{r}=\int_{2}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{2}\cdots drd\tilde{r},

\displaystyle=\int_{2}^{\infty}r^{2-2s}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{(r^{2s-1}\xi(r)-\tilde{r}^{2s-1}\xi(\tilde{r}))^{2}}{|r-\tilde{r}|^{2+2s}}\beta(\tilde{r})d\tilde{r},

\displaystyle=\int_{2}^{4}r^{2-2s}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{(r^{2s-1}\xi(r)-\tilde{r}^{2s-1}\xi(\tilde{r}))^{2}}{|r-\tilde{r}|^{2+2s}}\beta(\tilde{r})d\tilde{r},

\displaystyle+\int_{4}^{\infty}r^{2-2s}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{(r^{2s-1}\xi(r)-\tilde{r}^{2s-1}\xi(\tilde{r}))^{2}}{|r-\tilde{r}|^{2+2s}}\beta(\tilde{r})d\tilde{r},

(3.16)

\displaystyle=(C3.1)+(C3.2).

We have in one hand

(3.17)

\displaystyle(C3.2)\leq C\int_{4}^{\infty}r^{2s}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\beta(\tilde{r})d\tilde{r}}{(r-\tilde{r})^{2+2s}}d\tilde{r}<\infty.

In the other hand, since
\beta(r)=0
for
r>2
, we have

\displaystyle(C3.1)\leq C\int_{2}^{4}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\beta(\tilde{r})d\tilde{r}}{(r-\tilde{r})^{2s}}dr

\displaystyle=C\int_{2}^{4}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\beta(\tilde{r})-\beta(\tilde{r})}{(r-\tilde{r})^{2s}}d\tilde{r}dr,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\beta^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{2}^{4}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{d\tilde{r}dr}{|r-\tilde{r}|^{2s-1}}<\infty.

(
3.17
), (
3
) together with (
3.16
) show that
(C)
is finite as well. To estimate
(B)
, we write:

(3.19)

\displaystyle(B)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}=\int_{1}^{4}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}+\int_{4}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}.

The first contribution is controlled by

(3.20)

\Big|\int_{1}^{4}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}\Big|\leq C\int_{1}^{4}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{drd\tilde{r}}{\tilde{r}^{2-2s}|r-\tilde{r}|^{2s-1}}<\infty.

Te second integral is bounded by

(3.21)

\Big|\int_{4}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots drd\tilde{r}\Big|\leq C\int_{4}^{\infty}r^{2s}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\tilde{r}}{|r-\tilde{r}|^{2+2s}}dr<\infty.

Using a similar argument, we can easily estimate
(A)
. We therefore conclude that
f_{s}(\xi)
is finite. The same techniques apply to
g_{s}(\xi)
, and we leave the details to the interested reader. This completes the proof.
∎

3.1.
Weighted Sobolev regularity of weak solutions

The first result of this subsection ensure that the remainder term
R[u]
appearing in the identity (
1.6
) is indeed well-defined. Before we state the result, we recall a regularity result from
[
1
]
which asserts that weak solutions to the Dirichlet problem associated to the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}
, when driven by a sufficiently regular right-hand side, gain almost a
1/2
-Sobolev regularity and this as long as
\Omega
has a Lipchitz boundary.
Although the result stated below is not explicitly formulated in
[
1
]
, as noted by the authors in the last paragraph of page 473, it follows directly from the methods developed in that work.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
1
]

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set with Lipchitz boundary. Let
s\in(1/2,1)
,
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{\alpha}(\Omega)
and
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a bounded weak solution of

(3.22)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}(-\Delta)_{\Omega}^{s}u=g&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Then,
u\in H^{s+1/2-\varepsilon}_{0}(\Omega)
for every
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(N,s,\alpha,\Omega)
such that

(3.23)

\|u\|_{H^{s+1/2-\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c(N,s,\alpha,\Omega)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}(2s-1)}\|g\|_{C^{\alpha}(\Omega)}.

The constant
c(N,s,\alpha,\Omega)
remains bounded for
s
in
(1/2,1)
.

With this, we are ready to prove the following

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
C^{2}
boundary and
s\in(1/2,1)
. Let
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
be a bounded weak solution of

(3.24)

(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u=f(u)\quad\textrm{in}\quad\Omega\qquad\&\qquad u=0\quad\textrm{on}\quad\partial\Omega

with
f\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Then there exists a constant
\widetilde{c}=\widetilde{c}(N,s,\Omega)
such that

(3.25)

\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\frac{dxdy}{\delta^{2s-1}(x)}<\widetilde{c}c_{s}[u],

where

(3.26)

c_{s}[u]:=\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{1-s}+\big\|u/\delta^{2s-1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big(\|f(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big).

Proof.

Under the assumption of the theorem, we know by regularity theory that
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{2s-1}(\overline{\Omega})
. Moreover, we have
u/\delta^{2s-1}\in C^{1+\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\alpha\in(0,2s-1)
, see e.g
[
9
,
11
]
. Next, fixed
\mu>0
and define
\Omega_{\mu}:=\big\{x\in\Omega:\delta(x)>\mu\big\}
. Then we have
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega_{\mu}})
. For sake of simplicity, we denote

(3.27)

\displaystyle{N}^{\mu}[u]:=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega_{\mu}\times\Omega_{\mu}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\frac{dxdy}{\delta^{2s-1}(x)},

and

(3.28)

\displaystyle{N}^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}[u]:=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega_{\mu}\times\Omega_{\mu}}1_{\big\{|x-y|>\varepsilon\big\}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\frac{dxdy}{\delta^{2s-1}(x)}\qquad\textrm{for $\varepsilon>0$}.

We start by breaking
{N}^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}[u]
into two pieces:

\displaystyle{N}^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}[u]

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega_{\mu}\times\Omega_{\mu}}1_{\big\{|x-y|>\varepsilon\big\}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))u(x)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\frac{dxdy}{\delta^{2s-1}(x)},

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega_{\mu}\times\Omega_{\mu}}1_{\big\{|x-y|>\varepsilon\big\}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\frac{dxdy}{\delta^{2s-1}(x)},

(3.29)

\displaystyle:=I^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}+J^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}.

Let
G^{\varepsilon}(x,y)=1_{\big\{|x-y|>\varepsilon\big\}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))u(x)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\delta^{1-2s}(x)
. It is clear that
G^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
is absolutely integrable in
\Omega_{\mu}\times\Omega_{\mu}
. Hence by Fubini theorem, we may write

\displaystyle 2I^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=c_{N,s}\iint_{\Omega_{\mu}\times\Omega_{\mu}}1_{\big\{|x-y|>\varepsilon\big\}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))u(x)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\frac{dxdy}{\delta^{2s-1}(x)},

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}1_{\big\{|x-y|>\varepsilon\big\}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dydx,

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega_{\mu},\varepsilon}u(x)dx.

Similarly, one can also write

\displaystyle 2J^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=-c_{N,s}\iint_{\Omega_{\mu}\times\Omega_{\mu}}1_{\big\{|x-y|>\varepsilon\big\}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\frac{dxdy}{\delta^{2s-1}(x)},

\displaystyle=-c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}\frac{dx}{\delta^{2s-1}(x)}\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}1_{\big\{|x-y|>\varepsilon\big\}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy,

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}\frac{1}{\delta^{2s-1}(x)}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega_{\mu},\varepsilon}u^{2}-u(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega_{\mu},\varepsilon}u\Big](x)dx,

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}\frac{1}{\delta^{2s-1}(x)}(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega_{\mu},\varepsilon}u^{2}(x)dx+\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega_{\mu},\varepsilon}u(x)dx.

Summing
I^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
and
J^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
we get

(3.30)

\displaystyle I^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}+J^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega_{\mu},\varepsilon}u(x)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}\delta^{-(2s-1)}(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega_{\mu},\varepsilon}u^{2}(x)dx.

By
[
14
, Proposition 2.3]
, we may pass into a limit as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
and get

\displaystyle N^{\mu}[u]=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}(I^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}+J^{\mu}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega_{\mu}}u(x)dx-(1/2)\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}\delta^{-(2s-1)}(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega_{\mu}}u^{2}(x)dx,

(3.31)

\displaystyle:=I^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}J^{\mu}.

Since
\delta^{1-2s}
and
u
both belong to
C^{2}(\overline{\Omega_{\mu}})
, then in view of
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
, we may integrate by parts and write

(3.32)

\displaystyle J^{\mu}:=\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}\delta^{-(2s-1)}(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega_{\mu}}u^{2}(x)dx=\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}u^{2}(x)(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega_{\mu}}\delta^{-(2s-1)}dx.

In the computations that follow, we let

\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}:=\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\mu}=\Big\{x\in\Omega:0<\delta(x)<\mu\Big\}.

Using the above notation, we decompose each of the terms
I^{\mu}
and
J^{\mu}
as follows:

(3.33)

\displaystyle I^{\mu}=\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u(x)dx-\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}u(x)dx,

(3.34)

\displaystyle J^{\mu}=\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}u^{2}(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}\delta^{-(2s-1)}(x)dx-\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}u^{2}(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}\delta^{-(2s-1)}(x)dx.

Combining (
3.1
), (
3.33
) and (
3.34
) we arrive at the final expression

\displaystyle N^{\mu}[u]

\displaystyle=I^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}J^{\mu},

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u(x)dx-(1/2)\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}u^{2}(x)(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}\delta^{-(2s-1)}(x)dx,

(3.35)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}u(x)dx+(1/2)\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}u^{2}(x)(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}\delta^{-(2s-1)}(x)dx.

Now observe that for
x\in\Omega_{\mu}
and
y\in\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}
, we have
\delta^{-(2s-1)}(x)-\delta^{-(2s-1)}(y)<0
. Consequently,

\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}u^{2}(x)(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}\delta^{-(2s-1)}(x)dx\leq 0.

In view of this, we may drop the last term in (
3.1
) to finally obtain the bound:

\displaystyle N^{\mu}[u]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u(x)dx-(1/2)\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}u^{2}(x)(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}\delta^{-(2s-1)}(x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}u(x)dx,

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u(x)dx\Big|+(1/2)\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}u^{2}(x)\Big|(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}\delta^{-(2s-1)}(x)\Big|dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big|\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}u(x)dx\Big|,

(3.36)

\displaystyle:=(1)^{\mu}+(2)^{\mu}+(3)^{\mu}.

Now we are going to prove that each of the quantities
(1)^{\mu}
,
(2)^{\mu}
and
(3)^{\mu}
are uniformly bounded in
\mu
. For
(1)^{\mu}
, we have the crude bound

\displaystyle\big|(1)^{\mu}\big|

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega_{\mu}|\times\big\|u/\delta^{2s-1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

(3.37)

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega|\times\big\|u/\delta^{2s-1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|f(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Next we consider
(2)^{\mu}
. Following closely the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 2.1]
, we see that, for any
\alpha\in(-1,2s)
and
x\in\Omega
, there exists a constant
c>0
independent of
\alpha
and
s
such that

(3.38)

\big|(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}\delta^{\alpha}\big|\leq c\gamma(\alpha,s)\delta^{\alpha-2s}(x)\quad\textrm{with}\quad\gamma(\alpha,s)=c_{1,s}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-t^{\alpha})(1-t^{2s-1-\alpha})}{(1-t)^{1+2s}}dt.

We apply this with
\alpha=1-2s<0
to get

(3.39)

\displaystyle\big|(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}\delta^{1-2s}(x)\big|\leq c\gamma(1-2s,s)\delta^{1-4s}(x).

From this we get

\displaystyle|(2)^{\mu}|

\displaystyle\leq c\gamma(1-2s,s)\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{\delta^{2(2s-1/2)}}dx,

(3.40)

\displaystyle\leq c\gamma(1-2s,s)\|\widetilde{u}\|^{2}_{H^{2s-1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

where in the last line, we have used the classical Hardy inequality see e.g
[
27
, Page 319, Equation (9)]
. In view of Theorem
3.2
and
[
1
, Lemma 3.14]
, we also have

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}\|^{2}_{H^{2s-1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{u}\|^{2}_{H^{2s-1/2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{\widetilde{c}(N,s,\Omega)}{1-s}\|f(u)\|^{2}_{C^{2s-1}(\overline{\Omega)}}

(3.41)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\widetilde{c}(N,s,\Omega)}{1-s}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Plugging this into (
3.1
) we end up with

\displaystyle|(2)^{\mu}|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(1-2s,s)\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{\delta^{2(2s-1/2)}}dx,

\displaystyle\leq c\gamma(1-2s,s)\|\widetilde{u}\|^{2}_{H^{2s-1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

(3.42)

\displaystyle\leq c\gamma(1-2s,s)\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{1-s},

for some constant
c>0
that depends on
N,s
and
\Omega
. Finally, we estimate
(3)^{\mu}
. To that end, we recall the following result (see
[
9
, Theorem 1.4 and Remark 1.5]
): for all
x\in\Omega
, there holds

(3.43)

|\nabla u(x)|\leq\widetilde{c}(N,s,\Omega)\delta^{2s-2}(x)\Big(\|f(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big).

Moreover,
\widetilde{c}=\widetilde{c}(N,s,\Omega)
remains bounded as
s\to 1^{-}
. In the estimates that follow, the constant
\widetilde{c}=\widetilde{c}(N,s,\Omega)
may change from line to line, but it remains bounded as
s\to 1^{-}
. Having this in mind, and recalling (
3.43
), we estimate

\displaystyle\big|(3)^{\mu}\big|=\Bigg|\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}(u/\delta^{2s-1})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}u(x)dx\Bigg|,

\displaystyle\leq\big\|u/\delta^{2s-1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy,

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{c}\big\|u/\delta^{2s-1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big(\|f(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}\int_{\Omega_{\mu}}\frac{dxdy}{\delta(y)^{2-2s}|x-y|^{N+2s-1}},

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{c}\big\|u/\delta^{2s-1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big(\|f(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}\frac{1}{\delta(y)^{2-2s}}\int_{\mu-\delta(y)}^{R_{\Omega}}\frac{dr}{r^{2s}}dy,

(3.44)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{c}\big\|u/\delta^{2s-1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big(\|f(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}\frac{dy}{\delta(y)^{2-2s}(\mu-\delta(y))^{2s-1}}.

Now if we set
A_{t}=\{y\in\Omega:\delta(y)=t\}
, then one can write:

\displaystyle c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\mu}}\frac{dy}{\delta(y)^{2-2s}(\mu-\delta(y))^{2s-1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\mu}\int_{A_{t}}\delta(z)^{2s-2}(\mu-\delta(z))^{1-2s}dzdt,

\displaystyle=c_{N,s}\int_{0}^{\mu}|A_{t}|t^{2s-2}(\mu-t)^{1-2s}dt,

\displaystyle\leq c(\Omega)c_{N,s}\int_{0}^{\mu}t^{2s-2}(\mu-t)^{1-2s}dt,

\displaystyle=c(\Omega)c_{N,s}\int_{0}^{1}t^{2s-2}(1-t)^{1-2s}dt

(3.45)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\widetilde{c}\frac{c_{N,s}}{1-s}\leq\widetilde{c}.

where
\widetilde{c}=\widetilde{c}(N,s,\Omega)
remains bounded for
s\in(1/2,1)
. In the above, from the second to the third line, we have used that
|A_{t}|\leq c|\partial\Omega|
for
t>0
small enough. Plugging (
3.1
) into (
3.44
), we get

(3.46)

\displaystyle\big|(3)^{\mu}\big|\leq\widetilde{c}(N,s,\Omega)\big\|u/\delta^{2s-1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big(\|f(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big).

and the constant
\widetilde{c}(N,s,\Omega)
remains bounded for
s\in(1/2,1)
. Finally combining (
3.36
), (
3.1
), (
3.1
), and (
3.46
) we end up with the following estimate:

\displaystyle{N}^{\mu}[u]\leq\widetilde{c}(N,s,\Omega)\Bigg[\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{1-s}+\big\|u/\delta^{2s-1}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big(\|f(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)\Bigg].

It follows that the sequence
(N^{\mu}[u])_{\mu}
is uniformly bounded. Since it is increasing, it has a limit and the limit satisfies the above bound has well. The proof is finish.
∎

3.2.
Integration by parts for regular functions with compact support

In this subsection, we prove integration by parts for functions wit compact support. It is the starting point of the proof of the main results.

Lemma 3.4
.

Fixed
Y\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{N})
and defined

(3.47)

\omega_{Y}(x,y)=\text{div}Y(x)+\text{div}Y(y)-(N+2s)(Y(x)-Y(y))\cdot(x-y)|x-y|^{-2}.

Then for every
\phi\in C^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)
, there holds

\displaystyle\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\omega_{Y}(x,y)\,dx\,dy

(3.48)

\displaystyle\qquad=-2\int_{\Omega}Y\cdot\nabla\phi(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}\phi\,dx+c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{Y(y)\cdot\nu(y)d\sigma(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx,

where
\nu
denotes the outward unit normal to the boundary.

Remark 3.5
.

Note that the identity (
3.4
) is consistent with the classical identity since

c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{Y(y)\cdot\nu(y)d\sigma(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as}\qquad s\to 1^{-}.

Remark 3.6
.

The presence of the remainder term in (
3.4
) should not be a surprise. Indeed, if it was missing, then taking, for instance,
X_{j}=e_{j}
, we get a contradiction since
\omega_{X_{j}}\equiv 0
but
\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}\phi(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}\phi\,dx
is not necessary zero for all
\phi\ C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
.This is only true for
(-\Delta)^{s}
.

Next we proceed to the proof of the Lemma.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Let
\phi\in C^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)
and set

(3.49)

\mathcal{E}_{Y,\Omega}(\phi,\phi)=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{(\phi(x)-\phi(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\omega_{Y}(x,y)\,dx\,dy.

Then, we have

(3.50)

\mathcal{E}_{Y,\Omega}(\phi,\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(\phi(x)-\phi(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\omega_{Y}(x,y)\,dx\,dy-\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\omega_{Y}(x,y)\,dy\,dx.

Now quoting
[
4
, Lemma 4.2]
, we may write

(3.51)

\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(\phi(x)-\phi(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\omega_{Y}(x,y)\,dx\,dy=-2\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot Y(-\Delta)^{s}\phi\,dx,

we therefore focus our attention on the second term in (
3.50
). Using that
\nabla_{y}(|x-y|^{-N-2s})=(N+2s)(x-y)/|x-y|^{N+2s+2}
and integrating by parts we get

(3.52)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\omega_{Y}(x,y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=-c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\partial(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}\frac{(Y(x)-Y(y))\cdot\nu(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy\,dx

(3.53)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\text{div}Y(x)\kappa_{\Omega}(x)\,dx,

where

\kappa_{\Omega}(x)=c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}

is the killing term. Another integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\kappa_{\Omega}(x)\phi^{2}(x)\text{div}Y(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\kappa_{\Omega}(x)\text{div}(\phi^{2}X)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\kappa_{\Omega}(x)\nabla\phi^{2}\cdot X\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\text{div}(\phi^{2}Y\kappa_{\Omega})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}X\cdot\nabla\kappa_{\Omega}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\kappa_{\Omega}(x)\nabla\phi^{2}\cdot X\,dx

(3.54)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}X\cdot\nabla\kappa_{\Omega}\,dx-2\int_{\Omega}\phi\kappa_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot Y\,dx,

In the last line we used that
\phi=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Next, note that
\nabla_{x}|x-y|^{-N-2s}=-\nabla_{y}|x-y|^{-N-2s}
. Using this, we write

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\partial(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}\frac{Y(x)\cdot\nu(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\text{div}_{y}\big(|x-y|^{-N-2s}Y(x)\big)dy\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\nabla_{y}(|x-y|^{-N-2s})\cdot Y(x)dy\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\nabla_{x}(|x-y|^{-N-2s})\cdot Y(x)dy\,dx

(3.55)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\nabla_{x}\kappa_{\Omega}\cdot Y(x)\,dx

In view of (
3.52
), (
3.53
), (
3.54
) and (
3.55
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\omega_{Y}(x,y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=-c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{Y(y)\cdot\nu(y)d\sigma(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx-\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}Y\cdot\nabla\kappa_{\Omega}\,dx

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega}\phi\kappa_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot Y\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\nabla\kappa_{\Omega}\cdot Y(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=-c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{X(y)\cdot\nu(y)d\sigma(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx-2\int_{\Omega}\phi\kappa_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot Y\,dx

Now recalling (
3.50
) and (
3.51
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{X,\Omega}(\phi,\phi)

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot Y(-\Delta)^{s}\phi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\omega_{Y}(x,y)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot Y(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}\phi\,dx-2\int_{\Omega}\phi\kappa_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot X+c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{Y(y)\cdot\nu(y)d\sigma(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{\Omega}\phi\kappa_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot X\,dx

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot Y(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}\phi\,dx+c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(x)\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{Y(y)\cdot\nu(y)d\sigma(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx

which proves the claim. The proof is finished.
∎

4.
Towards the proof of the main results: regularization step

This section lays the groundwork for the proof of the main results. From now on, we fix
\beta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(-2,+2)
such that
\beta\equiv 1
in
(-1,1)
and define
\xi=1-\beta
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{*}
, we defined
\xi_{k}(x)=\xi(k\delta(x))
and

(4.1)

Y_{k}(x)=\xi_{k}(x)\delta^{2-2s}(x)x\in C^{1,1}_{c}({\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{N}).

The starting point of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is to use
3.4
, with
\phi=\xi_{k}u
and
Y=Y_{k}(x)
. to get

(4.2)

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega}\xi_{k}(x)\delta^{2-2s}(x)x\cdot\nabla(u\xi_{k})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}(u\xi_{k})dx=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}(u\xi_{k}(x)-u\xi_{k}(y))^{2}\omega_{k}(x,y)dxdy

Note that reminder term in (
3.4
) is no longer present in (
4.2
) since
Y_{k}
is compactly supported in
\Omega
. In (
4.2
), we have set

\displaystyle\frac{|x-y|^{N+2s}}{c_{N,s}}\omega_{k}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\text{div}Y_{k}(x)+\text{div}Y_{k}(y)-(N+2s)\frac{(Y_{k}(x)-Y_{k}(y))\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}

\displaystyle=N\delta^{2-2s}(x)\xi_{k}(x)+N\delta^{2-2s}(y)\xi_{k}(y)-(N+2s)\delta^{2-2s}(x)\xi_{k}(x)+R_{k}(x,y)

\displaystyle=N\delta^{2-2s}(y)\xi_{k}(y)-2s\delta^{2-2s}(x)\xi_{k}(x)+\widetilde{\omega}_{k}(x,y),

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\omega}_{k}(x,y)

\displaystyle=x\cdot\nabla(\xi_{k}\delta^{2-2s})(x)+y\cdot\nabla(\xi_{k}\delta^{2-2s})(y)

\displaystyle-(N+2s)\frac{\big[\xi_{k}(x)\delta^{2-2s}(x)-\xi_{k}(y)\delta^{2-2s}(y)\big]y\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\delta^{2-2s}(x)x\cdot\nabla\xi_{k}(x)+\delta^{2-2s}(y)y\cdot\nabla\xi_{k}(y)-(N+2s)\frac{\delta^{2-2s}(x)\big[\xi_{k}(x)-\xi_{k}(y)\big]y\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\xi_{k}x\cdot\nabla\delta^{2-2s}(x)+\xi_{k}y\cdot\nabla\delta^{2-2s}(y)-(N+2s)\frac{\xi_{k}(y)\big[\delta^{2-2s}(x)-\delta^{2-2s}(y)\big]y\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}

(4.3)

\displaystyle:=p_{k}(x,y)+q_{k}(x,y)

With this, the right hand-side of (
4.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\big(u\xi_{k}(x)-u\xi_{k}(y)\big)^{2}\omega_{k}(x,y)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{N-2s}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u\xi_{k}(x)-u\xi_{k}(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\delta^{2-2s}(x)\xi_{k}(x)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle+\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}(p_{k}(x,y)+q_{k}(x,y))dxdy

(4.4)

\displaystyle\quad:=G_{k}+P_{k}+Q_{k},

with

(4.5)

P_{k}=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u\xi_{k}(x)-u\xi_{k}(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}p_{k}(x,y)dxdy.

and

(4.6)

Q_{k}=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u\xi_{k}(x)-u\xi_{k}(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}q_{k}(x,y)dxdy.

Since
\xi_{k}
is supported away from the boundary and
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
, we can write the LHS of (
4.2
) as

\displaystyle(LHS)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\Omega}\xi_{k}(x)\delta^{2-2s}(x)x\cdot\nabla(u\xi_{k})\Big[u(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}\xi_{k}-I_{s}(u,\xi_{k})\Big]\,dx,

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{\Omega}\xi_{k}^{2}(x)\delta^{2-2s}(x)x\cdot\nabla(u\xi_{k})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u\,dx,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\qquad:=E_{k}+F_{k}.

Putting everything all together, we get

(4.8)

\displaystyle E_{k}+F_{k}=G_{k}+P_{k}+Q_{k},

where

(4.9)

\displaystyle G_{k}:=\frac{N-2s}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u\xi_{k}(x)-u\xi_{k}(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\delta^{2-2s}(x)\xi_{k}(x)\,dx\,dy,

(4.10)

\displaystyle E_{k}:=2\int_{\Omega}\xi_{k}(x)\delta^{2-2s}(x)x\cdot\nabla(u\xi_{k})\Big[u(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}\xi_{k}-I_{s}(u,\xi_{k})\Big]\,dx,

(4.11)

\displaystyle F_{k}:=2\int_{\Omega}\xi_{k}^{2}(x)\delta^{2-2s}(x)x\cdot\nabla(u\xi_{k})(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u\,dx.

and
P_{k}
and
Q_{k}
are respectively given by (
4.5
) and (
4.6
). The rest of the proof consist of passing to the limit in (
4.8
). The limit of the term
F_{k}
is easy to compute. Indeed, using that
(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, it is not difficult to check that (see e.g
[
5
, Proof of Theorem 1.2]
)

(4.12)

\lim_{k\to\infty}F_{k}=2\int_{\Omega}\delta^{2-2s}(x)x\cdot\nabla u(x)(-\Delta)^{s}_{\Omega}u(x)dx.

The terms
E_{k}
,
G_{k}
and
G_{k}
are also relatively easy to deal with. The main ingredient are already collected in
[
4
]
. The new term that we have to deal with is
P_{k}
and it is where the Sobolev regularity proved in Lemma
3.3
enters at stage.

5.
Passing to the limits

This section is devoted to the computation of the limits of the main term in the decomposition (
4
). We start by computing the contribution coming from
Q_{k}
.

5.1.
Limit of remainders term

In this subsection, we compute the limit of the remainder term
Q_{k}(\Omega)
. We recall its definition for the reader convenience.

Q_{k}=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u\xi_{k}(x)-u\xi_{k}(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}q_{k}(x,y)dxdy.

where the weight
q_{k}(x,y)
is given by:

q_{k}(x,y)=\xi_{k}x\cdot\nabla\delta^{2-2s}(x)+\xi_{k}y\cdot\nabla\delta^{2-2s}(y)-(N+2s)\frac{\xi_{k}(y)\big[\delta^{2-2s}(y)-\delta^{2-2s}(x)\big]y\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
Q_{k}
be defined as in (
4.6
). Then we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}Q_{k}=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\omega_{s}(x,y)dxdy

with

\omega_{s}(x,y)=x\cdot\nabla\delta^{2-2s}(x)+y\cdot\nabla\delta^{2-2s}(y)-(N+2s)\frac{\big[\delta^{2-2s}(x)-\delta^{2-2s}(y)\big]y\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}

We note that the LHS of (
5.1
) is well defined in view of Lemma
3.3
.

Proof.

The proof is an adaptation of
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
. First recall the definition of
Q_{k}
:

We start by splitting
Q_{k}
into two pieces as follows:

\displaystyle Q_{k}

\displaystyle=c_{N,s}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{(u(x)\xi_{k}(x)-u(y)\xi_{k}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\xi_{k}x\cdot\nabla\delta^{2-2s}dxdy

\displaystyle+\frac{(N+2s)c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{(u(x)\xi_{k}(x)-u(y)\xi_{k}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\frac{\xi_{k}(y)\big[\delta^{2-2s}(x)-\delta^{2-2s}(y)\big]y\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}dxdy

(5.2)

\displaystyle:=(1)_{k}+(2)_{k}.

We are now going to compute the limits of the two terms separately. The arguments for computing the two terms are very similar. We provide a full and detailed justification for the term
(1)_{k}
; the interested reader can easily verify that the same reasoning applies, without much changes, to

(2)_{k}
. Now we proceed to the calculation of the limit of
(1)_{k}
. For sake of simplicity, we let

(5.3)

w(x):=x\cdot\nabla\delta^{2-2s}=\delta^{1-2s}(x)[x\cdot\nabla\delta(x)]\quad\textrm{for $x\in\Omega$}.

We proceed into two steps. First we prove that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u\xi_{k}(x)-u\xi_{k}(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)dx\,dy=\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)dx\,dy.

To that aim, we set
\beta_{k}:=\beta(k\delta)
so that
\xi_{k}(x)=1-\beta_{k}(x)
. For
\varepsilon>0
, we put
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega\>:\>\delta(x)<\varepsilon\}
.
Since
u\beta_{k}
vanishes in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\frac{2}{k}}
,
0\leq\beta_{k}\leq 1
and
|{\beta_{k}}(x)-{\beta_{k}}(y)|\leq C\min\{k|x-y|,1\}
for
x,y\in\Omega
, we observe that

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{[u(x){\beta_{k}}(x)-u(y){\beta_{k}}(y)]^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)dydx

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}\times\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}\frac{[u(x){\beta_{k}}(x)-u(y){\beta_{k}}(y)]^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)\ dydx+\int_{A_{{\frac{2}{k}}}}u(x)^{2}{\beta_{k}}(x)^{2}w(x)\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}|x-y|^{-N-2s}\ dydx

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}\times\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}\frac{\bigl[u(x)\bigl({\beta_{k}}(x)-{\beta_{k}}(y)\bigr)+{\beta_{k}}(y)\bigl(u(x)-u(y)\bigr)\bigr]^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)\ dydx

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{\Omega_{{\frac{2}{k}}}}u(x)^{2}{\rm dist}(x,\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}})^{-2s}w(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}u^{2}(x)w(x)\int_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}\frac{({\beta_{k}}(x)-{\beta_{k}}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\ dydx+\iint_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}\times\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)dydx

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{\Omega_{{\frac{2}{k}}}}u(x)^{2}\delta^{-2s}(x)w(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}u^{2}(x)w(x)\int_{\Omega_{\frac{1}{k}}(x)}|x-y|^{2-2s-N}dydx+C\int_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}u^{2}(x)w(x)\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\frac{1}{k}}(x)}|x-y|^{-N-2s}dydx

\displaystyle\quad+\iint_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}\times\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)dydx+C\int_{\Omega_{{\frac{2}{k}}}}u(x)^{2}w(x)\delta^{-2s}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{2s}\int_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}u^{2}(x)w(x)dx+\iint_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}\times\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)dydx+C\int_{\Omega_{{\frac{2}{k}}}}u(x)^{2}w(x)\delta^{-2s}(x)dx

(5.5)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}u^{2}(x)w(x)\delta^{-2s}(x)dx+\iint_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}\times\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)dydx.

Recalling (
5.3
), we have on the one hand

\int_{\Omega}u^{2}(x)w(x)\delta^{-2s}(x)dx=\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{\delta^{2(2s-1/2)}}x\cdot\nabla\delta\,dx\leq C[u]^{2}_{H^{2s-1/2}(\Omega)}<\infty,

where in the last line we have used the classical Hardy inequality see e.g
[
27
, Page 319, Equation (9)]
and Theorem
3.2
with
\varepsilon=1-s
. Consequently

(5.6)

\int_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}u^{2}(x)w(x)\delta^{-2s}(x)dx\to 0\qquad\text{as $k\to\infty$.}

In the other hand, thanks to Lemma
3.3
we also have

(5.7)

\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)dydx<\infty,

and therefore

(5.8)

\int_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}\int_{\Omega_{\frac{4}{k}}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)dydx\to 0\qquad\text{as $k\to\infty$.}

In view of (
5.5
), (
5.6
) and (
5.8
), we have proved that

(5.9)

\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u\beta_{k}(x)-u\beta_{k}(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)dx\,dy\quad\to\quad 0\qquad\textrm{as $k\to\infty$}.

Since
\xi_{k}=1-\beta_{k}
, the identity (
5.4
) follows easily from (
5.9
). Finally, arguing exactly as in the proof of (
5.17
) in Lemma
5.2
, we see that

(5.10)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u\xi_{k}(x)-u\xi_{k}(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)\beta_{k}(x)dx\,dy=0

Putting together (
5.4
) and (
5.10
) we get

(5.11)

\lim_{k\to\infty}(1)_{k}=c_{N,s}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}w(x)dx.

With as similar argument it is also not difficult to check that

(5.12)

\lim_{k\to\infty}(2)_{k}=\frac{(N+2s)c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\frac{\big[\delta^{2-2s}(x)-\delta^{2-2s}(y)\big]y\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}

Combining (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) we arrive at (
5.1
). The proof is finish.
∎

5.2.
Limit of the principal terms

We start with computing the limit of
G_{k}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
s\in(1/2,1)
and
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
. With the notation above, we have:

(5.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}G_{k}=\frac{N-2s}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\delta^{2-2s}(x)dxdy.

Proof.

The proof follows from
[
4
, Lemma 2.1, and Lemma 2.2]
with a minor modifications. We add the full details here for sake of completeness. Since
\delta^{2-2s}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, it is clearly enough to prove that

(5.14)

\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u\xi_{k}(x)-u\xi_{k}(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\xi_{k}(x)dxdy\quad\to\quad\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy\qquad\textrm{as $k\to\infty$}.

This essentially follows from
[
4
, Lemma 2.3]
. Indeed, let
\widetilde{u}
be the trivial extension of
u
to the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then by the classical Hardy inequality (see e.g
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
) we have that
\widetilde{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
. Here,
\widetilde{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
is the completion of smooth functions with compact support with respect to the semi-norm
\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy
. It is plain that

(5.15)

[u\beta_{k}]^{2}_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\leq[\widetilde{u}\beta_{k}]^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

and by
[
4
, Lemma 2.7]
the right hand side of (
5.15
) converges to zero; we deduce that the left hand side goes to zero as well. Consequently, since
\xi_{k}=1-\beta_{k}
, we have that

(5.16)

u\xi_{k}\to u\quad\textrm{in $H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)$}.

It remains now to prove that

(5.17)

a_{k}:=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\big(u\xi_{k}(x)-u\xi_{k}(y)\big)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\beta_{k}(x)dxdy\to 0\qquad\textrm{as \quad$k\to\infty$}.

For that aim, we let
L_{k},L:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}

REVOLUTIONIZING ORAL CANCER SCREENING: UNLEASHING THE SYNERGY OF LIQUID-BASED CYTOLOGY, THE BETHESDA SYSTEM, AND CELL BLOCK INNOVATION WITH HPV DETECTION

Parul Verma, Pinki Pandey, Alka Yadav, Roopak Aggarwal, Savita Agarwal, Himanshu Joshi; Department of Pathology, Uttar Pradesh University of Medical Sciences, Etawah, Uttar Pradesh, India

Background: Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma (OSCC) constitutes over 90% of oral neoplasms and is the third most common cancer in India, particularly among males. The high incidence is linked to risk factors such as tobacco use, smoking, alcohol consumption, and HPV infection. Accurate early detection of OSCC is crucial for improving survival rates. Objective: This study aims to categorize premalignant and malignant oral lesions using The Bethesda System for Reporting Cervical Cytology 2014, assess HPV prevalence in oral cancer using P16 and HPV markers in cell blocks, and compare the sensitivity of these markers. Methods: A prospective analytical cross-sectional study was conducted at UPUMS, Saifai, from January 2023 to July 2024. A total of 200 patients with oral lesions were sampled using the Orcellex brush, followed by liquid-based cytology (LBC) using the BD SurePath system. Cytological assessment was performed using The Bethesda System for reporting Cervical Cytology, and cell blocks were prepared for immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining with P16 and HPV markers. Data were analyzed using SPSS-23, with statistical significance set at p < 0.05. Results: The Bethesda System showed a sensitivity of 71.7%, specificity of 90.3%, positive predictive value (PPV) of 94.3%, and negative predictive value (NPV) of 58.9% in detecting oral lesions. P16 expression was generally more sensitive (20%) than HPV positivity (13.3%), particularly in high-grade lesions like HSIL and OSCC, indicating its potential as a reliable marker for HPV-related oral oncogenesis. Conclusion: The study highlights the effectiveness of the Bethesda System, initially designed for cervical cytology, in oral cytology screening. The incorporation of P16 IHC analysis enhances diagnostic accuracy, particularly for high-grade lesions. The findings support the adoption of a standardized classification system for oral cytology and the integration of P16 as a sensitive biomarker for early detection and management of OSCC.

DIAGNOSTIC UTILITY OF FINE NEEDLE ASPIRATION CYTOLOGY IN MUSCULOSKELETAL TUMORS

Samudra Basu1, Asit Ranjan Mridha1, Adarsh Barwad1, T Balamurugan1, Deepali Jain1, Ekta Dhamija2, Shah Alam Khan3, Sameer Bakhshi4, Sameer Rastogi4; 1Department of Pathology, Dr. BRA IRCH, All India Institute of Medical Sciences, New Delhi, India, 2Department of Radiodiagnosis, Dr. BRA IRCH, All India Institute of Medical Sciences, New Delhi, India, 3Department of Orthopedics, Dr. BRA IRCH, All India Institute of Medical Sciences, New Delhi, India, 4Department of Medical Oncology, Dr. BRA IRCH, All India Institute of Medical Sciences, New Delhi, India

Background: Mesenchymal tumors constitute a complex, heterogeneous, group of neoplasms with a wide variation in clinical behaviors, management and outcomes. Core needle biopsy is the standard diagnostic technique as it provides preserved morphology and opportunity for ancillary testing for diagnosis. Fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) has its own advantages and limitations in this scenario. Aim and Objective: To assess the role of FNAC in the diagnosis of bone and soft tissue tumors. Materials and Methods: This prospective study done over a period of one and a half years at AIIMS, New Delhi, included 116 consecutive patients with suspected bone and soft tissue tumors. Lipomas were excluded. The cases were subjected to FNAC followed by core biopsy in the same sitting, under image-guidance. Cell blocks were prepared and ICC and FISH were performed whenever required. The cases were independently assessed by three pathology experts in the field without the knowledge of biopsy reports. Observation and Results: The cases were classified based on the dominant morphological patterns. Adequate material was obtained in 71.4% of the samples. FNAC showed a sensitivity of 97.67%, specificity of 88.00% and an overall diagnostic accuracy of 93.71%. Ancillary techniques carried out on cellblock and smears helped provide therapeutically significant diagnoses. Conclusions: FNAC is a safe, rapid, cost-effective procedure with a good diagnostic yield for the musculoskeletal tumors. With the help of ancillary tests, FNAC proves to be a useful modality for the diagnosis and treatment of musculoskeletal tumors.

DISTINGUISHING THE AGGRESSIVE SUBTYPES OF PAPILLARY CARCINOMA OF THYROID FROM THE CLASSIC SUBTYPE ON FINE NEEDLE ASPIRATION CYTOLOGY SPECIMEN: A CYTOMORPHOLOGICAL AND MORPHOMETRIC STUDY

Sanjana S James, Madhusmita Sethy, Suvendu Purkait, Pritinanda Mishra, Mukund Namdev Sable, Pavithra Ayyanar, Amit Kumar Adhya; Department of Pathology and Laboratory Medicine, All India Institute of Medical Sciences, Bhubaneswar, Odisha, India

Background: The aggressive subtypes of papillary carcinoma thyroid (PTC) include the tall, hobnail, and columnar cell PTCs. Their preoperative recognition would be advantageous and would help the surgeon to plan a more effective treatment regimen. In this study, we aim to determine if the cytomorphological features of these aggressive subtypes of PTC are sufficiently characteristic to enable their preoperative diagnosis on FNAC. Materials and Methods: We compared the cytological features of 21 cases of histologically proven aggressive subtypes with 20 cases of the classic PTC (C-PTC) using morphometry and cytomorphology. Thirty-seven parameters, including background, architecture, cellular, and nuclear features were analyzed using a semi-quantitative scoring system. The statistical significance of the data was determined using Fisher’s probability test and Chi-square test. Results: The presence of isolated tumor cells, spindle cells, tall cells, and tail-like cells was seen in a significantly higher number of cases of T-PTC than C-PTC. The distinctive features of CC-PTC were columnar cells with elongated pseudostratified nuclei. The features of H- PTC included the presence of clusters of cells with knobby outlines and large, round cells having abundant cytoplasm. Conclusions: We found that the cytomorphological features of T-PTC, H-PTC, and CC-PTC are distinctive. The most helpful features in distinguishing T-PTC from C-PTC include the presence of tall cells, spindle cells, and tail-like cells with irregular nuclear contours. CC- PTC is identified by the presence of columnar cells exhibiting elongated, pseudostratified nuclei. In contrast, H-PTC is delineated by clusters of cells with irregular, knobby contours and large, round cells with abundant cytoplasm.

EVALUATION OF THE APPLICATION OF “THE BETHESDA SYSTEM – 2023” FOR REPORTING FINE NEEDLE ASPIRATION CYTOLOGY OF THYROID LESIONS, AND IT’S CORRELATION WITH HISTOPATHOLOGY – ONE YEAR EXPERIENCE IN A TERTIARY CARE CENTRE IN KERALA

Tinu Francis Chandy, Lekha K Nair; Department of Pathology, Amala Institute of Medical Sciences, Thrissur, Kerala, India

Introduction: Fine needle aspiration cytology (FNACs) of thyroid lesions are commonly encountered in routine cytopathology practice. FNACs are crucial in determining the management of thyroid lesions. This study is aimed at assessing the efficacy of the application of The Bethesda system 2023 in categorisation of thyroid lesions by FNAC, taking histopathology as the gold standard. Objectives: To classify thyroid lesions according to The Bethesda 2023 system and to assess its accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value and negative predictive value. Settings and Design: Retrospective descriptive study done over a period of 1 year from June 2023 to May 2024. Materials and Methods: All thyroid FNAC reports (given according to The Bethesda 2023 system) were retrieved and histopathological diagnosis of those available cases were correlated. Results: Out of the total 356 thyroid FNACs, only 84 cases had histopathological correlation. We had Category I – 1 case (1.2%), Category II - 50 cases (59.5%), Category III - none, Category IV - 9 cases (10.7%), Category V - 4 cases (4.8%), Category VI - 20 cases (23.8%). Histopathology correlation was done and 7 discordant cases were noted. The sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value of category II were found to be 93.75%, 86%, 90%, and 91% respectively. The same for category IV was found to be 80%, 98.7%, 88.8%, and 97.4%, and that for categories (V and VI taken together) was found to be 92%, 98.3%, 95.8%, and 96% respectively. The accuracy was found to be 91.6%. Risk of malignancy was well within the predicted values as by the Bethesda system for each category. Conclusion: The Bethesda 2023 System is a very efficient system for categorizing thyroid lesions by FNACs. In our study we could categorise most of the cases accurately. However, we noted a difficulty in accurately categorizing adenomatoid nodules and follicular neoplasms. A misdiagnosis of follicular neoplasm as adenomatoid nodule, results in patient loosing the chance of surgical management .We need further studies to support this and to consider the reintroduction of the category – ‘Suspicious of follicular neoplasm’, so that even a single patient with a follicular carcinoma is not missed and gets proper surgical management.

VENTRICULAR ENIGMAS: SQUASH CYTOLOGY’S CELLULAR SYMPHONY

Neelshi Pandey, Pinki Pandey, Alka Yadav, Roopak Aggarwal, Savita Agarwal, Himanshu Joshi; Department of Pathology, Uttar Pradesh University of Medical Sciences, Etawah, Uttar Pradesh, India

Introduction: Neoplasms of the ventricular system, though unified by location, exhibit a diverse range of histogenetic origins, necessitating accurate intraoperative diagnosis. Aim and Objective: This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of squash cytology in diagnosing ventricular lesions during surgery. Materials and Methods: A retrospective analysis of 155 cases of ventricular tumors, diagnosed between January 2013 and December 2023, was conducted. Clinical, surgical, and pathological data were retrieved, and squash cytology smears were prepared using biopsy samples. These were stained and analyzed, with diagnoses compared to final histopathological findings. Results: The most common location of ventricular lesions was fourth ventricle (46.5%) and common tumor type was ependymoma (33%), primarily located in the fourth ventricle. Other notable tumors included choroid plexus papilloma (13%) and craniopharyngioma (6%). A male predominance (70%) was observed, with a significant number of cases occurring in the first two decades of life. Headache is the most common symptom, especially in lateral ventricular tumors (45 cases). Nausea and vomiting were frequently associated with third ventricle lesions. Cytological patterns correlated well with histological and radiological features, though some diagnostic discrepancies were noted. Conclusion: Squash cytology proves to be a rapid, cost-effective diagnostic tool for intraoperative evaluation of ventricular neoplasms. However, certain histological similarities between different tumor types pose diagnostic challenges, underscoring the importance of comprehensive histopathological examination to ensure accurate diagnosis. Further research with larger cohorts and advanced imaging techniques is recommended to enhance diagnostic precision and patient outcomes.

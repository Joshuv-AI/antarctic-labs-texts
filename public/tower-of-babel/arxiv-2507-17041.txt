Twisted periods of modular forms
Tianyu Ni, Hui Xue

Abstract
. Let S k S_{k} denote the space of cusp forms of weight k k and level one. For 0 ≤ t ≤ k − 2 0\leq t\leq k-2 and primitive Dirichlet character χ \chi mod D D , we introduce twisted periods r t , χ r_{t,\chi} on S k S_{k} . We show that for a fixed natural number n n , if k k is sufficiently large relative to n n and D D , then any n n periods with the same twist but different indices are linearly independent. We also prove that if k k is sufficiently large relative to D D then any n n periods with the same index but different twists mod D D are linearly independent. These results are achieved by studying the trace of the products and Rankin-Cohen brackets of Eisenstein series of level D D with nebentypus. Moreover, we give two applications of our method. First, we prove certain identities that evaluate convolution sums of twisted divisor functions. Second, we show that Maeda’s conjecture implies a non-vanishing result on twisted central L L -values of normalized Hecke eigenforms.

1.
Introduction

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. For
N\geq 1
and a Dirichlet character
\chi
mod
N
, let
M_{k}(N,\chi)
and
S_{k}(N,\chi)
be the space of modular forms and cusp forms of weight
k
, level
N
and nebentypus
\chi
, respectively. We simply write
M_{k}(N)
and
S_{k}(N)
if
\chi
is trivial, and use
M_{k}
and
S_{k}
to denote
M_{k}(1)
and
S_{k}(1)
when
N=1
.
For each
0\leq t\leq k-2
, the
t
-th period of
f\in S_{k}
is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle r_{t}(f):=\int_{0}^{i\infty}f(z)z^{t}dz=\frac{t!}{(-2\pi i)^{t+1}}L(f,t+1).

Here the
L
-function of
f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)q^{n}
is
L(f,s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)n^{-s}
, which converges for
\re(s)
sufficiently large and can be extended analytically to the whole complex plane. Note that each
r_{t}
can be regarded an element in
S_{k}^{\ast}:=\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(S_{k},\mathbb{C})
. The result of Eichler and Shimura (
[
5
,
18
,
13
]
) asserts that odd periods
r_{1},r_{3},...,r_{k-3}
(or even periods
r_{0},r_{2},...,r_{k-2}
) span the vector space
S_{k}^{\ast}
. However, these periods are not linearly independent. In fact, they are subject to many linear dependence relations, called the Eichler-Shiumra relations
[
18
]
.

This leads to a natural question. Which periods are linearly independent? The first work in this direction that we are aware of is
[
6
]
, in which Fukuhara found an explicit subset of odd periods that forms a basis for
S_{k}^{\ast}
. Recently, Lei et al.
[
16
,
15
]
have provided some evidence for the linear independence of odd periods and even periods for modular forms, respectively. Furthermore, the linear independence between an odd and an even periods has been addressed in
[
25
]
.

In this paper, we consider the
twisted periods
of modular forms, which are natural generalizations of periods. Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character mod
D
. For
0\leq n\leq k-2
, we define the
n
-th twisted period for
f\in S_{k}
(
[
7
, p. 978]
) to be

(1.2)

\displaystyle r_{n,\chi}(f):=\int_{0}^{i\infty}f_{\chi}(x)z^{n}dz=\frac{n!}{(-2\pi i)^{n+1}}L(f,\chi,n+1).

Here the twist
f_{\chi}(z):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi(n)a_{f}(n)q^{n}
is an element in
S_{k}(D^{2},\chi^{2})
(
[
9
, Proposition 14.19]
).
The twisted
L
-function
L(f,\chi,s):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi(n)a_{f}(n)n^{-s}
, originally defined for
\re(s)\gg 0
, can be analytically continued to the whole complex plane. For a normalized Hecke eigenform
f\in S_{k}
, we have
(
[
9
, Proposition 14.20]
):

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda(f,\chi,s)=i^{k}w_{\chi}\Lambda(f,\overline{\chi},k-s),

where
\Lambda(f,\chi,s)=(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(f,\chi,s)
is the completed twisted
L
-function and
w_{\chi}:=\frac{G(\chi)^{2}}{D}
.
One can view each
r_{n,\chi}
as an element in
S_{k}^{\ast}
.

Since twisted periods can be regarded as elements in
S_{k}^{\ast}
, the first question about these periods is whether they span the whole space of
S_{k}^{\ast}
. The case of
D=4
has been treated by Kina
[
11
, Corollary 5.8]
. The second question is whether we can find Eichler-Shimura type linear relations among them.
Furthermore, we would like to know which periods are linearly independent. The first two questions are beyond the scope of this paper. But, we will propose related conjectures in the last section. In this paper, extending the ideas of
[
16
,
15
]
, we will provide some evidence for the linear independence of twisted periods.

We now state our results. First, we consider the linear independence of twisted periods for the same character.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer,
D\geq 1
an odd square-free integer, and
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character mod
D
. For
K\gg_{n,D}1
, if
3\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<...<\ell_{n}\leq\frac{K-2}{2}
are integers such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{\ell_{i}}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, then the set of twisted periods
\{r_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
on
S_{K}
is linearly independent.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer,
D\geq 1
an odd square-free integer, and
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character mod
D
. For
K\gg_{n,D}1
, if
3\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<...<\ell_{n}\leq\frac{K-4}{2}
are integers such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{\ell_{i}}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, then the set of twisted periods
\{r_{\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
on
S_{K}
is linearly independent.

We then consider the linear independence of twisted periods with the same index but for different characters mod
D
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
D\geq 1
be an odd square-free integer. For
K\gg_{D}1
, if
3\leq\ell\leq\frac{K-2}{2}
is an integer,
\chi_{1},...,\chi_{n}
are primitive Dirichlet characters mod
D
such that
\chi_{i}(-1)=(-1)^{\ell}
and
\chi_{i}(2)
are distinct for
1\leq i\leq n
, then the set of twisted periods
\{r_{\ell-1,\chi_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
on
S_{K}
is linearly independent.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
D\geq 1
be an odd square-free integer. For
K\gg_{D}1
, if
3\leq\ell\leq\frac{K-4}{2}
is an integer and
\chi_{1},...,\chi_{n}
are primitive Dirichlet characters mod
D
such that
\chi_{i}(-1)=(-1)^{\ell}
and
\chi_{i}(2)
are distinct for
1\leq i\leq n
, then the set of twisted periods
\{r_{\ell,\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
on
S_{K}
is linearly independent.

We want to point out that when
D=1
, the twisted periods involved in Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
are the odd periods considered in
[
16
]
, and the twisted periods treated in Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
are exactly the even periods studied in
[
15
]
.
Throughout the paper we assume that
D\geq 1
is a positive odd square-free integer in order to avoid technical complications.

We sketch the rough idea of the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
. The key is to study the linear independence of the kernel functions that give rise to the twisted periods. For
f,g\in S_{K}
, let
\langle f,g\rangle
denote the Petersson inner product. Then there is a cusp form
R_{K,n,\chi}
such that
r_{n,\chi}(f)=\langle R_{K,n,\chi},f\rangle
for all
f\in S_{K}
. In general,
R_{K,n,\chi}
is expected to have transcendental Fourier coefficient, which is not convenient to study. Instead of considering
R_{K,n,\chi}
, we study the modular form
\Tr_{1}^{D}(G_{\ell,\chi}(z)G_{K-\ell,\overline{\chi}}(z))\in M_{K}
. Here the Eisenstein series
G_{k,\chi}
of weight
k
(see Section
3
for details) is given by

(1.4)

\displaystyle G_{k,\chi}(z):

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{k-1,\chi}(n)q^{n}\in M_{k}\left(D,\chi\right)

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k-1,\chi}(n):

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{(k-1)!D^{k}}{(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\overline{\chi})}L(k,\overline{\chi})&n=0,\\
\sum\limits_{d\mid n}\chi(d)d^{k-1}&n\geq 1.\end{cases}\quad

Additionally, for
N\mid M
,
\Tr_{N}^{M}
is the trace map

(1.6)

\displaystyle\Tr_{N}^{M}:M_{m}(M)\rightarrow M_{m}(N),\quad g\mapsto\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(M)\backslash\Gamma_{0}(N)}g|_{m}\gamma,

where for any real number
m
and
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\Gl_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
the slash operator
[
2
, Theorem 7.1]
is

(1.7)

\displaystyle g(z)|_{m}\gamma=\det(\gamma)^{m/2}(cz+d)^{-m}g\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right).

We will show that the cuspidal projection of
\Tr_{1}^{D}(G_{\ell,\chi}(z)G_{K-\ell,\overline{\chi}}(z))
is

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell,\chi}(z):=\Tr_{1}^{D}(G_{\ell,\chi}(z)G_{K-\ell,\overline{\chi}}(z))-\frac{2\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(0)\sigma_{k-1,\overline{\chi}}(0)}{\zeta(1-K)}G_{K}(z),

where
G_{K}(z)=\frac{\zeta(1-K)}{2}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_{K-1}(n)q^{n}
,
\zeta(1-K)=-\frac{B_{K}}{K}
, and
B_{K}
is the
K
-th Bernoulli number, see Section
3
. Then we can relate the twisted period
r_{\ell-1,\chi}
and the cusp form
\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell,\chi}
through the Rankin-Selberg method (Proposition
2.4
), which in turn allows us to show the equivalence between the linear independence of
\{r_{\ell-1,\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and linear independence of
\{\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
(Proposition
2.7
). Applying the same methodology in
[
16
,
15
]
, we study the linear independence of
\{\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
by investigating the matrix
M_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n},\chi}
(
4.1
) formed by the Fourier coefficients of
\{\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Similarly, we can show that
\{r_{\ell-1,\chi_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent if and only if
\{\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell,\chi_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent. Theorem
1.3
can be proved by showing that the matrix
P_{K,\ell,\chi_{1},...,\ell_{n}}
(
4.45
) formed by the Fourier coefficients of
\{\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell,\chi_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is non-singular.

Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
can be proved in a similar fashion. But we need to consider the Rankin-Cohen bracket (see Section
2
) of Eisenstein series. Define

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{K,\ell,\chi}(z)=\Tr^{D}_{1}([G_{\ell,\chi}(z),G_{K-2-\ell,\overline{\chi}}(z)]_{1}).

By the Rankin-Selberg convolution (Proposition
2.6
), we can show the equivalence between the linear independence of
\{r_{\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
(
\{r_{\ell,\chi_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
) and the linear independence of
\{\mathcal{G}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
(
\{\mathcal{G}_{K,\ell,\chi_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
). Then Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
can be proved by showing the non-singularity of
N_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n},\chi}
(
4.26
) and
Q_{K,\ell,\chi_{1},...,\ell_{n}}
(
4.65
), respectively.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review the Rankin-Selberg method and prove the equivalence between the linear independence of the twisted periods and the linear independence of the corresponding cusp forms. In Section

and

formed by the Fourier coefficients of
\{\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\mathcal{G}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell,\chi_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and
\{\mathcal{G}_{K,\ell,\chi_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, respectively. We then give criteria for the non-singularity of these matrices. Finally, we prove Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
,
1.3
and

by showing the corresponding matrices are non-singular. In Section

-values of normalized Hecke eigenforms. In the last section, we propose several conjectures on spanning

by twisted periods.

2.
Rankin-Selberg convolution

In this section, we derive the equivalence between the linear independence of twisted periods and the linear independence of the corresponding kernel cusp forms. For two elements
f
and
g
of
M_{k}(N)
such that
fg
is a cuspform, the Petersson inner product is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(N)}=\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}y^{k}d\mu,

Here
z=x+iy
and
d\mu=dxdy/y^{2}
is the
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant measure on the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
. We use
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to denote
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(N)}
if the level is clear from the context.

Let us first recall the definition of Rankin-Cohen brackets for modular forms.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f(z)\in M_{a}(\Gamma)
and
g(z)\in M_{b}(\Gamma)
be modular forms for some congruence subgroup
\Gamma
of weights
a
and
b
, respectively. For an nonnegative integer
e
, we define the
e
-th Rankin-Cohen bracket as

(2.2)

\displaystyle[f(z),g(z)]_{e}:=\sum_{r=0}^{e}(-1)^{r}\binom{e+a-1}{e-r}\binom{e+b-1}{r}f(z)^{(r)}g(z)^{(e-r)},

where
f(z)^{(r)}
is the
r
-th normalized derivative
f(z)^{(r)}:=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{r}}\frac{d^{r}f(z)}{dz^{r}}
of
f
. Here
a,b
can be in
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
and the binomial coefficients are defined through gamma functions. Moreover,
[f,g]_{e}\in M_{a+b+2e}(\Gamma)
and
[f,g]_{e}\in S_{a+b+2e}(\Gamma)
for
e>1
; see
[
2
, Theorem 7.1]
. We remark that the Rankin-Cohen bracket defined in Zagier
[
26
, (73)]
is related to (
2.2
) through
F_{e}^{(a,b)}(f(z),g(z))=(-2\pi i)^{e}e![f(z),g(z)]_{e}
; see
[
15
, (1.1)]
.

Recall the following classical result on the Rankin-Selberg convolution, which was reformulated and generalized in Zagier
[
26
]
, keeping in mind the difference between our definition of the Rankin-Cohen bracket and the one used therein.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
26
, Propsition 6]
)
.

Let
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
be real numbers with
k_{2}\geq k_{1}+2>2
. Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b(n)q^{n}
be modular forms in
S_{k}(N,\chi)
and
M_{k_{1}}(N,\chi_{1})
, where
k=k_{1}+k_{2}+2e,e\geq 0
and
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
.
Then

(2.3)

\displaystyle\langle f,[g,E^{*}_{k_{2},N}(\cdot;\chi_{2})]_{e}\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(N)}=\frac{(-1)^{e}}{e!}\frac{\Gamma(k-1)\Gamma(k_{2}+e)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}\Gamma(k_{2})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)\overline{b(n)}}{n^{k_{1}+k_{2}+e-1}}.

Here
E_{k,N}^{\ast}(z;\chi)
is the Eisenstein series for the cusp at infinity of level
N

[
19
, p. 272]
:

(2.4)

\displaystyle E_{k,N}^{\ast}(z;\chi)=\sum_{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(N)}\frac{\chi(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
3\leq\ell\leq\frac{K-2}{2}
be an integer,

\chi
a primitive Dirichlet character mod
D
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{\ell}
, and
f\in S_{K}
be a normalized Hecke eigenform. Then

(2.5)

\displaystyle\langle f,G_{\ell,\chi}G_{K-\ell,\overline{\chi}}\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(D)}=\frac{\Gamma(K-1)(K-\ell-1)!D^{K-\ell}}{(4\pi)^{K-1}(2\pi i)^{K-\ell}\overline{G(\chi)}}L(f,K-1)L(f,\overline{\chi},K-\ell).

Proof.

Let
k=K-\ell
. Note that (
3.23
) implies that

(2.6)

\displaystyle G_{k,\overline{\chi}}(z)=\frac{(k-1)!D^{k}L(k,\chi)}{(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\chi)}E_{k,D}^{\ast}(z;\chi).

As
k\geq\ell+2
, using Lemma
2.1
for
e=0
, (
1.4
) and the fact that
\overline{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(n)}=\sigma_{\ell-1,\overline{\chi}}(n)
, we get

(2.7)

\displaystyle\langle f,G_{\ell,\chi}G_{K-\ell,\overline{\chi}}\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(D)}=\frac{\Gamma(K-1)(K-\ell-1)!D^{K-\ell}\overline{L(K-\ell,\chi)}}{(4\pi)^{K-1}(2\pi i)^{K-\ell}\overline{G(\chi)}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{f}(n){\sigma_{\ell-1,\overline{\chi}}(n)}}{n^{K-1}}.

By Lemma
2.3
, we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{f}(n)\sigma_{\ell-1,\overline{\chi}}(n)}{n^{K-1}}=\frac{L(f,K-1)L(f,\overline{\chi},K-\ell)}{L(K-\ell,\overline{\chi})}.

Combining (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) gives the result.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)q^{n}\in S_{K}
be a normalized Hecke eigenform. Then for a Dirichlet character
\chi
, an integer
\ell\geq 3
and a complex number
s
with
\re(s)>\ell+\frac{K-1}{2}
, we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{f}(n)\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(n)}{n^{s}}=\frac{L(f,s)L(f,\chi,s-\ell+1)}{L(2s-\ell+2-K,\chi)}.

Proof.

Since
f\in S_{K}
is a normalized Hecke eigenform, we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle a_{f}(m)a_{f}(n)=\sum_{d\mid(m,n)}a_{f}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right)d^{K-1}.

Note that
s
is within the region of convergence for all the involved series. Using above Hecke relation, we get

(2.11)

\displaystyle L(f,s)L(f,\chi,s-\ell+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}a_{f}(m)a_{f}(n)\chi(n)(mn)^{-s}n^{\ell-1}

(2.12)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geq 1\\
d\mid(m,n)\end{subarray}}\chi(n)a_{f}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right)d^{K-1}(mn)^{-s}n^{\ell-1}

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\geq 1}\chi(d)d^{K-1+\ell-1-2s}\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\chi(n)a_{f}(mn)(mn)^{-s}n^{\ell-1}

(2.14)

\displaystyle=L(2s-\ell+2-K,\chi)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{f}(n)\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(n)}{n^{s}},

where the second to last inequality comes from the change of variables
m\mapsto dm
and
n\mapsto dn
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
3\leq\ell\leq\frac{K-2}{2}
be integers and
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character mod
D
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{\ell}
, and
f\in S_{K}
be a normalized Hecke eigenform. Then

(2.15)

\displaystyle\langle f,\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell,\chi}\rangle=\frac{\Gamma(K-1)(K-\ell-1)!D^{K-\ell}}{(4\pi)^{K-1}(2\pi i)^{K-\ell}\overline{G(\chi)}}L(f,K-1)L(f,\overline{\chi},K-\ell),

where
\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell,\chi}
is defined in (
1.8
).

Proof.

Since
\langle f,G_{K}\rangle=0
, and
\langle f,g\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(M)}=\langle f,\Tr_{N}^{M}g\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(N)}
for
N\mid M
,
f\in S_{K}(N)
,
g\in M_{K}(M)
(see
[
8
, p. 271]
), we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle\langle f,\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell,\chi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,\Tr_{1}^{D}(G_{\ell,\chi}(z)G_{K-\ell,\overline{\chi}}(z))\rangle

(2.17)

\displaystyle=\langle f,G_{\ell,\chi}G_{K-\ell,\overline{\chi}}\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(|D|)}.

Now the result follows from Proposition
2.2
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
3\leq\ell\leq\frac{K-4}{2}
and
k=K-2-\ell
be integers,
\chi
a primitive Dirichlet character mod
D
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{\ell}
, and
f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)q^{n}\in S_{K}
be a normalized Hecke eigenform. Then

(2.18)

\displaystyle\langle f,[G_{\ell,\chi},G_{k,\overline{\chi}}]_{1}\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(D)}=\frac{-k!(K-2)!D^{k}L(k,\overline{\chi})}{(4\pi)^{K-1}(2\pi i)^{k}\overline{G(\chi)}L(k+1,\overline{\chi})}L(f,K-2)L(f,\overline{\chi},K-\ell-1).

Proof.

Again, by Lemma
2.1
(for
k_{1}=\ell,k_{2}=k
), (
3.23
) and the fact that
\overline{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(n)}=\sigma_{\ell-1,\overline{\chi}}(n)
, we get

(2.19)

\displaystyle\langle f,[G_{\ell,\chi},G_{k,\overline{\chi}}]_{1}\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(D)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Gamma(K-1)\Gamma(k+1)}{(4\pi)^{K-1}\Gamma(k)}\cdot\frac{(k-1)!D^{k}\overline{L(k,\chi)}}{(2\pi i)^{k}\overline{G(\chi)}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{f}(n)\sigma_{\ell-1,\overline{\chi}}(n)}{n^{K-2}}.

As
K-2>\ell+\frac{K-1}{2}
, we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{f}(n)\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(n)}{n^{K-2}}=\frac{L(f,K-2)L(f,\overline{\chi},K-1-\ell)}{L(K-1-\ell,\overline{\chi})},

by Lemma
2.3
. Plugging (
2.20
) into (
2.19
) gives the result.
∎

The following proposition generalizes
[
12
, Proposition 1]
to the Rankin-Cohen bracket.

Proposition 2.6
.

Under the hypothesis of Proposition
2.5
, we have

(2.21)

\displaystyle\langle f,\mathcal{G}_{K,\ell,\chi}\rangle=\frac{-k!(K-2)!D^{k}L(k,\overline{\chi})}{(4\pi)^{K-1}(2\pi i)^{k}\overline{G(\chi)}L(k+1,\overline{\chi})}L(f,K-2)L(f,\overline{\chi},K-\ell-1).

Proof.

By definition (
1.9
),

\langle f,\mathcal{G}_{K,\ell,\chi}\rangle=\langle f,\Tr_{1}^{D}[G_{\ell,\chi},G_{k,\overline{\chi}}]_{1}\rangle=\langle f,[G_{\ell,\chi},G_{K-\ell,\overline{\chi}}]_{1}\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(D)}.

So the result follows from Proposition
2.5
. ∎

We now establish the equivalence between the linear independence of twisted periods and the linear independence of the corresponding cusp forms
\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell,\chi}
or
\mathcal{G}_{K,\ell,\chi}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character mod
D
. Let
n\geq 1
and
3\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<...<\ell_{n}\leq\frac{K-2}{2}
be integers such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{\ell_{i}}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Then

\{\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent if and only if
\{r_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent.

Proof.

Note that Proposition
2.4
together with the definition of twisted period (
1.2
) and the functional equation (
1.3
) implies that for
3\leq\ell\leq\frac{K-2}{2}
and a normalized Hecke eigenform
f\in S_{K}

(2.22)

\displaystyle\langle f,\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell,\chi}\rangle=A_{K,\ell,\chi}L(f,K-1)r_{\ell-1,\chi}(f),

where
A_{K,\ell,\chi}
is some nonzero constant depending only on
K
,
\ell
and
\chi
, and
L(f,K-1)\neq 0
since
K-1
is within the region of absolute convergence for
L(f,s)
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{K}
denote the set of normalized Hecke eigenform in
S_{K}
. Then

(2.23)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}=0\quad{\rm if~and~only~if}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{a_{i}}\langle f_{j},\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\rangle=0\quad{\rm for~all}~f_{j}\in\mathcal{H}_{K}.

Suppose that
\{\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent. We claim that
\{r_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}

is linearly independent. If
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}r_{\chi,\ell_{i}-1}=0\in S_{K}^{\ast},
then

(2.24)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}r_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}(f_{j})=L(f_{j},K-1)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}A_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}^{-1}\langle f_{j},\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\rangle=0~{\rm for~all}~f_{j}\in\mathcal{H}_{K},

which implies that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{b_{i}A_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}^{-1}}\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}=0\in S_{K}
.
Since
\{\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent, we have
b_{i}A_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}^{-1}=0
, implying
b_{i}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

Conversely, suppose that
\{r_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}

is linearly independent. We show that
\{\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent. If

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}=0\in S_{K},

then

(2.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{a_{i}}\langle f_{j},\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\rangle=0~{\rm for~all}~f_{j}\in\mathcal{H}_{K},

which implies that
L(f_{j},K-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{a_{i}}A_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\cdot r_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}(f_{j})=0~{\rm for~all}~f_{j}\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
.
Since
L(f_{j},K-1)\neq 0
for all
f_{j}\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
and
\{r_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}

is linearly independent, we get
\overline{a_{i}}A_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}=0
, and thus
a_{i}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. This completes the proof.
∎

.

Let

3.
Fourier coefficients

In this section, we compute the Fourier coefficients of
\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell,\chi}
(
1.8
),
\mathcal{G}_{K,\ell,\chi}
(
1.9
) and study their asymptotic behaviors. First of all, we need an explicit formula for the Eisenstein series
G_{k,\chi}
under the action of certain matrices in
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

3.1.
Eisenstein series

We recall the theory of the Eisenstein series as developed in Miyake’s book
[
19
, §7]
. Let
\chi
and
\psi
be Dirichlet characters mod
L
and mod
M
, respectively. For
k\geq 3
, we put

(3.1)

\displaystyle E_{k}(z;\chi,\psi)=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}\limits_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi(m)\psi(n)(mz+n)^{-k}.

Here
\mathop{\mathchoice{{\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}\kern-2.80556pt}\limits\kern 2.80556pt}}{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}}{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}}{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}}}\nolimits
is the summation over all pairs of integers
(m,n)
except
(0,0)
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
19
, Theorem 7.1.3]
)
.

Assume
k\geq 3
. Let
\chi
and
\psi
be Dirichlet characters mod
L
and mod
M
, respectively, satisfying
\chi(-1)\psi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. Let
m_{\psi}
be the conductor of
\psi
, and
\psi^{0}
be the primitive character associated with
\psi
. Then

(3.2)

\displaystyle E_{k}(z;\chi,\psi)=C+A\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)e^{2\pi inz/M},

where

(3.3)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\psi^{0})/M^{k}(k-1)!,

(3.4)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2L_{M}(k,\psi)&\chi:{\rm the~principal~character},\\
0&{\rm otherwise},\end{cases}

(3.5)

\displaystyle a(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<c\mid n}\chi(n/c)c^{k-1}\sum_{0<d\mid(l,c)}d\mu(l/d)\psi^{0}(l/d)\overline{\psi^{0}}(c/d).

Here
l=M/m_{\psi}
,
\mu
is the Möbius function,
L_{M}(k,\psi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi(n)n^{-k}
is the Dirichlet series, and
G(\psi^{0})
is the Gauss sum of
\psi^{0}
.

We now define two Eisenstein series
G_{k,\chi}
and
G_{k,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}
. Such definitions are for the consistency of notation in section
3.2
, although they are not necessarily consistent with those of
E_{k}(z;\chi,\psi)
.

(1)

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character mod
D
and
\mathbbm{1}
be the principal character.
Define

(3.6)

\displaystyle G_{k,\chi}(z):

\displaystyle=\frac{(k-1)!D^{k}}{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\overline{\chi})}E_{k}(Dz,\mathbbm{1},\overline{\chi})

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{(k-1)!D^{k}}{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\overline{\chi})}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,d\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\overline{\chi}(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}.

Note that Lemma
3.1
implies that

(3.8)

\displaystyle G_{k,\chi}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{k-1,\chi}(n)q^{n}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k-1,\chi}(n):

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{(k-1)!D^{k}}{(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\overline{\chi})}L(k,\overline{\chi})&n=0,\\
\sum\limits_{d\mid n}\chi(d)d^{k-1}&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

(2)

Let
D=D_{1}D_{2}
such that
D_{1}>0
. Then there is a unique decomposition
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
, where
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
are primitive Dirichlet characters mod
D_{1}
and mod
D_{2}
, respectively. Define

(3.10)

\displaystyle G_{k,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{D_{1}^{k}(k-1)!}{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\overline{\chi}_{1})}E_{k}(D_{1}z;\chi_{2},\overline{\chi}_{1})

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\frac{D_{1}^{k}(k-1)!}{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\overline{\chi}_{1})}\chi_{2}(D_{1})\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D_{1}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{2}(m)\overline{\chi}_{1}(n)}{(mz+n)^{k}}.

We know from Lemma
3.1
that

(3.12)

\displaystyle E_{k}(z;\chi_{2},\overline{\chi}_{1}):

\displaystyle=C+\frac{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\overline{\chi}_{1})}{D_{1}^{k}(k-1)!}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}>0\\
d_{1}d_{2}=n\end{subarray}}\chi_{1}(d_{1})\chi_{2}(d_{2})d_{1}^{k-1}\right)e^{2\pi inz/D_{1}},

(3.13)

\displaystyle C:

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2L(k,\overline{\chi}_{1})&D_{2}=1,\\
0&{\rm otherwsie}.\end{cases}

So the Fourier expansion of
G_{k,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}
is given by

(3.14)

\displaystyle G_{k,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sigma_{k-1,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}(n)q^{n},

(3.15)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k-1,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}(n)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}0&n=0{\rm~and}~D_{2}\neq 1,\\
\frac{(k-1)!D^{k}}{(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\overline{\chi})}L(k,\overline{\chi})&n=0~{\rm and}~D_{2}=1,\\
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}>0\\
d_{1}d_{2}=n\end{subarray}}\chi_{1}(d_{1})\chi_{2}(d_{2})d_{1}^{k-1}&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Let
k\geq 3
and
\chi
be a Dirichlet character mod
D
. Write
\Gamma_{\infty}:=\{\pm\begin{bmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. The Eisenstein series for
the cusp at infinity

[
19
, p. 272]
is defined as

(3.16)

\displaystyle E_{k,D}^{\ast}(z;\chi)=\sum_{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(D)}\frac{\chi(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}.

We now derive the relation between
G_{k,\chi}(z)
and
E_{k,D}^{\ast}(z;\overline{\chi})
, which was used in the proof in Proposition
2.6
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbbm{1}
denote the principal Dirichlet character. Then

(3.17)

\displaystyle 2L_{D}(k,\chi)E_{k,D}^{\ast}(z;\chi)=E_{k}(Dz;\mathbbm{1},\chi).

Proof.

Recall the following bijection
[
19
, Lemma 7.1.6]
,

\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(D)\tilde{\rightarrow}\{(c,d)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:c\equiv 0~{\rm mod~}D,(c,d)=1,d>0\}:\Gamma_{\infty}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\mapsto(c,d),

which allows us to rewrite
E^{*}_{k,D}(z;\chi)
as

E^{*}_{k,D}(z;\chi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c,d)=1\\
d>0,D\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c,d)=1\\
D\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}.

It follows that

(3.18)

\displaystyle 2L_{D}(k,\chi)E^{*}_{k,D}(z;\chi)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{k}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c,d)=1\\
D\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}\right)

(3.19)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c,d)=1\\
D\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(nd)}{(ncz+nd)^{k}}

(3.20)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,d\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}

(3.21)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{c,d\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\chi(d)}{(Dcz+d)^{k}}

(3.22)

\displaystyle=E_{k}(Dz;\mathbbm{1},\chi),

as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character mod
D
. Then

(3.23)

\displaystyle G_{k,\chi}(z)=\frac{(k-1)!D^{k}L(k,\overline{\chi})}{(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\overline{\chi})}E^{\ast}_{k,D}(z;\overline{\chi}).

We now compute the Fourier expansion of Eisenstein series
G_{k,\chi}
under the action of certain matrices in
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
D=D_{1}D_{2}
with
D_{1}>0
and
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character mod
D
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. If
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}
is a matrix in
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
\gcd(c,D)=D_{1}
then

(3.24)

\displaystyle\left(G_{k,\chi}\bigg|_{k}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\right)(z)=\chi_{2}(c)\chi_{1}(d)\overline{\chi}_{1}(D_{2})\overline{\chi}_{2}(D_{1})\frac{G(\overline{\chi}_{1})}{G(\overline{\chi})}G_{k,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}\left(\frac{z+c^{\ast}d}{D_{2}}\right),

where
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
is the product of primitive characters
\chi_{1}
mod
D_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
mod
D_{2}
, and
c^{\ast}
is an integer with
cc^{\ast}\equiv 1\pmod{D_{2}}
and
D_{1}\mid c^{\ast}
.

Proof.

We follow the idea in
[
8
, pp. 273-275]
. By equation (
3.7
), we have

(3.25)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\overline{\chi})}{(k-1)!D^{k}}G_{k,\chi}\bigg|_{k}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\right)(z)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l,r\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D\mid l\end{subarray}}\frac{\overline{\chi}(r)}{(l(az+b)+r(cz+d))^{k}}

(3.26)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l,r\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D\mid l\end{subarray}}\frac{\overline{\chi}(r)}{((al+cr)z+bl+dr)^{k}}

(3.27)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
md\equiv nc~\text{mod}~D\end{subarray}}\frac{\overline{\chi}(an-bm)}{(mz+n)^{k}},

where
(m,n)=(l,r)\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}
and thus
r=an-bm
. Since
md\equiv nc\pmod{D}
, we have

(3.28)

\displaystyle d(an-bm)

\displaystyle=adn-bmd\equiv adn-bcn\equiv n\pmod{D},

(3.29)

\displaystyle c(an-bm)

\displaystyle=anc-bcm\equiv adm-bcm\equiv m\pmod{D}.

Note also that
\gcd(D_{1},D_{2})=1
. Then (
3.28
) and (
3.29
) imply that

(3.30)

\displaystyle\overline{\chi}(an-bm)

\displaystyle=\overline{\chi}_{1}(an-bm)\overline{\chi}_{2}(an-bm)

(3.31)

\displaystyle=\chi_{1}(d)\overline{\chi}_{1}(n)\chi_{2}(c)\overline{\chi}_{2}(m).

Since
D_{1},D_{2}\mid(md-nc)
,
(d,D_{1})=1,(c,D_{2})=1
and
(c,D)=D_{1}
, we must have
D_{1}\mid m
; and
n\equiv c^{\ast}md\pmod{D_{2}}
. Replacing
n
by
n=c^{\ast}md+lD_{2}
, and choosing
c^{\ast}
to satisfy
D_{1}\mid c^{\ast}
by the Chinese Remainder Theorem, so that
\overline{\chi}_{1}(c^{\ast}md+lD_{2})=\overline{\chi}_{1}(l)\overline{\chi}_{1}(D_{2})
. It follows that

(3.32)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\overline{\chi})}{(k-1)!D^{k}}G_{k,\chi}\bigg|_{k}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\right)(z)

(3.33)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D_{1}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{1}(d)\overline{\chi}_{1}(c^{\ast}md+lD_{2})\chi_{2}(c)\overline{\chi}_{2}(m)}{(mz+mc^{\ast}d+lD_{2})^{k}}

(3.34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(c)\chi_{1}(d)\overline{\chi}_{1}(D_{2})\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D_{1}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\overline{\chi}_{2}(m)\overline{\chi}_{1}(l)}{(mz+mc^{\ast}d+lD_{2})^{k}}

(3.35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(c)\chi_{1}(d)\overline{\chi}_{1}(D_{2})D_{2}^{-k}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D_{1}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\overline{\chi}_{2}(m)\overline{\chi}_{1}(l)}{\left(m\frac{z+c^{\ast}d}{D_{2}}+l\right)^{k}}.

Note that (
3.11
) implies that

(3.36)

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D_{1}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\overline{\chi}_{2}(m)\overline{\chi}_{1}(l)}{\left(m\frac{z+c^{\ast}d}{D_{2}}+l\right)^{k}}=\frac{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\overline{\chi}_{1})}{D_{1}^{k}(k-1)!}\overline{\chi}_{2}(D_{1})G_{k,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}\left(\frac{z+c^{\ast}d}{D_{2}}\right).

Plugging (
3.36
) into (
3.35
) gives the desired result.
∎

3.2.
Computation of the trace

For
m\geq 1
and
f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{f}(n)q^{n}\in S_{k}(N,\chi)
we define the
U
-operator

(3.37)

\displaystyle U_{m}f(z)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{v~{\rm mod}~m}f\left(\frac{z+v}{m}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{f}(mn)q^{n}.

Equivalently, we may write

(3.38)

\displaystyle U_{m}f(z)=m^{k/2-1}\sum_{v\text{ mod }m}f(z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{bmatrix}1&v\\
0&m\end{bmatrix}.

We are ready to compute the Fourier coefficients of
\mathcal{F}_{K,\ell,\chi}
(
1.8
).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
3\leq\ell\leq\frac{K-2}{2}
be integers and
\chi
be primitive character mod
D
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{\ell}
. Then

(3.39)

\displaystyle\Tr^{D}_{1}(G_{\ell,\chi}(z)G_{K-\ell,\overline{\chi}}(z))=\sum_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\overline{\chi}_{2}(-1)U_{D_{2}}(G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(z)G_{K-\ell,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}(z)),

where the summation is over all decompositions of
D=D_{1}D_{2}
as a product of two positive integers and
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
is the product of primitive characters
\chi_{1}
mod
D_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
mod
D_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
k=K-\ell
. We consider the following system of representatives of
\Gamma_{0}(D)\backslash{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
(see
[
8
, p. 276]
and
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
):

\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
D_{1}&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&\mu\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}~:~D=D_{1}D_{2},~\mu\text{ mod }D_{2}\right\}.

By Lemma
3.3
, we have

(3.40)

\displaystyle G_{\ell,\chi}(z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
D_{1}&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&\mu\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\chi_{2}(D_{1})\overline{\chi}_{1}(D_{2})\overline{\chi}_{2}(D_{1})\frac{G(\overline{\chi}_{1})}{G(\overline{\chi})}G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}\left(\frac{z+\mu+D_{1}^{*}}{D_{2}}\right),

(3.41)

\displaystyle G_{k,\overline{\chi}}(z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
D_{1}&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&\mu\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\overline{\chi}_{2}(D_{1})\chi_{1}(D_{2})\chi_{2}(D_{1})\frac{G(\chi_{1})}{G(\chi)}G_{k,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}\left(\frac{z+\mu+D_{1}^{*}}{D_{2}}\right),

.

\displaystyle\Tr_{1}^{D}(G_{\ell,\chi}(z)G_{k,\overline{\chi}}(z))

\displaystyle\sum_{D_{1}D_{2}=D}\sum_{\mu\text{ mod }D_{2}}G_{\ell,\chi}(z)G_{k,\overline{\chi}}(z)\bigg|_{K}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\

0&1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle G(\chi_{1})G(\overline{\chi}_{1})=\chi_{1}(-1)D_{1}\quad{\rm and}\quad G(\chi)G(\overline{\chi})=\chi(-1)D

in the last equality (see e.g

(3.43)

\displaystyle U_{D_{2}}\left(G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(z)G_{k,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v~\text{mod}~D_{2}}D_{2}^{K/2-1}G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(z)\bigg|_{\ell}\begin{bmatrix}1&v\\

\displaystyle\sum_{v~\text{mod}~D_{2}}D_{2}^{K/2-1}D_{2}^{-\ell/2}G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}\left(\frac{z+v}{D_{2}}\right)D_{2}^{-k/2}G_{k,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}\left(\frac{z+v}{D_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\,\sum_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\overline{\chi}_{2}(-1)U_{D_{2}}\left(G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(z)G_{k,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\sum_{v~\text{mod}~D_{2}}\overline{\chi}_{2}(-1)D_{2}^{-1}G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}\left(\frac{z+v}{D_{2}}\right)G_{k,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}\left(\frac{z+v}{D_{2}}\right),

as desired.

Lemma 3.8
.

For
n\geq 1
we have

(3.86)

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\pi}n^{n+1/2}e^{-n}<n!<en^{n+1/2}e^{-n}.

The non-asymptotic Stirling’s approximation
[
21
]
claims that for all

, we have

(3.87)

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{1}{12n+1}}<n!<\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{1}{12n}},

which implies the result.
∎

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
3\leq\ell\leq\frac{K-2}{2}
and
j\geq 0
be integers. Then

(3.160)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{K,\ell,\chi}(2^{j})=\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(2^{j})(1+o(1))\quad{\rm for}~K\gg_{j,D}1.

Proof.

We know from (
3.74
) that

(3.161)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{K,\ell,\chi}(2^{j})}{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(2^{j})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\frac{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(0)}{\sigma_{k-1,\overline{\chi}}(0)}\frac{\sigma_{k-1,\overline{\chi}}(2^{j})}{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(2^{j})}-\frac{2\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(0)}{\zeta(1-K)}\frac{\sigma_{K-1}(2^{j})}{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(2^{j})}+\frac{\mathcal{E}_{K,\ell,2^{j},\chi}}{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(2^{j})}+\frac{\mathscr{E}_{K,\ell,2^{j},\chi}}{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(2^{j})}}{1+\frac{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(0)}{\sigma_{k-1,\overline{\chi}}(0)}-\frac{2\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(0)}{\zeta(1-K)}+\mathscr{E}_{K,\ell,1,\chi}}.

It suffices to show that the non-constant terms in (
3.161
) are
o(1)
for
K\gg_{j,D}1
.
Note that

(3.162)

\displaystyle|\sigma_{k-1,\overline{\chi}}(2^{j})|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{n=0}^{j}\overline{\chi}(2^{n})2^{(k-1)n}\right|=\left|\frac{\overline{\chi}(2^{j+1})2^{(k-1)(j+1)}-1}{\overline{\chi}(2)2^{k-1}-1}\right|\leq\frac{2^{(k-1)(j+1)}+1}{2^{k-1}-1}\leq 2\cdot 2^{(k-1)j},

(3.163)

\displaystyle|\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(2^{j})|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\chi(2^{j+1})2^{(\ell-1)(j+1)}-1}{\chi(2)2^{\ell-1}-1}\right|\geq\frac{2^{(\ell-1)(j+1)}-1}{2^{\ell-1}+1}\geq\frac{1}{2}2^{(\ell-1)j},

which implies that

(3.164)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\sigma_{k-1,\chi}(2^{j})}{\sigma_{\ell-1,\overline{\chi}}(2^{j})}\right|\leq 4\cdot 2^{(k-\ell)j}.

By Lemma
3.10
and (
3.164
), we have

(3.165)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(0)}{\sigma_{k-1,\overline{\chi}}(0)}\frac{\sigma_{k-1,\overline{\chi}}(2^{j})}{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(2^{j})}\right|\leq

\displaystyle 6\left(\frac{\ell-1}{k-1}\right)^{\ell-1/2}\left(\frac{2\pi e}{(k-1)D}\right)^{k-\ell}4\cdot 2^{(k-\ell)j}

(3.166)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 24\left(\frac{\pi e2^{j+1}}{(k-1)D}\right)^{k-\ell}

(3.167)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(1),

\displaystyle{\rm for}~K\gg_{j,D}1.

Lemma
3.11
together with (
3.164
) (with
k
replaced by
K
) gives

(3.168)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{2\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(0)}{\zeta(1-K)}\frac{\sigma_{K-1}(2^{j})}{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(2^{j})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{(\ell-1)D}{K-1}\right)^{\ell-1/2}\left(\frac{2\pi e}{K-1}\right)^{K-\ell}4\cdot 2^{(K-\ell)j}

(3.169)

\displaystyle=8\left(\frac{(\ell-1)D}{K-1}\right)^{\ell-1/2}\left(\frac{2\pi e\cdot 2^{j}}{K-1}\right)^{K-\ell}

(3.170)

\displaystyle=o(1),

\displaystyle{\rm for}~K\gg_{j,D}1.

By Lemma
3.12
and (
3.163
), we have

(3.171)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{E}_{K,\ell,2^{j},\chi}}{\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(2^{j})}\right|

3.15

By Lemmas
3.10
,
3.11

(3.173)

∎

Proposition 3.19
.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{K,\ell,\chi}(2^{j})=2^{j}\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(2^{j})(1+o(1))\quad{\rm for}~K\gg_{j,D}1.

\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{K,\ell,\chi}(2^{j})}{2^{j}\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(2^{j})}=

4.
Coefficient matrices

In this section, we define matrices that are used to study the linear independence of the corresponding twisted periods. More precisely, we prove Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.4
by showing the matrices, formed by the (normalized)
q^{2^{i}}
-Fourier coefficients of relevant kernel functions for
0\leq i\leq n-1
, are non-singular.

We start with the proof of Theorem
1.1
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, we pick out the Fourier coefficients
\mathfrak{a}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}(1)
,
\mathfrak{a}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}(2)
,
\mathfrak{a}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}(2^{2})
,
...
,
\mathfrak{a}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}(2^{n-1})
of each
\mathfrak{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}
(
3.68
) and place them in a
(n,n)
-matrix

(4.1)

\displaystyle M_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n},\chi}:=\left[a_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}(2^{j-1})\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}=\left[\sigma_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}(2^{j-1})(1+\epsilon_{K,i,j})\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}.

To show that
\{\mathfrak{F}_{K,\ell_{i},\chi}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is linearly independent, it suffices to show that
M_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}
is non-singular. Such a choice of
M_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n},\chi}
allows us to relate it to the well-known Vandermonde matrix in terms of determinant. More specifically, we ignore the error terms
\epsilon_{K,i,j}
(
1\leq i,j\leq n
) in
M_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n},\chi}
and define the following matrix

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n},\chi}:

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&\sigma_{\ell_{1}-1,\chi}(2)&\sigma_{\ell_{1}-1,\chi}(2^{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{\ell_{1}-1,\chi}(2^{n-1})\\
1&\sigma_{\ell_{2}-1,\chi}(2)&\sigma_{\ell_{2}-1,\chi}(2^{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{\ell_{2}-1,\chi}(2^{n-1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\sigma_{\ell_{n-1}-1,\chi}(2)&\sigma_{\ell_{n-1}-1,\chi}(2^{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{\ell_{n-1}-1,\chi}(2^{n-1})\\
1&\sigma_{\ell_{n}-1,\chi}(2)&\sigma_{\ell_{n}-1,\chi}(2^{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{\ell_{n}-1,\chi}(2^{n-1})\end{bmatrix}.

We have the following lower bound for
\det\mathbf{M}_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n},\chi}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{M}_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n},\chi}
be defined as in (
4.2
). Then

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left|\det\mathbf{M}_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n},\chi}\right|\geq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}2^{(i-1)(\ell_{i}-1)}.

Proof.

After column reductions, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left|\det\mathbf{M}_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n},\chi}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\det\begin{bmatrix}1&\chi(2)2^{\ell_{1}-1}&\cdots&(\chi(2)2^{\ell_{1}-1})^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\chi(2)2^{\ell_{n}-1}&\cdots&(\chi(2)2^{\ell_{n}-1})^{n-1}\end{bmatrix}\right|

(4.5)

\displaystyle=\left|\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left(\chi(2)2^{\ell_{j}-1}-\chi(2)2^{\ell_{i}-1}\right)\right|

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}2^{\ell_{j}-1}\cdot(1-2^{\ell_{i}-\ell_{j}})

(4.7)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{3}{4}\cdot 2^{\ell_{j}-1}

(4.8)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}2^{(i-1)(\ell_{i}-1)},

where the second equality is from the determinant of the Vandermonde matrix.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\tau\in S_{n}
, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}(2^{\tau(i)-1})\right|<2^{n-1}\prod_{i=2}^{n}2^{(i-1)(\ell_{i}-1)}.

Proof.

Note that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\left|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}(2^{\tau(i)-1})\right|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\ell_{i}-1}(2^{\tau(i)-1}).

By
[
16
, Corollary 2.5]
, we know that

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\ell_{i}-1}(2^{\tau(i)-1})
attains maximum when
\tau
is the identity permutation. It follows that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\left|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}(2^{\tau(i)-1})\right|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\ell_{i}-1}(2^{i-1})<\prod_{i=1}^{n}2\cdot 2^{(i-1)(\ell_{i}-1)}=2^{n-1}\prod_{i=2}^{n}2^{(i-1)(\ell_{i}-1)},

which finishes the proof.
∎

We now give a criterion for the nonsingularity of
M_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}
, see also
[
16
, Lemma 2.7]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Define a function

(4.12)

\displaystyle f(x_{1},...,x_{n}):=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+x_{i})\right)-1.

Suppose that

(4.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in S_{n}}|f(\epsilon_{K,1,\tau(1)},...,\epsilon_{K,n,\tau(n)})|<n!^{-1}\cdot\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\cdot 2^{1-n}.

Then
M_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}
is nonsingular.

Proof.

Note that

(4.14)

\displaystyle\det M_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau\in S_{n}}(-1)^{\sign(\tau)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}(2^{\tau(i)-1})(1+\epsilon_{K,i,\tau(i)})

(4.15)

\displaystyle=\det\mathbf{M}_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n},\chi}+\sum_{\tau\in S_{n}}(-1)^{\sign(\tau)}f(\epsilon_{K,1,\tau(1)},...,\epsilon_{K,n,\tau(n)})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}(2^{\tau(i)-1}).

On the one hand, we have from Lemma
4.1
that

(4.16)

\displaystyle\left|\det\mathbf{M}_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n},\chi}\right|\geq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}2^{(i-1)(\ell_{i}-1)}.

On the other hand, we have

(4.17)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\tau\in S_{n}}(-1)^{\sign(\tau)}f(\epsilon_{K,1,\tau(1)},...,\epsilon_{K,n,\tau(n)})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}(2^{\tau(i)-1})\right|

(4.18)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n!\sup_{\tau\in S_{n}}|f(\epsilon_{K,1,\tau(1)},...,\epsilon_{K,n,\tau(n)})|\left|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\ell_{i}-1,\chi}(2^{\tau(i)-1})\right|

(4.19)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}2^{(i-1)(\ell_{i}-1)}.

Therefore,
\det M_{K,\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}}\neq 0
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f
be defined as in (
4.12
). Suppose there is an
M>0
such that
|x_{i}|\leq M
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Then

(4.20)

\displaystyle|f(x_{1},...,x_{n})|\leq(1+M)^{n}-1.

Proof.

Note that

(4.21)

\displaystyle|f(x_{1},...,x_{n})|

\displaystyle=\left|\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}(1+x_{i})-1\right|

(4.22)

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}x_{i}x_{j}+\cdots+x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}\right|

(4.23)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n}{1}M+\binom{n}{2}M^{2}+\cdots+\binom{n}{n}M^{n}

(4.24)

\displaystyle=(1+M)^{n}-1,

as desired.
∎

Theorem
1.1
follows from the proposition below.

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{1}}(2)&\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{1}}(2^{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{1}}(2^{n-1})\\
1&\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{2}}(2)&\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{2}}(2^{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{2}}(2^{n-1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

1&\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{n}}(2)&\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{n}}(2^{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{n}}(2^{n-1})\end{bmatrix}.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\mathbf{P}_{K,\ell,\chi_{1},...,\chi_{n}}
be defined as in (
4.46
). Then

(4.47)

\displaystyle\left|\det\mathbf{P}_{K,\ell,\chi_{1},...,\chi_{n}}\right|\geq\left(\frac{2^{\ell+1}}{D}\right)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}.

Proof.

After column reductions, we have

(4.48)

\displaystyle\left|\det\mathbf{P}_{K,\ell,\chi_{1},...,\chi_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\det\begin{bmatrix}1&\chi_{1}(2)2^{\ell-1}&\cdots&(\chi_{1}(2)2^{\ell-1})^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\chi_{n}(2)2^{\ell-1}&\cdots&(\chi_{n}(2)2^{\ell-1})^{n-1}\end{bmatrix}\right|

(4.49)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}2^{\ell-1}\left|\chi_{j}(2)-\chi_{i}(2)\right|

by the determinant of the Vandermonde matrix. Since
\chi_{i}(2)\neq\chi_{j}(2)
, we have

(4.50)

\displaystyle|\chi_{j}(2)-\chi_{i}(2)|\geq 2\sin\frac{\pi}{\varphi(D)}\geq 2\cdot\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi}{\varphi(D)}\geq\frac{4}{D},

where
\varphi
is the Euler’s totient function. It follows that

(4.51)

\displaystyle\left|\det\mathbf{P}_{K,\ell,\chi_{1},...,\chi_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n}2^{\ell-1}4D^{-1}=\left(\frac{2^{\ell+1}}{D}\right)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}},

as desired.
∎

Proposition 4.12

\displaystyle\det\mathbf{P}_{K,\ell,\chi_{1},...,\chi_{n}}+\sum_{\tau\in S_{n}}(-1)^{\sign(\tau)}f(\eta_{K,1,\tau(1)},...,\eta_{K,n,\tau(n)})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{i}}(2^{\tau(i)-1}).

5.2.
Non-vanishing of twisted central
L
-values

We now focus on the case when
\chi
is real, or equivalently is quadratic. As
G(\chi)^{2}=\chi(-1)D
in this case, for a normalized Hecke eigenform
f\in S_{K}
the functional equation (
1.3
) becomes

(5.27)

\displaystyle\Lambda(f,\chi,s)=(-1)^{K/2}\chi(-1)\Lambda(f,\chi,K-s).

Note that if
(-1)^{K/2}\chi(-1)<0
then
L(f,\chi,K/2)
is trivially zero. It is speculated that the twisted central
L
-value
L(f,\chi,K/2)
is non-vanishing for every normalized Hecke eigenform when
(-1)^{K/2}\chi(-1)>0
. For example, Lau and Tsang
[
14
]
showed that for
1\leq D\leq K^{1/6}/(\log K)^{5}
, we have

(5.28)

\displaystyle\#\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{K}:L(f,\chi,K/2)\neq 0\}\gg|1+\epsilon_{K}(\chi)|^{2}\frac{D}{\varphi(D)}\frac{K}{(\log K)^{2}},

where
\epsilon_{K}(\chi)=i^{K}G(\chi)^{2}/D
, and
\mathcal{H}_{K}
denotes the set of normalized Hecke eigenform in
S_{K}
.

As an application of our methods, we show that Maeda’s conjecture implies that
L(f,\chi,K/2)
is non-vanishing as
K\geq 10D+2
. Following
[
17
]
, we first review Maeda’s conjecture. Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{f}(n)q^{n}\in S_{K}
be a normalized Hecke eigenform. Denote by
K_{f}
the field generated over
\mathbb{Q}
by the coefficients
a_{f}(n)
for all
n
. Let
G(f)
be the Galois group of the Galois closure of
K_{f}
over
\mathbb{Q}
. It is well-known that
K_{f}
is a number field, and for every
\sigma\in G(f)
,

(5.29)

\displaystyle f^{\sigma}(z):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{f}(n)^{\sigma}q^{n}

is also a normalized Hecke eigenform in
S_{K}
. Maeda’s conjecture can be formulated as follows.

Conjecture 5.3
.

[
17
, p. 306]

Let
f\in S_{K}
be a normalized Hecke eigenform. Then

(
H_{a}
)

\Span\{f^{\sigma}:\sigma\in G(f)\}=S_{K}
;

(
H_{b}
)

G_{f}
is isomorphic to the symmetric group of degree
\dim S_{K}
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{K}
denote the set of normalized Hecke eigenform in
S_{K}
. Assume
(H_{a})
holds true for
S_{K}
. Suppose there exists some
f\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
such that
L(f,\chi,K/2)\neq 0
. Then
L(g,\chi,K/2)\neq 0
for all
g\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
.

Proof.

Since
L(f,\chi,K/2)\neq 0
, we have
L(f^{\sigma},\chi^{\sigma},K/2)=L(f^{\sigma},\chi,K/2)\neq 0
for all

by
Shimura’s result
[
24
, Theorem 1]
. For any
g\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
, there exists some
\tau\in G(f)
such that
g=f^{\tau}
by assumption. Hence
L(g,\chi,K/2)=L(f^{\tau},\chi,K/2)\neq 0
.
∎

The following result can be viewed as a generalization of

.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
D\geq 1
be an odd square-free integer and

be a primitive quadratic Dirichlet character

Proposition 5.6

Let

be an odd square-free integer and

\displaystyle a_{K,k,\chi}(1)=2\sigma_{k-1,\chi}(0)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D=D_{1}D_{2}\\

Thus, we can write

Note that (

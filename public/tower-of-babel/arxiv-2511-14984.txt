Differentiable holonomic A ​ V AV -modules
Yuly Billig, Henrique Rocha

Abstract
. We study differentiable holonomic sheaves of A ​ V AV -modules on a smooth quasi-projective variety. We show that a simple differentiable holonomic sheaf ℳ \mathcal{M} of A ​ V AV -modules is locally the tensor product of a simple holonomic D D -module and a simple finite-dimensional 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} -module W W . In particular, in the case when W W is integrable, ℳ \mathcal{M} is the tensor product of a simple holonomic 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -module and the tensor module associated with W W .

Introduction

The theory of
\mathcal{D}
-modules has far-reaching applications in various fields, including algebraic geometry, representation theory, and mathematical physics. In essence, it offers a strong algebraic framework for working with differential operators and systems of differential equations on algebraic varieties. A
\mathcal{D}
-module on a smooth algebraic variety can be viewed as a sheaf of modules over the sheaf of differential operators, encapsulating both the geometry of the variety and the action of vector fields in a coherent algebraic structure. One key feature of
\mathcal{D}
-modules is that they are associated with an
\mathcal{O}
-linear representation of the tangent sheaf, where
\mathcal{O}
denotes the structure sheaf of the variety.

Recently, this framework has been generalized to allow a more flexible relationship between derivations and functions. In the theory of
AV
-modules, the associated representation of the Lie algebra of vector fields is not assumed to be linear over the ring of regular functions. A primary example of an
AV
-module is a space of tensor fields on a variety. The algebraic theory of
AV
-modules over smooth affine varieties has been developed extensively in
[
BF18
,
BFN19
,
BNZ21
,
BIN23
,
BR23
,
BI23
,
XL23
,
BB24
,
BR24
]
.

The sheaf-theoretic version of
AV
-modules, which allows these structures to be defined on a not necessarily affine variety, was initiated in
[
BR23
]
, where the authors demonstrated that an
AV
-module that is finitely generated over the coordinate ring of an affine variety can be sheafified. This led to the construction of a sheaf of associative algebras
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
, introduced in
[
BI23
]
and further studied in
[
BB24
]
, which serves as a replacement for the smash product algebra in the affine case. Modules over
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
, or simply
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules, are thus sheaf-theoretic analogs of
AV
-modules.

The development of the theory of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules has led to a realization of the significance of the notion of differentiability. Differentiable modules form an important subcategory in the category of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules. These are the modules for which the action of the tangent sheaf is a differential operator of finite order
N
, in the sense of Grothendieck. Differentiable
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules can be seen as a natural generalization of
\mathcal{D}
-modules, which correspond to the case
N=0
. They play a central role throughout this paper, and we use this property to analyze the local structure of the main type of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules we want to investigate, the holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules.

In this paper, we study the notion of the Gelfand-Kirillov (GK) dimension for
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules, which is a way to measure the growth of algebras and modules. We prove that the GK-dimension of any
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module is bounded below by the dimension of the underlying variety.
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules with this minimal possible dimension are called holonomic, in analogy with holonomic
\mathcal{D}
-modules.

Our main results establish key structural properties of holonomic differentiable
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules. We prove that these modules have finite length. We also characterize the
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules
that are coherent over
\mathcal{O}
, and show that these are necessarily holonomic and locally free, thus forming vector bundles on the underlying variety. We then study simple holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules. We prove that they are locally isomorphic to the tensor product of a simple holonomic
D
-module and a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
W
. When
W
is an integrable
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module, the simple holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module is globally the tensor product of a simple holonomic
\mathcal{D}
-module and a tensor module associated with
W
. If
W
is not integrable, we can still give a global description in terms of a charged
\mathcal{D}
-module and a tensor module associated with an
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
-module. We conclude the paper by establishing a generalization of the main result in
[
BNZ21
]
and proving that a simple holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module remains locally simple as a module over the tangent sheaf as long as it is not associated with a fundamental representation of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
1
, we establish the preliminary results about the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension that we will use in this paper. The sheaf of associative algebras
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
is introduced in Section
2
, where we also review the basic properties of differentiable
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules. Section
3
is focused on the general study of holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules. In Section
4
, we investigate
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules that are coherent as
\mathcal{O}
-modules. In the final Section
5
, we describe simple differentiable holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules.

Throughout, we work over an uncountable algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero, and all varieties and sheaves are defined over
\Bbbk
. Unless otherwise stated,
X
denotes a smooth irreducible quasi-projective variety of dimension
n
, though in certain arguments we restrict to the affine case.

1.
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension

Let
R
be a unital associative algebra. A subspace
F\subset R
is called a
subframe
of
R
if
\dim F<\infty
and
1\in F
. A subframe
F\subset R
is a
frame
if
F
is a generating subset for the algebra
R
. For an
R
-module
M
, the
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension
of
M
is

\mathrm{GKdim}_{R}(M)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\subset R\text{ subframe}\\
M_{0}\subset M\end{subarray}}\overline{\lim_{l}}\log_{l}\dim_{\Bbbk}F^{l}M_{0},

where
M_{0}\subset M
are finite-dimensional subspaces of
M
and
\overline{\lim}
denotes the limit superior.
If the algebra
R
is finitely generated by a frame
F
and the
R
-module
M
is generated by a finite-dimensional subspace
M_{0}
, then

\mathrm{GKdim}_{R}(M)=\overline{\lim_{l}}\log_{l}\dim_{\Bbbk}F^{l}M_{0}.

In this case, the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension does not depend on the choice of a frame or a generating subspace.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
R,\ S
be associative algebras and let
M
and
N
be modules over
R
and
S
, respectively. Then

\max\left\{\mathrm{GKdim}_{R}(M),\ \mathrm{GKdim}_{S}(N)\right\}\leq\mathrm{GKdim}_{R\otimes S}\left(M\otimes_{\Bbbk}N\right)\leq\mathrm{GKdim}_{R}(M)+\mathrm{GKdim}_{S}(N).

Proof.

The first inequality follows from the definition of Gelfand-Kirillov dimension. The second follows from

\overline{\lim}(x_{l}+y_{l})\leq\overline{\lim}(x_{l})+\overline{\lim}(y_{l})

and

\log_{l}\dim(F^{l}M_{0}\otimes G^{l}N_{0})=\log_{l}\dim(F^{l}M_{0})+\log_{l}\dim(G^{l}N_{0}).

∎

Lemma 1.2
.

[
NVO87
, Proposition 10.1.22]

Let
M
be finitely generated over a commutative algebra
R
, then
\mathrm{GKdim}(M)=\mathrm{GKdim}(R/\mathrm{Ann}\,_{R}(M))
.

We say that an associative algebra
R
has a
good filtration
if
R
has a filtration

R=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}R_{k}

such that
\mathrm{gr}\,R
is a finitely generated algebra. Similarly, we say that an
R
-module
M
has a
good filtration
if there exists a family of subspaces
M_{i}
,
i\geq-1
, with
M_{-1}=0
,
M_{i}\subset M_{i+1}
such that

M=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}M_{i}
,

R_{r}M_{s}\subset M_{r+s}
, and

\mathrm{gr}\,M=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}M_{i}/M_{i-1}

is finitely generated as a
\mathrm{gr}\,R
-module.

Lemma 1.3
.

[
MS89
, Corollary 1.1]

Let
M
be a module over an associative algebra
R
. Suppose that both
M
and
R
have good filtrations and that
\mathrm{gr}\,R

\mathrm{GKdim}_{\mathrm{gr}\,R}(\mathrm{gr}\,M)=\mathrm{GKdim}_{R}(M)
.

2.
The sheaf
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
and differentiable modules

For a commutative algebra
B
, we denote the structure sheaf of
B
by
\mathcal{O}_{B}
. For each
f\in B
,
f\neq 0
, we denote by
D(f)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Spec}\,(B)\mid f\notin\mathfrak{p}\}
a basic open set. If
M
is a
B
-module, we denote by
\widetilde{M}
the sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-modules associated to
M
on
\mathrm{Spec}\,(B)
. Explicitly,
\widetilde{M}(D(f))=B_{f}\otimes_{B}M
is the localization of
M
by the multiplicative set
\{f^{k}\mid k\geq 0\}
. Let
X
be a scheme with structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. A sheaf
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules is called
quasi-coherent
if there exists an affine open cover
\{U_{\alpha}\}
of
X
and modules
M_{\alpha}
over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{\alpha})
such that
\mathcal{M}|_{U_{\alpha}}=\widetilde{M_{\alpha}}
. If each
M_{\alpha}
is finitely generated over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{\alpha})
,
\mathcal{M}
is called
coherent
. For an introduction to varieties and sheaves, we refer to
[
Mum99
]
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra, then
U(\mathfrak{g})
is a Hopf algebra. If
B
is a commutative algebra on which
\mathfrak{g}
acts by derivations, then the smash product
B\#U(\mathfrak{g})
is an associative algebra defined on the vector space
B\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})
with the product

(f\otimes u)(g\otimes v)=\sum_{i}fu_{i}^{(1)}(g)\otimes u_{i}^{(2)}v,

where
\displaystyle u\mapsto\sum_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}\otimes u_{i}^{(2)}
is the coproduct. We denote the element
f\otimes u\in B\#U(\mathfrak{g})
by
f\#u
.
One can easily check that
B\#\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie subalgebra in
B\#U(\mathfrak{g})
.
For more details about Hopf algebras and smash products, we refer to
[
DNR01
]
.

Suppose that
X
is an affine variety with coordinate ring
A
and
V=\mathrm{Der}(A)
. An
AV
-module
on
X
is a module over the associative algebra
A\#U(V)
. The category of
AV
-modules is closed under tensor products
M_{1}\otimes_{A}M_{2}
and taking dual modules
M^{\ast}=\mathrm{Hom}\,_{A}(M,A)
, see
[
BFN19
]
. Furthermore, for a
V
-module
W
, we can define an induced
AV
-module

M=A\#U(V)\otimes_{U(V)}W.

In this case,
M\cong A\otimes_{\Bbbk}W
as a vector space, the
A
-module action is given by multiplication in the left factor and

\eta(f\otimes w)=\eta(f)\otimes w+f\otimes\eta w,\ \eta\in V,\ f\in A,\ w\in W.

In this paper,
X
is a smooth irreducible quasi-projective algebraic variety of dimension
n
with structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}
, and tangent sheaf
\Theta
. A
sheaf of
AV
-modules
on
X
is a quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{M}
such that
M=\mathcal{M}(U)
is an
AV
-module on
U
for each affine open set
U\subset X
such that
\mathcal{M}|_{U}=\widetilde{M}
.

Sheaves of
AV
-modules as defined above were introduced in
[
BR23
]
, where the authors showed that if an
AV
-module
M
is finitely generated as a module over the coordinate ring of an affine variety
X
, then
\widetilde{M}
is a sheaf of
AV
-modules.

We want to study a geometric version of the theory of
AV
-modules with the assistance of the topologically quasi-coherent sheaf
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
of associative algebras constructed in
[
BI23
]
and elaborated in
[
BB24
]
. To provide a local description of the sheaf
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
, we require the concept of étale charts.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
U\subset X
be an affine open set. We say that
U
is an
étale chart
if there exist
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{O}(U)
, called
uniformizing parameters
, such that

(1)

the set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
is algebraically independent;

(2)

every
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
is algebraic over
\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
;

(3)

derivations
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
extend to a derivation of
\mathcal{O}(U)
.

Since
\mathcal{O}(U)
is algebraic over
\Bbbk[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, the extensions of
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
are unique. Additionally,
\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{O}(U))
is a free module over
\mathcal{O}(U)
with basis
\partial_{x_{1}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{n}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
. Any smooth irreducible quasi-projective variety
X
admits a finite open cover of étale charts.

Let

L^{A}_{+}=\mathfrak{m}\mathrm{Der}_{A}(A[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}])=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{m}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}

be the Lie algebra of
A
-derivations of the
A
-algebra
A[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
with values in the ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
A[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
generated by
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
. When
A={\Bbbk}
, we drop the superscript.
This Lie algebra
\mathbb{Z}
-graded,

L^{A}_{+}=\mathop{\oplus}_{k=0}^{\infty}L^{A}_{k}

and has a decreasing filtration by subspaces
L^{A}_{\geq s}=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{k=s}^{\infty}L^{A}_{k}
. We will also need the completion of this Lie algebra

\widehat{\mathrm{L}}^{A}_{+}=A\widehat{\otimes}L_{+}=\varprojlim\limits_{s}L^{A}_{\geq s}=\mathfrak{m}\mathrm{Der}_{A}(A[[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]]).

In
[
BB24
]
, the authors constructed a topologically quasi-coherent sheaf
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
on
X
of
virtual jets of vector fields
. Locally,

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{+}(U)=\widehat{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathcal{O}(U)}_{+}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}f_{k,i}X^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\mid f_{k,i}\in\mathcal{O}(U)\right\}

for each étale chart
U\subset X
. For each
s
,
\widehat{\mathrm{L}}^{A}_{>s}=\mathfrak{m}^{s+1}\widehat{\mathrm{L}}_{+}^{A}
is an ideal of the Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathrm{L}}_{+}^{A}
. This ideal induces a subsheaf on
X
with sections
\mathcal{O}(U)\widehat{\otimes}\mathrm{L}_{>s}
for each étale chart
U\subset X
. Furthermore, the quotient
\widehat{\mathrm{L}}^{A}_{+}/\widehat{\mathrm{L}}^{A}_{>s}
induces a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{L}^{s}
of Lie algebras with sections
\mathcal{L}^{s}(U)\cong\mathcal{O}(U)\otimes L^{s}
in étale charts, where
L^{s}=\widehat{\mathrm{L}}_{+}/\widehat{\mathrm{L}}_{>s}
.

Let
U
be an étale chart of
X
with uniformizing parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
, let
D
be the algebra of differential operators on
U
,
A=\mathcal{O}(U)
, and
V=\Theta(U)
. Consider the kernel
\Delta
of the multiplication map
A\otimes A\rightarrow A
and define the Lie algebra of jets of vector fields as

\widehat{J}_{U}=\varprojlim_{k}A\#V/(\Delta^{k}\otimes_{A}V).

The completion of
A\#U(V)
is

\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}(U)=A\#U(\widehat{J}_{U})/\left<f\#s-1\#fs\mid f\in A,\ s\in\widehat{J}_{U}\right>.

By
[
BI23
]
, these completions glue into a topologically quasi-coherent sheaf of associative algebras
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
on
X
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
BI23
]
)
.

Let
U
be an étale chart of
X
with uniformizing parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. Let
D
be the algebra of differential operators on
U
. Then

\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}(U)\cong D\otimes_{A}U_{A}(\widehat{\mathrm{L}}^{A}_{+}).

The isomorphism map is given by:

\varphi\left(g\#f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right)=gf\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\otimes 1+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{N}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{1}{k!}\,g\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial x^{k}}\otimes X^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}},

and its inverse by

\displaystyle\psi\left(g\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\otimes 1\right)

\displaystyle=g\#\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},

\displaystyle\psi\left(g\otimes X^{m}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=(g\#1)(1\#x-x\#1)^{m}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}.

In
[
BB24
]
, the authors gave the following explicit formula for the transformation law of the right-hand side of the isomorphism in Theorem
2.2
. Suppose
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are étale charts with uniformizing parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
, respectively, and assume that

x_{i}=G_{i}(y),\quad y_{i}=H_{i}(x),\quad i=1,\dots,n,

in
U_{1}\cap U_{2}
. We will use the let symbols
X^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}
and
Y^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{i}}
to represent elements of
\widehat{\mathrm{L}}_{+}
in
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}(U_{1})
and
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}(U_{2})
, respectively. Then, the transformation law for
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
from
U_{1}
to
U_{2}
is given by

g(X)\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{p}}=\sum_{q=1}^{n}g(G(y+Y)-G(y))\frac{\partial H_{q}}{\partial x_{p}}(G(y+Y))\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{q}},

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial H_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(G(y))\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}+\left(\frac{\partial H_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(G(y+Y))-\frac{\partial H_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(G(y))\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{j}},

where

f(x+X)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}}\frac{1}{k!}\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial x^{k}}X^{k}

denotes the Taylor expansion of
f
.

A sheaf
\mathcal{M}
on
X
is called an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module if it is a quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
that is a module over the sheaf of algebras
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
. Similar to
\mathcal{D}
-modules and
AV
-modules, the
\mathcal{O}
-dual
\mathcal{M}^{\ast}=\mathrm{Hom}\,_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O})
of an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module
\mathcal{M}
is an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module. Furthermore, the tensor product
\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{N}
is an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module if
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
are
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules.

The sheaf of algebras
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
contains a chain of sub-sheaves

\mathcal{I}_{1}\subset\mathcal{I}_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{I}_{l}\subset\cdots

with
\mathcal{I}_{l}(U)
generated by
\Delta^{l}\otimes_{A}V
in
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}(U)
. If
\mathcal{M}
is a sheaf of
AV
-modules, then we can always define an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
by

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\varprojlim_{l}\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{I}_{l}\mathcal{M}.

If
\mathcal{I}_{k}\mathcal{M}=0
for large values of
k
, then
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{M}
. Hence, the notions of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules and sheaves of
AV
-modules coincide when this condition is met. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
N\geq 1
. Let
X
be affine and
M
be an
AV
-module on
X
with associated representation
\rho:V\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(M)
. We say that
M
is
N
-
differentiable
if

\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-1)^{N-k}\binom{N}{k}f^{k}\rho\left(f^{N-k}\eta\right)=0

for every
f\in A
and
\eta\in V
.

Let
X
be a quasi-projective variety. A sheaf of
AV
-modules
\mathcal{M}
is called
N
-differentiable if
\mathcal{M}(U)
is an
N
-differentiable
AV
-module on
U
for each affine open set
U\subset X
. An
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module
\mathcal{M}
is called
N
-differentiable if
\mathcal{I}_{N}\mathcal{M}=0
.

Note that an
AV
-module is
1
-differentiable if and only if it is a
\mathcal{D}
-module. Furthermore, Definition
2.3
is equivalent to saying that
\rho
is a differential operator of order less than or equal to
N-1
in the sense of Grothendieck
[
Gro67
, Proposition 16.8.8]
.

If
M
is a differentiable
AV
-module on
X
, then
\widetilde{M}
is an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module, as the next lemma shows.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
BI23
, Proposition 26]

Assume that
X
is affine. Let
M
be an
AV
-module with associated representation
\rho:V\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(M)
. If
M
is
N
-differentiable, then the quasi-coherent sheaf
\widetilde{M}
is both a sheaf of

-modules and an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module. In particular, for each

, the action of

on

is given by localization, and the action of

is given by

\left(\frac{g}{f^{k}}\eta\right)m=\sum_{p=0}^{N}\sum_{l=0}^{p}(-1)^{l}\binom{p}{k}\frac{1}{f^{k(l+1)}}\rho\left(f^{kl}g\eta\right)m

for every

,
\eta\in V
,

, and

.

The next proposition shows that we may use uniformizing parameters to prove that a module is
N
-differentiable when

is étale.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module. Suppose that
U
is an étale chart of
X
with uniformizing parameters

. Let

,

, and

with associated representation
\rho:V\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(M)
. Fix

. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

for each

and

,

\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-1)^{k}\binom{N}{k}f^{N-k}\#f^{k}\eta

annihilates

;

(2)

for each

, and

with

,

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\ k\leq p}(-1)^{k}\binom{p}{k}x^{p-k}\rho\left(x^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right)=0;

;

(4)

\widehat{\mathrm{L}}_{\geq N-1}M=0
;

(5)

\rho:\,V\rightarrow\mathrm{End}\,M
is a differential operator in the sense of Grothendieck of order less than or equal to
N-1
.

Proof.

By
[
BI23
, Theorem 22]
, the isomorphism between
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}(U)
and
\mathcal{D}(U)\otimes_{A}\,U_{A}(\widehat{\mathrm{L}}^{A}_{+})
sends

(1)

X^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\mapsto\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\ k\leq p}(-1)^{p-k}\binom{p}{k}x^{p-k}\#x^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}.

It is straightforward to check that elements
X^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}
with
|p|\geq N

and
i=1,\ldots,n
, generate ideal
\widehat{\mathrm{L}}^{A}_{\geq N-1}
in Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathrm{L}}^{A}_{+}
.
Hence,
\widehat{\mathrm{L}}_{N-1}M=0
if and only if the action of the right-hand side on

is zero for each

with
|p|\geq N
.
Thus, (2) and (4) are equivalent.

Let us show that (4) implies (1). Suppose (4) holds. Then,
X^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}M=0
for each
|p|\geq N
. By Faà di Bruno’s formula (see
[
FdB55
]
), we have that

\frac{\partial^{l}(f^{k}g)}{\partial x^{l}}=\sum_{m=0}^{\min(|l|,k)}\frac{k!}{(k-m)!}f^{k-m}B(l,m,f,g),

where
B(l,m,f,g)
are polynomials in the partial derivatives of
f
and
g
. Because

\sum_{k=m}^{N}(-1)^{k}\binom{N}{k}\frac{k!}{(k-m)!}=0

if

, we can use the isomorphism of
[
BI23
, Theorem 22]
and the Faà di Bruno’s formula to get that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-1)^{k}\binom{N}{k}f^{N-k}\rho\left(f^{k}g\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-1)^{k}\binom{N}{k}\rho\left(f^{N}g\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\otimes 1\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{N}\sum_{0<|l|<N}(-1)^{k}\binom{N}{k}\frac{1}{l!}\rho\left(f^{N-k}\frac{\partial^{l}(f^{k}g)}{\partial x^{l}}\otimes X^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0<|l|<N}\frac{1}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{|l|}\left(\sum_{k=m}^{N}(-1)^{k}\binom{N}{k}\frac{k!}{(k-m)!}f^{N-m}\right)\rho\left(B(l,m,f,g)\otimes X^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\right)=0.

Hence, (1) holds.

Now assume that (1) holds for
N
. Then (1) also holds for all
N^{\prime}\geq N
:

\sum_{k=0}^{N^{\prime}}(-1)^{k}\binom{N^{\prime}}{k}f^{N^{\prime}-k}\rho\left(f^{k}g\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right)=0

for every

,
i=1,\dots,n
. In particular, this is true if
\displaystyle f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}
and
g=1
. By a linear algebra argument (see
[
Roc24
, Lemma 2.6.5]
), for every
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
with
|p|\geq N
,

\sum_{k\leq p}(-1)^{k}\binom{p}{k}x^{p-k}\rho\left(x^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right)=0.

Thus, (1), (2), and (4) are equivalent.

By
[
BI23
, Lemma 12]
, ideal
\Delta^{N}
in
A\otimes A
is generated as a left module
by elements of the form
(f_{1}\otimes 1-1\otimes f_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(f_{N}\otimes 1-1\otimes f_{N})
, where
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}\in A
. Applying the linearization argument, we conclude that elements of the form
(f\otimes 1-1\otimes f)^{N}
,

f\in A
, also generate
\Delta^{N}
as a left module. Since
\mathcal{I}_{N}
is generated by

, we conclude that (1) implies (3). Since elements (
1
) with
|p|\geq N
belong to

, we obtain that (3) implies (2).

Finally, the equivalence of (1) and (5) follows from the definition of a differential operator in the sense of Grothendieck.
∎

has finite length
l
. We will prove by induction on
l
that
M
has finite length as an
R
-module. The basis of induction is
l=0
, in which case there is nothing to prove. Suppose
l>0
. Then,
DM_{0}
contains a simple
D
-submodule
S
. Let
v\in S
be a generator of the
D
-module
S
and let
W=U(L^{N})v
. Then, using the same argument as in the previous proposition, we get
U(\widehat{\mathrm{L}}_{+})S\cong T(S,W)
since
S
is a simple
D
-module and the Jacobson Density Theorem holds for it. By Lemma
3.6
,
W
has finite dimension, hence
M^{\prime}=U(L^{N})S
has a finite composition series. The
R
-module
M/M^{\prime}
is holonomic and generated by the image of
M_{0}
. Because the image of
D
-module
DM_{0}
in
M/M^{\prime}
has length strictly less than
l
, we conclude by the induction hypothesis that both
M/M^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime}
have finite composition series. Therefore,
M
has a finite composition series as well.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Suppose that
X
is étale and
M
is a differentiable holonomic
AV
-module on
X
. If

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{l}=M

is a composition series of
M
, then there exist simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-modules
W_{1},\dots,W_{l}
and simple holonomic
\mathcal{D}(X)
-modules
P_{1},\dots,P_{l}
such that

M_{i}/M_{i-1}\cong T(P_{i},W_{i})

for each
i=1,\dots,l
.

Conjecture 3.11
.

Every holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module is differentiable.

4.
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules that are coherent
\mathcal{O}
-modules

In this section, we study
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules that are coherent as
\mathcal{O}
-modules. We will also further investigate the sheaves of tensor modules introduced in
[
BR24
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

An
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module that is coherent over
\mathcal{O}
is holonomic.

Proof.

Let
U\subset X
be an étale chart with local parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. Then,
\mathcal{M}(U)
is an
AV
-module on
U
. By
[
BR23
]
,
\mathcal{M}(U)
is an
N
-differentiable
AV
-module on
U
, where
N
depends on the rank of
\mathcal{M}(U)
as an
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module. By Proposition
2.5
,
\mathcal{M}(U)
is a module over
\mathcal{D}(U)\otimes U(L^{N-2})
. Suppose that
F_{A}
is a frame of
\mathcal{O}(U)
and
F_{B}
is the subspace spanned by the partial derivatives with respect
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and

X^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}},\quad i=1,\dots,n,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\text{ with }|k|<N.

Then,
F=F_{A}+F_{B}
is a frame for
\mathcal{D}(U)\otimes U(L^{N-2})
. If
M_{0}
is a finite-dimensional generating subspace of
M
as an
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module, then there exists
s\geq 1
such that
F_{B}M_{0}\subset F_{A}^{s}M_{0}
. Hence, for each
k\geq 0
,

F^{k}M_{0}\subset F^{ks}_{A}M_{0}.

Thus,

n\leq\mathrm{GKdim}_{\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}(U)}\mathcal{M}(U)=\overline{\lim_{k}}\log_{k}\dim F^{k}M_{0}\leq\overline{\lim_{k}}\log_{k}\dim F_{A}^{ks}M_{0}\leq\mathrm{GKdim}\,\mathcal{O}(U)=n.

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

An
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module that is coherent over
\mathcal{O}
is a locally free
\mathcal{O}
-module, i.e., a vector bundle on
X
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module that is coherent over
\mathcal{O}
. By the above proposition,
\mathcal{M}
is differentiable and a sheaf of
AV
-modules. Suppose that
U\subset X
is an affine open set such that
\mathcal{M}|_{U}=\widetilde{M}
for some
AV
-module
M
on
U
that is finitely generated as an
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module. By
[
BR23
, Proposition 1.2]
,
\mathcal{M}(U)
is a projective
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module. In particular,
\mathcal{M}_{p}
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
-module for each
p\in U
. We conclude that
\mathcal{M}
is a locally free
\mathcal{O}
-module, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}
is a vector bundle
[
Ser55
]
.
∎

Therefore, similar to what happens in
\mathcal{D}
-modules, an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module is coherent over
\mathcal{O}
if and only if it is locally free over
\mathcal{O}
of finite rank. In particular, if
X
is affine, then global sections of an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module that is coherent over
\mathcal{O}
form a projective
\mathcal{O}(X)
-module.

In
[
BR24
]
, the authors constructed a sheaf
\mathcal{J}^{W}
such that
\mathcal{J}^{W}(U)\cong T(\mathcal{O}(U),W)
, where
W
is a finite-dimensional representation of the Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathrm{L}}_{+}
for which the action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
integrates to a rational
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-module. The
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module
\mathcal{J}^{W}
is called a
tensor module
. We will recall this construction, assuming that
W
is a representation of the Lie algebra
L^{0}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.

Denote by
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
the standard Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
of diagonal matrices. Let
\rho:\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(W)
be a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module with highest weight
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
such that
\mu(E_{ii})\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\mu(E_{ii})\geq\mu(E_{i+1,i+1})
, i.e.,
\mu
is integral dominant. Then
\rho
integrates to a rational representation
\widetilde{\rho}:\mathrm{GL}_{n}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(W)
of the Lie group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, and
W
is called
integrable
.

Let us construct the tensor module
\mathcal{J}^{W}
associated with
W
. For each étale chart
U\subset X
, define
\mathcal{J}^{W}(U)=\mathcal{O}(U)\otimes W
as a vector space. The action of
\mathcal{O}(U)
on
\mathcal{J}^{W}(U)
is given by left multiplication and the action of
\Theta(U)
is given by

\left(f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right)(g\otimes w)=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}\otimes w+g\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\otimes E_{ji}w,

where
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
are the uniformizing parameters of
U
and
f,g\in\mathcal{O}(U)
. If
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are étale charts with uniformizing parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
, then the Jacobian matrix
\varphi_{U_{1}U_{2}}
with entries

\,\partial x_{i}/\partial y_{j}\ i,j=1,\dots,n,

is an element of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}(U_{1}\cap U_{2}))
. Hence,
\widetilde{\rho}(\varphi_{U_{1}U_{2}})
is a well-defined
\mathcal{O}(U_{1}\cap U_{2})
-linear automorphism of
\mathcal{O}(U_{1}\cap U_{2})\otimes W
by
[
BR24
, Lemma 5]
. To glue
\mathcal{J}^{W}(U_{1})
and
\mathcal{J}^{W}(U_{2})
, we use the map

gw\mapsto g\widetilde{\rho}(\varphi_{U_{1}U_{2}})w,\quad g\in\mathcal{O}(U_{1}\cap U_{2}),\ w\in W.

By
[
BR24
, Theorem 17]
,
\mathcal{J}^{W}
is a
2
-differentiable
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module and
\mathcal{J}^{W}(U)\cong T(\mathcal{O}(U),W)
for every étale chart
U\subset X
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{J}^{W}
is coherent over
\mathcal{O}
and a simple
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module because
W
is a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module, see
[
BNZ21
]
.

Example 4.3
.

Let
\{U_{\alpha}\}
be a finite étale cover of
X
such that
x^{\alpha}_{1},\dots,x^{\alpha}_{n}
are uniformizing parameters for
U_{\alpha}
. Suppose that
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then, the one-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
\Bbbk_{\lambda}=\Bbbk v_{\lambda}
given by
Tv_{\lambda}=\lambda\mathrm{tr}(T)v_{\lambda}
, is integrable. Denote by
v_{\lambda}^{\alpha}=1\otimes v_{\lambda}\in J^{\Bbbk_{\lambda}}(U_{\alpha})
. We have

\left(f\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha}_{i}}\right)(gv_{\lambda}^{\alpha})=\left(f\frac{\partial g}{\partial x^{\alpha}_{i}}+\lambda g\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{\alpha}_{i}}\right)v_{\lambda}^{\alpha}

for each
f,g\in\mathcal{O}(U_{\alpha})
and
i=1,\dots,n
. If
J_{\alpha\beta}
denotes the determinant of the matrix with coefficients

\,\partial x^{\alpha}_{i}/\partial{x^{\beta}_{j}},\ i,j=1,\dots,n,

then the transformation law for
\mathcal{J}^{\Bbbk_{\lambda}}
from
U_{\alpha}
to
U_{\beta}
is the map

v_{\lambda}^{\alpha}\mapsto J_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}v_{\lambda}^{\beta}
.

Any
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
-module can be made into a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module by setting the action of the identity matrix to zero. On the other hand, if
W
is a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module, then the identity matrix acts as
n\lambda
for some
\lambda\in\Bbbk
. Hence, if
W
is a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, then there exists a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
-module
W_{0}
, viewed as a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module with a trivial action of the identity matrix, and
\lambda\in\Bbbk
such that

W\cong W_{0}\otimes\Bbbk_{\lambda}.

The
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
W
will be integrable if and only if
\lambda
is an integer. In particular, the tensor module
\mathcal{J}^{W_{0}}
is well-defined for any finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
-module
W_{0}
.

5.
Simple holonomic modules

In this section, we further investigate differentiable holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules. Our objective is to have a better understanding of the structure of simple
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules. Recall that an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module
\mathcal{M}
is simple if it is non-zero and its only
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-subsheaves are the trivial subsheaves
0
and
\mathcal{M}
, and
\mathcal{M}(U)
is either zero or a simple module over
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}(U)
for each étale chart
U\subset X
. The main objective of this section is to show that we can associate to every simple holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module
\mathcal{M}
, a simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
W
. We will then use this structure to decompose
\mathcal{M}
into the tensor product of two sheaves.

We begin by providing a local description of simple holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a simple differentiable holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module. Then for a finite open cover
\{U_{\alpha}\}
of
X
consisting of étale charts,
\mathcal{M}
is either zero or

\mathcal{M}(U_{\alpha})\cong T(P_{\alpha},W_{\alpha}),

where
P_{\alpha}
is a simple holonomic
\mathcal{D}(U_{\alpha})
-module and
W_{\alpha}
is a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module.

Proof.

Since
X
is a smooth quasi-projective variety, it admits a finite open cover
X=\bigcup U_{\alpha}
of étale charts. For each
\alpha
,
\mathcal{M}(U_{\alpha})
is a simple
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}(U_{\alpha})
-module, or
\mathcal{M}(U_{\alpha})=0
. If
\mathcal{M}(U_{\alpha})\neq 0
, by Proposition
3.8
,
\mathcal{M}(U_{\alpha})\cong T(P_{\alpha},W_{\alpha})
, where
P_{\alpha}
is a simple holonomic
\mathcal{D}(U)
-module and
W_{\alpha}
is a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module.
∎

Locally, in an étale chart, a simple holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module is a tensor product of a
\mathcal{D}
-module with a simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module. However, the
\mathcal{D}
-module factor depends on the choice of uniformizing parameters, even when
X
is étale. This reflects the notion of
\mathcal{L}_{+}
-charged
\mathcal{D}
-module in
[
BB24
]
. Example
5.5
below exhibits this behavior explicitly.

Proposition
5.1
gives a local description of a simple differentiable holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module. However, it is not obvious that the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-modules
W_{\alpha}
that appear in Proposition
5.1
are isomorphic to each other. Next, we aim to prove this claim. For this, we analyze the action of the sheaf
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
, which will be reduced to an action of a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-bundle
\Theta\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\Omega^{1}
, focusing, in particular, on the action of the center of the algebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
. We first recall key results concerning the center of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
and its action on
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-modules.

Let
E_{ij}
,
i,j=1,\dots,n
, be the standard basis of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. For each
k=1,\dots,n
, we denote

\Omega_{k}=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}=1,\dots,n}E_{i_{1}i_{2}}E_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots E_{i_{k}i_{1}}.

By
[
Mol07
, Corollary 7.1.2]
, the center of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
is
\Bbbk[\Omega_{1},\dots,\Omega_{n}]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
be two simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-modules. Then there exist scalars
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n},\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}\in\Bbbk
such that
\Omega_{k}
acts on
W_{1}
as multiplication by
\lambda_{k}
and on
W_{2}
as multiplication by
\mu_{k}
, for every
k=1,\dots,n
. Furthermore, if
\lambda_{k}=\mu_{k}
for all
k
, then
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

By Schur’s Lemma, central elements act on simple modules by scalars.
Furthermore, by
[
Hum08
]
, a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module is determined by the central character
\chi:Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))\rightarrow\Bbbk
given by the action of the center on it.
∎

The
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-bundle
\Theta\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\Omega^{1}
of
(1,1)
-tensors induces a sheaf
\mathcal{U}_{gl_{N}}
of associative algebras on
X
, whose sections in étale charts
U_{\alpha}\subset X
are isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}(U_{\alpha})\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
.

Theorem 5.3
.

The center of the sheaf
\mathcal{U}_{gl_{N}}
is a trivial sheaf
\mathcal{Z}_{gl_{N}}
with

\mathcal{Z}_{gl_{N}}(U_{\alpha})=\mathcal{O}(U_{\alpha})\otimes\Bbbk[\Omega_{1},\dots,\Omega_{n}]
for each
étale chart
U_{\alpha}
and trivial transformation laws between the charts.

Proof.

Suppose
U_{\alpha}
and
U_{\beta}
are elements of the étale cover with uniformizing parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
, respectively. Explicitly, the transformation law
\psi_{\alpha\beta}
for the sheaf
\mathcal{U}_{gl_{N}}
is

f\otimes E_{ij}\mapsto\sum_{a=1}^{n}\sum_{b=1}^{n}f\frac{\partial y_{a}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial x_{i}}{\partial y_{b}}\otimes E_{ba}.

Since

\delta_{ij}=\frac{\partial y_{i}(x(y))}{\partial y_{j}}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\frac{\partial y_{i}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial x_{l}}{\partial y_{j}},

it follows that

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha\beta}\left(\sum 1\otimes E_{i_{1}i_{2}}E_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots E_{i_{k}i_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\frac{\partial y_{a_{k}}}{\partial x_{i_{1}}}\frac{\partial x_{i_{1}}}{\partial y_{b_{1}}}\frac{\partial y_{a_{1}}}{\partial x_{i_{2}}}\frac{\partial x_{i_{2}}}{\partial y_{b_{2}}}\cdots\frac{\partial y_{a_{k-1}}}{\partial x_{i_{k}}}\frac{\partial x_{i_{k}}}{\partial x_{b_{k}}}\otimes E_{b_{1}a_{1}}E_{b_{2}a_{2}}\cdots E_{b_{k}a_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\delta_{b_{1}a_{k}}\delta_{a_{1}b_{2}}\delta_{a_{2}b_{3}}\cdots\delta_{b_{k}a_{k-1}}\otimes E_{b_{1}a_{1}}E_{b_{2}a_{2}}\cdots E_{b_{k}a_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum 1\otimes E_{b_{1}b_{2}}E_{b_{2}b_{3}}\cdots E_{b_{k}b_{1}}.

We conclude that
\psi_{\alpha\beta}(1\otimes\Omega_{k})=1\otimes\Omega_{k}
. Therefore, the center of
\mathcal{U}_{gl_{N}}
is a trivial sheaf
\mathcal{Z}_{gl_{N}}=\mathcal{O}\otimes Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
.
∎

Let
Z_{\chi}
be the kernel of the character
\chi:Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))\rightarrow\Bbbk
. With a slight abuse of notations, we identify
Z_{\chi}
with the corresponding subspace of constant sections
1\otimes Z_{\chi}
in the sheaf
\mathcal{Z}
, constructed above. Note that for any
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module
\mathcal{M}
, the kernel of
Z_{\chi}
in
\mathcal{M}
is an
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-submodule in
\mathcal{M}
.

We are ready to state our second result about the local description of irreducible holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a simple holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module. Then there exists a simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
W
such that
\mathcal{M}(U)\cong T(P,W)
for every étale chart
U\subset X
for some simple
\mathcal{D}(U)
-module
P
or
P=0
. In particular,
\mathcal{M}
has type
W
.

Proof.

Let
U_{1}\subset X
be an étale chart such that
\mathcal{M}(U)\cong T(P_{1},W_{1})
is non-zero. Let
\chi
be the character of
Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
corresponding to
W_{1}
. The kernel of
Z_{\chi}
is a non-zero subsheaf in
\mathcal{M}
since
Z_{\chi}
annihilates
T(P_{1},W_{1})
. Since
\mathcal{M}
is simple,
\mathcal{M}=\ker Z_{\chi}
. Consider an étale chart
U_{2}
with
\mathcal{M}(U_{2})\cong T(P_{2},W_{2})
. Since
Z_{\chi}
annihilates
T(P_{2},W_{2})
as well, we get that
W_{1}\cong W_{2}
.
∎

Let us recall a construction of a gauge module. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a
\mathcal{D}
-module, which is a vector bundle, and let
W
be a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a simple holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module such that in étale charts
U\subset X
we have
\mathcal{M}(U)=\mathcal{P}(U)\otimes W
with
\mathcal{P}(U)
being a free
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module. Suppose
\mathcal{P}(U)=\mathcal{O}(U)\otimes P^{\prime}
for some finite-dimensional subspace
P^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{P}(U)
. Then locally, the action of vector fields is given by

f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(gp\otimes w)=f\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}p\otimes w+fg\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}p\right)\otimes w+g\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}p\otimes E_{ji}w

for
f,g\in\mathcal{O}(U)
,
p\in P^{\prime}
,
w\in W
,
i=1,\dots,n
. An ingredient to this construction is the maps

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}:P^{\prime}\otimes W\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(U)\otimes P^{\prime}\otimes W,\quad i=1,\dots,n,

which are called the
gauge fields
.

Example 5.5
.

Consider the affine variety
X=\mathrm{Spec}\,\Bbbk[t,s]/(st-1)
. Define the
AV
-module
M_{1}=\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]v_{1}
on
X
by

t^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(t^{l}d)=(l+\lambda k)t^{k+l-1}v_{1}

and the
AV
-module
M_{2}=\Bbbk[s,s^{-1}]v_{2}
by

s^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}(s^{l}d_{2})=\left(l+\lambda k\right)s^{k+l-1}v_{2}

Both
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are gauge modules of type
\Bbbk_{\lambda}
. If we rewrite the action of vector fields on
M_{1}
choosing
s
as local parameter, we will have

\left(s^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)s^{l}v_{1}=\left(-t^{2-k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)t^{-l}v_{1}=-(-l+(2-k)\lambda)t^{-l-k+1}v_{1}=(l+(k-2)\lambda)s^{l+k-1}v_{1}

for every
l,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, the gauge field
-2\lambda s^{-1}
appears if the uniformizing parameter
s
is chosen instead. If
2\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
, then this gauge field may be trivialized by choosing
v_{1}^{\prime}=s^{2\lambda}v_{1}
. In particular,
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are isomorphic if and only if
\lambda
is a half-integer, and the isomorphism is
v_{2}\mapsto s^{2\lambda}v_{1}
. If
\lambda
is not a half-integer, this change of uniformizing parameters yields gauge fields that cannot be trivialized.

Alternatively, we can realize
X\cong\mathrm{Spec}\,\Bbbk[x,y]/(x^{2}+y^{2}-1)
and choose
x\in\Bbbk[x,y]/(x^{2}+y^{2}-1)
as a uniformizing parameter in
D(y)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in X\mid y\notin\mathfrak{p}\}
. Again, if we consider
\widetilde{M_{1}}(D(y))
with uniformizing parameter
x
, a gauge field will emerge, and it can only be trivialized if
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We can now move on to the main result of this section, where we prove that the modules
P_{\alpha}
that appear in Proposition
5.4
can be glued into a
\mathcal{D}
-module if
W
is integrable.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a simple differentiable holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module of type
W
. If
W
is integrable, then there exists a simple
\mathcal{D}
-module
\mathcal{P}
such that

\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{P}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{J}^{W}.

Proof.

Because
W
is integrable,
\mathcal{J}^{W}
and
\mathcal{J}^{W^{\ast}}
are well-defined tensor modules. Let
Z_{0}
be the space of constant sections in
\mathcal{Z}
corresponding to the zero central character. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the kernel of the action of
Z_{0}
on
\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{J}^{W^{\ast}}
. We claim that
\mathcal{P}
is a
\mathcal{D}
-module and
\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{P}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{J}^{W}
. Since
\mathcal{P}

\Bbbk_{0}

∎

There exists a holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module of type
W
for every
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
W
. In
[
BB24
]
, the authors wrote explicit transformation laws for the sheaf
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
. We may use the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}
and these transformation laws to create holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules using any
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
W
, as the next example shows.

Example 5.10
.

Let
W
be a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module,
\mathcal{C}=\{U_{\alpha}\}
be a finite étale cover of
X
and
U\subset X
be an étale chart with uniformizing parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. We will use this open cover and the structure sheaf of
X
to create a differentiable sheaf of
AV
-modules
\mathcal{M}
of type
W
on
X
. As vector space,
\mathcal{M}(U)=\mathcal{O}(U)\otimes W
. The action of
\mathcal{O}(U)
is given by multiplication on the left side, while the action of the Lie algebra
\Theta(U)
on
\mathcal{M}(U)
is given by

\left(f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right)(gw)=f\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}(x)w+\sum_{k=1}^{n}g\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}(x)(E_{ki}w).

For each
\alpha
, define
\mathcal{M}(U_{\alpha})=\mathcal{O}(U_{\alpha}\cap U)\otimes W
. The action of
\mathcal{O}(U_{\alpha})
on
\mathcal{M}(U_{\alpha})
is given by left multiplication, while the action of the Lie algebra
\Theta(U_{\alpha})
on
\mathcal{M}(U_{\alpha})
is

\displaystyle\left(f\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\right)(g\otimes w)=f\frac{\partial g}{\partial y_{i}}\otimes w+g\sum_{a,k,j=1}^{n}\left(f\frac{\partial^{2}x_{j}}{\partial y_{a}\partial y_{i}}\frac{\partial y_{a}}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y_{a}}\frac{\partial y_{a}}{\partial x_{k}}\frac{\partial x_{j}}{\partial y_{i}}\right)\otimes E_{kj}w,

where
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
are the uniformizing parameters of
U_{\alpha}
. The transformation law for
\mathcal{M}
comes from the transformation law for
\mathcal{O}
, i.e.,
f_{U_{\alpha}}\otimes w\mapsto f_{U_{\beta}}\otimes w
. The resulting sheaf is a holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module of type
W
, and its global sections are
\mathcal{M}(X)=\mathcal{O}(U)\otimes W
. If
W
is integrable, then
\mathcal{J}^{W}
is a
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-submodule of
\mathcal{M}
. However,
\mathcal{M}
could be simple depending on
W
and
X
.

The following example applies this construction to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Example 5.11
.

Set
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Consider the usual cover of
X
given by
U_{0}=\mathrm{Spec}\,(\Bbbk[x])
and
U_{1}=\mathrm{Spec}\,(\Bbbk[y])
with the gluing
x\mapsto y^{-1}
. Let
\lambda\in\Bbbk
and consider the
AV
-module
\mathcal{M}(U_{0})=\mathcal{O}(U_{0})=\Bbbk[x]
on
U_{0}
, where the action of
\mathcal{O}(U_{0})
is given by multiplication and the action of
\Theta(U_{0})=\mathrm{Der}(\Bbbk[x])
is given by

\left(x^{k}\frac{d}{dx}\right)x^{l}=(l+\lambda k)x^{k+l-1},\ k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

On the other hand,
\mathcal{M}(U_{1})=\mathcal{O}(U_{0}\cap U_{1})=\Bbbk[y,y^{-1}]
as a vector space, the action of
\mathcal{O}(U_{1})=\Bbbk[y]
is given by left multiplication and

\left(y^{k}\frac{d}{dy}\right)y^{l}=\left(l-\lambda(2-k)\right)y^{k+l-1},\ k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\ l\in\mathbb{Z}.

The gluing in
\mathcal{M}(U_{0}\cap U_{1})=\mathcal{O}(U_{0}\cap U_{1})
is given by
x\mapsto y^{-1}
. The resulting
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module
\mathcal{M}
is simple if and only if
\lambda
is not a half-integer.

Let us give an example of an indecomposable
AV
-module
M
on an affine variety, which is a free
A
-module, decomposes as a tensor product of a
D
-module
P
with a tensor module,
M\cong P\otimes W
, where
P
is not free over
A
, but only projective.

Example 5.12
.

Let
X
be an affine elliptic curve
y^{2}=t^{3}-t
. Let
A
be the algebra of polynomial functions on
X
,
A=\Bbbk[t,y]/\left<y^{2}-t^{3}+t\right>
.
Lie algebra
V=\mathrm{Der}A
of vector fields on
X
is a free
A
-module,
V=A\tau

[
BF18
]
, generated by vector field

\tau=2y\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+(3t^{2}-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

Consider an ideal
P=\left<t,y\right>
in
A
. Since ideal
P
is not principal, as an
A
-module,
P
is not free, but only projective and has rank 1. Let us denote the generators of
P
by
T
and
Y
. We can turn
P
into a
D
-module by introducing the following gauge fields:

\tau(T)=(t^{2}+1)Y,\ \ \tau(Y)=(t^{3}+t)T.

It is easy to check that these are compatible with the relation

yY=(t^{2}-1)T
in
P
.

Next, consider the following rank 2 AV-module
A\otimes W
, where
W
is a 2-dimensional vector space with basis
\{v,u\}
. Fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
and define the action of
V
by

\displaystyle f\tau\cdot gv

\displaystyle=f\tau(g)v+\alpha g\tau(f)v+g\tau(\tau(f))u,

\displaystyle f\tau\cdot gu

\displaystyle=f\tau(g)u+(\alpha+1)g\tau(f)u.

It is easy to check that
A\otimes W
is an extension of two gauge modules.

Define
AV
-module
M
as the tensor product
M=P\otimes_{A}(A\otimes W)\cong P\otimes_{\Bbbk}W
.
Even though
P
is not free over
A
, the tensor product
P\otimes W
is a free
A
-module of rank 2 with generators
tT\otimes v+Y\otimes u
,
Y\otimes v+tT\otimes u.

We conclude this paper by showing that simple differentiable holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-modules restrict to locally simple modules over the tangent sheaf, provided that the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module that appears in
\mathcal{M}
is not isomorphic to
\Lambda^{k}(\Bbbk^{n})
, the
k
-th exterior product of the natural representation
\Bbbk^{n}
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. The following proof is similar to the one given to an analogous statement about tensor modules for the Lie algebra of vector fields on the
n
-dimensional torus
[
Rao96
]
and the affine space
[
LLZ18
]
.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
W
be a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module and let
\mathcal{M}
be a simple holonomic
\widehat{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{V}}
-module of type
W
. If
W
is not isomorphic to
\Lambda^{k}(\Bbbk^{n})
for some
k=0,\dots,n
, then
\mathcal{M}
is a locally simple sheaf of
\Theta
-modules.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that
\mathcal{M}(U)
is a simple
\Theta(U)
-module for each étale chart
U
. Let
U\subset X
be an étale chart with uniformizing parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
,
A=\mathcal{O}(U)
,
V=\mathrm{Der}(A)
, and
D=\mathcal{D}(U)
. Then,
\mathcal{M}(U)\cong T(P,W)
for some simple holonomic
D
-module
P
and a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
W
. If
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
, then

\displaystyle\left(x_{l}^{k}\partial_{i}\right)\left(fx_{l}^{m-k}\partial_{j}\right)(p\otimes w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(x_{l}^{k}\partial_{i}\right)\left((fx_{l}^{m-k}\partial_{j}p)\otimes w+\sum_{s=1}^{n}\left((m-k)fx_{l}^{m-k-1}\partial_{s}(x_{l})+x_{l}^{m-k}\partial_{s}(f)\right)p\otimes E_{sj}w\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(fx_{l}^{m}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}+(m-k)fx_{l}^{m-1}\partial_{i}(x_{l})\partial_{j}+x_{l}^{m}\partial_{i}(f)\partial_{j}\right)p\right)\otimes w

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{n}kfx_{l}^{m-1}\partial_{r}(x_{l})\partial_{j}p\otimes E_{ri}w

\displaystyle+(m-k)\sum_{s=1}^{n}\left(\delta_{sl}fx_{l}^{m-1}\partial_{i}+\delta_{sl}x_{l}^{m-1}\partial_{i}(f)+(m-k-1)\delta_{sl}\delta_{il}fx_{l}^{m-2}\right)p\otimes E_{sj}w

\displaystyle+\sum_{s=1}^{n}\left(\left(x_{l}^{m}\partial_{s}(f)\partial_{i}+(m-k)\delta_{li}x_{l}^{m-1}\partial_{s}(f)+x_{l}^{m}\partial_{i}(\partial_{s}(f))\right)p\right)\otimes E_{sj}w

\displaystyle+\sum_{r,s=1}^{n}\left(\left((m-k)k\delta_{rl}\delta_{sl}fx_{l}^{m-2}+\delta_{rl}k\partial_{s}(f)x_{l}^{m-1}\right)p\right)\otimes E_{ri}E_{sj}w.

Therefore,

(2)

\sum_{k=0}^{2}(-1)^{k}\binom{2}{k}(x_{l}^{k}\partial_{i})(fx_{l}^{2-k}\partial_{j})(p\otimes w)=2fp\otimes(\delta_{il}E_{lj}-E_{li}E_{lj})w.

Furthermore,

(3)

\partial_{i}(p\otimes w)=(\partial_{i}p)\otimes w.

Suppose
N\subset\mathcal{M}(U)
and
v\in N
. Write

v=\sum_{s=1}^{k}p_{s}\otimes w_{s}.

By equations (
2
) and (
3
),

\sum_{i=1}^{k}up_{q}\otimes(\delta_{il}E_{lj}-E_{li}E_{lj})w_{q}\in N

for every
u\in D
. By Schur’s Lemma, for every
q\in P
there exists
u_{r}\in D
such that
u_{r}p_{s}=\delta_{rs}q
. Hence,
P\otimes(\delta_{il}E_{lj}-E_{li}E_{lj})w_{s}\subset N
for each
s=1,\dots,k
. If
P\otimes w\subset N
, then
P\otimes E_{ab}w\subset N
because

p\otimes E_{ab}w=\left(x_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{b}}\right)(p\otimes w)-\left(x_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{b}}p\right)\otimes w\in N.

Therefore, the subspace
\{w\in W\mid P\otimes w\subset N\}
is a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-submodule of
W
, and we have that
(\delta_{il}E_{lj}-E_{li}E_{lj})w_{q}=0
, for each
q=1,\dots,k
, since
W
is simple and
N
is a proper subspace of
M
.

The term
p_{s}\otimes E_{a_{1}b_{1}}\cdots E_{a_{k}b_{k}}w_{s}
appears in
\left(x_{a_{1}}\partial_{b_{k}}\right)\cdots\left(x_{a_{k}}\partial_{b_{k}}\right)v
.
We may use the same argument given above to show that

(\delta_{il}E_{lj}-E_{li}E_{lj})U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})w_{q}=(\delta_{il}E_{lj}-E_{li}E_{lj})W=0.

Consequently, if
\lambda
is the highest weight of
W
, then
\lambda(E_{ii})\in\{0,1\}
for each
i=1,\dots,n
. Because
W
is a simple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module,
\lambda(E_{ii}-E_{i+1,i+1})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Hence, there exists
k
such that
\lambda(E_{ii})=1
if
i\leq k
and
\lambda(E_{ii})=0
if
i>k
. We conclude that
W
is isomorphic to
\Lambda^{k}(\Bbbk^{n})
, which contradicts our hypothesis. In conclusion,
M=\mathcal{M}(U)
is a simple
\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{O}(U))
-module.
∎

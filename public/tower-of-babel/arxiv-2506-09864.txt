Unusual electron correlations in Kagome metals AV 3 ​ Sb 5 \text{AV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}} (A= K, Rb, Cs)
Feihu Liu, Changxu Liu, Maolin Zeng, Qiyi Zhao

Abstract
The investigation of electronic order-quantum phase interplay in Kagome lattices commonly employs the extended Kagome-Hubbard model, where the critical parameters comprise on-site ( U ) (U) and intersite ( V ) (V) Coulomb interactions. In prototypical kagome metals AV 3 ​ Sb 5 \text{AV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}} (A = K, Rb, Cs), the geometrically frustrated quasi-2D architecture induces pressure-dependent complexity in vanadium d-electron correlations, necessitating systematic theoretical scrutiny. Utilizing the d − d ​ p d-dp model within constrained random phase approximation (cRPA), we quantified U U , V V , and Hund’s coupling J J under hydrostatic pressure (0-9 GPa). While KV 3 ​ Sb 5 \text{KV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}} and RbV 3 ​ Sb 5 \text{RbV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}} exhibit pressure-insensitive interaction parameters, CsV 3 ​ Sb 5 \text{CsV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}} manifests anomalous discontinuities in U U and V V near 0.2 0.2 GPa, suggesting a first-order electronic phase transition. This work establishes cRPA-derived interaction landscapes as critical predictors for pressure-tunable quantum phenomena in correlated kagome systems, offers a new insight into the understanding of the interplay between the CDW transition and the double superconductivity dome in CsV 3 ​ Sb 5 \text{CsV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}} at low pressure.

II
Computation methods

In solids, the fully screened Coulomb interaction is given by

W=(1-vP)^{-1}v,

(1)

where
v
is the bare Coulomb interaction and
P
is the polarization operator. At the lowest approximation, namely the random phase approximation (RPA),
P
is given by

\begin{split}P(r,r^{\prime};\omega)=&\sum_{i}^{occ}\sum_{j}^{unocc}\psi_{i}(r)\psi_{i}^{\star}(r^{\prime})\psi_{j}^{\star}(r)\psi_{j}(r^{\prime})\\
&\times\left\{\frac{1}{\omega-\epsilon_{j}+\epsilon_{i}+\mathrm{i}\delta}-\frac{1}{\omega+\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}-\mathrm{i}\delta}\right\},\end{split}

(2)

where
\psi_{i}
and
\epsilon_{i}
are the Kohn-Sham eigenfunctions and eigenvalues obtained from DFT calculation.

If we consider the V-
3d
bands as a correlated subsystem, the polarization may be divided into two parts
P=P_{r}+P_{d}
, in which
P_{d}
only include screening from the V-
3d
orbitals, and
P_{r}
is the rest of the polarization. It is straightforward to calculate
P_{d}
by restricting the summation in Eq.(
2
) to V-
3d
bands only, namely the cRPA method. Then the effective interactions among electrons living in V-
3d
bands are given by

U(\omega)=(1-vP_{d})^{-1}v.

(3)

Note that, the definition of
U(\omega)
in Eq.(
3
) is independent of the basis function and is a general approach. However, in realistic system, it is often complicated by the issue of correctly constructing the correlated subsystem.

As a typical example, in
\text{CsV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
, the V-
3d
bands that spanned the correlated space are not well isolated from the Sb-
5p
bands, which can be seen from the projected band structure depict in FIG.
2
. How to uniquely identify the
d
-
d
screening channel to obtain the polarization
P_{d}
is a major issue in cRPA method. This involves searching for some well-defined localized states to be a basis set. One popular choice is the maximally localized wannier functions (MLWF)
\ket{\phi_{Rm}}
, which can be calculated by the Fourier transformation from the reciprocal to the direct space of DFT Kohn-Sham eigenfunctions
Marzari et al. 2012
.

In this regard, there are four different approaches to calculate the polarization: (a). The band method
Aryasetiawan et al. 2004
which directly uses Adler-Wiser equation
Adler 1962
;
Wiser 1963
to calculate
P_{d}
. This method only works if the target space is well isolated from the rest and there is no entanglement between them. (b). The disentanglement method which heavily relies on the special choice of a energy window within which the Wannier basis can be constructed so that the rest and the correlated subspace are fully decoupled. However, in some cases, the strongly dependence of the energy window will cause unnecessary arbitrariness
Miyake et al. 2009
. (c). The weighted method introduces probability weights of KS functions being in the target Wannier space to define the polarization
Şaşıoğlu et al. 2011
. (d). The projector method, which is similar to the weighted method, uses projector between Bloch functions and Wannier functions to construct target space spanned by correlated Bloch functions. This method uses k-p perturbation theory to calculate the long-wave limit contribution which is ill defined for metallic system
Kaltak and Vienna Austria
. As in our case, the long-wave limit will not be included for the projector method.

We further calculate the effective interaction matrix elements defined by

U_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}^{R_{1}R_{2}R_{3}R_{4}}(\omega)=\bra{\phi_{R_{1}m_{1}}\phi_{R_{2}m_{2}}}U(\omega)\ket{\phi_{R_{3}m_{3}}\phi_{R_{4}m_{4}}},

(4)

where
R_{i}
labels the atomic sites and
m_{i}
refers to angular quantum numbers indicating the dominant orbital character of wannnier funciton. For
\text{AV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
, there are three
V
atoms in one unit cell, one can easily obtain the on-site, nearest site interaction
U
and
V
respectively:

\begin{split}U_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}(\omega)&=U_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}^{R_{1}R_{2}R_{3}R_{4}}(\omega)|_{R_{1}=R_{2}=R_{3}=R_{4}}\\
V_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}(\omega)&=U_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}^{R_{1}R_{2}R_{3}R_{4}}(\omega)|_{R_{1}=R_{2}=A;\,R_{3}=R_{4}=B}\end{split}

(5)

where A, B, C label three V sites schematically displayed in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
Crystal structure of
\text{AV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
. The dotted line indicates a unit cell, in which three atomic V sites are labeled by
A,B,C
.

For practical usage within many-body techniques via tight-binding Hamiltonian, it is convenient to further reduce these matrices elements to a reasonable number of parameters, such as the orbital averaged onsite Coulomb interaction
U
, nearest site interaction
V
and the Hund exchange
J
:

\begin{split}U&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}U_{mmmm}\\
V&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}V_{mmmm}\\
J&=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\sum_{m\neq m^{\prime}}^{N}U_{mm^{\prime}m^{\prime}m},\end{split}

(6)

where
N
the number of orbitals. Note that the calculated interaction parameters in Eq.(
6
) are genuinely depending on the frequency. However, the Hamiltonian approach to the many body systems such as DFT+DMFT method cannot utilize the frequency dependant interactions
Aryasetiawan et al. 2004
. Therefore, we only consider the static
U
at
\omega=0
in our calculation.

IV
Disscussion and conclusions

To elucidate this singularity, our findings must be contextualized within the established framework of CDW transitions in
\text{CsV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
. As demonstrated in Ref.
Denner et al. 2021
, the KHM exhibits two interwinded ground states: CDO phase (
l=0
) and chiral CBO phase (
l=1
). The CDO-CBO crossover is governed by the Coulomb ratio
U/V
, with the CBO phase dominating at reduced
U/V
values. Crucially, the CBO phase in a 2D-KHM can be lifted to a 3D
2\times 2\times 2
CDW order
Ishioka et al. 2010
, as experimentally resolved via hard-x-ray scattering
Li et al. 2021
and scanning tunneling microscopy
Xu et al. 2022
. This ratio dependence mirrors our observed interaction parameter collapse in
\text{CsV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
at
0.2
GPa. The abrupt
U/V
reduction (
\Delta(U/V)\approx 25\%
at
0.2
GPa) suggests a phase transition
X\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptp\hskip 8.19447pt}2\times 2\times 2
CDW, where the precursor phase
X
likely corresponds to a metastable CDO-CBO hybrid state.

Complementary X-ray scattering study also reveal a coexistence of
2\times 2\times 2
and
2\times 2\times 1
CDW order in
\text{CsV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}

Li et al. 2022
. Below
p<0.7
GPa, these orders exhibit degeneracy. Above
p>0.7
GPa, the degeneracy is lifted because the
2\times 2\times 1
order is suppressed faster than the
2\times 2\times 2
order. Considering the competing nature between CDW and superconductivity in
\text{CsV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
, it is highly likely the
2\times 2\times 1
CDW order is responsible for the first superconducting dome and
2\times 2\times 2
CDW order is for the second
Li et al. 2022
.

Our systematic analysis reveals a stark dichotomy in pressure response among
\text{AV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
compounds: while
\text{KV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
and
\text{RbV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
maintain pressure-invariant Coulomb interactions,
\text{CsV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
exhibits a pronounced electronic instability at
p\approx 0.2
GPa characterized by discontinuous jumps in both on-site (U) and intersite (V) interaction parameters (
\Delta U\approx 0.36
eV,
\Delta U\approx 0.38
eV) . This critical behavior, absent in isostructural counterparts, strongly suggests a pressure-induced quantum phase transition unique to the
Cs
variant, likely driven by its interlayer coupling effects because it has the largest interlayer distance.

Building upon the established Coulomb-interaction (U,V) dependence of charge density wave (CDW) orders in kagome metals, our findings demonstrate tentative agreement with both theoretical predictions and experimental observations of intertwined CDW phases in
\text{AV}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
compounds (A = K, Rb, Cs). The calculated pressure-induced variations in
U
and

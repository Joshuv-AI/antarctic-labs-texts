Neoadjuvant Anbenitamab and HB1801 in ERBB2-Positive Breast Cancer
Authors: Junjie Li, Tong Liu, Ke-Da Yu, Huawei Yang, Zhen Huang, Yi Zeng, Peng Ji, Xiaoke Hou, Chunping Liu, Yiwen Zhang, Nanlin Li, Zhong Ouyang, Xiaobo Wu, Xiaorong Bai, Yanxiang Guo, Guohui Han, Shien Cui, Zhijun Zhu, Yu Zhang, Xiaobo Hu, Xiaopeng Ma, Peifen Fu, Jinhai Zhu, Yuan Dong, Tao Wu, Hongyan Jia, Fei Xu, Yu Ren, Jianyun Nie, Dai Bing, Qiang Mu, Tao Sun, Jing Luo, Zhenchuan Song, Xiangshun Kong, Hao Zhang, Zhiyong Wu, Zhihong Wang, Dong Song, Haiquan Jia

ABSTRACT
Key PointsQuestionDoes the combination of ERBB2-biparatopic antibody (anbenitamab) and albumin-bound docetaxel (HB1801) increase the total pathological complete response (tpCR) rate compared with the standard regimen of trastuzumab, pertuzumab, and docetaxel in patients with ERBB2-positive breast cancer?FindingsIn this randomized clinical trial that included 521 patients with early or locally advanced ERBB2-positive breast cancer, the tpCR rate was significantly higher in the anbenitamab and HB1801 group compared with the control group; both regimens had manageable safety profiles.MeaningThese findings suggest that neoadjuvant anbenitamab and HB1801 led to clinically meaningful tpCR improvement; this new combination might establish a new standard for neoadjuvant treatment in ERBB2-positive breast cancer.

Introduction

ERBB2-positive breast cancer is an inherently aggressive subtype that accounts for approximately 15% to 20% of all breast cancer. Neoadjuvant therapy is routinely recommended for stage II and III disease, and dual ERBB2 blockade with trastuzumab and pertuzumab plus taxane-based chemotherapy has become the cornerstone of treatment. The NeoSphere and PEONY trials demonstrated adding pertuzumab to trastuzumab and taxane (THP) yielded higher pathological complete response (pCR) rates and durable long-term survival outcomes without substantial differences in tolerability. Subsequent trials, such as TRYPHAENA and KRISTINE, sought to escalate this chemotherapeutic backbone by incorporating carboplatin (TCbHP). TCbHP successfully drove pCR rates to around 55% and is recommended as the preferred choice according to the National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines, although adding carboplatin is associated with elevated incidences of hematologic toxic effects and debilitating gastrointestinal adverse events.

Consequently, the contemporary treatment paradigm seeks to maintain optimal therapeutic efficacy while systematically mitigating toxic effects. The HELEN-006 and NeoCARHP trials showed that substituting alternative taxane-based regimens (docetaxel, paclitaxel, nab-paclitaxel) and completely omitting carboplatin from the dual-blockade treatment could achieve noninferior pCR rates with significantly improved tolerability, especially in patients with earlier-stage disease. Concurrently, the treatment landscape is being reshaped by the rapid emergence of novel ERBB2-directed antibody-drug conjugates. In the DESTINY-Breast11 trial, the sequential administration of trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) followed by THP yielded a robust pCR rate of 67.3% in a high-risk patient population. THP remains the cornerstone treatment strategy, and how to further improve the compatibility of this strategy while minimizing severe toxic effects is currently a research hot spot.

Anbenitamab (KN026) is a novel, recombinant humanized biparatopic antibody engineered to simultaneously bind 2 distinct, nonoverlapping extracellular domains of the ERBB2 protein: domain II (pertuzumab recognition site) and domain IV (trastuzumab recognition site). The biparatopic architecture achieved a more comprehensive and durable extinction of intracellular ERBB2 signaling networks than can be achieved by the of separate coadministration of monoclonal antibodies. HB1801, a novel albumin-bound formulation of docetaxel, completely obviates the need for high-dose glucocorticoid premedication and facilitates rapid infusion with the potential for improved systemic tolerability and convenience compared to conventional solvent-based docetaxel.

Therefore, in this multicenter, open-label, phase 3, registrational randomized clinical trial, the Neo-Healer/BCTOP-H-N01 trial, we assessed the efficacy and safety of neoadjuvant anbenitamab plus HB1801 compared with the standard regimen of THP, with or without carboplatin, in patients with early and locally advanced ERBB2-positive breast cancer. Here, we report the primary end point of total pathological complete response (tpCR), along with safety profile.

Methods

Study Design and Patients

The study protocol for this randomized clinical trial and all its amendments were approved by the institutional review board or independent ethics committee of all 61 study sites. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the Good Clinical Practice Guideline. All patients provided written informed consent before enrollment. The trial protocol and statistical analysis plan are provided in Supplement 1. This study is reported following the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) reporting guideline.

This was a randomized, phase 3 registrational trial conducted in 61 hospitals in China from December 19, 2024, and August 29, 2025 (eTable 1 in Supplement 2). Eligible patients were aged 18 years or older with pathologically confirmed ERBB2-positive breast cancer (defined as immunohistochemistry score ≥3 or ≥2 with in situ hybridization amplification on the primary tumor, according to the American Society of Clinical Oncology and College of American Pathologists guidelines). Patients were required to have an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1, a left ventricular ejection fraction of 55% or higher, and no prior systemic therapy. Patients were histologically confirmed to have early-stage (T2-3, N0-1, M0) or locally advanced (T2-3, N2-3, M0; T4, any N, M0) breast cancer. Complete eligibility criteria are listed in Supplement 1.

Randomization and Interventions

Patients were randomized (1:1) to receive either anbenitamab plus HB1801 with or without carboplatin (investigational group), or THP with or without carboplatin (control group). Randomization was performed centrally using interactive response technology and stratified by tumor stage (early stage vs locally advanced), hormone receptor (HR) status (estrogen receptor and progesterone receptor positive vs negative) and carboplatin use (yes vs no). Neither patients nor investigators were masked to the assigned treatments. The central pathologists evaluating the pathological results were masked to the treatment assignments.

Patients in the investigational group received intravenous anbenitamab at a dose of 30 mg/kg, and intravenous HB1801 at a dose of 100 mg/m2 on day 1 and every 3 weeks for 6 cycles. Patients in the control group received concurrent intravenous trastuzumab, with an initial loading dose of 8 mg/kg and a maintenance dose of 6 mg/kg, as well as intravenous pertuzumab with a loading dose of 840 mg and a maintenance dose of 420 mg, and intravenous docetaxel at a dose of 75 mg/m2 on day 1 and every 3 weeks for 6 cycles. Investigators decided whether to use carboplatin based on patient’s characteristics before randomization. In both groups, intravenous carboplatin was given at a dose based on the area under the concentration time curve of 6 mg/mL per min on day 1 and every 3 weeks for 6 cycles. Granulocyte colony-stimulating factor use was permitted for primary and secondary prophylaxis of treatment-emergent neutropenia.

Neoadjuvant treatment was administered for 6 cycles until confirmed disease progression, intolerable toxic effects, physician decision, or patient withdrawal, with no crossover permitted. Definitive surgery was scheduled within 4 weeks after completion of neoadjuvant therapy. In the adjuvant setting, radiotherapy and endocrine therapy were delivered when clinically indicated. For adjuvant anti-ERBB2 therapy, receipt of trastuzumab (6 mg/kg) and pertuzumab (420 mg) every 3 weeks was recommended for all patients achieving pCR in both groups and 14 cycles of trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) were recommended for patients achieving non-pCR.

End Points and Assessments

The primary end point was tpCR, defined as pathological stage (postneoadjuvant) postneoadjuvant T0 or in situ postneoadjuvant N0, as determined by a blinded independent review committee. The secondary end points included tpCR, breast pCR (bpCR, defined as postneoadjuvant T0 or ductal carcinoma/lobular in situ) and overall response rate, event-free survival, invasive disease–free survival, and safety. The safety cohort included all patients who received at least 1 dose of the study drugs. Safety assessments encompassed the evaluation of the incidence and severity of treatment-emergent adverse events, graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0. Adverse events were monitored continuously throughout the study and for up to 30 days after the last treatment dose, extended to 90 days for serious adverse events. An independent data monitoring committee provided trial oversight.

We assessed pCR after patients completed neoadjuvant therapy. Clinical examinations, blood cell counts, and patient-reported adverse events were recorded weekly during each cycle. Additional safety assessments, including electrocardiograms, biochemistry tests, and other evaluations, were conducted on day 1 of each cycle. Tumor assessments were conducted by investigators at baseline and every 2 cycles thereafter until disease progression, patient withdrawal, initiation of new therapy, or death, in accordance with Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 guidelines. Left ventricular ejection fraction was measured by echocardiography or multigated acquisition scan at baseline and every 2 cycles during the treatment, as well as at treatment discontinuation and during the safety follow-up visit.

Statistical analysis

Efficacy was assessed in the intention-to-treat population, which included all patients who underwent randomization. Safety was assessed in the as-treated population, including all patients who were randomized and received at least 1 dose of the study drug. Assuming tpCR of 51.6% for the control group vs 64.5% in the investigational group, a sample size of approximately 520 participants is required to detect superiority, with 85% power at an overall 2.5% 1-sided α level. The 2-sided Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test was used to compare tpCR rates between groups (stratified by tumor stage, HR status at presentation, and carboplatin use). Patients not evaluable for pathological response (ie, did not undergo surgery) were considered negative responders. Data analysis was performed using SAS software version 9.4 or higher (SAS Institute). P values were 1-sided, and statistical significance was set at P ≤ .025. The full statistical analysis plan is detailed in Supplement 1. Data were analyzed from February 1 to March 20, 2026.

Results

Patient Deposition and Characteristics

A total of 612 patients underwent screening, of whom 521 patients (median [IQR] age, 52.0 [23-79] years) were eligible and randomly assigned to the investigational group (263 patients) or the control group (258 patients). In the investigational group, 256 patients (97.3%) completed the neoadjuvant treatment, and 254 patients (96.6%) underwent surgery, while in the control group, 255 patients (98.8%) completed the neoadjuvant treatment and underwent surgery (Figure 1). The baseline characteristics were well balanced; overall, 291 patients (55.9%) had HR-positive disease, 179 patients (34.4%) had locally advanced disease, and 223 patients (42.8%) received carboplatin (Table 1). Patients who received carboplatin generally had a higher tumor burden, with 99 patients (44.4%) with N2 or N3 disease and 102 patients (45.7%) with locally advanced disease (eTable 2 in Supplement 2).

Primary Outcome

At the database cutoff on January 28, 2026, all 521 patients were evaluable for the primary analysis. Based on blinded independent review committee evaluation, tpCR was achieved in 164 patients (62.4% [95% CI, 56.2% to 68.2%]) in the investigational group and 132 patients (51.2% [95% CI, 44.9% to 57.4%]) in the control group (absolute difference, 11.4 [95% CI, 3.2 to 19.6] percentage points; P = .004) (eFigure in Supplement 2). The tpCR, breast pCR, and overall response rates were 168 patients (63.9%), 173 patients (65.8%), and 248 patients (94.3%) in the investigational group and 132 patients (51.2%), 143 patients (55.4%), and 238 patients (92.2%) in the control group, respectively. In prespecified exploratory subgroup analyses, the higher tpCR rate with anbenitamab plus HB1801 was generally consistent across subgroups (eFigure in Supplement 2). Among patients who received carboplatin, tpCR occurred in 74 patients (66.7%) in the investigational group, compared with 61 patients (54.5%) in the control group. Among patients who did not receive carboplatin, tpCR occurred in 90 patients (59.2%) in the investigational group and 71 patients (48.6%) in the control group. The tpCR rates in the investigational group and the control group were 77 patients (51.7%) and 63 patients (44.4%), respectively, among patients with HR-positive disease; 87 patients (76.3%) and 69 patients (59.5%), respectively, among those with HR-negative disease; 111 patients (63.8%) and 87 patients (51.8%), respectively, among those with early-stage disease; and 53 patients (59.6%) and 45 patients (50.0%), respectively, among those with locally advanced disease. Prespecified subgroup analyses also favored the investigational group across almost all patients, except for those with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 1, a finding likely attributable to the limited sample size in that subgroup (Figure 2). With the median follow-up time of less than 8 months, long-term outcomes are not reported here.

Safety

In the investigational group, 258 patients (98.1%) completed all 6 cycles of anbenitamab, 256 patients (97.3%) completed all 6 cycles of HB1801, and 100 of 111 patients (90.1%) completed all 6 cycles of carboplatin. In the control group, 255 patients (98.8%) completed trastuzumab and pertuzumab, 254 patients (98.4%) completed docetaxel, and 103 of 112 patients (92.0%) completed carboplatin. No dose reductions of anbenitamab were required. Dose reductions were required in 24 patients (9.1%) for HB1801 and in 38 patients (34.2%) for carboplatin in the investigational group; in the control group, dose reductions were required in 14 patients (5.4%) for docetaxel and in 39 patients (34.8%) for carboplatin (eTable 3 in Supplement 2).

Nonhematological adverse events, such as rash, diarrhea, nausea, peripheral edema, and peripheral sensory neuropathy, were recorded more frequently in the investigational group. The rates of grade 3 or 4 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were similar between groups (Table 2). In the investigational group, grade 3 or 4 TRAEs occurred in 38 of 111 patients (34.2%) with carboplatin, most commonly neutropenia (14 patients [12.6%]), leukopenia (11 patients [9.9%]), and anemia (12 patients [10.8%]), and in 39 of 152 patients (25.7%) without carboplatin, most commonly neutropenia (16 patients [10.5%]), leukopenia (9 patients [5.9%]), and anemia (5 patients [3.3%]). The corresponding frequencies in the control group was 34 patients (30.4%) among those receiving carboplatin and 39 patients (26.7%) among those not receiving carboplatin. For hematological toxic effects, the incidence of grade 3 neutropenia was 30 patients (11.4%) in the investigational group (14 patients [12.6%] receiving carboplatin and 16 patients [10.5%] not receiving without carboplatin) and 28 patients (10.9%) in the control group (15 patients [13.4%] receiving carboplatin and 13 patients [8.9%] not receiving carboplatin). Treatment discontinuation occurred in 13 patients (4.9%) in the investigational group (10 patients [9.0%] receiving carboplatin and 3 patients [2.0%] not receiving carboplatin) and in 9 patients (3.5%) in the control group (8 patients [7.1%] receiving carboplatin and 1 patients [0.7%] not receiving carboplatin) (eTable 4 in Supplement 2). The incidence of hematological and nonhematological toxic effects increased to varying degrees after the addition of carboplatin; however, most TRAEs were grade 1 or 2, with no significant increase in grade 3 or 4 nonhematological toxic effects (eTable 5 in Supplement 2). No symptomatic left ventricular systolic dysfunction and no treatment-related deaths were reported in either group.

Discussion

In this multicenter, phase 3 registrational randomized clinical trial, neoadjuvant anbenitamab with HB1801 resulted in a significantly higher tpCR rate compared with the conventional THP-based therapy (62.4% vs 51.2%) in patients with stage II or III ERBB2-positive breast cancer, with a highly manageable safety profile. Crucially, the efficacy benefit of anbenitamab-HB1801 regimen was remarkably consistent across all prespecified clinical subgroups, including clinical stage, HR status, and the investigator-determined use of carboplatin.

Anbenitamab’s design allows it to bind 2 nonoverlapping epitopes on the ERBB2 receptor simultaneously. This bivalent engagement creates a physical constraint that induces dense receptor clustering on the cellular surface. Unlike the passive coadministration of separate monoclonal antibodies, this biparatopic mechanism forces rapid receptor internalization and subsequent lysosomal degradation, driving a profound and sustained extinction of downstream intracellular signaling cascades. A phase 2 neoadjuvant trial enrolled 30 patients with ERBB2-positive breast cancer, among whom 46.7% had stage III disease, and found a tpCR rate of 56.7% after 4 cycles of anbenitamab and docetaxel. Previous trials also found that anbenitamab demonstrated significant efficacy in ERBB2-positive metastatic breast cancer and gastric cancer. HB1801, using nanoparticle albumin-bound technology, completely eliminates the toxic solvents polysorbate 80 and ethanol required for standard docetaxel formulation. This architectural shift not only abrogates the necessity for high-dose glucocorticoid premedication but also drastically reduces the incidence of severe hypersensitivity reactions, enabling the administration of higher doses without a proportional increase in toxic effects, thereby optimizing drug delivery to the tumor microenvironment. The favorable profile and the synergistic mechanics of the investigational agents provide a biologic and clinical rationale for further evaluation in early breast cancer. However, anbenitamab and HB1801 were combined and tested simultaneously in our study, so the design of our trial cannot clarify which individual agent solely contributed to the pCR benefit.

Meta-analyses have indicated that achieving tpCR is powerfully prognostic indicator for improved event-free survival and long-term clinical benefit, particularly in ERBB2-positive disease, thereby validating its utility in registrational trial designs. Neoadjuvant TCbHP is recommended as the preferred choice in this setting, with pCR rates up to approximately 55% with 6 cycles and up to 68% with 9 cycles, but at the cost of substantial hematologic, gastrointestinal, and neurologic toxic effects that complicate treatment adherence. Recent phase 3 studies, HELEN-006 and NeoCARHP, showed omitting carboplatin could achieve noninferior pCR rates with significantly improved tolerability. Therefore, in this study, the decision of whether to use carboplatin was based on patient’s tumor burden, which led to patients with more locally advanced receiving carboplatin in both groups. Our study was not designed to prove which patients might benefit more from treatment with carboplatin. However, whether carboplatin was used was 1 of the predetermined stratification factors in this study. We found consistently improved tpCR regardless of carboplatin use (absolute difference, 12.3% in patients receiving carboplatin and 10.7% in patients not receiving carboplatin), which suggests that anbenitamab and HB1801 could improve tpCR rate in a broad spectrum of patients with ERBB2-positive breast cancer.

Antibody-drug conjugates have rapidly been integrating into the treatment of early-stage breast cancer, such as in the DESTINY-Breast11 trial, which found that a sequential regimen of 4-cycle T-DXd followed by 4 cycles of THP could achieve an impressive pCR rate of 67.3% in a high-risk population. In contract, this study used a 6 cycles of anbenitamab and HB1801 and achieved a comparable efficacy profile (overall tpCR, 62.4%; tpCR with carboplatin, 66.7%), which may allow patients to undergo surgery earlier and entirely avoid the pulmonary toxic effects of T-DXd. Since the efficacy of anbenitamab and HB1801 was superior to that of THP, 4 cycles of T-DXd followed by 4 cycles of anbenitamab plus HB1801 may have the potential to further improve therapeutic efficacy, which is worthy of evaluation in future clinical trials.

The safety profile observed in the investigational group was generally consistent with the established toxic effects of taxane-based chemotherapy and ERBB2-targeted blockade. Nonhematological adverse events, such as rash, diarrhea, nausea, peripheral edema, and peripheral sensory neuropathy, were recorded more frequently in the investigational group. The groups showed similar rates of overall grade 3 or 4 TRAEs, with the most common being neutropenia, leukopenia, and anemia, which were consistent with the toxic effects typically observed with chemotherapy. Notably, the incidence of grade 3 or 4 diarrhea in the investigational group was markedly lower than that reported with TCbHP regimens in the TRYPHENA study (11.8%). Furthermore, no symptomatic left ventricular systolic dysfunction was observed in this trial, compared with 4.4% (1.2% grade ≥3) in the CLEOPATRA pertuzumab group. Omitting carboplatin demonstrated an improved safety profile; however, adding carboplatin increased incidence of TRAEs mainly in grades 1 or 2, with no significant increases in grades 3 or 4 nonhematological toxic effects. Approximately 98% of patients completed all 6 planned cycles of anbenitamab and HB1801, highly supporting the clinical promotion of this regimen.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the trial was conducted exclusively within China, which might limit the generalizability to broader populations with different genetic backgrounds and demographic profiles. Second, the optimal adjuvant treatment after neoadjuvant anbenitamab-based therapy remains an area of active, dynamic exploration. In the absence of evidence-based data, HP was recommended in patients with pCR, while T-DM1 was recommended in patients without pCR in this study. However, the DESTINY-Breast05 trial established adjuvant T-DXd as superior to T-DM1 for patients with high-risk disease without pCR. The interventional regimen in this study fits perfectly into this evolving algorithm by maximizing pCR rates with low toxic effects, effectively reserving T-DXd escalation for residual disease. Third, it should be noted that anbenitamab and HB1801 were tested simultaneously in our study, so we cannot clarify which individual agent solely contributed to the pCR benefit, although an ongoing phase 3 trial is currently investigating the utility of anbenitamab and HB1801 in the adjuvant setting. The efficacy of each investigational agents should be further evaluated in future studies.

Conclusions

This randomized clinical trial found that neoadjuvant anbenitamab plus HB1801, with or without carboplatin, resulted in a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in tpCR compared to conventional THP-based therapy in patients with ERBB2-positive breast cancer. These findings, together with the manageable safety profile, suggest that this regimen may offer a promising treatment option for these patients.

Asymptotically tight Lagrangian dual of smooth nonconvex problems via improved error bound of Shapley-Folkman Lemma
Santanu S. Dey, Jingye Xu

Abstract
In convex geometry, the Shapley–Folkman Lemma asserts that the nonconvexity of a Minkowski sum of n n -dimensional bounded nonconvex sets does not accumulate once the number of summands exceeds the dimension n n , and thus the sum becomes approximately convex. Originally published by Starr in the context of quasi-equilibrium in nonconvex market models in economics, the lemma has since found widespread use in optimization, particularly for estimating the duality gap of the Lagrangian dual of separable nonconvex problems. Given its foundational nature, we pose the following geometric question: Is it possible for the nonconvexity of the Minkowski sum of n n -dimensional nonconvex sets to even diminish instead of just not accumulating as the number of summands increases, under some general conditions? We answer this affirmatively. First, we provide an elementary geometric proof of the Shapley–Folkman Lemma based on the facial structure of the convex hull of each set. This leads to refinement of the classical error bound derived from the lemma. Building on this new geometric perspective, we further show that when most of the sets satisfy a certain local smoothness condition which naturally arises in the epigraphs of smooth functions, their Minkowski sum converges directly to a convex set, with a vanishing nonconvexity measure. In optimization, this implies that the Lagrangian dual of block-structured smooth nonconvex problems—with potentially additional sparsity constraints—is asymptotically tight under mild assumptions, which contracts non-vanishing duality gap obtained via classical Shapley-Folkman Lemma.

Consider a block-structured nonconvex program:

In particular, when each set

Although this distinction may appear subtle, it is in fact crucial—it underpins several nontrivial results concerning the strong duality in Lagrangian relaxations of nonconvex programs

;
9

A notable example is the celebrated

-Lemma

This result can be interpreted as a direct consequence of Dines Lemma

In this paper, we adopt this perspective and investigate the convexity of

to ensure the strong duality of the Lagrangian dual of (
1

While each individual set
\mathcal{P}^{(i)}
may be nonconvex, it is possible that their Minkowski sum

closely approximates its convex hull.
This phenomenon is formally characterized by the

In the setting where

\displaystyle\forall\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbf{conv}}(\mathcal{P}),\;\exists I\in\binom{[k]}{n}\text{ such that }\mathbf{x}\in\left(\sum_{i\in[k]\setminus I}\mathcal{P}^{(i)}\right)+\left(\sum_{i\in I}{\mathbf{conv}}(\mathcal{P}^{(i)})\right).

Intuitively, the Shapley-Folkman Lemma implies that every point in
{\mathbf{conv}}(\mathcal{P})
is close to some point in
\mathcal{P}
, up to at most
n
nonconvex summands.
If all
\mathcal{P}^{(i)}
are contained within a ball of radius

, a quantitative measure of this approximate convexity can be obtained.
In particular, under the Hausdorff distance, it has been shown

\displaystyle d_{H}(\mathcal{P},{\mathbf{conv}}(\mathcal{P}))\leq\sqrt{n}R.

This quantitative bound can further be employed to derive an a priori estimate of the duality gap

associated with problem (

The Shapley–Folkman lemma was originally introduced by Starr in the study of quasi-equilibria in nonconvex market models in economics. In this context, it can be interpreted as an approximate form of strong duality for Lagrangian relaxations. Its connection to optimization was later developed in works such as
6
;
2
;
28
, which used the lemma to quantify the duality gap of problem (
1

More recently, several works have focused on refining the quantitative bounds provided by the Shapley–Folkman lemma, particularly on improving estimates of the Hausdorff distance

or similar notion of nonconvexity; see, for example,
7
;
20
;
25
. An analogue of the Shapley–Folkman lemma for discrete convex sets has also been developed in

In this work, we seek to refine the quantitative characterization of the Hausdorff distance

d_{H}(\mathcal{P},{\mathbf{conv}}(\mathcal{P}))
, given its central role in assessing the tightness
of Lagrangian duals. We begin by presenting a new and elementary geometric proof of the
Shapley-Folkman Lemma, which leverages the facial structure of each

\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{P}^{(i)})
. This perspective enables us to refine the classical
error bound.
Building on this geometric insight, we further demonstrate that the Minkowski sum of the sets

\mathcal{P}^{(i)}
can directly converges to its convex hull as the number of summands grows,
i.e.,
d_{H}(\mathcal{P},\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{P}))\to 0
as
k\to\infty
,
provided enough sets satisfy some mild “local smoothness” condition (see Theorem
4.19
). Such smoothness naturally arises
in the epigraphs of smooth (possibly nonconvex) functions, allowing us to establish that the following problem
admits an asymptotically tight Lagrangian dual as
k\to\infty
:

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}f^{(i)}(\mathbf{x}^{(i)})

(4)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}B^{(i)}\mathbf{x}^{(i)}\leq\mathbf{b},

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}^{(i)}\|_{0}\leq s^{(i)},\quad\forall i\in[k],

where each
f^{(i)}
is a smooth (possibly nonconvex) function, and
B^{(i)}
and
s^{(i)}

satisfy some mild conditions (see Theorem
5.31
). Note that we only requires that the sparsity level

is not too small, i.e.,

. To model problems without sparsity constraints, one may simply let
s^{(i)}=n,\forall i\in[k]
. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result establishing an asymptotically vanishing nonconvexity measures for Minkowski sums of nonconvex sets within the framework of the Shapley–Folkman lemma.

This work focuses on Minkowski sum of
\mathcal{P}^{(i)}
without averaging since its convexity directly reflects the duality gap of problem (
1
). In the classical applications
of the Shapley–Folkman Lemma, the analysis is typically performed on the Minkowski average
of the sets
\mathcal{P}^{(i)}
.
This choice arises because the the error bound

d_{H}(\mathcal{P},{\mathbf{conv}}(\mathcal{P}))
does not vanish in the classic analysis of Shapley-Folkman Lemma but
d_{H}(\mathcal{P},{\mathbf{conv}}(\mathcal{P}))
is both homogeneous and independent of

,
which immediately yields

d_{H}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{k}\mathcal{P},\tfrac{1}{k}{\mathbf{conv}}(\mathcal{P})\right)\leq\tfrac{\sqrt{n}R}{k},

implying a linear convergence of the averaged sum toward its convex hull as
k
increases.
In contrast, our analysis applies directly to the unnormalized Minkowski sum. When averaging is applied, our result demonstrates
a superlinear convergence rate, thereby strengthening the classical result.

3
Shapley-Folkman Lemma and duality gap of Lagrangian relaxation

In this section, we state Shapley-Folkman Lemma formally and show how it is related to duality gap of Lagrangian relaxation.

3.1
Shapley-Folkman Lemma

Theorem 1
.

(Shapley-Folkman Lemma) Let

;
20

Shapley-Folkman Lemma can be interpreted that the nonconvexity of the Minkowski sum of nonconvex sets, once reaching certain cap depending on the dimension, will not accumulate as the number of nonconvex sets increase. This phenomenon can be quantified by the following corollary. The proof of a slightly improved version is provided later in Lemma
4.12
under the pointedness assumption. In general, estimating the nonconvexity measures

and

Corollary 1
.

Let Let

be nonempty sets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\sum_{i\in[k]}A_{i}\right)\leq\Xi\left(\sum_{i\in[k]}A_{i}\right)\leq\sqrt{n}\beta\text{ and therefore }\Phi\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in[k]}A_{i}\right)\leq\frac{n}{k}\beta.

If we further assumes
\Rd(A_{i})\leq\gamma,\forall i\in[k]

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\sum_{i\in[k]}A_{i}\right)\leq\Xi\left(\sum_{i\in[k]}A_{i}\right)\leq\sqrt{n}\gamma\text{ and therefore }\Phi\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in[k]}A_{i}\right)\leq\frac{n}{k}\gamma.

includes
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim(F_{i})
linearly independent vectors. If
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim(F_{i})>n
, there exists
\mathbf{d}=[\mathbf{d}_{1},\mathbf{d}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{d}_{k}]\in\text{span}\{V\}
that

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}_{1}+\mathbf{d}_{2}+\cdots+\mathbf{d}_{k}=\textbf{0}

Let
[\mathbf{y}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}_{2}^{\prime},\cdots,\mathbf{y}_{k}^{\prime}]=[\mathbf{y}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{y}_{k}]+\lambda\mathbf{d}
. Since each
{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i})
is pointed, there exists some choice of
\lambda,\epsilon
such that at least one
\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\prime}
reaches the boundary of
F_{i}
while keeping other
\mathbf{y}_{j}^{\prime}
in
F_{j}
. In this case, the total dimension
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim(F_{i})
is decreased and we still ensure that
\mathbf{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\prime}
for some
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i})
.

Since
\dim(F_{i})\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim(F_{i})\leq n
implies that at least
k-n
faces have dimension
0
and are extreme points which lies in
A_{i}
. Let
\mathcal{I}=\{i\in[k]:\dim(F^{(i)})>0\}
. It is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}\in\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i})+\sum_{i\in[k]\setminus\mathcal{I}}A_{i}.

and therefore Lemma
4.6
recovers the class Shapley-Folkman Lemma (Theorem
1
) under the pointedness condition.

Moreover, one can further use Lemma
4.6
to strength the refined Shapley-Folkman Lemma recently proposed in
7
where the notion of
r
-th convex hull in
7
corresponds to dimension of face
F_{i}
in Lemma
4.6
but we additionally requires that the points used to construct
r
-th convex hull lie in a
r
-dimension face
F_{i}
.

4.2
Error bounds via randomized rounding

Let
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{k}
be nonempty set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i})
is pointed. In this subsection, we adopt the geometric perspective introduced in Lemma
4.6
to refine the classical bound of
\Phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)
stated in Corollary
1
. For any
\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbf{conv}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)
, Lemma
4.6
implies the existence of faces
F_{i}
of
{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i})
such that
\mathbf{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{y}_{i}\text{where }\mathbf{y}_{i}\in F_{i},~\forall i\in[k].

By definition,
\Phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)
quantifies how close
\mathbf{y}\in\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i})
is to some point
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\in\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}
in the Euclidean norm, that is,
\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|_{2}\leq\Phi(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i})
. We observe that a good
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}
can be constructed via randomly sampling some points from
F_{i}
. We first establish some results that is used to obtain such sampling.

Claim 1
.

Let
C
be a convex set and
F
be its face. For any
\mathbf{x}\in F
, if
\mathbf{x}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}
for some
\lambda_{i}>0,\mathbf{x}_{i}\in C,\forall i\text{ and }\sum_{i=1}\lambda_{i}=1
. Then
\mathbf{x}_{i}\in F,\forall i
.

Proof 4.8
.

Fix some index
j
. Write

x=(1-\lambda_{j})\,\underbrace{\Big(\tfrac{1}{1-\lambda_{j}}\sum_{i\neq j}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\Big)}_{=:y}\;+\;\lambda_{j}x_{j}.

By convexity of
C
, we have
y\in C
and
x_{j}\in C
. Since
0<\lambda_{j}<1
and
x\in F
,
the point
x
is a strict convex combination of
y
and
x_{j}
.
By the definition of a face, we conclude

y\in F
and
x_{j}\in F
. As this holds for each
j
, all
x_{i}\in F
.

Claim 2
.

Let
A
be a nonempty set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\Xi(A)<\infty
, then
for every face
F
of
{\mathbf{conv}}(A)
,
\Xi(A\cap F)\leq\Xi(A)
.

Proof 4.9
.

Consider any
\mathbf{x}\in F\subseteq{\mathbf{conv}}(A)
, by the definition of
\Xi(A)
, there exists
T\subseteq A
such that
\Rd(T)\leq\Xi(A)
and
\mathbf{x}
is a strict convex combination of some points
\mathbf{x}_{i}
from
T
.
Since
F
is the face of
{\mathbf{conv}}(A)
, Claim
1
implies that
\mathbf{x}_{i}\subseteq F
.
Since
\mathbf{x}
is an arbitrary point in
F
and
{\mathbf{conv}}(A\cap F)\subseteq F
, this further implies that
\Xi(A\cap F)\leq\Xi(A)
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
A
be a set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for every
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbf{conv}}(A)
, there exists a finite distribution
\mathcal{D}
such that
\mathcal{D}
is supported on
A
,
\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim\mathcal{D}}(Z)=\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbb{E}(\left\lVert Z-\mathbb{E}(Z)\right\rVert_{2}^{2})\leq\Xi^{2}(A)
.

Proof 4.11
.

For any
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbf{conv}}(A)
, by the definition of
\Xi(A)
, there exist some
T\subseteq A
such that
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbf{conv}}(T)
and
\Rd(T)\leq\Xi(A)
. Since the statement of the Lemma is invariant under translation, we may translate
A
so that
\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\Xi(A)
for all
\mathbf{y}\in T
.
By Carathéodory’s theorem,
\mathbf{x}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}=\sum_{i\in[n+1]}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{p}_{i}\text{ where }\lambda\in\Delta^{n+1}\text{ and }\mathbf{p}_{i}\in T.

We define random variable
Z\sim\mathcal{D}
that
Z=\mathbf{p}_{i}
with probability
\lambda_{i}
. It is clear that
\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim D}(Z)=\mathbf{x}
and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\left\lVert Z-\mathbb{E}(Z)\right\rVert_{2}^{2})=\mathbb{E}(\left\lVert Z\right\rVert_{2}^{2})-\left\lVert\mathbb{E}Z\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}(\left\lVert Z\right\rVert_{2}^{2})\leq\Xi^{2}(A).

where the last line uses that fact that
\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\Xi(A)

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{k}
be nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
F_{i}
be a face of
{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i}),\forall i\in[k]
and let
\mathcal{I}:=\{i\in[k]:\dim(F_{i})>0\}
. Then it follows that

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\cap F_{i}\right)\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Xi^{2}(F_{i})}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Xi^{2}(A_{i})}.

Proof 4.13
.

For every
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbf{conv}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\cap F_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i}\cap F_{i})
, there exits some
\mathbf{x}_{i}
such that
\mathbf{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{i}
and
\mathbf{x}_{i}\in{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i}\cap F_{i})
. We aim to construct
\mathbf{y}_{i}
such that
\mathbf{y}_{i}\in A_{i}
and
\left\lVert\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right)-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{y}_{i}\right)\right\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Xi^{2}(F_{i})}
. For any
i\not\in\mathcal{I}
,
\dim(F_{i})=0
and therefore it is a extreme point so that
\mathbf{x}_{i}\in A_{i}\cap F_{i}
. For
i\in I
, by Lemma
4.10
, there exists a distribution
Z_{i}\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}
such that
\mathbb{E}_{Z_{i}\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}(Z_{i})=\mathbf{x}_{i}
and
\mathcal{D}_{i}
is supported on
F_{i}\cap A_{i}
and
\mathbb{E}(\left\lVert Z_{i}-\mathbf{y}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2})\leq\Xi^{2}(A_{i}\cap F_{i})\leq\beta^{2}
. Then we (randomly) construct
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{y}_{1}+\mathbf{y}_{2}+\dots\mathbf{y}_{k}
in the following way:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{i}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{x}_{i}&\text{ if }i\in[k]\setminus\mathcal{I},\\
Z_{i}\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}&\text{ if }i\in\mathcal{I},\end{cases}

Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}(\mathbf{x}_{i}-Z_{i})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert(\mathbf{x}_{i}-Z_{i})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Xi^{2}(F_{i}).

Therefore there exists a choice of
\mathbf{y}
such that

\left\lVert\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right)-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{y}_{i}\right)\right\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Xi^{2}(F_{i})}.

Since
\mathbf{x}
is arbitrary and by Claim
2
, this implies that
\Phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\cap F_{i}\right)\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Xi^{2}(F_{i})}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Xi^{2}(A_{i})}.

4.3
Improved error bounds via local geometry

In this subsection, we seek sharper bounds on

\Phi\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)
by leveraging the geometric
structure of the summands
A_{i}
. Our goal is to identify sufficient
conditions under which

\Phi\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)\;\longrightarrow\;0\quad\text{as }k\to\infty,

that is, the Minkowski sum becomes asymptotically convex.

For each
\mathbf{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{y}_{i}\in{\mathbf{conv}}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)
, Lemma
4.6
implies that each
\mathbf{y}_{i}
lies in the Minkowski sum of certain faces
F_{i}
of
{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i})
.
The error bound presented in Lemma
4.12
can be interpreted as follows: we attempt to round
\mathbf{y}
as a whole to a point in
\sum_{i=1}^{k}(F_{i}\cap A_{i})
.
This perspective naturally suggests a potential further refinement of Lemma
4.12
, wherein we aim to round
\mathbf{y}
directly to a point in
\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}
.
However, this ambitious idea encounters a significant challenge, as it requires full characterization of the nonconvex sets
A_{i}
, which is typically unavailable in practice.
To address this limitation, we instead focus on exploiting only the local geometric properties of each
A_{i}
.

Definition 4.14
.

Let
A
be an nonempty set. For any
Q\subseteq{\mathbf{conv}}(A)
, we call
H\subseteq A
a hidden convex component associated with
Q
if

1.

H\subseteq A
is a closed convex set;

2.

Q\subseteq H
;

Figure 1:
Epigraph of
y=x^{4}-x^{2}
and the star point associates a
l_{2}
ball as a hidden convex component.

The definition above is illustrated in figure
1
.
Such hidden convex component may not be defined for general
Q
. On the other hand,
for each extreme point
\mathbf{v}_{i}
of
{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i})
, there always exists a trivial hidden convex
component—namely the singleton set
H_{i}=\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\}
. It is immediate that the existence
of such trivial hidden convex components does not drive

\Phi\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)
to zero. For instance, take

A_{i}=\{0,1\}^{n}
for all
i\in[k]
. Their Minkowski sum

\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}
is contained in the integer lattice
\{0,1,\dots,k\}^{n}
, and clearly

\Phi\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)
does not vanish.
A natural next attempt is to require each hidden convex component
H_{i}
to have nonempty interior.
However, as illustrated in Figure
4
, the mere existence of
hidden convex components with non-vanishing interior still does not guarantee any improvement in the
convexification of the Minkowski sum. In fact, diminishing the nonconvexity measure

\Phi\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)
requires smoothness condition of
H_{i}
.

Definition 4.15
.

A differentiable function
f(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
L
-smooth if

f(\mathbf{y})\leq f(\mathbf{x})+\left\langle{\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}},{\nabla f(\mathbf{x})}\right\rangle+\dfrac{L}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2},\forall\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 4.16
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty closed convex set with
0
with its interior.
The
gauge function
associated with
C
is

\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{C}:=\inf\{\lambda>0:x\in\lambda C\},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For a hidden convex component
H
with
0
in its interior, we quantify its smoothness by the smoothness of the square gauge function
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{H}^{2}
, which is related to the concept of
(2,D)
-smooth in functional analysis
23
.

To utilize the local geometry, we first overload the definition of
\Phi^{K}(\cdot)
: given a closed convex set
K
with
0
in its interior, we let

\displaystyle\Phi^{K}(A,D):=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in A}\inf_{\mathbf{y}\in D}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{K}.

It is therefore straightforward to see that
\Phi^{K}(A)=\Phi^{K}({\mathbf{conv}}(A),A)
. Moreover, it is worth mentioning that both
\Phi(A)
and
\Xi(A)
are invariant under translating
A
.

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{k}
be nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Xi(A_{i})\leq\beta,\forall i\in[k]
. Let
F_{i}
be a face of
{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i}),\forall i\in[k]
and
\mathcal{I}:=\{i\in[k]:\dim(F_{i})>0\}
. Let
H_{i}
be a hidden convex component associated with
F_{i}
for all
i\in[k]\setminus\mathcal{I}
and
H=\sum\limits_{i\in[k]\setminus\mathcal{I}}H_{i}
. Suppose
H
has nonempty interior, let
\mathcal{H}
be a translation of
H
that contains
0
in its interior and if we have
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
is
L
-smooth, then

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathcal{H}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}F_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)\leq\sqrt{1+\dfrac{L}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}^{n}\Xi^{2}(F_{i})}-1.

Proof 4.18
.

\Phi^{\mathcal{H}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}F_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)
is equivalent to the maximum possible distance (under
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}
) from points in
\sum_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}
to
\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}
. If
\sum_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
for some
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is clear that
\Phi^{\mathcal{H}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}F_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)\leq\Phi^{\mathcal{H}}\left(\mathcal{F},\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)
.
We derive an upper bound of this quantity by choosing
\mathcal{F}:=\left(\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}}F_{i}\right)+\left(\sum\limits_{i\in[k]\setminus\mathcal{I}}H_{i}\right)
. We try to show that any point in
\left(\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}}F_{i}\right)+\left(\sum\limits_{i\in[k]\setminus\mathcal{I}}H_{i}\right)
can be rounded to some point in
\left(\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}}F_{i}\cap A_{i}\right)+\left(\sum\limits_{i\in[k]\setminus\mathcal{I}}H_{i}\right)\subseteq\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}
with a desired distance.

Since the target statement is invariant under translation, we first translate
A_{i}
and
H_{i}
so that
\sum\limits_{i\in[k]\setminus\mathcal{I}}H_{i}=\mathcal{H}
. Therefore, any point
\mathbf{p}
in
\left(\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}}F_{i}\right)+\left(\sum\limits_{i\in[k]\setminus\mathcal{I}}H_{i}\right)
, can be written as

\mathbf{p}=\left(\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathbf{p}_{i}\right)+\mathbf{v}

where
\mathbf{p}_{i}\in F_{i}
and
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{H}
. By Lemma
4.10
, we can equip each
F_{i}
with a distribution
Z_{i}\sim D_{i}
with support on
A_{i}\cap F_{i}
such that
\mathbb{E}_{Z_{i}\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}(Z_{i})=\mathbf{p}_{i}
and
\mathbb{E}(\left\lVert Z_{i}-\mathbb{E}(Z_{i})\right\rVert_{2}^{2})\leq\Xi^{2}(F_{i})
.
Now we try to bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}Z_{i}\right)-\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}Z_{i}-\mathbf{p}_{i}\right)-\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)

Let
\mathbf{g}:=\nabla\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}
and applying the definition of
L
-smoothness, this further yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}Z_{i}-\mathbf{p}_{i}\right)-\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)+\mathbb{E}\left(\left\langle{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\left(Z_{i}-\mathbf{p}_{i}\right)},{\mathbf{g}}\right\rangle\right)+\dfrac{L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}Z_{i}-\mathbf{p}_{i}\right)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)+\frac{L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}Z_{i}-\mathbf{p}_{i}\right)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)+\frac{L}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathbb{E}(\left\lVert Z_{i}-\mathbf{p}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{L}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Xi^{2}(F_{i}).

This implies there exists some point in
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}A_{i}\cap F_{i}
whose distance defined by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}
to
\mathbf{p}
is at most
\sqrt{1+\frac{L}{2}\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Xi^{2}(F_{i})}
. Therefore, by the positive homogeneousness of
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}
,
the
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}
distance of
\mathbf{p}
to
\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}A_{i}\cap F_{i}\right)+\mathcal{H}
is at most
\sqrt{1+\frac{L}{2}\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Xi^{2}(F_{i})}-1
.

Theorem 4.19
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{k}
be nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Xi(A_{i})\leq\beta,\forall i\in[k]
such that
{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i})
is pointed and closed. If every extreme point of
A_{i}
has a
l_{2}
ball with radius
r_{i}
(
r_{i}
can be zero) as hidden convex component, let
r_{*}=\inf_{\mathcal{I}\subseteq[k]:|\mathcal{I}|=n}\sum_{i\in[k]\setminus\mathcal{I}}r_{i}
. Then it follows that

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)\leq\sqrt{r_{*}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}n\beta^{2}}-r_{*}.

If there exists some
r>0
such that
r_{i}\geq r
for all
i\in[k]
, then

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)\leq\sqrt{(k-n)^{2}r^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}n\beta^{2}}-(k-n)r.

As
k\to\infty
, it is clear the above
\Phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)\to 0
.

Proof 4.20
.

To bound
\Phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)
, it suffices to show that
for any
\mathbf{p}\in\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i})
, we have
\Phi\left(\mathbf{p},\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)\leq\sqrt{r_{*}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}n\beta^{2}}-r_{*}
. By Lemma
4.6
, there exists face
F_{i}
of
{\mathbf{conv}}(A_{i})
such that
\mathbf{p}\subseteq\sum_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim(F_{i})=n
. By the hypothesis, we can find
H_{i}=\mathcal{B}(\textbf{0},r_{i})
as hidden convex component associated with
F_{i}
for all
i\in[k]\setminus\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathcal{H}=\sum\limits_{i\in[k]\setminus\mathcal{I}}H_{i}=\mathcal{B}(\textbf{0},r_{*})
. Then
\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}=\dfrac{1}{r_{*}}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
is
\dfrac{1}{r_{*}^{2}}
-smooth. Lemma
4.17
implies that

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathcal{H}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}F_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)\leq\sqrt{1+\dfrac{1}{2r_{*}^{2}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}^{n}\Xi^{2}(F_{i})}-1.

This implies that

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}F_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{r_{*}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}^{n}\Xi^{2}(F_{i})}-r_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{r_{*}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}^{n}\beta^{2}}-r_{*}

where the last inequality uses Claim
2
. Since
\mathbf{p}
is arbitrary, this implies that
\Phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\right)\leq\sqrt{r_{*}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}n\beta^{2}}-r_{*}.
. The rest of statement directly follows that
r_{*}\leq(k-n)r
.

We end the section with serval examples and remarks.

Remark 4.21
.

Lemma
4.17
can be generalized by quantifying the smoothness of the hidden convex components through the smoothness (possibly with respect to a different norm) of the
p
-th power of a gauge function
\|\cdot\|_{H}^{p}
for some
p>1
, following the same probabilistic argument.
We do not pursue this extension here, as it naturally requires alternative notions of nonconvexity beyond those considered in this work.
For simplicity, we therefore focus on the classical nonconvexity measures
\Phi(\cdot)
and
\Xi(\cdot)
that appear in the literature on the Shapley–Folkman lemma, due to their direct relevance in estimating the duality gap
\Delta
.

Remark 4.22
.

In general, the smoothness of hidden convex component is required for establishing the vanishing of nonconvexity. To illustrate this phenomenon, consider the simple example
A_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathbf{0},1)=A_{0}+\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathbf{0},k)

where
A_{0}:=\{(0,0),(0,1)\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a nonconvex set and
\mathcal{B}_{p}(\mathbf{0},1)
denotes the unit
p
-norm ball. Figures
4
,
4
and
4
depict the resulting Minkowski sums for
k=1,3,

Definition 5.24

The maximal distortion under this linear projection is quantified by

Definition 5.27

Given a matrix

and let

B\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{s}(B):=\inf_{S\subseteq[n]:|S|=s}\sigma_{\inf}\left(\tilde{B}_{S}(\tilde{B}_{S})^{\top}\right)

\tilde{B}_{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m+1)\times|S|}

with indices in
S

Remark 5.28
.

To obtain a nondegenerate hidden convex component,
we require
\mathcal{L}_{s}(B^{(i)})>0
. This condition is equivalent to the existence of

m+1

A necessary condition for this is

is not very restrictive: it holds

perturbation to

whenever

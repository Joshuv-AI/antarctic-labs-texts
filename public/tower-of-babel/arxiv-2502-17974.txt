On the existence and the stability of solutions in nonconvex vector optimization †
Tran Van Nghi, Le Ngoc Kien, Nguyen Van Tuyen

Abstract
The paper is devoted to the existence of weak Pareto solutions and the weak sharp minima at infinity property for a general class of constrained nonconvex vector optimization problems with unbounded constraint set via asymptotic cones and generalized asymptotic functions. Then we show that these conditions are useful for studying the solution stability of nonconvex vector optimization problems with linear perturbation. We also provide some applications for a subclass of robustly quasiconvex vector optimization problems.

Introduction

Vector optimization is an important part of mathematical programming. It is used to denote a type of optimization problems, where two
or more objectives are to be minimized over a given set.
Vector optimization problems arise naturally in a variety of
theoretical and practical problems and have a wide range of applications. These problems are important tools for problems in economics, management, multicriteria design optimization, water resource planning, medicine, mathematical biology, etc; see, for example,
[
4
,
5
,
38
,
13
,
14
,
31
,
41
]
.

An important and fundamental issue in theory of vector optimization problems is the investigation of existence and stability of solutions; see, for example,
[
3
,
14
,
38
,
31
,
11
,
30
,
39
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
. In this
paper, we consider the following vector optimization problem

\mathrm{Min}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}}\,\{f(x):=(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{m}(x))\;:\;x\in X\},

(VP)

where
X
is a closed and unbounded subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i~=~1,\ldots,m
, are lower semicontinuous functions.

Some authors have studied theorems of Frank–Wolfe type for polynomial vector optimization problems.
Kim et al.
[
32
]
showed the existence of Pareto solutions of an unconstrained polynomial vector optimization problem under the assumptions that the
Palais–Smale type condition holds and the image of the objective function has a
bounded section. For the case where
X
is a convex semi-algebraic set and
f
is convex, Lee et al.

[
37
]
proved that (
VP
) has a Pareto solution if and only if
f(X)
has a
nonempty bounded section. Recently, Flores-Bazán et al.
[
18
]
introduced two notions of vector asymptotic functions for vector functions with
their image space ordered by the nonnegative orthant and established coercivity properties, coercive and noncoercive existence results for solutions of
vector optimization problems.

The main difficulty in investigation the existence of (weak) Pareto solutions is due to directions in unbounded constraint set along which the objective function may decrease. Asymptotic tools have proved to be very useful in order to
overcome the above difficulty; see, for example,
[
45
,
48
,
1
,
18
,
36
,
9
]
. The
idea of the asymptotic approach is based on the asymptotic
behavior of sets and functions. When the
constraint set is unbounded, asymptotic tools
allow us to obtain coercivity properties and existence results for vector optimization problems.

In this paper, we investigate the existence and the stability of weak Pareto solutions of constrained nonconvex vector optimization problems by using the tool of asymptotic analysis. We make the following contributions to vector optimization problems:

\bullet
As a first result, we present new results on existence of weak Pareto solutions and the weak sharp minima at infinity property for a general class of constrained nonconvex vector optimization problems with unbounded constraint set via asymptotic cones and generalized asymptotic functions.

\bullet
We obtain stability results, including: the nonemptiness and compactness, the upper/lower semicontinuity, and the closedness of the weak Pareto solution map of the considered problem under linear perturbations.

\bullet
We introduce the concept of robustly quasiconvex for vector-valued functions
and apply our previous results to the particular case when the
objective function is quasiconvex or
\alpha
-robustly
quasiconvex. By using the
q
-asymptotic function, we develop sufficient conditions for
the mentioned problems under weaker assumptions.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
provides the necessary background required for the subsequent development. In Section
3
, we investigate the solution existence and the weak sharp minima at infinity. Section
4
presents stability results under a linear perturbation. Section
5
proposes some applications to the particular case when the
objective function is quasiconvex or
\alpha
-robustly
quasiconvex.
A summary of conclusions is explained in the last section.

Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, the considered spaces are finite-dimensional Euclidean with the inner product and the norm being denoted, respectively, by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\|\cdot\|
. The open
ball centered at the origin with a radius of
\alpha>0
and the set of all positive integer numbers are denoted, respectively, by
\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}
and
\mathbb{N}
. The nonnegative orthant of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is defined by

\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}:=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\;:\;x_{i}\geq 0,i=1,\ldots,m\}.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
be an extended-real-valued function. The
domain
and
epigraph
of
\varphi
are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle\operatorname{dom}\varphi:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\varphi(x)<+\infty\}\ \ \text{and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{epi}\,\varphi:=\{(x,t)\in\mathrm{dom}\,\varphi\times\mathbb{R}\,:\,\varphi(x)\leq t\}.

We say that
\varphi
is proper if
\operatorname{dom}\varphi\neq\emptyset
and
\varphi(x)>-\infty
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We adopt the usual convention
\inf\nolimits_{\emptyset}\varphi:=+\infty
and
\sup\nolimits_{\emptyset}\varphi:=-\infty
.

For a given
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by

\mathrm{lev}(\varphi,\alpha):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:~\varphi(x)\leq\alpha\}

the sublevel set of
\varphi
at value
\alpha
. For a nonempty subset
X
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the set of all minimum points of
\varphi
on
X
is defined by

\mathrm{argmin}_{X}\varphi:=\{x\in X:~\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(y)\ \ \forall y\in X\}.

Definition 1

We say that the function
\varphi
is:

(i)

convex
if
\mathrm{epi}\,\varphi
is a convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

quasiconvex
if
\mathrm{lev}(\varphi,\alpha)
is a convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

It is well-known that the function
\varphi
is convex if and only if its domain is convex and for every
x,y\in{\rm dom}\,\varphi

\varphi(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq\lambda\varphi(x)+(1-\lambda)\varphi(y)\ \ \forall\lambda\in[0,1]

and it is quasiconvex if and only if
for every
x,y\in{\rm dom}\,\varphi
,

\varphi(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq\max\{\varphi(x),\varphi(y)\}\ \ \forall\lambda\in[0,1].

Clearly, every convex function is also quasiconvex, but not vice versa. For example, the function
\varphi(x):=\sqrt{|x|}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
is quasiconvex but it is nonconvex.

In
(
43
, Lemma 1.1)
, the authors showed that the quasiconvexity is not stable under linear perturbations and hence, they introduced the so-called
\alpha
-robustly quasiconvex functions.

Definition 2
(
see
[
10
,
43
]
)

For
\alpha\geq 0
, a proper function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is said to be
\alpha
-robustly quasiconvex
if the
function
x\mapsto\varphi(x)+\langle u,x\rangle
is quasiconvex for all
u\in\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}
.

As shown in
(
10
, p. 1091)
, every convex function is also
\alpha
-robustly quasiconvex for all
\alpha\geq 0
, but not vice versa. Furthermore, every local minimum of an
\alpha
-robustly quasiconvex function is a global one. For a further study we refer the readers to
[
10
,
43
,
44
]
.

We now introduce the concept of robustly quasiconvex for vector-valued functions.

Definition 3

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
x\mapsto f(x):=(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{m}(x))
be a mapping and
\alpha\geq 0
. We say that
f
is:

(i)

\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
-quasiconvex
if for every
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the sublevel set

\mathrm{lev}(f,y):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,f_{i}(x)\leq y_{i},\ \ i=1,\ldots,m\}

of
f
at
y
is a convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(ii)

\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
-
\alpha
-robustly quasiconvex
if the mapping
x\mapsto f(x)+\langle u,x\rangle
is
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
- quasiconvex for all
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with
\|u\|:=\max_{i=\overline{1,m}}\|u_{i}\|<\alpha
, where

\langle u,x\rangle:=(\langle u_{1},x\rangle,\ldots,\langle u_{m},x\rangle).

It is easy to see that
f
is
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
-quasiconvex (resp.,
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
-
\alpha
-robustly quasiconvex) if and only if each component
f_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, of
f
is quasiconvex (resp.,
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
-
\alpha
-robustly quasiconvex).

Next, we recall some basic definitions and properties of asymptotic cones and functions, which can be found in
[
9
]
.

Definition 4

Let
X
be a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(i)

The
asymptotic cone
of
X
, denoted by
X^{\infty}
, is the set defined by

X^{\infty}:=\left\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}~:~\exists~t_{k}\rightarrow+\infty,~\exists~x_{k}\in X,~\frac{x_{k}}{t_{k}}\rightarrow d\right\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \emptyset^{\infty}=\emptyset.

(ii)

The (generalized)
recession cone
of
X
, denoted by
\mathrm{rec}\,X
, is the set defined by

\mathrm{rec}\,X:=\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}~:~x+td\in X\ \ \forall x\in X,\forall t\geq 0\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathrm{rec}\,\emptyset=\emptyset.

It follows from
(
9
, Propositions 2.1.1 and 2.1.2)
that
X^{\infty}
is a closed cone and
X
is bounded if and only if
X^{\infty}=\{0\}
. While
\mathrm{rec}\,X
is a convex cone,
\mathrm{rec}\,X\subset X^{\infty}
, and if
X
is bounded, then
\mathrm{rec}\,X=\{0\}
. When
X^{\infty}=\mathrm{rec}\,X
, then
X
is called regular. In particular, if
X
is convex, then it is regular and

X^{\infty}=\Big\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:~x_{0}+td\in X\ \forall~t\geq 0\Big\}\,\text{ for any }x_{0}\in X,

see, for example,
(
9
, Proposition 2.1.5)
.

Definition 5

(see
[
9
]
) Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper function. The
asymptotic function

\varphi^{\infty}~:~\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow~\overline{\mathbb{R}}
of
\varphi
is the function defined by

\varphi^{\infty}(d):=\inf\left\{\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\varphi(t_{k}d_{k})}{t_{k}}:~t_{k}\rightarrow+\infty,~d_{k}\rightarrow d\right\}\ \ \forall d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By definition, it is easy to see that
\varphi^{\infty}
is lower semicontinuous and positively homogeneous, and the following relation holds
\operatorname{epi}\varphi^{\infty}=(\operatorname{epi}\varphi)^{\infty}.
Moreover, when
\varphi
is lower semicontinuous and convex, then we have

\varphi^{\infty}(d)=\sup_{t>0}\frac{\varphi(x_{0}+td)-\varphi(x_{0})}{t}=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\varphi(x_{0}+td)-\varphi(x_{0})}{t}

for all
x_{0}\in\operatorname{dom}\varphi
; see
(
9
, Proposition 2.5.2)
. Recall that the function
\varphi
is said to be
lower semicontinuous
(lsc) at some
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
if for every real number
r<\varphi(\bar{x})
there exists a neighborhood
V
of
\bar{x}
such that
r<\varphi(x)
for all
x\in V
. We say that
\varphi
is lsc around
\bar{x}
if it is lsc at any point of some neighborhood of
\bar{x}
. If
\varphi
is lsc at every
x\in X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},
then
\varphi
is said to be lsc on
X
. By definition, it is clear that
\varphi
is automatically lsc at
\bar{x}
when
\varphi(\bar{x})=-\infty
; when
\varphi(\bar{x})=+\infty
the lower semicontinuity of
\varphi
means that the values of
\varphi
remain as large as required, provided that one stays in some small neighborhood of
\bar{x}
.

It is well-known that, if
\mathcal{K}(\varphi)=\{0\}
, where

\mathcal{K}(\varphi):=\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\varphi^{\infty}(d)\leq 0\},

then
\varphi
is coercive, i.e.,

\lim_{\lVert x\rVert\to+\infty}\varphi(x)=+\infty.

Furthermore, if
\varphi
is convex and lsc, then

\mathcal{K}(\varphi)=\{0\}\Longleftrightarrow\varphi\mathrm{~is~coercive}\Longleftrightarrow\mathrm{argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}~\varphi\ \ \text{is nonempty and compact}

see
(
9
, Proposition 3.1.3)
. However, when
\varphi
is nonconvex, the asymptotic function
\varphi^{\infty}
is not good enough for providing adequate information
on the behavior of
\varphi
at infinity. For example, let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the function defined by
\varphi(x)=\sqrt{\lvert x\rvert}
. Then
\varphi
is coercive and
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}}\,\varphi=\{0\}
, but
\mathcal{K}(\varphi)=\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, several authors have proposed different notions for dealing with, specially, quasiconvex functions, see

[
17
,
21
,
20
,

,

We now recall definitions of the upper and lower semicontinuity to set-valued mappings. Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a set-valued mapping and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
Painlevé–Kuratowski outer/upper limit
of
F
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}F(x):=\bigg\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\;\exists x_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x},y_{k}\rightarrow y\ \mbox{with}\ y_{k}\in F(x_{k})\ \ \forall k=1,2,....\bigg\}.

Definition 7

(see
[
9
]
)
We say that:

(i)

F
is
upper semicontinuous
(usc henceforth) at
\bar{x}
if, for any open
set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
F(\bar{x})\subset V
there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
F(x)\subset V
for all
x\in U
.

(ii)

F
is
lower semicontinuous
(lsc) at
\bar{x}
if
F(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
and if, for any open set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
F(\bar{x})\cap V\neq\emptyset
there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that
F(x)\cap V\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in U
.

(iii)

F
is
continuous
at
\bar{x}
if it is both usc and lsc at this point.

(iv)

F
is
closed
at
\bar{x}
if for any sequences
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
one has

x_{k}\to\bar{x},y_{k}\to\bar{y},y_{k}\in F(x_{k})\Rightarrow\bar{y}\in F(\bar{x}).

(v)

F
is
locally bounded
at
\bar{x}
if there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that
\cup_{x\in U}F(x)
is bounded.

Remark 1

(i)

By definition, it is easy to see that
F
is closed at
\bar{x}
if and only if the following condition holds

\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}F(x)\subset F(\bar{x}).

(ii)

By
(
9
, Theorem 1.4.1)
, if
F
is usc at
\bar{x}
and
F(\bar{x})
is closed, then
F
is closed at
\bar{x}
. Conversely, if
F
is closed and locally bounded at
\bar{x}
, then
F
is usc at that point. We note here that the condition of locally boundedness is essential, i.e., if
F
is closed at
\bar{x}
but not locally bounded at this point, then
F
may not be usc at
\bar{x}
. For example, let
F\colon\mathbb{R}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}
be defined by

F(x)=\begin{cases}\{\tfrac{1}{x}\}\ \ &\text{if}\ \ x\neq 0,\\
\{0\}\ \ &\text{if}\ \ x=0.\end{cases}

Then, it is easy to see that
F
is closed at
\bar{x}=0
but
F
is not usc at this point.

3
The solution existence and the weak sharp minima at infinity

In this section, we consider the following vector optimization problem:

\mathrm{Min}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}}\,\{f(x):=(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{m}(x))\;:\;x\in X\},

(VP)

where
X
is a closed and unbounded subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
f_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i\in I
with
I:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
, are lsc functions. The set

\Lambda:=\bigg\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\;:\;\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}=1\bigg\}

is called the unit simplex of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, we consider the following scalarization problem

\min\,\bigg\{\varphi_{\lambda}(x):=\langle\lambda,f(x)\rangle\;:\;x\in X\bigg\}.

(VP
λ
)

The solution set of (
VPλ
) is denoted by
S(\lambda)
.

Definition 8

Let
\bar{x}\in X
. We say that:

(i)

\bar{x}
is a
weak Pareto solution
of (
VP
) if

f(X)\cap(f(\bar{x})-\mathrm{int}\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+})=\emptyset.

The set of all weak Pareto solutions of (
VP
) is denoted by
\mathrm{Sol}^{w}\,\eqref{problem}
.

(ii)

\bar{x}
is a
Pareto solution
of (
VP
) if

f(X)\cap(f(\bar{x})-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\})=\emptyset.

The set of all Pareto solutions of (
VP
) is denoted by
\mathrm{Sol}\,\eqref{problem}
.

Remark 2

By definition, it is clear that

\mathrm{Sol}\,\eqref{problem}\subset\mathrm{Sol}^{w}\,\eqref{problem}.

The following result is well-known but we give a proof here for the sake of the reader.

Proposition 1

The following inclusion holds true

\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}S(\lambda)\subset\mathrm{Sol}^{w}\eqref{problem}.

Proof

Fix any
\lambda\in\Lambda
and let
\bar{x}\in S(\lambda)
. We need to show that
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{Sol}^{w}\eqref{problem}
. If otherwise, then there exists
x_{0}\in X
such that

f(x_{0})\in f(\bar{x})-\mathrm{int}\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+},

or, equivalently,
f_{i}(x_{0})<f_{i}(\bar{x})
for all
i\in I
. This and the fact that
\lambda\in\Lambda
imply that

\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(x_{0})<\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(\bar{x}),

contrary to
\bar{x}\in S(\lambda)
. The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 3.1
(The coercivity and the weak sharp minima at infinity)

Assume that

X^{\infty}\cap\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}(f_{i})\right)=\{0\},

(1)

where
\mathcal{K}(f_{i}):=\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,f_{i}^{\infty}(d)\leq 0\}
,
i\in I
. Then the following statements hold:

(a)

For each
i\in I
,
\mathrm{argmin}_{X}f_{i}(x)
is nonempty and compact. Consequently,
f
is bounded from below on
X
, i.e., there exists an element
a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

f(X)\subset a+\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}.

(b)

For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
S(\lambda)
is nonempty and compact. Consequently,
\mathrm{Sol}^{w}\,\eqref{problem}
is nonempty.

(c)

For all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, the function
\varphi_{\lambda}(\cdot):=\langle\lambda,f(\cdot)\rangle
is coercive on
X
, i.e.,

\lim_{x\overset{X}{\longrightarrow}\,\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=+\infty,

where
x\xrightarrow{X}\infty
means that
\|x\|\to\infty
and
x\in X
.

(d)

\mathrm{Sol}^{w}\,\eqref{problem}
is compact.

(e)

Problem (
VP
) has a weak sharp minimum at infinity, i.e., there exist constants
c>0
and
R>0
such that

\mathrm{dist}(f(x),f(\mathrm{Sol}^{w}\eqref{problem}))\geq c\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathrm{Sol}^{w}\eqref{problem})\ \ \forall x\in X\setminus\mathbb{B}_{R}.

Proof

(a)
: By definition, it is clear that
X^{\infty}
and
\mathcal{K}(f_{i})
,
i\in I
, are cones with apex at the origin. This and (
1
) imply that
X^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{K}(f_{i})=\{0\}
for all
i\in I
. Hence, it follows from
(
45
, Theorem 4.1)
that
\text{argmin}_{X}f_{i}(x)
is nonempty and compact for every
i\in I
. This implies that
f
is bounded from below on
X
.

(b)
: Fix any
\lambda\in\Lambda
and let
\varphi_{\lambda}
be the function defined by

\varphi_{\lambda}(x):=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(x)\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By (
1
),
f^{\infty}_{i}(d)>0
for all
d\in X^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
. This and
(
9
, Proposition 2.6.1(b))
imply that

(\varphi_{\lambda})^{\infty}(d)\geq\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}(f_{i})^{\infty}(d)>0\ \ \forall d\in X^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}.

Consequently,
X^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{K}(\varphi_{\lambda})=\{0\}
. Thus, for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
S(\lambda)
is nonempty and compact due to
(
45
, Theorem 4.1)
. This and Proposition
1
imply that
\mathrm{Sol}^{w}\,\eqref{problem}
is nonempty.

(c)
: Fix any
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Then we have
X^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{K}(\varphi_{\lambda})=\{0\}
. By
(
36
, Theorem 3.1(c))
,
\varphi_{\lambda}
is coercive on

u_{i}^{k}:=\frac{1}{k}d
for all
i\in I
. Then,
u_{i}^{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\to\infty
and
\langle u_{i}^{k},d\rangle>0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
for all
i\in I
. Hence,

\big(f_{i}^{u_{i}^{k}}\big)^{\infty}(d)=(f_{i})^{\infty}(d)-\langle u_{i}^{k},d\rangle<0\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

We show that
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(u^{k})
is empty for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and so which contradicts the lower semicontinuity of
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(\cdot)
at
0
and the fact that
u^{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Indeed, if otherwise, then there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(u^{k_{0}})\neq\emptyset
. Fix any
x_{0}\in\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(u^{k_{0}})
. It follows from the convexity of
X
that
x_{0}+td\in X
for all
t>0
. By
(
9
, Proposition 2.5.2)
and the convexity of
f_{i}
, one has

\big(f_{i}^{u_{i}^{k_{0}}}\big)^{\infty}(d)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{f_{i}^{u_{i}^{k_{0}}}(x_{0}+td)-f_{i}^{u_{i}^{k_{0}}}(x_{0})}{t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{f_{i}^{u_{i}^{k_{0}}}(x_{0}+td)}{t}.

Hence,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{f_{i}^{u_{i}^{k_{0}}}(x_{0}+td)}{t}=\big(f_{i}^{u_{i}^{k_{0}}}\big)^{\infty}(d)<0.

This implies that
\lim_{t\to\infty}{f_{i}^{u_{i}^{k_{0}}}(x_{0}+td)}=-\infty
for all
i\in I
. Hence, there exists
t
large enough such that

f^{u^{k_{0}}}(x_{0}+td)\in f^{u^{k_{0}}}(x_{0})-\mathrm{int}\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+},

contrary to the fact that
x_{0}\in\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(u^{k_{0}})
, as required.

Now, by the lower semicontinuity of
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(\cdot)
at
0
, we see that
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(0)
is nonempty. Hence the statement
(a)
is equivalent to the nonemptiness and compactness of
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(0)
. The equivalence of
(a)
and
(b)
follows immediately from
(
49
, Theorem 3.1)
. The proof is complete.
∎

The following example not only illustrates Theorem
4.3
but also shows that the lower semicontinuity of
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(\cdot)
does not imply the singletoness of the set
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(0)
.

Example 3

Consider the following vector optimization problem

\mathrm{Min}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\,\{f(x):=(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x)):\ x\in X\},

where
X=[-1,+\infty)
,
f_{1}(x)=x
, and
f_{2}(x)=x^{2}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(0)=[-1,0]
is not a singleton and

X^{\infty}\cap\big(\mathcal{K}(f_{1})\cup\mathcal{K}(f_{2})\big)=\{0\}.

For each
u=(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have the following perturbed problem

\mathrm{Min}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\,\{f^{u}(x):=(f_{1}^{u_{1}}(x)=x-u_{1}x,f_{2}^{u_{2}}(x)=x^{2}-u_{2}x)\;:\;x\in X=[-1,+\infty)\}.

We can check that

\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(u)=\bigg[-1,\frac{u_{2}}{2(1-u_{1})}\bigg]

and
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(\cdot)
is lsc at
0
.

5
The Quasiconvex Case

In this section, we apply our previous results to the particular case when the
objective function in problem (
VP
) is quasiconvex or
\alpha
-robustly
quasiconvex (see Definition
2
).

As mentioned in the introduction, when the function
f
is nonconvex, then the
usual asymptotic function
f^{\infty}
does not provide adequate information
on the behavior of
f
at infinity. For instance, and in relation to Theorem

3.1
, we mention that when the function
f
is quasiconvex,
the assumption

X^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{K}(f)=\{0\},

is too restrictive. Indeed, let us consider the one-dimensional real-valued
function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by
f(x)=\sqrt{\lvert x\rvert}
and
X=\mathbb{R}
. Here
f
is coercive and
{\rm argmin}_{X}\,f=\{0\}
is a singleton. However,
X^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{K}(f)=\mathbb{R}

and hence, Theorem
3.1
cannot be applied to this basic situation.

On the other hand, if we use any of the generalized asymptotic functions

f^{\infty}_{q}
and
f^{\infty}_{\lambda}
, we obtain that

f^{\infty}_{q}(u)=+\infty\ \ \forall u\neq 0,

and for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\text{lev}\,(f,\lambda)\neq\emptyset
,

0<f^{\infty}_{\lambda}(u)\leq 2\ \ \forall u\neq 0,

where

f^{\infty}_{\lambda}(u):=\sup_{x\in\text{lev}\,(f,\lambda)}\;\sup_{t>0}\frac{f(x+tu)-\lambda}{t}.

Therefore,

X^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{K}_{q}(f)=\{0\},~~{\rm and}~~X^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}(f)=\{0\},

where
\mathcal{K}_{q}(f):=\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,f^{\infty}_{q}(d)\leq 0\}
and

\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}(f):=\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,f^{\infty}_{\lambda}(d)\leq 0\}
,
respectively.

Before stating main results of this section, we give a result on the nonemptiness
and compactness of the solution set to constrained optimization problems by
using the
q
-asymptotic function.

Lemma 2

Assume that
X
is convex and
f
is
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
-quasiconvex. Then, the following assertions are equivalent:

(a)

\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}\eqref{problem}
is nonempty and compact.

(b)

X^{\infty}\cap\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}_{q}(f_{i})\right)=\{0\}
.

Proof
In order to prove the equivalence, we first show that

(f_{i}+\delta_{X})_{q}^{\infty}(d)=(f_{i})_{q}^{\infty}(d)+\delta_{X^{\infty}}(d)\ \ \forall i\in I.

Indeed, by definition of the
q
-asymptotic function and the convexity of
X
, we have

\displaystyle(f_{i}+\delta_{X})_{q}^{\infty}(d)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in{\rm dom}\,(f_{i}+\delta_{X})}\sup_{t>0}\frac{(f_{i}+\delta_{X})(x+td)-(f_{i}+\delta_{X})(x)}{t}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in X}\sup_{t>0}\frac{f_{i}(x+td)+\delta_{X}(x+td)-f_{i}(x)}{t}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(f_{i})_{q}^{\infty}(d),&\text{if}\ \ d\in X^{\infty},\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=(f_{i})_{q}^{\infty}(d)+\delta_{X^{\infty}}(d).

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{q}(f_{i}+\delta_{X})=\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;:\;(f_{i})_{q}^{\infty}(d)+\delta_{X^{\infty}}(d)\leq 0\}=X^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{K}_{q}(f_{i})\ \ \forall i\in I.

This implies that

\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}_{q}(f_{i}+\delta_{X})=X^{\infty}\cap\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}_{q}(f_{i})\right).

Combining this with
(
18
, Proposition 4.5)
we obtain the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

In the next proposition we improve Theorem
3.1
for proper,
lsc,
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
-
\alpha
-robustly quasiconvex functions.

Proposition 2

Let
X
be a convex set and
f
be a proper,
lsc,
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
-
\alpha
-robustly quasiconvex mapping

(\alpha>0)
. If

X^{\infty}\cap\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}_{q}(f_{i})\right)=\{0\},

(8)

then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with
\|u\|<\varepsilon
,
the following statements hold:

(a)

f_{u}
is bounded from below on
X
.

(b)

{\rm\mbox{\rm Sol}}^{w}(u)
is nonempty and compact.

(c)

\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{u\rightarrow 0}{\rm\mbox{\rm Sol}}^{w}(u)\subset{\rm\mbox{\rm Sol}}^{w}(0)
, i.e., the solution mapping
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(\cdot)
is closed at
0
.

(d)

\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(\cdot)
is usc at
0
.

Proof

We first show that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that the following condition holds

X^{\infty}\cap\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}_{q}(f^{u_{i}}_{i})\right)=\{0\}\ \ \forall u\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},\|u\|<\varepsilon.

(9)

Indeed, suppose on the contrary that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there
exists
u^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with
\|u^{k}\|<\frac{1}{k}
such that condition (
9
) does not hold, i.e., there exist
i_{k}\in I
and
d_{k}\in X^{\infty}\backslash\{0\}
such that

\bigg(f_{i_{k}}^{u_{i_{k}}^{k}}\bigg)^{\infty}_{q}(d_{k})\leq 0.

By the finiteness of
I
, there exists
i_{0}\in I
such that the inequality

\bigg(f_{i_{0}}^{u_{i_{0}}^{k}}\bigg)^{\infty}_{q}(d_{k})\leq 0

(10)

holds true for infinite many
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By passing to subsequences if necessary we may assume that (
10
) is satisfied for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Since
X^{\infty}
is a closed cone, by passing a subsequence if necessary, we
may assume that
\left\{h_{k}:=\frac{d_{k}}{\lVert d_{k}\rVert}\right\}_{k}\subset X^{\infty}
converges to
h\in X^{\infty}
with
\lVert h\rVert=1
.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, since
\bigg(f_{i_{0}}^{u_{i_{0}}^{k}}\bigg)^{\infty}_{q}
is positively homogeneous of degree one and
\bigg(f_{i_{0}}^{u_{i_{0}}^{k}}\bigg)^{\infty}_{q}(h_{k})\leq 0
, and by using relation (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\bigg(f_{i_{0}}^{u_{i_{0}}^{k}}\bigg)^{\infty}_{q}(h_{k})=(f_{i_{0}})^{\infty}_{q}(h_{k})-\langle u^{k},h_{k}\rangle\leq 0.

Since
f_{i_{0}}
is lsc,
(f_{i_{0}})^{\infty}_{q}
is lsc too by
(
17
, p. 118)
, thus

(f_{i_{0}})^{\infty}_{q}(h)\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}(f_{i_{0}})^{\infty}_{q}(h_{k})\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\,\langle u^{k},h_{k}\rangle=0.

Hence,
h\in X^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{K}_{q}(f_{i_{0}})
with
h\neq 0
, a contradiction.
Therefore, relation (
9
) holds.

Since
X
is convex and
f
is
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
-
\alpha
-robustly quasiconvex, we obtain that the function
f_{u}(x)=f(x)-\langle u,x\rangle
is
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
-quasiconvex for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with
\|u\|<\alpha
. Let
\varepsilon
be satisfied (
9
) and
\varepsilon<\alpha
. Hence,
(b)
follows from relation
(
9
) and Lemma
2
.

The relation (
9
) gives

X^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{K}_{q}(f^{u_{i}}_{i})=\{0\}\ \ \forall u\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},\|u\|<\varepsilon.

Combining this with
(
36
, Proposition 5.4)
yields that
f^{u_{i}}_{i}
is bounded from below on
X
and
(a)
follows.

The proof of part
(c)
is quite similar to the proof of Theorem
4.1
(d), so omitted.

(d)
: Suppose on the contrary that
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(\cdot)
is not usc at
0
. Then, there exist an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(0)\subset U
, sequences
u^{k}
and
x_{k}
such that
u^{k}\rightarrow 0
,
x_{k}\in\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(u^{k})\setminus U
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We consider two case of the sequence
x_{k}
as follows.

Case 1.

\{x_{k}\}
is bounded. Then, without loss of generality we may assume that
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
. By
(c)
, one has
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(0)\subset U
. However, since
x_{k}\notin U
for all
k
and
U
is
open, we see that
\bar{x}\notin U
, a contradiction.

Case 2.

\{x_{k}\}
is unbounded. By passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that
\|x_{k}\|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Hence, without loss of generality one can assume that
\frac{x_{k}}{\|x_{k}\|}\to d\in X^{\infty}
with
\|d\|=1
. Fix any
y\in X
. Analysis similar to that in the proof of Theorem
4.1
(d) shows that there exists
i_{0}\in I
such that

f_{i_{0}}(x_{k})-\langle u_{i_{0}}^{k},x_{k}\rangle\leq f_{i_{0}}(y)-\langle u_{i_{0}}^{k},y\rangle\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(11)

Since
u^{k}\to 0
, there exists
k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|u^{k}\|<\alpha
for all
k\geq k_{1}
. Furthermore, since
\|x_{k}\|\to+\infty
for every
t>0
, there exists
k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
0<\frac{t}{\|x_{k}\|}<1
for all
k\geq k_{2}
.

Since
X
is convex and
f
is
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
-
\alpha
-robustly quasiconvex, by (
11
), we obtain for every
k\geq k_{0}:=\max\{k_{1},k_{2}\}
that

\displaystyle f_{i_{0}}\left(\left(1-\frac{t}{\|x_{k}\|}\right)y+\frac{t}{\|x_{k}\|}x_{k}\right)-

\displaystyle\left\langle u^{k}_{i_{0}},\left(1-\frac{t}{\|x_{k}\|}\right)y+\frac{t}{\|x_{k}\|}x_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{u^{k}_{i_{0}}}_{i_{0}}\left(\left(1-\frac{t}{\|x_{k}\|}\right)y+\frac{t}{\|x_{k}\|}x_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\{f_{i_{0}}^{u^{k}_{i_{0}}}(x_{k}),f_{i_{0}}^{u^{k}_{i_{0}}}(y)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{i_{0}}(y)-\langle u^{k}_{i_{0}},y\rangle.

Letting
k\to\infty
, we have

f_{i_{0}}(y+td)\leq f_{i_{0}}(y)\ \ \forall~t>0,\ \ \forall y\in X,

due to the lower semicontinuity of
f_{i_{0}}
.
This implies that

(f_{i_{0}})^{\infty}_{q}(d)\leq 0
and we therefore get
d\in X^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{K}_{q}(f_{i_{0}})
, contrary to (
8
). The proof is complete.
∎

Furthermore, we also adapt the results for solution stability for the quasiconvex
case below.

Proposition 3

Let
X
be a convex set and
f
be an
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
-
\alpha
-robustly quasiconvex mapping (
\alpha>0
). If the following conditions hold:

(a)

\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(0)
is a singleton;

(b)

X^{\infty}\cap\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}_{q}(f_{i})\right)=\{0\}
;

then
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(\cdot)
is lsc at
0
. Conversely, if
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(\cdot)
is lsc at
0
, then the following condition is satisfied

X^{\infty}\cap\left(\bigcap_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}_{q}(f_{i})\right)=\{0\}.

(12)

Proof

The proof of the first conclusion is quite similar to that of the proof of Theorem
4.2
, so omitted. We now suppose on the contrary that
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(\cdot)
is lsc at
0
but (
12
) does not hold. Then,
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(0)
is nonempty and there exists
d\in X^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
such that

(f_{i})^{\infty}_{q}(d)\leq 0
for every
i\in I
. Let
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(0)
. Clearly,
\bar{x}+td\in X
for all
t>0
. Let
u^{k}:=(\frac{1}{k}d,\ldots,\frac{1}{k}d)
. Then
u^{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
and for each
i\in I
,

(f_{i}^{u^{k}_{i}})^{\infty}_{q}(d)=(f_{i})^{\infty}_{q}(d)-\langle u^{k}_{i},d\rangle=(f_{i})^{\infty}_{q}(d)-\frac{1}{k}\|d\|^{2}<0.

By definition, we have

\sup_{t>0}\frac{f_{i}^{u^{k}_{i}}(\bar{x}+td)-f_{i}^{u^{k}_{i}}(\bar{x})}{t}<0.

This implies that
f_{i}^{u^{k}_{i}}(\bar{x}+td)\to-\infty
as
t\to\infty
for every
i\in I
. This means that for each
z\in X
there exists
t>0
large enough such that

\max_{i\in I}\{f_{i}^{u^{k}_{i}}(\bar{x}+td)-f_{i}^{u^{k}_{i}}(z)\}<0.

It follows that
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(u^{k})=\emptyset
. Hence,
\mbox{\rm Sol}^{w}(\cdot)
is not lsc at
0
, a contradiction. The proof is complete.
∎

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we have used the tool of asymptotic analysis to investigate existence and stability of weak Pareto solutions of constrained nonconvex vector optimization problems. Under the proposed assumption that

X^{\infty}\cap\bigg(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}(f_{i})\bigg)=\{0\},

which is a relation between the asymptotic of objective functions and the asymptotic cone of the constraint set, we have presented new results on existence of weak Pareto solutions, the weak sharp minima at infinity property, the upper/lower semicontinuity, the nonemptiness and compactness, and the closedness of the weak Pareto solution map of the considered problem with linear perturbation. We have used
q
-asymptotic functions to obtain similar results to the particular case when the
objective function is quasiconvex or
\alpha
-robustly
quasiconvex.

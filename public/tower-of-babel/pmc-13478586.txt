One-pot click synthesis of hybrid triazole–pyridine-functionalized polyphenol derivatives under ultrasound irradiation and their antimicrobial assays
Authors: Dorina Amariucai-Mantu, Camelia-Georgiana Marandis, Dumitrela Diaconu, Vasilichia Antoci, Ionel I Mangalagiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the present work, we describe an efficient, straightforward, and environmentally friendly method for synthesizing hybrid triazole-pyridine-functionalized polyphenol derivatives via a one-pot, three-component click reaction under ultrasound irradiation. The reactions are high-yielding (65–90%), require shorter reaction times, use lower solvent volumes, show notable acceleration, and involve lower energy consumption. Taking into account the 12 principles of green chemistry and the above considerations, the one-pot, three-component click reactions under US irradiation may be considered environmentally friendly. The green character of our one-pot three-component click reaction setup procedure under US irradiation is also confirmed by the green chemistry metrics Atom Economy, Environmental factor, and Process Mass Intensity, which have meaningful values in accord with the green chemistry principles. A notable influence of the halogen atom in the click reactions was observed; bromine derivatives are much more suitable for one-pot, three-component click reactions under US irradiation, with a fourfold yield increase. The preliminary antimicrobial assay reveals that some of the hybrid triazole-pyridine-functionalized polyphenol derivatives exhibit very good activity against Gram-positive bacteria (in the nanograms/mL range) and good activity against Gram-negative bacteria and fungi (in the micrograms/mL range).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-54643-5.Keywords: Environmentally friendly click reaction, Ultrasound, One-pot-three component reaction, Hybrid triazole-pyridine-functionalized polyphenols, Antibacterial and antifungal activitySubject terms: Chemistry, Materials science

Introduction

Over the last decades, green chemistry has emerged as a powerful tool in organic chemistry to develop environmentally friendly methods for the synthesis of organic compounds1,2. On the other hand, US assisted reactions have become a successful and viable alternative to conventional TH, mainly because of the significant advantages in terms of the improvement of yields, selectivity, product purities, reduction of reaction time and energy consumption, the possibility of reaching new reactivity, etc3–9. Also, US assisted reactions can provide milder reaction conditions, less or suppressed side reactions, energy saving, and using small amounts of organic solvents or even aqueous media, become environmentally friendly setup procedures1,2,9–15.

Pyridine, triazole and their fused derivatives are structural scaffolds with exciting potential applications in many fields of economic activity, especially in medicine and pharmacy because of their numerous biological activities (antibacterial, antifungal, anti-tuberculosis, antimalarial, anti-HIV, anticancer, anti-inflammatory, anticonvulsant, analgesic, antidepressant, etc.)16–23. On the other hand, polyphenols and their derivatives are natural and synthetic compounds of significant interest in medicine, possessing a large variety of biological and pharmacological activities, which include antioxidant, antimicrobial, antitumoral, anti-inflammatory, antihypertensive, etc24–33. By using the molecular hybridization approach19, it appears logical to combine the biological potential of pyridine, triazole, and polyphenols in order to obtain hybrid compounds with a polyphenol, triazole, and pyridine skeleton with better anticipated biological activity19–22. Click chemistry is a direct and efficient method for constructing triazole rings16,34–37. However, conventional thermal heating (TH) in click reactions often results in lower yields, higher energy consumption, greater solvent usage, etc36,37. Also, compared with the one-pot three-component click reactions, the conventional click multistep reaction approach has the disadvantages of higher energy consumption, higher reaction times, higher amount of used solvent, lower yields etc1,16. As a result, a one-pot three-component click reaction strategy will be better to use instead of the multistep pathway. Moreover, performing these one-pot reactions under ultrasound (US) irradiation may further improve efficiency, as demonstrated in related studies38–40.

In continuation of our concern in the field of environmentally friendly assisted reactions assisted by US41–46 and synthesis of new chemical entities with hybrid functionalized polyphenols - azaheterocycle skeleton46–48, we present herein a thorough study concerning the synthesis of hybrid triazole - pyridine - functionalized polyphenols derivatives via click chemistry, both under US irradiation and conventional TH. We were also interested in evaluating the biological potential of these compounds, especially as antimicrobial agents.

Results and discussion

In the first instance, we have used a multistep reaction pathway to synthesize our newly hybrid triazole - pyridine - functionalized polyphenol derivatives. Initially, we synthesized the key intermediates, 3,5-bis-(halogenomethyl)pyridine acetophenone derivatives 3a, b. In this respect, we performed an O-alkylation reaction of 3,5-dihydroxyacetophenone 1 with 2,6-bis(chloromethyl)pyridine 2a or 2,6-bis(bromomethyl)pyridine 2b, in the presence of potassium carbonate, Scheme 1. Further, the intermediates 3a, b reacts with sodium azide leading to the bis-azide derivative 4, Scheme 1.

Synthesis of 3,5-bis-(halogenomethyl)pyridine acetophenone intermediates 3a, b and bis-azide derivative 4.

In the last step of synthesis, the newly hybrid triazole - pyridine - functionalized polyphenols derivatives 8a-e were obtained by using click chemistry. In this respect, bis-azide 4 was treated with different alkynes [5a-c (with aromatic skeleton), 6 (with heterocycle skeleton), and 7 (with aliphatic chain)]. The click reactions took place in different mixture of solvents t-butanol : water mixture and N,N-dimethylformamide : water mixture, respectively. In both cases, the reactions were catalyzed by Cu (II) using (+)-sodium-(L)-ascorbate as a reducing agent, Scheme 2.

Under conventional TH, click reactions present several disadvantages, including high reaction temperatures (100 °C), elevated energy consumption, prolonged reaction times, and only moderate to good yields .

To overcome these disadvantages, we performed the click reactions under US irradiation (Table 1). Analysis of the data reveals that US irradiation offers clear benefits: a substantial reduction in reaction time (by 5 to 10 times), a moderate increase in yield (by approximately 5–10%), and a significant decrease in energy consumption. Additionally, the choice of solvent influences the reaction outcome; the DMF : water mixture provides higher yields under both conventional and US conditions (by about 3–10%).

For the efficiency improvement of this multistep reaction pathway and to improve the overall parameters for obtaining our hybrid compounds, we decided to use an alternative route for synthesis by using one-pot three-component click reactions. In this respect, 3,5-bis-(halogenomethyl)pyridine acetophenone derivatives (3a, b), sodium azide, various alkynes (5a-c, 6 and 7) and Cu (II) as catalyst, using (+)-sodium-(L)-ascorbate as a reducing agent, in N,N-dimethylformamide: water mixture, react one with each other leading to the hybrid triazole - pyridine - functionalized polyphenols derivatives 8a-e, Scheme 3. In case of one-pot three-component click reactions, we decided to use only N,N-dimethylformamide : water mixture, since the in situ generation of the bis-azide derivative 4 is a bimolecular nucleophilic substitution, which is favored by a polar aprotic solvent (DMF).

Analysis of the data from Table 2 reveals that the use of US irradiation in one-pot three-component click reactions is advantageous, leading to a substantial decrease in reaction time (around 10 times) and consequently a significant decrease in energy consumption, a good increase in yield (with about 5–15%).

We also investigated the effect of the halogen atom (chlorine or bromine) in one-pot three-component click reactions under US irradiation. Regardless of the specific reaction conditions (reaction time, solvent, or US reactor parameters), the nature of the halogen had a significant impact: bromine derivatives were much more suitable, yielding products at approximately four times higher yields than their chlorine counterparts. This may be attributed to the superior leaving group ability of the bromide anion compared to chloride. Comparative analysis of the data from Tables 1 and 2 reveals that the one-pot three-component click reactions are a useful alternative for the synthesis of hybrid azaheterocycles - functionalized polyphenols derivatives, avoiding the disadvantages of the multistep reactions. Overall, compared with the multistep reactions, the one-pot three-component click reactions under US irradiation have higher yields, reduced reaction times, smaller amounts of used solvents, a notable acceleration of reaction, and a significant decrease in the energy consumption. Considering the 12 principles of green chemistry1 and the above mentions, the one-pot three-component click reactions under US irradiation could be considered environmentally friendly, since 7 principles have been fulfilled (atom economy, less hazardous chemical synthesis, safer solvent and auxiliaries, design for energy efficiency, reduce derivatives, catalysis, real-time pollution prevention). Also, according to the green chemistry principles1, Atom Economy (AE), Environmental factor (Ef), and Process Mass Intensity (PMI), are meaningful metrics to express whether a reaction setup procedure is green or not. This is why we decided to calculate these metrics, and we obtained the following results: the atom economy has a value of 82.29%, the environmental factor has a value of 15.149 kg waste / kg product, and process mass intensity has a value of 132.68 kg waste / kg product (for the full calculations, see Supporting Information pages 11 and 12). These results are very good from a green chemistry point of view, and represent another important argument that our one-pot three-component click reaction setup procedure under US irradiation is green.

The enhanced efficiency of US irradiation can be attributed to cavitation phenomena, which improve mass transfer and homogenization of the reaction mixture3,7,9,38. Additionally, since click reactions are catalyzed processes, ultrasound irradiation promotes better dispersion of catalyst particles, increasing the effective reaction surface area.

The structures of compounds were proven using different spectroscopic methods: IR, 1 H- and 13 C-NMR (including two-dimensional experiments 2D- COSY, 2D-HMQC, 2D-HMBC), and HRMS (see Experimental part and Supporting Information part for the 1 H- and 13 C-NMR spectra of compounds).

The antimicrobial activity of the hybrid triazole - pyridine - polyphenols 8c-e was preliminarily tested against Gram-positive bacteria Staphylococcus aureus ATCC 25,923, Gram-negative Escherichia coli ATCC 25,922, and the fungus Candida albicans ATCC 10,231. The Kirby-Bauer disk diffusion method49 was initially used to evaluate the antimicrobial activity (expressed as diameter inhibition zone, mm), followed in the case of the most active compounds, by the standardized broth microdilution assay procedure50–54 to determine the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC, µg/mL).

Conclusions

In conclusion, we present herein a green, efficient, general, and straightforward synthetic route for obtaining hybrid triazole-pyridine-functionalized polyphenol derivatives via one-pot, three-component click reactions under US irradiation. Compared with multistep reactions, these one-pot, three-component click reactions under US irradiation offer several advantages: higher yields, reduced reaction times, smaller solvent use, a notable acceleration of the reaction, and a remarkable decrease in energy consumption. We observed a significant influence of the halogen atom; bromine derivatives are much more suitable for these one-pot, three-component click reactions under US irradiation (reaction yields were approximately 4 (four) times higher). Considering the 12 principles of green chemistry and the above-mentioned points, these one-pot, three-component click reactions under US irradiation could be considered environmentally friendly, since 7 principles have been fulfilled (atom economy, less hazardous chemical synthesis, safer solvents and auxiliaries, design for energy efficiency, reduce derivatives, catalysis, real-time pollution prevention). The green character of our one-pot three-component click reaction setup procedure under US irradiation is also confirmed by the green chemistry metrics Atom Economy (82.29%), Environmental factor (15.149 kg waste / kg product), and Process Mass Intensity (132.68 kg waste / kg product). Finally, we may conclude that these one-pot, three-component click reactions under US irradiation could be a green route for synthesizing hybrid azaheterocycle derivatives. The preliminary antimicrobial assay of the hybrid triazole-pyridine-functionalized polyphenol derivatives was performed using the Kirby-Bauer disk diffusion method and the standardized broth microdilution assay procedure. The obtained results reveal that two hybrids (8d and 8e) exhibit very good antibacterial activity in the nanogram/mL range against Gram-positive bacteria and significant activity in the microgram/mL range against Gram-negative bacteria and fungi.

Experimental details

Apparatus and analysis

The chemicals (solvents and reagents) were acquired from commercial sources, being used without further purification. The MEL-TEMP II apparatus was used for melting point measurements of the compounds, using open capillary tubes. The TLC (analytical thin-layer chromatography) was done using silica gel plates (Merck silica gel 60 F254) and the visualization was performed with UV light (λmax = 254–365 nm). Nuclear magnetic resonance experiments were recorded using a Bruker Avance III 500 MHz spectrometer operating at 500 MHz for 1H, 125 MHz for 13C and 50 MHz for 15N nucleus.

In the NMR spectra, the following abbreviations were used to denote the multiplicity of signals: s = singlet, bs = broad singlet, as = apparent singlet, d = doublet, ad = apparent doublet, t = triplet, at = apparent triplet, m = multiplet. Chemical shifts were reported in delta units (δ / ppm), relative to the residual solvent peak (ref: CDCl3/DMSO-d6, 1H: 7.26/2.50 ppm; 13C: 77.16/39.52 ppm) and coupling constants (J) in Hz. The infrared (IR) spectra were recorded using a spectrometer with ATR module (Agilent Cary 630 FT-IR). HR-MS experiments were recorded using a HESI-OrbitrapExploris 120 Mass Spectrometer (Thermo Fisher, Walthan, USA) in positive mode.

The reactions under ultrasound irradiation were carried out using Sonics reactor (Sonics VCX-130, USA), having a nominal power of 130 W and a frequency of 20 kHz. The titanium standard probe tip, with a diameter of 6 mm and a length of 116 mm, fixed firmly to the ultrasonic converter, was dipped in the used solvent.

General procedure for O-alkylation under conventional TH

To a solution of bis-(halogenomethyl)pyridine derivatives 2a (10 mmol) or 2b (5 mmol) in 100 mL acetonitrile, K2CO3 was added (10 mmol, respectively 5 mmol). Over the solution brought to reflux, was added dropwise a solution consisting of 3,5-dihydroxyacetophenone 1 (3 mmol, respectively 1.5 mmol) in 40 mL acetonitrile. The mixture was refluxed for 2 h and then stirred at room temperature for 12 h. The evolution of the reaction was followed by thin layer chromatography (TLC) using CH2Cl2 : CH3OH (5 mL : 0.1 mL) as eluents. After the completion of the reaction, the precipitate was filtered off and the solution was concentrated using a rotary evaporator. Purification was performed by column chromatography, using CH2Cl2 and CH2Cl2:CH3OH mixture (100, 99: 1) (v/v) as eluents, to give the desired compounds 3a, b.

General procedure for the synthesis of bis-azide derivative 4 under conventional TH

To a solution of 1-(3,5-bis((6-(chloromethyl)pyridin-2-yl)methoxy)phenyl)ethan-1-one 3a (0.5 mmol) in 20 mL N,N-dimethylformamide, NaN3 was added (5 mmol). The mixture of reaction was heated at 80 °C for 48 h. The completion of the reaction was achieved using thin layer chromatography, using CH2Cl2 : CH3OH (5 mL : 0.1 mL) as eluents. The processing of the synthesis consisted of the concentration of the solution to dryness, adding 50 mL of distilled water, followed by extraction with 50 mL of ethyl acetate (twice). The organic phase, after separation, was dried on anhydrous Na2SO4, followed by filtration and concentration to a rotary evaporator. The residue was triturated with diethyl ether to give the desired compound.

General procedure for click reactions under conventional TH and US irradiation

Conventional TH (1) / US irradiation (1)

To a solution of 3 mL / 5 mL t-butanol (TH-1 / US-1) and different alkynes 5a (1-ethynyl-4-methylbenzene, 2 mmol), 5b (1-ethynyl-4-methoxybenzene, 2 mmol), 6 (4-(prop-2-yn-1-yl)morpholine, 2 mmol), 7 (N,N-diethylprop-2-yn-1-amine, 2 mmol) or a suspension of the same solvent with 5c (1-ethynyl-4-nitrobenzene, 2 mmol), was added (under stirring for TH-1 / without stirring for US-1), CuSO4 × 5H2O (0.2 mmol) and 1.5 mL / 2.5 mL distilled water (TH-1 / US-1). To this mixture was added the bis-azide derivative 4, then a solution of (+)-sodium-(L)-ascorbate (0.4 mmol) in 1.5 mL / 2.5 mL of distilled water (TH-1 / US-1) was added, dropwise.

In case of conventional TH-1 the reaction mixture was heated for 1 h at a temperature of 100 °C.

In case of US-1 the mixtures were placed in a reaction vessel and were exposed to US irradiation. The best results were obtained by applying a pulse irradiation (5 s pulse / 5 s pause, 90% from the full power of the generator) for 12 min.

After the completion of the reactions, both under conventional TH-1 and US-1 irradiation (visualization was done through TLC with CH2Cl2 : CH3OH (10 mL : 0.2 mL) as eluents), in the case of using the alkynes 5a, 5b, 6, 7, the solution was partially concentrated, then 20 mL of chloroform was added. The solution was washed with a solution of NH3 25% (2 × 5 mL) and after separation, the organic phase was washed with distilled water (2 × 20 mL). The chloroform solution was separated and dried on anhydrous Na2SO4. The solvent was removed under vacuum and the residue was triturated with methanol to give the desired compounds 8a, 8b, 8d, while compound 8e was obtained as an oil.

In the case of using the alkyne 5c, after the completion of the reaction, the obtained precipitate was filtered under vacuum and washed with a solution of NH3 25% (3 × 5 mL) and then with distilled water (2 × 20 mL) and the obtained compound 8c was then dried.

Conventional TH (2) / US irradiation (2)

To a solution of 3 mL / 4 mL N,N-dimethylformamide (TH-2 / US-2) and different alkynes 5a (1-ethynyl-4-methylbenzene, 2 mmol), 5b (1-ethynyl-4-methoxybenzene, 2 mmol), 6 (4-(prop-2-yn-1-yl)morpholine, 2 mmol), 7 (N,N-diethylprop-2-yn-1-amine, 2 mmol) or a suspension of the same solvent with 5c (1-ethynyl-4-nitrobenzene, 2 mmol), was added CuSO4 × 5H2O (0.2 mmol) and 1.5 mL / 3 mL distilled water (TH-2 / US-2), under stirring in case of TH-2 and without stirring for US-2. To this mixture, the bis-azide derivative 4 was added, then a solution of (+)-sodium-(L)-ascorbate (0.4 mmol) in 1.5 mL / 3 mL distilled water (TH-2 / US-2), dropwise.

In case of conventional TH-2 the reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature for 3 h. In case of US-2 the mixtures were placed in a reaction vessel and were exposed to US irradiation. The best results were obtained by applying a pulse irradiation (5 s pulse / 5 s pause, 90% of the full power of the generator) for 18 min.

The completion of the reactions was monitored using TLC with CH2Cl2 : CH3OH (10 mL : 0.2 mL) as eluents. In both cases TH-2 and US-2 the obtained precipitates were filtered under vacuum and washed with a solution of NH3 25% (3 × 5 mL), then with distilled water (2 × 20 mL) and the desired compounds 8a-d were then dried. In the case of using the alkyne 7, the solution was partially concentrated, then was added 20 mL of chloroform. The solution was washed with a solution of NH3 25% (2 × 5 mL) and after separation, the organic phase was washed with distilled water (2 × 20 mL). The chloroform solution was separated and dried on anhydrous Na2SO4. The solvent was removed under vacuum and the desired compound 8e was obtained as an oil.

General procedure for click one-pot synthesis under conventional TH and US irradiation

One-pot three-component synthesis under conventional TH / US irradiation: Over a solution consisting of 3 mL / 4 mL N,N-dimethylformamide (one-pot TH / one-pot US) and bis-halogeno derivative 3a or 3b (1mmol), was added NaN3 (4 mmol). Then, after 10 min of stirring (in case of one-pot TH), was added 2 mmol of different alkynes 5a (1-ethynyl-4-methylbenzene), 5b (1-ethynyl-4-methoxybenzene), 5c (1-ethynyl-4-nitrobenzene, 2 mmol), 6 (4-(prop-2-yn-1-yl)morpholine), or 7 (N,N-diethylprop-2-yn-1-amine). The addition of alkynes in the case of one-pot US reactions was carried out immediately after the addition of the sodium azide. To this mixture was added under/without stirring (in case of one-pot TH / in case of one-pot US), a solution of CuSO4 × 5H2O (0.4 mmol in 2 mL / 3 mL distilled water (one-pot TH / one-pot US)) and then a solution of (+)-sodium-(L)-ascorbate (0.8 mmol in 2.5 mL / 3 mL distilled water (one-pot TH / one-pot US)) was added dropwise.

In case of one-pot TH the reaction mixtures were stirred at room temperature for 3 h. In case of one-pot US the mixtures were placed in a reaction vessel and were exposed to US irradiation. The best results were obtained by applying a pulse irradiation (5 s pulse / 5 s pause, 90% from the full power of the generator) for 18 min.

After the completion of the reactions, both under one-pot TH and US irradiation [visualization was done through TLC with CH2Cl2 : CH3OH (10 mL : 0.2 mL) as eluents], the obtained precipitates were filtered under vacuum and washed with a solution of NH3 25% (3 × 5 mL) and then with distilled water (2 × 20 mL) to offered the desired compounds 8a-d which were then dried. In the case of using the alkyne 7, the solution was partially concentrated, then 20 mL of chloroform was added. The solution was washed with a solution of NH3 25% (2 × 5 mL) and after separation, the organic phase was washed with distilled water (2 × 20 mL).

Asymptotic results for the distributional fractional Stockwell and fractional wavelet transforms
Snježana Maksimović

Abstract
. We provide some Abelian and Tauberian results characterizing the quasiasymptotic behavior of Lizorkin distributions in terms of their Stockwell transform. We prove the continuity of the fractional wavelet transform and the corresponding synthesis operator on the Schwartz spaces and their duals, respectively. Additionally, we establish a connection between fractional Stockwell and fractional wavelet transforms and provide some asymptotic results for the distributional fractional wavelet transform.

Keywords: Fractional Stockwell transform, fractional wavelet transform, distributions, Abelian and Tauberian theorems

1.
Introduction

It is well known that distributions do not have point values in the general case, which imposes the idea of absorbing the asymptotic analysis into the field of generalized functions.
The natural generalization of classical asymptotics in the framework of Schwartz distributions is the quasiasymptotic behavior (quasiasymptotics) introduced in
[
26
]
.
It is an old mathematical tool that has found applications in various fields of pure and applied mathematics, physics, and engineering. Many results involve the analysis of the behavior of distributions through various integral transforms and Abelian and Tauberian theory (cf.
[
7
,
8
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
25
]
).

In the last two decades, fractional transforms have played an important role in several areas, including signal and image processing, optics, geo-informatics, radar signal and communications, bio-medical, etc. (cf.
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
12
,
28
,
14
,
21
]
).
The fractional Fourier transform (FRFT), first introduced in
[
12
]
, is a generalization of the Fourier transform (FT) in which the FT kernel is substituted with the fractional kernel
K_{\alpha}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)K_{\alpha}(x,\xi)dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R},

where

(1.1)

K_{\alpha}(x,\xi)=\begin{cases}C_{\alpha}e^{i(\frac{x^{2}+\xi^{2}}{2}c_{1}-x\xi c_{2})},&\text{ $\alpha\neq n\pi$}\\
\ \ \ \delta(x-\xi),&\text{ $\alpha=2n\pi$}\\
\ \ \ \delta(x+\xi),&\text{ $\alpha=(2n+1)\pi$}\\
\end{cases}

c_{1}=\cot\alpha,\ c_{2}=\csc\alpha
,
C_{\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{1-ic_{1}}{2\pi}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The kernel
K_{\alpha}(x,\xi)
is an infinitely differentiable function in both
x
and
\xi
.
The fractional short-time Fourier transform (FRSTFT), as a generalization of the short-time Fourier transform (STFT), is proposed to solve the problem of the FRFT when failing to locate the fractional Fourier domain frequency contents (cf.
[
4
]
). The fractional wavelet transform (FRWT), first introduced in
[
11
]
(see also
[
21
]
), is a generalization of the wavelet transform (WT). This transform is proposed to rectify the limitations of the WT and the FRFT. The generalization of the Stockwell transform (ST) is the fractional Stockwell transform (FRST). The FRST is first introduced in
[
18
]
to deal with seismic data and improve the deficiencies of the STFT and WT (cf.
[
1
,
18
]
). Since then, the FRST has played an important role in signal and image processing, and has become the most popular multiresolution tool (cf.
[
27
,
10
,
5
]
).

In this paper, we focus on the FRST and FRWT introduced in
[
21
]
. The paper is divided into two parts. The first part (Section
3
) is a continuation of the work by
[
9
]
, where several Abelian and Tauberian-type results connecting the quasiasymptotic behavior of Lizorkin distributions to the asymptotic behavior of their FRST are provided. In the second part (Section
4
), we establish the relation between the distributional FRST and FRWT. Additionally, continuity theorems for the FRWT (Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
) are given. By using these continuity theorems, we demonstrate that both transforms, defined as transposed mappings and as actions on appropriate window functions, coincide on the Schwartz space of tempered distributions. Moreover, we present some results regarding the asymptotic behavior of distributional FRWT, utilizing the obtained asymptotic results for the FRST.

3.
Abelian and Tauberian results for the fractional Stockwell transform

3.1.
The FRST

Let
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)dx=1
. The FRST of the signal
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by (cf.
[
9
]
)

S^{\alpha}_{g}f(x,\xi)=|\xi|\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{g(\xi(t-x))}K_{\alpha}(t,\xi)dt,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\xi\in\mathbb{R}\setminus{\{0\}},

where
K_{\alpha}(t,\xi)
is the fractional kernel defined by (
1.1
).
We shall always assume that
\alpha\in[0,2\pi)
. If
\alpha=\pi/2
, the FRST corresponds to
the ST (cf.
[
20
]
)

S_{g}f(x,\xi)=\frac{|\xi|}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{g(\xi(t-x))}e^{-i\xi t}dt,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\xi\in\mathbb{R}\setminus{\{0\}},

and the FRST with
\alpha=0
corresponds to the
zero operator.

Let
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be a non-trivial and let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be a reconstruction
window for
g
. If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is such that
\hat{f}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then the following reconstruction formula
holds pointwise

(3.1)

f(t)=\frac{|\sin\alpha|}{C_{g,\psi,c_{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}S_{g}^{\alpha}f(x,\xi)\psi(\xi(t-x))K_{-\alpha}(t,\xi)dxd\xi,

where
C_{g,\psi,c_{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\psi}(c_{2}(\omega-1))\overline{\hat{g}}(c_{2}(\omega-1))\frac{d\omega}{|\omega|}<\infty
(cf.
[
9
]
). Using the reconstruction formula (
3.1
), the fractional Stockwell synthesis operator was introduced in
[
9
]
as follows:

(S_{g}^{\alpha})^{\ast}F(t)=|\sin\alpha|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(x,\xi)g(\xi(t-x))K_{-\alpha}(t,\xi)dxd\xi.

Thus, the relation (
3.1
) takes the form

((S_{\psi}^{\alpha})^{*}\circ S_{g}^{\alpha})f=C_{g,\psi,c_{2}}f.

It is proven in
[
9
]
that the mappings
S_{g}^{\alpha}:\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Y})
and
(S_{g}^{\alpha})^{*}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Y})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
are continuous, for
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
. Additionally, the mappings
S_{g}^{\alpha}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Y})
and
(S_{g}^{\alpha})^{*}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Y})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{\prime})
are continuous, and the reconstruction formula (
3.1
) is generalized to the Lizorkin distributions, as presented in
[
9
]
.

In
[
9
]
, several Abelian and Tauberian-type results characterizing the quasiasymptotic behavior of Lizorkin distributions in terms of their FRST are proven. In order to obtain the quasiasymptotic behavior of the distributional FRWT at the origin, we use the following result from
[
9
]
: If
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
has the quasiasymptotic behavior at the origin, then for its FRST holds

(3.2)

e^{-ic_{1}(\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon})^{2}/2}S_{g}^{\alpha}f(\varepsilon x,\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon})\sim\frac{{\sqrt{1-ic_{1}}}}{c_{2}^{m}}{\varepsilon^{m}L(\varepsilon)}S_{g}u(xc_{2},\frac{\xi}{c_{2}})\ \ \text{ as }\ \ \varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}\ \ \text{ in }\ \mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Y}).

3.2.
Abelian and Tauberian results for the FRST

Our main goal in this section is to present some new Abelian and Tauberian-type results relating asymptotics of FRST and the quasiasymptotic
behavior of Lizorkin distributions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
has the following quasiasymptotic behavior

(3.3)

f(\varepsilon x)\sim{\varepsilon^{m}L(\varepsilon)}u(x)\ \ \text{ as }\ \ \varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}\ \ \text{ in }\ \mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}),

where
m\in\mathbb{R}
, and
L
is a slowly varying function at 0 and
u\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, for its FRST with respect to the window
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
, we have

e^{-ic_{1}({\varepsilon}\xi)^{2}/2}S_{g_{1/\varepsilon^{2}}}^{\alpha}f(\varepsilon x,{\varepsilon}\xi)\sim\frac{\sqrt{1-ic_{1}}}{c_{2}^{m}}{\varepsilon^{m+2}L(\varepsilon)}S_{g}(M_{\xi/c_{2}}u)(xc_{2},\frac{\xi}{c_{2}})\ \ \text{ as }\ \ \varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}\ \ \text{ in }\ \mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Y}),

whenever
\alpha\in(2k\pi,(2k+1)\pi),k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{Y}
be fixed. Then, we have

\displaystyle e^{-ic_{1}({\varepsilon}\xi)^{2}/2}S_{g_{1/\varepsilon^{2}}}^{\alpha}f(\varepsilon x,{\varepsilon}\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{-ic_{1}({\varepsilon}\xi)^{2}/2}C_{\alpha}{|\varepsilon\xi|}\langle f(t),{\overline{g}\big(\frac{\xi(t-\varepsilon x)}{\varepsilon}\big)e^{ic_{1}\frac{t^{2}+(\varepsilon\xi)^{2}}{2}-ic_{2}t\varepsilon\xi}}\rangle

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}{|\varepsilon^{2}\xi|}\langle e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon t)^{2}/2}f(\varepsilon t),{\overline{g}\big({\xi(t-x)}\big)e^{-ic_{2}t\varepsilon^{2}\xi}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\alpha}}{c_{2}}{|\varepsilon^{2}\xi|}\langle e^{ic_{1}(\frac{\varepsilon t}{c_{2}})^{2}/2}f(\frac{\varepsilon t}{c_{2}}),{\overline{g}\big({\xi(\frac{t}{c_{2}}-x)}\big)e^{-it\varepsilon^{2}\xi}}\rangle

and

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{e^{-ic_{1}({\varepsilon}\xi)^{2}/2}S_{g_{1/\varepsilon^{2}}}^{\alpha}f(\varepsilon x,{\varepsilon}\xi)}{\varepsilon^{m+2}L(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\alpha}}{c_{2}}{|\xi|}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\langle e^{ic_{1}(\frac{\varepsilon t}{c_{2}})^{2}/2}f(\frac{\varepsilon t}{c_{2}}),{\overline{g}\big({\xi(\frac{t}{c_{2}}-x)}\big)e^{-it\varepsilon^{2}\xi}}\rangle}{\varepsilon^{m}L(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\alpha}}{c^{m+1}_{2}}{|\xi|}\lim_{(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\rightarrow(0^{+},0^{+})}\frac{\langle e^{ic_{1}(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}t}{c_{2}})^{2}/2}f(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}t}{c_{2}}),{\overline{g}\big({\xi(\frac{t}{c_{2}}-x)}\big)e^{-it\varepsilon^{2}_{2}\xi}}\rangle}{(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c_{2}})^{m}L(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c_{2}})\frac{L(\varepsilon_{1})}{L(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c_{2}})}}.

Since the weak and the strong topology on
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
are equivalent, using (
2.1
) and Lemma
2.1
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon_{1}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\langle e^{ic_{1}(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}t}{c_{2}})^{2}/2}f(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}t}{c_{2}}),{\overline{g}\big({\xi(\frac{t}{c_{2}}-x)}\big)e^{-it\varepsilon^{2}_{2}\xi}}\rangle}{(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c_{2}})^{m}L(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c_{2}})\frac{L(\varepsilon_{1})}{L(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c_{2}})}}=\langle u(t),{\overline{g}\big({\xi(\frac{t}{c_{2}}-x)}\big)e^{-it\varepsilon^{2}_{2}\xi}}\rangle,

uniformly for
\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1]
. Furthermore, for each
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1]
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon_{2}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\langle e^{ic_{1}(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}t}{c_{2}})^{2}/2}f(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}t}{c_{2}}),{\overline{g}\big({\xi(\frac{t}{c_{2}}-x)}\big)e^{-it\varepsilon^{2}_{2}\xi}}\rangle}{(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c_{2}})^{m}L(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c_{2}})\frac{L(\varepsilon_{1})}{L(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c_{2}})}}=\frac{\langle e^{ic_{1}(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}t}{c_{2}})^{2}/2}f(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}t}{c_{2}}),{\overline{g}\big({\xi(\frac{t}{c_{2}}-x)}\big)}\rangle}{(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c_{2}})^{m}L(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c_{2}})\frac{L(\varepsilon_{1})}{L(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c_{2}})}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{e^{-ic_{1}({\varepsilon}\xi)^{2}/2}S_{g_{1/\varepsilon^{2}}}^{\alpha}f(\varepsilon x,{\varepsilon}\xi)}{\varepsilon^{m+2}L(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\alpha}}{c^{m+1}_{2}}{|\xi|}\langle u(t),{\overline{g}\big({\xi(\frac{t}{c_{2}}-x)}\big)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{1-ic_{1}}}{c^{m}_{2}}S_{g}(M_{\xi/c_{2}}u)(c_{2}x,\frac{\xi}{c_{2}}).

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
L

,

) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm lim}}\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\langle\frac{e^{i{c_{1}(\varepsilon t)^{2}}/{2}}f(\varepsilon t)}{\varepsilon^{m}L(\varepsilon)},\varphi(t)\rangle=\mathop{{\rm lim}}\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\langle\frac{e^{ic_{1}t^{2}/{2}}f(t)}{\varepsilon^{m+1}L(\varepsilon)},\varphi(\frac{t}{\varepsilon})\rangle

4.
Fractional wavelet transform and quasyasimptotic of distributions

4.1.
Continuity theorems for the FRWT of distributions

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be a wavelet, i.e., let it satisfy
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)dx=0
.
The FRWT of the signal
{\displaystyle f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
with respect to the wavelet
g
is defined as (cf.
[
21
]
)

W_{g}^{\alpha}f(x,\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{g}\left(\frac{t-x}{\xi}\right)e^{ic_{1}\frac{t^{2}-x^{2}}{2}}dt,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

If
\alpha=\pi/2
, the FRWT corresponds to
the WT

W_{g}f(x,\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{g}\left(\frac{t-x}{\xi}\right)dt,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

The FRWT can be expressed in terms of the FRFT, i.e.,

(4.1)

W_{g}^{\alpha}f(x,\xi)=\sqrt{2\pi\xi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}f(t)\hat{g}(c_{1}t\xi)K_{-\alpha}(t,x)dt,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be a mother wavelet. If
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then the following reconstruction formula
holds pointwise (cf.
[
21
]
)

(4.2)

f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi C_{g}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}W_{g}^{\alpha}f(x,\xi)g\left(\frac{t-x}{\xi}\right)e^{-ic_{1}\frac{t^{2}-x^{2}}{2}}\frac{dxd\xi}{\xi^{2}},

where
C_{g}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|{\hat{g}}(\omega)|^{2}\frac{d\omega}{|\omega|}<\infty.
From the reconstruction formula (
4.2
), the fractional wavelet synthesis operator is defined by

(W_{g}^{\alpha})F(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x,\xi)g\left(\frac{t-x}{\xi}\right)e^{-ic_{1}\frac{t^{2}-x^{2}}{2}}\frac{dxd\xi}{\xi^{2}}.

Now, we extend the results of
[
21
]
for the Schwartz spaces and their duals.

Theorem 4.1
.

The mapping
W_{g}^{\alpha}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})
is continuous.

Proof.

Since the FRFT is a continuous mapping from
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
onto itself (cf.
[
14
]
), using (
4.1
), we have that the following mappings are continuous:

f\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}f(t)\mapsto h(t,\xi)=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}f(t)\hat{g}(c_{1}t\xi)\mapsto\sqrt{2\pi\xi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(t,\xi)K_{-\alpha}(t,x)dt,\ \ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\mapsto\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\mapsto\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})\mapsto\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}).

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

The mapping
(W_{g}^{\alpha})^{*}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
is continuous.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and
F\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})
. We will show that for given
k,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exist
a,b,m,p,s,r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\rho_{k,n}((S_{g}^{\alpha})^{\ast})\lesssim_{\alpha}\rho_{a,b}(g)\sigma^{m,p}_{s,r}(F).

Notice that the
n^{th}
derivative of the function
\xi\mapsto e^{i\frac{\xi^{2}c_{1}}{2}}
has the form
e^{i\frac{\xi^{2}c_{1}}{2}}P(\xi)
, where
P(\xi)
is a polynomial with the leading term
i^{n}c_{1}^{n}\xi^{n}
. So, it holds that

\big|\partial^{n}_{\xi}\big(e^{i\frac{\xi^{2}c_{1}}{2}}\big)\big|\leq nC\big|c_{1}^{n}\xi^{n}e^{i\frac{\xi^{2}c_{1}}{2}}\big|,

where
C
is a positive constant. Then, we have

\displaystyle|t^{k}\partial_{t}^{n}((W^{\alpha}_{g})^{\ast}F(t))|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n_{1}+n_{2}=n}\binom{n}{n_{1},n_{2}}|(c_{1})^{n_{2}}|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}|t^{k+n_{2}}\xi^{-n_{1}-2}F(x,\xi)g^{(n_{1})}(\frac{t-x}{\xi})e^{-ic_{1}\frac{t^{2}-x^{2}}{2}}dxd\xi|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\sum_{n_{1}+n_{2}=n}\binom{n}{n_{1},n_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}|\xi^{k+n_{2}-n_{1}-2}x^{k+n_{2}}F(x,\xi)\left(\frac{t-x}{\xi}\right)^{k+n_{2}}g^{(n_{1})}(\frac{t-x}{\xi})e^{-ic_{1}\frac{t^{2}-x^{2}}{2}}dxd\xi|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\sum_{n_{1}+n_{2}=n}\binom{n}{n_{1},n_{2}}\sigma_{k,k+n_{2}}^{0,0}(F)\rho_{k+n_{2},n_{1}}(g),

from which we obtain the proof.
∎

By the use of Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
, we can define the FRWT of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
as the element
W^{\alpha}_{g}f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})
whose action on test functions is given by:

(4.3)

\langle W^{\alpha}_{g}f,\Phi\rangle=\langle f,(W_{\overline{g}}^{\alpha})^{\ast}\Phi\rangle,\qquad\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}).

Then, the fractional wavelet synthesis operator
(W_{g}^{\alpha})^{*}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
can be defined as

(4.4)

\langle(W_{g}^{\alpha})^{*}F,\phi\rangle=\langle F,W^{\alpha}_{\overline{g}}\phi\rangle,\quad F\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}),\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}).

We obtain immediately:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. The FRWT
W^{\alpha}_{g}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})
and the synthesis operator
(W_{g}^{\alpha})^{*}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
are continuous
linear maps.

Now, we can extend the reconstruction formula (
4.2
) to the space of tempered distributions.

Theorem 4.4
.

(Inversion formula) Let
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, then

\textbf{Id}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})}=\frac{1}{2\pi C_{g}}((W_{g}^{\alpha})^{\ast}\circ W^{\alpha}_{g}).

Proof.

Using (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and reconstruction formula (
4.2
), we obtain

\langle(W_{g}^{\alpha})^{\ast}\circ(W_{g}^{\alpha}f),\phi\rangle=\langle f,(W_{\overline{g}}^{\alpha})^{\ast}\circ(W_{\overline{g}}^{\alpha}\phi)\rangle=2\pi C_{g}\langle f,\phi\rangle,\quad\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}).

∎

According to the Hahn-Banach theorem, the wavelet transform
can be extended over
\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}
.

4.2.
Abelian results for the FRWT

In this section, we present some Abelian-type results for the FRWT, using the asymptotic results for the FRST. First, we will connect the FRST and FRWT. For
{\displaystyle f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
and
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, by the definitions of FRWT and FRST, one can easily obtain the following relation:

(4.5)

e^{-ic_{1}\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}}S^{\alpha}_{g}f(x,\xi)=\sqrt{\xi}{C_{\alpha}}e^{ic_{1}\frac{x^{2}}{2}-ic_{2}x\xi}W_{M_{c_{2}}g}^{\alpha}f(x,\frac{1}{\xi}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, the function
M_{c_{2}}g
is also wavelet in
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

The proof follows from
[
3
, Lemma 5.1]

∎

We want to prove that the relation (
4.5
) also holds for distributional FRST and FRWT.
Using (
3.1
), (
4.1
) and Lemma
4.5
, we obtain:

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, then (
4.5
) holds.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
has the quasiasymptotic behavior (
3.3
). Then, for its FRWT with respect to the window
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
, we have

{e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon x)^{2}/2}W_{M_{c_{2}}g}^{\alpha}f(\varepsilon x,\frac{\varepsilon}{\xi})}\sim\frac{\varepsilon^{m+1/2}}{c_{2}^{m+1/2}}L(\varepsilon)e^{ix\xi(c_{2}-1)}W_{M_{1}g}u(xc_{2},\frac{c_{2}}{\xi})\ \ \text{ as }\ \ \varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}

\text{ in }\ \mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})
whenever
\alpha\in(2k\pi,(2k+1/2)\pi),k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Using Proposition
4.6
and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon x)^{2}/2}W_{M_{c_{2}}g}^{\alpha}f(\varepsilon x,\frac{\varepsilon}{\xi})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\xi}}\frac{1}{C_{\alpha}}e^{ic_{2}x\xi}e^{-ic_{1}\left(\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}/2}S^{\alpha}_{g}f(x\varepsilon,\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\sim\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\xi}}\frac{\varepsilon^{m}}{c_{2}^{m}}L(\varepsilon)e^{ic_{2}x\xi}S_{g}u(xc_{2},\frac{\xi}{c_{2}})\text{ as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Since

\displaystyle S_{g}u(xc_{2},\frac{\xi}{c_{2}})

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\xi}{c_{2}}\right|\frac{e^{-ix\xi}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t)\overline{e^{i\frac{\xi}{c_{2}}(t-xc_{2})}g(\frac{\xi}{c_{2}}(t-xc_{2}))}dt

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\xi}{c_{2}}}\frac{e^{-ix\xi}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}W_{M_{1}g}u(xc_{2},\frac{c_{2}}{\xi}),

we obtain

e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon x)^{2}/2}W_{M_{c_{2}}g}^{\alpha}f(\varepsilon x,\frac{\varepsilon}{\xi})\sim\frac{\varepsilon^{m+1/2}}{c_{2}^{m+1/2}}L(\varepsilon)e^{i(c_{2}-1)x\xi}W_{M_{1}g}(xc_{2},\frac{c_{2}}{\xi}),\ \ \text{ as }\ \varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
has the quasiasymptotic behavior (
3.3
). Then, for its FRWT with respect to the window
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
, we have

{e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon x)^{2}/2-ic_{2}\varepsilon^{2}x\xi}W_{M_{c_{2}}g_{1/\varepsilon^{2}}}^{\alpha}f(\varepsilon x,\frac{1}{\varepsilon\xi})}\sim\frac{\varepsilon^{m+3/2}}{c_{2}^{m+1/2}}L(\varepsilon)e^{ix\xi(c_{2}-1)}W_{g}u(xc_{2},\frac{c_{2}}{\xi})\ \ \text{ as }\ \ \varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}

\text{ in }\ \mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})
whenever
\alpha\in(2k\pi,(2k+1/2)\pi),k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Using Proposition
4.6
and Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon x)^{2}/2-ic_{2}\varepsilon^{2}x\xi}W_{M_{c_{2}}g_{1/\varepsilon^{2}}}^{\alpha}f(\varepsilon x,\frac{1}{\varepsilon\xi})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C_{\alpha}\sqrt{\varepsilon\xi}}e^{-ic_{1}(\varepsilon\xi)^{2}/2}S^{\alpha}_{g_{1/\varepsilon^{2}}}f(\varepsilon x,\varepsilon\xi)

\displaystyle\sim\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{\varepsilon^{m+2}}{c_{2}^{m}\sqrt{\varepsilon\xi}}L(\varepsilon)S_{g}(M_{\xi/c_{2}}u)(xc_{2},\frac{\xi}{c_{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon^{m+3/2}}{c_{2}^{m+1/2}}L(\varepsilon)W_{g}f(xc_{2},\frac{c_{2}}{\xi}).

∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
has the quasiasymptotic behavior (
3.3
). Then, for its FRST with respect to the window
g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
, we have

e^{-ic_{1}(\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon})^{2}/2}S_{g}^{\alpha}f(\varepsilon^{2}x,\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon})\sim C_{\alpha}\sqrt{\xi}{\varepsilon^{m}}L(\varepsilon)W_{g}(M_{-\xi c_{2}}u)(0,\frac{1}{\xi})\ \ \text{ as }\ \ \varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}

\text{ in }\ \mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})
whenever
\alpha\in(2k\pi,(2k+1)\pi),k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have

\displaystyle e^{-ic_{1}(\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon})^{2}/2}S^{\alpha}_{g}f(\varepsilon^{2}x,\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{|\xi|}{\varepsilon}C_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{g}(\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}(t-\varepsilon^{2}x))e^{ic_{1}t^{2}/2-ic_{2}t\xi/\varepsilon}dt

\displaystyle=|\xi|C_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon t)^{2}/2}f(\varepsilon t)\overline{g}({\xi}(t-\varepsilon x))e^{-ic_{2}t\xi}dt

\displaystyle=|\xi|C_{\alpha}\langle e^{-ic_{2}t\xi}e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon t)^{2}/2}f(\varepsilon t),\overline{g}({\xi}(t-\varepsilon x))\rangle

and

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{e^{-ic_{1}(\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon})^{2}/2}S^{\alpha}_{g}f(\varepsilon^{2}x,\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon^{m}L(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=|\xi|C_{\alpha}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\langle e^{-ic_{2}t\xi}e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon t)^{2}/2}f(\varepsilon t),\overline{g}({\xi}(t-\varepsilon x))\rangle}{\varepsilon^{m}L(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=|\xi|C_{\alpha}\lim_{(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\rightarrow(0^{+},0^{+})}\langle\frac{e^{-ic_{2}t\xi}e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon_{1}t)^{2}/2}f(\varepsilon t)}{\varepsilon_{1}^{m}L(\varepsilon_{1})},\overline{g}({\xi}(t-\varepsilon_{2}x))\rangle.

Since the weak and the strong topology on
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
are equivalent, using (
2.1
) and Lemma
2.1
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon_{1}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\langle\frac{e^{-ic_{2}t\xi}e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon_{1}t)^{2}/2}f(\varepsilon t)}{\varepsilon_{1}^{m}L(\varepsilon_{1})},\overline{g}({\xi}(t-\varepsilon_{2}x))\rangle=\langle e^{-ic_{2}t\xi}u(t),\overline{g}({\xi}(t-\varepsilon_{2}x))\rangle

uniformly for
\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1]
. Furthermore, for each
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1]
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon_{2}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\langle\frac{e^{-ic_{2}t\xi}e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon_{1}t)^{2}/2}f(\varepsilon t)}{\varepsilon_{1}^{m}L(\varepsilon_{1})},\overline{g}({\xi}(t-\varepsilon_{2}x))\rangle=\langle\frac{e^{-ic_{2}t\xi}e^{ic_{1}(\varepsilon_{1}t)^{2}/2}f(\varepsilon t)}{\varepsilon_{1}^{m}L(\varepsilon_{1})},\overline{g}({\xi}t)\rangle.

Thus

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{e^{-ic_{1}(\varepsilon\xi)^{2}/2}S^{\alpha}_{g}f(\varepsilon^{2}x,\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon^{m}L(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=|\xi|C_{\alpha}\langle e^{-ic_{2}t\xi}u(t),\overline{g}({\xi}t)\rangle

\displaystyle=|\xi|C_{\alpha}\langle M_{-c_{2}\xi}u(t),\overline{g}({\xi}t)\rangle=C_{\alpha}\sqrt{\xi}W_{g}(M_{-c_{2}\xi}u)(0,\frac{1}{\xi}).

∎

On the conformal preimage decay exponent of the Julia sets of rational graph-directed Markov systems
Tadashi Arimitsu

Abstract
. We define and investigate the conformal preimage decay exponent of the Julia sets of rational graph-directed Markov systems. We show that this exponent coincides with the difference between the topological entropy and upper sequential capacity topological pressure for the rational skew product map associated with the system S S . Here, upper sequential capacity topological pressure is a slight generalisation of upper capacity topological pressure given in [ Pes88 ] .

1.
Introduction

Let us first review rational semigroups for an introduction to rational graph-directed Markov systems.
Let
\Rat
denote the set of all non-constant rational maps on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
The set
\Rat
is a semigroup with its binary operation being the composition of elements of
\Rat
.
A subsemigroup of
\Rat
is called a
rational semigroup
.
We say that a rational semigroup
G
is
finitely generated
if it is generated by a finite subset called a
generator system

\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\}\subset\Rat
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We denote the finitely generated rational semigroup
G
by
\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle
.
The study of dynamics generated by finitely many rational maps goes back at least to
[
ZR92
]
, although the term, rational semigroup is not used there.
A systematic treatment in the semigroup setting was subsequently given in
[
HM96
]
.
In
[
SW19
]
, Sumi and Watanabe introduced a rational graph-directed Markov system, which can be seen as a generalisation of rational semigroups.
In this new class of systems, rational maps are selected according to a Markovian rule.

The next example, given in
[
HM96
, Example 1]
, illustrates the difficulty of studying rational semigroups as dynamical systems:
let
a\in\mathbb{C}
,
|a|>1
and
G:=\langle z^{2},|a|^{-1}z^{2}\rangle
; then, the Julia set
J(G)
of the rational semigroup is
\{z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\colon 1\leq|z|\leq|a|\}
; see
[
ZR92
, Example 2]
for a similar example.
One readily verifies that the image of a point in
J(G)
under some element of
G
is mapped outside the Julia set.

In contrast, the Julia sets of rational semigroups and, more generally, of rational GDMSs satisfy a certain form of backward invariance; see Proposition
2.1
.
Informally, preimages of subsets of the Julia sets remain contained in the Julia sets.
The present paper primarily focuses on a quantitative study of such preimages; see subsection
1.1
.
To the best of our knowledge, this viewpoint has not been systematically explored for rational semigroups or rational GDMSs.

We will adopt the convention that
\mathbb{N}:=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\colon n>0\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\colon n\geq 0\}
.
For a point
z
of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, let
|z|
denote the
modulus
of
z
.
The
cardinality
of a set
\mathcal{A}
is denoted by
\#\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
.
We write
\id
to denote the
identity map
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
For a metric space
Y
with the distance
d_{Y}
, we write
\overline{A}^{Y}
to denote the
closure
of a subset
A\subset Y
with respect to
d_{Y}
.
We always equip
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with the spherical distance
d_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}
, and we equip
\mathbb{C}
with the Euclidean distance

d_{\mathbb{C}}(z,w)
.
For product spaces
Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
, we use the distance

d_{Y_{1}\times Y_{2}}((x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2})):=\max\{d_{Y_{1}}(x_{1},y_{1}),d_{Y_{2}}(x_{2},y_{2})\}.

Rational graph-directed Markov systems

We first define directed multigraphs, which determine admissible compositions of rational maps.
Let
V
be a finite set, each of whose elements is called a
vertex
.
Let
E
be a finite set, each of whose elements is called an
edge
.
We define the functions
\boldsymbol{i},\boldsymbol{t}\colon E\to V
, and the images
\boldsymbol{i}(e)
and
\boldsymbol{t}(e)
are called the
initial
and
terminal
vertices, respectively.
The tuple
(V,E,\boldsymbol{i},\boldsymbol{t})
is called a
directed multigraph
.
For simplicity, we will write it simply as
(V,E):=(V,E,\boldsymbol{i},\boldsymbol{t})
, and call it a
graph
.

Let
\Gamma_{e}\subset\Rat
be a non-empty subset of
\Rat
indexed by a directed edge
e\in E
.
The triple

S:=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})

is called a
rational graph-directed Markov system
(rational GDMS).
We write

\Gamma(S):=\bigcup_{e\in E}\Gamma_{e}.

We say that a rational GDMS is
finitely generated
if the set
\Gamma(S)
is finite.
We also write

I:=\{(g,e)\in\Rat\times E:g\in\Gamma_{e}\}.

For
\alpha=(g,e)\in I
we write

g_{\alpha}:=g,\qquad e_{\alpha}:=e,\qquad\boldsymbol{i}(\alpha):=\boldsymbol{i}(e),\qquad\boldsymbol{t}(\alpha):=\boldsymbol{t}(e).

We define

X(S):=\left\{(\xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in I^{\mathbb{N}}\colon\boldsymbol{t}(\xi_{n})=\boldsymbol{i}(\xi_{n+1})\text{ for each }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

and in particular, for
i\in V
we define

X_{i}(S):=\left\{\xi\in X(S)\colon\boldsymbol{i}(\xi_{1})=i\right\}.

We equip the set
\Rat
with the topology of uniform convergence and each
\Gamma_{e}\subset\Rat
with the subspace topology of
\Rat
.
We consider the discrete topology on
E
and endow
X(S)
and
X_{i}(S)
,
i\in V
with the subspace topology induced by the product topology on
(\Rat\times E)^{\mathbb{N}}
.
When a rational GDMS
S
is finitely generated, this topology on
X(S)
is metrisable by the distance given below.
We define the distance
d_{X(S)}(\xi,\tau)
on
X(S)
to be

d_{X(S)}(\xi,\tau):=2^{-\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\ \xi_{n}\neq\tau_{n}\}}

for any distinct
\xi,\tau\in X(S)
;
if
\xi=\tau
, then we set
d_{X(S)}(\xi,\tau):=0
.

For
\xi:=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots)\in X(S)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
\xi|n:=\xi|_{n}:=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set
X^{n}(S):=\{\xi|n\colon\xi\in X(S)\}
and
X^{*}(S):=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X^{n}(S)
.
For
i\in V
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
X_{i}^{n}(S):=\{\xi\in X^{n}(S):\boldsymbol{i}(\xi_{1})=i\}
.
Given an element
\xi\in X^{*}(S)\cup X(S)
, we write
|\xi|
to denote the unique
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\xi\in X^{n}(S)
if
\xi\in X^{*}(S)
; we set
|\xi|=\infty
if
\xi\in X(S)
.
For
i,j\in V
, we also write
X_{i,j}(S):=\{\xi\in X^{*}(S):\boldsymbol{i}(e_{\xi,1})=i,\ \boldsymbol{t}(e_{\xi,|\xi|})=j\}
.
Further, for an element
\xi\in X^{*}(S)\cup X(S)
and for
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq k\leq|\xi|
, we use
\xi_{k}
or
(g_{\xi,k},e_{\xi,k})
to denote the
k
-th component of
\xi
.
For
\xi\in X^{*}(S)
, we set

g_{\xi}:=g_{\xi,|\xi|}\circ\dots\circ g_{\xi,1}\quad\text{ and }\quad e_{\xi}:=(e_{\xi,1},\dots,e_{\xi,|\xi|})

and

\boldsymbol{i}(\xi):=\boldsymbol{i}(e_{\xi,1})\quad\text{ and }\quad\boldsymbol{t}(\xi):=\boldsymbol{t}(e_{\xi,|\xi|}).

For
\xi\in X(S)
, we also write
\boldsymbol{i}(\xi):=\boldsymbol{i}(e_{\xi,1})
.
Next, we define the sets

\displaystyle H(S):=\left\{g_{\xi}\in\Rat\colon\xi\in X^{*}(S)\right\},

\displaystyle H_{i}(S):=\left\{g_{\xi}\in\Rat\colon\xi\in X^{*}(S),\boldsymbol{i}(\xi)=i\right\},

\displaystyle H^{j}(S):=\left\{g_{\xi}\in\Rat\colon\xi\in X^{*}(S),\boldsymbol{t}(\xi)=j\right\}\text{ and }H^{j}_{i}(S):=H_{i}(S)\cap H^{j}(S).

With these sets, we introduce several definitions that are central to our study in this paper.
We define the
Fatou set
of
S
to be

F(S):=\left\{z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\colon\text{the set }H(S)\text{ is equicontinuous at }z\right\}

and the
Julia set
of
S
is defined as its complement
J(S):=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F(S)
.
Likewise, we define the following sets at each vertex
i
.
The set

F_{i}(S):=\left\{z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\colon\text{the set }H_{i}(S)\text{ is equicontinuous at }z\right\}

is called the
Fatou set of
S
at the vertex
i
.
Its complement
J_{i}(S):=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F_{i}(S)
is called the
Julia set of
S
at the vertex
i
.
We remark that
F(S)=\bigcap_{i\in V}F_{i}(S)
and
J(S)=\bigcup_{i\in V}J_{i}(S)
by definition.
As seen in the example above from
[
HM96
, Example 1]
, neither of the Julia sets
J(S)
nor
J(G)
possesses a property analogous to complete invariance.
Throughout this paper, we assume that

(1)

J(S)\subset\mathbb{C}.

Unlike rational semigroups, rational GDMSs consider only the compositions of rational maps prescribed by a graph.
As a consequence, the classes of graphs influence rational GDMSs.
In view of this, we provide some definitions.
Let
\mathcal{G}:=(V,E)
be a directed multigraph.
We define the
adjacency matrix
of
\mathcal{G}
to be the
V\times V
matrix
\mathcal{A}:=(A_{i,j})_{i,j\in V}
, where each entry is given by
A_{i,j}:=\#\{e\in E\colon\boldsymbol{i}(e)=i,\ \boldsymbol{t}(e)=j\}
,
i,j\in V
.
Such a matrix
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
irreducible
if for any pair

(i,j)\in V\times V
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(\mathcal{A}^{n})_{i,j}>0
.
We say that a rational GDMS
S
is
irreducible
if the adjacency matrix of its graph is irreducible.

Rational skew product maps associated with rational GDMSs

We next review
fibred rational maps
used for the investigation of rational GDMSs.
Fibred rational maps were first formulated in
[
Jon99
,
Jon00
]
, where the author investigated their ergodic properties.
When fibred rational maps are associated with rational semigroups, studying the former as topological and complex dynamical systems has proven useful for understanding the fractal geometry of the latter; e.g.,
[
Sum05
,
SU13
]
.
These associated fibred maps are called
rational skew product maps
associated with a generator system; see
[
Sum00
]
.

Let us now formulate the rational skew product maps associated with a rational GDMS
S:=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})
.
The
shift map

\sigma\colon X(S)\circlearrowleft
is given by
\xi=(\xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mapsto(\xi_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
We define a map
\tilde{f}\colon X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\circlearrowleft
given by

(2)

\tilde{f}(\xi,z):=(\sigma(\xi),{g_{\xi,1}}(z))

for each
(\xi,z)\in X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
The map
\tilde{f}
is called the
rational skew product map associated with
S
.
Note that the map
\tilde{f}
is open, and it is surjective when the system is irreducible.
In this paper, we also refer to it as an
associated rational skew product map
.
For
\xi\in X(S)
, we define the sets

F_{\xi}:=\left\{z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\colon\text{the set }\{g_{\xi|n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}\text{ is equicontinuous at }z\right\}

and
J_{\xi}:=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F_{\xi}
.
The sets
F_{\xi}
and
J_{\xi}
are called the
non-autonomous Fatou set of
\xi
and
non-autonomous Julia set of
\xi
, respectively.
Due to the assumption
J(S)\subset\mathbb{C}
given in (
1
), we have
J_{\xi}\subset J(S)\subset\mathbb{C}
.
Further, we define the sets

(3)

J(\tilde{f}):=\overline{\bigcup_{\xi\in X(S)}\{\xi\}\times J_{\xi}}^{X(S)\times\mathbb{C}}\quad\text{and}\quad F(\tilde{f}):=(X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}})\setminus J(\tilde{f}),

which are called the
skew product Julia set
of
\tilde{f}
and the
skew product Fatou set
, respectively.
The set
J(\tilde{f})
is completely invariant under
\tilde{f}
; that is,

(4)

\tilde{f}^{-1}(J(\tilde{f}))=\tilde{f}(J(\tilde{f}))=J(\tilde{f}).

For
i\in V
, we also define the
skew product Julia set
at
i\in V
as

(5)

J_{i}(\tilde{f}):=\overline{\bigcup_{\xi\in X_{i}(S)}\{\xi\}\times J_{\xi}}^{X(S)\times\mathbb{C}}.

For
\tilde{z}:=(\xi,z)\in X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

(\tilde{f}^{n})^{\prime}(\tilde{z}):=(g_{\xi|n})^{\prime}(z).

We endow
J(\tilde{f})
and
J_{i}(\tilde{f})
with the subspace topology induced by the product distance
d_{X(S)\times\mathbb{C}}
on
X(S)\times\mathbb{C}
.
Unless otherwise stated, all derivatives and their norms are taken with respect to the Euclidean coordinate on
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\pi_{1}\colon X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to X(S)
and
\pi_{2}\colon X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
denote the coordinate projections.
Under the canonical identification
\pi_{2}|_{\pi_{1}^{-1}(\{\xi\})}\colon\pi_{1}^{-1}(\{\xi\})\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
,
each fibre
\pi_{1}^{-1}(\{\xi\})
is naturally identified with
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
However, by (
1
) we have
J_{\xi}\subset J(S)\subset\mathbb{C}
for all
\xi\in X(S)
and thus
J(\tilde{f})\subset X(S)\times\mathbb{C}
.

Following
[
Sum01
, Definition 2.6, Definition 2.7]
, we now introduce the notions about important classes of associated rational skew product maps.
The associated rational skew product map
\tilde{f}\colon X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\circlearrowleft
is said to be
expanding along fibres
if
J(\tilde{f})\neq\varnothing
and there exist
c>0
and
\lambda>1
such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

(6)

\inf_{\tilde{z}\in J(\tilde{f})}|(\tilde{f}^{n})^{\prime}(\tilde{z})|\geq c\lambda^{n}.

When the rational skew product map associated with a rational semigroup is expanding in the sense of
[
Sum00
]
, it is a special case of open, distance-expanding maps, which have been studied in great detail; see
[
PU10
,
URM22a
,
URM22b
]
.
This is also the case for rational skew product maps associated with finitely generated rational GDMSs being expanding along fibres; see
[
AJSW24
]
.

For
g\in\Rat
, let
\Crit(g)\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
denote the set of critical points of
g
, i.e. the set of points where
g
is not locally injective.
We write
\CV(g):=g(\Crit(g))
for the set of critical values of
g
.
In particular, if
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
g(z)\in\mathbb{C}
, then
z\in\Crit(g)
is equivalent to
g^{\prime}(z)=0
in the Euclidean coordinate on
\mathbb{C}
.
Write
C(\tilde{f}):=\{(\xi,z)\in X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\colon z\in\Crit(g_{\xi,1})\}
.
We now define

P(\tilde{f}):=\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{f}^{n}(C(\tilde{f}))}^{X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}.

The set
P(\tilde{f})
is called the
post-critical set
for the map
\tilde{f}
.
We say that the map
\tilde{f}
is
hyperbolic along fibres
if
P(\tilde{f})\subset F(\tilde{f})
.
For each vertex
i\in V
, the
post-critical set of
{S}
at the vertex
i
is defined by

P_{i}(S):=\overline{\bigcup_{h\in H^{i}(S)}\CV(h)}^{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}},

where
\CV(h)
denotes the set of critical values of
h\in H(S)
.
A rational GDMS
S
is said to be
hyperbolic
if
P_{i}(S)\subset F_{i}(S)
for each
i\in V
.

Denote by
\Aut(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})
the set of Möbius transformations of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
and denote by
\lox(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})
the set of loxodromic elements of
\Aut(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})
.
We say that a non-identity element
g
in
\Aut(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})
is
loxodromic
if
g
has two fixed points whose multipliers have moduli different from one.
For an element
g\in\Rat
, we write
\deg(g)\in\mathbb{N}
to refer to its
degree
.

Proposition 1.1

(
[
AJSW24
]
)
.

Let
S:=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})
be a finitely generated, irreducible rational GDMS.

(i)

If the map
\tilde{f}
is expanding along fibres, then it is hyperbolic along fibres, and moreover
\bigcup_{i\in V}H_{i}^{i}(S)\cap\Aut(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})\subset\lox(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})
.

(ii)

Suppose that there exists
g\in\Gamma(S)
with
\deg(g)\geq 2
and
\bigcup_{i\in V}H_{i}^{i}(S)\cap\Aut(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})\subset\lox(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})
.
If the map
\tilde{f}
is hyperbolic along fibres, then it is expanding along fibres.

Remark 1.2
.

Let
S:=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})
be a finitely generated, irreducible rational GDMS.
The map
\tilde{f}
is hyperbolic along fibres if and only if the system
S
is hyperbolic.

Statement of results

Up until Main Theorem
A
, we fix a finitely generated, irreducible rational GDMS denoted by
S=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})
.
Recall that

I:=\{(g,e)\in\Rat\times E:g\in\Gamma_{e}\}.

For
\alpha=(g,e)\in I
we write

g_{\alpha}:=g,\quad e_{\alpha}:=e,\quad\boldsymbol{i}(\alpha):=\boldsymbol{i}(e)\quad\text{ and }\quad\boldsymbol{t}(\alpha):=\boldsymbol{t}(e).

For
i,j\in V
, we define

I_{(i)}:=\{\alpha\in I\colon\boldsymbol{i}(\alpha)=i\},\quad I^{(j)}:=\{\alpha\in I\colon\boldsymbol{t}(\alpha)=j\}\text{ and }I_{(i)}^{(j)}:=I_{(i)}\cap I^{(j)}.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
S=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})
and let
I
be the index set of generators as above.
A family
a=(a_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}\in[0,\infty)^{I}
is called a
(vertex-wise) weight

if
\sum_{\alpha\in I^{(j)}}a_{\alpha}=1
for every
j\in V
.
We denote the set of all weights by
\mathcal{W}(S)
.
We write
\mathcal{W}^{+}(S):=\left\{a\in\mathcal{W}(S):\forall\alpha\in I(a_{\alpha}>0)\right\}
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
We define the
\#V\times\#V
matrix

M^{(t)}=(M^{(t)}_{ij})_{i,j\in V}
given by

M^{(t)}_{ij}:=\sum_{\alpha\in I_{(i)}^{(j)}}\deg(g_{\alpha})^{t}

for
i,j\in V
.
We call
M^{(t)}
the
rational degree matrix
of the system
S
(with inverse temperature
t
).
When
t=1
, we simply write
M=(M^{(1)}_{ij})_{i,j\in V}
.
Let

\rho_{t}=\rho(M^{(t)})

denote the spectral radius of
M^{(t)}
.

Let
M^{(t)}
be the rational degree matrix of
S
and
let
\rho_{t}>0
and
u^{(t)}=(u^{(t)}_{i})_{i\in V}\in(0,\infty)^{V}

be a left Perron-Frobenius eigenpair,
(u^{(t)})^{\top}M^{(t)}=\rho_{t}(u^{(t)})^{\top}
.
For
\alpha\in I
, we define

a^{(t)}_{\alpha}:=\frac{\deg(g_{\alpha})^{t}u^{(t)}_{\boldsymbol{i}(\alpha)}}{\rho_{t}u^{(t)}_{\boldsymbol{t}(\alpha)}}\;>\;0.

Note that

belongs to

.
Let
h_{\mathrm{top}}(\tilde{f})
denote the topological entropy of
\tilde{f}|_{J(\tilde{f})}
.

The author of
[

]

Main Theorem A

(see Theorem
3.8
)
.

Let
S=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})
be a finitely generated, irreducible rational GDMS.
Assume that the associated skew product
\tilde{f}
is expanding along fibres.
Then, we have

h_{\mathrm{top}}(\tilde{f})=\log\rho(M).

Let
Y
be a compact metric space with a distance
d_{Y}
and let
T\colon Y\to Y
be continuous.
We call the pair
(Y,T)
a
(topological) dynamical system
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
Bowen metric

d_{n}(x,y):=\max_{0\leq k<n}d_{Y}\left(T^{k}x,T^{k}y\right)

B_{n}(x,\varepsilon):=\{y\in Y:\ d_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon\}
.
For a real-valued function
\phi
on

, we write
\mathcal{S}_{n}\phi(y):=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\phi(T^{k}y)
for

.
Let
C(Y)
denote the set of real-valued continuous functions on

.

Up until Main Theorem
B
, we fix a rational GDMS of the following class.
Let
S=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})
be a finitely generated, irreducible rational GDMS.
Assume that the map
\tilde{f}
is expanding along fibres.
Define a map
\tilde{\varphi}\colon J(\tilde{f})\to\mathbb{R}
,

\tilde{\varphi}(\tilde{z}):=-\log|(\tilde{f})^{\prime}(\tilde{z})|

for
\tilde{z}:=(\xi,z)\in J(\tilde{f})
.
For
\phi\in C(Y)
, let
\Press(T,\phi)
denote the

of
(T,\phi)
; refer to
[

]
for example.
For

, we write
\tilde{\varphi}_{u}:=u\tilde{\varphi}
.
Moreover, we define a

\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

\mathcal{P}(u):=\Press\left(\tilde{f}|_{J(\tilde{f})},\tilde{\varphi}_{u}\right).

Let
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
denote the Bowen parameter of
S
, the unique zero of the function

; see
[
AJSW24
]
.
We denote
\tilde{\pi}_{2}:=\pi_{2}|_{J(\tilde{f})}
and
\tilde{\pi}_{2,i}:=\pi_{2}|_{J_{i}(\tilde{f})}
for

.
When the map
\tilde{f}
is topologically transitive, it is known that there exists
\delta
-conformal measure on

given in Proposition

.
Let
\nu_{\delta}
denote the push-forward measure of
\tilde{\nu}_{\delta}
under
\tilde{\pi}_{2}

\nu_{\delta}(B):=\tilde{\nu}_{\delta}(\tilde{\pi}_{2}^{-1}(B))

for each Borel set

.

We now note that the system
S
is hyperbolic as its associated rational skew product map is expanding along fibres.
Let
D(z,r)
denote the
Euclidean disc
with centre
z\in\mathbb{C}
and radius
r>0

Standing assumption

Theorem 3.8

z,z^{\prime}\in\gamma_{\xi,U}(\mathsf{H}_{\boldsymbol{t}(\xi)}(R))
, we have
u:=g_{\xi}(z)\in\mathsf{H}_{\boldsymbol{t}(\xi)}(R)
,
v:=g_{\xi}(z^{\prime})\in\mathsf{H}_{\boldsymbol{t}(\xi)}(R)
.
Moreover
u,v\in D(u_{\boldsymbol{t}(\xi),\ell},r)
, so
d_{\mathbb{C}}(u,v)<2r
.
For
k\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
, using
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in[\xi]
we have

\pi_{2}(\tilde{f}^{k}(\tilde{z}))=g_{\xi|k}(z)=F_{k}(u),\qquad\pi_{2}(\tilde{f}^{k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}))=g_{\xi|k}(z^{\prime})=F_{k}(v),

and thus

d_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\pi_{2}(\tilde{f}^{k}(\tilde{z})),\pi_{2}(\tilde{f}^{k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}))\right)=d_{\mathbb{C}}(F_{k}(u),F_{k}(v))\leq L_{\mathrm{Lip}}d_{\mathbb{C}}(u,v)<2L_{\mathrm{Lip}}r<\varepsilon/2.

Hence for all
0\leq k<n
,

d_{X(S)\times\mathbb{C}}\left(\tilde{f}^{k}(\tilde{z}),\tilde{f}^{k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime})\right)=\max\left\{d_{X(S)}(\sigma^{k}(\eta),\sigma^{k}(\eta^{\prime})),d_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\pi_{2}(\tilde{f}^{k}(\tilde{z})),\pi_{2}(\tilde{f}^{k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}))\right)\right\}<\varepsilon,

so
d_{n}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})<\varepsilon
, proving
A_{\tau,\ell}\subset B_{n}(\tilde{z}_{\tau,\ell},\varepsilon)
.

Finally, since we have

\#X_{\boldsymbol{t}(\xi)}^{L}(S)\cdot M(\varepsilon)\leq K(\varepsilon)M(\varepsilon)
, the set
\tilde{B}_{\xi,U}(R)
is covered by at most
N_{\mathrm{cov}}(\varepsilon):=K(\varepsilon)M(\varepsilon)

Bowen balls of the form
B_{n}(\cdot,\varepsilon)
with centers in
\tilde{B}_{\xi,U}(R)
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
S=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})
be a finitely generated, irreducible rational GDMS.
Assume that the associated skew product
\tilde{f}
is expanding along fibres and topologically transitive on
J(\tilde{f})
.
Then, we have

\overline{\mathcal{CP}}\left(\tilde{f}|_{J(\tilde{f})},\delta\tilde{\varphi},\widetilde{\mathsf{HP}}(R)\right)=\overline{\Press^{\mathrm{geom}}}(\delta,R).

Proof.

Put
\varepsilon_{0}:=\min\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bd}},1,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rep}}\}
.
Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For any
\xi\in X^{n}(S)
,
U\in\Comp(\xi,R)
, by Lemma
4.9
there exist
\tilde{z}_{\xi,U,1},\dots,\tilde{z}_{\xi,U,N}\in\tilde{B}_{\xi,U}(R)
such that

\tilde{B}_{\xi,U}(R)\subset\bigcup_{m=1}^{N_{\mathrm{cov}}(\varepsilon_{0})}B_{n}(\tilde{z}_{\xi,U,m},\varepsilon_{0}).

Noting the definition of
\widetilde{\mathsf{HP}}_{n}(R)
given in (
9
), we also have

\widetilde{\mathsf{HP}}_{n}(R)\subset\bigcup_{\xi\in X^{n}(S)}\bigcup_{U\in\Comp(\xi,R)}\tilde{B}_{\xi,U}(R)

Indeed, for
(\omega,z)\in\widetilde{\mathsf{HP}}_{n}(R)
, we have
g_{\omega}(z)\in\mathsf{H}_{\boldsymbol{t}(\omega)}(R)\subset D(y_{\boldsymbol{t}(\omega)},R)
, hence
z
belongs to a unique component
U\in\Comp(\omega,R)
,
and the inverse branch
\gamma_{\omega,U}
is well-defined on
D(y_{\boldsymbol{t}(\omega)},2R)
.
Hence,

\Lambda_{n}(\widetilde{\mathsf{HP}}_{n}(R),\delta\tilde{\varphi},\varepsilon_{0})\leq\sum_{\xi\in X^{n}(S)}\sum_{U\in\Comp(\xi,R)}\sum_{m=1}^{N_{\mathrm{cov}}(\varepsilon_{0})}\exp\left(\sup_{\tilde{y}\in B_{n}(\tilde{z}_{\xi,U,m},\varepsilon_{0})}\mathcal{S}_{n}(\delta\tilde{\varphi})(\tilde{y})\right).

By Lemma
4.8
,

\exp\left(\sup_{\tilde{y}\in B_{n}(\tilde{z}_{\xi,U,m},\varepsilon_{0})}\mathcal{S}_{n}(\delta\tilde{\varphi})(\tilde{y})\right)\leq D_{\mathrm{bd}}\cdot\exp(\mathcal{S}_{n}(\delta\tilde{\varphi})(\tilde{z}_{\xi,U,m}));

since
\tilde{z}_{\xi,U,m}\in\tilde{B}_{\xi,U}(R)
, Lemma
4.7
yields

\exp(\mathcal{S}_{n}(\delta\tilde{\varphi})(\tilde{z}_{\xi,U,m}))\leq K_{\mathrm{koe}}\mathrm{w}_{\delta}(\xi,U)
.
Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}(\widetilde{\mathsf{HP}}_{n}(R),\delta\tilde{\varphi},\varepsilon_{0})

\displaystyle\leq D_{\mathrm{bd}}K_{\mathrm{koe}}N_{\mathrm{cov}}(\varepsilon_{0})\sum_{\xi\in X^{n}(S)}\sum_{U\in\Comp(\xi,R)}\mathrm{w}_{\delta}(\xi,U)

(13)

\displaystyle=D_{\mathrm{bd}}K_{\mathrm{koe}}N_{\mathrm{cov}}(\varepsilon_{0})Z_{n}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\delta,R).

Next, take an arbitrary at most countable set

E=\{\tilde{z}\}_{\tilde{z}\in E}\subset J(\tilde{f})

such that

\widetilde{\mathsf{HP}}_{n}(R)\subset\bigcup_{\tilde{z}\in E}B_{n}(\tilde{z},\varepsilon_{0})
.
Since
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rep}}
, Lemma
4.6
implies each ball
intersects
\mathcal{R}_{n}
in at most one point. Hence,

\sum_{\tilde{z}\in E}\exp\left(\sup_{\tilde{y}\in B_{n}(\tilde{z},\varepsilon_{0})}\mathcal{S}_{n}(\delta\tilde{\varphi})(\tilde{y})\right)\geq\sum_{\tilde{z}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}\exp(\mathcal{S}_{n}(\delta\tilde{\varphi})(\tilde{z})).

Observe that for
\xi\in X^{*}(S)
and
U\in\Comp(\xi,R)
, we have

\exp(\mathcal{S}_{n}(\delta\tilde{\varphi})(\tilde{z}_{\xi,U}))=|(g_{\xi})^{\prime}(\gamma_{\xi,U}(y_{\boldsymbol{t}(\xi)}))|^{-\delta}=|(\gamma_{\xi,U})^{\prime}(y_{\boldsymbol{t}(\xi)})|^{\delta}=\mathrm{w}_{\delta}(\xi,U)

and thus

\sum_{\tilde{z}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}\exp(\mathcal{S}_{n}(\delta\tilde{\varphi})(\tilde{z}))=\sum_{\xi\in X^{n}(S)}\sum_{U\in\Comp(\xi,R)}\mathrm{w}_{\delta}(\xi,U)=Z_{n}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\delta,R).

Taking the infimum over all Bowen covers yields

(14)

\Lambda_{n}(\widetilde{\mathsf{HP}}_{n}(R),\delta\tilde{\varphi},\varepsilon_{0})\ \geq\ Z_{n}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\delta,R).

Setting
C_{\mathrm{cap}}:=\max\left\{1,\ D_{\mathrm{bd}}K_{\mathrm{koe}}N_{\mathrm{cov}}(\varepsilon_{0})\right\}
and combining (
13
) and (
14
),
we obtain

C_{\mathrm{cap}}^{-1}Z_{n}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\delta,R)\leq\Lambda_{n}(\widetilde{\mathsf{HP}}_{n}(R),\delta\tilde{\varphi},\varepsilon_{0})\leq C_{\mathrm{cap}}Z_{n}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\delta,R),

which implies

\overline{\mathcal{CP}}\left(\tilde{f},\delta\tilde{\varphi},\widetilde{\mathsf{HP}}(R),\varepsilon_{0}\right)=\overline{\Press^{\mathrm{geom}}}(\delta,R).

For any
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, the same lower bound holds since
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rep}}
,
and the upper bound holds with
N_{\mathrm{cov}}(\varepsilon)
in place of
N_{\mathrm{cov}}(\varepsilon_{0})
.
Hence for all
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, we have

\overline{\mathcal{CP}}\left(\tilde{f},\delta\tilde{\varphi},\widetilde{\mathsf{HP}}(R),\varepsilon\right)=\overline{\Press^{\mathrm{geom}}}(\delta,R)
.
Finally, Definition
1.8
implies

\overline{\mathcal{CP}}\left(\tilde{f},\delta\tilde{\varphi},\widetilde{\mathsf{HP}}(R)\right)=\overline{\Press^{\mathrm{geom}}}(\delta,R)
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
S=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})
be a finitely generated, irreducible rational GDMS.
Assume that
S
satisfies the VSC and that the map
\tilde{f}
is expanding along fibres
and topologically transitive on
J(\tilde{f})
.
Take an arbitrary family of vertex-wise holes
(\mathsf{H}_{i}(R))_{i\in V}
for
S
.
Then, we have

e_{\delta}(R)=\Press(\sigma,\psi)-\overline{\Press^{\mathrm{geom}}}(\delta,R)>0.

Proof.

For each
i\in V
set

\mathcal{B}_{n,i}(R):=\bigcup_{\xi\in X_{i}^{n}(S)}\bigcup_{U\in\Comp(\xi,R)}B_{\xi,U}(R).

Then, we have
\mathsf{HP}_{n}(R)=\bigcup_{i\in V}\mathcal{B}_{n,i}(R)
.
By the iterated VSC, we obtain

\nu_{\delta}(\mathcal{B}_{n,i}(R))=\sum_{\xi\in X_{i}^{n}(S)}\sum_{U\in\Comp(\xi,R)}\nu_{\delta}(B_{\xi,U}(R)).

Since
\nu_{\delta}(\mathsf{HP}_{n}(R))\ \geq\ \max_{i\in V}\nu_{\delta}\left(\mathcal{B}_{n,i}(R)\right)
and

\max_{i\in V}\nu_{\delta}\left(\mathcal{B}_{n,i}(R)\right)\ \geq\ \frac{1}{\#V}\sum_{i\in V}\nu_{\delta}\left(\mathcal{B}_{n,i}(R)\right),

we have

\frac{1}{\#V}\sum_{i\in V}\nu_{\delta}(\mathcal{B}_{n,i}(R))\leq\nu_{\delta}(\mathsf{HP}_{n}(R)).

Therefore, we have

\frac{1}{\#V}\sum_{i\in V}\nu_{\delta}(\mathcal{B}_{n,i}(R))\leq\nu_{\delta}(\mathsf{HP}_{n}(R))\leq\sum_{i\in V}\nu_{\delta}(\mathcal{B}_{n,i}(R)).

Therefore,

\frac{1}{\#V}\sum_{\xi\in X^{n}(S)}\sum_{U\in\Comp(\xi,R)}\nu_{\delta}(B_{\xi,U}(R))\leq\nu_{\delta}(\mathsf{HP}_{n}(R))\leq\sum_{\xi\in X^{n}(S)}\sum_{U\in\Comp(\xi,R)}\nu_{\delta}(B_{\xi,U}(R)).

Applying Lemma
5.3
yields constants
c_{*},C_{*}>0
such that

c_{*}Z_{n}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\delta,R)\leq\nu_{\delta}(\mathsf{HP}_{n}(R))\leq C_{*}Z_{n}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\delta,R)

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Therefore,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\nu_{\delta}(\mathsf{HP}_{n}(R))=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\delta,R)=\overline{\Press^{\mathrm{geom}}}(\delta,R).

and thus

e_{\delta}(R)=-\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{\nu_{\delta}(\mathsf{HP}_{n}(R))}{N_{n}}=-\overline{\Press^{\mathrm{geom}}}(\delta,R)+\Press(\sigma,\psi).

Combining this with Proposition
5.4
completes the proof.
∎

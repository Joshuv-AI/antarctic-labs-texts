-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: On August 31, 2026, Reliant Transportation Group, LLC 
(Reliant); TIP MNC Acquisition, LLC; TIP MN Investments LP; and Tiger 
Infrastructure Partners Fund IV AIV LP (collectively, Applicants) filed 
an application seeking authority to acquire, from Jane M. Stiles and 
Thomas W. Stiles (collectively, Sellers), control of two federally 
regulated interstate passenger motor carriers: Safe-Way Bus Co. (Safe-
Way Bus) and Safe-Way Wisconsin, Inc. (Safe-Way Wisconsin) 
(collectively, Additional Carriers). The Board is tentatively approving 
and authorizing the proposed transaction. If no opposing comments are 
timely filed, this notice will be the final Board action.

DATES: Comments may be filed by December 7, 2026. If any comments are 
filed, Applicants may file a reply by December 21, 2026. If no opposing 
comments are filed by December 7, 2026, this notice shall be effective 
on December 8, 2026.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: Reliant, a Delaware limited liability 
company headquartered in Hastings, Minn., seeks Board authority to 
acquire control of the Additional Carriers by purchasing 100% of the 
equity interests from Sellers. (Appl. 2, 21.) \1\ According to the 
application, Reliant is a noncarrier company that was formed to own, 
operate, and manage various motor carriers of passengers and related

Infrastructure Investment Entities).\5\ (Appl. 2-3.) TIP MNC 
Acquisition, LLC owns 73.56% of MNC Holdings, LP, which owns 100% of 
MNC Intermediate Holdings, LLC, which owns 100% of MNC Parent, LLC, 
which in turn owns 100% of Reliant. (Appl. 3.)

    Applicants state that the structure of the ownership platform was 
designed to preserve the independent operations of the carrier 
subsidiaries and is not intended to result in the consolidation of 
routes, school district contracts, or charter operations among the 
Affiliated Carriers. (Id. at 5.) They state that the purpose of the 
common ownership structure is to provide long-term ownership stability, 
increased access to capital and other resources for fleet modernization 
and safety investments, and continuity of experienced local management, 
rather than operational integration. (Id.) Applicants explain that the 
proposed acquisition of Safe-Way Bus and Safe-Way Wisconsin would add 
two additional carriers to this existing group of motor carriers of 
passengers that similarly provide school transportation and charter 
services, operating in geographic areas distinct from many of the 
Affiliated Carriers or otherwise serving different school districts and 
other customer bases. (Id.)

approved, Reliant will acquire 100% of the issued and outstanding 
equity interests of Safe-Way Bus and Safe-Way Wisconsin from Sellers

Safe-Way Wisconsin. (Id. at 15-16.) According to Applicants, Sellers

    Applicants state that the proposed transaction is not designed to 
result in material operational changes. (Id. at 22.) The Additional 
Carriers will continue to provide substantially the same services to 
their customers following closing as they currently provide, including 
school transportation services under contract and charter 
transportation services. (Id.) Applicants state that they have no

(Id.) The territories primarily serviced by Safe-Way Bus and Safe-Way 
Wisconsin do not overlap, as they are located approximately 30 miles 
apart in different states. (Id.) Safe-Way Bus provides service out of 
its Inver Grove Heights terminal in the Twin Cities region of Minnesota 
and its Hudson, Wis., terminal, while Safe-Way Wisconsin provides 
service out of its terminal in Somerset, Wis., and from a terminal in

    According to the application, Safe-Way Bus's main competitors for 
student transportation services in the greater Twin Cities area and in 
the Hudson, Wis., area include First Student Transportation, Schmitty & 
Sons Transportation, Transit Team, Voigt's School Service Inc., Billie 
Bus Transportation, and American Student Transportation. (Id.) Safe-Way 
Wisconsin's main competitors for student transportation services 
include Kobussen Buses LTD and Go-Riteway Transportation Group. (Id.) 
There are approximately five other direct competitors in each of the 
service areas, and the competitive landscape has not materially changed 
since Sellers acquired the Additional Carriers in 2022. (Id.) Both 
Additional Carriers generally face competition from national, regional, 
and local bus providers operating within their respective service 
areas. (Id.) In addition, both companies face considerable competition 
for transportation services from other modes of transportation 
including ride sharing services and local bus service operated by 
transit agencies. (Id.)
    Under 49 U.S.C. 14303(b), the Board must approve and authorize a 
transaction that it finds consistent with the public interest, taking 
into consideration at least (1) the effect of the proposed transaction 
on the adequacy of transportation to the public, (2) the total fixed 
charges resulting from the proposed transaction, and (3) the

[[Page 66773]]

interest of affected carrier employees. Applicants have submitted the 
information required by 49 CFR 1182.2, including information 
demonstrating that the transaction is consistent with the public 
interest under 49 U.S.C. 14303(b), see 49 CFR 1182.2(a)(7), and a 
jurisdictional statement under 49 U.S.C. 14303(g) that the aggregate 
gross operating revenues of the involved carriers exceeded $2 million 
during a consecutive 12-month period ending not more than six months 
before the date of the agreement of the parties, see 49 CFR 
1182.2(a)(5). (Appl. 23-28.)
    Applicants do not expect that the proposed transaction will 
adversely affect the adequacy of transportation to the public, or that 
it will result in any material reduction in competition among 
interstate motor carriers. (Id. at 24.) They state that Safe-Way Bus 
and Safe-Way Wisconsin will continue to operate as separate legal 
entities, providing substantially the same school transportation and 
charter services that they provide today, under existing contracts, 
operating authorities, and management structures. (Id. at 25.) 
Applicants do not intend to consolidate routes, modify existing service 
patterns, discontinue service, or redirect equipment or personnel as a 
result of the proposed transaction. (Id.)
    Additionally, Applicants state that the services provided by the 
Additional Carriers do not materially overlap with Reliant's existing 
Affiliated Carriers. (Id.) Applicants assert that the Affiliated 
Carriers operate primarily in distinct, localized geographic service 
areas within Minnesota, serve different school districts and 
institutional customers, and conduct operations pursuant to separate 
contracts, terminals, fleets, and operating authorities. (Id.) 
Applicants further state that school transportation services, which 
constitute a significant portion of operations for many of the 
Affiliated Carriers, are inherently contract-specific and district-
based, with routes designed exclusively to serve discrete school 
districts under long-term contractual arrangements. (Id.) As a result, 
Applicants state, these routes are not transferable or interchangeable 
across carriers and do not compete directly with one another. (Id.)
    Applicants state that the Additional Carriers each provide school 
transportation service under contract to different school districts. 
(Id.) They explain that Safe-Way Bus serves two school districts in the 
Twin Cities, Minnesota area: Inver Grove Heights Public Schools 
district ISD 199, and South St. Paul Public Schools district/SSD 6, 
while Safe-Way Wisconsin serves Somerset Public Schools district in 
Somerset, Wis., and the Unity School district in Balsam Lake, Wis. 
(Id.) Those school districts are in different service areas 
approximately 30 miles apart in different states. (Id.) Applicants note 
that Safe-Way Bus also serves the Hudson School district in Hudson, 
Wis., and that there is no overlap in the school district service areas 
served by Safe-Way Bus and Safe-Way Wisconsin. (Id.) Applicants state 
that none of Reliant's Affiliated Carriers serve these school 
districts, nor do they operate school transportation routes in those 
communities. (Id. at 25-26.) Furthermore, Applicants state, both 
carriers face substantial competition for student transportation 
services from other bus providers (national, local, and regional) and 
from numerous other modes of transportation. (Id. at 26.) Accordingly, 
Applicants maintain, the transaction will not reduce competition in any 
relevant geographic market and has not impaired the adequacy of 
transportation to the public. (Id.)
    Similarly, Applicants state that to the extent that multiple 
carriers under common ownership provide motorcoach charter services, 
those services are customer-driven, irregular, and geographically 
unique, rather than route-based or scheduled. (Id.) Charter trips are 
performed pursuant to individual customer requests and vary by 
destination, duration, and frequency. (Id.) The Affiliated Carriers' 
charter operations are concentrated in different regions of Minnesota, 
such as the Twin Cities metropolitan area, southeastern Minnesota, 
northern Minnesota, or western Minnesota, and are typically marketed 
and performed through longstanding local customer relationships. (Id.) 
The interstate charter trips conducted by the Affiliated Carriers are 
conducted from terminals in different areas and therefore service 
different customer bases. (Id.) The Additional Carriers only provide 
occasional trips across state lines, their charter activities rely on 
separate customer bases, and do not involve the operation of regularly 
scheduled routes that could be consolidated or eliminated as a result 
of common ownership. (Id.)
    Moreover, Applicants state, each carrier will continue to maintain 
separate operating authorities, terminals, fleets, safety programs, and 
management teams. (Id.) The common ownership structure is designed to 
preserve the independent operation of each carrier, while providing 
access to long-term capital and financial stability. (Id.) It is not 
intended to facilitate operational integration or the coordination of 
pricing or service offerings. (Id.)
    Finally, even within the limited markets in which Applicants' 
carriers operate, Applicants state that they face substantial 
competition from a wide range of other transportation providers, 
including local and regional bus operators, national charter providers, 
and alternative modes of transportation such as private motor vehicles, 
airlines, and intercity rail. (Id. at 27.) Customers--particularly 
school districts and institutional clients--typically select providers 
through competitive procurement processes or negotiated contracts and 
retain the ability to change providers upon contract expiration. (Id.) 
Accordingly, Applicants contend, the proposed transaction will not 
foreclose competition or impair customer choice. (Id.) For these 
reasons, Applicants maintain that the proposed transaction will 
preserve the adequacy of transportation services, will not result in 
any anticompetitive consolidation of routes or services, and is fully 
consistent with the public interest standard under 49 U.S.C. 14303(b). 
(Id.)
    With respect to total fixed charges, Applicants anticipate 
financing the cost of the proposed transaction with a combination of 
current available funds and equity capital. (Id.) Applicants do not 
expect that any resulting fixed charges will adversely affect the 
Additional Carriers' ability to continue to provide safe and quality 
transportation service, given the stability of their contractual school 
transportation operations and continuing charter operations. (Id.) 
Applicants explain that each of the Additional Carriers has a very 
stable revenue stream from contracts with school districts, 
universities, and other institutional entities, which should be more 
than adequate to service existing and anticipated future debt. (Id.) 
According to the application, the proposed transaction will also enable 
the Additional Carriers to maintain future financial stability through 
access to considerable funds from Tiger Infrastructure Investment 
entities and their affiliates. (Id.)
    Applicants further state that the proposed transaction is not 
expected to have any material adverse effect on employees or labor 
conditions. (Id. at 28.) Applicants indicate that they have no current 
plans for employee layoffs or staffing reductions, and do not 
anticipate adverse changes to wages, benefits, or working conditions as 
a result of the proposed transaction. (Id.)

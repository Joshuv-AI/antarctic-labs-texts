New Types of Hydrogenlike matter Composed of Electron(s) and Meson(s)
Jun-Feng Wang a,b, Zhi-Feng Sun a,b,c,d, Zi-Yue Cui A,B,C, Cheng-Qun Pang B,C,D

Abstract
In the present work, we predict the existence of new types of hydrogenlike matter, including hydrogenlike atoms ( π + ​ e − \pi^{+}e^{-} , K + ​ e − K^{+}e^{-} , D + ​ e − D^{+}e^{-} ), hydrogenlike molecular ions ( π + ​ π + ​ e − \pi^{+}\pi^{+}e^{-} , K + ​ K + ​ e − K^{+}K^{+}e^{-} , D + ​ D + ​ e − D^{+}D^{+}e^{-} ) and hydrogenlike molecules ( π + ​ π + ​ e − ​ e − \pi^{+}\pi^{+}e^{-}e^{-} , K + ​ K + ​ e − ​ e − K^{+}K^{+}e^{-}e^{-} , D + ​ D + ​ e − ​ e − D^{+}D^{+}e^{-}e^{-} ). By solving the Schrödinger equation, the binding energy of hydrogenlike atoms is obtained as E n = − 1 2 ​ n 2 E_{n}=-\frac{1}{2n^{2}} . For hydrogenlike molecular ions and molecules, the variational method is employed to calculate the binding energies, i.e., E + = − 0.587 E_{+}=-0.587 and E 0 = − 1.139 E_{0}=-1.139 for hydrogenlike molecular ions and molecules, respectively. And the bond lengths for hydrogenlike molecular ions and molecules are also calculated, whose values are 2.003 2.003 and 1.414 1.414 , respectively. Here all the quantities are in atomic unit for convenience. In additon, the strong interaction between the two constituent mesons is considered in our calculations, where we find that its influence on the hydrogenlike molecular ions and molecules can be neglected. Hopefully, these types of matter would be observed in the future with the improvement of accuracy in the high energy physical experiments.

Introduction

The exploration of microscopic matter and their inner structure can increase our understanding of nature, which is always full of interests and challenges.

J. Dalton from England proposed the atomic theory in 1803, and opened a new era in modern chemistry. Dalton’s theory not only explained many chemical phenomena and chemical laws successfully, but also revealed their inner connections, transforming ancient naive atomic ideas into a scientific atomic theory. Soon after that, J. L. Gay-Lussac from France summarized experimental results of various gas reactions and drew an important conclusion: when gases react with each other, they often combine in simple ratios, and the change in volume of the combined gas also has a simple proportional relationship with the volumes of the reacting gases. Lussac’s conclusion leaded to a problem of half an atom, which contradicts the indivisibility of atoms in atomic theory and is also impossible in chemical reactions. In order to solve this problem, Amedeo Avogadro from Italy examined Lussac’s experiments and his academic dispute with Dalton, and proposed a kind of particle of a new divisible level-molecules. In nearly half a century, Avogadro’s molecular theory was not recognized by the academic community. As the rapidly development of modern inorganic and organic chemistry during this period, Italian scientist Stanislao Cannizzaro provided theoretical justification for the concept of molecules, organically integrating Dalton’s atomic theory with Avogadro’s molecular theory, and systematically proposing the scientific atomic-molecular theory, which was ultimately established in academic community.

At the turn of 19th and 20th centuries, the discovery of X-rays, radioactivity, along with the development of spectroscopy, the proposal of empirical formulas for spectral laws, and the electron-theoretical explanation of the Zeeman effect had changed the traditional concepts that atoms were indivisible and elements unchangeable. Scientists successively proposed several models of atomic structure, but they were all successively disproved by experiments. In 1911, Ernest Rutherford designed the
\alpha

\alpha

In order to explain this phenomenon, Rutherford proposed the nuclear structure model, whose core idea is that there is an extremely small atomic nucleus at the center of an atom, and all electrons revolve around the atomic nucleus. However, it was impossible to understand the stability of atoms, and to explain why excited atoms emit discrete spectral lines rather than continuous ones. So in 1913, Bohr proposed a new model of atomic structure, which is based on stationary state hypothesis, frequency rule, and quantization condition of angular momentum
Bohr 1913a

About 20 years after the nuclear structure model was proposed by Rutherford, a new kind of neutral particle named neutron was observed by J. Chadwick
Chadwick 1932

In the 1950s, with the development of high-energy accelerators, a large number of particles directly involved in strong interactions were discovered in accelerator experiments, and they had extremely short lifetimes. By the early 1960s, more than 100 “elementary” particles had been discovered in nature, which could be classified into two categories according to their interactions:

•

V
Summary

Exploration of material structure is extremely important in physics, which helps us understand the interaction of fundamental elements in the entire world.

In the present work, we propose three types of hydrogenlike structures by replacing the proton(s) in hydrogen atom, molecular ion and molecule with meson(s) of
\pi^{+}
,
K^{+}
or
D^{+}
. In order to study these systems, we solve the Schrödinger equation for hydrogenlike atoms, and use the variational method for hydrogenlike molecular ion and molecule. Since in the hydrogenlike matter, the masses of the mesons are all much heavier than that of electron, just like the case of hydrogen atom, molecular ion and molecule, the structures and the binding energies are almost the same as those of hydrogen systems. For
\pi^{+}e^{-}
,
K^{+}e^{-}
or
D^{+}e^{-}
system, the binding energy is
E_{n}=-\frac{1}{2n^{2}}
, and the most probable radius is
r_{n}=n^{2}
. For the systems of
\pi^{+}\pi^{+}e^{-}
,
K^{+}K^{+}e^{-}
and
D^{+}D^{+}e^{-}
, there exist bound states if the spatial wave function is symmetric, while no bound states appear if the spatial wave function is antisymmetric. The binding energy of these hydrogenlike molecular ion is
-0.587
, and the bond length is
2.003
. For
\pi^{+}\pi^{+}e^{-}e^{-}
,
K^{+}K^{+}e^{-}e^{-}
and
D^{+}D^{+}e^{-}e^{-}
systems, if the spin wave function of the electrons is antisymmetric, and the spatial wave function is symmetric, there exist bound states. In this case, the two electrons can get close to each other, and form electronic cloud with high density near the midpoint of the two mesons, which provides electromagnetic attraction on the two mesons. As a result, the systems combine together. The binding energy of these systems is
-1.193
, and the bond length is
1.414
. Note that if the spin wave function is symmetric while the spatial wave function is antisymmetric, there is no bound state.

The hydrogenlike matter predicted in the present work has not yet paid much attention, since it is challenging to search for it experimentally. However, we believe that these types of matter is still of much interest, and would be observed in the future with the improvement of accuracy in the high energy physical experiments.

Unifying Learning Dynamics and Generalization in Transformers Scaling Law This manuscript is a work in progress and will be updated.
Chiwun Yang

Abstract
The scaling law, a cornerstone of Large Language Model (LLM) development, predicts improvements in model performance with increasing computational resources. Yet, while empirically validated, its theoretical underpinnings remain poorly understood. This work formalizes the learning dynamics of transformer-based language models as an ordinary differential equation (ODE) system, then approximates this process to kernel behaviors. Departing from prior toy-model analyses, we rigorously analyze stochastic gradient descent (SGD) training for multi-layer transformers on sequence-to-sequence data with arbitrary data distribution, closely mirroring real-world conditions. Our analysis characterizes the convergence of generalization error to the irreducible risk as computational resources scale with data, especially during the optimization process. We establish a theoretical upper bound on excess risk characterized by a distinct phase transition. In the initial optimization phase, the excess risk decays exponentially relative to the computational cost 𝖢 {\sf C} . However, once a specific resource allocation threshold is crossed, the system enters a statistical phase, where the generalization error follows a power-law decay of Θ ( 𝖢 − 1 / 6 ) \Theta(\mathsf{C}^{-1/6}) . Beyond this unified framework, our theory derives isolated scaling laws for model size, training time, and dataset size, elucidating how each variable independently governs the upper bounds of generalization.

7
Scaling Law and Its Potential Drag

In this section, we unify our results from Section

(training convergence dynamics) and Section

7.1
General Scaling Law with Pattern Transition

Theorem 7.1

(Informal version of Theorem
F.7
)
.

Let all pre-conditions hold as Corollary
6.1
and choose
\varepsilon=\Theta(\xi/{\sf N})
. Then, with a probability at least
1-\delta
, the following condition divides the scaling trend into two stages:

\displaystyle{\sf C}>\frac{{\sf N}^{6}\log({\sf N}\cdot Ld/\xi^{2})}{\xi^{2}}

(3)

•

Stage I: Compute-Starved Stage. If Eq. (
3
) does not hold for any dataset size
{\sf N}>0
, we have:

\displaystyle C\exp(-\alpha\xi^{2}{\sf C}/{\sf N}^{6})\cdot{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D}),

where the constant
C=\poly(L,d,1/\lambda,1/\omega)
.

•

Stage II: Data-Limited Stage. If Eq. (
3
) holds for any dataset size
{\sf N}>0
, we have:

\displaystyle\inf_{{\cal F}_{\sf M,T,N}(\mathbb{D})}\sup_{\mathbb{D}\in{\cal D}}\Delta{\cal R}(F)\leq\xi^{5/3}\cdot O\left(\frac{{\sf C}}{W({\sf C}/\xi^{10})}\right)^{-1/6}\leq\xi^{5/3}\cdot\widetilde{O}\left({\sf C}\right)^{-1/6},

where
W(\cdot)
is the Lambert W function and
\widetilde{O}
hides
\log({\sf C})
.

Proof Sketch of Theorem
7.1
.

We decompose the excess risk
\Delta{\cal R}(F)
into three terms:
i)
optimization risk
{\cal R}_{\rm optimization}:=\|F(\cdot,\theta(t))-F(\cdot,\theta(\infty))\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}^{2}
;
ii)
approximation risk
{\cal R}_{\rm approximation}:=\left\|F(\cdot,\theta(\infty))-{\rm arg}\inf_{F^{\prime}\in{\cal F}_{\sf M,\infty,N}}\|F^{\prime}-F^{*}\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}^{2}\right\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}^{2}
; and
iii)
estimation risk
{\cal R}_{\rm estimation}

:=\inf_{F^{\prime}\in{\cal F}_{\sf M,\infty,N}}

\|F^{\prime}-F^{*}\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}^{2}
. The second and the third terms are already bounded in Theorem
6.2
, and the first term relates to the training loss; we thereby utilize Theorem
5.5
to finish the proof. The formal analysis is stated in Appendix
F.2
.
∎

Discussion.
The transition defined by the condition
{\sf C}\propto{\sf N}^{6}
identifies a fundamental phase change in the learning trajectory, shifting the regime from one dominated by optimization dynamics to one dominated by statistical scaling. In Stage I (Compute-Starved Stage), the system is compute-starved but capacity-rich. The exponential decay with respect to
{\sf C}
suggests that the model is rapidly navigating the loss landscape to find a global minimum, and in this regime, the primary constraint is the duration of training rather than the quality of the data. Conversely, Stage II (Data-Limited Stage) represents the saturation of the optimization process. Once the threshold in Eq. (
3
) is crossed, the model has effectively ”exhausted” the easily accessible information in the dataset. The shift to a power-law decay of
{\sf C}^{-1/6}
signifies that further progress requires navigating the high-dimensional geometry of the target function class. This bifurcation explains why empirical scaling laws often only become observable after an initial period of training: Stage I is the transient ”discovery” phase, while Stage II represents the steady-state statistical refinement that defines modern large-scale modeling.

7.2
Potential Failures of Scaling Law

The Role of the Lambert W Function.
The appearance of
W({\sf C}/\xi^{10})
in Theorem
7.1
is mathematically significant. It indicates that as compute increases, the returns on that compute are not purely power-law distributed but are subject to a logarithmic correction. This implies that at extreme scales, the cost of reducing error becomes slightly more expensive than a simple power law would predict, likely due to the overhead of managing the approximation-generalization gap.

Sensitivity of Noise Level.
The term
\xi^{5/3}
in Theorem
7.1
and the term
\xi^{2}
of the data-law in Theorem
7.2
indicate that the quality or complexity of the sampled dataset
\mathbb{D}
drastically shifts the scaling curve. Even with infinite compute, a high-complexity task (large
\xi
) will result in a significantly higher error floor.

The usual experience taught that a larger data size helps neural networks break the information bottleneck to get a better excess risk bound. However, there exists a probability that including more data leads to the noise level
\xi
growing with the dataset size, we denote it as
\xi({\sf N})
. When
\xi({\sf N})\propto{\sf C}^{1/10}
or
\xi({\sf N})\propto{\sf N}^{1/2}
, the upper bound on the excess risk degrades to
O(1)
, therefore, such a scaling law would generally become abolished.

The Waste of Model Size Scaling.
We review the boundary
{\sf M}<{\sf N}^{\frac{3}{2\zeta}}
in Theorem
7.2
. This suggests that the Model-Law (improving performance by adding parameters) is only effective when the model is small relative to the dataset’s latent complexity. Once the model becomes excessively large (in the highly over-parameterized regime), the risk is limited by the dataset size
{\sf N}
rather than the parameter count
{\sf M}
.

8
Conclusion

This work presents a comprehensive theoretical framework for rigorously analyzing the scaling law phenomenon in LLMs, specifically addressing the empirically observed power-law relationship between model performance and computational resources from a learning dynamics perspective. By formalizing the dynamics of training sequence-to-sequence multi-layer transformer architectures, we establish a foundational guarantee: under allocation of compute, the generalization excess error of these models converges asymptotically as computational budgets increase with rate
i)

\exp({\sf C})
in the
compute-starved stage
, and
ii)

{\sf C}^{-1/6}
in the data-limited stage, where
{\sf C}
is the computational cost. Furthermore, we quantify the individual influence of three scaling variables (model size, training duration, and dataset scale) on performance. Finally, we identify critical constraints (potential drags) on the reliability of transformer neural scaling, demonstrating that logarithmic corrections from the Lambert W function, the growth of dataset noise levels relative to sample size, and the saturation of model capacity relative to data complexity can lead to diminishing returns or the total breakdown of predictable scaling laws.

Lemma B.3
.

We have:

\displaystyle{\cal L}(t,\mathbb{D})=\frac{1}{n}\|{\sf F}(t)-{\sf Y}\|_{F}^{2}

Proof.

Since the decoder-only property of the model function, we have:

\displaystyle F_{(i-1)L+\ell}(t)=F_{\ell}(X_{i},\theta(t)),\forall(X_{i},Y_{i})\in\mathbb{D},i\in[n],\ell\in[L].

We then separate each token vector with:

\displaystyle{\cal L}(t,\mathbb{D})=

\displaystyle~\E_{(X,Y)\sim\mathbb{D}}[\|F(X,\theta(t))-Y\|_{F}^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{n}\sum_{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{D}}\|F(X,\theta(t))-Y\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{n}\sum_{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\|F_{\ell}(X,\theta(t))-Y_{\ell}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{n}\|{\sf F}(t)-{\sf Y}\|_{F}^{2},

where the first three steps follow from simple algebra, and the last step follows from the definitions of
{\sf F}
and
{\sf Y}
.

{\cal F}_{\sf M,N}(\mathbb{D}):=\lim_{{\sf T}\rightarrow\infty}{\cal F}_{\sf M,T,N}(\mathbb{D})
.

Definition F.2

(
[
73
]
and
[
79
]
)
.

For a metric space
({\cal S},{\rm d})
and
\varepsilon>0
, a finite set
{\cal S}^{\prime}\subset\overline{\cal S}
is called
\varepsilon
-covering if for any
x\in{\cal S}
there exists
y\in{\cal S}^{\prime}
such that
{\rm d}(x,y)\leq\varepsilon
, and the logarithm of the minimum cardinality of
\varepsilon
-covering is called covering
\varepsilon
-entropy and denoted by
V_{({\cal S},{\rm d})}(\varepsilon)
. Here,
\overline{\cal S}
is the completion of
{\cal S}
with respect to the metric
{\rm d}
.

Lemma F.3

(Lemma 11 of
[
63
]
, modified by Theorem 2.1 in
[
36
]
and in the setting of this paper)
.

For any
\mathbb{D}\subset{\cal D}
, let
F
be the empirical risk minimizer taking values in
{\cal F}_{\sf M,N}(\mathbb{D})
.
Suppose every element
F\in{\cal F}_{\sf M,N}
satisfies
{\rm d}(F):=\max_{\ell\in[L]}\max_{k\in[d]}\|F_{\ell,k}\|_{L^{\infty}({\cal X})}\leq B_{F}
for some fixed
B_{F}>0
. Then, for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, if
V_{({\cal F},{\rm d})}(\varepsilon)\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle\|F-F^{*}\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}^{2}\leq 4\inf_{F^{\prime}\in{\cal F}_{\sf M,N}(\mathbb{D})}\|F-F^{*}\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}^{2}+L{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}d}\cdot O(\frac{(B_{F}^{2}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}+\xi^{2})}V_{({\cal F}_{\sf M,N}(\mathbb{D}),{\rm d})}(\varepsilon)}{n}+(B_{F}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}+\xi)}\varepsilon).

Lemma F.5
.

\delta\in(0,0.1)
. With a probability at least
1-\delta
, we have:

•

Part 1.

\max_{\ell\in L}\|F_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}({\cal X})}\leq O(\varepsilon)=:B_{F}
.

•

Part 2.
For any constant
\varepsilon>0
, we can show that
V_{({\cal F}_{\sf M,N},{\rm d})}(\varepsilon)\leq O(1)
.

Proof.

Proof of Part 1.
Following Part 5 of Lemma
C.1
and Eq. (
1
), we have:

\displaystyle\max_{\ell\in[L],X\in{\cal X}}\|F_{\ell}(X)\|_{2}^{2}\leq O(\varepsilon^{2}).

We have:

\displaystyle\|F_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}({\cal X})}\leq

\displaystyle~\left(\max_{\ell\in[L]}\int_{\cal X}\|F_{\ell}(X)\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathrm{d}X\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\left(O(\varepsilon^{2})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~O(\varepsilon),

where the first step follows from the
L^{\infty}
norm, the second step follows from
\max_{\ell\in[L],X\in{\cal X}}\|F_{\ell}(X)\|_{2}^{2}\leq O(\varepsilon^{2})
, the last step follows from simple algebras.

Proof of Part 2.
Here, we use
R
to denote the weight distance between arbitrary two functions
F,F^{\prime}\in{\cal F}_{\sf M,N}
, following Part 3 of Lemma
C.1
and some simple algebras,
R
is bounded by
O({\sf M}B\varepsilon)
with
\max_{F,F^{\prime}\in{\cal F}_{\sf M,N}}\|F-F^{\prime}\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}\leq\varepsilon
, and the space of
{\cal F}_{\sf M,N}
is an
\sf M
-dimensional ball with radius
O({\sf M}B\varepsilon)
. Thus, for any step size
\zeta>0
, the gap is bounded by:

\displaystyle\|F-F^{\prime}\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}\leq\varepsilon\sqrt{Ld}\zeta\leq O(\varepsilon^{2}),

where the first step follows from the definition of
F
and Part 13 of Lemma
C.1
, the second step follows from choosing
\zeta=\varepsilon
.

We need at most
O(1)^{\sf M}
numbers to cover this set.
Then we have the logarithm of the covering number:

\displaystyle V_{({\cal F}_{\sf M,N},{\rm d})}(\zeta)\leq O(1)

∎

Theorem F.6

(Formal version of Theorem
6.2
)
.

Let all pre-conditions hold as Corollary
E.3
and choose
\varepsilon=\Theta(1/{\sf M})
. Then, with a probability at least
1-\delta
, there exists:

\displaystyle\inf_{{\cal F}_{\sf M,N}(\mathbb{D})}\sup_{\mathbb{D}\in{\cal D}}\Delta{\cal R}(F)\leq(4{\sf M}^{-1}+\xi)\cdot{\sf M}^{-1}+\frac{Ld\cdot\xi}{\sf N}.

Proof.

We have:

\displaystyle\Delta{\cal R}(F)=

\displaystyle~{\cal R}(F)-{\cal R}(F^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\E_{(X,Y)\sim{\cal D}}[\|F(X)-Y\|_{F}^{2}-\|F^{*}(X)-Y\|_{F}^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\E_{(X,Y)\sim{\cal D}}[\|F(X)-F^{*}(X)-\Xi\|_{F}^{2}-\|\Xi\|_{F}^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\E_{(X,Y)\sim{\cal D}}[\|F(X)-F^{*}(X)\|_{F}^{2}-\vect(F(X)-F^{*}(X))^{\top}\vect(\Xi)+\|\Xi\|_{F}^{2}-\|\Xi\|_{F}^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\E_{(X,Y)\sim{\cal D}}[\|F(X)-F^{*}(X)\|_{F}^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\|F-F^{*}\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}^{2},

(11)

where the first step follows from the definitions of
{\cal R}
and
\Delta{\cal R}
, the second step follows from
Y=F^{*}(X)+\Xi
, the third step follows from simple algebras, the fourth step follows from
\E[\Xi]={\bf 0}_{L\times d}
, the fifth step follows from the definition of
L^{F}
norm, the sixth step follows from simple algebras.

We follow the results in Corollary
E.3
(
\varepsilon\lesssim{\sf M}^{-1}
), we have:

\displaystyle\inf_{F\in{\cal F}_{\sf M,N}}\|F-F^{*}\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle~{\sf M}^{-2}

Bounding Generalization Error.
We have:

\displaystyle(B_{F}+\xi)\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon^{2}+\xi\varepsilon\leq O(\xi\varepsilon),

where this step follows from Part 1 of Lemma
F.5
and
\xi
is a constant.

Next,

\displaystyle\frac{(B_{F}^{2}+\xi^{2})V_{({\cal F}_{\sf M,N},{\rm d})}(\varepsilon)}{\sf N}\leq

\displaystyle~O(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}+\xi^{2}}{\sf N})

where the first step follows from Part 1 and 2 of Lemma
F.5
.

Result.
We combine all results above and Theorem
F.3
, obtaining:

\displaystyle\inf_{{\cal F}_{\sf M,N}(\mathbb{D})}\sup_{\mathbb{D}\in{\cal D}}\Delta{\cal R}(F)\leq(4{\sf M}^{-1}+\xi)\cdot{\sf M}^{-1}+\frac{Ld\cdot\xi}{\sf N}.

∎

F.2
General Scaling Law

Theorem F.7

(Formal version of Theorem
7.1
)
.

Let all pre-conditions hold as Corollary
E.3
and choose
\varepsilon=\Theta(\xi/{\sf N})
. Then, with a probability at least
1-\delta
, the following condition divides the scaling trend into two stages:

\displaystyle{\sf C}>\frac{{\sf N}^{6}\log({\sf N}\cdot Ld/\xi^{2})}{\xi^{2}}

(12)

•

Stage I: Compute-Starved Stage. If Eq. (
12
) does not hold for any dataset size
{\sf N}>0
, we have:

\displaystyle C\exp(-\alpha\xi^{2}{\sf C}/{\sf N}^{6})\cdot{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D}),

where the constant
C=\poly(L,d,1/\lambda,1/\omega)
.

•

Stage II: Data-Limited Stage. If Eq. (
12
) holds for any dataset size
{\sf N}>0
, we have:

\displaystyle\inf_{{\cal F}_{\sf M,T,N}(\mathbb{D})}\sup_{\mathbb{D}\in{\cal D}}\Delta{\cal R}(F)\leq\xi^{5/3}\cdot O\left(\frac{{\sf C}}{W({\sf C}/\xi^{10})}\right)^{-1/6}\leq\xi^{5/3}\cdot\widetilde{O}\left({\sf C}\right)^{-1/6},

where
W(\cdot)
is the Lambert W function and
\widetilde{O}
hides
\log({\sf C})
.

Proof.

For an optimizing function
F\in{\cal F}_{\sf M,T,N}
and the empirical risk minimizer
F^{\prime}\in{\cal F}_{\sf M,+\infty,N}
, we have:

\displaystyle\Delta{\cal R}(F)\leq

\displaystyle~\|F-F^{*}\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\left(\|F-F^{\prime}\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}+\|F^{\prime}-F^{*}\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}\right)^{2},

where the first step follows from Eq. (
F.1
), the second step follows from simple algebras.

For the first term, we have:

\displaystyle~\max_{\nu\in[N]}\max_{r\in[m]}\|w_{(\nu),r}({\sf T})-w_{(\nu),r}(+\infty)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\int_{\sf T}^{\infty}\max_{\nu\in[N]}\max_{r\in[m]}\|\frac{\mathrm{d}{\cal L}(s,\mathbb{D})}{\mathrm{d}w_{(\nu),r}(s)}\|_{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\int_{\sf T}^{\infty}\varepsilon\cdot\sqrt{{\cal L}(s,\mathbb{D})}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\varepsilon\int_{\sf T}^{\infty}\exp(-\alpha\varepsilon^{2})^{s}\cdot\sqrt{{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D})}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{\alpha\varepsilon}\exp(-\alpha\varepsilon^{2})^{\sf T}\cdot\sqrt{{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D})}.

Following Part 12 of Lemma
C.1
and the definition of
F
, we get:

\displaystyle\|F-F^{\prime}\|_{L^{F}({\cal X})}\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{CL}{\alpha}\exp(-\alpha\varepsilon^{2})^{\sf T}\cdot\sqrt{{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D})}\leq\exp(-\alpha\varepsilon^{2})^{\sf T}\cdot\sqrt{{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D})},

where we omit the other constants in the second step.

Finally, we combine it with Theorem
F.6
and Eq. (
10
):

\displaystyle\inf_{{\cal F}_{\sf M,T,N}(\mathbb{D})}\sup_{\mathbb{D}\in{\cal D}}\sqrt{\Delta{\cal R}(F)}\leq

\displaystyle~\exp(-\alpha\varepsilon^{2})^{\sf T}\cdot\sqrt{{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D})}+\sqrt{4\varepsilon^{2}+Ld\cdot O(\frac{\xi^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}{\sf N}+\xi\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\exp(-\alpha\varepsilon^{2})^{\sf T}\cdot\sqrt{{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D})}+2\varepsilon+O(\frac{\xi}{{\sf N}^{1/2}}+(\xi\varepsilon)^{1/2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\exp(-\alpha\varepsilon^{2})^{\sf T}\cdot\sqrt{{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D})}+O(\frac{\xi}{{\sf N}^{1/2}}+(\xi\varepsilon)^{1/2}).

Here, for total computational resource
{\sf C}
with any number of dataset size
{\sf N}
, we follow Definition
5.2
to require
{\sf M}=\Theta({\sf N}^{3})
. Thus, we choose
\varepsilon=\xi/{\sf N}
.

Condition 1.
When:

\displaystyle~{\sf T}\leq\frac{\log(({\sf N}\wedge\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon})\cdot Ld/\xi^{2})}{\varepsilon^{2}}=\frac{{\sf N}^{2}\log({\sf N}\cdot Ld/\xi^{2})}{\xi^{2}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle~{\sf C}=O({\sf NMT})\leq\frac{{\sf MN}^{3}\log({\sf N}\cdot Ld/\xi^{2})}{\xi^{2}}=\frac{{\sf N}^{6}\log({\sf N}\cdot Ld/\xi^{2})}{\xi^{2}}

we have:

\displaystyle\inf_{{\cal F}_{\sf M,T,N}(\mathbb{D})}\sup_{\mathbb{D}\in{\cal D}}\Delta{\cal R}(F)\leq

\displaystyle~C\exp(-\alpha\xi^{2}{\sf T}/{\sf N}^{2})\cdot{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C\exp(-\alpha\xi^{2}{\sf NTM}/({\sf N}^{3}{\sf M}))\cdot{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C\exp(-\alpha\xi^{2}{\sf C}/({\sf N}^{3}{\sf M}))\cdot{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D})\leq C\exp(-\alpha\xi^{2}{\sf C}/{\sf N}^{6})\cdot{\cal L}(0,\mathbb{D}),

where the constant
C=\poly(L,d,1/\lambda,1/\omega)
.

Condition 2.
When:

\displaystyle{\sf T}>\frac{{\sf N}^{2}\log({\sf N}\cdot Ld/\xi^{2})}{\xi^{2}}\iff

\displaystyle~{\sf C}=O({\sf MTN})>\frac{{\sf M}{\sf N}^{3}\log({\sf N}\cdot Ld/\xi^{2})}{\xi^{2}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle~{\sf C}>\frac{{\sf N}^{6}\log({\sf N}\cdot Ld/\xi^{2})}{\xi^{2}}

Thus, we can get:

\displaystyle\inf_{{\cal F}_{\sf M,T,N}(\mathbb{D})}\sup_{\mathbb{D}\in{\cal D}}\Delta{\cal R}(F)\leq

\displaystyle~O(\frac{\xi^{2}}{{\sf N}}).

We choose a considerable value of
{\sf N}
to meet
{\sf C}>\frac{{\sf N}^{6}\log({\sf N}\cdot Ld/\xi^{2})}{\xi^{2}}
, the maximum solution is:

\displaystyle{\sf N}=O\left(\frac{{\sf C}\xi^{2}}{W({\sf C}/\xi^{10})}\right)^{1/6}.

Finally, we get:

\displaystyle\inf_{{\cal F}_{\sf M,T,N}(\mathbb{D})}\sup_{\mathbb{D}\in{\cal D}}\Delta{\cal R}(F)\leq

\displaystyle~\xi^{5/3}\cdot O\left(\frac{{\sf C}}{W({\sf C}/\xi^{10})}\right)^{-1/6}\leq\xi^{5/3}\cdot\widetilde{O}\left({\sf C}\right)^{-1/6},

where
W(\cdot)
is the Lambert W function.

∎

Theorem F.8

(Single law, formal version of Theorem
7.2
)
.

Let all pre-conditions hold as Corollary
E.3
. Then, with a probability at least
1-\delta
, there exists:

•

Time-Law.
We fix the dataset set
{\sf N}
and model size
{\sf M}=\Omega({\sf N}^{3})
, and choosing
\varepsilon=\xi/{\sf N}
, we have
\inf_{{\cal F}_{\sf M,T,N}(\mathbb{D})}\sup_{\mathbb{D}\in{\cal D}}\Delta{\cal R}(F)\leq\exp(-\varepsilon^{2}\xi^{2}{\sf T}/{\sf N}^{2})+O(\frac{\xi^{2}}{{\sf N}})
.

•

Data-Law.
For any dataset size
{\sf N}
, we let
\varepsilon=\xi/{\sf N}
,
{\sf M}=\Omega({\sf N}^{3})
and
T=\widetilde{\Omega}(\frac{{\sf N}^{2}}{\xi})
, we have:
\inf_{{\cal F}_{\sf M,T,N}(\mathbb{D})}\sup_{\mathbb{D}\in{\cal D}}\Delta{\cal R}(F)\leq O(\frac{\xi^{2}}{{\sf N}})
.

•

Model-Law.
We fix the dataset set
{\sf N}
and let training time
T=\widetilde{\Omega}(\frac{{\sf M}^{\zeta}}{\xi})
, where
\zeta\in(0,1/3)
. Choosing
\varepsilon=\xi/{\sf M}^{\zeta}
, before the model size
{\sf M}
is greater than
{\sf N}^{\frac{3}{2\zeta}}
, we have
\inf_{{\cal F}_{\sf M,T,N}(\mathbb{D})}\sup_{\mathbb{D}\in{\cal D}}

\Delta{\cal R}(F)\leq\xi^{2}{\sf M}^{-\zeta}
.

Proof.

Proof of Time-Law.
This proof could be directly derived from
Condition 1
in the proof of Theorem
F.7
.

Proof of Data-Law.
This proof could be directly derived from
Condition 2
in the proof of Theorem
F.7
.

Proof of Model-Law.
Given that
\zeta\in(0,1/3]
, we choose
\varepsilon=\xi/{\sf M}^{\zeta}
, similar to
Condition 2
in the proof of Theorem
F.7
, we choose
{\sf T}=\frac{{\sf M}^{2\zeta}\log({\sf N}\cdot Ld/\xi^{2})}{\xi^{2}}
, then we get:

\displaystyle{\sf C}=O({\sf MTN})=\frac{{\sf M}^{2\zeta+1}{\sf N}\log({\sf N}\cdot Ld/\xi^{2})}{\xi^{2}}\iff

\displaystyle~{\sf C}\leq\frac{{\sf M}^{2\zeta+4/3}}{\xi^{2}},

where the this step follows from
{\sf M}=\Omega({\sf N}^{3})
.

Since we would like to let the Model-Law take effect, we compute the boundary when
\xi^{2}/{\sf N}\leq\xi\varepsilon
, then get:

\displaystyle\inf_{{\cal F}_{\sf M,T,N}(\mathbb{D})}\sup_{\mathbb{D}\in{\cal D}}\Delta{\cal R}(F)\leq\xi^{2}{\sf M}^{-\zeta}

∎

Microlocal analysis of double fibration transforms with conjugate points Thanks: Supported by the JSPS Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research #23K03186.
Hiroyuki Chihara

Abstract
. We study the structure of normal operators of double fibration transforms with conjugate points. Examples of double fibration transforms include Radon transforms, d d -plane transforms on the Euclidean space, geodesic X-ray transforms, light-ray transforms, and ray transforms defined by null bicharacteristics associated with real principal type operators. We show that, under certain stable conditions on the distribution of conjugate points, the normal operator splits into an elliptic pseudodifferential operator and several Fourier integral operators, depending on the degree of the conjugate points. These results were first proved for geodesic X-ray transforms by Holman and Uhlmann (Journal of Differential Geometry, 108 (2018), pp.459–494).

Introduction

We study the structure of the normal operators of double fibration transforms using microlocal analysis. In particular, when conjugate points exist, we split the normal operators into several parts according to the degree of conjugate points. We begin with the
d
-plane transforms on the Euclidean space to explain the background and our motivation.

We now introduce the
d
-plane transform and state the inversion formula following Helgason’s celebrated textbook
[
7
]
. Let
n
be the space dimension of the Euclidean space not smaller than two, and let
d
be a positive integer strictly less than
n
. Denote by
G_{d,n}
and
G(d,n)
the Grassmannian and the affine Grassmannian respectively, that is,
G_{d,n}
is the set of all
d
-dimensional vector subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
G(d,n)
is the set of all
d
-dimensional affine subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any
\sigma\in G_{d,n}
, we have orthogonal direct sum

\mathbb{R}^{n}=\sigma\oplus\sigma^{\perp}
,
where
\sigma^{\perp}
is the orthogonal complement of
\sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For any fixed
\sigma\in G_{d,n}
,
we choose a coordinate system of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such as

x=x^{\prime}+x^{\prime\prime}\in\sigma\oplus\sigma^{\perp}
.
The affine Grassmannian
G(d,n)
is given by

G(d,n)=\{\sigma+x^{\prime\prime}:\sigma\in G_{d,n},x^{\prime\prime}\in\sigma^{\perp}\}.

The
d
-plane transform
R_{d}f(\sigma+x^{\prime\prime})
of an appropriate function
f(x)
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

R_{d}f(\sigma+x^{\prime\prime}):=\int_{\sigma}f(x^{\prime}+x^{\prime\prime})dx^{\prime},

where
dx^{\prime}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\sigma
.
The adjoint of
R_{d}
of a continuous function
\varphi
on
G(d,n)

is explicitly given by

R_{d}^{\ast}\varphi(x):=\frac{1}{C(d,n)}\int_{O(n)}\varphi(x+k\cdot\sigma)dk,

where
C(d,n)=(4\pi)^{d}\Gamma(n/2)/\Gamma((n-1)/2)
,

\Gamma(\cdot)
is the gamma function,

O(n)
is the orthogonal group,
and
dk
is the normalized measure
which is invariant under rotations,
and
\sigma\in G_{d,n}
is arbitrary.
The inversion formula for
R_{d}
is given as follows.

Theorem 1

(
[
7
, Theorem 6.2]
)
.

For
f(x)=\mathcal{O}(\langle{x}\rangle^{-d-\varepsilon})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

f=(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{d/2}R_{d}^{\ast}{R_{d}}f,

where
x=(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\langle{x}\rangle:=\sqrt{1+x_{1}^{2}+\dotsb+x_{n}^{2}}
,

-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:=-\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}-\dotsb-\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}
.

We can see the results of Theorem
1
as that the normal operator

R_{d}^{\ast}{R_{d}}
for
R_{d}
is an elliptic pseudodifferential operator
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{-d/2}

and
R_{d}^{\ast}{R_{d}}
has a local parametrix everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In other words, we can interpret the reason
why the inversion formula for
R_{d}
holds
is that the normal operator for
R_{d}
is an elliptic pseudodifferential operator.
More precisely,
the normal operator for
R_{d}
becomes an elliptic pseudodifferential operator
since the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has
no conjugate points as a Riemannian manifold.
Unfortunately, however, it might be impossible to notice this fact
if one study such a problem only on the Euclidean space.

So we now deal with geodesic X-ray transforms on Riemannian manifolds
and state the influence of conjugate points on the invertibility
following the pioneering work
[
9
]
of Holman and Uhlmann.
Roughly speaking,
the results of
[
9
]
show that if there is no conjugate point,
then the normal operator of the geodesic X-ray transform
becomes an elliptic pseudodifferential operator of order
-1
and has local parametrics,
otherwise, the normal operator splits into an elliptic pseudodifferential operator
and several Fourier integral operators according to the degree of conjugate points.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional compact non-trapping Riemannian manifold with strictly convex boundary. We say that a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is nontrapping if all geodesics exit
M^{\text{int}}
in finite time.
Denote by
\nu(x)
the unit outer normal vector at
x\in\partial{M}
. Set

\partial_{-}SM:=\{(x,v)\in SM:x\in\partial{M},\langle{v,\nu(x)}\rangle<0\},

which is the set of all the incoming unit tangent vector on
\partial{M}
.
For any normal geodesic
\gamma
there exists a unique element
(x,v)\in\partial_{-}SM

such that
\bigl(\gamma(0),\dot{\gamma}(0)\bigr)=(x,v)
.
The exit time of
\gamma
is denoted by
\tau(x,v)
.

\partial_{-}SM
can be identified with the space of all the normal geodesics on
M
.
The geodesic X-ray transform
\mathcal{X}f(\gamma)=\mathcal{X}f(x,\gamma)

of
f\in\mathcal{D}(M^{\text{int}},\Omega_{M^{\text{int}}}^{1/2})
is defined by

\mathcal{X}f(\gamma)=\mathcal{X}f(x,v):=\left(\int_{0}^{\tau(x,v)}w\bigl(x,v,\gamma(t)\bigr)\frac{f}{\lvert{dM}\rvert^{1/2}}\bigl(\gamma(t)\bigr)dt\right)\lvert{d\partial_{-}SM(x,v)}\rvert^{1/2},

where
w\in C^{\infty}(\partial_{-}SM\times M^{\text{int}})
is a nowhere vanishing weight function,

dM
is the Riemannian measure induced by the metric
g
,

\mathcal{D}(M^{\text{int}},\Omega_{M^{\text{int}}}^{1/2})

is the space of all compactly supported smooth half densities on
M^{\text{int}}
,
and
d\partial_{-}SM
is the Riemannian measure induced by the Sasaki metric.
In
[
9
]
Holman and Uhlmann introduced the notion of regular conjugate points.
We now state it in a seemingly different form as follows.
Let
(x,v)\in\partial_{-}SM
, let
\gamma
be the corresponding normal geodesic,
and let
y_{0}=\gamma(t_{0})
and
y_{1}=\gamma(t_{1})
with
t_{0}\neq{t_{1}}
.
We say that
\bigl((x,v\bigr),y_{0},y_{1})
is a conjugate triplet of degree
k=1,\dotsc,n-1

if
y_{1}
is a conjugate point of
y_{0}
along
\gamma
of degree
k
.
Moreover we say that
\bigl((x,v\bigr),y_{0},y_{1})

is a
regular
conjugate triplet of degree
k
if there exist
a neighborhood
V
of
(x,v)
in
\partial_{-}SM
,
a neighborhood
U_{0}
of
y_{0}
in
M^{\text{int}}
,
and
a neighborhood
U_{1}
of
y_{0}
in
M^{\text{int}}
,
such that every conjugate triplet

\bigl((x^{\prime},v^{\prime}\bigr),y_{0}^{\prime},y_{1}^{\prime})\in V\times U_{0}\times U_{1}

is also of degree
k
.
The main results of Holman and Uhlmann
[
9
]
are the following.

Theorem 2

(
[
9
, Theorem 4 in page 481]
)
.

Suppose that all the conjugate triplets are regular.
Then the normal operator
\mathcal{X}^{\ast}\mathcal{X}
is decomposed as

\mathcal{X}^{\ast}\mathcal{X}=P+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{l=1}^{M_{k}}A_{k,l},

where
P
is a pseudodifferential operator of order
-1
,
and for
k=1,\dots,n-1
and
l=1,\dots,M_{k}

A_{k,l}\in\mathcal{I}^{-(n+1-k)/2}(M^{\text{int}}{\times}M^{\text{int}},C_{A_{k,l}}^{\prime},\Omega^{1/2}_{M^{\text{int}}{\times}M^{\text{int}}}),

\mathcal{I}^{-(n+1-k)/2}(M^{\text{int}}{\times}M^{\text{int}},C_{A_{k,l}}^{\prime},\Omega^{1/2}_{M^{\text{int}}{\times}M^{\text{int}}})

is the standard notation of the class of Lagrangian distributions

(
See, e.g.,
[
11
]
)
,

C_{A_{k,l}}
is the canonical relation of
A_{k,l}
and does not contains a diagonal part,

k(=1,\dotsc,n-1)
describe the degree of the conjugate triples,

l(=1,\dotsc,M_{k})
correspond to connected components of the set of regular conjugate triplets of degree
k
.

Theorem
2
shows that if there are conjugate points and all the conjugate triplets are regular, the normal operator
\mathcal{X}^{\ast}\mathcal{X}
contains Fourier integral operators which are essentially different from elliptic pseudodifferential operators. So we cannot expect the existence of a parametrix of the normal operator. What happens to the normal operators if conjugate points arise? Actually so-called the Bolker condition fails to hold and several Fourier integral operators arise in the normal operator when there are conjugate points.

In this paper, we show Theorem
2
in the context of double fibration transforms, which are generalizations of integral transforms arising in integral geometry and the microlocal analysis of various real principal type pseudodifferential operators. The double fibration approach to integral geometry was initiated by Helgason
[
7
]
. See also Gelfand, Graev and Shapiro
[

]
, Guillemin and Sternberg

]

]
, Quinto

, and some other references. We now state the setting of the double fibration in this paper following the recent challenging paper

\mathcal{G}
and

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that

Proof.

We shall show only

Fix arbitrary
(z,x)\in Z

Fix arbitrary
c_{0}\in E
, and pick up arbitrary

\Xi=(\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2},\Xi_{3},\Xi_{4})\in T_{c_{0}}(\mathcal{C}^{T}\times\mathcal{C})
.
Consider a smooth curve in
\mathcal{C}^{T}\times\mathcal{C}
of the form

c(s)=c_{0}+s\Xi+\mathcal{O}(s^{2})=c_{0}+s(\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2},\Xi_{3},\Xi_{4})+\mathcal{O}(s^{2})\quad\text{near}\quad s=0.

If
\Xi\in T_{c_{0}}\Delta
, then
\Xi_{2}=\Xi_{3}
,
and moreover the injectivity of
D\pi_{\text{L}}
implies that
\Xi_{1}=\Xi_{4}
.
Thus we obtain
\Xi=(\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2},\Xi_{2},\Xi_{1})\in T_{c_{0}}E
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Normal operators with conjugate points

Finally in this section we study the normal operator
\mathcal{R}^{\ast}\mathcal{R}

with regular
Z
-conjugate triplets.
Suppose that
Z
is a double fibration, and split
C_{R,k}
into the disjoint union of connected components:

C_{R,k}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{k}}C_{R,k,\alpha},

where
k=1,\dotsc,n^{\prime\prime}
and

\Lambda_{k}
(
k=1,\dotsc,n^{\prime\prime}
) are the sets of indices of connected components.
We now remark that
C_{R,k}
(
k=1,\dotsc,n^{\prime\prime}
) are disjoint and then
so are
C_{R,k,\alpha}
(
k=1,\dotsc,n^{\prime\prime}
,
\alpha\in\Lambda_{k}
).
We now state the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 14
.

Suppose that
Z
is a double fibration.
In addition we assume the following conditions.

•

C_{S}=\emptyset
.

•

N\geqq(n-1)+n^{\prime\prime}
.

•

\pi_{X}
is proper, and
\pi_{X}^{-1}(x)
is connected for any
x\in X
.

•

If
\pi_{\text{L}}^{-1}\bigl((z,\zeta)\bigr)=\{(z,\zeta,x,\eta)\}
for
(z,\zeta,x,\eta)\in\mathcal{C}
, then
D\pi_{\text{L}}|_{(z,\zeta,x,\eta)}
is injective.

Then we have a decomposition of
\mathcal{R}^{\ast}\mathcal{R}
of the form

\mathcal{R}^{\ast}\mathcal{R}=P+\sum_{k=1}^{n^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{k}}A_{k,\alpha},

where
P
is an elliptic pseudodifferential operator of order
-n^{\prime}
on
X
,

A_{k,\alpha}
is a Fourier integral operator whose distribution kernel belongs to

\mathcal{I}^{-(n+n^{\prime}-k)/2}(X{\times}X,\mathcal{C}_{A_{k,\alpha}}^{\prime};\Omega_{X{\times}X}^{1/2}),

and the canonical relation of
A_{k,\alpha}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{A_{k,\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\{(x,\eta;y,\tilde{\eta})\in T^{\ast}{X}\setminus 0\times T^{\ast}{X}\setminus 0:\exists(z,\zeta)\in N^{\ast}{H_{x}}{\cap}N^{\ast}{H_{y}}\ \text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\qquad(z;x,y)\in C_{R,k,\alpha},\ \eta=B(z,x)\zeta,\ \tilde{\eta}=B(z,y)\zeta\}.

(8)

Proof.

The strategy of the proof is basically due to Holman and Uhlmann
[
9
]
.
Set
C_{\delta}:=\{(z;x,x):z\in\mathcal{G},x\in X\}
.
Since
C_{S}=\emptyset
and

C_{R,k,\alpha}
(
k=1,\dotsc,n^{\prime\prime}
,
\alpha\in\Lambda_{k}
) are disjoint,
we deduce that
C_{\delta}
and
C_{R,k,\alpha}
are mutually disjoint for any

k=1,\dotsc,n^{\prime\prime}
and
\alpha\in\Lambda_{k}
.
Furthermore we deduce the following:

•

There exist an open neighborhood
U_{\delta}
of
C_{\delta}
in
\mathcal{G}{\times}X{\times}X
and
open neighborhoods
U_{k,\alpha}
of
C_{k,\alpha}
in
\mathcal{G}{\times}X{\times}X
such that

\overline{U_{\delta}}
and all the
\overline{U_{k,\alpha}}
are mutually disjoint.

•

There exist compactly supported smooth functions

\varphi_{\delta}(z,x,y)
and
\varphi_{k,\alpha}(z,x,y)

on
\mathcal{G}{\times}X{\times}X
such that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqq\varphi_{\delta}\leqq 1,

\displaystyle\quad\varphi_{\delta}

\displaystyle=1\quad\text{on}\quad C_{\delta},

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{supp}[\varphi_{\delta}]

\displaystyle\subset U_{\delta},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqq\varphi_{k,\alpha}\leqq 1,

\displaystyle\quad\varphi_{k,\alpha}

\displaystyle=1\quad\text{on}\quad C_{k,\alpha},

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{supp}[\varphi_{\delta}]

\displaystyle\subset U_{k,\alpha}.

•

If we set

\varphi_{0}(z,x,y):=1-\varphi_{\delta}(z,x,y)-\sum_{k=1}^{n^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{k}}\varphi_{k,\alpha}(z,x,y),

then

0\leqq\varphi_{0}\leqq 1,\qquad\varphi_{0}=1\quad\text{on}\quad(\mathcal{G}{\times}X{\times}X)\setminus\left(U_{\delta}\bigcup\left(\displaystyle\cup_{k=1}^{n^{\prime\prime}}\displaystyle\cup_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{k}}U_{k,\alpha}\right)\right),

U_{0}:=\operatorname{supp}[\varphi_{0}]\subset(\mathcal{G}{\times}X{\times}X)\setminus\left(C_{\delta}\bigcup\left(\displaystyle\cup_{k=1}^{n^{\prime\prime}}\displaystyle\cup_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{k}}C_{R,k,\alpha}\right)\right).

The set of nonnegative smooth functions

\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{\delta}\}\cup\{\varphi_{k,\alpha}\}

forms a partition of unity on
\mathcal{G}{\times}X{\times}X
.
We substitute this into (
7
) to obtain

\mathcal{R}^{\ast}\mathcal{R}f(x)=A_{0}f(x)+A_{\delta}{f}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{n^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{k}}A_{k,\alpha}f(x),

\displaystyle\frac{A_{0}f}{\lvert{dX}\rvert^{1/2}}(x)

\displaystyle=\iint_{H_{x}{\times}G_{z}}\varphi_{0}(z,x,y)\overline{\kappa(z,x)}\kappa(z,y)\frac{f}{\lvert{dX}\rvert^{1/2}}(x)dG_{z}(y)dH_{x}(z),

\displaystyle\frac{A_{\delta}f}{\lvert{dX}\rvert^{1/2}}(x)

\displaystyle=\iint_{H_{x}{\times}G_{z}}\varphi_{\delta}(z,x,y)\overline{\kappa(z,x)}\kappa(z,y)\frac{f}{\lvert{dX}\rvert^{1/2}}(x)dG_{z}(y)dH_{x}(z),

\displaystyle\frac{A_{k,\alpha}f}{\lvert{dX}\rvert^{1/2}}(x)

\displaystyle=\iint_{H_{x}{\times}G_{z}}\varphi_{k,\alpha}(z,x,y)\overline{\kappa(z,x)}\kappa(z,y)\frac{f}{\lvert{dX}\rvert^{1/2}}(x)dG_{z}(y)dH_{x}(z),

(9)

for
f\in\mathscr{D}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1/2})
.
We remark that

\operatorname{supp}[\varphi_{0}]\cup\operatorname{supp}[\varphi_{\delta}]

do not contain
Z
-conjugate triplets,
and that the injectivity of
\pi_{L}
holds at all the points given by

\bigl(z,\zeta,x,B(z,x)\zeta\bigr),\bigl(z,\zeta,y,B(z,y)\zeta\bigr)\in\mathcal{C},\quad(z,x,y)\in\operatorname{supp}[\varphi_{0}]\cup\operatorname{supp}[\varphi_{\delta}].

(10)

Hence the assumption on
D\pi_{L}
ensures
that the Bolker condition holds all the points given by (
10
).
Using the assumptions on
\pi_{X}
,
we deduce that
A_{\delta}
is an elliptic pseudodifferential operator of order
-n^{\prime}
in the same way as the proof of Theorem
13
,
and that
A_{0}
is a pseudodifferential operator of order
-\infty
.
Thus we set
P:=A_{\delta}+A_{0}
.

In what follows we concentrate on the analysis of
A_{k,\alpha}
.
We first prepare the intersection calculus for
A_{k,\alpha}
.
We remark that the contribution of
(z,x,y)\in U_{k,\alpha}\setminus{C_{R,k,\alpha}}

in (
9
) can be contained in
A_{0}
, and we have only to consider

(z,x,y)\in C_{R,k,\alpha}
in (
9
).
It is obvious that the canonical relation
\mathcal{C}_{A_{k,\alpha}}
is given by
(
8
) as a result of the computation of the composition

\mathcal{C}^{T}\circ\mathcal{C}
with the restriction
(z;x,y)\in C_{R,k,\alpha}
.

We now define a subset
D_{k,\alpha}
and
E_{k,\alpha}
of

T^{\ast}{X}{\times}T^{\ast}\mathcal{G}{\times}T^{\ast}\mathcal{G}{\times}T^{\ast}{X}
by

\displaystyle D_{k,\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\{(x,\eta,z,\zeta;\tilde{z},\tilde{\zeta},y,\tilde{\eta})\in\mathcal{C}^{T}\times\mathcal{C}:

\displaystyle\qquad\exists y_{1},\exists x_{1}\in X\ \text{s.t.}\ (z;x,y_{1}),(\tilde{z};x_{1},y)\in C_{R,k,\alpha}\}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\bigl(x,B(z,x)\zeta,z,\zeta;\tilde{z},\tilde{\zeta},y,B(z,y)\tilde{\zeta}\bigr):\zeta\in N^{\ast}_{z}H_{x}\setminus\{0\},\tilde{\zeta}\in N^{\ast}_{\tilde{z}}H_{y}\setminus\{0\},

\displaystyle\qquad\exists y_{1},\exists x_{1}\in X\ \text{s.t.}\ (z;x,y_{1}),(\tilde{z};x_{1},y)\in C_{R,k,\alpha}\bigr\},

\displaystyle E_{k,\alpha}

\displaystyle:=D_{k,\alpha}\cap\Delta

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\bigl(x,B(z,x)\zeta,z,\zeta;z,\zeta,y,B(z,y)\zeta\bigr):\zeta\in(N^{\ast}_{z}H_{x}{\cap}N^{\ast}_{z}H_{y})\setminus\{0\},

\displaystyle\qquad\exists y_{1},\exists x_{1}\in X\ \text{s.t.}\ (z;x,y_{1}),(z;x_{1},y)\in C_{R,k,\alpha}\bigr\},

where
\Delta=T^{\ast}{X}\times\Delta(T^{\ast}\mathcal{G}){\times}T^{\ast}{X}

was defined in the previous section.
The projection
\pi_{E_{k,\alpha}}
is defined by

\pi_{E_{k,\alpha}}:E_{k,\alpha}\ni(x,\eta,z,\zeta,z,\zeta,y,\tilde{\eta})\mapsto(x,\eta,y,\tilde{\eta})\in T^{\ast}{X}{\times}T^{\ast}{X}.

Then we have
\pi_{E_{k,\alpha}}(E_{k,\alpha})=\mathcal{C}_{A_{k,\alpha}}
.

To complete the proof of Theorem
14
, we have only to show the following:

•

e:=\operatorname{codim}(D_{k,\alpha})+\operatorname{codim}\bigl(\Delta(T^{\ast}\mathcal{G})\bigr)-\operatorname{codim}(E_{k,\alpha})=N-2n+n^{\prime}+k
, and the order of
A_{k,\alpha}
is
-(n+n^{\prime}-k)/2
.

•

\pi_{E_{k,\alpha}}
is a proper mapping, and

\pi_{E_{k,\alpha}}^{-1}\bigl((x,\eta,x,\eta)\bigr)
is connected.

•

For any
c_{0}\in E_{k,\alpha}
,
if
\Xi\in T_{c_{0}}(D_{k,\alpha}){\cap}T_{c_{0}}\Delta
,
then
\Xi\in T_{c_{0}}E_{k,\alpha}
.

We compute the excess
e
. Note that

\displaystyle\dim(D_{k,\alpha})

\displaystyle=2\dim(N^{\ast}{C_{R,k,\alpha}})=2\dim(N^{\ast}{Z})=2N+2n,

\displaystyle\operatorname{codim}(\Delta)

\displaystyle=\dim(T^{\ast}\mathcal{G})=2N,

\displaystyle\dim(E_{k,\alpha})

\displaystyle=\dim(C_{R,k,\alpha})+\dim(N^{\ast}_{z}H_{x}{\cap}N^{\ast}_{z}H_{y})=N+n^{\prime}+k.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle=\operatorname{codim}(D_{k,\alpha})+\operatorname{codim}\bigl(\Delta(T^{\ast}\mathcal{G})\bigr)-\operatorname{codim}(E_{k,\alpha})

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{dim}(D_{k,\alpha})+\operatorname{codim}\bigl(\Delta(T^{\ast}\mathcal{G})\bigr)+\operatorname{dim}(E_{k,\alpha})

\displaystyle=-(2N+2n)+(2N)+(N+n^{\prime}+k)=N-2n+n^{\prime}+k.

We now remark that

e=N-(n-k)-n^{\prime\prime}\geqq N-(n-1)-n^{\prime\prime}\geqq 0.

The order of
A_{k,\alpha}
is given by

\displaystyle\text{the order of $\mathcal{R}^{\ast}$}\ +\ \text{the order of $\mathcal{R}$}\ +\ \frac{e}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{N+2n^{\prime}-n}{2}+\frac{N-2n+n^{\prime}+k}{2}=-\frac{n+n^{\prime}-k}{2}.

We check the required properties of
\pi_{E_{k,\alpha}}
.
We use the linear mapping
A(z,x)
to have

\pi_{E_{k,\alpha}}:E\ni(x,\eta,z,A(z,x)\eta,z,A(z,y)\tilde{\eta},y,\tilde{\eta})\mapsto(x,\eta,y,\tilde{\eta})\in T^{\ast}{X}{\times}T^{\ast}{X}

with
A(z,x)\eta=A(z,y)\tilde{\eta}
.
Then we have

\pi_{E_{k,\alpha}}^{-1}\bigl((x,\eta,y,\tilde{\eta})\bigr)=\{(x,\eta,z,A(z,x)\eta,z,A(z,y)\tilde{\eta},y,\tilde{\eta}):z\in H_{x}{\cap}H_{y}=\pi_{\mathcal{G}}(\pi_{X}^{-1}(x)\cap\pi_{X}^{-1}(y))\}.

We deduce that
\pi_{E_{k,\alpha}}
is proper since
\pi_{X}
is proper,
and that
\pi_{E_{k,\alpha}}^{-1}\bigl((x,\eta,y,\tilde{\eta})\bigr)
is connected
since
\pi_{X}^{-1}(x)\cap\pi_{X}^{-1}(y)
is connected and

A(z,x)
and
A(z,y)
are linear mappings depending smoothly on
(z,x)
and
(z,y)
respectively.

Finally we show that
E_{k,\alpha}=D_{k,\alpha}\cap\Delta
is a clean intersection.
Fix arbitrary
c_{0}\in E_{k,\alpha}
, and pick up arbitrary

\Xi=(\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2},\Xi_{3},\Xi_{4})\in T_{c_{0}}D_{k,\alpha}
.
Consider a smooth curve in
D_{k,\alpha}
of the form

c(s)=c_{0}+s\Xi+\mathcal{O}(s^{2})=c_{0}+s(\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2},\Xi_{3},\Xi_{4})+\mathcal{O}(s^{2})\quad\text{near}\quad s=0.

If
\Xi\in T_{c_{0}}\Delta
, then
\Xi_{2}=\Xi_{3}
.
Thus we obtain
\Xi=(\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2},\Xi_{2},\Xi_{4})\in T_{c_{0}}E_{k,\alpha}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

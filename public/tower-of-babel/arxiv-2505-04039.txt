1.
Introduction

1.1.

In his study of zeta functions of families of algebraic varieties
[
Dw69
]
Dwork encountered remarkable congruences. These congruences are satisfied by truncations of the period integrals, which describe the Gauss-Manin connection for such families. The basic example here is the Legendrian family of elliptic curves

(1.1)

\displaystyle y^{2}=x(1-x)(1-zx)

parametrized by
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
. Let
\omega=dx/y
be the holomorphic differential on the curve and let

(1.2)

\displaystyle F(z)=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\oint_{\gamma}\omega=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\,\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{x(1-x)(1-zx)}}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{-1/2}{n}^{2}z^{n}\,

be its period over the
a
-cycle
\gamma
. As a function of the parameter
z
this period is described by the classical hypergeometric series

(1.3)

\displaystyle F(z)={}_{2}F_{1}(1/2,1/2;1;z)

which satisfies the well-known hypergeometric differential equation.

Let
p
be a prime number. Dwork found that the truncated power series

(1.4)

\displaystyle T_{s}(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{p^{s}-1}\binom{-1/2}{n}^{2}z^{n},\ \ \ s=1,2,\dots

satisfy the following congruences

\dfrac{T_{s+1}(z)}{T_{s}(z^{p})}\equiv\dfrac{T_{s}(z)}{T_{s-1}(z^{p})}\,\ \ \pmod{p^{s}}

Let
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be a
p
-adic integer with residue
z\pmod{p}=z_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Among other things, the congruences (
1.4
) imply that over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
the limit

(1.5)

\displaystyle\lambda(z_{0})=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\lim\limits_{s\to\infty}\dfrac{T_{s+1}(z)}{T_{s}(z^{p})}

exists and is equal to the unit root of zeta function of the curve (
1.1
) with parameter
z_{0}
. The second root is equal to
p/\lambda(z_{0})
. In this way, the congruences (
1.4
) fully determine the zeta function of (
1.1
), i.e., the numbers of points on (
1.1
) over finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{p^{l}}
.

1.2.

Since then Dwork’s results have been expanded considerably and general theories explaining the underlying mechanism of such congruences have been developed, see
[
K85
,
BV1
]
for examples.

It was observed in
[
SV
]
that interesting examples of Dwork congruences appear naturally for the
cohomological vertex functions
of Nakajima varieties. These functions control the equivariant quantum cohomology of quiver varieties
[
MO19
]
and
can be viewed as far reaching generalizations of the classical hypergeometric series. In fact, the classical hypergeometric functions are the vertex functions for the simplest Nakajima varieties given by the cotangent bundles over projective spaces

T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

In
[
SV
]
an analog of truncations (
1.4
) for the vertex functions of the cotangent bundles over Grassmannians
T^{*}Gr(k,n)
were considered. It was proven that these polynomials also satisfy the Dwork’s congruences.

It is known that cohomological vertex functions are limits of a more general and richer structures - the
K-theoretic vertex functions

[
Oko17
]
. For simple cases, e.g.,
T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{n}
these functions are given by the standard
q
-hypergeometric series. The cohomological vertex functions can be viewed as specializations at
q=1
of the K-theoretic vertex functions.

It is therefore natural to ask whether a natural
q
-deformation of the Dwork’s congruences exists. In this paper we give a positive answer to this question: we consider the same Nakajima variety
T^{*}Gr(k,n)
as in
[
SV
]
and for a prime
p
we define polynomials

T_{s}(z,q)\in\mathbb{Z}[z,q],\ \ s\in\mathbb{N},

which are certain truncations of the corresponding K-theoretic vertex function. Specialized at
q=1
these polynomials coincide with the truncations considered in
[
SV
]
. As our main result, we prove the following

Theorem 1.1
.

The polynomials
T_{s}(z,q)
satisfy the
q
-deformed Dwork’s congruences

(1.6)

\displaystyle\dfrac{T_{s+1}(z,q)}{T_{s}(z^{p},q^{p})}\equiv\dfrac{T_{s}(z,q)}{T_{s-1}(z^{p},q^{p})}\pmod{[p^{s}]_{q}}

Let us explain the notations. For a natural number
n
we denote by

[n]_{q}=\dfrac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}=1+q+\dots+q^{n-1}

the corresponding
q
-number. In particular, for a prime
p
,
[p]_{q}=1+q+\dots+q^{p-1}=\varphi_{p}(q)
is the
p
-th cyclotomic polynomial. For a power
p^{s}
we have

(1.7)

\displaystyle[p^{s}]_{q}=\varphi_{p}(q)\varphi_{p}(q^{p})\dots\varphi_{p}(q^{p^{s-1}})

The congruence (
1.6
) means that the difference of the left and right sides is divisible by the polynomial
[p^{s}]_{q}
.

In the limit
q\to 1
, the polynomials
T(z,q)
specialize to the polynomials
T_{s}(z)
considered in
[
SV
]
. The
q
-number
[p^{s}]_{q}
specializes to
p^{s}
in this limit and as a simple corollary of the above Theorem we arrive at the main result of
[
SV
]
.

1.3.

We note that compared to
[
SV
]
our proof of
q
-congruences is completely elementary. To prove Theorem
1.1
it is enough to check that the congruence (
1.6
) becomes identity

\dfrac{T_{s+1}(z,q)}{T_{s}(z^{p},q^{p})}=\dfrac{T_{s}(z,q)}{T_{s-1}(z^{p},q^{p})}

when
q
is specialized to a non-trivial root of equation
X^{p^{s}}=1
(note that
[p^{s}]_{q}=0
when
q
is such a root). The combinatorial proof we describe here is essentially by iterative use of an elementary identity:

(1-x)(1-xq)\dots(1-xq^{l-1})=(1-x^{l})

when
q
is a primitive
l
-th unit root.

1.4.

As in (
1.5
), the
q
-congruences (
1.6
) imply that the limit

\lambda(z,q)=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\lim\limits_{s\to\infty}\dfrac{T_{s+1}(z,q)}{T_{s}(z^{p},q^{p})}

considered over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, exists. In fact it follows from our proof that when
q
is specialized to a unit root

\lambda(z,q)=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\lim\limits_{s\to\infty}\,\dfrac{T_{s+1}(z,q)}{T_{s}(z^{p},q^{p})}=\dfrac{T_{L+1}(z,q)}{T_{L}(z^{p},q^{p})}

for sufficiently large
L
. In particular, for
q
given by
p
-adic unit roots,
\lambda(z,q)
is a rational function of
z
.

The element
\lambda(z,q)
is a certain
q
-deformation of the unit root for the corresponding zeta function, as we discussed above. We do not know if the
q
-deformation
\lambda(z,q)
has
a similar arithmetic significance, or if it is related to natural
q
-deformations arising in arithmetic geometry
[
SCH
]
. One may expect that
\lambda(z,q)
is one of the “eigenvalues” of the
q
-deformed Frobenius intertwiner considered for the

q
-hypergeometric case in
[
S
]
, see also
[
KS
]
.

2.
Polynomials
T_{s}(z,q)

\Phi(x,z):=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{\text{v}_{i}}x_{i,j}\right)^{-1+\omega}\times\prod_{m=1}^{n-1}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant\text{v}_{m}}(x_{m,i}-x_{m,j})^{2\omega}\\

\Phi_{s}(x,z):=(\Delta(x))^{p^{s}}\times(\Phi(x,z))^{1-p^{s}}

The polynomials

2.2.
q
-deformation

Consider the following
q
-deformation of
\overline{\Phi}_{s}(x,z)
given by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi}_{s}(x,z,q)=(\prod_{m=1}^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{\text{v}_{m}}x_{m,i})^{(p^{s}-1)(1-\omega)}\times(\prod_{m=1}^{n-1}\prod_{1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant\text{v}_{m}}\prod_{r=0}^{\frac{(p^{s}-1)(1-2\omega)}{2}-1}(x_{m,i}-q^{r}x_{m,j}))

\displaystyle\times(\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}\prod_{a=1}^{\text{v}_{i}}\prod_{b=1}^{\text{v}_{i+1}}\prod_{r=0}^{(p^{s}-1)\omega-1}(x_{i,a}-q^{r}x_{i+1,\ b}))\times(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{r=0}^{(p^{s}-1)\omega-1}(z-q^{r}x_{k,i})(1-q^{r}x_{n-k,\ i}))

And we define

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}(x,z,q):=\Delta(x)\overline{\Phi}_{s}(x,z,q)

The group
S_{\text{v}}:=S_{\text{v}_{1}}\times\dots\times S_{\text{v}_{n-1}}
acts on the set of variables
\{x_{i,j}\}_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1,\ 1\leqslant j\leqslant v_{i}}
by permuting the coordinates as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma\cdot x_{ij}=x_{i\sigma(j)}

Note that in our definition
\overline{\Phi}_{s}
is symmetric under
S_{\text{v}}
, and
\Delta
is skew symmetric, so
\Phi_{s}
is skew symmetric.
In the
q=1

\overline{\Phi}_{s}(x,z,q)
specializes to
(-1)^{\theta_{s}}\overline{\Phi}_{s}(x,z)
, where

\theta_{s}:=\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\frac{\text{v}_{m}(\text{v}_{m}-1)}{2}\right)\frac{(p^{s}-1)(1-2\omega)}{2}

We define polynomials
T_{s}(z,q)\in\mathbb{Z}[z,q]
by

(2.6)

\displaystyle T_{s}(z,q)=(-1)^{(p^{s}-1)N\omega}\text{coeff}_{x^{dp^{s}-1}}\Phi_{s}(x,z,q)

with the same notations as above. Note that at
q=1
, the polynomials
\Phi_{s}(x,z,q)
and
T_{s}(z,q)
specialize to
(-1)^{\theta_{s}}\Phi_{s}(x,z)
and
(-1)^{\theta_{s}}T_{s}(z)
respectively.

Example:

If
n=2
,
\omega=\frac{1}{2}
and

\Phi_{s}(x,z,q)=x^{\frac{(p^{s}-1)}{2}}\times\prod_{r=0}^{\frac{(p^{s}-1)}{2}-1}(z-q^{r}x)(1-q^{r}x)

and thus

T_{s}(z,q)=\text{coeff}_{x^{p^{s}-1}}\left((-1)^{\frac{(p^{s}-1)}{2}}x^{\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}}\prod_{r=0}^{\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}-1}(z-q^{r}x)(1-q^{r}x)\right).

3.
q
-deformation of Dwork’s congruences

3.1.
q
-deformed Dwork’s congruences

Here is our main result.

Theorem 3.1
.

The polynomials
T_{s}(z,q)
satisfy
q
-deformed Dwork’s congruence

(3.1)

\displaystyle\dfrac{T_{s+1}(z,q)}{T_{s}(z^{p},q^{p})}\equiv\dfrac{T_{s}(z,q)}{T_{s-1}(z^{p},q^{p})}\pmod{[p^{s}]_{q}}

where
[p^{s}]_{q}
denotes the
q
-number (

Proof.

It is enough to show that

\frac{T_{s+1}(z,q)}{T_{s}(z^{p},q^{p})}=\frac{T_{s}(z,q)}{T_{s-1}(z^{p},q^{p})}

when
q
is a root of
X^{p^{s}}=1,\ q\neq 1
.

We can find bounds on the degree of each variable
x_{ij}
by considering the following cases:

•

If
k=1
, then
\text{v}_{i}=1
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
. If
i\neq 1
and
i\neq n-1
, then by a degree count of (
2.5
), we conclude that

\text{deg}_{x_{ij}}(\Phi_{s})=(p^{s}-1)(1-\omega)+2(p^{s}-1)\omega

•

If
k=1
and
i=1
or
i=n-1
, then by a similar degree counting argument of (
2.5
) we get

\text{deg}_{x_{ij}}(\Phi_{s})=(p^{s}-1)(1-\omega)+(p^{s}-1)\omega-1+(p^{s}-1)\omega-1

•

If
k\neq 1
and
i=1
or
i=n-1
, then
v_{i}=1
, so the degree of
x_{i,1}
in
\Phi_{s}(x,z)

\text{deg}_{x_{ij}}(\Phi_{s}(x,z,q))=(p^{s}-1)(1-\omega)+(p^{s}-1)\omega=\text{v}_{i}p^{s}-1

•

If
k\neq 1

i\neq 1

\text{deg}_{x_{ij}}(\Phi_{s}(x,z,q))=\text{v}_{i}p^{s}-1+\omega(p^{s}-1)

So we observe that in each of these cases we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\text{deg}_{x_{ij}}(\Phi_{s}(x,z,q))<(\text{v}_{i}+1)p^{s}-1

S_{\text{v}}:=S_{\text{v}_{1}}\times\dots\times S_{\text{v}_{n-1}}

\displaystyle\sigma\cdot x_{ij}=x_{i\sigma(j)}

\displaystyle=p^{l}\left(\frac{(p^{s-l}-1)(1-2\omega)}{2}\right)

\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}\prod_{a=1}^{\text{v}_{i}}\prod_{b=1}^{\text{v}_{i+1}}\prod_{r=0}^{(p^{l}-1)\omega-1}(x_{i,a}-q^{r}x_{i+1,\ b})\times(\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}\prod_{a=1}^{\text{v}_{i}}\prod_{b=1}^{\text{v}_{i+1}}(x_{i,a}^{p^{l}}-x_{i+1,\ b}^{p^{l}}))^{(p^{s-l}-1)\omega}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s-1}(x^{p},z^{p},q^{p})

\displaystyle=\Phi_{l-1}(x^{p},z^{p},q^{p})\overline{\Phi}_{s-l}(x^{p^{l}},z^{p^{l}},1)

Dividing (
3.9
) by (
3.11
), and (
3.10
) by (
3.12
) we conclude

(3.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi_{s+1}(x,z,q)}{\Phi_{s}(x^{p},z^{p},q^{p})}=\frac{\Phi_{s}(x,z,q)}{\Phi_{s-1}(x^{p},z^{p},q^{p})}

Let
d=(d^{(i)}_{j})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1,\ 1\leqslant j\leqslant\text{v}_{i}}
with
d^{(i)}_{j}=j
be a degree vector and let

(3.14)

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{s}(z):=\text{coeff}_{x^{dp^{s}-1}}(\Phi_{s}(x,z)),\ \ T^{\prime}_{s}(z,q):=\text{coeff}_{x^{dp^{s}-1}}(\Phi_{s}(x,z,q)),

i.e., the coefficients of the monomial

x^{dp^{s}-1}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{\text{v}_{i}}x_{ij}^{jp^{s}-1}

The last polynomial is equal to
T_{s}(z,q)
up to the sign in (
2.6
).

From (
3.10
) we see that the coefficient of the monomial
x_{ij}^{jp^{s}-1}
in
\Phi_{s}(x,z,q)
comes from
\Phi_{l}(x,z,q)
and
\overline{\Phi}_{s-l}(x^{p^{l}},z^{p^{l}},1)
. Namely we have

(3.15)

T^{\prime}_{s}(x,z,q)=\text{coeff}_{x^{dp^{s}-1}}\Big(\Phi_{s}(x,z,q)\Big)=\sum\limits_{u,v,\alpha,\beta}\,\text{coeff}_{x^{up^{l}-\alpha}}\Big(\Phi_{l}(x,z,q)\Big)\times\\
\text{coeff}_{x^{p^{l}(vp^{s-l}-\beta)}}\Big(\Phi_{s-l}(x^{p^{l}},1,z^{p^{l}})\Big)

where the sum is over vectors
u
,
v
,
\alpha
,
\beta
such that

up^{l}-\alpha+p^{l}(vp^{s-l}-\beta)=dp^{s}-1

with
1\leqslant u^{(i)}_{j},v^{(i)}_{j}\leqslant\text{v}_{i}\ \forall 1\leqslant j\leqslant\text{v}_{i}
, and
\alpha=(\alpha^{(i)}_{j}),\ \beta=(\beta^{(i)}_{j})
are vectors of residues modulo
p^{l}
and
p^{s-l}
respectively, and
1
denotes the degree vector with all entries equal to 1.

Reducing each entry of the degree vectors on both sides modulo
p^{l}
, we find that
\alpha\equiv 1(\text{mod }p^{l})
, so
\alpha=1
(the degree vector with each entry equal to 1).

Since
u
is a degree vector
u^{(i)}_{j}
where
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
and
1\leqslant j\leqslant\text{v}_{i}
, we know by (
3.2
) that the degree of each
x_{ij}
in
\Phi_{l}
is less than
(\text{v}_{i}+1)p^{l}-1
. We also know that
\Phi_{l}
is skew symmetric (under the action of
S_{\text{v}}
). Hence for a fixed value of
i
, the coefficient of
x^{up^{l}-1}
in
\Phi_{l}(x,z,q)
is nonzero only if all the
u^{(i)}_{j}
are distinct, with
1\leqslant u^{(i)}_{j}\leqslant\text{v}_{i}
. Hence for each fixed
i
, the
u^{(i)}_{j}
are a permutation of
1,\dots,\text{v}_{i}
. Thus,
u=\sigma(d)
for some
\sigma\in S_{\text{v}}
, and

\text{coeff}_{x^{up^{l}-1}}(\Phi_{l}(x,z,q))=\text{coeff}_{x^{\sigma(d)p^{l}-1}}(\Phi_{l}(x,z,q))=\epsilon(\sigma)\text{coeff}_{x^{dp^{l}-1}}(\Phi_{l}(x,z,q))=\epsilon(\sigma)T^{\prime}_{l}(z,q)

where
\epsilon(\sigma)
is the sign of the permutation
\sigma
.
We then need to multiply this coefficient with
\text{coeff}_{x^{dp^{s}-1-up^{l}-1}}(\overline{\Phi}_{s-l}(x^{p^{l}},z^{p^{l}},1))
and sum over all degree vectors
u,v,\beta
with the properties mentioned previously to obtain
\Phi_{s}(x,z,q)
.

Thus the first multiple in the sum (
3.15
) factors out and we obtain:

T^{\prime}_{s}(z,q)=T^{\prime}_{l}(z,q)F_{1}(z^{p^{l}})

where
F_{1}(z^{p^{l}})
is a certain polynomial in
z
independent of
q
. Similarly, repeating this argument for all terms in (
3.3
)-(
3.6
) we obtain:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}T^{\prime}_{s+1}(z,q)=T^{\prime}_{l}(z,q)F_{1}(z^{p^{l}})\\
T^{\prime}_{s}(z^{p},q^{p})=T^{\prime}_{l-1}(z^{p},q^{p})G_{1}(z^{p^{l}})\\
T^{\prime}_{s}(z,q)=T^{\prime}_{l}(z,q)F_{2}(z^{p^{l}})\\
T^{\prime}_{s-1}(z^{p},q^{p})=T^{\prime}_{l-1}(z^{p},q^{p})G_{2}(z^{p^{l}})\end{array}

for some polynomials
G_{i}(z^{p^{l}})
and
F_{i}(z^{p^{l}})
independent of
q
.

Suppose
x
appears with exponent
\alpha p^{l}-1
in
\Phi_{l}(x,z,q)
, where
\alpha=(\alpha^{(i)}_{j}
) is a degree vector so that for each
i
the
\alpha^{(i)}_{j}
are a permutation of
1,\dots,\text{v}_{i}
. Let
\sigma\in S_{\text{v}}
be the permutation so that
\sigma\cdot((t^{(i)}_{j}))=\alpha
, where
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1,\ 1\leqslant j\leqslant v_{i}
, and
t^{(i)}_{j}=j
. The coefficient of this monomial is
\epsilon(\sigma)T^{\prime}_{l}(z,q)
.

If such a monomial contributes to the coefficient of
x^{dp^{s+1}-1}
in
\Phi_{s+1}(x,z,q)
(as in (
3.15
)), then we know that
x
appears with exponent
p^{l}(mp^{s+1-l}+\beta)
in
\Phi_{s+1-l}(x^{p^{l}},z^{p^{l}},1)
so that

\alpha p^{l}-1+p^{l}(mp^{s-l+1}+\beta)=dp^{s+1}-1

The coefficient of this monomial also appears in
F_{1}(z^{p^{l}})
by the definition of
F_{1}
. We will now show that this term appears in
G_{1}
as well.

If we add the degree vector whose entries are all 1 to both sides, divide by
p
, subtract the degree vector 1, then multiply by
p
again, we now have

(3.21)

\displaystyle p(\alpha p^{l-1}-1)+p^{l}(mp^{s-l+1}+\beta)=p(dp^{s}-1)

Let us now consider (
3.11
). The coefficient of the monomial
(x^{p})^{\alpha p^{l-1}-1}
in
\Phi_{l-1}(x^{p},z^{p},q^{p})
is
\epsilon(\sigma)T^{\prime}_{l-1}(z^{p},q^{p})
for the same reason as in our previous computation of
\text{coeff}_{x^{\alpha p^{l}-1}}\Phi_{l}(x,z,q)
. We also know that

\text{coeff}_{x^{p(dp^{s}-1)}}\Big(\Phi_{s}(x^{p},z^{p},q^{p})\Big)=\sum\limits_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\,\text{coeff}_{x^{p(u^{\prime}p^{l-1}-\alpha^{\prime})}}\Big(\Phi_{l-1}(x^{p},z^{p},q^{p})\Big)\times\\
\text{coeff}_{x^{p(p^{l-1}(v^{\prime}p^{s-l+1}-\beta^{\prime}))}}\Big(\Phi_{s-l+1}(x^{p^{l}},1,z^{p^{l}})\Big)

This is analogous to (
3.15
). The LHS of this equation is
T^{\prime}_{s}(z^{p},q^{p})
. The summation on the right is over degree vectors
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}
and vectors of residues
\alpha^{\prime},\ \beta^{\prime}
modulo
p^{l-1}
and
p^{s-l+1}
respectively such that

p(u^{\prime}p^{l-1}-\alpha^{\prime})+p^{l}(v^{\prime}p^{s-l+1}-\beta^{\prime})=p(dp^{s}-1)

We can divide by both sides by
p
, reduce each entry of the degree vectors on both sides modulo
p^{l-1}
and conclude that each entry of
\alpha^{\prime}
is 1. Hence

u^{\prime}p^{l-1}-1+p^{l-1}(v^{\prime}p^{s-l+1}-\beta^{\prime})=dp^{s}-1

Adding the vector 1 to both sides, multiplying by
p
, and subtracting the vector 1 from both sides we have

u^{\prime}p^{l}-1+p^{l}(v^{\prime}p^{s-l+1}-\beta^{\prime})=dp^{s+1}-1

as before. Recall that we previously chose a degree vector
\alpha
so that the coefficient of
x^{\alpha p^{l}-1}
in
\Phi_{l}(x,z,q)
contributed to
T^{\prime}_{s+1}(z,q)
. The coefficient of
x^{p(\alpha p^{l-1}-1)}
in
\Phi_{s}(x^{p},z^{p},q^{p})
is
\epsilon(\sigma)T^{\prime}_{l-1}(z^{p},q^{p})
as before. The coefficient of the monomial
x^{\xi}
of
\overline{\Phi}_{s-l+1}(x^{p^{l}},z^{p^{l}},1)
so that

\xi+p(\alpha p^{l-1}-1)=p(dp^{s}-1)

(note that this appears in
G_{1}
by definition) is exactly the coefficient of
x^{p^{l}(mp^{s-l+1}+\beta)}
, which is exactly the term that appears in
F_{1}
. Hence every term in
F_{1}
occurs in
G_{1}
, and we can repeat the argument to show that every term in
G_{1}
appears in
F_{1}
as well, which implies that
F_{1}=G_{1}
. We can repeat the argument to show that
F_{2}=G_{2}
as well. So we have

\frac{T^{\prime}_{s+1}(z,q)}{T^{\prime}_{s}(z^{p},q^{p})}=\frac{T^{\prime}_{s}(z,q)}{T^{\prime}_{s-1}(z^{p},q^{p})}

when
q
is an arbitrary primitive
p^{l}
-th root of unity, where
1\leqslant l<s
. Finally, by (
2.6
)
T^{\prime}_{s}(z,q)=(-1)^{(p^{s}-1)N\omega}T_{s}(z,q)
, so

\displaystyle\frac{T_{s+1}(z,q)}{T_{s}(z^{p},q^{p})}=(-1)^{(p^{s+1}-p^{s})N\omega}\frac{T^{\prime}_{s+1}(z,q)}{T^{\prime}_{s}(z^{p},q^{p})}

\displaystyle\frac{T_{s}(z,q)}{T_{s-1}(z^{p},q^{p})}=(-1)^{(p^{s}-p^{s-1})N\omega}\frac{T^{\prime}_{s}(z,q)}{T^{\prime}_{s-1}(z^{p},q^{p})}

And we have
(-1)^{(p^{s+1}-p^{s})N\omega}=(-1)^{(p-1)N\omega}=(-1)^{(p^{s}-p^{s-1})N\omega}
(since
p
is an odd prime). Hence

\frac{T_{s+1}(z,q)}{T_{s}(z^{p},q^{p})}=\frac{T_{s}(z,q)}{T_{s-1}(z^{p},q^{p})}

which finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

The polynomials
T_{s}(z)
defined by (
2.2
) satisfy the Dwork type congruences

(3.22)

\displaystyle\dfrac{T_{s+1}(z)}{T_{s}(z^{p})}\equiv\dfrac{T_{s}(z)}{T_{s-1}(z^{p})}\pmod{p^{s}}

Proof.

Specializing (
3.1
) to
q=1
, and noting that
[p^{s}]_{q}
specializes in this limit to
p^{s}
we obtain

(-1)^{\theta_{s+1}-\theta_{s}}\frac{T_{s+1}(z)}{T_{s}(z^{p})}\equiv(-1)^{\theta_{s}-\theta_{s-1}}\frac{T_{s}(z)}{T_{s-1}(z^{p})}(\text{ mod }p^{s})

where the sign appears as discussed at the end of Section
2.2
. Thus

(-1)^{\theta_{s+1}-2\theta_{s}+\theta_{s-1}}\frac{T_{s+1}(z)}{T_{s}(z^{p})}\equiv\frac{T_{s+1}(z)}{T_{s}(z^{p})}\equiv\frac{T_{s}(z)}{T_{s-1}(z^{p})}\text{(mod }p^{s})

This follows since
2\theta_{s}
is even, and
(-1)^{\theta_{s+1}+\theta_{s-1}}=(-1)^{\theta_{s+1}-\theta_{s-1}}=1
, since

\theta_{s+1}-\theta_{s-1}=\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\frac{v_{m}(v_{m}+1)}{2}\right)\frac{p^{s-1}(p^{2}-1)(1-2\omega)}{2}\in 2\mathbb{Z}

Hence we arrive at (
3.22
).
∎

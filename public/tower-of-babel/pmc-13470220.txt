Smart meter health state prediction based on residual-connected self-attention ConvLSTM network
Authors: Aibeiduo Li, Ting Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate health state prediction of smart meters is essential for ensuring metering reliability and enabling condition-based maintenance in advanced metering infrastructure. Existing approaches based on conventional machine learning or standard deep learning architectures struggle to simultaneously capture complex spatiotemporal degradation patterns, model long-range dependencies, and maintain stable training in deeper network configurations. This paper proposes a health state prediction framework built upon a Residual-Connected Self-Attention Convolutional Long Short-Term Memory (ConvLSTM) network. The framework introduces residual shortcut connections across stacked ConvLSTM layers to mitigate gradient vanishing and network degradation, and integrates a self-attention mechanism to dynamically recalibrate spatiotemporal feature representations according to their diagnostic relevance. An end-to-end pipeline encompassing multi-dimensional feature construction, data preprocessing, and health state classification is developed. Experiments conducted on a real-world dataset of 12,860 smart meters over 36 months show that, under the tested provincial grid conditions, the proposed model attains 94.38% overall accuracy and 89.94% macro-F1, outperforming LSTM, ConvLSTM, CNN-LSTM, and Transformer baselines. Ablation studies confirm the synergistic contribution of both self-attention and residual connections, while attention weight visualisations cross-verified by SHAP-based attribution reveal that the model autonomously concentrates on critical degradation signatures. We acknowledge that broader cross-regional validation remains an open task and outline this together with computational scalability and interpretability as the principal directions for future work.

Introduction

The ongoing transformation of conventional power grids into intelligent, digitally interconnected energy networks has placed smart meters at the very centre of modern electricity infrastructure, with the cumulative number of installed units reaching several hundred million worldwide1. These devices serve as the primary interface between end-users and the grid, supporting bidirectional communication, real-time consumption monitoring, demand response coordination, and dynamic tariff implementation2. In China, near-universal residential and commercial coverage driven by the State Grid Corporation has reshaped the landscape of electricity metering and grid management3.

Despite their critical role, smart meters are not immune to performance degradation under prolonged operation. Continuous exposure to fluctuating ambient temperatures, humidity, voltage transients, and electromagnetic interference gradually erodes measurement accuracy and component integrity4, while ageing of current sensors, analog-to-digital converters, and timing crystals introduces systematic drift that may go undetected for months5. Such degradation distorts revenue calculations, undermines consumer trust, and compromises grid optimisation algorithms that rely on metering data quality6. More critically, undetected meter failures may mask abnormal load patterns indicative of incipient grid faults or unauthorised consumption, posing tangible risks to operational reliability7. Recent investigations on condition monitoring of rotating and electromechanical equipment have likewise highlighted the value of proactive degradation tracking under variable operating regimes8,9, an insight that translates naturally to large-scale meter populations subject to seasonal and load-driven variation. Recognising these concerns, regulatory agencies and power enterprises have increasingly embraced proactive health state assessment as a means of replacing costly scheduled calibration with condition-based maintenance.

Early efforts to evaluate smart meter health relied predominantly on statistical approaches, where threshold-based rules or degradation-trend fitting was applied to historical error data collected during periodic verification campaigns10. While straightforward, these methods cannot capture the nonlinear degradation trajectories that characterise real-world meter populations. The broader uptake of data-driven paradigms then introduced machine learning techniques—support vector machines, random forests, and gradient boosting—to classify meter operational states or predict remaining useful life from multidimensional feature sets11,12. These approaches outperform statistical predecessors yet still depend on manual feature engineering and rarely model temporal evolution adequately.

The rise of deep learning has opened new avenues for health state prediction by enabling automatic hierarchical feature extraction from raw or minimally processed data streams. Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and their gated variants—particularly Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks—have been widely applied to sequential meter data13, and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) excel at extracting local spatial patterns from structured input representations. The ConvLSTM architecture, which replaces fully connected operations within LSTM gates with convolutions, fuses spatial and temporal modelling within a unified recurrent framework and has shown strong performance across spatiotemporal prediction tasks14. Closely related work on time-series sensor data has further explored hybrid frameworks that combine anomaly detection with few-shot classification to alleviate label scarcity in wireless sensor networks15,16, underscoring that data-efficient learning and architectural hybridisation are complementary directions worth pursuing. Nevertheless, applying ConvLSTM directly to smart meter health prediction remains challenging in ways that are insufficiently addressed.

Three difficulties stand out. First, as network depth grows to accommodate complex degradation patterns, gradient attenuation and feature dilution induce the well-documented problem of deep network degradation, where additional layers paradoxically impair performance17. Second, standard ConvLSTM treats all spatiotemporal features uniformly, whereas certain time steps and spatial locations carry disproportionately greater diagnostic weight; the absence of selective emphasis constrains predictive capacity18. Third, most existing studies evaluate components in isolation and lack an integrated end-to-end pipeline that connects preprocessing, feature learning, and health state output19. Recent work on uncertainty-aware sensorless anomaly detection has further argued that reliable indicators and decomposition-based diagnostic structures are essential for trustworthy condition assessment20. These limitations collectively call for architectural innovations that strengthen deep spatiotemporal modelling while preserving training stability and prediction fidelity.

Motivated by these observations, this work proposes a health state prediction framework built upon a Residual-Connected Self-Attention ConvLSTM architecture. We first introduce residual connections that establish identity shortcut pathways across ConvLSTM layers to preserve gradient flow and mitigate degradation in deeper networks21. We then embed a self-attention mechanism within the ConvLSTM pipeline so that the model can dynamically recalibrate feature representations by weighting spatiotemporal elements according to their diagnostic relevance22. We finally encapsulate these components within an end-to-end pipeline that converts multi-source meter operational data directly into health state predictions, eliminating labour-intensive intermediate feature engineering. The design draws on the complementary strengths of convolutional local feature extraction, recurrent sequential modelling, attention-driven global context aggregation, and residual learning for stable deep training. To frame the empirical study, this paper investigates three concrete research questions. RQ1 — to what extent do residual shortcuts and self-attention each contribute to recognition of minority health categories under heavy class imbalance, and is their combined effect synergistic rather than additive? RQ2 — does the proposed module remain stable as stacking depth increases, and at what depth do diminishing returns set in? RQ3 — do the spatiotemporal regions emphasised by self-attention align with feature attributions obtained from a model-agnostic interpretability tool, and does this alignment hold across health categories?

The principal contributions of this paper, organised around these questions, are the following. A residual-connection strategy tailored to ConvLSTM is developed, allowing deeper prediction networks without sacrificing convergence or accuracy. A self-attention module embedded in the recurrent pathway substantially improves the capture of critical degradation signatures buried in high-dimensional spatiotemporal data. An integrated framework is constructed and evaluated on real-world smart meter data, demonstrating consistent advantages over existing baselines on prediction accuracy and minority-class sensitivity, with a candid acknowledgement of single-region validation as a current boundary23. The remainder of this paper is organised as follows. Section “Theoretical foundations” formalises the problem and describes the dataset characteristics. Section “Health state prediction framework based on residual-connected self-attention ConvLSTM” details the proposed model architecture. Sections “Experiments and result analysis” and “Discussion” present the experimental setup and results. Section “Conclusion” concludes the paper.

Theoretical foundations

Principles of ConvLSTM networks

The Convolutional Long Short-Term Memory (ConvLSTM) network extends the conventional LSTM by replacing fully connected multiplications in each gate with convolution operations, thereby preserving spatial structure within the input tensor throughout the recurrent computation24. Standard LSTM cells process one-dimensional vectors and consequently discard any spatial arrangement present in the original data. ConvLSTM addresses this shortcoming by operating directly on three-dimensional tensors, where the two spatial dimensions remain intact as information propagates across time steps25.

These properties motivate our adoption of ConvLSTM as the backbone for smart meter health state prediction.

Self-attention mechanism

Self-attention, originally formulated within the Transformer architecture, provides a mechanism for each element in a sequence to attend to all other elements and thereby capture global dependencies without regard to positional distance22. This formulation allows the model to adaptively concentrate on the most informative positions regardless of their temporal or spatial offset—a property that distinguishes self-attention from fixed-window convolution and distance-constrained recurrent connections30. For time series analysis, this global receptive field is particularly valuable because degradation-relevant patterns may emerge at irregular intervals across long observation horizons. Recent work in predictive maintenance and sequential health monitoring has confirmed that integrating self-attention with recurrent or convolutional backbones substantially improves the model’s sensitivity to subtle yet critical state transitions31. We draw on this insight in our framework by embedding self-attention within the ConvLSTM pipeline, enabling dynamic re-weighting of spatiotemporal features according to their predictive relevance for meter health assessment.

Residual connection mechanism

Residual learning, first proposed in the context of very deep convolutional networks, addresses a counterintuitive phenomenon: simply stacking more layers does not guarantee better performance and can, in fact, degrade accuracy even on training data17. The core idea is elegantly simple—rather than forcing a layer block to learn the desired mapping \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H\left( x \right)$$\end{document} directly, a shortcut connection allows the block to learn only the residual function \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F\left( x \right)$$\end{document}:9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{Formally}}:y=F\left( {x,\left\{ {{W_i}} \right\}} \right)+x$$\end{document}

where x and y denote the input and output of the residual block, respectively. When input and output dimensions differ, a linear projection \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${W_s}$$\end{document} is applied to the identity path:10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{with}}:y=F\left( {x,\left\{ {{W_i}} \right\}} \right)+{W_s}x$$\end{document}

During backpropagation, the additive identity shortcut ensures that gradients flow through the skip path with a Jacobian contribution of at least unity, substantially mitigating the vanishing gradient problem that plagues deep architectures32. This property is what makes residual connections so effective—the network can never perform worse than the identity mapping, providing a stable optimization baseline regardless of depth.

From the perspective of our framework, integrating residual connections with stacked ConvLSTM layers is both natural and theoretically well motivated. Each ConvLSTM layer produces spatiotemporal feature maps of consistent dimensionality, which makes identity shortcuts directly applicable without dimensional adaptation. Moreover, when self-attention modules are inserted between ConvLSTM layers, residual paths around the attention blocks preserve the original recurrent features while allowing the attention output to refine them incrementally33. This combination—residual shortcuts stabilizing gradient propagation, ConvLSTM capturing spatiotemporal dynamics, and self-attention re-weighting salient features—forms the architectural foundation of the proposed prediction framework.

Health state feature analysis and data preprocessing

Characterizing the health state of a smart meter demands a multidimensional indicator system that reflects both internal degradation processes and external operating conditions. Table 1 summarizes the principal feature categories adopted in this study. Metering error, arguably the most direct health indicator, quantifies the deviation between measured and reference energy values under standardized load conditions34. Power consumption of the meter itself tends to rise as electronic components age, serving as an indirect proxy for hardware deterioration. Communication success rate captures the reliability of the data transmission module, while operating temperature and cumulative service duration provide contextual information about environmental stress and aging progression35.

These boundaries were determined through a two-step procedure: an initial cut-off was placed at the empirical 70th, 90th, and 97th percentiles of the score distribution, in line with the prior class proportions reported by the utility’s failure investigation records, and the boundaries were then snapped to the nearest 0.05 grid for interpretability. To validate the labelling scheme, we compared the resulting labels against an independent expert review of 1,200 randomly sampled meters and obtained a Cohen’s κ of 0.81, indicating substantial agreement and supporting the labelling protocol.

Raw operational records inevitably contain missing entries and anomalous readings caused by communication dropouts or sensor glitches. Missing values were handled through linear interpolation for gaps shorter than three consecutive samples and through K-nearest-neighbour imputation (K = 5, Euclidean distance over standardised features) for longer intervals. Outliers were identified using the three-sigma criterion and replaced with local 7-sample window medians. To construct the spatiotemporal input tensor required by ConvLSTM, we organise the normalised features of geographically co-located meter clusters into 4 × 4 grid-structured matrices at each time step, where spatial dimensions encode the topological arrangement within a distribution area and the channel dimension carries the multi-variate feature set37. Pearson correlation analysis is applied beforehand to eliminate redundant features with inter-correlation coefficients exceeding 0.95. The resulting dataset is partitioned chronologically into training, validation, and test subsets at a 7:1:2 ratio, ensuring that no future information leaks into the training phase. Sample code for the full preprocessing pipeline (interpolation, outlier handling, normalisation, AHP weight computation, and tensor construction) and an anonymised data subset have been packaged and uploaded to the Related Files section of this submission to support reproducibility.

Design of the residual-connected self-attention ConvLSTM module

The central building block of the proposed framework is a composite module that augments the standard ConvLSTM cell with a self-attention layer and a residual shortcut path. Figure 1 illustrates the internal architecture of this module. The design rationale is straightforward: ConvLSTM excels at local spatiotemporal feature extraction but treats all output channels with equal importance, while self-attention can recalibrate these features by modeling inter-channel and inter-position dependencies globally. The residual path, in turn, stabilizes the entire computation by ensuring unimpeded gradient propagation.

This residual pathway is critical for two reasons. First, it guarantees that the module can default to an identity-like mapping when the learned transformations are not yet beneficial, which is especially important during the initial training phase of deep stacked configurations. Second, it provides an unobstructed gradient highway—during backpropagation, the additive shortcut contributes a direct gradient term that bypasses both the attention and ConvLSTM computations, effectively preventing the vanishing gradient phenomenon even when multiple modules are cascaded39.

From a dimensional perspective, the entire module maintains a consistent tensor shape of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T \times C \times H \times W$$\end{document} from input to output. This uniformity is by design: it permits arbitrary stacking depth without requiring intermediate projection layers, which would introduce additional parameters and potential information bottlenecks. The self-attention component adds computational overhead proportional to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O\left( {{N^2}C^{\prime}} \right)$$\end{document}, but since the spatial resolution of meter cluster grids is typically modest (e.g., 8 × 8 or 16 × 16), this cost remains manageable in practice. The interplay between local convolutional receptive fields within ConvLSTM, global dependency modeling via self-attention, and stable deep training through residual shortcuts constitutes the core architectural contribution of this work.

Experiments and result analysis

Experimental setup and evaluation metrics

The experimental dataset originates from operational records of a provincial power grid company in eastern China, encompassing 12,860 three-phase smart meters deployed across urban and suburban distribution areas. The data spans 36 months (January 2021 to December 2023), with feature records sampled at daily intervals. After preprocessing as described in section “Health state feature analysis and data preprocessing”, the final dataset comprises 1,542,720 daily observation vectors. Missing samples accounted for approximately 2.4% of the raw observations, of which 71% were within three consecutive steps and were handled by linear interpolation, with the remainder imputed by K-nearest-neighbour imputation. The class distribution is notably imbalanced: healthy samples account for approximately 68.3%, sub-healthy for 18.7%, deteriorated for 9.5%, and faulty for only 3.5%, motivating the weighted loss design in Eq. (17)43. To preserve temporal causality, the chronological 7:1:2 train/validation/test split was strictly applied at the meter-month level rather than at the individual-sample level, ensuring that no observation from a meter in the test period appears in the training set.

All experiments were conducted on a workstation equipped with an Intel Xeon Gold 6248R CPU, 128 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA A100 (80 GB) GPU. The software environment consists of Python 3.9, PyTorch 1.13, and CUDA 11.7. Each model was trained five times with different random seeds, and we report the mean and standard deviation of all metrics to ensure statistical reliability.

To rigorously assess the contribution of each architectural component, we compare the proposed model against five baseline methods and two ablation variants. Standard LSTM serves as a purely temporal baseline without spatial modeling capacity44. ConvLSTM provides the spatiotemporal backbone without attention or residual enhancements. CNN-LSTM, which cascades convolutional feature extractors with LSTM layers, represents the conventional two-stage paradigm45. A Transformer encoder adapted for time series classification tests whether pure attention-based architectures can match recurrent approaches on this task46. For ablation analysis, we define two variants: RA-ConvLSTM-NoRes (self-attention ConvLSTM without residual connections) and RA-ConvLSTM-NoAtt (residual ConvLSTM without self-attention). Taken together, these ablation and interpretability results substantiate two central claims of this work. Self-attention enhances the model’s sensitivity to discriminative degradation patterns by dynamically redistributing representational emphasis, particularly benefiting minority class recognition. Residual connections, meanwhile, provide the structural foundation that makes deeper and more expressive network configurations feasible without optimisation instability. The two mechanisms complement each other—attention enriches feature quality while residual paths ensure that these enriched features propagate reliably through the network hierarchy.

Result discussion and computational efficiency analysis

The experimental results presented in sections “ Experimental setup and evaluation metrics” and “Ablation experiments and analysis” consistently demonstrate that the proposed framework outperforms all baseline methods. But why does it work better? We believe the answer lies in the complementary interplay of three mechanisms operating at different representational scales.

From the perspective of feature extraction, the ConvLSTM backbone captures local spatial correlations among neighboring meters within a distribution area—meters on the same feeder line or transformer group often exhibit correlated degradation trajectories due to shared electrical and environmental conditions50. The self-attention layer then extends this local receptive field to a global scope, allowing the model to detect distributed degradation patterns that span the entire spatial grid. Neither mechanism alone achieves this dual-scale coverage, which explains why the full model surpasses both ablation variants by meaningful margins.

Regarding long-range temporal dependencies, the gated recurrence of ConvLSTM already provides a stronger memory mechanism than standard convolution-based approaches. However, as the observation window extends to hundreds of time steps, even LSTM-family architectures can lose sensitivity to early-stage degradation signatures that foreshadow later failures. The self-attention mechanism compensates for this limitation by establishing direct connections between any two time positions, effectively bypassing the sequential bottleneck inherent to recurrent processing51. The convergence curves in Fig. 5 provide indirect evidence of this effect: models with self-attention reach higher performance plateaus, suggesting they extract more informative temporal representations.

The role of residual connections, while less glamorous, is no less important. As demonstrated in Fig. 7, deep ConvLSTM stacks without skip paths suffer from degradation that nullifies the benefits of added capacity. Residual shortcuts resolve this by preserving gradient magnitude across layers and providing the network with an implicit ensemble of paths at varying depths, which has been shown to improve both optimization landscape smoothness and generalization33.

The trade-off between complexity and performance is, in our view, decidedly favorable. The proposed model achieves a 4.96%-point improvement in macro-F1 over vanilla ConvLSTM at the cost of a 2.1 millisecond increase in per-sample inference time. The Transformer, by contrast, demands more computation yet delivers inferior accuracy, suggesting that its self-attention-only architecture is less well suited to this particular spatiotemporal prediction task than the hybrid recurrent-attentive design we propose. These findings affirm that the framework is not only accurate but also practically deployable in large-scale smart meter health management systems.

Discussion

The experimental findings reported in section “Experiments and result analysis” confirm that the proposed residual-connected self-attention ConvLSTM framework achieves strong predictive performance for smart meter health state classification on the studied dataset. Beyond the numerical results, several broader questions deserve consideration regarding the conditions under which this framework performs well and where its boundaries lie.

A natural question is whether the framework generalises beyond the specific dataset and meter population examined here. The architecture, by design, makes few assumptions about the physical characteristics of the monitored devices—it operates on generic multi-dimensional spatiotemporal tensors and learns discriminative representations directly from data. This suggests reasonable transferability to other equipment types that produce structured time-series observations, such as distribution transformers, circuit breakers, and power quality monitoring devices. That said, the spatial grid construction step described in Sect. 3.1 does assume a meaningful topological arrangement among monitored units, and equipment deployed in isolated configurations without clear spatial relationships would benefit less from the convolutional spatial modelling. We deliberately refrain from claiming universal applicability before cross-regional and cross-vendor validation has been carried out.

Our experiments used a fixed feature dimensionality of six indicator channels. In preliminary trials, we observed that the model remains robust when feature dimensions range from four to twelve, but performance begins to plateau beyond eight channels as redundant features introduce noise without additional discriminative value. Similarly, reducing the training set below approximately 40% of the full dataset caused noticeable accuracy drops, particularly for minority health categories. This sensitivity to data volume is not unique to our approach—it is an inherent characteristic of deep learning methods that rely on statistical pattern extraction from finite samples. For deployment in regions with shorter operational histories or sparser data collection, transfer learning from data-rich domains could be a viable mitigation strategy, though we have not yet validated this experimentally.

We must also be candid about the limitations of the current work. First, the dataset originates from a single provincial grid, and meter types, climate conditions, and grid topologies vary considerably across different regions. Validation on geographically diverse datasets is necessary before strong claims about universal applicability can be made. Second, while the weighted cross-entropy loss and label smoothing alleviate class imbalance to some extent, faulty meters remain the most challenging category to identify, with recall still falling short of what operational teams might require for safety-critical applications. More sophisticated sampling strategies or cost-sensitive learning formulations could push minority class detection further, and this remains an open direction for improvement.

Third, and perhaps most importantly, the model’s decision-making process retains a degree of opacity. The attention weight visualisations in Fig. 8 and the SHAP-based cross-verification in Fig. 10 already provide convergent evidence that the model emphasises diagnostically meaningful features, but a fully mechanistic explanation that engineering teams could audit operationally remains an open challenge. Integrating intrinsic-interpretability components or physics-informed constraints would significantly enhance practical utility.

Despite these limitations, the framework establishes a solid foundation for condition-based smart meter management. Its modular design readily accommodates future enhancements, whether through additional input modalities, alternative attention formulations, or integration with physics-informed constraints that could improve both accuracy and interpretability.

Conclusion

This paper presented a residual-connected self-attention ConvLSTM framework for smart meter health state prediction. The core contribution is an integrated module that fuses three complementary capabilities within a single architectural unit: convolutional recurrent processing for spatiotemporal feature extraction, self-attention for global dependency modeling and adaptive feature re-weighting, and residual shortcuts for stable gradient propagation across deep stacked configurations. These components do not merely coexist—they interact synergistically, as the ablation study confirmed that removing either self-attention or residual connections produces performance losses that exceed simple additive expectations.

Experimental validation on a real-world dataset of 12,860 smart meters over 36 months demonstrated that, on the studied provincial grid, the proposed framework achieves 94.38% overall accuracy and 89.94% macro-F1, outperforming standard LSTM, ConvLSTM, CNN-LSTM, and Transformer baselines. Attention visualisation, cross-verified by SHAP-based feature attribution, further indicated that the model concentrates on diagnostically meaningful features—metering error drift, abnormal power consumption, and communication anomalies—without explicit guidance.

From a theoretical standpoint, this work offers a new perspective on network architecture design for equipment condition prediction, demonstrating that carefully orchestrated combinations of recurrent, convolutional, attentive, and residual mechanisms can overcome individual limitations that have constrained prior approaches. On the practical side, the framework provides power utilities with a data-driven tool for transitioning from rigid scheduled calibration programs toward intelligent, condition-based meter replacement and maintenance planning—a shift that can yield substantial cost savings and improve metering reliability across large-scale AMI deployments.

We acknowledge boundaries: the dataset covers a single geographic region and meter manufacturer, the handling of extreme class imbalance remains imperfect, and model interpretability warrants deeper investigation. Three research directions appear particularly promising. First, incorporating multi-source heterogeneous data—including meteorological records, grid event logs, and unstructured inspection reports—could enrich the input representation and improve predictive fidelity. Second, deploying the framework within a federated learning paradigm would enable collaborative model training across multiple utility companies without sharing sensitive operational data. Third, developing lightweight model variants through knowledge distillation or structured pruning would facilitate edge deployment on resource-constrained metering infrastructure.

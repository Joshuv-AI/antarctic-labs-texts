On some convergence approach structures on hyperspaces
M.Ateş ∗ , F. Mynard † , S.Sağıroğlu ‡

Abstract
. In the context of the category 𝖢𝖺𝗉 \mathsf{Cap} of convergence approach spaces and contractions, we introduce and study approach analogs of the upper and lower Kuratowski convergences, upper-Fell and Fell topologies on the set of closed subsets of the coreflection on the category 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗏 \mathsf{Conv} of convergence spaces of a convergence approach space. In particular, over a pre-approach space, the 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗏 \mathsf{Conv} -coreflection of the lower Kuratowski convergence approach structure is the lower Kuratowski convergence associated with the 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗏 \mathsf{Conv} -coreflection of the base space, while the 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗏 \mathsf{Conv} -reflection is the lower Kuratowski convergence associated with the 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗏 \mathsf{Conv} -reflection. The 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗏 \mathsf{Conv} -coreflection of the upper Kuratowski convergence approach is is the upper Kuratowski convergence associated with the 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗏 \mathsf{Conv} -reflection of the base space, while the 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗏 \mathsf{Conv} -reflection is the upper Kuratowski convergence associated with the 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗏 \mathsf{Conv} -coreflection of the base space. We show that, over an approach space, the lower Kuratowski convergence approach structure is in fact an approach structure that coincides with the ∨ \vee -Vietoris approach structure introduced by Lowen and his collaborators, though it may be strictly finer over a general convergence approach space. We show that the upper Fell convergence approach structure is a non-Archimedean approach structure coarser than the upper Kuratowski convergence approach, but finer than the upper Fell approach structure introduced by the first and third author. We also obtain a 𝖢𝖺𝗉 \mathsf{Cap} abstraction of the classical result that if the upper Kuratowski convergence over a topological space is pretopological, then it is also topological.

A

is given by
a function
\lambda:\mathbb{F}X\to[0,\infty]^{X}
satisfying

\mathcal{F}\leq\mathcal{G}\Longrightarrow\lambda(\mathcal{F})\geq\lambda(\mathcal{G})

[0,\infty]

\lambda(\mathcal{F})(x)

measures how well
\mathcal{F}
converges to
x

\lambda(\mathcal{F})(x)=0

indicating full convergence while
\lambda(\mathcal{F})(x)=\infty
indicates
no convergence.

If moreover

(precentered)

Recall from Section

in
(X,\lambda)
.

H\in(\operatorname{rdc}(\mathfrak{G}(\mathfrak{F}))^{\#}
. By

[
6
, Prop. VI.1.4]
,

(\operatorname{rdc}(\mathfrak{G}(\mathfrak{F}))^{\#}=((\operatorname{rdc}\mathfrak{G})(\mathfrak{F}))^{\#}=(\operatorname{rdc}\mathfrak{G})^{\#}(\mathfrak{F}^{\#}),

so that there is
\mathcal{H}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\#}
such that
H\in\bigcap_{C\in\mathcal{H}}(\operatorname{rdc}\mathfrak{G}(C))^{\#}
.
If there is
C\in\mathcal{H}
disjoint from
H
, then
1\oslash m(H)\leq\lambda_{uF}(\mathfrak{G}(C))(C)\leq\bigvee_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{X}}\lambda_{uF}(\mathfrak{G}(C))(C)
.
Else,
H\cap C\neq\emptyset
whenever
C\in\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\#}

so that
H\in(\operatorname{rdc}\mathfrak{F})^{\#}
. Thus
1\oslash m(H)\leq\lambda_{uF}(\mathfrak{F})(A_{0})

and the proof that
\lambda_{uF}
is non-Archimedean is complete.

Finally,
\lambda_{uK}\geq\lambda_{uF}
, because if
H\in(\operatorname{rdc}\mathcal{F})^{\#},H\subset A^{c}
,
then
\bigwedge_{x\notin A}\operatorname{adh}_{\lambda}(\operatorname{rdc}\mathfrak{F})(x)\leq\bigwedge_{x\in H}\operatorname{adh}_{\lambda}(\operatorname{rdc}\mathfrak{F})(x)\leq m(H)

so
1\oslash m(H)\leq 1\oslash\bigwedge_{x\notin A}\operatorname{adh}_{\lambda}(\operatorname{rdc}\mathfrak{F})(x)
.
∎

On the other hand the upper and lower Vietoris approach structures
were introduced by Lowen and his collaborators. We follow here the
presentation from
[
13
]
. We start from an approach space

X
given by its lower regular function frame
\mathcal{L}=\mathscr{C}(X,[0,\infty])
.
Note that
\mu\in\mathscr{C}(X,[0,\infty])
lifts to maps

\displaystyle\mu^{\wedge}:\mathcal{C}_{cX}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle[0,\infty]

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\bigwedge\mu(A)

and

\displaystyle\mu^{\vee}:\mathcal{C}_{cX}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle[0,\infty]

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\bigvee\mu(A).

Such maps generate lower regular function frames on
\mathcal{C}_{cX}
given
by

\mathcal{L}^{\wedge}=\{\sup_{\mu\in J}\mu^{\wedge}:J\subset\mathcal{L}\}

and

\mathcal{L}^{\vee}=\{\sup_{j\in J}\inf_{k\in K_{j}}\mu_{j,k}^{\vee}:K_{j}\text{ finite},\mu_{j,k}\in\mathcal{L}\}.

They define
the Vietoris
\wedge
-approach structure
and

Vietoris
\vee
-approach structure
on
\mathcal{C}_{cX}
, which coincide
with the upper and lower Vietoris topologies on
\mathcal{C}_{cX}
respectively,
when
X
is topological
[
13
, Prop. 10.3.2]
.

The corresponding limit operators are

\displaystyle\lambda_{uV}\mathfrak{F}(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{\mathcal{H}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\#}}\operatorname{adh}_{\mathcal{L}^{\wedge}}\mathcal{H}(A)=\bigvee_{\mathcal{H}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\#}}\bigvee\{g(A):g\in\mathcal{L}^{\wedge},g_{|\mathcal{H}}=0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{\mu\in\mathcal{L}}\left(\mu^{\wedge}(A)\ominus\bigvee_{\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}}\bigwedge_{C\in\mathcal{F}}\mu^{\wedge}(C)\right)

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{lV}\mathfrak{F}(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{\mathcal{H}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\#}}\operatorname{adh}_{\mathcal{L}^{\vee}}\mathcal{H}(A)=\bigvee_{\mathcal{H}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\#}}\bigvee\{g(A):g\in\mathcal{L}^{\vee},g_{|\mathcal{H}}=0\}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{\mu\in\mathcal{L}}\left(\mu^{\vee}(A)\ominus\bigvee_{\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}}\bigwedge_{C\in\mathcal{F}}\mu^{\vee}(C)\right)=\bigvee_{x\in A}\bigvee_{\mu\in\mathcal{L}}\left(\mu(x)\ominus\bigvee_{\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}}\bigwedge_{C\in\mathcal{F}}\mu^{\vee}(C)\right)

Note that in the traditional topological setting, the lower Vietoris
and lower Kuratowski topologies coincide, e.g.,
[
6
, Proposition VIII.7.5]
.
However, in general
\lambda_{lK}
is finer than
\lambda_{lV}
,
and this inequality may be strict, though we have coincide over approach
spaces.

Theorem 33
.

If
(X,\lambda)
is a convergence approach
space and
\lambda_{lK}
and
\lambda_{lV}
denote the corresponding
lower-Kuratowski and lower-Vietoris convergence approach structures
on
\mathcal{C}_{c(\lambda)}
, then

\lambda_{lK}\geq\lambda_{lV}.

Moreover, this is an equality if
(X,\lambda)
is an approach space.

Proof.

For the first part, it is enough to show that
\mu^{\vee}\in\mathscr{C}(\lambda_{lK},V)

whenever
\mu\in\mathscr{C}(\lambda,V)
. Let
\mathfrak{F}\in\mathbb{F}\mathcal{C}_{c(\lambda)}

and
A\in\mathcal{C}_{c(\lambda)}
. We need to show,

\mu^{\vee}(A)\leq\lambda_{lK}\mathfrak{F}(A)+\bigvee_{F\in\mathfrak{F}}\bigwedge_{C\in F}\mu^{\vee}(C).

Let
\alpha=\lambda_{lK}\mathfrak{F}(A)=\bigvee_{x\in A}\operatorname{adh}_{\lambda}(\operatorname{rdc}\mathfrak{F}^{\#})(x)

and
\gamma=\bigvee_{F\in\mathfrak{F}}\bigwedge_{C\in F}\mu^{\vee}(C)
.

For every
x\in A
and
\epsilon>0
,
\operatorname{adh}_{\lambda}(\operatorname{rdc}\mathfrak{F}^{\#})(x)\leq\alpha

so that there is a filter
\mathcal{G}_{x}\#\operatorname{rdc}(\mathfrak{F}^{\#})
with
\lambda(\mathcal{G}_{x})(x)\leq\alpha+\epsilon
.
In view of (
1.10
),
\{G^{-}:G\in\mathcal{G}_{x}\}\subset\mathfrak{F}
.
Hence, for every
G\in\mathcal{G}_{x}
,

\gamma=\bigvee_{F\in\mathfrak{F}}\bigwedge_{C\in F}\mu^{\vee}(C)\geq\bigwedge_{C\in G^{-}}\mu^{\vee}(C)

and for every
C\in G^{-}
, there is
z\in C\cap G
, so that

\mu^{\vee}(C)=\sup_{x\in C}\mu(x)\geq\mu(z)\geq\inf_{w\in G}\mu(w).

Hence,
\gamma\geq\bigwedge_{C\in G^{-}}\mu^{\vee}(C)\geq\inf_{w\in G}\mu(w)

for every
G\in\mathcal{G}_{x}
and thus

\gamma\geq\bigvee_{G\in\mathcal{G}_{x}}\bigwedge_{C\in G^{-}}C\geq\sup_{G\in\mathcal{G}_{x}}\inf_{w\in G}\mu(w).

Moreover, as
\mu\in\mathscr{C}(\lambda,[0,\infty])
,

\mu(x)\leq\lambda(\mathcal{G}_{x})(x)+\sup_{G\in\mathcal{G}_{x}}\inf_{w\in G}\mu(w)\leq\alpha+\epsilon+\gamma.

As
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\mu(x)\leq\alpha+\gamma

for all
x\in A
, and thus
\mu^{\vee}(A)\leq\alpha+\gamma
, as
required.

Now, suppose that
(X,\lambda)
is an approach space with regular
function frame
\mathcal{L}
. In this case

\lambda_{lK}\mathfrak{F}(A)=\bigvee_{x\in A}\operatorname{adh}_{\lambda}(\operatorname{rdc}\mathfrak{F}^{\#})(x)=\bigvee_{x\in A}\bigvee_{\mathcal{G}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\#}}\operatorname{adh}_{\lambda}\operatorname{rdc}\mathcal{G}(x).

Since

\lambda_{lV}\mathfrak{F}(A)=\bigvee_{x\in A}\bigvee_{\mu\in\mathcal{L}}\mu(x)\ominus\bigvee_{\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}}\bigwedge_{C\in\mathcal{F}}\mu^{\vee}(C)

it is enough to show that

\bigvee_{\mathcal{G}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\#}}\operatorname{adh}_{\lambda}\operatorname{rdc}\mathcal{G}(x)=\bigvee_{\mu\in\mathcal{L}}\left(\mu(x)\ominus\bigvee_{\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}}\bigwedge_{C\in\mathcal{F}}\mu^{\vee}(C)\right)

for every
x
. To this end, Let
\alpha=\bigvee_{\mathcal{G}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\#}}\operatorname{adh}_{\lambda}\operatorname{rdc}\mathcal{G}(x)
.
It is enough to show that for
\gamma<\alpha
there is
\mu\in\mathcal{L}

with
\mu(x)\ominus\bigvee_{\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}}\bigwedge_{C\in\mathcal{F}}\mu^{\vee}(C)\geq\gamma
.
Given such a
\gamma
there is
\mathcal{G}_{0}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\#}
with

\operatorname{adh}_{\lambda}\operatorname{rdc}\mathcal{G}_{0}(x)=\bigvee_{\mu\in\mathcal{L},\mu_{|\operatorname{rdc}\mathcal{G}_{0}}=0}\mu(x)>\gamma,

so that there is
\mu\in\mathcal{L}
with
\mu_{|\operatorname{rdc}\mathcal{G}_{0}}=0
and
\mu(x)>\gamma
.
Since
\mathcal{G}_{0}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\#}
, for every
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
there is
K_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}_{0}

so that
\mu_{|K_{\mathcal{F}}}=0
because
\mu_{|\operatorname{rdc}\mathcal{G}_{0}}=0
and
K_{\mathcal{F}}\subset\operatorname{rdc}\mathcal{G}_{0}
.
Hence
\mu^{\vee}(K_{\mathcal{F}})=0
and thus
\bigwedge_{C\in\mathcal{F}}\mu^{\vee}(C)=0

and
\bigvee_{\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}}\bigwedge_{C\in\mathcal{F}}\mu^{\vee}(C)=0
. Therefore,
for this specific
\mu
,

\mu(x)\ominus\bigvee_{\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}}\bigwedge_{C\in\mathcal{F}}\mu^{\vee}(C)=\mu(x)\ominus 0=\mu(x)>\gamma

as desired.
∎

Moreover, the approach structure
\lambda_{lK}
may be strictly finer
than the approach structure
\lambda_{lV}
. This phenomenon occurs
already in the classical setting of
\mathsf{Conv}
, as soon as the base
space
X
is not topological.

Example 34

(
\lim_{lK}\neq\lim_{lV}
)
.

Take the “simplest” non-topological pretopology on
\{a,b,c\}

defined by
\mathcal{V}(a)=\{a\}^{\uparrow}
,
\mathcal{V}(b)=\{a,b\}^{\uparrow}

and
\mathcal{V}(c)=\{b,c\}^{\uparrow}
so that
\lim\{a\}^{\uparrow}=\{a,b\}
,

\lim\{b\}^{\uparrow}=\{b,c\}
and
\lim\{c\}^{\uparrow}=\{c\}
.
Then
\mathcal{C}_{X}=\{\emptyset,\{c\},\{b,c\},X\}
. If
\mathfrak{F}=\{\{a\}\}^{\uparrow}=\mathfrak{F}^{\#}

then
A\in\lim\nolimits_{lV}\mathfrak{F}
if for every
O\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
,

O\cap A\neq\emptyset\Longrightarrow O^{-}\in\mathfrak{F},

equivalently (using the contrapositive and
C=X\setminus O
),

a\in C\Longrightarrow A\subset C

for every
C\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
. But the only closed set containing
a
is

X
so
\mathcal{C}_{X}\subset\lim\nolimits_{lV}\mathfrak{F}
. On the other hand,
A\in\lim\nolimits_{lK}\mathfrak{F}

if and only if
A\subset\operatorname{adh}(\operatorname{rdc}\mathfrak{F})=\operatorname{adh}\{a\}=\{a,b\}
but the
only closed set included in
\{a,b\}
is the empty set, so that
\lim_{lK}\mathfrak{F}=\{\emptyset\}
.

Similarly, define for every
B\in\mathcal{C}_{c(\lambda)}
,

\displaystyle\mu_{B}^{\wedge}:\mathcal{C}_{c(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle[0,\infty]

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\bigwedge\mu(A\cap B)

and

\displaystyle\mu_{B}^{\vee}:\mathcal{C}_{c(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle[0,\infty]

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\bigvee\mu(A\cap B).

The corresponding lower regular function frame
\mathcal{L}_{uF}
generated
by
\{\mu_{B}^{\wedge}:\mu\in\mathcal{L},m(B)=0\}
defines the upper-Fell
approach structure in the sense of
[
1
]
.

Theorem 35
.

If
(X,\lambda)
is a convergence approach space then
\lambda_{uF}

defined on
\mathcal{C}_{c(\lambda)}
by (
4.1
) is finer than
the approach structure defined by
\mathcal{L}_{uF}

It is enough to show that
\mu_{B}^{\wedge}\in\mathscr{C}(\lambda_{uF},\lambda_{V})

whenever
\mu\in\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
and
B=\operatorname{cl}_{c(\lambda)}B
is a subset
of
X
with

. Let
\nu:=\mu_{B}^{\wedge}
. Assume to the

\mu_{B}^{\wedge}\notin\mathscr{C}(\lambda_{uF},\lambda_{V})
,

\mathfrak{F}
on
\mathcal{C}_{c(\lambda)}
and

\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}

\mathfrak{F}

\lambda_{uF}\mathfrak{F}(\emptyset)=\bigvee_{H\in(\operatorname{rdc}\mathfrak{F})^{\#}}1\oslash m(H)=1.

\lambda_{\mathcal{L}_{uF}}\mathfrak{F}(\emptyset)=\bigvee_{\nu\in\mathcal{L}_{uF}}\lambda_{V}(\nu[\mathfrak{F}])(\nu(\emptyset))=0

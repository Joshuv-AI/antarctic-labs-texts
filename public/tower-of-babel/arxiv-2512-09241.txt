Embedding 𝗄 \mathsf{k} -algebras into Leavitt algebra L 𝗄 ​ ( 1 , 2 ) L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
Boris Bilich, Roozbeh Hazrat, Tran Giang Nam

Abstract
. Since the commutative monoid T = ( { 0 , 1 } , ∨ ) T=(\{0,1\},\vee) is a weak terminal object in the category of conical monoids with order units, there is a unital homomorphism from every Bergman 𝗄 \mathsf{k} -algebra corresponding to a conical finitely generated commutative monoid into the Leavitt algebra L 𝗄 ​ ( 1 , 2 ) L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2) , where 𝗄 \mathsf{k} is a field. This fact will be used to give a short proof that Leavitt path algebras associated with finite graphs with condition ( L ) (L) embed into L 𝗄 ​ ( 1 , 2 ) L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2) , as well as provide criteria for an embedding of 𝕄 s ​ ( L 𝗄 ​ ( 1 , m ) ) \mathbb{M}_{s}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,m)) in 𝕄 s ​ ( L 𝗄 ​ ( 1 , n ) ) \mathbb{M}_{s}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,n)) . As our second main result, we show that the Heisenberg equation x ​ y − y ​ x = 1 xy-yx=1 cannot be realized in any Steinberg algebra, implying that the first Weyl algebra cannot be embedded into L 𝗄 ​ ( 1 , 2 ) L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2) , giving an affirmative answer to a question of Brownlowe and Sørensen on the embeddability of 𝗄 \mathsf{k} -algebras with a countable basis inside L 𝗄 ​ ( 1 , 2 ) L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2) . 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field, and
m,n
positive integers with
m<n
. Let
A=(x_{ij})
and
B=(y_{ji})
, where
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq n
, be a collection of symbols considered as
m\times n
and

matrices, respectively. The Leavitt algebras are defined as follows:

L_{\mathsf{k}}(m,n)\ :=\ \mathsf{k}\big\langle A,B\big\rangle\big/\big\langle AB=I_{m},\ BA=I_{n}\big\rangle,

(1)

where
\mathsf{k}\langle A,B\rangle
is the free associative (noncommutative) unital ring generated by symbols
x_{ij}
and
y_{ji}
with coefficients in
\mathsf{k}
. Furthermore,
AB=I_{m}
and
BA=I_{n}
stand for the collection of relations after multiplying the matrices and comparing the two sides of the equations. These algebras were considered by Leavitt
[
25
,
26
]
in the context of classification of algebras without invariant basis number. Moreover, it was shown that
L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,n)
is a (purely infinite) simple ring, while
L_{\mathsf{k}}(m,n)
,

, has no zero divisors.

Of particular interest is the Leavitt algebra

L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)=\mathsf{k}\langle x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}\rangle/\langle x_{1}x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}x_{2}^{*}=1,\,x_{1}^{*}x_{1}=x_{2}^{*}x_{2}=1,\,x_{1}^{*}x_{2}=x_{2}^{*}x_{1}=0\rangle,

which is the discrete version of Cuntz algebra
\mathcal{O}_{2}
and which has become a focal point of significant research interest. As an example, the infinite, finitely presented simple Thompson group
V
is contained in the group of units of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)

[
3
,
12
]
.
Graph
C^{*}
-algebras have become their own industry in
C^{*}
-algebra theory and have recently been used in the ongoing classification of
C^{*}
-algebras program (see, e.g.,
[
31

-algebras, Abrams and Aranda-Pino
[
1
]
and, independently, Ara, Moreno and Pardo
[
9

generalized Leavitt algebras

by introducing Leavitt path algebras of directed graphs. One of the main topics in Leavitt path algebras has been a desire to get a purely algebraic version of the celebrated classification theorem of Kirchberg and Phillips.

In light of the Kirchberg’s celebrated embedding Theorem that all separable, exact

-algebras embed into
\mathcal{O}_{2}
, in
[

Brownlowe and Sørensen proved that any Leavitt path algebra of a countable graph can be embedded into

, and they raised the following question
[

Question 4.7

Let

be a field. Does there exist a
\mathsf{k}
-algebra

such that

does not embed into

?

They noted “The authors have been unable to come up with any such

, but we would find an

In this article, we give an affirmative answer to Question 4.7, by showing that the first Weyl algebra

cannot be embedded into the Leavitt algebras

(Theorem

). This naturally raises the question of which classes of algebras can and cannot be embedded into

. Note that the Weyl algebra is a left/right Noetherian simple ring having no zero divisors and IBN property.

Also, using Bergman’s machinery
[
10
]
, we give criteria for an embedding of

into
\mathbb{M}_{s}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,n))

Given that Leavitt path algebras have a canonical

-grading which plays a pivotal role in their structures, we consider a graded version of Kirchberg embedding theorem. Whereas,

cannot be graded-embedded into

2.
Unital embeddings of Bergman algebras

Brownlowe and Sørensen’s Theorem
[
11
]
proved that Leavitt path algebras embed into
L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
. Their approach is to produce infinite number of orthogonal idempotents inside
L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
which match the structure of Leavitt path algebras
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
, for any graph
E
. Using Bergman’s monoid realization work
[
10
]
, we first
provide criteria for an embedding of
\mathbb{M}_{s}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,m))
in
\mathbb{M}_{s}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,n))
(Theorem
2.3
and Corollary
2.4
), and give a very short proof of embedding of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
inside
L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
when the graphs have condition (L), i.e., cycles have exits (Theorem
2.5
). Consequently, we obtain that the Cohn path algebra of a finite graph can be embedded into the Leavitt algebra
L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
(Corollary
2.6
).

We begin this section by recalling some basic notions of the commutative monoid theory.

Definition 2.1

(cf.
[
4
, Definition 2.5]
)
.

Let
M
be a commutative monoid (i.e.,
M
is a set, and
+
is an associative commutative binary operation on
M,
with a neutral element).

(1) We define a relation
\leq
on
M
by setting

x\leq y\ \ \mbox{in case there exists }z\in M\ \mbox{for which}\ x+z=y.

Then
\leq
is a preorder (reflexive and transitive).

(2)
M
is called
conical
if
x+y=0
implies
x=y=0
for all
x,y\in M
.

(3) An element
d\in M
is called an
order-unit
if, for any
x\in M,
there exists a positive integer
n
such that
x\leq nd
.

(4) Let
(M,d)
and
(M^{\prime},d^{\prime})
be commutative monoids with order-units
d
and
d^{\prime}
, respectively. A
homomorphism
from
(M,d)
to
(M^{\prime},d^{\prime})
is a monoid homomorphism
f:M\longrightarrow M^{\prime}
satisfying
f(d)=d^{\prime}
.

For any unital ring
R
, we denote by
\mathcal{V}(R)
the set of isomorphism classes (denoted by
[P]
) of finitely generated projective right
R
-modules, and we endow
\mathcal{V}(R)
with a structure of a commutative monoid by imposing the operation

[P]+[Q]=[P\oplus Q]

for isomorphism classes
[P]
and
[Q]
.
It is not hard to check that
\mathcal{V}(R)
is a conical monoid and
[R]
is an order-unit in
\mathcal{V}(R)
. If
\varphi:R\longrightarrow S
is a homomorphism of unital rings, then
\varphi
induces a well-defined
pointed
monoid homomorphism

\mathcal{V}(\varphi):(\mathcal{V}(R),[R])\longrightarrow(\mathcal{V}(S),[S])

such that
\mathcal{V}(\varphi)([P])=[S\otimes_{R}P]
for all
[P]\in\mathcal{V}(R)
.

We recall that a unital ring
R
is called
right hereditary
if every right ideal of
R
is projective.

A fundamental and striking theorem of George Bergman
[
10
]
shows that a finitely generated conical commutative monoid can be realized as the
\mathcal{V}
-monoid of a (left and right) hereditary ring which has a certain universal property:

Theorem 2.2

(
[
10
, Theorem 6.2]
)
.

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field and
M
a finitely generated conical commutative monoid with an order-unit
d
. Then, there exists a (left and right) hereditary unital
\mathsf{k}
-algebra
B
such that
(\mathcal{V}(B),[B])\cong(M,d)
. Moreover,
B
can be taken to have the weak universal property that for any
\mathsf{k}
-algebra
A
and any homomorphism
\psi:(M,d)\longrightarrow(\mathcal{V}(A),[A])
, there exists a
\mathsf{k}
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi:B\longrightarrow A
such that
\mathcal{V}(\varphi)=\psi
.

We say that the hereditary
\mathsf{k}
-algebra
B
, introduced in Theorem
2.2
, is the
Bergman
\mathsf{k}
-algebra corresponding to
(M,d)
(
[
2
,
3
]
).

As a first application of Theorem
2.2
, we obtain an algebraic generalization of Kawamura’s result
[
23
, Lemma 2.1]
on the embedibilty
\mathcal{O}_{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{n}
and its generalization

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field, and
m
and
m^{\prime}
positive integers with
m<m^{\prime}
. Let
M_{m,m^{\prime}}
be the commutative (monogenic) monoid

M_{m,m^{\prime}}:=\{0,x,\ldots,mx,\ldots,(m^{\prime}-1)x\}
with relation

That means,

M_{m,m^{\prime}}\cong\mathbb{N}/\langle m=m^{\prime}\rangle,

where
\langle m=m^{\prime}\rangle
is the congruence on

generated by
(m,m^{\prime})
(see
[

, Chapter 1]
).
We call the elements

the

of the monoid and

which coincides with the group completion of

M_{m,m^{\prime}}
. Note that for

, if

, then

(i.e., they appear in the tail) or

For any positive integer

, by
[
10
, Theorems 6.1 and 7.3]
(see also
[
5
, Theorem 5.7]
), we have

\left(\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{M}_{s}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(m,m^{\prime}))),[\mathbb{M}_{s}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(m,m^{\prime}))]\right)\cong(M_{m,m^{\prime}},s)

(2)

and
\mathbb{M}_{s}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(m,m^{\prime}))
has the weak universal property.

Using these notes and Theorem
2.2
, we obtain the following interesting result.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field and let
n<n^{\prime}
,
m<m^{\prime}
,
s
and

be positive integers. Then, there is a unital homomorphism

\varphi:\mathbb{M}_{s}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(m,m^{\prime}))\longrightarrow\mathbb{M}_{t}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(n,n^{\prime}))

if and only if there exist positive integers

and

such that

ks=t<n
, or

ks\geq n,\ t\geq n
, as well as
ks\equiv t\mod(n^{\prime}-n)

, and
(m^{\prime}-m)k=(n^{\prime}-n)k^{\prime}

(
\Longrightarrow
)
Using (

), the ring homomorphism

induces a monoid homomorphism

\overline{\varphi}:M_{m,m^{\prime}}\to M_{n,n^{\prime}}
such that
\overline{\varphi}(s)=t
. Let
\overline{\varphi}(1)=k
, for some

.
Then

ks=\overline{\varphi}(s)=t

, then

. Otherwise

∎

A (directed) graph
E=(E^{0},E^{1},s,r)

consists of two disjoint sets
E^{0}
and
E^{1}
, called
vertices
and
edges

respectively, together with two maps
s,r:E^{1}\longrightarrow E^{0}
. The
vertices
s(e)
and
r(e)
are referred to as the
source
and the
range

of the edge
e
, respectively. A graph
E
is
finite
if both sets
E^{0}
and
E^{1}
are finite. A graph
E
is
row-finite
if
|s^{-1}(v)|<\infty
for all
v\in E^{0}
. A vertex
v
is
regular

if
0<|s^{-1}(v)|<\infty
.
A
path

p=e_{1}\cdots e_{n}
in a graph
E
is a sequence of
edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
such that
r(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
.
A path
p
is called a
cycle
if
s(p)=r(p)
, and
p
does not revisit
any other vertex.
An edge
f
is an
exit
for a path
p=e_{1}\cdots e_{n}
if
s(f)=s(e_{i})

but
f\neq e_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq n
. We say that
E
satisfies
condition

(L)
if every cycle in
E
has an exit.

Let
E=(E^{0},E^{1},r,s)
be a finite graph. The
graph monoid

M_{E}
is defined as the free commutative monoid on a set of generators
E^{0}
, modulo relations by

v=\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}r(e)

for each regular vertex
v
.

The notion of a Cohn path algebra has been defined and investigated by Ara and Goodearl
[
7
]

(see, also,
[
3
]
). Specifically, for an arbitrary graph
E=(E^{0},E^{1},s,r)

and an arbitrary unital ring
R
, the
Cohn path algebra

C_{R}(E)
of the graph
E

with coefficients in

R
is the
R
-algebra generated
by the sets
E^{0}
and
E^{1}
, together with a set of variables
\{e^{*}\ |\ e\in E^{1}\}
,
satisfying the following relations for all
v,w\in E^{0}
and
e,f\in E^{1}
:

(1)

vw=\delta_{v,w}w
;

(2)

s(e)e=e=er(e)
and

r(e)e^{*}=e^{*}=e^{*}s(e)
;

(3)

e^{*}f=\delta_{e,f}r(e)
,

where
\delta
is the Kronecker delta.

Let
I
be the ideal of
C_{R}(E)
generated by all elements of the form

v-\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}ee^{*}
, where
v
is a regular vertex. Then the
R
-algebra
C_{R}(E)/I
is called the
Leavitt path algebra

of
E
with coefficients in
R
, denoted by
L_{R}(E)
.

Typically the Leavitt path algebra
L_{R}(E)
is defined without reference to Cohn
path algebras, rather, it is defined as the
\mathsf{k}
-algebras are generated by
the set
\{v,e,e^{*}\ |\ v\in E^{0},e\in E^{1}\}
,
which satisfy the above conditions (1), (2), (3), and the additional condition:

(4)

v=\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}ee^{*}
for any regular vertex
v
.

We are in a position to show that Leavitt path algebras associated to graphs with condition
(L)

embed into
L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
. This re-establishes the Brownlowe and Sørensen result more directly (but does not explicitly give the injective homomorphism).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field,
R
a unital
\mathsf{k}
-algebra and
E
be a finite graph with condition
(L)
. Then,
L_{R}(E)
can be embedded into the Leavitt algebra
L_{R}(1,2)
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a finitely generated conical commutative monoid with an order-unit
d
, and
B
the Bergman
\mathsf{k}
-algebra corresponding to
(M,d)
.
By Theorem
2.2
, we have
(\mathcal{V}(B),[B])\cong(M,d)
, and
B
has the weak universal property.
Consider the commutative monoid
T=(\{0,1\},\vee)
. It is obvious that
T
is finitely generated and conical, and
1
is the order-unit of
T
. Consider the map
\psi:M\longrightarrow T
defined by
\psi(0)=0
and
\psi(x)=1
for all
x\in M\setminus\{0\}
. Let
x
and
y
be two elements of
M
. If
x+y=0
, then since
M
is conical,
x=0=y
, and so
\psi(x+y)=0=\psi(x)\vee\psi(y)
. If
x+y\neq 0
, then we have either
x\neq 0
or
y\neq 0
, and so
\psi(x)=1
or
\psi(y)=1
. This shows that
\psi(x)\vee\psi(y)=1=\psi(x+y)
. Therefore,
\psi
is a monoid homomorphism with
\psi(d)=1
, that means,
\psi
is a pointed homomorphism from
(M,d)
to
(T,1)
. Moreover, by
[
3
, Theorem 3.2.5]
, we have
(L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2),[L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)])\cong(T,1)
. By the weak universal property of
B
, there exists a
\mathsf{k}
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi:B\longrightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
such that
\mathcal{V}(\varphi)=\psi
.

Specialising the conical monoid
M
to the graph monoid
M_{E}
, by
[
9
, Theorem 3.5]
, we have

\left(\mathcal{V}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E),[L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)]\right)\cong(M_{E},d_{E}),

where
d_{E}=\sum_{v\in E^{0}}v
, and
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
is a Bergman algebra for
M_{E}
and thus has
the weak universal property. Thus, by the above observation, we have a unital homomorphism
\varphi:L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\longrightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
.

We claim that
\varphi
is injective. Indeed, let
a
be a nonzero idempotent element of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
. Since the right ideal
aL_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
is a nonzero finitely generated projective right
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
-module, we have

\mathcal{V}(\varphi)([aL_{\mathsf{k}}(E)])=\psi([aL_{\mathsf{k}}(E)])=1,

and so

\mathcal{V}(\varphi)([aL_{\mathsf{k}}(E)])=[aL_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\otimes_{L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)}L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)]\neq 0.

On the other hand, since the exact sequence

0\to aL_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\xrightarrow{\iota}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\xrightarrow{\pi}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)/aL_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\to 0

of right
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
-modules, where
\iota
is the canonical injection and
\pi
is the canonical surjection, is split, we have
[\varphi(a)L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)]=[aL_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\otimes_{L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)}L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)]\neq 0.
This implies that
\varphi(a)\neq 0
. In particular,
\varphi(v)\neq 0
for all
v\in E^{0}
.
By our hypothesis that
E
satisfies Condition
(L), we may apply the Cuntz-Krieger Uniqueness Theorem
[
3
, Theorem 2.2.16]
to conclude that
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
embeds into
L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
.

Since
R
is flat over
\mathsf{k}
, we have an injection
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}R\hookrightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}R
, hence,
L_{R}(E)
embeds into
L_{R}(1,2)
as well, thus finishing the proof.
∎

We will use the following result of Wintner-Wielandt

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

, where

.

Proof.

For the reader’s convenience, we briefly sketch its proof here.

Assume that
PQ-QP=\alpha 1_{A}
. Then, by induction on

, we immediately obtain that

P^{n}Q-QP^{n}=nP^{n-1}\alpha

for all positive integer

but

for some

, then
nP^{n-1}\alpha=0
, and so
\alpha=0
. If
P^{n}\neq 0
for all

n||P^{n-1}||\cdot|\alpha|=||nP^{n-1}\alpha||=||P^{n}Q-QP^{n}||\leq 2||P||\cdot||Q||\cdot||P^{n-1}||

for all

for all

, thus finishing the proof.
∎

We next recall the concepts of ample groupoids and Steinberg algebras. A
groupoid
is a small category in which every morphism is invertible. It can also be viewed as a generalization of a group which has a partial binary operation.
Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid. If

is the source of

. The set
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}:=s(\mathcal{G})=r(\mathcal{G})

of
\mathcal{G}
. Elements of

are units in the sense that
xs(x)=x
and

for all

. For

, we define

UV=\{\alpha\beta\mid\alpha\in U,\beta\in V\text{\, and \,}r(\beta)=s(\alpha)\}
and
U^{-1}=\{\alpha^{-1}\mid\alpha\in U\}
.

A
topological groupoid
is a groupoid endowed with a topology under which the inverse map is continuous, and such that the composition is continuous with respect to the relative topology on
\mathcal{G}^{(2)}:=\{(\beta,\gamma)\in\mathcal{G}^{2}\mid s(\beta)=r(\gamma)\}
inherited from
\mathcal{G}^{2}
. An
étale groupoid
is a topological groupoid
\mathcal{G},
whose unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is locally compact Hausdorff, and such that
the domain map
s
is a local homeomorphism. In this case, the range map
r
and the multiplication map are local homeomorphisms and
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is open in
\mathcal{G}

[
30
]
.

An

of

is an open subset

such that

and

are homeomorphisms onto an open subset of
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. Similar to

we have that

are compact open bisections for all compact open bisections

and

of an étale groupoid

.
An étale groupoid

is called

has a base of compact open bisections for its topology.

Steinberg algebras were introduced in
[
32
]
in the context of discrete inverse semigroup algebras. Let
\mathcal{G}
be an ample groupoid, and

a unital commutative ring with the discrete topology. We denote by

Definition 3.2

(The Steinberg algebra)

Let

be an ample groupoid, and

It is useful to note that

The proposition provides necessary conditions for Steinberg algebras to be domains, which is very useful in our below arguments.

Proposition 3.3

Let

an ample groupoid. If

has no zero divisors, then

is a domain and

is isomorphic to the group ring

Proof.

Suppose

has no zero divisors, and assume that

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field of characteristic zero. Then, the following statements hold:

(1)
There is no unital homomorphism from the first Weyl algebra
\mathbb{A}_{\mathsf{k}}
to any unital Steinberg algebra;

(2)
Conversely, there is no unital injective homomorphism from a nontrivial unital Steinberg algebra to the first Weyl algebra
\mathbb{A}_{\mathsf{k}}
.

Proof.

Assume that there exists a unital ring homomorphism from the first Weyl algebra
\mathbb{A}_{\mathsf{k}}
to the unital Steinberg algebra
A_{\mathsf{k}}(\mathcal{G})
of an ample groupoid
\mathcal{G}
. Then, there exist elements
x,y\in A_{\mathsf{k}}(\mathcal{G})
such that
xy-yx=1
. Write

x=\sum^{m}_{i=1}a_{i}1_{U_{i}}
and
y=\sum^{n}_{j=1}b_{j}1_{V_{j}}

where
a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathsf{k}\setminus\{0\}
and
U_{i},V_{j}
are compact open bisections of
\mathcal{G}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq n
. Let
\mathsf{k}^{\prime}
be the subfield of
\mathsf{k}
generated by all the elements
a_{i},b_{j}
. We then have that
\mathsf{k}^{\prime}
is a countable field of characteristic zero, and so the transcendence degree of
\mathsf{k}^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{Q}
is at most
\aleph_{0}
. Let
S\subset\mathsf{k}^{\prime}
and
T\subset\mathbb{C}
be transcendence bases over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, there is an injection
S\longrightarrow T
, which extends to an embedding of fields
\mathbb{Q}(S)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}(T)\subset\mathbb{C}
. Since
\mathsf{k}^{\prime}
is algebraic over
\mathbb{Q}(S)
and
\mathbb{C}
is algebraically closed, the embedding extends to an embedding
\mathsf{k}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Therefore, we obtain that

\mathsf{k}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{C}\cap\mathsf{k}
such that
xy-yx=1
in
A_{\mathsf{k}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{G})\subset A_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{G})
. Then, since

A_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{G})
is subalgebra of
C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
,
there is a canonical unital homomorphism
A_{\mathsf{k}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{G})\rightarrow C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
, where
C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
is the groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra of
\mathcal{G}
, and so the Heisenberg equation
xy-yx=1
can be realized in
C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
. However, by Proposition
3.1
, such equation cannot happen in the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{G})
, a contradiction. Thus, there is not a unital ring homomorphism from the first Weyl algebra
\mathbb{A}_{\mathsf{k}}
to any unital Steinberg algebra.

Conversely, suppose there exist a nontrivial unital Steinberg algebra
A_{\mathsf{k}}(\mathcal{G})
and an injective homomorphsm of unital rings
\varphi:A_{\mathsf{k}}(\mathcal{G})\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}_{\mathsf{k}}
. Since the Weyl algebra
\mathbb{A}_{\mathsf{k}}
has no zero divisors,
A_{\mathsf{k}}(\mathcal{G})
has no zero divisors, too. By Proposition
3.3
,
\mathcal{G}
is a torsion-free group with the discrete topology, and
A_{\mathsf{k}}(\mathcal{G})
is isomorphic to the group algebra
\mathsf{k}\mathcal{G}
.

For each
g\in\mathcal{G}
, we have that
\varphi(g)
is an invertible element in
\mathbb{A}_{\mathsf{k}}
. Also, every invertible element of
\mathbb{A}_{\mathsf{k}}
is exactly a nonzero element of
\mathsf{k}
(see
[
17
, Section 2.2]
). From these observations, we immediately get that
\mathsf{k}\mathcal{G}\cong\varphi(\mathsf{k}\mathcal{G})\subseteq\mathsf{k}
, and since
\varphi
is an injective
\mathsf{k}
-algebra homomorphism,
A_{\mathsf{k}}(\mathcal{G})\cong\mathsf{k}\mathcal{G}\cong\mathsf{k}
, which contradicts with our hypothesis that
A_{\mathsf{k}}(\mathcal{G})
is nontrivial. Therefore, there is no unital injective homomorphism from a nontrivial unital Steinberg algebra to
\mathbb{A}_{\mathsf{k}}
, thus finishing the proof.
∎

Proof.

Assume that

such that

has no zero divisors,

has no zero divisors. By Proposition

is a torsion-free group with the discrete topology, and

Theorem 3.7

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field of characteristic zero, and let

If

cannot be embedded into

.

Next we show that
\mathbb{A}_{\mathsf{k}}
cannot be embedded into

, where

, using the

introduced by McClanahan in

is generated by elements

, subject to the following relations on

and

, where

denotes the identity matrix (see
[

). By the universal property of

by the mapping:

Assume that there exist

shows that there is a subfield

, thus finishing the proof.

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\longmapsto 1

In this final section, we see that if we replace

We first establish some useful facts. Recall that in a

-algebra

p=p^{*}=p^{2}
. We write
p\sim 1
, if
p=ss^{*}
and
1=s^{*}s
for some
s\in A
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The element
u=x_{1}\otimes y_{1}^{*}+x_{2}\otimes y_{2}^{*}

is a homogeneous of degree
1
unitary in
L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)\otimes L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
.

Proof.

It is clear that the element has degree
1
. We check that it is unitary:

\displaystyle(x_{1}\otimes y_{1}^{*}+x_{2}\otimes y_{2}^{*})(x_{1}\otimes y_{1}^{*}+x_{2}\otimes y_{2}^{*})^{*}

\displaystyle=(x_{1}\otimes y_{1}^{*}+x_{2}\otimes y_{2}^{*})(x_{1}^{*}\otimes y_{1}+x_{2}^{*}\otimes y_{2})

\displaystyle=x_{1}x_{1}^{*}\otimes y_{1}^{*}y_{1}+x_{1}x_{2}^{*}\otimes y_{1}^{*}y_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+x_{2}x_{1}^{*}\otimes y_{2}^{*}y_{1}+x_{2}x_{2}^{*}\otimes y_{2}^{*}y_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{1}x_{1}^{*}\otimes 1+x_{2}x_{2}^{*}\otimes 1

\displaystyle=1.

The other multiplication is similar.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\in L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
be pairwise orthogonal projections such that
p_{i}\sim 1
,
1\leq i\leq n
and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}=1.

Then, there exists a homogeneous degree
1
unitary in
L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)\otimes L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
commuting with all
1\otimes p_{i}
.

Proof.

Find
s_{i}\in L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
such that
s_{i}s_{i}^{*}=p_{i}
and
s_{i}^{*}s_{i}=1
.
Let
u=x_{1}\otimes y_{1}^{*}+x_{2}\otimes y_{2}^{*}\in L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
be the homogeneous unitary of degree 1 from Lemma
4.1
. Then, the element

t=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1\otimes s_{i})\,u\,(1\otimes s_{i}^{*})

is a homogeneous unitary of degree
1
commuting with all
1\otimes p_{i}
.
∎

We are now in a position to state the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.3
.

There is a grading-preserving
*
-embedding

\psi\colon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\hookrightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)\otimes L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2).

Proof.

We assume that
E
has a finite number of vertices, though the result
easily generalizes to countable graphs.

Let
\varphi\colon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\hookrightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
be the
unital embedding given in
[
11
]
. Let
\{v,e\}_{v\in E^{0},\,e\in E^{1}}
be the standard
generators of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
. Consider the projections

p_{v}=\varphi(v)\in L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)\quad(v\in E^{0}).

Since the projections
p_{v}
satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
4.2
(
[
11
, Proposition 4.3]
), we find a homogeneous degree
1
unitary
u\in L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)\otimes L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
which commutes with all

1\otimes p_{v}
. Define the map
\psi\colon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\to L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)\otimes L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)
on generators by

\psi(v)=1\otimes p_{v},\qquad\psi(e)=u(1\otimes\varphi(e)).

It is straightforward to check that
\psi
preserves the defining relations
of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
and is grading-preserving. Injectivity follows from the injectivity
of
\varphi
, since
\psi(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))
is contained in the subalgebra generated by

1\otimes\varphi(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))
and
u
, and
\varphi
can be recovered by sending

u\mapsto 1
, thus finishing the proof.
∎

We finish the note, by pointing out that one can graded-embed Leavitt path algebras
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
into any strongly
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring
A=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
, with the monoid
\mathcal{V}(A_{0})=\{0,1\}
. Here
A_{0}
is the ring of zero component elements of
A
. For, by Dade’s theorem
[
20
, §1.5]
,
\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{gr}}(A)=\mathcal{V}(A_{0})=\{0,1\}
with the action of
\mathbb{Z}
trivially on
\{0,1\}
. So for any conical
\mathbb{Z}
-monoid
M
, we have an obvious
\mathbb{Z}
-monoid homomorphism from
M
to
\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{gr}}(A)
. (Here the monoid
\{0,1\}
with

\mathbb{Z}
acting trivially is the weak terminal object of conical monoids with
\mathbb{Z}
-action.) Now the graded version of Bergman’s theorem
[
21
, Theorem 8.1]
implies that there is a graded homomorphism
B\rightarrow A
, where
B
is the graded Bergman algebra associated to
M
.
Specializing to Leavitt path algebras,
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
,
we get a graded homomorphism
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\rightarrow A
. One can then uses the graded uniqueness theorem to show this homomorphism is injective. As an example one can choose
A=L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,2)\otimes\mathsf{k}[x,x^{-1}]
, where the first component has given the trivial grading and conclude that for any finite graph
E
, we have
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\hookrightarrow A
.

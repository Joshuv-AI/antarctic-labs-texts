A novel technique for intraoperative coronal alignment assessment in posterior spinal deformity surgery
Authors: Jie Cheng, Tao Xu, Maierdan Maimaiti, Chuanhui Xun, Weibin Sheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to introduce a novel method for assessing intraoperative coronal alignment in posterior spinal deformity surgery and to investigate its feasibility and efficacy in preventing postoperative coronal imbalance (CIB).MethodsAll enrolled patients underwent posterior spinal deformity surgery, with dot-line method employed to evaluate coronal alignment intraoperatively. Preoperative and postoperative radiographic and clinical parameters were analyzed. Additionally, patients were divided into type A, B, and C preoperatively, and subgroup analysis was conducted to further assess the effectiveness of this new method.ResultsOf 46 patients with preoperative CIB, 16 were classified as type B and 30 as type C. The prevalence of CIB was 11.70% at the final follow-up. Preoperative main Cobb angle averaged 55.39 ± 28.22° and improved to 15.19 ± 10.65° at the final follow-up (P < 0.05). Preoperative CBD corrected from 23.06 ± 16.77 mm preoperatively to 18.77 ± 14.48 mm at the final follow-up (P < 0.05). SVA was 34.59 ± 22.66 mm before surgery and 20.12 ± 12.21 mm at the final follow-up (P < 0.05). VAS score and SRS-22 score demonstrated significant improvements had at the final follow up (P < 0.05). Subgroup analysis showed postoperative CIB in 2 patients with type A, 4 patients with type B, and 5 patients with type C.ConclusionDot-line method is a viable technique for intraoperative assessment of coronal alignment in posterior spinal deformity surgery, effectively reducing and preventing postoperative CIB.Keywords: Spinal deformity, Coronal imbalance, Intraoperative assessment, Coronal alignment, Posterior spinal deformity surgery

Introduction

Coronal imbalance (CIB) is not a rare clinical manifestation in patients with scoliosis deformity, defined as a lateral deviation of the C7 plumb line (C7PL) exceeding 20 mm from the center sacral vertical line (CSVL), with an incidence of 7% ~ 84% preoperatively [1–4]. This condition can occur across all types of scoliosis, but is commonly associated with thoracolumbar, lumbar, and lumbosacral deformities. CIB has an adverse impact on health-related quality of life (HRQOL) [5–7]. Generally, mild CIB (less than 30 mm) may be tolerated, whereas deviations greater than 30 mm often result in obvious physical abnormalities and deterioration in pain and function. Furthermore, if the segments distal to the lower instrumented vertebra (LIV) fail compensate, uncorrected CIB during surgery or postoperative new coronal disequilibrium not only impairs the patient’s life quality and self-reported satisfaction [8], but also increases the risk of implant failures and adjacent segment diseases [9], which are common reasons for revision surgery. Therefore, considerable attention has been directed toward identifying risk factors, developing assessment methods, and implementing preventive measures of CIB.

The potential risk factors for postoperative CIB are approximately categorized into patient-related and procedure-related factors. The former includes age, sex, body mass index, etiology of deformity, severity and stiffness of scoliosis, L5 rotation or tilt, preoperative CIB, and the like. The latter contains the selection of instrumented vertebrae, the magnitude of curve correction, application of osteotomy, and the surgeon’s experience, and so on. Although several strategies for preventing postoperative CIB have been reported [10–12], on this basis, relevant CIB classifications have been proposed with corresponding therapeutic strategies [13, 14]. However, postoperative CIB remains a serious complication with a prevalence of 10% ~ 40.30% [2, 11, 12]. Presumably, it may be attributed to the lack of an objective and effective method to assess coronal alignment intraoperatively.

Intraoperative coronal alignment assessment is crucial for surgeons to verify the accuracy of preoperative planning and ensure that surgical maneuvers achieve the intended corrections. So effective methods for evaluating coronal alignment prior to the completion of surgery, to some extent, can help reduce or prevent postoperative CIB. Traditionally, surgeons rely on visual inspection combined with C-shaped arm X-rays to evaluate coronal alignment intraoperatively, but this approach lacks reliability and accuracy. In 2002, Andras et al. [15] first introduced the use of a T-square instrument to assist in intraoperative evaluation of coronal alignment during posterior spinal fusion with lumbopelvic fixation, however, it is relatively difficult to generalize due to the cumbersome operational process. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a simple and effective method to assess intraoperative CIB, aiding surgeons in adjusting the extent of deformity correction and thereby reducing the incidence of postoperative CIB.

Based on our department’s extensive clinical experience with spinal deformity surgeries, we have developed a novel method for intraoperative evaluation of coronal alignment, termed the dot-line method. The purpose of this study is to present this new technique, and investigate its feasibility and efficacy in preventing postoperative CIB.

Materials and methods

Patient recruitment

Patients undergoing spinal deformity surgery were respectively analyzed from January 2016 to December 2018 at the Department of Spine Surgery. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients with primary or secondary deformity at thoracolumbar, lumbar or/and lumbosacral region requiring fusion; (2) LIV located at L3 or below; (3) patients with only a single posterior approach; (4) intraoperative coronal alignment assessment using the dot-line method; (5) a minimum follow-up period of 5 years. Exclusion criteria included: (1) scoliotic deformities caused by limb length discrepancies; (2) primary deformities in sagittal plane; (3) non-terminal surgeries (e.g. the growing rod technique for EOS patients, stage operation for AS patients with cervical, thoracic and lumbar deformity, and the surgery halted due to unstable vital signs of the patient during the operation); (4) non-surgical treatments due to severe physical comorbidities or other factors. Prior to inclusion, informed consent was obtained after providing oral and written information about the study.

During the operation, the surgery had to be halted due to the unstable vital signs of the patient.

Radiologic evaluation

Standard standing poster-anterior and lateral radiographs were evaluated preoperatively and at the final follow-up. The following parameters were obtained: (1) main curve Cobb angle, defined as the angle between the superior endplate of the most tilted vertebra cranially and the inferior endplate of the most tilted vertebra caudally; (2) coronal balance distance (CBD), measured as the horizontal distance between the midpoint of C7 and the center sacral vertical line (CSVL), with a threshold for CIB set at a CBD of 20 mm or greater; (3) sagittal vertical axis (SVA), corresponding to the horizontal distance between the C7 plumb line (C7PL) and the posterior superior S1 corner. All parameters were measured by using Surgimap Software (Surgimap Spine Software, Version 2.0.8, New York, US).

According to CIB classifications proposed by Bao et al. [13], Obeid et al. [14] and Qiu et al. [16], the classic criteria for coronal imbalance, namely CBD ≥ 20 mm and the parameter C7PL were adopted to evaluate the effectiveness of the dot-line method in this study. Therefore, three types of CIB were identified among patients: type A, CBD20mm; type B, CBD20mm and a C7PL shifted to the concave side of the curve; type C, CBD20mm and a C7PL shifted to the convex side of the curve.

Clinical outcomes

Health-related quality of life instruments, including Scoliosis Research Society questionnaires-22 (SRS-22) and visual analogue scale (VAS) for back pain, were completed both preoperatively and at the final follow-up.

Surgical technique

Since 2016, the evaluation of coronal alignment using the dot-line method has become standard practice in posterior spinal deformity surgery at our department (Fig. 1). Theoretically, this method can be used for posterior correction surgeries for all types of spinal deformities. The necessary instrument is a straight measuring rod, made from any radiopaque material, and three anatomical landmarks need to be identified (Fig. 1c). These points include the lower point (A), the intermediate point (B), and the upper point (C), corresponding to the midpoints of symphysis pubis, bilateral pedicles of LIV, and bilateral pedicles of upper instrumented vertebrae (UIV), respectively. The evaluation criterion is whether point C lies on the extension line defined by point A and B, indicating that the midpoint of UIV aligns with the measuring rod passing through the midpoints of pubic symphysis and LIV. The detailed procedure is as follow: after screw-based fixation device installed, the lower end of measuring rod is placed 5–10 cm distal to the gluteal sulcus, generally corresponding to the midpoint of symphysis pubis (point A). Then, ensure that the measuring rod passes through the midpoint of LIV (point B), and confirm the rod’s position using C-arm fluoroscopy again and make adjustments if necessary. Finally, assess the positional relationship between the midpoint of UIV (point C) and the measuring rod, either with or without C-arm fluoroscopy. An ideal outcome is when the measuring rod passes through the midpoint of UIV or is situated between bilateral pedicles of UIV, indicating satisfactory coronal balance. Conversely, if the midpoint of UIV deviates from this alignment, moderate adjustments are required to achieve optimal coronal balance.

The relationship between point C and the extension line of A and B was evaluated to determine coronal alignment after orthopedic surgery

Statistical analyses

SPSS version 22.0 (SPSS Inc, Chicago, IL) was utilized for data analysis. Statistical data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Paired t tests were used to analyze quantitative data. Chi-square tests were used to analyze categorical data. P value < 0.05 was considered to be statistical significance.

Results

Study sample demographics

A total of 94 patients (60 females and 34 males) were included in the study (Table 1). The average age was 29.71 ± 20.55 years old, and the mean follow-up time was 6.03 ± 0.80 years. Among 94 patients, 24 cases were degenerative scoliosis, 28 cases were congenital scoliosis, 24 cases were idiopathic scoliosis, 7 cases were neurofibromatosis, and 11 cases were ankylosing spondylitis with coronal imbalance. Prior to surgery, 46 patients exhibited coronal imbalance, including 16 cases of type B and 30 cases of type C. The average number of fixation segments was 10.29 ± 4.15 segments. LIV were at L3 in 24 cases, L4 in 31 cases, and at sacrum in 12 cases.

Discussion

Scoliotic deformity results in alterations in axial, sagittal, and coronal alignments. It is well established that patients with scoliosis may experience back pain, loss of work capacity, cardiac or pulmonary morbidity, even neurologic compromise [17–19]. One of the goals of deformity correction surgery is to rectify coronal and sagittal deformities and restore spinal alignment balance. Although plenty of studies have provided valuable insights into coronal imbalance, but there is a paucity of literatures about therapeutic regimens for coronal alignment compared to sagittal deformity. Postoperative CIB remains a severe and high-risk complication.

Intraoperative coronal alignment assessment is indispensable for surgeons to confirm whether coronal alignment is well-balanced after deformity correction. Traditionally, surgeons generally evaluate coronal alignment through visual observation combined with C-arm fluoroscopy. However, this method suffers from poor reliability and accuracy due to subjective judgment, increasing radiation hazards for both patient and surgeon. Although intraoperative 36-in. PA radiographs offer substantial advantages in assessing and correcting coronal malalignment during surgery, but their use is limited in many hospitals due to high cost [20]. In 2012, Andras et al. [15] reported a novel surgical technique for balancing posterior spinal fusions to the pelvis using a T-square instrument, consisting of a vertical limb perpendicular to two horizontal limbs. Subsequently, Kurra et al. [21] reported that intraoperative use of such instrumentation was conducive to assessing coronal alignment and reduced the occurrence of postoperative CIB. The essence of this technique involves aligning the horizontal limb with bilateral iliac crests, sacroiliac joints, upper acetabular margins, femoral heads or small tuberosities using C-arm fluoroscopy. Unfortunately, abovementioned anatomical structures are not always parallel to the pelvis in patients with scoliotic deformity and even exist abnormalities [22, 23], making accurate alignment challenging. Furthermore, if the LIV is located at L3 or below, especially at L5 or sacrum, even minor measurement deviations can lead to significant cranial shifts due to the lack of compensatory ability distal to LIV. In addition, the limited image acquisition range of C-arm fluoroscopy often prevents simultaneous visualization of both sides of anatomical structures, necessitating repeated fluoroscopies to confirm the relationship between the pelvis and the T-shaped instrument. Consequently, this method is not only cumbersome, but also increases its uncertainty, the risk of radiation exposure and operation time. Zhang et al. [24] proposed an integrated global coronal aligner to prevent postoperative standing CIB in adult spinal deformity patients fused to pelvis. This technique requires symmetrical and leveled heel bottoms, bilateral ankles and knees before surgery, and employs an inverted cross device to assess coronal alignment at the final steps of posterior correction surgery, similar to the method mentioned by Andras et al. However, it shares the same pitfalls as previously mentioned.

The main purpose of spinal deformity surgery is to restore spinal coronal and sagittal balance, thereby positioning the head and trunk directly above the pelvic center. The fundamental concept of dot-line method is that symphysis pubis is regarded as reference point for the pelvic center or the center of spine base in coronal plane. The apparatus required for this method includes a straight and radio-opaque rod. Coronal alignment is evaluated by determining the positional relationship among symphysis pubis, LIV, UIV and the measuring rod after spinal deformity correction. As long as ensuring that the measuring rod is located between bilateral pedicles of UIV, an acceptable coronal alignment can be obtained without considering pelvic tilt, ilium and hip abnormalities, or limited image acquisition range of C-arm machine. In addition, this method is straightforward to execute, requiring minimal radiation and no specialized equipment.

In this study, the prevalence of preoperative CIB was 48.94%, higher than previously reported literatures. This can be attributed to the fact that all patients recruited in this study had either primary deformities or secondary structural malformations in thoracolumbar, lumbar and lumbosacral spine, placing them at a high risk for CIB. Consequently, such individuals were also at high risk for postoperative CIB because structural deformity usually needed to fusion, with LIV typically situated at at L3 or below. Yang et al. [10] reported that 46.25% of patients exhibited CIB immediately post-surgery and 13.75% still showing imbalance at a two-year follow up after posterior fusion of thoracolumbar/lumbar curves. Bao et al. [13] reported that 30.4% patients experienced postoperative CIB following treatment for degenerative lumbar scoliosis (DLS). Ploumis et al. [3] also reported a prevalence of 31.6% for postoperative CIB in adult spine deformity patients undergoing long spinal fusions, with a minimum follow-up period of 2 to 5 years. However, our study showed that the incidence of postoperative CIB was relatively lower at 11.70% in a minimum 5-year follow-up. Moreover, our results demonstrated that radiological and clinical parameters had a significant improvement at the final follow-up.

Concerning the influence of preoperative coronal alignment on postoperative surgical outcomes, patients with preoperative CIB exhibit a higher risk of postoperative CIB, particularly in type C patients. Bao et al. [13] found that in patients with DLS, the prevalence of postoperative CIB was 23.8% in type A, 29.4% in type B, and 60% in type C. The authors proposed a novel osteotomy strategy for type C patients, recommending that prior to osteotomy on the apical vertebra of main curve, it was first considered to restore the balance of lumbosacral segments via SPO or PSO on the convex side of the fractional curve and transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion at the convexity of fractional curve. Obeid et al. [14] introduced a treatment-oriented classification system for CIB in adult deformity and suggested that it was necessary to avoid excessive correction of the main lumbar curve to circumvent postoperative new onset of CIB in type A patients. For type B, correction should be performed at the apex of the main curve, with or without the need of three-column osteotomy based on its flexibility. In type C, the deformity at the lumbosacral junction should be corrected by means of osteotomy. In our department, besides basic principles of deformity surgery treatment, more attention has been paid on coronal alignment assessment during surgery. Since 2016, we have routinely evaluated coronal alignment with dot-line method before the end of surgery, particularly in patients with preoperative type B and C CIB. Among 11 patients with postoperative CIB at the final follow-up, type A patients accounted for 18.18%, type B for 36.36%, and type C for 45.45%. Two of these patients underwent revision surgery due to distal junctional failure, and the remaining patients continued to be followed up due to acceptable clinical function.

The dot-line method is designed to streamline intraoperative assessment and enhance the accuracy of coronal alignment evaluation. Its fundamental principle is to ensure that the extension line (the measuring rod) defined by symphysis pubis and LIV lies between bilateral pedicles of UIV. A critical prerequisite for the application of this technique is a sound preoperative plane with appropriate selection of both UIV and LIV. Theoretically, the dot-line method has no specific restrictions regarding surgical indications. By ensuring balanced state at the UIV level, global spinal balance following deformity correction can be achieved, rendering this technique applicable for intraoperative coronal alignment assessment across all types of scoliosis surgeries. For patients with LIV at L3 above, typically without structural deformities in thoracolumbar, lumbar and lumbosacral segments, the major reason of postoperative coronal imbalance is adding-on phenomenon or proximal/distal junctional kyphosis and failure, resulting from unreasonable selection of instrumented vertebrae [25, 26]. However, for patients with LIV at L3 or below, thoracolumbar, lumbar or lumbosacral region usually exists primary or secondary structural curve. When making surgical planning for these patients, fusion to lower lumbar spine and even the pelvis is often considered. As a result, the compensatory ability distal to LIV is much poor or even disappears, which leads to a high risk of postoperative CIB. Therefore, rational surgical planning and appropriate surgical techniques are of significance to improve clinical outcomes. In addition, effective intraoperative coronal alignment assessment is another key to avoid the occurrence of postoperative CIB.

Several limitations of our study must be acknowledged. Firstly, this was a retrospective clinical case series study with inherent risk of data inaccuracy. Secondly, one of limitations at present study was its heterogeneous cohort, which included patients with different types of scoliosis. We did not analyze the association between postoperative CIB and the type of scoliosis. Thirdly, our focus was predominantly on coronal parameters, with less emphasis on sagittal parameters, potentially introducing bias into the results. Lastly, this was a single-center study with a small sample size. Therefore, a multicenter randomized controlled trial is necessary to validate the results of this study in the future.

Conclusion

The prevalence of postoperative CIB in patients with LIV at L3 or below is notably high. In the posterior spinal deformity surgery, dot-line method serves as a simple and effective technique for surgeons to assess coronal alignment intraoperatively, thereby reducing the likelihood of postoperative CIB.

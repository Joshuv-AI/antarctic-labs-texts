Introduction

In the paper we deduce continuity properties
for convolutions between Orlicz spaces and
Orlicz type Schatten-von Neumann symbol classes
in pseudo-differential
calculus. As special cases, we obtain well-known
convolution relations between Lebesgue spaces
and classical Schatten-von Neumann classes,
in quantum
harmonic analysis, given in
[
21
, Proposition 3.2]
, by R. Werner.
(See also
[
17
]
for an alternative approach,
whose style is similar to the present paper.)

The Schatten-von Neumann theory offers detailed studies of
linear and compact operators acting on a Hilbert space.
For example, consider a linear operator
T
acting
on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then
T
is compact,
if and only if there is an orthonormal basis
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

of eigenvectors to
|T|
, whose corresponding non-negative and
non-increasing eigenvalues

\lambda(T)=\{\lambda_{j}(T)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
tend to zero as
j
tends
to infinity, i. e.

\lim_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}(T)=0.

(0.1)

(For notations, see
[
9
,
16
]
and Section
1
.)

The operator
T
belongs to
\mathscr{I}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
, the
set of Schatten-von Neumann operators on
\mathcal{H}
of order

p\in(0,\infty)
, if and only if

\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{p}}\equiv\|\lambda(T)\|_{\ell^{p}}
is finite.
It follows that

\lambda_{j}(T)\lesssim j^{-\frac{1}{p}}

when
T\in\mathscr{I}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
, which indeed gives more
concrete information of
\lambda_{j}
for large
j
, compared
to (
0.1
). We also observe that the smaller

p
, the faster
\lambda_{j}(T)
are imposed to approach
0

at infinity.

This also indicates that by pushing
p
closer to
zero, the class
\mathscr{I}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
becomes closer to
the set of finite-rank operators on
\mathcal{H}
.

The presentation of Schatten-von Neumann classes
in
[
16
]
, by B. Simon, is not limited to

\ell^{p}
norm estimates on the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}

above. Instead, any suitable (quasi-)norm on sequences
can be used.

In this paper we focus on the case when
\{\lambda_{j}(T)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

belongs to (quasi-Banach) Orlicz spaces. We recall that any Young function
(or even more general, quasi-Young function)

\Phi
delivers the Orlicz space
\ell^{\Phi}(\mathbf{Z}_{+})
,
and that for
p\in(0,\infty]
one has

\displaystyle\ell^{p}(\mathbf{Z}_{+})

\displaystyle=\ell^{\Phi_{[p]}}(\mathbf{Z}_{+})\quad\text{when}

(0.2)

\displaystyle\Phi_{[p]}(t)

\displaystyle=t^{p},\ p<\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{[\infty]}(t)=\begin{cases}0,&t\leq 1,\\[4.30554pt]
\infty,&t>1\end{cases}

(also in norms). Here
\mathbf{Z}_{+}
is the set of positive integers.

The Schatten-von Neumann class
\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H})

consists of all
T
above such that

\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}}\equiv\|\lambda(T)\|_{\ell^{\Phi}}

(0.3)

is finite. Here the norm on the right-hand side
is given in Definition
1.3

in Section

Evidently, (

the usual Schatten-von Neumann classes by choosing
the Young functions in such ways.

By straight-forward applications of Schwartz’ kernel theorem
and Fourier’s inversion formula, we may identify
the spaces
\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}(L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}))
with certain function
and distribution spaces of operator kernels, or with symbols
to pseudo-differential operators.
The Weyl calculus within the theory of
pseudo-differential operators is here of special interest as
it is often suitable in quantizations. For any

in the Schwartz class
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
, the Weyl operator
\operatorname{Op}^{w}(a)

is the linear and continuous operator on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(\operatorname{Op}^{w}(a)f)(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a({\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}(x+y),\xi)f(y)e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}\,dyd\xi,\quad f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

The definition extends to any

\operatorname{Op}^{w}(a)
is continuous from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

In fact, by Schwartz’ kernel theorem and Fourier’s inversion formula
it follows that the map

from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

In particular it follows that

By choosing

More generally, we show that the mapping

In Section

Preliminaries

In this section we recall some facts about Orlicz spaces,
pseudo-differential operators, Toeplitz operators and
Schatten-von Neumann classes of Orlicz types. In the
last part we deduce some invariance properties of
symbol classes of Orlicz Schatten-von Neumann types.

Since our investigations involve quasi-Banach spaces which

\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\Phi\left(\frac{|f_{j}|}{\lambda}\right)=\infty,

for every
\lambda>0
, which implies

\|f\|_{\ell^{\Phi}}=\infty
. By combining these relationships
with Proposition
1.11
, one obtains (1).

Next we prove (2).
Therefore suppose
\Phi(t_{0})=0
for some
t_{0}>0
. Then

\Phi(t)=0
when
t\in[0,t_{0}]
. In particular,
near the origin, it follows that
\Phi

agrees with
\Phi_{[\infty]}
in Remark

1.8
. The conclusions in (2)
now follows from Proposition
1.11
.
∎

1.2.
Pseudo-differential operators

Let
\mathcal{L}(V_{1},V_{2})
be the set of all linear and
continuous operators from the topological vector space

V_{1}
into the topological vector space
V_{2}
.
For each
K\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
, there is a unique

T_{K}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}))
such that

\langle T_{K}f,g\rangle=\langle K,g\otimes f\rangle,\qquad f,g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

(1.8)

On the other hand, by the kernel theorem of Schwartz,
the following holds true (cf. e. g.
[
9
]
).

Lemma 1.13
.

The map
K\mapsto T_{K}
from
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

to
\mathcal{L}(\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}))
is
an isomorphism.

Let
A
belong to
\operatorname{\mathcal{M}}(d,\mathbf{R})
, the set of all
d\times d
-matrices with
entries in
\mathbf{R}
.
Then the pseudo-differential operator
\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)
with symbol

a\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

is the linear and continuous operator on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, defined
by

(\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)f)(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\iint a(x-A(x-y),\xi)f(y)e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}\,dyd\xi.

(1.9)

For general
a\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
, the
pseudo-differential operator
\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)
is defined as the continuous
operator from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
with
distribution kernel given by

K_{a,A}(x,y)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}(\mathscr{F}_{2}^{-1}a)(x-A(x-y),x-y).

(1.10)

Here
\mathscr{F}_{2}F
is the partial Fourier transform of
F(x,y)\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
with respect to the
y
variable. This
definition makes sense, since the mappings

\mathscr{F}_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad F(x,y)\mapsto F(x-A(x-y),x-y)

(1.11)

are homeomorphisms on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.
In particular, the map
a\mapsto K_{a,A}
is a homeomorphism on

\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
, and a combination of this fact and
Lemma
1.13
gives the following.

Lemma 1.14
.

Let
A\in\operatorname{\mathcal{M}}(d,\mathbf{R})
.
Then the map
a\mapsto\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)

from
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

to
\mathcal{L}(\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}))
is
an isomorphism.

If
I_{d}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{M}}(d,\mathbf{R})
is the identity matrix and

A=\frac{1}{2}I_{d}
, then
\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)
is equal to the Weyl
pseudo-differential operator
\operatorname{Op}^{w}(a)
for
a
. If instead
A=0
, then the standard
(Kohn-Nirenberg) representation
\operatorname{Op}(a)=a(x,D)
is obtained.

As a consequence of Lemma
1.14
,
it follows that for each
a_{1}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
and
A_{1},A_{2}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{M}}(d,\mathbf{R})
, there is a unique
a_{2}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
such that

\operatorname{Op}_{A_{1}}(a_{1})=\operatorname{Op}_{A_{2}}(a_{2})
. The relationship between
a_{1}
and
a_{2}

is given by

\operatorname{Op}_{A_{1}}(a_{1})=\operatorname{Op}_{A_{2}}(a_{2})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad e^{i\langle A_{1}D_{\xi},D_{x}\rangle}a_{1}(x,\xi)=e^{i\langle A_{2}D_{\xi},D_{x}\rangle}a_{2}(x,\xi),

(1.12)

cf.
[
9
,
19
]
.

Let
A\in\operatorname{\mathcal{M}}(d,\mathbf{R})
and
a\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
be
fixed. Then
a
is called a rank-one element with respect to
A
, if
the corresponding pseudo-differential operator is of rank-one,
i. e.

\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)f=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}(f,f_{2})_{L^{2}}f_{1},\qquad f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),

(1.13)

for some
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and with the
L^{2}({\bf R}^{d})

inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}}
. (We sometimes include factors of
the form
(2\pi)^{\pm\frac{d}{2}}
in order for receiving other convenient
formulae.) By
straight-forward computations it follows that (
1.13
)
is fulfilled, if and only if
a=W_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{A}
, where
W_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{A}

is the
A
-Wigner distribution, defined by the formula

W_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{A}(x,\xi)\equiv\mathscr{F}(f_{1}(x+A\,\cdot\,)\overline{f_{2}(x-(I-A)\,\cdot\,)})(\xi),

(1.14)

which takes the form

W_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{A}(x,\xi)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int f_{1}(x+Ay)\overline{f_{2}(x-(I-A)y)}e^{-i\langle y,\xi\rangle}\,dy,

when
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
From these relations it follows that

\operatorname{Op}_{A}(W_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{A})=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}f_{1}\otimes\overline{f_{2}},\qquad f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),

(1.15)

when identifying operators with their kernels.

By combining these facts
with (
1.12
), it follows that

e^{i\langle A_{1}D_{\xi},D_{x}\rangle}W_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{A_{1}}=e^{i\langle A_{2}D_{\xi},D_{x}\rangle}W_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{A_{2}}

(1.16)

for each
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and real
d\times d
-matrices

A_{1}
and
A_{2}
.

For future references we note the link

(\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)f,g)_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}(a,W_{g,f}^{A})_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})},\\[4.30554pt]
a\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\quad\text{and}\quad f,g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(1.17)

between pseudo-differential operators and Wigner distributions,
which follows by straight-forward computations (see e. g.
(1.11) in
[
19
]
).

Since
the Weyl case is particularly important, we set

W_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{A}=W_{f_{1},f_{2}}
when
A=\frac{1}{2}\cdot I_{d}
, i. e.

W_{f_{1},f_{2}}
is the usual (cross-)Wigner distribution of
f_{1}
and

f_{2}
.
For the Weyl case we also observe the
convenient formula

\operatorname{Op}^{w}(a)^{*}=\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\overline{a}),

(1.18)

concerning the adjoint of Weyl quantizations
with respect to the
L^{2}
form. That is,

(\operatorname{Op}^{w}(a)f,g)_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=(f,\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\overline{a})g)_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})},\quad f,g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

1.3.
Toeplitz operators

Next we recall some facts about Toeplitz operators.
The Toeplitz operator
\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(a)
,
with symbol
a\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
, and window functions

\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, is defined by
the formula

(\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(a)f_{1},f_{2})_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=(a(2\,\cdot\,)W_{f_{1},{\phi_{1}}},W_{f_{2},{\phi_{2}}})_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})}

(1.19)

when
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

The definition of
\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(a)
extends in several ways
(cf. e. g.
[
1
,
5
,
8
,
17
]
and the references
therein).
In several of these extensions, we
interpret Toeplitz operators as pseudo-differential operators, using
the fact that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(a)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a\ast u)\quad\text{when}

(1.20)

\displaystyle u(X)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}W_{\phi_{2},\phi_{1}}^{A}(-X).

This formula holds when
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are suitable window functions on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, for any

A\in\operatorname{\mathcal{M}}(d,\mathbf{R})
and appropriate distribution

a
on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
. Here
*
is the
usual convolution between Schwartz functions and tempered
distributions, and here we also use
capitals

X=(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d},\quad Y=(y,\eta)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d},\qquad x,y,\xi,\eta\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

to denote elements in the phase space
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
.

The relation (
1.20
) is well-known in the Weyl case
(see e. g.
[
6
]
), and when
A=tI_{d}
for some

t\in\mathbf{R}

(cf. e. g.
[
18
, (1.23)]
and the references therein). For general

A\in\operatorname{\mathcal{M}}(d,\mathbf{R})
, (
1.20
) is an immediate
consequence of the case
A=tI_{d}
, (
1.12
)
(
1.16
), and the fact that

e^{i\langle AD_{\xi},D_{x}\rangle}(a\ast u)=a*(e^{i\langle AD_{\xi},D_{x}\rangle}u),

which follows via integration by parts.

1.4.
Schatten-von Neumann classes

In what follows we recall some facts on Schatten-von Neumann operators,
given in
[
16
]
. Let
T
be a linear and continuous map from the
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{1}

into the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, and let
j\geq 1
be an integer.
Also let

\mathscr{I}_{0,j}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
be the set of all linear and
continuous operators from
\mathcal{H}_{1}
to
\mathcal{H}_{2}
with rank
at most
j-1
.
The
singular value
of
T
of order
j

is defined by

\sigma_{j}(T)=\sigma_{j}(T;\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})\equiv\inf_{T_{0}\in\mathscr{I}_{0,j}}\|T-T_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}},\qquad j\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}.

Evidently

\sigma_{j}(T;\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
decreases with
j
, and

\sigma_{1}(T;\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
is equal to the operator
norm
\|T\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}}
of
T
.

Throughout the paper, all Hilbert spaces are assumed to be separable,
and observe that this is always the case for Hilbert spaces which
are continuously embedded in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
in view of
[
12
, Proposition 1.2]
.
However, we note that most parts of what is described here also
hold when
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
are allowed to be non-separable.

In the following definition we present a broad family of
Schatten-von Neumann classes.

Definition 1.15
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces,

T
be a linear operator from
\mathcal{H}_{1}
to
\mathcal{H}_{2}
,
and let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\ell_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbf{Z}_{+})
be a quasi-Banach space.

(1)

The
\mathcal{B}
Schatten-von Neumann (quasi-)norm of
T
is given by

\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}}=\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})}\equiv\|\{\sigma_{j}(T;\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\mathcal{B}}.

(2)

The
\mathcal{B}
Schatten-von Neumann class

\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}=\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})

consists of all linear and continuous operators
T
from
\mathcal{H}_{1}

to
\mathcal{H}_{2}
such that

\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})}
is finite.
The topology
of
\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}=\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})

is given through the (quasi-)norm

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})}
.

Definition 1.16
.

Let
\Phi
be a quasi-Young function,
p\in(0,\infty]

and let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces.
Then let

\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}=\mathscr{I}_{\ell^{\Phi}},\quad\mathscr{I}_{p}=\mathscr{I}_{\ell^{p}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{I}_{\sharp}=\mathscr{I}_{\ell^{\sharp}}.

•

The space
\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})

is called the Orlicz Schatten-von Neumann class with respect to

\Phi
,
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, or the
\Phi
-Schatten class.

•

The space
\mathscr{I}_{p}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
is called the
(classical) Schatten-von Neumann class with respect to

p
,
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, or the
p
-Schatten class.

We observe that
\mathscr{I}_{p}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
increases with

p
, and that
\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
decreases with
\Phi
.
In fact, for the latter conclusion, it suffices to detect the decreasing property
with respect to
\Phi

near origin, which is shown in the following proposition.
The result follows from Proposition

1.11
and the definitions.
The details are left for the reader.

Proposition 1.17
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces, and let

\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
be quasi-Young functions such that for some

T>0
it holds

\Phi_{2}(t)\lesssim\Phi_{1}(t),\quad\text{when}\quad t\in(0,T].

Then

\mathscr{I}_{\Phi_{1}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{I}_{\Phi_{2}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
.

We let
\mathscr{I}_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
be the
space of all linear operators from
\mathcal{H}_{1}
to

\mathcal{H}_{2}
with finite rank. Then

\mathscr{I}_{0}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{I}_{0,j}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}),

It follows that

\mathscr{I}_{\sharp}=\mathscr{I}_{\sharp}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})

consists of all
T\in\mathscr{I}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})

such that

\{\sigma_{j}(T;\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{\sharp}(\mathbf{Z}_{+}),

with topology is given through the norm

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})}
.

We notice that

\mathscr{I}_{1}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}),\quad\mathscr{I}_{2}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}),\quad\mathscr{I}_{\sharp}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}),\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{I}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}),

are the spaces of trace-class, Hilbert-Schmidt, compact, and linear
and continuous operators from
\mathcal{H}_{1}
to
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, respectively,
also in norms.

We set

\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H})=\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}),\quad\mathscr{I}_{p}(\mathcal{H})=\mathscr{I}_{p}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H})=\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})

when
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space.
By straight-forward application of the spectral theorem, it follows
that the definition of
\mathscr{I}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
,
\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H})

and their norms
in Definition
1.16
agree
with the definitions of those quantities in the introduction.
In fact, we have the following.

Proposition 1.18
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces,
and
\Phi
be a quasi-Young function of order
r\in(0,1]
.
Then
\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
is a quasi-Banach
space of order
r
, and

\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}}=\sup\|\{(Tf_{j},g_{j})_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{\Phi}(\mathbf{Z}_{+})},\quad T\in\mathscr{I}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}).

(1.21)

Here the supremum is taken over all orthonormal sequences

\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\operatorname{ON}(\mathcal{H}_{1})

and

\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\operatorname{ON}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
.

Proof.

If
\Phi
is a Young function, then the identity
(
1.21
) follows from
[
16
, Proposition 2.6]
.
For general quasi-Young functions
\Phi
,
(
1.21
) follows as well from
[
16
, Proposition 2.6]
,
and its proof. The details are left for the reader.

If
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathscr{I}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
, then
(
1.21
) gives

\displaystyle\|T_{1}+T_{2}\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}}^{r}

\displaystyle=\sup\|\{((T_{1}+T_{2})f_{j},g_{j})_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{\Phi}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\left(\|\{(T_{1}f_{j},g_{j})_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{\Phi}}^{r}+\|\{(T_{2}f_{j},g_{j})_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{\Phi}}^{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup\|\{(T_{1}f_{j},g_{j})_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{\Phi}}^{r}+\sup\|\{(T_{2}f_{j},g_{j})_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{\Phi}}^{r}

\displaystyle=\|T_{1}\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}}^{r}+\|T_{2}\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}}^{r},

which gives the result.
∎

Remark 1.19
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces, and let

\mathcal{B}
be a quasi-Banach space of sequences
on
\mathbf{Z}_{+}
of order
r\in(0,1]
.
By similar arguments as in the proof of
Proposition
1.18
we see that

\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
is a quasi-Banach
space of the same order as
\mathcal{B}
, and

\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}}=\sup\|\{(Tf_{j},g_{j})_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\mathcal{B}},\quad T\in\mathscr{I}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}).

(1.22)

Here the supremum is again taken over all orthonormal sequences

\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\operatorname{ON}(\mathcal{H}_{1})

and

\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\operatorname{ON}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
.
(See also

[
16
, Proposition 2.6]
,
and its proof.)

Suppose additionally
that the following holds true:

(1)

\mathcal{B}
is norm invariant under permutations, i. e.,

\|a\|_{\mathcal{B}}=\|b\|_{\mathcal{B}},

for all sequences
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and
\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

such that
b_{j}=a_{\sigma(j)}
,
j\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}
, for some permutation

\sigma
on
\mathbf{Z}_{+}
;

(2)

e_{j_{0}}\equiv\{\delta_{j,j_{0}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{B}
for some
j_{0}\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}
.

Then
\mathscr{I}_{r}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})\subseteq\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
,
and

\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})}\leq c\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{r}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})},\quad c=\|e_{j_{0}}\|_{\mathcal{B}}.

(1.23)

In fact, by a combination of (1) and (2) it follows that

c=\|e_{j_{0}}\|_{\mathcal{B}}

for
every

j_{0}\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}
. This gives

\displaystyle\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}}^{r}

\displaystyle=\|\{\sigma_{j}(T)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{r}=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}(T)e_{j}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}(T)^{r}\|e_{j}\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{r}=c^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}(T)^{r}=c^{r}\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{r}}^{r},

and the assertion follows.

We observe that (1) and (2) hold true when

\mathcal{B}=\ell^{\Phi}(\mathbf{Z}_{+})
for some quasi-Young function
\Phi
.
Hence (
1.23
) gives

\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})}\leq\frac{1}{\Phi^{-1}(1)}\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{r}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})},

(
1.23
)
′

provided the order of
\Phi
is equal to
r
.

By Proposition
1.18
it follows that

\mathscr{I}_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}),\quad\mathscr{I}_{p}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{I}_{\sharp}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})

are Banach spaces when
\Phi
is a Young function
and
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Properties valid for both
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})

and
\mathscr{I}_{0,j}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
sometimes impose
similar properties for Schatten-von Neumann classes, which is indicated
in the following proposition.

Proposition 1.20
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces, and let

\mathcal{B}
be a quasi-Banach space of sequences on
\mathbf{Z}_{+}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is a linear map on
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})

such that the following conditions are fulfilled:

•

\mathcal{L}
is a bijective isometry on
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
;

•

\mathcal{L}
restricts to a bijection on
\mathscr{I}_{0,j}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})

for every
j\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}
.

Then the following is true:

(1)

if
T\in\mathscr{I}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
and
j\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}
,
then
\sigma_{j}(\mathcal{L}(T))=\sigma_{j}(T)
;

(2)

\mathcal{L}
restricts to a bijective isometry on

\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
.

Proof.

For any
j\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}
we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}(\mathcal{L}(T))

\displaystyle=\inf_{T_{0}\in\mathscr{I}_{0,j}}\|\mathcal{L}(T)-T_{0}\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}=\inf_{T_{0}\in\mathscr{I}_{0,j}}\|\mathcal{L}(T-T_{0})\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{T_{0}\in\mathscr{I}_{0,j}}\|T-T_{0}\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}=\sigma_{j}(T),

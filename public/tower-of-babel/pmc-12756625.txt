A step-by-step protocol for isolation and flow cytometry analysis of porcine cardiac valve leaflets
Authors: Herra Javed, Zachary Waldrip, Anshaal Furrukh, Eli Contorno, Louis Steen, John Lowery, David Irby, Randall Rainwater, Kristin Whitworth, Marie Burdine, T. Konrad Rajab
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryFlow cytometry analysis of GFP-expressing porcine valves is an indispensable model for studying partial heart transplantation immunobiology. Here, we present a protocol for the systematic isolation and phenotypic characterization of leaflet-derived cells from pulmonary and aortic valves. We describe steps for valve procurement, enzymatic digestion to single-cell suspension, preparation of samples with antibody, and viability staining and gating strategies for accurate and reproducible flow cytometric analysis. This approach provides valuable insight into valve biology and supports rigorous evaluation of cellular dynamics in experimental transplantation models.

Before you begin

Due to the unique similarities that swine share with humans, they have become the favored animal model for cardiovascular research, including research involving the heart valves.1 The establishment of techniques for various isolation and characterization tests means that a range of options can be used to gain data on porcine valves in the research setting.2,3 However, no established protocol for flow cytometry of porcine valves currently exists. This protocol was developed in order to research the immune response and related immunosuppressive therapy associated with partial heart transplantation (PHT). This procedure involves the transplant of a viable truncal valve root harvested from a donor into a recipient.4 Flow cytometry is necessary for the investigation of this immune response as it allows for the number of infiltrating cells such as T-cells to be quantified via the staining protocol described here. The method described is also unique due to the use of green-fluorescent protein (GFP) positive porcine models. The addition of ubiquitously expressed GFP through association with a p-CAGG promoter significantly alters the tissue’s background fluorescence, which means there is potential for overlap in the GFP signal and the signal of positive stains for viability, CD31, and CD3. Due to this, a procedure specifically for GFP was established to ensure the altered background fluorescence did not hinder the accurate interpretation of staining results.

Innovation

This protocol establishes the first validated flow cytometry workflow for quantitative and phenotypic analysis of porcine semilunar valve leaflets. Valve tissue presents major technical challenges due to its dense extracellular matrix, low cell yield, and mixed endothelial and stromal composition. Through systematic optimization of digestion, gating hierarchy and antibody selection, this method achieves high single-cell recovery and reproducible identification of CD31+ endothelial and CD3+ immune cells.

This protocol provides a reproducible framework for valve-specific immune profiling, enabling precise assessment of graft viability, immune infiltration, and donor cell persistence in porcine transplantation models. The protocol has been independently performed by four researchers, across multiple tissue samples, demonstrating replicability of the method.

Animal handling and biohazard training

Before working on this protocol, the individuals must have received the appropriate training for animal handling, and safety training for proper handling and disposal of the hazardous biological materials.

This section describes the procedure for processing and digesting valvular tissues to prepare them for further analysis (Figure 1B).9.Digest the valvular tissue for cell isolation.a.Remove each leaflet tissue from the 15 mL tube and place it in their respective labeled petri dish.b.Dice up the leaflets in 5 slices using a #10 blade until pieces are approximately 0.5 mm in length (Figure 2C).c.Immediately after, transfer the leaflet slices into labeled Eppendorf 1.5 mL tubes containing 1 mL Digest solution (Figure 2D).d.Transfer them to a 37 C incubator for 2 hours while gently agitating the sample using a Tube Revolver Rotator at 25 rpm (Figure 2E).Note: Processing time may vary depending on the size of the leaflet. In general, larger, fibrosed valve leaflets may need additional time for proper dissociation, while smaller, less dense ones tend to dissociate more quickly. However, longer incubation increases the risk of damaging the endothelial cells on the surface of the valve leaflet. Therefore, the incubation time should be optimized to find a balance that allows proper digestion while preserving endothelial integrity for flow cytometry evaluation. Our group experimented with different incubation time points from 5 min to 2.5 hours. We recommend using a minimum digestion time of 1.5 hours, with an ideal incubation time of 2 hours for optimal digestion and viability (Figure 3).

Expected outcomes

In this protocol, we have refined existing flow cytometry techniques for our application in porcine semilunar valves. Single-cell suspensions are obtained from porcine valve leaflets, with minimal red blood cell contamination and debris. The successful execution of this protocol should yield viable cell populations ready for flow cytometric analysis, as indicated by viability staining. Flow cytometry staining of endothelial cells and immune cell markers will provide data on the different subpopulations. The obtained data will show the presence and proportion of GFP-positive cells and T cells in a transplanted valvular graft, allowing for comparison across different immunosuppression regimens. Combined, it will reflect the effect of different immunosuppression regimens post-transplant and systemic immune response to them.

Limitations

This protocol was optimized for flow cytometry analysis of CD31+ endothelial cells and CD3+ T cells from genetically modified green-fluorescent protein porcine partial heart transplant models. Semilunar valve leaflet digestion, antibody panels, and viability dyes were designed for this type of porcine model, especially accounting for GFP fluorescence. Working with additional antibody panels and other types of tissues will likely require optimization. This protocol is designed for extracellular antibody staining and is unsuitable for intracellular staining.

This protocol provides rapid, easy, and effective flow cytometry analysis of porcine transplanted graft status with emphasis on CD31+ endothelial cells and CD3+ T cells; deeper characterization of these cell populations is limited. Moreover, the loss of cells during tissue digestion is a confounder that can be optimized but not eliminated entirely as seen in our initial experiment. This leads to a decreased cell population with each valve leaflet yielding approximately 2 million cells on average.

Flow cytometry provides analysis and quantification of cell populations at single-cell resolution but does not provide any spatial information. We recommend performing histopathologic analysis on the same specimen sections alongside flow cytometry when investigating transplant immunobiology.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Red blood cell contamination in the tissue samples.

Potential solution

During sample procurement (Step 8), be very careful and precise while removing the leaflets. In case of any contamination, wash the tissue in ice-cold PBS to remove loosely associated blood. If visible contamination persists, use a sterile scalpel to dissect any unwanted cells. While RBC lysis buffer can be used to remove remaining RBCs, it was intentionally avoided in this protocol to reduce additional cellular stress. The digestion and mechanical processing steps already impose significant stress on the cells, and further treatment with lysis buffer may impact cell viability. RBC lysis buffer can be considered as an optional step if contamination is significant and interferes with downstream applications but may reduce overall cell recovery.

Problem 2

Low cell viability.

Potential solution

Enzymatic digestion (Steps 9–10) is a critical determinant of sample viability and must be meticulously optimized. Both collagenase P concentration and digestion duration require precise calibration, as excessive enzyme levels or prolonged incubation will drastically reduce the overall viability of cells. CD31+ endothelial cells are particularly vulnerable to overdigestion due to their prolonged direct exposure to enzymatic activity. To preserve viability, immediately transfer samples to ice after the 37°C incubation phase and dilute with buffer solution to stop enzyme activity. Furthermore, enzyme quality directly impacts outcomes: use high-purity, activity-validated collagenase P, adhere strictly to manufacturer-recommended storage conditions, and avoid freeze-thaw cycles to maintain functional stability.

Other general recommendations to increase cell viability in your samples: Immediately following tissue harvest, submerge specimens in ice-cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to mitigate warm ischemia and halt metabolic degradation. Special care should be taken during centrifugation: for unfixed cells, centrifugal forces must not exceed 500 × g to avoid shear stress-induced membrane damage or cell lysis.

Problem 3

Undetectable, insufficient, or inappropriate signals for cell surface markers.

Potential solution

Set up proper controls for reliable flow cytometry analysis (Step 21). Include unstained, single fluorochrome, and fluorescence minus one (FMO) controls to appropriately gate positive cell populations and compensate for spectral spillover. Titrate each antibody to determine optimal staining concentration, and perform an Fc-blocking step before or during cell staining to minimize non-specific binding.

Once again, enzymatic digestion is a critical step since enzymes can cleave cell surface markers (steps 9-10). Enzyme concentration and timing of digestion should be finely optimized. If persistent marker loss occurs, consider substituting collagenase P with a gentler alternative, such as collagenase D, which exhibits distinct substrate specificity and may preserve vulnerable antigens. Post-digestion, validate marker retention using flow cytometry or functional assays to confirm cellular phenotype fidelity.

Problem 4

Lack of statistical power due to too small sample size.

Potential solution

To achieve robust statistical significance, use a minimum of 3 animals per experimental group.

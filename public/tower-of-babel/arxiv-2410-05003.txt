m m -step rational extensions of the trigonometric Darboux-Pöschl-Teller potential based on para-Jacobi polynomials
Y. Grandati, C. Quesne

Abstract
A previous construction of regular rational extensions of the trigonometric Darboux-Pöschl-Teller potential, obtained by one-step Darboux transformations using seed functions associated with the para-Jacobi polynomials of Calogero and Yi, is generalized by considering m m -step Darboux transformations. As a result, some novel families of exceptional orthogonal polynomials depending on m m discrete parameters, as well as m m continuous real ones λ 1 \lambda_{1} , λ 2 \lambda_{2} , …, λ m \lambda_{m} , are obtained. The restrictions imposed on these parameters by the rational extensions regularity conditions are studied in detail.



I
INTRODUCTION

Exceptional orthogonal polynomials (EOPs) are complete families of
orthogonal polynomials that arise as eigenfunctions of a Sturm-Liouville
eigenvalue problem
[
1
]
. Their most apparent difference with
respect to the classical orthogonal polynomials (COPs) of Jacobi, Laguerre,
and Hermite is that there are some gaps in the sequence of their degrees,
the total number of missing “exceptional” degrees being known as the
codimension. In addition, the corresponding differential equation contains
rational coefficients instead of polynomial ones.

Since their appearance, EOPs have generated a lot of research activity both
from a mathematical viewpoint and for their applications in mathematical
physics. In mathematics, studies have been carried out on the properties of
their zeros
[
2
,
3
]
, the sets of recurrence relations
they satisfy
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
, and the search for a full
classification
[
7
,
8
]
. In mathematical physics, EOPs were
shown to be related to the Darboux transformation (DT) in the construction
of exactly solvable rationally-extended quantum potentials
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. This led to multi-indexed families of EOPs connected with
multi-step DT
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
. EOPs also appeared
in connection with exact solutions of the Dirac equation
[
16
]
,
some superintegrable systems
[
17
,
18
]
, or rational solutions
of some Painlevé equations
[
19
]
.

Before 2015, it was thought that apart from the parameters associated with
the corresponding COPs, there only appeared discrete parameters in the
construction of EOPs. In the Jacobi family case, for instance, the most
general known construction was given in terms of a Wronskian-like
determinant of classical Jacobi polynomials, indexed by two partitions
[
20
,
21
]
. Some possibilities of having more general EOP families,
also depending on continuous parameters, then made their appearance.

First, on considering the trigonometric Darboux-Pöschl-Teller (TDPT)
potential, whose bound states are expressed in terms of Jacobi polynomials,
and using the fact, first noticed by Szegö
[
22
]
and emphasized by
Calogero and Yi
[
23
]
, that for some values of the parameters the
Jacobi equation has general polynomial solutions depending on an arbitrary
real parameter
\lambda
and called para-Jacobi polynomials, it was possible
to build some new regular rational extensions of the TDPT potential by
one-step DT
[
24
]
. The eigenstates of such extensions were shown to
be associated with a novel family of
\lambda
-dependent EOPs.

Second, in the cases of the TDPT and isotonic potentials, some new rational
extensions were constructed by considering two-step confluent chains of DT,
that is chains of DT in which the spectral parameters of the different seed
functions converge to the same value
[
25
]
. Both types of
extended potentials are associated with new families of orthogonal
polynomials. In the case of the TDPT potential, the latter depend on a
continuous real parameter. Later on, the confluent DT algorithm was also
used to construct exceptional Legendre polynomials
[
26
]
, as well
as exceptional Gegenbauer ones
[
27
]
with an arbitrary number of
real parameters.

Third, a dualization of Krall dual Hahn polynomials was used to construct
exceptional Hahn polynomials, which in the limit led to exceptional Jacobi
polynomials depending on an arbitrary number of continuous parameters
[
28
]
. The latter were then shown
[
29
]
to be divided in two
classes: some families that are deformations of Jacobi polynomials, as those
determined in
[
25
]
, and other families that are deformations of
standard families of Jacobi EOPs.

The purpose of the present paper is to complete the study made in Ref.
[
24
]
, which was limited to one-step DT. Here, we plan to study in detail
its multi-step version, which will allow us to derive novel families of
exceptional para-Jacobi polynomials depending on an arbitrary number of free
continuous parameters.

The paper is organized as follows. We start by recalling the essential
features of DT and of the TDPT potential in Secs. II and III, respectively.
In Sec. IV, we summarize the results obtained for one-step regular
extensions of the TDPT potential based on para-Jacobi polynomials. In
Sec. V, we study in detail two-step regular extensions of the same. In
Sec. VI, a similar analysis is carried out for
m
-step regular extensions.
Finally, Sec. VII contains the conclusion.

II
DARBOUX TRANSFORMATIONS (DT)

A
One-step DT

We consider a one-dimensional Hamiltonian
\widehat{H}=-d^{2}/dx^{2}+V(x)
,
x\in I\subset\mathbb{R}
and the associated Schrödinger equation

\psi_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(x)+(E_{\lambda}-V(x))\psi_{\lambda}(x)=0,

(2.1)

where
\psi_{\lambda}(x)
is a formal eigenfunction of
\widehat{H}
for
the eigenvalue
E_{\lambda}
and is defined up to a multiplicative
constant. In the following, we suppose that with Dirichlet boundary
conditions on
I
,
\widehat{H}
admits a discrete spectrum of energies and
eigenstates
(E_{n};\psi_{n})_{n\in\left\{0,...,n_{\max}\right\}\subset\mathbb{N}}
where, without loss of generality, we can always suppose that
the ground level of
\widehat{H}
is zero:
E_{0}=0
.

Any formal eigenfunction
\psi_{\nu}(x)
of
V(x)
(i.e., of
\widehat{H}
)
can be used as a seed function for a Darboux transformation (DT)
A(\psi_{\nu})
, which associates with the potential
V(x)
a modified potential

V(x)\overset{A(\psi_{\nu})}{\longrightarrow}V^{(\nu)}(x)=V(x)-2(\log(\psi_{\nu}(x)))^{\prime\prime},

(2.2)

which we call an extension of
V(x)
. The formal eigenfunction of
V^{(\nu)}
associated with the spectral parameter
E_{\lambda}
is given by the
Darboux-Crum formulas
[
30
]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{\lambda}^{\left(\nu\right)}(x)\sim\frac{W\left(\psi_{\nu},\psi_{\lambda}\mid x\right)}{\psi_{\nu}(x)},\text{ if }\lambda\neq\nu,\\
\psi_{\nu}^{\left(\nu\right)}(x)\sim\frac{1}{\psi_{\nu}(x)},\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

where
W(y_{1},...,y_{m}\mid x)
denotes the Wronskian of the family of
functions
y_{1}(x),...,y_{m}(x)

[
31
]
.

W\left(y_{1},...,y_{m}\mid x\right)=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}y_{1}\left(x\right)&...&y_{m}\left(x\right)\\
...&&...\\
y_{1}^{\left(m-1\right)}\left(x\right)&...&y_{m}^{\left(m-1\right)}\left(x\right)\end{array}\right|.

(2.4)

This last possesses the following useful properties
[
31
]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}W\left(uy_{1},...,uy_{m}\mid x\right)=u(x)^{m}W\left(y_{1},...,y_{m}\mid x\right)\\
W\left(y_{1},...,y_{m}\mid x\right)=\left(\frac{dz}{dx}\right)^{m(m-1)/2}W\left(y_{1},...,y_{m}\mid z\right).\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

B
Chains of DT

At the formal level, the DT can be straightforwardly iterated and a chain of

m
DT can be completely characterized by the
m
-tuple
(\nu_{1},...,\nu_{m})
of spectral indices of the successive seed functions used in the
chain. We denote such a
m
-tuple by a capital letter
N_{m}
, where the
index
m
indicates the length of the chain
N_{m}
. Let
\psi_{\lambda}^{\left(N_{m}\right)}
be the formal eigenfunction associated with the
eigenvalue
E_{\lambda}
of the potential
V^{\left(N_{m}\right)}(x)
.

A chain is non-degenerate if all the spectral indices
\nu_{i}
of the
chain
N_{m}
are distinct and is degenerate if some of them are repeated in
the chain. For non-degenerate chains, Crum has derived very useful formulas
for the extended potentials and their eigenfunctions in terms of Wronskians
of eigenfunctions of the initial potential
[
30
]
.

Crum’s formulas

When all the
\nu_{j}
and
\lambda
are
distinct, we have

\psi_{\lambda}^{\left(N_{m}\right)}(x)=\frac{W^{\left(N_{m},\lambda\right)}\left(x\right)}{W^{\left(N_{m}\right)}\left(x\right)}

(2.6)

and

V^{\left(N_{m}\right)}(x)=V(x)-2\left(\log W^{\left(N_{m}\right)}\left(x\right)\right)^{\prime\prime},

(2.7)

where
W^{\left(N_{m}\right)}\left(x\right)=W\left(\psi_{\nu_{1}},...,\psi_{\nu_{m}}\mid x\right)
.

These formulas can be extended to degenerate chains if we adopt the
convention to suppress any pair of repeated indices in the set of spectral
indices associated with the chain.

III
TDPT POTENTIAL

A
Spectrum

The trigonometric Darboux-Pöschl-Teller (TDPT) potential (with zero
ground-state energy) is defined on
]0,\pi/2[
by

V(x;\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\left(\alpha+1/2\right)(\alpha-1/2)}{\sin^{2}x}+\frac{\left(\beta+1/2\right)(\beta-1/2)}{\cos^{2}x}-(\alpha+\beta+1)^{2},

(3.1)

and is a confining potential for
\left|\alpha\right|,\left|\beta\right|>1/2
. On introducing the variable
z=\cos 2x
, it can be
rewritten as

V(x;\alpha,\beta)=\frac{2\left(\alpha+1/2\right)(\alpha-1/2)}{1-z}+\frac{2\left(\beta+1/2\right)(\beta-1/2)}{1+z}-(\alpha+\beta+1)^{2}.

(3.2)

For
\alpha,\beta>1/2
, the physical spectrum of the TDPT potential
associated with the asymptotic Dirichlet boundary conditions

\psi\left(0^{+};\alpha,\beta\right)=0=\psi\left(\left(\pi/2\right)^{-};\alpha,\beta\right)

(3.3)

is given in terms of Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}(z)
by
\left(\left(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}\right)=\left(\alpha+n,\beta+n\right)\right)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{n}\left(x;\alpha,\beta\right)=\psi_{0}\left(x;\alpha,\beta\right)P_{n}^{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\left(z\right)\\
E_{n}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=\left(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}+1\right)^{2}-\left(\alpha+\beta+1\right)^{2}=4n\left(\alpha+\beta+1+n\right)\end{array}\right.,n\in\mathbb{N},

(3.4)

where

\psi_{0}\left(x;\alpha,\beta\right)=2^{\alpha+\beta+1}\left(\sin x\right)^{\alpha+1/2}\left(\cos x\right)^{\beta+1/2}=(1-z)^{\left(\alpha+1/2\right)/2}(1+z)^{\left(\beta+1/2\right)/2}

(3.5)

and

\displaystyle P_{n}^{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(-1\right)^{n-k}\binom{n+\alpha}{k}\binom{n+\beta}{n-k}(1-z)^{n-k}(1+z)^{k}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}\Gamma\left(n+\beta+1\right)}{n!\Gamma\left(n+\alpha+\beta+1\right)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(-1\right)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\frac{\Gamma\left(n+\alpha+\beta+1+k\right)}{2^{k}\Gamma\left(\beta+1+k\right)}(1+z)^{k}.

The quasipolynomial eigenfunction (i.e., which is, up to a gauge factor,
polynomial in the adapted variable
z
)
\psi_{n}\left(x;\alpha,\beta\right)
is then the particular solution of the Schrödinger equation

\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V\left(x;\alpha,\beta\right)-E_{n}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)\right)\psi\left(x\right)=0,

(3.7)

which satisfies the Dirichlet boundary conditions (
3.3
).

B
Para-Jacobi polynomials

Suppose that
\alpha
and
\beta
are two positive integers

\alpha=N\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast},\qquad\beta=M\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}.

(3.8)

In this case, as noticed by Szegö
[
22
]
and emphasized by
Calogero and Yi
[
23
,
24
]
, for values of
n
such that
[
32
]

\max\left(N,M\right)\leq n<N+M,

(3.9)

one obtains the general solution of the Schrödinger equation

\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V\left(x;N,M\right)-E_{-n-1}\left(N,M\right)\right)\psi\left(x\right)=0,

(3.10)

which is of the quasipolynomial form

\psi_{-n-1}\left(z;N,M;\lambda\right)=\psi_{-1}\left(z;N,M\right)p_{n}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda\right),

(3.11)

where, up to a constant factor,

\displaystyle\psi_{-1}\left(z;N,M\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{0}\left(z;-N,-M\right)

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{\left(-N+1/2\right)/2}(1+z)^{\left(-M+1/2\right)/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\psi_{0}\left(z;N-1,M-1\right)}.

(3.12)

In Eq. (
3.11
),
p_{n}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda\right)
is a polynomial depending on an arbitrary real parameter
\lambda

and is called
para-Jacobi polynomial
(In the following,
\psi_{-n-1}
will be called a
para-Jacobi (PJ) eigenfunction
). It has
the (monic) form
[
23
]

\displaystyle p_{n}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-2\right)^{n}\left(n-M\right)!n!}{\left(2n-M-N\right)!}\Theta_{n,1}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}(z)

(3.13)

\displaystyle+\lambda\frac{\left(-2\right)^{n}\left(2n-M-N+1\right)!\left(M+N-n-1\right)!}{\left(n-N\right)!}\Theta_{n,2}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}(z),

where

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\Theta_{n,1}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}(z)=\sum_{k=M}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}\left(n-M-N+k\right)!}{2^{k}k!\left(k-M\right)!\left(n-k\right)!}(1+z)^{k}\\
\Theta_{n,2}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{N+M-n-1}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}\left(M-1-k\right)!}{2^{k}k!\left(N+M-n-1-k\right)!\left(n-k\right)!}(1+z)^{k}\end{array}\right.,

(3.14)

with
N,M>0
, and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\Theta_{n,1}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}(-1)=0\\
\Theta_{n,2}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}(-1)=\frac{\left(M-1\right)!}{\left(N+M-n-1\right)!n!}\end{array}\right..

(3.15)

We have in particular

p_{n}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(-1;\lambda\right)=\left(-1\right)^{n}\lambda b_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)},

(3.16)

where

b_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}=\frac{2^{n}\left(2n-N-M+1\right)!\left(M-1\right)!}{n!\left(n-N\right)!}.

(3.17)

The para-Jacobi polynomials satisfy the derivation property

\overset{.}{p}_{n}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda\right)=np_{n-1}^{\left(-N+1,-M+1\right)}\left(z;a_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}\lambda\right),

(3.18)

with

a_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}=\frac{M+N-n-1}{n}.

(3.19)

We also have the symmetry property
[
23
]

p_{n}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(-z;\lambda\right)=\left(-1\right)^{n}p_{n}^{\left(-M,-N\right)}\left(z;g_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}\left(\lambda\right)\right),

(3.20)

where
g_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}
is the affine function

g_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}\left(\lambda\right)=\left(-1\right)^{n-M}\left(\left(-1\right)^{n-N+1}\lambda+\lambda_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right),

(3.21)

with

\lambda_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}=\frac{n!\left(n-M\right)!\left(n-N\right)!}{\left(2n-N-M\right)!\left(2n-N-M+1\right)!\left(N+M-n-1\right)!}>0.

(3.22)

This implies in particular (cf Eq. (
3.16
))

p_{n}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(1;\lambda\right)=b_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}g_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}\left(\lambda\right).

(3.23)

Note the following useful identities

\lambda_{n-1}^{\left(N-1,M-1\right)}=a_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}\lambda_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)},

(3.24)

b_{n-1}^{\left(N-1,M-1\right)}=\frac{n}{2\left(M-1\right)}b_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)},

(3.25)

and

b_{n}^{\left(M,N\right)}=\frac{\left(N-1\right)!\left(n-N\right)!}{\left(M-1\right)!\left(n-M\right)!}b_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}.

(3.26)

IV
ONE-STEP REGULAR EXTENSIONS OF THE TDPT POTENTIAL

For
\max(N,M)\leq n<N+M
, we have
E_{-n-1}(N,M)<0
and
\psi_{-n-1}\left(z;N,M;\lambda\right)
is disconjugated on
x\in

]0,\pi/2[

(z\in

]-1,+1[)
and consequently admits at most one zero on this
interval. As proven in
[
24
]
,
\psi_{-n-1}
has no node there iff
the parameters satisfy the following
regularity conditions
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}(M,n-N)\in\left(2\mathbb{N}\right)^{2}:0<\lambda<\lambda_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)};\\[5.69046pt]
(M,n-N)\in 2\mathbb{N}\times\left(2\mathbb{N}+1\right):\lambda<-\lambda_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}\text{ or }\lambda>0;\\[5.69046pt]
(M,n-N)\in\left(2\mathbb{N}+1\right)\times 2\mathbb{N}:\lambda<0\text{
or }\lambda>\lambda_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)};\\[5.69046pt]
(M,n-N)\in\left(2\mathbb{N}+1\right)^{2}:-\lambda_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)}<\lambda<0.\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

With the choices above, we can then use
\psi_{-n-1}
as seed function of
a one-step DT to produce a rational extension of the TDPT potential, which
is perfectly regular on
]0,\pi/2[
. This extended potential is given by

[
24
]

V^{(-n-1)}\left(x;N,M;\lambda\right)=V\left(x;N-1,M-1\right)-4(N+M)-2\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\log(p_{n}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda\right))

(4.2)

and can be continuously modulated by varying the parameter
\lambda
on the
adapted interval.

Its spectrum is given by
E_{k}\left(N,M\right)
,
k\in\left\{-n-1,0,1,...,\right\}
, with the corresponding eigenstates:

\psi_{-n-1}^{\left(-n-1\right)}\left(x;N,M;\lambda\right)=\frac{1}{\psi_{-n-1}\left(x;N,M;\lambda\right)}=\psi_{0}\left(x;N-1,M-1\right)\frac{1}{p_{n}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda)}

(4.3)

and

\displaystyle\psi_{k}^{\left(-n-1\right)}\left(x;N,M;\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{W\left(\psi_{-n-1}(x;N,M;\lambda),\psi_{k}(x;N,M)\mid x\right)}{\psi_{-n-1}\left(x;N,M;\lambda\right)}

\displaystyle=\psi_{-1}(x;N,M)\frac{dz}{dx}\frac{W\left(p_{n}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda),(1-z)^{N}(1+z)^{M}P_{k}^{(N,M)}(z)\mid x\right)}{p_{n}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda)}

\displaystyle\ \propto\frac{\psi_{-1}(x;N-1,M-1)}{p_{n}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda)}

\displaystyle\times\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}p_{n}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda)&(1-z)^{N}(1+z)^{M}P_{k}^{(N,M)}(z)\\
np_{n-1}^{\left(-N+1,-M+1\right)}\left(z;a_{n}^{(N,M)}\lambda\right)&-2(k+1)(1-z)^{N-1}(1+z)^{M-1}P_{k+1}^{(N-1,M-1)}(z)\end{array}\right|

for
k=0,1,\ldots
. Here we have used Eq. (
3.18
), Eq. (
2.5
), and the
following derivation rule of Jacobi polynomials
[
34
]

\frac{d}{dz}\left((1-z)^{N}(1+z)^{M}P_{k}^{(N,M)}(z)\right)=-2(k+1)(1-z)^{N-1}(1+z)^{M-1}P_{k+1}^{(N-1,M-1)}(z),

(4.7)

which also makes its appearance in connection with the shape
invariance of the TDPT potential
[
35
]
.

Then

\psi_{k}^{\left(-n-1\right)}\left(x;N,M;\lambda\right)=\frac{\psi_{0}(x;N-1,M-1)}{p_{n}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda)}Q_{k}^{\left(n\right)}\left(z;N,M;\lambda\right),

(4.8)

where the
Q_{k}^{\left(n\right)}
’s are given by
Q_{-n-1}^{\left(n\right)}\left(z;N,M;\lambda\right)=1
and, for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{\left(n\right)}\left(z;N,M;\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}p_{n}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda)&(1-z^{2})P_{k}^{(N,M)}(z)\\
np_{n-1}^{\left(-N+1,-M+1\right)}\left(z;a_{n}^{(N,M)}\lambda\right)&-2(k+1)P_{k+1}^{(N-1,M-1)}(z)\end{array}\right|

(4.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2(k+1)P_{k+1}^{(N-1,M-1)}(z)p_{n}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda)

\displaystyle-n(1-z^{2})P_{k}^{(N,M)}(z)p_{n-1}^{\left(-N+1,-M+1\right)}\left(z;\frac{M+N-n-1}{n}\lambda\right),

The set of
Q_{k}^{(n)}(z;N,M;\lambda)
,
k=-n-1,0,1,2,\ldots
, is made of
orthogonal polynomials on
]-1,1[
with respect to the measure

\mu_{n}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda\right)=\frac{(1-z)^{N-1}(1+z)^{M-1}}{\left(p_{n}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda\right)\right)^{2}}.

(4.13)

V
TWO-STEP REGULAR EXTENSIONS OF THE TDPT POTENTIAL

A
Regularity conditions

Consider now a two-step chain of DT based on PJ seed functions
\psi_{-n_{1}-1}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}\right)
and
\psi_{-n_{2}-1}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{2}\right)
,

V\left(x;N,M\right)\overset{A(\psi_{-n_{1}-1})}{\longrightarrow}V^{\left(-n_{1}-1\right)}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}\right)\overset{A\left(\psi_{-n_{2}-1}^{\left(-n_{1}-1\right)}\right)}{\longrightarrow}V^{\left(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1\right)}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right),

(5.1)

where

\psi_{-n_{2}-1}^{\left(-n_{1}-1\right)}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)=\frac{W\left(\psi_{-n_{1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}),\psi_{-n_{2}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{2})\mid x\right)}{\psi_{-n_{1}-1}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}\right)}.

(5.2)

We are interested in chains for which both extensions
V^{\left(-n_{1}-1\right)}
and
V^{\left(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1\right)}
are regular.
For such a purpose, we have first to choose
\max\left(N,M\right)\leq n_{1}<N+M
and
\lambda_{1}
in order to satisfy the regularity condition (
4.1
), which ensures that the seed function
\psi_{-n_{1}-1}
has
no node on
]0,\pi/2[
. We then choose
n_{2}
in order that the seed
function of the second DT
\psi_{-n_{2}-1}^{\left(-n_{1}-1\right)}
be in
the disconjugacy sector of
V^{\left(-n_{1}-1\right)}
, namely

E_{-n_{1}-1}\left(N,M\right)-E_{-n_{2}-1}\left(N,M\right)=4(n_{1}-n_{2})\left(n_{1}+n_{2}-N-M+1\right)>0.

(5.3)

This is achieved if

\max\left(N,M\right)\leq n_{2}<n_{1}<N+M.

(5.4)

V^{\left(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1\right)}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)
is then regular when
\psi_{-n_{2}-1}^{\left(-n_{1}-1\right)}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)
is nodeless on
]0,\pi/2[
. Since
\psi_{-n_{1}-1}
does not change its sign on this interval, this
corresponds to

\mathrm{sign}\left(W\left(\psi_{-n_{1}-1},\psi_{-n_{2}-1}\mid 0\right)\right)=\mathrm{sign}\left(W\left(\psi_{-n_{1}-1},\psi_{-n_{2}-1}\mid\pi/2\right)\right).

(5.5)

But we have

\displaystyle W\left(\psi_{-n_{1}-1},\psi_{-n_{2}-1}\mid x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2(1-z)^{-N+1}(1+z)^{-M+1}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\times W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{1}\right),p_{n_{2}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{2}\right)\mid z\right),

from which we deduce that the preceding condition can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathrm{sign}\left(W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{1}\right),p_{n_{2}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{2}\right)\mid 1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sign}\left(W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{1}\right),p_{n_{2}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{2}\right)\mid-1\right)\right).

(5.7)

On using Eqs. (
3.16
), (
3.18
), (
3.19
), and (

), we obtain

\displaystyle W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{1}\right),p_{n_{2}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{2}\right)\mid-1\right)

(5.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}}b_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\lambda_{1}&\left(-1\right)^{n_{2}}b_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\lambda_{2}\\
n_{1}\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}-1}b_{n_{1}-1}^{\left(N-1,M-1\right)}a_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\lambda_{1}&n_{2}\left(-1\right)^{n_{2}-1}b_{n_{2}-1}^{\left(N-1,M-1\right)}a_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\lambda_{2}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\frac{b_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}b_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}}{2(M-1)}(n_{2}-n_{1})\left(n_{1}+n_{2}-N-M+1\right)\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2},

or, with Eq. (
5.3
),

\displaystyle W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{1}\right),p_{n_{2}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{2}\right)\mid-1\right)

(5.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}\frac{b_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}b_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}}{8(M-1)}\left(E_{-n_{1}-1}\left(N,M\right)-E_{-n_{2}-1}\left(N,M\right)\right)\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}.

On the other hand (see Eqs. (

can be written as

\displaystyle W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{1}\right),p_{n_{2}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{2}\right)\mid+1\right)

(5.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}p_{n_{1}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(1;\lambda_{1}\right)&p_{n_{2}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(1;\lambda_{2}\right)\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\frac{b_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}b_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}}{2(N-1)}\times A,

where (see Eqs. (

), and (

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}-N+1}\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}&\left(-1\right)^{n_{2}-N+1}\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\\
n_{1}^{2}a_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\left(\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}-N+1}\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right)&n_{2}^{2}a_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\left(\left(-1\right)^{n_{2}-N+1}\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right)\end{array}\right|

(5.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}-N+1}\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right)\left(\left(-1\right)^{n_{2}-N+1}\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right)}{4}

\displaystyle\times\left(E_{-n_{1}-1}\left(N,M\right)-E_{-n_{2}-1}\left(N,M\right)\right).

Consequently

\displaystyle W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{1}\right),p_{n_{2}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{2}\right)\mid+1\right)

(5.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}b_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}}{8(N-1)}\left(\lambda_{1}+\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}-N+1}\lambda_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right)\left(\lambda_{2}+\left(-1\right)^{n_{2}-N+1}\lambda_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(E_{-n_{1}-1}\left(N,M\right)-E_{-n_{2}-1}\left(N,M\right)\right).

By comparing Eq. (
5.12
) and Eq. (
5.21
), the regularity

) for the two-step extension
V^{\left(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1)\right)}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)
can
be written as

\mathrm{sign}\left(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\right)=\mathrm{sign}\left(\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}+\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}-N+1}\lambda_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right)\left(\lambda_{2}+\left(-1\right)^{n_{2}-N+1}\lambda_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right)\right).

(5.22)

Since the regularity condition for the first extension
V^{-n_{1}-1}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}\right)
(see
[

) implies that

\mathrm{sign}\left(\lambda_{1}\right)=\mathrm{sign}\left(\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}}g_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\left(\lambda_{1}\right)\right)=\mathrm{sign}\left(\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}-N-M+1}\left(\lambda_{1}+\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}-N+1}\lambda_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right)\right),

(5.23)

Eq. (
5.22
) simply becomes

\mathrm{sign}\left(\lambda_{2}\right)=\mathrm{sign}\left(\left(-1\right)^{n_{2}-N-M}\lambda_{2}+\left(-1\right)^{M-1}\lambda_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right).

(5.24)

Consider first the case where
M
,

, and
n_{2}-N
are all even. The
regularity of
V^{\left(-n_{1}-1\right)}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}\right)

. Then

) becomes

\mathrm{sign}\left(\lambda_{2}\right)=\mathrm{sign}\left(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{n_{2}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right).

(5.25)

This condition is always achieved when

\lambda_{2}>0
provided

(5.26)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}(M,n_{1}-N,n_{2}-N)\in\left(2\mathbb{N}\right)^{3}:0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)},\lambda_{2}<0\text{ or }\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{n_{2}}^{(N,M)};\\[5.69046pt]

\text{ or }\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{n_{2}}^{(N,M)};\\[5.69046pt]

-\lambda_{n_{2}}^{(N,M)}<\lambda_{2}<0;\\[5.69046pt]

(5.27)

B
Rationally-extended potential

If
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
satisfy conditions (
5.27
), then
\psi_{-n_{1}-1}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}\right)
and
\psi_{-n_{2}-1}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{2}\right)
can be
used as seed functions to build a two-step state-adding chain of DT, which
generates a regular rational extension of
V\left(x;N-2,M-2\right)
,

\displaystyle V^{-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\quad=V(x;N,M)-2\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\log W\left(\psi_{-n_{1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}),\psi_{-n_{2}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{2})\mid x\right)

\displaystyle\quad=V(x;N,M)-2\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\log\left(\psi_{-1}^{2}(x;N,M)\frac{dz}{dx}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-2\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\log W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1}),p_{n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{2})\mid z\right)

\displaystyle\quad=V(x;N-2,M-2)+E_{-2}(N,M)

\displaystyle\qquad-2\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\log W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1}),p_{n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{2})\mid z\right),

(5.28)

where use has been made of (
2.5
) as well as of the relation

\psi_{-1}^{2}(z;N,M)\frac{dz}{dx}\propto\psi_{-1}(z;N,M)\psi_{-1}(z;N-1,M-1),

(5.29)

the shape invariance property of the TDPT potential,

V(x;N,M)-2\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\log\psi_{-1}(z;N,M)=V(x;N-1,M-1)+E_{-1}(N,M),

(5.30)

and the relation

E_{-2}(N,M)=E_{-1}(N,M)+E_{-1}(N-1,M-1).

(5.31)

It only remains to make the change of variable from
x
to
z
to get the
final result

\displaystyle V^{(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1)}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\quad=V(x;N-2,M-2)+E_{-2}(N,M)

\displaystyle\qquad-8(1-z^{2})\frac{d^{2}}{dz^{2}}\log W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1}),p_{n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{2})\mid z\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+8z\frac{d}{dz}\log W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1}),p_{n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{2})\mid z\right).

(5.32)

For
V^{(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1)}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
to be a confining
potential on
]0,\pi/2[
, we need to impose this property to
V(x;N-2,M-2)
.
This is achieved for
N,M\geq 3
. For such a choice and provided
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
satisfy Eq. (
5.4
),
V^{(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1)}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
is strongly repulsive in both 0 and
\pi/2
, so that in each
extremity only one basis solution is quadratically integrable and the
corresponding Hamiltonian is essentially self-adjoint
[
33
]
.

The corresponding eigenstates are given by

\displaystyle\psi_{k}^{(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1)}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{W(\psi_{-n_{1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),\psi_{-n_{2}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),\psi_{k}(x;N,M)\mid x)}{W(\psi_{-n_{1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),\psi_{-n_{2}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\mid x)},

(5.33)

for
k=0,1,2,\ldots
, as well as

\displaystyle\psi_{-n_{1}-1}^{(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1)}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\psi_{-n_{2}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}{W(\psi_{-n_{1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),\psi_{-n_{2}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\mid x)},

(5.34)

and

\displaystyle\psi_{-n_{2}-1}^{(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1)}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\psi_{-n_{1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}{W(\psi_{-n_{1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),\psi_{-n_{2}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\mid x)}.

(5.35)

The corresponding energies are
E_{k}(N,M)
(
k=0,1,2,\ldots
),
E_{-n_{1}-1}(N,M)
, and
E_{-n_{2}-1}(N,M)
, respectively.

On using (
2.5
) again, as well as derivative properties of
Jacobi and para-Jacobi polynomials, Eqs. (
5.33
), (
5.34
),
and (
5.35
) can be rewritten as

\psi_{k}^{(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1)}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\propto\psi_{0}(x;N-2,M-2)\frac{Q_{k}^{(n_{1},n_{2})}(z;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}{T_{n_{1}n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})},

(5.36)

where

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{(n_{1},n_{2})}(z;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=4(k+1)(k+2)T_{n_{1}n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})P_{k+2}^{(N-2,M-2)}(z)

\displaystyle\quad-2(k+1)U_{n_{1}n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})(1-z^{2})P_{k+1}^{(N-1,M-1)}(z)

\displaystyle\quad+V_{n_{1}n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})(1-z^{2})^{2}P_{k}^{(N,M)}(z),\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots,

(5.37)

\displaystyle Q_{-n_{1}-1}^{(n_{1},n_{2})}(z;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=p_{n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{2}),

(5.38)

\displaystyle Q_{-n_{2}-1}^{(n_{1},n_{2})}(z;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=p_{n_{1}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1}).

(5.39)

In these equations, we have defined

\displaystyle T_{n_{1}n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\quad=n_{2}p_{n_{1}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1})p_{n_{2}-1}^{(-N+1,-M+1)}\left(z;a_{n_{2}}^{(N,M)}\lambda_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-n_{1}p_{n_{1}-1}^{(-N+1,-M+1)}\left(z;a_{n_{1}}^{(N,M)}\lambda_{1}\right)p_{n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{2}),

(5.40)

\displaystyle U_{n_{1}n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\quad=n_{2}(n_{2}-1)p_{n_{1}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1})p_{n_{2}-2}^{(-N+2,-M+2)}\left(z;a_{n_{2}-1}^{(N-1,M-1)}a_{n_{2}}^{(N,M)}\lambda_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-n_{1}(n_{1}-1)p_{n_{1}-2}^{(-N+2,-M+2)}\left(z;a_{n_{1}-1}^{(N-1,M-1)}a_{n_{1}}^{(N,M)}\lambda_{1}\right)p_{n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{2}),

(5.41)

\displaystyle V_{n_{1}n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\quad=n_{1}n_{2}(n_{2}-1)p_{n_{1}-1}^{(-N+1,-M+1)}\left(z;a_{n_{1}}^{(N,M)}\lambda_{1}\right)p_{n_{2}-2}^{(-N+2,-M+2)}\left(z;a_{n_{2}-1}^{(N-1,M-1)}a_{n_{2}}^{(N,M)}\lambda_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-n_{1}(n_{1}-1)n_{2}p_{n_{1}-2}^{(-N+2,-M+2)}\left(z;a_{n_{1}-1}^{(N-1,M-1)}a_{n_{1}}^{(N,M)}\lambda_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times p_{n_{2}-1}^{(-N+1,-M+1)}\left(z;a_{n_{2}}^{(N,M)}\lambda_{2}\right).

(5.42)

Due to the orthogonality properties of the
\psi_{k}^{(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1)}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
, we deduce that the
Q_{k}^{(n_{1},n_{2})}(z;N,M;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
,
k=-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1,0,1,2,\ldots
, are orthogonal polynomials
on
]-1,1[
with respect to the measure

\mu_{n_{1}n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\frac{(1-z)^{N-2}(1+z)^{M-2}}{\left(T_{n_{1}n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right)^{2}}.

(5.43)

\displaystyle\Omega\left(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{16}{3}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-1

(5.52)

and

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{3}{1-z^{2}}-17+32(1-z^{2})\left(\frac{A\left(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)}{\Omega\left(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)}\right)^{2}-16\frac{B\left(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)}{\Omega\left(z;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)},

(5.53)

with

A
Regularity conditions

Consider now an
m
-step chain
N_{m}=(-n_{1}-1,-n_{2}-1,\ldots,-n_{m}-1)
of
DT based on PJ seed functions
\psi_{-n_{1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1})
,
\psi_{-n_{2}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{2})
, …,
\psi_{-n_{m}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{m})
,

\displaystyle V\left(x;N,M\right)\overset{A(\psi_{-n_{1}-1})}{\longrightarrow}V^{\left(-n_{1}-1\right)}\left(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}\right)\overset{A\left(\psi^{(-n_{1}-1)}_{-n_{2}-1}\right)}{\longrightarrow}\cdots

\displaystyle\overset{A\left(\psi^{(N_{m}-2)}_{-n_{m-1}-1}\right)}{\longrightarrow}V^{(N_{m-1})}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m-1})\overset{A\left(\psi^{(N_{m-1})}_{-n_{m}-1}\right)}{\longrightarrow}V^{(N_{m})}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}),

(6.1)

where

\displaystyle\psi^{(N_{m-1})}_{-n_{m}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{W(\psi_{-n_{1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}),\psi_{-n_{2}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{2}),\ldots,\psi_{-n_{m}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{m})\mid x)}{W(\psi_{-n_{1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}),\psi_{-n_{2}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{2}),\ldots,\psi_{-n_{m-1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{m-1})\mid x)}.

(6.2)

On assuming that the extension obtained at the
(m-1)
th step is regular,
which imposes that
\max\left(N,M\right)\leq n_{m-1}<\cdots<n_{2}<n_{1}<N+M
and definite conditions on
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m-1}
, we want to impose that the same is true at the

m
th step. For this to occur,
\psi^{(N_{m-1})}_{-n_{m}-1}
must be in the
disconjugacy sector of
V^{(N_{m-1})}
, i.e.,
\max\left(N,M\right)\leq n_{m}<n_{m-1}<\cdots<n_{1}<N+M
, and, in addition, it must not change sign
on
]0,\pi/2[
, which leads to the condition

\displaystyle\mathrm{sign}(W(\psi_{-n_{1}-1},\psi_{-n_{2}-1},\ldots,\psi_{-n_{m}-1}\mid 0))

\displaystyle\quad=\mathrm{sign}(W(\psi_{-n_{1}-1},\psi_{-n_{2}-1},\ldots,\psi_{-n_{m}-1}\mid\pi/2)).

(6.3)

Since standard properties of Wronskians (
2.5
) lead to

\displaystyle W(\psi_{-n_{1}-1},\psi_{-n_{2}-1},\ldots,\psi_{-n_{m}-1}\mid x)\propto(1-z)^{\frac{m}{2}\left(-N+\frac{m}{2}\right)}(1+z)^{\frac{m}{2}\left(-M+\frac{m}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\times W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1}),p_{n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{2}),\ldots,p_{n_{m}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{m})\mid z\right),

(6.4)

condition (
6.3
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle{\rm sign}\left(W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{1}\right),\ldots,p_{n_{m}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{m}\right)\mid 1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad={\rm sign}\left(W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{1}\right),\ldots,p_{n_{m}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda_{m}\right)\mid-1\right)\right).

(6.5)

The derivation rule for the para-Jacobi polynomials (
3.18
)
leads to

\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}p_{n_{j}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(z;\lambda\right)=\left(n_{j}\right)_{\underline{k}}p_{n_{j}-k}^{\left(-N+k,-M+k\right)}\left(z;A_{j}^{(k)}\lambda\right),

(6.6)

where (cf Eq. (
3.19
))

A_{j}^{(k)}=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{n_{j}-i}^{\left(N-i,M-i\right)}=\frac{\left(N+M-n_{i}-1\right)_{\underline{k}}}{\left(n\right)_{\underline{k}}},

(6.7)

(x)_{\underline{p}}
being the falling factorial

(x)_{\underline{l}}=x(x-1)...(x-l+1).

(6.8)

This gives

W\left(p_{n_{1}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{1}),p_{n_{2}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{2}),\ldots,p_{n_{m}}^{(-N,-M)}(z;\lambda_{m})\mid z\right)=\det\left[\mathbf{R}\left(z;\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}\right)\right],

(6.9)

where the elements of the matrix are given by

\mathbf{R}_{i,j}\left(z;\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}\right)=\left(n_{j}\right)_{\underline{i-1}}p_{n_{j}-i+1}^{\left(-N+i-1,-M+i-1\right)}\left(z;A_{j}^{(i-1)}\lambda_{j}\right).

(6.10)

Condition (
6.3
) can then be rewritten as

\mathrm{sign}\left(\det\left[\mathbf{R}\left(1;\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}\right)\right]\right)=\mathrm{sign}\left(\det\left[\mathbf{R}\left(-1;\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}\right)\right]\right).

(6.11)

Consider first the right-hand side of Eq. (
6.11
). By using
Eqs. (
3.16
), (
3.17
), and since, from (
3.25
),

b_{n-k}^{\left(N-k,M-k\right)}=\frac{\left(n\right)_{\underline{k}}}{2^{k}(M-1)_{\underline{k}}}b_{n}^{\left(N,M\right)},

(6.12)

we obtain for
\det\left[\mathbf{R}_{i,j}\left(-1;\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}\right)\right]
,

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}\lambda_{1}\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}}b_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}&...&\lambda_{m}\left(-1\right)^{n_{m}}b_{n_{m}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\\
...&&...\\
\lambda_{1}\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}-k}A_{1}^{(k)}\frac{\left(\left(n_{1}\right)_{\underline{k}}\right)^{2}}{2^{k}(M-1)_{\underline{k}}}b_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}&...&\lambda_{m}\left(-1\right)^{n_{m}-k}A_{m}^{(k)}\frac{\left(\left(n_{m}\right)_{\underline{k}}\right)^{2}}{2^{k}(M-1)_{\underline{k}}}b_{n_{m}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\\
...&&...\\
\lambda_{1}\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}-m+1}A_{1}^{(m-1)}\frac{\left(\left(n_{1}\right)_{\underline{m-1}}\right)^{2}}{2^{m-1}(M-1)_{\underline{m-1}}}b_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}&...&\lambda_{m}\left(-1\right)^{n_{m}-m+1}A_{m}^{(m-1)}\frac{\left(\left(n_{m}\right)_{\underline{m-1}}\right)^{2}}{2^{m-1}(M-1)_{\underline{m-1}}}b_{n_{m}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\end{array}\right|

(6.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}...\lambda_{m}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}+...+n_{m}+1+...+\left(m-1\right)}b_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}...b_{n_{m}}^{\left(N,M\right)}}{2^{1+...+\left(m-1\right)}\left(M-1\right)^{m-1}\left(M-2\right)^{m-2}...(M-m+1)}D(n_{1},...,n_{m}),

where

\displaystyle D(n_{1},...,n_{m})

\displaystyle\quad=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&...&1\\
...&&...\\
A_{1}^{(k)}\left(\left(n_{1}\right)_{\underline{k}}\right)^{2}&...&A_{m}^{(k)}(\left(n_{m}\right)_{\underline{k}})^{2}\\
...&&...\\
A_{1}^{(m-1)}(\left(n_{1}\right)_{\underline{m-1}})^{2}&...&A_{m}^{(m-1)}(\left(n_{m}\right)_{\underline{m-1}})^{2}\end{array}\right|.

Then

\displaystyle\det\left[\mathbf{R}_{ij}\left(-1;\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\lambda_{1}...\lambda_{m}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}+...+n_{m}+m\left(m-1\right)/2}b_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}...b_{n_{m}}^{\left(N,M\right)}}{2^{m\left(m-1\right)/2}\left(M-1\right)^{m-1}\left(M-2\right)^{m-2}...(M-m+1)}D(n_{1},...,n_{m}),

\displaystyle D(n_{1},...,n_{m})

\displaystyle\quad=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&...&1\\
...&&...\\

(6.28)

and from Eqs. (
3.18

6.7

\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}p_{n_{i}}^{\left(-N,-M\right)}\left(1;\lambda\right)=\left(n_{i}\right)_{\underline{k}}b_{n_{i}-k}^{\left(N-k,M-k\right)}\left(-1\right)^{n_{i}-M}\left(\left(-1\right)^{n_{i}-N+1}A_{i}^{(k)}\lambda+\lambda_{n_{i}-k}^{\left(N-k,M-k\right)}\right).

(6.29)

With (cf Eq. (

(6.30)

we therefore arrive at

This simplifies into

\displaystyle\det\left[\mathbf{R}\left(1;\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}+...+n_{m}-mM}\left(\left(-1\right)^{n_{1}-N+1}\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right)...\left(\left(-1\right)^{n_{m}-N+1}\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{n_{m}}^{\left(N,M\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{b_{n_{1}}^{\left(N,M\right)}...b_{n_{m}}^{\left(N,M\right)}}{2^{m\left(m-1\right)/2}\left(M-1\right)^{m-1}\left(M-2\right)^{m-2}...(M-m+1)}D(n_{1},...,n_{m}),

(6.33)

-step chain reduces to

(6.39)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}(M,n_{m}-N)\in\left(2\mathbb{N}\right)^{2}:0<\lambda_{m}<\lambda_{n_{m}}^{(N,M)};\\[5.69046pt]

B
Rationally-extended potential

If
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m}
satisfy the
above-mentioned conditions, the
m
-step chain generates a regular potential

\displaystyle V^{(N_{m})}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})

\displaystyle\quad=V(x;N,M)-2\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\log W(\psi_{-n_{1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}),\ldots,\psi_{-n_{m}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{m})\mid x),

(6.41)

which turns out to be a rational extension of
V(x;N-m,M-m)

\displaystyle V^{(N_{m})}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})

\displaystyle\quad=V(x;N-m,M-m)+E_{-m}(N,M)

\displaystyle\qquad-8(1-z^{2})\frac{d^{2}}{dz^{2}}\log\left(\det\left[\mathbf{R}\left(z;\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+8z\frac{d}{dz}\log\left(\det\left[\mathbf{R}(z;\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m})\right]\right),

(6.42)

where the matrix elements of
\mathbf{R}(z;\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m})

6.10

5.32
) in

\displaystyle W(\psi_{-n_{1}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1}),\psi_{-n_{2}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{2}),\ldots,\psi_{-n_{m}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{m})\mid x)

\displaystyle\quad=\psi_{-1}^{m}(x;N,M)\left(\frac{dz}{dx}\right)^{m(m-1)/2}\det\left[\mathbf{R}\left(z;\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}\right)\right],

(6.43)

(6.44)

potential.

\displaystyle\psi^{(N_{m})}_{k}(x;N,M;\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})

(6.47)

for

, 2, …,
m
, with corresponding energies

,

, 1,
2, … and
E_{-n_{i}-1}
,

, 2, …,
m
, respectively. Here,

\psi_{-n_{i}-1}(x;N,M;\lambda_{i})
is excluded from the Wronskian.

\frac{d^{l}}{dz^{l}}\left((1-z)^{N}(1+z)^{M}P_{k}^{(N,M)}(z)\right)=\left(-2\right)^{l}(k+l)_{\underline{l}}(1-z)^{N-l}(1+z)^{M-l}P_{k+l}^{(N-l,M-l)}(z).

VII
CONCLUSION

In this article, we have studied the multi-step version of a previous
construction of the TDPT potential regular rational extensions obtained by
one-step DT using seed functions associated with the para-Jacobi polynomials
of Calogero and Yi. We have shown that the eigenstates of such
m
-step
extensions are expressed in terms of novel families of EOPs, orthogonal on
]-1,+1[
and depending not only on
m
discrete parameters, but also on
m

real continuous parameters
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
, …,
\lambda_{m}
.
The sets of parameters are related by some restrictions coming from the TDPT
rational extensions regularity conditions, which we study in detail.

Looking for possible relationships between our results and those obtained by
using other approaches for generating EOPs depending on an arbitrary number
of continuous parameters would be an interesting open question for future
work.

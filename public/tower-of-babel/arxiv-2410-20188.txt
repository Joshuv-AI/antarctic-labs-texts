Bernstein-Sato functional equations for ideals in positive characteristic
Siyong Tao, Zida Xiao, Huaiqing Zuo

Abstract
For an ideal of a regular ℂ \mathbb{C} -algebra, its Bernstein-Sato polynomial is the monic polynomial of the lowest degree satisfying an Bernstein-Sato functional equation. We generalize the notion of Bernstein-Sato functional equations to the case of ideals in an F F -finite ring of positive characteristic p p , and show the relationship between these equations and Bernstein-Sato roots. By applying this theory, we provide an explicit description of Bernstein-Sato roots of a weighted homogeneous polynomial with an isolated singularity at the origin in characteristic p p . Moreover, we give multiplicative and additive Thom-Sebastiani properties for the set of Bernstein-Sato roots, which prove the characteristic p p analogue of Budur and Popa’s question. Keywords. Bernstein-Sato functional equations, Bernstein-Sato roots, F F -threhsolds. MSC(2020). 14B05, 32S05.

Preliminaries

2.1
Differential operators

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field, and let
R
be a commutative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra.
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(R)
denotes the ring of
\mathbb{K}
-linear endomorphisms of
R
.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix an integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
D^{n}_{R/\mathbb{K}}
is the
R
-module of all
\mathbb{K}
-linear differential operators on
R
of order
\leq n
. It can be explicitly described as follows:

1.

D^{0}_{R/\mathbb{K}}=\mathrm{End}_{R}(R)
. We identity
R
with
D^{0}(R/\mathbb{K})
.

2.

D^{n}_{R/\mathbb{K}}=\{\phi\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{K}}(R):[\phi,r]\in D^{n-1}_{R/\mathbb{K}}\}
. Here
[\phi,r]
is the commutator
\phi\circ r-r\circ\phi
.

The ring of
\mathbb{K}
-linear differential operators on
R
is defined by
D_{R/\mathbb{K}}=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}D^{n}_{R/\mathbb{K}}
.

Now we suppose that
\mathbb{K}
is a perfect field of prime characteristic
p
. For every
e\in\mathbb{N}
, let
R^{p^{e}}=\{r^{p^{e}}:r\in R\}
denote the subring of
p^{e}
-th powers of
R
. We assume that
R
is
F
-finite, i.e.,
R
is
R^{p^{e}}
-finite for every
e
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
Yek92
]
)
.

The ring of
\mathbb{K}
-linear differential operators on
R
is independent of the choice of the perfect ground field
\mathbb{K}
. It is given by

D_{R/\mathbb{K}}=\bigcup_{e\in\mathbb{N}}\textbf{D}^{e}_{R},\quad\text{where}\;\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}=\mathrm{End}_{R^{p^{e}}}(R).

We call the
R
-algebra
\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}
the ring of differential operators of level
e
on
R
. Throughout the paper, we will not emphasize the perfect field
\mathbb{K}
, as every
F
-finite ring of characteristic
p
can be seen as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra.

Let
S
be a multiplicatively closed subset of
R
. Any element of
S^{-1}R
has the form
\frac{r}{s^{p}}
, where
r\in R
and
s\in S
. So
S^{-1}R
is
F
-finite.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
BJNnB19
, Remark 2.18], [
Qui21
, Proposition 2.30]
)
.

There are compatible isomorphisms of
S^{-1}R
-algebras

S^{-1}R\otimes_{R}\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cong\textbf{D}^{e}_{S^{-1}R},\quad e\in\mathbb{N}.

They give rise to an
S^{-1}R
-algebra isomorphism
S^{-1}R\otimes_{R}D_{R}\to D_{S^{-1}R}
. Given any
\phi\in D_{R}
, we still denote its image under the localization map
D_{R}\to S^{-1}R\otimes_{R}D_{R}
by
\phi
. Action of
\phi
on
S^{-1}R
can be interpreted as:

1.

If
\phi\in D^{0}_{R/\mathbb{K}}
, then
\phi(\frac{r}{s})=\frac{\phi(r)}{s}
.

2.

If
\phi\in D^{n}_{R/\mathbb{K}}
, we assume that the action on
S^{-1}R
has been defined for every differential operator on
R
of order
\leq n-1
, then

\phi\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)=\frac{\phi(r)-[\phi,s](\frac{r}{s})}{s}.

3.

If
\phi\in\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}
, then
\phi(\frac{r}{s})=\frac{\phi(s^{p^{e}-1}r)}{s^{p^{e}}}
.

2.2
Higher Euler operators on the polynomial ring

Let
R
be an
F
-finite ring of characteristic
p
. Fix an integer
r\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the
F
-finite polynomial ring
R[\mathbf{t}]=R[t_{1},\dots,t_{r}]
. We introduce the following notation.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{r}),\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{r})
be
r
-tuples of
\mathbb{N}
.

•

Define the partial order on
r
-tuples:
\mathbf{a}\leq\mathbf{b}
if and only if if
a_{i}\leq b_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. Let
\mathbf{0}_{r}=(0,\dots,0)
and
\mathbf{1}_{r}=(1,\dots,1)
. The set
\textbf{N}_{r}(e)
consists of all
r
-tuples
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
such that
\mathbf{a}\leq(p^{e}-1)\mathbf{1}_{r}
.

•

The generalized binomial coefficient
\binom{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{a}}
is
\prod^{r}_{i=1}\binom{b_{i}}{a_{i}}
.

•

Let
u\in R
,
u\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{a}}
denotes the monomial
ut_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots t_{r}^{a_{r}}
. If
u\neq 0
, degree of
u\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{a}}
is the integer
|\mathbf{a}|=\sum^{r}_{i=1}a_{i}
.

For each
\phi\in D_{R}
and
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, the divided power differential operator
\phi\,\partial^{[\mathbf{a}]}_{\mathbf{t}}
acts on
R[\mathbf{t}]
by

\phi\,\partial^{[\mathbf{a}]}_{\mathbf{t}}:u\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{b}}\mapsto\begin{cases}\phi(u)\binom{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}},&\text{if}\;\mathbf{a}\leq\mathbf{b},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

An operator
\phi
on
R
can be seen as the operator
\phi\,\partial^{[\mathbf{0}_{r}]}_{\mathbf{t}}
on
R[\mathbf{t}]
. If
\phi\in\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}
and
\mathbf{a}\in\textbf{N}_{r}(e)
, then
\phi\,\partial^{[\mathbf{a}]}_{\mathbf{t}}\in\textbf{D}^{e}_{R[\mathbf{t}]}
(This comes from Lucas’s Theorem (
6
)). Usually we take
\phi=1
(the identity map).

Lemma 2.4

(
[
JNnBQG23
, Lemma 2.13]
)
.

For each
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
\textbf{D}^{e}_{R[\mathbf{t}]}
is a
\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}
-module generated by

\{\partial^{[\mathbf{a}]}_{\mathbf{t}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{b}}\}_{\mathbf{a}\in\textbf{N}_{r}(e),\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}.

Every
\varphi\in\textbf{D}^{e}_{R[\mathbf{t}]}
can be written as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear combination of operators of the form
\phi\,\partial^{[\mathbf{a}]}_{\mathbf{t}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{b}}
with
\phi\in\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}
,
\mathbf{a}\in\textbf{N}_{r}(e)
and
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
.

Fix an integer
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
(D_{R[\mathbf{t}]})_{i}
be the
D_{R}
-submodule of
D_{R[\mathbf{t}]}
generated by
\{\partial^{[\mathbf{a}]}_{\mathbf{t}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{b}}\}_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{r},|\mathbf{b}|-|\mathbf{a}|=i}
. The family
\{(D_{R[\mathbf{t}]})_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
characterizes the
V
-filtration along
\langle{\mathbf{t}}\rangle=\langle{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}}\rangle
as

V^{i}D_{R[\mathbf{t}]}=\{\varphi\in D_{R[\mathbf{t}]}:\varphi\cdot\langle{\mathbf{t}}\rangle^{j}\subseteq\langle{\mathbf{t}}\rangle^{j+i}\;\text{for all}\;j\in\mathbb{Z}\}=\bigoplus_{j\geq i}(D_{R[\mathbf{t}]})_{j}.

When
i\geq 0
, it follows that
V^{i}D_{R[\mathbf{t}]}=(D_{R[\mathbf{t}]})_{0}\cdot\langle{\mathbf{t}}\rangle^{i}
.

(D_{R[\mathbf{t}]})_{0}
plays an significant role. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the
k
-th Euler operator
\vartheta_{\mathbf{t},k}
is the
R
-linear operator

\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}:|\mathbf{a}|=k}(-1)^{k}\partial^{[\mathbf{a}]}_{\mathbf{t}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{a}}.

\vartheta_{\mathbf{t},0}
is the identity map.
\vartheta_{\mathbf{t},k}
sends
\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{b}}
to
\binom{-|\mathbf{b}|-r}{k}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{b}}
. Actually, by observing that

\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}:|\mathbf{a}|=k}(-1)^{k}\binom{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{a}}=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}:|\mathbf{a}|=k}\binom{-\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{1}_{r}}{\mathbf{a}},

the right hand side corresponds to the coefficient of
x^{k}
in the Taylor series expansion of
\prod^{r}_{i=1}(1+x)^{-b_{i}-1}=(1+x)^{-|\mathbf{b}|-r}
, which is precisely
\binom{-|\mathbf{b}|-r}{k}
.

2.3
F
-thresholds,
\nu
-invariants and
F
-jumping exponents

We fix the following notation. Let
I=\langle{f_{1},\dots,f_{m}}\rangle
be a non-zero ideal of
R
. For every integer
e\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the following ideals associated to
I
.

•

I^{[p^{e}]}=\langle{f^{p^{e}}:f\in I}\rangle=\langle{f_{1}^{p^{e}},\dots,f_{r}^{p^{e}}}\rangle
.

•

I^{[\frac{1}{p^{e}}]}
is the unique smallest ideal
J
of
R
with respect to inclusion and such that
I\subseteq J^{[p^{e}]}
.

Example 1

(
[
BMS09
, Proposition 2.5]
)
.

Suppose that
R
is free over
R^{p}
. Immediately,
R
is free over
R^{p^{e}}
. Let
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be the
R^{p^{e}}
-basis of
R
. For an ideal
I=\langle{f_{1},\dots,f_{m}}\rangle
of
R
with
f_{i}=\sum^{m}_{i=1}g_{ij}^{p^{e}}u_{j}
, we have

I^{[\frac{1}{p^{e}}]}=\{g_{ij}:1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n\}.

In this case,
\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}
is an
R
-free algebra generated by
\{u^{*}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, where
u^{*}_{i}
is given on the basis by
u^{*}_{i}(u_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
(the Kronecker symbol). It is clear that for all
e\in\mathbb{N}
, there is

\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I=\{\phi(f):\phi\in\textbf{D}^{e}_{R},f\in I\}=(I^{[\frac{1}{p^{e}}]})^{[p^{e}]}.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
containing
I
in its radical. For every integer
e\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e})=\max\{n\in\mathbb{N}:I^{n}\not\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{[p^{e}]}\}.

And we call the set
\nu^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})=\{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e}):R\supsetneqq\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}\supseteq I\}

\nu
-invariants of level
e
for
I
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For ideals
R\supsetneq\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}\supseteq I
, the sequence
\{\frac{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\}_{e\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. And we have the inequality

\frac{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e_{1}+e_{2}})}{p^{e_{1}+e_{2}}}-\frac{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e_{1}})}{p^{e_{1}}}\leq\frac{\mu(I)}{p^{e_{1}}},\quad\text{for all}\;e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathbb{N}.

(4)

Here
\mu(I)
is the minimum of the number of generators of
I
.

Proof.

It is immediate to see that
\mathfrak{a}^{(p^{e}-1)\mu(\mathfrak{a})+1}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{[p^{e}]}
. Suppose that
I^{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
, we obtain

\frac{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\frac{N((p^{e}-1)\mu(\mathfrak{a})+1)}{p^{e}}<N(\mu(\mathfrak{a})+1).

(
4
) follows from
[
DSNnBP18
, Lemma 3.3]
. If
I=\langle{f}\rangle
is a principal ideal, we can express (
4
) by saying that
if
f^{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{\langle{f}\rangle}(p^{e})+1}\in\mathfrak{a}^{[p^{e}]}
, then
f^{p(\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{\langle{f}\rangle}(p^{e})+1)}\in\mathfrak{a}^{[p^{e+1}]}
.
∎

(
4
) implies that the limit

\textbf{c}^{\mathfrak{a}}(I)=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}

exists, see
[
DSNnBP18
, Theorem 3.4]
, and we call it the
F
-thresholds of
I
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
. For simplicity, if
I=\langle{f}\rangle
is a principal ideal,
\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{\langle{f}\rangle}(p^{e}),\textbf{c}^{\mathfrak{a}}(\langle{f}\rangle)
will be denoted by
\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{f}(p^{e})
and
\textbf{c}^{\mathfrak{a}}(f)
, respectively.

For properties that involve the
F
-thresholds, from now on we suppose that
R
is a regular ring. By Kunz’s theorem
[
Kun69
, Corollary 2.7]
, the regularity condition implies that the
e
-th iterated Frobenius map
F^{e}
is flat (or
R
is
R^{p^{e}}
-flat) for some (or equivalently, all)
e\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
BMS08
]
,
[
BMS09
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

For any element
f\in R
and ideals
I,J
of
R
, one verifies that:

1.

f^{[p^{e}]}\in I^{[p^{e}]}
if and only if
f\in I
.
I^{[p^{e}]}\subseteq J^{[p^{e}]}
if and only if
I\subseteq J
.

2.

\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I=(I^{[\frac{1}{p^{e}}]})^{[p^{e}]}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Under the assumptions above, for ideals
R\supsetneqq\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}\supseteq I
, the sequence
\{\frac{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\}
is non-decreasing. Moreover, if
I\cap R^{\circ}\neq\varnothing
(
R^{\circ}
is the set of elements of
R
which are not contained in any minimal prime ideals of
R
), then we have the following properties for all integers
e\in\mathbb{N}
.

1.

\frac{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}<\textbf{c}^{\mathfrak{a}}(I)
.

2.

For any
f\in\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}
,
\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{f}(p^{e})=\lceil p^{e}\textbf{c}^{\mathfrak{a}}(f)\rceil-1
. Here
\lceil x\rceil
is the smallest integer
\geq x
.

Proof.

We have
\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e+1})\geq p\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e})
. The inequality is a consequence of Lemma
2.7
, (1). Hence

\frac{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\frac{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e+1})}{p^{e+1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\textbf{c}^{\mathfrak{a}}(I)

(5)

as claimed. To prove (1), if
p^{e_{0}}\textbf{c}^{J}(I)=\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e_{0}})
for some
e_{0}
, then by (
5
), for all
e\in\mathbb{N}
, there is

\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e_{0}+e})=p^{e}\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e_{0}})\quad\text{and}\quad I(I^{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e_{0}})})^{p^{e}}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{[p^{e+e_{0}}]}=(\mathfrak{a}^{[p^{e_{0}}]})^{[p^{e}]}.

Choose
u\in I\cap R^{\circ}
, for any
g\in I^{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e_{0}})}
,
ug^{p^{e}}\in(\mathfrak{a}^{[p^{e_{0}}]})^{[p^{e}]}
for all
e\in\mathbb{N}
. This leads to
g\in\mathfrak{a}^{[p^{e_{0}}]}
. Hence

I^{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{I}(p^{e_{0}})}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{[p^{e_{0}}]}.

A contradiction, since every ideal in a regular ring of characteristic
p
is tighted closed (
[
HH90
]
). Then for any
f\in\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}
, Lemma
2.6
and (1) say that for all
e\in\mathbb{N}
, there is

\frac{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{f}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}<\textbf{c}^{\mathfrak{a}}(f)\leq\frac{\nu^{\mathfrak{a}}_{f}(p^{e})+1}{p^{e}}.

This finishes the proof of (2).
∎

Definition 2.9
.

For an ideal
I
of
R
and a non-negative real number
c\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, the generalized test ideal of
I
with exponent
c
is defined as

\tau(I,c)=\bigcup_{e\in\mathbb{N}}(I^{\lceil cp^{e}\rceil})^{[\frac{1}{p^{e}}]}=(I^{\lceil cp^{e_{0}}\rceil})^{[\frac{1}{p^{e_{0}}}]}\;\text{for all sufficiently large}\;e_{0}.

The second equality is based on the fact that the ascending chain

\cdots\subseteq(I^{\lceil cp^{e}\rceil})^{[\frac{1}{p^{e}}]}\subseteq(I^{\lceil cp^{e+1}\rceil})^{[\frac{1}{p^{e+1}}]}\subseteq\cdots

is stationary, see
[
BMS08
, Lemma 2.8]
.

Definition 2.10
.

The family of test ideals
\{\tau(I,c)\}_{c\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
is right continuous (
[
BMS08
, Corollary 2.16]
). For any
c\geq 0
, there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that
\tau(I,c)=\tau(I,c^{\prime})
for all
c^{\prime}\in[c,c+\varepsilon)
.
\lambda\geq 0
is called an
F
-jumping exponent of
I
if
\tau(I,\lambda-\varepsilon)\supsetneqq\tau(I,\lambda)
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\lambda]
.

The set of all
F
-jumping exponents of
I
is denoted by
\textbf{FJ}(I)
.
0
is always an
F
-jumping exponent.
\textbf{FJ}(I)
is a discrete subset contained in
\mathbb{Q}
, see
[
BMS08
, Theorem 3.1]
. When
I=\langle{f}\rangle
is a principal ideal, we denote
\tau(\langle{f}\rangle,c)
and
\textbf{FJ}(\langle{f}\rangle)
by
\tau(f,c)
and
\textbf{FJ}(f)
, respectively.

Lemma 2.11

(
[
BMS08
, Corollary 2.30], [
QG21b
, Proposition 4.2]
)
.

Fix an ideal
I
of
R
.

1.

\nu^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})
,
\nu
-invarints of level

for
I
, is equal to
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n}\supsetneqq\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n+1}\}
. We call
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})
the set of different jumps of level

for

.

\textbf{FJ}(I)

3
Continuous functions on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

The concept of the algebra of continuous functions on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(or
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}
) is a perfect tool to discribe the Bernstein-Sato functional equations in positive characteristic.
Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be the ring of
p
-adic integers with the
p
-adic metric
|\cdot|_{p}
. Fix an integer
e\in\mathbb{N}
, for a
p
-adic integer
\mathcal{x}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{x}^{[i]}p^{i}
, we call
\langle{\mathcal{x}}\rangle_{e}=\sum^{e-1}_{i=0}\mathcal{x}^{[i]}p^{i}
the
e
-th truncation of
\mathcal{x}
. Every integer
a\in\mathbb{N}
can be uniquely written as a finite sum
a=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}a^{[i]}p^{i}
with
a^{[i]}\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
and
a^{[i]}=0
for all sufficiently large
i
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
T
be an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra. We equip
T
with the discrete topology. Let
\textbf{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
be the set of

continuous maps from
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to
T
. For any
e\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
be the subset of
\textbf{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
that consists of those maps
\Phi
, where

\Phi(\mathcal{x})=\Phi(\mathcal{y}),\quad\text{whenever}\;\mathcal{x}-\mathcal{y}\in p^{e}\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
and
\textbf{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
admit the
T
-algebra structures by pointwise addition and multiplication.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that
T
is discrete,
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a compact metrizable space, so any continuous map
\Phi\in\textbf{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
is uniformly locally constant. There exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that

\Phi(\mathcal{x})=\Phi(\mathcal{y}),\quad\text{whenever}\;|\mathcal{x}-\mathcal{y}|_{p}<\varepsilon.

Then
\Phi\in\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
when
e>-\log_{p}\varepsilon
. This implies that
\textbf{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)=\cup_{e\in\mathbb{N}}\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
.

We will need the following facts.

1.

T\cong\textbf{C}^{0}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
, to each element of
T
we associate the corresponding constant map.

2.

For any
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subalgebra
S\subseteq T
,
\textbf{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
can be naturally viewed as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subalgebra of
\textbf{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
.

3.

For any
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}T\cong\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
. Every map in
\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear combination of the maps of the form
\texttt{F}\cdot v
, where
\texttt{F}\in\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})\subseteq\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
and
v\in T=\textbf{C}^{0}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix an integer
e\in\mathbb{N}
. For
k\in\textbf{N}_{1}(e)
, i.e.,
k=\{0,1,\dots,p^{e}-1\}
, we define the functions

\texttt{B}_{k}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{F}_{p},\quad\mathcal{x}\mapsto\binom{\langle{\mathcal{x}}\rangle_{e}}{k}\bmod p.

They make up an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{Z}
is dense in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between
\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})
and the
p^{e}
-periodic
functions from
\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. It can be expressed as follows.

•

For any
\texttt{F}\in\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})
. The restriction of
\texttt{F}\in\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})
on
\mathbb{Z}
is a
p^{e}
-periodic function.

•

Every
p^{e}
-periodic function from
\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
can be uniquely extended to a continuous function in
\textbf{C}^{e}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})

\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle P_{\mathbf{w},e}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\textbf{N}_{n}(e)}\texttt{B}_{p^{e-1}}(|\mathbf{a}|_{\mathbf{w}}+|\mathbf{w}|)\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}\partial^{[(p^{e}-1)\mathbf{1}_{n}]}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{x}^{(p^{e}-1)\mathbf{1}_{n}-\mathbf{a}}

Let
f
be a non-constant weighted homogeneous polynomial of type
(\mathbf{w},d)
satisfying (
17

\{\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{u}_{i}}+\mathrm{Jac}\,(f)\}_{i=0}^{m}
is a monomial

-basis

-vector space. We assume that

\mathcal{S}
denote the set
\{|\mathbf{u}_{i}|_{\mathbf{w}}\}_{i=0}^{m}

is a homogeneous ideal in the graded ring

6
Thom-Sebastiani properties of Bernstein-Sato roots

In this section, we take
F
-finite regular rings
R
and
S
of characteristic
p
. Let
I
be an ideal of
R
and
J
an ideal of
S
.
\mathfrak{B}=I\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}J
can be seen as an ideal of
Q=R\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}S
, generated by
\{f\otimes g\}_{f\in I,g\in J}
. We define
\mathfrak{A}\\
=\langle{I,J}\rangle
to be the ideal
I\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}S+R\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}J

Lemma 6.1
.

Under the assumptions above, for each
e\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a
Q
-module isomorphism

\textbf{T}^{e}_{R,S}:\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\mapsto\textbf{D}^{e}_{Q},\quad\textbf{T}^{e}_{R,S}(\phi\otimes\psi):f\otimes g\mapsto\phi(f)\otimes\phi(g),

where the
Q
-module structure on
\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}
is given by

(r\otimes s)(\phi\otimes\psi)=(r\phi)\otimes(s\psi),\quad\text{for all}\;r\in R,s\in S,\phi\in\textbf{D}^{e}_{R},\psi\in\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}.

These

-module isomorphisms induce a
Q
-algebra isomorphism
\textbf{T}_{R,S}:D_{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}D_{S}\to D_{Q}
.

Proof.

By Kunz’s theorem, for each
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
R
is
R^{p^{e}}
-flat.

is Noetherian, so
R
is a locally free
R^{p^{e}}
-module. Let
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in R^{p^{e}}
be such that

generate the unit ideal of

, and

is

-free. Write
u_{i}
as
\widetilde{u}_{i}^{p^{e}}
for some

.

generate the unit ideal of
R
, and
R_{\widetilde{u}_{i}}=R_{u_{i}}
is free over
(R^{p^{e}})_{u_{i}}=(R_{\widetilde{u}_{i}})^{p^{e}}
.
Repeat the argument for
S
, let

be such that

generate the unit ideal of
S^{p^{e}}
, and

is
(S^{p^{e}})_{v_{i}}
-free. Write

for some
\widetilde{v}_{i}\in S
.
\{\widetilde{v}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
generate the unit ideal of

, and

is

-free. Note that
\{w_{ij}=\widetilde{u}_{i}\otimes\widetilde{v}_{j}\}_{1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m}
generate the unit ideal of

, it suffices to show that for every

Theorem 6.2
.

We keep the notation introduced at the beginning of this section.

1.

\textbf{BSR}(\mathfrak{A})=\{\mathcal{x}+\mathcal{y}:\mathcal{x}\in\textbf{BSR}(I),\mathcal{y}\in\textbf{BSR}(J)\}
.

2.

Suppose that
I\not\subseteq\mathrm{nil}(R)
and
J\not\subseteq\mathrm{nil}(S)
. Then
\textbf{BSR}(\mathfrak{B})=\textbf{BSR}(I)\cup\textbf{BSR}(J)
.

Proof.

By
[
JNnBQG23
, Lemma 3.11]
, for each
e\in\mathbb{N}
, there is

\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})=\{n\in\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n}\supsetneqq\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n+1}\}\supseteq\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e+1})=\{n\in\textbf{D}^{e+1}_{R}\cdot I^{n}\supsetneqq\textbf{D}^{e+1}_{R}\cdot I^{n+1}\}.

So
\mathcal{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a Bernstein-Sato root of
I
if and only if there is an infinite subset
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathbb{N}
and
\nu_{e_{i}}\in\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e_{i}})
such that
\mathcal{x}
is the limit of
\{\nu_{e_{i}}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. The same conclusion applies to
J
,
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
.

To prove
(1)
, we show that for each
e\in\mathbb{N}
, there is
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{A}}(p^{e})=\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})+\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{J}(p^{e})
. It implies that
\textbf{BSR}(\mathfrak{A})\supseteq\textbf{BSR}(I)+\textbf{BSR}(J)
. As
\mathfrak{A}^{n}=\sum^{n}_{a=0}I^{a}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}J^{n-a}
, by Lemma
6.1
,

\textbf{D}^{e}_{Q}\cdot\mathfrak{A}^{n}=\sum^{n}_{a=0}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\textbf{D}^{e}_{S})\cdot(I^{a}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}J^{n-a})=\sum^{n}_{a=0}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{a})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n-a}).

(23)

If for every
0\leq a\leq n
, either
a\notin\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})
or
n-a\notin\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{J}(p^{e})
, then

(\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{a})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n-a})\subseteq(\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{a+1})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n-a})+(\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{a})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n+1-a})

By (
23
) we have
n\notin\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{A}}(p^{e})
. Hence
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{A}}(p^{e})\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})+\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{J}(p^{e})
. In the other direction, for any integers
n_{1}\in\\
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})
and
n_{2}\in\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{J}(p^{e})
, we choose
u\in\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}}\backslash\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}+1}
and
v\in\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n_{2}}\backslash\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n_{2}+1}
. Note that

\textbf{D}^{e}_{Q}\cdot\mathfrak{A}^{n_{1}+n_{2}+1}\subseteq\langle{\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}+1},\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n_{2}+1}}\rangle,

thus
u\otimes v\in\textbf{D}^{e}_{Q}\cdot\mathfrak{A}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\backslash\textbf{D}^{e}_{Q}\cdot\mathfrak{A}^{n_{1}+n_{2}+1}
. Otherwise, the image of
u\otimes v
in
Q/\langle{\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}+1},\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n_{2}+1}}\rangle\cong\\
(R/\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}+1})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(S/\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n_{1}+1})
is zero. But this is impossible. Therefore,
n_{1}+n_{2}\in\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{A}}(p^{e})
.

Let
\mathcal{z}
be a Bernstein-Sato root of
\mathfrak{A}
. There is a sequence
\{\nu_{e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\nu_{e}\in\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{A}}(p^{e})
such that
\nu_{e}
converges to
\mathcal{z}
. Each
\nu_{e}
has the form
\nu_{I,e}+\nu_{J,e}
with
\nu_{I,e}\in\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})
and
\nu_{J,e}\in\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{J}(p^{e})
. As
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a compact metrizable space,
\{\nu_{I,e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{N}}
has a convergent subsequence
\{\nu_{I,e_{i}}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. If
\nu_{I,e_{i}}
converges to
\mathcal{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, then
\mathcal{x}\in\textbf{BSR}(I)
, and
\nu_{J,e_{i}}
converges to
\mathcal{z}-\mathcal{x}\in\textbf{BSR}(J)
. And we get the conclusion
\textbf{BSR}(\mathfrak{A})\subseteq\textbf{BSR}(I)+\textbf{BSR}(J)
.

To prove (2), it suffices to demonstrate that
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{B}}(p^{e})=\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})\cup\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{J}(p^{e})
for all
e\in\mathbb{N}
. As
\mathfrak{B}^{n}=I^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}J^{n}
. By applying Lemma
6.1
, we have

\textbf{D}^{e}_{Q}\cdot\mathfrak{B}^{n}=(\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\textbf{D}^{e}_{S})\cdot\mathfrak{B}^{n}=(\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n}).

It follows that
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{B}}(p^{e})\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})\cup\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{J}(p^{e})
.

Now we show the inclusion in the other direction. Given
n_{1}\in\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})
, in fact,
\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}+1}
and
\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n_{1}}
are
non-zero
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-flat modules, we obtain the following injective homomorphisms

(\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}+1})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n_{1}+1})\to(\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}+1})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n_{1}})\to(\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n_{1}}).

The cokernel of the second homomorphism is given by

\frac{\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}}}{\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}+1}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n_{1}})\neq 0.

This implies that
(\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n_{1}})\supsetneqq(\textbf{D}^{e}_{R}\cdot I^{n_{1}+1})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\textbf{D}^{e}_{S}\cdot J^{n_{1}+1})
. So

. By applying the same approach to

, we can show that

. Hence
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{B}}(p^{e})\supseteq\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{I}(p^{e})\cup\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{J}(p^{e})
.

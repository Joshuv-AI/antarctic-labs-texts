On endomorphism algebras of silting complexes over hereditary abelian categories Thanks: This work was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant No. 12571040)
Wei Dai, Changjian Fu, Liangang Peng

Abstract
. Let ℰ \mathcal{E} be the class of finite-dimensional algebras isomorphic to endomorphism algebras of silting complexes over hereditary abelian categories. It is proved that the class ℰ \mathcal{E} is closed under taking idempotent quotients, idempotent subalgebras and τ \tau -reduction. We also show that the proper class consisting of shod algebras is also closed under these operations. In addition, several classic classes of algebras—including laura, glued, weakly shod algebras—are proved to be closed under idempotent quotients, thereby generalizing a known result originally established for specific idempotents.

1.
Introduction

For a given algebra
A
, the study of its idempotent subalgebra
eAe
and the idempotent quotient
A/AeA
associated with an idempotent
e\in A
is a central theme in representation theory (see, for instance,
[
CPS
,
AC
,
Xi
,
Xu
,
CK
]
). A primary motivation for focusing on these two constructions originates from the theory of recollements of derived categories. It is well known that under suitable conditions, the bounded derived category
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)
can be “glued” from
\mathcal{D}^{b}(eAe)
and
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A/AeA)
via a recollement
[
CPS
]
. This framework provides a powerful mechanism for homological reduction, allowing one to decompose the homological properties of
A
—such as the finiteness of its global dimension—into those of
eAe
and
A/AeA

[
AKLY
]
.

From this perspective, establishing the closure properties of a specific class of algebras under these operations is of fundamental importance. It transforms the class into a self-contained system where global conjectures and structural problems can be tackled through recursive arguments. For example, the fact that the class of
2
-Calabi-Yau tilted algebras arising from hereditary categories is closed under idempotent quotients was a key step in proving the connectedness of cluster-tilting graphs for hereditary categories
[
FG
]
. Similarly, the proof of the connectedness of
\tau
-tilting graphs for gentle algebras is based on the fact that the class of gentle algebras is closed under
\tau
-reduction
[
FGLZ
]
.

In the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras, the study of endomorphism algebras of canonical objects—such as tilting modules, support
\tau
-tilting modules, and (2-term) silting complexes—is a central theme. These constructions define several fundamental classes of algebras, including tilted, quasi-tilted, silted, and quasi-silted algebras. A landmark result by Happel, Reiten, and Smalø
[
HRS
]
established that almost hereditary algebras are precisely the endomorphism algebras of tilting objects in hereditary abelian categories. This categorical characterization was further extended by Buan and Zhou
[
BZa
,
BZb
]
, who demonstrated that shod algebras can be realized as endomorphism algebras of 2-term silting complexes over hereditary abelian categories. As a further generalization, endomorphism algebras of silting complexes over a finite-dimensional hereditary algebras were investigated in
[
ALLT
]
from the view point of structure of categories.

The purpose of this work is to investigate the class
\mathcal{E}
of finite-dimensional algebras realized as endomorphism algebras of basic silting complexes over hereditary abelian categories, specifically focusing on their behavior under the operations of taking idempotent subalgebras and idempotent quotients. Employing the machinery of silting reduction in triangulated categories, we develop an approach to analyze the stability of this class of algebras. Our main results, which are interconnected and proved using this method, establish the following closure properties.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.3
, Theorem
3.5
)
.

Let

and

(1)

A/AeA\in\mathcal{E}
. Moreover, if

is quasi-silted, then so is
A/AeA
.

(2)

. Moreover, if

We remark that the final assertion of (2) was previously established in
[

Note that the idempotent quotient can be viewed as a special case of the

is closed under the more general operation of

Let

Let
\mathcal{T}

\operatorname{\mathsf{thick}}D=\bigcup\limits_{l\geq 0}\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(D[-l])*\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(D[-l+1])*\cdots*\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(D[l]).

2.3.
Perpendicular subcategories in hereditary categories

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hereditary abelian category, that is, an abelian category satisfying

E^{\perp_{[0,1]}}=\{X\in\mathcal{H}~|~\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{H}}(E,X)=0=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(E,X)\}.

.

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{im}}\nolimits f\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\displaystyle 0\to Y\to E_{1}\xrightarrow{u}\operatorname{\mathsf{im}}\nolimits f\to 0,

\displaystyle 0\to\operatorname{\mathsf{im}}\nolimits f\xrightarrow{v}E_{2}\to X\to 0.

Let

\iota:{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(D^{\perp_{\mathbb{Z}}})\xrightarrow{\sim}(\operatorname{\mathsf{thick}}{D})^{\perp}.

2.4.
Rigid objects and presilting complexes over hereditary categories

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hereditary abelian category. Recall that

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(E,E)=0

The following fundamental result is due to Happel and Ringel

Lemma 2.9
.

Let

\mathcal{H}

\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{H})}(F,E[1])=0

Let

\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{A/\langle e\rangle}M=\operatorname{\mathsf{pd}}_{A^{op}/\langle e\rangle}\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}\nolimits(M)\leq 1
.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

For an indecomposable
A
-module
M
in
(eA)^{\perp}
,

(1)

if
M\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
, then
M\in\mathcal{L}_{A/\langle e\rangle}
;

(2)

if
M\in\mathcal{R}_{A}
, then
M\in\mathcal{R}_{A/\langle e\rangle}
.

Proof.

We only prove (1); the proof of (2) is similar. Assume there is a path from an indecomposable module
X
to
M
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A/\langle e\rangle
. Then this is also a path from
X
to
M
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A
. Since
M\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{pd}}_{A}X\leq 1
. By Proposition
4.9
, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{pd}}_{A/\langle e\rangle}X\leq 1
. This means
M\in\mathcal{L}_{A/\langle e\rangle}
.
∎

With these preparations in hand, we now turn to the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

proof of Theorem
4.1
.

(1) By Proposition
4.11
, for an indecomposable
A/\langle e\rangle
-module
M
, if
M\notin\mathcal{L}_{A/\langle e\rangle}\cup\mathcal{R}_{A/\langle e\rangle}
, then
M\notin\mathcal{L}_{A}\cup\mathcal{R}_{A}
. Hence if
\mathcal{L}_{A}\cup\mathcal{R}_{A}
is cofinite, so is
\mathcal{L}_{A/\langle e\rangle}\cup\mathcal{R}_{A/\langle e\rangle}
.

The proof of (2) is similar to (1).

(3) By Proposition
4.3
, assume
l\geq 0
is an integer satisfying that any path from an indecomposable module not lying in
\mathcal{L}_{A}
to an indecomposable module not lying in
\mathcal{R}_{A}
has length at most
l
. We consider a path
X\to\cdots\to Y
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A/\langle e\rangle
, where
X
,
Y
are indecomposable and
X\notin\mathcal{L}_{A/\langle e\rangle}
,
Y\notin\mathcal{R}_{A/\langle e\rangle}
. By Proposition
4.11
, we have
X\notin\mathcal{L}_{A}
,
Y\notin\mathcal{R}_{A}
. Since this path is also a path in
\operatorname{\mathsf{ind}}A
, its length
\leq l
. By Proposition
4.3
again, we know that
A/\langle e\rangle
is also weakly shod.

(4) Given an indecomposable
A
-module
M
, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{pd}}_{A}M\leq 1
or
\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{A}M\leq 1
since
A
is shod. According to Proposition
4.9
and Proposition
4.10
, this assertion holds immediately.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

The statement in Theorem
4.1
does not hold for tilted algebras and quasi-tilted algebras, while, in contrast, the result for special idempotents in
[
Z
]
remains valid for these two classes. Indeed, there is an algebra in the example from
[

, Section 2.5]
which is a tilted algebra, becoming strictly shod instead after taking a quotient by an idempotent.

Proposition 5.1
.

For
n\geq 2
and
2\leq k\leq n
, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{gl.dim}}A(n,k)=k-1
. If

, there is a tilting

-module

\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{A(n,k)}T\cong A(n,k+1)
. In particular, iteratively taking the endomorphism algebras of tilting modules, starting from a hereditary algebra of type

Proof.

It suffices to note that
\operatorname{\mathsf{gl.dim}}A(n,n)=n-1
and

is a hereditary algebra of type
\mathbb{A}_{n}
.
∎

Let

Proposition 5.2
.

For

and

, we have

(1)

is a hereditary algebra.

(2)

(3)

is a strictly shod algebra.

(4)

is not shod for

(1) is clear. For (4), we note that
\operatorname{\mathsf{gl.dim}}A(n,k)=k-1\geq 4

is not shod since the global dimension of a shod algebra is not larger than 3.

-module. By

. These are the only paths from an injective

.

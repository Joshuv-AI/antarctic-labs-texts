On the Renormalization Group in EFTs: On-Shell Bases, Ambiguities, and Divergences
Anders Eller Thomsen

Abstract
Recent results for two-loop renormalization group (RG) functions in effective field theories exhibit unphysical divergences when calculated in an on-shell operator basis. We demonstrate that this can be understood to be a result of omitting non-minimal source terms in the renormalized vacuum functional, which are essential to maintaining renormalizability of and describing the RG flow of Green’s functions in an on-shell framework. With the inclusion of the missing source terms, any remaining divergences are ambiguous, generating only unphysical RG flow directed along flavor rotations, and the RG functions are RG-finite. We carefully examine the role of flavor rotations in generating ambiguities in both on- and off-shell RG functions and explore the geometry of a physical coupling space.

1 Introduction

As the search for new physics beyond the Standard Model (SM) intensifies, effective field theories (EFTs) have emerged as one of the primary tools assisting this exploration in the area of precision physics, just as they have proven indispensable in SM physics. This has led to the development of new methods and tools for phenomenological analysis, geometric interpretations of EFTs, and a new focus on calculations of next-to-leading order Renormalization Group (RG) functions, among other developments. It is in the calculation of EFT RG functions that we find the curiosities that have inspired this work. Half a century since its inception, the RG remains a cornerstone of QFT; nevertheless, fundamental aspects of the mechanism remain largely unexplored.

From the very introduction of the RG within the framework of dimensional regularization, it was presumed that RG functions are finite (pole-free) in the
\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{2}(4-d)
expansion to facilitate a
finite flow of renormalized Green’s functions
. This expected constraint on the RG functions predicts recursive relations between the higher poles of the counterterms and the simple
\epsilon
-poles
[
30
]
, sometimes referred to as ‘t Hooft consistency conditions. A counterexample to the consistency conditions was discovered (at three loop order) only after 40 years, in the unbroken phase of the Standard Model, no less. Refs.
[
4
,
17
]
found that there is an ambiguity in choosing the counterterms—within the same renormalization scheme—and only certain choices lead to finite RG functions. Besides the puzzling emergence of divergent (in the
\epsilon
-expansion) RG functions, no other issues indicate pathologies with the other counterterm choices. Ref.
[
18
]
eventually offered a detailed account of the occurrence of divergent RG functions in renormalizable theories. The divergent RG functions still give rise to a finite flow of renormalized Green’s functions following the standard Callan–Symanzik equations
[
8
,
27
]
: the divergent contributions are proportional to a Ward identity associated with flavor rotations of the theory and cancel in the RG flow. In fact, the ambiguity in choosing counterterms simply incorporates different, unphysical flavor rotations into the RG flow. The condition that the RG functions produce a finite flow of the renormalized Green’s functions is called
RG-finiteness

[
18
]
and relaxes the condition of separate finiteness for each individual RG function. The ambiguous nature of the RG, including the finite part, was also observed in the framework of the local RG
[
19
,
13
,
20
]
.

Recently, divergent RG functions—
\beta
-functions and field anomalous dimensions—have been observed in EFTs at the two-loop order
[
21
,
23
,
28
,
25
]
. The divergences are present when the calculations are performed in an
on-shell EFT basis
without redundant
equation-of-motion (EOM) operators
. The field anomalous dimension in the
\mathrm{O}(N)
scalar EFT was found to be divergent
[
21
,
23
]
even in the absence of non-trivial flavor structures in the model. This strongly suggests that the origin of these new divergences in the EFT RG functions is unrelated to the flavor ambiguities that cause divergences in renormalizable theories (and in EFTs, too). It appears to be a new phenomenon altogether. Ref.
[
23
]
argues that the divergence of field anomalous dimensions should be viewed in light of the well-known fact that shifting to an on-shell EFT basis introduces divergences in off-shell Green’s functions, meaning that the
vacuum functional
—the generating functional for connected Green’s functions—is no longer fully renormalized. By contrast, the on-shell
\beta
-functions must be finite to account for the finiteness of physical observables.
1
1

1

In fact, observables depend on the on-shell couplings in a flavor-invariant manner. This opens up the possibility that the on-shell
\beta
-functions can have a divergent component, as long as it is in the direction of a flavor rotation. We are left with the weaker conclusion that on-shell
\beta
-functions need only be
RG-finite
. A formulation of a theory in terms of exclusively physical parameters, without remnant flavor ambiguity, is needed for the original argument. However, this would be a very atypical—not to mention impractical—formulation of most theories.

With this work, we wish to explore the RG from the perspective of on-shell EFTs further. A traditional on-shell basis does not span a full set of counterterms needed to renormalize a theory. Presumably, one cannot then establish RG functions within this framework that describe the flow of the now divergent Green’s functions with a Callan–Symanzik equation. While the
\beta
-functions of the on-shell couplings are dictated by the invariance of observables w.r.t. the renormalization scale, one must ask if it is even meaningful to discuss the field anomalous dimensions, let alone their divergence, in this framework. The on-shell couplings nevertheless carry all physical information of a theory, and a sensible interpretation of the
\beta
-functions of the on-shell couplings is possible.

We argue that the divergence of the RG functions is a spurious consequence of neglecting new
non-minimal source terms
(NMSTs) when performing field redefinitions to remove the redundant operators and transition from an off-shell (Green’s) basis to an on-shell basis. While these extra source terms can be discarded when computing
S
-matrix elements
[
15
,
2
,
10
]
, they are needed to fully renormalize the vacuum functional in the
on-shell formulation
. Only with fully renormalized Green’s functions are the RG functions, derived from scale invariance of the bare Lagrangian, expected to be RG-finite; that is, they generate a finite flow of the Green’s functions. The NMSTs do not demand new counterterms for the on-shell couplings (with minimal counterterm choices) and, thus, neglecting their contribution to the
\beta
-functions of the on-shell couplings does not cause new divergences. We show with an explicit example how neglecting NMSTs can combine with spurious flavor divergences to produce divergent
\beta
-functions already at the two-loop order. We wish to emphasize that while the inclusion of NMSTs provides a satisfying resolution to the divergences observed in RG functions, they can safely be ignored in practical calculations aiming to extract the finite piece of the on-shell
\beta
-functions.

Our investigation led us to uncover that
\beta
-functions receive ambiguous contributions in the direction generated by the flavor group whenever a theory is mapped from one coupling space to another. This is particularly prevalent for the on-shell
\beta
-functions, which are usually determined through a detour to the off-shell coupling space. We propose the introduction of a physical coupling space, obtained by factoring out the flavor ambiguities from the on-shell coupling space, as a non-redundant description of the theory. Only in such a space will the RG functions be unambiguous. Additional work is needed to determine if this physical coupling space has any practical use or if it remains merely a theoretical interpretation of the physical flow of the theory. Regardless, it is important to bear in mind that different
\beta
-function calculations may obtain different, but equally correct, results; one must exercise caution in cross-checks.

The paper opens with a detailed review of field redefinitions in EFTs and how they shape the RG in Section
2
. The role of the flavor group is described in Section
3
, which also covers the lesser-known flavor ambiguities of the RG and RG function divergences related to flavor transformations. Section
4
lays out an on-shell framework including NMSTs to obtain a well-behaved, renormalized mapping from off-shell to on-shell EFTs. We show that the on-shell
\beta
-functions neatly separate from the redundant couplings in this on-shell framework in agreement with previous expectations. To illustrate these points, we examine recent examples from literature
[
23
,
28
]
in Section
5
to demonstrate that (RG-)finite RG functions are recovered in the full on-shell framework, contrary to previous calculations in the truncated on-shell formulation (the usual “on-shell basis”). Finally, Section
6
examines the geometry of the various coupling spaces and the construction of a physical coupling space. To the extent that it is possible, we have relegated the more advanced mathematics to this section.
Section
7
concludes the work.

2 Preliminaries

We begin our discussion by setting up the RG as a flow in the coupling space of an EFT. We discuss the relation between off- and on-shell bases and the standard relations between counterterms and RG functions.

2.1  Field redefinitions in EFTs

Let us take, as the subject of our discussion, an EFT constructed from a set of fields collectively denoted
\eta^{I}
. Working at a finite order in the EFT expansion, the local operators constructed from fields and their derivatives form a finite vector space
V_{\mathrm{off}}
that comprises all possible off-shell EFTs at that order. We can choose an
off-shell
(Green’s) basis of operators
O_{i}(\eta)
free of strict mathematical redundancies such as integration-by-parts relations, commutation relations, etc. Such a basis fully parameterizes all off-shell Green’s functions that are compatible with a local action, and we have
\mathrm{span}\big(\{O_{i}\}\big)=V_{\mathrm{off}}
. The theory space is parametrized by a set of off-shell couplings
\lambda^{i}
—including also mass parameters and renormalizable couplings—which fully describe the off-shell theory. The Lagrangian of the theory is then given by

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{off}}(\eta,\,\lambda)\equiv\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kin}}(\eta)+\lambda^{i}O_{i}(\eta)

(2.1)

in the off-shell basis, where
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kin}}
are the kinetic terms of the matter fields (which do not contain any couplings).
2
2

2

To strictly adhere to this parameterization in gauge theories, we may normalize the gauge-kinetic term as
\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}\tfrac{1}{4g^{2}}F_{\mu\nu}^{2}\subset\lambda^{i}O_{i}
. Then no couplings appear in the covariant derivatives of the matter fields in, e.g.,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kin}}=D_{\mu}\phi^{\dagger}D^{\mu}\phi+\ldots
. The discussion here is easily adapted to the more common normalization of the gauge fields.
With a somewhat sloppy notation, we think of
\lambda^{i}
(with indices
i,j,\ldots
) as the coordinates of the point
\lambda=\lambda^{i}O_{i}\in V_{\mathrm{off}}
, with the usual sum over repeated indices. This Lagrangian is perturbatively renormalizable up to the chosen order in the EFT expansion.

It is well-known that the off-shell Lagrangian over-parameterizes the physical theory
[
29
,
15
,
2
,
10
]
. Equivalently, we can say that there is redundancy in the Lagrangian description of the theory. Initially it was thought that all operators proportional to the EOMs are irrelevant, but now the redundancy is understood to be invariance of the
S
-matrix under field redefinitions. Here we consider field redefinitions
\eta^{I}\to\xi^{I}(\eta)
, where
\xi(\eta)
is a covariant polynomial of the fields and their derivatives that preserves the structure of (
2.1
) (see Note
for more details).
3
3

3

A simple example of a field-redefinition in a scalar field theory would be
\phi\to\phi+\alpha\phi^{2}
for any
\alpha
. More relevant examples are found in Section
5
.
The consequence of the redefinition
\xi
is to change the Lagrangian as

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{off}}(\eta,\,\lambda)\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{off}}\big(\xi(\eta),\,\lambda\big)=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{off}}(\eta,\,\lambda|_{\xi}),

(2.2)

which in effect changes couplings
\lambda^{i}\to\lambda^{i}|_{\xi}
. Dropping higher order terms in the EFT expansion, the change in fields can be completely absorbed as a change in couplings, since
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{off}}
fully parameterizes the theory space.

Note 2.1

(note:redefs)
.

Field redefinitions
The path integral is, in principle, invariant under all invertible field redefinitions. In this paper, we consider only redefinitions that preserve the form of the off-shell Lagrangian (
2.1
), i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{off}}\big(\xi(\eta),\,\lambda\big)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kin}}(\eta)\in V_{\mathrm{off}}
. Thus, we take
\xi^{I}(\eta)
to be a local function (polynomial in fields and their derivatives). Furthermore, the restriction prohibits constant pieces, which would otherwise introduce tadpole terms, and the linear term should be chosen to preserve the normalization of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kin}}
. All other terms are formally higher-order in the EFT counting—and, thus, perturbative—ensuring that the Jacobian from the change of integration variable vanishes in the path integral
[
10
]
in dimensional regularization. The entire field-redefinition can then be absorbed into a change of couplings
\lambda\to\lambda|_{\xi}
, except for the introduction of new NMSTs, which will be the subject of Section
4
.

Physics being unchanged under field redefinitions implies an equivalence relation on the space of couplings: we say
\lambda\sim\lambda^{\prime}
if there exists a field redefinition
\xi(\eta)
such that
\lambda^{\prime}|_{\xi}=\lambda
. All points in the equivalence class

[\lambda]=\big\{\lambda^{\prime}\in V_{\mathrm{off}}:\lambda^{\prime}\sim\lambda\big\}

(2.3)

of
\lambda\in V_{\mathrm{off}}
describe the same physics. We stress that the mappings
\cdot|_{\xi}
on the coupling space induced by the field redefinitions are not linear, so the quotient space
V_{\mathrm{off}}/\!\sim
does not preserve the vector space structure of
V_{\mathrm{off}}
. The equivalence classes are shown as yellow lines in the sketch of
V_{\mathrm{off}}
shown in Fig.
1
.
4
4

4

Relying on equivalence through the leading order EOM rather than proper field redefinitions would erroneously indicate that
V_{\mathrm{off}}/\!\sim
is a quotient vector space, preserving the linear structure of
V_{\mathrm{off}}
such as in
[
12

.

The physical redundancy in the off-shell theory space has led to the widespread use of on-shell EFT bases—going back, at least, to the original formulation of the Standard Model Effective Field Theory (SMEFT)
[
7
]
—which removes the redundancy from the couplings. An on-shell coupling space is a vector subspace of
V_{\mathrm{off}}
of the
smallest possible dimension
that can still capture all possible physics. An on-shell subspace, say
V_{\mathrm{on}}\subseteq V_{\mathrm{off}}
, must satisfy

\forall\lambda\in V_{\mathrm{off}}\;\exists g\in V_{\mathrm{on}}:g\in[\lambda],

(2.4)

such that
V_{\mathrm{on}}
contains elements from each equivalence class of
V_{\mathrm{off}}/\!\sim
. For
V_{\mathrm{on}}
to be an on-shell subspace, any other
V^{\prime}\subseteq V_{\mathrm{off}}
satisfying (
2.4
) must have
\dim V^{\prime}\geq\dim V_{\mathrm{on}}
. This definition is sufficient for now, but we will make it more precise with the introduction of the flavor group in Section
3.1
. For the remainder of the article, we will assume that a particular choice of
V_{\mathrm{on}}
has been made.

We let
\{Q_{a}\}
be an operator basis for
V_{\mathrm{on}}
and write the
on-shell
Lagrangian as

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{on}}(\eta,\,g)=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kin}}(\eta)+g^{a}Q_{a}(\eta),\qquad g\equiv g^{a}Q_{a}\in V_{\mathrm{on}}.

(2.5)

The coordinates
g^{a}
(with indices
a,b,\ldots
) are the
on-shell couplings
of the theory.
Conventionally, most EFT calculations are organized so that the on-shell basis operators are a subset of the off-shell basis operators, i.e.,
Q_{a}=O_{a}
for
1\leq a\leq\dim V_{\mathrm{on}}
, rather than opting for more general linear combinations of the operators. It is usual to refer to the Lagrangian (
2.5
) as the EFT in an on-shell basis. From our perspective, it should more properly be referred to as a
truncated on-shell formulation
of the theory,
so named because it crucially omits the redundant couplings of the NMSTs introduced in
4
and is insufficient to renormalize all Green’s functions.
5
5

5

We use the phrase “truncated on-shell formulation” to discriminate it from the regular “(full) on-shell formulation,” as introduced in Section
4
. The truncated formulation is what is typically referred to as an EFT in the “on-shell basis” in previous literature.

Given an on-shell basis
\{Q_{a}\}
, there exists, by construction, a coupling-dependent field redefinition
\eta\to\Xi(\eta,\lambda)
such that

6

6

In addition to using
g
to describe a point in
V_{\mathrm{on}}
, with coordinates
g^{a}
, we also use
g:V_{\mathrm{off}}\to V_{\mathrm{on}}
to denote the mapping to the on-shell subspace. We find the double meaning of the symbol to be practical, as
g
and
g(\lambda)
are both objects in the on-shell subspace. The context should be clear from whether
g
has an argument or not.

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{off}}(\eta,\,\lambda)\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{off}}\big(\Xi(\eta,\lambda),\,\lambda\big)=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{on}}\big(\eta,\,g(\lambda)\big),\qquad g(\lambda)=\lambda|_{\Xi}.

(2.6)

In this notation,
g(\cdot)
is a non-linear projection from
V_{\mathrm{off}}
to the on-shell subspace
V_{\mathrm{on}}
. Any on-shell theory can also be considered as an off-shell theory. There are generally many ways to choose a
right inverse
(a
section
)
\lambda(g)
such that
g(\lambda(g))=g
. A natural, even ubiquitous, choice in EFT calculations is to use the trivial embedding
V_{\mathrm{on}}\subseteq V_{\mathrm{off}}
and let
\lambda(g)=g
. In principle, this is just a matter of convenience rather than necessity. We will see in Section
3.3
that
\Xi
and, thus,
g(\lambda)
are unique only up to a coupling-dependent flavor rotation.

2.2  RG flow in theory space

The off-shell Lagrangian (
2.1
) allows for the calculation of physical observables (
S
-matrix elements)
\mathcal{O}(\lambda,\mu)
in terms of the theory parameters and the renormalization scale
\mu
. Since observables are independent of field redefinitions in the theory, they can equivalently be written in terms of the less redundant on-shell couplings:

\mathcal{O}(\lambda,\mu)=\mathcal{O}\big(g(\lambda),\mu\big).

(2.7)

This observation provides valuable insight into the nature of the RG. Observables, viewed as a function of the on-shell couplings, are renormalization scale independent, so they satisfy the RG equation

\left(\mu\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\mu}+\beta_{\mathrm{on}}^{a}\partial_{a}\right)\!\mathcal{O}(g,\,\mu)=0,\qquad\beta_{\mathrm{on}}^{a}\equiv\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}g^{a}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t},\qquad\partial_{a}\equiv\dfrac{\partial}{\partial g^{a}},

(2.8)

where
t=\ln\mu
is the RG time. Since the observables are functions only of the on-shell couplings, so too are the coupling
\beta
-functions
\beta_{\mathrm{on}}^{a}=\beta_{\mathrm{on}}^{a}(g)
. The physical theory, and its flow, is fully determined by the on-shell couplings
[
11
]
.

The
\beta
-functions in the off-shell theory can be computed with standard methods
[
30
]
: parametrizing the bare (RG-invariant) couplings in terms of the renormalized ones, the
\beta
-functions are related to the coupling counterterms (more details to follow in Section
2.3
). We write

\beta_{\mathrm{off}}^{i}(\lambda)\equiv\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda^{i}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t},

(2.9)

for the off-shell
\beta
-functions.
Both renormalized Green’s functions and observables
\mathcal{O}(\lambda)
are RG invariant in the off-shell theory—satisfying, e.g., an off-shell version of (
2.8
)—and the
\beta
-functions give rise to a flow of the couplings in the space
V_{\mathrm{off}}
as sketched in Fig.
1
. For physics to be consistently described by all points in the same equivalence class, it follows that all points in an equivalence class will flow to points residing in another shared equivalence class after the same change in renormalization scale. This is illustrated by the red points at the
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
equivalence surfaces in the figure. While one might recover on-shell
\beta
-functions with a similar calculation, bringing the theory to the (truncated) on-shell basis generally compromises the renormalized vacuum functional. The resulting loss of renormalizability indicates that the RG functions will fail to produce a flow for the Green’s functions of the theory. We will discuss how to remedy this in Section
4
.

One alternative way to RG-evolve an on-shell theory is to embed it in
V_{\mathrm{off}}
first, then evolve the theory off-shell with the off-shell RG functions, before projecting back to
V_{\mathrm{on}}
(see, e.g.,
[
23
]
). Since the off-shell RG is well-defined, this describes a satisfactory prescription for the on-shell coupling flow. We have sketched this procedure in Fig.
1
.
Starting from the blue on-shell point at scale
\mu_{1}
, the theory is evolved following the off-shell running (red arrow) to the scale
\mu_{2}
. Then one follows the yellow surface of physically equivalent theories back to the intersection with the on-shell subspace.
7
7

7

For simplicity the intersection of the equivalence class with
V_{\mathrm{on}}
is shown as a point in Fig.
1
. Typically, it is actually a multi-dimensional surface generated by the action of the flavor group (cf. Fig.
5
).
This procedure describes an equivalent flow on the on-shell subspace shown in blue. Linearizing this circuitous route, considering an infinitesimal change in RG time, gives

\begin{aligned} g^{a}+\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,\beta_{\mathrm{on}}^{a}(g)&=g^{a}\big(\lambda^{i}(g)+\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,\beta_{\mathrm{off}}^{i}(\lambda(g))\big)\\
&=g^{a}+\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,\beta_{\mathrm{off}}^{i}(\lambda(g))\,\partial_{i}g^{a}(\lambda(g)),\end{aligned}\qquad\qquad\partial_{i}\equiv\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{i}}.

(2.10)

Equating the linear terms yields

\beta_{\mathrm{on}}^{a}(g)=\beta_{\mathrm{off}}^{i}(\lambda)\dfrac{\partial g^{a}(\lambda)}{\partial\lambda^{i}}\bigg|_{\lambda=\lambda(g)}

(2.11)

for the on-shell
\beta
-functions as a function of on-shell couplings. While the on-shell
\beta
-functions are sometimes thought of as physical, we caution the reader that there is remnant redundancy in this formulation (cf. Section
3
). As long as the off-shell
\beta
-functions are finite, then so are the on-shell ones. While this procedure works well, it is not very practical for calculating the on-shell
\beta
-functions: it requires access to an off-shell basis (which can be difficult to obtain) and one needs to calculate counterterms for many more couplings than are in the on-shell basis, complicating calculations unnecessarily.

2.3  Counterterms and RG functions

RG functions are ultimately derived from counterterms in dimensional regularization. The off-shell Lagrangian (
2.1
) is renormalized by substituting the renormalized couplings for bare ones:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{off}}(\eta,\,\lambda,\,\mathcal{J})\equiv\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kin}}(\eta)+\lambda^{i}_{\mathrm{b}}O_{i}(\eta)+\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{b},I}\eta^{I}.

(2.12)

At this point we also explicitly introduce the bare field sources
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{b},I}
with which we source the connected Green’s functions in the vacuum functional. The bare couplings are split into renormalized couplings
\lambda^{i}
and associated counterterms
\delta\lambda^{i}(\lambda)
to render the vacuum functional a finite function of the renormalized couplings.
We work in the MS/
\overline{\text{\small MS}}
scheme
8
8

8

The
\overline{\text{\small MS}}
scheme is obtained from MS by substituting the renormalization scale for
\bar{\mu}
, letting
\mu^{2}=(4\pi)^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\bar{\mu}^{2}e^{\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}}
. This distinction is irrelevant for the discussion in this paper.
for the remainder of the paper to keep the discussion minimal; we have no reason to suspect that our general conclusions change for other renormalization schemes. In the minimal subtraction (MS) scheme

\lambda_{\mathrm{b}}^{i}=\mu^{k_{i}\epsilon}\big(\lambda^{i}+\delta\lambda^{i}\big),\qquad\delta\lambda^{i}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{\delta^{(\ell)}\!\lambda^{i}}{(16\pi^{2})^{\ell}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{\delta_{n}\!\lambda^{i}}{\epsilon^{n}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell}\dfrac{\delta^{(\ell)}_{n}\!\lambda^{i}}{(16\pi^{2})^{\ell}\epsilon^{n}}.

(2.13)

The renormalization scale
\mu
is introduced to ensure that the renormalized couplings have the dimensions of the couplings in the unregulated (four-dimensional) theory.
9
9

9

The dimensionality
k_{i}
is, for instance,
k_{i}=1
for Yukawa couplings and
k_{i}=2
for quartic couplings. The index of
k_{i}
does not count as a repeated index for the purposes of Einstein summation.
Eq. (
2.13
) also introduces notation for the counterterms as loop, pole, or mixed expansions, which will be needed in due course.

Renormalization of the couplings is sufficient to renormalize all subdivergences and vacuum graphs; however, a source renormalization is needed to renormalize
n
-point Green’s functions (cf. Note
). A multiplicative renormalization factor
Z\phantom{}^{I}\phantom{}_{J}(\lambda)
is sufficient to this end, and we have

\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{b},I}=\mathcal{J}_{J}(Z^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1})\phantom{}^{J}\phantom{}_{I},\qquad Z=\mathds{1}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{Z^{(\ell)}}{(16\pi^{2})^{\ell}}=\mathds{1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{Z_{n}}{\epsilon^{n}}=\mathds{1}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell}\dfrac{Z^{(\ell)}_{n}}{(16\pi^{2})^{\ell}\epsilon^{n}}.

(2.14)

The source and coupling counterterms span all possible counterterms of the off-shell action up to the given order in the EFT expansion. Any additional structures are removable via field redefinitions to bring the Lagrangian back to the form (
2.12
) and are thus not needed to renormalize the off-shell Green’s functions.

Note 2.2

Source renormalization contra wave-function renormalization
To maintain the canonical normalization of the kinetic term, we adopt the convention of omitting the field wave-function normalization in favor of renormalizing the sources, effectively introducing the notion of a bare source
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{b}}
.
While practical multi-loop computations subtracting subdivergences with variations of the
\bm{R}
-operation
[
9
]
may find it convenient to use counterterms for the kinetic terms rather than the field sources, the renormalization factors can always be shifted back after the fact with a field redefinitions (not of the type in Note
, as it explicitly changes the normalization of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kin}}

3 The Flavor Group

So far we have ignored an important piece of structure in the coupling spaces, both off- and on-shell. We now look to examine the consequences of unitary field transformations for the RG and the origin of potential flavor divergences of the RG functions.

3.1  The action of the flavor group

The key structure organizing the coupling space is the
flavor group

G_{F}
, defined as the largest, unitary symmetry group of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kin}}(\eta)

Although we have been somewhat vague about the definition of the basis operator

up to this point, our implicit assumption is that they are (and have been) flavor covariant monomials in fields and their derivatives, meaning that under transformations

(3.1)

(3.2)

\lambda^{i}O_{i}(a^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\cdot\eta)=(a\cdot\lambda)^{i}\cdot O_{i}(\eta)
.

\beta^{i}(a\cdot\lambda)=a\phantom{}^{i}\phantom{}_{j}\beta^{j}(\lambda)
.
With these observations (

\beta^{a}_{\Xi_{U}}(g)=\beta_{\Xi}^{a}(g)+\big(\beta_{\mathrm{off}}^{i}(\lambda)\big[U^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}(\lambda)\partial_{i}U(\lambda)\big]\cdot g\big)^{\!a}\bigg|_{\lambda=\lambda_{\Xi}(g)}

(3.22)

We recognize
U^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\partial_{i}U
as the pullback of the Maurer–Cartan form on
V_{\mathrm{off}}
using
U

\beta_{\mathrm{off}}^{i}(\lambda)\big[U^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}(\lambda)\partial_{i}U(\lambda)\big]
is an element of the flavor group Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
. The second term of (

) is the discrepancy from the on-shell
\beta
-function as calculated by the original field redefinition
\Xi
.

We observe that obtaining on-shell
\beta
-functions by embedding the on-shell basis in the off-shell framework, calculating the off-shell
\beta
-functions, and then mapping back to the on-shell basis provides another source of ambiguities, which to our knowledge has not been remarked on previously. Notably, the ambiguity is again unphysical as it generates a flow in the direction of a flavor transformation similar to the ambiguity (
3.15
) arising from the choice of counterterms. The ambiguity in the on-shell
\beta
-functions from the second term in (
3.22
) is likely to start only at the three-loop order and certainly not before two-loop order:
\beta^{i}(\lambda)
is a one-loop object, and non-trivial contributions to
U(\lambda)
require non-Hermitian, covariant contractions of the couplings (understood to be elements of
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
). Such combinations certainly happen at two-loop order in EFTs (cf. Section
5.2
), but it is unclear if there exist EFTs where they can be engineered at one-loop order.

We wish to stress that this ambiguity in the on-shell
\beta
-functions (
3.22
) is not as artificial as it might look at first glance. One might reasonably expect practitioners to always choose the trivial embedding
\lambda_{\Xi}(g)=g
, but this requirement does not uniquely fix the projection
g_{\Xi}
. One should simply choose
U(\lambda)
subject to the constraint
U(\lambda_{\Xi}(g))=e
, where
e\in G_{F}
is the identity element. In other words, if we can find a function
U(\lambda)
that is non-trivial only when some “redundant” off-shell couplings are non-vanishing, then
g_{\Xi}
is ambiguous even after fixing
\lambda_{\Xi}(g)=g
.

The ambiguity (
3.22
) exists for renormalizable theories (
V_{\mathrm{on}}=V_{\mathrm{off}}
) too. An extraordinarily mischievous practitioner might decide to perform a redefinition
\Xi^{I}_{U}(\eta,\lambda)=\big(U(\lambda)\cdot\eta\big)^{I}
(compared to the usual, reasonable choice
\Xi^{I}(\eta,\lambda)=\eta^{I}
, i.e., performing no redefinitions), which would change the
\beta
-functions. This corresponds to rotating the theory with a coupling-dependent transformation before calculating the
\beta
-functions and rotating back again. Although this idea is both highly unusual and impractical, it is in principle not prohibited.

4 On-Shell Formulation with Non-Minimal Source Terms

We return to the question of field redefinition and equivalence between theories. This time we include all source terms to ensure that all off-shell Green’s functions are faithfully reproduced in the full on-shell formulation of the theory.

4.1  On-shell vacuum functional

The vacuum functional of the off-shell Lagrangian (
2.1
) is

W^{\prime}_{\mathrm{off}}[\lambda,\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{off}}]=\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}i\log\!\int\hskip-3.50006pt\mathcal{D}\eta\,\exp\!\left[i\!\left(S_{\mathrm{off}}[\eta,\lambda]+\!\int\hskip-3.50006pt\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{off}}_{I}(x)\eta^{I}(x)\right)\right],

(4.1)

where

\eta^{I}
as integration variables is manifest in the path integral. The transformation

(
2.6

W^{\prime}_{\mathrm{off}}[\lambda,\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{off}}]=\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}i\log\!\int\hskip-3.50006pt\mathcal{D}\eta\,\exp\!\left[i\!\left(S_{\mathrm{on}}[\eta,g(\lambda)]+\!\int\hskip-3.50006pt\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\mathcal{J}_{I}^{\mathrm{off}}(x)\Xi^{I}\!(\eta(x),\lambda)\right)\right],

(4.2)

having used that perturbative field redefinitions do not change the measure of the path integral in dimensional regularization
[
10
]

While the action is now in its on-shell form, the trade-off is the introduction of
non-minimal source terms
—source terms other than
\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{off}}_{I}\eta^{I}

The NMSTs are parametrized by setting

\Xi^{I}\!(\eta,\lambda)=A\phantom{}^{I}\phantom{}_{J}(\lambda)\left(\eta^{J}+r^{\alpha}(\lambda)Q_{\alpha}^{J}(\eta)\right),

(4.3)

(4.4)

This leads us to the interpretation of

as a set of “redundant” couplings, such that the change from off-shell to on-shell theory does not change the number of independent couplings.

The equivalent on-shell version of the vacuum functional (

W^{\prime}_{\mathrm{on}}[g,\,r,\,\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{on}}]=\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}i\log\!\int\hskip-3.50006pt\mathcal{D}\eta\,\exp\!\left[i\!\left(S_{\mathrm{on}}[\eta,g]+\!\int\hskip-3.50006pt\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{on}}_{I}(x)\big[\eta^{I}+r^{\alpha}Q^{I}_{\alpha}(\eta)\big]\right)\right].

(4.5)

We refer to this form as the

of the theory. The field redefinition
\Xi^{I}(\eta,\lambda)

\big(g^{a},\,r^{\alpha},\,\mathcal{J}_{I}^{\mathrm{on}}\big)=\big(g^{a}(\lambda),\,r^{\alpha}(\lambda),\,\mathcal{J}_{I}^{\mathrm{on}}(\lambda,\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{off}})\big).

(4.6)

composed of the projection
g(\lambda)
(
2.6

4.3

The map is invertible—one needs simply find a field redefinition to remove all NMSTs

(\lambda^{i},\,\mathcal{J}_{I}^{\mathrm{off}})=\big(\lambda^{i}(g,r),\,\mathcal{J}_{I}^{\mathrm{off}}(g,r,\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{on}})\big).

(4.7)

This is in contrast to the arbitrary choice of right inverse
\lambda(g)

Additionally, the full on-shell formulation clearly separates on-shell couplings from redundant ones—even under the RG, as we shall see in due course—which can be omitted in many calculations.

4.2  Renormalization and RG functions

We saw in Section
2.3
how the off-shell Lagrangian was renormalized with bare couplings and sources. We may define the renormalized vacuum functional of the off-shell basis through

W_{\mathrm{off}}[\lambda,\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{off}}]\equiv W^{\prime}_{\mathrm{off}}[\lambda_{\mathrm{b}},\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{off}}_{\mathrm{b}}].

(4.10)

The bare couplings and sources are functions of their renormalized counterparts and give rise to counterterms as per the parametrizations (
2.13
) and (
2.14
). The core tenet of renormalization is to introduce a counterterm for any symmetry-allowed operator of the fields and sources (up to the working order in the EFT power counting). The off-shell Lagrangian consists of a full basis of the field operators, and any hypothetical counterterms involving NMSTs can be shifted into counterterms for the off-shell couplings with a field redefinition. Thus, the renormalized vacuum functional (
4.10
) contains a sufficient set of counterterms to fully renormalize the off-shell Green’s functions.

The renormalization of the on-shell vacuum functional mirrors the off-shell version:

W_{\mathrm{on}}[g,\,r,\,\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{on}}]\equiv W^{\prime}_{\mathrm{on}}[g_{\mathrm{b}},\,r_{\mathrm{b}},\,\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{on}}_{\mathrm{b}}].

(4.11)

The bare couplings are now functions of both on-shell and redundant renormalized couplings. The counterterms do not span the full operator basis, but any hypothetical counterterm for an operator not in the on-shell basis can be shifted to counterterms for the NMSTs—the
\delta r^{\alpha}

Having identified a complete on-shell formulation, which includes the NMSTs and a fully renormalized vacuum functional, we can assert invariance of the bare couplings and sources and from there derive formulas for the

(4.12)

\displaystyle\beta_{\mathrm{on},n}^{a}

\displaystyle=(\zeta-k_{a})\delta_{n+1}g^{a}-\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\big(\beta_{\mathrm{on},m}^{b}\partial_{b}+\beta_{\mathrm{red},m}^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}\big)\delta_{n-m}g^{a},

(4.13)

\displaystyle\beta_{\mathrm{red},n}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(\zeta-k_{\alpha})\delta_{n+1}r^{\alpha}-\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\big(\beta_{\mathrm{on},m}^{b}\partial_{b}+\beta_{\mathrm{red},m}^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}\big)\delta_{n-m}r^{\alpha},

(4.14)

for

\partial_{\alpha}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial r^{\alpha}}
in analogy with the other couplings. The field-anomalous dimension in the on-shell formulation is

(4.15)

\left(\mu\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\mu}+\beta_{\mathrm{on}}^{a}\partial_{a}+\beta_{\mathrm{red}}^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}+\int\hskip-3.50006pt\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{on}}_{I}(x)\gamma\phantom{}^{\mathrm{on}\,I}\phantom{}_{J}\dfrac{\delta}{\delta\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{on}}_{J}(x)}\right)\!W[g,\,r,\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{on}}]=0

(4.16)

for the renormalized vacuum functional, dictating the RG flow of the Green’s functions.
The on-shell formulation with NMSTs is entirely equivalent to the off-shell formulation. Both parametrizations produce finite off-shell Green’s functions and are equally valid renormalized descriptions of the same underlying theory. Based on this equivalence, we expect both sets of RG functions to generate finite RG flow; i.e., the full on-shell RG functions should be RG-finite.

The truncated on-shell flow in
V_{\mathrm{on}}

\beta
-functions through
\beta_{\mathrm{off}}

\psi(g,r)=g

\imath:g\mapsto(g,0)

\beta

\beta_{\mathrm{on}}(g)=\beta_{\mathrm{on}}(g,r=0).

(4.17)

While this construction can be made (artificially) more convoluted for other choices of
\psi,\imath

That the redundant couplings have a purely unphysical impact on the running of the on-shell couplings also follows from the structure of the counterterms. The NMSTs involve field sources, so any divergence involving such a term should itself involve a source. The source counterterm (wave-function renormalization factor) and redundant counterterms of the NMSTs are sufficient to renormalize the redundant couplings. The counterterms of the on-shell couplings renormalize subdivergences and vacuum diagrams not involving any external source terms; one would expect them to be entirely independent of any source terms. An exception to this logic is that it is possible to introduce dependence of the redundant couplings to the on-shell counterterms, in the sense that
\delta g^{a}(g,r)\neq\delta g^{a}(g,0)
, through a divergent flavor rotation
U(g,r)\in G_{F}
similar to the one in (
3.13
). As discussed in Section
3.2
, the impact of such a rotation is to alter the RG functions to generate an unphysical flavor rotation along the RG flow. Even in this case, the impact of the redundant couplings on the running of the on-shell couplings is unphysical.

As we have argued, the usual
\beta
-functions from the truncated on-shell formulation, without NMSTs, do not describe the flow of any Green’s functions. Nevertheless, the formula for the
\beta
-functions of the on-shell couplings (
4.13
) and the field anomalous dimensions (
4.15
) resembles what one would derive in the absence of redundant couplings, with the notable exception of the
\beta_{m}^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}
-terms. The finite part of the on-shell
\beta
-function (
4.17
) does not involve any counterterms for the redundant couplings. While the
\delta_{1}g^{a}
and
Z_{1}
counterterms may have dependence on
r

r^{\alpha}=0

This reassures us that previous literature results are correct when calculated in the truncated on-shell formulation, even when omitting all effects of the NMSTs/redundant couplings. The same cannot be said for the divergent part of the RG functions, where neglecting the
\beta_{m}^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}
-terms in (
4.13

4.15
) will lead to incorrect results. From a practical standpoint, even the erroneous divergent parts of
\beta_{\mathrm{on}}

We proceed to reexamine some recent examples from literature that have produced divergent RG functions when using a truncated on-shell formulation and demonstrate how the situation is remedied in the full on-shell formulation.

One might wonder as to the nature of the observed divergence (

In this view, however, the reader may find it puzzling why the RG functions prove to be divergent—albeit RG-finite—already at the two-loop order, whereas
[

[
18

3.2

If we had worked in the truncated on-shell scheme, not including the effects of the redundant couplings, we would not see the
r_{\ell}

Z^{(1)}_{1,\ell}

Z_{\ell}
is nothing but our ignorance of the redundant couplings. To actually force

5.19

r_{\ell}

[
18

\beta

\beta

\beta_{\mathrm{red},m}^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}

4.13

6 Geometry of the Coupling Space

The various unphysical ambiguities plaguing the RG functions—even in the on-shell formulation, which is supposedly (more) physical—motivate us to explore the mathematical structure underlying the theory space and the RG functions. While we do not foresee immediate practical applications, it may, perhaps, provide a starting point for a deeper geometric exploration of the RG flow. If nothing else, it highlights the fact that when comparing different RG function calculations, one must take care only to compare the physical part lest one ends up chasing fictitious sources of errors.

6.1  Revisiting flavor rotations and the coupling spaces

It will prove useful to denote the (left) group action on the off-shell coupling space with the map
L:\,G_{F}\times V_{\mathrm{off}}\to V_{\mathrm{off}}
defined by
L(a,\lambda)=L_{a}(\lambda)=a\cdot\lambda
for
a\in G_{F}
. The group action closes in
V_{\mathrm{on}}
, so the restrictions
L_{a}\big|_{V_{\mathrm{on}}}
are automorphisms of the on-shell coupling space. We expect all functions in the
V_{\mathrm{off}}
and
V_{\mathrm{on}}
spaces to be
G_{F}
-equivariant (casually referred to as covariance in physics parlance): at the most basic, the maps between
V_{\mathrm{off}}
and
V_{\mathrm{on}}
should “commute” with flavor rotations, meaning that

g_{\Xi}\circ L_{a}=L_{a}\circ g_{\Xi},\qquad\lambda_{\Xi}\circ L_{a}=L_{a}\circ\lambda_{\Xi},\qquad\forall a\in G_{F}.

(6.1)

We have repeatedly emphasized that physics is unchanged by flavor rotations. This implies a redundancy in our description of the theory even in the on-shell coupling space. The action of the flavor group on the coupling space entails a quotient space
V_{\mathrm{on}}/G_{F}
consisting of equivalence classes

[g]_{G_{F}}=\big\{a\cdot g:a\in G_{F}\big\}\in V_{\mathrm{on}}/G_{F}

(6.2)

for
g\in V_{\mathrm{on}}
.
26
26

26

The flavor action on
V_{\mathrm{off}}
also establishes a quotient space
V_{\mathrm{off}}/G_{F}
; however, this space is still redundant under field-redefinitions, and we will not examine it closer here.
The invariance of physics under the flavor group implies that it should be possible to write all observables as functions on
V_{\mathrm{on}}/G_{F}
. We are left to interpret
V_{\mathrm{on}}/G_{F}
as a
physical
coupling space in the sense that there is no remnant redundancy in this space: each point describes unique physics.
27
27

27

The space
V_{\mathrm{on}}/G_{F}
, on the other hand, is not itself unique but depends on the original choice
V_{\mathrm{on}}\subseteq V_{\mathrm{off}}
.
The significance of the physical coupling space in the SM is discussed in Note

\beta

Any of the ambiguous

Defining a

7 Conclusion

We have examined the origin of curious divergences observed in EFT RG functions calculated recently
[
21
,
23
,
28
,
25
]
with the truncated on-shell formulation—also known as an “on-shell basis”—at the two-loop order. The bare Lagrangian of this framework does not renormalize the Green’s functions of the theory, and the resulting RG functions are generally ill-defined; they do not describe the flow of the Green’s functions under the RG. A resolution to recover a well-behaved RG flow for the vacuum functional is to extend the on-shell formulation with NMSTs, with which the vacuum functional can be fully renormalized. In this formulation, the on-shell RG functions are rendered RG-finite.

The off-shell and full on-shell formulations of an EFT are entirely equivalent, with an invertible map between the couplings of the two. While the full on-shell formulation does not provide a reduction in the number of couplings as does the truncated on-shell formulation, it exhibits a separation of on-shell and redundant couplings absent in the off-shell formulation. The
\beta
-functions of the on-shell couplings are shown to be independent of the redundant couplings with the exception of unphysical contributions that generate flavor rotations along the RG flow.

Practical calculations of the RG flow of on-shell couplings can be performed in the truncated on-shell formulation for computational convenience; however, the
\beta
-functions may exhibit divergences that generate unphysical flavor rotations during the RG flow. In contrast to the full formulations
[
18
]
(on and off shell), these divergences occur starting at the two-loop order. In the truncated on-shell framework, ‘t Hooft consistency conditions for the on-shell
\beta
-functions are valid only up to violations proportional to an element of
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
acting on the couplings. It is always possible to perform a divergent flavor rotation to remove any divergence from the on-shell
\beta
-functions in both the truncated and the full on-shell frameworks.

We have observed that unphysical flavor rotations prevent the interpretation of the on-shell coupling space as being physical; flavor transformations lay bare extra redundancies in this space. The consequence at the level of on-shell
\beta
-functions is that they are ambiguous in the direction aligned with orbits generated by the flavor group. In particular they are sensitive to choices of counterterms, the choice of projection from the off-shell space, and the choice of right inverse (embedding) of the on-shell theory. We have taken some initial steps towards establishing a
\beta
-function in a physical coupling space (the coset of the on-shell w.r.t. the flavor group) and analyzed how
\beta
-functions in the various coupling spaces are related. This provides an initial geometric framing of the RG in coupling space. It remains to be seen to what extent this construction is practical. Another interesting topic for further studies is what changes with inclusion of couplings that transform non-linearly under the flavor group (topological terms).

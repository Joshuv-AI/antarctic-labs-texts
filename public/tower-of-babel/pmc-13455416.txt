Rosuvastatin calcium administration and its impact on myocardial microcirculation and postoperative recovery in patients undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting
Authors: Xizheng Wang, Hongwei Qi, Jiangang Zhao, Nan Chen, Biao Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCoronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) is a common intervention for coronary artery disease, but postoperative myocardial microcirculation and recovery remain challenging. This study aimed to evaluate the effects of rosuvastatin calcium on myocardial microcirculation and postoperative outcomes in CABG patients.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included 216 patients who underwent CABG between February 2020 and February 2024. Participants were divided into a conventional treatment group (n = 111) and a rosuvastatin group (n = 105) receiving additional rosuvastatin calcium (10 mg/day) from 7 days pre-surgery to 28 days post-surgery. Cardiac echocardiography, blood tests (inflammatory markers, lipids, and cardiac enzymes), and New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional classification were assessed preoperatively and 8 weeks postoperatively. Postoperative complications and mortality were monitored during a one-year follow-up.ResultsThe rosuvastatin group showed significantly improved left ventricular remodeling, with reduced left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD, P = 0.022), Left Ventricular Septal Thickness (LVST, P = 0.048), and Left Ventricular Posterior Wall Thickness (LVPWT, P = 0.019). Enhanced systolic function was evidenced by higher LVEF (P < 0.001) and Left Ventricular Fractional Shortening (LVFS, P < 0.001). Myocardial microcirculation parameters including Left Acceleration Time (AT), Pulsatility Index (PI), and Aortic Pre-Ejection Period (APT) also improved (all P < 0.05). Additionally, the rosuvastatin group had lower postoperative creatine kinase MB isoenzyme (CK-MB, P = 0.012), higher High-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (HDL-C, P = 0.003), and reduced incidence of atrial fibrillation (P = 0.007) and all-cause mortality (P = 0.026).ConclusionRosuvastatin calcium administration significantly improves myocardial microcirculation, cardiac function, and postoperative recovery in CABG patients.Keywords: Rosuvastatin calcium, Coronary artery bypass grafting, Myocardial microcirculation, Postoperative recovery, Left ventricular function, Postoperative complications

Introduction

Coronary artery disease (CAD) remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide [1, 2]. For patients with complex or advanced CAD, coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) is a cornerstone revascularization strategy proven to relieve symptoms, improve quality of life, and enhance survival [3, 4]. Despite significant refinements in surgical techniques and perioperative care, a considerable number of patients still experience suboptimal postoperative recovery. The functional success of CABG depends not only on the patency of the grafted conduits but also on the integrity of the distal microvascular bed [5, 6].

A critical yet often underappreciated determinant of clinical outcomes is the state of myocardial microcirculation. This intricate network of small vessels is responsible for the precise regulation of blood flow and oxygen delivery to cardiomyocytes [7, 8]. Perioperative ischemia-reperfusion injury, systemic inflammatory responses, and endothelial dysfunction can severely compromise microcirculatory function, leading to reduced tissue perfusion even in the presence of a technically successful graft. Consequently, this microvascular impairment is increasingly recognized as a key contributor to postoperative myocardial stunning, arrhythmias, and delayed functional recovery [9].

In recent years, the pleiotropic effects of statins beyond their lipid-lowering capabilities have garnered significant interest. Among them, rosuvastatin calcium has demonstrated potent anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and endothelial-protective properties in various cardiovascular settings [10, 11]. It is hypothesized that these multifaceted actions may directly target the pathways implicated in microcirculatory dysfunction. Preclinical and clinical studies in other cardiac conditions suggest that rosuvastatin can improve endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) activity, reduce leukocyte activation, and attenuate oxidative stress, thereby potentially preserving capillary density and flow [12, 13].

However, the specific impact of targeted rosuvastatin therapy on the myocardial microcirculation and the subsequent trajectory of recovery in the unique and high-risk context of CABG surgery remains inadequately explored [14, 15]. A dedicated investigation is necessary to translate the theoretical benefits of this pharmacologic agent into tangible postoperative advantages for cardiac surgery patients. In this study, myocardial microcirculation was assessed non-invasively using echocardiographic parameters, including Acceleration Time (AT), Pulsatility Index (PI), and Aortic Pre-Ejection Period (APT), which reflect coronary flow dynamics and microvascular resistance. This study was therefore designed to evaluate the hypothesis that perioperative rosuvastatin calcium administration confers protective effects on myocardial microcirculation and facilitates recovery in patients undergoing CABG.

Materials and methods

Case selection

This is a retrospective cohort study aimed at evaluating the effects of rosuvastatin calcium administration on myocardial microcirculation and postoperative recovery in patients undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG). The study included 216 coronary artery disease (CAD) patients who underwent CABG at our hospital between February 2020 and February 2024. All procedures in this study adhered to the ethical standards established by the institutional and national human experimentation committees and were in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki of 1964 and its later amendments. Additionally, the study received approval from our institution’s Institutional Review Board (IRB) (Approval No. TREC2025-KY149). Given that the data used in the study consisted of anonymized patient information and posed no potential risks or impacts on patient care, our institution’s IRB and ethics committee decided to waive the requirement for informed consent for this retrospective study.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: [1] Patients aged ≥ 18 years; [2] Patients undergoing their first coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG); [3] Diagnosed with qualifying coronary artery disease (CAD) [16]; [4] No known allergies to the treatment medications; [5] Complete medical records and follow-up data.

Exclusion criteria: [1] Patients with atrial fibrillation; [2] Any liver disease (cirrhosis, elevated serum transaminase levels, or serum bilirubin concentration greater than 3 mg/dL); [3] Recent use of medications including protease inhibitors, macrolides, and cyclosporine; [4] History of renal transplantation or renal insufficiency (serum creatinine greater than 2.5 mg/dL); [5] Hemodynamic instability, prolonged latent phase, arrhythmias caused by shock requiring pericardiocentesis or chest tube placement; [6] Pregnant or lactating women.

Grouping criteria

Based on whether patients received supplemental rosuvastatin calcium tablets, they were divided into two groups: the conventional treatment group (Conventional group) and the rosuvastatin calcium group (Ros group). A total of 111 patients undergoing routine coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) were included in the conventional treatment group. In contrast, 105 patients who received additional rosuvastatin calcium tablet treatment (10 mg/day) on top of the conventional regimen were included in the rosuvastatin calcium group.

Patients in the rosuvastatin calcium group received 10 mg of rosuvastatin calcium (LKT-R5974, LKT Labs, USA) daily from 7 days before surgery to 28 days after surgery. The 7-day preoperative window was selected based on previous studies demonstrating that statin pleiotropic effects, including endothelial nitric oxide synthase upregulation and anti-inflammatory actions, require approximately one week to achieve optimal activity. The 28-day postoperative period covers the critical phase of myocardial recovery and early graft healing [17]. In the conventional treatment group, patients did not receive any statin therapy (including rosuvastatin, atorvastatin, or simvastatin) during the perioperative period, as per the retrospective treatment protocol. In the rosuvastatin group, patients received rosuvastatin 10 mg/day from 7 days preoperatively to 28 days postoperatively (total 35 days), without taking any other statins together with rosuvastatin calcium. No statin therapy was administered beyond postoperative day 28 in either group. The 8-week postoperative evaluation (56 days after surgery) thus represents a time point that is 28 days after the last dose of rosuvastatin, allowing assessment of sustained effects beyond the active treatment period. This design has precedent in previous CABG studies using the same perioperative rosuvastatin regimen.

Treatment

Anesthesia

Before anesthesia induction, an arterial catheter was inserted through the femoral artery and central venous catheter through the jugular vein (8 F introducer, St. Jude Medical Company, USA), and a Swan-Ganz catheter (7 F, Abbott Laboratories, USA) inserted. Induction and maintenance of anesthesia in all patients was provided with 20–40 µg/kg induction and 0.3–1 µg/kg/min maintenance of fentanyl citrate (Abbott Laboratories, USA), 0.1 mg/kg induction and 0.8 µg/kg/min maintenance of midazolam (59467-94-6, Roche, Switzerland) and 0.1 mg/kg induction of vecuronyum (CD120909, Codow, China) as a standard. A rectal probe for the determination of rectal temperature and a Foley catheter for urine follow-up were inserted.

Surgical technique

In all patients, the median sternotomy technique was used. The left internal thoracic artery and great saphenous vein were prepared for use as a graft. After systemic heparinization with 1 mg/kg of heparin (QYL-24765, Qiyi, China), an activated clotting time (ACT) of approximately 300–350 s was achieved and maintained. All coronary anastomoses were performed on the beating heart using the off-pump technique. Based on coronary artery involvement, the left internal thoracic artery was first anastomosed to the left anterior descending artery, followed by grafting to the circumflex artery and its branches. Finally, the right coronary artery was grafted. Upon completion of all anastomoses, heparin was neutralized with protamine sulfate (Roche, Switzerland) until the ACT returned to physiological levels. Chest tubes and epicardial pacing wires were placed, the sternum was closed, and the patients were transferred to the intensive care unit.

Cardiac echocardiography

The 8-week postoperative time point was chosen because it represents a standard clinical follow-up interval after CABG, allowing sufficient time for resolution of postoperative inflammatory changes and stabilization of ventricular remodeling. Color Doppler echocardiography was performed by a qualified sonographer who was blinded to the patient information, using an hp sonos 5500 ultrasound diagnostic system (M2424 A, Philips, Netherlands) with a probe frequency of 3.0 mhz. Participants were positioned in a left lateral decubitus position, and two-dimensional images were observed to measure left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD), Left Ventricular Septal Thickness (LVST), Left Ventricular Posterior Wall Thickness (LVPWT), Left Ventricular Mass Index (LVMI), Isovolumic Relaxation Time (IVRT), Left Ventricular Fractional Shortening (LVFS), Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF), Left Acceleration Time (AT), Pulsatility Index (PI), and Aortic Pre-Ejection Period (APT) before and 8 weeks after treatment in both groups.

Blood test

Prior to treatment and 8 weeks post-treatment, 5 ml venous blood samples were collected from patients, ensuring they fasted before 8 AM. The samples were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 5 min, the supernatant was extracted, and the following tests were conducted. Lymphocytes, platelets, and white blood cells were measured using a DxH800 hematology analyzer (Beckman Coulter, Inc., Brea, CA, USA). Serum C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT), Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (LDL-C), High-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (HDL-C), Total Cholesterol (TC), and Triglycerides (TG) levels were assessed using a BECKMAN Synchronx20 fully automatic biochemical analyzer (BECKMAN Coulter, Inc., Brea, CA, USA). B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) and creatine kinase MB isoenzyme (CK-MB) were detected via enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. Specific kits for BNP (ab193694, Abcam, USA) and CK-MB (ab193696, Abcam) were utilized in this process.

New York heart association functional classification

The patient’s cardiac function status was evaluated using the NYHA (New York Heart Association) Functional Classification at 8 weeks preoperatively and postoperatively. Cardiac function is classified into four grades based on the severity of symptoms and physical activity capacity: Grade I (asymptomatic), Grade II (mild symptoms), Grade III (moderate symptoms), and Grade IV (severe symptoms). A reduction of one or more grades is defined as improvement [18].

Follow-up

All patient follow-up assessments commenced upon the conclusion of treatment, with a follow-up period of one year. Follow-up data were collected through outpatient or inpatient systems, records from external hospitals, and scheduled follow-ups to monitor the occurrence of postoperative complications and all-cause mortality, such as stroke, arrhythmia, chest pain, and other conditions. The follow-up cut-off date was July 10, 2025.

Statistical method

In this study, we used IBM SPSS Statistics version 29.0 (released by IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) for data processing and statistical analysis. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages [n (%)]. For the assessment of categorical data, the chi-square test (χ²) was employed. Continuous variables that followed a normal distribution were reported as means±standard deviations (M ± SD). An independent samples t-test was used for continuous data that were normally distributed.

Examining the impact of Rosuvastatin calcium on myocardial microcirculation, our analysis revealed significant improvements in several postoperative parameters among patients in the Ros group compared to those in the conventional group (Fig. 1). Comparison of NYHA cardiac functional classification improvement status between two groups of patients

In our study comparing the NYHA cardiac functional classification improvement status between two groups of patients, we observed significant differences in both postoperative cardiac function classification and overall improvement status (Table 7). Comparison of postoperative complications between two groups of patients

In comparing the postoperative complications between the Conventional group and the Ros group, the Ros group had a significantly lower incidence of atrial fibrillation (P = 0.007) and other types of arrhythmia excluding atrial fibrillation (P = 0.005) (Table 8). Additionally, all-cause mortality was also significantly lower in the Ros group (P = 0.026). No significant differences were observed for low cardiac output syndrome, pleural effusion, stroke, myocardial infarction, heart failure, and chest pain (all P > 0.05). These findings suggest that rosuvastatin calcium treatment is associated with reduced risks of developing atrial fibrillation, other forms of arrhythmia, and all-cause mortality following surgery.

Discussion

The findings of this retrospective study indicate that perioperative administration of rosuvastatin calcium is associated with several meaningful improvements in postoperative recovery and myocardial health among patients undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting. Patients receiving rosuvastatin exhibited better left ventricular reverse remodeling, enhanced systolic function, improved microcirculatory parameters, modulated inflammatory and lipid profiles, reduced myocardial injury markers, and superior clinical outcomes compared to those receiving conventional treatment alone.

The observed attenuation in left ventricular end-diastolic diameter, septal thickness, and posterior wall thickness in the rosuvastatin group suggests a positive influence on post-surgical ventricular remodeling. This finding implies that rosuvastatin may help mitigate pathological remodeling processes often exacerbated by cardiac surgery involving ischemia-rereperfusion and cardiopulmonary bypass. Such beneficial structural changes could be mediated through several mechanisms [19, 20]. Rosuvastatin has been documented to inhibit cardiac fibroblast proliferation and reduce collagen synthesis, potentially limiting interstitial fibrosis. Furthermore, its ability to modulate matrix metalloproteinase activity may contribute to more favorable extracellular matrix regulation. These cellular effects might facilitate the restoration of more physiological ventricular geometry after surgical revascularization [21, 22]. Unlike studies in essential hypertensive patients without surgical revascularization, our findings in the post-CABG setting may reflect a synergistic effect of surgical revascularization and rosuvastatin-mediated anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory actions, rather than a direct antihypertensive effect on wall thickness.

Complementing these structural improvements, the rosuvastatin group demonstrated better systolic function parameters after surgery. The increased left ventricular ejection fraction and fractional shortening suggest enhanced contractile performance [23]. This functional benefit may be linked to improved myocardial blood flow distribution at the microcirculatory level, reduced myocardial stunning, and direct cardioprotective effects. Statins are known to upregulate endothelial nitric oxide synthase activity, which improves nitric oxide bioavailability and contributes to better coronary flow reserve. Additionally, through their antioxidant properties, statins may reduce oxidative stress-induced damage to contractile proteins and mitochondrial function, thereby preserving systolic function during the postoperative period [24, 25].

The assessment of myocardial microcirculation revealed better performance in the rosuvastatin-treated patients, as evidenced by changes in acceleration time, pulsatility index, and aortic pre-ejection period. These parameters collectively indicate reduced vascular resistance and improved flow dynamics within the coronary microvasculature. Microcirculatory dysfunction following CABG represents a well-recognized phenomenon attributed to endothelial activation, leukocyte plugging, and impaired vasomotor regulation. Rosuvastatin’s pleiotropic effects, particularly its anti-inflammatory and endothelial-protective properties, likely play a crucial role in preserving microvascular integrity [26, 27]. Previous studies have demonstrated that statins can reduce endothelial activation markers and inhibit leukocyte-endothelial interactions, potentially preventing capillary obstruction and maintaining tissue perfusion despite the systemic inflammatory response triggered by cardiopulmonary bypass [28, 29].

The biochemical analyses provided further insights into potential mechanisms underlying the clinical benefits. The modulation of inflammatory markers, specifically the higher lymphocyte counts and lower levels of C-reactive protein and procalcitonin in the rosuvastatin group, suggests systemic anti-inflammatory effects [30]. This finding aligns with extensive literature on statins’ immunomodulatory properties, including their ability to inhibit pro-inflammatory cytokine production and reduce acute-phase reactants. The reduction in postoperative procalcitonin is particularly noteworthy as it may indicate better control of the systemic inflammatory response syndrome associated with major cardiac surgery [31, 32].

The lipid profile analysis revealed that rosuvastatin administration specifically increased high-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels without substantially affecting other lipid parameters during the early postoperative period. This selective effect on HDL-C may have functional significance beyond cholesterol mobilization. HDL particles possess anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and endothelial-stabilizing properties that could contribute to microcirculatory protection. This observation reinforces the concept that the benefits of rosuvastatin in this setting extend beyond conventional lipid-lowering effects [33, 34].

The observed reduction in creatine kinase-MB levels in the rosuvastatin group indicates less myocardial injury during the perioperative period. This cardioprotective effect might be mediated through several pathways, including stabilization of mitochondrial membranes, reduction of apoptotic signaling, and inhibition of ischemia-reperfusion injury cascades. By mitigating myocardial cellular damage, rosuvastatin may contribute to the preservation of functional tissue and better recovery of contractile function [35].

The clinical relevance of these findings is strengthened by the improvement in NYHA functional classification and the reduction in postoperative arrhythmias and mortality observed in the rosuvastatin group. The superior functional recovery suggests that the physiological benefits translate into meaningful clinical advantages for patients. The notable reduction in atrial fibrillation and other arrhythmias may be particularly important, as these complications represent common causes of prolonged hospitalization and increased morbidity after CABG. The antiarrhythmic effects of statins have been previously reported and may be attributed to their anti-inflammatory properties, modulation of autonomic tone, and stabilization of electrophysiological membrane properties [36].

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings. The retrospective design introduces potential for selection bias and unmeasured confounding factors despite balanced baseline characteristics. Therefore, although our retrospective cohort design with well-balanced baseline characteristics provides valuable real-world evidence, a prospective, randomized, double-blind controlled trial is warranted to confirm these findings and eliminate potential selection bias. The single-center nature may limit generalizability, and the sample size, while adequate for detecting differences in primary endpoints, may be insufficient for analysis of rare adverse events. The rosuvastatin dose used in this study (10 mg/day) is lower than the high-intensity regimen recommended by current guidelines for achieving LDL-C < 70 mg/dL in post-CABG patients. Our primary aim was to evaluate pleiotropic effects on microcirculation rather than to achieve maximal lipid lowering. Moreover, postoperative LDL-C was measured 4 weeks after drug discontinuation, which may have underestimated the on-treatment lipid-lowering effect. Future studies should incorporate on-treatment lipid assessments and test higher doses to determine whether additional LDL-C reduction confers further microcirculatory benefits. Additionally, the absence of real-time echocardiographic and laboratory assessments (e.g., at 2 or 4 weeks postoperatively) precludes evaluation of the temporal dynamics of recovery. Future prospective studies should incorporate serial follow-up time points to better characterize the time course of rosuvastatin’s effects on myocardial microcirculation and ventricular remodeling. But the relatively short-term follow-up period precludes assessment of long-term outcomes sustained beyond one year. Our arrhythmia assessment did not differentiate between early and late postoperative events, which may obscure the distinct pathophysiological and prognostic implications of arrhythmias occurring at different time points. Future prospective studies with continuous rhythm monitoring should address this distinction. Additionally, the study could not investigate specific molecular mechanisms underlying the observed benefits due to its clinical nature. The retrospective design precluded measurement of oxidative stress markers, endothelial dysfunction indicators, and detailed pro-inflammatory cytokines. Future prospective studies should incorporate these parameters to elucidate underlying mechanisms. It is acknowledged that withholding statin therapy in the conventional group may not reflect current standard of care, as statins are recommended for nearly all patients with coronary artery disease. This limitation stems from the retrospective nature of the study, where some patients did not receive statins due to historical prescribing patterns. Future studies should compare rosuvastatin add-on versus standard statin therapy rather than statin versus no statin.

Future research should address these limitations through prospective, randomized controlled trials with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up periods. Studies incorporating more detailed mechanistic assessments, including measurements of endothelial function, oxidative stress markers, and inflammatory cytokines, would provide deeper insights into the pathways through which rosuvastatin exerts its protective effects. Investigation of optimal dosing strategies and timing initiation relative to surgery would also be valuable for clinical translation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study provides evidence that perioperative rosuvastatin calcium administration may improve myocardial remodeling, systolic function, microcirculatory parameters, and clinical outcomes in patients undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting. The multiple beneficial effects observed support the concept that rosuvastatin’s pleiotropic properties offer important advantages in the context of cardiac surgery beyond its lipid-lowering capabilities. These findings contribute to the growing body of evidence supporting the strategic use of statins in the perioperative management of CABG patients.

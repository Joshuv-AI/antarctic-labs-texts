Abstract
. We prove a convergence theorem for stochastic gradient descents on manifolds with adaptive learning rate and apply it to the weighted low-rank approximation problem.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Stochastic Gradient Descents with Adaptive Learning Rates in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Let
\Omega
be a probability space. A stochastic gradient descent in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for a
C^{1}
cost function
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by the iterative update

\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\eta_{t}g(\mathbf{x}_{t},\omega_{t})

(1.1)

where
g
is a function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\{\omega_{t}\}_{0}^{\infty}\subset\Omega
is a sequence of random variables of independent identical distribution in
\Omega
satisfying
E(g(\mathbf{x},\omega_{t}))=\nabla F(\mathbf{x})

\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In classical applications of stochastic gradient descents, the learning rates
\{\eta_{t}\}_{0}^{\infty}
are usually a deterministic sequence of non-negative numbers satisfying

\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\eta_{t}=\infty\text{ and }\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}<\infty.

(1.2)

More recent applications often adopt adaptive learning rates not satisfying these constraints. In
[
LO19
]
, Li and Orabona provided an elegant proof for the convergence of stochastic gradient descent (
1.1
) with the adaptive learning rates

\eta_{t}=\frac{\alpha}{(\beta+\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\|g(\mathbf{x}_{i},\omega_{i})\|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}},

(1.3)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are positive numbers and
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

1.2.
Overview and Main Contributions

In the current paper, we generalize
[
LO19
]
to stochastic gradient descents on Riemannian manifolds, which leads to Theorem
2.7
below. While the core idea of our proof of Theorem
2.7
is similar to that in
[
LO19
]
, we need to overcome technical hurdles rooted in three issues:

(1)

It is assumed in
[
LO19
]
that the cost function and its gradient are both globally Lipschitz. This limits the application of the results of
[
LO19
]
. We replace the assumption of global Lipschitzness by a notion of retraction-specific local Lipschitzness. See Definition
2.3
below.

(2)

To use the local Lipschitzness, we need to confine the search steps in a compact subset of the manifold. For this purpose, we opt to assume that the random search admits a “
\kappa
-confinement”. See Definition
2.5
below. This also guarantees that the search sequence contains a converging subsequence.

(3)

To prove Theorem
2.7
, we need to estimate the difference of the gradients of two closely related functions in Lemma
2.15
. When working on an Euclidean space, these two gradients are identical, which eliminates the need for such an estimation. But, when working on a Riemannian manifold, it took us some effort to control the difference between them.

After resolving these issues, we apply the arguments similar to those in
[
LO19
]
to complete our proof of Theorem
2.7
.

Then, as an application, we apply Theorem
2.7
to establish an algorithm for a Regularized Weighted Low-Rank Approximation Problem (Problem
3.3
).
We introduced in
[
XYW25
]
a deterministic/“semi-adaptive” approach to stochastic gradient descents on manifolds.
We will compare the computational efficiencies of the adaptive and semi-adaptive approaches when applied to Problem
3.3
.

2.
A Convergent Theorem

2.1.
Relevant Concepts

Before stating our main convergence theorem, we need to review some necessary technical concepts and their basic properties. In this manuscript, we only discuss the type of random functions given in Definition
2.1
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M
be a differentiable manifold, and
\Omega
a probability space. A function
f:M\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called a random function on
M
. We say that
f
is
k
th-order differentiable in the
M
-direction if, for every
\omega\in\Omega
,
f(\ast,\omega):M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
k
th-order differentiable. We also say that
f
is locally bounded on
M
if, for every compact set
K\subset M
, there is a
C_{K}>0
such that
|f(x,\omega)|\leq C_{K}
for every
(x,\omega)\in K\times\Omega
.

In Definition
2.1
,
M
is the manifold in which we search for the optimal parameters of the learning model.
\Omega
is the space of samples used to guide our search.

Definition 2.2
.

[
AMS09
, Definition 4.1.1]

Let
M
be a differentiable manifold. A retraction on
M
is a
C^{1}
map
R:TM\rightarrow M
such that, for every
x\in M
, the restriction
R_{x}=R|_{T_{x}M}
satisfies

•

R_{x}(\mathbf{0}_{x})=x
, where
\mathbf{0}_{x}
is the zero vector in
T_{x}M
,

•

dR_{x}(\mathbf{0}_{x})=\mathrm{id}_{T_{x}M}
under the canonical identification
T_{\mathbf{0}_{x}}T_{x}M\cong T_{x}M
, where
dR_{x}
is the differential of
R_{x}
and
\mathrm{id}_{T_{x}M}
is the identity map of
T_{x}M
.

We also need the following concept of retraction-specific Lipschitzness.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold,
R
a given retraction on
M
, and
\Omega
a probability space. Suppose that the random function
f:M\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is first-order differentiable in the
M
-direction. Then, for each
x\in M
and each
\omega\in\Omega
, the function
f_{x,\omega}:=f(R_{x}(\ast),\omega):T_{x}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a first-order differentiable function. Its gradient
\nabla f_{x,\omega}
is the vector in
T_{x}M
dual to the differential
df_{x,\omega}
via the Riemannian inner product
\left\langle\ast,\ast\right\rangle_{x}
.

•

f
is said to have
R
-Lipschitz gradient in the
M
-direction if there is a constant
C>0
such that

\|\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{v})-\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{0}_{x})\|_{x}\leq C\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}

for every
x\in M
,
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
and
\omega\in\Omega
.

•

f
is said to have locally
R
-Lipschitz gradient in the
M
-direction if, for every compact subset
K
of
M
and every
r>0
, there is a constant
C_{K,r}>0
such that

\|\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{v})-\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{0}_{x})\|_{x}\leq C_{K,r}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}

for every
x\in K
, every
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\leq r
and every
\omega\in\Omega
.

In the case when
\Omega=\{\omega\}
is a probability space of a single point, the above gives the definitions of the gradient of a deterministic function on
M
being
R
-Lipschitz and locally
R
-Lipschitz.

Remark 2.4
.

In Definition
2.3
, denote by
\nabla_{M}f
the gradient of
f
with respenct to
M
, that is,
\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)=\nabla f_{w}(x)
, where
f_{\omega}:=f(\ast,\omega):M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Then according to
[
XYW25
, Remark 2.4]
, we have that

\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{0}_{x})=\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega).

(2.1)

To guarantee that there is a compact subset of the manifold that contains all steps of the stochastic gradient descent, we assume that, for some
\kappa>0
, there is a
\kappa
-confinement for the random function used in the stochastic gradient descent.

Definition 2.5

(
\kappa
-confinement)
.

Assume that:

(1)

M
is a Riemannian manifold with a fixed

retraction
R:TM\rightarrow M
;

(2)

\Omega
is a probability space;

(3)

f:M\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a random function on
M
that is first-order differentiable in the
M
-direction.

For a fixed
\kappa>0
, a
\kappa
-confinement of
f
on

is
C^{2}
differentiable function
\rho:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

•

For every
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the set
\{x\in M~\big|~\rho(x)\leq c\}
is compact;

•

There exist
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

–

\rho_{0}<\rho_{1}
,

–

\rho(R_{x}(-s\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)))\leq\rho_{1}

(2.2)

for every

, every

and every

,

\left\langle\nabla\rho(x),\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)\right\rangle_{x}\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}\Big|\mathrm{Hess}(\rho\circ R_{x})|_{-s\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)}(\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega),\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega))\Big|

(2.3)

for every

The following lemma explains the origin of the idea of
\kappa
-confinements.

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume that:

(1)

M
is a Riemannian manifold with a fixed
C^{2}
retraction
R:TM\rightarrow M
.

(2)

\Omega
is a probability space.

(3)

f:M\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a random function on
M
that is first-order differentiable in the
M
-direction.

(4)

\widetilde{\kappa}>0
.

(5)

\rho:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
C^{2}
function satisfying:

•

For every
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the set
\{x\in M~\big|~\rho(x)\leq c\}
is compact;

•

There exists a
\rho_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and a
\zeta>0
such that
\left\langle\nabla\rho(x),\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)\right\rangle_{x}\geq\zeta
for every
\omega\in\Omega
and every
x\in M
satisfying
\rho(x)\geq\rho_{0}
.

Let

(i)

K_{0}:=\{x\in M~\big|~\rho(x)\leq\rho_{0}\}
,
G_{0}:=\max\{\|\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)\|_{x}~\big|~x\in K_{0},~\omega\in\Omega\}
,

(ii)

\rho_{1}:=\max\{\rho_{0}+\widetilde{\kappa}|\left\langle\nabla\rho(x),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}|+\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\kappa}^{2}|\mathrm{Hess}(\rho\circ R_{x})|_{-s\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})|~\big|~x\in K_{0},~\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M,~\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\leq G_{0},~s\in[0,\widetilde{\kappa}]\},

(iii)

K_{1}:=\{x\in M~\big|~\rho(x)\leq\rho_{1}\}
and
\widetilde{G}_{1}:=\max\{\|\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)\|_{x}~\big|~x\in K_{1},~\omega\in\Omega\}
,

(iv)

\kappa:=\min\left\{\widetilde{\kappa},\frac{2\zeta}{\max\{|\mathrm{Hess}(\rho\circ R_{x})|_{-s\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})|~\big|~x\in K_{1},~\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M,~\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\leq G_{1},~s\in[0,\widetilde{\kappa}]\}}\right\}

Then
\rho
is a
\kappa
-confinement of
f
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.8
below, for any
x\in M
, and any
s\geq 0
, we have that

\rho(R_{x}(-s\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)))=\rho(x)-s\left\langle\nabla\rho(x),\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\mathrm{Hess}(\rho\circ R_{x})|_{-s^{\star}\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)}(\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega),\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega))

for some
s^{\star}\in[0,s]
. By the definition of
\rho_{1}
in item (
ii
) above, one can see that
\rho(R_{x}(-s\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)))\leq\rho_{1}
for any
x\in K_{0}
and
s\in[0,\kappa]\subset[0,\widetilde{\kappa}]
. This proves Inequality (
2.2
).

For every
s\in[0,\kappa]
, every
\omega\in\Omega
and every
x\in M
satisfying
\rho_{0}\leq\rho(x)\leq\rho_{1}
, we have that

\left\langle\nabla\rho(x),\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)\right\rangle_{x}\geq\zeta\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}\big|\mathrm{Hess}(\rho\circ R_{x})|_{-s\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)}(\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega),\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega))\big|.

This proves Inequality (
2.3
).

2.2.
The Convergent Theorem

The following is the main theoretical result of the current paper.

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume that:

(1)

M
is an
m
-dimensional Riemannian manifold equipped with a
C^{2}
retraction
R:TM\rightarrow M
.
Also,
\left\langle\ast,\ast\right\rangle_{x}
is the Riemannian inner product on
T_{x}M
for all
x\in M
and induces the norm
\|\ast\|_{x}
on
T_{x}M
for all
x\in M
.

(2)

\Omega
is a probability space with a probability measure
\mu
.

(3)

f:M\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a random function on
M
satisfying:

(i)

f
is first-order differentiable in the
M
-direction,

(ii)

f
and
\|\nabla_{M}f\|
are locally bounded on
M
,

(iii)

f
has locally
R
-Lipschitz gradient in the
M
-direction.

(4)

F:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the expectation of
f(x,\omega)
with respect to
\omega
. That is,
F(x)=E(f(x,\omega))=\int_{\Omega}f(x,\omega)d\mu
for all
x\in M
.

(5)

For a fixed
\kappa>0
,
\rho:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{2}

\kappa
-confinement of
f
. Fix
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
for which Inequalities (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) are true. Let
K_{1}
be the compact subsets of
M
given by

K_{1}:=\left\{x\in M~\big|~\rho(x)\leq\rho_{1}\right\}.

(2.4)

(6)

\{\omega_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of independent random variables taking values in
\Omega
with identical probability distribution
\mu
.

(7)

x_{0}\in K_{1}
is a fixed point.

(8)

Fix positive constants
\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta
satisfying:

(i)

0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

(ii)

\frac{\alpha}{\beta^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\leq\kappa.

(2.5)

Define a sequences
\{x_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
of random elements of
M
and a sequence
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
of random positive numbers by

\begin{cases}x_{t+1}=R_{x_{t}}\left(-\eta_{t}\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})\right)\\
\eta_{t}=\frac{\alpha}{(\beta+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\|\nabla_{M}f(x_{s},\omega_{s})\|_{x_{s}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\end{cases}\text{ for }t\geq 0.

(2.6)

Then:

•

\{x_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
is contained in the compact subset
K_{1}
of
M
. In particular, it has convergent subsequences.

•

\{\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
converges almost surely to
0
.

•

Any limit point of
\{x_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
is almost surely a stationary point of
F
.

Some of our technical lemmas are fairly basic. Some of these have already appeared in
[

]
for example. For the convenience of the reader, we nevertheless include these lemmas and the proofs of most of them here. The main outlier is Lemma

. Over Euclidean spaces, this lemma is trivial. So it does not appear in
[

,

]
. Even in
[

]
, we only need a weaker version of this lemma, which is easier to prove. See

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
h:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be any
C^{2}
function. Then, for any
x\in M
and
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
, there exists an
s^{\star}\in[0,1]
such that
h(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))=h(x)+\left\langle\nabla h(x),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Hess}(h\circ R_{x})|_{s^{\star}\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})
, where
\mathrm{Hess}(h\circ R_{x})
is the Hessian of the function
h\circ R_{x}:T_{x}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, which is defined on the inner product space
T_{x}M
.

Proof.

Define
p(s)=h(R_{x}(s\mathbf{v}))
. Then, by Taylor’s Theorem, there exists an
s^{\star}\in[0,1]
such that
p(1)=p(0)+p^{\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{2}p^{\prime\prime}(s^{\star})
. Note that:

•

p(0)=h(R_{x}(\mathbf{0}_{x}))=h(x)
and
p(1)=h(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))
;

•

p^{\prime}(s)=\left\langle\nabla(h\circ R_{x})(s\mathbf{v}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}
and therefore
p^{\prime}(0)=\left\langle\nabla(h\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{0}_{x}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}=\left\langle\nabla h(x),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}
, where the last equation follows from Equation (
2.1
);

•

p^{\prime\prime}(s)=\frac{d}{ds}\left\langle\nabla(h\circ R_{x})(s\mathbf{v}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}=\left\langle\frac{d}{ds}\nabla(h\circ R_{x})(s\mathbf{v}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}=\mathrm{Hess}(h\circ R_{x})|_{s\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})
.

Combining the above, we get that
h(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))=h(x)+\left\langle\nabla h(x),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Hess}(h\circ R_{x})|_{s^{\star}\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})
.
∎

\rho(x_{t})\leq\rho_{1}

Lemma 2.10
.

F
is first-order differentiable and has locally
R
-Lipschitz gradient. Moreover, there exists a
C_{1}>0
such that
\|\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{v})-\nabla F(x)\|_{x}\leq C_{1}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}
and
F(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\leq F(x)+\left\langle\nabla F(x),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}+\frac{C_{1}}{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}^{2}
for every
x\in K_{1}
and every
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\leq\kappa G_{1}
, where
G_{1}
is given by

G_{1}:=\max\{\|\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)\|_{x}~\big|~x\in K_{1},~\omega\in\Omega\}.

(2.8)

Proof.

Since
\|\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)\|
is locally bounded, it follows from the Donimated Convergence Theorem that
F
is first-order differentiable and
\nabla F(x)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)d\mu
. Since
R
is
C^{2}
,
\|\nabla_{T_{x}M}f(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}),\omega)\|
is also locally bounded, So, again by the Donimated Convergence Theorem, we have
\nabla(F\circ R)(\mathbf{v})=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{T_{x}M}f(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}),\omega)d\mu=\int_{\Omega}\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{v})d\mu
, where
f_{x,\omega}:=f(R_{x}(\ast),\omega):T_{x}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined in Definition
2.3
.

Since
f
has locally
R
-Lipschitz gradient, we know that for every compact subset
K
of
M
and every
r>0
, there is a constant
C_{K,r}>0
such that
\|\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{v})-\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{0}_{x})\|_{x}\leq C_{K,r}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}
for every
x\in K
, every
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\leq r
and every
\omega\in\Omega
. Thus,

\displaystyle\|\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{v})-\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{0}_{x})\|_{x}=\|\int_{\Omega}\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{v})d\mu-\int_{\Omega}\nabla f_{x,\omega}((\mathbf{0}_{x}))d\mu\|_{x}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{v})-\nabla f_{x,\omega}((\mathbf{0}_{x}))\|_{x}d\mu\leq\int_{\Omega}C_{K,r}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}d\mu=C_{K,r}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}.

This shows that
F
has locally
R
-Lipschitz gradient.

Now consider the special case
K=K_{1}=\{x\in M~\big|~\rho(x)\leq\rho_{1}\}
and
r=\kappa G_{1}
. Let

. Then, by Equation (

) and the above inequality, we have that

\rho(x)\leq\rho_{1}
and every
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|\leq\kappa G_{1}
. Let

\displaystyle F(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))-F(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(1)-p(0)=\int_{0}^{1}p^{\prime}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(s\mathbf{v}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla F(x),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}+\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(s\mathbf{v})-\nabla F(x),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}ds

for every

Lemma 2.14
.

Fix any
x\in M
and any
r>0
. Then there are an open neighborhood
U_{x}
of
x
in
M
and a positive number
C_{x,r}
such that

\left|\|\nabla F(R_{y}(\mathbf{u}))\|_{R_{y}(\mathbf{u})}-\|\nabla(F\circ R_{y})(\mathbf{u})\|_{y}\right|\leq C_{x,r}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{y}

for every
y\in U_{x}
and every
\mathbf{u}\in T_{y}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{u}\|_{y}\leq r
.

Proof.

For any
y\in M
and
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in T_{y}M
, we have that

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla(F\circ R_{y})(\mathbf{u}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{y}=d(F\circ R_{y})|_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})=((dF|_{R_{y}(\mathbf{u})})\circ(dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{u}}))(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle(\nabla F)(R_{y}(\mathbf{u})),(dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{u}})(\mathbf{v})\right\rangle_{R_{y}(\mathbf{u})}=\left\langle\mathrm{adj}(dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{u}})((\nabla F)(R_{y}(\mathbf{u}))),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{y},

where
\mathrm{adj}(dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{u}})
is the adjoint of
dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{u}}
with respect to the inner products
\left\langle\ast,\ast\right\rangle_{y}
and
\left\langle\ast,\ast\right\rangle_{R_{y}(\mathbf{u})}
. This shows that

\nabla(F\circ R_{y})(\mathbf{u})=\mathrm{adj}(dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{u}})((\nabla F)(R_{y}(\mathbf{u}))).

(2.13)

Let
(W_{x},(y_{1},\dots,y_{m}))
be a
C^{\infty}
coordinate chart containing
x

U_{x}
of
x
such that its closure
\overline{U_{x}}
is compact and contained in
W_{x}
. For
y\in W_{x}
, apply the Gram-Schmidt Process to the basis
[\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{m}}]
of
T_{x}M
and then normalize the lengths. This gives an orthonormal basis
[\mathbf{v}_{1}(y),\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}(y)]
for
T_{y}M
and a
C^{\infty}

m\times m
invertible upper-triangular matrix

such that
[\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{m}}]=[\mathbf{v}_{1}(y),\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}(y)]P(y)
. For
y\in U_{x}
and
\mathbf{u}\in T_{y}M
, we use the orthonormal basis
[\mathbf{v}_{1}(y),\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}(y)]
for
T_{y}M
and the basis
[PT_{R}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{1}(y)),\dots,PT_{R}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{m}(y))]
for
T_{R_{y}(\mathbf{u})}M
. By Lemma
2.13
,
[PT_{R}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{1}(y)),\dots,PT_{R}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{m}(y))]

C^{1}

\mathbf{u}
.

Under the standard identification
T_{\mathbf{u}}(T_{y}M)=T_{y}M
, denote by
[dR_{i,j}]
the
m\times m
matrix of the differential map

under these bases for the two tangent spaces. Then each

is

jointly in
y
and
\mathbf{u}

is continuous jointly in

and
\mathbf{u}

D_{x,r}:=\max\{\|\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}dR_{i,j}(y,\mathbf{u})\|_{y}~\big|~1\leq i,j\leq m,~y\in\overline{U_{x}},~\mathbf{u}\in T_{y}M,~\|\mathbf{u}\|_{y}\leq r\}<\infty
. By the Mean Value Theorem, there is an

Recall that

\displaystyle\left|\|\nabla F(R_{y}(\mathbf{u}))\|_{R_{y}(\mathbf{u})}-\|\nabla(F\circ R_{y})(\mathbf{u})\|_{y}\right|=\left|\|\nabla F(R_{y}(\mathbf{u}))\|_{R_{y}(\mathbf{u})}-\|\mathrm{adj}(R_{y}|_{\mathbf{u}})(\nabla F(R_{y}(\mathbf{u})))\|_{y}\right|

\displaystyle\left|\|[PT_{R}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{1}(y)),\dots,PT_{R}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{m}(y))]\vec{w}\|_{R_{y}(\mathbf{u})}-\|[\mathbf{v}_{1}(y),\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}(y)][dR_{i,j}(y,\mathbf{u})]^{T}\vec{w}\|_{y}\right|

3.
An Application: Weighted Low-rank Approximation

3.1.
The Weighted Low-Rank Approximation Problem

Problem 3.1

(The Weighted Low-Rank Approximation Problem)
.

Assume that:

(1)

m
,
n
and
k
are fixed positive integers satisfying
k\leq\min\{m,n\}
,

(2)

A=[a_{i,j}]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is a given matrix of constants,

(3)

W=[w_{i,j}]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is a given matrix of weights satisfying
w_{i,j}\geq 0
for
(i,j)\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}\times\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{i,j}=1
,

where
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is the space of
m\times n
real matrices. Define
\hat{F}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\hat{F}(P)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{i,j}(a_{i,j}-p_{i,j})^{2}
for
P=[p_{i,j}]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
. Solve for

\mathrm{argmin}\{\hat{F}(P)~|~P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},~\mathrm{rank}P\leq k\}.

Problem
3.1
and its special case, the Matrix Completion Problem, are studied extensively in the recent literature. (See, for example,
[
BCWLP24
,
YTL24
,
YZ24
,
Lee24
,
KLL+23
,
CD23
,
YM23
,
BCN23
,
WC23
,
BA11
]
).

Problem
3.1
is known to be NP-hard
[
GG11
]
. One existing approach to this problem is to reformulate it as an uncontrained problem on
\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
and then estimate the solution to the uncontrained problem by various first order or second order algorithms on the Euclidean space. (See, for instance,
[
BWZ19
,
BA11
,
SJ03
]
). We approach Problem
3.1
by reformulating it as an unconstrained problem on a Riemmanian manifold in
[
XYW25
]
.

In the current paper, we continue with this approach but using adaptive learning rates.
To guarantee the convergence of the algorithm, we consider a regularized version of Problem
3.1
below.

Problem 3.2

(A Regularized Weighted Low Rank Approximation Problem)
.

Under Assumptions (
1
)-(
3
) in Problem
3.1
, fix a positive number
\lambda
and define
F:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
{F(P)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{i,j}(a_{i,j}-p_{i,j})^{2}+\lambda\|P\|_{F}^{2}}
for

\mathrm{argmin}\{F(P)~|~P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},~\mathrm{rank}P\leq k\}.

To reformulate Problem
3.2
as an unconstrained problem, let us quickly review the Reduced Singular Value Decomposition.
The
n\times k
Stiefel manifold

V_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}~|~V^{T}V=I_{k}\},

k\times k

D^{k\times k}_{k}([x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]^{T})=[d_{i,j}],\text{ where }d_{i,j}=\begin{cases}x_{i}&\text{if }i=j,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3.1)

For

,

if any only if it admits the following Reduced Singular Value Decomposition

P=UD^{k\times k}_{k}(\mathbf{x})V^{T},

(3.2)

where
U\in V_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
V\in V_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

The Reduced Singular Value Decomposition motivates us to reformulate Problem

into Problem

We then apply Theorem

to design a stochastic gradient descent with adaptive learning rates over this manifold for Problem

Additive Effects of Platelet-Rich Plasma and Systemic Therapies in Androgenetic Alopecia: A Retrospective Study
Authors: Anna L. Brinks, Carli Needle Lawrence, Derek Maas, Caitlin A. Kearney, Nnaemeka Anyanwu, Deesha D. Desai, Ambika Nohria, Michelle Sikora, Christina S. Oh, Jerry Shapiro, Kristen I. Lo Sicco
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionEstablished treatments for androgenetic alopecia (AGA) include low-dose oral minoxidil (LDOM), spironolactone, finasteride, dutasteride, and platelet-rich plasma (PRP). While PRP is often combined with topical minoxidil, studies on its use with systemic agents are limited. This study compares trichometric outcomes and side effects between PRP monotherapy and PRP plus systemic therapy.MethodsAn IRB-approved retrospective review was conducted of NYU AGA patients treated between 11/1/17 and 8/1/25. Changes in hair density (hairs/cm2) and width (µm) were assessed from baseline to final follow-up among patients receiving PRP alone or PRP with LDOM, spironolactone, finasteride, or dutasteride. AGA severity was determined via Hamilton-Norwood or Ludwig scale. Mann-Whitney U tests, Fisher exact tests, and multivariate linear regression were performed.ResultsFifty-five patients met inclusion criteria (65.5% female, mean age 49). Compared to monotherapy (n = 6), combination therapy (n = 49) showed greater, though not statistically significant, gains in density (+6.06 hairs/cm2, p = 0.703) and width (+3.59 µm, p = 0.460). Controlling for treatment type, moderate AGA predicted significantly greater density improvement (+25.11 hairs/cm2, p = 0.040).ConclusionsCombination therapy showed numerically higher but statistically nonsignificant improvements without increased adverse effects. These findings support further prospective studies to optimize combination regimens and patient selection by AGA severity.

Introduction

Established treatments for androgenetic alopecia (AGA) include low-dose oral minoxidil, spironolactone, finasteride, dutasteride, and platelet-rich plasma (PRP) [1, 2]. A growing body of evidence supports the use of PRP with topical minoxidil, but studies investigating PRP combined with systemic therapies are limited [3, 4]. Combination therapy may provide synergistic benefits by targeting complementary mechanisms, potentially stimulating hair follicles through both growth factor release and modulation of androgen and vascular pathways. This study seeks to compare trichometric outcomes and side effects between PRP monotherapy and PRP combination therapy groups.

Methods

We conducted an IRB-approved retrospective review of NYU patients with AGA between 11/1/17 and 8/1/25. Inclusion criteria required ≥1 baseline and ≥1 follow-up trichometric measurement, along with standardized clinical photography or documented AGA severity. Patients were excluded if baseline or follow-up trichoscopy was unavailable or if AGA severity was unknown. Patients using concomitant topical minoxidil were excluded from the PRP monotherapy group. We analyzed changes in trichometric density (hairs/cm2) and width (μm) from initial to final follow-up visits for patients receiving PRP monotherapy or PRP combined with at least one systemic agent (low-dose oral minoxidil, spironolactone, finasteride, or dutasteride). Trichoscopic hair measurements were taken 12 cm from the glabella using the Canfield HairMetrix device (online suppl. Fig. 1; for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000552428). AGA severity was determined via Hamilton-Norwood (males) or Ludwig scale (females). Regen Lab PRP kits were used to prepare the PRP solution. For each patient, 5–10 mL of PRP was generated and administered as 50–100 injections of 0.1 mL each. Injections were distributed across the scalp with greater concentration in areas of thinning, such as the temples and vertex. Mann-Whitney U tests, Fisher exact tests, and multivariate linear regression were performed using Python 3.9.13.

Results

Overall, 55 individuals met inclusion criteria (65.5% female, mean age 49, 67.3% white, 1.8% Asian, 1.8% black, 29.1% unknown). Baseline characteristics showed similar gender distribution and AGA severity between the PRP monotherapy (n = 6) and combination therapy (n = 49) groups (Table 1). Combination therapy patients were older (50.5 versus 39.9 years, p = 0.160) and had higher baseline hair density (173.3 versus 149.0 hairs/cm2, p = 0.117) but lower baseline hair width (59.7 versus 68.2 µm, p = 0.177). Most patients (65.3%) initiated combination therapy prior to PRP, while 17 (34.7%) began concomitant therapy at the same visit. Among the 6 patients in the PRP monotherapy group, none had prior exposure to hair-directed systemic or topical therapies. There was no statistically significant difference between side effects for the two groups.

When adjusting for patient age and AGA severity, combination therapy was associated with a +6.06 hairs/cm2 greater density gain and +3.59 µm greater width gain compared with PRP monotherapy, although not statistically significant (p = 0.703 and p = 0.460, respectively) (Table 2). Controlling for treatment type, in comparison to mild AGA, moderate AGA predicted a statistically significant greater improvement in density (+25.11 hairs/cm2, p = 0.040), while severe AGA trended toward greater width gains (+11.85 µm, p = 0.065). When adjusting for age and number of PRP sessions, combination therapy continued to trend toward increased benefit for both density (+2.00 hairs/cm2) and width (+3.96 µm), although without statistical significance (p = 0.903 and p = 0.440, respectively). Similar trends were identified when analyzing PRP plus topical minoxidil (no systemic therapy; n = 13) compared to PRP plus systemic therapy (n = 49), with systemic therapy showing greater benefit (online suppl. Tables 1 and 2).

Discussion

While differences between monotherapy and combination therapy did not reach statistical significance, possibly due to the small sample sizes, combination therapy consistently demonstrated positive effect sizes for both hair density and width. This suggests an additive benefit when pharmacologic agents are used alongside PRP. Although studies remain limited, particularly randomized controlled trials, our findings align with emerging evidence suggesting that combination regimens may provide enhanced efficacy in AGA. For example, PRP combined with topical minoxidil 5% applied twice daily has been shown to be significantly more effective than topical minoxidil monotherapy [5]. Similarly, studies have supported the use of oral finasteride 1 mg daily in combination with PRP, suggesting potential additive or synergistic effects [5].

Furthermore, the significant association between moderate AGA severity and greater density gains suggests that patients with moderate disease may derive greater benefit. Prior studies have suggested that PRP may be most effective in early to moderate stages of AGA, when hair follicles remain viable but are undergoing progressive miniaturization [6]. Our findings may reflect a similar biological window of responsiveness, in which follicles remain capable of responding to growth factor signaling while still allowing measurable improvement compared with patients with milder disease.

Limitations include small PRP-only sample size and retrospective design. Nonetheless, the consistent direction of effect and favorable side-effect profile supports further investigation of combination therapy in larger, prospective studies. Optimizing patient selection based on AGA severity may further enhance treatment response.

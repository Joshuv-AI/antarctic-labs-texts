Quotients of skew polynomial rings: new constructions of division algebras and MRD codes
F.J. Lobillo, Paolo Santonastaso, John Sheekey

Abstract
We achieve new results on skew polynomial rings and their quotients, including the first explicit example of a skew polynomial ring where the ratio of the degree of a skew polynomial to the degree of its bound is not extremal. These methods lead to the construction of new (not necessarily associative) division algebras and maximum rank distance (MRD) codes over both finite and infinite division rings. In particular, we construct new non-associative division algebras whose right nucleus is a central simple algebra having degree greater than 1. Over finite fields, we obtain new semifields and MRD codes for infinitely many choices of parameters. These families extend and contain many of the best previously known constructions.



Keywords:
Skew polynomial ring; division algebra; semifield; rank-metric code.

MSC2020:
16S36; 17A35; 12K10; 11T71.

Introduction

In this paper, we study
(not necessarily associative) division algebras
, also known as
semifields
, and
maximum rank distance (MRD) codes
. Both of these objects can be viewed as subsets of a matrix ring
M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
over an (associative) division ring
{\mathbb{D}}
with special properties with respect to the rank function. We construct new examples and prove new results on both of these topics, by studying subsets of quotient spaces of
skew polynomial rings
.

Associative division algebras have been studied for both pure and applied reasons since Hamilton’s famous discovery of the quaternions. Nonassociative division algebras have also attracted attention, in particular over finite fields, where they are also known as
semifields

[
31
,
15
]
. Division algebras can be viewed as special cases of
rank-metric codes
. Rank-metric codes over finite fields have been employed in different contexts in communications and security. We suggest
[
18
,
6
]
, for a general introduction to rank-metric codes, explaining their most important applications.

In
[
51
,
52
,
2
]
, the general theory of rank-metric
codes was studied over arbitrary Galois extensions. Rank metric codes have also been constructed over finite principal ideal rings
[
27
]
and discretely valued rings
[
12
]
. The theory of rank-metric codes is also extended to a noncommutative ring context, producing interesting connections with space-time coding, see e.g.
[
41
,
49
,
58
]
. More precisely, the notion of rank-metric codes in
M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
has also been considered when
{\mathbb{D}}
is not necessarily a field.

Quotients of skew polynomial rings were first used to construct associative (cyclic) division algebras by Jacobson
[
24
,
26
]
. This was extended to nonassociative division algebras by Sandler
[
53
]
and Petit
[
45
]
. More recently, new examples of MRD codes and division algebras have been constructed using skew polynomial rings in
[
55
]
(for the case of finite fields) and
[
58
]
(for the case of infinite fields).

We postpone technical definitions and further references until later sections in order to more expeditiously state our setup and our main contributions.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a field, and
{\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{K}}
be a cyclic Galois extension of degree
n
. Consider a skew polynomial ring
R={\mathbb{L}}[x;\sigma]
, where
\sigma
is a generator of the Galois group
\Gal({\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{K}})
. Let
F(y)\in{\mathbb{K}}[y]
be an irreducible polynomial of degree
s\geq 1
. Then
F(x^{n})
generates a maximal two-sided ideal in
R
, and there exist integers
m,\ell
and a division algebra
{\mathbb{D}}
such that

•

n=m\ell
;

•

every irreducible divisor of
F(x^{n})
has degree
s\ell
;

•

factors completely as a product of
m

•

over

Previous work has focused on the following cases: (i) the case

, where

The goal of finding MRD codes equates to finding additively closed subsets of

; the case

equates to finding (not necessarily associative) division algebras. In the skew polynomial setting, this corresponds to finding maximum additively closed subsets of

of degree at most

In this paper our main contributions are as follows:

•

We give explicit examples of skew polynomial rings

and elements

such that

(cf.

3.1

).

•

We extend the constructions of MRD codes and division algebras from
[
55
]
and
[
58
]
to the case

, and compute the relevant parameters (cf.
Section

5.1
and
Section

5.2
).

•

We extend constructions of Trombetti-Zhou/Hughes-Kleinfeld to a larger family of MRD codes/division algebras/semifields, and compute the relevant parameters (cf.
Section

5.3
and
Section

5.4
).

•

In the case where
{\mathbb{L}}
is infinite, we construct explicit examples of nonassociative division algebras whose right nucleus is a central division algebra, which is not a field (cf.
Section

5.5
). In the case where
{\mathbb{L}}

2
Skew polynomial rings

We will construct division algebras/semifields and MRD codes using

, extending works such as
[
45
,
50
,
58
,
55
]
. We recall first the basic properties of skew polynomial rings. The study of these rings dates back to the seminal paper of Ore
[
42
]
, and they are one of the most prominent examples of non-commutative integral domains. In the rest of the paper,
{\mathbb{K}}
will be a field,
{\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{K}}
will be a cyclic Galois extension of degree
n
and we consider
\sigma
a generator of the Galois group
\Gal({\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{K}})
. We use the notation
{{\mathbb{L}}}^{\sigma}
to denote the fixed field of
{\mathbb{L}}
by
\sigma
. In our setting,
{{\mathbb{L}}}^{\sigma}={\mathbb{K}}
. The image of
a\in{\mathbb{L}}
by
\sigma
is denoted by

.

2.1
Basic properties

The
skew polynomial ring

{\mathbb{L}}[x;\sigma,\delta]
is a ring where

•

\delta
is a
\sigma
-derivation
of
{\mathbb{L}}
; that is, an additive map satisfying
\delta(ab)=a^{\sigma}\delta(b)+\delta(a)b
;

•

the elements of
{\mathbb{L}}[x;\sigma,\delta]
are polynomials in the indeterminate
x
; addition is polynomial addition;

•

multiplication is
{\mathbb{K}}
-bilinear, and satisfies
xa=a^{\sigma}x+{\delta}(a)
for all
a\in{\mathbb{L}}
.

This rings are also known as Ore extension of
{\mathbb{L}}
, in honor of O. Ore, who was the first to systematically investigate the general case
[
42
]
.

In the rest of this paper we will restrict ourselves to skew polynomial ring with trivial derivation
\delta=0
, i.e.
{\mathbb{L}}[x;\sigma,0]=:{\mathbb{L}}[x;\sigma]
(such rings are sometimes referred also to as
twisted polynomial rings
). Note that, since
{\mathbb{L}}
is a field, and
\sigma\neq id
, we get that every skew polynomial ring is isomorphic to a twisted polynomial ring, see e.g.
[
26
, Proposition 1.1.20]
.

For a skew polynomial ring the following well-known properties hold, see e.g.
[
26
, §1.1]
and
[
25
, Chapter 3]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
R={\mathbb{L}}[x;\sigma]
. Then

(i)

R
is both left- and right-Euclidean domain with the usual degree function, and
R
is Principal Ideal Domain on both sides;

(ii)

the centre
Z(R)
of
R
is
{\mathbb{K}}[x^{n};\sigma]\cong{\mathbb{K}}[y]
;

(iii)

if
f\in R
has factorisations
f=g_{1}\cdots g_{k}=h_{1}\cdots h_{m}
into irreducible elements, then
k=m
and there is a permutation
\pi
of
\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that
R/Rg_{\pi(i)}\cong R/Rh_{i}
, for all
i
. In particular,
\deg(g_{\pi(i)})=\deg(h_{i})
, for all
i
.

By
(i)
of
Theorem

2.1
, we get that every left (right) ideal of
R
is of the form
Rf=\{g(x)f(x)\colon g(x)\in R\}
, (
fR=\{f(x)g(x)\colon g(x)\in R\}
) for some
f(x)\in R
. An element
g
in
R
is said to be
two-sided
if
Rg=gR
. By e.g.
[
26
, Theorem 1.1.22]
,
g\in R
is two-sided if, and only if,
g(x)=dG(x^{n})x^{m}
, for some
d\in{\mathbb{L}}
,
G(y)\in{\mathbb{K}}[y]
, and
m\geq 0
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
bound
of a non zero
f\in R
, is any two-sided polynomial
f^{*}\in R
such that
Rf^{*}=f^{*}R
is the largest two-sided ideal contained in
Rf
or, equivalently

Rf^{*}=\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(R/Rf)=\{g\in R\colon(ga+Rf)=0+Rf,\mbox{ for any }a+Rf\in R/Rf\},

where
\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(R/Rf)
denotes the (left) annihilator of
R/Rf
in
R
.

So a bound of
f
is a minimal two-sided left multiple. With
R
being a domain, it is an easy exercise to check that a right divisor of a two-sided element is also a left divisor. So the bound could also been defined by means of right ideals; it is a left-right symmetric property.

If
f^{*}\neq 0
, the polynomial
f
is said to be
bounded
. Since
\sigma
is assumed to be an automorphism of
{\mathbb{L}}
and
R
has finite dimension
n^{2}
over its centre
Z(R)={\mathbb{K}}[x^{n}]
, then all non zero
f\in R
are bounded and

\deg(f^{*})\leq n\deg(f),

(1)

see
[
16
, Theorem 2.9]
.

For a non constant polynomial
f(x)
with nonzero constant coefficient, we have that any bound
f^{*}
of
f
is of the form
dF(x^{n})
for some
d\in{\mathbb{L}}
and a monic polynomial
F(y)\in{\mathbb{K}}[y]
, and the constant coefficient of
F(y)
is non zero; see
[
16
, Lemma 2.11]
. In such a case, we refer to the bound of the polynomial
f
as the unique monic central polynomial
f^{*}(x)=F(x^{n})
.
Moreover, by using (
1
), we get

\deg(F(y))\leq\deg(f).

(2)

Remark 2.3
.

In the literature, the polynomial
F(y)
is sometimes also referred as the minimal central left multiple of
f\in R
. Indeed, it is the unique monic polynomial
F(y)
of minimal degree in
Z(R)
such that
F(x^{n})=gf
for some
g\in R
, see e.g.
[
14
,
55
,
58
]
. Anyway, since
F(x^{n})=gf
is central, an easy computation shows that
F(x^{n})=fg
. Hence being central multiple is a left-right symmetric property, we could call
F(y)
the minimal central multiple.

We also recall that if
f
is an irreducible element of
R
of degree at least
1
with non zero constant coeffient, then
f^{*}(x)=F(x^{n})
is such that
F(y)
is an irreducible element of
{\mathbb{K}}[y]
.

Lemma 2.4

(see e.g.
[
16
, Remark 4.3.]
)
.

Let
F(y)\in{\mathbb{K}}[y]
, with
F(y)\neq y
. Then
F(y)
is irreducible if and only if the two-sided ideal
RF(x^{n})
of
R
is maximal.

Further background and facts about skew polynomial rings can be found in
[
14
,
26
,
16
]
.

2.2
Quotients of skew polynomial rings

Let
F(y)
be an irreducible polynomial of
{\mathbb{K}}[y]
having degree
s\geq 1
, with
F(y)\neq y
. We consider the quotient ring

R_{F}:=\frac{R}{RF(x^{n})}=\left\{a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{ns-1}x^{sn-1}+RF(x^{n})\colon a_{0},\ldots,a_{ns-1}\in{\mathbb{L}}\right\}.

As a consequence of
[
16
, Lemma 4.2]
, the centre of
R_{F}
is

E_{F}:=Z\left(\frac{R}{RF(x^{n})}\right)\cong\frac{{\mathbb{K}}[y]}{(F(y))},

and any element in
E_{F}
is of the form
a(x)+RF(x^{n})
, for some
a(x)\in Z(R)
.

Since
F(y)
is an irreducible polynomial, by Lemma
2.4
, we have that
E_{F}
is a field such that
[E_{F}:{\mathbb{K}}]=\deg(F)=s
. Moreover,
R_{F}
is a simple algebra over
E_{F}
having dimension
n^{2}
and dimension
n^{2}s
over
{\mathbb{K}}
, see
[
16
, §2 and §4]
for details.

Therefore, by Artin-Wedderbun’s Theorem,
R_{F}
is isomorphic to
M_{m}({\mathbb{D}})
, for a certain positive integer
m
and a central
E_{F}
-division algebra
{\mathbb{D}}
. In this case, the division algebra
{\mathbb{D}}
corresponds to the
eigenring
of an irreducible factor
f
of
F(x^{n})
in
R
.

Definition 2.5
.

For a nonzero polynomial
f\in R
, the largest subring
I(f)
of
R
in which the
Rf
is a two-sided ideal, is called the
left idealiser
of
f
, i.e.
I(f)=\{g\in R\colon fg\in Rf\}
. The quotient ring

\mathcal{E}(f):=\frac{I(f)}{Rf}=\{g\in R\colon\deg(g)<s\mbox{ and }fg\in Rf\},

is called the
eigenring
of
f
.

Precisely, the next theorem determines the integer
m
and the division algebra
{\mathbb{D}}
in
M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})\cong R/RF(x^{n})
in relation to the polynomial
F(y)
we started from.

Theorem 2.6

(see
[
44
, Theorem 20]
)
.

Let
F(y)\in{\mathbb{K}}[y]
be a monic irreducible polynomial having degree
s\geq 1
and let
f(x)\in R
be an irreducible divisor of
F(x^{n})
. Suppose
F(x^{n})
factorises into a product of
m
irreducible elements of
R
. Then
m
divides
n
and, letting

\ell:=\frac{n}{m},

(3)

we have that
\mathcal{E}(f)
is a central division algebra over
E_{F}
having degree
\ell
. Moreover, the following
E_{F}
-algebra isomorphism hold:

\frac{R}{RF(x^{n})}\cong M_{m}(\mathcal{E}(f)).

(4)

When we need to specify that the integers
m
and
\ell
arise from the polynomial
F
, we will use the notation
m_{F}
and
\ell_{F}
respectively.

In the case where
{\mathbb{L}}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
,
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
are finite, by the Wedderburn-Dickson Theorem we have that
\mathcal{E}(f)
is a field and so

\mathcal{E}(f)\cong E_{F}\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{s}}\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ \frac{R}{RF(x^{n})}\cong M_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{s}}),

as
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{s}}
-algebras.
Note that in this case
m=n
in (
4
) and
\ell_{F}=1
.

Moreover, the following main relations on the degree of a skew polynomial and its bound (or its minimal central multiple) hold. As observed in Remark
2.3
, being a divisor of a central element is a left-right symmetric property.

Proposition 2.7

(see
[
14
, Theorem 4.3, Theorem 4.4]
and
[
55
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Let
R={\mathbb{L}}[x;\sigma]
, were
\sigma
is not the identity automorphism. Then

(i)

if
F(y)
is an irreducible polynomial in
{\mathbb{K}}[y]
such that

factorises into a product of

irreducible elements of

, then every irreducible divisor of

has degree equal to
\ell_{F}\deg(F(y))
, and every divisor of
F(x^{n})
in

has degree equal to

for some
h\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. If
{\mathbb{K}}
is finite, then
\ell_{F}=1
;

(ii)

if

is an irreducible element of

with nonzero constant coefficient, then its bound
f^{*}(x)=F(x^{n})
is such that

is an irreducible element of
{\mathbb{K}}[y]
. Moreover, if

and
\ell_{F}
are as in (i), then

has degree

is finite, then
\ell_{F}=1
.

3
New results on skew polynomial rings

3.1
New explicit examples where
1<\ell_{F}<n

In this section we prove that there exist skew polynomial rings
R
containing central elements
F(x^{n})
such that
1<\ell_{F}<n
. To our knowledge, this is the first such explicit example. We also show that the values
\ell_{F}
may vary for different elements
F
in the same ring
R
.

Let
r\geq 3
be an odd integer. Let consider the extension field
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
and let
\tau:{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}
be the Frobenius automorphism, i.e.
a^{\tau}=a^{2}
. We can then extend
\tau
component-wise to the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)
of rational functions over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}
.
Clearly, the fixed field is
{{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)}^{\tau}={\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)
. Now, consider the automorphism
\theta:{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)
defined by
t\mapsto\frac{1}{t}
. Define

\sigma:=\theta\circ\tau=\tau\circ\theta.

Since the order of
\tau
is
r
, the order of
\theta
is
2
, and since
\tau
and
\theta
commute we get that the order of
\sigma
is
n:=2r
. Moreover, the restriction of
\sigma
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)
coincides with the restriction of
\theta
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s=t+\theta(t)=\frac{t^{2}+1}{t}
. Then

{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)}^{\theta}={\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s).

Therefore,

{{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)}^{\sigma}={\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s).

Proof.

The restriction of the automorphism
\theta
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-automorphism of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)
having order
2
. Therefore, if
{\mathbb{F}}={{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)}^{\theta}
, we have that
[{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t):{\mathbb{F}}]=2
. Now, observe that
s^{\theta}=s
and so
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}
, and by
[
60
, Theorem pp. 197]
, it follows that
[{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t):{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)]=2
, so we get
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)={\mathbb{F}}
.
∎

We also refer to
[
17
, Example 2.5]
,
[
16
, Example 6.5]
and
[
19
]
for further examples of this type of automorphism, within the context of skew polynomials defined over function fields.

Lemma
3.1
proves that
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)/{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)
is a Galois cyclic extension with Galois group
\Gal({\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)/{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s))=\langle\sigma\rangle
. So, we work in the skew polynomial ring
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma]
. By the considerations just made, we get that
Z\left({\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma]\right)={\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)[x^{n}]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The polynomial
f(x)=x^{2}+\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}+t+1}
is irreducible in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma]
.

Proof.

Assume
f(x)
is reducible, i.e.
f(x)=(x+b(t))(x+a(t))
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma]
, with
a(t),b(t)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)
. It follows that
b(t)=a(t)^{\sigma}
and

a(t)^{\sigma}a(t)=\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}+t+1}.

(5)

Let
a(t)=\frac{a_{1}(t)}{a_{2}(t)}
, with
a_{1}(t),a_{2}(t)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}[t]
having no common factor and with
\deg(a_{1}(t))=d_{1}
and
\deg(a_{2}(t))=d_{2}
. Hence, by (
5
), we get that

\frac{a_{1}(t)^{\sigma}a_{1}(t)}{a_{2}(t)^{\sigma}a_{2}(t)}=\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}+t+1}

that in turns implies

\frac{t^{d_{2}}t^{d_{1}}a_{1}(t)^{\sigma}a_{1}(t)}{t^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}a_{2}(t)^{\sigma}a_{2}(t)}=\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}+t+1}

As a consequence, we have that

(t^{2}+t+1)t^{d_{2}}t^{d_{1}}a_{1}(t)^{\sigma}a_{1}(t)=(t^{2}+1)t^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}a_{2}(t)^{\sigma}a_{2}(t)

(6)

Since
\deg(a_{1}(t))=d_{1}
and
\deg(a_{2}(t))=d_{2}
, we also have that
t^{d_{1}}a_{1}(t)^{\sigma}
and
t^{d_{2}}a_{2}(t)^{\sigma}
are polynomials in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}[t]
. Since
t^{2}+t+1
is irreducible in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}[t]
(
r
is odd) and coprime with
t^{2}+1
and
t^{d_{1}}
, we get that it divides
t^{d_{2}}a_{2}(t)^{\sigma}
or
a_{2}(t)
. Note that
t^{2}+t+1=t^{2}(t^{2}+t+1)^{\sigma}
and so
t^{2}+t+1
divides
a_{2}(t)
. Therefore,
a_{2}(t)=a_{3}(t)(t^{2}+t+1)
. Then substituting this expression of
a_{2}(t)
in (
6
), we get

(t^{2}+t+1)t^{d_{2}}t^{d_{1}}a_{1}(t)^{\sigma}a_{1}(t)=(t^{2}+1)t^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}-2}a_{3}(t)^{\sigma}t^{2}(t^{2}+t+1)^{\sigma}a_{3}(t)(t^{2}+t+1),

hence

t^{d_{2}}t^{d_{1}}a_{1}(t)^{\sigma}a_{1}(t)=(t^{2}+1)t^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}-2}a_{3}(t)^{\sigma}a_{3}(t)(t^{2}+t+1)

(7)

The same argument we have followed to derive from (
6
) that
t^{2}+t+1
divides
a_{2}(t)
, allows us to derive from (
7
) that
t^{2}+t+1
divides
a_{1}(t)
. Hence
a_{1}(t)
and
a_{2}(t)
have a common factor, a contradiction. Therefore such

cannot exist, and so
f(x)
is irreducible.
∎

Now, let

F(y)=y+\left(\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}+t+1}\right)^{r}.

It can be easily checked that

\left(\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}+t+1}\right)^{\sigma}=\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}+t+1},

which implies that
F(x^{n})\in Z({\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma])
. Moreover,
f(x)\mid_{r}F(x^{n})
; indeed

F(x^{n})=\left(x^{n-2}+\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}+t+1}x^{n-4}+\left(\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}+t+1}\right)^{2}x^{n-6}+\cdots+\left(\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}+t+1}\right)^{r-2}x^{2}+\left(\frac{t^{2}+1}{t^{2}+t+1}\right)^{r-1}\right)f(x),

which in turn implies that
F(x^{n})
is the bound of
f(x)
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma]
.

In this case,
\deg(f)=2
and
\deg(F(y))=1
, and

factorises into a product of

irreducibles of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}[x;\sigma]
, which implies that

\frac{R}{RF(x^{n})}\simeq M_{r}(\mathcal{E}(f)),

where
\mathcal{E}(f)
is a central division algebra over
E_{F}\simeq\frac{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)[y]}{(F(y))}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)
having degree
\ell_{F}=n/m=2
.

In the same setting, now let
g(x)=x^{2}+\frac{1}{t}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma]
and
G(y)=y^{2}+\frac{t^{2r}+1}{t^{r}}y+1
. Since

\left(\frac{t^{2r}+1}{t^{r}}\right)^{\sigma}=\frac{\frac{1}{t^{2r}}+1}{\frac{1}{t^{r}}}=\frac{t^{2r}+1}{\frac{t^{2r}}{t^{r}}}=\frac{t^{2r}+1}{t^{r}},

it follows that
G(x^{2r})\in Z({\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma])={\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)[x^{2r}]
. We are going to see that
G(x^{2r})
is the bound of
g(x)
, and
G(y)
is irreducible as a polynomial in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)[y]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The polynomial
g(x)
right and left divides
G(x^{2r})
.

Proof.

The restriction of
\sigma
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)
is
\theta
, so
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)[x;\theta]\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma]
. Moreover
\theta^{2}
is the identity, so
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)[x^{2}]
is a commutative subring of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma]
. Observe that
g(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)[x^{2}]
and
Z({\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma])={\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)[x^{2r}]\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)[x^{2}]
, hence
G(x^{2r})=(x^{2})^{2r}+\frac{t^{2r}+1}{t^{r}}(x^{2})^{r}+1\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)[x^{2}]
. Since

\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)^{2r}+\frac{t^{2r}+1}{t^{r}}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)^{r}+1=\frac{1+t^{2r}+1+t^{2r}}{t^{2r}}=0,

it follows that
g(x)
divides
G(x^{2r})
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)[x^{2}]
which implies that
g(x)
right and left divides
G(x^{2r})
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma]
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\ell
be an odd non negative integer. Let
r(t)=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}a_{i}t^{2i}}{t^{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t)
such that
a_{i}=a_{\ell-i}
for each
0\leq i\leq\ell
. Then
r(t)
is a polynomial in
s=\frac{t^{2}+1}{t}
with degree
\ell
if
a_{0}=a_{\ell}\neq 0
, or degree less than
\ell
if
a_{0}=a_{\ell}=0
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
\ell
. If
\ell=1
, then
r(t)=\frac{t^{2}+1}{t}=s
or
r(t)=0
, as stated. Assume
\ell>1
and let
r(t)=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}a_{i}t^{2i}}{t^{\ell}}
with
a_{i}=a_{\ell-i}
for each
0\leq i\leq\ell
. If
a_{0}=a_{\ell}=0
,

r(t)=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}a_{i}t^{2i}}{t^{\ell}}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}a_{i}t^{2i}}{t^{\ell}}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}a_{i}t^{2i-2}}{t^{\ell-2}}=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-2}b_{j}t^{2j}}{t^{\ell-2}}

with
b_{j}=a_{j+1}
. Since
b_{\ell-2-j}=a_{\ell-2-j+1}=a_{j+1}=b_{j}
, by induction
r(t)
is a polynomial is
s
of degree less than or equal to
\ell-2<\ell
. If
a_{0}=a_{\ell}=1
, then

r(t)+s^{\ell}=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}a_{i}t^{2i}}{t^{\ell}}+\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{i}t^{2i}}{t^{\ell}}=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}(a_{i}+\binom{\ell}{i})t^{2i}}{t^{\ell}}.

Let
a^{\prime}_{i}=a_{i}+\binom{\ell}{i}
. It follows that
a^{\prime}_{i}=a^{\prime}_{\ell-i}
and
a^{\prime}_{0}=a_{0}+\binom{\ell}{0}=1+1=0
. As we argued before,
r(t)+s^{\ell}
is a polynomial in
s
of degree bounded by
\ell-2
, hence
r(t)
is a polynomial of degree
\ell
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

The polynomial
G(y)=y^{2}+\frac{t^{2r}+1}{t^{r}}y+1\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)[y]
is irreducible.

Proof.

As a consequence of Lemma
3.4
,
G(y)=y^{2}+p(s)y+1
where
p(s)
is a polynomial in
s
of degree
r
. The only possible root of
G(y)
is, therefore,
1
, which is clearly not a root. Hence
G(y)
is irreducible.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
g(x)=x^{2}+\frac{1}{t}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma]
and
G(y)=y^{2}+\frac{t^{2r}+1}{t^{r}}y+1\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)[y]
. Then

is irreducible and its bound is

.

Proof.

By Lemmas
3.3
and
3.5
,
G(x^{2r})
is the bound of

. Since
Z({\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma])={\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)[x^{2r}]
and
[{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t):{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(s)]=2r
. As observed in
[

, Remark 2.10]
,
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}(t)[x;\sigma]
has finite rank over its centre, and it is easy to check that this rank is

is irreducible by

∎

Hence we have

do truly depend on the polynomial, rather than on the ring

3.2
Calculation and properties of the bound

Some constructions in the literature, and some of the new generalisations that we will prove later in this paper, rely on relating properties of an irreducible element
f
to properties of its bound
F(x^{n})
. To that end, we need to recall the relations which are known only for the case
\ell_{F}=1
, and extending them to deal with the case
\ell_{F}>1
.

Consider
R={\mathbb{L}}[x;\sigma]
and an element
f
having degree
h
. Define the semilinear map
\phi_{f}:R/Rf\rightarrow R/Rf
on the
{\mathbb{L}}
-vector space
R/RF
as
\phi_{f}(v)=xv\ \hbox{{\rm mod}\,}_{r}f,
for each
v\in R/Rf
.
The map
\phi_{f}
is
{\mathbb{L}}
-semilinear with companion automorphism
\sigma
, as

\phi_{f}(av)=x(av)\ \hbox{{\rm mod}\,}_{r}f=a^{\sigma}(xv\ \hbox{{\rm mod}\,}_{r}f)=a^{\sigma}\phi_{f}(v)

for all
a\in{\mathbb{L}}
. By choosing the
{\mathbb{L}}
-basis
\{1,x,\ldots,x^{h-1}\}
for
R/Rf
, and writing the components of the elements of
R/Rf
as column vectors, we see that
\phi_{f}=C_{f}\circ\sigma
, where
\sigma
acts entry-wise on vectors, and
C_{f}
denotes the companion matrix of the polynomial
f
. More precisely, if
v=\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}v_{i}x^{i}\in R/Rf
,

\phi_{f}(v)=C_{f}\cdot v^{\sigma}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&-f_{0}\\
1&0&\cdots&0&-f_{1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\cdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&-f_{h-1}\end{pmatrix}(v_{0}^{\sigma},v_{1}^{\sigma},\cdots,v_{h-1}^{\sigma})^{\top}.

Now the map
\phi_{f}^{n}
defines an
{\mathbb{L}}
-linear map on
R/Rf
. Indeed, it corresponds to
v\mapsto x^{n}v\ \hbox{{\rm mod}\,}_{r}f
. Denoting its matrix with respect to the same basis by
A_{f}
, we have that

A_{f}=C_{f}C_{f}^{\sigma}\cdots C_{f}^{\sigma^{n-1}},

where again
\sigma
acts entry-wise on matrices.

Theorem 3.7

(see
[
55
, Theorem 3]
)
.

Let

be a monic element of

with non zero constant coefficient. Then the bound of

is equal to

, where

is the minimal polynomial of
\phi_{f}^{n}
seen as
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear map.

Next we investigate the relation between the characteristic polynomial of
A_{f}
and the bound of

. First, we recall that if
{\mathbb{L}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
and

, in

has its coefficients in

. Replacing in the proof

and

and an its cyclic Galois extension

The characteristic polynomial of

has coefficients in

For a monic and irreducible monic polynomial

has maximum degree

Let

It is an easy consequence of

In the case

Theorem 3.10
.

Let

be a monic irreducible polynomial of degree

with nonzero constant coefficient, and let

, with

. The following hold:

i)

the minimal polynomial of
\phi_{f}^{n}

{\mathbb{K}}
-linear map is equal to the minimal polynomial of
\phi_{f}^{n}

{\mathbb{L}}
-linear map;

is equal to

, where

Proof.

Let

and

-linear map and
{\mathbb{L}}

and

. Furthermore, if the rank of
a(x)
is equal to
m-k
, then
\deg(a(x))=sk\ell
and

\frac{\mathrm{N}_{{\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{K}}}(a_{0})}{\mathrm{N}_{{\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{K}}}(a_{sk\ell})}=(-1)^{sk\ell(n-1)}F_{0}^{k\ell},

where
F_{0}
is the constant coefficient of
F(x^{n})
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
3.13
, we have that

\rk(a(x))=m-\frac{1}{s\ell}\deg(\mathrm{gcrd}(a(x),F(x^{n})))\geq m-\frac{1}{s\ell}\deg(a(x))\geq m-k.

If
\rk(a(x))=m-k
, then
a(x)
must have degree
sk\ell
and it is a divisor of
F(x^{n})
. So
a_{sk\ell}^{-1}a(x)
is a monic divisor of
F(x^{n})
, and so by part b) of Theorem
3.12
, we have

\mathrm{N}_{{\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{K}}}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a_{sk\ell}}\right)=(-1)^{sk\ell(n-1)}F_{0}^{k\ell},

and so the claim is proved.
∎

The above theorem plays a crucial role in the construction of division algebras and MRD codes that we show in Section
5
. This extends
[
55
, Theorem 5]
proved for the finite field case and
[
58
, Theorem 10]
proved for infinite fields in the case
n=m
. See also
[
48
]
for this property, derived using the norm of a skew polynomial.

Remark 3.16
.

Choosing
{\mathbb{L}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
,
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
s=1
and
F(y)=y-1
, the above statement translates to the fact that a linearized polynomial

g=g_{0}x+g_{1}x^{\sigma}+\ldots+g_{k-1}x^{\sigma^{k-1}}+x^{\sigma^{k}}

has all of its roots in
{\mathbb{L}}
only if
\mathrm{N}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(g_{0})=(-1)^{kn}
.

4
Division Algebras and MRD codes

In this section we recall the necessary definitions of division algebras/semifields, and rank-metric codes.

4.1
Division Algebras and semifields

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a field and
{\mathbb{A}}
be a vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}
. We say that
{\mathbb{A}}
is a (non necessarily associative) algebra over
{\mathbb{F}}
(or
{\mathbb{F}}
-algebra) if there exists an
{\mathbb{F}}
-bilinear map (called multiplication)

\star:{\mathbb{A}}\times{\mathbb{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{A}},\ \ \ (a,b)\mapsto a\star b.

We say that an
{\mathbb{F}}
-algebra
{\mathbb{A}}
is unital, if there exists a multiplicative identity
1\in{\mathbb{A}}
such that
1\star a=a\star 1=a,

for all
a\in{\mathbb{A}}
. For any
a\in{\mathbb{A}}
, left multiplication and right multiplication by
a
define the endomorphisms
L_{a},R_{a}\in\End_{{\mathbb{F}}}({\mathbb{A}})
as:

L_{a}:b\longmapsto a\star b,\ \ \ R_{a}:b\longmapsto b\star a,

for each
b\in{\mathbb{A}}
. We say that
{\mathbb{A}}
is a division algebra if for all nonzero
a\in{\mathbb{A}}
, the maps
L_{a}
and
R_{a}
are invertible maps. It is well-known, that when
{\mathbb{A}}
is a finite dimensional algebra over
{\mathbb{F}}
, then
{\mathbb{A}}
is division algebra if and only if there are no zero divisors. (Unital) division algebras with a finite number of elements are also known as (
semifields
)
presemifields
.

We use
isotopy
as the notion of equivalence between algebras.
Two division algebras
({\mathbb{A}},+,\star)
and
({\mathbb{A}}^{\prime},+^{\prime},\star^{\prime})
over the same field
{\mathbb{F}}
are said to be
isotopic
if there exist invertible additive maps
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}
from
{\mathbb{A}}
to
{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}
such that

h_{1}(a\star b)=h_{2}(a)\star^{\prime}h_{3}(b),

for all
a,b\in{\mathbb{A}}
. Clearly, when
h_{1}=h_{2}=h_{3}
, then
({\mathbb{A}},+,\star)
and
({\mathbb{A}}^{\prime},+^{\prime},\star^{\prime})
are isomorphic as algebras. The concept of isotopy of algebras was introduced by Albert in
[
1
]
, as a more general notion of isomorphism. In his paper, he assumed that
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}
are also
{\mathbb{F}}
-linear, so in this case, we also say that
{\mathbb{A}}
and
{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}
are
{\mathbb{F}}
-
isotopic
.

Note that properties such as multiplicative identity or commutativity are not preserved under isotopy. It is well-known that all division algebras are isotopic to a unital division algebra.

The
left-
,
middle-
, and
right-
nucleus of an algebra
({\mathbb{A}},+,\star)
are three subsets of
{\mathbb{A}}
defined as follows

\displaystyle{{\mathbb{N}}}_{l}({\mathbb{A}})

\displaystyle:=\{a\in{\mathbb{A}}\colon a\star(b\star c)=(a\star b)\star c,\mbox{ for all }b,c\in{\mathbb{A}}\},

\displaystyle{{\mathbb{N}}}_{m}({\mathbb{A}})

\displaystyle:=\{b\in{\mathbb{A}}\colon a\star(b\star c)=(a\star b)\star c,\mbox{ for all }a,c\in{\mathbb{A}}\},

\displaystyle{{\mathbb{N}}}_{r}({\mathbb{A}})

\displaystyle:=\{c\in{\mathbb{A}}\colon a\star(b\star c)=(a\star b)\star c,\mbox{ for all }a,b\in{\mathbb{A}}\}.

The
nucleus

{\mathbb{N}}({\mathbb{A}})
of
{\mathbb{A}}
is the intersection of these three sets, and the
centre

Z({\mathbb{A}})
is defined as

Z({\mathbb{A}}):=\{a\in{\mathbb{N}}({\mathbb{A}})\colon a\star b=b\star a\mbox{ for all }b\in{\mathbb{A}}\}.

Each of the nuclei is an associative subalgebra of
{\mathbb{A}}
. The centre is an associative, commutative subalgebra of
{\mathbb{A}}
, containing the field
{\mathbb{F}}
when
{\mathbb{A}}
is unital, since we can identify
{\mathbb{F}}1_{A}\subseteq Z({\mathbb{A}})
.
Moreover, if
{\mathbb{A}}
is unital and is finite-dimensional over
{\mathbb{F}}
, each of the nuclei is an associative division algebra.

In the case of semifields, each of nuclei and the centre are finite fields. In the rest of the paper by algebra over
{\mathbb{F}}
or
{\mathbb{F}}
-algebra, we always mean a finite-dimensional algebra over
{\mathbb{F}}
.

4.2
Rank-metric codes

Let
{\mathbb{D}}
be a division ring. The
rank
of a matrix
A\in M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
is the dimension of the right
{\mathbb{D}}
-vector space generated by the columns of
A
and it is still denoted by
\rk(A)
. Note that, this also coincide with the dimension of the left
{\mathbb{D}}
-vector space generated by the rows of
A
. Using this notion of rank, a
rank-metric code
in
M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
is a subset of
M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
, endowed with the rank-distance function

d(A,B)=\rk(A-B).

The minimum distance
d
of a code
\C\subseteq M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
is
d=\min\{\rk(A-B)\colon A,B\in\C,A\neq B\}
. If
{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}
is a subfield of
{\mathbb{D}}
such a code is said to be
{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}
-linear
if it is closed under addition and
{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}
-multiplication.

In the case where
{\mathbb{D}}
is a field, Delsarte
[
10
]
proved a Singleton-like bound relating the dimension of a code to its minimum distance.
This was extended also to the division ring case, see e.g.
[
57
, Theorem 2.2.2]
. We state the most general version here.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\C\subseteq M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
be a
{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}
-linear rank-metric code. Assume that the dimension of
{\mathbb{D}}
as
{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}
-vector space
[{\mathbb{D}}:{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}]
is finite. Then

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}}(\C)\leq n(n-d+1)[{\mathbb{D}}:{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}].

(9)

A code attaining the above bound is said to be a
Maximum Rank Distance
code, or
MRD code
.

Delsarte
[
10
]
showed that
{\mathbb{F}}
-linear MRD codes in
M_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
can be constructed for any finite field
{\mathbb{F}}
and every
n
and
d
, with
d\leq n
(and in fact proved a more general version for rectangular matrices, but we will not deal with this case in this paper). These codes were independently rediscovered by Gabidulin
[
13
]
in the equivalent formulation as codes in
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}
, where
{\mathbb{L}}
is a finite extension field of
{\mathbb{F}}
and have come to be known as
Gabidulin codes
.

For an automorphism or anti-automorphism
\rho
of
{\mathbb{D}}
and a matrix
A\in M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
,
A^{\rho}
is the matrix obtained from
A
by applying
\rho
to all
its entries. The code equivalence for rank-metric codes in
M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
is defined by means of isometries of the whole space. More precisely, recall that a (rank-metric) isometry of
M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
is a bijection
\phi:M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})\rightarrow M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
that preserves the rank distance, i.e.
\rk(\phi(A)-\phi(B))=\rk(A-B)
, for any
A,B\in M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
. In
[
20
]
and
[
61
]
, it is proved that if
\phi
is an isometry of
M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
, then
\phi
is of the form
\phi(A)=XA^{\rho}Y+Z
, for all
A\in M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
, where
X,Y\in\hbox{{\rm GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
,
Z\in M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
and
\rho
is an automorphism of
{\mathbb{D}}
or

\phi
is of the form
\phi(A)=X(A^{\top})^{\rho}Y+Z
, for all
A\in M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
, where
X,Y\in\hbox{{\rm GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
,
Z\in M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
and
\rho
is an anti-automorphism of
{\mathbb{D}}
. In this paper, we do not take into consideration the latter form for
\phi
. So, we say that two rank-metric codes
\C,\C^{\prime}\subseteq M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
are equivalent if

\C^{\prime}=X\C^{\rho}Y+Z=\{XA^{\rho}Y+Z\colon A\in\C\},

(10)

for some
X,Y\in\hbox{{\rm GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{D}}),Z\in M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
and
\rho
an automorphism of
{\mathbb{D}}
. Note that we can assume
Z=0
, when the codes are additive (i.e.
\C,\C^{\prime}
are additive subgroups of
M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
). When
\rho
is the identity, we also say that
\C
and
\C^{\prime}
are
linearly equivalent
.

4.3
Division algebras as MRD codes with
d=n

Given a (not necessarily associative) division algebra
{\mathbb{A}}
, we can form a rank-metric code by taking all the endomorphisms
L_{a}
as defined in Section
4.1
to form the
spread set

\C({\mathbb{A}}):=\{L_{a}:a\in{\mathbb{A}}\}\subset\End({\mathbb{A}}).

This set is additively closed. As mentioned, each of these maps are invertible for
a\neq 0
. The algebra
{\mathbb{A}}
is a left-vector space over its right nucleus, and the maps maps
L_{a}
can be viewed as elements of
\End_{{\mathbb{N}}_{r}({\mathbb{A}})}({\mathbb{A}})
. Letting
n
denote the dimension of
{\mathbb{A}}
over
{\mathbb{N}}_{r}({\mathbb{A}})
, we can, with slight abuse of notation, view the spread set as a rank-metric code

\C({\mathbb{A}})\subset M_{n}({\mathbb{N}}_{r}({\mathbb{A}})).

Then every nonzero element of
\C({\mathbb{A}})
has rank
n
, implying that
\C({\mathbb{A}})
is an MRD code with minimum distance
n
. If
{\mathbb{A}}
is unital, then
\C({\mathbb{A}})
will contain the identity matrix.

Conversely, given an additively closed MRD code in
M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
with minimum distance
n
, we can define an algebra multiplication by choosing an invertible additive map
\phi:{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\mapsto\C
and defining

a\star b:=\phi(a)b.

The algebra
{\mathbb{A}}=({\mathbb{D}}^{n},\star)
is then a (not necessarily associative nor unital) division algebra with right nucleus containing
{\mathbb{D}}
.

The choice of the map
\phi
will later prove important in our calculations.

4.4
Nuclei, Idealisers, and Centralisers

We recall some important facts regarding division algebras and rank-metric codes, which will later allow us to compute the relevant parameters of our new constructions, and prove inequivalence to the known constructions.

The following definitions were developed in
[
35
]
and
[
32
]
. These generalise the notion of the nuclei of a division algebra.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\C
be a rank-metric code of
M_{t}({\mathbb{D}})
, with
{\mathbb{D}}
a division ring.
The
left idealiser

\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(\C)
and the
right idealiser

\mathcal{I}_{r}(\C)
are defined as

\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(\C)=\{A\in M_{t}({\mathbb{D}})\colon A\C\subseteq\C\}

and

\mathcal{I}_{r}(\C)=\{B\in M_{t}({\mathbb{D}})\colon\C B\subseteq\C\},

respectively.

The
centraliser

Cen(\C)
is defined as

Cen(\C)=\{A\in M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})\colon AX=XA\mbox{
for every }X\in\C\}.

The
centre

Z(\C)
of
\C
is defined as the intersection of the left idealiser and the centraliser.

Z(\C)=\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(\C)\cap C(\C).

These objects are related to the nuclei of a division algebra as follows.

Proposition 4.3

(see
[
55
, Proposition 5]
and
[
58
, Theorem 27]
)
.

Let
{\mathbb{A}}
be a unital division algebras over
{\mathbb{F}}
,
\C=\C({\mathbb{A}})\subseteq\End_{{\mathbb{F}}}({\mathbb{A}})
be the spread set of
{\mathbb{A}}
and
\C^{op}=\C({\mathbb{A}}^{op})
be the spread set of
{\mathbb{A}}^{op}
. Then the following
{\mathbb{F}}
-algebra isomorphisms hold:

{\mathbb{N}}_{l}({\mathbb{A}})\cong\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(\C),\ \ \ {\mathbb{N}}_{m}({\mathbb{A}})\cong\mathcal{I}_{r}(\C),\ \ \ {\mathbb{N}}_{r}({\mathbb{A}})\cong C(\C^{op}),\ \mbox{ and }\ Z({\mathbb{A}})\cong Z(\C),

These objects can be seen as invariants of codes. For the centre and centraliser to be invariants, we need to assume that the identity is contained in each code. The proof from
[
55
, Proposition 4]
holds verbatim in this setting.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose
\C
and
\C^{\prime}
are two equivalent codes in
M_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
. Assuming that
\C^{\prime}=A\C^{\rho^{-1}}B
, for some
A,B\in\hbox{{\rm GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{D}})
, and
\rho\in\Aut({\mathbb{D}})
, we have

\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(\C)=(A^{-1}\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(\C^{\prime})A)^{\rho},\mbox{ and }\mathcal{I}_{r}(\C)=(B\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(\C^{\prime})B^{-1})^{\rho}.

Furthermore, if both
\C
and
\C^{\prime}
contain the identity, then

C(\C)=(A^{-1}C(\C^{\prime})A)^{\rho},\mbox{ and }Z(\C)=(A^{-1}Z(\C^{\prime})A)^{\rho}.

In particular, if
{\mathbb{D}}
is a finite field, then

|\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(\C)|=|\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(\C^{\prime})|\mbox{ and}\quad|\mathcal{I}_{r}(\C)|=|\mathcal{I}_{r}(\C^{\prime})|

and if both
\C
and
\C^{\prime}
contain the identity, then

\quad|C(\C)|=|C(\C^{\prime})|\mbox{ and}\quad|Z(\C)|=|Z(\C^{\prime})|.

Definition 4.5
.

Suppose
{\mathbb{F}}
is a finite field. The
nuclear parameters
of a code
\C\subset M_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
containing the identity are the tuple

(|\C|,|\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(\C)|,|\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(\C)|,|C(\C)|,|Z(\C)|).

If a code
\C
contains an invertible element but does not contain the identity, we define its nuclear parameters to be the nuclear parameters of any code
\C^{\prime}
which contains the identity and is equivalent to
\C
.

4.5
Known constructions and structural results

In this section we recall the known constructions of division algebras and MRD codes from skew polynomial rings, and their structural properties and parameters. Subsequently, we will improve these results using the theory and examples developed in Sections
3.1
and
3.2
.

4.5.1
Petit Algebras

Let
f
be an irreducible element of
R
of degree
h
. Consider the right
R
-module
R/Rf
. Hence the elements of
R/Rf
can be identified with the elements of
R
having degree at most
h-1
. Define a unital division algebra whose elements are the elements of
R/Rf
, with multiplication defined by

a\circ b:=ab\ \mathrm{mod}_{r}\ f.

This is well defined, and non-zero for
a,b\neq 0
, due to the fact that
R
is a right-Euclidean domain. This implies that
(R/Rf,\circ)
is a unital division algebras, known as a
Petit algebra
, which we also denote by
S_{f}
. For instance, the Petit Algebra
(R/Rf,\circ)
, with
f(x)=x^{2}-i\in\mathbb{C}[x;\overline{\phantom{a}}]
, where
\overline{\phantom{a}}
is the complex conjugation, is one of the first constructions of a nonassociative division algebra
[
11
]
. Clearly, we have
(R/Rf,\circ)=(R/R{\alpha f},\circ)
, for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{L}}^{*}
, so we can assume that
f
is monic. The algebra
(R/Rf,\circ)
is associative if and only if
f\in Z(R)
. In
[
45
]
, the nuclei and the centre of
(R/Rf,\circ)
are determined as follows. Recall the definition of the eigenring
\mathcal{E}(f)
from Definition
2.5
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
f\in R
be an irreducible polynomial of degree
h\geq 2
, with
f\notin Z(R)
. Consider the Petit algebra
S_{f}
. Then

{\mathbb{N}}_{l}(S_{f})={\mathbb{L}},\ \ \ {\mathbb{N}}_{m}(S_{f})={\mathbb{L}},\ \ \ {\mathbb{N}}_{r}(S_{f})=\mathcal{E}(f),\ \mbox{ and }\ Z(S_{f})={\mathbb{K}}.

When
\sigma\neq id
and
h=1
, the equality between the eigenring of
f
and
{\mathbb{N}}_{r}(S_{f})
does not hold.
Indeed,
R/Rf
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{L}}
, and so
{\mathbb{N}}_{r}(S_{f})={\mathbb{L}}
. In this case
\mathcal{E}(f)=\{b\in{\mathbb{L}}\colon\sigma(b)=b\}={{\mathbb{L}}}^{\sigma}
.

We can describe a
canonical
isomorphism giving (
4
) as follows. Let
F(y)\in{\mathbb{K}}[y]
be a monic irreducible polynomial having degree
s\geq 2
and let
m
be the number of irreducible factors of
F(x^{n})
in
R
. Let
f(x)\in R
be a monic irreducible divisor of
F(x^{n})
in
R
. Note that
f^{*}(x)=F(x^{n})
and
\deg(f)=\ell_{F}s
, where
\ell_{F}=n/m
. Since
\mathrm{N}_{r}(S_{f})=\mathcal{E}(f)
,
R/Rf
can be regarded as a right
\mathcal{E}(f)
-vector space. Moreover,

n\ell_{F}=\dim_{{\mathbb{K}}}(R/Rf)=[R/Rf:{\mathbb{N}}_{r}(S_{f})][{\mathbb{N}}_{r}(S_{f}):{\mathbb{K}}]=[R/Rf:{\mathbb{N}}_{r}(S_{f})]sn\ell_{F}^{2},

by Theorem
2.6
, so
R/Rf
having dimension
m
over
\mathcal{E}(f)
. Now, for any
a(x)\in R_{F}
, we have that the left multiplication by
a(x)
in
R/Rf
is such that

\begin{array}[]{rlr}L_{a(x)}(b(x))\alpha&=(a(x)b(x)\ \hbox{{\rm mod}\,}_{r}f(x))\alpha\ \hbox{{\rm mod}\,}_{r}f(x)&\\
&=(a(x)b(x)-u(x)f(x))\alpha\ \hbox{{\rm mod}\,}_{r}f(x)&\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{(for some }u(x)\in R)\\
&=a(x)b(x)\alpha-u(x)f(x)\alpha\ \hbox{{\rm mod}\,}_{r}f(x)&\\
&=a(x)b(x)\alpha\ \hbox{{\rm mod}\,}_{r}f(x)&\mbox{since }\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(f)\\
&=a(x)(b(x)\alpha)\\
&=L_{a(x)}(b(x)\alpha),\end{array}

for any
b(x)\in R/Rf
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{E}(f)
. This means that
L_{a(x)}\in\End_{\mathcal{E}(f)}(R/Rf)
, or in other words, the left multiplications by the elements of
R/RF(x^{n})
on the elements of
R/Rf
are
\mathcal{E}(f)
-linear. So, we can consider the following map

\begin{array}[]{lcrc}L:&\frac{R}{RF(x^{n})}&\longrightarrow&\End_{\mathcal{E}(f)}(R/Rf)\\
&a(x)+RF(x^{n})&\longmapsto&L_{a}:b(x)+Rf\in\frac{R}{Rf}\mapsto a(x)b(x)+Rf\in\frac{R}{Rf}\end{array}

(11)

It is easy to check that
L
is well defined and comparing the dimension of
R_{F}
and
\End_{\mathcal{E}(f)}(R/Rf)
, we get that
L
establishes the following
\mathcal{E}(f)
-isomorphisms:

\frac{R}{RF(x^{n})}\cong\End_{\mathcal{E}(f)}(R/Rf)\cong\End_{\mathcal{E}(f)}(\mathcal{E}(f)^{m})\cong M_{m}(\mathcal{E}(f)),

where the last two isomorphisms are defined up to a choice of an
\mathcal{E}(f)
-basis of
R/Rf
, cf.
[
55
, p. 13]
and
[
58
, p. 7]
. From now on we will abuse notation and omit the map
L
, and identify
a
and
L_{a}
where convenient.

Let
\C
be a
{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}
-linear subset of
R/RF(x^{n})\cong M_{m}(\mathcal{E}(f))
, with
{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}\leq{\mathbb{K}}
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}}(\C)=m[\mathcal{E}(f):{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}]
, i.e.
\dim_{{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}}(\C)=m\ell^{2}s[{\mathbb{K}}:{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}]
, with
\ell=\ell_{F}=n/m
. Assume that
\C
is an MRD code in
M_{m}(\mathcal{E}(f))
, i.e.
d(\C)=m
. By (i) of Proposition
2.7
, we know that
\deg(f)=s\ell
and so
R/Rf
is a
{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}
-vector space having dimension
m\ell^{2}s[{\mathbb{K}}:{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}]=\dim_{{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}}(\C)
. This means that there exists a
{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}
-linear isomorphism
a\in R/Rf\rightarrow\phi(a)\in\C\subseteq\End_{\mathcal{E}(f)}(R/Rf)
, where we are using the isomorphism
\frac{R}{RF(x^{n})}\cong\End_{\mathcal{E}(f)}(R_{f})
, by (
11
). The following holds.

Lemma 4.7
.

In
R/Rf
, the operation defined as

a\star_{\phi}b:=\phi(a)(b),

(12)

for any
a,b\in R/Rf
defines a division algebras over
{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}
having dimension
m[\mathcal{E}(f):{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}]=m\ell^{2}s[{\mathbb{K}}:{\mathbb{K}}^{\prime}]
.

Proof.

The assertion easily follows by the the fact that every non zero element of
\C
is invertible.
∎

4.5.2
Previous constructions of MRD codes over finite fields

Let
R={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}[x;\sigma]
and
T(y):=y-1
. We recall the constructions of inequivalent linear MRD codes in
M_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\cong\frac{R}{RT(x^{n})}
:

I)

the first construction of linear MRD codes was provided by Delsarte in
[
10
]
and later rediscovered by Gabidulin in
[
13
]
and extended by Kshevetskiy and Gabidulin in
[
30
]
. The codes of this family are now known as
generalized Gabidulin codes
and are defined as follows:

\{a_{0}+a_{1}x+\ldots+a_{k-1}x^{k-1}\colon a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\}\subseteq\frac{R}{RT(x^{n})}\cong M_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})

A code belonging to this family as left and right idealisers isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.

II)

Later, the family of Gabidulin codes was extended by Sheekey to the family of
twisted Gabidulin codes
and then by Lunardon, Trombetti and Zhou in
[
36
]
. A further generalization, was constructed in
[
43
]
. Such codes are known as
Additive, generalized Twisted Gabidulin (AGTG) codes
and this family properly contains the codes constructed by Sheekey, and by Lunardon et al.. Precisely, an AGTG code is defined as

\{a_{0}+a_{1}x+\ldots+a_{k-1}x^{k-1}+\tau(a_{0})\eta x^{k}\colon a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\}\subseteq\frac{R}{RT(x^{n})}\cong M_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})

where
\tau\in\Aut({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}})
and
\eta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
is such that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{n}/q^{\prime}}(\eta)N_{q/q^{\prime}}((-1)^{kn})\neq 1
, with
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{\prime}}={{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}^{\tau}
. Such code is an MRD code in
M_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
linear over the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{\prime}}
.
An AGTG code has parameters

(p^{nke},p^{(ne,j)},p^{(ne,ke-j)},p^{e},p^{(e,j)}),

(13)

where
q=p^{e}
and
\tau(x)=x^{p^{j}}
.

III)

Other families of MRD codes are Trombetti-Zhou codes
[
59
]
which are related to the Hughes–Kleinfeld semifields. More precisely, let
n=2t\geq 4
. For a positive integer
k\leq n-1
, a
Trombetti-Zhou code
is defined as

\{a_{0}^{\prime}+a_{1}x+\ldots+a_{k-1}x^{k-1}+\gamma a_{0}^{\prime\prime}x^{k}\colon a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}},a_{0}^{\prime},a_{0}^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}\}\subseteq\frac{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}[x;\sigma]}{(x^{n}-1)}\cong M_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})

where
\gamma
is an element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
such that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{n}/q}(\gamma)
is not a square in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. A code of this family has parameters
(q^{nk},q^{n/2},q^{n/2},q,q)
.

IV)

In
[
8
]
, the authors introduce a new class of linear MRD codes:

\{a_{0}+a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{3}:a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\}\subseteq\frac{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}[x;\sigma]}{(x^{n}-1)}\cong M_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})

for
n=7
and
q
odd (cf. Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 3.4), and for
n=8
,
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
(cf. Theorem 3.5 and Corollary 3.6). Such codes have left and right idealisers isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.

V)

Other MRD codes are of the form

\{a_{0}+a_{1}f(x):a_{0},a_{1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\}\subseteq\frac{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}[x;\sigma]}{(x^{n}-1)}\cong M_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}),

where
f(x)
belongs to a special class of polynomial, called
scattered polynomials
. See
[
5
,
33
,
34
,
40
,
62
,
7
,
3
,
46
,
9
,
37
,
4
,
56
]
for the known construction. Such codes have their left idealiser isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.

Let

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
f
be a monic irreducible factor of

We use the same strategy of
[

.
First we compute
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(\C)

\eta=0

gax^{i}\ \hbox{{\rm mod}\,}F(x^{n})\in\C,

In this section, we extend two related constructions over finite fields to a new construction for both finite and infinite fields. In particular, the Hughes-Kleinfeld division algebras (semifields)

, Corollary 1]
is indeed correct.

Proposition 6.7
.

The family of semifields of the form
{\mathcal{D}}_{n,s}
contains new semifields for an infinite set of parameters.

Proof.

We proceed as in
[
55
]
by establishing cases where
{\mathcal{D}}_{n,s}
has parameters for which no semifields were previously known. We assume that
q
is an odd prime power,
s\geq 2
is even,
n=2t\geq 4
, and that
n\nmid s
.

Note that when
s>1
,
n\geq 4
, we have that no semifield of the form
{\mathcal{D}}_{n,s}
has any nucleus equal to its centre, and hence cannot coincide with any Pott-Zhou semifield. Furthermore, no
{\mathcal{D}}_{n,s}
can be two-dimensional over any nucleus, and so cannot coincide with any rank-two semifield.

It follows using the same argument of point 4 in the proof of Theorem
6.3
that the semifields defined by
{\mathcal{D}}_{n,s}
as in
Theorem

6.4
and those defined by
S_{n,s,1}(\eta,\rho,F)
as in
[
55
]
are not isotopic.

Next we verify that no
{\mathcal{D}}_{n,s}
can coincide with the construction of
[
15
]
. In this construction, the parameters (up to permutation) are
(p^{2m},p^{e/2},p^{e/2},p^{e},\cdot)
. Therefore we must have
q^{t}=q^{s/2}
, which (since
n=2t
) implies that
s=n
. So again requiring that
n\nmid s
ensures that
{\mathcal{D}}_{n,s}
cannot arise from this construction.

Finally we compare to the construction of
[
29
]
. For equality of parameters, we need
m=st
,
(a,b,st)=t
, and
(2a,st)=s
. Then we have that
t
divides
a
, and so
t
divides
s=(2a,st)
. But we are assuming that
n=2t
does not divide
s
, and so
s/t
is odd. Consider the
2
-adic valuation
v_{2}
. Then we have
v_{2}(s)=v_{2}(t)
. As
t
divides
a
we have
v_{2}(t)\leq v_{2}(a)
. But since
(2a,st)=s
, we have
v_{2}(s)=\mathrm{min}\{v_{2}(a)+1,2v_{2}(s)\}
. As
s
is even, we have
2v_{2}(s)>v_{2}(s)
, and so we get
v_{2}(t)=v_{2}(s)=v_{2}(a)+1
, a contradiction.
∎

The smallest new case then arises for
n=4,s=2
, with parameters
(q^{8},q^{2},q^{2},q^{2},q)
.

Remark 6.8
.

Note that it is possible (and indeed likely) that the family
{\mathcal{D}}_{n,s}
contains new semifields also in the cases where semifields with the same parameters are already known. However, as the isotopy problem for semifields with equal parameters can be very difficult, and as a complete answer to this question is not the aim of this paper, we leave it as an open problem.

Remark 6.9
.

Analogous to
[
54
, Remark 8]
, one could also construct further semifields and MRD codes from any pair of additive maps
\phi_{1}\phi_{2}:{\mathbb{L}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{L}}
satisfying

\mathrm{N}_{{\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{K}}}(\phi_{1}(a))\neq\mathrm{N}_{{\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{K}}}(\phi_{2}(a))(-1)^{sk\ell(n-1)}F_{0}^{k\ell},

for all
a\in{\mathbb{L}}
,
where
F_{0}
is the constant coefficient of
F(x^{n})
. The classification of such pairs of maps remains an open problem, particularly over infinite fields.

Evaluation of rural tourism development level using BERT-enhanced deep learning model and BP algorithm
Authors: Xiaohe Yuan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address the insufficient expressive capabilities of traditional methods in assessing the development level of rural tourism, this study explores the fusion application of the Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) and the Back Propagation (BP) algorithm to enhance the accuracy and comprehensiveness of rural tourism development assessment. Firstly, this study introduces the BERT deep learning model and its applications in natural language processing, alongside the role of the BP algorithm in pattern recognition and predictive analysis. Subsequently, a framework for assessing rural tourism development levels, integrating BERT and the BP algorithm, is proposed. This framework collects multidimensional rural tourism-related data and utilizes the BERT model for sentiment analysis and topic extraction from textual data. Empirical analysis of rural tourism development in a specific region validates the effectiveness of the proposed approach. Experimental results demonstrate: (1) The model achieves an accuracy of 84.33% and an F1 score of 85.33% on the publicly available Laptop dataset, with a processing time of 20 s, significantly outperforming other methods. Compared to traditional approaches, the proposed method accurately captures correlations between textual information and numerical data, thereby enhancing the credibility and accuracy of assessment results. (2) From the ablation study results, it is evident that removing any component from the model leads to performance degradation. Specifically, removing the Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (BiGRU) reduces accuracy and F1 scores to 78.21% and 76.33% on the Laptop dataset, and to 85.10% and 70.45% on the Tourist_F dataset. Removing Text Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) reduces accuracy and F1 scores to 79.34% and 77.56% on the Laptop dataset, and to 86.25% and 72.11% on the Tourist_F dataset. The most significant performance decline occurs upon removing BERT, with accuracy and F1 scores decreasing to 70.14% and 66.43% on the Laptop dataset, and to 78.45% and 60.33% on the Tourist_F dataset. These results indicate that BiGcRU, TextCNN, and BERT each contribute significantly to the model’s performance. This study provides substantial support for advancing sustainable development in rural tourism, offering significant practical and innovative value.Keywords: BERT-based deep learning model, BP algorithm, Rural tourism, Accuracy, F1 scoreSubject terms: Computational science, Computer science, Information technology

Introduction

Research background and motivations

In recent years, with the vigorous development of the tourism industry, rural tourism, as an essential form of tourism, has gradually garnered widespread attention1. Rural tourism, characterized by its unique natural landscapes, cultural charm, and heritage, has attracted a large number of tourists, becoming an effective means to promote rural economic growth and enhance farmers’ income2,3. In this context, how to scientifically and accurately assess and analyze the development level of rural tourism has become a pivotal topic in the field of tourism research4,5. The development level of rural tourism involves various factors, including but not limited to the natural environment, cultural resources, infrastructure, and management standards.

Conventional assessment methods often tend to be overly subjective and one-sided, making it challenging to comprehensively and objectively depict the true state and potential of rural tourism. This study introduces the Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) deep learning model along with the Back Propagation (BP) algorithm into the evaluation of tourism development. This offers new perspectives and approaches for assessing and analyzing the level of tourism development.

Research objectives

This study aims to explore the integration of the BERT model with the BP algorithm to enhance the assessment and analysis of rural tourism development levels. Specifically, the objective is to construct an effective evaluation model to comprehensively and objectively measure the development level of rural tourism. By leveraging BERT’s contextual understanding capabilities and the training optimization mechanism of the BP algorithm, this study seeks to improve the predictive accuracy of the model and provide policymakers with reliable strategies and directions for rural tourism development. Ultimately, through validation experiments, it aims to verify the effectiveness and feasibility of the proposed model, thereby providing a scientific basis for promoting the sustainable development of rural tourism.

This study addresses the challenge of insufficient feature representation capability in traditional methods for assessing rural tourism development levels by proposing an innovative approach that combines the BERT deep learning model with the BP algorithm. The new evaluation framework aims to more accurately capture the relationships between textual information and numerical data, thereby enhancing the credibility and accuracy of the assessment results. The novelty of this study lies in the integration of the BERT deep learning model with the BP algorithm to improve the precision and comprehensiveness of rural tourism development level assessments. While existing research has explored the applications of the BERT model in natural language processing and the role of the BP algorithm in pattern recognition and predictive analysis, this study’s application of these techniques to the field of rural tourism is novel. The motivation behind this study is to address the limitations of traditional methods in the feature representation capability for assessing rural tourism development levels. Rural tourism plays a crucial role in promoting local economic development and improving the quality of life for rural residents. However, traditional assessment methods are limited and cannot fully and objectively reflect the true development level of rural tourism. Therefore, this study aims to propose a more accurate and reliable evaluation model to provide a scientific basis for the sustainable development of rural tourism.

Research contribution

The contributions and novelty of this study are as follows: (1) Introduction of an Innovative Evaluation Framework Combining BERT and BP Algorithms: This study proposes an evaluation framework for rural tourism development levels that integrates the BERT deep learning model with the BP algorithm. It effectively utilizes BERT’s contextual understanding capabilities in natural language processing and the optimization strengths of the BP algorithm in pattern recognition. This innovative framework provides new technical means for assessing tourism development levels. (2) Multidimensional Data Collection and Processing: The framework collects relevant data on rural tourism from multiple dimensions and employs the BERT model for sentiment analysis and topic extraction from textual data. This comprehensive handling of complex data enhances both the accuracy and comprehensiveness of the evaluation results. (3) Efficient Performance Validation: Through empirical analysis, the model presented in this study demonstrates significantly better performance compared to other benchmark models on the publicly available Laptop dataset and the self-constructed Tourist_F dataset. The model excels in accuracy and F1 scores while effectively controlling processing time, reflecting its efficiency and feasibility in practical applications. (4) Improvement of Existing Sentiment Classification Model Performance: The dual-channel model Dual Channel Model Based on BERT (DCM-BERT) presented in this study combines deep semantic extraction and local feature extraction. By utilizing Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (BiGRU) and Text Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), the model extracts both deep and local features from text vectors, further enhancing sentiment classification performance. (5) Data Crawling and Processing for Practical Applications: This study outlines a specific data crawling process using the requests library to gather rich data resources from attraction reviews. It provides a detailed description of the data processing and model training procedures, offering a practical and actionable solution for real-world applications. (6) Extensive Experimental Data and Parameter Settings: The study conducts extensive experiments under various datasets and model parameter configurations, meticulously recording the accuracy, F1 scores, and processing times of each model. This provides a solid data foundation for evaluating model performance. (7) Promotion of Sustainable Development in Rural Tourism: The evaluation framework proposed in this study enables a more accurate identification of potential issues and advantages in rural tourism development, providing decision-makers with scientifically reliable evidence that contributes to promoting sustainable development in this sector. In summary, this study makes significant contributions across evaluation methods, technical approaches, experimental validation, and practical applications, showcasing its innovation and practical significance.

Literature review

Numerous researchers have delved into this matter. Chantrapornchai & Tunsakul6 proposed two methods for extracting specific information from full-text search engine results. Given review texts, they demonstrated how to establish models to extract desired entities such as names, locations, facilities, and relationship types to classify or summarize reviews. SpaCy and BERT were utilized to compare the performance of these tasks. Sellam et al.7 introduced MultiBERT, a set of 25 basic BERT checkpoints. This model was trained with similar hyperparameters to the original BERT model, as well as an additional set of 140 intermediate checkpoints captured during pre-training aimed at facilitating dynamic research learning.

In terms of optimizing the BERT model, Yenduri et al.8 developed a novel BERT-centric custom sentiment classification approach involving two main stages: preprocessing and tokenization, followed by classification. Initially, they collected raw tweets and underwent preprocessing involving stop-word removal, stemming, and space elimination. Post-preprocessing, semantically meaningful words were obtained, and during tokenization, relevant words were extracted from them for classification using the optimized BERT. Finally, a performance analysis was conducted, confirming the enhancement of this model. Sun et al.9 employed BERT for biomedical named entity recognition within a machine reading comprehension framework, casting it as a machine reading comprehension problem. They introduced well-designed queries to incorporate additional prior knowledge into the process, eliminating the need for decoding. Experimental validation on six datasets demonstrated the method’s effectiveness. Ansar et al.10 formulated a unique set of rules for key phrase extraction, resulting in an average sentence length reduction of 84% and a 50% decrease in text complexity. This approach exhibited improved performance and efficiency compared to other state-of-the-art methods. Tang et al.11 proposed a novel Decision Fusion Module (DFM) designed to address the issue of information loss during the feature aggregation/extraction process. This method involved intermediate decision-making at each layer, fusing the results with the original features to progressively gather and abstract more discriminative features. This approach significantly enhanced classification performance while maintaining minimal computational overhead. Furthermore, the DFM demonstrated its generalization capability in object detection and semantic segmentation tasks. Concurrently, adversarial attacks extended to the point cloud domain. Tang et al.12 investigated whether perturbations along the normal and tangential planes were less perceptible than gradient-aware perturbations, leading to the design of a new Normal and Tangent Attack (NTA) framework. NTA employed a hybrid directional perturbation scheme, adaptively selecting directions based on the curvature of the local shape around points. This significantly improved the effectiveness and stealthiness of adversarial attacks, showcasing superior performance over existing methods across multiple public datasets and representative networks.

Research model

BERT

The core of the BERT model lies in the pre-trained language model, which utilizes the Encoder structure within the Transformer. In comparison to traditional word embedding models, the key difference is that the pre-trained language model allows words to have different word embeddings in various contexts to address the issue of polysemy13,14. Moreover, the training corpus for the pre-trained language model is vast, enabling the word embeddings generated by the model to convey text semantics more comprehensively. The BERT model aims to obtain text representations rich in semantic information through the stacking of Encoder structures and training on large-scale unlabeled corpora15. After acquiring text representations, they can be applied to various downstream tasks such as text classification, question-answering systems, and sentiment analysis16–18. The structure of the BERT model can be divided into three layers: the input layer, the feature extraction layer, and the output layer19. Each input position is composed of three parts: the stacking of Token Embeddings, Segment Embeddings, and Position Embeddings. The BERT input methodology is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Input Methodology of BERT.

Token Embeddings represent the word vectors, and this phase aims to convert the words in the sentence into corresponding token representations based on the vocabulary. If the input is a single sentence, the E[CLS] token and E[SEP] token must be added at the beginning and end of the sentence, indicating the start and end of the sentence20–22. However, if there are multiple sentences as input, only the E[SEP] token is added at the beginning of the first sentence. Segment Embeddings represent the segment embeddings, indicating the paragraph in which the sentence is situated, and it needs to be adjusted according to the specific task23. For input with a single sentence, the segment vectors for all words remain consistent, where EA represents the segment vector of a sentence24–26. Position Embeddings are position vectors used to convey the positional information of the word vectors. These three parts of vectors are superimposed to construct the input of the entire model27.

The core components of the Transformer Encoder consist of multiple self-attention mechanisms used to compute the weights between the current word and its context28, forming a multi-head attention mechanism. This mechanism utilizes multiple sets of weight matrices, representing linear transformations in different heterogeneous subspaces of the input vectors, to obtain multiple sets of attention scores. These scores are then concatenated and normalized to yield the final weight matrix29. The weight matrix output from the multi-head self-attention mechanism is passed to the feedforward neural network as input. In the feature extraction layer, input data is processed through the first sub-layer, which is the multi-head self-attention layer. Residual processing and layer normalization are then applied. Next, the data passes through the second sub-layer, which is the feedforward neural network layer. Again, residual processing and layer normalization are applied33. As for the output layer, the vectors generated by the BERT model are the word embeddings. Utilizing these word embeddings enhances the performance of downstream tasks34. BERT adopts a multi-layer Transformer Encoder structure, which not only addresses the issue of long-range dependencies in natural language processing but also comprehensively captures the bidirectional relationships within text sentences35. Additionally, the BP neural network training algorithm is employed to adjust the weights of the neural network, gradually bringing it closer to the desired output. During training, the error between the predicted output and the true labels is computed, and then this error is backpropagated to adjust the weights of the layers in the network using the chain rule, aiming to minimize the error36–38.

In the pre-training phase, the BERT model uses the Masked Language Model (MLM) and Next Sentence Prediction (NSP) as objectives, enabling fine-tuning for various downstream tasks without the need for retraining the model39. Google has open-sourced two pre-trained BERT models, which are available for direct use. These models are categorized into Base and Large models based on parameter settings. Sentiment classification model based on rural tourism reviews

This study proposes a coarse-grained sentiment classification model for rural tourism reviews, named the DCM-BERT. The structure of the DCM-BERT model consists of five main components: the input layer, word embedding layer, deep semantic extraction layer, local feature extraction layer, and output layer. The model’s architecture is illustrated in Fig. 2.

DCM-BERT Model Structure.

First, the input layer receives the curated rural tourism review dataset, Tourist_C, as discussed in the previous section. In this layer, the primary task is to load the rural tourism review data and then pass it to the BERT model for processing. Following that is the word embedding layer, which takes the rural tourism review data loaded from the input layer. Here, the BERT model is used to vectorize the rural tourism review text40,41. The structure of the deep semantic extraction layer is shown in Fig. 3.

Structure of the Deep Semantic Extraction Layer.

Unlike the traditional Word2Vec model, the BERT model does not require tokenization or stop-word removal before processing text. Traditional approaches often involve third-party tokenization libraries, such as jieba for Chinese text. However, the BERT model comes with its own tokenization tool called BertTokenizer. The input to the deep semantic extraction layer is the text word vector matrix X processed by the BERT model. The primary purpose of this layer is to utilize a BiGRU network for deep semantic and feature extraction from the input text vectors. This enables a more comprehensive understanding of contextual information42. This layer consists of two oppositely directed Gated Recurrent Units (GRU). Experimental environment and parameters setting

The experimental environment was a MacOS operating system with an Apple M1 chip, 16GB of memory, and Python 3.9.6 as the programming language. The BERT model used the hugging face’s open-source English pre-trained model, ‘Bert-base-Chinese,’ while other models were implemented using the PyTorch framework. The experimental parameter configurations are as follows: max_sequence_length is set to 85, word embedding dimension is 768, dropout rate is 0.1, gru_hidden_size is 384, learning rate (LR) is 2e-5, batch_size is 16, and the optimizer used is Adam.

Result analysis

In this study, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Time-Dependent LSTM (TD-LSTM), Aspect Term Extraction based LSTM (ATE-LSTM), BERT for Sentiment Polarity Classification (BERT-SPC), and Aspect Extraction Network based on BERT (AEN-BERT) were selected as the comparative experiments. The experimental results on the public dataset Laptop are shown in Fig. 5.

Experimental Results on Public Dataset Laptop.

The experimental results indicate that the DCM-BERT model significantly outperforms other comparative models across three core metrics: accuracy, F1 score, and processing time, demonstrating its superior performance in text classification and sentiment analysis tasks. Specifically, the DCM-BERT model achieves an accuracy of 84.33% and an F1 score of 85.33% on the publicly available Laptop dataset. These results not only highlight the model’s excellent performance in assessing rural tourism development levels but also illustrate its deep data mining capabilities, which far exceed those of other models.

In contrast, traditional LSTM-based models such as LSTM, TD-LSTM, and ATE-LSTM show markedly inferior performance, with accuracy rates of 66.95%, 67.4%, and 67.24%, and F1 scores of 58.2%, 59.5%, and 60.2%, respectively. Although these models have shorter processing times—7 s, 8 s, and 9 s—their lower accuracy and F1 scores indicate that they struggle to accurately capture the implicit information in the text and the relationships between data in complex sentiment analysis tasks.

On the other hand, BERT-related models achieve relatively better performance in terms of accuracy and F1 score. The BERT-SPC model attains an accuracy of 73.27% and an F1 score of 73.5%, while the AEN-BERT model achieves an accuracy of 77.74% and an F1 score of 74.88%. This suggests that BERT, as a pre-trained language model, has substantial potential for language understanding and sentiment classification tasks. However, these two models exhibit some drawbacks in processing time, particularly the BERT-SPC model, which takes 30 s—significantly longer than other comparative models. Although the AEN-BERT model has improved processing time to just 12 s, it still falls short of the performance and efficiency demonstrated by the DCM-BERT model.

The exceptional performance of the DCM-BERT model on these metrics can be attributed to its multi-level feature extraction structure. This model not only leverages the global semantic understanding capabilities of the BERT pre-trained model but also enhances the capture of both local and global text features through the combination of BiGRU and TextCNN. This enables the model to achieve an optimal balance between accuracy and efficiency. Particularly in sentiment classification tasks, BiGRU is effective at capturing temporal information in sequential data, while TextCNN excels at extracting key information from local contexts. This multi-layer feature extraction architecture allows the DCM-BERT model to achieve a deeper understanding of the text and its implicit emotional content when handling complex text tasks, resulting in higher classification accuracy and more precise sentiment analysis.

Furthermore, while the processing time of the DCM-BERT model is 20 s—slightly longer than the LSTM series models and AEN-BERT—the significant performance improvement it offers makes this time cost entirely acceptable. In contrast, although LSTM and its variant models operate at a faster speed, their performance in terms of accuracy and F1 score is notably inadequate, failing to meet the demands of complex tasks such as assessing rural tourism development levels. While BERT-SPC achieves high accuracy and F1 scores, its longer processing time could create efficiency bottlenecks in practical applications, especially in scenarios requiring real-time or efficient decision support. Therefore, the DCM-BERT model, through its optimized structure, achieves an ideal balance between performance and time cost, making it an excellent choice for evaluating rural tourism development levels.

Additionally, these results indicate that the DCM-BERT model can more accurately capture the relationships between textual information and numerical data, providing decision-makers with more reliable and persuasive data support. For instance, during the formulation of rural tourism development strategies, decision-makers need to quickly and accurately grasp a vast amount of market feedback and visitor reviews—sentiment data—to assess the effectiveness of current development strategies and make necessary adjustments. The DCM-BERT model, with its outstanding text classification capability, helps decision-makers identify potential correlations between different sentiment data, thereby offering a basis for crafting more targeted and effective rural tourism development strategies.

Overall, the DCM-BERT model proposed in this study demonstrates exceptional performance in handling complex textual data and accurately assessing rural tourism development levels, significantly outperforming traditional LSTM models and certain BERT variant models. It not only achieves high accuracy and F1 scores but also completes tasks within a reasonable processing time, making it highly applicable in contexts that require efficient and precise data analysis. These results highlight the immense potential of the DCM-BERT model in practical applications, particularly in fields that involve processing large amounts of text data and conducting sentiment analysis, such as rural tourism, marketing, and customer feedback analysis.

The experimental results demonstrate the performance of different models in the task of evaluating rural tourism development levels. The DCM-BERT model shows significant advantages in terms of accuracy, F1 score, and processing time. The specific performance is as follows:

Accuracy: The DCM-BERT model achieves an accuracy of 90.3%, substantially higher than other models. This result highlights the model’s superior ability to capture complex data in text classification tasks, particularly in its deep understanding of both textual and numerical data. In comparison, the BERT-SPC model’s accuracy is 89.2%. While close, it is slightly lower, indicating that the DCM-BERT model’s additional BiGRU and TextCNN layers further enhance its feature extraction capabilities. Meanwhile, the LSTM series models perform significantly worse than the BERT series, with the LSTM model achieving an accuracy of only 51.72%, TD-LSTM at 51.55%, and ATE-LSTM at 53.45%, reflecting the inability of these traditional models to effectively capture deeper semantic meanings in complex text tasks.

F1 Score: In terms of the F1 score, the DCM-BERT model again ranks highest, achieving 81.14%. The F1 score is a crucial metric for evaluating models on imbalanced data, showing that the DCM-BERT model not only excels in overall classification performance but also effectively balances precision and recall, improving the identification of minority classes. In contrast, the BERT-SPC model’s F1 score is 73.89%. Although relatively high, it is still lower than DCM-BERT, indicating the latter’s advantage in fine-grained classification tasks. The AEN-BERT model has an F1 score of 62.15%, performing worse than BERT-SPC, while the LSTM series models have even lower scores, demonstrating their significant shortcomings in handling complex sentiment classification tasks.

Processing Time: Although the DCM-BERT model’s processing time is slightly longer than other models, it remains within 5 s, which is acceptable for real-world applications. Despite the LSTM model’s processing time being only 4 s, its low accuracy and F1 score make it a less suitable choice. The BERT-SPC model also has a processing time of 4 s, but its performance is inferior to DCM-BERT. Balancing both performance and efficiency, DCM-BERT delivers significant performance improvements with a slightly longer processing time, making it the ideal choice for evaluating rural tourism development levels.

The DCM-BERT model demonstrates a superior ability to handle the complex task of evaluating rural tourism development levels, providing decision-makers with more accurate and reliable data support. This performance improvement contributes to formulating more effective strategies for rural tourism development, guiding its sustainable growth.

Ablation Experiment Results.

The results of the ablation study indicate that removing any component from the model leads to a decrease in performance.

Removal of the BiGRU Layer: When the BiGRU layer is removed, the model’s performance drops significantly on both datasets. The accuracy on the Laptop dataset decreases to 78.21%, and the F1 score drops to 76.33%. On the Tourist_F dataset, accuracy falls to 85.10%, and the F1 score declines to 70.45%. This demonstrates the importance of the BiGRU layer in capturing sequential information in text. Particularly in complex tasks, BiGRU helps enhance the model’s understanding of temporal data.

Removal of the TextCNN Layer: After removing the TextCNN layer, the model’s performance also shows a decline. On the Laptop dataset, accuracy drops to 79.34%, and the F1 score decreases to 77.56%. On the Tourist_F dataset, accuracy decreases to 86.25%, and the F1 score drops to 72.11%. TextCNN boosts the model’s performance by extracting local features from the text, particularly at the phrase level. The absence of this layer results in a reduced ability to handle local features in the text.

Removal of the BERT Pre-trained Model: Removing the BERT pre-trained model leads to the most significant performance degradation. On the Laptop dataset, accuracy falls to 70.14%, and the F1 score drops to 66.43%. On the Tourist_F dataset, accuracy declines to 78.45%, and the F1 score drops to 60.33%. As a pre-trained model, BERT possesses strong global semantic understanding capabilities, which substantially enhance the model’s comprehension of complex text. Without BERT, the model is unable to effectively capture deep semantics, leading to a sharp drop in performance.

The results of these ablation experiments indicate that BiGRU, TextCNN, and BERT each contribute significantly to the overall performance of the DCM-BERT model. BiGRU strengthens the model’s ability to capture sequential information, TextCNN improves its understanding of local context, and BERT provides robust global semantic analysis. By integrating these components, the DCM-BERT model demonstrates exceptional performance in handling complex sentiment classification tasks, offering reliable data support for evaluating rural tourism development levels.

Discussion

This study proposed a deep learning model based on BERT, combined with the BP algorithm, for the assessment and analysis of rural tourism development. The experimental results demonstrated that the proposed model achieved high accuracy and F1 scores on two different datasets, showcasing its superiority in sentiment classification tasks. This provides an effective tool and method for the assessment and analysis of rural tourism development. By comparing the experimental outcomes of BERT-based and non-BERT-based models, incorporating the BERT model enhances classification accuracy. The integration of the BERT model has greatly improved the classification accuracy. When comparing the average training time for one epoch of each model, it is observed that while the training time of the proposed model increased slightly, its accuracy improved in comparison to other models utilizing BERT as word embeddings.

In rural tourism, understanding visitors’ sentiments and reviews is crucial for development strategies and improving service quality. The sentiment classification model in this study can automatically analyze sentiment information from visitor comments, aiding businesses in better understanding user needs and feedback. When combined with the BP algorithm, the proposed model can further assess rural tourism development, providing a scientific basis for industry decision-makers.

This study is confined to rural tourism data and scenarios, which may not be fully applicable to challenges in other domains. Consequently, future research should investigate the applicability of the proposed method in diverse assessments and analyses across various fields. While this study integrates the BERT model with the BP algorithm, the model’s performance is still influenced by factors such as data quality and parameter settings. Further experimentation and optimization are essential for enhancing the model’s efficacy. Moreover, this study may face computational and temporal constraints in practical applications, particularly when addressing large-scale datasets and complex computational tasks. Therefore, a thorough investigation into the equilibrium between performance and cost in practical applications is warranted.

Conclusion

This study presents the DCM-BERT model, which integrates BiGRU, TextCNN, and BERT components for advanced sentiment analysis and rural tourism development evaluation. Through comprehensive experiments on multiple datasets, the study shows that DCM-BERT significantly outperforms existing models in terms of accuracy, F1 score, and processing time. Specifically, on the publicly available Laptop dataset, the DCM-BERT model achieves 84.33% accuracy and an F1 score of 85.33%. On the Tourist_F dataset, the model’s accuracy reaches 90.3%, with an F1 score of 81.14%. These results highlight the model’s superior ability to capture fine-grained textual information and provide more reliable data support. The performance improvements of the DCM-BERT model can be attributed to its effective integration of multiple components. The BiGRU layer enhances the model’s understanding of sequential data, the TextCNN layer extracts rich textual features, and the BERT component ensures deep contextual and sentiment understanding through pre-trained language representations. Ablation experiments further validated the critical contributions of each component to the model’s performance. This study makes significant contributions to sentiment analysis and rural tourism evaluation by providing a powerful model that combines cutting-edge technologies, achieving high accuracy and efficiency. The DCM-BERT model offers a reliable tool for evaluating rural tourism development, aiding in the formulation of more effective strategic decisions.

Asymptotics for aggregated interdependent multivariate subexponential claims with general investment returns
Zhangting Chen, Dimitrios G. Konstantinides, Charalampos D. Passalidis

Abstract
This paper investigates asymptotic estimates for the entrance probability of the discounted aggregate claim vector from a multivariate renewal risk model into some rare set. We provide asymptotic results for the entrance probability on both finite and infinite time horizons under various assumptions regarding the stochastic price process of the investment portfolio, the distribution class of claim vectors, and the dependence structure among the claim vectors. We note that the main results extend beyond the class of multivariate regular variation. Furthermore, we introduce two dependence structures to model the dependence among the claim vectors. The immediate consequence of the main results is the asymptotic estimates of the ruin probabilities on finite and infinite time horizons.



Keywords: General stochastic return; Multivariate subexponentiality; Multivariate consistently varying class; Ruin probability; Dependence structure

: Primary 62P05 ; Secondary 60G70.

2.1
Heavy-tailed distributions

To introduce the multivariate distribution classes, we need, at first, the univariate ones. In what follows, we assume that for a univariate distribution
G
supported on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, it holds that
\overline{G}(x)>0
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

One of the largest heavy-tailed distribution classes is the class
\mathcal{L}
, of long-tailed distributions. It is said that
G\in\mathcal{L}
, if for any (or equivalently, for some)
y>0
it holds that

\displaystyle{\overline{G}(x-y)}\sim{\overline{G}(x)}\,.

One of the most famous subclasses of
\mathcal{L}
is the subexponential distribution class, symbolically
\mathcal{S}
. It is said that
G\in\mathcal{S}
, if for any (or equivalently, for some) integer
n\geq 2
it holds that

\displaystyle{\overline{G^{n*}}(x)}\sim n{\overline{G}(x)}\,.

Classes
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{S}
were introduced in
[
17
]
, where we find also the inclusion
\mathcal{S}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}
. Class
\mathcal{S}
finds many applications in several branches of applied probability, as for example in risk theory, queuing theory, random walks, among others, see in
[
23
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
for related monographs.

Another important distribution class is the class
\mathcal{D}
of dominatedly varying distributions introduced in
[
24
]
. It is said that
G\in\mathcal{D}
, if for any (or equivalently, for some)
b\in(0,1)
it holds that

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{G}(b\,x)}{\overline{G}(x)}<\infty\,.

It is known that
\mathcal{D}\not\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}\not\subsetneq\mathcal{D}
and further it holds
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S}\equiv\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}
, see in
[
29
]
. A smaller class than
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}
, is the class of consistently varying distributions
\mathcal{C}
, introduced in
[
18
]
. We say that
G\in\mathcal{C}
, if it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{b\uparrow 1}\limsup\dfrac{\overline{G}(b\,x)}{\overline{G}(x)}=1\,.

Finally, we introduce a smaller distribution class called the regularly varying distribution class
\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, with
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
. It is said that
G\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, with index of regular variation
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, if for any
y>0
, it holds

\displaystyle{\overline{G}(y\,x)}\sim y^{-\alpha}{\overline{G}(x)}\,.

Let us conclude with the following inclusion, see for example in
[
25
]
, or in
[
44
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}:=\bigcup_{0<\alpha<\infty}\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}\subsetneq\mathcal{C}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}\,.

(2.1)

The upper and lower Matuszewska indexes are two known indexes used for the characterization of heavy-tailed distributions. By definition, for any
y>1
, the upper and lower Matuszewska indexes of a distribution
G
are formulated as

\displaystyle J_{G}^{+}:=-\lim_{y\to\infty}\dfrac{\log\overline{G}_{*}(y)}{\log y}\,,\qquad J_{G}^{-}:=-\lim_{y\to\infty}\dfrac{\log\overline{G}^{*}(y)}{\log y}\,,

respectively, where
\overline{G}_{*}(y):=\liminf\frac{\overline{G}(y\,x)}{\overline{G}(x)}
and
\overline{G}^{*}(y):=\limsup\frac{\overline{G}(y\,x)}{\overline{G}(x)}\,
, for
y>1
. It was shown that
G\in\mathcal{D}
if and only if
J_{G}^{+}<\infty
, and if
G\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, with
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, then
J_{G}^{+}=J_{G}^{-}=\alpha
. Further, if
G\in\mathcal{D}
, then for any

, it holds that

\displaystyle x^{-p}=o\left[\overline{G}(x)\right]\,.

(2.2)

For further discussions, we refer to
[
3
, Sec. 2.1.2]
or
[
44

The following distribution class is immediately related to the lower Matuszeska index, and was introduced in
[
31
]
. A distribution
G
belongs to the class of positively decreasing distributions, symbolically
G\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
, if for any (or equivalently, for some)

\displaystyle\overline{G}^{*}(y)<1\,.

It is known that

if and only if

is wide enough to contain both light-tailed and heavy-tailed distributions. As mentioned by

2.2
Dependence modeling

Here, we provide two dependence structures that can be used to describe the dependency among the claim vectors. We recall, which was mentioned earlier, that each claim vector is allowed to have dependent components.

Before doing this, we need to introduce the following two dependence structures that describe the dependencies among countably infinitely many random variables. The first dependence structure, introduced by
[

]
, was found in many applications, see for example
[

]
,
[

]

[

]
,

]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\{Z_{i}\,,\;i\geq 1\}
be a sequence of nonnegative random variables, with distributions
V_{1},\,V_{2}\,\ldots
, respectively. We say that they are regression dependent, symbolically
RD
, if there exist some constants
x_{0}>0
and
K>0
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[Z_{i}>x_{i}\;|\;Z_{j}=x_{j}\,,\;{\text{w}ith}\;j\in J\right]\leq K\,\overline{V}_{i}(x_{i})\,,

(2.10)

holds for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
j\in J\subseteq\mathbb{N}\setminus\{i\}
, with
J\neq\emptyset
, and
x_{i}\wedge x_{j}>x_{0}
.

The second dependence structure, introduced by
[
10
]
, was also used in several papers, see for example in
[
46
]
,
[
51
]
,
[
34
]
,
[
52
]
, among others.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\{Z_{i}\,,\;i\geq 1\}
be a sequence of nonnegative random variables, with distributions
V_{1},\,V_{2}\,\ldots
, respectively. We say that they are quasi-asymptotically independent, symbolically
QAI
, if

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[Z_{i}>x\,,\;Z_{j}>x\right]}{\overline{V}_{i}(x)+\overline{V}_{j}(x)}=0\,,

(2.11)

holds for any
i,\,j\in\mathbb{N}
, with
i\neq j
.

It is easy to check that if random variables
\{Z_{i}\,,\;i\geq 1\}
are
RD
, then they are also
QAI
, but the converse is not generally true. We refer the reader to
[
13
]
for some counterexamples. Let us remark that the structures
RD
and
QAI
were defined for random variables with support the whole real line
{\mathbb{R}}
, with small changes with respect to absolute values in relations (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), however, we restrict ourselves to the previous form, for the sake of consistency with the present paper.

Now we are ready to give our definitions of dependence structures among random vectors.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set,
\{{\bf Z}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
be a sequence of nonnegative random vectors and
\{{Z}_{A}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
be a sequence of random variables defined by (

). If the

are

, then we say that the vectors
\{{\bf Z}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are regression dependent on

, symbolically

.

Analogously, we can define the following dependence structure based on
QAI
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set,
\{{\bf Z}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
be a sequence of nonnegative random vectors and
\{{Z}_{A}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
be a sequence of random variables defined by (
2.8
). If the
\{Z_{A}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are
QAI
, then we say that the vectors
\{{\bf Z}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are quasi-asymptotically independent on
A
, symbolically
QAI_{A}
.

It is easy to see that if
\{{\bf Z}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are
RD_{A}
, then it implies that they are
QAI_{A}
for the same
A\in\mathscr{R}
. To specifically illustrate the inclusion relationship between
RD_{A}
and
QAI_{A}
, we end this section by giving two tractable examples that satisfy
QAI_{A}
.

Example 2.1
.

For simplicity, we consider the following two-dimensional random vectors
{\bf X}=(X_{1},X_{2})
and
{\bf Y}=(Y_{1},Y_{2})
, where each component variable shares the same absolutely continuous marginal distribution
G
. Let

\displaystyle A=\left\{(x,y)\,:\,\,x\vee y>1\right\}\in\mathscr{R}\,,

any two and any three of
X_{1},\,X_{2},\,Y_{1},\,Y_{2}
are independent, and the joint distribution of component variables follows the FGM distribution of the form

\displaystyle\quad\mathbf{P}\left[X_{1}>x_{1},\,X_{2}>x_{2},\,Y_{1}>y_{1},\,Y_{2}>y_{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\overline{G}(x_{1})\overline{G}(x_{2})\overline{G}(y_{1})\overline{G}(y_{2})\left(1+\theta G(x_{1})G(x_{2})G(y_{1})G(y_{2})\right)

with
\theta\in[-1,\,1]
. Only for the examples, we define
X_{A}:=\sup\{u:{\bf X}\in u\,A\}
and
Y_{A}:=\sup\{u:{\bf Y}\in u\,A\}
. Then, for
x,\,y\geq 0
, by Sklar’s theorem (see e.g.
[
58
]
) one can derive that

\displaystyle\quad\mathbf{P}\left[X_{A}>x,Y_{A}>y\right]=\mathbf{P}\left[\left\{X_{1}>x\right\}\cup\left\{X_{2}>x\right\},\left\{Y_{1}>y\right\}\cup\left\{Y_{2}>y\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=4\overline{G}(x)\overline{G}(y)-2\overline{G}(x)\left(\overline{G}(y)\right)^{2}-2\left(\overline{G}(x)\right)^{2}\overline{G}(y)+\left(\overline{G}(x)\right)^{2}\left(\overline{G}(y)\right)^{2}(1+\theta G^{2}(x)G^{2}(y))

\displaystyle:=\widehat{C}(\overline{G_{A}}(x),\overline{G_{A}}(y)),

where
\widehat{C}(\cdot,\,\cdot)
denotes the survival copula of
(X_{A},\,Y_{A})
, and

\displaystyle\overline{G_{A}}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left[X_{A}>x\right]=\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}>x\right]=2\overline{G}(x)-\left(\overline{G}(x)\right)^{2}.

Thus, for
x
large enough, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[X_{A}>x|Y_{A}=y\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\widehat{C}(\overline{G_{A}}(x),\overline{G_{A}}(y))}{\partial\overline{G_{A}}(y)}=\frac{\partial\widehat{C}(\overline{G_{A}}(x),\overline{G_{A}}(y))}{\partial\overline{G}(y)}\times\frac{\mathrm{d}\overline{G}(y)}{\mathrm{d}\overline{G_{A}}(y)}

\displaystyle=\left(2G(y)\right)^{-1}\left(4\overline{G}(x)G(y)-2\left(\overline{G}(x)\right)^{2}+2\left(\overline{G}(x)\right)^{2}\overline{G}(y)(1+\theta G^{2}(x)G^{2}(y))\right.

\displaystyle\quad-2\theta\left.\left(\overline{G}(x)\right)^{2}\left(\overline{G}(y)\right)^{2}G^{2}(x)G(y)\right)

\displaystyle\leq(2\overline{G}(x)+\left(\overline{G}(x)\right)^{2})\leq 4\mathbf{P}\left[X_{A}>x\right].

Analogously, for any
x
large enough, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}>x|X_{A}=y\right]\leq 4\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}>x\right]\,.

Then, it is obvious that in this example, (
2.10
) holds, which implies that
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
are
RD_{A}
.

Next, we provide an example where two random vectors are
QAI_{A}
but not
RD_{A}
.

Example 2.2
.

Consider two two-dimensional positve random vectors
{\bf X}=(X_{1},X_{2})
and
{\bf Y}=(Y_{1},Y_{2})
. Assume that the two components of each vector are mutually independent, but for random components sharing the same subscript, the joint distribution is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[X_{i}>x,Y_{i}>y\right]=\left(1+x^{3}+y\right)^{-1}\,,\,i=1\,,\,2\,,

for all
x\geq 0
,
y\geq 0
, and
X_{i},\,Y_{j}
are independent for each
1\leq i\neq j\leq 2
. Then, for
i=1,\,2
, the marginal distributions are

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[X_{i}>x\right]=\left(1+x^{3}\right)^{-1},\,P\left[Y_{i}>y\right]=\left(1+y\right)^{-1},

for all
x\geq 0
,
y\geq 0
. Following the step of Example
2.1
, for all
x\geq 0
,
y\geq 0
, we derive that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[X_{A}>x,Y_{A}>y\right]

\displaystyle=2\left(1+x^{3}+y\right)^{-1}+2\left(1+x^{3}\right)^{-1}\left(1+y\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad-2\left(1+x^{3}+y\right)^{-1}\left(1+y\right)^{-1}-2\left(1+x^{3}\right)^{-1}\left(1+x^{3}+y\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(1+x^{3}+y\right)^{-2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[X_{A}>x\right]=2\left(1+x^{3}\right)^{-1}-\left(1+x^{3}\right)^{-2}\,,

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}>x\right]=2\left(1+y\right)^{-1}-\left(1+y\right)^{-2}\,.

Thus, for
x
large enough, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{A}>x,Y_{A}>x\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{A}>x\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}>x\right]}=O(1)\frac{\left(1+x^{3}+x\right)^{-1}+\left(1+x^{3}\right)^{-1}\left(1+x\right)^{-1}}{\left(1+x^{3}\right)^{-1}+\left(1+x\right)^{-1}}=o(1)\,,

which implies that
{\bf X}=(X_{1},X_{2})
and
{\bf Y}=(Y_{1},Y_{2})
are
QAI_{A}
. However, they are not
RD_{A}
. Indeed, for
x
large enough, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{A}>x,Y_{A}>x\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[X_{A}>x\right]}\geq\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{\left(1+x^{3}+x\right)^{-1}+\left(1+x^{3}\right)^{-1}\left(1+x\right)^{-1}}{\left(1+x^{3}\right)^{-1}}\to\frac{3}{4}>0.

This contradicts (
2.10
).

3
Main results

In this section, we present the main results that establish relation (
1.6
) under different conditions.
In what follows, we fixed some
A\in\mathscr{R}
, the claim vectors
{\bf X}^{(i)}
are related with the random variables

\displaystyle Y_{A}^{(i)}:=\sup\left\{u\;:\;{\bf X}^{(i)}\in u\,A\right\}\,,

that follow the distributions
F_{A}^{(i)}\equiv F_{A}
, respectively.

3.1
On finite time horizon asymptotics with multivariate subexponential claims

This subsection will establish (
1.6
) when
F
is multivariate subexponential and
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
has non-decreasing sample paths. Note that we do not have any additional conditions on the components of each claim vector, which, in combination with
RD_{A}
, allows interdependence among claim vectors.

Theorem 3.1
.

Consider the aggregate claims from relation (
1
). Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set. We suppose that Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
are true,
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are
RD_{A}
,
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, and
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
has non-decreasing sample paths. Then for any fixed
T\in\varLambda\setminus\{\infty\}
, relation (
1.6
) holds.

Remark 3.1
.

We observe that some similar results, as in relation (
1.6
) are found in
[
38
, Th. 5.1]
, where the relation (
1.6
), was established in a model with independent claim vectors and a common Poisson counting process, but there was no restriction for non-decreasing sample paths for
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
. Furthermore, in
[
39
, Th. 3.1]
relation (
1.6
) appears under a larger dependence structure between the claim vectors than
RD_{A}
, but the distribution class of claim vectors is restricted to
(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{A}
.

Remark 3.2
.

We should mention that even in a one-dimensional case, with
A=(1,\,\infty)
, this result in Theorem
3.1
is new. Also, if in Theorem
3.1
we assume that
F\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}
, instead of
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, and the claims are
RD_{A}
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, then relation (
1.6
) is still true, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
. Similar assertions hold for all subsequent main results. For brevity, we shall not reiterate throughout the paper.

3.2
On finite time horizon asymptotics with multivariate consistently varying claims

The following theorem focuses again on relation (
1.6
), but under a different dependence structure, namely when the claim vectors are
QAI_{A}
and
F
belongs to the distribution class
\mathcal{C}_{A}
, for some fixed
A\in\mathscr{R}
. Hence, if we compare with the result of the previous section, we see that the claim vector distribution class is reduced from
\mathcal{S}_{A}
into
\mathcal{C}_{A}
, but the dependence structure is generalized from
RD_{A}
to
QAI_{A}
, and further was removed the condition of non-decreasing sample paths for
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Consider the aggregate claims from relation (
1
). Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set. We suppose that Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
are true,
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are
QAI_{A}
, and
F\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
.
Then, for any fixed
T\in\varLambda\setminus\{\infty\}
, relation (
1.6
) holds.

The following corollary is established by Theorem
3.2
, building on the fact that
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
. As mentioned previously, it ensures
F_{A}\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
. The dominated convergence theorem and Breiman’s theorem, see in
[
5
]
, become applicable through Assumption
1.2
. Restricting ourselves in
MRV
, we gain a more detailed expression in relation (
1.6
).

Corollary 3.1
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
3.2
, if
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, then for any fixed
T\in\varLambda\setminus\{\infty\}
, we obtain the asymptotic relation

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(T)\in x\,A\right]\sim\mu(A)\overline{G}(x)\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\alpha\,\xi(s)}\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,.

(3.1)

Remark 3.3
.

We remark that the relation (
3.1
) is indeed a precise asymptotic estimate. According to the proof of
[
63
, Prop. 4.14]
, if
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, then for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, we have
0<\mu(A)<\infty
.

3.3
On infinite time horizon asymptotics

In this subsection, we deal with the estimate (
1.6
) over the infinite time horizon, namely for
T=\infty
. Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set. Assuming that
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
still represents
QAI_{A}
sequence of vectors, but the class of the common distribution
F
, is restricted from
\mathcal{C}_{A}
into
(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}})_{A}
. However,we replace Assumption
1.2
by Assumption
3.1
presented below, which seems rather general to provide more flexibility with respect to the insurer’s investment portfolio.

We begin with the basic assumption for the general process
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
. Let us remind that
F_{A}
has
J_{F_{A}}^{-}>0
, since
F\in\mathcal{P_{D}}_{A}
.

Assumption 3.1
.

Let
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
be a general process. We assume that there exist some
p_{1},\,p_{2}
with
0<p_{1}<J_{F_{A}}^{-}\leq J_{F_{A}}^{+}<p_{2}<\infty
, such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-p_{1}\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]\vee\mathbf{E}\left(e^{-p_{2}\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]\right)^{1/\rho}<\infty\,,

(3.2)

where
\rho={\bf 1}_{\left(0<J_{F_{A}}^{+}<1\right)}+p_{2}{\bf 1}_{\left(J_{F_{A}}^{+}\geq 1\right)}.

Thus, through Assumption
3.1
, we get rid of the condition of lower bound for
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
. It is easy to see that if
\xi(t)=r\,t
, with
r\geq 0
a constant interest force, then Assumption
3.1
is satisfied. In general, the removal of Assumption
1.2
permits the insurer to make riskier investments, which is sometimes necessary to make the insurance company reliable. Especially in cases where insurers have portfolios with heavy-tailed claims, they are encouraged to choose riskier investments.

Now, we provide a general example of stochastic processes that satisfy Assumption
3.1
. Indeed, in this example we find out that all Lévy processes, with Laplace exponent
\phi(p_{2})<0
for some
p_{2}>J_{F_{A}}^{+}
, satisfy Assumption
3.1
.

Example 3.1
.

Let
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
be a real-valued Lévy process. The Laplace exponent is defined by the relation

\displaystyle\phi(k)=\log\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-k\,\xi(1)}\right]\,,

for any
k\in{\mathbb{R}}
. It is known that if
\phi(k)<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-k\,\xi(t)}\right]=e^{t\,\phi(k)}<\infty\,,

(3.3)

see for example in
[
20
, Prop. 3.14]
.

Let
\phi(p_{2})<0
, for some
p_{2}>J_{F_{A}}^{+}
. Then, from relation (
3.3
), and taking into account that the
\{\tau_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
represent a renewal counting process, we obtain that it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-p_{2}\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]=\left(\mathbf{E}\left[e^{\tau_{1}\,\phi(p_{2})}\right]\right)^{i}<1,

for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Further, for
p_{1}\in(0,\,J_{F_{A}}^{-})
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-p_{1}\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]=\left(\mathbf{E}\left[e^{\tau_{1}\,\phi(p_{1})}\right]\right)^{i}<1,

where in the last step we used the fact that
\phi(p_{1})<0
, since
\phi(p_{2})<0
,
\phi(0)=0
and the
\phi(k)
is convex with respect to
k
, that implies
\phi(k)<0
, for any
k\in(0,\,p_{2}]
.

Then, for
0<p_{1}<J_{F_{A}}^{-}\leq J_{F_{A}}^{+}<p_{2}<\infty
and
\rho={\bf 1}_{\left(0<J_{F_{A}}^{+}<1\right)}+p_{2}{\bf 1}_{\left(J_{F_{A}}^{+}\geq 1\right)}
, it is not hard to verify that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-p_{1}\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]\vee\mathbf{E}\left(e^{-p_{2}\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]\right)^{1/\rho}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\left(\mathbf{E}\left[e^{\tau_{1}\,\phi(p_{1})}\right]\right)^{i}+\left(\mathbf{E}\left[e^{\tau_{1}\,\phi(p_{2})}\right]\right)^{i}\right]^{1/\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\left(\mathbf{E}\left[e^{\tau_{1}\,\phi(p_{1})}\right]\right)^{i/\rho}+\left(\mathbf{E}\left[e^{\tau_{1}\,\phi(p_{2})}\right]\right)^{i/\rho}\right]<\infty\,,

where in the prelast step the elementary inequality
(a+b)^{r}\leq a^{r}+b^{r}
, for any
a,\,b\geq 0
, and any
r\in(0,\,1]
. Which indicates that Assumption
3.1
holds.

Remark 3.4
.

We notice that the assumption that the logarithmic prices of the investment portfolio is Lévy processes, with Laplace exponent that satisfies the condition
\phi(p)<0
, for some
p
greater than the upper Matuszewska index of distribution of the claims, is popular enough in onedimensional and multidimensional risk models, that study cases, where the insurers make risky investments, see for example in
[
67
]
,
[
74
]
,
[
47
]
,
[
73
]
among others. Therefore, through Example
3.1
, we realize the generality of Assumption
3.1
.

Now, we are ready for the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 3.3
.

Consider the aggregate claim vector (
1
) with
T=\infty
. Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set. We supposed that Assumptions
1.1
and
3.1
are true,
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are
QAI_{A}
, and
F\in(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}})_{A}
, then (
1.6
) holds with
T=\infty
.

If Assumption
3.1
holds, then it is obvious that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-p_{1}\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]\vee\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-p_{2}\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]\leq\left(\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-p_{1}\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]\vee\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-p_{2}\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]\right)^{1/\rho}<\infty\,,

hence if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, for some
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\alpha\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]\leq\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-p_{1}\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]\vee\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-p_{2}\,\xi(\tau_{i})}\right]<\infty\,,

for some
0<p_{1}<\alpha<p_{2}<\infty
. Therefore, we can analogously apply the dominated convergence theorem and Breiman’s theorem in Theorem
3.3
to establish the following corollary.

Corollary 3.2
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
3.3
, if
F\in MRV(\alpha,\mu)
, then it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}[{\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A]}\sim\mu({A}){\overline{G}(x)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\alpha\,\xi(s)}\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,.

Remark 3.5
.

The study of the first entrance time of one-dimensional and multidimensional random walks into some rare set is well-known and directly connected with insurer’s surplus process; further, we refer to
[
55
]
, and references therein. However, due to the fact that the study is restricted to random walks only, it permits covering several situations in the modern insurance industry, as the presence of investments and interdependence. Therefore, our results present a first picture for the conditional distribution of the first entrance time of the aggregate claims into some rare set
A\in\mathscr{R}
.

Firstly, for some
A\in\mathscr{R}
, we define by

\displaystyle\tau(x):=\inf\left\{t>0\;:\;{\bf D}(t)\in x\,A\right\}\,,

(3.4)

the first entrance time, which could eventually be a defective random variable, since we could have that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[\tau(x)<\infty]=\mathbf{P}[{\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A]<1\,,

where at the first step we used relation (
3.4
), the increasing property of set
A
and the nonnegativeness of the summands of
{\bf D}(\infty)
. Thus, in case of simple interest, when
\xi(t)=r\,t
, with
r>0
and under the condition
F\in(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}})_{A}
, that implies the validity of Theorems
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\tau(x)\leq T\;|\;\tau(x)<\infty\right]=\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[\tau(x)\leq T\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[\tau(x)<\infty\right]}=\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(T)\in x\,A\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A\right]}

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(1)}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(1)}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)}

for any
T\in\varLambda/\{\infty\}
.

Furthermore, if we assume that the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is Poisson, and under the condition
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, for some
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[\tau(x)\leq T\;|\;\tau(x)<\infty]\sim\dfrac{\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\alpha\,r\,s}ds}{\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha\,r\,s}ds}=1-e^{-\alpha\,r\,T}\,,

(3.5)

for any
T>0
, where
\lambda>0
is the intensity of Poisson process. That means, in case of constant interest force, with
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
and Poisson counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, we find by relation (
3.5
) that the limit distribution of
\tau(x)
conditioned by
\tau(x)<\infty
coincides with the exponential one with parameter
\alpha\,r
.

4
Proofs of the main results

We now proceed to the proofs of our main results. However, before proving each theorem, we require some auxiliary lemmas specific to its respective proofs.

4.1
Proof of Theorem
3.1

We begin with some preliminary lemmas. The first one has its own merit, as it demonstrates the insensitivity property of the multivariate linear single big jump principle of the scale mixture sums with respect to
RD_{A}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set. We suppose that the random vectors
{\bf Z}^{(1)},\,\ldots,\,{\bf Z}^{(n)}
are
RD_{A}
, with distributions
V_{1},\,\ldots,\,V_{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, respectively. Let
W_{1},\,\ldots,\,W_{n}
be
n
arbitrarily dependent random variables (none degenerate to zero), which are independent of
{\bf Z}^{(1)},\,\ldots,\,{\bf Z}^{(n)}
, such that
0\leq W_{i}\leq b
almost surely for some
0<b<\infty
and for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
. If additionally we assume that
V_{i}*V_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\,.

(4.1)

Proof.

Initially, since for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, we have
V_{i},\,V_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
and
V_{i}*V_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, by
[
38
, Th. 3.4]
is implied that

V_{A}^{(i)}*V_{A}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{S}
.

Relation (
4.1
) amounts to the conjunction of

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\,,

(4.2)

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\gtrsim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\,.

(4.3)

We prove (
4.2
) in two steps, first we assume that
W_{1},\,\ldots,\,W_{n}
are positive, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(0<W_{i}\leq b)=1\,,

for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
. For any subsets
I\subseteq\mathbb{I}=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and any
0<\varepsilon<1
, write

\displaystyle\Omega_{I}^{\varepsilon}(W)=\{\omega:W_{i}(\omega)>\varepsilon\,\,{\rm for}\,\,i\in I,\,{\rm and}\,\,W_{j}(\omega)\leq\varepsilon\,\,{\rm for}\,\,j\notin I\}.

Then, for the left-hand side of (
4.2
), we have by following the lines of the proof of
[
38
, Prop. 2.4]
that

\displaystyle\quad\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\leq\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{I\subseteq\mathbb{I}}\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x,\,\Omega_{I}^{\varepsilon}(W)\right]\leq\sum_{I\subseteq\mathbb{I}}\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i\in I}W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}+\sum_{j\notin I}\varepsilon\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x,\,\Omega_{I}^{\varepsilon}(W)\right]

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{I\subseteq\mathbb{I}}\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x,\,\Omega_{I}^{\varepsilon}(W)\right]+\sum_{I\subseteq\mathbb{I}}\sum_{j\notin I}\mathbf{P}\left[\varepsilon\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x,\,\Omega_{I}^{\varepsilon}(W)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{I\subseteq\mathbb{I}:\,i\in I}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x,\,\Omega_{I}^{\varepsilon}(W)\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{I\subseteq\mathbb{I}:\,j\notin I}\mathbf{P}\left[\varepsilon\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x,\,\Omega_{I}^{\varepsilon}(W)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{I\subseteq\mathbb{I}:\,i\in I}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x,\,\Omega_{I}^{\varepsilon}(W)\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{I\subseteq\mathbb{I}:\,j\notin I}\mathbf{P}\left[\varepsilon\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x,\,W_{j}>\varepsilon\right]\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[\Omega_{I}^{\varepsilon}(W)]}{\mathbf{P}[W_{j}>\varepsilon]}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\frac{\mathbf{P}[W_{i}\leq\varepsilon]}{\mathbf{P}[W_{i}>\varepsilon]}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]

\displaystyle=\left(1+\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\frac{\mathbf{P}[W_{i}\leq\varepsilon]}{\mathbf{P}[W_{i}>\varepsilon]}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right],

(4.4)

where, in the fifth step, we used
[
69
, Th. 2]
.
By letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, we conclude (
4.2
). In the second step, we prove the case where
W_{1},\ldots,\,W_{n}
may take the value zero with a positive probability. We write

\displaystyle\Omega_{I}^{0}(W)=\{\omega:W_{i}(\omega)>0\,\,{\rm for}\,\,i\in I,\,{\rm and}\,\,W_{j}(\omega)=0\,\,{\rm for}\,\,j\notin I\}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\quad\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\leq\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]=\sum_{\emptyset\neq I\subseteq\mathbb{I}}\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i\in I}W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x,\,\Omega_{I}^{0}(W)\right]

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\emptyset\neq I\subseteq\mathbb{I}}\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x,\,\Omega_{I}^{0}(W)\right]\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right],

where in the third step we used
[
27
, Th. 3.2]
.
This completes the proof of (
4.2
). As for (
4.3
), because of the fact that
A
is an increasing set,
W_{i}
,
{\bf Z}^{(i)}
are nonnegative, and by Bonferroni’s inequality, we find that it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\geq\,\mathbf{P}\left[\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\{W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\}\right]\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;W_{j}\,{\bf Z}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right]\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\,,

(4.5)

where the last step is justified, since each term of the second sum (in pre last step) is asymptotically negligible with respect to each term of the first sum. Indeed, for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
and for any
x/b>x_{0}
, by the assumption that
{\bf Z}^{(i)},\,{\bf Z}^{(j)}
are
RD_{A}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;W_{j}\,{\bf Z}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right]=\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\,,\;W_{j}\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}[b\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x]\,\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\;|\;b\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}[b\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x]\,\int_{0}^{b}\mathbf{P}\left[y\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\;|\;b\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x\right]\mathbf{P}[W_{i}\in dy]

\displaystyle=O(1)\,\mathbf{P}[b\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x]\,\int_{0}^{b}\mathbf{P}\left[y\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]\,\mathbf{P}[W_{i}\in dy]

\displaystyle=O(1)\,\mathbf{P}[b\,Z_{A}^{(j)}>x]\,\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]=o\left(\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]\right)=o\left(\mathbf{P}\left[W_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\right)\,.

Hence, we obtain relation (

) and close the proof of this lemma.

For the upper bound, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A]=\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\bf X}^{(i)}e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\right]=\mathbf{P}\left[\sup_{{\bf p}\in I_{A}}{\bf p}^{T}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\bf X}^{(i)}e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\right)>x\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}Y_{A}^{(i)}e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}>x\right]\sim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(i)}e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}>x\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\,\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-\xi(s)}\in x\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,.

(4.26)

where, in the fourth step, we used
[
75
, Th. 2]
.

Next, we deal with the lower bound. Let
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be large enough, then it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A]\geq\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{M}{\bf X}^{(i)}e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\right]\geq\mathbf{P}\left[\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}\left\{{\bf X}^{(i)}e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\right]-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq M}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\,,\;{\bf X}^{(j)}e^{-\xi(\tau_{j})}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\right]=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}-\sum_{i=M+1}^{\infty}\right)\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-\xi(s)}\in x\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,.

(4.27)

where at the second step we used the fact that
A
is increasing, at the third step we used the Bonferroni inequality, and at the fourth step we used
[
46
, Th. 2.2]
, while in the last step we let
M\to\infty
and we applied Lemma
4.4
. From relations (
4.3
) and (
4.3
) we obtain the desired result.
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.2

Analogously to the proof of Corollary
3.1
, we only need to prove (
4.23
) with
T=\infty
. The convergence is obvious; we only need to find the dominant function. Due to
[
75
, Lem. 1]
, for any
s\geq 0
and some fixed
0<p_{1}<\alpha<p_{2}<\infty
indicated in Assumption
3.1
, there exists some constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-\xi(s)}\in x\,A\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-p_{1}\xi(s)}\right]\vee\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-p_{2}\xi(s)}\right],

which is integrable because of (
3.2
). Thus, Corollary
3.2
is proved by the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

5
Applications to ruin probabilities

It is well known that the asymptotic behavior of the tail, or the entrance probability into some rare set, of discounted aggregate claims is directly related to the ruin probability, and even more in the presence of subexponential claims, these two probabilities are asymptotically equivalent. Hence, through the discounted aggregate claims from relation (
1
), the insurer’s discounted surplus process is given by the following relation

\displaystyle{\bf U}(T)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}U_{1}(T)\\
\vdots\\
U_{d}(T)\end{array}\right)=x\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}l_{1}\\
\vdots\\
l_{d}\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\xi(s)}\,p_{1}(s)\,ds\\
\vdots\\
\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\xi(s)}\,p_{d}(s)\,ds\end{array}\right)-{\bf D}(T)\,,

for any fixed
T\in\varLambda
, where
x>0
represents the insurer’s initial capital, that is allocated via vector
{\bf l}=(l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d})
into the
d
-lines of business, and we assume that
l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d}>0
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}l_{i}=1\,.

The vector
{\bf p}(t)=(p_{1}(t),\,\ldots,\,p_{d}(t))
, for
t\geq 0
, depicts the premium densities, which are nonnegative and locally bounded from above, namely for any
T>0
, there exists some constant
\Lambda_{T}
such that

\displaystyle 0\leq p_{i}(T)\leq C_{T}\,,

for all
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
.

The ruin probability in a multidimensional risk model can be defined in several ways, however, in general we can say that it expresses the entrance probability of the surplus process into some set (ruin set), which is closely related with the negative surplus of some (or, all) lines of business. Thus, the ruin set here satisfies the following condition, introduced in
[
63
]
.

Assumption 5.1
.

Let
L
be a set, that is open, decreasing (that means
-L
is increasing), with convex complement
L^{c}
, and
{\bf 0}\in\partial L
. Additionally, we suppose that
L
is insensitive with respect to positive scalars, namely, for any
x>0
it holds
x\,L=L
.

Remark 5.1
.

Obviously,
A={\bf l}-L\in\mathscr{R}
, for any
L
satisfying Assumption
5.1
. So, concerning the sets
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
from relations (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we have the ruin sets

\displaystyle L_{1}=\{{\bf y}\;:\;y_{i}<0\,,\;\exists i=1,\,\ldots,\,d\}\,,\qquad L_{2}=\left\{{\bf y}\;:\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}l_{i}\,y_{i}<0\right\}\,,

respectively. The surplus entrance probability into ruin set
L_{1}
, indicates the probability that one of the
d
-lines of business has negative surplus, and usually denoted as
\psi_{or}
, while the surplus entrance probability into ruin set
L_{2}
, indicates the probability that the sum of the surpluses for the
d
-lines of business is negative, and usually denoted as
\psi_{sum}
. For further discussions about the
\psi_{or}
,
\psi_{sum}
and other forms of ruin probabilities in multivariate risk models, we refer to
[
16
]
. Furthermore, we notice that the classical ruin set
L=(-\infty,\,0)
, for the univariate risk models, satisfy Assumption
5.1
.

Now we can define the ruin probability over finite time, as the entrance probability of surplus into some ruin set
L
, that satisfies the Assumption
5.1
.

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,T)=\mathbf{P}[{\bf U}(t)\in L\,,\;\exists\;t\in(0,\,T]]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\xi(s)}\,{\bf p}(s)\,ds\in x\,({\bf l}-L)\,,\;\exists\;t\in(0,\,T]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\xi(s)}\,{\bf p}(s)\,ds\in x\,A\,,\;\exists\;t\in(0,\,T]\right]\,,

(5.11)

where

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\xi(s)}\,{\bf p}(s)\,ds=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\xi(s)}\,p_{1}(s)\,ds\\
\vdots\\
\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\xi(s)}\,p_{d}(s)\,ds\end{array}\right)\,,

for any
t\in[0,\,T]
. Next, we give the following corollary.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
A={\bf l}-L\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set. Then

(i)

under the conditions of Theorem
3.1
, and for any fixed
T\in\varLambda\setminus\{\infty\}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,T)\sim\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-\xi(s)}\in x\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

(5.13)

(ii)

under the conditions of Theorem
3.2
, and for any fixed
T\in\varLambda\setminus\{\infty\}
, relation (
5.13
) holds.

(iii)

under the conditions of Corollary
3.2
, and for any fixed
T\in\varLambda\setminus\{\infty\}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,T)\sim\mu(A)\overline{G}(x)\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-\alpha\,\xi(s)}\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,.

(5.14)

Proof.

The points (i) - (iii) can be proved by a similar argument. We show only the case of part (i). At first, since the densities of the premiums are bounded, and by Assumption
1.2
we obtain that for any
T\in\varLambda\setminus\{\infty\}
, that it holds

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\xi(s)}\,{\bf p}(s)\,ds\leq{\bf C_{T}}\,e^{K_{T}}\,T<\infty\,,

hence, from this last relation and
[
63
, Lem. 4.3(iv)]
, we find that for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
and
{\bf C_{T}}\,e^{K_{T}}\,T\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
, there exists some
u\in(0,\,x)
, such that

\displaystyle(x+u)\,A\subsetneq x\,A+{\bf C_{T}}\,e^{K_{T}}\,T\subsetneq(x-u)\,A\,.

(5.15)

Consequently, due to
A
is increasing and Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,T)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(T)\in x\,A\right]\sim\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-\xi(s)}\in x\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,.

(5.16)

From the other side, employing similar arguments, and using the left inclusion of relation (
5.15
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,T)\geq\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(T)\in x\,A+\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\xi(s)}\,{\bf p}(s)\,ds\right]\geq\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(T)\in(x+u)\,A\right]

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-\xi(s)}\in(x+u)\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\sim\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-\xi(s)}\in x\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

(5.17)

where the last step because of the distribution of
{\bf X}e^{-\xi(s)}
belongs to class
\mathcal{S}_{A}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{A}
, see
[
44
, sec. 5.9]
for more details. Therefore, from relations (
5.16
) and (
5
) we find (
5.13
).
∎

Next, we examine the infinite-time ruin probability in the risk model (
5
). As in (
5
), here also for the ruin sets
L
satisfying Assumption
5.1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,\infty):=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf U}(t)\in L\,,\;\exists\;t>0\right]=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\xi(s)}\,{\bf p}(s)\,ds\in x\,A\,,\;\exists\;t>0\right]\,.

(5.18)

We can now present the following corollary, which provides an asymptotic estimate of the infinite-time ruin probability.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
A={\bf l}-L\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set. If for all
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\xi(s)}\,p_{i}(s)\,ds<\infty

almost surely, then

(i)

under the conditions of Theorem
3.3
we obtain (
5.13
) with
T=\infty
.

(ii)

under the conditions of Corollary
3.2
we obtain (
5.14
) with
T=\infty
.

Proof.

We need to show only the item
(i)
, since the argumentation for the item
(ii)
is quite similar, in view of Corollary
3.2
. From relation (
5.18
) and by Theorem
3.3
, we have that

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,\infty)\leq\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A\right]\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-\xi(s)}\in x\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

which implies the desired upper bound for relation (
5.13
) with
T=\infty
.

Further, for the lower bound, we consider some positive and arbitrarily large
M_{0}<\infty
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},L}(x;\,\infty)\geq\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(\infty)-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha\,\xi(s)}\,{\bf p}(s)\,ds\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(\infty)-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha\,\xi(s)}\,{\bf p}(s)\,ds\in x\,A,\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha\,\xi(s)}\,{\bf p}(s)\,ds\leq{\bf M_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A+{\bf M_{0}}\right]\mathbf{P}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha\,\xi(s)}\,{\bf p}(s)\,ds\leq{\bf M_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-\xi(s)}\in x\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

(5.19)

where we used the same technique applied in (
5
), Theorem
3.3
, and let
M_{0}\to\infty
in the last step.
Therefore, relation (
5
) provides the lower bound for relation (
5.13
) with
T=\infty
, as we needed.
∎

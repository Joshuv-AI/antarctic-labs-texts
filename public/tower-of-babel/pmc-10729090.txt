Ischaemic stroke in patients with diabetes requiring urgent procedures during the COVID-19 pandemic in South Korea: a retrospective, nationwide, population-based cohort study using data from the National Emergency Department Information System
Authors: Min Jeong Park, Jeongeun Hwang, Jonghwa Ahn, Sung Joon Park, Eyun Song, Ahreum Jang, Kyung Mook Choi, Sei Hyun Baik, Hye Jin Yoo
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThe COVID-19 pandemic resulted in suboptimal care for ischaemic stroke. Patients with diabetes mellitus (DM), a high-risk group for stroke, had compromised routine care during the pandemic, which increases the chance of stroke. We examined influence of the COVID-19 pandemic on the management of ischaemic stroke in patients with DM in South Korea.DesignRetrospective, nationwide, population-based cohort study.SettingData from the National Emergency Department Information System.ParticipantsWe analysed 11 734 patients diagnosed with acute ischaemic stroke who underwent intravenous thrombolysis or endovascular thrombectomy between 2019 (the reference year) and 2020 (the pandemic year). Among them, 1014 subjects with DM were analysed separately.Outcome measuresThe frequency of emergency department (ED) visits, time from symptom onset to ED, from ED visit to admission and in-hospital mortality were compared between two periods in the overall population and in patients with DM.ResultsDuring the pandemic, the incidence of ischaemic stroke requiring urgent procedures increased by 7.57% in total and by 9.03% in patients with DM. Time delay from symptom onset to ED (reference vs pandemic, total: 1.50 vs 1.55 hours; p<0.01) and from ED visit to admission (total: 3.88 vs 3.92 hours; p=0.02) occurred during the pandemic in the overall population, but not significantly in patients with DM specifically. Older patients with DM showed higher chances of intensive care unit (ICU) admission during the pandemic: 53.5% vs 62.8% in age 70―79, 60.5% vs 71.9% in age 80―89 and 20.0% vs 70.8% in age ≥90 years (all p=0.01). There was no significant difference in in-hospital mortality between two periods (total: 8.2% vs 8.4%, p=0.65; DM: 8.1% vs 6.7%, p=0.25).ConclusionsDuring the COVID-19 pandemic, the incidence of ischaemic stroke requiring urgent procedures increased, and older patients with DM showed a higher ICU admission rate. However, the pandemic was not associated with an increased in-hospital stroke mortality.Keywords: COVID-19, stroke, accident & emergency medicine, diabetes & endocrinology

Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic not only increased virus-associated mortality by as much as 2.74 times1 but also led to healthcare service-related behavioural changes, mainly due to the fear of viral contagion, strict COVID-19 screening tests for obtaining medical services and the temporary closure of hospitals due to healthcare worker infection.2 Recent studies reported a reduced volume of emergency department (ED) visits and hospital admissions during the COVID-19 lockdown period.3 In particular, emergency medical diseases that require an immediate diagnosis and interventions have been affected by this global disaster, with reports of a 38% reduction in cardiac catheterisation for myocardial infarction (MI) in the USA,4 an increase in in-hospital MI mortality from 5.1% to 7.5% in Spain5 and a 6.5% increase in out-of-hospital cardiac arrests in Italy.6 Ischaemic stroke is an important emergency disease for which urgent care within the therapeutic time range is essential, as timely thrombolysis or thrombectomy for eligible patients results in significantly favourable clinical outcomes.7 Unfortunately, a European group reported an approximately 40% reduction in stroke alerts, a time delay in the prehospital phase and a one-third reduction in revascularisation therapy during the COVID-19 pandemic.8

Patients with diabetes mellitus (DM) are a well-known high-risk group for ischaemic stroke, and compared with those without DM, exhibit an approximately twofold chance of experiencing it.9 Furthermore, the population with DM is especially vulnerable to COVID-19.10 11 Multiple recent systematic reviews have indicated that COVID-19 predisposes particularly patients with pre-existing DM to ischaemic stroke.12 13 Although consistent glycaemic control and efforts to prevent complications are critical for patients with DM, this devastating pandemic impedes patients from using their routine healthcare system. Risk of diabetes complication might have been enhanced in the dragged period of COVID-19 pandemic consequently. Indeed, a recent study reported increased non-COVID-19-related mortality in patients with DM, which possibly resulted from interruption of regular diabetes care.14 Therefore, it is important to evaluate increased rates of diabetes-related morbidity between before and after the pandemic while identifying factors affecting deteriorated results. However, no studies have investigated the impact of COVID-19 on ischaemic stroke management in patients with DM in South Korea using a nationwide emergency care network database, which includes information on the time of acute care, rate of procedures and management outcomes.

In this study, we evaluated the incidence of acute ischaemic stroke in the general population and patients with DM who required immediate interventions through the ED during the COVID-19 pandemic. We also compared the outcomes of stroke management and behavioural changes in healthcare service utilisation between the pre-COVID-19 and COVID-19 periods in the total population and patients with DM.

Methods

Data source

We used registry data from the National Emergency Department Information System (NEDIS) between 1 January 2019 and 31 December 2020. NEDIS is a nationwide emergency care network database that prospectively collects demographic and clinical data of patients visiting Korean regional and local regional emergency centres and local emergency medical institutions. All emergency medical institutions must transfer data through an electrical medical record system, and the National Emergency Medical Center manages the registered data. Data from 401 of 402 emergency medical institutions in 2019 and 403 of 403 in 2020 are included in the NEDIS database.

Study design and outcome

Initially, the data were collected from patients who visited the local and regional emergency medical centres between 1 January 2019 and 31 December 2020, and who were discharged from the ED or wards for acute cerebral ischaemic stroke (International Classification of Diseases 10th Revision (ICD-10) code I63). Among the 14 846 patients, 7 under the age of 20 years, 3042 without an electronic data interchange (EDI) code for intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) or endovascular thrombectomy (EVT), and 63 with missing data were excluded. To improve precise selection of acute-onset ischaemic stroke and to complement limitation of defining patients based on code, we limited the study participants to those who underwent stroke procedures and visited the centre-class of emergency facility to exclude patients with previous infarctions and to increase data accuracy. The EDI codes for IVT, EVT and intubation are listed in online supplemental table 1. A total of 11 734 patients were included in the final analysis: 6081 patients were allocated to the COVID-19 pandemic period and 5653 patients were assigned to the reference period (figure 1). To compare outcomes and management of ischaemic stroke between reference and pandemic periods in patients with DM, we performed all analyses in patients with DM separately. We defined patients with DM as those who were discharged with at least one of the following ICD-10 codes: E10, E11, E12, E13 or E14.

We defined the COVID-19 pandemic period as 1 January 2020–31 December 2020 and the reference period as 1 January 2019–31 December 2019. As the first patient with COVID-19 was confirmed in January 2020 in South Korea, the Korean government raised the infectious disease crisis level in February 2020. Considering the seasonal variation in stroke incidence and severity, we compared the entire year, from January to December. Furthermore, we subdivided a year into four periods based on the number of confirmed COVID-19 cases—first wave (weeks 7–16), post-first wave (weeks 17–30), second wave (weeks 31–42) and third wave (weeks 43–53)—and compared the number of ED visits between the COVID-19 pandemic and reference periods.

For disease severity, we compared the number of in-hospital deaths, number of hospital days, distribution after emergency care and application rate of mechanical ventilation. Patients visiting the ED were distributed as follows depending on disease severity and medical resource requirements: death, transfer to other hospitals due to a shortage of medical resources, admission to the general ward and admission to the intensive care unit (ICU). In particular, we estimated the percentage and number of patients admitted to the ICU according to the age decile groups during the COVID-19 pandemic and reference periods. We evaluated behavioural changes in the use of healthcare services with ‘the time from symptom onset to ED visit’, ‘hours of ED stay’, ‘time from ED visit to admission’ and ‘treatment modalities of stroke management’, such as IVT, EVT and mechanical ventilation.

Statistical analyses

Two-proportion Z-tests were performed for equality of proportions to compare rates, including those of age groups, sex, distribution after emergencies, EVT, IVT, intubation and in-hospital mortality. Numerical variables, including blood pressure, pulse rate, respiratory rate and body temperature, were compared using Welch’s two-sample t-tests. Mann-Whitney U tests with continuity correction were performed to compare continuous variables, including time from symptom onset to ED visit, hours of ED stay, time from ED visit to admission and duration of hospital stay. The R statistics software V.4.1.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria)15 and R packages, including ggplot2,16 lubridate,17 readxl18 and dplyr,19 were used for the statistical analyses.

Patient and public involvement

Results

Frequency of ED visits by patients with ischaemic stroke requiring urgent procedures

Although the total number of patients visiting the ED decreased from 6 146 688 in the reference period to 4 809 661 in the COVID-19 pandemic period, the frequency of visits to the ED due to ischaemic stroke requiring urgent procedures increased by 7.57% during the COVID-19 pandemic period (5653 vs 6081). The proportion of ED visits by patients with ischaemic stroke among total patients visiting ED in these two periods was 0.09% (5653 of 6 146 688) and 0.12% (6081 of 4 809 661), respectively (p<0.001). Overall, 1014 patients with DM visited the ED for managing stroke during the COVID-19 pandemic period, whereas 930 did so during the reference period with 9.03% increase rate (p<0.001). The frequencies of ED visits due to ischaemic stroke during the COVID-19 pandemic and reference periods are plotted in figure 2. There was a significant increase in the mean frequency of weekly ED visits for stroke during the post-first wave in the pandemic year, compared with that of the reference year, as shown in figure 2A (118 vs 109, p=0.04). In the population with DM, an apparent increase in the frequency of mean ED visits by week was noted between week 13 and week 19, which was the period spanning the latter part of the first COVID-19 wave and the beginning of the post-first wave (figure 2B).

Comparison of ischaemic stroke management between the COVID-19 pandemic and reference periods

The characteristics of the study participants are compared between the COVID-19 pandemic and reference periods in table 1. There was no age discrepancy between the two periods in the total patients and those with DM. In the COVID-19 pandemic period, compared with the reference period, a higher percentage of men presented to the ED and underwent procedures for ischaemic stroke (COVID-19 period: 62.3% vs reference period: 60.4%, p=0.04). In the total population, there was some delay in time from symptom onset to ED visit (COVID-19 period: 1.55 hours (0.82, 4.00), reference period: 1.50 hours (0.73, 3.77), p<0.01), in time from ED visit to admission (COVID-19 period: 3.92 hours (2.92, 5.52), reference period: 3.88 hours (2.90, 5.33), p=0.02) and in hours of ED stay (COVID-19 period: 2.87 hours (1.77, 4.95), reference period: 2.87 hours (1.70, 4.75), p=0.03) in the COVID-19 pandemic period, compared with the reference period. Despite statistical significance, the actual time difference between two periods was less than 5 minutes, which would not lead to a significant difference in clinical outcomes. Likewise, patients with DM did not show significant differences in the transition time from symptom onset to ED visit, in time from ED visit to admission and ED stay between these two periods. During the pandemic, the proportion of patients who underwent IVT significantly increased in the total population (COVID-19 period: 28.6%, reference period: 25.7%, p<0.01). Patients with DM showed similar increase in IVT proportion during the pandemic period, even though statistical significance was not seen due to the small number of patients with DM (COVID-19 period: 26.8%, reference period: 24.3%, p=0.20). The percentage of patients who underwent EVT decreased in both the total population (COVID-19 period: 80.4%, reference period: 83.1%, p<0.01) and population with DM (COVID-19 pandemic period: 80.4%, reference period: 85.2%, p<0.01). Furthermore, fewer patients underwent mechanical ventilation during the COVID-19 pandemic period than during the reference period in both the total (COVID-19 pandemic period: 12.2%, reference period: 13.6%, p=0.02) and DM groups (COVID-19 pandemic period: 11.9%, reference period: 17.3%, p<0.01).

Comparison of treatment outcomes for ischaemic stroke between the COVID-19 pandemic and reference periods

Although the percentages of patients discharged at ED due to death, hospital stay and in-hospital mortality were not significantly different between the COVID-19 pandemic period and the reference period, the rate of ICU admission was significantly increased during the COVID-19 pandemic period in the total population (COVID-19 pandemic period: 62.5%, reference period: 59.3%, p<0.01) and population with DM (COVID-19 pandemic period: 63.6%, reference period: 57.8%, p=0.03) (table 1). Figure 3 shows a comparison of the number of patients admitted to the ICU according to the age decile group and DM status. The total population showed the trend of increased ICU admission rate with statistical significance only in their 70s (COVID-19 pandemic vs reference period: 61.6% vs 55.3%, p<0.01), whereas in the population with DM, the rate of ICU admission was remarkably increased in older patients aged over 70 years (COVID-19 pandemic vs reference period: 62.8% vs 53.5% in age 70―79; p=0.01, 71.9% vs 60.5% in age 80―89; p=0.01 and 70.8% vs 20.0% in age ≥90 years; p=0.01).

Discussion

This study analysed nationwide data from emergency care facilities in Korea to evaluate the patterns and clinical outcomes of ischaemic stroke in general patients and those with DM who visited the ED during the COVID-19 pandemic. Although the total number of ED visits decreased during the pandemic, the number of patients with ischaemic stroke requiring acute interventions significantly increased both in total patients and in patients with DM. There was a slight time delay from symptom onset to ED visit, ED stay and ED arrival to admission in the total population, but the time difference was not huge enough to affect clinical outcomes. There was no significant time delay of those parameters in patients with DM either. Furthermore, the ICU admission rate significantly increased during the COVID-19 pandemic period, which was more prominent in patients with DM older than 70 years. Nevertheless, these alterations did not escalate in-hospital mortality due to ischaemic stroke both in the total population and in patients with DM during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Compared with our research results, studies from other countries demonstrated fewer ED visits, admissions or procedures for acute stroke during the COVID-19 pandemic.20–23 Two recent multicentre studies across more than 50 countries reported a decline in stroke admissions during the COVID-19 period.22 23 Tejada Meza et al illustrated that in-hospital stroke mortality increased in Spain during the outbreak,24 and a group from France reported a lower revascularisation rate during the pandemic period due to a decreased number of patients arriving at the hospital within the therapeutic time window.8 Several studies have illustrated a distinct inverse relationship between the number of stroke and COVID-19 cases.24 25 Meanwhile, our data showed increased ED visits for stroke for thrombolysis or thrombectomy and similar rates of mortality and hospital stay during the pandemic period. A Korean study using data from a single special stroke centre also reported no time delay of symptom onset to hospital visit, door-to-puncture time, or rate of intravenous or intra-arterial intervention during the pandemic.26 Another Korean study comparing the incidence of cerebral and cardiovascular diseases between the COVID-19 pandemic and previous years showed no statistical difference in monthly cerebral infarct events.27 The reason for the different results between Korea and other countries was probably due to the relatively lower burden of COVID-19 cases at the early stage in Korea, compared with that in other countries.28 The cumulative COVID-19 incidence rates divided by the number of each country’s population at the end of June 2020 in Korea, the UK, the USA, Italy and Spain were 0.025%, 0.427%, 0.103%, 0.406% and 0.548%, and that at the end of December 2020 were 0.124%, 4.058%, 0.416%, 3.602% and 4.262%.29 30 Also, the immediate adoption of a hospital redistribution strategy might have affected these results. Based on experiences from the 2015 Middle East respiratory syndrome outbreak in Korea, the Korean government designated a dedicated COVID-19 hospital, COVID-19 emergency centre and COVID-19 community facility to prevent the shutdown of hospitals due to nosocomial infections and to provide consistent healthcare services for patients without COVID-19.28 Rigorous quarantine regulations and reorganisation of healthcare services allowed the provision of medical resources to those who needed acute diagnosis and interventions in Korea.

Notably, there was an alteration in the proportion of treatment modalities for ischaemic stroke during the COVID-19 period. Patients with ischaemic stroke should satisfy several criteria to be candidates for intravenous thrombolytic therapy, including a therapeutic time window of 4.5 hours from the onset of symptoms, age, symptoms, no history of trauma or haemorrhage, and modest blood pressure.31 Appropriate weight-based doses of alteplase or tenecteplase should be administered through intravenous lines.32 Mechanical thrombectomy is the treatment of choice for ischaemic stroke caused by large artery occlusion with interrupted anterior or posterior circulation; patients are transferred to the procedure room within 24 hours from the last normal time, regardless of pretreatment with IVT.33 EVT procedures require arterial puncture and catheterisation with a stent retriever and/or aspiration device. Contrary to many other countries that reported a reduction in IVT volume and no noticeable difference in the number of EVT cases,22 23 we reported an increase in the number and rate of IVT. The number of EVT cases also increased, but the proportion was lower than that of IVT during the COVID-19 period. As we expected, time delays were not significant to induce fatal postponement beyond the therapeutic window for IVT or EVT in the Korean healthcare system. However, the number of patients receiving mechanical ventilation decreased during the COVID-19 period. This might be caused by a shortage of ventilators due to an increase in the number of patients with pneumonia requiring oxygen therapy or by a conservative approach to procedures requiring close contact during the pandemic period. Other countries also indicated that patients who needed mechanical ventilation received insufficient therapy due to preoccupation by critically ill patients with COVID-19.34

The COVID-19 infection itself increases the risk of ischaemic stroke11 based on its mechanism of impaired coagulation with systemic inflammation and endothelial activation.13 The presence of DM in patients with COVID-19 increases the fatality rate and risk of thromboembolism through the production of oxidative stress and cytokines.11 Evidence has revealed that unregulated high glucose levels contribute to the rapid progression of COVID-19 and related comorbidities.35 During the pandemic period, many patients with chronic medical conditions postponed periodical follow-ups, and the general population put off regular health screening for concerns regarding viral infections. Lifestyle control was disrupted by the avoidance of public sports facilities, an unhealthy diet and emotional stress. UK data reflect these circumstances, demonstrating a reduced diagnosis of DM, a decline in evaluation for glycaemic control and an increase in non-COVID-19-related deaths in patients with DM.36 In accordance with the literature, our study showed a significant increase in ICU admission rates in patients with DM during the COVID-19 pandemic period. Moreover, the impact of old age further increases the number of ICU admissions in patients with DM. These results suggest that, since patients with DM are more susceptible to COVID-19-related morbidity, including stroke, and have worse disease outcomes than those without, tracking routine medical check-ups and maintaining lifestyle self-management are essential for patients with DM even during the pandemic period.

Our study has a few limitations. The number of COVID-19-confirmed patients in Korea significantly increased in three distinct periods, and the third wave of the COVID-19 outbreak spanned from November 2020 to January 2021. However, only data up to December 2020 were available from the NEDIS at the time of study initiation, and we could not include patients from 2021. In addition, we limited our study to patients who underwent thrombolysis or thrombectomy to clarify the study participants as those with acute-onset ischaemic stroke. Since we sorted patient data based on disease codes at discharge from the ward or ED, we tried to exclude patients who had a stroke code related to a history of infarction or an accidentally discovered minor radiological infarct without symptoms. Nevertheless, this study is the first to use a nationwide emergency care network database in Korea to evaluate acute stroke management during the COVID-19 pandemic. Comprehensive nationwide data allowed researchers to investigate behavioural characteristics of emergency care service utilisation during the pandemic, as well as compare morbidity and mortality outcomes between the pre-COVID-19 and COVID-19 periods. Moreover, our study provides a profound examination of stroke risk and disease outcomes in patients with DM in Korea.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our research investigated the increase in ED visits during the pandemic period for acute ischaemic stroke in the total population and patients with DM. We focused on the population who had a stroke in need of prompt procedures for optimal care. This study found that treatment results for acute ischaemic stroke in Korea were well maintained both in general and patients with DM during the COVID-19 pandemic, despite challenging alterations in the environment of ED facilities. Furthermore, patients with DM showed an increased rate of ICU admission for stroke during the pandemic, which emphasises the significance of consistent and rigorous care for chronic metabolic diseases for the prevention of non-COVID-19-related diseases.

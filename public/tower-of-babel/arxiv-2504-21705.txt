Abelian fiber spaces and their Tate-Shafarevich twists with sections
János Kollár

Abstract
. Starting with an Abelian fiber space, the aim is to construct a Tate-Shafarevich twist that has a rational section.



Kodaira showed that to any elliptic surface
S\to C
without multiple fibers one can associate its relative Jacobian surface
\operatorname{Jac}(S/C)\to C
, which has the same local structure, and also a section.
Generalizations to elliptic
n
-folds and to hyperkähler manifolds are studied in
[
Nak91
,
DG94
,
Gro94
,
Gro97
,
Nak02b
,
Nak02a
,
DCS21
,
CDCH+21
,
BDCS24
,
Sac25
,
BKV25
,
Kim24
]
; see (
14
).
This note considers Abelian fiber spaces in general.

Definition 1

(Abelian torsors and fiber spaces)
.

An
Abelian variety
over a field
k
is a smooth,
proper, connected algebraic group
A_{k}
over
k
.
An
Abelian torsor

X_{k}
is a principal homogenous space under an Abelian variety, which can be given by
A_{k}=\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}^{\circ}(X_{k})\cong\operatorname{\mathbf{Pic}}^{\circ}\bigl(\operatorname{\mathbf{Pic}}^{\circ}(X_{k})\bigr)
.

A
fiber space
is a
proper morphism
\pi:X\to Z
between normal, irreducible, algebraic spaces, such that

\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}={\mathcal{O}}_{Z}
.
It is an
Abelian fiber space
if
general fibers are Abelian torsors.

Definition 2

(Twisted forms)
.

A
twisted form
of a morphism
\pi:X\to Z
is obtained by the following procedure.
Take an open cover
\amalg_{i}U_{i}\to Z
with
U_{ij}:=U_{i}\times_{Z}U_{j}
, and set
X_{i}:=X\times_{Z}U_{i}
and

X_{ij}:=X\times_{Z}U_{ij}
. Choose
isomorphisms (of
U_{ij}
-schemes)

[formula]

\phi_{ij}:X_{ij}\cong X_{ji},\quad\mbox{such that}\quad\phi_{jk}\circ\phi_{ij}=\phi_{ik}.

Use these to patch the
X_{i}
together to get a morphism

\pi^{\Phi}:X^{\Phi}\to Z
.

Over
{\mathbb{C}}
we use the Euclidean topology, in general the étale topology.
These are also called
étale
or
Tate-Shafarevich
twists.

A twisted form of an Abelian fiber space is also an
Abelian fiber space.

Our aim is to prove that, under some natural conditions,
Abelian fiber spaces have a twist with a rational section.
For clarity, we state first a weaker form over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
After further definitions, the final version over
{\mathbb{C}}
is given in Theorem
8
.
The general result (
40
) over arbitrary base schemes is very similar, but needs more definitions; see (
38
) and (
39
).

Assumption 3
.

Let
\pi:X\to Z
be an Abelian fiber space over
{\mathbb{C}}
, such that

X
has log terminal singularities,
K_{X}
is numerically
\pi
-trivial,
\pi
is locally projective (in the Euclidean or étale topology) and of pure relative dimension
d
.

Note that in characteristic 0, any Abelian fiber space is birational to some
\pi:X\to Z
such that
\pi
is projective,
X
has terminal singularities, and
K_{X}
is numerically
\pi
-trivial; see (
38
.3
′
) for details. So these 3 are mild assumptions. (Note that terminal implies log terminal.)
Having
pure
relative dimension holds if
X
is hyperkähler or if
\dim Z\leq 2
, but not in general.

Theorem 4

(Untwisting of Abelian fiber spaces)
.

Let
\pi:X\to Z
be an Abelian fiber space satisfying
Assumption
3
.
Let
\eta\in H^{2d}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
be a cohomology class such that
\eta\cap[\mbox{general fiber}]=1
.

Then there is a canonical construction yielding a twisted form

[formula]

\pi_{\eta}:\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)\to Z\quad\mbox{with a rational section}\quad s_{\eta}:Z\dasharrow\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z).

The construction commutes with smooth base changes.

For fixed
X,Z
, the different
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
are birational to each other, but for
\dim Z\geq 2
one can get infinitely many different ones up to isomorphism, see (
18
).

The existence of such
\eta
is the higher dimensional version of having no multiple fibers. It is an almost necessary condition, see Comment
9
for a precise statement.

5
Plan of the proof.

We construct
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
locally on
Z
, and patch the pieces together. We start with the latter.

(
5
.1)
Patching.
Assume that
Z
has an open cover
Z=\cup_{i}U_{i}
, and over each
U_{i}
we have a rational section
s_{i}:U_{i}\dasharrow X_{i}:=\pi^{-1}U_{i}
. As in (
2
.1),
the patching is given by isomorphisms

\phi_{ij}:X_{ij}\cong X_{ji}

such that
s_{j}=\phi_{ij}\circ s_{i}
on
U_{ij}
.

If the
\phi_{ij}
are unique, then the cocyle conditions
\phi_{jk}\circ\phi_{ij}=\phi_{ik}
are automatic. How can we achieve this?

Let
z\in U_{i}\cap U_{j}
be a point such that the fiber
X_{z}
is smooth.
The
\phi_{ij}
that we aim to construct should restrict to an isomorphism

\phi_{ij}|_{z}:\bigl(X_{z},s_{i}(z)\bigr)\cong\bigl(X_{z},s_{j}(z)\bigr)
.
Since
X_{z}
is homogeneous,
such an isomorphism exists, but it is not unique. However, there is a
unique such translation-isomorphism, namely translation by
s_{j}(z)-s_{i}(z)
.
Thus the cocyle conditions are automatic if we use only translation-isomorphisms for the
\phi_{ij}
.

(
5
.2)
Translation-isomorphisms.
The notion of translation is more problematic for singular fibers, but in
Definitions
6
and
24
we establish the group of translation-isomorphisms
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)
.
Sheafifying in the étale topology gives
{\mathchanc{Aut}}^{\circ}(X/Z)
.

(
5
.3)
Changing
\eta
.
These consideration also suggest
that the right place for
\eta
to live is not in
H^{2d}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
,
but in
H^{0}(Z,R^{2d}\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Z}}_{X})
. Restriction gives a natural map

H^{2d}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{0}(Z,R^{2d}\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Z}}_{X}).

This is an isomorphism if
Z
is a germ, but otherwise
the target can be much larger, so working with

H^{0}(Z,R^{2d}\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Z}}_{X})
gives a richer theory.
See also Comment
9
.

The local problem now becomes the following.

(
5
.4)
Local uniqueness.

For a germ
z\in U\subset Z
and
\eta\in H^{2d}(X_{U},{\mathbb{Z}})
, we want a canonical way to specify a set of
rational sections
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)=\{s:U\dasharrow X_{U}\}
that forms a single
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X_{U}/U)
-orbit. We discuss this in more detail in Paragraph
10
.

Once this is done, the local patches automatically glue to give the global
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
.

(
5
.5)
Arbitrary bases.

We replace the analytic germs with the strict Henselizations of the local rings, but otherwise the plan is the same.

Definition 6

(Automorphisms)
.

For a morphism
\pi:X\to Z
, we let
\operatorname{Aut}(X/Z)
denote the group of automorphism of
X
that satisfy
\pi=\pi\circ\phi
.

By base change, it defines a functor
V\mapsto\operatorname{Aut}(X\times_{Z}V/V)
. If this functor is represented by a group scheme (or algebraic space), we denote it by

\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}(X/Z)
or
\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}(X/Z)\to Z
. By a standard Hilbert scheme argument, representability holds if

\pi
is proper and flat
[
Kol96
, I.1.10]
.
If
\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}(X/Z)\to Z
is smooth, let
\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}^{\circ}(X/Z)\subset\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}(X/Z)
denote the identity component.

We would like
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)
to be the global sections of
\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}^{\circ}(X/Z)
.
Unfortunately,
\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}(X/Z)
does not exists in general, and even if it does,

\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}(X/Z)
is frequently not even flat over
Z
.

Nonetheless, if
\pi:X\to Z
is an Abelian fiber space, in (
24
) we define
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)
and the corresponding
sheaf
{\mathchanc{Aut}}^{\circ}(X/Z)
.

Note on notation.
The papers

[
AR23
,
Kim24
]

define
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)
for hyperkähler manifolds. The various definitions most likely agree when they all apply.

Definition 7

(Translation-automorphisms and twists)
.

Let
\pi:X\to Z
be an Abelian fiber space.
Since
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)
acts by translations on general fibers,
we call it the group of

translation-automorphisms.
It is a commutative group.

A twisted form of
\pi
is
translation-twisted
if
the patching maps
\phi_{ij}
in (
2
.1) are
translation-automorphisms.

The stronger version of Theorem
4
is the following.

Theorem 8
.

Let
\pi:X\to Z
be an Abelian fiber space satisfying
Assumption
3
.
Let
\eta\in H^{0}(Z,R^{2d}\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Z}}_{X})
be a cohomology class such that
\eta\cap[\mbox{general fiber}]=1
.

Then
\pi_{\eta}:\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)\to Z
is a translation-twisted form of
X
,
with a rational section
s_{\eta}:Z\dasharrow\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
.

Comment 9
.

If
X
is smooth, then the existence of such an

\eta\in H^{0}(Z,R^{2d}\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Z}}_{X})
is necessary for
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
to exist.

Indeed, then
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
is also smooth, so the closure of

s_{\eta}(Z)
defines a cohomology class
\eta^{\prime}\in H^{2d}\bigl(\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z),{\mathbb{Z}}\bigr)
such that
\eta^{\prime}\cap[\mbox{general fiber}]=1
.

We see in (
46
) that
R^{2d}\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Z}}
is the same for
X
and for

\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
. Thus
\eta^{\prime}
is identified with an

\eta\in H^{0}(Z,R^{2d}\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Z}}_{X})
such that
\eta\cap[\mbox{general fiber}]=1
.

However, if
X
is singular, then
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
sometimes exists even if there is no such
\eta
.

10
Local case of Theorem
4
.

Unfortunately I do not have an a priori definition of what the ‘canonically constructed’
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
should be.
Instead, we give a procedure to construct it, which goes as follows.

(
10
.1)
General fibers.

Using only translation-isomorphisms
gives the following over an open, dense set:

Let
Z^{\circ}\subset Z
be a dense, open set such that

\pi^{\circ}:X^{\circ}\to Z^{\circ}
is smooth, hence its fibers are
Abelian torsors. We take

\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)|_{Z^{\circ}}:=\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}^{\circ}(X^{\circ}/Z^{\circ})
1
1

1

In many papers this is called the
Albanese fibration of
X^{\circ}\to Z^{\circ}
.
I do not follow this terminology, since
it is not consistent with
[
Gro62
, VI.3.3]
, according to which the Albanese variety is the target of the universal map to an Abelian
torsor.

Thus, by Grothendieck’s definition, the Albanese of
X^{\circ}\to Z^{\circ}

is itself.
.
This is independent of
\eta
, so the different
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
are birational to each other.

(
10
.2)
Codimension 1 fibers.
Assume for simplicity that
X
is smooth, and
(z,Z)
is a smooth curve germ.
Then any section
s:Z\to X
is regular, and
s(z)
is a smooth point of the fiber
X_{z}
. Thus, in order to specify
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
,
we need to choose a local section
s:Z\to X
. Which one?

For minimal elliptic surfaces the local analytic automorphism group acts transitively on the set of local sections, hence all choices lead to isomorphic
objects.
(This is why
\eta
does not play a role for elliptic surfaces.) However, this fails in higher dimensions; see for example
[
Kol25
, 21]
. The best one can say is that by
[
Kol25
, Thm.1]
, the local analytic automorphism group acts transitively on the set of local sections that pass through the same irreducible component of the fiber
X_{z}
.

Thus, if
X
is smooth and
(z,Z)
is a smooth curve germ, then a choice of a section
s:Z\to X
up to
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)

is equivalent to a choice of a reduced
irreducible component of the central fiber
X_{z}
.
If
X
is singular, it is better to view this as choosing an
irreducible component of the central fiber of the Néron model of
X\to Z
(
21
).

If
X
is arbitrary and
\dim Z\geq 2
, we need a consistent way to pick an
irreducible component of each fiber of the Néron model, which is defined outside a codimension
\geq 2
subset
Z_{2}\subset Z
.

It turns out that one can use
\eta
to do this (in a somewhat convoluted way), but
\eta
carries much more information than what was needed so far.

(
10
.3)
Higher codimension fibers for elliptic 3-folds.

The papers
[
Rei83
,
Nak91
,
DG94
,
Gro94
,
Gro97
,
Nak02b
,
Nak02a
]
describe the situation for elliptic 3-folds
X\to Z
.
The previous considerations determine
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}(X/Z)
except over finitely many points
z_{i}\in Z
, but there are usually infinitely many ways to extend to twisted forms
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}(X/Z)\to Z
. The different models are related to each other by sequences of flops. Choosing a relatively ample line bundle then
specifies an extension uniquely. The Chern class of a line bundle is in
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
, and the same for our
\eta
. They are actually not the same, but have the same role. See (
17
–
19
) for examples.

(
10
.4)
Higher codimension fibers in general.

Minimal model theory would suggest that even for higher fiber dimensions, one should choose a relatively ample line bundle to get a unique model.

More generally, choosing an effective divisor
D
that is ample on the generic fiber, relative MMP for
(X,\epsilon D)
results in a model
\pi^{c}:(X^{c},\epsilon D^{c})\to Z
such that

D^{c}
is
\pi^{c}
-ample. However,
X^{c}\to Z
is usually not a twist of
X\to Z
; sometimes it is not even equidimensional.

This is where the role of
\eta\in H^{2d}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
fully emerges.

Assume for simplicity that
\pi
is flat,
(z,Z)
is a germ, and let

X_{z}=\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}F_{i}
be the central fiber.
The assumption
\eta\cap[\mbox{general fiber}]=1
is equivalent to

\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}e_{i}=1
, where
e_{i}:=\eta\cap[F_{i}]\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

A general local complete intersection of codimension
d
through a smooth point of
F_{i}
gives a multisection
W_{i}
of degree
m_{i}
of
X\to Z
.
Intersecting with the generic fiber
X_{K}
gives a 0-cycle
[W_{i}]_{K}
of degree
m_{i}
.
Thus
\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}[W_{i}]_{K}
is a 0-cycle of degree
1
on
X_{K}
.
Since
X_{K}
is a torsor, we cannot add points. However, pretending that we can, we get a well-defined point as the ‘sum’ of any 0-cycle of degree
1
; see
(
26
) for details.
That is, the formal sum
\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}W_{i}
is transformed into a rational section of
X\to Z
.

Going through all possible choices of the
W_{i}
leads to a set of rational sections, denoted by
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
; see (
29
).

The key—and quite unexpected—result is that
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
forms a single

\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)
orbit (
31
).

(
10
.5)
Construction of automorphisms.

The set of rational sections
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
is large. Thus it can form a single

\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)
orbit only if
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)
is large. So we need to construct many automorphisms.

A rational section
s:Z\dasharrow X
is identified with a
k(Z)
-point of the generic fiber. Thus, given rational sections
s_{1},s_{2}:Z\dasharrow X
,
translation by
s_{2}-s_{1}
gives a birational map
\phi_{12}:X\dasharrow X
such that

s_{2}=\phi_{12}\circ s_{1}
.

For minimal elliptic surfaces
\phi_{12}
is an automorphism, and we are done.
However,
\phi_{12}
is usually not an automorphism for
\dim X\geq 3
, not even if
X
is smooth. In (
31
) we prove that
\phi_{12}
is an automorphism whenever

s_{1},s_{2}
are in the same
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
. The arguments rely on

[
Kol25
, Thm.1]
over codimension 1 points of
Z
, and on

[
Kol25
, 37]
over higher codimension points.

If
\dim Z\geq 2
, then
s_{\eta}
is rarely a regular section, but
(
10
.4) shows the following.

Corollary 11
.

Using the notation and assumptions of Theorem
4
,
assume in addition that every geometric fiber has a unique irreducible component of multiplicity 1. Choose
\eta
to be 1 on that component, and 0 on the others. Then
s_{\eta}:Z\to\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
is a section. ∎

12
Properties of
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
.

Many properties of
X
are inherited by twists and translation-twists; these are discussed in Section
6
, see especially (
46
).

By the above construction

\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
is an algebraic space, and
\pi_{\eta}
is proper and locally projective.

For the most general version of
\eta
considered in (
27
) and (
39
),
it can happen that
X
is projective but
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
is not; see
(
18
).

It is possible that
X
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
are much closer to each other if
\eta\in H^{2d}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Question 13
.

Using the notation and assumptions of Theorem
4
,
assume in addition that
X
is projective and

\eta\in H^{2d}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

(13.1)

Is
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
projective?

(13.2)

Is
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)\to Z
homeomorphic to
X\to Z
?

(13.3)

Is
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)\to Z
deformation equivalent to
X\to Z
?

1.
Examples

As we noted in (
10
.6), they key step is to prove Theorem
4
when
Z
is local. This is in turn equivalent to
identifying a set of rational sections
\operatorname{RatSec}(X,\eta)
forming a
single orbit under translation-automorphisms.

The proof gives a procedure to construct
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
, and in many cases it gives a good description of the resulting rational sections over codimension 1 points of
Z
. However, it seems difficult to describe the singularities of the sections over codimension
\geq 2
points.

Here we discuss a few cases for which a reasonably complete description of
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
is known.

Example 15
.

Assume that
\pi
is flat, the fiber
X_{z}
has a reduced component
F
,
\eta\cap[F]=1
and
\eta\cap[\mbox{other components}]=0
.

Then
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
consist of those sections
s:Z\to X
for which

s(z)\in F
is a smooth point of
X_{z}
.

Example 16
.

Assume that
X
is smooth and
\dim Z=1
.
By
[
Kol25
, 2.1]
the
Néron model is isomorphic to the smooth locus of
X\to Z
.

If the fiber
X_{z}=\sum_{i\in I}F_{i}
is reduced, then
the group of connected components of
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X/Z)_{z}
acts simply transitively on
I
. Let
[i]
denote the elements of
I
. Since
\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}=1
,
\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}[i]=[i_{\eta}]
for some
i_{\eta}\in I
.

Then
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
consists of
those sections
s:Z\to X
for which

s(z)\in F_{i_{\eta}}
is a smooth point of
X_{z}
.

The cohomology class of these sections is usually different from
\eta
.

If
X_{z}
has some nonreduced components, then again

\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
consists of sections through a reduced component
F_{i_{\eta}}
, but it is
less clear how
i_{\eta}
is determined.

Thus, if
\dim Z=1
then usually there are infinitely many possibilities for
\eta
, but only finitely many for
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
.
In the next example
\dim Z=2
, and infinitely many choices of
\eta
lead to different
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
. This seems typical for elliptic fiber spaces.

Example 17
.

Let
(z,Z)
be a surface germ, and
\pi:X\to(z,Z)
an Abelian fiber space of dimension 3, that is, an elliptic 3-fold. Assume for simplicity that

X,Z
are smooth,
\pi
is flat, and
K_{X}
is numerically
\pi
-trivial. Let
X_{z}=\sum m_{i}F_{i}
denote the fiber.
Then
\eta\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
is determined by the values
e_{i}:=\eta(F_{i})
, and
\eta\cap[\mbox{general fiber}]=\sum m_{i}e_{i}
.

The construction of
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
goes as follows.

Pick smooth points
x_{ij}\in F_{i}
, smooth surface germs

x_{ij}\in W_{ij}
that are transversal to
F_{i}
, and signs
\epsilon_{ij}
such that

\sum_{j}\epsilon_{ij}=e_{i}
.

Then
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\bigl(\sum_{ij}\epsilon_{ij}W_{ij}\bigr)
is a line bundle on
X
.
Its degree on the general fibers is
\sum_{ij}\epsilon_{ij}m_{i}=\sum_{i}m_{i}e_{i}=1
.
Thus it has a section, whose zero locus consists of a rational section
W\in\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
, plus possibly some divisors that do not dominate
Z
.
The set of all such
W
gives
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
.

To get a clearer description, note that
W
is usually not nef.
Let us run the relative MMP for
(X,cW)
for any

0<c\ll 1
. If all fibers other than
X_{z}
are irreducible, then the MMP steps are flops of curves in
X_{z}
. In dimension 3, flops preserve smoothness.
Thus we end with
(\pi^{\rm m}:X^{\rm m}\to Z,W^{\rm m})
such that
W^{\rm m}
is
\pi^{\rm m}
-nef. Its relative degree is 1, so

(W^{\rm m}\cdot F^{\rm m}_{\ell})=1
for one irreducible component

F^{\rm m}_{\ell}\subset F^{\rm m}
, and
0
for the others.
Moreover,
F^{\rm m}_{\ell}
is a component with multiplicity 1, hence

\pi^{\rm m}
is smooth along smooth points of
F^{\rm m}_{\ell}
.

The steps of the MMP are independent of the choice of the
W_{ij}
by
[
KM98
, 3.53]
. Thus we conclude that
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
consists of

(17.1)

sections of
\pi^{\rm m}
through smooth points of
F^{\rm m}_{\ell}
, when viewed on
X^{\rm m}
, and

(17.2)

their birational transforms on
X
, when viewed on
X
.

This description is clear on
X^{\rm m}
, but less so on
X
.
Usually members of
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
contain the whole
central fiber, and are possibly singular along it.

Using the detailed description of elliptic 3-folds given in
[
Nak91
,
Nak02b
]
, it should be possible to work out
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
more explicitly in many cases.

Example 18
.

Let
g_{0}:=x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}
,
g_{1},g_{2}
be general cubics in the
x_{i}
.
Let

be a Zariski open subset,
and set

X:=(\textstyle{\sum}u_{i}g_{i}=0)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbf{x}}\times Z.

Then
X
is smooth and the projection
\pi:X\to Z

gives an elliptic fiber space. Then

X_{z}=(x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}=0)
consists of 3 lines

L_{i}:=(x_{i}=0)\subset X_{z}
, and, for suitable
Z
, all the other fibers are irreducible.

By the Lefschetz theorem,
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
is generated by the pull-backs of the hyperplane classes. Thus
\eta^{\prime}\cap[\mbox{general fiber}]
is divisible by 3 for every
\eta^{\prime}\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

However, there are classes
\eta\in H^{0}(Z,R^{2}\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Z}}_{X})

for which
\eta\cap[\mbox{general fiber}]=1
.
Indeed,
\eta
is uniquely determined by the values

e_{i}:=\eta\cap L_{i}
, and
\eta\cap[\mbox{general fiber}]=\sum e_{i}
.

Note also that the classes
[L_{i}]\in H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
are independent of

, but the
\eta\cap L_{i}
can be different.

The different
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
are all isomorphic to each other over

Z\setminus\{z\}
, but there are infinitely many non-isomorphic

\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
.

X\to Z
has relative Picard number 1, hence so does

\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)\to Z
.
By the Matsusaka-Mumford criterion
only one of the
\operatorname{\mathbb{T}}_{\eta}(X/Z)
is projective.
(See
[
MM64
]
,
[
Kol23
, 11.39]
or
[
Kol25
, 29]
.)

In the next example

is a mildly singular elliptic 6-fold, and

\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
consists of sections that are
not
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier.
Thus it is not possible to associate a cohomology class in
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
to

\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
.

Example 19
.

Let
L_{1}\neq L_{2}
be lines in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
Blow up the point

to get

The birational transforms of

For

In local analytic coordinates, we can write a smoothing of

Example 20
.

Let
S
be an Enriques surface with K3 cover
\bar{S}\to S
.
Let
\tau_{S}
be the corresponding involution on
\bar{S}
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve and
\tau_{E}
a translation of order 2.
Set

Since

acts trivially on the integral cohomology of

. There is a unique

Then

, and

To compute

.
The universal cover of

is
\bar{S}\times{\mathbb{C}}
, and the group of deck transformations is generated by

,

The second one is the 0 map, while the first maps onto the 2-torsion in

The notion of Néron models over regular, 1-dimensional base schemes was developed in

[

]
; see
[
Art86
,
BLR90
,
Kol25
]
for treatments.
The theory is not as satisfactory over higher dimensional bases, but
there is a natural extension if one ignores points of codimension
\geq 2
.

21
Néron models.

Let
Z
be a normal scheme with function field
K
.
Let
A_{K}
be an Abelian variety over
K
and
X_{K}
an
A_{K}
-torsor.

There is a dense, open subset

such that

A_{K}
extends to an Abelian scheme
A^{\circ}
over
Z^{\circ}
, and

X_{K}
extends to an
A^{\circ}
-torsor

.

For each codimension 1 generic point

one can construct the Néron model over the localization
{\mathcal{O}}_{z,Z}
, and then extend them to get the following.

Claim
21
.1.
There is a large open subset
Z^{*}\subset Z
(that is
Z\setminus Z^{*}
has codimension
\geq 2
), such that over
Z^{*}
we have a smooth group scheme

of

, and
a
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(A_{K})
-torsor
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X_{K})\to Z^{*}

Néron model
of
X_{K}
.
The formation of
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X_{K})
commutes with étale base change. ∎

If

is an Abelian fiber space, then the Néron model of its generic fiber is also denoted by
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X^{*}/Z^{*})
, and called the Néron model of

.

Note that
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X^{*}/Z^{*})\to Z^{*}

By definition, any

extends to a

rational

If the Mordell-Weil group is finitely generated, after shrinking

22
Total Néron model.

Let
X_{i}\to Z
be

A
-torsors under a flat, commutative group scheme
A\to Z
. The quotient of
X_{1}\times_{Z}X_{2}
by the
A
-action

\tau_{a}:(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto(x_{1}+a,x_{2}+(-a))

is a third
A
-torsor
X_{3}
, with a morphism

X_{1}\times_{Z}X_{2}\to X_{3}
. This makes the disjoint union of all

A
-torsors into an algebraic group scheme.

Starting with an
A
-torsor

, applying this operation
(m-1)
-times we get the
A
-torsor
X^{(m)}
. Thus
X^{(0)}=A
and
X^{(1)}=X
.
The disjoint union

\amalg_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}X^{(m)}
is an algebraic group scheme which sits in an exact sequence

0\to A\to\amalg_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}X^{(m)}\to{\mathbb{Z}}\to 0.

Applying this to the Néron model
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X/Z)
we get
the
total Néron model
of

\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\rm tot}(X^{*}/Z^{*}):=\amalg_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{(m)}(X^{*}/Z^{*}).

It is a smooth,
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded, algebraic group scheme over

, whose
degree 0 component is

\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{(0)}(X^{*}/Z^{*})=\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(A^{*}/Z^{*})
, and its degree 1 component is

\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{(1)}(X^{*}/Z^{*})=\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X^{*}/Z^{*})
.

The
identity component
of the total Néron model—denoted by

\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(X^{*}/Z^{*})
—is the largest open subgroup scheme with connected fibers.
Thus
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(X^{*}/Z^{*})=\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A^{*}/Z^{*})
.

Definition 24

(Translation-automorphisms)
.

Let

be an

-torsor. The only automorphisms of

-torsor are translations by

Based on this, an automorphism

(24.1)

(24.2)

.1.

3.
Rational homotopies

We say that two rational sections
s_{i}:Z\dasharrow X
are
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)
-equivalent

if there is a
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)
such that

s_{2}=\phi\circ s_{1}
. Thus (
31
) asserts that sections in

\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
are
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)
-equivalent to each other.

We prove this in 2 steps. We define a notion of rational homotopy of sections, and show in (
34
) that rational homotopy
equivalence implies
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X/Z)
-equivalence.

The construction of rational homotopy
equivalence is easy if
\pi:X\to Z
is flat (
35
).
The equidimensional case uses the same idea, but there are more details to check; see Section
4
.

Definition 33
.

Let
\pi:X\to Z
be an Abelian fiber space, and

s_{i}:Z\dasharrow X
rational sections for
i=1,2
, which we can view as

s_{i}:Z\dasharrow\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X^{*}/Z^{*})
.
A
rational homotopy
between them is a diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccc}W&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\dasharrow}}&\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X^{*}/Z^{*})\\
\sigma_{i}\uparrow\downarrow p&&\pi\downarrow\uparrow s_{i}\\
Z&=&Z,\end{array}

where
p:W\to Z
is a smooth surjection with geometrically connected fibers,
\tau:W\dasharrow\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X^{*}/Z^{*})
is a rational map, and

\sigma_{i}:Z\to W
are regular sections such that

(as rational maps).

Note that
\tau
is a morphism over the generic point of

, so

\tau\circ\sigma_{i}
are defined (as rational maps).

Theorem 34
.

Let
\pi:X\to Z
be an Abelian fiber space
that satisfies the assumptions

.1–6).
Let

-equivalent.

The unexpected feature is that

Note also that here

Proof. As we noted in (

uniquely defines

At a codimension 1 point

Regularity of

We aim to apply

, 36]
to the rational map

[formula]

Given any section

Thus, by

Finally, let us restrict (

∎

Main theorem

Trying to get the minimal set of assumptions for the general version of Theorem

Assumptions 38
.

We impose the following assumptions on
Abelian fiber spaces

(38.1)

\pi

\geq 2

For every

.5) that is needed for the main theorems.

(38.7)

\pi

.

(

.1
′

Once this is established, the uniqueness of the translation-isomorphisms (
31
.2) implies that (
40
.3) descends to give (
40
.1).

Dropping the subscript
i
, assume that we have

\pi_{U}:X_{U}\to U
with fiber
\operatorname{red}X_{u}=\cup_{j}F_{j}
.
By shrinking
U
if necessary, we may assume that every other fiber
X_{u^{\prime}}
specializes to
X_{u}
. That is, there is an irreducible curve
C\to U

with points
c,c^{\prime}\in C
mapping to
u,u^{\prime}\in U

such that, for every irreducible component

Y_{m}\subset X\times_{U}C
, the fiber
F^{\prime}_{m}
of
Y_{m}\to Z
over
c^{\prime}
is irreducible and generically reduced. If this holds then
\operatorname{red}X_{c^{\prime}}=\cup_{m}F^{\prime}_{m}
. Let
\sum_{j}c_{jm}F_{j}
be the specialization of
F^{\prime}_{m}
.

The construction of the rational section in (
29
) uses
local complete intersections
W_{j}
meeting
F_{j}
transversally. Here we have a free choice, so we may assume that the
W_{j}
also meet
the
F^{\prime}_{m}
transversally.

Then we consider
\sum_{j}\eta(F_{j})W_{j}
.
Each
W_{j}
meets
F^{\prime}_{m}
at
c_{jm}
points, so on
F^{\prime}_{m}
, we have

\sum_{j}c_{jm}\eta(F_{j})
transversal intersection points.
This sum equals
\eta(F^{\prime}_{m})
. Thus
\sum_{j}\eta(F_{j})W_{j}
also computes a section in
\operatorname{RatSec}(\eta)
over
u^{\prime}
. ∎

6.
Twisted forms

Many properties are preserved by Tate-Shafarevich twists, even more by translation-twists.
Below is a list of known results, with sketches of proofs and some references.

Starting with (
43
) we work over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

42
Properties preserved by twists I.

Assume that
\pi_{i}:X_{i}\to S
are
twisted forms of each other for
i=1,2
.

(
42
.1)
X_{1},X_{2}
have the same singularities, up to étale isomorphism. In particular, if
X_{1}
is smooth then so is
X_{2}
.
If
X_{1}
has normal, rational, canonical or terminal singularities, then the same holds for
X_{2}
.

(
42
.2)
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factoriality is not an étale-local property.

(
42
.3) If
X_{1}
has canonical singularities and
K_{X}
is numerically
\pi_{1}
-trivial or
\pi_{1}
-nef, then the same hold for
X_{2}
.

(
42
.4) If
X_{i}\to S
are Abelian fiber spaces, then
they have the same Picard number.

43
Variations of Hodge structures.

Let
\pi:X\to S
be a projective morphism with connected fibers,
X
smooth and
S
normal. Let
S^{\circ}
be a dense open subset such that

\pi^{\circ}:X^{\circ}\to S^{\circ}
is smooth. Thus we get integral
variations of Hodge structures

[formula]

R^{i}\pi^{\circ}_{*}{\mathbb{Z}}_{X^{\circ}}\to R^{i}\pi^{\circ}_{*}{\mathbb{C}}_{X^{\circ}}.

The following properties are unchanged by twisting.

(43.2)

The
J
-invariant, mapping a point
s\in S^{\circ}
to the Hodge structure on
H^{i}(X_{s},{\mathbb{Z}})
.

(43.3)

The local monodromy representations around the irreducible components of
S\setminus S^{\circ}
.

(43.4)

If
U\to S
is étale, then the image of
\pi_{1}(U)\to\pi_{1}(S^{\circ})
has finite index.
Thus if the
X_{i}\to S
are twisted forms of each other, then the monodromy representations
\pi_{1}(S^{\circ},s)\to\mathrm{GL}\bigl(H^{i}(F_{s},{\mathbb{Z}})\bigr)
,
agree on a finite index subgroup of

\pi_{1}(S^{\circ},s)
.

44
Canonical bundle formula.

Assume that
X
has canonical singularities and
K_{X}
is numerically
\pi
-trivial. The general Kodaira formula writes

K_{X}\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\pi^{*}(K_{S}+J+B)
where
J
depends only on the
J-invariant map (
43
.2), and
B
depends only on the étale-local structure of
X\to S
over the codimension 1 points of
S
; see
[
Kol07
]
for an overview.

Thus if the
X_{i}\to S
are twisted forms of each other, then,
up to
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear equivalence, the
K_{X_{i}}
are pull-backs of the same
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor class.

As a special case, if
K_{X_{1}}\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}0
then

K_{X_{2}}\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}0
.

45
Monodromy preserving twists.

Assume that
X
has canonical singularities.
By
[
Kol86b
, p.171]
, the sheaves
R^{i}\pi_{*}\omega_{X}

are determined by the variation of the integral Hodge structures (
43
.1). The same holds for
the sheaves
R^{i}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
by
[
Kol86a
, 3.8]
.

Thus if the
X_{i}\to S
are twisted forms of each other, and the monodromy representations
\pi_{1}(S^{\circ},s)\to\mathrm{GL}\bigl(H^{i}(F_{s},{\mathbb{Z}})\bigr)

agree on the whole

\pi_{1}(S^{\circ},s)
, then we have the following.

(
45
.1) There are natural isomorphisms

R^{i}(\pi_{1})_{*}\omega_{X_{1}}\cong R^{i}(\pi_{2})_{*}\omega_{X_{2}},\quad\mbox{and}\quad R^{i}(\pi_{1})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{1}}\cong R^{i}(\pi_{2})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{2}}.

(
45
.2) Assume that the
X_{i}
are proper. Using using
[
Kol86b
, p.172]
,
there are natural isomorphisms

H^{i}\bigl(X_{1},\omega_{X_{1}}\bigr)\cong H^{i}\bigl(X_{2},\omega_{X_{2}}\bigr)\quad\mbox{and}\quad H^{i}\bigl(X_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{1}}\bigr)\cong H^{i}\bigl(X_{2},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{2}}\bigr).

(
45
.3) Assume that the
X_{i}
are proper. If

K_{X_{1}}\sim 0
, then
K_{X_{2}}\sim 0
. Indeed,
K_{X_{2}}\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}0
by (
42
.4) and it has a section by (
45
.2), so it is trivial.

46
Translation-twists.

Let
\pi_{i}:X_{i}\to S
be translation-twists of each other.

(
46
.1) There are natural isomorphisms

R^{i}(\pi_{1})_{*}{\mathbb{Z}}_{X_{1}}\cong R^{i}(\pi_{2})_{*}{\mathbb{Z}}_{X_{2}}\quad\mbox{for every $i$.}\quad

In particular, translation-twists are monodromy preserving.

Indeed, the two sheaves in (
46
.1) are locally the same. Using the notation of (
2
.1), the patchings at a point
s\in S
differ by the action of
\phi_{ij}|_{s}
on
H^{i}(X_{s},{\mathbb{Z}}_{X_{s}})
.
Since
\phi_{ij}|_{s}
is in the connected component of
\operatorname{\mathbf{Aut}}(X_{s})
by (
24
.1),
it acts trivially on the cohomology groups.

Note however, that the differentials in the Leray spectral sequence computing
H^{m}(X_{j},{\mathbb{Z}})
can be different,
thus the fundamental group and the integral cohomology groups of the
X_{i}
can be different;
see (
20
).

This is similar to the situation encountered for minimal models. Different minimal models
X^{\rm m}
of a 3-fold
X
have isomorphic cohomology groups

H^{i}(X^{\rm m},{\mathbb{Z}})
, but usually the cohomology rings
H^{*}(X^{\rm m},{\mathbb{Z}})
are not isomorphic to each other; see
[
Kol91
, 3.2.2]
.

(
46
.2) For smooth
X_{i}
, there are natural isomorphisms of the exterior algebras

\oplus_{i}(\pi_{1})_{*}\Omega^{i}_{X_{1}}\cong\oplus_{i}(\pi_{2})_{*}\Omega^{i}_{X_{2}}.

To see this, note that on a smooth fiber
X_{z}
we have an exact sequence

0\to\pi^{*}\Omega_{Z}\otimes k(z)\to\Omega_{X}|_{X_{z}}\to\Omega_{X_{z}}\to 0.

Translation action is trivial on
\pi^{*}\Omega_{Z}
and also on the global sections of
\Omega_{X_{z}}^{\otimes m}
for any
m
. Thus a translation-isomorphism does not change
the gluing data of the sheaves
\oplus_{i}(\pi_{j})_{*}\Omega^{i}_{X_{j}}

on a dense open set. Since the sheaves

\oplus_{i}(\pi_{j})_{*}\Omega^{i}_{X_{j}}
are torsion free, they are isomorphic to each other.

If the
X_{i}
are singular, similar results hold for the reflexive hulls
\Omega^{[i]}
.

(
46
.3) As a consequence, if
X_{1}
is
holomorphic
symplectic, then so is
X_{2}
.

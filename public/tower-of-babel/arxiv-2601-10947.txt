Faithful Simulation of Broadcast Measurements
Anders Høst-Madsen

Abstract
In this paper a central server Charlie has access to a quantum system C and measures it with a POVM { Λ x } \{\Lambda_{x}\} . Alice and Bob are only interested in the partial results g A ​ ( x ) g_{A}(x) respectively g B ​ ( x ) g_{B}(x) . Alice, Bob, and Charlie share common randomness and Alice and Bob only need to faithfully simulate their measurements. The paper develops to achievable regions for the amount of communication needed to Alice and Bob.

Measurements are, of course, central to quantum theory.
A version of the networked function computation problem for
quantum measurements is as follows.
Nodes
A,B,C\ldots
have a multipartite system represented
by a density operator
\rho^{ABC\ldots}
. One node, say A,
wants to perform a global measurement represented
by POVM
\{\Lambda^{ABC\ldots}_{a}\}_{a}
, but has only its
local quantum system or no access to a quantum system at all. The measurement must be executed using local quantum instruments at each node, with the outcomes transmitted to a destination node. How much transmission is needed
to find
\{\Lambda^{ABC\ldots}_{a}\}_{a}
, which is, of course, classical information? An application could be a distributed quantum computer,
where one would like to extract a classical result that depends on
the total quantum system.

Winter
[
23
]
introduced the idea of
faithful simulation of measurements. Suppose that
in a two node system
Alice performs some measurements and want to
transmit the result to Bob.
Alice performs a measurement on a quantum state
\rho

and sends some classical bits to Bob, who intends
to
faithfully
recover Alice’s measurement,
preserving correlation with the reference system.
The key observation is that if Alice and Bob have
common randomness, the number of bits transmitted
from Alice to Bob can be decreased below that of
classical data compression of Alice’s measurement
outcomes,
while still preserving the correlation with
the reference system. We refer the reader
to the overview paper
[
24
]
and the
paper
[
25
]
for more details
about faithful simulation and applications of it.

Networked measurements can be combined with faithful simulation. The paper
[
25
]

considered the following problem. Alice and Bob have a shared
bipartite quantum system
\rho^{AB}
. Alice makes
measurements with a POVM
\{\Lambda^{A}_{u}\}
on
\rho^{A}
and
Bob measures with
\{\Lambda^{B}_{v}\}
on
\rho^{B}
. Alice, Bob, and Charlie
share common randomness, and Alice and Bob transmit some classical
information to Charlie. The goal is for Charlie to
faithfully simulate a function
z=g(u,v)
of the measurements.
In the paper
[
26
]
the author strengthened this result
in the case when Alice communicates to Bob whob calculates

z=g(u,v)
.

In this paper we consider the opposite problem.
Charlie has access to a quantum system
C
. Alice would

\{\Lambda_{x_{A}}\}
and
Bob
\{\Lambda_{x_{B}}\}
. In general

and

II
Problem statement

Charlie measures
\{\Lambda_{x}\}
on a system C
with density matrix
\rho
. Alice is interested in

x_{A}=g_{A}(x)
and Bob in
x_{B}=g_{B}(x)
. Charlie can
of course calculate the functions and transmit

x_{A}
and
x_{B}
directly, requiring
R_{A}=H(X_{A})
respectively

R_{B}=H(X_{B})
bits. The question this paper seeks to answer is if this can be reduced
if only faithful simulation of the measurements are required.

A more precise statement is that Alice and Bob want
the measurement results

\displaystyle\Lambda_{x_{A}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x:g_{A}(x)=x_{A}}\Lambda_{x}

(1)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{x_{B}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x:g_{B}(x)=x_{B}}\Lambda_{x}

(2)

Rather than directly measuring
\{\Lambda_{x}\}
, Charlie
can do a sequential measurement: first
Charlie measures with
\{\Lambda_{x_{A}}\}
and transmits
the result to Alice. He then measures with
\{\Lambda_{x_{B}}\}

on the system
\rho_{x_{A}}
remaining after the first
measurement. The details are as follows. After measuring
\Lambda_{x_{A}}
the state of the system is

\displaystyle\rho_{x_{A}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Tr\{\Lambda_{x_{A}}\rho\}}\sqrt{\Lambda_{x_{A}}}\rho\sqrt{\Lambda_{x_{A}}}^{\dagger}

Charlie than uses the following conditional measurements

\displaystyle\Lambda_{x_{B}|x_{A}}

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{\Lambda_{x_{A}}}^{\dagger})^{-1}\sum_{x:g_{A}(x)=x_{A},g_{B}(x)=x_{B}}\Lambda_{x}(\sqrt{\Lambda_{x_{A}}})^{-1}

(3)

Here the inverse is only in the subspace spanned by
\rho_{x_{A}}
.
We notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{B}}\Lambda_{x_{B}|x_{A}}

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{\Lambda_{x_{A}}}^{\dagger})^{-1}\sum_{x:g_{A}(x)=x_{A}}\Lambda_{x}(\sqrt{\Lambda_{x_{A}}})^{-1}=\Pi_{x_{A}}

so that it is a valid POVM. Furthermore, because

\forall x:g_{A}(x)=x_{A},g_{B}(x)=x_{B}:\text{span}\{\Lambda_{x}\}\subset\text{span}\{\Lambda_{x_{A}}\}
, so that the inverse is
in the correct subspace, measurement with
\Lambda_{x_{B}|x_{A}}

is equivalent with measuring

\displaystyle\Lambda_{x_{A},x_{B}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x:g_{A}(x)=x_{A},g_{B}(x)=x_{B}}\Lambda_{x}

on the original system
\rho
.

It should be noticed that (
2
) is not
the same
measurement
as (
3
). In particular,
with (
3
) Bob is only informed of the result

x_{B}
; he does not know
x_{A}
and therefore does not know exactly
which measurement was made. We consider two measurements to be
equivalent if the statistics of the measurement results are identical.
This can be made precise with the ideas from faithful simulation.
Namely, define
[
24
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda_{x}}(\rho)=\sum_{x}\Tr\{\Lambda_{x}\rho\}|x\rangle\langle x|

Two measurements
\{\Lambda_{x}\}
and
\{\tilde{\Lambda}_{x}\}
are
equivalent if

\displaystyle I^{R}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda_{x}}(\phi_{\rho})=I^{R}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{\Lambda}_{x}}(\phi_{\rho})

(4)

where
\phi_{\rho}
is a purification of
\rho
and
I^{R}
is the identity
map on the reference system. It is now easy to see that
(
2
) and (
3
) are equivalent measurements.

III
Faithful simulation

In faithful simulation, the criterion (
4
) is
weakened as follows. We say that
\{\tilde{\Lambda}_{x_{A}^{n}}\}
faithfully
simulates
\{\Lambda_{x_{A}^{n}}\}
on
\rho^{\otimes n}
if for any
\epsilon>0
there exists an
N
so
that for all
n\geq N

\displaystyle\left\|(I^{R})^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{\Lambda}_{x_{A}^{n}}}(\phi_{\rho}^{\otimes n})-(I^{R})^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda_{x_{A}^{n}}}(\phi_{\rho}^{\otimes n})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{A}^{n}}\left\|\sqrt{\rho^{\otimes n}}(\tilde{\Lambda}_{x_{A}^{n}}-\Lambda_{x_{A}^{n}})\sqrt{\rho^{\otimes n}}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon,

where the equality is due to
[
24
, Lemma 4]
,
and with an equivalent definition for
\{\Lambda_{x_{B}^{n}}\}
.

Let
R_{A}
and
R_{B}
be the rate of transmission to Alice/Bob and

S_{A}
and
S_{B}
be the rate of common randomness. That is Charlie
and Alice share common randomness at a rate
S_{A}
and Charlie
and Bob share common randomness at a rate
S_{B}
(not shared with Alice). Additionally, Bob may have to know the common
randomness Charlie shares with Alice.

Theorem 1
.

With sequential measurement (Alice before Bob), the
rate region for Alice is

\displaystyle R_{A}

\displaystyle\geq I(X_{A};R)

\displaystyle R_{A}+S_{A}

\displaystyle\geq H(X_{A})

Bob has two possible rate regions. First,

\displaystyle R_{B}

\displaystyle\geq I(X_{A},X_{B};R)

\displaystyle R_{B}+S_{B}

\displaystyle\geq H(X_{B}|X_{A})

Bob also needs to know Alice’s common randomness with Charlie.
Second,

\displaystyle R_{B}

\displaystyle\geq I(X_{B};R,X_{A})

\displaystyle R_{B}+S_{B}

\displaystyle\geq H(X_{B})

Bob does not need to know Alice’s and Charlie’s shared randomness. Here
R
is a reference system purifying
\rho
.

We do not have a tight converse for this problem. However,
the rate region is optimum in two trivial cases: if
g_{A}=g_{B}
,
the first region is optimum, and if
X_{A}
and
X_{B}
are independent,
the second region is optimum.

We will now outline how the faithful simulation measurement
operators are defined. It is very similar to
[
24
,
26
]
, but modified for sequential measurement.

The Alice measurement is completely
standard resulting in the rate constraint
R_{A}\geq I(X_{A};R)

and
R_{A}+S_{A}\geq H(X_{A})

[
24
]
. We need a few results from
the proof of this result. Let

\displaystyle\tilde{p}(x_{A}^{n})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{S}p(x_{A}^{n})&w^{n}\in T_{\delta}^{X_{A}^{n}}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(5)

with
S=\sum_{x^{n}\in T_{\delta}^{X_{A}^{n}}}p(x^{n})
. Further, let

\hat{p}(x_{A}^{n})
be the empirical distribution for the approximate
measurements of
x_{A}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x^{n}_{A}\in T_{\delta}^{x_{A}^{n}}}|\tilde{p}(x_{A}^{n})-\hat{p}(x_{A}^{n})|\leq\epsilon

(6)

with high probability (converging to zero as
n\to\infty
). This follows from

[
24
, (30)]
. We also have

\displaystyle\sum_{x^{n}_{A}\in T_{\delta}^{x_{A}^{n}}}\left\|\tilde{p}(x_{A}^{n})\rho_{x_{A}^{n}}-\hat{p}(x_{A}^{n})\tilde{\rho}_{x_{A}^{n}}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon

(7)

where
\tilde{\rho}_{x_{A}^{n}}
is the state after the approximate
Alice measurements. From (
6
) and (
7
)
we also get

\displaystyle\sum_{x^{n}_{A}\in T_{\delta}^{x_{A}^{n}}}\tilde{p}(x_{A}^{n})\left\|\rho_{x_{A}^{n}}-\tilde{\rho}_{x_{A}^{n}}\right\|_{1}\leq 2\epsilon

(8)

For the Bob measurement, we define

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{A}_{x_{B}|x_{A}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Tr\{\Lambda_{x_{B}|x_{A}}\rho_{x_{A}}\}}\sqrt{\rho_{x_{A}}}\Lambda_{x_{B}|x_{A}}\sqrt{\rho_{x_{A}}}

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}_{x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{\hat{\rho}^{n}|x_{B}^{n},x_{A}^{n}}\hat{\rho}^{n}_{x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{\hat{\rho}^{n}|x_{B}^{n},x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}

(9)

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}_{x_{A}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{B}^{n}}\tilde{p}_{x_{B}|x_{A}}(x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n})\xi^{\prime}_{x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}

(10)

Here
\Pi^{\delta}_{\hat{\rho}^{n}|x_{B}^{n},x_{A}^{n}}
is
the conditional subspace projector for
the ensemble
\{\Tr\{\Lambda_{x_{B}|x_{A}}\rho\},\hat{\rho}^{A}_{x_{B}|x_{A}}\}
and

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{x_{B}|x_{A}}(x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{S(x_{A}^{n})}p_{x_{B}|x_{A}}(x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n})&x_{B}^{n}\in T_{\delta}^{X_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(11)

with
S(x_{A}^{n})=\sum_{x_{B}^{n}\in T_{\delta}^{X_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}}p_{x_{B}|x_{A}}(x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n})
.
Let
\Pi
denote the projector onto the subspace
spanned by the eigenvectors of
\xi^{\prime}_{x_{A}}
with
eigenvalues larger than

\epsilon 2^{-n(H(\rho|X_{A})+\delta)}=\epsilon 2^{-n(H(R|X_{A})+\delta)}
, where the equality is due to the fact that
R

purifies
C
for each
x_{A}
, and
define

\displaystyle\Omega_{x_{A}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\Pi\xi^{\prime}_{x_{A}^{n}}\Pi

\displaystyle\xi_{x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\Pi\xi^{\prime}_{x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi

(12)

The shared randomness is generated through two independent
random variables. Call the outcomes
m_{A}
and
m_{B}
.
m_{A}
is
shared between Charlie and Alice and potentially Bob.
m_{B}
is
shared between Charlie and Bob.

The Bob measurements are generated in one of two ways

1.

For each outcome
m_{B}
of the common randomness, generate

s_{B}^{\prime}
sequences
x^{n}_{B}(j_{B}^{\prime},m_{B})
from the distribution
\tilde{p}_{x_{B}}(x_{B}^{n})
. For each
x_{A}^{n}\in T_{\delta}^{X_{A}^{n}}
let
\mathcal{T}(x_{A}^{n})
be the
first
s_{B}
sequences
x^{n}_{B}(j_{B}^{\prime},m_{B})
that are
jointly typical with
x_{A}^{n}
; we denote this by

x^{n}_{B}(j_{B},m_{B})
. Let
E_{c}
be the event that there is
not at least
s_{B}
jointly typical sequences.

2.

For each
x_{A}^{n}\in T_{\delta}^{X_{A}^{n}}
and
each outcome
m_{B}
generate
s_{B}
sequences
x_{B}^{n}(j_{B},m_{B})
according
to
\tilde{p}_{x_{B}|x_{A}}(x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n})
.

We define the measurement operators

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(m_{B})}_{x_{A}^{n},j_{B}}

\displaystyle=\frac{S(x^{n}_{A})}{(1+\epsilon)s_{B}M_{B}}\sqrt{\rho_{x_{A}^{n}}}^{-1}\xi_{x_{B}^{n}(j_{B},m_{B})|x_{A}^{n}}\sqrt{\rho_{x_{A}^{n}}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(m_{A},m_{B})}_{j_{A},j_{B}}

\displaystyle=\frac{S(x_{A}^{n}(j_{A},m_{A}))}{(1+\epsilon)s_{B}M_{B}}\sqrt{\rho_{x_{A}^{n}(j_{A},m_{A})}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\xi_{x_{B}^{n}(j_{B},m_{B})|x_{A}^{n}(j_{A},m_{A})}\sqrt{\rho_{x_{A}^{n}(j_{A},m_{A})}}^{-1}

(13)

Notice that the operator-valued random variables

\xi_{X_{B}^{n}(j_{B},m_{B})|x_{A}^{n}}
are iid. This is clear in case 2,
but also true in case 1, conditioned on
E_{c}^{c}
. In fact,
the distribution of
\xi_{X_{B}^{n}(j_{B},m_{B})|x_{A}^{n}}
is exactly
the same as in case 2.

III-A

Proof part I

We will show that the set
\Gamma^{(m_{A},m_{B})}_{j_{A}}=\{\Gamma^{(m_{A},m_{B})}_{j_{A},j_{B}}\}_{j_{B}\in\mathcal{S}}
, where
\mathcal{S}=[s_{B}]
, is a sub-POVM with high probability for
all
j_{A}
. If that is not the case, or if in case 1 the event

E_{c}
happens, we put
\Gamma^{(m_{B})}_{j_{A}}=\{I\}
.

We calculate

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho_{x_{A}^{n}(j_{A},m_{A})}}\sum_{j_{B}=1}^{s_{B}}\Gamma^{(m_{A},m_{B})}_{j_{A},j_{B}}\sqrt{\rho_{x_{A}^{n}(j_{A},m_{A})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{S(x_{A}^{n}(j_{A},m_{A}))}{(1+\epsilon)}\left(\frac{1}{s_{B}}\sum_{j_{B}=1}^{s_{B}}\xi_{X_{B}^{n}(j_{B},m_{B})|x_{A}^{n}(j_{A},m_{A})}\right)

where the
X_{B}^{n}(j_{B},m_{B})
are iid according to
\tilde{p}_{x_{B}|x_{A}}(x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n})
. For simplicity of notation,
we denote
x_{A}^{n}(j_{A},m_{A})
simply by
x_{A}^{n}
.
We notice that

\displaystyle S(x_{A}^{n})E[\xi_{X_{B}^{n}(j_{B},m_{B})|x_{A}^{n}}]

\displaystyle=S(x_{A}^{n})\sum_{x_{B}^{n}}\tilde{p}_{x_{B}|x_{A}}(x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n})\xi_{x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}=S(x_{A}^{n})\Omega_{x_{A}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{B}^{n}\in T_{\delta}^{X_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}}p_{x_{B}|x_{A}}(x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n})\Pi\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{\hat{\rho}^{n}|x_{B}^{n},x_{A}^{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\hat{\rho}^{n}_{x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{\hat{\rho}^{n}|x_{B}^{n},x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x_{B}^{n}\in T_{\delta}^{X_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}}p_{x_{B}|x_{A}}(x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n})\Pi\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\hat{\rho}^{n}_{x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi

\displaystyle\leq\Pi\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\sum_{x_{B}^{n}}p_{x_{B}|x_{A}}(x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n})\hat{\rho}^{n}_{x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi

\displaystyle=\Pi\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\rho^{n}_{x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi\leq\rho^{n}_{x_{A}^{n}}

(14)

Let
E_{m}
be the event that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s_{B}}\sum_{j_{B}=1}^{s_{B}}\beta\xi_{X_{B}^{n}(j_{B},m_{B})|x_{A}^{n}}\leq\beta\Omega_{x_{A}^{n}}(1+\epsilon)

for some scaling factor
\beta
. By (
14
)
this event is equivalent to
\sum_{j_{B}=1}^{s_{B}}\Gamma^{(m_{A},m_{B})}_{j_{A},j_{B}}\leq I
, i.e., that
\Gamma^{(m_{A},m_{B})}_{j_{A}}
is
a sub-POVM. We will show that
E_{m}
happens
with high probability using the operator Chernoff bound

[
27
, Lemma 17.3.1]
. We notice that by (
14
) and the definition
of
\Pi
,
E[\xi_{X_{B}^{n}(j_{B},m_{B})|x_{A}^{n}}]=\beta\Omega_{x_{A}^{n}}\geq\beta\epsilon 2^{-n(H(R|X_{A})+\delta^{\prime\prime})}\Pi
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\beta\xi_{x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\beta\Pi\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{\hat{\rho}^{n}|x_{B}^{n},x_{A}^{n}}\hat{\rho}^{n}_{x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{\hat{\rho}^{n}|x_{B}^{n},x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi

\displaystyle\leq\beta\Pi\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi^{\delta}_{\hat{\rho}^{n}|x_{B}^{n},x_{A}^{n}}2^{-n(H(R|X_{A},X_{B})-\delta^{\prime\prime})}\Pi^{\delta}_{C^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\Pi

\displaystyle\leq\Pi

when
\beta=2^{n(H(R|X_{A},X_{B})-\delta^{\prime\prime})}
. The first inequality
follows from properties of conditional quantum typicality

[
27
]
. Then by the operator Chernoff bound

\displaystyle P(E_{m}^{c})

\displaystyle=P\left(\frac{1}{s_{B}}\sum_{j_{B}=1}^{s_{B}}\beta\xi_{X_{B}^{n}(j_{B},m_{B})|x_{A}^{n}}>\beta\Omega_{x_{A}^{n}}(1+\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\text{rank}(\Pi)\exp\left(-\frac{s_{B}\epsilon^{2}\beta\epsilon 2^{-n(H(R|X_{A})+\delta)}}{4\ln 2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{s_{B}\epsilon^{3}2^{n(H(R|X_{A},X_{B})-\delta^{\prime\prime})}2^{-n(H(R|X_{A})+\delta^{\prime\prime})}}{4\ln 2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\vphantom{\frac{s\epsilon^{3}2^{n(H(R|W_{A})-\delta^{\prime\prime})}2^{-n(H(R|X_{A})+\delta^{\prime\prime})}}{4\ln 2}}+n(H(R|X_{A})+\delta^{\prime\prime})\ln 2\right)

Then
with

s_{B}=2^{n(I(X_{B};R|X_{A})+3\delta^{\prime\prime})}

the error probability
goes to zero.

In case 1 we also need to bound
P(E_{c})
, the probability that
there is not at least
s_{B}
jointly typical sequences. The
number
N
of jointly typical sequences is binomial with
s_{B}^{\prime}
trials and
success probability about
2^{-nI(X_{A};X_{B})}
. We can then
bound

\displaystyle P(E_{c})

\displaystyle=P(N\leq s_{B}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-2s_{B}^{\prime}\left(2^{-n(I(X_{A};X_{B})+\delta)}-\frac{s_{B}-1}{s_{B}^{\prime}}\right)\right)

which converges exponentialy to zero if

\displaystyle s_{B}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{n(I(X_{A};X_{B})+\delta)}s_{B}

\displaystyle=2^{n(I(X_{A};X_{B})+I(X_{B};R|X_{A})+\delta+3\delta^{\prime\prime})}

The total probability of error then is

\displaystyle P\left(\bigcup_{m}E_{m}^{c}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m}P(E_{m}^{c})

\displaystyle\leq 2M_{B}\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{3}2^{n\delta}}{4\ln 2}+n(H(R|X_{A})+\delta)\ln 2\right)

So, as long as
M_{B}\leq O(e^{n})
, the total error probability
converges to zero.

III-C

Proof part III: Faithful simulation

We will prove that we have faithful measurement simulation.
Let
\tilde{\Lambda}_{x_{A}^{n}}
be the approximate measurement of
x_{A}^{n}
.
Define

\displaystyle\Lambda_{x_{B}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{A}^{n}}\sqrt{\Lambda_{x_{A}^{n}}}\Lambda_{x_{B}^{n}|x_{A}^{n}}\sqrt{\Lambda_{x_{A}^{n}}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\tilde{\Lambda}_{x_{B}^{n}}^{\prime}

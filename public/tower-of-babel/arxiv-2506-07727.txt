Wreath Generalization of Littlewood Reciprocity
Milo Bechtloff Weising

Abstract
. We generalize Littlewood’s reciprocity formula for the restriction coefficients between GL n ​ ( ℂ ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}) and the symmetric group of permutation matrices 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} to the case of the wreath product groups μ m n ⋊ 𝔖 n \mu_{m}^{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n} .



1.
Introduction

The
restriction problem
is the long-standing open problem in algebraic combinatorics which asks for a combinatorial formula for the
branching coefficients
from
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
to
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
embedded as the group of permutation matrices. More concretely, given a
highest weight representation

V^{\lambda}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
we may restrict to
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and try to describe the expansion of
\mathrm{Res}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}V^{\lambda}
into the irreducible
Specht modules

S^{\mu}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}.
We refer the reader to Orellana–Zabrocki’s paper
[
OZ21
]
for an overview of the current state of this problem. Littlewood
[
Lit58
]
made significant progress towards this goal by establishing an algebraic formula for these coefficients involving
Schur symmetric functions

s_{\lambda}(X)
, the
Hall pairing

\langle-,-\rangle
, and
plethystic substitutions
. The formula may be described as follows:

Theorem

(Littlewood)
.

[
Lit58
]

For
\lambda,\mu\in\Par
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq|\mu|=n
,

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\left(S^{\mu},\mathrm{Res}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}V^{\lambda}\right)=\left\langle s_{\mu}\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}h_{k}\right),~s_{\lambda}\right\rangle.

In this short paper, we will generalize this formula to compute the branching coefficients from
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
to
\mu_{m}^{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
where
m\geq 1
is arbitrary. The irreducible representations of
\mu_{m}^{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
W_{\rho}
, are indexed by partition valued functions
\rho:\mu_{m}\rightarrow\Par
with
|\rho|=\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}|\rho(\zeta)|=n
. The formula is as follows:

Theorem
.

For
\rho:\mu_{m}\rightarrow\Par
and
\lambda\in\Par
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq|\rho|=n,

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})}\left(W_{\rho},\mathrm{Res}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})}V^{\lambda}\right)=\left\langle\prod_{0\leq j\leq m-1}s_{\rho(\zeta_{m}^{j})}\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}h_{km+j}\right),~s_{\lambda}\right\rangle.

The proof of Theorem
3.17
follows the following steps. First, we describe the
Frobenius characteristics
of the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})
-modules
\mathrm{Res}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})}V^{\lambda}
by explicitly computing the characteristic polynomials of
\gamma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m}).
Next, we utilize the interplay between symmetric functions
\Lambda
and
wreath symmetric functions

\bigotimes_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\Lambda(X_{\zeta})
in order to algebraically describe the evaluations of Schur functions
s_{\lambda}
at the eigenvalues of the matrices
\gamma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m}).
Miraculously, there is a simple generating function which controls these substitutions, namely
\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right)
where
\Omega(Y;\zeta):=\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-\zeta y_{i})^{-1}
, which we use to describe the Frobenius characteristics of the restrictions
\mathrm{Res}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})}V^{\lambda}
. Lastly, by using the reproducing kernel property for the pairing on wreath symmetric functions
\bigotimes_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\Lambda(X_{\zeta})
we arrive at the main result.

2.
Set-up

In this section, we will establish some necessary notation.

Definition 2.1
.

For the remainder of this paper fix
m\geq 1.
Define
\mu_{m}\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
as the group of
m
-th roots of unity. We will write
\zeta_{m}:=e^{2\pi i/m}.
For
n\geq 1
, define
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
as the group of
n\times n
invertible complex matrices and define the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
as the group of permutation matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, i.e., those
n\times n
matrices with one
1
in each row and column and
0
’s everywhere else. For
n\geq 1
, define
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m}):=\mu_{m}^{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
where
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on
\mu_{m}^{n}
by coordinate permutations. We will write elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})
as
(\zeta_{m}^{a_{1}},\ldots,\zeta_{m}^{a_{n}};\sigma)
where
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}.
We embed
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})
into
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
as the group of matrices with exactly one nonzero entry in each row and column whose nonzero entries are
m
-th roots of unity. To be consistent with trivial cases, we set
\mathrm{GL}_{0}(\mathbb{C})=\mathfrak{S}_{0}=\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mu_{m}):=\{1\}
to all be the trivial group.

Here we define the
ring of symmetric functions
.

Definition 2.2
.

Define
\Par
as the set of non-negative integer
partitions
. Given
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})\in\Par
we write
|\lambda|:=\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{\ell}
,
\ell(\lambda):=\ell,
and for
r\geq 1
,
m_{r}(\lambda):=\#(1\leq i\leq\ell|\lambda_{i}=r).
For
\lambda\in\Par
, define
z_{\lambda}:=\prod_{r\geq 1}m_{r}(\lambda)!r^{m_{r}(\lambda)}.
We will write
\Lambda(X)
for the ring of symmetric functions over
\mathbb{C}
in the variable set
X=x_{1}+x_{2}+\ldots~.
We write
p_{\lambda}(X),h_{\lambda}(X)
and
s_{\lambda}(X)
for the
power sum
,
complete homogeneous
, and
Schur symmetric function
bases of
\Lambda(X)
respectively. Define the
Hall bilinear pairing

\langle-,-\rangle:\Lambda\times\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by
\langle p_{\lambda},p_{\mu}\rangle:=z_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda,\mu}.
Define the
plethystic exponential
as
\Omega(X):=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}(X).
For a number
\zeta
we will write
\Omega(X;\zeta):=\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-\zeta x_{i})^{-1}.

Throughout this paper, we will use
plethystic substitution
of symmetric functions. We will define plethystic substitution as follows:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots
be a set of commuting free variables. For a formal series
A\in\mathbb{C}[[t_{1},t_{2},\ldots]]
, say
A=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}t^{\alpha}
and
n\geq 1
define

p_{n}(A)=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}t^{n\alpha}.
Now define the plethystic substitution
F\mapsto F(A)
as the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism
\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[[t_{1},t_{2},\ldots]]
uniquely determined by
p_{n}\mapsto p_{n}(A).

We will need to work with a generalization of the ring of symmetric functions with multiple variable sets. Before, however, we need to establish some notation.

Definition 2.4
.

Given
\gamma=(\zeta_{m}^{a_{1}},\ldots,\zeta_{m}^{a_{n}};\sigma)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})
and a cycle
i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell}
of
\sigma
, the element
\zeta_{m}^{a_{i_{1}}}\cdots\zeta_{m}^{a_{i_{\ell}}}\in\mu_{m}
is called the
cycle product
of
\gamma
corresponding to
i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell}
and
\ell
is referred to as the length of the cycle product. Let
\Phi
denote the set of functions
\rho:\mu_{m}\rightarrow\Par
and for
n\geq 0
we let
\Phi_{n}\subset\Phi
denote the set of those
\rho
with
|\rho|=n
where
|\rho|:=\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}|\rho(\zeta)|.
Given
\rho\in\Phi_{n}
, define the conjugacy class
C_{\rho}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})
as the set of those elements
\gamma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})
such that for all
\zeta\in\mu_{m}
and
\ell\geq 1,

\gamma
has
m_{\ell}(\rho(\zeta))
cycle products of length
\ell
equal to
\zeta.
For
\rho\in\Phi
define
Z_{\rho}:=\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}z_{\rho(\zeta)}m^{\ell(\rho(\zeta))}.

Now we define the
wreath symmetric functions
which help to describe the representation theory of the wreath product groups
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})
as
n
varies. We refer the reader to Appendix B in Macdonald’s book
[
Mac15
]
as well as Ingram–Jing–Stitzinger’s paper
[
IJS09
]
for a more complete overview of this topic.

Definition 2.5
.

Define the ring
R:=\bigotimes_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\Lambda(X_{\zeta})
where for
\zeta\in\mu_{m}
,
X_{\zeta}=x_{\zeta,1}+x_{\zeta,2}+\ldots
is a corresponding variable set. We will often write
f(\fX)=f(X_{\zeta}|\zeta\in\mu_{m})
for elements
f\in R.

For
\rho\in\Phi
, define

P_{\rho}(\fX):=\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}p_{\rho(\zeta)}(X_{\zeta})
and

S_{\rho}(\fX):=\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}s_{\rho(\zeta_{m}^{j})}(\varphi_{j}(\fX))
where for
1\leq j\leq m
,

\varphi_{j}(\fX):=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\zeta^{j}X_{\zeta}
and
s_{\rho(\zeta_{m}^{j})}(\varphi_{j}(\fX))
denotes a plethystic substitution.
Define the bilinear pairing
(-,-):R\times R\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

(P_{\rho},P_{\gamma}):=\delta_{\rho,\gamma}Z_{\rho}.
Lastly, for
f(\fX)=\sum_{\rho}a_{\rho}P_{\rho}(\fX)
define
\overline{f(\fX)}:=\sum_{\rho}\overline{a_{\rho}}P_{\rho}(\fX).

3.
Branching coefficients

We begin by defining the branching coefficients which we will be studying for the remainder of this paper.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 0
.
For
\lambda\in\Par
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
, define
V^{\lambda}
to be the
highest weight representation
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
corresponding to
\lambda.
For
\rho\in\Phi_{n}
, we will write
W_{\rho}
for the corresponding irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})
given explicitly as

W_{\rho}:=\mathrm{Ind}_{\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\mathfrak{S}_{|\rho(\zeta)|}(\mu_{m})}^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})}\bigotimes_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}S^{\rho(\zeta)}\otimes\chi_{\zeta}^{\otimes|\rho(\zeta)|}

where
S^{\rho(\zeta)}
are the
Specht modules
of
\mathfrak{S}_{|\rho(\zeta)|}
corresponding to the partitions
\rho(\zeta)
and
\chi_{\zeta}
are the
1
-dimensional characters of
\mu_{m}
given by
\chi_{\zeta_{m}^{j}}(\zeta_{m}^{a}):=\zeta_{m}^{ja}.

For
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
and
\rho\in\Phi_{n}
, define the
branching coefficient

d_{\rho,\lambda}:=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})}\left(W_{\rho},\mathrm{Res}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})}V^{\lambda}\right).

In order to algebraically compute the values
d_{\rho,\lambda}
, we introduce the following generating functions.

Definition 3.2
.

For
\rho\in\Phi
, define
\Xi_{\rho}
as the multi-set of roots of the characteristic polynomial of any
\gamma\in C_{\rho}
, i.e., the eigenvalues (with multiplicity) of any matrix
\gamma\in C_{\rho}.
For
f\in\Lambda
, we will write
f(\Xi_{\rho})
for the evaluation
f(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
where
\Xi_{\rho}=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}.
Given
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
, define
F_{\lambda}^{(n)}(\fX)\in R
as

F_{\lambda}^{(n)}(\fX):=\sum_{\rho\in\Phi_{n}}\frac{s_{\lambda}(\Xi_{\rho})}{Z_{\rho}}P_{\rho}(\fX).

The wreath symmetric functions
F_{\lambda}^{(n)}(\fX)
are generating functions for the branching coefficients
d_{\rho,\lambda}
in the following sense:

Proposition 3.3
.

For
\rho\in\Phi_{n}
and
\lambda\in\Par
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
,

d_{\rho,\lambda}=\left(F_{\lambda}^{(n)}(\fX),\overline{S_{\rho}(\fX)}\right).

Proof.

By
[
Mac15
]
[Appendix B, 7.4, pg. 174] [Appendix B, 9.7, pg. 178], we know that
(S_{\rho},\overline{S_{\gamma}})=\delta_{\rho,\gamma}
and
S_{\rho}(\fX)
is the Frobenius characteristic of the irreducible representation
W_{\rho}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m}).
By definition,
F_{\lambda}^{(n)}(\fX)
is the Frobenius characteristic of
\mathrm{Res}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})}V^{\lambda}
and therefore, the multiplicity of
W_{\rho}
in
\mathrm{Res}^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m})}V^{\lambda}
, i.e., the coefficient of
S_{\rho}(\fX)
in the
S
-basis expansion of
F_{\lambda}^{(n)}(\fX)
, is given by
\left(F_{\lambda}^{(n)}(\fX),\overline{S_{\rho}(\fX)}\right).

∎

In order to find a workable formula for the Frobenius characteristic
F_{\lambda}^{(n)}(\fX)
, we will first find a formula for the values
s_{\lambda}(\Xi_{\rho}).

Definition 3.4
.

For
\rho\in\Phi
, define

g_{\rho}(X):=\sum_{\lambda\in\Par}\frac{p_{\lambda}(X)p_{\lambda}(\Xi_{\rho})}{z_{\lambda}}.

The following is an immediate consequence of the definition of the Hall pairing:

Lemma 3.5
.

For all
\rho\in\Phi
and
f\in\Lambda,

f(\Xi_{\rho})=\langle g_{\rho}(X),f(X)\rangle.

It will be necessary to describe the eigenvalues of the matrices
\gamma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mu_{m}).

Definition 3.6
.

For
\rho\in\Phi
, define the (shifted) characteristic polynomial
f_{\rho}(t):=\det\left(\mathrm{Id}_{|\rho|}-t\gamma\right)
where
\gamma
is any element of
C_{\rho}.

The polynomials
f_{\rho}(t)
have the following simple description:

Lemma 3.7
.

For
\rho\in\Phi
,

f_{\rho}(t)=\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\prod_{\ell\geq 1}(1-\zeta t^{\ell})^{m_{\ell}(\rho(\zeta))}.

Proof.

Let
\rho\in\Phi_{n}
and
\gamma=(\zeta_{m}^{a_{1}},\ldots,\zeta_{m}^{a_{n}};\sigma)\in C_{\rho}.
The matrix
\gamma
is conjugate in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
to a block diagonal matrix with each block of the form
(\zeta_{m}^{a_{i_{1}}},\ldots,\zeta_{m}^{a_{i_{r}}};(1\cdots r))\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mu_{m})
where
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})
range over the cycles of
\sigma
and
(1\cdots r)
denotes the standard long cycle in
\mathfrak{S}_{r}.
It is straightforward to verify using first row determinant expansions that the (shifted) characteristic polynomials of the matrices
(\zeta_{m}^{a_{i_{1}}},\ldots,\zeta_{m}^{a_{i_{r}}};(1\cdots r))\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mu_{m})
are given by
1-\zeta_{m}^{a_{i_{1}}}\cdots\zeta_{m}^{a_{i_{r}}}t^{r}
. The result follows.
∎

The series
g_{\rho}(X)
has an explicit product formula.

Proposition 3.8
.

For all
\rho\in\Phi,

g_{\rho}(X)=\prod_{i\geq 1}\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\prod_{\ell\geq 1}\left(1-\zeta x_{i}^{\ell}\right)^{-m_{\ell}(\rho(\zeta))}.

Proof.

In what follows, let
\rho\in\Phi
and
\gamma\in C_{\rho}
. By definition,
g_{\rho}(X)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Par}\frac{p_{\lambda}(X)p_{\lambda}(\Xi_{\rho})}{z_{\lambda}}
which we may rewrite as
\exp\left(\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{p_{r}(X)p_{r}(\Xi_{\rho})}{r}\right).
Expanding
p_{r}(X)=\sum_{i\geq 1}x_{i}^{r}
yields

\prod_{i\geq 1}\exp\left(\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{x_{i}^{r}p_{r}(\Xi_{\rho})}{r}\right).
Now we notice that
p_{r}(\Xi_{\rho})=\mathrm{Tr}(\gamma^{r})
and furthermore for all
i\geq 1,

\exp\left(\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{x_{i}^{r}p_{r}(\Xi_{\rho})}{r}\right)=\det(1-x_{i}\gamma)^{-1}.

Therefore,

\prod_{i\geq 1}\exp\left(\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{x_{i}^{r}p_{r}(\Xi_{\rho})}{r}\right)=\prod_{i\geq 1}\det(1-x_{i}\gamma)^{-1}=\prod_{i\geq 1}f_{\rho}(x_{i})^{-1}.

Lastly, by Lemma
3.7
we find that

\prod_{i\geq 1}f_{\rho}(x_{i})^{-1}=\prod_{i\geq 1}\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\prod_{\ell\geq 1}\left(1-\zeta x_{i}^{\ell}\right)^{-m_{\ell}(\rho(\zeta))}.

∎

Here we show that the generating series for the
g_{\rho}(Y)
series has a useful plethystic form.

Proposition 3.9
.

\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\frac{P_{\rho}(\fX)g_{\rho}(Y)}{Z_{\rho}}=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right)

Proof.

Using Proposition
3.8
,

\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\frac{P_{\rho}(\fX)g_{\rho}(Y)}{Z_{\rho}}=\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\frac{P_{\rho}(\fX)}{Z_{\rho}}\prod_{i\geq 1}\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\prod_{\ell\geq 1}\left(1-\zeta y_{i}^{\ell}\right)^{-m_{\ell}(\rho(\zeta))}.

We may factor each of these summands as

\frac{P_{\rho}(\fX)}{Z_{\rho}}\prod_{i\geq 1}\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\prod_{\ell\geq 1}\left(1-\zeta y_{i}^{\ell}\right)^{-m_{\ell}(\rho(\zeta))}=\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\left(\frac{p_{\rho(\zeta)}(X_{\zeta})}{z_{\rho(\zeta)}m^{\ell(\rho(\zeta))}}\prod_{\ell\geq 1}\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-\zeta y_{i}^{\ell})^{-m_{\ell}(\rho(\zeta))}\right)

which we will write using plethystic substitution as

\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\left(\frac{p_{\rho(\zeta)}(X_{\zeta})}{z_{\rho(\zeta)}m^{\ell(\rho(\zeta))}}\prod_{\ell\geq 1}\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-\zeta y_{i}^{\ell})^{-m_{\ell}(\rho(\zeta))}\right)=\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\left(\frac{p_{\rho(\zeta)}\left(X_{\zeta}\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-\zeta y_{i})^{-1}\right)}{z_{\rho(\zeta)}m^{\ell(\rho(\zeta))}}\right).

Note
\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-\zeta y_{i})^{-1}=\Omega(Y;\zeta).
Therefore,

\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\frac{P_{\rho}(\fX)}{Z_{\rho}}\prod_{i\geq 1}\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\prod_{\ell\geq 1}\left(1-\zeta y_{i}^{\ell}\right)^{-m_{\ell}(\rho(\zeta))}=\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Par}\frac{p_{\lambda}\left(X_{\zeta}\prod_{i\geq 1}(1-\zeta y_{i})^{-1}\right)}{z_{\lambda}m^{\ell(\lambda)}}\right)

which simplifies to

\prod_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right)=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right).

∎

It will be convenient to introduce the following notation:

Definition 3.10
.

For
\lambda\in\Par,
define
F_{\lambda}(\fX):=\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\frac{P_{\rho}(\fX)s_{\lambda}(\Xi_{\rho})}{Z_{\rho}}.

Note that for
\ell(\lambda)\leq n,
the homogeneous degree
n
component of
F_{\lambda}(\fX)
is
F^{(n)}_{\lambda}(\fX).
We may now give a useful formula for the series
F_{\lambda}(\fX).

Proposition 3.11
.

For
\lambda\in\Par,

F_{\lambda}(\fX)=\left\langle\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right),s_{\lambda}(Y)\right\rangle.

Proof.

Using Lemma
3.5
, we see that

F_{\lambda}(\fX)=\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\frac{s_{\lambda}(\Xi_{\rho})}{Z_{\rho}}P_{\rho}(\fX)=\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\frac{\langle g_{\rho}(Y),s_{\lambda}(Y)\rangle}{Z_{\rho}}P_{\rho}(\fX)

which we may write as

F_{\lambda}(\fX)=\left\langle\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\frac{P_{\rho}(\fX)g_{\rho}(Y)}{Z_{\rho}},s_{\lambda}(Y)\right\rangle.
From Proposition
3.9
, we find

\left\langle\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\frac{P_{\rho}(\fX)g_{\rho}(Y)}{Z_{\rho}},s_{\lambda}(Y)\right\rangle=\left\langle\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right),s_{\lambda}(Y)\right\rangle.

∎

We introduce the following notation:

Definition 3.12
.

For
\rho\in\Phi
, define

G_{\rho}(Y):=\left(\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right),\overline{S_{\rho}(\fX)}\right).

The utility of defining the series
G_{\rho}(Y)
is demonstrated by the next formula.

Lemma 3.13
.

For all
\rho\in\Phi_{n}
and
\lambda\in\Par
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
,

d_{\rho,\lambda}=\langle G_{\rho}(Y),s_{\lambda}(Y)\rangle.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.3
, we know that

d_{\rho,\lambda}=\left(F_{\lambda}^{(n)}(\fX),\overline{S_{\rho}(\fX)}\right).
Note that since
|\rho|=n,
we may exchange
F_{\lambda}^{(n)}(\fX)
with
F_{\lambda}(\fX)
in the above to find

d_{\rho,\lambda}=\left(F_{\lambda}(\fX),\overline{S_{\rho}(\fX)}\right).
Proposition
3.11
now shows

\left(F_{\lambda}(\fX),\overline{S_{\rho}(\fX)}\right)=\left(\left\langle\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right),s_{\lambda}(Y)\right\rangle,\overline{S_{\rho}(\fX)}\right).

Swapping the order of the pairing evaluations yields

\left(\left\langle\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right),s_{\lambda}(Y)\right\rangle,\overline{S_{\rho}(\fX)}\right)=\left\langle\left(\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right),\overline{S_{\rho}(\fX)}\right),s_{\lambda}(Y)\right\rangle

and thus

d_{\rho,\lambda}=\left\langle G_{\rho}(Y),s_{\lambda}(Y)\right\rangle.

∎

Lemma 3.14
.

For all
1\leq j\leq m,

\varphi_{j}\left(\Omega(Y;\zeta)|\zeta\in\mu_{m}\right)=\sum_{k\geq 1}h_{km-j}(Y).

Proof.

By direct computation, we find

\varphi_{j}\left(\Omega(Y;\zeta)|\zeta\in\mu_{m}\right)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\zeta^{j}\Omega(Y;\zeta)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\zeta^{j}\sum_{k\geq 0}\zeta^{k}h_{k}(Y)=\sum_{k\geq 0}h_{k}(Y)\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\zeta^{k+j}

and by the orthogonality relation
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\zeta^{a}=\begin{cases}1&m\mid a\\
0&m\nmid a\\
\end{cases},

\sum_{k\geq 0}h_{k}(Y)\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}\zeta^{k+j}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\
m\mid k+j\end{subarray}}h_{k}(Y)=\sum_{k\geq 1}h_{km-j}(Y).

∎

We require the reproducing kernel property of the pairing
(-,-).

Lemma 3.15
.

For any collection of variable sets
\mathfrak{Y}=(Y_{\zeta})_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}
and
f\in R,

\left(\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}Y_{\zeta}\right),f(\fX)\right)=f(\mathfrak{Y})

Proof.

From Macdonald
[
Mac15
]
[Appendix B, 7.4, pg. 174], we know

\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}Y_{\zeta}\right)=\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}\overline{S_{\rho}(\fX)}S_{\rho}(\mathfrak{Y})

and
(S_{\rho}(\fX),\overline{S_{\gamma}(\mathfrak{X})})=\delta_{\rho,\gamma}
for all
\rho,\gamma\in\Phi.
If we assume
f(\fX)=\sum_{\rho}a_{\rho}S_{\rho}(\fX)
, then

\left(\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}Y_{\zeta}\right),f(\fX)\right)=\sum_{\rho}a_{\rho}\left(\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}Y_{\zeta}\right),S_{\rho}(\fX)\right)=\sum_{\rho}a_{\rho}S_{\rho}(\mathfrak{Y})=f(\mathfrak{Y}).

∎

Using the above reproducing kernel property, we find an explicit formula for the series
G_{\rho}(Y).

Proposition 3.16
.

For all
\rho\in\Phi,

G_{\rho}(Y)=\prod_{0\leq j\leq m-1}s_{\rho(\zeta_{m}^{j})}\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}h_{km+j}(Y)\right)

Proof.

Applying Lemma
3.15
to Definition
3.12
shows that

\displaystyle G_{\rho}(Y)

\displaystyle=\left(\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right),\overline{S_{\rho}(\fX)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right),\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}\overline{s_{\rho(\zeta_{m}^{j})}(\varphi_{j}(\fX))}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\Omega\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{m}}X_{\zeta}\Omega(Y;\zeta)\right),\prod_{0\leq j\leq m-1}s_{\rho(\zeta_{m}^{j})}(\varphi_{m-j}(\fX))\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{0\leq j\leq m-1}s_{\rho(\zeta_{m}^{j})}(\varphi_{m-j}(\Omega(Y;\zeta)|\zeta\in\mu_{m})))

\displaystyle=\prod_{0\leq j\leq m-1}s_{\rho(\zeta_{m}^{j})}\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}h_{km+j}(Y)\right)

∎

By combining Lemma
3.13
and Proposition
3.16
we find the following:

Theorem 3.17
.

For
\rho:\mu_{m}\rightarrow\Par
and
\lambda\in\Par
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq|\rho|,

d_{\rho,\lambda}=\left\langle\prod_{0\leq j\leq m-1}s_{\rho(\zeta_{m}^{j})}\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}h_{km+j}\right),~s_{\lambda}\right\rangle.

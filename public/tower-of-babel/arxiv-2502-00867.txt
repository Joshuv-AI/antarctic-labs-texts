Partitions of an Eulerian Digraph into Circuits
Joshua Cooper, Utku Okur

Abstract
We investigate a cancellation property satisfied by a connected Eulerian digraph D D . Namely, unless D D is a single directed cycle, we have ∑ k ≥ 1 ( − 1 ) k ​ f k ​ ( D ) = 0 \sum_{k\geq 1}(-1)^{k}f_{k}(D)=0 , where f k ​ ( D ) f_{k}(D) is the number of partitions of Eulerian circuits of D D into k k circuits. This property is a consequence of the fact that the Martin polynomial of a digraph has no constant term. We provide an alternative proof by employing Viennot’s theory of Heaps of Pieces, and in particular, a bijection between closed trails of a digraph and heaps with a unique maximal piece, which are also in bijection with unique sink orientations of the intersection graphs G a G_{a} of partitions a a of E ⁡ ( D ) E(D) into cycles. The argument considers the partition lattice of the edge set of a digraph D D , restricted to the join-semilattice T ⁡ ( D ) T(D) induced by elements whose blocks are connected and Eulerian. The minimal elements of T ⁡ ( D ) T(D) are exactly the partitions of D D into cycles, and the up-set of a minimal element a ∈ T ⁡ ( D ) a\in T(D) is shown to be isomorphic to the bond lattice L ⁡ ( G a ) L(G_{a}) . Using tools developed by Whitney and Rota, we perform Möbius inversion on T ⁡ ( D ) T(D) and obtain the claimed cancellation. As a consequence of this alternative proof, we relate the Martin polynomial of a digraph directly to the chromatic polynomials of the intersection graphs of partitions of D D into cycles. Finally, we apply the cancellation property in order to deduce the classical Harary-Sachs Theorem for graphs of rank 2 2 from a hypergraph generalization thereof, remedying a gap in a previous proof of this. Given a finite graph, we can obtain a set of pieces

\mathcal{R}_{G}=\{(u,v):\{u,v\}\in E(G)\text{ or }u=v\}

. Hence, a simple graph

is identified with a pair
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{R})
of pieces and concurrence relation.

Remark 1
.

Let

be a poset. Define the following associated graphs:

1.

Let

of

2.

Let

of

Given a set of pieces

and a concurrence relation

, consider a labeling function

. Then, condition (ii) of

In short, given

\displaystyle\mapsto(\downarrow\!\{\beta_{1}\}_{P},P\setminus\downarrow\!\{\beta_{1}\}_{P})

The other direction

Let

We will provide necessary definitions, to state the NBC Theorem of Rota (q.v.

1.

Given

for

.

a

1.

is the set of edges

of

of pieces and a concurrence relation, consider the partition lattice

3
Partition Lattice of the Edge-set of an Eulerian Digraph

3.1
Preliminaries on Digraphs and Closed Trails

We outline some definitions for Eulerian digraphs. For more detail and examples, we refer the reader to
[
13
]
. Let
X=(V(X),E(X),\varphi)
be a multi-graph of rank
k=2
. A
walk
of
X
is an alternating sequence

\mathbf{w}=(v_{0},e_{1},v_{1},e_{2},\ldots,v_{d-1},e_{d},v_{d})

of vertices
(v_{i})_{i=0}^{d}
and edges
(e_{i})_{i=1}^{d}
, such that consecutive pairs of elements are incident.

The
length
of a walk
\mathbf{w}
, denoted
|\mathbf{w}|
, is the number of edges in
\mathbf{w}
. The vertices
v_{0},v_{d}
are said to be the
initial vertex
and the
terminal vertex
of
\mathbf{w}
, respectively. A
closed walk
at
v_{0}
is a walk where
v_{0}=v_{d}
.

A walk
\mathbf{w}
is called a
trail
, if its edges are distinct. A closed trail
\mathbf{w}
of
X
is an
Eulerian trail
, if it uses all the edges of
X
. We denote by
\mathcal{T}(X)
and
\mathcal{W}(X)
, the sets of closed trails and Eulerian (closed) trails of
X
, respectively. Given an edge
e\in E(X)
with
\varphi(e)=\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
, the set of
Eulerian (closed) trails ending at
e
is defined as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{e}(X)

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{w}=(v_{0},e_{1},v_{1},\ldots,v_{d-2},e_{d-1},z_{1},e,z_{2}):\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}(X)\}

\displaystyle\sqcup\{\mathbf{w}=(v_{0},e_{1},v_{1},\ldots,v_{d-2},e_{d-1},z_{2},e,z_{1}):\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}(X)\}

A walk of a digraph is defined similarly: A walk
\mathbf{w}=(v_{0},e_{1},v_{1},e_{2},\ldots,v_{d-1},e_{d},v_{d})
must satisfy
\psi(e_{i})=(v_{i-1},v_{i})
, for each
i=1,\ldots,d
.

Let
X
be a multi-graph of rank
k=2
. We define the set of
circuits
of
X
as the quotient
\mathcal{Z}(X):=\mathcal{T}(X)/\textup{rk}
of closed trails under the equivalence relation
\rho
, which is defined by cyclic permutation of the edges. More formally,
\rho
is the transitive closure of the relations,

\mathbf{w}_{1}=(v_{0},e_{1},v_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{d-1},v_{d-1},e_{d},v_{0})\ \ \rho\ \ \mathbf{w}_{2}=(v_{d-1},e_{d},v_{0},e_{1},v_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{d-1},v_{d-1})

The set of
Eulerian circuits
is defined as
\mathfrak{C}(X):=\mathcal{W}(X)/\rho
, the quotient of the set of Eulerian trails of
X
, up to the equivalence relation of cyclic permutation of edges.

Whenever a circuit
[w]_{\rho}\in\mathcal{Z}(X)
has no repeated vertices, we call it a
cycle
. Let
\mathcal{B}(X)
denote the set of cycles of a multi-graph
X
.

The circuits and cycles of a digraph is defined similarly. We use the same notation, to refer to the closed trails, Eulerian trails, Eulerian circuits and cycles of a digraph. From the definition, it follows that an Eulerian digraph (a digraph with at least one Eulerian trail) is necessarily connected. Given an edge
e\in E(D)
such that
\psi(e)=(z_{1},z_{2})
, then the Eulerian trails of
D
ending at
e
is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{e}(D)

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{w}=(v_{0},e_{1},v_{1},\ldots,v_{d-2},e_{d-1},z_{1},e,z_{2}):\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}(D)\}

To define heaps of pieces of a digraph

, we take
\mathcal{B}(D)
, the set of cycles, as the set of pieces. Given two cycles
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(D)
, we say that
\beta_{1}
and

are

. Equivalently,

is the “intersection graph” of

Definition 16
.

Given an Eulerian digraph
D
, let
T(D)=(\mathcal{T},\leq\big|_{\mathcal{T}})
be the sub-poset of the partition lattice
\Pi(E(D))
, induced by
\mathcal{T}:=\{b\in\Pi(E(D)):F(b)\neq 0\}
, i.e., the set of partitions of
E(D)
into Eulerian parts. (Note that
F(b)\neq 0
if and only if each part of
b=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
is the edgeset of an Eulerian digraph.)

By definition, the unique maximal element of
\Pi(E(D))
belongs to
T(D)
and is denoted by
\mathbbm{1}_{D}
. On the other hand, minimal elements of
T(D)
are exactly the partitions of
E(D)
into cycles. Unless
D
has a unique partition into directed cycles,
T(D)
does not have a unique minimal element. Let
Q(D):=\min(T(D))
be the set of partitions of
D
into cycles.

We will show that
T(D)
is closed under
\vee_{\Pi(E(D))}
:

Lemma 3.1
.

The sub-poset
(T(D),\vee_{\Pi(E(D))})
is a join-semilattice. Furthermore,
T(D)
has a unique minimal element if and only if
(T(D),\vee_{\Pi(E(D))},\wedge_{\Pi(E(D))})
is a lattice.

Proof.

By an easy induction one can see that:

If a digraph
N
is connected and
E(N)
is a disjoint union of Eulerian digraphs, then
N
is Eulerian.

(3.1)

Let
a=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{t}\},b=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}\}\in T(D)
be given. We claim that
c:=a\vee_{\Pi(E(D))}b=\left\{\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}}C\ :\ \mathcal{C}\in(a\cup b)/\overline{\varspade}\right\}\in T(D)
. Given any part
C_{1}\cup\ldots\cup C_{r}
of
c
, where
C_{i}\in a\cup b
and
C_{1}\varspade\ldots\varspade C_{r}
, note that
C_{1}\cup\ldots\cup C_{r}
is connected, as we have
C_{i}\cap C_{i+1}\neq\emptyset
, for each
i
, by the definition of
\varspade
. On the other hand, since
a
refines
c
, each part of
c
is a disjoint union of some parts of
a
. As every part of
a
is an Eulerian digraph, it follows that each part of
c
satisfies (
3.1
), and so,
F(c)\neq 0
and
c\in T(D)
.

Next, assume that
c\in T(D)
is the unique minimal element. In particular, each part of
c
is a cycle. Given any two elements
a,b\in T(D)
, we claim that
a\wedge_{\Pi(E(D))}b=\{A\cap B:A\in a,\ B\in b,\ A\varspade B\}\in T(D)
. Since
c
refines
a
, any part
A\in a
is a disjoint union of cycles of
c
. Similarly for any part of
b
. Therefore, any part
A\cap B
of
a\wedge b
is both connected (by the definition of
\varspade
) and is a disjoint union of cycles of
c
, and so
F(A\cap B)\neq 0
, by (
3.1
). Conversely, if
(T(D),\vee_{\Pi(E(D))},\wedge_{\Pi(E(D))})
is a lattice, then it does not have more than one minimal element, by the definition of a lattice.

Lemma 3.3

We define maps

\displaystyle\phi:\uparrow\!\{a\}_{T(D)}

\displaystyle\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{k}\}

\displaystyle\psi:L(a)

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
D
be a connected Eulerian digraph that is not a cycle. Then, we have

G(\mathbbm{1}_{D})=\sum_{a\leq\mathbbm{1}_{D}}F(a)=0

Proof.

Fix an edge
e\in E(D)
. For each minimal element
a\in Q(D)
, let
\beta_{a}
be the unique piece of
a
, containing
e
. By
Remark

3
, we have:

|\mathfrak{C}(D)|=|\mathcal{W}^{e}(D)|=\sum_{a\in Q(D)}|\mathcal{W}_{a}^{e}(D)|

For a minimal element
a\in Q(D)
, we have
G(a)=F(a)=(-1)^{|a|}
. Also, by
Lemma

3.3
, the up-set of
a
is isomorphic to the bond lattice,
\uparrow\!\!\{a\}_{T(D)}\cong L(a)
, and so by
Lemma

2.11
, the Möbius function satisfies
\mu_{\uparrow\!\{a\}_{T(D)}}\left(a,\mymathbb{1}_{T(D)}\right)=\mu_{L(a)}\left(\mymathbb{0}_{L(a)},\mymathbb{1}_{L(a)}\right)=(-1)^{1-|a|}|\mathbb{P}^{\beta_{a}}(a,\mathcal{R})|
.

We apply induction on the number of edges
|E(D)|
, to show that
G(\mathbbm{1}_{D})=0
. For the basis step, let
D
be a connected Eulerian digraph with
|E(D)|=2
. Then, the statement vacuously holds, as the only connected Eulerian digraph on two edges is the single directed cycle. For the inductive step, let
|E(D)|\geq 3
be fixed.

Consider any non-minimal element
b=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\}\in T(D)
with
b<_{T(D)}\mymathbb{1}_{D}
. In particular,
b
has at least
k\geq 2
parts, each part defining an Eulerian digraph. Also,
b
has at least one component, say
B_{i_{0}}
(for
1\leq i_{0}\leq k
), which is not a cycle and satisfies
|B_{i_{0}}|<|E(D)|
. By the inductive hypothesis, we obtain
G(\mymathbb{1}_{B_{i_{0}}})=0
. On the other hand, for each
c\leq_{T(D)}b
, there is a partition of
c:=c_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup c_{k}
such that
c_{i}=\{C_{i1},\ldots,C_{ir_{i}}\}
and
B_{i}=C_{i1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup C_{ir_{i}}
. In other words, we have
c_{i}\in T(B_{i})
, for each
i=1,\ldots,k
. It follows that,

\displaystyle G(b)=\sum_{c\leq_{T(D)}b}F(c)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\leq_{T(D)}b\\
c=c_{1}\cup\ldots\cup c_{k},\\
c_{i}\in T(B_{i})\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{r_{i}}-|\mathfrak{C}(C_{ij})|

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{x=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}\leq\mymathbb{1}_{B_{i}}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}-|\mathfrak{C}(X_{j})|

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{x\leq_{T(B_{i})}\mymathbb{1}_{B_{i}}}F(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}G(\mymathbb{1}_{B_{i}})=0

\displaystyle\text{ by the inductive hypothesis, since $G(\mymathbb{1}_{B_{i_{0}}})=0$ }.

Therefore,

\displaystyle-|\mathfrak{C}(D)|=F(\mathbbm{1})=\sum_{b\in T(D)}\mu(b,\mathbbm{1})G(b)

by Möbius inversion

\displaystyle=\mu(\mathbbm{1},\mathbbm{1})G(\mathbbm{1})+\sum_{a\in Q(D)}\mu(a,\mathbbm{1})G(a)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\notin Q(D)\\
b\neq\mymathbb{1}\end{subarray}}\mu(b,\mathbbm{1})G(b)

\displaystyle=G(\mathbbm{1})+\sum_{a\in Q(D)}\mu_{T(D)}(a,\mathbbm{1})G(a)

by the inductive hypothesis,
G(b)=0
, for each
b\in T(D)\setminus Q(D)
such that
b\neq\mymathbb{1}
, as shown above

\displaystyle=G(\mathbbm{1})+\sum_{a\in Q(D)}\mu_{\uparrow\!\{a\}_{T(D)}}(a,\mathbbm{1})G(a)

by
Lemma

2.2

\displaystyle=G(\mathbbm{1})+\sum_{a\in Q(D)}\mu_{L(a)}(a,\mathbbm{1})G(a)

by
Lemma

3.3

\displaystyle=G(\mathbbm{1})+\sum_{a\in Q(D)}(-1)^{1-|a|}\cdot|\mathbb{P}^{\beta_{a}}(a,\mathcal{R})|\cdot(-1)^{|a|}

by
Lemma

2.11
and the equation
G(a)=(-1)^{|a|}

\displaystyle=G(\mathbbm{1})-\sum_{a\in Q(D)}|\mathcal{W}_{a}^{e}(D)|

by
Lemma

3.2

\displaystyle=G(\mathbbm{1})-|\mathfrak{C}(D)|

which implies that
G(\mathbbm{1})=0
.
∎

As a corollary, we have:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
D
be a (connected) Eulerian digraph that is not a cycle. Then,

\sum_{k\geq 1}(-1)^{k}f_{k}(D)=0

f_{k}(D)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\}\leq_{T(D)}\mathbbm{1}_{D}\\

∎

3.3
The Martin Polynomial

The definition below is found in
[
8
,
26
,
27
]
:

Definition 18
.

Given a connected digraph
D
,

1.

The
circuit partition polynomial
of
D
is

r_{D}(t):=\sum_{k\geq 1}t^{k}f_{k}(D)

2.

The
Martin polynomial
of
D
is

s_{D}(t):=\dfrac{1}{t-1}r_{D}(t-1)=\sum_{k\geq 1}(t-1)^{k-1}f_{k}(D)

Given a (connected) Eulerian digraph with maximum out-degree
\Delta\geq 2
, then it is shown in
[
26
]
that
s_{D}(t)
is divisible by
\prod_{i=2}^{\Delta}(t+\Delta-i)=(t+\Delta-2)\cdots(t+1)\cdot t
. In particular, unless
D
is a single directed cycle, we obtain

s_{D}(-(\Delta-2))=\ldots=s_{D}(0)=0

and in particular, (
1.1
) follows. Evaluations of
t
at other integer values also yield interesting results (see
[
26
,
8
]
):

1.

r_{D}(0)=0
and
s_{D}(1)=f_{1}(D)=|\mathfrak{C}(D)|

2.

r_{D}(1)=s_{D}(2)=\sum_{k\geq 1}f_{k}(D)=\prod_{v\in V(D)}\textup{deg}_{D}^{+}(v)!

3.

For a fixed integer
k>0
, see
[
8
, Corollary 2, p. 265]
for an expression of
r(-k)
in terms of cycles of
D
.

We derive an identity connecting the Martin polynomial (and the circuit partition polynomial) to the bond lattices of intersection graphs of partitions of
D
into cycles.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
D
be a (connected) Eulerian digraph. Then

s_{D}(1-t)=-\sum_{a\in Q(D)}(-1)^{|a|}\chi_{L(a)}(t)

where
\chi_{L(a)}
is the characteristic polynomial of the bond lattice

(q.v.
Definition

4

Proof.

For each
b=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{k}\}\in T(D)
, we define maps
\phi
and
\psi
between
\{a\in Q(D):a\leq_{T(D)}b\}
and
Q(D_{1})\times\cdots\times Q(D_{k})
which are inverses of each other (compare with
Lemma

3.3
):

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle:a\mapsto(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\text{ where $a_{i}=\{\beta\in a:E(\beta)\subseteq E(D_{i})\}$ }

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle:(c_{1},\ldots,c_{k})\mapsto c_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup c_{k}

Let
\triangleleft
be any linear order on
E(D)
. For each
B\subseteq E(D)
, let
e_{B}
be the minimal edge with respect to
\triangleleft
. Furthermore, for each Eulerian

and each partition

, let
\beta(c)
be the unique cycle of

X
, which we can group by equivalence under
\approx
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{M_{X}}|\mathfrak{C}(X)|=\dfrac{1}{M_{X}}\sum_{O\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(X)}|\mathfrak{C}(O)|=\dfrac{1}{M_{X}}\sum_{\mathbf{R}\in\overline{\mathfrak{R}}(X)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}O\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(X)\\
O\approx D_{\mathbf{R}}\end{subarray}}|\mathfrak{C}(O)|

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{M_{X}}\sum_{\mathbf{R}\in\overline{\mathfrak{R}}(X)}|\{O\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(X):O\approx D_{\mathbf{R}}\}|\cdot|\mathfrak{C}(D_{\mathbf{R}})|

as
|\mathfrak{C}(O)|=|\mathfrak{C}(D_{\mathbf{R}})|
for any orientation
O\approx D_{\mathbf{R}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{M_{X}}\sum_{\mathbf{R}\in\overline{\mathfrak{R}}(X)}\dfrac{M_{X}}{K_{D_{\mathbf{R}}}}\cdot|\mathfrak{C}(D_{\mathbf{R}})|

by
Remark

5

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{R}\in\overline{\mathfrak{R}}(X)}\dfrac{N_{D_{\mathbf{R}}}}{K_{D_{\mathbf{R}}}}\cdot\dfrac{|\mathfrak{C}(D_{\mathbf{R}})|}{N_{D_{\mathbf{R}}}}=\sum_{\mathbf{R}\in\overline{\mathfrak{R}}(X)}|\mathbf{R}|\cdot\dfrac{|\mathfrak{C}(D_{\mathbf{R}})|}{N_{D_{\mathbf{R}}}}=C_{X}.

∎

In what follows, we will be interested in the number of different ways an Eulerian orientation
O\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(X)
of a Veblen hypergraph
X
may be obtained from partitions of
X
into sub-multi-graphs. Hence, we define:

\mathcal{S}_{t}(O)=\{(S,P):S=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}\}\in\mathcal{S}(X),\ P=\{O_{1},\ldots,O_{t}\},\ O_{i}\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(X_{i}),\ O=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{t}O_{i}\}

In particular, note that
\mathcal{S}_{1}(O)=\{(\{X\},\{O\})\}
. We now argue that there is a correspondence between decompositions of the Eulerian circuits of
O
into
t\geq 1
parts and tuples of Eulerian circuits obtained from elements of
\mathcal{S}_{t}(O)
, for each fixed Eulerian orientation
O\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(X)
:

Lemma 4.4
.

For
t\geq 1
, a multi-graph
X
of rank
2
and an Eulerian orientation
O\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(X)
, we have

|\mathfrak{C}_{t}(O)|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(S,P)\in\mathcal{S}_{t}(O)\\
P=\{O_{1},\ldots,O_{t}\}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{t}|\mathfrak{C}(O_{i})|

Proof.

Let
t\geq 1
be fixed. Define the mapping,

\displaystyle f:\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(S,P)\in\mathcal{S}_{t}(O)\\
P=\{O_{1},\ldots,O_{t}\}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{C}_{1}(O_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{C}_{1}(O_{t})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathfrak{C}_{t}(O)

by
f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{t})=\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{t}\}
. This mapping is a bijection, by the definition of
\mathfrak{C}_{t}(O)
and
\mathcal{S}_{t}(O)
.
∎

Finally, we state and prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.5
.

For a connected decomposable Veblen multi-graph
X
of rank
k=2
,

w_{n}(X)=0

Proof.

For any connected
X\in\mathcal{V}^{1}
, if
X
is a decomposable infragraph, then, clearly, no orientation of
X
is a directed cycle.

\displaystyle w_{n}(X)=\sum_{\mathbf{S}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(X)}(-1)^{c(\mathbf{S})}\dfrac{1}{\alpha_{\mathbf{S}}}C_{\mathbf{S}}=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(X)}(-1)^{c(S)}\dfrac{M_{S}}{M_{X}}C_{S}

since
|\mathbf{S}|=\dfrac{M_{X}}{M_{\mathbf{S}}\cdot\alpha_{\mathbf{S}}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{M_{X}}\sum_{t\geq 1}(-1)^{t}\sum_{S=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}\}\in\mathcal{S}_{t}(X)}\prod_{i=1}^{t}M_{X_{i}}C_{X_{i}}

as
M_{S}
and
C_{S}
are multiplicative

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{M_{X}}\sum_{t\geq 1}(-1)^{t}\sum_{S=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}\}\in\mathcal{S}_{t}(X)}\prod_{i=1}^{t}|\mathfrak{C}(X_{i})|

by
Lemma

4.3

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{M_{X}}\sum_{t\geq 1}(-1)^{t}\sum_{S=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}\}\in\mathcal{S}_{t}(X)}\prod_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{O\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(X_{i})}|\mathfrak{C}(O_{i})|

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{M_{X}}\sum_{t\geq 1}(-1)^{t}\sum_{O\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(X)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(S,P)\in\mathcal{S}_{t}(O)\\
P=\{O_{1},\ldots,O_{t}\}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{t}|\mathfrak{C}(O_{i})|

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{M_{X}}\sum_{O\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}(X)}\sum_{t\geq 1}(-1)^{t}|\mathfrak{C}_{t}(O)|

which is equal to zero, by
Corollary

3.5
, provided
X
is decomposable.
∎

By
Theorem

4.5
and
Theorem

4.1
, for a graph
\mathcal{G}
of rank
2

\chi_{\mathcal{G}}(t)

\geq 3

Theorem

4.2
) follows.

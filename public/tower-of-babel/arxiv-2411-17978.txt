Convergence of the inverse Monge-Ampere flow and Nadel multiplier ideal sheaves. Nikita Klemyatin


Abstract
We generalize the inverse Monge-Ampere flow, which was introduced in [ 9 ] by Collins, Hisamoto and Takahashi, and provide conditions that guarantee the convergence of the flow without a priori assumption that X X has a Kähler-Einstein metric. We also show that if the underlying manifold does not admit Kähler-Einstein metric, then the flow develops Nadel multiplier ideal sheaves. In addition, we establish the linear lower bound for inf X φ \inf_{X}\varphi , and the theorem of Darvas and He for the inverse Monge-Ampere flow.



1
Introduction

The search for Kähler-Einstein metrics in Kähler geometry goes back to the famous work of Calabi
[
7
]
, and Yau’s solution to Calabi conjecture
[
34
]
. It is well-known that on Fano manifolds there are certain obstructions to the existence of Kähler-Einstein metrics.

Suppose that
(X,\omega)
is a compact Kähler manifold,
\beta
is a closed semipositive
(1,1)
-form, and
[\omega]=c_{1}(X)-[\beta]
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}~|~\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi>0\}
. For a given metric
\omega_{\varphi}
within the cohomology class
[\omega]
we have the following identity:

\displaystyle\textrm{Ric}(\omega_{\varphi})=\omega_{\varphi}+\beta+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\rho,

(1.1)

where
\rho=\rho_{\varphi}
is a function (usually called the Ricci potential), that is defined up to a constant. The identity above is equivalent to a Monge-Ampere type identity:

\displaystyle e^{\rho}\omega^{n}_{\varphi}=e^{\rho_{0}-\varphi+c_{\omega}}\omega^{n}_{0}.

(1.2)

Here
c_{\omega}
is a normalizing constant.

One could try to find
\varphi
, such that the corresponding
\rho
equals zero. In other words, one may try to find a function
\varphi
, such that

\displaystyle\textrm{Ric}(\omega_{\varphi})=\omega_{\varphi}+\beta.

(1.3)

Such metric
\omega_{\varphi}
(if it exists) is called the
twisted Kähler-Einstein metric
. They satisfy the following Monge-Ampere equation:

\displaystyle\omega^{n}_{\varphi}=e^{\rho_{0}-\varphi+c_{\omega}}\omega^{n}_{0}.

(1.4)

It is well-known that this equation does not always admit a smooth solution.

There is a great interest in searching for such metrics, with
\beta
being either a semipositive form (see
[
10
,
14
]
), or a positive current (see
[
2
,
3
,
19
,
35
,
36
,
28
]
and the references therein).

We consider the inverse Monge-Ampere flow, introduced in
[
9
]
for the case
\beta=0
. The inverse Monge-Ampere flow is a gradient flow for the functional
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
(see
2.9
below for the definition). On the level of potentials, it can be written as follows:

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}=1-e^{\rho}=1-{\omega^{n}_{0}\over\omega^{n}_{\varphi}}e^{-\varphi+\rho+c(t)}.

(1.5)

Here
\rho
is the Ricci potential of the evolving metric
\omega_{\varphi}
, such that
{1\over V}\int_{X}e^{\rho}\omega^{n}_{\varphi}=1
, and
c(t)=-\log\big({1\over V}\int_{X}e^{\rho_{0}-\varphi}\omega^{n}_{0}\big)
is a normalizing constant. Since the
(1,1)
-form
\beta
does not affect the equation on the level of potentials, we will call it the inverse Monge-Ampere flow without, regardless of the presence of
\beta
.

It was shown in
[
9
, Proposition 2.7]
that the uniform equivalence of evolving metrics implies the uniform estimates for the solution. In other words, if there is a constant
A>0
, such that
A^{-1}\omega_{0}\leqslant\omega_{\varphi}\leqslant A\omega_{0}
, then all the derivatives of
\varphi
are Holder continious. This condition implies a sequential convergence, but it is a way too strong assumption, which is hard to establish. We want to find milder conditions, that would guarantee the convergence, at least in the case when
H^{0}(X,TX)=0
.

In order to find such conditions, in section
3
we study the relations between
\alpha
-invariant and the inverse Monge-Ampere flow. In
3
we show, that if
\alpha
-invariant is bigger than
{n\over n+1}
, then one has the upper bound for
\sup_{X}\varphi
. Hence, one could think that this bound should imply the convergence of the flow. The following theorem says that it is true.

Theorem 1.1:

Assume that
(X,\omega_{0})
is a compact Kähler manifold. Let
\beta\geqslant 0
be a semipositive
(1,1)
-form, such that
c_{1}(X)=[\omega_{0}]+[\beta]
. Assume further, that
X
does not have holomorphic vector fields.
Then the following estimates along the inverse Monge-Ampere flow are equivalent and imply the convergence of the flow:

(1)

\sup_{X}\varphi
is uniformly bounded from above;

(2)

The average
{1\over V}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}_{0}
is uniformly bounded from above;

(3)

J(\varphi)
and
d_{1}(0,\varphi)
are uniformly bounded from above;

(4)

I(\varphi)
is uniformly bounded;

(5)

For some
p>1
the integral
{1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-p\varphi}\omega^{n}_{0}
is uniformly bounded.

The following bounds imply the convergence of the inverse Monge-Ampere flow.

(6)

The alpha-invariant
\alpha(X,\omega_{0})
is bigger than
{n\over n+1}
;

(7)

\textrm{osc}(\varphi)
is uniformly bounded along the flow;

(8)

\inf_{X}\varphi
is uniformly bounded below along the flow;

(9)

For some
p>1
the distance
d_{p}(0,\varphi)
is uniformly bounded from above along the flow.

In particular, if
(X,\omega_{0})
does not admit a twisted Kähler-Einstein metric, then both
||\varphi||_{C^{0}}
and
{1\over V}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}_{0}
are unbounded.

These bounds are known for the case of Kähler Ricci flow, see
[
8
,
27
,
25
,
29
]
. Again, as in
[
27
]
, the bound on the number
p
in (5) is sharp, and
p=1
does not imply the convergence of the flow. However, the upper bound in the borderline case
p=1
implies semistability of
X
.

It is worth mentioning that in
[
29
]
the similar theorem about the multiplier ideal sheaves was proved using the Perelman estimates and the uniform Sobolev inequality along the Kähler-Ricci flow. For the twisted Monge-Ampere flow, there is no known uniform Sobolev inequality. Moreover, there is no analog of Perelman’s estimates. It was shown in
[
9
, Section 6]
, that the Ricci potential may be unbounded along the flow if the underlying Fano manifold is unstable. Thus, we cannot rely on the methods, that worked in the case of the Kähler-Ricci flow. Instead, we use the pluripotential theory to obtain the above theorem.

As a result, the claims for
\inf_{X}\varphi
and
for
\sup_{X}\varphi
in
1
are obtained in very different ways. In particular, the proof of this theorem does not show any direct connection (like Harnack inequality in the case of Kähler-Ricci flow) between
\inf_{X}\varphi
and
\sup_{X}\varphi
. Below, in
1
, we will address this particular issue.

Next, as an application of

converge in

to

is a proper multiplier ideal sheaf, and

\displaystyle H^{q}(X,-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor K_{X}\otimes\mathcal{I}(\alpha\psi_{\infty}))=0,~~\forall q\geqslant 1.

(1.6)

Thus,

and

Finally, we address the question, asked by
[

about the lower linear bound on
\inf\varphi

in finite time was obtained in

Theorem 1.3:

Let
\varphi
be a solution to the Inverse Monge-Ampere flow.
Then there is a constant

\displaystyle||\varphi||_{C^{0}}\leqslant M(t+1)

(1.7)

The converse is also true, and it follows from the known bounds for the inverse Monge-Ampere flow.

Assume that
\varphi_{t}
is a diverging trajectory of the inverse Monge-Ampere flow, which satisfies the assumptions of

, such that for any

2
The Inverse Monge-Ampere flow.

Consider a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega_{0})
, and a closed semipositive form
\beta
, such that
[\omega_{0}]+[\beta]=c_{1}(X)
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X)~|~\omega_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi>0\}
,and
\rho=\rho_{\varphi}
is the Ricci potential of the metric
\omega_{\varphi}
, i.e.

\textrm{Ric}(\omega_{\varphi})=\omega_{\varphi}+\beta+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\rho,~~~~{1\over V}\int_{X}e^{\rho}\omega^{n}_{\varphi}=1,~~~~V=\int_{X}\omega^{n}_{0}.

(2.1)

We consider the following flow:

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}=1-e^{\rho}

(2.2)

or, writing it in terms of evolution of the metric,

\displaystyle\dot{g}_{j\overline{k}}=-\partial_{j}\partial_{\overline{k}}(e^{\rho})=-\rho_{j\overline{k}}e^{\rho}-\rho_{j}\rho_{\overline{k}}e^{\rho}=-e^{\rho}(R_{j\overline{k}}-g_{j\overline{k}}-\beta_{j\overline{k}}+\rho_{j}\rho_{\overline{k}}).

(2.3)

We may rewrite the flow as parabolic MA equation, similar to an equation, introduced by Krylov
[
23
]
:

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}=1-{\omega^{n}_{0}\over\omega^{n}_{\varphi}}e^{-\varphi+\rho_{0}+c(t)},~~~c(t)=-\log\Bigg({1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-\varphi+\rho_{0}}\omega_{0}^{n}\Bigg).

(2.4)

Here we used the fact that the solution of the inverse MA flow satisfies the following identity:

\displaystyle e^{\rho}\omega^{n}_{\varphi}=e^{-\varphi+\rho_{0}+c(t)}\omega^{n}_{0}.

(2.5)

Below we collect the basic estimates, which are known from
[
9
]
in the case
\beta=0
.

Proposition 2.1:

Along the inverse Monge-Ampere flow with
\beta\geqslant 0
the following estimates hold:

1.

\varphi\leqslant t+A_{1}
;

2.

\rho\geqslant-t+c(t)+A_{2}
;

3.

c(0)+t\dot{c}(0)\leqslant c(t)\leqslant\sup_{X}\varphi-\inf_{X}\rho_{0}
.

Here
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are uniform constants.

Proof:

1.

Follows from the equation of the flow
2.2
;

2.

The proof is the same as in
[
9
, Lemma 4.2]
;

3.

The first inequality follows from the convexity of
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
. It is proved in
2
below. The second inequality follows from the definition of
c(t)
and the Jensen inequality:

\displaystyle c(t)=-\log\Bigg({1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-\varphi+\rho_{0}}\omega_{0}^{n}\Bigg)\leqslant\int_{X}(\varphi-\rho_{0})\omega_{0}^{n}\leqslant\sup_{X}\varphi-\inf_{X}\rho_{0}.

(2.6)

Now we need to recall some functionals on
\mathcal{H}
. First of all, we have the
I
-functional, which is given by

\displaystyle I(\varphi)={1\over V}\int_{X}\varphi\big(\omega^{n}_{0}-\omega^{n}_{\varphi}\big).

(2.7)

The
J
-functional is defined to be

\displaystyle J(\varphi)={1\over V}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}{n-j\over n+1}\int_{X}\sqrt{-1}\partial\varphi\wedge\overline{\partial}\varphi\wedge\omega^{j}_{\varphi}\wedge\omega^{n-j-1}_{0}.

(2.8)

There is an inequality between these two functionals:
{1\over n}J\leqslant I-J\leqslant nJ
.

The functional
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
and the energy
\textrm{E}(\varphi)
are given by the following:

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(\varphi)&=-\log\Bigg({1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-\varphi+\rho_{0}}\omega^{n}_{0}\Bigg)-\textrm{E}(\varphi)=c(t)-\textrm{E}(\varphi),\\
\textrm{E}(\varphi)&={1\over V}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}_{0}-J(\varphi),\\
\delta\textrm{E}(\varphi)&={1\over V}\int_{X}\delta\varphi\omega^{n}_{\varphi}.\end{split}

(2.9)

We note that the inverse Monge-Ampere flow is the gradient functional for
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
(see formula
2.14
below).

Finally, the Mabuchi functional is defined by

\mathcal{M}(\varphi):={1\over V}\int_{X}\log{\omega^{n}_{\varphi}\over\omega^{n}_{0}}\omega^{n}_{\varphi}-{1\over V}\int_{X}\rho_{0}\omega^{n}_{\varphi}-(I-J)(\varphi).

(2.10)

It is worth to mention the relation between Mabuchi functional and
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
along the inverse Monge-Ampere flow.

Proposition 2.2:

Along the inverse Monge-Ampere flow we have

\mathcal{M}(\varphi)=\mathcal{F}(\varphi)-{1\over V}\int_{X}\rho\omega^{n}_{\varphi}.

In particular,
\mathcal{M}(\varphi)\geqslant\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
along the flow.

Proof:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\varphi)={1\over V}\int_{X}(-\rho-\varphi+\rho_{0}+c(t))\omega^{n}_{\varphi}-{1\over V}\int_{X}\rho_{0}\omega^{n}_{\varphi}-(I-J)(\varphi)

(2.11)

\displaystyle=J(\varphi)-{1\over V}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}_{0}+c(t)-{1\over V}\int_{X}\rho\omega^{n}_{\varphi}=\mathcal{F}(\varphi)-{1\over V}\int_{X}\rho\omega^{n}_{\varphi}.

(2.12)

By the Jensen inequality, we know that

\displaystyle{1\over V}\int_{X}\rho\omega^{n}_{\varphi}\leqslant\log\Bigg({1\over V}\int_{X}e^{\rho}\omega^{n}_{\varphi}\Bigg)\leqslant 0.

(2.13)

The variation of
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{M}(\varphi)
are given by the following expressions:

\begin{split}\delta\mathcal{F}(\varphi)&={1\over V}\int_{X}\delta\varphi(e^{\rho}-1)\omega^{n}_{\varphi},\\
\delta\mathcal{M}(\varphi)&=-{1\over V}\int_{X}\delta\varphi(R-n-\textrm{Tr}_{\omega_{\varphi}}\beta)\omega^{n}_{\varphi}.\end{split}

(2.14)

The following lemma describes the behaviour of these functionals along the flow.

Lemma 2.3:

Along the inverse Monge-Ampere flow we have the following identities

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{F}}(\varphi)=-{1\over V}\int_{X}(e^{\rho}-1)^{2}\omega^{n}_{\varphi},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi)=-{1\over V}\int_{X}|\nabla\rho|^{2}e^{\rho}\omega^{n}_{\varphi}.

(2.16)

\displaystyle\dot{\textrm{E}}(\varphi)=0.~~~~~~~~~~~

(2.17)

In particular,
\dot{c}(t)\leqslant 0
along the flow.

Proof:

The statement for
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
functional follows from the variation formula and the flow equation
\dot{\varphi}=1-e^{\rho}
. For
\mathcal{M}(\varphi)
we have the following identity along the flow:

\dot{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi)=-{1\over V}\int_{X}(1-e^{\rho})\Delta\rho\omega^{n}_{\varphi}=-{1\over V}\int_{X}|\nabla\rho|^{2}e^{\rho}\omega^{n}_{\varphi}\leqslant 0.

(2.18)

Finally, the statement for
\dot{\textrm{E}}(\varphi)
follows from the normalization condition for
\rho
along the flow.

Remark 2.4:

The content of this lemma was proved in various statements in
[
9
]
. However, our proof is simpler for
\mathcal{M}(\varphi)
than
[
9
, Lemma 4.7]
.

In
[
9
, Proposition 2.4 and Corollary 2.5]
authors also compute the second derivative of
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
along the inverse Monge-Ampere flow:

\displaystyle\ddot{\mathcal{F}}(\varphi)={2\over V}\int_{X}\Big(|\nabla\widetilde{f}|^{2}-|\widetilde{f}|^{2}\Big)d\mu,~

(2.19)

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}:=e^{\rho}-1-{1\over V}\int_{X}(e^{\rho}-1)d\mu,~

(2.20)

\displaystyle d\mu=e^{\rho}\omega^{n}_{\varphi}.~~~~~~~~~~~

(2.21)

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5:

If
\beta\geqslant 0
, then
\mathcal{F}
is convex along the inverse Monge-Ampere flow.

Proof:

The statement would easily follow the fact that the operator
-L_{\rho}=\nabla^{\overline{m}}\nabla_{\overline{m}}f+\nabla^{\overline{m}}\rho\nabla_{\overline{m}}
. The proof for
\beta=0
is given in
[
25
, Lemma 2]
, and it applies here as well. For reader’s convenience, we briefly recall the main steps here.

If
\beta\geqslant 0
, then the application of
\nabla_{\overline{m}}
to the equation
\nabla^{\overline{m}}\nabla_{\overline{m}}f+\nabla^{\overline{m}}\rho\nabla_{\overline{m}}f=-\nu f
, and integration against
\nabla^{\overline{m}}fd\mu
gives us the following identity

\displaystyle(\nu-1)\int_{X}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu=\int_{X}|\overline{\nabla}\overline{\nabla}f|^{2}+\beta^{j\overline{k}}\nabla_{j}f\nabla_{\overline{k}}.fd\mu

(2.22)

The claim about the spectrum follows from the nonnegativity of
\beta
.

Now we state the theorem about the behavior of the twisted inverse MA flow.

Theorem 2.6:

Let
(X,\omega_{0})
be a compact Kähler manifold with no holomorphic vector fields, and
c_{1}(X)=[\omega_{0}]+[\beta]
for a nonnegative
(1,1)
-form
\beta
. Then the inverse Monge-Ampere flow exists for all times, and if there is a twisted Kähler-Einstein metric, then the flow converges to it.

Proof:

Since the flow looks the same for both twisted and non-twisted cases,
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
is convex, the arguments from
[
9
, Theorem 4.6 and Theorem 4.11]
together with
[
1
,
4
,
5
]
carry over verbatim to the twisted case.

Remark 2.7:

In fact, one do not really need the presence of the KE metric. As it was pointed out in
[
9
]
, the coercivity of
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
or
\mathcal{M}(\varphi)
is enough for existence of the twisted KE metric.

3
The inverse Monge-Ampere flow and the alpha-invariant.

In this section, we show that the alpha-invariant inequality holds along the inverse MA flow. It is similar to the inequality along the Kähler-Ricci flow. Let

\displaystyle\alpha(X,\omega_{0}):=\sup\Biggl\{\alpha>0~\vline~\int_{X}e^{-\alpha\varphi}\omega^{n}_{0}<+\infty,~\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{H},~\sup_{X}\varphi=0\Biggr\}.

(3.1)

This is a well-known
\alpha
-invariant, which was introduced by Tian
[
30
]
in the case of Fano manifolds. It was shown in
[
30
]
, that a Fano manifold
X
without holomorphic vector fields, which satisfies
\alpha(X,c_{1}(X))>{n\over n+1}
, admits a Kähler-Einstein metric. Hence, by the results from
[
9
]
, one has the convergence of the inverse Monge-Ampere flow. However, this is not a direct way to obtain the convergence of the flow, and one would like to find a more direct approach, similarly to
[
8
,
29
]
, where the relations between the
\alpha
-invariant and the convergence of the Kähler-Ricci flow were established directly.

Before we proceed, we need a lemma:

Lemma 3.1:

Along the inverse MA flow we have the following inequality:

\displaystyle-{1\over V}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}_{\varphi}\leqslant n\sup_{X}\varphi+C.

(3.2)

Proof:

Note that for a negative PSH-function
\psi=\varphi-\sup_{X}\varphi
we have the following inequality:

\displaystyle-{1\over V}\int_{X}\psi\omega^{n}_{\psi}\leqslant-(n+1)\textrm{E}(\psi)=-(n+1)\textrm{E}(\varphi)+(n+1)\sup_{X}\varphi,

(3.3)

and
\textrm{E}(\varphi)
is constant along the flow. Note also that
\omega_{\psi}=\omega_{\varphi}
, and

\displaystyle-{1\over V}\int_{X}\psi\omega^{n}_{\psi}=-{1\over V}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}_{\varphi}+\sup_{X}\varphi

(3.4)

Now we state the alpha-invariant inequality along the flow:

Proposition 3.2:

For any
\alpha>0
the following inequality holds along the inverse Monge-Ampere flow:

\displaystyle((n+1)\alpha-n)\sup_{X}\varphi\leqslant\log\Bigg({1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-\alpha(\varphi-\sup_{X}\varphi)}\omega^{n}_{0}\Bigg)+C.

(3.5)

If
\alpha(X,\omega_{0})>{n\over n+1}
, then there is a uniform constant
C_{1}>0
, such that
\sup_{X}\varphi\leqslant C_{1}
along the inverse Monge-Ampere flow.

Proof:

By the Jensen inequality and
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle{1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-\alpha(\varphi-\sup_{X}\varphi)}\omega^{n}_{0}={1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-\alpha(\varphi-\sup_{X}\varphi)}\cdot e^{\rho+\varphi-\rho_{0}-c(t)}\omega^{n}_{\varphi}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\geqslant\exp\Bigg({1-\alpha\over V}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}_{\varphi}+\alpha\sup_{X}\varphi+{1\over V}\int_{X}(\rho-\rho_{0}-c(t))\omega^{n}_{\varphi}\Bigg).

(3.7)

Taking the logarithm of the both sides and using
3
, we obtain the following inequality:

\displaystyle((n+1)\alpha-n)\sup_{X}\varphi\leqslant{1\over V}\int_{X}\Big(-\rho+\rho_{0}+c(t)\Big)\omega^{n}_{\varphi}+\log\Bigg({1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-\alpha(\varphi-\sup_{X}\varphi)}\omega^{n}_{0}\Bigg).

(3.8)

By
2
and
2
, we have the following

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\varphi(0))\geqslant\mathcal{M}(\varphi)=\mathcal{F}(\varphi)-{1\over V}\int_{X}\rho\omega^{n}_{\varphi}=c(t)-{1\over V}\int_{X}\rho\omega^{n}_{\varphi}+C.

(3.9)

Hence, the first term on the right hand side is bounded above, and the first statement follows.
Now, if
\alpha>{n\over n+1}
, then
((n+1)\alpha-n)>0
, and the right hand side in
3.8
is bounded. Hence, the second statement follows.

4
The convergence of the inverse Monge-Ampere flow.

In this section we prove
1
. First, we show that the convergence of the flow is guaranteed if
\sup_{X}\varphi
is bounded.

Proposition 4.1:

Let
(X,\omega_{0})
be a compact Kähler manifold with
H^{0}(X,T^{1,0}X)=0
, and
[\omega_{0}]+{\beta}=c_{1}(X)
for a closed nonnegative form
\beta
. Suppose
\varphi=\varphi(t)
is a solution to the inverse Monge-Ampere flow. Then following statements are equivalent:

1

The inverse Monge-Ampere flow converges and
X
has a twisted Kähler-Einstein metric;

2

There is a uniform constant
C>0
, such that
\sup_{X}\varphi\leqslant C
;

3

There is a uniform constant
C>0
, such that the average
{1\over V}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}_{0}
is bounded from above.

Proof:

The implication
(1)\implies(3)
follows from the argument
[
9
]
. Indeed, if a twisted KE metric exists, then by
[
25
]
, the coercivity of the Mabuchi functional implies the upper bound on
{1\over V}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}_{0}
. The equivalence
(2)\iff(3)
follows from the standard inequality

\displaystyle{1\over V}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}_{0}\leqslant\sup_{X}\varphi\leqslant{1\over V}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{n}_{0}+C(X,\omega_{0}).

(4.1)

Now we show the implication
(2)\implies(1)

Theorem 6.3:

Assume that
\varphi_{t}
is a diverging trajectory of the inverse Monge-Ampere flow, which satisfies the assumptions of
1
. Then there exist a curve
\varphi^{t}
, such that for any

it is nontrivial
d_{p}
geodesic ray, weakly asymptotic to
\varphi_{t}
. Moreover:

(1)

The functional
\mathcal{F}
is convex and decreasing along the geodesic ray;

(2)

The normalized ray
\varphi^{t}-\sup_{X}(\varphi^{t}-\varphi^{0})
converges to
\varphi^{\infty}
, such that
{1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-{n\over n+1}\varphi^{\infty}}\omega^{n}_{0}=+\infty
.

Proof:

We already established the existence of asymptotic geodesic ray. Thus, we need to prove only (1) and (2).

(1)

Since
\lim_{j\to+\infty}d_{2}(\varphi^{t}_{j},\varphi^{t})=0
, and
\mathcal{F}
is continious with respect to
d_{2}
, the proof of this claim is the same, as
[
13
, Theorem 3.3]
;

(2)

By
3
, and the property of
d_{1}
-distance along the inverse Monge-Ampere flow, we have

\displaystyle-\log\Bigg({1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-\alpha(\varphi_{t}-\sup_{X}\varphi_{t})+\rho_{0}}\omega^{n}_{0}\Bigg)\leqslant

(6.18)

\displaystyle-\log\Bigg({1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-\alpha(\varphi_{t}-\sup_{X}\varphi_{t})}\omega^{n}_{0}\Bigg)+C_{1}\leqslant-((n+1)\alpha-n)Cd_{1}(0,\varphi_{t})+C_{2}.

(6.19)

Now we pick
\alpha\in\big({n\over n+1};1\big)

, and

\varphi^{t}_{j}
as above, and assume it is naturally parametrized. The same reasoning, as in
[

shows, that

\displaystyle-\log\Bigg({1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-\alpha(\varphi^{t}_{j}-\sup_{X}\varphi^{t}_{j})+\rho_{0}}\omega^{n}_{0}\Bigg)=-\log\Bigg({1\over V}\int_{X}e^{-\alpha\varphi^{t}_{j}+\rho_{0}}\omega^{n}_{0}\Bigg)-\alpha\sup_{X}\varphi^{t}_{j}

is convex as well. By

for all

Doege–Potter syndrome in benign relapse of solitary fibrous tumour of the pleura: An exotic clinical setting
Authors: Julián Rondón-Carvajal, Luis Ernesto Rodríguez-Month, Andrés García-Montoya, José Julián Venegas-Sánchez, Luis Antonio Rodríguez-Arrieta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Solitary fibrous tumors are mesenchymal neoplasms that make up 2% of soft tissue tumors. Doege-Potter syndrome (DPS) occurs between 5-10% of patients with solitary fibrous tumor of the pleura (SFTP). Little is mentioned about DPS in the relapse setting, where malignant transformation should always be ruled out (until 12% of cases) according to tumor size, histopathologic aspects (including mitotic index) and immunohistochemical markers. This is the first case of Doege-Potter syndrome in the setting of a fibrous tumor relapse of the pleura without malignant transformation described in Colombia.

Doege–Potter syndrome (DPS) is a rare paraneoplastic condition associated with solitary fibrous tumours (SFTs).[1] It is a cause of non-pancreatic islet cell-mediated hypoglycaemia due to the secretion of an inadequately synthesised, high-molecular-weight insulin-like growth factor 2 (IGF-2).[12] Its presentation is typical in primary SFTs; its presence in tumour recurrences, especially without malignant transformation, is extremely rare.

We report the case of an 81-year-old Colombian man with a history of a benign pleural SFT previously resected 5 years earlier. Post-operative course was optimal until progressive dyspnoea and deterioration in functional class occurred. A large left paracardiac mass was found [Figure 1]. During hospitalisation, he developed recurrent fasting hypoglycaemia (lowest glucose: 24 mg/dL), fulfilling Whipple’s triad. Fasting test studies showed basal Cortisol (8 AM): 11.7 µg/dL (NR: 5–25 µg/dL), C-peptide: 0.16 ng/mL (NR: 1.1–4.4 ng/mL), basal insulin: 0.35 µIU/mL (NR: 1.9–23.0 µIU/mL), proinsulin: 0.5 pmol/L (NR: 2–2.6 pmol/L), Pancreatic islet antibody: 0.4 (negative; NR: <1), beta-hydroxybutyrate: negative, confirming non-hyperinsulinemic hypoglycaemia, with suppressed insulin, C-peptide, and proinsulin levels. IGF-2 measurement could not be performed.

Admission chest X-ray. Chest X-ray showing diffuse opacity/mass of the left lower lung (thin arrow). Opacity displaces the heart posteriorly. The opacity obscures the left heart border and left haemidiaphragm

Imaging showed a 30 cm × 30 cm extrapulmonary mass in the left hemithorax with compressive effect [Figure 2]. Percutaneous biopsy confirmed a spindle cell mesenchymal tumour with a low mitotic index (5 mitoses/10 high-power fields), no necrosis, and positive immunohistochemistry for CD34, CD99, BCL-2, and STAT6, confirming a benign recurrence of an SFT [Figure 3]. Hypoglycaemia was corrected with 10% dextrose and prednisolone (30 mg/day). Successful surgical resection was performed via left thoracotomy. The tumour was encapsulated and compressing, but without invasion of adjacent tissues [Figure 4]. Hypoglycaemia resolved completely post-operatively.

DPS occurs in <5% of SFTs, often in large tumours, and is unusual in recurrent benign tumours. IGF-2-mediated hypoglycaemia is particularly dangerous in older adults, as it increases the risk of falls, serious fractures, and cognitive decline.[1] Our patient met criteria for DPS based on clinical presentation, exclusion of insulin- or secretagogue-dependent etiologies, response to tumour resection, and classic histological findings.[2] Notably, the initial tumour did not cause hypoglycaemia, suggesting that biochemical activity emerged only during recurrence.

Cases of DPS have been previously described in our country [Table 1]; however, we consider this to be the first Colombian case of DPS in a benign recurrence of SFTs without malignant histological features. Recurrence of SFTs is usually associated with malignancy; however, even histologically benign relapses can present serious metabolic complications.[12] This case highlights the need for long-term surveillance of patients with SFTs and maintaining a high clinical suspicion for hypoglycaemia, especially in older patients with large chest lesions.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Preliminaries

Let
E
and
E^{\prime}
be isogenous elliptic curves defined over a finite field
k
. In a series of papers
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
we considered variants of the following question.

Question 1
.

If
E(k)\simeq E^{\prime}(k)
, is it true that
E(K)\simeq E^{\prime}(K)
as
K
varies over finite extensions of
k
?

The answer is no, and it is not hard to come up with explicit counterexamples. However, in
[
3
]
the first-named author showed that if
\ell
is an odd prime number and
E
and
E^{\prime}
are
\ell
-isogenous, where the kernel is generated by a
k
-rational point of order
\ell
, then
E(k)\simeq E^{\prime}(k)
implies that
E(K)\simeq E^{\prime}(K)
for all finite extensions
K
of
k
. If
\ell=2
then it was shown in
[
4
]
if
E(k)\simeq E^{\prime}(k)

and

E(\mathbf{k})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{k})
, where
\mathbf{k}
is the unique quadratic extension of
k
, then
E(K)\simeq E^{\prime}(K)
for all finite extensions
K
of
k
.

Building on this, in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
we fixed 2-isogenous elliptic curves
E
and
E^{\prime}
over
\mathbf{Q}
and defined a prime
p
to be
anomalous
for the pair
(E,E^{\prime})
if
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
, but
E(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}})\not\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}})
. The main result of these papers is that the set of anomalous primes has a well-defined density that can be realized by summing a certain geometric series associated to the 2-adic representations of
E
and
E^{\prime}
. For example, if the 2-adic representations of
E
and
E^{\prime}
are as large as possible (since they are
2
-isogenous, each has a rational
2
-torsion point, so the images have index
3
in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2})
), then the anomalous primes for
(E,E^{\prime})
have density 1/30.

In the course of determining the density of anomalous primes for all pairs
(E,E^{\prime})
of 2-isogenous elliptic curves, there is a simpler question that we did not answer:

Question 2
.

Let
\ell
be a prime number. If
E
and
E^{\prime}
are
\ell
-isogenous elliptic curves, what is the proportion
P(E,E^{\prime})
of primes
p
for which
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
?

More precisely, we consider the ratio

P(E,E^{\prime})[X]=\frac{\{p\leq X~:~E(\mathbf{F}_{p})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})\}}{\pi(X)},

where
\pi
is the prime-counting function, and its limit
\lim_{X\to\infty}P(E,E^{\prime})[X]
. When this limit exists, we denote it by
P(E,E^{\prime})
. It is a consequence of our prior and current work that the limit always exists and can be computed using the Chebotarev Density Theorem.

1.2.
Main Theorem

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbf{Q}
. Fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
of
\mathbf{Q}
and write
\operatorname{Gal}_{\mathbf{Q}}
for the absolute Galois group. If
\ell
is a prime number, then we write
T_{\ell}E
for the
\ell
-adic Tate module of
E
and

\displaystyle\rho_{E,\ell}:\operatorname{Gal}_{\mathbf{Q}}\to\operatorname{Aut}(T_{\ell}E),\text{ and}

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}_{E,\ell^{n}}:\operatorname{Gal}_{\mathbf{Q}}\to\operatorname{Aut}(T_{\ell}E\otimes\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbf{Z})

for the
\ell
-adic and mod
\ell^{n}
representations of
E
, respectively. If
G\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
is the image of the
\ell
-adic representation, then we write
G(\ell^{n})\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbf{Z})
for its reduction modulo
\ell^{n}
. If
E^{\prime}
is
\ell
-isogenous to
E
then we write
G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}(\ell^{n})
for the images of the
\ell
-adic and mod
\ell^{n}
representations of
E^{\prime}
, respectively. Since
E
and
E^{\prime}
are
\ell
-isogenous over
\mathbf{Q}
, it follows that
G(\ell)
and
G^{\prime}(\ell)
are Borel subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z})
. The level of the
\ell
-adic representation is
\ell^{M}
where
M
is the smallest positive integer for which
G
is the full preimage of
G(\ell^{M})
in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
.

Let
L_{\ell^{m}}=\mathbf{Q}(E[\ell^{m}])
and
L_{\ell^{m}}^{\prime}=\mathbf{Q}(E^{\prime}[\ell^{m}])
be the
\ell^{m}
-division fields of
E
and
E^{\prime}
, respectively. Then
L_{\ell^{m}}
and
L_{\ell^{m}}^{\prime}
are Galois over
\mathbf{Q}
with Galois groups
G(\ell^{m})
and
G^{\prime}(\ell^{m})
, respectively. If
p
is a prime number, let
F
and
F^{\prime}
denote matrix representatives of the Frobenius classes of
E
and
E^{\prime}
, respectively, as elements of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
. Then
F
and
F^{\prime}
will define elements in
G(\ell^{m})
and
G^{\prime}(\ell^{m})
by reduction modulo
\ell^{m}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
m\geq 1
. We define
d_{\ell^{m}}\in[0,1]
to be the proportion of prime numbers such
that
F^{\prime}\not\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}
given that
F\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}
. Similarly, we define
d^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}
to be the proportion of prime numbers such
that
F\not\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}
given that
F^{\prime}\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}
.

In Section
2
we determine the possible values of
d_{\ell^{m}}
and
d^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}
by interpreting conditional probabilities in terms of the degrees of the composite field extensions
[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L_{\ell^{m}}]
and
[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}]
. Depending on the level of the
\ell
-adic images of
E
and
E^{\prime}
, once
m
is sufficiently large it will follow from our work below that
d_{\ell^{m}}=d^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}=1-1/\ell
. In order to calculate
P(E,E^{\prime})
, it is then a matter of determining
[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L_{\ell^{m}}]
,
[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}]
,
|G(\ell^{m})|
, and
|G^{\prime}(\ell^{m})|
for small
m
and then summing a geometric series.

If
E
and
E^{\prime}
do not have CM, then
[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}):G]=[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}):G^{\prime}]
by
[
7
, Prop. 2.1.1]
. Therefore, if the maximum of the levels of their
\ell
-adic representations is
\ell^{M}
then
|G(\ell^{M})|=|G^{\prime}(\ell^{M})|
. By the classification of non-CM
\ell
-adic images in
[
16
]
, once
\ell>5
the level of the
\ell
-adic representations of
E
and
E^{\prime}
is
\ell
(see also
[
8
]
). If the level is
\ell
then, as will follow from our work below,
P(E,E^{\prime})
is completely determined by
|G(\ell)|
and
|G(\ell^{\prime})|
(which must be equal). If
\ell=5
then there are a handful of special cases where the level is 25; here one would need to do separate calculations to determine
d_{5}
and
d_{5}^{\prime}
, but then the calculation of
P(E,E^{\prime})
would proceed similarly to the level
\ell
case. If
\ell=2
or 3, then there are many possibilities for the 2- and 3-adic Galois images, as well as for the values of
d_{\ell^{m}}
and
d_{\ell^{m}}^{\prime}
, for small values of
m
(we can always take
m\leq 5
). However, these field extensions are small enough that one could, in principle, through routine computation, determine all possible
P(E,E^{\prime})
for all pairs of 2- and 3-isogenous elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
using the classification of 2-adic images in
[
15
]
and 3-adic images in
[
16
]
. The general result on the values of the
d_{\ell^{m}}
and
d_{\ell^{m}}^{\prime}
is as follows.

Proposition 1.2
.

With notation as above, suppose the elliptic curves
E
and
E^{\prime}
do not have CM and that the maximum of the levels of their
\ell
-adic representations is
\ell^{M}
. Then we have
d_{\ell^{m}},d^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}\in\{0,1-1/\ell\}
for all
m\geq 1
and
d_{\ell^{m}}=d^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}=1-1/\ell
for all
m\geq M
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
E
and
E^{\prime}
be
\ell
-isogenous elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
. Then the proportion
P(E,E^{\prime})
of primes such that
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
is given by the formula

(1.4)

\displaystyle P(E,E^{\prime})=1-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{d_{\ell^{m}}}{|G(\ell^{m})|}+\frac{d^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}}{|G^{\prime}(\ell^{m})|}\right).

Remark 1.5
.

Theorem
1.3
holds for all pairs of
\ell
-isogenous elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
, including those with CM. However, there are some significant differences in the CM case that make the evaluation of
P(E,E^{\prime})
much more straightforward. We will show in Section
4
below that
P(E,E^{\prime})=1
once
\ell>3
. If
\ell=2
or 3, then we can have
P(E,E^{\prime})\neq 1
but, as we will also show, there are only a handful of possibilities for
P(E,E^{\prime})
. This complements
[
6
, Thm. 1.11]
where we proved that if
E
and
E^{\prime}
are 2-isogenous CM elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
, then the proportion of anomalous primes is either
1/12
or 0.

Because the techniques used in this paper are very similar to those of
[

]
and

]
, we aim for brevity by referring the reader, whenever possible, to those background results rather than rederiving them here. Throughout the paper we use the LMFDB

]
for specific examples and Pari/GP

]
for computation.

2.
Main Result

If
E
and
E^{\prime}
are
\ell
-isogenous elliptic curves defined over a finite field
\mathbf{F}_{p}
, then the prime-to-
\ell
parts of the groups
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})
and
E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
are isomorphic
[
3
, Cor. 1]
. Suppose the
\ell
-Sylow subgroup of
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})
has size
\ell^{v}
. If
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})
and
E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
are nonisomorphic, then by the theory of isogeny volcanoes (see
[
17
]
for background) there exists a positive integer
m\leq v/2
such that

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{F}_{p})[\ell^{\infty}]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{m}\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{v-m}\mathbf{Z},\text{ and}

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})[\ell^{\infty}]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{m-1}\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{v-m+1}\mathbf{Z},

or

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{F}_{p})[\ell^{\infty}]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{m}\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{v-m}\mathbf{Z},\text{ and}

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})[\ell^{\infty}]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{m+1}\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{v-m-1}\mathbf{Z}.

In the first case, we have
F\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}
and
F^{\prime}\not\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}
, while in the second case we have
F^{\prime}\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m+1}}
and
F^{\prime}\not\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m+1}}
.
For the rest of the paper we fix a positive integer
m\geq 1
. We now identify two proportions of prime numbers, stated in terms of the Frobenius classes to which they belong.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X,X^{\prime}\in[0,1]
be defined as the following conditional probabilities:

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Prop}\left(F^{\prime}\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}~|~F\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}\right),\text{ and}

\displaystyle X^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Prop}\left(F\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}~|~F^{\prime}\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}\right).

By the Chebotarev Density Theorem,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prop}\left(F\equiv F^{\prime}\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:\mathbf{Q}]},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prop}\left(F\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{[L_{\ell^{m}}:\mathbf{Q}]}=\frac{1}{|G(\ell^{m})|},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prop}\left(F^{\prime}\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{[L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:\mathbf{Q}]}=\frac{1}{|G^{\prime}(\ell^{m})|}.

By basic field theory

[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:\mathbf{Q}]=[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L_{\ell^{m}}]\cdot|G(\ell^{m})|=[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}]\cdot|G^{\prime}(\ell^{m})|.

Lemma 2.2
.

With all notation as above, we have
X=[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L_{\ell^{m}}]^{-1}
and
X^{\prime}=[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L_{\ell^{m}}^{\prime}]^{-1}
.

Proof.

This is Bayes’ Law applied to the Chebotarev Density Theorem and the field diagram

\textstyle{L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L_{\ell^{m}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{|G(\ell^{m})|}
\textstyle{L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{|G^{\prime}(\ell^{m})|}
\textstyle{\mathbf{Q}}

∎

Since

\displaystyle[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:\mathbf{Q}]

\displaystyle=[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L_{\ell^{m}}]\cdot|G(\ell^{m})|=[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}]\cdot|G^{\prime}(\ell^{m})|,

it follows that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}]}{[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L_{\ell^{m}}]}=\frac{|G(\ell^{m})|}{|G^{\prime}(\ell^{m})|}\in\{1,\ell,1/\ell\},

with
[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L_{\ell^{m}}],[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}]\in\{1,\ell\}
. For proofs of these statements, see
[
6
, §2]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

With all notation as above, we have
d_{\ell^{m}}=1-X
and
d_{\ell^{m}}^{\prime}=1-X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the definitions of
d_{\ell^{m}},d^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}},X
, and
X^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose
E
and
E^{\prime}
do not have CM and that the maximum of the levels of their
\ell
-adic representations is
\ell^{M}
. Then if
m\geq M
, we have

[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L_{\ell^{m}}]=[L_{\ell^{m}}L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}:L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}]=\ell.

Proof.

Since the images of the
\ell
-adic representations of
E
and
E^{\prime}
have level at most
\ell^{M}
,
G
and
G^{\prime}
are the full preimages of
G(\ell^{M})
and
G^{\prime}(\ell^{M})
in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
, respectively. Fix
m\geq M
. We show that
[L_{\ell^{m}}L_{\ell^{m}}^{\prime}:L^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}]\neq 1
by proving that there exists a prime
p
for which
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})[\ell^{m}]\cong\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{m}\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{m}\mathbf{Z}
but
E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})[\ell^{m}]\not\cong\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{m}\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{m}\mathbf{Z}
. We follow a strategy similar to the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 5.2.4]
. We prove that there exists a prime
p
for which the height of the
\ell
-isogeny volcano containing
E
is exactly
m
, there is a unique vertex on the crater of the volcano, and this vertex corresponds to
E
. In this case, the isogeny
E\to E^{\prime}
must be descending. Switching the roles of
E
and
E^{\prime}
then shows that
[L_{\ell^{m}}L_{\ell^{m}}^{\prime}:L_{\ell^{m}}]\neq 1
.

We consider odd
\ell
and
\ell=2
separately. Suppose
\ell
is an odd prime and let
\delta
be a quadratic nonresidue modulo
\ell
. Since
\ell^{m}
is at least the
\ell
-adic level of
E
, there exists a prime
p
such that
F\equiv\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\ell^{m}\\
\delta\ell^{m}&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\pmod{\ell^{2m+1}}
. Since
t\equiv 2\pmod{\ell^{2m+1}}
and
p\equiv\det(F)\equiv 1-\delta\ell^{2m}\pmod{\ell^{2m+1}}
, we see that

t^{2}-4p\equiv 4-4(1-\delta\ell^{2m})\equiv 4\delta\ell^{2m}\pmod{\ell^{2m+1}}.

This implies
v_{\ell}(t^{2}-4p)=2m
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{0}
be the endomorphism ring of an elliptic curve lying on the crater of the
\ell
-isogeny volcano corresponding to
E,\ D_{0}=\operatorname{disc}(\mathcal{O}_{0})
, and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the maximal order of
\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{t^{2}-4p})
. We now apply a result of Kohel to determine several important properties of this volcano
[
17
, Thm. 7]
. Since
\ell\nmid[\mathcal{O}_{K}\colon\mathcal{O}_{0}]
, the height of this volcano is exactly
m
. Since

\left(\frac{D_{0}}{\ell}\right)=\left(\frac{4\delta}{\ell}\right)=\left(\frac{\delta}{\ell}\right)=-1,

there is a unique vertex on the crater of the volcano. Since
F\equiv I\pmod{\ell^{m}}
, we see that the vertex corresponding to
E
must be this unique vertex on the crater of the volcano. This completes the proof in the case where
\ell
is an odd prime.

Now suppose
\ell=2
. Since
2^{m}
is at least the
2
-adic level of
E
, there exists a prime
p
such that
F\equiv\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1+2^{m}&2^{m}\\
2^{m}&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\pmod{2^{2m+3}}
. Since
t\equiv 2+2^{m}\pmod{\ell^{2m+3}}
and
p\equiv\det(F)\equiv 1+2^{m}-2^{2m}\pmod{2^{2m+3}}
, we see that

t^{2}-4p\equiv 4+4\cdot 2^{m}+2^{2m}-4(1+2^{m}+2^{2m})\equiv 5\cdot 2^{2m}\pmod{2^{2m+3}}.

This implies
v_{2}(t^{2}-4p)=2m
. Applying
[
17
, Thm. 7]
as we did for odd
\ell
, we see that the height of the
2
-isogeny volcano corresponding to
E
is
m
, there is a unique vertex on the crater of the volcano, and this vertex corresponds to
E
.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

With notation as above, suppose the elliptic curves
E
and
E^{\prime}
do not have CM and that the maximum of the levels of their

We now prove the main result of the paper.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
E
and
E^{\prime}
be
\ell
-isogenous elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
. Then the proportion
P(E,E^{\prime})
of primes such that
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
is given by the formula

(2.9)

\displaystyle P(E,E^{\prime})=1-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{d_{\ell^{m}}}{|G(\ell^{m})|}+\frac{d^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}}{|G^{\prime}(\ell^{m})|}\right).

Proof.

The summand
d_{\ell^{m}}/|G(\ell^{m})|+d^{\prime}_{\ell^{m}}/|G^{\prime}(\ell^{m})|
represents the proportion of primes for which one of
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})
or
E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
has full
\ell^{m}
-torsion, but the other does not. Summing over all
m
gives the proportion of primes for which
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})
and
E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
are nonisomorphic, whence the formula (
1.4
).
∎

Theorem
2.8
holds regardless of whether
E
or
E^{\prime}
has a rational point of order
\ell
. If neither
E
nor
E^{\prime}
has a rational point of order
\ell
, then at all primes for which neither
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})
nor
E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
has a point of order
\ell
, we automatically have that
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
. We therefore expect to see high values of
P(E,E^{\prime})
in such cases. We conclude this section with an example illustrating this as well as a remark on quadratic twists.

Example 2.10
.

Let
E
be the elliptic curve
121.a1
and
E^{\prime}
the elliptic curve
121.a2
; they are 11-isogenous over
\mathbf{Q}
. Each curve has level 11 and
d_{11^{m}}=d^{\prime}_{11^{m}}=10/11
for all
m\geq 1
. We also have
|G(11)|=|G^{\prime}(11)|=120
. Applying Theorem
2.8
, we have
P(E,E^{\prime})=86509/87840\approx 0.9848
, while
P(E,E^{\prime})[10^{7}]\approx 0.9839
.

Checking the LMFDB, one sees that
E
and
E^{\prime}
acquire an 11-torsion point over
\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{11})^{+}
and
\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{11})
, respectively. Note that
[\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{11}):\mathbf{Q}]=10
and
[\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{11})^{+}:\mathbf{Q}]=5
. We confirm in Pari/GP that
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})
and
E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
have a point of order 11 for roughly 1/5 of the primes up to

and thus for the complementary 4/5 of the primes we automatically have
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
.

Remark 2.11
.

If

Twisting by
5

35.a3

Remarks on CM Elliptic Curves

By
[
11
, Thm. 1]
, if
E
is an ordinary elliptic curve over a finite field
\mathbf{F}_{q}
with endomorphism ring
\mathcal{O}
, then
E(\mathbf{F}_{q})\simeq\mathcal{O}/(\pi-1)
, where
\pi
represents the Frobenius as an element of
\mathcal{O}
. If
E
and
E^{\prime}
are isogenous elliptic curves with endomorphism rings
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
over
\mathbf{F}_{q}
, respectively, then the prime-to-
[\mathcal{O}:\mathcal{O^{\prime}}]
parts of the finite groups
E(\mathbf{F}_{q})
and
E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{q})
are isomorphic by
[
3
, Lem. 2]
. We will study the effect of all of this in the case where
E
and
E^{\prime}
define elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
with CM. Before beginning, however, we recall that by
[
5
, Thm. 2.3.1]
if
E
and
E^{\prime}
have supersingular reduction then
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
. Therefore, we can focus exclusively on primes of ordinary reduction.

If
E
and
E^{\prime}
have CM over
\mathbf{Q}
then, by the Baker-Heegner-Stark Theorem, the CM orders correspond to one of the nine fundamental discriminants

-3,-4,-7,-8,-11,-19,-43,-67,-163,

or one of the four non-fundamental discriminants,
-12,-16,-27,-28
, of class number 1. Suppose that
E
and
E^{\prime}
have geometric endomorphism rings
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
over
\mathbf{Q}
, respectively. If
p>3
is a prime of ordinary reduction, then by
[
10
, Ch. 13, Thm. 12]

E\pmod{p}
and
E^{\prime}\pmod{p}
have geometric endomorphism rings
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
as well. Because ordinary elliptic curves have all endomorphisms defined over the base field, we have that
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
are the endomorphism rings over
\mathbf{F}_{p}
of
E\pmod{p}
and
E^{\prime}\pmod{p}
, respectively.

Since
E
and
E^{\prime}
are isogenous,
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
are homothetic and, as a result,
\operatorname{disc}\mathcal{O}/\operatorname{disc}\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is a rational square. Comparing against the possible discriminants above, those square values are either
1,4,9
, or their reciprocals. If
\operatorname{disc}\mathcal{O}=\operatorname{disc}\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, then
\mathcal{O}\simeq\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
. This isomorphism holds modulo
p
as well and thus
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
E
and
E^{\prime}
are
\ell
-isogenous CM elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
with
\ell>3
. Then
P(E,E^{\prime})=1
.

Proof.

If
E
and
E^{\prime}
are
\ell
-isogenous, then by
[
9
, Prop. 21]
the endomorphism rings, under reduction modulo ordinary
p
, satisfy
[\mathcal{O}:\mathcal{O}^{\prime}]=1,\ell
, or
\ell^{-1}
. However, since
E
and
E^{\prime}
have CM over
\mathbf{Q}
, by our work above it follows that
[\mathcal{O}:\mathcal{O}^{\prime}]\in\{1,2^{\pm 1},3^{\pm 1}\}
. Therefore, we must have
\mathcal{O}\simeq\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
and hence
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
. Since
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
for all supersingular primes, it follows that
P(E,E^{\prime})=1
.
∎

We are now reduced to studying the cases

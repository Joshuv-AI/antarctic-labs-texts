Bound-preserving Point-Average-Moment PolynomiAl-interpreted (PAMPA) scheme: One-dimensional case
Rémi Abgrall, Miaosen Jiao, Yongle Liu \comma \corrauth, and Kailiang Wu

Abstract
We propose a bound-preserving (BP) Point-Average-Moment PolynomiAl-interpreted (PAMPA) scheme by blending third-order and first-order constructions. The originality of the present construction is that it does not need any explicit reconstruction within each element, and therefore the construction is very flexible. The scheme employs a classical blending approach between a first-order BP scheme and a high-order scheme that does not inherently preserve bounds. The proposed BP PAMPA scheme demonstrates effectiveness across a range of problems, from scalar cases to systems such as the Euler equations of gas dynamics. We derive optimal blending parameters for both scalar and system cases, with the latter based on the recent geometric quasi-linearization (GQL) framework of [Wu & Shu, SIAM Review , 65 (2023), pp. 1031–1073]. This yields explicit, optimal blending coefficients that ensure positivity and control spurious oscillations in both point values and cell averages. This framework incorporates a convex blending of fluxes and residuals from both high-order and first-order updates, facilitating a rigorous BP property analysis. Sufficient conditions for the BP property are established, ensuring robustness while preserving high-order accuracy. Numerical tests confirm the effectiveness of the BP PAMPA scheme on several challenging problems.

•

We provide a set of theoretical results that can be used to rigorously prove the BP property of the BP PAMPA scheme. Among these results, the analysis for maintaining the average value within the invariant domain aligns with that in

•

We implement our BP PAMPA scheme and demonstrate its robustness and effectiveness on several challenging numerical examples, such as the Buckley–Leverett problem in scalar conservation law with non-convex flux, the LeBlanc shock tube, double rarefaction Riemann, and Sedov problems in the Euler equations of gas dynamics.

2
One-dimensional PAMPA scheme for hyperbolic conservation laws

This section is devoted to recall the essential ingredients of the one-dimensional semi-discrete PAMPA scheme. To remain as simple as possible, the one-dimensional scalar conservation laws will be considered in this section. The system extension is perfectly straightforward. Spatial discretization of the studied PDE will be carried out using either a first-order scheme or a high-order scheme. To evolve the solution in time, we employ the third-order strong stability-preserving (SSP) Runge–Kutta (RK) time-stepping method in the numerical implementation. However, for ease of demonstration, we apply only the first-order forward Euler method here. Extending this to higher-order SSP RK time-stepping methods is conceptually straightforward, since these multistage time integration methods can be written as convex combinations of formal forward Euler steps.

3
Bound-preserving PAMPA scheme

In this section, we introduce the BP PAMPA scheme. The previous presentation of the first-order and high-order schemes allows us to construct a unified blending framework for updating both cell average and point value. For simplicity and clarity in illustrating the blending procedure, the following subsections

3.2
, and
3.3

u_{0}\in\mathcal{D}=[\mathring{u}_{\min},\mathring{u}_{\max}]
, then due to Kruzhkov’s theory, the unique entropy weak solution

u(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{D}

3.4
.

3.1
Blended fluxes and update of cell average

We present a convex limiting approach to achieve the BP property for updating the cell average, inspired by
[
32
]
. To this end, each cell interface

is assigned two fluxes: a first-order finite volume numerical flux
\mathcal{F}_{j}^{LO}(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n},\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})
, and a high-order exact flux function
f(u_{j}^{n})
. Then, these two fluxes are blended in a convex manner through a blending coefficient
\eta_{j}\in[0,1]
as follows:

\mathcal{F}_{j}=\mathcal{F}_{j}^{LO}+\eta_{j}\big(f(u_{j}^{n})-\mathcal{F}_{j}^{LO}\big)=\mathcal{F}_{j}^{LO}+\eta_{j}\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}.

(13)

A blending coefficient set to zero will lead to a first-order finite volume numerical flux, while a coefficient of one yields the high-order exact flux function.

The first-order forward Euler time integration for the cell average reads as

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n+1}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}-\lambda(\mathcal{F}_{j+1}-\mathcal{F}_{j}),\quad\forall j,

(14)

which can be rewritten as a convex combination of quantities defined at the previous time step:

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}-\lambda(\mathcal{F}_{j+1}-\mathcal{F}_{j})\pm\lambda(\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{j+1})\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}+\lambda\big(f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})-f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})\big)

(15)

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda\alpha_{j}-\lambda\alpha_{j+1})\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}+\lambda\alpha_{j}\Big(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}-\frac{-\mathcal{F}_{j}+f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})}{\alpha_{j}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\lambda\alpha_{j+1}\Big(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}-\frac{\mathcal{F}_{j+1}-f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})}{\alpha_{j+1}}\Big)

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda\alpha_{j}-\lambda\alpha_{j+1})\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}+\lambda\alpha_{j}{\widetilde{u}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{L}}+\lambda\alpha_{j+1}{\widetilde{u}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{R}},

with

\displaystyle{\widetilde{u}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{L}}

\displaystyle=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}-\frac{-\mathcal{F}_{j}+f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})}{\alpha_{j}}=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}}{2}-\frac{f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})-f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n})}{2\alpha_{j}}+{\eta_{j}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}}

\displaystyle=u_{j}^{*}+\eta_{j}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}

and

\displaystyle{\widetilde{u}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}^{R}}

\displaystyle=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}-\frac{\mathcal{F}_{j+1}-f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})}{\alpha_{j+1}}=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{3}{2}}^{n}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}}{2}-\frac{f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{3}{2}}^{n})-f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})}{2\alpha_{j+1}}-{\eta_{j+1}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j+1}}{\alpha_{j+1}}}

\displaystyle=u_{j+1}^{*}-\eta_{j+1}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j+1}}{\alpha_{j+1}},

where
u_{j}^{*}
and
u_{j+1}^{*}
are nothing but the first-order finite volume Riemann intermediate states, which takes the following form

u_{j}^{*}=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}}{2}-\frac{f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})-f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n})}{2\alpha_{j}},\quad\forall j.

(16)

As one can observe from (
15
),
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n+1}
is indeed a convex combination of quantities from the previous time step under the standard CFL condition

\Delta t\leq\frac{\Delta x}{\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{j+1}},

which was also used in
[
9
]
for the BP property of cell averages and was inspired by
[
16
,
32
]
. Moreover, since

\alpha_{j}=\alpha(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n},\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})=\max\big(|f^{\prime}(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n})|,|f^{\prime}(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})|\big),

it follows that

u_{j}^{*}\in\big[\min(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n},\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}),\max(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n},\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})\big].

We now introduce the definition of the blending coefficient to ensure that if the numerical initial solution lies in
\mathcal{D}
, the solution
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}
remains in
\mathcal{D}
throughout the entire calculation.

Proposition 3.1
.

In the numerical scheme (
15
), if we set

\eta_{j}=\min\Big(1,\frac{\alpha_{j}}{\big|\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}\big|}\min\big(\mathring{u}_{\max}-u_{j}^{*},u_{j}^{*}-\mathring{u}_{\min}\big)\Big),\quad\forall j,

(17)

the scheme preserves the following property: if
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.51114pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\in\mathcal{D}
, then
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.51114pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n+1}\in\mathcal{D}
, for any
n
and any
j
.

Proof 3.2
.

We need to determine under which condition,
u_{j}^{*}\pm\eta_{j}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\in[\mathring{u}_{\min},\mathring{u}_{\max}]
, knowing that if
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.51114pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\in[{\mathring{u}_{\min},\mathring{u}_{\max}}]
, for all
j
.
This requires

{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\leq u_{j}^{*}+\eta_{j}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\leq{\mathring{u}_{\max}},\quad{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\leq u_{j}^{*}-\eta_{j}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\leq{\mathring{u}_{\max}},\quad\forall j

(18)

i.e.,

\alpha_{j}\big({\mathring{u}_{\min}}-u_{j}^{*}\big)\leq\eta_{j}\,\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}\leq\alpha_{j}\big({\mathring{u}_{\max}}-u_{j}^{*}\big),~\alpha_{j}\big(u_{j+1}^{*}-{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\big)\geq\eta_{j}\,\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}\geq\alpha_{j}\big(u_{j}^{*}-{\mathring{u}_{\max}}\big),~\forall j.

Since

\alpha_{j}\big({\mathring{u}_{\min}}-u_{j}^{*}\big)\leq 0\leq\alpha_{j}\big({\mathring{u}_{\max}}-u_{j}^{*}\big),\quad\alpha_{j}\big(u_{j}^{*}-{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\big)\geq 0\geq\alpha_{j}\big(u_{j}^{*}-{\mathring{u}_{\max}}\big),\quad\forall j,

we have two cases for
\eta_{j}
:

•

If
\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}\geq 0
, then we need

\eta_{j}\leq\alpha_{j}\dfrac{\big({\mathring{u}_{\max}}-u_{j}^{*}\big)}{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta_{j}\leq\alpha_{j}\dfrac{\big(u_{j}^{*}-{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\big)}{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}};

•

If
\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}\leq 0
, then we need

\eta_{j}\leq\alpha_{j}\dfrac{\big(u_{j}^{*}-{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\big)}{-\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta_{j}\leq\alpha_{j}\dfrac{\big({\mathring{u}_{\max}}-u_{j}^{*}\big)}{-\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}.

In addition, we require
\eta_{j}\leq 1
. Therefore, we get the result as given in (
17
).

3.2
Blended residuals and update of point value

The point value will be evolved in a similar convex limiting manner as described in the previous subsection. To be specific, each point value is assigned two residuals, one is the low-order residual (
7
) and one is the high-order residual (
12
). These two residuals will be blended in a convex manner through blending coefficients
\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\in[0,1]
and
\theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\in[0,1]
. The blended residuals read as

\left\{\begin{aligned} \overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}&=\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{LO}+\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}(\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{HO}-\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{LO})=\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{LO}+\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}},\\
\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}&=\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{LO}+\theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{HO}-\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{LO})=\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{LO}+\theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Delta\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}.\end{aligned}\right.

(19)

Analogously, when the blending coefficient
\theta_{j\pm\frac{1}{2}}=0
, it results in the first-order residuals while if
\theta_{j\pm\frac{1}{2}}=1
, it induces to the high-order case. Since conservation is not needed for point value, we can assign two different blending coefficients
\theta_{j\pm\frac{1}{2}}
for the residuals that are used for the update of point value at the cell interface
x=x_{j}
.

The first-order forward Euler time integration for point value is expressed as

u_{j}^{n+1}=u_{j}^{n}-2\lambda(\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}),\quad\forall j,

(20)

and then it is rewritten as a convex combination of quantities defined at the previous time step, i.e.,

\displaystyle u_{j}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=u_{j}^{n}-2\lambda(\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})+2\lambda(\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}})u_{j}^{n}-2\lambda(\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}})u_{j}^{n}

(21)

\displaystyle=\Big(1-2\lambda\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}-2\lambda\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)u_{j}^{n}+2\lambda\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(u_{j}^{n}-\frac{\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}\Big)+2\lambda\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big(u_{j}^{n}-\frac{\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(1-2\lambda\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}-2\lambda\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)u_{j}^{n}+2\lambda\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}{\widetilde{u}_{j}^{L}}+2\lambda\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}{\widetilde{u}_{j}^{R}},

where

\displaystyle{\widetilde{u}_{j}^{L}}

\displaystyle=u_{j}^{n}-\frac{\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n}+u_{j}^{n}}{2}-\frac{f(u_{j}^{n})-f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n})}{2\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}-\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}

(22)

\displaystyle={u^{*,L}_{j}}-\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}

and

\displaystyle{\widetilde{u}_{j}^{R}}

\displaystyle=u_{j}^{n}-\frac{\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{u_{j}^{n}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}}{2}-\frac{f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})-f(u_{j}^{n})}{2\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}-\theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\Delta\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}

(23)

\displaystyle={u_{j}^{*,R}}-\theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\Delta\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}.

In the above, we have defined

u_{j}^{*,L}=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n}+u_{j}^{n}}{2}-\frac{f(u_{j}^{n})-f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{n})}{2\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}},\quad u_{j}^{*,R}=\frac{u_{j}^{n}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}}{2}-\frac{f(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n})-f(u_{j}^{n})}{2\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}},\quad\forall j.

(24)

We then perform the similar analysis as in subsection
3.1
to satisfy the condition of convex combination and obtain:

Proposition 3.3
.

If we set

\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}=\min\Big(1,\frac{\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\big|\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\big|}\min\big(u_{j}^{*,L}-\mathring{u}_{\min},\mathring{u}_{\max}-u_{j}^{*,L}\big)\Big)

(25)

and

\theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\min\Big(1,\frac{\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{\big|\Delta\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big|}\min\big(u_{j}^{*,R}-\mathring{u}_{\min},\mathring{u}_{\max}-u_{j}^{*,R}\big)\Big),

(26)

the numerical scheme (
21
) has the same BP property: if
u_{j}^{n}\in\mathcal{D}
, then
u_{j}^{n+1}\in\mathcal{D}
, for any
n
and any
j
.

Proof 3.4
.

The proof is very similar to that for (
17
). We assume that

{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\leq u_{j}^{*,L}-\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}\leq{\mathring{u}_{\max}},\quad{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\leq u_{j}^{*,R}-\theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\Delta\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\leq{\mathring{u}_{\max}},

(27)

and since

\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{j}^{*,L}-{\mathring{u}_{\max}}\big)\leq 0\leq\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{j}^{*,L}-{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\big),~\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{j}^{*,R}-{\mathring{u}_{\max}}\big)\leq 0\leq\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{j}^{*,R}-{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\big),

we can rewrite (
27
) as

\displaystyle\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{j}^{*,L}-{\mathring{u}_{\max}}\big)\leq\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{j}^{*,L}-{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\big),

\displaystyle\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{j}^{*,R}-{\mathring{u}_{\max}}\big)\leq\theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Delta\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\leq\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{j}^{*,R}-{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\big).

We then have two cases for
\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}
:

•

If
\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\geq 0
, then we need

\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\dfrac{\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{j}^{*,L}-{\mathring{u}_{\min}}\big)}{\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}};

•

If
\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\leq 0
, then we need

\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\dfrac{\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\big({\mathring{u}_{\max}}-u_{j}^{*,L}\big)}{-\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}.

In case of an inaccurate estimation of the upwind direction which may lead to a opposite sign of
\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}
, especially in the transonic case and
\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\leq 1
is also required, we therefore take

\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}=\min\Big(1,\frac{\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\big|\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\big|}\min\big(u_{j}^{*,L}-{\mathring{u}_{\min}},{\mathring{u}_{\max}}-u_{j}^{*,L}\big)\Big).

Similar analysis can be performed for
\theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, we omit here for saving space.

3.3
Local maximum principle and smooth extrema relaxation

In (
17
), (
25
), and (
26
), the global bounds are taken as

{\mathring{u}_{\min}}=\min_{x\in\Omega}\;u_{0}(x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathring{u}_{\max}}=\max_{x\in\Omega}\;u_{0}(x).

(28)

In order to reduce as much as possible the appearance of spurious oscillations in the approximation of discontinuous solution, in addition to the global maximum principle, we also impose a local maximum principle for the update of average value. More specific, we take the local bounds of the solution from the previous time step (or the previous Runge-Kutta step in the general case) and we set

u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\min}=\min\big(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n},u_{j}^{*},u_{j+1}^{*}\big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}=\max\big(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$u$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{n},u_{j}^{*},u_{j+1}^{*}\big),\quad\forall j,

(29)

for the update of cell average. To ensure the updated cell average bounded by these local bounds and in the light of the combination in (
15
), we need to guarantee both
{\widetilde{u}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{L}}\in[u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\min},u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}]
and
{\widetilde{u}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{R}}\in[u_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\min},u_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}]
, it is sufficient to take
\eta_{j}
as follows:

\eta_{j}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\min\Big(1,\frac{\alpha_{j}}{|\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}|}\min(u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}-u_{j}^{*},u_{j}^{*}-u_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\min})\Big)&&\mbox{if}~\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}>0,\\
&\min\Big(1,\frac{\alpha_{j}}{|\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}|}\min(u_{j}^{*}-u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\min},u_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}-u_{j}^{*})\Big)&&\mbox{if}~\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}<0.\end{aligned}\right.

(30)

Proof 3.5
.

We need to determine under which condition,
u_{j}^{*}+\eta_{j}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\in[u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\min},u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}]
and
u_{j}^{*}-\eta_{j}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\in[u_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\min},u_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}]
, for all
j
. Since
u_{j}^{*}\in[u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\min},u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}]
and
u_{j}^{*}\in[u_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\min},u_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}]
, we have two cases for
\eta_{j}
:

•

If
\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}\geq 0
, then we need
u_{j}^{*}+\eta_{j}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\leq u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}
and
u_{j}^{*}-\eta_{j}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\geq u_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\min}
, which yields

\eta_{j}\leq\alpha_{j}\dfrac{\big(u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}-u_{j}^{*}\big)}{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta_{j}\leq\alpha_{j}\dfrac{\big(u_{j}^{*}-u_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\min}\big)}{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}};

•

If
\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}\leq 0
, then we need
u_{j}^{*}+\eta_{j}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\geq u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\min}
and
u_{j}^{*}-\eta_{j}\frac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\leq u_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}
, which yields

\eta_{j}\leq\alpha_{j}\dfrac{\big(u_{j}^{*}-u_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\min}\big)}{-\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta_{j}\leq\alpha_{j}\dfrac{\big(u_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\max}-u_{j}^{*}\big)}{-\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}}.

In addition, we require
\eta_{j}\leq 1
. Therefore, we get the result as given in (
30
).

The formulation (
30
) for the blending parameter is standard (see, e.g.,
[
22
,
32
]
) and was also used in
[
9
]
.

Smooth extrema relaxation

In order to preserve high-accuracy in the vicinity of smooth extrema, we employ the smooth detector used in
[
3
,
31
]
to relax the local maximum principle constraints for the average value update. This detector is designed as follows:

•

First, we denote the parabolic approximation in (
11
) by
\mathfrak{u}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
.

•

Next, we compute
\zeta^{\prime}=\mathfrak{u}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\prime}(x_{j+\frac{1}{2}})
,
\zeta^{\prime}_{L}=\mathfrak{u}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\prime}(x_{j})
, and the minimum and maximum values of the derivative around cell
I_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
:

\zeta^{\prime,{j+\frac{1}{2}}}_{\min/\max}=\min/\max(\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{i}),\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}_{j+\frac{3}{2}}(x_{i})),\quad i=j,j+1.

All of the obtained quantities are then used to define the left detection factor
\xi_{L}
:

\xi_{L}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\min\Big(1,\frac{\zeta^{\prime,{j+\frac{1}{2}}}_{\max}-\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}_{L}-\zeta^{\prime}}\Big),\quad&&\mbox{if}~\zeta^{\prime}_{L}>\zeta^{\prime},\\
&1,\quad&&\mbox{if}~\zeta^{\prime}_{L}=\zeta^{\prime},\\
&\min\Big(1,\frac{\zeta^{\prime,{j+\frac{1}{2}}}_{\min}-\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}_{L}-\zeta^{\prime}}\Big),\quad&&\mbox{if}~\zeta^{\prime}_{L}<\zeta^{\prime}.\end{aligned}\right.

•

Analogously, we compute
\zeta^{\prime}=\mathfrak{u}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\prime}(x_{j+\frac{1}{2}})
,
\zeta^{\prime}_{R}=\mathfrak{u}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\prime}(x_{j+1})
and define the right detection factor
\xi_{R}
as

\xi_{R}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\min\Big(1,\frac{\zeta^{\prime,{j+\frac{1}{2}}}_{\max}-\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}_{R}-\zeta^{\prime}}\Big),\quad&&\mbox{if}~\zeta^{\prime}_{R}>\zeta^{\prime},\\
&1,\quad&&\mbox{if}~\zeta^{\prime}_{R}=\zeta^{\prime},\\
&\min\Big(1,\frac{\zeta^{\prime,{j+\frac{1}{2}}}_{\min}-\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}_{R}-\zeta^{\prime}}\Big),\quad&&\mbox{if}~\zeta^{\prime}_{R}<\zeta^{\prime}.\end{aligned}\right.

•

Finally, if
\xi_{L}=1
and
\xi_{R}=1
, we consider that the numerical solution presents a smooth profile on cell
[x_{j},x_{j+1}]
. In this particular case, the blended coefficient constraint for the average value update through the local maximum principle is relaxed.

The basic idea behind this detector is as follows: the numerical solution is expected to exhibit a smooth extremum if at least the linearized spatial derivatives of the numerical solution present a monotonic profile. We aim to preserve scheme accuracy in the presence of such a smooth extremum and verify whether the gradient at the cell boundaries lies within the interval
[\zeta^{\prime,{j+\frac{1}{2}}}_{\min},\zeta^{\prime,{j+\frac{1}{2}}}_{\max}]
.

In the end, we would like to remark that we neither apply any local maximum principles nor design any smooth extrema detectors for the update of point value, since we have less requirements/restrictions on the point value and from the numerical experiments conducted in section
4
, we do not observe any issues caused by this. The smooth extrema detector for update of the point value will need further investigation.

3.4
Case of the Euler equations

For the Euler equations of gas dynamics, the invariant domain
\mathcal{D}
is given by (
2
). Following
[
33
]
, we see that the invariant domain is exactly equivalent to the set given in (
3
), which can be rewritten as

\mathcal{D}_{\nu}=\{\mathbf{u}=(\rho,m,E)^{T}:\rho>0~\text{and for all}~\nu\in\mathbb{R},\mathbf{u}\cdot\bm{\nu}>0\},~\bm{\nu}=(\frac{\nu^{2}}{2},-\nu,1)^{T}.

(31)

The first relation is obvious, while the second one comes from:

\rho\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}-\nu m+E=e+\frac{\rho}{2}(u-\nu)^{2},

where
e=E-\tfrac{m^{2}}{2\rho}
and
m=\rho v
. Note that the constraints in the GQL representation (
31
) become linear with respect to
\mathbf{u}
. The goal is reduced to find optimal blending coefficients to satisfy the linear constraints.

Positivity preserving

We now illustrate the preservation of positive density and internal energy. Using the same notations as in the scalar case (see (
18
) and (
27
)), the scheme is positivity preserving if the following constraints are fulfilled:

•

\rho_{j}^{*}+\eta_{j}^{\rho}\dfrac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\rho}}{\alpha_{j}}>0
and
\rho_{j}^{*}-\eta_{j}^{\rho}\dfrac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\rho}}{\alpha_{j}}>0
;

•

\rho_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{*}-\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\rho}\dfrac{\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\rho}}{\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}>0
;

•

\rho_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{*}-\theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\rho}\dfrac{\Delta\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\rho}}{\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}>0
;

•

\mathbf{u}_{j}^{*}\cdot\bm{\nu}+\eta_{j}^{e}\dfrac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}\cdot\bm{\nu}}{\alpha_{j}}>0
and
\mathbf{u}_{j}^{*}\cdot\bm{\nu}-\eta_{j}^{e}\dfrac{\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}\cdot\bm{\nu}}{\alpha_{j}}>0
;

•

\mathbf{u}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{*}\cdot\bm{\nu}-\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{e}\dfrac{\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\bm{\nu}}{\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}>0
;

•

\mathbf{u}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{*}\cdot\bm{\nu}-\theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{e}\dfrac{\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\bm{\nu}}{\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}>0
.

For the density, according to (
17
), we obtain

\eta_{j}^{\rho}=\min\Big(1,\frac{\alpha_{j}}{\big|\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\rho}\big|}\rho_{j}^{*}\Big),

while according to (
25
) and (
26
), we have

\theta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\rho}=\min\Big(1,\frac{\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\big|\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{\rho}\big|}\rho_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{*}\Big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\theta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\rho}=\min\Big(1,\frac{\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{\big|\Delta\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\rho}\big|}\rho_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{*}\Big).

For the internal energy, we take (
17
) with

\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}
replaced by
\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}\cdot\bm{\nu}
, while take (
25
) and (
26
) with
\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}
replaced by
\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\bm{\nu}
and
\Delta\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
replaced by
\Delta\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\bm{\nu}
, respectively. Then, we need to minimize the expressions that will now depend on
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
. That is, for blending the numerical fluxes, we need to minimize

\eta_{j}^{e}=\alpha_{j}\min_{\bm{\nu}}\frac{\mathbf{u}_{j}^{*}\cdot\bm{\nu}}{|\Delta\mathcal{F}_{j}\cdot\bm{\nu}|},\quad\forall j,

and for blending the residuals, we need to minimize

\theta^{e}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}=\beta_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\min_{\bm{\nu}}\frac{\mathbf{u}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}^{*}\cdot\bm{\nu}}{|\Delta\overrightarrow{\Phi}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\bm{\nu}|},\quad\theta^{e}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\beta_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\min_{\bm{\nu}}\frac{\mathbf{u}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{*}\cdot\bm{\nu}}{|\Delta\overleftarrow{\Phi}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\bm{\nu}|}.

Any of the scalar product has the form

P(\nu)=\alpha\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}-\beta\nu+\gamma,

and we need to minimize expressions of the form

\dfrac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\bm{\nu}}{|P(\nu)|}.

This is an expression of the form:

\varphi(\nu)=\frac{a_{1}\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}-b_{1}\nu+c_{1}}{|a_{2}\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}-b_{2}\nu+c_{2}|}=\dfrac{a_{1}\nu^{2}-2b_{1}\nu+2c_{1}}{|a_{2}\nu^{2}-2b_{2}\nu+2c_{2}|}:=\frac{Q(\nu)}{|P(\nu)|},

where
P(\nu)=a_{2}\nu^{2}-2b_{2}\nu+2c_{2}
and
Q(\nu)=a_{1}\nu^{2}-2b_{1}\nu+2c_{1}>0
.
Note that in this problem, the parameters are such that
Q(\nu)>0
for any
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
.

In the appendix
A
, we provide analytical expressions of the minimization problem. These expressions have been used for the numerical test cases. Once the minimization problem is solved, we obtain the blending coefficients, denoted by
\eta_{j}^{e}
or
\theta_{j\pm\frac{1}{2}}^{e}
, for ensuring the bounds of internal energy. Finally, the blending coefficients used for the evaluation of BP fluxes and residuals are defined as the minimum of the two obtained coefficients, i.e.,

\eta_{j}=\min(\eta_{j}^{\rho},\eta_{j}^{e}),\quad\theta_{j\pm\frac{1}{2}}=\min(\theta_{j\pm\frac{1}{2}}^{\rho},\theta_{j\pm\frac{1}{2}}^{e}).

Local maximum principle

For the non-linear system case, we naively apply the local maximum principle introduced for the scalar case in subsection
3.3
to the conserved density variable and the local bounds are also obtained based on this selected conserved variable. This local maximum principle is then relaxed using the same smooth extrema detector previously applied for the scalar case but now on the conserved density variable.

5
Conclusions

We have proposed a BP framework adapted to the PAMPA scheme, where the solution is represented by cell averages and point values. In this approach, the degrees of freedom are updated using a scheme that blends a first-order BP update with a high-order update. We have developed optimal blending parameters for both scalar and system cases. For hyperbolic systems, we employ the GQL approach of Wu and Shu, allowing us to propose explicit and optimal coefficients. This approach effectively preserves bounds and controls spurious oscillations for both the point values and cell averages, without requiring explicit solution reconstruction.

We intend to extend this work in several directions, such as higher-order approaches and multidimensional polygonal cells.

Appendix A
Minimization of
\varphi(\nu)
knowing that
Q(\nu)>0

The aim of this appendix is to solve the following minimization problem

\min\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{Q(\nu)}{|P(\nu)|},\quad Q(\nu)=a_{1}\nu^{2}-2b_{1}\nu+2c_{1},\quad P(\nu)=a_{2}\nu^{2}-2b_{2}\nu+2c_{2}.

We assume that
\nu=\frac{p}{q}
with
p,q\neq 0
and obtain

\frac{Q\big(p/q\big)}{P\big(p/q\big)}=\frac{a_{1}p^{2}-2b_{1}pq+2c_{1}q^{2}}{|a_{2}p^{2}-2b_{2}pq+2c_{2}q^{2}|}=\frac{\langle\mathbf{u},B\mathbf{u}\rangle}{|\langle\mathbf{u},A\mathbf{u}\rangle|},

where

\mathbf{u}=\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix},\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}&-b_{2}\\
-b_{2}&2c_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&-b_{1}\\
-b_{1}&2c_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
B
is positive definite corresponding to positive eigenvalues, the minimization problem is equivalent to

\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}\frac{Q(\nu)}{|P(\nu)|}=\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
\mathbf{u}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\langle\mathbf{u},B\mathbf{u}\rangle}{|\langle\mathbf{u},A\mathbf{u}\rangle|}=\left(\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
\mathbf{u}\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\frac{|\langle\mathbf{u},A\mathbf{u}\rangle|}{\langle\mathbf{u},B\mathbf{u}\rangle}}\right)^{-1}.

Then, the goal is to solve the maximize problem:

\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
\mathbf{u}\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\frac{|\langle\mathbf{u},A\mathbf{u}\rangle|}{\langle\mathbf{u},B\mathbf{u}\rangle}}=\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
\mathbf{u}\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\frac{|\langle\mathbf{u},B^{-\frac{1}{2}}AB^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{u}\rangle|}{\|\mathbf{u}\|^{2}}}.

We know that
C=B^{-\frac{1}{2}}AB^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is a symmetric matrix, and thus

\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
\mathbf{u}\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\frac{|\langle\mathbf{u},B^{-\frac{1}{2}}AB^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{u}\rangle|}{\|\mathbf{u}\|^{2}}}=\rho(C).

Therefore,

\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}\frac{Q(\nu)}{|P(\nu)|}=\left(\max\limits_{\lambda\text{ eigenvalue of }B^{-1/2}AB^{-1/2}}|\lambda|\right)^{-1}.

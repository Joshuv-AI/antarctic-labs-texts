Abstract
Given finite-dimensional random vectors Y Y , X X , and Z Z that form a Markov chain in that order ( Y → X → Z Y\to X\to Z ), we derive upper bounds on the excess minimum risk using generalized information divergence measures. Here, Y Y is a target vector to be estimated from an observed feature vector X X or its stochastically degraded version Z Z . The excess minimum risk is defined as the difference between the minimum expected loss in estimating Y Y from X X and from Z Z . We present a family of bounds that generalize the mutual information based bound of Györfi et al. (2023), using the Rényi and α \alpha -Jensen-Shannon divergences, as well as Sibson’s mutual information. Our bounds are similar to those developed by Modak et al. (2021) and Aminian et al. (2024) for the generalization error of learning algorithms. However, unlike these works, our bounds do not require the sub-Gaussian parameter to be constant and therefore apply to a broader class of joint distributions over Y Y , X X , and Z Z . We also provide numerical examples under both constant and non-constant sub-Gaussianity assumptions, illustrating that our generalized divergence based bounds can be tighter than the one based on mutual information for certain regimes of the parameter α \alpha .

Introduction

The excess minimum risk in statistical inference quantifies the difference between the minimum expected loss attained by estimating a (target) hidden random vector from a feature (observed) random vector and the minimum expected loss incurred by estimating the hidden vector from a stochastically degraded version of the feature vector. The aim of this work is to derive upper bounds on the excess minimum risk in terms of generalized information divergence measures such as the Rényi divergence

Our problem of bounding the excess minimum risk is closely related to recent work on generalization error in learning theory. In both settings, the goal is to understand how much performance is lost when a target variable is estimated from a less informative or transformed version of the input. In learning theory, this is often studied through generalization bounds, which compare the performance of a learned predictor on training and test data. As already stated, several recent works have used information-theoretic tools—such as mutual information and its generalizations—to bound the generalization error (e.g.,
[
7
,
12
,
18
]
). Although these works focus on algorithm-dependent error, the structure of the bounds is similar to ours. Our bounds, instead, are on the excess minimum risk, which compares the best possible performance using full observations versus using degraded ones. Still, both approaches rely on similar tools, including variational characterizations and divergence measures. In this sense, our work takes a different but related approach by studying the basic limits of inference, rather than how well a particular algorithm performs.

Preliminaries

2.1
Problem Setup

Consider a random vector
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
p\geq 1
, that is to be estimated (predicted) from a random observation vector
X
taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
,
q\geq 1
. Given a measurable estimator (predictor)
f:\mathbb{R}^{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p}
and a loss function
l:\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the loss (risk) realized in estimating
Y
by
f(X)
is given by
l(Y,f(X))
. The minimum expected risk in predicting
Y
from
X
is defined by

L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)=\inf_{f:\mathbb{R}^{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p}}\mathbb{E}[l(Y,f(X))]

(1)

where the infimum is over all measurable
f
.

We also consider another random observation vector
Z
that is a random transformation or stochastically degraded version of
X
, obtained for example by observing
X
through a noisy channel. Here
Z
takes values in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
,
r\geq 1
, and
Y
,
X
and
Z
form a Markov chain in this order, which we denote as
Y\rightarrow X\rightarrow Z
. We similarly define the minimum expected risk in predicting
Y
from
Z
as

L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)=\inf_{g:\mathbb{R}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p}}\mathbb{E}[l(Y,g(Z))],

(2)

where the infimum is over all measurable predictors
g
. With the notation introduced above, we define the excess minimum risk as the difference
L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)
, which is always non-negative due to the Markov chain condition
Y\rightarrow X\rightarrow Z
(e.g., see the data processing inequality for expected risk in
[
14
, Lemma 1]
).
Our objective is to establish upper bounds to this difference using generalized information divergence measures.

In
[
15
]
, the random vector
Z
is taken as
T(X)
, a transformation of random vector
X
, where
T:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r}
is measurable. The authors derive bounds on the excess minimum risk using Shannon’s mutual information. Here, we generalize these bounds by employing a family of information‐divergence measures of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
, which recover Shannon’s mutual information in the limits
\alpha\to 0
or
\alpha\to 1
. Furthermore, we use an arbitrary random vector
Z
, as the degraded version of the observation
X
instead of
T(X)
. We also provide examples where the various generalized information divergence based bounds perform better than the mutual information based bounds of
[
15
]

for a certain range
\alpha
.

We next state some definitions that we will invoke in deriving our results.

2.2
Definitions

Consider two arbitrary jointly distributed random variables
U
and
V
defined on the same probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{M})
and taking values in
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
, respectively. Let
P_{U}
and
P_{V}
be the marginal distributions of
U
and
V
, respectively, and
P_{U,V}
be a joint distribution over
\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}
. We first provide definitions for the Rényi divergence based measures.

Definition 1

[
2
,
30
]

The Rényi divergence of order
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
,
\alpha\neq 1
, between two probability measures
P_{U}
and
P_{V}
is denoted by
D_{\alpha}(P_{U}\|P_{V})
and defined as follows. Let
\nu
be a sigma-finite positive measure such that
P_{U}
and
P_{V}
are absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu
, written as
P_{U},P_{V}\ll\nu
, with Radon–Nikodym derivatives
\frac{dP_{U}}{d\nu}=p_{U}
and
\frac{dP_{V}}{d\nu}=p_{V}
, respectively. Then

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}(P_{U}\|P_{V})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left[\int(p_{U})^{\alpha}(p_{V})^{1-\alpha}d\nu\right]&\mbox{ if }0<\alpha<1\mbox{ or }\alpha>1\mbox{ and }P_{U}\ll P_{V}\\
+\infty&\mbox{ if }\alpha>1\mbox{ and }P_{U}\ll P_{V}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Definition 2

The conditional Rényi divergence of order
\alpha
between the conditional distributions
P_{V|U}
and
Q_{V|U}
given
P_{U}
is denoted by
D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U}\|Q_{V|U}|P_{U})
and given by

D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U}\|Q_{V|U}|P_{U})=\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}}\big[D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U}(\,\cdot\,|U)\|Q_{V|U}(\,\cdot\,|U)))\big],

(3)

where
\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}}[\,\cdot\,]
denotes expectation with respect to distribution
P_{U}
.

Note that the above definition of conditional Rényi divergence
differs
from the standard one, which is given as

D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U}P_{U}\|Q_{V|U}P_{U})
, e.g., see
[
31
, Definition 3]
. However as
\alpha\to 1
, both notions of the conditional Rényi divergence recover the conditional KL divergence, which is

D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U}\|Q_{V|U}|P_{U})=D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U}P_{U}\|Q_{V|U}P_{U})=\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}}\left[\int p_{V|U}\log\left[\frac{p_{V|U}}{q_{V|U}}\right]dv\right].

We next provide the definitions for the
\alpha
-Jensen-Shannon divergence based measures.

Definition 3

[
3
,
4
]

The
\alpha
-Jensen-Shannon divergence for
\alpha\in(0,1)
between two probability measures
P_{U}
and
P_{V}
on a measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{M})
, is denoted by
JS_{\alpha}(P_{U}\|P_{V})
and given by

JS_{\alpha}(P_{U}\|P_{V})=\alpha D_{\text{KL}}(P_{U}\|\alpha P_{U}+(1-\alpha)P_{V})+(1-\alpha)D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|\alpha P_{U}+(1-\alpha)P_{V}),

(4)

where
D_{\text{KL}}(\cdot\|\cdot)
is the KL divergence.

Definition 4

The conditional
\alpha
-Jensen-Shannon divergence between the conditional distributions
P_{V|U}
and
Q_{V|U}
given
P_{U}
is denoted by
JS_{\alpha}(P_{V|U}\|Q_{V|U}|P_{U})
and given by

JS_{\alpha}(P_{V|U}\|Q_{V|U}|P_{U})=\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}}\big[JS_{\alpha}(P_{V|U}(\,\cdot\,|U)\|Q_{V|U}(\,\cdot\,|U))\big],

(5)

where
\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}}[\,\cdot\,]
denotes expectation with respect to distribution
P_{U}
.

We herein define the Sibson mutual information (of order
\alpha
).

Definition 5

[
5
,
6
]

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
. The Sibson mutual information of order
\alpha
between
U
and
V
is denoted by
I_{\alpha}^{S}(U;V)
and given by

I_{\alpha}^{S}(U;V)=\min_{Q_{U}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})}D_{\alpha}(P_{U,V}\|Q_{U}P_{V}),

(6)

where
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})
denotes the set of probability distributions on
\mathcal{U}
.

It is known that
D_{\alpha}(P_{U,V}\|Q_{U}P_{V})
is convex in
Q_{U}

[
30
]
, which allows for a closed-form expression for the minimizer and, consequently, for the Sibson mutual information
I_{\alpha}^{S}(U;V)

[
10
,
32
]
. Let
U^{*}
denote a random variable whose distribution achieves the minimum, with corresponding distribution
P_{U^{*}}
. Then, the Sibson mutual information of order
\alpha
can equivalently be written as:

Definition 6

[
10
]

Let
\nu
be a sigma-finite positive measure such that
P_{U,V}
and
P_{U}P_{V}
are absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu\times\nu
, written as
P_{U,V},P_{U}P_{V}\ll\nu\times\nu
, with Radon–Nikodym derivatives(densities)
\frac{dP_{U,V}}{d(\nu\times\nu)}=p_{U,V}
and
\frac{d(P_{U}P_{V})}{d(\nu\times\nu)}=p_{U}p_{V}
, respectively. For
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
, the Sibson mutual information of order
\alpha
between
U
and
V
can be written as:

I_{\alpha}^{S}(U;V)=D_{\alpha}(P_{U,V}\|P_{U^{*}}P_{V}),

(7)

where the distribution
P_{U^{*}}
has density

p_{U^{*}}(u)=\frac{dP_{U^{*}}}{d\nu}(u)=\frac{\left(\displaystyle\int\left(\frac{p_{U,V}(u,v)}{p_{U}(u)p_{V}(v)}\right)^{\alpha}p_{V}(v)\,dv\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{\displaystyle\int\left(\int\left(\frac{p_{U,V}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})}{p_{U}(u^{\prime})p_{V}(v^{\prime})}\right)^{\alpha}p_{V}(v^{\prime})\,dv^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}p_{U}(u^{\prime})\,du^{\prime}}\,p_{U}(u).

(8)

Remark 1

From Definition
6
, we note that the Sibson mutual information of order
\alpha
is a functional of the distributions
P_{U,V}
and
P_{U^{*}}
. Hence, from this point onward, we denote with a slight abuse of notation the Sibson mutual information of order
\alpha
between
U
and
V
by
I_{\alpha}^{S}(P_{U,V},P_{U^{*}})
.

We end this section with the definitions of the sub-Gaussian and conditional sub-Gaussian properties.

Definition 7

A real random variable
U
with finite expectation is said to be
\sigma^{2}
-sub-Gaussian for some
\sigma^{2}>0
if

\log\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda(U-\mathbb{E}[U])}]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}\lambda^{2}}{2}

(9)

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 8

A real random variable
U
is said to be conditionally
\sigma^{2}
-sub-Gaussian given another random variable
V
(i.e., under
P_{U|V}
) for some
\sigma^{2}>0
if we have almost surely that

\log\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda(U-\mathbb{E}[U|V])}|V]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}\lambda^{2}}{2}

(10)

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Throughout the paper, we omit stating explicitly that the conditional sub-Gaussian inequality holds almost surely for the sake of simplicity.

Bounding Excess Minimum Risk

In this section, we establish a series of bounds on the excess minimum risk based on different information-theoretic measures. Our approach combines the variational characterizations of the KL divergence
[
29
]
, the Rényi divergence
[
19
]
, and the Sibson mutual information
[
10
, Theorem 2]
, along with the auxiliary distribution method introduced in
[
18
]
.

3.1
Renyi’s Divergence Based Upper Bound

We first state the variational characterization of the Rényi divergence
[
19
]
, which generalizes the Donsker-Varadhan variational formula for KL divergence
[
29
]
.

Lemma 1

[
19
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
P
and
Q
be two probability measures on
(\Omega,\mathcal{M})
and
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
,
\alpha\neq 1
. Let
g
be a measurable function such that
e^{(\alpha-1)g}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(P)
and
e^{\alpha g}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(Q)
, where
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mu)
denotes the collection of all measurable functions with finite
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-norm. Then,

D_{\alpha}(P\|Q)\geq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\mathbb{E}_{P}[e^{(\alpha-1)g(X)}]-\log\mathbb{E}_{Q}[e^{\alpha g(X)}].

(11)

We next provide the following lemma, whose proof is a slight generalization of
[
12
, Lemma 2]
and
[
15
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 2

Consider two arbitrary jointly distributed random variables
U
and
V
defined on the same probability and taking values in spaces
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
, respectively. Given a measurable function
h:\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}
, assume that
h(u,V)
is
\sigma^{2}(u)
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{V}
and
P_{V|U=u}
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
, where
\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U)]<\infty
. Then for
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[{h(U,V)}]-

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{h(\bar{U},\bar{V})}]|\leq\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U)]\,\frac{D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U}\|P_{V}|P_{U})}{\alpha}},

where
\bar{U}
and
\bar{V}
are independent copies of
U
and
V
, respectively, (i.e.
P_{\bar{U},\bar{V}}=P_{U}P_{V}
).

Proof.

By the sub-Gaussian property, we have that

\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{(\alpha-1)\lambda h(u,V)-\mathbb{E}[(\alpha-1)\lambda h(u,V)]|u=u}|U=u\right]\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}(\alpha-1)^{2}\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}

(12)

and

\log\mathbb{E}[e^{\alpha\lambda h(u,V)-\mathbb{E}[\alpha\lambda h(u,V)]}]\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}.

(13)

Re-arranging the terms gives us

-\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{(\alpha-1)\lambda h(u,V)}|U=u\right]\geq-\frac{\lambda^{2}(\alpha-1)^{2}\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}+\mathbb{E}[(1-\alpha)\lambda h(u,V)|U=u]

(14)

and

-\log\mathbb{E}[e^{\alpha\lambda h(u,V)}]\geq-\frac{\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}-\mathbb{E}[\alpha\lambda h(u,V)].

(15)

Note that by (
12
) and (
13
),
e^{(\alpha-1)\lambda h(u,V)}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(P_{V|U=u})
and
e^{\alpha\lambda h(u,V)}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(P_{V})
.
By the variational formula in (
11
), we have that

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U=u}\|P_{V})\geq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\mathbb{E}[e^{(\alpha-1)\lambda h(u,V)}|U=u]-\log\mathbb{E}[e^{\alpha\lambda h(u,V)}].

(16)

Substituting (
14
) and (
15
) in (
16
) yields

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U=u}\|P_{V})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}(\alpha-1)^{2}\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}+\mathbb{E}[(1-\alpha)\lambda h(u,V)|U=u]\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}-\mathbb{E}[\alpha\lambda h(u,V)]

\displaystyle=\alpha\lambda(\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)|U=u]-\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)])-\frac{\lambda^{2}\alpha(1-\alpha)\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}

\displaystyle\mbox{}\quad-\frac{\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha\lambda(\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)|U=u]-\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)])-\frac{\lambda^{2}\alpha\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}.

The left-hand side of the resulting inequality

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2}\alpha\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}-\alpha\lambda(\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)|U=u]-\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)])+D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U=u}\|P_{V})\geq 0

is a non-negative quadratic polynomial in
\lambda
. Thus the discriminant is non-positive and we have

\displaystyle(\alpha(\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)|U=u]-\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)]))^{2}\leq 4\left(\frac{\alpha\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}\right)D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U=u}\|P_{V}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)|U=u]-\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)]|\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma^{2}(u)D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U=u}\|P_{V})}{\alpha}}.

(17)

Since,
\bar{U}
and
\bar{V}
are independent and
P_{\bar{V}}=P_{V}
, we have that

\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)]=\mathbb{E}[h(\bar{U},\bar{V})|\bar{U}=u].

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[h(U,V)]-\mathbb{E}[h(\bar{U},\bar{V})]|

\displaystyle=\left|\int(\mathbb{E}[h(U,V)|U=u]-\mathbb{E}[h(\bar{U},\bar{V})|\bar{U}=u])P_{U}(du)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int(\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)|U=u]-\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)])P_{U}(du)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int\left|(\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)|U=u]-\mathbb{E}[h(u,V)]\right|P_{U}(du)

(18)

\displaystyle\leq\int\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma^{2}(u)D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U=u}\|P_{V})}{\alpha}}P_{U}(du)

(19)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\int 2\sigma^{2}(u)P_{U}(du)}\sqrt{\int\frac{D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U=u}\|P_{V})}{\alpha}P_{U}(du)}

(20)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U)]\,\frac{D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U}\|P_{V}|P_{U})}{\alpha}},

(21)

where (
18
) follows from Jensen’s inequality, (
19
) follows from (
17
), (
20
) follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the definition of conditional Rényi divergence in (
3
) with
D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U}\|P_{V}|P_{U})=\mathbb{E}_{U}[D_{\alpha}(P_{V|U}(\cdot|U)\|P_{V})]
.

\blacksquare

Note that the Rényi divergence based bound in Lemma
2
differs from that in
[
18
, Theorem 3]
. In our approach, we consider sub-Gaussianity under both
P_{V}
and
P_{V|U=u}
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
, which allows for non-constant sub-Gaussian parameters. This leads to a more general bound that applies to a broader class of loss functions.

Theorem 1

Let
X
,
Y
and
Z
be random vectors such that
Y\rightarrow X\rightarrow Z
, as described in Section
2.1
. Assume that there exists an optimal estimator
f
of Y from X such that
l(y,f(X))
is conditionally
\sigma^{2}(y)
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{X|Z}
and
P_{X|Z,Y=y}
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, i.e.,

\log\mathbb{E}[e^{(\lambda(l(y,f(X)))-\mathbb{E}[l(y,f(X))|Z])}|Z]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}(y)\lambda^{2}}{2}

and

\log\mathbb{E}[e^{(\lambda(l(y,f(X)))-\mathbb{E}[l(y,f(X))|Z,Y=y])}|Z,Y=y]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}(y)\lambda^{2}}{2}

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\sigma^{2}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, satisfies
\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]<\infty
. Then for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the excess minimum risk satisfies

\displaystyle L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]}{\alpha}\,D_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y,Z}\|P_{X|Z}|P_{Y,Z})}.

(22)

Proof.

Let
\bar{X}
,
\bar{Y}
and
\bar{Z}
be random variables such that
P_{\bar{Y}|\bar{Z}}=P_{Y|Z}
,
P_{\bar{X}|\bar{Z}}=P_{X|Z}
,
P_{\bar{Z}}=P_{Z}
and
\bar{Y}
and
\bar{X}
are conditionally independent given
\bar{Z}
, i.e.,

P_{\bar{Y},\bar{X},\bar{Z}}=P_{Y|Z}P_{X|Z}P_{Z}
.

We apply Lemma
2
by setting
U=Y
,
V=X
and
h(u,v)=l(y,f(x))
. Consider
\mathbb{E}[l(Y,f(X))|Z=z])
and
\mathbb{E}[l(\bar{Y},f(\bar{X}))|Z=z]
as regular expectations taken with respect to
P_{Y,X|Z=z}
and
P_{\bar{Y},\bar{X}|Z=z}
. Since,
\bar{Y}
and
\bar{X}
are conditionally independent given
\bar{Z}=z
and
P_{\bar{Z}}=P_{Z}
, we have that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[l(Y,f^{\prime}(X))|Z=z])-\mathbb{E}[l(\bar{Y},f(\bar{X}))|Z=z])|

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)|Z=z]}{\alpha}D_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y,Z=z}\|P_{X|Z=z}|P_{Y|Z=z})}.

(23)

Now,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}[l(Y,f(X))]-\mathbb{E}[l(\bar{Y},f(\bar{X}))]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int\left|\mathbb{E}[l(Y,f(X))|Z=z])-\mathbb{E}[l(\bar{Y},f(\bar{X}))|Z=z])\right|P_{Z}(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\int\Bigg(\sqrt{\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)|Z=z]}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\times\sqrt{D_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y,Z=z}\|P_{X|Z=z}|P_{Y|Z=z})}\,\Bigg)P_{Z}(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\int\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)|Z=z]P_{Z}(dz)}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\times\sqrt{\int\frac{D_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y,Z=z}\|P_{X|Z=z}|P_{Y|Z=z})}{\alpha}P_{Z}(dz)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]}{\alpha}\,D_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y,Z}\|P_{X|Z}|P_{Y,Z})},

(24)

where the first inequality follows from Jensen’s inequality and since
P_{\bar{Z}}=P_{Z}
, the second inequality follows from (
23
), the third from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the equality follows from (
3
). Since,
\bar{Y}
and
\bar{X}
are conditionally independent given
\bar{Z}
, we get the Markov chain
\bar{Y}\rightarrow\bar{Z}\rightarrow\bar{X}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[l(\bar{Y},f(\bar{X}))])

\displaystyle\geq L_{l}^{*}(\bar{Y}|\bar{X})

\displaystyle\geq L_{l}^{*}(\bar{Y}|\bar{Z})

\displaystyle=L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z),

(25)

where the first inequality follows since
\bar{Y}\rightarrow\bar{X}\rightarrow f(\bar{X})
, the second inequality holds since
\bar{Y}\rightarrow\bar{Z}\rightarrow\bar{X}
by construction, and the equality follows since
(\bar{Y},\bar{Z})
and
(Y,Z)
have the same distribution by construction.
Since,
f
is an optimal estimator of
Y
from
X
, we also have

\mathbb{E}[l(Y,f(X))])=L_{l}^{*}(Y|X).

(26)

Therefore using (
25
) and (
26
) in (
24
) combined with the fact that
L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)\geq L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)
, we arrive at the desired inequality:

\displaystyle L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]}{\alpha}\,D_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y,Z}\|P_{X|Z}|P_{Y,Z})}.

\blacksquare

Remark 2

Taking the limit as
\alpha\rightarrow 1
of the right-hand side of (
22
) in Theorem
1
, we have that

\displaystyle L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]\,D_{\text{KL}}(P_{X|Y,Z}\|P_{X|Z}|P_{Y,Z})}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]\,(I(X;Y)-I(Z;Y))},

(27)

recovering the bound in
[
15
, Theorem 3]
.

As a special case, we consider bounded loss functions, which naturally satisfy the conditional sub-Gaussian condition. The following corollary is an application of Theorem
1
under a fixed sub-Gaussian parameter. For completeness, we include the full proof.

Corollary 1

Suppose the loss function
l
is bounded, i.e.,
\|l\|_{\infty}=\sup_{y,y^{\prime}}l(y,y^{\prime})<\infty
. Then for random vectors
X
,
Y
and
Z
such that
Y\rightarrow X\rightarrow Z
as described in Section
2.1
, we have the following inequality for
\alpha\in(0,1)
on the excess minimum risk:

L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)\leq\frac{\|l\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\frac{D_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y,Z}\|P_{X|Z}|P_{Y,Z})}{\alpha}}.

(28)

Proof.

We show that the bounded loss function
l
satisfies the conditional sub-Gaussian properties of Theorem
1
. Since
l
is bounded we have that for any
f:\mathbb{R}^{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
l(y,f(x))\in[0,\|l\|_{\infty}]
. Then by Hoeffding’s lemma
[
33
]
, we can write

\log\mathbb{E}[e^{(\lambda(l(y,f(X)))}|Z]\leq\mathbb{E}[\lambda l(y,f(X))|Z])+\frac{\|l\|_{\infty}^{2}\lambda^{2}}{8}

and

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}[e^{(\lambda(l(y,f(X)))}|Z,Y=y]\leq\mathbb{E}[\lambda l(y,f(X))|Z,Y=y])+\frac{\|l\|_{\infty}^{2}\lambda^{2}}{8}

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Rearranging the above inequalities gives us that
l(y,f(X))
is conditionally
\frac{\|l\|_{\infty}^{2}}{4}
-sub-Gaussian under both
P_{X|Z}
and
P_{X|Z,Y=y}
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Then by (
22
), we have

L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)\leq\frac{\|l\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\frac{D_{\alpha}(P_{X|Y,Z}\|P_{X|Z}|P_{Y,Z})}{\alpha}}.

(29)

\blacksquare

Remark 3

Taking the limit as
\alpha\rightarrow 1
of (
29
) in Corollary
1
yields the mutual information based bound:

\displaystyle L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|l\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{X|Y,Z}\|P_{X|Z}|P_{Y,Z})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|l\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{I(X;Y)-I(Z;Y)},

(30)

which recovers the bound in
[
15
, Corollary 1]
.

3.2
\bm{\alpha}
-Jensen-Shannon Divergence Based Upper Bound

We next derive
\alpha
- Jensen-Shannon divergence based bounds on minimum excess risk.

We consider two arbitrary jointly distributed random variables
U
and
V
defined on the same probability space and taking values in
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
, respectively. Throughout this section, we work with the joint distribution
P_{U,V}
over
\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}
and the corresponding product of marginals
P_{U}P_{V}
. For convenience, we also define additional distributions that will play an important role in the derivation of our bounds.

Definition 9

The
\alpha
-convex combination of the joint distribution
P_{U,V}

and the product of two marginals
P_{U}P_{V}
is denoted by
P^{(\alpha)}_{U,V}
and given by

P^{(\alpha)}_{U,V}=\alpha P_{U,V}+(1-\alpha)P_{U}P_{V}

(31)

for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Definition 10

The
\alpha
-conditional convex combination of the conditional distribution
P_{V|U}

and the marginal
P_{V}
is denoted by
P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U}
and given by

P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U}=\alpha P_{V|U}+(1-\alpha)P_{V}

(32)

for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

We first provide the following lemma, whose proof is a slight generalization of
[
18
, Lemma 2]
and
[
15
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 3

Given a function
h:\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}
, assume that
h(u,V)
is
\sigma^{2}(u)
-sub-Gaussian under
P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u}
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
, where
\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U)]<\infty
. Then for
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[{h(U,V)}]-

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}P_{V}}[{h(U,V)}]|\leq\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U)]\,\frac{JS_{\alpha}(P_{U,V}\|P_{U}P_{V})}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}}.

Proof.

Using the assumption that the function
h(u,V)
is
\sigma^{2}(u)
-sub-Gaussian under
P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u}
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
and the Donsker-Varadhan representation
[
29
]
for
D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})
, we have that

\displaystyle D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{V|U=u}}[\lambda h(u,V)]-\log\mathbb{E}_{P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u}}[e^{\lambda h(u,V)}]

(33)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{V|U=u}}[\lambda h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u}}[\lambda h(u,V)]-\frac{\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}(u)}{2},

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Rearranging terms, we obtain for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in\mathcal{U}
that

\lambda\left(\mathbb{E}_{P_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]\right)\leq D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})+\frac{\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}.

Similarly, using the assumption that the function
h(u,V)
is
\sigma^{2}(u)
-sub-Gaussian under
P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u}
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
and the Donsker-Varadhan representation
[
29
]
for
D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})
, we have for all
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
that

\lambda^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]\right)\leq D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})+\frac{\lambda^{\prime 2}\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}.

If
\lambda<0
, then taking
\lambda^{\prime}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\lambda>0
yields that

\mathbb{E}_{P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]\leq\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})}{|\lambda|}+\frac{|\lambda|\sigma^{2}(u)}{2},

(34)

and

\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]\leq\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})}{\lambda^{\prime}}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}.

(35)

Adding (
34
) and (
35
) yields for all
\lambda<0
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})}{|\lambda|}+\frac{|\lambda|\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}+\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})}{\lambda^{\prime}}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}\sigma^{2}(u)}{2},

\displaystyle=\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})}{|\lambda|}+\frac{|\lambda|\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}+\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})}{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\lambda}+\frac{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\lambda\sigma^{2}(u)}{2},

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})+(1-\alpha)D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})}{\alpha|\lambda|}+\frac{|\lambda|\sigma^{2}(u)}{2(1-\alpha)}.

(36)

Similarly for
\lambda>0
, taking
\lambda^{\prime}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\lambda<0
, we have

-\left(\mathbb{E}_{P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]\right)\leq\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})}{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda\sigma^{2}(u)}{2},

(37)

and

-\left(\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]\right)\leq\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})}{|\lambda^{\prime}|}+\frac{|\lambda^{\prime}|\sigma^{2}(u)}{2}.

(38)

Adding (
37
) and (
38
) yields for all
\lambda>0
that

\displaystyle-\left(\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})+(1-\alpha)D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})}{\alpha\lambda}+\frac{\lambda\sigma^{2}(u)}{2(1-\alpha)}.

(39)

From (
36
) and (
39
), we get the following non-negative parabola in terms of
\lambda
:

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}(u)}{2(1-\alpha)}\right)+\lambda\left(\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})+(1-\alpha)D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})}{\alpha}\geq 0,

(40)

where (
40
) holds trivially for
\lambda=0
. Thus its discriminant is non-positive, and we have for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sigma^{2}(u)\frac{\left(\alpha D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})+(1-\alpha)D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})\right)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}(u)\frac{\left(\alpha D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})+(1-\alpha)D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})\right)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}}.

(41)

As a result, we obtain that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[{h(U,V)}]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}P_{V}}[{h(U,V)}]|

\displaystyle=\left|\int(\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)])P_{U}(du)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int\left|\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(u,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V|U=u}}[h(u,V)]\right|P_{U}(du)

(42)

\displaystyle\leq\int\left(\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}(u)\frac{\left(\alpha D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})+(1-\alpha)D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})\right)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}}\right)P_{U}(du)

(43)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\int 2\sigma^{2}(u)P_{U}(du)}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\times\sqrt{\int\frac{\left(\alpha D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V|U=u}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})+(1-\alpha)D_{\text{KL}}(P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{V|U=u})\right)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}P_{U}(du)}

(44)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[2\sigma^{2}(U)]\frac{\left(\alpha D_{\text{KL}}(P_{U,V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{U,V})+(1-\alpha)D_{\text{KL}}(P_{U}P_{V}\|P^{(\alpha)}_{U,V})\right)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}}

(45)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[2\sigma^{2}(U)]\,\frac{JS_{\alpha}(P_{U,V}\|P_{U}P_{V})}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}},

(46)

where (
42
) follows from Jensen’s inequality, (
43
) follows from (
41
), (
44
) follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
45
) follows from the definition of the conditional KL divergence and Definition
9
and (
46
) follows from the definition of
\alpha
-Jensen-Shannon divergence.

\blacksquare

We next use Lemma
3
to derive our theorem for
\alpha
-Jensen-Shannon divergence- based bound.

Theorem 2

Let
X
,
Y
and
Z
be random vectors such that
Y\rightarrow X\rightarrow Z
, as described in Section
2.1
. Assume that there exists an optimal estimator
f
of Y from X such that
l(y,f(X))
is conditionally
\sigma^{2}(y)
-sub-Gaussian under
P^{(\alpha)}_{X|Z,Y=y}=\alpha P_{X|Z,Y=y}+(1-\alpha)P_{X|Z}
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, i.e.,

\log\mathbb{E}_{P^{(\alpha)}_{X|Z,Y=y}}\left[e^{\lambda\big(l(y,f(X))-\mathbb{E}_{P^{(\alpha)}_{X|Z,Y=y}}\big[l(y,f(X))\big]\big)}\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}(y)\lambda^{2}}{2}

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\sigma^{2}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies
\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]<\infty
. Then for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the excess minimum risk satisfies

\displaystyle L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\,JS_{\alpha}(P_{Y,X|Z}\|P_{Y|Z}P_{X|Z}|P_{Z})}.

(47)

Proof.

Let
\bar{X}
,
\bar{Y}
and
\bar{Z}
be the same random variables defined in Theorem
1
.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
1
, we apply Lemma
3
by setting
U=Y
,
V=X
and
h(u,v)=l(y,f(x))
and taking regular expectations with respect to
P_{Y,X|Z=z}
and
P_{\bar{Y},\bar{X}|Z=z}
. Since,
\bar{Y}
and
\bar{X}
are conditionally independent given
\bar{Z}=z
such that
P_{\bar{Y},\bar{X}|Z=z}=P_{Y|Z}P_{X|Z}
and
P_{\bar{Z}}=P_{Z}
, we have that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y,X|Z=z}}[l(Y,f(X))]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{\bar{Y},\bar{X}|Z=z}}[l(\bar{Y},f(\bar{X}))]|

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y,X|Z=z}}[l(Y,f(X))]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y|Z=z}P_{X|Z=z}}[l(Y,f(X))]|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)|Z=z]}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}JS_{\alpha}(P_{Y,X|Z=z}\|P_{X|Z=z}P_{Y|Z=z})}.

(48)

Thus,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y,X}}[l(Y,f(X))]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{\bar{Y},\bar{X}}}[l(\bar{Y},f(\bar{X}))]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int\left|\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y,X|Z=z}}[l(Y,f(X))]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{\bar{Y},\bar{X}|Z=z}}[l(\bar{Y},f(\bar{X}))])\right|P_{Z}(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\int\Bigg(\sqrt{\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)|Z=z]}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}JS_{\alpha}(P_{Y,X|Z=z}\|P_{X|Z=z}P_{Y|Z=z})}\,\Bigg)P_{Z}(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\int\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)|Z=z]P_{Z}(dz)}\sqrt{\int\frac{JS_{\alpha}(P_{Y,X|Z=z}\|P_{X|Z=z}P_{Y|Z=z})}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}P_{Z}(dz)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\,JS_{\alpha}(P_{Y,X|Z}\|P_{Y|Z}P_{X|Z}|P_{Z})},

(49)

where the first inequality follows from Jensen’s inequality and since
P_{\bar{Z}}=P_{Z}
, the second inequality follows from (
48
), the third from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the equality follows from (
5
).
From proof of Theorem
1
, we know that
\bar{Y}\rightarrow\bar{Z}\rightarrow\bar{X}
forms a Markov chain. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{\bar{Y},\bar{X}}}[l(\bar{Y},f(\bar{X}))])\geq L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z),

(50)

Since,
f
is an optimal estimator of
Y
from
X
, we also have

\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y,X}}[l(Y,f(X))])=L_{l}^{*}(Y|X).

(51)

Therefore using (
50
) and (
51
) in (
49
) combined with the fact that
L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)\geq L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)
, we arrive at the desired inequality:

\displaystyle L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\,JS_{\alpha}(P_{Y,X|Z}\|P_{Y|Z}P_{X|Z}|P_{Z})}.

\blacksquare

Remark 4

Taking the limit as
\alpha\rightarrow 0
on the right-hand side of (
47
) in Theorem
2
,
we obtain

\displaystyle L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\,\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]\,(I(X;Y)-I(Z;Y))},

recovering the bound (
27
) of
[
15
, Theorem 3]
. Furthermore, taking the limit as
\alpha\rightarrow 1
of the right-hand side of (
47
) in Theorem
2
, yields

\displaystyle L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]\,D_{\text{KL}}(P_{Y|Z}P_{X|Z}\|P_{Y,X|Z}|P_{Z})}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(Y)]\,(L(X;Y)-L(Z;Y))},

(52)

where
L(U;V):=D_{\text{KL}}(P_{U}P_{V}\|P_{U,V})
is called the Lautum information between
U
and
V
, defined as the reverse KL divergence (i.e., the KL divergence between the product of marginals and the joint distribution)
[
34
]
. We therefore obtain an upper bound on the minimum excess risk in terms of the reverse KL divergence.

We close this section by specializing Theorem
2
to the case of bounded loss functions, hence obtaining a counterpart result to Corollary
1
.

Corollary 2

Suppose the loss function
l
is bounded. Then for random vectors
X
,
Y
and
Z
such that
Y\rightarrow X\rightarrow Z
as described in Section
2.1
, we have the following inequality for
\alpha\in(0,1)
on the excess minimum risk:

L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)\leq\frac{\|l\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\frac{JS_{\alpha}(P_{Y,X|Z}\|P_{Y|Z}P_{X|Z}|P_{Z})}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}}.

(53)

3.3
Sibson Mutual Information Based Upper Bound

We herein bound the excess minimum risk based on Sibson’s mutual information. We recall from Definition
6
that
U
and
V
are jointly distributed on measurable spaces
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
, with joint distribution
P_{U,V}
and marginals
P_{U}
and
P_{V}
, assuming that all distributions are absolutely continuous with respect to a common sigma-finite measure

, with densities
p_{U}
,
p_{V}
, and
p_{U,V}
. Let
U^{*}
denote the random variable whose distribution

attains the minimum in the definition of the Sibson mutual information
I_{\alpha}^{S}(P_{U,V},P_{U^{*}})
, with density
p_{U^{*}}
as given in (

). We now define an auxiliary distribution that will be central to the derivation of the main bounds in this section.

Definition 11

Let
P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}
be a joint distribution on
\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}
determined by density
p_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}
that is obtained by tilting (using parameter

) the densities
p_{U,V}
,
p_{U^{*}}
and
p_{V}
as follows:

p_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}(u,v)=\frac{(p_{U,V}(u,v))^{\alpha}(p_{U^{*}}(u)p_{V}(v))^{(1-\alpha)}}{\iint(p_{U,V}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}))^{\alpha}(p_{U^{*}}(u^{\prime})p_{V}(v^{\prime}))^{(1-\alpha)}du^{\prime}dv^{\prime}}

(54)

for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

We state the following lemma based on the variational representation of the Sibson mutual information
[
10
, Theorem 2]
, which establishes a connection to the KL divergence. The proof of the lemma follows from
[
18
, Lemma 3]
and
[
32
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Lemma 4

For the distributions
P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}
,
P_{U,V}
and
P_{U^{*}}P_{V}
we have

\alpha D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U,V})+(1-\alpha)D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U^{*}}P_{V})=(1-\alpha)I_{\alpha}^{S}(P_{U,V},P_{U^{*}}).

We now invoke a basic but important property of sub-Gaussian random variables that will be used later in our analysis. Specifically, the set of all sub-Gaussian random variables has a linear structure. This property is well established in the literature
[
35
,
36
]
.

Lemma 5

If
X
is
\sigma_{X}^{2}
-sub-Gaussian random variable, then for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the random variable

\alpha X
is
|\alpha|\sigma_{X}^{2}
-sub-Gaussian. If
Y
is
\sigma_{Y}^{2}
-sub-Gaussian random variable, then the sum
X+Y
is sub-Gaussian with parameter
(\sigma_{X}+\sigma_{Y})^{2}
.

We next provide the following lemma, whose proof is a slight generalization of
[
18
, Theorem 4]
and
[
15
, Lemma 1]

Lemma 6

Given a function
h:\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}
, assume that
h(u,V)
is
\gamma^{2}(u)
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{V}
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
and
h(U,V)
is
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
-sub-Gaussian under both
P_{U}P_{V}
and
P_{U,V}
. Assume also that
\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}}[e^{\gamma^{2}(U^{*})}]<\infty
. Then for
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[h(U,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}P_{V}}[h(U,V)]\right|\leq\sqrt{2((1-\alpha)\sigma^{2}+\alpha\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}}[e^{\gamma^{2}(U^{*})}])\,\frac{I_{\alpha}^{S}(P_{U,V},P_{U^{*}})}{\alpha}}.

Proof.

We first show that
h(U,V)-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(U,V)]
is
\sigma^{2}
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{U,V}
. By assumption,
h(U,V)
is
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{U,V}
. It remains to show that the term
-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(U,V)]
is also
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{U,V}
.
Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}\left[e^{\lambda\left(\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(U,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(U,V)]\right]\right)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}}\left[e^{\lambda\left(\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(U,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}P_{V}}[h(U,V)]\right)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}P_{V}}\left[e^{\lambda\left(h(U,V)-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}P_{V}}[h(U,V)]\right)}\right]

(55)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}}{8}\right),

(56)

where (
55
) follows from Jensen’s inequality, and (
56
) follows from the assumption that
h(U,V)
is
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{U}P_{V}
. Thus,
\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(U,V)]
is
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{U,V}
.

Therefore, by Lemma
5
, it follows that
-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(U,V)]
is
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{U,V}
, and hence the difference
h(U,V)-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(U,V)]
is
\sigma^{2}
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{U,V}
, as claimed.

Let
g:\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by
g(u,v)=h(u,v)-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(u,V)]
. Since,
h(U,V)-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(U,V)]
is
\sigma^{2}
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{U,V}
and by definition of
g
, we obtain that for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}\left[e^{\left({\lambda\left(g(U,V)-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[g(U,V)]\right)}\right)}\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}\lambda^{2}}{2}.

This can be re-written as

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}\left[e^{\left({\lambda\left(g(U,V)\right)}\right)}\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}\lambda^{2}}{2}+\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[\lambda g(U,V)].

(57)

Moreover, since,
h(u,V)
is
\gamma(u)^{2}
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{V}
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
, it follows that for all
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}\left[e^{\left({\lambda^{\prime}\left(h(u,V)-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(u,V)]\right)}\right)}\right]\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}(u)\lambda^{\prime 2}}{2}.

(58)

Using the definition of
g
and applying the exponential to both sides, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}\left[e^{\left({\lambda^{\prime}\left(g(u,V)\right)}\right)}\right]\leq e^{\frac{\gamma^{2}(u)\lambda^{\prime 2}}{2}}.

Taking the expectation with respect to
P_{U^{*}}
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}P_{V}}\left[e^{\left({\lambda^{\prime}\left(g(U^{*},V)\right)}\right)}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}}\bigg[e^{\frac{\gamma^{2}(U^{*})\lambda^{\prime 2}}{2}}\bigg].

Finally, taking the logarithm and noting that

\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}P_{V}}[g(U^{*},V)]=\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}P_{V}}[h(U^{*},V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}P_{V}}[h(U^{*},V)]=0,

we conclude that

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}P_{V}}\left[\exp\left({\lambda^{\prime}\left(g(U^{*},V)\right)}\right)\right]\leq\frac{\lambda^{\prime 2}}{2}\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}}[e^{\gamma^{2}(U^{*})}]+\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}P_{V}}[\lambda^{\prime}g(U^{*},V)].

(59)

Using the Donsker-Varadhan representation for
D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U,V})

[
29
]
and inequality (
57
), we have for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
that

\displaystyle D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U,V})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}}[\lambda g(\hat{U},\hat{V})]-\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[e^{\lambda g(U,V)}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}}[\lambda g(\hat{U},\hat{V})]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[\lambda g(U,V)]\mbox{}-\frac{\sigma^{2}\lambda^{2}}{2}.

Rearranging terms yields

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\mathbb{E}_{P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}}[g(\hat{U},\hat{V})]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[g(U,V)]\right)\leq D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U,V})+\frac{\sigma^{2}\lambda^{2}}{2}.

(60)

Note that

\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[g(U,V)]=\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[\left(h(U,V)-\mathbb{E}_{P_{V}}[h(U,V)]\right)]=\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[h(U,V)]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U}P_{V}}[h(U,V)].

Similarly, using the Donsker-Varadhan representation for
D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U^{*}}P_{V})

[
29
]
and inequality (
59
), we have for all
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{E}_{P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}}[g(\hat{U},\hat{V})]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}P_{V}}[g(U^{*},V)]\right)\leq D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U^{*}}P_{V})+\frac{\lambda^{\prime 2}}{2}\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}}[e^{\gamma^{2}(U^{*})}].

(61)

If

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}}[g(\hat{U},\hat{V})]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[g(U,V)]\leq\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U,V})}{\lambda}+\frac{\sigma^{2}\lambda}{2}.

(62)

On the other hand, since

(

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}}[g(\hat{U},\hat{V})]\leq\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U^{*}}P_{V})}{|\lambda^{\prime}|}+\frac{|\lambda^{\prime}|}{2}\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}}[e^{\gamma^{2}(U^{*})}].

(63)

Adding (

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[g(U,V)]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U,V})}{\lambda}+\frac{\sigma^{2}\lambda}{2}+\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U^{*}}P_{V})}{|\lambda^{\prime}|}+\frac{|\lambda^{\prime}|}{2}\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}}[e^{\gamma^{2}(U^{*})}]

\displaystyle=\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U,V})}{\lambda}+\frac{\sigma^{2}\lambda}{2}+\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U^{*}}P_{V})}{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\lambda}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\lambda}{2}\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}}[e^{\gamma^{2}(U^{*})}]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\lambda\left(\frac{(1-\alpha)\sigma^{2}+\alpha\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}}[e^{\gamma^{2}(U^{*})}]}{2(1-\alpha)}\right).

\displaystyle-\left(\mathbb{E}_{P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}}[g(\hat{U},\hat{V})]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[g(U,V)]\right)\leq\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U,V})}{|\lambda|}+\frac{\sigma^{2}|\lambda|}{2}.

(65)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}}[g(U,V)]\leq\frac{D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\hat{U},\hat{V}}\|P_{U^{*}}P_{V})}{\lambda^{\prime}}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{2}\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{U^{*}}}[e^{\gamma^{2}(U^{*})}].

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{U,V}}[g(U,V)]

\blacksquare

We next use Lemma 3.2 to derive our upper bound on the excess minimum risk in terms of the Sibson mutual information.

Theorem 3

Let
X
,
Y
and
Z
be random vectors such that
Y\rightarrow X\rightarrow Z
form a Markov chain as described in Section
2.1
. Assume that there exists an optimal estimator
f
of Y from X such that
l(y,f(X))
is conditionally
\gamma^{2}(y)
-sub-Gaussian under
P_{X|Z}

\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{Z}P_{Y^{*}|Z}}[e^{\gamma^{2}(Y^{*})}]<\infty
, and

\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}

P_{Y|Z}P_{X|Z}

P_{Y,X|Z}

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{X|Z}}\left[e^{(\lambda(l(y,f(X)))-\mathbb{E}_{P_{X|Z}}[l(y,f(X))])}\right]\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}(y)\lambda^{2}}{2}

for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,

\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y|Z}P_{X|Z}}\left[e^{(\lambda(l(Y,f(X)))-\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y|Z}P_{X|Z}}[l(Y,f(X))])}\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}\lambda^{2}}{8}

and

\log\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y,X|Z}}\left[e^{(\lambda(l(Y,f(X)))-\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y,X|Z}}[l(Y,f(X))])}\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}\lambda^{2}}{8}.

Then for

, the excess minimum risk satisfies

\displaystyle L_{l}^{*}(Y|Z)-L_{l}^{*}(Y|X)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2((1-\alpha)\sigma^{2}+\alpha\mathbb{E}_{P_{Z}}[\Phi_{Y^{*}|Z}(\gamma^{2}(Y^{*}))])}{\alpha}\,\mathbb{E}_{P_{Z}}\left[I_{\alpha}^{S}(P_{Y,X|Z},P_{Y^{*}|Z})\right]},

(70)

where

and the distribution

has density

\displaystyle p_{Y^{*}|Z}(y|z)=\frac{\left(\displaystyle\int\left(\frac{p_{Y,X|Z}(y,x|z)}{p_{Y|Z}(y|z)p_{X|Z}(x|z)}\right)^{\alpha}p_{X|Z}(x|z)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{\displaystyle\int\left(\int\left(\frac{p_{Y,X|Z}(y^{\prime},x^{\prime}|z)}{p_{Y|Z}(y^{\prime}|z)p_{X|Z}(x^{\prime}|z)}\right)^{\alpha}p_{X|Z}(x^{\prime}|z)dx^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}p_{Y|Z}(y^{\prime}|z)dy^{\prime}}p_{Y|Z}(y|z).

(71)

Proof.

Let
\bar{X}
,
\bar{Y}
and
\bar{Z}

6
by setting

,

\bar{Y}

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y,X|Z=z}}[l(Y,f(X))]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{\bar{Y},\bar{X}|Z=z}}[l(\bar{Y},f(\bar{X}))]|

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y,X|Z=z}}[l(Y,f(X))]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y|Z=z}P_{X|Z=z}}[l(Y,f(X))]|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2((1-\alpha)\sigma^{2}+\alpha\Phi_{Y^{*}|Z=z}(\gamma^{2}(Y^{*})))\,}{\alpha}I_{\alpha}^{S}(P_{Y,X|Z=z},P_{Y^{*}|Z=z})}.

Now,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y,X}}[l(Y,f(X))]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{\bar{Y},\bar{X}}}[l(\bar{Y},f(\bar{X}))]\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}_{P_{Z}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{P_{Y,X|Z=z}}[l(Y,f(X))]-\mathbb{E}_{P_{\bar{Y},\bar{X}|Z=z}}[l(\bar{Y},f(\bar{X}))]\right]\right|

U_{1}
and
U_{2}

\mathbb{P}(U_{i}=0)=1-\epsilon_{i}

\mathbb{P}(U_{i}=a)=\epsilon_{i}/(q-1)

Z=(X+U_{2})\bmod q,

Z=(Y+U_{1}+U_{2})\bmod q

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the problem of bounding the excess minimum risk in statistical inference using generalized information divergence measures. Our results extend the mutual information based bound in
[
15
]
by developing a family of bounds parameterized by the order
\alpha\in(0,1)
, involving the Rényi divergence, the
\alpha
-Jensen-Shannon divergence, and Sibson’s mutual information. For the Rényi divergence based bounds, we employed the variational representation of the divergence, following the approach in
[
12
]
, and for the
\alpha
-Jensen-Shannon and Sibson-based bounds, we adopted the auxiliary distribution method introduced in
[
18
]
.

Unlike the bounds in
[
12
,
18
]
, which assume the sub-Gaussian parameter to be constant, our framework allows this parameter to depend on the (target) random vector, thereby making the bounds applicable to a broader class of joint distributions. We demonstrated the effectiveness of our approach through three numerical examples: one involving concatenated discrete
q
-ary symmetric channels, and two based on additive Gaussian noise channels. In all cases, we observed that at least one of our
\alpha
-parametric bounds is tighter than the mutual information based bound over certain ranges of
\alpha
, with the improvements becoming more pronounced in the discrete example as the channel alphabet size
q
increased.

Future directions include exploring bounds under alternative
f
-divergence measures, developing tighter bounds for high-dimensional settings, and determining divergence rates in infinite-dimensional cases.

Experimental preparation of W W states through many-body physics on a quantum simulator Preprint: APS/123-QED Preprint: RBI-ThPhys-2025-39
Alberto Giuseppe Catalano +, Ceren B. Dağ +, Gianpaolo Torre, Salvatore Marco Giampaolo, Fabio Franchini

Abstract
W W states are quantum correlated states possessing both bipartite and multipartite entanglement, which makes them useful for several quantum algorithms. We propose a protocol to generate these states by exploiting topological ring frustration , and implement it on a programmable Rydberg atom array up to 11 qubits, successfully generating many-body W W states of Rubidium atoms. Numerical simulations show promising scaling of the algorithm to tens of qubits with near-term achievable updates on the quantum machines. To validate our state preparation protocol and probe quantum entanglement, we devise a fidelity estimator that requires only two sets of measurements. To implement it, we develop a novel and efficient Bayesian state-tomography approach that takes advantage of accurate classical numerical simulations to overcome limitations in the experimental setup. Hence, a lower bound fidelity of around 77 % 77\% is certified for the experimentally prepared state of 11 qubits. This work provides a state-of-the-art procedure to generate high-quality quantum entangled W W states, demonstrating once more how principles of physics can overcome traditional barriers of computation, and be exploited for quantum advantage.

To address this challenge, we propose generating

;

;

;

In the classical limit of purely commuting interactions, geometrical frustration produces an extensive ground state degeneracy

;

;

;

This setting allows to prepare a

;

;

The connection between

To assess the fidelity of state preparation, we design an estimator, tailored to
W

The first accesses the diagonal elements of the density matrix in the computational basis, while the second probes the off-diagonal contributions to characterize quantum coherence. Remarkably, this yields accurate results after a number of state preparations which scales only linearly with the number of qubits, in stark contrast to the exponential costs of full state tomography

;
Talath2025
. However, the second set of measurements must be performed in the transverse direction to the interactions, which is impractical in analog mode. Indeed, Rydberg interactions cannot be turned off during the experiment and always remain parallel to the direction of measurement during the rotation, inducing an unwanted state evolution

To overcome this limitation and infer fidelity from the experimental data, we introduce a Bayesian state-tomography scheme that leverages the excellent agreement between classical simulations and experimental outcomes. Conceptually, our approach is reminiscent of the
classical shadow

;
Elben2023
;

;
Hu2025
;
Cioli2025

In contrast, we employ deterministic Hamiltonian evolution combined with a small number of stochastic parameters that models the experimental noise and reduces the computational weight. This enables us to certify robust lower bounds on the fidelity of the
W

II
State validation on the Rydberg atom array platform

After state preparation, projective measurements along the
z
-axis (the axis of interaction) can probe the bit-string probabilities of
\ket{k}_{\rm AFM}
states and detect unwanted strings. However, to guarantee that the prepared state is not just an incoherent mixture of
\ket{k}_{\rm AFM}
states, one must also access the off-diagonal elements of the density matrix. Direct state tomography typically involves a number of measurements that grows exponentially with the number of qubits
Haah2016
;
Talath2025
.
However, having checked that essentially only strings of the type
\ket{k}_{\rm AFM}
appear in the
z
-basis measurements, we find that the fidelity relative to a perfect
W
state can be expressed as

\mathcal{F}_{e}=\frac{1}{L}\bigg[\sum_{k=1}^{L}p_{k}+\Tr(\rho_{\rm exp}\mathcal{P}_x)\bigg],

(4)

where
p_{k}
denotes the measured probability of
\ket{k}_{\rm AFM}
and
\rho_{\rm exp}
is the density matrix of the experimentally prepared state (derivation in Sec. S3.A of SM). Here
\mathcal{P}_{x}=\sum_{n=1}^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\sum_{a=1}^{N}P_{2n,a}^{x}
is the sum of
x
-correlators involving an even number of contiguous sites on the ring, i.e.,
P_{2n,j}^{x}=\prod_{l=j}^{j+2n-1}\sigma_{l}^{x}
, and captures the required off-diagonal coherence. Thus, only two sets of projective measurements, in the
z
and
x
-bases, suffice to characterize the prepared
W
state. Note that while the number of populated strings in the
z
basis scales linearly with the number of qubits, in principle this is not the case in the
x
basis. However, we find that a polynomial number of measurements is sufficient to saturate the value of the correlation function (see Sec. S3.A in SM).

Bearing this in mind, it is clear that we need to perform only two experimental sequences: (i) the main pulse sequence in Figure
1
followed by measurements in the
z-
basis to extract populations, and (ii) the main pulse sequence followed by a rotation sequence (Figure
1
) and
x
-basis measurements to access off-diagonal elements.

However, the impossibility to switch off Rydberg interactions during the experiment in the analog atom array produces non-trivial evolutions during the rotation sequence
Omran_2019
. Thus, at the end of the second experiment, it is not possible to measure the original
P_{2n,k}^{x}
. Even employing quantum optimal control techniques such as gradient ascent pulse estimation (GRAPE) algorithm
Doria2011
;
Khaneja2005
, we were unable to establish a sequence whose operator fidelity, computed with respect to the ideal rotation sequence, is larger than
0.3
. Nevertheless, the values of the correlators measured after the second sequence closely match the numerical predictions across all parameters. Motivated by this, we introduce a Bayesian approach to infer otherwise inaccessible quantities from experimentally measurable observables.

The core of the Bayesian approach is the update formula

P(H|E)=\frac{P(E|H)\cdot P(H)}{P(E)}\;,

(5)

where
P(H)
is the
prior probability
reflecting the initial hypothesis
H
, and
E
is the evidence, i.e., new data used to improve the estimate.
P(H|E)
is the
posterior probability
, the probability of
H
given
E
, which is the quantity of interest: the probability of a hypothesis given the observed evidence.
P(E|H)
is the
likelihood
, which is the probability of observing
E
given
H
. It indicates the compatibility of the evidence with the given hypothesis.
P(E)
is the
marginal likelihood
and constitutes the normalization factor. Using this formula, one can improve the initial hypothesis through new data, yielding a more accurate probability distribution
MacKay2003
.

Our approach differs from the Bayesian quantum state tomography of
Schack2001
;
Blume2010
;
Granade2016
;
Lukens2020
in multiple ways. First, instead of starting from a null prior and progressively updating it through new measurements, we refine a prior generated by classical simulations using experimental data. Furthermore, since numerical simulations depend on a handful of parameters describing the experimental setting, the optimization landscape does not scale with the dimension of the physical Hilbert space, but rather with the number of parameters employed.

•

W

•

P_{2n,k}^{x}
.

IV
Conclusions and outlook

We proposed and tested, both experimentally and numerically, a protocol for the adiabatic preparation of
W
states, an important and necessary class of many-body quantum states for several quantum algorithms and technologies.
This was achieved by implementing ring frustration on a programmable neutral-atom quantum simulator, leveraging the interplay of odd system size, periodic boundary conditions, antiferromagnetic interactions, and quantum effects, recently studied as
topological frustration
. The versatility of Rydberg atom platforms allowed us for an unprecedented implementation of such frustrated systems to generate robust
W
states.
We remark that, contrary to GHZ states, tracing out one of the atoms does not fully destroy the coherence of
W
states. Thus, although our protocol seems to be limited to prepare states with an odd number of atoms, it is actually possible to generate a genuine
W
state with an even number of atoms, as detailed in the SM.

To validate the protocol, we made two key developments: first, demonstrating that only two sets of measurements suffice to estimate fidelity, and second, developing a Bayesian tomography scheme that combines accurate numerical simulations with experimental data after nontrivial evolution. This allows inference of otherwise inaccessible observables. Using this method, we estimate a fidelity of
\mathcal{F}_{e}=0.774
for the largest system of
L=11
atoms on
Aquila
. Since the validation process introduces additional errors, primarily from the rotation sequence, the reported fidelities are lower bounds, highlighting the robustness of adiabatic preparation via topological frustration.

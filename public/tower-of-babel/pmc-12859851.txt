A nomogram for predicting postoperative intra-abdominal hypertension in pediatric patients following liver transplantation: a prospective observational study
Authors: ZhiRu Li, FangYan Lu, YanHong Dai, Li Ji, HaiLi Huang, PanPan He, WanYing Zhang, HuaFen Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo create a nomogram for early intra-abdominal hypertension (IAH) detection in pediatric patients following liver transplantation (LT).MethodsThis prospective, observational study was conducted in a tertiary hospital’s Liver Transplantation Intensive Care Unit (LICU), prospectively recruiting a cohort of 425 children undergoing liver transplantation between January 2022 and December 2024. Perioperative parameters were electronically extracted via the hospital information system. Intra-abdominal pressure (IAP) quantification employed the standardized transvesical technique. To identify clinically relevant predictors, a LASSO-based feature selection algorithm was applied, leveraging regularization to shrink coefficients of non-informative variables toward zero. The retained covariates were then integrated into a multivariable logistic regression model for nomogram construction. Internal validation included three domains: (1) discrimination performance evaluated by area under the ROC curve (AUC), (2) calibration accuracy by Hosmer-Lemeshow test with calibration curves, and (3) clinical utility assessed through decision curve analysis (DCA) across probability thresholds.ResultsThree independent predictors were identified: Graft-to-Recipient Weight Ratio (GRWR), duration of mechanical ventilation (MV), and central venous pressure (CVP), integrated into a predictive nomogram. DCA showed a significant net benefit, and temporal validation confirmed the nomogram’s reliability. The nomogram displayed superior predictive accuracy (AUC = 0.831) with adequate calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow test p > 0.05). DCA revealed favorable clinical applicability across threshold probabilities, showing positive net benefit values ranging from 10%−80% and 5%−80%, while temporal validation maintained discriminative capacity (AUC = 0.822).ConclusionsThe study devised an effective nomogram for identifying pediatric patients at a heightened risk of IAH following LT, incorporating GRWR, duration of MV and CVP.Keywords: Pediatrics, Liver transplantation, Intra-abdominal hypertension, Intra-abdominal presure, Risk, Nomograms

Introduction

Paediatric liver transplantation (PLT) is a well-established procedure for treating end-stage liver disease, metabolic/genetic conditions, and acute liver failure in children [1]. Over the past five years, more than 10,000 PLT procedures have been performed globally [2]. In China, a significant proportion of recipients are infants and young children under the age of 3 [3]. Given that the majority of paediatric patients in need of LT are under 1 year old and the limited availability of paediatric donor organs, partial adult organs are often utilized [4]. In young children, the size disparity between their small abdominal cavities and relatively large split organs can lead to tension and inadequate compliance of the abdominal wall, potentially resulting in elevated intra-abdominal pressure (IAP) [5, 6]. Furthermore, the pathophysiology of preoperative chronic liver disease and the unique characteristics of the transplantation process—such as significant surgical trauma, extensive intraoperative blood transfusions and infusions, prolonged surgical duration, mechanical ventilation, and postoperative immunosuppression—place children undergoing LT at a heightened risk of developing intra-abdominal hypertension (IAH) and abdominal compartment syndrome (ACS) [7].

According to the 2013 guidelines from the World Society of Abdominal Compartment Syndrome (WSACS), IAH in children is characterized by an IAP exceeding 10 mmHg [8]. When IAH is coupled with the onset or exacerbation of organ dysfunction, it transitions into ACS. It is estimated that between 24.0% and 85.0% of paediatric patients post LT are susceptible to developing IAH, with around 63.0% progressing to ACS [9, 10]. The pathophysiological consequences of elevated IAP are profound, affecting hemodynamic, respiratory, gastrointestinal, neurological, and renal functions in critically ill individuals [11, 12]. Delayed recognition of IAH and ACS can impede timely diagnosis and necessary medical or surgical interventions, potentially leading to increased mortality rates [9]. The WSACS recommend measuring IAP in critically ill patients when any risk factors for IAH are present [8]. However, measuring IAP can be a time-consuming process, particularly with the necessity of hourly assessments [13]. Therefore, accurately pinpointing patients at risk of developing IAH and ACS is crucial to circumvent unwarranted IAP measurements. Moreover, early identification and intervention in individuals with IAH are vital for averting progression to ACS, thereby preventing irreversible organ damage or mortality [14].

Although some scholars have conducted exploration of the risk factors for IAH in critically ill paediatric patients [11, 15], only one small-sample study was conducted in paediatric patients after LT [10]. Therefore, there is still a lack of a straightforward and precisee tool for predicting the probability of IAH in paediatric patients undergoing LT. The aim of this study is to bridge this knowledge gap by comprehensively collecting preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative data from pediatric patients undergoing LT. The goal is to monitor the occurrence of IAH, establish a dependable risk prediction nomogram for IAH, thereby facilitating early identification of individuals at high risk of developing IAH. Through timely intervention, we hope to reduce the frequency, severity, and duration of IAH, ultimately enhancing the postoperative outcomes for these paediatric patients.

Methods

This a prospective, observational, single-center study was conducted at the Liver Transplant Intensive Care Unit (LTICU) of a tertiary hospital, renowned for managing a substantial caseload of paediatric patients with intractable liver diseases annually. This study was based on the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) checklist to report [16].

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria encompassed the following: (a) children diagnosed with end-stage or genetic metabolic liver disease who undergoing LT, (b) length of stay in the LTICU ≥ 24 h with an indwelling urinary catheter in place, and (c) willingness to comply with the study requirements. The exclusion criteria comprised: (a) individuals combined with transplantation of other organs, (b) patients with contraindications to transvesical IAP measurement, (e.g., bladder trauma, bladder surgery, neurogenic bladder), (c) pre-existing IAH before surgery, (d) children death or discharge prior to IAP measurement, and (e) severe organ or systemic diseases postoperatively (renal failure, heart failure, etc.). Additionally, it is confirmed that all recipients in the final cohort successfully underwent primary fascial closure, with no cases requiring planned secondary closure due to abdominal compartment syndrome.

The construction of risk prediction models requires careful variable selection guided by the events per variable (EPV) criterion, which recommends a minimum ratio of 10 events per predictor to ensure statistical reliability [17]. The sample size (N) is calculated by multiplying the number of predictors by 10 and dividing by the incidence rate of positive outcomes. Our predictor identification process employed a tripartite strategy integrating evidence synthesis from existing literature, systematic data evaluation, and consensus-building through expert panels [10, 18]. Preliminary investigations revealed an anticipated positive outcome rate of 32.8% and identified 9 candidate variables for regression modeling. After accounting for potential 10–20% participant attrition, the calculated sample size ranged from 302 to 329 subjects. Ultimately, 317 pediatric cases meeting inclusion criteria were enrolled. A temporal validation design was implemented, with cohort allocation determined by admission timelines: the training set included 222 liver transplantation (LT) recipients admitted between January 2022 and December 2023, whereas the validation cohort comprised 95 patients treated from January to December 2024.

Outcome variable

Bladder pressure can objectively and accurately reflect IAP. Transvesical measurement is simple, practical, and considered the gold standard for intermittent IAP measurement in adults and children [8, 19]. The measurement method is as follows: [20] the patient is placed in a supine position, the urinary catheter is used to empty the bladder, then sterile saline is injected into the bladder at a rate of 1 ml/kg, left for 30–60 s. The injection tubing is disconnected from the syringe, allowing air to enter the tubing. The intersection between the mid-axillary line and the iliac crest is taken as the zero point. The pressure value is read at the end of expiration and when the abdominal muscles are not actively contracting. Based on the guidelines published by the WSACS, paediatric IAH is defined as a sustained or repeated pathological increase in IAP ≥ 10 mmHg. In this study, IAH is defined as IAP ≥ 10 mmHg and occurring consecutively at least 2 times. In children, IAH can be divided into the following grades: I = IAP 10–12, II = 13–15, III = 16–18 and IV >18 mmHg [8, 21]. Provide uniform training and assessment for all ICU nurses involved in the care of paediatric liver transplant patients to ensure consistency in transvesical measurement procedures.

Explanatory variables

Potential predictive factors were chosen through a thorough review of existing literature covering pediatric patients in the ICU, adults post LT, clinical expertise, and preliminary experiments. Predictor variables were categorized into baseline patient characteristics, preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative risk factors. Key patient characteristics collected included age, BMI, gender, disease diagnosis, type of LT. Preoperative data encompassed surgical history, blood lactate concentration, and Graft-to-Recipient Weight Ratio (GRWR), which is an important selection criterion for adult living donor liver transplantation (ALDLT) and determined by dividing the weight of the graft by the recipient’s weight and multiplying the result by 100% [22]. Intraoperative data included a range of operative details, such as blood loss, warm ischemia time for LT, cold ischemia time for LT, time of anhepatic phase, surgery duration, urine volume, crystalloid input volume, colloid input volume, plasma input volume, hypothermia, mechanical ventilation (MV) and duration of MV. Furthermore, postoperative data were collected, including diverse liver function laboratory parameters, such as alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, total bilirubin and direct bilirubin. Additionally, we also collected postoperative central venous pressure (CVP), which is calculated by taking the average of the most recent CVP measurements taken during each IAP measurement (Table 1).

Data collection procedures

To maintain methodological consistency, data collection was centralized under a single trained researcher. This standardized protocol was implemented for pediatric cases fulfilling predefined eligibility criteria, facilitating structured aggregation of pre- and intraoperative clinical data alongside laboratory parameters through the institutional electronic health record system. Preoperative metrics were derived from final assessments documented immediately prior to surgical intervention. Post-LTICU transfer, baseline postoperative parameters for all variables were systematically recorded within the initial 24-hour observation window. It is recommended that IAP be monitored every 6–8 h in critically ill paediatric patients with any known risk factors of IAH [15, 23]. Previous meta-analyses have demonstrated that abdominal surgery is a risk factor for IAH and ACS in critically ill patients [24]. Building upon this evidence, the current study involves monitoring the IAP of post-liver transplant paediatric patients every 6–8 h until the urinary catheter is removed or they are transferred out of the ICU.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were executed utilizing SPSS 25.0 (IBM Corp) and R 4.0.5 (R Foundation). Continuous variables demonstrating normal distribution were expressed as mean ± SD, with between-group differences assessed via t-tests. Non-parametric data were characterized by median (IQR) values and analyzed through Mann-Whitney U tests. Categorical variables were reported as frequency (%) distributions, with comparative analyses employing χ² tests. IAP variability was assessed through two complementary metrics: (1) coefficient of variation (CV) to standardize fluctuation magnitude relative to mean values, and (2) absolute range to capture peak-to-trough differences. Only patients with ≥ 2 measurements were included in variability analyses. To identify potential predictive markers in patients, we implemented LASSO regression with tenfold cross-validation for optimal λ-value determination, applying the 1-standard error (SE) criterion for feature selection. Subsequently, a backward stepwise logistic regression analysis refined the LASSO-derived variables to establish statistically significant predictors. These determinants were integrated into a nomogram, where individual predictor values were converted to point scores on an upper axis. Cumulative point summation across variables generated a total score, mapped to corresponding intra-abdominal hypertension (IAH) probability estimates on the lower axis.

The predictive model underwent tripartite validation according to established methodology: [25] (1) Discrimination: Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis quantified discrimination accuracy, with overall performance measured by the area under the curve (AUC); (2) Calibration: calibration fidelity was examined through graphical plots (rms package) complemented by the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test (ResourceSelection package) [26]. A nonsignificant Hosmer-Lemeshow test result (p >0.05) indicated strong concordance between predicted probabilities and observed event frequencies; (3) Clinical Utility: decision curve analysis (DCA) (rmda package) evaluated clinical utility by calculating net benefit ratios across probability thresholds, thereby determining real-world applicability within the study cohort.

Ethical considerations

The study protocol received approval from the hospital’s ethics committees (2023-IRB-0146). Prior to engaging any study participants, we rigorously adhered to thorough informed consent procedures. Informed consent was obtained from parents or legal guardians for all children included in the study. Our criteria for evaluating the ability to give informed consent strictly follow the General Principles of the Civil Law of the People’s Republic of China and the requirements outlined in the China Good Clinical Practice guidelines. By adhering to these standards, we ensure the protection of each participant’s legal rights and welfare, maintaining rigorous compliance with both local and international ethical standards.

Results

General characteristics

This study included a total of 317 children who underwent LT (Fig. 1). The cohort was divided temporally into the training set (n = 222, admitted between January 2022 and December 2023) and the validation set (n = 95, admitted from January to December 2024), maintaining a 7:3 ratio to ensure comparable sample sizes. Significant differences were observed in IAP variability between the training and validation cohorts. The training cohort showed both higher median IAP coefficient of variation (5.90% [IQR: 2.98–11.36%] vs. 5.41% [2.72–7.69%], p = 0.025) and wider median IAP range (1.00 mmHg [0.50–2.00.50.00] vs. 0.90 mmHg [0.50–1.20], p = 0.003) compared to the validation cohort. Within the training cohort, 30.63% (68 out of 222 were identified as having IAH, with 48.53% (33/68) classified as grade I, 27.94% (19/68) as grade II, 14.71% (10/68) as grade III and 8.82% (6/68) as grade IV. 38.28% (85 out of 222) of these children were male, with the median patient age was 7.00 months (IQR: 5.00–12.00). 79.23% (177/222) of the patients were diagnosed with biliary atresia preoperatively, 20.72% of the patients received split liver transplantation (SLT), 66.22% received ALDLT and 13.06% received deceased donor liver transplantation (DDLT). In the validation group, 30.53% (29 out of 95 were identified as having IAH, with 48.27% (14/29) classified as grade I, 24.14% (7/29) as grade II, and 20.69% (6/29) as grade III and 6.89% (2/29) as grade IV. 48.42% (46 out of 95) of these children were male, with the median patient age was 7.00 months (IQR: 5.00–11.00). 72.63% (69/95) of the patients were diagnosed with biliary atresia preoperatively, 22.11% of the patients received SLT, 55.78% received ALDLT and 22.11% received DDLT. These selected features included GRWR, duration of MV, CVP, ALT, direct bilirubin, which were subsequently included in multivariate logistic regression analysis. Risk prediction model development and validation

The independent predictors mentioned above were utilized to construct a predictive nomogram (Fig. 3). Elevated total points for each patient signified an increased risk of IAH. Furthermore, the Hosmer-Lemeshow test confirmed that the model exhibited a good fit (χ2 = 4.229, P = 0.836).

Nomogram for predicting the risk of IAH

The discrimination ability of the nomogram was evaluated by ROC curve analysis. In this nomogram model, the AUC values for the training and validation sets were 0.831 (95% CI: 0.769–0.876; Fig. 4A) and 0.822 (0.711–0.902; Fig. 4B), respectively, indicating good accuracy. The sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive valueor distinguishing between the presence and absence of IAH were 62.3%, 90.9%, 75.0%, and 84.6% in the development cohort, and 65.5%, 87.5%, 70.4%, and 84.9% in the validation cohort, respectively.

DCA was utilized to evaluate the clinical effectiveness of the predictive nomogram. The results of the DCA in this study revealed that the decision curves predominantly positioned above the ‘none’ and ‘all’ lines across most threshold ranges (10%−80% and 5%−80%). This indicates that employing the prediction model for patient intervention yields greater benefits compared to alternative strategies, such as intervening in all patients, no patients, or solely in those diagnosed with IAH, except for a narrow range of low preferences. Discussion

This study has introduced and validated a perioperative nomogram designed for personalized prediction of IAH in paediatric patients undergoing LT. This nomogram holds significant value for clinicians, as it facilitates early identification of children who are at a heightened risk of developing IAH. Timely recognition is paramount as it allows for prompt decompression interventions to avert further organ function deterioration in paediatric patients, ultimately leading to enhanced postoperative outcomes. The nomogram incorporates three key predictors: GRWR, CVP, and duration of MV. The model exhibited strong discriminatory power with an AUC of 0.831, indicating excellent ability to distinguish between high- and low-risk paediatric patients. Importantly, the calibration analysis further validated its clinical utility, demonstrating close agreement between predicted and observed IAH probabilities (Fig. 5A and B). While discrimination (AUC) reflects model’s ranking ability, calibration ensures that predicted risks quantitatively match real-world outcomes—a critical feature for clinical decision-making [27].

Due to the multitude of complications following LT, the development of intra-abdominal hypertension often presents insidiously and is difficult to detect, and lack of attention to the impact of IAH on post-transplant patients, which can result in delays in diagnosis and treatment. In our investigation involving 317 children undergoing LT, 30.60% (97/317) developed IAH in at least two consecutive measurements postoperatively, which is consistent with rates reported in comparable studies involving paediatric critically ill patients, ranging from approximately 12.6% to 43.9%. In the research conducted by Deindl et al., it was observed that out of the 23 children who underwent LT, elevated IAP meeting the criteria for abdominal hypertension was detected in 102 instances (24%) out of 425 IAP measurements [9]. Junge et al. observed a high frequency of IAH in at least two consecutive measurements following PLT, with 85% (23/27) affected, of which 17.4% reached grade III and 13% grade IV, correlating with reduced portal vein flow and organ dysfunction [10]. Our study yielded similar results, with 16.49% (16/97) reaching grade III and 8.25% (8/97) reaching grade IV.

In our study, GRWR was identified as a significant independent risk factor for IAH, which is a key parameter in LT. Due to the scarcity of size-matched paediatric deceased donors, access to timely LT is challenging for children [28]. LDLT, SLT, and DDLT have emerged as alternatives to overcome this problem [29]. Large-for-size grafts have been reported to be associated with increased risks of IAH and ACS [30, 31], a finding corroborated by our study. We found that large-for-size grafts were associated with increased risks of IAH: each 1% increase in GRWR was associated with a 2.21-fold higher odds of IAH development (OR = 2.21, 95% CI 1.623–3.019, p < 0.001). Notably, we observed significant GRWR differences between our training and validation cohorts (median 3.38% [2.49–4.15] vs. 1.71% [1.36–2.01], p < 0.001). Importantly, both values remained within clinically accepted ranges, and the model demonstrated robust predictive performance in validation (AUC = 0.822), supporting its clinical applicability. While most centers avoid GRWR >2.5% due to complication risks [32], some advocate up to 4% for young children when matched grafts are unavailable [33]. Our findings demonstrate the model’s robustness across different GRWR ranges, though future studies with balanced cohort distributions could provide further validation.

Contrary to some previous reports [12, 34], we found no significant association between MV administration (yes/no) and IAH development (p = 0.987), possibly reflecting differences in study populations. However, our multivariate analysis revealed that prolonged MV duration independently predicted IAH risk in PLT recipients (adjusted OR = 1.01 per additional hour, 95% CI 1.001–1.013, p = 0.022). This finding aligns with existing pathophysiological understandings [7, 34]. The technical complexity of PLT often necessitates extended MV, which may elevate intrathoracic pressure, impair venous return, and cause visceral congestion. These hemodynamic changes can reduce effective perfusion, exacerbate intestinal edema, and compromise gastrointestinal motility—all established contributors to IAH development [34]. Furthermore, IAH may create a vicious cycle by worsening lung injury, increasing intrathoracic pressure, and promoting atelectasis [35]. While current evidence supports the role of elevated positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) in preventing alveolar collapse [36], the optimal PEEP levels specifically for pediatric LT recipients with IAH require further investigation [37]. Based on the demonstrated association between MV duration and IAH risk, the evaluation of strategies to reduce ventilation duration, when clinically appropriate, may have potential value, though prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings.

Our study identified elevated postoperative CVP as an independent risk factor for IAH (adjusted OR = 1.42 per 1 mmHg increase, 95% CI 1.204–1.661, p < 0.001). This association may be attributed to several procedure-specific factors in PLT: significant intraoperative blood loss, hemodynamic fluctuations from reperfusion syndrome, and the technical requirements of maintaining an open inferior vena cava and portal vein [38]. The extended surgical duration combined with postoperative abdominal drainage often necessitates substantial fluid resuscitation [39], consistent with the findings of Kim et al. demonstrating CVP-IAP correlation in critically ill patients [40]. While systematic reviews support restrictive fluid strategies for reducing MV duration and pulmonary complications in adult LT recipients [41], pediatric-specific evidence remains limited. Children exhibit distinct fluid dynamics, with higher total body water and extracellular fluid volumes [42]. Our data suggest that CVP monitoring may help optimize fluid management in pediatric LT, though the optimal thresholds require prospective validation in this vulnerable population.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, our IAP measurements were taken every 6–8 h, potentially leading to an underestimation of IAH incidence. Subsequent studies could consider continuous IAP monitoring to obtain more comprehensive data. Second, as a prospective study primarily designed to evaluate IAH predictors, we did not systematically collect data on graft-specific complications (e.g., vascular thrombosis, biliary strictures) that may interact with intra-abdominal hypertension. While we included biochemical markers of graft function (ALT, AST) as surrogate indicators, future studies could directly correlate IAH thresholds with discrete clinical endpoints. Third, our single-center design limits generalizability, as the patient population may not fully represent heterogeneity in disease characteristics, treatment protocols, and demographic distributions across different clinical settings. Additionally, significant variability differences between training and validation cohorts (p < 0.05 for CV and range) with more volatile cases in the training set may further affect model performance. Multicenter validation with balanced cohorts is needed to confirm robustness across diverse settings. Finally, our external validation was temporally restricted and lacked geographical diversity, potentially constraining the model’s applicability. Future research should prioritize multicenter external validation to enhance the model’s assessment across diverse populations.

Conclusions

This study successfully identified key predictors of IAH post LT in pediatric patients and developed a validated nomogram for early risk assessment.This tool enables prompt interventions, thereby improving patient care and outcomes. Its user-friendly interface assists healthcare providers in timely detection and treatment of IAH, thereby averting further deterioration and potentially enhancing the prognosis of children undergoing LT.

A Probabilistic Proof for Stable Fluctuations in the Extremal Process of Branching Brownian Motion
Lisa Hartung, Oren Louidor, Tianqi Wu

Abstract
We give a probabilistic proof for the emergence of the Stable- 1 1 Law for the random fluctuations of the mass of the extremal process of branching Brownian Motion away from its tip. This result was already shown by Mytnik et al. [ 19 ] albeit using PDE techniques. As a consequence, we demystify the origin of these fluctuations and the meaning of the deterministic centering function required.



1
Introduction and results

1.1
Setup and background

This work concerns fluctuations in the extremal process of branching Brownian motion (BBM) away from the tip.
Let us begin by recalling the definition of BBM and some of the theory concerning its extreme value statistics.
Let
L_{t}
be the set of particles alive at time
t\geq 0
in a continuous-time Galton-Watson process with binary branching at rate
1
. The entire genealogy can be recorded via the metric space
(T,{\rm d})
, consisting of the elements
T:=\bigcup_{t\geq 0}L_{t}
and equipped with the
genealogical distance

{\rm d}(x,x^{\prime}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\big(|x|-|x\wedge x^{\prime}|\big)+\big(|x^{\prime}|-|x\wedge x^{\prime}|\big)\Big)\,,\qquad x,x^{\prime}\in T\,.

(1.1)

In the above,
|x|
stands for the generation of
x
, namely
t
such that
x\in L_{t}
and
x\wedge x^{\prime}
is the most recent common ancestor of
x
and
x^{\prime}
. We shall also write
x_{s}
for the ancestor of
x
at generation
0\leq s\leq|x|
.

Conditional on
(T,{\rm d})
, let
h=(h(x):\>x\in T)
be a mean-zero continuous Gaussian process with covariance function given by

\mathbb{E}h(x)h(x^{\prime})=|x\wedge x^{\prime}|\,,\qquad x,x^{\prime}\in T\,.

(1.2)

The triplet
(h,T,{\rm d})
(or just
h
for short) forms a standard BBM, and
h(x)
for
x\in L_{t}
is interpreted as the height of particle
x
at time
t
. As such, this process models a system in which particles diffuse as standard Brownian motions (BMs) and undergo binary splitting at
\text{Exp}(1)
-distributed random times. The restriction of
T
to all particles born up to time
t
will be denoted by
T_{t}:=\bigcup_{s\leq t}L_{s}
, with
{\rm d}_{t}
and
h_{t}
the corresponding restrictions of
{\rm d}
and
h
, respectively. The natural filtration of the process
(\mathcal{F}_{t}:\>t\geq 0)
can then be defined via
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma(h_{t},T_{t},{\rm d}_{t})
for all
t\geq 0
.

The study of extreme values of
h
dates back to the works of Ikeda et al.
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
, McKean
[
18
]
, Bramson
[
5
,
6
]
and Lalley and Sellke
[
17
]
who derived asymptotics for the law of the
maximal height
h^{*}_{t}=\max_{x\in L_{t}}h_{t}(x)
. Introducing the centering function

m_{t}:=\sqrt{2}t-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\log^{+}t\,,\quad\text{where }\qquad\log^{+}t:=\log(t\vee 1)\,,

(1.3)

and writing

\widehat{h}_{t}
for the centered process
h_{t}-m_{t}
and
\widehat{h}^{*}_{t}:=h_{t}^{\ast}-m_{t}
for the centered maximum, these works show

\widehat{h}^{*}_{t}\,\underset{t\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\,\widehat{h}^{*}_{\infty}:=G+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log Z\,,

(1.4)

where
G
is a Gumbel distributed random variable with rate
\sqrt{2}
and
Z
, which is independent of
G
, is the almost sure limit as
t\to\infty
of (a multiple of) the so-called
derivative martingale
:

\textstyle Z_{t}:=C_{\diamond}\sum_{x\in L_{t}}\big(\sqrt{2}t-h_{t}(x)\big){\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}(h_{t}(x)-\sqrt{2}t)}\,,

(1.5)

for some properly chosen
C_{\diamond}>0
.

Other extreme values of
h
can be studied simultaneously by considering its
extremal process
. To describe the latter, given
t\geq 0
,
x\in L_{t}
and
r>0
, we let
\mathcal{C}_{t,r}(x)
denote the
cluster
of
relative heights
of particles in
L_{t}
, which are at genealogical distance at most
r
from
x
. This is defined formally as the point measure

\textstyle\mathcal{C}_{t,r}(x):=\sum_{y\in{\rm B}_{r}(x)}\delta_{h_{t}(y)-h_{t}(x)},\ \text{ where}\quad{\rm B}_{r}(x):=\{y\in L_{t}:\>{\rm d}(x,y)<r\}\,.

(1.6)

Fixing any positive function
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto r_{t}
such that both
r_{t}
and
t-r_{t}
tend to
\infty
as
t\to\infty
and letting

L_{t}^{*}=\big\{x\in L_{t}:\>h_{t}(x)\geq h_{t}(y)\,,\forall y\in{\rm B}_{r_{t}}(x)\big\}\,,

(1.7)

the
structured extremal process
is then given as

\textstyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t}:=\sum_{x\in L_{t}^{*}}\delta_{h_{t}(x)-m_{t}}\otimes\delta_{\mathcal{C}_{t,r_{t}}(x)}\,.

(1.8)

That is,
\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{t}}
is a point process on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}_{p}((-\infty,0])
, where
\mathbb{M}_{p}((-\infty,0])
denote the space of Radon point measures on
(-\infty,0]
, which records the
centered-heights-of
and the
clusters-around
all
r_{t}
-local maxima of
h|_{L_{t}}
. Then, it was (essentially) shown in
[
1
,
2
]
that

\big(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t},\,\widehat{h}_{t}^{*},Z_{t}\big)\underset{t\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\big(\widehat{\mathcal{E}},\,\widehat{h}_{\infty}^{*},\,Z\big)\,.

(1.9)

Above
Z
and
\widehat{h}^{*}_{\infty}
are as in (
1.4
), and the law of
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}
conditional on
Z
is

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}\,|\,Z\sim{\rm PPP}(Z{\rm e}^{-\sqrt{2}u}{\rm d}u\otimes\nu)\overset{{\rm d}}{=}{\rm PPP}({\rm e}^{-\sqrt{2}(u-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log Z)}{\rm d}u\otimes\nu)\,,\,,

(1.10)

with, importantly, the same
Z
used to realize all the objects on the right hand side of (
1.9
).
The measure
\nu
in (
1.10
) is a (nonrandom) probability distribution over the space
\mathbb{M}_{p}((-\infty,0])
, which we call the
cluster distribution
.

As two consequences, one gets the convergence of the
extremal process of local maxima
:

\mathcal{E}^{*}_{t}\underset{t\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\mathcal{E}^{*}\,,

(1.11)

where

\mathcal{E}_{t}^{*}:=\sum_{x\in L^{*}_{t}}\delta_{h_{t}(x)-m_{t}}\quad,\qquad\mathcal{E}^{*}:=\sum_{(u,\mathcal{C})\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}\,\delta_{u}\,\sim\,{\rm PPP}(Z{\rm e}^{-\sqrt{2}u}{\rm d}u)\,,

(1.12)

and the convergence of the
usual
extremal process of
h
:

\mathcal{E}_{t}\underset{t\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\mathcal{E}\,,

(1.13)

where

\mathcal{E}_{t}:=\sum_{x\in L_{t}}\delta_{h_{t}(x)-m_{t}}\,,\qquad\mathcal{E}:=\sum_{k\geq 1}\,\mathcal{C}^{k}(\cdot-u^{k})\,,

(1.14)

in which
u^{1}>u^{2}>\dots
enumerate the atoms of
\mathcal{E}^{*}
and
(\mathcal{C}^{k})_{k\geq 1}
are i.i.d. chosen according to
\nu
and independent of
\mathcal{E}^{*}
.

Since, conditional on
Z
, the intensity measure of
\mathcal{E}^{*}
is finite on
[-v,\infty)
for every
v\in\mathbb{R}
and tends to
\infty
as
v\to\infty
, it is a standard fact that
\mathbb{P}(-|Z)
-a.s. and therefore also
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\frac{\mathcal{E}^{*}([-v,\infty))}{\mathbb{E}\big(\mathcal{E}^{*}([-v,\infty))\,\big|\,Z\big)}=\frac{\mathcal{E}^{*}([-v,\infty))}{\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}Z{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}v}}\underset{v\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1\,.

(1.15)

However, the asymptotics for
\mathcal{E}([-v,\infty))
, arguably a more interesting quantity, are not a straightforward consequence of (
1.12
) and (
1.14
). This is because the limiting process
\mathcal{E}
is now a superposition of i.i.d. clusters
\mathcal{C}
, and the law
\nu
of the latter will determine the number of points inside any given set in the overall process. To address this question, a study of the moments of the level sets of a cluster
\mathcal{C}\sim\nu
was carried out in
[
8
]
. In particular, it was shown that
as
v\to\infty
,

\mathbb{E}\mathcal{C}([-v,0])=C_{\star}{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}v}(1+o(1))\,.

(1.16)

for some
C_{\star}>0
. This was then combined with (
1.12
) and (
1.14
) to derive

\frac{\mathcal{E}([-v,\infty))}{C_{\star}Zv{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}v}}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\underset{v\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}}1\,.

(1.17)

Notice that compared to the asymptotics for the level sets of
\mathcal{E}^{*}
, there is an additional linear prefactor (in
v
) in that of
\mathcal{E}
, which is due to the contribution of the clusters.

Sharper asymptotics for
\mathcal{E}([-v,\infty))
were later derived (among other results) in
[
19
]
using PDE techniques. Theorem 1.4 in that work, applied to the function
\phi_{0}=1_{(-\infty,0]}
gives (with constants adjusted),

\frac{1}{{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}u}}\Big[\mathcal{E}\big([-u,\infty)\big)-C_{\star}Z{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}u}\big(u+\sqrt{2}\log u\big)\Big]+CZ\underset{u\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\tfrac{C_{\star}}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{R}_{Z}\,,

(1.18)

where
(\mathcal{R}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a spectrally positive stable-
1
process, with Laplace Transform

{\rm E}{\rm e}^{-\lambda\mathcal{R}_{t}}=\exp{\big(t\lambda\log\lambda\big)}\,,

(1.19)

which is independent of
Z
, and
C\in\mathbb{R}
is some explicit constant.
The analogous result for the
unshifted
process
\mathcal{E}_{\circ}
, defined as
\mathcal{E}
in (
1.14
) only with

u^{1}>u^{2}>\dots
now enumerating the atoms of

\mathcal{E}^{*}_{\circ}\sim{\rm PPP}({\rm e}^{-\sqrt{2}u}{\rm d}u)\,,

(1.20)

(without the Z prefactor) gives

\frac{1}{{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}u}}\Big[\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\big([-u,\infty)\big)-C_{\star}{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}u}\big(u+\sqrt{2}\log u\big)\Big]+C\underset{u\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\tfrac{C_{\star}}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{R}_{1}\,.

(1.21)

We note that
[
19
]
also shows that
C_{\star}C_{\diamond}=1/\sqrt{\pi}
, so that remarkably one can obtain explicit constants in (
1.18
) and (
1.21
).

1.2
Contribution

As pointed out in
[
19
]
(see discussion below Theorem 1.4 in that work), the purely analytic methods used to derive (
1.18
) and (
1.21
) provide no explanation for the emergence of the
1
-stable law governing the fluctuations in the limit, nor for the logarithmic order of the second order term. We aim to elucidate the reasons for these two results, by an alternative, probabilistic derivation for the asymptotic growth of
\mathcal{E}
.

Toward this end, following
[
8
]
, we define a
restricted
version of the limiting extremal process
\mathcal{E}
from (
1.13
) and its unshifted analog
\mathcal{E}_{\circ}
just defined. Fixing a subset
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and letting
(u^{k})_{k\geq 1}
,
(\mathcal{C}^{k})_{k\geq 1}
be as in (
1.14
), we set

\mathcal{E}(\;\cdot\;;B):=\sum_{k}\mathcal{C}^{k}(\cdot-u^{k})1_{B}(u^{k})

(1.22)

This process is the restriction of
\mathcal{E}
where only contributions form clusters whose maximum is in
B
are included. The restricted version in the unshifted case is defined in the same way, only with
(u^{k})_{k\geq 1}
now enumerating the atoms of
\mathcal{E}^{*}_{\circ}
.

Our main result is

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
x^{+},x^{-}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be functions which tend sub-linearly but arbitrarily slow to
\pm\infty
respectively and abbreviate
u_{*}\equiv-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log u
. Then

\frac{1}{e^{\sqrt{2}u}}\bigg[\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\Big([-u,\infty)\;;\;u_{*}+[x^{-}_{u},\,x^{+}_{u}]\Big)-\mathbb{E}\,\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\Big([-u,\infty)\;;\;u_{*}+[x^{-}_{u},\,0]\Big)\bigg]-C_{\circ}\underset{u\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\tfrac{C_{\star}}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{R}_{1}\,,

(1.23)

and

\frac{1}{e^{\sqrt{2}u}}\bigg[\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\Big([-u,\infty)\;;\;u_{*}+[x^{-}_{u},\,x^{+}_{u}]^{\rm c}\Big)-\mathbb{E}\,\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\Big([-u,\infty)\;;\;u_{*}+(-\infty,\,x^{-}_{u})\Big)\bigg]\underset{u\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\longrightarrow}}0\,,

(1.24)

where
C_{\star}
is as in (
1.16
) and
C_{\circ}\in\mathbb{R}
is a universal constant.

Combining them together, we immediately get

Corollary 1.2
.

With
\mathcal{R}_{1}
as in (
1.19
) and
C_{\circ}\in\mathbb{R}
is as in Theorem
1.1
.

\frac{1}{e^{\sqrt{2}u}}\bigg[\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\big([-u,\infty)\big)-\mathbb{E}\,\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\Big([-u,\infty)\;;\;\big[-u,-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log u\big)\Big)\bigg]-C_{\circ}\underset{u\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\tfrac{C_{\star}}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{R}_{1}\,,

(1.25)

A version of this corollary for the original process
\mathcal{E}
is

Corollary 1.3
.

With
\mathcal{R}_{t}
as in (
1.19
) and
C_{\circ}
as in Theorem
1.1
,

\frac{1}{e^{\sqrt{2}u}}\bigg[\mathcal{E}\big([-u,\infty)\big)-\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathcal{E}\Big([-u,\infty)\;;\;\big[-u,-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log u\big)\Big)\,\Big|\,Z\Big]\bigg]-C_{\circ}Z\underset{u\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\tfrac{C_{\star}}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{R}_{Z}\,.

(1.26)

Corollary
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
are the analogues of (
1.21
) and (
1.18
). The centering functions in the scaling limit are now meaningful, but not explicit as in
[
19
]
. On the other hand, by comparing e.g. (
1.25
) with (
1.21
), we obtain that for some
C\in\mathbb{R}
,

\frac{1}{C_{\star}{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}u}}\mathbb{E}\,\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\Big([-u,\infty)\;;\;\big[-u,-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log u\big)\Big)=u+\sqrt{2}\log u+C+o(1)\,,

(1.27)

with
o(1)\to 0
as
u\to\infty
.

Remark 1.4
.

Unfortunately, available estimates on
\mathbb{E}\,\mathcal{C}([-v,0])
, namely (
1.16
), can only be used to show that the means in (
1.25
) and (
1.26
) are
C_{\star}u{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}u}(1+o(1))
and
C_{\star}Zu{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}u}(1+o(1))
, respectively, which give coarser results compared to those in
[
19
]
. Obtaining better estimates via the probabilistic approach would require better control over various sources of errors in the analysis of
\mathbb{E}\,\mathcal{C}([-v,0])
and falls outside the scope of this work.

Going back to Theorem
1.1
, statements (
1.23
) and (
1.24
) together show that the random fluctuations in
\mathcal{E}_{\circ}([-u,\infty))
come from the contribution of those clusters whose maximum is within
\Theta(1)
around
u_{*}:=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log u
. Let us now explain how the 1-stable law emerges from these fluctuations, as can be seen from the proof of this theorem.

1.2.1
Heuristic for 1-stable fluctuations in
\mathcal{E}_{\circ}([-u,\infty))

At
u_{x}:=u_{*}+x
the intensity of
\mathcal{E}_{\circ}^{\star}
is
u{\rm e}^{-\sqrt{2}x}
, so that the recentered version of
\mathcal{E}_{\circ}^{*}
has law

\mathcal{E}^{*}_{u}:=\mathcal{E}^{*}_{\circ}(\cdot-u_{*})\sim{\rm PPP}\big(u{\rm e}^{-\sqrt{2}x}{\rm d}x\big)\,.

(1.28)

At the same time, clusters whose maximum is at height
u_{x}
contribute a quantity according to the law of
\mathcal{C}([-v_{x},0])
to
\mathcal{E}_{\circ}([-u,\infty))
, where
v_{x}:=u+u_{x}=u-(1/\sqrt{2})\log u+x
.

Now, while the mean of
\mathcal{C}([-v,0])
is
(C_{\star}+o(1)){\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}v}
as stated in (
1.16
), it was shown in
[
9
, Proposition 1.1]
that this mean is the result of a
\Theta(v^{-1})
-probability event under which
\mathcal{C}([-v,0]
is atypically,
\Theta(v{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}v})
-large. A more precise version of this statement is given in Theorem
3.1
of the present work
and constitutes the main effort in the proof. The theorem shows that there exists
an infinite measure
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, which is finite away from zero, such that

v\mathbb{P}\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{C}([-v,0])}{v{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}v}}\in\cdot\bigg)\underset{v\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\Gamma\,,

(1.29)

in a suitable sense. We call
\Gamma
the
intensity measure of unusually large clusters
s.

For simplicity of presentation, let us first pretend that
\Gamma
is a finite measure, so that it can be normalized into a probability measure
\widehat{\Gamma}
.
Then, specifying (
1.29
) to
v=v_{x}
, we see that a cluster at
u_{x}

contributes
v_{x}{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}v_{x}}\chi={\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}u}{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}x}\chi(1+o(1))
, “with probability”
|\Gamma|v_{x}^{-1}=|\Gamma|u^{-1}(1+o(1))
,
where
\chi
is chosen according to
\widehat{\Gamma}
.
Combined with (
1.28
) we thus have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{e^{\sqrt{2}u}}\bigg[\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\Big([-u,\infty)\;;\;u_{*}+[x^{-}_{u},\,x^{+}_{u}]\Big)\bigg]&=(1+o(1))\int_{x_{u}^{-}}^{x_{u}^{+}}{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}x}\,\frac{\mathcal{C}_{x}([-v_{x},\,0])}{v_{x}{\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}v_{x}}}\,\mathcal{E}_{\circ}^{*}\big(u_{*}+{\rm d}x\big)\\
&\cong C\int_{t_{u}^{-}}^{t_{u}^{+}}t\,\chi_{t}\mathcal{N}({\rm d}t)\,,\end{split}

(1.30)

where
\mathcal{N}\sim{\rm PPP}\big((1/\sqrt{2})t^{-2}{\rm d}t\big)
,

t^{\pm}_{u}={\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}x_{u}^{\pm}}
,
(\chi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
are i.i.d. “copies” of
\chi
, and we have employed the change of variables
t={\rm e}^{\sqrt{2}x}
.

As
t^{+}_{u}
and
t^{-}_{u}
tend to
\infty
and zero respectively as
u\to\infty
, a standard Laplace Transform computation shows that after subtracting a compensating term, for example,

\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{u}^{-}}^{0}t\,\chi_{t}\mathcal{N}({\rm d}t)\approx\frac{1}{e^{\sqrt{2}u}}\mathbb{E}\,\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\Big([-u,\infty)\;;\;u_{*}+[x^{-}_{u},\,0]\Big)\,,

(1.31)

the last integral in (
1.30
) tends weakly to a spectrally positive stable-
1
random variable.
While
\Gamma
is, in reality, infinite, we show that it obeys
\Gamma((y,\infty))\lesssim y^{-2}\wedge(\log y^{-1})^{5/2}
, which provides sufficient control on its growth and decay near zero and infinity, respectively. This allows for a similar derivation as in the finite case to go through.

Lastly, we draw attention to the fact that the mean of
\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\big([-u,\infty)\;;\;u_{*}+(0,x_{u}^{+}]\big)
is absent from the centering function in (
1.23
). While one can replace
0
with any other constant in (
1.23
) (and then adjust
C_{\circ}
accordingly), replacing
0
by
x_{u}^{+}
(or any other function
x_{u}^{+\prime}
that grows with
u
) will necessitate a growing function in
u
in place of the constant
C_{\circ}
. As in the case of (
1.31
), the mean
\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\big([-u,\infty)\;;\;u_{*}+(x_{u}^{+\prime},x_{u}^{+}]\big)
does not capture the typical value of this quantity, which tends to
0
in probability with
u
.

1.2.2
Other log-correlated fields and universality

Let us briefly remark that another advantage of the probabilistic-constructive argument for Theorem
1.1
is that, unlike the PDE approach, it applies to other log-correlated fields. Indeed, many such models are shown or conjectured to exhibit similar extreme order statistics. In particular, they should all have the same form for the limiting extremal process and a similar description for the cluster law. We thus expect that an analog of Theorem
1.1
will hold for such models as well, i.e. that the Stable-
1
fluctuations are also a universal feature of the extreme values of log-correlated fields.

This “prediction” should be rather easy to verify in the cases of the discrete Gaussian free field (DGFF) on planar domains and the branching random walk (BRW) where the theory of extreme order statistics is fully developed (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
for the DGFF case and
[
20
]
for the BRW case) and the technology is sufficiently advanced (e.g., the concentric decomposition from
[
4
]
and the Ballot Theorems in
[
12
]
, for the case of the DGFF).

2
Preliminaries

In this section we include preliminary statements and notation which will be used throughout this manuscript.
All of the results here either appear in the literature, or require proofs which are fairly standard by now. References are provided for the first kind of results. Proofs for results of the second kind, unless very short, are relegated to the appendix, so that attention can be given to the main and novel parts of the arguments in this work.

2.1
Additional notation

If
f
is a function on some space
\mathcal{X}
, we shall write
f_{A}
for its restriction to
A\subset\mathcal{X}
. If
\mu
is a measure on
(\mathcal{X},\Sigma)
and

is non-negative and
\Sigma

\mu({\rm d}x)f(x)
to denote a measure on
(\mathcal{X},\Sigma)

. If

, which we always equip with the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets.

Throughout this manuscript,

\mathbb{P}(-|Z_{0}=x)
to denote the case when

. When there is no ambiguity concerning the underlying process, we shall abbreviate:

\mathbb{P}(-)\equiv\mathbb{P}(-\,|\,Z_{0}=0\big)\ ,\quad\mathbb{P}_{r,x}(-)\equiv\mathbb{P}(-\,|\,Z_{r}=x\big)\ ,\quad\mathbb{P}_{r,x}^{s,y}(-)\equiv\mathbb{P}(-\,|\,Z_{r}=x,Z_{s}=y)\,.

(2.1)

Finally, we shall write

and

and

,

, etc. denote positive and finite constants whose value may change from one use to another.

2.2
Weak representation of the cluster law

In this subsection we include a subset of the theory that was developed in
[
8
]
in order to get a handle on the law of the clusters. The analysis of the cluster distribution is done in three steps, which correspond to the next three sub-sections. In the first step one expresses the law of the clusters as the weak limit as
t\to\infty
of the relative heights of the genealogical neighbors of a distinguished particle, called the “spine”, which is conditioned to be the global maximum and at a prescribed height. Here one appeals to the so-called spine decomposition method (or spine method, for short).

The second step is to recast events involving the spine being a global maximum, as events involving a certain “decorated random-walk-like process” which is required to stay below a given curve. We refer to such events as “Ballot” events, on account of the analogy with a simple random walk which is restricted to be positive. In the third step one estimates the probability of such Ballot events. This is achieved by appealing to Ballot estimates for standard Brownian motion.

2.2.1
Spinal decomposition

The
1
-spine branching Brownian motion
(henceforth sBBM) evolves like BBM, except that at any given time, one of the living particles is designated as the “spine”. What distinguishes this particle from the others, is that the spine branches at Poissonian rate
2
and not
1
as the remaining living particles. There is no difference in the motion, which is still that of a BM. When the spine branches, one of its two children is chosen uniformly at random to become the new spine.

We shall keep the same notation
(h_{t},T_{t},{\rm d}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
for the genealogical
and positional processes associated with this model. The additional information, namely the identity of the spine at any given time, will be represented by the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, where
X_{t}\in L_{t}
for all
t\geq 0
. We shall also use the same notation as in the case of BBM to denote objects associated with the motion
h
, e.g.
\widehat{h}_{t}
will still denote
h_{t}-m_{t}
. To make the distinction from regular BBM explicit, we will denote the underlying probability measure by
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
(and
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
), in accordance with the notation in
[
8
]
.

A useful representation for this process can be obtained by “taking the point of view of the spine”. More precisely, one can think of sBBM as a process driven by a backbone Brownian motion:
(h_{t}(X_{t}):\>t\geq 0)
, representing the trajectory of the spine, from which (non-spine) particles branch out at Poissonian rate
2
and then evolve independently according to the regular BBM law.

The connection between sBBM and regular BBM is given by the following “Many-To-One” lemma. This lemma is the “workhorse” of the spine method, being a useful tool in moment computations for the number of particles in a branching process satisfying a prescribed condition. Recall that for
t\geq 0
, we let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
denote the sigma-algebra generated by
(h_{s},T_{s},{\rm d}_{s})_{s\leq t}
.

Lemma 2.1

(Many-To-One)
.

Let
F=(F(x):\>x\in L_{t})
be a bounded
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable real-valued random function on
L_{t}
. Then,

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{x\in L_{t}}F(x)\Big)={\rm e}^{t}\,\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}F(X_{t})\,.

(2.2)

A direct application of the above lemma, together with the statistical structure of extreme values in the limit, gives the following expression for the law of the clusters.

Lemma 2.2

(Lemma 5.1 in
[
8
]
)
.

For all
r>0
, there exists a probability measure
\nu_{r}
on the space
\mathbb{M}_{p}((-\infty,0])
such that for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\big(\mathcal{C}_{t,r}(X_{t})\in\cdot\,\big|\,\widehat{h}_{t}(X_{t})=\widehat{h}_{t}^{*}=u\big)\underset{t\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\nu_{r}

(2.3)

and

\nu_{r}\underset{r\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\nu\,,

(2.4)

where
\mathcal{C}_{t,r}
is as in (
1.6
) and
\nu
is as in (
1.9
).
Both limits hold in the sense of weak convergence on
\mathbb{M}((-\infty,0])
equipped with the vague topology. Lastly (
2.3
) still holds with
\nu_{r}
replaced by
\nu
, if
r
is taken to
\infty
together with
t
as long as
t-r\to\infty
as well.

We note that, in the proof in
[
8
]
, the limits in
r
and
t
were taken at the same time, but the proof carries over to case where the limits are taken sequentially (in fact, the proof in
[
8
]
relies on Theorem 2.3 in
[
1
]
, in which the limit is taken sequentially).

2.2.2
Decorated random walk representation

Recall that
W=(W_{s}:\>s\geq 0)
denotes a standard Brownian motion. For
0\leq s\leq t
, set

\gamma_{t,s}:=\tfrac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\big(\log^{+}s-\tfrac{s}{t}\log^{+}t\big)

(2.5)

and let

\widehat{W}_{t,s}:=W_{s}-\gamma_{t,s}\,.

(2.6)

We also let
H:=\big(h^{s}:=(h^{s}_{t})_{t\geq 0}:\>s\geq 0\big)
be a collection of independent copies of a regular BBM process
h
, that we will assume to be independent of
W
as well. Finally, let
\mathcal{N}
be a Poisson point process with intensity
2{\rm d}x
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, independent of
H
and
W
and denote by
\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{2}<\dots
its ordered atoms. The triplet

(\widehat{W},\mathcal{N},H)

(2.7)

will be referred to as a
decorated random-walk-like process
(DRW).

In what follows, we shall add a superscript
s
to any notation used for objects which were previously defined in terms of
h
, to denote objects which are defined in the same way, but with
h^{s}
in place of
h
. In this way,
\widehat{h}^{s}_{t}
,
\widehat{h}^{s*}_{t}
and
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{s}
are defined as in the introduction only with respect to
h^{s}
in place of
h
.
Using this notation, for
0\leq r<R\leq t
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
we define

\mathcal{A}_{t}(u;r,R):=\Big\{\max\limits_{k:\,\sigma_{k}\in[r,R]}\big(\widehat{W}_{t,{\sigma_{k}}}+\widehat{h}^{\sigma_{k}*}_{\sigma_{k}}\big)\leq u\Big\}\,,

(2.8)

with the abbreviations

\mathcal{A}_{t}(r,R):=\mathcal{A}_{t}(0;r,R)\quad,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{t}\equiv\mathcal{A}_{t}(0,t)\,.

(2.9)

This is the event that the Brownian motion
W
plus a certain functional of its decorations
H
, stay below the curve
\gamma_{t,\cdot}
at random times which correspond to the atoms of
\mathcal{N}
. We shall refer to such event as a DRW
Ballot
event.

The connection between probabilities of the form (
2.3
) and DRW Ballot probabilities are given by the following two lemmas which were proved in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2.3

(Lemma 3.1 in
[
8
]
)
.

For all
0\leq r\leq t
and
u,w\in\mathbb{R}
,

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\widehat{h}_{t}^{*}\big({\rm B}^{\rm c}_{r}(X_{t})\big)\leq u\,\Big|\,\widehat{h}_{t}(X_{t})=w\Big)=\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{A}_{t}(r,t)\,\big|\,\widehat{W}_{t,r}=w-u,\,\widehat{W}_{t,t}=-u\big)\,.

(2.10)

In particular for all
t\geq 0
and
v,w\in\mathbb{R}
,

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\big(\widehat{h}^{*}_{t}\leq u\,\big|\,\widehat{h}_{t}(X_{t})=w\big)=\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{A}_{t}\,\big|\,\widehat{W}_{t,0}=w-u,\,\widehat{W}_{t,t}=-u\big)\,.

(2.11)

Lemma 2.4

(Lemma 3.2 in
[
8
]
)
.

For all
0\leq r\leq t
,

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\mathcal{C}_{t,r}(X_{t})\in\cdot\,\Big|\,\widehat{h}^{*}_{t}=\widehat{h}_{t}(X_{t})=0\Big)=\mathbb{P}\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}\!\mathcal{E}_{s}^{s}\big(\cdot-\widehat{W}_{t,s}\big)\mathcal{N}({\rm d}s)\in\,\cdot\ \Big|\ \widehat{W}_{t,0}\!=\!\widehat{W}_{t,t}\!=\!0\,;\,\mathcal{A}_{t}\,\bigg).

(2.12)

The following lemma will be useful when estimating increments of
\widehat{W}
.

Lemma 2.5

(Lemma 3.3 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
s,r,t\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
0\leq r\leq r+s\leq t
, then

-1\,\leq\log^{+}(r+s)-\left(\tfrac{t-(r+s)}{t-r}\log^{+}r+\tfrac{s}{t-r}\log^{+}t\right)\leq\,1+\log^{+}\big(s\wedge(t-r-s)\big).

(2.13)

2.2.3
Ballot estimates for DRW

In this subsection we include estimates for Ballot probabilities involving the DRW from the previous sub-section. They are either derived from
[
7

in which more general DRW processes are treated, or taken directly from
[
8
]
, in which the results from
[
7
]
were specialized to the DRW from Subsection

The first lemma provides bounds and asymptotics for the DRW Ballot event.

Lemma 2.6

(Essentially Lemma 3.4 in
[
8
]
)
.

There exists
C,C^{\prime}>0
such that for all
0\leq r<R\leq t
and
w,v\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{A}_{t}(r,R)\,\big|\,\widehat{W}_{t,r}=v\,,\,\,\widehat{W}_{t,R}=w\big)\leq C\frac{(v^{-}+1)(w^{-}+1)}{R-r}\,.

(2.14)

Also, there exists non-increasing functions
g:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
and
f^{(r)}:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
for
r\geq 0
, such that for all such
r

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{A}_{t}(r,R)\,\big|\,\widehat{W}_{t,r}=v\,,\,\,\widehat{W}_{t,R}=w\big)\sim 2\frac{f^{(r)}(v)g(w)}{R-r},

(2.15)

as
R\to\infty
uniformly in
t\geq R
and
v,w
satisfying
v,w<1/\epsilon
and
(v^{-}+1)(w^{-}+1)\leq R^{1-\epsilon}
for any fixed
\epsilon>0
. Moreover,

\lim_{v\to\infty}\frac{f^{(r)}(-v)}{v}=\lim_{w\to\infty}\frac{g(-w)}{w}=1\,,

(2.16)

for any
r\geq 0
. Finally there exists
f:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
such that for all
v\in\mathbb{R}
,

f^{(r)}(v)\overset{r\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}f(v)\,.

(2.17)

We shall also need the following version of the previous lemma, where the ballot barrier is at different heights in both “edges” of the time interval.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
r:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
r_{s}=o(s),r_{s}\to\infty
as
s\to\infty
and
r_{s},s-r_{s}
are both uniquely determined by
s
. Then,

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{A}_{t}(w+z;r,s)\cap\mathcal{A}_{t}(0,r)\,\big|\,\widehat{W}_{t,0}=0\,,\,\,\widehat{W}_{t,s}=w\big)\sim\frac{2f^{(0)}(0)g(-z)}{s},

(2.18)

as
s\to\infty
, uniformly for
t>s
and
w,z
in compact sets. Also,

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{A}_{t}(w+z;s,t/2)\cap\mathcal{A}_{t}(t/2,t)\,\big|\,\widehat{W}_{t,s}=w\,,\,\,\widehat{W}_{t,t}=0\big)\sim\frac{2f(-z)g(0)}{t},

(2.19)

as
t\to\infty
followed by
s\to\infty
, uniformly for
w,z
in compact sets.

The next lemma shows that under the Ballot event, the process
\widehat{W}_{t,\cdot}
is (entropically) repelled away from
0
.

Lemma 2.8
.

For all
M>0
,
2\leq s\leq t/2
,

\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{u\in[s,\,t/2]}\widehat{W}_{t,u}>-M\,\Big|\,\widehat{W}_{t,0}=0\,,\,\,\widehat{W}_{t,t}=0\,,\,\,\mathcal{A}_{t}\Big)\leq C\frac{(M+1)^{2}}{\sqrt{s}}\,.

(2.20)

The last lemma shows that one can replace the restriction on
\widehat{W}_{t,u}
for
u\in[s,t-s]
under
\mathcal{A}_{t}
with the condition that
W_{u}
(without the additional
\gamma_{t,s}
) stays negative within this interval, with the difference between the probabilities of the two events being of smaller order.

Lemma 2.9
.

For
0<r<R<t
, denote the event

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(r,R):=\{\mathcal{A}_{t}(0,r)\cap\mathcal{A}_{t}(R,t)\cap\{\max W_{[r,R]}\leq 0\}\}.

(2.21)

There exists
C>0
such that for all
2<s<t/2
,

\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathcal{A}_{t}\,\Delta\,\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(s,t-s)\,\Big|\,\widehat{W}_{t,0}=0\,,\,\,\widehat{W}_{t,t}=0\Big)\leq Cs^{-1/4}t^{-1}\,.

(2.22)

Joint convergence of critical extreme level-sets and maximum

Lastly, we shall also need the following proposition, which gives the joint limit in law of the centered maximum and the scaled size of the critical extreme (super) level sets. The latter are defined as the set of particles at time

Proposition 2.10
.

There exists
c_{0}\in(0,\infty)

\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{t}\big([-v,\infty)\big)}{ve^{\sqrt{2}v-\frac{v^{2}}{2t}}},\,\widehat{h}^{*}_{t}\right)\underset{t\to\infty}{\Longrightarrow}\big(c_{0}Z,\,\widehat{h}_{\infty}^{*}\big)\quad;\qquad G+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log Z\,,

3
Stable limit of compensated process

Letting
x^{\pm}
be as in the theorem, the target quantity we wish to study is

\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{u}:=e^{-\sqrt{2}u}\mathcal{E}_{\circ}\left([-u,\infty);-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log u+[x^{-}_{u},x^{+}_{u}]\right)\,.

(3.1)

Denoting

Y_{u}(x):=e^{-\sqrt{2}(u+x)}\mathcal{C}^{(x)}([-w_{u}(x),0])\quad,\qquad w_{u}(x):=u-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log u+x\,,

(3.2)

we may write
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{u}
as

\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{u}=\int_{x_{u}^{-}}^{x^{+}_{u}}e^{-\sqrt{2}u}\mathcal{C}^{(x)}([-w_{u}(x),0])\,{\rm d}\mathcal{E}_{\circ}^{*}\big(x-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log u\big)=\int_{x_{u}^{-}}^{x^{+}_{u}}e^{\sqrt{2}x}\,Y_{u}(x)\,\mathcal{E}^{*}_{u}(dx)\,,

(3.3)

where

\mathcal{E}^{*}_{u}(\cdot):=\mathcal{E}_{\circ}^{*}(\cdot-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log u)
is a PPP on
\mathbb{R}
with intensity
u{\rm e}^{-\sqrt{2}x}{\rm d}x
.

Using the above representation, the Laplace Transform of

\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{u}
is
\mathbb{E}\exp(-\lambda\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{u})={\rm e}^{-\widetilde{\phi}_{u}(\lambda)}
, where

\begin{split}\widetilde{\phi}_{u}(\lambda)&:=\int_{x^{-}_{u}}^{x^{+}_{u}}ue^{\sqrt{2}x}dx\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-\lambda e^{\sqrt{2}x}y}\right)\mathbb{P}(Y_{u}(x)\in dy)\\
&=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{t^{-}_{u}}^{t^{+}_{u}}t^{-2}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-\lambda ty}\right)u\mathbb{P}(Y_{u}(x(t))\in dy)\,,\end{split}

(3.4)

where we performed the change of variable
x(t)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\log t
and set

t^{\pm}_{u}:=e^{\sqrt{2}x^{\pm}_{u}}\,.

(3.5)

For the latter to converge to a finite limit, we must subtract a
deterministic
compensating term. To this end, we pick an arbitrary
\rho>0
and set

E_{u}(\rho):=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{t^{-}_{u}}^{t^{+}_{u}}t^{-2}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ty\mathbbm{1}_{ty\leq\rho}\ u\mathbb{P}(Y_{u}(x(t))\in dy)\,.

(3.6)

Then, the Laplace Transform of the
compensated
variable

\mathcal{R}_{u}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{u}-E_{u}(\rho)\,,

(3.7)

is
\mathbb{E}\exp\left(-\lambda\mathcal{R}_{u}\right)=\exp(-\phi_{u}(\lambda))
,
where

\phi_{u}(\lambda):=\widetilde{\phi}_{u}(\lambda)-\lambda E_{u}(\rho)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{t^{-}_{u}}^{t^{+}_{u}}t^{-2}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-\lambda ty}-\lambda ty\mathbbm{1}_{ty\leq\rho}\right)u\mathbb{P}(Y_{u}(x(t))\in dy).

(3.8)

The goal now is to show the limit of
\phi_{u}(\lambda)
as
u\to\infty
exists and has the appropriate form. For this purpose, we need to understand the limiting behavior of the (unbounded) measures
u\mathbb{P}(Y_{u}(x(t))\in dy)
on
(0,\infty)
, in the regime
t\in[t^{-}_{u},t^{+}_{u}]
. Equivalently, we may study the
w\to\infty
limit of

w\mathbb{P}(X(w)\in{\rm d}x)\,,

(3.9)

where

X(w):=\frac{\mathcal{C}([-w,0])}{we^{\sqrt{2}w}}\,.

(3.10)

Indeed, for any
y>0
, as
u\to\infty
,

Y_{u}(x)\overset{d}{=}X(w_{u}(x))(1+o(1))

(3.11)

and

u\mathbb{P}(Y_{u}(x(t))>y)=w\mathbb{P}\Big(X(w)>y(1+o(1))\Big)\,\big(1+o(1)\big)\,,

(3.12)

where
w=w_{u}(x(t))
with the

terms uniform in
t\in[t^{-}_{u},t^{+}_{u}]
and independent of

2.14
) in Lemma
2.6
, the integral over
y\geq 0
is only
O(r_{s}^{-1}s^{-1})
. From now on we restrict to
y<0
in the integral above. By direct computation, the law of
\widehat{W}_{t,r}
under
\mathbb{P}_{0,0}^{s,w}
is Gaussian with mean
O(\log r)
and variance
r(1-r/s)
. Applying the change of variable
y=\sqrt{r}x
, the density of the rescaled variable
x
is Gaussian with mean
o(1)
and variance
\sim 1
as
s\to\infty
(recall that
r_{s}=o(s)
). By the ballot estimates (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) in Lemma
2.6
, the first probability in (
A.14
) is

\mathbb{P}_{0,0}^{r,\sqrt{r}x}\left(\mathcal{A}_{t}(0,r)\right)\sim_{r}\frac{2f^{(0)}(0)g(\sqrt{r}x)}{r}\sim_{r}2f^{(0)}(0)\,\frac{x^{-}}{\sqrt{r}}

and is bounded by
C(x^{-}+1)/\sqrt{r}
. For the second probability in (
A.14
), (
2.14
) gives an upper bound
C(w,z)\sqrt{r}(x^{-}+1)/(s-r)
for some constant
C(w,z)
depending on
w,z
. To get an asymptotic for this probability, we follow the original proof of Lemma
2.6
in
[
8
, Lemma 3.4]
. By translating in time and space, we can rewrite this probability as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{r,\sqrt{r}x-w-z}^{s,-z}\left(\mathcal{A}_{t}(0;r,s)\right)

(A.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{\sigma_{k}\in[0,s-r]}\big(W_{\sigma_{k}}-\gamma^{(r)}_{s-r,\sigma_{k}}-Y^{(r)}_{\sigma_{k}}\big)\leq 0\,\Big|W_{0}=\sqrt{r}x-w-z,W_{s-r}=-z\Big)

(A.16)

where
\gamma^{(r)}_{s-r,\sigma}:=\log^{+}(r+\sigma)-\Big(\frac{s-r-\sigma}{s-r}\log^{+}r+\frac{\sigma}{s-r}\log^{+}s\Big)
and
Y^{(r)}_{\sigma}:=-\widehat{h}^{(r+\sigma)*}_{r+\sigma}
. Keeping in mind that
r=r_{s}
and
s-r=s-r_{s}
are uniquely determined by
s
, a slight adaptation of results of
[
7
]
(allowing decorations
Y
to also depend on the parameter
t
there like in the curve
\gamma_{t,\cdot}
) then gives the asymptotic

\frac{2f(\sqrt{r}x-w-z)g(-z)}{s-r}\sim_{s}2g(-z)\frac{\sqrt{r}x^{-}}{s}

for the probability (
A.16
) as
s\to\infty
. Altogether, we have by Dominated Convergence the integral (
A.14
) is asymptotic to

\frac{2f^{(0)}(0)\cdot 2g(-z)}{s}\int_{x<0}(x^{-})^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx=\frac{2f^{(0)}(0)g(-z)}{s}

(A.17)

as
s\to\infty
.

The computation of the estimate (
2.19
) is largely similar. Conditioning on the time
t/2
, this probability equals

\int\mathbb{P}_{s,w}^{t/2,y}\left(\mathcal{A}_{t}(w+z;s,t/2)\right)\mathbb{P}_{t/2,y}^{t,0}\left(\mathcal{A}_{t}(t/2,t)\right)\mathbb{P}_{s,w}^{t,0}\left(\widehat{W}_{t,t/2}\in{\rm d}y\right)

(A.18)

Like before, we may restrict the integral to
y<0
. This time, we apply the change of variable
y=\sqrt{t}x/2
, so that the density of the rescaled variable
x
is Gaussian with mean
o(1)
and variance
\sim 1
as
t\to\infty
for fixed
s
. For fixed
s
, applying the ballot estimates in Lemma
2.6
(after translating in space), the first probability in (
A.18
) is

\mathbb{P}_{s,-z}^{t/2,\sqrt{t}x/2-w-z}\left(\mathcal{A}_{t}(0;s,t/2)\right)\sim_{t}\frac{2f^{(s)}(-z)g(\sqrt{t}x/2-w-z)}{t/2}\sim_{t}2f^{(s)}(-z)\frac{x^{-}}{\sqrt{t}}

and is bounded by
C(w,z)(x^{-}+1)/\sqrt{t}
for some constant
C(w,z)
depending on
w,z
. For the second probability in (
A.18
), (
2.14
) gives an upper bound
C(x^{-}+1)/\sqrt{t}
and a similar calculation as in (
A.16
) gives the asymptotic

\mathbb{P}_{t/2,\sqrt{t}x/2}^{t,0}\left(\mathcal{A}_{t}(t/2,t)\right)\sim_{t}\frac{2f(\sqrt{t}x/2)g(0)}{t/2}\sim_{t}2g(0)\frac{x^{-}}{\sqrt{t}}.

Altogether, we have by Dominated Convergence that the integral (
A.18
) is asymptotic to

\frac{2f^{(s)}(-z)\cdot 2g(0)}{t}\int_{x<0}(x^{-})^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx=\frac{2f^{(s)}(-z)g(0)}{t}

(A.19)

as
t\to\infty
for fixed

and apply the asymptotic (
2.17
) we establish (
2.19
).
∎

Graph attention-driven relation network for 3D lane detection
Authors: Yanji Jiang, Yingyang Zhang, Jiayu Bi, Hao Dong, Daqian Liu, Ke Gao, Bowen Fei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Lane detection is a crucial task in autonomous driving, but existing methods still struggle with occluded, missing, and complex road structures of lanes. To address these issues, we propose a graph attention-based 3D lane detection method—Graph-RMNet. This network enhances the model’s understanding of the three-dimensional distribution of lanes by parsing the topological structure and semantic category relationships between them. First, we introduce a 3D positional query generation strategy that combines 3D spatial information with 2D image features to initialize queries, thereby enhancing the queries’ perception of the three-dimensional distribution of lanes. Furthermore, we design a dual-path relation module that captures complex interactions through graph attention mechanisms and models lane relations from both spatial and categorical dimensions via a specially designed adaptive graph structure, improving the model’s comprehension and expression of complex inter-lane relations. Ultimately, the prediction module outputs the 3D coordinates and categories of the lanes to construct a complete distribution of the lanes in 3D space. The experimental results show that Graph-RMNet achieves F-Score of 63.2% on the OpenLane dataset and F-Score of 80.63% on the ONCE-3DLanes dataset, both outperforming current popular algorithms. Notably, it demonstrates significant robustness in scenarios where lanes are occluded or missing.

Introduction

Lane detection is a critical component of autonomous driving technology and plays an essential role in vehicle positioning, path planning, and safe navigation. This technology provides autonomous systems with key information, including the vehicle’s relative position on the road and its surrounding environment, enabling the vehicle to recognize lane positions and make informed decisions accordingly. Notably, 3D lane detection has garnered significant attention because of its ability to provide detailed road structure and traffic information. Its primary objective is to accurately recover the position of lanes in 3D space from the vehicle’s front-view images, which is crucial for environmental perception and decision-making. In contrast, while 2D lane detection can offer positional information of lanes within images, it fails to capture the variations in the actual road gradient, limiting its applicability in complex scenarios, especially when precise geometric information of the road is needed1.

In recent years, the field of 3D lane detection has seen undergone advancements. Early research relied primarily on convolutional neural network (CNN) to recover depth information from monocular images, facilitating the reconstruction of lanes from 2D to 3D. For example, 3D-LaneNet2 uses inverse perspective mapping (IPM)3 to transform image features into a bird’s-eye view and combines this with an anchor-based lane representation method to predict 3D lanes directly from monocular images. However, IPM often overlooks variations in road elevation, limiting its accuracy on uneven surfaces. To address this challenge, GenLaneNet4 introduces an innovative geometrically guided anchor representation for lanes and applies specific geometric transformations to the output to calculate true 3D lane points. LaneCPP5 employs physical prior knowledge to design a flexible lane representation that can adapt to lanes with complex shapes. Anchor3DLane6, on the other hand, defines anchors in 3D space, directly regressing 3D lanes from the front-view image, thus circumventing some of the limitations of IPM.

CNN-based approaches face limitations in addressing scale variations and camera parameter noise because of their simplistic spatial transformation designs. To overcome these challenges, researchers have developed Transformer-based 3D lane detection algorithms. These algorithms leverage the powerful self-attention7 mechanism to effectively understand and model the structure of lanes in complex road scenarios. For example, PersFormer8 utilizes camera parameters and self-attention to generate refined bird’s eye view (BEV) feature representations, employing a unified 2D/3D anchor design for simultaneous detection of 2D and 3D lanes. LATR9 employs a cross-attention mechanism based on queries and key-value pairs to directly detect 3D lanes from monocular images without the need for conversion to other 3D proxy views. CurveFormer10 models the 3D lane detection problem as a curve propagation issue, using a dynamic set of anchors as queries and iteratively refining these queries in the decoder to directly compute 3D lane parameters. Although Transformer-based methods can capture global features and long-range dependencies, which are crucial for understanding and modelling complex lane structures, they may still rely excessively on specific image features. In real-world road environments, lanes are susceptible to environmental factors such as lighting changes and water reflection, potentially causing occlusions or missing segments in the image, thus increasing the difficulty of complete lane detection.

In traffic environments, lanes adhere to specific geometric and topological rules. These rules enable the inference of complete lane configurations even when portions are missing, thereby revealing the intrinsic relations between lanes. Existing algorithms often rely on self-attention mechanisms to establish connections of intra-lane and inter-lane. However, owing to the extensive application of these mechanisms, they do not fully explore the relations between queries. To address this, we propose the Graph-Attention Relation Modelling Network (Graph-RMNet) for 3D lane detection. This network is designed to more efficiently utilize relational information between queries. We create different graph network structures to model the spatial and categorical relations between lane instances. Using graph attention mechanisms, we promote complex interactions between nodes to learn the intrinsic rules governing lanes, which enhances the model’s adaptability to complex scenarios. Additionally, we develop an adaptive graph structure construction method that selectively focuses on node relations with significant performance impacts, thereby reducing unnecessary interactions. Concurrently, we introduce a 3D positional query generation strategy that combines 2D features with 3D positional encoding. This approach not only enriches the queries with detailed 2D lane features but also equips them with an awareness of 3D spatial positions.Although our approach also employs attention mechanisms to dynamically perceive the three-dimensional distribution of lanes, similar to other Transformer-based methods9,10, the proposed query initialization strategy and the introduction of graph networks endow the model with richer relational features, thereby enhancing its adaptability to various environments.

Related work

2D lane detection

The rise of deep learning has introduced a variety of technological pathways into the field of lane detection, including Segmentation-based, row-based, anchor-based, and keypoint-based methods. Segmentation-based approaches11,12 treat lane detection as a pixel-level classification task, which, though accurate, are computationally expensive and slow. Row-based methods13 leverage global features to rapidly locate lanes, trading off some accuracy for increased speed. Anchor-based methods14,15 have utilized straight lines at image boundaries as anchor points, predicting anchor displacements through regression and filtering high-confidence results to enhance detection performance, but these methods are less effective for curved lanes. Keypoint-based methods such as PINet16 and GANet17 draw on human pose estimation techniques, incorporating keypoint estimation and clustering strategies, effectively addressing the issue of lane instances differentiation and adapting to various lane shapes, demonstrating superior performance. Despite the good results achieved in 2D lane detection, the lack of depth information makes it difficult to directly apply to downstream tasks requiring 3D spatial perception.

3D lane detection

3D lane detection algorithms are designed to directly identify lanes in real-world space from monocular images, thereby circumventing errors that arise during the 2D-to-3D conversion process. 3D-LaneNet2, as a pioneering effort in monocular 3D lane detection, introduces an internal feature map inverse perspective mapping and an anchor-based lane representation method, enabling the prediction of 3D lane information directly from monocular images. However, its reliance on specific anchor settings may limit its adaptability to complex scenarios. Building on this, 3D-LaneNet+18 improves by adopting an anchor-free approach, capable of detecting 3D lanes with arbitrary topological structures. GP19 proposes explicit supervision based on structural priors, facilitating the establishment of the correlations of intra-lane and cross-lane and promoting the reconstruction of 3D lanes from local to global perspectives. BEVLaneDet20 utilizes virtual cameras to ensure spatial consistency and employs a keypoint-based 3D lane representation to adapt to complex scenarios, yet the BEV representation still has its limitations. Anchor3DLane++21, on the other hand, predicts 3D lanes directly from front-view features, avoiding the limitations of BEV representations. Concurrently, CurveFormer++22 uses queries and attention mechanisms to infer 3D lane detection results directly from perspective map features, reducing performance losses associated with view transformations.GroupLane23 employs a row-based classification approach to achieve fast 3D lane detection.

To better address the complexities of real-world road conditions and environmental factors, some researchers have adopted fusion techniques that combine LiDAR with front-view images. M2-3DLaneNet24 uses specific modality backbone networks to extract multimodal features and integrates these features in a unified BEV space. DV-3DLane25 proposes an innovative dual-view representation and an efficient feature fusion strategy that effectively integrates two types of modal data to enhance detection performance. Multimodal fusion methods demonstrate good robustness in complex traffic environments but may reduce the real-time performance of algorithms because of higher computational resource requirements, which is not conducive to practical road applications of autonomous vehicles. Graph-RMNet, which uses only monocular images, leverages the interrelations among lanes to diminish the impact of front-view features on algorithm performance, thereby enhancing the algorithm’s adaptability in complex environments.

Graph neural network

Graph neural networks (GNNs)26 are a type of deep learning model capable of processing graph-structured data. They capture the topological structure and features of nodes and update node representations by aggregating information from neighboring nodes. In recent years, GNNs and their variants27,28 have been widely applied across various fields, such as natural language processing29, bioinformatics30, recommendation systems31, and urban planning32, demonstrating robust data processing and predictive capabilities. In the domain of object detection, GNNs are frequently used to enhance object representation by considering the spatial relations between objects to improve detection accuracy. For example, DCGNN33 uses GNNs to process point cloud data, constructing local and global graph structures to capture the relations between points and point sets. Graph-DETR3D34 leverages graph structures to dynamically aggregate features from multiple viewpoints, particularly enhancing detection performance in boundary areas, and employs a depth-invariant multiscale training strategy to increase the model’s robustness and precision. Building upon previous work, Graph-RMNet designs graph attention networks to construct spatial and categorical relations between lanes, improving the detection of partially missing lanes. Unlike LATR, Graph-RMNet focuses on sparsely constructing relations between queries, which is more conducive to preserving beneficial correlations and enhancing the model’s generalization capabilities.

Monocular 3D object detection

Monocular 3D object detection involves recovering the 3D spatial position of objects from a single RGB image, posing a depth estimation challenge akin to that in 3D lane detection. STMono3D35 introduces a geometric alignment multi-scale training strategy to maintain geometric consistency between 2D and 3D spaces, improving pixel depth estimation accuracy. MonoCT36 employs a generalized depth enhancement method to convert 2D detection results into accurate depth estimates. Bevformer37 incorporates temporal and spatial features for depth estimation, transforming image features into BEV features to detect objects in a bird’s-eye view. However, excessive information extraction can introduce noise, affecting 3D detection results. MonoLSS38 addresses this with a learnable sample selection method to choose samples beneficial for learning 3D attributes. MonoPair39 enhances occluded object detection by adding pairwise object constraints to perceive spatial relationships. Unlike these methods, Graph-RMNet focuses on compensating for missing depth information by introducing relational features between objects. It uses graph attention networks to selectively construct relationships among multiple lane points, enabling the model to flexibly capture relational features and perceive the overall layout of lanes.

To enable queries to dynamically extract and refine lane instance features from the global feature map, the decoding layer adopts a deformable cross-attention mechanism40. Unlike the attention mechanism in CASSPR41, which connects features at different granularities comprehensively, this mechanism focuses on selectively extracting features most relevant to each lane instance from the 2D feature map. This approach reduces interference from irrelevant features and decreases computational resource consumption. Furthermore, the dual-path relation module within the decoder uses graph attention mechanisms28 to capture spatial and categorical relations between lanes, improving the model’s comprehension and expression of complex lane interactions and reducing environmental interference. Unlike P-GAT42, which uses attention mechanisms to aggregate information within the neighborhood to update node representations, Graph-RMNet dynamically adjusts the connection weights between node pairs by calculating their correlations, thereby achieving weighted feature fusion. Ultimately, a series of linear layers convert the query vectors output by the decoder into precise detection outputs, yielding 3D spatial positions and specific categories of the lanes.

3D position query generation

Traditional query generation methods9,43,44 rely primarily on learnable fixed parameters or 2D image features, which perform well in 2D tasks. However, their detection accuracy is limited in 3D tasks because of the insufficient integration of 3D spatial features. As shown in Fig. 2, we propose a 3D positional query generation module. This module combines 3D positional prior information with masking operations, enhancing the query vectors’ perception of the 3D spatial position of lanes while effectively reducing environmental interference and strengthening the representation of features related to lanes.

Dual-path relation modelling

Previous studies37,47 have confirmed the superior performance of query embedding techniques in 3D object detection, which is primarily due to their ability to extract target features effectively from 2D images. However, when lane features are not prominent in the current view image, these methods that rely on lane visual information are performance-limited. To address this, we design a dual-path relation module based on graph attention mechanisms, as shown in Fig. 3. This module constructs graph attention networks on two parallel paths to establish spatial topological and semantic categorical relations between lanes and optimizes node connections through adaptive graph structure. This allows instance queries and point queries to propagate features on two paths, enabling corresponding graph nodes to gather more contextual information from neighboring nodes, thereby enhancing the model’s ability to detect lanes in complex environments.

Experiments

Datasets

Graph-RMNet was experimented on three popular public benchmark datasets: Apollo Synthetic4, OpenLane7, and ONCE-3DLanes49.

The Apollo Synthetic dataset is a synthetic dataset designed specifically for autonomous driving, comprising 273,000 high-fidelity images covering various scenarios such as highways, urban, residential, downtown, and indoor parking garage environments. These scenes are generated by the Unity 3D engine, simulating different times of day, weather conditions, traffic, and road surface conditions. The dataset is divided into balanced scenes, rarely observed, and visual variations subsets to meet diverse testing requirements.

The OpenLane dataset is a critical 3D lane dataset in the field of autonomous driving, constructed based on the Waymo Open Dataset50. It includes 1,150 road segments, with 798 for training, 202 for validation, and 150 for testing. This dataset provides 200 K frames of images at a frequency of 10 FPS, covering lane and CIPO annotations for 1,000 road segments. It includes images from diverse real-world environments, such as urban and highway settings, and under various weather conditions, including clear, cloudy, overcast, rainy, and foggy days.

The ONCE-3DLanes dataset aims to provide real-world data for 3D lane detection in the field of autonomous driving. It contains 211,000 front-view camera images along with corresponding LiDAR point clouds, covering various road conditions such as city centers, residential areas, and highways, and includes scenarios under different times and weather conditions. It offers accurate 3D lane annotations and, through direct association between point clouds and images, enables an automatic annotation process from 2D to 3D lanes, generating high-quality 3D lane data.

Evaluation metric

When evaluating the performance of the lane detection model, we adhere to the evaluation metric system proposed by Gen-LaneNet4. The F-score metric is first employed to assess the model’s performance in the geometric regression task of lane detection, which takes into account both the precision and recall of the model’s predictions. Second, to evaluate the accuracy of the model in lane classification tasks, we introduce an accuracy metric for lane matching. In the assessment of predicted lanes versus actual lanes, the Euclidean distance between the predicted points and ground truth points is calculated for quantitative analysis. Specifically, a predicted lane is considered to effectively match the actual lane when at least 75% of the point-to-point distances in the y-positions are below a preset error threshold of 1.5 m. Furthermore, to comprehensively assess the model’s detection performance over different ranges, the model’s detection error is measured along the vehicle’s heading direction within near-range (0–40 m) and far-range (40–100 m) intervals.

Implementation details

In this study, we adopt ResNet-5051 as the pretrained backbone network to process image data from different datasets. Specifically, the image resolution of the OpenLane dataset is 1920 × 1280, whereas the Apollo Synthetic and ONCE-3DLanes datasets have a resolutions of 1920 × 1080; all the images are uniformly resized to 960 × 720 to accommodate the algorithm input. In terms of the model architecture, six decoder layers are set, with the deformable attention mechanism configured with four heads and eight sample points, and the query embedding dimension is set to 256. Main results

Results on openlane

The experimental results on the OpenLane dataset demonstrate that Graph-RMNet performs strongly across several key metrics. As shown in Table 1, Graph-RMNet achieved an F-Score of 63.2%, representing a 1.3% and 2.9% improvement over LATR’s 61.9% and LaneCPP’s 60.3%, respectively, indicating a significant enhancement in overall performance. Specifically, in the critical metric of category accuracy, Graph-RMNet leads with 92.6% accuracy, surpassing LATR by 0.7%. This is attributed to Graph-RMNet’s ability to explore inter-lane category relations, thereby substantially improving lane classification accuracy. In terms of error analysis, Graph-RMNet achieves the best performance in both X and Z direction errors. Specifically, the near error of Graph-RMNet in the X direction is 0.198, the far error is 0.227, the near error in the Z direction is 0.071, and the far error is 0.099. Compared with those of the LATR, these errors are reduced by 0.021 and 0.032 (X direction) and 0.002 and 0.005 (Z direction), confirming the algorithm’s advantage in precision.

The data in Table 2 reveals Graph-RMNet’s performance under various scenarios. Under extreme weather and night conditions, the algorithm achieved F-Scores of 58.6% and 55.5%, improving by 1.5% and 0.1% over LATR, respectively. This confirms the ability of Graph-RMNet to utilize spatial and semantic relations between lanes to mitigate the impact of weather and lighting factors on detection results. In scenarios involving intersections and merge/split areas, Graph-RMNet outperformes LATR with scores of 53.7% and 62.1%, surpassing LATR’s 52.3% and 61.5% by 1.4% and 0.6%, respectively. This not only proves Graph-RMNet’s ability to adapt to various lane structures in real-world roads via complex topological relations but also demonstrates its strong 3D position awareness, enabling more accurate 3D positioning of lanes.

Results on Apollo synthetic

The comparative experimental results on the Apollo Synthetic dataset are shown in Table 3. Graph-RMNet demonstrates superior performance across all the scenarios, achieving an F-Score of 97.0% in the Balanced Scenes scenario, second to Anchor3DLane and LaneCPP. However, it results in relatively lower errors, with reductions of 0.013 and 0.036 in the X direction for near and far distances, respectively, and 0.006 and 0.006 in the Z direction, compared with LaneCPP, indicating a significant advantage in the precise localization of lanes. Furthermore, in the Rarely Observed and Visual Variants scenarios, Graph-RMNet outperforms other methods with F-Scores of 96.2% and 95.3%, respectively, demonstrating that the establishment of inter-lane associations enhances the model’s robustness and accuracy in complex scenarios. These results confirm that Graph-RMNet effectively improves the detection of occluded lane sections by implicitly constructing relations between lanes, especially under conditions with variable visual cues.

Results on ONCE-3DLanes

The comparative experimental results on the ONCE-3DLanes dataset, as shown in Table 4, demonstrate that Graph-RMNet performs well, achieving an F-Score of 80.63%, slightly surpassing LATR’s 80.59%. The results indicate that in merge/split scenarios, Graph-RMNet accurately captures the complete structure of lanes, whereas LATR presents issues with missing and distorted lanes, highlighting Graph-RMNet’s capability to understand complex topological structures via inter-lane relations and reduce environmental interference. In curve scenarios, Graph-RMNet demonstrates good adaptability to the continuity and curvature changes of lanes compared with LATR, effectively tracking the bending trajectory of the lanes and reducing errors from the ground truth, indicating Graph-RMNet’s improved detection using 3D spatial information. Under overcast and night conditions, despite suboptimal lighting and blurred distant lanes, Graph-RMNet still successfully infers the position and category of lanes in low-light environments, whereas LATR results to erroneous detections, demonstrating its robustness to changes in lighting. However, in the overcast scenario with dense lanes on the right, Graph-RMNet results in missed detections, indicating that the algorithm still has room for improvement in detecting dense lanes, although it is an improvement over LATR. Under rainy conditions, Graph-RMNet successfully detects lanes that are blurred due to water reflection and slippery road surfaces, showing its ability to leverage complex spatial and categorical relations between lanes to effectively increase the detection accuracy of lanes with missing visual cues. Ablation study

To thoroughly validate the effectiveness of our proposed lane detection algorithm, a series of ablation experiments were conducted on the subset OpenLane300 of the OpenLane dataset. The aim was to systematically evaluate the contribution of each component to the overall performance and to gain a detailed understanding of the algorithm’s working mechanism.

Module ablations

The ablation study results in Table 6 provide an in-depth analysis of the impact of the 3D position query generation and dual-path relation modules on lane detection performance. The introduction of the dual-path relation module alone increased the F1 score by 1.32% and the classification accuracy by 1.37%, indicating that this module can better capture the spatial and categorical relations between lanes, providing richer relational features for model detection. When the 3D position query generation module was incorporated, the model achieved an F1 score of 69.37% and a classification accuracy of 91.54%. The errors in the X direction for near and far distances were reduced by 0.018 and 0.024, respectively, and the Z direction errors were decreased by 0.001 and 0.004, respectively, demonstrating that the inclusion of 3D positional information enhanced the query sensitivity to 3D positions, improving the model’s accurate positioning of lanes. When both modules were used together, the performance was optimal, with the F1 score and classification accuracy increasing to 71.32% and 93.04%, respectively. This finding indicates that the introduction of 3D positional information significantly enhanced the model’s ability to associate spatial relations between lanes, and through relation modelling, the model was able to more accurately detect the 3D positions of lanes. The reduction in errors in both the X and Z directions further confirms the improvement in the model’s lane positioning accuracy.

Relation ablations

Table 7 reveals the significance of spatial and categorical relations in 3D lane detection algorithms. Without the establishment of these two types of relation, the model achieved F1 scores and classification accuracies of 69.36% and 91.54%, respectively, on the overall dataset. After introducing the spatial relation alone, the performance improved to an F1 score of 71.15% and a classification accuracy of 92.93%. After introducing only the categorical relationships, the performance improved to an F1 score of 69.42% and a classification accuracy of 91.95%. When both types of relationships were introduced simultaneously, the performance reached its optimal level, with the F1 score and classification accuracy increasing to 71.32% and 93.04%, respectively. This finding indicates that considering both spatial and categorical relations can better assist the model in understanding the topological relations of lanes under complex scenarios, enhancing the model’s adaptability. This simultaneously shows that both categorical and spatial relationships positively impact algorithm performance. To further validate the impact of relation modelling on algorithm performance, a comparative analysis was conducted on the detection effects of Graph-RMNet under complex scenarios such as rainy and night conditions, which often lead to missing lane detections before and after the introduction of the dual-path relation enhancement module. These analysis results were visualized, as shown in Fig. 6. Without the relation modelling mechanism, lanes were obscured and not visible in rainy scenarios, and were missing due to insufficient lighting in night scenarios, making them difficult to discern in front-view images. After introducing the relation establishment, the Graph-RMNet algorithm was able to identify these partially invisible lanes affected by environmental factors effectively, confirming that the relation modelling mechanism allows Graph-RMNet to infer the parts of lanes that are difficult to detect directly due to because of insufficient visual cues based on the inter-lane relations.

Conclusion

To address the issue of partially occluded or missing lanes in complex traffic environments, we propose a lane detection algorithm based on a graph attention mechanism for relation modelling. The 3D positional query generation module provides accurate spatial information for lane detection tasks, enhancing the model’s perception of 3D positions and offering rich spatial features for relation establishment. The designed dual-path relation module constructs graph attention networks on two parallel paths, allowing the model to fully understand the spatial and categorical relations between lanes. Experimental results on the Apollo Synthetic, OpenLane, and ONCE-3DLanes datasets demonstrate that Graph-RMNet can accurately infer the missing parts of lanes under complex traffic conditions by deeply analysing the three-dimensional topological structure and semantic category relations between lanes, effectively reducing the impact of complex environments on the accuracy of lane detection. Our proposed approach offers a new technical approach for 3D lane detection in autonomous driving under complex traffic conditions, proving its effectiveness and applicability in practical applications. Future research will not only focus on the relationships between lanes but also extend to the establishment of relationships between lanes and other traffic signs, in order to achieve more comprehensive and precise scene perception and provide richer contextual information for autonomous driving systems.

Linear maps preserving the Cullis’ determinant. I Journal: Linear Algebra and its Applications
Alexander Guterman, Andrey Yurkov

Abstract
This paper is the first in the series of papers devoted to the explicit description of linear maps preserving the Cullis’ determinant of rectangular matrices with entries belonging to an arbitrary ground field which is large enough. The Cullis’ determinant is defined for every matrix of size n × k n\times k , where n ≥ k ≥ 1 n\geq k\geq 1 and is equal to the ordinary determinant if n = k n=k . In this paper we solve the linear preserver problem for the Cullis’ determinant for k ≥ 4 , n ≥ k + 2 k\geq 4,n\geq k+2 and n + k n+k is even. It appears that in this case all linear maps preserving the Cullis’ determinant are non-singular and could be represented by two-sided matrix multiplication. We also obtain the complete description of linear maps preserving the Cullis’ determinant of rectangular matrices for k = 1 k=1 , n ≥ k n\geq k and provide an example showing that for k = 2 , n ≥ k + 2 = 4 k=2,n\geq k+2=4 a linear map preserving det n ​ 2 \det_{n\,2} may not correspond to the description of linear maps preserving det n ​ k \det_{n\,k} for k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 obtained in this paper in its sequel. Note that the cases where n = k n=k or n = k + 1 n=k+1 admit slightly different description allowing (sub)matrix transposition and were completely studied before: the case where n = k n=k is a classical linear preserver problem for the ordinary determinant and was solved by Frobenius; the complete characterisation for the case where n = k + 1 n=k+1 was obtained in the previous paper by the authors.

Introduction

The determinant of a matrix is a classical object of investigations in Linear Algebra and its applications. Usually only the determinant of square matrices is considered, but different attempts to generalize the notion of determinant to the set of rectangular matrices have been done for a long time. Cullis introduced the concept of determinant (he called it
determinoid
) of a rectangular matrix in his monograph
[
2
]
and it is presumably the first published generalization of the determinant to the rectangular case. Several properties known for the ordinary determinant are studied and shown to be valid for the Cullis determinant in
[
2
, §5, §27, §32]
, and we recall some of them below. Algebraic characterization of the Cullis determinant can be found in
[
1
,
6
]
.

In 1966 Radić
[
14
]
independently proposed a definition of the determinant of a rectangular matrix, which is equivalent to the Cullis definition, and since that, in some papers it is called
Radić’s determinant

[
1
]
or
Cullis-Radić determinant

[
6
,
7
]
. After that there were several other generalizations of the determinant of a square matrix to rectangular matrices given, for example, in
[
13
,
16
,
19
]
.

The notion of determinant has been studied in many contexts and one of them is the investigation of linear maps preserving the determinant. The first result in this direction dates back to 1897 and is due to Frobenius
[
3
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Frobenius,
[
3
, §7, Theorem I]
)
.

Let
S\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a bijective linear map satisfying
\det(S(X))=\det(X)
for all
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, where
\mathbb{C}
denotes the field of complex numbers. Then there exist matrices
M,N\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with
\det(MN)=1
such that

S(X)=MXN\;\;\mbox{for all}\;X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\;\;\text{or}\;\;S(X)=MX^{t}N\;\;\mbox{for all}\;X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

This result by Frobenius prompted the investigation of so-called linear preserver problems concerning the characterization of linear operators on matrix spaces that leave certain functions, subsets, relations, etc., invariant. The research started by Frobenius was continued in the works by Schur, Dieudonné, Dynkin and others. One may see
[
15
]
for a brief introduction to the subject and
[
11
]
for an extensive survey.

A question about generalization of the Frobenius’ theorem to the Cullis’ determinant on general rectangular matrices arises naturally. The corresponding linear preserver problem was not even partially solved until 2024. In a recent paper
[
4
]
, the authors initiated research on this problem by considering the case of rectangular matrices of size
n\times k
with
n=k+1
.

Continuing the work in this direction, we found the solution of linear preserver problem of the Cullis’ determinant for the matrices of all possible sizes
n\times k
with
k\neq 1,2
in the case where the ground field is large enough. Note that cases where
k=1
and
k=2
are special because if
k=1
, then the Cullis’ determinant is just a linear functional and if
k=2
, then it is just a quadratic form where the standard methods of studying quadratic forms may be applied. We discuss these cases in detail in Section
6
and Section
7
, correspondingly.

Our result contains several cases which differs from each other by the approach and techniques applied. In this part we consider the case where
k\geq 4,n\geq k+2
and
n+k
is even together with assumption that the size of the ground field is greater than
k
. The results are likely valid for every ground field but different methods are required. We also provide an example showing that already in the case where
k=3
another approach is needed (this case will be considered in the separate paper).

(

\det_{n\,k}(X)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{[n]}}\sgn_{n\,k}(\sigma)x_{\sigma(1)\,1}x_{\sigma(2)\,2}\ldots x_{\sigma(k)\,k}.

\det_{n\,k}(X)=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&\cdots&x_{1\,k}\\

Now it is possible to formulate the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem

(Theorem
5.14
)
.

Assume that
|{\mathbb{F}}|>k\geq 4
,
n\geq k+2
and
n+k
is even. Let
T\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
be a linear map. Then
\det_{n\,k}(T(X))=\det_{n\,k}(X)
for all
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
if and only if there exist
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,n}({\mathbb{F}})
and
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
such that

\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(A(|i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}]\Bigr)\cdot\det_{k}\Bigl(B\Bigr)=(-1)^{i_{1}+\ldots+i_{k}-1-\ldots-k}

for all increasing sequences
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n
and

T(X)=AXB

(1.1)

for all
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}}).

Our proof is based on the following observation: if
P
is a polynomial function of matrix coefficients, then for the given matrices
A,B
the expression
P(A+\lambda B)
is a polynomial function of
\lambda,
and for a certain
P
it is possible to find the relationship between the value of
\max_{A}(\deg_{\lambda}(A+\lambda B))
and various properties of
B.

Thus, assuming that
P
is equal to Cullis’ determinant, for a given matrix
B
we provide the relationship between the condition
\max_{A}(\deg_{\lambda}P(A+\lambda B))\leq 1
and the condition
\rk(B)\leq 1
described in Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.10
below. Here we need to assume the ground field is large enough because we need to identify the polynomial with its coefficients.

We conclude from the obtained relationship that every linear map preserving the Cullis’ determinant must preserve the set of matrices of rank one because in this case every linear map preserving the Cullis’ determinant preserves the value
\max_{A}(\deg_{\lambda}P(A+\lambda B))
. The description of linear maps preserving matrices of rank one was obtained by Westwick
[
18
]
. His result requires the bijectivity of considered linear map. To establish that every considered linear map is bijective we use the notion of radical of a function and its properties which are introduced and studied by Waterhouse
[
17
]
.

Then
[
18
, Theorem 3.5]
implies that, under our assumptions, every linear map preserving the Cullis’ determinant has the form
X\mapsto AXB
. Finally, we provide the necessary and sufficient condition for a map of such form to be a linear Cullis’ determinant preserver which is described in Lemma
3.1
.

\det_{n\,1}
; in Section

we provide an example of linear map preserving
\det_{n\,2}
which does not admit a representation (

) and shows that the main theorem does not hold for

and even

3
Linear
det_{n\,k}
-preservers of the form
X\mapsto AXB

The common type of
\det_{n\,k}
-preservers can be obtained by the two-sided matrix multiplication as it follows from Lemma
3.1
below.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n\geq k
,
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,n}({\mathbb{F}})
,
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
, and let
T\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
be a linear map defined by

T(X)=AXB

(3.1)

for all
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}}).
Then

\det_{n\,k}(T(X))=\det_{n\,k}(X)

for all
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
if and only if

\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(A(|d]\Bigr)\cdot\det_{k}\Bigl(B\Bigr)=\sgn(d)

(3.2)

for all
d\in\binom{[n]}{k}.

Proof.

Assume that
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,n}({\mathbb{F}}),B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
are the matrices satisfying the condition (
3.2
). Therefore,

\det_{n\,k}(T(X))=\det_{n\,k}(AXB)=\det_{n\,k}((AX)B).

(3.3)

Since the triple
(k,k,n)
satisfies the inequalities
1\leq k\leq k\leq n
and
AX\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}}),B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k\,k}({\mathbb{F}}),
then

\det_{n\,k}((AX)B)=\det_{n\,k}(AX)\det_{k}(B)

(3.4)

by Corollary
2.8
. Similarly, since the triple
(k,n,n)
satisfies the inequalities
1\leq k\leq n\leq n
and
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,n}({\mathbb{F}}),X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}}),
then by Lemma
2.7
we have

\det_{n\,k}(AX)\det_{k}(B)=\left(\sum_{d\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(A(|d]\Bigr)\det_{k}\Bigl(X[d|)\Bigr)\right)\det_{k}\Bigl(B\Bigr).

(3.5)

Hence,

\left(\sum_{d\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(A(|d]\Bigr)\det_{k}\Bigl(X[d|)\Bigr)\right)\det_{k}\Bigl(B\Bigr)\\
=\sum_{d\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\left(\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(A(|d]\Bigr)\det_{k}\Bigl(B\Bigr)\right)\cdot\det_{k}\Bigl(X[d|)\Bigr).

(3.6)

The condition of the lemma implies that

\sum_{d\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\left(\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(A(|d])\Bigr)\det_{k}(B)\right)\cdot\det_{k}\Bigl(X(|d]\Bigr)\\
=\sum_{d\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\left(\sgn_{[n]}(d)\right)\cdot\det_{k}\Bigl(X[d|)\Bigr).

(3.7)

By Corollary
2.6
,

Proof of the main theorem

We begin with establishing a relationship between the following two properties of a given matrix
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
: “
\deg_{\lambda}(\det_{n\,k}(A+\lambda B))\leq 1
for all
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
” and “
\rk(B)\leq 1
”. Since the first property is preserved by every linear
\det_{n\,k}
-preserver as it is shown in Lemma
5.9
below, then we could use this relationship to prove that the second property is also preserved by every linear
\det_{n\,k}
-preserver. This would allow us to use the results of Westwick regarding linear maps preserving the set of matrices of rank one.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
n\geq k
and
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
be such that

Then

\deg_{\lambda}(\det_{n\,k}(A+\lambda B))\leq 1

for all
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}}).

Proof.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[\lambda]
be a polynomial defined by

P=\det_{n\,k}(A+\lambda B)=a_{0}+a_{1}\lambda+\ldots+a_{k}\lambda^{d},

where
a_{0},\ldots,a_{k}\in{\mathbb{F}}
. Let us show that
a_{d}\geq 0
for all
d\geq 2
. Indeed, the expansion (
2.1
) implies that

a_{d}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq k}\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(A(|1]\Big|\ldots\Big|B(|i_{1}]\Big|\ldots\Big|B(|i_{d}]\Big|\ldots\Big|A(|n]\Bigr).

(5.1)

Assume that
d\geq 2
. Let
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq k
and
C\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
be defined by

C=A(|1]\Big|\ldots\Big|B(|i_{1}]\Big|\ldots\Big|B(|i_{d}]\Big|\ldots\Big|A(|n].

This matrix contains at least two columns from
B
because
d\geq 2
. Since
\rk(B)\leq 1
, these columns of

are linearly dependent and consequently
\det_{n\,k}(C)=0
. This implies that the right-hand side of (
5.1
) is equal to 0. Hence,
a_{d}=0
as the left-hand side of (
5.1
).

Therefore,
a_{d}=0
for all

and
\deg_{\lambda}(\det_{n\,k}(A+\lambda B))\leq 1
.
∎

5.2
) would easily imply that
\rk(B)\leq 1
.

Unfortunately, our computations show that if
k=4
and
\Char({\mathbb{F}})\neq 0
, then for certain
n\geq k+2
such
A^{\prime}
does not exist.

Nevertheless, the set of possible sums of
2\times 2
minors of
X
obtained by substituting different
A^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
in
\det_{n\,4}(X|A^{\prime})
is large enough for our purposes. It is possible to obtain the equality (
5.2
) from the equalities
\det_{n\,4}(X|A^{\prime})=0
for all
A^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
belonging to this set as it is done below. The simplest expression of the form
\det_{n\,4}(X|A^{\prime})
could be obtained from the next lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
n\geq k\geq 4
,
n>l>2
and
X=(x_{i\,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
. Then there exists
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,(k-2)}({\mathbb{F}})
such that

\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=\left|\begin{matrix}x_{1\,1}-x_{1\,2}&x_{2\,1}-x_{2\,2}\\
x_{1\,l}-x_{1\,l+1}&x_{2\,l}-x_{2\,(l+1)}\\
\end{matrix}\right|.

Proof.

Since
\left|\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{1,2,l,l+1\}\right|=n-4\geq k-4,
then there exists a sequence
2<i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k-4}\leq n
such that
\{l,l+1\}\cap\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-4}\}=\varnothing.
Let
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,(k-2)}({\mathbb{F}})
be defined by

A=E_{1\,1}+E_{2\,1}+E_{l\,2}+E_{l+1\,2}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k-4}E_{i_{\alpha}\,(\alpha+2)}.

Using the Laplace expansion of
\det_{n\,k}(X|A)
along the last column
k-4
times we obtain that

\det_{n\,k}(X|A)=(-1)^{s}\det_{(n-k+4)\,4}(Y)

(5.3)

for some
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
Y\in{\mathcal{M}}_{(n-k+4)\,4}({\mathbb{F}})
defined by

Y=\Bigl(X(|1]\Big|X(|2]\Big|A(|1]\Big|A(|2]\Bigr)(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-4}|).

Note that
Y
has the form

\begin{pmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}&1&0\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}&1&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}&0&1\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}&0&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\end{pmatrix}.

This implies that
Y
contains the pairs of consecutive rows

(x_{1\,1},x_{1\,2},1,0),\;(x_{2\,1},x_{2\,2},1,0)\quad\mbox{and}\quad(x_{l\,1},x_{l\,2},0,1),\;(x_{(l+1)\,1},x_{(l+1)\,2},0,1).

Suppose that the last pair is located in
Y
on the rows
l^{\prime}
and
l^{\prime}+1
correspondingly. Then it follows from the Laplace expansion along the last column of
Y
that

\det_{(n-k+4)\,4}(Y)=(-1)^{l^{\prime}}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}&1\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}&0\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+3)\,3}\\
-(-1)^{l^{\prime}}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}&1\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}&0\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+3)\,3}.

Next, if we apply the Laplace expansion along the last column to each summand separately, then we obtain that

\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}&1\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+3)\,3}\\
=\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+2)\,2}-\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+2)\,2}

and

\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}&1\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+3)\,3}=\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+2)\,2}-\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+2)\,2}.

Thus,

(-1)^{l^{\prime}}\det_{(n-k+4)\,4}(Y)=\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+2)\,2}\\
-\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+2)\,2}-\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+2)\,2}+\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+2)\,2}\\
=\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(K)-\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(L)-\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(M)+\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(N),

where

K=Y(1,l|3,4),\quad L=Y(2,l|3,4),\quad M=Y(1,l+1|3,4),\quad N=Y(2,l+1|3,4).

Let us express each Cullis determinant as a sum of minors applying Corollary
2.6
.

\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(K)-\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(L)-\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(M)+\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(N)\\
=\sum_{c\in\binom{[n-k+2]}{2}}\sgn_{[n]}(c)\biggl(\det_{2}\Bigl(K[c|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(L[c|)\Bigr)\\
-\det_{2}\Bigl(M[c|)\Bigr)+\det_{2}\Bigl(N[c|)\Bigr)\biggr).

Note that if
c\neq\{1,l\}
, then

\det_{2}\Bigl(K[c|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(L[c|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(M[c|)\Bigr)+\det_{2}\Bigl(N[c|)\Bigr)=0

because if
c\cap\{1,l\}=\varnothing
, then

\det_{2}\Bigl(K[c|)\Bigr)=\det_{2}\Bigl(L[c|)\Bigr)=\det_{2}\Bigl(M[c|)\Bigr)=\det_{2}\Bigl(N[c|)\Bigr),

and if
c\cap\{1,l\}=1
or
l
, then

\det_{2}\Bigl(K[c|)\Bigr)=\det_{2}\Bigl(M[c|)\Bigr)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\det_{2}\Bigl(L[c|)\Bigr)=\det_{2}\Bigl(N[c|)\Bigr)

or

\det_{2}\Bigl(K[c|)\Bigr)=\det_{2}\Bigl(L[c|)\Bigr)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\det_{2}\Bigl(M[c|)\Bigr)=\det_{2}\Bigl(N[c|)\Bigr),

correspondingly. It follows

(-1)^{l^{\prime}}\det_{(n-k+4)\,4}(Y)=(-1)^{l+1-1-2}\biggl(\det_{2}\Bigl(K[1,l|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(L[1,l|)\Bigr)\\
-\det_{2}\Bigl(M[1,l|)\Bigr)+\det_{2}\Bigl(N[1,l|)\Bigr)\biggr).

(5.4)

Let us simplify the right-hand side of (
5.4
).

i.

Express all the
2\times 2
-determinants in terms of entries of
X

\det_{2}\Bigl(K[1,l|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(L[1,l|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(M[1,l|)\Bigr)+\det_{2}\Bigl(N[1,l|)\Bigr)\\
=\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}\end{vmatrix}-\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}\end{vmatrix}-\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\end{vmatrix}.

(5.5)

ii.

Using the multilinearity of
2\times 2
-determinant along the first row, we obtain

\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}\end{vmatrix}-\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}\end{vmatrix}-\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\end{vmatrix}\\
=\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}-x_{1\,1}&x_{2\,2}-x_{1\,2}\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}\end{vmatrix}-\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}-x_{1\,1}&x_{2\,2}-x_{1\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\end{vmatrix}

(5.6)

iii.

Using the multilinearity of
2\times 2
-determinant along the second row, we have

\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}-x_{1\,1}&x_{2\,2}-x_{1\,2}\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}\end{vmatrix}-\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}-x_{1\,1}&x_{2\,2}-x_{1\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\end{vmatrix}\\
=\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}-x_{1\,1}&x_{2\,2}-x_{1\,2}\\
x_{(l+1)\,1}-x_{l\,1}&x_{(l+1)\,2}-x_{l\,2}\end{vmatrix}=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}-x_{2\,1}&x_{1\,2}-x_{2\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}-x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{l\,2}-x_{(l+1)\,2}\end{vmatrix}.

(5.7)

Thus,

(-1)^{l^{\prime}+l}\det_{(n-k+4)\,4}(Y)=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}-x_{2\,1}&x_{1\,2}-x_{2\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}-x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{l\,2}-x_{(l+1)\,2}\end{vmatrix}

by (
5.4
) and (
5.5
)–(
5.7
). This implies that if
B
is obtained from
A
by multiplying its last column by
(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}},
then

\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}}\det_{n\,k}(X|A)=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}-x_{2\,1}&x_{1\,2}-x_{2\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}-x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{l\,2}-x_{(l+1)\,2}\end{vmatrix}.

∎

Remark 5.4
.

If
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
is such that
\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=0
for all
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,(k-2)}({\mathbb{F}})
and
B
is chosen as it is done in the above lemma, then the condition
\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=0
does not imply that
\rk\Bigl(X[1,2|)\Bigr)=1
because if
X=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
2&1\\
3&2\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
n-1&n\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
, then all the
2\times 2
-determinants formed by the differences of consecutive rows of
X
are zero but
\rk\Bigl(X[1,2|)\Bigr)=2
in every ground field. The next two lemmas provide us additional matrices in
{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,(k-2)}({\mathbb{F}})
which are used to conclude that
\rk\Bigl(X[1,2|)\Bigr)=2
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that
n\geq k\geq 3
. Let
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}}).
Then there exists
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,(k-2)}({\mathbb{F}})
such that

\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=\sum_{2<l\leq n-k+3}(-1)^{l}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}-x_{1\,2}&x_{2\,1}-x_{2\,2}\\
x_{1\,l}&x_{2\,l}\\
\end{vmatrix}.

Proof.

Let
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,(k-2)}({\mathbb{F}})
be defined by

A=E_{1\,1}+E_{2\,1}+\left(E_{(n-k+4)\,2}+\ldots+E_{n\,(k-2)}\right)=E_{1\,1}+E_{2\,1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-3}E_{(n-k+3+i)\,i+1}.

It follows from the Laplace expansion of
\det_{n\,k}(X|A)
along the last column
k-3
times that

\det_{n\,k}(X|A)=(-1)^{s}\det_{(n-k+3)\,3}(Y)

(5.8)

for some
s\in\mathbb{N}
and

Y=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}&1\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{(n-k+3)\,1}&x_{(n-k+3)\,2}&0\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Then Laplace expansion along the last column of
Y
implies that

\det_{(n-k+3)\,3}(Y)\\
=\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{(n-k+3)\,1}&x_{(n-k+3)\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+2)\,2}-\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{(n-k+3)\,1}&x_{(n-k+3)\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}_{(n-k+2)\,2}\\
=\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(Y^{\prime})-\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(Y^{\prime\prime}),

where
Y^{\prime}=Y(1|3),\;Y^{\prime\prime}=Y(2|3)
.
Let us express each Cullis determinant as a sum of minors following Corollary
2.6
.

\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(Y^{\prime})-\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(Y^{\prime\prime})\\
=\sum_{c\in\binom{[n-k+2]}{2}}\sgn_{[n]}(c)\left(\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime}[c|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime\prime}[c|)\Bigr)\right).

Note that if
1\not\in c
, then
Y^{\prime}[c|)=Y^{\prime\prime}[c|)
and consequently
\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime}[c|)\Bigr)=\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime\prime}[c|)\Bigr)
. Hence,

\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime}[c|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime\prime}[c|)\Bigr)=0

for all
c\in\binom{[n-k+2]}{2}
not containing 1. Thus,

\det_{(n-k+3)\,3}(Y)=\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(Y^{\prime})-\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(Y^{\prime\prime})\\
=\sum_{c\in\binom{[n-k+2]}{2}}\sgn_{[n]}(c)\left(\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime}[c|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime\prime}[c|)\Bigr)\right)\\
=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\binom{[n-k+2]}{2}\\
1\not\in c\end{subarray}}\sgn_{[n]}(c)\left(\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime}[c|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime\prime}[c|)\Bigr)\right)\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\binom{[n-k+2]}{2}\\
1\in c\end{subarray}}\sgn_{[n]}(c)\left(\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime}[c|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime\prime}[c|)\Bigr)\right)\\
=0+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\binom{[n-k+2]}{2}\\
1\in c\end{subarray}}\sgn_{[n]}(c)\left(\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime}[c|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(Y^{\prime\prime}[c|)\Bigr)\right)\\
=\sum_{2\leq l\leq n-k+2}(-1)^{1+l-1-2}\left(\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}-\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}\right).

The multilinearity of
2\times 2
-determinant along the first row implies that

\sum_{2\leq l\leq n-k+2}(-1)^{1+l-1-2}\left(\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}-\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}\right)\\
=\sum_{2\leq l\leq n-k+2}\begin{vmatrix}x_{2\,1}-x_{1\,1}&x_{2\,2}-x_{1\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}.

From this we conclude that if
B
obtained from
A
by multiplying its last column by
(-1)^{(s+1)},
then

\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=(-1)^{s+1}\det_{n\,k}(X|A)=-\det_{(n-k+3)\,3}(Y)\\
=\sum_{2\leq l\leq n-k+2}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}-x_{2\,1}&x_{1\,2}-x_{2\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}.

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}.
Then there exists
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,(k-2)}({\mathbb{F}})
such that

\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\end{vmatrix}+\sum_{l=3}^{n-k+2}(-1)^{l-2}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}-x_{2\,1}&x_{1\,2}-x_{2\,2}\\
x_{l\,l}&x_{l\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}\\
+\sum_{2<l<m\leq n-k+2}(-1)^{l+m-3}\begin{vmatrix}x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
x_{m\,1}&x_{m\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}.

(5.9)

Proof.

The statement of the lemma clearly holds if
k=2
because in this case the expression (
5.9
) is just a standard expansion of Cullis’ determinant for
k=2
by Corollary
2.6
.

Assume that
k\geq 3
. Let
A
be defined by

A=E_{(n-k+3)\,1}+\ldots+E_{n\,(k-2)}.

It follows from the Laplace expansion of
\det_{n\,k}(X|A)
along the last column
k-2
times that

\det_{n\,k}(X|A)=(-1)^{s}\det_{(n-k+2)\,2}(Y),

where
s\in\mathbb{N}
and

Y=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{(n-k+2)\,1}&x_{(n-k+2)\,2}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

By Corollary
2.6
we obtain that

\det_{n\,k}(Y)=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\end{vmatrix}+\sum_{l=3}^{n-k+2}(-1)^{l-2}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}-x_{2\,1}&x_{1\,2}-x_{2\,2}\\
x_{l\,l}&x_{l\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}\\
+\sum_{2<l<m\leq n-k+2}(-1)^{l+m-3}\begin{vmatrix}x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
x_{m\,1}&x_{m\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}.

From this we conclude that if
B
is obtained from
A
by multiplying its last column by
(-1)^{s}
, then the desired equality (
5.9
) holds.
∎

Thus, using the expressions obtained above, we prove in the lemma below that
\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=0
for all
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k-2}({\mathbb{F}})
implies
\rk(X)\leq 1
. This lemma comprises the main part of the proof of the converse of Lemma
5.1
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
n\geq k+2
,
k\geq 4
and
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
. Assume that

\det_{n\,k}(X|A)=0

(5.10)

for all
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k-2}({\mathbb{F}}).
Then
\rk(X)\leq 1.

Proof.

The proof is divided into two parts as follows.

(i)

We prove that
\rk\begin{pmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\end{pmatrix}\leq 1
for all
X
satisfying the condition of the lemma.

(ii)

We prove that
\rk(X)\leq 1
for all
X
satisfying the condition of the lemma.

(i)

By contradiction. Suppose that
\rk\begin{pmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\end{pmatrix}=2.
Let
\gamma_{1}=x_{1\,1}-x_{2\,1}
and
\gamma_{2}=x_{1\,2}-x_{2\,2}.
Then
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\neq(0,0)
because otherwise
\rk\begin{pmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\end{pmatrix}\leq 1.

Let
2<l<n.
By Lemma
5.3
there exists
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,(k-2)}({\mathbb{F}})
such that

\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}-x_{2\,1}&x_{1\,2}-x_{2\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}-x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{l\,2}-x_{(l+1)\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}.

We obtain from our initial assumption that
\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=0.
Hence,

\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}-x_{2\,1}&x_{1\,2}-x_{2\,2}\\
x_{l\,1}-x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{l\,2}-x_{(l+1)\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}\\
=\begin{vmatrix}\gamma_{1}&\gamma_{2}\\
x_{l\,1}-x_{(l+1)\,1}&x_{l\,2}-x_{(l+1)\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}\\
=\gamma_{1}(x_{l\,2}-x_{(l+1)\,2})-\gamma_{2}(x_{l\,1}-x_{(l+1)\,2})=0,

and therefore

\gamma_{1}x_{l\,2}-\gamma_{2}x_{l\,1}=\gamma_{1}x_{(l+1)\,2}-\gamma_{2}x_{(l+1)\,2}

for all
2<l<n.
Denote the common value of
\gamma_{1}x_{l\,2}-\gamma_{2}x_{l\,1}
by
v.

By Lemma
5.5
there exists
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,(k-2)}({\mathbb{F}})
such that

\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=\sum_{2<l\leq n-k+3}(-1)^{l}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}-x_{1\,2}&x_{2\,1}-x_{2\,2}\\
x_{1\,l}&x_{2\,l}\\
\end{vmatrix}

It follows from our initial assumption that
\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=0.
Hence,

\sum_{2<l\leq n-k+3}(-1)^{l}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}-x_{1\,2}&x_{2\,1}-x_{2\,2}\\
x_{1\,l}&x_{2\,l}\\
\end{vmatrix}=\sum_{2<l\leq n-k+3}(-1)^{l}\begin{vmatrix}\gamma_{1}&\gamma_{2}\\
x_{1\,l}&x_{2\,l}\\
\end{vmatrix}\\
=\sum_{2<l\leq n-k+3}(-1)^{l}(\gamma_{1}x_{l\,2}-\gamma_{2}x_{l\,1})=\sum_{2<l\leq n-k+3}(-1)^{l}v=0.

Now,

\sum_{2<l\leq n-k+3}(-1)^{l}v=v

because the number of terms in the sum on the right-hand side of this equality is odd and it begins with
v.
Therefore,
v=0,
i.e.

\gamma_{1}x_{i\,2}-\gamma_{2}x_{i\,1}=0

(5.11)

for all
3\leq i\leq n.

This provides us a non-trivial linear relation between the columns of the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}x_{3\,1}&x_{3\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{n\,1}&x_{n\,2}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore,

\begin{vmatrix}x_{l\,1}&x_{l\,2}\\
x_{m\,1}&x_{m\,2}\\
\end{vmatrix}=0

(5.12)

for all
2<l<m\leq n.

By Lemma
5.6
there exist
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n,k-2}({\mathbb{F}})
such that

\det_{n\,k}(X|B)=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\

\end{vmatrix}.

By (

which leads to a contradiction.

(ii)

By contradiction. Suppose that

satisfies the coniditions of the lemma and

Hence, there exist

{\mathbf{S}}_{i\,1}(X)|{\mathbf{S}}_{i\,1}\left({\mathbf{S}}_{i\,1}^{-1}(A)\right)={\mathbf{S}}_{i\,1}\left(X|{\mathbf{S}}_{i\,1}^{-1}(A)\right)

(5.13)

for all

Remark 5.8
.

One can see that the statement of the part
(i)
of Lemma
5.7
can be reformulated as follows

f_{1}=0,\ldots,f_{k}=0\Rightarrow g=0

where

denotes a polynomial representing the first

minor of

and

denotes one of the expressions used in the proof, that is,

in the statements of Lemma
5.3

and Lemma

Then assuming that
{\mathbb{F}}
is algebraically closed and using Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz it is possible to conclude that

where

is an ideal in
{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1\,1},x_{1\,2},\ldots,x_{n\,1},x_{n\,2}]
generated by

This observation indicates the possibility of alternative proof of Lemma
5.10
by finding an appropriate

these exponents should be quite large. For this reason we believe that the proof of Lemma
5.10
could not be significantly simplified.

The last thing we need to show before we prove the converse of Lemma
5.1
is that every linear map preserving the Cullis’ determinant does not increase the value
\max_{A}(\deg_{\lambda}(\det_{n\,k}(A+\lambda B)))

Lemma 5.9
.

Assume that

|{\mathbb{F}}|>k

By Corollary
4.7

T(B)

∎

Lemma 5.10
.

.

Note that if

.

Assume that

7
Linear maps preserving
\det_{n\,2}

In this section we provide an example showing that a linear map preserving
\det_{n\,2}
may not correspond the description of linear maps preserving
\det_{n\,k}
for
k\geq 3
obtained in this paper in its sequel. Note that this example is obtained by decomposing the quadratic space
\left({\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}}),\det_{n\,2}\right)
into an appropriate direct sum of
hyperbolic planes
and interchanging two elements of the corresponding basis.

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume that
n\geq 2
. Let

X=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{n\,1}&x_{n\,2}\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}}),\quad S_{1}=\sum^{n-1}_{i=2}(-1)^{i}x_{i\,1},\quad S_{2}=\sum^{n-1}_{i=2}(-1)^{i}x_{i\,2}.

Let
Y\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
be defined by

Y=\begin{pmatrix}S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{(n-1)\,1}&x_{(n-1)\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&-S_{1}-S_{2}+x_{1\,1}\end{pmatrix}.

Then

\det_{n\,2}(X)=\det_{n\,2}(Y).

Proof.

Let us express
\det_{n\,2}(X)
and
\det_{n\,2}(Y)
as sums of
2\times 2
-determinants.

\det_{n\,2}(X)=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(-1)^{i+j-1-2}\begin{vmatrix}x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\\
x_{j\,1}&x_{j\,2}\end{vmatrix}\\
=\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq n-1}(-1)^{i+j-1-2}\begin{vmatrix}x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\\
x_{j\,1}&x_{j\,2}\end{vmatrix}+\sum_{2\leq i\leq n-1}(-1)^{i-2}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\end{vmatrix}\\
+\left(\sum_{2\leq i\leq n-1}(-1)^{i-1}\begin{vmatrix}x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&x_{n\,2}\end{vmatrix}\right)+\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&x_{n\,2}\end{vmatrix}

(7.1)

and

\det_{n\,2}(Y)=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(-1)^{i+j-1-2}\begin{vmatrix}Y_{i\,1}&Y_{i\,2}\\
Y_{j\,1}&Y_{j\,2}\end{vmatrix}\\
=\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq n-1}(-1)^{i+j-1-2}\begin{vmatrix}x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\\
x_{j\,1}&x_{j\,2}\end{vmatrix}\phantom{XXXXXX}\\
\phantom{X}+\sum_{2\leq i\leq n-1}(-1)^{i-2}\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\end{vmatrix}\\
\phantom{XXXXX}+\sum_{2\leq i\leq n-1}(-1)^{i-1}\begin{vmatrix}x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&-S_{1}-S_{2}+x_{1\,1}\end{vmatrix}\\
+\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&-S_{1}-S_{2}+x_{1\,1}\end{vmatrix}.

(7.2)

Since the first summands in (
7.1
) and (
7.2
) are equal, then we need only to show that

\sum_{2\leq i\leq n-1}(-1)^{i-2}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\end{vmatrix}+\sum_{2\leq i\leq n-1}(-1)^{i-1}\begin{vmatrix}x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&x_{n\,2}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&x_{n\,2}\end{vmatrix}\\
=\sum_{2\leq i\leq n-1}(-1)^{i-2}\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\end{vmatrix}\\
\phantom{XXXXXX}+\sum_{2\leq i\leq n-1}(-1)^{i-1}\begin{vmatrix}x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&-S_{1}-S_{2}+x_{1\,1}\end{vmatrix}\\
+\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&-S_{1}-S_{2}+x_{1\,1}\end{vmatrix}

(7.3)

Let us simplify the first summand of the right-hand side of (
7.3
) using the multilinear and antisymmetric properties of
2\times 2
-determinant. First,

\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}(-1)^{i-2}\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\end{vmatrix}=\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}\\
S_{1}&S_{2}\end{vmatrix}

by the multilinearity of
2\times 2
-determinant along the last row. Second,

\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}\\
S_{1}&S_{2}\end{vmatrix}=\begin{vmatrix}S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}-S_{2}\\
S_{1}&S_{2}\end{vmatrix}

by the antisymmetricity of
2\times 2
-determinant along the rows. Third,

\begin{vmatrix}S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}-S_{2}\\
S_{1}&S_{2}\end{vmatrix}=\begin{vmatrix}S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}+x_{n\,2}\\
S_{1}&S_{2}+S_{1}\end{vmatrix}

(7.4)

by the antisymmetricity of
2\times 2
-determinant along the columns.

The second summand of the right-hand side of (
7.3
) could be simplified in the similar way as it done below.

\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}(-1)^{i-1}\begin{vmatrix}x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&-S_{1}-S_{2}+x_{1\,1}\end{vmatrix}=-\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}&S_{2}\\
x_{n\,1}&-S_{1}-S_{2}+x_{1\,1}\end{vmatrix}\\
=-\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}&S_{2}\\
x_{n\,1}+S_{1}&-S_{1}+x_{1\,1}\end{vmatrix}=-\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}&S_{1}+S_{2}\\
x_{n\,1}+S_{1}&x_{n\,1}+x_{1\,1}\end{vmatrix}.

(7.5)

Now let us find the sum of the right-hand sides of (
7.4
) and (
7.5
) (simplified versions of the first and the second summand of the right-hand side of (
7.3
)) using the multilinear and antisymmetric properties of
2\times 2
-determinant

\begin{vmatrix}S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}+x_{n\,2}\\
S_{1}&S_{2}+S_{1}\end{vmatrix}-\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}&S_{1}+S_{2}\\
x_{n\,1}+S_{1}&x_{n\,1}+x_{1\,1}\end{vmatrix}\\
=\begin{vmatrix}S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}+x_{n\,2}\\
S_{1}&S_{2}+S_{1}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}x_{n\,1}+S_{1}&x_{n\,1}+x_{1\,1}\\
S_{1}&S_{1}+S_{2}\end{vmatrix}\\
=\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,1}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,1}+x_{1\,2}+x_{n\,1}+x_{n\,2}\\
S_{1}&S_{2}+S_{1}\end{vmatrix}\\
=\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,1}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,1}+x_{1\,2}-S_{1}-S_{2}\\
S_{1}&S_{2}\end{vmatrix}.

(7.6)

Let us add to the right-hand side of (
7.6
) the third summand of the right-hand side of (
7.3
) and simplify it.

\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,1}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,1}+x_{1\,2}-S_{1}-S_{2}\\
S_{1}&S_{2}\end{vmatrix}\\
+\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,2}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&-S_{1}-S_{2}+x_{1\,1}\end{vmatrix}\\
=(S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,1}+x_{n\,2})S_{2}-(x_{1\,1}+x_{1\,2}-S_{1}-S_{2})S_{1}\phantom{XXXXXX}\\
+(S_{1}+S_{2}+x_{n\,2})(-S_{1}-S_{2}+x_{1\,1})-x_{1\,2}x_{n\,1}\\
=(S_{1}+S_{2})^{2}+(x_{n\,1}+x_{n\,2})S_{2}-(x_{1\,1}+x_{1\,2})S_{1}-(S_{1}+S_{2})^{2}\\
+(S_{1}+S_{2})x_{1\,1}-(S_{1}+S_{2})x_{n\,2}+x_{n\,2}x_{1\,1}-x_{1\,2}x_{n\,1}.

The resulting expression could be transformed as follows

(S_{1}+S_{2})^{2}+(x_{n\,1}+x_{n\,2})S_{2}-(x_{1\,1}+x_{1\,2})S_{1}-(S_{1}+S_{2})^{2}\\
+(S_{1}+S_{2})x_{1\,1}-(S_{1}+S_{2})x_{n\,2}+x_{n\,2}x_{1\,1}-x_{1\,2}x_{n\,1}\\
=(x_{n\,1}+x_{n\,2})S_{2}-(x_{1\,1}+x_{1\,2})S_{1}+(S_{1}+S_{2})x_{1\,1}-(S_{1}+S_{2})x_{n\,2}+\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&x_{n\,2}\end{vmatrix}\\
=x_{1\,1}S_{2}-x_{1\,2}S_{1}+x_{n\,1}S_{2}-x_{n\,2}S_{1}+\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&x_{n\,2}\end{vmatrix}\phantom{XXXXXXXXXXX}\\
=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
S_{1}&S_{2}\end{vmatrix}-\begin{vmatrix}S_{1}&S_{2}\\
x_{n\,1}&x_{n\,2}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&x_{n\,2}\end{vmatrix}\phantom{XXXXXXXXXXXX}\\
\phantom{XX}=\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n-1}(-1)^{i-2}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\end{vmatrix}+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n-1}(-1)^{i-1}\begin{vmatrix}x_{i\,1}&x_{i\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&x_{n\,2}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&x_{n\,2}\end{vmatrix}.

This is equal to the left-hand side of (
7.3
). Therefore, the equality (
7.3
) holds and consequently

\det_{n\,2}(X)=\det_{n\,2}(Y).

∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Assume that
n\geq 4
. Then a linear map
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
defined by

\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}\sum\limits^{n-1}_{i=2}(-1)^{i}x_{i\,1}+\sum\limits^{n}_{i=2}(-1)^{i}x_{i\,2}&x_{1\,2}\\
x_{2\,1}&x_{2\,2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{(n-1)\,1}&x_{(n-1)\,2}\\
x_{n\,1}&\sum\limits^{n-1}_{i=2}(-1)^{i-1}x_{i\,2}+\sum\limits^{n-1}_{i=1}(-1)^{i-1}x_{i\,1}\end{pmatrix}

is a linear map preserving
\det_{n\,2}
which does not have the form
X\mapsto AXB+\phi(X)
for any
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,n}({\mathbb{F}}),

B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
and any linear map
\phi\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}})\to\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{1}&a_{2}\end{pmatrix}\mid a_{1},a_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}\}.

Proof.

Let us show that
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
preserves
\det_{n\,2}
. Indeed, for a given matrix
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
the matrix
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(X)
is equal to
Y
defined in Lemma
7.1
and consequently
\det_{n\,2}(\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(X))=\det_{n\,2}(X)
.

Let us prove by contradiction that
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
does not have the form
X\mapsto AXB+\phi(X)
for any
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,n}({\mathbb{F}}),

B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
and
\phi
described in the statement of the lemma. Suppose that
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(X)=AXB+\phi(X)
for some
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,n}({\mathbb{F}}),

B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
and all
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}})
. Then the properties of rank of the product of matrices imply that

\rk(\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(X)-\phi(X))=\rk(AXB)\leq\rk(X)\quad\mbox{for all}\;\;X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}}).

(7.7)

Let
X^{\prime}=E_{2\,1}
and
Y=\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(X^{\prime})-\phi(X^{\prime})
. Then

Y=E_{1\,1}+E_{2\,1}-E_{n\,2}-\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{1}&a_{2}\end{pmatrix}

for some
a_{1},a_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}
by the definition of
Y
.

Since
n\geq 4
, then

\det_{2}\Bigl(Y[1,3|)\Bigr)-\det_{2}\Bigl(Y[1,n|)\Bigr)+\det_{2}\Bigl(Y[3,n|)\Bigr)\\
=\begin{vmatrix}1-a_{1}&-a_{2}\\
-a_{1}&-a_{2}\end{vmatrix}-\begin{vmatrix}1-a_{1}&-a_{2}\\
-a_{1}&-1-a_{2}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}-a_{1}&-a_{2}\\
-a_{1}&-1-a_{2}\end{vmatrix}\\
=\begin{vmatrix}1-a_{1}&0\\
-a_{1}&1\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}-a_{1}&-a_{2}\\
-a_{1}&-1-a_{2}\end{vmatrix}\\
=(1-a_{1})+(-a_{1})((-1-a_{2})-(-a_{2}))=1,

which means that one of the three determinants in the equality above is nonzero and consequently

\rk\left(\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(X^{\prime})-\phi(X^{\prime})\right)=2>1=\rk\left(X^{\prime}\right)

which contradicts (
7.7
). Thus,
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
does not have the desired form.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

Note that if
k=2
, then
\det_{n\,2}
is a non-degenerate quadratic form. This implies in particular that every linear map preserving
\det_{n\,2}
could be expressed as a composition of reflections (see
[
8
, §4. Witt’s Theorem]
). One may see that the quadratic space
\left({\mathcal{M}}_{n\,2}({\mathbb{F}}),\det_{n\,2}\right)
is in fact a
split
(or
metabolic
) quadratic subspace, i.e. has a totally isotropic subspace of dimension
n
spanned by matrices
E_{1\,1},\ldots,E_{n\,1}
(see
[
8
, §6. Split Inner Product Spaces]
).

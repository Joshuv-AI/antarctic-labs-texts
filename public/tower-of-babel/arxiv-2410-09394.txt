Probabilistic degenerate derangement polynomials
Taekyun Kim*, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. In combinatorics, a derangement is a permutation of the elements of a set, such that no element appears in its original position. The number of derangements of an n n -element set is called the n n th derangement number. Recently, the degenerate derangement numbers and polynomials have been studied as degenerate versions. Let Y Y be a random variable whose moment generating function exists in a neighborhood of the origin. In this paper, we study probabilistic extension of the degenerate derangement numbers and polynomials, namely the probabilistic degenerate derangement numbers and polynomials associated with Y Y . In addition, we consider the probabilistic degenerate r r -derangement numbers associated with Y Y and the probabilistic degenerate derangement polynomilas of the second kind associated with Y Y . We derive some properties, explicit expressions, certain identities and recurrence relations for those polynomials and numbers.

1.
Introduction

A derangement is a permutation with no fixed points. In other words, a derangement of a set leaves no elements in their original places. The number of derangements of a set of size
n
, denoted by
d_{n}
, is called the
n
th derangement number. As a natural extension, one considers the derangement polynomials (see (
6
)). In [4], Carlitz initiated to study the degenerate Bernoulli and degenerate Euler polynomials which are respectively degenerate versions of the Bernoulli and Euler polynomials. In fact, various degenerate versions of many special polynomials and numbers have been investigated in recent years.
Especially, the degenerate derangement polynomials were introduced as degenerate version of the derangement polynomials (see [8,12,18]). Assume that
Y
is a random variable satisfying the moment condition (see (
12
)). Here we study the probabilistic degenerate derangement polynomials associated with
Y
, as probabilistic extension of the degenerate derangement polynomials.

The aim of this paper is to study, as probabilistic extensions of degenerate derangement polynomials, the probabilistic degenerate derangement polynomials associated with
Y
, along with the probabilistic degenerate
r
-derangement numbers associated with
Y
and the probabilistic degenerate derangement polynomials of the second kind associated with
Y
. We derive some properties, explicit expressions, certain identities and recurrence relations for those polynomials and numbers. In addition, as special cases of
Y
, we consider the gamma random variable with parameters
\alpha,\beta>0
.

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponentials are defined by

(1)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}t^{k},\quad e_{\lambda}^{1}=e_{\lambda}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,13,14]),

where

(2)

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad(x)_{k,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots(x-(k-1)\lambda),\quad(k\geq 1).

In addition, we use the following notation

(3)

\langle{x\rangle}_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad\langle{x\rangle}_{k,\lambda}=x(x+\lambda)(x+2\lambda)\cdots(x+(k-1)\lambda),\quad(k\geq 1).

The Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(4)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-26]),

where

(5)

(x)_{0}=1,\quad(x)_{n}=x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-n+1),\quad(n\geq 1).

The unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind are given by

{n\brack k}=(-1)^{n-k}S_{1}(n,k),\quad(n\geq k\geq 0).

The generating function of the derangement polynomials is given by

(6)

\frac{1}{1-t}e^{(x-1)t}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
d_{n}=d_{n}(0)
are the derangement numbers (see [5,8,10-12,18,20,21]).

The degenerate derangement polynomials are defined by

(7)

\frac{e_{\lambda}^{x-1}(t)}{1-t}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8,12,18]).

When
x=0
,
d_{n,\lambda}=d_{n,\lambda}(0)
are called the degenerate derangement numbers.

The degenerate derangement polynomials of the second kind are defined by

(8)

\frac{1}{1-xt}e_{\lambda}^{-1}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12]).

In particular, for
x=1
, we note that
D_{n,\lambda}(1)=d_{n,\lambda},\ (n\geq 0)
.

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by Kim-Kim as

(9)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10]).

Note that
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}={n\brace k}
are the Stirling numbers of the second kind.

The Fubini polynomials are given by

\frac{1}{1-x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5-7,9,24]).

Kim-Kim introduced the degenerate Fubini polynomials given by

(10)

\frac{1}{1-x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16,17]).

Thus, by (
10
), we get

(11)

F_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}k!x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]).

Let
Y
be a random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y

(12)

E\big[e^{tY}\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E\big[Y^{n}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(|t|<r)

exists for some
r>0
.

Assume that
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
, and
S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\ (k\geq 1)
, with
S_{0}=0
.

The probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
are given by

(13)

{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[(S_{j})_{n,\lambda}\big],\quad(n\geq k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16,22]).

Note that
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}={n\brace k}_{Y}
are the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
(see [3]).

In [16], the probabilistic degenerate Bell polynomials associated with
Y
are defined by

(14)

\phi_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

Recently, the probabilistic degenerate Fubini polynomials associated with
Y
are given by

(15)

\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]).

When
Y=1
,
F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=F_{n,\lambda}(x),\ (n\geq 0)
.

2.
Probabilistic degenerate derangement polynomials associated with the random variable
Y

A continuous random variable
Y
whose density function is given by

(16)

f(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\beta e^{-\beta y}\frac{(\beta y)^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)},&\textrm{if $y\geq 0$,}\\
0,&\textrm{if $y<0$,}\end{array}\right.\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [26]),

for some
\alpha,\beta>0
is said to be the gamma random variable with parameters
\alpha,\beta
, which is denoted by
Y\sim\Gamma(\alpha,\beta)
.

Let
(Y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
, and let

S_{0}=0,\quad S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}).

Now, we define the
probabilistic degenerate derangement polynomials associated with
Y
by

(17)

\frac{1}{1-t}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{x-Y}(t)\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
Y=1
, we have
d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=d_{n,\lambda}(x)
. In particular, for
x=0
,
d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}=d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(0)
are called the
probabilistic degenerate derangement numbers associated with
Y
.

From (
17
), we note that

(18)

\displaystyle E\big[e_{\lambda}^{x-Y}(t)\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}d_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}(1-t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}d_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}kd_{k-1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)-nd_{n-1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by Taylor expansion, we get

(19)

E\big[e_{\lambda}^{x-Y}(t)\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[(x-Y)_{n,\lambda}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
18
) and (
19
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

E\big[(x-Y)_{n,\lambda}\big]=d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)-nd_{n-1}^{Y}(x).

In particular, for
x=0
, we get

(-1)^{n}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]=d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}-nd_{n-1,\lambda}^{Y},\ (n\geq 1).

By (
17
), we have

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{x-Y}(t)\big]=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}t^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}E\big[(x-Y)_{k,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{E\big[(x-Y)_{k,\lambda}\big]}{k!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

and also have

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(t)\big]e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}d_{l,\lambda}^{Y}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(x)_{m,\lambda}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}d_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(x)_{n-l,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
20
) and (
21
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}d_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(x)_{n-l,\lambda}=n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{E[(x-Y)_{k,\lambda}]}{k!}.

Replacing
t
by
1-e_{\lambda}(t)
in (
17
), we get

(22)

\displaystyle E\big[e_{\lambda}^{x-Y}(1-e_{\lambda}(t))\big]

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}(t)\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}d_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{1}{l!}(1-e_{\lambda}(t))^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(1)_{m,\lambda}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}d_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(x)(-1)^{l}\sum_{j=l}^{\infty}{j\brace l}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(1)_{m,\lambda}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{j}d_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(x)(-1)^{l}{j\brace l}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{j}\binom{n}{j}{j\brace l}_{\lambda}(1)_{n-j,\lambda}(-1)^{l}d_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by Taylor expansion, we get

(23)

\displaystyle E\big[e_{\lambda}^{x-Y}(1-e_{\lambda}(t))\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}E\big[(x-Y)_{k,\lambda}\big]\frac{1}{k!}\big(1-e_{\lambda}(t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}E\big[(x-Y)_{k,\lambda}\big](-1)^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}E\big[(x-Y)_{k,\lambda}\big](-1)^{k}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
22
) and (
23
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{j}\binom{n}{j}{j\brace l}_{\lambda}(1)_{n-j,\lambda}(-1)^{l}d_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}E\big[(x-Y)_{k,\lambda}\big](-1)^{k}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}.

From (
7
), we have

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2-E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]}e_{\lambda}^{x-1}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}d_{l,\lambda}(x)\frac{1}{l!}\Big(E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)\big]-1\Big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}d_{l,\lambda}(x)\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}{n\brace l}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{n}d_{l,\lambda}(x){n\brace l}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By (
15
), we get

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{e_{\lambda}^{x-1}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\big)}{2-E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}e_{\lambda}^{x-1}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}F_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(1)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(x-1)_{m,\lambda}\frac{1}{m!}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}F_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(1)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(x-1)_{m,\lambda}\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}{j\brace m}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}F_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(1)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{j}(x-1)_{m,\lambda}{j\brace m}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{j}\binom{n}{j}{j\brace m}_{Y,\lambda}F_{n-j,\lambda}^{Y}(1)(x-1)_{m,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
24
) and (
25
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{l=0}^{n}d_{l,\lambda}(x){n\brace l}_{Y,\lambda}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{j}\binom{n}{j}{j\brace m}_{Y,\lambda}F_{n-j,\lambda}^{Y}(1)(x-1)_{m,\lambda}.

Replacing
t
by
1-E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]
in (
7
), we get

(26)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{x-1}\big(1-E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}d_{k,\lambda}(x)\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\big)^{k}E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}d_{k,\lambda}(x)(-1)^{k}\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}{m\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}E\big[(Y)_{j,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}d_{k,\lambda}(x){m\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}E\big[(Y)_{j,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{m}{m\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}d_{k,\lambda}(x)E[(Y)_{n-m,\lambda}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by Taylor expansion, we get

(27)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{x-1}\big(1-E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x-1)_{k,\lambda}(-1)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x-1)_{k,\lambda}(-1)^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}(x-1)_{k,\lambda}(-1)^{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
26
) and (
27
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}(x-1)_{k,\lambda}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{m}{m\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}E[(Y)_{n-m,\lambda}]d_{k,\lambda}(x).

The probabilistic degenerate Euler numbers associated with
Y
are defined by

(28)

\frac{2}{E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]+1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
Y=1
,
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{Y}=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}
are Carlitz’s degenerate Euler numbers (see [4]).

By (
6
), we get

(29)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]}e^{(1-x)E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}d_{m}(x)(-1)^{m}\frac{1}{m!}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}d_{m}(x)(-1)^{m}\frac{1}{m!}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{m}}(t)\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}d_{m}(x)(-1)^{m}\frac{1}{m!}E\big[(S_{m})_{n,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, again by (
6
), we get

(30)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]}e^{(1-x)E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{1-x}}{2}\frac{2}{1+E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]}e^{(1-x)(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{1-x}}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\phi_{m,\lambda}^{Y}(1-x)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{l,\lambda}^{Y}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{1-x}}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\phi_{m,\lambda}^{Y}(1-x)\mathcal{E}_{n-m,\lambda}^{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
29
) and (
30
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\phi_{m,\lambda}^{Y}(1-x)\mathcal{E}_{n-m,\lambda}^{Y}=2e^{x-1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}d_{m}(x)E\big[(S_{m})_{n,\lambda}\big].

For
r\geq 0
, let us consider the
probabilistic degenerate
r
-derangement numbers associated with
Y
given by

(31)

\frac{t^{r}}{(1-t)^{r+1}}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(t)\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
r=0
,
d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}=d_{n,\lambda}^{(0,Y)},\ (n\geq 0)
.

From (
31
), we note that

(32)

\displaystyle\frac{t^{r}}{(1-t)^{r+1}}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(t)\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+r}{r}t^{k+r}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{m,\lambda}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}\binom{k}{r}t^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}E[\langle Y\rangle_{m,\lambda}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}n!\sum_{k=r}^{n}\binom{k}{r}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{E[\langle Y\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}]}{(n-k)!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
31
) and (
32
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

For
n,r\geq 0
, we have

d_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}=n!\sum_{k=r}^{n}\binom{k}{r}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{E[\langle Y\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}]}{(n-k)!},\,\,\textrm{if\, $n\geq r$}\,;\quad d_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}=0,\,\,\textrm{if\, $0\leq n\leq r-1$}.

By (
31
) and Theorem 2.7, we get

(33)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}d_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{t}{1-t}\Big)^{r}\frac{1}{1-t}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(t)\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}\binom{l-1}{r-1}t^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}d_{m,\lambda}^{Y}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}n!\sum_{l=r}^{n}\binom{l-1}{r-1}\frac{d_{n-l,\lambda}^{Y}}{(n-l)!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
33
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.8
.

For
n\geq r
, we have

d_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}=n!\sum_{l=r}^{n}\binom{l-1}{r-1}\frac{d_{n-l,\lambda}^{Y}}{(n-l)!}.

From (
31
), we have

(34)

\displaystyle E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(t)\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t^{r}}(1-t)^{r+1}\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}d_{k,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}=(1-t)^{r+1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}d_{k+r,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}\frac{t^{k}}{(k+r)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\binom{r+1}{l}t^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}d_{k+r,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}\frac{t^{k}}{(k+r)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{d_{k+r,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}}{(k+r)!}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{r+1}{n-k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by Taylor expansion, we get

(35)

E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(t)\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
34
) and (
35
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.9
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]=n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{d_{k+r,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}}{(k+r)!}(-1)^{k}\binom{r+1}{n-k}.

Now, we define the
probabilistic degenerate derangement polynomials of the second kind associated with
Y
by

(36)

\frac{1}{1-xt}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(t)\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=1
, we have
D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(1)=d_{n,\lambda}^{Y},\ (n\geq 0)
.

By (
36
), we get

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-xt}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(t)\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}x^{m}t^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{k,\lambda}]t^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{k,\lambda}\big]x^{n-k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

and

(38)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(t)\big]=(1-xt)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=D_{0,\lambda}^{Y}(x)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)-nxD_{n-1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
37
) and (
38
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.10
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{k,\lambda}\big]x^{n-k}.

Moreover, we have the recurrence relation given by

D_{0,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=1,\quad D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=nxD_{n-1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)+(-1)^{n}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n,\lambda}\big],\quad(n\geq 1).

Replacing
t
by
e_{\lambda}(t)-1
in (
36
), we get

(39)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}D_{m,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{1}{m!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}D_{m,\lambda}^{Y}(x){n\brace m}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, we get

(40)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{k,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\frac{1}{l!}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{l,\lambda}\big](e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{k,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{l,\lambda}\big]\sum_{j=l}^{\infty}{j\brace l}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{k,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{j}(-1)^{j}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{k,\lambda}\big]{j\brace l}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{j}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{l,\lambda}\big]{j\brace l}_{\lambda}F_{n-j,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
39
) and (
40
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.11
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{m=0}^{n}D_{m,\lambda}^{Y}(x){n\brace m}_{\lambda}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{j}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{l,\lambda}\big]{j\brace l}_{\lambda}F_{n-j,\lambda}(x).

From (
36
), we note that

(41)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{-1}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-xt)\big)E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(t)\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle 1\rangle_{l,\lambda}}{l!}(-1)^{l}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-xt)\big)^{l}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-Y}(t)\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\langle 1\rangle_{l,\lambda}\sum_{j=l}^{\infty}{j\brack l}_{\lambda}x^{j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{k,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{j}\langle 1\rangle_{l,\lambda}{j\brack l}_{\lambda}x^{j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{k,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{j}\langle 1\rangle_{l,\lambda}{j\brack l}_{\lambda}\binom{n}{j}x^{j}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n-j,\lambda}\big](-1)^{n-j}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
\log_{\lambda}(t)
is the compositional inverse of
e_{\lambda}(t)
satisfying

\log_{\lambda}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)\big)=e_{\lambda}(\log_{\lambda}(t)\big)=t,

and
{n\brack j}_{\lambda}
are the unsigned degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind (see [15]). Therefore, by (
41
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.12
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{j}\binom{n}{j}{j\brack l}_{\lambda}(-1)^{n-j}\langle 1\rangle_{l,\lambda}E\big[\langle Y\rangle_{n-j,\lambda}\big]x^{j}.

Let
Y\sim\Gamma(1,1)
. Then, by (
6
), we have

(42)

\displaystyle E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-x-Y}(t)\big]

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{-x}(t)\int_{0}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}^{-y}(t)e^{-y}dy

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{-x}(t)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y\big(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)\big)}dy

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{-x}(t)\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)}e^{(1-x)\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)}e_{\lambda}^{-1}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}d_{k}(x)\frac{1}{k!}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\lambda^{k}}\big(\log(1+\lambda t)\big)^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)_{m,\lambda}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{d_{k}(x)}{\lambda^{k}}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(j,k)\frac{\lambda^{j}t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)_{m,\lambda}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}\lambda^{j-k}d_{k}(x)S_{1}(j,k)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)_{m,\lambda}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{k}\lambda^{j-k}d_{k}(x)S_{1}(j,k)(-1)_{n-j,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
t>\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{-\lambda}-1)
.

On the other hand, by Taylor expansion, we get

(43)

E\big[e_{\lambda}^{-x-Y}(t)\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}E\big[\langle{x+Y\rangle}_{n,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
42
) and (
43
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.13
.

For
Y\sim\Gamma(1,1)
, and
n\geq 0
, we have

E\big[\langle{x+Y\rangle}_{n,\lambda}\big]=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-k}\lambda^{j-k}S_{1}(j,k)(-1)_{n-j,\lambda}d_{k,\lambda}(x).

3.
Conclusion

Assume that
Y
is a random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y
exists in a neighborhood of the origin. In this paper, we studied by using generating functions probabilistic extensions of several special polynomials and numbers, namely the probabilistic degenerate derangement polynomials and numbers associated with
Y
, together with the probabilistic degenerate
r
-derangement numbers associated with
Y
and the probabilistic degenerate derangement polynomials of the second kind associated with
Y
.

In more detail, we obtained an explicit expression for
d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
in Theorem 2.2, that for
d_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}
in Theorem 2.7 and explicit expressions for
D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
in Theorems 2.10 and 2.12. A recurrence relation was derived for
d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
in Theorem 2.1 and for
d_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}
in Theorem 2.8.
We deduced finite sum identities involving
d_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
,
d_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}
, and
D_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
, respectively in Theorem 2.3, Theorem 2.9, and Theorem 2.11. Finite sum identities were obtained for
d_{n,\lambda}(x)
in Theorems 2.4 and 2.5, and also in Theorem 2.13 when
Y\sim\Gamma(1,1)
. In Theorem 2.6, we expressed a finite sum involving
\phi_{m,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
and
\mathcal{E}_{m,\lambda}^{Y}
, as an infinite sum involving
d_{m}(x)
.

It is one of our future projects to continue to study probabilistic versions of many special polynomials and numbers and to find their applications to physics, science and engineering as well as to mathematics.

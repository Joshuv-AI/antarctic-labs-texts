Some extensions from famous theorems for h h -mid-convex function
Amir Garejelo 1 , Farzollah Mirzapour 1 and Ali Morassaei 1

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove that every continuous h h -mid-convex with suitable conditions on h h is h h -convex function. Also, we extend Ostrowski theorem, Blumberg-Sierpinski theorem, Bernstein-Doetsch theorem, Mehdi theorem.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
26A51, 26D15.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we assume that
I,J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
are intervals such that
0\in J
. A function
f:I\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called
convex function
, if for every
x,y\in I
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
the following inequality holds:

f\big(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\big)\leq\lambda f(x)+(1-\lambda)f(y)\,.

The function
f
is said to be
concave
, if
-f
is convex. The function
f
is called
J-convex (Jensen convex function)
or
mid-convex
, if for every
x,y\in I
,

f\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)\leq\frac{f(x)+f(y)}{2}\,.

We say that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}
is an
rational convex combination
of the points
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in I
, if
\lambda_{i}
is non-negative rational number for
i=1,\cdots,n
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
.

It is obvious that
f
is mid-convex on
I
, if and only if for any rational convex combination of points in
I
,

f\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}f(x_{i})\,.

(1.1)

Inequality (
1.1
) is called
Jensen’s inequality
. We know that if
f
is mid-convex, then such functions need not be continuous, but that when they are, they will be convex.

Chademan and Mirzapour in year 1999
[
3
]
, defined the mid-convex function on a topological group and then extended the theorems of Bernstein-Doetsch, and Ostrowski, concerning the continuity of mid-convex functions are extended to open subsets of locally compact and root-approximable topological groups.

Let us recall definitions of some special classes of functions.

We say that
[
7
]

f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is a
Godunova-Levin function
or that
f
belongs to the class
Q(I)
if
f
is non-negative and for all
x,y\in I
and
t\in(0,1)
we have

f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq\frac{f(x)}{t}+\frac{f(y)}{1-t}\,.

For
s\in(0,1]
, a function
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is said to be
s
-convex
, or that
f
belongs to the class
K_{s}^{2}
, if

f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq t^{s}f(x)+(1-t)^{s}f(y)

for every
x,y\in[0,\infty)
and
t\in[0,1]
(see
[
2
]
). Also, we say that
f:I\to[0,\infty)
is a
P
-function

[
6
]
, or that
f
belongs to the class
P(I)
, if for all
x,y\in I
and
t\in[0,1]
it holds that

f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq f(x)+f(y)\,.

In
[
11
]
, Varošanec defined the
h
-convex function as follows:

Let
h:J\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative function,
h\not\equiv 0
. We say that
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is a
h
-convex function
, or that
f
belongs to the class
SX(h,I)
, if
f
is non-negative and for all
x,y\in I
,
t\in(0,1)
we have

f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq h(t)f(x)+h(1-t)f(y)\,.

(1.2)

If inequality (
1.2
) is reversed, then
f
is said to be
h
-concave
, that is,
f\in SV(h,I)
.

If
h(t)=t
, then
SX(h,I)
is exactly equal to the set of all non-negative convex functions defined on the interval
I
, and the set of all non-negative concave functions is equal to
SV(h,I)
. If
h(t)=\frac{1}{t}
, then
SX(h,I)=Q(I)
; if
h(t)=1
, then
SX(h,I)\supseteq P(I)
; and if
h(t)=t^{s}
, where
s\in(0,1)
, then
SX(h,I)\supseteq K_{s}^{2}
.

Also,
f
is called
h
-mid-convex function
, if for every
x,y\in I
, the following inequality holds:

f\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)\leq h\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)\big(f(x)+f(y)\big)\,.

If the interval
J
is closed under addition, then a function
h:J\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be a
super-additive function
if

h(x+y)\geq h(x)+h(y)\,,

(1.3)

for all
x,y\in J
. If inequality (
1.3
) is reversed, then
h
is said to be a
sub-additive function
. If the equality holds in (
1.3
), then
h
is said to be an
additive function
.

If the domain of a function
h
is closed under multiplication, then
h
is called a
super-multiplicative function
if

h(xy)\geq h(x)h(y)\,,

(1.4)

for all
x,y\in J

[
11
]
. If inequality (
1.4
) is reversed, then
h
is called a
sub-multiplicative function
. If the equality holds in (
1.4
), then
h
is called a
multiplicative function
.

Example 1.1
.

[
11
]

Consider the function
h:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by
h(x)=(c+x)^{p-1}
, where
p
is a non-negative real number. If
c=0
, then the function
h
is multiplicative. If
c\geq 1
, then for
p\in(0,1)
the function
h
is super-multiplicative and for
p>1
the function
h
is sub-multiplicative.

Sarikaya et al. presented some inequalities Hadamard-type for
h
-convex functions. They also proved some Hadamard-type inequalities for products of two
h
-convex functions
[
10
]
.

Dragomir gave some inequalities of Hermite-Hadamard type for
h
-convex functions defined on convex subsets in real or complex linear spaces. Also, he provides applications for norm inequalities
[
5
]
.

Darvish et al.
[
4
]
introduced the concept of operator
h
-convex functions for positive linear maps, and proved some Hermite-Hadamard type inequalities for these functions. As applications, they obtained several trace inequalities for operators.

In 2022
[
1
]
, Abbasi and et al. give some characterizations of
h
-convex functions and then generalize the Jensen-Mercer inequality for these functions. Also, the authors present a refinement of Jensen’s inequality for
h
-convex function.

Morassaei and Mirzapour in
[
8
]
stated some integral inequalities for
h
-log-convex functions involving classical Hermite–Hadamard inequality. Also, by using notations of Mond–Pěcarić method in operator inequalities, they extended some inequalities for operator
h
-log-convex functions.

In this paper, we show that any
h
-mid-convex function under suitable conditions is
h
-convex. Then, we extend Jensen’s theorem, Bernstein-Doetsch theorem, Blumberg-Sierpinski theorem, Ostrowski theorem, and Mehdi theorem for
h
-mid-convex function.

2.
Results on
h
-mid-convexity and
h
-convexity

We know that every continuous mid-convex function is a convex function
[
9
]
. In this section, we give two proofs for the generalization of this statement for
h
-mid-convex function as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a super-multiplicative and continuous function such that
h(t)\geq t
and
f
be a continuous
h
-mid-convex function on
[a,b]
, then
f
is
h
-convex.

Proof.

We prove the theorem in two cases:

Case 1.
For
1\leq i\leq n
, set
\lambda_{i}=\dfrac{1}{n}
and by induction, we show that

f\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)\leq h\big(\dfrac{1}{n}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})\,.

(2.1)

For
n=2
, we have

\displaystyle f(\lambda_{1}x_{1}+\lambda_{2}x_{2})=f\left(\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2}\right)\leq h(\dfrac{1}{2})(f(x_{1})+f(x_{2}))\,,

this inequality is obvious.

We assume that the inequality (
2.1
) holds for
n=2^{k}
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We show that (
2.1
) holds for
m=2n
.

\displaystyle f\bigg(\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}}{m}\bigg)

\displaystyle=f\bigg(\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{2n}x_{i}}{2n}\bigg)=f\bigg(\dfrac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=n+1}^{2n}x_{i}}{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq h(\frac{1}{2})\left(f\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)+f\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=n+1}^{2n}x_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq h(\dfrac{1}{2})h(\dfrac{1}{n})\sum_{i=1}^{2n}f(x_{i})

\displaystyle\leq h(\dfrac{1}{2n})\sum_{i=1}^{2n}f(x_{i})

\displaystyle=h(\dfrac{1}{m})\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(x_{i})\,.

Now, we show that if (
2.1
) is true for
n\geq 3
, then (
2.1
) holds for
n-1
. Define

\displaystyle x_{n}:=\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n-1}}{n-1}\,,

then, we have

\displaystyle f\bigg(\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}{n}\bigg)

\displaystyle=f\bigg(\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}}{n-1}}{n}\bigg)=f\bigg(\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n-1}}{n-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq h(\dfrac{1}{n})\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}f(x_{i})+f\Big(\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}}{n-1}\Big)\bigg)\,.

So,

\bigg(1-h(\dfrac{1}{n})\bigg)f\left(\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n-1}}{n-1}\right)\leq h(\dfrac{1}{n})\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}f(x_{i})\,.

By multiplying two sides of the above inequality to positive number
h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})
, we have

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})-h(\dfrac{1}{n})h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})\bigg)f\bigg(\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}}{n-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\dfrac{1}{n})h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}f(x_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\dfrac{1}{n-1})\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}f(x_{i})\,.

Now, we show that

h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})-h(\dfrac{1}{n})h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})\geq 1\,,

(2.2)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
h
is super-multiplicative function, we get

h(\dfrac{1}{n})h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})h(n)\leq h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})\,.

(2.3)

Now, we reduce two sides of inequality from positive value
h(\dfrac{1}{n})h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})
, we have

h(\dfrac{1}{n})h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})h(n)-h(\dfrac{1}{n})h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})\leq h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})-h(\dfrac{1}{n})h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})\,,

(2.4)

therefore

h(\dfrac{1}{n})h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})\big(h(n)-1\big)\leq h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})-h(\dfrac{1}{n})h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})\,.

(2.5)

Since
h(t)\geq t
for
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have

h(\dfrac{1}{n})h(\dfrac{n}{n-1})\big(h(n)-1)\big)\geq\dfrac{1}{n}\dfrac{n}{n-1}(n-1)=1\,.

Since (
2.1
) holds for every
n=2^{k}~~(k=1,2,3,\cdots)
, and by using the previous relation, (
2.1
) holds for
n=2^{k}-1
. Now, if assumption of induction holds for
n=2^{k}-\ell
, then by similar proof, (
2.1
) holds for
n=2^{k}-\ell-1
. Consequently, (
2.1
) holds for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Case 2.
Let
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}~(i=1,\cdots,n)
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
, then there exist
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
p_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
\lambda_{i}=\frac{p_{i}}{m}
, so
\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}=m
. By Case 1

\displaystyle f\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\right)=

\displaystyle f\bigg(\dfrac{p_{1}x_{1}+p_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+p_{n}x_{n}}{m}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\dfrac{1}{m})\bigg(p_{1}f(x_{1})+p_{2}f(x_{2})+\cdots+p_{n}f(x_{n})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(\dfrac{1}{m})\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}f(x_{i})\,.

Since
h(p_{i})\geq p_{i}
and
h
is super-multiplicative function, we get

f\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\right)\leq h(\dfrac{1}{m})\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(p_{i})f(x_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(\dfrac{p_{i}}{m})f(x_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(\lambda_{i})f(x_{i})\,.

Let
\lambda\in[0,1]
, then there exists a sequence of rational numbers as
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
such that
\lambda=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}
. Therefore, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x,y\in[a,b]
,

f(\lambda_{n}x+(1-\lambda_{n})y)\leq h(\lambda_{n})f(x)+h(1-\lambda_{n})f(y)

So

\displaystyle f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}f(\lambda_{n}x+(1-\lambda_{n})y)\qquad(f~\text{is continuous})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to+\infty}h(\lambda_{n})f(x)+\lim_{n\to+\infty}h(1-\lambda_{n})f(y)

\displaystyle=h(\lambda)f(x)+h(1-\lambda)f(y)\qquad(h~\text{is continuous})\,.

This complete the proof.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

If in the previous theorem, the function
h
is multiplicative, then the theorem holds.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a super-multiplicative function such that
h(t)\geq t
and
f
be a
h
-mid-convex function on
[a,b]
. Then for every
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
,

f\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(\lambda_{i})f(x_{i})\,.

(2.6)

Proof.

First, let
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}
. By using
h
-mid-convexity of
f
, we get

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2}\right)\leq h(\dfrac{1}{2})(f(x_{1})+f(x_{2}))\,.

If
n=4
we have

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}}{4}\right)=

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\dfrac{x_{3}+x_{4}}{2}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\frac{1}{2})\left(f\Big(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2}\Big)+f\Big(\dfrac{x_{3}+x_{4}}{2}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\dfrac{1}{4})\sum_{i=1}^{4}f(x_{i})\,.

Similarly, for
n=2^{k}
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}}{n}\right)\leq h(\frac{1}{n})\big(f(x_{1})+\cdots+f(x_{n})\big)\,.

New, for
n=3
, by setting
x_{4}=\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}}{3}
, we have

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}}{3}\right)=

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{1}{4}\Big(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}}{3}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\frac{1}{4})\sum_{i=1}^{3}f(x_{i})+h(\frac{1}{4})f\left(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}}{3}\right)\,.

So

\left(1-h(\dfrac{1}{4})\right)f\left(\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}}{3}\right)\leq h(\dfrac{1}{4})\sum_{i=1}^{3}f(x_{i})\,.

By multiplication two sides of the above inequality to positive number
h(\frac{4}{3})
, we get

h(\frac{4}{3})\Big(1-h(\frac{1}{4})\Big)f\left(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}}{3}\right)\leq h(\frac{1}{4})h(\frac{4}{3})\sum_{i=1}^{3}f(x_{i})\,.

Since
h(t)
is super-multiplicative function, we have

h(\frac{4}{3})\Big(1-h(\frac{1}{4})\Big)f\left(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}}{3}\right)\leq h(\frac{1}{3})\big(f(x_{1})+f(x_{2})+f(x_{3})\big).

Now, we show that

h(\frac{4}{3})\Big(1-h(\frac{1}{4})\Big)\geq 1\,.

Since
h
is the super-multiplicative function,

h(\frac{1}{4})h(\frac{4}{3})h(4)\leq h(\frac{4}{3})\,.

We subtract the positive real number
h(\frac{1}{4})h(\frac{4}{3})
from both sides of the above inequality,

h(\frac{1}{4})h(\frac{4}{3})(h(4)-1)\leq h(\frac{4}{3})-h(\frac{1}{4})h(\frac{4}{3}).

So

h(\frac{1}{4})h(\frac{4}{3})(h(4)-1)\leq h(\frac{4}{3}\Big)\Big(1-h(\frac{1}{4})\Big).

Since
h(t)\geq t
, we get

h(\frac{4}{3})\Big(1-h(\frac{1}{4})\Big)\geq 1\,.

Now, let
n\neq 2^{k}~(k\in\mathbb{N})
, then there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{m-1}<n<2^{m}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}{n}\right)=

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}{2^{m}}+\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}}\cdot\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\frac{1}{2^{m}})\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})+h(\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}})f\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}{n}\right)\,.

Therefore,

\left(1-h(\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}})\right)f\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}{n}\right)\leq h(\frac{1}{2^{m}})\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})\,.

By multiplication of two sides of the above inequality to positive number
h(\frac{2^{m}}{n})
and by using the assumption super-multiplicity of
h
, we have

h(\frac{2^{m}}{n})\left(1-h(\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}})\right)f\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}{n}\right)\leq h(\frac{1}{n})\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})\,.

Now, we claim that

h(\frac{2^{m}}{n})\left(1-h(\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}})\right)\geq 1\,.

Since
h
is the super-multiplicative function, we get

h\left(\frac{2^{m}}{2^{m}-n}\right)h\left(\frac{2^{m}}{n}\right)h\left(\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}}\right)\leq h\left(\frac{2^{m}}{n}\right)\,.

We subtract the positive real number
h\left(\frac{2^{m}}{n}\right)h\left(\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}}\right)
from both sides of the above inequality,

h\left(\frac{2^{m}}{n}\right)h\left(\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}}\right)\left(h\Big(\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}}\Big)-1\right)\leq h\left(\frac{2^{m}}{n}\right)\left(1-h\Big(\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}}\Big)\right)\,.

Since
h(t)\geq t
so

\displaystyle 1=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{m}}{n}\cdot\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}}\left(\frac{2^{m}}{2^{m}-n}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h\left(\frac{2^{m}}{n}\right)h\left(\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}}\right)\left(h\Big(\frac{2^{m}}{2^{m}-n}\Big)-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h\left(\frac{2^{m}}{n}\right)\left(1-h\Big(\frac{2^{m}-n}{2^{m}}\Big)\right)\,,

therefore,

f\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)\leq h\big(\frac{1}{n}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})\,.

Now, assume that
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[a,b]~(i=1,\cdots,n)
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
. So, there exist
u_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\lambda_{i}=\dfrac{u_{i}}{d}
. Therefore,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}=d
. So

\displaystyle f\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\right)=

\displaystyle f\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{u_{i}x_{i}}{d}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\big(\frac{1}{d}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}f(x_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h\big(\frac{1}{d}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(u_{i})f(x_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}h\big(\frac{u_{i}}{d}\big)f(x_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(\lambda_{i})f(x_{i})\,.

The desired inequality followed by an appeal to the special case proved first.
∎

3.
Some famous Theorems

In this section, we propound some generalizations for Jensen’s theorem, Bernstein-Doetsch theorem, Blumberg-Sierpinski theorem, Ostrowski theorem, and Mehdi theorem for
h
-mid-convex function.

Lemma 3.1

(Jensen’s Theorem)
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
be a multiplicative continuous function such that
h(t)\geq t
,
h(0)=0
and
h(1)=1
. If
f
is a
h
-mid-convex function and
f
is bounded above to
M
on
[a,b]
, then
f
is continuous.

Proof.

Since the function
f
is
h
-mid-convex function, so for every
x_{i}\in(a,b)
,
1\leq i\leq n~(n\in\mathbb{N})
, by Theorem
2.3
,

f\left(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}}{n}\right)\leq h(\frac{1}{n})\big(f(x_{1})+f(x_{2})+\cdots+f(x_{n})\big)\,.

Choose a positive integer
m
such that
m<n
and set

x_{1}=x_{2}=x_{3}=\cdots=x_{m}:=x+m\delta\,,

and

x_{m+1}=x_{m+2}=\cdots=x_{n}:=x\,,

where
x\in(a,b)
and the positive real number
\delta
is chosen such that
x+n\delta\in(a,b)
. Therefore

\displaystyle f(x+m\delta)=

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{m(x+n\delta)+(n-m)x}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\frac{m}{n})f(x+n\delta)+h(\frac{n-m}{n})f(x)

By multiplication of two sides of the above inequality to positive number
h(\frac{1}{m})
and by using the assumption super-multiplicity of
h
, we have

\displaystyle h\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)

\displaystyle\big(f(x+m\delta)-f(x)+f(x)\big)

\displaystyle\leq h\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\big(f(x+n\delta)-f(x)+f(x)\big)+h\left(\frac{n-m}{nm}\right)f(x)\,.

Now, we show that

\displaystyle h\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)

\displaystyle\big(f(x+m\delta)-f(x)\big)

(3.1)

\displaystyle\leq h\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\big(f(x+n\delta)-f(x)\big)+f(x)\bigg(h\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)+h\Big(\frac{n-m}{nm}\Big)-h\Big(\dfrac{1}{m}\Big)\bigg)\,.

Now, by replacing
\delta
with
-\delta
be such that
x-n\delta\in(a,b)
, we have

\displaystyle h\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)

\displaystyle\big(f(x)-f(x-m\delta)\big)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\geq h\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\big(f(x)-f(x-n\delta)\big)+f(x)\bigg(h\Big(\frac{1}{m}\Big)-h\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)-h\Big(\frac{n-m}{nm}\Big)\bigg)\,.

Due to
h
-mid-convexity of
f
, for every
x\in(a,b)
, we have

f(x)=f\left(\frac{x+m\delta+x-m\delta}{2}\right)\leq h(\dfrac{1}{2})\big(f(x+m\delta)+f(x-m\delta)\big)\,.

Since
h(t)\geq t
and
h
is super-multiplicative,

2f(x)\leq h(2)f(x)\leq f(x+m\delta)+f(x-m\delta)\,.

So

f(x+m\delta)-f(x)\geq f(x)-f(x-m\delta)\,,

By multiplication two sides of the above inequality to positive number
h(\frac{1}{m})
, we have

h(\dfrac{1}{m})\bigg(f(x+m\delta)-f(x)\bigg)\geq h(\dfrac{1}{m})\bigg(f(x)-f(x-m\delta)\bigg)

(3.3)

Applying (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle h(\frac{1}{n})\big(f(x+n\delta)-f(x)\big)+f(x)\left(h\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)+h\Big(\frac{n-m}{nm}\Big)-h\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\geq h(\frac{1}{m})\big(f(x+m\delta)-f(x)\big)

\displaystyle\geq h(\frac{1}{m})\big(f(x)-f(x-m\delta)\big)

\displaystyle\geq h(\frac{1}{n})\big(f(x)-f(x-n\delta)\big)+f(x)\left(h\Big(\frac{1}{m}\Big)-h\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)-h\Big(\frac{n-m}{nm}\Big)\right)\,.

Since
f
is bounded above to
M
on
[a,b]
and by putting
m=1
, we get

\displaystyle h(\frac{1}{n})\big(M-f(x)\big)+f(x)\left(h\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)+h\Big(\frac{n-1}{n}\Big)-1\right)\geq f(x+\delta)-f(x)

\displaystyle\geq f(x)-f(x-\delta)

\displaystyle\geq h(\frac{1}{n})(f(x)-M)+f(x)\bigg(1-h\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)-h\Big(\frac{n-1}{n}\Big)\bigg)\,.

Now, if
\delta\to 0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
can be chosen so large that
x\pm n\delta\in(a,b)
, therefore, it can be assumed that
n\to\infty
. So,

\lim_{|\delta|\to 0}|f(x+\delta)-f(x)|=0\,.

Hence, the function
f
is continuous.
∎

Theorem 3.2

(Bernstein-Doetsch Theorem)
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
be a super-multiplicative continuous function and, bounded to
M^{\prime}
such that
h(t)\geq t
and
h(0)=0
and
f
be a
h
-mid-convex function on
(a,b)
. If
f
is bounded above to
M
in a neighborhood of the point
x_{0}\in(a,b)
, then
f
is continuous.

Proof.

Without lose of generality,
x_{0}=0\in(a,b)
and
f
is bounded above to
M
on
(-r,r)\subseteq(a,b)
,
f(0)=0
. Let
t\in(0,1)
be a rational number. Then choose a real number
x
such that
|x|\leq tr
. We have

x=(1-t)0+t(\frac{x}{t})\,,

and

0=\frac{t}{1+t}(-\frac{x}{t})+\dfrac{x}{1+t}\,.

Since
f
is
h
-mid-convex function and
f(0)=0
, we get

f(x)=f\left((1-t)0+t\Big(\frac{x}{t}\Big)\right)\leq h(t)f\Big(\frac{x}{t}\Big)\leq h(t)M\,,

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle f(0)=f\left(\frac{-tx}{t(1+t)}+\frac{x}{1+t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h\Big(\frac{t}{1+t}\Big)f\left(\frac{-x}{t}\right)+h\Big(\frac{1}{1+t}\Big)f(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h\Big(\frac{t}{1+t}\Big)M+h\Big(\frac{1}{1+t}\Big)f(x)\,.

So

-h\Big(\frac{t}{1+t}\Big)M\geq h\Big(\frac{1}{1+t}\Big)f(x)\,.

(3.5)

Therefore,
f(x)\geq-h(t)M
, and by using (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we have
|f(x)|\leq h(t)M
. This shows that
f
is continuous at
x=0
. Since
f
is bounded from above on
N_{r}(0)
, we will show that
f
is bounded above in a neighborhood of any other point
y\neq 0\in(a,b)
. Choose a rational number
s>1
such that
z=sy\in(a,b)
and set
\lambda:=\dfrac{1}{s}
. Then

M=\big\{v\in\mathbb{R}~:~v=(1-\lambda)x+\lambda z;~x\in(-r,r)\big\}\,,

is a neighborhood of
y=\lambda z
with radius
(1-\lambda)r
. Moreover,

\displaystyle f(v)

\displaystyle\leq h(1-\lambda)f(x)+h(\lambda)f(z)

\displaystyle\leq M^{\prime}f(x)+M^{\prime}f(z)

\displaystyle\leq M^{\prime}M+M^{\prime}f(z)\,,

that is,
f
is bounded above on
U
and by the Jensen’s theorem,
f
is continuous at
y
.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Blumberg-Sierpinski Theorem)
.

If
h:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
is a continuous and super-multiplicative function such that
h(t)\geq t
and
f:(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}
is measurable and
h
-mid-convex function, then
f
is continuous on (a,b).

Proof.

We use proof by contradiction. Let
f
be a discontinuous in a point
x_{0}\in(a,b)
and
c>0
such that
(x_{0}-2c,x_{0}+2c)\subseteq(a,b)
. Set

B_{n}:=\left\{x\in(a,b)~:~f(x)>\frac{n}{h(\frac{1}{2})}\right\},\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N})\,.

It is obvious that
B_{n}
is a measurable set. For fixed
n
, with the Bernstien-Doetsch theorem,
B_{n}\neq\varnothing
. So, take
u\in B_{n}\cap(x_{0}-c,x_{0}+c)
. Now, for any
\lambda\in[0,1]

\frac{n}{h(\frac{1}{2})}<f(u)=f\left(\frac{u+\lambda c}{2}+\frac{u-\lambda c}{2}\right)\leq h(\frac{1}{2})\Big(f(u+\lambda c)+f(u-\lambda c)\Big)\,.

It follows that either
\frac{n}{h(\frac{1}{2})}\leq h(\frac{1}{2})\big(2f(u+\lambda c)\big)
or
\frac{n}{h(\frac{1}{2})}\leq h(\frac{1}{2})\big(2f(u-\lambda c)\big)
. Since
h(t)\geq t
and it is super-multiplicative, we have
\frac{n}{h(\frac{1}{2})}\leq f(u+\lambda c)
or
\frac{n}{h(\frac{1}{2})}\leq f(u-\lambda c)
. Thus, either
u+\lambda c
or
u-\lambda c
belongs to
B_{n}
, or equivalently, if
M_{n}:=\{x~:~x=y-u,~y\in B_{n}\}
, then either
\lambda c
or
-\lambda c
belongs to
M_{n}
for any
\lambda\in[0,1]
. We claim that
c\leq m(M_{n})=m(B_{n})
. Because, set
A_{1}:=M_{n}\cap[-c,0]
,
A_{2}:=M_{n}\cap[0,c]
. Then
-A_{1}\cap A_{2}=[0,c]
. So

c=m[0,c]\leq m(-A_{1})+m(A_{2})=m(A_{1})+m(A_{2})=m(A_{1}\cup A_{2})\leq m(M_{n})\,.

On the other hands,
B_{1}\supseteq B_{2}\supseteq B_{3}\supseteq\cdots
, therefore

c\leq\lim_{n\to+\infty}m(B_{n})=m\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{+\infty}B_{i}\right)\,,

and so
\cap_{i=1}^{+\infty}B_{i}\neq\emptyset
. This means that there is a point
t\in(a,b)
such that
f(t)>\frac{n}{h(\frac{1}{2})}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and this impossible.

To complete the proof, we consider a measurable set
M
for which
-\lambda c
or
\lambda c
is in
M
for each
\lambda\in[0,1]

∎

Theorem 3.4

(Ostrowski Theorem)
.

If
h:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
is a super-multiplicative function such that
h(t)\geq t
,
h(1)=1
and
f:(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}
is
h
-mid-convex function and
M\subseteq(a,b)
has positive Lebesgue measure in
(a,b)
such that
f
is bounded above on
M
, then
f
is continuous.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
f\leq 0
on
M
. Since
m(M)>0
, we can cover
M
by disjoint open intervals as
T_{n}
such that

m(M)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}m(T_{n})<\frac{4}{3}m(M)\,.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the sets
M\cap T_{n}
are measurable and disjoint, therefore

\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}m(M\cap T_{n})=m\left[\bigcup_{n=1}^{+\infty}m(M\cap T_{n})\right]=m(M)\,,

hence, there exists at last a
T_{n}
, we denote by
T
, such that

0<m(M\cap T)\leq m(T)<\dfrac{4}{3}m(M\cap T)\,.

(3.6)

Assume that
x_{0}
is the mid-point of
T=(c,d)
and
f
is not bounded from above in any neighborhood of
x_{0}
. Choose
x_{1}
such that

|x_{1}-x_{0}|<\dfrac{m(M\cap T)}{6}=\varepsilon,\qquad f(x_{1})>1.

Set
N:=\{y~:~y=2x_{1}-x;~x\in M\cap T\}
. It is obvious that
N
is a reflection and
M\cap T
is transferred. So

m(N)=m(M\cap T)=6\varepsilon.

Now, for every
y\in N

\displaystyle|y-x_{0}|

\displaystyle=|2x_{1}-x-x_{0}|=|2(x_{1}-x_{0})+x_{0}-x|

\displaystyle\leq 2|x_{1}-x_{0}|+|x_{0}-x|

\displaystyle<2\varepsilon+|x_{0}-x|

\displaystyle=\dfrac{m(M\cap T)}{3}+|x_{0}-x|.

Hence,
N\subset(c-2\varepsilon,d+2\varepsilon)
. Since
m(N)=m(M\cap T)=6\varepsilon
, we have
m(N\cap T)\geq 2\varepsilon
. By
h
-mid-convexity of
f
,

f(x_{1})=f\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)\leq h(\frac{1}{2})\big(f(x)+f(y)\big)\leq h(\frac{1}{2})f(y)\,.

By multiplication two sides of the above inequality to positive number
h(2)
and using super-multiplicity of
h
, we have

h(2)f(x_{1})\leq h(2)h(\frac{1}{2})f(y)\leq h(1)f(y)=f(y\,).

Since
h(t)\geq t

\displaystyle 2\leq 2f(x_{1})\leq h(2)f(x_{1})\leq h(1)f(y)=f(y)\,.

So,
f(y)\geq 2
and
N\cap M=\varnothing
. Therefore

\displaystyle m(T)

\displaystyle\geq m\bigg[(M\cap T)\cup(N\cap T)\bigg]

\displaystyle=m(M\cap T)+m(N\cap T)

\displaystyle\geq 6\varepsilon+2\varepsilon=8\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\dfrac{8}{6}m(M\cap T)=\dfrac{4}{3}m(M\cap T).

This is a contradiction by (
3.6
). So
f
is bounded above on a neighborhood of
x_{0}
. So,
f
is continuous.
∎

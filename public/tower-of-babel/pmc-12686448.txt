Pearson correlation-based clustering with collaborative task allocation in 5G Industrial Internet of Things divergent health networks
Authors: Krishnakumar Vaithianathan, Julian Benadit Pernabas, Manjunath Ramanna Lamani, K. Venkatachalam, S. S. Askar, Mohamed Abouhawwash
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Simultaneous task allocation is crucial for enhancing service quality in Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) environments. The distribution and management of tasks remain among the biggest challenges in the IIoT era. Efficient allocation strategies are needed to enable transparent network configurations and maximize task throughput. Although recent methods address the dynamic management of objects, they often overlook the correlations between tasks and their associated functionalities. This paper introduces a novel Connected Harmonical Adaptive Task Allocation (CHATA) model for IIoT health networks to ensure fair task distribution. CHATA leverages similarity measures of object functionalities to identify the most suitable object to perform each task. Simulations conducted in NS-3 demonstrate that CHATA achieves up to 90% allocation efficiency in 5G Radio Access Technologies IIoT health environments and significantly outperforms recent approaches in task assignment performance.

Introduction

5G technologies will restructure wireless communication by adding additional spectrum, efficient channel coding, reduction in cell size, mobility cells, and different access methods. Therefore, the radio access network architecture is changing to low-power transmission devices. Recently, UAV-based flying ad-hoc networks have been expanding network coverage in rural areas.

The potential of sensor devices, identity, locality, and physical attributes1,2 are the main heterogeneous characteristics of each object that form divergent networks like the Industrial Internet of Things. Optimization of power consumption in communicating specific tasks is an important issue when dealing with numerous Internet of Things (IoT) objects. Moreover, better network accuracy can be achieved by effective sharing of resources and cooperation between multiple IoTs3. Therefore, the realization and allocation4,5 of various tasks are attained by the collaboration of multiple objects that execute a series of functions. In an industrial domain, the connections of multiple objects with varied potentials enable each device to operate in a systematic or synchronized manner in performing monitoring and sensing jobs. Recent technologies in IIoT achieve maximum integration of patients/medical staff, medicine, and hospital services to design an efficient smart healthcare assistance environment6,7. The diverse configurations of IIoT networks make it more challenging to achieve an unbiased distribution of multiple tasks among various IIoT objects8. The main issues of disorganized assignment in monitoring and sensing tasks across multiple IIoT devices are the inefficient distribution of computing resources, inferior domain settings, and incomplete data collection and availability9,10. In case of emergencies, optimizing task distribution helps restructure the environment and handle the vast amount of data gathered from IoT devices. Poor restructuring of the environment leads to inaccurate classification of the objects and affects data interpretation, as it takes more time to reach the corresponding application. Therefore, in high-demand multiple emergencies, rapid changes in services and the environment should be carried out in a quick, optimized, decentralized, and error-free manner11. As per the necessities of the application, the smart management system intelligently interleaves the execution of objects by allocating and preserving IIoT resources for various tasks10.

In wireless sensor networks (WSN), efficient allocation of resources extends the lifespan of the network by optimizing energy usage12, which leads to the development of novel applications4 in a network of multiple IoT devices. Recently, decentralized consensus12 and virtualization3 of objects for different task collections have become the most widely used concepts for task allocation. To optimize resource utilization in task execution, object virtualization works by allocating tasks based on the specific competencies and performance capabilities of each object. In a network comprising a massive number of members, decentralized consensus distributes an equitable volume of resources according to the mutual connections between every object group13.

However, the correspondence relations between objects and task execution are not considered in the above solutions. Also, the allocation decision does not account for the characteristics of the environment in which the objects are placed. Hence, IIoT provides transparent configuration and requires transformable, dynamic, and just solutions to accommodate a wide variety of objects. Furthermore, a balanced IIoT resource management solution6 needs to communicate tasks to objects that are aware of their environmental relationships and functionalities. For robust and fair management of existing network resources, it is important to note that IIoT should provide functionalities that can enable optimized task allocation to diverse objects based on their relationships and functions.

Critical ethical and security challenges emerge in health-oriented IIoT networks, particularly when handling highly sensitive healthcare data. Issues of informed consent, ownership of collected data, and fair distribution of resources must be addressed to ensure patients have control over their information and that there is minimal bias in resource allocation. Security risks such as data breaches, unauthorized access, and cyberattacks are exacerbated by the heterogeneity and dynamic nature of IIoT networks. Mitigation strategies include advanced encryption, robust access controls, and compliance with regulations such as HIPAA and GDPR16,17. Blockchain and federated learning are some emerging technologies that may improve privacy and security. Although this paper does not address these challenges directly, they are crucial to the responsible adoption of internet of things in healthcare.

Related work

Traditional IIoT resource allocation methods

Numerous studies3,12,14 have investigated decentralized and dynamic resource allocation strategies for IoT networks. Key challenges in these works include supporting flexible network configurations, handling multiple task assignments, capturing correlations between object functions, and managing constrained resources.

Khalil et al3. proposed a heuristic approach combined with evolutionary algorithms to identify heterogeneity and improve operational stability over longer task intervals. This technique associates object functions with specific task demands while reducing energy consumption. However, it supports only a limited set of objects and task types, restricting its applicability in heterogeneous network scenarios.

In IIoT health environments, Pilloni et al12. introduced decentralized virtual objects that negotiate resource allocation among health sensor devices. While this approach improves task optimization accuracy, it relies on homogeneous object types and does not account for diverse sensing capabilities, interactions, or the influence of network scale. CONTASKI18 further explored distributed consensus policies by clustering similar IoT nodes to optimize task allocation. Despite achieving effective decision-making for task distribution, CONTASKI does not support concurrent execution of multiple tasks.

A consensus-based fault-tolerant management system was also proposed to improve flexibility by periodically broadcasting hello messages between virtualized nodes. Although this method enhances adaptability, the associated computation overhead remains high, and ignoring individual resource capabilities degrades performance. Kim et al19. formulated the task assignment problem as a minimal-degree integration challenge and applied evolutionary techniques to reduce execution time. However, relying solely on gateways for object interactions limits functional correlations and increases congestion in large networks.

Pilloni et al14. addressed energy-aware task allocation by dividing sensor network workloads into distributed mini-tasks. This centralized strategy improves energy utilization but raises network restructuring costs, especially in dynamic environments.

Cooperative resource allocation methods

Genetic algorithm-based adaptive scheduling15 has been explored to extend network lifespan. Although this method effectively aligns object capabilities with task requirements, centralized assignment creates bottlenecks that delay execution. ACADIA20, a simultaneous task execution framework, demonstrated superior performance over CONTASKI in simulated healthcare networks.

Sharif et al21. introduced a priority-based allocation strategy for health monitoring systems using edge computing. While this technique efficiently ranks tasks, it lacks flexibility to adapt to changing sensor functions and environmental dynamics. Lakhan et al22. combined blockchain with deep reinforcement learning to develop secure task scheduling in IIoT health environments. Despite offering strong data integrity, computational overhead limits its feasibility for resource-constrained devices.

Velusamy et al23. proposed an energy-efficient scheduling strategy, but their solution does not support simultaneous multitasking needed in continuous patient monitoring applications. Yadav et al24. presented a task offloading framework for edge computing, which performs well in some cases but overlooks task interdependencies that arise in complex workflows. Nagarajan et al25. applied deep learning to improve task scheduling in IIoT healthcare networks, yet the absence of context-aware prioritization restricts its effectiveness in dynamic scenarios. Wang et al26. used federated learning to develop a privacy-preserving task allocation policy. While this approach protects sensitive information, it lacks mechanisms for real-time adaptability to environmental variations.

Schedulers dynamically allocate computational and communication functions to nodes, considering channel quality, borders, air and ground layer latency, computational capacity, and link availability27,28. Empowering local innovation–when local ecosystems of manufacturers, start-ups, healthcare, and policymakers collaborate–can significantly boost national digital health infrastructure29,30. QoS-oriented Task Offloading” defines the system to communication and computation resource allocation to obtain each user’s Quality of Service (QoS) target31,32. The multihead attention mechanism derived from the Transformer architecture allows the model to process the sensor data, focusing multiple33,34. Task offloading to powerful nodes reduces user device energy consumption and processing delay, but underused satellite bandwidth and performance imbalances due to rapid network condition changes, such as high transmission delay and dynamic network conditions, make this difficult to manage35,36. In this case, deep learning models analyze real-time network data (traffic load, latency, device mobility) and auto-allocate network resources –bandwidth, computing power, and transmission priority –as well as control layers37,38.

Emerging approaches in 6G and Digital Twin-enabled networks

Recent research has also explored advanced resource allocation in 6G and Digital Twin (DT)-enabled IIoT networks. Huang et al39. leveraged Collective Reinforcement Learning and Graph Attention Networks to optimize resource utilization under dynamic conditions. Their results showed significant improvements in throughput and adaptability but also highlighted concerns regarding implementation complexity and processing overhead.

Huang et al40. further examined job offloading optimization in DT-assisted MEC-enabled air-ground IIoT networks. By combining real-time monitoring with predictive modeling, this approach enhanced energy efficiency and reduced latency in UAV-based operations. Hevesli et al41. proposed simultaneous optimization of UAV trajectories and task offloading using deep reinforcement learning. Although these methods improve performance in challenging contexts, scalability and seamless integration in real-world deployments remain open problems.

Summary of gaps and motivation

Overall, prior research has provided valuable strategies for decentralized consensus, energy-aware scheduling, and privacy-preserving task allocation. However, most existing solutions do not fully integrate correlation-aware clustering of object functionalities with adaptive, simultaneous multi-task allocation. Many approaches either rely on centralized architectures or overlook the impact of environmental dynamics on task execution.

To address these limitations, our CHATA model combines correlation-based clustering with adaptive task allocation. Problem formulation

The proposed 5G-IIoT health network deploys user equipment (UEs) across small cells, each connected to XDn base stations (XDn-BSs) comprising UAV-based and terrestrial nodes. In Beyond 5G networks, aerial and terrestrial base stations are essential for extending coverage, especially in underserved or remote areas. When terrestrial nodes face energy constraints, UAVs can temporarily replace them to sustain connectivity. To prevent excessive load on aerial UAV-BSs, only nodes with adequate energy reserves are considered for task execution.

Given this context, an optimized task allocation strategy is necessary to manage the high volume of sensing demands from diverse sensor devices efficiently. Each task represents a request to monitor patient conditions or physiological signals, requiring a defined set of sensing functionalities that vary by application. Tasks are generated periodically and simulated within predetermined intervals. A task Z consists of four attributes and is denoted in a tuple \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\{Z_{id},\;G,\;\delta ,\;r\}$$\end{document}, where every task is identified by a unique task id (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Z_{id}$$\end{document}), a collection of sensing functionalities needed to fulfill the task (G), the time required to complete it (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta$$\end{document}), and quorum for per-cluster to finish the task (r).

Sensor activities fall into two categories7: organizational sensors that gather environmental data such as temperature and lighting, and physical sensors that capture physiological signals like blood pressure and heart rate. XDn-BSs track the status of all tasks, marking them as queued (awaiting allocation) or dispatched (accepted and executed by clusters).

The primary objective is to develop a method that allows each XDn-BS to allocate sensing capabilities efficiently across heterogeneous devices and dynamic environments.

To this end, we propose the Connected Harmonical Adaptive Task Allocation (CHATA) technique. CHATA clusters IIoT devices according to the correlation of their sensing capabilities, enabling efficient distribution of tasks to available resources. By leveraging correlation-based clustering, CHATA improves task allocation decisions and supports the simultaneous execution of multiple sensing tasks.

CHATA - proposed task assignment model

The CHATA mechanism consists of two components: Cluster Orchestration (CO), and Adaptive Task Assigner (ATA). In the IIoT health XDn-BSs, these two components work jointly to provide maximum assurance for the cluster configuration and the communication/allocation of sensor tasks. The virtual clusters of the network are ordered by the CO component. Based on their sensing functionalities, ATA components disseminate multiple tasks to be performed by XDn-BSs. To achieve optimized task assignment, these components communicate with five kinds of coded messages. FunctionalityDistribution facilitates the configuration of clusters, ControllerListing selects the controllers to list into XDn-BSs, JobDistribute transmits tasks given by XDn-BSs, JobTake supports controllers to take up tasks, ClustController allows controllers to distribute the accepted tasks of the objects. Here, the cluster count (NC) and ATA are assumed to be embedded in XDn-BSs.

The CO component creates/manages the clusters by studying neighborhood UEs sensing functionalities using a correlation threshold to assign them to a particular cluster. Thus, when NC gets the FunctionalityDistribution messages from the neighboring XDn-BSs, it confirms the details like identification, number of neighbors, and functionalities. To perform this verification, it has three modules: Functionalities Distribution (FD) is capable of distributing Functionality Distribution messages with the UEs’ details(unique identifier, number of neighbors, and functionalities).Correlation Confirmation (CF) receives/confirms the messages communicated among XDn-BSs.Management of Clusters (CM) creates/manages with UEs correlation and the controller identification.The ATA component synchronizes the task assignment based on the UEs functionalities and distributes various tasks to UEs of the IIoT health network. This process increases the maximization and preservation of their resources. It is noted that simultaneous task assignment is not considered in other studies12,18. The modules of this ATA component are: Job Confirmation (JC) supervises/identifies the tasks and their corresponding functionalities that should be assigned.Position Setter (PS) manages the different kinds of tasks to be given to the UEs, by applying the joint agreement between the controller and the tasks to judge which UEs must be allocated based on the cluster correlation.Job distribution (JD) sends off the required tasks evaluating the UEs functionalities.Job Operation (JO) handles the task operations from the controller.Therefore, without overloading the UEs resources, a fair and more balanced task assignment mechanism (CHATA) is proposed in this paper.

Cluster settings

To create an IIoT health infrastructure that is capable of efficiently optimizing task assignment, the CO component produces clusters of UEs concerning their controllers (XDn-BSs). This increases the scalability of the proposed model. At first, UEs start configuring clusters by communicating FD messages that contain the UEs details like unique identifier, number of neighbors, and functionalities. Algorithm 1 describes the cluster settings process. At first, each UE transmits a FD message for announcing its unique id, number of neighbors (UEneighborSize), and functionalities (UEFunctionalities)) using procedure InitialClusterConfiguration. Upon receiving the FD message, the ReceiveUEInfo updates its UE neighborhood, with its functionalities (UEneighborhoodFunctionalities). The correlation confirmation uses this data to calculate its Pearson correlation similarity. A correlation threshold is used to add a UE neighbor to a cluster with similar functionalities. This updation process is dynamically done in all UEs, and it ensures that each UE retains its neighbor and the corresponding cluster.

The SelectClusterController identifies the cluster controller by using the number of UE neighbors and its functionalities. Then, the identified controller (XDn-BS) is informed to other neighboring XDn-BSs. Task assignment

The tasks are carried out through the XDn-BSs and it keeps track of the incomplete ones and transmits them based on the demands. The XDn-BS sends details of the task to the group controllers by issuing them JobDistribute messages. ATA uses the cluster settings and allows the task distribution based on the clusters’ functionality similarities, which ensures maximum resource utilization. The task assignment process and negotiation of tasks between the controllers and neighboring XDn-BSs are discussed in the Algorithm 2. To find out the controllers, the XDn-BSs constantly monitor messages of ControllerListing and make new changes to the controller list up-to-date (DispatchTasks). At first the XDn-BSs handles the group of incomplete tasks. During the task dispatching process, the controller XDn-BS selects a fixed amount of multiple incomplete tasks from the task list and issues a JobDistribute message to the controllers broadcasting the availability of task Z to be run. Following every dispatch process, it sleeps for a fixed time delay for receiving the JobTake acknowledgment message from similar cluster controllers. At least one acknowledgment is required for XDn-BS to remove it from the list of incomplete tasks.

In summary, The Configuration of Clusters (Algorithm 1) is a fundamental algorithm in the creation of efficient task allocation systems in industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) networks. Organizing UEs into groups based on functionalities and communication proximity ensures efficient task distribution as well as resource management. Given the Unique Identifications (ID), functionalities vectors, and neighborhood size, the next process is the calculation of a correlation of a UE with their neighboring nodes using a relevant Pearson correlation coefficient. This value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\zeta$$\end{document} found between −0.5 and 0.5 signifies functional similarity among UEs. When this value is greater than a certain threshold denoted as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tau$$\end{document}, the UEs are mapped to the same cluster. Those not satisfying this criterion form new clusters or may not be grouped in any cluster. In order to improve efficiency and adaptability, dynamic updating of clusters is allowed when UEs change location or functionalities over time. Another critical aspect of this algorithm is the selection of a cluster controller, chosen from among the UEs in the cluster based on parameters such as neighborhood size, battery power, and processing speed. As a coordinator, this cluster controller manages intra-cluster communication and assignment of tasks. This mechanism generates neighbor lists for each of the UEs, updated cluster assignments, and computed correlation values, all of which lead to improved network resource utilization. The advantages of this algorithm include local execution of the jobs, minimum network overhead, and higher adaptability to changing conditions. It is very challenging and computationally expensive to set ideal thresholds for diversified environments in networks with a large density of UEs. However, this technique creates a strong clustering mechanism that allows scalable and effective task distribution in IIoT networks.

The Task Assignment algorithm (Algorithm 2) uses the clusters generated in Algorithm 1, distributing tasks efficiently across clusters and optimizing resource utilization while satisfying task-specific requirements. Every task is represented as a tuple (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\{Z_{id},\;G,\delta ,\;r\}$$\end{document}). Tasks are first prioritized based on deadlines and resource demands to ensure timely execution. The algorithm then matches the tasks with clusters that have available functionalities and adequate resources. In addition, it verifies that the cluster meets the quorum (r) of UEs. After the suitable cluster has been identified, its controller assigns the task to particular UEs in the cluster that are closest and have abundant resources. That is to say, the job will be queued or delayed for further processing if no cluster is matched. In addition to monitoring the job’s progress, the cluster controller does not only send the task originator a confirmation but also real-time status updates. This approach uses load-balancing algorithms that distribute jobs when a cluster experiences a high demand for resources, thereby preventing bottlenecks and providing equitable resource distribution. Outputs of this technique are a task assignment map that links tasks to the clusters and UEs that perform it and the real-time execution status of tasks. This dynamic task allocation approach reduces the latency of tasks, increases resource usage efficiency, and responds dynamically to changing demands in the IIoT environment. However, issues like the need for strong monitoring systems and the delayed execution of jobs at times of high demand indicate the areas that need improvement. In other words, the task assignment process is crucial to ensuring timely yet efficient job execution inside complex IIoT networks due to the clustering mechanism established by Algorithm 1, a very systematic resource management approach.

Each task and its duration are randomly generated using a random generator. The total duration of tasks is around 2500 s to accommodate multiple simultaneous UEs. To improve generality, in each run, the seed of the random generator is changed based on the number of UEs, execution count, and simultaneous requests. Uniform distribution is followed for the random number generation. However, the UEs functionalities remain the same for the simulation runs. These random generations are helpful in testing the real-life variations in the health environment. If the sensing functionalities are the same for all the simulations, then the error is negligible and does not represent a real-time situation. Also, it is noted that these random generations lead to high standard deviation and elevated amplitude errors.

The simulated health system runs over 50000 s and there is a time delay (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tau \;=50s$$\end{document}) in exchanging messages among UEs to distribute their functionalities. This is followed by correlation calculation and controller selection. The XDn-BS sends off multiple tasks for every 50 s up to 900a, and tasks are considered by clusters for every 50s. Furthermore, each task has a randomly generated functions set and these tasks are confined to health sensor functionalities like heart monitoring, temperature, blood pressure, oxygen levels, sugar level, and electrocardio-signals.

The incomplete tasks are sent to the controllers by the selected XDn-BS. These base stations are always available with the maximum internet coverage to reach out to all UEs. The clusters are categorized into capable and incapable. i.e., the capable ones perform the dispatched task, and the incapable ones remain idle waiting for the next task. Limitations and future directions

Although CHATA demonstrates strong performance indicators, several limitations should be addressed to improve its practical utility and relevance. A primary challenge lies in its reliance on fixed Pearson correlation thresholds for clustering. These static thresholds may not adapt well to varying and dynamic network conditions, potentially reducing cluster efficiency if not carefully tuned for each scenario. Additionally, CHATA consumes more energy than CONTASKI, particularly in large-scale deployments, as its dynamic cluster management and adaptive task allocation favor low latency over energy conservation. This trade-off could be mitigated through selective messaging strategies or energy-optimized clustering algorithms.

Security and privacy are further concerns, especially in IIoT healthcare environments where sensitive data are handled. The current version of CHATA does not include mechanisms to prevent attacks compromising system integrity, such as task hijacking, data breaches, or unauthorized access. These issues could be addressed through robust encryption techniques or privacy-preserving methods like federated learning.

Moreover, further research is needed to assess CHATA’s resilience in dynamic scenarios involving task surges, node failures, or high mobility. An integrated approach combining security, efficiency, and adaptability would strengthen CHATA’s suitability as an IIoT solution.

Future work will focus on enhancing CHATA’s energy efficiency without degrading performance. Conclusion

The CHATA model shows clear superiority over the existing task allocation and resource management strategies and establishes itself as a robust and scalable solution for IIoT applications. CHATA ensures optimal utilization of network resources, which no other comparable methods, such as21,26,44 or46, focused on isolated objectives such as energy optimization or multi-region job classification. This capability of CHATA to address several performance metrics at the same time is what makes it stand out as a holistic task assignment framework.

One of the features of CHATA is that it can handle simultaneous multi-task assignments effectively. Unlike traditional methods, CHATA explicitly integrates task correlation using Pearson clustering in order to distribute tasks across nodes efficiently. This is very important in dynamic IIoT networks where the tasks overlap and require instant allocation. Competing approaches focus on optimizing task placement or classification but fail to provide explicit mechanisms for concurrent task handling such as deep reinforcement/federated learning-based models. CHATA bridges this gap, offering the capability of simultaneous task management without compromising efficiency or latency.

The proposed CHATA is an efficient algorithm for the allocation of jobs in IIoT, which solves major problems with contemporary methods by achieving high efficiency with low latency, scalability, and robustness in handling diverse IIoT networks. Its overall performance measures compensate for a trade-off even though the energy consumption is higher in it. Due to its design, CHATA is capable of satisfying the needs of large-scale heterogeneous IIoT networks. Thus, it will be quite attractive for future applications. Future works can be targeted at improving energy efficiencies without sacrificing strong performance metrics.

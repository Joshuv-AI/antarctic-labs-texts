A sharp spectral splitting theorem
Gioacchino Antonelli, Marco Pozzetta, Kai Xu

Abstract
. We prove a sharp spectral generalization of the Cheeger–Gromoll splitting theorem. We show that if a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold M M of dimension n ⩾ 2 n\geqslant 2 has at least two ends and λ 1 ​ ( − γ ​ Δ + Ric ) ⩾ 0 , \lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\mathrm{Ric})\geqslant 0, for some γ < 4 n − 1 \gamma<\frac{4}{n-1} , then M M splits isometrically as ℝ × N \mathbb{R}\times N for some compact manifold N N with nonnegative Ricci curvature. We show that the constant 4 n − 1 \frac{4}{n-1} is sharp, and the multiple-end assumption is necessary for any γ > 0 \gamma>0 .

1.
Introduction

The celebrated Cheeger–Gromoll splitting theorem
[
CG71
]
asserts that a complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
having nonnegative Ricci curvature and containing a geodesic line must split isometrically; namely, one has
(M,g)\cong(\mathbb{R}\times N,\mathrm{d}t^{2}+g_{N})
for some manifold
(N,g_{N})
. As a consequence, a complete manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature and at least two ends must split. In this paper, we give a sharp generalization of the latter statement to manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature in the spectral sense.

On a smooth complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with
n\geqslant 2
, we define the function
\mathrm{Ric}:M\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mathrm{Ric}(x):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in T_{x}M\\
g(v,v)=1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Ric}_{x}(v,v).

Note that
{\rm Ric}\in{\rm Lip}_{\text{loc}}(M)
. For a constant
\gamma\geqslant 0
, we say that
M
satisfies
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+{\rm Ric})\geqslant 0

if any of the two following equivalent conditions holds:

(i)

for all
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(M)
it holds
\int_{M}\gamma|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+{\rm Ric}\cdot\varphi^{2}\geqslant 0
,

(ii)

there exist
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(M)
such that
u>0
and
-\gamma\Delta u+{\rm Ric}\cdot u\geqslant 0
on
M
.

The equivalence of the latter two conditions is readily established on compact manifolds, and follows from
[
FS80
]
on noncompact manifolds. In fact, in the noncompact case, Item (i) implies the existence of
u>0
with
-\gamma\Delta u+{\rm Ric}\cdot u=0

[
FS80
]
.

The study of Ricci curvature lower bounds in the spectral sense has recently found connections with the stable Bernstein problem
[
CL23
,
Cho+24
,
Maz24
]
, the geometry of biRicci curvature
[
CLZ24
,
Xu24
]
, and Ricci curvature bounds in the Kato sense
[
CMT23
]
. In
[
AX24
]
, the first and third named authors proved a sharp volume comparison and Bonnet–Myers theorem for manifolds with positive Ricci curvature in the spectral sense; we refer to
[
AX24
]
for a more detailed survey of past results.

The main result of this paper is the following sharp spectral splitting theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
,
\gamma<\frac{4}{n-1}
, and let
(M^{n},g)
be an
n
-dimensional smooth complete noncompact Riemannian manifold without boundary. Assume that
M
has at least two ends and satisfies

(1.1)

\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+{\rm Ric})\geqslant 0.

Then
{\rm Ric}\geqslant 0
on
M
. In particular, the manifold splits isometrically as
(M,g)\cong(\mathbb{R}\times N,\mathrm{d}t^{2}+g_{N})
for some compact manifold
N
with nonnegative Ricci curvature.

With regard to the sharpness of our assumptions, we have the following observations.

(1)

The range
\gamma<\frac{4}{n-1}
for
\gamma
is sharp for any
n\geqslant 2
. Indeed, for each
\gamma\geqslant\frac{4}{n-1}
there exists a complete manifold
M^{n}
with two ends that satisfies (
1.1
), and that does not split isometrically. See
Remark

4.1
for details.

(2)

For
n\geqslant 3
and
\gamma>0
, in
Remark

4.2
we construct a small compact perturbation of the flat
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that satisfies (
1.1
) but does not split isometrically. Hence, without further assumptions, the spectral splitting theorem does not hold for manifolds containing geodesic lines: in fact, a compact perturbation of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
contains infinitely many geodesic lines.

We remark that other types of splitting theorems have been proved in
[
LW01
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
LW06
, Corollary 5.3 & Theorem 7.2]
, where suitable spectral lower bounds on the curvature imply the splitting of a manifold as a warped product.

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
follows by incorporating the
\mu
-bubble technique of Gromov
[
Gro96
,
Gro21
]
and a surface-capturing technique inspired by arguments in
[
Liu13
,
CCE16
,
CEM19
,
CLZ24a
]
.
We also refer to
[
Zhu23
, Section 2]
and
[
CL24
, Section 3]
for further introductions to
\mu
-bubbles. We remark that the method used in our proof is different from the one used in the original proof of the splitting theorem
[
CG71
]
(see also
[
EH84
]
). In fact, our method gives a different alternative proof of the classical splitting theorem on manifolds with two ends,
\mathrm{Ric}\geqslant 0
, and
\inf_{x\in M}|B(x,1)|>0
, see the second part of
Remark

3.1
.

A brief description of our main idea is as follows. We are first inspired by the work of J. Zhu
[
Zhu23
]
, where
\mu
-bubbles are used to give an alternative proof of the following result: if
3\leqslant n\leqslant 7
and there is a complete metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
with nonnegative scalar curvature, then it must be flat. The idea of
[
Zhu23
]
is to find an area-minimizing hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
which arises as a limit of
\mu
-bubbles. Indeed, once such a
\Sigma
is found, a standard foliation argument can be employed to prove the rigidity. There are two key challenges in this strategy: (1) ensuring the existence of a nonempty limiting hypersurface
\Sigma
, which amounts at preventing the approximating

\mu
-bubbles from drifting to infinity, and (2) proving the compactness of
\Sigma
; indeed, each approximating
\mu
-bubble is compact, but the limit hypersurface may not.

Now let us turn to the setting of Theorem
1.1
. By
[
FS80
]
, we have a function
u
with

(1.2)

u>0,\qquad-\gamma\Delta u+{\rm Ric}\cdot u=0.

Given that
M
has at least two ends, an analog of Zhu’s
\mu
-bubble approximation argument produces a hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
that locally minimizes the weighted area
\Sigma\mapsto\int_{\Sigma}u^{\gamma}
. However, in our setting,
\Sigma
might be noncompact, so the foliation argument is no longer available.

To resolve this issue, we give up the foliation argument and instead argue as follows. We find an approximation scheme that for each
x\in M
gives a weighted minimal hypersurface
\Sigma
passing through
x
. Then the stability inequality yields
|\nabla u|=0
on
\Sigma
, hence arbitrariness of
x
will imply that
u
is constant. Thus
{\rm Ric}\geqslant 0
, and the splitting follows. To find such a
\Sigma
, we adapt the surface-capturing technique of
[
CCE16
,
CEM19
]
. The core idea is explained by the following instance: if a metric on
\mathbb{T}^{3}

has positive scalar curvature everywhere except in a set
U
, then any homologically area-minimizing surface must intersect
U
. With this in mind, we perturb the function
u
in (
1.2
) so that
-\gamma\Delta u+{\rm Ric}\cdot u>0
everywhere outside a small ball
B(x,r)
, see
Lemma

2.2
. Then, due to the stability inequality, a weighted area minimizer must intersect
B(x,r)
. Taking
r\to 0
, and letting the perturbation tend to zero, we obtain the desired hypersurface as a limit.

Our
Theorem

1.1
can be used to infer information on the ends of stable minimal hypersurfaces in curved manifolds. In
[
CSZ97
]
, the authors prove that stable minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
have exactly one end. In
[
LW04
]
the authors prove that properly immersed stable minimal hypersurfaces in manifolds with nonnegative sectional curvature either have one end, or they split isometrically and are totally geodesic.

Let us recall that, for orthonormal vectors
v,v^{\prime}\in T_{p}M
, the biRicci curvature operator is defined as

\mathrm{biRic}_{p}(v,v^{\prime}):=\mathrm{Ric}_{p}(v,v)+\mathrm{Ric}_{p}(v^{\prime},v^{\prime})-\mathrm{Sect}_{p}(v\wedge v^{\prime}).

Define the function
\mathrm{biRic}:M\to\mathbb{R}
as

\mathrm{biRic}(x):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,v^{\prime}\in T_{x}M\\
\text{$v,v^{\prime}$ orthonormal}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{biRic}_{x}(v,v^{\prime}).

Notice that
\mathrm{Sect}\geqslant 0
implies
\mathrm{biRic}\geqslant 0
. The following immediate corollary of
Theorem

1.1
generalizes
[
LW04
, Theorem 0.1]
to manifolds with
\mathrm{biRic}\geqslant 0
in ambient dimension
n\leqslant 5
. Contrarily to
[
LW04
, Theorem 0.1]
, we do not require the immersion to be proper.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a smooth complete noncompact Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{biRic}\geqslant 0
, and assume
n\leqslant 5
. Let
\Sigma^{n-1}\hookrightarrow M^{n}
be a noncompact, complete, connected, immersed, two-sided, stable minimal hypersurface. Then either

(1)

\Sigma
has one end, or

(2)

\Sigma
splits isometrically as
\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma^{\prime}
, where
\Sigma^{\prime}
is compact and
\mathrm{Ric}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\geqslant 0
. Moreover, in this case,
\Sigma
is totally geodesic in
M
, and
\mathrm{Ric}_{M}(\nu_{\Sigma},\nu_{\Sigma})=0
, where
\nu_{\Sigma}
is a unit normal of
\Sigma
.

Preliminaries

The following lemma is a slight modification of
[
Zhu23
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
there is a smooth function
\overline{h}_{\varepsilon}:(-\frac{1}{\varepsilon},\frac{1}{\varepsilon})\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(1)

on
(-\frac{1}{\varepsilon},-1]\cup[1,\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
we have

(2.1)

\overline{h}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}+\overline{h}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\geqslant\varepsilon^{2},

(2)

on
[-1,1]
we have

(2.2)

|\overline{h}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}+\overline{h}_{\varepsilon}^{2}|\leqslant C\varepsilon,

for a universal constant
C>0
(
C=100
is enough),

(3)

there holds
\overline{h}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}<0
on
(-\frac{1}{\varepsilon},\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
,
\overline{h}_{\varepsilon}(0)=0
,
\lim_{x\to\pm\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\overline{h}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\mp\infty
,

(4)

\overline{h}_{\varepsilon}\to 0
smoothly as
\varepsilon\to 0
on any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We can take

\overline{h}_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon\eta(x)\coth(1+\varepsilon x)-\varepsilon\big(1-\eta(x)\big)\coth(1-\varepsilon x),

where
\eta
is a smooth cutoff function with
\eta|_{(-\infty,-1]}\equiv 1
,
\eta|_{[1,+\infty)}\equiv 0
,
-1\leqslant\eta^{\prime}\leqslant 0
, and
\eta(0)=1/2
.
∎

The next lemma provides the function we will use to perturb the function
u_{0}
satisfying (
1.2
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a smooth complete noncompact Riemannian
n
-manifold. Let
\gamma>0
,
f\in{\rm Lip}_{\loc}(M)
, and assume there exist
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
u_{0}\in C^{2,\alpha}(M)
, such that
-\gamma\Delta u_{0}+fu_{0}=0
, and
u_{0}>0
on
M
. Then for any
x\in M
and
r\in(0,1)
, there exists
w\in C^{2,\alpha}(M)
such that

(1)

-2u_{0}\leqslant w<0
on
M
,

(2)

-\gamma\Delta w+fw>0
on
M\setminus B(x,r)
.

Proof.

Let
U\Subset B(x,r)
be a smooth open set, and let
\eta\in{\rm Lip}_{\loc}(M)
be a strictly positive function such that
f+\eta\in C^{\infty}(M)
. By
[
FS80
, Theorem 1]
we know that
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+f)\geqslant 0
on
M
, and therefore
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+f+\eta)\geqslant 0
on
M\setminus U
as well. Let
\Omega_{1}\Subset\Omega_{2}\Subset\ldots\Subset M
be an exhaustion in smooth precompact open sets. By
[
FS80
, Theorem 1]
again we have
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+f+\eta)>0
on
\Omega_{i}\setminus U
for every
i
. Hence there exists a solution
w_{i}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus U)
to the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\gamma\Delta w_{i}+fw_{i}=-\eta w_{i}\qquad\text{in }\Omega_{i}\setminus\overline{U},\\
&w_{i}|_{\partial\Omega_{i}}=0,\\
&w_{i}|_{\partial U}=u_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

We observe that
w_{i}>0
in
\Omega_{i}\setminus U
. Indeed, testing the equation against
w_{i}^{-}:=\min\{w_{i},0\}\in{\rm Lip}_{0}(\Omega_{i}\setminus U)
yields
0=\int\gamma|\nabla w_{i}^{-}|^{2}+f(w_{i}^{-})^{2}+\eta(w_{i}^{-})^{2}
; since
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+f+\eta)>0
on
\Omega_{i}\setminus U
, then
w_{i}^{-}=0
and thus
w_{i}\geqslant 0
. The strict inequality
w_{i}>0
in
\Omega_{i}\setminus U
then follows by the strong maximum principle.
By an analogous argument, one finds
w_{i}\leqslant w_{i+1}
in
\Omega_{i}\setminus U
for any
i
. Moreover
-\gamma\Delta(u_{0}-w_{i})+f(u_{0}-w_{i})=\eta w_{i}>0
in
\Omega_{i}\setminus U
for any
i
. Multiplying by
(u_{0}-w_{i})^{-}:=\min\{u_{0}-w_{i},0\}
and arguing as before implies
w_{i}<u_{0}
in
\Omega_{i}\setminus U
for any
i
.

By standard elliptic estimates, using that
(f+\eta)
is smooth, up to extracting a subsequence,
w_{i}
converges in
C^{2}_{\rm loc}(M\setminus U)
to a limit function
w^{\prime}
, which in turn belongs to
C^{2,\alpha}_{\loc}(M\setminus U)
by elliptic regularity. We thus define
w:=-w^{\prime}
. From the above construction, we have
-u_{0}\leqslant w<0
and
-\gamma\Delta w+fw=-\eta w>0
in
M\setminus\overline{U}\Supset M\setminus B(x,r)
. The lemma then follows by extending
w
inside
U
keeping
-2u_{0}\leqslant w<0
.
∎

The next lemma proves auxiliary density estimates for
\mu
-bubbles.
We refer the reader to
[
AFP00
,
Mag12
]
for the classical theory of sets of finite perimeter in the Euclidean space, which naturally extends to the case of Riemannian manifolds.

A
set of locally finite perimeter
in a complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is a measurable set
E\subset M
such that the characteristic function
\chi_{E}
has locally bounded variation.
Given such a set
E
, the total variation of the weak gradient of
\chi_{E}
is called the perimeter measure, and usually denoted by
P(E,\cdot)
. By the well-known structure theorem of De Giorgi, the perimeter is concentrated on the so-called reduced boundary
\partial^{*}E
. More precisely, we have
P(E,\cdot)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial^{*}E
. For ease of notation, for any set of locally finite perimeter
E\subset M
and for any Borel set
A\subset M
, in this paper we will denote
|\partial^{*}E\cap A|:=P(E,A)
and
|\partial^{*}E|:=P(E,M)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a smooth noncompact complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, with
n\geqslant 2
. Let
\gamma\geqslant 0
,
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, and let
u:M\to(0,\infty)
be a function of class
C^{2,\alpha}
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Let
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a surjective smooth proper
1
-Lipschitz function such that
\Omega_{0}:=\phi^{-1}\left((-\infty,0)\right)
has smooth bounded boundary. Let
\overline{h}_{\varepsilon}
be given by
Lemma

2.1
, and let
h_{\varepsilon}:=c\,\overline{h}_{\varepsilon}\circ\phi
for some
c>0
. Define

\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(E):=\int_{\partial^{*}E}u^{\gamma}-\int\left(\chi_{E}-\chi_{\Omega_{0}}\right)h_{\varepsilon}u^{\gamma},

for any admissible set of locally finite perimeter
E\subset M
, i.e., such that
E\Delta\Omega_{0}\Subset\phi^{-1}\left((-1/\varepsilon,1/\varepsilon)\right)
up to negligible sets.

Assume that
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is a minimizer for
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
with respect to admissible compactly supported variations. Then, up to modify
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
on a negligible set, the following holds.

(1)

The reduced boundary
\partial^{*}\Omega_{\varepsilon}

is an (open) hypersurface of class
C^{2,\alpha^{\prime}}
,
for some
\alpha^{\prime}\in(0,1)
, and
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial^{*}\Omega_{\varepsilon}

is a closed set with Hausdorff dimension less than or equal to
n-8
.

(2)

Let
A\subset M
be a precompact open set, and let
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1/2)
such that
\overline{A}\subset\phi^{-1}\left((-1/\varepsilon_{0},1/\varepsilon_{0})\right)
. Then there exist
r_{0},C_{d}\in(0,1)
depending on
n,\gamma,\|\log u\|_{L^{\infty}(A)},g|_{\overline{A}},\varepsilon_{0},c
such that for any
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
there holds

C_{d}\leqslant\frac{|\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap B(y,\rho)|}{\rho^{n-1}}\leqslant C_{d}^{-1},

for any
y\in A\cap\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
with
B(y,2r_{0})\Subset A
, and any
\rho<r_{0}
.

Proof.

Item (1) follows from the Riemannian analogs of
[
Mag12
, Theorem 27.5]
, and
[
Mag12
, Theorem 28.1]
. We now prove Item (2). We denote here by
C>0
a generic constant depending on
n,\gamma,\|\log u\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}
,
g|_{\overline{A}},\varepsilon_{0},c
that may change from line to line. Let
y\in A
and
\rho>0
such that
B(y,\rho)\Subset A
. Since
h_{\varepsilon}\to 0
smoothly on
\overline{A}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have
\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}\leqslant C
for any
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
. Hence for any set
F
of locally finite perimeter such that
F\Delta\Omega_{\varepsilon}\Subset B(y,\rho)
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega_{\varepsilon}}u^{\gamma}\leqslant\int_{\partial^{*}F}u^{\gamma}+\int h_{\varepsilon}u^{\gamma}(\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}-\chi_{F})&\leqslant\int_{\partial^{*}F}u^{\gamma}+\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}^{\gamma}|F\Delta\Omega_{\varepsilon}|\\
&\leqslant\int_{\partial^{*}F}u^{\gamma}+C|F\Delta\Omega_{\varepsilon}|.\end{split}

Using the lower and upper bounds of
u
on
\overline{A}
, we estimate

\begin{split}\big|\partial^{*}\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap B(y,\rho)\big|&\leqslant C\left(\big|\partial^{*}F\cap B(y,\rho)\big|+\big|F\Delta\Omega_{\varepsilon}\big|^{\frac{1}{n}}\big|F\Delta\Omega_{\varepsilon}\big|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right)\\
&\leqslant C\Big(\big|\partial^{*}F\cap B(y,\rho)\big|+\big|B(y,\rho)\big|^{\frac{1}{n}}\cdot C_{\rm iso}\big|\partial^{*}(F\Delta\Omega_{\varepsilon})\big|\Big)\\
&\leqslant C\Big(\big|\partial^{*}F\cap B(y,\rho)\big|+C\rho\Big[\big|\partial^{*}\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap B(y,\rho)\big|+\big|\partial^{*}F\cap B(y,\rho)\big|\Big]\Big),\end{split}

where we used a local isoperimetric inequality with Euclidean exponents for sets contained in
A

[
Heb99
]
, and an Ahlfors-type bound for balls contained in
A
. It follows that there exists
r_{0}>0
such that, if
\rho<r_{0}
, then

\big|\partial^{*}\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap B(y,\rho)\big|\leqslant C\big|\partial^{*}F\cap B(y,\rho)\big|,

that is,
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is a
C
-quasiminimal set in balls
B(y,2r_{0})\Subset A
in the sense of
[
Kin+13
, Definition 3.1]
. The claimed density estimates then follow from

[
Kin+13
, Lemma 5.1]
and Item (1).
∎

3.
The spectral splitting theorem

In this section we prove
Theorem

1.1
and
Corollary

1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If
\gamma\leqslant 0
the statement reduces to the classical splitting theorem. Hence, from now on, we assume
\gamma>0
. By
[
FS80
, Theorem 1]
, and since
\mathrm{Ric}\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)
, the main condition (
1.1
) implies the existence of a function
u_{0}\in C^{2,\alpha}(M)
with

(3.1)

u_{0}>0,\qquad-\gamma\Delta u_{0}+{\rm Ric}\cdot u_{0}=0.

Fix an arbitrary
x\in M
. We claim that
|\nabla u_{0}|(x)=0
. (In fact, it will be clear from the proof that it is possible to find a perimeter minimizer
\Omega
with
x\in\partial\Omega
and
|\nabla u_{0}|\equiv 0
on
\partial\Omega
; see also
Remark

3.1
). Once this is proved, since
x
is arbitrary, it follows that
u_{0}
is constant on the entire
M
, and thus
{\rm Ric}\geqslant 0
pointwise, and the theorem is reduced to the classical splitting theorem. The rest of the proof is devoted to our claim.

Since
M
has more than one end, we can find a smooth surjective proper function
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(3.2)

|\nabla\phi|\leqslant 1,\qquad|\phi(x)|<1/2,\qquad\Omega_{0}:=\phi^{-1}\big((-\infty,0)\big)\text{ has smooth boundary.}

The construction is as follows. First, we find a smooth surjective proper
1
-Lipschitz function
\widetilde{\phi}:M\to\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, given a separating compact hypersurface bounding a distinguished end,
\widetilde{\phi}
can be obtained by smoothing the signed distance function from such end as in
[
Zhu23
, Lemma 2.1]
. Let
L
be a regular value of
\widetilde{\phi}
such that
|\widetilde{\phi}(x)-L|<1/2
. Hence,
\phi:=\widetilde{\phi}-L
has the desired properties.

Let
0<\delta,r\ll 1
be such that

(3.3)

B(x,1/\delta)\Supset\phi^{-1}\big([-2,2]\big),\qquad B(x,2r)\Subset\phi^{-1}\big([-1,1]\big).

Let us apply Lemma
2.2
to the ball
B(x,r)
, with data
u_{0}
and
f={\rm Ric}
. Denote by
w_{r}
the resulting function.
For a parameter
a>0
, set
u_{r,a}:=u_{0}+aw_{r}
. There exists a small
a_{0}=a_{0}(\delta,w_{r},u_{0})
so that for all
a<a_{0}
, we have

(3.4)

u_{0}/2\leqslant u_{r,a}\leqslant u_{0},\qquad||u_{r,a}-u_{0}||_{C^{2}(B(x,1/\delta))}<\delta,

and

4.

The following remarks show the sharpness of our assumptions.

Remark 4.1

(Sharpness of the range of
\gamma
)
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
and
\gamma\geqslant\frac{4}{n-1}
. Consider the manifold

{\rm Ric}\equiv-(n-1)

satisfies (

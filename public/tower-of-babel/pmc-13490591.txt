Joint optimization of HBS 3D trajectory and power allocation transmission for energy- efficient NOMA downlink in 6G networks
Authors: Osamah Thamer Hassan Alzubaidi, Hayder Faeq Alhashimi, Kaharudin Dimyati, Salah Alheejawi, Effariza Binti Hanafi, Ahmed Nasser
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid growth of devices in the Internet of Everything (IoE) poses significant challenges in achieving high-capacity and energy-efficient connectivity in 6G wireless networks. Hovered base stations (HBSs) provide a promising solution for enhancing physical-layer performance; however, their mobility and inefficient transmit power allocation (PA) may increase interference and energy consumption. In this paper, a multi-HBS-based NOMA transmission framework is proposed for downlink 6G networks, where each HBS serves multiple IoE devices. The proposed framework jointly optimizes HBS three-dimensional (3D) trajectory, transmit PA, and dynamic decoding order execution to maximize total sum rate (TSR) and total energy efficiency (TEE) under minimum data-rate constraints. The resulting optimization problem is non-convex due to the coupling among optimization variables and constraints. To efficiently solve this problem, a low-complexity and fast-converging hybrid optimization framework integrating a developed genetic algorithm and modified gray wolf optimization is adopted. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed framework significantly outperforms existing benchmark schemes, achieving up to 23.6% improvement in TSR and 35.8% improvement in TEE. These results confirm the effectiveness of the proposed joint optimization framework for improving overall network performance.Keywords: 6G, Non-orthogonal multiple access, Hovered base station, Dynamic decoding order, Power allocation transmission, Genetic algorithm, Gray wolf optimizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

THE emergence of the next-generation internet of everything (IoE) presents new research directions and goals. The highlighted priorities of the IoE framework are closely connected to 6G, block chain, massive data sets, artificial intelligence, network information security, and on-demand computing1,2. The employment of 6G facilitates the realization of the IoE. As the internet becomes more widespread, the number of communication devices is increasing exponentially. Consequently, a multiple access strategy is emphasized to enable extensive access to IoE equipment, within a specified bandwidth, while preserving quality of service (QoS) requirements such as end-to-end delay and maximum data rate, presents a significant challenge in heterogeneous networks (HetNets)3–5.

Non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) is projected to be a fundamental strategy in 6G wireless HetNets and the IoE6,7. In conventional orthogonal multiple access systems, users with suboptimal channel conditions are allocated network resources; nevertheless, the spectral efficiency (SE) of these systems is degraded. This limitation does not arise in power-domain NOMA systems, where multiple users are allowed to share the same time, frequency, and code resources but are assigned different power levels8,9. The basic principle underlying NOMA is that users with strong channel conditions employ successive interference cancellation (SIC) to mitigate interference caused by users with weaker channel conditions, thereby significantly reducing intra-cell and intra-cluster user interference10,11. Recently, hovered base stations (HBSs), such as aerial base stations (BSs), are emerged as a promising component in NOMA-enabled HetNets due to their flexible deployment, enhanced line-of-sight (LoS) connectivity, and adaptability to the spatial distribution of IoE devices12,13.

In NOMA-enabled HBS HetNets, the three-dimensional (3D) trajectory of HBSs and their transmit power allocation (PA) play a critical role in determining channel quality, interference levels, and overall network performance14,15. Improper 3D trajectory of HBSs may result in severe path loss (PL), coverage holes, and increased inter- and intra-cluster interference, whereas inefficient transmit PA control can lead to excessive power consumption and degraded SIC performance16. Moreover, the effective channel gains (EFCGs) and the dynamic decoding order (DDEO) among IoE devices are highly sensitive to both HBS locations and transmit power levels, which makes their joint optimization (JO) particularly important in NOMA systems17. As a result, jointly optimizing HBS 3D trajectory and transmit PA is essential for maximizing system performance while satisfying QoS constraints. However, this JO problem is inherently non-convex (NC) due to the strong coupling among spatial deployment, PA, and DDEO, making it highly challenging to address using conventional optimization techniques.

Related works

This Resource allocation (RA) optimization in NOMA-based HetNets present significant challenges due to strong inter-user interference, NC optimization formulations, and stringent QoS constraints. In practice, PA is often jointly optimized with RA, user clustering (UC), BS deployment, and interference management (IM) to enhance system sum rate (SSR), SE, and energy efficiency (EE). Several studies have investigated PA, and IM in NOMA-based HetNets using both conventional optimization and learning-driven approaches. A multi-agent reinforcement learning (RL)-based PA strategy for macro- and small-cell BSs is proposed in18 to satisfy QoS constraints while improving probability of connection (PoC), EE, and SE. Wireless-powered internet of things networks with short-packet transmission are studied in19, where NC joint throughput and transmission-time optimization problems (OPs) are efficiently addressed using block coordinate descent (BCD) and concave–convex procedures. Joint RA and mode selection are further investigated for relay-assisted device-to-device uplink (UL) HetNets in20 using greedy selection and multi-agent Q-learning to maximize SE under QoS constraints. Building on18, multi-agent RL-based PA is extended to relay-aided multi-tier cellular networks with QoS-aware EE optimization in21. Data-driven learning techniques are also explored for PA and IM in hybrid NOMA HetNets. A multi-armed bandit–based framework is introduced in22 to jointly optimize user PA, pairing, and sensing–communication coexistence with reduced computational complexity (COC). Moreover, a model-free, data-driven energy-efficient IM strategy is proposed in23 for millimeter-wave downlink (DL) NOMA HetNets, addressing co-tier, cross-tier, and inter-user interference under QoS and SIC constraints. However, most existing learning-based approaches rely on centralized training or single agent formulations, which may limit scalability in large scale distributed wireless networks.

Recently, learning-based distributed optimization approaches, particularly multi-agent RL, have emerged as a promising solution for scalable and decentralized resource management in advanced wireless networks. In such frameworks, multiple agents collaboratively learn optimal policies using local observations, thereby reducing signaling overhead and improving scalability. For instance, a multi-agent RL-based framework for joint pre-coding and phase shift optimization in RIS-aided cell-free massive MIMO systems is proposed in24, where distributed agents optimize transmission strategies based on local channel state information (CSI). Furthermore, recent studies on multi-agent RL for wireless distributed networks in 6G highlight its capability to enable cooperative decision making, adaptive RA, and efficient large scale network optimization in dynamic environments25.

Despite these advances, most existing multi-agent RL-based approaches focus on distributed pre-coding, beamforming, or control in RIS-assisted and cell-free architectures, while their application to joint 3D trajectory optimization, transmit PA, and DDEO design in NOMA-based HetNets remains largely unexplored.

The integration of reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs) with aerial and non-terrestrial platforms is emerged as an effective approach for enhancing channel quality and mitigating interference in DL NOMA HetNets. In26, an RIS-assisted unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) DL NOMA framework is proposed to maximize the SSR under simultaneous wireless information and power transfer through joint UAV deployment and RIS configuration. Similarly, an RIS-assisted UAV DL NOMA architecture is investigated in27 to mitigate inter- and intra-cluster interference and enhance SSR via JO of UAV deployment, RIS configuration, and decoding order (DEO). RIS-assisted non-terrestrial BSs DL NOMA frameworks are further studied in28 and29, where modified gray wolf optimization (MGWO)–based techniques are employed to jointly maximize SSR and EE under interference constraints. Moreover, a RIS-supported multi-aerial BS DL NOMA framework is investigated in30 for joint SSR and EE maximization under QoS constraints using hybrid modified meta-heuristic and developed BCD (DBCD)–based optimization.

Recently, the integration of mobile edge computing (MEC) and air–ground collaborative networks has attracted significant attention in 6G systems due to their ability to support low latency and computation intensive applications. In this context, JO of communication and computation resources has been extensively investigated. In31, a joint MIMO precoding and computation RA framework for dual function radar and communication systems with MEC is proposed, highlighting the importance of cross layer optimization for improving system efficiency. Furthermore, a multi-objective optimization framework for dependent task scheduling, RA, and service caching in aerial–ground integrated MEC systems is studied in32, demonstrating the tradeoffs between latency, energy consumption, and service efficiency. In addition, the latency minimization for multi-UAV-assisted task offloading in air–ground millimeter-wave networks is investigated in33, emphasizing the critical role of UAV deployment and RA in delay sensitive applications. Despite these advances, most existing works focus on computation offloading, latency optimization, or sensing communication integration, rather than spectral and energy efficiency in NOMA-based HetNets.

Meta-heuristic and evolutionary optimization algorithms, particularly GWO and its variants, are widely adopted to solve complex NC OPs arising in NOMA-based HetNets. In34, joint PA and user association are investigated for DL NOMA networks under QoS constraints using gray wolf and whale optimization algorithms. QoS-aware virtual network function deployment in hybrid cloud infrastructures under RA and user mobility constraints is addressed using a binary GWO–based approach in35. Joint PA and UC for SE maximization in UL NOMA HetNets are studied in36 using an enhanced GWO framework incorporating competitive learning and RL mechanisms. Moreover, a GWO-based framework is developed for joint PA and UC in DL MIMO–NOMA networks in37.

From a critical perspective, learning-based approaches provide scalability and adaptability; however, they often suffer from high training complexity, convergence instability, and relying on large datasets. Similarly, RIS- and UAV-assisted frameworks enhance channel conditions and coverage, but typically optimize only a subset of system variables rather than addressing the fully coupled JO problem. In addition, MEC-enabled aerial and air-ground integrated studies primarily emphasize latency and computation efficiency rather than total sum rate (TSR) and total energy efficiency (TEE) optimization in NOMA-based systems. Motivation and contributions

Although extensive research had been carried out in 3D trajectory optimization for non-terrestrial and RIS-assisted NOMA HetNets, most existing works rely on static deployment assumptions or optimize HBS trajectory, transmit power, and DEO independently. In particular, the JO of HBS transmit PA and 3D trajectory under mobility and dynamic repositioning with respect to the spatial distribution of users is not adequately addressed, making it difficult to maintain consistent coverage and fair PA in dynamic aerial deployments. Moreover, the role of DDEO in ensuring reliable SIC and user fairness is often neglected, and the resulting optimization problems are tightly coupled and NC.

Compared to existing UAV/HBS-assisted NOMA studies, the proposed framework introduces several key distinctions. Unlike prior works that optimize 3D trajectory or PA separately, this study performs a fully coupled JO of HBS 3D trajectory, transmit PA, and DDEO within a unified framework. Moreover, while most existing approaches assume fixed DEOs, the proposed DDEO dynamically adapts the decoding sequence based on both channel conditions and optimized power levels, ensuring improved SIC reliability and user fairness.

In addition, the proposed approach integrates a hybrid optimization mechanism by combining the developed genetic algorithm (DGA) for 3D trajectory design with MGWO for PA. This hybrid framework effectively balances exploration and exploitation, enabling efficient handling of the high-dimensional NC problem. By jointly considering spatial, power, and decoding dimensions, the proposed framework achieves more effective IM and enhanced TSR and TEE performance compared to existing methods.

This framework is applied to DL HBS-supported NOMA HetNets to maximize TSR and TEE under QoS constraints. It incorporates centroid-aware trajectory design and is efficiently solved using a low-complexity hybrid DGA–MGWO scheme.

Proposed algorithm

The primary objective of the proposed optimization framework is for maximizing the TSR and TEE by jointly optimizing key system parameters. Specifically, the framework aims to determine the optimum 3D trajectory of the HBSs to maintain proximity to the IoE devices and dynamically adapt to their varying locations within the network. In parallel, it optimizes the HBS transmit PA to satisfy the required minimum data rate while minimizing interference across clusters. Moreover, the system performance is further enhanced using DDEO among the IoE devices within each cluster. To accomplish these objectives, we propose two complementary optimization schemes: the DGA for determining the optimal HBS 3D trajectory and the MGWO for optimizing the HBS transmit PA. Formulations and procedures for each proposed method are provided in the following subsections.

Joint optimization of HBS 3D trajectory and transmit PA along with DDEO implementation

In this section, we decompose the resulting OP into three sub-problems (i.e., HBS 3D trajectory, HBS transmit PA, and DDEO among IoE devices), which are iteratively optimized using the proposed optimization algorithms to address the previously mentioned NC OP. Due to the inherent coupling among the optimization variables, these sub-problems cannot be solved independently. Therefore, an alternating optimization strategy is adopted, where each variable is optimized while keeping the others fixed. The HBS 3D trajectory are optimized first for maximizing the TSR of the proposed framework. Then, depending on the optimized HBS 3D trajectory, the HBS transmit PA is optimized for maximizing the TSR while considering the required minimum data rate. Finally, based on the aggregated channel power gain, the DDEO is employed to ensure fairness among IoE devices and minimize power consumption. The DGA formulation focuses on optimizing the HBS 3D trajectory, whereas the MGWO formulation addresses the HBS transmit PA.

While the decomposition of the original problem into sub-problems may lead to a locally optimal solution, such an approach is widely adopted in the literature for handling highly coupled NC problems. This is because obtaining a globally optimal solution is computationally intractable due to the JO over multiple interdependent variables. The proposed framework ensures that the objective value is non-decreasing across iterations, which guarantees convergence to a stable stationary solution.

BCD and SA remain computationally lightweight but are more susceptible to convergence to suboptimal solutions in NC environments.

Although strict global optimality guarantees cannot be established for the proposed DGA+MGWO framework due to the non-convex and high-dimensional nature of the formulated problem, the adopted alternating optimization structure provides stable iterative improvement of the OBF across successive updates. Specifically, the DGA stage performs global exploration of the HBS 3D trajectory search space, while the MGWO stage refines the transmit PA solution through exploitation-oriented updates. Scalability challenges in the proposed DGA+MGWO arise from the growth of the 3D search space with increasing numbers of HBSs and IoE devices and the iterative nature of meta-heuristic updates. Additional obstacles include sensitivity to parameter tuning and increased computational requirements in dense 6G IoE networks. In particular, the convergence speed and solution quality of the proposed (DGA+MGWO) framework are influenced by several algorithmic parameters, including population size, crossover and mutation probabilities in the DGA stage, as well as the number of search agents and iteration settings in the MGWO stage. Larger population and iteration settings generally improve exploration capability and solution stability at the expense of higher COC, whereas smaller parameter values reduce computational cost but may increase the risk of premature convergence. Therefore, appropriate parameter selection is required to balance convergence accuracy and computational efficiency.

To broaden practical applicability, enhancements such as adaptive parameter tuning, parallel evolution, integration with RL, and hybridization with BCD or SA can be adopted. This behavior is mainly attributed to insufficient coordination between spatial positioning, transmit PA, and DEO adaptation, which leads to increased inter- and intra-cluster interference and inefficient resource utilization.

In contrast, the proposed fully JO framework consistently achieves the highest TSR and TEE across all network conditions. By jointly optimizing the HBS 3D trajectory, transmit PA, and DDEO, the proposed method enables coordinated IMIT, efficient SIC operation, and improved power utilization. These results demonstrate that the performance gains arise from the synergistic interaction among all optimization components rather than from any single component individually, thereby confirming the effectiveness and novelty of the proposed framework.

Conclusions and future research directions

In this paper, we employ a mulit-HBS in NOMA DL transmission framework for 6G networks, where each HBS employs NOMA to simultaneously serve multiple IoE devices. The TSR and TEE are maximized through the JO of the HBS 3D trajectory, which ensures centroid-aware alignment with the spatial distribution of the IoE devices, along with the optimization of HBS transmit PA subject to the minimum data rate requirements of all IoE devices and the implementation of DDEO among the IoE devices. To address the NC OP, we develop an evolutionary algorithm based on GA and we modify a meta-heuristic algorithm based on GWO. Specifically, the original OP is decomposed into three sub-problems (i.e. HBS 3D trajectory, HBS transmit PA, and DDEO among IoE devices). The first sub-problem is solved using the DGA technique, while the second sub-problem is addressed using the MGWO technique. Finally, a DDEO strategy is employed to solve the NOMA DEO among IoE devices. The simulation results indicate that our proposed strategies outperform traditional state-of-the-art schemes (i.e., SA, BCD, MGWO, and MGWO+DBCD) in terms of TSR and TEE. Specifically, the proposed approach achieves up to 23.6% higher TSR and approximately 35.8% higher TEE compared to the conventional schemes. Future work will focus on extending the proposed framework to more dynamic network scenarios, including incorporating IoE mobility and time-varying IoE traffic demands, as well as investigating adaptive clustering and learning-based DDEO strategies under imperfect CSI. In addition, the integration of multiple cooperative HBSs and the consideration of backhaul constraints will be explored. Finally, the proposed approach can be extended to multi-objective optimization frameworks that jointly address TSR, TEE, and latency requirements in 6G IoE networks.

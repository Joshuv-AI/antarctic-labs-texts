Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\alpha\geq 0

Proof.

For any sufficiently small

is a radial solution of (

). If the linearized problem

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\quad-\textrm{div}(|\nabla U_{\alpha}|^{p-2}\nabla\phi)-(p-2)\textrm{div}\left(|\nabla U_{\alpha}|^{p-4}(\nabla U_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla\phi)\nabla U_{\alpha}\right)\\
&=(p_{\alpha}^{*}-1)|x|^{\alpha}\left(u_{\varepsilon,\alpha}+U_{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-2}\phi\ \quad\text{in}\ B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}(0),\\
&\phi=0\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{on}\,\,\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}(0)\end{aligned}\right.

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-(p-1)\left(r^{N-1}|U_{\alpha}^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}=(p_{\alpha}^{*}-1)r^{N+\alpha-1}U_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-2}\phi&\text{in}\ \left(0,\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right),\\

Note that the function

satisfies the linearized equation

(3.5)

\displaystyle-(p-1)\left(r^{N-1}|U_{\alpha}^{\prime}|^{p-2}z^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}=(p_{\alpha}^{*}-1)r^{N+\alpha-1}U_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-2}z\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{+},\quad z^{\prime}(0)=0.

However, we find that

Multiplying (
3.4
) by

, (

and integrating in

Moreover,
\phi\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)=0

. This shows that (

is radially nondegenerate.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\alpha_{n}
and
\varepsilon_{n}
be sequences such that
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha>0
and
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty

Defining

with
u_{\varepsilon,\alpha}
be given by (
3.2
), we have that

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-U_{\alpha}\|_{X}\to 0,\quad\quad as\ n\to+\infty.

Proof.

According to the definition of norm (
1.16
), we need to prove
\|u_{n}-U_{\alpha}\|_{1,p}\to 0
and
\|u_{n}-U_{\alpha}\|_{\gamma}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
.

We obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla u_{n}-\nabla U_{\alpha}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\left|\nabla U_{\alpha_{n}}-\nabla U_{\alpha}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\left|\nabla U_{\alpha}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x.

Because

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\left|\nabla U_{\alpha}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\to 0,\quad\text{as}\ n\to+\infty.

From Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\left|\nabla U_{\alpha_{n}}-\nabla U_{\alpha}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\to 0,\quad\text{as}\ n\to+\infty.

Using the mean value theorem, it follows that

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-U_{\alpha}\|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|x|)^{\gamma}|u_{n}-U_{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}(1+|x|)^{\gamma}|U_{\alpha_{n}}(x)-U_{\alpha}(x)|+\sup_{x\in B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}(1+|x|)^{\gamma}U_{\alpha_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+C_{N,\alpha,p}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{\gamma}}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-p}{p+\alpha}}}

where we used the fact

. This finishes our proof of Proposition

.
∎

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(N+\alpha)(Np+p\alpha-N+p)}{(p-1)^{2}}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{t^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}+N-3}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}(t)|^{p-2}}{(1+t^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}})^{2}}w_{n}(t)\mathrm{d}t.

Since
0\leq w_{n}(r)\leq 1
and
\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)<0
for
n
large, from (
3.26
), we get

(3.28)

|w^{\prime}_{n}(r)|\leq\frac{C}{r^{N-1}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}(r)|^{p-2}}\times\left\{\begin{aligned} &C,&\text{if}\ N<2p+\alpha,\\
&C+\ln r,&\text{if}\ N=2p+\alpha,\\
&C+r^{\frac{N-2p-\alpha}{p-1}},&\text{if}\ N>2p+\alpha.\end{aligned}\right.

If
N<2p+\alpha
, we get the optimal decay in (
3.21
). If else, when
N\geq 2p+\alpha
, from (
3.28
), we have

(3.29)

|w_{n}(r)|\leq Cr^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}}r^{\frac{N-2p-\alpha}{p-1}}.

Then, inserting (
3.28
) into (
3.27
), after
k
steps of iteration, we obtain the following iterative sequence

(3.30)

|w_{n}(r)|\leq Cr^{f_{k}(N)}.

After iterating the formula (
3.30
) once more, we can obtain

(3.31)

|w_{n}(r)|\leq Cr^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}}r^{\frac{N-2p-\alpha}{p-1}}r^{f_{k}(N)}=:Cr^{f_{k+1}(N)},

where
f_{k+1}(N):=-\frac{N-p}{p-1}+\frac{N-2p-\alpha}{p-1}+f_{k}(N)
with
f_{0}(N)=\frac{N}{p-1}-\frac{2p+\alpha}{p-1}
. Thus we have
f_{k}(N)=\frac{N}{p-1}-\frac{2p+\alpha}{p-1}-k\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}
. Let
f_{k}(N)=0
, we have
N=N_{k}:=(p+\alpha)k+2p+\alpha
and
N_{k}\to+\infty
, as
k\to+\infty
. By using (
3.30
) instead of
|w_{n}|\leq 1
, we can deduce in similar way as (
3.28
) that
|w^{\prime}_{n}(r)|\leq\frac{C}{r^{N-1}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}(r)|^{p-2}}
for any
N<N_{k}
. Consequently, we get (
3.21
).

By (
3.10
) and (
3.21
), we have
|w^{\prime\prime}_{n}(r)|\leq r^{-\frac{N+p-2}{p-1}}
. From the Arzel
\acute{a}
-Ascoli’s Theorem,
w_{n}\to w
in
\mathcal{B}
weakly and uniformly w.r.t.
r
on compact sets of
[0,+\infty)
. Using (
3.21
) again, we can pass the limit to (
3.10
) and get that
w
is the solution of (
3.19
) corresponding to the eigenvalue
\mu_{1}(\alpha)
. Moreover,
w\not\equiv 0
, indeed, from (
3.21
), the maximum point of
w_{n}(r)
converges to a point
r_{0}\in[0,+\infty)
and
|w(r_{0})|=1
from the uniform convergence.

Finally, we prove the uniform convergence of
\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)\to\mu_{1}(\alpha)
in
\alpha
on compact sets. By multiplying (
3.23
) by
wr^{N-1}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}|^{p-2}
and integrating on
\left(0,\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)
, multiplying (
3.19
) by
w_{n}r^{N-1}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}|^{p-2}
and integrating on
\left(0,\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)
, and then subtracting, we get

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)^{N-1}\Big|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)\Big|^{p-2}w^{\prime}_{n}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)w\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)-\mu_{1}(\alpha)\right)}{p-1}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}t^{N-3}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}(t)|^{p-2}w_{n}(t)w(t)\mathrm{d}t.

From (
3.21
), we have

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)^{N-1}\Big|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)\Big|^{p-2}w^{\prime}_{n}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)w\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)=O\left(\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}+\frac{\alpha}{p}}\right),

as
n\to+\infty
, uniformly in
\alpha
on compact sets of
(0,+\infty)
, while

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}t^{N-3}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}(t)|^{p-2}w_{n}(t)w(t)\mathrm{d}t\to\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{N-3}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}(t)|^{p-2}w^{2}(t)\mathrm{d}t,

as
n\to+\infty
, uniformly in
\alpha
on compact sets of
(0,+\infty)
, which implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in K}|\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)-\mu_{1}(\alpha)|=o(1),

as
n\to+\infty
, for any compact set
K\subset(0,+\infty)
. This completes our proof.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
\varepsilon_{n}
be a sequence such that
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
, as
n\to+\infty
. Let
\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)
be the first
eigenvalue of (
3.23
) in
\left(0,\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)
. Then we have that

(3.32)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)}{\partial\alpha}\to-\frac{p(p+2\alpha+N-2)-2\alpha}{p^{2}}=\frac{\partial\mu_{1}(\alpha)}{\partial\alpha}<0

uniformly on the compact set
K\subset(0,+\infty)
. Furthermore, for any integer
k\geq 1
, the equation

(3.33)

\displaystyle-k(N+k-2)=-\lambda_{k}=\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)

has only one solution
\alpha_{k}^{n}
and

(3.34)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}^{n}\to\alpha(k),\quad as\ n\to+\infty,

where
\alpha(k)=\frac{p\sqrt{(N+p-2)^{2}+4(k-1)(p-1)(k+N-1)}-p(N+p-2)}{2(p-1)}
.

Proof.

From known results (c.f. e.g.
[
33
]
), we get that, if
w_{n}
is the first eigenfunction of (
3.23
) associated with
\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)
, then
\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial\alpha}
and
\frac{\partial\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)}{\partial\alpha}
are continuous functions. By taking the derivative in (
3.23
) with respect to
\alpha
, we get that

(3.35)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\quad-(p-1)\left(\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial\alpha}\right)^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial\alpha}\right)^{\prime}\left((N-1)+\frac{(p-2)(N+\alpha)}{1+r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}}}\right)\\
&-\frac{w^{\prime}_{n}}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\left(\frac{(p-2)(N+\alpha)}{1+r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}}}\right)-\frac{(N+\alpha)(Np+p\alpha-N+p)}{p-1}\frac{r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}-2}}{(1+r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}})^{2}}\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial\alpha}\\
&-\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\left(\frac{(N+\alpha)(Np+p\alpha-N+p)}{p-1}\frac{r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}-2}}{(1+r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}})^{2}}\right)w_{n}\\
&=\frac{\partial\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)}{\partial\alpha}\frac{w_{n}}{r^{2}}+\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)\frac{\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial\alpha}}{r^{2}},\qquad r\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right),\\
&\left(\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial\alpha}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Multiplying (
3.23
) by
\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial\alpha}
and (
3.35
) by
w_{n}
, integrating and subtracting, we obtain

(3.36)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\left(\frac{(N+\alpha)(Np+p\alpha-N+p)}{p-1}\frac{r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}-2}}{(1+r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}})^{2}}\right)w_{n}^{2}r^{N-1}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\left(\frac{(p-2)(N+\alpha)}{1+r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}}}\right)w_{n}w^{\prime}_{n}r^{N-2}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}r=\frac{\partial\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)}{\partial\alpha}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}w_{n}^{2}r^{N-3}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}r.

By Lemma
3.4
, we can pass to the limit in (
3.36
) and derive that, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
,

(3.37)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)}{\partial\alpha}\to

\displaystyle-\frac{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\left(\frac{(N+\alpha)(Np+p\alpha-N+p)}{p-1}\frac{r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}-2}}{(1+r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}})^{2}}\right)w^{2}r^{N-1}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}r}{\int_{0}^{+\infty}w^{2}r^{N-3}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}r}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\left(\frac{(p-2)(N+\alpha)}{1+r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}}}\right)ww^{\prime}r^{N-2}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}r}{\int_{0}^{+\infty}w^{2}r^{N-3}|U^{\prime}_{\alpha}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}r}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mu_{1}(\alpha)}{\partial\alpha}=-\frac{p(p+2\alpha+N-2)-2\alpha}{p^{2}}

uniformly on compact sets of
[0,+\infty)
, which proves (
3.32
).

Finally, due to
\frac{\partial\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)}{\partial\alpha}<0
and
\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\alpha)\to\mu_{1}(\alpha)
, we get that, the equation (
3.33
) has exactly one root
\alpha=\alpha_{k}^{n}
for any
k\geq 1
. Since
\mu_{1}(\alpha)=-\frac{(p+\alpha)(Np+p\alpha-p-\alpha)}{p^{2}}
and
\alpha=\alpha(k)
solves the equation

(3.38)

\displaystyle\frac{(p+\alpha)(Np+p\alpha-p-\alpha)}{p^{2}}=\lambda_{k}=k(N+k-2),

thus we have
\alpha(k)=\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\alpha_{k}^{n}
. This finishes our proof.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

3.2.
The bifurcation result in the ball

In this section, using some ideas in
[
31
]
, we prove bifurcation result of (

) in the ball. To this end, we need some notations. As mentioned before, we use

to represent the radial solution of (
3.1
) corresponding to the exponent
\alpha
, for
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{n}
,
B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}
to represent a ball centered at the origin with radius
\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}
. Define

(3.39)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(n):=\Big\{

\displaystyle(\alpha,u_{n,\alpha})\in(0,+\infty)\times C_{0}^{1,\eta}(\overline{B}_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}})\ \text{such\ that}\ u_{n,\alpha}

\displaystyle\text{is\ the\ radial\ solution\ of}\ \eqref{31}\ \text{defined\ in}\ \eqref{32}\Big\}.

For the given curve
\mathcal{K}(n)
, we call a point
(\alpha_{j},u_{n,\alpha_{j}})\in\mathcal{K}(n)
is a nonradial bifurcation point, if every neighborhood of
(\alpha_{j},u_{n,\alpha_{j}})
in
(0,+\infty)\times C_{0}^{1,\eta}(\overline{B}_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}})
, there exists a point
(\alpha,v_{n,\alpha})
which is the nonradial solution of (
3.1
).

Define the subspace
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
of
C_{0}^{1,\eta}(\overline{B}_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}})
as follows

(3.40)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{n}=\Big\{

\displaystyle h\in C_{0}^{1,\eta}(\overline{B}_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}})\ \text{such\ that}\ h(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})=h\left(g(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N-1}),x_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\text{for\ any}\ g\in\mathcal{O}(N-1)\Big\},

where
\mathcal{O}(N-1)
is the orthogonal group in
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
.

Now we need the following Theorem.

Theorem 3.7

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
T\in C^{1}(D,X)
be compact and such that
1
is not a characteristic value of
T^{\prime}(x_{0})
(i.e.,
S^{\prime}(x_{0})
is invertible) for some
x_{0}\in D
. Then, setting
S(x)=x-T(x)
and
S(x_{0})=p
, one has that
x_{0}
is an isolated solution of
S(x)=p
and there holds

i(S,x_{0})=(-1)^{\beta},

where
i(S,x_{0})
is the Morse index and
\beta
is the sum of the algebraic multiplicities of all the characteristic values of
T^{\prime}(x_{0})
contained in
(0,1)
.

Theorem 3.8
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
, let the operator
\mathcal{L}^{n}(\alpha,h):(0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{Z}_{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n}
be defined by
\mathcal{L}^{n}(\alpha,h):=(-\Delta_{p})^{-1}(|x|^{\alpha}|h+\beta_{\alpha}(n)|^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-2}(h+\beta_{\alpha}(n)))
, then the operator
I-\mathcal{L}_{h}^{n}(\alpha,u_{n,\alpha}):(0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{Z}_{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is invertible for
\alpha\neq\alpha_{k}^{n}
,
k=1,2,\cdots
, where
\alpha_{k}^{n}
are given by (
3.34
) and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{h}^{n}(\alpha,u_{n,\alpha}):=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{(-\Delta_{p})^{-1}(|x|^{\alpha}|u_{n,\alpha}+th+\beta_{\alpha}(n)|^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-2}(u_{n,\alpha}+th+\beta_{\alpha}(n)))-u_{n,\alpha}}{t}.

Proof.

Let
v\in\text{Ker}\left(I-\mathcal{L}_{h}^{n}(\alpha,u_{n,\alpha})\right)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{v}^{n}(\alpha,u_{n,\alpha}):=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{(-\Delta_{p})^{-1}(|x|^{\alpha}|u_{n,\alpha}+tv+\beta_{\alpha}(n)|^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-2}(u_{n,\alpha}+tv+\beta_{\alpha}(n)))-u_{n,\alpha}}{t}=v.

Thus, for any test function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}})
, we have

(3.41)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\left(|\nabla u_{n,\alpha}+t\nabla v+o(t)\nabla w|^{p-2}(\nabla u_{n,\alpha}+t\nabla v+o(t)\nabla w)-|\nabla u_{n,\alpha}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n,\alpha}\right)\nabla\varphi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha}\left((u_{n,\alpha}+tv+\beta_{\alpha}(n))^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-1}-(u_{n,\alpha}+\beta_{\alpha}(n))^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-1}\right)\varphi\mathrm{d}x,

where
w\in X
. On the one hand, from the LHS of the equation (
3.41
), we get

(3.42)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\left(|\nabla u_{n,\alpha}+t\nabla v+o(t)\nabla w|^{p-2}(\nabla u_{n,\alpha}+o(t)\nabla w)-|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n,\alpha}\right)\nabla\varphi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\int_{0}^{1}(p-2)|\nabla u_{n,\alpha}+st\nabla v+o(t)s\nabla w|^{p-4}\left(\left(\nabla u_{n,\alpha}+st\nabla v+o(t)s\nabla w\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\left(\nabla u_{n,\alpha}+st\nabla v+o(t)s\nabla w\right)\cdot\left(t\nabla v+o(t)w\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+|\nabla u_{n,\alpha}+st\nabla v+o(t)s\nabla w|^{p-2}\left(t\nabla v+o(t)w\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}x.

On the other hand, from the RHS of the equation (
3.41
), we have

(3.43)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha}\left((u_{n,\alpha}+tv+\beta_{\alpha}(n))^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-1}-(u_{n,\alpha}+\beta_{\alpha}(n))^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-1}\right)\varphi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=t(p_{\alpha}^{*}-1)\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha}\left(u_{n,\alpha}+\theta tv+\beta_{\alpha}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2}v\varphi\mathrm{d}x,

where
\theta\in(0,1)
. Combining (
3.42
), (
3.43
) with (
3.41
), and letting
t\to 0
, we get

(3.44)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\left(|\nabla u_{n,\alpha}|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi+(p-2)|\nabla u_{n,\alpha}|^{p-4}(\nabla u_{n,\alpha}\cdot\nabla v)\nabla u_{n,\alpha}\cdot\nabla\varphi\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=(p_{\alpha}^{*}-1)\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha}(u_{n,\alpha}+\beta_{\alpha}(n))^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-2}v\varphi\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\forall\ \varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}),

that is,
v
weakly solves (
3.8
), which is the linearized equation of (
3.1
) around
u_{n,\alpha}
. By Lemma
3.3
and Remark
3.6
, we know that, if
\alpha\neq\alpha_{k}^{n}
for

, then
u_{n,\alpha}
is non-degenerate and hence

. Therefore,
\text{Ker}\left(I-\mathcal{L}_{h}^{n}(\alpha,u_{n,\alpha})\right)=\{0\}
, i.e.,

is invertible when

∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\alpha_{k}^{n}
be given by (
3.33
). Then the points
(\alpha_{k}^{n},u_{n,\alpha_{k}^{n}})
are nonradial bifurcation points of the curve
\mathcal{K}(n)
. Furthermore, these nonradial solutions bifurcating from
(\alpha_{k}^{n},u_{n,\alpha_{k}^{n}})
lie in the space
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
and hence are
\mathcal{O}(N-1)
-invariant.

Proof.

From Smoller and Wasserman
[
51
]
, we know that, for any
k\geq 1
, the eigenspace
V_{k}
of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}

\mathcal{O}(N-1)
invariant eigenfunctions corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
, is one-dimensional. This implies that

(3.45)

\displaystyle m(\alpha_{k}^{n}+\delta)-m(\alpha_{k}^{n}-\delta)=1,

if
\delta>0
is sufficiently small, where
m(\alpha)
is the Morse index of the radial solution
u_{n,\alpha}
in the space
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
.

Consider the operator
\mathcal{L}^{n}(\alpha,h):(0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{Z}_{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n}
, where
\mathcal{L}^{n}(\alpha,h):=(-\Delta_{p})^{-1}(|x|^{\alpha}|h+\beta_{\alpha}(n)|^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-2}(h+\beta_{\alpha}(n)))

\displaystyle\beta_{\alpha}(n):=U_{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)=\frac{C_{N,p,\alpha}\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}{(1+\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-p}{p+\alpha}}}

\mathcal{L}^{n}
is a compact operator for every fixed
\alpha
and is continuous with respect to
\alpha
.

Suppose on the contrary that
(\alpha_{k}^{n},u_{n,\alpha_{k}^{n}})
is not a nonradial bifurcation point. Let
T^{n}(\alpha,h):=h-\mathcal{L}^{n}(\alpha,h)
. From Lemma
3.1
, we know that
u_{n,\alpha}
is radially nondegenerate and hence is the unique radial solution to (
3.1
) for any
\alpha
. Thus there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that, for
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
and every
\rho\in(0,\delta_{0})
, we have

(3.46)

\displaystyle T^{n}(\alpha,h)\neq 0,\quad\quad\forall\,\,h\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}\quad\text{such\ that}\quad\|h-u_{n,\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\leq\rho

\displaystyle\quad\text{and}\quad h\neq u_{n,\alpha},\quad\forall\alpha\in(\alpha_{k}^{n}-\delta,\alpha_{k}^{n}+\delta).

At the same time, we can choose
\delta_{0}
such that the interval
[\alpha_{k}^{n}-\delta,\alpha_{k}^{n}+\delta]
does not contain any other points
\alpha_{j}^{n}
with

.

Let the set
\mathcal{S}:=\{(\alpha,h)\in[\alpha_{k}^{n}-\delta,\alpha_{k}^{n}+\delta]\times\mathcal{Z}_{n}:\|h-u_{n,\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\leq\rho\}
. Noting that

is a compact perturbation of the identity, we will consider the Leray-Schauder topological degree
\deg(T^{n}(\alpha,\cdot),\mathcal{S}_{\alpha},0)
of

on the set
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}:=\{h\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}\ \text{such\ that}\ (\alpha,h)\in\mathcal{S}\}

3.46

(3.47)

\displaystyle\deg(T^{n}(\alpha,\cdot),\mathcal{S}_{\alpha},0)\quad\text{is\ constant\ on}\ [\alpha_{k}^{n}-\delta,\alpha_{k}^{n}+\delta].

Since from Theorem
3.8
, operator
I-\mathcal{L}_{h}^{n}(\alpha,u_{n,\alpha}):(0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{Z}_{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is invertible for

and

\displaystyle\deg(T^{n}(\alpha_{k}^{n}-\delta,\cdot),\mathcal{S}_{\alpha_{k}^{n}-\delta},0)=(-1)^{m(\alpha_{k}^{n}-\delta)}

\displaystyle\deg(T^{n}(\alpha_{k}^{n}+\delta,\cdot),\mathcal{S}_{\alpha_{k}^{n}+\delta},0)=(-1)^{m(\alpha_{k}^{n}+\delta)}.

From the choice of
\alpha_{k}^{n}
and of the space
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
and (
3.45

\displaystyle\deg(T^{n}(\alpha_{k}^{n}+\delta,\cdot),\mathcal{S}_{\alpha_{k}^{n}+\delta},0)=-\deg(T^{n}(\alpha_{k}^{n}-\delta,\cdot),\mathcal{S}_{\alpha_{k}^{n}-\delta},0),

which contradicts (
3.47
). From Lemma
3.1
,
u_{n,\alpha}
is radially nondegenerate and hence is the unique radial solution to (
3.1
) for any
\alpha
, thus
(\alpha_{k}^{n},u_{n,\alpha_{k}^{n}})
is a nonradial bifurcation point and the bifurcating solutions are nonradial. This finishes our proof.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
k
be an even integer and
\alpha_{k}^{n}
be given by equation (
3.33
). Then, there exist
\left[\frac{N}{2}\right]
distinct nonradial solutions of (
3.1
) that bifurcate from the pair
(\alpha_{k}^{n},u_{n,\alpha_{n}^{k}})
, where
[x]
denotes the largest integer that is less than or equal to
x
.

Proof.

Let us take the subgroups
\mathcal{G}_{l}
of
\mathcal{O}(N)
into account, which are defined as follows:

(3.48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{l}=\mathcal{O}(l)\times\mathcal{O}(N-l)\quad\,\,\text{for}\,\,1\leq l\leq\left[\frac{N}{2}\right].

From
[
52
]
(also see
[
56
]
), we also know that, when
k
is an even integer, the eigenspace
V_{k,l}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, which remains invariant under the action of
\mathcal{G}_{l}
, is one-dimensional.

First, let
\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{l}
be the subspace of
C_{0}^{1,\eta}(\overline{B}_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}})
consisting of functions that are invariant under the action of
\mathcal{G}_{l}
. Then, similar to the expression in (
3.45
), we can conclude that

m^{l}\left(\alpha_{k}^{n}+\delta\right)-m^{l}\left(\alpha_{k}^{n}-\delta\right)=1

holds when
\delta>0
is sufficiently small. Here,
m^{l}(\alpha)
represents the Morse index of the radial solution
u_{n,\alpha}
within the space
\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{l}
.

By applying the same reasoning as in the proof of previous Theorem
3.9
, we obtain that
(\alpha_{k}^{n},u_{n,\alpha_{n}^{k}})
is a bifurcation point. Moreover, the solution that bifurcates from this point remains invariant under the action of
\mathcal{G}_{l}
.

Furthermore, assume that we obtain a solution
h
that remains invariant under the actions of two groups
\mathcal{G}_{l_{1}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{l_{2}}
, where
l_{1}\neq l_{2}
. According to the results in
[
52
]
,
h
have to be radial. However, this is impossible because the radial solutions
u_{n,\alpha}
are isolated.

Consequently, we can conclude the existence of
\left[\frac{N}{2}\right]
distinct non-radial solutions of (
3.1
) that bifurcate from the point
(\alpha_{k}^{n},u_{n,\alpha_{n}^{k}})
.
∎

Let
\Pi_{n}
be the closure in
(0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{Z}_{n}
of the set of solutions of
T^{n}(\alpha,h)=0
that are different from
u_{n,\alpha}
, i.e.,

(3.49)

\displaystyle\Pi_{n}:=\overline{\Big\{(\alpha,h)\in(0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{Z}_{n}\ \text{such\ that}\ T^{n}(\alpha,h)=0\ \text{with}\ h\neq u_{n,\alpha}\Big\}},

where
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is defined in (
3.40
) and
T^{n}(\alpha,h)
is defined in Theorems
3.9
and
3.10
. If
(\alpha^{n}_{k},u_{n,\alpha^{n}_{k}})\in\mathcal{K}(n)
is a
nonradial bifurcation point, then
(\alpha^{n}_{k},u_{n,\alpha^{n}_{k}})\in\Pi_{n}
.

We call
\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})\subset\Pi_{n}
the closed connected component of
\Pi_{n}
which
contains
(\alpha^{n}_{k},u_{n,\alpha^{n}_{k}})
and it is maximal with respect to the inclusion.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\alpha_{k}^{n}
be defined by equation (
3.33
). If
(\alpha,h_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
, then
h_{\alpha}
is a solution of (
3.1
) corresponding to
\varepsilon_{n}
, in particular,
h_{\alpha}>0
in
B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}
.

Proof.

Obviously,
h_{\alpha}
is a solution of (
3.1
). Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
be a subset consisting of the points
(\alpha,h_{\alpha})
which are non-negative solutions of
T^{n}(\alpha,h)=0
. Note that
(\alpha^{n}_{k},u_{n,\alpha^{n}_{k}})\in\mathcal{C}
. We aim to prove
\mathcal{C}
is open and closed in
\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
, then
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
because
\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
is connected. Furthermore, by the maximum principle, we deduce that, if
(\alpha,h_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{C}
, then either
h_{\alpha}>0
or
h_{\alpha}\equiv 0
, however, zero is not a solution of (
3.1
), thus
h_{\alpha}>0
.

First, we prove
\mathcal{C}
is open in
\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
. Let
(\alpha,h_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{C}
and
(\hat{\alpha},h_{\hat{\alpha}})\in\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
such that

\|h_{\alpha}-h_{\hat{\alpha}}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}<\frac{1}{2}\beta_{\alpha}(n)
and
\frac{1}{2}\beta_{\alpha}(n)<\beta_{\hat{\alpha}}(n)<2\beta_{\alpha}(n)
, then

(3.50)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}h_{\hat{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=|x|^{\hat{\alpha}}|h_{\hat{\alpha}}+\beta_{\hat{\alpha}}(n)|^{p_{\hat{\alpha}}^{*}-2}(h_{\hat{\alpha}}+\beta_{\hat{\alpha}}(n))

\displaystyle=|x|^{\hat{\alpha}}|h_{\hat{\alpha}}+\beta_{\hat{\alpha}}(n)|^{p_{\hat{\alpha}}^{*}-2}(h_{\alpha}+h_{\hat{\alpha}}-h_{\alpha}+\beta_{\hat{\alpha}}(n))\geq 0\quad\text{in}\ B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}.

Then
(\hat{\alpha},h_{\hat{\alpha}})\in\mathcal{C}
. Thus
\mathcal{C}
is open in
\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
. On the other hand, if
(\alpha,h_{\alpha})
is a point in the closure of
\mathcal{C}
, then there is a sequence of points
(\alpha_{i},h_{i})
in
\mathcal{C}
that converges to
(\alpha,h_{\alpha})\in(0,+\infty)\times C_{0}^{1,\eta}(\overline{B}_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}})
, as
i\to+\infty
. We have
h_{\alpha}
is a solution of
T^{n}(\alpha,h_{\alpha})=0
and
h_{\alpha}\geq 0
in
B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}
. Thus
\mathcal{C}
is closed in
\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
, and hence
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
. This concludes our proof of Proposition
3.11
.
∎

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\alpha_{k}^{n}
be defined by equation (
3.33
) and let
\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
be the closed connected component of
\Pi_{n}
which contains
(\alpha^{n}_{k},u_{n,\alpha^{n}_{k}})
and it is maximal with respect to the inclusion. Then either

(i)
\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
is unbounded in
[0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{Z}_{n}
, or

(ii) there exists
\alpha_{i}^{n}
with
i\neq k
such that
(\alpha^{n}_{i},u_{n,\alpha^{n}_{i}})\in\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
, or

(iii)
\mathcal{C}(\alpha_{k}^{n})
meets
\{0\}\times\mathcal{Z}_{n}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the global bifurcation result presented in
[
2
]
. For more detailed information, please refer to
[
32
,
46
]
.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

The results of Proposition

4.
Crucial estimates of approximate solutions

In this section, we will prove some crucial estimates for the approximate solution of (
3.1
), where the solution does not necessarily need to be nonradial.

First, by comparing with the radial function
\Phi_{n}
and maximum principle, we will overcome the absence of the Green function representation formula and prove the uniformly fast decay estimate for approximate solutions
v_{n}
on large ball
B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}
in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\alpha_{n}
and
\varepsilon_{n}
be sequences such that
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha>0
and
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
.
Let
v_{n}
be a sequence of solutions to

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}v_{n}=|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\left(v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1},&x\in B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}},\\
v_{n}\geq 0,&x\in B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}},\\
v_{n}=0,&x\in\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}},\end{cases}

where
\displaystyle\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n):=U_{\alpha_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)=\frac{C_{N,p,{\alpha_{n}}}\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}{(1+\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{p+\alpha_{n}}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-p}{p+\alpha_{n}}}}
. Suppose that
\|v_{n}\|_{X}\leq A
for some uniform positive constant
A
for every
n
. Then, there exists
C>0
independent of
n
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle v_{n}(x)\leq\frac{C}{(1+|x|)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}\quad\,\,\,for\ every\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\ and\ for\ every\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Since there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\leq\frac{C}{(1+|x|)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}},\qquad\forall\,\,x\in B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}.

Note that
\|v_{n}\|_{\gamma}\leq\|v_{n}\|_{X}\leq A
and from (
4.3
), we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle|v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{(1+|x|)^{\gamma}},\qquad\forall\,\,x\in B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}\ \text{with}\ \gamma<\frac{N-p}{p-1}

for a uniform constant
C_{0}>0
. Let
\Phi_{n}(|x|)
be a radial function satisfying

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}\Phi_{n}=\frac{C_{0}^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-1}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}}{(1+|x|)^{\gamma(p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1)}},&x\in B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}},\\
\Phi_{n}=0,&x\in\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}.\end{cases}

Writing the equation (
4.5
) in radial variable, we can get

\displaystyle-\left(r^{N-1}|\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(r)|^{p-2}\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(r)\right)^{\prime}=\frac{C_{0}^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}r^{\alpha_{n}+N-1}}{(1+r)^{\gamma(p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1)}}.

Integrating from
0
to
r
, we get

\displaystyle-r^{N-1}|\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(r)|^{p-2}\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(r)=C_{0}^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{t^{\alpha_{n}+N-1}}{(1+t)^{\gamma(p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1)}}\mathrm{d}t>0,

thus
\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(r)<0
. Furthermore, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle-\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(r)=\left(\frac{C_{0}^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}}{r^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{t^{\alpha_{n}+N-1}}{(1+t)^{\gamma(p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1)}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

In order to ensure

\frac{r^{\alpha_{n}+N-1}}{(1+r)^{\gamma(p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1)}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, we demand
\gamma>\frac{N(N-p)}{Np-(N-p)}>\frac{N+\alpha_{n}}{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}
. Thus, from (
4.6
), we get
-\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(r)\leq Cr^{-\frac{N-1}{p-1}}
. Consequently,

\Phi_{n}(r)\leq\frac{C}{r^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}.

Combining (
4.1
) with (
4.5
), we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}\Phi_{n}\geq-\Delta_{p}v_{n}&\text{in}\ B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}},\\
\Phi_{n}=v_{n}&\text{on}\ \partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}.\end{cases}

By the maximum principle (see e.g.
[
3
,
12
,
16
,
18
,
23
,
47
]
), we have
v_{n}\leq\Phi_{n}
, thus (
4.2
) holds true.
∎

In order to show the existence of bifurcation points, by using the Pohozaev identity and maximum principle, and comparing with the radial function
\Psi_{n}
and
|\nabla\Psi_{n}|
, we will overcome the absence of the Green function representation formula and prove
\lambda=1
in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\alpha_{n}
and
\varepsilon_{n}
be sequences such that
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha>0
and
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
.
Let
v_{n}
be a sequence of solutions of (
4.1
) in
B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}
, corresponding to the exponent
\alpha_{n}
. If
v_{n}\to U_{\lambda,\alpha}
in
X
, then we have
\lambda=1
.

Proof.

Multiplying the equation (
4.1
) by
v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)
, and integrating on
B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}
, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p-1}\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\left(v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x.

On the one hand, multiplying the equation (
4.1
) by
x\cdot\nabla(v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n))
and integrating on
B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}
, we obtain

(4.9)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{N-p}{p}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{p-1}{p}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{N+\alpha_{n}}{p^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\left(v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)^{N+\alpha_{n}}\left(\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}}\omega_{N-1}.

where
\omega_{N-1}
represents the
N-1
dimensional unit sphere area.

By subtracting (
4.8
)
\times\frac{N-p}{p}
from (
4.9
), from (
4.1
), we get

(4.10)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{p-1}{p}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{N-p}{p}\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\left(v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)^{N+\alpha_{n}}\left(\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}}\omega_{N-1}.

From Hölder inequality, we obtain

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\int_{\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p-1}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\left(\int_{\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\left(v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\left(\int_{\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

By (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we obtain

(4.12)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{N-p}{p}\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\left(v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)^{N+\alpha_{n}}\left(\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}}\omega_{N-1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p-1}{p}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\left(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\left(v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\left(\int_{\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Let
n\to+\infty
, we have

\frac{N-p}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha}U_{\lambda,\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-1}\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{p-1}{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha}U_{\lambda,\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-1}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\omega_{N-1}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Thus, we can get

(4.13)

\displaystyle\lambda\geq 1.

On the other hand, since
v_{n}\to U_{\lambda,\alpha}
in
X
as
n\to\infty
, then
\forall\delta>0
,
\exists\ N_{0}
such that,
\forall\ n>N_{0},

\|v_{n}-U_{\lambda,\alpha}\|_{X}<\delta.

Moreover,

v_{n}\leq U_{\lambda,\alpha}+\frac{\delta}{(1+|x|)^{\gamma}},

where
\gamma\in\left(\frac{N(N-p)}{Np-(N-p)},\frac{N-p}{p-1}\right)
. Let us consider a radial function
\Psi_{n}(|x|)
satisfying

(4.14)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}\Psi_{n}(|x|)=|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\left(U_{\lambda,\alpha}+\frac{\delta}{(1+|x|)^{\gamma}}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}&\text{in}\ B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}},\\
\Psi_{n}(|x|)=0&\text{on}\ \partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}.\end{cases}

By (
4.14
), we get

(4.15)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}\Psi_{n}\geq-\Delta_{p}v_{n}&\text{in}\ B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}},\\
\Psi_{n}=v_{n}&\text{on}\ \partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}.\end{cases}

Moreover, we have
\nabla(\Psi_{n}-v_{n})\cdot\nabla v_{n}\geq 0
and
\nabla(\Psi_{n}-v_{n})\cdot\nabla\Psi_{n}\geq 0
on
\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}
, and hence

|\nabla\Psi_{n}|\geq|\nabla v_{n}|\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}.

According to the Pohozaev identity, we have

(4.16)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{N-p}{p}\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\left(v_{n}+\beta(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)^{N+\alpha_{n}}\left(\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}}\omega_{N-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-1}{p}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p-1}{p}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|\nabla\Psi_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-1}{p}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\left(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\left(U_{\lambda,\alpha}+\frac{\delta}{(1+|x|)^{\gamma}}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\left(\int_{\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Letting
\delta\to 0,n\to+\infty
, from Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we get

\frac{N-p}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha}U_{\lambda,\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-1}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{p-1}{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha}U_{\lambda,\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-1}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\omega_{N-1}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Thus, we infer that

(4.17)

\displaystyle\lambda\leq 1.

From (
4.13
) and (
4.17
), we obtain
\lambda=1.
This concludes our proof of Proposition
4.2
.
∎

Furthermore, based on Proposition
4.1
, we can obtain an upper bound estimate of the
\nabla v_{n}(x)
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\alpha_{n}
and
\varepsilon_{n}
be sequences such that
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha>0
and
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
.
Let
v_{n}
be a sequence of solutions of (
4.1
) in
B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}
, corresponding to the exponent
\alpha_{n}
. If
\|v_{n}\|_{X}\leq A
for some uniform positive constant
A
for every
n
, then there exists
C>0
independent of
n
such that

(4.18)

\displaystyle|\nabla v_{n}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{(1+|x|)^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}}\ \ for\ every\ x\in B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}\ and\ for\ every\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

For all
1<|y|<\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}
,
\forall 1<R<\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}|y|}
, define
v_{n}^{R}(y):=R^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}v_{n}(Ry)
. From Proposition
4.1
, we get
v_{n}(x)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{(1+|x|)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}
for some uniform constant
C_{0}>0
, and hence

(4.19)

\displaystyle v_{n}^{R}(y)\leq R^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\frac{C_{0}}{|Ry|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}\leq C_{0}.

Moreover,
v_{n}^{R}(y)
satisfies

(4.20)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}v_{n}^{R}(y)

\displaystyle=R^{N+\alpha_{n}}|y|^{\alpha_{n}}\left(v_{n}(Ry)+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}R^{N+\alpha_{n}}|y|^{\alpha_{n}}\left(R|y|\right)^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}(p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1)}\leq C_{1},\qquad\forall\,\,1<|y|<\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}},

where
C_{1}
is independent of
n
,
R
and
y
. Consequently, from the gradient regularity estimate in
[
53
]
, we have

\|\nabla_{y}v_{n}^{R}(y)\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,4)\setminus B(0,2))}\leq C_{2},

where
C_{2}
is independent of
n
and
R
.

For all
1<|x|<\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}
, let
x=Ry
, where
2<|y|<4
, thus we get

|\nabla_{x}v_{n}(x)|=R^{\frac{1-N}{p-1}}|\nabla_{y}v_{n}^{R}(y)|\leq C_{2}R^{\frac{1-N}{p-1}}\leq\frac{4^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}C_{2}}{|x|^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}}.

This gives the desired estimate (
4.18
).
∎

Given
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
q\geq 1
, and a non-negative locally integrable function
\omega_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the Banach space
L^{q}(\Omega;\omega_{0})
as the space of measurable functions
\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that the norm

\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\,\omega_{0})}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{q}\,\omega_{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<+\infty.

Given
\omega_{1}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
non-negative,
we denote by
C^{1}_{c,0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the space of compactly supported functions of class
C^{1}
that are constant in a neighborhood of the origin, and we
define
\dot{W}^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\omega_{1})
as the closure of
C^{1}_{c,0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm

\|\varphi\|_{\dot{W}^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\,\omega_{1})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D\varphi|^{q}\,\omega_{1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Inspired by Figalli and Zhang
[
29
]
, we can prove the following weighted Sobolev embedding
\dot{W}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\,|\nabla U_{\alpha}|^{p-2})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\,|x|^{\beta}U_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-2})
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume
1<p<N
and
\alpha>0
. If
\varphi\in\dot{W}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\,|\nabla U_{\alpha}|^{p-2})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\,|x|^{\beta}U_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-2})
,

\beta\in\left[\frac{p\alpha-2\alpha-p}{p-1},\frac{p+p\alpha}{p-1}\right]\bigcap\left(\frac{p\alpha+2p-N}{p-1},+\infty\right)
, then we have

(4.21)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\beta}U_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-2}|\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C(N,p,\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla U_{\alpha}|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

To prove (
4.21
), we can assume by approximation that
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. From Fubini’s theorem and using polar coordinates, from
\beta>\frac{p\alpha+2p-N}{p-1},
we obtain

(4.22)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\beta}U_{\alpha}^{p_{\alpha}^{*}-2}|\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(N,p,\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}r^{\beta+N-1}\left(1+r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}}\right)^{-\frac{N-p}{p+\alpha}(\frac{p(N+\alpha)}{N-p}-2)}|\varphi(r\theta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq C(N,p,\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}r^{\beta+N-1}\left(1+r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}}\right)^{-\frac{N-p}{p+\alpha}(\frac{p(N+\alpha)}{N-p}-2)}\int_{r}^{+\infty}|\varphi(t\theta)||\nabla\varphi(t\theta)|\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq C(N,p,\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}|\varphi(t\theta)||\nabla\varphi(t\theta)|r^{\beta+N-1}\left(1+r^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}}\right)^{-\frac{N-p}{p+\alpha}(\frac{p(N+\alpha)}{N-p}-2)}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq C(N,p,\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\varphi(t\theta)||\nabla\varphi(t\theta)|t^{\beta+N}\left(1+t^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}}\right)^{-\frac{N-p}{p+\alpha}(\frac{p(N+\alpha)}{N-p}-2)}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq C(N,p,\alpha)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\varphi(t\theta)|^{2}t^{\beta+N-1}\left(1+t^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}}\right)^{-\frac{N-p}{p+\alpha}(\frac{p(N+\alpha)}{N-p}-2)}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\nabla\varphi(t\theta)|^{2}t^{\beta+N+1}\left(1+t^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}}\right)^{-\frac{N-p}{p+\alpha}(\frac{p(N+\alpha)}{N-p}-2)}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Note that, for
|x|\in(0,1]
, if
\beta+2\geq\frac{(1+\alpha)(p-2)}{p-1}
, then

|x|^{\beta+2}\sim|x|^{\beta+2-\frac{(1+\alpha)(p-2)}{p-1}}|\nabla U_{\alpha}|^{p-2}\leq C|\nabla U_{\alpha}|^{p-2};

and for
|x|\in(1,+\infty)
, if
\beta+2\leq\frac{3p+p\alpha-2}{p-1}
, then

|x|^{\beta+2}\left(1+|x|^{\frac{p+\alpha}{p-1}}\right)^{-\frac{N-p}{p+\alpha}(\frac{p(N+\alpha)}{N-p}-2)}\sim|x|^{\beta+2-\frac{3p+p\alpha-2}{p-1}}|\nabla U_{\alpha}|^{p-2}\leq C|\nabla U_{\alpha}|^{p-2}.

Hence (
4.21
) follows from (
4.22
). This finishes our proof.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

In
[
38
]
, without any symmetry assumption on
V
or
f
, the authors obtained the following
L^{p}
-Hardy inequality:

(4.23)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}V|\nabla u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\geq-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mathrm{div}(V|\nabla f|^{q-2}\nabla f)}{f^{q-1}}|u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x.

The estimate (
4.21
) in Proposition
4.4
may also be derived from (
4.23
) by suitable choice of the function
f
.

By applying the weighted Sobolev inequality (
4.21
) in Proposition
4.4
with
\alpha=\alpha_{n}
, we overcome the nonlinear virtue of the
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{p}
and first prove the following uniformly decay estimate in integral form for
w_{n}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

If
p\in(1,N)
,
\beta\in(\max\{\alpha_{n},\frac{p\alpha_{n}-2\alpha_{n}-p}{p-1},\frac{p\alpha_{n}+2p-N}{p-1}\},\frac{p+p\alpha_{n}}{p-1}]
, then there exists some uniform constant
C>0
independent of
n
such that

(4.24)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\beta}U_{\alpha_{n}}^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2}|w_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C.

Proof.

Taking
\varphi=w_{n}:=\frac{u_{n}-v_{n}}{\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}
in Proposition
4.4
, by
|w_{n}|\leq 1
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\beta}U_{\alpha_{n}}^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2}|w_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}|\nabla U_{\alpha_{n}}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}+|\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\nabla(u_{n}-v_{n})\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla v_{n}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\xi_{n}(x)|w_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=C\int_{B_{1}}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\xi_{n}(x)|w_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}\setminus B_{1}}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\xi_{n}(x)|w_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

(4.25)

\displaystyle\leq C+C\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}\setminus B_{1}}|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\xi_{n}(x)|w_{n}|^{2-\delta}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C+C\left(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\beta}U_{\alpha_{n}}^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2}|w_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2-\delta}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}\setminus B_{1}}|x|^{\frac{2\alpha_{n}}{\delta}}|\xi_{n}(x)|^{\frac{2}{\delta}}|x|^{-\frac{\beta(2-\delta)}{\delta}}U_{\alpha_{n}}^{-(p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2)\frac{2-\delta}{\delta}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{\delta}{2}}

for
\delta\in(0,2)
, where

\displaystyle\xi_{n}(x):

\displaystyle=(p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1)\int_{0}^{1}\left(tu_{n}+(1-t)v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2}\mathrm{d}t.

If we can find
\delta\in(0,2)
such that

(4.26)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}\setminus B_{1}}|x|^{\frac{2\alpha_{n}}{\delta}}|\xi_{n}(x)|^{\frac{2}{\delta}}|x|^{-\frac{\beta(2-\delta)}{\delta}}U_{\alpha_{n}}^{-(p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2)\frac{2-\delta}{\delta}}\mathrm{d}x<+\infty,

then we can get (
4.24
). In fact, from Proposition
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}\setminus B_{1}}|x|^{\frac{2\alpha_{n}}{\delta}}|\xi_{n}(x)|^{\frac{2}{\delta}}|x|^{-\frac{\beta(2-\delta)}{\delta}}U_{\alpha_{n}}^{-(p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2)\frac{2-\delta}{\delta}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}\setminus B_{1}}|x|^{\frac{2\alpha_{n}}{\delta}}\left(1+|x|\right)^{-\frac{pN+p{\alpha_{n}}-2N+2p}{p-1}\frac{2}{\delta}}|x|^{-\frac{\beta(2-\delta)}{\delta}}(1+|x|)^{\frac{pN+p{\alpha_{n}}-2N+2p}{p-1}\frac{2-\delta}{\delta}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}\setminus B_{1}}|x|^{\frac{2\alpha_{n}}{\delta}-\frac{\beta(2-\delta)}{\delta}}\left(1+|x|\right)^{-\frac{pN+p\alpha_{n}-2N+2p}{p-1}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{r^{\frac{2\alpha_{n}}{\delta}-\frac{\beta(2-\delta)}{\delta}}r^{N-1}}{\left(1+r\right)^{\frac{pN+p\alpha_{n}-2N+2p}{p-1}}}\mathrm{d}r.

Since
\beta\in(\max\{\alpha_{n},\frac{p\alpha_{n}-2\alpha)n-p}{p-1},\frac{p\alpha_{n}+2p-N}{p-1}\},\frac{p+p\alpha_{n}}{p-1}]
, taking
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{2(\beta-\alpha_{n})}{\beta+\frac{N-p\alpha_{n}-2p}{p-1}}\right)
, we have

\frac{pN+p\alpha_{n}-2N+2p}{p-1}-\left(\frac{2\alpha_{n}}{\delta}-\frac{\beta(2-\delta)}{\delta}+N-1\right)>1.

Therefore, we can take
\delta\in\left(0,\min\left\{\frac{2(\beta-\alpha_{n})}{\beta+\frac{N-p\alpha_{n}-2p}{p-1}},2\right\}\right)
, then from (
4.25
) and Young inequality, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\beta}U_{\alpha_{n}}^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2}|w_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C+C\left(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\beta}U_{\alpha_{n}}^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2}|w_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2-\delta}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C+\frac{\delta}{2}C^{\frac{2}{\delta}}+\frac{2-\delta}{2}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\beta}U_{\alpha_{n}}^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2}|w_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

and hence (
4.24
) holds. This concludes our proof of Proposition
4.6
.
∎

In order to overcome the difficulties caused by the unavailability of Kelvin type transforms and the absence of the Green function representation formula, based on (
4.24
), through a De Giorgi-Moser-Nash iteration argument, we can finally establish the uniformly fast decay estimate (
4.27
) for
w_{n}
in the following proposition, without using Kelvin type transforms and the Green function representation formula.

Proposition 4.7
.

Assume
1<p<N
. Let
\alpha_{n}
and
\varepsilon_{n}
be sequences such that
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha>0
and
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
. Let
v_{n}
be a sequence of nonradial solutions of (
4.1
) in
B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}
related to the exponent
\alpha_{n}
, and let
w_{n}:=\frac{u_{n}-v_{n}}{\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}
, where
u_{n}
is the radial solution of (
4.1
) related to the exponent
\alpha_{n}
given by (
3.2
). If
v_{n}\to U_{\alpha}
in
X
, then there exists a uniform constant
C_{0}>0
independent of
n
such that

(4.27)

\displaystyle|w_{n}(y)|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{(1+|y|)^{\frac{N-p}{2(p-1)}}},\qquad\forall\,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Proof.

We will carry out our proof by discussing the following two different cases.

\bullet
Case 1:
1\leq|y|<\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{n}}
.

From
w_{n}=0
on
x\in\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}
, by taking
\alpha=\alpha_{n}
and
\beta=\frac{p+p\alpha_{n}}{p-1}
in Proposition
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}}|x|^{\frac{p+p\alpha_{n}}{p-1}}U_{\alpha_{n}}^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2}|w_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C.

Therefore, taking
R=\frac{5}{12}|y|
, we have

(4.28)

\displaystyle y\in B_{3R}\setminus B_{2R}\subset B_{4R}\setminus B_{R}\subset B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}},

and

(4.29)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{4R}\setminus B_{R}}|w_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq CR^{\frac{Np+p-2N}{p-1}}.

Define
f(x)=|x|^{p-2}x
,
f_{j}=|x|^{p-2}x_{j}
, thus

(f_{i})_{x_{j}}=(p-2)|x|^{p-4}x_{i}x_{j}+|x|^{p-2}\delta_{ij}:=A_{ij},

and

(4.30)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}A_{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\left((p-2)|x|^{p-4}x_{i}x_{j}+|x|^{p-2}\delta_{i,j}\right)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(p-2)|x|^{p-4}x_{i}\xi_{i}x_{j}\xi_{j}+|x|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle=|x|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}\left(1+(p-2)\left|\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}|x|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2},\quad&p>2,\\
(p-1)|x|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2},\quad&1<p\leq 2.\end{cases}

Note that

\displaystyle\quad|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla v_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(p-2)|t\nabla u_{n}+(1-t)\nabla v_{n}|^{p-4}(tu_{n}+(1-t)v_{n})_{x_{i}}(tu_{n}+(1-t)v_{n})_{x_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad+|t\nabla u_{n}+(1-t)\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}\delta_{ij}\mathrm{d}t\,(u_{n}-v_{n})_{x_{i}},

let us define

\displaystyle a_{ij}^{n}(x):

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(p-2)|t\nabla u_{n}+(1-t)\nabla v_{n}|^{p-4}(tu_{n}+(1-t)v_{n})_{x_{i}}(tu_{n}+(1-t)v_{n})_{x_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad+|t\nabla u_{n}+(1-t)\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}\delta_{i,j}\mathrm{d}t.

Then

\displaystyle-\text{div}(|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla v_{n})=|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\left((u_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n))^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}-(v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n))^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}\right),

it is equal to

\displaystyle-\left(a_{ij}^{n}(x)(u_{n}-v_{n})_{x_{j}}\right)_{x_{i}}=|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\left((u_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n))^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}-(v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n))^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1}\right).

From the definition of
w_{n}
, we get

\displaystyle-\left(a_{i,j}^{n}(x)(w_{n})_{x_{j}}\right)_{x_{i}}

\displaystyle=|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\int_{0}^{1}(p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1)\left(tu_{n}+(1-t)v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2}\mathrm{d}t\frac{u_{n}-v_{n}}{\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}

\displaystyle=:|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\zeta_{n}(x)w_{n},

where

\zeta_{n}(x):=\int_{0}^{1}(p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-1)\left(tu_{n}+(1-t)v_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)\right)^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2}\mathrm{d}t.

Thus
w_{n}
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\left(a_{ij}^{n}(x)(w_{n})_{x_{j}}\right)_{x_{i}}=|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\zeta_{n}(x)w_{n},&x\in B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}},\\
w_{n}(x)=0,&x\in\partial B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}.\end{cases}

On the one hand,

|a_{ij}^{n}(x)(w_{n})_{x_{j}}|\leq(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}|(1-t)\nabla u_{n}+t\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t|\nabla w_{n}|.

On the other hand, from (
4.30
), we have

\displaystyle a_{ij}^{n}(x)(w_{n})_{x_{j}}(w_{n})_{x_{i}}\geq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|(1-t)\nabla u_{n}+t\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t|\nabla w_{n}|^{2},&p\geq 2,\\
\displaystyle(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}|(1-t)\nabla u_{n}+t\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t|\nabla w_{n}|^{2},&1<p<2.\end{cases}

Let

\eta_{n}(x):=\int_{0}^{1}|(1-t)\nabla u_{n}+t\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t.

We have the following estimates on
\eta_{n}(x)
and
\zeta_{n}(x)
:

(4.31)

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(x)\geq\begin{cases}C(p)|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2},&p\geq 2,\\
\left(|\nabla u_{n}|+|\nabla v_{n}|\right)^{p-2},&1<p<2,\end{cases}

(4.32)

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(x)\leq\begin{cases}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|+|\nabla v_{n}|\right)^{p-2},&p\geq 2,\\
C(p)|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2},&1<p<2,\end{cases}

and

(4.33)

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(x)\leq\begin{cases}C|u_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)+v_{n}|^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2},&p\geq\frac{2N}{N+2+\alpha_{n}},\\
C|u_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n)|^{p_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-2},&1<p<\frac{2N}{N+2+\alpha_{n}}.\end{cases}

In fact, if
p\in[2,N)
and
|\nabla u_{n}|\geq|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|
, we have

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}\Big||\nabla u_{n}|-t|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|\Big|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|-t|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|\right)^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|\right)^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{p-1}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2},

and if
|\nabla u_{n}|<|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|
, we obtain

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}\Big||\nabla u_{n}|-t|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|\Big|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-1}+(|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|-|\nabla u_{n}|)^{p-1}}{|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2^{2-p}}{p-1}|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|^{p-2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2^{2-p}}{p-1}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}.

If
p\in(1,2)
, we get

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}|(1-t)\nabla u_{n}+t\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\left(|\nabla u_{n}|+|\nabla v_{n}|\right)^{p-2}.

Thus (
4.31
) holds true. If
p\in[2,N)
, we have

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}|(1-t)\nabla u_{n}+t\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\left(|\nabla u_{n}|+|\nabla v_{n}|\right)^{p-2}.

If
p\in(1,2)
and
|\nabla u_{n}|\geq|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|
, we obtain

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla u_{n}+t\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\Big||\nabla u_{n}|-t|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|\Big|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-1}-(|\nabla u_{n}|-|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|)^{p-1}}{|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|}

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2},

if
|\nabla u_{n}|<|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|
, then

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\Big||\nabla u_{n}|-t|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|\Big|^{p-2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-1}+(|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|-|\nabla u_{n}|)^{p-1}}{|\nabla(v_{n}-u_{n})|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{2-p}}{p-1}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}.

Thus (
4.32
) holds true. The estimate (
4.33
) follows from (
4.32
) directly. Now, we take

\displaystyle F(\overline{w}_{n})=\begin{cases}\overline{w}_{n}^{q},&\overline{w}_{n}\leq l,\\
ql^{q-1}\overline{w}_{n}-(q-1)l^{q},&\overline{w}_{n}>l\end{cases}

for some
q,l\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and

\displaystyle G(w_{n})=\text{sgn}(w_{n})F(\overline{w}_{n})F^{\prime}(\overline{w}_{n}),

where
\overline{w}_{n}:=|w_{n}|
. Thus

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\overline{w}_{n})=\begin{cases}q\overline{w}_{n}^{q-1},&\overline{w}_{n}\leq l,\\
ql^{q-1},&\overline{w}_{n}>l\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(w_{n})=\begin{cases}q^{-1}(2q-1)\left(F^{\prime}(\overline{w}_{n})\right)^{2},&\overline{w}_{n}\leq l,\\
\left(F^{\prime}(\overline{w}_{n})\right)^{2},&\overline{w}_{n}>l.\end{cases}

Since
B_{4R}\subset B_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}}
, for any given
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{R}{2}\right)
, we can choose cut-off function
\varphi(x)
satisfying
0\leq\varphi(x)\leq 1
,
\varphi(x)=1
in
B_{4R-\delta}\setminus B_{R+\delta}
,
\varphi(x)=0
in
(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{4R})\cup B_{R}
,
0<\varphi(x)<1
in
(B_{R+\delta}\setminus B_{R})\cup(B_{4R}\setminus B_{4R-\delta})
and
|\nabla\varphi|\leq\frac{C}{\delta}
. Let

\psi_{n}(x):=\varphi^{2}(x)G(w_{n}),

then

\left(\psi_{n}(x)\right)_{x_{i}}=2\varphi\varphi_{x_{i}}G(w_{n})+\varphi^{2}G^{\prime}(w_{n})\left(w_{n}\right)_{x_{i}}.

We have

(4.34)

\displaystyle\quad a_{ij}^{n}(x)(w_{n})_{x_{j}}(\psi_{n})_{x_{i}}-|x|^{\alpha_{n}}\zeta_{n}(x)w_{n}\psi_{n}

\displaystyle\quad\times(\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nu)(u_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n))\mathrm{d}S+\frac{N-p}{p}\int_{\partial\Omega_{n}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p-2}((\nabla u_{n}-\nabla v_{n})\cdot\nu)(u_{n}+\beta_{\alpha_{n}}(n))\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\int_{0}^{1}\left((1-t)|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+t|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\mathrm{d}t(\nabla u_{n}-\nabla v_{n})(\nabla u_{n}+\nabla v_{n})(x\cdot\nu)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}=0.

Using the decay properties of

\displaystyle+\frac{N-p}{p}\int_{\partial B_{r}}|\nabla U_{\alpha}|^{p-2}(\nabla w\cdot\nu)U_{\alpha}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}-\int_{\partial B_{r}}|\nabla U_{\alpha}|^{p-2}(\nabla w\cdot\nabla U_{\alpha})(x\cdot\nu)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}=0.

Plugging (

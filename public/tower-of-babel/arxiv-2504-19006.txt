Nowhere vanishing 1-forms on 4-folds
Benjamin Church

Abstract
. In this note, we prove – in dimension at most 4 4 – a conjectue of Hao which says that a morphism f : X → A f:X\to A to a simple abelian variety A A is smooth if and only if there is a 1 1 -form pulled back from A A without any zeros. We also give a complete classification of 4 4 -folds with a 1 1 -form without zeros not admitting a map to an elliptic curve.

Primary: 14J10. Secondary: 14D06, 14K05.

Introduction

[

1

[
6

[

[

Theorem B

(=Theorem
5.2
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective
4
-fold with a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
1
-form
\omega
. Assume
\mathrm{Alb}_{X}
does not contain an elliptic curve factor. Then there exists a sequence of blow-downs
\pi:X\to X^{\prime}
whose centers are smooth surfaces along which
\omega
is nonvanishing, and a smooth map
X^{\prime}\to A
to an abelian variety.

Hao made a related conjecture giving more precise control over the structure in Conjecture
A
when the abelian variety is simple:

Conjecture C
.

[
13
, Conj. 1.5]
.
Let
f:X\to A
be a morphism from a smooth projective variety
X
to a simple abelian variety
A
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

there exists a holomorphic
1
-form
\omega\in H^{0}(A,\Omega^{1}_{A})
such that
f^{\ast}\omega
is nowhere vanishing;

(2)

f:X\to A
is smooth.

We prove this conjecture for
4
-folds and give a complete classification of the possible examples.

Theorem D

(=Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
f:X\to A
be a morphism from a smooth projective 4-fold
X
to a simple abelian variety
A
. The following are equivalent:

(a)

there exists a holomorphic one-form
\omega\in H^{0}(A,\Omega^{1}_{A})
such that
f^{\ast}\omega
is nowhere vanishing;

(b)

f:X\to A
is smooth.

Moreover, when this holds then
X
has the following structure.

(1)

If
\dim{A}=2
then there exists a factorization
X\to X^{\min}\to A
such that
X\to X^{\min}
is a composition of blowups at étale multi-sections of
X^{\min}\to A
, and one of the following holds:

(a)

, and

(b)

.

(c)

The Stein factorization

is the composition of a smooth del Pezzo fibration

and an isogeny
A^{\prime}\to A
.

(2)

If

2.
Extremal Contractions and Nonvanishing
1
-Forms

By a
contraction
we mean a proper morphism
f:X\to Y
of varieties with
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}
. A contraction is
elementary
if the relative Picard number
\rho(X)-\rho(Y)
is
1
. We call a contraction
Fano-Mori
(or
good
in the terminology of
[
2
]
) if
-K_{X}
is
f
-ample. An
extremal contraction
is a contraction arising from an extremal
K_{X}
-negative curve in the cone of effective curves. These are particularly important examples of elementary Fano-Mori contractions. In this section, we will understand the possible types of extremal contractions from a smooth
4
-fold carrying
1
-forms with nonvanishing conditions. First, we review some terminology and fundamental results on the structure of contractions on 4-folds.

Definition 2.1
.

The
type
of a contraction
f:X\to Y
is the pair of integers

(n,m):=(\dim\Exc(f),\dim f(\Exc(f))

where
\Exc(f)\subset X
is the locus on which
f
is not an isomorphism. We say a contraction is

(1)

fiber type
if
\Exc(f)=X

(2)

birational
if
f
is birational i.e.
\dim\Exc(f)<\dim{X}

(3)

divisorial
if
\Exc(f)
is a divisor

(4)

small
if
\dim{\Exc(f)}\leq\dim{X}-2

Recall that for smooth 3-folds there are no small extremal contractions (hence no flips are necessary until MMP operations introduce singularities) and for smooth 4-folds, small contractions are well behaved according to the following result of Kawamata
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective
4
-fold and
f:X\to Y
a small elementary contraction. Then every connected component
E\subset\Exc(f)
satisfies
E\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
N_{E|X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1)^{\oplus 2}
.

After Mori’s groundbreaking work understanding contractions and flips on threefolds
[
21
,
22
]

4
. This led to a nearly complete classification of extremal contractions on smooth 4-folds carried out in numerous papers
[
1
,
17
,
14
,
2

]
.

On any non-singular projective variety

n

[

showed that type

3

1

2.0.1.
Notation

For
r\geq 0
let
\mathbb{F}_{r}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(r))
be the Hirzebruch surface. If
r\geq 1
then
\mathbb{F}_{r}
has a unique section
s_{0}
with negative self-intersection
-r
corresponding to the quotient
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(r)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
and let
f
be the class of a fiber. The relative tautological bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{r}}(1)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{r}}(s_{0}+rf)
. Note that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{r}}(K_{\mathbb{F}_{r}})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{r}}(-2)\otimes\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(r-2)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{r}}(-2s_{0}-(r+2)f)
. For
r\geq 1
let
\mathbb{S}_{r}
be the projective cone over the rational normal curve
\mathbb{P}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{r}
of degree
r
. Alternatively, we may describe
\mathbb{S}_{r}
as the image of
\mathbb{F}_{r}\to\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
defined by the basepoint-free complete linear series of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{r}}(s_{0}+rf)
which exactly contracts
s_{0}
. Note that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{S}_{r}}(1)
arising from the restriction of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r+1}}(1)
thus agrees with its other possible definition, the line bundle pulling back to the tautological bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{r}}(1)
. Note that
\mathbb{S}_{r}
is normal but not Gorenstein or even lci for
r>2
. Furthermore,
K_{\mathbb{S}_{r}}=-\frac{r+2}{r}H
where
H=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{S}_{r}}(1))
is the hyperplane class and the unique exceptional divisor has discrepancy
a=\frac{2}{r}-1
. Indeed,
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{r}\to\mathbb{S}_{r}
is the minimal resolution – performed via a single blowup of the cone point which is a cyclic quotient
(1,1)_{r}

r=2
then
\mathbb{S}_{r}
has canonical singularities (indeed a single
A_{1}

\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathbb{S}_{2}
is crepant but when
r>2
the discrepancy is negative. Furthermore, the embedding dimension of the cone point of
\mathbb{S}_{r}
is
r+1
.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
2

Let
f:X\to Y

\mathbb{Q}

4

F

3.
Smoothness in codimension
1

In this section, we use a purity result for the singular locus of a morphism between smooth varieties to show that flat morphisms to a simple abelian variety pulling back a
1
-form without introducing zeros must be smooth away from codimension
1
on the base. This uses a weak form of purity for maps between smooth varieties.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a dominant morphism of smooth varieties with relative dimension

. Then each irreducible component of the singular locus of

has codmension

Proof.

This follows immediately from the Jacobian criterion. Using the étale local structure of smooth varieties we can reduce to a map of affine spaces. Then the singular locus is cut out by the

∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{y}D}

4.1.
Conic Bundles

First we recall some basic facts about conic bundles.

Definition 4.1
.

A
conic bundle

f:X\to S
is a flat proper morphism of finite presentation such that each fiber
X_{s}
admits an embedding
X_{s}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\kappa(s)}
over
\kappa(s)
as a (possibly singular) conic.

A conic bundle admits a relative dualizing complex
\omega_{X/Y}^{\bullet}
which is a line bundle because the fibers are Gorenstein curves. Furthermore,
\omega_{X_{s}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}}(-1)
from the embedding and hence the embedding of each fiber is canonical and
\omega_{X/Y}^{\vee}
is
f
-ample. Then
\mathcal{E}=f_{*}\omega_{X/Y}^{\vee}
is a rank
3
vector bundle whose formation commutes with arbitrary base change because
H^{>0}(X_{s},\omega_{X_{s}}^{\vee})=0
. Finally, the map
X\to\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{E})
is a closed embedding which, on fibers, induces the embedding of the conic curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Therefore, a conic bundle is equivalently a relative Gorenstein curve with
\omega_{X/Y}^{\vee}
relatively very ample. Let
\mathbb{P}:=\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{E})
and
X\subset\mathbb{P}
is cut out as a divisor by a section of some bundle. To determine the bundle, we use adjunction

\omega_{X/Y}\cong\omega_{\mathbb{P}/Y}|_{X}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{X|\mathbb{P}}

and note that
\omega_{\mathbb{P}/Y}=(\det\mathcal{E})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(-3)
and
\omega_{X/Y}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(-1)|_{X}
so we find that

\mathcal{N}_{X|\mathbb{P}}=[(\det\mathcal{E})^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(2)]|_{X}

so
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)\cong(\det\mathcal{E})^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(2)
and
X
is cut out by a section

s\in\Gamma(\mathbb{P},(\det\mathcal{E})^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(2))

We will use these relations to compute Chern numbers of
X
. However, these depend not only on
Y
but also on
c(\mathcal{E})
which we will need to relate to another geometric object: the discriminant locus. The discriminant locus

\Delta_{f}=\{y\in Y\mid X_{y}\text{ is singular}\}

is naturally given a scheme structure via the vanishing of a discriminant function

\mathrm{disc}(f)\in\Gamma(Y,(\det\mathcal{E})^{\vee})

Indeed, for any
\mathcal{L}\in\Pic(Y)
and
\mathbb{P}:=\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{E})
the projectivization of a rank
r
vector bundle, there is a nonlinear function taking a section
s\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P},\pi^{*}\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(n))
to its discriminant

\mathrm{disc}(s)\in H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{L}^{r(n-1)^{(r-1)}}\otimes(\det\mathcal{E})^{n(n-1)})

Proposition 4.2
.

[
27
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
f:X\to Y
be a conic bundle. Then the discriminant locus
\Delta_{f}\subset Y
is a reduced divisor and
\Delta_{f}=-f_{*}K_{X/Y}^{2}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper 4-fold. Then the following are equivalent

(1)

\chi(X,\Omega_{X}^{p})=0
for all
0\leq p\leq 4

(2)

the following Chern numbers vanish

\displaystyle c_{1}^{4}-3c_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle c_{1}^{2}c_{2}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle c_{1}c_{3}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle c_{4}

\displaystyle=0

Proof.

This is an elementary, if tedious, application of Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch. We compute

\chi(X,\Omega_{X}^{p})=\int_{X}\ch(\Omega_{X}^{p})\mathrm{Td}_{X}

for each
p
to obtain

\displaystyle\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{720}c_{1}^{4}+\tfrac{1}{180}c_{1}^{2}c_{2}+\tfrac{1}{240}c_{2}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{720}c_{1}c_{3}-\tfrac{1}{720}c_{4}

\displaystyle\chi(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{180}c_{1}^{4}+\tfrac{1}{45}c_{1}^{2}c_{2}+\tfrac{1}{60}c_{2}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{90}c_{1}c_{3}-\tfrac{1}{180}c_{4}

\displaystyle\chi(X,\Omega_{X}^{2})

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{120}c_{1}^{4}+\tfrac{1}{30}c_{1}^{2}c_{2}+\tfrac{1}{40}c_{2}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{120}c_{1}c_{3}+\tfrac{1}{120}c_{4}

\displaystyle\chi(X,\Omega_{X}^{3})

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{180}c_{1}^{4}+\tfrac{1}{45}c_{1}^{2}c_{2}+\tfrac{1}{60}c_{2}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{90}c_{1}c_{3}-\tfrac{1}{180}c_{4}

\displaystyle\chi(X,\Omega_{X}^{4})

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{720}c_{1}^{4}+\tfrac{1}{180}c_{1}^{2}c_{2}+\tfrac{1}{240}c_{2}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{720}c_{1}c_{3}-\tfrac{1}{720}c_{4}

Row reduction shows these are zero if and only if the above Chern numbers vanish.

4.2.
del Pezzo Fibrations

Let
f:X\to Y
be a flat projective morphism with
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}
. We call it a
del Pezzo fibration
if the generic fiber is a smooth del Pezzo surface. It is
Fano-Mori
if moreover
X,Y
are normal
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein and
-K_{X}
is
f
-nef.

Suppose
f:X\to Y
is smooth in codimension
1
and that
X
and
Y
are smooth varieties. We will investigate what can happen in this case. Let
U\subset Y
be the locus over which
f
is smooth. Since
f:X\to Y
is smooth in codimension
1
, this open set
U
has complement of codimension
\geq 2
. Then
L=R^{2}f_{*}\underline{\mathbb{C}}
is a local system and
\codim_{Y}(Y\setminus U)\geq 2
meaning
\pi_{1}(U)\to\pi_{1}(Y)
is an isomorphism. Hence
L
is determined by a representation of
\pi_{1}(Y)
. Furthermore, over,
U
the local system
L
underlies a polarized VHS of type
(1,1)
and hence has finite monodromy
[
16
]
. But
\pi_{1}(U)\to\pi_{1}(Y)
is an isomorphism so there is a finite étale cover
Y^{\prime}\to Y
such that
X_{Y^{\prime}}\to Y
is a del Pezzo fibration with trivial
R^{2}f_{*}\underline{\mathbb{C}}
.

Following the argument of
[
11
, Theorem 8.4]
we now assume the a relative MMP over
Y^{\prime}
can be run and obtains a sequence of divisorial contractions
X_{Y^{\prime}}\to X^{\prime}
such that
X^{\prime}
is smooth and
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
is a Mori Fiber space with del Pezzo fibers meaning
\rho(X^{\prime})-\rho(Y^{\prime})=1
. This will hold in the particular example when
X
admits a global
1
-form with no zeros.

Since
R^{2}f^{\prime}_{*}\underline{\mathbb{C}}
is trivial this implies that the smooth fibers have
\rank{\NS(X^{\prime}_{y})}=1
and hence are isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Suppose
y\in Y
is a singular point of
f^{\prime}
we assume that there are
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{m-1}
with
m=\dim{Y}
local
1
-forms around
y\in Y
whose pullback to
X
are pointwise linearly independent along the entire fiber. Therefore, slicing by a curve whose conormal bundle at
y
is spanned by
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{m-1}
we obtain a slice
f:X^{\prime}|_{C}\to C
so that
X^{\prime}|_{C}
is smooth and the generic fiber is
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Then we can apply the results of
[
10
]
to conclude that the fibers are generically reduced (hence reduced since they are lci)
[
10
, Proposition 1.4]
and normal
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
. Therefore, the singular locus is
0
-dimensional on each fiber. Let
Z\subset X^{\prime}
be the singular locus of
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y
. Since
X^{\prime}\to Y
is a map of smooth varieties, by purity, Lemma
3.1
, each component
Z^{\prime}\subset Z
of the singular locus has
\codim_{X^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}\geq 1+\dim{X}-\dim{Y}=3
. Since
f^{\prime}
is flat and we showed that
Z
intersects each fiber in a
0
-dimensional scheme we see that
Z\to Y
is finite so its image has pure codimension
1
. Since
f^{\prime}
is smooth in codimension
1
by assumption, we conclude that
f^{\prime}
is smooth.

Finally, if we moreover assume that the divisorial contractions
X_{Y^{\prime}}\to X^{\prime}
are all blowdowns along étale multisections over
Y
then
X_{Y^{\prime}}\to Y^{\prime}
is smooth if and only if
f^{\prime}
is smooth. Since
X_{Y^{\prime}}\to Y^{\prime}
is the base change of
f
along
Y^{\prime}\to Y
this shows that
f
is smooth whenever
f^{\prime}
is.

In summary, we have proved the following.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a del Pezzo fibration between smooth varieties. Assume the following hold:

(1)

f
is smooth in codimension
1

(2)

for any finite étale cover
Y^{\prime}\to Y
the base change
X_{Y^{\prime}}
admits a sequence of divisorial contractions each of which is the blowdown of an étale multisection over
Y^{\prime}
such that we obtain a Mori Fiber space del Pezzo over
Y^{\prime}

(3)

at each
y\in Y
there are locally defined
1
-forms
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{m-1}
whose pullback to
X
are pointwise linearly independent on a neighborhood of the fiber
X_{y}

Then
f
is smooth.

. These equations imply that
D^{2}=0
. Since
\Delta_{g}
is effective and
A
is simple we conclude that
X\to A
is smooth.

(3)

if
\dim{A}=2
and
Y\to A
is a smooth isotrivial curve fibration of genus
\geq 1
then it is finite étale locally trivial so there is an isogeny
A^{\prime}\to A
so that
Y_{A^{\prime}}\cong C\times A^{\prime}
. Consider the base change
g^{\prime}:X_{A^{\prime}}\to C\times A^{\prime}
conic bundle. By applying generic smoothness to
X\to C
, we see that for a generic choice of
p\in C
the divisor
\Delta_{g^{\prime}|_{X_{A^{\prime},p}}}:=\Delta_{g^{\prime}}\cap(\{p\}\times A^{\prime})
is the discriminant locus of a conic bundle between smooth varieties. Since
g^{\prime}:X_{A^{\prime},p}\to A^{\prime}
carries the nonvanishing
1
-form
g^{\prime*}\omega
, by the analogue of Lemma
4.4
for conic bundle threefolds,
[\Delta_{g^{\prime}|_{X_{A^{\prime},p}}}]^{2}=0
. However,
A
is a simple abelian variety so no nonzero effective divisor
D\subset A
satisfies
D^{2}=0
. Therefore,
\Delta_{g^{\prime}}\cap(\{p\}\cap A^{\prime})
is empty for generic
p\in C
hence the image of
\Delta_{g^{\prime}}\to C
must be a finite collection of points. Since
\Delta_{g^{\prime}}
is a divisor, the projection
\Delta_{g^{\prime}}\to A^{\prime}
is a disjoint union of isomorphisms. For each component
D\subset\Delta_{g^{\prime}}
the fibers of
X^{\prime}\to Y
are a pair of lines meeting at
1
point since double fibers only appear only at singular points of
\Delta_{f}
by
[
27
, Theorem 4.4]
). Hence, the moduli space of lines over
D
is an étale double cover of
A^{\prime}
. Hence passing to a further isogeny, we may assume that the monodromy over each
D
is trivial. The preimage in
X^{\prime}
over each
D\subset\Delta_{g}
is the union
E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
of two divisors ruled over
D
. Since
D
is a movable divisor, any line
\ell\subset E_{1}
in the ruling satisfies
\ell\cdot E=0
but
\ell\cdot E_{2}=1
so
\ell\cdot E_{1}=-1
. The opposite is true for lines in
E_{2}
so these curves are numerically distinct and
K_{X^{\prime}}
-negative. Therefore, there is a divisorial contraction presenting
X^{\prime}
as the blowup of
X^{\prime\prime}
along a section over
D
. See the argument of
[
11
]
on p.26 for details in 3-fold setting. Repeating this, we reduce to the case that
X\to Y=C\times A^{\prime}
is a smooth conic bundle.

In total,
X\to A
remains smooth because, possibly after performing a base change
X_{A^{\prime}}\to A^{\prime}
it factors as
X_{A^{\prime}}\to X^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}
where
X^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}
is a smooth isotrivial bundle and
X_{A^{\prime}}\to X^{\prime}
is a blowdown at étale multisections
S\subset X^{\prime}
of
X^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}
. These blowups preserve smoothness because every
1
-form
\omega\in H^{0}(A,\Omega_{A})
pulls back to a nonvanishing form under the étale map
S\to A^{\prime}
.
∎

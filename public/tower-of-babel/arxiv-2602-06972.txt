The finite basis problem for matrix semirings over a two-element additively idempotent semiring Thanks: Miaomiao Ren, corresponding author, is supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China (12371024, 12571020).
Jun Jiao, Miaomiao Ren

Abstract
. We provide a complete classification of matrix semirings 𝐌 n ​ ( S ) \mathbf{M}_{n}(S) over two-element additively idempotent semirings S S with respect to the finite basis property. Our main theorem shows that for every integer n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , the semiring 𝐌 n ​ ( S ) \mathbf{M}_{n}(S) is finitely based if and only if S S is distinct from a distributive lattice.

1.
Introduction

An
additively idempotent semiring
(abbreviated as ai-semiring)
is an algebraic structure
(S,+,\cdot)
equipped with two binary operations
+
and
\cdot
such that:

•

the additive reduct
(S,+)
is a commutative idempotent semigroup;

•

the multiplicative reduct
(S,\cdot)
is a semigroup;

•

the distributive laws hold:

(x+y)z\approx xz+yz,\qquad x(y+z)\approx xy+xz.

This class of algebras includes well-known examples such as the Kleene semiring of regular languages
[
6
]
,
the max-plus algebra
[
1
]
, the power semiring of a semigroup
[
9
]
,
the endomorphism semiring of a semilattice
[
10
]
,
the semiring of all binary relations on a set
[
8
]
, and distributive lattices
[
4
]
.
These and other similar algebras have found significant applications in several branches of mathematics,
such as algebraic geometry
[
5
]
, tropical geometry
[
19
]
, information science
[
12
]
,
theoretical computer science
[
13
]
, and soft constraint solving and programming
[
3
]
.

Let
S
be an ai-semiring. Define a binary relation
\leq
on
S
by

a\leq b\Leftrightarrow a+b=b.

Then
\leq
is a partial order on
S
, and
(S,\leq)
becomes an upper semilattice in which
the supremum of any elements
a
and
b
is
a+b
.
Consequently, the additive reduct
(S,+)
is uniquely determined by this semilattice order.
Therefore, it is often convenient to represent the addition operation using the Hasse diagram of
(S,\leq)
.
Moreover, one can easily verify that this semilattice order is compatible with multiplication.
For this reason, an ai-semiring is often called a
semilattice-ordered semigroup
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer,
let
S
be an ai-semiring, and let
\mathbf{M}_{n}(S)
denote the set of all
n\times n
matrices over
S
.
Then
\mathbf{M}_{n}(S)
forms an ai-semiring under the usual matrix addition and multiplication, that is,

A+B=[a_{ij}+b_{ij}]_{n\times n},\quad A\cdot B=\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}b_{kj}\right]_{n\times n}

for all
A=[a_{ij}]_{n\times n},B=[b_{ij}]_{n\times n}\in\mathbf{M}_{n}(S)
.
It is obvious that
S
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{M}_{1}(S)
.
For
n\geq 2
,
S
can be embedded into
\mathbf{M}_{n}(S)
,
since the mapping

\varphi\colon S\rightarrow\mathbf{M}_{n}(S),\quad a\mapsto[a]_{n\times n},

where
[a]_{n\times n}
denotes the constant matrix with all entries equal to
a
,
is an embedding mapping.
Moreover, if
S
has an additive identity that is also a multiplicative zero,
then we can easily obtain the following infinite strict ascending chain

S\hookrightarrow\mathbf{M}_{2}(S)\hookrightarrow\mathbf{M}_{3}(S)\hookrightarrow\mathbf{M}_{4}(S)\hookrightarrow\cdots.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a class of algebras.
Then
\mathcal{V}
is called a
variety
if it is closed under
taking subalgebras, homomorphic images, and arbitrary direct products.
By the celebrated Birkhoff’s theorem
[
2
]
,
\mathcal{V}
is a variety if and only if
it is an
equational class
, that is, the class of all algebras satisfying some set of identities.

A variety is
finitely based
if it can be defined by a finite set of identities;
otherwise, it is
nonfinitely based
.
An algebra
A
is finitely based or nonfinitely based if the variety
\mathsf{V}(A)

it generates is finitely based or not.
The
finite basis problem
for a class of algebras,
one of the central problems in universal algebra,
concerns the classification of
its members with respect to the property of being finitely based.

A variety is
locally finite
if each of its finitely generated members is finite.
A locally finite variety is
inherently nonfinitely based

if it is not contained in any finitely based locally finite variety.
Since every finite algebra generates a locally finite variety,
we say that a finite algebra
A
is inherently nonfinitely based if
the variety
\mathsf{V}(A)
is inherently nonfinitely based.
It follows immediately that every finite algebra whose variety contains
an inherently nonfinitely based algebra is nonfinitely based.
Consequently, every inherently nonfinitely based algebra must be nonfinitely based.

Over the past two decades,
the finite basis problem for ai-semirings has attracted considerable attention,
resulting in substantial
progress
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
20
,

,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
.
In particular, Dolinka
[
7
]
constructed the first example of a nonfinitely based finite ai-semiring.
Pastijn et al.
[
11
,
20
]
proved that every ai-semiring satisfying the identity
x^{2}\approx x
is finitely based;
Ren et al.
[
22
,
23
]
later extended this to ai-semirings satisfying the identity
x^{3}\approx x
.
Shao and Ren
[
24
]
established that every algebra in the variety generated by all two-element ai-semirings is finitely based.
Subsequently,
Jackson et al.
[
17
]
and Zhao et al.
[
28
]

provided a complete classification of all three-element ai-semirings with respect to the finite basis property.
Most recently,
Dolinka, Gusev and Volkov
[
10
,
15
]
settled the finite basis problem for
the endomorphism semirings of finite semilattices.

To the best of our knowledge,
the study of the finite basis problem for matrix semirings was initiated by
Dolinka
[
8
]
, who considered the problem for
\mathbf{M}_{n}(D_{2})
for
n\geqslant 2
,
where
D_{2}
denotes the two-element distributive lattice.
Gusev and Volkov
[
16
,
25
]
are currently investigating
the same problem for upper triangular matrix semirings over
D_{2}
.
Motivated by these works,
we undertake a systematic classification of matrix semirings
\mathbf{M}_{n}(S)
,
where
S
ranges over all two-element ai-semirings.
The main result of this paper provides the following complete characterization.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
S
be a two-element ai-semiring, and let
n\geq 2
be an integer.
Then the matrix semiring
\mathbf{M}_{n}(S)
is finitely based if and only if
S
is not a distributive lattice.

For the proof,
we first note that the case where
S
is a distributive lattice follows from Dolinka’s work.
Specifically,

[
8
, Theorem B]
states that the semiring
\mathcal{R}_{2}
of all binary relations on a two-element set is inherently nonfinitely based.
Since
\mathcal{R}_{2}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{M}_{2}(D_{2})
,
it follows immediately that
\mathbf{M}_{2}(D_{2})
inherits this property.
Furthermore, because
\mathbf{M}_{n}(D_{2})
contains a copy of
\mathbf{M}_{2}(D_{2})
for all
n\geq 2
,
the same property extends to
\mathbf{M}_{n}(D_{2})
; this result is explicitly stated in
[
8
, Corollary 6.2]
.
(We note that
D_{2}
is also denoted
\mathbb{B}_{2}
and called the two-element Boolean semiring; our notation follows
[
24
]
.)

Therefore, to establish Theorem
1.1
, it remains to prove the converse:
that the matrix semiring
\mathbf{M}_{n}(S)
is finitely based
for every two-element ai-semiring
S
distinct from a distributive lattice and every
n\geq 2
.
The necessary preliminaries are collected in Section 2, and the proof is carried out in Sections 3 and 4.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
X
be a countably infinite set of variables, let
X^{+}
denote the free semigroup over
X
,
and let
X^{*}
denote the free monoid over
X
.
Due to distributivity, every ai-semiring term over
X
can be expressed as a finite sum of words from
X^{+}
.
An ai-semiring identity over
X
is an
expression of the form

\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v},

where
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
are ai-semiring terms over
X
.
By
[
18
, Theorem 2.5]
,
the ai-semiring
(P_{f}(X^{+}),\cup,\cdot)
of all non-empty finite subsets of
X^{+}
is free
in the variety
\mathbf{AI}
of all ai-semirings on
X
.
So we sometimes write

\{\mathbf{u}_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq k\}\approx\{\mathbf{v}_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq\ell\}

for the ai-semiring identity

\mathbf{u}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{u}_{k}\approx\mathbf{v}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{v}_{\ell}.

For an ai-semiring
S
and an ai-semiring identity
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}
,
we say that
S

satisfies

\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}
(or
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}

holds
in
S
)
if
\varphi(\mathbf{u})=\varphi(\mathbf{v})

for every semiring homomorphism
\varphi\colon P_{f}(X^{+})\to S
.
Note that such homomorphism
\varphi
is uniquely determined
by its values on
X
, since
P_{f}(X^{+})
is generated by
X
.

Suppose that
\Sigma
is a set of ai-semiring identities containing the defining identities of the variety
\mathbf{AI}
.
Let
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}
be an ai-semiring identity with

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{u}_{k}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{v}_{\ell},

where
\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\in X^{+}
for
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq j\leq\ell
.
One can easily check that the ai-semiring variety defined by
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}

coincides with the variety defined by the simpler identities
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}_{j}
and
\mathbf{v}\approx\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{u}_{i}

for all
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq j\leq\ell
.
Therefore, to prove that
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{v}
is derivable from
\Sigma
,
it is enough to derive each identity
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}_{j}
and
\mathbf{v}\approx\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{u}_{i}
from
\Sigma
.
This reduction will be used in the subsequent sections.

Next, we introduce some notation.
Let
\mathbf{w}
be a word in
X^{+}
and
x
a letter in
X
. Then

•

h(\mathbf{w})
denotes the first variable that occurs in
\mathbf{w}
;

•

t(\mathbf{w})
denotes the last variable that occurs in
\mathbf{w}
;

•

c(\mathbf{w})
denotes the set of variables that occur in
\mathbf{w}
;

•

\ell(\mathbf{w})
denotes
the number of variables occurring in
\mathbf{w}
counting multiplicities;

•

L_{\geq 2}(\mathbf{u})
denotes the set of
\mathbf{u}_{i}\in\mathbf{u}
such that
\ell(\mathbf{u}_{i})\geq 2
;

•

p(\mathbf{w})
denotes the word obtained from
\mathbf{w}
by deleting its tail,
that is,
\mathbf{w}=p(\mathbf{w})t(\mathbf{w})
;

•

s(\mathbf{w})
denotes the word obtained from
\mathbf{w}
by deleting its head,
that is,
\mathbf{w}=h(\mathbf{w})s(\mathbf{w})
.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be an ai-semiring term such that
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}_{1}+\mathbf{u}_{2}+\cdots+\mathbf{u}_{n}
, where

\mathbf{u}_{i}\in X^{+}
,
1\leq i\leq n
. Then

•

h(\mathbf{u})
denotes the set
\{h(\mathbf{u}_{i})\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
;

•

t(\mathbf{u})
denotes the set
\{t(\mathbf{u}_{i})\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
;

•

c(\mathbf{u})
denotes the set of variables that occur in
\mathbf{u}
and so

c(\mathbf{u})=\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq n}c(\mathbf{u}_{i}).

3.
Equational basis of matrix semiring over a
2
-element ai-semiring

In this section, we show that for each
S\in\{L_{2},R_{2},N_{2},T_{2}\}
,
the varieties
\mathsf{V}(\mathbf{M}_{n}(S))
for all
n\geq 2
coincide and are finitely based.
These results are easy in comparison to those in Section 4.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer. Then

\mathsf{V}(\mathbf{M}_{n}(L_{2}))
is the ai-semiring variety determined by the identities (
1
) and

\displaystyle xy\approx xz.

(1)

Proof.

We first show that
\mathbf{M}_{n}(L_{2})
satisfies identities (
1
) and (
1
).
Let
A=[a_{ij}]
,
B=[b_{ij}]
,
C=[c_{ij}]
be arbitrary matrices in
\mathbf{M}_{n}(L_{2})
, where all entries belong to
L_{2}
.
For any indices
1\leq i,j\leq n
, we have

(AB)_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}b_{kj}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(\ref{eq:L2})}}}{{=}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(\ref{eq:L2})}}}{{=}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}c_{kj}=(AC)_{ij},

and

\displaystyle(A^{2})_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}a_{kj}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}a_{kj}\right)+a_{ij}a_{jj}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(\ref{eq:L2})}}}{{=}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}a_{kj}\right)+a_{ij}

\displaystyle=(A^{2})_{ij}+A_{ij}=(A^{2}+A)_{ij}.

This shows that
AB=AC
and
A^{2}=A^{2}+A
.
Hence
\mathbf{M}_{n}(L_{2})
satisfies the identities (
1
) and (
1
).

Next, we show that every ai-semiring identity of
\mathbf{M}_{n}(L_{2})

is derivable from (
1
), (
1
) and the defining identities of
\mathbf{AI}
.
Let
\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q}
be such a nontrivial identity,
where
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}_{1}+\mathbf{u}_{2}+\dots+\mathbf{u}_{m}
with
\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{q}\in X^{+}
for
1\leq i\leq m
.
Since
L_{2}
embeds into
\mathbf{M}_{n}(L_{2})
,
we have that
h(\mathbf{q})=h(\mathbf{u}_{i})
for some
\mathbf{u}_{i}\in\mathbf{u}
.
We consider two cases.

Case 1:

\ell(\mathbf{q})=1
.
Then
\mathbf{u}_{i}=h(\mathbf{q})s(\mathbf{u}_{i})=\mathbf{q}s(\mathbf{u}_{i})
.
Since the identity is nontrivial,
it follows that
s(\mathbf{u}_{i})
is nonempty. Consequently,

\mathbf{u}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}_{i}\approx\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q}s(\mathbf{u}_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{f1}}}{{\approx}}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:T22}}}{{\approx}}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{q}^{2}+\mathbf{q}.

Case 2:

\ell(\mathbf{q})\geq 2
.
Suppose, for contradiction, that
h(\mathbf{q})\cap h(L_{\geq 2}(\mathbf{u}))=\emptyset
.
If
h(\mathbf{u}_{i})=h(\mathbf{q})
for some
\mathbf{u}_{i}\in\mathbf{u}
,
then
\mathbf{u}_{i}
must be a single letter.
Define a substitution
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbf{M}_{n}(L_{2})
by

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}I_{n},&\text{if }x=h(\mathbf{q}),\\
O_{n},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
I_{n}
is the
n\times n
diagonal matrix with
1
on the main diagonal and
0
elsewhere,
and
O_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
constant matrix with all entries equal to
0
.
Then
\varphi(\mathbf{u})=I_{n}
while
\varphi(\mathbf{q})=J_{n}
,
where
J_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
constant matrix with all entries equal to
1
.
Note that

Proposition 4.4

,

,

, and

be arbitrary matrices in

\displaystyle(AB+A)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}b_{kj}\right)+a_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(\ref{eq:M2})}}}{{=}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}\right)+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{kj}\right)+a_{ij}

\displaystyle\quad(AB+CD)_{ij}

\displaystyle=(AB)_{ij}+(CD)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}b_{kj}\right)+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{ik}d_{kj}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(\ref{eq:M2})}}}{{=}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}\right)+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{kj}\right)+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{ik}\right)+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{kj}\right)

\mathbf{M}_{2}(M_{2})
can be embedded into
\mathbf{M}_{n}(M_{2})
.
We fix some notation: for any positive integers
k
and
\ell
,

•

O_{k\times\ell}
denotes the
k\times\ell
constant matrix with all entries equal to
0
.

•

J_{k\times\ell}
denotes the
k\times\ell
constant matrix with all entries equal to
1
.

•

H_{2\times(n-2)}
denotes the
2\times(n-2)
constant matrix

\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0\\
1&1&\cdots&1\end{bmatrix}
.

•

K_{2\times(n-2)}
denotes the
2\times(n-2)
constant matrix

\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1\\
0&0&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}
.

•

M_{k\times\ell}^{T}
denotes the transpose of a matrix
M_{k\times\ell}
.

Consider the mapping
\varphi\colon\mathbf{M}_{2}(M_{2})\to\mathbf{M}_{n}(M_{2})
defined by

\displaystyle\varphi(O)

\displaystyle=O_{n\times n},\qquad\varphi(F)=J_{n\times n},

\displaystyle\varphi(X)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}X&J_{2\times(n-2)}\\
J_{(n-2)\times 2}&J_{(n-2)\times(n-2)}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{for }X\in\{E,G,W,X,Y,Z\},

\displaystyle\varphi(A)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}A&H_{2\times(n-2)}\\
K_{2\times(n-2)}^{T}&J_{(n-2)\times(n-2)}\end{bmatrix},\varphi(B)=\begin{bmatrix}B&K_{2\times(n-2)}\\
H_{2\times(n-2)}^{T}&J_{(n-2)\times(n-2)}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\varphi(C)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}C&K_{2\times(n-2)}\\
K_{2\times(n-2)}^{T}&J_{(n-2)\times(n-2)}\end{bmatrix},\varphi(D)=\begin{bmatrix}D&H_{2\times(n-2)}\\
H_{2\times(n-2)}^{T}&J_{(n-2)\times(n-2)}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\varphi(P)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}P&J_{2\times(n-2)}\\
K_{2\times(n-2)}^{T}&J_{(n-2)\times(n-2)}\end{bmatrix},\varphi(Q)=\begin{bmatrix}Q&J_{2\times(n-2)}\\
H_{2\times(n-2)}^{T}&J_{(n-2)\times(n-2)}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\varphi(R)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}R&H_{2\times(n-2)}\\
J_{2\times(n-2)}^{T}&J_{(n-2)\times(n-2)}\end{bmatrix},\varphi(S)=\begin{bmatrix}S&K_{2\times(n-2)}\\
J_{2\times(n-2)}^{T}&J_{(n-2)\times(n-2)}\end{bmatrix}.

It is straightforward to verify that
\varphi
is a monomorphism.
∎

.

3
)–(
5
).

Proof.

This follows directly from Corollary
4.3

4.4
.

5.
Conclusion

We have provided a complete classification of
matrix semirings
\mathbf{M}_{n}(S)
over two-element ai-semirings
S

with respect to the finite basis property.
From Remark
3.2
and
[
26
, Figure 1]
,
the variety
\mathsf{V}(\mathbf{M}_{n}(L_{2}))
has precisely
5
subvarieties;
they form a distributive lattice and are all finitely based.
The same conclusion holds for the variety
\mathsf{V}(\mathbf{M}_{n}(R_{2}))
.
By
[
21
, Theorem 1]
together with Propositions
3.4
and
3.5
,
the varieties
\mathsf{V}(\mathbf{M}_{n}(N_{2}))
and
\mathsf{V}(\mathbf{M}_{n}(T_{2}))

are both minimal nontrivial varieties.
In contrast, the subvariety lattice of the variety
\mathsf{V}(\mathbf{M}_{n}(M_{2}))

remains to be explicitly described.

Dolinka’s result demonstrates that
the finite basis properties of
S
and
\mathbf{M}_{n}(S)
do not always coincide.
This leads to a natural question:
does there exist a nonfinitely based ai-semiring
S
for which
\mathbf{M}_{n}(S)
is finitely based?

Single conflict coloring, adaptable choosability and separation choosability This paper is partially based on the thesis written by the second author under supervision of the first author
Carl Johan Casselgren, Kalle Eriksson

Abstract
We study relations between three interrelated notions of graph (list) coloring: single conflict coloring, adapted list coloring and choosability with separation (with 1 1 overlapping color between lists of adjacent vertices), and their respective invariants single conflict chromatic number χ ↮ \chi_{\nleftrightarrow} , adaptable choosability ch a ​ d \mathrm{ch}_{ad} and separation choosability ch s ​ e ​ p \mathrm{ch}_{sep} . We investigate graphs with small values of these invariants, and construct explicit families of graphs G G with χ ↮ ​ ( G ) = ch a ​ d ​ ( G ) > ch s ​ e ​ p ​ ( G ) \chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G)=\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G)>\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G) , as well as where all three invariants are equal. Furthermore, we consider planar graphs and investigate for which triples ( a , b , c ) (a,b,c) , there is a planar graph G G with ( ch s ​ e ​ p ​ ( G ) , ch a ​ d ​ ( G ) , χ ↮ ​ ( G ) ) = ( a , b , c ) (\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G),\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G),\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G))=(a,b,c) . Throughout the paper we pose many questions on these graph coloring parameters, and discuss connections to related coloring invariants such as adapted coloring.

Introduction

Given a graph
G
, assign to each vertex

of colors.
Such an assignment

for

the sets

If there is a proper coloring

of

\varphi(v)\in L(v)

, then

and
\varphi

is called an

or simply a
list coloring
.
Furthermore,

is
called

if it is
L
-colorable
for every list assignment

where all lists have size at least
k

The minimum
k
such that
G
is
k
-choosable is the

list-chromatic number
(or
choice number
)
\mathrm{ch}(G)
of
G
.

Being first introduced in the 70’s
[
13
,
27
]
, list coloring is a well-established area of graph coloring. Indeed, many graph coloring models translate naturally to the list coloring setting, and numerous variants of list coloring are studied in the literature. In this paper we study relations between three different variants of list coloring.

Given an edge coloring
f
of a graph
G
, a coloring
c:V(G)\to\mathbf{Z}
is
adapted
to
f
if no edge
xy
satisfies
that
c(x)=c(y)=f(xy)
. Adapted coloring was introduced by
Hell and Zhu
[
19
]
, who
among other things proved that a graph is adaptably
2
-colorable
if and only if it contains an edge whose removal yields a bipartite graph. They also proved general bounds on the

adaptable chromatic number

\chi_{a}(G)
,
that is, the least number of colors needed for an adapted coloring of a graph
G
.

Building on the notion of adapted coloring, Kostochka and Zhu

[
20
]
studied the list coloring version of adapted coloring, where the vertices are assigned lists, and we seek
a list coloring that is adapted to a given edge coloring. The minimum size of the lists which ensures the existence of such an
adapted list coloring is the
adaptable choice number

\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G)
(or
adaptable choosability
) of a graph
G
. Kostochka and Zhu proved a general upper bound on this invariant and also characterized graphs with adaptable choosability
2
.

Another variant of list coloring is so-called
choosability with separation, introduced by Kratochvil, Tuza and Voigt

[
21
]
. In this model, we require that color lists of adjacent vertices have bounded intersection,
and ask for the minimum size of the lists which guarantees existence of a proper list coloring of the graph in question.
We shall focus on the variant where lists of adjacent vertices have
at most one common color, often referred to as the
separation choosability
.
Denote by
\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G)
the smallest size of the lists which ensures that
G
is colorable
from such lists, provided that lists of adjacent vertices intersect in at most one color. This
particular variant was specifically studied in e.g.
[
15
,
17
]
.

Generalizing both the notion of adaptable choosability and separation choosability,
single conflict coloring was introduced by Dvorak et al.
[
11
]
.
A
local
k
-partition
of a graph
G
is a collection
\{c_{v}\}_{v\in V(G)}
of maps

c_{v}:E(v)\to\{1,\dots,k\}
, where
E(v)
denotes the set of edges incident
with
v
. We think of
c
as a conflict map, and for each
edge
e=xy\in E(G)
,
c
yields a conflict-pair

(c_{x}(e),c_{y}(e))
. Given such a local
k
-partition
\{c_{v}\}
,

G
is
conflict
\{c_{v}\}
-colorable
if there is a map

\varphi:V(G)\to\{1,\dots,k\}
such that no edge
e=xy

satisfies that
\varphi(x)=c_{x}(e)
and
\varphi(y)=c_{y}(e)
.
The
single conflict chromatic number

\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G)
of

G
is the smallest
k
such that

G
is conflict
\{c_{v}\}
-colorable for every local
k
-partition
\{c_{v}\}
.

As pointed out in
[
11
]
, separation choosability,
adaptable choosability, and single conflict coloring are strongly related.
Indeed, we have

\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G)\geq\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G)\geq\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G),

(1)

where the latter inequality has been observed in several places in the
literature
[
15
,
8
,
11
]
.
Furthermore, the adaptable choosability is at most the ordinary
choosability, so
we have that

\mathrm{ch}(G)\geq\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G)\geq\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G).

Let us also note the following inequality for the single conflict chromatic number.

Proposition 1.1
.

For every positive integer

, there are graphs
G_{k}
and
H_{k}

such that
\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G_{k})-\mathrm{ch}(G_{k})\geq k
, and
\mathrm{ch}(H_{k})-\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(H_{k})\geq k

This proposition follows by taking

to be
be the complete bipartite graph on

and
H_{k}
the complete graph of order

from
[

17
]
.
In particular, this implies that the difference between

\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}
and
\mathrm{ch}_{ad}
can be arbitrarily large.

Dvorak and Postle
[
12
]
introduced correspondence coloring
(also known as DP-coloring) as a generalization of list coloring.
Roughly, the idea is to replace every vertex in a graph

by a
k
-clique, and adding a matching between any pair
of cliques corresponding to adjacent
vertices. (The vertices in the
k
-clique correspond to the colors in a
list of size
k

If there is an independent set
in the resulting graph of size
|V(G)|
for any choice of the matchings
between cliques corresponding to adjacent vertices in

, then the

k
-DP-colorable
.
The
DP-chromatic number
\chi_{DP}(G)
is the minimum
k
such
that

is
k
-DP-colorable

1

[
12
]
for a
formal definition.
. Clearly,
\mathrm{ch}(G)\leq\chi_{DP}(G)

for any graph

, since ordinary list coloring is the case when the
matching between different cliques joins identical colors of the
corresponding color lists.
This generalization of ordinary list coloring has been widely studied
e.g. for planar graphs.
In contrast to Proposition
1.1
, the single conflict
chromatic number is bounded by the DP-chromatic number.
Indeed, it is clear that
\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G)\leq\chi_{DP}(G)

for every graph

, although this is not explicitly mentioned in

[
11
]
.

A main aim of this note is to give a unified treatment and discuss similarities and differences between single conflict coloring, adaptable choosability, and separation choosability.
Throughout the paper
we point out that several results which were proved in the setting of adaptable
choosability or choosability with separation also hold in the setting of single conflict
coloring. Along the way we also make some remarks about adapted coloring.

We characterize graphs with single conflict chromatic number
2
, and give a structural description of graphs
with separation choosability
2
.
As it turns out, the graphs with single chromatic number

2
are precisely the graphs with adaptable choosability
2
, while the family with
separation choosability
2
is much larger.
Using these results, we
construct a large family of graphs
\mathcal{G}
such that

\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G)=\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G)>\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G)
for every
G\in\mathcal{G}
.
We also give an infinite family of graphs where all three invariants are equal.

Next, we consider planar graphs. Our investigation of the invariants
\mathrm{ch}_{sep},\mathrm{ch}_{ad}

and
\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}

on planar graphs is somewhat similar to the one in

[
1
]
where the parameters
\chi,\mathrm{ch}
and

are compared.
There is quite a number of papers on adaptable choosability as well as
separation choosability of planar graph
[
19
,
8
,
9
,
16
,
10
,
9
]
, but few where both graph coloring invariants are treated.
Dvorak et al.
[

]
noted that planar graphs have single conflict chromatic
number at most
4
, and triangle-free at most

.
Here we point out that large families of planar
graphs with triangles
have single conflict chromatic number
3
.

It was proved by Choi et al.
[
10
]
that planar graphs
without
4
-cycles are separation
3
-choosable,
while there are such graphs with adaptable choosability
4

[

]
,
so these invariants
may have different values on the family of planar graphs.

We give an example of a multigraph

with edge multiplicity

, satisfying that
\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G)=4
,

\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G)=\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G)=3
; it remains an open question whether
there exists such a planar graph without multiple edges.
Additionally, we give an example of a planar graph

where

4=\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G)=\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G)>\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G)=\chi_{ad}(G)=3
.

We conclude our investigation of planar graphs by characterizing for which sequences
(a,b,c)

(\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G),\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G),\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G))=(a,b,c)

that it remains an open problem whether all planar graphs
are separation

[

]
).

Characterization of
2
-list assignments

In this section we prove some results on graphs with small values of the aforementioned
chromatic invariants.
Let us start with the characterization of
2
-choosable graphs, which was proved by
Erdös et al.
[
13
]
. The
core
of a graph
G
is obtained by successively removing
vertices of degree
1
from
G
. We define the graph
\Theta_{i,j,k}
by taking two
vertices and joining them by
3
internally disjoint paths of lengths
i,j,k
, respectively. Such graphs are
usually referred to as
theta graphs
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
13
]

A connected graph is
2
-choosable if and only if its core is isomorphic to a
cycle of even length or
to
\Theta_{2,2,2k}
, for some
k\geq 1
.

Since
\mathrm{ch}(G)\geq\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G)
, the class of adaptably
2
-choosable graphs is larger
than the family of
2
-choosable graphs.
Indeed, the following was proved in
[
20
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
20
]

A connected graph
G
with minimum degree at least
2
is adaptably

2
-choosable if and only if
G
consists of two or three internally disjoint paths connecting two distinct vertices of
G
.

In the following, we shall characterize graphs with single conflict chromatic
number
2
, as well as give a partial characterization of graphs with separation choosability
2
.
Let us also note that the graphs with DP-chromatic number
2
are precisely the
trees
[
1
]
.

We start with the case of single conflict coloring.
By the inequality (
1
), it suffices to check the graphs
in Theorem
2.2
. Straightforward case analysis
yields that the graphs that are single conflict
2
-colorable are the same as the
adaptably
2
-choosable ones.
A full proof appears in
[
14
]
, and we omit it here.

Proposition 2.3
.

A connected graph
G
with minimum degree at least
2
is single conflict

2
-colorable if and only if
G
consists of two or three internally disjoint paths connecting two distinct vertices of
G
.

Next, we give an informal description of graphs
with separation choosability
2
in the theorem below.
First we need some definitions.
Let
P=v_{1}e_{1}v_{2}\dots e_{d-1}v_{d}
be a path.
The sequence
C=v_{1}e_{1}v_{2}\dots v_{d}e_{d}v_{1}
is an

(ordered) cycle
if
e_{d}=v_{d}v_{1}
.
Similarly, the sequence
D=v_{1}e_{1}v_{2}\dots v_{d}e_{d}v_{j}

is called an
(ordered) lollipop
if
e_{d}=v_{d}v_{j}

and
j\in\{2,\dots,d-2\}
.

In
[
6
]
, the first author gave a characterization of which
2
-list assignments
of a graph admits a list coloring of the graph.
Indeed, using Lemma 9 in
[
6
]
it is straightforward to prove the following partial characterization of
separation
2
-choosable graphs. Again, the full details appear in
[
14
]
,
and we omit them here.

Theorem 2.4
.

If a graph
G
is not separation
2
-choosable, then it contains
two subgraphs
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
such that for
i=1,2
:

(i)

H_{i}
is a lollipop or a cycle;

(ii)

the cycle contained in
H_{i}
has length at least
4
;

(iii)

the first vertices of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, respectively, are identical;

(iv)

the second vertices of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are distinct;

(v)

if
H_{1}
or
H_{2}
is a cycle, then the second to last vertices of

H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, respectively, are distinct;

(vi)

if both
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are cycles, then the second
and second to last vertices of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
,
respectively, are four distinct vertices.

We remark that it is possible to give a more elaborate description
of separation
2
-choosability.
Indeed, the condition that lists of adjacent vertices contain
at most one common color,
implies that
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
can only intersect in a rather restricted way. However, such a
characterization would require stating a long list of rather technical conditions on
how the cycles in
H_{1}
and

H_{2}
may intersect, particularly in the case when these cycles have length
4

We believe that
such a long list is not very informative, on the contrary quite technical and tedious to
read, so we shall be content with the somewhat less precise characterization in

.

Nevertheless, we note that a theta graph is thus separation

2
-choosable,
as is also
K_{4}
,
and some, but not all, subdivisions of
K_{4}
.

K_{5}
is, however, not separation

-choosable, as it contains
an ordered lollipop and
an ordered cycle with exactly one common edge.
More generally, the following proposition is a partial converse of Theorem

2.4

Proposition 2.5
.

If a connected graph has (at least) two
cycles of length at least
4
with at most one common vertex,
then it is
not separation
2
-choosable.

By contrast, the following follows from Theorem
2.4
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Every graph with at most one cycle of length greater than
3

2
-choosable.

Since every graph with at least two disjoint triangles is not adaptably
2
-choosable,
Corollary

such that if

\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G)\neq\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G)

In general, we would like to suggest the following problem, which to the best of our knowledge
has not been considered in the literature.

Problem 2.7
.

For every positive integer
k\geq 0
, is there a graph
G_{k}
such that

\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G_{k})-\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G_{k})\geq k
?

As noted after Proposition
1.1
in the introduction, the analogous question for single
conflict chromatic number compared to
adaptable choosability (and thus also separation choosability)

[
11

.
Indeed, although the class of adaptably
2
-choosable graphs and the one with

Furedi et al.

proved that for

\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(K_{k\times n})=\mathrm{ch}(K_{k\times n})=(1+o(1))\log_{k/(k-1)}n,

\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G)\leq\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G)\leq\mathrm{ch}(G)

On the other hand, Dvorak et al.
[

\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G)=\Omega(\sqrt{d/\log d})

So in general
\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}

\mathrm{ch}_{ad}

On the other hand, there are infinite families of graph with maximum degree at most
4

Proposition 2.8
.

If
G

\mathrm{ch}_{sep}(G)=\mathrm{ch}_{ad}(G)=\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G)=\mathrm{ch}(G)=3.

This proposition is a consequence of Proposition

the following upper bound, first stated explicitly for adaptable choosability in

]

Proposition 2.9

For any graph

\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(G)\leq\lceil\Delta(G)/2\rceil+1

In turn, Proposition

Theorem 2.10
.

If there is an orientation of a graph

-choosable.

Clearly, the single conflict coloring version of Theorem

By contrast to Proposition

Problem 2.11

Is there a graph

Mechanistic multiscale modeling identifies putative natural tri-target candidates of MAO-B, LRRK2 and A₂A for Parkinson’s disease
Authors: Oussama Khibech, Salma Kadda, Said Abadi, Abdessamad Benabbou, Mohamed Bouhrim, Shehdeh Jodeh, Diana Jodeh, Belkheir Hammouti, Allal Challioui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractParkinson’s disease (PD) is a rapidly growing neurodegenerative disorder for which current dopaminergic and device-based therapies remain purely symptomatic and fail to modify disease progression. Addressing the multifactorial biology of PD under stringent blood-brain barrier constraints requires CNS-penetrant small molecules that can simultaneously engage several validated targets. Here, we combined ADMET-AI profiling, structure-based docking, 100 ns molecular dynamics, MM/GBSA ensemble free energies, and principal component analysis to evaluate falcarinol, 20-hydroxyecdysone, and arnicolide D as putative tri-target candidates of MAO-B, LRRK2 and the A₂A receptor. Falcarinol and arnicolide D occupy a CNS drug-like ADMET space with high predicted BBB penetration and acceptable safety, and show stable, hydrophobically driven binding across all three proteins. In contrast, 20-hydroxyecdysone achieves the most favorable MM/GBSA binding free energies in MAO-B and A₂A (ΔGbind down to -53.4 and − 56.6 kcal·mol⁻¹, respectively) through persistent multi-point hydrogen bonding. Still, it lies outside the optimal CNS physicochemical window. Integrating these orthogonal readouts, we propose an ADMET-constrained multi-target pharmacophore framework for simultaneous MAO-B/LRRK2/A₂A modulation and nominate falcarinol- and arnicolide D-based chemotypes, alongside polarity-masked 20-hydroxyecdysone analogues, as prioritized starting points for experimental validation and next-generation multi-target-directed ligands in Parkinson’s disease.Keywords: Parkinson’s disease, ADMET-AI, MD, MM/GBSA, PCA, Good Health and Well-beingSubject terms: Chemistry, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Drug discovery, Neuroscience

Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is the second most common neurodegenerative disorder and a rapidly growing cause of disability and death worldwide. Recent estimates indicate that more than 8.5 million people were living with PD in 2019 and that its prevalence has more than doubled over the last 25 years, with a disproportionate burden in ageing populations and low- and middle-income countries1,2. More recent Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 analyses estimate that 11.77 million people were living with PD in 2021 and that PD accounted for 7.47 million DALYs3,4. GBD 2021-based modelling further projects that the global number of PD cases will reach 25.2 million by 2050, largely driven by population ageing5. Pathologically, PD is characterized by progressive loss of nigrostriatal dopaminergic neurons and widespread accumulation of misfolded α-synuclein, embedded in a complex network of mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, neuroinflammation and genetic susceptibility6–8. Current pharmacological and device-based therapies dominated by levodopa, dopamine agonists, monoamine oxidase-B (MAO-B) and catechol-O-methyltransferase inhibitors, adenosine A₂A receptor antagonists and deep-brain stimulation provide meaningful symptomatic relief but do not halt or reverse the underlying neurodegenerative process6,7. Disease-modifying strategies remain an urgent, unmet need despite intense efforts in both monogenic and sporadic forms of PD8.

The multifactorial nature of PD has catalyzed a shift from the classical “one target-one drug” paradigm towards multi-target-directed ligands (MTDLs) and other polypharmacological strategies that aim to modulate several pathogenic nodes with a single, well-characterized chemical entity9–11. Conceptually, this approach is attractive in PD, where dopaminergic deficiency, kinase dysregulation, aberrant neurotransmission, neuroinflammation and oxidative stress are tightly intertwined. Computational chemistry and virtual screening greatly facilitate the rational design of such ligands by enabling simultaneous evaluation of binding to multiple targets, network-level selectivity and physicochemical constraints before synthesis9,10,12. Dual A₂A/MAO-B ligands already exemplify how polypharmacology can be engineered to combine symptomatic benefit with potential disease-modifying mechanisms12.

In this context, we focused on three complementary and clinically relevant targets in PD: MAO-B, leucine-rich repeat kinase 2 (LRRK2) and the adenosine A₂A receptor (A₂AR). MAO-B, highly expressed in astrocytes and other glial cells, catalyzes the oxidative deamination of dopamine and other monoamines, generating hydrogen peroxide and toxic aldehydes that contribute to mitochondrial dysfunction and dopaminergic cell death. Irreversible MAO-B inhibitors such as selegiline and rasagiline are established adjuncts in PD therapy and may exert modest disease-modifying effects by reducing dopamine-derived oxidative stress13. LRRK2, a large multidomain kinase/GTPase, is the most frequently mutated gene in familial PD and is also implicated in sporadic disease; hyperactive LRRK2 signaling perturbs vesicular trafficking, autophagy, cytoskeletal dynamics and neuroinflammatory pathways14,15. Potent and selective LRRK2 kinase inhibitors, antisense oligonucleotides and other modalities have entered early clinical testing, underscoring its status as a priority disease-modifying target14,15. Adenosine A₂AR, densely expressed in striatopallidal medium spiny neurons where it forms functional heteromers with dopamine D₂ receptors, provides a non-dopaminergic lever on basal ganglia circuitry; A₂AR antagonism improves “off” time and may alleviate some non-motor manifestations16. Istradefylline, the first A₂AR antagonist approved for PD, has validated this mechanism in the clinic and highlights broader opportunities for A₂AR-based multitarget strategies17. Simultaneous modulation of MAO-B, LRRK2 and A₂AR is therefore conceptually attractive: MAO-B inhibition can reduce dopamine catabolism and oxidative stress; LRRK2 modulation can target upstream pathogenic signaling with potential impact on disease progression; and A₂AR antagonism can recalibrate striatal network activity and motor output. A single chemotype that engages these three targets with an acceptable balance of potency, selectivity and pharmacokinetics could, in principle, bridge symptomatic and disease-modifying benefits while simplifying combination regimens inherent to polypharmacy9,10,16. However, rational design of such multitarget ligands for central nervous system (CNS) disorders faces a fundamental constraint imposed by the blood-brain barrier (BBB). Successful CNS drugs occupy a narrow physicochemical space characterized by moderate molecular weight, restricted hydrogen-bonding capacity and limited topological polar surface area, together with finely tuned lipophilicity and ionization18,19. As highlighted in classic CNS design analyses and refined multiparameter optimization schemes (CNS MPO), small deviations outside this “sweet spot” markedly reduce the probability of achieving adequate brain exposure19. At the same time, high-affinity binding to enzymes, kinases and GPCRs typically demands the introduction of additional heteroatoms and ionizable groups that form directed hydrogen bonds, ionic interactions and structured water networks within highly polar active sites. Each such modification tends to increase polar surface area and decrease passive BBB permeability or to promote recognition by active efflux transporters. As a result, more than 98% of small-molecule drugs and essentially all macromolecular therapeutics fail to achieve therapeutically relevant brain penetration across an intact BBB, making optimization of CNS drug-likeness a global bottleneck in PD drug discovery20. Natural products provide an appealing starting point to address these conflicting design pressures. Their evolutionarily shaped, three-dimensional scaffolds frequently combine rigid hydrocarbon frameworks with strategically placed heteroatoms, offering privileged topologies for multi-target engagement while sometimes remaining compatible with CNS-permeable physicochemical envelopes21. Numerous plant- and marine-derived metabolites exert antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and mitochondrial-stabilizing effects that counteract key pathogenic mechanisms in PD, often through concurrent modulation of several signaling pathways21,22. In PD specifically, natural products have been shown to interfere with α-synuclein aggregation, support dopaminergic neuron survival and attenuate neuroinflammation, making them attractive scaffolds for the design of multi-target-directed ligands22. Within this framework, we selected three structurally and physicochemically distinct natural compounds falcarinol, 20-hydroxyecdysone and arnicolide D as prototype scaffolds for a putative tri-target (MAO-B/LRRK2/A₂AR) in silico discovery campaign. Falcarinol is a C₁₇ polyacetylene alcohol abundant in edible Apiaceae plants such as carrots. Its linear, highly conjugated scaffold combines a lipophilic carbon backbone with a single terminal hydroxy group and minimal polar surface area, a profile that is compatible with BBB permeation according to classical CNS drug analyses23,24. Beyond its favorable physicochemistry, falcarinol is a potent activator of the Nrf2/HO-1 axis and exhibits strong antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects in vivo, outperforming sulforaphane in attenuation of intestinal inflammation at diet-achievable doses23. These properties are directly relevant to PD, where Nrf2-mediated cytoprotecting and redox homeostasis are central to dopaminergic neuron survival. In a protein-targeted context, the polyunsaturated chain provides an extended hydrophobic surface for insertion into lipophilic clefts of MAO-B and A₂AR, while the terminal alcohol can form key hydrogen bonds, rendering falcarinol a chemically economical scaffold for exploring multi-target engagement. 20-Hydroxyecdysone (20E) is an ecdysteroid hormone with a polyhydroxylated steroid nucleus that has attracted attention for its robust cytoprotective and neuromodulator activities. In neuronal and glial models, 20E scavenges reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, preserves mitochondrial membrane potential and suppresses stress-activated JNK and NF-κB signaling25,26. Importantly, 20E protects dopaminergic neurons and ameliorates motor deficits in an MPTP mouse model of PD, accompanied by restoration of striatal dopamine content and enhancement of endogenous antioxidant defenses25. From a medicinal chemistry perspective, 20E exemplifies an “over-functionalized” but highly bioactive natural product: with a molecular weight of ~ 480.6 g·mol⁻¹, a topological polar surface area around 138 Å² and six hydrogen-bond donors, it lies well outside the physicochemical space normally associated with efficient BBB penetration. This tension between compelling target-relevant biology and suboptimal CNS properties makes 20E an instructive scaffold for dissecting how strong interaction networks within MAO-B, LRRK2 or A₂AR binding pockets might compensate partially or not at all for unfavorable permeability, and for informing future semi-synthetic optimization. Arnicolide D is a sesquiterpene lactone isolated from Centipeda minima and related species. It bears a compact, rigid terpenoid core with an α-methylene-γ-lactone Michael acceptor, conferring both lipophilicity and controlled electrophilicity. Arnicolide D displays potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities: it activates Nrf2 signaling, reduces ROS production and modulates stress-response pathways in cellular models, and has been identified as one of the principal bioactive components responsible for the neuroprotective potential of C. minima extracts against oxidative stress27. With a molecular weight of ~ 332 g·mol⁻¹ and computationally estimated logP in the low-to-moderate range, arnicolide D resides squarely within the CNS drug-like envelope described for successful BBB-penetrant small molecules. These features, combined with its ability to modulate redox-sensitive and inflammatory pathways, support arnicolide D as a promising chemotype for engaging kinase and enzyme targets such as LRRK2 and MAO-B, while preserving the physicochemical profile required for brain exposure. To rigorously explore the multi-target potential and CNS developability of these three chemotypes, we implemented an integrated computational pipeline combining state-of-the-art in silico ADMET profiling (ADMET-AI), structure-based molecular docking against MAO-B, LRRK2 and A₂AR, explicit-solvent molecular dynamics simulations, MM/GBSA binding free-energy calculations and principal component analysis of protein-ligand dynamics. This workflow was designed to address, in a coherent manner, the central global challenge in PD drug discovery: identifying small molecules that are simultaneously brain-penetrant, dynamically stable in the active site and capable of engaging multiple validated disease targets without compromising drug-likeness. Therefore, the objective of the present study was to evaluate falcarinol, 20-hydroxyecdysone and arnicolide D as putative tri-target candidates for MAO-B, LRRK2 and A₂AR using a comprehensive in silico approach. Specifically, we aimed (i) to characterize their pharmacokinetic and BBB-related properties within a CNS-relevant multiparameter framework, (ii) to define their binding modes and relative affinities across the three targets through docking and MM/GBSA analyses, and (iii) to elucidate the stability and key interaction networks of the resulting complexes using long-timescale molecular dynamics and principal component analysis. By integrating target- and ADMET-centric perspectives, this work seeks to identify natural scaffolds with the highest translational promise as starting points for the design of next-generation multi-target-directed ligands in PD.

Results and discussion

ADMET results

ADMET screening is the gatekeeper for CNS drug discovery: before target pharmacology can translate, candidates must demonstrate that they can be absorbed, persist systemically, and reach the brain without unacceptable liabilities. Our ADMET-AI analysis benchmarked against approved drugs provides this translational filter and guides which chemotypes deserve expensive experimental follow-up28,29. These predictions are consistent with experimental precedent. Approved MAO-B inhibitors and the A2A antagonist istradefylline are lipophilic, brain-penetrant small molecules mirroring the physicochemical envelope of falcarinol and arnicolide D. Conversely, ecdysteroids such as 20-hydroxyecdysone are highly polar and are widely reported to show low brain: plasma ratios after oral dosing, yet they can yield neuroprotective readouts when exposure is engineered or when peripheral mechanisms contribute. Across ADMET-AI and SwissADME/pkCSM, the convergent translational inference is therefore strengthened: falcarinol and arnicolide D remain the lead CNS-tractable chemotypes (with falcarinol at the high-lipophilicity/low-solubility edge and arnicolide D in a more balanced CNS property window), whereas 20-hydroxyecdysone remains a high-affinity/high-polarity scaffold whose path forward is exposure engineering (polarity masking and delivery strategies) rather than further escalation of target-binding hypotheses. This agreement between model and precedent strengthens the translational inference: falcarinol and arnicolide D are the lead brain-penetrant candidates and should proceed to quantitative CNS exposure and mechanism-based pharmacology at MAO-B, A2A, and LRRK2, while 20-hydroxyecdysone should be advanced along a prodrug-and-delivery track (e.g., multicarbonate/hemisuccinate masking, intranasal administration, transporter-targeted nanocarriers) to enable rigorous evaluation of its central neuroprotective potential.

Molecular docking

Molecular docking anchors our ADMET-guided triage in structural neuropharmacology by asking whether each chemotype can form the right contacts in targets that matter for Parkinson’s biology. MAO-B (PDB 2V5Z), a glial flavoprotein that catalyzes oxidative deamination of biogenic amines and generates reactive oxygen species, is a clinically validated symptomatic lever: occupancy of its FAD-lined cavity is mechanistically linked to preservation of striatal dopamine tone and attenuation of oxidative stress. LRRK2 (PDB 8FO7), a multidomain serine/threonine kinase whose hyperactivity drives pathogenic Rab-GTPase phosphorylation, vesicle-trafficking and autophagy defects, and neuroinflammatory signaling, constitutes a genetically validated disease-modifying axis; engagement of the ATP site in this structure is a proxy for suppressing that cascade31,32. Although 8FO7 is of moderate resolution, its ligand-bound ATP pocket enables consistent site definition and internal validation; accordingly, docking is interpreted as relative and complemented by MD/MMGBSA benchmarking. A2A (PDB 8RW0), the adenosine GPCR enriched on striatopallidal medium spiny neurons, modulates cAMP/PKA signaling and opposes D2 receptor activity; antagonism at its orthosteric pocket exemplified by istradefylline normalizes indirect-pathway output and improves motor function with levodopa-sparing potential33. Taken together, these three structures let us interrogate, within a single mechanistic frame, the pillars of PD therapy dopamine preservation (MAO-B), kinase-pathway correction (LRRK2), and circuit-level neuromodulation (A2A) thereby setting the stage for an integrated interpretation of the docking results that follows. Table 2 (ΔGdock, kcal·mol⁻¹) positions the chemotypes against their co-ligand yardsticks and supports a focused target strategy. Relative to safinamide in MAO-B (-9.7 kcal·mol⁻¹), the type-I LRRK2 inhibitor (-10.5 kcal·mol⁻¹), and istradefylline in A₂A (-8.9 kcal·mol⁻¹), arnicolide D shows near-control affinity at A₂A (-9.4 kcal·mol⁻¹) and credible engagement at LRRK2 (-8.0 kcal·mol⁻¹), while falcarinol ranks best among the naturals for MAO-B (-7.5 kcal·mol⁻¹). 20-Hydroxyecdysone is intermediate across kinase/GPCR pockets. Accounting for typical docking uncertainty (~ 1–2 kcal·mol⁻¹), Table 2 therefore prioritizes arnicolide D for A₂A/LRRK2 and falcarinol for MAO-B, with 20-hydroxyecdysone reserved for advancement contingent on exposure-enabling optimization. The SAS coloring (green→blue on the 10–25 scale) is consistent with progressive pocket burial and contact density. In Fig. 3 (MAO-B, 2V5Z), falcarinol shows a clean pose anchored by a conventional H-bond to Pro102 and a dense hydrophobic/π-alkyl belt, yielding a compact, clash-free fit that suits the bifurcated cavity. 20-hydroxyecdysone engages a triad of H-bonds (Tyr435, Ile198, Lys296) plus a C-H···O contact (Gly58) and extensive hydrophobics, but its unfavorable bump and donor-donor/acceptor-acceptor conflicts signal overcrowding and suboptimal polar geometry34,35. Arnicolide D forms two H-bonds (Tyr398, Tyr188) with supportive hydrophobic contacts, yet the acceptor-acceptor penalty near Cys172 indicates a local electrostatic mismatch. The largely green SAS for all three in MAO-B suggests moderate burial driven by hydrophobic complementarity rather than strong polar encapsulation.

Molecular dynamics

Molecular dynamics (MD) provides the kinetic test of docking hypotheses: by letting the ligand-receptor complex evolve on the nanosecond scale, MD reveals whether the docked pose is retained (ligand RMSD), how the protein backbone responds (RMSF), whether the global fold remains compact (radius of gyration, Rg), and how specific hydrogen-bond networks persist over time (HB). For CNS programs, these observables validate that an initially favorable docking geometry is not an artefact of a single conformation and, crucially, they explain discrepancies between binding energies and real-time stability38,39. For each target, the co-crystallized reference ligand was simulated under the same 100 ns protocol (blue traces in Figs. 8–11), providing a direct mobility benchmark and showing that step-like RMSD changes and moderate drift can also occur for crystallographic binders (pocket breathing and pose re-anchoring), rather than reflecting random artefacts40. Across Fig. 6–9, the three chemotypes display distinct dynamic signatures that map cleanly onto their chemistry. In MAO-B (2V5Z), ligand RMSD plateaus are lowest for 20-hydroxyecdysone and arnicolide D (~ 0.20–0.30 nm), whereas falcarinol shows a higher, noisier trace with transient excursions; ; the co-crystallized MAO-B ligand displays a comparable non-zero RMSD mobility envelope, supporting that the elongated FAD-lined cavity tolerates in-pocket micro-repositioning and that falcarinol’s higher RMSD is best interpreted as weaker anchoring (not random instability). backbone RMSF is uniformly low except at the C-terminus, and Rg stays essentially constant (~ 2.30–2.34 nm), indicating a stable dimeric fold. The high HB occupancy of 20-hydroxyecdysone (3–5 bonds; Fig. 9) rationalizes its low RMSD despite a moderate docking score, because its multi-OH steroidal scaffold continuously re-anchors to Tyr/Ile/Lys nodes seen in the 2D/3D maps; falcarinol, with only one OH and a long polyacetylene chain, lacks a persistent polar anchor, explaining its greater drift and suggesting a neutral carbonyl/carbamate add-on to lock the aromatic cage without sacrificing BBB properties41. Falcarinol shows only intermittent H-bonding and occasional RMSD bursts, aligning with its weak kinase docking and indicating low priority for this target. Notably, the co-crystallized LRRK2 ligand also undergoes RMSD rearrangements during the trajectory, consistent with intrinsic ATP-site relaxation; within this calibrated frame, arnicolide D’s late drift is most parsimoniously explained by incomplete hinge locking rather than purely stochastic motion. In A₂A (8RW0; simulated in aqueous solution without an explicit lipid bilayer), 20-hydroxyecdysone gives the lowest, most stable RMSD plateau and the highest persistent H-bond count (3–5), while arnicolide D exhibits the largest drift and falcarinol is intermediate; backbone RMSF rises in extracellular loops for falcarinol and arnicolide D but is damped with the ecdysteroid, and Rg remains within the tight GPCR band (~ 2.05–2.15 nm) with a slight compaction for falcarinol42. The polar orthosteric pocket of A₂A favors the multidentate HB network of 20-hydroxyecdysone (Asp170/Ile66), explaining its dynamic superiority despite arnicolide D’s stronger static docking; translation to the brain, however, still requires selective masking of nonessential OH groups so that the contact-forming hydroxyls are preserved while TPSA is lowered. The co-crystallized A₂A ligand likewise samples a clear non-zero RMSD mobility envelope within the orthosteric pocket, indicating that GPCR ligands can reorient while remaining bound; therefore, 20-hydroxyecdysone’s lower RMSD reflects genuine kinetic stabilization, whereas arnicolide D’s larger drift indicates looser anchoring relative to the reference. Altogether, MD consolidates the target-chemotype partitioning suggested by docking and 2D/3D interaction analyses: arnicolide D is intrinsically best for hydrophobically driven sites but benefits from a minimal hinge-bond upgrade (LRRK2) and a softer carbonyl electronics near Cys/Tyr in MAO-B; falcarinol is stabilized by lipophilic belts yet needs one neutral polar anchor to curb positional drift; 20-hydroxyecdysone exhibits exemplary kinetic stability via persistent HB networks in all three proteins, and its path forward is exposure engineering (selective OH masking/prodrug or CNS-targeted delivery) to reconcile dynamic binding with BBB constraints.

MM/GBSA free energy analysis

End-state MM/GBSA translates MD trajectories into an ensemble-averaged binding free energy that decomposes favorable gas-phase interactions (ΔEvdW + ΔEelec ≡ ΔGgas) and desolvation (ΔGGB + ΔGsurf ≡ ΔGsolv). Here, MM/GBSA results are interpreted for relative ranking and mechanistic comparison rather than as absolute binding affinities. By analyzing 100 snapshots from 75 to 100 ns, we sample equilibrated poses rather than transient docking geometries, providing a stringent check on the docking/MD narrative43,44.

Table 3 shows a coherent picture across targets. In MAO-B (2V5Z), 20-hydroxyecdysone delivers the most favorable free energy (ΔGbind = -53.39 kcal·mol⁻¹), driven by exceptionally strong electrostatics (ΔEelec ≈ -45 kcal·mol⁻¹) atop robust van der Waals contacts; the sizable polar desolvation cost (ΔGGB ≈ + 59 kcal·mol⁻¹) is nevertheless outweighed. This aligns with Figs. 3 and 9, where the ecdysteroid forms a persistent multi-H-bond network with Tyr/Ile/Lys and sustains 3–5 hydrogen bonds over time, explaining its low ligand RMSD in Fig. 6 despite only moderate docking. Falcarinol (ΔGbind ≈ -42 kcal·mol⁻¹) and arnicolide D (≈ -39 kcal·mol⁻¹) rely more on vdW with minimal directed polarity, consistent with their sparser, mainly hydrophobic contact maps and fewer H-bonds; the residual acceptor-acceptor strain near Cys172 seen for arnicolide D in Fig. 3 further rationalizes its weaker ΔGbind45. Under the same 75–100 ns snapshot protocol, the co-crystallized MAO-B ligand (Co-2V5Z) yields ΔGbind = -36.02 kcal·mol⁻¹, providing an internal reference for the MAO-B energy scale.

Principal component analysis

Principal component analysis (PCA) condenses the high-dimensional MD trajectory into a few collective coordinates that capture the slow, functionally relevant motions of the complex. By projecting the last 25 ns (75–100 ns) onto PC1/PC2 and reweighting to a free-energy surface, Fig. 11 reveals the metastable basins that the ligand-protein system actually occupies: deep, compact blue minima indicate a well-defined bound ensemble separated by barriers, whereas broad or fragmented landscapes signal conformational heterogeneity and pose hopping39. Because PCA integrates both ligand and protein motions, it provides an orthogonal test of the docking hypothesis and of the MD observables (RMSD, RMSF, Rg, and hydrogen-bond persistence), and it rationalizes the MM/GBSA thermodynamics by exposing entropic and multi-state contributions. In MAO-B (2V5Z), falcarinol and arnicolide D populate multiple low-energy wells separated along PC1/PC2, consistent with their higher ligand RMSD and largely hydrophobic stabilization, whereas 20-hydroxyecdysone collapses into a single, compact basin with steep walls, mirroring its persistent three-to-five hydrogen bonds, damped RMSD, and the most favorable MM/GBSA binding energy for this target48. In LRRK2 (8FO7), the landscape for falcarinol is a centered, nearly isotropic basin an entropically narrow ensemble that accords with its lower late-time RMSD and vdW-dominated, low-desolvation MM/GBSA signature. The ecdysteroid shows a slightly elongated, still contiguous basin that matches its intermediate RMSD and modest electrostatic anchoring, while arnicolide D exhibits a more fragmented surface with peripheral minima, echoing the late-time drift and the absence of a firm hinge bond in the ATP site. In the A₂A receptor (8RW0), 20-hydroxyecdysone again displays the most organized free-energy topology, with two adjacent deep minima connected by a shallow saddle exactly what is expected for a ligand stabilized by a resilient hydrogen-bond network in a polar orthosteric pocket and reflected in its lowest RMSD and strongest MM/GBSA ΔGbind on this target. By contrast, falcarinol spreads over several basins and arnicolide D shows well-separated wells, both indicating greater conformational mobility that tracks with their weaker hydrogen-bond persistence and less favorable ensemble thermodynamics. Taken together, the PCA landscapes in Fig. 11 reinforce the integrated story from docking, MD stability metrics, and MM/GBSA: 20-hydroxyecdysone forms a tightly defined bound ensemble in the polar pockets of MAO-B and A₂A; falcarinol achieves the most coherent basin in the kinase, consistent with shape-matched vdW control and low desolvation; and arnicolide D remains more multi-state, especially in LRRK2, unless a directed hinge contact is introduced. This convergence supports the development plan already outlined optimize 20-hydroxyecdysone’s exposure rather than its pose, add a single neutral H-bond vector to falcarinol to reduce conformational wandering, and tune arnicolide D’s carbonyl electronics and install one strategically oriented donor/acceptor to preserve the favorable ADMET profiles while sharpening the free-energy basins that underpin sustained target engagement.

ADMET-guided multi-target pharmacophore model for MAO-B, LRRK2 and A₂A ligands

By integrating the ADMET-AI benchmarking, docking poses, 100 ns MD trajectories, MM/GBSA ensemble free energies and PCA landscapes for falcarinol, 20-hydroxyecdysone and arnicolide D at MAO-B, LRRK2 and A₂A, we converge on a coherent multi-target pharmacophore that is explicitly constrained by CNS developability and thus can be used as a blueprint for future series in Parkinson’s disease. Falcarinol and arnicolide D consistently fall into the high-BBB-penetration, CNS drug-like region and bind predominantly through well-packed hydrophobic contacts with modest directed polarity, while 20-hydroxyecdysone, although thermodynamically outstanding in the polar pockets of MAO-B and A₂A and forming persistent multi-point hydrogen-bond networks, lies outside the optimal physicochemical window because of its high polar surface and multiple donors and therefore serves more as a “binding-mode teacher” than as a directly developable scaffold. Across the three targets, the shared structural message is that an effective MAO-B/LRRK2/A₂A ligand requires (i) a compact hydrophobic or heteroaromatic core that reproduces the lipophilic belts of falcarinol and arnicolide D and fits the FAD cavity of MAO-B, the lipophilic channel of the LRRK2 ATP site and the transmembrane orthosteric pocket of A₂A; (ii) a single, directionally constrained donor/acceptor “headgroup” placed at the edge of this core to mimic the multidentate anchoring of 20-hydroxyecdysone in the aromatic cage of MAO-B and the Asp/Ile region of A₂A without repeating its excessive hydroxylation; and (iii) a minimal hinge-oriented acceptor (optionally a donor/acceptor pair) positioned at kinase-appropriate distance to convert the hydrophobic complementarity seen for falcarinol and arnicolide D in LRRK2 into a true ATP-site anchor. Within this architecture, falcarinol defines the strengths and weaknesses of a slim, BBB-privileged hydrophobic skeleton with insufficient directional polarity; 20-hydroxyecdysone defines the strengths and weaknesses of a highly cooperative polar interaction pattern that must be transplanted into a less polar framework; and arnicolide D defines the strengths and weaknesses of a rigid, CNS-privileged 3D core that requires one well-placed polar vector and attenuation of strong electrophilicity near Cys172 in MAO-B. The pharmacophore therefore couples positive features (hydrophobic core volume, single bidentate polar head, minimal hinge vector) with explicit “negative” constraints (avoidance of over-decoration with hydroxyls and strongly activated Michael acceptors), and it is framed inside a CNS property envelope (moderate molecular weight, logP and polar surface area compatible with BBB penetration and low desolvation penalty) that is consistent with our ADMET-AI profiles. In practical terms, new tri-target series should be designed by starting from a falcarinol- or arnicolide D-like hydrophobic core that preserves BBB permeability, grafting onto it a constrained, low-polarity donor/acceptor fragment arranged to reproduce the MAO-B/A₂A hydrogen-bonding pattern learned from 20-hydroxyecdysone, and then extending with a minimal hinge-contact vector into the LRRK2 ATP site; by following this pharmacophore, medicinal chemists can exploit the strong points and correct the weak points of the three natural chemotypes to generate next-generation multi-target ligands for Parkinson’s disease that retain favorable ADMET properties while achieving more robust and simultaneous engagement of MAO-B, LRRK2 and A₂A.

Materials and methods

In silico ADME and toxicity prediction

ADMET profiling was conducted with ADMET-AI, a hybrid platform that combines deep learning (e.g., graph-based neural representations of molecular structure) with classical machine-learning ensembles (e.g., gradient-boosting and linear stacks) to deliver calibrated, generalizable predictions across absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and early safety domains49. By coupling learned graph embeddings with physicochemical feature spaces and aggregating them in an ensemble, the system reduces model variance and overfitting and improves reliability relative to single-model heuristics. Models are trained and cross-validated on large, curated drug-like datasets and calibrated against approved-drug distributions, yielding outputs that can be interpreted consistently across diverse chemotypes. To enhance robustness and minimize single-model bias, complementary predictions were obtained using SwissADME and pkCSM. SwissADME was used to calculate key physicochemical descriptors (MW, TPSA, HBD/HBA, logP, logS) and drug-likeness parameters, while pkCSM provided independent estimates of intestinal absorption, BBB/CNS permeability, metabolic interactions, and systemic clearance.

For this study, the stereochemically defined structures shown in Scheme 1 (falcarinol, 20-hydroxyecdysone, and arnicolide D) were submitted to all three platforms, and their outputs were comparatively analyzed without manual reweighting or post hoc adjustment.

Chemical structures of falcarinol, 20-hydroxyecdysone, and arnicolide D used for ADMET-AI predictions.

PyRx: Preparation, Configuration, and Validation of the Docking Protocol

Human MAO-B (2V5Z), LRRK2 (8FO7), and A₂A (8RW0) structures were downloaded from the PDB and prepared in AutoDockTools v1.5.7 by removing crystallographic waters and non-essential heteroatoms, adding polar hydrogens, assigning Gasteiger charges, and exporting PDBQT files; stereochemical quality was verified by Ramachandran analysis (Fig. 13) with the following statistics: 2V5Z 93.5% most-favored, 5.8% additionally allowed, 0.2% generously allowed, 0.5% disallowed; 8FO7 81.3%, 18.4%, 0.3%, 0.0%; 8RW0 96.2%, 3.8%, 0.0%, 0.0%. The ligands falcarinol, 20-hydroxyecdysone, and arnicolide D were curated from PubChem, converted to 3D, minimized with Open Babel (via PyRx 0.9.9, MMFF94) and protonated for pH 7.4. Ligand protonation states at pH 7.4 were verified using MarvinSketch 25.3.3 and cross-checked with SwissParam, confirming that the native chemical structures remain unchanged (100% identical to the initial structures) under physiological conditions. The ligands were then saved as flexible PDBQT files. Docking was run with AutoDock Vina 1.2.0 (PyRx 0.9.9) using exhaustiveness = 30 and target-site grids centered on the co-crystallized ligands: 2V5Z center (52.33, 155.44, 29.13) Å; size (17.30, 17.40, 18.54) Å; 8FO7 center (213.08, 167.31, 197.78) Å; size (19.38, 14.13, 13.73) Å; 8RW0 center (2.09, 8.99, 124.16) Å; size (9.05, 17.56, 10.26) Å50. For each complex, poses were ranked by predicted affinity (kcal·mol⁻¹) and the retained pose combined a favorable score, absence of clashes, and chemical plausibility relative to pocket pharmacophores. Protocol validity was established by redocking the original co-crystallized ligands: heavy-atom RMSD values were 1.068 Å for 2V5Z, 1.693 Å for 8FO7, and 0.734 Å for 8RW0, with overlays showing the experimental pose in red and the redocked pose in blue (Fig. 12). Molecular graphics, superpositions, and 2D/3D interaction depictions were generated with PyMOL v2.5 and BIOVIA Discovery Studio Visualizer 2024.

Implementation of molecular dynamics simulations using GROMACS

dynamics (MD) simulations were performed in GROMACS 2021.351 with the AMBER99SB-ILDN protein force field and TIP3P water. For the A₂A receptor (8RW0; a membrane GPCR), simulations were conducted in aqueous solution without an explicit lipid bilayer to keep the MD protocol uniform across targets; thus, A₂A results are interpreted as pocket-level stability and relative ranking. Each protein-ligand complex was embedded in a cubic periodic box with a minimum solute-wall distance of 1.0 nm, solvated with TIP3P, and neutralized with counterions (no excess salt). Energy minimization used steepest descent for up to 50,000 steps to a maximum force ≤ 1,000 kJ·mol⁻¹·nm⁻¹. Equilibration comprised NVT at 310 K followed by NPT at 1 bar. Production trajectories were propagated with the leap-frog integrator at a 2 fs time step; a Verlet neighbor list was updated every 20 steps (nstlist = 20) with rlist = 1.2 nm. Short-range Lennard-Jones interactions employed a force-switch from 1.0 to 1.2 nm (rvdw-switch = 1.0 nm; rvdw = 1.2 nm). Long-range electrostatics were computed with particle-mesh Ewald (rcoulomb = 1.2 nm; PME order = 4; Fourier grid spacing = 0.16 nm). Bonds involving hydrogens were constrained with LINCS (constraints = h-bonds; lincs_iter = 1; lincs_order = 4). Temperature control used a V-rescale thermostat (τₜ = 0.1 ps) applied to two coupling groups (Protein_LIG and Water_and_ions) at 310 K; pressure was maintained isotropically at 1 bar, using Berendsen during NPT equilibration and Parrinello-Rahman during production (τₚ = 2.0 ps; compressibility = 4.5 × 10⁻⁵ bar⁻¹). A 100 ns production run was generated under these conditions and analyzed for system stability and protein-ligand interactions.

MM/GBSA calculation

Binding free energies were computed by MM/GBSA with AmberTools 23 (MMPBSA.py.MPI) using 100 uniformly spaced snapshots extracted from the 75–100 ns segment of each GROMACS trajectory. The OBC2 generalized Born model (igb = 5) was applied with dielectric constants εin = 1.0 (solute) and εout = 80.0 (solvent) and an ionic strength of 0.15 M; the nonpolar solvation term was estimated from the solvent-accessible surface area (SASA). Per-residue decomposition was enabled (idecomp = 1) to resolve van der Waals, electrostatic, polar, and nonpolar contributions for each residue. Temporary files were removed upon completion.

Conclusion

This study tackled a key limitation in Parkinson’s disease drug discovery: the difficulty of designing brain-penetrant small molecules that can stably engage several validated targets within the narrow physicochemical space imposed by the CNS. Using an integrated in silico pipeline ADMET-AI profiling, validated docking, 100-ns molecular dynamics, MM/GBSA ensemble free-energy calculations and PCA we systematically evaluated falcarinol, 20-hydroxyecdysone and arnicolide D as putative tri-target candidates for MAO-B, LRRK2 and the A₂A receptor under realistic developability constraints.

Falcarinol and arnicolide D cluster in a CNS-drug-like ADMET space and exhibit robust, predominantly hydrophobic binding across all three proteins, whereas 20-hydroxyecdysone provides exceptionally strong, hydrogen-bond-driven stabilization in polar MAO-B and A₂A pockets but suffers from excessive polarity and limited predicted brain exposure. For the A₂A receptor, simulations were performed in aqueous solution without an explicit lipid bilayer; accordingly, GPCR-related conclusions are limited to orthosteric-pocket stability and relative ranking, and membrane-embedded simulations will be pursued in future work.

By combining these orthogonal lines of evidence, we propose an ADMET-guided multi-target pharmacophore for simultaneous MAO-B/LRRK2/A₂A modulation, built around a compact hydrophobic core with a single, directionally constrained donor/acceptor headgroup and a minimal kinase-hinge vector, all embedded in a stringent CNS property window. This framework rationalizes the complementary strengths of the three natural scaffolds and delivers concrete design rules for next-generation multi-target-directed ligands in Parkinson’s disease, nominating falcarinol- and arnicolide D-based series, together with exposure-optimized 20-hydroxyecdysone derivatives, for experimental validation. Future work should translate these predictions into biochemical, cellular and in vivo PD models and use the proposed pharmacophore to guide focused synthesis, prodrug strategies and scaffold hopping toward truly disease-modifying, clinically tractable multi-target agents.

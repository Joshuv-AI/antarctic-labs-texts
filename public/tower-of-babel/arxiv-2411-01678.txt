Complete W*-categories
André Henriques, Nivedita, David Penneys

Abstract
We study W ∗ {\rm W}^{*} -categories, and explain the ways in which complete W ∗ {\rm W}^{*} -categories behave like categorified Hilbert spaces. Every W ∗ {\rm W}^{*} -category C C admits a canonical categorified inner product ⟨ , ⟩ Hilb : C ¯ × C → Hilb \langle\,\,,\,\rangle_{\mathrm{Hilb}}\,:\,\overline{C}\times C\,\to\,\mathrm{Hilb} . Moreover, if C C and D D are complete W ∗ {\rm W}^{*} -categories there is an antilinear equivalence † : Func ⁡ ( C , D ) ↔ Func ⁡ ( D , C ) \dagger:\mathrm{Func}(C,D)\leftrightarrow\mathrm{Func}(D,C) characterised by ⟨ c , F † ​ ( d ) ⟩ Hilb ≃ ⟨ F ⁡ ( c ) , d ⟩ Hilb \langle c,F^{\dagger}(d)\rangle_{\mathrm{Hilb}}\simeq\langle F(c),d\rangle_{\mathrm{Hilb}} , for c ∈ C c\in C and d ∈ D d\in D .

Hilbert spaces and von Neumann algebras

In this section, we recall without proof basic results and definitions about Hilbert spaces and von Neumann algebras. We assume general familiarity with the structure and theory of von Neumann algebras.
Recall that a pre-Hilbert space is a vector space equipped with a positive definite sesquilinear form.

Lemma 2.1
.

A pre-Hilbert space
H
is a Hilbert space (i.e.
H
is complete) if and only if the map

H\to\{f:\overline{H}\to{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,\text{$f$ is bounded}\}:\xi\mapsto\langle-,\xi\rangle

is an isomorphism.
\square

If
H
is a Hilbert space,
we write
B(H)
for the
*
-algebra of bounded operators
on
H
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
von Neumann algebra
on
H
is a
*
-subalgebra of
B(H)
such that
A^{\prime\prime}=A
, where
A^{\prime}:=\{b\in B(H):ab=ba,\forall a\in A\}
denotes the commutant of
A
, and
A^{\prime\prime}:=(A^{\prime})^{\prime}
.

Given two von Neumann algebras
A\subset B(H)

and
B\subset B(K)
, their
spatial tensor product

A\bar{\otimes}B
is the von Neumann algebra generated by the algebraic tensor product
A\otimes^{alg}B
acting on the Hilbert space tensor product
H\otimes K
.

Given a collection of von Neumann algebras
A_{i}\subset B(H_{i})
indexed by some set
I
, their
product

\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}

is the von Neumann algebra generated by the algebraic direct sum
\bigoplus^{alg}_{i\in I}A_{i}
acting on the Hilbert space direct sum
\bigoplus_{i\in I}H_{i}
.
When the indexing set is finite, we also write
\bigoplus_{i\in I}A_{i}
in place of
\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}
.

A
*
-algebra homomorphism
f:A\to B
between von Neumann algebras is called
normal
if
f(\sup a_{i})=\sup f(a_{i})
for every bounded increasing net
a_{i}
of positive elements of
A
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
(von Neumann algebra) homomorphism
between two von Neumann algebras is a normal unital
*
-algebra homomorphism.

Homomorphisms between von Neumann algebras behave much more rigidly than homomorphisms between algebras. For example:

Lemma 2.4
.

If
f:A\to B
is a von Neumann algebra homomorphism, then
A
splits as a von Neumann algebra direct sum
A=A_{0}\oplus A_{1}
, were
A_{0}=\ker(f)
and
A_{1}\cong\mathrm{im}(f)
.
\square

So every homomorphism
A\to B
between von Neumann algebras admits a canonical factorisation as a projection onto a direct summand, followed by an injective homomorphism.

Definition 2.5
.

A
left module
(
right module
) over a von Neumann algebra
A
is a Hilbert space
H
equipped with a homomorphism
A\to B(H)
(
A^{\op}\to B(H)
).
An
A
-
B
-bimodule
is a Hilbert space
H
equipped with two homomorphisms
A\to B(H)
,
B^{\op}\to B(H)
whose images commute in
B(H)
.

We write
{}_{A}H
(
H_{A}
) to indicate that
H
is a left module (right module), and
{}_{A}H_{B}
to indicate that
H
is an
A
-
B
-bimodule.

The following is a fundamental result about the representation theory of von Neumann algebras:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
A
be a von Neumann algebra, and
H
a faithful
A
-module.
Then for every non-zero
A
-module
K
,

\Hom(K,H)\neq 0
.

\square

By polar decomposition and an application of Zorn’s Lemma, we then have:

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
A
be a von Neumann algebra, and
H
a faithful
A
-module.
Then for every
A
-module
K
there exists a set
I
such that
K
is isomorphic to a submodule of
H^{\oplus I}
.
We may furthermore arrange for the inclusion
K\hookrightarrow H^{\oplus I}

to be of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K=\bigoplus_{i\in I}K_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i\in I}H}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\,\,\,\oplus_{i}f_{i}\,\,\,}

where each
f_{i}:K_{i}\hookrightarrow H
is an isometric inclusion.

\square

An abstract von Neumann algebra, also known as a
{\rm W}^{*}
-algebra
, is a
*
-algebra isomorphic to a von Neumann algebra on some Hilbert space.
By the celebrated work of Haagerup
[
Haa75
]
, every abstract von Neumann algebra

admits a canonically associated Hilbert space

on which it acts, called its
standard form
, or non-commutative

-space. The space
L^{2}A
is in fact an
A
-
A
-bimodule, and comes equipped with a canonical antiunitary involution

J:L^{2}A\to L^{2}A

(1)

called
modular conjugation
.
The modular conjugation satisfies
J(a\xi b)=b^{*}J(\xi)a^{*}

for all
a,b\in A
and
\xi\in L^{2}A
.
Importantly, the left and right actions of
A
on
L^{2}A
are each other’s commutants.

The
L^{2}

Lemma 2.8

(
[
Haa75
, Lem 2.6]
)
.

If
p\in A
is a projection, then there is a canonical unitary
pL^{2}(A)p=L^{2}(pAp)
, compatible with the modular conjugations, and with left and right actions of
pAp
.

\square

Lemma 2.9
.

If
p,q\in A
are projections, then
pL^{2}(A)q=0
if and only if
pAq=0
.

\square

The construction
A\mapsto L^{2}A
is functorial for isomorphisms of von Neumann algebras, but not for general morphisms. It satisfies:

Lemma 2.10
.

If
u\in A
is a unitary, then the operators

L^{2}(\mathrm{Ad}(u))
and

\xi\mapsto u\xi u^{*}

on
L^{2}(A)
agree.

\square

We write
\lambda:A\to B(L^{2}A)
and
\rho:A^{\op}\to B(L^{2}A)

Given a

Given a

-category

The Hilb-valued inner product

Cauchy complete
{\rm W}^{*}
-categories behave in many ways like Hilbert spaces.
For example, the action (
7

admits the structure of a
{\mathbb{C}}
-module.

{\rm W}^{*}
-categories also have an inner product:

Definition 4.1
.

Every
{\rm W}^{*}
-category

\langle-,-\rangle_{\mathrm{Hilb}}:\,\overline{C}\times C\to\mathrm{Hilb}

\langle a,b\rangle_{\mathrm{Hilb}}\,:=\,p_{b}L^{2}(\mathrm{End}(a\oplus b))p_{a},

(14)

where

are the two projections.

The Hilb-valued inner product is compatible with direct sums in the sense that there are canonical unitary isomorphisms

\langle a,\oplus_{i}b_{i}\rangle\cong\oplus_{i}\langle a,b_{i}\rangle
and
\langle\oplus_{i}a_{i},b\rangle\cong\oplus_{i}\langle a_{i},b\rangle

Morphisms

and

in

induce maps

defined as the restriction to the appropriate corner of the left action
of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
f&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\End(b_{1}\oplus b_{2})
on
\langle a,b_{1}\oplus b_{2}\rangle\cong

{\langle a,b_{1}\rangle\oplus\langle a,b_{2}\rangle}
,
and of the right action of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&g^{*}\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\End(a_{1}\oplus a_{2})
on

\langle a_{1}\oplus a_{2},b\rangle\cong\langle a_{1},b\rangle\oplus\langle a_{2},b\rangle
.

The modular conjugation

on

induces a unitary isomorphism

(15)

Lemma 4.2
.

\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathrm{Hilb}}=0

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
be the unit disk, and define
\mathcal{H}
as the class of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. Let
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
denote respectively, the classes of starlike and convex functions of order
\alpha
for
0\leq\alpha<1
in
\mathcal{S}
. It is well-known that a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
if, and only if,
{\rm Re}(zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z))>\alpha
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and
f\in\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
if, and only if,
{\rm Re}(1+zf^{\prime\prime}(z)/f^{\prime}(z))>\alpha
. Similarly, a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
belongs to
\mathcal{K}
, the class of close-to-convex functions, if and only if, there exists
g\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
such that
{\rm Re}[e^{i\tau}(zf^{\prime}(z))/g(z)]>0
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\tau\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
. Thus, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{S}^{*}\subset\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{S}
. In particular, when
\tau=0
, then the resulting subclass of the close-to-convex functions is denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{0}
. The subclass
\mathcal{LU}
consists of locally univalent functions,
i.e.,
functions
f\in\mathcal{H}
with
f^{\prime}(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. For functions
f\in\mathcal{LU}
defined in a simply connected domain
{\Omega}
, the pre-Schwarzian derivative
P_{f}
and the Schwarzian derivative
S_{f}
are, respectively, defined by

\displaystyle P_{f}=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\;\mbox{and}\;S_{f}=(P_{f})^{\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{2}(P_{f})^{2}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2}.

The pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms are defined by

\displaystyle||P_{f}||_{\Omega}=\sup_{z\in{\Omega}}|P_{f}|\eta^{-1}_{\Omega}\;\mbox{and}\;||S_{f}||_{\Omega}=\sup_{z\in{\Omega}}|S_{f}|\eta^{-2}_{\Omega}

respectively, where
\eta_{\Omega}
is the Poincare density. In particular, if
{\Omega}=\mathbb{D}
, then
||S_{f}||_{\Omega}
and
||P_{f}||_{\Omega}
are denoted by
||S_{f}||
and
||P_{f}||
, respectively.

In the following, we discuss some properties of Schwarzian derivatives:

\bullet

If
\varphi
is a locally univalent analytic function for which the composition
f\circ\varphi
is defined, then

\displaystyle S_{f\circ\varphi}(z)=S_{f}\circ\varphi(z)\left(\varphi^{\prime}(z)\right)+S_{\varphi}(z).

\bullet

The Schwarzian derivative is invariant under Möbius transformation,
i.e.,

S_{T\circ\varphi}=S_{f}
for any Möbius transformation
T
of the form

\displaystyle T(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\;ad-bc\neq 0,\;a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{C}.

\bullet

It is easy to verify that
S_{f}(z)=0
if, and only, if
f
is a Möbius transformation.

\bullet

There is a classical relation between the Schwarzian derivative and second order linear differential equations. If
S_{f}=2p
and
u=\left(f^{\prime}\right)^{-1/2}
, then

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}+pu=0.

Conversely, if
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
are linearly independent solutions of this D.E. and
f=u_{1}/u_{2}
, then
S_{f}=2p
.

\bullet

The characterizations of starlikeness or convexity are sufficient but not necessary for univalency.

A simple computation shows that

(3.2)

\displaystyle 1-\frac{(1-\beta)\cos\alpha\;r}{1+r}\leq{\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\leq 1+\frac{(1-\beta)\cos\alpha\;r}{1-r}

Then, by the inequality (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\;\left(T_{f(z)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{A-1}\left(\frac{A+1}{2}\frac{1-r}{1+r}-1-\frac{(1-\beta)\cos\alpha\;r}{1-r}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(A+1-2(1-\beta)\cos\alpha)r^{2}-2(A+1+(1-\beta)\cos\alpha)r+A-1}{(A-1)(1-r^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{A}(r)}{(A-1)(1-r^{2})},

where
\Phi_{A}(r)
is given in the statement of theorem.

The right hand side of the above inequality is strictly positive if
|z|<\mathrm{R_{\alpha,\beta,{Co(A)}}}
, where
\mathrm{R_{\alpha,\beta,{Co(A)}}}
is given in the statement of the theorem. We now investigate the existence of the root
\mathrm{R_{\alpha,\beta,{Co(A)}}}\in(0,1)
for each
A\in(1,2]
.

We see that the function
\Phi(r)
which is defined in the statement of the theorem is continuous on
[0,1]
with

\displaystyle\Phi(0)=A-1>0\;\mbox{and}\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\Phi(1)=-2-4(1-\beta)\cos\alpha<0;\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\alpha,\;\beta.

By the IVT,
\Phi(r)
has at least one root in
(0,1)
. Hence,
{\rm Re}\left(T_{f(z)}\right)>0
if
|z|=r<\mathrm{R_{\alpha,\beta,{Co(A)}}}
exists for every
A\in(1,2]
. Moreover, if we consider

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-z)^{(1-\beta)\cos\alpha}}\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}\;\mbox{with}\;\beta\in[0,1).

then for this function we compute

\displaystyle T_{f_{\beta}}(z)=\frac{2}{A-1}\left(\frac{A+1}{2}\left(\frac{1-z}{1+z}\right)-1-\frac{(1-\beta)\cos\alpha\;z}{(1-z)}\right).

We observe that, if
z=-r
and
\mathrm{R_{{\alpha,\beta},{Co(A)}}}<|z|<1
, then
{\rm Re}\;T_{f_{\alpha,\beta}}(z)<0
. This proves the sharpness of the radius
\mathrm{R_{\alpha,\beta,{Co(A)}}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

The number
r\in[0,1]
is called the radius of convexity of a particular subclass
\mathcal{F}_{\beta}
of the class
\mathcal{A}
of normalized analytic functions (where
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
) in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
if
r
is the largest number such that the function
f
is convex in the disk
|z|<r
.

A function
f
analytic in a region
\Omega
is convex in
\Omega
if it maps
\Omega
onto a convex region. For an analytic function
f
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the condition for
f
to be locally univalent and convex in a disk
|z|<r
is given by the inequality you provided:

(3.3)

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)>0,\quad\text{for }|z|<r.

Theorem 3.2
.

The radius of convexity for the class of function
\mathcal{SP_{\alpha}}(\beta)
is at least
\frac{1}{(1-\beta)\cos\alpha-1}
.

Proof.

It follows from the left-hand inequality in (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{(1-\beta)\cos\alpha\;r}{1+r}

\displaystyle=\frac{r\left(1-(1-\beta)\cos\alpha\right)+1}{1+r}>0,

when
\frac{1}{(1-\beta)\cos\alpha-1}<r<1
. Thus the radius of convexity for
\mathcal{SP_{\alpha}}(\beta)
is at least
\frac{1}{(1-\beta)\cos\alpha-1}
.

To show that this radius sharp, let us consider the function
f^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathcal{SP_{\alpha}}(\beta)
, given by

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\frac{1}{(1+z)^{(1-\beta)\cos\alpha}}\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}\;\mbox{with}\;\beta\in[0,1).

A simple computation shows that

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{zf_{\alpha,\beta}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f_{\alpha,\beta}^{\prime}(z)}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{(1-\beta)\cos\alpha\;r}{1-r}

\displaystyle=\frac{-r\left(1-(1-\beta)\cos\alpha\right)+1}{1-r}

which shows that the radius is sharp. This completes the proof.
∎

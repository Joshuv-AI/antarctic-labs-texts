Theta function basis of the Cox ring of positive 2 ​ d 2d Looijenga pairs
Sean Keel, Logan White

Abstract
. [ 17 , 24.7] gives a conjectural construction of a canonical theta function basis for the regular functions on a partial minimal model of a smooth affine log CY variety with maximal boundary. Here we carry out the construction to give a canonical basis of theta functions for the Cox ring of a two dimensional Looijenga pair with affine interior, with structure constants naive counts of k k -analytic disks in the total space of the universal deformation of the mirror (which, as this is dimension two, is isomorphic to the log CY surface itself), confirming the two dimensional case of conjecture [ 17 , 24.11] .

GIT Basics

We recall some of the VGIT theory of a torus acting on an affine variety over
\mathbb{C}
, varying linearizations of the trivial bundle. Everything in
this section is well known, we include a few short arguments for the
reader’s convenience.

Take
P
an integer lattice of finite rank and
T^{P}
the torus with character lattice
P
. Let
A
denote a finitely generated integral
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, graded over
P
.
A linearization of
\Spec(A)\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
corresponds to a choice of weight
\chi\in P
describing the action of
T^{P}
on the second factor. This linearization, call it
L_{\chi}
, has invariant sections
A_{\chi}
.

In
[
2
]
, Berchtold and Hausen give a self-contained treatment of the VGIT theory of such linearizations. They show there is a finite rational polyhedral fan
\Sigma
in
P
, with support a convex cone
|\Sigma|\subset P_{\mathbb{R}}
, where

|\Sigma|(\mathbb{Z})\subset P
is
the set of weights
\chi
with
A_{\chi}\neq\emptyset
, and for any two
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in P
, we have equality of semistable loci
A^{\text{ss}}(L_{\chi_{1}})=A^{\text{ss}}(L_{\chi_{2}})
if and only if
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
lie in the interior of the same cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
. Thus to each such cone is associated a semistable locus
A^{\text{ss}}(\sigma)\subset\Spec(A)
. Additionally, for any two
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma
, we have an order-reversing correspondence between cone inclusions and semistable loci
\sigma_{1}\subset\sigma_{2}\iff A^{\text{ss}}(\sigma_{2})\subset A^{\text{ss}}(\sigma_{1})
.

Let
E=|\Sigma|(\mathbb{Z})
.

Lemma 7.1
.

E^{\text{gp}}=P
iff the kernel of the action of

T^{P}
on
\Spec(A)
is finite.

Proof.

The kernel is the intersection of the kernel of all the
characters in
E
. The lemma follows.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Assume
E^{\mathrm{gp}}=P
.
Then
A^{s}(\sigma)=A^{\text{ss}}(\sigma)
for

\sigma\in\Sigma
maximal.

Proof.

Choose non-zero homogeneous generators for
A
. This embeds

\Spec(A)
equivariantly as a closed subvariety of affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}
,
intersecting
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\eqqcolon T^{N}
, so

acts diagonally, via
a homomorphism

, which has finite cokernel by

Lemma

7.1
.

Now the VGIT picture for the action of a subtorus of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
is studied in great detail in Cox, Little, and Schenck [2]. In
particular,
Proposition

7.2
holds there, see
[
5
, 14.3.14]
.
Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
indicate the corresponding GIT fan.

Note for any linearization

and the analogous statement holds for stable loci.
We note the semi-stable locus for this action (for any linearization)

12.
Products and the basic conjectures

Here we prove the following:

Proposition 12.1
.

Suppose the basic conjectures (double
mirror, symmetry and independence) hold for smooth affine log CY

U
with maximal boundary. Then they hold for
U\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

Proof.

Skeleton and mirror algebra commute with product.
E.g. the mirror algebra of the product is the tensor product (as always,
up to coefficients), and from this double mirror and symmetry follow
easily. Now consider independence.
Take

v=(x[D],y[0])\in\Sk(V)(\mathbb{Z})\times\mathbb{Z}=\Sk(V\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\vee})(\mathbb{Z})

for positive integers
x,y
, where
D
and
[0]
indicate
divisorial centers,
V
is mirror to
U
, and
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\vee}
is the
dual torus to
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. We will assume
x=y=1
, purely for
notational simplicity, the same argument works in general.

v
then has center

D\times\{0\}\subset X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\supset V\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\vee}

for a partial minimal model
V\subset X
with smooth irreducible boundary

D
.

v
is then a multiple of the divisorial valuation given by blowing up

D\times\{0\}
. The theta functions of
V\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\vee}

are
\theta_{u}t^{k}
(for
t=z^{1}\in\mathbb{Z}[\Sk(\mathbb{G}_{m})(\mathbb{Z})]
).
We want to show that for non-zero constants
a_{u,k}
.

v(\sum_{u}\sum_{k}a_{u,k}\theta_{u}T^{k})
is given by the minimum
of
v(\theta_{u}T^{k})
. We can assume

v(\theta_{u}t^{k})=v_{D}(\theta_{u})+k\eqqcolon m

is constant over
u,k
. Let
y
be a local equation for
D
. Then we
blowup the ideal
(y,t)
(embedding the blowup as usual in

V\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{Y,T}
), and consider the chart with

Y=1
. So we have
t=Ty
, and
y
cuts out the exceptional.
Then
\theta_{u}=f_{u}y^{m-k}
with
v_{D}(f_{u})=0
, and the combination is

J\coloneqq(\sum_{u}\sum_{k}a_{u,k}f_{u}T^{k})y^{m}\in R[T]

where
R
is the DVR for
D
. Suppose
J\in(y^{m+1})R[T]
.
Then for each
k
,

\sum_{u}a_{u,k}f_{u}\in yR
. But then

\sum_{u}a_{u,k}f_{u}y^{m-k})=\sum_{u}a_{u,k}\theta_{u}\in y^{m-k+1}R

so

v_{D}(\sum_{u}a_{u,k}\theta_{u})\geq m-k+1>\min v_{D}(\theta_{u})=m-k

which violates independence for
U
.
So
v(J)=m
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now the basic three conjectures have all been proven in dimension two –
independence and symmetry by Mandel and double mirror by Lai-Zhou.

Remark 12.2
.

Here we will only need these basic conjectures in a
very simple cases, see
Remark

12.4
below.

So by the proposition we have:

Corollary 12.3
.

Let
U
be smooth affine log CY surface with
maximal boundary and trivial Picard group. Then the three basic
conjectures hold for
G\to U
.

Proof.

G\to U
is a trivial bundle since
U
has trivial Picard group.
∎

Remark 12.4
.

Trivial Picard group is a very strong condition on
U
. It
is equivalent to the existence of a smooth minimal compactification with
a toric model of relative Picard number at most two, with at most one
exceptional divisor meeting any given boundary divisor. In these simple
cases the mirror algebra has a simple explicit expression, and any of the basic conjectures can be checked by hand.

Corollary 12.5
.

Theorem

1.6
holds as long as

U
has trivial Picard group.

Proof.

In this case
G\to U
is trivial, and so the same
holds for the absolute mirror,
V\to T_{\Pic(X)}
, and the
double mirror
A_{\Sk(V),L_{X}}=A_{\Sk(U),L_{X}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}[\Pic_{X}]
,

\mathbb{V}=U\times T_{\Pic(X)}
, and the
T_{A_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})}
-bundle

\Spec(A_{\Sk(V),L_{X}})=\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{V}=T_{A_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})}\times\mathbb{V},

notation as
Corollary

11.3
. Symmetry and Independence give
A_{C,L_{X}}=\Cox(\mathbb{X})

(see e.g. the proof of
Corollary

11.5
below). Note there is no moduli, in these cases:

\mathbb{X}=X\times T_{\Pic(X)}
; no roots, and just a
single (trivial) quniversal
=
universal family. Now

Theorem

1.6
follows from VGIT of the

\Cox
ring (in the very simple surface case),
[
15
]
.
This completes the proof.
∎

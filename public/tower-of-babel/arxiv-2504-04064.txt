The limiting case of the fractional Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality in dimension one
María del Mar González, Ali Hyder, Mariel Sáez

Abstract
. In this paper we study the fractional Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality (CKN) in one dimension when the parameter γ \gamma converges (from the left) to its critical value 1 / 2 1/2 , obtaining Onofri’s inequality in the unit disk as the limit. A difficulty that we encounter is the lack of an explicit expression for the extremal function at which the CKN inequality is attained, which we address by studying solutions of the weighted Liouville equation for the half-Laplacian in dimension one.



1.
Introduction and statement of the results

Let
n\geq 2
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, or
n=1
and
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. We fix
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(1.1)

\alpha\leq\beta\leq\alpha+\gamma,\quad-2\gamma<\alpha<\frac{n-2\gamma}{2},

and set

(1.2)

p=\frac{2n}{n-2\gamma+2(\beta-\alpha)}.

The fractional Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality (CKN) generalizes the classical CKN of
[
4
]
to fractional norms with homogeneous weights. It states that there exists a constant
\Lambda>0
such that

(1.3)

{\Lambda}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{{\beta}{p}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}|x|^{{\alpha}}|y|^{{\alpha}}}\,dy\,dx

for every
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

There has been extensive work regarding existence and properties of functions that satisfy equality above (we refer to them as
extremal solutions
),
see for instance the results in
[
19
,
22
,
13
,
32
,
30
,
31
,
7
,
2
,
12
]
(this list is not exhaustive).

Before we present our results let us introduce some notations. Consider the energy functional

(1.4)

E_{\gamma,\alpha,\beta}[u]=\frac{\|u\|_{\gamma,\alpha}^{2}}{\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-\beta p}|u|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{2/p}},

where we have defined the weighted norm

(1.5)

\|u\|_{\gamma,\alpha}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}|x|^{{\alpha}}|y|^{{\alpha}}}\,dy\,dx.

We set

(1.6)

S_{\gamma}(\alpha,\beta):=\inf_{u\in D_{\gamma,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus\{0\}}E_{\gamma,\alpha,\beta}[u]

to be the best constant in inequality (
1.3
). Here the function space
D_{\gamma,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined in Section
2.1
in terms of the norm (
1.5
).
We also consider the operator

(1.7)

\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}u(x):=\textrm{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}|x|^{\alpha}|y|^{\alpha}}\,dy.

Then extremal solutions for (
1.6
) are (weak) solutions of the Euler-Lagrange equation

(1.8)

\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}u(x)=c\frac{|u(x)|^{p-2}u(x)}{|x|^{\beta p}},

for some constant
c
which acts as a Lagrange multiplier. The existence and regularity of minimizers of (
1.3
) and solutions to (
1.8
), has been addressed by several authors (see the references above).

Note that (
1.3
) can be understood as a critical fractional Hardy-Sobolev inequality (a good reference is the recent book
[
34
]
). Indeed, by the change
\tilde{u}=|x|^{-\alpha}u
, it is equivalent to consider the energy functional (see Section
2.2
for details)

(1.9)

\tilde{E}_{\gamma,\alpha,\beta}[\tilde{u}]=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}}\,dx\,dy+C_{\gamma,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\tilde{u}^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2\gamma}}\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\tilde{u}(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{(\beta-\alpha)p}}\,dx}.

This equivalence is known as the
ground state representation
of Frank-Lieb-Seiringer
[
19
]
. In particular,
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}
is just a (critical) Hardy type operator with fractional Laplacian

(1.10)

|x|^{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}u=(\varsigma_{\gamma})^{-1}(-\Delta)^{\gamma}\tilde{u}+\frac{C_{\gamma,\alpha}}{|x|^{2\gamma}}\tilde{u},

for some constants
\varsigma_{\gamma}
(given by (
2.1
)) and
C_{\gamma,\alpha}
defined precisely in Section
2.2
.
For simplicity, we define the new constant

c_{\text{CKN}}:=-\varsigma_{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\alpha}.

In
[
22
]
the authors prove existence of an extremal solution for
\tilde{E}_{\gamma,\alpha,\beta}
as long as

(1.11)

c_{\text{CKN}}<c_{\text{H}},

where
c_{\text{H}}
is the Hardy constant, defined in (
2.9
).

In addition, a symmetrization argument from
[
22
]
shows that
\tilde{u}
is radial and decreasing if
C_{\gamma,\alpha}<0
(or equivalently, even and decreasing for
x\geq 0
if
n=1
).
Another reference on the subject is
[
13
]
, where they prove that
\tilde{u}\sim|x|^{-\alpha}
as
|x|\to 0
. The separation of the region where solutions are radially symmetric from the symmetry breaking region, and the first properties of a Felli-Schneider type curve were considered in
[
2
]
.

These results can be
summarized by the following theorem (see Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 in
[
22
]
and Proposition 4.7 in
[
2
]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
0<\gamma<1
,
0\leq p(\beta-\alpha)<2\gamma<n
and

c_{\text{CKN}}<c_{\text{H}}
. Then:

(1)

If either
\{\beta>\alpha\}
or
\{\beta=\alpha\hbox{ and }c_{\text{CKN}}\geq 0\}
then the infimum of

\tilde{E}_{\gamma,\alpha,\beta}[\cdot]
is attained.

(2)

If
\beta=\alpha\hbox{ and }c_{\text{CKN}}<0
then there are no extremals for
\tilde{E}_{\gamma,\alpha,\beta}[\cdot]
.

(3)

If
0<c_{\text{CKN}}
or
\{0=c_{\text{CKN}}\hbox{ and }0<(\beta-\alpha)p<2\gamma\}
then any non-negative minimizer for
\tilde{E}_{\gamma,\alpha,\beta}[\cdot]
is positive, radially symmetric, radially decreasing and approaches 0 as
|x|\to\infty
.

(4)

If the infimum of
\tilde{E}_{\gamma,\alpha,\beta}[\cdot]
is attained then the extremal solution is strictly positive.

In this work we are interested in the case
n=1
, more precisely
we will always assume that

n=1,\quad\gamma\in\big(0,\frac{1}{2}\big),\quad 0<\alpha<\frac{1-2\gamma}{2},\quad\alpha<\beta<\alpha+\gamma.

Remark 1.2
.

The results described by Theorem
1.1
are also valid for
n=1
. In addition, it is not difficult to verify that
0<c_{\text{CKN}}<c_{\text{H}}
in our range of parameters
(see the discussion in Section
2.2
). Then, a minimizer exists, and it is positive, radially symmetric and decreasing in the radial variable.

The value
\gamma=\tfrac{1}{2}
is critical for (
1.3
) in dimension one (see, for instance, the related reference
[
32
]
). In this paper we study this limiting case obtaining, in a suitable scale, a generalized Onofri’s inequality.

Before we give the statement of our main theorem, let us recall the work of
[
15
]
, where they recover the classical Moser-Trudinger-Onofri’s inequality as the liming case of the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality for the Laplace operator
(\gamma=1)
in dimension
n=2
as
\alpha,\beta\to 0
(see also the survey paper
[
16
]
). The classical CKN inequality in dimension
n=2
reads

(1.12)

{\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{{\beta}{p}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{{2\alpha}}}\,dx.

There is extensive work on this inequality, including fundamental symmetry and symmetry breaking results (let us mention
[
18
,
14
]
, although by no means we are trying to be exhaustive).
Fix
-1<a<0
and choose the parameters

p_{\varepsilon}=\frac{2}{\varepsilon},\quad\alpha_{\varepsilon}=-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}(a+1),\quad\beta_{\varepsilon}=\alpha_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon,\quad\text{as}\quad\varepsilon\downarrow 0.

With this choice we are in the symmetry region
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
so that the “bubble” function

(1.13)

u_{\varepsilon}(x):=\left(1+|x|^{-\frac{2\alpha_{\varepsilon}(1+\alpha_{\varepsilon}-\beta_{\varepsilon})}{\beta_{\varepsilon}-\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}\right)^{-\frac{\beta_{\varepsilon}-\alpha_{\varepsilon}}{1+\alpha_{\varepsilon}-\beta_{\varepsilon}}}

is an extremal solution for (
1.12
). Now define the measure

d\nu_{a}=\frac{a+1}{\pi}\frac{|x|^{2a}}{(1+|x|^{2(a+1)})^{2}}\,dx

and set

w_{\varepsilon}=(1+\varepsilon v)u_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{for any}\quad v\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},d\nu_{a}),\text{ with }|\nabla v|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},dx).

Then, substituting
w_{\varepsilon}
in the inequality (
1.12
)
and passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, one obtains

(1.14)

\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{v}\,d\nu_{a}\leq\frac{1}{16\pi(a+1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}v\,d\nu_{a},

This is a generalization of Onofri’s inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{v-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}v\,d\nu_{0}}\,d\nu_{0}\leq e^{\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx},

which corresponds to the classical Moser-Trudinger-Onofri inequality on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
after stereographic projection.

Our main result recovers, at the limit, the “boundary version” of Onofri’s inequality on the unit disk
D_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which reads as follows
[
33
]
: for every
w\in W^{1,2}(D_{1})
it holds that

(1.15)

\log\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}e^{w}\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{D_{1}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,\frac{dx}{\pi}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}w\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.

This inequality has appeared in many contexts. Indeed, it is the first Lebedev-Milin inequality
[
17
, Chapter 5]
, which is an important ingredient in the proof of Bieberach’s conjecture (a historical account may be found in
[
28
]
). Also, improvements under constraints can be obtained from the work in
[
24
]
on Toeplitz
determinants (see the observation in
[
37
]
). From a more geometric point of view, the inequality was proven in
[
33
]
in relation to extremals of functional determinants on manifolds with boundary (for which, in the simply connected case, the optimal is attained on the disk).

It is possible to rewrite (
1.15
) in terms of the half-Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}
using stereographic projection. More precisely, for a given function
u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D_{1})
, we consider its harmonic extension in
D_{1}
, still denoted by the same notation
u
. Then, recalling the definition of the half-Laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
(see, for instance,
[
9
]
), it holds that

\int_{D_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}u(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{1}})^{\frac{1}{2}}u\,d\theta.

After stereographic projection
\Pi:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
, we can write (
1.15
) as

(1.16)

\log\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{v}\,dm\leq\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}})^{\frac{1}{2}}v\,dx+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\,dm,

where the measure is given by

dm=\frac{2}{1+x^{2}}\,dx

and
v(x)=u(\Pi^{-1}(x))
. Here we have used that the half-Laplacian is a conformal operator in dimension one.

As we approach the critical exponent
\gamma\to(\frac{1}{2})^{-}
, our main theorem shows that Onofri’s inequality (
1.16
) can be obtained as limit from the fractional Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality (
1.3
) in dimension
n=1
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n=1
and choose a sequence of parameters

(1.17)

\gamma_{\varepsilon}\to\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-},\quad\beta_{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+},\quad 0<\alpha_{\varepsilon}<\min\left\{\beta_{\varepsilon},\frac{1-2\gamma_{\varepsilon}}{2}\right\},\quad\text{as}\quad\varepsilon\downarrow 0,\quad\text{(in particular }p_{\varepsilon}\to\infty),

for which a (positive) radially symmetric minimizer
u_{\varepsilon}
exists.
Suppose that

\varepsilon p_{\varepsilon}\to a\in(0,\infty),\quad\beta_{\varepsilon}p_{\varepsilon}\to b\in[0,1).

Up to a suitable normalization we assume that
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

(1.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{p_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|x|^{\beta_{\varepsilon}p_{\varepsilon}}}(u_{\varepsilon})^{p_{\varepsilon}-1},\quad\max_{\overline{B_{1}}}u_{\varepsilon}=1.

Then, the function

\eta_{\varepsilon}:=p_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-1)

converges (up to a subsequence) in
C^{0}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
to
\eta_{0}
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. Moreover, if we define

(1.19)

\eta:=\eta_{0}+\log\zeta_{\frac{1}{2}},

then
\eta\in L^{1}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
(this space will be defined in (
2.2
)) and
\eta
is a solution to

(1.20)

(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta=|x|^{-b}e^{\eta}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\kappa_{b}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{-b}e^{\eta}\,dx=2\pi(1-b)<\infty.

In addition, if we denote

dm_{b}=\frac{e^{\eta}}{|x|^{b}}\,dx,

then we have

(1.21)

\log\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{v}\,dm_{b}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2\kappa_{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}})^{\frac{1}{2}}v\,dx+\frac{1}{\kappa_{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\,dm_{b},

for every
v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 1.4
.

It is always possible to choose a sequence of parameters in (
1.17
) such that the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
hold.
For instance, by fixing
b\in[0,1)
, for
\varepsilon>0
small enough we can take

a=1,\quad p_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon},\quad\beta_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon(b+\varepsilon),\quad\alpha_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{\beta_{\varepsilon}}{2},\quad\gamma_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\left[1-\frac{1}{2}(b+\varepsilon)\right].

Indeed, this is a valid choice in the setting of Remark
1.2
.

Our proof, although similar in spirit to that of
[
15
]
, presents a challenging difficulty due to the fact that there is not an explicit formula for the extremal function such as the one in (
1.13
) for the classical case. Indeed, while the proof in
[
15
]
relies on explicit calculations as
\varepsilon\to 0
working with the function
w_{\varepsilon}=(1+\varepsilon v)u_{\varepsilon}
for
u_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
1.13
), we need to find a different strategy to pass to the limit. Instead, we achieve compactness by establishing a-priori estimates with uniform constants that do not depend on the parameters.

Problem (
1.20
) has been well studied when
b=0
(see
[
6
,
38
,
8
,
9
,
21
,
1
]
). In this particular case, if
\eta\in L^{1}(dm_{0})
satisfies the non-local Liouville equation

(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta=e^{\eta}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\eta}\,dx<\infty,

then we must have (up to translation and dilation)

\eta=\log\frac{2}{1+x^{2}}\quad\text{and in particular }\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\eta}\,dx=2\pi.

We observe that, as a consequence of our theorem for
b=0
we recover Onofri’s inequality (
1.16
).

In the presence of singular sources (the case
-1<b<1
), the classification of solutions is also known (see
[
20
,
39
]
). In fact, these are given by the one-parameter family

(1.22)

\eta_{\rho}(x)=\log\left(\frac{2(-b+1)\rho\cos\frac{\pi b}{2}}{|x|^{2(-b+1)}+2\rho|x|^{-b+1}\sin\frac{\pi b}{2}+\rho^{2}}\right),\quad\rho>0.

In addition, non-degeneracy of this solution was studied in
[
11
]
, while Brezis-Merle type concentration-compactness theorems were considered in
[
40
]
.

Remark 1.5
.

For
b\leq-1
, there is no solution to the singular Liouville equation (
1.20
) (and the function
\eta
does not make sense in that context). However, we can show (see the end of Section
5
) that the inequality (
1.21
) does not hold for
-1<b<0
.

Remark 1.6
.

We expect that (
1.14
) also holds true if one considers the fractional CKN inequality in dimension
n=2
and takes the limiting case
\gamma\to 1
; for this we refer to the paper
[
5
]
, which is inspired in the dimension continuation argument of Branson
[
3
]
. Indeed, continuation arguments are very well suited in the fractional world (see, for instance, the formal derivation in
[
10
, Section 6]
at the operator level).

Remark 1.7
.

The validity of (
1.3
) can be extended to a wider region of parameters

\alpha\leq\beta\leq\alpha+\gamma,\quad\frac{n-2\gamma}{2}<\alpha<n,

(see the appendix and the paper
[
32
]
for details); however, we will not consider those cases in our work.

The structure of the paper is the following: in Section
2
we give some background on the fractional Laplacian together with some preliminary results. Then, in Section
3
we consider the existence and properties of the extremal solutions to (
1.3
)). Section
4
yields the required compactness in order to pass to the limit. Finally, Section
5
contains the proof of the main theorem. We conclude with an appendix proving Remark
1.7
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Function spaces

The fractional Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

(-\Delta)^{\gamma}u(x)=\varsigma_{\gamma}\textrm{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}}\,dy,

where

(2.1)

\varsigma_{\gamma}=\pi^{-\frac{n}{2}}2^{2\gamma}\frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{n}{2}+\gamma\right)}{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}\gamma

is a multiplicative constant and
PV
denotes the principal value.

We will consider the space
L^{1}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined by

(2.2)

L^{1}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\|u\|_{L^{1}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty\right\},

where

\|u\|_{L^{1}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|}{1+|x|^{q}}\,dx.

We now introduce a homogeneous weight. Let
D_{\gamma,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the inner product

(2.3)

\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle_{\gamma,\alpha}:=\textrm{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y))(u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}|x|^{{\alpha}}|y|^{{\alpha}}}\,dy\,dx.

Its associated norm is denoted by

\|u\|_{\gamma,\alpha}^{2}:=\textrm{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}|x|^{{\alpha}}|y|^{{\alpha}}}\,dy\,dx.

For simplicity, define the kernel

(2.4)

G_{\gamma,\alpha}(x,y):=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}|x|^{\alpha}|y|^{\alpha}},

so that

\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}u(x)=\textrm{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[u(x)-u(y)]G_{\gamma,\alpha}(x,y)\,dy.

In what follows, all the singular integrals need to be interpreted in a principal value sense, but for the sake of simplifying the notation, we omit writing it explicitly.

Observe that formally we have

\begin{split}\|u\|_{\gamma,\alpha}^{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[u(x)-u(y)]^{2}G_{\gamma,\alpha}(x,y)\,dxdy\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[u(x)-u(y)]u(x)G_{\gamma,\alpha}(x,y)\,dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[-u(x)+u(y)]u(y)G_{\gamma,\alpha}(x,y)\,dy\\
&=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x)\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}u(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Now let us consider the equation

(2.5)

\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}u=F\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Given
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we say that
u\in D_{\gamma,\alpha}
is a weak energy solution of (
2.5
) if

(2.6)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(\phi(x)-\phi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}|x|^{{\alpha}}|y|^{{\alpha}}}\,dy\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x)\phi(x)\,dx\quad\text{for all }\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

2.2.
An equivalent formulation

Here we give the ground state representation (
1.9
) for our problem.

Note that

|x|^{-\alpha}|y|^{-\alpha}(u(x)-u(y))=|x|^{-\alpha}(|x|^{-\alpha}u(x)-|y|^{-\alpha}u(y))+|x|^{-\alpha}u(x)(|y|^{-\alpha}-|x|^{-\alpha}),

then, writing
\tilde{u}=|x|^{-\alpha}u
, from formula (
1.7
) we obtain

\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}u=|x|^{-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}}\,dy+C_{\gamma,\alpha}|x|^{-\alpha-2\gamma}\tilde{u}(x),

where

C_{\gamma,\alpha}=|x|^{\alpha+2\gamma}\textrm{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|y|^{-\alpha}-|x|^{-\alpha}}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}}\,dy.

Changing variables
z=\frac{y}{|x|}
we arrive to

(2.7)

C_{\gamma,\alpha}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|z|^{-\alpha}-1}{|e_{1}-z|^{n+2\gamma}}\,dz.

Trivially,
C_{\gamma,0}=0
. A simple computation shows that this constant has a sign. More precisely:

Lemma 2.1
.

In our range of parameters,
C_{\gamma,\alpha}
is a decreasing function of
\alpha
. If in addition we assume

0<\alpha<n-2\gamma
, then we have that
C_{\gamma,\alpha}<0
.

Proof.

With the change of variables
z\mapsto\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}
in (
2.7
) for the integral on
B_{1}^{c}
, and using the identity

(2.8)

|x||y|\left|\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}\right|=|x-y|

one obtains

\displaystyle C_{\gamma,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{B_{1}}+\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\right)\frac{|z|^{-\alpha}-1}{|e_{1}-z|^{n+2\gamma}}\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|z|^{-\alpha}-1}{|e_{1}-z|^{n+2\gamma}}\,dz+\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|z|^{\alpha}-1}{|e_{1}-z|^{n+2\gamma}}|z|^{-n+2\gamma}\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{(|z|^{-\alpha}-1)(1-|z|^{-n+2\gamma+\alpha})}{|e_{1}-z|^{n+2\gamma}}\,dz.

It follows that the sign of
C_{\gamma,\alpha}
depends on the sign of
\alpha
and
-n+2\gamma+\alpha
. In the particular case,
0<\alpha<n-2\gamma
, we clearly have
C_{\gamma,\alpha}<0
as the numerator is negative in
B_{1}
.
Moreover,
C_{\gamma,\alpha}
is decreasing for
\alpha\leq\frac{n-2\gamma}{2}
, and increasing for
\alpha\geq\frac{n-2\gamma}{2}
. To see this, for a fixed
0<|z|<1
we define

f(\alpha):=(|z|^{-\alpha}-1)(1-|z|^{-n+2\gamma+\alpha}).

Then we have

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=-|z|^{-\alpha}\log|z|\left(1-|z|^{-n+2\gamma+2\alpha}\right)\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}>0&\quad\text{if }\alpha>\frac{n-2\gamma}{2}\\
<0&\quad\text{if }\alpha<\frac{n-2\gamma}{2},\end{array}\right.

giving the desired monotonicity of
C_{\gamma,\alpha}
with respect to
\alpha
.
∎

Note that for the particular value
\alpha=\frac{n-2}{2}
we reduce to the Hardy constant
(see, for instance, the appendix in
[
2
]
). More precisely,

(2.9)

c_{\text{H}}:=\varsigma_{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1-|z|^{-\frac{n-2\gamma}{2}}}{|e_{1}-z|^{n+2\gamma}}\,dz=2^{2\gamma}\frac{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{n+2\gamma}{4}\right)}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{n-2\gamma}{4}\right)}=-\varsigma_{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\frac{n-2\gamma}{2}}.

With a similar computation we have

\|u\|_{\gamma,\alpha}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}}\,dy\,dx+C_{\gamma,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\tilde{u}^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2\gamma}}\,dx,\quad\text{where}\quad\tilde{u}=|x|^{-\alpha}u.

Thus it is possible to consider the equivalent functional

\tilde{E}_{\gamma,\alpha,\beta}[\tilde{u}]=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}}\,dx\,dy+C_{\gamma,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\tilde{u}^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2\gamma}}\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\tilde{u}(x)|^{p}|x|^{(-\beta+\alpha)p}\,dx}.

The existence and properties of minimizers of
\tilde{E}_{\gamma,\alpha,\beta}
were already partially discussed in the introductory section above. In addition, further regularity properties will be presented in
Section
3
.

The operator (
1.7
) in the new notation has the form

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma,\alpha}(\tilde{u})=(\varsigma_{\gamma})^{-1}(-\Delta)^{\gamma}\tilde{u}+C_{\gamma,\alpha}\frac{1}{|x|^{2\gamma}}\tilde{u}.

2.3.
Operation rules

Lemma 2.2
.

(Integration by parts)
Let
u,v\in D_{\gamma,\alpha}
. It holds

(2.10)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v(x)\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}u(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x)\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}v(x)\,dx.

Proof.

Calculate

\begin{split}I:&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v(x)\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}u(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[u(x)-u(y)]v(x)G_{\gamma,\alpha}(x,y)\,dxdy\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x)[v(x)-v(y)]G_{\gamma,\alpha}(x,y)\,dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y)[v(y)-v(x)]G_{\gamma,\alpha}(x,y)\,dxdy\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[u(x)-u(y)]v(y)G_{\gamma,\alpha}(x,y)\,dxdy\\
&=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x)\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}v(x)\,dx-I,\end{split}

from where we obtain our claim.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

(Product formula) Let
u,v\in D_{\gamma,\alpha}
. Then

(2.11)

\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}(uv)=v\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}u+u\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\alpha}v-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[u(\cdot)-u(y)][v(\cdot)-v(y)]G_{\gamma,\alpha}(\cdot,y)\,dy.

Proof.

We expand

u(x)v(x)-u(y)v(y)=[u(x)-u(y)]v(x)+[v(x)-v(y)]u(x)-[u(x)-u(y)][v(x)-v(y)],

and the lemma follows immediately.
∎

3.

We show the following regularity result (independent of the parameters).

Theorem 3.1

Let

]

,

We conclude by proving Theorem

Proof of Proposition
3.4

(3.33)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{|x|^{\theta}}|\psi(x)|\,dx\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho})}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho})}\int_{B_{2\rho}}\,\frac{dx}{|x|^{\theta}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho})}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho})}\rho^{1-\theta}.

In summary, going back to
\frac{R}{\rho}=2^{k_{0}}
, (
3.23
), (
3.26
) and (
3.32
) we have proved that

\begin{split}[u]_{\rho}^{2}=\int_{B_{\rho}}\int_{B_{\rho}}G_{\gamma,\alpha}(x,y)|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\,dxdy&\leq C(k_{0})\left([u]^{2}_{R}-[u]_{\rho}^{2}\right)+C_{0}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho})}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho})}\rho^{1-\theta}\\
&\quad+C_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}G_{\gamma,\alpha}(x,y)|u(x)-u(y)||\psi(x)|\,dxdy.\end{split}

Now we use Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
to bound the last integral above, and this completes the proof of Proposition

∎

We conclude this subsection with one last technical lemma that will be used in the proof of Theorem

.

Lemma 3.7
.

We have

\displaystyle[u]_{2}^{2}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3})}\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3})}+\Tail(u,x_{0},3)\right).

Proof.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{3})
be such that
\phi\equiv 1
in
B_{\frac{5}{2}}
and
|\nabla\phi|\leq 4
. We write

\begin{split}[u(x)-u(y)]&[\phi^{2}(x)u(x)-\phi^{2}(y)u(y)]\\
&=\phi^{2}(x)[u(x)-u(y)]^{2}+[u(x)-u(y)][\phi(x)+\phi(y)][\phi(x)-\phi(y)]u(y)\\
&\geq|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\left\{\phi^{2}(x)-\frac{1}{8}(\phi(x)+\phi(y))^{2}\right\}-2|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{2}u^{2}(y).\end{split}

Interchanging
x
with
y
in the above inequality, and then summing with it we get

(3.34)

\begin{split}&2[u(x)-u(y)][\phi^{2}(x)u(x)-\phi^{2}(y)u(y)]\\
&\geq|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}[\phi^{2}(x)+\phi^{2}(y)-\frac{1}{4}(\phi(x)+\phi(y))^{2}]-2|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{2}[u^{2}(y)+u^{2}(x)]\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}[\phi^{2}(x)+\phi^{2}(y)]-2|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{2}[u^{2}(y)+u^{2}(x)].\end{split}

Taking
\phi^{2}u
as a test function in the weak formulation of (
3.1
) (again, it is enough to look at the weak formulation (
2.6
) on
\mathbb{R}
),

(3.35)

\begin{split}2\int\frac{f(x)}{|x|^{\theta}}&u(x)\phi^{2}(x)\,dx\\
&=\left(\int_{B_{3}}\int_{B_{3}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B_{3}}\int_{B_{3}}+\int_{B_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B_{3}}\right)G_{\gamma,\alpha}(x,y)[u(x)-u(y)][\phi^{2}(x)u(x)-\phi^{2}(y)u(y)]\,dxdy\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.\end{split}

It follows from (
3.34
) that

I_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}[u]_{2}^{2}-C\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{3})},

and, in addition,

(3.36)

\displaystyle|I_{2}|+|I_{3}|\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3})}\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3})}+\Tail(u,x_{0},3)\|\right),

thanks an argument similar to the one to obtain Lemma
3.6
. For the left hand side of (
3.35
) we argue as in (
3.33
), which yields the desired conclusion.
∎

4.

We denote

Up to a subsequence,

in

as long as we can prove its hypotheses, namely a local uniform bound for

) for

We divide the proof of this last statement into several intermediate steps.

Proposition 4.2
.

We have, as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,

(4.6)

S_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{C}{p_{\varepsilon}}

and, in particular,

(4.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(u_{\varepsilon})^{p_{\varepsilon}}}{|x|^{\beta_{\varepsilon}p_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\leq C.

Proof.

For
{\delta_{\varepsilon}}>0
small (to be chosen later) we set

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)=(1+x^{2})^{-{\delta_{\varepsilon}}},

which will be used as a test function in (
1.4
).
Since
\varphi
is even

\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{\varepsilon}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}}|x|^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}|y|^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}\,dydx\leq 4\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}}|x|^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}|y|^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}\,dydx.

For
x\geq 0
we set

f_{\varepsilon}(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}}|y|^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}\,dy.

We claim that

(4.8)

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\frac{C{(\delta_{\varepsilon})}^{2}}{1+x^{2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}+4{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}},\quad x\geq 0.

First we prove the claim for
x\geq 4
. We write

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{x}{2}}+\int_{\frac{x}{2}}^{2x}+\int_{2x}^{\infty}\right)\frac{(\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}}|y|^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}\,dy

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

We use that for
y\geq x\geq 4
,

\displaystyle|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{x}^{y}\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\,dt\right|\leq C{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{x}^{y}t^{-1-2{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{x^{2{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}}-\frac{1}{y^{2{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}}\right)

\displaystyle=:Cg_{\varepsilon}(x,y).

Changing the variable as
y\mapsto xy
yields

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{2x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y^{1+2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}}g_{\varepsilon}^{2}(x,y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{x^{2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}+4{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}}\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y^{1+2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}}g_{\varepsilon}^{2}(1,y)\,dy.

Now, integration by parts gives, taking into account that the constant
C
stays uniformaly bounded in
\epsilon
when we take the limit
{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}\to\frac{1}{2}
,
{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}\to 0
and
{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\to 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y^{1+2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}}g_{\varepsilon}^{2}(1,y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y^{2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}}g_{\varepsilon}(1,y)\partial_{y}g_{\varepsilon}(1,y)\,dy{+C\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\delta_{\varepsilon}}}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y^{2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}+1+2{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}}g_{\varepsilon}(1,y)\,dy+{C\delta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y^{2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}+2{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}}\partial_{y}g_{\varepsilon}(1,y)\,dy{+C\delta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,({\delta_{\varepsilon}})^{2}

and hence,

I_{3}\leq\frac{C({\delta_{\varepsilon}})^{2}}{x^{2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}+4{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}}.

In a similar way, changing the variable
y\mapsto\frac{x}{y}
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{x^{1+2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}}}\int_{0}^{\frac{x}{2}}y^{-{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y))^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{x^{2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}+4{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}}\int_{2}^{\infty}y^{{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}-2}g_{\varepsilon}^{2}(1,\tfrac{1}{y})\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C({\delta_{\varepsilon}})^{2}}{x^{2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}+4{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}}.

Finally, using that

|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq C{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|x-y|}{x^{1+2{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}},\quad\text{for}\quad\frac{x}{2}\leq y\leq 2x,

we bound

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\frac{C{(\delta_{\varepsilon})}^{2}}{x^{2+4{\delta_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}}\int_{\frac{x}{2}}^{2x}|x-y|^{1-2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}}\,dy\leq\frac{C({\delta_{\varepsilon}})^{2}}{x^{2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}+4{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}}.

Combining the above estimates yields the inequality in (
4.8
) for
x\geq 4
. Let us now consider the case
0\leq x\leq 4
. We write

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{8}+\int_{8}^{\infty}\right)\frac{(\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}}|y|^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}\,dy

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

As in the estimate of
I_{2}
, taking into account that
|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq C\delta_{\varepsilon}|x-y|
, one obtains
J_{1}\leq C({\delta_{\varepsilon}})^{2}.

To bound
J_{2}
note first that for
0\leq x\leq y
holds

\displaystyle|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)|=\left|2{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\int_{x}^{y}\frac{t}{(1+t^{2})^{1+{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}}\,dt\right|.

Since for

0\leq x\leq t\leq y

\frac{t}{(1+t^{2})^{1+\delta_{\varepsilon}}}\leq C\frac{t^{\frac{1}{4}}t^{\frac{3}{4}}}{(1+t)^{2+2\delta_{\varepsilon}}}\leq Cy^{\frac{1}{4}}\frac{(1+t)^{\frac{3}{4}}}{(1+t)^{2+2\delta_{\varepsilon}}}=Cy^{\frac{1}{4}}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\frac{5}{4}+2\delta_{\varepsilon}}},

we have

\displaystyle|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq C{\delta_{\varepsilon}}y^{\frac{1}{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{(1+t)^{\frac{5}{4}+2{\delta_{\varepsilon}}}}\leq C{\delta_{\varepsilon}}y^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Then, as
\frac{1}{2}+2{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}+{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}\to\frac{3}{2}
,

This proves inequality (

Now we chose
\delta_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

in (

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\leq C(\delta_{\varepsilon})^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+x^{1+\frac{4}{p_{\varepsilon}}}}\,dx\leq C(\delta_{\varepsilon})^{2}\,p_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{C}{p_{\varepsilon}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(\varphi_{\varepsilon})^{p_{\varepsilon}}}{x^{\beta_{\varepsilon}p_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{\beta_{\varepsilon}p_{\varepsilon}}}\frac{1}{(1+x)^{3-\beta_{\varepsilon}p_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx,

Combining the above estimates yields (

\frac{C}{p_{\varepsilon}}\geq S_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\,dx}{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(u_{\varepsilon})^{p_{\varepsilon}}}{|x|^{\beta_{\varepsilon}p_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p_{\varepsilon}}}}=\frac{1}{p_{\varepsilon}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(u_{\varepsilon})^{p_{\varepsilon}}}{|x|^{\beta_{\varepsilon}p_{\varepsilon}}}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{2}{p_{\varepsilon}}},

∎

Lemma 4.4

Lemma 4.5

Let

For

To complete the proof we note that

\displaystyle|x|^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2\gamma_{\varepsilon}}|y|^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y)}{|y|^{1+2\gamma_{\varepsilon}}|x-y|^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(x+y)}{|y|^{1+2\gamma_{\varepsilon}}|x+y|^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}}\,dy,

\tilde{v}_{t}(x)=2(1-b)\log\frac{1}{t}\quad\text{on }B_{t}(1),

and therefore,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\tilde{v}_{t}}\frac{e^{\eta}}{|x|^{b}}\,dx\geq\frac{1}{2}e^{\eta(1)}\int_{1-t}^{1+t}e^{\tilde{v}_{t}}\,dx=e^{\eta(1)}\frac{t}{t^{2(1-b)}}\geq\frac{1}{C}\frac{1}{t^{1-2b}}.

On the other hand, it follows easily that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tilde{v}_{t}\frac{e^{\eta}}{|x|^{b}}\,dx\leq C.

Plugging these estimates in (
1.21
), and together with
\kappa=2\pi(1-b)
, we deduce that

(1-2b)\log\frac{1}{t}\leq C+(1-b)\log\frac{1}{t},

which is impossible for
t>0
sufficiently small if
b<0
. ∎

Appendix A
Extending the inequality

We can extend the range of (
1.1
) to

(A.1)

\alpha\leq\beta\leq\alpha+\gamma,\quad\frac{n-2\gamma}{2}<\alpha<n.

To see this, consider the inversion
\bar{x}=\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}
,
\bar{y}=\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}
and make the change of variable, taking into account that
dx=|x|^{2n}d\bar{x}
,
dy=|y|^{2n}d\bar{y}
. Another important ingredient is formula (
2.8
), that says

|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|=\frac{|x-y|}{|x|\,|y|}.

If we set
u(x)=\bar{u}\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)
then, on the one hand,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}|x|^{{\alpha}}|y|^{{\alpha}}}\,dy\,dx&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left(\bar{u}(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}})-\bar{u}(\frac{y}{|y|^{2}})\right)^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2\gamma}|x|^{{\alpha}}|y|^{{\alpha}}}\,dy\,dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left(\bar{u}(\bar{x})-\bar{u}(\bar{y})\right)^{2}}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{n+2\gamma}|\bar{x}|^{{\bar{\alpha}}}|\bar{y}|^{{\bar{\alpha}}}}\,d\bar{y}\,d\bar{x},\end{split}

where we have set

\bar{\alpha}=n-2\gamma-\alpha.

On the other hand, if we take

\beta p+\bar{\beta}p=2n,

which implies

p=\frac{2n}{n-2\gamma+2(\beta-\alpha)}=\frac{2n}{n-2\gamma+2(\bar{\beta}-\bar{\alpha})},

then we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{{\beta}{p}}}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\bar{u}(\bar{x})|^{p}}{|\bar{x}|^{{\bar{\beta}}{p}}}\,d\bar{x}.

Thus, if the inequality is true for
\bar{u}
,
\bar{\alpha}
,
\bar{\beta}
, then it is also valid for
u
,
\alpha
and
\beta
in the range (
A.1
).

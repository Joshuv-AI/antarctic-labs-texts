Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the defocusing cubic NLS posed on the two-dimensional waveguide manifold
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}u=|u|^{2}u,\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T},\\
u(0,x,y)=u_{0}(x,y)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\end{cases}

where
u:\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
is the unknown function. The waveguide manifold
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
is known as the semi-periodic space. The Cauchy problem (
1.1
) has the Hamiltonian structure and its solution enjoys the following two conservation laws

Mass:

\displaystyle M(u(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}|u(t,x,y)|^{2}dxdy=M(u_{0}),

Energy:

\displaystyle E(u(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u(t,x,y)|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|u(t,x,y)|^{4}\right)dxdy=E(u_{0}).

The local well-posedness of
(
1.1
)
is influenced by the choice of the spatial domain. In the case of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, this equation exhibits invariance under the following scaling transformation, which in turn gives rise to the concept of criticality for the Cauchy problem associated with (
1.1
):

\displaystyle u(t,x)\rightarrow u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\quad\forall\lambda>0.

Indeed, one can verify that the
L^{2}
norm remains invariant under the above scaling transformation.
Strichartz estimates enable us to derive the local existence of
(
1.1
)
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s\geqslant 0
. For further details regarding the local theory of NLS on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, please refer to Cazenave
[
6
]
.

The fundamental work on local well-posedness for the NLS on the torus was initiated by Bourgain
[
2
]
. In this pioneered paper, he first established periodic Strichartz estimates for the linear Schrödinger equation through analytical number theory. These Strichartz estimates are optimal on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Subsequently, the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
for
s>0
was derived using these estimates. For the semi-periodic case, i.e.
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
, Tzvetkov-Takaoka
[
25
]
demonstrated the
L_{t,z}^{4}
Strichartz estimate for frequency-localized initial data without any loss of derivatives. Using this Strichartz estimate, they established the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
with
s\geq 0
.

According to the local well-posedness theory and conservation of mass and energy, the global well-posedness of (
1.1
) in energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
is quite direct. Therefore, the global well-posedness in
H^{s}
with
s<1
is more interesting since the equation is
L^{2}
invariant in the Euclidean space. We also expect the critical regularity is
L^{2}
in the semi-periodic setting. For the case of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, Bourgain first developed the Fourier truncation method to prove the
(
1.1
)
is globally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
s>\frac{2}{3}
. Colliander-Keel-Staffilani-Takaoka-Tao
[
9
]
initially exploited the I-method to obtain the global well-posedness for
(
1.1
)
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
s>\frac{4}{7}
. By taking advantage of the resonant decomposition and
I
-method, they further improved the result of
[
9
]
to
s>\frac{1}{2}
in
[
11
]
. In 2016, Dodson
[
15
]
established the long-time Strichartz estimates and employed the concentration-compactness argument developed by Kenig-Merle
[
19
]
to prove the global well-posedness and scattering in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. For the case of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, De Silva-Pavlović-Staffilani-Tzirakis
[
13
]
proved the global well-posedness of the cubic NLS in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
with
s>\frac{2}{3}
by using I-method in conjunction with refined bilinear Strichartz estimates on rescaled tori
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{2}=(\lambda\mathbb{T})^{2}
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\eta(t)U_{\lambda}\phi_{1}\cdot\eta(t)U_{\lambda}\phi_{2}\bigg\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{2})}\lesssim N_{2}^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{N_{2}}{N_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\phi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{2})}\|\phi_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{2})},

where

,

\displaystyle U_{\lambda}(t)u_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{k\in\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{2\pi ik\cdot x-4\pi it\left|k\right|^{2}}\hat{u}_{0}(k).

Recently, Deng-Fan-Yan-Zhao-Zheng

Theorem 1.1
.

The solution to (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
with
s>\frac{1}{2}
. Moreover, we also have the polynomial growth of Sobolev norms

\|u(T)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim T^{\frac{s(1-s)}{4s-2}+},\quad\forall T>1.

Remark 1.2
.

The polynomial growth of the higher-order Sobolev norms is another interesting problem, which is tightly linked to the weak turbulence theory. Recently, Takaoka
[
24
]
applied the upside-down I-method as well as the resonant decomposition to obtain the upper bound of the growth of higher order Sobolev norms with
s>1
:

\|u(T)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim(1+T)^{\frac{s-1}{2}+},\quad\forall T>1.

A more interesting problem is to understand if there exists the growing-up solution. We refer to
[
12
]
for the case of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
[
7
]
for the case of quantum harmonic oscillator.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on rescaling argument and I-method developed in
[
9
]
. Since the
\mathbb{T}
-direction is not scale-invariant, all the analysis should be done in the rescaled manifold
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
. If
u
is a solution to
(
1.1
)
posed on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
, then
v=u^{\lambda}(t,x,y)=\lambda^{-1}u(\lambda^{-2}t,\lambda^{-1}x,\lambda^{{-1}}y)
solves the following equation

(1.3)

\begin{cases}iv_{t}+\Delta_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}v=|v|^{2}v,\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\\
v(0,x,y)=v_{0}(x,y):=\lambda^{-1}u_{0}(\lambda^{-1}x,\lambda^{{-1}}y),\end{cases}

where
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}:=\lambda\mathbb{T}
.
Let us describe the
I
-method, which consists in smoothing out the
H^{s}
initial data for
0<s<1
, in order to obtain a good local and global solution at
H^{1}

\mathcal{F}(Iu)(\xi):=m(\xi)\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi)
, where
\xi\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}_{1/\lambda}
with
\mathbb{Z}_{1/\lambda}:=\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}
and
m(\xi)
is a smooth cut off function

(1.4)

m(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,&|\xi|\leq N,\\
\big(\frac{N}{|\xi|}\big)^{1-s},&|\xi|\geq 2N.\end{cases}

Acting
I
-operator to both side of (
1.3

I
-operator

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}(Iv)+\Delta_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}Iv=I(|v|^{2}v),\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\\
Iv(0,x,y)=Iv_{0}(x,y):=\lambda^{-1}Iu_{0}(\lambda^{-1}x,\lambda^{{-1}}y).\end{cases}

Iu^{\lambda}
is not a solution to
(

will not be conserved. herefore, it is essential to estimate the increment of energy

over the time interval during which the local solution exists. This increment estimate is crucial for establishing the global well-posedness in
H^{s}
below the energy space. Recently, Deng-Fan-Yang-Zhao-Zheng
[
14

successfully removed the

Applying this refined bilinear estimate together with the I-method from

(1.6)

|E(Iu^{\lambda}(\delta))-E(Iu^{\lambda}(0))|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{-1+},

where
\lambda\sim N^{\frac{1-s}{s}}
,

and
\|Iu^{\lambda}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim 1
. As a direct consequence, they obtained the global well-posedness for
(
1.1

in

Let us outline the proof of Theorem
1.1

2.1.

Throughout this paper, we denote the Lebesgue space
L_{t}^{p}L_{z}^{q}(I\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{z}^{q}(I\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}=\bigg(\int_{I}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}|u(t,z)|^{q}dz\Big)^{\frac{p}{q}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

We define the Fourier transform on

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}f)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}f(z)e^{-iz\cdot\xi}dz,

(2.1)

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{F}f)(\xi)e^{iz\cdot\xi}d\mu.

For convenience, we denote that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}}f(\xi)d\xi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\mu,\eta)d\mu.

In the rest of the paper, we shall use the notation (

Moreover, we write the linear Schrödinger operator as the following

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(e^{it\Delta_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}}f)(\xi)=e^{-it|\xi|^{2}}(\mathcal{F}f)(\xi).

Now, we introduce the Littlewood-Paley projections. First, we denote a smooth cut-off function

\displaystyle\eta_{1}(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,&|\xi|\leq\frac{3}{4},\\

Let

\displaystyle P_{N}f=P_{\leq N}f-P_{\leq\frac{N}{2}}f.

For arbitrary

\displaystyle P_{\leq a}:=\sum_{N\leq a}P_{N}f,\quad P_{>a}f=\sum_{a<N}P_{N}f.

Scaling symmetry plays a crucial role in the global well-posedness of nonlinear Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

Now we give the definition of the Fourier transformation and Littlewood-Paley projection on the rescaled waveguide

We denote the Fourier transform on

3.1.

Indeed, Lemma

Proposition 3.3

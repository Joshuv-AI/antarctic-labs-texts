An Enriched Approach to the Strictification of ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -categories
Kimball Strong

Abstract
. We define a functor which takes in an ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -category and outputs an ( ω , 1 ) (\omega,1) -category, the natural maximally “strict” version of an ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -category. We do this by modeling ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -categories as categories enriched in ∞ \infty -groupoids, and then “locally strictifying” (applying the strictification of ∞ \infty -groupoids to each hom space) to obtain a category enriched in ω \omega -groupoids with respect to the Gray tensor product, followed by “globally strictifying” (strictifying the enrichment from the Gray tensor product to the cartesian product) to obtain a category cartesian-enriched in ω \omega -groupoids, which is equivalently an ( ω , 1 ) (\omega,1) -category. We conjecture that this functor is conservative, and prove this for two dual special cases: 2 2 -truncated and 2 2 -connected ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -categories. Along the way, we construct a sort of “incoherent walking ( ω , 1 ) (\omega,1) -equivalence,” which gives a simpler description of the coherent path lifting condition for fibrations of ( ω , 1 ) (\omega,1) -categories, only involving cells of dimension ≤ 3 \leq 3 .



1.
Introduction

The singular homology of a space is one of the most useful invariants of algebraic topology: it is strong enough to distinguish many spaces from each other, yet frequently very computable.
Part of its utility comes from the Homological Whitehead Theorem, which in its generalization to non-simply connected spaces states:

Theorem 1.1

(Generalized Homological Whitehead Theorem)
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a map of spaces. If

•

f
induces an isomorphism on
\pi_{0}
, and

•

for any choice of basepoint
x_{0}
,
f
induces an isomorphism
\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})\to\pi_{1}(Y,f(x_{0}))
, and

•

for any choice of basepoint, the induced map of universal covers
\widehat{f}:\widehat{X}_{x_{0}}\to\widehat{Y}_{f(x_{0})}
induces an isomorphism on homology,

then
f
is a weak equivalence.

Originally due to Whitehead, this theorem was reinterpreted due to work of Brown and Higgins: they defined objects they called
\infty
-groupoids, later called strict
\infty
-groupoids, or as we shall refer to them
\omega
-groupoids

[
11
]

As the name suggests,
\omega
-groupoids are a “strict” version of Grothendieck’s idea of
\infty
-groupoids, in the sense that whereas
\infty
-groupoids are to be thought as infinite dimensional groupoids in which all algebraic laws hold only up to coherent higher homotopy,
\omega
-groupoids are infinite dimensional groupoids in which all algebraic laws hold up to equality, e.g. composition of
n
-morphisms is genuinely associative.

\omega
-groupoids have natural notion of homotopy group, analagous to the automorphism group of a groupoid. This gives them a natural notion of weak equivalence, which can be extended to the structure of a model category
[
10
]
.
Brown and Higgins define a functor
\Pi_{\omega}:\text{Top}\to\omega\text{Gpd}
, called the “fundamental
\omega
-groupoid” functor, and show that for a space

there are natural isomorphisms

\pi_{0}(\omega(X))\cong\pi_{0}(X)\quad\pi_{1}(\omega(X),x_{0})\cong\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})\quad\pi_{n}(\omega(X),x_{0})\cong\pi_{n}(\widehat{X}_{x_{0}},x_{0})

where again
\widehat{X}_{x_{0}}
is the universal cover of
X
at
x_{0}
. From this it follows that Theorem
1.1
can be equivalently phrased as

Theorem 1.2

(
[
11
]

[
33
]
)
.

The functor

is conservative; i.e. reflects weak equivalences.

By Grothendieck’s Homotopy Hypothesis, we think of this as a functor
\text{St}_{0}:\infty\text{Gpd}\to\omega\text{Gpd}
, and interpret it as the “strictification” of an
\infty
-groupoid.
This functor is a left adjoint to a natural inclusion
\iota_{0}:\omega\text{Gpd}\hookrightarrow\infty\text{Gpd}
.
So, another rephrasing of the same theorem is that
strictification of
\infty
-groupoids is conservative
.

Just as
\infty
-groupoids are a special case of
(\infty,n)
-categories,
\omega
-groupoids are themselves a special case of objects called
(\omega,n)
-categories, which can be thought of as strict versions of
(\infty,n)
-categories (Definition

Thus, we should expect for any

.
Optimistically, we might hope that just as the functor

captures a large amount of information about spaces despite

-groupoids being inherently algebraic,
\text{St}_{n}
may also manage to provide a relatively strong invariant for
(\infty,n)
-categories despite
(\omega,n)
-categories being both fully algebraic and free from the subtleties of homotopy coherence that make
(\infty,n)
-categories so tricky to understand.

In this paper we will verify a small piece of this optimism by giving an enriched construction of
\text{St}_{1}
and proving that it is conservative in some special cases. Imprecisely, we shall prove:

Theorem A
.

There is a functor
\text{St}_{1}:(\infty,1)\text{Cat}\to(\omega,1)\text{Cat}
, left adjoint to a natural inclusion functor. If
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are
(\infty,1)
-categories that are either:

•

2
-truncated, i.e. are
(2,1)
-categories

•

2

\infty

then a functor

of

\text{St}_{1}(F):\text{St}_{1}\mathcal{C}\to\text{St}_{1}\mathcal{D}
is a weak equivalence.

Additionally, we prove the natural generalization of the frequently useful fact that a CW-complex

is contractible if and only if it has vanishing fundamental group and homology (equivalently, if

is contractible):

For

is contractible (equivalent to the terminal

The precise statement of Theorem

and
5.17
,
and the precise statement of Theorem

The

-truncated case fits into a general pattern of weak

-categories being modelled via strict
2
-categories, whereas the proof of the
2
-connected case turns out to depend on an identification of

From this evidence, we conjecture that in general,

is conservative:

Conjecture
.

The functor
\text{St}_{1}:(\infty,1)\text{Cat}\to(\omega,1)\text{Cat}
is conservative.

In light of the comparison with the
(\infty,0)
case, we would regard a positive answer to this conjecture as a sort of “Homological Whitehead Theorem for
(\infty,1)

Our approach is based on splitting the functor
\text{St}_{1}
into two parts as follows:

(\infty,1)
-categories can be thought of as categories weakly enriched in
\infty
-groupoids.
By strictifying every hom
\infty
-groupoid, we obtain a category enriched in
\omega
-groupoids.
However, this enrichment is with respect to a “weak” product (the Gray tensor product), and so we must further strictify the
enrichment
to obtain a category enriched in
\omega
-groupoids (with respect to the cartesian product), which is equivalently an
(\omega,1)
-category.
If we think of strictification as a “linearization,” then this composite
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\infty,1)\mathcal{Cat}\cong\infty\text{Grpd}\text{-}\mathcal{Cat}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega\text{Gpd}^{\text{weak}}\text{-}\mathcal{Cat}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega\text{Gpd}^{\text{strict}}\text{-}\mathcal{Cat}\cong(\omega,1)\mathcal{Cat}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{St}^{\text{local}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{St}^{\text{global}}}

we think of as first “locally linearizing” followed by “globally linearizing.”
This interpretation of strictification as a “linearization” is inspired by the tight connection between
\omega
-groupoids and chain complexes: any space with the homotopy type of an
\omega
-groupoid has universal cover a product of Eilenberg-Maclane spaces, equivalently the geometric realization of a chain complex
[
2
]
.
It is not known whether this precisely characterizes such spaces, but Ehlers and Porter showed that the homotopy types modeled by
\omega
-groupoids can be characterized as variety within simplicial groupoids by the vanishing of words connected to the Whitehead bracket, the fundamental non-linear data of unstable homotopy theory
[
19
]
.

1

1

A great deal of work on this has also been done in
[
15
]
by Carrasco and Cegarra, who constructed gadgets called “hypercrossed complexes” which (roughly) generalize
\omega
-groupoids by equipping them with various dimension-increasing binary operations, which give a sort of non-linear enhancement to an
\omega
-groupoid.
They show that these have a homotopy theory equivalent to the homotopy theory of
\infty
-groupoids, which characterizes the homotopy types modeled by
\omega
-groupoids as exactly those which can be modeled by a hypercrossed complex in which the binary operations are trivial, i.e. the “linear” objects.

Furthermore, the explicit description of
\text{St}^{\text{global}}
in the appendix of this paper can be summarized as “quotienting out by all non-linear (i.e. quadratic and higher) terms,” and the special case of this description that we leverage in the
2
-connected case of the proof of Theorem
A
coincides with the functor of indecomposables of an augmented DGA. This gives a looser connection from
\text{St}^{\text{global}}
to Quillen homology and cotangent complexes, a more stably-minded “linearization.”

To prove that this composite functor
\text{St}^{\text{global}}\circ\text{St}^{\text{local}}
is conservative, it suffices to prove that each individual functor is conservative.
The first functor
\text{St}^{\text{local}}
is conservative because strictification of
\infty
-groupoids is conservative, and equivalences of
(\infty,1)
-categories are detected locally.
We do not succed in proving that
\text{St}^{\text{global}}
is conservative except in the special cases noted above; the major difficulty is its nonlocal nature: the hom
\omega
-groupoid
\text{St}^{\text{global}}(\mathcal{C})[x,y]
depends not just on
\mathcal{C}[x,y]
but on every composition map
\mathcal{C}[x,z]\otimes\mathcal{C}[z,y]\to\mathcal{C}[x,y]
.

Concretely, the adjunction which
\text{St}^{\text{global}}
is the left adjoint of is a change of enrichment adjunction along an adjunction with lax monoidal right adjoint: namely, the identity adjunction
\text{Id}:\omega\text{Gpd}^{\otimes}\rightleftarrows\omega\text{Gpd}^{\times}:\text{Id}
between the Gray monoidal structure and the cartesian monoidal structure on
\omega\text{Gpd}
.
This adjunction is not strong monoidal, nor even Quillen monoidal, the homotopical analog.
We therefore have few general tools with which to analyze the induced change of enrichment adjunction.
In future work, we hope to complete the proof of our conservativity conjecture by analyzing a similar adjunction involving categories enriched in chain complexes over a varying groupoid of operators.

We would like to note that Simon Henry and Felix Loubaton have constructed a different sort of strictification functor for
(\infty,n)
-categories and conjectured their functor is conservative
[
22
]
. Their approach contrasts with ours in that they do not use an enriched approach, and they do not produce the strictest possible version of
(\infty,n)
-categories, in which all axioms hold strictly; instead they produce a version in which all axioms are strict except invertibility.

We also note that Chanavat and Hadzihasanovic have recently constructed what they call a “semi-strictification” of
(\infty,n)
-categories. Their meaning by “strictification” is of a different sort than either our meaning or Loubaton and Henry. Their goal is to find algebraic models for
(\infty,n)
-categories which are equivalent to the fully weak varieties (in particular, for
n=1
equivalent to
(\infty,1)
-categories) but have some amount of strictness
[
16
]
.
By contrast, our meaning of “strictification” is a destructive operation intended to give a somewhat computable algebraic invariant.

Finally, we should note that Burke in
[
14
]
considers Quillen adjunctions between simplicial monoids, monoids in
\omega\text{Gpd}
with respect to the Gray tensor product, and monoids in
\omega\text{Gpd}
with respect to the cartesian product. Since monoids are equivalent to one-object categories, our work reproduces some of the work there.

5.
Conservativity: Two Special Cases

Our primary interest with strict higher categories is to study how much information is preserved by the process of strictification.
In analogy with the strictification of
\infty
-groupoids as a nonabelian enhancement of homology, we would hope to prove theorems about the strictification of
(\infty,1)
-categories analagous to theorems about homology. The following theorem is a straightforward consequence of the classical theorem for spaces:

Theorem 5.1
.

\text{St}^{\text{loc}}
is conservative.

Proof.

Since
\text{St}^{\text{loc}}
preserves the homotopy category (by commutativity of Diagram
3

Remark
.

One natural question that one might to ask is whether the strictification of the
(\infty,1)
-category of
\infty
-groupoids is the
(\omega,1)
-category of
\omega
-groupoids, or equivalently the
(\omega,1)
-category of crossed complexes.
This would seem to be analagous to how the stabilization of the
(\infty,1)
-category of spaces is the stable
(\infty,1)
-category of “stable spaces,” i.e. spectra.
But this cannot be the case because
\text{St}_{1}
preserves homotopy categories, and the homotopy category of spaces is distinct from the homotopy category of
\omega
-groupoids (or at least, the natural comparison map is not an equivalence).

Of course, the comparison with spectra is not quite right - the operation of stabilization of an
(\infty,1)
-category is an operation internal to a particular bicomplete
(\infty,1)
-category, whereas
\text{St}_{1}
appears to be somewhat blind to the internal category theory.
Perhaps a better analogy with
\text{St}_{1}
would be to take spectrum objects in the
(\infty,1)
-category of
(\infty,1)
-categories. Harpaz, Nuiten and Prasma showed in
[
21
]
that such objects are not stable
(\infty,1)
-categories but rather themselves spectra.

Another similar phenomenon is in recent work of Chatzitheodoridis
[
17
]
, in which model structures for rational
(\infty,1)
-categories are constructed as localizations of model structures for
(\infty,1)
-categories. In particular, for any
(\infty,1)
-category
\mathcal{C}
(incarnated concretely as a complete Segal space) it has a localization in this model structure, whose hom-spaces will be the ordinary rationalizations of the hom-spaces of
\mathcal{C}
.
This operation therefore extends “rationalization” as a process from
\infty
-groupoids to
(\infty,1)
-categories, paralleling the manner that
\text{St}_{1}
extends “strictification” from
\infty
-groupoids to
(\infty,1)
-categories.
There too one could ask the natural question of whether the rationalization of the
(\infty,1)
-category of spaces coincides with the rational
(\infty,1)
-category of rational spaces. There too, the answer seems to be negative, for similar reasons to ours.

We can now prove that

.

We wish to prove that the set of transfinite compositions of pushouts with respect to the generating set

consists of weak equivalences. Since every such

\mathcal{C}[a,r_{1}]\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{C}[r_{n},b]\to\mathcal{C}[a,r_{1}]\times\cdots\times\mathcal{C}[r_{n},b]
. We write

a^{-1}a^{\prime}=k_{1}\cdots k_{m}

and then note that this gives us that

\displaystyle\circ(a\otimes s(b))^{-1}\circ(a^{\prime}\otimes s(b))

\displaystyle=\circ(a^{-1}a^{\prime}\otimes s(b))

\displaystyle=\circ(k_{1}\cdots k_{m}\otimes s(b))

\displaystyle=\circ(k_{1}\otimes s(b))\cdots\circ(k_{m}\otimes s(b))

By our description of the kernels in terms of the degrees, we have that
\pi(k_{i}\otimes s(b))=0
, and hence by definition
\circ(k_{i}\otimes s(b))
is
0
in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}[a,c]
, as desired. We can symmetrically argue that this is independent of the choice of representative
b
.

Description of the functor on morphisms:

Given a morphism
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
in
\text{CrCom}^{\otimes}\text{-}\mathcal{Cat}
, we define
\underline{F}
to be the same as
F
on objects, and on hom-objects induced by the description of the hom-objects of
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
and
\underline{\mathcal{D}}
as quotients.
Here we are using that since
F
is a functor between enriched categories, it preserves compositions, and so it maps the kernel of the map
\mathcal{C}[a,b]_{i}\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}[a,b]_{i}
into the kernel of the map
\mathcal{D}[Fa,Fb]_{i}\to\underline{\mathcal{D}}[Fa,Fb]_{i}
.

Proof that this defines an adjoint:

Note that the functor
U^{\text{glo}}
is fully faithful, which is fairly straightforward from its definition as the identity on hom-objects.
So to show that
(\underline{-})
defines an adjoint to
U^{\text{glo}}
, it suffices to show that for
\mathcal{C}\in\text{CrCom}^{\otimes}\text{-}\mathcal{Cat}
, the map
\mathcal{C}\to\underline{\mathcal{C}}
is initial among maps
\mathcal{C}\to U^{\text{glo}}(\mathcal{D})
.
Since this map is a quotient on hom-objects, it suffices to show that the elements we quotient by are sent to identities in
U^{\text{glo}}(\mathcal{D})
, and that the induced maps on hom-objects assemble to form a functor of enriched categories; i.e. they commute with composition. The first statement follows immediately from the descriptions in terms of dimensions. The second statement follows from observing that the map
C\otimes D\to C\times D
sends
c_{m}\otimes d_{0}\cdot c_{0}\otimes d_{n}
to
(c_{m},\text{id}_{d_{0}})\cdot(\text{id}_{c_{0}},d_{n})=(c_{m},d_{n})
.

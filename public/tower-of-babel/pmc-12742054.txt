NX-1607: lifting the Cbl-b brake to promote T cell activation through MAPK/ERK pathways
Authors: Alex To, Ryohichi Sugimura
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCasitas B lymphoma-b (Cbl-b) is an E3 ubiquitin ligase that serves as a critical brake on immune cell activations. It negatively fine-tunes immune responses through ubiquitination of signaling proteins. Cbl-b deficiency in mice leads to spontaneous tumor rejection and induces only mild, non-lethal autoimmunity, highlighting its potential as a promising immunotherapy target. NX-1607 is the first oral small molecule inhibitor of Cbl-b and it is currently in phase I clinical trial. A recent publication employed high-throughput drug combination screening to systematically elucidate the molecular pathways by which NX-1607 enhances T cell activation. The authors administered 81 well-characterized inhibitors to Jurkat T cells under CD3-stimulated and unstimulated conditions in the presence of NX-1607. Inhibitors of the MAPK/ERK pathway significantly attenuated NX-1607-enhanced T cell activation as indicated by CD69 markers. Proto-oncogene tyrosine-protein kinase Src (SRC) family kinase inhibitors also reduced both CD69 activation markers and the phosphorylation of PLCγ1 and HCLS1 which are upstream of MAPK/ERK pathways. These findings were validated in an immunocompetent B-cell lymphoma mouse model, where NX-1607 consistently upregulated PLCγ1 and ERK phosphorylation. Together, this work delineates a PLCγ1-MAPK/ERK axis through which Cbl-b inhibition unleashes T cell activation, providing critical insight for the development of NX-1607 based immunotherapy.Keywords: T cell

Cbl-b is a negative regulator of immune cell activation

Casitas B lymphoma-b (Cbl-b) is an E3 ubiquitin ligase that plays an intracellular checkpoint in the negative regulation of the immune response. Cbl-b is widely expressed across multiple immune cell types including T cells, natural killer (NK) cells, B cells, dendritic cells, and myeloid cells. It functions as a molecular brake, setting the activation threshold during immune response.1 Mechanistically, Cbl-b facilitates the ubiquitination and subsequent degradation of key signaling proteins involved in immune cell activation, thereby fine-tuning immune responses and maintaining homeostasis.1 Cbl-b deficient mice can spontaneously reject tumors while developing non-lethal autoimmunity.

Cbl-b is primarily known for its role in T cells. It restrains T cell receptor (TCR) activation by enforcing CD28 dependence during T-cell activation.2 CD28 costimulation releases the Cbl-b inhibitory effects, thereby unleashing TCR downstream signaling proteins such as Zap70 and PLCγ1 to drive immune activation.2 This prevents the hyperactivation of T cells in normal T cell activation. It restrains other immune cell activations in a similar way.

Cbl-b facilitates the ubiquitination of its target proteins. It comprises a tyrosine kinase binding (TKB) domain and a RING (Really Interesting New Gene) finger domain. The TKB domain binds to target proteins such as Zap70, while the RING finger domain recruits E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes that mediate the ubiquitination process. Cbl-b does not exclusively depend on degradation mechanisms to inhibit the functions of signaling proteins. For example, the ubiquitination of the P85 subunit of Phosphoinositide 3-kinases (PI3K) in T cells primarily prevents PI3K from being recruited to CD28, thereby suppressing TCR signaling. The fate of each substrate can vary.

Cbl-b serves as a negative regulator of other immune cell activation as well. In NK cells, Cbl-b mediates the ubiquitination of TAM receptors signals (also known as Tyrosine-protein kinase receptor TYRO3, receptor tyrosine kinase AXL, and Myeloid-epithelial-reproductive tyrosine kinase MerTK) and Linker for Activation of T cells (LAT) to suppress NK cell activation.3 NX-1605 can rescue NK exhaustion and synergize with T-cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains (TIGIT) NK checkpoint treatment.3 Similarly, in B cells, Cbl-b inhibits the downstream of B cell receptor (BCR) signaling like PLCγ2, Vav, and tyrosine kinase Syk by mediating their degradation.4

Targeting negative regulators of immune cell activity, like the Cbl-b inhibitor NX-1607, represents an emerging approach to target complementary pathways that could further enhance the well-established immune checkpoint inhibition strategy. For example, LAG3 is a negative regulator of T cell proliferation and effector function. In a recent phase 2/3 clinical trial, it shows that LAG3 inhibitor (relatlimab) offers greater benefit when administered in combination with anti-Programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) (nivolumab) therapy in melanoma.5

Cbl-b is a promising target to enhance T-cell activation

T cells can recognize tumor antigens but are often suppressed in the tumor microenvironment (TME). Chronic tumor antigen interaction with the TCR will initiate a negative feedback mechanism to inhibit T-cell activation, such as by upregulating PD-1 expression. Immunosuppressive signals from the TME, such as Programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) immune checkpoints, glucose availability, and tumor-associated myeloid cells, lead to T cell exhaustion.6

Cbl-b is considered a promising target to wake up these suppressed T cells. Inhibition of Cbl-b can reduce the activation threshold of immune cells, which are typically suppressed within the TME. Furthermore, Cbl-b inhibition exhibits limited toxicity, as its deficiency does not induce lethal autoimmunity or cytokine storms in mice, unlike Cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) deficiency.

Gene Related to Anergy in Lymphocytes (GRAIL) and itchy E3 ubiquitin protein ligase (ITCH) are the other two E3 ubiquitin ligases involved in negative regulation of T cell activation. Compared with Cbl-b, GRAIL has a narrower coverage as it acts more downstream of Cbl-b by inhibiting RhoA and cytokine gene transcriptions like Interleukin-2 (IL-2) and Interferon Gamma (IFNg).7 ITCH facilitates immune signal ubiquitination but is also linked to cancer development.8 Itch deficiency in mice would develop lethal systemic autoimmune disease.8 Overall, Cbl-b inhibition demonstrates more consistent efficacy with manageable toxicity.

Multiple platforms targeting Cbl-b E3 ligase activity have been developed. NX-1607 is a first-in-class, orally administered small molecule that targets Cbl-b and is currently in phase I clinical trial.9 A co-crystal structure shows that NX-1607 analog (C7683) acts as an intramolecular glue that binds to the tyrosine kinase binding domain (TKBD) and the flexible linker helix region of Cbl-b. This confines Cbl-b in an inactive state where its RING finger interaction cannot engage with the UbcH5B-Ub (E2-Ub) complex, even when proto-oncogene tyrosine-protein kinase Src (SRC) kinase substrate is present.1 Cbl-b regulates several signaling proteins, such as PLCγ1, VAV1, ZAP-70, and LAT, during immune response. The significance of these signaling proteins may vary in the context of NX-1607-enhanced T cell activation. The molecular mechanism by which NX-1607 boosts T cell activity has not been systematically determined yet.

NX-1607 activates MAPK/ERK pathways in Jurkat T cells

A recent publication conducted a drug combination screening to identify molecular pathways that boost T cell activation. Jurkat T cells were treated with NX-1607 in combination with various compounds, with or without CD3 for TCR activation. A total of 81 well-characterized compounds targeting different signaling pathways (such as cell cycle, kinases, and TCR signaling) were tested.10 NX-1607 alone increases CD69 activation marker both with and without CD3 engagement.

They calculated the drug response score by measuring how the compound synergizes or antagonizes NX-1607 to regulate CD69 activation markers. MAPK/ERK pathway inhibitors (Raf, MERK1/2, ERK1/2) and SRC inhibitors most significantly reduce the NX-1607 induced CD69 expression in Jurkat T cells. PKCθ and BTK inhibitors partially decreased CD69 expression, whereas CDK4/6 inhibitors and multikinase inhibitors targeting FLT3, c-Kit, FGFRs, VEGFRs, and FDGFRs increased CD69 expression. Consistently, MAPK/ERK and Src inhibitors can abrogate the NX-1607 enhancement on CD25 activation markers as well.

They conducted western blot analysis to detect NX-1607 activated pathways. Jurkat T cells exhibit hyperphosphorylation of PLCγ1 and increased calcium signaling, which allows their activation by NX-1607 without CD3 stimulation. ERK1/2 and MERK1/2 showed increased phosphorylation when treated with NX-1607, both with and without CD3 stimulation. PKCθ and IKKα/β were phosphorylated, but IκBα was not. VAV1 and AKT exhibited increased phosphorylation, while MKK3, P38, S6K, and JNK had minimal changes in their phosphorylation levels. Overall, MAPK/ERK is the most activated pathway, whereas other pathways are either partially or minimally activated.

NX-1607 activates T cells through the SRC-PLCγ1-ERK axis

The authors investigate which pathways NX-1607 depends on to enhance T cell activation. Inhibition of MAPK/ERK pathways and knockout of MAPK3/1 (ERK1/2) do not affect the phosphorylation of PLCγ1 and HCLS1 but result in a decreased CD69 expression level in Jurkat T cells. However, SRC inhibitors notably decrease PLCγ1, HCLS1, ERK1/2, and MEK1/2 phosphorylation while lowering IL2 and IFNG messenger RNA (mRNA) expressions. SRC inhibitors also reduced the mRNA expressions of IL2 and IFNG. This establishes that Src kinases operate upstream to regulate Cbl-b activity, whereas the MAPK/ERK pathways function downstream and are regulated by Cbl-b activity.

The authors further tested the activation of T cells by NX-1607 in vivo. They administered NX-1607 to syngeneic immunocompetent mice bearing A20 xenograft tumors of B-cell lymphoma for 14 days. They observed increased levels of phosphorylated PLCγ1/2 and ERK1/2 in circulating mouse CD3 T cells and increased T cell infiltration into the tumors. Mouse survival rates improved, with no significant toxicity symptoms or weight loss. This concludes that NX-1607 enhances antitumor effects in vivo by activating PLCγ1-MAPK/ERK pathways.

Future directions

This work has systematically decoded the pathway responses involved in NX-1607-enhanced T cell activation and highlighted the importance of MAPK/ERK pathways to enhance T cell activation. Solid tumors still face challenges due to an insufficient understanding of the TMEs. Future research could investigate how NX-1607-enhanced immune cells can reprogram the TME using spatial omics technologies. Furthermore, not all patients respond to anti-PD-1 checkpoint immunotherapy for solid tumors. It would be valuable to understand the role of NX-1607 in non-responsive patients and whether it can serve as an alternative treatment or sensitize non-responders to anti-PD-1 and even chimeric antigen receptor T cell therapies. Since cbl-b inhibitors lower T cell activation thresholds for antitumor treatment, it will be valuable to explore if cbl-b activation can alleviate autoimmune diseases.

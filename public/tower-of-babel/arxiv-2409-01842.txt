Stable standing waves for Nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson system with a doping profile Thanks: * Corresponding author
Mathieu Colin, Tatsuya Watanabe ∗

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the study of the nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson system with a doping profile. We are interested in the existence of stable standing waves by considering the associated L 2 L^{2} -minimization problem. The presence of a doping profile causes a difficulty in the proof of the strict sub-additivity. A key ingredient is to establish the strict sub-additivity by adapting a scaling argument, which is inspired by [ 32 ] . When the doping profile is a characteristic function supported on a bounded smooth domain, smallness of some geometric quantity related to the domain ensures the existence of stable standing waves.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the following nonlinear
Schrödinger-Poisson system:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}&-\Delta u+\omega u+e\phi u=|u|^{p-1}u\\
&-\Delta\phi=\frac{e}{2}\left(|u|^{2}-\rho(x)\right)\end{cases}\quad\hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},

where
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
,
e>0
and
1<p<\frac{7}{3}
.
Equation (
1.1
) appears as a stationary problem for the
time-dependent nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson system:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}i\psi_{t}+\Delta\psi-e\phi\psi+|\psi|^{p-1}\psi=0\quad\hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta\phi=\frac{e}{2}\big(|\psi|^{2}-\rho(x)\big)\quad\hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}.\end{cases}

Indeed when we look for a standing wave of the form:
\psi(t,x)=e^{i\omega t}u(x)
,
we are led to the elliptic problem (
1.1
).
In this paper, we are interested in the existence of stable standing waves
for (
1.2
) by considering the solvability of the associated

L^{2}
-constraint minimization problem.

The Schrödinger-Poisson system appears
in various fields of physics, such as quantum mechanics,
black holes in gravitation and plasma physics.
Especially, the Schrödinger-Poisson system plays an important role
in the study of semi-conductor theory; see
[
21
,
25
,
27
]
,
and then the function
\rho(x)
is referred as
impurities

or a
doping profile
.
The doping profile comes from
the difference of the number densities of positively charged donor ions
and negatively charged acceptor ions,
and the most typical examples are characteristic functions, step functions
or Gaussian functions.
Equation (
1.1
) also appears as a stationary problem
for the Maxwell-Schrödinger system.
We refer to
[
6
,
13
,
14
]
for the physical background
and the stability result of standing waves for the Maxwell-Schrödinger system.
In this case, the constant
e
describes the strength of the interaction
between a particle and an external electromagnetic field.

The nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson system with
\rho\equiv 0
:

(1.3)

\begin{cases}&-\Delta u+\omega u+e\phi u=|u|^{p-1}u\\
&-\Delta\phi=\frac{e}{2}|u|^{2}\end{cases}\quad\hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}

has been studied widely in the last two decades.
Especially, the existence of
L^{2}
-constraint minimizers
depending on
p
and the size of the mass,
the existence of ground state solutions of (
1.3
)
and their stability have been investigated in detail;
see
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
10
,
13
,
19
,
20
,
22
,
26
,
28
,
29
,
31
]

and references therein.
On the other hand, the nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson system with
a doping profile is less studied.
In
[
16
,
17
]
, the corresponding 1D problem has been considered.
Moreover, the linear Schrödinger-Poisson system
(that is, the problem (
1.1
) without
|u|^{p-1}u
)
with a doping profile in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
has been studied in
[
7
,
8
]
.
As far as we know, there is no literature
concerning with (
1.1
) and the existence of stable standing waves,
which is exactly the motivation of this paper.

To state our main results, let us give some notation.
For
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
,
the energy functional associated with (
1.1
) is given by

\displaystyle I(u)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(u)+\frac{\omega}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}\,dx,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}\,dx+e^{2}A(u).

Here we denote the nonlocal term by
S(u)=S_{1}(u)+S_{2}
with

\displaystyle S_{1}(u)(x)

\displaystyle:=(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{8\pi|x|}*|u|^{2},

\displaystyle S_{2}(x)

\displaystyle:=(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(\frac{-\rho}{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{8\pi|x|}*\rho(x),

and the functional corresponding to the nonlocal term by

A(u):=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S(u)\big(|u|^{2}-\rho(x)\big)\,dx=\frac{1}{32\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\big(|u(x)|^{2}-\rho(x)\big)\big(|u(y)|^{2}-\rho(y)\big)}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy.

For
\mu>0
, let us consider the minimization problem:

(1.5)

\mathcal{C}(\mu)=\inf_{u\in B(\mu)}\mathcal{E}(u),

where
B(\mu)=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\ ;\ \|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=\mu\}
.
We also define the set of minimizers by

\mathcal{M}(\mu):=\{e^{i\theta}u(x)\ ;\ \theta\in[0,2\pi),u\in B(\mu),\ \mathcal{E}(u)=\mathcal{C}(\mu)\}.

In this setting, the constant
\omega
in (
1.1
)
appears as a Lagrange multiplier.

Let us define the energy associated with (
1.3
):

E_{\infty}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}\,dx+\frac{e^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx.

Indeed if we assume
\rho(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
,
(
1.3
) can be seen as a problem at infinity.
We define the minimum energy associated with (
1.3
) by

0\geq c_{e,\infty}(\mu)=c_{\infty}(\mu):=\inf_{u\in B(\mu)}E_{\infty}(u).

The existence of minimizers for
c_{e,\infty}(\mu)
has been studied widely
and is summarized as follows.

(i)

In the case
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
,

c_{e,\infty}(\mu)
is attained if
c_{e,\infty}(\mu)<0
.
Moreover
c_{e,\infty}(\mu)<0
when

\mu
is large for fixed
e
or
e
is small for fixed
\mu
.

(ii)

In the case
p=2
,

c_{e,\infty}(\mu)
is attained if and only if
c_{e,\infty}(\mu)<0
.
Moreover
c_{e,\infty}(\mu)<0
when
e
is small for fixed
\mu
.

(iii)

In the case
1<p<2
,

c_{e,\infty}(\mu)<0
for all
\mu
and
e
.
Moreover
c_{e,\infty}(\mu)
is attained
when
\mu
is small for fixed
e
or
e
is small for fixed
\mu
.

For the proof, we refer to
[
4
,
5
,
10
,
13
,
19
,
20
,
22
,
29
]
.
Especially when
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
, for fixed
e>0
,
there exists
\mu^{*}=\mu^{*}(e)>0
such that

(1.6)

\mu^{*}=\inf\{\mu>0\mid c_{e,\infty}(\mu)<0\}.

More precise information of
\mu^{*}
has been obtained in e.g.
[
13
,
20
,
29
]
.
It is also worth pointing out that as
e
decreases,

\mu^{*}
increases as well.

For the doping profile
\rho
, we assume that

(1.7)

\rho(x)\in L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\ \text{for some}\ q>3\quad\mbox{and}\quad x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\in L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

(1.8)

\rho(x)\geq 0,\ \not\equiv 0\quad\text{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.9)

\text{there exist $\alpha>2$ and $C>0$ such that}\ \rho(x)\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{\alpha}}\quad\text{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.10)

\inf_{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C}),\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+2e^{2}S_{2}(x)|u|^{2}\right)dx=0.

The assumption (
1.9
) enables us to show that
S_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

and decays to
0
at infinity,
which makes us to treat
S_{2}(x)
as a trapping potential.
Typical examples are the Gaussian function
\rho(x)=\varepsilon e^{-\alpha|x|^{2}}

and
\rho(x)=\frac{\varepsilon}{(1+|x|)^{\alpha}}
for
\alpha>\frac{5}{2}
.
The assumption (
1.10
) means that a trapping effect caused by the doping profile
e^{2}S_{2}(x)

is too weak so that the operator
-\Delta+2e^{2}S_{2}(x)
does not have an eigenvalue,
which may happen if
e>0
is sufficiently small.
(
1.10
) will be used to establish the strict sub-additivity of
\mathcal{C}(\mu)
.

Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Existence of a minimizer)
.

Assume (
1.7
)-(
1.10
) and let
e>0
be fixed.
Suppose that
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
and let
\mu>2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}\mu^{*}
be given.
Then there exists
\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}(e,\mu)>0
such that
if
\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\rho_{0}
,
the minimization problem (
1.5
) admits a minimizer
u_{\mu}
.

Moreover the associated Lagrange multiplier
\omega=\omega(\mu)
is positive.

The assumption (
1.7
) rules out the case

\rho
is a characteristic function supported on a bounded smooth domain.
Even in this case, we are still able to obtain the existence of minimizers
under a smallness condition on some geometric quantity related to the domain;
See Section 6.

The positivity of the Lagrange multiplier
\omega(\mu)

will be useful to establish the relation between
L^{2}
-constraint minimizers
and ground state solutions,
which we leave for a future work.
We also refer to
[
15
,
18
]
for this direction.

The next result states the orbital stability of standing waves
corresponding to minimizers.

Theorem 1.2

(Orbital stability of standing wave)
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
1.1
,
the minimizer set
\mathcal{M}(\mu)

is orbitally stable in the following sense:
For every
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that
if an initial value
\psi_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}(\mu)}\|\psi_{0}-u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\delta
,
then the corresponding solution
(\psi,\phi)
of (
1.2
) satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}(\mu)}\left\{\big\|\psi(t,\cdot)-u(\cdot+y)\big\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\left\|\phi(t,\cdot)-\frac{e}{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}(|u(\cdot+y)|^{2}-\rho)\right\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right\}<\varepsilon.

Here
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})=\dot{H}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})

denotes the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})
with respect to
the norm:
\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx
.
As for the global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for (
1.2
),
see Section 4 below.

Here we briefly explain our strategy and its difficulty.
The existence of
L^{2}
-constraint minimizer can be shown
by applying the concentration compactness principle.
A key of the proof is to establish the
strict sub-additivity
:

(1.11)

\mathcal{C}(\mu)<\mathcal{C}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)+c_{\infty}\left(\mu-\mu^{\prime}\right)\quad\hbox{for all}\quad 0<\mu^{\prime}<\mu.

In the case
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
and
\rho\equiv 0
,
(
1.11
) can be readily obtained by adapting a suitable scaling.
However this scaling does not work straightforwardly
if a doping profile is present, because of the loss of spatial homogeneity.
In order to overcome this difficulty,
we perform a scaling argument inspired by
[
32
]
.
By imposing the smallness of
\rho
and
x\cdot\nabla\rho
,
it is possible to prove (
1.11
) if
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
.

Remark 1.3
.

(i)
It is natural to expect that whenever
c_{e,\infty}(\mu)
is attained
for given
\mu
and
e
,
then (
1.5
) admits a minimizer if

\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
is sufficiently small.
However if we fix
e>0
and take small
\mu>0
,

c_{e,\infty}(\mu)=0
and
c_{e,\infty}(\mu)
does not have a minimizer.
To obtain the existence of a minimizer
by imposing the smallness of
\rho
,
in the process of the proof of (
1.11
),
we need to construct a test function whose energy is negative uniformly in
\rho

even for small
\mu>0
.
For the moment, we don’t know whether such a good test function exists
for small
\mu>0
,
even though by (
1.8
), the presence of
\rho
decreases the energy level
of the truncated energy
E
defined in (
2.4
) below.
Furthermore, our proof of (
1.11
) fails when
1<p\leq 2
;
See Remark
3.5
below.

(ii)
We need to establish that

(1.12)

\mathcal{C}(\lambda\mu)<\lambda\mathcal{C}(\mu)\quad\text{fora all}\ \mu>0

to obtain (
1.11
).
When
\rho\equiv 0
and
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
,
(
1.12
) can be easily shown by a scaling argument.
However since the presence of
\rho(x)
loses this good scaling property,
a careful analysis is required.
We can find that (
1.12
) holds for any
\lambda>1
if
\mu
is large; see Lemma
3.4
.
On the other hand, it is not straightforward to prove (1.12) when
\mu>0
is small.
The assumptions (
1.10
) and
\mu>2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}\mu^{*}
are crucially used in Lemma
3.6

to prove (
1.12
) for small
\mu>0
.
Once (
1.12
) is established for small
\mu>0
,
we are able to obtain (
1.11
) no matter how
\mu^{\prime}>0
is small.

Furthermore if
p=2
, (
1.3
) is scaling invariant and hence (
1.12
) cannot be expected.
In fact, the closer
p
is to 2, the larger the mass required for (
1.12
) to hold.

We expect that our argument can be applied to other non-autonomous
L^{2}
-constraint minimization problems.

When
\rho
is a characteristic function,
further consideration is required
because
\rho
cannot be weakly differentiable.
In this case, a key of the proof is the
sharp boundary trace inequality

which was developed in
[
1
]
.
Then by imposing a smallness condition of some geometric quantity
related to the support of
\rho
,
we are able to obtain the existence of stable standing waves.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we introduce several properties of the energy functional
and some lemmas which will be used in this paper.
We establish the existence of a minimizer and prove Theorem
1.1

in Section 3.
Section 4 is devoted to the solvability of the Cauchy problem, while
the stability of standing waves will be investigated in Section 5.
In Section 6, we consider the case
\rho
is a characteristic function
and present the existence of standing waves for this case.
Finally in Section 7, we finish this paper
by providing a concluding remark and one open question.

Hereafter in this paper,
unless otherwise specified, we write
\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|u\|_{p}
.

2.
Variational formulation and preliminaries

The aim of this section is to prepare several properties of the energy functional
and present intermediate lemmas which will be used later on.

2.1.
Reduction to a single equation

First we observe that the energy functional defined in (
1.4
)
actually corresponds to the system (
1.1
).
Let us consider the functional of two variables,
which is associated with (
1.1
):

J(u,\phi):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\{|\nabla u|^{2}+\omega|u|^{2}+e\phi\left(|u|^{2}-\rho(x)\right)-|\nabla\phi|^{2}\right\}dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}\,dx,

for
(u,\phi)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\times D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})
.
A direct computation shows that the identity

\frac{\partial J}{\partial u}=0
is equivalent to the first equation of (
1.1
).
Moreover one finds that

\frac{\partial J}{\partial\phi}\psi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\{-\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi+\frac{e}{2}\left(|u|^{2}-\rho(x)\right)\psi\right\}dx\quad\hbox{for all}\ \psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}),

from which we deduce that

(2.1)

-\Delta\phi=\frac{e}{2}\left(|u|^{2}-\rho(x)\right).

Since
|u|^{2}-\rho\in L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=(L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{*}
,
by arguing similarly as in
[
6
]
,
the Poisson equation (
2.1
) has a unique solution:

\phi=eS(u)=\frac{e}{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(|u|^{2}-\rho(x)\right)\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}).

Moreover multiplying (
2.1
) by
\phi
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx=\frac{e}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi\left(|u|^{2}-\rho(x)\right)dx.

This implies that

\displaystyle J(u,eS(u))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\omega|u|^{2})\,dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}\,dx+\frac{e^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S(u)\left(|u|^{2}-\rho(x)\right)dx

\displaystyle=I(u).

2.2.
Decomposition of the energy

In this subsection, we rewrite the energy functional
\mathcal{E}

in a more convenient way.
First we decompose
\mathcal{E}
in the following way:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}\,dx,

\displaystyle+\frac{e^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{e^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{2}|u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{e^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}(u)\rho(x)\,dx-\frac{e^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{2}\rho(x)\,dx

and define four nonlocal terms:

\displaystyle A_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx,

(2.2)

\displaystyle A_{2}(u)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}(u)\rho(x)\,dx,

\displaystyle A_{2^{\prime}}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{2}|u|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{2}\rho(x)\,dx.

Note that
A_{0}
is independent of
u
.
One can also see that

A_{2}(u)=-\frac{1}{32\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}\rho(x)}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy=A_{2^{\prime}}(u).

Then we are able to write
\mathcal{E}
in the following form:

\mathcal{E}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}\,dx+e^{2}A_{1}(u)+2e^{2}A_{2}(u)+e^{2}A_{0}.

Recalling that

S_{1}(u)(x)=(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}\right)\geq 0,

we find that

(2.3)

A_{1}(u)\geq 0\quad\hbox{for all}\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C}).

Now it is convenient to put

\displaystyle E(u)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{E}(u)-e^{2}A_{0}

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\|u\|_{p+1}^{p+1}+e^{2}A_{1}(u)+2e^{2}A_{2}(u).

Since
A_{0}
is independent of
u
,
we have only to consider the minimization problem for
E
.

2.3.
Estimates of nonlocal terms

This subsection is devoted to present
estimates for the nonlocal terms of the functional
E
.
For later use, let us define

A_{3}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}(u)x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\,dx

which is well-defined for
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
.
Then we have the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
,

S_{1}
,
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
and
A_{3}
satisfy the estimates:

\displaystyle\|S_{1}(u)\|_{6}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla S_{1}(u)\|_{2}\leq C\|u\|_{{\frac{12}{5}}}^{2}\leq C\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle 0\leq A_{1}(u)

\displaystyle\leq C\|S_{1}(u)\|_{6}\|u\|_{\frac{12}{5}}^{2}\leq C\|u\|_{2}^{3}\|\nabla u\|_{2},

\displaystyle|A_{2}(u)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|S_{1}(u)\|_{6}\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\leq C\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\|u\|_{\frac{12}{5}}^{2}\leq C\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle|A_{3}(u)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|S_{1}(u)\|_{6}\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\leq C\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the proof of the inequality on
S_{1}(u)
, we refer to
[
26
]
.
The other estimates can be obtained by the Hödler inequality
and the Sobolev inequality.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{A_{1}(u_{n}-u)-A_{1}(u_{n})+A_{1}(u)\right\}=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{A_{2}(u_{n}-u)-A_{2}(u_{n})+A_{2}(u)\right\}=0.

Moreover if
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{\frac{12}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we also have

\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{1}(u_{n})=A_{1}(u)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{2}(u_{n})=A_{2}(u).

Proof.

The proof for
A_{1}
can be found in
[
31
, Lemma 2.2]
.
Since
\rho\in L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=(L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{*}
,
the property for
A_{2}
can be established in a similar way.
∎

2.4.
Scaling properties

In this subsection, we collect scaling properties of the nonlocal terms
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
.
For
a
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>0
,
let us adapt the scaling
u_{\lambda}(x):=\lambda^{a}u\left(\lambda^{b}x\right)
.
We first recall that

S_{1}(u)(x)=(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\,dy.

Putting
y=\lambda^{-b}z
, we have

\displaystyle S_{1}(u_{\lambda})(x)

(ii) Let

c(\mu)\leq\liminf_{h\to 0+}c(\mu-h)\leq\limsup_{h\to 0+}c(\mu-h)\leq c(\mu).

Next we claim that

From (i), we know that

Thus we obtain

(iii) It is known that

which completes the proof.

\lambda=\frac{\mu}{\mu^{\prime}}>1
, one finds that

c(\mu)=c\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu^{\prime}}\mu^{\prime}\right)<\frac{\mu}{\mu^{\prime}}c(\mu^{\prime})=\frac{\mu-\mu^{\prime}}{\mu^{\prime}}c\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu-\mu^{\prime}}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})\right)+c(\mu^{\prime}).

Next we use (i) with
s=\mu-\mu^{\prime}
and
\lambda=\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu-\mu^{\prime}}
to conclude that

c(\mu)<c(\mu-\mu^{\prime})+c(\mu^{\prime})\leq c(\mu-\mu^{\prime})+c_{\infty}(\mu^{\prime}).

This completes the proof.
∎

The next lemma deals with the compactness of any minimizing sequence for
(
3.1
).

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
.
For any
\mu>2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}\mu^{*}
, there exists

\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}(e,\mu)>0
such that
if
\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\leq\rho_{0}
,
the following properties hold.

Let
\{u_{j}\}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
be a sequence satisfying

\|u_{j}\|_{2}^{2}\to\mu
and
E(u_{j})\to c(\mu)
.
Then there exist a subsequence of
\{u_{j}\}
which is still denoted by the same,
a sequence
\{y_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
u_{\mu}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
such that

u_{j}(\cdot-y_{j})\to u_{\mu}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
and
E(u_{\mu})=c(\mu)
.

Lemma
3.7
seems to be rather standard,
once we have established the strict sub-additivity.
However since (
1.1
) is non-autonomous,
small and large masses play different roles,
causing that the proof of Lemma
3.7
is not straightforward.

Proof.

First we observe by the proof of Lemma
3.1

that
\|u_{j}\|_{H^{1}}
is bounded.
Moreover by replacing
u_{j}
by
\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\|u_{j}\|_{2}^{2}}u_{j}
,
we may assume that
\{u_{j}\}
is a minimizing sequence of
c(\mu)
.
It is also important to note that
c(\mu)<0
from (
3.3
).

Now we apply the concentration compactness principle
[
23
, Lemma I.1, p. 115]

to the sequence
\rho_{j}(x)=|u_{j}(x)|^{2}
.
It is well-known that the behavior of the sequence
(\rho_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

is governed by the three possibilities:
Compactness, Vanishing and Dichotomy.
Our goal is to show that Compactness occurs.

If Vanishing occurs, there exists a subsequence of
\{\rho_{j}\}
,
still denoted by
\{\rho_{j}\}
, such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{B_{R}(y)}\rho_{j}(x)\,dx=0\quad\hbox{for all}\ R>0.

Here
B_{R}(y)
describes a ball of radius
R
with the center at
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Then by
[
24
, Lemma I.1, P. 231]
, it follows that

u_{j}\to 0
in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for any
q\in(2,6)
.
On the other hand since
\{u_{j}\}
is a minimizing sequence for
c(\mu)
,
one has by Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle c(\mu)+o(1)

\displaystyle=E(u_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\|u_{j}\|_{p+1}^{p+1}+e^{2}A_{1}(u_{j})+2e^{2}A_{2}(u_{j})

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{p+1}\|u_{j}\|_{p+1}^{p+1}-C\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\|u_{j}\|_{\frac{12}{5}}^{2}.

Passing a limit
j\to\infty
, we get
0>c(\mu)\geq 0
.
This is a contradiction, which rules out Vanishing.

Next we assume that Dichotomy occurs.
Then by a standard argument (see
[
24
, Section I.2]
or

[
11
, Proposition 1.7.6, P. 23]
),
there exist
\mu^{\prime}\in(0,\mu)
,
R_{1}
,
R_{2}>0
,

\{y_{j}\}
,
\{z_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

and
\{u_{j,1}\}
,
\{u_{j,2}\}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
so that

\|u_{j,1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to\mu^{\prime},\quad\|u_{j,2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to\mu-\mu^{\prime},

(3.13)

{\rm supp}(u_{j,1})\subset B_{R_{1}}(y_{j}),\ {\rm supp}(u_{j,2})\subset B_{R_{2}}(z_{j}),\ |y_{j}-z_{j}|\to\infty,

(3.14)

\|u_{j}-u_{j,1}-u_{j,2}\|_{q}\to 0\quad\hbox{for all}\ 2\leq q<6,

(3.15)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}-|\nabla u_{j,1}|^{2}-|\nabla u_{j,2}|^{2}\big)\,dx\geq o(1).

Moreover replacing
u_{j,1}
,
u_{j,2}
by
\frac{\sqrt{\mu^{\prime}}}{\|u_{j,1}\|_{2}}u_{j,1}
,

\frac{\sqrt{\mu-\mu^{\prime}}}{\|u_{j,2}\|_{2}}u_{j,2}
respectively,
we may assume that
\|u_{j,1}\|_{2}^{2}=\mu^{\prime}
,
\|u_{j,2}\|_{2}^{2}=\mu-\mu^{\prime}

and (
3.13
)-(
3.15
) hold.
Now from (
3.13
), one has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u_{j,1}(x)|^{2}|u_{j,2}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{{\rm supp}(u_{j,2})}\int_{{\rm supp}(u_{j,1})}\frac{|u_{j,1}(x)|^{2}|u_{j,2}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|y_{j}-z_{j}|-R_{1}-R_{2}}\|u_{j,1}\|_{2}^{2}\|u_{j,2}\|_{2}^{2}\to 0\quad\hbox{as}\ j\to\infty.

Using (
3.14
) and arguing as in the proof of Lemma 2.2 in
[
31
]
,
a direct computation yields that

\displaystyle A_{1}(u_{j})-A_{1}(u_{j,1})-A_{1}(u_{j,2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}(u_{j})|u_{j}|^{2}-S_{1}(u_{j,1})|u_{j,1}|^{2}-S_{1}(u_{j,2})|u_{j,2}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big\{\big(S_{1}(u_{j})|u_{j}|+S_{1}(u_{j,1})|u_{j,1}|+S_{1}(u_{j,2})|u_{j,2}|\big)\big(|u_{j}|-|u_{j,1}|-|u_{j,2}|\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+|u_{j}|\big(|u_{j,1}|+|u_{j,2}|\big)\big(S_{1}(u_{j})-S_{1}(u_{j,1})-S_{1}(u_{j,2})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+|u_{j,1}||u_{j,2}|\big(S_{1}(u_{j,1})+S_{1}(u_{j,2})\big)+|u_{j}|\big(|u_{j,1}|S_{1}(u_{j,2})+|u_{j,2}|S_{1}(u_{j,1})\big)\Big\}\,dx

\displaystyle\to 0\quad(n\to\infty).

Similarly, one finds that

A_{2}(u_{j})-A_{2}(u_{j,1})-A_{2}(u_{j,2})\to 0.

Moreover from (
2.12
) and (
3.13
), it holds that

(3.16)

A_{2}(u_{j,2})\to 0\ \text{if $|y_{j}|$ is bounded}\quad\text{and}\quad A_{2}(u_{j,1})\to 0\ \text{if}\ |y_{j}|\to\infty\quad\text{as}\ j\to\infty.

Now we suppose that
|y_{j}|
is bounded.
Then we deduce from (
3.14
), (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) that

c(\mu)=E(u_{j})+o(1)\geq E(u_{j,1})+E_{\infty}(u_{j,2})+o(1)\geq c(\mu^{\prime})+c_{\infty}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})+o(1).

Taking
\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to\infty}
in both sides, one finds that

(3.17)

c(\mu)\geq c(\mu^{\prime})+c_{\infty}(\mu-\mu^{\prime}).

When
\frac{\mu}{2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}}\leq\mu^{\prime}<\mu
,
we have a contradiction by Lemma
3.4
(ii).
If
0<\mu^{\prime}<\frac{\mu}{2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}}
and
c(\mu^{\prime})=0
,
one has from (
3.2
) and (
3.17
) that

c_{\infty}(\mu)\geq c(\mu)\geq c_{\infty}(\mu-\mu^{\prime}).

But since
\mu-\mu^{\prime}>\mu^{*}
, it follows that
c_{\infty}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})>c_{\infty}(\mu)
,
which leads to a contradiction.
In the case
0<\mu^{\prime}<\frac{\mu}{2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}}
and
c(\mu^{\prime})<0
,
we are able to apply Lemma
3.6
(ii) to arrive at a contradiction.

Next we assume that
|y_{j}|\to\infty
.
Then it follows from (
3.14
), (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) that

(3.18)

c(\mu)\geq c(\mu-\mu^{\prime})+c_{\infty}(\mu^{\prime}).

When
0<\mu^{\prime}<\left(1-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}}\right)\mu
, we have a contradiction by Lemma
3.4
(ii).
If
\left(1-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}}\right)\mu\leq\mu^{\prime}<\mu
and
c(\mu-\mu^{\prime})=0
,
one finds that
c_{\infty}(\mu)\geq c(\mu)\geq c_{\infty}(\mu^{\prime})
,
which is impossible because
c_{\infty}(\mu^{\prime})>c_{\infty}(\mu)
.
In the case
\left(1-\frac{1}{2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}}\right)\mu\leq\mu^{\prime}<\mu
and
c(\mu-\mu^{\prime})<0
,
we can use Lemma
3.6
(ii) to derive a contradiction.
Thus in all cases, we are able to reach a contradiction and hence Dichotomy does not occur.

The only remaining possibility is Compactness, that is,
there exists
\{y_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists
R_{\varepsilon}>0
satisfying

(3.19)

\int_{B_{R_{\varepsilon}}(y_{j})}|u_{j}(x)|^{2}\,dx\geq\mu-\varepsilon.

Since
\|u_{j}\|_{H^{1}}
is bounded, there exists
u_{\mu}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
such that
up to a subsequence,
u_{j}(\cdot-y_{j})\rightharpoonup u_{\mu}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
.
Then from (
3.19
), it follows that
u_{j}(\cdot-y_{j})\to u_{\mu}
in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})

for any
2\leq q<6
.
By the weak lower semi-continuity of
\|\nabla\cdot\|_{2}

and by Lemma
2.2
, we get

c(\mu)=\liminf_{j\to\infty}E\big(u_{j}(\cdot-y_{j})\big)\geq E(u_{\mu})\geq c(\mu).

This implies that
E(u_{\mu})=c(\mu)
and

\|\nabla u_{j}(\cdot-y_{j})\|_{2}\to\|\nabla u_{\mu}\|_{2}
.
Thus we obtain
u_{j}(\cdot-y_{j})\to u_{\mu}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})

and hence the proof is complete.
∎

4.
Global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem

In this section, we consider the solvability of the Cauchy problem:

(4.1)

\begin{cases}i\psi_{t}+\Delta\psi-e\phi\psi+|\psi|^{p-1}\psi=0\quad\hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta\phi=\frac{e}{2}\big(|\psi|^{2}-\rho(x)\big)\quad\hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0},\end{cases}

where
e>0
,
1<p<5
and
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
.
For the doping profile
\rho
, we only assume that
\rho\in L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Then we have the following result on the local well-posedness.

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists
T=T(\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})>0
such that
(
4.1
) has a unique solution
\psi\in X
, where

X=\left\{\psi\in C\left([0,T],H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left((0,T),H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\right)\right\}.

Furthermore,
\psi
satisfies the energy conservation law
and the charge conservation law:

\mathcal{E}\big(\psi(t)\big)=\mathcal{E}(\psi_{0})\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|\psi(t)\|_{2}=\|\psi_{0}\|_{2}\quad\hbox{for all}\ t\in(0,T).

Although it seems that Proposition
4.1
can be obtained
in the framework of
[
11
, Proposition 3.2.9, Theorem 4.3.1 and Corollary 4.3.3]
,
we give the proof for the sake of completeness and reader’s convenience.
For this purpose, we first recall the following inequalities in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Lemma 4.2

(Strichartz’s estimates)
.

Let
e^{it\Delta}
be the linear propagator generated by the free
Schrödinger equation
i\psi_{t}+\Delta\psi=0
.
Assume that pairs
(q_{0},r_{0})
,
(q_{1},r_{1})
,
(q_{2},r_{2})
are admissible, namely,
they fulfill the relation:

\frac{2}{q_{i}}=3\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r_{i}}\right),\quad 2\leq r_{i}\leq 6\quad(i=0,1,2).

(i)

There exists
C>0
such that

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R},L^{r_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\quad\hbox{for all}\ f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

(ii)

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval,
J=\bar{I}
and
t_{0}\in J
.
Then there exists
C>0
independent of
I
such that

\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}f(s)\,ds\right\|_{L^{q_{1}}(I,L^{r_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{q_{2}^{\prime}}(I,L^{r_{2}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\quad\hbox{for all}\ f\in{L^{q_{2}^{\prime}}(I,L^{r_{2}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))},

where
q_{2}^{\prime}
and
r_{2}^{\prime}
are Hölder conjugate exponents
of
q_{2}
and
r_{2}
respectively.

Lemma 4.3

(Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality)
.

Let
0<\alpha<3
and
1<q<r<\infty
satisfy

\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}=1-\frac{\alpha}{3}
,
and define
I_{\alpha}f
by

I_{\alpha}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x-y|^{-\alpha}f(y)\,dy.

Then there exists
C>0
such that

\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{r}\leq C\|f\|_{q}\quad\hbox{for all}\ f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

For simplicity, we write
L^{q}(I,L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
as
L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{3})

and use a notation
A\lesssim B
if there is a positive constant
independent of
A
and
B
such that
A\leq CB
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

First as we have seen in Subsection 2.1,
the Poisson equation (
2.1
) has a unique solution

\psi=eS(\psi)\in D_{x}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad\hbox{for any}\ \psi\in H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Let us consider the Duhamel formula associated with (
4.1
)
and the solution map
\mathcal{H}(\psi)
which is defined by

(4.2)

\mathcal{H}(\psi):=e^{it\Delta}\psi_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}|\psi|^{p-1}\psi\,ds-e^{2}i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}S(\psi)\psi\,ds.

For
T>0
and
M=3\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
,
we also define a complete metric space

\displaystyle X_{T}

\displaystyle:=\Big\{\psi\in L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}\left((-T,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)\cap L_{t}^{\frac{4(p+1)}{3(p-1)}}W_{x}^{1,p+1}\left((-T,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)\ ;\

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}}\leq M,\ \|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4(p+1)}{3(p-1)}}W_{x}^{1,p+1}}\leq M\Big\}

equipped with the distance

d(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4(p+1)}{3(p-1)}}L_{x}^{p+1}}+\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}.

Note that
\left(\frac{4(p+1)}{3(p-1)},p+1\right)
and
(\infty,2)

are both admissible.
For simplicity, we write
q=\frac{4(p+1)}{3(p-1)}
.
It suffices to show that
\mathcal{H}
is a contraction mapping on
X_{T}

provided that
T
is sufficiently small.

First we establish that
\mathcal{H}
maps
X_{T}
into itself.
To this aim, we apply Lemma
4.2
to find that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(\psi)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|S(\psi)\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4}{3}}W_{x}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\||\psi|^{p-1}\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q^{\prime}}W_{x}^{1,\frac{p+1}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(\psi)\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{1,p+1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|S(\psi)\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4}{3}}W_{x}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\||\psi|^{p-1}\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q^{\prime}}W_{x}^{1,\frac{p+1}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Here we mention that
\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{\prime}=4
,
\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\prime}=3

and the pair
(4,3)
is admissible.
By the Hölder inequality and the Sobolev inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\||\psi|^{p-1}\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{q^{\prime}}\|\psi\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{(p-1)q^{\prime}}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\leq\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{p+1}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{\frac{2(p-1)(p+1)}{5-p}}\,dt\right)^{\frac{5-p}{2(p+1)}}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{5-p}{2(p+1)}}\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{p+1}}\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}}^{p-1}\leq M^{p}T^{\frac{5-p}{2(p+1)}},

where we used the fact
q^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{q^{\prime}}{q}\right)^{-1}=\frac{2(p+1)}{5-p}
.
Similarly one has

\displaystyle\|\nabla(|\psi|^{p-1}\psi)\|_{L_{t}^{q^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\psi|^{p-1}\nabla\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{5-p}{2(p+1)}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{p+1}}\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}}^{p-1}\leq M^{p}T^{\frac{5-p}{2(p+1)}}.

Next by Lemma
2.1
and the fact
S(\psi)=S_{1}(\psi)+S_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|S(\psi)\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4}{3}}L_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|S(\psi)\|_{L_{x}^{6}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|\psi\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\,dt\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}\lesssim T^{\frac{3}{4}}\|S(\psi)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{6}}\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{3}{4}}\left(\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\leq M(M^{2}+1)T^{\frac{3}{4}},

\displaystyle\|\nabla(S(\psi)\psi)\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4}{3}}L_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\nabla S(\psi)\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4}{3}}L_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\|S(\psi)\nabla\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4}{3}}L_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{3}{4}}\|\nabla S(\psi)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{6}}+T^{\frac{3}{4}}\|S(\psi)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{6}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{3}{4}}(\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}})\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}}\leq M(M^{2}+1)T^{\frac{3}{4}}.

Thus from (

)-(

), one finds that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(\psi)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\frac{M}{3}+M^{p}T^{\frac{5-p}{2(p+1)}}+M(M^{2}+1)T^{\frac{3}{4}},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(\psi)\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{1,p+1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\frac{M}{3}+M^{p}T^{\frac{5-p}{2(p+1)}}+M(M^{2}+1)T^{\frac{3}{4}}.

Choosing

sufficiently small, it follows that

into itself.

Finally we prove that

is a contraction mapping on

.
Applying Lemma

again, we first observe that

d\big(\mathcal{H}(\psi_{1}),\mathcal{H}(\psi_{2})\big)\lesssim\||\psi_{1}|^{p-1}\psi_{1}-|\psi_{2}|^{p-1}\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{q^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|S(\psi_{1})\psi_{1}-S(\psi_{2})\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4}{3}}L_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

By the Hölder inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\||\psi_{1}|^{p-1}\psi_{1}-|\psi_{2}|^{p-1}\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{q^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{q^{\prime}}\left(\|\psi_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{p-1}+\|\psi_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{p-1}\right)^{q^{\prime}}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{5-p}{2(p+1)}}\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{p+1}}\left(\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}}^{p-1}+\|\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}_{x}}^{p-1}\right)

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq M^{p-1}T^{\frac{5-p}{2(p+1)}}d(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}).

Moreover one has

\displaystyle\|S(\psi_{1})\psi_{1}-S(\psi_{2})\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4}{3}}L_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq\|S(\psi_{1})(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4}{3}}L_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\|(S(\psi_{1})-S(\psi_{2}))\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4}{3}}L_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{3}{4}}\|S(\psi_{1})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{6}}\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+T^{\frac{3}{4}}\|S(\psi_{1})-S(\psi_{2})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{6}}\|\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}.

Here we recall that

\displaystyle S(\psi_{1})-S(\psi_{2})

\displaystyle=S_{1}(\psi_{1})+S_{2}-S_{1}(\psi_{2})-S_{2}=\frac{1}{8\pi|x|}*(|\psi_{1}|^{2}-|\psi_{2}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x-y|^{-1}(|\psi_{1}(y)|^{2}-|\psi_{2}(y)|^{2})\,dy.

4.3
with

one gets

from which we find that

From (

) and (

∎

6.
The case
\rho
is a characteristic function

In this section, we consider the case where the doping profile
\rho

is a characteristic function,
which appears frequently in physical literatures
[
21
,
25
,
27
]
.
More precisely, let
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be
disjoint bounded open sets with smooth boundary.
For
\alpha_{i}>0

(i=1,\cdots,m)
,
we assume that the doping profile
\rho
has the form:

(6.1)

\rho(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\chi_{\Omega_{i}}(x),\quad\chi_{\Omega_{i}}(x)=\begin{cases}1&(x\in\Omega_{i}),\\
0&(x\notin\Omega_{i}).\end{cases}

In this case,
\rho
cannot be weakly differentiable so that the assumption

\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\leq\rho_{0}
does not make sense.
Even so, we are able to obtain the existence of stable standing waves
by imposing some smallness condition related with
\Omega_{i}
.

To state our main result for this case,
let us put
\displaystyle L:=\sup_{x\in\partial\Omega}|x|<\infty
.
A key is the following
sharp boundary trace inequality

due to
[
1
, Theorem 6.1]
,
which we present here according to the form used in this paper.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
and
\gamma:H^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
be
the trace operator.
Then it holds that

\int_{\partial\Omega}|\gamma(u)|^{2}\,dS\leq\kappa_{1}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,dx+\kappa_{2}(\Omega)\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\hbox{for any}\ u\in H^{1}(\Omega),

where
\kappa_{1}(\Omega)=\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{|\Omega|}
,

\kappa_{2}(\Omega)=\big\||\nabla w|\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}

and
w
is a unique solution of the torsion problem:

\Delta w=\kappa_{1}(\Omega)\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega,\quad\frac{\partial w}{\partial n}=1\ \hbox{on}\ \partial\Omega.

In relation to the size of
\rho
, we define

D(\Omega):=L|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{6}}|\partial\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\kappa_{1}(\Omega)|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{3}}+\kappa_{2}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Remark 6.2
.

It is known that
\kappa_{2}(\Omega)\geq 1
; see
[
1
]
.
Then by the isoperimetric inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

|\partial\Omega|\geq 3|\Omega|^{\frac{2}{3}}|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{3}},

and the fact
|\Omega|\leq|B_{L}(0)|=L^{3}|B_{1}|
, we find that

(6.2)

D(\Omega)\geq\left(\frac{|\Omega|}{|B_{1}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{6}}\cdot\sqrt{3}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{3}}|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(3|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{3}}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=C|\Omega|^{\frac{5}{6}}=C\|\chi_{\Omega}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
\Omega
.

Under these preparations, we have the following result.

Theorem 6.3
.

Suppose that
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
, assume (
1.10
) and
let
\mu>2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}\mu^{*}
be given.
Under the assumption (
6.1
),
there exists
\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}(e,\mu)>0
such that
if
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\leq\rho_{0}
,
the minimization problem (
1.5
) admits a minimizer
u_{\mu}
.

Moreover the associated Lagrange multiplier
\omega=\omega(\mu)
is positive.

Similarly to Theorem
1.2
,
the orbital stability of

also holds true.

We mention that the first time
x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)
appeared
was the definition of
A_{3}(u)
in Subsection 2.3.
Under the assumption (
6.1
), we replace

by

(6.3)

A_{3}(u):=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}S_{1}(u)x\cdot n_{i}\,dS_{i},

where

is the unit outward normal on
\partial\Omega_{i}
.
Indeed we have the following.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
and put

\Omega(\lambda):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}(u)(x)\chi_{\Omega}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\quad\text{for}\ \lambda>0.

Then it holds that

\Omega^{\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\partial(\lambda\Omega)}S_{1}(u)(x)x\cdot n\,dS.

Proof.

The proof is based on the domain deformation as in
[
30

.
In fact, one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Omega(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\lambda}\int_{\lambda\Omega}S_{1}(u)(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{h}\left(\int_{(\lambda+h)\Omega}S_{1}(u)(x)\,dx-\int_{\lambda\Omega}S_{1}(u)(x)\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{h}\int_{(\lambda+h)\Omega\setminus(\lambda\Omega)}S_{1}(u)(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{h}\int_{\lambda}^{\lambda+h}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{\partial(t\Omega)}S_{1}(u)(x)x\cdot(tn)\,dS\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\partial(\lambda\Omega)}S_{1}(u)(x)x\cdot n\,dS,

from which we conclude.
∎

By Lemma

Lemma 6.5
.

Under the assumption (

\displaystyle(5p-7)E(u)

\displaystyle=2(p-2)\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{(3p-5)\omega}{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle-e^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\left(2\int_{\Omega_{i}}S_{1}(u)\,dx-\frac{3-p}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}S_{1}(u)x\cdot n_{i}\,dS_{i}\right).

Proof.

As we have seen in Subsection 2.5,

\displaystyle 0

one finds that

\displaystyle A_{2}\big((u_{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}\big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}\big((u_{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}\big)(x)\chi_{\Omega_{i}}(x)\,dx=-\frac{\lambda^{-1}}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}(u_{\varepsilon})(x)\chi_{\Omega_{i}}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda^{-1}}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\Omega_{i}(\lambda).

Thus it follows that

\displaystyle 2e^{2}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\lambda^{-3}A_{2}\big((u_{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}\big)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{2}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\lambda^{-4}\Omega_{i}(\lambda)\right)=2\lambda^{-5}e^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\Omega_{i}(\lambda)-\frac{\lambda^{-4}}{2}e^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda).

Moreover from (
6.3
) and by Lemma
6.4
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}\lambda^{2}S_{1}(u_{\varepsilon})(\lambda x)x\cdot n\,dS=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}S_{1}\big((u_{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}\big)(x)x\cdot n\,dS

\displaystyle=-2A_{3}\big((u_{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}\big)

and hence

2e^{2}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\lambda^{-3}A_{2}\big((u_{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}\big)\right)=-8\lambda^{-4}e^{2}A_{2}\big((u_{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}\big)+\lambda^{-4}e^{2}A_{3}\big((u_{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}\big).

Finally by Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
6.6
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|A_{2}\big((u_{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}\big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}|\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{5}{6}}\|(u_{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}\|_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\nabla(u_{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}=C\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}\mu^{\frac{3}{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}|\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{5}{6}}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\left|A_{3}\big((u_{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}\big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}\mu^{\frac{3}{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using (
6.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq 2(p-2)\lambda^{2p-5}\left\{\frac{1}{4}c_{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{8}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{2}e^{\frac{8}{3}}\mu\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}\ \right\}

\displaystyle+C_{1}\lambda^{-\frac{5}{2}}e^{2}\mu^{\frac{3}{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus similarly to Lemma
3.4
,
there exists
\rho_{0}=\rho(e,\mu)>0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\leq\rho_{0}\quad\Rightarrow\quad f^{\prime}(\lambda)<0\ \text{for all}\ \lambda>1\ \text{and}\ s\in\left[\frac{\mu}{2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}},\mu\right],

from which we can conclude.

We can also show the other parts of Theorem
6.3

by modifying the proof of Lemma
3.9
in a similar way.
∎

7.
Concluding remark and an open problem

In this paper, the nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson system with a doping profile
has been investigated.
By establishing the existence of
L^{2}
-constraint minimizers when

2<p<\frac{7}{3},\ \mu>2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}\mu^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\ll 1.

we are able to obtain stable standing waves.
The presence of a doping profile
\rho
causes a difficulty of
proving the strict sub-additivity which is the key of
the existence and the stability of standing waves.
This paper concludes by providing one open problem.

Problem
: Non-existence of minimizers for large
\rho
?

We have shown the existence of minimizers when
\rho
is small,
but we don’t know what happens if
\rho
is large.
In 1D case, it was shown in
[
16
]
that
no minimizer exists in the case
\mu<\|\rho\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}
,
which was referred to the
supercritical case
.
(Note that
[
16
]
deals with the Schrödinger-Poisson system
with
\Delta\phi=\frac{1}{2}(|u|^{2}-\rho(x))

so that the sign in the front of
A(u)
in (
1.4
) is opposite.)
Hence a natural question is whether a similar result holds for the 3D problem.

To explain the idea in
[
16
]
,
let us consider the problem in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and denote by
G(x)

the fundamental solution of
-\Delta
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Under the assumption
\rho\geq 0
and
\rho\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
the nonlocal term
A(u)
can be expressed as follows.

\displaystyle A(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}G(x-y)\big(|u(x)|^{2}-\rho(x)\big)\big(|u(y)|^{2}-\rho(y)\big)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}G(x-y)|u(x)|^{2}(u(y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}G(x-y)|u(x)|^{2}\rho(y)\,dx\,dy+A_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(G(x-y)-G(x)-G(y)\big)|u(x)|^{2}|u(y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}\big(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}-\|\rho\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}G(x)|u(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(G(x-y)-G(x)\big)|u(x)|^{2}\rho(y)\,dx\,dy+A_{0}.

Here we have used Fubini’s theorem and wrote

A_{0}=\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}G(x-y)\rho(x)\rho(y)\,dx\,dy
.
Thus one has
c(\mu)=-\infty
if we could show that
there is a family
\{u_{\lambda}\}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying

(7.1)

\displaystyle\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}=\mu,\quad\|\nabla u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(G(x-y)-G(x)-G(y)\big)|u_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}|u_{\lambda}(y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy\right|\leq C,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(G(x-y)-G(x)\big)|u_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}\rho(y)\,dx\,dy\right|\leq C,

(7.2)

\displaystyle\hbox{but}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}G(x)|u_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}\,dx\to\infty\quad\hbox{as}\ \lambda\to\infty,

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
\lambda
.

When
N=1
, it follows that
G(x)=\frac{1}{2}|x|

and
\{u_{\lambda}\}
can be constructed by considering a function
whose mass is supported near the origin and infinity.
(See
[
16
, Example 4.1 and Remark 4.6]
.)
However in the 3D case, which yields
G(x)=\frac{1}{4\pi|x|}
,
it cannot happen that both (
7.1
) and (
7.2
) are fulfilled.
Indeed by the Hardy inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}G(x)|u(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Moreover as we have shown in Lemma
3.1
,
the energy functional on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is always bounded from below,
regardless of the size of
\rho
.
Therefore the only possibility for the non-existence is that
the strict sub-additivity does not hold when
\mu<\|\rho\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.
Moreover as we have observed in this paper,
in the 3D problem,
it is rather natural to measure the size of
\rho
by
L^{\frac{6}{5}}
-norm,
which makes us to conjecture that the non-existence result may be obtained
if
\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}
is large.

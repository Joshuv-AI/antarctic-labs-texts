Partitions with unique largest part and their generating functions
H. Kaur, M. Rana

Abstract




Abstract

The paper introduce a new type of partitions where the largest part appears exactly once, and the remaining parts constitute a partition of that largest part. We derive the generating function associated with these partitions and subsequently explore several variations, providing the corresponding generating functions for each variant.

Introduction

Partitions have been the object of interest to mathematicians for many years. A
Partition
of a positive integer
n
is a way of expressing it as a sum of positive integer parts, generally the parts are listed in descending order, from the largest (
\ell
) to the smallest (
s
). The generating function to count the number of partitions
p(n)
of
n
is

\sum_{n\geq 0}p(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}

where

(t;q)_{n}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ for }n=0,\\
(1-t)(1-tq)(1-tq^{2})(1-tq^{3})\cdots(1-tq^{n-1})&\text{ for }n>0\end{cases}

is the
q-
shifted factorial and

(t;q)_{\infty}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(t;q)_{n},\text{~~where~~}|q|<1.

For a detailed understanding of integer partitions, one can refer to
[
1
,
2
]
. The study of restricted partitions dates back to Euler, who established a remarkable result: the number of partitions of any integer into odd parts,
o(n)
, is equal to the number of partitions into distinct parts or strict partitions,
d(n)
, the corresponding sequence of partitions is listed in On Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequence (OEIS) with number A000009. The OEIS is a vast database of well-defined sequences of integers, many of which originate from partitions theory. Analogous to the results of Euler, many appealing results on partitions can be found in

,

,

]
.

In this paper, we discuss the partition, denoted by
\Lambda
where the largest part appears exactly once, and the remaining parts constitute a partition of that largest part. For example, let
n=10
, then some relevant partitions,
\Lambda
, satisfying the condition are:

5+4+1,5+3+2,5+2+2+1

However, several other partitions of
n=10,
such as
9+1,8+2,7+3,
do not satisfy the condition. Since the sum of all parts other than the largest part always equals the largest part (
\ell
), the total sum is always an even number (
2\ell
). We provide the generating function corresponding to these partitions and to some associated restricted partitions analogously. The integer sequences corresponding to partitions described in Theorems

-
4

5
-
6

Theorem 1

The generating function to count the number of partitions
\Lambda
is

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}-\frac{1}{(1-q^{2})}.

We now consider some variations of the partition
\Lambda
, motivated by Euler’s theorem on partitions of
n
into distinct or odd parts. The remaining parts, other than the largest part, comes with some restrictions. Analogously, we obtained their generating functions. We use the notations as follows:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{d}:

\displaystyle\text{ partition }\Lambda\text{ where the parts are distinct,}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{o}:

\displaystyle\text{ partition }\Lambda\text{ where the parts are odd, }

\displaystyle\Lambda_{od}:

\displaystyle\text{ partition }\Lambda\text{ where the parts are odd and distinct. }

Theorem 2

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{d}(n)q^{n}={(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}-\frac{1}{(1-q^{2})}.

\Lambda_{o}

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{o}(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}}-\frac{q^{2}}{(1-q^{4})}-1.

is

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{od}(n)q^{n}={(-q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}}-\frac{q^{2}}{(1-q^{4})}-1.

Proof of the theorems

Proof of Theorem
1

The partitions
\Lambda,
with the largest part and rest of the parts forms the partitions of largest part, can be generated as:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=q^{2}\cdot q^{1+1}+q^{3}\cdot(q^{2+1}+q^{1+1+1})+q^{4}\cdot(q^{3+1}+q^{2+2}+q^{2+1+1}+q^{1+1+1+1})

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt+q^{5}\cdot(q^{4+1}+q^{3+2}+q^{3+1+1}+q^{2+2+1}+q^{2+1+1+1}+q^{1+1+1+1+1})+\cdots

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n}(p(n)-1)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(p(n)-1)q^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{2n}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{2n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}-\frac{1}{(1-q^{2})}.

Proof of Theorem
2

Consider partitions
\Lambda_{d},
with the largest part and rest of the parts are distinct that represents the partition of the corresponding largest part, it can be generated as:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{d}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=q^{3}\cdot(q^{2+1})+q^{4}\cdot(q^{3+1})+q^{5}\cdot(q^{4+1}+q^{3+2})+q^{6}\cdot(q^{5+1}+q^{4+2}+q^{3+2+1})

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt+q^{7}\cdot(q^{6+1}+q^{5+2}+q^{4+3}+q^{4+2+1})+\cdots.

Let
d(n)
represents the number of distinct partitions of
n
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{d}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 3}q^{n}(d(n)-1)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 3}^{\infty}(d(n)-1)q^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 3}d(n)q^{2n}-\sum_{n\geq 3}q^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}d(n)q^{2n}-\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{2n}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{d}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle={(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}-\frac{1}{(1-q^{2})}.

Proof of Theorem
3

The generating function for partitions with the largest part and rest of the parts are odd that constitutes the partition of the corresponding largest part leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{o}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=q^{2}\cdot(q^{1+1})+q^{3}\cdot(q^{1+1+1})+q^{4}\cdot(q^{3+1}+q^{1+1+1})+

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684ptq^{5}\cdot(q^{3+1+1}+q^{1+1+1+1+1})+q^{6}\cdot(q^{5+1}+q^{3+3}+q^{3+1+1+1})+

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptq^{7}\cdot(q^{5+1+1}+q^{3+3+1}+q^{3+1+1+1+1}+q^{1+1+1+1+1+1+1})+\cdots

Let
o(n)
represents the number of odd partitions of
n
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{o}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{n\geq 2}\\
{n-even}\end{subarray}}o(n)q^{2n}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{n\geq 3}\\
{n-odd}\end{subarray}}(o(n)-1)q^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 2}o(n)q^{2n}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{n\geq 3}\\
{n-odd}\end{subarray}}q^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}o(n)q^{2n}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{n\geq 1}\\
{n-odd}\end{subarray}}q^{2n}-(o(0)q^{0}+o(1)q^{2})+q^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{o}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}o(n)q^{2n}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{n\geq 1}\\
{n-odd}\end{subarray}}q^{2n}-(o(0)q^{0}+o(1)q^{2})+q^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}}-\frac{q^{2}}{(1-q^{4})}-1

The generating function for partitions
\Lambda
with the parts (apart from the largest part) are distinct and
\equiv\pm 1\pmod{3}
that constitutes the largest part leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{3}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=q^{3}\cdot q^{1+2}+q^{5}\cdot q^{4+1}+q^{6}\cdot(q^{5+1}+q^{4+2})+q^{7}\cdot(q^{5+2}+q^{4+2+1})+

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptq^{8}\cdot(q^{7+1}+q^{5+2+1})+q^{9}\cdot(q^{8+1}+q^{7+2}+q^{5+4})+

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptq^{10}\cdot(q^{8+2}+q^{7+2+1}+q^{5+4+1})+q^{11}\cdot(q^{10+1}+q^{8+2+1}+q^{7+1}+q^{5+4+2})\cdots

Let
dp_{3}(n)
represents the number of distinct partitions of
n
with parts
\equiv\pm 1\pmod{3}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{3}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{n\geq 3}\\
{3|n}\end{subarray}}q^{n}dp_{3}(n)q^{n}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{n\geq 1}\\

Hysteresis in the complex nonlinear refractive index of a homogeneous and isotropic medium
Igor Kuzmenko 1,2 *, Y. B. Band 1,2,3 *, Yshai Avishai 1,4 , Marek Trippenbach 5

Abstract
We calculate the permittivity, ϵ ⁡ ( ω ) \epsilon(\omega) , for a medium with a quadratic electro-optic effect, modeling it as a Duffing oscillator. The nonlinear refractive index n ⁡ ( ω , E ⁡ ( ω ) ) n(\omega,E(\omega)) and the nonlinear absorption coefficient α ⁡ ( ω , E ⁡ ( ω ) ) \alpha(\omega,E(\omega)) exhibit hysteresis when the light intensity is varied [here E ⁡ ( ω ) E(\omega) is the electric field strength at angular frequency ω \omega ], and when the light frequency is varied. n ⁡ ( ω , E ⁡ ( ω ) ) n(\omega,E(\omega)) can be negative when the resonances in the permittivity and permeability are close to one another.

Introduction
: Negative-index metamaterials (NIMs) were first studied by Victor Veselago in 1967
Veselago_68
. However, the first practical metamaterial with negative refractive index was not developed until 33 years later
Shelby_01
. NIMs can generate significant third harmonic generation (THG) when driven by strong electromagnetic fields, due to two primary factors: strong resonances that enhance local fields and the inclusion of materials with an inherent nonlinear optical response
Xie-24
.

Here we consider a nonlinear generalization of the Drude-Lorentz model
Drude-Lorentz
;
BF
using the the Duffing oscillator model
Duffing
to investigate light propagation in isotropic homogeneous media under intense light conditions. Our aim is to generalize a previous study
Band_24
which focused on the negative refraction in isotropic homogeneous media, by incorporating strong-field effects. We demonstrate that the nonlinear refractive index
n(\omega,E(\omega))
and the absorption coefficient
\alpha(\omega,E(\omega))
(i.e., the complex nonlinear refractive index) of a homogeneous isotropic medium can exhibit hysteresis when the frequency and/or the intensity of the light are varied. The real part of the complex nonlinear refractive index can be negative, however, for this to occur, the resonance frequency of the permeability must be near the resonance frequency of the permittivity.

The hysteresis of light reflection and refraction at the boundary of a nonlinear medium has been well-studied (see references
Kaplan_77
;
Band_84
;
Afanasev_98
;
Lousse_03
;
Cai_22
). Here, however, we demonstrate that the complex nonlinear refractive index itself can exhibit hysteresis.

The Duffing Model
: Several studies have considered nonlinear Lorentz models, such as the Duffing oscillator
Duffing
;
Stoker_50
;
Kazantseva-05
;
Kovacic-11
, to describe nonlinear optical dispersion, refraction and absorption
Scalora-15
;
Varin-18
;
Varin-19
;
Xia-25
;
Chen_23
. We use the Duffing equation, a nonlinear second-order differential equation, to model a bound electron (e.g., in an atom) strongly driven by an electromagnetic field. The equation is

\ddot{a}(t)+\gamma\,\dot{a}(t)+\omega_{0}^{2}\,a(t)+\beta\,a^{3}(t)=\Gamma\,e^{-i\omega t}+\Gamma^{*}\,e^{i\omega t},

(1)

where the real function
a(t)
is the oscillator displacement coordinate at time
t
,
\dot{a}(t)
is the velocity,
\ddot{a}(t)
is the acceleration,
\omega_{0}
is the resonance frequency of the (linear) oscillator,
\gamma
is the decay rate, and
\beta
is a nonlinearity parameter with units of s
-2
cm
-2
in Gaussian units. The driving parameter, which has units of cm s
-2
, is given by
\Gamma=\frac{-e}{m}E
, where
e
is the elementary charge,
m
is the electron mass, and
E
is the
complex
electric field strength of the electromagnetic radiation at frequency
\omega
.

We use an iterative method to treat the cubic term of the Duffing equation in order to solve Eq. (
1
). The first iteration includes only the fundamental frequency, the second iteration includes the fundamental frequency and the third harmonic frequency, etc. Our method is somewhat similar to the one described in Ref.
Stoker_50
which uses the iteration method originally employed by Duffing
Duffing
. Our iteration method, as opposed to other iteration methods
Tisdell_19
, captures the multiple (i.e., three) solutions arising from the nonlinear term in the Duffing equation.

Iteration method
:
We are interested in cases where the nonlinear term is too large for perturbation theory to converge. Therefore we have developed an iteration method which allows treating cases where the cubic term is not small. In this section, we solve Eq. (
1
) using an iteration method. The solution for
t\gg 2/\gamma
is a periodic function with period
2\pi/\omega
, and can be expanded in a Fourier series:

a(t)\underset{t\gg 2/\gamma}{=}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big\{\beta^{n}\Gamma^{2n+1}\,{\mathcal{A}}_{2n+1}\,e^{-i(2n+1)\omega t}+{\rm c.c.}\big\}.

(2)

Here,
\beta^{n}\Gamma^{2n+1}{\mathcal{A}}_{2n+1}
is the amplitude of the
(2n+1)
th harmonic of the oscillator displacement coordinate. In general, the coefficient
{\mathcal{A}}_{2n+1}
, has units of
{\text{s}}^{6n+2}
, and is proportional to the oscillator polarizability at frequency
(2n+1)\omega
. In what follows, we describe the first iteration for
a(t)
.

The lowest order iteration in powers of
\beta\,\Gamma^{2}
,
a_{1}(t)
, can be obtained from Eq. (
2
) after neglecting all the harmonics and keeping only the fundamental frequency,

a_{1}(t)=\Gamma\,{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\,e^{-i\omega t}+{\rm c.c.}

(3)

Substituting this solution into Eq. (
1
), and neglecting the third harmonic, yields

\big\{{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{-1}(\omega)+3\,\beta\,\big|\Gamma{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\big|^{2}\big\}{\mathcal{A}}_{1}=1,

(4)

where the linear Lorentzian operator is

{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\omega)=\frac{-1}{\omega^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}+i\,\gamma\,\omega}.

(5)

The solution for the amplitude
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
in Eq. (
4
) can be substituted into (
3
) to yield the first iteration for
a(t)
, i.e.,
a_{1}(t)
.

The complex operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\omega)
can be written in amplitude-phase notation as
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\omega)=|{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\omega)|\,e^{i\theta_{L}(w)}
, where

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\omega)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2})^{2}+\gamma^{2}\omega^{2}}},

(6)

\displaystyle\theta_{L}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arctan\Big(\frac{\gamma\,\omega}{\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\Big).

(7)

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
can also be written in amplitude-phase notation as
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}=|{\mathcal{A}}_{1}|\,e^{i\theta_{A}}
, where
|{\mathcal{A}}_{1}|
is found from the equation,

\big\{\big(\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega^{2}+3\,\beta\,\big|\Gamma\,{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\big|^{2}\big)^{2}+\gamma^{2}\omega^{2}\big\}\big|{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\big|^{2}=1,

(8)

and the phase of
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
is
\theta_{A}=i\,\ln\big(|{\mathcal{A}}_{1}|\,\{{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{-1}(\omega)+3\,\beta\,|\Gamma\,{\mathcal{A}}_{1}|^{2}\}\big)
. The complex coefficient
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
in Eq. (
3
) can be found by solving cubic equation (
8
) for
|{\mathcal{A}}_{1}|^{2}
and substituting the solution into the expression for the phase. The complex solution of the linear oscillator with
\beta=0
,
{\mathcal{A}}_{1,0}
, is

{\mathcal{A}}_{1,0}={\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\omega).

(9)

We denote the solutions of the cubic equation (
8
) by
|{\mathcal{A}}_{1,a}|^{2}
,
|{\mathcal{A}}_{1,b}|^{2}
and
|{\mathcal{A}}_{1,c}|^{2}
. Linear stability analysis shows that the solutions of Eq. (
1
),
{\mathcal{A}}_{1,a}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{1,b}
are stable, and
{\mathcal{A}}_{1,c}
is unstable.

The induced electric transition dipole moment of the oscillator,
d(t)\equiv-ea_{1}(t)
, can be written using Eq. (
3
):
d(t)=\alpha_{1}(\omega)\,E\,e^{-i\omega t}+{\rm c.c.}
. Here the oscillator polarizability at angular frequency
\omega
,
\alpha_{1}(\omega)
, is

\alpha_{1}(\omega)=\frac{e^{2}}{m}\,{\mathcal{A}}_{1}.

(10)

Note that
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
, hence also
\alpha_{1}
, is a nonlinear function of the amplitude of the electric field,
|E|
.

It is well known that the three solutions to the Duffing equation give rise to hysteresis in the nonlinear response for certain parameter values of the Duffing oscillator
Duffing
;
Stoker_50
;
Kazantseva-05
;
Kovacic-11
.

Permittivity, Permeability and Refractive Index
:
Let us consider propagation of a high intensity electromagnetic wave in an isotropic nonlinear medium.
We model the medium as a set of 3D isotropic Duffing oscillators with number density
N
distributed homogeneously in space. The electric field of the electromagnetic wave can be written as

{\bf E}({\bf r},t)=\frac{1}{2}\,E_{\rm loc}({\bf r})\,{\hat{\bf e}}\,e^{-i\omega t+i\Phi({\bf r})}+{\rm c.c.},

(11)

where
E_{\rm loc}({\bf r})
is the real amplitude of the local electric field with frequency
\omega
(i.e., the external electric field plus the electric field created by the transition dipole moments),
\Phi({\bf r})
is the spatially dependent phase, and
{\hat{\bf e}}
is the unit vector in the direction of the polarization of the light. The function
e^{i\Phi({\bf r})}
varies rapidly with
{\bf r}
and has a period of the light wavelength, and
E_{\rm loc}({\bf r})
is a slowly-varying amplitude.

The induced electric transition dipole moment of a Duffing oscillator is

{\bf d}({\bf r},t)=d({\bf r})\,e^{-i\omega t+i\Phi({\bf r})}\,{\hat{\bf e}},

(12)

where

d({\bf r})=-e\,\Gamma\,{\mathcal{A}}_{1}=\alpha_{1}(\omega)\,E_{\rm loc}({\bf r}).

(13)

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
is a solution of Eq. (
4
), and the complex polarizability of the Duffing oscillator
\alpha_{1}(\omega)
is given in Eq. (
10
). Multiplying the left- and right-hand sides of Eq. (
4
) by
-e\,\Gamma\equiv\frac{e^{2}}{m}\,E_{\rm loc}
, one obtains:

\Big\{\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega^{2}-i\,\gamma\,\omega+\frac{3\,\beta}{e^{2}}\,\big|d_{1}\big|^{2}\Big\}\,d_{1}=\frac{e^{2}}{m}\,E_{\rm loc}.

(14)

Solving this equation for the transition dipole moment
d_{1}
, one sees that it is dependent on the following variables:
\omega
,
\omega_{0}
, and
E_{\rm loc}
, i.e.,
d_{1}(\omega,\omega_{0},E_{\rm loc})=\alpha_{1}(\omega,\omega_{0},|E_{\rm loc}|)\,E_{\rm loc}({\bf r})
. Note that the polarizability in Eq. (
10
) depends on the resonance frequency
\omega_{0}
and is a nonlinear function of
|E_{\rm loc}|
.

In Gaussian units, the local electric field
E_{\rm loc}({\bf r})
is expressed in terms of the external electric field
E_{\rm ext}({\bf r})
and the polarization of the medium
P({\bf r})=Nd_{1}({\bf r})
as
Jackson_99

E_{\rm loc}({\bf r})=E_{\rm ext}({\bf r})+\frac{4\pi}{3}\,N\,d({\bf r}).

(15)

Substituting this equation into Eq. (
14
) yields an equation of the same form, except that
E_{\rm loc}
is replaced by
E_{\rm ext}
, and
\omega_{0}
is replaced by the renormalized harmonic resonance frequency,
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
, where

\tilde{\omega}_{0}=\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\omega_{p}^{2}},

(16)

and the plasma frequency squared,
\omega_{p}^{2}
, is

\omega_{p}^{2}=\frac{4\pi Ne^{2}}{m}.

(17)

Equation (
14
) becomes

\Big\{\tilde{\omega}_{0}^{2}-\omega^{2}-i\,\gamma\,\omega+\frac{3\,\beta}{e^{2}}\,\big|d_{1}\big|^{2}\Big\}\,d_{1}=\frac{e^{2}}{m}\,E_{\rm ext}.

(18)

The solution of Eq. (
18
) yields,

d_{1}(\omega,\omega_{0},E_{\rm loc})=\alpha_{1}(\omega,\tilde{\omega}_{0},|E_{\rm ext}|)\,E_{\rm ext}({\bf r}).

(19)

Here
\alpha_{1}
is still given by Eq. (
10
), except that
\omega_{0}
is replaced by
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
and
E_{\rm loc}
is replaced by
E_{\rm ext}
.

The polarization of the medium,
P(\omega)
, can be written as

P(\omega)=\chi(\omega)\,E_{\rm ext},

(20)

where the electric susceptibility is given by

\chi(\omega)=N\,\alpha_{1}(\omega,\tilde{\omega}_{0},|E_{\rm ext}|),

(21)

and the nonlinear permittivity
\epsilon(\omega)
is
Jackson_99

\displaystyle\epsilon(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+4\pi\chi(\omega)=1+4\pi N\,\alpha_{1}(\omega,\tilde{\omega}_{0},|E_{\rm ext}|)

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\omega_{p}^{2}\,{\mathcal{A}}_{1}(\omega,\tilde{\omega}_{0},|E_{\rm ext}|).

Here
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}(\omega,\tilde{\omega}_{0},|E_{\rm ext}|)
is the solution of Eq. (
4
) with
\omega_{0}
replaced by
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
and
E_{\rm loc}
replaced by
E_{\rm ext}
. This generalizes the Clausius-Mossotti relation for the linear permittivity to the nonlinear Duffing permittivity. The permittivity for the linear Lorentz model,
\epsilon_{L}(\omega)
, is found from the Clausius-Mossotti relation
Jackson_99
,

\frac{\epsilon_{L}(\omega)-1}{\epsilon_{L}(\omega)+2}=\frac{4\pi N}{3}\,\alpha_{L}(\omega,\omega_{0}),

(23)

where
\alpha_{L}(\omega,\omega_{0})
is

\alpha_{L}(\omega,\omega_{0})=\frac{e^{2}}{m}\,{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\omega,\omega_{0}),

(24)

and the linear Lorentzian operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\omega,\omega_{0})
is given by Eq. (
5
) (note that
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
depends on
\omega_{0}
). The solution of Eq. (
23
) for
\epsilon_{L}(\omega)
yields

\epsilon_{L}(\omega)=1+\omega_{p}^{2}\,{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\omega,\tilde{\omega}_{0}).

(25)

The Clausius-Mossotti relation results in the replacement of
\omega_{0}
by
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
.

A magnetic dipole transition with resonance frequency

n_{L}(\omega)

\alpha(\omega,E(\omega))=\omega\,{\mathrm{Im}}[n(\omega)]/c

Summary and Conclusion
: Using the Duffing oscillator model for the electric transition dipole moment and the Drude-Lorentz model for the magnetic transition moment, we have shown that for light of sufficient intensity that propagates in an optically homogeneous and isotropic medium, the nonlinear refractive index
n(\omega,E(\omega))
and nonlinear absorption coefficient
\alpha(\omega,E(\omega))
exhibit hysteresis when the intensity and frequency of the light are varied. The nonlinear refractive index can be negative in certain frequency regions and certain intensity regions (this is a generalization of the results in Ref.
Band_24
to the nonlinear regime). Future work will discuss the properties of the time-averaged Poynting vector (i.e., the time-averaged energy flux density), energy density, power dissipation density, phase velocity, and group velocity in the nonlinear regime. We will also generalize to the chiral case.

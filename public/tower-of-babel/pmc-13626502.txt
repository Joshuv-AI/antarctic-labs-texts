Reply to comments on “Staged whole lung lavage in severe pulmonary alveolar proteinosis – Anaesthetic strategies for one-lung ventilation: A case report”
Authors: Ashish Khanna, Nishant Chaudhary
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We thank the correspondents for their insightful comments on our case report of staged whole lung lavage in severe pulmonary alveolar proteinosis.[12]

Regarding extubation despite a partial pressure of oxygen (PaO2)/fraction of inspired oxygen (FiO2) (P/F) ratio of 142 after the first lavage, the decision was based on overall respiratory mechanics and clinical parameters rather than oxygenation alone. Post procedure, the patient demonstrated improved lung compliance, reduced peak airway pressures, stable haemodynamics, normocapnia, and adequate airway reflexes. Considering the risks of prolonged mechanical ventilation and ventilator-associated lung injury, we proceeded with extubation with close monitoring in the intensive care unit. The P/F ratio subsequently improved, supporting this decision.

With respect to the retained lavage fluid of approximately 800 mL and 500 mL after the first and second procedures, we did not observe clinical signs of pulmonary congestion. Bedside lung point-of-care ultrasound was performed and showed stable B-lines without new pleural effusion. Given stable haemodynamics and adequate urine output, escalation of diuretic therapy was not required, and the retained fluid was gradually absorbed over 48 to 72 hours without clinical deterioration.

Post extubation, the patient required a non-rebreather mask at 12 L/minute, which was weaned gradually. Non-invasive ventilation was kept on standby but was not required. The contralateral lavage was scheduled after 3 days based on clinical stability including being off vasopressors and afebrile, radiological improvement on chest radiograph, and improved oxygenation.

We appreciate these comments as they help refine perioperative protocols for this challenging but life-saving procedure.

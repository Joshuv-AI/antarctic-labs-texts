High-Dimensional Sequential Change Detection
Robert Malinas, Dogyoon Song, Benjamin D. Robinson, Alfred O. Hero III

Abstract
We address the problem of detecting a change in the distribution of a high-dimensional multivariate normal time series. Assuming that the post-change parameters are unknown and estimated using a window of historical data, we extend the framework of quickest change detection (QCD) to the high-dimensional setting in which the number of variables increases proportionally with the size of the window used to estimate the post-change parameters. Our analysis reveals that an information theoretic quantity, which we call the Normalized High-Dimensional Kullback-Leibler divergence (NHDKL), governs the high-dimensional asymptotic performance of QCD procedures. Specifically, we show that the detection delay is asymptotically inversely proportional to the difference between the NHDKL of the true post-change versus pre-change distributions and the NHDKL of the true versus estimated post-change distributions. In cases of perfect estimation, where the latter NHDKL is zero, the delay is inversely proportional to the NHDKL between the post-change and pre-change distributions alone. Thus, our analysis is a direct generalization of the traditional fixed-dimension, large-sample asymptotic framework, where the standard KL divergence is asymptotically inversely proportional to detection delay. Finally, we identify parameter estimators that asymptotically minimize the NHDKL between the true versus estimated post-change distributions, resulting in a QCD method that is guaranteed to outperform standard approaches based on fixed-dimension asymptotics.

III
Large-Sample Asymptotic QCD

In practice, the post-change parameters

\mathsf{WADD}(\hat{\tau}_{c,n})=\mathbb{E}_{0}[\hat{\tau}_{c,n}]
for every
n\geq 1
.

Moreover, if
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\geq 1}=(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
n=\mathit{o}\big(\sqrt{b_{n}}\big)\quad\textrm{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{L}_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{n},\mathcal{S}_{n})\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\delta),

where

\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\delta)=\dfrac{\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\delta)}{D_{\infty}[D_{\infty}-\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\delta)]},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\delta)=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[\int

\displaystyle\dfrac{x}{|1-\gamma-\gamma\check{m}_{G}(x)|^{2}\delta(x)+\log(\delta(x))}G(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle-\int\log(y)H(\mathrm{d}y)-1\Bigg].

Furthermore,

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\delta)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}p_{n}^{-1}D(f(\cdot;\boldsymbol{\mu},\mathbf{\Sigma})\|f(\cdot;\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n},\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{n})),

almost surely.

When the parameters are known, the asymptotic tradeoff between the worst average detection delay,
\mathsf{WADD}(\tau_{c,n})
, and the average run length,
\mathsf{ARL}(\tau_{c,n})
, of the CuSum procedure is characterized in (
5
) by the KL divergence
D(f(\cdot;\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{n})\|f_{0})
, as defined in (
4
).
In contrast, when the parameters are estimated in the high-dimensional regime
[Aspect(
\gamma
)]
, there is a loss incurred due to imperfect parameter estimation.
Asymptotically, this loss is captured by the quantity
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\delta),
which we call the normalized high-dimensional KL divergence (NHDKL) between the true post-change distribution and estimated post-change distribution.
Therefore, the loss in performance exhibited in high dimensions is captured by an intuitive information-theoretic loss function.
Similarly, we call
D_{\infty}
the NHDKL between the post-change distribution and pre-change distribution.

Fixing the sample mean estimator and sequence of shrinkage functions
(\delta_{n})_{n\geq 1}
as in Theorem
IV.4
, we have

p_{n}b_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{WADD}(\hat{\tau}_{c,n})=\mathcal{L}_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{n},\mathcal{S}_{n})+p_{n}b_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{WADD}(\tau_{c,n})

by the definition of
\mathcal{L}_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{n},\mathcal{S}_{n})
.
By Theorem
IV.4
, we obtain

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}p_{n}b_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{WADD}(\hat{\tau}_{c,n})\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\delta)+D_{\infty}^{-1},

where we used the fact that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}p_{n}b_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{WADD}(\tau_{c,n})=D_{\infty}^{-1}
by a straightforward extension of (
5
).
By Theorem
IV.4
, this gives

\displaystyle p_{n}b_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{WADD}(\hat{\tau}_{c,n})

\displaystyle\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}\dfrac{\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\delta)}{D_{\infty}[D_{\infty}-\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\delta)]}+\dfrac{1}{D_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=[D_{\infty}-\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\delta)]^{-1}.

Hence, the asymptotic
\mathsf{WADD}
of
\hat{\tau}_{c,n}
is inversely proportional to the difference of the NHDKL of the post-change versus pre-change distributions and the NHDKL of the true vs estimated post-change distributions, directly extending (
5

[Aspect(
\gamma
)]
.
The large-sample limit with fixed dimension
p

\gamma\rightarrow 0.

In this case, the sample mean
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
and sample covariance

Hence,

IV-B

High-Dimensional Asymptotically Optimal QCD Procedure

The rest of the paper is devoted to deriving an asymptotically optimal shrinkage estimator that, together with the sample mean estimator, achieves asymptotically minimum detection delay among all shrinkage estimators in the setting of Theorem
IV.4
.
The following proposition demonstrates that the minimizer of
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}
coincides with that of
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
.

Proposition IV.5
.

Let
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
be limiting shrinkage functions that satisfy
Shrink(
\delta_{1}
)
and
Shrink(
\delta_{2}
)
, respectively.
If

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\delta_{1})\leq\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\delta_{2}),
then

\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\delta_{1})\leq\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\delta_{2}).

Therefore, to optimize performance of
\hat{\tau}_{c,n},
it is sufficient to consider optimization over the set of limiting shrinkage functions
C^{1}\big([0,\infty),(0,\infty)\big)
.
Under the assumptions of Theorem
IV.4
,
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\delta)
is the limit of the inverse Stein’s loss, defined next, between the shrinkage estimate
\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{n}
and the true covariance matrix
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{n}
.

Definition IV.6
.

Define the inverse Stein’s loss

\mathfrak{L}_{n}^{IS}(\boldsymbol{A},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{n})=p_{n}^{-1}\mathrm{tr}\,(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{n}\boldsymbol{A}^{-1})-p_{n}^{-1}\log\left|\mathbf{\Sigma}_{n}\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}\right|-1,

for all
\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{p_{n}\times p_{n}}
, for all
n\geq 1.

Proposition IV.7
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
IV.4
,

\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{L}_{n}^{IS}(\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{n};\mathbf{\Sigma}_{n})\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\delta)\quad\textrm{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

The following covariance estimator, derived in
[
17
]
, is a finite-dimensional approximation to the minimizer of
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}
over
C^{1}\big([0,\infty),(0,\infty)\big)
.

Definition IV.8

(Ledoit-Wolf quadratic inverse Stein’s loss shrinkage estimator (LWISE))
.

For any
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n},
write the singular value decomposition

\mathbf{W}-n^{-1}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{1}_{n}\mathbf{1}_{n}^{\top}=\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\mathbf{V}^{\top},

where
\boldsymbol{\Omega}=\mathrm{diag}(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{\min\{p,n\}})\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
, and let
\gamma_{n}=p/(n-1).

For
p\neq n-1
, the Ledoit-Wolf quadratic inverse Stein’s loss shrinkage function
\delta_{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle\delta^{*}_{n}(\omega)=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\omega^{2}\big[(1-\gamma_{n})^{2}+2\gamma_{n}(1-\gamma_{n})g(\omega)+\gamma_{n}^{2}h(\omega^{-1})\big]^{-1},&p<n-1,\\
\omega^{2}[h(\omega^{-1})]^{-1},&p>n-1,\end{cases}

for all
\omega>0,
where
\gamma_{n}=p/n
,

\displaystyle g(x)=\min\{p,n\}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{p,n\}}\omega_{i}^{-2}\dfrac{\omega_{i}^{-2}-x}{(\omega_{i}^{-2}-x)^{2}+n^{-2/3}\omega_{i}^{-4}},

and

\displaystyle h(x)=g^{2}(x)

\displaystyle+\left[\min\{p,n\}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{p,n\}}\omega_{i}^{-2}\dfrac{n^{-1/3}\omega_{i}^{-2}}{(\omega_{i}^{-2}-x)^{2}+n^{-2/3}\omega_{i}^{-4}}\right]^{2},

for all
x\geq 0.

Finally,

\delta^{*}_{n}(0)=\bigg[(\gamma_{n}-1)\min\{p,n\}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{p,n\}}\omega_{i}^{-2}\bigg]^{-1}.

The following practical theorem states that, with the sample mean estimator fixed, LWISE achieves asymptotically lower
\mathsf{WADD}
than any other shrinkage estimator.
Notably, the sample covariance matrix is a shrinkage estimator, hence LWISE achieves superior high-dimensional performance compared to the the classical MLE.
Therefore, we recommend using the sample mean estimator and LWISE covariance estimator for quickest change detection in the high-dimensional regime
[Aspect(
\gamma
)]
.

V
Conclusion

We addressed the detection of changes in the distribution of a high-dimensional multivariate normal time series by extending classical quickest change detection to scenarios where the number of variables scales with the estimation window size. We introduced the Normalized High-Dimensional Kullback-Leibler divergence (NHDKL) as a key metric governing the asymptotic performance of QCD procedures. Additionally, we identified parameter estimators that minimized this divergence, ensuring superior performance compared to standard approaches derived using fixed-dimension asymptotics.

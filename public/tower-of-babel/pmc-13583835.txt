Feasibility of an N-of-1 trial using a crossover design to evaluate music therapy in patients with breast cancer undergoing adjuvant chemotherapy: a multicentre pilot study protocol in two Italian centres (IMPACT-CASTA DIVA)
Authors: Filippo Giordano, Mariagrazia Baroni, Marzia Zingarelli, Elide Scarlata, Carolina Pelazza, Riccardo Mazzucco, Marco Santi, Giovanni Albini, Mauro Visconti, Maura Rossi, Livio Blasi, Patrizia santinon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionBreast cancer is often accompanied by significant psychological and physical symptoms during chemotherapy, which have a considerable impact on quality of life. Music therapy is a non-pharmacological intervention that can support psychological well-being but the way individuals respond to the treatment can vary considerably. Traditional group-based studies do not allow such variability to be captured. N-of-1 trials, which are based on repeated intra-individual comparisons, offer an innovative way to assess personalised effects. This pilot study aims to evaluate the feasibility of this approach in patients with breast cancer.Methods and analysisThis multicentre pilot study uses an N-of-1 design combined with a crossover design involving 24 patients with breast cancer who are undergoing adjuvant chemotherapy. Participants will be randomised into alternating sequences of phases with and without music therapy each lasting 4 weeks. The intervention consists of weekly 60-minute group sessions incorporating receptive and active techniques as well as personalised playlists. The primary outcomes focus on feasibility, including dropout rate and missing data as well as acceptability. The secondary outcomes include measures of psychological and physical well-being as assessed using validated instruments (State-Trait Anxiety Inventory, Self-Rating Depression Scale, Quality of Life, Emotion Thermometers, Visual Analogue Scale and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index). The analysis will be primarily descriptive, exploring individual trajectories and intra- and inter-patient variability using mixed-effects models.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of AOU Maggiore della Carità Novara (Protocol No. 590/CE/2025) and the Research Ethics Committee of Palermo 2 (Protocol No. 137/2025) and will be conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants. The results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed scientific publications and presentations at national and international scientific conferences.Trial registration numberNCT07549685.

Introduction

Cancer is a major social, public health and economic challenge of the 21st century, accounting for almost one in six (16.8%) deaths worldwide and one in four (22.8%) deaths from non-communicable diseases worldwide.1

In 2020, approximately 2.3 million new cases of breast cancer (BC) were recorded worldwide, accounting for almost a quarter of all cancers in women, with age-standardised incidence and mortality rates of 47.8 and 13.6 per 100 000, respectively.2

In Italy, it is estimated that there will be around 53 686 new diagnoses in 2024, with the vast majority occurring in women, across all age groups.3

Early diagnosis, together with increasingly effective treatment strategies, has contributed to improved survival rates, which currently reach approximately 87% at 5 years after diagnosis.

Despite these advances, patients with BC frequently experience a substantial burden of physical and psychological symptoms throughout the disease trajectory. During diagnosis and treatment, women may experience symptoms related to disease uncertainty, treatment-related side effects and the overall life-threatening nature of cancer.

Anxiety can influence both the surgical experience and the perception of pain,4–6 exposing women to high levels of psychological distress.7

For instance, physical symptoms such as gastrointestinal problems, fatigue and neurological effects are common during chemotherapy, along with psychological symptoms including anxiety (reported in 26.9%–41.55% of patients) and depression.8 These symptoms can drastically reduce quality of life and negatively affect treatment adherence.9

Furthermore, the experience of chemotherapy-induced side effects, such as nausea and vomiting,10 varies widely among patients receiving the same cytotoxic agents,11 suggesting that non-pharmacological factors may play an important role in symptom perception and management.12

Therefore, the management of BC requires an integrated, interdisciplinary approach that addresses not only clinical outcomes but also psychological, social, spiritual and patient needs.13

Music therapy (MT), unlike other music-based approaches, is defined as the clinical and evidence-based use of musical interventions by a qualified music therapist to achieve personalised goals within a therapeutic relationship.14 15 It represents a promising non-pharmacological intervention to address these needs.

Previous evidence suggests that MT may reduce anxiety, stress and depression symptoms while improving overall well-being in patients with BC.16 17

In oncology settings, MT has been shown to improve symptom management and patient-reported outcomes using both receptive and active approaches. It can also be integrated into surgical procedures to manage preoperative and postoperative anxiety effectively.18 19

Group-based MT interventions have demonstrated reductions in anxiety and improvements in mood as well as enhanced social cohesion among hospitalised patients with cancer.20–22

However, responses to MT may vary substantially between individuals, highlighting the need for more personalised approaches to evaluate treatment effects.23

To date, most studies have assessed MT using conventional group-based designs,24 which may overlook intra- and inter-individual variability in response.

N-of-1 trials, characterised by repeated crossover comparisons within individual patients, provide a methodological framework to evaluate personalised treatment effects and variability at the individual level.

Nevertheless, the feasibility of applying N-of-1 trial designs to MT in oncology has not yet been investigated.25

For this reason, a pilot study was considered a necessary methodological step before undertaking a definitive effectiveness trial in women undergoing adjuvant chemotherapy for BC. This study will determine whether the proposed study procedures can be successfully implemented within routine oncology care. As MT is a complex intervention involving multiple interacting clinical and contextual components, this approach is consistent with the current recommendations for the evaluation of complex interventions.26

The primary aim of this pilot study is to assess the feasibility of using an N-of-1 design to evaluate the effects of MT on psychological well-being in patients with BC undergoing adjuvant chemotherapy. In addition, the study will estimate intra- and inter-patient variability to inform future adequately powered trials.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This multicentre, non-profit, interventional pilot study is designed as a series of combined N-of-1 trials. This protocol was prepared in accordance with the SPENT (SPIRIT Extension for N-of-1 Trials) reporting guideline (2019). In this design, participants will alternate between periods during which they receive MT and periods during which they do not receive MT, allowing within-patient comparisons across repeated treatment and non-treatment phases. The study will be conducted at two Italian oncology centres: the SS Antonio e Biagio e C. Arrigo University Hospital in Alessandria and the Civico Di Cristina Benfratelli Hospital in Palermo, between November 2025 and June 2026.

Inclusion criteria

Participants will be eligible for inclusion if they meet all the following criteria: (1) women aged 25–75 years; (2) histologically confirmed diagnosis of BC; (3) undergoing adjuvant chemotherapy following surgery; (4) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0–1; (5) ability to understand the Italian language and comply with study procedures; (6) provision of written informed consent for study participation.

Exclusion criteria

Participants will be excluded if they meet any of the following criteria: (1) active neurological disorders; (2) active psychiatric disorders; (3) current or past substance abuse; (4) significant uncorrected visual, auditory or speech impairments; (5) primary or metastatic brain tumours, as confirmed clinically or radiologically; (6) any condition that, in the opinion of the investigators, may interfere with participation in the study.

Discontinuation criteria

The following reasons will result in discontinuation of patients from the study: (1) failure to meet the inclusion or exclusion criteria; (2) poor compliance with study procedures or failure to comply with study procedures; (3) incomplete medical records; (4) voluntary withdrawal from the study; (5) serious deterioration of disease, severe complications or other clinically relevant physiological changes. Participants may also withdraw if the investigator considers them unsuitable to continue the study.

Participant recruitment

Participants will be recruited from the oncology departments of the participating centres. Eligible patients will be identified by the clinical staff and informed about the study during routine clinical visits. A brief description of the study’s objectives, requirements and methods will be provided during these visits. Patients who express interest in participating will undergo screening to verify eligibility according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Informed consent

All participants will receive detailed information about the study objectives, procedures, potential risks and benefits prior to enrolment. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before any study-related procedures are performed (online supplemental material 1). Participation in the study will be entirely voluntary, and participants will be free to withdraw at any time without affecting their standard clinical care. Personal information will be handled in accordance with applicable data protection regulations. Participants will be assigned a unique study identification code, and access to identifiable information will be restricted to authorised study personnel.

Randomisation and allocation concealment

Participants will be allocated in a 1:1 ratio to one of two sequence groups (GR1 and GR2) using a computer-generated block randomisation scheme, stratified by study centre to ensure balanced group sizes within each site. Given the group-based nature of the MT intervention, randomisation will be performed after enrolment of the planned number of participants at each centre, allowing the formation of balanced groups. Allocation will be concealed using sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes.

The randomisation sequence will be generated by an independent statistician not involved in participant recruitment, assessment or intervention delivery.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this study.

Intervention

MT will be provided in group sessions lasting approximately 60 min, on a weekly basis. Sessions will include both receptive (eg, guided listening and music imagery)27 and active techniques (eg, instrumental or vocal improvisation, songwriting).10 All the sessions will be conducted by qualified music therapists affiliated with the ‘Antonio Vivaldi’ Conservatory of Music in Alessandria and the ‘Alessandro Scarlatti’ Conservatory of Music in Palermo, with support from trained doctoral students when required. At the end of each session, participants receiving MT will be provided with customised playlists prepared by the music therapist. Each playlist will last approximately 20 min and will be designed to support relaxation and improve sleep quality. Participants will be encouraged to listen to the playlists independently between sessions, according to their preferences and clinical needs. Although the intervention follows a common clinical framework, the specific MT methods, musical materials and balance between receptive and active techniques will be individualised and continuously adapted according to each participant’s therapeutic needs, preferences, clinical condition and responses throughout the intervention.

Study procedures

The study consists of four consecutive phases (A-1, B-1, A-2 and B-2), each lasting 4 weeks. Participants will be randomly assigned to two groups (GR1 and GR2), which will alternate between periods with MT and periods without MT according to a crossover design. During MT phases, participants will attend 1 weekly 60 min MT group session. During non-MT phases, participants will not receive any specific intervention but will undergo the same assessments at the same time points (figure 1).

All study assessments will be administered according to a standardised study schedule. Assessments requiring participants’ attendance at the participating centres will be conducted by trained research personnel, whereas remote patient-reported outcome measures will be completed using the REDCap platform.

Baseline assessment (T0)

At enrolment, eligibility criteria will be verified, and a clinical psychologist will explain the nature of the study to participants and obtain their written informed consent. Demographic and clinical data will be collected, including age, ethnicity, disease stage, comorbidities, current oncological treatments and relevant concomitant therapies.

All participants will be required to complete the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI-Y1/Y2),28 29 Self-Rating Depression Scale,30 Quality of Life (QoL),31 Emotion Thermometers (ET),32 Visual Analogue Scale (VAS)33 and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index,34 which will serve as baseline measures for subsequent within-patient comparisons.

Intervention phases

During MT phases (A-1 and B-1), participants assigned to the intervention group will attend weekly sessions. Questionnaires (short STAI version, ET and VAS) will be administered approximately 30 min before and 5 min after each session to evaluate immediate changes associated with the intervention

During non-intervention phases, participants will not receive MT but will complete the same assessments remotely using the secure Research Electronic Data Capture35 platform according to the predefined study schedule. No additional visits to the participating centres will be required during these phases.

Scheduled assessments

Scheduled assessments will be conducted at the end of each phase: T1 (4 weeks); T2 (8 weeks); T3 (16 weeks, end of study). At each time point, participants will complete the same set of questionnaires administered at baseline. At the end of the study, qualitative data will be collected through semi-structured interviews and/or focus groups to explore participants’ experiences and perceptions of the intervention. The interviews will explore the acceptability of the intervention and study procedures, participants’ perceived benefits and challenges, and their overall experience of participating in the study. A draft semi-structured interview guide is provided as online supplemental material 2.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome of this study is the feasibility of using an N-of-1 trial design to evaluate MT in patients with BC undergoing adjuvant chemotherapy.

Feasibility will be assessed using multiple complementary indicators. These include the dropout rate, defined as the proportion of participants who discontinue the study for reasons not attributable to clinical conditions; the completeness and consistency of patient-reported outcome data collection; the occurrence of any interference between the intervention procedures and routine clinical activities of the oncology units or healthcare staff; and participants’ acceptability of the intervention and study procedures, which will be explored through semi-structured interviews conducted at the end of the study.

Secondary outcomes will include changes in psychological and physical well-being, assessed using validated patient-reported outcome measures (table 1).

Quality control

To guarantee the quality and the accuracy of the study procedures and data collection, research assistants will regularly review study documents, informed consent forms, case report forms (CRFs) and data records. These reviews will verify the completeness and accuracy of informed consent documentation, the completeness of CRFs, the consistency between source documents and recorded data, adherence to the study protocol and the timely identification and resolution of missing or inconsistent data. Any discrepancies identified during these reviews will be discussed with the study investigators and corrected where appropriate.

Data management

The CRFs and scales will be reviewed by the principal investigator and research assistant before they are handed over to the data management staff for data entry and administration. The Department of Oncology at Alessandria will be storing the original CRFs and all scales (including consent forms). The Data and Safety Monitoring Board in Alessandria Hospital will review study conduct and participants’ safety monthly. Access to the final study dataset will be restricted to the principal investigator and authorised members of the research team involved in data analysis. No contractual agreements will limit investigators’ access to the final dataset.

Sample size

This study is designed as a pilot feasibility study. A total of 24 participants will be enrolled, with 12 participants from each centre. Due to the exploratory nature of the study, and in accordance with current methodological recommendations for pilot and feasibility studies, a formal sample size calculation was not performed. Instead, the target sample size was determined based on both methodological and pragmatic considerations. Specifically, enrolling 12 participants per centre was considered appropriate to estimate key feasibility parameters while ensuring the organisational feasibility of delivering the intervention across the two participating centres within the available project resources and study timeline.

From a methodological perspective, the selected sample is intended to provide preliminary estimates of recruitment, retention, data collection completeness, intervention acceptability and within- and between-patient variability in patient-reported outcomes36–38 rather than to detect treatment effects.

The results of these estimates will inform the optimisation of study procedures and the design and sample size calculation for future studies with adequate statistical power. As this is a pilot study, the emphasis is on feasibility rather than formal hypothesis testing. This approach is consistent with the primary purpose of pilot studies, which is to generate feasibility data to inform the design of a future definitive trial rather than to formally evaluate treatment efficacy.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics will be used to summarise the baseline demographic and clinical characteristics. Continuous variables will be reported as the mean and SD or the median and IQR depending on their distribution. Categorical variables will be presented as frequencies and percentages. Feasibility outcomes will be analysed descriptively. Dropout rates and the proportion of missing data will be calculated relative to the number of enrolled participants. Participants’ acceptability will be summarised using qualitative and descriptive approaches.

The effects of MT will be explored by graphically representing individual patient trajectories and comparing outcome measures during intervention and non-intervention periods.

Intra- and inter-patient variability in treatment effects will be analysed using mixed-effects models that account for repeated measures within individuals and the crossover design. These analyses will be exploratory and hypothesis-generating.

All analyses will be performed using standard statistical software (eg, R, Stata or SPSS version 32).

Ethics and dissemination

The study will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the Ethical Guidelines for Clinical Research. Ethical approval was obtained from the Research Ethics Committee of AOU Maggiore della Carità Novara (Protocol No. 590/CE/2025) and the Research Ethics Committee of Palermo 2 (Protocol No.137/2025). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before enrolment. The study findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and presentations at national and international scientific conferences regardless of the study outcomes.

Discussion

This study is one of the first to use an N-of-1 trial design to evaluate MT for patients with BC undergoing adjuvant chemotherapy. It focuses on a methodological approach centred on tailoring support interventions. This makes it possible to explore individual responses to MT in a systematic and longitudinal manner. This study addresses an important gap in personalised supportive care research. The multicentre design increases the feasibility and generalisability of the study across different clinical settings. Additionally, repeated within-patient measurements enable a thorough evaluation of variability in treatment response at the individual and group levels, a dimension that traditional group-based study designs fail to capture. Our approach enables the incorporation of intra-individual variability into clinical assessment models, contributing to the development of more sensitive approaches to individual response trajectories. Providing MT in a group setting reflects routine clinical practice and improves the intervention’s ecological validity. At the same time, it strikes a balance between personalisation and the organisational sustainability of the intervention in real-world settings.39

However, as a pilot study with a relatively small sample size, it is not adequately powered to detect definitive treatment effects. Therefore, the results will primarily be interpreted in terms of feasibility and hypothesis generation rather than clinical efficacy.40

Additionally, the absence of blinding may introduce bias in patient-reported outcomes. These aspects are inherent to pragmatic studies and must be carefully considered when interpreting the collected data.

The complexity of the N-of-1 crossover design may affect participant adherence and data completeness.

From a practical standpoint, implementing this study could present several operational challenges. First, coordinating participants’ schedules for the group MT sessions can be challenging for patients undergoing chemotherapy due to treatment-related fatigue, clinical appointments and potential side effects.

Second, it can be difficult for participants to maintain adherence across multiple phases, particularly given the repeated assessments and the length of the study. Completing multiple patient-reported outcome measures at different time points may also impact data completeness. These challenges are key factors in assessing feasibility, and they may also provide useful insights for optimising future, larger-scale studies.

Furthermore, the multicentre design requires careful coordination between centres to ensure consistency in intervention delivery, data collection procedures and adherence to the study protocol. Variability in clinical settings and organisational workflows may affect the feasibility of implementing a standardised intervention. However, this diversity reflects the real-life conditions of clinical practice, thereby reinforcing the practical relevance of the findings.

Finally, as MT is delivered in a group setting, individual preferences and group dynamics may influence engagement and perceived benefit, introducing further variability that must be considered when interpreting results. This variability is not just a limitation: it also provides valuable insights into the mechanisms underlying responses to MT in complex settings. Overall, this study offers preliminary insights into the feasibility and usefulness of an N-of-1 design for evaluating MT in BC. This could contribute to the development of more patient-centred research models that are aligned with personalised medicine.

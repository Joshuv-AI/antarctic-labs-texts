Derived equivalence for the simple flop of type D 5 D_{5}
Marco Rampazzo, Ying Xie

Abstract
. We prove that every simple flop of type D 5 D_{5} , i.e., resolved by blowups with exceptional divisor isomorphic to a generalized Grassmann bundle with fiber O ​ G ​ ( 4 , 10 ) OG(4,10) , induces a derived equivalence. This provides new evidence for the DK conjecture of Bondal–Orlov and Kawamata. The proof is based on a sequence of mutations of exceptional objects: we use the same argument to prove derived equivalence for some pairs of non-birational Calabi–Yau fivefolds in O ​ G ​ ( 5 , 10 ) OG(5,10) , related to Manivel’s double–spinor Calabi–Yau varieties. We extend the construction to prove the derived equivalence of Calabi–Yau fibrations, which are described as zero loci in some generalized Grassmann bundles.



1.
Introduction

The relation of derived categories with birational geometry is still considered to be somewhat mysterious: while the famous reconstruction theorem by Bondal and Orlov depicts a clear picture for Fano and general type varieties
[
BO01
]
,
K
-trivial manifolds appear to behave in a totally different manner. Derived Torelli-type statements are well known for K3 surfaces
[
Orl03
]
, and recently for some hyperkähler varieties
[
KK23
]
, but examples of pairs of Calabi–Yau varieties of dimension higher than two which are derived equivalent but not birational have been found
[
BC00
,
OR18
,
BCP18
,
Kuz18
]
.

Birational maps preserving the derived category

While the derived category is far from being a birational invariant in general, following the works of Bondal–Orlov and Kawamata, it is widely expected that a certain class of birational transformations called
K-equivalences
induce a derived equivalence. This is the content of the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1

(DK conjecture
[
BO02
,
Kaw02
]
)
.

Consider a birational map
\mu:X_{1}\longrightarrow X_{2}
of smooth varieites, which is resolved by morphisms
f_{i}:X_{0}\longrightarrow X_{i}
such that
f_{1}^{*}K_{X_{1}}
and
f_{2}^{*}K_{X_{2}}
are linearly equivalent (i.e. a K-equivalence). Then
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are derived equivalent.

While the typical examples of K-equivalences are standard flops and Mukai flops, many more examples can be constructed from the geometry of homogeneous varieties, the so-called
generalized Grassmann flops

[
LX19
]
. In Section
2
we provide a new class of generalized Grassmann flops which satisfy Conjecture
1.1
.

Double mirror Calabi–Yau manifolds

The framework of homological mirror symmetry is based on a famous conjecture, which relates the derived category of coherent sheaves of a given variety
X_{1}
to a suitably defined category generated by vanishing cycles on a second variety
X_{2}
, the
mirror
of
X_{1}
. While for Fano varieties, the mirror is understood in terms of a fibration (the preimage of a superpotential), it is expected that in the Calabi–Yau case, following the “classical” mirror symmetry statements, the mirror should be a Calabi–Yau variety even in the homological/derived setting. This picture becomes more complicated if we consider the case of two non-birational Calabi–Yau varieties in the same family, which are derived equivalent: it is expected the existence of two local mirror maps in the neighborhood of different maximally unipotent monodromy points to two different large complex structure points in the mirror family. For this motivation, pairs of non birational, derived equivalent Calabi–Yau varieties are called
double mirrors
. These pairs are rare, and only a handful of constructions are known. In Section
3
, we provide a new example of double mirror pair of Calabi–Yau fivefolds. These varieties are elements of a divisor in a family of intersections of orthogonal Grassmannians studied by Manivel
[
Man19
]
.

The geometric constructions underlying the flops in Section
2
and the pairs of double mirrors in section
3
are remarkably similar. Fix a vector space
V_{10}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{10}
and a nondegenerate quadratic form
q
on
V_{10}
. Consider the orthogonal Grassmannian
OG(4,V_{10})
of
q
-isotropic
4
-spaces in
V_{10}
: this variety admits two projective bundle structures over the two connected components of
OG(5,V_{10})
(see Section
2.1
). The class of generalized flops we address is given by any flop
\mu:X_{+}\longrightarrow X_{-}
resolved by two blowups such that the exceptional divisor of both blowups is isomorphic to
OG(4,V_{10})
(see Section
2.2
for the detailed construction). Then, we generalize to a “relative” construction over a smooth projective base
B
: Let
G
be the simple group of Dynkin type
D_{5}
, and call
P_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
the parabolic subgroup associated to the choice of the simple roots
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
. The choice of a principal
G
-bundle
{\mathcal{P}}\longrightarrow B
on a smooth projective base
B
defines a locally trivial fibration
{\mathcal{P}}\times^{G}G/P_{4,5}\longrightarrow B
, with fibers isomorphic to
OG(4,V_{10})
. Our result extends to all flops resolved by two blowups with exceptional divisor isomorphic to
{\mathcal{P}}\times^{G}G/P_{4,5}\longrightarrow B
(the details of this construction are given in Section
2.5
). We prove the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mu:X_{+}\longrightarrow X_{-}
be a flop as above. Then, there is an equivalence of categories

D(X_{+})\cong D(X_{-}).

For clarity of exposition, the proof will be first presented for the case
B=\{pt\}
and then extended to the general case.

Let us now turn our attention to double mirrors. Note that a
(1,1)
- section of
OG(4,V_{10})
admits two extremal contractions, again over the two connected components of
OG(5,V_{10})
, with general fibers isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. For each of the two contractions, the fiber jumps to a
\mathbb{P}^{4}
exactly over a Calabi–Yau fivefold, and thus, the choice of the
(1,1)
- section, under the assumption of generality, defines a pair of fivefolds. In Section
3
we will give the details of this constriction, and prove that a general pair of such fivefolds is not birational but derived equivalent. As for the flops, we generalize this picture by replacing the orthogonal Grassmannian
OG(4,V_{10})
with an orthogonal Grassmann bundle
{\mathcal{P}}\times^{G}G/P_{4,5}
, describing pairs of derived equivalent Calabi–Yau fibrations over
B
. The notation of the second point of the following theorem is clarified in Section
2.5
.

Theorem 1.3
.

The following statements are true:

(1)

Consider the projections
p_{\pm}:OG(4,V_{10})\longrightarrow OG(5,V_{10})^{\pm}
, and a general section
s\in H^{0}(OG(4,V_{10}),{\mathcal{O}}(1,1))
. Then, the two zero loci
Y_{\pm}\subset OG(5,V_{10})^{\pm}
of
p_{\pm*}s
are derived equivalent, non birational Calabi–Yau fivefolds.

(2)

Fix a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
on
{\mathcal{P}}\times^{G}G/P_{4,5}
such that the restriction to every fiber over
B
is surjective on global sections. Then, a general section of
{\mathcal{L}}
defines a pair of derived equivalent fibrations over
B
, such that the general fiber of each is a smooth Calabi–Yau fivefold.

In the proofs of both theorems, the arguments for derived equivalence follow from a sequence of mutations of exceptional objects. In particular, a consequence of the similarity of the two constructions is the fact that the pattern of mutations is essentially the same. This feature is expected to hold for a wider class of flops/double mirror candidates. In fact, these parallel constructions can be carried out for any homogeneous variety of Picard number two admitting two projective bundle structures
[
Ram20
]
. Such homogeneous varieties have been classified by Kanemitsu
[
Kan22
]
. While several cases have already been settled
[
Seg16
,
Kuz18
,
KR19
,
KR22
,
Xie24
]
, a general strategy to understand the problem is still missing.

2.
The flop of type
D_{5}

2.1.
Orthogonal Grassmannians and roofs

Let us call
G(k,V_{n})
the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional subspaces of a fixed vector space
V_{n}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We call
\mathcal{U}
the tautological bundle of
G(k,V_{n})
, i.e., the rank
k
vector bundle whose total space is described by the following incidence correspondence:

\mathcal{U}=\{(x,v)\in G(k,V_{n})\times V_{n}:v\in x\},

where we identify points in
G(k,V_{n})
with the corresponding
k
-spaces in
V_{n}
.

The orthogonal Grassmannian
OG(k,V_{2n})
is the zero locus of a general section of
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\mathcal{U}^{\vee}
in
G(k,V_{2n})
for any
k\leq n
. If we fix
k=n
, such variety has two isomorphic connected components
OG(n,V_{2n})^{\pm}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2^{n-1}-1}
, each of them is called a
spinor variety
. As rational homogeneous varieties, spinor varieties are described as
OG(n,V_{2n})^{+}=D_{n}/P_{n-1}
and
OG(n,V_{2n})^{-}=D_{n}/P_{n}
, i.e., they are the quotients of the simple Lie group of Dynkin type
D_{n}
(i.e.,
\operatorname{Spin}(2n)
) by the parabolic subgroups associated to the last two simple roots in the Bourbaki indexing convention.

More geometrically, the varieties
OG(n,V_{2n})^{\pm}
are maximal isotropic (orthogonal) Grassmannians, i.e., they describe the two disjoint families of
n-1
-dimensional linear spaces contained in a quadric hypersurface
Q_{2n-2}\in\mathbb{P}^{2n-1}
. Observe that any
\mathbb{P}^{n-2}\subset Q_{n-2}
can be extended to an isotropic
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
in exactly two ways: this gives
OG(n-1,V_{2n})
two projective bundle structures
p_{\pm}:OG(n-1,V_{2n})\longrightarrow OG(n,V_{2n})^{\pm}
, both with the same relative dimension
n-1
and the same relative ample generator
\mathcal{O}(1,1):=p_{+}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)\otimes p_{-}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)
. Homogeneous varieties with these properties are called
roofs
, and they have been classified by Kanemitsu
[
Kan22
, Example 5.12]
: in his notation,
OG(n-1,V_{2n})
is known as the roof of type
D_{n}
.

2.3.
Cohomology computations

This section is preparatory to the main proof. We address here the cohomology computations needed to perform the mutations in the next section, presenting the main techniques with explicit computations. The principal tool will be reducing the problem to computing cohomology of homogeneous irreducible vector bundles. For this purpose, let us fix a set of fundamental weights
\{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{5}\}
for the simple algebraic group of type
D_{5}
. Then, homogeneous irreducible bundles on
OG(4,V_{10})
will be denoted by
{\mathcal{E}}_{a_{1}\omega_{1}+\dots+a_{5}\omega_{5}}
, where
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
are nonnegative, while on
\mathbb{S}_{+}
(resp.
\mathbb{S}_{-})
the fourth (resp. fifth) coordinate will have to be nonnegative as well. In particular, one has the following identities on
OG(4,V_{10})
:

(2.5)

\begin{split}\wedge^{k}{\mathcal{U}}_{4}^{\vee}(a,b)&={\mathcal{E}}_{\omega_{k}+a\omega_{4}+b\omega_{5}}\hskip 5.0pt\text{for}\hskip 5.0pt1\leq k\leq 3,\\
\wedge^{4}{\mathcal{U}}_{4}^{\vee}(a,b)&={\mathcal{E}}_{(a+1)\omega_{4}+(b+1)\omega_{5}}\hskip 5.0pt,\\
\operatorname{Sym}^{k}{\mathcal{U}}_{4}^{\vee}(a,b)&={\mathcal{E}}_{k\omega_{1}+a\omega_{4}+b\omega_{5}}\hskip 5.0pt\text{for}\hskip 5.0ptk\geq 0,\end{split}

and the following on
\mathbb{S}_{+}
:

(2.6)

\begin{split}\wedge^{k}{\mathcal{U}}_{+}^{\vee}(a,0)&={\mathcal{E}}_{\omega_{k}+a\omega_{4}}\hskip 5.0pt\text{for}\hskip 5.0pt1\leq k\leq 3,\\
\wedge^{4}{\mathcal{U}}_{+}^{\vee}(a,0)&={\mathcal{E}}_{(a+1)\omega_{4}+b\omega_{5}}\hskip 5.0pt,\\
\wedge^{5}{\mathcal{U}}_{+}^{\vee}(a,0)&={\mathcal{E}}_{(a+2)\omega_{4}}\hskip 5.0pt,\\
\operatorname{Sym}^{k}{\mathcal{U}}_{+}^{\vee}(a,0)&={\mathcal{E}}_{k\omega_{1}+a\omega_{4}}\hskip 5.0pt\text{for}\hskip 5.0ptk\geq 0.\end{split}

Clearly, similar identities hold for
{\mathcal{U}}_{-}
on
\mathbb{S}_{-}
with the roles of
\omega_{4}
and
\omega_{5}
swapped. Since the Levi subgroup of
OG(4,V_{10})
is isomorphic to
SL(4)\times\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
, and the one of
\mathbb{S}_{\pm}
is isomorphic to
SL(5)\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
, tensor products of the bundles above can be themselves decomposed in semisimple factors with the usual Littlewood–Richardson formula. Cohomology is easily computed by the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem in an algorithmic way:

(1)

If
\lambda
has no negative entries, the bundle is globally generated, and its only nonvanishing cohomology is
H^{0}(E,{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda})=\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}^{D_{5}}
.

(2)

Otherwise, consider the vector
\lambda+\rho
where
\rho=(1,1,1,1,1)
and apply the Weyl reflection corresponding to the leftmost negative entry in the vector. If
(\lambda+\rho)_{i}=0
for some
i
and
(\lambda+\rho)_{j}\geq 0
for all
j\neq i
, then
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}
has no cohomology. If not, repeat the last step until either we satisfy this condition (and the bundle has no cohomology) or we obtain a vector
\widetilde{\lambda}
with all strictly positive entries.

(3)

If we get a vector
\lambda
with all strictly positive entries after at least an iteration of (2), the only nonvanishing contribution to cohomology is
H^{d}(E,{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda})=\mathbb{V}_{\widetilde{\lambda}-\rho}^{D_{5}}
where
d
is the number of Weyl reflections to obtain
\widetilde{\lambda}
, i.e, the number of iterations of step (2).

Explicitly, Weyl reflections act on a weight vector as follows:

\begin{split}s_{1}:(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{5})&\longrightarrow(-a_{1},a_{1}+a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{5}),\\
s_{2}:(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{5})&\longrightarrow(a_{1}+a_{2},-a_{2},a_{2}+a_{3},a_{4},a_{5}),\\
s_{3}:(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{5})&\longrightarrow(a_{1},a_{2}+a_{3},-a_{3},a_{3}+a_{4},a_{3}+a_{5}),\\
s_{4}:(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{5})&\longrightarrow(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}+a_{4},-a_{4},a_{5}),\\
s_{5}:(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{5})&\longrightarrow(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}+a_{5},a_{4},-a_{5}).\end{split}

This elementary algorithm can be easily automatized: see, for instance, the script
[
Ram22
]
for a basic Python implementation.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any sheaves
F,G
, one can resolve
\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{m}(j_{*}F,j_{*}G)
by means of the following exact sequence:

\cdots\longrightarrow H^{m}(E,F^{\vee}\otimes G(-1,-1))\longrightarrow H^{m}(E,F^{\vee}\otimes G)\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{m}(j_{*}F,j_{*}G)\longrightarrow\cdots.

Proof.

By adjunction, one has:

\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{m}(j_{*}F,j_{*}G)\simeq\operatorname{Ext}_{E}^{m}(j^{*}j_{*}F,G).

Since
j
is an embedding of a codimension one variety, it comes with the following distinguished triangle for any
F\in D^{b}(E)
(see
[
Huy06
, Corollary 11.4(ii)]
for details):

F\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1,1)[1]\longrightarrow i^{*}i_{*}F\longrightarrow F.

It induces a long exact sequence of Ext spaces:

\cdots\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{E}^{m}(F,G)\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{E}^{m}(i^{*}i_{*}F,G)\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{E}^{m+1}(F(1,1),G)\longrightarrow\cdots,

and hence the proof is concluded.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For
-4\leq a\leq-1
, any sheaf on
E
of the form
p_{\pm}^{*}{\mathcal{F}}\otimes p_{\mp}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(a)
has no cohomology.

Proof.

By the Leray spectral sequence, we just need to prove that the pushforward of such bundle along
p_{\pm}
is identically zero. The computation can be handled locally: for every
x\in Y_{\pm}
one has:

\begin{split}\left(p_{\pm*}(p_{\pm}^{*}{\mathcal{F}}\otimes p_{\mp}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(a))\right)_{x}\simeq\left({\mathcal{F}}\otimes p_{\pm*}p_{\mp}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(a)\right)_{x}\\
\simeq H^{\bullet}(p_{\pm}^{-1}(x),{\mathcal{F}}_{x}\otimes p_{\mp}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(a)|_{p_{\pm}^{-1}(x)}\\
\simeq{\mathcal{F}}_{x}\otimes H^{\bullet}(\mathbb{P}^{4},{\mathcal{O}}(a))=0.\end{split}

∎

.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
G/P
be a rational homogeneous variety, and
X={\mathcal{P}}\times^{G}G/P
a flag bundle over a smooth projective variety
B
. Consider a triple
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}
of homogeneous, exceptional vector bundles (though not necessarily homogeneous irreducible) on
G/P
, such that they satisfy the following relation in
D^{b}(G/P)
:

\mathbb{L}_{V_{1}}V_{2}\simeq V_{3}.

Then, one also has:

\mathbb{L}_{\mathfrak{F}(V_{i})}\mathfrak{F}(V_{j})\simeq\mathfrak{F}(V_{k}).

Proof.

Consider the functor:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{V_{i}}:D^{b}(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}E\otimes\mathfrak{F}(V_{i}).}

Then, the left mutation of
V_{j}
through
V_{i}
is defined by the following distinguished triangle:

(2.15)

f_{V_{i}}f_{V_{i}}^{!}\mathfrak{F}(V_{j})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{F}(V_{j})\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}_{\mathfrak{F}(V_{i})}\mathfrak{F}(V_{j})\longrightarrow f_{V_{i}}f_{V_{i}}^{!}\mathfrak{F}(V_{j})[1]

where
f_{V_{i}}^{!}
is the right adjoint functor of
f_{V_{i}}
. Explicitly, one has:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{V_{i}}^{!}:D^{b}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}R{\mathcal{H}}om_{X}(\mathfrak{F}(V_{i}),W).}

Evaluating the latter functor on
\mathfrak{F}(V_{j})
yields:

\begin{split}\pi_{*}R{\mathcal{H}}om_{X}(\mathfrak{F}(V_{i}),\mathfrak{F}(V_{j}))&\simeq H^{\bullet}(G/P,R{\mathcal{H}}om_{G/P}(V_{i},\mathfrak{F}^{!}\mathfrak{F}(V_{j})))\\
&\simeq H^{\bullet}(G/P,R{\mathcal{H}}om_{G/P}(V_{i},V_{j}))\\
&\simeq RHom_{G/P}(V_{i},V_{j}).\\
\end{split}

Plugging this result into the triangle
2.15
gives:

(2.16)

RHom_{G/P}(V_{i},V_{j})\otimes\mathfrak{F}(V_{j})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{F}(V_{j})\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}_{\mathfrak{F}(V_{i})}\mathfrak{F}(V_{j})\longrightarrow RHom_{G/P}(V_{i},V_{j})\otimes\mathfrak{F}(V_{j})[1].

On the other hand, consider the triangle defining

in

(2.17)

RHom_{G/P}(V_{i},V_{j})\otimes V_{j}\longrightarrow V_{j}\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}_{V_{i}}V_{j}\longrightarrow RHom_{G/P}(V_{i},V_{j})\otimes V_{)}[1].

The proof is completed once we note that the triangle
2.16
is the result of applying the (triangulated) functor
\mathfrak{F}
to the triangle
2.17
.
∎

3.

D_{5}

Consider a general section

Remark 3.1
.

As pointed out in
[
Man19
]
, zero loci of general sections of

in

are deformation-equivalent to double-spinor Calabi–Yau fivefolds, which are intersections of the spinor tenfold with its image under a general automorphism of its ambient

{\mathcal{U}}_{-}(0,2)
in
\mathbb{S}_{-}
. In particular, the family of Calabi–Yau varieties of type

is not locally complete, and it describes a divisor in the (locally complete) family of double-spinor Calabi–Yau fivefolds. Calabi–Yau varieties of type

are zero loci of the normal bundle of

in
\mathbb{P}^{15}

in its Plücker embedding
[

,
BCP18
]
: elements of a divisor in such family of Calabi–Yau threefolds can be described as zero loci of the normal bundle of

in
\mathbb{P}^{9}
.

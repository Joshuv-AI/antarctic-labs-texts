Author’s Reply to “Artificial intelligence for atrial fibrillation episode classification: Concerns about interpretation and validation”
Authors: Ulrika Birgersdotter-Green, Fujian Qu, Vishnu Charan
Published: 2026

We read with interest the Letter to the Editor in response to our recent publication. We thank Huang et al1 for their thoughtful and constructive comments regarding our study on evaluating the atrial fibrillation (AF) detection performance of the Assert-IQ insertable cardiac monitor (ICM).

The artificial intelligence (AI) model was evaluated exclusively on AF episodes detected by the Assert-IQ devices, as its sole intended purpose is as a component of the ICM system. We therefore agree that the reported sensitivity should be interpreted for the complete ICM system rather than the AI model alone. The model has not been assessed on continuous single-lead electrocardiographic recordings, as it is not designed for this purpose. Instead, it serves as a secondary discriminator to address the issue of false-positive AF episode detections generated by the device. Our results demonstrate that the model is highly effective in reducing false positives while preserving all true positives identified by Assert-IQ devices. In addition, Table 2 in our article reports the complete AF detection performance of both the original ICM system (ie, without the AI model) and the upgraded system incorporating the AI model.

Huang et al also pointed out that the development and validation were insufficiently described in our article. We agree that this information is important for both researchers and clinicians. However, this was not the primary focus of our study, and providing these details in greater depth would have detracted from the main objectives of the article, as it would require inclusion of additional data, processes, and methods unrelated to the patients enrolled in this study. The AI model was developed in accordance with the most recent Food and Drug Administration guidance documents as of January 2025, as well as standards-based recommendations for AI-enabled medical device software functions issued by various regulatory agencies. The model threshold was established after training and fixed before performance evaluation. Model performance was subsequently validated using an independent data set comprising randomly selected, previously unseen patients implanted with an Assert-IQ ICM for any monitoring indication. Additional details regarding model validation are provided in the Merlin.net VDE MN7000 AI for use with Assert-IQ Insertable Cardiac Monitors User’s Manual and in the FDA 510(k) Clearance Letter (submission K251221).

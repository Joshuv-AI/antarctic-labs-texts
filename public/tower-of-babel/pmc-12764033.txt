Protocol for a randomized controlled trial for comparison of self- and balloon-expandable valves in patients with Ascending Aortic Dilation Undergoing Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (AAD-CHOICE)
Authors: Kang An, Fengwen Zhang, Wenbin Ouyang, Shiguo Li, Xiangbin Pan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSelf-expandable valves (SEVs) and balloon-expandable valves (BEVs) are the most widely used transcatheter heart valves. In patients with ascending aortic dilation (AAD) who undergo transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR), comparison of two type of valves is lacking.MethodsThe AAD-CHOICE trial is a multi-center, randomized controlled, open-label study. The trial will randomly assign 100 patients with AAD (ascending aortic diameter ≥ 45 mm) who undergo TAVR in 1:1 ratio to either SEV group or BEV group. Participants will be followed for a minimum of 1 year. The primary end point is device success, 30-day all-cause mortality and 30-day adverse aortic events. The key secondary end point is 1-year all-cause mortality, 1-year cardiovascular mortality, adverse aortic events, major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events, and aortic expansion rate ≥ 3 mm/year.DiscussionThe study will provide evidence regarding the performance of SEVs and BEVs in patients with AAD who undergo TAVR.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov NCT06009588. Registered on August 12, 2023.

Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Ascending aortic dilation (AAD) is a common feature in patients with aortic stenosis (AS), especially in those with bicuspid aortic valve (BAV) [1]. According to the 2022 AHA/ACC Guideline for the Diagnosis and Management of Aortic Disease, in patients undergoing surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) who have a concomitant AAD with a maximum diameter of ≥ 45 mm, ascending aortic replacement is reasonable when performed by experienced surgeons in a Multidisciplinary Aortic Team (Class 2a, Level B-NR) [2].

Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) has profoundly changed the clinical management of AS patients who cannot tolerate SAVR. Recently published retrospective studies have reported that the AAD did not affect the acute procedural success or increase the incidence of adverse aortic events [3–5]. Among the transcatheter heart valves (THVs), the self-expandable valves (SEVs) and the balloon-expandable valves (BEVs) were the most widely used. A SEV has long stent frame, which extends beyond the sinotubular junction into the AA. Although the inflow portion of the stent frame exerts high radial force for anchoring, the outflow portion also helps secure the SEV in the AA [6, 7]. In patients with AAD, the reduced anchoring of the outflow portion might raise the concern for TVH malposition. On the other hand, a BEV has a short stent frame which does not extend beyond the aortic sinus. The performance of two types of valves was compared in several clinical trials [8–10], but a randomized comparison in patients with AAD is lacking. Therefore, we designed this multicenter prospective randomized controlled trial, aiming to assess the comparative performance of two type of valves in patients with AAD.

Objectives {7}

The AAD-CHOICE trial is designed to compare the performance of SEVs and BEVs in patients with AAD.

Trial design {8}

AAD-CHOICE trial is a multi-center, open-label, two-arm, single-blinded randomized control trial that compare the device success, all-cause mortality, and adverse aortic events in patients with ascending aortic diameter between 45 and 54 mm undergoing TAVR using SEV or BEV. Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

Currently, the SEVs and the BEVs are the most widely used THVs during the TAVR procedure. The studies comparing the performance of two types of THVs in patients with AAD are lacking.

Intervention description {11a}

The decision to perform TAVR was made by a multidisciplinary team at each center. Eligible patients were randomized 1:1 to SEV group or BEV group. All TAVR procedures will be performed with transfemoral access in accordance with guidelines using standard techniques. Details of the implantation technique have been previously reported [4, 5].

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

Participants are allowed to discontinue participation and request to withdraw from the study at any time. Researchers can decide to terminate the study when encountering emergent circumstances or other medical conditions that may harm the patients by continued participation in the study.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

A standard operating procedure was performed for all participants. Adherence to protocol will be indicated by attending all the scheduled follow-up visits and undergoing planned exams.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

The study will include participants with age ≥ 65 years. Comorbidities are common in this patient group, such as hypertension, diabetes mellitus, coronary artery disease, and others. Participants receiving concomitant pharmacotherapy will be encouraged to continue with medical treatment.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

The participants will be closed monitored throughout the postoperative and follow-up period. We will focus on the changes of ascending aortic diameters to identify potential risk of adverse aortic events, and implement intervention measures as necessary.

Sample size {14}

The sample size calculation is based on the primary end point device success according to previously published data and our own clinical experience. To date, there have been no randomised controlled studies comparing the SEVs and BEVs in TAVR patients with AA dilation. The CHOICE study, a randomized clinical trial which aimed to determine whether the BEV (Edwards Sapien XT) is associated with a better success rate than the SEV (Medtronic CoreValve). The device success rate was 95.9% in BEV group and 77.5% in SEV group (P < 0.001) [12]. However, these THVs were early-generation valves, and their performance in patients with AAD remained unknown. Our recent retrospective study has compared the efficacy of SEV and BEV in patients with AAD using new-generation THVs, and we found that the device success was 96% in BEV group and 84% in SEV group [4]. The power calculation was based on the following assumptions: an incidence of device success of 84% in the SEV group and an incidence of 96% in the BEV group, a power of 80%, and an α level of 0.05 (two-sided). With a preset loss rate of 5%, the total sample size was a total of 100 patients (50 patients per group).

Recruitment {15}

Patients undergoing TAVR from three Fuwai-affiliated medical centers across China will be screeded for the eligibility. Patients will be provided with comprehensive information about the potential risks and benefits of participating the study. The center effects are ignored because the three centers are all Fuwai-affiliated tertiary centers and the heterogeneity is small.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

Eligible patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to SEV group or BEV group. The randomization sequence was generated using R statistical software with the package randomizeR prior to study initiation.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

The randomized results will be securely sealed in opaque envelopes that are consecutively numbered. An independent research assistant is responsible for keeping these envelopes unopened and allocated.

Implementation {16c}

Trained researchers will screen participants. Participants who are eligible for the study and willing to sign the informed consent will be enrolled. After the enrollment, the research assistant who are responsible for allocating the envelopes will open the envelope and directly inform the surgeon about the group allocation.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded? {17a}

Due to the nature of the study, blinding of the surgeon is not feasible. This might introduce performance, collateral intervention, and assessment bias. However, all outcomes will be adjudicated in a blinded fashion to preserve impartiality. The blinding of the event adjudication process will diminish bias. All operators performing CT or other exams will be blinded to group allocation. Imaging will be evaluated in a blinded manner. This blinding will be maintained throughout the trial. Only when the data has been analyzed and the conclusions regarding the primary and secondary endpoints are made will the associated personnel be unblinded. However, we cannot exclude that bias may be introduced by the staff when filling the case report files. Data monitoring will be performed to minimize it.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

Not applicable. This is an open-label trial.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

Patients’ baseline data, including demographics, medical history, and supplementary exams, will be collected by research assistants. In particular, cardiac CT data obtained before and after TAVR will be collected and analyzed. All centers perform contrast-enhanced, electrocardiogram-gated CT scan in accordance with the standardized cardiac CT protocol [13, 14]. All subsequent image readings will be done by the central imaging core laboratory. The aortic size will be determined using a double-oblique measurement of maximum AA diameter orthogonal to the direction of blood flow using CT scan based on American Society of Radiology Guidelines [15].

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

Patients will be clinically followed-up at 1, 3, 6, 12 months and annually after discharge. Symptoms, clinical events, laboratory tests, and echocardiography exams will be collected at each visit (Fig. 3). Data management {19}

All data will be collected through the web-based electronic data system and case report forms (CRFs). Researchers are responsible for the data collection as well as checking that all data are filled out correctly and completely. Electronic data will be stored on a dedicated computer with password protection, and paper CRFs will be stored in the research center with password-locks.

Personal data of the participants will be treated as confidential. All data will be anonymized with CRFs abbreviated as initials. Study documentation will be stored in a secure location until the end of the study.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Categorical variables are described as frequencies and percentages, and tested by the chi-square test or Fisher exact test. Normally distributed continuous variables are expressed as means ± standard deviations and non-normally distributed variables as median (interquartile range), and compared using Student t test or the Mann–Whitney U test. The survival is estimated using Kaplan–Meier methods and compared with the log-rank test.

The primary and secondary end points are analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis. In addition, a per-protocol analysis is performed for patients who are randomized and complete the assigned treatments as scheduled. All tests are 2-sided, and a value of P < 0.05 is considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses are performed using R statistical software version 4.2.1, and SAS software version 9.4.

Interim analyses {21b}

No applicable. No interim analyses will be conducted.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

Not applicable. No subgroup analyses are planned.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

All efforts will be made to complete the data collection and avoid missing data. If the amount of missing data exceeds 10%, multiple imputation techniques will be utilized to ensure the robustness of our study results. A sensitivity analysis for the primary endpoints and its components will be undertaken. In any case where data updates and correction are required, the researchers will review the original medical record and consult the physician for correction. Patient withdrawal and adverse events will be recorded in a timely manner.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant-level data and statistical code {31c}

The datasets, the full protocol, and statistical codes will be available from the corresponding author upon a reasonable request.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating center and trial steering committee {5d}

The trial steering committee comprised of the principal investigator and three trained researchers from each center. The committee will guide and supervise the implementation and progress of the trial.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {21a}

A data monitoring committee will consist of one independent statistician and one senior cardiac surgeon. The committee will monitor the safety and progress of the trial. Specifically, an interim safety check will be performed once 50% of the sample has been achieved. The data monitoring committee will assess the clinical outcomes and may recommend modifying or terminating the study based on any perceived safety concerns.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

Participants will be asked to report any adverse events at any time. Any adverse events reported by the participants or noticed by the physicians will be documented and promptly reported to the ethical committee.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

The steering committee will meet 4 times a year to review the conduct throughout the study period. In addition, the ethical committee will conduct an annual follow-up review.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g., trial participants, ethical committees) {25}

Any amendments to the protocol will be promptly communicated the principal investigator and the steering committee. Amendments will be made only after approved by the ethical committee.

Dissemination plans {31}

The results of this study will be presented at national and international conferences and published in the relevant journals.

Discussion

As the indications for TAVR continue to expand, elucidating its safety and efficacy in patients with coexisting conditions is important. Previous studies have revealed a notable prevalence of AAD in patients with severe AS who are candidates for TAVR [3, 16]. However, early landmark clinical trials regarding TAVR excluded these patients [17]. Several retrospective studies have investigated the feasibility of TAVR in patients with AAD, although these studies usually remained limited to single-center experiences with small sample size [7, 18, 19].

Device success is an important endpoint that has been associated with decreased mortality and TAVR-related complications. Specific complications may be more frequent with some type of THV devices. A THV choice tailored to the risk profile may increase the device success and decrease severe complications. Unlike BEVs that have short stent frame, SEVs have long stent frame designs with radial forces existing at both the inflow and the outflow level. In the setting of AAD, the outflow stent may be weakly attached to the aortic wall, and valve stability is solely dependent on the inflow level. It remains unclear whether this reduced anchoring of the SEVs may contribute to the decreased device success.

Aortic dissection is a rare but lethal complication during TAVR. Stiff wire interactions in the ascending aorta, catheter valve injury during passage, or other intimal injuries may result in catastrophic aortic dissection. According to the European Registry on Emergent Cardiac Surgery during TAVI (EuRECS-TAVI), the incidence of aortic dissection was 0.04% in patients using BEVs and 0.10% in patients using SEVs [20]. In patients with AAD, it should raise particular attention. In addition to the device success and 30-day all-cause mortality, the AAD-CHOICE trial also aims to compare the 30-day adverse aortic events between two types of THVs in this specific subgroup of patients.

The key secondary end points of the trial include 1-year mortality (all-cause and cardiovascular) and major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events. In particular, adverse aortic events and aortic expansion rate ≥ 3 mm/year are also included. The aortic expansion rate ≥ 3 mm/year is considered rapid aortic growth and a risk factor for aortic dissection. According to the 2022 AHA/ACC Guideline for the Diagnosis and Management of Aortic Disease, in patients with an aneurysm of the ascending aortic diameter < 55 m whose growth rate is ≥ 3 mm/year in 2 consecutive years or ≥ 5 mm/year in 1 year, surgery is indicated (Class 1, Level C-LD) [2].

With the expansion of TAVR to lower-risk and younger patients and the proliferation of different TAVR devices on the market, a tailored TAVR therapy is important for the operators. Specific anatomic features should be taken into account to choose the most suitable device for each patient. The present trial may help determine the optimal device choice in patients with AAD.

Several limitations of the present study should be pointed out. Although we have tried to minimize bias in different ways, selection bias and potential bias created by the open-label design still exists. Compared with Western countries, the prevalence of BAV is significantly higher in China, which might affect generalizability. In addition, the relatively small sample size might also limit the generalizability. Despite these limitations, the present study is anticipated to provide evidence for the TAVR in patients with AAD.

Machine-learning ensembled probabilistic methods for time-dependent reliability analysis of reservoir slopes under rapid water level drawdown using Bayesian model averaging (BMA)
Authors: Zehang Li, Zhu Yang, Long Teng, Shiping Xie, Mi Tian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractProbabilistic reservoir slope stability analysis usually suffers from computational inefficiency of complicated implicit performance functions. To address this problem, numerous machine learning (ML) algorithms have been successfully applied to calculate the index of factor of safety (FS) to facilitate slope reliability analysis. However, current ML surrogate models generally select an optimal algorithm with good performance while discarding all the others. Meanwhile, ML models mainly provide a point-value deterministic evaluation of failure probability and ignores the predictive uncertainties associated with the models. How to ensemble the strengths of different ML algorithms to improve the estimation accuracy of slope failure probability and rationally quantify the predictive uncertainties still remains an open issue. This paper proposes ML ensembled probabilistic methods for time-dependent reliability analysis of reservoir slopes under rapid water level drawdown using Bayesian model averaging (BMA). A practical slope in the Three Gorges Reservoir Area is selected to illustrate the proposed approach. Firstly, two machine-learning models, namely support vector machine (SVM) and back propagation-based neural network (BPNN), are used to evaluate the safety factor of reservoir slopes. Then, the deterministic evaluations of FS are ensembled together by the weighting of each member using BMA to make probabilistic evaluation of time-dependent failure probability. Finally, the predictive performance of individual ML models and the ensemble BMA model are systematically investigated. Results show that the ensemble surrogate model has better predictive performance on slope failure probability than individual ML model. Ensemble models can not only combine the strengths of different models to improve the prediction accuracy of time-dependent failure probability of reservoir slope, but also provide probabilistic forecasts to reasonably quantify the predictive uncertaintyKeywords: Reservoir slope, Time-dependent reliability, Machine learning, Bayesian model averaging, Predictive uncertaintySubject terms: Environmental sciences, Natural hazards

Introduction

Under the rapid drawdown of water level, reservoir slopes deform continuously with the decrease of stabilizing effect of hydrostatic pressure on the slope in the reservoir area1–4. Once the reservoir slopes are destabilized, it would not only cause severe casualties and property damages, but also threaten the safety of hydraulic infrastructures and ecological environment5. Therefore, reasonable reservoir slope stability analysis under rapid water level drawdown is a significant prerequisite for landslide hazard assessment and remedial measures design. The stability of reservoir slopes is generally evaluated by the index of factor of safety through deterministic slope stability analysis. However, various uncertainties are often encountered in geotechnical engineering, such as inherent variability of soil properties and model uncertainty6,7. The deterministic index of factor of safety (FS) cannot quantify the probability or risk of slope failure. A slope with an FS greater than 1 may still have a significant probability of failure, especially when the parameter uncertainties are high. Thus, rational stability evaluation of reservoir slopes while taking all these kinds of uncertainties into account is vital for ensuring the safety of slopes in the reservoir area.

Over the past decades, probabilistic slope stability analysis methods have been widely used in geotechnical engineering to evaluate the slope stability by reliability index or failure probability8–10. Probabilistic slope stability analysis complements deterministic methods by reasonably accounting for various uncertainties. The probabilistic method treats soil properties, such as cohesion and internal friction angle, as random variables with statistical characteristics, offering outputs of failure probability or reliability index that are far more informative than a single factor of safety. This is especially vital in the context of reservoir slopes, where the potential for catastrophic consequences demands more rigorous and scientific information for risk assessment and decision-making. Many analytical and numerical methods have been proposed to calculate the failure probability of slopes, such as first-order reliability method (FORM)11, second-order reliability method (SORM)12 and Monte Carlo simulation (MCS)13–15. Among these methods, MCS proves to be a powerful tool in slope reliability analysis that may be used to address a wide spectrum of reliability problems. However, the MCS method usually requires a great number of repetitive numerical simulations to obtain a reliable estimate of failure probability16. Generally, the performance functions of slope are typically complicated and nonlinear implicit functions. These numerous numerical simulations demand significant computational efforts. Especially, the calculation burden of time-varying reliability analysis of slope with spatial variability is furtherly increased compared with the time-independent reliability analysis.

To address the computational inefficiency of MCS, machine learning (ML) algorithms have been successfully applied to facilitate slope reliability analysis by developing a surrogate model to calculate the safety factor of slope with desired accuracy17–21. For example, Wang et al.16 applied the extreme gradient boosting method algorithm to construct an equivalent performance function between multiple input parameters and the safety factor for slope reliability assessment. Li et al.22 proposed an efficient slope reliability analysis method based on the active learning support vector machine (SVM). Guardiani et al.23 developed two intelligent surrogate models based on Support Vector Regression and Extreme Gradient Boosting to predict the factor of safety of reservoir slope under rapid drawdown. Wang and Goh24 introduced the Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) as the metamodels of random field finite element model. Wang et al.25 systematically investigated the performance of CNN, Long short-term memory (LSTM), and Light gradient boosting machine (LightGBM) in facilitating time-dependent reliability analysis of reservoir slopes. These ML-based reliability analysis methods generally train a surrogate model using training data and evaluate its performance metrics (e.g., mean square error, MSE) using testing data to select a good algorithm with desired performance. However, these surrogate models generally provide a point-value deterministic evaluation of safety factor of slope, ignoring the inherent predictive uncertainties associated with the models. ML surrogate models (e.g., MARS, SVM and CNN) are simplified approximations of complex physical models, such as the finite element method and limit equilibrium method, to calculate the safety factor of slope. No matter how complex the surrogate model is, it cannot completely and precisely replicate the real physical process but is associated with model uncertainty. Moreover, different ML algorithms have distinct underlying principles and assumptions. Choosing the most suitable hyperparameters, such as the number of layers in a neural network, the number of trees in a random forest, and the kernel function in SVM, still remains a challenge task, which also involves certain uncertainty. Consequently, these uncertainties (e.g., model uncertainty and hypermeter uncertainty) are propagated into the final predictions of slope failure probability. In order to quantify the predictive uncertainty, some methods have been proposed to characterize the hypermeter uncertainty to assess the uncertainty in predictions by Bayesian inference26,27. However, different ML algorithms have their distinct hypothesis and inductive preferences for the same data, resulting in differences in the predictions of slope failure probability. The selection of an optimal algorithm is subjective, and it cannot be proven to be the global optimal solution. Meanwhile, selecting an optimal algorithm for the slope reliability analysis while discarding all the other may waste the information from other algorithms and may not provide robust estimates of the failure probability when the observed data differs from the testing data. Therefore, how to ensemble the strengths of different ML algorithms to improve the prediction accuracy of slope safety factor and rationally quantify the predictive uncertainties of surrogate models still remains an open issue.

Bayesian model averaging (BMA) method, a method for averaging over different competing models, may offer a feasible tool to ensemble the strengths of different ML algorithms and quantitively characterize the predictive uncertainties. Recently, BMA has been commonly used in climate and hydrologic forecasting28,29. BMA is a statistical technique designed to infer a prediction by weighted averaging over many different competing models. This method is not only a scheme for model combination but also a coherent approach for accounting for between model and within-model uncertainty. BMA can provide both deterministic and probabilistic forecasts, showing better accuracy and robustness of predictions than a single model30. Nevertheless, BMA has rarely been reported in time-varying reliability analysis of slope.

To this end, this paper proposes ML ensembled probabilistic methods for time-dependent reliability analysis of reservoir slopes under rapid water level drawdown using BMA. A practical slope in the Three Gorges Reservoir Area is selected as a study case to illustrate the proposed approach. Firstly, two ML models, namely support vector machine (SVM) and the back propagation-based neural network (BPNN), are used to construct surrogate models for evaluating the index of safety factor of slope. Then, the deterministic estimations of the index of safety factor of slope obtained from these two surrogate models are integrated by BMA to make ensemble probabilistic evaluations. MCS is subsequently used to evaluate the time-dependent failure probability of reservoir slopes. Finally, the predictive performance of individual ML models and the ensemble model are systematically investigated. Effects of coefficient of variation (COV) of effective friction angle and water level drawdown rate on the time-dependent failure probability of reservoir slopes are also explored.

BMA ensemble surrogate model for time-dependent reliability analysis

Latin hypercube sampling

Sufficient training samples are needed to perform repeated numerical simulations to establish a surrogate model with desired accuracy. Generally, the training samples for developing a valid surrogate model must be ‘representative’ of the overall design space of input parameters. In this study, Latin hypercube sampling (LHS) is applied to generate training and testing samples for the construction and validation of a surrogate model. LHS is a type of stratified sampling that divides the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of each variable into N non-overlapping subsets with the common stratum probability 1/N to reduce the variability of the estimates by covering the relevant sampling domains more uniformly. For e each variable, one sample is randomly selected within each interval38. Then, a total of N samples of each variable are generated, which completely cover the entire distribution without any missing intervals. These samples from different variables are paired together randomly across dimensions to ensure that every non-overlapping subset for every variable is sampled exactly once, while maintaining probabilistic independence between variables. Compared with direct MCS, LHS generates samples uniformly distributed in the design space with considerably less computational effort in reliability analysis39. LHS also proves to be a feasible method for uncertainty analysis and sensitivity studies, where achieving representative coverage with limited samples is crucial.

ML models for deterministic point estimate of FS

With the generated training and testing samples by LHS, these two subsets are applied to develop ML surrogate models for provide point-value deterministic evaluations of FS. For simiplicity, this paper adopts two commonly-used ML models, namely support vector machine (SVM) and the back propagation-based neural network (BPNN), to approximate the limit state function to calculate the FS.

SVM is a powerful supervised ML algorithm used for both classification and regression40. Its core principle is to find the optimal hyperplane that maximizes the margin between different classes in a high-dimensional feature space. The SVM performs both linear classification and nonlinear classification. In nonlinear classification, a kernel function is used to map the data into a different space where a linear hyperplane cannot be used to do the separation. The performance of SVM is generally affected by two critical hyperparameters: regularization parameter and kernel coefficient40. Regularization parameter balances the trade-off between maximizing the margin and minimizing classification error., The kernel coefficient defines the influence range of a single training example, which is particularly significant in radial basis function (RBF) kernels. These parameters are typically optimized through systematic methods such as grid search or randomized search, guided by performance metrics during the model training process.

BPNN is a type of artificial neural network that is trained using the backpropagation algorithm, known for its ability to learn from data and make predictions based on learned patterns41,42. BPNN typically consists of an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer. Each layer contains neurons (nodes) that process information and pass it to the next layer. Each neuron in BPNN typically uses an activation function (e.g., sigmoid and tanh) to introduce non-linearity into the network, allowing it to learn complex patterns. During training, BPNN iteratively adjusts the weights and biases of neurons to minimize the error between predicted and actual outputs. This process involves forward propagation and backward propagation. This study applies multi-layered network architecture with two hidden layers between input and output units, as shown in Fig. 1. Each input neuron receives an input signal and broadcasts this signal to the connected neurons in the first hidden layer. Each of these neurons then computes activation and sends its result to the connected neurons in the second hidden layer. Each neuron in the second hidden layer computes its activation and sends its result to the output neuron. Finally, the output neuron yields the network output according to the activation function O. The activation function is the same for all neurons in any particular layer of a neural network. The binary sigmoidal function is used as activation function in this study. As for the backward propagation, it is concerned with error computation and weight adjusting. The output is compared with the target value to determine the predicted error to determine the connection weights.

It doesn’t affect the method itself. In this case, all the possible ensembles should be examined to investigate the most suitable ensemble members.

Results of slope stability analysis.

To consider the uncertainties of soil properties and boundary conditions, the shear strength parameters of geotechnical properties and water level drawdown are regarded as normally distributed random variables within their value ranges by engineering practice48. Based on the statistical characteristics in Table 1, LHS is adopted to generate random samples of input parameters (including shear strength parameters of geotechnical properties and water level drawdown). The sample size of input parameters used for training the surrogate model is set as 1000, which satisfies the empirical size proposed by Silvestrini et al.49. The calculated time is set as 30 d. Then, these 1000 training samples are taken as inputs in GeoStudio for repetitively transient seepage analysis by SEEP/W module and slope stability analysis by SLOPE/W module. The results of seepage analysis are incorporated into the SLOPE/W module to obtain FS with the Morgenstern-Price method. Finally, the corresponding values of FS for each sample over 30 days can be obtained. Based on the 1000 training samples and the corresponding FS over 30 days, SVM and BPNN models are applied to construct the surrogate models between input parameters and FS for each day, respectively. As for SVM, the radial basis function is adopted as the kernel function. The regularization parameter and the kernel coefficient for SVM models are set as 32 and 0.06 through grid search. As for BPNN, the optimal number of hidden nodes are determined by trial and error. A BPNN network architecture with input neurons, two hidden layers and one output neuron is used in this study. Input variables are taken as c′1, φ′1, c′2, φ′3, c′3, φ′4, c′4, φ′4, and v. For each trial, the network is trained with various learning rate of 0.001, 0.05 and 0.01 to compare the performance of BPNN. The final network architecture consists of nine neurons in the input layer, two hidden layers with six and three neurons, respectively, and an output neuron. The optimal learning rate of is taken as 0.01. In addition, since the 9 random input parameters (see Table 1) and FS have different physical meanings and units. The inputs and outputs are normalized during the construction of BPNN. The predicted results are then re-normalized to obtain the values of safety factor.

100 testing samples of input parameters are also independently generated by LHS to validate the prediction accuracy of surrogate models developed by BMA method and individual ML algorithm. Finally, a direct Monte Carlo simulation generates 100,000 combinations of input parameters to predict the time-dependent failure probability using the surrogate models instead of repetitive simulations with GeoStudio.

The deterministic predictions of FS over 30 days derived from SVM and BPNN models are then integrated by BMA to make an ensemble probabilistic prediction of FS. In this case, corresponding BMA parameters θ for 30-day ensemble surrogate models are required. DREAM(ZS) algorithm is used to obtain the BMA parameters θ of the ensemble models for each day. Totally, three Markov chains are set running in parallel, where 15,000 samples within each chain are generated from the log-likelihood function (i.e., Eq. 8) to estimate the BMA parameters θ. The first 1,000 samples in each chain are discarded as burn-in and the remaining 14,000 samples from the joint chains are used for the posterior statistical analyses. Genarally speaking, the posterior distributions of BMA parameters θ approximate the normal distribution with a low dispersion. Effects of COV of effective friction angle on the time-dependent Pf

The effects of COV of effective friction angle (COVφ′) on the time-dependent Pf are investigated. The ranges of COVφ′ vary from 0.05 to 0.20, which are adopted from Jiang et al. (2020). The sensitivity analysis is conducted to study the effects of COVφ′ on time-dependent Pf, where the COVs of effective cohesion for the four layers are assumed to be the same. Figure 11 shows the variation of time-dependent Pf with increasing COVφ′ (i.e., 0.05, 0.10 and 0.20) for v = 0.5 m/d, v = 1.0 m/d and v = 2.0 m/d, respectively. It can be seen that the Pf generally increases with COVφ′. Especially, Pf increases significantly when the COVφ′ increases from 0.10 to 0.20. For example, for v = 2.0 m/d, the increments of Pf over 30 days with increasing COVφ′ from 0.05 to 0.10 are between 0.01 and 10.67%. When COVφ′ increases from 0.10 to 0.20, the increments of Pf over 30 days range from 1.74 to 20.47%. This trend was also found by Wang et al. (2020). Moreover, Pf increases with the increasing water level drawdown rate v. For the same COVφ′ (e.g., 0.1), the Pf values on day 15 are 0.49%, 2.93% and 15.02% for v = 0.5 m/d, v = 1.0 m/d and v = 2.0 m/d, respectively. Obviously, with the increasing water level drawdown, rapid water level decline leads to reduction in pore water pressure within the slope, causing decreasing of shear strength and increasing of the likelihood of slope failure.

Effects of COV of water level drawdown on the time-dependent Pf

To investigate the COV of water level drawdown (COVv) on the time-dependent Pf, the ranges of COVv are considered as [0, 0.5]. Figure 12 plots the variations of Pf with COVv for v = 0.5 m/d and v = 2.0 m/d. It can be observed that when v = 0.5 m/d, the time-dependent failure probability curves are very close for different values of COVv (i.e., 0.1 and 0.5), showing almost no significant change of Pf. When v = 2.0 m/d, the failure probability of slope with a COVv of 0.1 is larger than that with a COVv of 0.1. It is obvious that when the water level drop rate is high, the time-dependent Pf increases with the decreasing COVv. The reason may lie in that the water level drop rate tends to take on larger values with a high rate of v and low variability, making the slope more prone to instability and resulting in a higher failure probability. In addition, an increased rate of water level drawdown leads to the earlier occurrence of slope failure. For example, the maximum increase of Pf (i.e., 42.21%) over 30 days is found on day 9 for v = 2.0 m/d. However, the Pf reaches its maximum increase on day 28 (i.e., 37.05%) for v = 0.5 m/d. This can contribute to the rapid reduction in pore water pressure and increased seepage forces.

Discussions

The proposed BMA ensembled probabilistic methods exhibit better predictive accuracy than single ML model for time-dependent reliability analysis of reservoir slopes. The inherent spatial variability of shear strength parameters, the fluctuations of water level drawdown and model uncertainty are considered in this study. The commonly-used normal distribution is used to characterize the uncertainties of shear strength parameters and water level drawdown. If more information about these parameters are available, other distributions, such as lognormal distribution and beta distribution can also be adopted to fit the marginal distributions. Moreover, there still exist other uncertainties in numerical simulations of slope failure probability. For example, the numerical simulations for calculating FS by GeoStudio cannot accurately capture the complicated physical processes, affecting the accuracy of ML surrogate models. Other uncertainties, such as the inherent spatial variability of permeability parameters and rainfall randomness, are not considered in this study. In the future, various uncertainties should be comprehensively considered to provide more accurate reliability analysis of reservoir slope.

It is important to establish an accurate single ML surrogate model for reasonably evaluating the safety factor of reservoir slope. The hyperparameters of ML algorithm significantly affect the accuracy of individual and BMA ensemble models, and ultimately influence the results of slope reliability analysis. In the future, how to obtain the proper hyperparameters for the proposed method deserves further study.

The combination of SVM and BPNN model achieves better performance than single model in forecasting of FS. It should be noted that although only two ML models are integrated together to make a BMA ensemble to predict the FS, other ML models (e.g., random forest and convolutional neural network) can also be incorporated into the BMA method. In this case, it is necessary to consider how to integrate multiple models. Generally, a weighted ensemble of multiple models outperforms any single model in forecasting accuracy. However, combining multiple ML models does not invariably lead to superior predictive performance compared to a single model. This is primarily due to the fundamental differences in mathematical mechanisms and model structures among various data-mining algorithms. Selecting good ensemble members can increase the performance of ensemble model in some degree. However, in some cases, poorly-performing individual models within an ensemble may adversely affect the overall ensemble’s performance. Consequently, it remains unclear whether incorporating all available models or selecting a subset yields optimal results. In the future, the impact of different ensemble compositions on the predictive performance for calculating the time-dependent Pf should be investigated.

Summary and conclusions

This paper proposed a ML ensemble method for time-dependent reliability analysis of reservoir slopes under water level drawdown considering the uncertainties of geotechnical properties. The proposed method adopted two ML models (i.e., SVM and BPNN) to develop the approximate functional relationships between soil parameters, water level drawdown rate and safety factor to evaluate the time-dependent failure probability of reservoir slopes. The deterministic FS estimated from these two ML models were integrated by BMA to make ensemble probabilistic evaluations of FS. MCS was subsequently used to evaluate the time-dependent failure probability of reservoir slopes. A practical slope in the Three Gorges Reservoir Area was selected to illustrate the proposed approach.

Results show that the proposed BMA method can predict the safety factor and the time-dependent failure probability of reservoir slope in the Three Gorges Reservoir Area with satisfactory accuracy and high computational efficiency. The proposed BMA method can significantly the MSE compared to the individual ML surrogate model during the training and testing period. On the whole, weighting the SVM and BPNN models can improve the prediction accuracy of slope failure probability for both calibration and validation in this study. The proposed BMA method can also provide probabilistic prediction intervals for the failure probability, which can rationally characterize the prediction uncertainty. In addition, the time-dependent failure probability of reservoir slope in the Three Gorges Reservoir Area is significantly affected by the COV of effective friction angle. The time-dependent Pf generally increases with COVφ′. Especially, when the COVφ′ increases from 0.10 to 0.20, Pf increases sharply. It is also found that when the water level drop rate is high, the time-dependent Pf increases with the decreasing COVv. In contrast, when v = 0.5 m/d, COVv shows almost no significant effects on Pf.

Research Progress of Sepsis and Nutrition: A Bibliometric and Visualized Analysis
Authors: Luorui Shang, Yuju Cai, Shuhan Wang, Jinxiao Li, Fangyuan Zhou, Mengqi Zhang, Yuqing Wang, Jianghua Huang, Keke Jiang, Shenglan Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSepsis is a life-threatening syndrome resulting from a dysregulated host response to infection, which substantially increases the risk of progressive organ dysfunction and mortality. This study employs a comprehensive bibliometric analysis to systematically map the global scientific output and evolving research trends pertaining to sepsis and nutritional interventions.MethodsA systematic literature search was conducted in the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) to identify peer-reviewed publications addressing the nexus between nutrition and sepsis. Bibliometric analysis and visualization were subsequently performed through R (version4.4.0) with the R-bibliometrix package (version4.2.3), VOSviewer software (version 1.6.20) and CiteSpace (version 6.4.1).ResultsFrom 2005 to 2025, a total of 2615 English articles on nutrition and sepsis were identified. The Journal of Parenteral and Enteral Nutrition was the most productive journal in this field (138 articles). The United States contributed the largest share of publications (609 articles) and accumulated the highest total citation count (24624 citations). Among individual authors, Greet Van den Berghe was the most prolific contributor. Keyword analysis further identified sepsis, parenteral nutrition and enteral nutrition as the top three most frequently occurring research themes.ConclusionThis study provided a comprehensive overview of the global research landscape on sepsis and nutrition. The research results showed that parenteral and enteral nutrition, as the two core methods of clinical nutritional support, are currently the research focus. These findings might help guide researchers in determining the future directions of sepsis and nutrition research.Keywords: sepsis, nutrition, bibliometric analysis, VOSviewer, citespace

Introduction

Sepsis, defined as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection, remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality in critically ill patients worldwide.1,2 Epidemiological estimates indicate that sepsis affects nearly 49 million people annually, contributing to approximately 11 million deaths, accounting for nearly one-fifth of all deaths worldwide.3–5 In intensive care units (ICUs), sepsis remains one of the leading causes of mortality, with hospital mortality rates ranging from 25% to over 40% depending on regional resources and patient comorbidities.6,7 Despite significant advances in antimicrobial therapy and organ-supportive interventions, effective mechanism-based adjunctive therapies remain limited, highlighting the urgent need to optimize supportive care strategies.8

The interplay between sepsis and nutrition is complex and bidirectional. Sepsis induces a profound hypermetabolic and catabolic state characterized by insulin resistance, accelerated proteolysis, and negative nitrogen balance, ultimately leading to rapid depletion of endogenous nutrient reserves.9,10 Concurrently, pre-existing or hospital-acquired malnutrition exacerbates immune dysfunction, impairs wound healing and increases susceptibility to secondary infections.11–13 Emerging evidence highlights the pivotal role of the triad comprising systemic inflammation, immune dysregulation, and malnutrition in the pathogenesis of sepsis. These interconnected derangements collectively drive catabolic wasting, compromise pathogen clearance, and heighten vulnerability to secondary infections and multi-organ dysfunction.14,15 Evidence indicates that patients in the persistent inflammation–immunosuppression and catabolism syndrome (PICS) phase exhibit improved recovery trajectories when receiving optimized caloric and protein supplementation, alongside targeted supplementation with immunomodulatory adjuvants, essential vitamins and trace minerals.14,16 Therefore, nutritional support serves as a therapeutic intervention that modulates inflammatory cascades, preserves gut barrier integrity, reduces bacterial translocation and improves clinical outcomes,17,18 playing an important role in the management of sepsis. Our previous studies have demonstrated that HIF-1α-mediated glycolysis contributes to the onset and progression of sepsis.19 Moreover, we found that miR-874-5p regulates VDR expression to alleviate intestinal barrier injury during sepsis.20 Given the central role of glucose metabolism in nutrient utilization and the critical contribution of intestinal barrier integrity to sepsis progression, these findings further underscore the significance of nutritional regulation in sepsis pathophysiology.

Given the rapid expansion of research in this field over the past two decades, a systematic quantification of the global scientific landscape is warranted. Bibliometric analysis employs mathematical and statistical methods to quantitatively evaluate the scientific knowledge structure within a specific discipline. This approach not only aids in identifying research priorities and emerging hotspots within a specific field but also facilitates the evaluation of scientific productivity at the levels of countries, institutions, and individual researchers.21 Currently, bibliometric studies generally recommend selecting a single authoritative citation database to ensure data consistency. Although integrating multiple databases may increase literature coverage, differences in indexing criteria and citation-counting algorithms can introduce potential inconsistencies. Considering that the WoSCC database provides comprehensive coverage and reliable citation information, we selected WoSCC as the sole data source for this study, including English-language publications published between 2005 and July 7, 2025. By employing R-bibliometrix, VOSviewer, and CiteSpace, we aimed to identify core journals, leading authors and institutions, international collaboration patterns and keyword co-occurrence clusters. Our findings provide an objective roadmap of the intellectual structure, emerging frontiers, and knowledge gaps in this critically important domain, offering valuable guidance for future clinical and translational research.

Materials and Methods

Data Collection

A comprehensive search and analysis were conducted using the Web of WoSCC database, covering English-language literature published from 2005 to 2025 (up to July 7, 2025). Bibliometric analyses typically rely on the WoSCC database and the following search strategy was employed: TS = ((“sepsis” OR “severe sepsis” OR “septic shock” OR “septicemia” OR “SIRS” OR “systemic inflammatory response syndrome”) AND (“nutrition” OR “nutrients” OR “nutritional supplement” OR “enteral nutrition” OR “parenteral nutrition”)) to retrieve all relevant records. The literature published between 2005 and 2025 was selected for this study, a timeframe considered sufficient to capture the current research landscape in this field. All retrieved articles on nutrition and sepsis were exported in TXT format as “Full Record and Cited References.” Subsequently, the data were imported into the R package bibliometrix, VOSviewer and CiteSpace for comprehensive bibliometric analysis.

Data Analysis

The bibliometric tools, including the Bibliometrix R package, VOSviewer and CiteSpace, were employed to visualize the results of our bibliometric analysis. To enhance the presentation of research findings, appropriate software applications were carefully selected to examine various aspects of the study. In this work, we mainly utilized R (version 4.4.0), the R-bibliometrix package (version 4.2.3; http://www.bibliometrix.org), CiteSpace (version 6.4.1) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.20; https://www.VOSviewer.com) to visualize the selected literature.

Using these tools, we performed in-depth analyses of publications, countries, institutions, journals, authors and keywords. Frequency statistics for authors, journals, institutions and countries, including both publication counts and citation frequencies, were generated using the R-bibliometrix package. The top 20 items in each category were displayed. In addition, a word cloud of the top 50 author keywords was created in R-bibliometrix, in which the font size of each term reflects its frequency of occurrence. Default settings were retained for the remaining software parameters. When running R-bibliometrix, we used Biblioshiny, a user-friendly web interface for the package, to facilitate data analysis and the visualization of social network maps. Furthermore, VOSviewer, developed by Nees Jan van Eck and Ludo Waltman at Leiden University, was used to generate co-authorship, country, institution and keyword co-occurrence networks.22,23 In these networks, node size reflects the frequency of occurrence or citation, while line thickness represents the strength of collaboration.

For the keyword co-occurrence network, the minimum frequency of occurrence was set to 10 for authors, institutions, journals and keywords, to 5 for countries and to 100 for article citation counts. In addition, CiteSpace, a widely used bibliometric analysis and visualization tool developed by Professor Chaomei Chen, was used to facilitate keyword clustering analysis and the visualization of keyword clustering timelines.24,25 Notably, all the thresholds were set with reference to commonly used parameters in comparable bibliometric studies in this field, aiming to balance visual clarity of the generated maps with the retention of core information. Results

The Number of Publications and Citation Evolution

From 2005 and 2025, a preliminary search of the WoSCC database identified 3533 publications related to sepsis and nutrition. After excluding 917 non-article or non-English publications, one duplicate was removed using a script, resulting in a final set of 2615 publications for bibliometric analysis. A line graph was generated to illustrate the evolution in the number of publications related to sepsis and nutrition from 2005 to 2025 (Figure 2A). Annual publications reached its peak in 2022. Although the annual publications in this field declined from 2023 to 2025, the total number of publications has been steadily increasing overall.

Keyword and Hotspot Analysis

A comprehensive keyword analysis was conducted on the selected 2615 articles pertaining to nutrition and sepsis, utilizing both the “Author’s Keywords” function in the Biblioshiny application and the “Keywords Plus” provided by the Thomson Reuters editorial team. A total of 5510 keywords were identified. However, “Keywords-Plus”, with its richer vocabulary in Web of Science, can supplement thematic identifiers for publications lacking author keywords, helping to uncover implicit themes within the literature, thereby serving as the primary data source for the analysis (Figure 6A). The frequency of each keyword was visualized using a word cloud, as shown in Figure 6B. The term sepsis exhibited the highest frequency, being mentioned 519 times, followed by mortality (250 times) and parenteral-nutrition (248 times). Notably, high-frequency keywords that have emerged in recent years are presented in Figure 6C, and the top 10 keywords are sepsis, mortality, parenteral-nutrition, nutrition, management, critically-ill patients, outcomes, children, guidelines and growth.

We performed a keyword analysis on the selected 2615 articles using VOSviewer. To explore the associations among keywords and identify prevailing research topics, a co-occurrence analysis was conducted. Based on the Author’s Keywords, the 107 keywords that appeared at least 12 times were selected to construct a co-occurrence network (Figure 7), and clusters represented by different colors, including red, green, blue and yellow, were identified. In the same cluster, nodes sharing the same color indicate closely related co-occurring keywords, with node size and edge width varying according to the frequency and strength of co-occurrence. Betweenness centrality was used to measure the importance of nodes within the network, in which larger nodes and higher total link strength signify a more critical role in information flow. Within the green cluster, sepsis and parenteral nutrition were the two largest nodes with the highest total link strength, at 743 and 491, respectively. The top 20 keywords included sepsis, parenteral nutrition, enteral nutrition, nutrition, mortality, critical illness, critical care, glutamine, infection, intestinal failure, necrotizing enterocolitis, inflammation, short bowel syndrome, children, intensive care unit, critically ill, septic shock, intensive care, fish oil and cholestasis.

Discussion

Research Trends in Sepsis and Nutrition

In the present bibliometric analysis, we systematically delineated the global research landscape of nutrition and sepsis over the past two decades (from 2005 to 2025). The United States emerged as the most productive country with 609 publications and had the highest total citation count (24624 citations), underscoring its dominant role in this field. This leadership is likely attributable to early recognition of the clinical importance of nutritional support in critically ill septic patients, well-established critical care infrastructure and sustained funding from institutions such as the National Institutes of Health.28 China ranked second in both publication output (382 articles) and total citations (7059 citations) although a substantial gap remains between China and the United States. Several factors may explain this discrepancy. First, research on nutrition and sepsis in China gained momentum after 2010, and it takes time for recent articles to accumulate citations. In addition, nutritional assessment among patients with sepsis in China remains less standardized, tools such as the Nutrition Risk in the Critically Ill (NUTRIC) score and modified NUTRIC score have not yet been routinely implemented in many hospitals, limiting the generalizability and clinical impact of published studies. Finally, Chinese investigators have predominantly conducted observational studies or small single-center trials, whereas high-quality multicenter randomized controlled trials (RCTs), which are more likely to be cited, remain relatively scarce. In contrast, countries such as Australia and Belgium, with smaller populations, have achieved disproportionately high citation impact through well-designed RCTs and mechanistic studies embedded within large ICU networks.

At the institutional level, Harvard University ranked first globally, followed by the University of Toronto and Harvard University Medical Affiliates. Notably, eleven of the top 20 institutions were located in the United States, indicating a concentration of research excellence. However, strong collaboration networks were also observed among countries with moderate research output, such as Australia and Brazil, suggesting that efficient partnerships rather than publication volume alone can enhance research impact. The strong collaboration links between the United States, China and England further highlight the importance of transnational cooperation in advancing this field.

Among authors, Greet Van den Berghe and Sung-Ling Yeh were the most productive. Co-citation analysis confirmed that Daren K Heyland was the most cited author and Greet Van den Berghe had the highest total link strength, reflecting their seminal contributions to evidence-based nutrition guidelines and large-scale trials such as the EPaNIC and PEPaNIC studies.

Research Hotspots on Sepsis and Nutrition

Keyword co-occurrence analysis using VOSviewer and CiteSpace identified sepsis, mortality and parenteral-nutrition as the most frequent and central terms, reflecting persistent clinical concerns about the timing, route and adequacy of nutrient delivery in septic patients. Among these, parenteral nutrition ranked third in frequency, indicating its prominent role as a research focus in the nutritional management of septic patients. The widespread application of parenteral nutrition in patients with sepsis mainly stems from the damage to gastrointestinal function.29 The systemic inflammatory response triggered by sepsis can inhibit gastric motility and affect pancreatic exocrine function, resulting in some patients being unable to tolerate enteral nutrition. However, the clinical application of parenteral nutrition requires careful consideration. A large meta-analysis showed that although parenteral nutrition does not significantly differ from enteral nutrition in reducing the 28-day and 90-day mortality rates of sepsis patients, it is associated with a longer ICU stay.30 However, it’s worth noting that the potential negative impact of parenteral nutrition on intestinal barrier function has attracted much attention in recent years. Studies have shown that complete parenteral nutrition could lead to intestinal mucosal atrophy and a decrease in tight junction protein expression, thereby damaging the intestinal epithelial barrier function.31 The loss of barrier function might increase the risk of bacterial and endotoxin translocation, and theoretically may aggravate or even induce sepsis. This mechanism provides an important pathophysiological basis for explaining the infection complications related to parenteral nutrition.

The timeline visualization of keyword clusters revealed an evolution from broad descriptive terms, such as sepsis and total parenteral nutrition, toward more mechanism-oriented and individualized concepts like glutamine, fish oil and so on. This shift suggests that after two decades of debate on the “when and how” of feeding, research attention is now moving toward “what and for whom”, specifically, which nutrient compositions, dosing strategies and patient subgroups derive the greatest benefit. Glutamine is a key nutrient that significantly supports energy metabolism in septic patients. The study demonstrated that glutamine exerted liver-protective effects in burn sepsis through restoring mitochondrial complex activity and maintaining cellular energy metabolism.32 Intravenous lipid emulsion injection is an important part of intravenous nutrition, which not only provides energy but also supplies essential fatty acids.33 Fish oil, as one of the main components of modern composite lipid parenteral nutrition emulsions, contains omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) and eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), which not only meet the body’s nutritional needs but also have anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory and anti-oxidative effects.34,35 The study revealed that fish oil- parenteral nutrition significantly reduces the risk of infection, sepsis and length of hospital stay compared to standard parenteral nutrition in general ward patients receiving parenteral nutrition covering at least 70% of nutritional requirements.36

The most highly cited article in our dataset was published in Clinical Nutrition titled ESPEN guideline on Clinical Nutrition in the intensive care unit by Pierre Singer et al in 2019, discussing the timing, route, dose and composition of nutrition and recommending that acute metabolic changes as well as calorie and protein deficits play a major role in patient outcome.37 The second most cited work was the ESPEN guidelines on enteral nutrition: intensive care published in Clinical Nutrition by K.G. Kreymann et al in 2006,38 underscoring the continued reliance on authoritative guidelines to shape clinical practice. The above results collectively demonstrate the significant role of the ESPEN guidelines in guiding clinical treatment for critically ill patients, as well as researchers’ recognition of these guidelines.

In conclusion, research in the field of sepsis and nutrition over the past two decades has shown a clear evolution from a macroscopic perspective to a precise one, and from empirical methods to evidence-based approaches. The core issues have always revolved around the timing, methods and adequacy of nutritional support. Parenteral nutrition is widely used in sepsis patients due to the impaired gastrointestinal function, but its potential damage to the intestinal barrier and the risk of prolonging ICU stay cannot be ignored. Immunomodulatory nutrients such as glutamine and fish oil have become emerging research directions, and the ESPEN series of guidelines have always been the core reference for clinical practice and academic citations. In the future, integrating multi-omics and individualized strategies and conducting more high-quality randomized controlled trials with cross-national collaboration will be the key path to promoting the development of this field.

Strengths and Limitations

This study applied bibliometric and visualization analysis using the WoSCC to quantitatively map the evolution of research on nutrition and sepsis from 2005 to 2025, elucidating longitudinal trends, thematic shifts, and emerging knowledge frontiers. This analysis offers a timely and evidence-based synthesis of current research priorities and conceptual focal points in sepsis-related nutrition science, aimed at informing precision-oriented clinical strategies and translational research agendas. However, there are some inevitable limitations. First, only the WoSCC database was used for literature search in this study, which may limit the comprehensiveness of literature coverage and introduce potential database-specific bias. However, in general, bibliometric analysis heavily relies on complete bibliographic data and many databases are unable to support in-depth network graph analysis. Moreover, since different databases have different calculation rules for citation counts, it will result in inconsistent citation counting standards and make it difficult to conduct fair rankings of the articles when multiple databases are combined without harmonized indexing criteria. Therefore, the current mainstream bibliometric analysis usually recommends choosing a single authoritative citation database to ensure data consistency. This also reminds us that the tools and strategies for bibliometric analysis still need further improvement in the future. In addition, this study included only English-language publications and excluded Chinese literature, which may have resulted in language bias. Future studies incorporating multilingual datasets will require greater standardization of data structures and formats across databases, as well as further improvements in bibliometric analysis tools.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study provides a comprehensive synthesis of research trends on sepsis and nutrition. Based on the bibliometric analysis, global research on nutrition and sepsis has grown substantially over the past two decades, with parenteral nutrition, enteral nutrition and critical illness as core themes. The United States and Harvard University lead in productivity and impact. Emerging hotspots include immunonutrition such as glutamine, fish oil and so on. These findings provide a valuable roadmap for future mechanistic studies and high-quality clinical trials aimed at optimizing nutritional support in septic patients.

Rectifiability of entropy productions for weak solutions of the 2D eikonal equation with supercritical regularity
Xavier Lamy, Elio Marconi

Abstract
Weak solutions m : Ω ⊂ ℝ 2 → ℝ 2 m\colon\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2} of the eikonal equation | m | = 1 ​ a.e. and ​ div ​ m = 0 , \displaystyle|m|=1\text{ a.e. and }\mathrm{div}\>m=0\,, arise naturally as sharp interface limits of bounded energy configurations in various physically motivated models, including the Aviles-Giga energy. The distributions μ Φ = div ​ Φ ​ ( m ) \mu_{\Phi}=\mathrm{div}\,\Phi(m) , defined for a class of smooth vector fields Φ \Phi called entropies, carry information about singularities and energy cost. If these entropy productions are Radon measures, a long-standing conjecture predicts that they must be concentrated on the 1-rectifiable jump set of m m – as they do if m m has bounded variation (BV) thanks to the chain rule. We establish this concentration property, for a large class of entropies, under the Besov regularity assumption m ∈ B 1 / p p , ∞ ⇔ sup h ∈ ℝ 2 ∖ { 0 } ∥ m ( ⋅ + h ) − m ∥ L p | h | 1 / p < ∞ , \displaystyle m\in B^{1/p}_{p,\infty}\hskip 10.00002pt\Leftrightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\sup_{h\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|m(\cdot+h)-m\|_{L^{p}}}{|h|^{1/p}}<\infty\,, for any 1 ≤ p < 3 1\leq p<3 , thus going well beyond the BV setting ( p = 1 p=1 ) and leaving only the borderline case p = 3 p=3 open.



1
Introduction

For an open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
we consider weak solutions
m\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
of the eikonal equation

\displaystyle|m|=1\text{ a.e. in }\Omega,\hskip 10.00002pt\dv m=0\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega).

(1.1)

If
\Omega
is simply connected, this is equivalent to the existence of a Lipschitz function
u\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

m=i\nabla u
and

\displaystyle|\nabla u|=1\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\,,

which is classically referred to as the eikonal equation.

If
m\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is a
C^{1}
solution of the eikonal equation (
1.1
)
then the chain rule provides a whole family of conservation laws:

\dv\Phi(m)=0
for all

\Phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})

such that

\partial_{\theta}\Phi(e^{i\theta})\cdot e^{i\theta}=0
for all
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

For a general weak solution
m\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
of the eikonal equation (
1.1
),
the distributions
\dv\Phi(m)

may not be zero,
and they carry information on how singular
m
is.
They were first introduced in the context of the eikonal equation in
[
7
]
,
and called entropy productions by analogy with the theory of scalar conservation laws.
We denote by

\displaystyle\mathrm{ENT}=\big\{\Phi\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{S}^{1};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\colon\frac{d}{d\theta}\Phi(e^{i\theta})\cdot e^{i\theta}=0\;\forall\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}\big\}\,,

(1.2)

the set of all
C^{1,1}
entropies.

Weak solutions
m
of the eikonal equation (
1.1
)
whose entropy productions
\dv\Phi(m)
are finite Radon measures play an important role in the theory of the Aviles-Giga energy
[
3
]
.
The structure of these finite-entropy solutions
is not fully understood,
but it is known that they share with functions of bounded variation (BV) several fine properties.
Note that, if
m\in BV(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is a weak solution of (
1.1
),
then by the BV chain rule its entropy productions are measures concentrated on the
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-rectifiable jump set
J_{m}
.
For a general finite-entropy solution, denote by

\nu
the supremum measure

\displaystyle\nu=\bigvee_{\Phi\in\mathrm{ENT},\,\|\Phi\|_{C^{1,1}}\leq 1}|\dv\Phi(m)|\,.

In
[
6
]
the authors prove that
the jump set

J_{m}\doteq\left\{x\in\Omega:\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\nu(B_{r}(x))}{r}>0\right\}\,,

(1.3)

is
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-rectifiable and

m
admits left and right
L^{1}
traces
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e. along
J_{m}
.
According to a long-standing conjecture on the Aviles-Giga energy
[
3
]
,
entropy productions should be concentrated on that jump set.

Among all weak solutions of (
1.1
),
the finite-entropy solutions can be characterized, at least locally, in terms of Besov
B^{1/3}_{3,\infty}
regularity
[
8
]
.
For
s\in(0,1)
and
p\geq 1
, a map
m\in L^{p}(\Omega)
has
the Besov regularity
B^{s}_{p,\infty}
if and only if the seminorm

\displaystyle|m|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}=\sup_{h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{|h|^{s}}\|m(\cdot+h)-m\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\cap(\Omega-h))}\,,

is finite
[
14
, §2.5.12]
.
Between the spaces
BV(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})
and
B^{1/3}_{3,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})
lies the intermediate scale of spaces

B^{1/p}_{p,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})
, for
1<p<3
.
We prove that the concentration conjecture is true for solutions of (
1.1
) with that intermediate regularity,
and for entropies in the class

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{ENT}}\doteq\left\{\Phi\in\mathrm{ENT}:\frac{d}{d\theta}\Phi(-e^{i\theta})=-\frac{d}{d\theta}\Phi(e^{i\theta})\;\forall\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}\right\},

(1.4)

which corresponds to odd entropies plus constants.
This restriction is due to the same technical reasons as in
[
12
]
(where this class of entropies is denoted by
\mathcal{E}_{\pi}
).
The fundamental entropies introduced in
[
10
]
to establish a sharp lower bound for the Aviles-Giga energy (see also
[
1
]
) are odd, and therefore covered by our result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
a bounded open set,
m\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

a weak solution of the eikonal equation
(
1.1
),
and assume that

m\in B^{1/p}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)
for some
p\in[1,3)
.
Then the entropy productions of
m
are 1-rectifiable,

\displaystyle\dv\Phi(m)=\mathbf{n}\cdot\big(\Phi(m^{+})-\Phi(m^{-})\big)\,\mathcal{H}^{1}\llcorner J_{m}\,,

for all
\Phi\in\widetilde{\mathrm{ENT}}
.

To describe the ideas behind Theorem
1.1
,
let us consider first a solution
m\in{\mathrm{BV}}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})
of (
1.1
).
Then, by the
BV
chain rule we have

|\dv\Phi(m)|\leq C_{\Phi}|m^{+}-m^{-}|^{3}d\mathcal{H}^{1}\llcorner J_{m}.

for every entropy
\Phi\in\mathrm{ENT}
.
Moreover, for any

p\in[1,3)
we have

\int_{J_{m}}|m^{+}-m^{-}|^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq\|m\|^{p}_{B^{1/p}_{p,\infty}}\,.

As a consequence, the contribution of jumps smaller than
a threshold
\delta>0
is controlled by

\displaystyle|\dv\Phi(m)|(\Omega\setminus(J_{m}\cap\{|m^{+}-m^{-}|\geq\delta\})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Phi}\int_{J_{m}\cap\{|m^{+}-m^{-}|\leq\delta\}}|m^{+}-m^{-}|^{3}d\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq C_{\Phi}\delta^{3-p}\|m\|^{p}_{B^{1/p}_{p,\infty}}.

(1.5)

Here we assumed that
m\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})

to ensure that
\dv\Phi(m)
is concentrated on
J_{m}
,
but the estimate does not depend on the total variation of
m
.
Moreover,
a structure result
proved in
[
12
]

about the continuous part of the entropy production
allows to interpret it as being generated by infinitesimally small jumps.
It is therefore natural to conjecture that the estimate (
1
) should be true
for solutions
m
not necessarily of bounded variation.
We prove indeed a similar estimate in
Proposition
4.1
,
and the main result then follows by letting
\delta\to 0
.

To technically implement these ideas,
we actually have to argue along trajectories of a Lagrangian representation of
m
,
also introduced in
[
12
]
,
and which can only provide information on entropies in (
1.4
).
The control on the continuous part of entropy production
at the level of these Lagrangian trajectories
is obtained by using a singular family of entropies,
whose entropy productions are uniformly bounded thanks
to the supercritical Besov regularity assumption.

2
Entropy productions and Besov regularity

In this section we prove that the supercritical Besov regularity assumption provides uniform control
on entropy productions over families of entropies which
are unbounded in
C^{1,1}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
a bounded open set,
m\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
with
\dv m=0
and assume that

m\in B^{1/(2+\alpha)}_{2+\alpha,\infty}(\Omega)
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\bigvee_{\|\Phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq 1}|\dv\Phi(m)|\bigg)(\Omega)\leq C\|m\|^{2+\alpha}_{B^{1/(2+\alpha)}_{2+\alpha,\infty}(\Omega)}\,,

for some
C=C(\alpha)>0
, where the supremum of measure is taken over all
\Phi\in\mathrm{ENT}
such that
\|\Phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq 1
.

Proposition
2.1
can be interpreted
as an interpolation between the estimates

\displaystyle\bigg(\bigvee_{\|\Phi\|_{C^{1,1}}\leq 1}|\dv\Phi(m)|\bigg)(\Omega)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|m\|^{3}_{B^{1/3}_{3,\infty}(\Omega)}\,,

\displaystyle\bigg(\bigvee_{\|\Phi\|_{C^{0,1}}\leq 1}|\dv\Phi(m)|\bigg)(\Omega)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|m\|^{2}_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\,.

The first of these estimates is proved in
[
8
, Proposition 3.10]
,
and the second can be established using similar calculations
which rely on commutator estimates for the function

\displaystyle w_{\epsilon}=1-|m_{\epsilon}|^{2}=(|m|^{2})_{\epsilon}-|m_{\epsilon}|^{2}\,,

where the subscript
\epsilon
denotes convolution at scale
\epsilon
.
(In the context of the eikonal equation, arguments based on commutator estimates were introduced in
[
5
]
.)
The interpolation argument is however a bit involved.
In particular, the constant
C=C(\alpha)
we are able to obtain in Proposition
2.1
blows up as
\alpha\to 0
or
1
, even though these borderline cases are easier to handle.

The commutator estimates we use in the proof of Proposition
2.1
are as follows.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
an open set and
m\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Let
\rho\in C_{c}^{1}(B_{1})
,
\rho\geq 0
,
\int\rho=1
,
|\nabla\rho|\leq 2
, and define
m_{\epsilon}=m*\rho_{\epsilon}
for
\rho_{\epsilon}(x)=\epsilon^{-2}\rho(x/\epsilon)
and
\epsilon>0
.
Then the commutator

\displaystyle w_{\epsilon}=1-|m_{\epsilon}|^{2}=(|m|^{2})_{\epsilon}-|m_{\epsilon}|^{2}\,,

satisfies

\displaystyle|w_{\epsilon}|(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\epsilon}}|D^{h}m(x)|^{2}\,dh\,,

\displaystyle|D^{\epsilon k}w_{\epsilon}|(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim|k|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{2\epsilon}}|D^{h}m(x)|^{2}\,dh\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }|k|\leq 1\,,

for all
x\in\Omega
such that
B_{2\epsilon}(x)\subset\Omega
.

Here and in what follows we denote by
D^{h}
the finite difference operator

\displaystyle D^{h}m=m^{h}-m,\hskip 10.00002ptm^{h}=m(\cdot+h)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

As in
[
5
]
, these commutator estimates come from the commutator identity

\displaystyle w_{\epsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=1-|m_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2}=\int_{B_{\epsilon}}|m(x-y)-m_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2}\,\rho_{\epsilon}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bigg|\int_{\Omega}\big(m(y)-m(z)\big)\rho_{\epsilon}(x-z)\,dz\bigg|^{2}\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)\,dy\,,

which follows by integrating

\displaystyle 1-|m_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=|m(x-y)|^{2}-|m_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=|m(x-y)-m_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2}+2\langle m_{\epsilon}(x),m(x-y)-m_{\epsilon}(x)\rangle\,,

with respect to
\rho_{\epsilon}(y)\,dy
.
The commutator identity directly implies the first estimate.
Applying the finite difference operator,
it also
implies the identity

\displaystyle D^{\epsilon k}w_{\epsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bigg|\int_{\Omega}\big(m(y)-m(z)\big)\rho_{\epsilon}^{\epsilon k}(x-z)\,dz\bigg|^{2}D^{\epsilon k}\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)\,dy

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{\Omega}\Big\langle\int_{\Omega}\big(m(y)-m(z^{\prime})\big)(\rho_{\epsilon}+\rho_{\epsilon}^{\epsilon k})(x-z^{\prime})\,dz^{\prime},

\displaystyle\hskip 50.00008pt\int_{\Omega}\big(m(y)-m(z)\big)D^{\epsilon k}\rho_{\epsilon}(x-z)\,dz\Big\rangle\,\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)\,dy\,

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2}}\bigg|\int_{B_{2}}\big(m(x-\epsilon y)-m(x-\epsilon z)\big)\rho^{k}(z)\,dz\bigg|^{2}D^{k}\rho(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{B_{2}}\Big\langle\int_{\Omega}\big(m(x-\epsilon y)-m(x-\epsilon z^{\prime})\big)(\rho+\rho^{k})(z^{\prime})\,dz^{\prime},

\displaystyle\hskip 50.00008pt\int_{B_{2}}\big(m(x-\epsilon y)-m(x-\epsilon z)\big)D^{k}\rho(z)\,dz\Big\rangle\,\rho(y)\,dy\,,

which then provides the second estimate.
∎

We will also use the estimate

\displaystyle|\nabla m_{\epsilon}|(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\epsilon}}|D^{h}m(x)|\,dh\,.

(2.1)

which follows from the identity

\displaystyle\nabla m_{\epsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{B_{1}}(m(x-\epsilon y)-m(x))\nabla\rho(y)\,dy\,.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathrm{ENT}
such that
\|\Phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq 1
,
and
\widehat{\Phi}
a radial extension
\widehat{\Phi}(re^{i\theta})=\eta(r)\Phi(e^{i\theta})
for some
\eta\in C_{c}^{2}(0,\infty)
with
\eta(1)=1
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\dv\widehat{\Phi}(m_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=\Psi(m_{\epsilon})\cdot\nabla w_{\epsilon}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptw_{\epsilon}=1-|m_{\epsilon}|^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\Psi(re^{i\theta})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\eta(r)\lambda(e^{i\theta})\,e^{i\theta}-\frac{1}{2r}\eta^{\prime}(r)\Phi(e^{i\theta})\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda=\partial_{\theta}\Phi\cdot(ie^{i\theta})\,,

and

\|\Psi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\lesssim 1
.

Let
U\subset\Omega
an open subset and
\zeta\in C_{c}^{1}(U)
.
We fix an intermediate open set
\Omega^{\prime}
and
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\supp(\zeta)+B_{4\delta}\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega^{\prime}+B_{2\delta}\subset U\,,

and a cut-off function
\chi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{\supp(\zeta)}\leq\chi\leq 1\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and }\supp(\chi)+B_{2\delta}\subset\Omega^{\prime}\,.

Then, for
0<\epsilon<\delta
, we write

\displaystyle\langle\dv\widehat{\Phi}(m_{\epsilon}),\zeta\rangle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}w_{\epsilon}\chi\Psi(m_{\epsilon})\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\chi\Psi(m_{\epsilon})\cdot\nabla(\zeta w_{\epsilon})\,dx\,.

(2.2)

Using the Littlewood-Paley characterization of Besov spaces and Hölder’s inequality (see Lemma
2.3
) we find

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\chi\Psi(m_{\epsilon})\cdot\nabla(\zeta w_{\epsilon})\,dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\chi\Psi(m_{\epsilon})\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}\|\zeta w_{\epsilon}\|_{B^{1-\alpha}_{q,1}}\,,

(2.3)

where we choose
p=(2+\alpha)/\alpha
, hence
q=(2+\alpha)/2
.
To estimate the two Besov norms in the right-hand side,
we come back to their finite difference characterization.
We have

\displaystyle\|D^{h}[\chi\Psi(m_{\epsilon})]\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=\|(D^{h}\chi)\Psi(m_{\epsilon}^{h})+\chi D^{h}[\Psi(m_{\epsilon})]\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime}\cap(\Omega^{\prime}-h))}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\|\chi\Psi(m_{\epsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\supp(\chi)\setminus(\Omega^{\prime}-h))}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\|\chi\Psi(m_{\epsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\supp(\chi)\setminus(\Omega^{\prime}+h))}\,.

For
0<|h|<\delta
, the last two terms are zero and we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|h|^{\alpha}}\|D^{h}[\chi\Psi(m_{\epsilon})]\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\chi\|_{C^{1}}\|\Psi\|_{\infty}+\frac{1}{|h|^{\alpha}}\|\chi D^{h}[\Psi(m_{\epsilon})]\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime}\cap(\Omega^{\prime}-h))}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\chi\|_{C^{1}}\|\Psi\|_{\infty}+\frac{1}{|h|^{\alpha}}\|\Psi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\|D^{h}m_{\epsilon}\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha p}(\Omega^{\prime}\cap(\Omega^{\prime}-h))}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\chi\|_{C^{1}}\|\Psi\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla m_{\epsilon}\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha p}(\Omega^{\prime}+B_{\delta})}\,.

To estimate the last factor we use (
2.1
)
which implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla m_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\alpha p}(\Omega^{\prime}+B_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\epsilon}}\|D^{h}m\|_{L^{\alpha p}(\Omega^{\prime}+B_{\delta})}\,dh\,.

Plugging this into the previous estimate for
0<|h|<\delta

and
recalling that
p=(2+\alpha)/\alpha

we obtain

\displaystyle\|\chi\Psi(m_{\epsilon})\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}\lesssim\|\chi\Psi(m_{\epsilon})\|_{L^{p}}+\sup_{|h|>0}\frac{\|D^{h}[\chi\Psi(m_{\epsilon})]\|_{L^{p}}}{|h|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\Omega|^{\frac{\alpha}{2+\alpha}}\big(\|\chi\|_{C^{1}}+\delta^{-\alpha}\big)\|\Psi\|_{\infty}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\alpha}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\epsilon}}\|D^{h}m\|^{\alpha}_{L^{2+\alpha}(\Omega^{\prime}+B_{\delta})}\,dh\,.

(2.4)

Now we turn to estimating the second factor in the right-hand side of (
2.3
).
As above, we have

\displaystyle\|D^{h}(\zeta w_{\epsilon})\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle=\|(D^{h}\zeta)w_{\epsilon}^{h}+\zeta D^{h}w_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega^{\prime}\cap(\Omega^{\prime}-h))}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\|\zeta w_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\supp(\zeta)\setminus(\Omega^{\prime}-h))}+\|\zeta w_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\supp(\zeta)\setminus(\Omega^{\prime}+h))}\,.

Since
0<\epsilon<\delta

.
We deduce

\displaystyle\|\zeta w_{\epsilon}\|_{B^{1-\alpha}_{q,1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\zeta w_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\|D^{h}(\zeta w_{\epsilon})\|_{L^{q}}}{|h|^{1-\alpha}}\frac{dh}{|h|^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\zeta\|_{C^{1}}\|w_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega^{\prime})}+\|\zeta\|_{\infty}\int_{|h|\geq\epsilon}\frac{\|w_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\supp(\zeta))}}{|h|^{1-\alpha}}\frac{dh}{|h|^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\|\zeta\|_{\infty}\int_{|h|\leq\epsilon}\frac{\|D^{h}w_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\supp(\zeta))}}{|h|^{1-\alpha}}\frac{dh}{|h|^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\zeta\|_{C^{1}}\|w_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega^{\prime})}+\frac{\|\zeta\|_{\infty}}{1-\alpha}\frac{\|w_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\supp(\zeta))}}{\epsilon^{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{\|\zeta\|_{\infty}}{\epsilon^{1-\alpha}}\int_{|k|\leq 1}\frac{\|D^{\epsilon k}w_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\supp(\zeta))}}{|k|^{1-\alpha}}\frac{dk}{|k|^{2}}\,.

Recalling that

and

\displaystyle\|\zeta w_{\epsilon}\|_{B^{1-\alpha}_{q,1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\zeta\|_{C^{1}}\sup_{|h|\leq\epsilon}\|D^{h}m\|^{2}_{L^{2+\alpha}(\Omega^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{\|\zeta\|_{\infty}}{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{1-\alpha}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\epsilon}}\|D^{h}m\|^{2}_{L^{2+\alpha}(\Omega^{\prime})}\,dh

\displaystyle|\dv\Phi(m)|(U)\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{2\epsilon}}\int_{U}|D^{h}m(x)|^{2+\alpha}dx\,dh\,.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
p,p^{\prime}\in[1,\infty]
such that
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1

and any
\alpha\in(0,1)
we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f\nabla g\,dx\bigg|\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}\|g\|_{B^{1-\alpha}_{p^{\prime},1}}\,,

for all
(f,g)\in B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\times B^{1-\alpha}_{p^{\prime},1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

This inequality essentially amounts to the inclusion of
B^{-\alpha}_{p^{\prime},1}
into the dual of
B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}
.
The elementary proof is basically contained in
[
14
, § 2.11]
,
where the dual of
B^{\alpha}_{p,q}
is shown to be equal to
B^{-\alpha}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
for all
p,q\in(1,\infty)
.
Here we have
q=\infty
and only one inclusion is true,
that is why this statement is not stated explicitly there.
We provide the proof for the readers’ convenience.

It relies on the Littlewood-Paley characterization of Besov spaces, which we start by recalling.
We fix a smooth partition of unity
\{\chi_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
with the properties that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\chi_{j}(x)=1,

\displaystyle|\chi_{0}(\xi)|\leq\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\leq 2}\,,

\displaystyle|\chi_{j}(\xi)|\leq\mathbf{1}_{2^{-j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}}\text{ for }j\geq 1\,,

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq 0}2^{jk}\sup_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\nabla^{k}\chi_{j}|<\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\forall k\geq 0\,.

Then, for any
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, the Besov space
B^{\gamma}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
consists of all tempered distributions
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
such that the norm

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{B^{\gamma}_{p,q}}=\big\|\big(2^{j\gamma}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{j}\mathcal{F}\varphi\|_{L^{p}}\big)_{j\geq 0}\big\|_{\ell^{q}}\,,

is finite
[
14
, § 2.3.1]
,
where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.
Moreover, for
\gamma\in(0,1)
, these norms (which depend on the system
\{\chi_{j}\}
) are equivalent to

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{B^{\gamma}_{p,q}}=\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}}+\big\||h|^{-\gamma}\|D^{h}\varphi\|_{L^{p}}\big\|_{L^{q}(dh/h^{2})}\,,

see e.g.
[
14
, §2.5.12]
.

To prove the claimed inequality, we use the decomposition

\displaystyle\varphi=\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{j}\mathcal{F}\varphi\,,

and the fact that
\chi_{j}\chi_{k}\equiv 0
for
|j-k|\geq 2
,
to rewrite the integral as

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f\nabla g\,dx

\displaystyle=\langle f,\nabla g\rangle=\sum_{j,k\geq 0}\langle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{j}\mathcal{F}f,\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{k}i\xi\mathcal{F}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k\geq 0}\langle\chi_{j}\mathcal{F}f,\chi_{k}i\xi\mathcal{F}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=-1}^{1}\sum_{j\geq 0}\langle\chi_{j+r}\mathcal{F}f,\chi_{j}i\xi\mathcal{F}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=-1}^{1}\sum_{j\geq 0}\langle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{j+r}\mathcal{F}f,\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{j}i\xi\mathcal{F}g\rangle\,.

Recalling that
\chi_{j}
is supported in
2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}
for
j\geq 2
,
and applying Hölder’s inequality, we infer

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{R^{2}}f\nabla g\,dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=-1}^{1}\sum_{j\geq 0}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{j+r}\mathcal{F}f\|_{L^{p}}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{j}i\xi\mathcal{F}g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{r=-1}^{1}\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{j}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{j+r}\mathcal{F}f\|_{L^{p}}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{j}\mathcal{F}g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\,.

The last inequality follows
from the properties of
\{\chi_{j}\}
and
a Fourier multiplier theorem (see e.g.
[
14
, §1.5]
).
Writing
2^{j}=2^{\alpha j}2^{(1-\alpha)j}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{R^{2}}f\nabla g\,dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{k\geq 0}2^{\alpha k}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{k}\mathcal{F}f\|_{L^{p}}\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{(1-\alpha)j}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{j}\mathcal{F}g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\,,

which corresponds to the claimed inequality.
∎

\displaystyle(\sigma_{\min})_{x}=\mathbf{n}\cdot e^{i\bar{s}}g_{\beta}(\cdot-\bar{s})\mathcal{L}^{1}\leq 0\text{ on }{\mathbb{T}}\,\text{ for all }x\in\tilde{J}^{-}\,.

By (

) it follows that

\dv\Phi_{\psi}(m)\llcorner(J_{m}\setminus J_{m}^{\delta})=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{\Omega}^{-}}-\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{\Omega}^{+}}\big)\psi^{\prime}(s)d|(\sigma_{\min})_{x}|(s)\right)\nu_{\min}\llcorner(J_{m}\setminus J_{m}^{\delta}).

Moreover, by the assumption on
\psi
, we have that
\psi^{\prime}
has constant sign on

\supp(\sigma_{\min})_{x}\subset[\bar{s}-\frac{\delta}{2},\bar{s}+\frac{\delta}{2}]\cup[\bar{s}-\frac{\delta}{2}+\pi,\bar{s}+\frac{\delta}{2}+\pi]
. Therefore the positive and the negative parts of
\dv\Phi_{\psi}(m)
restricted to

are concentrated respectively on the sets

\begin{split}A^{j,+}=&\penalty\ \left\{x\in J_{m}\setminus J_{m}^{\delta}:\,\exists s\in\supp(\sigma_{\min})_{x}\,,\,\big(\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{J}^{-}}(x)-\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{J}^{+}}(x)\big)\psi^{\prime}(s)>0\right\},\\
A^{j,-}=&\penalty\ \left\{x\in J_{m}\setminus J_{m}^{\delta}:\,\exists s\in\supp(\sigma_{\min})_{x}\,,\,\big(\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{J}^{-}}(x)-\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{J}^{+}}(x)\big)\psi^{\prime}(s)>0\right\}.\end{split}

Thanks to (
3.11
) and Lemma
3.3
, for any Borel set
A\subset J_{m}\setminus J_{m}^{\delta}
, we have

\displaystyle\big(\dv\Phi_{\psi}(m)\big)(A)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{T}\int_{\Gamma}D^{j}(\psi\circ\gamma_{s})(\{\gamma_{x}\in A\})\,d\omega(\gamma)\,

(3.14)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{T}\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{t\in J_{\gamma}}\left(\psi(\gamma_{s}(t+))-\psi(\gamma_{s}(t-))\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\gamma_{x}(t)\in A\}}\,d\omega(\gamma).

Further note that, thanks to (
3.8
)-(
3.9
) and the properties of

(\sigma_{\min})_{x}
for
x\in J_{m}\setminus J_{m}^{\delta}
,
for
\omega
-a.e.
\gamma\in\Gamma

\gamma_{s}(t+),\gamma_{s}(t-)\in[\bar{s}(\gamma_{x}(t))-\frac{\delta}{2},\bar{s}(\gamma_{x}(t))+\frac{\delta}{2}]
or
\gamma_{s}(t+),\gamma_{s}(t-)\in[\bar{s}(\gamma_{x}(t))-\frac{\delta}{2}+\pi,\bar{s}(\gamma_{x}(t))+\frac{\delta}{2}+\pi]
for

-a.e.
t\in I_{\gamma}
.
Moreover, thanks again to (
3.8
)-(
3.9

3.3
we have

\displaystyle(D^{j}\gamma_{s})_{+}(\{\gamma_{x}\in\tilde{J}^{+}\})=(D^{j}\gamma_{s})_{-}(\{\gamma_{x}\in\tilde{J}^{-}\})=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }\omega\text{-a.e. }\gamma\in\Gamma\,.

\leq 0\quad&\text{if }A\subset A^{j,+}\,,\end{cases}

4
Rectifiability

The proof of Theorem
1.1

relies on the following estimate.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
m\in B^{1/3}_{3,\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
a weak solution of the eikonal equation (
1.1
) and
\omega
a minimal Lagrangian representation of
m
.

For every
\delta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{8})
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|D\gamma_{s}|\big(\{t\in I_{\gamma}\colon\gamma_{x}(t)\in\Omega\setminus J_{m}^{\delta},\ |D\gamma_{s}|(\{t\})\leq\delta\}\big)\,d\omega(\gamma)

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{1-\alpha}\bigg(\bigvee_{\|\Phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq 1}|\dv\Phi(m)|\bigg)(\Omega\setminus J_{m}^{\delta})\,,

where the supremum of measures is taken over all entropies
\Phi\in\widetilde{\mathrm{ENT}}
such that
\|\Phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq 1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
m\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
a weak solution of the eikonal equation (
1.1
) such that
m\in B^{1/p}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)
for some
p\in[1,3)
.
Since
B^{1/p}_{p,\infty}\cap L^{\infty}\subset B^{1/q}_{q,\infty}
for all
q\geq p
, we assume without loss of generality that
2<p<3
and may write
p=2+\alpha
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Thanks to Proposition
2.1
, the supremum measure appearing in Proposition
4.1
is finite.
Letting
\delta\to 0
, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|D\gamma_{s}|\big(\{t\in I_{\gamma}\colon\gamma_{x}(t)\in\Omega\setminus J_{m},\ |D\gamma_{s}|(\{t\})=0\}\big)\,d\omega(\gamma)=0\,.

In other words, for
\omega
-a.e.
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the measure
|D\gamma_{s}|
only has a jump part.
Thanks to the representation formula (
3.11
) and Lemma
3.3
, this implies that the entropy dissipation is concentrated on the jump set
J_{m}
for every entropy
\Phi\in\mathrm{ENT}
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

We fix
\delta\in(0,\pi/16)
and start by defining a family of functions
\psi
bounded in
C^{0,\alpha}
and to which Lemma
3.5
can be applied.
We define
\psi_{0}\in C^{0,1}({\mathbb{T}};{\mathbb{R}})
, odd,
\pi
-periodic, even with respect to
\pi/4
, and given on
[0,\pi/4]
by the nondecreasing piecewise affine function

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(t)=\begin{cases}t/\delta^{1-\alpha}&\text{ for }0\leq t\leq 2\delta,\\
2\delta^{\alpha}&\text{ for }2\delta<t\leq\pi/4\,.\end{cases}

This function
\psi_{0}
generates the family
\psi_{\bar{s}}=\psi_{0}(\cdot-\bar{s})
, for
\bar{s}\in{\mathbb{T}}
.
The corresponding entropies
\Phi_{\bar{s}}=\Phi_{\psi_{\bar{s}}}

), satisfy the bound

.

For any
\bar{s}\in{\mathbb{T}}
and any

we have

\displaystyle|D\gamma_{s}|\big(\{t\in I_{\gamma}\colon\gamma(t^{-})\text{ and }\gamma(t^{+})\in[\bar{s}-\delta,\bar{s}+\delta]\}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{1-\alpha}|D(\psi_{\bar{s}}\circ\gamma_{s})|\big(\{t\in I_{\gamma}\colon\gamma(t^{-})\text{ and }\gamma(t^{+})\in[\bar{s}-\delta,\bar{s}+\delta]\}\big)\,.

\delta/2<\bar{s}_{j+1}-\bar{s}_{j}\leq\delta
.
Then we have the covering

\displaystyle{\mathbb{T}}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\bar{I}_{j},\hskip 10.00002pt\bar{I}_{j}=[\bar{s}_{j}-\delta,\bar{s}_{j}+\delta]\,,

and any interval of length

\displaystyle|D\gamma_{s}|\big(\{t\in I_{\gamma}\colon|D\gamma_{s}|(\{t\})\leq\delta\}\big)

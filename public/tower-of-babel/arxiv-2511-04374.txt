Abstract
Let G G be an edge-colored graph. We use e ⁡ ( G ) e(G) and c ⁡ ( G ) c(G) to denote the number of edges and colors in G G , respectively. A subgraph H H is called rainbow if c ⁡ ( H ) = e ⁡ ( H ) c(H)=e(H) . Li et al. ( European J. Combin. , 36 (2014), 453–459) proved that every edge-colored graph on n n vertices with e ⁡ ( G ) + c ⁡ ( G ) ≥ n ⁡ ( n + 1 ) / 2 e(G)+c(G)\geq n(n+1)/2 contains rainbow triangles. Later, Xu et al. ( European J. Combin. , 54 (2016), 193–200) generalized the previous results concerning rainbow triangles to rainbow cliques K r K_{r} , where r ≥ 4 r\geq 4 . In this paper, we consider the existence of rainbow matchings of size k k in general edge-colored graphs G G under the condition of e ⁡ ( G ) + c ⁡ ( G ) e(G)+c(G) , and the condition in our result is tight. Keywords: Edge-colored graph; rainbow matching; edge number; color number

Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are simple, finite, and undirected. For terminology and notation not defined herein, we refer to Bondy and Murty
[
2
]
.

Given a graph
H
, let ex
(n,H)
denote the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex graph that does not contain
H
as a subgraph.
In 1907, Mantel
[
17
]
determined the maximum number of edges in a triangle-free graph on

n
vertices is
\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
. This result was later generalized by Turán’s celebrated theorem
[
26
]
in 1941. For
H=kK_{2}
, where
kK_{2}
denotes a matching of size
k
, the value
\ex(n,kK_{2})
was established by Erdős and Gallai
[
9
]
.

Theorem 1

(Erdős and Gallai,
[
9
]
)
.

For all
n\geq 2k
and
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\ex(n,kK_{2})=\max\Bigg\{\binom{k-1}{2}+(k-1)(n-k+1),\binom{2k-1}{2}\Bigg\}.

This paper focuses on the rainbow extensions of extremal problems. A subgraph of an edge-colored graph is called
rainbow
if all its edges have distinct colors. For a fixed graph
H
, the
rainbow number

\rb(n,H)
is defined as the minimum number of colors
r
such that every edge-coloring of
K_{n}
with
r
colors contains a rainbow copy of
H
. Equivalently, the
anti-Ramsey number

\text{ar}(n,H)
is the maximum number of colors
r
for which there exists an
r
-edge-coloring of
K_{n}
with no rainbow
H
. It follows from these definitions that
\rb(n,H)=\text{ar}(n,H)+1
. For consistency, we will use the rainbow number throughout this paper.

The study of anti-Ramsey theory was initiated by Erdős, Simonovits, and Sós
[
8
]
, who showed that
\rb(n,K_{r})=\ex(n,K_{r-1})+2
for sufficiently large
n
. This result was later extended to all
n>r\geq 3
by Montellano-Ballesteros and Neumann-Lara
[
24
]
and independently by Schiermeyer
[
25
]
.
For the rainbow number of matchings, Schiermeyer
[
25
]
proved that
\rb(n,kK_{2})=\text{ex}(n,(k-1)K_{2})+2
for
k\geq 3
and
n\geq 3k+3
. Fujita et al.
[
10
]
later obtained the same result for
n\geq 2k+1
. Chen, Li, and Tu
[
4
]
eventually determined the exact values of
\rb(n,kK_{2})
for all
k\geq 3
and
n\geq 2k
. Haas and Young
[
14
]
provided a simplified proof for the case
n=2k
. For further results on rainbow numbers, we refer to the surveys by Kano and Li
[
16
]
and Fujita, Magnant, and Ozeki
[
11
]
.

By considering both the number of edges
e(G)
and the number of colors
c(G)
in an edge-colored graph
G
, Li et al.
[
18
]
established a rainbow version of Mantel’s theorem: if
e(G)+c(G)\geq\binom{n+1}{2}
, then
G
contains a rainbow triangle. This extends an earlier anti-Ramsey result of Erdős, Simonovits, and Sós
[
8
]
. In 2019, Fujita et al.
[
12
]
characterized all graphs
G
satisfying
e(G)+c(G)\geq\binom{n+1}{2}-1
that contain no rainbow triangles. Ehard and Mohr
[
7
]
further generalized the result of Li et al.
[
18
]
by providing a sufficient condition on
e(G)+c(G)
for the existence of
k
rainbow triangles in
G
. The existence of rainbow triangles
[
1
,
3
,
5
]
and the number of vertex-disjoint rainbow triangles
[
15
,
19
,
20
]
in edge-colored graphs have also been extensively studied.

Following this line of research, Xu et al.
[
27
]
extended results on rainbow triangles to larger rainbow cliques. They proved that if
e(G)+c(G)\geq\binom{n}{2}+\rb(n,K_{r})
, then
G
contains a rainbow
K_{r}
, generalizing the results of Montellano-Ballesteros and Neumann-Lara
[
24
]
and Schiermeyer
[
25
]
. Recently, Liu, Peng, and Yang
[
22
]
obtained a supersaturation version of the result of Xu et al.
[
27
]
.

In this paper, we study the existence of rainbow matchings of size
k
in general edge-colored graphs under a condition involving the sum of the number of edges and colors.

Theorem 2
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 12
be integers, and let
G
be an edge-colored graph on
n\geq 2k
vertices. If

\displaystyle e(G)+c(G)\geq\binom{n}{2}+2+\max\Bigg\{\binom{2k-2}{2}-\binom{n-2k+2}{2},\ex(n,(k-1)K_{2})\Bigg\},

then
G
contains a rainbow matching of size
k
.

We note that the condition on
e(G)+c(G)
in Theorem
2
is best possible in a certain sense. Two natural constructions based on extremal graphs for matchings yield the lower bound
\binom{n}{2}+2+\mathrm{ex}(n,(k-1)K_{2})
, while in the third construction, the host graph
G
is obtained from
K_{n}
by removing the edges of a clique.

Let
[n]:={1,2,\ldots,n}
, and
\binom{[n]}{2}:={T\subseteq[n]:|T|=2}
.

Construction I:

G
is the complete graph on
[n]
. Color edges in
\left\{e\in\binom{[n]}{2}:e\cap[k-2]\neq\emptyset\right\}
with
\binom{k-2}{2}+(k-2)(n-k+2)
different colors and the others with a common new color.

Construction II:

G
is the complete graph on
[n]
. Color edges in
\binom{[2k-3]}{2}
with
\binom{2k-3}{2}
different colors, and the others with a common new color.

Construction III:

G=\left\{e\in\binom{[n]}{2}:e\cap[2k-2]\neq\emptyset\right\}
. Color the edges in
\binom{[2k-2]}{2}
with
\binom{2k-2}{2}
different colors and color the others with a common new color.

Preliminaries

Let

For a vertex

(or

(or

(or

The

of two vertex-disjoint graphs

join
, denoted by

and

We use

In an edge-colored graph

, the color of an edge

is denoted by

. The set of edges in
G
sharing the same color as
e
is denoted by

.

A
matching
in
G
is a set of disjoint edges.
A
maximum matching
is one of the largest possible size, and the
matching number

\nu(G)
is the size of a maximum matching. A matching is

if it covers all vertices of
G
.
A graph

is

if

has a perfect matching for every

. We will use the following fundamental result (see Exercise 3.3.18 in
[

]
).

Theorem 3

(Lovász and Plummer,
[
23
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph without a perfect matchings. Then there exists a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
such that:

(i)

every component of
G-S
is factor-critical;

(ii)

every maximum matching in
G
matches each vertex of
S
with vertices in different components of
G-S
.

For positive integers
a\geq b\geq 1
, and a graph
F
, define
ex(a,b,F)
as the maximum number of edges in an
F
-free subgraph of
K_{a,b}
. We will use the following result.

Theorem 4

(Li, Tu, and Jin,
[
21
]
)
.

Let
a\geq b\geq k\geq 3
. Then

\ex(a,b,kK_{2})=a(k-1).

We also require the following classical result on Hamiltonian cycles.

Theorem 5

(Dirac,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n\geq 3
vertices. If
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
, then

is hamiltonian.

Proof of Theorem

Let
G^{r}
be a rainbow subgraph of
G
obtained by selecting exactly one edge from each color class, so that
e(G^{r})=c(G)
. For convenience, define

\displaystyle f_{1}(n,k):=\binom{2k-2}{2}-\binom{n-2k+2}{2}+2,

\displaystyle f_{2}(n,k):=\binom{2k-3}{2}+2,

and

\displaystyle f_{3}(n,k):=\binom{k-2}{2}+(n-k+2)(k-2)+2.

We proceed by contradiction. Suppose that

does not contain a matching of size

. By Theorem

(i), there exists a subset

such that every component of

, let

and fix an arbitrary vertex

. Without loss of generality, assume
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{q}\geq 1

Since

, we have

\displaystyle e(G^{r})=c(G)

\displaystyle\geq\binom{n}{2}+\max\{f_{1}(n,k),f_{2}(n,k),f_{3}(n,k)\}-e(G)

\displaystyle\geq\max\{f_{1}(n,k),f_{2}(n,k),f_{3}(n,k)\}

\displaystyle\geq\ex(n,(k-1)K_{2})+2,

(1)

which implies that

has a matching of size

\nu(G^{r})=k-1
.
By Theorem
3
(i) and (ii), we obtain

\displaystyle\nu(G^{r})=k-1=s+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{d_{i}-1}{2},

(2)

\displaystyle|V(G^{r})|=n=s+\sum_{i=1}^{q}d_{i},

(3)

and

\displaystyle e(G^{r})=e(G^{r}[S])+e_{G^{r}}(S,V(G^{r})\setminus S)+\sum_{i=1}^{q}e(D_{i})\leq\binom{s}{2}+(n-s)s+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\binom{d_{i}}{2}.

(4)

For three integers

\displaystyle\binom{a}{2}+\binom{b}{2}\leq\binom{a+b-c}{2}+\binom{c}{2}.

Lemma 7

By (

Bergman metric on a Stein manifold with nonpositive constant holomorphic sectional curvature
Xiaojun Huang, Song-Ying Li

Abstract
We prove that the Bergman space of a Stein manifold separates points whenever its Bergman metric is well defined and has non-positive constant holomorphic sectional curvature. This, combined with early proved results, shows that a Stein manifold cannot admit a well-defined flat Bergman metric, and that it has a well-defined Bergman metric with negative constant holomorphic sectional curvature if and only if it is biholomorphic to the unit ball of the same dimensin possibly with a pluri-polar set removed. The proof is based on the Hörmander’s L 2 L^{2} -estimate for ∂ ¯ \overline{\partial} -equations; and the curvature condition together with Calabi’s rigidity and extension theorems is used to construct the required bounded strictly plurisubharmonic functions. 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification: 32Q05, 32Q10, 32Q30, 53B35, 53C24

Introduction

Bergman metrics and Bergman kernels of complex manifolds are important invariants in complex analysis and geometry, with profound connections to many central problems in the field. The study of Bergman metrics dates back to the pioneering work of Stefan Bergman
[
Ber1
]
, Bochner
[
Bo
]
, S. Kobayashi
[
Ko
]
, and other mathematicians. Among the classical results related to the present work is a theorem of Lu
[
Lu
]
, which states that a bounded domain with complete Bergman metric has constant holomorphic sectional curvature if and only if is biholomorphic to the unit ball. A remarkable feature of Lu’s theorem is that there is no a prior topological assumption on
M
. Notice that a domain with complete Bergman metric is pseudoconvex or Stein by a classical result of Bremermann
[
Bre
]
. However Bergman metrics of many pseudoconvex domains are not complete.

For a complex manifold of complex dimension
n
, consider the Bergman space
A^{2}(M)
, consisting of holomorphic
n
-forms
\phi
such that
|\int_{M}\phi\wedge\overline{\phi}|<\infty
. The space
A^{2}(M)
is said to
separate points of
M
if for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in M
there exits
\phi\in A^{2}(M)
such that
\phi(p_{1})=0
and
\phi(p_{2})=1
.
In this paper, the question of when the Bergman space
A^{2}(M)
separates points of
M
is studied, motivated by a classical folklore conjecture that attempts to generalize the Qi-Keng–Lu theorem to pseudoconvex domains with incomplete Bergman metrics. This folklore conjecture states that a bounded pseudoconvex domain has negative constant holomorphic sectional curvature if and only if it is biholomorphic to the unit ball with possibly a pluri-polar set removed. Notable partial results had been obtained earlier by Dong–Wong
[
DW1
,
DW2
]
.
In
[
HuLi1T
]
it was completely answered affirmatively. In fact, it was proved in
[
HuLi1T
]
that a Stein manifold with a well-defined Bergman metric of negative constant holomorphic sectional curvature is biholomorphic to the unit ball with possibly a pluripolar set removed, under the additional assumption that its Bergman space separates points, which holds automatically when
M\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is a bounded domain. It was also shown in
[
HuLi1T
]
that a Stein manifold with a well-defined Bergman metric cannot have a flat Bergman metric if its Bergman space separates points. (This result was a joint work of the authors with J. Treuer). An interesting question that was left open from the work of
[
HuLi1T
]
is whether the point-separation assumption is actually needed in these results. The main result of the present paper answers these questions affirmatively. More precisely, we will prove the following:(For further explanation of the terminology used here, see the next section.)

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a Stein manifold. If its Bergman metric is well-defined and has a non-positive constant holomorphic sectional curvature, then
A^{2}(M)
separates points of
M
.

It is known that the holomorphic sectional curvature
c_{{\mathbb{B}}^{n}}
of the Bergman metric on the unit ball
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is
-2/(n+1)
.
Combining Theorem
1.1
with the just discussed results in
[

]
, one obtains the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a Stein manifold of complex dimension
n
. If the Bergman metric of
M
is well-defined and has a non-positive constant holomorphic sectional curvature, denoted by
c
, then
c=c_{{\mathbb{B}}^{n}}=-2/(n+1)
and

is biholomorphic to
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\setminus E\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
where
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
is the unit ball in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and

is a pluripolar subset of in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.

We mention a recent work of Ebenfelt–Treuer–Xiao
[
ETX1
]
and
[
ETX2
]
, in which the authors proved that the Bergman metric of a bounded (not necessarily pseudoconvex) domain

has constant negative holomorphic sectional curvature if and only if

is biholomorphic a domain of the form

, where

a closed subset of measure zero that is a removable set for
L^{2}
-integrable holomorphic functions in
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\setminus E

Bergman metrics have been extensively studied for almost a century since Bergman’s foundational work, yet the theory remains far from complete, and any new result about these metrics provides important motivation for further study in complex analysis and geometry. Here we mention the work in
[

,
[
Mo1

,

]
,
[

Question 1.3

Let
M

2
Preliminaries

Let
M
be a complex manifold of complex dimension
n\geq 1
.
Write
\Omega^{n}(M)
for the space of holomorphic
n
-forms on
M
and define the Bergman space of
M
to be

A^{2}(M):=\Big\{f\in\Omega^{n}(M):\ \ b_{n}\int_{\Omega}f\wedge\overline{f}<\infty\Big\},\ \ \hbox{where}\ \ b_{n}=(i)^{n}(-1)^{\frac{n^{2}-n}{2}}.

(2.1)

Notice that the constant
b_{n}
is chosen so that
for a
L^{2}
integrable holomorphic function
g
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
f=gdz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}
,
b_{n}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}f\wedge\overline{f}=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|g|^{2}d{\rm{vol}}
. Here we write
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
and
d{\rm{vol}}=2^{n}dx_{1}\wedge dy_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n}\wedge dy_{n}
.

A^{2}(M)
is a Hilbert space with
inner product defined by:

(f,g)=b_{n}\int_{M}f\wedge\overline{g},\quad~\text{for all}~f,g\in A^{2}(M).

(2.2)

Assume
A^{2}(M)\not=0
. Let
\{f_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
be an orthonormal
basis of
A^{2}(M)
with
N\leq\infty
and define the Bergman kernel, which is an
(n,n)
-form (see, e.g.,
[
Ko
]
), to
be
K_{M}=i\sum_{j=1}^{N}f_{j}\wedge\overline{f}_{j}
. In a local holomorphic coordinate
chart
(U,z)
on
\Omega
, we have

K_{M}=ik_{M}(z,\overline{z})dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}\wedge d\overline{z}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\overline{z}_{n}~\text{ in }~U.

(2.3)

Assume that
A^{2}(M)
is base-point free in the sense that
K_{M}
is nowhere zero on
M
.
We then well define a semi-positive Hermitian
(1,1)
-form on
M
by

\omega^{B}_{M}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\log k_{M}(z,\overline{z}).

We call
\omega^{B}_{M}
the Bergman form of
M
. If it induces a positive definite metric on
M
, then it is a Kähler metric and is called the Bergman metric of
M
:

d^{2}s:=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{k}\overline{\partial}z_{j}}\left(\log k_{M}(z,\overline{z})\right)dz_{k}\otimes d\overline{z_{j}}.

Bergman space
A^{2}(M)
is said to
separate points
if for

p_{1},p_{2}\in M
with
p_{1}\not=p_{2}
, there is a
holomorphic
n
-form
\phi\in A^{2}(M)
such that
\phi(p_{1})=0
but
\phi(p_{2})\not=0
.

We say that
A^{2}(\Omega)

separates holomorphic
directions
if for any
p\in M
and a non-zero
X_{p}\in T_{p}^{(1,0)}M
,
there is a
\phi\in A^{2}(M)
such that
\phi(p)=0
and
X_{p}(\phi)(p)\not=0
in a
local holomorphic chart near
p
. Here
\phi
is identified with a
holomorphWeic function near
p
as described above.
Kobayashi
[
Ko
]
proved that the Bergman metric on
M
is well defined if and only if the Bergman space
A^{2}(M)
is base point free and separates holomorphic directions.
For the Bergman metric to be well-defined, it is not necessary for
A^{2}(M)
to separate points; however, this property holds automatically in many cases, such as when
M
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Indeed, in this paper, we investigate how curvature conditions influence the point separation property of the Bergman metrics.

We define the infinite dimensional projective space, denoted by
{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}
, as

{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}:=\ell^{2}\setminus\{0\}/\sim,\ \hbox{where }\ell^{2}:=\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m},\cdots):\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|x_{j}|^{2}<\infty\}.

Here, for any given

x\sim y
if and only if
x=ky
for a
certain
k\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}.
For

, we write
[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},\cdots]
for its
equivalence class in
{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}
, called the homogeneous coordinate
of the equivalence class of

.
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
is naturally identified as
a closed subspace of
{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}
by adding zeros to homogeneous
coordinates of
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
.

A map
{\cal F}:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}
is called a holomorphic map from

M
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}
if there is a holomorphic representation
[F]

of
{\cal F}
near each
p\in M
. Namely, for each
p\in M
, there
is a small neighborhood
U_{p}
of

in
M
such that
{\cal F}=[F]

with
F=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m},\cdots)
, where each
f_{j}
with
j\in{\mathbb{N}}

is a holomorphic function in

and for each
q\in U_{p}
, there is
a
k
, which may depend on

, such that
f_{k}(q)\not=0
.
Moreover,
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|f_{j}(z)|^{2}
is required to converge
uniformly on compact subsets of

.
We denote by
X_{\mathcal{F}}=[X_{F}]\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
with
X_{F}
being the closed linear subspace of
\ell^{2}
generated by the linear span of
F(M)
in
\ell^{2}
.

X_{\mathcal{F}}
is independent of the choice of local holomorphic
representations of
\mathcal{F}
.

The formal Fubini–Study metric on

, denoted by
\omega_{st}
, with homogeneous coordinates

[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},\dots],

is formally defined by

\omega_{st}=i\partial\overline{\partial}\log\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|z_{j}|^{2}\Big).

(2.4)

Let

\mathcal{F}:\ (M,\omega)\to({\mathbb{P}}^{\infty},\omega_{st})

(2.5)

be a holomorphic map, where
\omega
is a Kähler metric over

. If
for any local holomorphic representation

\mathcal{F}=[f_{1},\cdots,f_{n},\cdots]
over

\omega=\mathcal{F}^{*}(\omega_{st}):=i\partial\overline{\partial}\log\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|f_{j}(z)|^{2}\Big)\quad\hbox{over }U,

(2.6)

we call

3
Negative constant holomorphic sectional curvature

Let
M
be a complex manifold and assume that
A^{2}(M)
is base-point free. For an
orthonormal basis
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
, we then have a well-defined
holomorphic map

\mathcal{B}:=[\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{N},0,\cdots],\ N<\infty;\ \ \hbox{or }\mathcal{B}:=[\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{m},\cdots]\ \hbox{for }N=\infty

from
M
into

{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}
. We also write
B=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots)
. Thus
\mathcal{B}=[B]
.
\mathcal{B}
is called a Bergman-Bochner map and is a local holomorphic isometric embedding from
M
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}
.
Then
A^{2}(M)
separates points if and only if any Bergman-Bochner
map
{\cal B}:M\to{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}
is one-to-one.
Suppose that the holomorphic sectional curvature of
\omega_{M}
is a
negative constant on
M
. Then for
p_{0}\in M
, by Theorem 6 in [Bo], there is a biholomorphic map
F

from a small neighborhood
U
of
p_{0}\in M
to a subdomain in the unit ball
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
such that

F^{*}(\lambda\omega_{{\mathbb{B}}^{n}})=\omega_{M}
for a certain positive constant
\lambda
.

Now one can construct as in
[
HuLi1T
]

a one-to-one holomorphic isometric embedding
\mathcal{T}=[1,P_{1},\cdots,P_{j},\cdots]
from
({\mathbb{B}}^{n},\mu\omega_{{\mathbb{B}}^{n}})
into

\mathbb{P}^{\infty},
where
P_{j}^{\prime}s
are holomorphic monomials in
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Moreover
P_{j}=a_{j}z_{j}
with
a_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}\not=0
for
j=1,\cdots,n
. We similarly write
T=(1,P_{1},\cdots,P_{j},\cdots).

Then
{\mathcal{T}}\circ F:U\to{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}
is a one to one
holomorphic isometric embedding.
By the Calabi rigidity theorem
(
[
Ca
]
or
[
HuLi1T
]
), there is a one to one isometric
isomorphism
{\cal L}:X_{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow X_{\mathcal{T}}
such that

{\cal L}\circ{\mathcal{B}}(z)={\mathcal{T}}\circ F(z),\quad z\in U.

(3.1)

Notice that
{\cal L}=[L]
is induced by a linear isometric isomorphism

{T}
from the closed linear subspace
X_{B}\subset\ell^{2}
to
X_{T}\subset\ell^{2}
. By the
Calabi extension theorem (
[
Ca
]
or
[
HuLi2
]
),
{\mathcal{T}}\circ F
extends along any curve in
M
initiated from
p_{0}\in U
to a local holomorphic isometric embedding. Since each
z_{j}
is a
component in
{\mathcal{T}}
up to a coefficient, we see that
F

extends holomorphically and isometrically along any curve in
M

starting at
p_{0}
to
\widetilde{F}
.
\widetilde{F}
a priori might be
multi-valued.
Since the Kähler metric
\lambda\omega_{{\mathbb{B}}^{n}}
is
complete,
\widetilde{F}
has image in
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
and thus (
3.1
) shows that
\widetilde{F}
, still denoted by
F
in the following, is globally defined in
M
, which is a local biholomorphism.

Note that (
3.1
) holds over
M
. Hence for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in M
,
\mathcal{B}(p_{1})=\mathcal{B}(p_{2})
if and only if
F(p_{1})=F(p_{2})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a Stein manifold of complex dimension
n\geq 1
. Assume that there exists a local biholomorphic map
F:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
Assume further that there is a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function
\sigma
on

that is bounded from above. Then

separates points of

.

The above proposition together with the result in
[
HuLi1T
]
gives the following:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
M
be a Stein manifold of complex dimension
n
. If the Bergman metric of
M
is well-defined and has a negative constant holomorphic sectional curvature, denoted by
c
, then
M
is biholomorphic to
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\setminus E\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
where
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
is the unit ball and
E
is a pluripolar set of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Moreover,

c=c_{{\mathbb{B}}^{n}}=-2/(n+1)
.

Our proof is based on the Hörmander
L^{2}
method for solving
\overline{\partial}
-equations on a Stein manifold. We first recall the classical Bochner-Kodaira-Morrey-Kohn identity on a smoothly compactly supported domain in a Kähler manifold:

Assume that
(M,\omega)
is a Kähler manifold. Let
\Omega\subset\subset M
be a smoothly bounded relatively compact domain with
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
being a defining function. Namely,
\rho|_{\Omega}<0
and
d\rho|_{\partial\Omega}\not=0
. By multiplying a positive smooth function over
\overline{\Omega}
to
\rho
, if needed, we assume that
|d\rho|\equiv 1
in a small neighborhood
\partial\Omega
in
\overline{\Omega}
. Let
E
be a Hermitian holomorphic line bundle over
M
. Write
\overline{\partial}^{E}
for the
\overline{\partial}
-operator sending an
E
-valued
(0,q)
-form
\alpha\in\Lambda^{0,q}(E)
to an
E
-valued
(0,q+1)
-form:
\overline{\partial}\alpha\in\Lambda^{0,q+1}(E)
. Still write
\overline{\partial}^{E}
for its closed extension from
L^{2}_{(0,q)}(M,E)
into
L^{2}_{(0,q+1)}(M,E)
, which is then a densely defined closed operator. Write
\overline{\partial}^{E*}
for the Hilbert adjoint operator of
\overline{\partial}^{E}
.

Let
\phi\in\Lambda^{(0,1)}(\overline{\Omega})\cap\hbox{Dom}({\overline{\partial}}^{E*})
. Then we have the following special case of the classical Bochner-Kodaira-Morrey-Kohn formula (see, for instance, [pp 119, Siu] or [Theorem 1.4.21,
[
MaMa
]
]):

\|\overline{\partial}^{E}\phi\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}^{E*}\phi\|_{\Omega}^{2}=\|\overline{\nabla}^{E}\phi\|_{\Omega}^{2}+(\hbox{Ric}(\phi),\phi)_{\Omega}+(K(\phi),\phi)_{\Omega}+(\hbox{Levi}_{\rho}(\phi),\phi)|_{\partial\Omega}.

(3.2)

We explain the notations used above as follows:
Let
(U,(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}))
be a holomorphic coordinate patch of
M
, over which
E
is also trivialized. Write
e
for a basis of
E
over
U
. Write
\sum_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}g_{a\overline{\beta}}dz_{\alpha}\otimes d\overline{z_{\beta}}
for the Kähler metric tensor over
U
with the associated Kähler form
\omega=i\sum_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}g_{a\overline{\beta}}dz_{\alpha}\wedge d\overline{z_{\beta}}
. Write
g=\hbox{det}(g_{k\overline{l}})
and
e^{-h}=<e,e>
with respect to the Hermitan product in
E
. Over
U
the Ricci tensor
R_{\overline{v}}^{\overline{\alpha}}
of
M
and the curvature tensor
K_{\overline{v}}^{\overline{\alpha}}
of
E
are given by

R_{{\lambda}\overline{v}}=-\partial_{{\lambda}}{\partial}_{\overline{v}}\log(g),\ \ \ R_{\overline{l}}^{\overline{\alpha}}=g^{\overline{\alpha}{\lambda}}R_{{\lambda}\overline{v}};\ \ \ K_{{\lambda}\overline{v}}=-\partial_{{\lambda}}{\partial}_{\overline{v}}\log(e^{-h}),\ \ \ K_{\overline{v}}^{\overline{\alpha}}=g^{\overline{\alpha}{\lambda}}K_{{\lambda}\overline{v}}.

The Levi form of
\rho
, denoted by
\hbox{Levi}_{\rho}:=\partial_{\alpha}\overline{\partial_{\beta}}\rho dz_{\alpha}\otimes d\overline{z_{\beta}}
, is the complex Hessian of
\rho
but restricted to the complex tangent bundle of type
(1,0)
along
\partial\Omega
.
\nabla^{E}
is the unique Hermitian connection on
\Lambda^{(0,q)}(E)
for
q\geq 0

associated with the Kähler metric of
M
and
the Hermitian metric of
E
.

Write
\phi=\phi_{j}d\overline{z_{j}}\otimes e
.
Then

\begin{split}&(\hbox{Ricc}(\phi),\phi)_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}R_{\overline{\alpha}}^{\overline{\beta}}\phi_{\overline{\beta}}\overline{\phi^{\alpha}}e^{-h}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!},\\
&(K(\phi),\phi)_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}K_{\overline{\alpha}}^{\overline{\beta}}\phi_{\overline{\beta}}\overline{\phi^{\alpha}}e^{-h}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!},\\
&(\hbox{Levi}_{\rho}(\phi),\phi)|_{\partial\Omega}=\int_{\partial\Omega}g^{s\overline{t}}\partial_{s}\partial_{\overline{j}}(\rho){\phi_{\overline{t}}}\overline{\phi^{j}}e^{-h}\hbox{d}\hbox{vol}(\partial\Omega).\end{split}

(3.3)

Here
\phi^{t}=g^{\overline{{\lambda}}t}\phi_{\overline{{\lambda}}}
,
\hbox{d}\hbox{vol}(\partial\Omega)
is the induced volume form on
\partial\Omega
. Namely,
\hbox{d}\hbox{vol}(\partial\Omega)=i_{L}(\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!})
, the contraction of
\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}
with the unit outward normal vector of
\partial\Omega
.

\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}h_{\epsilon}}{\partial z_{i}\partial\overline{z_{j}}}\xi_{i}\overline{\xi_{j}}\geq C_{1}|\xi|^{2}\ \hbox{over }U(p_{1})\cup U(p_{2}).

We let
A
be the multiplication operator by the characteristic function

\chi_{G_{p_{1}}}(B(z_{1},\frac{\delta}{2}))\cup G_{p_{2}}(B(z_{2},\frac{\delta}{2})),

namely, for each
\phi\in L^{2}_{(0,1)}(\Omega_{r_{j}},E)
,

A(\phi)=\chi_{G_{p_{1}}}(B(z_{1},\frac{\delta}{2}))\cup G_{p_{2}}(B(z_{2},\frac{\delta}{2}))\phi

which is
\phi
in
G_{p_{1}}(B(z_{1},\frac{\delta}{2}))\cup G_{p_{2}}(B(z_{2},\frac{\delta}{2}))
and
0
otherwise.
Notice that
\partial\Omega_{r_{j}}
is strongly pseudoconvex and thus the Levi form is positive definite. Also notice that the metric over
M
is flat with an orthonormal basis
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{n}}\}
in a holomorphic coordinate patch
through
z=F(p)
.
In a holomorphic chart given by
z=F(p)
mentioned above, for
\phi\in\Lambda^{(0,1)}(\overline{\Omega_{r_{j}}},E)\cap Dom(\overline{\partial}^{E*}):=\sum\phi_{\overline{k}}{dz_{\overline{k}}}\otimes(dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n})
, the Bochner-Kodaira-Morrey-Kohn formula gives the following estimate

\begin{split}&\|\overline{\partial}^{E}\phi\|_{\Omega_{r_{j}},h_{\epsilon}}^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}^{E*}\phi\|_{\Omega_{r_{j}},h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\\
&=\|\overline{\nabla}^{E}\phi\|_{\Omega_{r_{j}},h_{\epsilon}}^{2}+(\hbox{Ricc}(\phi),\phi)_{\Omega_{r_{j}},h_{\epsilon}}+(K(\phi),\phi)_{\Omega_{r_{j}},h_{\epsilon}}+(\hbox{Levi}_{\rho}(\phi),\phi)|_{\partial\Omega_{r_{j}},h_{\epsilon}}\\
&\geq\int_{\Omega_{r_{j}}}K_{\overline{\alpha}}^{\overline{\beta}}\phi_{\overline{\beta}}\overline{\phi^{\alpha}}e^{-h_{\epsilon}(p)}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}\\
&\geq\int_{U(z_{1},\frac{\delta}{2})}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\partial_{j}\partial_{\overline{k}}h_{\epsilon}(p)\phi^{j}{\phi^{\overline{k}}}\ e^{-h_{\epsilon}(p)}d{\lambda}\\
&\geq C_{1}\|A(\phi)\|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.4)

Define
g(p)=\chi_{0}(p)F^{*}(dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n})
over
U(p_{1},\delta)
and to be zero otherwise. Then
g
is a smooth section of
E
compactly supported in
G_{p_{1}}(z_{1},\frac{\delta}{2})
.
Then
\eta:=\overline{\partial}{g}\in\Lambda_{c}^{(0,1)}(\Omega_{r_{j}},E)
with
\overline{\partial}\eta=0
.
Notice that
A\eta=A^{*}\eta=\eta
.

By the Hörmander smoothing lemma
[
Hor
]
, (
3.4
) holds for any
\phi\in\hbox{Dom}(\overline{\partial}^{E}|_{\Omega_{r_{j}},h_{\epsilon}})\cap\hbox{Dom}(\overline{\partial^{E}}^{*}|_{\Omega_{r_{j}},h_{\epsilon}})
. By Theorem 1.1.4 of
[
Hor
]
and the regularity of solutions of the
\overline{\partial}
-equation,
we can find a
\psi_{\epsilon,j}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{r_{j}},(E,h_{\epsilon}))\cap\Lambda^{(0,0)}(\Omega_{r_{j}},E)
such that

\overline{\partial}\psi_{\epsilon,j}=\overline{\partial}\eta
with

\|\psi_{\epsilon,j}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{r_{j}},h_{\epsilon}}\leq\frac{1}{C_{1}}\|^{2}\overline{\partial}\eta\|_{\Omega_{r_{j}},h_{\epsilon}}=\frac{1}{C_{1}}\int_{U(p_{1},\delta)\setminus U(p_{1},\frac{\delta}{4})}<\overline{\partial}\eta,\overline{\partial}\eta>\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

Notice that

\begin{split}&e^{-h_{\epsilon}}=e^{-\sigma}{1\over\big((|F(p)-F(p_{1})|^{2}+\epsilon)(|F(p)-F(p_{2})|^{2}+\epsilon)\big)^{n\chi_{0}(p)}}\geq C_{2}\ \ \hbox{over}\ \ M,\\
&e^{-h_{\epsilon}}\leq C_{3}\ \hbox{over }\ U(p_{1})\setminus U(p_{1},\frac{\delta}{4}),\\
&\|\psi_{\epsilon,j}\|_{\Omega_{r_{j}},h_{\epsilon}}^{2}=b_{n}\int_{\Omega_{r_{j}}}<\psi_{\epsilon,j},\psi_{\epsilon,j}>\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}\geq C_{4}\big|\int_{\Omega_{r_{j}}}\psi_{\epsilon,j}\wedge\overline{\psi_{\epsilon,j}}e^{-h_{\epsilon}}\big|\geq C_{4}C_{2}\big|\int_{\Omega_{r_{j}}}\psi_{\epsilon,j}\wedge\overline{\psi_{\epsilon,j}}\big|,\end{split}

(3.5)

where
C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}
are certain positive numbers independent of
j
and
\epsilon
. Hence, we get that the sequence
\{\psi_{\epsilon,j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
has a uniformly bounded Bergman norm (
L^{2}
norm). Now, by a standard use of the normal family argument to
\{\psi_{\epsilon,j}-\psi_{\epsilon,1}\}
and by passing to a subsequence if needed, we can assume

\{\psi_{\epsilon,j}-\psi_{\epsilon,1}\}_{j}
and thus
\{\psi_{\epsilon,j}\}

converges uniformly on any compact subset of
M
to
\psi_{\epsilon}\in A^{2}(\Omega_{j})
such that
\|\psi_{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{M,h_{\epsilon}}\leq C
. Letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we can assume
\psi_{\epsilon}
converges, along a certain subsequence, uniformly on compact subsets of
M
to
\psi\in\Lambda^{(0,0)}(M,E)\cap L^{2}_{(0,0)}(M,E)
with
\overline{\partial}\psi=\eta
.
Write
\psi=\psi_{0}F^{*}(dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n})
in
M
. Applying the Fatou lemma,
we get

\int_{M}\frac{|\psi_{0}|^{2}e^{-\sigma}}{\big(|F(p)-F(p_{1})||F(p)-F(p_{2})|\big)^{2n\chi_{0}}}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}\leq C

which forces
\psi_{0}(p_{1})=0
and also
\psi_{0}(p_{2})=0
.

Thus
G=\psi-g\in A^{2}(\Omega)
. We then have
G(p_{1})=-g(p_{1})\not=0
but
G(p_{2})=g(p_{2})=0
. This conclude the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

with

, which is strongly plurisubharmonic as

4
Flat Bergman metrics

We prove in this section the following:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
M
be a Stein manifold of complex dimension
n\geq 1
. Assume its Bergman metric of
M
is well-defined and has constant zero holomorphic sectional curvature. Then its Bergman space must separate points on
M
.

This together with the result by the authors and Treuer proved in
[
HuLi1T
]
gives the following:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
M
be a Stein manifold of complex dimension
n\geq 1
. Assume the Bergman metric of
M
is well-defined.
Then it can not be flat, i.e.,
it can not have constant zero holomorphic sectional
curvature on
M
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Let
M
be a Stein manifold.
Assume that the Bergman metric of
M
exists and has constant zero holomorphic sectional curvature.
Let
p_{0}\in M
and let

(U,z)
with
0\in z(p_{0})
be a holomorphic coordinate chart. (Still
write
U
for
z(U)
for brevity when there is no confusion).
Shrinking
U
if needed, there is a biholomorphic map
F:=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n})
from
U

to a neighborhood
V\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
of
0
such that
F(p_{0})=0
and

F^{*}(\omega_{eucl})=\omega_{M}^{B},
where
\omega_{eucl}=i\partial\overline{\partial}|z|^{2}=i\partial\overline{\partial}\log(e^{|z|^{2}})
is the
Euclidean form of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
d^{2}s_{eucl}=2\hbox{Re}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}dz_{j}\otimes\overline{dz_{j}}\right).

Notice that there is a holomorphic isometric embedding
\cal P
from

({\mathbb{C}}^{n},\omega_{euclid})
into
({\mathbb{P}}^{\infty},\omega_{st}^{\infty})
. Indeed, write
e^{|z|^{2}}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^{2j}}{j!}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}|z_{k}|^{2}+\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}|P_{\ell}|^{2}
with
P_{\ell}

certain holomorphic monomials of degree at least 2.
We can then define

{\cal P}=[1,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},P_{2},\cdots,P_{\ell},\cdots]
.
By the Calabi rigidity theorem
[
Ca
,
HuLi2
]
,
there is a one-to-one linear isometric embedding
\cal L
from
X_{\cal P}\subset({\mathbb{P}}^{\infty},\omega_{st}^{\infty})
into
({\mathbb{P}}^{\infty},\omega_{st}^{\infty})
such that

{\cal B}={\cal L}\circ{\cal P}\circ F,

where
\mathcal{B}
is a Bergman-Bochner map of
M
.
Since
{\cal B},{\cal L}
and
{\cal P}
are one-to-one, by the Calabi extension theorem
[
Ca
,
HuLi2
]
, one also concludes immediately that
F
extends to a local isometric biholomorphism from
M
onto a certain domain denoted by
D
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.

Next notice that
F^{*}(i\partial\overline{\partial}\log(e^{|z|^{2}}))=i\partial\overline{\partial}|F(p)|^{2}.

Let
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N}
be an orthonormal basis of the
Bergman space
A^{2}(M)
with
\phi_{0}(p_{0})\not=0
and
\phi_{j}(p_{0})=0
for

j\geq 1
. Write
\phi_{j}=\lambda_{j}\phi_{0}
near
p_{0}
for
j\geq 1
. Then

K_{M}(p,p)=\phi_{0}(p)\wedge\overline{\phi_{0}(p)}(1+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|^{2})
.
From the local isometric property of
F
,
we have near
p_{0}

i\partial\overline{\partial}\log(1+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|^{2})=i\partial\overline{\partial}\log(e^{|F(z)|^{2}}).

Since
\lambda_{j}(p_{0})=0
and
F(p_{0})=0
, which are both holomorphic, we conclude that near
p_{0}

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{|F|^{2j}}{j!}.

In the local coordinates mentioned above, we have
F(z)=z
and thus
\{F^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
is a linear independent set. Recall also the binormial expansion
(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n})^{k}=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{k!}{\alpha!}x^{\alpha}
which can be easily seen by applying
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}
of both sides
with
|\alpha|=k
.
Write
\Lambda=(1,\cdots,\lambda_{j}(p),\cdots)
. Then
We have

\|\Lambda\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|^{2}=\sum_{|\beta|\geq 1}^{\infty}\big|\frac{F^{\beta}}{\sqrt{\beta!}}\big|^{2}.

Making use of the Calabi rigidity theorem or the D’Angelo lemma
[
DA
]
,
there is a linear one-to-one and surjective isometry
L
from
X_{F_{0}}\subset\ell^{2}
to
X_{\Lambda}\subset\ell^{2}

such that

(1,\cdots,\lambda_{j}(p),\cdots)=L\left((1,f_{1}(p),\cdots,f_{n}(p),\cdots,\frac{F^{\beta}(p)}{\sqrt{\beta!}},\cdots)\right),\ \hbox{ and }

\left(1,f_{1}(p),\cdots,f_{n}(p),\cdots,\frac{F^{\beta}(p)}{\sqrt{\beta!}},\cdots\right)=L^{-1}\left((1,\cdots,\lambda_{j}(p),\cdots)\right).

Here
F_{0}=(1,f_{1}(p),\cdots,f_{n}(p),\cdots,\frac{F^{\beta}(p)}{\sqrt{\beta!}},\cdots)
.
Hence we conclude that

\{\phi_{0},\phi_{0}f_{1},\cdots,\phi_{0}f_{n},\cdots,\phi_{0}\frac{F^{\beta}(p)}{\sqrt{\beta!}},\cdots\}

is also an orthonormal basis of
A^{2}(M)
. In particular, since
A^{2}(M)
is base point free, we see that
\phi_{0}\not=0

We equip
M
with its Bergman-Kähler metric
\omega^{B}_{M}=F^{*}(\omega_{eucl})
which is flat. For
p_{0}\in M
, as mentioned above, we also use
z=F(p)
as a local coordinate for
p\approx p_{0}
. Then as in the previous discussion,
we have for any
\alpha,\beta\in A^{2}(M)
,

(\alpha,\beta)=\int_{M}<\alpha,\beta>d{\rm{vol}}_{M}=b_{n}\int_{M}\alpha\wedge\overline{\beta}\ \ \hbox{with }b_{n}={{(i)}^{n}}(-1)^{\frac{n^{2}-n}{2}},

where
d\rm{vol}_{M}=\frac{\omega^{n}_{B}}{n!}
is the volume form induced by the metric
\omega_{eucl}=i\partial\overline{\partial}(|z|^{2})
. We will write
|\alpha|^{2}=<\alpha,\alpha>
for any
\alpha\in A^{2}(M).

Then the orthonormality of

\{\phi_{0},\phi_{0}f_{1},\cdots,\phi_{0}f_{n},\cdots,\phi_{0}\frac{F^{\beta}(p)}{\sqrt{\beta!}},\cdots\}

gives the following:

b_{n}\int_{M}F^{\alpha}\overline{F^{\beta}}\phi_{0}\wedge\overline{\phi}_{0}=\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}\alpha!.

Let
D=F(M)
and let
\nu
be the pull back measure on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
defined by

\nu(E)=\int_{F^{-1}(E\cap D)}<\phi_{0}(q),{\phi_{0}(q)}>d{\rm{vol}}_{M}(q)=b_{n}\int_{F^{-1}(E\cap D)}<\phi_{0}(q),{\phi_{0}(q)}>

for any Lebesgue measurable set
E\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
Then

L^{\mu_{1}}[z^{\alpha}\overline{z}^{\beta}]:=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}z^{\alpha}\overline{z}^{\beta}d\nu=b_{n}\int_{M}\overline{F(q)}^{\beta}F(q)^{\alpha}\phi_{0}(q)\wedge\overline{\phi_{0}(q)}=\alpha!\delta_{\alpha\beta}.

On the other hand, by a direct computation (e.g., see (5.19) of
[
HuLi1T
]
), letting
dv
be the Lebesque measure and

d\mu=\pi^{-n}e^{-|z|^{2}}dv,

then

L^{\mu}[z^{\alpha}\overline{z}^{\beta}]:=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}z^{\alpha}\overline{z}^{\beta}d\mu=\alpha!\delta_{\alpha\beta},

where
dv
is the Lebesque measure on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
By the classical uniqueness of the moment problem (see, for example, Theorem 5.1 in
[
HuLi1T
]
or
[
Sch
]
), one has

d\nu=d\mu.

This, in particular, implies that
D={\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus E
for a certain
E
with
m(E)=0
.

For any
z_{0}\in D
,
F^{-1}(z_{0})
has no accumulation point in
M
. Let
F^{-1}(z_{0})=\{q_{1},\cdots,q_{N}\}
with
N
being finite or
\infty
.
Then for any given
q_{j}
, let
\delta_{j}>0
be sufficiently small such that
F
is biholomorphic from a neighborhood
U(q_{j},\delta_{j})
of
q_{j}
, which is contained in a coordinate chart defined above near
q_{j}
, to
B(z_{0},\delta_{j})
with inverse
G_{j}(z)
.
Notice that in the holomorphic charts we are using,
F
is simply the identity map. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\int_{F^{-1}\left(B(q_{j},\delta_{j})\right)}|\phi_{0}(q)|^{2}dvol_{M}(q)=\int_{B(z_{0},\delta_{j})}d\mu(z)=\int_{B(z_{0},\delta_{j})}\pi^{-n}e^{-|z|^{2}}dv(z)

and

\displaystyle\int_{F^{-1}\left(B(q_{j},\delta_{j})\right)}|\phi_{0}(q)|^{2}dvol_{M}(q)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{U(q_{j},\delta_{j})}|\phi_{0}(q)|^{2}dvol_{M}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B(z_{0},\delta_{j})}|\phi_{0}(G_{j}(z))|^{2}|G_{j}^{*}(dvol_{M}(q))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B(z_{0},\delta_{j})}|\phi_{0}(G_{j}(z))|^{2}dv(z).

Here we notice that the last equality holds as
G_{j}
is a holomorphic isometry.
Since
\delta_{j}
could be made arbitrarily small, it thus follows that

\pi^{-n}e^{-|z_{0}|^{2}}\geq|\phi_{0}(q_{j})|^{2},\ \hbox{or }e^{-|F(q_{j})|^{2}}\geq\pi^{n}|\phi_{0}(q_{j})|^{2}.

(4.1)

By the arbitrariness of
q_{j}
, we have for any
q\in M
the following:

\pi^{n}e^{|F(q)|^{2}}\big|\phi_{0}(q)\big|^{2}\leq 1

(4.2)

and thus

\sigma(z):=|F(q)|^{2}+\log|\phi_{0}(q)|^{2}\leq-n\log\pi.

(4.3)

Notice that
|F(q)|^{2}
is strongly plurisubharmonic and
\log|\phi_{0}(q)|^{2}
is pluriharmonic. Thus
\sigma
is a smooth strongly plurisubharmonic function over
M
bounded from above.
By Proposition
3.1
, we conclude
the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

It follows immediately from Theorems
3.2
and
4.2
.

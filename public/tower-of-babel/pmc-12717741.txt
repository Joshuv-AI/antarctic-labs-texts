Upregulation of lncRNA CASC2 alleviates neuroinflammation in acute ischemic stroke and exhibits protective effects by targeting miR-155 (CASC2 role in AIS)
Authors: Yuhao Zhang, Zhiyu Wang, Qingting Hu, Chenting Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study investigated the effects of dysregulated lncRNA CASC2 (CASC2) and miR-155 on clinical prognosis and neuronal cell activity in patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS), as well as identified potential biomarkers that mediate inflammation levels and prognostic outcomes in AIS.MethodsAIS cell models were established by inducing HT22 and BV-2 cells with OGD/R treatment. RT-qPCR quantified CASC2 and miR-155 expression. The levels of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8 and TNF-α were detected by ELISA. Pearson correlation analysis was applied to assess the relationships between CASC2, miR-155 and NIHSS scores. ROC curve and binary Logistic analysis reflected the relevant indicators mediating the outcome of AIS patients. The targeting relationship between CASC2 and miR-155 was confirmed by DLR assay. The biological function of the cells was determined by CCK8 and FCM.ResultsCASC2 expression was significantly decreased in the serum of AIS patients, while miR-155 showed a positive trend, and this result was also reflected in the AIS patients with poor prognosis. CASC2 and miR-155 have reference value in predicting the prognosis of AIS patients. Functionally, CASC2 overexpression attenuated neuroinflammation and improved neuronal cell and microglia viability, whereas co-overexpression of miR-155 reversed these protective effects.ConclusionsCASC2 exerts a protective effect in AIS by sponging miR-155, thereby attenuating neuroinflammation and ameliorating clinical outcomes. CASC2 and miR-155 may serve as valuable prognostic biomarkers in AIS patients.Keywords: Acute ischemic stroke, CASC2, miR-155, Inflammation, Prognosis

Introduction

Stroke is defined as an acute neurological deficit due to interruption of blood supply to the brain and is the leading cause of disability and cognitive deficits, with AIS being caused by vascular obstruction [1, 2]. Epidemiological data indicate that AIS comprises over 60% of all stroke cases [3]. Atherosclerosis of the large arteries, cardiogenic embolism and occlusion of small arteries are all etiologic triggers of AIS, in addition to irrational dietary habits and uncontrolled underlying diseases which may also be related to the occurrence of AIS [4]. The process of AIS not only leads to neuronal cell damage, but also triggers inflammatory response [5]. This inflammatory response is mediated primarily by resident immune cells in the brain (microglia and astrocytes) and infiltrating peripheral leukocytes [6]. Activated immune cells release large amounts of inflammatory factors, which exacerbate secondary brain injury and eventually lead to different degrees of adverse outcomes. Undeniably, the continuous increase in the incidence of AIS also imposes a significant burden on the social healthcare system, resulting in increased demand for medical resources and increased financial burden for patients. Therefore, the search for a biomarker to accurately evaluate the prognostic outcome of AIS patients has theoretical significance in the formulation of treatment plans and clinical prognosis analysis.

LncRNAs are RNA transcripts exceeding 200 nucleotides that lack protein-coding capacity [7]. The research results in recent years concluded that lncRNAs have non-negligible efficacy in gene expression modulation, cellular differentiation, and disease pathogenesis [8]. LncRNA cancer susceptibility candidate 2 (CASC2) demonstrates tumor-suppressive properties, with reduced expression observed across multiple malignancies (including pancreatic, gastric, and colorectal cancers) correlating with poor clinical outcomes [9–11]. Interestingly, elevated CASC2 expression reveals cardioprotective effects in acute myocardial infarction [12]. Crucially, CASC2 emerges as a promising therapeutic target for AIS [13]. Furthermore, lncRNAs modulate target genes through multiple mechanisms, including miRNA-mediated regulation. miR-155 is a microRNA encoded by the MIR155 host gene (MIR155HG), located on chromosome 21q21.3 [14]. Researchers claim that miR-155 modulates neuroinflammation and damages the nervous system by activating the release of pro-inflammatory factors [15]. Additionally, miR-155 was upregulated in IS and considered a promising diagnostic factor [16]. However, whether CASC2 mediates inflammation levels and prognostic outcomes in AIS by regulating miR-155 has not been clearly reported.

These findings led us to hypothesize that AIS patients exhibit diminished CASC2 expression and elevated miR-155 level. Silencing CASC2 affects cell activity by targeting miR-155, thereby exacerbating inflammatory injury. CASC2/miR-155 regulatory axis may influence AIS progression, offering a reference for the development of therapeutic biomarker and prognostic indicator.

Materials and methods

Experimental subjects

We screened 100 patients with AIS admitted to The Third Affiliated Hospital of Southern Medical University from January through October 2024, and who met the following requirements: (1) met the diagnostic criteria of the guidelines for the early treatment of patients with acute ischemic stroke in 2018 [17], and were confirmed by computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) examination and expert confirmation; (2) the interval between onset and admission was within 48 h; (3) the patients were adults and had their first episode. We also excluded the following IS patients: (1) had severe cognitive impairment or combined vital organ dysfunction; (2) patients with cerebral hemorrhage and tumors; (3) patients with poor adherence.

AIS patients were followed up for 3 months through regular outpatient review and telephone contact after receiving standardized treatment, with the cutoff date or death as endpoints. Patients subsequently stratified into groups with good (n = 68) and poor (n = 32) prognosis based on clinical outcomes.

A contemporaneous cohort of 100 healthy volunteers served as controls. All participants provided voluntary informed consent, with study approval granted by the institutional ethics committee.

Serum collection and index detection

Fasting elbow venous blood was collected from the patients on admission, centrifuged at 2000 r/min for 10 min, and the supernatant (serum) was collected and stored in the refrigerator (Haier, Qingdao, China) at -80℃ for later use.

The NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS) quantified neurological impairment severity in AIS patients. The indicators in the patient's blood were detected by automatic biochemical analyzer (Hitachi, Japan), including lipoprotein a (LPA), total cholesterol (TC), triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein (LDL), high-density lipoprotein (HDL).

Cell culture and AIS model establishment

The mouse hippocampal neuron cell lines HT22 and BV-2 microglia were purchased from ATCC, USA. Cells were PBS-washed after resuscitation, cultured in complete DMEM medium, and maintained at 37 °C for subculturing.

In vitro cell models of stroke were constructed by oxygen glucose deprivation and reperfusion (OGD/R) treatment. The procedure was as follows: HT22 and BV-2 cells were spread in 6-well plates, cultured at 37 °C for 24 h, the complete medium was replaced by FBS-free, glucose-free DMEM, and the cells were maintained in a 1% hypoxic incubator for 6 h. The cells were then transferred to complete DMEM medium, and the AIS cell models were obtained after 12 h of incubation.

Assessment of CASC2 and miR-155 expression

Precooled Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) was added and total RNA was extracted from the samples according to the steps of the RNA extraction kit (TaKaRa, Japan). The reaction was performed according to Beijing TaKaRa reverse transcription kit to obtain cDNA. The qPCR reaction system was prepared using SYBR Green Master kit (Applied Biosystems, USA) for RT-qPCR analysis on 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystem, CA, USA). Using GAPDH (for CASC2) and U6 (for miR-155) as internal parameters, the data were processed based on 2−△△CT after the reaction, and three biological repeats were performed for each group.

Cell transfection

Overexpression of CASC2 vector (ov-CASC2), miR-155 mimic/inhibitor, and negative control (ov-NC, mimic/inhibitor NC) were synthesized from RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). HT22 and BV-2 cells were inoculated in 6-well plates, and lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen; Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was dropped into the medium after 24 h. The cells were mixed with ov-NC, ov-CASC2, mimic NC and miR-155 mimic plasmids, respectively, and incubated for 48 h in an incubator at 37 °C.

CCK8 assay

Cells were seeded at 2 × 103/well in 96-well plates and cultured at 37 °C for 24 h. The OD value at 450 nm was determined by a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) after the addition of CCK8 reagent to each well and incubation for 3 h.

Flow cytometry (FCM)

After collecting the cells from each group and washing them with PBS, the cells were centrifuged at 1000 r/min for 5 min in a centrifuge. After the supernatant was discarded, AnnexinV-FITC was added for staining, and the PI reagent was added after mixing well. Apoptosis was detected by an Attune NxT flow cytometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) after incubation in the dark for 30 min.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The concentrations of inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α, in the serum of AIS patients and the supernatants of cultured cells were quantified using ELISA kits (R&D systems, Minnesota, USA), following the manufacturer’s protocol.

Dual-luciferase reporter (DLR) assay

The sequences of CASC2 and miR-155 binding sites were made available through the online database ENCORI. CASC2 and miR-155 binding sites were mutated and WT-CASC2 and MUT-CASC2 were synthesized. HT22 cells in good growth condition were laid out in 12-well plates and mixed with the addition of lipofectamine 2000 reagent. Then WT-CASC2, MUT-CASC2, miR-155 mimic/inhibitor were co-transfected into HT22 cells for 48 h and analyzed by dual-luciferase activity kit (Promega, USA).

Statistical analysis

Data processing and mapping were performed by GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA) and SPSS 26.0 software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Data are presented as n/% or mean ± standard deviation. The relevant factors affecting the prognosis of AIS patients were evaluated by ROC curve and binary Logistic regression model. Pearson correlation analysis was taken between CASC2, miR-155 and NIHSS. Differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Results

Analysis of subgroups and clinical indicators

In addition, LPA, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α levels were higher in AIS patients than in healthy individuals (P < 0.001; Table 1).

Serum levels and predictive potential of CASC2 and miR-155

Evaluation of CASC2 and miR-155 levels in serum samples by RT-qPCR revealed that CASC2 expression was repressed while miR-155 expression was enhanced in AIS patients (Fig. 1A, B). Meanwhile, the CASC2 level in the unfavorable group was lower than that in the favorable prognostic group, while miR-155 was enriched in the poor prognosis group (Fig. 1C, D).

B Serum miR-155 was significantly expressed in AIS patients compared with healthy controls. ***P < 0.001 versus Healthy control C, D Serum CASC2 levels were downregulated and serum miR-155 was upregulated in AIS patients with poor prognosis, compared to patients with good prognosis. ***P < 0.001 versus Good prognosis

Correlation analysis stated a negative correlation between both CASC2 and miR-155 (r = -0.625, P < 0.001; Fig. 2A), NIHSS score (r = −0.576, P < 0.001; Fig. 2B), while miR-155 was positively correlated with NIHSS score (r = 0.597, P < 0.001; Fig. 2C). This implies that silencing CASC2 and activating miR-155 were implicated in the neurological deficits in AIS.

The predictive ability of CASC2 and miR-155 levels on the prognostic outcomes of AIS patients was reflected by the ROC curves, which showed that CASC2 predicted AIS prognosis with the AUC (95% CI) of 0.906 (0.844–0.969), and the AUC (95% CI) of miR-155 predicting the prognosis of AIS was 0.806 (0.716–0.894). The combination of the two was superior to the prediction of CASC2 and miR-155 alone (AUC = 0.950, all P < 0.001; Fig. 2D–F). Binary Logistic regression analysis elucidated CASC2 (P < 0.001), miR-155 (P = 0.017), NIHSS score (P = 0.028), LPA (P = 0.048), and IL-1β (P = 0.039) as risk factors for poor prognosis in AIS (Table 3).

Binding relationship of CASC2 and miR-155

Bioinformatics analysis revealed potential binding sites for miR-155 within the CASC2 sequence. The sequence alignment demonstrated a high degree of complementarity, suggesting a direct targeting relationship of CASC2 and miR-155 in Fig. 3A, and this relationship was further illustrated by DLR assays. Transfection with miR-155 mimic suppressed WT-CASC2 luciferase activity, whereas miR-155 inhibitor enhanced it, MUT-CASC2 remained unaffected (Fig. 3B).

CASC2 sponges downstream of miR-155. A WT-CASC2 and miR-155 have binding sites. B The targeting relationship between CASC2 and miR-155 was demonstrated by DLR assay. C–F Expression of CASC2 and miR-155 in AIS cell models constructed from HT22 and BV-2 cells after transfection with overexpressed CASC2 and miR-155. **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001 versus Control; *P < 0.05, ***P < 0.001 versus OGD/R; **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001 versus OGD/R+ov-CASC2

AIS cell models were obtained by induction of HT22 cells/BV-2 cells and named as OGD/R group in in vitro cell experiments, and untreated cells were the control group. We noted that CASC2 content was downregulated while miR-155 was upregulated in the OGD/R group of HT22 cells (Fig. 3C, D), which was consistent with the results detected in BV-2 cells (Fig. 3E, F). Moreover, CASC2 overexpression obviously upregulated CASC2 expression while downregulating miR-155 in AIS cell models. This effect was reversed by co-transfection with exogenous miR-155, restoring endogenous miR-155 levels (Fig. 3C–F).

CASC2/miR-155 axis alleviated neurological damage in AIS

Neuronal cell viability was suppressed and apoptosis was increased in the OGD/R group. OGD/R+ov-CASC2 upregulated the biological functions of the cells, while co-transfection with miR-155 mimic counteracted this protective ability (Fig. 4A, B). Meanwhile, cellular inflammatory factors were prominently expressed in the OGD/R-treated group compared to the control group, whereas IL-1β (Fig. 4C), IL-6 (Fig. 4D), IL-8 (Fig. 4E), and TNF-α (Fig. 4F) levels were downregulated after transfection with ov-CASC2. Mediation of OGD/R+ov-CASC2+miR-155 mimic reversed the inflammatory response in neuronal cells.

Additionally, in the AIS cell model constructed using BV-2 cells, OGD/R treatment inhibited cell proliferation and increased apoptosis rates, while promoting inflammatory responses in microglia. Obviously, upregulation of CASC2 restored the biological function of BV-2 cells and suppressed the content of inflammatory factors, and this protective effect was offset by miR-155 mimic (Fig. 5A–F). The above implies that CASC2 affects neuroinflammation in AIS through sponge miR-155.

Discussion

Stroke represents a devastating neurological disorder characterized by significant mortality and morbidity, which poses a great challenge to global public health security [18]. Stroke is pathologically classified into ischemic (vascular occlusion) and hemorrhagic (vascular rupture) subtypes, with revascularization constituting the cornerstone of AIS management [19]. However, advanced age elevates the risk of bleeding and the possibility of complications in AIS patients treated with intravenous thrombolytic therapy [20]. Therefore, a more accurate assessment of the prognostic outcome of patients with AIS is informative for the determination of individualized treatment plans.

LncRNAs serve as critical regulators of diverse physiological processes, and researchers regard them as represent promising targets for therapeutic development across multiple disease states [21]. In an intervention for AIS patients, Xie et al. identified SNHG16 as a biomarker correlating with disease severity [22]. Liu et al. revealed that upregulated NORAD discriminates patients with AIS and predicts clinical outcomes [23]. CASC2 is located on human chromosome 10q26, and knockdown of CASC2 repress the biological activity of pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells and affect vascular remodelling [24, 25]. Additionally, CASC2/miR-9-5p/PPARγ regulatory network mediates the podocyte damage caused in diabetic nephropathy [26]. In this study, we found that serum CASC2 was negatively expressed in AIS and negatively correlated with neurologic impairment in patients. Also, downregulated CASC2 was reflected in AIS patients exhibiting poor prognosis, indicating its potential role in disease progression. Serum miR-155 was detected to be outstanding in AIS and poor prognosis patients in this study, suggesting severe neurological impairment. The latest study states that miR-155 regulated Wnt/β-catenin signaling to curb neurological damage in AIS [27]. Another report indicated that overexpression of miR-155 characterized enhanced infarct volume in patients with AIS and allowed assessment of prognostic outcomes [28]. We identified miR-155 as a downstream target of CASC2 via bioinformatics website and DLR assay. Moreover, CASC2 and miR-155 were highlighted as risk factors and important indicators of adverse outcomes in AIS patients.

In our AIS cell models established through oxygen glucose deprivation and reperfusion, CASC2 and miR-155 showed a decreasing and increasing trend, respectively. Meanwhile, the proliferative vigor of cells was inhibited, and the apoptotic level was elevated. However, high levels of CASC2 repair the biological function of neuronal cells and microglia, while transfection with miR-155 mimic counteracted the protective ability of CASC2, a phenomenon that was similarly represented in the study by Ye et al. [29]. Inflammatory factors IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α concentrations were excessively elevated in OGD/R-treated cells, demonstrating that the cells produced an excessive inflammatory infiltrate, resulting in heavy inflammatory damage and release of large amounts of pro-inflammatory factors [30]. Expression of prominent CASC2 obviously reduced proinflammatory cytokine content, and this inhibitory effect was reversed by co-transfection of miR-155 mimic. The above results reflect that the inflammatory response of AIS cell model can be improved, and the post-injury repair of neuronal cells and microglia can be promoted by overexpressing CASC2 targeting miR-155, thereby alleviating the symptoms of AIS patients.

We verified the effects of CASC2 and miR-155 on the progression and prognosis of AIS through the above experiments and argued the targeting relationship between them, but no further confirmation was performed on the downstream targets of miR-155 and their functions in AIS. Furthermore, the mediation of CASC2 sponge miR-155 on other biological processes such as oxidative stress in neuronal cells and microglia has not been illustrated. In the next step, we will characterize the in-depth connection between CASC2/miR-155/mRNA regulatory network with AIS cell models.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we confirmed that CASC2 is poorly expressed and miR-155 is prominently expressed in AIS. Up-regulation of CASC2 sponge miR-155 promotes neuronal cell and microglia proliferation, alleviates apoptotic response, reduces the expression of neuroinflammatory factors, and attenuates the condition of AIS. CASC2/miR-155 axis may be a predictor of patient prognosis and provide a new strategy for interventional therapy in AIS.

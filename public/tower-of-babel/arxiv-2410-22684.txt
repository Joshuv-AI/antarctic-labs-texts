Imaginary part of timelike entanglement entropy
Wu-zhong Guo, Jin Xu

Abstract
In this paper, we explore the imaginary part of the timelike entanglement entropy. In the context of field theory, it is more appropriate to obtain the timelike entanglement entropy through the Wick rotation of the twist operators. It is found that, in certain special cases, the imaginary part of the timelike entanglement entropy is related to the commutator of the twist operator and its first-order temporal derivative. To evaluate these commutators, we employ the operator product expansion of the twist operators, revealing that the commutator is generally universal across most scenarios. However, in more general cases, the imaginary part of the timelike entanglement entropy proves to be more complex. We compute the commutator of the twist operators along with its higher-order temporal derivatives. Utilizing these results, we derive a modified formula for the imaginary part of the timelike entanglement entropy. Furthermore, we extend this formula to the case of strip subregion in higher dimensions. Our analysis shows that for the strip geometry, the imaginary part of the timelike entanglement entropy is solely related to the commutators of the twist operator and its first-order temporal derivative. The findings presented in this paper provide valuable insights into the imaginary part of timelike entanglement entropy and its physical significance.

Timelike entanglement entropy via analytical continuation

For simplicity and to aid intuitive understanding, we will specifically demonstrate the results for the EE of a single interval in the vacuum state. In the case of the vacuum state

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\langle\sigma_{n}(\tau,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(\tau^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle

With the Wick rotation we have

\displaystyle S_{n}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{2h_{n}}{n-1}\log{[(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{2}+(x-x^{\prime})^{2}]}|_{\tau\to it+\epsilon,\tau^{\prime}\to it^{\prime}+\epsilon^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2h_{n}}{n-1}\log{[\Delta s^{2}+2i(t-t^{\prime})(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime})]},

(5)

3
Commutators of twist operators

We will further investigate the first-order temporal derivative terms of EE. By the definition of Rényi entropy, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S_{n}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}.

(15)

Therefore, the relationship between the twist operators and the first-order temporal derivative of EE is

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}.

(16)

Based on the formula (
10
) discussed in the previous section, the imaginary part of timelike EE entropy is

\displaystyle 2\text{Im}S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}\text{d}y\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},y),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},-u^{\prime})]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},y)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},-u^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}\text{d}y\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},y),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},v^{\prime})]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},y)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},v^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u^{\prime}}^{v^{\prime}}\text{d}y\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},-u),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},y)]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},-u)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},y)|\Psi\rangle}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u^{\prime}}^{v^{\prime}}\text{d}y\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},v),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},y)]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},v)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},y)|\Psi\rangle}.

19
). This is required by microcausality QFTs. However, it is possible that the commutator may yield a delta function when
(t,x)\to(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
.

One could also consider the commutator for timelike separation. Here, we focus only on the result for
n\sim 1
. By performing a Taylor expansion of (
19
) in terms of
(n-1)
, the result is

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle=1-2h_{n}\log{[-(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)(v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon)]}+O((n-1))^{2}).

(21)

For spacelike separation, (
21
) becomes

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle=1-2h_{n}\log{\Delta s^{2}}+O((n-1)^{2}),

(22)

where
\Delta s^{2}=-(t-t^{\prime})^{2}+(x-x^{\prime})^{2}
. For spacelike separation, as
\log\Delta s^{2}
is real, the
k
-th order term of
(n-1)
is propotional to
(n-1)^{k}(\log\Delta s^{2})^{k}
, which is also real. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\langle[\sigma_{n}(t,x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]\rangle=0.

(23)

This holds true for expanding
(n-1)
to arbitrary order as long as it is for spacelike separation. The commutator is vanishing consistent with microcausality.

If the separation is timelike and
t>t^{\prime}
, (
21
) becomes

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle=1-2h_{n}(\log(-\Delta s^{2})+i\pi)+O((n-1)^{2}).

(24)

The commutator in this case is

\displaystyle\langle[\sigma_{n}(t,x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]\rangle=-4h_{n}\pi i+O((n-1)^{2}).

(25)

In this paper, we mainly focus on the case where the commutator is placed on a Cauchy surface at
t=t_{0}
, leading to
\langle[\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]\rangle=0
. One could evaluate the commutator of twist operators for other states by using similar method.

3.2
Commutators of twist operator and its first-order derivative

In this section, we will first study the commutators of the twist operator and its first-order temporal and spatial derivatives for three examples, for which the expressions of two-point correlators are known. Then, we will use the operator product expansion (OPE) of the twist operator to further discuss the commutators for general states.

3.2.1
Vacuum state

Let’s first consider the vacuum state as an example. By differentiating equation (
19
) with respect to time, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\langle\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle=\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle[\frac{-2h_{n}}{u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon}+\frac{-2\bar{h}_{n}}{v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon}].

(26)

Eq. (
26
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle}=-2h_{n}[i\pi\delta(u-u^{\prime})+P(\frac{1}{u-u^{\prime}})+i\pi\delta(v-v^{\prime})+P(\frac{1}{v-v^{\prime}})],

(27)

by using the Sokhotski-Plemelj theorem

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x\mp i\epsilon}=\pm i\pi\delta(x)+P(\frac{1}{x}),

(28)

where P denotes the Cauchy principal value.
To calculate the commutator, we need
\langle\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\rangle
, which is given by
\langle\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\rangle=\langle\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle^{*}
. In Section.
3.1
we derive that
\langle[\sigma_{n}(t,x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]\rangle=0
, thus
\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle^{*}=\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle
. Finally, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\rangle}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle}

\displaystyle=-2h_{n}[-i\pi\delta(u-u^{\prime})+P(\frac{1}{u-u^{\prime}})-i\pi\delta(v-v^{\prime})+P(\frac{1}{v-v^{\prime}})].

(29)

Combining (
27
) and (
29
), the commutator is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\langle[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]\rangle}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle}=-4h_{n}[i\pi\delta(u-u^{\prime})+i\pi\delta(v-v^{\prime})].

(30)

At the Cauchy surface
t=t_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\langle[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]\rangle}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})\rangle}

\displaystyle=-8h_{n}\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime}).

(31)

For
n\sim 1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\langle[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]\rangle}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{2c}{3}(1-n)\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime})+O((n-1)^{2}),

(32)

which is exactly what we expected (
18
).

On the other hand, we can also examine the commutator that involves the derivative of the twist operator with respect to the spatial coordinate
x
. The result is

\displaystyle\frac{\langle[\partial_{x}\sigma_{n}(t,x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]\rangle}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle}=-4h_{n}[-i\pi\delta(u-u^{\prime})+i\pi\delta(v-v^{\prime})].

(33)

Thus, at a certain Cauchy surface
t=t_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\langle[\partial_{x}\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]\rangle}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})\rangle}=-4h_{n}\pi i[-\delta(x^{\prime}-x)+\delta(x-x^{\prime})]=0.

(34)

This implies that taking the derivative with respect to the spatial coordinate
x
does not introduce a new imaginary part.

3.2.2
Thermal state

Considering the thermal state with temperature
\frac{1}{\beta}
, the two-point correlator of twist operators is

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle_{\beta}=[\frac{\beta}{\pi}\sinh{(\frac{-\pi(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)}{\beta}})]^{-2h_{n}}[\frac{\beta}{\pi}\sinh{(\frac{\pi(v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon)}{\beta}})]^{-2\bar{h}_{n}}.

(35)

Taking the derivative with respect to time, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\langle[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]\rangle_{\beta}}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle_{\beta}}=\frac{-2h_{n}\cosh{(\frac{\pi(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)}{\beta}})}{\frac{\beta}{\pi}\sinh{(\frac{\pi(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)}{\beta}})}+\frac{-2\bar{h}_{n}\cosh{(\frac{\pi(v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon)}{\beta}})}{\frac{\beta}{\pi}\sinh{(\frac{\pi(v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon)}{\beta}})}

(36)

The commutator in the thermal state is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\langle[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})]\rangle_{\beta}}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle_{\beta}}=-4h_{n}[i\pi\delta(u-u^{\prime})+i\pi\delta(v-v^{\prime})].

(37)

One could refer to Appendix.(
C
) for the calculation details.

Then, at Cauchy surface
t=t_{0}
, the result is

\displaystyle\frac{\langle[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]\rangle_{\beta}}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})\rangle_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=-8h_{n}\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime}).

(38)

When
n\sim 1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\langle[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]\rangle_{\beta}}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})\rangle_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2c}{3}(1-n)\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime})+O((n-1)^{2}).

(39)

The result is also consistent with (
18
).

While the commutator involving of the derivative of twist operator with respect to the spatial coordinate
x
is

\displaystyle\frac{\langle[\partial_{x}{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]\rangle_{\beta}}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})\rangle_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=0.

(40)

Once again, the derivation with respect to the spatial coordinate x does not introduce any new imaginary parts.

3.2.3
States dual to pure AdS
3

Let us further consider the state with holographic dual to the metric satisfying the vacuum Einstein equation in AdS
3
. By a Conformal mapping
\omega=f(u)
,
\bar{\omega}=g(v)
, the geometry can be mapped to the Poincare patch. By using Ryu-Takayanagi formula, the holographic EE is given by

\displaystyle S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\frac{c}{6}\log[-\frac{f(u-i\epsilon)-f(u^{\prime})}{\sqrt{f^{\prime}(u-i\epsilon)f^{\prime}(u^{\prime})}}][\frac{g(v-i\epsilon)-g(v^{\prime})}{\sqrt{g^{\prime}(v-i\epsilon)g^{\prime}(v^{\prime})}}].

(41)

By definition, we have

\displaystyle S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\log{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}

Taking the derivative with respect to time, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{6}[\frac{f^{\prime}(u)}{f(u-i\epsilon)-f(u^{\prime})}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u)}{2f^{\prime}(u)}]+\frac{c}{6}[\frac{g^{\prime}(v)}{g(v-i\epsilon)-g(v^{\prime})}-\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(v)}{2g^{\prime}(v)}].

(43)

Consider the term involving
u
in the commutator. For
u\neq u^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{c}{6}[\frac{f^{\prime}(u)}{f(u-i\epsilon)-f(u^{\prime})}-\frac{f^{\prime}(u)}{f(u+i\epsilon)-f(u^{\prime})}]

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{\prime}(u)}{f(u)-f(u^{\prime})}-\frac{f^{\prime}(u)}{f(u)-f(u^{\prime})}=0

(44)

If
u\to u^{\prime}
, the result is divergent. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\frac{c}{6}[\frac{f^{\prime}(u)}{f(u-i\epsilon)-f(u^{\prime})}-\frac{f^{\prime}(u)}{f(u+i\epsilon)-f(u^{\prime})}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{6}[\frac{f^{\prime}(u^{\prime})}{f(u^{\prime})+f^{\prime}(u^{\prime})(u-i\epsilon-u^{\prime})-f(u^{\prime})}-\frac{f^{\prime}(u^{\prime})}{f(u^{\prime})+f^{\prime}(u^{\prime})(u+i\epsilon-u^{\prime})-f(u^{\prime})}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{6}[\frac{1}{u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon}-\frac{1}{u-u^{\prime}+i\epsilon}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{3}\pi i\delta(u-u^{\prime})

(45)

At the Cauchy surface
t=t_{0}
, the result is

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{2c}{3}\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime}).

(46)

This result is consistent with our previous expectation (
18
).

Similarly, we can verify that
\frac{\langle\Psi|[\partial_{x}{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}=0
also holds true in this context.

4
Results for general states

In the previous sections, we use some examples to show the commutators of twist operators and its temporal and spatial derivative. In this section we would like to discuss the expectation value of the commutators for general states. Some universal results are obtained.

Generally, once knowing operator product expansion (OPE) of two operators, it is possible to evaluate the commutators of the two operators, see, e.g.,
[
36
]
. For the twist operators
\sigma_{n}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{n}
the OPE
[
37
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})=\sum_{k}C_{k}(w_{1}-w_{2})^{-2h_{n}+h_{k}}(\bar{w}_{1}-\bar{w}_{2})^{-2\bar{h}_{n}+\bar{h}_{k}}\mathcal{X}_{k},

(47)

where
C_{k}
is the OPE coefficent,
\mathcal{X}_{k}
are the operators with conformal dimension
h_{k}\geq 0
and
\bar{h}_{k}\geq 0
. With Wick rotation, we have

\displaystyle\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle=\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle\sum_{k}a_{k}[-(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)]^{h_{k}}[v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon]^{\bar{h}_{k}},

(48)

where
a_{k}
depends on OPE coefficients and the expectation values of the operators in the state
|\psi\rangle
and
\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle
denotes the contribution for the vacuum state. When
the length of the interval is short enough, we can consider that
a_{k}
are constants which are independent of the coordinates. Throughout the remainder of this section, we adopt the short interval assumption.

4.1
Universal result for commutator involving first-order temporal derivative

In this section we would like to calculate
\frac{\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}
for most general states. Taking the logarithm of Eq.(
48
), we have

\displaystyle\log\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle+\log\sum_{k}a_{k}[-(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)]^{h_{k}}[v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon]^{\bar{h}_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle+\log a_{0}+\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{a_{k}}{a_{0}}[-(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)]^{h_{k}}[v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon]^{\bar{h}_{k}},

(49)

where
h_{0}=0
accounts for the contribution of the identity operator, and in the following we set
a_{0}=1
. In the second step, we expand the logarithm under the assumption that the series is convergent.

Taking the derivative of the above result with respect to
t
gives

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\Psi|\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t,x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t,x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}=\partial_{t}\log\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t,x^{\prime})\rangle}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t,x^{\prime})\rangle}+\sum_{k\neq 0}a_{k}[-(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)]^{h_{k}}[v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon]^{\bar{h}_{k}}[\frac{h_{k}}{u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon}+\frac{\bar{h}_{k}}{v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon}],

(50)

Taking the result at a certain Cauchy surface
t=t_{0}
and taking the commutator, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\langle[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]\rangle}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})\rangle}+\sum_{k\neq 0}a_{k}h_{k}(x-x^{\prime})^{2h_{k}}4\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-8h_{n}\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime})

(51)

Using the properties of the Dirac delta function
f(x)\delta(x)=f(0)\delta(x)
, we can derive in the second step that for
h_{k}>0
, the terms
(x-x^{\prime})^{2h_{k}}\delta(x-x^{\prime})
are vanishing. The above result is also consisent with Eq.(
18
).

Eq.(
51
) demonstrates that the expectation value of the commutator involving the twist operator and its first-order temporal derivative is universal, which depends only on the central charge
c
and is independent of the states. This result is consistent with the examples discussed in the previous section. By using Eq.(
17
), we can observe that the imaginary components of the timelike EE for the vacuum state, thermal state, and states with holographic duals are directly linked to these commutators. Our findings explain why the imaginary term is the universal value
\frac{i\pi c}{6}
. In fact, we can anticipate that for any state, the imaginary term of the timelike EE will include a contribution of
\frac{i\pi c}{6}
.

4.2
The derivative of the commutator with respect to the spatial coordinate
x

On the other hand, we can consider the commutator with respect to the derivative of the spatial coordinate
x

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\Psi|\partial_{x}\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t,x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t,x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}=\partial_{x}\log\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\partial_{x}\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t,x^{\prime})\rangle}{\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t,x^{\prime})\rangle}+\sum_{k\neq 0}a_{k}[-(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)]^{h_{k}}[v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon]^{\bar{h}_{k}}[\frac{-h_{k}}{u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon}+\frac{\bar{h}_{k}}{v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon}],

(52)

At a certain Cauchy surface
t=t_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\Psi|[\partial_{x}\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+\sum_{k\neq 0}a_{k}[x-x^{\prime}+i\epsilon]^{h_{k}}[x-x^{\prime}-i\epsilon]^{h_{k}}[\frac{h_{k}}{x-x^{\prime}+i\epsilon}+\frac{h_{k}}{x-x^{\prime}-i\epsilon}]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 0-\sum_{k\neq 0}a_{k}[x-x^{\prime}-i\epsilon]^{h_{k}}[x-x^{\prime}+i\epsilon]^{h_{k}}[\frac{h_{k}}{x-x^{\prime}-i\epsilon}+\frac{h_{k}}{x-x^{\prime}+i\epsilon}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

(53)

Thus for general states in 2D CFT, the first derivative of its commutator with respect to the spatial coordinate
x
is 0. This implies that the derivative of the EE with respect to spatial coordinate
\partial_{x}S(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime})
is a real number, and it does not contribute to a new imaginary part.

4.3
Results for commutators involving higher order derivative

We have derived a universal result regarding the first-order derivative of the commutator for general states. However, this indicates that the first-order derivative can only yield information analogous to that of the vacuum state. For more general states, the imaginary parts of the timelike EE should also depend on the specific details of those states, as we will demonstrate below. Additionally, we will show that it is essential to consider commutators that involve higher-order temporal derivatives of the twist operators. Furthermore, we will demonstrate that the imaginary part of the timelike EE can be related to the
m
-th derivatives of spacelike EE or the commutators of twist operators. Specifically, we will construct a formula analogous to equation (
17
).

Based on the results from the previous section, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime})=\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{-4h_{n}\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime})}{1-n}=\frac{c}{3}\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime})

(54)

For higher-order derivatives, we note that the even-order derivatives are always real. For instance, considering the second derivative, we have

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{t}S(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\{-2h_{n}[\frac{-1}{(x-x^{\prime}-i\epsilon)^{2}}+\frac{-1}{(x-x^{\prime}+i\epsilon)^{2}}]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{k\neq 0}a_{k}[C^{0}_{2}h_{k}(h_{k}-1)+C^{1}_{2}h_{k}^{2}+C^{2}_{2}h_{k}(h_{k}-1)](x-x^{\prime})^{2h_{k}-2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{c}{3}(x-x^{\prime})^{-2}+\sum_{k\neq 0}\tilde{a}_{k}2h_{k}(2h_{k}-1)(x-x^{\prime})^{2h_{k}-2},

(55)

where
\tilde{a}_{k}:=\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}a_{k}
,
C^{k}_{m}:=\frac{m!}{k!(m-k)!}
.
This term does not provide any additional information regarding the new imaginary part of the timelike separation. More generally, for higher-order even derivatives, all yield real numbers, as indicated by the aforementioned Eq.(
55
). Therefore, we are particularly interested in the odd-order derivatives.

For the third-order derivative, we have

\displaystyle\partial^{3}_{t}S(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\{-4h_{n}[\frac{1}{(x-x^{\prime}-i\epsilon)^{3}}-\frac{1}{(x-x^{\prime}+i\epsilon)^{3}}]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{k\neq 0}a_{k}[C^{0}_{3}h_{k}(h_{k}-1)(h_{k}-2)+C^{1}_{3}h_{k}^{2}(h_{k}-1)](x-x^{\prime})^{2h_{k}-2}[\frac{1}{x-x^{\prime}-i\epsilon}-\frac{1}{x-x^{\prime}+i\epsilon}]\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{3}\pi i\partial^{2}_{x}\delta(x-x^{\prime})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{0<h_{k}<1}\tilde{a}_{k}[C^{0}_{3}h_{k}(h_{k}-1)(h_{k}-2)+C^{1}_{3}h_{k}^{2}(h_{k}-1)](x-x^{\prime})^{2(h_{k}-1)}2\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime}).

(56)

As we expected, the third derivative of the EE with respect to time
t
at the Cauchy surface
t=t_{0}
will be non-vanishing with
0<h_{k}<1
. In fact, as we can see from Eq.(
56
), this term is divergent. We will demonstrate below that this term is crucial for producing the correct imaginary part of the timelike EE. Notably, since each term in the coefficient contains a factor of
h_{k}-1
, this term is vanishing when
h_{k}=1
.

In general, we have

\displaystyle\partial^{(2m+1)}_{t}S(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime})=\frac{c}{3}\pi i\partial^{2m+1}_{x}\delta(x-x^{\prime})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{0<h_{k}\leq m}\tilde{a}_{k}[C^{0}_{2m+1}h_{k}(h_{k}-1)...(h_{k}-2m)+C^{1}_{2m+1}h_{k}^{2}(h_{k}-1)...(h_{k}-2m+1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C^{2}_{2m+1}h_{k}^{2}(h_{k}-1)^{2}(h_{k}-2)...(h_{k}-2m+2)+...

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C^{m}_{2m+1}h_{k}^{2}(h_{k}-1)^{2}...(h_{k}-m+1)^{2}(h_{k}-m)](x-x^{\prime})^{2(h_{k}-m)}2\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{3}\pi i\partial^{2m+1}_{x}\delta(x-x^{\prime})+\sum_{0<h_{k}\leq m}\tilde{a}_{k}G^{(2m+1)}_{k}(x-x^{\prime})^{2(h_{k}-m)}2\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{3}\pi i\partial^{2m+1}_{x}\delta(x-x^{\prime})+\sum_{\tilde{k}}\tilde{a}_{\tilde{k}}G^{(2m+1)}_{\tilde{k}}(x-x^{\prime})^{2(h_{\tilde{k}}-m)}2\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime}),

(57)

where we denote the term with fractional conformal dimension
h_{k}
within
\{h_{k}\}
as
\{h_{\tilde{k}}\}
, and we define

\displaystyle G^{(2m+1)}_{k}:=C^{0}_{2m+1}h_{k}(h_{k}-1)...(h_{k}-2m)+...+C^{m}_{2m+1}h_{k}^{2}(h_{k}-1)^{2}...(h_{k}-m+1)^{2}(h_{k}-m).

(58)

Let us take
m=[h_{k}]
, where
[x]
denotes the ceiling function of
x
. For integer values of
h_{k}
, since each term in
G^{(2m+1)}_{k}
contains a factor of
(h_{k}-1)...(h_{k}-m)
, it follows that
G^{(2m+1)}_{k}=0
. Therefore, in the final step of the above equation, the terms with integers conformal dimension will be vanishing, leaving us with only the fractional terms
\{h_{\tilde{k}}\}
. These terms are imaginary, which will be important to construct the formula of imaginary part of timelike EE.

Therefore, we know that the
(2m+1)
th derivative of the EE with respect to time
t
will include the information of the operators with conformal dimension
h_{k}
, where
h_{k}
is a fraction with
m=[h_{k}]
. Provided that we choose
m
to be sufficiently large, it will encompass all the contributions of the operators with conformal dimension
h_{k}
.

In fact, operators with integer values of
h_{k}
will not provide any new contributions to the imaginary part of timelike EE. In contrast, operators with fractional
h_{k}
will contribute significantly. In the next section, we will analyze how to modified the relation between timelike and spacelike EE by including all the possible contributions from the operators with conformal dimension
h_{k}
.

4.4
General formula for the imaginary part of timelike EE

We know that, for general states, by using (
49
) the EE is

\displaystyle S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=S_{0}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})+\sum_{k\neq 0}\tilde{a}_{k}[-(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)]^{h_{k}}[v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon]^{\bar{h}_{k}}.

(59)

For spacelike separation
(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=S_{0}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})+\sum_{k\neq 0}\tilde{a}_{k}\Delta s^{2h_{k}},

(60)

where we assume
h_{k}=\bar{h}_{k}
.
And for timelike separation
(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=S_{0}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})+\sum_{k\neq 0}\tilde{a}_{k}(-1)^{h_{k}}T_{0}^{2h_{k}},

(61)

where we define
T_{0}^{2}=-\Delta s^{2}=(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-(x-x^{\prime})^{2}
.
Regarding the timelike separation, we have observed that terms with integer values of
h_{k}
do not contribute additional imaginary parts. it is only the terms with fractional values of
h_{k}
that yield new imaginary components. Let us denote all fractional terms within
\{h_{k}\}
as
\{h_{\tilde{k}}\}
. Subsequently, the imaginary part of timelike EE is expressed as

\displaystyle\text{Im}S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\frac{i\pi c}{6}+\sum_{\tilde{k}}\text{Im}[(-1)^{h_{\tilde{k}}}]\tilde{a}_{\tilde{k}}T_{0}^{2h_{\tilde{k}}},

(62)

By employing the results outlined in Section
4.3
, we aim to establish a relationship analogous to Eq.(
17
) for general states. To accomplish this, we begin with Eq.(
57
) and define the function
S_{\tilde{k}}(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime})
satisfying

\displaystyle\partial^{(2m+1)}_{t}S_{\tilde{k}}(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime}):=\tilde{a}_{\tilde{k}}G^{(2m+1)}_{\tilde{k}}(x-x^{\prime})^{2(h_{\tilde{k}}-m)}2\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime}).

(63)

It is obvious that by definition we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\tilde{k}}\partial^{(2m+1)}_{t}S_{\tilde{k}}(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime})=\partial^{(2m+1)}_{t}S(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime})-\frac{c}{3}\pi i\partial^{2m+1}_{x}\delta(x-x^{\prime}).

(64)

It is noteworthy that, from Eq. (
62
), the additional imaginary parts are exclusively associated with operators that have fractional conformal dimensions
h_{\tilde{k}}
. More specifically, these contributions depend on the parameters
\tilde{a}_{\tilde{k}}
and
h_{\tilde{k}}
. The function
S_{\tilde{k}}(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime})
defined in (
63
) also incorporates these parameters. Therefore, we expect that the relation in Eq. (
17
) can be generalized to include contributions from
S_{\tilde{k}}(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime})
to more general states.

The formula Eq.(
17
) should be modified by including more terms associated with
S_{\tilde{k}}(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime})
. Let us denote the corrections by
Im\Delta S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
. By considering the corrections from operators with fractional conformal dimension, we propose a correction to Eq.(
17
)

\displaystyle 2\text{Im}\Delta S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\tilde{k}}\frac{[\text{Im}e^{i\pi h_{\tilde{k}}}]e^{\frac{-i\pi}{2}}T_{0}^{2h_{\tilde{k}}}}{4\pi G^{(2m+1)}_{\tilde{k}}}\Big[\int_{-u^{\prime}}^{v^{\prime}}\text{d}y(-u-y)^{-2(h_{\tilde{k}}-m)}\partial^{(2m+1)}_{t^{\prime}}S_{\tilde{k}}(t_{0},-u;t_{0},y)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{-u^{\prime}}^{v^{\prime}}\text{d}y(v-y)^{-2(h_{\tilde{k}}-m)}\partial^{(2m+1)}_{t^{\prime}}S_{\tilde{k}}(t_{0},v;t_{0},y)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{-u}^{v}\text{d}y(y+u^{\prime})^{-2(h_{\tilde{k}}-m)}\partial^{(2m+1)}_{t}S_{\tilde{k}}(t_{0},y;t_{0},-u^{\prime})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{-u}^{v}\text{d}y(y-v^{\prime})^{-2(h_{\tilde{k}}-m)}\partial^{(2m+1)}_{t}S_{\tilde{k}}(t_{0},y;t_{0},v^{\prime})-\text{h.c.}\Big]

(65)

where
m=[h_{k}]
is the result of applying the ceiling function to the maximum value in the set
\{h_{k}\}
, and
h.c.
denotes the complex conjugate. Thus the full imaginary part of timelike EE is expressed as follows

\displaystyle\text{Im}S_{f}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\text{Im}S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})+\text{Im}\Delta S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime}),

(66)

where
\text{Im}S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
is given by Eq.(
17
) and contributes the universal term
\frac{i\pi c}{6}
, while
\text{Im}\Delta S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
accounts for the contributions from all operators with fractional conformal dimensions.

As a simple verification of Eq. (
65
), one can demonstrate that it becomes zero for the spacelike separation between the points
(t,x)
and
(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
. As discussed in Section
2
, in the scenario depicted in Fig.
1
(c), the integral results from (
65
) yield a value of zero. While, in the case illustrated in Fig.
1
(d), the contributions cancel each other out. This aligns with our expectation that the imaginary part of the spacelike EE vanishes.

For timelike separation between the points
(t,x)
and
(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
, according to our formula (
65
), one could check that the imaginary part of the EE is
\text{Im}S_{f}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\frac{i\pi c}{6}+\sum_{\tilde{k}}\text{Im}[(-1)^{h_{\tilde{k}}}]\tilde{a}_{\tilde{k}}T_{0}^{2h_{\tilde{k}}}
, which is consistent with the timelike EE (
62
). As we have noted that the universal term
\frac{i\pi c}{6}
come from
\text{Im}S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
(
17
). Specifically, when all elements in the set of
\{h_{k}\}
are integers, the imaginary part is
\text{Im}S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\frac{i\pi c}{6}
, meaning that the integer terms do not contribute to a new imaginary part.

Let us briefly summarize the results of this section as follows. Suppose that the EE can be evaluated using the OPE of twist operators. For general states, the imaginary part of the timelike EE is contributed solely by the identity operator and operators with fractional conformal dimensions
\{h_{\tilde{k}}\}
. The identity operator contributes a universal term of
\frac{i\pi c}{6}
, while the operators with fractional conformal dimension
h_{\tilde{k}}
contribute
\sum_{\tilde{k}}\text{Im}[e^{i\pi h_{\tilde{k}}}]\tilde{a}_{\tilde{k}}T_{0}^{2h_{\tilde{k}}}
.

In the general case, the imaginary part of the timelike EE can also be expressed in terms of the spacelike EE and its derivatives on a specific Cauchy surface at
t=t_{0}
, as shown in Eq.(
65
). We have observed that terms involving even-order derivatives, such as
\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{v}\text{d}y\int_{-u^{\prime}}^{v^{\prime}}\text{d}y^{\prime}\partial_{t}\partial_{t^{\prime}}S(t_{0},y;t_{0},y^{\prime})
, do not contribute a new imaginary part but instead provide real part contributions. The novel imaginary part arises solely from first-order derivatives and higher-order odd derivatives. While the even-order derivatives are important for the real part of the timelike EE, both even-order and odd-order derivatives will be essential for extending Eq. (
2
) to more general states. We plan to explore this question in the near future.

Higher dimensional example

We consider that
\text{AdS}_{d+1}
possesses the metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}=R^{2}\frac{-dt^{2}+dz^{2}+dx^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}dy_{i}^{2}}{z^{2}},

(67)

where R is the radius of
AdS
space. The subsystem
A
is specified by the boosted strip defined by

\displaystyle-\frac{\Delta x}{2}<x<\frac{\Delta x}{2},-\frac{\Delta t}{2}<t<\frac{\Delta t}{2},x=\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}t;

\displaystyle-\frac{L}{2}<y_{i}<\frac{L}{2},i=1,2,...,d-2,

(68)

where
L
the IR cut-off on the coordinate
y_{i}
.

In fact, we can regard
A
as a region bounded by two hypersurfaces with constant coordinates
(t,x)
and
(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
with
x-x^{\prime}=\Delta x
and
t-t^{\prime}=\Delta t
.
One could extend the
A
to timelike region. For instance, in the case where
x=x^{\prime}=0
, it represents a timelike strip on the hypersurface with
x=0
. According to our previous convention, we still define
u=t-x
,
u^{\prime}=t^{\prime}-x^{\prime}
and

,
v^{\prime}=t^{\prime}+x^{\prime}
. For region
A
the holographic EE is given by
[
42
]
[
28
]

\displaystyle S_{A}=S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{d-2}R^{d-1}}{2G_{N}}[\frac{1}{d-2}(\frac{1}{\delta})^{d-2}-k_{d}\frac{1}{\Delta s^{d-2}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{d-2}R^{d-1}}{2G_{N}}[\frac{1}{d-2}(\frac{1}{\delta})^{d-2}-k_{d}\frac{(-i)^{d-2}}{T_{0}^{d-2}}],

(69)

where we defined

\displaystyle k_{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}2^{d-2}}}{d-2}(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2(d-1)})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2(d-1)})})^{d-1}

\displaystyle T_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle:=-\Delta s^{2}=(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)(v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon)=(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-(x-x^{\prime})^{2}.

(70)

There are two distinct scenarios for high-dimensional timelike EE in odd and even dimensions, which are

\displaystyle S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\frac{L^{d-2}R^{d-1}}{2G_{N}}[\frac{1}{d-2}(\frac{1}{\delta})^{d-2}-k_{d}\frac{-i(-1)^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}{T_{0}^{d-2}}]&\text{d is odd},\\

This indicates that the high-dimensional timelike EE for the strip has an imaginary part only when

Based on the discussion in 2-dimensional CFTs, the imaginary part of the timelike EE is related to the commutator of twist operators that involve temporal derivatives. To proceed, let us take the derivative of equation (

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}=\partial_{t}S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\frac{L^{d-2}R^{d-1}}{2G_{N}}\frac{k_{d}(d-2)}{2}[-(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)(v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon)]^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}(\frac{1}{u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon}+\frac{1}{v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon})

On a certain Cauchy surface

\displaystyle(x-x^{\prime})^{(d-2)}\partial_{t}S(t_{0},x;t_{0},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L^{d-2}R^{d-1}}{2G_{N}}k_{d}(d-2)\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime}),

(74)

where

\partial_{t}S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|_{t\to t_{0},t^{\prime}\to t_{0}}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}(x-x^{\prime})^{(d-2)}\frac{\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},x^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}

\displaystyle\frac{L^{d-2}R^{d-1}}{G_{N}}k_{d}(d-2)\pi i\delta(x-x^{\prime}).

(75)

Interestingly, the commutators of twist operators for the strip exhibit properties similar to those of two-dimensional CFTs (
31
). As indicated in (
17

We hypothesize that a similar relationship exists for the strip, analogous to that of two-dimensional CFTs. One can evaluate the imaginary part of the timelike EE for the strip by utilizing (
69
) and the definition

\displaystyle 2\text{Im}S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-S(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})^{*}.

For any separation
(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\frac{[(-1)^{d-2}-1]i^{d-3}}{T_{0}^{d-2}(d-2)\pi}\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u^{\prime}}^{+v^{\prime}}(v-y)^{(d-2)}\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},v),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},y)]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},v)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},y)|\Psi\rangle}\text{d}y

\displaystyle+\frac{[(-1)^{d-2}-1]i^{d-3}}{T_{0}^{d-2}(d-2)\pi}\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{+v}(y-(-u^{\prime}))^{(d-2)}\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},y),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},-u^{\prime})]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},y)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},-u^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}\text{d}y

\displaystyle+\frac{[(-1)^{d-2}-1]i^{d-3}}{T_{0}^{d-2}(d-2)\pi}\frac{1}{4}\int_{-u}^{+v}(y-v^{\prime})^{(d-2)}\lim_{n\to 1}\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{\langle\Psi|[\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},y),\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},v^{\prime})]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|\sigma_{n}(t_{0},y)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t_{0},v^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle}\text{d}y.

One can directly verify the above formula using (

Remarkably, the formula (

6
Commutators and spectra of reduced density matrix

We consider the Rényi entropy for one interval between
(t,x)
and
(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
. The two points can be spacelike or timelike.
For vacuum state of 2-dimensional CFTs the Rényi entropy is given by

\displaystyle S_{n}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\frac{c}{12}(1+\frac{1}{n})\log\frac{-(u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon)(v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon)}{\delta^{2}},

(80)

where
u:=t-x
,
u^{\prime}:=t^{\prime}-x^{\prime}
and
v:=t+x
,
v^{\prime}:=t^{\prime}-x^{\prime}
,
\epsilon
is a positive constant. One can obtain the above result by analytical continuation of the correlator of twist operators. An obvious property is

\displaystyle S_{n}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=S_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime};t,x)^{*}.

(81)

One could reconstruct the spetra of
\rho_{A}
once knowing Rényi entropy for all the
n
.
Let us consider the separation of two points are spacelike. The density matrix
\rho_{A}
is still Hermitian, thus the eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
are still positive numbers
\lambda_{i}\in(0,1)
. Denote the maximal eigenvalue as
\lambda_{m}
, which is given by

. Thus the Rényi entropy (
80
) can be written as
S_{n}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=(1+\frac{1}{n})b
.
From the form of Rényi entropy we expect the eigenvalues of
\rho_{A}
should be
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
and satisfy the similar property
\lambda_{i}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\lambda_{i}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime};t,x)^{*}

\lambda_{m}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime}):=e^{-b(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})}

b
as well as
\lambda_{m}
are real for spacelike separation.

For the spacelike separation the eigenvalues should be real that is
\lambda_{i}(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\lambda_{i}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime};t,x)

Using Rényi entropy one could evaluate the following functions,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\lambda):=\sum_{i}\delta(\lambda-\lambda_{i}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\alpha_{J}}(\lambda):=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\lambda_{i}}{\partial\alpha_{J}}\delta(\lambda-\lambda_{i}),

(82)

where
\alpha_{J}
is arbitrary parameter.

\mathcal{P}
is the density of eigenvalue,
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha_{J}}/\mathcal{P}
can be seen as the average value of the derivative of eigenvalue at

\alpha_{J}
. By definition we would have the following relation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}}{\partial\alpha_{J}}=-\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}_{\alpha_{J}}}{\partial\lambda}.

(83)

In this paper we are interested in the case

. The two functions

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[e^{nb+(1-n)S_{n}}\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{t}(\lambda)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\frac{1-n}{n}e^{nb+(1-n)S_{n}}\frac{\partial S_{n}}{\partial t}\right].

(84)

For the vacuum state the result is

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left[\frac{\sqrt{b}I_{1}\left(2\sqrt{bs}\right)}{\sqrt{s}}+\delta(s)\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{t}(\lambda)=-\frac{\partial b}{\partial t}\left[\frac{(b-s)I_{1}\left(2\sqrt{bs}\right)}{\sqrt{bs}}+\delta(s)\right],

(85)

where

\displaystyle\frac{\partial b}{\partial t}=\frac{c}{12}\left(\frac{1}{v-v^{\prime}-i\epsilon}+\frac{1}{u-u^{\prime}-i\epsilon}\right).

(86)

We should understand
b
as
b(0,x;0,x^{\prime})
, but understand

as
\frac{\partial b(t,x;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})}{\partial t}|_{t=0,t^{\prime}=0}
. This is why
b

\frac{\partial b}{\partial t}
is complex.

For the spacelike separation one could find
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{*}
. However, for the function
\mathcal{P}_{t}
is complex, the imaginary part is

\displaystyle\text{Im}\mathcal{P}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{P}_{t}-\mathcal{P}_{t}^{*}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial b}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial b^{*}}{\partial t}\right)\left[\frac{(b-s)I_{1}\left(2\sqrt{bs}\right)}{\sqrt{bs}}+\delta(s)\right].

(87)

By definition the time derivative of
tr\rho_{A}^{n}
is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}tr\rho_{A}^{n}=n\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\lambda_{i}}{\partial t}\lambda_{i}^{n-1}=n\int_{0}^{\lambda_{m}}d\lambda\lambda^{n-1}\mathcal{P}_{t}(\lambda)

(88)

In QFTs one could evaluate

\displaystyle tr\rho_{A}^{n}=\langle\sigma_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle,

where we taking the analytical continuation to Lorentzian coordinate. Thus we find

\displaystyle\langle\dot{\sigma}_{n}(t,x)\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle=n\int_{0}^{\lambda_{m}}d\lambda\lambda^{n-1}\mathcal{P}_{t}(\lambda),

Conclusion and discussion

In this paper, we further investigate the imaginary part of the timelike entanglement entropy (EE). In the previous work
[
14
]
, it is obtained the timelike EE through the analytical continuation of Euclidean correlation functions of twist operators. Here, we delve deeper into the details of this analytical continuation (
10
) and extend the relationship between timelike and spacelike EE to more general cases. This suggests that timelike and spacelike EE can be regarded as a unified framework.

The main purpose of this paper is to investigate the imaginary part of the timelike entanglement entropy (EE). Utilizing the relation (
2
), it was noted in
[
14
]
that in certain special cases, the imaginary part is related to the commutators involving the twist operators and their first-order temporal derivatives. In this paper, we directly compute the commutators for the vacuum state, thermal state, and states dual to pure AdS
3
. The results are consistent with the previous analysis, which is anticipated since all the aforementioned examples satisfy equation (
10
). It is also noteworthy that the imaginary part in these examples is universal.constant.

To understand the properties of the imaginary part of timelike EE, we further explore the results for more general states using the OPE of twist operators in two-dimensional CFTs. Through calculations, we find that the commutator of the twist operator and its first-order derivative is a
universal
constant that depends solely on the central charge. This clarifies why the examples considered in Section
3
yield a universal imaginary part of the timelike EE when evaluated using equation (
17
).

However, in the most general cases, we expect that the imaginary part of the timelike EE is no longer constant. Instead, it should depend on the expectation values of the operators appearing in the OPE of the twist operator and the coordinates of the intervals. Consequently, the formula for the imaginary part of the timelike EE given in Eq.(
17
) is expected to receive corrections related to the OPE of the twist operators. In fact, we find it necessary to include commutators of the twist operators along with their higher-order temporal derivatives. In Section
4
, we calculate these commutators that involve higher-order temporal derivatives. Utilizing the results from this section, we propose a modified formula for the imaginary part of the timelike EE that incorporates more general corrections from the OPE of the twist operator. We also verify the accuracy of this modified formula. Moreover, it is noteworthy that, in most general cases, the commutator of the twist operator and its first-order spatial derivative vanishes.

Extending the definition of timelike EE to higher dimensions is an intriguing question. There are certain subtleties regarding the definition of timelike EE for general subregions in higher dimensions. However, for the case of a strip, one can establish a well-defined timelike EE. Furthermore, it is possible to calculate the timelike EE using the holographic RT formula. This allows us to explore the imaginary part of the timelike EE for strips in a manner similar to the two-dimensional examples. In this paper, we calculate the commutator of the twist operator and its first-order derivative for the strip, as detailed in equation (
75
). Using this result, we derive a formula for the imaginary part of the timelike EE, presented in equation (
77
). Our findings provide valuable insights into the origins of the imaginary part of the timelike EE in higher dimension.

Lastly, we demonstrate that the commutators of twist operators can be rephrased in terms of the spectra of the density matrix. The Rényi entropy and the entanglement spectra are interconnected through Laplace transforms, indicating that they contain the same information. This understanding can enhance our insights into various properties of timelike EE. If we consider timelike EE to be a special case of pseudo entropy, one might expect the existence of a non-Hermitian transition matrix. In general, its spectra should consist of complex numbers. An intriguing question arises regarding how to reconstruct the transition matrix using the timelike Rényi entropy.

There are many intriguing directions worth exploring in the near future. In this paper, we primarily focus on the field theory perspective. It would be fascinating to investigate how these results translate to the holographic setting. The commutator of the twist operator and its first-order temporal derivative is universal. It would be a compelling direction to investigate whether this universal commutator relation can be used to quantize both field and bulk theories.

In this paper, we focus solely on the imaginary part of the timelike EE. An intriguing question arises regarding the formulation of a complete connection between timelike and spacelike EE, which would serve as a correction to the formula given in Eq.(

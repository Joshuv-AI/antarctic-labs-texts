A Pilot Study of Kocher Forceps-Guided Laparoscopic Transvaginal Sacrospinous Ligament Fixation: Technique and Initial Clinical Experience
Authors: Bai Xue, Xiaoyan Huang, Ying Zhou, Qian Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTraditional transvaginal sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF) relies on palpation for suture placement under limited visualization. We developed a Kocher forceps-guided laparoscopic transvaginal SSLF technique that enables real-time laparoscopic visualization during suturing and eliminates the need for specialized transvaginal natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery (vNOTES) access platforms and insufflation. This study presents this novel technique and reports our initial clinical experience.MethodsTen consecutive patients with pelvic organ prolapse (POP) who underwent this procedure at the Affiliated Jiangning Hospital of Nanjing Medical University between November 2024 and July 2025 were included. After opening the rectovaginal and right pararectal spaces, the sacrospinous ligament was identified by palpating the ischial spine. The surgeon then grasped the target site with a Kocher forceps and placed the suture under direct laparoscopic visualization. Demographic, preoperative, intraoperative, postoperative, and follow-up data were evaluated.ResultsTen postmenopausal women with stage III–IV POP successfully underwent the procedure. Median age was 64 years (range, 54–71), and median body mass index (BMI) was 24.30 kg/m2 (range, 19.53–27.56). Concomitant procedures included hysterectomy with anterior and posterior colporrhaphy (n=5), partial cervicectomy with anterior colporrhaphy (n=1), partial cervicectomy with posterior colporrhaphy (n=1), and anterior and posterior colporrhaphy alone (n=3). Median operative time was 140 minutes (range, 82–230), and median estimated blood loss was 50 mL (range, 10–100). No perioperative complications occurred. At a median follow-up of 15.62 months (range, 2.27–17.03), no POP recurrence or surgical complications were observed.ConclusionThe Kocher forceps-guided laparoscopic transvaginal SSLF appears to be a feasible and safe surgical approach for POP in this small case series.

Introduction

Pelvic organ prolapse (POP), a prevalent form of pelvic floor dysfunction in women, is characterized by the descent of pelvic organs from their typical anatomical position due to weakened or damaged supportive structures. Apical prolapse is characterized by the descent of the uterus, cervix, or vaginal vault (following hysterectomy).1,2 Apical support is critical in POP surgery. Proper apical restoration corrects about 55% of anterior and 30% of posterior vaginal wall defects, while improper restoration is associated with a significantly increased risk of postoperative recurrence.3–6 While several surgical options exist for apical prolapse, no single procedure is universally recommended as the optimal choice in current guidelines.2,7

Sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF) remains a standard surgical option for apical prolapse repair. The procedure can be carried out through abdominal, laparoscopic, or vaginal routes, with the vaginal approach being the most commonly utilized. Traditional transvaginal SSLF relies primarily on palpation for suture placement, with limited surgical visualization.8–10 Given that the sacrospinous ligament (SSL) lies deep within the pelvis, adjacent to the bowel and surrounded by critical neurovascular structures, this technique not only increases procedural difficulty and the risk of injury to surrounding nerves, vessels, and rectum during suturing but also presents significant training challenges for novice surgeons.11 In recent years, the enhanced visualization provided by laparoscopic techniques has led some surgeons to adopt transvaginal natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery (vNOTES) for SSLF.12–16 This approach effectively addresses the issue of poor visualization encountered when suturing the SSL during the traditional vaginal approach. However, specialized vNOTES access platforms are costly and preclude direct palpation of the SSL. To address these limitations, we simplified the technique based on previous work by omitting specialized platforms and insufflation, developing the “Kocher forceps-guided laparoscopic transvaginal SSLF”. This approach maintains the advantage of direct ligament palpation while allowing suture placement under direct vision. It also offers a cost-saving benefit.

This study aimed to introduce the Kocher forceps-guided laparoscopic transvaginal SSLF technique. Specifically, we sought to evaluate its feasibility and safety in achieving direct visualization of the SSL to guide suture placement. The procedure was performed using only three retractors, a Kocher forceps, a laparoscope, and a laparoscopic needle holder. Unlike previously reported endoscopic and visual-guided SSLF methods, our technique uses a Kocher forceps to securely grasp and stabilize the SSL, avoids CO2 insufflation, requires no dedicated vNOTES port or specialized devices, and offers substantially lower procedural costs.

Materials and Methods

All consecutive patients who underwent Kocher forceps-guided laparoscopic transvaginal SSLF at the Affiliated Jiangning Hospital of Nanjing Medical University between November 2024 and July 2025 were included in this evaluation. Based on clinical indications, additional procedures including hysterectomy, partial cervicectomy, anterior colporrhaphy, and posterior colporrhaphy were performed as needed. All procedures were performed by a single senior gynecologist with extensive experience in POP surgery. This was a retrospective case series. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Jiangning Hospital of Nanjing Medical University(Approval No. 2026–03-033-K01) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Clinical data collected included patient age, menopausal status, body mass index (BMI), gravidity, parity, delivery mode, preoperative Pelvic Organ Prolapse Quantification (POP-Q) stage, concomitant procedures, total operative time, estimated blood loss, perioperative complications, last follow-up POP-Q stage and last follow-up duration. Total operative time was measured from skin incision to wound closure, encompassing all concomitant procedures. Prolapse recurrence was defined as either the presence of POP-Q stage ≥II in any compartment or the need for surgical retreatment.

Operative technique

Step 1: Following positioning in the lithotomy position, general anesthesia was administered.

Step 2: About 50 mL of normal saline (0.9% sodium chloride solution) was injected between the posterior vaginal wall and the rectum to create a hydrodissection cushion. A longitudinal incision was made in the midline of the posterior vaginal wall, approximately 2–3 cm from the hymen and from the vaginal apex, respectively.

Step 3: The rectovaginal space was bluntly dissected with a finger in the direction from the 4‑o’clock to 8‑o’clock position. The right pararectal space was then dissected sharply and bluntly toward the ischial spine. After palpating and localizing the right ischial spine, the SSL was identified and a Kocher forceps was used to grasp and fix the anchoring point approximately 2 cm medial to the ischial spine. The right pararectal space was further expanded by pushing the peritoneal sac on the ventral side of the SSL toward the sacrococcygeal direction, and three vaginal retractors were inserted.

Step 4: A 30°, 10‑mm laparoscope was introduced into the pararectal space. Under laparoscopic guidance, the SSL and the anchoring point secured by the Kocher forceps were clearly visualized (Figure 1A). Step 5: Under direct laparoscopic vision, a round-bodied needle (1/2 circle, 10×20 mm) carrying a nonabsorbable suture was used to place two sutures (as a loop) at the tip of the Kocher forceps, with approximately 1 cm between the two stitches (Figure 1B–D). The sutures were passed without beyond the superior border of the SSL and without penetrating its full thickness. They were then gently pulled to confirm the fixation strength.

Step 6: The incision was extended toward the vaginal apex. The two sutures were separately placed and anchored to the vaginal apex at the level of the most distal attachment of the uterosacral‑cardinal ligament complex. For patients with the uterus or cervix, the sutures were fixed to the cervical fascia at the same level. The upper part of the vaginal incision was closed continuously with a 2‑0 absorbable suture, starting from the apex. All non‑absorbable sutures at the apex were completely buried to prevent suture exposure. The knots were tied to reposition and fix the vaginal apex onto the SSL, ensuring that no “bridge” of suture material remained between the apex and the ligament. The lower segment of the vaginal incision was then sutured.

Step 7: Hemostasis was rechecked, and a drainage strip was placed if necessary. Antiseptic solution was applied intravaginally, and a gauze pack was placed for 24 hours.

Discussion

Since its introduction in 1958, SSLF has accumulated nearly 70 years of clinical experience, establishing itself as a safe and effective procedure, primarily performed via the transvaginal route.17 However, the widespread adoption of traditional transvaginal SSLF has been hindered by its reliance on the surgeon’s palpation and subjective judgment, a consequence of inadequate surgical exposure. Although certain devices have been developed to perform SSLF through a small dissection area under non‑visualized conditions, their use has been associated with relatively high rates of complications and reoperation.18 Furthermore, non-visualized SSLF complicates the identification and management of hemorrhage.19 The potential advantages of performing SSLF under direct visualization remain substantial.20–22 However, the traditional transvaginal SSLF is limited by poor visualization, especially during the step of suturing the SSL, which sometimes must be performed guided solely by palpation. Consequently, there is a compelling need for a transvaginal SSLF technique that provides enhanced visualization.

Gynecologists are actively exploring visualization techniques for transvaginal SSLF, among which the vNOTES approach has been the most extensively studied. In a study by Qin et al12, single-port transvaginal natural orifice transluminal endoscopic SSLF was successfully completed in 18 patients with POP-Q stage III–IV prolapse, demonstrating satisfactory short-term outcomes and a low incidence of complications. Liu et al13 evaluated transvaginal single-port extraperitoneal laparoscopic SSLF in a case series of nine patients with POP-Q stage III or IV apical prolapse and found the procedure to be both safe and effective. In a randomized controlled trial by Lyu et al14, 60 patients with POP-Q stage III–IV prolapse were divided into two groups: 30 underwent single-port laparoscopic-assisted transvaginal natural cavity endoscopic sacrospinous ligament suspension (SvNOTES group), while the other 30 underwent conventional transvaginal SSLF. Outcomes including operative time, intraoperative blood loss, length of hospital stay, postoperative complications, and postoperative POP-Q stage were compared between the two approaches. The results showed that, compared with traditional SSLF, the SvNOTES group achieved a higher anchor success rate, reduced intraoperative blood loss, and lower complication rates. However, operative time and hospital stay were longer in the SvNOTES group. The vNOTES approach for SSLF demonstrated superior objective and subjective outcomes compared with conventional vaginal surgery, with notably greater benefits observed in patients who also underwent bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy.15 These findings suggest that visualized laparoscopic-assisted transvaginal SSLF is not only feasible and safe but also superior to the conventional transvaginal approach in terms of achieving higher success rates and a lower complication profile.

Given the excellent surgical field of view provided by laparoscopy, some gynecologists have applied laparoscopic techniques to SSLF, which contributed to the development of the SvNOTES approach. Building on this concept, we simplified the procedure and developed the “Kocher forceps‑guided laparoscopic transvaginal SSLF”. In our approach, after palpating and identifying the SSL, the surgeon uses a Kocher forceps to grasp the target site, followed by suture placement under direct laparoscopic vision. This method preserves the advantage of direct palpation for accurate ligament identification—a hallmark of the traditional transvaginal approach—while enabling precise visual suturing. Suturing is performed without finger guidance. Instead, a Kocher forceps is used to grasp and stabilize the SSL. This not only improves visualization of the surgical field but also minimizes the risk of finger injury to the operator. Furthermore, our procedure does not require CO2 insufflation or the establishment of a single‑port platform, thereby reducing both surgical costs and the risks associated with insufflation. Of note, laparoscopic suturing is a fundamental skill with which most gynecologists are already familiar. For those with a solid understanding of pelvic floor anatomy, this technique can be acquired after a short training period.

Recognizing that CO2 insufflation in SvNOTES procedures carries potential risks, including gas embolism, and that dedicated vNOTES platforms are often costly, several investigators have sought alternative methods to achieve visualization during transvaginal SSLF.14 Gasless surgery offers the advantage of avoiding general anesthesia, allowing spinal anesthesia to be used, and is associated with reduced postoperative pain. In light of these benefits, Gautier Chene et al23 performed gasless vNOTES for SSL in two female cadavers using a specific suture capture device and a transvaginal access platform. Given the high cost of specialized vNOTES access platforms, some centers have adopted homemade single‑port devices.14 Initially, we also employed a homemade single‑port approach for this procedure. However, we encountered practical challenges including difficult port placement, gas leakage, and the formation of retroperitoneal emphysema. Consequently, some gynecologists have attempted to perform visualized transvaginal SSLF without relying on dedicated vNOTES platforms. For instance, Nurullah Peker et al9 reported performing SSLF under endoscopic visualization using a 5 mm diagnostic hysteroscope and Deschamps ligature needles, a method that closely parallels our surgical technique. Nonetheless, we introduce a distinct procedural step by routinely applying a Kocher forceps instead of a finger to secure the SSL prior to suture placement, an adaptation we consider to improve surgical visualization and facilitate suture placement. Gautier Chene et al10,24 achieved visual guidance during transvaginal SSLF by utilizing a 1.9 mm fiber optic system coupled with a specific suture capture device. However, the high cost of these instruments, approximately 500 euros for the fiber optic system and 900 euros for the reusable suture capture device, remains a practical limitation.

Based on the vNOTES approach for SSLF, we developed the “Kocher forceps‑guided laparoscopic transvaginal SSLF,” which eliminates the need for specialized vNOTES access platforms and CO2 insufflation. This procedure enables visualized suture placement on the SSL while reducing surgical costs and avoiding the potential adverse effects associated with insufflation. Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged, including the small sample size, the absence of a control group, and the relatively short follow-up period. In addition, some standardized clinical outcomes, such as sexual function and quality‑of‑life scores, were not collected. These outcomes will be incorporated in future prospective studies using questionnaires. Therefore, further investigations with larger cohorts, comparative study designs, and extended follow-up are required to confirm the feasibility and safety of this surgical technique.

Conclusion

The Kocher forceps-guided laparoscopic transvaginal SSLF appears to be a feasible and safe surgical approach for the treatment of POP in this small case series. By combining the advantages of direct ligament palpation with enhanced visualization for suture placement, this simplified technique eliminates the need for specialized vNOTES platforms and insufflation, offering a cost-effective alternative with favorable short-term outcomes. We acknowledge that the small sample size is insufficient to draw definitive conclusions regarding feasibility, safety, and cost-related factors. Further studies with larger sample sizes, comparative study designs, and longer follow-up periods are warranted to validate these preliminary findings.

Dimension Polynomials for Affine Partial Difference Algebraic Groups Thanks: This work was financially supported by EPSRC via the Doctoral Training Partnership Studentship.
Orla McGrath

Abstract
. We develop the theory of difference algebraic groups in the case where we have finitely many pairwise commuting difference operators. We show that the defining ideal of a difference algebraic group is finitely generated as a difference ideal, and this result allows us to prove the existence of a dimension polynomial for any partial difference algebraic group.

Primary: 14L17, Secondary: 14L15, 12H10

Introduction

While an affine algebraic group is a subgroup of the general linear group that is defined by some polynomial equations, an affine difference algebraic group is a subgroup of the general linear group that is defined by difference polynomial equations.

Firstly, for the benefit of the reader with no background in difference algebra, we will state a special case of our main results in the language of algebraic symbolic dynamics. For an affine group scheme
\mathcal{G}
of finite type (over some field), we can consider the
n
-
dimensional group shift
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{N}^{n}}
equipped with the shift maps

\sigma_{j}\colon\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{N}^{n}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{N}^{n}}
given by
\sigma_{j}((g_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}})=(g_{\alpha+e_{j}})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}
, where
e_{j}
is the
j
-th standard basis vector for
1\leq j\leq n
.
An affine difference algebraic group is then analogous to a closed subgroup scheme
G
of
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{N}^{n}}
which is closed under each of the shift maps. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
G_{i}
be the scheme theoretic image of
G
in
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{N}^{n}_{\leq i}}
, where
\mathbb{N}_{\leq i}^{n}=\{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\ |\ \alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}\leq i\}
. In this setup, the main results in this paper are as follows. There exists
m\geq 1
and some closed subgroup
\mathcal{H}\leq\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{N}^{n}_{\leq m}}
such that
G
is fully determined by
\mathcal{H}
and the fact that
G
is closed under each of the shift maps. That is,

\displaystyle G=\{(g_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\ |\ (g_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\beta+\mathbb{N}^{n}_{\leq m}}\in\mathcal{H}\ \text{for all}\ \beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}.

Further, there exists a numerical polynomial
\phi(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
of degree less than or equal to
n
such that for large enough
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\phi(i)=\dim(G_{i})
.

Now we will give a brief summary of the concepts and results in this paper in the language of difference algebra.

•

Suppose that we have fields

from the category of

Lemma 2.2

Let

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(X)=\{f\in k\{y_{1},\dots,y_{s}\}\ |\ f(x)=0\ \text{for all $k$\penalty\hbox{-}\penalty\hskip 0.0pt\ignorespaces$\sigma$\penalty\hbox{-}\penalty\hskip 0.0pt\ignorespaces algebras $R$ and all $x\in X(R)$}\}

-

It is straightforward to check that

-

We know that

Let

Since any

Given some

and a

Proposition 2.5

A functor from

Firstly, suppose that

for some

-

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

and

be
\sigma
-

. There is a one-to-one correspondence between morphisms

of

-

of

\sigma

This is the difference analogue of the Yoneda Lemma [
[

]
, Section 1.3, page 6]. Let

-
algebraic group with respect to
\mathcal{G}
if the following hold:

•

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
G[i]
is a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup of
\mathcal{G}[i]
, defined by Hopf ideal
\mathbb{I}(G[i])
of

,

•

for every

,
\mathbb{I}(G[i-1])\subseteq\mathbb{I}(G[i])
,

•

for every

.

We see that similarly to the Zariski closures of a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
, these generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic groups are some kind of ‘splitting’ of a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group with respect to an algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
over
k
. The union of defining ideals

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(G)=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{I}(G[i])

defines a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup
G
of
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

We will show that
\mathbb{I}(G)
is a
\sigma
-
Hopf ideal of
k\{\mathcal{G}\}
.
Since for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{I}(G[i])
is a Hopf ideal of
k[\mathcal{G}[i]]
, and the ideals
(\mathbb{I}(G[i]))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
form an ascending chain, their union
\mathbb{I}(G)
is a Hopf ideal of
\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}k[\mathcal{G}[i]]=k\{\mathcal{G}\}
. Further, given
f\in\mathbb{I}(G)
,
f\in\mathbb{I}(G[i])
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and hence for any
1\leq j\leq n
,

\sigma_{j}(f)\in\sigma_{j}(\mathbb{I}(G[i]))\subseteq\mathbb{I}(G[i+1])\subseteq\mathbb{I}(G)
. That is,
\mathbb{I}(G)
is a
\sigma
-
Hopf ideal of
k\{\mathcal{G}\}
and therefore, by
Proposition

3.7
, defines a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup
G
of
\mathcal{G}
.
∎

We see that for a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup
G
of an algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
, we can either form the Zariski closures
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
, or find a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
such that
\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{I}(G[i])=\mathbb{I}(G)
. These both correspond to ascending chains of ideals for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
which union to
\mathbb{I}(G)
, but there is a unique choice for the Zariski closures, and many choices for such a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group.

4.1.
Building a Natural Structure

Let
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group with respect to an algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
over
k
. Consider

\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{I}(G[i])=\mathbb{I}(G)

which, by
Lemma

4.2
, defines a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup
G
of
\mathcal{G}
.
We can construct the Zariski closures
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
as in
Section

3.3
. We call
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
the
Zariski closures
of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
. If
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are the Zariski closures of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
G_{i}\leq G[i]
.

Since for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{I}(G_{i})
is finitely generated as an ideal of
k[\mathcal{G}[i]]
, and
\mathbb{I}(G_{i})\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{I}(G[j])
, we see that there must exist some
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathbb{I}(G_{i})\subseteq\mathbb{I}(G[j])
.
Define

\displaystyle j_{i}=\max\{i,\min\{j\ |\ \mathbb{I}(G_{i})\subseteq\mathbb{I}(G[j])\}\}\in\mathbb{N}

for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
. That is,
j_{i}
is the smallest natural number that is greater than or equal to
i
and that
\mathbb{I}(G_{i})\subseteq\mathbb{I}(G[j_{i}])
.
Further, notice that

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(G_{i})=\mathbb{I}(G[j_{i}])\cap k[\mathcal{G}[i]],

and
j_{i}
is the smallest natural number greater than or equal to
i
for which this is true. We call
(j_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
the
Zariski indicators
of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
.

Given a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup
G
of some algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
,
immediately we see that if
(j_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are the Zariski indicators of the Zariski closures
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
, then for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
j_{i}=i
.

Example 4.5
.

Consider the generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and its corresponding Zariski closures
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
from
Example

4.4
. For
0\leq i\leq 3
,
\mathbb{I}(G_{i})=\mathbb{I}(G[i])
, so
j_{i}=i
, and for all
i\geq 4
,
\mathbb{I}(G_{i})\not\subseteq\mathbb{I}(G[i])
, but
\mathbb{I}(G_{i})\subseteq\mathbb{I}(G[i+1])
. That is, for all
i\geq 4
,
j_{i}=i+1
.

Intuitively, the Zariski indicators
(j_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are a measure of how different our generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group is to its corresponding Zariski closures.
Notice that in
Example

4.4
, the generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group given was intuitively ‘one step behind’ the Zariski closures. Then in
Example

4.5
, we see that for all large enough
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
j_{i}=i+1
for this particular generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group with respect to an algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
over
k
. Let
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Zariski closures of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{G}
and let
(j_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Zariski indicators of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
. Suppose that there exists an
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(G_{i+1})=(\mathbb{I}(G_{i}),\sigma_{1}(\mathbb{I}(G_{i})),\dots,\sigma_{n}(\mathbb{I}(G_{i})))

for all
i\geq m
. If
j_{m}-m=0
, then
\mathbb{I}(G[i])=\mathbb{I}(G_{i})
for all
i\geq m
.

Proof.

Firstly notice that if
j_{i}=i
, then
\mathbb{I}(G[i])=\mathbb{I}(G_{i})
. So this proof reduces to showing that if
j_{m}=m
, then for all
i\geq m
,
j_{i}=i
. This can be proven by induction on the value of
i\geq m
. Clearly the base case holds, so suppose that for some
i\geq m
,
j_{i}=i
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(G_{i+1})

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{I}(G_{i}),\sigma_{1}(\mathbb{I}(G_{i})),\dots,\sigma_{n}(\mathbb{I}(G_{i})))

as
i\geq m

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{I}(G[i]),\sigma_{1}(\mathbb{I}(G[i])),\dots,\sigma_{n}(\mathbb{I}(G[i])))

by inductive hypothesis

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathbb{I}(G[i+1])

by definition

so
j_{i+1}\leq i+1
, hence
j_{i+1}=i+1
as required.
∎

4.2.
The Projections of a Generalised Difference Algebraic Group

Recall that the key difference between the Zariski closures
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup
G
of
\mathcal{G}
and a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
is that for
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\pi[i+1](G_{i+1})=G_{i}
, whereas
\pi[i+1](G[i+1])\leq G[i]
. We would like to consider what the image of
G[i+1]
under the map
\pi[i+1]
would be. We will see that these images will form a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group that is ‘closer’ to the Zariski closures than our original generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a generalised difference algebraic group with respect to some algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
over
k
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
F[i]=\pi[i+1](G[i+1])
. We refer to the sequence
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
as the
projections of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
under
\pi
.

That is, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\pi[i+1]\colon G[i+1]\rightarrow F[i]
is a quotient map.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group with respect to an algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
over
k
. The projections
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
under
\pi
form a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group with respect to
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Notice that for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
F[i]
is the closed subgroup of
\mathcal{G}[i]
defined by

\mathbb{I}(F[i])=\mathbb{I}(G[i+1])\cap k[\mathcal{G}[i]]
.
The fact that the sequence
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies
4.1a
then follows from the fact that the sequence
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies
4.1a
.
∎

Given a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
and the projections
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
under
\pi
, since

\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{I}(G[i])=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{I}(F[i])

by definition, the Zariski closures of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
are the same as the Zariski closures of
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group with respect to an algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
over
k
and let
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the projections of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
under
\pi
. As in
Section

4.1
, let
(j_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Zariski indicators of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
respectively. If
j_{i}>i
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
, then
f_{i}\leq j_{i}-1
.

Proof.

Let
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Zariski closures of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
(and hence of
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
) with respect to
\mathcal{G}
.
Recall that for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
j_{i}
and
f_{i}
are the smallest natural numbers greater than or equal to
i
such that
\mathbb{I}(G_{i})\subseteq\mathbb{I}(G[j_{i}])
and
\mathbb{I}(G_{i})\subseteq\mathbb{I}(F{[f_{i}]})
respectively.
Suppose that
j_{i}>i
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(G_{i})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}(G[j_{i}])\cap k[\mathcal{G}[i]]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}(G[j_{i}])\cap k[\mathcal{G}[j_{i}-1]]\cap k[\mathcal{G}[i]]

as
j_{i}>i

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}(F{[j_{i}-1]})\cap k[\mathcal{G}[i]]

so
\mathbb{I}(G_{i})\subseteq\mathbb{I}(F[j_{i}-1])
, and
j_{i}-1\geq i
, that is,
f_{i}\leq j_{i}-1
as required.
∎

We already know that Zariski closures behave ‘nicely’ in the ordinary case (
Theorem

3.18
), so perhaps we can say more about a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group if it behaves similarly to its Zariski closures. An advantage of working with the projections of a generalised difference algebraic group is that the projections are closer to the shared Zariski closures than the original group is.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group with respect to
\mathcal{G}
and let
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the projections of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
. Let
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Zariski closures of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to an algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
over
k
. Suppose that there exists some
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(G_{i+1})=(\mathbb{I}(G_{i}),\sigma_{1}(\mathbb{I}(G_{i})),\dots,\sigma_{n}(\mathbb{I}(G_{i})))

for all
i\geq m
.
Let
(j_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Zariski indicators of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
respectively. If

Example 4.11
.

Consider the generalised
\sigma
-

(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

4.3.
The Extensions of a Generalised Difference Algebraic Group

We now introduce a construction which will allow us to be able to consider, for example,
G[i]
or
{{}^{\sigma_{j}}}G[i]
as a subgroup of
\mathcal{G}[i+1]
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq j\leq n
. This construction will be useful in the proof of the ideal generation property for generalised difference algebraic groups.

Firstly, for a subset
A\subseteq T_{\sigma}
, we put
\mathcal{G}_{A}=\prod_{\tau\in A}{{}^{\tau}}\mathcal{G}
. Therefore, for any
A\subseteq B\subseteq T_{\sigma}
, there is a quotient map
\mathcal{G}_{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{A}
, and
k[\mathcal{G}_{A}]\subseteq k[\mathcal{G}_{B}]
. Notice that

\mathcal{G}_{T_{\sigma}[i]}=\mathcal{G}[i]
and
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma_{j}(T_{\sigma}[i])}={{}^{\sigma_{j}}}\mathcal{G}[i]

for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq j\leq n
. This notation allows us to introduce a new definition.

Definition 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an algebraic group over
k
.
Suppose that we have subsets
A\subseteq B\subseteq T_{\sigma}
.
Given a closed subgroup
\mathcal{H}\leq\mathcal{G}_{A}
defined by the ideal
\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq k[\mathcal{G}_{A}]
, let

\widehat{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}_{B}}

be the closed subgroup of
\mathcal{G}_{B}
defined by
\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{H})k[\mathcal{G}_{B}]
, the ideal of
k[\mathcal{G}_{B}]
generated by
\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{H})
. We call
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}_{B}}

Lemma 4.13

\mathcal{G}

For a closed subgroup

\displaystyle\mu\left(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}_{C}}\right)=\widehat{\mu_{1}(\mathcal{H})}^{\mathcal{G}_{B}}

\mathcal{G}_{A}

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\colon\mathcal{G}_{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{A\cap B},\quad\mu_{2}\colon\mathcal{G}_{C\backslash A}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{B\backslash(A\cap B)}.

Further, by properties of projection maps,

1

Definition 6.1
.

.
We say that a generalised

Notice that to say that

\displaystyle G[i+1]=\widehat{G[i]}^{\mathcal{G}[i+1]}\cap\widehat{{{}^{\sigma_{1}}}G[i]}^{\mathcal{G}[i+1]}\cap\cdots\cap\widehat{{{}^{\sigma_{n}}}G[i]}^{\mathcal{G}[i+1]}

for large enough
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

We will use results from
Section

5
to prove that the kernels
(H[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\pi
restricted to some generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to an algebraic group

satisfy the ideal generation property. That is, we will see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(H[i+1])=(\mathbb{I}(H[i]),\sigma_{1}(\mathbb{I}(H[i])),\dots,\sigma_{n}(\mathbb{I}(H[i])))

for large enough
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Notice that

are the original endomorphisms, not the twisted ones that we form in

.
Firstly, we will prove that if the ideal generation property holds for the kernels of any generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group, then it holds for any generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group.

\mathbb{I}(G)=[\mathbb{I}(G_{m})]
, and since
\mathbb{I}(G_{m})
is a finitely generated ideal,
\mathbb{I}(G)
is finitely
\sigma
-
generated as required.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

Let

be a finitely
\sigma
-
generated

-
\sigma
-
Hopf algebra. Then any

-

is finitely

Proof.

We know by

that

is the representing algebra for some

-

, which can be embedded into

for some

by

. That is,

for some

.

Let

Proof.

Given a

, recall the definition of the

,

Theorem 6.9
.

Let

.

of

, there is some finite set

, there is a finitely

Quotients of Difference Algebraic Groups

Now we will adapt arguments from [
[
34
]
, Section 3, pages 489-490] to generalise the existence of the quotient of a difference algebraic group by any normal difference closed subgroup to the partial case.
This section uses concepts from [
[
22
]
, Section 3] and results on augmentation ideals from [
[
30
]
, Section 4].

7.1.
Normal Subgroups and Normal Hopf Ideals

Definition 7.1
.

Let
G
be a
\sigma
-
algebraic group over
k
. A
\sigma
-
closed subgroup
N
of
G
is called
normal
if for every
k
-
\sigma
-
algebra
R
,
N(R)
is a normal subgroup of
G(R)
.

For an element
a
of a
k
-
\sigma
-
Hopf algebra
A
, we write
\Delta(a)=\sum a_{1}\otimes a_{2}
. Due to coassociaitivity, we can write
(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{id})\circ\Delta=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta=\sum a_{1}\otimes a_{2}\otimes a_{3}
without confusion.

Consider, for a
\sigma
-
algebraic group
G
, the adjoint mapping
\psi\colon G\times G\rightarrow G
, where for each
k
-
\sigma
-
algebra
R
,
\psi_{R}\colon G(R)\times G(R)\rightarrow G(R)
is of the form
(g,h)\mapsto ghg^{-1}
. This has dual map
\psi^{*}\colon k\{G\}\rightarrow k\{G\}\otimes_{k}k\{G\}
, such that
a\mapsto\sum(a_{1}S(a_{3})\otimes a_{2})
.

A
\sigma
-
closed subgroup
N
of
G
is normal if and only if the restriction
\psi\colon G\times N\rightarrow N
is well-defined. This happens if and only if the induced map
\psi^{*}\colon k\{G\}/I\rightarrow(k\{G\}\otimes_{k}k\{G\})/(k\{G\}\otimes_{k}I)
is well-defined, where
I
is the defining ideal of
N
in
G
. This is well-defined if and only if
\psi^{*}(I)\subseteq k\{G\}\otimes_{k}I
.

This motivates the definition for a normal difference Hopf ideal, which is the difference analogue to a normal Hopf ideal as defined in [
[
22
]
, Section 3.4, page 35].

Definition 7.2
.

A
\sigma
-
Hopf ideal
I
of a
k
-
\sigma
-
Hopf algebra
A
is
normal
if
\psi^{*}(a)=\sum(a_{1}S(a_{3})\otimes a_{2})\in A\otimes_{k}I
for any
a\in I
.

By definition, a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup
N
of
G
is normal if and only if its defining ideal
I\subseteq k\{G\}
is a normal
\sigma
-
Hopf ideal.

7.2.
Quotients

Given a morphism
\phi:G\rightarrow H
of
\sigma
-
algebraic groups over
k
, the
kernel

\ker(\phi)
of
\phi
is the functor from
k
-
\sigma
-
\operatorname{Alg}
to
\operatorname{Sets}
that takes a
k
-
\sigma
-
algebra
R
to
\ker(\phi)(R)=\ker(\phi_{R})\subseteq G(R)
. The kernel
\ker(\phi)
of
\phi
is in fact a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup of
G
, defined by the
\sigma
-
Hopf ideal
\phi^{*}(\mathfrak{m}_{k\{H\}})k\{G\}
of
k\{G\}
, that is, the ideal of
k\{G\}
that is generated by the image of the augmentation ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{k\{H\}}
of
k\{H\}
under the dual morphism to
\phi
[
[
33
]
, Section 2.1, page 14].

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
G
be a
\sigma
-
algebraic group over
k
, and let
N
be a normal
\sigma
-
closed subgroup of
G
. Then there exists a
\sigma
-
algebraic group
G/N
and a morphism
\pi:G\rightarrow G/N
of
\sigma
-
algebraic groups with
N\leq\ker(\pi)
such that for any morphism
\phi:G\rightarrow H
of
\sigma
-
algebraic groups with
N\leq\ker(\phi)
, there exists a unique morphism of
\sigma
-
algebraic groups
\psi:G/N\rightarrow H
such that
\phi^{\prime}\circ\pi=\phi
.

Proof.

We know that
I=\mathbb{I}(N)
is a normal
\sigma
-
Hopf ideal of
k\{G\}
, so it is well defined to consider

\displaystyle k\{G\}(I)=\{f\in k\{G\}\ |\ \Delta(f)-f\otimes 1\in k\{G\}\otimes_{k}I\},

which, by Lemma 4.4 in [
[
30
]
, Section 4, page 11],
is a
k
-
Hopf subalgebra of
k\{G\}
. Since the comultiplication
\Delta\colon k\{G\}\rightarrow k\{G\}\otimes_{k}k\{G\}
is a morphism of
k
-
\sigma
-
algebras,

k\{G\}(I)
is in fact a
k
-
\sigma
-
Hopf subalgebra of
k\{G\}
. By [
[
30
]
, Section 4, page 12],
k\{G\}(I)
is the greatest
\sigma
-
Hopf
-
subalgebra of
k\{G\}
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{k\{G\}(I)}\subseteq I
, and in fact,
I=\mathfrak{m}_{k\{G\}(I)}k\{G\}
, the
\sigma
-
Hopf ideal of
k\{G\}
generated by the augmentation ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{k\{G\}(I)}
of
k\{G\}(I)
.
By
Lemma

6.8
,
k\{G\}(I)
is finitely
\sigma
-
generated and hence by
Proposition

3.6
, is the representing algebra for some
\sigma
-
algebraic group over
k
, which we call
G/N
.

Let
\pi\colon G\rightarrow G/N
be the morphism of
\sigma
-
algebraic groups over
k
that is dual to the inclusion
\pi^{*}\colon k\{G/N\}\hookrightarrow k\{G\}
of
k
-
\sigma
-
Hopf algebras. Now,
\ker(\pi)
is the
\sigma
-
closed subgroup of
G
defined by the
\sigma
-
Hopf ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{k\{G/N\}}k\{G\}
of
k\{G\}
. Since
k\{G/N\}=k\{G\}(I)
, we see that
\ker(\pi)
is defined by
\mathfrak{m}_{k\{G\}(I)}k\{G\}=I
, that is,
\ker(\pi)=N
.
Therefore, we have found a
\sigma
-
algebraic group
G/N
and a morphism
\pi\colon G\rightarrow G/N
with
N\leq\ker(\pi)
, let us now show this satisfies the required property.

Suppose that
\phi\colon G\rightarrow H
is a morphism of
\sigma
-
algebraic groups with
N\leq\ker(\phi)
. As
\phi^{*}:k\{H\}\rightarrow k\{G\}
is a morphism of
k
-
\sigma
-
Hopf algebras, it respects the counit and hence
\phi^{*}(\mathfrak{m}_{k\{H\}})k\{G\}=\mathfrak{m}_{\phi^{*}(k\{H\})}k\{G\}
. We know that
\mathbb{I}(\ker(\phi))=\phi^{*}(\mathfrak{m}_{k\{H\}})k\{G\}\subseteq I
. Since
k\{G/N\}
is the largest Hopf subalgebra of
k\{G\}
with
\mathfrak{m}_{k\{G/N\}}k\{G\}\subseteq I
, we see that
\phi^{*}(k\{H\})\subseteq k\{G/N\}
. That is, we have well-defined restriction of codomain
\phi^{\prime*}\colon k\{H\}\rightarrow k\{G/N\}
. Since
\pi^{*}\colon k\{G/N\}\rightarrow k\{G\}
is the inclusion, we see that
\pi^{*}\circ\phi^{\prime*}=\phi^{*}
, and further that
\phi^{\prime*}
is the unique morphism of
k
-
\sigma
-
Hopf algebras with this property. Hence, letting
\phi^{\prime}\colon G/N\rightarrow H
be the dual morphism to
\phi^{\prime*}
,
\phi^{\prime}
is the unique morphism of
\sigma
-
algebraic groups such that
\phi^{\prime}\circ\pi=\phi
as required.
∎

For a
\sigma
-
algebraic group
G
over
k
and a normal
\sigma
-
closed subgroup
N
of
G
, a morphism
\pi:G\rightarrow G/N
satisfying the universal property in
Theorem

7.3
is called a
quotient
of
G\operatorname{mod}N
.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
G
be a
\sigma
-
algebraic group over
k
and let
N
be a normal
\sigma
-
closed subgroup of
G
.
A morphism of
\sigma
-
algebraic groups
\pi^{\prime}:G\rightarrow(G/N)^{\prime}
is a quotient of
G\operatorname{mod}N
if and only if
\ker(\pi^{\prime})=N
and
\pi^{\prime*}:k\{(G/N)^{\prime}\}\rightarrow k\{G\}
is injective.

Proof.

Let
G/N
and
\pi:G\rightarrow G/N
be the
\sigma
-
algebraic group and the morphism of
\sigma
-
algebraic groups defined in the proof of
Theorem

7.3
. We have already shown that
\ker(\pi)=N
and that
\pi^{*}:k\{G/N\}\rightarrow k\{G\}
is injective.

Now, suppose that we have
\sigma
-
algebraic group
(G/N)^{\prime}
and a morphism
\pi^{\prime}:G\rightarrow(G/N)^{\prime}
such that
\pi^{\prime}
is a quotient of
G\operatorname{mod}N
. Then there is a unique morphism
\phi:(G/N)^{\prime}\rightarrow G/N
such that
\phi\circ\pi^{\prime}=\pi
, and a unique morphism
\psi:G/N\rightarrow(G/N)^{\prime}
such that
\psi\circ\pi=\pi^{\prime}
. As
(\psi\circ\phi)\circ\pi^{\prime}=\pi^{\prime}
, by uniqueness,
\psi\circ\phi=\operatorname{id}_{H}
, and similarly we see that
\phi\circ\psi=\operatorname{id}_{G/N}
. That is,
\phi
is an isomorphism of
\sigma
-
algebraic groups. Therefore, the dual morphism
\phi^{*}:k\{G/N\}\rightarrow k\{(G/N)^{\prime}\}
is an isomorphism of
k
-
\sigma
-
Hopf algebras such that
\pi^{\prime*}\circ\phi^{*}=\pi^{*}
. Since
\pi^{*}
is injective,
\pi^{\prime*}
is also injective.
As
\phi^{*}
is an isomorphism of
k
-
\sigma
-
Hopf algebras,
\phi^{*}(\mathfrak{m}_{k\{G/N\}})=\mathfrak{m}_{k\{(G/N)^{\prime}\}}
. Using this along with the fact that
\pi^{\prime*}\circ\phi^{*}=\pi^{*}
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(\ker(\pi^{\prime}))=\pi^{\prime*}(\mathfrak{m}_{k\{(G/N)^{\prime}\}})k\{G\}=\pi^{*}(\mathfrak{m}_{k\{G/N\}})k\{G\}=\mathbb{I}(N),

and this proves that
\ker(\pi^{\prime})=N
as required.

Conversely, suppose that
\ker(\pi^{\prime})=N
and that
\pi^{\prime*}
is injective.
Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(N)=\mathbb{I}(\ker(\pi^{\prime}))=\pi^{\prime*}(\mathfrak{m}_{k\{(G/N)^{\prime}\}})k\{G\}.

As
k\{G/N\}

is the unique
k
-
\sigma
-
Hopf subalgebra of
k\{G\}
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{k\{G/N\}}k\{G\}=\mathbb{I}(N)
by [
[
30
]
, Section 4, page 12], we see that
\pi^{\prime*}(k\{(G/N)^{\prime}\})=k\{G/N\}
. That is,
(G/N)^{\prime}
and
G/N
are isomorphic as
\sigma
-
algebraic groups. It is then straightforward to check that
\pi^{\prime}:G\rightarrow(G/N)^{\prime}
satisfies the required properties to be a quotient of
G\operatorname{mod}N
.
∎

Notice that in the proof of
Lemma

7.4
we have proven that given a
\sigma
-
algebraic group
G
and a normal
\sigma
-
clsoed subgroup
N
of
G
, the quotient of
G\operatorname{mod}N
is unique up to isomorphism.

8.
Dimension Polynomials

Recall that the
dimension

\dim(\mathcal{H})
of an algebraic group
\mathcal{H}
over
k
is equal to the Krull dimension of the coordinate ring
k[\mathcal{H}]
[
[
33
]
, Section 3.2, page 86].
A polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
is called
numerical
if for large enough
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
f(t)\in\mathbb{Z}
[
[
17
]
, Section 2.1, page 45].

Definition 8.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an algebraic group over
k
.
We say that there exists a
dimension polynomial
for a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
if there exists a numerical polynomial
\phi(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
such that for large enough
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\phi(i)=\dim(G[i])
. We call
\phi(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
the dimension polynomial for
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. The degree of the dimension polynomial is the degree of
\phi(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group with respect to an algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
over
k
. Suppose that there exists a numerical polynomial
\phi(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
of degree at most
n-1
such that

\displaystyle\dim(G[i+1])-\dim(G[i])=\phi(i)

for large enough
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists a dimension polynomial of degree at most
n
for
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\dim(G[i+1])-\dim(G[i])=\phi(i)
for all
i\geq m
, where
\phi(i)
has degree
r\leq n-1
. Then we have that

\dim(G[i+1])=\dim(G[m])+\sum_{j=m}^{i}\phi(j)
for
i\geq m
.
By Proposition 2.1.6 in [
[
17
]
, Chapter II, page 49], there exists a numerical polynomial
\psi(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
of degree
r+1
such that

\displaystyle\psi(i)

\displaystyle=\phi(m+1)+\phi(m+2)+\cdots+\phi(m+(i-m))=\dim(G[i+1])-\dim(G[m+1])

for any
i\geq m
. Then, let
\psi^{\prime}(t)=\psi(t-1)+\dim(G[m+1])
and notice that
\psi^{\prime}(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
is a numerical polynomial of degree
r+1
such that for
i\geq m+1
,
\psi^{\prime}(i)=\dim(G[i])
. That is, there exists a dimension polynomial of degree at most
n
for
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

To prove the existence of the dimension polynomial, we will proceed in a similar way to the proof of the ideal generation property. Firstly we will prove that if there is a dimension polynomial for the kernels of a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group, then there is a dimension polynomial for the generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group itself.

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an algebraic group over
k
. Suppose that for any generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
, there exists a dimension polynomial of degree at most
n-1
for the kernels
(H[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\pi
restricted to
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Then there exists a dimension polynomial of degree at most
n
for any generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
and let
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Zariski closures of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
.
Let
(H_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the kernels of
\pi
restricted to
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. By assumption, there exists a dimension polynomial
\phi(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
of degree at most
n-1
for
(H_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
For every
i\geq 1
, since
\pi[i]\colon G_{i}\rightarrow G_{i-1}
is a quotient map, by [
[
21
]
, Section 5c, page 104], we have that

\dim(G_{i})-\dim(G_{i-1})=\dim(H_{i})
.
That is, there exists a numerical polynomial
\phi(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
of degree at most
n-1
such that

\dim(G_{i})-\dim(G_{i-1})=\phi(i)

for large enough
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, by
Lemma

8.2
, there exists a dimension polynomial of degree at most
n
for
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Now, let
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group with respect to
\mathcal{G}
, and let
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Zariski closures of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be the smallest natural number such that

\mathbb{I}(G_{i+1})=(\mathbb{I}(G_{i}),\sigma_{1}(\mathbb{I}(G_{i})),\dots,\sigma_{n}(\mathbb{I}(G_{i})))

for all
i\geq m
. Such an
m
exists by
Theorem

6.4
. Let
(j_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Zariski indicators of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
. We will prove that there exists a dimension polynomial for generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic groups by induction on the value of
j_{m}-m\geq 0
.
We have already shown that there exists a dimension polynomial of degree at most
n
for the Zariski closures of any
\sigma
-
algebraic group (and hence for the Zariski closures of any generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group).
If
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is such that
j_{m}-m=0
, for every
i\geq m
,
\mathbb{I}(G[i])=\mathbb{I}(G_{i})
by
Lemma

4.6
, and hence the dimension polynomial for the sequence
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is also a dimension polynomial for the sequence
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. Suppose that there exists a dimension polynomial of degree at most
n
for any generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
such that
j_{m}-m<p
for some
p\geq 1
.

Let
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group with respect to
\mathcal{G}
such that
j_{m}-m=p\geq 1
and let
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the projections of
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
under
\pi
.
Lemma

4.8
tells us that
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group with respect to
\mathcal{G}
, and by
Corollary

4.10
, we can apply the inductive hypothesis to
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. That is, there exists a dimension polynomial
\phi(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
of degree at most
n
for
(F[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
By assumption, there exists a dimension polynomial
\psi(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
of degree at most

for the kernels

.
Since for every

That is,

for large enough

.
Letting
\Phi(t)=\phi(t-1)+\psi(t)
, we see that

is a dimension polynomial for

of degree at most
n
.
∎

8.1.
Dimension Polynomials in the Ordinary Case

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an algebraic group over an ordinary
\sigma
-
field

\sigma
-
algebraic group

with respect to

.

Proof.

Let

be the kernels of

, for large enough

for

By

, there exists a dimension polynomial of degree at most

for any (ordinary) generalised
\sigma
-
algebraic group
(G[i])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
.
∎

8.3.

We have seen (in

Let

\sigma
-

over
k
By

or
\sigma
-

Proof.

We adapt the argument from the ordinary case in [

, Section 3, page 529]. By

. Let

-

We will now see how the difference dimension, difference type and typical difference dimension defined for a difference algebraic group in

9.1.
Difference Modules

Given a
\sigma
-

-

-

Therefore,

Proof.

Using the constructions above, for each

For each

Therefore, the dimension polynomials

9.3.
Relating Difference Field Extensions to Difference Algebraic Groups

In this section, we will show that given a difference algebraic group
G
over a
\sigma
-
field
k
(where
\sigma_{j}\colon k\{G\}\rightarrow k\{G\}
is injective for each
1\leq j\leq n
) such that
k\{G\}
is an integral domain, we can find a corresponding
\sigma
-
field extension
k\langle G\rangle
of
k
. Then we will prove that
k\langle G\rangle
and
G
have the same
\sigma
-
dimension,
\sigma
-
type and typical
\sigma
-
dimension respectively. Firstly we recall some concepts and results from [
[
18
]
, Section 4].

Let
k
be a field and let
L
be an overfield of
k
. Recall that a set
B\subseteq L
is called a transcendence basis of
L
over
k
if
B
is algebraically independent over
k
and
L
is algebraic over
k(B)
(the smallest subfield of
L
containing both
k
and
B
). Any two transcendence bases of
L
over
k
have the same cardinality, called the transcendence degree of
L
over
k
, which we denote
trdeg_{k}L
.

For a
\sigma
-
field
k
and a
\sigma
-
overfield
L
, given a subset
A\subseteq L
, denote by
k\langle A\rangle
the smallest
\sigma
-
field containing both
k
and
A
. We call
k\langle A\rangle
the
difference field extension
(
\sigma
-
field extension) of
k
generated by
A
. We say that a
\sigma
-
field extension
L
of
k
is
finitely
\sigma
-
generated
if
L=k\langle A\rangle
for some finite set
A\subseteq L
. We recall Theorem 4.2.1 from [
[
18
]
, Section 4.2, page 255] proving the existence of dimension polynomials for finitely difference generated difference field extensions.

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
k
be a
\sigma
-
field, and let
k\langle A\rangle
be the
\sigma
-
field extension of
k

\sigma
-
generated by a finite set
A=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\}
for some
s\geq 1
. Then there exists a numerical polynomial
\phi(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
of degree at most
n
such that for large enough
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\phi(i)=trdeg_{k}k(\{\tau a_{j}\ |\ \tau\in T_{\sigma}[i],\ 1\leq j\leq s\})
.

We know that for a finitely
\sigma
-
generated
\sigma
-
field extension
L=k\langle A\rangle
of
k
, its dimension polynomial is of the form
\psi(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}c_{i}\binom{t+i}{i}
, where
c_{0},\dots,c_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
c_{d}\neq 0
, and
d\leq n
. Further, by [
[
18
]
, Theorem 4.2.1], the degree
d
of the polynomial and the integer
c_{d}
are invariant of the choice of
\sigma
-
generators
A
.

The integers
d
and
c_{d}
are called the
difference type
and
typical difference dimension
of the
\sigma
-
field extension
L
over
k
respectively. Further, we define the
difference dimension
of
L
over
k
to be
c_{d}
if
d=n
, and
0
if
d<n
.

We will now see the partial analogue to Proposition 3.17 in [
[
35
]
, Section 3, page 531]. Note that if a
k
-
\sigma
-
algebra
R
is an integral domain, with
\sigma_{j}:R\rightarrow R
injective for each
1\leq j\leq n
, its field of fractions is also a
k
-
\sigma
-
algebra.

Theorem 9.5
.

Let
G
be a
\sigma
-
closed subgroup of an algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
over a
\sigma
-
field
k
such that for each
1\leq j\leq n
,
\sigma_{j}\colon k\{G\}\rightarrow k\{G\}
is injective. Suppose that the
k
-
\sigma
-
Hopf algebra
k\{G\}
is an integral domain, and therefore its field of fractions
k\langle G\rangle
is a
\sigma
-
field extension of
k
. There exists a finite
\sigma
-
generating set
A
of the
\sigma
-
field extension
k\langle G\rangle
such that the dimension polynomials for the
\sigma
-
field extension
k\langle A\rangle
of
k
and the Zariski closures of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
whose existence are proven in
Theorem

9.4
and
Theorem

8.5
respectively are equal.
Therefore,
k\langle G\rangle
and
G
have the same
\sigma
-
dimension,
\sigma
-
type and typical
\sigma
-
dimension respectively.

Proof.

Let
G_{0}
be the
0
-th order Zariski closure of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
, and let
A
be a finite generating set of the
k
-
algebra
k[G_{0}]\subseteq k\{G\}
. Then
k\{G\}
is generated as a
k

\sigma
-
algebra by

\sigma
-
generated as a
\sigma
-

For each

Then for each

,

is the field of fractions of

. By [
[

\dim(G_{i})

Therefore, the dimension polynomials

\sigma
-
dimension,
\sigma
-
type and typical
\sigma
-
dimension respectively.
∎

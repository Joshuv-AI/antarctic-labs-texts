The failure of the Ehlers–Kundt conjecture in the impulsive case
Authors: Moriz L. Frauenberger, James D. E. Grant, Roland Steinbauer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In 1962, Ehlers and Kundt conjectured that plane waves are the only class of complete Ricci-flat pp-waves, i.e. metrics on \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathbb {R}}^4$$\end{document}R4 of the form \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$ ds^2=2du\,dv+dx^2+dy^2+H(x,y,u)du^2\,. $$\end{document}ds2=2dudv+dx2+dy2+H(x,y,u)du2.Recently, Flores and Sánchez gave a proof of the conjecture in the fundamental case of spatially polynomially bounded profile functions H. However, impulsive pp-waves, i.e. waves with concentrated profile functions of the form \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H(x,y,u)=f(x,y)\,\delta (u)$$\end{document}H(x,y,u)=f(x,y)δ(u) (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta $$\end{document}δ, the Dirac measure) have been found to be complete for arbitrary (smooth) spatial profile functions f. We summarise completeness results for several classes of impulsive wave spacetimes achieved during the last years and discuss them in the context of the Ehlers–Kundt conjecture.

The class of pp-waves is an important family of exact solutions to the Einstein equations.

Remark 1.2

The causal behaviour of plane wave metrics is quite well understood. It was shown by Penrose [40] that plane wave metrics are not globally hyperbolic. This result was refined in [16],4 where it was shown that (Ricci-flat) plane wave metrics are strongly causal, stably causal and causally continuous, but they are not causally simple (and, hence, not globally hyperbolic).

The Ehlers–Kundt conjecture

In their 1962 article [15, pp. 97], Ehlers and Kundt proposed the following problem, suggesting that plane waves play a privileged role among pp-waves:

“Prove the plane waves to be the only g-complete pp waves, no matter which topology one chooses.”

This statement is now referred to as “the Ehlers–Kundt conjecture.” Intuitively speaking, a complete, Ricci-flat pp-wave describes a gravitational field without singularities (i.e. it is geodesically complete) and without internal matter sources (i.e. it satisfies the vacuum Einstein equations). By analogy with Maxwell’s equations in vacuum, where the simplest solutions are monochromatic plane waves, the Ehlers–Kundt conjecture asserts that the gravitational plane waves play the corresponding role as the most elementary, globally regular, source-free gravitational fields. This analogy between gravitational and electromagnetic waves has been emphasised in the physics literature. (See, for instance, [7, §8].)

In a more precise formulation, noting that only Ricci-flat metrics are considered in [15], the conjecture can be stated as follows.

Positive results

A special case of the Ehlers–Kundt conjecture, the so-called polynomial Ehlers–Kundt conjecture, in which H is assumed to be bounded by a polynomial, has been solved by Flores and Sánchez in [20]. The relationship between the geodesic structure of pp-waves and Newtonian dynamical systems was realised in [15]. This connection was refined in [12, 18], where the Ehlers–Kundt conjecture was translated into a problem concerning the escape of trajectories in Newtonian potentials defined by harmonic functions. Completeness for impulsive waves

Impulsive gravitational waves

In this section, we turn to impulsive waves or, more precisely, to the impulsive version of the metric (1.2). Generally, impulsive gravitational waves model short but violent pulses of gravitational or other radiation. They were introduced by Penrose in the late 1960’s and considered in detail in [41].9 Over the decades, the models have been widely generalised to include a non-vanishing cosmological constant as well as gyratonic terms [27, Ch. 20]. They have been found to arise as ultrarelativistic limits of Kerr–Newman and other static spacetimes (see, e.g., [1, 2, 30, 38]), which makes them interesting models for quantum scattering in general relativistic spacetimes (see, e.g., [8, 51]), the wave memory effect [56, 58] and entanglement harvesting (see [26] and references therein). Moreover, they have found applications in astrophysics [3], and serve as key examples in mathematical investigations of low-regularity spacetimes. (See [52] and forthcoming works in synthetic Lorentzian geometry.)

Remark 2.7

In the special case where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(N, {\textbf{h}})$$\end{document}(N,h) is Euclidean space and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f(x) \equiv 1$$\end{document}f(x)≡1, the INPW metric may be viewed as a gluing of two Minkowski half-spaces across a null hypersurface. Motivated by these considerations, we shall now obtain a more natural completeness statement within the framework of nonlinear distributional geometry.

Finally, we remark that the above result establishes the failure of the Ehlers–Kundt conjecture in the impulsive case.

Corollary 2.13

The Ehlers–Kundt conjecture fails for INPWs which are geodesically complete irrespective of the (smooth) profile function f.

Conceptually, completeness ceases to be a rigidity criterion in the presence of a distributional wave profile. This not only settles the natural “impulsive EK” analogue in the negative, but also underscores the subtle role played by regularity in the geometric and dynamical characterizations of exact wave spacetimes.

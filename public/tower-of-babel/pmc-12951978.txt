MAPK signaling reprogramming via integrative TCM-Western medicine strategy: mechanistic interactions between bioactive herbal components and chemotherapy in ovarian cancer therapy — a comprehensive review
Authors: Shiwen Wang, Fangyuan Liu, Fengjuan Han
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Ovarian cancer (OC) ranks as the sixth leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women globally, with a five-year survival rate below 50%. These malignancies may originate from the ovarian surface epithelium, gonadal stroma, or germ cells, where the central therapeutic challenge lies in the development of chemoresistance. Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) demonstrates unique advantages in enhancing chemosensitivity, arresting cell cycle progression, suppressing proliferation, inducing apoptosis, inhibiting angiogenesis, mitigating chemotherapeutic adverse effects, and improving postoperative quality of life. The mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling network—comprising ERK, p38, and JNK pathways—orchestrates cellular proliferation, death, and microenvironment remodeling through a three-tiered kinase cascade (MAPKKK→MAPKK→MAPK). Its aberrant activation forms bidirectional regulatory circuits with tumor-adaptive signaling network rewiring, driving platinum resistance. Although conventional chemotherapy transiently inhibits core MAPK nodes (e.g., ERK phosphorylation), it fails to block compensatory bypass mechanisms such as PI3K/WNT pathways. Through systematic interrogation of PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and CNKI databases, this study proposes a dual-mode synergistic strategy termed “pathway targeting-dynamic network remodeling.” This approach deciphers the efficacy-enhancing mechanisms of integrated TCM-Western therapy from spatiotemporal signaling dynamics, establishing a theoretical framework for developing precision combination therapies guided by network pharmacology and computational modeling.Graphical abstract

Introduction

Ovarian cancer remains the most lethal gynecologic malignancy, with its 5-year survival rate stagnating at 45%−50% over the past decade, primarily due to chemoresistance development [1]. MAPK is a serine-threonine protein kinase that plays a controlling role in cellular processes such as embryogenesis, cell fertilization, cell proliferation and cell death. When stimulated by extracellular signals, intracellular signals, or traumatic injury, these signals bind to receptors and release signaling factors that activate the corresponding MAPK signaling pathways through a specific tertiary cascade system -- MAPKK kinase (MAPKK/MKK/MEKK/MLK), MAPKK kinase (MAPKK/MKK/MEKK), and MAPK (MAPK). pathway and triggers the transcription of related downstream factors [2, 3]. The MAPK family consists of three major signaling pathways: extracellular regulated kinase (ERK), p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (p38 MAPK), and c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) [4]. The ERK signaling pathway regulates cell proliferation, while the p38 MAPK and JNK signaling pathways regulate cell proliferation, cell death and inflammation [5]. Recent studies reveal that aberrant MAPK signaling activation and tumor microenvironment-driven signaling network reprogramming form bidirectional regulatory circuits that jointly mediate platinum resistance [6]. While conventional chemotherapeutic agents can inhibit core MAPK pathway nodes (e.g., ERK phosphorylation), they fail to reverse tumor cell compensation mechanisms via PI3K/WNT bypass signaling networks [7]. In contrast, TCM bioactive components like curcumin disrupt resistance-associated network topology by modulating MAPK pathway spatiotemporal oscillation characteristics (period ≈ 6–8 h) [8], whereas celastrol enhances paclitaxel sensitivity through reprogramming MAPK-PPARγ crosstalk network entropy (ΔH ≈ 0.38) [9]. This review systematically elucidates the molecular basis by which integrated Chinese-Western medicine strategies reverse ovarian cancer chemoresistance via “pathway targeting-dynamic network remodeling” dual mechanisms, establishing a theoretical framework for developing precision combination therapies based on spatiotemporal signaling dynamics.

The integrative TCM-Western medicine strategy discussed in this review is designed to address the most critical unmet need in ovarian oncology. Epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) accounts for over 90% of cases and is the leading cause of ovarian cancer-related mortality, primarily driven by the development of chemoresistance. Meanwhile, non-epithelial subtypes are of great significance due to their unique etiology, genetic background, and first-line treatment strategies.

The ultimate objective of this integrative strategy is to inform future clinical applications. As clinical trials for combination therapies are predominantly conducted in the EOC population, restricting our preclinical evidence base to established EOC models ensures that the mechanistic insights and conclusions derived are directly interpretable and translatable to the patient cohort most likely to benefit. MAPK and ovarian cancer

ERK and ovarian cancer

The extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) subgroup constitutes an essential branch of the MAPK superfamily, principally governing mitogenic responses and lineage specification. Experimental evidence reveals that ERK1 (MAPK3) and ERK2 (MAPK1) exhibit cooperative interaction in driving ovarian carcinogenesis via the evolutionarily conserved Ras→Raf→MEK→ERK signaling module [18]. Mechanistically, these terminal kinases implement autoregulatory suppression of upstream RAF isoforms through phosphorylation-dependent inactivation. Such retrograde control mechanisms permit precise temporal regulation of pathway activity while preserving cellular equilibrium.

Functioning as the primary signaling mediator, the MEK-ERK axis transduces extracellular stimuli received through membrane-spanning receptors into nuclear transcriptional programs via phosphorylation cascades. Notably in high-grade serous ovarian carcinomas, sustained ERK1/2 activation triggers a phenotypic metamorphosis characterized by dissolution of epithelial junctions and acquisition of front-rear polarity [19]. This transition to a mesenchymal state, hallmarked by vimentin upregulation and E-cadherin suppression, confers augmented motility and tissue penetration capacity.

Of particular therapeutic relevance, the ERK5 (MAPK7) pathway demonstrates functional redundancy in sustaining mitogenic signaling during pharmacological blockade of ERK1/2 [20]. Clinical trial data indicate that combinatorial targeting of both MAPK branches reduces acquired resistance incidence from 68% to 43% (Δ = 25%, p < 0.01) compared with monotherapy regimens in platinum-resistant ovarian malignancies [21]. These findings underscore the necessity for developing bispecific kinase inhibitors to circumvent adaptive resistance mechanisms.

Cell cycle potentiation

Through phosphorylation-dependent stabilization of G1/S transition mediators (e.g., Cyclin D1, CHK2, CDC4), ERK1/2 orchestrates cyclin-CDK complex assembly, thereby driving cell cycle progression through G1/S checkpoint transition. This mitogenic signaling is amplified via formation of ERK-kinase suppressor of Ras (KSR) scaffolds at the nuclear periphery.

Apoptotic constraint

ERK1/2-mediated inhibitory phosphorylation of pro-apoptotic effectors, including caspase-9 (at Thr125) and p53 (at Ser315), attenuates mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization. Concurrent stabilization of Bcl-2 family members through direct interaction with Bad phosphorylated at Ser112 establishes an anti-apoptotic threshold.

Anabolic programming

ERK1/2 coordinates nucleolar biogenesis via ELK1-dependent transcriptional activation of RNA polymerase I (targeting 45 S rRNA) and III (regulating 5 S rRNA/tRNA). Parallel phosphorylation of S6 kinase (S6K1) at Thr389 enhances ribosome assembly efficiency, while MNK1-mediated eIF4E phosphorylation (Ser209) promotes cap-dependent translation initiation.

p38 MAPK and ovarian cancer

The p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway constitutes a critical branch of the MAPK family that responds to diverse extracellular stimuli. Activation occurs through a three-tiered kinase cascade [22]. Emerging evidence demonstrates the pathway’s involvement in ovarian cancer progression through regulation of cellular differentiation, programmed cell death, metastatic potential, and chemoresistance mechanisms [23]. The mammalian genome encodes six distinct isoforms exhibiting tissue-specific expression patterns: p38α (MAPK14) with two splice variants (α1/α2), p38β (MAPK11) containing β1/β2 isoforms, along with p38γ (MAPK12) and p38δ (MAPK13) [24, 25]. Mechanistically, apoptosis signal-regulating kinase 1 (ASK1) initiates p38 activation through dual phosphorylation of MKK3/4/6. Concurrently, reactive oxygen species (ROS) sustain pathway activity by post-translational inhibition of multiple phosphatases including PP5, PP2C, WIP-1, and MAP kinase phosphatases (MKPs), thereby preserving the phosphorylated state of signaling components throughout the cascade [26].

The p38 MAPK signaling pathway orchestrates dual regulation of proliferative and apoptotic processes in ovarian malignancies [27]. Within neoplastic cells, this kinase cascade modulates downstream cell fate determinants through phosphorylation-mediated control of cyclin-dependent kinases (CDK2/4 at Thr160/Tyr15) and Bcl-2 homology domain 3 (BH3)-only proteins. Such molecular reprogramming drives dysregulated cell cycle progression (G1/S transition rate increased by 2.8-fold) and confers apoptosis resistance (caspase-3 activity reduced to 35% baseline), collectively accelerating tumorigenesis and disease recurrence [28, 29].

This signaling axis critically governs metastatic competence through transcriptional network engineering. Pathway activation triggers multisite phosphorylation of DNA-binding domains in ATF-2 (Thr69/71), ETS1 (Ser251/Thr38), and NF-κB p65 (Ser536), enhancing their transactivation potential by 40–65% in luciferase reporter assays. Mechanistically, ATF-2 serves as a molecular bridge that recruits c-Jun to AP-1 consensus sequences (TGACTCA motif) through its bZIP domain. The coordinated action of phosphorylated AP-1 (Fos/Jun heterodimers) and ETS factors induces 3.5-fold upregulation of urokinase plasminogen activator (uPA) transcription, establishing a proteolytic cascade (uPA→plasmin→MMP-2/9) that degrades extracellular matrix components (collagen IV degradation > 72%) to facilitate peritoneal dissemination.

JNK and ovarian cancer

The c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) family, alternatively designated as stress-activated protein kinases (SAPKs), constitutes a distinct subgroup within the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling cascade. This family comprises three isoforms: JNK1, JNK2, and JNK3. Notably, JNK3 demonstrates tissue-specific expression patterns localized to cerebral tissue, cardiac muscle, testicular organs, and pancreatic islets, whereas JNK1 and JNK2 exhibit ubiquitous distribution across mammalian tissues [30]. JNK activation proceeds through a three-tiered kinase cascade, wherein mixed lineage kinases (MLKs) or MEKK1/4 phosphorylate and activate MKK4/7, which in turn directly phosphorylate JNK [31].

This signaling pathway demonstrates responsiveness to diverse cellular stressors, including but not limited to pathogenic challenges, inflammatory mediators, reactive oxygen species, genomic instability, osmotic imbalance, and cytoskeletal reorganization [32]. Phosphorylation of downstream transcriptional regulators, specifically Ets-like protein-1 (Elk-1) and activating transcription factor 2 (ATF2), by activated JNK enhances their DNA-binding affinity and transcriptional regulatory capacity [30, 33, 34]. Functional analyses reveal JNK’s pleiotropic involvement in critical cellular processes including survival mechanisms, proliferative regulation, programmed cell death, oncogenic transformation, and developmental morphogenesis [35]. These multifunctional characteristics position the JNK signaling pathway as a promising target for molecular therapeutic interventions.

The JNK signaling pathway facilitates neoplastic cell migration and invasion through coordinated regulation of cytoskeletal remodeling and proteolytic degradation of extracellular matrix components, enabling tumor cell transmigration across anatomical barriers and subsequent tissue infiltration. Mechanistically, this pathway contributes to chemoresistance mechanisms by conferring therapeutic resistance against chemotherapeutic agents. Multidrug resistance (MDR), defined as a pathological state where malignant cells develop resistance to structurally unrelated chemotherapeutic compounds with distinct molecular targets, represents a critical clinical challenge. This phenomenon manifests as tumor cell resistance to single-agent therapy followed by cross-resistance development to pharmacologically diverse agents. Notably, MDR constitutes a principal contributor to chemotherapy failure and ultimately leads to therapeutic refractoriness in cancer management [36]. Experimental evidence establishes significant crosstalk between MAPK signaling cascades and MDR pathogenesis. Pharmacological inhibition of JNK/SAPK enzymatic activity enhances tumor cell chemosensitivity, while strategic blockade of JNK signaling elevates cellular drug response thresholds [37, 38].

Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase (MAPK) and non-epithelial ovarian cancer

Non-epithelial ovarian malignancies, including granulosa cell tumors (GCTs) and yolk sac tumors (YSTs), are characterized by distinct oncogenic drivers that may serve as potential therapeutic targets. Among these, aberrant MAPK pathway activation has been identified as a central event in tumorigenesis and progression. In adult-type GCTs, the hallmark FOXL2 C134W mutation is recognized as a key genetic alteration. Recent evidence indicates that this mutation promotes sustained MAPK/ERK signaling, facilitating unchecked cellular proliferation and impaired apoptosis. Specifically, mutant FOXL2 induces constitutive phosphorylation of ERK1/2, resulting in upregulated cyclin D1 expression and accelerated G1/S phase transition [17].

Nevertheless, research efforts face considerable challenges. The low incidence of non-epithelial ovarian cancers has constrained the establishment of representative cellular and animal models, with most investigations relying on a narrow set of cell lines (e.g., KGN, COV434) that may not reflect clinical diversity. Furthermore, the molecular landscape of YSTs—particularly regarding MAPK pathway involvement—remains poorly elucidated compared to that of GCTs. Perhaps most critically, the translational development of natural compounds is hindered by insufficient pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profiling, underscoring the necessity for more advanced disease models and mechanistic studies. TCM active components modulating MAPK signaling in ovarian cancer therapy

Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) demonstrates distinctive therapeutic merits in ovarian cancer management through its holistic regulatory mechanisms, comprehensive therapeutic outcomes, and reduced adverse effects. Accumulating clinical evidence confirms TCM’s effectiveness in enhancing quality of life, alleviating disease-related symptoms, and mitigating chemotherapy-induced toxicity in ovarian cancer patients. The MAPK signaling pathway serves as a critical regulator of multiple oncogenic processes, including cellular proliferation, apoptotic resistance, metastatic progression, and chemoresistance development in ovarian malignancies. Emerging pharmacological research reveals that both single-compound TCM agents and herbal formulations exert anti-tumor effects through precise modulation of MAPK pathway components, presenting novel therapeutic strategies for ovarian carcinoma intervention.

Flavonoids

Dihydromyricetin (DMY), a naturally occurring flavonoid compound abundantly present in various Ampelopsis species, demonstrates multifaceted biological activities including free radical scavenging, antioxidant protection, antithrombotic properties, neoplastic suppression, and anti-inflammatory responses. Mechanistic studies reveal that DMY exerts concentration-dependent pro-apoptotic effects on HO-8910 human ovarian carcinoma cells through modulation of the ERK/MAPK signaling cascade. This phytochemical significantly upregulates expression of pro-apoptotic mediators caspase-3 and Bax while simultaneously suppressing Bcl-2 protein levels, collectively contributing to programmed cell death and tumor growth inhibition [39]. Further investigations elucidate DMY’s capacity to induce Golgi apparatus structural alterations through caspase-3 activation mediated by JNK and ERK transduction pathways. The compound specifically reduces GRASP65 (Golgi reassembly stacking protein of 65 kDa) expression, resulting in characteristic Golgi ultrastructural changes including edema, fragmentation phenomena, and subsequent apoptotic progression. These findings collectively indicate that DMY’s anti-ovarian cancer efficacy potentially involves GRASP65 regulation through cross-talk between JNK and ERK signaling networks [40].

Chrysin (5,7-dihydroxyflavone), chemically designated as 5,7-dihydroxy-2-phenyl-4 H-chromen-4-one, represents a naturally derived flavonoid compound with documented pharmacological properties. This bioactive agent is predominantly isolated from Oroxylum species (family Bignoniaceae), particularly Oroxylum indicum (L.) Kurz. Chrysin exhibits broad-spectrum therapeutic effects, including suppression of inflammatory mediator synthesis, attenuation of inflammatory cascades, induction of programmed cell death, inhibition of neoplastic proliferation, anti-angiogenic activity, free radical neutralization, and cellular protection against oxidative stress [41]. Experimental evidence demonstrates that chrysin induces cell cycle arrest at G1 phase through p38 MAPK activation in C6 glioma cells, thereby suppressing gliomal proliferation. This regulatory mechanism suggests chrysin’s potential to modulate oncogenic signaling pathways [42]. Recent advancements in structural modification have yielded chrysin derivatives with enhanced antitumor efficacy compared to the parent compound. Notably, 8-nitrochrysin (5,7-dihydroxy-8-nitroflavone), synthesized through regioselective nitration at the C8 position of the flavonoid backbone, demonstrated enhanced pro-apoptotic efficacy in HO-8910 human ovarian carcinoma cells. Mechanistic studies reveal that 8-nitrochrysin upregulates both p38 MAPK and phosphorylated p38 MAPK (p-p38 MAPK) expression, elevates caspase-3 enzymatic activity, and depletes intracellular glutathione (GSH) reserves, collectively driving apoptotic execution [43].

Kaempferol, a bioactive flavonoid compound, exhibits multi-target therapeutic properties including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anti-carcinogenic activities [44]. Mechanistically, tumor necrosis factor-related apoptosis-inducing ligand (TRAIL) initiates apoptotic cascades through death receptor (DR4/DR5) activation on malignant cell surfaces. Pharmacological investigations demonstrate kaempferol’s dual regulatory function in upregulating pro-apoptotic DR4/DR5 expression while suppressing anti-apoptotic protein levels via ERK/JNK/CHOP signaling axis modulation. Notably, kaempferol enhances cellular sensitivity to TRAIL-mediated apoptosis through marked upregulation of CHOP expression and MAPK-dependent death receptor activation [45].

Tangeritine (Citrus unshiu peel-derived flavonoid), predominantly found in citrus peel, exhibits antioxidant, anticancer, and neuroprotective properties. Emerging evidence indicates this bioactive compound induces apoptosis in ovarian cancer SKOV3 cells through multiple mechanisms: decelerating disease progression by suppressing cellular proliferation, impairing invasive potential, and inhibiting tumor metastasis. The therapeutic effects are mediated through modulation of oxidative stress markers and subsequent inhibition of the Ras/MEK/ERK signaling pathway. Specifically, tangeritine downregulates reactive oxygen species generation, which correlates with decreased phosphorylation levels of key pathway components, ultimately attenuating malignant cell survival and metastatic capacity [46].

Hydroxysafflor yellow A (HSYA), a bioactive flavonoid compound derived from Carthamus tinctorius L. (family Asteraceae), demonstrates significant pharmacological potential including anti-inflammatory, anti-apoptotic, and neoplasm-inhibiting properties [47]. Experimental evidence reveals that HSYA exerts tumor-suppressive effects through molecular pathway modulation [48]. Mechanistic studies indicate that this natural product downregulates WD repeat and SOCS box-containing protein 1 (WSB1) expression, subsequently reducing extracellular signal-regulated kinase (Erk) phosphorylation levels in human Skov3 ovarian carcinoma cells. These molecular alterations ultimately lead to significant suppression of cellular proliferation, suggesting critical involvement of the MEK/ERK signaling cascade in HSYA-mediated antiproliferative effects [49].

Ginkgetin (also known as ginkgo biflavone), a biflavonoid derived from Ginkgo biloba L., demonstrates anticancer, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties. Sirtuin-1 (SIRT1), a class III histone deacetylase, modulates critical cellular processes including apoptosis, proliferation, autophagy, and tumorigenesis. Mechanistic studies reveal that Ginkgo biloba extract suppresses ovarian cancer progression through dual inhibition of the JAK2/STAT3 and MAPK signaling cascades. Specifically, it reduces phosphorylation levels of ERK (p-ERK) and STAT3 (p-STAT3) in tumor tissues while downregulating SIRT1 expression. Experimental data indicate that ginkgetin-mediated suppression of p-STAT3, SIRT1, and p-ERK correlates with inhibited proliferation, induced apoptosis, and attenuated migratory/invasive capacities of ovarian cancer cells, thereby impeding metastatic progression [50].

Furthermore, curcumin demonstrates dual regulatory effects on neoplastic processes: while suppressing tumor cell proliferation through Ras-ERK signaling pathway inhibition, it paradoxically activates the Ras/ERK cascade and downstream caspase activation in gastric adenocarcinoma (AGS) cells, culminating in programmed cell death [51]. Notably, naringin effectively downregulates cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) expression in SKOV3 ovarian carcinoma cells via coordinated modulation of p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (p38 MAPK) and extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 (ERK1/2) pathways, thereby achieving significant suppression of ovarian cancer progression (Wang et al., 2019). Mechanistic investigations confirm that both phytochemicals modulate the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling network, simultaneously inhibiting ovarian cancer cell proliferation, triggering apoptotic pathways, and overcoming chemotherapeutic resistance [52, 53]. Additionally, baicalin—a flavone glycoside predominantly isolated from Scutellaria baicalensis Georgi roots—exhibits multiple physiological activities including anti-inflammatory, antiallergic, and potent antineoplastic properties.

Terpenoids

Gentiopicroside (GPS), a bioactive secoiridoid glycoside primarily isolated from the roots and rhizomes of Gentiana scabra Bunge and Gentiana macrophylla Pall, demonstrates multifaceted pharmacological properties including anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective, and antitumor activities. Experimental evidence reveals that GPS exerts inhibitory effects on proliferation and migration while inducing apoptosis in human ovarian epithelial carcinoma cells [54]. Mechanistic investigations using graded concentrations (high, medium, low) of GPS in HO8910 cell models demonstrated dose-dependent modulation of apoptotic regulators: upregulated expression of p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (p38 MAPK) and pro-apoptotic Bax, concurrent with downregulation of anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 [55]. These findings collectively indicate that GPS triggers apoptosis in ovarian cancer cells through p38 MAPK pathway activation and subsequent Bax/Bcl-2 ratio imbalance.

Triptolide, a bioactive diterpenoid triepoxide isolated from Tripterygium wilfordii Hook.f. (family Celastraceae), represents a key phytoconstituent in traditional Chinese medicine. Experimental evidence demonstrates its potent antiproliferative effects against ovarian carcinoma cells through mechanistically distinct pathways [56]. Despite its therapeutic potential, the parent plant exhibits dose-limiting reproductive, nephro-, and hepatotoxicities. To address these limitations, a liposome-calcium phosphate hybrid nanocarrier system loaded with triptolide has been developed, demonstrating enhanced colloidal stability, improved aqueous solubility, prolonged systemic circulation, and reduced female reproductive toxicity compared to the free compound. Notably, the Tripterygium-derived compound in combination with its calcium phosphate nanovector induces apoptotic execution in SKOV3 ovarian adenocarcinoma cells through a dual mechanism involving excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation and subsequent mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway activation [57].

Polysaccharides

Ligustrum lucidum polysaccharide (LLP), the primary bioactive constituent isolated from Ligustrum lucidum W.T.Aiton, exhibits antitumor activity and immunomodulatory properties. Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), a member of the human epidermal growth factor receptor (HER) family, regulates critical physiological processes including cellular growth, proliferation, and differentiation. Experimental studies using graded concentrations of LLP in HO8910 human ovarian carcinoma cells demonstrate dose-dependent suppression of Bcl-2 expression and phosphorylation ratios (p-EGFR/EGFR and p-MAPK/MAPK), concurrent with marked upregulation of Bax and apoptosis induction. Notably, EGFR overexpression attenuates LLP’s anti-ovarian efficacy, suggesting compensatory pathway activation [58]. These findings collectively demonstrate that LLP induces apoptosis in ovarian cancer cells through EGFR/MAPK signaling axis inhibition.

Ardisia gigantifolia Stapf-derived polysaccharides (AGP), isolated from the plant’s roots, stems, and leaves, exhibit dual antioxidant and neoplasm-inhibiting biological activities. Pharmacological evaluation reveals concentration-dependent reductions in cellular viability and proliferative capacity of human SKOV3 and A2780 ovarian carcinoma cells following AGP treatment. Quantitative protein analysis demonstrates significant downregulation of anti-apoptotic B-cell lymphoma 2 (Bcl-2) and effector caspases (−3, −8, −9) in AGP-exposed cells, concurrent with upregulated expression of pro-apoptotic Bcl-2-associated X protein (Bax) and activated caspase-9 [59]. Notably, AGP administration elevates Bax protein levels while differentially modulating mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway components: phosphorylation states of extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) and c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) show initial enhancement followed by progressive attenuation with increasing AGP concentrations. This biphasic regulatory pattern confirms AGP’s dual therapeutic mechanism involving proliferation suppression through MAPK pathway inhibition and apoptosis induction via mitochondrial-mediated caspase activation.

Saponins

Protodioscin (PD), a naturally occurring furostanol saponin distributed across multiple plant taxa, exhibits antitumor and anti-inflammatory activities as demonstrated by contemporary pharmacological investigations [60]. The nuclear protein Ki67, a DNA-binding proliferation marker, correlates positively with proliferative cell fraction, tumor growth kinetics, and malignant progression [61]. Conversely, Bcl-2—an anti-apoptotic protein in the Bcl-2 family—enhances neoplastic cell survival through transcriptional upregulation in malignant cells [62] In contrast, the pro-apoptotic Bax protein induces mitochondrial cytochrome c release, sequentially activating initiator caspase-9 and executioner caspase-3 to execute programmed cell death [63]. Cleaved caspase-3, the activated form of this apoptotic effector protease, serves as both a biochemical executor of apoptosis and a quantitative indicator of apoptotic progression [64]. Experimental data reveal that PD modulates these critical regulators: downregulating Ki67 and Bcl-2 expression while upregulating Bax and cleaved caspase-3 levels in ovarian cancer models [65]. Pharmacological inhibition of JNK/p38 MAPK signaling abrogated PD-mediated suppression of SKOV3 cell viability, confirming pathway dependency. These findings collectively demonstrate that PD attenuates ovarian cancer cell viability and motility through JNK/p38 MAPK pathway activation.

Polyphyllin I, a steroidal saponin isolated from rhizomes of Paris vietnamensis (Takht.) H.Li and Paris verticillata M.Bieb. (Melanthiaceae family), demonstrates multifunctional pharmacological activities including anti-inflammatory, antiviral, and potent antineoplastic properties [66]. This phytochemical exhibits dual regulatory functions in cellular signaling pathways: serving as both a c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) pathway activator and a phosphoinositide-dependent kinase-1 (PDK1)/protein kinase B (Akt)/mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling axis inhibitor [67]. Mechanistically, Polyphyllin I induces apoptotic execution in ovarian cancer (OC) cells through two synergistic mechanisms: (1) upregulation of c-Jun transcription factor expression with consequent JNK pathway activation, and (2) initiation of mitochondria-dependent caspase cascade activation. Comparative dose-response analysis revealed enhanced pro-apoptotic efficacy at 1.5 µg/mL concentration compared to 0.75 µg/mL treatment in OC cell models [68].

Alkaloids

Evodiamine, a primary bioactive alkaloid constituent of Tetradium ruticarpum (A.Juss.) T.G.Hartley in traditional Chinese medicine, exhibits vasodilatory and anti-inflammatory properties. Treatment of highly metastatic HO-8910PM ovarian cancer cells with Evodiamine for 24 h induced marked upregulation of cleaved caspase-3, −8, −9, and Bax, alongside significant downregulation of Bcl-2 expression. Furthermore, Evodiamine demonstrated potent inhibition of PI3K/Akt and ERK1/2 MAPK signaling pathways, as evidenced by reduced phosphorylation levels of PI3K (p-PI3K) and Akt (p-Akt), along with suppressed activation of ERK1/2 MAPK. Mechanistic analyses revealed that this indoloquinazoline alkaloid triggers cell cycle arrest and apoptosis through coordinated modulation of PI3K/Akt, ERK1/2 MAPK, and p38 MAPK pathways, thereby attenuating ovarian cancer cell proliferation [69].

Other compounds

Echinacoside, a bioactive phenylethanoid glycoside primarily isolated from Cistanche deserticola Y.C.Ma (family Orobanchaceae), demonstrates multifaceted pharmacological properties including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antiviral activities. Experimental investigations reveal its therapeutic potential in ovarian cancer management, as evidenced by significant attenuation of p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) phosphorylation levels in SKOV3 ovarian carcinoma cells following echinacoside treatment. Quantitative cellular assays demonstrate dose-dependent reductions in both EdU incorporation rates (proliferation marker) and overall cell viability. These observations collectively indicate that echinacoside suppresses SKOV3 cell proliferation, inhibits invasive potential, and diminishes cancer stemness characteristics, with mechanistic studies confirming its inhibitory effect on p38 MAPK phosphorylation cascades [70].

Rheum palmatum L., a traditional Chinese medicinal herb, is pharmacologically characterized by heat-clearing, detoxifying, and blood circulation-promoting properties. Chrysophanol, a bioactive anthraquinone derivative isolated from Rheum palmatum L., demonstrates antitumor efficacy through multifaceted mechanisms. Experimental studies reveal that chrysophanol dose-dependently decreases ovarian cancer cell viability while enhancing apoptotic cell death [71]. Furthermore, in OVCAR3 ovarian carcinoma models, chrysophanol suppresses cellular proliferation and invasive capacity via modulation of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling cascade, thereby exerting antitumor effects on ovarian cancer progression.

Genipin, an iridoid glycoside derived from Gardenia jasminoides J.Ellis (Rubiaceae) and Eucommia ulmoides Oliv. (Eucommiaceae), exhibits broad-spectrum pharmacological properties encompassing antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, anti-angiogenic, and antineoplastic activities. In SKOV3 and A2780 ovarian carcinoma models, genipin treatment significantly upregulated pro-apoptotic Bax protein, activated caspase-3 cleavage, and enhanced p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) phosphorylation, while concurrently downregulating anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 expression. Mechanistic analysis revealed genipin-mediated suppression of neoplastic cell proliferation, invasion, and migration through reactive oxygen species (ROS)-dependent activation of the p38MAPK signaling cascade. This pathway modulation ultimately induced apoptosis in both SKOV3 and A2780 cell lines, with dose-dependent efficacy observed across experimental concentrations [72].

Tanshinone IIA, a lipophilic diterpenoid quinone derived from the traditional Chinese herb Salvia miltiorrhiza Bunge, demonstrates broad-spectrum cytotoxicity against multiple neoplastic cell types through mechanisms including apoptosis induction, differentiation promotion, and suppression of tumor cell invasion/metastasis. In ovarian cancer models, this bioactive compound exhibits dual therapeutic effects: (1) inducing programmed cell death and arresting proliferation, and (2) attenuating malignant aggressiveness and migratory capacity while potentiating chemosensitization [73]. Mechanistic studies further confirm that tanshinone IIA mediates its antitumor activity via selective modulation of the p38 MAPK signaling cascade, establishing its role as a pathway-targeted phytotherapeutic agent [74]. Active components of traditional Chinese medicine regulating the MAPK signaling pathway in the treatment of non-epithelial ovarian cancer

Although platinum-based chemotherapy remains the conventional treatment, its effectiveness is often transient. Bioactive phytochemicals derived from traditional medicinal sources have recently attracted attention for their capacity to modulate oncogenic signaling. Curcumin, for instance, has been shown to suppress viability and trigger apoptotic cell death in GCT models. This effect is mediated through concurrent inhibition of both MAPK/ERK and PI3K/AKT cascades, culminating in caspase-dependent apoptosis [75]. Similarly, oridonin—a diterpenoid isolated from Rabdosia rubescens—activates the JNK and p38 stress-response pathways, indicating its potential applicability in managing these malignancies [76].

Combined TCM-chemotherapeutic modulation of MAPK signaling in ovarian cancer

Doxorubicin (ADR)

Doxorubicin (ADR), an anthracycline chemotherapeutic agent, exhibits broad-spectrum antineoplastic activity through multiple molecular mechanisms. The compound exerts its cytotoxic effects by intercalating into DNA base pairs, thereby inhibiting topoisomerase II activity and disrupting nucleic acid biosynthesis [77]. Additionally, ADR induces oxidative stress via free radical generation, resulting in significant oxidative damage to both cellular membranes and genomic DNA [78]. Cell cycle analyses demonstrate preferential activity during G2/M and S phase arrest [79], contributing to its clinical utility in malignancies such as ovarian carcinoma [80] and mammary adenocarcinoma. Current therapeutic protocols frequently employ ADR in combination regimens with cyclophosphamide or paclitaxel to enhance therapeutic efficacy [81].

DMY down-regulates the expression of MDR1 mRNA and P-gp through the MAPK/ERK pathway and significantly inhibits the function of P-gp, thereby enhancing the cell toxicity of adriamycin (ADR) [82]. When HSYA is combined with doxorubicin, it can increase Skov3 sensitivities to chemotherapy drugs and diminish cell survival, indicating that the combination of HSYA with chemotherapy treatments has a synergistic impact in the treatment of ovarian cancer [83].

Cisplatin (CDDP, cis-diamminedichloroplatinum(II))

Cisplatin, a platinum-based chemotherapeutic agent, serves as a cornerstone of cytotoxic anticancer therapy. It primarily exerts antitumor effects by interfering with DNA replication and repair through the formation of DNA crosslinks, which impede cell division and ultimately induce apoptosis in cancer cells [84]. Additionally, cisplatin activates the reactive oxygen species (ROS) pathway, contributing to oxidative cellular damage [85]. Clinically, it is extensively utilized in the treatment of solid tumors, including ovarian cancer, testicular carcinoma, and non-small cell lung cancer [86]. To enhance therapeutic efficacy, cisplatin is frequently administered in combination with agents such as paclitaxel and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), with recent trials highlighting synergistic effects in drug-resistant tumors [87].

The combination of 8-NOCHR and cisplatin resulted in upregulation of P38MAPK and P-P38MAPK expression and cell cycle arrest in ovarian cancer cells, ultimately inhibiting proliferation and promoting apoptosis of HO-8910 cells. Compared with high-dose cisplatin alone, the combination of 8-NOCHR and low-dose cisplatin proved effective in the treatment of ovarian cancer, both in terms of reducing the toxic side effects of cisplatin-based chemotherapy and reducing tumor resistance [43]. Recent studies have shown that kaempferol can enhance the inhibitory effect of cisplatin on the growth of OVCAR-3 ovarian cancer cells by promoting apoptosis of ovarian cancer cells through down-regulation of c-myc [88]. Tangeritine can reduce the resistance of human ovarian cancer cells to low-dose cisplatin-induced cell death, thereby overcoming the resistance of human ovarian cancer cells to the growth-inhibitory effects of cisplatin [89]. HSYA can induce apoptosis through the JNK and P38MAPK signaling pathways, enhancing vulnerability of cisplatin-resistant cells. In addition, the study developed for the first time that HSYA’s anti-apoptotic impact is associated with the reverse of chemoresistance in cultivated ovarian cancer cells [90]. Baicalein is primarily extracted from Scutellaria baicalensis Georgi and is one of the flavonoid compounds with the highest content in this plant. Hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α) is abnormally regulated at a high level in tumors and is associated with angiogenesis, metastasis, and drug resistance. Baicalein can reduce the expression of HIF-1α, increase the sensitivity of ovarian cancer cells to cisplatin, and decrease the activation of MAPK/ERK in ovarian cancer cells, indicating that targeting HIF-1α with Baicalein may be an effective cancer treatment strategy [91]. Astragalus Polysaccharides (APS) is a water-soluble heteropolysaccharide extracted from the roots and stems of Astragalus membranaceus, and it is the main active component in Astragalus membranaceus, possessing immunomodulatory and antioxidant functions. APS increased the sensitivity of SKOV3 ovarian cancer cells to cisplatin by decreasing Bcl2 expression, increasing Bax and caspase 3 expression, and activating JNK1/2 [92]. Several studies have shown tanshinone IIA to produce antitumor effects through the P38MAPK signaling pathway. Tanshinone IIA not only down-regulates the anti-apoptotic protein survivin by activating the P38MAPK signaling pathway, thus triggering apoptosis in cisplatin-resistant ovarian cancer cells, but also reduces cisplatin resistance in cisplatin-resistant ovarian cancer cells by lowering cisplatin-resistance of cisplatin by regulating the cisplatin-resistant ERCC1 and LRP genes [93].

Paclitaxel

Paclitaxel represents a widely utilized chemotherapeutic agent. Its primary mechanism involves microtubule stabilization through promoting tubulin polymerization and maintaining structural integrity of microtubules, thereby preventing depolymerization [94]. This action induces cell cycle arrest at G2/M phase and subsequent apoptosis initiation via Bcl-2 family protein modulation [95]. The antiangiogenic properties of paclitaxel manifest through suppression of tumor-associated neovascularization by downregulating VEGF signaling pathways, resulting in indirect inhibition of malignant proliferation [96].

Metabolic processing of paclitaxel occurs predominantly via hepatic cytochrome P450 isoenzymes CYP3A4 and CYP2C8, with recent pharmacogenomic studies identifying specific SNPs affecting metabolic efficiency [97]. Pharmacokinetic interactions require clinical vigilance as concomitant use of CYP3A4 inhibitors may elevate plasma concentrations (potentiating toxicity risks), while CYP3A4 inducers could diminish therapeutic efficacy through accelerated drug clearance [98].

This antineoplastic agent demonstrates critical clinical utility in multiple oncological contexts, including primary and recurrent ovarian carcinoma management [99], metastatic breast cancer therapy [99], and adjuvant treatment protocols for mammary malignancies. Administration strategies may involve sequential delivery with cisplatin to circumvent metabolic interference - specifically avoiding platinum-induced inhibition of paclitaxel clearance that elevates toxicity potential, as demonstrated in phase III clinical trials [100]. Alternative combination regimens include concurrent administration with anthracycline derivatives such as doxorubicin, with recent meta-analyses confirming enhanced progression-free survival rates [101].

TCM active components in OC therapy via MAPK signaling network

Curcumin

Curcumin, a natural polyphenol derived from Curcuma longa, has demonstrated significant therapeutic potential in ovarian cancer by targeting key signaling pathways. Lin revealed that curcumin [103]. suppresses proliferation and induces apoptosis in human ovarian cancer cells (SKOV3 and OVCAR3) through dual inhibition of the MAPK (ERK/JNK) and PI3K/Akt pathways. At an IC50 of 15 µM, curcumin treatment resulted in G2/M phase arrest, elevated Bax/Bcl-2 ratios, and caspase-3 activation, while synergizing with paclitaxel to overcome chemoresistance. However, its clinical utility is limited by poor bioavailability (< 1%) and rapid systemic clearance.

To address these challenges, Yallapu comprehensively reviewed advanced nanomedicine strategies, demonstrating that nanoformulations (e.g., liposomes, PLGA nanoparticles) enhance curcumin’s solubility, tumor targeting, and pharmacokinetics [104]. For instance, liposomal curcumin (50 mg/kg) reduced tumor volume by 60% in murine ovarian cancer models, outperforming free curcumin (30% reduction), and further potentiated MAPK/NF-κB pathway inhibition. These studies collectively highlight a translational roadmap: mechanistic validation of curcumin’s multi-pathway antitumor effects [103] coupled with nanotechnology-driven delivery optimization [104] could bridge the gap between preclinical promise and clinical efficacy.

Baicalein

Baicalein, a flavonoid derived from Scutellaria baicalensis, exhibits dual therapeutic benefits in ovarian cancer by synergistically enhancing chemosensitivity and mitigating oxidative stress through MAPK signaling modulation. Chen demonstrated that baicalein reverses cisplatin resistance in A2780/DDP ovarian cancer cells by suppressing MAPK/ERK pathway activation, specifically inhibiting phosphorylation at ERK1/2 Thr202/Tyr204 sites [105]. This intervention reduced the cisplatin IC50 by 3-fold and promoted Bax-mediated mitochondrial apoptosis, coupled with downregulation of ERCC1 and phospho-ATR-Chk1, key mediators of DNA damage repair. In vivo, baicalein (30 mg/kg) combined with cisplatin achieved 60% tumor volume reduction and suppressed pulmonary metastasis (P < 0.01).

However, oxidative stress-induced mitochondrial dysfunction remains a challenge. Wang elucidated that baicalein activates the ASK1-MAPK/p38 axis to restore redox homeostasis, reducing mitochondrial ROS levels by 45% and elevating glutathione peroxidase (GPx) activity 1.8-fold in ovarian cancer models [106]. Notably, baicalein (25 µM) preserved mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) and attenuated NF-κB-driven pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α). Collectively, these findings propose a dual mechanism: MAPK/ERK inhibition to overcome chemoresistance [105] and MAPK/p38-mediated oxidative stress alleviation [106], positioning baicalein as a promising candidate for targeted combination therapies in ovarian cancer.

Ginsenoside Rg3

Recent studies have demonstrated the multifaceted therapeutic potential of ginsenoside Rg3 in ovarian cancer management through distinct molecular mechanisms. Liu revealed that Rg3 exerts anti-angiogenic effects by specifically targeting cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) within tumor microenvironments [107]. This biological activity was mediated through suppression of the MAPK/AP-1 signaling axis, subsequently downregulating vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) expression and impairing neovascularization in malignant tissues.

Triptolide

Recent pharmacological advances have delineated triptolide’s capacity to orchestrate polypharmacological interventions in ovarian malignancies via spatially distinct molecular engagements. Breakthrough work by Zhou deciphered the compound’s tumor-suppressive paradigm through strategic incapacitation of the TAK1-MAPK/NF-κB signaling axis [108]. Their molecular profiling revealed concomitant suppression of MAPK cascade activation (p-ERK1/2 reduction >60%) and impeded RelA/p65 nuclear localization (3.8-fold decrease), effectively neutralizing pro-tumorigenic transcriptional programs. This coordinate pathway interception precipitated caspase-3-mediated apoptotic commitment (Annexin V + cells: 42.1 ± 3.2%) alongside tumor necrosis factor-α downregulation (58% inhibition vs. controls).

In a paradigm-shifting approach, Liu devised hypoxia-activated triptolide prodrugs exhibiting CSC-directed lethality (IC50 reduction from 48nM to 9nM in ALDH + populations) [109]. Structural modification via β-glucuronidase-cleavable linkers enabled tumor-selective bioactivation (tumor/normal tissue ratio: 6.7:1), circumventing ABC transporter-mediated resistance through P-glycoprotein affinity modulation (Kd shift from 12µM to 0.8µM). These orthogonal therapeutic modalities establish triptolide derivatives as precision-guided agents with dual-strike capabilities against both differentiated tumor masses and chemoresistant CSC niches.

Quercetin

Emerging pharmacological insights reveal quercetin’s dual-axis intervention in ovarian cancer pathobiology through redox-modulated signaling networks. Zhang established that quercetin (50 µM) suppresses metastatic dissemination via coordinated inhibition of the MAPK/STAT3 signaling crosstalk, demonstrating >65% reduction in pulmonary metastases in orthotopic models through TWIST1 downregulation (3.2-fold) and E-cadherin restoration [110]. Mechanistic profiling identified phospho-ERK1/2 (IC50: 18.7 ± 2.3 µM) and STAT3 nuclear translocation (72% inhibition at 24 h) as primary molecular checkpoints.

Complementing these findings, Li delineated quercetin’s pro-apoptotic circuitry mediated through ROS-MAPK/autophagy axis bifurcation [111]. Their time-resolved analysis revealed stage-dependent effects: early-phase (0–6 h) JNK/p38 hyperactivation (2.8-fold ROS surge) preceded late-phase (12–24 h) autophagic flux blockade (LC3-II accumulation: 4.1 ± 0.7 vs. control), ultimately triggering BAX-mediated mitochondrial apoptosis (caspase-3 activation: 58.3% at 48 h). These spatiotemporally distinct mechanisms converge to position quercetin as a redox-adaptive therapeutic capable of simultaneous metastasis suppression and apoptosis induction through differential modulation of MAPK effector systems. Recent advances in phytochemical combination therapy have elucidated curcumin’s capacity to potentiate paclitaxel efficacy through multimodal pathway modulation. Guan demonstrated that curcumin (15 µM) synergistically enhances paclitaxel cytotoxicity (combination index: 0.32 ± 0.05) by dual-pathway inactivation, suppressing MAPK/ERK signaling (p-ERK1/2 reduction: 68.4 ± 3.1%) and NF-κB nuclear translocation (RelA/p65 inhibition: 72% at 24 h) in SKOV3 xenografts [112]. This coordinate blockade reduced tumor volume by 58.7% vs. monotherapy through Bcl-2/Bax ratio inversion (3.2-fold change).

In parallel developments, Sundaram engineered β-cyclodextrin-based nano-curcumin achieving 9.3-fold enhanced bioavailability [113]. Their nanoparticle system demonstrated tumor-selective accumulation (tumor/liver ratio: 4.7:1) while reversing paclitaxel resistance via ABCB1 transporter downregulation (P-gp expression: 22% of controls) and JNK-mediated apoptosis potentiation (caspase-3 activation: 3.8-fold increase vs. free drug). These pharmacokinetically optimized formulations establish curcumin derivatives as multimodal chemosensitizers capable of simultaneously overcoming biological barriers and amplifying paclitaxel’s therapeutic window in ovarian malignancies.

Conclusions and perspectives

This review elucidates the innovative value of spatiotemporal MAPK signaling network remodeling through integrated Chinese-Western medical strategies in ovarian cancer therapy. Mechanistic studies reveal that TCM bioactive components (e.g., baicalin, triptolide) synchronize phosphorylation dynamics of MAPK modules (ERK/JNK/p38) with chemotherapeutic agents, creating chrono-pharmacological synergism. The “molecular clock-metabolic gradient” dual-driver model overcomes compensatory MAPK activation in conventional therapies, elevating drug-resistant clone eradication rates to 67% (vs. 23% with monotherapy).

While this review comprehensively synthesizes the compelling in vitro and in vivo preclinical evidence supporting the synergistic reprogramming of MAPK signaling by integrative TCM-Western medicine strategies, a conspicuous gap exists in the inclusion of human clinical data, including large-scale trials or even small cohort studies. Major barriers include: (1) The polypharmacology and complex composition of herbal extracts pose significant challenges to standardization, pharmacokinetic analysis, and quality control—prerequisites for regulatory-compliant trials; (2) Mechanistic studies in preclinical models remain essential to establish proof-of-concept and elucidate synergistic pathways before clinical translation; (3) Trial design complexities, such as optimizing combination ratios and identifying relevant biomarkers, alongside unclear regulatory pathways, further hinder clinical advancement.

Current challenges include: (1) Undefined spatiotemporal pharmacodynamic interfaces of multi-component TCM synergism, lacking single-cell resolution tracing technologies (e.g., live-cell FRET imaging); (2) Absence of computational models for optimal treatment sequencing, where intermittent combination regimens dominate current studies (81%, n = 45) versus phase-matched sequential strategies (7%); (3) Clinical translation hindered by traditional evaluation systems, with 93% of registered trials (ClinicalTrials.gov) adhering to RECIST criteria without dynamic MAPK activity monitoring. (4) the multi-component and multi-target nature of TCM formulations presents significant challenges for clinical translation.

Future directions should prioritize: ① Decoding MAPK heterogeneity impacts on therapeutic efficacy via single-cell RNA-seq coupled with spatial transcriptomics (Visium platform); ② Engineering smart nanocarriers (pH/ROS-responsive liposomes) for tumor-targeted co-delivery of TCM-chemotherapeutics with controlled release kinetics; ③ Establishing patient-derived organoid-chip platforms integrating “signaling-metabolism-phenotype” triaxial prediction models, empowered by single-cell spatiotemporal omics; ④ Developing liquid biopsy-based dynamic assessment frameworks tracking MAPK pathway mutant allele fractions (MAF) in ctDNA. Through interdisciplinary convergence, integrated Chinese-Western strategies may pioneer a paradigm shift from “single-target inhibition” to “network dynamics modulation” in precision oncology. ⑤ computational modeling and AI-driven platforms are urgently needed to simulate drug interactions, predict patient-specific responses, and optimize combinatory dosing and scheduling. These tools are critical for guiding clinical trial design, supporting regulatory evaluation, and ultimately facilitating the precise integration of TCM with conventional therapeutics in real-world oncology practice.

FADS2 and ALDOC as potential adipose tissue biomarkers in obesity: responses to low-calorie diet-feeding
Authors: Shaojun Chen, Lihua Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundObesity is a global epidemic and is the cause of a profound health crisis worldwide. A low-calorie diet (LCD) can alleviate obesity, but the precise mechanisms involved have yet to be thoroughly elucidated.MethodsLeveraging publicly available transcriptomic data from the adipose tissue of individuals with obesity collected before and after the LCD intervention, we conducted a differential expression analysis, employed machine-learning techniques to identify candidate genes, subsequently using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) values to interpret feature contributions and weighted gene coexpression network analysis (WGCNA) to identify hub genes. We then used the gene expression profile obtained to screen for potential small molecule drug candidates for obesity.ResultsInitially, we identified 296 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) within the GSE95640 dataset, an adipose tissue transcriptomic dataset obtained from individuals with obesity who had undergone an LCD intervention. We next identified eight genes with important properties using two machine-learning algorithms. The SHAP analysis revealed that FADS2 was the most important feature gene, with CCDC162P and ALDOC ranking as the second and third most significant contributors, respectively. Further WGCNA analysis identified FADS2and ALDOC as two mediators of the obesity-ameliorating effects of the LCD intervention. Moreover, cross-validation using a separate dataset (GSE24432), integrated DEG profiling, and machine-learning algorithms confirmed that ALDOC and FADS2 were prospective markers of obesity. L1000CDS2 analysis identified 50 small molecule candidates, 42 of which were unique, which replicated the effects of the LCD on gene expression in obese adipose tissue. Among these, T542500 (hycamptamine hydrochloride monohydrate) ranked top with respect to targeting both ALDOC and FADS2. Moreover, molecular dynamics (MD) analysis revealed that the T542500–ALDOC and T542500–FADS2 complexes exhibited transient stability, remaining bound for 375ns and 385ns, respectively, before they dissociated.ConclusionsALDOC and FADS2 represent biomarkers in obese adipose tissue that are affected by LCD consumption, and T542500 represents a potential small molecule candidate for the treatment of obesity, because it targets both ALDOC and FADS2 and mimics the beneficial effects of an LCD. Owing to the relatively short duration of stable binding, T542500 requires further structural optimization to meet the pharmacodynamic requirements for a therapeutic substance. Nevertheless, given the inherent constraints of computational models, wet-lab experimental validation of the anticipated findings is necessary.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-025-07424-z.

Obesity, according to the latest medical consensus, is defined as a state characterized by an overabundance of adipose tissue [1]. Obesity has largely negative health consequences, because it is associated with multiple disease-causing processes, including inflammatory responses, endocrine dysregulation, disrupted appetite/satiety regulation, and insulin insensitivity, which collectively increase the risk of mortality, contribute to chronic persistence/recidivism despite treatment, and drive the development of comorbidities that impair health [1, 2]. Accordingly, obesity is regarded as a pathological condition [2].

Overweight and obesity together constitute a global epidemic and health crisis, posing immediate and ongoing threats to health worldwide. The prevalence of overweight and obesity has risen globally across all regions and countries between 1990 and 2021, and this trend is expected to be maintained [3]. The global prevalence of obesity increased by 155.1% in males and 104.9% in females between 1990 and 2021, and during this period, the prevalence of overweight and obesity in children and adolescents doubled and that of obesity specifically tripled [3, 4]. In 2021, approximately 1 billion adult men and 1.11 billion adult women with overweight or obesity, and the total number is predicted to increase to approximately 3.80 billion by 2050 [3]. At that time in 2021, 93.1 million children aged 5–14 years and 80.6 million young people aged 15–24 years will also have obesity [4]. Between 2022 and 2050, it is predicted that the global prevalence of overweight will remain steady but that the prevalence of obesity will rise rapidly to 2030 and remain high until 2050 [4]. Obesity is now recognized to be a principal adjustable determinant of global mortality and disability-adjusted life years, making it one of the top preventable health threats causing premature illness and death worldwide [3, 4].

Caloric restriction, as a key dietary modification strategy results from the need to counteract excessive energy intake, servers as the primary approach to obesity management and the cornerstone of weight control [2, 5]. In the present study, we sought to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the effects of an low caloric diet (LCD) on obesity through an omics analysis and to identify compounds with therapeutic potential that mimic its beneficial effects. To this end, we first acquired a relevant transcriptomic dataset from the GEO database (NCBI), and then used an integrated analytical approach including differential expression analysis, machine-learning algorithms, SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) interpretability, and weighted gene coexpression network analysis (WGCNA) analysis to identify critical hub genes. We then conducted computational screening to identify small molecules that mimic the effects of the LCD on gene expression in obesity.

Methods

Raw data retrieval

The GSE95640 dataset used to study the effects of an LCD on obesity was an integral part of the Diet, Obesity, and Genes (DIOGenes) intervention study, a pan-European, multicenter, randomized controlled dietary intervention program (NCT00390637) [6, 7]. Briefly, people with overweight or obesity were enrolled in clinical centers in eight European countries, and were followed for 8 weeks during a weight-loss intervention using a low-calorie diet (3,300 kJ/day) [7], which induced a mean weight loss of 10.3 kg [7]. The study was approved by the local ethics committees, and written informed consent was obtained from all the participants [6, 7].

The GSE95640 dataset met the following criteria: (1) the samples were from humans; (2) the dataset consisted of data collected before and after treatment; (3) each group consisted of ≥ 10 samples; and (4) it consisted of original data that could be used in additional studies.

Identification and analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

DEGs in the GSE95640 dataset were identified using the R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) package “limma” through the online GEO2R platform. The selection criteria were as follows: an adjusted p-value (adj.p) < 0.05 and |log2fc| >0.58, equating to a ≥ 1.5-fold difference in expression. Genes with log2FC > 0.58 were regarded as being upregulated and those with log2FC < − 0.58 as downregulated. DEGs that met both criteria were chosen and manually verified for further evaluation.

Next, the DEGs were subjected to volcano plot visualization, Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment, and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis on a bioinformatics platform (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/) (on May 5, 2025).

Machine-learning-based hub gene identification

In this step, two machine-learning methodologies were used, namely least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and random forest (RF) analysis. Specifically, for LASSO regression, the glmnet package within R was uses, and for the RF method, the randomForest package was used. The genes identified by both LASSO and RF analyses were designated as potential biomarkers of the effects of the LCD intervention on obesity.

SHAP-based interpretability assessment

To address the “black box” challenge inherent in traditional machine learning and enhance model interpretability, we employed SHAP method to analyze Random Forest models trained for obesity intervention through LCD. This approach enabled us to quantify the contributions of key genetic features to obesity-related outcomes. The primary R packages employed in this step were “Tidymodels” (for model development) and “shapviz” (for SHAP-based visualization).

Weighted gene coexpression network analysis

The WGCNA toolkit provides a robust set of tools for the correlation network analysis of extensive, high-dimensional datasets, thereby empowering researchers to construct networks, identify highly correlated gene clusters (modules), and assess the significance of genes and their module affiliation [8]. In the present study, the WGCNA package in the TBtools II software was used to construct a gene coexpression network for the relevant genes in the adipose tissue of the participants with obesity. The methodology involved initial data preprocessing, during which data normalization was performed, followed by filtering, to yield the top 8,000 genes for the construction of a scale-free network. For gene coexpression module detection, a minimum module size of 30 genes and a cuttree height of 0.25 were used. Following this procedure, key modules and genes were selected for further analysis.

After confirmation by cross-checking the results of the LASSO, RF, and WGCNA assessments, the hub genes were regarded as biomarkers of the effects of the LCD on obesity. The relative expression levels and the areas under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves (AUCs) for each of these biomarkers were calculated using the raw data from the GSE95640 dataset.

Validation analysis

GSE24432 is another gene expression dataset of adipose tissue that was created during a study of 40 women with overweight or obesity before and after an LCD intervention [9]. Significant DEGs were identified, then feature genes were identified using machine-learning algorithms. Following this, gene expression and ROC analyses were conducted. The methods used were the same as those described for GSE95640.

Single-gene gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

GSEA was performed to explore the functions of the obesity-related hub genes. Initially, according to the expression levels of individual signature genes, the participants who provided samples for the GSE95640 dataset were divided into low- and high- expressing cohorts. Thereafter, the standardized GSEA4.3.3 software was used to identify biological pathways that were associated with each group.

Infiltration of immune cells

CIBERSORT is a deconvolution algorithm that leverages gene expression data and a predefined signature matrix (LM22) to estimate the relative proportions of 22 distinct immune cell types within heterogeneous tissue samples [10]. We next used the CIBERSORT algorithm to analyze the nature of the immune cell infiltration of the tissues represented by the GSE95640 dataset.

Drug repositioning

The LINCS L1000 library is a comprehensive data archive covering 978 genome-wide markers that predicts the expression patterns of 81% of indirectly measured transcripts, thereby providing an economical and efficient approach for use in drug repositioning studies [11]. To identify compounds that mimic the effects of an LCD on obesity, and therefore have therapeutic potential, the upregulated and downregulated DEGs in GSE95640 were entered into the L1000CDS2 analysis platform (https://maayanlab.cloud/L1000CDS2). The top 50 closest mimics of the effects of the LCD on the gene expression profile of obese adipose tissue were then retrieved.

Molecular docking and MD studies

The compound T542500, also known as hycamptamine hydrochloride hydrate, and the candidate proteins fructose bisphosphate aldolase C (ALDOC) and fatty acid desaturase 2 (FADS2) identified using the above analyses were subjected to molecular docking and MD simulations. The molecular structure of T542500 (PubChem CID: 5515) was retrieved from PubChem. Experimentally obtained protein crystal structures for ALDOC and FADS2 are not available in the RCSB PDB database (search performed on June 27, 2025); therefore, their predicted structures were downloaded from the AlphaFold database using the Uniprot IDs O95864 and P09972.

AutoDock Vina software was used to perform the molecular docking analysis. Additionally, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, each lasting 500 nanoseconds, were performed using the Gromacs 2022 software package. The methods and parameter configurations used for these processes were as previously reported [12]. The free energies of binding for the protein–ligand assemblies were calculated using the molecular mechanics Poisson–Boltzmann surface area (MM/PBSA) approach. Subsequently, the MD trajectories generated were analyzed using the VMD and PyMOL software tools [12].

Results

DEGs identified

The gene expression pattern linked to the effect of an LCD on obesity derived from the GSE95640 dataset is displayed on a volcano plot in Fig. 1A. Using the criteria of |logFC| ≥0.58 and adj. p < 0.05, and after checking data quality manually, a total of 296 DEGs were identified, comprising 90 upregulated and 206 downregulated genes, for further in-depth analysis (Supplementary Table S1).

Functional enrichment analyses were performed to better understand the roles of the DEGs. The top three biological processes were monocarboxylic acid biosynthetic process, fatty acid metabolic process, and carboxylic acid biosynthetic process (Fig. 1B). A Sankey diagram provides a clear illustration of the distribution profile of the identified genes within specific KEGG pathways. The three highest-ranked pathways were fatty acid metabolism, the biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids, and AMPK signaling (Fig. 1C).

Machine-learning analysis

Hub genes were identified from the 296 DEGs using the LASSO and RF algorithms. Using the binominal deviation analysis depicted in Fig. 2A–B, the LASSO model retained 32 hub genes (Supplementary Table S2). The top 20 candidate genes were then ranked using the RF algorithm in descending order, according to their variable importance (Fig. 2C). Eight hub genes, namely LINC00484, glutathione peroxidase 3 (GPX3), ATP binding cassette subfamily B member 5 (ABCB5), fibulin 7 (FBLN7), LINC00632, aldolase, fructose-bisphosphate C (ALDOC), coiled-coil domain containing 162 (CCDC162P), and fatty acid desaturase 2 (FADS2), were identified by both the LASSO and RF approaches (Fig. 2D).

Next, the dynamic tree-cutting tool with a cutting height of 0.25 was used to identify genes with similar expression patterns. For GSE95640, 10 distinct modules were identified, with the pink module showing the closest correlation (0.4, p < 0.001), suggesting a moderately close link to a specific trait and indicating its potential involvement in the effects of LCD on obesity (Fig. 4C). Validation analysis

The GSE24432 dataset was used for a validation analysis. Using the same criteria of |logFC| ≥0.58, adj.P < 0.05, and after a manual check, 38 DEGs (5 upregulated and 33 downregulated) were identified in GSE24432 (Fig. 5A, Supplementary Table S4). The RF algorithm ranked the top 20 candidate genes using variable importance, and this yielded FADS2 and ALDOC as the first- and fourth-ranked genes (Fig. 5B). In addition, as indicated by the binominal deviation plot shown in Fig. 5C, LASSO regression identified six key genes, namely sushi repeat containing protein, X-linked (SRPX), FADS2, angiopoietin like 8 (ANGPTL8), FADS1, matrix remodeling associated 5 (MXRA5), and amyloid beta precursor protein binding family B member 1 interacting protein (APBB1IP).

Immune infiltration evaluation

CIBERSORT was used to determine the nature of the immune infiltration in the adipose tissue samples from individuals with obesity by analyzing data relating to 22 immune cell types. This revealed that the LCD caused a significant increase in “Macrophage M0” infiltration and significant decreases in “Macrophage M1” and “Mast cell resting” infiltration (Fig. 8A). This implies that there was less adipose tissue inflammation in people who had been consuming an LCD. In addition, ALDOC expression exhibited a negative correlation with “Macrophages M0”, but positive correlations with “Mast cells resting,” “Macrophages M1,” and “natural killer (NK) cells resting” (Fig. 8B). Moreover, FADS2 expression demonstrated negative correlations with “Macrophages M0” and “Dendritic cells resting”, but positive correlations with “NK cells resting,” “Monocytes,” “Macrophages M1,” and “Mast cells resting” (Fig. 8B). These results imply that the key genes ALDOC and FDAS2 might be involved in the immunoprotective effects of an LCD through effects on immune cells, and mast cells, and macrophages in particular.

Candidate therapeutic small compounds identified using the gene signatures

We identified 42 unique small molecules using the online L1000CDS2 platform from the top 50 that were predicted to mimic the effects of an LCD on obesity-linked gene expression in adipose tissue in the GSE95640 dataset (Supplementary Table S5). The clustering outcome derived from the L1000CDS2 analysis indicated that, for the provided gene signature, 22 compounds directly affected the expression of FADS2, ALDOC, or both (Fig. 9A). Compound T542500 (Fig. 9B), also known as hycamptamine hydrochloride monohydrate (Pubchem ID: 5515), ranked highest with respect to its ability to target both FADS2 and ALDOC, and was selected for MD simulations.

Results of the MD simulation analysis

The root mean square deviation (RMSD) fluctuated in synchrony with that of ALDOC in the ALDOC–T542500 complex during the 0–370 ns timeframe, indicating that the T542500 maintained its binding pose relative to ALDOC over this period (Fig. 10A). However, the RMSD of the complex structure displayed notable fluctuations between 370 ns and 400 ns (Fig. 10A). On the basis of additional data, including the radius of gyration (Rg) (Fig. 10B), the root mean square fluctuation (RMSF) (Fig. 10C), the distance between the docking site and the molecule centroid (Fig. 10D), the buried solvent accessible surface area (buried SASA) (Fig. 10E), and simulation trajectory snapshots (Fig. 10F), we deduced that these fluctuations were likely to have been caused by a change in the binding site of the small molecule during the simulation, caused by its dissociation from the original binding site.

In addition, amino acids such as Arg134, Gly112, and Lys42 interacted with T542500 through VDW forces (Fig. 11C). Overall, the ALDOC–T542500 complex exhibited dynamic stability over the 0–370 ns period, but T542500 might have dissociated from the complex after 370 ns (Figs. 10 and 11).

Discussion

Given that the most important role of adipose tissue is energy storage, it is not surprising that diet intake affects its size, function, and effects on metabolism [13]. For instance, LCD reliably reduced adipose tissue mass and induced short-term (< 6 months) weight loss [5], another independent 24-week LCD intervention reported moderate weight reduction in obesity [14]. Similarly, an 8-week LCD reduced the body weight and adipose tissue volume, and in particular the volumes of the visceral and subcutaneous fat depots in the lower abdominopelvic region, assessed using magnetic resonance imaging [15]. Furthermore, 20 patients who consumed a very low-calorie ketogenic diet (VLCKD) for 2 months lost more weight and visceral and liver fat than 19 who consumed a standard LCD, indicating that a VLCKD may be beneficial for patients with nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) [16]. However, the molecular mechanisms of these effects require further investigation.

The identification of markers of obesity, including multiomic signatures and sociobehavioral data, is being revolutionized by machine-learning techniques that enable high-throughput integration, predictive modeling, and precision stratification to permit personalized obesity management [17]. However, the effective use of these approaches to identify markers of obesity remains a major challenge [17]. Through an integrated analysis framework that combined two machine-learning algorithms with SHAP for interpretable feature attribution and WGCNA for co-expression network analysis, we have identified two genes, ALDOC and FADS2, as biomarkers of the effects of an LCD on obese adipose tissue (Figs. 2, 3 and 4). We obtained consistent findings using the validation dataset GSE24432 (Fig. 5).

ALDOC, along with ALDOA and ALDOB, belongs to the aldolase family and participates in metabolism, and glycolysis in particular [18]. A study of the consumption of a very LCD for 5 weeks followed by a regular diet for 3 weeks showed that ALDOC, a component of the actin cytoskeleton, facilitates the association between F-actin and GLUT4, sequesters GLUT4 intracellularly, and, together with TUBB5/ANXA2, reduces GLUT4 scaffolding, thereby increasing glucose uptake and reducing plasma glucose, and implying that it might represent a biomarker of the effects of an LCD [19]. ALDOC also plays a crucial role in the regulation of intramuscular and intermuscular fat deposition by activating the AKT–mTOC1 axis, which promotes lipid accumulation [20]. These functions are consistent with our finding that ALDOC may represent a biomarker of the effect of an LCD on obesity (Figs. 2, 3 and 4).

FADS2 is key enzyme in lipid metabolism; it converts saturated to monounsaturated fatty acids, modifies polyunsaturated fatty acid desaturation and the lipid composition of human blood and tissue, affects plasma lipid concentrations, is associated with various health conditions, and is involved in gene–diet interactions [21]. FADS2, in conjunction with SCD1, affects lipid ratios that are inversely related to a gain in adiposity and positively related to height gain between 3 and 12 months, implying that it is a candidate biomarker of both early growth and obesity [22]. FADS2, by mediating the conversion of ALA to EPA/DHA, interacts with the diet to prevent visceral adiposity and adipocyte enlargement, and the FADS2 genotype influences the effect of dietary n-3LCPUFA supplementation that leads to a healthier eWAT phenotype [23]. High-fat diet (HFD) consumption increases the expression of FADS2, along with that of FADS1, a component of the linoleic acid metabolic pathway, thereby contributing to abnormal lipid metabolism and potentially influencing depression-like behavior [24]. Notably, the initial research team behind the GSE95640 study proposed that FADS2 plays a significant role in the altering lipid metabolism in response to LCD, which is at least partially due to the reduction in de novo lipogenesis within the visceral adipose tissue [25]. Taken together with the present findings, ALDOC and FADS2 may be key mediators and biomarkers of the effects of an LCD on obesity.

Obesity has a profound effect on immunity, which is primarily characterized by abnormal inflammatory responses secondary to metabolic stress, alterations to hormones that govern immune cell metabolism and function, disruption to signals from adipose tissue to immune cells, and changes to the metabolism of lipids and related molecules, thereby weakening the body’s defense against disease [26]. In obesity, the polarization of macrophages toward the proinflammatory M1 phenotype, along with T-cell exhaustion and functional alterations to B cells, NK cells, and dendritic cells, drives chronic subclinical inflammation and a systemic immune imbalance, which collectively contribute to metabolic deterioration [27]. The LCD caused a significant increase in macrophage M0 infiltration and a significant decrease in macrophage M1 infiltration (Fig. 8A). Notably, the expression of both ALDOC and FDAS2 negatively correlated with the macrophage M0 signal and positively correlated with the macrophage M1 signal (Fig. 8B).

FADS2 has been shown to be a Δ6-desaturase in RAW 264.7 macrophages, where it introduces n-11 double bonds into odd-chain MUFAs and modifies n-10/n-12 in even-chain MUFAs, thereby increasing fatty acid diversity [28]. FADS2 has been identified as one of the genes that is suppressed in MERTK+/hi M2c macrophages by baicalin, which suggests that it might slow the progression of NAFLD by modulating lipid metabolic pathways [29]. In addition, ALDOC, which is expressed at high levels in gastric cancer (GC), drives macrophage differentiation toward a pro-tumorigenic M1-like phenotype and inhibits anti-tumor M2 polarization, thereby fostering an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and promoting the progression of GC [30]. Thus, LCD affects obesity by influencing the functions of immune cells through ALDOC and/or FDAS2.

Compound T542500, which mimicked the gene expression pattern induced by LCD in obese adipose tissue, was ranked top in terms of its targeting of both ALDOC and FADS2 (Fig. 9). It is the hydrochloride salt monohydrate form of topotecan, a well-established topoisomerase I inhibitor that is approved for the treatment of certain types of cancer [31]. Topotecan increases phospholipid transfer protein gene expression, markedly reduces serum high-density lipoprotein concentration, and increases circulating triglyceride and cholesterol concentrations in treated mice [32]. In a patient with myelodysplasia, topotecan was shown to ameliorate tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α)-induced insulin resistance, a condition that is exacerbated by TNF-α suppression of insulin receptor tyrosine kinase activity through the positive effect of TNF-α on tyrosine kinase-induced phosphorylation of insulin receptor substrate I [33]. Topotecan had a more pronounced therapeutic effect in the low-risk prognosis score group of patients with endometrial cancer and obesity, implying that it may have potential as a chemotherapeutic agent for this particular patient subset [34]. Moreover, topotecan has immunomodulatory effects, and it likely attenuates the development of ulcerative colitis in rats by inhibiting the RORγT transcription factor and downstream mediators in Th-17 cells [35]. Therefore, T542500 may represent a potential therapeutic agent for obesity.

A systematic search of PubMed revealed a lack of experimental studies regarding the effects of T542500 on ALDOC or FASD2 (performed on June 18, 2025). Furthermore, the lack of protein crystal structures for ALDOC or FADS2 significantly limits our understanding of their precise structure. Therefore, MD simulations were performed to characterize the binding of T542500 to ALDOC and FADS2, which revealed that T542500 can remain bound to ALDOC for 370 ns and to FADS2 for 380 ns (Figs. 10, 11, 12 and 13). Figures 10, 11, 12 and 13 provide details of the interactions between T542500 and ALDOC and FADS2 and provide insights into the complex structural mechanisms underlying these interactions. These findings indicate that T542500 may exercise its effects through these two hub proteins. However, the 370-ns and 380-ns residence times of T542500 are far below those needed for drug efficacy. In addition, topotecan, which is derived from camptothecin (an alkaloid of the Chinese tree Camptotheca acuminata), exhibits significant cytotoxicity [36]. It is also associated with the side effect of bone marrow suppression (https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB01030). This suggests that T542500 is unsuitable for treating chronic disease like obesity. Consequently, T542500 can only serve as a lead compound and will require further structural optimization if it is to become therapeutically useful.

Summary

In the present study, we aimed to identify hub genes that are responsible for the effects of an LCD on obesity and novel drugs for the treatment of obesity. Initially, we identified 296 DEGs in the GSE95640 adipose tissue transcriptome dataset derived from individuals with obesity who underwent an LCD intervention, and subsequently used two machine-learning algorithms to identify eight key genes, with SHAP values used to interpret their contributions to the predictive performance. Integration with the results of WGCNA revealed that ALDOC and FADS2 are markers of the beneficial effects of LCD on obesity. Moreover, cross-validation using a separate dataset (GSE24432), integrated DEG profiling, and machine-learning algorithms confirmed that ALDOC and FADS2 are prospective markers of obesity. Following this, L1000CDS2 analysis identified 50 small molecule candidates, including 42 unique molecules, which replicated the effects of LCD on gene expression in the adipose tissue samples from individuals with obesity. Of these, T542500 ranked top in terms of targeting both ALDOC and FADS2. Moreover, MD analysis revealed that T542500–ALDOC and T542500–FADS2 complexes are formed and remain transient stable for short time periods, after which they dissociate. In summary, ALDOC and FADS2 are key biomarkers of the effects of an LCD in obese adipose tissue, and T542500 represents a potential small molecule therapeutic candidate that targets both of these proteins and mimics the benefits of an LCD. Owing to the relatively short duration of stable binding, further structural optimization of T542500 will be required to meet the pharmacodynamic requirements of a potential therapeutic substance. However, due to the fundamental limitations of computational models, experiments verification through wet-lab studies is essential to substantiate the anticipated findings.

Comparative Evaluation of Clinical Anesthetic Efficacy of 4% Articaine with 24-Gauge Needle in Buccal Nerve Block Compared to 2% Lignocaine with 20-Gauge Needle in Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block in Children with Dentoalveolar Abscess
Authors: Jyoti Sharma, Nitin Sharma, Divya Gera, Richa R Nagori, Deepanshu Sharma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAims and backgroundLocal anesthetics play a crucial role in pain management in pediatric dentistry, where anxiety and fear are common among young patients. This study aimed to compare the anesthetic efficacy of 2% lignocaine with a 20-gauge needle in an inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB) and 4% articaine with a 24-gauge needle in a buccal nerve block (BNB) during the extraction of dentoalveolar abscesses in children aged 5–11 years.Materials and methodsA 12-month randomized controlled trial involving 100 healthy children was conducted following ethical standards. Patients were assigned to receive either lignocaine or articaine anesthesia. Pain assessments were conducted using the Wong–Baker Faces Pain Rating Scale (W–BFRS) and visual analog scale (VAS). Postoperative follow-ups were performed to evaluate pain, biting incidents, and numbness duration.ResultsThe study demonstrated a strong positive correlation between W–BFRS and VAS ratings, indicating effective pain assessment. While both techniques showed efficacy, the BNB + local infiltration (LI) technique exhibited lower rates of reanesthesia and discomfort compared to IANB + LI. Follow-up assessments revealed shorter numbness duration and reduced biting incidents with the BNB + LI technique.ConclusionThis study underscores the importance of considering patient comfort and experience when selecting anesthesia techniques in pediatric dentistry. Articaine emerges as a favorable option, offering improved pain management and patient satisfaction compared to lignocaine. Both the IANB + LI and BNB + LI techniques are viable options, with the choice influenced by adverse events and patient preferences. This research contributes valuable insights into optimizing pain management for pediatric dental procedures, particularly in cases of dentoalveolar abscesses.How to cite this articleSharma J, Sharma N, Gera D, et al. Introduction

Local anesthetics are essential for pain management in dentistry, helping to reduce patient anxiety and fear related to dental operations. Research is ongoing to develop safer and more potent local anesthetics, with maxillary infiltration failure rates ranging from 0 to 36% and inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB) failure rates ranging from 44 to 81%.1 Articaine, developed in Germany in 1969, is a widely used local anesthetic in dentistry and has little systemic toxicity due to its rapid degradation into an inactive metabolite. Lignocaine, an injectable and topical version of amide local anesthetic, was first introduced in 1948 and is available in various formulations.2

Neural blocks in dental procedures have several drawbacks, including a higher failure rate of 15% and an increased risk of issues like trismus, bleeding, or paresthesia. Infections in deciduous teeth can result from untreated carious lesions, leading to severe inflammation or pulpal necrosis and a subsequent tooth abscess.3 Dental trauma can also cause tooth abscesses, which can either stay localized or spread and cause diffuse cellulitis, potentially leading to fatal systemic consequences.

Anxiety is a common problem faced by pediatric dentists, as children often reject dental care due to fear of pain and trauma.4 Pain reaction ratings and direct observation reports from the child or accompanying parent are two main techniques for assessing dental fear and pain management problems during local anesthesia administration. This randomized, single-blind study aims to determine and compare the anesthetic effects of 2% lignocaine with a 20-gauge needle in IANB and 4% articaine with a 24-gauge needle in buccal nerve block (BNB) in the extraction of dentoalveolar abscesses using a pain rating scale.

Methodology

A 12-month randomized controlled trial was carried out at Rajasthan Dental College and Hospital, which involved 100 healthy children, following ethical standards from the Helsinki Declaration of 1964 and the International Conference on Harmonization. The study involved oral examinations, patient groups, and radiographic examinations. Patients were randomly assigned to groups based on anesthetic agents, with 2% lignocaine and 4% articaine. The study involved ethical committee approval, thorough history, clinical examination, and patient diagnosis. An anesthetic solution for nerve block was prepared, and pain and behavior assessments were conducted. Postoperative instructions were given, and follow-up calls were taken after 24 hours.

Operative Procedure

Group A: 2% Lignocaine with a 20-Gauge Needle

The patient was draped and anesthetic solutions were prepared for the IANB and local infiltration (LI). Marks were placed for needle penetration and injection. The nerve block was successfully completed, and pain was continuously monitored throughout the treatment. Postoperative instructions were provided, and a follow-up phone call was conducted after 24 hours to ensure patient well-being.

Group B: 4% Articaine with a 24-Gauge Needle

The patient was draped and anesthetic solutions were prepared for a BNB and LI. Landmarks were used to guide needle penetration and injection. The nerve block was successfully completed, and pain was continuously monitored throughout the treatment. Postoperative instructions were provided, and a follow-up phone call was conducted after 24 hours to ensure patient well-being.

Evaluation Intervals

Pain was measured during the injection of anesthesia and pain during the extraction procedure was evaluated.

Postoperative Follow-up

The patient was evaluated 24 hours postoperation to record pain using the Wong–Baker scale and VAS. Parents were asked about their child's pain, bites, numbness, and duration of numbness. Data were collected from parents.

Results

According to Table 1, this study examined the correlation between pain assessments using the W–BFRS and the VAS during dental anesthetic procedures. Results showed a strong positive linear association between W–BFRS and VAS ratings for IANB + LI procedures with lignocaine and a significant positive correlation with VAS ratings for IANB + LI procedures with articaine. The correlation coefficients were also significant for extraction procedures in both IANB + LI and BNB with LI. These findings suggest that both scales effectively capture patients' pain experiences, enabling dental practitioners to accurately assess and manage pain during procedures. This supports the use of both W–BFRS and VAS as reliable tools for pain assessment in clinical practice, enhancing patient care and treatment outcomes.

Follow-up

Based on Table 2, the study evaluated the effectiveness of two dental anesthesia techniques, IANB + LI and BNB + LI, in pediatric patients. Results showed that 68% of patients experienced moderate pain after the procedure, with 76% reporting incidents of biting their lips or tongue. However, none of the patients reported lingering numbness, suggesting quick sensory recovery. Interestingly, 92% experienced numbness lasting >30 minutes, indicating a prolonged sensory effect. In contrast, 30% of patients reported discomfort, with an average pain score of 0.30. Biting incidents were less frequent, and numbness duration was shorter. The BNB + LI technique may be a preferred method for pediatric dental procedures due to its lower pain incidence, reduced biting incidents, and shorter duration of numbness. Further research is needed to validate these findings and explore additional factors influencing anesthesia outcomes in pediatric dental care.

Discussion

Local anesthetics work by binding to specific receptors within the sodium channel, resulting in decreased or eliminated sodium permeability.5 They are particularly effective in blocking high-frequency nerve impulses due to their ability to reach their site of action during a channel's inactive state following depolarization.6 Articaine, a 4% local anesthetic solution, is unique due to its thiophene moiety and biotransformation in the liver and plasma. It is used in manufacturing and has higher doses due to its equal analgesic effectiveness and decreased systemic toxicity.7 Lignocaine, a 2% solution, is a common local anesthetic used to temporarily cause numbness or lack of feeling in a particular part of the body. It acts by blocking nerve signals in the body, preventing the transmission of pain sensations to the brain.8A dental appointment can be uncomfortable and stressful, especially for children, who often display behavioral signs of distress. Pain assessment parameters include self-reporting, observation, and behavioral measurement. Depending on the child's developmental stage, it is crucial to select a suitable scale. Projective scales have been developed to assess fear and anxiety in children, such as W–BFRS for pain assessment and the VAS for fear and anxiety. The Frankl Behavior Rating Scale is also used for behavior assessment in pediatric dentistry research and clinical practice. Wong and Baker found that children aged 3–18 years prefer the faces scale over other pain assessment scales, but no single scale demonstrated superiority in validity or reliability. The W–BFRS was found to be more sensitive than the VAS in this investigation. The VAS is the most widely used pain assessment scale in acute pain research, but its use in children has inherent difficulties due to their cognitive abilities.9

Conversely, VAS use in children has inherent difficulties. According to Pinkham et al., children do not have the same cognitive abilities as adults to quantify and qualify abstract phenomena, and younger children may not cognitively be able to use self-report measures of pain, such as the VAS. This study aimed to investigate how well children accepted their dental treatment under local anesthetic, how satisfied parents were with the procedure, and how it affected their child. The aim was to analyze the dental injection experience and compare the two approaches (IANB and BNB) to determine whether one causes less discomfort or is more well-liked by the children. The study compared the anesthetic effectiveness of lignocaine (IANB + LI) and articaine (BNB + LI) during extraction using W–BFRS. The notion of computerized devices was presented by Vitale et al., who emphasized the benefit of regulating injection speed, which may affect pain perception.10 Studies have shown that 4% articaine is better for adult buccal infiltration (BI) than 2% lidocaine, and Katyal clarified that articaine works better as an anesthetic than lignocaine for common dental treatments.1 The effectiveness of single BI of articaine is equal to buccal and palatal infiltration of lignocaine, according to Kumar et al. and Monteiro et al. compared the anesthetic effectiveness of BI with 1.8 mL of 4% articaine and IANBs with 1.8 mL of 2% lignocaine in patients with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis.11 Ahmad et al. found that 4% articaine can be administered efficiently to provide deep anesthesia in individuals with irreversible pulpitis.12 A significant difference was observed in mean values and SD of W–BFRS during injection of lignocaine (IANB + LI) and articaine (BNB + LI), with articaine showing the most significant values. Research by Bahrololoomi and Rezaei examined the anesthetic effectiveness of a single buccal injection with 4% articaine compared to a standard IANB with 2% lidocaine during bilateral extraction of a mandibular primary molar.13 In conclusion, the study highlights the importance of considering other anesthetic strategies before treatment procedures to maximize success rates and minimize pain.

This study focuses on the use of articaine in dental procedures compared to lignocaine. Articaine is found to have a quicker onset, longer duration of anesthesia, and shorter discomfort time compared to lignocaine. It also requires less reanesthesia, as it is more effective. Previous studies have also found that articaine has superior anesthetic properties compared to lignocaine. Jain and John found that articaine had a longer duration of anesthesia and less pain compared to lignocaine.14 Maruthingal et al. found that articaine significantly outperformed lidocaine in producing pulpal anesthesia.15 Aakanksha et al. found that articaine produced less bleeding, early onset, slightly longer duration of action, and comfort for patients.16 Ghosh et al. found that 4% articaine with 1:1,00,000 adrenaline is a better anesthetic solution, with less bleeding, early onset, slightly longer duration of action, and better patient comfort.17 Kulkarni and Parkar found that articaine produced superior outcomes than lignocaine in LI anesthesia for anterior and premolar extractions.18 Overall, the findings support the use of articaine in dental procedures.

Conclusion

The study examined the use of local anesthetics, specifically articaine and lignocaine, in pediatric dentistry for tooth extractions. It discussed the use of pain assessment tools, with a preference for the W–BFRS and VAS for assessing children's pain and anxiety during dental procedures. The study supported the growing trend favoring articaine over lignocaine in pediatric dental anesthesia, citing faster onset, longer duration, and better patient experience as key factors. The study contributed valuable insights into the comparative efficacy of anesthetic techniques in pediatric dental extractions, with the positive behavioral response associated with both the IANB + LI and BNB + LI techniques indicating their viability in managing pain and improving the overall experience for pediatric patients. The choice between the two techniques may be influenced by adverse events and patient preferences. The study concludes the importance of considering multiple factors, including patient comfort and experience, when selecting anesthetic techniques for pediatric dental procedures. The focus on comparing BNB and IANB techniques in the context of dentoalveolar abscess sets it apart, offering comprehensive insights into optimizing pain management for this specific patient population.

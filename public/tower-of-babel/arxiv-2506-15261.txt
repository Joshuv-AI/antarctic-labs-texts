Quantum-corrected black hole thermodynamics
from the gravitational path integral

Abstract




Exploring quantum effects from black hole thermodynamics has always been a pivotal topic.
In recent years, the free energy landscape and ensemble-averaged theory based on the Euclidean path integral approach have provided further understanding of the statistical aspects of the black hole system.
We investigate the quantum-corrected thermodynamics of the Reissner-Nordström-AdS black hole by including off-shell geometries in the path integral.
We obtain a one-loop effective action by considering the subleading-order terms in the ensemble-averaged theory, and verify that the effective thermodynamic quantities consistent with the effective action define a valid thermodynamics.
Furthermore, the phase diagram was modified by the off-shell effects, resulting in a more abundant phase structure.
We discover that the traditional black hole thermodynamics can be recovered in the semi-classical limit. The region of first-order phase transitions shrinks and zero-order phase transitions emerge when off-shell effects are included.
The results provide new perspectives for understanding the quantum corrections in black hole thermodynamics.

It is natural to expect that when off-shell geometries with conical singularity are included in the path integral, the ensemble-averaged quantities are corrected by the off-shell effects. The corrected quantities should recover the black hole thermodynamics result in the semi-classical limit.
Moreover, the ensemble-averaged quantities should define a valid thermodynamics, and we hope they can provide more insights into the phase structure.
In this paper, our main focus is on the ensemble-averaged thermodynamics.
The questions we want to answer are whether quantum-corrected thermodynamics gives rise to a well-defined thermodynamic description, and how the off-shell geometries affect the phase structure of the RN-AdS black hole.

By studying the ensemble-averaged thermodynamics in more detail, we have gained further understanding of black hole thermodynamics.
Evaluating the ensemble-averaged theory up to the subleading-order correction of

The physical quantities derived from the ensemble-averaged theory should be regarded as quantum-corrected quantities, and we show that those quantities define a valid thermodynamic system.
The phase diagram of the RN-AdS black hole is corrected by the off-shell effects, and we have a richer phase structure, including a zero-order phase transition in the corrected thermodynamics.
More specifically, in the case of a first-order phase transition between small and large black holes, this transition tends to happen at a lower ensemble temperature for larger dimensionless Newton’s constant
G_{\text{N}}

The ensemble-averaged theory

In this section, we review recent developments on deriving black hole thermodynamics from an ensemble-averaged theory inherited from the gravitational Euclidean path integral

By including more Euclidean geometries with conical singularity in the phase space, we can calculate the density matrix and probability distribution of the geometric configurations via the Euclidean path integral.
This means we are including more off-shell geometries in the gravitational path integral, and it turns out that the semi-classical approximation of the theory naturally gives rise to the black hole thermodynamics of the RN-AdS black hole.

3
Quantum corrected black hole thermodynamics

For the case when the system size is larger than the Planck length but the Planckian physical effects can not be ignored, we can use a Gaussian distribution to approximate the probability distribution shown in Fig.
2a
.
In this section, we employ the probability distribution to clarify the implications of off-shell geometries for black hole thermodynamics.

First of all, let us consider the ensemble with given temperature
T_{\text{E}}
, charge
Q
, and pressure
P
more carefully.
The partition function should be written as an integral over the energy of each state
E
as

Z=\int e^{-\beta E}D(E)\text{d}E\,,

(8)

with density of state
D(E)
.
Note that
D(E)
denotes the number of quantum states per unit energy interval with energy
E
, which is the exponential of the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy.
It can be further written as

Z=\int e^{-\beta E+S(E)}\text{d}E=\int e^{-I_{\text{E}}(E)}\text{d}E\,.

(9)

Thus, in Eq. (
7
), the parameter that is used to label different states and thus needs to be averaged over should be the energy (mass) of the spacetime
E
.
E(r_{\text{h}})
denotes the energy of the spacetime with horizon radius
r_{\text{h}}
. Although a conical singularity in their Euclidean counterpart presents, the bare energy
E(r_{\text{h}})
can be expressed as

E(r_{\text{h}})=\frac{r_{\text{h}}}{2G_{\text{N}}}\left(1+\frac{r_{\text{h}}^{2}}{L^{2}}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r_{\text{h}}^{2}}\right)\,.

(10)

As shown in Eq. (
7
), all the physical quantities should be ensemble averaged with respect to the probability distribution shown in Fig.
2
.
For relatively small
G_{\text{N}}
, using the Gaussian distribution to approximate the distribution, means we can expand everything to the second order of
\delta=r_{\text{h}}-r_{\text{H}}
.

r_{\text{H}}
is the on-shell horizon radius, and for the case shown in Fig.
2a
we have
r_{\text{H}}=0.126
and
r_{\text{H}}=0.917
.
The most direct quantity to be averaged over is the energy, and we have

\displaystyle\bar{E}=\left<{E}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z}\int e^{-I_{\text{E}}}(\partial_{\beta}I_{\text{E}})~(\partial_{r_{\text{h}}}E)~\text{d}r_{\text{h}}

(11)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\partial I_{\text{E}}}{\partial\beta}+\frac{1}{2I_{\text{E}}^{\prime\prime}}\cdot\frac{\partial I_{\text{E}}^{\prime\prime}}{\partial\beta}+\mathcal{O}(G_{\text{N}}^{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\beta}\left[I_{\text{E}}+\frac{1}{2}\ln I_{\text{E}}^{\prime\prime}\right]+\mathcal{O}(G_{\text{N}}^{1})\,,

where the prime represents derivative with respect to
r_{\text{h}}
.
In the second line, we have used the Gaussian distribution to approximate the probability distribution. Dropping the subsubleading-order terms, we can further rewrite the averaged energy as

\displaystyle\bar{E}=\frac{r_{\text{H}}}{2G_{\text{N}}}\left(1+\frac{r_{\text{H}}^{2}}{L^{2}}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r_{\text{H}}^{2}}\right)+\frac{L^{2}Q^{2}+3r_{\text{H}}^{4}}{2L^{2}\left(\beta Q^{2}-2\pi r_{\text{H}}^{3}\right)+6\beta r_{\text{H}}^{4}}

(12)

It can be easily seen from Eq. (
12
), the leading order contribution that is proportional to
1/G_{\text{N}}
is exactly the semi-classical result of the black hole thermodynamics.
The subleading correction proportional to
G_{\text{N}}^{0}
may play an important role for relatively large
G_{\text{N}}
.
From Eq. (
11
), we get a one-loop effective action

\Gamma_{\text{eff}}=I_{\text{E}}+\frac{1}{2}\ln I_{\text{E}}^{\prime\prime}\,,

(13)

and the partition function can be approximated as

Z\approx e^{-\Gamma_{\text{eff}}}\,,

(14)

in one-loop approximation.

Note that the tree-level scattering amplitude is proportional to vertices, and subleading-order terms of
G_{\text{N}}
capture the loop corrections in the possible quantum theory of gravity.
So the finite
G_{\text{N}}
effects in the Euclidean path integral that we are trying to capture can be regarded as loop corrections to the black hole physics.
We analytically study the one-loop correction to the black hole thermodynamics here; higher-loop corrections need further studies.
The situation when subleading-order of
G_{\text{N}}
corrections to black hole thermodynamics are considered should be regarded as “quantum (or loop) corrected black hole thermodynamics”.

It can be shown that the quantity
\bar{\Phi}
conjugating to the given charge
Q
and
\bar{V}
conjugating to the given pressure
P
, can be derived with respect to the given Gaussian distribution.
We have

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}=\left<{\Phi}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z}\int e^{-I_{\text{E}}}\left(\frac{\partial_{Q}I_{\text{E}}}{\beta}\right)~\left(\partial_{r_{\text{h}}}E\right)~\text{d}r_{\text{h}}

(15)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{Q}\left[I_{\text{E}}+\frac{1}{2}\ln I_{\text{E}}^{\prime\prime}\right]+\mathcal{O}(G_{\text{N}}^{1})\,,

and

\displaystyle\bar{V}=\left<{V}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z}\int e^{-I_{\text{E}}}\left(\frac{\partial_{P}I_{\text{E}}}{\beta}\right)~\left(\partial_{r_{\text{h}}}E\right)~\text{d}r_{\text{h}}

(16)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{P}\left[I_{\text{E}}+\frac{1}{2}\ln I_{\text{E}}^{\prime\prime}\right]+\mathcal{O}(G_{\text{N}}^{1})\,.

The above
\bar{\Phi}
and
\bar{V}
exactly match the quantities being derived from the one-loop effective action, i.e.

\bar{\Phi}=\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{Q}\Gamma_{\text{eff}}\,,~~~~~~~~\bar{V}=\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{P}\Gamma_{\text{eff}}\,.

(17)

It is interesting to notice that the averaged free energy should be reinterpreted as the potential conjugate to Newton’s constant

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}=\left<{F}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z}\int e^{-I_{\text{E}}}\left(\frac{I_{\text{E}}}{\beta}\right)~\left(\partial_{r_{\text{h}}}E\right)~\text{d}r_{\text{h}}

(18)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{G_{\text{N}}}{\beta}\partial_{G_{\text{N}}}\left[I_{\text{E}}+\frac{1}{2}\ln I_{\text{E}}^{\prime\prime}\right]+\mathcal{O}(G_{\text{N}}^{1})\,.

This means that

\frac{\bar{\mu}}{G_{\text{N}}}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_{G_{\text{N}}}\Gamma_{\text{eff}}\,.

(19)

Note that
G_{\text{N}}
is the dimensionless Newton’s constant, so
1/G_{\text{N}}
here can be regarded as the center charge
c\propto N^{2}
in the dual boundary theory. Our interpretation here should match the black hole thermodynamics with varying center charge
[

,

,

.

The one-loop effective entropy can be derived from the effective action as

S_{\text{eff}}=-(1-\beta\partial_{\beta})\ln Z=-(1-\beta\partial_{\beta})\Gamma_{\text{eff}}=\frac{\pi r^{2}_{\text{H}}}{G_{\text{N}}}-(1-\beta\partial_{\beta})\left(\frac{1}{2}\ln I_{\text{E}}^{\prime\prime}\right)\,.

(20)

The leading-order contribution is exactly the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, and we have a logarithmic correction as the subleading-order contribution.
Define the one-loop effective free energy
F_{\text{eff}}=\Gamma_{\text{eff}}/\beta
, the first law can be written as

\displaystyle\text{d}F_{\text{eff}}=S_{\text{eff}}~\text{d}T_{\text{E}}+\bar{\Phi}\text{d}Q+\bar{V}\text{d}P-\bar{\mu}~\text{d}\ln G_{\text{N}}\,.

(21)

It is easy to show that
\bar{E}
can be related to
F_{\text{eff}}
through a Legendre transformation
F_{\text{eff}}=\bar{E}-T_{\text{E}}S_{\text{eff}}
, so we also have

\displaystyle\text{d}\bar{E}=T_{\text{E}}~\text{d}S_{\text{eff}}+\bar{\Phi}\text{d}Q+\bar{V}\text{d}P-\bar{\mu}~\text{d}\ln G_{\text{N}}\,.

(22)

The first law of black hole thermodynamics is the essential relation. All the other thermodynamic laws can be verified without much effort. With well-defined thermodynamic laws, we can say that we have valid loop-corrected black hole thermodynamics.

Note that we have gained logarithmic corrections in both the effective entropy and the one-loop effective action.
logarithmic corrections are generally expected for the subleading-order quantum correction, for example

Taking the“Brick wall” model

5
Conclusion and discussion

In this paper, we have studied the quantum-corrected black hole thermodynamics due to the off-shell geometries in the gravitational path integral.
The density matrix and probability distribution of the geometries, both on-shell and off-shell, can be obtained by the Euclidean path integral method.
The probability distribution of the off-shell geometries can be non-zero for finite
G_{\text{N}}
, as shown in Fig.
2
.
Using the density matrix and the probability distribution, all the physical quantities should be defined as ensemble-averaged ones.
Moreover, in the semi-classical limit
G_{\text{N}}\to 0
, the ensemble-averaged quantities recover the quantities we usually see in the black hole thermodynamics.

Based on the ensemble-averaged theory, we can obtain the corrected quantities by working out the corresponding integral.
For relatively small
G_{\text{N}}
, expanding the integral around the on-shell configuration is a good approximation.
This gives us the one-loop corrected quantities.
It can be easily checked that the one-loop corrected quantities satisfy the four thermodynamic laws; thus, we have a well-defined thermodynamics.
It is worth emphasizing that the one-loop effective action consistent with all the corrected quantities is associated with a logarithmic correction.
Correspondingly, there is a logarithm term in the effective entropy and the effective free energy.
The logarithm term originates from the off-shell geometries in the gravitational path integral, rather than quantum matter.

The phase transition behavior is thoroughly analyzed in the corrected thermodynamics.
The phase diagrams are obtained for different
G_{\text{N}}
values characterizing the amount of off-shell geometries included.
In the semi-classical limit, all the phase transition behavior of RN-AdS black holes can be recovered.
However, with larger values of
G_{\text{N}}
, the influence of off-shell geometries on the coexistence curve is pretty obvious as shown in Fig.
5
.
The region where we have first-order phase transitions becomes smaller for larger
G_{\text{N}}
, and the transitions tend to happen at lower temperatures.
There is an extra region where we have zero-order phase transitions for the RN-AdS black holes.
The critical point where the second-order phase transition happens is not modified.
And the region between the first-order and second-order transition, as shown by the darkest color in Fig.
6a
and
6b
, is where the zero-order phase transition happens.
As expected, the deviation from the transitional black hole thermodynamics and the zero-order phase transitions region increases for larger
G_{\text{N}}
.
The zero-order phase transitions are explicitly demonstrated through the behavior of the effective free energy in Fig.
6
.
Zero-order transition is rare in traditional black hole thermodynamics, but we believe this phenomenon should be common when logarithmic corrections are included, although always been ignored in the literature.

It is worth mentioning that, at first glance, the critical behaviour illustrated by the red curve in Fig.
3b
is somewhat strange.
The critical behaviour is different than what we commonly see in standard black hole thermodynamics.
Interesting new physics might be hidden in the second-order phase transition, and one may find a microscopic description of the critical behaviour.
We would like to leave this phenomenon for further studies.

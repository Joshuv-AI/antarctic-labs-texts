Social media’s impact on environmental awareness: a marginal treatment effect analysis of WeChat usage in China
Authors: Peixi Xie, Yuan Zhang, Renxing Chen, Ziang Lin, Nan Lu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractEnvironmental awareness serves as an intrinsic motivator for individuals’ engagement in environmental protection behaviors, and social media platforms can foster such awareness. Drawing on the 2018 China General Social Survey data (CGSS 2018), this study employs non-parametric and semi-parametric approaches within the framework of marginal treatment effect (MTE) to examine the influence of WeChat usage on the environmental awareness of Chinese residents. The findings unveil significant heterogeneity in the impact of frequent WeChat usage on individuals’ levels of environmental awareness. Through estimation of the average treatment effect, treatment group average treatment effect, and non-treatment group average treatment effect, the study reveals that WeChat exerts a stronger positive influence on the environmental awareness of residents with a high propensity for using the platform, while it has no discernible effect on the environmental awareness of residents with a low inclination to engage with WeChat. The growth of environmental knowledge facilitated by WeChat plays a crucial role in shaping environmental awareness. Furthermore, this research underscores the influence of real-life social network structures on information exchange within the WeChat platform in China. This observation suggests that WeChat transcends its role as a mere information transmission tool and becomes intricately woven into the social network fabric of its users, thus mirroring certain characteristics of information dissemination within offline social networks. These insights imply that targeted strategies can be devised to bolster residents’ environmental awareness by capitalizing on the interconnectedness of real-life social networks and leveraging the information exchange potential of WeChat. By doing so, it is possible to enhance environmental protection endeavors and encourage more sustainable behaviors among individuals.Keywords: Social media, Environmental awareness, WeChat, Social network, Information dissemination, Heterogeneous treatment effect

Introduction

The human activity is primarily responsible for the causes of global environmental problems [1, 2], such as the greenhouse effect, water scarcity, air pollution, and extinction of species [3, 4]. Over the past few decades, China has experienced rapid economic growth with an average annual growth rate of over 8%, making it the second-largest economy in the world [5]. The issue of pollution in China is closely intertwined with its economic and social development [6], the degradation of the environment has been identified as one of the most pressing and challenging issues of contemporary Chinese society [7, 8]. Individuals and society often have to strike a balance between economic growth and environmental protection, but this doesn’t necessarily mean that economic development is inherently harmful to the environment [9]. Environmental degradation is fundamentally a moral issue that necessitates joint efforts from both enterprises and individuals to foster sustainable development. In light of this, Farooq Q et al. introduced the concepts of social and environmental collective protection (SECP) and social and environmental collective development (SECD), which serve as critical frameworks for analyzing how enterprises can meet their social and environmental responsibilities [10]. Social media plays a pivotal role in raising environmental awareness among businesses and individuals, and this paper focuses on exploring this aspect.

The use of social media is essential for fostering environmental awareness. Environmental awareness is a key factor in achieving sustainable social and economic development at both local and global levels, and its growth is shaped by modern mass communication [11].According to a review of social media research, little is known about how social media affects the perception and behavior of consumers in relation to sustainability [12]. Different social media platforms cater to various needs, attracting diverse types of users [13]. It is difficult to evaluate the knowledge effects of social media due to the diverse and complex information environment, media logic, and dissemination characteristics of each social media platform. Additionally, many studies have focused on an overall analysis of social media or specific platforms, ignoring the differences between platforms [14–16]. Research has also begun to focus on how different social media platforms affect environmental awareness in distinct ways. Gómez-Casillas and Gómez discovered that in Latin America, YouTube has the strongest and most significant positive impact on users’ awareness of climate change, followed by Instagram, Twitter, and WhatsApp. In contrast, Facebook and LinkedIn have little to no effect on awareness, and some other platforms even have a negative influence on users’ understanding of climate change [17].

WeChat, a social media platform developed by a Chinese private enterprise, has attracted 1.37 billion monthly active users in China [18]. Beyond its core social media features—such as text and voice messaging, image and video sharing, location sharing, and gaming—WeChat is expanding its innovative tools to integrate with traditional industries, drive the growth of the digital economy, and further strengthen its user base [19]. In mainland China, mobile social networking services like WeChat distinguish themselves from other internet products and services due to their distinct public relations model and social interaction dynamics. WeChat is known for its privacy features, with communication primarily occurring within “Moments” and public information accounts [20]. Most of a user’s contacts on WeChat are family members, friends, or acquaintances from their offline life [21]. As a result, WeChat emphasizes communication within small groups and close circles, reducing the reliance on other social media platforms and limiting opportunities for citizens to share opinions publicly on a broader scale [22]. This shift has accelerated the rise of individualism, the decline and transformation of communities, and has redefined the concept of public space among Chinese users [23].

To gain a deeper understanding of WeChat, we compare it with Twitter. From a communication logic perspective, WeChat and Twitter are two distinct social media platforms. WeChat is a social tool that primarily relies on users’ social relationships and autonomous selection for information filtering, while Twitter is relatively more dependent on algorithm recommendations. WeChat is a familiar social network derived from instant messaging tools, while Twitter is a stranger social network established under the ecology of celebrities, internet celebrities, and media content. WeChat users primarily use the platform to interact with friends and share life content, while Twitter users primarily use the platform to stay up-to-date on news and follow and obtain content of interest [22].

Due to these unique features, WeChat challenges existing research on the role of social media in addressing environmental issues. Research specifically examining the impact of WeChat on residents’ environmental awareness within the Chinese context remains limited. Given WeChat’s widespread use and profound influence in China, it is essential to explore the relationship between its usage and environmental awareness among Chinese citizens. This study utilizes data from the 2018 China General Social Survey and uses counterfactual causal inference along with the marginal treatment effect (MTE) [24, 25]. An assessment of the impact of WeChat use on individual environmental awareness is being conducted, and the heterogeneity of this effect is also being explored.

Literature review

In recent decades, the rapid expansion of media outlets—including cable television, the internet, and social media—has significantly increased public access to environmental information. This shift has profoundly influenced various aspects of social life, including production activities, daily routines, social interactions, and cognitive processes, ultimately impacting individuals’ survival and development [26]. Notably, the internet has become the primary source of news and information on environmental issues, such as climate change and air pollution, surpassing traditional media [27].

Social media, characterized by its high interactivity and engagement, has a more direct impact on users’ environmental awareness. It has emerged as a crucial platform for a diverse range of stakeholders—including organizations, climate activists, influencers, scientists, and policymakers—to raise global awareness of climate change and encourage pro-environmental behavior [28]. Yunhong Hao et al. have identified that social media influences corporate employees’ sense of social responsibility, and they have developed foundational theories and analytical frameworks to understand this impact [29]. Researchers have utilized social media platforms, such as Weibo, as key data sources for studying environmental awareness, discovering that Chinese citizens are increasingly attentive to climate change on social media and generally exhibit a positive attitude [30]. A study involving staff at higher education institutions found that social media use enhances users’ environmental knowledge and awareness [31].

Social media also plays a significant role in promoting pro-environmental behaviors, often surpassing traditional media in this regard [32]. User-generated content that links pro-environmental knowledge and awareness is particularly effective in fostering online communities centered on environmental issues and in boosting participation in related activities [33]. It also helps motivate residents to adopt eco-friendly technologies; for instance, research in Pakistan showed that social media significantly influenced the relationship between personal norms and the intention to adopt biogas technology [34]. WeChat users have the ability to transcend geographic limitations and spread alternative information, which increases public environmental awareness, shapes public opinion, and mobilizes action for environmental causes [35]. The widespread use of social media has also bolstered environmental governance efforts; studies using Weibo have demonstrated that the public pressure expressed on these platforms has become a significant driver of environmental policy and action [36].

The use of social media can significantly promote green consumption. By engaging with social media, consumers are more likely to develop a preference for green products [37] and adopt environmentally friendly consumption habits [38]. This influence extends to young people, where social media plays a role in shaping their green consumption behaviors and strengthening their commitment to sustainability [39]. Participation in environmental activities on social media boosts users’ environmental awareness, which, in turn, positively impacts their green consumption choices [40], such as purchasing green food [41] and green cosmetics [42]. Furthermore, media coverage of environmental issues can sway consumer decisions; for instance, reports on climate change or global warming have been linked to increased sales of hybrid vehicles [43]. Researchers have also categorized social media usage patterns, noting that active engagement with social networking sites is positively associated with green consumption through enhanced public self-awareness [44].

These studies provide a foundation for examining the relationship between social media use and environmental awareness. However, most discussions focus on average effects, often neglecting the diverse impacts that social media use may have on users’ environmental awareness. To address this gap, and in accordance with the principle of heterogeneity [45], this paper explores how WeChat use specifically influences environmental awareness from a heterogeneity perspective.

The pro-environmental consciousness and green consumption patterns of social media users are not solely influenced by the platforms themselves; users also actively contribute to the content on these platforms. For example, research has shown that information sharing on social media positively influences the green consumption behavior of China’s younger generation [46]. Users also reinterpret environmental knowledge through personal narratives; a study on Swedish-language blogs found that personal green blogs transform complex concepts of sustainability into relatable everyday practices, gaining legitimacy and trust through these daily life stories, which in turn shapes environmental communication [47].

The mechanisms through which social media fosters environmental awareness and green consumption are an important area of study. Marketing by businesses on social media [48], along with sustainable communication and the dissemination of environmental information, has been shown to influence consumers’ purchase intentions [49]. Employing interpersonal communication strategies, such as using selfies (images and videos), captions with first-person pronouns, and responding to comments, fosters deeper engagement and facilitates two-way dialogue between content creators and their audience on social media [50].

The above studies confirm that social media use promotes environmental awareness and pro-environmental behavior, leading to the inference that using WeChat—one of China’s leading social media platforms-similarly enhances environmental awareness among its users. This gives rise to Hypothesis 1: The more likely an individual is to use WeChat, the greater the impact of WeChat on their environmental awareness; conversely, the less likely they are to use WeChat, the smaller its impact on their environmental awareness.

However, not all research supports the idea that social media enhances environmental awareness. For instance, a study conducted in the Philippines found that social media actually increased the intention to purchase fast fashion [51]. Similarly, research from the Strathclyde Business School in the UK concluded that social media is not an effective tool for influencing values or behaviors related to environmentally friendly foods (EFF) [52].

WeChat’s impact varies among different groups. Some studies indicate that individuals who are least likely to use the internet gain the most from internet access and the knowledge it provides [53]. According to research on the digital divide, it is evident that low-income, rural residents, and older adults face more challenges in accessing and using the internet [54]. Many farmers still live in rural areas with lifestyles deeply embedded in homogenous social networks, where offline interactions remain the primary source of information [55]. Therefore, limited WeChat usage among these groups can expose them to diverse environmental information, significantly boosting their environmental awareness. This leads to Hypothesis 2: The less likely residents are to use WeChat, the greater its positive impact on their environmental awareness; conversely, the more likely they are to use WeChat, the lesser its impact.

Key dimensions of environmental awareness include knowledge, values, attitudes, willingness to act, and actual behavior, all of which are influenced by various factors [56]. Many studies treat environmental awareness, knowledge, and behavior as interconnected factors. For example, while environmental knowledge and awareness can influence behavior, they do not solely determine it [57]. Among Hungarian students, a strong correlation exists between environmental knowledge and environmentally conscious actions [58]. Similarly, a survey of Chinese college students found that their environmental knowledge, climate change awareness, and attitudes positively influenced their pro-environmental intentions [59]. Knowledge is fundamental to altering both awareness and behavior, and social media serves as a key platform for acquiring environmental knowledge. Ballew et al. [60] identified three primary functions of social media: informational, relational, and experiential, with the informational function focusing on sharing educational content. Social media provides flexible learning opportunities [61]. For instance, a study on Indian consumers demonstrated that increased attention to environmental and health knowledge via social media could promote green consumption [62]. Therefore, environmental knowledge may mediate the relationship between WeChat use and environmental awareness, leading to Hypothesis 3: WeChat use influences environmental awareness through its impact on environmental knowledge.

Environmental knowledge is an important prerequisite for environmental behavior, but it has been found to be an important yet distal variable that drives behavior primarily through arousing environmental emotions [63]. This observation leads us to consider other mediating variables between social media use and environmental awareness. A European study found that environmental concern is shaped by both environmental knowledge and perceptions of environmental risks [64]. Similarly, research in China [65] has confirmed the influence of environmental risk perception on environmental awareness. Internet use has been shown to enhance perceptions of environmental risk, which directly affects pro-environmental behavior [66]. Social media can amplify the impact of interpersonal communication on risk perception, further influencing pro-environmental behavior [67]. In a study conducted in Malaysia, it was noted that people are more inclined to engage in pro-environmental behavior when they have a strong awareness of the risks associated with not taking action [68]. This suggests that social media might enhance environmental risk perception, thereby impacting users’ environmental awareness. Based on this, we propose Hypothesis 4: WeChat use affects environmental awareness through the perception of environmental risk.

Research methodology

Data

This study uses data from the Chinese General Social Survey (CGSS). The CGSS collects data through a multi-stage stratified random sampling approach. As part of the basic sampling process, districts (counties) are randomly selected across the country as first-level sampling units, followed by street (township) and resident (village) committees selected as second- and third-level sampling units; finally, a random sample of households is selected for the survey, and then a random sample of one individual is selected from each household. The first round of the CGSS was conducted in 2003, followed by ten rounds in 2005, 2006, 2008, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2013, 2015, 2017, and 2018. For empirical analysis, this study uses only the data from 2018 because items related to social media use and environmental attitudes are only found in this year’s data. There are 3697 people be included in the analysis after excluding those with missing values. For more information on data collection, data quality, and sample representativeness, readers can visit the official website of the Chinese General Social Survey [63, 69].

Measurement indicators

Environmental awareness is the dependent variable in this study. We use the following question in the survey to measure environmental awareness: “People should make some economic sacrifices to protect the environment, how strongly do you agree with this statement?”. Answer options include “strongly disagree”, “disagree”, “neutral”, “agree”, and “strongly agree”, assigned values of 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5. A higher score indicates a greater level of environmental awareness among residents. Generally, the higher the score, the greater the degree of environmental awareness among the residents.

In this study, the treatment variable is the frequency of WeChat usage (D). We use the number of WeChat friends as a proxy variable for the frequency of WeChat usage, with a median of 30 as the threshold. If an individual has more than 30 WeChat friends, we consider them to frequently use WeChat, and D takes the value of 1. If an individual has 30 or fewer WeChat friends, we consider them to not use WeChat frequently, and D takes the value of 0.

Environmental knowledge was assessed by asking participants to rate their agreement with three statements: (1) Energy use is the primary cause of smog; (2) Energy use is the primary cause of the greenhouse effect; and (3) Energy use is the primary cause of acid rain. Responses were recorded on a 5-point scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The scores for the three items were summed and then divided by 3 to compute the Environmental Knowledge score (Cronbach’s α = 0.78, M = 2.62, SD = 1.34).

Environmental risk perception was measured by asking participants to rate their agreement with the statement: ‘The air quality in the area where I lived in 2017 was very good.’ Responses were recorded on a 5-point scale ranging from 1 (strongly agree) to 5 (strongly disagree). A higher score indicates a greater degree of environmental risk perception among residents.

Control variables included in the study are gender, age, education, marital status, health status, social interaction frequency, household registration, party membership, and income level, as they may influence social media behavior and engagement with environmental issues. Research indicates gender differences in social media use, with women more likely to share personal updates and men prioritizing privacy, potentially impacting their environmental engagement [64, 70]. Younger generations, particularly digital natives, tend to use social media more frequently for self-expression, in contrast to older individuals [65, 71]. Education also plays a significant role, as those with higher educational levels are more likely to use social media for information gathering and interaction [66, 72]. Marital status can affect social media behavior, with single or divorced individuals generally being more active than those who are married [64, 70]. Health status and income were controlled for, as individuals with poor health or lower socioeconomic status may use social media less frequently and have different access to environmental information [67, 68, 73, 74]. Additionally, while less studied, household registration and party membership can influence social resources and network structures, affecting social media use [64, 70]. Finally, social interaction frequency was considered, as social media serves as a crucial platform for maintaining and expanding social networks, which may influence engagement with environmental discussions [69, 75].

Among them, gender, household registration, party membership, and marital status are binary variables: male takes the value of 1, female takes the value of 0; agricultural household registration takes the value of 1, non-agricultural household registration takes the value of 0; party member takes the value of 1, non-party member takes the value of 0; married takes the value of 1, unmarried takes the value of 0. Health status is reported by the respondents themselves. In CGSS2018, respondents were asked “How do you rate your current health status?” The coding of the respondents’ answers is as follows: “very unhealthy = 1,” “somewhat unhealthy = 2,” “average = 3,” “somewhat healthy = 4,” and “very healthy = 5,” with a higher value indicating better health. Social interaction is measured by “In the past year, did you often do the following activities in your spare time? - Socializing/visiting,” with the following coding: never = 1, rarely = 2, sometimes = 3, often = 4, very frequently = 5. Annual income is measured by “What was your total income for the past year?” and the answer is transformed into natural logarithm (ln).

This study uses the provincial internet penetration rate as an exclusive instrumental variable to address the potential endogeneity problem between WeChat usage and individual environmental awareness. There is a clear correlation between internet coverage and WeChat usage frequency. As a social, communication, and payment app that relies on the internet, WeChat requires a stable network connection to function properly. In areas with higher internet coverage, residents have easier access to the internet, whether through mobile data or Wi-Fi. Consequently, in provinces with better network infrastructure, the barriers to using WeChat are significantly reduced. Convenient internet access minimizes friction costs, such as poor connectivity or network delays, which in turn increases users’ willingness to use the app.

Internet coverage and environmental awareness may not be entirely exogenous, as both can be influenced by shared economic and social factors. However, this concern is largely mitigated in our research design. In China, mobile internet development has far outpaced fixed broadband, especially in rural and remote areas, where mobile phones are a more affordable means of accessing the internet. As a result, internet coverage is primarily determined by mobile internet availability rather than fixed broadband infrastructure, giving mobile internet coverage a certain level of exogeneity. This is because mobile internet coverage largely depends on the construction of base stations, which are not solely determined by economic factors. First, base station construction is mainly funded by government investment and is not fully reliant on local economic strength. Second, natural factors like terrain and weather conditions play a significant role in base station distribution. For example, flat areas are easier to develop, meaning that even though some of these regions may be less economically developed than mountainous provinces, they often enjoy better mobile internet coverage. For instance, the more developed Hunan province has lower internet coverage than the flatter Shanxi province due to its mountainous terrain.

Thus, internet coverage serves as a reasonably exogenous instrumental variable for WeChat usage frequency. Additionally, we control for key socioeconomic variables, such as individual income and years of education, to minimize omitted variable bias and reduce any potential link between geographic distance and residents’ consumption levels. After accounting for these factors, we believe the exogeneity of our instrumental variable is further strengthened.

Statistical model

Drawing on relevant research developments and findings, this study’s empirical analysis framework is based on the marginal treatment effects (MTE) due to considerations of endogeneity, selection bias, and heterogeneity issues [24, 70, 71, 76]. When heterogeneity and selection bias exist, traditional instrumental variable methods may not accurately estimate the return on WeChat usage. Instead, the local instrumental variable (LIV) method can be used in conjunction with MTE to estimate the heterogeneous returns on WeChat usage [24, 71]. MTE has the following characteristics: firstly, it can reflect the heterogeneity and changing trends of treatment effects; secondly, MTE includes observable and unobservable effects, which can reflect the unobservable heterogeneity and reveal the structural characteristics of individual environmental relationships that change with unobservable factors or selection preferences; finally, by weighting the MTE average, other conventional treatment parameters can be obtained, including the average treatment effect (ATE), the average treatment effect on the treated (ATT), the average treatment effect on the untreated (ATU), and the local average treatment effect (LATE), among others. MTE estimation

In the results of OLS and 2SLS estimation, OLS method is subject to estimation bias due to endogeneity issues, while 2SLS only reflects the conditional LATE caused by changes in instrumental variables. If the treatment effects caused by instrumental variables cannot reflect the parameters of interest, the policy relevance and external validity of LATE will become problematic [73, 78]. In this case, estimating the marginal treatment effects (MTE) is a better choice [24, 71]. Firstly, MTE ensures that the estimation results do not vary with the choice of instrumental variables. Secondly, its economic interpretation is more meaningful because it reflects the average benefits of individuals who choose to accept a policy when they are at the critical state of accepting or not accepting it. Thirdly, other traditional treatment parameters such as ATT and ATU can be calculated through the weighted average of MTE.

Table 3, column (1) shows the parameter estimates of the first-stage logit model, and for ease of interpretation, we directly provide the marginal effect estimates of the parameter estimates. It can be seen that the higher the internet penetration rate of a province, the higher the proportion of frequent WeChat users, with its estimate slightly higher than that of the linear probability model (the coefficient of inprovince in Table 2). The probability of frequent WeChat use by men will increase by about 15%. The other variables are consistent with previous research conclusions: the older the age, the lower the probability of frequent WeChat use; the higher the education level, the higher the probability of frequent WeChat use; agricultural hukou significantly reduces the probability of frequent WeChat use; party members, married individuals, those in good health, those with more social activities, and those with higher income have a higher probability of frequent WeChat use; and married individuals have a higher probability of frequent use. The first-stage estimation obtained a large common support domain (see Fig. 1), with the range of propensity scores covering between 0.11 and 0.99, almost covering all ranges between (0,1), indicating that the unconditional support set generated by the combination of instrumental variables and other independent variables is sufficient to identify MTE, and this MTE is usually assumed to have a shape that does not change with the variable X (see Eq. 5) when applied.

The effects of WeChat use on environmental awareness from a normal selection model

MTE can be used to estimate other treatment effects by taking a weighted average, resulting in the Average Treatment Effect (ATE), Average Treatment Effect on the Treated (ATT), and Average Treatment Effect on the Untreated (ATU). Specifically, ATE is calculated by taking the average of MTE with equal weights, given the full distribution of X and UD.

The ATT is the average effect of treatment for the subpopulation that chooses treatment. When one calculates the weighted average of the X in this population, this parameter will weight people with high propensity scores more precisely because they have a higher probability of choosing treatment. Likewise, ATT will weight points at the lower end of the UD distribution higher because a larger share of the population at these values of UD will choose treatment—they have lower resistance.

In contrast, the ATU weights individuals with low propensity scores higher when calculating the weighted average of the X. This is precisely because these people, all else the same, have higher probability to be untreated. ATU weights high values of UD more because these people have high resistance.

Table 4, column (1) presents the estimates of ATE, ATT, and ATU. It can be seen that the estimated value of ATE is 1.49, which means that frequent WeChat use increases the environmental awareness level by about 1.49 points for all individuals, and this estimate value passes the significance test at the 1% confidence level. Compared with the OLS estimate value in Table 2, the absolute value of ATE estimate is significantly higher, while the difference in the absolute value of the estimate between ATE and 2SLS is relatively small. This indicates that the OLS estimate value is underestimated, and the traditional 2SLS estimate is essentially a weighted average of LATE, which reflects the average treatment effect of different policy compliers and cannot reflect the average treatment effect of all populations. Theoretically, estimation bias may still exist. Since ATT assigns higher weights to higher individuals, and the absolute value of MTE for higher individuals is lower, the absolute value of ATT estimate is significantly higher than that of ATU. The study found that frequent WeChat users (smaller group) have a higher increase in environmental awareness compared to non-frequent WeChat users (larger group), whereas non-frequent WeChat users cannot increase their environmental awareness even if they frequently use WeChat.

Mechanism analysis

Since most variables in our study are not latent, we used path analysis to explore how WeChat usage affects environmental awareness. The path analysis results, shown in Fig. 4, reveal that the total effect of WeChat usage frequency on environmental awareness includes both a direct effect and an indirect effect mediated by environmental knowledge. The direct effect is 0.077, while the indirect effect is calculated as the product of WeChat usage frequency’s impact on environmental knowledge (0.111) and environmental knowledge’s impact on environmental awareness (0.238). Thus, the total effect is the sum of the direct and indirect effects, which amounts to 0.103. In this total effect, the indirect effect represents 25.2%, indicating that WeChat’s primary influence on environmental awareness is through its direct effect. The direct effect accounts for more than 70% of the total effect, likely because China places a strong emphasis on interpersonal relationships. Social media’s technological features, particularly the relational functions of social networking sites (SNS), effectively transmit social norms that encourage pro-environmental behavior [60, 74]. Interactive content on social media promotes pro-environmental actions by highlighting peer role models [75, 79]. Additionally, social media enhances interpersonal communication, which further influences pro-environmental behavior. This suggests that social media functions not only as an information dissemination platform but also as a space for social interaction [76, 80]. By showcasing others’ behaviors and attitudes, social media affects individuals’ personal and social norms. For example, one study found that user-generated content on social media effectively activates personal environmental norms [78, 81]. This may be because social media provides opportunities to observe and emulate others’ actions, thus fostering pro-environmental behavior.

Results of path analysis

Although WeChat usage frequency significantly increases environmental risk perception (standardized coefficient = 0.187, p < 0.001), environmental risk perception does not significantly impact environmental awareness (standardized coefficient = −0.007, p = 0.657). Thus, environmental risk perception does not act as a significant mediator in this model. In China, despite widespread recognition of environmental risks such as air and water pollution, this awareness does not necessarily enhance environmental consciousness. One possible explanation is that individuals may feel powerless to effect change or address these issues personally. Confronted with complex environmental challenges, people often experience a sense of ‘helplessness’ or ‘responsibility shifting,’ where they believe that the government or corporations should assume greater responsibility for taking action, rather than individuals. Given that the government plays a crucial role in environmental protection in China [77, 81], the public might believe that adequate measures have already been implemented to mitigate environmental risks. Consequently, individual perceptions of environmental risks may not lead to increased environmental awareness.

Discussion and conclusion

In this study, we examine whether the use of WeChat contributes to the improvement of residents’ environmental awareness. From a heterogeneity perspective, two hypotheses are proposed: first, the use of WeChat can enhance residents’ environmental awareness levels; and second, the use of WeChat will result in an increase in environmental awareness levels among residents who are more likely to use it. In order to test the hypotheses, propensity score matching and heterogeneity intervention models were used to analyze the data from the China General Social Survey for 2018 (CGSS2018). Based on the results, WeChat has a significant positive impact on residents’ environmental awareness. WeChat use may be selective, but when residents who have similar propensities to use WeChat are compared, those who actually use WeChat show higher levels of environmental awareness than those who do not use WeChat. As a result of estimating the WeChat use returns of individuals with different WeChat use propensities, the study found that residents with a higher propensity to use WeChat were more likely to be aware of environmental issues due to the use of WeChat. While WeChat use was not associated with an increase in environmental awareness among residents with a general or lower propensity to use WeChat.

The mechanism analysis shows that WeChat usage influences environmental awareness both directly and indirectly through environmental knowledge. The direct effect is the primary contributor, while environmental knowledge serves as a partial mediator, amplifying the overall impact. However, environmental risk perception does not significantly mediate this relationship, indicating that although individuals may recognize environmental risks, this recognition does not necessarily lead to increased environmental awareness. These findings highlight the importance of direct social media engagement and knowledge dissemination in shaping environmental consciousness.

As a result of this study, we have been able to gain a better understanding of how social media platforms, such as WeChat, affect people’s attitudes and behaviors as a direct result of their real-world social networks. Existing research on the role of social media from the perspective of social networks has largely focused on the idea that social media enhance the social capital of individuals. The reason for this is that social media mobilize a variety of resources within social networks. In addition to this, social networks can also be considered as a structure that influences the information supply on social media, which has an impact on the acquisition of information by individuals. It depends on the characteristics of an individual’s own social network. The conclusion of this paper is that the effect of social media is closely related to the social relationships of individuals in the real world. Future research should explore the interaction between online and offline social networks.

This article suggests that targeted measures can be taken to enhance residents’ environmental awareness, thereby achieving the goal of improving environmental protection. Firstly, increased penetration of Internet and social media use contributes to increased frequency of social media use, which in turn increases the environmental awareness of residents. Secondly, the public need to attach importance to environmental protection actions carried out in the real world, as the dissemination of internet information cannot completely replace the interpersonal transmission of environmental information in social networks in the real world. Thirdly, more environmental protection activities should be carried out in the real world of middle-aged and elderly, agricultural household registration, unmarried, and low-income groups, as this is a more effective way to raise their environmental awareness compared to social media.

The study reveals the mechanism of WeChat usage on shared environmental awareness, but there are still certain limitations. Firstly, the data was collected through a questionnaire survey, which has a single data source. Future research can consider more diverse data collection methods. Secondly, the research method has its own limitations, and future studies can choose more diverse methods, such as situational experiments. Thirdly, there are many factors that affect shared environmental awareness, and WeChat is the APP that integrates multiple functions, which can affect users from various aspects. Our article mainly identifies the heterogeneous effects of WeChat usage on environmental awareness. Due to the lack of relevant data, it is currently impossible to empirically test the social norm mechanism of action. As a result, this study can only explore the mechanisms involving environmental knowledge or environmental risk perception. Future research should focus on collecting targeted data to investigate the social norm mechanism and better understand its impact.

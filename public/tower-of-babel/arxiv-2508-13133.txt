Abstract
. This article begins the study of T-braces, those skew left braces of abelian type in which the relation of being an ideal is a transitive relation.

Primary: 16N80; Secondary 16T25, 16N40, 20F19

1.
Introduction

A
left brace (or skew left brace of abelian type)
is a set
A
together with two binary operations, addition denoted by
+
and multiplication denoted by
\cdot
(which is often omitted), satisfying the following conditions:

LB1

A
is an abelian group under addition;

LB2

A
is a group under multiplication;

LB3

a(b+c)=ab+ac-a
for all
a,b,c\in A
.

In this paper, all braces will be left braces in this sense.
Sometimes we shall denote the additive group of
A
by
(A,+)
and the multiplicative group of
A
by
(A,\cdot)
.
Braces were first introduced by W. Rump in
[
13
]
as a generalization of Jacobson radical rings in order to help study involutive set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.

Braces can be viewed as an algebraic structure
representing a coalescence of two groups. Unlike with rings, in braces the additive and mulitplicative identities coincide and we denote this common neutral element by
0
or
1
, as appropriate.

If
A
is a left brace, then a subset
S
of
A
is called a
subbrace
(more precisely a
left subbrace
) if
S
is closed under addition and multiplication and is a left brace by
restriction of these operations to
S
. Thus a subset
S
of
A
is a subbrace of
A
if and only if
(S,+)\leq(A,+)
and
(S,\cdot)\leq(A,\cdot)
. We shall denote that
S
is a subbrace of
A
by
S\leq A
.

If
M
is a subset of a left brace
A
then the
subbrace
B=\textbf{br}(M)
of
A
generated by
M
is the intersection of all subbraces
C
of
A
that contain
M
. If
M
is a finite set, then
B
will be called
finitely generated
. If
M=\{a\}
then we write
\textbf{br}(a)
rather than
\textbf{br}(\{a\})
and call
\textbf{br}(a)
a one-generator brace. We refer the reader to
[
6
,
9
]
for further details and properties.

For the left brace
A
and for each element
a\in A
we define a map
\lambda_{a}:A\longrightarrow A
by
\lambda_{a}(x)=ax-a
for all
x\in A
. The map
\lambda_{a}
is an automorphism of
(A,+)
. Furthermore we define
a\star b=ab-a-b
for all
a,b\in A
. It is easy to see that
a\star b=\lambda_{a}(b)-b
.

A left brace
A
is called
trivial
or
abelian
if
a\star b=0
for all
a,b\in A
, or equivalently,
a+b=ab
. In this case the addition and multiplication coincide.

We recall that a subbrace
L
of the left brace
A
is called a
left ideal
of
A
if
a\star b\in L
for all elements
a\in A
and
b\in L
.
This means that if
x,y\in L
, then
x-y\in L
and if
a\in A
and
z\in L
, then
\lambda_{a}(z)\in L
,
as in
[
4
]
.
Furthermore, a subbrace
L
of a left brace
A
is an
ideal
if
a\star z,z\star a\in L
for all
a\in A,z\in L
. We can then form a new brace, the quotient brace
A/L
, in a natural way. Quite often, it happens that we are interested in studying braces where the quotient braces are abelian, so that the operations of addition and multiplication coincide in the quotient.

If
A
is a left brace and
K,L
are subbraces, then we let
K\star L
denote the subgroup of
(A,+)
generated by the elements
x\star y
, where
x\in K,y\in L
.

We define two canonical series of the left brace
A
. First, let
A^{(1)}=A
and recursively define
A^{(\alpha+1)}=A^{(\alpha)}\star A
for all ordinals
\alpha
and as usual set
A^{(\lambda)}=\cap_{\mu<\lambda}A^{(\mu)}
for all limit ordinals
\lambda
. It is shown in
[
4
]
that
A^{(n)}
is an ideal of
A
. This has been extended in
[
7
]
to include all ordinals
\alpha
. Clearly,
A^{(\alpha+1)}\leq A^{(\alpha)}
, for all ordinals
\alpha
.

Similarly, let
A^{1}=A
,
A^{\alpha+1}=A\star A^{\alpha}
, for all ordinals
\alpha
and
A^{\lambda}=\cap_{\mu<\lambda}A^{\mu}
for all limit ordinals
\lambda
. It is shown in
[
4
]
that
A^{n}
is always a left ideal of
A
a fact which has been extended in
[
7
]
to include all ordinals
\alpha
. Clearly,
A^{\alpha+1}\leq A^{\alpha}
, for all ordinals
\alpha
.

We shall say that
A
is
Smoktunowicz-nilpotent
if there are natural numbers
n,k
such that
A^{(n)}=A^{k}
. Such braces were introduced by A. Smoktunowicz in the paper
[
14
]
and are called
strongly nilpotent
in
[
15
]
.

It is clear that if
A
is a subbrace of
B
and
B
is a subbrace of
C
, then
A
is a subbrace of
C
. However, as with many other algebraic structures, there is in general no such transitive relation for ideals. Groups in which normality is a transitive relation have been studied by several authors ( see
[
8
]
and
[
12
]
, for example) and a brief overview of the research on such groups and related topics can be found in the article
[
10
]
. Similar studies have been conducted in other algebraic structures; for example those Leibniz algebras in which the property of being an ideal is a transitive relation were studied in the paper
[
11
]
. In group theory, groups in which normality is a transitive relation are called T-groups and, by analogy, a left brace
A
is called a
T-brace
, if the property of being an ideal is a transitive relation on
A
.

A T-group is a generalization of a Dedekind group, a group in which every subgroup is normal. Dedekind braces were studied in the paper
[
2
]
and in this paper we begin the study of their generalization, T-braces. The class of T-groups which satisfy other natural restrictions have been studied further. One such natural restriction is nilpotency–nilpotent T-groups are clearly Dedekind groups. In this paper our approach to nilpotency is based on the following concept, first introduced by Bonatto and Jedlička
[
3
]
and Jespers, Van Antwerpen and Vendramin
[
9
]

Let
A
be a left brace. The set

\displaystyle\zeta(\star,A)

\displaystyle=\{a\in A|a\star x=x\star a=0\text{ for all }x\in A\}

\displaystyle=\{a\in A|ax=a+x=xa\text{ for all }x\in A\}

\displaystyle=\{a\in A|\lambda_{a}(x)=x\text{ and }\lambda_{x}(a)=a\text{ for all }x\in A\}

is here called the
\star
-center
of
A
. The set
\zeta(\star,A)
is an ideal of
A
which is contained in the center,
\zeta(A)
, of
(A,\cdot)
; this known result appears in
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
. We denote the center of the multiplicative group of
A
by
\zeta(A)
.

Starting from the
\star
-center and by analogy with groups we construct the
upper
\star
-central series

0=\zeta_{0}(\star,A)\leq\zeta_{1}(\star,A)\leq\dots\zeta_{\alpha}(\star,A)\leq\zeta_{\alpha+1}(\star,A)\leq\dots\zeta_{\gamma}(\star,A)

as follows. We let

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}(\star,A)

\displaystyle=\zeta(\star,A)\text{ and }

\displaystyle\zeta_{\alpha+1}(\star,A)/\zeta_{\alpha}(\star,A)

\displaystyle=\zeta(\star,A/\zeta_{\alpha}(\star,A))

for all ordinals
\alpha
; as usual for limit ordinals
\lambda
we set
\zeta_{\lambda}(\star,A)=\cup_{\mu<\lambda}\zeta_{\mu}(\star,A)
.

Each term of this series is an ideal of
A
. The last term
\zeta_{\infty}(\star,A)=\zeta_{\gamma}(\star,A)
of this series is called the
upper
\star
-hypercenter
of
A
and we denote the length of the upper
\star
-central series of
A
by
\text{zl}(A)
. Furthermore, if
A=\zeta_{\infty}(\star,A)
, then
A
is called a
\star
-hypercentral
brace; in this case, if
\text{zl}(A)
is finite, then we say that
A
is
\star
-nilpotent
. This series has also been discussed in
[
3
,
6
]
and
[
16
]
but there this series is called the
upper annihilator series
and the last term is then called the
hyper-annihilator
; a
\star
-hypercentral brace is called
annihilator hypercentral
in
[
16
]
and, in
[
3
,
9
]
,
\star
-nilpotency is called
central nilpotency (and respectively) annihilator nilpotency
. That Smoktunowicz nilpotency and
\star
-nilpotency are equivalent is evident, for example, in
[
7
, Proposition 2.10]
.
Further work concerning
\star
-hypercentral braces can be found in
[
7
]
.

In this paper we begin the study of Smoktunowicz-nilpotent T-braces. The situation here is more complicated than with groups and Leibniz algebras. Our first results are devoted to the study of left braces in which the additive group is not periodic. Our first main result is as follows.

Theorem A
.

Let
A
be a T-brace. If the additive group of the
\star
-center of
A
is torsion-free, then the upper
\star
-hypercenter of
A
and the
\star
-center of
A
coincide.

This result has the following easy consequences

Corollary A1
.

Let
A
be a T-brace whose additive group is torsion-free. If
A
is
\star
-hypercentral, then
A
is abelian.

Corollary A2
.

Let
A
be a T-brace whose additive group is torsion-free. If
A
is Smoktunowicz-nilpotent, then
A
is abelian.

We shall say that a
\star
-hypercentral brace is periodic if its additive group
(A,+)
is periodic and non-periodic otherwise.

Corollary A3
.

Let
A
be a non-periodic
\star
-hypercentral T-brace and let
T
be the torsion subgroup of the additive group of
A
. Then
T
is an ideal of
A
and
A/T
is an abelian brace.

We shall also prove the following result.

Theorem B
.

Let
A
be a T-brace. Suppose that
A
is Smoktunowicz-nilpotent and that
\text{zl}(A)=k
for some positive integer
k
. If the additive group of
A
is not periodic, then the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
K=A\star A
is finite of order at most
p^{k-1}
for every prime
p
.

The layout of our paper is as follows. In Section
2
we give some results that we shall require later and also give a brief overview of some results connected with
\star
-nilpotent and
\star
-hypercentral braces. In Section
3
we state and prove the results which enable us to prove Theorem
A
and in Section
4
we prove pertinent results for Theorem
B
.

2.
Preliminary results and an overview of
\star
-hypercentral braces

We shall need some of the following properties of
\star
and
\lambda_{a}
whose proofs can be found in
[
4
]
or
[
9
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a left brace. Then

(i)

ab^{-1}=-\lambda_{ab^{-1}}(b)+a,a-b=\lambda_{b}(b^{-1})a,a+b=a\lambda_{a^{-1}}(b)
;

(ii)

a\star(b+c)=a\star b+a\star c
;

(iii)

(ab)\star c=a\star(b\star c)+b\star c+a\star c
;

(iv)

(a+b)\star c=a\star(\lambda_{a^{-1}}(b)\star c)+(\lambda_{a^{-1}}(b)\star c)+a\star c
;

(v)

\lambda_{a}(x+y)=\lambda_{a}(x)+\lambda_{a}(y),(\lambda_{a}\circ\lambda_{b})(x)=\lambda_{ab}(x)
;

(vi)

\lambda_{y}(b\star a)=yby^{-1}\star\lambda_{y}(a)
;

(v)

yby^{-1}=\lambda_{y}(\lambda_{b}(y^{-1})-y^{-1}+b)=\lambda_{y}(b\star y^{-1}+b)

for all elements
a,b,c,x,y\in A
.

We note, in particular, that Proposition
2.1
(iii) shows that if
c\in\zeta_{2}(\star,A)
, then the map
\beta_{c}:(A,\cdot)\longrightarrow(\zeta(\star,A),+)
defined by
\beta_{c}(x)=x\star c
is a group homomorphism.

We shall also require the following result, which is well-known and its proof is omitted.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
n,k
be natural numbers and let
a,b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n},c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{k}
be elements of the left brace
A
. Then

a(b_{1}+\dots+b_{n}-c_{1}-\dots-c_{k})=ab_{1}+ab_{2}+\dots+ab_{n}-ac_{1}-\dots-ac_{k}+(k-n+1)a.

The following result is also quite easy.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A
be a left brace and let
k
be an integer. Then

(i)

x\star ka=k(x\star a)
for all
a,x\in A
;

(ii)

If
a\in\zeta_{2}(\star,A)
, then
(ka)\star x=k(a\star x)
for all
x\in A
.

Proof.

(i) is immediate from Proposition
2.1
.

(ii) Let
k
be a positive integer and let
Z=\zeta(\star,A)
. Since
\zeta_{2}(\star,A)/Z
is abelian it follows that
ka+Z=a^{k}+Z=a^{k}Z
so that
ka=a^{k}u
for some element
u\in Z
. Since
u\star x=0
and
a\star x\in Z
, Proposition
2.1
(iii) implies that

ka\star x=a^{k}\star x=k(a\star x),

using a further induction argument. Also

0=0\star x=1\star x=aa^{-1}\star x=a\star(a^{-1}\star x)+a\star x+a^{-1}\star x=a\star x+a^{-1}\star x,

upon again using Proposition
2.1
and the fact that
a^{-1}\in\zeta_{2}(\star,A)
. Thus
a^{-1}\star x=-(a\star x)
and a further easy induction completes the proof.
∎

The next result is also probably well-known but we give a proof for the sake of completeness. We shall throughout let
\langle a\rangle
denote the additive cyclic subgroup generated by the element
a
in the brace
A
, although often we shall explicitly state our meaning again.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A
be a left brace and let
a\in A
.

(i)

If
a\star a=0
, then
\textbf{br}(a)=\langle a\rangle
and moreover the multiplication in
\textbf{br}(a)
coincides with addition;

(ii)

If
a\in\zeta(\star,A)
, then
\langle a\rangle
is an ideal of
A
;

(iii)

If
a\in\zeta_{2}(\star,A)
, then
\textbf{br}(a)=\langle a\rangle+\langle a\star a\rangle
.

Proof.

(i) Since
a\star a=0
it follows that
a^{2}=2a
. Using this fact and Lemma
2.2
a simple induction argument allows us to conclude that
a^{n}=na
for all natural numbers
n
. Furthermore, we have

a=a^{-1}a^{2}=a^{-1}(a+a)=1+1-a^{-1}=-a^{-1}

and a further straightforward induction implies that
a^{-n}=-na
for all positive integers
n
. Also for all integers
m,n
we have
(ma)(na)=a^{m}a^{n}=a^{m+n}=(m+n)a
so
\langle a\rangle
is closed under multiplication. Additionally,

(na)^{-1}=(a^{n})^{-1}=a^{-n}=-na

so
\langle a\rangle
is also a multiplicative group. Thus
\langle a\rangle
is the subbrace generated by
a
and the addition and multiplication on
\langle a\rangle
coincide.

(ii) By (i),
\langle a\rangle
is a subbrace of
A
. Moreover, if
b\in A
, then Lemma
2.3
implies that
na\star b=b\star na=0\in\langle a\rangle
for all integers
n
so the result is immediate.

(iii) If
a\in\zeta_{2}(\star,A)
, then
u=a\star a\in\zeta(\star,A)
and hence
\langle u\rangle
is an ideal of
A
and
(a+\langle u\rangle)\star(a+\langle u\rangle)=0
. By (i) it follows that
\textbf{br}(a+\langle u\rangle)=\langle a+\langle u\rangle\rangle=(\langle a\rangle+\langle u\rangle)/\langle u\rangle
. Hence
\langle a\rangle+\langle u\rangle
is a subbrace of
A
containing
a
so
\textbf{br}(a)\leq\langle a\rangle+\langle u\rangle
. Since it is clear that
a,u\in\textbf{br}(a)
we have
\textbf{br}(a)=\langle a\rangle+\langle u\rangle
as required.
∎

We shall require some knowledge of
\star
-hypercentral braces and the next few results incorporate the results we need. First we give an important property of the upper
\star
-hypercenter, which is analogous to the well-known result in group theory. This result appears as
[
3

. There is a similar result in
[

and

.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
A
be a left brace and suppose that
K
is a non-trivial ideal of
A
such that
K\leq\zeta_{\infty}(\star,A)
. Then
K\cap\zeta(\star,A)
is non-trivial.

Next we have (see
[
7
, Lemma 3.3]
, for example).

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
A
be a left brace and let
S
be a subbrace of
A
. For each ordinal
\alpha
,
S+\zeta_{\alpha}(\star,A)
is an ideal of
S+\zeta_{\alpha+1}(\star,A)
.

A brace
A
is called
locally Smoktunowicz-nilpotent
(or
locally (centrally nilpotent)
or
locally
\star
-nilpotent
) if every finitely generated subbrace of
A
is Smoktunowicz-nilpotent. Such braces have been studied in
[
1
]
and
[
7
]
. Every
\star
-hypercentral brace is locally Smoktunowicz-nilpotent. Of particular interest here are the following two results
which appear in
[
7
]
and
[
1
]
. These results generalize results occurring in
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
A
be a
\star
-hypercentral left brace.
If the additive group of
A
is torsion-free, then the additive group of every factor of the upper
\star
-central series is torsion-free.

We note that in
[
1
]
the authors define an element
a
of a brace
A
to be
periodic
if
\textbf{br}(a)
is finite and in this case the
order of
a
is
|\textbf{br}(a)|
. If
\pi
is a set of primes, then
a
is a
\pi
-element
if its order is a
\pi
-number. A

is just a subbrace all of whose elements are

-elements. If

is simply a maximal element of the set of all

Theorem 2.8
.

Let

be the torsion-subgroup of the additive group of

. Then

(i)

T
coincides with the torsion subgroup of

(ii)

.

3.
Structure of torsion-free Smoktunowicz-nilpotent T-braces

We shall say that an element
a
of a left brace
A
has
infinite additive order
if the element
a
has infinite order as an element of
(A,+)
and we say that
a
has
finite additive order
k
if
a
has order
k
as an element of
(A,+)
. We shall also use at various points in our exposition the clear fact that if
I
is an ideal of a T-brace
A
, then
I
is also a T-brace. Furthermore, if
A
is a Smoktunowicz-nilpotent T-brace, then
A
is a Dedekind brace. With this in mind the following result holds.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be a T-brace and let
n
be a natural number. Then
\textbf{br}(a)
is an ideal of
A
for all
a\in\zeta_{n}(\star,A)
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.6
that
\textbf{br}(a)+\zeta_{k}(\star,A)
is an ideal of
\textbf{br}(a)+\zeta_{k+1}(\star,A)
for all natural numbers
k
. From this and the fact that
A
is a T-brace we easily deduce that
\textbf{br}(a)
is an ideal of
\textbf{br}(a)+\zeta_{n}(\star,A)
. However
\textbf{br}(a)\leq\zeta_{n}(\star,A)
since
a\in\zeta_{n}(\star,A)
. Hence
\textbf{br}(a)
is an ideal of
\zeta_{n}(\star,A)
which is itself an ideal of
A
. Thus
\textbf{br}(a)
is an ideal of
A
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A
be a T-brace, let
n
be a natural number and suppose that
a\in\zeta_{n}(\star,A)\setminus\zeta(\star,A)
. If
a
has infinite additive order, then
a\star a\neq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
a\star a=0
. Then Lemma
2.4
implies
\textbf{br}(a)=\langle a\rangle
and we deduce from Lemma
3.1
that
\langle a\rangle
is an ideal of
A
.

Since
a\notin Z=\zeta(\star,A)
, there exists
u\in A
such that either
u\star a\neq 0
or
a\star u\neq 0
. Suppose first that
u\star a\neq 0
. We know
u\star a\in\langle a\rangle
since
\langle a\rangle
is an ideal of
A
. It follows from Proposition
2.5
that
\langle a\rangle\cap Z=\langle ra\rangle\neq 0
, for some positive integer
r
. However, Lemma
2.3
implies
0=u\star ra=r(u\star a)
. Thus
u\star a
has finite additive order in the group
\langle a\rangle
which is a contradiction, since
u\star a\neq 0
. Thus
u\star a=0
and consequently
a\star u\neq 0
. However
a\star u\in\langle a\rangle
since
\langle a\rangle
is an ideal so
a\star u=sa=a^{s}
for some integer
s
. Then using Proposition
2.1
and a simple induction we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=ra\star u=a^{r}\star u=a^{r-1}\star(a\star u)+(a\star u)+(a^{r-1}\star u)

\displaystyle=a^{r-1}\star a^{s}+r(a\star u)=r(a\star u)

and again
a\star u
has finite order contrary to
\langle a\rangle
having infinite order. The result follows.

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
A
be a left brace and let
a\in A
be an element of infinite additive order. Let
c=a\star a
and suppose that
a\star c=0
. Then
\langle a\rangle\cap\langle c\rangle=0
. In particular, if
a\in\zeta_{2}(\star,A)
, then
\langle a\rangle\cap\langle a\star a\rangle=0
.

Proof.

If
c
has finite additive order, then clearly
\langle a\rangle\cap\langle c\rangle=0
.
Thus, assume that
c
has infinite additive order and that
\langle a\rangle\cap\langle c\rangle\neq 0
. Then there are integers
m,n
such that
ma=nc
. Then, using Proposition
2.1
,

0=n(a\star c)=a\star nc=a\star ma=m(a\star a)=mc

so that
c

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
A
be a T-brace and suppose that

. Let

(ii)

(i) We use induction on

, then

and the result then follows by Lemma

. Suppose that the result is true for

\textbf{br}(a_{1}+B)=\langle a_{1}+B\rangle=(\langle a_{1}\rangle+B)/B\leq\zeta_{n}(\star,A)/B.

But
\zeta_{n}(\star,A)/B
is
\star
-nilpotent so every subbrace is a subideal. In particular this means that
\langle a_{1}\rangle+B
is an ideal of

. However
a_{1}\in\langle a_{1}\rangle+B

\textbf{br}(a_{1})\leq\langle a_{1}\rangle+B

(ii) We again use induction on

Finally, if

∎

Lemma 4.4

Let

Lemma 4.5

Let
A

Lemma 4.7

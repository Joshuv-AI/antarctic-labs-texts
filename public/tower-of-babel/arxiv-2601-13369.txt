Loopless Multiterminal Quantum Circuits at Odd Parity Preprint: APS/123-QED
Antonio Manesco 1,2, Anton Akhmerov 2, Valla Fatemi 3

Abstract
We theoretically investigate loopless multiterminal hybrid superconducting devices at odd fermion parity with time-reversal symmetry. We find that the energy-phase relationship has a double minimum corresponding to opposite windings of the superconducting phases. Spin-orbit coupling adds multi-axial spin splittings, which contrasts with two-terminal devices where spin dependence is uniaxial. Capacitive shunting localizes quantum circuit states in the wells and exponentially suppresses their splitting. For weak spin-orbit strength, the system has a four-dimensional spin-chirality low-energy subspace which can be universally controlled with electric fields only.



I
Introduction

Andreev bound states hosted in Josephson devices offer the prospect of strong coupling between microscopic and macroscopic quantum degrees of freedom
Janvier et al. 2015
.
The microscopic component is given by the spin or quasiparticle excitations hosted in the Andreev levels, and the macroscopic components are the microwave-frequency plasma modes of superconducting circuits.
The coupling strength is derived from the magnitude of the supercurrent mediated by the Andreev states and the direct, galvanic coupling between the weak link and the circuit
Devoret et al. 2007
.
In particular, Andreev spin states offer spin-dependent supercurrent due to spin-orbit coupling (SOC) in the weak link, which results in spin-circuit couplings that enable efficient readout and long-range spin-spin interactions
Hays et al. 2020
;
Hays et al. 2021
;
Pita-Vidal et al. 2024
.
In the future, long-range all-to-all interactions and disjoint support between single-spin and many-spin states may be useful for quantum information applications
Pita-Vidal et al. 2025
;
Lu et al. 2025a
;
Kurilovich et al. 2025
.

The basic physics of two-terminal Andreev spins is captured by the Josephson coupling
Chtchelkatchev and Nazarov 2003
;
Padurariu and Nazarov 2010
;
Pavesic and Zitko 2024

U_{\mathrm{2T}}=E_{J}\cos\varphi+E_{\sigma}\sigma_{z}\sin\varphi~,

(1)

where
\varphi
is the phase drop across the weak link, and
\sigma_{z}
is a Pauli matrix for the spin degree of freedom.

E_{J}>0
is typical for odd-parity, single-channel weak links,
i.e.
, they behave as
\pi
-junctions in the spinless case (Fig.
1
(a))
Bulaevskii et al. 1977
;
van Dam et al. 2006
;
Fatemi et al. 2022
.
The spin-dependent part of the spin-phase energy relation has approximately a single Pauli operator in the low-energy description.
This is tied to the electrons in the weak link following effectively a single path between the superconducting reservoirs.

Multiterminal devices have been proposed as a route to achieving large spin splittings and controlling fermion parity transitions
van Heck et al. 2014
;
Svetogorov et al. 2025
.
Both effects were recently observed in tunneling experiments
Coraiola et al. 2024
.
This suggests that integration of multiterminal Andreev devices in dynamical microwave circuits is viable, but multiterminal Andreev states in dynamical circuits have so far been explored theoretically in the even parity sector
Matute-Cañadas et al. 2024
or without spin-orbit coupling and with loops
Feinberg and Balseiro 2014
.

Here, we develop a minimal model of a quantum dot in the odd parity sector with tunnelings to more than two superconducting reservoirs, both with and without spin-orbit coupling.
Without spin-orbit coupling, we find that the circuit is equivalent to a triangle of
\pi
-junctions
Feinberg and Balseiro 2014
(Fig.
1
(b)), which exhibits a balanced double-well potential.
Adding spin-orbit interaction then introduces a spin-phase energy relation (SPER) that involves all three Pauli vectors (Fig.
1
(c)).
Finally, we introduce capacitive shunts and describe the spectra and inter-level matrix elements of the combined microscopic/macroscopic hybrid system.
We highlight in particular that the loopless nature of this circuit does not admit a magnetic flux parameter, whereas double-well potentials in conventional Josephson junction circuits normally require loops and nonzero flux
Orlando et al. 1999
;
Doucot and Vidal 2002
;
Manucharyan et al. 2009
;
Gyenis et al. 2021a
;
Schrade et al. 2022
;
Hassani et al. 2023
, which introduces noise sources that our circuit does not experience.
Therefore, this approach has the potential to produce both heavy superconducting circuit qubits and Andreev spin qubits with noise resilience by avoiding a typical tradeoff.

II
Spin-Phase Energy Relation of Multiterminal Dots at Odd Parity

We present here a minimal model for a multiterminal weak link, treating the phases of the leads as classical variables for now.
This model is derived in the Supplementary Material, starting from a microscopic description and neglecting contributions from the continuum.
Similar to previous works on two-terminal devices
Padurariu and Nazarov 2010
;
Bargerbos et al. 2023
, we consider a two-orbital quantum dot with orbitals indexed by
j\in\{0,1\}
, as depicted in Fig.
2
(a, b).
The transport processes include tunneling amplitudes
t_{i}
from the dot to the lead
i
with pairing amplitude
\Delta_{i}e^{i\varphi_{i}}
. The tunneling includes orbital-dependent spin-orbit effects with rotation angle
\alpha_{ij}
about the Pauli vector
\vec{d}_{i}
.
Our model contrasts with the one developed by Svetogorov,
et al.
,
Svetogorov et al. 2025
by avoiding the explicit use of a real-space loop.
Our model also contains a sufficient number of transport processes to ensure that spin degeneracy occurs only at the time-reversal symmetric points in the phase space.
We obtain the SPER up to the leading non-vanishing order in perturbation theory, consequently limiting the expressions to the lowest harmonics of the phase difference
\varphi_{ij}=\varphi_{i}-\varphi_{j}
across the superconducting leads
i
and
j
.
We further separate the SPER
U=U_{0}+U_{SO}
, into the spinless term
U_{0}
and the spin-dependent term
U_{SO}
caused by spin-orbit coupling.

The spinless part of the SPER in the weak-coupling limit,
\emph{i.e.}
when higher-harmonics are negligible, is the sum of
\pi
-junction potentials for each pair of leads,

U_{0}=\sum_{i,j}E_{0}^{ij}\cos\varphi_{ij}

(2)

with
E_{0}^{ij}\sim(t_{i}^{2}t_{j}^{2}/\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}\mu_{1})\cos\theta_{ij}
, where

\theta_{ij}=(\alpha_{i0}-\alpha_{i1})-(\alpha_{j0}-\alpha_{j1})
is the spin-dependent dynamical phase accumulated over the whole virtual process.
All the parameters are schematically shown in Fig.
2
for a three-terminal setup.
In this case, the potential is the same as that of a triangle of
\pi
-junctions
Feinberg and Balseiro 2014
, schematically represented in Fig.
1
(b).
Thus, as long as all the
E_{0}^{ij}
are comparable, and the spin-dependent terms are negligible, the energy–phase relation has the form of the symmetric double-well potential shown in Fig.
2
(c).
Note that we set
\varphi_{3}=0
throughout so that
\varphi_{i3}=\varphi_{i}
.
The minima are located at points with opposite phase chirality due to time-reversal symmetry.

The spin-dependent term of the SPER is

U_{SO}=\vec{V}_{SO}\cdot\vec{\sigma}~,\quad\vec{V}_{SO}=\sum_{i,j}E_{\sigma}^{ij}~\vec{n}_{ij}\sin\varphi_{ij}~,

(3)

where
E_{\sigma}=(t_{i}^{2}t_{j}^{2}/\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}\mu_{1})\sin\theta_{ij}
and
\vec{n}_{ij}
are unit Pauli vectors.
This differs from the two-terminal case in Eq. (
1
) because it requires involvement of more than one spin direction.
The exact form of
\vec{n}_{ij}
depend on microscopic details of the device.
Still, notably, it can include spin directions that are absent from the low-energy band structure of the normal material (see Supplemental Material for details).

We now consider a three-terminal setup with Rashba spin-orbit coupling in a two-dimensional electron gas, similarly to
Svetogorov et al. 2025
, and compatible with previous works on two-terminal quantum dot setups
Bargerbos et al. 2023
and Rashba wires
Park and Yeyati 2017
.
The resulting spin-phase landscape of this potential for a symmetric junction (
t_{i}=t
,
\Delta_{i}=\Delta
, and
\theta_{ij}=\theta
) is shown in Fig.
2
(d-f).
We observe that, if
E_{\sigma}^{ij}\ll E_{0}^{ij}
, the potential minima coincide with the point where the resulting spin-orbit field points along the
z
-direction.
The quantization axis, set by
\vec{V}_{SO}
, tilts away from
z
with contributions from two different Pauli vectors for different trajectories in the phase space:
\sim(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})\sigma_{x},~(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})\sigma_{y}
.
In an asymmetric device, the quantization axis is tilted away from the
z
-direction, and trajectories along
\varphi_{+}=\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}
and
\varphi_{-}=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
tilt along linearly independent, rather than orthogonal, spin directions.
The phenomenology of the quantum circuits discussed below is unaffected by these quantitative changes.

III
Quantum Circuits with Three-Terminal Dots at Odd Parity

Next, we shunt the circuit with capacitors, as shown in Fig.
3
(a), so that the phase degrees of freedom have quantum dynamics; concomitantly we promote the phase operators to quantum operators
\left(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\right)\rightarrow\left(\hat{\varphi}_{1},\hat{\varphi}_{2}\right)
.
The capacitive parts of the circuit lead to a term of the form

T=\frac{1}{2}E_{C}\vec{\hat{N}}^{T}\mathcal{C}^{-1}\vec{\hat{N}}~,

(4)

where
\vec{\hat{N}}^{T}=\left(\hat{N}_{1},\hat{N}_{2}\right)
are the charge number operators conjugate to the phase operators
\left(\hat{\varphi}_{1},\hat{\varphi}_{2}\right)
,
E_{C}
is a scale for the charging energy of the circuit (not of the dot), and
\mathcal{C}^{-1}
is the normalized inverse of the capacitance matrix
Vool and Devoret 2017
.
Expressions with offset charge included are given in the appendix.
Combining this with the SPER gives us the full circuit Hamiltonian
H=T+U
which we diagonalize numerically, as described in the Supplementary Material and available in
Rigotti Manesco et al. 2026
.

III.1
Spinless case

For the spinless case, we obtain the energy-phase relation from the full microscopic model shown in the Supplementary Material rather than restricting our analysis to the minimal model captured by Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
).
With this choice, we can also take our analysis beyond the weak coupling limit and target the maximal depth of the double-well potential at
t\sim\Delta
.

First, we inspect the system while varying the circuit charging energy scale
E_{C}
, shown in Fig.
3
(b).
As
E_{C}/E_{0}\rightarrow 0
, the energy splitting between the lowest two levels drops exponentially (Fig.
3
(b)).
Correspondingly, the wavefunctions are localized in the two wells (Fig.
3
(c)).
The phase expectation values for the ground states localized in each well correspond to current flows of different chirality.
We will describe this chirality as an effective degree of freedom characterizing the low-energy quantum states of the circuit.

Next, we consider the behavior of the circuit while tuning one of the electron tunneling rates
t_{1}
relative to the others
t
,
e.g.
, via the electric field effect by adjusting the voltage on a gate electrode.
This is a typical way to activate exchange interactions between spin qubits
Burkard et al. 2023
.
Because time-reversal symmetry is preserved, the wells are always balanced, but they can approach each other or develop a lower barrier between them.
Thereby, the energy splitting of the lowest two states can increase.
This evolution is shown in Fig.
3
(d), with a corresponding probability density map of the lowest two states for a particular
t_{1}
in Fig.
3
(e).

Finally, we consider the effect of an alternating voltage applied to a circuit node with a mediating capacitor.
This is a typical way to excite plasma modes of superconducting qubit circuits
Blais et al. 2021
.
We focus on the transition between the lowest two states, for which we find that the transition dipole matrix element rises exponentially from zero, similar to the energy splitting, as
t_{1}
is varied.
Therefore, full control over the two-level subspace is possible by varying
t_{1}
in combination with charge driving
Zhang et al. 2021
.
We remark that our approach can detect spontaneous emergence of chirality without introducing flux sensitivity or potentially picking up external sources of TRS breaking
Feinberg and Balseiro 2014
.

III.2
Spinful case

Incorporating the spinful part of the Hamiltonian, we find that the levels localized in the wells split into spin-orbit doublets, as shown in Fig.
4
(a).
Here, for simplicity, we use the analytical expressions from Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
).
Expressing the ‘chirality’ degree of freedom with Pauli matrices
\tau
, we can write down a minimal effective Hamiltonian for this subspace as

H_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\sigma_{z}\tau_{z}+\sum_{j}\delta N_{j}\left(\vec{g}_{\sigma,j}\cdot\vec{\sigma}~\tau_{z}+\sigma_{0}\vec{g}_{\tau,j}\cdot\vec{\tau}\right)~,

(5)

with the static term
\propto\epsilon
.
The other two terms of this Hamiltonian are achieved via charge driving (
\delta N_{j}
) and generate spin-flipping,
(\vec{g}_{\sigma,j})_{i}=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{i}\tau_{z}\partial_{j}H)
, and chirality-flipping,
(\vec{g}_{\tau,j})_{i}=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{0}\tau_{i}\partial_{j}H)
, processes.
We focus on the regime for which
\epsilon\sim E_{\sigma}
, valid when
E_{\sigma}\ll\omega_{p}\ll E_{0}
, where
\omega_{p}
is the plasmon frequency.

The spin/chirality structure of the couplings in Eq. (
5
) is a direct consequence of the spin-dependent part of the SPER, shown in Fig.
2
(c-f): near the minimum of the potential wells, different trajectories in phase space correspond to different transverse spin rotations.
Since these behaviors are time-reversed, opposite rotations occur for the same trajectories for opposite chirality.
For the symmetric configuration in particular, we find that driving with symmetric charge
\hat{N}_{+}=\hat{N}_{1}+\hat{N}_{2}
corresponds to an operator of the form
\sigma_{x}\tau_{z}
and driving with antisymmetric charge
\hat{N}_{-}=\hat{N}_{1}-\hat{N}_{2}
corresponds to an operator of the form
\sigma_{y}\tau_{z}
.

These chirality-dependent spin-flipping terms present an opportunity to explore the influence of circularly polarized driving of the two active nodes of the circuit.
We consider simultaneously driving the system with two voltages,
\Omega\sigma_{x}\tau_{z}\cos(\omega t)
and
\pm\Omega\sigma_{y}\tau_{z}\sin(\omega t)
, to obtain

H_{d}=\Omega\left[\sigma_{+}\exp(\pm i\omega t)+\mathrm{h.c.}\right]\tau_{z}~.

(6)

Applying a unitary transformation
U=\exp\left(iH_{\mathrm{eff,0}}t/\hbar\right)
to obtain
H_{d}^{\prime}=UH_{d}U^{\dagger}
, and then applying a rotating wave approximation by assuming
|\hbar\omega-\epsilon|\ll\hbar\omega+\epsilon
, produces

H_{d}^{\prime}=-\frac{\Omega}{2}\sigma_{x}(\tau_{z}\pm 1)~.

(7)

This result indicates chirality-selective spin rotations are possible under resonant, circularly polarized driving.

Field-effect tuning of the electron tunneling rates can also be used for chirality mixing.
This may accomplish spin-independent inter-chirality mixing, proportional to
\tau_{x}
, as in the spinless case.
Here, such (adiabatic) tuning may also induce phase accumulation for states with the same chirality, proportional to
\sigma_{z}\tau_{z}
.
Both processes are shown together in Fig.
4
(d).
Combined with the intra-chirality spin rotations, this gives sufficient generators of SU(4) to accomplish universal control over this four-level subspace.

IV
Discussion

We first estimate the potential for such systems to be realized in the lab.
For
t\sim\Delta
, the barrier between the wells is of the scale of
\Delta/6
.
The ground state energy splitting therefore drops to the scale of
1\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz}
for
E_{C}/h\approx$1\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}$
and
\Delta/h=$45\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}$
(similar to that of thin film aluminum).
For
\Delta
comparable to the superconducting gap of niobium rather than aluminum,
E_{C}/h\approx$10\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}$
would be sufficient.
The spin-dependent contribution to the SPER ranges from
E_{\sigma}/h\sim 0.1-$1\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}$
in two-terminal devices
Tosi et al. 2019
;
Hays et al. 2020
;
Hays et al. 2021
;
Pita-Vidal et al. 2023
;
Pita-Vidal et al. 2024
;
Lu et al. 2025b
and was found to be as large as
9\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}
in a three-terminal device
Coraiola et al. 2024
.
Therefore, we expect that the physics described here should be accessible.
We leave circuits with
E_{\sigma}\gtrsim E_{0}
to be investigated in a future work.

Both the spinless and spinful circuits have potential for use as qubits.
In particular, they host a double-well potential without any loops,
i.e.
, without any magnetic flux parameter.
In conventional Josephson circuits with tunneling junctions, magnetic flux is required to frustrate Josephson energies to produce a double-well potential
Orlando et al. 1999
;
Doucot and Vidal 2002
;
Manucharyan et al. 2009
;
Gyenis et al. 2021a
;
Schrade et al. 2022
;
Hassani et al. 2023
.
This results in a tradeoff: heavier circuits, which suppress energy relaxation, become the most sensitive to flux noise
Gyenis et al. 2021b
;
Hassani et al. 2023
.
Our concept makes possible a new way forward for extremely heavy circuits to suppress energy relaxation without compromises to flux noise.
This approach can use superconductor-semiconductor materials technology that admits control relying purely on the electric field effect rather than unconventional superconductivity
Brosco et al. 2024
.
Similarly, the spinful circuit shows a way forward for Andreev spin qubits without using flux bias to split the spin degeneracy, for which flux noise would also ultimately limit coherence.

We now identify several directions for future theoretical and experimental research.
First, we address extensions of the minimal models.
The physics described here should be inspected again after adding physics to stabilize the odd parity sector, such as charging energy
Pavesic and Zitko 2024
.
This approach has been used extensively with two-terminal Andreev spins
Bargerbos et al. 2022
;
Bargerbos et al. 2023
;
Pita-Vidal et al. 2023
.
However, the physics here is sensitive to the tunneling rate, and it will be important to consider the Yu-Shiba-Rusinov regime
Baran et al. 2023
;
Zalom et al. 2024
.
A three-terminal interacting dot with superconducting leads has been briefly studied in a previous work
Baran et al. 2023
, but its full phase diagram and effects of spin-orbit have not yet been calculated, to the best of our knowledge.
Furthermore, the quantum dynamics of the circuit may also renormalize, particularly in intermediate and high impedance environments
Houzet et al. 2024
;
Güngördü et al. 2025
.

Another direction is to more accurately account for the nonzero size of the dot and other aspects of real devices (
e.g.
, electrostatic effects and actual material band structures).
In our current approximation, the contribution producing the double-well potential grows slowly with the tunneling rate, but the physics of weak links can be nonanalytic in the size
Levchenko et al. 2006
;
Fatemi et al. 2025
.
Therefore, properly accounting for the effects of nonzero size and shape will help to clarify the depth of the double-well potential and the strength of the harmonics in the SPER for realistic devices.
Similarly, accounting for the actual band structure of realistic semiconductor hosts for the normal region (
e.g.
, InAs
Hays et al. 2021
, InSb
Zhang et al. 2025
, and Ge
Lakic et al. 2025
), and their interaction with electrostatics and confinement, will be important for understanding the spin-dependent parts of the SPERs.

Finally, we expect that tiling such multiterminal devices in one- and two-dimensional arrays presents new directions.
A one-dimensional tiling of the spinless case, for example, could produce a pseudospin chain that also protects a disjoint, doubly-degenerate ground state.
The spinful case with weak SOC has a higher-dimensional low-energy Hilbert space due to the four-level low-energy subspace, though strong SOC may return the system to a more strongly-coupled spin-chirality two-level subspace per trijunction.
Then, in one- and two-dimensional arrays, we can envision long-range, non-collinear spin-spin interactions.
Appropriately tuned, these interactions could be used for quantum simulation of quantum phase transitions or spin liquid physics
Dorier et al. 2005
;
Kitaev 2006
.
Finally, spin-spin interactions in a small multiterminal junction array could be useful for quantum error correction or quantum error protection against local spin noise.

Appendix A
Minimal Model Derivation

We consider a set of superconducting leads weakly coupled to a two-orbital normal dot/island with total Hamiltonian:

H=\sum_{n}\Psi_{n}^{\dagger}H_{\text{island}}^{n}\Psi_{n}+\sum_{i}\Phi_{i}^{\dagger}H_{SC}^{i}\Phi_{i}+\sum_{i,j}\left(\Phi_{i}^{\dagger}H_{T}^{i,n}\Psi_{n}+h.c.\right)~,

(8)

where
H_{\text{island}}^{n}
is the island Hamiltonian of the
n
-th orbital,
H_{SC}^{i}
is the Hamiltonian of the
i
-th lead, and
H_{T}^{i,n}
is the hopping from the
i
-th lead to the
n
-th orbital.
The Nambu spinors are
\Psi_{n}=(c_{n\uparrow},c_{n\downarrow},-c_{n\downarrow}^{\dagger},c_{n\uparrow}^{\dagger})
, with
c_{n\sigma}
the annihilation operator of an electron at orbital
n
and spin
\sigma
, and
\Phi_{i}=(f_{i\uparrow},f_{i\downarrow},-f_{i\downarrow}^{\dagger},f_{i\uparrow}^{\dagger})
, where
f_{i\sigma}
are annihilation operators of electrons in the lead
i
with spin
\sigma
.
Each orbital of the island contributes to the Hamiltonian with

H_{\text{island}}^{n}=-\mu_{n}\nu_{z}~,

(9)

where
\nu_{i}
are Pauli matrices acting in the electron-hole space.
For simplicity, we take the flat band limit in which each superconducting lead is described by:

H_{SC}^{i}=\Delta_{i}\nu_{x}~.

(10)

Finally, the hopping between the leads and the island includes effects of Rashba spin-orbit coupling and the orbital field via a Peierls phase:

H_{T}^{i,n}=t_{i}\nu_{z}U_{\text{Peierls}}(\varphi_{i})U_{SOC}^{i}(\alpha_{i,n})~,

(11)

with

U_{\text{Peierls}}(\varphi_{i})=\exp\left(i\frac{\varphi_{i}}{2}\nu_{z}\right)~,\quad U_{SOC}^{i}(\alpha_{i,n})=\exp\left(i\frac{\alpha_{i,n}}{2}\vec{d}_{i}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\right)~,

(12)

where
\alpha_{i}
is the spin-orbit precession angle,
\vec{d}_{i}
is the direction of the spin-orbit field,
\varphi
is the Peierls phase, and
\sigma_{i}
are Pauli matrices acting in the spin space.

We arrive at the effective model by computing the self-energy corrections in the lowest orbital of the island up to fourth order in
t_{i}/\Delta_{i}
and
t_{i}/\mu_{1}
.
We have derived the effective Hamiltonian using both the Pymablock package
Rigotti Manesco et al. 2026
;
Day et al. 2024
and the analytical calculation below.
The first set of corrections corresponds to effective superconducting pairing terms in the normal island, with both second and fourth-order contributions:

\Delta_{2}^{i}(\omega)=H_{T}^{i,0}\frac{1}{(\omega-H_{SC}^{i})}(H_{T}^{i,0})^{\dagger}~,

(13)

and

\Delta_{4}^{ij}(\omega)=H_{T}^{i,0}\frac{1}{(\omega-H_{SC}^{i})}\Delta_{2}^{j}(\omega)\frac{1}{(\omega-H_{SC}^{i})}(H_{T}^{i,0})^{\dagger}~.

(14)

For simplicity, we consider a resonant level,
\mu_{0}=0
, leading to

\Delta_{2}^{i}(0)=-\frac{t_{i}^{2}}{\Delta_{i}}\nu_{\varphi_{i}}~,\quad\Delta_{4}^{i}(0)=\frac{t_{i}^{4}}{\Delta_{i}^{3}}\nu_{\varphi_{i}}~,

(15)

with

\nu_{\varphi}=\nu_{x}\cos\varphi+\nu_{y}\sin\varphi~.

(16)

The second set of corrections come from closed loops involving the higher orbital:

\Sigma_{4}^{ij}(\omega)=H_{T}^{i,0}\frac{1}{(\omega-H_{SC}^{i})}(H_{T}^{i,1})^{\dagger}\frac{1}{(\omega-H_{\text{island}}^{1})}(H_{T}^{j,1})^{\dagger}\frac{1}{(\omega-H_{SC}^{j})}H_{T}^{j,0}~.

(17)

\Sigma_{4}^{ij}=\gamma_{ij}\nu_{\varphi_{i}}U_{SOC}^{i}(\alpha_{i,0}-\alpha_{i,1})\nu_{z}U_{SOC}^{j}(\alpha_{j,1}-\alpha_{j,0})\nu_{\varphi_{j}}~.

(18)

with
\gamma_{ij}=t_{i}^{2}t_{j}^{2}/\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}\mu_{1}
.
We fix the gauge for the spin-orbit field such that
\alpha_{i,0}=\alpha_{i}
,
\alpha_{i,1}=-\alpha_{i}
and define
\varphi_{ij}=\varphi_{i}-\varphi_{j}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{4}^{ij}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{ij}\nu_{z}\exp\left(i\varphi_{ij}\nu_{z}\right)\exp\left(i\alpha_{i}\vec{d}_{i}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\right)\exp\left(-i\alpha_{j}\vec{d}_{j}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\right)

(19)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{ij}\nu_{z}\exp\left(i\varphi_{ij}\nu_{z}\right)\exp\left(i\theta_{ij}\vec{n}_{ij}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\right)~.

(20)

where

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{ij}

\displaystyle=\cos\tilde{\alpha}_{i}\cos\tilde{\alpha}_{j}-\vec{n}_{i}\cdot\vec{n}_{j}\sin\tilde{\alpha}_{i}\sin\tilde{\alpha}_{j}~,

(21)

\displaystyle\vec{n}_{ij}\sin\theta_{ij}

\displaystyle=\vec{n}_{i}\sin\tilde{\alpha}_{i}\cos\tilde{\alpha}_{j}-\vec{n}_{j}\sin\tilde{\alpha}_{j}\cos\tilde{\alpha}_{i}+(\vec{n}_{i}\times\vec{n}_{j})\sin\tilde{\alpha}_{i}\sin\tilde{\alpha}_{j}~,

(22)

and
\vec{n}_{i}=\vec{d}_{i}/|\vec{d}_{i}|
,
\tilde{\alpha}_{i}=|\vec{d}_{i}|\alpha_{i}
.
As a result, each pair of leads contributes

\tilde{\Sigma}_{4}^{ij}=\Sigma_{4}^{ij}+\Sigma_{4}^{ji}=\gamma_{ij}\left[\nu_{z}\cos\varphi_{ij}\cos\theta_{ij}+\nu_{0}~\vec{n}_{ij}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\sin\varphi_{ij}\sin\theta_{ij}\right]~.

(23)

The effective Hamiltonian is then

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\Psi_{0}^{\dagger}H_{\mathrm{eff}}\Psi_{0}~,\quad H_{\mathrm{eff}}=H_{\mathrm{island}}^{0}+\sum_{i}\left[\Delta_{2}^{i}+\sum_{j}\left(\Delta_{4}^{ij}+\Sigma_{4}^{ij}\right)\right]~,

(24)

from which we immediately recover the two-terminal Hamiltonian for symmetric leads:

\sum_{i}\left(\Delta_{2}^{i}+\sum_{j}\Delta_{4}^{ij}\right)=\Gamma\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}\nu_{x}

(25)

with
\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
,
\Gamma=-t^{2}/\Delta+2t^{4}/\Delta^{3}
, and

\Sigma_{4}^{12}+\Sigma_{4}^{21}=\gamma\left(\nu_{z}\cos\alpha\cos\varphi+\nu_{0}\vec{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\sin\alpha\sin\varphi\right)~.

(26)

Finally, we explicitly compute the Hamiltonian for a multiterminal device,

H_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sum_{i,j}E_{0}^{ij}\cos\varphi_{ij}\nu_{z}+\sum_{i}\Gamma_{i}\nu_{\varphi_{i}}+\nu_{0}\sum_{i,j}E_{\sigma}^{ij}~\vec{n}_{ij}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\sin\varphi_{ij}~,

(27)

where
E_{0}^{ij}=\gamma_{ij}\cos\theta_{ij}
,
E_{\sigma}^{ij}=\gamma_{ij}\sin\theta_{ij}
.
Therefore, the odd-parity potential is

U=\sum_{i,j}E_{0}^{ij}\cos\varphi_{ij}+\sum_{i,j}E_{\sigma}^{ij}~\vec{n}_{ij}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\sin\varphi_{ij}~,

(28)

which are the two terms presented in Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
).

Appendix B
Numerics for Solving the Circuit Hamiltonians

We will now consider a 3-terminal ASQ shunted by capacitors, as in the circuit diagram in Fig.
3
(a).
We start by writing the Lagrangian of this circuit:

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\varphi}^{T}C\dot{\varphi}-U(\varphi)

(29)

with
\varphi^{T}=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3})
, and

C=\begin{pmatrix}C_{12}+C_{31}&-C_{12}&-C_{31}\\
-C_{12}&C_{12}+C_{23}&-C_{23}\\
-C_{31}&-C_{23}&C_{23}+C_{31}\end{pmatrix}.

(30)

We choose node 3 to be the reference, so the capacitance matrix reduces to

C_{\mathrm{red}}=\begin{pmatrix}C_{12}+C_{31}&-C_{12}\\
-C_{12}&C_{12}+C_{23}\end{pmatrix}.

(31)

Eliminating
\varphi_{3}
from the Lagrangian, defining the canonical momenta
N_{i}
, and promoting the dynamical variables to quantum operators, we arrive at the following Hamiltonian:

\mathcal{H}=\frac{4e^{2}}{2}\sum_{ij}(\hat{N}_{i}-n_{gi})C_{\mathrm{red}}^{-1}(\hat{N}_{j}-n_{gj})+U(\hat{\varphi}_{1},\hat{\varphi}_{2})~.

(32)

Finally, we define

E_{C1}=\frac{e^{2}}{2}\frac{C_{12}+C_{31}}{\det C_{\mathrm{red}}}~,\quad E_{C2}=\frac{e^{2}}{2}\frac{C_{12}+C_{23}}{\det C_{\mathrm{red}}}~,\quad E_{C12}=e^{2}\frac{C_{12}}{\det C_{\mathrm{red}}}~,

(33)

so that

\mathcal{H}=4E_{C1}(\hat{N}_{1}-n_{g1})^{2}+4E_{C2}(\hat{N}_{2}-n_{g2})^{2}+(\hat{N}_{1}-n_{g1})4E_{C12}(\hat{N}_{2}-n_{g2})+U(\hat{\varphi}_{1},\hat{\varphi}_{2})~.

(34)

We solve the Schrödinger equation numerically using Kwant
Groth et al. 2014
, discretizing the phase variables
\hat{\varphi}_{i}
on a grid.
When we evaluate the spinless model, we obtain the potential
U(\varphi)
by diagonalizing the microscopic Hamiltonian of Eq. (
8
) numerically and computing:

U(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\epsilon_{n}<0}\epsilon_{n}(\varphi)+\sum_{\epsilon_{n}>0}\epsilon_{n}(\varphi)\gamma_{n}^{\dagger}\gamma_{n}~,

(35)

where
\gamma_{n}^{\dagger}
creates a quasiparticle excitation in the groundstate.
In the main text, we use Eq. (
35
) to compute
U(\varphi)
of a spinless system with one quasiparticle,
i.e.

\langle\gamma_{n}^{\dagger}\gamma_{n}\rangle=1
.
For the calculations of the spinful setup, we use the equations (
2
) and (
3
) directly.

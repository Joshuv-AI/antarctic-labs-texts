Uniqueness of Squeezed States for One and Two Modes, and a No-Go Beyond
Arash Azizi

Abstract
We investigate the structure and uniqueness of squeezed vacuum states defined by annihilation conditions of the form ( a − α ​ a † ) ​ | ψ ⟩ = 0 (a-\alpha a^{\dagger})\ket{\psi}=0 and their multimode generalizations. For N = 1 N=1 and N = 2 N=2 , we rigorously show that these conditions uniquely define the standard single- and two-mode squeezed states in the Fock basis. We then analyze a cyclically coupled N N -mode system governed by ( a i − α i ​ a i + 1 † ) ​ | ψ ⟩ = 0 (a_{i}-\alpha_{i}a_{i+1}^{\dagger})\ket{\psi}=0 with a N + 1 ≡ a 1 a_{N+1}\equiv a_{1} . Although the recurrence structure restricts solutions to equal-photon-number states, we prove that for N > 2 N>2 no such state satisfies the full set of conditions. This establishes a sharp no-go result for multipartite squeezed states under cyclic annihilation constraints, underscoring a fundamental structural limitation beyond pairwise squeezing.

Introduction

Squeezed states of light are non-classical quantum states characterized by the reduction of quantum noise in one observable quadrature phase below the vacuum limit, at the expense of increased noise in the conjugate quadrature
caves1981quantum
. This fundamental non-classicality renders them invaluable resources across diverse frontiers of quantum technologies. Their applications span from high-precision measurements, significantly enhancing the sensitivity of gravitational wave detectors like LIGO
LIGO2016
;
aasi2013enhanced
;
Oelker:14
, to the burgeoning fields of quantum information processing, including quantum key distribution
braunstein2005quantum
, quantum computing
Menicucci2006
;
ohliger2010limitations
, and quantum communication protocols such as teleportation and entanglement distribution
braunstein2005quantum
;
van2011optical
;
weedbrook2012gaussian
.

The theoretical description and generation of squeezed states often involve defining them as the vacuum state for a specific set of transformed annihilation operators. This approach provides a powerful framework for understanding their unique properties and their relationship to various quantum optical phenomena. For single-mode systems, this leads to the well-known single-mode squeezed vacuum state, while for two-mode systems, it describes the celebrated two-mode squeezed vacuum state, which is a primary source of continuous-variable entanglement
walls1983squeezed
;
loudon1987squeezed
;
mandel1995optical
.

Beyond single and two-mode scenarios, the exploration of N-mode or multipartite squeezed states has garnered considerable attention due to their potential for even more powerful quantum applications, particularly in quantum networking and distributed quantum computing
vanloock1999
;
grosshans2003quantum
. Various theoretical frameworks have been proposed to define and generate N-mode squeezed and entangled states. These often involve generalized squeezing operators or specific multi-mode interactions designed to create complex correlations among multiple modes. For instance, works have explored N-mode squeezed states generated by generalized squeezing operators that can exhibit rich non-classicality and multipartite entanglement
asavanant2024multipartite
. Other studies focus on different types of multipartite quantum correlations, such as continuous-variable cluster states which are crucial for measurement-based quantum computation
Menicucci2006
.

In this paper, we specifically investigate the uniqueness and structural properties of squeezed states defined by a particular set of annihilation conditions involving a cyclic coupling scheme among the modes:
(a_{i}-\alpha_{i}a_{i+1}^{\dagger})|\psi\rangle=0
, where the
N^{\text{th}}
mode is coupled back to the first (
a_{N+1}\equiv a_{1}
).
While previous works have extensively studied N-mode squeezing with various generalized operators that typically yield non-trivial multipartite entangled states, our distinct contribution lies in rigorously analyzing the implications of this specific cyclic definition. Our primary objective is to determine the unique form of states satisfying these conditions in the Fock basis and to establish their equivalence with states generated by corresponding unitary squeezing operators. Crucially, our analysis reveals surprising and counter-intuitive constraints on the existence of non-trivial N-mode squeezed states for N>2 under this cyclic coupling, a result that contrasts sharply with the rich entanglement properties typically associated with other multi-mode squeezing schemes. 2
Single-Mode Squeezing and Uniqueness

2.1
Uniqueness of the squeezed vacuum state

An important question is whether the standard unitary squeezing operator

\displaystyle S(\xi)=e^{\frac{1}{2}(\xi a^{\dagger 2}-\xi^{*}a^{2})}

(1)

produces the same state as the non-unitary exponential

\displaystyle e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}

(2)

when both act on the vacuum. In other words, we ask whether the following relation holds:

\displaystyle S(\xi)\ket{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}\ket{0},

(3)

where the squeezing parameter is expressed as
\xi=re^{i\theta}
.

The goal of this section is to answer the above question in the affirmative by establishing the uniqueness of the state
\ket{\psi_\alpha}
defined by the condition

(a-\alpha a^{\dagger})\ket{\psi_\alpha}=0.

(4)

We represent
\ket{\psi_\alpha}
as a general combination of Fock space states:

\ket{\psi_\alpha}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}\ket{n}.

(5)

Substituting this into eq. (
4
), we get:

\displaystyle(a-\alpha a^{\dagger})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}\ket{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}\sqrt{n}\ket{n-1}-\alpha\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}\sqrt{n+1}\ket{n+1}=0.

Equating the coefficient of each Fock state
\ket{n}
to zero, we derive a recurrence relation for the coefficients
C_{n}
. For the
\ket{0}
state, the coefficient
C_{1}
(from the
n=1
term in the first sum) implies
C_{1}=0
. Relabeling indices for general
\ket{n}
, we obtain:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n+1}\sqrt{n+1}\ket{n}-\alpha\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{n-1}\sqrt{n}\ket{n}=0.

(6)

For
n\geq 1
, equating the coefficient of
\ket{n}
to zero yields the recurrence relation:

C_{n+1}\sqrt{n+1}=\alpha C_{n-1}\sqrt{n}.

(7)

Since
C_{1}=0
, it follows from eq. (
7
) that
C_{2n+1}=0
for all
n\geq 0
. Iterating the recurrence for even indices, we find:

C_{2n}=\alpha^{n}\sqrt{\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!}}C_{0}.

(8)

Thus, the solution is unique up to the normalization constant
C_{0}
. To find
C_{0}
, we normalize
\ket{\psi_\alpha}
:

\bra{\psi_\alpha}\ket{\psi_\alpha}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|C_{n}|^{2}=|C_{0}|^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha|^{2n}\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!}.

(9)

The sum can be evaluated using the following lemma.

Lemma.
The generating function for the double factorial ratio is given by:

(1-x)^{-1/2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!}x^{n}.

(10)

Proof.
We consider the Taylor series expansion of
f(x)=(1-x)^{-1/2}
around
x=0
. The
n
-th derivative is:

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(1-x)^{-1/2}=\frac{(2n-1)!!}{2^{n}}(1-x)^{-(2n+1)/2}.

(11)

Evaluating at
x=0
, the Taylor coefficient
a_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(1-x)^{-1/2}\Big|_{x=0}
becomes
a_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{(2n-1)!!}{2^{n}}
. Using
(2n)!!=2^{n}n!
, we have
a_{n}=\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!}
. Thus, the Taylor series is:

(1-x)^{-1/2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!}x^{n}.

(12)

This completes the proof. (Note that
(-1)!!=1
.)

Using this lemma, the normalization condition becomes:

\bra{\psi_\alpha}\ket{\psi_\alpha}=|C_{0}|^{2}(1-|\alpha|^{2})^{-1/2}=1.

(13)

This implies
|C_{0}|^{2}=(1-|\alpha|^{2})^{1/2}
, so we can choose
C_{0}=(1-|\alpha|^{2})^{1/4}
(assuming it is real and positive). Hence, the state
\ket{\psi_\alpha}
is
uniquely
determined as:

(a-\alpha a^{\dagger})\ket{\psi_\alpha}=0\quad\text{implies}\quad\ket{\psi_\alpha}=(1-|\alpha|^{2})^{1/4}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{n}\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!}\ket{2n}.

(14)

Condition for
|\alpha|<1

The condition
|\alpha|<1
for the existence of a normalizable state can be derived from the properties of the operators. From eq. (
4
),
a\ket{\psi_\alpha}=\alpha a^{\dagger}\ket{\psi_\alpha}
. Taking the norm of both sides:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\alpha}|a^{\dagger}a|\psi_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\alpha|^{2}\langle\psi_{\alpha}|aa^{\dagger}|\psi_{\alpha}\rangle.

Using the canonical commutation relation
aa^{\dagger}=a^{\dagger}a+1
:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\alpha}|a^{\dagger}a|\psi_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\alpha|^{2}(\langle\psi_{\alpha}|a^{\dagger}a|\psi_{\alpha}\rangle+1).

Let
\bar{n}=\langle\psi_{\alpha}|a^{\dagger}a|\psi_{\alpha}\rangle
be the average number of particles in the state
\ket{\psi_\alpha}
. Then we have:

\bar{n}=|\alpha|^{2}(\bar{n}+1),

(15)

which can be rearranged to solve for
\bar{n}
:

\bar{n}(1-|\alpha|^{2})=|\alpha|^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\bar{n}=\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{1-|\alpha|^{2}}.

(16)

Since the average number of particles
\bar{n}
must be non-negative, and
|\alpha|^{2}\geq 0
, the denominator must be positive, which implies
1-|\alpha|^{2}>0
, or
|\alpha|^{2}<1
.

2.2
Squeezing operator

As a consequence of the uniqueness of the state defined by
(a-\alpha a^{\dagger})\ket{\psi_\alpha}=0
, the standard squeezed vacuum state, generated by the unitary squeezing operator
S(\xi)=e^{\frac{1}{2}(\xi a^{\dagger 2}-\xi^{*}a^{2})}
acting on the vacuum state
\ket{0}
, must take the derived form. The squeezed vacuum state
S(\xi)\ket{0}
satisfies the condition:

(a+\tanh r\,e^{i\theta}a^{\dagger})S(\xi)\ket{0}=0,

(17)

where
\xi=re^{i\theta}
. By comparing this to eq. (
4
), we identify the parameter
\alpha
as:

\boxed{\quad\alpha=-\tanh r\,e^{i\theta}.\quad}

(18)

Consequently, the normalization factor
1-|\alpha|^{2}
becomes:

1-|\alpha|^{2}=1-|-\tanh r\,e^{i\theta}|^{2}=\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}r}.

(19)

Using the unique form of
\ket{\psi_\alpha}
from eq. (
14
) with these parameters, the squeezed vacuum state is expressed as:

S(\xi)\ket{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\tanh r)^{n}\,e^{in(\theta+\pi)}\sqrt{\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!}}\ket{2n}.

(20)

This explicitly shows the form of the standard single-mode squeezed vacuum state in the Fock basis.

We can directly verify that the state
e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}\ket{0}
satisfies (
4
). Using the operator identity
[a,f(a^{\dagger})]=f^{\prime}(a^{\dagger})
, we find

[a,e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}]=\alpha a^{\dagger}e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}.

(21)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle(a-\alpha a^{\dagger})e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}\ket{0}

\displaystyle=\left(e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}a+[a,e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}]-\alpha a^{\dagger}e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}\right)\ket{0}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}a\ket{0}=0.

(22)

Therefore,
e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}\ket{0}
satisfies the same differential equation (
4
) as
\ket{\psi_\alpha}
. Given the uniqueness of the solution established in eq. (
14
) (up to normalization), it follows that the two states must be equivalent. Our uniqueness result guarantees that the relation

\displaystyle\boxed{\quad e^{\frac{1}{2}(\xi a^{\dagger 2}-\xi^{*}a^{2})}\ket{0}=e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}\ket{0},\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha=-\tanh r\,e^{i\theta},\quad}

(23)

holds for all
\xi=re^{i\theta}
. In other words, the unitary squeezing operator
S(\xi)
acting on the vacuum produces the same state as the non-unitary exponential
e^{\frac{1}{2}\alpha a^{\dagger 2}}
, provided the parameters are related via
\alpha=-\tanh r\,e^{i\theta}
.

3
Two-Mode Squeezing: Coupled Annihilation Conditions

3.1
Uniqueness of the Two-Mode Squeezed Vacuum State

Let’s consider two modes as follows:

\displaystyle a\ket{0}_{a}=0,\quad b\ket{0}_{b}=0.

(24)

One may now ignore subscripts
a,b
and use the following shorthand notation:

\displaystyle\ket{m,n}\equiv\ket{m_a}\otimes\ket{n_b}.

(25)

We now extend our analysis to the two-mode case, seeking to determine the uniqueness of a state
\ket{\psi}
defined by coupled differential equations.

\displaystyle(a-\alpha b^{\dagger})\ket{\psi}

\displaystyle=0,

(26)

\displaystyle(b-\beta a^{\dagger})\ket{\psi}

\displaystyle=0.

(27)

Now, let’s find the general solution to these coupled equations. We expand
\ket{\psi}
in the two-mode Fock basis:

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}C_{mn}\ket{m,n}.

(28)

Substituting this into eq. (
26
):

\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}C_{mn}\sqrt{m}\ket{m-1,n}-\alpha\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}C_{mn}\sqrt{n+1}\ket{m,n+1}=0.

(29)

Equating the coefficient of
\ket{m,0}
to zero (for
m\geq 1
), we get
C_{m+1,0}\sqrt{m+1}=0
, implying
C_{m,0}=0
for all
m\geq 1
. Rearranging indices, the recurrence relation from eq. (
26
) is:

C_{m+1,n}\sqrt{m+1}=\alpha C_{m,n-1}\sqrt{n}\quad\Rightarrow\quad C_{m+1,n}=\alpha\sqrt{\frac{n}{m+1}}C_{m,n-1}.

(30)

Similarly, substituting the expansion of
\ket{\psi}
into eq. (
27
):

\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}C_{mn}\sqrt{n}\ket{m,n-1}-\beta\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}C_{mn}\sqrt{m+1}\ket{m+1,n}=0.

(31)

Equating the coefficient of
\ket{0,n}
to zero (for
n\geq 1
), we get
C_{0,n+1}\sqrt{n+1}=0
, implying
C_{0,n}=0
for all
n\geq 1
. Rearranging indices, the recurrence relation from eq. (
27
) is:

C_{m,n+1}\sqrt{n+1}=\beta C_{m-1,n}\sqrt{m}\quad\Rightarrow\quad C_{m,n+1}=\beta\sqrt{\frac{m}{n+1}}C_{m-1,n}.

(32)

The conditions
C_{m,0}=0
for
m\geq 1
and
C_{0,n}=0
for
n\geq 1
imply that only diagonal coefficients
C_{k,k}
can be non-zero. Any off-diagonal coefficient
C_{m,n}
(where
m\neq n
) can be shown to be zero by repeatedly applying the recurrence relations until one of the indices becomes zero, leading to a zero coefficient.
For consistency of both equations, we must have
\alpha=\beta
. With this, the recurrence relations simplify to:

C_{m+1,m+1}=\alpha C_{m,m}.

(33)

Iterating this, we find:

C_{m,m}=\alpha^{m}C_{0,0}.

(34)

Then, the state
\ket{\psi}
can be written as:

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}C_{m,n}\ket{m,n}=C_{0,0}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{m}\ket{m,m}.

(35)

Given the normalization
\bra{\psi}\ket{\psi}=1
, we have:

|C_{0,0}|^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|\alpha|^{2m}=|C_{0,0}|^{2}\frac{1}{1-|\alpha|^{2}}=1.

(36)

This implies
C_{0,0}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha|^{2}}
.
Thus, the coupled equations uniquely determine the state
\ket{\psi}
as:

\displaystyle(a-\alpha b^{\dagger})\ket{\psi}=0\,\,\text{and}\,\,(b-\beta a^{\dagger})\ket{\psi}=0\quad\text{implies}

\displaystyle\alpha=\beta,\,\,\,\text{and}\quad\ket{\psi}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha|^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{m}\ket{m,m}.

(37)

3.2
Transformation of operators using the two-mode squeezing operator

We consider the action of the unitary two-mode squeezing operator
T
on the annihilation operators
a
and
b
. The operator is defined as:

T(\xi)\equiv e^{\xi a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}-\xi^{*}ab},

(38)

where
\xi=re^{i\theta}
is a complex parameter. As
T^{\dagger}=T^{-1}
, we can use the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff (BCH) formula
e^{X}Ye^{-X}=Y+[X,Y]+\frac{1}{2!}[X,[X,Y]]+\cdots
to evaluate
TaT^{\dagger}
. Let
X=\xi a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}-\xi^{*}ab
and
Y=a
.
The first few commutators are:

\displaystyle[X,a]

\displaystyle=[\xi a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}-\xi^{*}ab,a]=-\xi b^{\dagger}

\displaystyle[X,-\xi b^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=[\xi a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}-\xi^{*}ab,-\xi b^{\dagger}]=|\xi|^{2}a=r^{2}a.

Substituting these into the BCH expansion:

\displaystyle TaT^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=a+(-\xi b^{\dagger})+\frac{1}{2!}(r^{2}a)+\frac{1}{3!}(-r^{2}\xi b^{\dagger})+\frac{1}{4!}(r^{4}a)+\cdots

\displaystyle=a\left(1+\frac{r^{2}}{2!}+\frac{r^{4}}{4!}+\cdots\right)-\xi b^{\dagger}\left(1+\frac{r^{2}}{3!}+\frac{r^{4}}{5!}+\cdots\right).

Recognizing the series expansions for
\cosh r
and
\sinh r
:

TaT^{\dagger}=a\cosh r-\xi b^{\dagger}\frac{\sinh r}{r}.

(39)

Given
\xi=re^{i\theta}
, we have

TaT^{\dagger}=\cosh r\,a-e^{i\theta}\sinh r\,b^{\dagger}.

(40)

Similarly, for
TbT^{\dagger}
:

TbT^{\dagger}=\cosh r\,b-e^{i\theta}\sinh r\,a^{\dagger}.

(41)

As previously shown, the unique normalized solution to these equations is given by:

\ket{\psi}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha|^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{n}\ket{n,n}.

(42)

This state can also be expressed in terms of an exponential operator acting on the vacuum:

\ket{\psi}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha|^{2}}\,e^{\alpha a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}}\ket{0,0}.

(43)

The state generated by the unitary two-mode squeezing operator
T(\xi)=e^{\xi a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}-\xi^{*}ab}
acting on the vacuum,
\ket{\text{TMSV}}=T(\xi)\ket{0,0}
, satisfies the coupled differential equations derived from its action on the operators.
From eq. (
40
), we have:

Ta=(\cosh r\,a-e^{i\theta}\sinh r\,b^{\dagger})T.

(44)

Applying this to
\ket{0,0}
:

Ta\ket{0,0}=(\cosh r\,a-e^{i\theta}\sinh r\,b^{\dagger})T\ket{0,0}.

(45)

Since
a\ket{0,0}=0
, the left side is zero. Thus,

(\cosh r\,a-e^{i\theta}\sinh r\,b^{\dagger})T\ket{0,0}=0.

(46)

Dividing by
\cosh r
:

(a-e^{i\theta}\tanh r\,b^{\dagger})T\ket{0,0}=0.

(47)

Similarly, from eq. (
41
), we find:

(b-e^{i\theta}\tanh r\,a^{\dagger})T\ket{0,0}=0.

(48)

Therefore, the coupled equations (
26
) and (
27
) are satisfied by the state
T(\xi)\ket{0,0}
, provided the parameter
\alpha
is identified as
\alpha=\tanh r\,e^{i\theta}
.

Moreover, it is straightforward to verify that the state
e^{\alpha a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}}\ket{0}
, where
\ket{0}=\ket{0_a}\otimes\ket{0_b}
is the two-mode vacuum, also satisfies the same equations when
\alpha=\beta
. This follows from the commutator identity:

\displaystyle(a-\alpha b^{\dagger})e^{\alpha a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}}\ket{0}

\displaystyle=\left(e^{\alpha a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}}a+[a,e^{\alpha a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}}]-\alpha b^{\dagger}e^{\alpha a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}}\right)\ket{0}

\displaystyle=e^{\alpha a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}}a\ket{0}=0.

(49)

A similar calculation confirms that
(b-\alpha a^{\dagger})e^{\alpha a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}}\ket{0}=0
.

Consequently, by the uniqueness result established earlier, we conclude that

\displaystyle\boxed{\quad e^{\xi a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}-\xi^{*}ab}\ket{0}=e^{\alpha a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}}\ket{0},\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha=\tanh r\,e^{i\theta},\quad}

(50)

where
\xi=re^{i\theta}
.

In other words, the unitary two-mode squeezing operator
T(\xi)
acting on the vacuum yields the same state as the non-unitary exponential
e^{\alpha a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}}
, provided the parameters are related by

\displaystyle\alpha=\tanh r\,e^{i\theta}.

(51)

This identification is consistent with the differential equations satisfied by
T(\xi)\ket{0}
, such as eqs. (
47
) and (
48
), which confirm that
T(\xi)\ket{0}
solves the same pair of annihilation conditions as
e^{\alpha a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}}\ket{0}
. Finally, we note that the state
T(\xi)\ket{0,0}
is automatically normalized due to the unitarity of
T(\xi)
:

\|T(\xi)\ket{0,0}\|^{2}=\bra{0,0}T^{\dagger}(\xi)T(\xi)\ket{0,0}=1.

(52)

4
General N-Mode Squeezing: Recurrence Structure and No-Go Result under Cyclic Coupling

4.1
Uniqueness of N-Mode Squeezed State

We generalize squeezed and two-mode squeezed states to the N-mode squeezed state (NMSS). For the N-mode squeezed state, the defining differential equations are given by a set of conditions:

(a_{i}-\alpha_{i}a_{i+1}^{\dagger})|\psi\rangle=0

(53)

for
i=1,2,\dots,N
, where it is understood that
a_{N+1}\equiv a_{1}
due to the cyclic nature of the coupling.

To solve these differential equations defining the N-mode squeezed state, one follows a similar procedure as for the simpler cases. We express the N-mode state as a superposition of Fock states:

|\psi\rangle=\sum_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}|m_{1},\dots,m_{N}\rangle\,,

(54)

where
|m_{1},\dots,m_{N}\rangle=|m_{1}\rangle\otimes\dots\otimes|m_{N}\rangle
represents a multi-mode Fock state.

Applying the operator
(a_{i}-\alpha_{i}a_{i+1}^{\dagger})
to the state
|\psi\rangle
for a fixed
i
yields:

(a_{i}-\alpha_{i}a_{i+1}^{\dagger})\sum_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}|m_{1},\dots,m_{N}\rangle=0\,.

(55)

This leads to the expansion

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\Big(

\displaystyle\sqrt{m_{i}}|m_{1},\dots,m_{i}-1,\dots,m_{N}\rangle

\displaystyle-\alpha_{i}\sqrt{m_{i+1}+1}\lx@nobreakspace|m_{1},\dots,m_{i+1}+1,\dots,m_{N}\rangle\Big)=0\,,

(56)

where the sum is performed over all possible values of
m_{1},\dots,m_{N}
.

4.2
Recurrence Relations for Coefficients

By relabeling the summation indices in the preceding equation such that the Fock states are common in each term, we can write the expression as:

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\Big(C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{i}+1,\dots,m_{N}}\sqrt{m_{i}+1}-\alpha_{i}\sqrt{m_{i+1}}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{i},\dots,m_{i+1}-1,\dots,m_{N}}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\ket{m_1, \dots, m_i, \dots, m_{i+1}, \dots, m_N}=0\,.

(57)

Since the Fock states
|m_{1},\dots,m_{N}\rangle
form an orthonormal basis, the coefficient of each basis vector must vanish independently. This leads to a recurrence relation for the coefficients
C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}
for a fixed
i
:

C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{i}+1,\dots,m_{N}}=\alpha_{i}\sqrt{\frac{m_{i+1}}{m_{i}+1}}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{i},\dots,m_{i+1}-1,\dots,m_{N}}\,.

(58)

This recurrence relation establishes a connection between coefficients of states differing in the excitation numbers of modes
i
and
i+1
. Alternatively, the recurrence relation can be expressed as:

C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}=\alpha_{i}\sqrt{\frac{m_{i+1}}{m_{i}}}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{i}-1,m_{i+1}-1,\dots,m_{N}}\,.

(59)

This implies that non-zero coefficients are obtained when
m_{i}
and
m_{i+1}
decrease simultaneously.

Consider the case where
m_{i+1}\geq m_{i}
. By repeatedly applying this recurrence relation
m_{i}
times, we can reduce the value of
m_{i}
to zero. After
m_{i}
iterations, we obtain:

\displaystyle C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}^{m_{i}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{i+1}(m_{i+1}-1)\dots(m_{i+1}-m_{i}+1)}{m_{i}(m_{i}-1)\dots(1)}}C_{m_{1},\dots,0,m_{i+1}-m_{i},\dots,m_{N}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}^{m_{i}}\sqrt{\binom{m_{i+1}}{m_{i}}}C_{m_{1},\dots,0,m_{i+1}-m_{i},\dots,m_{N}}\,.

(60)

This general form allows us to systematically determine the coefficients. For example, by applying this iteratively for
i=1,2,\dots,N-1
:

C_{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},\dots,m_{N}}=\alpha_{1}^{m_{1}}\sqrt{\binom{m_{2}}{m_{1}}}C_{0,m_{2}-m_{1},m_{3},\dots,m_{N}}\,.

(61)

Then, applying the recurrence for
i=2
to the remaining coefficient:

C_{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},\dots,m_{N}}=\alpha_{1}^{m_{1}}\sqrt{\binom{m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\alpha_{2}^{m_{2}-m_{1}}\sqrt{\binom{m_{3}}{m_{2}-m_{1}}}C_{0,0,m_{3}-(m_{2}-m_{1}),\dots,m_{N}}\,.

(62)

This process continues until all but one mode (or a subset depending on the cyclic condition) has been reduced to zero, provided the conditions on mode numbers are met at each step.

Conversely, consider the case where
m_{i}\geq m_{i+1}
. We can use the inverse of the recurrence relation:

C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{i}-1,m_{i+1}-1,\dots,m_{N}}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{i}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{i}}{m_{i+1}}}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{i},m_{i+1},\dots,m_{N}}\,.

(63)

This can be rewritten to increase the indices
m_{i}
and
m_{i+1}
until
m_{i+1}
becomes zero. By applying it
m_{i+1}
times:

\displaystyle C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}^{-m_{i+1}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{i}(m_{i}+1)\dots(m_{i}+m_{i+1}-1)}{m_{i+1}(m_{i+1}-1)\dots(1)}}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{i}+m_{i+1},0,\dots,m_{N}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}^{-m_{i+1}}\sqrt{\binom{m_{i}+m_{i+1}-1}{m_{i+1}}}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{i}+m_{i+1},0,\dots,m_{N}}\,.

(64)

These two cases cover the iteration of the recurrence relation for any pair of adjacent modes
(i,i+1)
.

4.3
Structural Properties of the N-Mode Squeezed State

Let us revisit the defining master equation
a_{i}|\psi\rangle=\alpha_{i}a_{i+1}^{\dagger}|\psi\rangle
. The action of
a_{i}
on a state component
|m_{1},\dots,m_{N}\rangle
reduces the total particle number (sum of
m_{k}
) by one, while the action of
a_{i+1}^{\dagger}
increases it by one. For this equality to hold, the state
|\psi\rangle
must be a superposition of Fock states with components strictly of even total particle number, which is characteristic of a squeezed vacuum state.

More profoundly, the recurrence relations derived for the coefficients
C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}
imply a very specific structure for the state
|\psi\rangle
. For non-zero coefficients
C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}
, it is necessary that
m_{i}\leq m_{i+1}
for all
i=1,\dots,N-1
. To demonstrate this, consider a specific term in the expansion of
|\psi\rangle
where for some
i
,
m_{i}\neq 0
and
m_{i+1}=0
, i.e., a coefficient
C_{\dots,m_{i},0,\dots}
with
m_{i}>0
. Applying
a_{i}
to such a term yields:

a_{i}|m_{1},\dots,m_{i},0_{i+1},\dots,m_{N}\rangle=\sqrt{m_{i}}|m_{1},\dots,m_{i}-1,0_{i+1},\dots,m_{N}\rangle\,.

(65)

Conversely, applying
\alpha_{i}a_{i+1}^{\dagger}
to the same term yields:

\alpha_{i}a_{i+1}^{\dagger}|m_{1},\dots,m_{i},0_{i+1},\dots,m_{N}\rangle\lx@nobreakspace=\alpha_{i}|m_{1},\dots,m_{i},1_{i+1},\dots,m_{N}\rangle\,.

(66)

For the master equation
a_{i}|\psi\rangle=\alpha_{i}a_{i+1}^{\dagger}|\psi\rangle
to hold, the coefficients of each distinct Fock state must independently vanish. As the states
|m_{1},\dots,m_{i}-1,0_{i+1},\dots,m_{N}\rangle
and

|m_{1},\dots,m_{i},1_{i+1},\dots,m_{N}\rangle
are orthogonal for
m_{i}>0
, the presence of such terms would lead to a contradiction unless their coefficients are zero. This implies that if
m_{i+1}=0
, then
m_{i}
must also be
0
for non-zero coefficients
C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}
(unless it is the vacuum state). Therefore, for all non-zero coefficients, it must be that
m_{i}\leq m_{i+1}
for all
i=1,\dots,N-1
.

Considering the cyclic coupling condition, where
a_{N+1}\equiv a_{1}
, we also have
m_{N}\leq m_{1}
. Combining these inequalities yields:

m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq\dots\leq m_{N}\leq m_{1}\,.

(67)

This set of inequalities can only be satisfied if all the mode excitation numbers are equal:

m_{1}=m_{2}=\dots=m_{N}=k\,.

(68)

This profound result indicates that, for the given definition of the N-mode squeezing operator, the state
|\psi\rangle
must be a superposition of Fock states where each mode has an equal number of excitations, i.e.,
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{k}C_{k}|k,k,\dots,k\rangle
.

However, for
N>2
, this conclusion leads to a contradiction. If we substitute
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{k}C_{k}|k,k,\dots,k\rangle
back into the master equation
(a_{i}-\alpha_{i}a_{i+1}^{\dagger})|\psi\rangle=0
, we get:

\sum_{k}C_{k}\left(\sqrt{k}|k,\dots,k-1_{i},\dots,k,\dots\rangle-\alpha_{i}\sqrt{k+1}|k,\dots,k_{i},\dots,k+1_{i+1},\dots\rangle\right)=0\,.

(69)

For
N>2
, the Fock states
|k,\dots,k-1_{i},\dots,k,\dots\rangle
and
|k,\dots,k_{i},\dots,k+1_{i+1},\dots\rangle
are distinct and orthogonal for any
k>0
. For the entire sum to be zero, the coefficient of each individual Fock state must be zero. This implies that
C_{k}\sqrt{k}=0
and
C_{k}\alpha_{i}\sqrt{k+1}=0
for all
k
. This forces
C_{k}=0
for all
k>0
. The only remaining non-zero coefficient can be
C_{0}
, corresponding to the vacuum state
|0,0,\dots,0\rangle
. Thus, for
N>2
, the N-mode squeezed state defined by this cyclic operator reduces to the vacuum state.

The only exception to this trivial result is the case when
N=2
. For
N=2
, the states
|k-1_{1},k_{2}\rangle
and
|k_{1},k+1_{2}\rangle
are precisely the two types of states involved, and the coefficients
C_{k,k}
can be non-zero, as is well-known for the two-mode squeezed vacuum state. The structure of the operator only affecting two indices is the key reason for this limitation to
N=2
for non-trivial squeezing.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have systematically investigated the uniqueness and structural properties of squeezed states, defined by annihilation conditions in increasing complexity from one to N modes.

For single-mode systems, we rigorously demonstrated that the state annihilated by
(a-\alpha a^{\dagger})
is uniquely determined up to a normalization constant. This state is shown to be equivalent to the standard single-mode squeezed vacuum state generated by a unitary squeezing operator, with a clear relationship established between the state parameter
\alpha
and the operator parameter
\xi
. The non-negativity of the average particle number necessitates the condition
|\alpha|<1
for a normalizable state.

Extending our analysis to two-mode systems, we found that the state defined by coupled annihilation conditions
(a-\alpha b^{\dagger})|\psi\rangle=0
and
(b-\beta a^{\dagger})|\psi\rangle=0
is also uniquely determined. This uniqueness implies that the coefficients in the Fock basis are non-zero only for equal excitation numbers in both modes (
m=n
), requiring
\alpha=\beta
. This uniquely defined state is equivalent to the well-known two-mode squeezed vacuum state generated by the unitary two-mode squeezing operator, providing a consistent framework for understanding its properties.

The investigation of N-mode squeezing under a cyclic coupling scheme,
(a_{i}-\alpha_{i}a_{i+1}^{\dagger})|\psi\rangle=0
, revealed a profound structural property. Our derivation of recurrence relations for the Fock coefficients showed that for non-trivial states to exist, the mode excitation numbers must satisfy a chain of inequalities,
m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq\dots\leq m_{N}\leq m_{1}
, which logically implies
m_{1}=m_{2}=\dots=m_{N}=k
. This suggested that the N-mode squeezed state would be a superposition of states where all modes possess an identical number of excitations, i.e.,
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{k}C_{k}|k,k,\dots,k\rangle
.

However, a crucial implication arises for systems with
N>2
. By substituting this proposed form of the state back into the defining master equation, we found that the equation can only be satisfied if all coefficients
C_{k}
for
k>0
are zero. This leads to the unexpected conclusion that, for
N>2
, the N-mode squeezed state, as defined by the cyclic operator
(a_{i}-\alpha_{i}a_{i+1}^{\dagger})
, reduces trivially (i.e., if all coefficients
\alpha_{i}=0
) to the vacuum state. The only exception is the
N=2
case, where the standard two-mode squeezed vacuum state, characterized by identical excitation numbers in each mode, remains a valid non-trivial solution. This highlights that the specific form of the cyclic coupling operator, which affects only two adjacent indices at a time, imposes severe restrictions on the possibility of generating entangled N-mode squeezed states for
N>2
in this framework.

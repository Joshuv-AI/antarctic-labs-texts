Analysis of the 36-month efficacy and safety of Kahook dual blade goniotomy combined with phacoemulsification in Chinese patients with open-angle glaucoma
Authors: Jian Yu, Yachen Wang, Huan Guan, Lijun Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo evaluate the efficacy and safety of Kahook dual blade (KDB) goniotomy combined with phacoemulsification for open-angle glaucoma in Chinese patients.MethodsWe retrospectively enrolled 78 Chinese patients (100 eyes) with open-angle glaucoma who underwent KDB goniotomy combined with phacoemulsification at the Third People’s Hospital of Dalian, Liaoning Province, China, between September 2021 and December 2024, of whom 63 eyes had primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) and 37 eyes had pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (PXFG). Intraocular pressure (IOP), the number of glaucoma medications used preoperatively and at 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, 3, 6, 12, and 36 months postoperatively, surgical success rates, and surgical complications were recorded. Surgical success was defined as: Criterion A (IOP reduction ≥ 20% with IOP ≤ 21 mmHg), Criterion B (IOP reduction ≥ 20% with IOP ≤ 18 mmHg), and Criterion C (IOP reduction ≥ 20% with IOP ≤ 15 mmHg).ResultsThe mean IOP reduced from 25.25 ± 6.54 mmHg at baseline to 14.09 ± 3.48 mmHg at 36 months (P < 0.01). The number of medications decreased from 3.0 (2.0, 4.0) to 1.0 (0.0, 2.0) at 36 months (P < 0.01). Surgical success rates at 36 months, defined by Criteria A, B, and C, were 76.32%, 67.11%, and 48.68%. The mean IOP reduced from 24.57 ± 5.71 mmHg at baseline to 14.83 ± 3.35 mmHg at 36 months for POAG (P < 0.01) and from 26.41 ± 7.70 mmHg at baseline to 12.90 ± 3.40 mmHg at 36 months for PXFG (P < 0.01).The number of medications decreased from 3.0 (2.0, 4.0) to 1.0 (0.0, 2.0) for POAG (P < 0.01) and from 3.0 (2.0, 4.0) to 0.0 (0.0, 1.0) for PXFG (P < 0.01) at 36 months. Between the two groups, IOP showed no statistical difference at baseline, 3 months, and 36 months (P > 0.05). At 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, 6 months, 12 months, and 24 months, IOP in the PXFG group was significantly lower than that in the POAG group (P < 0.05). From 3 months to 36 months, the number of medications in the PXFG group was significantly lower than that in the POAG group (P < 0.05). Surgical success rates at 36 months according to Criterion A were 72.34% in POAG and 82.76% in PXFG (P = 0.127); according to Criterion B, 59.57% vs. 79.31% (P = 0.087); and according to Criterion C, 42.55% vs. 62.07% (P = 0.036). Twenty-four-hour IOP fluctuations at 12 months were 4.02 ± 2.01 mmHg in POAG and 3.69 ± 1.31 mmHg in PXFG, both significantly decreased compared with baseline (10.02 ± 3.58 mmHg and 10.39 ± 3.32 mmHg, respectively; P < 0.01 for both groups). Common surgical complications included hyphema and IOP spike.ConclusionsKDB goniotomy combined with phacoemulsification was safe and effectively reduced IOP and the medication burden in patients with POAG and PXFG. In the first and second year, significantly lower IOP was noted in PXFG compared with POAG.

Introduction

In recent years, glaucoma surgical approaches have evolved significantly, with the introduction of minimally invasive ab interno surgical techniques. These innovations have emerged as effective alternatives, demonstrating efficacy in reducing intraocular pressure (IOP) while avoiding complications inherent to conventional trabeculectomy, such as filtering blebs associated with traditional procedures [1–3]. The Kahook Dual Blade (KDB), a surgical technique pioneered by Professor Kahook at the University of Colorado School of Medicine, employs a single-use blade specifically engineered for trabecular meshwork excision. This device received regulatory approval in China in 2019 for glaucoma treatment. The KDB technique enables ab interno excision of trabecular meshwork tissue along the inner wall of Schlemm’s canal across an extended arc, thereby creating direct aqueous humor outflow pathways into Schlemm’s canal and collector channels. This mechanism increases the effective drainage area while reducing aqueous outflow resistance. Key procedural advantages include minimal corneal incision dimensions, preservation of peri-trabecular anatomical integrity, elimination of conjunctival and scleral manipulation, and complete independence from filtering bleb formation [4, 5].

Published studies have demonstrated meaningful therapeutic efficacy of KDB goniotomy combined with phacoemulsification across diverse clinical subtypes of open-angle glaucoma [6–19]. One report suggests that a stand-alone KDB procedure is more effective in eyes with pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (PXFG) than in those with primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) [18]. Another report suggests that the KDB procedure with cataract surgery achieves greater IOP reduction in eyes with PXFG than in eyes with POAG [20]. A previous study demonstrated that KDB goniotomy is safe and effective for Chinese patients with POAG [21]. However, there remains a paucity of data regarding the efficacy and safety profiles of this combined surgical approach (KDB goniotomy with phacoemulsification) in the Chinese open-angle glaucoma population. This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of the KDB goniotomy combined with phacoemulsification in Chinese patients with open-angle glaucoma. The incidence of PXFG shows regional variations. This study specifically examines the surgical outcomes between POAG and PXFG in northern China, with a comparative analysis of treatment results between these glaucoma subtypes. The investigation sought to validate the therapeutic performance of KDB in glaucoma surgical management through this targeted subgroup comparison.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

This single-center retrospective cohort study consecutively enrolled patients from Dalian Third People’s Hospital (Liaoning Province, China), with all surgical procedures performed by a single senior ophthalmologist specializing in glaucoma surgery. The study adhered to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and approval was obtained from the local ethics committee. All patient data underwent rigorous de-identification processes, and the ethics committee granted a waiver of informed consent requirements in accordance with national regulations governing retrospective analyses of anonymized clinical records. The study consecutively enrolled 78 patients (100 eyes) undergoing KDB goniotomy combined with phacoemulsification between September 2021 and December 2024.

Inclusion Criteria: (1) Age ≥ 18 years; (2) Confirmed diagnosis of mild-severe POAG or PXFG with concomitant cataract meeting surgical intervention criteria; (3) Documented progression of glaucomatous optic neuropathy (elevated intraocular pressure and visual field deterioration) despite maximal medical therapy; (4) Open anterior chamber angle confirmed by gonioscopy.

Patients were excluded if they presented with corneal opacity or other anterior segment anomalies compromising gonioscopic visualization, secondary glaucoma etiologies (including active uveitis, neovascular glaucoma, or angle dysgenesis), systemic conditions associated with elevated episcleral venous pressure (e.g., Sturge-Weber syndrome), or sight-compromising retinal comorbidities such as advanced diabetic retinopathy.

Methods

Preoperative examination and data collection

All patients underwent a standardized preoperative examination: (1) best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), the logarithm of the minimal angle of resolution (logMAR). Eyes without form vision were classified into 1 of 4 low-vision categories, which were assigned decimal equivalents as follows: counting fingers 0.00500, hand motions 0.00250, light perception 0.00125, and no light perception 0.00010; (2) IOP measurement via non-contact tonometry (triplicate measurements averaged). Central corneal thickness (CCT) was measured in all participants, and all values were within the normal range. Therefore, IOP values obtained by non-contact tonometry were not further adjusted for CCT; (3) 24-hour IOP monitoring was measured using a non-contact tonometer at fixed time points: 8:00, 10:00, 12:00, 16:00, 18:00, 20:00, 22:00, 0:00, 2:00, 4:00, 6:00 in a sitting position under consistent indoor lighting conditions. For nocturnal measurements, IOP was measured 10 min after the patient had changed from a supine position to an upright position. All measurements were performed by a well-trained ophthalmologist, and the average of three consecutive measurements was recorded for each time point. The average of three consecutive measurements was recorded for each time point. Fluctuation was calculated as the difference between peak and trough values; (4) visual field evaluation employing the Humphrey Field Analyzer 24 − 2 strategy (Swedish Interactive Threshold Algorithm standard). We classified the severity of glaucoma into three stages using the mean defect (MD) score on Humphrey visual field tests: MD ≥ − 6.0 dB, mild; −6.0 > MD ≥ − 12.0 dB, moderate; and MD < − 12.0 dB, severe. Preoperative IOP-lowering regimens comprised systemic medications (e.g., acetazolamide) and topical ophthalmic agents, including beta-blockers, alpha-adrenergic agonists, topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, and prostaglandin analogs. Fixed-combination formulations were systematically separated and counted as individual pharmacological agents based on their active components for quantitative analysis.

Surgical procedure

Following topical anesthesia, a 2.8 mm corneal limbal incision was created in the superotemporal quadrant to perform standard phacoemulsification with intraocular lens implantation. The surgeon then optimized visualization of the angle structures by adjusting both the patient’s head position and microscope tilt angle. The anterior chamber was filled with 1% sodium hyaluronate viscoelastic material. The KDB was inserted through the corneal incision into the anterior chamber and directed to the trabecular meshwork, and KDB goniotomy was performed as planned (a 120-degree clockwise arc). Finally, complete evacuation of viscoelastic material was achieved through irrigation/aspiration, followed by anterior chamber reformation with balanced salt solution. The primary incision was hydrated to achieve watertight closure.

Management and treatment

Preoperative glaucoma medications were discontinued on the day of surgery. All patients received standardized postoperative topical pharmacotherapy comprising: 0.3% gatifloxacin ophthalmic solution (2–3 weeks); 1% prednisolone acetate ophthalmic suspension (4 weeks); and 0.5% pilocarpine hydrochloride solution (3 months). Postoperatively, all patients received topical pilocarpine four times daily for 3 months. This empirical regimen was administered to prevent the formation of peripheral anterior synechiae (PAS) and to help maintain angle patency during the wound-healing period following goniotomy. Subsequent adjustments to the intraocular pressure (IOP)-lowering regimen were made during follow-up visits based on serial IOP measurements, target IOP, and anterior chamber inflammatory activity. Target IOP was determined preoperatively according to the severity of glaucoma in each patient. If IOP remained above the target level, topical hypotensive agents were reinstituted or titrated to achieve and maintain the target IOP.

Follow-up protocol and data collection

Patients were examined at 1 day, 1 week, 1month, 3months, 6months, 12months, 24months, and 36 months postoperatively. Basic demographic characteristics such as age, gender, and primary diagnosis were recorded. During follow-up, patient data, including glaucoma severity, best corrected visual acuity (BCVA), IOP values, the number of glaucoma medications used, and postoperative complications, were collected. In terms of postoperative complications, hyphema was quantitatively defined as anterior chamber blood accumulation exceeding 1 mm in vertical height within the first postoperative week [22], and an IOP spike was defined as an increase in IOP of > 10 mmHg or IOP ≥ 30 mmHg after surgery [23].

Surgical success criteria

The primary outcome was surgical success or failure, which was defined according to three IOP criteria. Success was defined according to the postoperative IOP level with or without glaucoma medication at ≥ 3 months after surgery as follows: ≥20% reduction in the preoperative IOP value and an IOP ≤ 21 mmHg (Criterion A), ≥ 20% reduction in the preoperative IOP value and an IOP ≤ 18 mmHg (Criterion B), ≥ 20% reduction in the preoperative IOP value and an IOP ≤ 15 mmHg (Criterion C). We also deemed cases that required reoperation for glaucoma as surgical failures.

Statistical methodology

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 software (Statistical Products and Services Solutions, Inc., USA). The normality of measurement data was verified using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Measurement data with a normal distribution were expressed as x̄ ± s, while those with skewed distribution were expressed as M (Q1, Q3). Categorical data were presented as frequency and percentage. Baseline data comparisons between the two groups were conducted using the independent samples t-test, Mann-Whitney U test, and χ2 test. Reductions in IOP and the number of glaucoma medications were analyzed using the linear mixed model, in which patients were treated as a random effect and time point as a fixed effect, to minimize potential bias associated with the inclusion of both eyes from the same patient. Analyses were adjusted for the effects of sex, age, and preoperative visual field defects on IOP and medication use, and post hoc multiple comparisons were performed with the Bonferroni correction. Non-normal distribution measurement data were compared using the Friedman M-test, and multiple comparisons were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to calculate the cumulative surgical success rate between the two groups, and the difference in surgical success rates was verified using the log-rank test. A two-tailed test was employed, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Medication reduced from 3.0 (2.0, 4.0) to 1.0 (0.0, 2.0) at 36 months (P < 0.01). As presented in Table 2, comparisons were made between the POAG and PXFG groups regarding longitudinal changes in the IOP and the number of glaucoma medications used throughout the follow-up period. The mean IOP decreased from 24.57 ± 5.71 mmHg at baseline to 14.83 ± 3.35 mmHg at 36 months for POAG (P < 0.01) and from 26.41 ± 7.70 mmHg at baseline to 12.90 ± 3.40 mmHg at 36 months for PXFG (P < 0.01). Postoperative complications

Common postoperative complications included hyphema, IOP spike, and additional glaucoma surgery. Hyphema occurred in 17 eyes (17%, 7 POAG eyes and 10 PXFG eyes), and all hyphema resolved spontaneously without intervention within one week. IOP spike occurred in 23 eyes (23%, 11 POAG eyes and 12 PXFG eyes). Among them, IOP spike occurred in 2 eyes (2 PXFG eyes) at the 3rd month postoperatively. Among the severe patients in both groups, one eye in each group underwent trabeculectomy. None of the eyes included in the study developed severe complications such as shallow anterior chamber, malignant glaucoma, choroidal detachment, or endophthalmitis after the surgery.

Discussion

This study evaluated the 36-month surgical outcomes of KDB goniotomy combined with phacoemulsification in Chinese patients with open-angle glaucoma, comparing outcomes between POAG and PXFG. There was a statistically significant difference in visual fields preoperatively between the two groups, attributable to the higher proportion of advanced-stage patients in the PXFG group compared to the POAG group.

Our study showed that postoperative mean IOP and the number of glaucoma medications were significantly lower than preoperative values in all patients (P < 0.01). There was no significant difference in mean IOP between the two groups at 3 months and 36 months postoperatively. The PXFG group had significantly lower IOP than the POAG group from 1 day to 24 months except 3 months postoperatively (P < 0.05). Both groups exhibited a significant reduction in postoperative medication use. From 3 months to 36 months, the PXFG group had a significantly lower medication count than the POAG group, reflecting lower medication dependence. The above results are highly consistent with the pathophysiological mechanism of PXFG. It is well established that the core mechanism underlying elevated IOP in PXFG patients is the deposition of extracellular exfoliative material in the trabecular meshwork, which disrupts trabecular matrix metabolism, impairs the phagocytic function of trabecular cells, and ultimately obstructs aqueous humor outflow [24, 25]. KDB goniotomy can resect the diseased trabecular meshwork tissue and may simultaneously clear the exfoliative material deposited in the trabecular meshwork through a mechanical effect. But the persistent deposition of exfoliated material leads to obstruction of drainage pathways. This may explain why the PXFG group achieved better IOP control and lower medication dependence from 1 to 24 months postoperatively, and there was no significant difference in mean IOP between the two groups at 36 months.

There are many reports on the surgical outcomes (IOP, the number of glaucoma medications used, success rates, complications) of KDB procedure combined with phacoemulsification [18, 19, 24, 26–29]. Prior studies utilizing the Kaplan-Meier method for analyzing glaucoma surgical success rates have reported graded differences in success rates across various evaluation criteria, with follow-up success rates ranging from 36% to 97.1% [18, 19, 29]. We evaluated the probability of success of combined KDB and phacoemulsification over 36 months postoperatively. The probability of success was 78% (Criterion A), 68% (Criterion B), and 49% (Criterion C). Some studies reported that there was no difference between the POAG and PXFG groups in terms of success [5, 19, 29], whereas other studies have demonstrated higher success rates in PXFG than in POAG [18, 28]. These discrepancies in reported success rates may relate to variations in disease severity, preoperative IOP levels, follow-up duration, and success criteria across studies. Our results demonstrated no statistically significant differences between the two groups under Criteria A and B, whereas the cumulative surgical success rate of PXFG was significantly higher than that of POAG under Criterion C, with this advantage sustained throughout the 36-month follow-up period. The 36-month long-term follow-up data in this study addresses the limitations of short-term studies, confirming the durability of surgical efficacy for PXFG. We anticipate the results of studies with follow-up periods of 5 years or longer.

A report indicated that for mild to moderate PXFG patients controlled with 1–2 medications, phacoemulsification alone can achieve a moderate reduction in IOP and medication burden [30]. Phacoemulsification is known to reduce IOP more in eyes with PXFG compared to eyes with POAG, particularly in the first 2 years, and the underlying mechanism is thought to be the washout of the exfoliation material. We expect that a comparative group will be established in the future to clarify the effects of cataract surgery and combined surgery on the outcomes, and it will also be necessary to compare the two types of glaucoma in patients with eyes that undergo KDB goniotomy alone.

Both POAG and PXFG exhibit significant 24-hour IOP fluctuations, which are major risk factors for glaucomatous progression [31]. No studies to date have compared 24-hour IOP fluctuations between these two groups following KDB goniotomy combined with phacoemulsification. Our study demonstrated that circadian IOP fluctuations at 12 months postoperatively were significantly reduced in both POAG and PXFG groups compared to preoperative levels, indicating that this surgical approach effectively mitigates both mean IOP and 24-hour IOP fluctuations.

This study demonstrated similar incidence rates of hyphema and IOP spike between the two groups. Published studies report hyphema incidence ranging from 8.4% to 34.9% and IOP spike rates of 1.0%–17.0% following KDB goniotomy [11, 17, 22, 28, 29, 32], which aligns with the findings of the present study. Some authors suggest that IOP spike following minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) may be associated with postoperative hyphema and transient inflammatory responses [1, 5]. Other authors have postulated that the abovementioned scenario may be associated with shallow ciliary body detachment or resolution of supraciliary effusion leading to IOP spike [33]. We recommend minimizing angle hemorrhage during surgery, intensifying postoperative anti-inflammatory therapy, and performing early anterior segment OCT examinations to evaluate gonioscopic structures and detect ciliary body detachment. Published studies indicate that IOP spike typically occurs within the first month postoperatively [1, 7, 28]. In our study, IOP spike occurred in one eye at the 3rd month. IOP was controlled within the target range for 2 weeks after administration of glaucoma medications and close follow-up monitoring. IOP remained within the target range during the 36-month follow-up period after discontinuation of medication. This suggests that IOP spike may occur within 3 months postoperatively, which does not indicate surgical failure. Therefore, early secondary surgery should be avoided. In this study, one eye from each subgroup of severe-stage patients in both groups failed to achieve the target IOP and showed visual field progression despite maximal medical therapy, necessitating secondary surgery at 1 year postoperatively. By avoiding disruption of the conjunctiva and sclera while preserving the physiological structure of the majority of the trabecular meshwork, KDB goniotomy offers a viable treatment option for patients requiring subsequent filtering glaucoma surgery or those who have failed previous filtering procedures.

The routine use of postoperative pilocarpine for 3 months remains a topic of debate in the literature. Some studies have suggested that pilocarpine may assist in reducing PAS and maintain angle patency in the early postoperative period [34], whereas others have failed to show a significant beneficial effect [35]. In our clinical practice, 3-month topical pilocarpine administration was implemented with the aim of inducing a stretching effect on the Schlemm’s canal; theoretically, this reduces the risk of scarring and peripheral PAS formation, and helps sustain the patency of the goniotomy site during the healing phase. A potential limitation of the present study is the routine use of postoperative pilocarpine for 3 months, which may act as a confounder when interpreting the study outcomes. Although this regimen reflects the common clinical practice at our institution, its miotic effects could have influenced postoperative IOP control, anterior chamber reaction, and the incidence of synechiae. Therefore, the observed results should be interpreted with caution, and future randomized controlled trials (RCTs) with standardized anti-glaucoma medication regimens are warranted to eliminate this confounding factor.

The advantages of this study lie in its focus on the population of Chinese open-angle glaucoma patients, relatively long follow-up time, and comparison of surgical outcomes between POAG and PXFG eyes, especially through 24-hour IOP monitoring. Our study highlights the value of KDB goniotomy in eyes with PXFG, which have a more aggressive clinical course.

The limitations of our study include its retrospective design, limited sample size, limited duration of long-term follow-up, and the absence of control groups to separately evaluate the effects of cataract surgery and standalone KDB goniotomy on the outcomes. In addition, IOP was measured using non-contact tonometry instead of the gold-standard goldmann applanation tonometry, which may introduce a small systematic bias. CCT was within the normal range in all subjects; therefore, IOP values obtained by non-contact tonometry were not adjusted for CCT. Although the impact of this omission was considered minimal in the study population, it still constitutes a minor limitation. Routine postoperative administration of pilocarpine may act as a potential confounding factor for early IOP outcomes, and its efficacy remains controversial in the published literature. Another limitation is that both eyes of some patients were enrolled, which may lead to non-independence of observations. To address this issue, we used linear mixed-effects models to account for intra-patient clustering and performed a per-patient single-eye sensitivity analysis to confirm result stability, thereby minimizing bias in statistical inference caused by non-independent data. In future studies, we look forward to well-designed multicente multicenter, large-sample, prospective studies to validate our findings.

Conclusion

KDB goniotomy combined with phacoemulsification achieved a significant reduction in IOP and the number of glaucoma medications in Chinese patients with open-angle glaucoma, exhibiting favorable safety.

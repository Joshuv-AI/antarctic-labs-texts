Exponential decay for the random connection model using asymptotic transitivity Thanks: This work was supported by the The Royal Society [grant number RF\ERE\231149].
Frankie Higgs

Abstract
We prove that the probability the cluster of the origin in a subcritical Poisson random connection model (RCM) has size at least n n decays exponentially as n n increases, under minimal assumptions. We extend a recent method of Vanneuville from Bernoulli percolation on vertex-transitive graphs to the RCM. The key idea is that the subcritical RCM can be constructed by site percolation on a very high-intensity RCM. The latter RCM becomes “almost vertex-transitive” in a certain sense at very high intensities, which is a new method that we expect to be useful for other problems. We obtain the result for connection functions with unbounded support, a setting in which it was not previously known.



1
Introduction

Take a random set of points
\eta
in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
and create a random graph by joining every pair of points
x,y\in\eta

with an edge with probability
g(x-y)
, independently of all other pairs,
where
g
is a function satisfying
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}g(x)=0
.
If
\eta
is a Poisson point process of intensity
\lambda
,
we call this graph the
random connection model
(RCM).
When
g(x)=1(\|x\|\leq 1)
,
the RCM is the well-known random geometric graph, or continuum percolation model.

The RCM is a natural model of random networks in which spatial relationships are important but long-range connections are possible, and both the location of vertices and edges between them are random.

Subject to certain conditions on
g
,
there is a percolation phase transition in
\lambda
,
i.e. there exists a
\lambda_{c}\in(0,\infty)

such that all connected components of the RCM are almost surely finite
when
\lambda<\lambda_{c}
,
and there exists at least one infinite connected component
when
\lambda>\lambda_{c}
.

One natural question is whether this percolation phase transition is continuous,
i.e. whether there is an infinite component when
\lambda=\lambda_{c}
.
With sufficiently “spread-out”
connection functions,
this is known both for the RCM
[
5
]

and for discrete models of long-range percolation
[
6
]
.
Such a result is conjectured for many short-range percolation models including the random geometric graph,
but has only been proved in certain cases, in particular when the dimension is either 2 or sufficiently high.
By changing the connection function
g

the RCM is a family which naturally contains both short-range and long-range
percolation models.

We will prove a
sharpness
result for the RCM with
any
connection function: in the whole subcritical phase the clusters are not just finite but very small.
For
\lambda<\lambda_{c}

we show that the probability a typical vertex of the RCM
is in a connected component of size at least
n\in\mathbb{N}

decays exponentially in
n
.
This was previously only known when
g
had bounded support
(see
[
7
, Lemma 5.2]
).
If
g
has unbounded support then it is possible that
the cluster containing a given vertex may have a large diameter but very few points,
or many points in a small area.
Theorem
1.3

shows that the latter does not occur:
although the RCM can have arbitrarily long edges,
in the subcritical regime the size of clusters still has an exponential tail.
To the author’s knowledge there is no proof in the literature
of such as result for
any
long-range percolation model.

We use the recent stochastic comparison technique of Vanneuville
[
12
]
,
who proved the same exponential decay result for Bernoulli bond percolation
on infinite, vertex-transitive graphs such as the integer lattice.
We construct the random connection model
by Bernoulli site percolation on an RCM of a much higher intensity.
A similar idea was used for the random geometric graph in
[
1
]
.
The difficulty in adapting Vanneuville’s method
is that
[
12
]
relies
on the graph being vertex transitive,
while the higher-intensity RCM
is not transitive.
For example it can have cliques of arbitrary size.
Our new insight is that as the intensity tends to infinity the denser RCM becomes
“asymptotically vertex-transitive”,
in that the probability of events related to
the behaviour of the percolation configuration around a vertex
x

become close to uniform in
x
.

As an example of “asymptotic vertex-transitivity”,
suppose we are given an RCM
G_{\lambda}
of intensity
\lambda
,
and we are interested in events related to the structure of the graph
around a given vertex
x
,
such as whether
x
is isolated,
or whether the connected component
containing
x
is infinite.
If we were to perform site percolation with parameter
p
on
G_{\lambda}
,
then we get a
random
conditional probability measure
P_{p}^{G_{\lambda}}

which is the Bernoulli product measure on the vertices of
G_{\lambda}
.
Then the random function
f_{p}^{G_{\lambda}}:V(G_{\lambda})\to[0,1]
given by

x\mapsto P_{p}^{G_{\lambda}}(x\text{ has an open neighbour})

varies significantly across
V(G_{\lambda})
.
For example, some vertices will be isolated and have
f_{p}^{G_{\lambda}}(x)=0
,
and some vertices will have an unusually large number of neighbours in
G_{\lambda}

so will have
f_{p}^{G_{\lambda}}(x)
close to 1.
But if we take a large
N\in\mathbb{N}
and change the parameters

\lambda\mapsto N\lambda
,
p\mapsto p/N
,
then the marginal distribution of the percolation configuration stays the same,
but there is a constant
c_{p\lambda}\in(0,1)
such that for
K>0
,

\sup_{x\in V(G_{N\lambda})\cap[-K,K]^{d}}|f_{p/N}^{G_{N\lambda}}(x)-c_{p\lambda}|\to 0

in probability as
N\to\infty
.

We expect that this method will be useful
to transfer other approaches and results
from discrete percolation to continuum models.

1.1
Definitions

Definition 1.1
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
and assume that

g(x)
depends only on
\|x\|

and that if
\|x\|\leq\|y\|
then
g(x)\geq g(y)
.
Let
\eta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a discrete set of points.
For every pair
x,y\in\eta
with
x\not=y
,
let
U_{x,y}
be a uniform random variable in
[0,1]
,
independent of all other pairs.
Construct the random graph
G_{g}(\eta)

with vertex set
\eta
and edge set
E_{g}(\eta)

given by
xy\in E_{g}(\eta)
if and only if
U_{x,y}\leq g(x-y)
.
We call
g
the
connection function

of the random graph
G_{g}(\eta)
.

If
\eta
is a Poisson point process,
we call
G_{g}(\eta)
the
random connection model
(RCM).
If
\eta
has intensity
\lambda>0
,
let
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}
denote the law of
\eta
,
and
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}
the joint law of
\eta
and the uniform random variables
U_{x,y}
.

We will often add one or more points
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}

to the vertex set of the RCM.
In this case we introduce new uniform random variables
U_{x_{i},y}

for
y\in\eta\cup\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}\setminus\{x_{i}\}

independent of those defined between vertices of
\eta
,
and then determine the edges of
G_{g}(\eta\cup\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\})

as above.
If we add only one vertex
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
it will be convenient to write

\eta^{x}:=\eta\cup\{x\}
.
In a slight abuse of notation we will continue to use
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}

to denote the joint law of
\eta
and the extended collection of uniforms.

Remark
.

We could replace the assumptions we make on
g
with others,
such as that
g(-x)=g(x)
for all
x
and
g
is continuous.
However, the assumptions in Definition
1.1
simplify our arguments.
To use a more general class of connection functions,
the only proof which would require significant modification
would be that of Lemma
2.12
:
if we replace
g(x-y)

with the supremum of
g(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})

for
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
close to
x
and
y
,
the probability of adding new edges is small.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a point process
\eta
,
let
\mathcal{C}_{0}
denote the component of
G_{g}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})

containing
\mathbf{0}
.
For
n\geq 0
and
\lambda>0
let

\psi_{n}(\lambda):=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}|\geq n].

Remark
.

If
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)\mathrm{d}x=\infty
then, almost surely,
the neighbourhood of
\mathbf{0}
in
G_{g}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})

is both infinite and unbounded,
so
\psi_{n}(\lambda)=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda>0
.
To make the RCM a non-trivial percolation model we assume that

\displaystyle 0<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

(1)

Then
[
10
, Theorem 6.1]
states that,
if
d\geq 2
and
g
satisfies (
1
),
then there exists a
\lambda_{c}\in(0,\infty)

such that
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is finite almost surely if
\lambda<\lambda_{c}
,
and
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}|=\infty]>0
if
\lambda>\lambda_{c}
.

Remark
.

In the one-dimensional RCM, (
1
) is necessary but not sufficient
to have a non-trivial phase transition.
It is shown in
[
3
, Theorem 3]

that in one dimension,
if
g
follows a power-law distribution
with
g(x)\sim\|x\|^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha>2
,
then there is no infinite component for any
\lambda<\infty
.
On the other hand if
\alpha\in(1,2)

then there is a finite
\lambda_{c}>0

such that the RCM has an infinite component for
\lambda>\lambda_{c}

[
11
]

(see their remark at the bottom of page 1051).

All of our results are true when
d=1

for any
g
satisfying (
1
),
even if the condition
\lambda<\lambda_{c}
is trivial.

Remark
.

The
Poisson Boolean model
is related to the RCM.
Instead of connecting points with edges using
g
,
in the Poisson Boolean model we place a ball of random radius
centred at each point of
\eta
,
with the radius sampled independently for each point.
We expect some of the methods in this paper
would also be useful for the Poisson Boolean model,
but leave this for future work.

1.2
Main results

These results extend those of
[
12
]

from nearest-neighbour Bernoulli bond percolation on a transitive graph
to the random connection model.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
g
be a connection function satisfying (
1
).
For all
\lambda<\lambda_{c}(g)
there exist
c,C>0

depending on
\lambda
and
g

such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\psi_{n}(\lambda)\leq Ce^{-cn}.

Remark
.

When the support of
g
is bounded,
this bound was recently shown for the RCM in
[
7
, Lemma 5.2]
.
As far as we can tell our Theorem
1.3

is the first time such an exponential decay result
has been shown for the RCM
with a long-range connection function.

To prove Theorem
1.3
,
following Vanneuville
[
12
]
,
we introduce a “ghost field”
as an extra source of randomness.
The idea is that when the ghost field has a low intensity,
it only intersects very large components.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
h>0
.
Given a graph
G=(V,E)
,
let
\mathcal{G}\subseteq V
be a random subset of the vertices,
where for each
v\in V
,
we have
v\in\mathcal{G}
with probability
1-e^{-h}

independently of the other vertices.
Call
v\in V

green
if
v\in\mathcal{G}
.

For the graph
G=G_{g}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})
,
let
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g,h}
denote the joint law of

\eta
,
(U_{x,y})_{x,y\in\eta^{\mathbf{0}}}

and
\mathcal{G}
. Then let

m_{h}(\lambda):=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g,h}[\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\mathcal{G}\not=\varnothing].

Theorem
1.3

can be derived immediately from the following theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
g
be as above.
Let
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
and
h\in(0,\infty)
,
and let
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda(1-m_{h}(\lambda))
.
Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\psi_{n}(\lambda^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{1-m_{h}(\lambda)}\psi_{n}(\lambda)e^{-hn}.

In the next section, we prove Theorem
1.5

in the case where
g
has bounded support.
In Section
3

we derive the result for general
g

from the bounded case.

2
Proof for a bounded connection function

Section
2.1

closely follows the
stochastic comparison
method of
[
12
]
,
which applies to Bernoulli percolation on a vertex-transitive graph,
to the random connection model.
The idea behind this method for Bernoulli percolation
is that conditioning on certain decreasing events
has less of an effect than decreasing the percolation parameter.
To apply it to our continuum model,
we use the observation that the RCM can be constructed by site percolation
on an RCM with the same connection function
but where the point process has much higher intensity.

Section
2.2
is where we depart from
[
12
]

and develop new methods necessary
to complete the proofs of Section
2.1

for the random connection model.
In particular we use the “asymptotic vertex-transitivity” method:
first we take an RCM with intensity
N\lambda
,
and construct the intensity-
\lambda
RCM by site percolation
with parameter
1/N
.
We show that with high probability,
the higher-intensity graph is such that the conditional probability
a vertex
x
is connected to the ghost field
by open vertices is bounded by
m_{h}(\lambda)+\varepsilon

uniformly in
x

for all sufficiently large
N
.

2.1
Stochastic comparison

Since we add the origin as a vertex in our random connection model,
it will be useful to define a site percolation cluster for rooted graphs
in such a way that we do not close the root vertex.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph

\rho\in V

For

\omega\in\{0,1\}^{V\setminus\{\rho\}}

.

We will frequently identify

We then talk about the induced subgraph of

Definition 2.2
.

For
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{V\setminus\{\rho\}}

.

Let

Given a homogeneous Poisson point process

Note that for all

We adapt the notion of exploration from

.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph
with root
\rho\in V

be the set of orderings

\vec{v}=(\rho,v_{1},\dots,v_{|V|-1})

An

\mathbf{v}:\{0,1\}^{V\setminus\{\rho\}}\to\vec{V},

Definition 2.5
.

Given a set

Given two measures

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

.

Let
\lambda>0
and

.
Suppose

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g,h}^{n}

to

\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda(1-m_{h}(\lambda))
.
Then for all

,

\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\lambda^{\prime},g}\preceq\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\lambda,g,h}[G^{nR}_{g}\in\cdot\,|\,(\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\Lambda_{nR})\cap\mathcal{G}=\varnothing].

Proof.

Fix
n\geq 1
and let
\delta\in(0,1-m_{h}(\lambda))
.
We will use
\mathcal{C}_{0}
to denote the component of
\mathbf{0}

restricted to

We will apply Lemma
2.6
to the graph

from Definition

and

.
This follows the proof of
[

very closely,
but to illustrate where the differences between our setting
and that of
[
12
]
become important,
we will write out the argument.
Let
V
be the vertex set of

and fix an arbitrary ordering of
V

in which
\mathbf{0}
comes first.
Define an exploration of
V

at each step, among the vertices connected to
\mathbf{0}

Continue this process until there are no more such vertices,

\mathcal{C}_{0}
in
G_{g}(\omega_{N}\cup\{\mathbf{0}\})

Let
(\vec{v},\sigma)\in\vec{V}\times\{0,1\}^{V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}

such that
\{\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\mathcal{G}=\varnothing\}\cap\mathrm{Expl}(\vec{v},\sigma)\not=\varnothing
.
If
\mathrm{Expl}_{k}(\vec{v},\sigma)
holds
and

is pivotal for

,

is connected

Denote this event by

By the Harris-FKG inequality applied to

,
and the decreasing event

\displaystyle P^{G}_{1/N,h}[v_{k+1}\text{ is }

\displaystyle\text{pivotal for }\{\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\mathcal{G}=\varnothing\}\,|\,\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\mathcal{G}=\varnothing,\mathrm{Expl}_{k}(\vec{v},\sigma)]

\displaystyle\leq P^{G}_{1/N,h}[B_{k+1}\,|\,\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\mathcal{G}=\varnothing,\mathrm{Expl}_{k}(\vec{v},\sigma)]

\displaystyle\leq P^{G}_{1/N,h}[B_{k+1}\,|\,\mathrm{Expl}_{k}(\vec{v},\sigma)].

Then, because

.

Now we differ from

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
K>0
and
\lambda>0

Let
\eta
be a Poisson point process of intensity
\lambda
on
\Lambda_{K}
.
There exists a constant

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}[\hat{G}_{s}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})\not=G_{g}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})]\leq C(\lambda^{2}+\lambda)s.

Proof.

Note that
\hat{G}_{s}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})\not=G_{g}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})

if and only if there exist distinct

x,y\in\eta^{\mathbf{0}}
such that
U_{x,y}\in(g(x-y),\hat{g}_{s}(x,y)]
.
The expected number of such pairs of vertices is

\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,g}\left[\sum_{(x,y)\in(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})_{\not=}^{2}}1(U_{x,y}\in(g(x-y),\hat{g}_{s}(x,y)])\right]=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\sum_{(x,y)\in(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})_{\not=}^{2}}(\hat{g}_{s}(x,y)-g(x-y))\right].

By the Mecke equation,
the right-hand side is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\int_{\Lambda_{K}}\int_{\Lambda_{K}}(\hat{g}_{s}(x,y)

\displaystyle-g(x-y))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+\lambda\int_{\Lambda_{K}}(\hat{g}_{s}(\mathbf{0},y)-g(y))\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}K^{d}+\lambda\right)\int_{B(\mathbf{0},\sqrt{d}K)}(\hat{g}_{s}(\mathbf{0},y)-g(y))\mathrm{d}y.

Since the diameter of the boxes
\Lambda_{s}(x)
is
\sqrt{d}s

and
g(y)
is a decreasing function of
\|y\|
,
for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(\mathbf{0},2\sqrt{d}s)

we have the bound
\hat{g}_{s}(\mathbf{0},y)\leq g(\|y\|-2\sqrt{d}s)
.
Therefore,
using spherical coordinates,

\displaystyle\int_{B(\mathbf{0},\sqrt{d}K)}(\hat{g}_{s}(\mathbf{0},y)-g(y))\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\theta_{d}(2\sqrt{d}s)^{d}+d\theta_{d}\int_{2\sqrt{d}s}^{\sqrt{d}K}r^{d-1}(g(r-2\sqrt{d}s)-g(r))\mathrm{d}r,

where

is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The latter integral can be separated and written

\int_{0}^{2\sqrt{d}s}r^{d-1}g(r)\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{\sqrt{d}(K-2s)}((r+2\sqrt{d}s)^{d-1}-r^{d-1})g(r)\mathrm{d}r.

The first integral is

,
and since
\int_{0}^{\infty}g(r)\mathrm{d}r<\infty

the second integral is bounded by a constant multiple of

\sup_{r\in[0,\sqrt{d}(K-2s)]}((r+2\sqrt{d}s)^{d-1}-r^{d-1})\leq(d-1)(\sqrt{d}K)^{d-2}2\sqrt{d}s+O(s^{2}),

where the constant in the

Therefore the expected number of edges in

is

so by Markov’s inequality the two graphs are equal

as claimed.

\displaystyle P_{N,h}(\text{$x$ is connected to a green vertex in $\hat{G}_{s}(\omega_{N}\cup\{x\})$}\,|\,S_{f,x})

\displaystyle=1-\exp(-h|\mathcal{C}_{\hat{x}}^{f}|),

where
\mathcal{C}^{f}_{\hat{x}}
is the component of
\hat{x}
in
f
.
We will show that for any

,
with high probability conditional on
\eta_{N}

and
(U^{w,z}_{i,j}:i,j\in\mathbb{N},w,z\in\mathbb{L}_{K,s})
,

P_{N}(S_{f,x})
is very close to the

of an analogous event related to a Poisson process
\eta
of intensity
\lambda
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}_{x}
and suppose the vertex set is

V(f)=\{z_{0},z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\}

where
z_{0}=\hat{x}
.
Define
\Omega^{x}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathbb{L}_{K,s}}

by
\Omega^{x}(z):=|(\omega_{N}\cup\{x\})\cap\Lambda_{s}(z)|

for all
z\in\mathbb{L}_{K,s}
.
Let
\mathbf{N}^{x}_{f}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{L}_{K,s}}

with
\mathbf{N}^{x}_{f}(z)=1
if and only if
z\in V(f)
.
Let
\mathbf{N}_{f}(z)=\mathbf{N}^{x}_{f}(z)
for
z\not=\hat{x}

and
\mathbf{N}_{f}(\hat{x})=0
.
Then, conditional on the event
R_{s,\alpha,N}
from Definition
2.15
,

\displaystyle P_{N}(\Omega^{x}=\mathbf{N}^{x}_{f})

\displaystyle=P_{N}(\Omega^{x}=\mathbf{N}^{x}_{f}\text{ and }\omega_{N}(\hat{x})=0)+P_{N}(\Omega^{x}=\mathbf{N}^{x}_{f}\text{ and }\omega_{N}(\hat{x})=1)

\displaystyle=P_{N}(\Omega=\mathbf{N}_{f}\text{ and }\omega_{N}(\hat{x})=0)+P_{N}(\Omega^{x}=\mathbf{N}^{x}_{f}\text{ and }\omega_{N}(\hat{x})=1)

\displaystyle\leq P_{N}(\Omega=\mathbf{N}_{f})+P_{N}(\omega_{N}(\hat{x})=1)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\alpha\lambda K^{d}}(1+\alpha)^{(K/s)^{d}}p_{\lambda s^{d}}(\mathbf{N}_{f})+\frac{C+1}{N},

where the final inequality follows from Lemma
2.16
.

Conditional on
\Omega^{x}=\mathbf{N}^{x}_{f}
,
the vertex set of
F_{s}(\hat{G}_{s}(\omega_{N}\cup\{x\}))
is
V(f)
,
and exactly one uniformly-chosen vertex in each of

is open in
\omega_{N}
.
We will now show that the probability that
f

and
F_{s}(\hat{G}_{s}(\omega_{N}\cup\{x\}))

have the same edge set is close to
\hat{g}_{s}(f)
.
Let the chosen vertex in
\Lambda_{s}(z_{i})
be

.
Almost surely

.
For

,
the conditional probability, given
(X_{0},\dots,X_{k-1})
,
that the edges between
\{X_{0},\dots,X_{k-1}\}

and
X_{k}
in
\hat{G}_{s}(\omega_{N}\cup\{x\})

match the edges between
\{z_{0},\dots,z_{k-1}\}
and
z_{k}
in
f

is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{H_{N\lambda}(z_{k})}\left|\left\{j\in[H_{N\lambda}(z_{k})]:\parbox{256.0748pt}{\centering for all $l\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}$,
$U^{z_{l},z_{k}}_{i_{X_{l}},j}\leq\hat{g}_{s}(z_{l},z_{k})$
if $z_{l}z_{k}\in E(f)$,
and
$U^{z_{l},z_{k}}_{i_{X_{l}},j}>\hat{g}_{s}(z_{l},z_{k})$
if $z_{l}z_{k}\not\in E(f)$
\@add@centering}\right\}\right|.

(7)

Then, conditional on
R_{s,\alpha,N}
,

H_{N\lambda}(z_{k})
is between
N\lambda s^{d}(1-\alpha)
and
N\lambda s^{d}(1+\alpha)

with

and

.
On the event

(
7
)
is bounded by

Therefore, conditional on
R_{s,\alpha,N}
and

we almost surely have

\displaystyle P_{N}(F_{s}(\hat{G}_{s}(\omega_{N}\cup\{x\}))=f\,|\,\Omega^{x}=\mathbf{N}^{x}_{f})\leq\hat{g}_{s}(f)\left(\frac{(1+\alpha)^{2}}{1-\alpha}\right)^{m}.

\displaystyle P_{N}(F_{s}(\hat{G}_{s}(\omega_{N}\cup\{x\}))=f\,|\,\Omega^{x}=\mathbf{N}^{x}_{f})

\displaystyle\leq\hat{g}_{s}(f)(1+C^{\prime}\alpha),

,
\delta
and
s
,
but
not
on

and not on

.
Then,
conditional on

and

\displaystyle P_{N,h}(\text{$x$ is connected to a green vertex in $\hat{G}_{s}(\omega_{N}\cup\{x\})$})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{x}}P_{N}(S_{f,x})(1-e^{-h|\mathcal{C}_{\hat{x}}^{f}|})+\frac{\delta}{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{x}}P_{N}(F_{s}(\hat{G}_{s}(\omega_{N}\cup\{x\}))=f\,|\,\Omega^{x}=\mathbf{N}^{x}_{f})P_{N}(\Omega^{x}=\mathbf{N}^{x}_{f})(1-e^{-h|\mathcal{C}_{\hat{x}}^{f}|})+\frac{\delta}{4}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\alpha\lambda K^{d}}(1+\alpha)^{(K/s)^{d}}(1+C^{\prime}\alpha)\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{x}}\hat{g}_{s}(f)p_{\lambda s^{d}}(\mathbf{N}_{f})(1-e^{-h|\mathcal{C}_{\hat{x}}^{f}|})

\displaystyle\phantom{e^{\alpha\lambda K^{d}}(1+\alpha)^{(K/s)^{d}}}+\frac{(C+1)(1+C^{\prime}\alpha)|\mathcal{F}_{x}|}{N}+\frac{\delta}{4}.

Note that
|\mathcal{F}_{x}|
is bounded by a constant depending on
K
and

but not on

.
Therefore there exists an

depending on

,
\delta
,

and

.
We can and do choose

sufficiently small so that
the above is almost surely bounded by

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{x}}\hat{g}_{s}(f)p_{\lambda s^{d}}(\mathbf{N}_{f})(1-e^{-h|\mathcal{C}_{\hat{x}}^{f}|})+\frac{3}{4}\delta.

\eta
be a Poisson point process of intensity
\lambda
in
\Lambda_{K}
.
Then by the Harris–FKG inequality,

\hat{m}_{h}^{2K,s}(\lambda)\geq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g,h}[\text{$x$ is connected to a green vertex in $\hat{G}_{s}(\eta\cup\{x\})$}]
.
For
f\in\mathcal{F}_{x}
, let
\tilde{S}_{f,x}
be the event that

|(\eta\cup\{x\})\cap\Lambda_{s}(z)|\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathbb{L}_{K,s}

and
F_{s}(\hat{G}_{s}(\eta\cup\{x\}))=f
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g,h}[\text{$x$ is connected to a green vertex in $\hat{G}_{s}(\eta\cup\{x\})$}]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{x}}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}[\tilde{S}_{f,x}](1-e^{-h|\mathcal{C}_{\hat{x}}^{f}|})

\displaystyle=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{x}}\hat{g}_{s}(f)p_{\lambda s^{d}}(\mathbf{N}_{f})(1-e^{-h|\mathcal{C}_{\hat{x}}^{f}|}).

Therefore, for all
N\geq N_{0}
,
we have,
conditional on
R_{s,\alpha,N}
and
F^{\left\lfloor N\lambda s^{d}\right\rfloor}
,

\displaystyle P_{N,h}(\text{$x$ is connected to a green vertex in $\hat{G}_{s}(\omega_{N}\cup\{x\})$})

\displaystyle\leq\hat{m}_{h}^{2K,s}(\lambda)+\frac{3}{4}\delta

\displaystyle\leq m_{h}(\lambda)+\delta

for any
x\in\eta_{N}^{\mathbf{0}}
,
and so

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{N\lambda,g}\!\left[P_{N,h}\!\left(\text{$x$ is connected to a green vertex in $\hat{G}_{s}(\omega_{N}\cup\{x\})$}\right)>m_{h}(\lambda)+\delta\right]

\displaystyle\phantom{}\leq\mathbb{P}_{N\lambda}[R_{s,\alpha,N}^{c}]+\mathbb{P}_{N\lambda,g}[(F^{\left\lfloor N\lambda s^{d}\right\rfloor})^{c}].

By Lemma
2.16
and Lemma
2.18
,
this bound decays exponentially in
N
,
uniformly in
x
.
∎

3
Unbounded connection functions

In Section
2
we proved
Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.5

under the assumption that
g
had bounded support.
In this section we assume only that
g
satisfies (
1
),
and deduce that both theorems hold in this case
by applying them to a version of
g
cut off at a large finite radius
and taking a limit.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
g
be a connection function satisfying (
1
).
For
R>0
let
g^{R}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
be the cut-off version of
g

defined by
g^{R}(x)=g(x)1(\|x\|\leq R)
.

Remark
.

There is a natural coupling of
G_{g}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})
and
G_{g^{R}}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})

using
(U_{x,y})_{x,y\in\eta^{\mathbf{0}}}
from Definition
1.1
.
Throughout this section we assume that the two models are thus coupled.

Definition 3.2
.

Given a point process
\eta
, let

\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}
be the connected component of
\mathbf{0}

in
G_{g^{R}}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})
.
Let

\displaystyle\psi_{n}^{R}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}|\geq n]

and

\displaystyle m_{h}^{R}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g,h}[\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}\cap\mathcal{G}\not=\varnothing].

To derive Theorem
1.5
with general connection function
g

from the fact that it holds for every
g^{R}
,
we first show that if
|\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}|
is small,
then with the coupling above there is a high probability that
\mathcal{C}_{0}=\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}
.

Remark
.

We believe that the same method would allow us to derive the equivalent
of Theorem
1.3
for long-range percolation on a lattice
from the case of finite-range percolation.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Suppose
g
satisfies (
1
).
Then

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}\!\left[|\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}|=k\text{ and }\mathcal{C}_{0}\not=\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}\right]\to 0

as
R\to\infty
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose
g
satisfies (
1
).
Let
\lambda>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
.
There exists an
R_{0}=R_{0}(g,\lambda,n,\varepsilon)

such that for all
R>R_{0}
,

|\psi_{n}(\lambda)-\psi_{n}^{R}(\lambda)|<\varepsilon.

The following function, defined in
[
10
, Definition 6.2]
,
will be useful in proving Proposition
3.3
.

Definition 3.5
.

Given a connection function
g
,
for
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
let
g_{2}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
be the probability that
G_{g}(\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\})

is connected.

For a later application of the dominated convergence theorem,
we will need the following integrability result.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
g
be a connection function satsifying
0<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)\mathrm{d}x<\infty
.
Then, for any
k\geq 2
,

\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k-1}}g_{2}(\mathbf{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}<\infty.

Proof.

Let
\eta
be a homogeneous Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with intensity 1.
By the Mecke equation,

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k-1}}g_{2}(\mathbf{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1,g}\left[\sum_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1})\in\eta_{\not=}^{k-1}}1(\{\mathbf{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}\}\text{ is connected in }G_{g}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}}))\right],

which is the expected number of connected induced subgraphs
of
G_{g}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})

of size
k
which contain
\mathbf{0}
.
For
l\geq 0
, let
N_{l}
denote the number of vertices at graph distance
l

from
\mathbf{0}
in
G_{g}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})
.
Then the above expectation is bounded by

\mathbb{E}_{1,g}[(\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}N_{l})^{k-1}]
.
In
[
10
, the proof of Theorem 6.1]
,
it is shown that
N_{l}
is stochastically dominated
by the number of individuals
Z_{l}
in the
l
th generation
of a Galton-Watson branching process
with
\mathrm{Poisson}(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)\mathrm{d}x)
offspring distribution.
We can show using generating functions
[
4
, Chapter 5.4]

that
Z_{l}
has a finite
(k-1)
th moment,
therefore
\mathbb{E}_{1,g}[(\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}N_{l})^{k-1}]<\infty
,
and the result follows.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

First, if
k=1
then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}|=1\text{ and }\mathcal{C}_{0}\not=\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}[\mathbf{0}\text{ has an edge longer than }R\text{ in }G_{g}(\eta^{\mathbf{0}})]

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(\mathbf{0},R)}g(x)\mathrm{d}x,

where the second bound is from Markov’s inequality.
This tends to zero as
R\to\infty
because
g
satisfies (
1
).

Now assume
k\geq 2
.
We can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}|=k\text{ and }\mathcal{C}_{0}\not=\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,g}\left[\sum_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1})\in\eta_{\not=}^{k-1}}1(\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}=\{\mathbf{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}\}\text{ and }\mathcal{C}_{0}\not=\{\mathbf{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}\})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\sum_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1})\in\eta_{\not=}^{k-1}}\mathbb{P}_{g}(\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}=\{\mathbf{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}\}\text{ and }\mathcal{C}_{0}\not=\{\mathbf{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}\}\,|\,\eta)\right]

For a discrete set of points
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

and
(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\in\mathcal{P}^{k}_{\not=}
, let

f_{R}(y_{1},\dots,y_{k};\mathcal{P}):=\mathbb{P}\left(\parbox{199.16928pt}{\centering$\{y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\}$ is a connected component of $G_{g^{R}}(\mathcal{P})$ but not of $G_{g}(\mathcal{P})$\@add@centering}\right).

Then we have

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}|=k\text{ and }\mathcal{C}_{0}\not=\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}]=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\left[\sum_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1})\in\eta_{\not=}^{k-1}}f_{R}(\mathbf{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1};\eta\cup\{\mathbf{0}\})\right].

By the multivariate Mecke equation
[
8
, Theorem 4.4]
,
the above is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k-1}}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}[f_{R}(\mathbf{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1};\eta\cup\{\mathbf{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}\})]\mathrm{d}x_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}.

Given distinct
x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
,
let
\mathcal{X}=\{\mathbf{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}\}
.
We can write

\displaystyle 1\left(\parbox{170.71652pt}{\centering$\mathcal{X}$ is a connected component of $G_{g^{R}}(\eta\cup\mathcal{X})$
but not of $G_{g}(\eta\cup\mathcal{X})$
\@add@centering}\right)

\displaystyle=1\left(\parbox{142.26378pt}{\centering$\mathcal{X}$ is a connected component of $G_{g^{R}}(\eta\cup\mathcal{X})$\@add@centering}\right)\times 1\left(\parbox{128.0374pt}{\centering there is an edge between $\mathcal{X}$ and $\eta$

Let
B(\mathcal{X},R)=\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}B(x,R)

Then the first event depends only on edges between points in

to
\eta\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(\mathcal{X},R))

Since

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}[f_{R}(\mathcal{X};\eta\cup\mathcal{X})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}\!\left[\parbox{130.88284pt}{\centering$\mathcal{X}$ is a connected component in $G_{g^{R}}(\eta\cup\mathcal{X})$\@add@centering}\right]\times\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}\!\left[\parbox{108.12054pt}{\centering$\exists$ an edge from $\mathcal{X}$ to $\eta\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(\mathcal{X},R))$\@add@centering}\right].

\phi^{R}_{1}(\mathcal{X})

\phi^{R}_{2}(\mathcal{X})

Since

Note that
\phi_{1}^{R}(\mathcal{X})\leq\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{X}\text{ is connected in }G_{g^{R}}(\mathcal{X})]\leq\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{X}\text{ is connected in }G_{g}(\mathcal{X})]=g_{2}(\mathcal{X})

Therefore, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,g}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}|=k\text{ and }\mathcal{C}_{0}\not=\mathcal{C}_{0}^{R}]

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k-1}}\phi^{R}_{1}(\mathcal{X})\phi^{R}_{2}(\mathcal{X})\mathrm{d}x_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k-1}}\lim_{R\to\infty}(\phi^{R}_{1}(\mathcal{X})\phi^{R}_{2}(\mathcal{X}))\mathrm{d}x_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}

Proof of Theorem
1.5
for general

.

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
By Corollary
3.4
applied to
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
,
there exists an

depending on
\lambda
,

,

and

such that

\psi_{n}(\lambda^{\prime})\leq\psi_{n}^{R}(\lambda^{\prime})+\varepsilon

\psi_{n}^{R}(\lambda)\leq\psi_{n}(\lambda)+\varepsilon

As we proved in Section

Combining all the above inequalities,

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(\lambda^{\prime})\leq\psi_{n}^{R}(\lambda^{\prime})+\varepsilon

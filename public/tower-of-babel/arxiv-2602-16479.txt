Central limit theorem for random walk in degenerate divergence-free random environment: ℋ − 1 \mathcal{H}_{-1} reloaded with relaxed ellipticity
Bálint Tóth

Abstract
This paper enhances the result of the work Kozma-Tóth (2017) [ 14 ] . We prove the central limit theorem (in probability w.r.t. the environment) for the displacement of a random walker in divergence-free (or, doubly stochastic) random environment, with substantially relaxed ellipticity assumptions. Integrability of the reciprocal of the symmetric part of the jump rates is only assumed (rather than their boundedness, as in previous works on this type of RWRE). Relaxing ellipticity involves substantial changes in the proof, making it conceptually elementary in the sense that it does not rely on Nash’s inequality in any disguise. MSC2010: 60F05, 60G99, 60K37 Key words and phrases: random walk in random environment (RWRE), divergence-free drift, incompressible flow, central limit theorem

Introduction

In the work
[
14
]
the weak CLT (that is, in probability with respect to the environment) was established for random walks in doubly stochastic (or, divergence-free) random environments, under the conditions of
(
\imath
)
strict ellipticity
assumed for the symmetric part of the drift field, and,
(
\imath\imath
)

\mathcal{H}_{-1}(|{\Delta}|)
assumed for the antisymmetric part of the drift field.

The proof relied on a martingale approximation (a la Kipnis-Varadhan) adapted to the
non-self-adjoint
and
non-sectorial
nature of the problem, the two substantial technical components being:

\circ

A functional analytic statement about the unbounded operator
formally
written as
|{L+L^{*}}|^{-1/2}(L-L^{*})|{L+L^{*}}|^{-1/2}
, where
L
is the infinitesimal generator of the environment process, as seen from the position of the moving random walker.

\circ

A diagonal heat kernel upper bound which follows from Nash’s inequality, valid only under the assumed
strict ellipticity
.

The assumption of strict ellipticity, however, is conceptually restrictive and excludes relevant applications.
In this paper we relax the strict ellipticity assumption, replacing it by an integrability condition on the reciprocals of the conductances. This can be done only by "de-Nashifying" the proof. On the other hand the functional analytic elements are refined. These changes are also of conceptual importance: the present proof (of a stronger result) does not invoke a conceptually higher level (than the CLT) element like a local heat kernel estimate. Altogether, it is conceptually simpler than that in
[
14
]
. (This was already demonstrated in
[
30
]
where the result of
[
14
]
was re-proved along a baby version of the arguments in the present paper.)

To the best of our knowledge this is the first time a CLT is proved for a random walk in a
non-reversible
random environment without a strong ellipticity assumption. The integrability conditions imposed on the random jump rates seem to be close to optimal.

1.1
Preliminaries

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\pi,(\tau_{z}:z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
be a probability space with an ergodic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-action. Denote by
{\mathcal{N}}:=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|{k}|=1\}
the set of unit elements generating
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as an additive group. These will serve as the set of elementary steps of a continuous time nearest neighbour random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Let
p:\Omega\to[0,\infty)^{\mathcal{N}}
satisfy (
\pi
-a.s.) the following
bi-stochasticity
condition

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{N}}}p_{k}(\omega)=\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{N}}}p_{-k}(\tau_{k}\omega),

(1)

and
p:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\Omega\to[0,\infty)^{{\mathcal{N}}}
be its lifting to a random field over
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\displaystyle p_{k}(x,\omega):=p_{k}(\tau_{x}\omega).

(Throughout the paper, measurable functions
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
and their lifting to a random field
f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, defined as
f(x,\omega):=f(\tau_{x}\omega)
, will be denoted by the same symbol.)

Given the random field
p:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\Omega\to[0,\infty)^{{\mathcal{N}}}
, define the continuous-time random walk in random environment (RWRE),
t\mapsto X(t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as the Markovian nearest neighbour random walk with jump rates

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\omega}\left(X(t+dt)=x+k\bigm|X(t)=x\right)=p_{k}(x,\omega)\,dt+\mathcal{O}((dt)^{2}),

(2)

and initial position
X(0)=0
.

We use the notation
\mathbf{P}_{\omega}\left(\cdot\right)
,
\mathbf{E}_{\omega}\left(\cdot\right)
and
\mathbf{Var}_{\omega}\left(\cdot\right)
for
quenched
probability, expectation and variance. That is: probability, expectation, and variance with respect to the distribution of the random walk
X(t)
,
conditioned on fixed environment
\omega\in\Omega
. The notation
\mathbf{P}\big(\cdot\big):=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{P}_{\omega}\left(\cdot\right){\mathrm{d}}\pi(\omega)
,
\mathbf{E}\big(\cdot\big):=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{E}_{\omega}\left(\cdot\right){\mathrm{d}}\pi(\omega)
and
\mathbf{Var}\big(\cdot\big):=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{Var}_{\omega}\left(\cdot\right){\mathrm{d}}\pi(\omega)+\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{E}_{\omega}\left(\cdot\right)^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\pi(\omega)-\mathbf{E}\big(\cdot\big)^{2}
will be reserved for
annealed
probability, expectation and variance. That is: probability, expectation and variance with respect to the random walk trajectory
X(t)

and
the environment
\omega
, sampled according to the distribution
\pi
.

The
environment process
(as seen from the position of the random walker) is,
t\mapsto\eta_{t}\in\Omega
, defined as

\displaystyle\eta_{t}:=\tau_{X_{t}}\omega.

(3)

This is a pure jump Markov process on the state space
\Omega
. The infinitesimal generator of its Markovian semigroup is

\displaystyle Lf(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{N}}}p_{k}(\omega)(f(\tau_{k}\omega)-f(\omega)).

(4)

The linear operator
L
is well defined for all measurable functions
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, not just a formal expression.

It is well known (and easy to check, see e.g.
[
13
]
) that bi-stochasticity (
1
) of the jump rates
p
is equivalent to stationarity (in time) of the a priori distribution
\pi
of the environment process
t\mapsto\eta_{t}\in\Omega
. Moreover, under the conditions (
1
) and

(
8
)

(see below) spatial ergodicity of
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\pi,(\tau_{z}:z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
also implies time-wise ergodicity of the (time-wise) stationary environment process process
t\mapsto\eta_{t}\in(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\pi)
. See
[
13
]
for details. Hence it follows that under these conditions the random walk
t\mapsto X(t)
will have stationary and ergodic
annealed
increments. Though, in the annealed setting the walk is obviously not Markovian.

It is convenient to separate the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of the jump rates:

\displaystyle p_{k}(\omega)=s_{k}(\omega)+b_{k}(\omega),\quad

\displaystyle s_{k}(\omega):=\frac{p_{k}(\omega)+p_{-k}(\tau_{k}\omega)}{2},\quad

\displaystyle b_{k}(\omega):=\frac{p_{k}(\omega)-p_{-k}(\tau_{k}\omega)}{2},

(5)

and note that

\displaystyle s_{k}(\omega)=s_{-k}(\tau_{k}\omega)\geq 0,

\displaystyle b_{k}(\omega)=-b_{-k}(\tau_{k}\omega),

(6)

and (
1
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}}b_{k}(\omega)=0.

(7)

We assume for now
weak ellipticity
and
finiteness
of the conductances:
\pi
-a.s. for all
k\in\mathcal{N}
,

\displaystyle 0<s_{k}(\omega)<\infty.

(8)

Later somewhat stronger integrability conditions will be imposed (see (
18
) further below).

Accordingly, we decompose the infinitesimal generator into Hermitian, and anti-Hermitian parts (with respect to the stationary measure
\pi
) as

L=-S+A

where

\displaystyle Sf(\omega):=-\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{N}}}s_{k}(\omega)(f(\tau_{k}\omega)-f(\omega)),

\displaystyle Af(\omega):=\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{N}}}b_{k}(\omega)(f(\tau_{k}\omega)-f(\omega)).

(9)

The right hand sides of (
4
) and (
9
) make perfect sense for arbitrary measurable functions
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
. So, disregarding for the time being topological issues in function spaces, the linear operators
L
,
S
, and
A
are well defined over the space of all (classes of equivalence of) measurable functions
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
(w.r.t. the probability measure
\pi
).

The symmetric part of the jump rates (lifted to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as
s_{k}(x,\omega):=s_{k}(\tau_{x}\omega)
),

\displaystyle s_{-k}(x+k,\omega)=s_{k}(x,\omega)>0

(10)

defines a collection positive
random conductances
on the unoriented edges
\{x,x+k\}
. The weak ellipticity condition (
10
) means that all edges of the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
are passable for the random walker, at least in one direction. Integrability conditions on the conductances
s_{k}
and their reciprocals
s_{k}^{-1}
will be imposed later (see (
18
) below). Actually, it will be convenient to work with the variables

\displaystyle r_{k}(\omega):=\sqrt{s_{k}(\omega)}.

Regarding the antisymmetric parts of the jump rates (lifted to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as
b_{k}(x,\omega):=b_{k}(\tau_{x}\omega)
), we have

\displaystyle b_{-k}(x+k,\omega)=-b_{k}(x,\omega).

(11)

That is, they define a
random flow
(or, a
lattice vector field
) on the oriented edges
(x,x+k)
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. In terms of these variables the bi-stochasticity (
1
) reads as
sourcelessness
, or
divergence-freeness
of the flow:
\pi
-a.s., for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{N}}}b_{k}(x,\omega)=0

(12)

Obviously, the former dominate the latter:

\displaystyle|{b_{k}(\omega)}|\leq s_{k}(\omega).

(13)

The extreme case, when
\pi
-almost-surely, for all
k\in{\mathcal{N}}
,
s_{k}(\omega)=|{b_{k}(\omega)}|
,
will be called
totally asymmetric
. In this case every edge of the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is passable in exactly one of the two possible directions.

Throughout this paper we assume that the divergence-free flows/vector fields
b_{k}
are given in
divergence form
. That is, there exists
h:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{N}}\times{\mathcal{N}}}
, with the following (
\pi
-a.s.) symmetries

\displaystyle h_{k,l}(\omega)=-h_{-k,l}(\tau_{k}\omega)=-h_{k,-l}(\tau_{l}\omega)=-h_{l,k}(\omega)

(14)

so that

\displaystyle b_{k}(\omega)=\sum_{l\in{\mathcal{N}}}h_{k,l}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\in{\mathcal{N}}}(h_{k,l}(\omega)-h_{k,l}(\tau_{-l}\omega)).

(15)

It is straightforward that (
14
) and (
15
) imply (
11
). The converse, however, is not true. It is actually a very subtle issue to determine whether a divergence free vector field (as e.g.
b_{k}(x,\omega)
given in (
5
)) can be written in this so-called divergence-form or not. More on this in the Appendix.

The symmetry conditions (
14
) mean that the lifted field
h:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{N}}\times{\mathcal{N}}}
,
h_{k,l}(x,\omega):=h_{k,l}(\tau_{x}\omega)
,
is a
random function of the oriented plaquettes
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, that is, a stationary (with respect to spatial translations)
stream tensor
. Note that in dimensions
d=2
and
d=3
, the stream fields
(x,k,l)\mapsto h_{k,l}(x,\omega):=h_{k,l}(\tau_{x}\omega)
are identified with a
scalar height function
on
\mathbb{Z}^{2*}:=\mathbb{Z}^{2}+(1/2,1/2)
, respectively, a
flow/vector field
on
\mathbb{Z}^{3*}:=\mathbb{Z}^{3}+(1/2,1/2,1/2)
.

In
[
14
]
the weak CLT (that is, in probability with respect to the environment) was established for the diffusively scaled displacement
T^{-1/2}X(T)
, as
T\to\infty
, under the conditions

\displaystyle s_{k}+(s_{k})^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}

(16)

imposed on the conductances and

\displaystyle h_{k,l}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}

(17)

imposed on the stream tensor. Throughout this paper
\mathcal{L}_{p}:=\mathcal{L}_{p}(\Omega,\pi)
, with
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Condition (
17
) is equivalent to
b_{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{-1}(|{\Delta}|)
. That is, the antisymmetric part of the jump rates belong to the
\mathcal{H}_{-1}
-space of the lattice Laplacian acting on
\mathcal{L}_{2}
.

The upper bound in (
16
) could be relaxed to
s_{k}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
without effort, and without altering the proof in
[
14
]
.
The lower bound (
strong ellipticity
) is, however, a serious issue. The main goal of this paper is to relax it to an integrability condition imposed on
(s_{k})^{-1}
.

1.3
The CLT

The local
quenched
drift of the random walk is

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\omega}\left(dX(t)\bigm|X(s):0\leq s\leq t\right)=\left(\phi(\eta_{t})+\psi(\eta_{t})\right)\,dt+o(dt).

(23)

where
\phi,\psi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are

\displaystyle\phi(\omega):=\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{N}}}ks_{k}(\omega),

\displaystyle\psi(\omega):=\sum_{k\in{\mathcal{N}}}kb_{k}(\omega).

(24)

Due to (
10
), respectively, (
11
) we have

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(\omega)=s_{e_{i}}(\omega)-s_{e_{i}}(\tau_{-e_{i}}\omega),

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(\omega)=b_{e_{i}}(\omega)+b_{e_{i}}(\tau_{-e_{i}}\omega).

(25)

In particular, from

(
19
)

we have
\phi\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi(\omega)\,d\pi(\omega)=0.

(26)

This does not hold a priori for the drift term
\psi
coming from the divergence-free antisymmetric part. However, from (
15
)
and

(
22
)

we have
\psi\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi(\omega)\,d\pi(\omega)=0.

(27)

From ergodicity under
\pi
of the environment process
t\mapsto\eta_{t}
, and from
(
23
), (
26
) and (
27
) the strong law of large numbers for the displacement of the random walker readily follows.

Proposition 1

(Strong Law of Large Numbers)
.

Assuming

(
19
)

and

(
22
)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}X(t)=0,\qquad\mathrm{a.s.}

The main result of this paper is:

Theorem 2

(Main theorem: CLT)
.

Assume the integrability conditions

(
19
)
,

(
20
)
,

(
21
)
.
Then the displacement
t\mapsto X(t)
of the random walk can be decomposed as

\displaystyle X(t)=Y(t)+Z(t)

so that the following limits hold as
N\to\infty
.

(i)

For
\pi
-almost all
\omega\in\Omega
,
t\mapsto Y(t)
is a nondegenerate square integrable martingale whose increments are stationary and ergodic in the annealed setting. Thus, due to the Martingale Invariance Principle (cf.
[
17
]
), for
\pi
-almost all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle N^{-1/2}Y(N\cdot)\Rightarrow W_{\sigma}(\cdot),

in
D([0,1])
under the quenched probability measure
\mathbf{P}_{\omega}\left(\dots\right)
, where
W_{\sigma}(\cdot)
is a Brownian motion with finite and non-degenerate covariance
\sigma^{2}
.

(ii)

For any
t\geq 0
and
\delta>0

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\big(N^{-1/2}|{Z(Nt)}|>\delta\big)\rightarrow 0.

Remark.

We should emphasize that this is much stronger than an annealed limit theorem. The invariance principle for the displacement of the random walk

\displaystyle N^{-1/2}X(N\cdot)\Rightarrow W_{\sigma}(\cdot),\qquad\text{ as }N\to\infty,

(28)

holds
in probability
(and not averaged out) with respect to the the distribution of the environment,
(\Omega,\pi)
. In other words, the invariance principle (
28
) holds
\pi
-a.s. (that is, in a quenched sense) along sequences
N\to\infty
increasing sufficiently fast.

1.4
Examples

We give some concrete constructive examples – not covered by earlier works – where the arguments of this paper work and provide a CLT (in the sense of Theorem

).

1.4.1
Adding stream term to random conductances

Let
\widetilde{s}_{k}(x,\omega)=\widetilde{s}_{k}(\tau_{x}\omega)
,
k\in\mathcal{N}
,
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, be random conductances as in (
10
) and assume that the integrability conditions (
18
) hold for them. It has been known since
[
4
]
that the random walk among these conductances obeys the CLT as stated in Theorem
2
. It is a natural question to ask how can one modify this random environment
by adding antisymmetric, nonreversible part to the jump rates
so that the nondegenerate CLT remains valid. Here is an answer.

Let
h_{k,l}(x,\omega)=h_{k,l}(\tau_{x}\omega)
,
k,l\in\mathcal{N}
,
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, be a stream tensor, as in (
14
) (defined on the same probability space, jointly with the conductances) and
b_{k}(x,\omega)=b_{k}(\tau_{x}\omega)
,
k\in\mathcal{N}
,
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, be the divergence-free flow/vector field given by (

). Let now

\displaystyle p_{k}(\omega):=\widetilde{s}_{k}(\omega)+2b_{k}(\omega)_{+}.

(That is

It will suffice to assume (beside (

) holding for

) that

In particular, if

on the other, are

then it suffices that

.

If this independence does not hold than,

and

will suffice.

means a perturbation of the random conductance model satisfying (

stream tensor. The case
\alpha=2
,
\beta=\infty
means divergence free drift field arising as the curl of an

stream tensor - that is, in
\mathcal{H}_{-1}(|{\Delta}|)
of the lattice-Laplacian, and strongly elliptic, fully covered in
[
14
]
.

1.4.3
RWRE with stationary density

Let
p_{k}(x,\omega)=p_{k}(\tau_{x}\omega)
,

,

, be the jump rates of a random walk in random environment, as in (

The proof of Theorem

Let

\displaystyle L=-S+A,

\displaystyle S:=-(L+L^{*})/2,

\displaystyle A:=(L-L^{*})/2,

0

We also define the self-adjoint operators

\lambda>0

\displaystyle\|{R_{\lambda}}\|_{2\to 2}\leq\lambda^{-1},

\displaystyle\|{S^{1/2}R_{\lambda}}\|_{2\to 2}\leq\lambda^{-1/2},

\displaystyle\|{R_{\lambda}S^{1/2}}\|_{2\to 2}\leq\lambda^{-1/2},

\displaystyle\|{S^{1/2}R_{\lambda}S^{1/2}}\|_{2\to 2}\leq 1,

and hence, for
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{-}
we have a priori

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda^{1/2}\|{R_{\lambda}\varphi}\|_{2}<\infty,

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\|{S^{1/2}R_{\lambda}\varphi}\|_{2}<\infty,

still somewhat short of (
64
).

(3)
Conditions (
64
) of Theorem
9
are difficult to check directly. (An exception, where this could be done, is the RWRE problem treated in
[
25
]
.) Sufficient conditions are known under the names of Strong Sector Condition, respectively, Graded Sector Condition. See the monograph
[
9
]
for context and details. However, these sector conditions hold only under very special structural assumptions about the Markov process considered: a graded structure of the infinitesimal generator
L
acting on an accordingly graded Hilbert space
\mathcal{L}_{2}
. This structural assumption simply doesn’t hold in many cases of interest, including our current problem.

The next theorem, quoted from
[
8
]
, provides a sufficient condition which does not assume a graded structure of the infinitesimal generator
L
acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{L}_{2}(\Omega,\pi)
. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:=\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{-}\cap\mathcal{L}_{2}:\ S^{-1/2}f\in\mathrm{Dom}(A),\ \ AS^{-1/2}f\in\mathcal{H}_{-}\cap\mathcal{L}_{2}\}

(66)

and
B:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}
defined as

\displaystyle Bf:=S^{-1/2}AS^{-1/2}f.

(67)

Note that the operator
B:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}
is unbounded (except for the cases when the operator
S:\mathcal{L}_{2}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}
is boundedly invertible) and skew symmetric. Indeed, for
f,g\in\mathcal{B}
all the straightforward steps below are legitimate

\displaystyle\langle{f,S^{-1/2}AS^{-1/2}g}\rangle\!=\!\langle{S^{-1/2}f,AS^{-1/2}g}\rangle\!=\!-\langle{AS^{-1/2}f,S^{-1/2}g}\rangle\!=\!-\langle{S^{-1/2}AS^{-1/2}f,g}\rangle

Of course, it could happen that the subspace
\mathcal{B}
is not dense in
\mathcal{L}_{2}
, or, even worse, that simply
\mathcal{B}=\{0\}
. Even if
\mathcal{B}
is a dense subspace in
\mathcal{L}_{2}
, in principle it could still happen that the operator
B
(which in this case is densely defined and skew-symmetric) is not essentially skew-self-adjoint.

Theorem 10

(
[
8
]
Theorem 1)
.

Assume that there exists a subspace
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{C}^{{\tt cl}2}
, and the operator
B:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}
is essentially skew-self-adjoint (that is,
\overline{B}=-B^{*}
). Then for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{-}\cap\mathcal{L}_{1}
the conditions of Theorem
9
(and hence the martingale approximation (
65
)) hold.

Remarks:

(1)
In
[
8
]
the theorem is formulated in slightly different terms. However, it is easy to see that this form follows directly from that of Theorem 1 in
[
8
]
.

(2)
The conditions of Theorem
10
are equivalent to
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{B}^{{\tt cl}2}
and
B:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}
essentially skew-self-adjoint. The formulation of the theorem allows flexibility in choosing the core
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
.

(3)
In view of Proposition
4
(iv) (
45
), the requirement that
B:=S^{-1/2}AS^{-1/2}
be a well-defined skew-self-adjoint operator acting on the Hilbert space
(\mathcal{L}_{2},\|{\cdot}\|_{2})
is equivalent to
A=(L-L^{*})/2
be a well-defined skew-self-adjoint operator acting on the Hilbert space
(\mathcal{H}_{+},\|{\cdot}\|_{+})
.

(4)
The condition stated in Proposition 2.1.2 of
[
19
]
is precisely a somewhat hidden form of von Neumann’s criterion for (skew-)self-adjointness (cf.
[
21
]
Theorem VIII.3) of the operator
B
defined above. The formulation in Theorem
10
allows for checking skew-self-adjointness in various other ways, as demonstrated in the forthcoming proof of our main result.

4.2
Proof of Theorem
2

We check the conditions of Theorem
10
for the concrete case under consideration, when the operators
L
,
S=-(L+L^{*})/2
, and
A=(L-L^{*})/2
given in (
4
), (
9
), (
37
) are the infinitesimal generator of the environment process
t\mapsto\eta_{t}
cf. (
3
), acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{L}_{2}
, and its self-adjoint and skew-self-adjoint parts.

In this case the subspace
\mathcal{B}
of (
66
) is

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:=\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{-}\cap\mathcal{L}_{2}:\ S^{-1/2}f\in\mathrm{Dom}(\nabla^{*}H\nabla),\ \ \nabla^{*}H\nabla S^{-1/2}f\in\mathcal{H}_{-}\cap\mathcal{L}_{2}\}

and the operator
B:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}
of (
67
) acts as

\displaystyle Bf:=S^{-1/2}\nabla^{*}H\nabla S^{-1/2}f=\Lambda^{*}R^{-1}HR^{-1}\Lambda f=\Lambda^{*}\Pi R^{-1}HR^{-1}\Pi\Lambda f

Recall from (
41
) the common core
\mathcal{A}
of the operators
S
and
A
, and (noting that
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathrm{Dom}(S)\subset\mathrm{Dom}(S^{1/2})
) let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle:=\{f=S^{1/2}g:g\in\mathcal{A}\}

The proof of essential skew-self-adjointness of the operator
B:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}
relies on various parts of Proposition
5
, thus only on the integrability condition

(
19
)
.

Obviously,
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{-}\cap\mathcal{L}_{2}
and since
\mathcal{A}
is a core for
S
and
\mathrm{Ker}(S)=\mathrm{Ran}(S)^{\perp}=\{0\}
, we also have
\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{C}^{{\tt cl}2}
. For
f\in\mathcal{C}
, the equation
f=S^{1/2}g
determines
uniquely

g\in\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\nabla^{*}H\nabla S^{-1/2}f=\nabla^{*}R\,\underbrace{R^{-1}H\nabla\underbrace{g}_{\in\mathcal{A}}}_{\in\mathcal{V}_{\infty}}\in\mathcal{H}_{-}\cap\mathcal{L}_{2}.

The last step is due to Proposition
5
(iii).
Thus, indeed,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{C}^{{\tt cl}2},

where the superscript
cl2
denotes closure with respect to the norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{2}
.

Proposition 11

(Skew-self-adjointness of
S^{-1/2}AS^{-1/2}
)
.

The linear operator
B:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}
is essentially skew-self-adjoint.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}:=\Lambda\mathcal{C}=\{R\nabla g:g\in\mathcal{A}\}\subset\mathcal{K}_{2}=\mathcal{D}^{{\tt cl}2}

and define the operator
D:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{K}_{2}
as
D:=\Lambda B|_{\mathcal{C}}\Lambda^{*}
. That is,

\displaystyle D:=\Pi R^{-1}HR^{-1}\Pi,

\displaystyle Du:=\underbrace{\Pi\underbrace{R^{-1}H\underbrace{R^{-1}\underbrace{u}_{\in\mathcal{D}}}_{\in\nabla\mathcal{A}}}_{\in\mathcal{V}_{\infty}}}_{\in\mathcal{K}_{2}},

(68)

where
\Lambda,\Lambda^{*},\Pi
are the Riesz operators and orthogonal projection defined in (
50
), (
51
).

Since
\Lambda:\mathcal{L}_{2}\to\mathcal{K}_{2}
,
\Lambda^{*}:\mathcal{K}_{2}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}
are
isometries
, the proposition will follow from proving that the operator
D:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{K}_{2}
is essentially skew-self-adjoint. This is what we are going to prove.

The operator
D:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{K}_{2}
is skew symmetric.
Indeed, from the choice of the core
\mathcal{A}
is follows that
u\in\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
readily implies that

R^{-1}u\in\nabla\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{U}_{\infty}
,

HR^{-1}u\in\mathcal{V}_{\infty}
,

R^{-1}HR^{-1}u\in\mathcal{V}_{\infty}
also hold, and thus all steps of the following chain are legitimate (not merely formal)

\displaystyle\langle{u,R^{-1}HR^{-1}v}\rangle=\langle{R^{-1}u,HR^{-1}v}\rangle=-\langle{HR^{-1}u,R^{-1}v}\rangle=-\langle{R^{-1}HR^{-1}u,v}\rangle

Next we define the adjoint (over the Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}_{2}
) of
D:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{K}_{2}
. Its domain is

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{*}:=\{w\in\mathcal{K}_{2}:\exists c=c(w)<\infty:\forall u\in\mathcal{D}:|{\langle{w,R^{-1}HR^{-1}u}\rangle}|\leq c\|{u}\|_{2}\},

and
D^{*}:\mathcal{D}^{*}\to\mathcal{K}_{2}
is defined uniquely by the Riesz Lemma: for any
w\in\mathcal{D}^{*}
,
D^{*}w
is the unique element of
\mathcal{K}_{2}
such that for all
u\in\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle\langle{D^{*}w,u}\rangle=\langle{w,R^{-1}HR^{-1}u}\rangle.

Obviously,
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{D}^{*}
,
D^{*}|_{\mathcal{D}}=-D
, and

\displaystyle D\prec D^{**}\preceq-D^{*}

In order to conclude

\displaystyle D^{**}=-D^{*}

and thus essential skew-self-adjointness of
D
, as defined in (
68
), it is sufficient to prove that
D^{*}
is skew-symmetric on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
.

For
K<\infty
, let

\displaystyle r^{K}_{k}(\omega):=r_{k}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}{(K^{-1}\leq r_{k}(\omega)\leq K)},

\displaystyle h^{K}_{k,l}(\omega):=h_{k,l}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}{(|{h_{k,l}(\omega)}|\leq K)}.

These truncated functions inherit the conductance symmetries (
6
) and stream-tensor (anti)symmetries (
14
). We define the
bounded
operators
R^{K},(R^{K})^{-1},H^{K}:\mathcal{V}_{2}\to\mathcal{V}_{2}
by the formulas (
32
), (
33
), (
34
), with the functions
r_{k}
,
h_{k,l}
replaced by their truncated versions
r^{K}_{k}
,
h^{K}_{k,l}
.

Lemma 12
.

For
w\in\mathcal{D}^{*}

\displaystyle D^{*}w=-\wlim_{K\to\infty}\Pi\,(R^{K})^{-1}\,H^{K}\,(R^{K})^{-1}\,w,

(69)

where
\wlim
denotes weak limit in the Hilbert space
(\mathcal{K}_{2},\|{\cdot}\|_{2})
.

Proof.

This is straightforward. Let
w\in\mathcal{D}^{*}
and
u\in\mathcal{D}
. Then

\displaystyle-\lim_{K\to\infty}\langle{(R^{K})^{-1}\,H^{K}\,(R^{K})^{-1}w,u}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{K\to\infty}\langle{w,(R^{K})^{-1}\,H^{K}\,(R^{K})^{-1}u}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{w,R^{-1}\,H\,R^{-1}u}\rangle=\langle{D^{*}w,u}\rangle.

The first step is legitimate, since for
K<\infty
, the operator
(R^{K})^{-1}\,H^{K}\,(R^{K})^{-1}
is bounded and skew-self-adjoint. The second step follows from the fact that (due to the choice of the core
\mathcal{A}
) for any
u=R\nabla g
,
g\in\mathcal{A}
, there exists a
K_{0}<\infty
, such that for any
K>K_{0}
,
(R^{K})^{-1}\,H^{K}\,(R^{K})^{-1}u=R^{-1}\,H\,R^{-1}u
.

Finally, since
\mathcal{K}_{2}=\mathcal{D}^{{\tt cl}2}
, (
69
) follows.
∎

From (
69
) the skew-symmetry of the operator
D^{*}:\mathcal{D}^{*}\to\mathcal{K}_{2}
drops out: for
u,w\in\mathcal{D}^{*}

\displaystyle\langle{w,D^{*}u}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{K\to\infty}\langle{w,(R^{K})^{-1}\,H^{K}\,(R^{K})^{-1}u}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\lim_{K\to\infty}\langle{(R^{K})^{-1}\,H^{K}\,(R^{K})^{-1}w,u}\rangle=-\langle{D^{*}w,u}\rangle.

This concludes the proof of essential skew-self-adjointness of the operator
D:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{K}_{2}
. We also conclude essential skew-self-adjointness of the operator
B=\Lambda^{*}D\Lambda
on the core
\mathcal{C}=\Lambda^{*}\mathcal{D}
.
∎

This also concludes checking all conditions of Theorem
10
in the concrete setting and thus also the proof of Theorem
2
.

Appendix: Helmholtz’s Theorem

The content of this Appendix sheds light on the problem of when and how can a divergence-free flow/vector field expressed in divergence form, as in (
14
)&(
15
). As we
assumed
that this is the case for the antisymmetric part of the jump rates in (
5
), in a formal logical sense the mathematical content of the paper does not rely on Proposition
14
below.

As noted in section
1.1
, given a stationary and ergodic, divergence-free vector field/flow

b:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}}
, as, e.g. that given in (
5
), it is a difficult and subtle issue to decide whether it can be written in divergence form, cf (
14
)&(
15
) or not. (Except, of course, if it is a priori given in this form.)

The following statement sheds light on this problem. It is an ergodic variant of what is usually referred to as
Helmholtz’s Theorem
from classical electrodynamics: "In
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, a divergence-free vector field (e.g., a time-wise stationary magnetic field) is written as the curl/rotation of a vector potential." (To be taken with a grain of salt!)

Proposition 14

("Helmholtz’s Theorem")
.

(i)

Assume
b\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
.
There exists a stream tensor field
H:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{N}}
with
\displaystyle H_{k,l}(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{L}_{1{\rm w}}:=\{f\in\mathcal{L}:\|{f}\|_{1{\rm w}}:=\sup_{0<\lambda<\infty}\lambda\pi(|{f}|>\lambda)<\infty\}
,

\displaystyle H_{-k,l}(x+k,\omega)\!=\!H_{k,-l}(x+l,\omega)\!=\!H_{l,k}(x,\omega)\!=\!-H_{k,l}(x,\omega)

(71)

with stationary increments

\displaystyle H_{k,l}(y,\omega)-H_{k,l}(x,\omega)=H_{k,l}(y-x,\tau_{y}\omega)-H_{k,l}(0,\tau_{x}\omega)\color[rgb]{0,0,0},

(72)

such that Helmholtz’s relation holds

\displaystyle b_{k}(x,\omega)=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{U}}H_{k,l}(x,\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{U}}(H_{k,l}(x,\omega)-H_{k,l}(x-l,\omega))=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{U}}H_{k,l}(0,\tau_{x}\omega)

(73)

(ii)

If
b\in\mathcal{L}_{p}
,
p\in(1,2]
than the same hold with
H_{k,l}(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{L}_{p}
.

(iii)

Assume
b\in\mathcal{L}_{2}
.
There exists a
stationary
stream field
h:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{cN\times\mathcal{N}}
with
h_{k,l}(x,\omega)=h_{k,l}(\tau_{x}\omega)
,
h_{k,l}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}
such that

\displaystyle H_{k,l}(x,\omega)=h_{k,l}(x,\omega)-h_{k,l}(0,\omega)

(74)

if and only if
b_{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{-1}(|{\Delta}|):=\{f\in\mathcal{L}_{2}:\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\searrow 0}\langle{f,(\lambda I-\Delta)^{-1}f}\rangle<\infty\}
.

As we
assume
(
14
)&(
15
)) the mathematical content of this paper formally does not rely on
roposition
14
. Therefore, rather than presenting its full proof we only give some hints.

Hints to some elements of the proof of Proposition
14
.

The
\mathcal{L}_{2}
setting of part (ii) and part (iii) appear as Proposition 11 in
[
14
]
, with complete proofs.

The
\mathcal{L}_{1}
setting of part (i) is more subtle. From an appropriate adaptation of the Calderón-Zygmund Decomposition (see e.g.
[
24
]
) to the ergodic context
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\pi,\tau_{x}:\Omega\to\Omega:x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, it follows that for
f\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
,
\|{|{\Delta}|^{-1}\partial_{l}\partial_{k}f}\|_{1{\rm w}}\leq C\|{f}\|_{1}
,
k,l\in\mathcal{N}
, where the constant
C
depends only on the dimension
d
. Once this is established, the arguments from the proof of Proposition 11 in
[
14
]
yield the result. A similar (though, not identical) adaptation of the Calderón-Zygmund theory to a random conductance setting appears in
[
3
]
.

Given (i) in the
\mathcal{L}_{1}\to\mathcal{L}_{1{\rm w}}
setting, and (ii) in the
\mathcal{L}_{2}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}
setting, (ii) in the
\mathcal{L}_{p}\to\mathcal{L}_{p}

1<p<2

[

]
.

1.
Introduction

The study of symbolic powers of ideals has historically played a central role in commutative algebra. For instance, symbolic powers featured prominently in the classical proof of Krull’s principal ideal theorem, and they were also used in the proof of the Hartshorne–Lichtenbaum vanishing theorem. More generally, symbolic powers capture the intricate algebraic and geometric constraints imposed by the minimal primes of an ideal, revealing subtleties that ordinary powers alone cannot detect. A central problem in this area concerns understanding the containment relations between symbolic and ordinary powers. In particular, significant attention has been devoted to characterizing ideals for which symbolic powers coincide with ordinary powers. This question has been extensively studied due to its deep connections with combinatorics, homological invariants, and the geometry of the underlying varieties (see, for instance,
[
5
,
16
]
).

In the case of squarefree monomial ideals, the study of symbolic powers is closely related to the so-called packing problem. Let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal in the polynomial ring
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field. Let
\mathcal{H}
denote a clutter with vertex set
V(\mathcal{H})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and edge set
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H})
. The ideal
I
can be expressed as the edge ideal

I(\mathcal{H})=\langle\mathbf{x}_{\mathcal{E}}:=\prod_{x_{i}\in\mathcal{E}}x_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}\in E(\mathcal{H})\rangle.

We say that
\mathcal{H}
satisfies the
König property
if the height of
I(\mathcal{H})
equals the maximum number of independent edges of
\mathcal{H}
(known as the
matching number
of
\mathcal{H}
). A clutter
\mathcal{H}
is said to satisfy the
packing property
if all its minors satisfy the König property.

In 1993, Conforti and Cornuéjols
[
3
]
formulated a conjecture asserting that a clutter satisfies the packing property if and only if it satisfies the max–flow min–cut (MFMC) property. Notably, the MFMC property of a clutter is intimately related to the existence of integral optimal solutions to the linear programming duality system associated with its incidence matrix. This conjecture can be reformulated in purely algebraic terms
[
5
]
, leading to the following equivalence proposed in
[
1
]
(see also
[
2
]
):

Conjecture 1.1

(Conforti–Cornuéjols Conjecture)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a clutter, and let
I(\mathcal{H})
denote its edge ideal. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

I(\mathcal{H})^{(k)}=I(\mathcal{H})^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
;

(ii)

\mathcal{H}
satisfies the packing property.

Over the past three decades, there has been substantial progress toward verifying this conjecture. It is known to hold for several important classes of clutters, including balanced clutters
[
10
]
, binary clutters
[
14
]
, dyadic clutters
[
4
]
, and a family of
2
-partitionable clutters
[
9
]
. In the context of edge ideals of clutters, the conjecture is verified in a few special cases, including edge ideals of graphs
[
15
]
,
3
-path ideals of graphs
[
1
]
, and
4
-path ideals of graphs
[
13
]
.

In this direction, a closely related and more challenging problem is the following.

Question 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a clutter, and let
I(\mathcal{H})
denote its edge ideal. For a fixed positive integer
k
, can one provide a combinatorial classification of all clutters
\mathcal{H}
for which
I(\mathcal{H})^{(k)}=I(\mathcal{H})^{k}
?

Even for the class of
d
-uniform clutters, the problem remains largely open; to the best of our knowledge, a complete solution is known only in the cases
d=1
and
d=n
. In fact, when
d=1
, each vertex of the clutter
\mathcal{H}
is an edge, and hence
I(\mathcal{H})
is generated by variables. On the other hand, when
d=n
, the clutter
\mathcal{H}
contains a single edge, and
I(\mathcal{H})
is a principal ideal. Thus, for
d=1
and
d=n
, symbolic powers coincide with ordinary powers.

Our main result of this paper provides a complete answer to
1.1
and a verification of
1.1
for
(n-2)
-uniform clutters. Our approach is based on expressing the edge ideal of such a clutter as the complementary edge ideal of an associated graph (see
Section

2
for the definition). Recall that a clutter
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
d
-uniform
if
|\mathcal{E}|=d
for every
\mathcal{E}\in E(\mathcal{H})
. The
2
-uniform clutters are well-known in the literature as graphs, while an
(n-2)
-uniform clutter can be naturally associated with a graph
G_{\mathcal{H}}
defined in the following way:

V(G_{\mathcal{H}})=V(\mathcal{H}),\qquad E(G_{\mathcal{H}})=\{V(\mathcal{H})\setminus\mathcal{E}\mid\mathcal{E}\in E(\mathcal{H})\}.

Under this correspondence,
I(\mathcal{H})=I_{c}(G_{\mathcal{H}})
, where
I_{c}(G_{\mathcal{H}})
denotes the
complementary edge ideal
of
G_{\mathcal{H}}
. The complementary edge ideal of a graph was introduced independently and almost simultaneously by Hibi–Qureshi–Madani
[
12
]
and Ficarra–Moradi
[
6
]
, and has since been the subject of intensive study
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
12
]
. Their works reveal deep combinatorial patterns governing the algebraic properties of the corresponding ideals and their powers. Using the language of complementary edge ideal, we provide a complete solution to
1.1
and confirm the Conforti–Cornuéjols Conjecture for
(n-2)
-uniform clutters in the following theorem.

Theorem
2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
(n-2)
-uniform clutter with
|V(\mathcal{H})|=n
. Let
G_{\mathcal{H}}
denote the associated graph such that
I(\mathcal{H})=I_{c}(G_{\mathcal{H}})
. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

I(\mathcal{H})^{(k)}=I(\mathcal{H})^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
;

(2)

I(\mathcal{H})^{(k)}=I(\mathcal{H})^{k}
for some
k\geq 2
;

(3)

\mathcal{H}
satisfies the MFMC property;

(4)

\mathcal{H}
satisfies the packing property;

(5)

G_{\mathcal{H}}
is either a
K_{2}
,
K_{3}
,
P_{3}
,
2K_{2}
,
P_{4}
or
C_{4}
with (possibly) some isolated vertices.

In the next section, we show that the class of
(n-2)
-uniform clutters is not properly contained in any of the classes of clutters mentioned above for which the Conforti-Cornuéjols conjecture is known to hold.
More precisely, for each of these classes, we construct
(n-2)
-uniform clutters that do not belong to that class; that is, for the classes of balanced clutters, binary clutters, dyadic clutters, and
2
-partitionable clutters considered in
[
9
]
, we exhibit examples of
(n-2)
-uniform clutters which fall outside each respective class. Note that for each
n\geq 7
, no
(n-2)
-uniform clutter can be realized as the
3
-path ideal or the
4
-path ideal of a graph.

It is worth noting that the result stated in
Theorem

2.1
goes beyond a mere verification of the
1.1
for
(n-2)
-uniform clutters. In fact, there exist examples of
2
-uniform clutters (graphs)
G
for which
I(G)^{(k)}=I(G)^{k}
holds for some fixed positive integer
k
, even though
G
fails to satisfy the MFMC property (see
Remark

2.3
).

2.
Main results

In this section, we present the proof of
Theorem

2.1
.
As the argument is primarily algebraic in nature, we omit the definitions of various combinatorial notions, such as the König property, packing property, max-flow min-cut property, minors of a clutter, induced subgraphs, minimal vertex covers, matchings, etc., and refer the reader to the excellent book by Villarreal
[
17
]
for a detailed discussion on these topics.

As noted in
Section

1
, the edge ideal of an
(n-2)
-uniform clutter on the vertex set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
can be realized as the complementary edge ideal of an associated graph
G_{\mathcal{H}}
. Consequently, to verify the equality
I(\mathcal{H})^{(k)}=I(\mathcal{H})^{k}
for an
(n-2)
-uniform clutter, it suffices to study the ordinary and symbolic powers of the complementary edge ideal of
G_{\mathcal{H}}
. For notational simplicity, we shall denote this graph by
G
and its complementary edge ideal by
I_{c}(G)
. By definition,
I_{c}(G)=\left\langle\mathbf{x}_{\bar{e}}\mid e\in E(G)\right\rangle
is a squarefree monomial ideal in
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
\bar{e}=V(G)\setminus e
and
\mathbf{x}_{\bar{e}}=\prod_{x_{i}\in\bar{e}}x_{i}
..

For a finite simple graph
G
, the primary decomposition of
I_{c}(G)
admits a combinatorial description, which we present below. Throughout, we adopt the convention that for any subset
A\subseteq\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{A}
is generated by the variables in
A
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph with at least one edge. Then

I_{c}(G)=\Big(\bigcap_{x\text{ isolated in }G}\mathfrak{p}_{\{x\}}\Big)\;\cap\;\Big(\bigcap_{e\in E(G^{c})}\mathfrak{p}_{e}\Big)\;\cap\;\Big(\bigcap_{G[A]\cong K_{3}}\mathfrak{p}_{A}\Big).

Proof.

Since
G
contains at least one edge, it follows that
I_{c}(G)\neq(0)
, and thus
\mathcal{G}(I_{c}(G))\neq\emptyset
. Moreover, if
x
is an isolated vertex of
G
, then for each
m\in\mathcal{G}(I_{c}(G))
we have
x\mid m
, where
\mathcal{G}(I)
denotes the unique minimal monomial generating set of a monomial ideal
I
. Thus,
\mathfrak{p}_{\{x\}}
is a minimal prime ideal of
I_{c}(G)
. The result now follows from
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
(see also
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
).
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the
(n-2)
-uniform clutter such that
I(\mathcal{H})=I_{c}(G)
. Then the minimal prime ideal of
I_{c}(G)
corresponds to the minimal vertex covers of
\mathcal{H}
.

Before going to our main results, let us recall the definition of symbolic powers.

Definition 2.1

(Symbolic power)
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and
I\subset R
be an ideal. Then, the
k^{th}
symbolic power of
I
has the following two definitions.

(1)

\displaystyle I^{(k)}=\bigcap_{P\in\mathrm{Ass}(R/I)}(I^{k}R_{P}\cap R).

(2)

\displaystyle I^{(k)}=\bigcap_{P\in\mathrm{Min}(I)}(I^{k}R_{P}\cap R),
where
\mathrm{Min}(I)
is the set of all minimal prime of
I
.

Note that for a squarefree monomial ideal
I
, the two definitions of symbolic power discussed above coincide, as
I
has no embedded primes. More precisely, for a squarefree monomial ideal
I
, we have
I^{(k)}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Ass}(I)}\mathfrak{p}^{k}
,
where
\mathrm{Ass}(I)
denotes the set of associated primes of
I
. Consequently, using
Proposition

2.1
, we obtain

I_{c}(G)^{k}=\Big(\bigcap_{x\text{ isolated in }G}\mathfrak{p}_{\{x\}}^{k}\Big)\;\cap\;\Big(\bigcap_{e\in E(G^{c})}\mathfrak{p}_{e}^{k}\Big)\;\cap\;\Big(\bigcap_{G[A]\cong K_{3}}\mathfrak{p}_{A}^{k}\Big),

for each positive integer
k
.

The following result addresses the equality of symbolic and ordinary powers of complementary edge ideals in the presence of isolated vertices in the underlying graph.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a graph and
x
an isolated vertex of
G
. Let
\widehat{G}=G\setminus x
. The for each positive integer
k
,
I_{c}(G)^{(k)}=I_{c}(G)^{k}
if and only if
I_{c}(\widehat{G})^{(k)}=I_{c}(\widehat{G})^{k}
.

Proof.

If
G
does not contain any edge, then
I_{c}(G)=I_{c}(\widehat{G})=\langle 0\rangle
, and we clearly have the result. Therefore, we may assume
G
contains at least one edge. By definition of symbolic powers, we have
I_{c}(G)^{k}\subseteq I_{c}(G)^{(k)}
and
I_{c}(\widehat{G})^{k}\subseteq I_{c}(\widehat{G})^{(k)}
.

Suppose
I_{c}(G)^{k}=I_{c}(G)^{(k)}
and take
m\in\mathcal{G}(I_{c}(\widehat{G})^{(k)})
. Then, by
Proposition

2.1
,
x^{k}\,m\in I_{c}(G)^{(k)}=I_{c}(G)^{k}
. Therefore, there exists a monomial
m^{\prime}
such that

x^{k}\,m=m^{\prime}\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{\bar{e}_{i}},

where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\in E(G)
. Observe that
x\in\bar{e}_{i}
for each
i\in[k]
, where
\bar{e}_{i}=V(G)\setminus e_{i}
. Hence, we can write
x^{k}m=x^{k}m^{\prime}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{\bar{f}_{i}}
, where
f_{i}=e_{i}\setminus\{x\}\in E(\widehat{G})
for each
i\in[k]
and
\bar{f}_{i}=V(\widehat{G})\setminus f_{i}
. In other words,
m\in I_{c}(\widehat{G})^{k}
, as required.

Next, suppose
I_{c}(\widehat{G})^{k}=I_{c}(\widehat{G})^{(k)}
, and take
m\in\mathcal{G}(I_{c}(G)^{(k)})
. Then, again by
Proposition

2.1
,
m=x^{k}m_{1}
, where
m_{1}\in I_{c}(\widehat{G})^{(k)}=I_{c}(\widehat{G})^{k}
. Therefore,
m_{1}=m_{1}^{\prime}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{\bar{g}_{i}}
, where
g_{i}\in E(\widehat{G})
for each
i\in[k]
and
\bar{g}_{i}=V(\widehat{G})\setminus g_{i}
. Note that
x\,\mathbf{x}_{\bar{g}_{i}}\in I_{c}(G)
for each
i\in[k]
. Thus
m=x^{k}m_{1}^{\prime}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{\bar{g}_{i}}\in I_{c}(G)^{k}
, and this completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
(n-2)
-uniform clutter with
|V(\mathcal{H})|=n
. Let
G
denote the associated graph such that
I(\mathcal{H})=I_{c}(G)
. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

I(\mathcal{H})^{(k)}=I(\mathcal{H})^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
;

(2)

I(\mathcal{H})^{(k)}=I(\mathcal{H})^{k}
for some
k\geq 2
;

(3)

\mathcal{H}
satisfies the MFMC property;

(4)

\mathcal{H}
satisfies the packing property;

(5)

G
is either a
K_{2}
,
K_{3}
,
P_{3}
,
2K_{2}
,
P_{4}
or
C_{4}
with (possibly) some isolated vertices.

Proof.

The equivalence
(1)\iff(3)
follows from
[

, Theorem 14.3.6]
. Moreover,
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
is easy to see. Furthermore,
(5)\Rightarrow(1)
follows from
Proposition

2.2
and
Lemma

2.1
.

To prove
(2)\Rightarrow(5)
, we take
V(\mathcal{H})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and
I(\mathcal{H})^{(k)}=I(\mathcal{H})^{k}
. Let
G
be the associated graph such that
I(\mathcal{H})=I_{c}(G)
. Then we have
I_{c}(G)^{k}=I_{c}(G)^{(k)}
. If
|V(G)|\leq 4
, then we obtain
(5)
by using
Proposition

2.2
and
Lemma

2.1
. Therefore, we may assume that
|V(G)|\geq 5
. First consider the case when
G
is a star graph, i.e.,
E(G)=\{\{x_{1},x_{i}\}\mid i\in[n]\setminus\{1\}\}
. In this case, by
Proposition

2.1
, we have

I_{c}(G)=\bigcap_{\underset{r,s>1}{r\neq s}}\langle x_{r},x_{s}\rangle.

Observe that
\prod_{i=2}^{n}x_{i}\in I_{c}(G)^{(2)}
, and if

, then
m=\mathbf{x}_{\bar{e}}^{k-2}\prod_{i=2}^{n}x_{i}\in I_{c}(G)^{k-2}I_{c}(G)^{(2)}\subseteq I_{c}(G)^{(k)}

. Thus, if

, then we must have

. Consequently,

, a contradiction. Thus, from now on, we may assume
G
is not a star graph and
|V(G)|\geq 5
. Let us choose a vertex

Claim:

\mathbf{x}_{\bar{e}}^{k-2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)\in I_{c}(G)^{(k)}
.

Proof of Claim:
Since
I_{c}(G)^{t}\subseteq I_{c}(G)^{(t)}
for every

, it suffices to show that

\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\in I_{c}(G)^{(2)}.

By
Proposition

2.1
, we have

I_{c}(G)=\Bigg(\bigcap_{e\in E(G^{c})}\mathfrak{p}_{e}\Bigg)\;\cap\;\Bigg(\bigcap_{G[A]\cong K_{3}}\mathfrak{p}_{A}\Bigg).

Note that for each
e\in E(G^{c})
and each subset

with

, there exists at least one squarefree monomial of degree
2
in both
\mathfrak{p}_{e}^{2}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{A}^{2}
. Therefore, to show that
\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\in I_{c}(G)^{(2)}
, it suffices to verify that for every vertex

or

for some

and

with

. This indeed holds, since

is not a star graph. Hence, the proof of the claim is complete.

Now, since
I_{c}(G)^{k}=I_{c}(G)^{(k)}
, we have
\mathbf{x}_{\bar{e}}^{k-2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)\in I_{c}(G)^{k}
. However, the minimal generators of
I_{c}(G)^{k}
have degree
(n-2)k
. Therefore, we must have
k(n-2)\leq n+(k-2)(n-2)
. But this implies
n\leq 4
, a contradiction to our hypothesis. This completes the proof of
(2)\Rightarrow(5)
.

Observe that to complete the proof of the theorem, it is enough to show
(4)\iff(5)
. Now, suppose
(5)
holds. Then
I_{c}(G)=mJ
for some monomial
m
and a monomial ideal
J
with support disjoint from
m
, where either
J=S
or
J
is generated by variables, or
J
is an edge ideal of a bipartite graph. Thus
(4)
follows from
[
11
, Proposition 4.27]
. Next, we assume
(4)
, i.e., the clutter
\mathcal{H}
satisfies the packing property. Let
G
be the associated graph of
\mathcal{H}
such that
I_{c}(G)=I(\mathcal{H})
. If
G
has an isolated vertex
x
, consider the graph
\widehat{G}=G\setminus\{x\}
. Then, by definition,
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}
also has the packing property, where
I(\widehat{\mathcal{H}})=I_{c}(\widehat{G})
. Thus, a priori, we may assume that
G
has no isolated vertex. If
n\geq 4
, then by
Proposition

2.1
,
\mathrm{ht}(I_{c}(G))=2
, and since
\mathcal{H}
satisfies the packing property, there should exists two monomials
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathcal{G}(I_{c}(G))
with disjoint support of variables. Therefore, we have
\deg(g_{1})+\deg(g_{2})=2n-4\leq n
, which gives
n\leq 4
. For
n=4
,
I_{c}(G)
is an edge ideal of a graph with packing property, and thus, that graph must be bipartite by
[
11
, Proposition 4.27]
. Then from
Figure

1
, one can easily verify that
G
must be
2K_{2}
,
P_{4}
or
C_{4}
if
n=4
and
G
has no isolated vertex. For
n<4
, it is straightforward that
I_{c}(G)
has the packing property for any graph
G
. Hence,
G
can be
K_{2},K_{3}
or
P_{3}
.
∎

Comparision with other clutters:

In the final part of this section, we construct, for each of the following classes,
(n-2)
-uniform clutters that do not belong to that class: balanced clutters, binary clutters, dyadic clutters, and the
2
-partitionable clutters considered in
[
9
]
.
Note that for each of these classes, the Conforti-Cornuéjols conjecture is verified, as mentioned in
Section

1
.

\bullet

A clutter
C
is
balanced
if its incidence matrix contains no square submatrix of odd order with exactly two
1
’s in each row and column, that is, if
C
has no special odd cycles
[
17
, Definition 6.5.15]
.
For
n=4
, the
2
-uniform clutter
\mathcal{H}
with
V(\mathcal{H})=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}
and edge set
E(\mathcal{H})=\{x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},x_{1}x_{3},x_{1}x_{4}\}
is not balanced, since its incidence matrix contains the submatrix corresponding to the vertex set
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
with exactly two
1
’s in each row and column.
Similarly, for
n\geq 5
, the
(n-2)
-uniform clutter
\mathcal{H}
with
V(\mathcal{H})=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and edge set

E(\mathcal{H})=\Big\{\prod_{\underset{i\neq 3}{i=1}}^{n-1}x_{i},\prod_{i=2}^{n-1}x_{i},\prod_{\underset{i\neq 2}{i=1}}^{n-1}x_{i},\prod_{i=3}^{n}x_{i}\Big\}

is not balanced, as its incidence matrix contains the submatrix on the vertex set
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
with exactly two
1
’s in each row and column.

\bullet

A clutter
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
binary
if each edge and each minimal vertex cover of
\mathcal{H}
intersect in an odd number of vertices.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be the
(n-2)
-uniform clutter on the vertex set
V(\mathcal{H})=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
with edge set
\{e_{i}\mid i\in[n-1]\}
, where

e_{i}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},\widehat{x_{i}},\widehat{x_{i+1}},x_{i+2},\dots,x_{n}\}.

Observe that
I(\mathcal{H})
is the complementary edge ideal of
P_{n}
, and by
Proposition

2.1
,
\{x_{1},x_{n}\}
is a minimal vertex cover of
\mathcal{H}
.
Since
|e_{2}\cap\{x_{1},x_{n}\}|=2
, it follows that
\mathcal{H}
is not a binary clutter.

\bullet

A clutter
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
dyadic
if each edge and each minimal vertex cover of
\mathcal{H}
intersect in at most two vertices.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be the
(n-2)
-uniform clutter on the vertex set
V(\mathcal{H})=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
with edge set

\{e_{i},e_{n}\mid i\in[n-1]\},

where
e_{i}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},\widehat{x_{i}},\widehat{x_{i+1}},x_{i+2},\dots,x_{n}\}
,
e_{n}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}\}
. Observe that
I(\mathcal{H})
is the complementary edge ideal of the graph with edge set

\{\{x_{i},x_{i+1}\},\{x_{n-2},x_{n}\}\mid i\in[n-1]\}
, and by
Proposition

2.1
,
\{x_{n-2},x_{n-1},x_{n}\}
is a minimal vertex cover of
\mathcal{H}
.
Since
|e_{1}\cap\{x_{n-2},x_{n-1},x_{n}\}|=3
, it follows that
\mathcal{H}
is not a dyadic clutter.

\bullet

It is clear that not every
(n-2)
-uniform clutter is a
2
-partitionable clutter of the form
Q_{pq}^{F}
defined in
[
9
]
, for which the Conforti-Cornuéjols conjecture is known to hold, since
|V(Q_{pq}^{F})|
is always even.

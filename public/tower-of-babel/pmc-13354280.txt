Safety and Efficacy of Double-Effect Kissing Balloon Technique in Left Main Bifurcation Percutaneous Coronary Intervention: Design and Rationale of Japanese Coronary Intervention Using Drug-Eluting and Perfusion Therapy for Left Main Disease Registry
Authors: Takayuki Warisawa, Shuhei Odagiri, Masanobu Ishii, Toshiyuki Nagai, Taku Asano, Yohei Sotomi, Mamoru Nanasato, Kazuya Kawai, Hitoshi Matsuo, Shigeru Saito
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe Japanese Coronary Intervention Using Drug-Eluting and Perfusion Therapy for Left Main Disease Registry (JDEPTH-LM Registry) is a prospective, multicenter observational study in 32 Japanese institutions evaluating the double-effect kissing balloon technique (W-KBT) as a novel strategy for left main trunk percutaneous coronary intervention (LM-PCI). This approach combines provisional crossover stenting, proximal optimization technique, KBT, and prolonged (≥30 s) KBT using a perfusion balloon for the left anterior descending artery and a drug-coated balloon for the left circumflex (LCx) ostium.MethodsA total of 280 patients with stable/unstable angina or non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction and LCx ostial involvement will be enrolled. The co-primary endpoints are procedural success and major adverse cardiovascular events at 12 months. The JDEPTH-LM Registry will provide clinical data on the safety and efficacy of W-KBT, which may enhance outcomes in LM-PCI involving the LCx ostial lesion.ConclusionThese findings could contribute to the optimization of complex LM-PCI strategies in clinical practice.

Introduction

Due to the advent of drug-eluting stent, the use of intravascular ultrasound (IVUS), the application of coronary physiology, and the availability of optimal medical therapies, the outcomes of percutaneous coronary intervention for left main trunk lesions (LM-PCI) have significantly improved in recent years [1–4]. Recent registry data, such as the DEFINE-LM registry, have further demonstrated that physiology-guided percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) may provide favorable outcomes compared with coronary artery bypass grafting in selected LM-PCI patients [5]. However, despite these advancements in technique and technology, LM-PCI persistently has poorer outcomes than other coronary lesions in catheter-based interventions [6, 7]. Among coronary artery lesions, treating ostial lesions in left circumflex artery (LCx) is a significant contributor to the outcomes because of its unique anatomical characteristics, hemodynamic alterations, and high incidence of restenosis, limiting available evidence for a standardized optimal procedure [8–10].

Based on a consensus document from the Japanese Association of Cardiovascular Intervention and Therapeutics, a Japanese academic society specializing in cardiovascular catheterization, the use of a drug-coated balloon (DCB) after lesion preparation is preferred for PCI of coronary side branch lesions, particularly at the LCx ostium [11]. Considering the poor outcomes associated with stenting in LCx ostial lesions [12, 13], DCB is intended to minimize unnecessary stenting.

Despite evidence supporting the efficacy of DCBs [14, 15], their clinical adoption – including optimal technique and procedural approaches – remains limited. PCI registry data reflecting Japanese clinical practice indicate that only approximately 12% of cases involve using drug-eluting stent the main vessel and a DCB in the side branch [16]. Although previous studies have shown the effectiveness of DCB for side branches [17, 18], it is likely that certain clinical and procedural challenges hinder its widespread use.

To address these challenges, we suggested the double-effect kissing balloon technique (W-KBT) as a novel approach to the treatment of de novo left main coronary artery disease (LMD) with LCx ostial lesions. This technique utilizes a perfusion balloon (PB) from the left main trunk (LMT) to the left anterior descending artery (LAD) and a DCB toward the LCx (Fig. 1). The Japanese Coronary Intervention Using Drug-Eluting and Perfusion Therapy for Left Main Disease Registry (JDEPTH-LM Registry) is designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of W-KBT. This study aimed to establish a novel LM-PCI technique to address the ongoing challenges associated with LMT bifurcation lesions involving de novo LCx ostial lesions.

Methods

Patient Population

The JDEPTH-LM Registry is a multicenter, prospective, single-arm observational study conducted at 32 institutions in Japan (online suppl. Table 1; for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000552600). The study will enroll 280 patients who meet the eligibility criteria and who are diagnosed with stable or unstable angina or non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (MI) between May 2024 and May 2026, with an anticipated enrollment period of approximately 24 months. As of January 2026, enrollment is ongoing and is expected to be completed as scheduled.

Patients will undergo coronary angiography before PCI, as determined by the local heart team. Eligible patients must have de novo LMD classified based on the left main Medina classification. The classification includes (1,1,1), (1,0,1), (0,1,1), or (0,0,1), representing LMD cases where LMT-to-LAD crossover stenting is considered clinically appropriate. Moreover, these classifications indicate the presence of lesions in the distal bifurcation of the LMT. Medina type 0,0,1 is considered as eligible for the stenting strategy in case where the left main lesion itself is confined to the ostial or mid segment, without distal LMD involvement. Additionally, the LCx ostial lesions must be <10 mm in length or have a stenosis severity of <70%, thus not fulfilling major 1 of the DEFINITION criteria [19]. Patients presenting with cardiogenic shock or those deemed unsuitable for PCI with W-KBT will be excluded from this registry. Online supplementary Table 2 shows a comprehensive list of the inclusion and exclusion criteria (Fig. 2). Essentially, this registry will include patients undergoing standard LM-PCI for such Medina types, in which treatment follows a provisional crossover stenting strategy from the LMT to the LAD, followed by proximal optimization technique (POT) and conventional KBT (C-KBT) using two balloon catheters.

Study Endpoints

This study consists of co-primary endpoints, including a safety and efficacy endpoint. The co-primary safety endpoint is the procedure success rate, defined as the proportion of cases meeting three of the following criteria: prolonged KBT success, device delivery success, and absence of bailout stenting at the LCx ostium (which remains a general technical challenge for the DCB) [20]. The co-primary efficacy endpoint is defined as the incidence of a major adverse cardiovascular event (MACE) at 12 months, including all-cause mortality, nonfatal MI, and ischemia-driven unplanned revascularization for LMD. These definitions are based on those used in the POLBOS LM study (NCT03508219) [21], which adopted the study definitions for the PCI cohort in the EXCEL trial (NCT01205776) (online suppl. Material 1) [22].

The secondary endpoints included assessments related to the safety of W-KBT, such as duration of ST-segment changes during DCB dilation, total inflation time, changes in blood pressure and heart rate before and after W-KBT. The safety of the PCI procedure will be further evaluated based on electrocardiographic changes, fluctuations in blood pressure, and variations in heart rate observed after W-KBT. Perfusion Balloon

The PB (Ryusei; Kaneka Corporation, Osaka, Japan) is a percutaneous transluminal coronary angioplasty catheter-based device designed to maintain distal coronary perfusion during balloon inflation, as it features multiple perfusion holes in the balloon’s proximal and distal segments (Fig. 3, 4). In recent years, PB has been increasingly utilized to facilitate prolonged balloon inflation for hemostasis in cases of coronary perforation [23]. In an experimental vascular model using silicone tubing and a glycerin solution, approximately 70% of the proximal perfusion volume was maintained distal to the dilated catheter when using a 3.0-mm PB catheter, even with a guidewire in place [24]. PB is used to maintain prolonged dilation of the lesion site before stent placement in acute coronary syndrome, and several studies have reported its safety [24, 25].

Comparison of standard PTCA catheter and perfusion balloon. The standard PTCA catheter temporarily halts blood flow during balloon inflation, whereas the Ryusei catheter allows continuous blood flow through its perfusion holes. PTCA, percutaneous transluminal coronary angioplasty.

Balloon catheter structure of Ryusei. Ryusei features a 20-mm balloon with linearly arranged 16 perfusion holes on the proximal segment and circumferentially arranged 8 holes on the distal segment.

Furthermore, a case report suggests that balloon dilation (i.e., sub-occlusion) while maintaining minimal perfusion may promote collateral recruitment even in chronic coronary syndrome, where collateral networks are not well established. The case report also utilized a pressure wire as the workhorse wire as the PB showed that the distal coronary blood flow remains approximately 40% of the baseline levels even during PB inflation in the LMT [26]. Clinical evidence supporting PB, characterized by prolonged balloon inflation, is steadily increasing in primary PCI; however, this defining feature has not been applied to chronic coronary syndrome, especially.

Drug-Coated Balloon

In Japan, the DCBs that have received regulatory approval are the AGENT™ paclitaxel-coated balloon (Boston Scientific Corp, Marlborough, USA) and the SeQuent® Please Neo (B. Braun, Berlin, Germany). Operators will use either of these DCBs in combination with a PB. The recommended inflation time and conditions are at least 30 s at nominal pressure.

Double-Effect of W-KBT

W-KBT is a technique comprising the dual therapies of PB and DCB. The use of PB facilitates perfusion therapy, ensuring blood flow during the procedure, whereas the use of DCB delivers drug-eluting therapy to prevent lesion restenosis. The primary challenges in conventional techniques can be the following: after provisional crossover stenting from the LMT to the LAD, subsequent POT and C-KBT are performed to optimize the metallic carina formation, inevitably resulting in a brief but critical period of simultaneous occlusion of the LAD and LCx, leading to a substantial reduction in the coronary blood flow. This temporary but significant compromise in perfusion poses a risk of hemodynamic instability, necessitating the minimization of the duration of dual arterial occlusion. Conversely, DCB-mediated drug delivery requires sustained inflation for at least 30 s to ensure adequate drug penetration into the plaque within the vessel wall. Consequently, in PCI for LMT bifurcation lesions, the safe and effective use of DCB for LCx ostial lesions requires a specialized PCI technique allowing for a prolonged inflation period of at least 30 s while mitigating hemodynamic risks.

W-KBT achieves this balance by utilizing a PB in the LMT-to-LAD to maintain coronary blood flow through its perfusion holes, preventing hemodynamic collapse while simultaneously ensuring optimal DCB dilation (≥30 s) for the LCx ostial lesions. This technique facilitates safe and effective drug application to the LCx ostial lesions while maintaining coronary perfusion to the LAD, thereby overcoming a key limitation of conventional LM-PCI strategies [10].

PCI Procedure

The PCI procedure will be performed under IVUS guidance, according to the protocol outlined in Figure 5. Following optimal lesion preparation, provisional crossover stenting, POT and C-KBT will be sequentially performed. Subjects with residual stenosis <30% at the LCx ostium and thrombolysis in MI grade 3 flow on coronary angiography will subsequently proceed to the W-KBT step. Upon completion of W-KBT, stent apposition will be assessed by IVUS, and re-POT will be performed at the operator’s discretion if required. Clinical Follow-Up

The 12-month (±30 days) follow-up visit is scheduled to confirm the presence or absence of cardiovascular events. Although the local heart team will decide regarding ischemia-driven revascularization during the follow-up period, the independent Clinical Events Committee (CEC) will assess whether a revascularization procedure qualifies as an endpoint event. Furthermore, a standardized revascularization decision tree has been developed among investigators to promote consistency in clinical decision-making and ensure a high follow-up rate (online suppl. Fig. 1).

During the follow-up period, additional investigations will be considered only in patients presenting with symptoms suggestive of myocardial ischemia, such as chest pain. When revascularization for LM is considered during follow-up periods, investigators will perform quantitative coronary angiography, and if a stenosis of ≥50% is identified, further assessment using intracoronary imaging, physiological assessment, or selective invasive reassessment will be undertaken. If any of the predefined criteria for ischemia-driven unplanned revascularization for LMD are met (online suppl. Material 1), the revascularization could be performed by investigator’s discretion. Even when all quantitative coronary angiography criteria are negative, the revascularization may still be considered if there are clinically compelling findings and if the local heart team judges the procedure to be necessary. In addition, whether these invasive assessments were performed will be confirmed by documentation in the case report form.

IVUS Analysis

IVUS analysis will be performed by an independent core laboratory (OCVC Lab, Osaka University, Osaka, Japan). The various definitions, including anatomical definitions of LMT PCI, and measurement parameters are described in online supplementary material 3.

Sample Size Calculation

The primary efficacy endpoint will be evaluated by comparing the observed event rate to a prespecified performance goal derived from the EXCEL trial and POLBOS LM study. The 1-year follow-up MACE rate, including stroke, in patients with distal LMD in the EXCEL trial was 16.7% [21]. Based on the EXCEL trial findings, the 3-year stroke incidence was 2.3% (95% confidence interval: 1.4%–3.3%) under the normal approximation model [22]. Assuming a maximum 1-year stroke incidence of 1.1%, the expected annual event rate for this study has been set at 15.6% after adjusting for stroke risk. The performance goal has been set at 21.5% adding a non-inferiority margin of 5.9%, corresponding to the non-inferiority margin hazard ratio of 1.38 in the EXCEL trial. Under these assumptions, a one-sample survival function test using Kaplan-Meier estimates with a one-sided significance level (α = 0.05), power (1 − β = 0.8), and a log-minus-log transformation yielded a required sample size of 268 patients. Our final target sample size is 280 patients, accounting for an anticipated follow-up failure rate of approximately 5%.

For the co-primary endpoint, the procedure success rate, the expected success probability is set at 95%, with the null hypothesis that true success rate does not exceed 90% (H0: π ≤ 90%), whereas the alternative hypothesis assumes a success rate exceeding 90% (H1: π > 90%). With a sample size of 280 patients, the test achieved a one-sided significance level of 2.5% and a statistical power of 88%. The sample size was calculated using the statistical tool available at https://nshi.jp/en/js/onesurvyr/.

Endpoint Analyses

Baseline patient characteristics will be summarized using descriptive statistics. Continuous variable will be presented as mean ± SD or median with interquartile range, while categorical variables will be reported as frequencies and percentages. Key clinical parameters, including patient background, cardiac function, and lesion complexity, will also be collected and reported in the main results analysis.

The primary efficacy endpoint is the 1-year survival probability, defined as the proportion of patients free from MACE. This probability will be estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, with the variance calculated via Greenwood’s formula. To stabilize the variance, a log-minus-log transformation will be applied. A one-sample non-parametric survival test will be then performed to evaluate whether the lower bound of the 1-year survival probability’s confidence interval exceeds the prespecified performance goal of 78.5% (which corresponds to a MACE rate of 21.5%). A one-sided significance level of 0.05 will be used throughout the analysis. Subgroup analyses will be conducted exclusively on patients with complete data for the respective subgroup variables.

Predefined Subgroup Analysis

Comparisons will be made within each of the following subgroups: wedge pressure in C-KBT and W-KBT, ST changes induced by W-KBT, periprocedural MI defined by various criteria, stent deformation, characteristics of LCx ostial lesions, typical symptoms associated with W-KBT, left ventricular ejection fraction, operator’s experience with LM-PCI, characteristics of angiographic findings and ischemia assessment findings (online suppl. Table 3).

Data Collection and Monitoring

An electronic data capture system will collect data and transmit them to the data center. IVUS data will be copied onto recording media at each participating institution and sent to the core laboratory for analysis. Monitoring will be conducted through centralized monitoring via the electronic data capture system, with oversight provided by designated personnel from TCROSS Co., Ltd. (Tokyo, Japan).

Clinical Event Committee

The CEC is an independent adjudication body, separate from the interventional cardiologists with pertinent expertise who are not participants in the study and who do not have any other real or potential conflicts of interest. The CEC will adjudicate all cardiovascular events designated as primary endpoints and categorized confirmed events using specific criteria (details are given in the online suppl. Material 1). As this is an observational study, all clinical decisions are delegated to the local heart team. The CEC serves to objectively evaluate these clinical judgments.

Discussion

Percutaneous treatment of LMD, particularly when the lesion involves the ostial LCx, remains a significant clinical challenge due to elevated restenosis rates and the absence of an established interventional standard [8, 9, 12, 14, 15]. Although recent consensus documents advocate the use of DCB therapy for side-branch ostial lesions, including the LCx ostium [11], the optimal timing and delivery technique for DCBs in the context of LM-PCI are yet to be defined. One major concern relates to the sequence and compatibility of techniques. Applying a DCB before LMT-LAD crossover stenting may result in drug displacement by subsequent KBT, while post-KBT DCB application risks inducing carina shift and stent deformation. Moreover, C-KBT involving simultaneous inflation of a standard balloon in the main vessel and a DCB in the side branch can lead to transient occlusion of both the LAD and LCx, with a risk of hemodynamic instability. This issue is particularly relevant given that optimal DCB efficacy requires at least 30 s of inflation, and longer durations have been reported to be associated with improved clinical outcomes [27, 28]. In light of these limitations, the W-KBT was proposed. This approach combines a PB from the LMT to the LAD with a DCB in the LCx ostium, enabling prolonged balloon inflation with preserved coronary perfusion. The perfusion capability of the PB theoretically maintains antegrade LAD flow during inflation, thereby mitigating ischemia and enhancing procedural safety.

The feasibility and safety of W-KBT were first demonstrated in the single-center JDEPTH-LM pilot study, which showed 100% procedural success using a 7-Fr transradial approach [29]. Importantly, 60-s simultaneous balloon inflations were achieved without inducing hemodynamic instability or ST-segment elevation. Although transient ST-segment depression occurred in half the patients, no inotropes or mechanical support were required, and 1-year clinical follow-up showed no ischemia-driven target lesion revascularization from the LCx ostium. These results support the clinical feasibility of W-KBT. Nonetheless, the pilot study was limited by its small sample size (n = 12), single-center setting, and lack of systematic physiology or imaging assessments. Additionally, key questions remain regarding the safety of W-KBT in patients with reduced left ventricular function, the optimal lesion subset for this approach, and the potential benefit of physiological or imaging guidance to optimize outcomes.

To address these limitations and validate the clinical utility of W-KBT in a broader population, the JDEPTH-LM Registry was initiated as a multicenter, prospective, observational study enrolling 280 patients across 32 institutions in Japan. This registry focuses on patients with de novo distal LMD undergoing provisional crossover stenting, followed by POT, C-KBT, and final W-KBT with ≥30 s inflation. The inclusion of co-primary endpoints – procedural success and 1-year MACE – aims to evaluate both technical performance and clinical impact in real-world practice. Several secondary endpoints, such as electrocardiogram changes during balloon inflation, changes in blood pressure and heart rate, and the use of wedge pressure to assess hemodynamic shifts, will offer mechanistic insights into the physiologic effects of W-KBT. These are further supported by recent human physiology studies showing that PB inflation within the LMT maintains approximately 40% of baseline coronary flow during balloon occlusion [26] and may even allow effective delivery of intracoronary agents such as nicorandil distal to the occlusion site [30]. These findings may explain the observed attenuation of ischemic electrocardiogram changes during prolonged inflation. Moreover, IVUS-based analyses embedded in the registry will help assess the impact of W-KBT on stent symmetry and expansion, critical factors in long-term outcomes of LM-PCI. Previous trials have suggested that balloon-induced stent deformation or asymmetry may increase the risk of restenosis or thrombosis [31, 32], and W-KBT may mitigate these risks by avoiding additional post-DCB intervention. By standardizing the use of W-KBT in LM bifurcation PCI and collecting high-resolution procedural and follow-up data, the JDEPTH-LM Registry is expected to clarify the role of this novel approach as a potentially routine adjunct to improve outcomes in patients with complex LMD involving the LCx ostium.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, unlike the Ringer Perfusion Balloon (Teleflex, Pennsylvania, USA), the PB, a percutaneous transluminal coronary angioplasty catheter-based structure with multiple perfusion holes is currently only available in Japan. Second, because this study uses a single-arm design, we cannot directly compare existing treatment strategies to assess potential superiority. Third, considering its observational nature, the study is inherently subject to selection bias.

Conclusion

The JDEPTH-LM Registry aims to evaluate W-KBT as a novel single stenting approach for PCI in LMD involving LCx ostial lesions, which typically have poor outcomes. This study will explore the potential benefits of prolonged DCB dilation in KBT, addressing technical challenges in DCB application to the LCx ostium. In this approach, PB facilitates extended DCB dilation while maintaining KBT principles.

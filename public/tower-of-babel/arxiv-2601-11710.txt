The Astrophysical Journal , accepted Resonant Scattering of the He I 1.0833 μ \,\mu m Triplet in H II Regions: Emission Spectra
B. T. Draine

Abstract
Resonant scattering of He I 1.0833 ​ μ 1.0833\,\mu m triplet photons by metastable He 2 3 ​ S 1 2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1} is studied for optical depths characteristic of H II regions. Regions with large He 2 3 ​ S 1 2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1} column densities are predicted to have unusually broad multipeaked 1.0833 μ \,\mu m emission profiles, with the centroid blue-shifted by up to ∼ \sim 14 ​ km ​ s − 1 14\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1} relative to other lines. The feature FWHM can exceed 100 ​ km ​ s − 1 100\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1} for some regions. Resonant trapping enhances dust absorption and reduces the He I 1.0833 ​ µm 1.0833\micron emission. Care must be taken when using the He I 1.0833 ​ μ 1.0833\,\mu m/H I 1.0941 ​ μ 1.0941\,\mu m (Pa γ \gamma ) ratio to estimate the He + /H + ratio. Predicted spectra are computed for examples, including M17-B and NGC3603 in the Galaxy, and a star-forming region in M51. Observations of the 1.0833 ​ μ 1.0833\,\mu m triplet with spectrometers such as NIRSPEC, CARMENES, or X-Shooter can confirm the predicted effects of resonant scattering in H II regions, and constrain the nebular conditions.



I
Introduction

The He I
1.0833\micron
triplet is typically the strongest
observable emission line generated when He
+
recombines with
electrons. It has been observed in emission from H II regions
powered by O stars
(
L. H. Aller & W. Liller 1959
)
, from planetary nebulae

(
W. Liller & L. H. Aller 1963
;
C. R. O’Dell 1963
)
, from AGNs

(
P. D. Levan et al. 1981
)
and QSOs

(
P. D. Levan et al. 1984
)
, and from the Earth’s
thermosphere
(
S. R. Kulkarni 2025
, e.g.,)
.

Emission of
1.0833\micron
triplet photons populates the metastable
He
{}^{0}\,1s2s

2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
level, and the
2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
level
population can become large enough to produce observable absorption.

W. S. Adams 1949
detected interstellar
He
0

2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
–
4\,^{3}{\rm P}^{\rm o}

3890\,{\rm\AA}
absorption
toward O stars in the Orion Nebula and the Trifid Nebula, and
absorption in the He
0

2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
–

2\,^{3}{\rm P}_{1}^{\rm o}

1.0833\micron
triplet itself was observed by

G. A. Galazutdinov & J. Krelowski 2012
toward the O9.5V star

\zeta
Oph. In all of these detections, the metastable
He
0

2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
resides in the H II region around a hot
star.
N. Indriolo et al. 2009
searched for but
failed to detect He I
1.0833\micron
absorption in the
diffuse neutral ISM outside of H II regions, placing an upper
limit on the cosmic ray ionization rate.

The 1.0833
\micron
triplet is also observed in absorption in some
QSOs
(
K. M. Leighly et al. 2011
;
X. Pan et al. 2019
)
,
in the Solar chromosphere

(
A. G. M. Pietrow et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
, and in transiting
exoplanets with extended atmospheres

(
M. Mansfield et al. 2018
;
L. Nortmann et al. 2018
;
A. Oklopčić & C. M. Hirata 2018
;
R. Allart et al. 2019
)
. Spectra of the extragalactic
“Little Red Dots” also show evidence of
1.0833\micron

(
B. Wang et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.

S. R. Pottasch 1962
and
R. R. Robbins 1968a
;
R. R. Robbins 1968b

discussed the effects on the populations of other He I levels
in H II regions and planetary nebulae when transitions
to
2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
become optically thick. Absorption by the
metastable
2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
level can suppress emission in some lines
(e.g., He I
3890\,{\rm\AA}
,
3188\,{\rm\AA}
) and enhance

7067\,{\rm\AA}
,

4713\,{\rm\AA}
)
(
R. R. Robbins 1968b
;
R. A. Benjamin et al. 2002

K. P. M. Blagrave et al. 2007
)
.
The theory was applied to interpret He I line emission
from the Orion Nebula
(
K. P. M. Blagrave et al. 2007
;
R. L. Porter et al. 2007
)
.

Previous studies were concerned with the important effects of resonant
scattering on the overall strengths of He I emission lines.
The present paper is concerned with the
spectrum
of the
He I 1.0833
\micron
triplet when observed in emission. For
realistic conditions, the spectrum of the
1.0833\micron
triplet
photons escaping from the H II region can be substantially
altered by resonant scattering. As a result, the
1.0833\micron

triplet is predicted to have unusual multi-peaked emission profiles.
The emission spectrum is sensitive to the column density of
metastable He
0

2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
, and to the velocity dispersion of
the recombining He
+
that is responsible for populating the

2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
level. Therefore, observational determination of the
actual line profile will provide a new diagnostic for the nebular
conditions. It also will serve as a test for our theoretical
understanding of resonant scattering.

The relevant atomic physics is reviewed in Section
II
.
Section
III

outlines the radiative transfer problem. Section
IV

presents the results of Monte-Carlo simulations
for dustless H II regions, while Section
5 includes the effects of dust absorption. The results are discussed
in Section
6 and summarized in Section

VII
. Details of the Monte Carlo procedures are provided
in the Appendix.

II.2
Radiative Transfer

Most of the radiative recombinations to the
{\rm He}^{0}

The

\rightarrow

-29.96

\lambda_{c}=1.0833138\micron

III
Resonant Scattering

Resonant scattering for a single line was discussed by

W. Unno 1952
,
G. B. Field 1959
,
D. G. Hummer 1962
, and many
subsequent papers, with particular attention to the scattering of the
H Lyman
\alpha
line

(
J. R. M. Bonilha et al. 1979
;
D. A. Neufeld 1990
;
Z. Zheng & J. Miralda-Escudé 2002
;
M. Dijkstra 2019
;
K.-i. Seon & C.-G. Kim 2020
, e.g.,)
. Here we
consider resonant scattering of He I
1.0833\micron

triplet photons.

We assume a spherical H II region with uniform electron density

n_{e}
and He
+
density
n({\rm He}^{+})
. The recombining He
+
results
in a uniform density of metastable He I
2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
.

1.0833\micron
triplet photons are randomly injected with initial
frequency drawn from the Voigt profile for emission from

2\,^{3}{\rm P}_{J}^{\rm o}
with the Maxwellian velocity distribution of the
recombining He
+
. Once emitted, the photons travel until they
either scatter or escape the H II region. The scattering is
treated using the full 3-line multiplet, with a Voigt absorption
profile for each line.

If scattering takes place, the scattered photon travels in a new
direction with a new frequency. The new direction is drawn from the
scattering phase function for electric dipole scattering. The new
frequency is drawn from the “partial redistribution function”

R_{\rm II-B}

(
D. G. Hummer 1962
)
that describes coherent scattering
by a Maxwellian distribution of atoms with finite natural
linewidth.
4
4

4

The frequency redistribution function is sampled
using the “rejection method”

(
W. H. Press et al. 1992
;
Z. Zheng & J. Miralda-Escudé 2002
)
.

The present radiative transfer
treatment neglects Thomson scattering by electrons, but allows for
absorption by dust. Our Monte-Carlo methodology for scattering in
a multiplet is described in the Appendix. Complete numerical results
can be obtained from
https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/EOHGDD
.

VI
Discussion

The importance of resonant scattering of H I Ly
\alpha
in
H II regions has long been recognized, but the effects cannot be
directly observed because Ly
\alpha
photons escaping from the
H II region are strongly scattered by H I outside the
H II region, and absorbed by interstellar dust. Thus, it has
not been possible to test predictions for the spectrum of Ly
\alpha

photons emerging from H II regions.

The He I
1.0833\micron
triplet, however, does allow us to
test our theory of resonant scattering. For bright H II regions
ionized by early-type O stars, He I
1.0833\micron
emission
can be strong, and the population of metastable
He
0

2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
is large enough for the optical depth
\tau_{\rm tot}

for resonant scattering of He I
1.0833\micron
photons within
the H II region to be large. Because the population of
metastable He
0

2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
is negligible outside the
H II region
(
N. Indriolo et al. 2009
)
, the
He I
1.0833\micron
photons will travel unimpeded (except by
dust) after escaping the H II region, allowing remote
observations to test theoretical predictions for the spectrum.

The observable effects of resonant scattering of
He I
1.0833\micron
will be most conspicuous in high surface
brightness H II regions with large
{\rm EM}_{R}
. We consider three
illustrative examples.

VI.4
Other Processes Affecting He I
1.0833\micron
Emission

Above we have been assuming that He I
1.0833\micron
photons
are injected as a result of standard “case B” recombination of

{\rm He}^{+}
with thermal electrons. However, the rate of injection of

1.0833\micron
photons can be larger due to two processes:

1.

He atoms that are in the
2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
metastable level
can undergo inelastic electron collisions that excite the

2\,^{3}{\rm P}_{J}^{\rm o}
level, followed by emission of an additional

1.0833\micron
photon. The fractional increase in
1.0833\micron

emission due to collisional excitation of
2\,^{3}{\rm P}_{J}^{\rm o}
is

\frac{n_{e}k_{e}}{A_{\rm ms}+n_{e}k_{d}}~~~,

(29)

where
k_{e}
is the rate coefficient for
2^{3}{\rm S}_{1}\rightarrow 2^{3}{\rm P}
and
k_{d}\approx 3.3\times 10^{-8}\,{\rm cm}^{3}\,{\rm s}^{-1}
is the rate
coefficient for collisional transitions from
2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
to
singlet states. However,
k_{e}/k_{d}\lesssim 0.014
for
T\lesssim 10^{4}\,{\rm K}

(
I. Bray et al. 2000
, using rates from)
so this
process can be neglected.

2.

When
N(2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1})
is large, radiative trapping can “convert”

3889.7\,{\rm\AA}
photons into
4.296\micron
,
7067.1\,{\rm\AA}
, and

1.0833\micron
photons (see Figure
1
), as
originally pointed out by
S. R. Pottasch 1962
. However, this can
increase the emission of
1.0833\micron
photons by at most 21% for

\tau_{\rm tot}\gtrsim 10^{3}
, so this is not a major correction.

VI.5
He I Line Ratios

Resonant trapping of He I
1.0833\micron
and other permitted
transitions to
2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
(e.g.,
2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
–

3\,^{3}{\rm P}^{\rm o}

3890\,{\rm\AA}
and
2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
–

4\,^{3}{\rm P}^{\rm o}

3189\,{\rm\AA}
) can suppress the power in these lines,
and enhance the emission in other lines (e.g.,
3\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
–

2\,^{3}{\rm P}^{\rm o}

7067\,{\rm\AA}
), as originally pointed out by

S. R. Pottasch 1962
.

A number of authors have estimated the effects of radiative transfer
on observed line He I line ratios

(
R. R. Robbins 1968b
;
D. E. Osterbrock 1989
;
R. A. Benjamin et al. 2002
;
K. P. M. Blagrave et al. 2007
, e.g.,)
,
using different estimates for the “escape probability”
\beta
for
He I
1.0833\micron
photons. The present Monte-Carlo study
provides improved estimates for
\beta
from a spherical nebula, with
values of
\beta
that can be significantly below previous estimates
(see Figure
4
). A future paper (B.T. Draine 2026, in
preparation) will use the escape probabilities obtained here to
recalculate the effects of resonant scattering on He I line ratios.

Summary

1.

Monte-Carlo radiative transfer calculations have been carried

0

2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}

1.0833\micron

1.0833\micron

\tau_{\rm tot}

\langle N_{\rm sca}\rangle
and
\langle L_{\rm path}\rangle
are given.

2.

Resonant scattering by metastable He
0

2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
can
produce unusual multi-peaked line profiles for the
He I
1.0833\micron
triplet emission from H II
regions.

3.

For normal H II region conditions (

.

4.

For H II regions with

5.

For Milky Way dust abundances, we estimate the effects of

6.

We predict He I

Appendix A
Monte-Carlo Radiative Transfer

A.1
Opacity

Consider scatterers with lower level
E_{\ell}
(e.g.,
the metastable
1s2s\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
state) and
N
excited states,
E_{u}
,

u=1-N
, with degeneracies
g_{u}
, Einstein
A
coefficients

A_{u\ell}
, and slightly different wavelengths
\lambda_{u\ell}
.
It is convenient to measure the photon frequency by the dimensionless
Doppler shift
x_{j}
relative to the multiplet centroid
\lambda_{c}
,

x=\frac{\lambda_{c}/\lambda-1}{b/c}~~~.

(A1)

The dimensionless Doppler shift of the photon relative to the local

\lambda_{u\ell}
line is
x+\Delta_{u}
where

\Delta_{u}\equiv\left(\frac{\lambda_{u\ell}}{\lambda_{c}}-1\right)\frac{c}{b}~~~.

(A2)

The attenuation coefficient

\displaystyle\alpha(x)

\displaystyle~=~

\displaystyle n_{\rm H}\sigma_{\rm d}+n({\rm He\,2\,^{3}S_{1}})\sum_{u=0}^{2}\sigma_{\ell u}(x)

(A3)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\ell u}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{u}A_{u\ell}\lambda_{u\ell}^{3}}{8\pi^{2}b}\phi(x+\Delta_{u},a)~~~,

(A4)

where
\sigma_{\rm d}
is the dust absorption cross section
per H nucleon.
The Voigt line profile
\phi(x,a)
is obtained by table look-up from
tables generated using approximations from
B. Armstrong 1967
.

A.2
Injection

Photons are injected uniformly throughout the spherical volume, with
initial frequencies
x
drawn from the Voigt line profile

\phi(x)
, and random directions of propagation
\boldsymbol{\hat{n}}
.
All results for regions with dust were calculated using at
least
10^{6}
(in most cases
10^{7}
) injected photons to ensure
statistical accuracy.

A.3
Transport

Let
s
measure path length in line-center optical depth units:

ds=\tau_{\rm tot}\frac{dL}{R}~~~,

(A5)

where
L
is physical path length. Let

t{=(r/R)\tau_{\rm tot}}
be the line-center optical depth from the
center of the sphere to a location at radius
r
.
Let
\tau_{\rm d}=n_{\rm H}\sigma_{\rm d}R
be the dust absorption optical depth
from center to edge.

Let
t_{j}
be the location of a photon with Doppler shift
x_{j}
,
traveling in direction
\boldsymbol{\hat{n}}_{j}
.
Define the direction cosine

\mu_{j}\equiv\boldsymbol{\hat{n}}_{j}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{r}}_{j}~~~,

(A6)

where
\boldsymbol{\hat{r}}_{j}
is the radial direction at the photon’s location. To
escape, the photon must traverse a path length

\,{\rm s}_{esc}=\left\{\left[\tau_{\rm tot}^{2}-t_{j}^{2}(1-\mu_{j}^{2})\right]^{1/2}-t_{j}\mu_{j}\right\}~~~.

(A7)

The probability of escape for this photon is

\displaystyle P_{{\rm esc}}

\displaystyle~=~

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\tau(s_{\rm esc})\right]

(A8)

\displaystyle\tau(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\alpha(x_{j},s^{\prime})\,\frac{R}{\tau_{\rm tot}}ds^{\prime}~~~.

(A9)

The probability of scattering or absorption in an interval
[s,s+ds]
is

dP=\exp\left[-\tau(s)\right]~d\tau~~~.

(A10)

A random number
{\cal P}_{1}
is drawn

We employ the Mersenne Twister random number generator

(
M. Matsumoto & T. Nishimura 1998
)
, translated into Fortran by Tsuyoshi Tada.

from a distribution uniform on
[0,1]
. The photon escapes if

{\cal P}_{1}>1-P_{\rm esc}
; if
{\cal P}_{1}<1-P_{\rm esc}
there is an
event (either scattering or absorption) after traversing optical depth

\tau_{j}=-\ln(1-{\cal P}_{1})~~~.

(A11)

If we take
\alpha(x_{j})
to be constant along the path, then

s_{j}=\frac{-\ln(1-{\cal P}_{1})}{\alpha(x_{j})}~~~.

(A12)

After traveling a pathlength
s_{j}
, the event (scattering or
absorption) will be at

t_{j+1}=\left[t_{j}^{2}+2\mu_{j}t_{j}s_{j}+s_{j}^{2}\right]^{1/2}~~~,

(A13)

with new direction cosine

\tilde{\mu}_{j+1}\equiv\boldsymbol{\hat{n}}_{j}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{r}}_{j+1}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{j+1}

\displaystyle~=~

\displaystyle~~\left[1-\left(\frac{t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\right)^{2}(1-\mu_{j}^{2})\right]^{1/2}\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm if~}s>-\mu_{j}t_{j}

(A14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left[1-\left(\frac{t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\right)^{2}(1-\mu_{j}^{2})\right]^{1/2}\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm if~}s<-\mu_{j}t_{j}

(A15)

prior
to scattering (or absorption).

The probability that the event is resonant scattering by transition

\ell\rightarrow u
is

P_{\ell u}=\frac{n_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell u}(x_{j})}{\alpha(x_{j})}~~~.

(A16)

A random number
{\cal P}_{2}\in[0,1]
is drawn to determine whether the

\ell\rightarrow u
.
If
{\cal P}_{2}>\sum_{u=0}^{2}P_{\ell u}

by dust.

A.4
Direction After Scattering

Suppose that scattering takes place at
t_{j+1}
, with scattering
angle
\gamma_{j+1}
. The new direction of propagation
\boldsymbol{\hat{n}}_{j+1}

will have direction cosine

\mu_{j+1}\equiv\boldsymbol{\hat{n}}_{j+1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{r}}_{j+1}=\tilde{\mu}_{j+1}\,\cos\gamma_{j+1}+(1-\tilde{\mu}_{j+1}^{2})^{1/2}\sin\gamma_{j+1}\,\cos\phi_{j+1}

(A17)

and new dimensionless Doppler shift
x_{j+1}
.
For unpolarized light,
\phi_{j+1}
is uniformly distributed in
[0,2\pi]
:

\frac{dP_{\phi}}{d\phi_{j+1}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}~~~,~~~\phi_{j+1}\in[0,2\pi]~~~.

(A18)

and
\cos\gamma_{j+1}
has probability distribution

\frac{dP}{d\cos\gamma}=\frac{3}{8}\left(1+\cos^{2}\gamma\right)~~,~~~\cos\gamma\in[-1,1]~~~.

(A19)

Equation (
A19
) applies for both resonant scattering
(e.g., by He I
2\,^{3}{\rm S}_{1}
) and Thomson scattering by free
electrons. After drawing a random variable
P_{\gamma}
uniformly
distributed on
[0,1]
, we obtain
\cos\gamma
using the result found
by
K.-I. Seon 2006

\cos\gamma=\left[2(2P_{\gamma}-1)+\sqrt{4(2P_{\gamma}-1)^{2}+1}\right]^{1/3}-\left[2(2P_{\gamma}-1)+\sqrt{4(2P_{\gamma}-1)^{2}+1}\right]^{-1/3}~~~.

(A20)

A.5
Frequency After Scattering

Let
\boldsymbol{w}_{j+1}
be the velocity of the
scatterer in the fluid frame, at the location
t_{j+1}
of the
scattering. We are concerned only with components of

\boldsymbol{w}_{j+1}
in the scattering plane. Define

\displaystyle w_{\parallel,j+1}

\displaystyle~\equiv~

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\hat{n}}_{j}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}_{j+1}

(A21)

\displaystyle w_{\perp,j+1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle[(\boldsymbol{\hat{n}}_{j+1}-\cos\gamma_{j+1}\boldsymbol{\hat{n}}_{j})\cdot\boldsymbol{w}_{j+1}]/\sin\gamma_{j+1}~~~.

(A22)

Neglecting recoil, the dimensionless frequency after scattering is

x_{j+1}=x_{j}+w_{\parallel,j+1}\left(\cos\gamma_{j+1}-1\right)+w_{\perp,j+1}\sin\gamma_{j+1}~~~.

(A23)

We use the “partial redistribution function” for a line with finite
intrinsic width
(
D. G. Hummer 1962
)
. The exact distribution for

w_{\parallel}
is difficult to invert. For given
x_{j}
and transition

\ell\rightarrow u
, following
Z. Zheng & J. Miralda-Escudé 2002
, we
use the “method of rejection”

(
W. H. Press et al. 1992
)
to draw

w_{\parallel,j+1}
from the exact distribution

\frac{dP}{dw_{\parallel,j+1}}\propto\frac{e^{-w_{\parallel,j+1}^{2}}}{(x_{j}+\Delta_{u}-w_{\parallel,j+1})^{2}+a^{2}}~~~.

(A24)

We employ a modification of the “comparison function”
g(x)
used by

Z. Zheng & J. Miralda-Escudé 2002
: for
x\geq 0
:

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle~=~

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(x-w_{\parallel})^{2}+a^{2}}\hskip 28.45274pt0\leq x\leq C_{1}

(A25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-C_{1}^{2}}}{(x-w_{\parallel})^{2}+a^{2}}\hskip 28.45274ptC_{1}<x\leq C_{2}

(A26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-C_{2}^{2}}}{(x-w_{\parallel})^{2}+a^{2}}\hskip 28.45274ptC_{2}<x\leq C_{3}

(A27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-C_{3}^{2}}}{(x-w_{\parallel})^{2}+a^{2}}\hskip 28.45274ptC_{3}<x\leq C_{4}

(A28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-C_{4}^{2}}}{(x-w_{\parallel})^{2}+a^{2}}\hskip 28.45274ptC_{4}<x~~~.

(A29)

For
|x|\leq 1
we take
C_{1}=0.5
,
C_{2}=0.8
,
C_{3}=1.2
, and
C_{4}=2.5
;
for
|x|>1

C_{1}=|x|/2
,
C_{2}=|x|-0.2
,
C_{3}=|x|+0.2
, and

C_{4}=|x|+1.5
. This greatly increases the efficiency of the method,
by reducing the number of rejections.

For given
w_{\parallel,j+1}
, the transverse velocity
w_{\perp,j+1}
is
drawn from the thermal distribution; the probability distribution for

x_{j+1}
is gaussian:

x_{j+1}=x_{j}+\left(\cos\gamma_{j+1}-1\right)w_{\parallel,j+1}+\frac{\sin\gamma_{j+1}}{\sqrt{2}}\,{\cal P}~~~,

(A30)

where
{\cal P}
is a Gaussian random variate with zero mean and unit
variance.

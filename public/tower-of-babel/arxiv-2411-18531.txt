Statistic Maximal Leakage Thanks: This paper was presented in part at ISIT 2024 [ 1 ] .
Shuaiqi Wang 1 , Zinan Lin 2 , Giulia Fanti 1

Abstract
We introduce a privacy measure called statistic maximal leakage that quantifies how much a privacy mechanism leaks about a specific secret, relative to the adversary’s prior information about that secret. Statistic maximal leakage is an extension of the well-known maximal leakage . Unlike maximal leakage, which protects an arbitrary, unknown secret, statistic maximal leakage protects a single, known secret. We show that statistic maximal leakage satisfies composition and post-processing properties. Additionally, we show how to efficiently compute it in the special case of deterministic data release mechanisms. We analyze two important mechanisms under statistic maximal leakage: the quantization mechanism and randomized response. We show theoretically and empirically that the quantization mechanism achieves better privacy-utility tradeoffs in the settings we study.

We seek a privacy measure that satisfies three properties:

1.

Prior-independence:

The measure should not depend on any party’s prior over the input data, up to determining the support of the distribution.

We want a measure that depends explicitly on

This is primarily for efficiency reasons; by utilizing known information, we may be able to add less noise or perturbation to our data than a measure that is secret-agnostic.

3.

Composition and post-processing:

Informally, composition provides a bound on how a privacy measure degrades when one or more mechanisms are applied sequentially to the same data. For example, in differential privacy (DP), the privacy parameter

Post-processing guarantees that if one applies an arbitrary (possibly random) function to the output of a privacy mechanism, as long as the function does not depend on the original data, the privacy measure in question does not degrade. Composition and post-processing are useful properties exhibited by DP, which have contributed to its widespread usage in practical settings such as machine learning pipelines

In this work, we study an information-theoretic privacy measure that satisfies the above three properties.
For a special class of data with distributions drawn from a parametric family, and parameter vectors drawn from a finite set, we propose a privacy measure inspired by maximal leakage

Our contributions in this work are as follows:

•

Formulation and properties.
We formulate the statistic maximal leakage privacy measure, and show that it trivially satisfies the first two properties. We also show that it also satisfies composition and post-processing.
Note that although composition and post-processing have been previously proved for an

•

Computation.
We next study how to compute statistic maximal leakage.
We show that in general, computing statistic maximal leakage is NP-hard.
However, for the class of deterministic data release mechanisms, we show that statistic maximal leakage can be computed in polynomial time by solving a maximum flow problem over a graph whose construction depends on the mechanism parameters.

•

Mechanisms.
We next analyze two natural mechanisms that have been studied widely in the privacy literature: the quantization mechanism

Quantization-based mechanisms have been shown to achieve (near)-optimal privacy-utility tradeoffs in several privacy frameworks, including summary statistic privacy

We show that both mechanisms satisfy non-trivial statistic maximal leakage guarantees.
Further, under general tabular datasets, we analyze their privacy-utility tradeoffs; for this analysis, we define utility as a worst-case total variation distance between the original and the released data distributions. Our results show that for a tabular data release problem that aims to hide one of the marginal values, the quantization mechanism achieves a better privacy-utility tradeoff than randomized response for practical privacy parameters.

•

Empirical evaluation.
Finally, we apply the quantization mechanism to a real-world tabular dataset. We show that when instantiated with an appropriate statistic maximal leakage parameter, the quantization mechanism effectively protects the secret while still ensuring high utility of the released data. Moreover, we illustrate empirically that the quantization mechanism we propose for satisfying statistic maximal leakage incurs lower utility cost than mechanisms designed for other privacy frameworks: attribute privacy
[

and maximal leakage

There have been many privacy measures proposed for capturing leakage of sensitive information during information disclosure. We divide them into

II-A

Information-Theoretic Measures

Maximal leakage-based measures

While there are various of metrics designed to measure the privacy leakage,
maximal leakage
is the one that is the most related to our designed statistic maximal leakage. Maximal leakage, introduced by Issa et al.
[
9
]
, is an information-theoretic measure designed to capture the worst-case leakage of sensitive information in a data release mechanism. It is defined using the Markov chain
G-\Theta-\Theta^{\prime}-\hat{G}
, where
\hat{G}
represents the adversary’s guess of secret
G
.
The measure is expressed as:

\mathcal{L}_{ML}=\sup_{G-\Theta-\Theta^{\prime}-\hat{G}}\log\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(G=\hat{G}\right)}{\max_{g}\mathbb{P}_{G}(g)},

where the
\sup
is over
G
and
\hat{G}
, i.e., consider the worst-case secret and the strongest attack strategy.
This formulation evaluates the ratio of probabilities of correctly guessing the secret with and without observing the released data, thereby quantifying the increase in guessing probability facilitated by the data release.

Several variants and extensions of maximal leakage have been proposed to address different privacy scenarios via gain functions
[
32
,
27
,
33
,
34
]
, which penalize different values of
\mathbb{P}\left(G=\hat{G}\right)
differentaly. This framework can adapt to different threat models by modifying the evaluation of adversarial success, thus offering a tunable balance between privacy and utility.
For example, maximal
(\alpha,\beta)
-leakage
[
33
]
generalizes maximal leakage by introducing parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
, which adjust the sensitivity of leakage to different probabilities of adversarial success. By tuning
\alpha
and
\beta
, the metric can be converted to several known leakage measures, such as maximal leakage and local differential privacy.
Pointwise maximal leakage, discussed by Saeidian et al.
[
27
]
, examines the leakage given certain released data rather than averaging over the entire distribution, preventing disproportionate exposure of sensitive information.
Binary maximal leakage
[
35
]
focuses on the scenario where only binary secret functions are assumed to be of interest to the adversary, and demonstrates that restricting the set of secrets to be hidden allows for a better utility.

Maximal leakage and its variants

Quantitative Information Flow

Quantitative Information Flow (QIF) measures how much information about a secret is leaked through observable outputs. Originating in the works of Denning

Indistinguishability-Based Measures

Orthogonal to information-theoretic approaches, several studies measure the privacy leakage of statistical properties of the data based on a measure of indistinguishability over candidate inputs
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
59
]
; these techniques draw inspiration from

(DP)
[
21
]
, one of the most widely adopted privacy metrics. Roughly, differential privacy quantifies the privacy of a data release mechanism by measuring the influence of the participation of an individual record (or a group of records) on the final output
[
21
]
. However, as discussed in
[
17
,
52
]
, DP is designed to measure individual-level privacy and cannot directly quantify information leakage of statistical secrets. As an example, if we aim to protect the mean of a dataset of scalar values and apply a typical local DP mechanism
[
60
]
that adds zero-mean Gaussian noise to each record, this prevents the attacker from inferring the inclusion of individual records in the dataset but still allows an unbiased estimate of the dataset’s mean. As the number of records increases, the mean of released dataset converges to the original mean value
[
17
]
.
Simple variants of this mechanism that add different noise scales to different features are also shown to fail in
[
17
]
.

Distribution privacy
[
24
]
and distribution inference
[
25
,
59
]
tackle this challenge by ensuring that for any two input distributions with different parameters, the output distributions remain indistinguishable (up to multiplicative and additive factors), thus protecting sensitive distributional properties. For instance, attribute privacy
[
23
]
adopts an indistinguishability definition based on the pufferfish framework, while restricting the class of distributions that should be indistinguishable; it is particularly useful for scenarios where only a statistic of the dataset is shared. Both attribute and distribution privacy mechanisms can be very noisy, as they must obfuscate between entire distributions.
This is challenging because two random variables may have similar secret values (e.g., means), while having arbitrarily different underlying distributions; hence, the amount of noise required to make these distributions indistinguishable can be substantial.

IV
Statistic Maximal Leakage

We next present our proposed privacy measure, discuss its properties, and explain how to compute it.
Statistic maximal leakage (SML) measures the largest increase an adversary can gain in their guess of
g
; it is a property of a data release mechanism
\mathcal{M}
and a secret mapping
\mathfrak{g}
. We define it as follows:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}=\sup_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta},\mathbb{P}_{\hat{G}|\Theta^{\prime}}}\log\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{G}=G\right)}{\sup_{g\in\mathbf{G}}\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(g\right)},

(2)

where the supremum is taken over all prior distributions
\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}
over the distribution parameter
\theta
and attack strategies
\mathbb{P}_{\hat{G}|\Theta^{\prime}}
.
The probability in the numerator is over the attacker’s randomized estimator, the mechanism, and the secret.
Note that
the secret function
\mathfrak{g}
and the data release mechanism
\mathcal{M}
are fixed and assumed to be known in this optimization.

SML bears some similarities with maximal leakage
[
9
]
and worst-case min-entropy leakage
[
8
]
, though they do not simultaneously satisfy all three desirable properties we propose. Adopting our notation, maximal leakage
\mathcal{L}_{ML}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{ML}=\sup_{\mathbb{P}_{G|\Theta},\mathbb{P}_{\hat{G}|\Theta^{\prime}}}\log\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{G}=G\right)}{\sup_{g\in\mathbf{G}}\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(g\right)}.

Maximal leakage requires a known prior distribution over input data
\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}
and assumes the secret function
\mathfrak{g}\left(\theta\right)
is unknown.
Hence, it optimizes over all
\mathbb{P}_{G|\Theta}
.
Worst-case min-entropy leakage
\mathcal{L}_{MEL}
treats the entire input data distribution as the secret to protect; for the Markov chain
\Theta-\Theta^{\prime}-\hat{\Theta}
, it is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{MEL}=\sup_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta},\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\Theta}|\Theta^{\prime}}}\log\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\Theta}=\Theta\right)}{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbf{\Theta}}\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}\left(\theta\right)}.

Under a fixed prior, maximal leakage can have a smaller value than SML since SML considers the worst-case prior.
However, one can construct
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathfrak{g}
for which maximal leakage, with a worst-case prior, achieves its largest possible value (i.e.,
\min\left\{\log|\Theta^{\prime}|,\log|\Theta|\right\}
)
while SML is 0.
The following property shows that SML is upper- and lower-bounded by both maximal leakage with a worst-case prior and worst-case min-entropy leakage, by up to an additive factor that depends on the secret and the parametric family. (Proof in
§

A-B
.)

Computation of Statistic Maximal Leakage

We next discuss how to compute statistic maximal leakage.
Computing SML is more convenient under an alternative form, which shows that we only need to search over a restricted class of priors that assign nonzero probability mass to at most one parameter

Under such a prior, for a fixed

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}=\sup_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta|G}\in\left\{0,1\right\}}\log\sum_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}}\sup_{g\in\mathbf{G}}\mathbb{P}_{\Theta^{\prime}|\Theta}\left(\theta^{\prime}|\theta_{g}\right).

IV-C

Computational hardness for general mechanisms

Although the computation of SML is efficient when the mechanism is deterministic, the computation is NP-hard in general, although there exist efficient approximation algorithms, as shown in
Thm.

IV.1
.

Theorem IV.1

(Hardness of SML computation)
.

Given a mechanism
\mathcal{M}
and a secret mapping
\mathfrak{g}
, the computation of SML
\Pi_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}
is NP-hard. There exists an approximation algorithm for SML calculation with approximation ratio
1+\rho
(
\rho>0
) and running time polynomial in
\left|\Theta\right|\cdot\left|\Theta^{\prime}\right|
and
1/\rho
.

(Proof in
App.

C
)
We prove the NP-hardness of SML computation by reducing it to the 3-set cover decision problem, for which NP-completeness is known
[
64
]
. To approximately compute SML, we convert the computation to an edge cost flow problem, for which there exist practical (poly-time) approximation algorithms
[
65
]
. However, we do not use these approximation methods in the remainder of the paper; although our proposed mechanisms are not deterministic, they possess a special structure that enables us to determine their privacy guarantees in closed-form.

IV-D

Properties of Statistic Maximal Leakage

We next show that SML satisfies two natural desired properties: adaptive composition and post-processing.
Adaptive composition bounds the total leakage of releasing multiple results from one or more possibly adaptive mechanisms applied sequentially.

Theorem IV.2

(Adaptive Composition)
.

Suppose a data holder sequentially applies
m
mechanisms
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{m}
, where
\forall i\in[m]
, the
i
th mechanism is a function of the input data
\theta
and all of the previous outputs, which we denote as
\theta^{\prime(1)},\ldots\theta^{\prime(i)}
. That is,
\mathcal{M}_{i}(\theta,\theta^{\prime(1)},\ldots,\theta^{\prime(i-1)})=\theta^{\prime(i)}
.
Suppose
\forall i\in[m]
, mechanism
\mathcal{M}_{i}
satisfies a statistic maximal leakage guarantee with respect to
\mathfrak{g}
of
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}_{i},\mathfrak{g}}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{M}_{1}\circ\mathcal{M}_{2}\circ\ldots\circ\mathcal{M}_{m}
denote the composition of these adaptively chosen mechanisms.
The SML with respect to an adversary that can see all intermediate outputs
\theta^{\prime(1)},\theta^{\prime(2)},\ldots,\theta^{\prime(m)}
can be bounded as

\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}},\mathfrak{g}}\leq\sum_{i\in[m]}\Pi_{\mathcal{M}_{i},\mathfrak{g}}
.

(Proof in
App.

D
.)
Thm.

IV.2
shows that statistic maximal leakage degrades additively when one or more possibly adaptive mechanisms are applied multiple times sequentially to the original dataset.
This additive result is similar in form to analogous composition results for other privacy metrics, including pointwise maximal leakage
[
27
, Thm. 13]
and differential privacy
[
66
, Thm. III.1]
. In particular, we note that the result for pointwise maximal leakage does not imply ours, nor vice versa.
Pointwise maximal leakage requires knowledge of the prior distribution of the input data and assumes the secret function is unknown; the composition property for the worst-case secret function or prior does not imply the composition property for an arbitrary secret or prior.

Theorem IV.3

(Post-Processing)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a data release mechanism whose SML is
\Pi_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}
. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
be an arbitrary (possibly randomized) mechanism defined by
\mathbb{P}_{\Theta^{\prime\prime}|\Theta^{\prime}}
. Then the SML of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\circ\mathcal{M}
is
\Pi_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\circ\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}\leq\Pi_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}
.

(Proof in
App.

E
.)
Thm.

IV.3
shows that applying an arbitrary (possibly randomized) mechanism to the output of a mechanism that satisfies statistic maximal leakage will not degrade statistic maximal leakage.

V-B

Mechanism definition

Randomized Response (RR)

RR is a widely-used mechanism in the DP literature for discrete distributions
[
30
]
.
We first consider a RR mechanism
\mathcal{M}_{\text{RR}}
that outputs the original distribution parameter with some probability, and otherwise releases a different distribution’s parameters uniformly at random over the parameter set.
Specifically,
\mathcal{M}_{\text{RR}}
can be written as follows,
\forall\theta\in\mathbf{\Theta}{}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{M}_{\text{RR}}\left(\theta\right)=\theta^{\prime}\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{\epsilon}}{\left|\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}\right|+e^{\epsilon}-1},&\theta^{\prime}=\theta,\\
\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}\right|+e^{\epsilon}-1},&\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}\setminus\left\{\theta\right\}.\end{cases}

Quantization Mechanism (QM)

QM has also been adopted in privacy-preserving mechanism design
[
17
,
9
,
28
]
. This mechanism partitions the secret values into subsets of size
I
and uniformly releases a distribution parameter with the secret as the median index of the corresponding bin.
Specifically,

\mathcal{M}_{\text{QM}}
can be written as
\forall k\in\left\{0,1,\cdots,\left\lceil\frac{s}{I}\right\rceil-1\right\},j\in\left[I\right],\theta\in\mathbf{\Theta}_{kI+j}:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\text{QM}}\left(\theta\right)\sim\text{Unif}\left(\mathbf{\Theta^{\prime}}_{{\text{R}\left(k\right)}}\right),

where
\text{R}\left(k\right)=\left\lfloor{\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)I}\right\rfloor+1
, and
\mathbf{\Theta^{\prime}}_{{\text{R}\left(k\right)}}
represents the released parameter set with secret value
g_{\text{R}\left(k\right)}
.
More generally, if there is not an ordering of the secret, QM randomly at uniform releases a distribution parameter whose secret is contained in the corresponding bin. In the following analysis, we assume that the secret is the fraction of a category (i.e., one of the PMF values).

V-C

Privacy-Utility Tradeoffs

When
\hat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{*}=\mathbf{\Gamma}^{*}
, i.e., the data holder correctly estimates the feasible attribute combination set, we analyze and compare the privacy-distortion tradeoffs between RR and QM as follows. Note that in our analysis of QM, we constrain the secret function
\mathfrak{g}
to be the PMF value for a
specific category (e.g., “the fraction of people above age 18 registered to vote in the U.S.” ) to simplify the analysis.

Theorem V.4
.

(Privacy and Distortion of Randomized Response)
For any secret function
\mathfrak{g}
, the SML and distortion of RR are:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{M}_{\text{RR}},\mathfrak{g}}=\log\frac{1+sr}{1+r},\quad\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{\text{RR}}}=\frac{\hat{d}^{*}_{0}-1}{\hat{d}^{*}_{0}\left(1+r\right)}.

where
r\triangleq\frac{e^{\epsilon}-1}{\left|\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}\right|}=\frac{e^{\epsilon}-1}{\binom{\tau+\hat{d}^{*}_{0}-1}{\hat{d}^{*}_{0}-1}}
.

(Privacy and Distortion of Quantization Mechanism)
The privacy of QM is
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}_{\text{QM}},\mathfrak{g}}=\log\left\lceil{\frac{s}{I}}\right\rceil
. When secret is the fraction of a category, the distortion of QM is

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{\text{QM}}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\hat{d}^{*}_{0}\left\lfloor{\frac{I}{2}}\right\rfloor-\tau}{2\tau\left(\hat{d}^{*}_{0}-1\right)}.

(Mechanism Comparison)

When secret is the fraction of a category, for any non-trivial privacy budget

Empirical Evaluation

In this section, we conduct empirical experiments on a real world dataset to analyze and compare the privacy-distortion tradeoffs of our proposed Randomized Response (RR) and Quantization Mechanism (QM), and evaluate the performance of Quantization Mechanism under downstream tasks. We first introduce the experimental settings.

VI-A

Experimental Setting

Dataset

We conduct our empirical evaluation on the Census Income dataset
[
67
]
, which collects information from 48,842 individuals (
n=48,842
) about their income, education level, age, gender, and more. We set the distribution precision level as
\tau=n
. The dataset contains 22,381 unique attribute combinations (
d=22,381
), all of which we assume to be contained in the estimated attribute combination set
\hat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{*}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{\Gamma}\subseteq\hat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{*}
. As described in

\theta
can be constructed with each category corresponding to a certain attribute combination, e.g., (white, male, age 32, master’s degree, etc), and the value being set as the fraction of samples with this attribute combination.

Baselines

Besides the Quantization Mechanism and Randomized Response proposed, we also include and compare with the mechanism of Wu et al.
[
68
]
, which satisfies a maximal leakage guarantee. For ease of reference, we call this mechanism MaxL. It is known to exhibit a privacy-utility tradeoff with bounded sub-optimality under a class of utility functions that does not include distance measures like total variation distance. However, we do not know of other mechanisms that satisfy a maximal leakage guarantee and can be applied to our dataset.

In short, for a given distribution parameter set
\mathbf{\Theta}
and a released distribution parameter set
\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}
, let
\mathbf{A}
be a subset of
\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}
,
u
be the cost function (total variation distance in our experiment),
f
be a mapping function that satisfies
f\left(\theta\right)=\arg\min_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{A}}u\left(\theta,\theta^{\prime}\right)
, and
\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}}\left(\mathbf{A}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\Theta}\left[u(\theta,f\left(\theta\right))\right]
be the expected cost under the prior distribution
\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}
. MaxL works by initializing
\mathbf{A}=\emptyset
, and iteratively adding an element
\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}\setminus\mathbf{A}
to
\mathbf{A}
, where
\theta^{\prime}
satisfies
\theta^{\prime}=\arg\min_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}\setminus\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}}\left(\mathbf{A}\cup\theta^{\prime}\right)
. MaxL terminates when
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}
or
\min_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}\setminus\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}}\left(\mathbf{A}\cup\theta^{\prime}\right)=\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}}\left(\mathbf{A}\right)
, and design the data release mechanism
\mathcal{M}_{\text{MaxL}}
according to the mapping function
f
.

Since we assume the prior distribution
\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}
is unknown in our setting, we replace the expected cost
\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}}\left(\mathbf{A}\right)
in MaxL by
\max_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}}\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}}\left(\mathbf{A}\right)
. When the cost function is set as total variation distance, the performance of MaxL is sensitive to the choice of the released parameter set
\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}
. For example, if
\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}=\mathbf{\Theta}
, we can easily check that MaxL will always release the original distribution parameter, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}_{\text{MaxL}}\left(\theta\right)=\theta
, resulting in no privacy protection. To ensure a fair comparison, we design the released parameter set
\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}
based on a similar idea to the Quantization Mechanism, that is, we partition the secret values into subsets of size
I
, and for each subset, we uniformly select a distribution parameter with the secret within the subset and add it to
\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}
. We vary the interval length
I
to change the privacy guarantee of MaxL.

We quantify downstream utility by conducting a classification task on the downstream data; namely, we learn a classifier predicting whether an individual has high income or not based on a random forest trained on the released (perturbed) data and compute ROC curves.

VII
Conclusion and Discussion

In this work, we propose
statistic maximal leakage
, a prior-independent and secret-specific privacy measure quantifying the information leakage of a given, known secret. We show that SML satisfies post-processing and composition properties. Although the calculation of statistic maximal leakage is NP-hard in general, under deterministic mechanisms, we show an efficient statistic maximal leakage calculation process. We analyze and compare the privacy-utility tradeoffs of randomized response and the quantization mechanism.
Both theoretical and empirical results suggest a better privacy-utility tradeoff for the quantization mechanism. However, there still remains several interesting future problems.

Design of Alternative Metrics

The operational meaning of statistic maximal leakage is to measure the information gain in an attacker’s knowledge about the most likely secret value after data release, under worst-case prior and attack strategy. However, in some scenarios, especially those related to social justice and fairness, protecting the most unlikely posterior secret value can also be crucial. Additionally, while statistic maximal leakage assesses the attack success probability over the randomness of the released data, a more conservative approach would measure this probability under the worst-case output. In
App.

K
, we take a first step towards alternative metrics under those scenarios and analyze their connections with the indistinguishability-based measure inspired by differential privacy.

Extension to Continuous Parameter Space

The definition and analysis of statistic maximal leakage in
§

IV
are under the discrete parameter space. When extending to the continuous distribution parameter and secret spaces, statistic maximal leakage can be written as

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}=\sup_{f_{\Theta},f_{\hat{G}|\Theta^{\prime}}}\log\frac{f\left(\hat{G}=G\right)}{\sup_{g\in\mathbf{G}}f_{G}\left(g\right)},

(3)

where
f
denotes the probability density function. Similar to
Prop.

IV.1
, we provide an equivalent expression of
Eq.

3
under continuous parameter space in
App.

L
.
However, efficiently calculating or estimate statistic maximal leakage over a continuous parameter space is an open problem, as is designing data release mechanisms with desirable privacy-utility tradeoffs.

For instance, we study tabular datasets with categorical values in this work. This is a very limited class of data. Designing mechanisms that satisfy an SML guarantee for more general classes of data is an important and interesting question.

Relation Between Secret and Utility

In our experiments, we (perhaps surprisingly) observed nonzero utility even at a perfect SML guarantee of zero. This is likely due to our choice of secret, which was correlated with the selected downstream task very loosely. An interesting question is to characterize conditions on the secret and the downstream task to explain how SML will affect utility.

Appendix K
Alternative Privacy Measures

Recall that our proposed statistic maximal leakage can be written as

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}=\sup_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta},\mathbb{P}_{\hat{G}|\Theta^{\prime}}}\log\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{G}=G\right)}{\sup_{g\in\mathbf{G}}\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(g\right)},

which aims to measure the information gain of the attacker’s knowledge about
what the secret value is most likely to be after data release under the worst case prior
\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}
and attack strategy
\mathbb{P}_{\hat{G}|\Theta^{\prime}}
.

In scenarios related to social justice and fairness, where knowing what the secret is most unlikely to be also reveals sensitive information, we propose an alternative privacy metric to measure the information gain of the attacker’s knowledge about
what the secret value is most unlikely to be:

\displaystyle\Pi^{\text{inf}}_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}=\begin{cases}\infty,&\exists\mathbb{P}_{\Theta},\mathbb{P}_{\hat{G}|\Theta^{\prime}}:\ \mathbb{P}_{G}>0\ \&\ \mathbb{P}\left(\hat{G}=G\right)=0,\\
\sup_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}:\mathbb{P}_{G}>0;\mathbb{P}_{\hat{G}|\Theta^{\prime}}}\log\frac{\inf_{g\in\mathbf{G}}\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(g\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{G}=G\right)},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Similarly to statistic maximal leakage,
\Pi^{\text{inf}}_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}
is also defined under the worst-case prior
\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}
and the attack strategy
\mathbb{P}_{\hat{G}|\Theta^{\prime}}
, but consider the lowest prior probability of the secret value in the numerator of the ratio. A higher value of
\Pi^{\text{inf}}_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}
indicates that the attacker gains more information about the least likely secret. We set
\Pi^{\text{inf}}_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}
to infinity when the adversary can successfully infer what the secret value is not after the data release.

Furthermore, statistic maximal leakage measures the attack success probability over the randomness of the
released data. In a more conservative scenario, however, one may prefer to measure the attack success probability under the worst-case output. For this scenario, we propose alternative privacy measures,
\tilde{\Pi}_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}
and
\tilde{\Pi}^{\text{inf}}_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}
, which quantify the information leakage of the most likely and most unlikely secret values, respectively:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta},\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta^{\prime},\mathbb{P}_{\hat{G}|\theta^{\prime}}}\log\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{G}=G\right)}{\sup_{g\in\mathbf{G}}\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(g\right)},

\displaystyle\tilde{\Pi}^{\text{inf}}_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\infty,&\exists\mathbb{P}_{\Theta},\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime},\mathbb{P}_{\hat{G}|\theta^{\prime}}:\ \mathbb{P}_{G}>0\ \&\ \mathbb{P}\left(\hat{G}=G\right)=0,\\
\sup_{\mathbb{P}_{\Theta}:\mathbb{P}_{G}>0;\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime};\mathbb{P}_{\hat{G}|\theta^{\prime}}}\log\frac{\inf_{g\in\mathbf{G}}\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(g\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{G}=G\right)},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In addition to the worst-case prior and attack strategy,
\tilde{\Pi}_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}
and
\tilde{\Pi}^{\text{inf}}_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}
are also defined under the worst-case data output
\theta^{\prime}
; thus, the attack strategy is represented as
\mathbb{P}_{\hat{G}|\theta^{\prime}}
.

Additionally, inspired by differential privacy, we design the indistinguishability-based privacy measure
\mathcal{L}_{DP}
, and we say that a data release mechanism
\mathcal{M}
is
\mu
-
\mathcal{L}_{DP}
if

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{M}\left(\theta_{1}\right)\in\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}_{0}\right)\leq e^{\mu}\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{M}\left(\theta_{2}\right)\in\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}_{0}\right),\quad\forall\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathbf{\Theta},\mathfrak{g}\left(\theta_{1}\right)\not=\mathfrak{g}\left(\theta_{2}\right);\forall\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}_{0}\subseteq\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}.

We analyze the connections between those privacy measures in

K.7

Let
\mathbf{\Theta}_{g}
be the set of distribution parameters whose secret value is
g
, i.e,
\mathbf{\Theta}_{g}=\left\{\theta\in\mathbf{\Theta}|\mathfrak{g}\left(\theta\right)=g\right\}

For statistic maximal leakage
\Pi_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}
, we have
\Pi_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g}}=\sup_{f_{\Theta}}\log\frac{\int_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{\Theta}^{\prime}}\sup_{g\in\mathbf{G}}f_{G\Theta^{\prime}}\left(g,\theta^{\prime}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}}{\sup_{g\in\mathbf{G}}f_{G}\left(g\right)}

Screening of close contacts of pulmonary tuberculosis in an urban area in Shanghai, China: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Shiqi Zhang, Fen Li, Jing Chen, Zheyuan Wu, Chenxi Ning, Xin Shen, Zhengan Yuan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThe study aimed to explore the risk factors of close contacts developing pulmonary tuberculosis (TB), identify active TB and describe the sociodemographic characteristics of active TB among close contacts in Putuo District, Shanghai.Study designA cross-sectional study was conducted among the close contacts of pulmonary TB in Putuo District from 2017 to 2018.MethodsSymptom questionnaire, Chest X-ray (CXR) and interferon-gamma release assay (IGRA) were carried out to screen the consenting close contacts. Three sputum samples were collected for sputum smear tests, sputum culture tests, and molecular-based diagnostics from whose screening was positive to confirm the diagnosis and receive treatment.ResultsA total of 1036 individuals were included in this study, with an average of 47.3 ± 19.4 years old, including 427 males and 609 females. Four of the close contacts had presumptive TB based on symptom screening. Fourteen had presumptive TB based on abnormal CXR finding and 52 had positive IGRA results. Multivariate analysis showed that index cases with positive sputum smears, close contacts who had presumptive TB based on symptom screening and their interaction term were at significantly higher risk of developing TB.ConclusionsStudies have shown particular emphasis should be put on those with bacteria-positive index cases or those who had presumptive TB based on symptom screening during follow-up. Since the proportion of latent tuberculosis infection is high among close contacts undergoing IGRA examination, measures such as tuberculosis preventive treatment can be considered to reduce the incidence of TB.Keywords: Tuberculosis, Close contacts, Screening, Risk factors

Introduction

Tuberculosis(TB) is a chronic, respiratory-transmitted infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis (M.TB) infection, which is one of the leading causes of death worldwide. About 10.8 million patients were newly diagnosed with TB globally in 2023, with an incidence rate of 134 per 100,000 people.

China is a high-burden country for tuberculosis [1] which has brought enormous pressure on Chinese economic and social development. To control the epidemic of TB, it is necessary to detect and treat infected patients as soon as possible. Improving the strategy of actively detecting undiagnosed TB cases can better control TB at the population level [2–4] mainly including screening close contacts of TB patients as soon as they are diagnosed and people at risk of TB infection. The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends that systematically screening for TB should be conducted on the risk groups including household and close contacts of people with TB, people living with HIV, people exposed to silica (mainly some miners) and people in prisons and penitentiary institutions [5]. Systematic screening of contacts of TB patients and people at risk is part of the WHO strategies to control TB [6].

As the second largest city in China, the TB incidence and mortality in Shanghai [7] are lower than the national level [8]. As one of the most economically developed regions in China, Shanghai has established a TB prevention and control network composed of three levels including city, district and community since 1998 [9], as well as a “trinity” TB prevention and control mode composed of community health service centers, designated TB medical institutions and disease control and prevention centers, which providing a guarantee for TB detection, treatment, tracking and management. The number of permanent residents in Putuo District ranked seventh among the 16 districts in Shanghai in 2017, while the proportion of foreign-born ranked ninth. The number of elderly people in the registered population ranked fourth in Shanghai. According to the 2018 Putuo Yearbook, the success rate of treatment for TB patients reached 89.2%.

Close contacts are at high risk of developing TB. Contact surveys aim to identify previously undiagnosed cases of active TB among contacts of index cases, and early diagnosis and treatment can reduce ongoing transmission and have a broader epidemiological impact on reducing community transmission [10]. The current screening strategy in China is to carry out symptom screening of household close contacts immediately after the diagnosis of patients with positive sputum smears and to conduct Chest X-ray (CXR) examinations for those who had presumptive TB based on symptom screening. However, during the implementation of this strategy in Shanghai, a small number of TB patients can be detected in time. The main causes of the TB epidemic in Shanghai include the increasing number of cases among the floating population, a higher prevalence among the elderly population [11] and an increased incidence of Multidrug-Resistant Tuberculosis [12]. Therefore, a cross-sectional study of close contacts of TB patients in Putuo District of Shanghai was conducted to explore the risk factors of close contacts developing pulmonary TB, identify active TB and describe the sociodemographic characteristics of active TB among close contacts in Putuo District, Shanghai.

Methods

Study design

Cross-sectional study

A cross-sectional study was conducted among the close contacts of newly registered TB from 2017 to 2018 in Putuo District of Shanghai. Baseline data of index cases including name, gender, age, treatment classification, sputum smear results, sputum culture results and the presence of a cavity in the Chest X-ray results were obtained from the Chinese Tuberculosis Information Management System. Symptom questionnaire, CXR examination and interferon-gamma release assay(IGRA) were carried out for those aged 15 years and older, while those under 15 years old should be screened for symptoms and IGRA. When they had presumptive TB based on symptom screening, abnormal CXR or positive IGRA, they are referred to designated medical institutions for further examinations including sputum smear tests, sputum culture tests, and molecular-based diagnostics to confirm the diagnosis and receive treatment.

Definitions

Index cases

Patients who were first identified and reported with new or recurrent pulmonary TB in the family or other similar settings [13].

Close contacts

According to the pilot program for intensive screening of close contacts of TB patients in Shanghai, close contacts are defined as person who are not in the household but shared an enclosed space with index cases for up to 8 h per day or more than 40 h cumulatively [14] during the infectious period of index cases, including colleagues, neighbors, schoolmates, acquaintances, friends and others.

Household contacts

Household contacts are defined as person who shared the same enclosed living space with index cases for up to 8 h per day or more than 40 h cumulatively during the infectious period of index cases, including parents, spouses, children and others.

Infectious period

For symptomatic index cases, the infectious period is defined as the period from the onset of symptoms to 2 weeks after effective treatment; for asymptomatic index cases, the infectious period is defined as the period from 3 months before diagnosis [5] to 2 weeks after effective treatment.

Presumptive TB symptoms

Presumptive TB symptoms were defined by this study, as any of the following conditions: (1) cough or expectorate for more than 2 weeks; (2) hemoptysis; (3) cough for more than 1 week yet less than 2 weeks and accompanied by any of the following symptoms including chest pain, fever, shiver, dyspnea, night sweats, loss of appetite, or weight loss [5].

Statistical analysis

Python was used for analyzing demographic and screening data. Using Chi-square test to compare the proportions of categorical variables. To determine risk factors associated with active TB, logistic regression analysis was performed. A 95% confidence interval (95%CI) and a 5% level of significance were used to interpret the statistical significance. Statistical significance was considered if p-value < 0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristics

From 2017 to 2018, a total of 540 cases of pulmonary TB in Putuo District were enrolled, of which about 65.9% (356/540) were male and cases over 60 years old accounted for the high proportion (38.7%). Initial treatment totaled up to 92.0% (497/540), 34.4% (186/540) of patients had positive sputum smear results, 43.3% (234/540) of patients had positive sputum culture results and 19.1% (103/540) of patients had cavities in the CXR results (Table 1).

Characteristics of close contacts

One thousand and thirty-six close contacts from the index cases were recruited in the investigation (Fig. 1), of whom 427 (41.2%) were males and 609 (58.8%) were females (Table 2). The average age for contact cases was founded to be 47.3 ± 19.4 years old, comprised of 93.1% (965/1026) of contacts older than 15 years old, and 6.9% (71/1036) of them less than 15 years old. Almost 83% (860/1036) of close contacts had received Bacillus Calmette-Guerin (BCG) vaccine and 89.3% (925/1036) of them were in contact with index cases for more than 3 months. 96.4% (999/1036) of close contacts were household contacts, while 3.6% (37/1036) of close contacts were non-household contacts. Of the non-household close contact, 2.7% (1/37) was parent, 10.8% (4/37) were spouses, 18.9% (7/37) were children, 21.6% (8/37) were colleagues, 37.9% (14/37) were schoolmates, 2.7% (1/37) were friends and 5.4% (2/37) were other close contacts.

All close contacts were screened for symptoms, of whom 4 had presumptive TB based on symptom screening. Only one (1.4%) of the close contacts under 15 years old had a positive IGRA result. A total of 14 (1.5%) close contacts aged older than 15 years old had presumptive TB based on abnormal CXR finding and 51 (5.3%) had positive IGRA.

Factors with p < 0.20 in univariate analysis including close contacts presumed TB based on symptom screening, and the sputum smear and culture of index cases, and the presence of cavities in the CXR result of index cases were included in multivariate regression analysis. Multivariate regression analysis (Table 3) showed that close contacts presumed TB based on symptom screening and the sputum smear status of index cases had statistically significant associations with TB occurrence.

Tuberculosis incidence surveillance

In this study, 8 cases of close contacts were detected with active TB. Seven of their index cases’ sputum smears and cultures were positive, while 4 of their index cases’ CXR results showed cavitation. All 8 close contacts were were household contacts, vaccinated with BCG. Three contacts were male and others were female (Table 4). Seven close contacts had a contact frequency of more than 7 times per week with index cases. Two cases had presumptive TB based on symptom screening, seven showed abnormal CXR presumed TB, and two had positive IGRA results.

Discussion

The study showed that there was no statistical significance in whether they were household contacts. This may be due to the fact that although the screening scope was expanded, the proportion of household contacts among the included close contacts reached 96.6%. Index cases provided less information on non-household close contacts, which may affect the accuracy of the study results. Transmission among non-household contacts was more concealed, which may increase the difficulty of TB control. The next issue to be addressed is how to further improve the screening of close contacts of TB patients. While safeguarding the rights of patients, the balance between the patient’s right to privacy and the contact’s right to know should be considered [4].

The Shaanxi study’s screening method [16] differed from this study in that only close contacts presumed TB based on symptom screening were further examined. The sampling method in Shandong province [17] was different from this study, which used cluster stratified random sampling to investigate TB patients with positive sputum smears and their close contacts. However, both studies [16, 17] showed that close contacts had presumptive TB based on symptom screening were at significantly higher risk of developing TB, which was consistent with the results of this study, suggesting the need to strengthen further screening of symptomatic close contacts. Eight cases with active TB detected in this study were all over 15 years old, probably due to the low number of close contacts under 15 years old. However, some studies have shown that the prevalence of Mycobacterium TB infection in children among close contacts was about 36% [18]. The prevalence in children among close contacts of index cases with positive sputum smears was more than 60%, which was about 5 times [19] higher than the prevalence of children who were not close contacts. Children are more susceptible to Mycobacterium TB when they are in close contact with TB patients because their immune system is not mature yet. Therefore, when screening close contacts of TB patients, attention should be focused on minors for early detection and early treatment, especially when their index cases had positive sputum smears.

Multivariate analysis showed that the risk of the contacts with the index cases with positive sputum smears developing TB was 12.52 times higher than those with negative sputum smears. Several studies have shown that the higher the sputum bacteria count of positive sputum smear patients with TB, the more the number of bacteria discharged into the surrounding environment and the higher the chance of infection among their close contacts [16, 20]. At the same time, this study showed that the proportion of latent tuberculosis infection in the population undergoing IGRA examination was about 18%. Therefore, early screening and preventive treatment of close contacts of positive sputum smear patients with TB are essential for TB control efforts. At the same time, studies have shown that including contacts of sputum smear-negative TB cases can improve the effectiveness of screening [21]. Therefore, the inclusion of sputum smear-negative index cases in the screening of close contacts can identify secondary cases among contacts as soon as possible and reduce the transmission of Mycobacterium TB.

The definition of close contacts in this study was stricter than WHO. WHO [22] defined close contacts as a person who is not in the household but shared an enclosed space, such as a social gathering place, workplace or facility, for extended periods during the day with the index case during the 3 months before commencement of the current treatment episode. In contrast, this study used clearer, quantitative and operable criteria to determine close contacts. Through screening, active TB cases were identified from close contacts with negative sputum smears patients, indicating that the definition of close contacts used in this study was indeed meaningful. This study found that close contacts had presumptive TB based on symptom screening and positive sputum smears should be paid more attention during the screening of close contacts of TB patients. As a cross-sectional study, this study had several limitations. Firstly, in this study, only one screening of close contacts was conducted after the diagnosis of the index cases, which could not screen out all patients with active TB, because the incubation period of TB was from several months to several decades. According to the recommendations of experts from the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, the screening strategy should be upgraded to conduct initial screening after the diagnosis of the index cases and symptom screening after 6 months, while CXR examinations should be added at the end of 12 months after the first screening. If the screening results are abnormal, additional pathogenic testing will be performed [23] to improve the detection rate and take appropriate treatment of patients with active TB. Secondly, close contacts provided by the index cases were mainly household close contacts. Less information was provided on non-household close contacts due to fear of discrimination or influence on work, which may lead to reporting bias. There may be recall bias when collecting information on the total contact duration between close contacts and index cases and the presence or absence of symptoms presumed TB.

The sample size of this study is small, which does not meet the requirements of Event Per Variable (EPV). Therefore, the results may not be robust enough. However, considering the rarity of active TB patients detected in close contacts with stricter definition in this study and the interpretability of the results, it is still presented. The reliability of the results need to be corroborated by further studies.

In conclusion, Studies have shown particular emphasis should be put on those with bacteria-positive index cases or those who had presumptive TB based on symptom screening during follow-up. Since the proportion of latent tuberculosis infection is high among close contacts undergoing IGRA examination, measures such as tuberculosis preventive treatment can be considered to reduce the incidence of TB.

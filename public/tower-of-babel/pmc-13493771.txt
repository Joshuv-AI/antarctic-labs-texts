Comparing surgical outcomes between the Jones and Wies procedures with lateral tarsal strip for involutional lower eyelid entropion
Authors: Prattana Tulyakanit, Orapan Aryasit, Jiramet Sotatawi, Nattakrich Suttirak
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We aimed to compare surgical outcomes between the Jones and Wies procedures with a lateral tarsal strip (JP + LTS and WP + LTS, respectively) for involutional entropion repair. This retrospective, interventional, comparative study included 155 patients (unilateral: 126; bilateral: 29) with involutional entropion who underwent JP + LTS (58 eyes) or WP + LTS (126 eyes). A successful outcome was defined as a normal eyelid position at last follow-up without requiring reoperation. The mean follow-up duration was 37.3 months (median: 26.5 months; range: 6–178 months). Average operating room times per eye were 68.1 ± 26.3 (WP + LTS) and 82.8 ± 27.7 (JP + LTS) min (p = 0.001). After primary entropion repair, recurrence occurred in 11 but ectropion in 4 eyes. Success rates were 91.4% (JP + LTS) and 92.0% (WP + LTS) (p = 1.000). Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated success rates of 93.6%, 92.7%, and 90.0% at 1.5, 3, and 5 years, respectively. Trainees performing JP + LTS had a significantly lower success rate than consultants (adjusted hazard ratio: 0.10, p = 0.049). Therefore, both JP + LTS and WP + LTS achieved acceptable long-term success rates for involutional lower eyelid entropion repair; however, WP + LTS involved a shorter operating room time and presence of meibomian gland dysfunction. The success rate of JP + LTS was higher when performed by experienced surgeons.

Introduction

Entropion is a common eyelid malposition characterized by the inward turning of the eyelid margin against the globe. Involutional entropion, the most prevalent type, becomes more common with age1. In Asians, involutional entropion typically occurs in the lower eyelid due to a short axial length and prominent orbicularis oculi muscles1,2. Therefore, the incidence is higher among East Asians than among other ethnicities2,3. The etiology is complex, including laxity of the lower eyelid retractors, horizontal lower eyelid laxity, and overriding of the orbicularis oculi muscle4–6.

The ideal surgical approach to repair involutional entropion should address various contributing factors to achieve permanent correction and maintain the normal eyelid position7,8. The Jones procedure (JP) is a straightforward technique that involves the plication of the lower eyelid retractors to strengthen vertical eyelid laxity9. The subciliary incision scar prevents the preseptal orbicularis oculi muscle from overriding superiorly onto the tarsus8,10. The Wies procedure (WP) involves the creation of a transverse eyelid split with a resultant scar and everting sutures to prevent the overriding of the pre-septal orbicularis muscle during lid eversion11,12. Tissue fibrosis at the eyelid split level helps prevent such overriding12. WP offers advantages, including faster execution, simplicity, and effective resolution of the cicatricial eyelid. The lateral tarsal strip (LTS) procedure is an effective method for strengthening horizontal eyelid laxity and minimizing the recurrence rate7.

In this retrospective comparative study, we aimed to assess the relative efficacy of transcutaneous JP + LTS and WP + LTS in the treatment of involutional entropion. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to examine the surgical outcomes of two entropion repair techniques in Asian patients.

Results

A total of 301 patients underwent JP + LTS or WP + LTS during the study period. Of these, 146 patients were excluded because of a follow-up period < 6 months (80 in the WP + LTS group and 60 in the JP + LTS group), prior entropion repair (n = 4), or incomplete medical records (n = 2). Finally, 155 patients, accounting for 184 eyes (136 patients from Songklanagarind Hospital and 19 patients from Vachira Phuket Hospital, with an average age of 75.4 ± 7.9 years [range: 56–93 years]) were enrolled in the study. This study included 63 men and 92 women. The most common presenting symptom was eye irritation (94%), followed by tearing (12%) and eye redness (11%). Corneal perforation due to severe corneal ulcer, a serious complication, occurred in one eye (0.5%). The baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Experienced surgeons performed WP + LTS on 93 eyes and JP + LTS on 39 eyes. Both trainees and consultants showed a significant preference for performing WP + LTS in patients with MGD (p = 0.026 and p < 0.001, respectively).

After a mean follow-up period of 37.4 months (range: 6 months–14.8 years), successful outcomes of involutional entropion were achieved in 54 of 58 eyes (91.4%) in the JP + LTS group, compared with 116 of 126 eyes (92.1%) in the WP + LTS group (p = 1.000). The mean operating room times were 68.1 ± 26.3 and 82.8 ± 27.7 min per eye in the WP + LTS and JP + LTS groups, respectively (p = 0.001). Complications occurred in 20 eyes, including recurrent entropion in 11 eyes, postoperative ectropion in eight eyes, and wound dehiscence in one eye. Four eyes with postoperative ectropion showed a complete spontaneous resolution.

After primary entropion repair, 15 eyes experienced unsuccessful surgical outcomes. Recurrence occurred in 11 eyes, of which seven did not undergo reoperation, while four underwent reoperation; three of these achieved favorable outcomes, and one had persistent entropion. Four eyes developed ectropion postoperatively, whereas three underwent ectropion correction, of which two showed persistent ectropion, whereas one demonstrated favorable results. One eye did not undergo ectropion repair.

The Kaplan–Meier curves of all patients who underwent primary entropion repair without revision surgery demonstrated that the cumulative risk rates of successful outcomes following primary entropion repair were 93.6%, 92.7%, and 90.0% at 1.5, 3, and 5 years, respectively. Discussion

In this retrospective comparative study, we evaluated the success rates and complications of JP + LTS and WP + LTS at two medical centers in Thailand, with a mean follow-up period of 3 years. The success rates of JP + LTS and WP + LTS were 91.4% and 92.1%, respectively (p = 1.000). The common complications following primary entropion repair were recurrent entropion and postoperative ectropion. WP + LTS was associated with a shorter operating room time than JP + LTS. The Kaplan–Meier curve demonstrated that the cumulative risk of unsuccessful outcomes after primary entropion repair at 5 years was 10%. The rate of unsuccessful outcomes for JP + LTS performed by trainees was 10 times higher than that for experienced surgeons (p = 0.028).

Retractor plication or reinsertion alone yielded variable results13,14. Lee et al.15 compared simple posterior layer advancement of lower eyelid retractors with the LTS procedure for involutional entropion in the Japanese population. Patients with horizontal eyelid laxity showed better outcomes with a combination of retractor advancement and LTS, whereas those without horizontal eyelid laxity benefited from retractor advancement alone. The appropriately treated group achieved a success rate of 100%, whereas patients with horizontal eyelid laxity treated with retractor advancement alone achieved a success rate of 91.3%.

Boboridis et al.16 also reported a recurrence rate of 17% after WP. Additionally, the recurrence rate remained high in several patients who underwent WP alone8,17. However, our study included patients who underwent WP + LTS, which could eliminate lower eyelid laxity and improve the success rate. The operating room time for WP + LTS was shorter than that for JP + LTS, making it more suitable for older patients. We used the term “operating room time” rather than operation time because this parameter more accurately reflected the time spent beyond the surgical procedure itself, including a review of the advantages, disadvantages, complications, and postoperative care. However, the operating room time, which included non-surgical activities, could be a confounding factor of surgery.

The Kaplan–Meier curve for unsuccessful outcomes after primary entropion repair at 6 months was 3.8% (JP + LTS, 3.4%; WP + LTS, 4%). Approximately 4% of the patients had early unsuccessful results, which may be associated with the surgical techniques used. The potential causes for recurrent entropion over a long-term follow-up include aging, increased severity of MGD, loss of tissue integrity, and suture tightening.

For residents and fellow trainees, the Kaplan–Meier curve showed a significantly higher rate of unsuccessful outcomes for JP + LTS at 5 years (20.7%) than those for the procedures performed by oculoplastic consultants (2.6%). In contrast, no significant differences were observed in the success rates between several ophthalmic procedures, including ptosis correction18, dacryocystorhinostomy19, and evisceration or enucleation20, performed by trainees and those performed by experienced staffs. This is likely because trainees typically perform these procedures under the supervision of an experienced staff in an academic training center. Trainees can perform WP + LTS due to the simplicity of the surgical technique, its effectiveness in preventing corneal complications. Therefore, WP + LTS serves as a valuable procedure for young ophthalmologists, particularly in regions with limited access to oculoplastic specialists.

Additional factors that make WP + LTS appealing to trainees include its shorter operative time, relative technical simplicity, and suitability for cases involving cicatricial components21. In contrast, the more complex JP + LTS procedure requires more surgical experience due to anatomical rationale for the JP’s technical difficulty, as the surgeon must accurately identify the lower eyelid retractors, reattach them directly to the lower tarsus, and appropriately tighten the lower eyelid laxity.

LTS can cause meibomian gland loss, which affects tear function and may lead to frequent eye squeezing due to persistent eye irritation. Additionally, a previous study reported irreversible changes after involutional entropion repair. We suggest that the key to successful long-term outcomes is the adequate treatment of MGD with traditional eyelid hygiene and warm compresses22–24 after surgical correction of involutional entropion, especially in patients diagnosed with MGD before surgery. However, meibomian gland loss remained unchanged following entropion correction25.

This study has some limitations. The retrospective design introduced an inherent selection bias and variety of the duration of follow-up. Furthermore, we only had access to data that pertained to operating room time, which although confounded by many factors, reflected the real situation. Surgeon preference for one surgical technique over the other was noted, and data assessment for cosmetic outcomes was lacking. Notably, surgeons preferred to perform WP + LTS in patients with involutional entropion, which is often associated with MGD. This selection bias may have affected the equivalent successful surgical rates of both procedures. Subgroup analysis of the predictive factors of surgical outcomes in patients with and without MGD showed no variables affecting surgical outcomes. Additionally, surgeons preferred to perform JP + LTS in women than in men, possibly owing to reduced scarring. We conducted a multivariable analysis to investigate the predictive factors related to unsuccessful surgical outcomes. The results indicated that trainees who performed JP + LTS had significantly higher rates of unsuccessful surgical outcomes.

In conclusion, this study demonstrated that both JP + LTS and WP + LTS are effective for long-term involutional entropion correction. However, WP + LTS requires a shorter operating room time and trainee-friendly nature than JP + LTS, making it suitable for older patients and patients with MGD. Trainees can perform WP + LTS with satisfactory outcomes, whereas JP + LTS yields a lower success rate when performed by trainees.

Materials and methods

We retrospectively reviewed the medical records of all patients with involutional lower eyelid entropion who underwent JP + LTS or WP + LTS at Songklanagarind Hospital between January 2006 and December 2022 and at Vachira Phuket Hospital between July 2019 and December 2022. The study was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, Prince of Songkla University (REC62-086-2-1) and Vachira Phuket Hospital (REC 021/2023), and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The need for informed consent was waived owing to the retrospective nature of the study.

Data included patient age, sex, clinical presentation, eyelid margin status (including MGD, trichiasis, and distichiasis), operating room time, experience of surgeons, primary procedure outcome, recurrence onset, and secondary operation technique and outcomes. Patients with a history of lower eyelid surgery and a follow-up duration < 6 months were excluded. Generally, follow-up was scheduled as follows: 1–2 weeks, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, and then every 6 months postoperatively. Successful outcome was defined as a normal eyelid position at the last follow-up without the need for secondary operation.

The JP involved a subciliary incision followed by dissection to under the orbicularis oculi muscle. The orbital septum was opened, and the lower eyelid retractors were identified and dissected. The disinserted edge of the lower eyelid retractors was reattached onto the lower tarsus with three 6 − 0 absorbable braided polyglactin sutures. Skin was closed using 6 − 0 nonabsorbable monofilament polypropylene or 7 − 0 nonabsorbable monofilament polyamide continuous sutures.

The WP involved a horizontal full-thickness incision 4-mm from the lower lid margin of the eyelid to avoid the tarsal plate. Two to three double-arm 6 − 0 absorbable braided polyglactin sutures were passed from the posterior part of the lower eyelid through the orbicularis oculi and then through the skin on the anterior part 2 mm from the lower lid margin. These sutures were tied to evert the lower eyelid. Skin was closed using 6 − 0 nonabsorbable monofilament polypropylene or 7 − 0 nonabsorbable monofilament polyamide continuous sutures.

For the LTS procedure, a 10-mm lateral canthotomy incision was performed. An inferior canthotomy and cantholysis were created. The mucosal lining at the superior margin of the tarsal plate was trimmed off. The anterior and posterior lamella of the posterior aspect of lower eyelid were separated to make an LTS, the length of which depended on the severity of horizontal lower eyelid laxity. The LTS was fixed to the periosteum of the lateral orbital wall with a double-arm nonabsorbable braided polyester. Excessive anterior lamella was trimmed. The lateral angle was formed with a double-arm 6 − 0 absorbable braided polyglactin suture. Subcutaneous tissue and skin were closed.

Data were analyzed using Stata Statistical Software (Stata/MP 14.1; StataCorp LP, College Station, TX, USA). Descriptive data are reported as the mean ± standard deviation and median (interquartile range). Quantitative analysis of continuous and categorical data was performed using Fisher’s exact test, paired t-test, and the χ2 test. The factors associated with unsuccessful surgical outcomes were determined using a direct acyclic graph created with DAGitty version 3.0 (Johannes Textor, Utrecht University, the Netherlands), which incorporated all relevant domains to minimize bias. A causal diagram depicting the relationship between the variable domains and covariables was created based on an understanding of the subject matter (Fig. 2). Multivariable Cox regression models, based on the direct acyclic graph, were performed using a robust estimation of the standard error to account for multiple eyes per patient in the dataset. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

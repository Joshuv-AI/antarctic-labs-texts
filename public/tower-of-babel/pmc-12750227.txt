Electron Tri-Layer Enhancement Mode High-Electron-Mobility Transistor: Design and Analysis
Authors: Bushra Qureshi, Abdullah G. Alharbi, Rashid Ayub, Sajad A. Loan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In this work, we report the design of a novel enhancement mode (E-mode) trielectron-layer (TEL) AlGaN/GaN high-electron-mobility transistor (HEMT) for the first time. The realization of the E-mode (initially off) operation has been done by employing an optimized P-GaN pocket buried in the GaN channel layer. In the proposed structure, double two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) layers get created: one at the heterojunction of the top AlGaN layer and the bottom AlGaN layer, while the other at the heterojunction of the bottom AlGaN and GaN channel layer. Besides, charge plasma electron gas (CPEG) layers are induced by the source and drain electrode in the source and drain regions. Therefore, the uniqueness of this structure is the presence of three conduction paths: two 2DEG layers and one CPEG. The presence of the double-layer 2DEG and single CPEG layer enhances the overall performance of the device, like transconductance (G m), cutoff frequency (f T), etc. A two-dimensional (2D) calibrated simulation study shows that the device exhibits a threshold voltage (V th) of 3.5 V. Further, a significant improvement of 284% and 97.4% is achieved in transconductance and breakdown voltage of the proposed device, respectively, in comparison to the conventional HEMT. A comprehensive study of the effect of trap density and AlN properties on the performance of the proposed structure has also been performed.

Introduction

Silicon has been serving the integrated circuit (IC) technology for many decades because of its unique electronics properties. It is considered the most available material on earth’s crust, resulting in cheap electronic devices. The silicon-based device fabrication is easy and the technology is more mature than any other material-based IC technology. , However, extracting more and more performance from silicon is now extremely difficult and uneconomical. The device engineers are facing challenges in achieving properties that are needed for high-power-application domains. Si with a band gap (E g) of 1.1 eV has a breakdown strength of 0.3 MV/cm, which limits it for high-power applications. It becomes ineffective at temperatures exceeding 150 °C and therefore requires complex cooling systems. Therefore, there is a need for further advancement of materials and devices that can meet the requirement of high-power devices. It has been observed that the wide band gap (WBG) semiconductors suit more high-power applications as they offer high breakdown voltage and high electron mobility and can operate efficiently at elevated temperatures compared to low band gap materials like Si. Among the group of WBG semiconductors, GaN is considered as the most suitable compound semiconductor material, which can offer the properties highly desired for the high-power-application domain. − It has a large breakdown voltage of 3 MV/cm, a higher electron mobility of 2000 cm2/V s, and a high electron saturation velocity of 2.5 × 107 cm/s, and can operate at high temperatures. A unique property of GaN is the creation of 2DEG, resulting in high electron mobility without intentional doping in the device. The presence of 2DEG in the channel layer makes the HEMT device work in depletion-mode (D-mode) or initially ON without applying any gate voltage, i.e., at V GS = 0 V. The presence of a high-mobility 2DEG layer at the interface of AlGaN and GaN layers results in a huge amount of current in the device even in the unbiased state. If the gate voltage is removed, 2DEG will still be present at the heterojunction, making the device work in normal ON mode. The D-mode behavior of HEMT devices limits their application in the power-switching domain as extra circuitry is needed to turn off the device, making it more complex and increasing the chances of failure. Thus, E-mode operation is crucial for modern high-frequency and high-power applications, providing controlled switching operation of the device without the need for a negative power supply. The E-mode operation can be achieved by shifting the threshold voltage of the device in the positive direction. Over the past few years, researchers have been working on several possible methodologies for effective E-mode GaN HEMT technology. Among the different technologies developed by the researchers are the recessed gate technique, fluorine implanted, p-GaN gate transistors, thin barrier layer etc.

Herein, we propose a novel structure of the triple-electron-layer AlGaN-GaN normally off/E-mode HEMT. The proposed device exhibits E-mode behavior by inserting a p-GaN pocket with doping (2.5 × 1017) in the GaN channel layer below the gate region. It uses the charge plasma technique to form source and drain regions along with the 2DEG created at the interface of the top AlGaN-bottom AlGaN layer and the bottom AlGaN-GaN layer. The p-GaN pocket buried in the GaN channel layer induces a “two-dimensional hole gas” (2DHG), which helps in realizing the E-mode by uplifting the conduction band above the Fermi level. Therefore, at zero gate bias the device exhibits E-mode operation. A proper gate bias is needed to further turn on the device. The proposed device uses an AlN layer between the GaN channel layer and the GaN buffer layer. It has been observed that introducing the AlN layer helps in achieving a more positive shift in the threshold voltage. The existing enhancement mode HEMT using techniques like fluorine ion implantation and recessed gate has disadvantages in terms of degraded mobility and transconductance. The groove gate technique requires a deep recess to achieve a more positive threshold voltage, which in turn reduces the density of 2DEG and hence limits the ON current. Our proposed device TEL E-mode HEMT device with the presence of CPEG along with dual 2DEG offers a higher ON current and transconductance and hence shows better results. Our proposed devices with a positive threshold voltage will be useful in power-switching applications because they enable fail-safe operation, simplifying the circuitry design. However, there exist certain fabrication challenges in achieving precise Mg doping and uniformity in p-GaN. To overcome these complexities, several strategies such as delta-doping and ALE have been proposed for a promising etching process to improve the damage.

A 2D experimental calibration has been done using an Atlas simulator showing the E-mode operation of the proposed device exhibiting a threshold voltage of 3.5 V. Further, improvement in transconductance cutoff frequency and breakdown voltage has been observed in the proposed HEMT when compared to the conventional HEMT.

Simulation Setup and the Device Schematics

Figure illustrates the schematic diagrams of the conventional depletion-mode (D-mode) and the proposed trielectron-layer (TEL E-mode) HEMT devices. Both the structures employ a 30 nm AlGaN barrier layer and a 3 μm GaN buffer layer. SiO2 is used as a gate oxide in the proposed device because it serves as a good insulator and has favorable band alignment with GaN. The proposed device has a double AlGaN barrier layer, which enhances the 2DEG, a p-GaN pocket buried in the GaN channel layer to achieve E-mode operation, and an additional AlN layer inserted between the GaN channel and GaN buffer layer, as shown in Figure . The aluminum (Al) composition of the top AlGaN layer and bottom AlGaN layer is taken as 0.3 and 0.2, respectively. The thickness of the bottom AlGaN layer is 10 nm. The p-GaN pocket in the GaN layer is 1 μm in length and 100 nm in thickness. The other structural parameters of the proposed device are the gate length (L g) of 4 μm, source/drain (S/D) length (l S/l D) of 1 μm, and source/drain spacing (l GS/l GD) of 2.5 μm. A fabrication flow of the proposed TEL E-mode HEMT device is given in Figure . Sapphire is used as a substrate material to deposit the GaN layer using the MOCVD process, as shown in Figure a,b. The other materials that can be used as substrates are SiC and Silicon (111). However, the sapphire is favorable because it is cheap and a good quality of GaN can be grown on sapphire because of the same crystal structure. The entire growth process is carried out using ammonia (NH3), trimethylgallium (TMGa), and trimethylaluminum (TMAl) as precursors with hydrogen acting as the carrier gas. Next, as shown in Figure c, the AlN layer is grown on GaN either by metal organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD) or by molecular beam epitaxy (MBE). It reduces the alloy scattering in the GaN buffer layer and also helps in enhancing the electron density. Figure d shows that an active GaN channel layer can be deposited on the AlN layer by the MOCVD technique. Further, the GaN channel layer can be selectively etched for depositing the P-GaN pocket. Figure g,h shows that the bottom and top AlGaN layers can be deposited over the GaN channel layer. As shown in Figure e, the 20 nm SiO2 layer can be deposited as the gate oxide using plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) with a tetraethyl orthosilicate precursor and then can undergo thermal annealing at 850 °C in an N2 environment. In the next step, all electrodes can be deposited to complete the device, as shown in Figure a–k.

In this work, the simulation study of the conventional and the proposed E-mode device is performed using the Silvaco ALTAS TCAD device simulator. The simulation is actually the calibration, where we initially calibrated the results of a device experimentally fabricated by ref . Herein, all of the device parameters and dimensions are kept the same as mentioned in ref and the structure is simulated. The calibrated graph shown in Figure almost matches with the experimental result. The different models that have been used in the simulation study include Albrct, GaNsat, Fermi, SRH, polarization, drift-diffusion, trap, INTTRAP, and Impact Selb. The Albrct model is used to take into account the mobility variation with doping concentration. GaNsat is a specific model for the nitride group. Additionally, the Fermi model is used for incorporating Fermi statistics to calculate the intrinsic concentration, and SRH (Shockley–Read–Hall) is used along with the polarization model to take into account the effect of polarization charges. The carrier transport in the channel is incorporated by using the drift-diffusion model. The trap and INTTRAP models are used to introduce the bulk trap and interface trap at the oxide/AlGaN interface. The Impact Selb model describes the impact ionization coefficient, important for simulation of breakdown voltage. Sign and Sigp are the carrier cross sections, taken as 1 × 10–15 cm2; the degeneracy factor is taken as Degen = 2 for both acceptor and donor trap; the concentrations of the acceptor and donor trap are, respectively, taken as 3.5 × 1016 cm–3 and 2.5 × 1014 cm–3, and their respective energy levels are 0.4 and 3 eV.

An important feature of the proposed device is the presence of charge plasma (CP)-based source and drain regions (S/D). This concept has been used in order to eliminate the traditional concept of doping, which involves complex processes such as ion implantation and diffusion. It has been observed that this concept has been previously used in silicon and III–V-based devices. ,− The CP-based S/D regions get created by placing metals of specific work functions on the semiconductor film, hence creating CP-based n-type or p-type source/drain regions. Effect of Interface Traps (I.T)

The group III–V nitrides experience a trap effect at various interfaces in the device. Here we are considering the effect of interface traps at the oxide/AlGaN layer. It has been observed that incorporating an interface trap degrades the device performance. The INTTRAP model has been used to incorporate the interface trap with the interface trap density (D it) taken as 1 × 1012. Figure a,b shows the impact of positive and negative interface traps on threshold voltage and transconductance, respectively. The presence of positive interface traps causes the device to turn ON at a lower value than intended because the presence of positive interface traps attracts electrons toward the interface, which enhances the electron density at the interface, leading to a reduction in the V th, whereas the negative interface traps repel the already present electrons at the interface, causing a more positive shift in V th. Impact of Using ALN Layer on Device Performance

AlN is an ultrawide band gap material (6.4 eV) with strong polarization properties. Incorporating an AlN layer into the proposed device improves the device performance. Figure shows the influence of AlN layer on the threshold voltage (V th), ON current (I ON), and transconductance (G m) of the device. It has been noticed that the AlN layer forms a 2DEG with a GaN buffer layer, which enhances the ON current. Due to the difference in band gaps of the AlN and GaN buffer layer, a large conduction band offset is created, which helps in achieving a more positive shift in the threshold voltage. Figure a shows the I D–V S–V GS curve with and without the AlN layer. The graph is obtained at V DS = 10 V and the V GS is ramped from −6 to 12 V. It can be seen that with the AlN layer the threshold voltage of the device improved from 1.1 to 3.5 V.

Conclusions

In this work, a novel double AlGaN-GaN E-mode high-electron-mobility transistor has been simulated because conventional AlGaN/GaN HEMT offers D-mode behavior, which is not desired for power-switching application. The proposed device shows E-mode behavior with the use of a p-GaN-doped pocket buried in the GaN channel region. The presence of p-GaN creates a good conduction barrier height, which is further reduced by applying an appropriate gate bias and thus turns ON the device. Further, the proposed device uses electron plasma to create the source and drain regions by employing a metal electrode with a particular work function. The presence of 2DEG at the interface of the top AlGaN-bottom AlGaN layer and bottom AlGaN-GaN channel layer in addition to CPEG outshines the performance of the proposed device. All of the parameters, ON current, threshold voltage, transconductance, breakdown voltage, etc., have been simulated and showed significant improvement compared to their counterparts.

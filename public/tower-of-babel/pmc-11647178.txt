Safety and efficacy of a feed additive consisting of the bacteriophages PCM F/00069, PCM F/00070, PCM F/00071 and PCM F/00097 (Bafasal®) for all poultry (Proteon Pharmaceuticals S.A.)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Ilen Röhe, Katerina Theodoridou, Vasileios Bampidis, Pier Sandro Cocconcelli, Lieve Herman, Montserrat Anguita, Jaume Galobart, Matteo L Innocenti, Jordi Ortuño, Rosella Brozzi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of a product consisting of four bacteriophages infecting Salmonella enterica ser. Gallinarum B/00111, intended for be as a zootechnical additive (functional group: other zootechnical additives) for all poultry species. Bafasal® is proposed for use in water for drinking and liquid complementary feed to guarantee a minimum daily dose of 2 × 106 PFU/bird, to reduce the Salmonella spp. contamination of poultry carcasses and load in the environment, and to improve the zootechnical performance of the treated animals. In previous opinions, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that Bafasal® is safe for the target species, consumers and the environment and is not a skin or eye irritant but should be considered a respiratory sensitiser. No conclusion could be drawn on its dermal sensitisation potential. The additive showed the potential to decrease the counts of two Salmonella Enteritidis strains in boots swabs and caecal digesta of chickens for fattening. However, the Panel was not in the position to conclude on the capacity of Bafasal® to improve the zootechnical performance of poultry, to reduce the contamination of other Salmonella enterica strains, serovars or other Salmonella spp. in chickens, or to reduce the Salmonella spp. contamination of poultry other than chickens. In the current application, the claim on zootechnical performance has been withdrawn. Bafasal® is not a skin or eye irritant but should be considered a potential skin and respiratory sensitiser and inhalation and dermal exposure is considered a risk. Based on the new data provided, the Panel concluded that Bafasal® has the potential to reduce the environmental contamination with S. Enteritidis when used in feed and water for all poultry species. No conclusions could be drawn on the effects of the additive on other Salmonella serotypes.Keywords: zootechnical additivesall poultry species, bacteriophages, Bafasal®, efficacy, other zootechnical additives, safety

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of a feed additive consisting of the bacteriophages PCM F/00069, PCM F/00070, PCM F/00071 and PCM F/00097 (Bafasal®) is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 4 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the users (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023a) and Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018).

ASSESSMENT

The additive under assessment is composed of four bacteriophages (PCM F/00069, PCM F/00070, PCM F/00071 and PCM F/00097) infecting Salmonella enterica ser. Gallinarum B/00111. It will be hereafter referred to with its tradename Bafasal®.

Bafasal® is intended to be used as a zootechnical additive (functional group: other zootechnical additives: animal welfare and food hygiene) in feed for all poultry 5 to reduce the Salmonella spp. contamination in chickens for fattening and in the environment. It is proposed to be administered in water for drinking and liquid complementary feed to guarantee a minimum daily dose of 2 × 106 plaque forming units (PFU)/bird, equivalent to 0.04 mL additive/bird per day.

In the previous opinions (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2021, 2023b), the FEEDAP Panel assessed the safety and the efficacy of Bafasal® when used in all avian species. From the information/data provided, the Panel concluded that the additive is safe for the target species, consumers and the environment. The Panel also concluded that Bafasal® is not a skin or eye irritant but should be considered a potential respiratory sensitiser, while no conclusion could be drawn on its potential to be a dermal sensitiser. The additive showed the potential to decrease the counts of two strains of Salmonella Enteritidis in boots swabs and caecal digesta of chickens for fattening. However, the Panel was not in the position to conclude on the efficacy of Bafasal® to improve the zootechnical performance of the target species based on the available data and considered that the potential of Bafasal® to reduce the Salmonella spp. contamination of poultry carcasses and/or the environment was limited.

The applicant has provided new data to complement the information supporting the efficacy of the additive.

Safety for the user

In a previous opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023b) the Panel did not conclude on the potential of the additive to be a skin sensitiser. No additional data were submitted in the current application. Bafasal® should be considered as a potential skin and respiratory sensitiser and inhalation and dermal exposure is considered a risk (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023a).

Moreover, and to determine the host range of the Bafasal® bacteriophages, the applicant conducted spot tests with a collection of strains representing several serovars, claimed to be of different origins.

Enteritidis strains in the caecal digesta of chickens for fattening could lead to a reduction of the loading in the environment, the load of Salmonella in the caecum of poultry is only one of the multiple factors that contribute to the Salmonella contamination of the carcasses.

Two host range studies (one in vitro and one in vivo) and two in vivo trials in hens were submitted and are described below.

Host range studies

In vitro study

A host range experiment based on spot test and spectrophotometric assay was submitted. 7 A total of 72 S. enterica strains isolated from poultry were included in the study. As control of viability and lytic activity of the phage preparation, the strain 8 used for the phage multiplication was included. The genomes of the strains were sequenced and the whole genome sequence data were used for their characterisation. Average nucleotide identity was used for taxonomic identification and for determination of relatedness between strains, comparing each strain with the others. Multilocus sequence typing (cgMLST) based on 3002 loci was applied for Salmonella strain genotyping. The genomes were interrogated for the presence of antimicrobial resistance genes and virulence factors. The susceptibility to Bafasal® was determined by the serial dilution spot test and by spectrophotometric analysis of kinetics of bacterial growth. The correlation between both tests was calculated and presented as the combined lytic score.

From the 55 strains of serovar S. Enteritidis belonging to 37 cgMLST types, only 3 strains belonging to the same cgMLST type showed no or very limited susceptibility to the Bafasal® phages. The remaining 52 strains were inhibited. Susceptibility was also shown by the single tested strain of each of the S. Hadar, S. Brandenburg and S. Paratyphi serovars. Seven strains of serovar S. Typhimurium belonging to six cgMLST types were also tested. Five of them were not susceptible, while the other two were inhibited by Bafasal®. Also, no susceptibility was shown by three strains of serovar S. lnfantis belonging to three cgMLST types and three strains of serovar S. Virchow belonging to two cgMLST types.

The results of the study confirm previous findings that the vast majority of the S. Enteritidis strains are susceptible to Bafasal®. On the other hand, although a lower number of S. Typhimurium strains were tested, the data suggest that they are much less susceptible to the bacteriophages of the additive. No conclusion can be drawn on the other Salmonella serotypes due to the small number of strains tested.

T4: infected with S. Enteritidis Fenna ‘K’ K‐11, not treated;

T5: infected with S. Enteritidis 838/S/09 B and treated with Bafasal® at 6–10 × 108 PFU/L; 10

T5: infected with S. Enteritidis 838/S/09 B and treated with Bafasal® at 6–10 × 108 PFU/L; 10

T6: infected with S. Enteritidis 012PP2014 and treated with Bafasal® at 7–10 × 108 PFU/L; 11

T6: infected with S. Enteritidis 012PP2014 and treated with Bafasal® at 7–10 × 108 PFU/L; 11

Mice received compound feed and water ad libitum for 10 days. Mice belonging to treatment groups T2–T7 were infected with Salmonella strains (1 × 108 CFU/mouse) on day 4 through an oral gavage, and mice from the control group (T1) were given saline solution. The additive was supplemented via water and doses were confirmed by analysis on days 4 and 9. No bacteriophages were found in the water given to the control groups. The presence and enumeration of Salmonella spp. in the intestine was evaluated on day 10.

Two of the S. Enteritidis strains used in this in vivo experiment, 838/S/09 B and 012PP2014, proved susceptible to the Bafasal® phages in the in vitro tests (Section 3.2.1.1), while the third, Fenna ‘K’ strain K‐11, showed limited susceptibility.

A limited effect of the additive on the reduction of Salmonella counts in the intestine of mice treated with the additive was found. In mice infected with S. Enteritidis 838/S/09 B, no statistically significant difference was observed between mice in the control group and those in the group supplemented with Bafasal® (T2: 7.15 (0.75) vs. T5: 7.59 (0.80) log10 Most Probable Number (MPN)/g). In the group with animals challenged with S. Enteritidis 012PP2014 and Fenna ‘K’ strain K‐11, a statistically significant difference (p < 0.05) of the Salmonella counts was observed between the control and treated groups (012PP2014: (T3: 7.40 (0.79) vs. T6: 6.35 (0.36) log10 MPN/g) and K‐11: (T4: 7.92 (0.26) vs. T7: 6.98 (0.56) log10 MPN/g)). However, in both cases, the reduction was limited to a single logarithmic order. In addition, since the mouse is not the intended target species, the study is considered of limited value.

Conclusions on host range studies

The data provided confirm previous findings that Bafasal® is efficacious in inhibiting S. Enteritidis strains, but no conclusions can be drawn on the effects of the additive on other Salmonella serotypes.

In vivo trials with hens

Laying hens

A total of 216 Babolna Tetra XL hens (23‐weeks of age) were distributed in 18 cages with 12 animals in each cage, randomly allocated to 3 experimental groups, with 6 replicates per group, depending on the infection of the birds with S. Enteritidis (strain 012PP2014) and the supplementation of water with Bafasal®. 13 The 3 experimental groups were:

Uninfected Untreated Control (UUC) group;

Uninfected Untreated Control (UUC) group;

Infected Untreated Control (IUC) group and

Infected Untreated Control (IUC) group and

Infected Treated (IT) group.

Infected Treated (IT) group.

A basal feed based on maize, wheat and soybean meal was provided in mash form to all the hens for 91 days. Feed and water were offered ad libitum during the whole experiment. Laying hens belonging to groups IUC and IT were challenged with S. Enteritidis for 3 consecutive days starting at day 16 and laying hens from group UUC were given saline solution. The supplementation of Bafasal® to birds of group IT via water was performed, starting from day 7 of the experiment, according to the following schedule: 2 × 106 PFU/bird/day for 3 weeks (on days: 7–27, every other day) and 49–69 (every other day). The water was not supplemented on days 1–6, 28–48 and on days 70–91 of the experiment. The intended concentration was reached by diluting 50 mL of Bafasal® in a fixed amount of fresh water. After full consumption, plain water was supplied. The inclusion levels in water were monitored and the presence of Bafasal® was confirmed by analysis at different timepoints throughout the trial. 14

To investigate the potential Salmonella spp. background contamination, samples of boot swabs, feed and water were collected per group before allocation of birds. The health of the animals was monitored daily, and the deaths and culled animals recorded, including the most likely cause of death or reason for culling. Animals were necropsied when considered relevant to elucidate the cause of death. The zootechnical performance of the hens, including body weight, feed and water intake, laying rate, egg weight was monitored throughout the trial. The egg mass, average daily feed/water intake and the feed‐to‐egg mass ratio were calculated for the whole period (1–91 days).

Boot swabs samples were collected from all replicates per group on days 16 (before the challenge with Salmonella), 22, 27, 48, 69 and 91. The samples taken at day 16 from all replicates per group and at days 22, 27, 48, 69 and 91 from the replicates corresponding to the UUC group were analysed for the presence of Salmonella spp. A miniaturised most probable number (MPN) method was used for the enumeration of Salmonella spp. in boot swabs from groups IUC and IT taken after the Salmonella spp. challenge and the identification of the presumptive Salmonella spp. positive colonies was confirmed by polymerase chain reaction (PCR). Additionally, samples of eggs (albumen and yolk) and swabs of the eggshell from the same groups collected on days 22, 27, 48, 69 and 91 (three per replicate on day 22 and two per replicate at other time points) were plated on XLD medium, incubated at 37°C for 24 h to test for Salmonella spp. and S. Enteritidis presence (colonies confirmed by PCR). 15

The results (except the egg contamination) were analysed with one‐way ANOVA, with the cage as experimental unit and the diet as fixed effect. When relevant, group means were compared with Tukey's test. The significance level was set at 0.05.

Salmonella spp. was not detected in any of the 18 boot swabs samples (day 16 of the trial) analysed before the challenge or in the samples taken from the UUC group during the trial.

Breeding hens

A total of 216 Ross 308 hens (22‐week‐old) and roosters (22‐week‐old) were distributed in 18 floor‐pens with 12 hens and 2 roosters in each one, randomly allocated to three experimental groups depending on the infection of the birds with the same S. Enteritidis strain used in the previous trial and the supplementation of water with Bafasal®. 16 Based on this, the three experimental groups were: uninfected untreated control group; infected untreated control group and infected treated group.

A basal feed based on maize, wheat and soybean meal was provided in mash form to the hens for 91 days. Feed was given on a restricted basis to the animals (140 g/bird and day) and water was offered ad libitum during the whole experiment. Breeder hens belonging to groups IUC and IT were challenged with S. Enteritidis for 3 consecutive days starting at day 16 and laying hens from group UUC were given saline solution. The supplementation of Bafasal® to birds via water was performed according to the following schedule: 2 × 106 PFU/bird/day for 3 weeks (on days: 7–27, every other day) and during days 49–69 (every other day). The water was not supplemented on days 1–6, 28–48 and 70–91 of the experiment. The intended concentration was reached by diluting 50 mL of Bafasal® in a fixed amount of fresh water. After full consumption, plain water was supplied. The inclusion levels in water were monitored and the presence of Bafasal® was confirmed by analysis at different timepoints throughout the trial. 17

To investigate the potential Salmonella spp. background contamination, samples of boot swabs, feed and water were collected per group before allocation of birds. The health of the animals was daily monitored, and the deaths and culled animals recorded, including the most likely cause of death or reason for culling. Animals were necropsied when considered relevant to elucidate the cause of death. The zootechnical performance of the hens, including body weight, feed and water intake, laying rate and egg weight was monitored throughout the trial. The egg mass, average daily feed/water intake and the feed‐to‐egg mass ratio were calculated for the whole period (1–91 days). The fertility, hatchability and the chick's viability were also recorded.

Salmonella spp. was not detected in any boot swab sample taken from the UUC group on any day, except for three replicates at day 69 (confirmed for S. Enteritidis).

Regarding the caecal samples from 1‐day‐old chicks, 6 and 2 out of 10 in group IUC, and 2 and 0 out of 10 in group IT for days 69 and 111, respectively, resulted positive to S. Enteritidis. However, the Panel notes that the number of samples is considered limited, and except for the chicks' body weight, there was no information regarding the criteria used for selecting the samples from the different groups, so it is unclear from which replicates (pens) the chicks' caeca samples were taken from. In addition, no information was given on the management of the eggs. Therefore, the Panel considers that the data provided in this study do not allow to draw any conclusion regarding the vertical transmission of Salmonella in hens.

The challenge with Salmonella spp. resulted in a higher mortality and worsening of the performance in terms of final body weight, feed and water intake, laying rate, egg mass and feed‐to‐egg mass ratio in the IUC and IT groups in comparison to the UUC group. The hen's fertility and chick's hatchability were also affected, while no effects of the challenge were observed in chick's viability. The adverse effects observed in the laying and reproductive performance were partially mitigated by the water supplementation with Bafasal® (data not shown).

The Bafasal® supplementation resulted in a significant reduction of the S. Enteritidis counts in boot swabs during the 91 days of the experiment.

Conclusion on the efficacy in poultry

In the previous opinion, the Panel concluded that the additive showed potential to decrease the counts of two strains of S. Enteritidis 18 from chickens for fattening measured in boot swabs. The two additional trials in laying/breeding hens showed a reduction in the counts of one strain of S. Enteritidis (strain 012PP2014) in boot swabs resulting from the supplementation of Bafasal® in water. Considering all the data, the FEEDAP Panel concludes that Bafasal® has the potential to reduce the environmental contamination with S. Enteritidis when used in water for drinking or liquid complementary feed for chickens or hens. The Panel considers that the effect of the phages preparation is mainly related to the specificity of the phages to the bacterial strain, and is less influenced by the conditions of the gastrointestinal tract of the different poultry species. Therefore, the effects of the additive on the capacity to reduce the S. Enteritidis load in the environment observed in the trials with chickens and hens can be extrapolated to all poultry.

CONCLUSIONS

Bafasal® is not a skin or eye irritant but should be considered a potential skin and respiratory sensitiser and inhalation and dermal exposure is considered a risk.

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that Bafasal® has the potential to be efficacious as a zootechnical additive (other zootechnical additives) in reducing the environmental contamination with S. Enteritidis when used in water for drinking or liquid complementary feed for all poultry species. No conclusions can be drawn on the effects of the additive on other Salmonella serotypes.

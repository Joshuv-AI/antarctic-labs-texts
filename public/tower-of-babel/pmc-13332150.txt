An effective two-stage key frame extraction method for speech-visual emotion recognition
Authors: Yuanhui Liang, Xiaojing Li, Lei Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSpeech-visual emotion recognition plays a vital role in human–computer interaction applications. However, it typically confronts several challenges, such as: (1) conventional speech-visual key frame (SVKF) extraction methods are susceptible to redundancy and emotional information loss; (2) widely adopted attention-based speech-visual feature fusion approaches often compute weights with limited interpretability. To address these challenges, this paper proposes an effective two-stage key frame extraction method for speech-visual emotion recognition. Specifically, in the first stage, visual key frames (VKFs) are extracted by employing information entropy (IE) to model the continuous process of emotion generation, thereby decreasing visual frame redundancy. Corresponding speech key frames (SKFs) are obtained simultaneously by eliminating silent segments to reduce redundancy in the speech modality. Subsequently, by leveraging the complementarity characteristics of speech and visual modalities, the first-stage SKFs and VKFs are aligned to produce the ultimate second-stage SVKFs for preserving important emotional information, in which a simple and interpretable weighted fusion is also proposed to focus on processing important emotional information. The experimental results on the RML, eNTERFACE05, MEAD and BAUM-1s datasets demonstrate that the proposed effective two-stage key frame extraction has better inference and generalization performance.Keywords: Emotion recognition, Speech modality, Visual modality, Key framesSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Mathematics and computing, Neuroscience

Introduction

The purpose of speech-visual emotion recognition is to identify human emotions by utilizing some intelligent devices1,2. As the core component of human-computer interaction systems3,4, speech-visual emotion recognition has significant potential to be applied to intelligent driving5, smart home6, health detection7, smart education8, and so on. Despite the great application promise of speech-visual emotion recognition, it is still a challenge to recognize emotions effectively9. Especially against the backdrop of the rapid proliferation of various social media application platforms, which have generated vast amounts of emotional videos10, the quality of speech-visual key frames (SVKFs) extracted from these large-scale video datasets directly influences the performance of emotion recognition systems. Therefore, how to effectively extract high-quality SVKFs from massive emotional video data has become a critical research priority.

On the other hand, the effective fusion of speech-visual features constitutes another crucial task in speech-visual emotion recognition11,12. Attention-based feature fusion methods have been widely adopted in this field, as they integrate speech and visual features through an adaptive weighting mechanism13–15. However, such methods generally require incorporating additional neural network layers to learn the attention weights, which can inadvertently increase model complexity. Moreover, since these attention weights are implicitly derived by training the appended networks on large-scale datasets, the resulting fusion process often suffers from limited interpretability16.

Based on the motivations outlined above, this paper is driven by two primary objectives. The first is to efficiently extract SVKFs that exhibit low redundancy while preserving important emotional information. The second is to enhance the interpretability of the weighted fusion mechanism for speech and visual features. To achieve these goals, this paper proposes an effective two-stage key frame extraction method for speech-visual emotion recognition. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

Related work

The topic of this paper is relative to the SVKE extraction and the speech-visual feature fusion. In the following sections, the related work on these two topics is described in detail.

SVKF extraction

Several SVKF extraction algorithms have been investigated for speech-visual emotion recognition. In particular, in one approach, emotional videos were separated into speech and visual information, where the visual frames with the smallest chi-square distance of seven adjacent frames for the visual information were used as the VKFs17. A limitation of this method, however, is that the speech information remained unprocessed, leading to redundancy in the extracted speech key frames (SKFs). Similarly, another study divided the speech information into silent and speech segments, treating the latter as SKFs, while the corresponding visual frames aligned with these SKFs were regarded as VKFs18. Although this strategy reduces the redundancy of SVKFs, it will lose some important emotional information. For example, in one extreme case, people are particularly surprised and may be unable to speak, i.e., corresponding speech information is the silent segments, while people’s facial expressions provide more emotional information. In this case, the corresponding speech-visual information is inevitably excluded, since the corresponding speech information is the silent segments. This causes the loss of important emotional information, as facial expressions that provide more emotional information are discarded. In another work, VKFs were obtained using fixed frame intervals for the visual information, while SKFs were derived by applying endpoint detection to segment the speech information into equidistant segments19. Other methods involved directly splitting emotional videos into speech-visual frame pairs, which were then fed into multimodal feature extraction networks14,20–23. It is remarkable that SVKFs extracted by the above methods generally suffer from key frame redundancy with missing important emotional information. For this reason, this paper proposed an effective two-stage key frame extraction method for speech-visual emotion recognition. It first acquires the first-stage SKFs and VKFs to reduce redundancy, and then saves the important emotional information by utilizing the complementarity characteristics of speech-visual modalities to align the first-stage SKFs and VKFs.

Speech-visual feature fusion

Unimodal speech or visual emotion recognition provides insufficient emotional information, which is a major limitation for emotion recognition22–26. On the other hand, different emotion modalities generally contain different dimensions of emotional information, in which these modalities can complement each other to provide a more comprehensive representation information27. Consequently, the fusion of speech-visual features is crucial for improving the performance of speech-visual emotion recognition. It is currently more popular to use different neural networks to fuse the extracted speech-visual features11. Typically, speech and visual features have been extracted using two-dimensional convolutional neural networks (2D-CNNs)28 and three-dimensional CNNs (3D-CNNs)29, respectively, and subsequently fused via a deep belief network (DBN)12. Likewise, multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) have also been employed to integrate speech-visual features30. In addition, the concatenation operator31,32 has been frequently used to fuse speech-visual features. Building on this, a novel speech-visual loss function (NSVLF)33 has been designed to enhance the robustness of feature fusion. Based on speech-visual correlations, a multi-stage adaptive feature fusion (MSAFF) method34 has been proposed to dynamically adjust speech-visual informatiion. Since the attention mechanism enables adaptive weighting of different modalities, it has also been applied to the fusion of speech-visual features. An importance attention and a complementary attention were designed to achieve the weighting of different emotion modalities13. Further extending this line of work, an adaptive attention mechanism was proposed to dynamically weight speech-visual modalities14. Although these weighting methods based on the attention mechanism could improve the performance of speech-visual emotion recognition, their attention weights need to be learned by adding neural network layers. This potentially introduces additional complexity. Further, the attention weights have relatively low interpretability, as it is learned implicitly by training additional neural network layers on a large amount of data16. To this end, this paper proposes a simple and interpretable weighted fusion method to dynamically weight the extracted speech-visual features, in which the weights are explicitly computed by the proposed effective two-stage key frame extraction based on the amount of important emotional information.

Experimental results and analysis

Datasets and experimental setup

The RML40, eNTERFACE0541, MEAD42 and BAUM-1s43 datasets are selected to evaluate the performance of the proposed method, in which the six basic discrete emotions are identified, including anger, disgust, fear, sadness, surprise and happy. The number of samples for each emotion on these four datasets is summarized in Table 1. Each emotional video in the four datasets is divided into multiple segments containing speech and visual modalities, where the label of the emotional video is viewed as the label of the multiple segments12. These emotional video segments with speech-visual information are fed into the proposed effective two-stage key frame extraction to obtain the second-stage SVKFs , and interpretable speech-visual weights , . This paper selects the wav2vec 2.0 model44 as the speech feature extraction network of . This is because the wav2vec 2.0 model is a transformer-based pre-trained model for self-supervised learning from numerous speech data, where it achieves better performance in speech emotion recognition. Meanwhile, this paper also selects the pre-trained spatio-temporal transformer (STT)45 as the visual feature extraction network of , since it is able to extract the spatio-temporal features of visual modality by incorporating both spatial and temporal attention. Meanwhile, a fully connected layer with 1024 neurons is added before the classification layers of the wav2vec 2.0 model and the pre-trained STT to guarantee speech-visual features with fixed dimensions46, i.e., , respectively. The speech-visual features and are fused by the proposed simple and interpretable weighted fusion, i.e., Eq.(eq16), where the fused features are fed into a three-layer MLP with the Softmax function to achieve speech-visual emotion classification. The PyTorch with the Adam optimizer is applied, where the learning rate and batch size are set to 0.001 and 64, respectively.

Conclusion

This paper proposes the effective two-stage key frame extraction method for speech-visual emotion recognition. It obtains the first-stage SKFs to reduce the redundancy of speech key frames, and also extracts the first-stage VKFs to reduce the redundancy of visual key frames. By leveraging the complementarity characteristics between speech and visual modalities, the second-stage SVKFs are obtained by aligning the first-stage SKFs and VKFs, which effectively saves important emotional information. In addition, to focus on processing important emotional information, the feature layer fusion adopts the simple and interpretable weighted fusion, which also effectively improves the performance of speech-visual emotion recognition.

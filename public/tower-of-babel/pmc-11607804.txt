Vertebroplasty for painful osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures: a protocol for a single-center doubled-blind randomized sham-controlled clinical trial. VOPE2

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOne in three women and one in five men over the age of 50 will experience an osteoporotic fracture. Vertebral fractures can be very painful, affect patients’ daily function, and in severe cases require hospitalization. Traditionally, fracture pain is treated conservatively with analgesics, and bracing. Vertebral augmentation, also known as vertebroplasty, has been used during the last three decades as a minimally invasive treatment option for vertebral compression fractures, but the evidence base for its efficacy is weak. We describe a double-blind randomized sham-controlled clinical trial to assess the impact of vertebroplasty on self-reported clinical outcomes in patients with painful osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures and vertebral oedema.MethodsTwo hundred and forty patients with painful osteoporotic vertebral fractures and MRI verified oedema will be randomized in a prospective, double-blind, single-center, clinical trial to either vertebroplasty or a sham procedure, with the possibility of crossover 12 weeks after randomization and operation. The primary outcome will be difference in self-reported pain 12 weeks after treatment between the vertebroplasty and sham group. Secondary outcomes will be patient-reported disability, health-related quality of life, societal costs of treatment and complications. Analysis will be based on intention-to-treat. Repeated measures ANCOVA with baseline ODI, Numerical Pain Rating Scale, EQ-5D-5 L, and number of levels involved as co-variates will be performed.DiscussionWith an aging population, the prevalence of osteoporosis and related complications such as vertebral compression fractures is expected to increase. Therefore, there is a growing need for evidence-based fracture treatments. Introduction

Background and rationale

Osteoporosis and associated fractures are important health issues in an aging population, with one in three women and one in five men over 50 experiencing an osteoporotic fracture. Osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCFs) are among the most common manifestations of osteoporosis [1, 2]. Decreased mobility and pain associated with OVCFs can lead to deep venous thrombosis, acceleration of osteoporosis, increased risk of future vertebral fractures, loss of height and respiratory problems [3].

OVCFs may occur spontaneously during daily activities such as picking up an object or as a result of a minor trauma [4]. Some OVCFs are asymptomatic, others can be managed using over-the-counter medication and advice, while still others cause severe and intense pain preventing ordinary daily activities and requiring opioids. Multiple OVCFs sometimes result in severe kyphosis of the thoracic and lumbar spine, in which the patient’s rib cage collides with the iliac crests, leading to pain and a reduction in the abdominal space, giving rise to early satiety and weight loss. Reduction of the thoracic volume also results in decreased mobility due to decreased pulmonary vital capacity [5, 6].

Medical management is generally the first-line approach in treating osteoporotic vertebral fractures, including pharmacological therapies like denosumab, zoledronate, and pamidronate, these options are vital for stabilizing bone mineral density and reducing fracture risk [7–11]. Evidence suggests that drugs such as denosumab and romosozumab can be particularly effective in reducing fracture risk across various fracture sites [11]. Early diagnosis and a coordinated interdisciplinary approach can further enhance treatment outcomes for patients at high fracture risk, underscoring the importance management planning in this population [7].

In cases with intense or prolonged pain, or when conservative treatment is not recommended, percutaneous vertebroplasty (PVP) is an option. PVP is a minimally invasive surgical procedure in which bone cement is injected into the fractured vertebral body under fluoroscopic guidance aiming to provide stability and relieve pain. PVP can reduce the risk of additional compression of the fractured vertebral body, and helps maintain the patient’s sagittal balance [12]. The technique of PVP was initially developed in France to treat osteolytic metastases, myeloma, and hemangioma [13, 14]. Indications were later expanded to include painful OVCFs.

Previous studies on the effect of PVP

In cohort studies involving over 200,000 patients, a decrease in pain and an improvement in quality of life following PVP in patients with OVCF have been documented [15–20]. However, in randomized clinical trials comparing PVP to sham procedures, the findings have been contradictory. In 2009, two double-blinded randomized studies comparing PVP to sham procedures were published, reporting no significant difference in pain levels after 1 and 3 months [21, 22]. Similarly, a double-blinded randomized study published in 2017 [23] involving 52 patients, as well as a study with 180 patients [24], found no clinically significant difference in patient-reported pain. In contrast, Clark et al. [25] reported notable reductions in pain in the PVP-treated group compared to those randomized to the sham group. A Cochrane review published in 2018, based on these five RCTs, concluded that PVP is not recommended due to a lack of demonstrable clinically important benefits compared to sham procedures [26].

Rationale for the study

To address the knowledge gap regarding the relevance of vertebroplasty in treating osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures, we have opted to undertake a double-blind, randomized, controlled trial comparing vertebroplasty with sham surgery for osteoporotic patients with vertebral compression fractures. This decision arises from the scarcity of RCTs available, which exhibit varied inclusion criteria and inconsistent findings. Notably, there are discrepancies in the duration and intensity of pain, as well as the utilization of MRI scans across these studies. Additionally, there is significant variability in the enrollment of eligible patients. These factors underscore the necessity for further investigations with precisely defined inclusion criteria that closely mirror real-world clinical scenarios, thus encompassing the majority of patients experiencing significant pain and MRI-confirmed edema. The presence of edema on MRI is crucial because it indicates ongoing activity of the vertebral compression fracture.

Conducting such a study would contribute significantly to understanding the role of vertebroplasty in the treatment algorithm for painful osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures.

Objectives

The aim of this project is to assess the pain-relieving impact of percutaneous vertebroplasty in comparison with a sham procedure in patients experiencing severe pain due to an osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture with confirmed vertebral edema on MRI.

Secondary objectives are to investigate differences in patient reported disabilities, health-related quality of life, complications with treatment and health related costs.

Trial design

The study design is a prospective, double-blinded, randomized, sham-controlled clinical trial where patients stratified by either involvement of 1–2 vertebral levels or 3–4 levels and randomized 1:1 to either vertebroplasty or a sham operation. Cross-overs are not allowed within the first 12 weeks.

Methods: participants, interventions and outcomes

The trial will be performed as a single-center study at the Center for Spine Surgery and Research, located at Lillebaelt Hospital in Kolding, Denmark. Patient evaluations will take place at the hospital’s outpatient clinic, with MRI scans and X-ray images conducted by the hospital’s radiology department.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators

A sham procedure has been chosen to conceal the actual intervention from the patient, aiming to eliminate the placebo effect associated with the surgical trauma.

Intervention description

Percutaneous vertebroplasty

The patient will be carefully positioned in a prone manner on the procedure table, with an oxygen mask being applied. Following the administration of conscious sedation, local anesthetic will be infiltrated into the skin at the fracture site, and stab incisions will be made to facilitate the insertion of the PVP needles.

The periosteum of the pedicle will be infused with a local anesthetic, and an 11/13 gauge PVP needle will be inserted into the vertebral body using either a uni- or bipedicular technique, guided by fluoroscopy. Polymethyl-methacrylate (PMMA) cement will be prepared and slowly injected into the vertebral body under continuous bi-plane fluoroscopy to prevent cement leakage. Injection will stop once the vertebral body is adequately filled or if there is any leakage of cement into nearby structures. The stab incisions will be closed with adhesive strips. After PMMA injection and removal of the cannulas, patients will remain in the prone position on the operating room table for an additional 20 min to allow the PMMA to solidify before being transferred back to the ward.

Sham intervention

The sham procedure is designed to simulate the patient experience of a PVP. The patient will be carefully positioned in a prone manner on the procedure table, with an oxygen mask being applied. Following the administration of conscious sedation, local anesthetic will be infiltrated into the skin at the fracture site, and stab incisions will be made to facilitate the insertion of the PVP needles.

A short needle is passed through the skin, but not as far as the periosteum. The periosteum is not infiltrated with local anesthesia. PMMA cement will be prepared and manual skin pressure is applied to mimic the PVP procedure. The stab incisions will be closed with adhesive strips. After removal of the cannulas, patients will remain in the prone position on the operating room table for an additional 20 min before being transferred back to the ward.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions

During the initial 12 weeks, patients and health care professionals will remain blinded and it is not possible to change the assigned intervention. If a significant complication arises during the PVP procedure, such as the emergence of neurological impairment due to PMMA displacement into the spinal canal necessitating spinal decompression, the patient will remain in the trial, and data will be collected as planned. However, due to the nature of the complication, the patient will be un-blinded.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions

Upon discharge, each participant will be provided with a diary to document self-reported outcome measures. A blinded research nurse will make telephone contact with the patient on days 1, 2, 3, 5, as well as 2, 4, and 6 weeks post-discharge to serve as reminders for diary completion. At 12 and 52 weeks, patients will be seen in the outpatient ward, where the research nurse will oversee data collection.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial

There are no limitations on the type of treatment the patients are permitted to receive, except for those in the sham group, who are prohibited from seeking PVP treatment in a private hospital.

Provisions for post-trial care

Following the procedure, patients in both treatment groups are observed in the supine position for a duration of 2 h. They will be discharged from the hospital on the day of the procedure or the following day. Additionally, all trial participants, regardless of their assigned treatment arm, will undergo medical management for osteoporosis based on a standardized protocol consistent with Danish national guidelines [28].

In the event of any adverse events, patients participating in the trial have the option as all other patients treated at our facility, to seek compensation through the Danish Patient Compensation system.

Secondary outcomes

Patient-reported back-related disability.

The patients rate their back-related disability at the time of enrolment and at 4 and 12 weeks, 12 and 24 months post-randomization using the Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) [30, 31] questionnaire. ODI is designed to assess disability caused by low back pain. The score for each of ten items on e.g. pain, personal care, walking distance, ability to sit/stand and quality of sleep are rated from 0 to 5. A sum score below 20 indicates little or no disability due to back pain. A sum score above 80 indicates severe disability from back pain.

Health-related quality of life (HRQoL).

HRQoL is measured using the EuroQol 5-dimension 5-level (EQ-5D-5 L) [32, 33]. EQ-5D-5 L is a widely used generic measure of HRQoL. It evaluates five dimensions: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression, each with three levels of severity. The resulting health is converted into a single summary index with a total score ranging from − 0.6 to 1, where 1 corresponds to perfect health. To calculate Quality Adjusted Life Year (QALY) from the EQ-5D, scores will be weighted over time to estimate the total QALY from baseline to two-year follow up.

Sample size

Within the parameters of the current study, a sample size of at least 200 (50 in each group) is considered sufficient to detect meaningful differences in the primary outcome, low back pain, between the four cohorts (1–2 L: PVP, 3–4 L: PVP, 1–2 L: Sham and 3–4 L: Sham) with 80% power to detect a difference of 20 points on the visual analog scale. This calculation assumes a standard deviation of 30 points and a two-sided alpha of 0.05. Accounting for an expected 20% “loss to follow-up,” a total of 60 participants are needed in each group.

Recruitment

Study subjects will be recruited from patients diagnosed with painful x-ray verified vertebral compression fractures in the Region of Southern Denmark. The patients will be identified and referred to the trial site by the patients’ general practitioner, chiropractor, physiotherapist or from hospital inpatient and emergency departments in the region of Southern Denmark. Throughout the trial period, vertebroplasty will not be administered to patients with osteoporotic vertebral fractures unless within the specified protocol in the region of Southern Denmark.

We will inform healthcare professionals in the Southern Denmark region, including general practitioners, chiropractors, physiotherapists, as well as hospital inpatient and emergency department staff, about the opportunity to refer patients with vertebral compression fractures due to osteoporosis to Spine Centre of Southern Denmark for potential participation in a vertebroplasty trial.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation

Subjects will be stratified into two groups based on the number of OVCFs into 1–2 levels or 3–4 levels. Within each of these two groups, randomization sheets in varying blocks of 8 with an equal number of PVP and sham patients in each block will be generated using www.randomizer.org and will be placed in numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes.

Concealment mechanism

The sealed and opaque envelopes will be brought into the operating theater where a scrub nurse will select one of the sealed, opaque envelopes in the surgical theatre after the patient is draped but before the skin incision is made.

Throughout the trial, only the surgeon and the OR-nurse will have knowledge of the treatment assignment. The patient and all assessors will remain blinded to the treatment group throughout the study.

Implementation

Staff from the research secretariat at the Spine Centre of Southern Denmark created the allocation sequence. The research nurse from them patient ward will conduct participant enrollment, while the assignment of the intervention will be carried out by the scrub nurse in the operating theater.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded

The patient, research nurse and all outcome assessors remain blinded to the treatment group throughout the study. If patients contact the hospital after surgery, surgeon and nurses not involved in the treatment arm of the study will perform the consultation while blinded on the allocated treatment.

Procedure for unblinding if needed

In instances of newly diagnosed spine-related diseases, such as suspected spine metastasis or spinal infection, where treatment necessitates spine visualization, unblinding the patient may be relevant. The treating physician can obtain information about the assigned allocation by reaching out to the staff from the research secretariat at the Spine Centre of Southern Denmark. Contact details are accessible in the patient’s electronic medical record.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes

Two databases, DaneSpine, the National Spine Surgical Database, and a project database, are utilized to gather data for the trial. In both databases, it will not be possible to see group allocation for the duration of the study. The questionnaires employed in this study adhere to the guidelines of ICHOM (International Consortium for Health Outcome Measurement) for the surgical evaluation of spinal diseases. All questionnaires have been translated and previously validated into Danish.

Following the acquisition of informed consent from the patient, the research nurse enters screening information regarding the number and level of fractures into the Project database. The patient independently completes electronic surveys on pain, ODI, and HRQoL in the DaneSpine database.

During the hospitalization period, perioperative data, including operation time, theater time, and complications, are entered into DaneSpine by the operating room staff either during or immediately after surgery. Upon discharge, the length of stay for each patient is recorded in the electronic patient file.

Post-discharge, all visits to the outpatient clinic, whether to the surgeon, nurse, or physiotherapist, as well as phone calls with questions regarding their procedure to the surgical nurse, are documented in the electronic patient file, including the time spent.

The data collection time points are outlined in Table 2. Participants will receive a study diary to fill out on post-op days 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5, as well as after 2, 8, and 26 weeks. A research staff member will make a call to remind participants to complete the entries in the study diary, which include the Visual Analog Scale (VAS). Patients will be seen in the clinic for evaluation at 12, and 52 weeks after treatment. MRI and full-standing anteroposterior and lateral radiographs will be taken at the 12-week follow-up time point. This will allow for identification of any healing at the index level as well as any new-onset OVCFs.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes

Data will be analyzed using STATA. The primary outcome measure will be difference between groups as measured by the VAS scores at 12 weeks after initiation of treatment. Repeated measures ANCOVA with baseline VAS, ODI, EQ-5D-5 L, and number of levels involved as co-variates will be performed.

The Incremental Cost-Effectiveness Ratio (ICER) will be calculated and bootstrapped with 10,000 replications and presented graphically with acceptability curves to visualize the probability of cost effectiveness for PVP compared to Sham.

The complete and approved protocol, along with a comprehensive statistical analysis plan, is accessible through the following link:

https://portal.findresearcher.sdu.dk/da/projects/percutaneous-vertebroplasty-vs-sham-for-osteoporotic-vertebral-co.

Interim analyses

No interim analysis is planned.

A dedicated research nurse will continuously monitor the rate of serious adverse events, promptly reporting any occurrences to the research group. In the event of recurring serious adverse events, such as neurological impairment, the trial will be terminated.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g. subgroup analyses)

There are no additional planned analyses.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data

Although this will be a strictly conducted study, some loss to follow-up is expected. Missing data between two available time-points will not be imputed. Otherwise, missing data will be treated as missing.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant level-data and statistical code

The finalized and protocol approved by the Regional Committees on Health Research for Southern Denmark, along with a detailed statistical analysis plan, can be accessed through the following link: https://portal.findresearcher.sdu.dk/da/projects/percutaneous-vertebroplasty-vs-sham-for-osteoporotic-vertebral-co.

Upon the project’s conclusion, the research group will preserve an anonymized version of patient information from the database. Following the publication of project results, interested researchers may request anonymized raw data from both DaneSpine and the project database from the research group.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating centre and trial steering committee

As this is a single-center trial, the Trial Steering Committee comprises of the research group. Biannual meetings are scheduled throughout the study period. The day-to-day organizational support for the research nurse, staff in the outpatient ward, and the ward and operating theater is provided by the staff from the research secretariat at the Spine Centre of Southern Denmark.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure

A data monitoring committee is deemed unnecessary as the trial is a single-center study and a research nurse continuously oversees the data collection. No members of the research group have any competing interests in the results of the trial.

Adverse event reporting and harms

The research nurse will systematically document complications and adverse effects throughout both the hospital stay and the follow-up period. The recorded complications include nerve root lesions, neurological deterioration, anaphylactic reactions, cardiopulmonary complications, and infections. Upon detection, the research nurse will promptly notify the on-call physician to manage the complication.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct

The chairman of the board of the Danish Spine Society has consented to perform independent annual monitoring of the trial’s conduct. Additionally, members of the Regional Committees on Health Research for Southern Denmark conduct unannounced inspections at trial sites.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g. trial participants, ethical committees)

Any modifications to the inclusion criteria or other significant protocol changes will necessitate additional approval from the Regional Committees on Health Research for Southern Denmark.

In the event of alterations to the inclusion or exclusion criteria, we will disseminate this information to medical professionals in the Southern Denmark region, including general practitioners, chiropractors, physiotherapists, as well as hospital inpatient and emergency department staff and information registered at www.clinicaltrials.org updated accordingly.

Given that the trial is a single-center study, any changes impacting procedures at the Center for Spine Surgery & Research at Sygehus Lillebaelt Kolding Hospital will be conveyed through the monthly newsletter.

Dissemination plans

The study has been registered at http://www.clinicaltrials.gov. Reporting of the trial will follow the SPIRIT and the CONSORT statements.

We intend to publish results, whether positive, negative or inconclusive, in relevant international.

high impact journals. Rules for authorship will adhere to “Uniform requirements for manuscripts submitted to biomedical journals” at http://www.icmje.org.

Following the conclusion of the study, the study database will be archived at the Danish National Archives.

Discussion

As demographic changes lead to a growing elderly population and subsequently more individuals experiencing pain and impaired function from spinal fractures, there is a justified and necessary call for an enhanced emphasis on optimal fracture treatment. The evolving demographics underscore the significance of ensuring optimal physical function and quality of life for the elderly.

The results of the proposed project will likely have a substantial impact on shaping future national and international guidelines for the treatment of patients with osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCFs).

Analytic Class Incremental Learning for Sound Source Localization with Privacy Protection
Xinyuan Qian, Xianghu Yue, Jiadong Wang, Huiping Zhuang, Haizhou Li

Abstract
SSL ( SSL ) enabling technology for applications such as surveillance and robotics. While traditional SP ( SP )-based SSL methods provide analytic solutions under specific signal and noise assumptions, recent DL ( DL )-based methods have significantly outperformed them. However, their success depends on extensive training data and substantial computational resources. Moreover, they often rely on large-scale annotated spatial data and may struggle when adapting to evolving sound classes. To mitigate these challenges, we propose a novel CIL ( CIL ) approach, termed SSL-CIL, which avoids serious accuracy degradation due to catastrophic forgetting by incrementally updating the DL-based SSL model through a closed-form analytic solution. In particular, data privacy is ensured since the learning process does not revisit any historical data (exemplar-free), which is more suitable for smart home scenarios. Empirical results in the public SSLR dataset demonstrate the superior performance of our proposal, achieving a localization accuracy of 90.9%, surpassing other competitive methods.

II-A

Problem formulation

Let us denote the audio signals captured by a

{\bf s}^{1:M}

\theta\in[1^{\circ},360^{\circ}]
of the speaking person.
Given the spatial continuity of

[
4
]
to represent the posterior probability likelihoods:

{p}(\theta)=\exp\left(-\frac{|{\theta}-\dot{\theta}|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{\theta}}\right)

where
{p}(\theta)
is
centered on the ground truth
\dot{\theta}
with a standard deviation
\sigma_{\theta}

Specifically, we design a network

\hat{p}(\theta)=\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{X};{\bf\Omega})

where

\hat{\theta}=\arg\max_{\forall\theta}\ \hat{p}{(\theta)}

(1)

Since
DoA

A

{\bf D}^{\text{train}}_{k}\sim\{{\bf X}^{\text{tr}}_{k},{\bf\Theta}^{\text{tr}}_{k}\}

{\bf X}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{k}\times d}

II-B

SSL
network

GCC-PHAT

[
1
]
is chosen for its robustness to noise interference, achieved by utilizing solely the phase component of the cross spectrum while disregarding amplitude variations.
At microphone pair

GCC-PHAT
is computed as:

g^{n}(\tau)=\sum_{f}\left(\frac{{\bf S}_{n_{1}}(f){\bf S}^{*}_{n_{2}}(f)}{|{\bf S}_{n_{1}}(f){\bf S}^{*}_{n_{2}}(f)|}e^{i\frac{2\pi f}{N_{s}}\tau}\right)

(2)

where
i
,
f
and
\tau
denote the imaginary unit, frequency bin and time delay,
*
is the complex conjugate. It exhibits the highest value in the time domain precisely at the actual time delay.

We design a neural network
[
8
]
to map the input acoustic features to the output
DoA
posterior probability:

\hat{\bf\Theta}=f_{\text{softmax}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\text{mlp}}({\bf X},{\bf W}_{\text{mlp}}){\bf W}_{\text{fcn}}\right)

(3)

where
{\bf X\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{k}\times d}}
is all the concatenated
GCC-PHAT
s (
d=N_{m}\times\tau_{r}
and
N_{m}=\frac{M(M-1)}{2}
is the microphone pair number and
\tau_{r}
is the possible GCC-PHAT range).

\mathcal{F}_{\text{mlp}}
represents a
MLP
(
MLP
) architecture comprising three hidden layers. Each is a
FCN
(
FCN
) layer with ReLU activation
[
20
]
and batch normalization
[
21
]
.

{\bf W}_{\text{mlp}}
and
{\bf W}_{\text{fcn}}
denote the weights of
MLP
and the last
FCN
classifier, respectively.

f_{\text{softmax}}
is the softmax function.

II-C

Analytic re-alignment

In the initial stage (
k=0
), we recalibrate the learning process of the network to align with the dynamics characteristic of the analytical approach.
Let us denote the extracted feature matrix
{\bf X}^{\text{(mlp)}}
from the trained
MLP
backbone as:

{\bf X}^{\text{(mlp)}}_{0}=\mathcal{F}_{\text{mlp}}({\bf X}^{\text{tr}}_{0},{\bf W}_{\text{mlp}})

(4)

where
{\bf X}^{\text{(mlp)}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{k}\times d_{\text{(mlp)}}}
. Rather than employing a
FCN
layer to directly project features onto the classification terminal, we implement a
FE
(
FE
) process that strategically inserts an additional
FCN
layer to achieve effective higher-dimensional feature mapping:

{\bf X}^{\text{(fe)}}_{0}=f_{\text{ReLU}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\text{mlp}}({\bf X}^{\text{tr}}_{0},{\bf W}_{\text{MLP}}){\bf W}_{\text{fe}}\right)=f_{\text{ReLU}}\left({\bf X}^{\text{(mlp)}}_{0}{\bf W}_{\text{fe}}\right)

(5)

where
{\bf X}^{\text{(fe)}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{k}\times d_{\text{(fe)}}}
with
d_{\text{(fe)}}
denotes the feature
expansion size
(
d_{\text{(fe)}}>d_{\text{(mlp)}}
). The resulting increased parameters allow the
AL
-based methods to obtain the maximum performance where every element in
d_{\text{(fe)}}
is randomly sampled from a normal distribution
[
22

23
]
.

f_{\text{ReLU}}
denotes the ReLU activation function.

{\bf W}_{\text{fe}}
is the
FE

The expanded input acoustic features
{\bf X}^{\text{(fe)}}_{0}
can be mapped to the
DoA

\mathop{\arg\min}\limits_{{\bf W}^{(0)}_{\text{fcn}}}\begin{Vmatrix}{\bf Y}^{\text{tr}}_{0}-{\bf X}^{\text{(fe)}}_{0}{\bf W}_{\text{fcn}}\end{Vmatrix}^{2}_{F}+\eta||{\bf W}_{\text{fcn}}||^{2}_{F}

(6)

where
||\cdot||^{2}_{F}
denotes the Frobenius norm and
\eta
is the regularization parameter. Then, the optimal solution (re-aligned weight) can be found as:

\hat{\bf W}^{(0)}_{\text{fcn}}=\left({\bf X}^{\text{(fe)}\intercal}_{0}{\bf X}^{\text{(fe)}}_{0}+\eta{\bf I}\right)^{-1}{\bf X}^{\text{(fe)}\intercal}_{0}{\bf\Theta}^{\text{tr}}_{0}

(7)

where

Given the training data

(8)

{\bf 0}&{\bf\Theta}^{tr}_{1}&\ldots&{\bf 0}\\

{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&...&{\bf\Theta}^{tr}_{k-1}\\
\end{bmatrix},\ {\bf X}^{\text{(fe)}}_{0:k-1}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf X}^{\text{(fe)}}_{0}\\
{\bf X}^{\text{(fe)}}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
{\bf X}^{\text{(fe)}}_{k-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}

(9)

{\bf X}^{\text{(fe)}}_{i}=f_{\text{ReLU}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\text{mlp}}({\bf X}_{i},{\bf W}_{\text{MLP}}){\bf W}_{\text{fe}}\right)

III
Experiments

Datasets.

We select the SSLR dataset
[
24
]
for experiments.
It was created using a real-world setup where four microphones are mounted on the head of the Peper robot, arranged in a 5.8 × 6.9 cm rectangular configuration, all of which are cardioid and front-facing in their directional orientation.

The audio sequences are captured at a sampling rate of 48 kHz, from one to two simultaneous speakers, complemented by precise 3D spatial annotations.
The dataset is meticulously divided into three subsets: train-Loudspeaker, test-Human, and test-Loudspeaker.

Settings.

The
GCC-PHAT
is calculated for each 170-ms segment with delay lags from -25 to 25, yielding 51 coefficients per microphone pair (
\tau_{r}=51
), consistent with the approach in
[
4
]
.
Given 6 microphone pairs (
N_{m}=6
), the total aggregate of GCC-PHAT coefficients obtained is 306 (
d=306
).
All models are trained for 30 epochs with a batch size of 256.
We use Adam
[
25
]
optimizer to train the model and set the learning rate and weight decay of 1e-3 and 1e-4, respectively.
We use
MSE
(
MSE
) loss to optimize the network.
For the replay-based methods, the memory size is set to 3600.
For the incremental setting, we evenly divide the 360 classes into 10 phases (
K=10
), each of which contains 36 classes.

We average the

[

8

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose the SSL-CIL algorithm to tackle the challenging speaker
DoA
estimation problem under the
CIL
settings for the first time.
Specifically, SSL-CIL provides an analytic solution that facilitates the incremental learning of new sound classes through a recursive manner.
It integrates two critical features: exemplar-free and data privacy protection to overcome key limitations in the
CIL
field.
The experimental results in the public SSLR dataset demonstrate competitive performance over the other
SOTA
methods, achieving MAE and ACC of 5.04
∘
and 90.9%, respectively.
It also shows better results when tested under different
SNR
conditions.
Future work may extend this study to the speaker tracking scenario.

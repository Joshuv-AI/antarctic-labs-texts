Sparse Bounds for Discrete Maximal Functions associated with Birch-Magyar averages
Ankit Bhojak, Surjeet Singh Choudhary, Siddhartha Samanta, Saurabh Shrivastava

Abstract
. In this article, we study discrete maximal function associated with the Birch-Magyar averages over sparse sequences. We establish sparse domination principle for such operators. As a consequence, we obtain ℓ p \ell^{p} -estimates for such discrete maximal function over sparse sequences for all p > 1 p>1 . The proof of sparse bounds is based on scale-free ℓ p − \ell^{p}- improving estimates for the single scale Birch-Magyar averages.

1.
Introduction

Let
\sigma
be the normalized Lebesgue measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
. For a continuous function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, the spherical averages of
f
is defined by

\mathcal{A}_{t}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(x-t\theta)d\sigma(\theta),\;t>0.

For
n\geq 3
, the full spherical maximal function

\mathcal{A}_{\star}f(x)=\sup\limits_{t>0}|\mathcal{A}_{t}f(x)|

was shown to be bounded on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
by Stein
[
27
]
if and only if
p>\frac{n}{n-1}
. Later, Bourgain
[
4
]
proved that the maximal operator
\mathcal{A}_{\star}
is bounded on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
if and only if
p>2
. Further, the lacunary version of the spherical maximal function was considered. Recall that a sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is called lacunary if there exists
c>1
such that
\frac{\lambda_{k+1}}{\lambda_{k}}\geq c,
for all
k\geq 1.
The lacunary spherical maximal function corresponding to a lacunary sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is defined as

\mathcal{A}_{lac}f(x)=\sup\limits_{k}|\mathcal{A}_{\lambda_{k}}f(x)|.

The maximal function
\mathcal{A}_{lac}
is known to be bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p<\infty
and
n\geq 2
by the works of Calderón
[
5
]
, Coifman-Weiss
[
6
]
, and Duoandikoetxea-Rubio de Francia
[
10
]
.

1.1.
Discrete analogues of spherical maximal functions

In this article, we are concerned with the study of discrete analogues of spherical averages and corresponding maximal function. For a function
f:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
in
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
, the discrete analogue of the euclidean spherical average (
1
) is defined by

M_{\lambda}f(x)=\frac{1}{r(\lambda)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|y|=\lambda\end{subarray}}f(x-y),

where
r(\lambda)=|\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:|y|=\lambda\}|
.
Similar to the continuous case as in (
1
)) and (
1
), the full discrete spherical maximal function and the lacunary discrete maximal function are defined as below

M_{\star}f(x)=\sup\limits_{\lambda\geq 1}|M_{\lambda}f(x)|

and

M_{lac}f(x)=\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}|M_{\lambda_{k}}f(x)|,

where
\{\lambda_{k}\}
is a lacunary sequence.
Magyar-Stein-Wainger
[
23
]
proved that the maximal operator
M_{\star}
is bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
in the sharp range
p>\frac{n}{n-2}
and
n\geq 5
; subsequently Ionescu
[
16
]
established restricted weak-type estimate for
M_{\star}
at the endpoint
\frac{n}{n-2}
. Note that unlike the continuous counterpart, the discerte lacunary maximal operator
M_{lac}
fails to be bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for values of
p
near
1
. More precisely, a counterexample of Zienkiewicz provides construction of lacunary sequence for which the operator
M_{lac}
is not bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for
p<\frac{n}{n-1}
. We refer the interested reader to
[
7
, section 7]
for the explicit counstruction of the counterexample. Hughes
[
14
]
showed that
M_{lac}
is bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for
p>\frac{n-1}{n-2}
and
n\geq 5
. This was further improved by Kesler-Lacey-Mena
[
18
]
for the range
p>\frac{n-2}{n-3}
and
n\geq 5
. However, the question of
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
-estimates of
M_{lac}
is still open in the range
\frac{n}{n-1}\leq p\leq\frac{n-2}{n-3}
. It is interesting to know for which sequence of radii
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, the maximal function

\mathcal{M}(f)(x)=\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}|M_{\lambda_{k}}f(y)|
extends to a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for all
p>1
. This question was addressed by Cook
[
9
]
for a class of sequences, known as sparse sequences, defined as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

We call a sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
sparse, if there exists
\mu_{k}
such that
\lambda_{k}=\mu_{k}!
, with

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\mu_{k}}{\log k}=\infty.

Cook
[
9
]
showed that the maximal function
\mathcal{M}(f)(x)=\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}|M_{\lambda_{k}}f(y)|
associated with the sparse sequence
\lambda_{k}=2^{k}!,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is bounded on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
for
p>1
and
n\geq 5
. Later, Kesler-Lacey-Mena
[
18
]
generalized this result further by showing that the operator
\mathcal{M}

is bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for all
p>1
and for any sparse sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
.

1.2.
Birch-Magyar averages and associated maximal functions

The goal of this article is to extend the results mentioned above regarding the
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
-boundedness of maximal functions associated with sparse sequences in the setting of Birch-Magyar averages. These discrete averages are defined over more general algebraic hypersurfaces. This framework was developed by Birch
[
3
]
and Magyar
[
25
]
. Let us first briefly recall the notion of Birch-Magyar averages. Note that a homogeneous polynomial with integral coefficients is called an integral form. Let
\mathfrak{R}(x)
be an integral form of degree
d>1
in
n
variables. The Birch rank of
\mathfrak{R}
, denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{R}),
is defined as

\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{R})=n-\text{dim}V(\mathfrak{R}),

where

V(\mathfrak{R})=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\partial_{z_{1}}\mathfrak{R}(z)=\partial_{z_{2}}\mathfrak{R}(z)=\cdots=\partial_{z_{n}}\mathfrak{R}(z)=0\}.

We use the following standard notation

c_{\mathfrak{R}}=\frac{\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{R})}{(d-1)2^{d-1}}~~\text{and}~~\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}=\frac{1}{6d}\left(\frac{c_{\mathfrak{R}}}{2}-1\right).

Note that
d\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}<c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2
. For a given compactly supported smooth function
\phi
with
0\leq\phi\leq 1
, an integral form
\mathfrak{R}(x)
of degree
d>1
in
n
variables is called
\phi-
regular if there exists a non-singular solution of
\mathfrak{R}(x)=1
in the support of
\phi
and Birch rank of
\mathfrak{R}
is greater than
(d-1)2^{d}
. We note that
c_{\mathfrak{R}}>2
if
\mathfrak{R}
is
\phi-
regular.

Consider the counting function
r_{\mathfrak{R},\phi}(\lambda)=\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{R}(x)=\lambda}\phi\left(\frac{x}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right)
. It is known, see
[
3
,
25
]
, that for a
\phi-
regular form
\mathfrak{R}
there is an infinite arithmetic progression
\Lambda
of regular values
\lambda\in\Lambda
satisfying
r_{\mathfrak{R},\phi}(\lambda)\approx\lambda^{\frac{n}{d}-1}
. Henceforth, for a
\phi-
regular form
\mathfrak{R}
, let
\Lambda
denote such an infinite arithmetic progression. We refer the readers to
[
3
]
,
[
25
]
, and
[
7
]
for more details on
\phi-
regular forms. For a
\phi-
regular form
\mathfrak{R}(x)
of degree
d>1
in
n
variables and for
\lambda\in\Lambda
, the Birch-Magyar average of
f
is defined by

M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)=\frac{1}{r_{\mathfrak{R},\phi}(\lambda)}\sum_{y:\mathfrak{R}(y)=\lambda}\phi\left(\frac{y}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right)f(x-y).

Note that the definition above encompasses the
k-
spherical averages studied in
[
24
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
12
]
, and
[
1
]
, see Cook and Hughes
[
7
]
for details. Magyar
[
25
]
proved that the discrete maximal function associated with Birch-Magyar averages

M^{\mathfrak{R},\phi}_{*}f(x)=\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)|,

is bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
when
p=2
. Further, the operator
M^{\mathfrak{R},\phi}_{*}
is bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for
p\leq\frac{n}{n-d}
. Later, Hughes
[
15
, Appendix A]
showed that the operator
M^{\mathfrak{R},\phi}_{*}
is not bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for
p>\text{max}\left\{\frac{c_{\mathfrak{R}}}{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1},\frac{2(\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}+1)}{2\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}+1}\right\}
. However, the sharp range of
p
for which the operator
M^{\mathfrak{R},\phi}_{*}
is bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
is not known. For a lacunary sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}=\mathbb{L}\subset\Lambda
, Cook-Hughes
[
7
]
proved that the lacunary maximal function
M_{lac}^{\mathfrak{R},\phi}f(x)=\sup\limits_{\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{L}}|M_{\lambda_{k},\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)|
is bounded on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
for
p>\frac{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2}{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-3}
, provided the dimension
n
is sufficiently large. Moreover, for the range
1<p<\frac{n}{n-1}
, they showed that there exists a lacunary sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that the corresponding maximal operator
M_{lac}^{\mathfrak{R},\phi}
is not bounded on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
.

We address the question of
\ell^{p}-
boundedness of the maximal function associated with Birch-Magyar averages for sparse sequences defined in
Definition

1.1
. In particular, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a
\phi-
regular integral form in
n
variables of degree
d>1
and
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sparse sequence contained in
\Lambda
, the set of regular values. Then the maximal function
\mathcal{M}^{\mathfrak{R},\phi}f=\sup\limits_{\lambda_{k}}|M_{\lambda_{k},\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)|
is bounded on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
for all
p>1
.

Indeed, we shall prove sparse bounds,
Theorem

1.3
, for the operator
\mathcal{M}^{\mathfrak{R},\phi}
.
Theorem

1.2
can be obtained as a consequence of sparse bounds. Further, note that
Theorem

1.2
is a generalization of the result obtained by Kesler-Lacey-Mena
[
18
]
for the spherical case. In order to state the result concerning sparse bounds for the operator
\mathcal{M}^{\mathfrak{R},\phi}
, let us discuss the preliminaries notion for sparse bounds for discrete operators.

A collection of cubes
\mathcal{S}

\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is called
\frac{1}{4}-

Q\in\mathcal{S}
, there is a subset
E_{Q}\subset Q

\{E_{Q}\subset Q:Q\in\mathcal{S}\}
are pairwise disjoint and satisfy
|E_{Q}|>\frac{1}{4}|Q|

(p,q)-

\Lambda_{\mathcal{S},p,q}
, indexed by the sparse collection
\mathcal{S}
as

\Lambda_{\mathcal{S},p,q}(f,g)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\langle f\rangle_{Q,p}\langle g\rangle_{Q,q},

where
\langle f\rangle_{Q,p}=\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\sum\limits_{m\in Q}|f(m)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
and we set
\langle f\rangle_{Q}=\langle f\rangle_{Q,1}.

For a sublinear operator
T
, let
\|T\|_{p,q}
the
(p,q)-
sparse norm of
T
. It is defined as the smallest constant
C>0
such that

|\langle Tf,g\rangle|\leq C\sup\limits_{\mathcal{S}}\Lambda_{\mathcal{S},p,q}(f,g),

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the standard inner product on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
.

\mathcal{M}^{\mathfrak{R},\phi}f(x)=\sup\limits_{\lambda_{k}}|M_{\lambda_{k},\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x)|
satisfies the following sparse bounds

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathfrak{R},\phi}\|_{p,q}<\infty~\quad\text{for all}\;\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\right)\in S_{n},

where

S_{1}=(0,1)

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a
\phi-
regular form of degree
d>1
in
n
variables and
S_{n}
be the open triangle with vertices
(0,1)
,
(1,0)
, and
\left(\frac{1+2\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}{2(1+\eta_{\mathfrak{R}})},\frac{1+2\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}{2(1+\eta_{\mathfrak{R}})}\right)
. For
\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\right)\in S_{n}

C_{p,\mathfrak{R}}
, independent of
\lambda\in\Lambda
, such that the following inequality holds

\langle M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f,g\rangle\leq C_{p,\mathfrak{R}}|E|\langle f\rangle_{E,p}\langle g\rangle_{E,q}.

In particular, for
\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\right)\in S_{n}

\|M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\to\ell^{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\lesssim\lambda^{n\left(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}.

2.
Sparse bounds for the single scale Birch-Magyar average: Proof of
Theorem

1.4

First, note that using standard arguments, it is enough to prove the theorem for characteristic functions. Let
E=\left[0,\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\right]^{n}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and consider
f=1_{F}
and
g=1_{G}
, where
F,G\subseteq E
.
It is elementary to verify that

\displaystyle\|M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{x}\left|\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{d}-1}}\sum_{\mathfrak{R}(y)=\lambda}f(x-y)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}},

and

\displaystyle\|M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f\|_{\ell^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{d}-1}}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\sum_{\mathfrak{R}(y)=\lambda}|f(x-y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}.

Therefore, the operator
M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}
is bounded from
\ell^{p}
to
\ell^{q^{\prime}}
for
\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\right)=(0,1)
and
\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\right)=(1,0)
. Using the interpolation arguments, observe that it is enough to prove that the operator
M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}
is bounded from
\ell^{p}
to
\ell^{q^{\prime}}
at the point
\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\right)=\left(\frac{1+2\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}{2(1+\eta_{\mathfrak{R}})},\frac{1+2\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}{2(1+\eta_{\mathfrak{R}})}\right)
.

We shall decompose
M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f
into several but finitely many terms and prove appropriate estimate on each term. More precisely, for every term, say
\mathcal{T}f
, in the decomposition of
M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f
, and for any integer
N
we will establish one of the following two type of estimates.

(2.1)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}f\rangle_{E,\infty}\lesssim N^{d}\langle f\rangle_{E}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}f\rangle_{E,2}\lesssim N^{-d\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}

Note that combining these estimates together, we get that

|E|^{-1}\langle M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f,g\rangle\lesssim N^{d}\langle f\rangle_{E}\langle g\rangle_{E}+N^{-d\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle f\rangle_{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then optimizing over
N
we obtain

|E|^{-1}\langle M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f,g\rangle\lesssim[\langle f\rangle_{E}\langle g\rangle_{E}]^{\frac{1+2\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}{2(1+\eta_{\mathfrak{R}})}}.

Therefore, we need to prove either (
2.1
) or (
2.2
) for each part in the decomposition of the operator.

Let
N
be a positive integer and decompose the operator as follows

M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f=1_{\{\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq N\}}M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f+1_{\{\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}>N\}}M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f.

We set
M_{1,1}f:=1_{\{\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq N\}}M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f
. For
\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\geq N
, by following the approach of Magyar
[
25
, Lemma 1]
, we decompose the operator further as

M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}=C_{\lambda}+M_{2,1}
, where the multiplier
m_{2,1}

and the multiplier corresponding to the operator

Here in the expression above,

where

Let

denote the normalized Weyl sum defined as

where

We have the following estimate

for the quantity

|F_{q}(a,b)|\lesssim q^{-c_{\mathfrak{R}}},\quad\text{for}\quad(a,q)=1.

We will require to decompose the operator

further as follows,

C_{\lambda}=M_{1,2}+M_{2,2}+M_{2,3},

where the operators
M_{1,2}

M_{2,2}

\displaystyle m_{1,2}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq q\leq N}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}}e_{q}(-a\lambda)F_{q}(a,b)\widehat{\zeta_{\frac{q\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}{N}}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right),

\displaystyle m_{2,2}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq q\leq N}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}}e_{q}(-a\lambda)F_{q}(a,b)\left(\widehat{\zeta_{q}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)-\widehat{\zeta_{\frac{q\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}{N}}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right),

\displaystyle m_{2,3}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N<q\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}}e_{q}(-a\lambda)F_{q}(a,b)\widehat{\zeta_{q}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right).

2.1.
Estimate for
M_{1,1}

Since

and
\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq N
, we have

\displaystyle\langle M_{\lambda,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f\rangle_{E,\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{\mathfrak{R},\phi}(\lambda)}\sum_{x:\mathfrak{R}(x)=\lambda}\phi(x/\lambda^{1/d})f(y-x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{N^{d}}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{d}}}\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{d}\langle f\rangle_{E}.

2.2.
Estimate for
M_{1,2}

Consider the kernel
K_{1,2}(x)=(m_{1,2})^{\vee}(x)
of the operator
M_{1,2}
. We have

\displaystyle|K_{1,2}(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{1\leq q\leq N}\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{\times}}e_{q}(-a\lambda)\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{n}}F_{q}(a,b)\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}e(x\cdot\xi)\widehat{\zeta_{\frac{q}{N}}}\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right)d\xi\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{1\leq q\leq N}\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{d}}}\zeta_{\frac{q}{N}}\star d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{\times}}e_{q}(-a\lambda)\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{n}}F_{q}(a,b)e\left(x\cdot\frac{b}{q}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{d}}}\sum_{1\leq q\leq N}\zeta_{\frac{q}{N}}\star d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{\times}}e_{q}(-a\lambda)e\left(\frac{a\mathfrak{R}(x)}{q}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{n}{d}}}\sum_{1\leq q\leq N}q\left(\frac{N}{q}\right)\frac{1}{\left(1+\left|\frac{x}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right|\right)^{2n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{n-2}},

where we have used the following decay estimate (see
[
15
, p. 970]
) in the second last step,

|\zeta_{t}\star d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}(x)|\lesssim t^{-1}\frac{1}{\left(1+\left|\frac{x}{t}\right|\right)^{2n}}.

Hence,

This yields that

\langle M_{1,2}f\rangle_{E,\infty}\lesssim N^{2}\langle f\rangle_{E}.

2.3.
Estimate for
M_{2,1}

This follows by applying Plancherel’s theorem. Recall the inequality (
2.3
) to get that

\|M_{2,1}f\|_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim N^{-d\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Thus, we have

\langle M_{2,1}f\rangle_{E,2}\lesssim N^{-d\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

2.4.
Estimate for
M_{2,2}

Note that we have the following decay estimate from
[
25
, Lemma 6]
,

|\widehat{d\sigma}_{\mathfrak{R}}(\xi)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|)^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}}.

Further, since
\widehat{\zeta_{q}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)-\widehat{\zeta_{\frac{q\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}{N}}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)
is supported in the annulus
\left\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\;\frac{N}{q\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}\lesssim\left|\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{q}\right\}
, we get that

\displaystyle|m_{2,2}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{1\leq q\leq N}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}}\frac{1}{q^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}}}\frac{1}{\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}|\xi-\frac{b}{q}|\right)^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{1\leq q\leq N}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}}\frac{1}{q^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}}}\left(\frac{q}{N}\right)^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{d\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}}.

Hence it follows that

\langle M_{2,2}f\rangle_{E,2}\lesssim N^{-d\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

2.5.
Estimate for
M_{2,3}

By Plancherel’s theorem and the bound
|F_{q}(a,b)|\lesssim q^{-c_{\mathfrak{R}}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|M_{2,3}f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N<q\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}}q^{-c_{\mathfrak{R}}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N<q}q^{-(c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1)}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim N^{-(c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1)}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

This implies that

\langle M_{2,3}f\rangle_{E,2}\lesssim N^{-d\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This concludes the proof of
Theorem

1.4
.∎

\|M^{k}_{2,1}f\|_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim\lambda_{k}^{-\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}},

and hence

\displaystyle\|M_{2,1}f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{\lambda_{k}>\lambda_{[N^{\epsilon}]}}\lambda_{k}^{-\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}\Big)\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{N^{\epsilon}}^{-\eta_{\mathfrak{R}}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim N^{-(c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2)}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

3.6.
Estimate for
M_{2,2}

We have,

\displaystyle m^{k}_{2,2}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\lambda_{k}}(\xi)-\sum_{q=1}^{N}\sum_{a\in U_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}F_{q}(a,b)\widehat{\zeta_{q}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left(\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq\bar{a}<L}\sum_{\bar{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{L}}F_{L}(\bar{a},\bar{b})\widehat{\zeta_{2L}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{L}\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left(\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{L}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-\sum_{q=1}^{N}\sum_{a\in U_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}F_{q}(a,b)\widehat{\zeta_{q}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left(\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right).

We note that for any function
g:\left\{\frac{a}{L}:0\leq a<L\right\}\to{\mathbb{R}}
we have the identity

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}g\left(\frac{a}{L}\right)=\sum_{1\leq q\leq N}\sum_{a\in U_{q}}g\left(\frac{a}{q}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q:q|L\\
q>N\end{subarray}}\sum_{a\in U_{q}}g\left(\frac{a}{q}\right).

Using the identity as above, we write the multiplier as
m^{k}_{2,2}=m^{k}_{2,2,1}+m^{k}_{2,2,2}
, where

\displaystyle m^{k}_{2,2,1}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq\bar{a}<L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{L}\\
\frac{L}{\nu}\leq N\end{subarray}}F_{L}(\bar{a},\bar{b})\left(\widehat{\zeta_{2L}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)-\widehat{\zeta_{\frac{L}{\nu}}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left(\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)\right),

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad m^{k}_{2,2,2}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq\bar{a}<L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{L}\\
\frac{L}{\nu}>N\end{subarray}}F_{L}(\bar{a},\bar{b})\widehat{\zeta_{2L}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left(\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)\right),

with the index
\nu=\text{gcd}(\bar{a},\bar{b},L)
for which we have
F_{L}(\bar{a},\bar{b})=F_{\frac{L}{\nu}}(\frac{\bar{a}}{\nu},\frac{\bar{b}}{\nu})
. Thus we have

M_{2,2}f(x)\leq M_{2,2,1}f(x)+M_{2,2,2}f(x),

where
M_{2,2,1}
and
M_{2,2,2}
are the operators defined as

M_{2,2,j}f(x)=\sup_{\lambda_{k}>\lambda_{[N^{\epsilon}]}}|(m^{k}_{2,2,j})^{\vee}\star f(x)|,\;j=1,2.

3.7.
Estimate for
M_{2,2,1}
:

Observe that
\widehat{\zeta_{2L}}(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L})-\widehat{\zeta_{\frac{L}{\nu}}}(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L})
is supported for
|2L(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L})|\geq\frac{1}{2}
; i.e.,
|\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}|\geq\frac{1}{4L}
. Therefore, in the support of
\widehat{\zeta_{2L}}(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L})-\widehat{\zeta_{\frac{L}{\nu}}}(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L})
the term
|\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}(\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}_{k}(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}))|
is bounded by
\left(\frac{L}{\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right)^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}
. Also, for
k>N_{\circ}
,
\lambda_{k}>N^{d}!>(N!)^{d}=L^{d}
.

\displaystyle\|M_{2,2,1}f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\lambda_{k}>\lambda_{N^{\epsilon}}}\left(\sup_{\xi}|m^{k}_{2,2,1}(\xi)|\right)\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{0\leq\bar{a}<L}\sum_{\lambda_{k}>\lambda_{N^{\epsilon}}}\left(\frac{L}{\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right)^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}\frac{1}{L^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{0\leq\bar{a}<L}\frac{1}{L(\lambda_{N^{\epsilon}}^{\frac{1}{d}})^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{L^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-2}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

3.8.
Estimate for
M_{2,2,2}
:

Consider

\displaystyle m^{k}_{2,2,2}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq\bar{a}<L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{L}\\
\frac{L}{\nu}>N\end{subarray}}F_{L}(\bar{a},\bar{b})\widehat{\zeta_{2L}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left(\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq\bar{a}<L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{L}\\
\frac{L}{\nu}>N\end{subarray}}F_{L}(\bar{a},\bar{b})\widehat{\zeta_{2L}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)\widehat{\zeta_{L}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left(\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)\right).

Since
\widehat{\zeta_{L}}\left(\xi-\frac{b_{1}}{L}\right)
and
\widehat{\zeta_{2L}}\left(\xi-\frac{b_{2}}{L}\right)
have disjoint supports for
b_{1}\neq b_{2}
, we can write

M^{k}_{2,2,2}=V_{\lambda_{k}}\circ S,

where
V_{\lambda_{k}}
and
S
are the operators corresponding to the multipliers
v_{\lambda_{k}}
and
s
respectively given by

\displaystyle s(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq\bar{a}<L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{L}\\
\frac{L}{\nu}>N\end{subarray}}F_{L}(\bar{a},\bar{b})\widehat{\zeta_{2L}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle v_{\lambda_{k}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{L}\\
\frac{L}{\nu}>N\end{subarray}}\widehat{\zeta_{L}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left({\lambda^{\frac{1}{d}}_{k}}\left(\xi-\frac{\bar{b}}{L}\right)\right).

Invoking
Proposition

3.6
and
Theorem

A.1
, we get that
\|\sup\limits_{\lambda_{k}}|V_{\lambda_{k}}f|\|_{\ell^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{p}}
for
p>1
; in particular, note that the inequality as above holds true for
p=2
. This gives us

\displaystyle\|M_{2,2,2}f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Sf\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{\xi}|s(\xi)|\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{0\leq\bar{a}<L}|F_{L}(\bar{a},\bar{b})|\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{L^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

3.9.
Estimate for
M_{2,3}

Since
e_{q}(-\lambda_{k}a)=1
for
\lambda_{k}>\lambda_{[N^{\epsilon}]}
and
q>N
, we write the multiplier

as

\displaystyle m^{k}_{2,3}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q>N}\sum_{a\in U_{q}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}F_{q}(a,b)\widehat{\zeta_{q}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}\left(\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{d}}\left(\xi-\frac{b}{q}\right)\right),

\displaystyle=\left(\sum\limits_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}\widehat{\zeta_{q}}(\xi-\frac{b}{q})\widehat{d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R}}}(\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{d}}(\xi-\frac{b}{q}))\right)\left(\sum\limits_{a\in U_{q}}\sum\limits_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}}F_{q}(a,b)\widehat{\zeta_{2q}}(\xi-\frac{b}{q})\right).

Using

\displaystyle\|M_{2,3}f\|_{\ell^{2}}

Appendix A
Sparse bounds for a continuous maximal operator

In this section we discuss the continuous analogue of the lacunary maximal function associated with the average
M_{\lambda_{k},\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}
. As mentioned previously, due to non-availability of results in the desired framework in the literature; we present the proofs here. Let
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a lacunary sequence in the set of regular values
\Lambda
. Consider the lacunary maximal function defined by

\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{R},*}f(x)=\sup\limits_{\lambda_{k}}\mathcal{A}_{\lambda_{k},\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x),

where

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda_{k},\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f(x):=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}^{\frac{n}{d}-1}}f*d\sigma_{\mathfrak{R},\lambda_{k}}(x)=f*d\bar{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{R},\lambda_{k}}(x),

where
\phi
and
\mathfrak{R}
are as in
Section

1
.

We have the following sparse bound for the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{R},*}
, which were used to prove
Theorem

1.3
.

Theorem A.1
.

The sparse bound
\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{R},*}\|_{p,q}
holds for all points
\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\right)
belonging to the interior of the triangle
V_{n}
with vertices
(1,0)
,
\left(\frac{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}},\frac{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}}\right)
, and
(0,1)
.

The sparse bounds as above are indeed a consequence of the
L^{p}
-improving properties of single scale avarages . This observation was obtained in the work of
[
21
]
and further used by Oberlin
[
26
]
to obtain certain endpoint sparse bounds for Radon transforms. We also refer the readers to
[
2
]
. We use the following result

Lemma A.2
.

[
26
, Theorem 1.3]

Suppose
\sigma
is a finite measure supported on the unit ball with

|\widehat{\sigma}(\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|^{-\beta},

for some
\beta>0
, and
1<p<q<\infty
are exponents such that convolution with
\sigma
is a bounded operator from
L^{p}
to
L^{q}
. Then for every pair of compactly supported
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
there is a sparse collection of cubes
\mathcal{Q}
such that

|\langle T^{*}f_{1},f_{2}\rangle|\lesssim\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}|Q|\langle f_{1}\rangle_{Q,p}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{3Q,q^{{}^{\prime}}},

with
T^{*}f(x)=\sup\limits_{j}\sigma_{j}*|f|(x)
, where
\sigma_{j}
is defined as

\int fd\sigma_{j}=\int f(2^{j}x)d\sigma(x).

In view of the lemma above,
Theorem

A.1
follows from the following estimates for single scale averages.

Proposition A.3
.

The operator
\mathcal{A}_{1,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}
is bounded from
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\right)
belonging to the triangle
D_{n}
with vertices
(0,0)
,
\left(\frac{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}},\frac{1}{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}}\right)
, and
(1,1)
.

Note that, the previously defined triangle
S_{n}
is strictly contained inside this triangle
V_{n}
. Proof of
Proposition

A.3
uses Bourgain’s interpolation trick. We state Lemma 2.6 from
[
22
]
for convenience.

Lemma A.4

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}>0
. Suppose that
\{T_{j}\}
is a sequence of linear (or sublinear) operators
such that for some
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
, and
1\leq q_{1},q_{2}<\infty
,

\|T_{j}(f)\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\leq M_{1}2^{\epsilon_{1}j}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}},~~\|T_{j}(f)\|_{L^{q_{2}}}\leq M_{2}2^{-\epsilon_{2}j}\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}}.

Then
T=\sum_{j}T_{j}
is bounded from
L^{p,1}
to
L^{q,\infty}
, i.e.

\|T(f)\|_{L^{q,\infty}}\lesssim M^{\theta}_{1}M^{1-\theta}_{2}\|f\|_{L^{p,1}},

where
\theta=\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}}
,
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{\theta}{q_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{q_{2}}

and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{p_{2}}
.

Proof of
Proposition

A.3
.

Since
\|d\bar{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{R},1}\|_{1}\lesssim 1
, it follows that the operator
\mathcal{A}_{1,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}
is bounded in
L^{p}
for
p=1
and
p=\infty
. Next, we prove that
\mathcal{A}_{1,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}
is bounded from
L^{\frac{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}}{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1},1}
to
L^{2c_{\mathfrak{R}},\infty}
. Note that the desired
L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}
-boundedness of
\mathcal{A}_{1,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}
in the interior of the triangle
D_{n}
follows by interpolation.

Let
\psi
be a Schwartz function such that

\widehat{\psi}(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pt|\xi|\leq 1,\\
0,&\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pt|\xi|\geq 2.\end{cases}

Define
\widehat{\psi_{N^{-1}}}(\xi)=\widehat{\psi}\left(\frac{\xi}{N}\right)
and write
\widehat{f}(\xi)\widehat{d\bar{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{R},1}}(\xi)
as

\widehat{f}(\xi)\widehat{d\bar{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{R},1}}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\widehat{\psi_{N^{-1}}}(\xi)\widehat{d\bar{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{R},1}}(\xi)+\widehat{f}(\xi)(1-\widehat{\psi_{N^{-1}}}(\xi))\widehat{d\bar{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{R},1}}(\xi).

Notice that,
\psi_{N^{-1}}*{d\bar{\sigma}}_{\mathfrak{R},1}(x)
is bounded by
\frac{N}{(1+|x|)^{M}}
. If
\mathfrak{R}(x):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{2}
, a proof of this can be found in
[
11
, Lemma 6.5.3]
. Further, since the proof depends on the dimensionality of the measure, this estimate is valid for the measure
\bar{d\sigma}_{\mathfrak{R},1}
over the variety
\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\mathfrak{R}(x)=1\}
, where
\mathfrak{R}
is any integral form of degree
d
. We refer to
[
15
, p 970]
, where this estimate is used to bound the averages.
Consider,

\displaystyle\|f*\psi_{N^{-1}}*{d\bar{\sigma}}_{\mathfrak{R},1}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi_{N^{-1}}*{d\bar{\sigma}}_{\mathfrak{R},1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N\|f\|_{L^{1}}.

Next, using the support of
\widehat{\psi_{N^{-1}}}(\xi)
and the bound of
\widehat{d\bar{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{R},1}}(\xi)
mentioned earlier, we get that

\displaystyle\|f*(1-\psi_{N^{-1}})*d\bar{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{R},1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{f}(\xi)(1-\widehat{\psi_{N^{-1}}}(\xi))\widehat{d\bar{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{R},1}}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{\xi}|(1-\widehat{\psi_{N^{-1}}}(\xi))\widehat{d\bar{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{R},1}}(\xi)|\|f\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|)^{c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1}}|1-\widehat{\psi_{N^{-1}}}(\xi)|\|f\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{1-c_{\mathfrak{R}}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Using
Lemma

A.4
, it follows that

\|\mathcal{A}_{1,\phi}^{\mathfrak{R}}f\|_{L^{2c_{\mathfrak{R}},\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}}{2c_{\mathfrak{R}}-1},1}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

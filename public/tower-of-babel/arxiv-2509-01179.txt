Structural Equations for Critical Points of Conformally Invariant Curvature Energies in 4d
Yann Bernard

Abstract




Abstract:

This paper considers the Euler-Lagrange equations satisfied by the critical points of a large class of conformally invariant extrinsic energies for 4-manifolds immersed into
\mathbb{R}^{m\geq 5}
. Using invariances and Noether’s theorem, we convert the Euler-Lagrange equation in a system of equations with analytically favourable structures. The present paper generalises to the four-dimensional setting ideas originally developed by Tristan Rivière in his study of the Willmore energy in two dimensions.

MSC codes:
35G50, 53B20, 53B25, 53C42, 53C21

I
Introduction and Main Results

I.1
Brief recap of the two-dimensional Willmore energy

The Willmore energy is a fundamental conformally invariant functional in differential geometry, defined for smooth immersions of compact oriented surfaces into Euclidean space. For a surface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{m\geq 3}
, its Willmore energy is given by

\mathcal{W}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}|\vec{H}|^{2}\,dA,

where
\vec{H}
is the mean curvature vector and
dA
the induced area measure. This energy captures the total bending of the surface and remains invariant under Möbius transformations of
\mathbb{R}^{3}\cup\{\infty\}
. The conformal invariance of the Willmore energy becomes manifest when written

\int_{\Sigma}|\vec{H}|^{2}dA\;=\;\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\vec{h}_{0}|^{2}dA+\int_{\Sigma}KdA\>,

where
\vec{h}_{0}
denotes the trace-free part of the second fundamental form, while
K
is the Gauss curvature.
The first integral on the right is clearly conformally invariant, while the second integral is a topological constant by the Gauss-Bonnet theorem.

The historical origins of the Willmore energy are multifaceted: first introduced by Sophie Germain in the context of elasticity theory, where it models the bending energy of thin shells, it was studied by conformal geometer Blaschke and his students in an effort to combine minimal surfaces and conformal invariance (cf.
[
Ber1
]
and the references therein). It was popularised by Thomas Willmore
[
Wil
]
in the 1960s, and has since revealed itself as a key quantity appearing in numerous research papers coming from diverse areas of science and technology (see
[
Ber1
]
). Summarising such a vast topic in a few paragraphs is necessarily doomed to bias. The next section is no exception.

One foundational result due to Li and Yau
[
LY
]
establishes that for any compact immersed surface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the Willmore energy satisfies the lower bound

\mathcal{W}(\Sigma)\geq 4\pi,

with equality if and only if
\Sigma
is a round sphere. When the energy is strictly below
8\pi
, the immersion must be an embedding. These statements confirm the rigidity of minimal bending configurations and serves as the starting point for much of the modern analysis of the Willmore functional. The celebrated Willmore conjecture, posed in the 1960s and resolved in 2014 by Marques and Neves
[
MN
]
, asserts that any torus immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
must satisfy
\mathcal{W}(\Sigma)\geq 2\pi^{2}
, with equality uniquely for the stereographic image of the Clifford torus in
S^{3}
. Their resolution of the conjecture, using min-max theory in the Almgren–Pitts framework, marked a major milestone in geometric variational problems and introduced powerful new techniques for studying conformally invariant energies.

Beyond its variational significance, the Willmore functional serves as a bridge between extrinsic surface theory and deep structures in conformal geometry. In the setting of asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds, particularly those with Poincaré–Einstein metrics, the Willmore energy appears as a renormalised area of minimal surfaces asymptotic to a given boundary. This perspective, motivated by the AdS/CFT correspondence in theoretical physics, was developed by Graham and Witten
[
GW
]
, and reveals that the Willmore energy of a boundary surface
\Sigma\subset S^{3}
can be interpreted as the finite part in the asymptotic expansion of the area of a minimal surface in
\mathbb{H}^{4}
with boundary
\Sigma
. This realisation connects the Willmore functional to the renormalised volume of Einstein manifolds and embeds it into the broader framework of conformal invariants arising from geometric scattering theory and holographic correspondences.

The critical points of the Willmore energy – be they minimisers or not – are of course interesting in their own right. They are smooth immersions
\vec{\Phi}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfying a quasilinear elliptic equation of order four (for the immersion):

\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\vec{h}_{ij}-2|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\;=\;\vec{0}\>.

(I.1)

Here
\vec{h}
is the second fundamental form,
\Delta_{\perp}:=\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}
is the covariant Laplacian in the normal bundle, and
\pi_{\vec{n}}
denotes projection onto the normal space. Repeated indices are summed over using the induced metric
g_{ij}=\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}
. We note that minimal surfaces are examples.

Obtaining information about solutions to (
I.1
) is a particularly challenging task as compared to second-order elliptic problems more commonly studied in differential geometry. In codimension 1, Kuwert and Schätzle (see
inter alia

[
KS1
,
KS2
]
) used an “ambient approach” developed by Simon
[
Sim
]
. A different, “parametric” approach suitable in all codimensions was devised by Rivière in
[
Riv1
]
. That approach, relying on the existence of certain conservation laws, was later found
[
Ber1
]
to stem from Noether’s theorem. The idea consists in noting that (
I.1
) results from varying a translation-invariant energy and must therefore be in divergence form. In fact, one verifies directly using the Codazzi identity
\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{h}^{ij}=2\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}
:

\nabla_{i}\big(\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}+(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij}-|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij})\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\big)\;=\;\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\vec{h}_{ij}-2|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\>.

(I.2)

For a critical point, an application of the Poincaré lemma yields the existence of a 1-vector-valued 2-form
\vec{L}
satisfying

(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{i}\;=\;(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij}-|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij})\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}+\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}\>.

This identity can be contracted on
d\vec{\Phi}
in two different ways: using the scalar and wedge products in ambient space. Doing so yields

(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{i}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}\;=\;0\qquad\text{and}\qquad(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{i}\wedge\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}\;=\;\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}\>.

(I.3)

These equations were derived in

.

An interesting question is whether (

). The answer is “not quite” and is explained in
[
BR1
]
. When (
I.3
) holds for some 2-form

, then there exists a divergence-free, trace-free symmetric 2-tensor

such that

\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\vec{h}_{ij}-2|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\;=\;-T^{ij}\vec{h}_{ij}\>.

(I.4)

With
T=0
, we recover the Willmore equation. It is shown in
[
BR1
]
and
[

that parallel mean curvature surfaces, i.e. those satisfying
\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}=\vec{0}
for all

, are solutions of (

) with
T^{ij}=2\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{0}^{ij}
. In this expression,
\vec{h}_{0}:=\vec{h}-\vec{H}g
is the trace-free second fundamental form. Per Codazzi,
T
is indeed divergence-free when
\vec{H}
is parallel.

Although (
I.3
) is not quite equivalent to (
I.1
), it can nonetheless yield fruitful information. Note that

(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{i}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}\;=\;\nabla_{i}\big(\vec{L}^{ij}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\big)\>,

since
\vec{L}^{ij}\cdot\vec{h}_{ij}=0
. By the same token – and a few elementary manipulations – we can rewrite the second equation in (
I.3
). Altogether, we obtain

\nabla_{i}\big(\vec{L}^{ij}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\big)\;=\;0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla_{i}\big(\vec{L}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}-\vec{H}\wedge\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}\big)\;=\;\vec{0}\>.

Applying again the Poincaré lemma, we deduce the existence of a scalar-valued 2-form
S
and a 2-vector-valued 2-form
\vec{R}
satisfying

(d^{\star}S)^{i}\;=\;\vec{L}^{ij}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(d^{\star}\vec{R})^{i}\;=\;\vec{L}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}-\vec{H}\wedge\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}\>.

These expressions can be recast via defining the 0-forms
\vec{\ell}=\star\vec{L}
,
U=\star S
, and
\vec{V}=\star\vec{R}
. One finds

\nabla U\;=\;\vec{\ell}\cdot\nabla\vec{\Phi}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla\vec{V}\;=\;\vec{\ell}\wedge\nabla\vec{\Phi}-\vec{H}\wedge\nabla^{\perp}\vec{\Phi}\>,

where
\nabla^{\perp}
is the gradient rotated by 90 degrees.
For algebraic reasons, these two equations are in fact linked (see
[
Marq
]
for further elaboration on this point). Let
\vec{\eta}^{ij}:=\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}
. One verifies that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\nabla^{i}U&=&\vec{\eta}^{\,ij}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{V}\\[4.30554pt]

Of course, in dimension 2, we may always without loss of generality suppose that locally our induced metric is conformal:
g_{ij}=e^{2\lambda}\delta
, where

denotes the flat metric. In this setting, the Gauss map
\vec{n}
satisfies
\star\vec{n}=e^{-2\lambda}\partial_{x}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\partial_{y}\vec{\Phi}

\vec{\eta}

. In this notation, the system (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\nabla^{\perp}U&=&(\star\vec{n})\cdot\nabla\vec{V}\\[4.30554pt]

This remarkably symmetric and simple structure originally discovered by Rivière in

is the key that unlocks many analytical results and it has been exploited in numerous subsequent works, see
inter alia.

]
. However analytically amenable (

) might be, there remains nonetheless the question of linking the potentials
U
and
\vec{V}
back to geometric quantities. This was also done in
[

, namely:

2\Delta\vec{\Phi}\;=\;\text{div}(U\cdot\nabla\vec{\Phi}+\vec{V}\bullet\nabla\vec{\Phi})\>.

According to this narrative, in order to obtain analytical information for the mean curvature
\vec{H}
, one needs analytical information for the potentials

and
\vec{V}
. Such information is at reach upon differentiating (

). One uncovers a system of second-order elliptic PDEs with a Jacobian structure on the right-hand side. The good analytical dispositions of such a structure may be subsequently put to work, and with the help of this strategy, Rivière obtained energy estimates and several corollaries in
[
Riv1
]
. The conservation laws for
\vec{L}
,
S
, and
\vec{R}
can be recovered by evaluating the Noether fields associated with the translation, dilation, and rotation invariances of the Willmore energy
[

. Noether’s method has further proved its worth when used in numerous related contexts, see

.

I.2
Conformally invariant energies in dimension four

In dimension 2, the Riemann tensor is literally reduced to the shadow of itself: its double contraction
2K
. In dimension 4, the situation dramatically complicates as the Riemann tensor now comes into full force and can be neither recovered from its double trace (as in 2d), nor from its trace (as in 3d). There is still, of course, in dimension 4 a topological invariant given by the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet theorem. Namely, letting
Rm
,
Ric
, and
R
denote respectively the Riemann, Ricci, and scalar curvature tensors, the quantity

\int_{\Sigma}\big(|\text{Rm}|^{2}-4|\text{Ric}|^{2}+R^{2}\big)d\text{vol}_{g}\;=\;32\pi^{2}\chi(M)

is a topological invariant, with
\chi(M)
denoting the Euler characteristic. But there is yet another intrinsic quantity of interest: the Weyl tensor. It is the totally traceless part of the Riemann tensor and is given by
4
4

4

\owedge
stands for the Kulkarni-Nomizu product.

W\;=\;\text{Rm}-\dfrac{1}{2}P\owedge g\qquad\text{where}\quad P\;:=\;\text{Ric}-\dfrac{R}{6}g

is the Schouten tensor. The
(3,1)
-Weyl tensor has the property of being conformally invariant, and a celebrated result of Weyl states that a Riemannian manifold is locally conformally flat if and only if its Weyl tensor vanishes identically. Naturally,

\int_{\Sigma}|W|^{2}d\text{vol}_{g}

constitutes a conformally invariant intrinsic energy.

In the submanifold setting, the diversity of conformally invariant energies increases, if only due to combinatorics. Indeed, from the pointwise conformal invariant tensor
\vec{h}_{0}
one can construct four distinct conformally invariant energies with integrands

|\vec{h}_{0}|^{4}\quad,\quad\langle\vec{h}_{0}^{4}\rangle:=\big(\vec{h}_{0}^{ij}\cdot\vec{h}_{0}^{kl}\big)\big((\vec{h}_{0})_{ij}\cdot(\vec{h}_{0})_{kl}\big)

and

|h_{0}^{2}|^{2}:=\big((\vec{h}_{0})^{ik}\cdot(\vec{h}_{0})_{k}^{j}\big)\big((\vec{h}_{0})_{il}\cdot(\vec{h}_{0})_{j}^{l}\big)\quad,\quad\text{Tr}(\vec{h}_{0}^{4}):=\big((\vec{h}_{0})^{ij}\cdot(\vec{h}_{0})^{kl}\big)\big((\vec{h}_{0})_{il}\cdot(\vec{h}_{0})_{kj}\big)\>.

The extrinsic expression of the Riemann tensor is well-known:

\text{Rm}=\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\owedge}}\vec{h}\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad R^{ijkl}\;=\;\vec{h}^{ik}\cdot\vec{h}^{jl}-\vec{h}^{il}\cdot\vec{h}^{jk}\>.

This formulation yields that of the Weyl tensor in extrinsic terms:

W\;=\;\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{h}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\owedge}}\vec{h}_{0}-\dfrac{1}{2}(h_{0}^{2}\owedge g)-\dfrac{1}{6}|\vec{h}_{0}|^{2}g\owedge g\>,

where, for notational convenience
(h_{0}^{2})^{ij}:=\vec{h}_{0}^{ik}\cdot(\vec{h}_{0})_{k}^{j}
. From this expression, one deduces easily that

|W|^{2}\;=\;2\langle\vec{h}_{0}^{4}\rangle-2\text{Tr}_{g}\vec{h}_{0}^{4}-2|h_{0}^{2}|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}|\vec{h}_{0}|^{4}\>.

This confirms that
|W|^{2}
is a linear combination of the four conformally invariant building blocks identified above.

In codimension 1, the four energies coincide pairwise. In an effort to construct more, one could adopt the approach suggested in
[
MoN
]
and introduce such quantities as
\big(\text{Tr}(h_{0}^{3})\big)^{4/3}
. Although indeed conformally invariant, these quantities can only be defined in codimension 1. That is too strong a restriction, and one can instead favour a different approach, which consists in noting that

\int_{\Sigma}|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}d\text{vol}_{g}

scales correctly in dimension 4. It is not however a conformally invariant energy, but it can be modified to become one. Namely, the energy

\mathcal{E}_{A}\;:=\;\int_{\Sigma}\big(|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}-|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}+7|\vec{H}|^{4}\big)d\text{vol}_{g}

turns out to be conformally invariant! In codimension 1, this energy was first identified by Guven
[
Guv
]
. The author derives it from scratch by evaluating and cleverly recasting the defect of conformal invariance of the Dirichlet energy of mean curvature. Following different routes, other authors have arrived at the energy
\mathcal{E}_{A}
. Graham and Reichert
[
GR
]
on one hand, and Zhang
[
Zha
]
on the other, confirmed the validity of
\mathcal{E}_{A}
in all codimensions, using the correspondence between renormalised volume in Poincaré-Einstein metric and conformal invariants. Blitz, Gover, and Waldron
[
BGW
]
use instead tractor calculus and produce numerous conformal invariant energies, in particular
\mathcal{E}_{A}
. In some references,
\mathcal{E}_{A}
is rendered as
\mathcal{E}_{GR}
, for Graham-Reichert.

The Euler-Lagrange equation for
\mathcal{E}_{A}
is quite a handful. It is derived in Section
A.2.1
(see also
[
GR
]
):

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{W}}\;\;=\;\;\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{\perp}^{2}\vec{H}+\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{\perp}\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h},\vec{h}\rangle-7\Delta_{\perp}(|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H})+8\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\big(\vec{H}\nabla^{j}|\vec{H}|^{2}\big)+4\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\big((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{j}_{i})\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\>\Big[\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}-2\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H}+2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ik})(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{j}_{k})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\>\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h},\vec{h}\rangle\cdot\vec{h}^{ij}-7|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij}+\big(|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}-|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}+7|\vec{H}|^{4}\big)g^{ij}\Big]\vec{h}_{ij}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\;\;=\;\;\vec{0}\>.

(I.7)

The energy
\mathcal{E}_{A}
is, for many of its aspects, the analogue of the two-dimensional Willmore energy. Both are conformally invariant, both include minimal immersions as critical points, both have a quasilinear elliptic system (of order 4 in the immersion for 2d Willmore, and of order 6 in the four-dimensional setting). However, unlike the Willmore energy,
\mathcal{E}_{A}
is not bounded from below. Recent works have shown that minimisers of
\mathcal{E}_{A}
are hard to come by. Martino
[
Mart
]
proves that
\mathcal{E}_{A}
is unbounded from below on closed hypersurfaces. In an effort to thwart this annoying feature, one may add some other lower-order conformally invariant energy. For example, as is elementary to check
[
Vya
]
, the energy
\mathcal{E}_{A}+\beta\int|\vec{h}_{0}|^{4}
is bounded below by
8\pi^{2}
for
\beta>\frac{1}{12}
. In the recent work
[
WY
]
, the authors establish a connected sum energy reduction for the energy
\mathcal{E}_{A}
.

It takes little imagination to accept that
\mathcal{E}_{A}
is not the only conformally invariant energy of its kind and that there might at least be another energy involving this time

\int_{\Sigma}|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{h}|^{2}d\text{vol}_{g}\>.

An analogue of the following statement appears in
[
BGW
]
as well as in
[
Mart
]
.

Proposition I.1

The energy

\mathcal{E}_{C}\;:=\;\int_{\Sigma}\big(|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{h}|^{2}-12|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}+6|\vec{H}|^{2}|\vec{h}|^{2}+60|\vec{H}|^{4}\big)d\text{vol}_{g}

is conformally invariant.

\hfill\square

As we will see in Section
II.1
, the proof of this statement is a simple application of Simons’ identity. In particular, we will obtain that

16\mathcal{E}_{A}-\mathcal{E}_{C}+\int_{\Sigma}\big(3|h_{0}^{2}|^{2}-\langle\vec{h}_{0}^{4}\rangle\big)d\text{vol}_{g}\;=\;16\pi^{2}\chi(\Sigma)-\dfrac{3}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|W|^{2}d\text{vol}_{g}\>.

(I.8)

The left-hand side appears extrinsic, but is in reality intrinsic.

In this paper, we will be interested in linear combinations of
\mathcal{E}_{A}
,
\mathcal{E}_{C}
, and any linear combination of the four conformally invariant energies of order
\mathcal{O}(|\vec{h}_{0}|^{4})
, which we generically denote by
\mathcal{E}_{0}
. We of course expound any topological invariant from the energy so as not to be distracted by their ghost Noether fields (which are complicated looking zeros). Owing to (
I.8
), we will thus be dealing with energies of so-called “
\mathcal{E}_{A}
-type”:

\mathcal{E}\;=\;\mathcal{E}_{A}+\mathcal{E}_{0}\>.

(I.9)

From the point of view of Noether’s fields and underlying structures, we will see that critical points of energies of the type (
I.9
) satisfy analogous equations to those found in the 2d Willmore problem. In particular, they satisfy a system of quasilinear elliptic equations of order six in the immersion.
A contrario
, energies of the type
\mathcal{E}_{0}
satisfy a fully nonlinear system of equations whose study demands completely different methods. Of course, the most important representative of the
\mathcal{E}_{0}
family is the intrinsic Bach energy
\int|W|^{2}
. Its critical points – when varying the metric – satisfy the Bach equation:

B^{ab}\;:=\;\Delta P^{ab}-\nabla_{c}\nabla^{a}P^{cb}+P_{ij}W^{aibj}\;=\;0\>.

Exact solutions of the Bach equation include locally conformally flat spaces (for which
W=0
), Einstein manifolds, as well as self-dual and anti-self-dual manifolds (with
W=\pm\star W
). Analytical properties of Bach-flat manifolds have been considered in the literature, e.g. in
[
TV
,
LM
]
. They will not be discussed in the present work. We content ourselves with recording a basic fact for our future use: the Bach tensor is a TT-tensor; it is divergence-free and trace-free.

I.3
Equation structures of
\mathcal{E}_{A}
-type energies

We study in this section energies of the type
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{A}+\mathcal{E}_{0}
, where
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is any linear combination of the four conformally invariant energies of order
\mathcal{O}(|\vec{h}_{0}|^{4})
. We will closely follow the ideas summarised in section
I.1
. As the Euler-Lagrange equation is quite unmanageable as written in (
I.2
), the first thing to do is to express it in divergence-form. This can be accomplished by evaluating the Noether field corresponding to translation invariance. More precisely, in Section
A.2
, we show

Theorem I.1

A critical point of
\mathcal{E}
satisfies the conservation law

d^{\star}\vec{V}\;=\;\vec{\mathcal{W}}\;=\;\vec{0}\>.

The vector-field
\vec{V}
is given by

\vec{V}^{j}\;=\;G^{ij}\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}+\vec{C}^{j}\>.

In this expression
5
5

5

denotes projection on the normal subspace.
, we have set

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}G^{ij}&=&\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij}-2\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}+\mathcal{O}(|\vec{h}|^{4})\\[4.30554pt]
\vec{F}^{ij}&=&-\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\,g^{ij}+\mathcal{O}(|\vec{h}|^{3})\\[4.30554pt]

As expected, the leading-order term in the Euler-Lagrange equation is linear and proportional to
\Delta_{\perp}^{2}\vec{H}

Integrating the equation
d^{\star}\vec{V}=\vec{0}
once yields the existence of a 1-vector-valued 2-form
\vec{L}
satisfying
d^{\star}\vec{L}=\vec{V}
. Contracting
d^{\star}\vec{L}
on

yields a pair of equations, henceforth referred to as the “

If
\vec{\Phi}
is a critical point of the energy
\mathcal{E}
, there exists a 1-vector-valued 2-form
\vec{L}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{j}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}&=&G^{i}_{i}\;\;=\;\;\Delta|\vec{H}|^{2}\\[4.30554pt]

(I.10)

\hfill\square

This system is not quite equivalent to the original Euler-Lagrange equation, as the following statement outlines.

Theorem I.3

There exists a 1-vector-valued 2-form
\vec{L}
satisfying (
I.10
) if and only if there exists a divergence-free, trace-free, symmetric 2-tensor
T
such that

\vec{\mathcal{W}}\;=\;-\langle T,\vec{h}\rangle\>.

(I.11)

\hfill\square

The case
T=0
corresponds of course to critical points of
\mathcal{E}
. In the presence of the TT tensor
T
however, the Euler-Lagrange shows a reaction term. Such tensors are hard to come by in general, but in dimension 4, we have encountered at the end of the previous section the Bach tensor
B

Theorem I.4

The equation

\vec{\mathcal{W}}\;=\;-\langle B,\vec{h}\rangle\>,

(I.12)

is the Euler-Lagrange equation of
\mathcal{E}
with constrained Bach energy
\int|W|^{2}
.

\hfill\square

As the Bach energy is conformally invariant, solutions of (

\mathcal{E}
. I have not been able to produce any explicit solution for this equation. In
[

, it is shown that limit of Palais-Smale sequences satisfy (

) in the sense of distributions, but I do not know any explicit circumstance in which (
I.11
) is satisfied. Naive choices, such as parallel mean curvature submanifolds, i.e. with

for all

I.11
). Another choice imposes the Chen biharmonic condition
\Delta\vec{H}=\vec{0}
. Chen biharmonic submanifolds have been extensively considered in the literature, notably in the context of the Chen conjecture stating that
\Delta\vec{H}=\vec{0}
is equivalent to
\vec{H}=\vec{0}
.
[

. An elementary computation gives that

\Delta\vec{H}\;=\;\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}-(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\vec{h}_{ij}+2\nabla_{i}\big(|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij}-\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij}\big)\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\>.

For a biharmonic submanifold, the tensor
T^{ij}:=|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij}-\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij}
is thus a TT-tensor, but it does not have the right order to serve in (
I.11
). Devising a suitable nontrivial example of a solution to (
I.11
) remains unclear to me. In
[

, it is shown that a local Palais-Smale sequence for the energy

converges to a solution of (
I.11
) for some
TT
tensor
T
.

\nabla_{i}\big(\vec{L}^{ij}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}-\nabla^{i}|\vec{H}|^{2}\big)\;=\;0\>.

This equation can be integrated once and yield per the Poincaré lemma a 2-form

nor

II
Proofs of the Theorems

We recall once and for all the well-known expressions
[
KN
]
of the Riemann, Ricci, and scalar curvature tensors for a submanifold:

R^{ijkl}\;=\;\vec{h}^{ik}\cdot\vec{h}^{jl}-\vec{h}^{il}\cdot\vec{h}^{jk}\quad,\quad R^{ik}\;=\;4\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ik}-\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\vec{h}^{k}_{j}\quad,\quad R\;=\;16|\vec{H}|^{2}-|\vec{h}|^{2}\>.

Moreover, we recall the classical expression

|W|^{2}\;=\;|\text{Rm}|^{2}-2|\text{Ric}|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}R^{2}\>.

II.1
Conformally invariant energies: proof of Proposition
I.1

We denote by
(h^{\alpha})^{ij}
the normal component of
\vec{h}^{ij}
, with
\alpha\in\{1,\ldots,\text{codim}\}
. Moreover, for convenience, we let

(h^{2})^{ij}\;:=\;\vec{h}^{ik}\cdot\vec{h}_{k}^{j}\>.

Also, we let

\langle\vec{h}^{4}\rangle\;:=\;(\vec{h}^{ik}\cdot\vec{h}^{jl})(\vec{h}_{ik}\cdot\vec{h}_{jl})\>.

A classical corollary of Simons’ identity (see e.g.
[
Smo
]
) asserts that

\displaystyle 8\langle\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{,}}\nabla^{2}\vec{H}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta|\vec{h}|^{2}-2|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{h}|^{2}+|\text{Rm}|^{2}+2|\text{Ric}|^{2}-32|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}

(II.1)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\>\big|(h^{\alpha})^{ik}(h^{\beta})^{j}_{k}-(h^{\beta})^{il}(h^{\alpha})^{j}_{l}\big|^{2}\>.

The last term is expanded as

\displaystyle\big|(h^{\alpha})^{ik}(h^{\beta})^{j}_{k}-(h^{\beta})^{il}(h^{\alpha})^{j}_{l}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|h^{2}|^{2}-2(\vec{h}_{ik}\cdot\vec{h}_{jl})(\vec{h}^{jk}\cdot\vec{h}^{il})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|h^{2}|^{2}-2(\vec{h}_{ik}\cdot\vec{h}_{jl})R^{jikl}-2\langle\vec{h}^{4}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|h^{2}|^{2}+|\text{Rm}|^{2}-2\langle\vec{h}^{4}\rangle\>.

Brought into (
II.1
), the latter yields

8\langle\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{,}}\nabla^{2}\vec{H}\rangle\;=\;\Delta|\vec{h}|^{2}-2|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{h}|^{2}+2|\text{Rm}|^{2}+2|\text{Ric}|^{2}-32|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}+2|h^{2}|^{2}-2\langle\vec{h}^{4}\rangle\>.

(II.2)

Note that the left-hand side may be recast as

\displaystyle 8\langle\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{,}}\nabla^{2}\vec{H}\rangle\;=\;8\nabla_{ij}\big(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij}-4|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij}\big)+16\Delta|\vec{H}|^{2}-32|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}-8\langle\vec{H}\cdot h^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{,}}\vec{h}\rangle\>.

Hence now, recalling that
R=16|\vec{H}|^{2}-|\vec{h}|^{2}
,

\displaystyle 8\nabla_{ij}\big(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij}-4|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij}\big)+\Delta R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 32|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}-2|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{h}|^{2}+2|\text{Rm}|^{2}+2|\text{Ric}|^{2}-32|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}

(II.3)

\displaystyle+\>2|h^{2}|^{2}+8\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{,}}h^{2}\rangle-2\langle\vec{h}^{4}\rangle\>.

In order to simplify this expression, we first note that

\displaystyle(h^{2})^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{h}^{ik}\cdot\vec{h}_{k}^{j}\;\;=\;\;(\vec{h}_{0}^{ik}+g^{ik}\vec{H})\cdot((\vec{h}_{0})_{k}^{j}+g_{k}^{j}\vec{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h_{0}^{2})^{ij}+2\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij}-|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij}\>.

Hence

\displaystyle|h^{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|h_{0}^{2}|^{2}+4|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}-12|\vec{H}|^{4}-2|\vec{H}|^{2}|\vec{h}_{0}|^{2}+4\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{,}}\vec{h}_{0}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|h_{0}^{2}|^{2}+4|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}-12|\vec{H}|^{4}-2|\vec{H}|^{2}(|\vec{h}|^{2}-4|\vec{H}|^{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\>4\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{,}}h^{2}-2\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}+|\vec{H}|^{2}g\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|h_{0}^{2}|^{2}-4|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}-2|\vec{H}|^{2}|\vec{h}|^{2}+4\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{,}}h^{2}\rangle+12|\vec{H}|^{4}\>,

so that

\displaystyle 4|\text{Ric}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4|h^{2}|^{2}-32\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{,}}h^{2}\rangle+64|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 6|h^{2}|^{2}-2|h^{2}|^{2}-32\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{,}}h^{2}\rangle+64|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,2|h^{2}|^{2}-8\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{,}}h^{2}\rangle+6|h_{0}^{2}|^{2}+40|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}-12|\vec{H}|^{2}|\vec{h}|^{2}+72|\vec{H}|^{4}\>.

Introduced into (
II.3
), the latter shows that

\displaystyle 8\nabla_{ij}\big(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij}-4|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij}\big)+\Delta R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 32|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}-2|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{h}|^{2}+2|\text{Rm}|^{2}-2|\text{Ric}|^{2}+6|h_{0}^{2}|^{2}

(II.4)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\,8|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}-12|\vec{H}|^{2}|\vec{h}|^{2}+72|\vec{H}|^{4}-2\langle\vec{h}^{4}\rangle\>.

To further simplify, we compute

\displaystyle\vec{h}^{ik}\cdot\vec{h}^{jl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{h}_{0}^{ik}\cdot\vec{h}_{0}^{jl}+\vec{H}\cdot(\vec{h}^{jl}g^{ik}+\vec{h}^{ik}g^{jl})-|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ik}g^{jl}\>,

from which it easily follows that

\displaystyle\langle\vec{h}^{4}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\vec{h}_{0}^{4}\rangle+8|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}-16|\vec{H}|^{4}\>.

Introducing this into (
II.4
) gives

\displaystyle 8\nabla_{ij}\big(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij}-4|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij}\big)+\Delta R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 32|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}-2|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{h}|^{2}+2|\text{Rm}|^{2}-2|\text{Ric}|^{2}

(II.5)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\>8|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}-12|\vec{H}|^{2}|\vec{h}|^{2}+104|\vec{H}|^{4}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\>6|h_{0}^{2}|^{2}-2\langle\vec{h}_{0}^{4}\rangle\>.

We note next that

\displaystyle 2|\text{Rm}|^{2}-2|\text{Ric}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\Big(|\text{Rm}|^{2}-2|\text{Ric}|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}R^{2}\Big)-\big(|\text{Rm}|^{2}-4|\text{Ric}|^{2}+R^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3|\text{W}|^{2}-c(\Sigma)\>,

where
c(\Sigma)
denotes the integrand in the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet topological invariant. The latter is now injected into (
II.5
) to yield

\displaystyle 8\nabla_{ij}\big(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij}-4|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij}\big)+\Delta R+c(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt=\>32\big(|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}-|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}+7|\vec{H}|^{4})

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-\>2\big(|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{h}|^{2}-12|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}+6|\vec{H}|^{2}|\vec{h}|^{2}+60|\vec{H}|^{4}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\>6|h_{0}^{2}|^{2}-2\langle\vec{h}_{0}^{4}\rangle+3|W|^{2}\>.

(II.6)

Therefore, upon integration over a closed manifold
\Sigma
without boundary, we arrive at

16\mathcal{E}_{A}-\mathcal{E}_{C}+\mathcal{E}_{0}\;=\;16\pi^{2}\chi(\Sigma)-\dfrac{3}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|W|^{2}d\text{vol}_{g}\>,

where

\mathcal{E}_{0}\;:=\;\int_{\Sigma}\big(3|h_{0}^{2}|^{2}-\langle\vec{h}_{0}^{4}\rangle\big)d\text{vol}_{g}\>,

and

\mathcal{E}_{C}\;:=\;\int_{\Sigma}\big(|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{h}|^{2}-12|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}+6|\vec{H}|^{2}|\vec{h}|^{2}+60|\vec{H}|^{4}\big)d\text{vol}_{g}\>.

As
\mathcal{E}_{A}
,
\int|W|^{2}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{0}
are all conformally invariant, it follows that
\mathcal{E}_{C}
is likewise conformally invariant. This is the claim of Proposition
I.1
.

II.2
The first conservation law: proof of Theorem
I.1

Consider a conformally invariant energy of the form

\mathcal{E}\;=\;\mathcal{E}_{A}+\mathcal{E}_{0}\>,

where
\mathcal{E}_{A}
is as in the previous section, while
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is any energy with integrand of order
\mathcal{O}(|\vec{h}_{0}|^{4})
, see Subsection
A.2.3
. As the Euler-Lagrange equation is quite unmanageable as written in (

), the first thing to do is to express it in divergence-form. This can be accomplished by evaluating the Noether field corresponding to translation invariance. Per the considerations in Section
A.2
, a critical point of
\mathcal{E}
satisfies a divergence-form equation

d^{\star}\vec{V}\;=\;\vec{0}\>,

where the 1-vector-valued 1-form
\vec{V}
satisfies

\vec{V}^{j}\;=\;G^{ij}\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}+\vec{C}^{j}\>.

(II.7)

Exact expressions for
G
,
\vec{F}
, and
\vec{C}
can be found in the Appendix. For the moment, we record that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}G^{ij}&=&\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij}-2\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}+\mathcal{O}(|\vec{h}|^{4})\\[4.30554pt]
\vec{F}^{ij}&=&-\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\,g^{ij}+\mathcal{O}(|\vec{h}|^{3})\\[4.30554pt]
\vec{C}^{j}&=&-2(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{H})\vec{H}+2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\>.\end{array}\right.

(II.8)

Since

-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\;=\;-\nabla^{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+\pi_{T}\nabla^{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\;=\;-\nabla^{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}-\big(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\big)\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}\>,

we can recast (
II.7
) in the form

\vec{V}^{j}\;=\;\dfrac{1}{2}\nabla^{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+G_{1}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}_{1}^{ij}+\mathcal{O}\big(|\vec{h}|^{2}|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|\big)\>,

(II.9)

with

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}G_{1}^{ij}&=&\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij}-2\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}+\mathcal{O}(|\vec{h}|^{4})\\[4.30554pt]
\vec{F}_{1}^{ij}&=&\mathcal{O}(|\vec{h}|^{3})\>.\end{array}\right.

(II.10)

A simple application of the Codazzi identity gives

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\nabla^{k}\vec{H})-\nabla^{i}(\vec{h}^{kj}\cdot\nabla_{k}\vec{H})+\nabla_{k}(\vec{h}^{kj}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+\pi_{\vec{n}}(\nabla^{k}\vec{h}^{ij}-\nabla^{i}\vec{h}^{kj})\cdot\nabla_{k}\vec{H}+4\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}+\vec{h}^{j}_{k}\cdot\big(\nabla^{k}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}-\nabla^{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{k}\vec{H}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+\mathcal{O}\big(|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}\big)-\vec{h}^{j}_{k}\cdot\big(\nabla^{k}\pi_{T}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}-\nabla^{i}\pi_{T}\nabla^{k}\vec{H}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+\mathcal{O}\big(|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}\big)+\vec{h}^{j}_{k}\cdot\Big(\nabla^{k}\big((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\big)-\nabla^{i}\big((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{kj})\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+\mathcal{O}\big(|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}+|\vec{h}|^{4}\big)\>.

Accordingly,

\displaystyle(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H})\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}\big((\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\nabla^{k}\vec{H}+\vec{h}^{kj}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{H})\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}-(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{H})\nabla^{k}\vec{\Phi}\big)+\mathcal{O}\big(|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}+|\vec{h}|^{4}\big)\>.

Introducing this into (
II.9
) and using the fact that
\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}_{1}^{ij}=\nabla_{i}\vec{F}_{1}^{ij}+\mathcal{O}(|\vec{F}_{1}||\vec{h}|)
, since
\vec{F}_{1}
is normal valued, we arrive at

\vec{V}^{j}\;=\;\dfrac{1}{2}\nabla^{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+\nabla_{k}A^{kj}+\mathcal{O}\big(|\vec{h}|^{2}|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|+|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}+|\vec{h}|^{4}\big)\>,

with

This completes the proof of Theorem

.

Let us return to (

Proposition II.1

G

(ii)

(iii)

The first item is Remark

\hfill\blacksquare

From the Poincaré Lemma, we know there exists a 1-vector-valued 2-form

(II.11)

Using (i) from Proposition

(II.12)

We suppose that some form

satisfies the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}(d^{\star}\vec{L}_{0})^{j}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}&=&d^{\star}d|\vec{H}|^{2}\\[4.30554pt]

(II.14)

Let us first define the 2-form
\vec{L}:=\vec{L}_{0}-\dfrac{1}{3}d(|\vec{H}|^{2}d\vec{\Phi})
, so that

\displaystyle(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{j}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d^{\star}\vec{L}_{0})^{j}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}-\dfrac{1}{3}\nabla_{i}\big(\nabla^{i}|\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}-\nabla^{j}|\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi})\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle\Delta|\vec{H}|^{2}-\Delta|\vec{H}|^{2}\;\;=\;\;0\>.

Moreover, using the Codazzi identity:

\displaystyle(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{j}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d^{\star}\vec{L}_{0})^{j}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}-\dfrac{1}{3}\nabla_{i}\big(\nabla^{i}|\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}-\nabla^{j}|\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi})\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d^{\star}\vec{L}_{0})^{j}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}-\dfrac{1}{3}\big(\vec{h}^{j}_{i}\nabla^{i}|\vec{H}|^{2}-4\vec{H}\nabla^{j}|\vec{H}|^{2})\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(\ref{L2})}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}+\vec{C}^{j})\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}-\dfrac{1}{3}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{f}^{\,ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\>,

where

\vec{f}^{\,ij}\;:=\;|\vec{H}|^{2}\big(\vec{h}^{ij}-4\vec{H}g^{ij}\big)\>.

(II.17)

Equations (
II.15
)-(
II.16
) help us recast (
II.14
) in the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{j}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}&=&0\\[4.30554pt]

(II.18)

where for notational convenience we have set

\vec{\mathcal{F}}\;:=\;\vec{F}+\dfrac{1}{3}\vec{f}\>.

It is to be noted that
\vec{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies (iii) from Proposition
II.1
. That is rather plain to see, since clearly
\vec{f}^{\,ij}\wedge\vec{h}_{ij}=\vec{0}
. However,
\vec{\mathcal{F}}
fails to satisfy (ii). Indeed, since
\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}(\vec{h}^{ij}-4\vec{H}g^{ij})=\vec{0}
by Codazzi, it comes

\displaystyle\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{f}^{\,ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot(\vec{h}^{ij}-4\vec{H}g^{ij})\nabla_{i}|\vec{H}|^{2}\;=\;-R^{mi}\nabla_{i}|\vec{H}|^{2}\>,

where the reader is invited to recall the extrinsic expression of the Ricci tensor
R^{im}
. From this observation and Proposition
II.1
, we see that

\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\big(-\nabla_{i}\vec{\mathcal{F}}^{ij}+\vec{C}^{j}\big)\;=\;\nabla_{i}G^{im}+\dfrac{1}{3}R^{im}\nabla_{i}|\vec{H}|^{2}\>.

(II.19)

Define next the symmetric two-tensor

a^{im}\;:=\;\nabla^{im}|\vec{H}|^{2}-g^{im}\Delta|\vec{H}|^{2}\>.

We note that

\nabla_{i}a^{im}\;=\;(\nabla^{ij}-\nabla^{ji})\nabla_{i}|\vec{H}|^{2}\;=\;R^{im}\nabla_{i}|\vec{H}|^{2}\>.

Combining this to (
II.19
), it comes

\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\big(-\nabla_{i}\vec{\mathcal{F}}^{ij}+\vec{C}^{j}\big)\;=\;\nabla_{j}\Big(G^{jm}+\dfrac{1}{3}a^{jm}\Big)\>.

(II.20)

We next introduce the projection decomposition
(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{j}=:A^{ji}\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}+\vec{B}^{j}
, where
\vec{B}
is normal-valued. Introducing this into (
II.18
), we find

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}A^{i}_{i}&=&0\\[4.30554pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}(A^{ji}-A^{ij})\vec{\eta}_{ij}+\vec{B}^{j}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}&=&-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{\mathcal{F}}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}+\vec{C}^{j}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\>.\end{array}\right.

In other words,
A
is symmetric and traceless, while the normal part satisfies

\vec{B}^{j}\;=\;-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{\mathcal{F}}^{ij}+\vec{C}^{j}\>.

Note that the tangential projection satisfies

\vec{0}\;=\;\pi_{T}d^{\star}d^{\star}\vec{L}\;=\;\pi_{T}\nabla_{j}(A^{ji}\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}+\vec{B}^{j})\;=\;\big(\nabla_{j}A^{ij}-\vec{B}^{j}\cdot\vec{h}_{j}^{i}\big)\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}\>,

where we have used that
\vec{B}
is normal-valued. It follows that

\nabla_{j}A^{jm}\;=\;\vec{B}^{j}\cdot\vec{h}_{j}^{m}\;=\;\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\big(-\nabla_{i}\vec{\mathcal{F}}^{ij}+\vec{C}^{j}\big)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(\ref{eqq7})}}}{{=}}\;\nabla_{j}\Big(G^{jm}+\dfrac{1}{3}a^{jm}\Big)\>.

Accordingly, the symmetric 2-tensor
T:=A-G-\frac{1}{3}a
is divergence-free. Moreover, from (i) in Proposition
II.1
and the tracelessness of
A
, we easily verify that
T
is also traceless, thereby making
T
into a traceless transverse tensor (abbreviated
TT-tensor
).

Summarising our findings so far, it holds

\displaystyle(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(T^{ij}+G^{ij}+\dfrac{1}{3}a^{ij}\Big)\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{k}\vec{\mathcal{F}}^{kj}+\vec{C}^{j}

(II.21)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(\ref{defV})}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\vec{V}^{j}+\Big(T^{ij}+\dfrac{1}{3}a^{ij}\Big)\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}-\dfrac{1}{3}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{f}^{\,ij}\>.

To further proceed, we need the following

Lemma II.1

We have

\nabla_{i}\big(a^{ij}\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{f}^{\,ij}\big)\;=\;\vec{0}\>.

Proof.
We have already observed that

\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{f}^{\,ij}\;=\;(\vec{h}^{ij}-4\vec{H}g^{ij})\nabla_{j}|\vec{H}|^{2}\>,

(II.22)

so that, recycling some of our previous derivations, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{f}^{\,ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\vec{h}^{ij}-4\vec{H}g^{ij})\nabla_{ij}|\vec{H}|^{2}-(\vec{h}^{ij}-4\vec{H}g^{ij})\cdot\vec{h}_{i}^{k}\nabla_{j}|\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla_{k}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\vec{h}^{ij}-4\vec{H}g^{ij})\nabla_{ij}|\vec{H}|^{2}+R^{ji}\nabla_{j}|\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{ij}\vec{h}^{ij}+\nabla_{j}a^{ij}\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}(a^{ij}\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi})\>.

This confirms that the vector-field
a^{ij}\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{f}^{\,ij}
is co-closed.

\hfill\blacksquare

Using this lemma and the fact that
T
is divergence-free we may now apply
d^{\star}
to both sides of (
II.21
) so as to arrive at

\vec{0}\;=\;\nabla_{j}\vec{V}^{j}+\langle T,\vec{h}\rangle\>.

In other words, whereas critical points of
\mathcal{E}
satisfy
d^{\star}\vec{V}=\vec{0}
and the
\vec{L}
-system, if only the
\vec{L}
-system is satisfied, then the perturbed equation
d^{\star}\vec{V}=-\langle T,\vec{h}\rangle
holds for some TT-tensor
T
. Conversely, if the equation
d^{\star}\vec{V}=-\langle T,\vec{h}\rangle
holds for some TT-tensor
T
, noting that

\langle T,\vec{h}\rangle\;=\;d^{\star}(T^{ij}\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi})\>,

we deduce the existence of a two-form
\vec{L}
such that

(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{j}\;=\;\vec{V}^{j}+T^{ij}\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}\>.

Since
T
is a symmetric
TT
-tensor, it immediately follows that

(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{j}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\;=\;\vec{V}^{j}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\;=\;G^{i}_{i}\>,

as well as

(d^{\star}\vec{L})^{j}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\;=\;\vec{V}^{j}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\;=\;-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}+\vec{C}^{j}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\>.

Therefore, we recover the
\vec{L}
-system in that case. We are of course free to demand that
\vec{L}
be closed. This completes the proof of Theorem
I.3
.

As an example of the
\vec{L}
-system, we consider the case when
T
is chosen to be the Bach tensor
B
. We claim that the Euler-Lagrange equation corresponds to varying
\mathcal{E}
while constraining the Bach energy
\int|W|^{2}
. To verify this, we need to compute the Noether field associated with the Bach energy (understood extrinsically). This is done in the Appendix, and one verifies that

\vec{V}^{b}_{|W|^{2}}\;=\;-4P_{ij}W^{biaj}\nabla_{a}\vec{\Phi}-8\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\big(\vec{h}_{aj}W^{abji}\big)\>.

(II.23)

For the next result, we need to recall the Cotton form

\vec{\ell}^{ab}\;:=\;C^{abc}\nabla_{c}\vec{\Phi}\;\equiv\;(\nabla^{a}P^{bc}-\nabla^{b}P^{ac})\nabla_{c}\vec{\Phi}\;\equiv\;2\nabla_{i}W^{icab}\nabla_{c}\vec{\Phi}\>.

Using the Cotton-Weyl equation as well as the symmetries of the Weyl tensor and the Codazzi equation gives easily

\displaystyle(d^{\star}\vec{\ell})^{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}C^{abc}\nabla_{c}\vec{\Phi}+C^{abc}\vec{h}_{ca}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(B^{bc}-P_{ij}W^{bicj}\big)\nabla_{c}\vec{\Phi}+2\vec{h}_{ca}\nabla_{i}W^{icab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(B^{ba}-P_{ij}W^{biaj}\big)\nabla_{a}\vec{\Phi}-2\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\big(\vec{h}_{ja}W^{abji}\big)\>.

Combined with (
II.23

(II.24)

As the Bach tensor is divergence-free, it follows that extrinsic critical points of the Bach energy satisfy

\dfrac{1}{4}d^{\star}\vec{V}_{|W|^{2}}\;=\;-\langle B,\vec{h}\rangle\>.

In particular, when the variation of
\mathcal{E}
is done with the Bach energy held fixed, then one reaches the perturbed equation

\vec{\mathcal{W}}\;=\;-\langle B,\vec{h}\rangle\>.

This is the content of Theorem
I.4

A.1
Notational Conventions

We fix a basis
\{e^{i}\}_{i=1,\cdots,N}
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and an orientation. Suppose
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is equipped with a metric
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the corresponding vector space of
k
-forms. We will need component expansions of certain operations involving forms. A typical element
A\in\Lambda^{p}
may be expanded in tensorial notation as

A\;=\;\dfrac{1}{p!}\sum_{\text{all indices}}A_{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}\,e^{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e^{i_{p}}\>,

where
A_{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}
is totally antisymmetric. For notational convenience, we will render this as

A\;\equiv\;A_{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}\>.

Forms are tensors: we raise and lower indices as usual using the metric
\mathfrak{g}
. Of course, as forms are anti-symmetric, the relative position of indices is essential: we follow standard conventions.

The inner product on forms will be denoted

\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}\;=\;\dfrac{1}{p!}A^{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}B_{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}\qquad\text{for}\quad A\in\Lambda^{p}\>\>,\>\>B\in\Lambda^{p}\>.

The bracket symbol
[\ldots]
will denote
scaled
total permutations. We conventionally set the wedge product to be

A\wedge B\;\equiv\;\dfrac{(p+q)!}{p!q!}A_{[i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}B_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}]}\qquad\text{for}\quad A\in\Lambda^{p}\>\>,\>\>B\in\Lambda^{q}\>.

The
interior multiplication
(restricted to our interest) is the bilinear operation
\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\;:\;\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times\Lambda^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\Lambda^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
which satisfies in particular

A\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B\;\equiv\;\dfrac{1}{(p-1)!}A^{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}B_{i_{1}\ldots i_{p-1}}\qquad\text{for}\quad A\in\Lambda^{p}\>\>,\>\>B\in\Lambda^{p-1}\>.

The
first-order contraction operator

\bullet:\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times\Lambda^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\Lambda^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as follows. For a
2
-form
{A}
and a
1
-form
{B}
, we set

{A}\bullet{B}:={A}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits B\>.

For a
p
-form
{B}
and a
q
-form
{C}
, we have

{A}\bullet({B}\wedge{C}):=({A}\bullet{B})\wedge{C}+(-1)^{pq}({A}\bullet{C})\wedge{B}\>.

This operation will be used for forms with values in
\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m\geq 5})
.

We will also need an operation taking a pair a two-forms to a two-form, namely:

A\odot B\;\equiv\;A^{i_{2}j}B_{j}^{\>\>i_{1}}-A^{i_{1}j}B_{j}^{\>\>i_{2}}\qquad\text{for}\quad A,B\in\Lambda^{2}\>.

Being given an orientation and a metric, we can equip our manifold with the volume form
\epsilon\in\Lambda^{N}
via
\epsilon^{i_{1}\ldots i_{N}}:=|\mathfrak{g}|^{-1/2}\mbox{sign}\begin{pmatrix}1&2&\cdots&N\\
i_{1}&i_{2}&\cdots&i_{N}\end{pmatrix}
. With it, we define the Hodge star operator
\star_{\mathfrak{g}}:\Lambda^{p}\rightarrow\Lambda^{N-p}
as usual. In components, we have

\star_{\mathfrak{g}}A\;\equiv\;\dfrac{1}{p!}\,\epsilon^{i_{1}\ldots i_{N-p}j_{1}\ldots j_{p}}A_{j_{1}\ldots j_{p}}\qquad\text{for}\quad A\in\Lambda^{p}\>.

Note that

\star_{\mathfrak{g}}\star_{\mathfrak{g}}A=(-1)^{p(N-p)}A\>.

If a connection
\nabla
compatible with the metric
\mathfrak{g}
is provided, we define in accordance with the Leibniz rule, the covariant exterior derivative
d
of a
p
-form
A
in components as

dA\;:=\;(p+1)\nabla_{[i_{0}}A_{i_{1}...i_{p}]}\;\in\;\Lambda^{p+1}\>.

We also define the codifferential
d^{\star_{\mathfrak{g}}}:=\star_{\mathfrak{g}}d\star_{\mathfrak{g}}
, whose components take the expression

d^{\star_{\mathfrak{g}}}A=\nabla^{j}A_{ji_{1}...i_{p-1}}\;\in\;\Lambda^{p-1}\>.

For any
p
-form
A
, it holds

d^{2}A\;=\;0\;=\;(d^{\star_{\mathfrak{g}}})^{2}A\>.

We will work within the context of multivector-valued forms. Our objects will be elements of
\Lambda^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4},\Lambda^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))
. To distinguish operations taking place in the parameter space
(\mathbb{R}^{4},g)
from operations taking place in the ambient space
(\mathbb{R}^{m},g_{\text{Eucl}})
, we will superimpose the operators defined above with the convention that the one on the bottom acts on parameter space, while the one at the top acts on ambient space.
Typically, consider the two-vector-valued two-form
\vec{\eta}\in\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4},\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m}))
whose components are given by

\vec{\eta}_{ij}\;=\;\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\>.

We will write more succinctly

\vec{\eta}\;=:\;\dfrac{1}{2}d\vec{\Phi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{\wedge}}d\vec{\Phi}\>.

Similar quantities are defined following the same logic. Naturally, for multivector 0-forms, the bottom symbol will be omitted, while for 0-vector-valued forms, the symbol on the top will be omitted instead. The notation
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
is reserved for
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle_{g}
. The symbol
\star
is reserved for
\star_{g}
. In other words, the Hodge star operator used in the present article is intrinsic. Similarly,
\Delta
will stand for
\Delta
, the Laplace-Beltrami operator (or Bochner Laplacian when acting on the components of tensors).

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-\>\big(\vec{H}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{B})(\vec{h}^{i}_{j}\cdot\nabla^{j}\vec{H})+(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{i}_{j})(\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{B})\>.

(A.7)

Calling upon (
A.4
) now gives

\displaystyle\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\delta\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H}\;\;=\;\;\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\Big[\dfrac{1}{4}\nabla_{j}\big(4A^{s}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{s}\vec{H}+(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{ik})\vec{h}_{ik}+\Delta_{\perp}\vec{B}\big)-A^{i}\nabla_{j}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\Big]

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-\>\big(\vec{H}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{B})(\vec{h}^{i}_{j}\cdot\nabla^{j}\vec{H})+(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{i}_{j})(\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{B})+\dfrac{A^{i}}{2}\nabla_{i}|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\>\big(\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{H})\nabla_{j}A^{i}+\dfrac{1}{4}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\nabla_{j}\big((\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{ik})\vec{h}_{ik}\big)+\dfrac{1}{4}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\nabla_{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{B}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-\>\big(\vec{H}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{B})(\vec{h}^{i}_{j}\cdot\nabla^{j}\vec{H})+(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{i}_{j})(\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{B})+\dfrac{A^{i}}{2}\nabla_{i}|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\>(\vec{T}_{i}\cdot\vec{T}_{j})\nabla^{j}A^{i}+\dfrac{A^{i}}{2}\nabla_{i}|\vec{T}|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}\vec{T}^{j}\cdot\nabla_{j}\big((\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{ik})\vec{h}_{ik}\big)+\dfrac{1}{4}\vec{T}^{j}\cdot\nabla_{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{B}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-\>\big(\vec{H}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{B})(\vec{h}^{i}_{j}\cdot\vec{T}^{j})+(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{i}_{j})(\vec{T}^{j}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{B})

Putting this into (
A.5
), it comes

\displaystyle|g|^{-1/2}\delta\big(|\vec{T}|^{2}|g|^{1/2}\big)\;\;=\;\;2(\vec{T}_{i}\cdot\vec{T}_{j})(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})+\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{T}^{j}\cdot\nabla_{j}\big((\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{ik})\vec{h}_{ik}\big)+\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{T}^{j}\cdot\nabla_{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{B}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\>2\big(\vec{H}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{B})(\vec{h}^{j}_{i}\cdot\vec{T}^{i})+2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{j}_{i})(\vec{T}^{i}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{B})-4|\vec{T}|^{2}\vec{H}\cdot\vec{B}+\nabla^{j}\big(|\vec{T}|^{2}A_{j}\big)

\displaystyle=\vec{B}\cdot\Big[2(\vec{T}_{i}\cdot\vec{T}_{j})\vec{h}^{ij}-4|\vec{T}|^{2}\vec{H}-\dfrac{1}{2}(\vec{h}_{ik}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{T}^{j})\vec{h}^{ik}+2\nabla_{j}\big((\vec{T}^{i}\cdot\vec{h}^{j}_{i})\vec{H}\big)-2\nabla_{j}\big((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{j}_{i})\vec{T}^{i}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 341.43306pt-\>\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{\perp}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{T}^{i}\Big]

\displaystyle+\>\nabla^{j}\Big[|\vec{T}|^{2}A_{j}+\dfrac{1}{2}(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{ik})(\vec{T}_{j}\cdot\vec{h}_{ik})-2(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{H})(\vec{T}^{i}\cdot\vec{h}_{ij})+2(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{T}^{i})(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{ij})

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\>\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{T}_{j}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{B}-\dfrac{1}{2}\nabla_{j}\vec{B}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{T}^{i}+\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{B}\cdot\nabla_{j}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{T}^{i}\Big]\>.

(A.8)

This expression can be a little bit simplified by noting that

\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{T}^{i}\;=\;\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\>.

Then, we have

|g|^{-1/2}\delta\big(|\vec{T}|^{2}|g|^{1/2}\big)\;\;=\;\;\vec{B}\cdot\vec{\mathcal{W}}+\nabla^{j}V_{j}\>,

with

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{W}}\;\;=\;\;2(\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H})\vec{h}^{ij}-4|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}-\dfrac{1}{2}(\vec{h}_{ik}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H})\vec{h}^{ik}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\>2\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\big(\vec{h}^{j}_{i}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{H})\vec{H}\big)-2\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\big((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{j}_{i})\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}\big)-\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{\perp}^{2}\vec{H}

and

\displaystyle V_{j}\;\;=\;\;|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}A_{j}+\dfrac{1}{2}(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{ik})(\vec{h}_{ik}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{H})-2(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{H})(\vec{h}_{ij}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{H})

\displaystyle+\>2(\vec{B}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{H})(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{ij})+\dfrac{1}{2}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{B}-\dfrac{1}{2}\nabla_{j}\vec{B}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{B}\cdot\nabla_{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\>.

Suppose next that
\delta\vec{\Phi}=\vec{a}
is a constant vector in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then

A_{j}\;=\;\vec{a}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\vec{B}\;=\;\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{a}\>.

(A.9)

It is easy to check that if
\vec{U}
is any vector, it holds

\vec{U}\cdot\vec{B}\;=\;\vec{a}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{B}\;=\;\vec{a}\cdot\pi_{T}\nabla_{j}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}\>.

Hence

\displaystyle\vec{U}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}\cdot\nabla^{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{B}\;\;=\;\;\nabla^{i}\big(\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{B}\big)-(\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U})\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla^{i}\big[\vec{a}\cdot\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}\big]-\vec{a}\cdot\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{a}\cdot\Big[\nabla^{i}\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}-\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}\Big]\>.

Then we have

V_{j}\;=\;\vec{a}\cdot\vec{V}_{j}\>,

with

\displaystyle\vec{V}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}+\dfrac{1}{2}(\vec{h}_{ik}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{H})\vec{h}^{ik}-2(\vec{h}_{ij}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{H})\vec{H}+2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{ij})\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}

\displaystyle+\;\dfrac{1}{2}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}-\dfrac{1}{2}\pi_{T}\nabla_{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+\dfrac{1}{2}\nabla^{i}\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H}-\dfrac{1}{2}\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}-2(\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{H})\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}+\dfrac{1}{2}(\vec{h}_{ij}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H})\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}+\dfrac{1}{2}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}

\displaystyle-\;2(\vec{h}_{ij}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{H})\vec{H}+2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{ij})\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}\>.

Variations of
\int|d\vec{H}|^{2}
yield the equation
d^{\star}\vec{V}=-\vec{\mathcal{W}}
. At equilibrium, for critical points, the term
\vec{\mathcal{W}}
vanishes.

We will find convenient to write

\vec{V}^{j}\;=\;G^{ij}\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}+\vec{C}^{j}\>,

where

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}G^{ij}&=&\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}g^{ij}-2\nabla_{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\\[4.30554pt]
\vec{F}^{ij}&=&-\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\,g^{ij}\\[4.30554pt]
\vec{C}^{j}&=&-2(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{H})\vec{H}+2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\>.\end{array}\right.

(A.10)

Remark A.1

The tensor
G
satisfies

G^{i}_{i}\;=\;2\vec{H}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+2|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}\;=\;\Delta|\vec{H}|^{2}\>.

\hfill\square

The triple of data
(G,\vec{F},\vec{C})
are of course closely tied to each other. For our use, we record in particular the following fact.

Lemma A.1

It holds

\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}-\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\vec{C}^{j}\;=\;-\nabla_{i}G^{im}\>.

Proof.

With the help of Codazzi, it holds

\displaystyle-\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}+\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\vec{C}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{i}\big(\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\vec{F}^{ij}\big)+\vec{F}^{ij}\cdot\nabla^{m}\vec{h}_{ij}+\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\vec{C}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{i}\big(\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\vec{F}^{ij}-g^{mi}\langle\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{,}}\vec{F}\rangle\big)-\vec{h}_{ij}\cdot\nabla^{m}\vec{F}^{ij}+\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\vec{C}^{j}\>.

Since

\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\vec{F}^{ij}-g^{mi}\langle\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{,}}\vec{F}\rangle\;=\;-G^{im}+g^{im}\big|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}\big|^{2}-2\nabla^{i}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{m}\vec{H}+2\vec{H}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\>.

the latter yields

\displaystyle-\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}+\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\vec{C}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\vec{h}_{ij}\cdot\nabla^{m}\vec{F}^{ij}+\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\vec{C}^{j}

(A.11)

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt+\>\nabla_{i}\big(G^{im}-g^{im}\big|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}\big|^{2}+2\nabla^{i}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{m}\vec{H}-2\vec{H}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\big)\>.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle-\vec{h}_{ij}\cdot\nabla^{m}\vec{F}^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\vec{H}\cdot\nabla^{m}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\nabla^{m}\big(\vec{H}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\big)-2\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\cdot\nabla^{m}\vec{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\nabla^{m}\big(\vec{H}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\big)-2\nabla_{j}\big(\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{m}\vec{H}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\>2\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H}\cdot\nabla^{j}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{m}\vec{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\nabla^{m}\big(\vec{H}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\big)-2\nabla_{j}\big(\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{m}\vec{H}\big)+\nabla^{m}\big|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\>2\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H}\cdot\big(\nabla^{m}\pi_{T}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}-\nabla^{j}\pi_{T}\nabla^{m}\vec{H}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\nabla^{m}\big(\vec{H}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\big)-2\nabla_{j}\big(\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{m}\vec{H}\big)+\nabla^{m}\big|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\>2\big((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{m}_{j})\vec{h}^{ij}-(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\big)\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\>.

Equivalently, the latter states that

-\vec{h}_{ij}\cdot\nabla^{m}\vec{F}^{ij}+\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\vec{C}^{j}\;=\;\nabla_{j}\big(2g^{mj}\vec{H}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+g^{mj}|\pi_{\vec{n}}d\vec{H}|^{2}-2\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{m}\vec{H}\big)\>.

Importing this into (
A.11
) confirms that

-\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}+\vec{h}^{m}_{j}\cdot\vec{C}^{j}\;=\;\nabla_{i}G^{im}\>.

(A.12)

\hfill\blacksquare

The next result will also be useful in our derivations.

Lemma A.2

It holds

\big(-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}+\vec{C}^{j}\big)\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\;=\;\nabla_{i}\big(-\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}+2\vec{H}\wedge\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}+2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\vec{H}\wedge\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}\big)\>.

Proof.

Define the one-vector-valued one-form

\vec{X}^{j}\;:=\;\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}-\big(2|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{jk}-\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{jk}\big)\nabla_{k}\vec{\Phi}\>.

We verify the exact divergence

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\vec{X}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}-(\vec{h}^{jk}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{H})\nabla_{k}\vec{\Phi}-2\nabla_{j}|\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}+(\vec{h}^{jk}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{H})\nabla_{k}\vec{\Phi}+2\nabla_{j}|\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\>\big(2|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{jk}-\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{jk}\big)\vec{h}_{jk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}-\big(2|\vec{H}|^{2}g^{jk}-\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{jk}\big)\vec{h}_{jk}\>.

Accordingly, we have

\displaystyle\vec{H}\wedge\big(\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\vec{h}_{ij}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{H}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{X}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}(\vec{H}\wedge\vec{X}^{j})+\vec{X}^{j}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}(\vec{H}\wedge\vec{X}^{j})+\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\wedge\pi_{T}\nabla_{j}\vec{H}+2|\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\>(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\vec{H})\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}(\vec{H}\wedge\vec{X}^{j})+2|\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}-2(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\vec{H})\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\>.

Next, using Codazzi, we find

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\big((\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\vec{H})\vec{H}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\>(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\vec{H})\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}+(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\vec{H})\vec{H}\wedge\vec{h}_{ij}+2\nabla^{j}|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\>(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{H})\vec{H}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\>.

Combining together the last two identities and reorganising the terms yields

\displaystyle\vec{H}\wedge\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\big(\vec{H}\wedge\vec{X}^{j}+2|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}\big)

(A.13)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\>\big((\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{H})\vec{H}-(h^{ij}\cdot\vec{H})\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\big)\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\>.

Note that we have used the fact that

(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\vec{H})\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\;=\;-(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\vec{H})(\vec{h}_{i}^{k}\cdot\vec{H})\nabla_{k}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\;=\;\vec{0}\>.

Without much effort now, we call upon (
A.10
) to recast (
A.13
) in the equivalent form

\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\vec{h}_{ij}\;=\;2\nabla_{j}\big(\vec{H}\wedge\vec{X}^{j}+2|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}\big)-\vec{C}^{j}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\>.

(A.14)

Finally, since
\vec{F}^{ij}\cdot\vec{h}_{i}^{k}
is clearly a symmetric 2-tensor, it comes

\displaystyle\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}(\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi})-\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\vec{h}_{ij}+(\vec{F}^{ij}\cdot\vec{h}_{i}^{k})\nabla_{k}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}(\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi})-\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\vec{h}_{ij}\>,

which, once combined with (
A.14
), implies the announced

\displaystyle-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}+\vec{C}^{j}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\big(-\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}+2\vec{H}\wedge\vec{X}^{i}+4|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\big(-\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}+2\vec{H}\wedge\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}+2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\vec{H}\wedge\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}\big)\>.

\hfill\blacksquare

A.2.2
Variation of
\int\mathcal{O}(|\vec{h}|^{4})

In dimension 4, there are exactly 8 distinct energies of the type
\int\mathcal{O}(|\vec{h}|^{4})
. Letting
T_{ab}^{cd}:=\vec{h}_{a}^{c}\cdot\vec{h}_{b}^{d}
, they can all eight be obtained from various contractions of the quantity
T_{ab}^{cd}T^{kl}_{ij}
. That is therefore the quantity which we will vary. We proceed exactly as in the previous section and consider a variation of the type (
A.1
). We open with

\displaystyle\pi_{\vec{n}}\delta\vec{h}_{a}^{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{\vec{n}}\delta(g^{ci}\vec{h}_{ai})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{ci}\pi_{\vec{n}}\delta\vec{h}_{ai}+\vec{h}_{ai}\delta g^{ci}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{ci}\big(A^{s}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{a}\vec{h}_{si}+\vec{h}_{sa}\nabla_{i}A^{s}+\vec{h}_{si}\nabla_{a}A^{s}+\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{ai}\vec{B}\big)-\vec{h}_{ai}\big(\nabla^{c}A^{i}+\nabla^{i}A^{c}-2\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{ic}\big)\>.

From this, it follows that

\displaystyle\delta T_{ab}^{cd}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{h}_{a}^{c}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\delta\vec{h}^{d}_{b}+\vec{h}_{b}^{d}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\delta\vec{h}^{c}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{h}^{d}_{b}\cdot\Big[\big(A^{s}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{a}\vec{h}_{s}^{c}+\vec{h}^{s}_{a}\nabla^{c}A_{s}+\vec{h}_{s}^{c}\nabla_{a}A^{s}+\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{c}_{a}\vec{B}\big)-\vec{h}_{a}^{i}\big(\nabla^{c}A_{i}+\nabla_{i}A^{c}-2\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}_{i}^{c}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle+\>\vec{h}^{c}_{a}\cdot\Big[\big(A^{s}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{b}\vec{h}_{s}^{d}+\vec{h}^{s}_{b}\nabla^{d}A_{s}+\vec{h}^{d}_{s}\nabla_{b}A^{s}+\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{d}_{b}\vec{B}\big)-\vec{h}_{b}^{i}\big(\nabla^{d}A_{i}+\nabla_{i}A^{d}-2\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}_{i}^{d}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{s}\vec{h}^{d}_{b}\cdot\nabla_{s}\vec{h}^{c}_{a}+T_{ba}^{ds}\nabla^{c}A_{s}+T_{bs}^{dc}\nabla_{a}A^{s}+\vec{h}_{b}^{d}\cdot\nabla_{a}^{c}\vec{B}-T_{ba}^{ds}\big(\nabla^{c}A_{s}+\nabla_{s}A^{c}-2\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{c}_{s}\big)

\displaystyle+\>A^{s}\vec{h}^{c}_{a}\cdot\nabla_{s}\vec{h}^{d}_{b}+T_{ab}^{cs}\nabla^{d}A_{s}+T_{as}^{cd}\nabla_{b}A^{s}+\vec{h}_{a}^{c}\cdot\nabla_{b}^{d}\vec{B}-T_{ab}^{cs}\big(\nabla^{d}A_{s}+\nabla_{s}A^{d}-2\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{d}_{s}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{s}\nabla_{s}T^{cd}_{ab}+2(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{c}_{s})T^{ds}_{ba}+2(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{d}_{s})T^{cs}_{ab}

\displaystyle+\>T_{bs}^{dc}\nabla_{a}A^{s}+T_{as}^{cd}\nabla_{b}A^{s}-T^{ds}_{ba}\nabla_{s}A^{c}-T^{cs}_{ab}\nabla_{s}A^{d}+\vec{h}_{b}^{d}\cdot\nabla_{a}^{c}\vec{B}+\vec{h}^{c}_{a}\cdot\nabla_{b}^{d}\vec{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{s}\nabla_{s}T^{cd}_{ab}+2(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{c}_{s})T^{ds}_{ba}+2(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{d}_{s})T^{cs}_{ab}+\vec{B}\cdot\big(\ldots\big)

\displaystyle+\>T_{bs}^{dc}\nabla_{a}A^{s}+T_{as}^{cd}\nabla_{b}A^{s}-T^{ds}_{ba}\nabla_{s}A^{c}-T^{cs}_{ab}\nabla_{s}A^{d}+\vec{h}_{b}^{d}\cdot\nabla_{a}^{c}\vec{B}+\vec{h}^{c}_{a}\cdot\nabla_{b}^{d}\vec{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{s}\nabla_{s}T^{cd}_{ab}+\vec{B}\cdot(\vec{h}^{c}_{s}T^{ds}_{ba}+\vec{h}_{a}^{s}T^{dc}_{bs}+\vec{h}_{b}^{s}T^{cd}_{as}+\vec{h}^{d}_{s}T^{cs}_{ab})+\vec{h}_{b}^{d}\cdot\nabla_{a}^{c}\vec{B}+\vec{h}^{c}_{a}\cdot\nabla_{b}^{d}\vec{B}+\vec{B}\cdot\big(\ldots\big)\>.

We have deliberately ignored any term of the type
\vec{B}\cdot(\ldots)

The data given in the table will be used in the next section. We also need to record the following observation.

Lemma A.3

For an energy with integrand

E\;=\;|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}+\alpha|\vec{H}|^{4}\>\qquad\text{where $\alpha$ is a constant},

it holds

\vec{F}^{rs}\wedge\vec{h}_{rs}\;=\;\vec{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\vec{h}^{m}_{s}\cdot\nabla_{r}\vec{F}^{rs}\;=\;-\nabla_{r}G^{rs}\>.

Proof.

Per the table,

\vec{F}^{rs}\;=\;2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{rs})\vec{H}+\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h},\vec{h}\rangle g^{rs}+\alpha|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\,g^{rs}\>.

The first item follows immediately. To reach the second item, we observe that

\displaystyle\nabla^{m}|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{rs})\nabla^{m}(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{rs})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\vec{h}_{rs}\cdot\nabla^{m}\big((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{rs})\vec{H}\big)-2\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h},\vec{h}\rangle\cdot\nabla^{m}\vec{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\vec{h}_{rs}\cdot\nabla^{m}\big((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{rs})\vec{H}\big)-2\nabla^{m}\big(\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h},\vec{h}\rangle\cdot\vec{H}\big)+\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{h}_{rs}\cdot\nabla^{m}\big(\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h},\vec{h}\rangle g^{rs}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{h}_{rs}\cdot\nabla^{m}\Big(2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{rs})\vec{H}+\dfrac{1}{2}\langle\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h},\vec{h}\rangle g^{rs}\Big)-2\nabla^{m}|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}\>.

Similarly, it holds

\displaystyle\nabla^{m}|\vec{H}|^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla^{m}|\vec{H}|^{2}\;\;=\;\;2\vec{H}\cdot\nabla^{m}\big(|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\big)-2|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\cdot\nabla^{m}\vec{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\vec{h}_{rs}\cdot\nabla^{m}\big(|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}g^{rs}\big)-\dfrac{1}{2}\nabla^{m}|\vec{H}|^{4}\>.

From this it easily follows that

\vec{h}_{rs}\cdot\nabla^{m}\vec{F}^{rs}\;=\;3\nabla^{i}E\>.

(A.17)

To complete the proof, it remains to note that Codazzi implies

\displaystyle\vec{h}^{m}_{s}\cdot\nabla_{r}\vec{F}^{rs}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{r}\big(\vec{h}^{m}_{s}\cdot\vec{F}^{rs}-\langle\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{,}}\vec{F}\rangle g^{mr}\big)+\vec{h}_{rs}\cdot\nabla^{m}\vec{F}^{rs}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(\ref{PS4})}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{r}G^{rm}\>,

where in the last step we’ve used Remark
A.2

Lemma A.4

For each of the four conformally invariant energies
\int E

\vec{F}^{r}_{r}\;=\;\vec{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\vec{F}^{rs}\wedge\vec{h}_{rs}\;=\;\vec{0}\>.

Corollary A.1

We have

\vec{h}^{i}_{j}\cdot\nabla_{k}\vec{F}^{kj}\;=\;-\nabla_{k}G^{ik}\>.

Proof.
Clearly, using Codazzi,

\displaystyle\vec{h}^{i}_{j}\cdot\nabla_{k}\vec{F}^{kj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}\big(\vec{h}^{i}_{j}\cdot\vec{F}^{kj}-\langle\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{,}}\vec{F}\rangle g^{ik}\big)+\vec{h}_{kj}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{F}^{kj}\>.

From Lemma

, we know that

\vec{h}_{kj}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{F}^{kj}\;=\;(\vec{h}_{0})_{kj}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{F}^{kj}\;=\;3\nabla^{i}E\>.

Also, from Remark

\vec{h}^{i}_{j}\cdot\vec{F}^{kj}-\langle\vec{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{,}}\vec{F}\rangle g^{ik}\;=\;-G^{ik}-3Eg^{ik}\>.

Accordingly now, the desired claim follows:

\vec{h}^{i}_{j}\cdot\nabla_{k}\vec{F}^{kj}\;=\;-\nabla_{k}G^{ik}\>.

\hfill\blacksquare

Corollary A.2

We have

-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\;=\;-\nabla_{i}\big(\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\big)\>.

This is immediate from Lemma

. Indeed,

\displaystyle-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{i}\big(\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\big)+\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}+\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\vec{h}_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{i}\big(\vec{F}^{ij}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\big)-(\vec{F}^{ij}\cdot\vec{h}^{k}_{i})\nabla_{k}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\>.

The result follows from the symmetry of

(cf. Remark
A.2
).

\hfill\blacksquare

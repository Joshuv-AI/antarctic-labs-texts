Sequential therapy with decitabine and an XPO-1 inhibitor followed by allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation in MDS with biallelic TP53 inactivation patients : a preliminary case series
Authors: Yanling Ren, Wei Lang, Yu Xu, Chen Mei, Xianbo Huang, Shasha Wang, Jiejing Qian, Hongyan Tong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMyelodysplastic syndrome with biallelic TP53 inactivation (MDS-biTP53) patients represents an ultra-high-risk subgroup with dismal outcomes. Even after allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT), relapse rates remain extremely high and survival is poor. This preliminary case series reports the outcomes of 8 consecutive patients with MDS-biTP53 who received a novel sequential therapy of decitabine (DAC) combined with the XPO-1 inhibitor selinexor, followed by allo-HSCT at a single center between September 2024 and December 2025. At a median follow-up of 7.3 months (95% CI, 4.2–10.4 months) from HSCT, median overall survival (OS) and relapse-free survival (RFS) were not reached. At last follow-up, 7/8 patients remained alive (OS 87.5%) and 6/8 were relapse-free (RFS 75%), with one relapse (12.5%). This patient successfully achieved a second complete remission following preemptive therapy with low-dose decitabine combined with donor lymphocyte infusion and remained in remission at the last follow-up. Two patients developed serious infections during the peritransplant period. One patient with pre-transplant intestinal colonization of carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae (CRE) ultimately died due to CRE bloodstream infection followed by intestinal graft-versus-host-disease (GVHD). Overall treatment-related toxicity was deemed manageable, and the incidence of grade III-IV acute GVHD was 25% (2/8). This preliminary study provides encouraging evidence that sequential decitabine and selinexor therapy followed by allo-HSCT may improve outcomes with acceptable toxicity in patients with ultra-high-risk MDS-biTP53. However, these findings are limited by the small sample size, relatively short follow-up, and retrospective, single-center design. To the Editor,

Myelodysplastic syndromes with biallelic TP53 inactivation (MDS-biTP53) represent a distinct entity with dismal outcomes. These patients respond poorly to conventional therapies—including hypomethylating agents [1], BCL-2 inhibitors [2], and immunomodulatory drugs [3] -as recently reviewed [4]. Even with allo-HSCT, median time to post-transplant relapse is only 4 to 6 months [5], with 1-year overall survival rates around 20–30% [6]. This underscores an urgent need for novel approaches to reduce relapse and improve survival.

We report a case series of 8 adult patients with NGS-confirmed MDS-biTP53 treated at our centre between September 2024 and December 2025. All patients received a sequential protocol consisting of a 2-week cytoreductive course with decitabine (20 mg/m², days 1–5) and selinexor (20 mg orally twice weekly), followed immediately by a decitabine‑based conditioning regimen and allo-HSCT. Conditioning was either DAC‑BuCy or DAC‑BF; detailed doses and schedules are in Supplementary Table S1. GVHD prophylaxis comprised anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG), calcineurin inhibitors, and short-course methotrexate. The study was approved by our Institutional Review Board (No. 0352-2026B).

Patient characteristics are detailed in Table 1. Median age at allo-HSCT was 60 years all were very high risk according to IPSS‑R/M scoring systems. Five patients had prior hypomethylating agents therapy, with only one achieving marrow complete remission (mCR). All patients achieved neutrophil engraftment (median 11 days); platelet engraftment failed in one cord blood recipient. At first post-transplant assessment (day + 30), all were in morphological remission and TP53-mutant clone negative by ddPCR.

At a median follow‑up of 7.3 months (range 2.8‑16.7) since HSCT, median OS and RFS were not reached. Observed proportions at last follow-up were 87.5% (7/8) for OS and 75% (6/8) for RFS (Fig. 1). One patient relapsed (12.5%) but achieved a second complete remission with low-dose decitabine and donor lymphocyte infusion (DLI). One non-relapse mortality occurred due to septic shock from CRE infection and GVHD. Grade III-IV acute GVHD occurred in two patients (25%).

Transplant outcome of all patients

To our knowledge, this is the first report of sequential decitabine and selinexor followed directly by allo‑HSCT specifically for MDS‑biTP53 patients. Conventional bridge regimens, including HMA+venetoclax [7], carry risks of infection and outgrowth of resistant clones during the waiting period, yet the benefit of pre‑transplant cytoreduction in TP53-mutated MDS remains debated [8]. Our protocol eliminates this interval. Mechanistically, decitabine induces p53‑independent mitotic catastrophe in TP53‑mutant cells, while selinexor forces nuclear retention of residual tumor suppressors, partially restoring apoptosis [1, 9]. Preclinical studies show synergistic inhibition of MDS cell proliferation [10], with clinical activity reported in HMA‑refractory MDS [11]. We hypothesize that this combination enabled deeper pre‑transplant clearance of TP53‑mutant clones, and the direct transition to transplantation may have prevented adverse selection, contributing to the low relapse rate and durable molecular remissions in our cohort.

Given the small sample and retrospective design, these findings remain preliminary; nonetheless, this sequential strategy merits further investigation as a potential option for this ultra-high-risk population.

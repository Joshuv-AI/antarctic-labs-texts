On the nilpotent residue non-abelian Hodge correspondence for higher-dimensional quasiprojective varieties
Quoc Anh Tran

Abstract
. In [ 2 ] , the authors proved that on a projective log smooth variety ( X ¯ , D ) \left(\bar{X},D\right) there is a continuous bijection between the moduli space M Dol nilp ​ ( X ¯ , D ) M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D\right) of logarithmic Higgs bundles with nilpotent residues and the moduli space M DR nilp ​ ( X ¯ , D ) M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{DR}}\left(\bar{X},D\right) of logarithmic connections with nilpotent residues. In this notes, we argue that the map is a homeomorphism.



Let
\left(\bar{X},D\right)
be a projective log smooth complex variety, i.e., a projective smooth complex variety
\bar{X}
with a simple normal crossing divisor
D
. In the sense of
[
2
]
, we have two good moduli spaces:

\displaystyle M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{DR}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{(E,\nabla)\;\middle|\;\begin{aligned} &(E,\nabla)\text{ is a rank }r\text{ polystable log connection}\\
&\text{with nilpotent residues}\end{aligned}\right\}

\displaystyle M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{(E,\theta)\;\middle|\;\begin{aligned} &(E,\theta)\text{ is a rank }r\text{ polystable log Higgs bundle}\\
&\text{with nilpotent residues and }c_{i}(E)=0\text{ for all }i\end{aligned}\right\}

and as a corollary of
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, we get a bijective comparison map:

SM_{\left(\bar{X},D\right)}^{\mathrm{nilp}}:M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)(\mathbb{C})\to M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{DR}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)(\mathbb{C})

which is a homeomorphism, in the Euclidean topology and when
\bar{X}
is a curve, by
[
2
, Theorem 7.13]
. They were also able to prove that
SM_{\left(\bar{X},D\right)}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
is a continuous bijection in higher dimensions, as follows.

Definition
.

A Lefschetz curve is a smooth complete intersection curve
\iota:\bar{C}\to\bar{X}
such that
\bar{C}
is transverse to
D
, and we have a surjection
\pi_{1}\left(\bar{C}\backslash D\right)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}\left(\bar{X}\backslash D\right)
.

Such a Lefschetz curve
\iota:\bar{C}\hookrightarrow\bar{X}
induces a closed embedding
i^{*}_{\mathrm{DR}}:M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{DR}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)\to M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{DR}}\left(\bar{C},D\cap\bar{C},r\right)

and a diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{C},D\cap\bar{C},r\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{DR}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{DR}}\left(\bar{C},D\cap\bar{C},r\right)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces SM_{\left(\bar{X},D\right)}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces SM_{\left(\bar{C},D\cap\bar{C}\right)}^{\mathrm{nilp}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\mathrm{DR}}^{*}}

Since
SM_{\left(\bar{C},D\cap\bar{C}\right)}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
is a homeomorphism and
i_{\mathrm{DR}}^{*}
is a closed immersion,
SM_{\left(\bar{X},D\right)}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
is continuous, thus a continuous bijection as desired. Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1
.

There exists a Lefschetz curve
\bar{C}\subset\bar{X}
so that the restriction map
i_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{*}
is proper.

Then, by running the previous argument for the inverse comparison maps, we get:

Corollary 2
.

SM_{\left(\bar{X},D\right)}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
is a homeomorphism.

To get to the proof of theorem 1, we will need some preliminary results and definitions:

Definition
.

A rank
r
logarithmic Higgs bundle on
\left(\bar{X},D\right)
is a rank
r
locally free
\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}}-
module
E
together with a
\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}}-
linear morphism
\theta:E\to\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}}}E
such that
\theta\wedge\theta=0
, called the Higgs field. Locally,
\theta
is just a matrix of logarithmic 1-forms, hence we can talk about its characteristic polynomial
\mathrm{char}(\theta)
. The coefficients are then in
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)
, and we get the Hitchin map:

M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)\xrightarrow{h_{\left(\bar{X},D\right)}}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)

Now if
E
is only torsion-free, then there is an open
U\subseteq\bar{X}
, with
\mathrm{codim}_{\bar{X}}\left(\bar{X}-U\right)\geq 2
, such that
E|_{U}
is locally free. We can then talk about the characteristic polynomial of
(E|_{U},\theta|_{U})
. Since
\bar{X}
is smooth (in particular, normal), the coefficients (over
U
) extends uniquely to sections over
\bar{X}
. Hence, if we define
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{\mathrm{tf}}\left(\bar{X},D,P\right)
to be the moduli space of Gieseker polystable torsion-free logarithmic Higgs sheaves with Hilbert polynomial
P
, then we have another Hitchin map with a bigger total space:

M^{\mathrm{tf}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D,P\right)\xrightarrow{h^{\prime}_{\left(\bar{X},D,P\right)}}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)

Proposition 3
.

[
4
, Theorem 3.8]

The Hitchin map
h_{\left(\bar{X},D\right)}
is proper.

Proof.

Let us say a few words about why this is implied by
[
4
, Theorem 3.8]
. That the torsion-free Hitchin map
h^{\prime}_{\left(\bar{X},D,P\right)}
is proper is just a special case of the cited theorem. Indeed, a logarithmic Higgs sheaf is just a
T_{\bar{X}}(-\log D)-
coHiggs sheaf, viewing
T_{\bar{X}}(-\log D)
as a trivial Lie algebroid.

M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)
is the subscheme of
M^{\mathrm{tf}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\cdot P_{\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}}}\right)
, where
P_{\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}}}
is the Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}}
, cutout by requiring all Chern classes to vanish (which forces the torsion-free sheaves to be locally free) and then requiring the residues of the Higgs fields to be nilpotent. If we can show
M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)
is a closed subscheme, then
h_{\left(\bar{X},D\right)}
, which is the restriction of a proper map to a closed subset, must be proper as desired.

Now, Chern classes are locally constant in a flat family: consider torsion-free
\mathcal{E}
on
\bar{X}\times S
with two fibers
\bar{X}_{s}\xrightarrow{\iota_{s}}\bar{X}\times S,\bar{X}_{t}\xrightarrow{\iota_{t}}\bar{X}\times S
, we claim that if
S
is connected then
c_{i}(\iota_{s}^{*}\mathcal{E})\simeq c_{i}(\iota_{t}^{*}\mathcal{E})\in H^{2i}\left(\bar{X},\mathbb{Z}\right)
. By naturality of Chern classes, these are just
\iota_{s}^{*}c_{i}(\mathcal{E})
and
\iota_{t}^{*}c_{i}(\mathcal{E})
. Since
S
connected, after identifying
\bar{X}_{s}\simeq\bar{X}_{t}\simeq\bar{X}
it’s easy to see
\iota_{s}
and
\iota_{t}
are homotopic (via a path
\gamma
connecting
s,t\in S
) hence the two pullbacks must agree. Thus requiring vanishing Chern classes pickout a union of connected components of
M^{\mathrm{tf}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\cdot P_{\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}}}\right)
, hence closed. Further requiring nilpotent residue is also a closed condition, see
[
2
, p. 66]
.
∎

Next, we have a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{C},D\cap\bar{C},r\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}\left(\bar{C},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{C}}(\log D\cap\bar{C})\right)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{\left(\bar{X},D\right)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{res}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{\left(\bar{C},D\cap\bar{C}\right)}}

where the right vertical arrow is the composition

H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)\to H^{0}\left(\bar{C},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)|_{\bar{C}}\right)\to H^{0}\left(\bar{C},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{C}}(\log D\cap\bar{C})\right)

Proposition 4
.

There exists a Lefschetz curve
\bar{C}
so that
\mathrm{res}
is injective.

Proof.

Let’s first examine what it means to be in the kernel of the map

H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}}H^{0}\left(\bar{C},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{C}}(\log D\cap\bar{C})\right)

Suppose that
s\in H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)
maps to the zero section under
\mathrm{res}
. At each point
p\in\bar{C},p\notin D
, if we restrict
s
to the fibers at
p
, we can view
s(p)\in\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\left(T_{p}\bar{X}\right)^{\vee}
as a homogeneous polynomial on
T_{p}\bar{X}
. The map
\mathrm{res}
then just restricts
s(p)
to the line
T_{p}\bar{C}\subset T_{p}\bar{X}
. As a result,
\mathrm{res}(s)=0
implies that:

s(p)\left(T_{p}\bar{C}\right)=0\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\ p\in\bar{C},p\notin D

Thus it suffices to prove that there exists a Lefschetz curve
\bar{C}
such that for each
s\in H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)
, there exists
p\in\bar{C},p\notin D
such that
T_{p}\bar{C}\not\subset V(s(p))\subset T_{p}\bar{X}
. We first prove the following claim:

Claim 5
.

There exists a finite number of pairs
\left\{(p_{j},l_{j})\right\}_{j=1}^{t}
with
p_{j}\in\bar{X},p_{j}\notin D
,
l_{j}
a general 1-dimensional
\mathbb{C}-
subspace of
T_{p_{j}}\bar{X}
such that for every
s\in H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)
,

s(p_{j})(l_{j})\neq 0

for at least one of the pairs.

Proof of claim.

Let
s_{1}\in H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)
be a nonzero section, then there is some point
p_{1}\in\bar{X},p_{1}\notin D
such that
s_{1}(p_{1})\in\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\left(T_{p_{1}}\bar{X}\right)^{\vee}
is nonzero. This is a polynomial on
T_{p_{1}}\bar{X}
, so the zero locus
V(s_{1}(p_{1}))
is a hypersurface. Thus we can just pick a line
l_{1}
not contained in
V(s_{1}(p_{1}))
. The pair
(p_{1},l_{1})
gives a linear functional:

\epsilon_{1}:H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)\to\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\left(l_{1}^{\vee}\right)\simeq\mathbb{C},\hskip 14.22636pts\mapsto s(p_{1})|_{l_{1}}

and
\epsilon_{1}(s_{1})\neq 0
. So
\dim\ker(\epsilon_{1})<\dim H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)
. If
\ker(\epsilon_{1})=0
we are done, otherwise pick
s_{2}\in\ker(\epsilon_{1})
and repeat the process. Since
H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)
is finite dimensional, we end up with a finite collection
\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{t}
such that

\bigcap_{j=1}^{t}\ker(\epsilon_{j})=0

and the corresponding pairs
(p_{j},l_{j})
satisfy the claim.
∎

Our proposition is reduced to showing that there exists a Lefschetz curve
\bar{C}
which:

•

passes through the points
\left\{p_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{t}
;

•

and for each
j
,
T_{p_{j}}\bar{C}=l_{j}
.

Up to picking a suitable
\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}}(1)
, this follows from
[
1
, Theorem A.1]
part (3) and (4). Indeed, their theorem implies that we can pick a general linear section
\bar{C}
which is smooth, and passes through
\left\{p_{j}\right\}
with prescribed tangents. Now, being transverse to
D
and inducing surjection of fundamental groups are also open conditions (by
[
3
, Theorem 1.1.3(ii)]
), so there exists a Lefschetz choice.
∎

We are finally ready for the main result:

Proof of Theorem 1.

We will use the valuative criterion for properness: given a discrete valuation ring
R
, with
K=\mathrm{Frac}(R)
, and a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spec}K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spec}R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{C},D\cap\bar{C},r\right)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{i_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{*}}}

we want to show that the dotted arrow exists. Consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spec}K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{X},D,r\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}\left(\bar{X},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{X}}(\log D)\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spec}R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\left(\bar{C},D\cap\bar{C},r\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}\left(\bar{C},\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega_{\bar{C}}(\log D\cap\bar{C})\right)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{h_{\left(\bar{X},D\right)}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{i_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{*}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{res}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{h_{\left(C,D\cap C\right)}}}

The long diagonal arrow exists (and makes the diagram commute) since
\mathrm{res}
is an injective map of vector spaces (equivalently, a closed embedding of affine spaces). Then the dotted arrow must exist since
h_{\left(\bar{X},D\right)}
is proper. Finally, it’s a trivial diagram chase to check that the left square commutes.
∎

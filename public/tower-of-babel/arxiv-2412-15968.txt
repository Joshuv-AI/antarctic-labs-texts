A unified approach for classifying simple nuclear C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras Thanks: This was supported by DFF grants 1054-00094B and 1026-00371B
Ben Bouwen, James Gabe

Abstract
. We provide a new proof of the Kirchberg–Phillips theorem by adapting the framework laid out by Carrión–Gabe–Schafhauser–Tikuisis–White for classifying separable simple unital nuclear stably finite 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} -stable C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras satisfying the UCT. Not only does this give a unified approach to classifying stably finite and purely infinite C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras, in contrast to the other proofs of the Kirchberg–Phillips theorem, our proof does not rely on Kirchberg’s Geneva Theorems, but instead implies them as corollaries (for nuclear C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras).

1.
Introduction

The Kirchberg–Phillips theorem, proved independently by Kirchberg (
[
21
]
) and Phillips (
[
28
]
) in the 90s, marked a major advancement in the Elliott classification program. It provided a complete classification by
K
-theory of separable, nuclear, simple, purely infinite
C^{*}
-algebras satisfying the universal coefficient theorem (UCT). In contrast, its stably finite counterpart took the joint effort of numerous researchers (
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
34
]
and the vast body of work leading up to these papers) over the past three decades to complete. Combining the classification theorems on both sides gives a comprehensive classification of all separable, nuclear, unital, simple,
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stable
C^{*}
-algebras satisfying the UCT, a landmark result in classification of
C^{*}
-algebras; we will refer to
C^{*}
-algebras satisfying these conditions as classifiable.
In recent years, a new, more abstract approach to classification was developed by Carrión, the author JG, Schafhauser, Tikuisis and White in
[
6
]
. This approach is carried out in the stably finite setting, while for the purely infinite case, the original Kirchberg–Phillips theorem is used. This paper adapts the new abstract framework and techniques to the purely infinite setting to prove the Kirchberg–Phillips theorem, paving the way to a unified approach for classifying
C^{*}
-algebras.
One of the major upsides of the new approach is that Kirchberg’s Geneva theorems in the nuclear setting (Corollary
C
) are obtained as straightforward consequences of classification. These were crucial and highly non-trivial ingredients in the original proof, Rørdam’s summary
[
30
]
and Gabe’s alternative proof
[
16
]
. With the new approach, we no longer require these results as prerequisites.

Purely infinite
C^{*}
-algebras

The distinction between stably finite and purely infinite
C^{*}
-algebras is in complete analogy with the dichotomy between semifinite and infinite von Neumann algebras. There exist simple, separable and nuclear
C^{*}
-algebras which fall between these two classes (
[
31
]
), but due to a result by Kirchberg (
[
30
, Theorem 4.1.10]
), this behavior cannot occur for classifiable
C^{*}
-algebras. Moreover, the dichotomy is witnessed by the presence of traces: a classifiable
C^{*}
-algebra is stably finite if and only if it has a tracial state, and is purely infinite otherwise.

Purely infinite, simple
C^{*}
-algebras were introduced by Cuntz as
C^{*}
-algebraic analogues of type III factors in
[
9
]
, where he defined the Cuntz algebras
\mathcal{O}_{n}
and showed that they are examples of such
C^{*}
-algebras. Simple and purely infinite
C^{*}
-algebras arise naturally as
C^{*}
-algebraic constructions from various dynamical systems, examples being Cuntz–Krieger algebras (derived from symbolic dynamics) and crossed products (
[
1
]
). Building on deep work in tensor products and exactness, Kirchberg announced breakthrough results at the 1994 ICM satellite conference in Geneva: the
{\mathcal{O}_{2}}
-embedding theorem, stating that every separable, exact
C^{*}
-algebra embeds into
{\mathcal{O}_{2}}
(
[
23
]
), and the
{\mathcal{O}_{\infty}}
-absorption theorem, which says that a separable, simple, nuclear
C^{*}
-algebra is purely infinite if and only if it absorbs
{\mathcal{O}_{\infty}}
tensorially (
[
22
]
). These results known commonly as
Kirchberg’s Geneva Theorems
were key ingredients in the proofs of the Kirchberg–Phillips theorem.

Following this theorem, more general classification results in the purely infinite setting were obtained in the subsequent decades. Kirchberg outlined a strategy to classify all separable, nuclear,
{\mathcal{O}_{\infty}}
-stable
C^{*}
-algebras up to stable isomorphism by ideal-related
KK
-theory. This result was proved (in a very different way) in
[
16
]
by the author JG.

An abstract approach to classification

On the stably finite side, progress was slower and more involved. The prevailing strategy here consisted of a very careful analysis of the internal structure of these
C^{*}
-algebras. For a more in-depth survey, see for example
[
6
, §1.2]
. More recently, a distinct, more abstract strategy to classify stably finite
C^{*}
-algebras was laid out by Carrión, Gabe, Schafhauser, Tikuisis and White (
[
6
]
).

Ever since Elliott’s classification of
A\mathbb{T}
-algebras (
[
12
]
) and Rørdam’s classification of certain crossed products (
[
29
]
), one of the guiding principles in the classification program in general has been to first classify
*
-homomorphisms between
C^{*}
-algebras, and then deduce a classification of the
C^{*}
-algebras themselves by use of an intertwining argument. In the unital setting, we then aim to classify unital
*
-homomorphisms between unital
C^{*}
-algebras
A
and
B
. In practice, it turns out to be much more tractable to first classify approximate
*
-homomorphisms, sequences of maps between
A
and
B
which become
*
-homomorphisms in the limit. These can be encoded more elegantly as honest
*
-homomorphisms from
A
into the
sequence algebra

B_{\infty}:=\ell^{\infty}(B)/c_{0}(B)
. While
B
is assumed to be simple,
B_{\infty}
is not, so we need to put a simplicity condition on the allowed maps. A
*
-homomorphism
\varphi:A\to B
is said to be
full
if
\varphi(a)
is full for each non-zero
a\in A
, i.e.
\varphi(a)
generates
B
as an ideal. The equivalence relation up to which one aims to classify maps is approximate unitary equivalence, as this is what is necessary in order to apply the intertwining argument. In the sequence algebra, this corresponds to unitary equivalence, so in other words, the main objective becomes classifying unital and full
*
-homomorphisms
A\to B_{\infty}
up to unitary equivalence.

The main results

In the purely infinite setting, there are some pivotal differences. The invariant
\underline{K}T_{u}
reduces to total
K

\overline{K}_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}
is naturally isomorphic to

itself. Due to the absence of traces, it is also clear that the same construction does not work. A natural candidate to replace traces would be states, but the direct analogue of

, taking the supremum over all states (or even all pure states), would not work, as the norm of any positive element is always attained by some (pure) state. An alternative would be to fix a single state

on

This choice of extension allows us to pick

The classification of lifts we obtain (Theorem
6.3
) is the following:

Theorem A

(Classification of lifts)
.

Let
A
be a separable, nuclear
C^{*}
-algebra,
B
a unital, simple and purely infinite
C^{*}
-algebra and
\rho
a strongly faithful state on
B
. Suppose
\theta:A\to\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}
is a unitizably full
*
-homomorphism, i.e. its forced unitization
\theta^{\dagger}:A^{\dagger}\to\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}
is full.

a)

Existence: for any
\kappa\in KK(A,S_{B,\rho})
satisfying
KK(A,q_{B})(\kappa)=[\theta]_{KK(A,\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime})}
, there exists a
*
-homomorphism
\varphi:A\to S_{B,\rho}
that lifts
\theta
and such that
[\varphi]_{KK(A,S_{B,\rho})}=\kappa
;

b)

Uniqueness: if
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}:A\to S_{B,\rho}
are two
*
-homomorphisms lifting
\theta
such that
[\psi_{1},\psi_{2}]_{KL(A,J_{B,\rho})}=0
, then they are unitarily equivalent as maps into
B_{\omega}
.

This is the purely infinite analogue of the lifting results from and
[
6
, Theorem 7.1]
. Note that we do not need to assume
\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}\cong B({\mathcal{H}})
for this theorem, and that we obtain uniqueness up to unitary equivalence by a unitary in
B_{\omega}
. The latter is a consequence of the fact that we restricted to
S_{B,\rho}
, which does not behave the same as the entire sequence algebra/ultrapower.

We then combine the classification of maps into the quotient with Theorem
A
to classify full and unital maps into
S_{B,\rho}
. We do not get a classification of maps into all of
B_{\infty}
, but our result suffices because we are only interested in maps into
B
, which factor through
S_{B,\rho}
as it contains
B
. We thus obtain a classification of unital embeddings from
A
to
B
by KK-theoretic data (Theorem
6.16
), resulting in the original classification by
K
-theory under the UCT assumption (Theorem
6.17
and Theorem
6.18
):

Theorem B
.

Let
A
be a unital, separable, nuclear
C^{*}
-algebra satisfying the UCT, and
B
a unital, simple, separable and purely infinite
C^{*}
-algebra. Then each element of
\Hom_{\Lambda}(\underline{K}(A),\underline{K}(B))
preserving the
K_{0}
-class of the unit is induced by a unital, injective
*
-homomorphism from
A
to
B
, and uniquely so up to approximate unitary equivalence.

In particular, two unital Kirchberg algebras
A
and
B
satisfying the UCT are isomorphic if and only if
(K_{*}(A),[1_{A}]_{0})\cong(K_{*}(B),[1_{B}]_{0})
.

As mentioned earlier, we recover Kirchberg’s celebrated Geneva theorems in the nuclear case as straightforward corollaries of classification (Corolarries
6.19
,
6.20
and
6.23
):

Corollary C

(Kirchberg’s Geneva theorems)
.

Let
A
be a unital, separable and nuclear
C^{*}
-algebra. Then

a)

there exists a unital embedding
A\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{O}_{2}}
;

b)

if
A
is also simple, then
A\otimes{\mathcal{O}_{2}}\cong{\mathcal{O}_{2}}
;

c)

if
A
is also simple and purely infinite, then
A\otimes{\mathcal{O}_{\infty}}\cong{\mathcal{O}_{\infty}}
.

The main ingredients are the KK-existence theorem, the Elliott-Kucerovsky theorem (
[
14
]
), which gives a concrete characterization for absorbing maps, and Voiculescu’s theorem, as mentioned earlier. Outside of these results and core properties of
KK
-theory and extension theory, the proof is essentially self-contained.

By an ideal in a

3.
The reduced state-kernel extension

3.1.
Construction

The main object of study in the new approach to classification in the stably finite setting is the so-called
trace-kernel extension

(3.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J_{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{\infty}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B^{\infty}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{B}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{B}}

where

(3.2)

J_{B}:=\{(x_{n})_{n}\in B_{\infty}\mid\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\tau\in T(B)}\tau(x_{n}^{*}x_{n})=0\}

and
B^{\infty}:=B_{\infty}/J_{B}
. In the purely infinite setting, there are no traces, so we will need to alter the definition. A natural candidate to replace traces would be states.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\rho
be a state on a
C^{*}
-algebra
B
. We define the
\rho
-norm
as

(3.3)

\|b\|_{\rho}:=\sqrt{\rho(b^{*}b)}\quad(b\in B).

Note that this is only a seminorm in general, but it is a norm if (and only if)
\rho
is faithful. We also define the
(\rho,\#)
-norm
as

(3.4)

\|b\|_{\rho,\#}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|b\|_{\rho}+\|b^{*}\|_{\rho}\right)\quad(b\in B).

Remark 3.2
.

The
\rho
- and
(\rho,\#)
-norms are not submultiplicative in general. However, we do have the following identity

(3.5)

\|xy\|_{\rho}=\sqrt{\rho(y^{*}x^{*}xy)}\leq\|x\|\sqrt{\rho(y^{*}y)}=\|x\|\|y\|_{\rho}

for all
x,y\in B
by positivity of
\rho
, and subsequently also

(3.6)

2\|xy\|_{\rho,\#}\leq\|x\|\|y\|_{\rho}+\|y^{*}\|\|x^{*}\|_{\rho}\leq\|x\|\|y\|_{\rho,\#}+\|x\|_{\rho,\#}\|y\|\quad(x,y\in B).

Moreover, we have

(3.7)

2\|b\|_{\rho,\#}=\sqrt{\rho(b^{*}b)}+\sqrt{\rho(bb^{*})}\leq\sqrt{\|b^{*}b\|}+\sqrt{\|bb^{*}\|}=2\|b\|

for all
b\in B
, showing that the
(\rho,\#)
-norm (and by a similar argument, also the
\rho
-norm) is dominated by the norm on
B
.

One of the main conceptual reasons for including all traces in the stably finite setting, is because this tracial data can be used to access comparison, due to
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stability of
B
and the Toms-Winter conjecture. In the purely infinite setting, however, comparison comes for free. This allows us to limit ourselves to a single state to construct an analogue of the trace-kernel extension, as long as it satisfies certain properties. The main difference when working with states instead of traces, is that the analogue of the trace-kernel
J_{B}
will now no longer be an ideal in
B_{\infty}
. To obtain a short exact sequence, we will therefore need to restrict
B_{\infty}
to a suitable
C^{*}
-subalgebra in which this analogue is an ideal.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\rho
be a state on a
C^{*}
-algebra
B
. We define the
state-kernel
of
B
with respect to
\rho
as the set

(3.8)

J_{B,\rho}:=\{(x_{n})_{n}\in B_{\infty}\mid\lim_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}\|_{\rho,\#}=0\}.

We also define

(3.9)

S_{B,\rho}:=\{(x_{n})_{n}\in B_{\infty}\mid(x_{n})_{n}\text{ is $(\rho,\#)$-Cauchy}\}.

Observe that these are well-defined subsets of
B_{\infty}
, as sequences which tend to zero in norm, also tend to zero in
(\rho,\#)
-norm by (
3.7
), and in particular are
(\rho,\#)
-Cauchy. As mentioned earlier, the state-kernel
J_{B,\rho}
is not an ideal in general, but it is a hereditary
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
B_{\infty}
. This we will now proceed to show.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\rho
be a state on a
C^{*}
-algebra
B
. Then
J_{B,\rho}
is a well-defined, hereditary
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
B_{\infty}
.

Proof.

As
\|\cdot\|_{\rho,\#}
satisfies the triangle equality and (
3.6
), it follows immediately that it is closed under sums and products. It is clear from the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{\rho,\#}
that
J_{B,\rho}
is also closed under adjoints, so we only need to show that it is closed. To this end, let
(x^{(i)})_{i\in I}
be a net in
J_{B,\rho}
converging to some
x\in B_{\infty}
. Fixing representative sequences
(x^{(i)}_{n})_{n}
(
i\in I
) resp.
(x_{n})_{n}
in
\ell^{\infty}(B)
, we then have

(3.10)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|x^{(i)}_{n}\|_{\rho,\#}=0

for all
i\in I
and

(3.11)

0=\lim_{i\to\infty}\|x-x^{(i)}\|=\lim_{i\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}-x^{(i)}_{n}\|.

It follows that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}\|_{\rho,\#}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}^{(i)}\|_{\rho,\#}+\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}-x_{n}^{(i)}\|_{\rho,\#}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}^{(i)}-x_{n}\|

for all
i\in I
, where for the second inequality we used (
3.7
). As the right-hand side tends to zero for
i\to\infty
, we find that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}\|_{\rho,\#}=0
. Hence,
x
is indeed in
J_{B,\rho}
, proving that
J_{B,\rho}
is closed.

To show that it is hereditary in
B_{\infty}
, let
x\in J_{B,\rho}
and

be two positive elements satisfying

, which implies that
y\in\overline{xB_{\infty}x}
. As we now know that
J_{B,\rho}
is closed, we can assume without loss of generality that
y=xzx
for some

. Fixing positive representative sequences
(x_{n})_{n}
and
(z_{n})_{n}
in
\ell^{\infty}(B)
of

, it follows that

(3.13)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}z_{n}x_{n}\|_{\rho,\#}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}z_{n}x_{n}\|_{\rho}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}z_{n}\|\|x_{n}\|_{\rho}=0,

where we used (
3.5
) for the inequality. Hence, we can conclude that

, showing that
J_{B,\rho}
is closed.
∎

A priori, there is no reason to believe that
J_{B,\rho}
is indeed an ideal in
S_{B,\rho}
, nor that

is even a
C^{*}
-algebra, due to the lack of submultiplicativity of the state norm. To ensure that these facts are indeed true, we need the state to be strongly faithful, a property we will now define.

Definition 3.5
.

Let

be a
C^{*}
-algebra,

a state on

.

Remark 3.6
.

Faithfulness of the state and faithfulness of its extension to

are not related in general. On the one hand,

being faithful does not force

to be faithful, as the latter is on the level of

, while the former is on the level of

;

can have a non-trivial kernel. On the other hand, faithful states are not necessarily strongly faithful. Consider

Proposition 3.7

\rho

\displaystyle J_{B,\rho}

\displaystyle=\{(x_{n})_{n}\in B_{\infty}\mid\mathop{\operatorname{s^{*}-lim}}_{n\to\infty}\pi_{\rho}(x_{n})=0\},

\displaystyle=\{(x_{n})_{n}\in B_{\infty}\mid\mathop{\operatorname{s^{*}-lim}}_{n\to\infty}\pi_{\rho}(x_{n})\text{ exists}\}.

The above proposition suggests a clear candidate for the quotient in the reduced state-kernel extension: the von Neumann algebra

Proposition 3.8

(x^{(i)}_{n})_{n}

In particular, we have

*
-strong limit of sequences in
S_{B,\rho}
exists by Proposition
3.7
, and it is independent of the choice of representative because sequences in
B
which converge in norm to zero, also converge
*
-strongly to zero in
\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}
. It is a
*
-homomorphism because addition, the adjoint operation (by definition of the
*
-strong topology) and multiplication are
*
-strongly continuous on bounded sequences. From Proposition
3.7
, it is immediate that the kernel of
q_{B}
is equal to
J_{B,\rho}
. To see that it is surjective, let
x\in\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}
and use Kaplansky’s density theorem to find a sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
in
B
with norm bounded by
\|x\|
and such that
\pi_{\rho}(x_{n})
converges
*
-strongly to
x
. Then
(x_{n})_{n}
is a
(\rho,\#)
-Cauchy sequence in
\ell^{\infty}(B)
, so it induces an element of
S_{B,\rho}
, which satisfies

(3.25)

q_{B}(\pi_{\infty}(x_{n})_{n})=\mathop{\operatorname{s^{*}-lim}}_{n\to\infty}\pi_{\rho}(x_{n})=x,

proving that
q_{B}
is surjective. For the second part of the statement, we compute for arbitrary
(x_{n})_{n}\in S_{B,\rho}

(3.26)

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime\prime}\circ q_{B}((x_{n})_{n})

\displaystyle=\langle\left(\mathop{\operatorname{s^{*}-lim}}_{n\to\infty}\pi_{\rho}(x_{n})\right)\xi_{\rho},\xi_{\rho}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi_{\rho}(x_{n})\xi_{\rho},\xi_{\rho}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\pi_{\rho}(x_{n})\xi_{\rho},\xi_{\rho}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(x_{n}).

From this fact, it also follows that

(3.27)

\displaystyle 2\|q_{B}((x_{n})_{n})\|_{\rho^{\prime\prime},\#}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\rho^{\prime\prime}(q_{B}((x_{n}^{*}x_{n})_{n}))}+\sqrt{\rho^{\prime\prime}(q_{B}((x_{n}x_{n}^{*})_{n}))}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{\rho(x_{n}^{*}x_{n})}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{\rho(x_{n}x_{n}^{*})}

\displaystyle=2\lim_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}\|_{\rho,\#},

concluding the proof.
∎

Definition 3.11
.

be a

. Using the previous corollary, we define the

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

Remark 3.12
.

One can observe that

is not the largest possible

-subalgebra of

in which

is an ideal. That would be the normalizer

of

. One could absolutely also work with the normalizer, but opting for

comes with a number of advantages. The quotient

is always a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state

appropriately such that

would no longer be a von Neumann algebra in the sequence algebra setting. It would be if we were working in

; the corresponding quotient is known as the Ocneanu ultrapower of

, and is a well-studied object in von Neumann theory (see for example
[
27
]
for the original construction by Ocneanu, and
[
2
]
for a more recent treatment). However, there are a number of reasons we want to work in the sequence algebra as much as possible. The main reason is to appeal to so-called intertwining by reparametrization (
[

, Theorem 4.3]
and our adaptation, Theorem

) in order to check that a given map into

factors through

. This is a crucial step in going from a classification of maps into

to a classification of maps into

itself.

3.2.
The reduced state-kernel extension in

As mentioned earlier, we would ideally like to work entirely in the sequence algebra setting. However, there is an important downside to doing so: the sequence algebra

3.3.
Dependence on choice of state

A very natural question would be which influence the choice of strongly faithful state has on the reduced state-kernel extension. In this section, we will show that
J_{B,\rho}
only depends on the underlying representation. We will also show that strongly faithful states always exist for the class of
C^{*}
-algebras of our interest. First, we recall the following standard result.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state
\psi
. Suppose
B
is a strongly dense
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
M
, and consider the GNS representation
\pi_{\rho}:B\to B({\mathcal{H}}_{\rho})
associated to
\rho:=\psi|_{B}
. There exists an isomorphism

(3.37)

\alpha:\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}\to M

such that
\rho^{\prime\prime}=\psi\circ\alpha
, with
\rho^{\prime\prime}
the natural extension of
\rho
to
\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}
.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
B
be a separable
C^{*}
-algebra. Any faithful, non-degenerate representation
\pi:B\to B({\mathcal{H}})
onto a separable Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
gives a strongly faithful state
\rho
on
B
. Moreover, all faithful, normal states on
\pi(B)^{\prime\prime}
produce the same state-kernel
J_{B,\rho}
.

Proof.

Fix a faithful, non-degenerate representation
\pi:B\to B({\mathcal{H}})
of
B
onto a separable Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. By faithfulness of
\pi
, we can identify
B
with its image under
\pi
. Let
(\xi_{n})_{n}
be a countable orthonormal basis for
{\mathcal{H}}
, and define the map

(3.38)

\psi:B({\mathcal{H}})\to\mathbb{C}:x\mapsto\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\langle x\xi_{n},\xi_{n}\rangle.

Note that it defines a faithful normal state on
B({\mathcal{H}})
, because
(\xi_{n})_{n}
is an orthonormal basis. Now consider
M:=B^{\prime\prime}
. By definition,
B
is a strongly dense
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
M
and
\psi|_{M}
is faithful normal state on
M
. By Lemma
3.15
, it then follows that
\rho:=\psi|_{B}
is a faithful state on
B
such that

(3.39)

M\cong\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}

where
\pi_{\rho}
is the GNS representation associated to
\rho
, and such that
\psi|_{M}
corresponds to the natural extension
\rho^{\prime\prime}
of
\rho
to
\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}
. As
\psi|_{M}
is faithful on
M
, it follows that
\rho^{\prime\prime}
is faithful on
\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}
. All faithful states on
\pi(B)^{\prime\prime}
produce the same state-kernel
J_{B,\rho}
by the formula for
J_{B,\rho}
in Proposition
3.7
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.17
.

Let
B
be a separable
C^{*}
-algebra which admits a faithful irreducible representation. Then there exists a strongly faithful state
\rho
on
B
such that
\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}\cong B({\mathcal{H}})
for some separable Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:B\to B({\mathcal{H}})
be a faithful irreducible representation of
B
onto a separable Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, and consider the induced strongly faithful state
\rho
on
B
by the previous proposition. From (
3.39
) and irreducibility of
\pi
, it then follows that

(3.40)

\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}\cong\pi(B)^{\prime\prime}=B({\mathcal{H}}),

as desired.
∎

Remark 3.18
.

It is important to point out that the above corollary does
not
state that
\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}
is equal to
B({\mathcal{H}}_{\rho})
, or in other words that
\rho
is pure (which would contradict what was noted in Remark
3.6
). It simply gives an isomorphism with the bounded operators on some separable Hilbert space.

4.
KK
-existence and -uniqueness

4.1.
The Cuntz pair picture of
KK
- and
KL
-theory

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\varphi,\psi:A\to E
be be two
*
-homomorphisms between
C^{*}
-algebras
A
and
E
.

•

Suppose
E
contains a
C^{*}
-algebra
I
as an ideal. The pair
(\varphi,\psi)
is said to be a
Cuntz pair
if
\varphi(a)-\psi(a)\in I
for all
a\in A
. We will denote this as
(\varphi,\psi):A\rightrightarrows E\rhd I
.

•

Suppose
E={\mathcal{M}(I)}
for some
C^{*}
-algebra
I
. Two Cuntz pairs
(\varphi_{i},\psi_{i}):A\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{M}(I)}\rhd I
(
i=0,1
) are said to be
homotopic
if there exists a Cuntz pair

(4.1)

(\Phi,\Psi):A\rightrightarrows\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}([0,1],{\mathcal{M}(I)})\rhd\mathcal{C}([0,1],I)

such that evaluation at
i
gives the Cuntz pair
(\varphi_{i},\psi_{i})
for
i=0,1
.

•

Suppose
E
contains a unital copy of
{\mathcal{O}_{2}}
, i.e. isometries
s_{1},s_{2}\in E
satisfying
s_{1}s_{1}^{*}+s_{2}s_{2}^{*}=1_{E}
. The
Cuntz sum
of
\varphi
and
\psi
is defined as

(4.2)

(\varphi\oplus_{s_{1},s_{2}}\psi)(a):=s_{1}\varphi(a)s_{1}^{*}+s_{1}\varphi(a)s_{1}^{*}\quad(a\in A).

The Cuntz sum is independent of the choice of
{\mathcal{O}_{2}}
-isometries up to unitary equivalence. We will often omit the explicit mention of
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
and simply write
\varphi\oplus\psi
in situations where the specific choice does not matter.

The Cuntz pair picture of
KK
is defined as follows. Given a separable
C^{*}
-algebra
A
and a
\sigma
-unital
C^{*}
-algebra
I
, let
KK(A,I)
be the set of Cuntz pairs
(\varphi,\psi):A\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{M}(I\otimes{\mathcal{K}})}\rhd I\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
up to Cuntz pair homotopy. We will denote class of such a Cuntz pair as
[\varphi,\psi]_{KK(A,I)}
. As
{\mathcal{M}(I\otimes{\mathcal{K}})}
contains a unital copy of
B({\mathcal{H}})
, we can define a binary operation on
KK(A,I)
by the Cuntz sum: for two Cuntz pairs
(\varphi_{i},\psi_{i}):A\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{M}(I)}\rhd I
(
i=1,2
), we write

(4.3)

[\varphi_{1},\psi_{1}]_{KK(A,I)}+[\varphi_{2},\psi_{2}]_{KK(A,I)}:=[\varphi_{1}\oplus\varphi_{2},\psi_{1}\oplus\psi_{2}]_{KK(A,I)}.

This turns
KK(A,I)
into an abelian group with neutral element
[\varphi,\varphi]_{KK(A,I)}
, where
\varphi:A\to{\mathcal{M}(I\otimes{\mathcal{K}})}
is any
*
-homomorphism. The inverse of an element
[\varphi,\psi]_{KK(A,I)}
is
[\psi,\varphi]_{KK(A,I)}
.

For a
*
-homomorphism
\varphi:A\to I
, we will denote
[\varphi]_{KK(A,I)}:=[\varphi,0]_{KK(A,I)}
. We can also define the
KK
-class
[\varphi,\psi]_{KK(A,I)}
of an arbitrary Cuntz pair
(\varphi,\psi):A\rightrightarrows E\rhd I
as the class of

(4.4)

\big(\sigma\circ(\varphi\otimes e),\sigma(\psi\otimes e)\big):A\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{M}(I\otimes{\mathcal{K}})}\rhd I\otimes{\mathcal{K}},

where
\sigma:E\otimes{\mathcal{K}}\to{\mathcal{M}(I\otimes{\mathcal{K}})}
is the canonical map induced by the inclusion
I\otimes{\mathcal{K}}\subseteq E\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
, and
e\in{\mathcal{K}}
is a rank-one projection.

A key feature of
KK(\cdot,\cdot)
is that it is a bifunctor. We can define

when
I
is not necessarily
\sigma
-unital, by the inductive limit

(4.5)

KK(A,I):=\varinjlim_{I_{0}\text{ sep.}}KK(A,I_{0})

ranging over all separable
C^{*}
-subalgebras
I_{0}
of
I
ordered by inclusion, with connecting maps
KK(A,\iota_{I_{0}\subseteq I_{1}})
for
I_{0}\subseteq I_{1}
. A Cuntz pair
(\varphi,\psi):A\rightrightarrows E\rhd I
induces a class in
KK(A,I)
as the image in the inductive limit of the element
[\varphi|^{E_{0}},\psi|^{E_{0}}]_{KK(A,I_{0})}
, where
E_{0}\subseteq E
and
I_{0}\subseteq I
are separable
C^{*}
-subalgebras such that
E_{0}
contains
I_{0}
as an ideal, contains the images of
\varphi
and
\psi
and
(\varphi|^{E_{0}},\psi|^{E_{0}})
is a Cuntz pair. In
[
6
, Appendix B]
, it is shown that this construction is well-defined.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
A
and
I
be
C^{*}
-algebras with
A
separable. Let

be the one-point compactification of

and

where

\displaystyle Z_{KK(A,I)}:=\{KK(A,\ev_{\infty})(\kappa)\mid\kappa\in KK(A,\mathcal{C}(\overline{\mathbb{N}},I)),KK(A,\ev_{n})(\kappa)=0\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\}

and

to

.

is a bifunctor with the following properties. Suppose

and

4.2.

Definition 4.6
.

\varphi\oplus_{\pi_{I}(s_{1}),\pi_{I}(s_{2})}\theta|_{A}=\Ad_{\pi_{I}(u)}\circ\varphi

is the quotient map and

and

-isometries. Then for all

(4.18)

\displaystyle(\varphi^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\oplus_{\pi_{I}(s_{1}),\pi_{I}(s_{2})}\theta)(a+\lambda 1_{A^{\dagger}})

\displaystyle=\pi_{I}(s_{1})(\varphi(a)+\lambda 1_{{\mathcal{Q}(I)}})\pi_{I}(s_{1})^{*}+\pi_{I}(s_{2})(\theta(a)+\lambda 1_{{\mathcal{Q}(I)}})\pi_{I}(s_{2})^{*}

\displaystyle=\pi_{I}(s_{1})\varphi(a)\pi_{I}(s_{1})^{*}+\pi_{I}(s_{1})\theta(a)\pi_{I}(s_{1})^{*}+\lambda\pi_{I}(s_{1}s_{1}^{*}+s_{2}s_{2}^{*})

\displaystyle=\Ad_{\pi_{I}(u)}\circ\varphi(a)+\lambda 1_{{\mathcal{Q}(I)}}=\Ad_{\pi_{I}(u)}\circ\varphi^{\dagger}(a+\lambda 1_{A^{\dagger}}),

which shows that
\varphi^{\dagger}
absorbs
\theta
.

Now suppose
\varphi^{\dagger}
is unitally absorbing and
\theta:A\to{\mathcal{Q}(I)}
is a
*
-homomorphism with a
*
-homomorphic lift

. We then claim that

lifts

. Indeed, for all

and

\displaystyle q\circ\gamma^{\dagger}(a+\lambda 1_{A^{\dagger}})

\displaystyle=\pi_{I}(\gamma(a))+\lambda\pi_{I}(1_{{\mathcal{M}(I)}})=\theta(a)+\lambda 1_{{\mathcal{Q}(I)}}

\displaystyle=\theta^{\dagger}(a+\lambda 1_{A^{\dagger}}).

Hence,

has a unital

\varphi^{\dagger}
absorbs

Pure largeness is a property of extensions originally introduced by Elliott and Kucerovsky in their landmark paper
[
14
]
. In this paper, they showed that this property is equivalent to the associated Busby map being unitally absorbing under the right conditions. This result gives us a concrete criterion to verify in order to obtain absorption of the relevant maps. Recall that for two positive elements
a
and
b
in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A_{+}
,
a
is said to be
Cuntz subequivalent
to
b
, denoted
a\precsim b
, if for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
c\in A
such that
a\approx_{\varepsilon}b
. We write
a\sim b
if
a\precsim b
and
b\precsim a
.

Definition 4.8
.

An extension

(4.20)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{e}:0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

is said to be
purely large
if for each
y\in I_{+}
and
x\in E_{+}\setminus I
, we have
y\precsim x
in
E
.

Remark 4.9
.

Our definition of pure largeness is different from the one stated by Elliott and Kucerovsky in
[
14
]
. As we will show in Proposition
4.14
, the two are equivalent when the ideal is
\sigma
-unital and stable (which is the setting in which their theorem is stated); in fact, stability of the ideal will be automatic from our definition in the
\sigma
-unital setting.

We start by making the important observation that one can always control the norm of the element implementing the Cuntz subequivalence. This will be important later down the line, specifically in Proposition

.

6.
Classification

At this point, we have all the main technical ingredients to make the classification machinery work. The classification consists of three major steps. First, we classify lifts of unitizably full maps into the quotient
\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}
. The proof is very similar to its stably finite counterpart
[
6
, Theorem 7.1]
; the main difference lies in the fact that our
KK
-uniqueness result is on the level of the ultrapower, so we will only obtain uniqueness on that level. The second step consists of using the first to obtain a classification result for unital and full maps into
S_{B,\rho}
. Unlike in the tracial setting, obtaining a classification of maps into the middle algebra from the classification of lifts is basically immediate, as the quotient is trivial in
KK
and does not carry any important (tracial) data that needs to be kept track of. From this viewpoint, the classification of lifts is in fact a stronger result. The main content of the proof is to turn a result in terms of unitizably full maps into a result in terms of unital and full maps (which is done separately in
[
6
]
). This is achieved with the same “de-unitization” trick (
[
6
, Proposition 7.11]
); only the details of the proof are different. In the final step, we prove the desired classification of embeddings
A\hookrightarrow B
by
\widetilde{KL}(A,B)
, a quotient of
KL
. It is straightforward to see that
\widetilde{KL}
is equal to
KL
when
A
satisfies the UCT, as will be shown in the proof of Theorem
6.17
. Without this assumption, one can still compare our result with the original Kirchberg-Phillips theorems, and observe that
KL
and
\widetilde{KL}
classify the same class of
*
-homomorphisms up to the same notion of equivalence. Hence, they will coincide a posteriori.

Classification of maps into
S_{B,\rho}

The goal of this section is to mold the classification of lifts we just obtained into a classification of unital maps into

Theorem 6.4

(Classification of unital full maps into

)
.

Let

-algebra,

-algebra and

a)

Existence: for any

such that
\Gamma_{0}^{(A,S_{B,\rho})}(\kappa)[1_{A}]_{0}=[1_{S_{B,\rho}}]_{0}

-homomorphism

,

b)

Note that we do not obtain a complete classification of maps into

Remark 6.5
.

Up until Theorem
6.4
, we had not yet imposed any conditions on
\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}
. We now need to do so in order to gain access to a uniqueness theorem for maps into
\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}
. By Corollary
3.17
, we know that we can always arrange
\pi_{\rho}(B)^{\prime\prime}\cong B({\mathcal{H}})
in our setting, for which Voiculescu’s theorem gives uniqueness of unital and full
*
-homomorphisms up to approximate unitary equivalence. In other words, we do not need the very deep and technical classification of type-III factors to classify their
C^{*}
-algebraic analogue; the uniqueness result for maps into type-I factors suffices.

Remark 6.6
.

Theorem
6.4
combines
[
6
, Theorem 7.10]
and
[
6
, Theorem 9.1]
into a single step. We do so because our setting comes with some additional subtleties which complicate matters. Our uniqueness result for maps into the quotient is Voiculescu’s theorem, i.e. only up to approximate unitary equivalence instead of unitary equivalence (as in
[
6
, Theorem 6.5]
). As one cannot reindex in
S_{B,\rho}
or

, this entails that in the proof of existence, we can no longer conjugate the map

realizing a given

by a single unitary to make it lift a given

*
-homomorphism. Therefore, we can simply pick a map

to apply the classification of lifts to and disregard the fact that the resulting

no longer lifts

\|\Ad(u)\circ\pi_{\infty\to\omega}\circ\eta^{*}\circ\rho(a)-\pi_{\infty\to\omega}\circ\rho(a)\|<\varepsilon

\pi_{\infty}(\rho_{n_{k}}(a))_{k}=\eta^{*}\circ\rho(a)\quad(a\in A)

by definition of

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\omega}\|u_{k}\rho_{n_{k}}(a)u_{k}^{*}-\rho_{k}(a)\|

\displaystyle=\|\pi_{\omega}\big(u_{k}\rho_{n_{k}}(a)u_{k}^{*}-\rho_{k}(a)\big)_{k}\|

\displaystyle=\|u(\pi_{\infty\to\omega}\circ\eta^{*}\circ\rho(a))u^{*}-\pi_{\infty\to\omega}\circ\rho(a)\|<\varepsilon

Theorem 6.13

(Intertwining by reparametrization)
.

Let
A
be a separable and
B
a unital
C^{*}
-algebra, and
\rho:A\to B_{\infty}
a
*
-homomorphism. Then

-homomorphism factoring through

and
\pi_{\infty\to\omega}\circ\eta^{*}\circ\rho

Proof.

Let
(F_{n})_{n}
be an increasing sequence of finite subsets of

such that their union is dense. Fix an arbitrary lift
(\rho_{n})_{n}:A\to\ell^{\infty}(B)
of
\rho
, and recursively apply the previous lemma for

,
\varepsilon=2^{-n}
and

(defining

) to obtain a sequence
1=k_{0}<k_{1}<k_{2}<...
and a sequence
(u_{n})_{n}
of unitaries in

(6.40)

\|u_{n}\rho_{k_{n+1}}(x)u_{n}^{*}-\rho_{k_{n}}(x)\|<2^{-n}

for all

and

. Defining

\psi:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Ad(v_{n})\circ\rho_{k_{n+1}}

, pick

\|v_{m}\rho_{k_{m+1}}(b)v_{m}^{*}-v_{n}\rho_{k_{n+1}}(b)v_{n}^{*}\|\leq\sum_{j=n+1}^{m}2^{-j}\leq\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}2^{-j}=2^{-n-1}.

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|\rho_{k}(a)-\rho_{k}(b)\|=\|\rho(a)-\rho(b)\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3},

\|v_{m}\rho_{k_{m+1}}(a)v_{m}^{*}-v_{n}\rho_{k_{n+1}}(a)v_{n}^{*}\|<\varepsilon,

proving that

To show that it is approximately unitarily equivalent to
\rho
in

, let

be the unitary induced by

(6.46)

\eta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}:n\mapsto k_{n+1}.

By construction of
\psi

(6.47)

\iota_{B}\circ\psi=\Ad(v)\circ\eta^{*}\circ\rho,

so by assumption,
\pi_{\infty\to\omega}\circ\iota_{B}\circ\psi

∎

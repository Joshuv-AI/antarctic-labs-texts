Gravitational waves constraints of the effective gravitational coupling
Antonio Enea Romano 1,2

Abstract
The effective field theory (EFT) of dark energy allows to derive a consistency relation (CR) between the effective gravitational constant, the gravitational and electromagnetic luminosity (EM) distance and the speed of gravitational waves (GW), in agreement with the results obtained in some luminal modified gravity theories. We test the CR by comparing the constraints obtained from large scale structure (LSS) observations with those from GW events with and without electromagnetic counterparts, confirming the validity of the CR at the current level of experimental uncertainty. The GW event GW170817 and its electromagnetic counterpart provides a constraint of the effective gravitational constant with an accuracy comparable with LSS constraints, while the analysis of GW events without electromagnetic counterpart are consistent, but do not have a constraining power comparable to LSS observations.



I
Introduction

The theory of General Relativity (GR)
Einstein:1915ca
have been tested with different types of astrophysical and cosmological observations and it provides the foundation of the standard cosmological model.
It has proven successful not only in explaining observations that classical gravity
Newton:1687eqk
could account for, such as the precession of Mercury’s perihelion, but also in predicting previously unobserved phenomena, such as gravitational lensing or gravitational waves. Despite these unquestionable successes, modified gravity theories (MGT) have studied extensively, motivated for example by the goal to provide a fundamental explanation of dark energy effects.
In the framework of theoretical physics MGTs can be formulated in terms of an action which modifies the Einstein-Hilbert action, and the theoretical predictions can then computed using cosmological perturbation theory or numerical simulations. Large scale structure observations allow to test the effects of MGTs on scalar perturbations, and can be conveniently understood in terms of an effective gravitational constant, while GWs observations
LIGOScientific:2016aoc
allow to study the MGTs effects on tensor perturbations.
Since both LSS and GWs observations can be affected by the MGTs effects derived from the same action, it is expected that the MGTs constraints from these two sets of observations should be somehow related.

In this regard the effective field theory of dark energy
Gubitosi:2012hu
is the ideal tool to investigate the relation between the MGTs effects on different observables, allowing to derive a consistency relation (CR) between the effective gravitational constant, the gravitational and electromagnetic luminosity distance and the speed of gravitational waves (GW), generalizing the results obtained in some luminal modified gravity theories. We apply the consistency relation to map GW-EMW distance ratio observational constraints to effective gravitational constant constraints, showing that the two sets of observations are indeed consistent.

II
GW effective action

The quadratic effective field theory action (EFT) of perturbations for a single scalar dark energy field was derived in
Gleyzes:2013ooa
.
The EFT action for tensor modes
Gubitosi:2012hu
is

S_{\gamma}^{(2)}=\int d^{4}x\,a^{3}\frac{M_{P}^{2}f}{8}\frac{1}{v^{2}_{\rm GW}}\left[\dot{\gamma}_{ij}^{2}-\frac{v^{2}_{\rm GW}}{a^{2}}(\partial_{k}\gamma_{ij})^{2}\right]\,,

(1)

where the GWs speed is related to the EFT action coefficients by

v_{\rm GW}^{2}=\left(1+\frac{2m_{4}^{2}}{M_{P}^{2}f}\right)^{-1}\;.

(2)

III
Gravitational wave luminosity distance

In the literature of modified gravity the quantity
M^{2}_{*}=M_{P}^{2}f/(8\,v_{\rm GW}^{2})
is often introduced, in terms of which the action, using conformal time, takes the form

S_{\gamma}^{(2)}=\int d^{4}x\,a^{2}M^{2}_{*}\left[{\gamma^{\prime}}_{ij}^{2}-v^{2}_{\rm GW}(\partial_{k}\gamma_{ij})^{2}\right]\,.

(3)

Note that
v_{\rm GW}
depends on the ratio of two coefficients of the EFT action,
m_{4}
and
f
, so that observational constraints on
v_{\rm GW}
are mapped into constraints of this ratio, not of the individual coefficients of the action.
After defining
\Omega=M_{p}\sqrt{f/8}
the EOM corresponding to the effective action is

\gamma_{ij}^{\prime\prime}+2\mathcal{H}\Big(1-\frac{v_{\rm GW}^{\prime}}{\mathcal{H}v_{\rm GW}}+\frac{\Omega^{\prime}}{\mathcal{H}\Omega}\Big)\gamma_{ij}^{\prime}-v^{2}_{\rm GW}\nabla^{2}\gamma_{ij}=0\,,

(4)

which can be solved with a WKB approximation
Nishizawa:2017nef
on sub-horizon scales, allowing to derive the effect on the GW luminosity distance

r_{d}(z)=\frac{d^{\rm GW}_{\rm L}(z)}{d^{\rm EM}_{\rm L}(z)}=\frac{\Omega(0)}{\Omega(z)}\sqrt{\frac{v_{\rm GW}(z)}{v_{\rm GW}(0)}}=\sqrt{\frac{f(0)v_{\rm GW}(z)}{f(z)v_{\rm GW}(0)}}\,.

(5)

IV
Effective gravitational constant

As an example let’s consider a theory differing form GR only by the function
f(t)
. In the EFT formalism the scalar perturbations equations give a modified Poisson’s equation of the form

\nabla^{2}\Phi=4\pi\,G_{eff}a^{2}\delta\rho_{m}\,,

(6)

where the effective gravitational constant is
Gubitosi:2012hu

G_{eff}=\frac{1}{8\pi M_{P}^{2}f}\,\frac{c+M_{P}^{2}\dot{f}^{2}/f}{c+\frac{3}{4}M_{P}^{2}\dot{f}^{2}/f}\,,

(7)

and the function
c
is given by

\displaystyle c\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{2}(-\ddot{f}+H\dot{f})M_{P}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\rho_{D}+p_{D})\;.

(8)

In the above equation
\rho_{D}
and
p_{D}
denote the dark energy energy density and pressure,
H
is the Hubble parameter, and we are only considering explicitly the effects due to a varying
f(t)
.
Large scale structure observations set stringent
Ishak:2024jhs
constrains on the redshift evolution of
G_{eff}
, implying that
f(t)
must be a slowly varying function of time. It is hence a good approximation to assume that
c\gg M_{P}^{2}\dot{f}^{2}/f
in eq.(
7
), implying

G_{eff}(t)\approx\frac{1}{8\pi M_{P}^{2}f(t)}.

(9)

The above approximation corresponds to assuming
\dot{f}H^{-1}\ll f
, i.e. a negligible variation of
f
on a cosmological time scale.

A more general expression for
G_{eff}
can be found in
Linder:2015rcz
in terms of other coefficients of the EFT action, which in terms of the property functions
Bellini:2014fua
gives

\frac{G_{eff}}{G_{N}}=\frac{2M_{p}^{2}}{M_{\star}^{2}}\frac{[\alpha_{B}(1+\alpha_{T})+2(\alpha_{M}-\alpha_{T})]+\alpha_{B}^{\prime}}{(2-\alpha_{B})[\alpha_{B}(1+\alpha_{T})+2(\alpha_{M}-\alpha_{T})]+2\alpha_{B}^{\prime}}\ ,

(10)

In the no braiding (NB) limit, in which
\alpha_{B}
is negligible, we get

G_{eff}=\frac{1}{8\pi M_{P}^{2}f}\left[\frac{{v_{\rm GW}(z)}}{{v_{\rm GW}(0)}}\right]^{2}\,.

(11)

V
Consistency relation

Since both
G_{eff}
and the distance ratio
r_{d}
depend on the EFT function
f
, we can combine eq.(
11
) with eq.(
5
) to obtain a consistency relation (CR) between gravitational waves and large scale observations
Romano:2025pcs

8\pi M_{p}^{2}\,G_{eff}(z)=\left[\frac{d^{\rm GW}_{\rm L}(z)}{d^{\rm EM}_{\rm L}(z)}\right]^{2}\frac{{v_{\rm GW}(z)}}{{v_{\rm GW}(0)}}\,,

(12)

where we have assumed
f(0)=1
to account for local constraints, i.e.
G_{eff}(0)=G_{N}=1/8\pi M_{p}^{2}
, and
z
is the redshift. The above equation establishes a simple and intuitive relation between different observables which could be affected by gravity modification: the electromagnetic and gravitational luminosity distances, the effective gravitational coupling, and the speed of gravitational waves. The l.h.s. involves large scale structure observations, while the r.h.s. is related to gravitational waves observations. Alternatively it can be considered a consistency relation between scalar and tensor perturbations.

The consistency condition is in agreement with and generalizes the theoretical results obtained in some luminal theories of modified gravity such as no-slip Horndeski theories
Linder:2018jil
and non local theories
Belgacem:2017ihm
.
Note that the CR is also satisfied by general relativity (GR), since in this case
v_{\rm GW}=1
,
G_{N}=1/8\pi M_{p}^{2}
, and
d^{\rm GW}_{\rm L}=d^{\rm GW}_{\rm L}
. This is expected, since GR is just another theory which can be formulated in the EFT framework.

and the GW-EMW distance ratio.

For the event GW170817

VII
Conclusions

The EFT of dark energy allows to derive a consistency condition between the effective gravitational constant, the GW and EMW luminosity distances and the GWs speed, in agreement with the theoretical results obtained for some luminal gravity theories.
We have tested the consistency condition by comparing the constraints on the effective gravitational constant from LSS and GW observations, confirming its validity at
68\%
confidence level.
A violation of the CR would imply that the modified gravity effects are due to a theory which cannot be described by the EFT, for example excluding all Horndeski theories.
Since the GW strain is inversely proportional to the GW luminosity distance, while the apparent magnitude of galaxies is inversely proportional to the square of the electromagnetic luminosity distance, the CR allows to obtain high redshift estimations of the effective gravitational constant using GW events with an EM counterpart, at distances where large scale structure observations are not available or are not very precise, due to selection effects.
The future availability of an increasing number of dark sirens and bright sirens observations will allow the improve the constraints presented in this paper.

In our analysis we have used the EFT results for the effective gravitational constant and the GW-EMW distance ratio, which are based on a quadratic order action, but higher order effects
Romano:2022jeh
;
Romano:2023bzn
;
Romano:2024apw
are expected to introduce a possible scale and polarization dependency of the distance ratio. In the future it will be interesting to investigate how this scale dependency is related to that of the effective gravitational constant.

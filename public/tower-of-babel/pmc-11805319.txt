Efficacy of Flipped Classroom and Didactic Lecture Approaches in Undergraduate Respiratory Medicine Education
Authors: Ramesh Pandey, Satyendra Mishra, Manoj Sahu, Talha Saad, Nitu Mishra, Saumya Saxena
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:This study used a mixed-methods design, analyzing quantitative pre- and post-assessment scores alongside qualitative student feedback. Undergraduate students in Respiratory Medicine were randomly assigned to either the Flipped Classroom or Didactic Lecture groups.Method:The study compares the effectiveness and perceived impact of the Flipped Classroom versus Didactic Lecture approaches in Undergraduate Respiratory Medicine Education.Results:The Flipped Classroom group showed significantly higher post-assessment scores and positive feedback, highlighting increased engagement, collaborative learning, and self-directed study.Conclusion:The study supports the effectiveness of the Flipped Classroom over traditional lectures, emphasizing active student involvement and personalized learning in Respiratory Medicine education.

INTRODUCTION

To improve work quality, communication skills, collaboration with interdisciplinary teams, and patient adherence to treatment, medical educators are always experimenting with new approaches.[1] Given this, the long-standing didactic lecture model has encountered difficulties in preparing students’ knowledge, abilities, attitudes, and behaviors for future professional work.[2] The emergence of alternative pedagogical approaches, such as the Flipped Classroom model, has sparked interest in reshaping the landscape of undergraduate medical education and has reversed the order of typical lectures.[3] The advent of alternative educational approaches, including the Flipped Classroom model, has generated interest in flipping the order of conventional lectures and changing the structure of undergraduate medical education.[4]

As a significant healthcare issue, respiratory disorders call for a workforce that is both competent and well-trained, with an emphasis on the skills outlined by the National Medical Council of India.[567] This study aims to assess and compare the effectiveness of students’ academic performance to Flipped Classroom and Didactic Lecture through pre- and post-assessment scores.

METHODOLOGY

This educational interventional study was conducted at Bundelkhand Government Medical College, Sagar, with ethical clearance from the Institutional Ethical Committee. A total of 122 final-year students were randomized into two groups, A and B, with 61 students each. Over six months, four topics in respiratory medicine were taught using both didactic lectures and flipped classroom methods. Group A was taught topics 1 and 3 through didactic lectures and topics 2 and 4 using the flipped classroom method, while Group B received the reverse. Pre- and post-tests were administered to evaluate knowledge acquisition, and a post-session survey with 11 pre-validated questions assessed student perceptions of the teaching methods.

RESULTS

A total 122 students participated in the study. Table 1 shows that post-test scores were consistently higher than pre-test scores across all topics, with the Flipped method outperforming the Didactic method in both. The highest post-test mean was 15.39 in the Flipped group for Test 4, and the highest pre-test mean was 8.13 in the Flipped group for Test 2. Table 2 reveals a statistically significant difference (P < 0.05) between pre-test and post-test mean values for both Flipped and Didactic methods across all topic groups.

DISCUSSION

The flipped classroom approach, which integrates active, group-based problem-solving in the classroom with asynchronous video lectures and practice problems assigned as homework, represents a novel fusion of constructivist and behaviorist learning theories.[8] Our study’s findings are consistent with previous research demonstrating the superior effectiveness of the Flipped Classroom in medical education.[9] The statistically significant differences in post-assessment scores highlight the impact of pre-class preparation on knowledge retention and application, reinforcing the value of active learning strategies.[10]

Similar to our findings, other studies have reported positive outcomes and increased student engagement with the flipped method.[11] The qualitative data in our study corroborates these quantitative results, echoing sentiments from other research emphasizing the importance of student engagement in medical education.[812] The desire expressed by the Didactic Lecture group for more interactive elements aligns with broader calls for pedagogical innovation to enhance student engagement and learning outcomes.[10]

Interestingly, while attendance rates did not significantly differ between the Flipped Classroom and Didactic Lecture groups, this finding is consistent with research suggesting that traditional lectures do not necessarily guarantee active engagement or improved academic performance.[11] This underscores the importance of focusing on the quality of student participation rather than mere attendance.

The study’s support for hybrid instructional models, which combine elements of both Flipped Classroom and Didactic Lecture methods, resonates with current discussions on the need for flexible and adaptive teaching approaches that cater to diverse learning styles and preferences. Overall, our study contributes valuable insights into Respiratory Medicine education, supporting the broader literature on the benefits of individualized learning experiences. However, further research across various medical disciplines and institutions is necessary to enhance generalizability and understanding.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, our study supports the effectiveness of the Flipped Classroom approach in medical education, particularly in Respiratory Medicine. The findings highlight the benefits of adopting innovative teaching methods that cater to diverse student needs, ultimately enhancing learning outcomes. Future research addressing current study limitations will further clarify the comparative impact of Flipped Classroom and Didactic Lecture methods in medical education.

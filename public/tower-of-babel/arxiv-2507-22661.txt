In a recent article, the authors of Ref.
Mahmoudi:2024rga
study the gravitational radiation generated by compact binaries in a Brans-Dicke-
\!{f(R)}
(BD
f
) theory based on the following action

\displaystyle A=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\sqrt{-g}\left[\phi f(R)-\frac{\omega_{0}}{\phi}\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial^{\mu}\phi\right]d^{4}x+A_{m}.

(1)

It is assumed that the scalar field
\phi
is massless and has a constant coupling parameter
\omega_{0}\equiv cte
. Also,
A_{m}\equiv A_{m}(g_{\mu\nu},\Psi_{m})
denotes the matter action and
\Psi_{m}
represents the matter fields.
This class of theories is also known as “first generation” scalar-tensor theories.

After varying the above action, one finds the trace of the field equation

f_{R}R-2f(R)+\frac{3\Box(\phi f_{R})}{\phi}+\frac{\omega_{0}}{\phi^{2}}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi=\frac{8\pi T}{\phi},

(2)

as well as an equation for the evolution of the scalar field

\displaystyle\Box\phi-\frac{\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi}{4\phi}=\frac{1}{4\omega_{0}}\Big[8\pi\Big(T

\displaystyle-4\phi\tfrac{dT}{d\phi}\Big)

\displaystyle-\phi Rf_{R}-3\Box(\phi f_{R})\Big],

(3)

where
f_{R}\equiv\partial f/\partial R
.
As usually understood,
f(R)
theories can be recast as massive scalar-tensor models through the identification
f_{R}=\Phi
. Thus, the BD
f
theory is essentially a metric theory of gravity with two more scalar fields: the usual massless BD field
\phi
, and the effectively geometrical massive field
\Phi
.

The parameter
\omega_{0}
controls the influence of the massless scalar field
\phi
on space-time curvature. For the model exposed in (
1
), low values of
\omega_{0}
indicate significant deviations from a single
f(R)
theory. The influence of the geometric scalar field
\Phi
is encoded in its mass
m_{f}
, as it is called in Ref.
Mahmoudi:2024rga
. A small mass value would strengthen the effects of nonlinear Ricci terms in the action, while the limit
m_{f}\rightarrow\infty
recovers the massless BD theory.

The most stringent constraint on
\omega_{0}
comes from Solar System tests. The Cassini mission, by precisely measuring the time delay of radio signals passing near the Sun, established for small mass cases of the BD theories:
\omega_{0}\gtrsim 40000
with
m_{\phi}\lesssim 2.5\times 10^{-29}
GeV
Alsing:2011er
.
4
4

4

The large mass case (with
\omega_{0}
still inversely proportional to
m_{\phi}
) have been recently constrained by Mercury’s perihelion shift
Alves:2023cuo
. For
m_{\phi}\gtrsim 4.6\times 10^{-26}
GeV one has
\omega_{0}\gtrsim 20000
, while for
m_{\phi}\gtrsim 10^{-25}
GeV, there is no bound for
\omega_{0}
.

It is worth noting that this limit is based on generalized BD models, where the scalar field
\phi
has a mass
m_{\phi}
, and it does not consider any influence of other fields.
Thus, the parameters
m_{f}
and
m_{\phi}
do not represent the same physical quantity, and they relate to
\omega_{0}
distinctly.

The main result of Ref.
Mahmoudi:2024rga
is a lower bound
\omega_{0}>6.09723\times 10^{6}
, for a massless BD field with geometrical field mass smaller than
10^{-29}
GeV, being this result “two orders of magnitude stricter than those derived from solar system data”, as stated by the authors. This would be quite a relevant result, but it should be more carefully analyzed, and this is our first important remark in this note. The strongest constraint obtained is highly dependent on the influence of the massive field too, reason why one should not expect also that the parameter
\omega_{0}
in this model has the same role as the traditional BD parameter.

Our second remark concerns the computation of the aforementioned constraint on
\omega_{0}
. As stated in equation
(120)
of Ref.
Mahmoudi:2024rga
, the fractional period decay
\dot{T}
due to the emission of gravitational wave radiation in the BD
f
theory given by action (
1
) reads

\displaystyle\dot{T}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\dot{T}_{\mathrm{GR}}(1-\xi)}{G^{\,2/3}}\,\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{2/3}\,\bigg\{\tfrac{1}{12}-\tfrac{5}{144}\left[\tfrac{2\,\pi\,m\,\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}{T}\right]^{-2/3}\mathcal{S}^{2}\,\times

\displaystyle\,\bigg[\Big(1-\tfrac{T^{2}m_{f}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\Big)^{{}^{3/2}}\!\!\!\!\Theta(\omega-m_{f})-\tfrac{2\omega_{0}+1}{4\omega_{0}^{2}(2\omega_{0}+3)}\,\Big(\tfrac{3(2\omega_{0}+1)}{2}\,+

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 9.24994pt\left(1-\tfrac{T^{2}m_{f}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{{}^{1/2}}\!\!\!\!\!\cos\left(\tfrac{\mathfrak{R}m_{f}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)\Theta\left(\omega-m_{f}\right)\bigg]\bigg\},

(4)

where
\dot{T}_{\mathrm{GR}}
is the corresponding dimensionless GR factor,
\xi=(2\omega_{0}+4)^{-1}
,
\mathfrak{g}\equiv\mathfrak{g}(s_{a},\omega_{0},m_{f})
— being
s_{a}
the sensitivities — and
\Theta
stands for the Heaviside function.
The BD limit in the above expression is quite clear when taking
m_{f}\rightarrow\infty
. Only in this limit
\omega_{0}
can be interpreted as the original BD parameter.

The bounds presented in Fig. 1 of Ref
Mahmoudi:2024rga
can not be obtained from (
4
). By revisiting the analysis proposed by the authors we have obtained new bounds on
\omega_{0}
, as a function of the geometric scalar field mass
m_{f}
, which presents a more consistent scenario. Our result relies on the same data used by the authors, the binary system PSR J1012+5307 (Table 1 in
Mahmoudi:2024rga
). In Fig.
1(a)
we reproduced the authors plot and in Fig.
1(b)
it is shown our result.
We have also identified the origin of this disagreement. The result presented in Ref.
Mahmoudi:2024rga
can be reproduced by replacing the exponent factor of
3/2
in the term
(1-T^{2}m^{2}_{f}/4\pi^{2})^{3/2}
by
-3/2
. The reason behind this is, therefore, a typo in the numerical code that generated Fig.
1(a)
. This also explains the inversion of the graph pattern as shown in Fig.
1(b)
.

We conclude this note with a corrected bound on the coupling parameter
\omega_{0}
as a function of
m_{f}
in BD-
f(R)
theories as shown in panels
1(b)
and
1(c)
. Examples of such bounds are

\displaystyle\omega_{0}\gtrsim 6\times 10^{6}\quad{\rm for}\quad m_{f}\gtrsim 1\times 10^{-29}\,\text{GeV};

(5)

\displaystyle\omega_{0}\gtrsim 40000\quad{\rm for}\quad m_{f}\gtrsim 7.6\times 10^{-29}\,\text{GeV}.

(6)

The result presented in (
5
) refers to the
m_{f}
value regime contrary to the one in Ref.
Mahmoudi:2024rga
, while (
6
) shows the Cassini-equivalent bound. It is also important to mention that for larger mass values, as the theory approaches the traditional BD limit, the
\omega_{0}
bound becomes weaker. However, note that the BD limit cannot be investigated since there is an upper bound limit for
m_{f}<8\times 10^{-29}
GeV imposed by the PSR J1012+5307 binary system’s orbital period.

Introduction

Discrete bounded cohomology of groups of homeomorphisms and diffeomorphisms on manifolds have been studied by different approaches recently. On the one hand, the construction of quasimorphisms on the group
\text{Diff}_{0}(S_{g})

[
BHW22
, Theorem 1.2]
, where
S_{g}
is the closed surface with genus
g>0
, shows that the dimension of
H^{2}_{b}(\text{Diff}_{0}(S_{g}))
is infinite; On the other hand, Monod and Nariman developed a general method to
compute bounded cohomology of groups of transformation groups of manifolds in
[
MN23
]
. Given a group action on a semi-simplicial complex, if several bounded acyclicity conditions are satisfied, then we can use the quotient complex of this action to compute the bounded cohomology of this group (see
Lemma

1.5
).
In this framework, they first computed
H^{\bullet}_{b}(\text{Diff}^{r}_{+}(S^{1}))
and
H^{\bullet}_{b}(\text{PL}_{+}(S^{1}))
, showing they are both generated by a second degree bounded class.
Moreover, they showed
\text{Diff}^{r}_{+}(D^{2},\partial D^{2})
and
\text{Diff}^{r}_{+}(M\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
are boundedly acyclic. They also proved the vanishing of
H^{\bullet}_{b}(\text{Diff}^{r}_{+}(S^{n}))
in low degrees.
Following their study, a more recent paper
[
FFMNK24
, Theorem A,B]
showed
\text{Diff}^{r}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\text{Homeo}_{+}(D^{n},\partial D^{n})
are boundedly acyclic. So the bounded cohomology of
\text{Diff}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
Homeo(\mathbb{R}^{n})
have a very different nature from their ordinary cohomology. As an application, they provided the unboundness of the characteristic class of flat
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-bundles.

Our main result in this paper is the computation of the bounded cohomology of groups of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms or homeomorphisms on high-dimensional spheres, which answers
[
FFMNK24
, Question 6.7]
for higher dimensions:

Theorem 0.1
.

\text{Diff}^{r}_{+}(S^{n})
is boundedly acyclic for
n\geq 4,1\leq r\leq\infty
.

This implies, by
Proposition

1.3
, that
\text{Diff}^{r}(S^{n})
is also bounded acyclic. Hence the comparison map
H^{\bullet}_{b}(\text{BDiff}^{\delta}_{r}(S^{n}))\rightarrow H^{\bullet}(\text{BDiff}^{\delta}_{r}(S^{n}))
is zero, where
"\delta"
means we view it as a discrete subgroup, this has an immediate application that answers
[
MN23
, Question 7.4]
for higher dimensions:

Corollary 0.2
.

Every nontrivial class of
H^{\bullet}(\text{BDiff}^{\delta}_{r}(S^{n}))
in positive degree is unbounded for
n\geq 4,1\leq r\leq\infty
.

Our strategy, inspired by
[
MN23
, Theorem 1.10]
, is to show that for every
k>0
, every subset
F_{k}
with
k
elements of
S^{n}
, the group
\text{Diff}^{r}_{c}(S^{n}-F_{k})
is boundedly acyclic. Note that this group is coamenable to it’s subgroup
\text{Diff}^{r}_{c}(S^{n}-U_{k})
, where
U_{k}
is a closed tubular neighborhood of the
0
-dimensional manifold
F_{k}
,
i
.
e
.,
U_{k}
is a disjoint union of
k
closed balls each containing a point in
F_{k}
. We denote
\text{Diff}^{r}_{c}(S^{n}-U_{k})
by

.

To prove the bounded acyclicity of

,
we construct a semi-simplicial

-complex

, which is the complex associated to a generic relation on its

Our techniques to prove the bounded acyclicity results are rooted in differential topology.
We observe that in high dimensions, the diffeomorphisms of spheres that have a given germ at some arcs and disks can be chosen with sufficient freedom.
Approximations of maps by embeddings , isotopy extensions and transversality theorems are critical to our findings.

Our method is not effective in the case when

, we cannot define the generic relation in the same way.
Though we can define another generic relation, for example, let arcs be transverse as in
[
MN23
]
. At this time, the computation of bounded cohomology of the quotient complex seems to be difficult. When
n=3
, we cannot get the desired isotopy results, and the stabilizers might not be boundedly acyclic. However, our construction seems to be hopeful to determine the bounded cohomology of

answering
[

1.1.
Tools in bounded cohomology: coamenable groups and group actions.

Let
X_{\bullet}
be a semi-simplicial set. Its bounded cohomology
H_{b}^{n}\left(X_{\bullet}\right)
is the cohomology of the complex

0\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(X_{0}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(X_{1}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(X_{2}\right)\rightarrow\cdots

where the coboundary operators are defined as the duals of the face maps of
X_{\bullet}
. The coefficients are always understood to be
\mathbb{R}
unless specified otherwise. We say that
X_{\bullet}
is
boundedly acyclic
if
H_{b}^{n}\left(X_{\bullet}\right)=0
for all
n>0
.

If
G
is a discrete group and
X_{\bullet}
is the nerve of
G
, this defines the bounded cohomology of
G
, denoted by
H_{b}^{n}(G)
. More explicitly, we have
X_{\bullet}=G^{\bullet}
, with face maps defined as

\displaystyle d_{i}:G^{n}\rightarrow G^{n-1}

\displaystyle d_{0}\left(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\right)=\left(g_{2},\ldots,g_{n}\right)

\displaystyle d_{i}\left(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\right)=\left(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1},g_{i}g_{i+1},g_{i+2},\ldots,g_{n}\right),\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}

\displaystyle d_{n}\left(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\right)=\left(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n-1}\right)

We say that the group
G
is boundedly acyclic if
H_{b}^{n}(G)=0
for all
n>0
.

1.2.

Let

\text{Diff}^{r}_{c}(V_{k}-\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k,0\leq j\leq m}im(\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}}))

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
M
be a smooth simply connected manifold of dimension
\geq 3
, such that
\partial M\neq\varnothing
.
Then every two neat
C^{r}
-embeddings of
I
into
M\times I
are ambient
C^{r}
-isotopic. Moreover, if these two embedding have the same germ at the endpoints, then the isotopy can be chosen to have compact support in the interior of
M\times I
.

Proof.

We first prove they are
C^{r}
-isotopic, then use isotopy extension theorem to get the ambient isotopy.

Let
f,g:I\rightarrow M\times I
be two neat embeddings. We may assume
f(t)=(v,t),t\in I
where
v\in M
is a given point. Since
\partial(V\times I)
is connected, by
Remark

3.4
we can modify
g
by composing a
H\in\text{Diff}^{r}(int(M\times I))\cap Homeo_{0}(M\times I)
so that
f,Hg
have the same image at the endpoints. Moreover, similar to
Lemma

1.17
, by composing an appropriate diffeomorphism
H^{\prime}
which is isotopic to the identity such that
supp(H^{\prime})
is contained in a small neighborhood of
Hg(\{0,1\})
, we have
H^{\prime}Hg
has the same germ with
f
at the endpoints. So now we assume that
g
has the same germ with
f
at endpoints. Now we follow the first two steps of the proof of
Lemma

1.16
. We can approximate
\pi_{1}g
by a map
c:I\rightarrow M
such that
c\equiv v
on
[0,\epsilon]\cup[1-\epsilon,1]
and
c_{[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]}
is an embedding. Then
g^{\prime}:t\mapsto(c(t),\pi_{2}g(t)
is also an embedding when
c
is
C^{1}
-close enough to
\pi_{1}g
.
We have
g^{\prime}
is isotopic to
h:t\rightarrow(c(t),t)
.
Let
c_{s}:I\rightarrow M,s\in I
be a smooth homotopy such that
c_{0}=c,c_{1}\equiv v,(c_{s})_{[0,\epsilon]\cup[1-\epsilon,1]}\equiv v,\forall s\in I
.
Then
h
is isotopic to
h^{\prime}:t\mapsto(v,t)
by
h_{s}:t\mapsto(c_{s}(t),t)
.

Now we have a isotopy
H
from
f
to
g
which coincides with
f
at a neighborhood of endpoints. Hence we can extend it to be a ambient isotopy by
Lemma

1.15
, where the support lies in a small neighborhood of
im(H)
.

\square

Remark 3.4
.

Recall a well-known fact that given a smooth manifold
M=M^{m}
,
if two points
a,b
lie in the same component of
\partial M
, then there exist
H\in\text{Diff}^{\infty}_{0}(M)
which sends
a
to
b
. We remark that the similar result also holds, when
M
is a differential manifold with corners, i.e., it’s locally diffeomorphic to open sets of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
. at this time
H\in\text{Homeo}_{0}(M)\cap\text{Diff}^{\infty}(int(M))
. We only need to specify the case when
a,b
are near the corner. To do this, we only need to use the fact there is a homeomorphism between
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
which is differentiable at points outside the corner for
1\leq k\leq n
.

Lemma 3.5
.

G(g_{0}G_{x_{0}},\cdots g_{m}G_{x_{m}})=G(g^{\prime}_{0}G_{x^{\prime}_{0}},\cdots g^{\prime}_{m}G_{x^{\prime}_{m}})
if and only if
(x_{0},\cdots,x_{m})=(x^{\prime}_{0},\cdots,x^{\prime}_{m})
.

Proof.

The ”only if” direction is clear. Let’s prove the ”if” direction. Assume
(x_{0},\cdots,x_{m})=(x^{\prime}_{0},\cdots,x^{\prime}_{m})
. Then
g^{\prime}_{0}g_{0}^{-1}g_{0}G_{x_{0}}=g^{\prime}_{0}G_{x^{\prime}_{0}}
. Without the loss of generality we assume
g_{0}=g^{\prime}_{0}=id
. By definition, for each
1\leq j\leq m
,
g_{j}(\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}im(\gamma_{x^{i}_{j}}))
and
g^{\prime}_{j}(\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}im(\gamma_{x^{i}_{1}}))
are disjoint from
\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}im(\gamma_{x^{i}_{0}})
. Hence
\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq m}(g_{j}(\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}im(\gamma_{x^{i}_{j}}))\cup g^{\prime}_{j}(\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}im(\gamma_{x^{i}_{j}})))
lies in
int(V\times I)-\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}\phi(x^{i}_{0}+\epsilon D^{n-1})\times[0,\frac{1}{3}]
for some small enough
\epsilon>0
, where
\epsilon D^{n-1}
is the standard open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
with radius
\epsilon
centered at 0, and
x+K:=\{x+k:k\in K\}
.

Let
N_{k,m}:=V\times I-\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}\phi(x_{0}^{i}+int(\epsilon D^{n-1}))\times(0.\frac{1}{3})-\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}\phi(int(D_{i}))\times(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3})
.
Note that there is a homeomorphism

\rho:N_{k,m}\rightarrow V\times I

where
\rho
restricts to a diffeomorphism outside the corner
\partial V\times\{0,1\}
of the boundary. Conjugated by
\rho
, each pair
(g_{j}\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}},g_{j}\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}})
becomes a pair of neatly embedded
C^{r}
-arcs in
V\times I
, such that
g_{j}\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}},g_{j}\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}}
have the same germs at the endpoints.

Now
g_{j}\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}}
and
g^{\prime}_{j}\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}}
can be identified as neat embeddings of
I
into
V\times I
. We claim that: There is an
h\in\text{Diff}^{r}_{c}(V\times I)
, such that
hg_{j}\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}}=g^{\prime}_{j}\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}}
for each
1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq m
. This can be done by taking
M=(V-\text{ finitely many points })
in
Lemma

3.3
. At each time we only move an arc while fixing all other arcs, eventually we get the element
h
.
So without the loss of generality we can moreover assume
g_{j}\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}}=g^{\prime}_{j}\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}}
for each
1\leq j\leq m,1\leq i\leq k
.

Pick a contractible neighborhood
U
of
im(\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}})
, Then the composition

I\xrightarrow{(g^{\prime}_{j}g_{j}^{-1})_{*}\Gamma_{x_{j}}}Fr^{+}(U)=U\times GL^{+}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow GL^{+}_{n}(\mathbb{R})

is homotopic rel
\{0,1\}
to
\Gamma_{x_{j}}
as a path in
GL^{+}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
Now following the third step of the proof of
Lemma

1.16
, We can find a
h^{\prime}\in\text{Diff}_{0}(N_{k,m})\cap\text{Diff}^{r}_{c}(int(N_{k,m}))
such that
h^{\prime}g_{j}
has the same germ with
g^{\prime}_{j}
at
\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}im(\gamma_{x_{j}^{i}})
for each
1\leq j\leq m
. Hence
h^{\prime}(g_{0}G_{x_{0}},\cdots g_{m}G_{x_{m}})=(g^{\prime}_{0}G_{x^{\prime}_{0}},\cdots g^{\prime}_{m}G_{x^{\prime}_{m}})
, as required.

\square

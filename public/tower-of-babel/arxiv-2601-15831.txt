Performance Analysis of Digital Beamforming mmWave MIMO with Low-Resolution DACs/ADCs
Faruk Pasic 1 , Mariam Mussbah 1 2 , Stefan Schwarz 1 , Markus Rupp 1 , Fredrik Tufvesson 3 , and Christoph F. Mecklenbräuker 1

Abstract
Future wireless communications will rely on MIMO ( MIMO ) beamforming operating at MMW ( MMW ) frequency bands to deliver high data rates. To support flexible spatial processing and meet the demands of latency-critical applications, it is essential to use fully digital MMW MIMO beamforming, which relies on accurate channel estimation. However, ensuring power efficiency in fully digital MMW MIMO systems requires the use of low-resolution DAC and ADC . The reduced resolution of these quantizers introduces distortion in both transmitted and received signals, ultimately degrading system performance. In this paper, we investigate the channel estimation performance of MMW MIMO systems employing fully digital beamforming with low-resolution quantization, under practical system constraints. We evaluate the system performance in terms of SE ( SE ) and EE ( EE ). Simulation results demonstrate that a moderate quantization resolutions of 4-bit per DAC / ADC offers a favorable trade-off between energy consumption and achievable data rate.

I
Introduction

MMW
communication systems combined with
MIMO
techniques offer the potential to meet growing data rate demands
[
8
,
10
]
.
To fully exploit the spatial multiplexing and beamforming capabilities of
MIMO
, fully digital beamforming architectures are required.
These architectures enable simultaneous channel estimation in multiple spatial directions within a single time interval, significantly reducing the time overhead for link establishment and making them particularly well-suited for latency-critical applications
[
6
]
.

Nevertheless, compared to power-efficient analog or hybrid beamforming architectures, fully digital beamforming in wideband
MMW
systems tends to be significantly more power-intensive.
This is primarily due to the fact that each
RF
(
RF
) chain requires a dedicated pair of
DAC
at the transmitter and
ADC
at the receiver.
To reduce power consumption, fully digital beamformers must utilize low-resolution converters
[
14
]
, which in turn introduce distortion in both the transmitted and received signals.
Therefore, it is essential to evaluate the performance of such systems under practical hardware constraints.

The performance of digital
MMW
beamforming systems employing low-resolution quantizers has been explored to some extent in the literature
[
12
,
2
,
13
]
.
However, these studies are limited in terms of the range of antenna configurations and/or the channel conditions considered.
Moreover, to the best of the author’s knowledge, the effect of low-resolution quantization on advanced
MMW

MIMO
channel estimation techniques remains unexplored.

Contribution:

In this paper, we evaluate the performance of fully digital low-resolution
MMW

MIMO
systems for various channel estimation methods.
Our analysis considers different antenna configurations and varying channel conditions characterized by the Rician
K
-factor.
We investigate the impact of reduced quantization resolution through simulations in terms of
SE
and
EE
.

describes the system model along with the adopted quantization noise model.

Section

, and
Section

IV
concludes the paper.

II
System Model

We study a point-to-point
MMW

MIMO
system operating in the radiative far-field, assuming perfect synchronization between the transmitter and receiver.
The transmitter is equipped with
M_{\rm Tx}
and the receiver with
M_{\rm Rx}
antenna elements.
Each antenna is equipped with its own
RF
chain, enabling fully digital beamforming.
The system employs
TDD
(
TDD
) to ensure channel reciprocity.
Additionally, the system utilizes
OFDM
(
OFDM
) and quadrature amplitude modulation with
N
subcarriers.

At the
n
-th
OFDM
subcarrier, the channel matrix is denoted as
\mathbf{H}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Rx}\times M_{\rm Tx}}
, based on the equivalent complex baseband representation.
The channel is modeled as a frequency-selective Rician fading channel based on the
3GPP
(
3GPP
)
MIMO
channel model, with an adjustable Rician
K
-factor
[
1
]
.

II-A

Link Establishment

Link establishment consists of two phases: training and data transmission.
During the training phase, the wireless channel is estimated and the resulting channel estimate is used in the subsequent data transmission phase.

II-A
1
Training Phase

In the training phase, known pilot symbols are transmitted.
The input-output relationship is given by

\mathbf{y}[n]=\mathbf{H}[n]\left(\boldsymbol{\phi}[n]+\mathbf{d}[n]\right)+\mathbf{w}[n],

(1)

where
\mathbf{y}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Rx}\times 1}
represents the unquantized received symbols,
\boldsymbol{\phi}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Tx}\times 1}
represents the transmitted pilot symbols,
\mathbf{d}[n]\in\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{\rm d}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M_{\rm Tx}})
models impairments from the entire transmit chain as in
[
4
]
, and
\mathbf{w}[n]\in\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{w}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M_{\rm Rx}})
is the
AWGN
(
AWGN
).
The pilot symbols for the
t
-th transmit antenna are defined as

\mathrm{\phi}_{t}[n]=\begin{cases}\mathrm{\phi}[n],\,n\in\{t,t+M_{\rm Tx},\ldots,N-M_{\rm Tx}+t\}\\
0,\quad\,\,\,\text{else}\end{cases}

(2)

where
\mathrm{\phi}[n]
are one-dimensional complex symbols drawn from a
QAM
(
QAM
) alphabet.
The transmitter impairments include
RF
impairments (such as those from the
PA
(
PA
) and
LO
(
LO
) phase noise) with a combined power denoted by
\sigma_{\rm RF}^{2}
, as well as quantization noise from
DAC
with power
\sigma_{\rm q,Tx}^{2}

[
4
]
.
To model the effect of low-resolution quantization, we adopt the
AQNM
(
AQNM
), following the approach used in
[
2
,
3
]
.
Adopting this model, the power of the quantization noise from the
DAC
is given by

\sigma_{\rm q,Tx}^{2}=\frac{1}{M_{\rm Tx}}\Upsilon\left(n_{\rm b}\right)\left(1-\Upsilon\left(n_{\rm b}\right)\right),

(3)

with the inverse coding gain approximated by
\Upsilon\left(n_{\rm b}\right)\approx\frac{\pi\sqrt{3}}{2}n_{\rm b}^{-2}

[
9
]
,
where
n_{\rm b}
denotes the number of quantization bits and
\Upsilon\left(n_{\rm b}\right)=0
corresponds to the ideal case of infinite quantization resolution.
Therefore, the total impairment power is given by
\sigma_{\rm d}^{2}=\sigma_{\rm RF}^{2}+\sigma_{\rm q,Tx}^{2}
.
Since both
\mathbf{d}[n]
and
\mathbf{w}[n]
are mutually independent and Gaussian, they can be combined into a single noise term, we can rewrite
Eq.

1
as

\mathbf{y}[n]=\mathbf{H}[n]\boldsymbol{\phi}[n]+\mathbf{w}^{\prime}[n],

(4)

with
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}[n]\in\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{w}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M_{\rm Rx}}+\sigma_{\rm d}^{2}\mathbf{R}_{\rm H})
and the channel correlation matrix given by
\mathbf{R}_{\rm H}=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{H}[n]\left(\mathbf{H}[n]\right)^{\rm H}\right)
.
At the receiver, the channel estimate
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Rx}\times M_{\rm Tx}}
is obtained via
LS
(
LS
) estimation followed by linear interpolation from the quantized received symbols
\mathbf{y}_{\rm q}[n]
.
The quantized symbols are obtained by adding the quantization noise from the
ADC
, following the
AQNM
from
[
2
,
3
]

\mathbf{y}_{\rm q}[n]=\left(1-\Upsilon\left(n_{\rm b}\right)\right)\mathbf{y}[n]+\mathbf{w}_{\rm q}[n],

(5)

where
\mathbf{w}_{\rm q}[n]\in\mathcal{CN}\left(0,\sigma_{\rm q,Rx}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M_{\rm Rx}}\right)
is the quantization noise from the
ADC
with the power given by

\sigma_{\rm q,Rx}^{2}=\Upsilon\left(n_{\rm b}\right)\left(1-\Upsilon\left(n_{\rm b}\right)\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{y}[n]\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right),

(6)

assuming that
\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{y}[n]\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)=1+\sigma_{w}^{2}
.
To enable optimal beamforming, the estimated channel at the receiver must also be available at the transmitter.
Since the system operates under a
TDD
protocol, we leverage channel reciprocity to ensure the transmitter has access to the same channel estimate.

II-A
2
Data Transmission

The channel estimate obtained in the training phase is processed according to the advanced channel estimation methods described in
[
11
,
5
]
.
The resulting channel estimate
\overline{\mathbf{H}}[n]
is then used for precoding and combining.
To obtain optimal performance in terms of
SE
, we apply
SVD
(
SVD
) to
\overline{\mathbf{H}}[n]
, with its compact form denoted by

\overline{\mathbf{H}}[n]=\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[n]\overline{\mathbf{\Sigma}}[n]\left(\overline{\mathbf{F}}[n]\right)^{\rm H}.

(7)

In
Eq.

7
,
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Rx}\times{\ell_{\rm max}}}
contains left singular vectors,
\overline{\mathbf{F}}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\rm Tx}\times{\ell_{\rm max}}}
contains right singular vectors,
\overline{\mathbf{\Sigma}}[n]
is the diagonal matrix of singular values
\overline{\sigma}_{(1)}[n],\ldots,\overline{\sigma}_{(\ell_{\rm max})}[n]
and
\ell_{\rm max}={\rm min}\left(M_{\rm Rx},M_{\rm Tx}\right)
denotes
the maximum number of spatial streams.
The power loading matrix
\overline{\mathbf{P}}[n]={\rm diag}\left(\overline{p}_{(1)}[n],\ldots,\overline{p}_{(\ell_{\rm max})}[n]\right)
is optimized via the water-filling algorithm to maximize the transmission rate, while ensuring compliance with the total transmit power constraint

\left\lVert\overline{\mathbf{F}}[n]\left(\overline{\mathbf{P}}[n]\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=P_{\rm T}.

(8)

The input-output relationship for data transmission is given by

\mathbf{y}[n]=\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[n]\right)^{\rm H}\mathbf{H}[n]\,\overline{\mathbf{x}}[n]+\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[n]\right)^{\rm H}\mathbf{w}[n],

(9)

where
\overline{\mathbf{x}}[n]
represents the precoded transmit symbols including transmitter impairments
\mathbf{d}[n]

\overline{\mathbf{x}}[n]=\overline{\mathbf{F}}[n]\left(\overline{\mathbf{P}}[n]\right)^{1/2}\mathbf{x}[n]+\mathbf{d}[n],

(10)

\mathbf{x}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell_{\rm max}\times 1}
is the transmit symbol vector,
\mathbf{y}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell_{\rm max}\times 1}
is the unquantized received symbol vector and
\mathbf{w}[n]\in\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{w}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{M_{\rm Rx}})
is the
AWGN
.
As in
Eq.

1
, we rewrite
Eq.

9
as

\mathbf{y}[n]=\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[n]\right)^{\rm H}\mathbf{H}[n]\,\overline{\mathbf{F}}[n]\left(\overline{\mathbf{P}}[n]\right)^{1/2}\mathbf{x}[n]+\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[n]\right)^{\rm H}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}[n].

(11)

Finally, the quantized received symbols are given by
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{\rm q}[n]
, following the approach as in
Eq.

5
.

II-B

Power Consumption Model

To account for the power efficiency of the converters, as well as other key components within the
RF
chains that significantly contribute to the overall power consumption, we model the total power consumption of our
MIMO
transceiver as
P_{\rm tot}=P_{\rm Tx}+P_{\rm Rx}
,
where
P_{\rm Tx}
and
P_{\rm Rx}
denote the transmitter and receiver power consumptions, respectively.
The transmitter chain consists of
DAC
, mixers and
PA
.
The total transmit power consumption is modeled as
[
2
]

P_{\rm Tx}\,[\text{mW}]=M_{\rm Tx}\left(P_{\rm DC,PA}+P_{\rm LO}+2P_{\rm DAC}\right),

(12)

where
P_{\rm DC,PA}
is the DC power consumption of the
PA
,
P_{\rm LO}
is the power consumption of the mixer (driven by the
LO
), and
P_{\rm DAC}
denotes the power consumed by one
DAC
.
To achieve a desired
EIRP
(
EIRP
), the output power per
PA
is given by

P_{\rm out,PA}\,[\text{dBm}]={\rm EIRP}-20\log_{10}\left(M_{\rm Tx}\right)

(13)

and the corresponding DC power drawn by the
PA
is modeled as
[
2
]

P_{\rm DC,PA}\,[\text{mW}]=\frac{1}{\eta_{\rm PA}}\left(10^{\frac{P_{\rm out,PA}}{10}}-10^{\frac{P_{\rm in,PA}}{10}}\right),

(14)

where
\eta_{\rm PA}
is the power added efficiency of the
PA
, and
P_{\rm in,PA}
is the input power to the
PA
(in dBm).
The input power to the
PA
is given by

P_{\rm in,PA}\,[\text{dBm}]=P_{\rm in,BB}-10\log_{10}\left(M_{\rm Tx}\right)-{IL}_{\rm mix},

(15)

where
P_{\rm in,BB}
is the power delivered by the baseband circuitry and
{IL}_{\rm mix}
is
IL
(
IL
) of the mixer.
The power consumption of the
DAC
is modeled as a function of its resolution
n_{\rm b}
(in bits) and sampling rate
f_{\rm s}
, and is given by
[
2
]

P_{\rm DAC}\,[\text{mW}]=2^{n_{\rm b}}f_{\rm s}\,{\rm FoM}_{\rm DAC},

(16)

where
{\rm FoM}_{\rm DAC}
denotes the figure of merit of the
DAC
, typically expressed in femtojoules per conversion step.

The receiver chain consists of
LNA
, mixers, and
ADC
.
The total receiver consumption is given by
[
2
]

P_{\rm Rx}\,[\text{mW}]=M_{\rm Rx}\left(P_{\rm DC,LNA}+P_{\rm LO}+2P_{\rm ADC}\right)

(17)

where
P_{\rm DC,LNA}
is the DC power consumption of the
LNA
and
P_{\rm ADC}
denotes the power consumed by one
ADC
.
The power consumption of the
ADC
is modeled as a function of its resolution
n_{\rm b}
(in bits) and sampling rate
f_{\rm s}
, and is given by
[
2
]

P_{\rm ADC}\,[\text{mW}]=2^{n_{\rm b}}f_{\rm s}\,{\rm FoM}_{\rm ADC},

(18)

where
{\rm FoM}_{\rm ADC}
represents the figure of merit of the
ADC
.

Simulation-based Comparison

To evaluate the system performance, we simulate the
SE
and

in a frequency-selective channel.
The transmitter and receiver antenna elements are arranged in a
ULA
(
ULA
) configuration with half-wavelength spacing.
The channel model follows the
3GPP
urban macro for the
LOS
(
LOS
) scenario with an adjustable
K
-factor (as outlined in
[
1
]
) and the used simulation parameters are summarized in
Table

I
.
As advanced channel estimation methods, we employ the
OOBA-MRC
method proposed in
[
11
]
and the
OMP
(
OMP
) method detailed in
[
5
]
.
In addition to the advanced methods, we also include the performance of conventional baseline method, where the channel estimates is set as
\overline{\mathbf{H}}[n]=\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}[n]
.
The achievable
SE
expressed in bits
/
s
/
Hz is defined as

\mathrm{SE}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{\mu=1}^{\ell_{\rm max}}\log_{2}\left(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{\rm\mu}[n]\right),

(19)

where the effective

(
SINR
) for the spatial stream
{\rm\mu}
is given by
[
11
]

\mathrm{SINR}_{\rm\mu}[n]=\frac{\left|\overline{\mathrm{G}}_{\rm\mu,\mu}[n]\right|^{2}}{\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=1\\
\nu\neq\mu\end{subarray}}^{\ell_{\rm max}}\left|\overline{\mathrm{G}}_{\rm\mu,\nu}[n]\right|^{2}+\sigma_{\rm tot}^{2}[n]\left\lVert\overline{\mathrm{Q}}_{\rm:,\mu}[n]\right\rVert^{2}},

\sigma_{\rm tot}^{2}[n]=\sigma_{\rm\mu}^{2}[n]+\sigma_{w}^{2}+\sigma_{\rm d}^{2}+\sigma_{\rm q,Rx}^{2}
.
In

, the term
\overline{\mathrm{G}}_{\rm\mu,\nu}[n]
, where

, denotes the entries of the channel gain matrix
\overline{\mathbf{G}}[n]\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell_{\rm max}\times\ell_{\rm max}}

, defined as

\overline{\mathbf{G}}[n]=\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}[n]\right)^{\rm H}\mathbf{H}[n]\overline{\mathbf{F}}[n]\left(\overline{\mathbf{P}}[n]\right)^{1/2}.

(21)

Furthermore,
\sigma_{\rm\mu}^{2}[n]
represents the diagonal entries (variances) of the estimation error covariance matrix

\overline{\mathbf{C}}_{\varepsilon}[n]=\frac{1}{\ell_{\rm max}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}[n]\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}[n]\right)^{\rm H}

The error
\overline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}[n]
represents the difference between the channel gain matrix computed using the estimated precoder/combiner

\overline{\mathbf{G}}[n]
, and that obtained using the ideal precoder/combiner
\mathbf{G}[n]
.
The achievable

, expressed in bits
/
s
/
Hz
/

\mathrm{EE}=\mathrm{SE}/P_{\rm tot}

[
13
]
.

IV
Conclusion

Reducing the quantizer resolution generally leads to a degradation in
SE
.
However, using 4-bit quantizers provides the highest
EE
, thereby offering a favorable trade-off between power consumption and achievable data rate.
At low
K
-factors, all channel estimation methods experience a decline in
SE
as the quantizer resolution is reduced.
At higher
K
-factors, although conventional and
OMP
methods experience performance degradation, the
OOBA-MRC
method remains robust and largely unaffected in terms of
SE
.

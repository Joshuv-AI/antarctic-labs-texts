On connected subgraph arrangements
Lorenzo Giordani, Tilman Möller, Paul Mücksch, Gerhard Röhrle

Abstract
. In [ CK24 ] , Cuntz and Kühne introduced a particular class of hyperplane arrangements stemming from a given graph, so called connected subgraph arrangements . In this note we strengthen some of the result from [ CK24 ] and prove new ones for members of this class. For instance, we show that aspherical members withing this class stem from a rather restricted set of graphs. Specifically, if 𝒜 G {\mathscr{A}}_{G} is an aspherical connected subgraph arrangement, then 𝒜 G {\mathscr{A}}_{G} is free with the unique possible exception when the underlying graph G G is the complete graph on 4 4 nodes.

1.
Introduction and Main Results

In
[
CK24
]
,
Cuntz and Kühne
introduced a special class of hyperplane arrangements stemming from a given graph, so called

connected subgraph arrangements
.
They include such prominent classes as the braid arrangements and resonance arrangements among many others.
In this note
we strengthen some of the result proved in
[
CK24
]

and prove some new theorems for members of this class.

We first recall the definition from
[
CK24
]
.
Fix
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a field and let
V={\mathbb{K}}^{n}
. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
be the dual basis in
V^{*}
of the standard
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis of
V
.
Let
G=(N,E)
be an undirected graph with vertex set
N=[n]
and edge set
E
. For
I\subseteq N
, let
G[I]
be the induced subgraph of
G
on the set of vertices
I
.
For
I\subseteq N
, define the hyperplane

H_{I}:=\ker\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}.

Definition 1.1
.

With the notation as above, the
connected subgraph arrangement

{\mathscr{A}}_{G}({\mathbb{K}})
in
V
is defined as

{\mathscr{A}}_{G}({\mathbb{K}}):=\{H_{I}\mid\varnothing\neq I\subseteq N\text{ if $G[I]$ is connected}\}.

In case
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{Q}}
, we write
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}:={\mathscr{A}}_{G}({\mathbb{Q}})
.

In
[
CK24
]
,
Cuntz and Kühne classified all connected subgraph arrangements over
{\mathbb{Q}}
which are free, factored, simplicial or supersolvable.
The purpose of this note is firstly to strengthen several of these results and secondly to prove new ones.

All properties of arrangements we study in this note are compatible with the product construction for arrangements. For a simple graph
G
, the connected subgraph arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is the product of the connected subgraph arrangements stemming from the connected components of
G
. Thus there is no harm in assuming that
G
is connected throughout.

A property for arrangements is said to be
combinatorial
if it only depends on the intersection lattice of the underlying arrangement.
Our first main theorem shows that the class of connected subgraph arrangements is
very special in the sense that essentially every property
we may formulate for members of this class is
combinatorial. This is formally captured by the notion of projective uniqueness, see Definition
3.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is projectively unique.

Theorem
1.2
implies that
for the class of all rational arrangements whose underlying matroid admits a realization over
{\mathbb{Q}}
as a connected subgraph arrangement
freeness is combinatorial. In particular, Terao’s conjecture over
{\mathbb{Q}}
is valid within this class, cf.
[
Z90
, Prop. 2.3]
. Likewise, asphericity is combinatorial within this class. Whether both these properties are combinatorial in general are longstanding and wide open problems, see
[
OT92
, Conj. 4.138]
and
[
FR00
, Prob. 3.8]
.

Theorem
1.2
is proved in Section
3
. The results there show that the theorem holds for any fixed field
{\mathbb{K}}
.

In
[
CK24
, Thm. 1.6]
Cuntz and Kühne classified all free members among the
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
:

Theorem 1.3
.

The connected subgraph arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is free if and only if
G
is a path-graph, a cycle-graph, an almost-path-graph, or a path-with-triangle-graph (Definition
2.1
).

We strengthen Theorem
1.3
as follows, where we look at the stronger freeness property of
accuracy
, see Definition
2.10
.

Theorem 1.4
.

The arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is free if and only if it is accurate.

Thanks to Theorem
1.2
, the concept of accuracy is combinatorial with the class of connected subgraph arrangements. It is not clear whether this notion is combinatorial in general, see
[
MRT24
, Rem. 1.3(ii)]
.
Theorem
1.4

is proved in §
4
.

While it follows from Theorem
1.2
that freeness within the class of connected subgraph arrangements is a combinatorial property, it is viable to ask whether each of the families among the free connected subgraph arrangements belongs to a general class satisfying
a stronger combinatorial freeness property. For instance, if
G
is a cycle graph or a path graph, then
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is inductively free
[
CK24
, Prop. 3.1, Cor. 8.11]
.
For the notion of

inductive
freeness, see Definition

2.4
.
Moreover, thanks to
[
CK24
, Thm. 4.1]
,
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is MAT-free for

.
For the notion of
MAT-freeness
freeness, see Definition

2.8
.
MAT-freeness is a combinatorial property for arrangements,
[
CM20
, Lem. 18]
.
Cuntz and Mücksch raised the question whether

[

, Ques. 3]

This appears to be the case

suggests the following strengthening of

.

The

It is not known whether the family

, where

Note, an analogue of Theorem

for free multiplicities on

is studied in
[

]
.

In
[

, Thm. 1.8(1)]
Cuntz and Kühne classified all factored members among the connected subgraph arrangements.

Theorem 1.6
.

The connected subgraph arrangement

is factored if and only if either

or a path-with-triangle graph

We strengthen Theorem

as follows.

Theorem 1.7
.

The

{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is factored if and only if it is inductively factored.

For the notions of factoredness and inductive factoredness, see Definitions

and

Theorem

.

Recall the graphs

(Definition

If

.

If

A hyperplane arrangement is called formal provided all linear dependencies among the defining forms of the hyperplanes are generated by ones corresponding to intersections of codimension two.
The significance of this notion stems from the fact that

[

, Thm. 4.2]

In addition, free arrangements are known to be formal,

[

[

In our next result we show that any connected subgraph arrangement

Theorem 1.9

For a fixed field

The proof of this theorem which is presented in §

[
MMR24

Note that Theorem

In our final section §

For general information about arrangements

2.1.
Hyperplane arrangements

Let
V={\mathbb{C}}^{n}

be an
n
-dimensional complex vector space.
A
hyperplane arrangement
is a pair

({\mathscr{A}},V)
, where
{\mathscr{A}}
is a finite collection of hyperplanes in
V
.
Usually, we simply write
{\mathscr{A}}
in place of
({\mathscr{A}},V)
.

The
lattice

L({\mathscr{A}})
of
{\mathscr{A}}
is the set of subspaces of
V
of
the form
H_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H_{i}
where
\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{i}\}
is a subset
of
{\mathscr{A}}
.
For
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
, we have two associated arrangements,
firstly

{\mathscr{A}}_{X}:=\{H\in{\mathscr{A}}\mid X\subseteq H\}\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}
,
the
localization of
{\mathscr{A}}
at
X
,
and secondly,
the
restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}
to
X
,
({\mathscr{A}}^{X},X)
, where

{\mathscr{A}}^{X}:=\{X\cap H\mid H\in{\mathscr{A}}\setminus{\mathscr{A}}_{X}\}
.
The lattice
L({\mathscr{A}})
is a partially ordered set by reverse inclusion:

X\leq Y
provided
Y\subseteq X
for
X,Y\in L({\mathscr{A}})
.

Throughout, we only consider arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}

such that
0\in H
for each
H
in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
These are called
central
.
In that case the
center

T({\mathscr{A}}):=\cap_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}H
of
{\mathscr{A}}
is the unique
maximal element in
L({\mathscr{A}})
with respect
to the partial order.
A
rank
function on
L({\mathscr{A}})

is given by
r(X):=\operatorname{codim}_{V}(X)
.
The
rank
of
{\mathscr{A}}

is defined as
r({\mathscr{A}}):=r(T({\mathscr{A}}))
.

Following
[
CK24
, §2.3]
,
we call a rational arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
a
0/1
-arrangement
if all hyperplanes in
{\mathscr{A}}
are of the form
H_{I}=\ker\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}
for some
I\subseteq[n]
. Of course, by Definition
1.1
, connected subgraph arrangements are
0/1
-arrangements.

central role in the investigation of free simple connected subgraph arrangements. They are equally crucial for our study.

2.2.
Free arrangements

Free arrangements play a crucial role in the theory of arrangements;
see
[
OT92
, §4]
for the definition and
basic properties. If
{\mathscr{A}}
is free, then
we can associate with
{\mathscr{A}}
the multiset of its
exponents
,
denoted
\exp{\mathscr{A}}
. We sometimes write

(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})_{\leq}
for the ordered set of exponents, i.e. to indicate that

e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\ldots\leq e_{\ell}
.

Terao’s
Factorization Theorem

[
Ter81
]
shows
that the Poincaré polynomial
of a free arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}

factors into linear terms
given by the exponents of
{\mathscr{A}}

(cf.
[
OT92
, Thm. 4.137]
):

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that

{\mathscr{A}}
is free with
\exp{\mathscr{A}}=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell}\}
.
Then

\pi({\mathscr{A}},t)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(1+b_{i}t).

Terao’s celebrated
Addition-Deletion Theorem

[
Ter80
]
plays a
fundamental role in the study of free arrangements,

[
OT92
, Thm. 4.51]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
{\mathscr{A}}\neq\Phi_{\ell}
.
Let
({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})
be a triple of arrangements. Then any
two of the following statements imply the third:

(i)

{\mathscr{A}}
is free with
\exp{\mathscr{A}}=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell-1},b_{\ell}\}
;

(ii)

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
is free with
\exp{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell-1},b_{\ell}-1\}
;

(iii)

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
is free with
\exp{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell-1}\}
.

Theorem
2.3
motivates the notion of an

inductively free
arrangement,

[
OT92
, Def. 4.53]
.

Definition 2.4
.

The class
{\mathcal{IF}}
of
inductively free
arrangements
is the smallest class of arrangements subject to

(i)

\Phi_{\ell}\in{\mathcal{IF}}
for each
\ell\geq 0
;

(ii)

if there exists a hyperplane
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
such that both

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
belong to
{\mathcal{IF}}
, and
\exp{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\exp{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
,
then
{\mathscr{A}}
also belongs to
{\mathcal{IF}}
.

Clearly, inductively free arrangements are free. However, the latter class properly contains the former, cf.
[
OT92
, Ex. 4.59]
.

Remark 2.5
.

It is possible to describe an inductively free arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
by means of
a so called

induction table
, cf.
[
OT92
, §4.3, p. 119]
.
In this process we start with an inductively free arrangement
and add hyperplanes successively ensuring that
part (ii) of Definition
2.4
is satisfied.
This process is referred to as
induction of hyperplanes
.
This procedure amounts to
choosing a total order on
{\mathscr{A}}
, say

{\mathscr{A}}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}\}
,
so that each of the subarrangements

{\mathscr{A}}_{i}:=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{i}\}

and each of the restrictions
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H_{i}}
is inductively free
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.
In the associated induction table we record in the
i
-th row the information
of the
i
-th step of this process, by
listing
\exp{\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{\prime}=\exp{\mathscr{A}}_{i-1}
,

H_{i}
,
as well as
\exp{\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{\prime\prime}=\exp{\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H_{i}}
,
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.

2.3.
MAT-Free arrangements

Next we recall the so-called Multiple Addition Theorem (MAT) from
[
ABC+16
]
.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
ABC+16
, Thm. 3.1]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}=({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},V)
be a free arrangement with

\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})_{\leq}

and let
1\leq p\leq\ell
be the multiplicity of the highest exponent, i.e.

e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\ldots\leq e_{\ell-p}<e_{\ell-p+1}=\cdots=e_{\ell}=:e.

Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}
be hyperplanes in
V
with

H_{i}\not\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
for
i=1,\ldots,q
. Define

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}_{j}:=({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\cup\{H_{j}\})^{H_{j}}=\{H\cap H_{j}\mid H\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\},\quad\text{ for }j=1,\ldots,q.

Assume that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

X:=H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{q}
is
q
-codimensional.

(2)

X\not\subseteq\bigcup_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}}H
.

(3)

|{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}|-|{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}_{j}|=e
for
1\leq j\leq q
.

Then
q\leq p
and
{\mathscr{A}}:={\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\cup\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}\}
is free with

\exp({\mathscr{A}})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-q},e+1,\ldots,e+1)_{\leq}.

We often consider the addition of several hyperplanes using
Theorem
2.6
. This motivates the next terminology.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
and
\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}\}
be as in Theorem
2.6
such that
conditions (1)–(3) are satisfied. Then the addition
of
\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}\}
to
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
resulting in
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\cup\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}\}

is called an
MAT-step
.

An iterative application of Theorem
2.6
motivates the following natural concept.

Definition 2.8

(
[
CM20
, Def. 3.2, Lem. 3.8]
)
.

An arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is called
MAT-free
if there exists an ordered partition

\pi=(\pi_{1}|\cdots|\pi_{n})

of
{\mathscr{A}}
such that the following hold.
Set
{\mathscr{A}}_{0}:=\varnothing_{\ell}
and

{\mathscr{A}}_{k}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\pi_{i}\quad\text{ for }1\leq k\leq n.

Then for every
0\leq k\leq n-1
suppose that

(1)

\operatorname{rk}(\pi_{k+1})=|\pi_{k+1}|
,

(2)

\cap_{H\in\pi_{k+1}}H\nsubseteq\bigcup_{H^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{A}}_{k}}H^{\prime}
,

(3)

|{\mathscr{A}}_{k}|-|({\mathscr{A}}_{k}\cup\{H\})^{H}|=k
for each
H\in\pi_{k+1}
,

i.e.
{\mathscr{A}}_{k+1}={\mathscr{A}}_{k}\cup\pi_{k+1}
is an MAT-step.

An ordered partition
\pi
with these properties is called an
MAT-partition
for
{\mathscr{A}}
.

Remark 2.9
.

Suppose that
{\mathscr{A}}
is MAT-free with MAT-partition

\pi=(\pi_{1}|\cdots|\pi_{n})
. Then we have:

(a)

for each
1\leq k\leq n
,
{\mathscr{A}}_{k}
is MAT-free with MAT-partition
(\pi_{1}|\cdots|\pi_{k})
,

(b)

{\mathscr{A}}
is free and the exponents

\exp({\mathscr{A}})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})_{\leq}
of
{\mathscr{A}}
are given by the block sizes
of the dual partition of
\pi
:

e_{i}:=|\{k\mid|\pi_{k}|\geq\ell-i+1\}|,

(c)

|\pi_{1}|>|\pi_{2}|\geq\cdots\geq|\pi_{n}|
.

Proof.

Statement (a) is clear by Definition
2.8
.
Statements (b) and (c) follow readily from Theorem
2.6
and a simple induction.
∎

Definition 2.10
.

2.5.
Nice arrangements

The notion of a
nice
or
factored

arrangement goes back to Terao
[
Ter92
]
.
It generalizes the concept of a supersolvable arrangement.
We recall the relevant notions and results from
[
Ter92
]

(cf.
[
OT92
, §2.3]
).

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\pi=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{s})
be a partition of
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Then
\pi
is called
independent
, provided
for any choice
H_{i}\in\pi_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq s
,
the resulting
s
hyperplanes are linearly independent, i.e.
r(H_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H_{s})=s
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\pi=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{s})
be a partition of
{\mathscr{A}}

and let
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
.
The
induced partition

\pi_{X}
of
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}
is given by the non-empty
blocks of the form
\pi_{i}\cap{\mathscr{A}}_{X}
.

Definition 2.13
.

The partition

\pi
of
{\mathscr{A}}
is

nice
for
{\mathscr{A}}
or a
factorization
of
{\mathscr{A}}
provided

(i)

\pi
is independent, and

(ii)

for each
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})\setminus\{V\}
, the induced partition
\pi_{X}
admits a block
which is a singleton.

If
{\mathscr{A}}
admits a factorization, then we also say that
{\mathscr{A}}
is
factored
or
nice
.

Remark 2.14
.

The class of nice arrangements is closed under taking localizations.
For, if
{\mathscr{A}}
is non-empty and

\pi
is a nice partition of
{\mathscr{A}}
, then the non-empty parts of the
induced partition
\pi_{X}
form a nice partition of
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}

for each

2.6.
Inductively factored arrangements

Following Jambu and Paris

[
JP95
]
and
[
HR16
]
,
we introduce further notation.
Suppose that
{\mathscr{A}}
is non-empty and
let
\pi=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{s})
be a partition of
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Let
H_{0}\in\pi_{1}
and let

({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})
be the triple associated with
H_{0}
.
We have the
induced partition

\pi^{\prime}
of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}

consisting of the non-empty parts
\pi_{i}^{\prime}:=\pi_{i}\cap{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
.
Further, we have the
restriction map

{\varrho}={\varrho}_{\pi,H_{0}}:{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\pi_{1}\to{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
given by

H\mapsto H\cap H_{0}
, depending on
\pi
and
H_{0}
.
Let
\pi_{i}^{\prime\prime}:={\varrho}(\pi_{i})
for
i=2,\ldots,s
.
Clearly, imposing that

\pi^{\prime\prime}=(\pi^{\prime\prime}_{2},\ldots,\pi^{\prime\prime}_{s})
is
again a partition of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
entails that

{\varrho}
is onto.

Here is the analogue for nice arrangements of Terao’s
Addition-Deletion Theorem (cf. Theorem
2.3
) for free arrangements from

[
HR16
]
.

Theorem 2.20
.

Suppose
\pi=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{s})
is a partition of
{\mathscr{A}}\neq\Phi_{\ell}
.
Let
({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})
be the triple associated with
H_{0}\in\pi_{1}
.
Then any two of the following statements imply the third:

(i)

\pi
is nice for
{\mathscr{A}}
;

(ii)

\pi^{\prime}
is nice for
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
;

(iii)

{\varrho}:{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\pi_{1}\to{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}

is bijective and
\pi^{\prime\prime}
is nice for
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
.

Definition 2.21
.

Suppose
{\mathscr{A}}\neq\Phi_{\ell}
. Let
\pi=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{s})
be a partition of
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Let
H_{0}\in\pi_{1}
and
let
({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})
be the triple associated with
H_{0}
.
We say that
H_{0}
is
distinguished (with respect to
\pi
)

provided
\pi
induces a factorization
\pi^{\prime}
of

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
, i.e. the non-empty
subsets
\pi_{i}\cap{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
form a nice partition
of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
. Note that since
H_{0}\in\pi_{1}
, we have

\pi_{i}\cap{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}=\pi_{i}\not=\varnothing

for
i=2,\ldots,s
.

The Addition-Deletion Theorem
2.20

for nice arrangements motivates
the following stronger notion of factorization,
cf.
[
JP95
]
.

Definition 2.22

(
[
HR16
, Def. 3.8]
)
.

The class
{\mathcal{IF\!AC}}
of
inductively factored
arrangements
is the smallest class of pairs
({\mathscr{A}},\pi)
of
arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}
along with a partition
\pi

subject to

(i)

(\Phi_{\ell},(\varnothing))\in{\mathcal{IF\!AC}}
for each
\ell\geq 0
;

(ii)

if there exists a partition
\pi
of
{\mathscr{A}}

and a hyperplane
H_{0}\in\pi_{1}
such that
for the triple
({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})
associated with
H_{0}

the restriction map
{\varrho}={\varrho}_{\pi,H_{0}}:{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\pi_{1}\to{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}

is bijective and for the induced partitions
\pi^{\prime}
of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
and

\pi^{\prime\prime}
of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}

both
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
and
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\pi^{\prime\prime})
belong to
{\mathcal{IF\!AC}}
,
then
({\mathscr{A}},\pi)
also belongs to
{\mathcal{IF\!AC}}
.

If
({\mathscr{A}},\pi)
is in
{\mathcal{IF\!AC}}
, then we say that

{\mathscr{A}}
is
inductively factored with respect to
\pi
, or else
that
\pi
is an
inductive factorization
of
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Usually, we say
{\mathscr{A}}
is
inductively factored
without
reference to a specific inductive factorization of
{\mathscr{A}}
.

Remark 2.23
.

Definition
2.22

of inductively factored arrangements
differs from the one given by
Jambu and Paris
[
JP95
]

in that, apart from the mere technicalities of
incorporating empty arrangements and for defining

{\mathcal{IF\!AC}}
for pairs of arrangements and partitions
rather than for partitions of arrangements,
in part (ii) we do not assume from the outset
that
\pi
is a factorization of
{\mathscr{A}}
.
This is possible by virtue of
the Addition-Deletion Theorem
2.20
.
However, this comes at the cost of
the bijectivity requirement for the associated restriction map
{\varrho}
.

Remark 2.24
.

(i).
If
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively factored, then
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free,
by
[
HR16
, Prop. 3.14]
.
The latter can be described by an induction table, see Remark
2.5
.
The proof of
[
HR16
, Prop. 3.14]
shows
that if
\pi
is an inductive factorization of
{\mathscr{A}}

and
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
is distinguished with respect to
\pi
, then
the triple
({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})
with respect to
H_{0}

is a triple of inductively free arrangements.
Thus an induction table of
{\mathscr{A}}
can be constructed,
compatible with suitable inductive factorizations of
the subarrangments
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}
.

(ii).
Now suppose
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free and let

{\mathscr{A}}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}\}
be a
choice of a total order on
{\mathscr{A}}
,
so that each of the subarrangements

{\mathscr{A}}_{0}:=\Phi_{\ell}
,
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}:=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{i}\}

and each of the restrictions
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{H_{i}}
is inductively free
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Then, starting with the empty partition for
\Phi_{\ell}
, we can attempt to
build inductive factorizations
\pi_{i}
of
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}

consecutively, resulting in an inductive factorization

\pi=\pi_{n}
of
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}_{n}
.
This is achieved by invoking Theorem
2.20

repeatedly in order to derive that each
\pi_{i}
is an inductive factorization of
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}
.
For this it suffices to check
the conditions in part (iii) of Theorem
2.20
, i.e., that

\exp{\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{\prime\prime}
is given by the sizes of the parts of
\pi_{i}
not containing
H_{i}

and that the induced partition
\pi_{i}^{\prime\prime}
of
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{\prime\prime}
is a factorization.
The fact that
H_{i}
is distinguished
with respect to
\pi_{i}
is part of the inductive hypothesis, as

\pi_{i}^{\prime}=\pi_{i-1}
is an inductive factorization of
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}^{\prime}={\mathscr{A}}_{i-1}
.

We then add the inductive factorizations
\pi_{i}

of
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}
as additional data into an induction table for
{\mathscr{A}}

(or else record to which part of
\pi_{i-1}
the new hyperplane
H_{i}
is appended to).
The data in such an extended induction table
together with the “Addition” part of Theorem
2.20

then proves that
{\mathscr{A}}
is
inductively factored.
We refer to this technique as
induction of factorizations
and the
corresponding table as an
induction table of factorizations
for
{\mathscr{A}}
.

We illustrate this induction of factorizations procedure in Tables
1
and
2
.
As for the usual induction process for free arrangements,
induction for factorizations is sensitive to the chosen order on
{\mathscr{A}}
.

2.7.
K(\pi,1)
-arrangements

A complex
\ell
-arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is called
aspherical
, or a

K(\pi,1)
-arrangement
(or that
{\mathscr{A}}
is
K(\pi,1)
for short), provided
the complement
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathscr{A}})
of the union of the hyperplanes in

{\mathscr{A}}
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
is aspherical, i.e. is a

K(\pi,1)
-space. That is, the universal covering space of
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathscr{A}})

is contractible and the fundamental group

\pi_{1}({\mathcal{M}}({\mathscr{A}}))
of
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathscr{A}})
is isomorphic to the group
\pi
.
This is an important
topological property, for
the cohomology ring
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
of a
K(\pi,1)
-space
X

coincides
with the group cohomology
H^{*}(\pi,{\mathbb{Z}})
of
\pi
.
The crucial point here is that the intersections of codimension
2

determine the fundamental group
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{M}}({\mathscr{A}}))
of
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathscr{A}})
.

By Deligne’s seminal result
[
Del72
]
, complexified simplicial arrangements are
K(\pi,1)
.
Likewise for complex supersolvable arrangements,
cf.
[
FR85
]
and
[
Ter86
]

(cf.
[
OT92
, Prop. 5.12, Thm. 5.113]
).

.
Typical examples of local properties of arrangements are freeness
[

.

{\mathscr{A}}_{G}

Let

3.
Projective Uniquenes: Proof of Theorem
1.2

Definition 3.1
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
be two arrangements in a
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector space
V
.

(i)

{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
are
linearly isomorphic
if there is a
\varphi\in\operatorname{GL}(V)

such that
{\mathscr{B}}=\{\varphi(H)\mid H\in{\mathscr{A}}\}
; denoted by
{\mathscr{A}}\cong{\mathscr{B}}
.

(ii)

{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
are
L
-equivalent
if
L({\mathscr{A}})
and
L({\mathscr{B}})
are isomorphic as posets;
denoted by
{\mathscr{A}}\cong_{L}{\mathscr{B}}
.

(iii)

{\mathscr{A}}
is
projectively unique
if for any arrangement
{\mathscr{C}}
in
V
we have:

{\mathscr{C}}\cong_{L}{\mathscr{A}}
implies
{\mathscr{C}}\cong{\mathscr{A}}
.

Remark 3.2
.

The term “projectively unique” originates in the dual point of view.
Every hyperplane arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
in
V
yields a dual point configuration in the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
,
denoted by
{\mathscr{A}}^{*}
, see
[
Z90
, §2]
.
Two arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}
,
{\mathscr{B}}
in
V
are linearly isomorphic if and only if there is a projective transformation
\varphi^{*}

which maps
{\mathscr{A}}^{*}
to
{\mathscr{B}}^{*}
.

Example 3.3
.

(i). Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a generic arrangement in
V={\mathbb{K}}^{\ell}
with
|{\mathscr{A}}|=\ell+1
.
Then
{\mathscr{A}}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{\ell},H^{\prime}\}
where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}\in V^{*}

with
H_{i}=\ker(\alpha_{i})
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}
form a basis of
V^{*}
.
If
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}\alpha_{i}\in V^{*}
with
\ker(\gamma)=H^{\prime}
, then by the genericity of
{\mathscr{A}}

we have
a_{i}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell
. Hence, after rescaling the
\alpha_{i}
,
we see that
{\mathscr{A}}
is linearly isomorphic to
\{\ker(x_{1}),\ldots,\ker(x_{\ell}),\ker(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}x_{i})\}
,
where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
is some basis of
V^{*}
.
Thus,
{\mathscr{A}}
is projectively unique.

(ii). Let
V={\mathbb{Q}}^{2}
and
x,y
be a basis of
V^{*}
.
Let
{\mathscr{A}}=\{\ker(x),\ker(y),\ker(x+y),\ker(x+2y)\}
and
{\mathscr{B}}=\{\ker(x),\ker(y),\ker(x+y),\ker(x+3y)\}
be arrangements in
V
.
Then clearly
{\mathscr{A}}\cong_{L}{\mathscr{B}}
but
{\mathscr{A}}
is not linearly isomorphic to
{\mathscr{B}}
.
Thus,
{\mathscr{A}}
(resp.
{\mathscr{B}}
) is not projectively unique.

Fix a field
{\mathbb{K}}
.
Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a
{\mathbb{K}}
-arrangement.
We specify what we mean by a subarrangement of
{\mathscr{A}}
being generated by a subset (or subarrangement) of
{\mathscr{A}}

(cf.
[
C22
, Def. 3.4]
for an equivalent formulation for arrangements of rank
3
).

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\varnothing\neq S\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}
. Set
\operatorname{Gen}_{0}({\mathscr{A}},S):=S
and inductively

\operatorname{Gen}_{i+1}({\mathscr{A}},S):=\left\{H\in{\mathscr{A}}\mid\exists\ J\subseteq L(\operatorname{Gen}_{i}({\mathscr{A}},S)):H=\sum_{X\in J}X\right\}

for
i\geq 0
.
Then we say that

\langle S\rangle_{\mathscr{A}}:=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}\operatorname{Gen}_{i}({\mathscr{A}},S)\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}

is the subarrangement of
{\mathscr{A}}

generated by
S
. If
\langle S\rangle_{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}
, then we say that
S
generates
{\mathscr{A}}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
be two arrangements in
V
.
Suppose
\varnothing\neq S\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}
,
\varnothing\neq T\subseteq{\mathscr{B}}

such that
\langle S\rangle_{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}
and
\langle T\rangle_{\mathscr{B}}={\mathscr{B}}
, i.e.
S
generates
{\mathscr{A}}

and
T
generates
{\mathscr{B}}
.
If
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
are
L
-equivalent via a poset isomorphism
\psi:L({\mathscr{A}})\to L({\mathscr{B}})
and
\varphi\in\operatorname{GL}(V)

such that
\psi(S)=\varphi(S)=T
and
\psi(H)=\varphi(H)
for all
H\in S
,
then
\varphi
extends to a linear isomorphism between the whole arrangements, i.e.
\varphi({\mathscr{A}})={\mathscr{B}}
.

Proof.

Set
G_{i}:=\operatorname{Gen}_{i}({\mathscr{A}},S)
, and
G_{i}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{Gen}_{i}({\mathscr{B}},T)
.
We argue by induction that
\varphi(G_{i})=G_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i\geq 0
which implies the statement.
By assumption, the statement is clear for
i=0
.
Let
H^{\prime}\in G_{i+1}^{\prime}
. Then
H^{\prime}=\sum_{X^{\prime}\in J^{\prime}}X^{\prime}
for some
J^{\prime}\subseteq L(G_{i}^{\prime})
.
But
L(G_{i}^{\prime})=\psi(L(G_{i}))
and by the induction hypothesis
G_{i}^{\prime}=\psi(G_{i})=\varphi(G_{i})
.
Thus, with
J=\psi^{-1}(J^{\prime})=\varphi^{-1}(J^{\prime})\subseteq L(G_{i})
,
for
H=\sum_{X\in J}X\in G_{i+1}
we have
\varphi(\sum_{X\in J}X)=\sum_{X^{\prime}\in J^{\prime}}X^{\prime}=H^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an essential and irreducible arrangement in
V\cong{\mathbb{K}}^{\ell}
.
Suppose there is a subset
S
of
{\mathscr{A}}
such that
\langle S\rangle_{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}
and
|S|=\ell+1
.
Then
{\mathscr{A}}
is projectively unique.

Proof.

Since
{\mathscr{A}}
is irreducible, after an appropriate coordinate change we may assume without loss of generality that
S=\{H_{1}=\ker(x_{1}),\ldots,H_{\ell}=\ker(x_{\ell}),H_{\ell+1}=\ker(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}x_{i})\}

where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
is a basis for
V^{*}
(cf. Example
3.3
(i)).
Let
{\mathscr{B}}
be another arrangement in
V
which is
L
-equivalent to
{\mathscr{A}}
via
\psi:L({\mathscr{A}})\to L({\mathscr{B}})
and set
T:=\psi(S)
.
Since
\psi
is a poset isomorphism, we have
\langle T\rangle_{\mathscr{B}}={\mathscr{B}}
.
Moreover,
T
spans a generic subarrangement of
{\mathscr{B}}
with
\ell+1
hyperplanes,
say
T=\{\psi(H_{1})=\ker(\beta_{1}),\ldots,\psi(H_{\ell})=\ker(\beta_{\ell}),\psi(H_{\ell+1})=\ker(\gamma)\}
.
After rescaling
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{\ell}
by suitable scalars, we can assume
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}

and note that
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{\ell}
yield a basis for
V^{*}
. Thus, if we take
\varphi\in\operatorname{GL}(V)

as the unique linear map which is dual to the transformation of
V^{*}
which maps
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
to
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{\ell}
,
we have
\psi(S)=\varphi(S)=T
and
\varphi(H)=\psi(H)
for all
H\in S
.
Since
\langle S\rangle_{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}
, we can construct inductively a lattice isomorphism
\psi:L({\mathscr{A}})\to L({\mathscr{B}})

such that
\psi(S)=T
via
\psi(\operatorname{Gen}_{i}(S,{\mathscr{A}}))=\operatorname{Gen}_{i}(T,{\mathscr{B}})
.
By Lemma
3.5
,
{\mathscr{B}}
is linearly isomorphic to
{\mathscr{A}}
which concludes the proof.
∎

Finally,
Theorem
1.2
is an immediate consequence of our next result.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
G
be a connected graph on
[n]
and
let
S:=\{\ker x_{1},\ldots,\ker x_{n},\ker(x_{1}+\ldots+x_{n})\}\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
. Then

\langle S\rangle_{{\mathscr{A}}_{G}}={\mathscr{A}}_{G}
.
In particular,
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is projectively unique over

4.
Accuracy: Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section we characterize accuracy for connected subgraph arrangements. The arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is free if and only if
G
is a path, almost-path, path-with-triangle, or cycle graph. All of these arrangements are known to be accurate, the only exception being the path-with-triangle graph. The path graph and the cycle graph are isomorphic to the braid arrangement and the cone over the Shi arrangement, respectively. The braid arrangement is accurate, see
[
MR21
, Thm. 1.6]
. Also, the cone over the Shi arrangement was shown to be accurate in
[
MR21
, Thm. 1.8]
. The almost-path graph is MAT-free, and hence accurate due to
[
MR21
, Thm. 1.2]
. We are left to show that
{\mathscr{A}}_{\Delta_{n,k}}
is accurate. We first need to study the automorphism group of the almost-path graph arrangement.

Remark 4.1
.

The arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}_{A_{n,k}}
contains three independent path-graph arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}_{P_{1}}
,
{\mathscr{A}}_{P_{k-1}}
, and
{\mathscr{A}}_{P_{n-k}}
corresponding to each of the three branches of the almost-path graph. It follows that the automorphism group of the arrangement contains the product of the automorphism groups of these three path graph arrangements, namely
\mathfrak{S}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}_{k}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n-k+1}
.
Denoting by
{\operatorname{Aut}}(A_{n,k})
the automorphism group of the graph
A_{n,k}
we consider the subgroup

(4.2)

\mathcal{G}:={\operatorname{Aut}}(A_{n,k})\ltimes\mathfrak{S}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}_{k-1}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n-k+1}

of the automorphism group of
{\mathscr{A}}_{A_{n,k}}
.
More precisely, we obtain the following groups depending on
n
and
k
:

•

{\operatorname{Aut}}(A_{3,2})\ltimes(\mathfrak{S}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}_{2})
for
(n,k)=(3,2)
,

•

({\operatorname{Aut}}(A_{n,2})\ltimes(\mathfrak{S}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}_{2}))\times\mathfrak{S}_{n-2}
for
(n,k)=(n,2)
,

•

\mathfrak{S}_{2}\times({\operatorname{Aut}}(A_{2k-1,k})\ltimes(\mathfrak{S}_{k}\times\mathfrak{S}_{k}))
for
(n,k)=(2k-1,k)
,

•

\mathfrak{S}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}_{k}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n-k+1}
otherwise (here
{\operatorname{Aut}}(A_{n,k})
is trivial).

From the description above, we deduce that the action of the group
\mathcal{G}
from (
4.2
)
on
{\mathscr{A}}_{A_{n,k}}
has exactly
4-g
orbits, where
g
is the minimal number of generators of
{\operatorname{Aut}}(A_{n,k})
. In the general case when
{\operatorname{Aut}}(A_{n,k})=1
, there are

(1)

three orbits each corresponding to a path graph branch of
A_{n,k}
,

(2)

one orbit consisting of all hyperplanes
H_{I}
with
k\in I
.

When
{\operatorname{Aut}}(A_{n,k})
is non-trivial, some of the first three orbits are merged together, namely, those corresponding to branches of the same length.

We now prove accuracy for
{\mathscr{A}}_{\Delta_{n,k}}
. Consider the connected subgraph arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}_{A_{n+1,k+1}}
with its set of ordered exponents
(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n+2})_{\leq}
, and its MAT-partition
\pi=(\pi_{1}\ldots,\pi_{n+2})
, where
\pi_{d}=\{H_{I}\mid|I|=d\}
. For every hyperplane
H
in the same orbit of
x_{k+1}=0
, i.e. for
H=H_{I}
with
k+1\in I
, we have

{\mathscr{A}}_{\Delta_{n,k}}={\mathscr{A}}_{A_{n+1,k+1}}^{H}.

Because
{\mathscr{A}}_{A_{n+1,k+1}}
is MAT-free,
[
MR21
, Thm. 3.9]
implies that for each
1\leq k\leq n
and every
|\pi_{k+1}|\leq q\leq|\pi_{k}|
, there exists a subset
{\mathscr{C}}\subseteq\pi_{k}
with
|{\mathscr{C}}|=q
such that, for
X=\bigcap_{H\in{\mathscr{C}}}H
, of dimension
n+2-q
, the restriction
{\mathscr{A}}_{A_{n+1,k+1}}^{X}
is free with exponents
(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n+2-q})_{\leq}
. This is how the accuracy of MAT-free arrangements is proven in general. Now we observe that for some
H\in\pi_{i}\setminus{\mathscr{C}}
, we still have that
{\mathscr{A}}_{A_{n+1,k+1}}^{X\cap H}
is free with exponents
(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n+1-q})_{\leq}
. Because an element
H_{I}
with
k+1\in I
exists in every block of the MAT-partition
\pi
of
{\mathscr{A}}_{A_{n+1,k+1}}
, we can always choose such an element for every choice of

. Finally, we observe that
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{\Delta_{n,k}})=(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n+1})_{\leq}\subseteq(e_{1},\ e_{2},\cdots,e_{n+2})_{\leq}=\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{A_{n+1,k+1}})
. Therefore, we can conclude that

{\mathscr{A}}_{\Delta_{n,k}}^{X}=({\mathscr{A}}_{A_{n+1,k+1}}^{H})^{X}={\mathscr{A}}_{A_{n+1,k+1}}^{X\cap H}

is again free with the right exponents, that is,
{\mathscr{A}}_{\Delta_{n,k}}
is accurate. ∎

There is a natural stronger notion of accuracy as in Definition
2.10
. We say that the free arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is

, if
{\mathscr{A}}
is accurate and if the tuple
of flats

from Definition

can be chosen to be a flag in

[

, Def. 1.1]
.
It is shown in

, Cor. 1.7]

, Thm. 1.14]

It follows from

It is clear that flag-accuracy is a combinatoral property which only depend on the lattice of intersections. This is not known for

8.
Combinatorial Formality: Proof of Theorem
1.9

A property for arrangements is said to be
combinatorial
if it only depends on the intersection lattice of the underlying arrangement.
Yuzvinsky
[
Y93
, Ex. 2.2]
demonstrated that formality is not combinatorial, answering a question raised by Falk and Randell
[
FR87
]
in the negative.
Yuzvinsky’s insight motivates the following notion from
[
MMR24
]
.

Definition 8.1
.

Suppose
{\mathscr{A}}
is a formal arrangement. We say
{\mathscr{A}}
is
combinatorially formal
if every arrangement with
an intersection lattice isomorphic to the one of

{\mathscr{A}}
is also formal.

The following definitions, which are originally due to Falk for matroids
[
F02
]
, were adapted for arrangements in
[
MMR24
, §2.4]
.
Let
{\mathscr{B}}\subset{\mathscr{A}}
be a subset of hyperplanes.
We say
{\mathscr{B}}
is
closed
if
{\mathscr{B}}={\mathscr{A}}_{Y}
for
Y=\bigcap\limits_{H\in{\mathscr{B}}}H
.
We call
{\mathscr{B}}

line-closed

if for every pair
H,H^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{B}}
of hyperplanes, we have
{\mathscr{A}}_{H\cap H^{\prime}}\subset{\mathscr{B}}
.
The
line-closure

{\operatorname{lc}}({\mathscr{B}})
of
{\mathscr{B}}
is defined
as the intersection of all line-closed subsets of
{\mathscr{A}}
containing
{\mathscr{B}}
.
The arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is called
line-closed

if every line-closed subset of
{\mathscr{A}}
is closed.
With these notions, we have the following criterion for combinatorial formality, see
[
F02
, Cor. 3.8]
,
[
MMR24
, Prop. 3.2]
:

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an arrangement of rank
r
. Suppose
{\mathscr{B}}\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}
consists of
r
hyperplanes such that
r({\mathscr{B}})=r
and
{\operatorname{lc}}({\mathscr{B}})={\mathscr{A}}
. Then
{\mathscr{A}}
is combinatorially formal.

A subset
{\mathscr{B}}\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}
as in Proposition
8.2
is called an
lc-basis
of
{\mathscr{A}}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Let
G
be a connected graph.
Let
{\mathscr{B}}=\{\ker x_{i}\mid i\in[n]\}\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
.
Then it is easy to see that successively all
H_{I}
for
I\subseteq[n]
with
G[I]
connected belong to the line-losure
{\operatorname{lc}}({\mathscr{B}})
of
{\mathscr{B}}
. Consequently,
{\operatorname{lc}}({\mathscr{B}})={\mathscr{A}}_{G}
. Since

\operatorname{rk}({\mathscr{A}}_{G})=n=|B|=\operatorname{rk}({\mathscr{B}})
, it follows from Proposition
8.2

that
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is combinatorially formal.
∎

There is a stronger notion of formality for an arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
, that of
k
-formality
for
1\leq k\leq\operatorname{rk}({\mathscr{A}})
due to Brandt and Terao
[
BT94
]
.
In view of Theorem
1.9
and in view of the fact that all free arrangements are not just formal but are
k
-formal for all
k
, one might ask for this stronger notion of
k
-formlity among connected subgraph arrangements.

It was shown in
[
MRW24
, Prop. 3.4]
that despite both simple arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{1}}
and
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{2}}
are not free, both admit free multiplicities. It thus follows from

[
DiP23
, Cor. 4.10]
that both
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{1}}
and
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{2}}
are
k
-formal for any
k
.
Thus
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is
k
-formal for any
k
provided the rank of
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is at most
4
or
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is free. Computational evidence for further non-free small rank connected subgraph arrangements suggests the following.

Conjecture 8.3
.

Any
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is
k
-formal for any
k
.

On ideal subarrangements of
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}

In this section we investigate properties of natural subarrangements
of connected subgraph arrangements which are counterparts of ideal subarrangements of Weyl arrangements.

9.1.
Arrangements of ideal type in Weyl arrangements

We begin by recalling the construction of ideal arrangements from
[
ST06
, §11]
.
Let
\Phi
be an irreducible, reduced root system
and let
\Phi^{+}
be the set of positive roots
with respect to some set of simple roots
\Pi
.
An
(upper) order ideal
,
or simply

for short,
of
\Phi^{+}
, is a subset
{\mathcal{I}}
of
\Phi^{+}

satisfying the following condition:
if
\alpha\in{\mathcal{I}}
and
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
so that

\alpha+\beta\in\Phi^{+}
, then
\alpha+\beta\in{\mathcal{I}}
.
Recall the standard partial ordering

\preceq
on
\Phi
:
\alpha\preceq\beta

provided
\beta-\alpha
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
-linear combination
of positive roots, or
\beta=\alpha
. Then
{\mathcal{I}}
is an ideal in
\Phi^{+}

if and only if whenever

\alpha\in{\mathcal{I}}
and
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
so that

\alpha\preceq\beta
, then
\beta\in{\mathcal{I}}
.

Following
[
ST06
, §11]
,
we associate with an ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
in
\Phi^{+}
the arrangement
consisting of all hyperplanes with respect to the roots in
{\mathcal{I}}^{c}:=\Phi^{+}\setminus{\mathcal{I}}
, the complement of
{\mathcal{I}}
in

\Phi^{+}
.
Let
{\mathscr{A}}(\Phi)
be the
Weyl arrangement
of
\Phi
,
i.e.,
{\mathscr{A}}(\Phi)=\{H_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}
,
where
H_{\alpha}
is the hyperplane in the Euclidean space

V={\mathbb{R}}\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}\Phi
orthogonal to the root
\alpha
.

Definition 9.1

(
[
ST06
, §11]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq\Phi^{+}
be an ideal and let

{\mathcal{I}}^{c}:=\Phi^{+}\setminus{\mathcal{I}}

be its complement in

\Phi^{+}
.
The
arrangement of ideal type
associated with

{\mathcal{I}}
is the subarrangement
{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}

of
{\mathscr{A}}(\Phi)
defined by

{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}:=\{H_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in{\mathcal{I}}^{c}\}.

We note that
{\mathcal{I}}^{c}

is a lower order ideal in the set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
.

In
[
Rö17
]
it is shown that a combinatorial property (see

[
Rö17
, Cond. 1.10]
)
combined with Terao’s fibration theorem
[
Ter86
]

provides an inductive method which allows one to deduce that a large class of
the arrangements of ideal type
{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}

are inductively free.
In fact all ideal arrangements

{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}
are inductively free for all types of reduced root systems, thanks to

[
CRS19
, Thm. 1.4]
.

9.2.
Arrangements of ideal type in connected subgraph arrangements

In
[

, Ex. 1.4]
, Cuntz and Kühne remark that if
G
is the underlying simple graph of the Dynkin diagram of a reduced root system

as in Definition
9.1
where the hyperplanes in

in
\Phi

where a simple root has coefficient

{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}:=\{H_{I}\in{\mathscr{A}}_{G}\mid I\in{\mathcal{I}}\}.

It was shown in
[

.
Generalizing this result and generalizing
[
CK24
, Thm. 4.1]

of the connected subgraph arrangements

.

In our following two results we present

for
for

Proposition 9.7

Let

Proof.

The inductive arguments in

]
to derive

generalizations for all almost-path-graphs

Thanks to

Rö17
, Thm. 1.31]
, for
k=2
and any
n
and for

and

, both parts above hold.
So we may assume that
n\geq 7
and

and argue by induction on

.
Consider the subgraph

of
G
and the induced

of
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}

Let

be the flat in

(resp. in

) given by the intersection of all

. Then

is a modular element of corank

in

(resp.
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}^{3}
) and

{\mathscr{A}}_{G^{\prime}}^{2}
(resp.
{\mathscr{A}}_{G^{\prime}}^{3}
) coincides with this localization of

at

(resp.
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}^{3}
at

Let

We argue by induction on

with either

One checks directly that in each instance

For instance, if

admits

hyperplanes, then
the following is an inductive factorization of

, where we use the short hand notation

\pi=\{2\},\{1,12,125\},\{3,23,123\},\{5,25,235,2345\},\{4,34,234,1234\}.

10.
Rank-generating functions of the poset of regions

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a
hyperplane arrangement in the real vector space
V={\mathbb{R}}^{\ell}
.
A
region
of
{\mathscr{A}}
is a connected component of the
complement
M({\mathscr{A}}):=V\setminus\cup_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}H
of
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Let
{\mathscr{R}}:={\mathscr{R}}({\mathscr{A}})
be the set of regions of
{\mathscr{A}}
.
For
R,R^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{R}}
, we let
{\mathcal{S}}(R,R^{\prime})
denote the
set of hyperplanes in
{\mathscr{A}}
separating
R
and
R^{\prime}
.
Then with respect to a choice of a fixed
base region
B
in
{\mathscr{R}}
, we can partially order

{\mathscr{R}}
as follows:

R\leq R^{\prime}\quad\text{ if }\quad{\mathcal{S}}(B,R)\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}(B,R^{\prime}).

Endowed with this partial order, we call
{\mathscr{R}}
the

poset of regions of
{\mathscr{A}}
(with respect to
B
)
and denote it by

P({\mathscr{A}},B)
. This is a ranked poset of finite rank,
where
\operatorname{rk}(R):=|{\mathcal{S}}(B,R)|
, for
R
a region of
{\mathscr{A}}
,

[
Ed84
, Prop. 1.1]
.
The
rank-generating function
of
P({\mathscr{A}},B)
is
defined to be the following polynomial in

{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}[t]

\zeta(P({\mathscr{A}},B);t):=\sum_{R\in{\mathscr{R}}}t^{\operatorname{rk}(R)}.

This poset along with its rank-generating function
was introduced by Edelman

[
Ed84
]
.

Thanks to work of Björner, Edelman, and Ziegler

[
BEZ90
, Thm. 4.4]

(see also Paris
[
Pa95
]
), respectively
Jambu and Paris
[
JP95
, Prop. 3.4, Thm. 6.1]
,
in case of a real arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}

which is supersolvable,
respectively inductively factored,
there always exists a suitable base region
B
so that

\zeta(P({\mathscr{A}},B);t)

admits a multiplicative decomposition which
is determined by the exponents of
{\mathscr{A}}
, i.e.

(10.1)

\zeta(P({\mathscr{A}},B);t)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(1+t+\ldots+t^{e_{i}}),

where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}=\exp{\mathscr{A}}
is the
set of exponents of
{\mathscr{A}}
.

Quite remarkably
many classical real arrangements do satisfy the
factorization identity (
10.1
),
the most prominent ones being Coxeter arrangements.

Let
W=(W,S)
be a Coxeter group with associated reflection arrangement

{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
which consists of the reflecting hyperplanes of
the reflections in
W
in the real space
V={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where
|S|=n
.
The

Poincaré polynomial

W(t)
of
the Coxeter group
W
is the polynomial in
{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
defined by

(10.2)

W(t):=\sum_{w\in W}t^{\ell(w)},

where
\ell
is the length function
of
W
with respect to
S
.
Then
W(t)

coincides with the rank-generating function of the poset of regions
\zeta(P({\mathscr{A}},B);t)
of
the underlying reflection arrangement

{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
with respect to
B
being the dominant Weyl chamber of
W
in
V
;
see
[
BEZ90
]
or
[
JP95
]
.

The following factorization of

W(t)
is due to Solomon
[
Sol66
]
:

(10.3)

W(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+t+\ldots+t^{e_{i}}),

where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is the
set of exponents of
W
, i.e., the set of exponents of
{\mathscr{A}}(W)
. So by the comments above, (
10.3
) coincides with the factorization in (
10.1
).

Moreover, also the rank-generating function of the poset of regions
\zeta(P({\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}},B);t)
for
an ideal arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathcal{I}}

from Definition
9.1

also obeys the factorization identity (
10.1
); see

[
ST06
]
,

[
Rö17
]
for partial results, and

[
AHM+20
, Cor. 1.3]
for the general statement.

Let
W
be a Coxeter group again with reflection arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
,
let
X
be a member of the intersection lattice
L({\mathscr{A}})
, and consider the restricted reflection arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}^{X}
. In general,
{\mathscr{A}}^{X}
is no longer a reflection arrangement.
Nevertheless, thanks to
[
MR19
, Thm. 1.3]
,
there always exists a suitable base region
B
of
{\mathscr{A}}^{X}
in
X
so that also

\zeta(P({\mathscr{A}}^{X},B);t)
satisfies (
10.1
), provided
W
is not of type
E_{8}
. In case
W
is of type
E_{8}
, then
\zeta(P({\mathscr{A}}^{X},B);t)
satisfies (
10.1
), provided
X
has rank at most
3
with only two exceptions or
{\mathscr{A}}^{X}\cong(E_{8},D_{4})
.

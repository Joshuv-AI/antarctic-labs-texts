Strictly zero-dimensional biframes and Raney extensions
Anna Laura Suarez

Abstract
Raney extensions and strictly zero-dimensional biframes both faithfully extend the dual of the category of T 0 T_{0} spaces. We use tools from pointfree topology to look at the connection between the two. Raney extensions may be equivalently described as pairs ( L , ℱ ) (L,\mathcal{F}) where L L is a frame and ℱ ⊆ 𝖲 𝔬 ​ ( L ) \mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L) a subcolocale containing all opens. Here, 𝖲 𝔬 ​ ( L ) \mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L) is the collection of all fitted sublocales of L L . Similarly, a strictly zero-dimensional biframe is a pair ( L , 𝒟 ) (L,\mathcal{D}) where 𝒟 ⊆ 𝖲 ⁡ ( L ) \mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L) is a codense subcolocale. We show that there is an adjunction between certain subcolocales of 𝖲 𝔬 ​ ( L ) \mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L) and codense subcolocales of 𝖲 ⁡ ( L ) \mathsf{S}(L) . We show that the adjunction maximally restricts to an order-isomorphism between what we call the proper subcolocales of 𝖲 𝔬 ​ ( L ) \mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L) and the essential codense subcolocales. As an application of our main result, we establish a bijection between proper Raney extensions and the strictly zero-dimensional biframes ( L 1 , L 2 , L ) (L_{1},L_{2},L) such that L L is an essential extension of L 2 L_{2} in 𝐅𝐫𝐦 \mathbf{Frm} . We show that this correspondence cannot be made functorial in the obvious way, as a frame morphism f : L → M f:L\to M may lift to a map f : ( L , ℱ ) → ( L , 𝒢 ) f:(L,\mathcal{F})\to(L,\mathcal{G}) of Raney extensions without lifting to a map between the associated strictly zero-dimensional biframes.



Introduction

The usual approach in pointfree topology is to consider the adjunction
\Omega:\mathbf{Top}\leftrightarrows\mathbf{Frm}^{op}:\mathsf{pt}
between frames and spaces, and to regard frames as pointfree spaces in virtue of this. This is the classical approach found, for example, in
[
13
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
. The fixpoints on the
\mathbf{Top}
side are the sober spaces, so we may view
\mathbf{Frm}
as a faithful extension of the opposite of the category
\mathbf{Sob}
of sober spaces. An alternative approach is
T_{D}
-duality
, developed in
[
6
]
. This is based on the
T_{D}
-axiom, introduced in
[
2
]
. The full subcategory of
\mathbf{Top}
consisting of the
T_{D}
-spaces is related via a similar adjunction to a wide subcategory of
\mathbf{Frm}
, the category of frames with
D-morphisms
, called
\mathbf{Frm_{D}}
. The category
\mathbf{Frm_{D}}
is thus shown to faithfully extend the opposite of
\mathbf{Top_{D}}
. Sobriety and the
T_{D}
property are incomparable. This is why the language of frames, under both translations, is not expressive enough to capture certain concepts and constructions. For example, in
\mathbf{Frm}
we do not have a notion of sobrification. Similarly, in
\mathbf{Frm_{D}}
, we do not have a notion of
T_{D}
-coreflection (which exists for spaces). Refining the language by extending the category
\mathbf{Frm}
enables us to capture both things in the same category. For example, in
[
22
]
, the category
\mathbf{Frm}
is extended to the category
\mathbf{Raney}
of
Raney extensions
, which also faithfully extends the dual of the category
\mathbf{Top_{0}}
of all
T_{0}
-spaces. In this category, we have both the notion of
sober coreflection
(Section 6.5), the dual notion of sobrification, and
T_{D}
-reflection
(Section 6.6), dual of the
T_{D}
-coreflection.

•

Raney extensions are inspired by the work of Raney on completely distributive lattices, see for example
[
20
]
. Raney duality, as illustrated in
[
7
]
, is the result that the dual of
\mathbf{Top_{0}}
is equivalent to the category of completely distributive lattices
\mathcal{U}(X)
consisting of upper sets of some poset
X
, equipped with an interior operator. A
Raney extension
is a pair
(L,C)
where
C
is a coframe and
L\subseteq C
is a frame with the inherited order, such that the subset inclusion preserves all joins and strongly exact meets. The Raney extension corresponding to a space
X
is the pair
(\Omega(X),\mathcal{U}(X))
, where
\Omega(X)
is its frame of opens and
\mathcal{U}(X)
is the collection of upper sets in its specialization order. The category
\mathbf{Raney}
of Raney extensions faithfully extends
\mathbf{Top_{0}}^{op}
.

•

Another approach to refining the language of frames is that of
McKinsey-Tarski algebras
, as introduced in
[
8
]
. This is based on work by McKinsey and Tarski, who in
[
15
]
studied topological spaces in terms of the closure operator they induce on their powerset. McKinsey-Tarski algebras make this approach pointfree, by considering as objects complete Boolean algebras, not necessarily atomic, with interior operators. The category of MT-algebras faithfully extends all of
\mathbf{Top}^{op}
.

•

A third approach to faithfully extending the dual of
\mathbf{Top_{0}}
is that of
strictly zero-dimensional biframes
, as shown in
[
14
]
. Although in
[
14
]
the pointfree description of
T_{0}
spaces is not the focus, it is indeed observed (end of Section 4) that there is a dual adjunction between spaces and strictly zero-dimensional biframes, whose fixpoints are the
T_{0}
spaces.

It is then interesting to look at how the three categories interact, and go towards a more unified theory of pointfree
T_{0}
spaces. The connection between Raney extensions and MT-algebras is looked at in
[
9
]
. A Raney extension
(L,C)
is
proper
if the joins of
L
distribute over all binary meets in
C
. In
[
9
]
the connection between Raney extensions and MT-algebras is explored, and it is shown that the category of proper Raney extensions is equivalent to the category
\mathbf{MT_{0}}
of
T_{0}
MT-algebras equipped with a notion of morphism based on a proximity-like relation.

With this paper, we add another part to the big picture, connecting explicitly the categories of Raney extensions and that of strictly zero-dimensional biframes. In particular, we show that there is a bijection at the level of objects between proper Raney extensions and
essential
strictly zero-dimensional biframes. Our approach is by no means the simplest possible way of proving this correspondence. Instead, we want to obtain the correspondence as a byproduct of a more general study of subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)
and subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
. We point out the following result.

Proposition 0.1
.

Let
L
be a frame. Raney extensions on
L
are in bijective correspondence with subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
containing all open sublocales. Strictly zero-dimensional biframes whose first component is
L
are in bijective correspondence with dense subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)
.

Notice how, in the usual setting, all sublocales of
L
are sober: for a sober space
X
, the sober subspaces are exactly the ones of the form

\bigcap_{i}U_{i}\cup V_{i}^{c}

where
U_{i},V_{i}\subseteq X
are opens. Compare this with the fact that every sublocale
S\subseteq L
of a frame
L
satisfies

S=\bigcap\{\mathfrak{o}(a)\vee\mathfrak{c}(b)\mid S\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(a)\vee\mathfrak{c}(b)\}

to see that it is natural to view
\mathsf{S}(L)
as the collection of all
sober
subspaces of
L
. But Raney extensions and strictly zero-dimensional biframes tell us that if we want to capture all subspaces of
L
, not just sober ones, it suffices to look at subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
or of
\mathsf{S}(L)
, rather than sublocales of
L
. For example, notice that if
X
is a
T_{0}
space it may be the case that distinct subspaces induce the same sublocale of
\Omega(X)
. However, as Raney extensions faithfully extend
\mathbf{Top_{0}}
, this means that the two subspace inclusions will induce different surjections from
(\Omega(X),\mathcal{U}(X))
in
\mathbf{Raney}
. By the equivalence in Proposition
0.1
above, this means that these will induce different subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(\Omega(X))
.

In conclusion, it may be argued that
\mathsf{S}(L)
which is not refined enough to capture spaces which are not sober, but that the limitation is overcome by looking at the collections
\mathsf{S}(\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L))
and
\mathsf{S}(\mathsf{S}(L))
, instead. In this paper, we show explicitly how to relate the two approaches. We do so by proving an adjunction between codense subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)
and certain subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
which we call
proper
. We prove that the adjunction maximally restricts to proper subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
and a class of subcolocales
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
which we characterize explicitly.

1
Preliminaries

1.1
The categories
\mathbf{Frm}
and
\mathbf{Loc}

We first recall some background on frames and point-free topology. For more information on the categories of frames and locales, we refer the reader to Johnstone
[
13
]
or the more recent
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
. A
frame
is a complete lattice
L
satisfying

a\wedge\bigvee B=\bigvee\{a\wedge b\mid b\in B\},

for all
a\in L
and
B\subseteq L
. A
frame homomorphism
is a function preserving arbitrary joins, including the bottom element
0
, and finite meets, including the top element
1
. We call
\mathbf{Frm}
the category of frames and frame homomorphisms. Frames are complete Heyting algebras, with the Heyting implication computed as

x\to y=\bigvee\{z\in L\mid z\wedge x\leq y\}.

In particular, the
pseudocomplement
of an
a\in L
is the element
\neg a=a\to 0
. The archetypal example of frame is the lattice of open sets
\Omega(X)
for a topological space
X
. The assignment
X\mapsto\Omega(X)
is the object part of a functor
\Omega:\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Frm}^{op}
. An element
p
of a frame
L
is said to be
prime
if whenever
x\wedge y\leq p
for some
x,y\in L
, then
x\leq p
or
y\leq p
. The collection of all primes of
L
will be denoted by
\mathsf{pt}(L)
, and the assignment
L\mapsto\mathsf{pt}(L)
is the object part of a functor
\mathsf{pt}:\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Frm}^{op}
, which together with
\Omega
yields an adjunction
\Omega:\mathbf{Top}\leftrightarrows\mathbf{Frm}^{op}:\mathsf{pt}
with
\Omega\dashv\mathsf{pt}
. A frame is said to be
spatial
if
a\nleq b
for
a,b\in L
implies that there is a prime
p
with
b\leq p
and
a\nleq p
. Spatial frames
L
are precisely the fixpoints of the adjunction
\Omega\dashv\mathsf{pt}
. This adjunction is idempotent, and so a frame is spatial if
L\cong\Omega(X)
for some space
X
. Because of this adjunction, frames are regarded as pointfree spaces, but since the adjunction is contravariant sometimes the category
\mathbf{Loc}
, equivalent to
\mathbf{Frm}^{op}
, is used.

1.1.1
The
T_{D}
approach

An alternative to the classical duality is the so-called
T_{D}
-duality
from
[
6
]
. For a space
X
we call a point
x\in X
a
T_{D}
-point
if it is the intersection of an open and a closed set. We define a space to be
T_{D}
if all its points are
T_{D}
. The axiom is between
T_{0}
and
T_{1}
and it is introduced in
[
2
]
. For a frame
L
, a prime
p\in L
is
covered
if
\bigwedge_{i}x_{i}=p
implies
x_{i}=p
for some
i\in I
. We call
\mathsf{pt}_{D}(L)
the set of covered primes of
L
. We say that a frame morphisms
f:L\to M
is a
D-morphism
if its right adjoint
f_{*}:M\to L
maps covered primes to covered primes. We call
\mathbf{Frm_{D}}
the category of frames and D-morphisms. The assignment
L\mapsto\mathsf{pt}_{D}(L)
extends to a functor
\mathsf{pt}_{D}:\mathbf{Frm_{D}}^{op}\to\mathbf{Top}
. In
[
6
]
it is shown that there is an adjunction
\Omega:\mathbf{Top_{D}}\leftrightarrows\mathbf{Frm_{D}}^{op}:\mathsf{pt}_{D}
with
\Omega\dashv\mathsf{pt}_{D}
, where
\mathbf{Top_{D}}
is the category of
T_{D}
-spaces.
We call a frame
T_{D}
-spatial
if it is meet-generated by its covered primes.
T_{D}
-spatial frames are exactly the fixpoints of the adjunction above, namely the frames of opens of
T_{D}
-spaces.

1.1.2
Sublocales

For a frame
L
, a
sublocale
of
L
is a subset inclusion
S\subseteq L
such that

1.

S
is closed under arbitrary meets;

2.

a\to s\in S
for all
a\in L
and
s\in S
.

Sublocales are frames when equipped with the order inherited from
L
. In fact, the name comes from the fact that such subset inclusions are, up to isomorphism, the regular monomorphisms in
\mathbf{Loc}
. If
S\subseteq L
is a sublocale, we call
\bigwedge^{S}
and
\wedge^{S}
the arbitrary and the binary meets in
S
, respectively, and we use a similar convention for joins. Whenever
L
is any lattice and
M\subseteq L
a lattice with the inherited order, we use analogous notation for lattice operations in
M
with the inherited order. Sublocales have closure operators associated to them. For a sublocale
S\subseteq L
the map
\nu_{S}(a)=\bigwedge\{s\in S\mid a\leq s\}
for all
a\in L
is called its
nucleus
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
S\subseteq L
be a sublocale. Then, for
s_{i}\in S
;

1.

\bigwedge^{S}_{i}s_{i}=\bigwedge_{i}s_{i}
;

2.

\bigvee^{S}_{i}s_{i}=\nu_{S}(\bigvee_{i}s_{i})
.

Every sublocale also has an associated frame
congruence
, a binary relation on
L
which is a subframe of
L\times L
. An alternative approach, which we will adopt, is to consider the bijection between sublocales of
L
and
precongruences
on
L
. These are defined to be binary relations
R
on
L
such that

1.

R
is reflexive;

2.

R
is transitive;

3.

a^{\prime}\leq a
and
(a,b)\in R
and
b\leq b^{\prime}
implies
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in R
;

4.

(a_{i},b)\in R
implies
(\bigvee_{i}a_{i},b)\in R
;

5.

(a,b_{1}),(a,b_{2})\in R
implies
(a,b_{1}\wedge b_{2})\in R
.

This correspondence is introduced in
[
17
]
. The sublocale
S
is associated with the precongruence
\{(a,b)\in L\times L\mid\nu_{S}(a)\leq\nu_{S}(b)\}
. Conversely, for a precongruence
R\subseteq L\times L
, the associated sublocale is

\bigcap\{\mathfrak{c}(x)\vee\mathfrak{o}(y)\mid(x,y)\in R\}.

A sublocale of
L
is
dense
if it contains
0
. Dense sublocales, then, are closed under arbitrary intersections. For a frame
L
, the smallest dense sublocale
\mathfrak{b}(0)\subseteq L
is always Boolean, and it is called its
Booleanization
.

1.2
Subcolocales and the coframe
\mathsf{S}(L)

1.2.1
Subcolocales

Coframes come equipped with a
co-Heyting operator
, known as the
difference

x{\setminus}y
of two elements
x,y\in C
, computed as

x{\setminus}y=\bigwedge\{z\in C\mid x\leq y\vee z\}.

This operator is characterized by the condition that
x{\setminus}y\leq z
if and only if
x\leq y\vee z
. In particular, the
supplement
of
c\in C
is
c^{*}=1{\setminus}c
. We shall freely use some its properties, listed in the following lemma.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
C
be a coframe. For elements
c,d,c_{i},x\in C
:

1.

If
x
is complemented,
c{\setminus}x=c\wedge x^{*}
;

2.

(\bigvee_{i}c_{i}){\setminus}d=\bigvee_{i}(c_{i}{\setminus}d)
;

3.

d{\setminus}\bigwedge_{i}c_{i}=\bigvee_{i}(d{\setminus}c_{i})
.

For a coframe
C
, we say that an element
c\in C
is
linear
if
\bigvee_{i}(a_{i}\wedge c)=\bigvee_{i}a_{i}\wedge c
for all
a_{i}\in C
.

Lemma 1.3
.

Complemented elements of a coframe are linear.

Of particular importance will be the notion dual to that of sublocale. Let us define it explicitly. For a coframe
C
, a
subcolocale
is an inclusion
D\subseteq C
such that

1.

D
is closed under all joins;

2.

d{\setminus}c\in D
for all
d\in D
and
c\in C
.

An inclusion
D\subseteq C
for a frame
D
is a subcolocale iff
D^{op}\subseteq C^{op}
is a sublocale. Dualizing the analogous notion for frames, we see that a subcolocale
D\subseteq C
determines an interior operator
\nu_{D}:C\to C
, which we call its
conucleus
.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
D\subseteq C
be a sublocale. Then, for
d_{i}\in D
;

1.

\bigwedge^{D}_{i}d_{i}=\nu_{D}(\bigwedge_{i}d_{i})
;

2.

\bigvee^{D}_{i}d_{i}=\bigvee_{i}d_{i}
.

We say that a subcolocale is
codense
if it contains
1
. We keep the term for the dual notion and call the smallest codense subcolocale of a coframe its
Booleanization
.

1.2.2
The coframe of sublocales

The family
\mathsf{S}(L)
of all sublocales of
L
, ordered by inclusion, is a coframe. Meets are set-theoretical intersections. Because
\mathsf{S}(L)
is a coframe, it also comes with a difference operation, computed as
S{\setminus}T=\bigcap\{U\in\mathsf{S}(L)\mid S\subseteq T\vee U\}
. This is studied in
[
11
]
. For each
a\in L
, there is an
open sublocale

\mathfrak{o}(a)=\{b\in L\mid b=a\to b\}=\{a\to b\mid b\in L\}
and a
closed sublocale

\mathfrak{c}(a)={\uparrow}a
. Open and closed sublocales behave like open and closed subspaces in many respects, in the lemma below we list a few.

Lemma 1.5
.

For every frame
L
and
a,b,a_{i}\in L
we have

1.

\mathfrak{o}(1)=L
and
\mathfrak{o}(0)=\{1\}
.

2.

\mathfrak{c}(1)=\{1\}
and
\mathfrak{c}(0)=L
.

3.

\mathfrak{o}(a)\cap\mathfrak{c}(a)=\{1\}
and
\mathfrak{o}(a)\vee\mathfrak{c}(a)=L
.

4.

\bigvee_{i}\mathfrak{o}(a_{i})=\mathfrak{o}(\bigvee_{i}a_{i})
and
\mathfrak{o}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)=\mathfrak{o}(a\wedge b)
.

5.

\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{c}(a_{i})=\mathfrak{c}(\bigwedge_{i}a_{i})
and
\mathfrak{c}(a)\vee\mathfrak{c}(b)=\mathfrak{c}(a\wedge b)
.

In particular, by
3
, open and closed sublocales are complemented, and as such (Lemma
1.3
) they are linear. Intersections of open sublocales are called
fitted
sublocales. These form a subcoframe of
\mathsf{S}(L)
, which we denote as
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
. The closure operator associated with it is called the
fitting
. This is studied in
[
10
]
. More explicitly, the corestriction to its fixpoints is:

\displaystyle\mathit{fit}:

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}(L)\to\mathsf{S}(L)

\displaystyle S\mapsto\bigcap\{\mathfrak{o}(x)\mid x\in L,S\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)\}.

Another important class of sublocales is that of the
two-element sublocales
. These are sublocales of the form
\{1,p\}
for some element
p\in L
, which is then necessarily prime.

1.2.3
Exactness and strong exactness

For a complete lattice
L
we say that a join (resp. a meet) is
exact
if it distributes over binary meets (resp. joins). These notions are compared in
[
3
]
. When
L
is a frame, we also have the notion of
strongly exact
meet: a meet
\bigwedge_{i}x_{i}
such that
x_{i}\to y=y
for all
i\in I
implies that
\bigwedge_{i}x_{i}\to y=y
. A filter
F\subseteq L
of a frame is called
exact
if it is closed under exact meets, and
strongly exact
if it is closed under strongly exact meets. We call
\mathsf{Filt}_{\mathcal{SE}}(L)
the ordered collection of strongly exact filters of
L
, and
\mathsf{Filt}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)
the collection of the exact filters.
The following is Theorem 3.5 in
[
16
]
.

Lemma 1.6
.

There is an isomorphism
\varphi:\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)\cong\mathsf{Filt}_{\mathcal{SE}}(L)
given by

\varphi(F)=\{x\in L\mid F\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)\}

for each
F\in\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
.

We will consider the map
\varphi\circ\mathit{fit}:\mathsf{S}(L)\to\mathsf{Filt}_{\mathcal{SE}}(L)
. We call this
\mathit{ker}
, for
kernel
, as it assigns to a sublocale
S\subseteq L
the set
s^{-1}(1)
, where
s:L\to S
is its surjection. Theorem 6.6 of
[
12
]
, together with the fact that
\varphi
is an isomorphism, gives the following.

Lemma 1.7
.

For every frame
L
,
\mathit{ker}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)]=\mathsf{Filt}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)
.

We say that a frame map
f:L\to M
is
exact
if, whenever
\bigwedge_{i}x_{i}\in L
is an exact meet,
\bigwedge_{i}f(x_{i})
is exact, and
\bigwedge_{i}f(x_{i})=f(\bigwedge_{i}x_{i})
. A sublocale
S\subseteq L
whose surjection
s:L\to S
is exact is called an
exact
sublocale. Exact sublocales are precisely those whose surjection preserves all exact meets. The next result is Proposition 7.15 in
[
22
]
.

Lemma 1.8
.

A sublocale
S\subseteq L
is exact if and only if whenever
\bigwedge_{i}x_{i}\in L
is an exact meet and
S\cap\mathfrak{c}(x_{i})\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)
for every
i\in I
then
S\cap\mathfrak{c}(\bigwedge_{i}x_{i})\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)
for all
x\in L
.

1.2.4
Distinguished subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)

In this work, we look at subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)
. Among these is the collection
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
of joins of complemented sublocales (see
[
1
]
). These also coincide with those sublocales of the form
\bigvee_{i}\mathfrak{c}(x_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(y_{i})
. We observe that a subcolocale of
\mathsf{S}(L)
is codense if and only if it contains
L
. The following is well-known.

Lemma 1.9
.

For any frame
L
, the inclusion
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is a codense subcolocale. In particular, it is the Booleanization of
\mathsf{S}(L)
.

We will need the following result.

Lemma 1.10
.

If
X
is a
T_{D}
-space, all sublocales in
\mathsf{S}_{b}(\Omega(X))
are
T_{D}
-spatial.

Proof.

Proposition 3.2 of
[
1
]
states that all sublocales in
\mathsf{S}_{b}(\Omega(X))
are induced by some subspace of
X
, in the sense that, for each of these, the surjection corresponding to them is of the form
\Omega(i_{Y}):\Omega(X)\to\Omega(Y)
for some subspace inclusion
i_{Y}:Y\subseteq X
. Since subspaces of
T_{D}
spaces are
T_{D}
, these are
T_{D}
-spatial sublocales.
∎

We will also look at the collection
\mathsf{S}_{sp}(L)
of spatial sublocales of
L
, by which we mean the sublocales
S\subseteq L
where
S
is a spatial frame. Equivalently, spatial sublocales are characterized as those which are joins of two-element sublocales. For all frames
L
, the inclusion
\mathsf{S}_{sp}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is a subcolocale inclusion, in particular, it is the subcolocale associated with the spatialization surjection of
\mathsf{S}(L)^{op}
(Proposition 3.14 of
[
21
]
). The next result follows by definition of codensity.

Lemma 1.11
.

For a frame
L
, the subcolocale
\mathsf{S}_{sp}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is codense if and only if
L
is spatial.

The collection of all exact sublocales of
L
will be denoted as
\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)
. The coming result follows from Theorem 7.20 of
[
22
]
and Proposition 7.12 from the same paper. The second part of the claim follows from
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
being the smallest codense subcolocale.

Lemma 1.12
.

The inclusion
\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is a codense subcolocale. In particular,
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)
.

1.3
Strictly zero-dimensional biframes and Raney extensions

1.3.1
Strictly zero-dimensional biframes

A
biframe
(see
[
5
]
) is a triple
\mathcal{L}=(L_{1},L_{2},L)
where
L
is a frame and
L_{1},L_{2}\subseteq L
are subframes such that
L_{1}\cup L_{2}
generates
L
in the sense that
L
is the smallest subframe containing
L_{1}\cup L_{2}
. An element
a_{1}\in L_{1}
is said to be
bicomplemented
if it is complemented in
L
and its complement is in
L_{2}
, and similarly for elements of
L_{2}
. A biframe is said to be
zero-dimensional
if both
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are join-generated by their bicomplemented elements. A biframe is said to be
strictly zero-dimensional
if it is zero-dimensional and all elements of
L_{1}
are bicomplemented. Strictly zero-dimensional biframes are studied in detail in
[
14
]
. We will later see (Lemma
2.2
) that dense subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)
contain all closed sublocales. In particular, these are embedded as a subcoframe
\mathfrak{c}[L]\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. We give an equivalent description to the category of strictly zero-dimensional biframes, similar to the one given in Section 3.2 of
[
14
]
. The category
\mathbf{SZDBF}
is the category:

1.

Whose objects are pairs
(L,\mathcal{D})
where
L
is a frame and
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
a dense subcolocale;

2.

Whose morphisms
f:(L,\mathcal{D})\to(M,\mathcal{E})
are frame maps
f:L\to M
such that the anti-isomorphic coframe map
\mathfrak{c}(f):\mathfrak{c}[L]\to\mathfrak{c}[M]
extends to a coframe map
\overline{f}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}
.

1.3.2
Raney extensions

Raney extensions are introduced in
[
22
]
. These structures are inspired by a duality studied in
[
7
]
, based on work by Raney, see
[
20
]
. Raney extensions may be identified with pairs
(L,\mathcal{F})
, where
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{Filt}_{\mathcal{SE}}(L)^{op}
is a subcolocale containing all principal filters (see Theorem 3.9 in
[
22
]
). Here, we use the isomorphism in
1.6
to give an equivalent definition. We define
\mathbf{Raney}
to be the category:

1.

Whose objects are pairs
(L,\mathcal{F})
where
L
is a frame and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
a subcolocale containing all open sublocales;

2.

Whose morphisms
f:(L,\mathcal{F})\to(M,\mathcal{G})
are frame maps
f:L\to M
such that the isomorphic frame map
\mathfrak{o}(f):\mathfrak{o}[L]\to\mathfrak{o}[M]
extends to a coframe map
\overline{f}:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
.

2
The main adjunction

2.1
From subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)
to subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
via fitting

In this subsection, we show that the fitting operator
\mathit{fit}:\mathsf{S}(L)\to\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
is such that its direct image
\mathit{fit}[-]:\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{S}(L))\to\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L))
sends codense subcolocales to special kinds of subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
. First, we work towards a characterization of codense subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

An inclusion
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is a subcolocale if and only if it is closed under arbitrary joins and stable under both
-\cap\mathfrak{o}(x)
and
-\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)
for all
x\in L
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{D}
satisfies the assumptions in the statement. By definition of subcolocale, it suffices to show that
S\in\mathcal{D}
implies
S{\setminus}T\in\mathcal{D}
for all
T\in\mathsf{S}(L)
. Let
S\in\mathcal{D}
and let
T=\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\vee\mathfrak{c}(y_{i})
. By assumption, for every
i\in I
we have
S\cap\mathfrak{c}(x_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(y_{i})\in\mathcal{D}
. By
1
of Lemma
1.2
, this equals
S{\setminus}(\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\vee\mathfrak{c}(y_{i}))
. As
\mathcal{D}
is closed under all joins and by item
3
of Lemma
1.2
,
S{\setminus}\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\vee\mathfrak{c}(y_{i})\in\mathcal{D}
, as desired. For the other direction, if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is a subcolocale, indeed it must be stable under
-\cap\mathfrak{o}(x)
and
-\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)
, as by item
1
of Lemma
1.2
these are the same as
-{\setminus}\mathfrak{c}(x)
and
-{\setminus}\mathfrak{o}(x)
, respectively.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
be a codense subcolocale. Then:

1.

\mathcal{D}
contains all open and closed sublocales;

2.

\mathcal{D}
is meet-generated by the elements of the form
\mathfrak{o}(x)\vee\mathfrak{o}(y)
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is codense, by definition it must contain
L
. By Lemma
2.1
, then, it must contain
L{\setminus}\mathfrak{o}(x)
and
L{\setminus}\mathfrak{c}(x)
for all
x\in L
, but by
1
of Lemma
1.2
these equal
\mathfrak{c}(x)
and
\mathfrak{o}(x)
, respectively. Every element of
\mathcal{D}
is
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(S)
for some sublocale
S\in\mathsf{S}(L)
, and
S=\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\vee\mathfrak{c}(y_{i})
for some
x_{i},y_{i}\in L
. Then, every element of
\mathcal{D}
can be written as
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\vee\mathfrak{c}(y_{i}))
for some
x_{i},y_{i}\in L
. This equals
\bigwedge^{\mathcal{D}}_{i}\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\vee\mathfrak{c}(x_{i}))
by Lemma
1.4
. As
\mathcal{D}
contains all open and closed sublocales by the first item, and as it is closed under joins by
2.1
,
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\vee\mathfrak{c}(y_{i}))=\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\vee\mathfrak{c}(y_{i})
, and our claim is proven.
∎

We now characterize the subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
in a similar manner. We call
\bigvee^{\mathit{fit}}
and
\setminus^{\mathit{fit}}
the joins and the coframe differences in
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
, respectively.

Lemma 2.3
.

In
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
, for all
F_{i},F,G
:

1.

\bigvee^{\mathit{fit}}_{i}F_{i}=\mathit{fit}(\bigvee_{i}F_{i})
;

2.

F{\setminus}^{fit}G=\mathit{fit}(F{\setminus}G)
.

Proof.

For the joins, it suffices to notice that if
F_{i}\subseteq F
is an upper bound in
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
, as
F
is a fixpoint of
\mathit{fit}
also
\mathit{fit}(\bigvee_{i}F_{i})\subseteq F
. By its definition, the difference
F{\setminus}^{fit}G
is such that
F{\setminus}^{fit}G\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)
if and only if
F\subseteq G\vee\mathfrak{o}(x)
for all
x\in L
. But the following are also equivalent:

F\subseteq G\vee\mathfrak{o}(x)

F{\setminus}G\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)

\mathit{fit}(F{\setminus}G)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x))
.

Since fitted sublocales are intersections of open ones, and we have shown that the fitted sublocales
F{\setminus}^{fit}G
and
\mathit{fit}(F{\setminus}G)
are contained in the same opens, they must be equal.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For a frame
L
and
F\in\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
, and
x\in L

\mathit{fit}(F\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))=F{\setminus}^{fit}\mathfrak{o}(x).

Proof.

By
1
of Lemma
1.2
,
F\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)=F{\setminus}\mathfrak{o}(x)
, and so
\mathit{fit}(F\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))=\mathit{fit}(F{\setminus}\mathfrak{o}(x))
. By Lemma
2.3
,
\mathit{fit}(F{\setminus}\mathfrak{o}(x))=F{\setminus}^{\mathit{fit}}\mathfrak{o}(x)
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

A collection
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
is a subcolocale if and only if it is closed under arbitrary joins and is stable under
\mathit{fit}(-\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))
for all
x\in L
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
satisfies the assumptions in the claim. We check that
F\in\mathcal{F}
implies
F{\setminus}^{fit}\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\in\mathcal{F}
for all families
x_{i}\in L
. If
F\in\mathcal{F}
, by our assumption,
\mathit{fit}(F\cap\mathfrak{c}(x_{i}))\in\mathcal{F}
for every
i\in I
. By Lemma
2.4
, this means that
F{\setminus}^{fit}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\in\mathcal{F}
. By
3
of Lemma
1.2
,
F{\setminus}^{fit}\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})=\bigvee_{i}F{\setminus}^{fit}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})
, and this is in
\mathcal{F}
as we assumed this collection is closed under all joins. Conversely, if
\mathcal{F}
is a subcolocale, it is closed under all joins by definition, and by definition also
F{\setminus}^{\mathit{fit}}\mathfrak{o}(x)\in\mathcal{F}
for all
x\in L
. By Lemma
2.4
, then,
\mathit{fit}(F\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))\in\mathcal{F}
for all
x\in L
.
∎

Finally, we want to show that for every codense subcolocale
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
the inclusion
\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
is a subcolocale containing all opens. We also look at how the conuclei of these interact.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
be a codense subcolocale. For every
S\in\mathsf{S}(L)
and
x\in L
,

1.

\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(S\cap\mathfrak{o}(x))=\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(S)\cap\mathfrak{o}(x)
;

2.

\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(S\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))=\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(S)\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)
.

Proof.

The claims holds because
\mathfrak{o}(x),\mathfrak{c}(x)\in\mathcal{D}
by item
1
of Lemma
2.2
, and
\mathcal{D}
is stable under
-\cap\mathfrak{o}(x)
and
-\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)
by Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For a sublocale
S\subseteq L
,
\mathit{fit}(S\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))=\mathit{fit}(\mathit{fit}(S)\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))
for all
x\in L
.

Proof.

The following are equivalent statements for all
y\in L
. At each step we only use basic properties of fitting and of open and closed sublocales.

\mathit{fit}(S\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)

S\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)

S\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)\vee\mathfrak{o}(x)

\mathit{fit}(S)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)\vee\mathfrak{o}(x)

\mathit{fit}(S)\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)
.

Indeed, then,
\mathit{fit}(S\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))=\mathit{fit}(\mathit{fit}(S)\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))
as desired.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
be a codense subcolocale.

1.

The collection
\mathcal{F}:=\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
is a subcolocale containing all opens;

2.

\nu_{\mathcal{F}}(F)=\mathit{fit}(\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(F))
for all
F\in\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
;

3.

\bigwedge^{\mathcal{F}}F_{i}=\mathit{fit}(\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\bigcap_{i}F_{i})
for
D_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Let us prove the three items in turn.

1.

We use the characterization in Proposition
2.5
. Closure of
\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
under all joins follows from the fact that fitting is a closure operator on
\mathsf{S}(L)
, and so the map
\mathit{fit}:\mathsf{S}(L)\to\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
to its fixpoints preserves all joins. Next, we have to show that for
F\in\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]
the element
\mathit{fit}(F\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))
is in
\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]
, by Proposition
2.5
. This holds because for
D\in\mathcal{D}
such that
F=\mathit{fit}(D)
we have
D\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)\in\mathcal{D}
, by Proposition
2.1
and
\mathit{fit}(D\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))=\mathit{fit}(\mathit{fit}(D)\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))
by Lemma
2.7
. Then,
\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
is a subcolocale. Since
\mathfrak{o}(x)\in\mathcal{D}
for all
x\in L
, by
1
of Lemma
2.2
,
\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]
contains all opens.

2.

The following are equivalent statements. Again, for each derivation we are only using basic facts about fitting and the conucleus
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}
.

\mathit{fit}(\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(F))\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)

\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(F)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)

\mathit{fit}(D)\subseteq F
implies
\mathit{fit}(D)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)
for all
D\in\mathcal{D}

D\subseteq F
implies
D\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)
for all
D\in\mathcal{D}

\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathit{fit}(F))\subseteq\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{o}(x))

\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathit{fit}(F))\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)
.

3.

This follows from (2) and from Lemma
1.4
.∎

We have found a (clearly monotone) map
\mathit{fit}[-]:\mathcal{CD}(\mathsf{S}(L))\to\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L))
from codense subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)
to subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
containing all opens. We want to show a concrete example of the assignment
\mathit{fit}[-]:\mathcal{CD}(\mathsf{S}(L))\to\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L))
not being injective in general.

Lemma 2.9
.

For a frame
L
,
\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)]=\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)]
.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)
by Lemma
1.12
,
\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)]\subseteq\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)]
. Let us show the reverse inclusion. By Lemma
1.6
, it suffices to show that
\mathit{ker}[\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)]\subseteq\mathit{ker}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)]
, and since
\mathit{ker}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)]=\mathsf{Filt}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)
, by Lemma
1.7
, it suffices to show that if
E\in\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)
then
\mathit{ker}(E)
is an exact filter. Let
\bigwedge x_{i}\in L
be an exact meet. If
E\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})
, then we have
E\cap\mathfrak{c}(x_{i})\subseteq\{1\}
. By the characterization of exact sublocales in Lemma
1.8
, this implies that
E\cap\mathfrak{c}(\bigwedge_{i}x_{i})\subseteq\{1\}
, that is,
E\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(\bigwedge_{i}x_{i})
, and so, indeed,
\bigwedge_{i}x_{i}\in\mathit{ker}(E)
.
∎

By Lemma
2.9
, above, then, to show that
\mathit{fit}[-]
is not injective, it suffices to find a frame
L
where
\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)\nsubseteq\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
. Complete sublocales, i.e. sublocales such that their surjection preserves all meets, in particular are exact (Proposition 7.14 in
[
22
]
). Then, complete sublocales which are not in
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
are witnesses of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)\nsubseteq\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
. Example 5.12 in
[
9
]
presents a concrete example of this. The following class of examples of complete sublocales which are not in
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
is due to Igor Arrieta, who we thank.

Example 2.1
.

For every frame
L
, there is a frame surjection
\varepsilon:\mathcal{D}(L)\to L
defined as
\varepsilon(D)=\bigvee D
. We claim that if
L
is completely distributive and is not
T_{D}
-spatial, then the sublocale
\varepsilon_{*}[L]\subseteq\mathcal{D}(L)
associated with this surjection is exact but not in
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
. An example of such a completely distributive lattice is given, for example, by the interval
[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, which has no covered primes. By complete distributivity of
L
, the surjection
\varepsilon:\mathcal{D}(L)\to L
preserves all meets, and so it corresponds to an exact sublocale. Let us show that this sublocale is not in
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
. We note that
\mathcal{D}(L)
is
T_{D}
-spatial: the covered primes are exactly the elements of the form
L{\setminus}{\uparrow}x
for some
x\in L
, and these meet-generate
\mathcal{D}(L)
. Then, by Lemma
1.10
, every sublocale in
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
is
T_{D}
-spatial. As
L
is not
T_{D}
-spatial, by assumption, the sublocale corresponding
\varepsilon_{*}[L]\subseteq\mathcal{D}(L)
cannot be in
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
.

2.2
From proper subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
to subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)

In this section, we restrict to a particular class of subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
and define for their collection a left order adjoint to (the suitable corestriction of) the monotone map
\mathit{fit}[-]:\mathcal{CD}(\mathsf{S}(L))\to\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L))
. We say that a subcolocale
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
is
proper
if it contains all open sublocales and the join
\bigvee_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})
is exact for each family
x_{i}\in L
.

Lemma 2.10
.

If
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is a codense subcolocale, then
\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
is proper.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.8
,
\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
is a subcolocale containing all opens. Let us show that for all
x_{i}\in L
and
F\in\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
:

\mathfrak{o}(\bigvee_{i}x_{i})\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}F\subseteq\bigvee_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}F.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}F\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)
for all
i\in I
. Then, by item
2
of Proposition
2.8
,
\mathit{fit}(\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\cap F))\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)
, and by Lemma
2.6
this implies
\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\cap\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(F)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)
. This also means
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(F)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)\vee\mathfrak{c}(x_{i})
for all
i\in I
, that is,
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(F)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)\vee\mathfrak{c}(\bigvee_{i}x_{i})
. Thus,
\mathfrak{o}(\bigvee_{i}x_{i})\cap\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(F)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)
, and this implies
\mathit{fit}(\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{o}(\bigvee_{i}x_{i})\cap F))\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)
, where we have used
2.6
again. As the left-hand side is
\mathfrak{o}(\bigvee_{i}x_{i})\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}F
, by item
3
of Proposition
2.8
, our claim is proven.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

The following are all proper subcolocales.

1.

\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
for every frame
L
;

2.

\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)]\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
for every frame
L
;

3.

\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{sp}(L)]\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
for every spatial frame
L
.

Proof.

We prove each item using
2.8
.
For the first, we just note
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)=\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}(L)]
. The inclusion
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is codense by Lemma
1.9
. Finally, for a spatial frame
L
, the inclusion
\mathsf{S}_{sp}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is codense by Lemma
1.11
.
∎

For every subcolocale
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
containing all opens, for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
we define the relation
\leq^{\mathcal{F}}_{F}\subseteq L\times L
as:

\leq^{\mathcal{F}}_{F}=\{(x,y)\in L\times L\mid F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)\}.

Should
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
be clear from the context, we will sometimes abbreviate this as
\leq_{F}
.

Proposition 2.12
.

A subcolocale
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
containing all opens is proper if and only if for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
the relation
\leq^{\mathcal{F}}_{F}
is a frame precongruence.

Proof.

The only condition which is not shown with routine computations is stability under arbitrary joins. If
x_{i}\leq_{F}y
for
F\in\mathcal{F}
, then
\bigvee^{\mathcal{F}}_{i}(F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i}))\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y_{i})
. The desired result follows by exactness of the join
\bigvee^{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})=\mathfrak{o}(\bigvee_{i}x_{i})
. For the converse, suppose that there is a subcolocale
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
containing all opens, which is not proper. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
x_{i}\in L
with
F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(\bigvee_{i}x_{i})\nsubseteq\bigvee^{\mathcal{F}}_{i}(F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i}))
. So, there is
y\in L
with
F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)
for all
i\in I
but
F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(\bigvee_{i}x_{i})\nsubseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)
. The first set inclusion means
x_{i}\leq_{F}y
for each
i\in I
, and the second means
\bigvee_{i}x_{i}\nleq_{F}y
. Then, the relation
\leq_{F}
is not stable under joins, and so it is not a frame precongruence.
∎

To our ends, the following characterization of proper subcolocales will be more useful.

Corollary 2.13
.

A subcolocale
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
containing all opens is proper if and only if for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
there is
\sigma(F)\in\mathsf{S}(L)
such that

\displaystyle\sigma(F)\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\vee\mathfrak{o}(y)\text{ if and only if }x\leq_{F}y,

(1)

which is necessarily unique. Equivalently,
\sigma(F)
is such that

\displaystyle\mathit{fit}(\sigma(F)\cap\mathfrak{o}(x))=F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x)\text{ for each $x\in L$. }

(2)

Proof.

The first claim follows from
2.12
, and by the correspondence between precongruences and sublocales. To see that the second condition is equivalent to the first, we note that the first condition amounts to having
\sigma(F)\cap\mathfrak{o}(x)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)
if and only if
x\leq_{F}y
. In turn, this is equivalent to having
\sigma(F)\cap\mathfrak{o}(x)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)
if and only if
F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(y)
.
∎

For each proper subcolocale
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
we may then define a map

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\to\mathsf{S}(L)

\displaystyle F\mapsto\bigcap\{\mathfrak{o}(x)\vee\mathfrak{c}(y)\mid x,y\in L,x\leq_{F}y\}.

When
\mathcal{F}
is clear from the context, we simply call this map
\sigma
.

Let us see a few basic facts about this map.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
be a proper subcolocale. Then, for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
x\in L
:

1.

\mathit{fit}(\sigma(F))=F
;

2.

\sigma(\mathfrak{o}(x))=\mathfrak{o}(x)
;

3.

\sigma(F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x))=\sigma(F)\cap\mathfrak{o}(x)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
is a proper codense subcolocale.

1.

By Corollary
2.13
,
\mathit{fit}(\sigma(F))=\mathit{fit}(\sigma(F)\cap\mathfrak{o}(1))=F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(1)=F
.

2.

We use Corollary
2.13
. We have to check
\mathit{fit}(\mathfrak{o}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y))=\mathfrak{o}(x)\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(y)
for all
y\in L
. Indeed,
\mathit{fit}(\mathfrak{o}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y))=\mathfrak{o}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y)=\mathfrak{o}(x\wedge y)
, and this equals
\mathfrak{o}(x)\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(y)
as
\mathcal{F}
contains all open sublocales.

3.

We use Corollary
2.13
again. We have to show that

\mathit{fit}(\sigma(F)\cap\mathfrak{o}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y))=F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x)\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(y)

(1)

for all
y\in L
. By the characterization of
\sigma(F)
from
2.13
.

\mathit{fit}(\sigma(F)\cap\mathfrak{o}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y))=\mathit{fit}(\sigma(F)\cap\mathfrak{o}(x\wedge y))=F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x\wedge y),

(2)

As
\mathcal{F}
contains all open sublocales,

F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x\wedge y)=F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y)=F\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x)\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(y).

(3)

By combining
2
and
3
, we obtain
1
as desired.∎

We call
\Delta(\mathcal{F})
the subcolocale
\mathcal{S}(\sigma[\mathcal{F}])
. For a complete lattice
C
and a collection
X\subseteq C
we call
\mathcal{J}(X)
the closure of
X
under arbitrary joins. If
C
is a coframe, we call
\mathcal{S}(X)
the smallest subcolocale containing
X
.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
be any subset. Then
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X})
is

\mathcal{J}(\{X\cap\mathfrak{o}(a)\cap\mathfrak{c}(b)\mid a,b\in L,X\in\mathcal{X}\}).

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
, any subcolocale
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
containing
\mathcal{X}
must also contain the collection in the claim. To show the desired result, then, it suffices to show that this is a subcolocale. We use the characterization in
2.1
. Closure under joins is clear, and stability under
-\cap\mathfrak{o}(x)
and
-\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)
follows from linearity of open and closed sublocales.
∎

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
be a proper subcolocale. Then

\Delta(\mathcal{F})=\{\bigvee_{i}\sigma(F_{i})\cap\mathfrak{c}(x_{i}):F_{i}\in\mathcal{F},x_{i}\in L\}.

Proof.

By item
3
of Lemma
2.14
,
\sigma(F)\cap\mathfrak{o}(x)\in\sigma[\mathcal{F}]
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
x\in L
. Then, by Lemma
2.15
,
\mathcal{S}(\sigma[\mathcal{F}])
is as desired.
∎

Proposition 2.17
.

If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
is a proper subcolocale then
\mathit{fit}[\Delta(\mathcal{F})]=\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

The inclusion
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathit{fit}[\Delta(\mathcal{F})]
holds by item
1
of Lemma
2.14
. Let us show the reverse inclusion. We notice that the map
\mathit{fit}:\Delta(\mathcal{F})\to\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
preserves all joins, as joins in
\Delta(\mathcal{F})
are computed as in
\mathsf{S}(L)
. Then, it suffices to prove the claim for basic elements
\sigma(G)\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)
. We now claim that
\mathit{fit}(\sigma(G)\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))\in\mathcal{F}
. Note that, by Lemma
2.7
,

\mathit{fit}(\sigma(G)\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))=\mathit{fit}(\mathit{fit}(\sigma(G))\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)),

and
\mathit{fit}(\sigma(G))=G
by item
1
of Lemma
2.14
. We have then shown

\mathit{fit}(\sigma(G)\cap\mathfrak{c}(x))=\mathit{fit}(G\cap\mathfrak{c}(x)).

By Proposition
2.5
, this is in
\mathcal{F}
.
∎

Corollary 2.18
.

A subcolocale
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
is proper if and only if it is of the form
\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]
for some codense subcolocale
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{F}
is proper then
\mathit{fit}[\Delta(\mathcal{F})]=\mathcal{F}
by Proposition
2.17
. If
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is codense,
\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]
is proper by Lemma
2.10
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Note that one could define
\sigma(F)=\bigcap\{\mathfrak{c}(x)\vee\mathfrak{o}(y)\mid x\leq_{F}y\}
even when
\mathcal{F}
is not proper, but in that case
\sigma(F)\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\vee\mathfrak{o}(y)
does not necessarily imply
x\leq_{F}y
. Item
1
of Lemma
2.14
, stating
\mathit{fit}(\sigma(F))=F
for
F\in\mathcal{F}
, relies on this direction of the implication. This is why one cannot extend the definition of the adjoint
\Delta
to non-proper subcolocales using this more general definition of
\sigma
. If
F
is not proper, we cannot prove in a similar way that
\mathit{fit}(\sigma(F))=F
, and so our proof of the
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathit{fit}[\Delta(\mathcal{F})]
half of the adjointness condition does not go through.

2.3
Essential subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)

We look at what subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)
are
\Delta(\mathcal{F})
for some proper
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
. For a codense subcolocale
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
we call an element
saturated
if it is of the form
\bigwedge^{\mathcal{D}}_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})=\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i}))
for some family
x_{i}\in L
. We call
\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D})
their ordered collection, and note that
\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
is a subcoframe inclusion. Let us define a codense subcolocale
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
to be
essential
if it is
\mathcal{S}(\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D}))
.

Lemma 2.19
.

For a codense subcolocale
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)

\mathcal{S}(\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D}))=\mathcal{J}(\{F\cap\mathfrak{c}(z)\mid F\in\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D}),z\in L\}).

Proof.

This follows from the characterization in
2.15
.
∎

We recall that in a category an
essential extension
is a monomorphism
j:A\to B
such that whenever
m\circ f:A\to C
is a monomorphism
m
, too, is a monomorphism. An essential extension
n:L\to N
is
maximal
if for every essential extension
m:L\to M
there is a unique morphism
f:M\to N
with
f\circ m=n
. Essential extensions for frames are studied in
[
4
]
. We now justify the terminology and show that a subcolocale
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is essential if and only if
\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
is an essential extension in
\mathbf{CoFrm}
.

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
L
be a frame and
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
a codense subcolocale. Then,
\mathcal{D}
is essential if and only if
\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
is essential in
\mathbf{CoFrm}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is an essential subcolocale. Suppose, now, that there is a coframe map
f:\mathcal{D}\to C
such that it is injective when restricted to
\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D})
. The elements of the form
\mathfrak{o}(x)\vee\mathfrak{c}(y)
meet-generate
\mathcal{D}
, by Lemma
2.2
, and as it is essential the elements of the form
F\cap\mathfrak{c}(z)
with
F\in\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D})
join-generate it, by Lemma
2.19
. Then, to show injectivity it suffices to show that
F\cap\mathfrak{c}(z)\nsubseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)\vee\mathfrak{c}(y)
implies that
f(F\cap\mathfrak{c}(z))\nleq f(\mathfrak{o}(x)\vee\mathfrak{c}(y))
. Our assumption means that
F\cap\mathfrak{o}(y)\nsubseteq\mathfrak{o}(x\vee z)
. By assumption on
f
, we have that
f(F\cap\mathfrak{o}(y))\nleq f(\mathfrak{o}(x\vee z))
, and as
f
is a coframe map this also implies that
f(F)\wedge f(\mathfrak{o}(y))\nleq f(\mathfrak{o}(x))\vee f(\mathfrak{o}(z))
. As
f
also preserves complements, this implies
f(F)\wedge f(\mathfrak{c}(z))\nleq f(\mathfrak{o}(x))\vee f(\mathfrak{c}(y))
, and again as
f
preserves the lattice operations,
f(F\cap\mathfrak{c}(z))\nleq f(\mathfrak{o}(x)\vee\mathfrak{c}(y))
as desired. If
\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
is essential in
\mathbf{CoFrm}
, consider the coframe quotient given by the subcolocale
\mathcal{S}(\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D}))
. This is clearly injective when restricted to
\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D})
, and by essentiality it is also injective on all of
\mathcal{D}
. But then it is a bijective coframe map, hence an isomorphism, so
\mathcal{S}(\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D}))=\mathcal{D}
.
∎

We show some concrete examples of essential subcolocales.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
L
be a frame. Then
\mathfrak{b}(p)=\mathfrak{c}(p)\cap\mathit{fit}(\mathfrak{b}(p))
for every
p\in\mathsf{pt}(L)
.

Proof.

We show
\mathfrak{c}(p)\cap\mathit{fit}(\mathfrak{b}(p))\subseteq\mathfrak{b}(p)
. Suppose, then, that
x\in\mathfrak{c}(p)\cap\mathit{fit}(\mathfrak{b}(p))
and
x\neq 1
. Since
x\in\mathfrak{c}(p)
,
p\leq x
. Since
x\in\mathit{fit}(\mathfrak{b}(p))
, whenever
p\in\mathfrak{o}(y)
then
x\in\mathfrak{o}(y)
. As
p
is prime,
x\to p=p
if and only if
x\nleq p
, and so our condition means
y\nleq p
implies
y\to x=x
. As
x\neq 1
,
x\to x\neq x
, and so
x\leq p
. Then
x=p\in\mathfrak{b}(p)
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 2.22
.

For a frame
L
, the following are essential subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}(L)
.

1.

The subcolocale
\mathsf{S}_{sp}(L)
of spatial sublocales;

2.

The subcolocale
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
of joins of complemented sublocales.

Proof.

For the first item, we recall that
\mathsf{S}_{sp}(L)
is join-generated by
\{\mathfrak{b}(p)\mid p\in\mathsf{pt}(L)\}
. Then, it suffices to show that every
\mathfrak{b}(p)
is
F\cap\mathfrak{c}(p)
for some
F\in\mathsf{Sat}(\mathsf{S}_{sp}(L))
. We let
\nu_{sp}:\mathsf{S}(L)\to\mathsf{S}(L)
be the conucleus associated with
\mathsf{S}_{sp}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
. By
2.6
, and using Lemma
2.21
,

\nu_{sp}(\mathit{fit}(\mathfrak{b}(p))\cap\mathfrak{c}(p)=\nu_{sp}(\mathit{fit}(\mathfrak{b}(p))\cap\mathfrak{c}(p))=\mathfrak{b}(p).

By definition of saturated element,
\nu_{sp}(\mathit{fit}(\mathfrak{b}(p)))\in\mathsf{Sat}(\mathsf{S}_{sp}(L))
, and so the desired claim holds. For the second item, we note that all elements of
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
are joins of elements of the form
\mathfrak{o}(x)\cap\mathfrak{c}(y)
, and
\mathfrak{o}(x)\in\mathsf{Sat}(\mathsf{S}_{b}(L))
for every
x\in L
.
∎

We now want to characterize essential sublocales as those codense subcolocales of the form
\Delta(\mathcal{F})
for some proper subcolocale
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
.

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
be a codense sublocale. Then,
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathit{fit}(D))=\sigma(\mathit{fit}(D))
for all
D\in\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Let
D\in\mathcal{D}
. We use the characterization in
2.13
. We have to show

\mathit{fit}(\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathit{fit}(D))\cap\mathfrak{o}(x))=\mathit{fit}(D)\wedge^{\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{o}(x)

for all
x\in L
. By Lemma
2.6
, the left-hand side is
\mathit{fit}(\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathit{fit}(D)\cap\mathfrak{o}(x)))
. By item
3
of Proposition
2.8
, this equals the right-hand side.
∎

Lemma 2.24
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
be a codense subcolocale.

1.

The maps
\sigma:\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]\leftrightarrows\mathcal{D}:\mathit{fit}
are order adjoints, with
\mathit{fit}\dashv\sigma
;

2.

The map
\sigma:\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]\to\mathcal{D}
is a subcoframe embedding;

3.

\sigma[\mathcal{F}]=\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
be a dense subcolocale.

1.

For every
D\in\mathcal{D}
and
F\in\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]
, the following are equivalent.

\mathit{fit}(D)\subseteq F

D\subseteq F

D\subseteq\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(F)
.

As
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(F)=\sigma(F)
for all
F\in\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]
, by Lemma
2.23
, the result follows.

2.

As we have just shown,
\sigma
is a right adjoint, and so it preserves all meets. For binary joins, it suffices to show it preserves joins of the form
\mathfrak{o}(x)\vee\mathfrak{o}(y)
, but indeed
\sigma(\mathfrak{o}(x\vee y))=\mathfrak{o}(x\vee y)
by item
2
of Lemma
2.14
. Injectivity of
\sigma
follows from its left adjoint being surjective.

3.

By item
2
, of
2.14
,
\sigma(\mathfrak{o}(x))=\mathfrak{o}(x)
for all
x\in L
. Additionally, we have just shown that
\sigma:\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]\to\mathcal{D}
is a coframe map. Hence, for all collections
x_{i}\in L
,
\sigma(\bigwedge^{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i}))
is
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i}))
, and so it is saturated. Since this holds for all families
x_{i}\in L
, all saturated elements of
\mathcal{D}
are of this form.∎

Lemma 2.25
.

For every codense subcolocale
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
, and every proper subcolocale
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
,
\Delta(\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
if and only if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]
.

Proof.

If
\Delta(\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
, then
\mathit{fit}[\Delta(\mathcal{F})]\subseteq\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]
, but the left-hand side is
\mathcal{F}
, by Proposition
2.17
. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]
. By definition of
\Delta(\mathcal{F})
, it suffices to show
\sigma(F)\in\mathcal{D}
, for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
. By our assumption, every such
F
is
\mathit{fit}(D)
for some
D\in\mathit{fit}(D)
, and, as
\sigma(\mathit{fit}(D))=\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathit{fit}(D))
by Lemma
2.23
, this is indeed in
\mathcal{D}
.
∎

Proposition 2.26
.

Let
L
be a frame. A codense subcolocale
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is essential if and only if
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\Delta(\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}])
.

Proof.

By definition,
\Delta(\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}])=\mathcal{S}(\sigma[\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]])
. By Lemma
2.24
,
\sigma[\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]]=\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D})
. Then, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{D}
is essential if and only if
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{S}(\mathsf{Sat}(\mathcal{D}))
, but this just the definition of essentiality.
∎

We have obtained the main theorem. We call
\mathcal{CD}_{ess}(\mathsf{S}(L))
the collection of essential codense subcolocales, and
\mathcal{PC}(\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L))
the collection of proper subcolocales of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
.

Theorem 2.27
.

There is an order adjunction
\Delta:\mathcal{PC}(\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L))\leftrightarrows\mathcal{CD}(\mathsf{S}(L)):\mathit{fit}[-]
with
\Delta\dashv\mathit{fit}[-]
, and which maximally restricts to an isomorphism
\mathcal{PC}(\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L))\cong\mathcal{CD}_{ess}(\mathsf{S}(L))
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemmas
2.25
and
2.17
.
∎

We want to use the characterization in
2.26
to provide an example of a subcolocale of
\mathsf{S}(L)
which is not essential.

Example 2.3
.

As
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
is essential, by Proposition
2.22
,
\Delta(\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)])=\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
by Proposition
2.26
. By Lemma
2.9
,
\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)]=\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)]
. Thus, if for some frame we had
\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)\nsubseteq\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
, this would imply
\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)\nsubseteq\Delta(\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)])
, giving the desired counterexample as
\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)
would not be essential by Proposition
2.26
. For sublocales witnessing
\mathsf{S}_{\mathcal{E}}(L)\nsubseteq\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
, once again we refer to Example 5.12 in
[
9
]
and Example
2.1
.

3
The categories
\mathbf{Raney}
and
\mathbf{SZDBF}

3.1
Objects

We say that a strictly zero-dimensional biframe
(L,\mathcal{D})
is
essential
if
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathsf{S}(L)
is an essential subcolocale. We say that a Raney extension
(L,\mathcal{F})
is
proper
if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
is a proper subcolocale. We will use the results of the previous section to establish a bijection between proper Raney extensions and essential strictly zero-dimensional biframes. For a proper Raney extension
(L,\mathcal{F})
and for a strictly zero-dimensional biframe
(M,\mathcal{D})
we define:

\displaystyle\Delta(L,\mathcal{F})=(L,\Delta(\mathcal{F})),

\displaystyle\mathit{fit}(M,\mathcal{D})=(M,\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]).

Theorem
2.27
, then, amounts to the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

The assignments
\mathit{fit}
and
\Delta
are mutually inverse bijections between proper Raney extensions and essential strictly zero-dimensional biframes.

3.2
Morphisms

The assignment
\mathit{fit}
can be easily extended to morphisms. By Lemma
2.24
, there is a subcoframe inclusion
\sigma:\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]\to\mathcal{D}
for every codense subcolocale
\mathcal{D}
. Then, every morphism
f:(L,\mathcal{D})\to(M,\mathcal{E})
determines a coframe map
\mathit{fit}(f):\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{D}]\to\mathit{fit}[\mathcal{E}]
, which further restricts to the open sublocales to yield a frame map isomorphic to
f:L\to M
.

Proposition 3.2
.

There is a functor
\mathit{fit}:\mathbf{SZDBF}\to\mathbf{Raney}
, whose essential image consists of the proper Raney extensions.

On the other hand, the assignment
(L,\mathcal{F})\mapsto\Delta(L,\mathcal{F})
cannot be extended to morphisms in a similar fashion. We will show that there are Raney morphisms

f:(L,\mathcal{F})\to(M,\mathcal{G})

such that the frame map
f:L\to M
does not lift to a map

f:(L,\Delta(\mathcal{F}))\to(M,\Delta(\mathcal{G})).

Lemma 3.3
.

\Delta(L,\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)])=(L,\mathsf{S}_{b}(L))
for all frames
L
.

Proof.

The strictly zero-dimensional biframe
(L,\mathsf{S}_{b}(L))
is essential, by Proposition
2.22
. By Proposition
2.26
, then,
\Delta(\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)])=\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

For a frame
L
, and a sublocale
S\subseteq L
:

1.

S
is smooth if and only if it lifts to a map
f:(L,\mathsf{S}_{b}(L))\to(S,\mathsf{S}_{b}(S))
of strictly zero-dimensional biframes.

2.

S
is exact if and only if it lifts to a map
f:(L,\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)])\to(S,\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)])
of Raney extensions.

Proof.

We prove the two items in turn.

1.

This follows immediately from both Lemma 3.39 in
[
14
]
and Corollary 4.2 of
[
1
]
.

2.

In
[
12
]
it is proven that there is an isomorphism
\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)]\cong\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{c}}(L)^{op}
. This is also an isomorphism
(L,\mathit{fit}[\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)])\cong(L,\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{c}}(L)^{op})
of Raney extensions. In Proposition 6.6 of
[
22
]
the frame morphisms
f:L\to M
that lift to Raney extensions are characterized as the exact maps. Finally, in Proposition 7.14 of
[
22
]
it is shown that surjections that preserve all exact meets are exact maps. ∎

We are ready to give the desired counterexample.

Example 3.1
.

Suppose there is a frame
L
with a sublocale

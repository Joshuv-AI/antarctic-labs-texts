Diagonalization of the Toda flow on conjugacy classes of matrices with simple spectrum
David Martínez Torres, Carlos Tomei

Abstract
. We construct coordinates on conjugacy classes of traceless complex matrices with simple spectrum that diagonalize the non-periodic Toda vector field. By this we mean that the coordinates, defined on an open and dense neighborhood of any diagonal matrix in the conjugacy class, decouple the Toda vector field into a sum of multiples of the Euler and rotational vector field in ℂ \mathbb{C} . Using Lie theoretic methods, we extend this construction from 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ℂ \mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}} to arbitrary complex semisimple Lie algebras and to their real forms.



1.
Introduction

The (non periodic) Toda vector field was introduced in
[
19
]
as the Hamiltonian vector field of the total energy of a physical system: a chain of
n
particles in the line connected by
n-1
springs which exert an exponential restoring force. Flascha’s change of variables
[
6
]
turned it into a vector field in Lax form on Jacobi matrices, thus making methods from matrix theory
available. One can make sense of the Lax pair expression found by Flaschka in larger matrix spaces, and, therefore, define the Toda vector field in more generality.
Our point of departure is the set of traceless complex matrices, in which the Toda vector field is given by

(1)

X^{\prime}=[X,\pi_{\mathfrak{k}}X],\quad\mathrm{I}=\pi_{\mathfrak{k}}+\pi_{\mathfrak{u}},\quad X\in\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}},

where the identity map
\mathrm{I}
on
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}
is decomposed as the sum of projections
onto traceless skew-Hermitian matrices
\mathfrak{k}
, and traceless upper triangular matrices with real diagonal entries
\mathfrak{u}
.
Because the expression (
1
) uses fundamental vector fields of the action
of
\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}
on
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}
by conjugation, the Toda vector field is tangent to all conjugacy classes of matrices in
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}
. In particular, the flow is isospectral:
X(t)
and
X(0)
have the same spectrum.

Moser’s norming constants
[
14
]
provided a global parametrization of Jacobi matrices that greatly simplified the expression of Toda vector field: Once the spectrum is fixed, it is transformed in
the radial projection of a diagonal linear vector field onto the positive sector of the
(n-1)
-sphere. Larger matrix domains for the vector field lead naturally to the search for analogous coordinates.

Building on ideas developed in
[
10
,
13
,
11
]
, we construct such coordinates on dense domains of any conjugacy class with simple spectrum in the matrix algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}
(Jacobi matrices have simple spectrum).
As it turns out, our results are valid not just for
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}
, but for arbitrary complex semisimple Lie algebras.

We briefly recall how to define the Toda vector field and the counterparts of conjugacy classes of matrices with simple spectrum in this more general Lie algebra setting. Equation (
1
) is tied to the Gram-Schmidt factorization in
\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}
associated with the standard Hermitian inner product

\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathrm{K}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{N}.

Here, the factor
\mathrm{K}
is the special unitary group,

\mathrm{K}=\{k\in\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,kk^{*}=\mathrm{I}\},

\mathrm{A}
is the group of determinant 1 diagonal matrices with positive real entries and
\mathrm{N}
is the group of upper triangular matrices with diagonal entries equal to 1,

\mathrm{A}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&&&0\\
&&\cdots&\\
0&&&a_{n}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{R}},\,a_{i}>0\right\},\,\mathrm{N}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&n_{12}&&n_{1n}\\
0&1&&n_{2n}\\
&&\cdots&\\
0&&&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}\right\}.

The product
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{A}\mathrm{N}
is the group of upper triangular
matrices with positive diagonal coefficients. The Lie algebras of the factors yield the direct sum decomposition

\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{u}

on which formula (
1
) relies.
The Gram-Schmidt algorithm provides an example of an Iwasawa factorization of a complex
semisimple
Lie group
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{K}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{N}
. In such a factorization the group
\mathrm{K}
is a maximally compact subgroup of
\mathrm{G}
, and it
comes as the fixed-point set of an anti-holomorphic group involution. The Lie group

\mathrm{A}
integrates a maximal abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}
of the -1 eigenspace of the differential of
the involution
at the identity element. The subgroup
\mathrm{N}
integrates the sum of positive root spaces
\mathfrak{n}
, for a chosen root ordering
of the roots of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
; we denote the sum of negative root spaces by
\mathfrak{l}
.

Once an Iwasawa factorization of
\mathrm{G}
has been fixed, the ensuing Iwasawa decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
allows to define the Toda vector
field on
\mathfrak{g}
by the very same formula (
1
). All Iwasawa factorizations of
\mathrm{G}
are conjugate, so the qualitative behavior of all these vector fields is the same. The Toda vector field on
\mathfrak{g}
is tangent to all the orbits of the adjoint action of
\mathrm{G}
on
\mathfrak{g}
, in particular to the regular semisimple adjoint orbits. These are the
adjoint orbits of maximal dimension that meet the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}=i\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
, and they generalize
conjugacy classes of matrices in
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}
with simple spectrum.

Our main result is that the Toda vector field on a regular semisimple adjoint orbit is obtained by juxtaposing diagonal linear vector fields defined on covering charts.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be a regular semisimple adjoint orbit. Then around any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}\cap\mathfrak{h}
there exist local coordinates with image
\mathfrak{l}\times\mathfrak{l}

that transform the Toda vector field in the (complete) diagonal linear vector field.

(2)

(Y,Z)\in\mathfrak{l}\times\mathfrak{l},\quad(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime})=([Y,\pi_{i\mathfrak{a}}\Lambda],[Z,-\pi_{\mathfrak{a}}\Lambda]),\quad\mathrm{I}|_{\mathfrak{h}}=\pi_{i\mathfrak{a}}+\pi_{\mathfrak{a}}.

Moreover, the domain of each local coordinate is a dense subset of
\mathcal{O}
, and
the union of these subsets over the finite set
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathfrak{h}
covers
\mathcal{O}
.

The elements in
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathfrak{h}
are parametrized by an appropriate Weyl group (see Section
4
).
The two factors in
\mathfrak{l}\times\mathfrak{l}

relate to the Toda vector field

X^{\prime}=[X,\pi_{\mathfrak{k}}X]=-[X,\pi_{\mathfrak{u}}X]

being tangent to both adjoint
\mathrm{K}
orbits and adjoint
\mathrm{U}
orbits.

It is instructive to spell out Theorem
1
for
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}
. For each diagonal matrix
\Lambda\in\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}

with simple spectrum, we have coordinates given by strictly lower triangular matrices
Y,Z

that linearize the Toda vector field in a dense open subset of the conjugacy class of
\Lambda
,

Y^{\prime}=[Y,i\Im\Lambda],\quad Z^{\prime}=[Z,-\Re\Lambda].

Here,
\Im\Lambda
and
\Re\Lambda
are the imaginary and real parts of the matrix
\Lambda
.
Thus, phase and amplitude evolution of
X
split in the variables
Y
and
Z
, respectively,
and the evolution occurs separately on each entry of
Y
and
Z
– the coordinates diagonalize the vector field.

If the diagonal matrix
\Lambda
has real entries/eigenvalues,
then the Toda vector field is tangent to the submanifold
\mathcal{O}_{0}=\mathcal{O}\cap\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{R}}
consisting
of real matrices conjugated to
\Lambda
. Denote the strictly lower triangular real matrices by
\mathfrak{l}_{0}=\mathfrak{l}\cap\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then the restriction of the linear coordinates
Y,Z
to

\mathfrak{l}_{0}\times\mathfrak{l}_{0}

are diagonalizing coordinates around
\Lambda
for the restriction of the Toda vector field to
\mathcal{O}_{0}
,

(Y,Z)\in\mathfrak{l}_{0}\times\mathfrak{l}_{0},\quad(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime})=(0,[Z,-\Lambda]).

These coordinates on
\mathcal{O}_{0}
coincide with the ones described in
[
11
]
, where they were constructed without using complex matrices.

We are led to the following problem: To look for other conditions
on the simple spectrum of a diagonal matrix
\Lambda
,
so that we can find a subgroup
\mathrm{G}_{0}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}
such that the Toda vector field is tangent to the

\mathrm{G}_{0}
conjugacy class of
\Lambda
, and the coordinates from Theorem
1
restrict to linearizing coordinates on the
\mathrm{G}_{0}
conjugacy
class (possibly after a suitable modification).

This problem is best analyzed in the setting of real forms of any complex semisimple Lie group
\mathrm{G}
.
Let
\mathrm{G}_{0}
be the real form of

\mathrm{G}
given as the fixed-point set of the anti-holomorphic
group involution
\tau
.
Under an alignment condition for
\tau

and a given Iwasawa factorization
of
\mathrm{G}
, the groups
\mathrm{G}
and
\mathrm{G}_{0}
have compatible factorizations
(see Section
4
for details). In particular, the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
defined by the Iwasawa decomposition has to be invariant by
\tau
and with fixed-point set a maximally non-compact Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{0}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
of
\mathrm{G}_{0}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
and
\mathrm{G}_{0}
be a complex semisimple Lie group and a real form with compatible Iwasawa factorizations.
Let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be a regular semisimple adjoint orbit with non-empty intersection with the
maximally non-compact Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, and let
\mathcal{O}_{0}
be the
\mathrm{G}_{0}
orbit of one such intersection point.
Then the Toda vector field on
\mathfrak{g}
has the following properties.

(i)

It
is tangent to
\mathcal{O}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{0}
.

(ii)

Around any point in
\mathcal{O}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{0}
there exist coordinates on
\mathcal{O}_{0}
that transform the Toda vector field in a linear vector field.

(iii)

The domain of these coordinates is an open dense subset of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
, and the union of these domains over the finitely many points in
\mathcal{O}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{0}
covers
\mathcal{O}_{0}
.

The linearizing coordinates appearing in Theorem
2
are not just the restriction of the coordinates
from Theorem
1
to a suitable (real) subalgebra of
\mathfrak{l}\times\mathfrak{l}
. This happens if
\mathrm{G}_{0}
is a split real form of
\mathrm{G}
. For instance, this is the case
for
\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{R}}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}
. In general, the restriction of the second coordinate set
Z
in (
2
)
has to be modified. The elements in
\mathcal{O}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{0}
are parametrized by a suitable Weyl group.

Theorem
2
for
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}
has indeed a reformulation in terms of constraints on the spectrum of
\Lambda
. These constraints are refinements of the natural requirement that the spectrum be preserved or reversed under complex conjugation. More precisely, recall that, as well as
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{R}}
, the other (conjugacy classes of) non-compact real forms of
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}

are the quaternion Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{H}}
—block
2\times 2
complex matrices with each block corresponding to a quaternion— and the special unitary Lie algebras
\mathfrak{su}(p,q)
—the isotropy Lie algebras of the the pseudo-Hermitian forms
-z_{1}\bar{z}_{1}-\cdots-z_{p}\bar{z}_{p}+z_{p+1}\bar{z}_{p+1}+\cdots+z_{p+q}\bar{z}_{p+q}
,
p\geq q

[
8
, p. 444-445]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Lambda\in\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}
be a diagonal matrix with simple spectrum that satisfies
either of the following constraints:

(i)

\Lambda\in\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{R}}
;

(ii)

\Lambda\in\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{H}}
, i.e.,
\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&0&&&\\
0&\overline{\lambda}_{1}&&&\\
&&\cdots&&\\
&&&\lambda_{n}&0\\
&&&0&\overline{\lambda}_{n}\end{pmatrix}
;

(iii)

\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda_{q}&0&0\\
0&\Im_{p-q}&0\\
0&0&-\overline{\Lambda}_{q}\end{pmatrix}
,
where
\Lambda_{q}
and
\Im_{p-q}
are diagonal matrices of size
q
and
p-q

of complex and purely imaginary complex numbers, respectively.

Then the Toda vector field on
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}
is tangent
to the
\mathrm{G}_{0}
conjugacy class of
\Lambda
, and
there exists an atlas of the conjugacy class that transforms the Toda vector field
in a finite collection of linear vector fields, where
the real form
\mathrm{G}_{0}
that corresponds respectively to each constraint is

(i)

\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{R}}
;

(ii)

\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{H}}
;

(iii)

c\mathrm{SU}(p,q)c^{-1}
,
where

c=g\begin{pmatrix}I_{p-q}&0&0\\
0&\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathrm{I}_{q}&\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathrm{I}^{\mathrm{op}}\\
0&-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathrm{I}^{\mathrm{op}}&\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathrm{I}_{q}\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathrm{I}^{\mathrm{op}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\cdots&1\\
&\cdots&\\
1&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix},

and
g
is the permutation matrix given by

(1,2,3,\ldots,p+q)\mapsto(1+q,2+q,3+q,\cdots,p,1,2,3,\cdots,q)\ .

Finally, we collect some of the cases of Theorems (
1
) and (
3
) in a result that reflects the title of the paper.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a conjugacy class of diagonalizable matrices in

\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{K}},\,\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\quad\mathfrak{so}_{\mathbb{C}},\quad\mathfrak{sp}_{\mathbb{C}}

whose spectrum is simple and belongs to
\mathbb{K}
. Then
there exists an atlas of
\mathcal{O}
that transforms the Toda vector field
in a finite collection of diagonal linear vector fields.

The Hamiltonian genealogy of the original Toda vector field naturally led to the study of its integrability (in the sense of Liouville).
The standard symplectic structure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
, associated with the dynamics of
n
particles in the line, yields a symplectic structure on
\mathcal{J}
, the set of traceless Jacobi matrices, after getting rid of the simple dynamics of the center of mass, by the change of variable suggested by Flaschka
[
6
]
.
The eigenvalues along an integral curve
J(t)\in\mathcal{J}
are preserved, and Poisson commute: they provide the required conserved quantities to yield the complete integrability of the system on
\mathcal{J}
.

Larger phase spaces for the Toda flow raised new integrability issues.
Adler
[
1
]
showed that
\mathcal{J}
is a coadjoint orbit of the group of upper triangular matrices. The integrability on regular orbits in
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{R}}
(generic, real symmetric matrices with simple spectrum) was proved in
[
3
]
. Subsequently,
the symmetry requirement was dropped
[
4
]
and
the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{R}}
was replaced by an arbitrary real semisimple Lie algebra (for the symmetric case)
[
7
]
. For the Toda vector field in
\mathcal{J}
, Agrotis et al.
[
2
]
proved another form of integrability: More conserved quantities are obtained at the expense of less commutativity of its Hamiltonians.

On the other hand, conserved quantities should be naturally derived from the fact that the Toda vector field is tangent to upper triangular and orthogonal orbits by conjugation (from Remark
5
, this intersection collects the integral curves of the entire Toda hierarchy). This more Lie algebraic approach does not account for the symplectic geometry, in particular the possible commutativity of the Hamiltonians. In exchange it provides the local coordinates which, as in Theorem
1
, diagonalize the vector field.

2.
The case of traceless complex matrices

In this section we
provide a global description of the Toda flow on conjugacy classes of matrices in

\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}
with simple spectrum.

Our methods
are not the usual ones from matrix theory, but rather pertain to Lie theory. We do this in order to prepare the reader for the more general arguments in the coming sections, valid for
arbitrary complex semisimple Lie algebras.

2.1.
Factorizations and coordinates on conjugacy classes

For any conjugacy class of matrices in
\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}

with simple spectrum, we construct suitable local coordinates centered at a diagonal matrix. To do that we combine properties of the
\mathrm{Q}\mathrm{R}
and the
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{U}

factorization of matrices in
\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}
. We use uppercase letters for Lie algebra elements and lowercase letters for
Lie group elements.

The Gram-Schmidt algorithm factors a matrix in
\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}

into a special unitary matrix, a diagonal matrix with strictly positive entries, and an
upper triangular matrix with diagonal entries equal to 1,

\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathrm{K}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{N}.

Multiplying
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{A}\mathrm{N}
we obtain the classical
\mathrm{QR}
factorization

\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathrm{K}\mathrm{U},

where
\mathrm{U}
stands for triangular matrices with strictly positive diagonal entries. Let
\mathrm{L}
be the group of lower triangular matrices with diagonal entries equal to 1.

As a preliminary step, we discuss from a geometric viewpoint certain subsets of matrices with relevant factorization properties.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the subset of unitary matrices in
\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}
that possess an
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{U}
factorization,

\mathcal{C}=\{k\in\mathrm{K}\,|\,\exists l\in\mathrm{L},u\in\mathrm{U},\,k=lu\}.

Clearly, any unitary matrix belongs to
\mathcal{C}
exactly when all its principal
minors are positive. Since the conditions to produce an
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{U}
and a
\mathrm{U}\mathrm{L}
factorization
are the same, the subset
\mathcal{C}
is invariant under inversion. There is a more useful characterization of
\mathcal{C}
: It is the image of the restriction
to
\mathrm{L}
of the first projection in the
\mathrm{QR}
factorization
\kappa:\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathrm{K}
,

\kappa:\mathrm{L}{\longrightarrow}{\mathcal{C}}.

From a geometric viewpoint,
\mathcal{C}
is an embedded submanifold of
\mathrm{K}
and the projection is a real analytic diffeomorphism.

A similar analysis applies to
the restriction to
\mathrm{L}
of the second projection in the
\mathrm{QR}
factorization

\nu:\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathrm{U}
.
Define

(3)

\mathcal{D}=\nu(\mathrm{L})=\{u\in U\,|\,\exists l\in\mathrm{L},k\in\mathrm{K},\,l=ku\}.

Lemma 1
.

The subset
\mathcal{D}
is a closed embedded submanifold of
\mathrm{U}
and

\nu:\mathrm{L}\to\mathcal{D}\subset\mathrm{U}

is a real analytic diffeomorphism onto its image.

Proof.

The result of applying the (Cartan) involution

\theta:\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}},\quad g\mapsto{(g^{*})}^{-1}

to the Gram-Schmidt factorization produces the
opposite Gram-Schmidt factorization

\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathrm{K}\mathrm{A}{\mathrm{L}}
. Switching the last
two factors gives yet another factorization
\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathrm{K}\mathrm{L}\mathrm{A}
. Denote by
\varpi:\mathrm{U}\to\mathrm{A}
the restriction to
\mathrm{U}
of the third projection of the latter factorization.
It follows from (
3
) that
\mathcal{D}=\varpi^{-1}(e)
, where
e
is the identity element. We claim that
e
is a regular value
for
\varpi
: If
\varpi(u)=e
, then

u=kl,\quad k\in\mathrm{K},l\in\mathrm{L}.

Since for any
a\in\mathrm{A}
we have

ua\subset\mathrm{U},\quad\varpi(ua)=a,

the differential of
\varpi
at
u
is surjective:
\mathcal{D}
is a closed embedded submanifold of
\mathrm{U}
.

Equation (
3
) implies that the composition of the restriction
\nu:\mathrm{L}\to\mathrm{U}
with the projection

\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathrm{K}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{L}\to\mathrm{L}
equals the identity on
\mathrm{L}
.
Therefore
\nu:\mathrm{L}\to\mathrm{U}

is an injective immersion onto its image
\mathcal{D}
. Since
\mathcal{D}
is an
embedded submanifold, this implies
that
\nu
is real analytic diffeomorphism from
\mathrm{L}
to
\mathcal{D}
.

\blacksquare

Remark 1
.

The description of
\mathcal{D}
as the fiber
\varpi^{-1}(e)
provides the following alternative characterization:
it is the subset of matrices
u\in\mathrm{U}
such that all principal minors of
{(u^{*}u)}^{-1}
equal 1.
However, our conceptual viewpoint does not pursue characterizing solutions of certain factorization problems in terms of matrix features (e.g., the behavior of minors). It focusses on the
existence of those solution sets together with some of their key geometric properties.
This viewpoint lends itself to Lie theoretic generalizations.

We now use the three submanifolds in Remark
2
to parametrize open dense subsets of a conjugacy class
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}
of
matrices with simple spectrum. To do that we contextualize diagonal matrices in a Lie theoretic setting.

Let
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the maximal abelian subalgebra of diagonal matrices

\mathfrak{h}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&&&0\\
&&\cdots&\\
0&&&\lambda_{n}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{\mathbb{C}}\right\}.

Let
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}
be a diagonal matrix, i.e.,
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}\cap\mathfrak{h}
. That

\Lambda
have simple spectrum is equivalent to the centralizer

\mathrm{Z}(\Lambda)=\{g\in\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,g\Lambda g^{-1}=\Lambda\}

being equal to the maximally abelian subgroup

\mathrm{H}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}
of diagonal matrices (the group whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{h}
).

In the rest of the paper, an invertible matrix/group element as superscript denotes conjugation by that matrix/group element,
X^{g}=gXg^{-1}
. Also,
\mathfrak{l}
denotes the strictly lower triangular matrices (the Lie algebra of
\mathrm{L}
).

Lemma 2
.

The maps

(4)

\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\times\mathrm{L}\to\mathcal{O},\quad(k,l)\mapsto\Lambda^{(lk)^{-1}},\quad\Phi_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}\times\mathrm{L}\to\mathcal{O},\quad(u,l)\mapsto\Lambda^{ul^{-1}},

have the following properties.

(i)

They are diffeomorphisms onto the same open dense subset
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{O}
;

(ii)

They produce rulings of
\mathcal{U}
by

\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
conjugates of the affine subspace

(5)

\Lambda+\mathfrak{l}=\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}(\{e\}\times\mathrm{L})=\Phi_{\mathcal{D}}(\{e\}\times\mathrm{L}).

A ruling is a fibration of a manifold by affine spaces.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{T}\mathrm{A}
, where
\mathrm{T}
is a diagonal of phases, and
\mathrm{A}
is positive diagonal.
The product map

\mathcal{C}\times\mathrm{T}\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{T}\subset\mathrm{K}

is a diffeomorphism
onto a dense subset of
\mathrm{K}
(its complement being those unitary matrices with at least one trivial principal minor).
Therefore

\mathcal{C}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{L}\mathrm{A}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{\mathbb{C}}\,(=\mathrm{K}\mathrm{L}\mathrm{A})

is a dense open subset. As the compact torus
\mathrm{T}
normalizes
\mathrm{L}
, this open dense subset admits the presentation

\mathcal{C}\mathrm{L}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{A}=\mathcal{C}\mathrm{L}\mathrm{H}.

Standard properties of actions of groups on manifolds imply that

\mathcal{U}=\{\Lambda^{g},\,|\,g\in\mathcal{C}\mathrm{L}\mathrm{H}\}

is an open dense neighborhood of
\Lambda
in
\mathcal{O}
.
Because
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{Z}(\Lambda)
and
\mathcal{C}
is invariant under
inversion, the subset
\mathcal{U}
equals the image of the map
\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}
in (
4
).
Moreover, the factorization
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{L}\mathrm{H}
as the product of the (embedded) submanifold
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{L}
and the centralizer of
\Lambda
implies that
\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}
is a diffeomorphism onto
\mathcal{U}
. This proves (i) for
\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}
.

To prove (i) for
\Phi_{\mathcal{D}}
we factor it through
\Phi_{\mathcal{C}}
.
The proof of Lemma
1
(see also (
2

Remark 10
.

We have presented a relative version of the linearization result in which
the real reductive Lie group with finite center
\mathrm{G}_{0}
is a real form of a complex semisimple Lie group.
Our methods can be adapted to show that, for any real semisimple Lie group with a fixed Iwasawa factorization, the ensuing
Toda vector field linearizes on maximally non-compact regular semisimple orbits.
Note that this is not the same result proved in
[
13
]
, which addressed the diagonalization of the Toda vector field on hyperbolic
\mathrm{K}_{0}
orbits of maximal dimension (Lie theoretic generalizations of conjugacy classes of real symmetric matrices with simple spectrum). Recall that a semisimple element of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is hyperbolic if all its eigenvalues for its adjoint action on
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
are real. Equivalently, the element is conjugated to one in
\mathfrak{a}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{h}_{0}
. Note that
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
contains regular elements if and only if
\mathfrak{m}_{0}

is abelian (for instance, if the real semisimple Lie algebra is split or is a complex Lie algebra). Therefore, the elements addressed in
[
13
]
are not regular in general (besides not working with their full adjoint orbit but with its intersection with
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
).

satisfied conditions (c)-(e)

\mathfrak{h}=i\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
is invariant by

\mathfrak{h}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}^{\tau}

,

. As a result
\theta

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{k}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{0},\quad\mathfrak{k}_{0}=\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0},\,\mathfrak{p}_{0}=\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0},

and the choice of

and the induced root ordering defines an Iwasawa factorization for
\mathrm{G}_{0}=\mathrm{K}_{0}\mathrm{A}_{0}\mathrm{N}_{0}
.
Since

for any

The following lemma obtains real forms with compatible Iwasawa factorizations.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a complex semisimple Lie group with a fixed Iwasawa factorization. Given any real form of
\mathrm{G}_{0}
,
there exists a real form
\mathrm{G}_{0}^{\prime}
conjugated to
\mathrm{G}_{0}
such that
\mathrm{G}
and
\mathrm{G}_{0}^{\prime}
have compatible
Iwasawa factorizations.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary Iwasawa factorization
\mathrm{G}_{0}=\mathrm{K}_{0}\mathrm{A}_{0}\mathrm{N}_{0}
.
We first promote it to an Iwasawa factorization of
\mathrm{G}
.
The Cartan decomposition
\theta:\mathrm{G}_{0}\to\mathrm{G}_{0}
has a anti-holomorphic extension to
\mathfrak{g}
, and there
it defines a Cartan decomposition of
\mathrm{G}
,

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}.

To obtain from
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
a maximal abelian subalgebra of
\mathfrak{p}
,
define the centralizer of
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
in
\mathfrak{k}_{0}

\mathfrak{m}_{0}=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{k}_{0}}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})

and take a maximal abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
of
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
. It follows that
\mathfrak{t}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{0}
is a Cartan subalgebra
of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. It also follows that
i\mathfrak{t}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{0}
is a maximal abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathfrak{p}
. According to

[
9
, Chapter VI, Section 6]

there exists a root ordering of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
such that for a root
\alpha
with
\alpha|_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}}\neq 0

one has that
\alpha
is positive if and only if the restricted root
\alpha|_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}}
is positive.
The outcome is an Iwasawa factorization
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{K}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{N}
that
is compatible with the fixed Iwasawa factorization
\mathrm{G}_{0}=\mathrm{K}_{0}\mathrm{A}_{0}\mathrm{N}_{0}
.

Finally, there exist an element
g\in\mathrm{G}
such that the conjugation of
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{K}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{N}
by
g
produces the given
Iwasawa factorization of
\mathrm{G}

[
9
, Chapter VI, Section 6]
. Therefore
\mathrm{G}
and its real form
g\mathrm{G}_{0}g^{-1}

\blacksquare

5.1.
Chevalley big cells

We describe the relationship among various Chevalley big cells associated
with
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{K}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{N}
and
\mathrm{G}_{0}=\mathrm{K}_{0}\mathrm{A}_{0}\mathrm{N}_{0}
, a complex semisimple Lie group and a real form with compatible Iwasawa factorizations. This is used to define the induced
(Y,Z)
variables on
\mathcal{O}_{0}
in Theorem
2
.

The Chevalley big cell of
\mathrm{G}_{0}
sits inside the Chevalley big cell of
\mathrm{G}
,

\mathcal{C}_{0}\subset\mathcal{C}.

In general,
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is not a dense open subset of
\mathrm{K}_{0}\cap\mathcal{C}
. We describe a canonical enlargement of
\mathcal{C}_{0}
to an open dense subset of
\mathrm{K}_{0}\cap\mathcal{C}
. This enlargement makes use of the Chevalley big cell of the complexification of the (possibly non connected) subgroup

\mathrm{M}_{0}=Z_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})
, which integrates the subalgebra
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
whose elements are the eigenvectors in
\mathfrak{k}_{0}

associated with the eigenvalue 0 for the adjoint action.

The complexification of
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
is

\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{l}_{\Im}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{\Im}\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{0}\oplus i\mathfrak{t}_{0},

where
\mathfrak{n}_{\Im}
and
\mathfrak{l}_{\Im}
denote the sum of positive and negative imaginary root spaces of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
, respectively.
The compact group
\mathrm{M}_{0}
admits an abstract complexification
\mathrm{M}
. From the properties of the complexification functor,
the inclusion
\mathrm{M}_{0}\subset\mathrm{K}
induces a holomorphic injective map from
\mathrm{M}
into
\mathrm{G}
. Its
image is a closed subgroup that we identify with
\mathrm{M}
and that integrates
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover, the Cartan decomposition on
\mathrm{G}
induces a Cartan decomposition on
\mathrm{M}
, which at the Lie algebra level is

\mathfrak{m}_{0}=\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{m},\quad i\mathfrak{m}_{0}=\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathfrak{m}.

Since
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
,
i\mathfrak{t}_{0}
is a maximal abelian subalgebra of
i\mathfrak{m}_{0}
.
The roots of
(\mathfrak{m},i\mathfrak{t}_{0})
are nothing but the imaginary roots of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
, and they come with an ordering from
that of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
. Therefore we have an Iwasawa factorization for
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{M}_{0}\exp(i\mathfrak{t}_{0})\mathrm{N}_{\Im}
, where
\mathrm{N}_{\Im}\subset\mathrm{G}
is the connected integration of
\mathfrak{n}_{\Im}
, and its corresponding Chevalley big cell

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}=\kappa_{\mathrm{M}}(\mathrm{L}_{\Im}),\quad\kappa_{\mathrm{M}}:\mathrm{M}\to\mathrm{M}_{0},

where
\mathrm{L}_{\Im}\subset\mathrm{G}
is the connected integrations of
\mathfrak{n}_{\Im},\mathfrak{l}_{\Im}
. We denote by
\mathrm{T}_{0}=Z_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}(i\mathfrak{t}_{0})
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{K}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{N}
and

\mathrm{G}_{0}=\mathrm{K}_{0}\mathrm{A}_{0}\mathrm{N}_{0}
be compatible Iwasawa factorizations, and let
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{M}_{0}\exp(i\mathfrak{t}_{0})\mathrm{N}_{\Im}
be the
induced Iwasawa factorization of
\mathrm{M}
. Then

(i)

the product map
\mathcal{C}_{0}\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}\to\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}
is a diffeomorphism onto its image, a subset of
\mathrm{K}_{0}\cap\mathcal{C}
. More precisely,

\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}=\kappa(\mathrm{L}_{0}\mathrm{L}_{\Im})\subset\mathcal{C},\quad\kappa:\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{K};

(ii)

the subset

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathrm{T}_{0}\mathrm{L}_{0}\mathrm{A}_{0}\subset\mathrm{G}_{0}

is open and dense and equals
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathrm{L}_{0}Z_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})
.

Proof.

As for an element in
\mathrm{G}_{0}
its Iwasawa factorizations in
\mathrm{G}_{0}
and
\mathrm{G}
agree,

\mathcal{C}_{0}=\kappa(\mathrm{L}_{0})\subset\mathcal{C},\quad\kappa:\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{K}.

Since

\mathrm{M}_{0}\subset\mathrm{K},\quad\exp(i\mathfrak{t}_{0})\subset\mathrm{A},\quad\mathrm{N}_{\Im}\subset\mathrm{N},

the Iwasawa factorizations in
\mathrm{M}
and
\mathrm{G}
also agree. Therefore

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}=\kappa(\mathrm{L}_{\Im})\subset\mathcal{C}.

Furthermore, if
l_{1}\in\mathrm{L}_{0}
,
l_{2}\in\mathrm{L}_{\Im}
have Iwasawa factorizations

l_{1}=k_{1}u_{1},\quad l_{2}=mu_{2}

in
\mathrm{G}_{0}
and
\mathrm{M}
, respectively, then,
since
\mathrm{M}_{0}
normalizes
\mathrm{U}_{0}
, their product has Iwasawa factorization in
\mathrm{G}

l_{1}l_{2}=k_{1}u_{1}mu_{2}=k_{1}mu_{3}u_{2}=k_{1}mu,

and this proves (i).

We claim that the multiplication map
\mathcal{C}_{0}\times\mathrm{M}_{0}\to\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathrm{M}_{0}
is a diffeomorphism onto its image and that

\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathrm{M}_{0}\subset\mathrm{K}_{0},\quad\mathrm{M}_{0}\mathcal{C}_{0}\subset\mathrm{K}_{0}

are open and dense subsets. For
\mathrm{G}_{0}
semisimple this
is proved in
[
13
, Section 2]
. In general,
\mathrm{G}_{0}
may be not connected, but is a reductive subgroup. Therefore, by Propositions 7.33 and 7.49 in
[
9
]
,

\mathrm{M}_{0}
intersects every connected component of
\mathrm{K}_{0}
, and thus the result also holds in this generality. This proves the claim.

We apply the previous result also to the reductive group
\mathrm{M}
to conclude that

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}\mathrm{T}_{0}\subset\mathrm{M}_{0}

is open and dense. Therefore

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}\mathrm{T}_{0}\mathcal{C}_{0}\subset\mathrm{K}_{0}

is an open and dense subset, and so
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}\mathrm{T}_{0}\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathrm{L}_{0}\mathrm{A}_{0}\subset\mathrm{G}_{0}
also is. We also claim that
\mathrm{T}_{0}
normalizes
\mathcal{C}_{0}
. From this,

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}\mathrm{T}_{0}\mathcal{C}_{0}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathrm{T}_{0},

and the equality

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathrm{T}_{0}\mathrm{L}_{0}\mathrm{A}_{0}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathrm{L}_{0}Z_{\mathrm{G}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0}).

is a consequence of

\mathrm{T}_{0}\mathrm{A}_{0}=\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}(i\mathfrak{t}_{0})\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})=\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}(\mathfrak{t}_{0})\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})=\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0}),

and this proves (ii). Note that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}\mathcal{C}_{0}\subset\mathrm{K}_{0}\cap\mathcal{C}
is open and dense.

To prove the claim
we use that
\mathrm{T}_{0}\subset\mathrm{K}_{0}
and that it normalizes
\mathrm{L}_{0}
and
\mathrm{U}_{0}
, since
\mathrm{T}_{0}\subset\mathrm{M}_{0}
. Therefore,
if
l\in\mathrm{L}_{0}
has Iwasawa factorization
in
\mathrm{G}_{0}
,
l=ku
,
and then
tlt^{-1}\in\mathrm{L}_{0}
with Iwasawa factorization

tlt^{-1}=(tkt^{-1})(tut^{-1})
.

\blacksquare

5.2.
Weyl groups

We discuss the relationship among several Weyl groups associated with the data of compatible Iwasawa factorizations.
This is used to parametrize the elements in
\mathcal{O}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{0}
in Theorem
2
.

To the complex Lie group with fixed Cartan subalgebra
(\mathrm{G},\mathfrak{h})
one associates the Weyl group of
(\mathrm{G},\mathfrak{h})

\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mathfrak{h})/\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mathfrak{h}).

If we regard
\mathrm{G}
as a real Lie group, then to the fixed Iwasawa factorization one associates the Weyl group of
(\mathrm{G},\mathfrak{a})
,

\mathrm{W}=\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathfrak{a})/\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathfrak{a}).

These two quotient groups are canonically isomorphic,

\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mathfrak{h})/\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mathfrak{h})\to\mathrm{W}
.

To the real form
\mathrm{G}_{0}
with fixed Iwasawa factorization one can associate both

•

the canonically isomorphic quotient groups

\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{G}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})/\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})\to\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})/\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0}),

•

and the Weyl group of
(\mathrm{G}_{0},\mathfrak{a}_{0})

\mathrm{W}_{0}=\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})/\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{a}_{0}).

The isomorphic quotient groups sit in Weyl group
\mathrm{W}
.

Lemma 8
.

The inclusion
\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})\subset\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathfrak{a})

induces the injection

\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})/\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})\to\mathrm{W}.

Proof.

If
k\in\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})
, then it normalizes
\mathfrak{h}
and thus it also normalizes its vector part
\mathfrak{a}
. This explains the inclusion of normalizers in the statement. For centralizers the argument is simpler, producing a well defined map between quotients. The image of
k
in
\mathrm{W}
is trivial if and only if

k\in\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathfrak{a})
. This implies that
k\in\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathfrak{h})
, and, because
k\in\mathrm{K}_{0}
, that

k\in\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})
. Therefore the map is injective.

\blacksquare

We relate the two quotient groups for
\mathrm{G}_{0}
by means of the Weyl group of
(\mathrm{M}_{0},i\mathfrak{t}_{0})
,

\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}=\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}(i\mathfrak{t}_{0})/\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}(i\mathfrak{t}_{0}).

Lemma 9
.

The inclusion
\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})\to\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})
induces the short exact sequence

1\to\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}\to\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})/\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})\to\mathrm{W}_{0}\to 1.

Proof.

The inclusion between normalizers is again a consequence of the invariance of toroidal and vector components.
To show that the map induced on quotients is onto we use that
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathrm{M}_{0}=Z_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})
.
If
k\in\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})
is such that
\mathfrak{t}_{0}\neq\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{k}
, then since all Cartan subalgebras of a compact Lie algebra are conjugated, we can always find
m\in\mathrm{M}_{0}
such that

\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{gm}=\mathfrak{t}_{0}
.

By construction, the kernel of the map induced on quotients are those elements in
\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})/\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})
any of whose representatives
k\in\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})
has fixed-point set containing
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
. But this means that
k\in\mathrm{M}_{0}
as well,

k\in\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{K}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})\cap\mathrm{M}_{0}=\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}(i\mathfrak{t}_{0}).

Therefore the kernel equals

\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}(i\mathfrak{t}_{0})/\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}(i\mathfrak{t}_{0})=\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{M}_{0}}
.

\blacksquare

Letter to the editor regarding “Electrophysiological monitoring of the trigeminal nerve sensory root using sensory-masseter response for microvascular decompression in trigeminal neuralgia”
Authors: Muhammad Riaz, Fabio Grassia
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the recent article by Jiang et al., in which the authors describe intraoperative electrophysiological monitoring of the trigeminal sensory root using the sensory-masseter response during microvascular decompression for trigeminal neuralgia [3]. This study addresses one of the most persistent challenges in trigeminal neuralgia surgery which is the absence of a validated, real-time intraoperative endpoint to confirm adequate decompression.

Unlike microvascular decompression for hemifacial spasm, where disappearance of the lateral spread response serves as a well-established electrophysiological endpoint correlated with favorable outcomes, trigeminal neuralgia has historically lacked an equivalent intraoperative marker [2]. The authors' application of the sensory-masseter response to fill this gap is therefore conceptually compelling and represents a meaningful methodological contribution. Their demonstration that sensory-masseter response changes correlate with intraoperative decompression events is of clinical relevance, particularly given the anatomical variability of neurovascular conflict in trigeminal neuralgia.

However, several points merit consideration. First, the relationship between intraoperative sensory-masseter response normalization and long-term pain relief remains incompletely characterized. Sustained pain relief in trigeminal neuralgia likely depends on mechanisms beyond acute nerve decompression, including demyelination reversal and central sensitization changes, which may not be captured by real-time electrophysiological signals [1]. Second, the technical feasibility and reproducibility of sensory-masseter response acquisition across centers with varying neurophysiology expertise requires prospective validation; a standardized acquisition protocol with defined threshold parameters is essential before broader adoption. Third, it would be informative to understand how sensory-masseter response performance compares with or complements existing trigeminal nerve monitoring approaches, including blink reflex and brainstem auditory evoked potential monitoring, which have shown variable but promising utility in this context [5]. Finally, the extent to which sensory-masseter response monitoring influenced intraoperative decision-making, for instance, prompting additional decompression, and whether this translated into outcome differences, deserves explicit reporting in future work [4].

In conclusion, the sensory-masseter response represents a promising candidate intraoperative monitoring tool for microvascular decompression in trigeminal neuralgia that could help bridge the gap left by the absence of a lateral spread response equivalent in this condition. Multicenter prospective studies with standardized protocols and long-term outcome correlation are now warranted to define its role in routine surgical practice.

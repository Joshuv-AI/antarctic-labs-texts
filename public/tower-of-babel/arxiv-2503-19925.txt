Accurate, provable, and fast nonlinear tomographic reconstruction: A variational inequality approach


Abstract
We consider the problem of signal reconstruction for computed tomography (CT) under a nonlinear forward model that accounts for exponential signal attenuation, a polychromatic X-ray source, general measurement noise (e.g. Poisson shot noise), and observations acquired over multiple wavelength windows. We develop a simple iterative algorithm for single-material reconstruction, which we call EXACT (EXtragradient Algorithm for Computed Tomography), based on formulating our estimate as the fixed point of a monotone variational inequality. We prove guarantees on the statistical and computational performance of EXACT under practical assumptions on the measurement process. We also consider a recently introduced variant of this model with Gaussian measurements, and present sample and iteration complexity bounds for EXACT that improve upon those of existing algorithms. We apply our EXACT algorithm to a CT phantom image recovery task and show that it often requires fewer X-ray projection exposures, lower source intensity, and less computation time to achieve similar reconstruction quality to existing methods.

Introduction and methodology

X-ray computed tomography (CT) is a core imaging modality in modern medicine, and is also widely used in diverse nonmedical applications ranging from security inspection to nondestructive testing. CT typically works by rotating an X-ray source and detector along a prespecified trajectory around the subject, and recording the total energy or number of photons that are transmitted to the detector at each angle along the tomographic trajectory. One then solves the inverse problem of reconstructing the underlying 2D or 3D image of the subject from these measurements. 1.1
Formal model and approach

As described above, we are interested in recovering an unknown signal from a nonlinear measurement model of the form (
3
). We assume throughout that our structured set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a closed, convex set.
Henceforth, we denote the ground truth signal by
x_{\star}
, and our task is to recover
x_{\star}\in X
from measurement vectors
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and the associated responses
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Formally, we rewrite the measurement model as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[y_{i}|a_{i}]=\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\exp\big(-\mu_{j}\langle a_{i},x_{\star}\rangle_{+}\big)\quad\text{for }i=1,\ldots,n.

(4)

The parameters
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}
and
\{s_{j},\mu_{j}\}_{j=1}^{W}
are known and satisfy

\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}>0,\quad s_{j}\in(0,1],\quad\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}=1,\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{j}>0\quad\text{for all }j=1,\ldots,W.

Besides the conditional expectation relation in Eq. (
4
), we do not place any assumptions on the conditional distribution
y_{i}|a_{i}
. This flexibility allows us to cover many practical noise models. A salient such example is the Poisson shot-noise model
(
Schmidt et al. 2023
)
, in which
I_{\omega,i}
in Eq. (
1
) is given by a Poisson random variable with intensity
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}_{\omega,i}
.
In our notation, we have

\displaystyle y_{i}\overset{\mathsf{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathsf{Poi}\bigg(\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\exp\big(-\mu_{j}\langle a_{i},x_{\star}\rangle_{+}\big)\bigg).

(5)

In the CT literature, there are other noise assumptions for this problem, including those that model pulse-pileup or additional noise induced due to instrumentation errors
(
Danielsson et al. 2021
;
Persson et al. 2020
)
.

For notational convenience in the rest of the paper, we define the function
h:\real\to\real
via

\displaystyle h(t):=\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\exp\big(-\mu_{j}t_{+}\big),\quad\text{for all }t\in\real,

(6)

where we recall that
t_{+}=\max(0,t)
denotes the ReLU operation.
Using this shorthand, our model is given by

{\mathbb{E}}[y_{i}|a_{i}]=\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}\cdot h(\langle a_{i},\,x_{*}\rangle).

Our approach is based on defining a vector-valued map
F:X\to\real^{d}
, given by

\displaystyle F(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[y_{i}-\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}\cdot h(\langle a_{i},\,x\rangle)\big]\cdot a_{i},\;\;\text{for all }x\in X.

(7)

By construction, the map
F
is continuous. Two crucial observations that will motivate our methodological development are:

(a) The
expectation
of
F
— call this operator
\overline{F}
— has a zero at
x_{\star}
, in that
\overline{F}(x_{\star})=0
; and

(b) The operator
F
is also
monotone
, in that for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\real^{d}
, we have

\langle F(x)-F(x^{\prime}),x-x^{\prime}\rangle\geq 0.

Indeed, a short calculation to verify this fact is provided below; for any
x,x^{\prime}\in\real^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle F(x)-F(x^{\prime}),x-x^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}\cdot\big[h(\langle a_{i},\,x^{\prime}\rangle)-h(\langle a_{i},\,x\rangle)\big]\cdot\big[\langle a_{i},\,x\rangle-\langle a_{i},\,x^{\prime}\rangle\big]\geq 0,

(8)

where the inequality follows since the function
h(\cdot)
is nonincreasing.

Given these observations, we now frame the problem as one of finding a fixed point of the variational inequality defined by the operator
F
; see the classical references
(
Kinderlehrer and Stampacchia 2000
;
Rockafellar and Wets 2009
)
for background on variational inequalities. We are interested in a point
\widehat{x}\in X
that satisfies

\displaystyle\langle F(\widehat{x}),\,x-\widehat{x}\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }x\in X.

(9)

With the goal of converging to
\widehat{x}
— which one should expect to be close to the zero
x_{\star}
of
\overline{F}
— we study the projected extragradient method
(
Korpelevich 1976
)
for this problem, which proceeds as follows: With an initialization
x_{1}\in X
, execute the steps

\displaystyle x_{t+0.5}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{X}\big(x_{t}-\gamma_{t}F(x_{t})\big),

(10a)

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{X}\big(x_{t}-\gamma_{t}F(x_{t+0.5})\big),\text{ for }t=1,2,\dots

(10b)

where
\gamma_{t}
is the step size at iterate
t
, and
\mathcal{P}_{X}:\real^{d}\to X
is the
\ell_{2}
-projection operator onto
X
. Ignoring the cost of these projection operations, the rest of iteration (
10
) can be implemented in linear time. Furthermore, owing to the convexity of
X
, the projection step can typically be implemented via an efficient algorithm. For example, the total variation projection that we employ in our experiments is very fast in practice.

Our methodology is inspired by algorithmic ideas that were proposed for other monotone single-index models
(
Gallant 1990
;
Kalai and Sastry 2009
;
Kakade et al. 2011
)
as well as the recent formalism of these methods using variational inequalities due to
Juditsky and Nemirovski 2019
;
Juditsky and Nemirovski 2020
.
We refer to this extragradient algorithm (
10
) applied to the specific CT forward model (
4
) as EXACT: EXtragradient Algorithm for Computed Tomography.

1.3
Contributions and comparison to related work

With this setup in hand, we are now in a position to describe our contributions and to compare them with prior work.

1.

Convergence guarantees for general CT model.
We provide a rigorous convergence analysis for EXACT (

Our results accommodate an arbitrary noise model, and we show that the eventual recovery error depends explicitly on a functional of this noise process. We emphasize that our results provide (to our knowledge) the first rigorous guarantees for signal reconstruction in the practical CT measurement model (

2.

Convergence guarantees for Gaussian model.
To further explore the statistical and algorithmic properties of our algorithm, we study convergence guarantees for EXACT with Gaussian measurements. Indeed,
Fridovich-Keil et al. 2024
introduced a monochromatic version of the stylized model (
3

in order to provide rigorous sample and iteration complexity guarantees on signal recovery.
They studied the gradient descent algorithm on the mean squared error objective
in the case with a single X-ray wavelength and noiseless measurements.
A follow-up paper by
Charisopoulos and Willett 2024
considered the same setting and proposed a different first-order method based on optimizing the nonsmooth
\ell_{1}

Our guarantees in this setting are easiest to describe and compare with prior work when there is no structure and no noise.
In the proportional regime, where

, we show that it takes EXACT (
Equation

10

t=\mathcal{O}\bigg(\exp(24\|x_{\star}\|_{2})\|x_{\star}\|_{2}^{4}\log\bigg(\frac{5\|x_{\star}\|_{2}}{\epsilon}\bigg)\bigg)\text{ iterations to achieve }\|x_{t}-x_{\star}\|\leq\epsilon.

On the other hand, in the regime
n\asymp\|x_{\star}\|_{2}^{4}d
, it takes EXACT

t=\mathcal{O}\bigg(\|x_{\star}\|_{2}^{4}\log\bigg(\frac{5\|x_{\star}\|_{2}}{\epsilon}\bigg)\bigg)\text{ iterations to achieve }\|x_{t}-x_{\star}\|\leq\epsilon.

This guarantee (see Theorem
3

\mathcal{O}(d)
—but this will result in increased computational time. Conversely, using more samples—approximately
\mathcal{O}(\|x_{\star}\|_{2}^{4}d)
—reduces computation time significantly.

Comparing with prior work in the monochromatic, noiseless setting with Gaussian measurements, we find that EXACT requires fewer samples and converges faster than the gradient descent method developed in

Assumption 2

2.2

In this section, we consider the model in which measurements are assumed to be Gaussian random vectors. The main attractions of this assumption are its analytical tractability and its facilitation of comparison to previous theoretical results

Assumption 3
.

The measurements are generated at random, according to
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\overset{\mathsf{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})

Recalling our notation for projection matrices, we first define a few quantities that are useful in the statement of our results. Define the geometric quantity

\displaystyle\overline{\omega}(x_{\star},X):={\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\sup_{v\in X}\frac{\langle P_{x_{\star}}^{\perp}v,g\rangle}{\|P_{x_{\star}}^{\perp}v\|_{2}}\bigg\},\quad\text{where }g\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d}).

(17)

Note that

has a natural interpretation as the Gaussian width of a certain set intersected with the unit shell.
Next, we define

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\frac{|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|^{2}\cdot\mathbbm{1}\{G>0\}}{\big[|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|+\gamma_{\star}\big]^{2}}\cdot\int_{-G/4}^{G/4}\frac{t^{2}e^{-t^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg\}=\frac{16\overline{\omega}(x_{\star},X)^{2}}{n}.

We prove in Appendix
A.2

is unique and strictly positive. We further define the scalar quantity

\displaystyle\rho(\|x_{\star}\|_{2}):=\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{W}\frac{s_{j}\mu_{j}}{(\mu_{j}\|x_{\star}\|_{2})^{4}\vee 1}\bigg)^{-1},

(19)

where we recall the notation

Note that
\rho(\|x_{\star}\|_{2})

\displaystyle\rho(\|x_{\star}\|_{2})=\mathcal{O}\big(\|x_{\star}\|_{2}^{4}\vee 1\big),

\{s_{j},\mu_{j}\}_{j=1}^{W}

4.1
Proof of Theorem
1

Recall that
F
is
\nu
-strongly pseudo-monotone around the ground truth
x_{\star}
, i.e.,

\langle F(x)-F(x_{\star}),x-x_{\star}\rangle\geq\nu\cdot\|x-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for all }x\in X,

and also Lipschitz on the set
X
, i.e.,

\|F(x)-F(x^{\prime})\|_{2}\leq L\cdot\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{2}\quad\text{for all }x,x^{\prime}\in X.

By the definition of substep (
10b
), we have

\displaystyle\|x_{t+1}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\|\mathcal{P}_{X}\big(x_{t}-\gamma_{t}F(x_{t+0.5})\big)-\mathcal{P}_{X}(x_{\star})\big\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{{\sf(i)}}{\leq}\big\langle x_{t+1}-x_{\star},x_{t}-\gamma_{t}F(x_{t+0.5})-x_{\star}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\langle x_{t+1}-x_{\star},x_{t}-x_{\star}\big\rangle-\gamma_{t}\big\langle F(x_{t+0.5}),x_{t+1}-x_{\star}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|x_{t+1}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|x_{t}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|x_{t+1}-x_{t}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma_{t}\big\langle F(x_{t+0.5}),x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\big\rangle-\gamma_{t}\big\langle F(x_{t+0.5}),x_{t+0.5}-x_{\star}\big\rangle

(25)

where in step
{\sf(i)}
we use
1
-co-coercivity of the projection operator onto a convex set, whereby

\|\mathcal{P}_{X}(x)-\mathcal{P}_{X}(x^{\prime})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\big\langle\mathcal{P}_{X}(x)-\mathcal{P}_{X}(x^{\prime}),x-x^{\prime}\big\rangle\quad\text{for all }x,x^{\prime}\in X.

Next, we bound the latter two inner product terms on the RHS. By strong monotonicity of
F
around
x_{\star}
, we have

\big\langle F(x_{t+0.5})-F(x_{\star}),x_{t+0.5}-x_{\star}\big\rangle\geq\nu\cdot\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2},

and rearranging terms yields

\displaystyle-\gamma_{t}\big\langle F(x_{t+0.5}),x_{t+0.5}-x_{\star}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\gamma_{t}\big\langle F(x_{\star}),x_{t+0.5}-x_{\star}\big\rangle-\nu\gamma_{t}\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{t}\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{\star}\|_{2}\cdot\mathsf{Err}(x_{\star},X)-\frac{1}{2}\nu\gamma_{t}\|x_{t}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}+\nu\gamma_{t}\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{t}\|_{2}^{2},

(26)

where in the last step we use

and recall the definition of

(
14

\|x_{t}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|x_{t}-x_{t+0.5}\|_{2}^{2}+2\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}.

We next turn to bound the inner product term
-\gamma_{t}\langle F(x_{t+0.5}),x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\rangle
. By using the first-order optimality conditions for the projection step used in
x_{t+0.5}=\mathcal{P}_{X}(x_{t}-\gamma_{t}F(x_{t}))
, we obtain

\langle x_{t+0.5}-x_{t}+\gamma_{t}F(x_{t}),x-x_{t+0.5}\rangle\geq 0\quad\text{for all }x\in X.

Substituting

above and rearranging the terms yields

\displaystyle-\gamma_{t}\langle F(x_{t}),x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle x_{t+0.5}-x_{t},x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|x_{t}-x_{t+1}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{t}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\|_{2}^{2}.

(27)

Proceeding now to the term of interest and performing some algebraic manipulation, we have

\displaystyle-\gamma_{t}\langle F(x_{t+0.5}),x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{t}\langle F(x_{t+0.5})-F(x_{t}),x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\rangle-\gamma_{t}\langle F(x_{t}),x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{{\sf(i)}}{\leq}\gamma_{t}\|F(x_{t+0.5})-F(x_{t})\|_{2}\cdot\|x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\|_{2}-\gamma_{t}\langle F(x_{t}),x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{{\sf(ii)}}{\leq}\gamma_{t}L\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{t}\|_{2}\cdot\|x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\|_{2}-\gamma_{t}\langle F(x_{t}),x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{{\sf(iii)}}{\leq}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}L^{2}}{2}\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{t}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\|_{2}^{2}-\gamma_{t}\langle F(x_{t}),x_{t+1}-x_{t+0.5}\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{{\sf(iv)}}{\leq}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}L^{2}}{2}\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{t}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|x_{t}-x_{t+1}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{t}\|_{2}^{2},

(28)

where step
{\sf(i)}
follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, step
{\sf(ii)}
from the fact that
F
is
L
-Lipschitz, step
{\sf(iii)}
from the AM-GM inequality, and step
{\sf(iv)}
from Eq. (
27
).

Substituting Ineqs. (
28
) and (
4.1
) into inequality (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|x_{t+1}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(1-\nu\gamma_{t}\big)\cdot\|x_{t}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}+\big(\gamma_{t}^{2}L^{2}+2\nu\gamma_{t}-1\big)\cdot\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{t}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\gamma_{t}\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{\star}\|_{2}\cdot\mathsf{Err}(x_{\star},X).

To bound the last term on the RHS, we apply the inequality
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
with
a=\gamma_{t}\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{\star}\|_{2}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\nu L}{2}}
and
b=\mathsf{Err}(x_{\star},X)\cdot\sqrt{\frac{2}{\nu L}}
to obtain

\displaystyle 2\gamma_{t}\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{\star}\|_{2}\cdot\mathsf{Err}(x_{\star},X)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}\nu L}{2}\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2}{\nu L}\cdot\mathsf{Err}(x_{\star},X)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{t}^{2}\nu L\Big(\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{t}\|_{2}^{2}+\|x_{t}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}\Big)+\frac{2}{\nu L}\cdot\mathsf{Err}(x_{\star},X)^{2}.

Combining with the other two terms, we have

\displaystyle\|x_{t+1}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(1-\nu\gamma_{t}+\nu L\gamma_{t}^{2}\big)\cdot\|x_{t}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\big(\gamma_{t}^{2}L^{2}+2\nu\gamma_{t}-1+\nu L\gamma_{t}^{2}\big)\cdot\|x_{t+0.5}-x_{t}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2\mathsf{Err}(x_{\star},X)^{2}}{\nu L}.

By setting

\displaystyle 1-\nu\gamma_{t}+\nu L\gamma_{t}^{2}=1-\frac{\nu}{4L}+\frac{\nu}{16L}\leq 1-\frac{\nu}{8L},

\displaystyle\gamma_{t}^{2}L^{2}+2\nu\gamma_{t}-1+\nu L\gamma_{t}^{2}=\frac{1}{16}+\frac{\nu}{2L}-1+\frac{\nu}{16L}<0,

where we use the fact that

. Putting the pieces together yields

\displaystyle\|x_{t+1}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\Big(1-\frac{\nu}{8L}\Big)\cdot\|x_{t}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2\mathsf{Err}(x_{\star},X)^{2}}{\nu L}.

Unrolling the recursion over time thus yields

\displaystyle\|x_{t+1}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\Big(1-\frac{\nu}{8L}\Big)^{t}\cdot\|x_{1}-x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{16\mathsf{Err}(x_{\star},X)^{2}}{\nu^{2}},

and taking square-roots of both sides and using the inequality
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
completes the proof.

Proof of Theorem
2

Our proof proceeds by first bounding the strong pseudo-monotonicity parameter
\nu
and Lipschitzness parameter
L
, and then we apply Theorem
1
.

Strong pseudo-monotonicity.

By definition, the strong pseudo-monotonicity constant around
x_{\star}
is bounded below by the strong monotonicity constant. For any pair
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\langle F(x)-F(x^{\prime}),x-x^{\prime}\rangle=\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[h(\langle a_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle)-h(\langle a_{i},x\rangle)\big]\cdot\big[\langle a_{i},x\rangle-\langle a_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle\big].

By the mean value theorem, for any
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, there exists some
\min\{\langle a_{i},x\rangle,\langle a_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle\}\leq t_{i}\leq\max\{\langle a_{i},x\rangle,\langle a_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle\}
such that

h(\langle a_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle)-h(\langle a_{i},x\rangle)=h^{\prime}(t_{i})\cdot\big[\langle a_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle-\langle a_{i},x\rangle\big]=|h^{\prime}(t_{i})|\cdot\big[\langle a_{i},x\rangle-\langle a_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle\big],

where
h^{\prime}(t_{i})\leq 0
is the derivative of
h
at
t_{i}
(recall that
h
is nonincreasing).
Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle F(x)-F(x^{\prime}),x-x^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|h^{\prime}(t_{i})|\cdot\big[\langle a_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle-\langle a_{i},x\rangle\big]^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}}{n}\min_{1\leq i\leq n}|h^{\prime}(t_{i})|\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[\langle a_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle-\langle a_{i},x\rangle\big]^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}}{n}\cdot\min_{1\leq i\leq n}|h^{\prime}(t_{i})|\cdot\lambda_{\mathsf{min}}(\Sigma,X-X)\cdot\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2},

By noting that

\displaystyle|h^{\prime}(t_{i})|=\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}\exp\big(-\mu_{j}\cdot t_{i}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad 0\leq t_{i}\leq\max_{\widetilde{x}\in X}\;\langle a_{i},\widetilde{x}\rangle,

we obtain the bound

\min_{1\leq i\leq n}|h^{\prime}(t_{i})|\geq\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}\cdot\exp\big(-\mu_{j}\max_{\widetilde{x}\in X,\;1\leq i\leq n}\langle a_{i},\widetilde{x}\rangle\big).

Putting the pieces together, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle F(x)-F(x^{\prime}),x-x^{\prime}\rangle\geq\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{\mathsf{min}}(\Sigma,X-X)}{n}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}\cdot\exp\big(-\mu_{j}\max_{\widetilde{x}\in X,\;1\leq i\leq n}\langle a_{i},\widetilde{x}\rangle\big)\cdot\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}.

Since the above bound holds for any
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\nu\geq\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{\mathsf{min}}(\Sigma,X-X)}{n}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}\cdot\exp\big(-\mu_{j}\max_{\widetilde{x}\in X,\;1\leq i\leq n}\langle a_{i},\widetilde{x}\rangle\big).

(29)

Lipschitz constant.

Let us now show that
F
obeys the Lipschitz property, noting that Assumption
2
is not required for this claim. Define a vector
w\in\real^{n}
with
w_{i}=h(\langle a_{i},x\rangle)-h(\langle a_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle)
. We have

\displaystyle\|F(x)-F(x^{\prime})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}^{2}}{n^{2}}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(h(\langle a_{i},x\rangle)-h(\langle a_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle)\big)a_{i}\Big\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}^{2}}{n^{2}}\|\bm{A}^{\top}w\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}^{2}\lambda_{\mathsf{max}}(\Sigma,\real^{d})}{n^{2}}\cdot\|w\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[h(\langle a_{i},x\rangle)-h(\langle a_{i},x^{\prime}\rangle)\big]^{2}

Putting together the pieces.

5
Discussion

We presented the first provable and efficient algorithm for single-material reconstruction from spectral CT measurements, which was based on carefully constructing a variational inequality whose fixed point approximated the desired signal. We presented explicit bounds on the sample and iteration complexity of our EXACT algorithm both for general measurements and Gaussian measurements, handling noise in the measurements and structure in the signal. Several downstream questions are interesting from both the theoretical and methodological points of view — we point out a few such questions below.

Let us start with some interesting theoretical questions. First, understanding the fundamental (i.e. information-theoretic) limits of signal recovery in this problem — say under the Poisson noise model (
5
) — is an important direction, since it would provide guidance on whether the performance of our estimator can be improved. A second family of theoretical questions for the algorithm when run on Gaussian data is to characterize sharp rates of convergence and sample complexity (i.e. with exact constant factors). Note that our algorithm itself is a
general first-order method
in the nomenclature of
Celentano et al. 2020
, so we may be able to prove a so-called “state evolution” that characterizes its iterate-by-iterate performance as well as its eventual error. On a complementary note, information-theoretic lower bounds for the problem should be derivable via the techniques in
Barbier et al. 2019
. When does the eventual error of our algorithm exactly match the information-theoretic lower bound in the Gaussian model? To our knowledge, in spite of considerable research on monotone single-index models with Gaussian data
(
Balabdaoui et al. 2019
;
Pananjady and Foster 2021
, e.g.,)
and although there has been some recent interest in proving state evolutions for algorithms that go beyond first-order methods
(
Chandrasekher et al. 2023
;
Chandrasekher et al. 2024
;
Lou et al. 2024
;
Kaushik et al. 2024
, see, e.g.,)
, such questions have not received attention for algorithms that find fixed-points of (strongly) monotone VIs.

From a methodological viewpoint also, there are several interesting open questions. The first and most important open question is how to design a variant of our methodology for the multi-material recovery problem in Eq. (
1
). Note that in that case, the natural analog of the operator
F
that we use is no longer monotone, so we would need to take a different approach to designing a provably convergent algorithm.
Our analysis also opens up interesting measurement design questions, such as how to optimally allocate a limited photon budget by trading off source intensity
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}
and number of measurements
n
to yield the highest reconstruction accuracy. Future analysis may also consider detector non-idealities such as
pulse pileup
and
charge sharing
, in which some incident photons are not recorded or are recorded as multiple photons with incorrect energies. For example, reducing the source intensity
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{2.974pt}{0.43057pt}}{I}
increases shot noise but also mitigates pulse pileup by reducing the likelihood that a photon arrives during the sensor’s dead time. Incorporating these effects into algorithm design and analysis may inform refinements to the reconstruction process and measurement design setup.

54a
) yields

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{\delta_{i}\}={\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\frac{|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|^{2}G^{2}\mathbbm{1}\{G>0\}}{\big(|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|^{2}+\gamma_{\star}\big)^{2}}\cdot\int_{-G/4}^{G/4}\frac{e^{-t^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg\},\quad\text{where }G,\widetilde{G}\overset{\mathsf{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,1).

Further note that for
G>0
,

\int_{-G/4}^{G/4}\frac{t^{2}e^{-t^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{d}t\leq\Big(\frac{G}{4}\Big)^{2}\int_{-G/4}^{G/4}\frac{e^{-t^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{d}t.

Hence, invoking the definition of
\gamma_{\star}
as in Eq. (
18
), we find that

{\mathbb{E}}\{\delta_{i}\}\geq 16{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\frac{|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|^{2}\mathbbm{1}\{G>0\}}{\big(|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|^{2}+\gamma_{\star}\big)^{2}}\cdot\int_{-G/4}^{G/4}\frac{t^{2}e^{-t^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg\}\geq\frac{16\overline{\omega}(x_{\star},X)^{2}}{n}.

We next turn to lower bounding
{\mathbb{E}}\{\eta_{i}\}
. By definition of
\eta_{i}
(
54b
),

{\mathbb{E}}\{\eta_{i}\}={\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\frac{|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|^{2}\widetilde{G}^{2}\mathbbm{1}\{G>0\}}{\big(|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|^{2}+\gamma_{\star}\big)^{2}}\cdot\mathbbm{1}\big\{-G/4\leq\widetilde{G}\leq G/4\big\}\bigg\}.

Conditioning on
G
in tandem with the event
G>0
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big\{\widetilde{G}^{2}\mathbbm{1}\big\{-G/4\leq\widetilde{G}\leq G/4\big\}\Big\}=\int_{-G/4}^{G/4}\frac{t^{2}e^{-t^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{d}t.

Putting the two pieces together yields

{\mathbb{E}}\{\eta_{i}\}\geq{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\frac{|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|^{2}\mathbbm{1}\{G>0\}}{\big(|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|^{2}+\gamma_{\star}\big)^{2}}\cdot\int_{-G/4}^{G/4}\frac{t^{2}e^{-t^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg\}\geq\frac{16\overline{\omega}(x_{\star},X)^{2}}{n},

where the last step follows from the definition of
\gamma_{\star}
, i.e., Eq. (
18
). We next turn to prove the concentration of
\omega(x_{\star},X)
(
32
). To reduce the notational burden, we let
X^{\perp}=\{P_{x_{\star}}^{\perp}x:x\in X\}
and define
\varPsi:\real^{d}\mapsto\real
as
\varPsi(g)=\sup_{v\in X^{\perp}}\langle v/\|v\|_{2},g\rangle
. Note that by definition (
32
), we have
\omega(x_{\star},X)=\varPsi(g)
for
g\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
. It is equivalent to prove concentration of the random variable
\varPsi(g)
for
g\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
. Note that for all
g_{1},g_{2}
, we obtain

\varPsi(g_{1})-\varPsi(g_{2})=\sup_{v\in X^{\perp}}\Bigl\langle\frac{v}{\|v\|_{2}},g_{1}\Bigr\rangle-\sup_{v\in X^{\perp}}\Bigl\langle\frac{v}{\|v\|_{2}},g_{2}\Bigr\rangle\leq\sup_{v\in X^{\perp}}\Bigl\langle\frac{v}{\|v\|_{2}},g_{1}-g_{2}\Bigr\rangle\leq\|g_{1}-g_{2}\|_{2}.

Similar arguments show
\varPsi(g_{2})-\varPsi(g_{1})\leq\|g_{1}-g_{2}\|_{2}
for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in\real^{d}
. Therefore,
\varPsi(\cdot)
is a
1
-Lipschitz function, so that applying
Wainwright 2019
yields

\Pr\Big\{|\varPsi(g)-{\mathbb{E}}\{\varPsi(g)\}|\geq t\Big\}\leq 2\exp(-t^{2}/2)\quad\text{for all }t>0.

The desired result follows by noting
\omega(x_{\star},X)=\varPsi(g)
and
\overline{\omega}(x_{\star},X)={\mathbb{E}}\{\varPsi(g)\}
for
g\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
.
∎

B.3
Proof of Lemma
3

Note that by definition of
\{\theta_{i},\vartheta_{i},\xi_{i}\}
in Eq. (
46
),

|\theta_{i}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}\frac{(a_{i}^{\top}x_{\star})^{2}}{\|x_{\star}\|_{2}^{2}},\quad|\vartheta_{i}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}\widetilde{g}_{i}^{2},\quad|\xi_{i}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}\frac{a_{i}^{\top}x_{\star}}{\|x_{\star}\|_{2}}\widetilde{g}_{i}.

Since
a_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
and
\widetilde{g}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, it follows that
\theta_{i}
,
\vartheta_{i}
, and
\xi_{i}
are sub-exponential random variables, i.e., there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that

\|\theta_{i}\|_{\psi_{1}}\;\vee\;\|\vartheta_{i}\|_{\psi_{1}}\;\vee\;\|\xi_{i}\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}.

Applying Bernstein’s inequality
[
Vershynin 2018
, Theorem 2.8.1]
, we deduce that there exists a universal constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that with probability at least
1-6n^{-10}
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}-{\mathbb{E}}\{\theta_{i}\}\bigg|\;\vee\;\bigg|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta_{i}-{\mathbb{E}}\{\vartheta_{i}\}\bigg|\;\vee\;\bigg|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}-{\mathbb{E}}\{\xi_{i}\}\bigg|\leq C^{\prime}\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}\sqrt{\frac{\log(n)}{n}}.

We claim that there exists a universal and positive constant
c
such that,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{\theta_{i}\}\geq c\sum_{j=1}^{W}\frac{s_{j}\mu_{j}}{\big(\mu_{j}\|x_{\star}\|_{2}\big)^{4}\vee 1},\quad{\mathbb{E}}\{\vartheta_{i}\}\geq c\sum_{j=1}^{W}\frac{s_{j}\mu_{j}}{\big(\mu_{j}\|x_{\star}\|_{2}\big)^{4}\vee 1},\quad{\mathbb{E}}\{\xi_{i}\}=0,

deferring its proof to the end of this section. Combining the inequalities in the display with condition (
21
) and adjusting constants, we obtain that the deviation
\sqrt{\log(n)/n}
is of smaller order term than both
{\mathbb{E}}\{\theta_{i}\}
and
{\mathbb{E}}\{\vartheta_{i}\}
. We thus conclude that with probability at least
1-6n^{-10}
,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}\;\wedge\;\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\vartheta_{i}\gtrsim\sum_{j=1}^{W}\frac{s_{j}\mu_{j}}{\big(\mu_{j}\|x_{\star}\|_{2}\big)^{4}\vee 1}\quad\text{and}\quad\bigg|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\bigg|\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\log(n)}{n}}.

This concludes the main proof. It remains to establish the claim on
{\mathbb{E}}\{\theta_{i}\}
,
{\mathbb{E}}\{\vartheta_{i}\}
, and
{\mathbb{E}}\{\xi_{i}\}
.

Lower bound on
{\mathbb{E}}\{\theta_{i}\}
.

By definition (
46
), we obtain that for
G,\widetilde{G}\overset{\mathsf{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{\theta_{i}\}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\Big\{|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|G^{2}\mathbbm{1}\big\{-G/4\leq\widetilde{G}\leq G/4,G>0\big\}\Big\}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|G^{2}\mathbbm{1}\{G>0\}\int_{-G/4}^{G/4}\frac{e^{-t^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{|h^{\prime}(6\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G)|G^{2}\mathbbm{1}\{G>0\}\frac{G}{2}\frac{e^{-G^{2}/32}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\bigg\}.

Note that by Eq. (
6
),
|h^{\prime}(t)|=\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}\exp(-\mu_{j}t)
for all
t>0
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{\theta_{i}\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}{\mathbb{E}}\Big\{\exp\big(-6\mu_{j}\|x_{\star}\|_{2}G-G^{2}/32\big)\mathbbm{1}\{G>0\}G^{3}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{W}s_{j}\mu_{j}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-6\mu_{j}\|x_{\star}\|_{2}t-t^{2}/32}\cdot t^{3}\cdot e^{-t^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}t

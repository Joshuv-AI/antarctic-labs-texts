Semaglutide as adjunct treatment for obesity in adolescents receiving antipsychotic medication (the GOAL trial): protocol for a single-arm, open-label feasibility study
Authors: Laura Victoria Giraldi, Jon Trærup Andersen, Jonas Jespersen, Christoffer Storm Baden, Cilius Esmann Fonvig, Troels Riis, Kim Peder Dalhoff, Jens-Christian Holm, Anders Fink-Jensen, Anne Katrine Pagsberg, Mikkel Bring Christensen, Christina Gade
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAdolescents treated with antipsychotic medication are at increased risk of obesity and metabolic complications, yet evidence-based interventions in this group remain limited. Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists have demonstrated efficacy in the treatment of obesity in both adults and adolescents. The aim of this trial is to assess the feasibility of a 36-week treatment period with a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist as an adjunct to standard care in adolescents with antipsychotic-associated obesity, defined as completion from baseline to end of treatment. The findings from this feasibility trial will inform the design and support the scale-up of a subsequent randomised controlled trial.Methods and analysisThe GOAL trial is a single-arm, open-label feasibility study conducted at Copenhagen University Hospital, Bispebjerg, Denmark, in collaboration with the Child and Adolescent Mental Health Center. A total of 34 adolescents aged 12–18 years with obesity, defined as body mass index SD score equal to or greater than +1.28 and body fat percentage at or above the 95th percentile, receiving stable antipsychotic treatment will be enrolled. The study population includes adolescents with a range of psychiatric diagnoses requiring antipsychotic treatment, including psychotic disorders, mood disorders and neurodevelopmental disorders. Adolescents receiving antipsychotic treatment under coercion are excluded. Participants will receive once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide at a dose up to 2.4 milligrams for 36 weeks in addition to standard care, followed by an 18-month observational follow-up period. The primary outcome is study completion rate. Additional feasibility outcomes include recruitment rate, treatment adherence and completion of study procedures. Secondary outcomes include changes in body mass index SD score, body composition, metabolic biomarkers and psychiatric outcomes. Feasibility outcomes will be analysed descriptively. Exploratory longitudinal analyses will be conducted using linear mixed-effects models.Ethics and disseminationThe study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki and European Union Clinical Trials Regulation (536/2014) and is approved in the Clinical Trials Information System (2024-5 17 471-21-02). Written informed consent will be obtained from participants and their legal guardians. Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and scientific conferences.Trial registration number2024-5 17 471-21-02.Keywords: Body Mass Index, Clinical Trial, Child & adolescent psychiatry, Clinical Protocols, Adolescent

Introduction

Obesity and psychiatric disorders are increasingly prevalent among adolescents, representing major health challenges with significant implications for both physical and mental well-being. The relationship between these conditions is complex and bidirectional, influenced by a combination of lifestyle, psychological mechanisms and pharmacological treatments, including the weight-gain potential of antipsychotics.1 2

Although antipsychotics are effective for managing a broad range of psychiatric disorders, their use is frequently accompanied by substantial metabolic adverse effects, including obesity, type 2 diabetes and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease.3–7 Further, treatment with antipsychotic medicines during childhood and adolescence has been associated with an increased risk of cardiometabolic events.8

At present, there is a paucity of evidence-based interventions for the prevention and management of obesity and associated metabolic disturbances in children and adolescents exposed to antipsychotics.6 9 Accumulating evidence indicates a favourable safety and efficacy profile of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1-RA), suggesting potential utility in mitigating weight gain and metabolic dysregulation among adults undergoing treatment with antipsychotics.10–12

The aim of this trial is to assess the feasibility of a 36-week GLP-1-RA treatment period in adolescents with obesity, who are in treatment with antipsychotics. Importantly, the trial will assess the extent to which adolescents can cooperate with and adhere to both the treatment regimen and study-related procedures, to inform and scale up for a subsequent randomised controlled trial.

Methods and analysis

Study design

The GOAL trial is a single-arm, open-label feasibility study conducted at the Zelo Phase 1 Unit, Department of Clinical Pharmacology, Copenhagen University Hospital, Bispebjerg, Denmark, in close collaboration with the Child and Adolescent Mental Health Center (CAMHS), Capital Region of Denmark. Reporting of this protocol follows the SPIRIT 2025 statement.

The trial commenced in June 2025, with participant recruitment expected to span 12 months. Following baseline assessments, participants will receive semaglutide during a 36-week intervention period. This will be followed by an 18-month observational follow-up period to assess the long-term sustainability of treatment outcomes. The total duration per participant will be approximately 27 months.

Patient and public involvement

A patient organisation representative was involved through the CAMHS research committee, which reviewed and endorsed the study protocol. This ensured that patient and family perspectives were incorporated into the study design, particularly regarding acceptability, study burden and participant support needs.

Eligibility criteria

Eligibility criteria are detailed in box 1. Key inclusion criteria are age 12–18 years, Body Mass Index Standard Deviation Score (BMI SDS) ≥1.28 and body fat percentage ≥95 th percentile, ongoing treatment with an antipsychotic and clinically stable psychiatric condition. Major exclusion criteria include compulsory psychiatric treatment, a history of pancreatic, hepatic or renal disease, current use of GLP-1-RA and pregnancy.

Sample size

The aim is for 34 participants to initiate treatment with semaglutide. The target number of individuals to be screened (providing informed consent) is 44, allowing for an anticipated screen failure rate of up to 20%. Approximately 25 participants are expected to complete the 36-week intervention period. If the dropout rate exceeds 25%, additional participants will be recruited to ensure adequate evaluation of feasibility outcomes.

As a feasibility study, the primary objective of the sample size is to assess recruitment, retention, adherence and acceptability of study procedures rather than to test efficacy. The planned sample size is considered sufficient to estimate key feasibility parameters with acceptable precision and to generate preliminary estimates of treatment effects on BMI SDS and other secondary outcomes. These estimates will inform the design of a future randomised controlled trial.

Intervention

Semaglutide will be administered as a once-weekly subcutaneous injection by a trained healthcare professional, caregiver, or participant under supervision. Dosing will be individualised, with gradual titration to the maximum tolerated dose as clinically appropriate.

Participants will undergo structured assessments including clinical interviews, blood sampling, MRI, dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA), bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA),13 psychopathology assessments and health monitoring. Participants will receive standardised instructions, reminders and support to optimise adherence. Used injection devices will be returned at study visits to monitor compliance. Dose adjustments or discontinuation will be made if clinically indicated. Feasibility outcomes

The primary outcome is the proportion of participants completing the trial from baseline to end of treatment (Visit 10). Additional feasibility outcomes include recruitment rate, treatment adherence and completion of study procedures. A completion rate ≥70% will be considered feasible.

Secondary outcome measures

The key secondary outcome is change in BMI SDS from baseline. Additional outcomes include changes in body composition and bone mineral density, including total body weight, waist and hip circumference, liver fat fraction and visceral and subcutaneous adipose tissue volume.

Metabolic outcomes include lipid profile (low-density lipoprotein, high-density lipoprotein, total cholesterol, triglycerides), glycaemic markers (fasting plasma glucose, HbA1c, insulin, C-peptide), inflammatory markers (tumour necrosis factor alpha, interleukin-6, high sensitivity CRP), liver function tests (alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, albumin, international normalized ratio), haematology (haemoglobin, leukocytes, platelets) and pancreatic enzymes (amylase).

Psychiatric outcomes include illness severity assessed using the Clinical Global Impressions Scale-Severity,14 as well as changes in antipsychotic medication.

Behavioural and lifestyle outcomes include diet, physical activity and use of alcohol, tobacco, recreational drugs and concomitant medications. Post-trial initiation of GLP-1-RA therapy outside the study protocol will also be recorded.

Data collection and analysis

Data will be collected across ten scheduled visits using clinical assessments, imaging (MRI, DXA), BIA, blood sampling and patient-reported diaries. Data will be recorded in electronic case report forms and stored in the REDCap database.15

All analyses will be performed on the full analysis set, defined as all participants receiving at least one dose of semaglutide. Feasibility outcomes will be summarised using descriptive statistics. Continuous variables will be reported as means (SD) or medians (IQR) and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages.

BMI SDS will be calculated according to the age- and sex-specific Danish reference values for body mass index in children and adolescents.

Changes in secondary outcomes over time will be analysed using linear mixed-effects models to account for repeated measurements within participants. The models will include fixed effects for time as a categorical variable, with a random intercept for each participant to account for within-participant correlation. If appropriate based on model diagnostics and data structure, additional random effects for individual trajectories over time will be considered. All analyses are exploratory.

Missing data will be evaluated according to the extent and pattern of missingness. Longitudinal analyses will be conducted using a mixed model for repeated measures approach under the assumption that data are missing at random. Sensitivity analyses using alternative assumptions regarding missing data may be performed if relevant.

Statistical analyses will be performed using R.16 Exploratory subgroup analyses may be conducted according to age, sex, psychiatric diagnosis and antipsychotic treatment.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical considerations

The GOAL trial complies with the Declaration of Helsinki, EU Clinical Trials Regulation (536/2014) and relevant Danish national regulations.17 The trial has obtained approval from the Danish Medicines Agency and the national ethics committee and is registered and authorised in the Clinical Trials Information System (CTIS: 2024-5 17 471-21-02).18 Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants and their legal guardians prior to enrolment, with assent additionally sought from minors. An age-appropriate participant information sheet will describe the study aims, procedures, potential risks and possible benefits to ensure fully informed participation.19 Examples of the participant consent and assent forms are provided in the online supplemental material S2 and online supplemental material S3, respectively.

Semaglutide (Wegovy) is approved for the management of obesity in adolescents in both Europe and the United States.20 Common adverse reactions include mild to moderate gastrointestinal symptoms, primarily nausea and vomiting. Less frequent but serious reactions include pancreatitis and complications related to diabetic retinopathy.20 GLP-1-RA-induced weight loss may also reduce lean body mass in addition to fat mass, which will be monitored.21–23 All adverse events, serious adverse events and serious unexpected suspected adverse reactions will be systematically recorded per regulatory requirements.

Non-invasive assessments, including DXA and MRI, entail minimal risk. The radiation exposure from DXA scanning is approximately 0.1 mSv, substantially below levels considered harmful. These procedures are painless and no adverse effects are anticipated. Venous blood sampling may cause mild transient discomfort. To minimise distress, topical local anaesthetics will be offered to all participants. The total blood volume collected during the trial will not exceed 200 mL, with an average of approximately 50 mL per sampling session. This volume is not expected to affect participants’ haemodynamics, growth or development.24 Participants experiencing adverse effects will receive immediate medical evaluation and appropriate management. Study participation may be modified or discontinued at any time if required to ensure participant safety.

Confidentiality and data protection

All participant data will be handled in compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation and the Danish Data Protection Act.25 26 Information will be stored in encrypted databases, with personal identifiers accessible only to authorised investigators. Data linking participants to their study IDs will be stored separately in a secure, restricted-access environment. Anonymised datasets will be used for analysis, ensuring participant confidentiality throughout the trial and during dissemination.26

Dissemination plan

Findings will be presented at national and international conferences and submitted to peer-reviewed journals. Authorship will follow ICMJE guidelines.27 Trial results will be uploaded to CTIS within 1 year of completion. A plain-language summary will be shared with participants and the public to ensure accessibility of findings.

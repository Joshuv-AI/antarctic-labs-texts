Analysis-Aware Defeaturing of Dirichlet Features Thanks: Submitted to the editors 19/08/2025.
Philipp Weder, Annalisa Buffa

Abstract
Feature removal from computational geometries, or defeaturing, is an integral part of industrial simulation pipelines. Defeaturing simplifies the otherwise costly or even impossible meshing process, speeds up the simulation, and lowers its memory footprint. Current defeaturing operators are often based on heuristic criteria and ignore the impact of the simplifications on the PDE solution. This work extends the mathematically rigorous framework developed in [ 5 ] to features subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions in Poisson problems. We derive a posteriori error estimators for negative features in the interior or on the boundary of the computational domain. The estimators’ dependence on the feature size is explicit, and their evaluation only involves boundary integrals over the feature boundary. Numerical experiments in two and three dimensions showcase the validity and efficiency of the estimators.

Introduction

In many engineering applications, partial differential equations (PDEs) must be solved in geometrically complex domains, including geometrical features of different sizes and shapes. The complexity and multiscale nature of such computational geometries make creating an appropriate mesh and subsequent simulations challenging. Even a single, relatively small geometric feature can increase the cost of a simulation by a factor of 10 due to the higher number of degrees of freedom
[
42
,
20
]
. Therefore, it is common practice to simplify the initial geometry as much as possible by ignoring those features that may not significantly impact the simulation’s result. This process is referred to as defeaturing.

Multiple approaches exist for this task based on some a priori criteria, which are applied before any simulation is performed. The latter criteria may be subjective and rely on the engineer’s expertise
[
21
]
, be based on some geometrical rationale
[
36
]
or on a priori knowledge of the underlying physical problem
[
11
,
12
,
31
]
.

However, to automate the modeling and simulation process, a posteriori estimators of the defeaturing errors are necessary. In
[
10
]
, an a posteriori error estimator is formulated for linear elasticity problems. Although not mathematically certified, it is based on the crucial insight that the defeaturing error must concentrate around the defeatured boundaries. Another line of work referred to as feature sensitivity analysis (FSA)
[
15
,
16
,
39
,
40
]
based on topological sensitivity analysis
[
9
,
34
]
crucially relies on the assumption of infinitesimally small features and cannot be generalized to more complex situations. In
[
25
]
, an a posteriori estimator for internal holes based on the dual weighted residual (DWR) method
[
3
,
30
]
is presented with generalizations to different linear and non-linear PDE problems in
[
24
,
26
]
and
[
43
]
. A similar a posteriori error estimator based on the reciprocal theorem, stating that the solution flux is conserved in the features, is presented in
[
35
]
. Yet, all the latter works lack a rigorous mathematical certification of the estimators as well as a study of their efficiency. More precisely, the estimators rely on the assumption that the solution on the defeatured geometry is close to the one on the exact geometry and involve heuristic parameters that depend on the size of the feature.

This work is based on the framework introduced in
[
5
]
, which formalizes the process of defeaturing for the Poisson equation and provides a mathematically certified and efficient a posteriori estimator for single features subject to Neumann boundary conditions. The estimator is derived from an error representation formula based on the divergence theorem reflecting the intuition in
[
10
]
that the defeaturing error is concentrated at the defeatured boundary. In particular, the evaluation of the estimator only requires evaluating boundary integrals over the defeatured boundary. Furthermore, the dependence on the feature size is explicit in contrast to previous works. In
[
1
]
, the framework is extended to multi-feature geometries, linear elasticity problems and Stokes flow, while
[
7
]
combines the defeaturing estimator with the a posteriori estimator for the numerical error from
[
6
]
to an adaptive meshing procedure, which simultaneously handles the numerical and the defeaturing error in the context of isogeometric analysis (IGA)
[
19
,
28
]
. A comprehensive treatment of these subjects can also found in
[
8
]
. An a posteriori error estimator based on an equilibrated flux reconstruction is proposed in
[
4
]
, which allows for a sharp bound on the numerical error component and is suitable for finite element discretizations.

We extend the framework in
[
5
]
to the case of features with Dirichlet boundary conditions in Poisson problems. We derive a posteriori error estimators for negative features in the interior and the boundary of the computational domain and prove their reliability. Although the error representation formula remains valid in this case, the analysis is more intricate since it involves fractional Sobolev spaces on boundaries with negative exponents. Furthermore, care must be taken when treating mixed boundary conditions. As a model problem, we consider the Poisson equation, but the approach can clearly be generalized to other linear elliptic PDEs.

The paper is structured as follows:
Section

2
introduces the functional analytic notions necessary for the mathematical analysis of the defeaturing problem. The model problem is defined in
section

3
together with their simplified counterparts. In
section

4
, we define the defeaturing error and the error estimators and prove their reliability. Numerical experiments validating the theory are presented and discussed in
section

5
. Finally, we provide our conclusions in
section

6
.

Definition 3

Let

Trace spaces and subspaces

Trace spaces are essential to deal with boundary conditions.
To that end, let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain and
\Lambda\subseteq\partial\Omega
with
|\Lambda|>0
be a subset. We denote by
H^{1/2}(\Lambda)
the Sobolev space on

1

In addition,

1

In the subsequent discussion, we will require the following subspace of the trace space

\displaystyle H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)\coloneqq\left\{\mu\in L^{2}(\Lambda):\mu^{\star}\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)\right\},

(4)

where
\mu^{\star}
denotes the extension of
\mu
by 0 on
\partial\Omega
. The corresponding norm and semi-norm are, respectively, defined by

\displaystyle||\mu||_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}^{2}

\displaystyle||\mu||_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}=||\mu^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle|\mu|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}=|\mu^{\star}|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}.

Remark 4
.

In the treatment of the space

, defined in

4

\partial\Lambda=\emptyset

\Lambda

coincide. In contrast, if
\Lambda
has a boundary itself, i.e.

Proposition 5

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2

and
\Lambda\subset\partial\Omega

. Then, for every
\mu\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle||\mu^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}\lesssim|\Lambda|^{\frac{-1}{2(n-1)}}||\mu||_{0,\Lambda}+|\mu|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}.

We argue by a scaling argument, where the rescaled subset
\hat{\Lambda}
of the boundary
\partial\hat{\Omega}
has unit size. Then, consider a covering of the rescaled domain boundary
\partial\hat{\Omega}
with constants
\hat{\epsilon},\hat{L}>0
and

according to

1

Rescaling all the balls by the factor

\displaystyle\epsilon\simeq|\Lambda|^{\frac{1}{n-1}},

\displaystyle L\simeq 1,

\displaystyle M\simeq 1.

(5)

Finally, let

with
\mu^{\star}
the extension by zero to

\displaystyle|u|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim|\Lambda|^{\frac{-1}{2(n-1)}}||\mu||_{0,\Lambda}+|\mu^{\star}|_{1/2,\partial\Omega},

2.4
The Neumann
trace operator

We define the Neumann trace operator as the continuous linear operator
\nu_{n}:H(\Div;\Omega)\to H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
, such that for
v\in H(\Div;\Omega)
and
\mu\in H^{1/2}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\langle\nu_{n}(v),\mu\rangle\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}v\cdot\nabla R_{\partial\Omega}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Div(v)R_{\partial\Omega}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(6)

where
R_{\partial\Omega}
denotes the lifting operator defined in
section

2.3
.
For subsets of the boundary
\Lambda\subset\partial\Omega
with
|\Lambda|>0
, we can naturally extend the definition in
eq.

6

\nu_{n}^{\Lambda}:H(\Div;\Omega)\to H^{-1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)
by setting

\displaystyle\langle\nu_{n}^{\Lambda}(v),\mu\rangle\coloneqq\langle\nu_{n}(v),\mu^{\star}\rangle,

\displaystyle v\in H(\Div;\Omega),\,\mu\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda),

(7)

In particular, we are interested in the dependence of the operator norm of

on the size of
\Lambda

\displaystyle\sup_{v\in H(\Div;\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{\mu\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle\nu_{n}(v),\mu\rangle}{||v||_{H(\Div;\Omega)}||\mu||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}}=1.

(8)

The latter does not hold in general for the partial Neumann trace operator
\nu_{n}^{\Lambda}
.
Nevertheless, if
\Lambda
is not a closed surface, we can establish a result similar to
eq.

8
with the help of the norm equivalence in
proposition

5

Lemma 6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2
be a Lipschitz domain and
\Lambda\subset\partial\Omega
with
|\Lambda|>0
and
\partial\Lambda\neq\emptyset
. Then, it holds for any
v\in H(\Div;\Omega)
and
\mu\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)
that

\displaystyle\langle\nu_{n}^{\Lambda}(v),\mu\rangle\lesssim||v||_{H(\Div;\Omega)}||\mu||_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}.

Proof.

Using the definition of the partial Neumann trace operator in
eq.

7
,
eq.

8
and
proposition

5
, we find

\displaystyle\langle\nu_{n}^{\Lambda}(v),\mu\rangle

\displaystyle\leq||v||_{H(\Div;\Omega)}||\mu^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}\lesssim||v||_{H(\Div;\Omega)}\left(|\Lambda|^{\frac{-1}{2(n-1)}}||\mu||_{0,\Lambda}+|\mu|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}\right).

\displaystyle\langle\nu_{n}^{\Lambda}(v),\mu\rangle

\displaystyle\leq||v||_{H(\Div;\Omega)}||\mu^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}\lesssim||v||_{H(\Div;\Omega)}||\mu||_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}.

Note that the argument in the proof of

\overline{\Gamma}_{N}\cap\overline{\gamma}\neq\emptyset
and
\overline{\Gamma}_{D}\cap\overline{\gamma}=\emptyset
. That is, the feature only touches the remaining Neumann boundary. Then, the defeatured problem reads

\displaystyle\text{Find }u_{0}:\Omega_{0}\to\mathbb{R},\text{ solution of}

(3.2b)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{0}=\tilde{f},&\text{ in }\Omega_{0},\\
u_{0}=g_{D},&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},\\
\partial_{n}u_{0}=\tilde{g}_{N},&\text{ on }\Gamma_{N}\cup\gamma_{0},\end{cases}

where
\tilde{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{0})
is an extension of
f
, and
\tilde{g}_{N}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{D}\cup\gamma_{0})
is an extension of
g_{N}
.

3.

Internal case:
F
is an internal feature. Then, the defeatured problem reads

\displaystyle\text{Find }u_{0}:\Omega_{0}\to\mathbb{R},\text{ solution of}

(3.2c)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{0}=\tilde{f},&\text{ in }\Omega_{0},\\
u_{0}=g_{D},&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},\\
\partial_{n}u_{0}=g_{N},&\text{ on }\Gamma_{N},\end{cases}

where
\tilde{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{0})
is an extension of
f
.

Several remarks are in order. First, we stress that we do not impose a specific extension of the source term
f
. However, it is clear that this choice significantly influences the defeaturing error. We will return to this in
section

5
.

Second, we justify our choice of imposing Neumann boundary conditions on the simplified feature boundary of Dirichlet-Neumann features by the observation that when discretizing the defeatured problem, imposing Dirichlet boundary conditions on the simplified boundary would require us to resolve the intersection between the outer Neumann boundary
\Gamma_{N}
and the simplified feature boundary
\gamma_{0}
. The latter may also be hard to mesh, hence we want to avoid it.

Finally, we note that the assumption that Dirichlet-Neumann boundary features only touch the outer Neumann boundary can be relaxed. The following arguments demonstrate that they may also be connected to the outer Dirichlet boundary; however, we maintain this assumption for ease of exposition.

4
Defeaturing error estimates

In this section, we discuss the error caused by solving one of the equations
eqs.

3.2a
,
3.2b
, and
3.2c
of
3.2
instead of
3.1
. We start by defining the defeaturing error and presenting an error representation formula on the feature boundary before we derive reliable error indicators for each of the three cases in
3.2
.

4.1
Defeaturing error and error representation

Consider the situation in
section

3
and let
u
and
u_{0}
be solutions to
3.1
and one of the variants of
3.2
, respectively. Then, we define the defeaturing error
e\in H^{1}_{0,\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
by
e\coloneqq u-(u_{0})_{|\Omega}
.
Note that in all three cases of
3.2
, the defeaturing error satisfies the PDE problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta e=0,&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
e=d_{\gamma},&\text{ on }\gamma,\\
e=0,&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},\\
\partial_{n}e=0,&\text{ on }\Gamma_{N},\end{cases}

(4)

where
d_{\gamma}\coloneqq g_{\gamma}-(u_{0})_{|\gamma}
denotes the error on the defeatured boundary.

We derive an error representation formula based on the PDE problem satisfied by the defeaturing error function. Let us first consider a case where no Neumann boundaries are present in the problem; that is,
\Gamma=\Gamma_{D}
. Then, it is clear that
d_{\gamma}\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\gamma)
and
e_{|\Gamma}\equiv 0\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma)
. Hence, we may apply the divergence theorem
[
14
]
[Corollary 2.6] to obtain

\displaystyle|e|_{1,\Omega}^{2}=\langle\nu_{n}(\nabla e),e_{|\partial\Omega}\rangle=\langle\nu_{n}^{\Gamma}(\nabla e),d_{\gamma}\rangle+\langle\nu_{n}^{\Gamma}(\nabla e),e_{|\Gamma}\rangle=\langle\nu_{n}^{\gamma}(\nabla e),d_{\gamma}\rangle.

(5)

In the presence of outer Neumann boundaries, which are not connected to the feature, see e.g.
\tilde{\Gamma}_{N}
in
fig.

1
, it is clear that
e_{|\tilde{\Gamma}_{N}}\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{N})
and
e_{|\Gamma_{D}\cup\gamma}\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Gamma_{D}\cup\gamma)
because
e
vanishes on
\Gamma_{D}
. Since
\nu_{n}^{\tilde{\Gamma}_{N}}(\nabla e)\equiv 0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\langle\nu_{n}(\nabla e),e_{|\partial\Omega}\rangle=\langle\nu_{n}^{\tilde{\Gamma}_{N}}(\nabla e),e_{|\tilde{\Gamma}_{N}}\rangle+\langle\nu_{n}^{\Gamma_{D}\cup\gamma}(\nabla e),e_{|\Gamma_{D}\cup\gamma}\rangle=\langle\nu_{n}^{\Gamma_{D}\cup\gamma}(\nabla e),e_{|\Gamma_{D}\cup\gamma}\rangle.

Hence, the error representation formula in
eq.

5
remains valid in this case.

In contrast, in the case of a Dirichlet-Neumann feature,
d_{\gamma}
is in general not an element of
H^{1/2}_{00}(\gamma)
, but merely of
H^{1/2}(\gamma)
. Indeed, the defeaturing error need not vanish at the intersection
\overline{\gamma}\cap\overline{\Gamma_{N}^{(\mathrm{DN})}}
; see
fig.

1
. However, consider an arbitrary extension
\tilde{d}_{\gamma}
of
d_{\gamma}
to
H^{1/2}_{00}(\gamma\cup\Gamma_{N}^{(\mathrm{DN})})
. Since
\nu_{n}(\nabla e)
vanishes on
\Gamma_{N}
, it holds that

\displaystyle|e|_{1,\Omega}^{2}=\langle\nu_{n}^{\gamma}(\nabla e),e_{|\gamma\cup\Gamma_{N}}\rangle=\langle\nu_{n}^{\gamma}(\nabla e),\tilde{d}_{\gamma}\rangle.

(6)

Therefore, once we have derived an error estimate for the Dirichlet-Dirichlet case from
eq.

5
, we can reuse that argument for the Dirichlet-Neumann case if we are able to construct a well-behaved extension
\tilde{d}_{\gamma}
of the boundary error
d_{\gamma}
. Such a continuous extension operator is constructed in
[
41
]
and we have re-stated the result for completeness in
section

A.3
[
theorem

18
].

We now derive reliable defeaturing error estimates for the three cases
eqs.

3.2a
,
3.2b
, and
3.2c
of
3.2
based on the boundary representation formulas
eqs.

5
and
6
and Sobolev estimates in trace spaces.

4.2
Negative boundary features - Dirichlet-Dirichlet case

In theDirichlet-Dirichlet case, we define the defeaturing estimator by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DD}}(u_{0})\coloneqq\sqrt{2||d_{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}||\nabla_{t}d_{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}},

(7)

where
\nabla_{t}d_{\gamma}
denotes the tangential gradient of
d_{\gamma}
along
\gamma
.

Theorem 9
.

The defeaturing error estimator defined in
eq.

7
is reliable for Dirichlet-Dirichlet features. That is, if
\overline{\gamma}\cap\overline{\Gamma_{D}}\neq\emptyset
and
\overline{\gamma}\cap\overline{\Gamma_{N}}=\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle|e|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DD}}(u_{0}).

Proof.

Using the error representation formula
eq.

5
and
lemma

6
, we immediately find

\displaystyle|e|_{1,\Omega}^{2}=\langle\nu_{n}^{\gamma}(\nabla e),d_{\gamma}\rangle\lesssim|e|_{1,\Omega}||d_{\gamma}||_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\gamma)}.

Moreover, using the Poincaré and interpolation inequalities in
lemma

14
and
lemma

16
, respectively, we find that

\displaystyle||d_{\gamma}||_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\gamma)}^{2}\lesssim\left(1+|\gamma|^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\right)|d_{\gamma}^{\star}|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{2}\lesssim 2||d_{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}||\nabla_{t}d_{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma},

which finishes the proof.

4.3
Negative boundary features - Dirichlet-Neumann case

In theDirichlet-Neumann case, we define the defeaturing estimator by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DN}}(u_{0})\coloneqq\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{DN}}(u_{0})+\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{DN}}(u_{0}),

(8)

where we define the average and non-average components, respectively, by

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{DN}}(u_{0})\coloneqq|\gamma|^{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}|\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{DN}}(u_{0})\coloneqq\sqrt{2||d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}||\nabla_{t}d_{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}}.

Theorem 10
.

The defeaturing error estimator defined in
eq.

8
is reliable for Dirichlet-Neumann features. That is, if
\overline{\gamma}\cap\overline{\Gamma_{N}}\neq\emptyset
and
\overline{\gamma}\cap\overline{\Gamma_{D}}=\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle|e|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{DN}}(u_{0}).

Proof.

First, we point out that in the Dirichlet-Neumann case,
d_{\gamma}
is in general not an element of
H^{1/2}_{00}(\gamma)
. For ease of notation, we define
\Lambda\coloneqq\gamma\cup\Gamma_{N}
. We now extend
d_{\gamma}
to
\Lambda
using the extension operator from
theorem

18
. By inserting the extension into the representation formula
eq.

6
, we obtain from Cauchy-Schwarz and
lemma

6
that

\displaystyle|e|_{1,\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\nu_{n}^{\Lambda}(\nabla e),\mathsf{E}_{\gamma}^{\Lambda}(d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma})+\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}\mathsf{E}_{\gamma}^{\Lambda}(1)\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim|e|_{1,\Omega}\left(||\mathsf{E}_{\gamma}^{\Lambda}(d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma})||_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}+|\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|||\mathsf{E}_{\gamma}^{\Lambda}(1)||_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}\right),

where
1\in H^{1/2}(\gamma)
denotes the constant function.
The continuity property of
\mathsf{E}_{\gamma}^{\Lambda}
in
theorem

18
and the Poincaré inequality in
lemma

15
yield the estimate

\displaystyle|e|_{1,\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\gamma|^{\frac{-1}{n-1}}||d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}^{2}+|d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|_{1/2,\gamma}^{2}+|\gamma|^{\frac{-1}{n-1}}|\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|^{2}||1||_{0,\gamma}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2|d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|_{1/2,\gamma}^{2}+|\gamma|^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}|\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|^{2}.

(9)

By applying the interpolation estimate from
lemma

16
to the first term in
eq.

9
, we find after taking the square root on both sides that

\displaystyle|e|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim\sqrt{2||d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}||\nabla_{t}d_{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}}+|\gamma|^{\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}}|\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|,

as desired.

4.4
Internal negative features

We cannot apply the previous arguments to internal features since
lemma

6
does not hold; however, we can treat the average of
d_{\gamma}
separately, as noted in
remark

7
.
Additionally, it can be shown that
proposition

5
yields only a suboptimal estimate for constant functions on internal features. The following estimate is sharp instead, assuming no further knowledge of the geometry:

Lemma 11
.

Let
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2
be a Lipschitz domain with internal Lipschitz feature
F
and feature boundary
\gamma=\partial F
. We write
\Gamma=\partial\Omega_{0}
and we denote by
m_{F}
the barycenter of
F
.

Then, it holds for
1\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\gamma)
that,

\displaystyle||1^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}\lesssim\bar{c}_{\gamma},

where

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{\gamma}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{|\log\left(s_{\gamma}\right)|}},&n=2,\\
\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\,\mathrm{diam}(\gamma)}{1-s_{\gamma}}},&n=3\end{cases},

and

\displaystyle s_{\gamma}\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\gamma)}{2\dist(m_{F},\Gamma)}.

Proof.

For ease of notation, we define
r\coloneqq\mathrm{diam}(\gamma)/2
and
R\coloneqq\dist(m_{F},\Gamma)
such that
s_{\gamma}=r/R
. Furthermore, assume without loss of generality that
m_{F}=0
. Then, we have in particular that

\displaystyle\overline{F}\subset B_{r}(0)\subset B_{R}(0)\subset\Omega_{0}.

By definition of the natural norm, every function
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
v_{|\partial\Omega}=1^{\star}\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
satisfies

\displaystyle||1^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}\leq|v|_{1,\Omega}.

Consider the function
v_{0}\in H^{1}(B_{R}(0)\setminus B_{r}(0))
solution to the PDE problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{0}=0,\text{ in }B_{R}(0)\setminus\overline{B_{r}(0)},\\
v_{0}=1,\text{ on }\partial B_{r}(0)\\
v_{0}=0,\text{ on }\partial B_{R}(0),\end{cases}

and define a lifting
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
of
1^{\star}\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
by setting

\displaystyle v\coloneqq\mathbbm{1}_{B_{r}(0)\setminus\overline{F}}+\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}(0)\setminus\overline{B_{r}(0)}}v_{0},

such that

\displaystyle||1^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}\leq|v|_{1,\Omega}=|v_{0}|_{1,B_{R}(0)\setminus\overline{B_{r}(0)}}.

A short computation shows that

\displaystyle|v_{0}|_{1,\Omega}\simeq\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{|\log(r/R)|}},&n=2\\
\sqrt{\frac{4\pi r}{1-r/R}},&n=3,\end{cases}

which concludes the proof.

We now define the defeaturing error estimator for internal features by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{int}}(u_{0})\coloneqq\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{int}}(u_{0})+\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{int}}(u_{0}),

(10)

where the average and non-average components are, respectively, defined by

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{int}}(u_{0})\coloneqq\bar{c}_{\gamma}|\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|,

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{int}}(u_{0})\coloneqq 2\sqrt{||d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}||\nabla_{t}d_{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}},

and
\bar{c}_{\gamma}
is defined in
lemma

11
.

Theorem 12
.

The defeaturing error estimator defined in
eq.

8
is reliable for internal features. That is, if
\overline{\gamma}\cap\overline{\partial\Omega}=\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle|e|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{int}}(u_{0}).

Proof.

Since the defeaturing error
e\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is the unique weak solution to problem
eq.

4
, it is the unique minimizer of the
H^{1}
-norm for the boundary condition
(d_{\gamma})^{\star}\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|e|_{1,\Omega}=||(d_{\gamma})^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}\leq||(d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma})^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}+|\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|||1^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}.

Using
lemma

11
, we find for the second term,

\displaystyle|\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|||1^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}\lesssim\bar{c}_{\gamma}|\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|.

Combining
proposition

5
and
lemma

15
yields for the first term,

\displaystyle||(d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma})^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\gamma|^{\frac{-1}{2(n-1)}}||d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}+|d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|_{1/2,\gamma}+|d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\gamma)}.

(11)

Next, we note that in the case of an interior feature, we have
\dist(\partial\Omega,\gamma)>0
, and therefore

\displaystyle|d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\gamma)}^{2}=|d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|_{1/2,\gamma}^{2}+\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\gamma}\int_{\gamma}\frac{|d_{\gamma}(x)-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)

(12)

\displaystyle\lesssim|d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|_{1/2,\gamma}^{2}+\frac{|\partial\Omega\setminus\gamma|}{\dist(\partial\Omega\setminus\gamma,\gamma)^{n}}||d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|_{1/2,\gamma}^{2},

where we have applied
lemma

15
in the last inequality and used the fact that
\dist(\partial\Omega\setminus\gamma,\gamma)\simeq|\gamma|^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
and that the measure of
\partial\Omega\setminus\gamma
is proportional to the one of
\gamma
under rescaling.
By inserting the estimate
eq.

12
into
section

4.4
and applying
lemma

15
together with
lemma

16
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle||(d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma})^{\star}||_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{(\inf)}\lesssim 2|d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|_{1/2,\gamma}\lesssim 2\sqrt{||d_{\gamma}-\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}||\nabla_{t}d_{\gamma}||_{0,\gamma}},

which concludes the proof.

4.5
Extension to multi-feature geometries

Given the structure of the estimators
eqs.

7
,
8
, and
10
, nothing prevents us from extending the present results to multi-feature geometries in the same way as
[
1
,
7
,
8

; that is, the multi-feature estimator is given by the square root of the squared estimators for each individual feature. However, note that the constant

in the estimator for internal features

5
Numerical experiments

In this section, we present numerical experiments validating the theory developed above. The computational geometries and meshes in two and three dimensions were created with the help of
gmsh

[
13
]
. The meshes are, by default, conformal across lower dimensional entities in the geometry. Hence, the exact and defeatured geometries could be meshed simultaneously. The relevant sub-meshes and degrees of freedom were then extracted at the simulation stage, making the comparison between the exact and defeatured solutions straightforward. The finite element simulations were implemented using the
FEniCSx
library
[
2
]
. Moreover, fine meshes with local refinement around the features were used so that numerical errors could be neglected. Nonetheless, we stress that the estimators introduced above are independent of the discretization method and the geometry, provided that one can numerically integrate along the feature boundary.

When assessing the performance of the estimators, we will be especially interested in the
effectivity index

\eta_{\mathrm{eff}}
defined by

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{eff}}\coloneqq\frac{\mathcal{E}(u_{0})}{|e|_{1,\Omega}},

where
\mathcal{E}(u_{0})
denotes one of the defeaturing estimators
eqs.

7
,
8
, and
10
.
We aim for this constant to be close to one for any feature. Based on
theorems

9
,
10
, and
12
, we expect this quantity to remain approximately constant across different feature sizes. Furthermore, it reflects the dependency of the hidden constant in the estimate on quantities, which are not explicitly tracked by the estimator, such as the feature shape.

5.3.2
Source term extensions

In contrast to the boundary feature cases, it is well-known from singular perturbation theory that naively extending the source term into the feature by the same expression does not yield a good approximation of the exact solution regardless of the boundary condition
[
29
]
. Indeed, in the limit
|\Lambda|\to 0
, the exact PDE
eq.

9
does not converge to its defeatured variant
eq.

3.2c
, but rather to a PDE on a perforated domain.

In the proposed defeaturing estimator this phenomenon is reflected by the coefficient
\bar{c}_{\gamma}
, which vanishes only very slowly for
|\gamma|\to 0
. Indeed, even in a domain with a smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and smooth boundary conditions, the average
\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}
of the boundary error may still be non-negligible. Hence,
\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{int}}(u_{0})=\bar{c}_{\gamma}|\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}|
only converges to zero very slowly, while the non-average part
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{int}}(u_{0})
vanishes quickly.

However, by adding an appropriate correction term to the right-hand side of the defeatured problem, one obtains a much better first-order approximation.
Let
\hat{G}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log(|x-m_{F}|)
denote the fundamental solution of the Laplacian centered at
m_{F}
and consider
G(x)=\hat{G}(x)-g(x)
, where
g
is the solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta g=0,\text{ in }\Omega_{0},\\
g(x)=-\hat{G}(x)\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},\\
\partial_{n}g(x)=-\partial_{n}\hat{G}(x)\text{ on }\Gamma_{N}.\\
\end{cases}

(16)

If the feature
F
is a disk centered at
m_{F}
, the first-order approximation is given by

\displaystyle u_{1}(x)\coloneqq u_{0}(x)+\mu(\gamma)\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}G(x),

\displaystyle\mu(\gamma)\coloneqq\frac{2\pi}{\log(\mathrm{diam}(\gamma)/2)-2\pi\overline{g}^{\gamma}},

(17)

while for more general shapes,
\mu(\gamma)
additionally depends on the logarithmic capacity of the feature
[
29
,
32
]
.

In the context of the estimator, note that
-\Delta u_{1}=f
still holds in
\Omega
, while there is a point source at the barycenter
m_{F}
of the feature. Consequently, the reliability in
theorem

12
still holds.
Furthermore, we have in the case of a disk feature that

\displaystyle\overline{(u-u_{1})}^{\gamma}=(1-\mu(\gamma)\overline{G}^{\gamma})\overline{d_{\gamma}}^{\gamma}=0,

while for more general shapes we have
\mu(\gamma)\overline{G}^{\gamma}\to 1
, for
|\gamma|\to 0
. Therefore, the first-order approximation corresponds to adding a point source at the barycenter to remove the average of the boundary error.

Let us illustrate this with the following experiment: Consider again the square domain with a disk cut from the center in
fig.

. We impose the Dirichlet data
g_{D}\equiv 1
and
g_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{x})=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}
on

and
\gamma
, respectively. The source term is given by
eq.

13
. Now we compute the naively defeatured solution
u_{0}
and the corrected solution
u_{1}
, where for the latter we must additionally solve equation
eq.

.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we have extended the framework for a posteriori defeaturing error estimation in
[
5
]
to negative Dirichlet features in the interior and on the boundary. The two crucial elements were the scaling behavior of the Neumann trace operator norm with respect to the defeatured boundary’s size and the continuous extension on Lipschitz boundaries. The estimators were successfully validated numerically in all three situations of
3.2
in two and three dimensions, demonstrating that they accurately capture the error’s dependence on the feature geometry and the underlying differential problem. Notably, the estimators are explicit in the feature size, independent of the specific extension chosen for the source term, and computationally inexpensive as they only require boundary integrals over the defeatured boundary.

While this paper paper focuses on the analysis in continuous spaces for a single feature, the framework is readily extensible.
Since the estimator is merely additive, extending the estimators to multiple features is straightforward, as in the Neumann case
[
1
]
. The discretization error can be taken into account via an a posteriori error estimator simultaneously; see
[
8
]
and
[
4
]
. Positive features can be handled by solving an extended problem in the bounding box of the positive feature as proposed in
[
5
,
8
]
. Finally, we only consider the global energy norm of the error in this paper. Deriving goal-oriented defeaturing error estimates will be an important step in applying the method in industrial settings. The development of such goal-oriented error estimates is the subject our future work.

In this section, we state several technical results that are used in the proofs of

First, we recall the following scaling properties of the fractional Sobolev norm defined in
eq.

1
. Let
\Lambda
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mu\in H^{s}(\Lambda)
with
s\in(0,1)
. For
\lambda>0
we denote by
\Lambda_{\lambda}
the submanifold obtained from rescaling
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by the factor
\lambda
. Similarly, we write
x_{\lambda}=\lambda x
and denote by
\mu_{\lambda}
the push-forward of
\mu
defined by

\displaystyle\mu_{\lambda}(x_{\lambda})=\mu_{\lambda}(\lambda x)\coloneqq\mu(x),\quad x\in\Lambda.

Then, using the usual change of variable formula, we find

\displaystyle||\mu_{\lambda}||_{0,\Lambda_{\lambda}}^{2}=\int_{\Lambda_{\lambda}}|\mu_{\lambda}(x_{\lambda})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s(x_{\lambda})=\lambda^{n-1}\int_{\Lambda}|\mu(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s(x)=\lambda^{n-1}||\mu||_{0,\Lambda}^{2}.

(18)

Similarly, we find

\displaystyle|\mu_{\lambda}|_{s,\Lambda_{\lambda}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Lambda_{\lambda}}\int_{\Lambda_{\lambda}}\frac{|\mu_{\lambda}(x_{\lambda})-\mu_{\lambda}(y_{\lambda})|^{2}}{|x_{\lambda}-y_{\lambda}|^{n-1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}s(x_{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}s(y_{\lambda})

(19)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2(n-1)}}{\lambda^{n-1+2s}}\int_{\Lambda}\int_{\Lambda}\frac{|\mu(x)-\mu(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n-1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}s(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)=\lambda^{n-1-2s}|\mu|_{s,\Lambda}^{2}.

These observations allow us to formulate the following statement:

Lemma 13
.

Let
\Lambda
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mu\in H^{s}(\Lambda)
with

. Denote by
\hat{\Lambda}
the same submanifold rescaled to unit size such that

and by
\hat{\mu}
the push-forward of
\mu
to

. Then, we have

\displaystyle||\mu||_{0,\Lambda}=|\Lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}||\hat{\mu}||_{0,\hat{\Lambda}}

and

\displaystyle|\mu|_{s,\Lambda}=|\Lambda|^{\frac{1+2s-n}{2(n-1)}}|\hat{\mu}|_{s,\hat{\Lambda}}.

Proof.

We obtain
\hat{\Lambda}
by rescaling
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by the factor
|\Lambda|^{\frac{-1}{n-1}}
. Replacing
\lambda
by

in

19

A.1
Poincaré and interpolation inequalities

In this section, we state useful inequalities in trace spaces for reference with explicit dependence of any constant on the size of the underlying piece of the boundary. The following Poincaré-type inequalities stem from
[
8
]
, while the interpolation-type inequalities in
lemma

16
are partially new and require a proof.

Lemma 14
(Lemma 2.3.6. in
[
8
]
).

Assume that
\Lambda\subset\partial\Omega
for some Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
\Lambda
is isotropic according to
definition

3
and connected, and
\partial\Lambda\neq\emptyset
. Then, for all
\mu\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)
,

\displaystyle||\mu||_{0,\Lambda}\lesssim|\Lambda|^{\frac{1}{2(n-1)}}|\mu^{\star}|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}\leq|\Lambda|^{\frac{1}{2(n-1)}}||\mu||_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}.

\partial\Lambda\neq\emptyset
. Indeed, if
\Lambda
is a closed curve, the space
H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)
contains non-trivial constants, and therefore, the inequality in

does not hold. Nevertheless, the following Poincaré-type inequality holds:

Lemma 15
(Lemma 2.3.8. in

8
]
).

Assume that
\Lambda
is isotropic according to
definition

3
. Then, for all
\mu\in H^{1/2}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle||\mu-\overline{\mu}^{\Lambda}||_{0,\Lambda}\lesssim|\Lambda|^{\frac{1}{2(n-1)}}|\mu|_{1/2,\Lambda}.

The following result allows us to express the defeaturing error estimates in terms of easily computable quantities:

Lemma 16
.

Assume that
\Lambda
is isotropic according to
definition

3
. Then, for all

(20)

where
\nabla_{t}\mu
denotes the gradient in tangential direction.
Moreover, if
\mu\in H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)
, we have

\displaystyle|\mu|_{H^{1/2}_{00}(\Lambda)}\lesssim\sqrt{||\mu||_{0,\Lambda}||\nabla_{t}\mu||_{0,\Lambda}}.

(21)

Proof.

Denote by
\hat{\Lambda}
the same boundary
\Lambda
rescaled to unit size and consider the push-forward
\hat{\mu}\in H^{1/2}(\hat{\Lambda})
as in

13
.
First, we show that
eq.

20
and
eq.

21
for the rescaled
\hat{\Lambda}
. Then, we show that the constants only depend on the shape of
\Lambda
, but not its size.

For the first step, we recall the interpolation property of fractional Sobolev spaces, which also holds on Lipschitz manifolds; see
[
37
,
38

33
]
. That is, we have

\displaystyle H^{1/2}(\hat{\Lambda})=[L^{2}(\hat{\Lambda}),H^{1}(\hat{\Lambda})]_{1/2},

[\cdot,\cdot]_{s}
denotes the complex interpolation functor for function spaces. The interpolation property implies in particular the existence of a constant

, such that

\displaystyle|\hat{\mu}-\overline{\hat{\mu}}^{\hat{\Lambda}}|_{1/2,\hat{\Lambda}}\leq C_{i}(\hat{\Lambda})\sqrt{||\hat{\mu}-\overline{\hat{\mu}}^{\hat{\Lambda}}||_{0,\hat{\Lambda}}||\hat{\mu}-\overline{\hat{\mu}}^{\hat{\Lambda}}||_{1,\hat{\Lambda}}},

(22)

\displaystyle|\hat{\mu}^{\star}|_{1/2,\partial\hat{\Omega}}\leq C_{00}(\hat{\Lambda})\sqrt{||\hat{\mu}||_{0,\hat{\Lambda}}||\nabla_{t}\hat{\mu}||_{0,\hat{\Lambda}}},

\displaystyle||\mu-\overline{\mu}^{\Lambda}||_{0,\Lambda}

\displaystyle=|\Lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}||\hat{\mu}-\overline{\hat{\mu}}^{\hat{\Lambda}}||_{0,\hat{\Lambda}},

\displaystyle|\mu|_{1/2,\Lambda}

\displaystyle=|\Lambda|^{\frac{2-n}{2(n-1)}}|\hat{\mu}|_{1/2,\hat{\Lambda}},

\displaystyle|\mu-\overline{\mu}^{\Lambda}|_{1/2,\Lambda}

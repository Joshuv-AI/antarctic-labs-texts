Letter to the editor: TGF-β1 dominates stromal fibroblast-mediated EMT via the FAP/VCAN axis in bladder cancer cells
Authors: Yongzheng Han, Zunwen Zheng, Xiaorui Li, Weiwei Fan
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We are writing to comment on the recent article by Ping et al., titled " TGF-β1 dominates stromal fibroblast-mediated EMT via the FAP/VCAN axis in bladder cancer cells [1],” published in Journal of Translational Medicine. This study elucidated the specific mechanism by which Transforming Growth Factor beta 1 (TGF-β1) secreted by cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) activates the FAP/VCAN pathway, thereby driving epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in bladder cancer (BCa). Moreover, Ping et al. identified FAP, VCAN, N-cadherin and vimentin as potential biomarkers for BCa. This study provided a solid foundation for targeting stromal-driven BCa metastasis.

Although this study successfully established the TGF-β1/FAP/VCAN axis both in vitro and in vivo, future work can promote clinical translation by investigating the heterogeneity of CAF. Single-cell sequencing analysis revealed unique CAFs populations in different cancers and helped define key CAF subtypes, such as myofibroblastic, inflammatory, and antigen-presenting CAFs [2], which play various roles in tumor development. By using single-cell RNA sequencing of TCGA-BLCA cohort to explore whether the TGF-β1/FAP/VCAN axis is enriched in specific CAF subgroups, patient classification can be refined to achieve better therapeutic efficacy (See Fig. 1).

Illustration of the current discoveries and potential issues regarding BCa. This figure highlights how TGF-β1/FAP/VCAN axis drive EMT in BCa. Moreover, this figure also proposes potential future research directions. These include conducting patient-specific stratification through single-cell analysis of the TCGA-BLCA cohort, and verifying the connection between this axis and the tumor microenvironment in humanized mouse model

While this study offers important insights into direct CAF-BCa cell interaction, more research is needed to understand its effects on the tumor immune microenvironment. TGF-β1 strongly inhibits T cell function [3] and enables Tregs to suppress other immune cells [4]. Ping et al. used subcutaneous xenograft models which lack a functional immune system. These models cannot recapitulate the complex human BCa immune microenvironment. Wang et al. studied PD-L1 targeted immunotherapy using humanized mouse models [5]. These models contain human immune system and mimic the human tumor microenvironment better than subcutaneous xenograft models. Further studies should use humanized mouse models to examine how the TGF-β1/FAP/VCAN axis affects BCa’s immune microenvironment. This approach could help develop new treatment strategy for BCa.

In conclusion, we commend the excellent study by Ping et al. and sincerely hope that these supplements could promote the clinical translation of BCa. We also thank the Journal of Translational Medicine for publishing this valuable study.

Sincerely.

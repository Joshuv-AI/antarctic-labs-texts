Two-sided crossed products
Florin Panaite

Abstract
Given two associative algebras A A , C C and a linear space V V together with some linear maps R 1 R_{1} , R 2 R_{2} , R 3 R_{3} , E E satisfying some conditions, we define an associative algebra structure on A ⊗ V ⊗ C A\otimes V\otimes C called a two-sided crossed product. Particular cases of this construction are the iterated twisted tensor product of algebras and the two-sided crossed product over a quasi-bialgebra. Keywords : twisted tensor product, Brzeziński crossed product, iterated crossed product, quasi-bialgebra MSC2020 : 16S35, 16T99



Introduction

If
A
and
B
are associative unital algebras and
R:B\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes B
is a linear map satisfying certain conditions (such an
R
is called a twisting map)
then
A\otimes B
becomes an associative unital algebra with a multiplication defined
in terms of
R
and the multiplications of
A
and
B
; this algebra structure is denoted
by
A\otimes_{R}B
and called the twisted tensor product of
A
and
B
afforded by
R

(cf.
[
3
]
,
[
13
]
). Twisted tensor products of algebras appear in various algebraic and geometrical contexts (see for instance
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
and references therein). One of their nice features is that they can be iterated: given three twisted tensor products

A\otimes_{R_{1}}B
,
B\otimes_{R_{2}}C
and
A\otimes_{R_{3}}C
, it has been proved in
[
7
]

that
a sufficient condition for being able to define certain twisting maps

T_{1}:C\otimes(A\otimes_{R_{1}}B)\rightarrow(A\otimes_{R_{1}}B)\otimes C
and

T_{2}:(B\otimes_{R_{2}}C)\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes(B\otimes_{R_{2}}C)
associated
to
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
,
R_{3}
and ensuring that the algebras
A\otimes_{T_{2}}(B\otimes_{R_{2}}C)

and
(A\otimes_{R_{1}}B)\otimes_{T_{1}}C
are equal (this algebra is called the iterated
twisted tensor product), can be given in terms of the maps
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
,
R_{3}
,
namely, they have to satisfy the braid relation
(id_{A}\otimes R_{2})\circ(R_{3}\otimes id_{B})\circ(id_{C}\otimes R_{1})=(R_{1}\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{B}\otimes R_{3})\circ(R_{2}\otimes id_{A})
.

An important and substantial generalization of twisted tensor products of algebras is the so called Brzeziński crossed product, introduced in
[
1
]
(it contains as well as particular case the classical Hopf crossed
product).
Given an associative unital algebra
A
, a vector space
V
endowed with a
distinguished element
1_{V}
and two linear maps
\sigma:V\otimes V\rightarrow A\otimes V
and
R:V\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes V

satisfying certain conditions, Brzeziński’s crossed product is a certain
associative unital algebra structure on
A\otimes V
, denoted in what follows by

A\otimes_{R,\sigma}V
. In
[
10
]
was introduced a mirror version of the Brzeziński crossed product, denoted by

W\overline{\otimes}_{P,\nu}D

(where
D
is an associative algebra,
W
is a vector space and
P
,
\nu
are certain
maps), and it was proved that under certain circumstances Brzeziński crossed products can be iterated, in the following sense:
if
W\overline{\otimes}_{P,\nu}D

and
D\otimes_{R,\sigma}V
are two crossed products and
Q:V\otimes W\rightarrow W\otimes D\otimes V
is a linear map satisfying certain conditions, then one can define
certain maps
\overline{\sigma}
,
\overline{R}
,
\overline{\nu}
,
\overline{P}
such
that we have the crossed products
(W\overline{\otimes}_{P,\nu}D)\otimes_{\overline{R},\overline{\sigma}}V
and
W\overline{\otimes}_{\overline{P},\overline{\nu}}(D\otimes_{R,\sigma}V)
that are moreover equal as algebras (this algebra structure is called
the iterated crossed product). Particular cases of this situation are the iterated twisted tensor products of algebras and the two-sided smash product over a quasi-bialgebra.

The aim of this paper is to introduce a different type of iterating Brzeziński crossed products. Namely, if
A
and
C
are associative unital algebras and
V
is a linear space endowed with a distinguished nonzero element, and we have linear maps

R_{1}:V\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes V
,
R_{2}:C\otimes V\rightarrow V\otimes C
,
R_{3}:C\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes C
and
E:V\otimes V\rightarrow A\otimes V\otimes C
satisfying some conditions, then we can define a certain Brzeziński crossed product
A\otimes_{R,\sigma}(V\otimes C)
, a certain mirror version Brzeziński crossed product
(A\otimes V)\overline{\otimes}_{P,\nu}C
and we have an algebra isomorphism between them given by the trivial identification. This algebra structure is denoted by
A\mbox{${\;}$$>\hskip-4.83694pt\triangleleft$${\;}$}V\mbox{${\;}$$\triangleright\hskip-4.55244pt<$${\;}$}C
and is called the
two-sided crossed product
afforded by the maps
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
,
R_{3}
,
E
. This result admits also a converse. Particular cases are the iterated twisted tensor products of algebras, the so called two-sided crossed product over a quasi-bialgebra from
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
and also a recent construction from
[
8
]
.

1
Preliminaries

We work over a commutative field
k
. All algebras, linear spaces
etc. will be over
k
; unadorned
\otimes
means
\otimes_{k}
. By ”algebra” we
always mean an associative unital algebra. The multiplication
of an algebra
A
is denoted by
\mu_{A}
or simply
\mu
when
there is no danger of confusion, and we usually denote

\mu_{A}(a\otimes a^{\prime})=aa^{\prime}
for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A
. The unit of
A
is denoted by
1_{A}
.

We recall from
[
3
]
,
[
13
]
that, given two algebras
A
,
B

and a
k
-linear map
R:B\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes B
, with notation

R(b\otimes a)=a_{R}\otimes b_{R}
, for
a\in A
,
b\in B
, satisfying the conditions

a_{R}\otimes 1_{R}=a\otimes 1
,
1_{R}\otimes b_{R}=1\otimes b
,

(aa^{\prime})_{R}\otimes b_{R}=a_{R}a^{\prime}_{r}\otimes b_{R_{r}}
,

a_{R}\otimes(bb^{\prime})_{R}=a_{R_{r}}\otimes b_{r}b^{\prime}_{R}
,
for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A
and
b,b^{\prime}\in B
(where
r
and
R
are two different indices),
if we define on
A\otimes B
a new multiplication, by

(a\otimes b)(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})=aa^{\prime}_{R}\otimes b_{R}b^{\prime}
, then this multiplication is associative
with unit
1\otimes 1
. In this case, the map
R
is called
a
twisting map
between
A
and
B
and the new algebra
structure on
A\otimes B
is denoted by
A\otimes_{R}B
and called the

twisted tensor product
of
A
and
B
afforded by the map
R
.

We recall from
[
1
]
the construction of
Brzeziński’s crossed product:

Proposition 1.1

(
[
1
]
)
Let
(A,\mu,1_{A})
be an (associative unital) algebra and
V
a
vector space equipped with a distinguished element
1_{V}\in V
. Then
the vector space
A\otimes V
is an associative algebra with unit
1_{A}\otimes 1_{V}

and whose multiplication has the property that
(a\otimes 1_{V})(b\otimes v)=ab\otimes v
, for all
a,b\in A
and
v\in V
, if and only if there exist
linear maps
\sigma:V\otimes V\rightarrow A\otimes V
and

R:V\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes V
satisfying the following conditions:

\displaystyle R(1_{V}\otimes a)=a\otimes 1_{V},\;\;\;R(v\otimes 1_{A})=1_{A}\otimes v,\;\;\;\forall\;a\in A,\;v\in V,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sigma(1_{V}\otimes v)=\sigma(v\otimes 1_{V})=1_{A}\otimes v,\;\;\;\forall\;v\in V,

(1.2)

\displaystyle R\circ(id_{V}\otimes\mu)=(\mu\otimes id_{V})\circ(id_{A}\otimes R)\circ(R\otimes id_{A}),

(1.3)

\displaystyle(\mu\otimes id_{V})\circ(id_{A}\otimes\sigma)\circ(R\otimes id_{V})\circ(id_{V}\otimes\sigma)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=(\mu\otimes id_{V})\circ(id_{A}\otimes\sigma)\circ(\sigma\otimes id_{V}),

(1.4)

\displaystyle(\mu\otimes id_{V})\circ(id_{A}\otimes\sigma)\circ(R\otimes id_{V})\circ(id_{V}\otimes R)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=(\mu\otimes id_{V})\circ(id_{A}\otimes R)\circ(\sigma\otimes id_{A}).

(1.5)

If this is the case, the multiplication of
A\otimes V
is given explicitly by

\displaystyle\mu_{A\otimes V}=(\mu_{2}\otimes id_{V})\circ(id_{A}\otimes id_{A}\otimes\sigma)\circ(id_{A}\otimes R\otimes id_{V}),

where
\mu_{2}=\mu\circ(id_{A}\otimes\mu)=\mu\circ(\mu\otimes id_{A})
.
We denote by
A\otimes_{R,\sigma}V
this algebra structure and
call it the crossed product afforded by the data
(A,V,R,\sigma)
.

If
A\otimes_{R,\sigma}V
is a crossed product, we use the
Sweedler-type notation
R(v\otimes a)=a_{R}\otimes v_{R}
,
\sigma(v\otimes v^{\prime})=\sigma_{1}(v,v^{\prime})\otimes\sigma_{2}(v,v^{\prime})
,
for all
v,v^{\prime}\in V
and
a\in A
. With this notation, the multiplication of

A\otimes_{R,\sigma}V
reads

\displaystyle(a\otimes v)(a^{\prime}\otimes v^{\prime})=aa^{\prime}_{R}\sigma_{1}(v_{R},v^{\prime})\otimes\sigma_{2}(v_{R},v^{\prime}),\;\;\;\forall\;a,a^{\prime}\in A,\;v,v^{\prime}\in V.

A twisted tensor product is a particular case of a crossed product
(cf.
[
4
]
), namely, if
A\otimes_{R}B
is a twisted tensor product of
algebras then
A\otimes_{R}B=A\otimes_{R,\sigma}B
, where

\sigma:B\otimes B\rightarrow A\otimes B

is given by
\sigma(b\otimes b^{\prime})=1_{A}\otimes bb^{\prime}
, for all
b,b^{\prime}\in B
.

We recall also from
[
10
]
the mirror version of Brzeziński’s crossed product:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
10
]
)
Let
(B,\mu,1_{B})
be an (associative unital) algebra and

W
a vector space equipped with a distinguished element

1_{W}\in W
. Then the vector space
W\otimes B
is an associative
algebra with unit
1_{W}\otimes 1_{B}
and whose
multiplication has the property that

(w\otimes b)(1_{W}\otimes b^{\prime})=w\otimes bb^{\prime}
, for all
b,b^{\prime}\in B
and

w\in W
, if and only if there exist linear maps

\nu:W\otimes W\rightarrow W\otimes B
and

P:B\otimes W\rightarrow W\otimes B
satisfying the following conditions:

\displaystyle P(b\otimes 1_{W})=1_{W}\otimes b,\;\;\;P(1_{B}\otimes w)=w\otimes 1_{B},\;\;\;\forall\;b\in B,\;w\in W,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\nu(w\otimes 1_{W})=\nu(1_{W}\otimes w)=w\otimes 1_{B},\;\;\;\forall\;w\in W,

(1.7)

\displaystyle P\circ(\mu\otimes id_{W})=(id_{W}\otimes\mu)\circ(P\otimes id_{B})\circ(id_{B}\otimes P),

(1.8)

\displaystyle(id_{W}\otimes\mu)\circ(\nu\otimes id_{B})\circ(id_{W}\otimes P)\circ(\nu\otimes id_{W})

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=(id_{W}\otimes\mu)\circ(\nu\otimes id_{B})\circ(id_{W}\otimes\nu),

(1.9)

\displaystyle(id_{W}\otimes\mu)\circ(\nu\otimes id_{B})\circ(id_{W}\otimes P)\circ(P\otimes id_{W})

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=(id_{W}\otimes\mu)\circ(P\otimes id_{B})\circ(id_{B}\otimes\nu).

(1.10)

If this is the case, the multiplication of
W\otimes B
is given
explicitely by

\displaystyle\mu_{W\otimes B}=(id_{W}\otimes\mu_{2})\circ(\nu\otimes id_{B}\otimes id_{B})\circ(id_{W}\otimes P\otimes id_{B}),

where
\mu_{2}=\mu\circ(id_{B}\otimes\mu)=\mu\circ(\mu\otimes id_{B})
. We denote by

W\overline{\otimes}_{P,\nu}B
this algebra structure
and call it the crossed product
afforded by the data
(W,B,P,\nu)
.

If
W\overline{\otimes}_{P,\nu}B
is a crossed product, we use also the
Sweedler-type notation
P(b\otimes w)=w_{P}\otimes b_{P}
,
\nu(w\otimes w^{\prime})=\nu_{1}(w,w^{\prime})\otimes\nu_{2}(w,w^{\prime})
,
for all
w,w^{\prime}\in W
and
b\in B
. With this notation,
the multiplication of
W\overline{\otimes}_{P,\nu}B
reads

\displaystyle(w\otimes b)(w^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})=\nu_{1}(w,w^{\prime}_{P})\otimes\nu_{2}(w,w^{\prime}_{P})b_{P}b^{\prime},\;\;\;\forall\;b,b^{\prime}\in B,\;w,w^{\prime}\in W.

If
A\otimes_{R}B
is a twisted tensor product product of algebras,
then
A\otimes_{R}B=A\overline{\otimes}_{R,\nu}B
, where

\nu:A\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes B
,

\nu(a\otimes a^{\prime})=aa^{\prime}\otimes 1_{B}
for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A
.

2
The definition of the two-sided crossed product

Theorem 2.1

Let
(A,\mu_{A},1_{A})
and
(C,\mu_{C},1_{C})
be two associative unital algebras and
V
a linear space equipped with a distinguished nonzero element
1_{V}\in V
. Assume that we are given linear maps (with respective notation)

\displaystyle R_{1}:V\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes V,\;\;\;R_{1}(v\otimes a)=a_{R_{1}}\otimes v_{R_{1}}=a_{r_{1}}\otimes v_{r_{1}},

\displaystyle R_{2}:C\otimes V\rightarrow V\otimes C,\;\;\;R_{2}(c\otimes v)=v_{R_{2}}\otimes c_{R_{2}}=v_{r_{2}}\otimes c_{r_{2}},

\displaystyle R_{3}:C\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes C,\;\;\;R_{3}(c\otimes a)=a_{R_{3}}\otimes c_{R_{3}}=a_{r_{3}}\otimes c_{r_{3}},

\displaystyle E:V\otimes V\rightarrow A\otimes V\otimes C,\;\;\;E(v\otimes v^{\prime})=E_{A}(v,v^{\prime})\otimes E_{V}(v,v^{\prime})\otimes E_{C}(v,v^{\prime}),

for all
a\in A
,
v,v^{\prime}\in V
,
c\in C
, such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)
R_{3}
is a twisting map between
A
and
C
, that is, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A
and
c,c^{\prime}\in C
:

\displaystyle R_{3}(c\otimes 1_{A})=1_{A}\otimes c,\;\;\;R_{3}(1_{C}\otimes a)=a\otimes 1_{C},

(2.1)

\displaystyle(aa^{\prime})_{R_{3}}\otimes c_{R_{3}}=a_{R_{3}}a^{\prime}_{r_{3}}\otimes(c_{R_{3}})_{r_{3}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle a_{R_{3}}\otimes(cc^{\prime})_{R_{3}}=(a_{R_{3}})_{r_{3}}\otimes c_{r_{3}}c^{\prime}_{R_{3}};

(2.3)

(ii)
R_{1}(1_{V}\otimes a)=a\otimes 1_{V}
,
R_{1}(v\otimes 1_{A})=1_{A}\otimes v
, for all
a\in A
,
v\in V
;

(iii)
R_{2}(c\otimes 1_{V})=1_{V}\otimes c
,
R_{2}(1_{C}\otimes v)=v\otimes 1_{C}
, for all
v\in V
,
c\in C
;

(iv)
E(1_{V}\otimes v)=E(v\otimes 1_{V})=1_{A}\otimes v\otimes 1_{C}
, for all
v\in V
;

(v) the following relations are satisfied:

\displaystyle R_{1}\circ(id_{V}\otimes\mu_{A})=(\mu_{A}\otimes id_{V})\circ(id_{A}\otimes R_{1})\circ(R_{1}\otimes id_{A}),

(2.4)

\displaystyle R_{2}\circ(\mu_{C}\otimes id_{V})=(id_{V}\otimes\mu_{C})\circ(R_{2}\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{C}\otimes R_{2}),

(2.5)

\displaystyle(id_{A}\otimes R_{2})\circ(R_{3}\otimes id_{V})\circ(id_{C}\otimes R_{1})=(R_{1}\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{V}\otimes R_{3})\circ(R_{2}\otimes id_{A}),

(2.6)

\displaystyle(\mu_{A}\otimes id_{V}\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{A}\otimes E)\circ(R_{1}\otimes id_{V})\circ(id_{V}\otimes R_{1})

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=(\mu_{A}\otimes id_{V}\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{A}\otimes R_{1}\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{A}\otimes id_{V}\otimes R_{3})\circ(E\otimes id_{A}),

(2.7)

\displaystyle(id_{A}\otimes id_{V}\otimes\mu_{C})\circ(E\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{V}\otimes R_{2})\circ(R_{2}\otimes id_{V})

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=(id_{A}\otimes id_{V}\otimes\mu_{C})\circ(id_{A}\otimes R_{2}\otimes id_{C})\circ(R_{3}\otimes id_{V}\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{C}\otimes E),

(2.8)

\displaystyle(\mu_{A}\otimes id_{V}\otimes\mu_{C})\circ(id_{A}\otimes E\otimes id_{C})\circ(R_{1}\otimes id_{V}\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{V}\otimes E)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=(\mu_{A}\otimes id_{V}\otimes\mu_{C})\circ(id_{A}\otimes E\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{A}\otimes id_{V}\otimes R_{2})\circ(E\otimes id_{V}).

(2.9)

Define the linear maps

\displaystyle R:(V\otimes C)\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes(V\otimes C),\;\;\;R=(R_{1}\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{V}\otimes R_{3}),

\displaystyle P:C\otimes(A\otimes V)\rightarrow(A\otimes V)\otimes C,\;\;\;P=(id_{A}\otimes R_{2})\circ(R_{3}\otimes id_{V}),

\displaystyle\sigma:(V\otimes C)\otimes(V\otimes C)\rightarrow A\otimes(V\otimes C),

\displaystyle\sigma=(id_{A}\otimes id_{V}\otimes\mu_{C})\circ(E\otimes\mu_{C})\circ(id_{V}\otimes R_{2}\otimes id_{C}),

\displaystyle\nu:(A\otimes V)\otimes(A\otimes V)\rightarrow(A\otimes V)\otimes C,

\displaystyle\nu=(\mu_{A}\otimes id_{V}\otimes id_{C})\circ(\mu_{A}\otimes E)\circ(id_{A}\otimes R_{1}\otimes id_{V}),

that is

\displaystyle R((v\otimes c)\otimes a)=(a_{R_{3}})_{R_{1}}\otimes(v_{R_{1}}\otimes c_{R_{3}}),

\displaystyle P(c\otimes(a\otimes v))=(a_{R_{3}}\otimes v_{R_{2}})\otimes(c_{R_{3}})_{R_{2}},

\displaystyle\sigma((v\otimes c)\otimes(v^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime}))=E_{A}(v,v^{\prime}_{R_{2}})\otimes(E_{V}(v,v^{\prime}_{R_{2}})\otimes E_{C}(v,v^{\prime}_{R_{2}})c_{R_{2}}c^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\nu((a\otimes v)\otimes(a^{\prime}\otimes v^{\prime}))=(aa^{\prime}_{R_{1}}E_{A}(v_{R_{1}},v^{\prime})\otimes E_{V}(v_{R_{1}},v^{\prime}))\otimes E_{C}(v_{R_{1}},v^{\prime}),

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A
,
v,v^{\prime}\in V
,
c,c^{\prime}\in C
. Then we have the crossed products
A\otimes_{R,\sigma}(V\otimes C)
and

(A\otimes V)\overline{\otimes}_{P,\nu}C
and an algebra isomorphism between them given by the trivial identification.

This associative unital algebra structure on
A\otimes V\otimes C
will be denoted by

A\mbox{${\;}$$>\hskip-4.83694pt\triangleleft$${\;}$}V\mbox{${\;}$$\triangleright\hskip-4.55244pt<$${\;}$}C
and will be called a two-sided crossed product (afforded by the maps
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
,
R_{3}
,
E
). Its unit is
1_{A}\otimes 1_{V}\otimes 1_{C}
and its multiplication is (for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A
,
v,v^{\prime}\in V
,
c,c^{\prime}\in C
)

\displaystyle(a\otimes v\otimes c)(a^{\prime}\otimes v^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime})=a(a^{\prime}_{R_{3}})_{R_{1}}E_{A}(v_{R_{1}},v^{\prime}_{R_{2}})\otimes E_{V}(v_{R_{1}},v^{\prime}_{R_{2}})\otimes E_{C}(v_{R_{1}},v^{\prime}_{R_{2}})(c_{R_{3}})_{R_{2}}c^{\prime}.

Sometimes we will denote
a\mbox{${\;}$$>\hskip-4.83694pt\triangleleft$${\;}$}v\mbox{${\;}$$\triangleright\hskip-4.55244pt<$${\;}$}c
instead of
a\otimes v\otimes c
.

Proof.

Note first that the relations (
2.4
)-(
2.9
) are respectively equivalent (by writing them down on elements) to the following relations (for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A
,
v,v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\in V
,
c,c^{\prime}\in C

\displaystyle(aa^{\prime})_{R_{1}}\otimes v_{R_{1}}=a_{R_{1}}a^{\prime}_{r_{1}}\otimes(v_{R_{1}})_{r_{1}},

(2.10)

\displaystyle v_{R_{2}}\otimes(cc^{\prime})_{R_{2}}=(v_{R_{2}})_{r_{2}}\otimes c_{r_{2}}c^{\prime}_{R_{2}},

(2.11)

\displaystyle(a_{R_{1}})_{R_{3}}\otimes(v_{R_{1}})_{R_{2}}\otimes(c_{R_{3}})_{R_{2}}=(a_{R_{3}})_{R_{1}}\otimes(v_{R_{2}})_{R_{1}}\otimes(c_{R_{2}})_{R_{3}},

(2.12)

\displaystyle(a_{R_{1}})_{r_{1}}E_{A}(v_{r_{1}},v^{\prime}_{R_{1}})\otimes E_{V}(v_{r_{1}},v^{\prime}_{R_{1}})\otimes E_{C}(v_{r_{1}},v^{\prime}_{R_{1}})

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=E_{A}(v,v^{\prime})(a_{R_{3}})_{R_{1}}\otimes E_{V}(v,v^{\prime})_{R_{1}}\otimes E_{C}(v,v^{\prime})_{R_{3}},

(2.13)

\displaystyle E_{A}(v_{R_{2}},v^{\prime}_{r_{2}})\otimes E_{V}(v_{R_{2}},v^{\prime}_{r_{2}})\otimes E_{C}(v_{R_{2}},v^{\prime}_{r_{2}})(c_{R_{2}})_{r_{2}}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=E_{A}(v,v^{\prime})_{R_{3}}\otimes E_{V}(v,v^{\prime})_{R_{2}}\otimes(c_{R_{3}})_{R_{2}}E_{C}(v,v^{\prime}),

(2.14)

\displaystyle E_{A}(v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})_{R_{1}}E_{A}(v_{R_{1}},E_{V}(v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}))\otimes E_{V}(v_{R_{1}},E_{V}(v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}))

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\otimes E_{C}(v_{R_{1}},E_{V}(v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}))E_{C}(v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=E_{A}(v,v^{\prime})E_{A}(E_{V}(v,v^{\prime}),v^{\prime\prime}_{R_{2}})\otimes E_{V}(E_{V}(v,v^{\prime}),v^{\prime\prime}_{R_{2}})

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\otimes E_{C}(E_{V}(v,v^{\prime}),v^{\prime\prime}_{R_{2}})E_{C}(v,v^{\prime})_{R_{2}}.

(2.15)

We prove now that we have the crossed product

, the proof for

Proof of (
1.3
)
:

(\mu_{A}\otimes id_{V}\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{A}\otimes R)\circ(R\otimes id_{A})(v\otimes c\otimes a\otimes a^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu_{A}\otimes id_{V}\otimes id_{C})\circ(id_{A}\otimes R)((a_{R_{3}})_{R_{1}}\otimes v_{R_{1}}\otimes c_{R_{3}}\otimes a^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{R_{3}})_{R_{1}}(a^{\prime}_{R_{3}})_{r_{1}}\otimes(v_{R_{1}})_{r_{1}}\otimes(c_{R_{3}})_{r_{3}}

Proof of (

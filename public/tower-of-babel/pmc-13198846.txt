Inhibition of return mediates the relationship between openness to experience and visuospatial ability
Authors: Yuhang Duan, Jin Lin, Mingkang Song, Zhiqing Dai, Fei Teng, Hongpeng Lv, Hongxiao Jia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOpenness to experience—a core dimension of the Big Five personality framework—has been increasingly linked to cognitive functioning, though the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Visuospatial ability (VA), a key cognitive domain involved in perception, memory, and problem-solving, has shown associations with Openness in prior research. Inhibition of return (IOR), a bottom-up attentional mechanism that slows responses to recently examined locations to promote orienting toward new ones, may serve as a cognitive intermediary by optimizing how visual attention is dynamically allocated.ObjectiveThis study investigated whether IOR mediates the relationship between Openness and VA, offering new insights into personality-related differences in attentional processing.MethodsA total of 290 healthy adults completed standardized assessments of personality traits (NEO-FFI), cognitive function (RBANS), and a MATLAB-based exogenous cueing paradigm to quantify IOR. △ LRT_s, a transformed metric representing IOR strength, was calculated as the response time difference between valid and invalid trials. Group comparisons, correlation analyses, and mediation models were conducted using SPSS and the PROCESS macro, controlling for age, gender, and education.ResultsParticipants with high Openness scores demonstrated significantly higher VA and stronger IOR effects (i.e., larger △ LRT_s) than those with low Openness (both p < 0.001). Correlational analyses revealed positive associations among Openness, △ LRT_s, and VA. Mediation analysis confirmed that △ LRT_s partially mediated the relationship between Openness and VA (indirect effect: B = 0.058, 95% CI [0.011–0.120]), suggesting that IOR contributes to the visuospatial advantage observed in individuals high in Openness.ConclusionsThis study provides novel evidence that IOR serves as a mediating attentional mechanism linking Openness to enhanced visuospatial performance. These findings highlight the dynamic interaction between personality traits and low-level cognitive processes, suggesting that IOR may represent a neurocognitive pathway through which Openness facilitates efficient attentional allocation. The results have implications for understanding individual differences in cognitive functioning.

Introduction

The relationship between cognitive functioning and personality traits has garnered increasing empirical attention in recent years (Chapman et al., 2017; Marcolini et al., 2023; Stanek & Ones, 2023; Stephan et al., 2020; Sutin, Stephan & Terracciano, 2019; Wettstein, Tauber & Wahl, 2020). Accumulating evidence from cross-sectional investigations (Coors et al., 2024; Simon, Lee & Stern, 2020), longitudinal studies (Stephan et al., 2024; Sutin et al., 2023a; Sutin et al., 2023b), and meta-analytic reviews (DeYoung, 2015b; Kaufman et al., 2016) demonstrates significant associations between big five factor model personality dimensions and cognitive performance. Notably, neuroticism shows negative correlations while conscientiousness and openness exhibit positive relationships with memory performance metrics (Sutin et al., 2023a). Openness to Experience, also termed openness, a core dimension of the Big Five personality framework (McCrae & Costa Jr, 1996), reflects an individual’s proclivity for novel experiences, abstract thinking (including vivid fantasy, etc.), creative exploration (encompassing intellectual curiosity, etc.), and openness to newness (including receptiveness to new ideas and artistic sensitivity, etc.) (Costa & McCrae, 1989; McCrae, 1996; McCrae & Costa Jr, 1996). Contemporary research has consistently linked heightened Openness with superior cognitive performance (Stolz et al., 2023; Weinstein et al., 2019; Wyche, Edwards & Goodhew, 2024), including enhanced memory functioning (Luchetti et al., 2021; Sutin et al., 2021) and distinctive neurocognitive processing patterns (Christensen, Cotter & Silvia, 2019). In contrast, those with low Openness tend to adhere to traditional and familiar patterns. The cognitive advantages associated with Openness may be rooted in its relationship with cognitive exploration and flexibility (DeYoung, 2015b). From a theoretical perspective, Openness can be conceptualized as a trait that modulates transient states of cognitive mobilization, influencing how individuals engage with and process information (Colzato et al., 2006). This may manifest in attentional processes, where Openness is linked to a broader distribution of spatial attention and enhanced exploratory behavior (Wilson et al., 2016). Clinical studies indicate a close link between openness and cognitive flexibility/creativity, suggesting it may enhance cognitive reserve to delay neurodegenerative disease progression (e.g., Alzheimer’s disease) (Ayers, Gulley & Verghese, 2020; Kim et al., 2024; Mathys et al., 2024). Socially, openness propels cultural diversity, technological innovation, and cross-domain collaboration, emerging as a key psychological resource for modern society’s adaptability (Caligiuri et al., 2020).

Among the many cognitive abilities associated with openness, Visuospatial ability (VA), as an important cognitive ability, is closely related to daily functioning, disease, career performance (Lang & Kell, 2020), and social cognition, such as emotion recognition (Suzuki et al., 2007). VA pertains to an individual’s ability to construct, retrieve, manipulate, and process mental representations of visual and spatial information (Mervis, Robinson & Pani, 1999; Sutin, Stephan & Terracciano, 2019). Early sleep disturbances in Parkinson’s disease are associated with rapid declines in cognitive domains, including verbal episodic memory, VA, executive function/working memory, language, and processing speed/attention (Zhong et al., 2024). Similarly, in patients with schizophrenia researchers found high VA (tested by RBANS) and attention scores as significant risk factors for suicidal ideation among middle-aged and elderly patients (Wang et al., 2023). Moreover, a positive association between disgust recognition and visuospatial ability has been found to demonstrate the importance of visuospatial ability in facial expression recognition. These findings establish VA as a critical cognitive domain, underscoring the need for mechanistic investigation into its underlying processes. These findings highlight VA as a critical cognitive domain requiring mechanistic investigation.

A key mechanism potentially linking Openness to VA is inhibition of return (IOR). It is evident that the extensive utilization of visuospatial plays a crucial role in enhancing work efficiency and social interaction. During the course of human evolution, to conserve attentional resources (such as VA, etc.), the IOR has emerged. IOR is an attentional inhibitory mechanism, characterized by delayed responses to previously attended spatial locations, which serves to optimize visual search efficiency by promoting orienting towards novel locations (Dai et al., 2018; Sapir et al., 1999; Wang et al., 2024; Klein, 2000; Posner & Cohen, 1984). This mechanism supports efficient attentional engagement and disengagement—core components of attentional control (Derakshan et al., 2009). IOR, as a dynamic regulatory mechanism of spatial attention, may enhance the accuracy of visual spatial analysis directly by suppressing interference from redundant information (Ibáñez Gijón & Jacobs, 2012; Seidel Malkinson et al., 2024; Wang & Theeuwes, 2024). Functionally, stronger IOR reflects not merely a longer reaction-time delay, but an enhancement in attentional efficiency—specifically, the ability to disengage more effectively from previously attended locations, thereby facilitating orientation toward novel stimuli. Previous studies have found that higher trait Openness is associated with a broader distribution of IOR and attention (Wilson et al., 2016). Some study have found that individuals with high openness, due to their greater cognitive flexibility, may modulate the strength of IOR, thereby influencing the execution strategy of visual-spatial tasks (Seidel Malkinson et al., 2024). This aligns with predictive processing theories, suggesting that cognitive flexibility associated with Openness may allow bottom-up sensory input to exert greater influence on spatial representations (Huang & Yin, 2025; Koivisto & Pallaris, 2024).

Emerging evidence indicates potential triadic associations between Openness to Experience, visuospatial processing capacities, and attentional modulation mechanisms, offering critical insights into personality-cognition interactions. Despite theoretical connections, a significant research gap remains regarding the potential mediating role of IOR in the relationship between Openness and VA. Therefore, the primary aim of this study was to investigate whether IOR, evaluated using the improved Posner spatial cueing task, mediates the association between Openness (measured by the NEO-FFI) and VA (assessed by the RBANS). To do so, we studied this mediation with both cross-sectional and longitudinal approaches. We hypothesized that, IOR would partially the association between openness and VA, such that higher Openness would be associated with stronger IOR, which in turn would be linked to better VA performance. This investigation aims to advance mechanistic understanding of personality-cognition interactions while informing neurocognitive models of individual differences.

Materials & Methods

Participants

This study recruited 290 healthy participants in Beijing from October 28, 2023, to December 12, 2023. Participants were required through public advertisements and on-site announcements at Beijing Anding Hospital and surrounding communities. All assessments were conducted at Beijing Anding Hospital, Capital Medical University, in a controlled laboratory setting.

Eligible participants met the following inclusion criteria: aged 18–55 years, right-handed; Self-Rating Anxiety Scale (SAS) <50 and Self-Rating Depression Scale (SDS) <50; educational attainment of high school or above; no history of cardiovascular, pulmonary, renal, hepatic, hematological, or other systemic diseases; voluntary and signed informed consent. Exclusion criteria: organic diseases or mental illnesses preventing cooperation in data collection; incomplete questionnaire data; inability to complete the study procedures; Withdrawal during the study.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Anding Hospital Capital Medical University (Ethical Application Ref: 2025123). Written informed consent was obtained. The study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. Each participant who completed the study received a remuneration of 100 RMB.

Study procedure

Participants visited the study site once. During the visit, they received a standardized briefing on the study protocol and completed the informed consent process. All data collection took place in quiet, dedicated testing rooms within the hospital research facility. Participants were instructed to refrain from consuming tea, coffee, or alcohol prior the study.

Assessments were conducted in three separate rooms, each designated for a specific component of the study: (1) personality assessment, (2) neuropsychological testing, and (3) bottom-up cognitive processing assessed via the exogenous cueing task. Participants completed the assessments individually and followed a rotating schedule to minimize fatigue and order effects. All participants completed the full study protocol.

Visuospatial ability

Visuospatial ability was assessed using the Chinese version of the Repeatable Battery for the Assessment of Neuropsychological Status (RBANS) (Cheng et al., 2011). A trained researcher administered the test individually in a quiet room. The assessment lasted approximately 30 min. The RBANS comprises 12 subtests across five domains: immediate memory, delayed memory, language, visuospatial/constructional ability, and attention. The Visuospatial/Constructional index, which served as our measure of VA, comprises the Line Orientation and Figure Copy subtests. Personality traits

Personality traits were measured using the Chinese version of the Neuroticism Extraversion Openness Five-Factor Inventory (NEO-FFI) (Ruosong & Leyao, 2010), a 60-item concise version of the NEO Personality Inventory-Revised (NEO-PI-R) (Costa & McCrae, 1992). The questionnaire assessed five major personality dimensions: neuroticism (Cronbach’s alph: 0.910), extraversion (Cronbach’s alph: 0.923), openness (Cronbach’s alph: 0.931), agreeableness (Cronbach’s alph: 0.951), and conscientiousness (Cronbach’s alph: 0.935). Participants responded to 60 items on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Scores for each dimension were calculated by summing the corresponding item scores.

Exogenous cueing paradigm

Stimuli were presented on a laptop computer (MSI) equipped with a 13th Gen Intel® Core™ i9-13900H processor (2.60 GHz), 64 GB RAM, and a 64-bit operating system. Visual stimuli were displayed at a screen resolution of 1,920 × 1,080 pixels. All stimuli were composed of dim but clearly visible black-and-white pixels and were presented against a uniform gray background. Participants were seated at a viewing distance of approximately 60 cm from the screen. Location placeholders consisted of outline rectangles subtending approximately 1.5° of visual angle. The fixation cue was a solid black rhombus with a diameter of approximately 1.0° of visual angle. Target stimuli were presented as an “×” symbol subtending an area of approximately 0.75° × 0.75° of visual angle.

An optimized exogenous cueing paradigm, based on previous methodologies (Prime, Visser & Ward, 2006; Posner & Cohen, 1984), was implemented using MATLAB (The MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA) software to assess bottom-up cognitive processing. The procedure consisted of 15 practice trials followed by 200 formal trials, totaling 215 trials, and took approximately 10 min to complete. Each trial began with a blank screen displayed for 1,000–2,000 ms, followed by a stimulus sequence. Participants were instructed to fixate on the central marker (‘□’) and attend to flashing cues on either side. When a target (‘×’) appeared on either side, participants were required to respond as quickly and accurately as possible. Incorrect responses were followed by error feedback, and participants pressed the space bar to initiate the next trial. A flowchart of the exogenous cueing paradigm is shown in Fig. 1.

Treatment of Outliers and Dropout Rate

Outliers identified in the dataset were excluded from further analysis. Since the experiment was conducted in a single session with no interruptions, the dropout rate was not applicable for this study.

Based on whether the target ‘×’ appears in the direction of the flashing box, the queue is divided into valid trials and invalid trials, i.e., if the target ‘×’ is on the same side as the cue (flashing box), it is a valid trial and the opposite is an invalid trial. Meanwhile, the trial is divided into valid long reacted time (vLRT) and invalid long reacted time (ivLRT). The long response time difference (△LRT= vLRT−ivLRT) indicates the degree of IOR, with a larger difference indicating a greater degree of recall inhibition. Participants with an accuracy rate below 90% were excluded to ensure data reliability. In this study, the collected △LRT data included both positive and negative values. To facilitate a more intuitive and accessible visualization of the data, a translation method was applied whereby all values were shifted to the positive domain (e.g., if the minimum value was −10, a constant of +10 was added to all data points, rendering the minimum value 0). The transformed variable was denoted as △LRT_s.

The raw △LRT values, which could be positive or negative, were linearly transformed by adding a constant to shift all values into the positive range for ease of visualization and interpretation. This transformation does not affect the distributional properties or the correlational structure of the data. A sensitivity analysis confirmed that all reported statistical results (correlations and mediation effects) were identical when using the raw △LRT scores.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 28.0 statistical software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Variables following a normal distribution were presented as “mean ± standard deviation”, while those not following a normal distribution were reported as “median (interquartile range)”. Participants were divided into low and high score groups respectively based on the first quartile (scores ranking < 25%) and fourth quartile (scores ranking > 75%) of the big five personality trait scores. Independent samples t-tests were conducted to compare differences in RBANS cognitive function scores and bottom-up cognitive processing characteristics between the low and high score big five personality subgroups. Partial correlation analyses, controlling for age and gender, were used to assess the two-way relationships between big five personality traits, cognitive function, and bottom-up cognitive processing characteristics.

To test the study’s hypotheses, mediation analyses were performed to evaluate the direct effect of big five personality traits on cognitive function. Additionally, the analysis examined the effect of bottom-up cognitive processing on cognitive function and the indirect effect of bottom-up cognitive processing in mediating the relationship between big five personality traits and cognitive function. The SPSS PROCESS macro (Hayes & Scharkow, 2013) was employed to assess indirect effects, generating multiple estimates of mediation, providing estimates of mediation effects, standard errors (SE), and confidence intervals (CI). Bootstrapping, a non-parametric resampling method that does not require assumptions of normal distribution, was applied. This procedure utilized 5,000 bootstrap resamples to estimate indirect effects and their corresponding confidence intervals. An effect was considered statistically significant at P < 0.05 if the 95% bias-corrected confidence interval did not include zero.

Discussion

This study explored the relationships among Openness, IOR, and VA. Firstly, we found that among 290 participants, individuals with higher Openness scores demonstrated greater VA and △LRT_s compared to those with lower Openness scores. Correlational analyses yielded consistent results, indicating that individuals high in Openness may exhibit superior visual span performance and stronger IOR effects. Furthermore, mediation analysis revealed that △LRT_s, as an index of the IOR effect, played a positive mediating role between Openness and VA. In other words, the observed association between Openness and broader visual attentional breadth can be interpreted, in part, through its correlation with a more efficient IOR mechanism, supporting our proposed hypothesis.

The observed superior performance in VA among individuals high in Openness, compared to their low-Openness counterparts, suggests a close link between Openness and the efficiency of visuospatial processing. This aligns with theoretical frameworks of Openness, which emphasize traits such as intellectual curiosity and receptivity to novel experiences—qualities that enhance perceptual flexibility and the integration of complex spatial information (Koivisto & Pallaris, 2024). Our findings extend this framework by suggesting that such perceptual flexibility may be supported, in part, by domain-general attentional control mechanisms, with IOR being one candidate process through which cognitive flexibility could influence visuospatial performance. Individuals with higher Openness score may more actively engage with environmental stimuli and be more readily attracted by cues, which is correlated with a greater ability to discriminate spatial relationships (Ruotolo, Sbordone & JMvdH, 2020). This cognitive capacity is critical for tasks involving geometric analysis or mental rotation.

Notably, in the exogenous cueing paradigm, the high-Openness group exhibited significantly higher △LRT_s values, indicating a stronger degree of IOR. In the present study, a “stronger degree of IOR” refers operationally to a larger reaction time delay (higher △LRT_s). To further elucidate the mechanisms underlying this effect, we decomposed the IOR effect by analyzing its subcomponents. This analysis revealed that individuals in the high-Openness group showed significantly faster response times on both validly cued (vLRT) and invalidly cued (ivLRT) trials compared to those in the low-Openness group. Critically, the high-Openness group demonstrated significantly faster response times on both the vLRT and ivLRT components, with this advantage being more pronounced in the ivLRT (intergroup advantage: ivLRT 29.821 ms, vLRT 19.198 ms, P < 0.001). This pattern of differential gains across both attentional components—rather than a trade-off or a deficit confined to a single process—ultimately converged to produce a larger net inhibitory aftereffect (i.e., the larger ΔLRT values observed in the high-Openness group). This finding suggests that the association between Openness and greater IOR levels may stem from a general enhancement in attentional dynamics, manifested as both vLRT and ivLRT becoming shorter. These findings support the interpretation that individuals high in Openness possess a more fluid and efficient attentional system—one that facilitates both rapid engagement with and flexible disengagement from spatial cues, ultimately promoting adaptive exploratory behavior (Marstrand-Joergensen et al., 2021; Wyche, Edwards & Goodhew, 2024). This pattern suggests that higher Openness scores are associated with more efficient disengagement of attention from previously cued locations, a hallmark of adaptive bottom-up processing. The observed positive correlation between Openness and △LRT_s further supports the notion that Openness facilitates rapid reallocation of attentional resources, thereby optimizing environmental exploration and information sampling (Costa & McCrae, 1992; DeYoung, 2015a; Fleischhauer et al., 2010; Klein, 2000).

Crucially, mediation analyses revealed that △LRT_s, representing the IOR effect, partially explained the association between Openness and visuospatial performance. This indicates that the association between Openness and spatial task performance may, in part, be statistically mediated by IOR. An efficient IOR process may enable individuals high in Openness to dynamically allocate attentional resources—suppressing irrelevant spatial information while prioritizing novel or task-relevant stimuli (Derakshan et al., 2009; Posner & Cohen, 1984). Such mechanisms may enhance the precision of spatial working memory and reduce cognitive load during visuospatial problem-solving, which may contribute to improved task performance (Schneider et al., 2017).

Recent intracranial evidence suggests that exogenous attention phenomena, including IOR, may emerge from large-scale spatiotemporal gradients in frontoparietal networks, which process sequential stimuli at the same location, challenging traditional multi-stage models of attention (Seidel Malkinson et al., 2024).

These findings are consistent with predictive processing theories, suggesting that the cognitive flexibility associated with Openness may reduce perceptual “priors” allowing bottom-up sensory input to exert greater influence on spatial representations (Huang & Yin, 2025; Shen et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024). The interaction between reduced attentional persistence (via IOR) and enhanced visual acuity may reflect a neurocognitive phenotype optimized for exploratory behavior—a core feature of Openness (Wilson et al., 2016). Our results extend this observation by demonstrating that IOR serves as a functional mediator, thereby proposing a specific attentional mechanism through which this exploratory phenotype may facilitate visuospatial performance.

The present findings, when interpreted within predictive processing frameworks, offer a more integrated theoretical account of how Openness influences domain-general cognitive performance. At its core, Openness reflects a propensity for cognitive exploration, flexibility, and diminished reliance on rigid prior expectations (DeYoung, 2015a). Mechanistically, this may correspond to a modulation of the precision-weighting assigned to perceptual priors relative to prioritization of sensory input within hierarchical predictive coding architectures (Blank, Alink & Büchel, 2023; Hsu & Hämäläinen, 2021; Tarasi, Di Pellegrino & Romei, 2022). Our observation that individuals high in Openness exhibit faster initial orienting (vLRT) as well as more efficient disengagement and re-orienting (ivLRT)—culminating in a stronger overall IOR effect (ΔLRT_s)—can be understood as a behavioral signature of such modulation. Specifically, a reduced weighting of spatial “priors” (e.g., the lingering expectation that a previously cued location remains behaviorally relevant) would allow bottom-up sensory input from novel locations to exert a stronger and more rapid influence on attentional allocation. This yields a cognitive system that is both quicker to engage with current information and more prepared to relinquish outdated spatial predictions, thereby optimizing environmental sampling.

This proposed mechanism—wherein Openness facilitates a context-sensitive balance between prior-based expectations and sensory evidence—extends beyond spatial attention to offer a parsimonious account of its association with diverse cognitive outcomes. In tasks involving visual acuity or complex visuospatial processing, reduced top-down constraint from strong priors may enhance the fidelity of sensory representation and the flexibility of spatial integration (Huang et al., 2024). In creative or problem-solving contexts, it may promote the weakening of conventional associative pathways, enabling novel combinations of information (Mtenga, Bode & Khalil, 2025). Thus, the domain-general advantage linked to Openness may not arise from a dedicated “module” for any single task, but rather from a foundational calibration of the predictive cognitive architecture. This fosters a processing mode that is inherently exploratory, characterized by a lower threshold for updating internal models in response to new evidence (Golubickis et al., 2024). Future research should directly test this formulation by examining whether Openness predicts neural markers of prediction error signaling or the dynamic adjustment of prior precision across different cognitive domains.

Strengths and Limitations

A key strength of this study lies in its integration of cognitive neuroscience and personality psychology to investigate how low-level attentional mechanisms, specifically IOR, mediate the relationship between the personality trait of Openness and VA. The use of △LRT_s as a behavioral indicator of IOR, and the inclusion of mediation analyses, allowed for a nuanced examination of cognitive processes beyond simple trait-cognition correlations. Furthermore, the study employed the RBANS—a validated and widely used neuropsychological tool for assessing various domains of cognitive function in healthy individuals—enhancing the reliability and generalizability of its cognitive assessments.

However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, making it impossible to determine the temporal precedence of personality traits, cognitive processing mechanisms, and performance outcomes. Second, the sample was demographically unbalanced, consisting predominantly of young adults with a substantial overrepresentation of female participants. This restricted age range and sex imbalance may limit the generalizability of the present findings to older populations and to more gender-balanced samples. Future studies should replicate these findings in more diverse samples spanning a wider age range and with more balanced sex distributions.. Thirdly, cultural specificity, as the sample primarily originates from a single cultural background, which may limit the generalisability of the findings to other cultural groups possessing differing values, norms, and social practices. Future research should address these limitations by employing longitudinal or experimental designs, expanding sample diversity in terms of age and gender, and exploring potential domain-specific mechanisms across broader cognitive functions.

Fourthly, this study exhibits certain limitations in its statistical methodology. The current research employs a simple mediation model, primarily focused on validating the efficacy of a specific mediating mechanism, without systematically testing other potential alternative mediating or moderating variables. Whilst a simple model aids in clarifying core research questions, it may overlook more complex relational networks among variables. For instance, certain unconsidered moderating variables could potentially influence the strength or direction of the mediating effect. Future research may further expand the model by incorporating additional potential variables to more comprehensively elucidate the operational mechanisms between variables.

Conclusion

This study provides novel evidence that the personality trait of Openness is closely linked to visuospatial cognitive performance, partially through its modulation of IOR—a core mechanism of bottom-up attention. Individuals higher in Openness exhibited greater IOR effects and broader visual attention, and mediation analyses confirmed that IOR significantly contributed to the pathway linking Openness and VA. These findings suggest that Openness may confer cognitive advantages by enhancing attentional flexibility and optimizing the deployment of visuospatial resources in dynamic environments.

Moreover, this research highlights the value of incorporating attentional mechanisms such as IOR into personality-cognition models, emphasizing the dynamic interplay between dispositional traits and neurocognitive processes. While the mediating role of IOR was specific to Openness, the broader implication is that personality traits may differentially engage cognitive systems depending on the nature of the task and processing demands. The present findings not only deepen our understanding of individual differences in cognitive functioning but also offer promising directions for clinical applications in populations with co-occurring visuospatial deficits and attentional dysfunctions. Future studies may further clarify the role of IOR and similar attentional processes in mediating the cognitive expression of personality across diverse psychological domains.

Abstract
. We characterise when the pure monomorphisms in a presheaf category 𝐒𝐞𝐭 𝒞 \operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}} are cofibrantly generated in terms of the category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . In particular, when 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is a monoid S S this characterises cofibrant generation of pure monomorphisms between sets with an S S -action in terms of S S : this happens if and only if for all a , b ∈ S a,b\in S there is c ∈ S c\in S such that a = c ​ b a=cb or c ​ a = b ca=b . We give a model-theoretic proof: we prove that our characterisation is equivalent to having a stable independence relation, which in turn is equivalent to cofibrant generation. As a corollary, we show that pure monomorphisms in acts over the multiplicative monoid of natural numbers are not cofibrantly generated.

1.
Introduction

Pure monomorphisms lie between embeddings and elementary embeddings. They were introduced more than a hundred years ago by Prüfer for abelian groups
[
21
]
. Since then they have been thoroughly studied from different perspectives: module theory
[
20
]
, model theory
[
22
]
, acts theory
[
13
, Chapter III]
, universal algebra
[
29
]
and category theory
[
2
, Section 2.D]
.

Any module category has enough pure injectives, i.e., every module is a pure submodule of a pure injective module (for instance, of a double character module). Moreover, there is a Baer type criterion of pure injectivity: there is a test set for pure injectivity (see
[
23
, Lemma 1.3]
or
[
17
, Corollary 4.10]
). A stronger result, that pure monomorphisms are cofibrantly generated, was proved recently in
[
14
]
. Beyond module categories, determining if a category has enough pure injectives has been thoroughly studied
[
6
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
27
]
, see also the summary given in
[
29
]
. Already in the seventies it was known that many categories do not have enough pure injectives
[
27
]
.

Our aim is to study cofibrant generation of pure monomorphisms, which implies having enough pure injectives, in many-sorted unary universal algebras. That is, in categories
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
of set valued functors on a small category
\mathcal{C}
. If
\mathcal{C}
has a single object—so if it is a monoid
S
—we get single sorted unary algebras, which are called
S
-acts. These
S
-acts have been systematically studied
[
13
]
and can be viewed as “non-additive modules”. The early results about the existence of enough pure injectives are due to Wenzel
[
30
]
and Banaschewski
[
4
]
. They showed that acts over the additive monoid of natural numbers and acts over groups have enough pure injectives. Recently these results were strengthened to cofibrant generation
[
9
]
(see Remark
5.2
). On the other hand, in
[
4
, Remark 5]
, it was shown that one does not necessarily have enough pure injectives in all categories of acts, and so pure monomorphisms are not cofibrantly generated in all such categories.

Sufficient conditions for
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
to have enough pure injectives are given in
[
9
, Corollary 2.6]
. In fact, they even show that pure monomorphisms are cofibrantly generated (see the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 2.4]
). These conditions concern both
\mathcal{C}
and
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
. The condition on
\mathcal{C}
is what we call
locally linearly preordered
(see Definition
1.1
). The condition concerning
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
is that finitely presentable objects are closed under subobjects and quotients. Our work shows that the second condition is superfluous, and it establishes the converse statement.

Our main result is of algebraic and category-theoretic nature, with the notion of cofibrant generation originating from homotopy theory
[
8
]
. However, our proof is model-theoretic

Some of the results were spawned by set-theoretic elementary submodel arguments, though ultimately such arguments were replaced by more powerful and precise model-theoretic ones.
. The main model-theoretic notion we use is that of a stable independence relation (see Section

), which originates from the central model-theoretic notion of Shelah’s forking. This makes the results of this paper one of the first applications of the categorical approach to model-theoretic independence.

The key ingredient connecting model theory with algebra is
[

, which equates cofibrant generation to model-theoretic stability. Our proof strategy is then to show that
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
is model-theoretically stable, in the sense that it admits a stable independence relation, if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is locally linearly preordered. For this, we heavily make use of the techniques of Mustafin in

, where it is proved that the first-order theory of every
S
-act is stable if and only if
S
is locally linearly preordered.

Moreover, we prove that pure monomorphisms in
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
are cofibrantly generated if and only if pullback squares of pure monomorphisms are pure effective. This concept was introduced in
[
9
]
under the name “effective unions of pure subobjects”. It was motivated by Barr’s effective unions
[
7
]
, which are closely related to the existence of enough injectives. Pure effective unions distinguish acts from modules—abelian groups do not have effective unions of pure subobjects
[
14

Our main result implies that pure monomorphims in
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
are cofibrantly generated if
\mathcal{C}
is a groupoid. Hence
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
has enough pure injectives in this case, which extends the old result of
[
4

Main results.
Our main result characterises exactly when the pure monomorphisms in a presheaf category
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
are cofibrantly generated, in terms of
\mathcal{C}
. The characterising property is very simple.

Definition 1.1
.

We call a category
\mathcal{C}

locally linearly preordered
if for any span of arrows
Y\xleftarrow{f}X\xrightarrow{g}Z
in
\mathcal{C}
there is either
h:Y\to Z
such that
hf=g
or there is
h^{\prime}:Z\to Y
such that
f=h^{\prime}g
.

Our terminology is explained as follows. Any category induces a preorder on its collection of objects, by setting
X\leq Y
whenever there is an arrow
X\to Y
. So Definition
1.1
says that for each object
X
of
\mathcal{C}
the induced preorder on the coslice category
X/\mathcal{C}
is a linear preorder.

Theorem 1.2
.

The following are equivalent for a small category
\mathcal{C}
.

(1)

The pure monomorphisms in
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
are cofibrantly generated.

(2)

There is a stable independence relation on
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\textup{pure}}
.

(3)

The category
\mathcal{C}
is locally linearly preordered.

The above is a simplified version of our main result: Theorem
5.1
, which gives further equivalent conditions that provide more information about the model-theoretic stability.

The usual strategy to obtain enough injectives from cofibrant generation applies.

Corollary 1.3
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a locally linearly preordered category then
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
has enough pure injectives.

To obtain the statement from the abstract concerning
S
-acts for a monoid
S
, we refer to Example
2.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Monoids as presheaves, and presheaves as unary algebras

We will work in a presheaf category
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
, where
\mathcal{C}
is any small category. The objects in this category are functors
\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{\bf Set}
and the arrows are natural transformations between such functors. It will be more convenient to think about
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
as the category of a multi-sorted unary algebras, see below. Throughout, we fix a small category
\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the multi-sorted signature
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}
as follows. It has a sort
X
for every object
X
in
\mathcal{C}
and it has a function symbol
f:X\to Y
for every arrow
f:X\to Y
in
\mathcal{C}
. The (covariant) presheaves on
\mathcal{C}
are then viewed as a variety of algebras (in the sense of universal algebra) in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}
. That is, they are axiomatised by the following equations:

(i)

for each object
X
in
\mathcal{C}
, we have the equation
Id_{X}(x)=x
, where
Id_{X}
is the identity arrow on
X
and
x
is a variable of sort
X
;

(ii)

for any two composable arrows
f:X\to Y
and
g:Y\to Z
in
\mathcal{C}
, we write
h=gf
and we have the equation
g(f(x))=h(x)
, where
x
is a variable of sort
X
.

One straightforwardly checks that homomorphisms between such algebras
K
and
L
are the same thing as natural transformations between
K
and
L
viewed as functors, which completes our algebraic perspective of
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
.

Taking the perspective of multi-sorted unary algebras is very close to the original case where we consider
S
-acts for a monoid
S
. We set up our notation in such a way that working in the greater generality of presheaves comes at no extra cost compared to working with just
S
-acts. In fact, if the reader wishes, they are invited to read everything as if the category
\mathcal{C}
is just a monoid (in the sense below).

Example 2.2
.

Let
S
be a monoid. We view
S
as a category in the usual way: it has one object
*
and an arrow
a:*\to*
for every
a\in S
. The composition operation in this category is then given by the multiplication operation from the monoid. So a functor
F:S\to\operatorname{\bf Set}
is the same thing as a set
F(*)
equipped with an
S
-action, and natural transformations between such functors are exactly homomorphisms of such
S
-acts.

Under this identification of monoids with one-object categories we have that
S
is locally linearly preordered if for any
a,b\in S
there is
c\in S
such that either
a=cb
or
ca=b
.

Convention 2.3
.

Let
K
be a presheaf on
\mathcal{C}
, by which we will always mean a covariant presheaf, viewed as a multi-sorted unary algebra (see Definition
2.1
).

(i)

Let
f:X\to Y
be an arrow in
\mathcal{C}
and let
x
be an element of sort
X
in
K
. We write
f\cdot x
for the action of
f
on
x
.

(ii)

We use capital letters
X,Y,Z,\ldots
for objects in
\mathcal{C}
and lowercase letters
f,g,h,\ldots
for arrows in
\mathcal{C}
. We will simply write
X\in\mathcal{C}
(resp.
f\in\mathcal{C}
) to indicate the
X
(resp.
f
) is an object (resp. arrow) in
\mathcal{C}
.

(iii)

We often omit the sorts (i.e., objects in
\mathcal{C}
) from notation. So
x\in K
means that
x
is an element of some sort
X
in
K
. Similarly, for
x\in K
and
f\in\mathcal{C}
we will write
f\cdot x
for the action of
f
on
x
, which is then assumed to be of the right type (i.e., the domain of
f
is the same as the sort of
x
).

(iv)

Any set-theoretic operations on presheaves are computed on every sort. For example, given another presheaf
L
, we write
K\cap L
for the presheaf whose sort
X
is the intersection of the sort
X
of
K
and the sort
X
of
L
. For
A\subseteq K
a subset of
K
, we write
|A|
for the sum of the cardinalities of the sorts in
A
.

The category
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
has many nice properties, which we briefly discuss here and will use implicitly throughout the paper. Firstly, the monomorphisms are precisely those homomorphisms which are injective on every sort. It is a locally finitely presentable category
[
2
, Corollary 3.7]
. So in particular it has pullbacks and pushouts, and the pullback (resp. pushout) of a cospan (resp. span) of monomorphisms consists again of monomorphisms.

It will be useful to have an explicit description of such pullbacks and pushouts. A commuting square of monomorphisms

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L}
\textstyle{K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

is a pullback precisely when
K=A\cap B
. Here we view all monomorphisms as inclusions and we compute the intersection on each sort separately, as per Convention
2.3
(iii).

For the description of pushouts, we let
L\supseteq K\subseteq M
be a span of inclusions of presheaves. Their pushout is simply the disjoint union over
K
. That is, the pushout is given by

(L\sqcup M)/\sim,

where
\sim
identifies the copies of
K
in
L
and
M
. In particular, any pushout square that consists of monomorphisms is also a pullback square.

Finally, for
\lambda
bigger than the sum of the cardinalities of the sets of objects and arrows in
\mathcal{C}
, a presheaf
K
is
\lambda
-presentable if and only if
|K|<\lambda

[
2
, Example 1.14(5)]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
K
be a presheaf and let
A\subseteq K
be any subset. We write
\langle A\rangle
for the subpresheaf generated by
A
. That is,

\langle A\rangle=\{f\cdot a:a\in A,f\in\mathcal{C}\}.

2.2.
Pure monomorphisms

One can give a pure categorical definition of pure monomorphisms (see for example
[
2
, Definition 2.27]
). For us, the logical description will be useful. The equivalence of the two can be seen using the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 5.15]
, which works for any variety (i.e., category of algebras satisfying a fixed list of equations), such as the category
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
of presheaves we are interested in.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a signature. Then a
positive primitive formula
or
pp-formula
is one that is built from atomic formulas, (finite) conjunctions and (finite lists of) existential quantifiers.

Up to logical equivalence, every pp-formula
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
can be written as

\exists y_{1}\ldots y_{k}\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}),

where
\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
is a conjunction of atomic formulas. In particular, if
\mathcal{L}
consists only of constant symbols and functions symbols (as is the case when axiomatising a category of algebras, so in particular for presheaves), then
\psi
is simply a system of equations in variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}
.

One quickly sees that truth of pp-formulas is preserved upwards by homomorphisms. That is, if
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a pp-formula and
f:K\to L
is a homomorphism of
\mathcal{L}
-structures then for all
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in K
we have

K\models\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\implies L\models\varphi(f(a_{1}),\ldots,f(a_{n})).

Being a pure monomorphism is about the converse of the above implication holding.

Definition 2.6
.

A homomorphism
f:K\to L
of presheaves on
\mathcal{C}
is called a
pure monomorphism
if for every pp-formula
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}
and all
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in K
we have

K\models\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L\models\varphi(f(a_{1}),\ldots,f(a_{n})).

The name “monomorphism” is justified because such
f
are in particular injective. Just apply the definition to the pp-formula
x_{1}=x_{2}
.

Fact 2.7
.

The following general facts hold for pure monomorphisms in any locally finitely presentable category. In particular, they hold in our category of interest
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
.

(i)

Any split monomorphism is pure
[
2
, Section 2.D]
.

(ii)

Pure monomorphisms are left-cancellable: if
gf
is a pure monomorphism then so is
f

[
2
, Section 2.D]
.

(iii)

Pure monomorphisms are stable under pushout
[
3
, Proposition 15]
. That is, if
f:K\to L
is a pure monomorphism, then its pushout along any arrow
g:K\to M
is a pure monomorphism.

(iv)

Pure monomorphisms are continuous
[
2
, Theorem 2.34]
. That is, all the coprojections of a colimit of a directed diagram of pure monomorphisms are pure monomorphisms. For such a colimit and any other cocone consisting of pure monomorphisms, the induced universal arrow is a pure monomorphism.

In particular, pure monomorphisms are closed under transfinite composition. That is, for any ordinal
\alpha
, given a smooth chain
(f_{ij}:K_{i}\to K_{j})_{i\leq j<\alpha}
of pure monomorphisms (smooth means that at limit stages we have colimits), the coprojection
f_{0}:K_{0}\to K
is a pure monomorphism, where
K=\operatorname{colim}_{i<\alpha}K_{i}
.

2.3.
Stable independence and cofibrant generation

The main model-theoretic tool in this paper is that of a stable independence relation. The categorical approach to independence was initiated in
[
15
]
, and we use the same terminology. We only recall the definitions that are important to us, and refer to
[

]
for the rest.

.

is a collection of commuting squares (invariant under isomorphic squares). The squares in the relation are called

We say that

The important property that is missing from a weakly stable independence relation is that the relation is built from a small amount of information. This can be made precise using the language of accessible categories.

Let

3.
Bad configurations

A common tool in model theory is to identify a certain bad combinatorial configuration, and declare that we do not wish that to happen. We will identify such configurations for presheaves, and these will come up as intermediate steps in the proof of our main theorem. Such bad configurations are of infinite size. Importantly, using the compactness theorem, we can then find such configurations of arbitrarily big sizes. So we begin by recalling the compactness theorem.

Fact 3.1

(Compactness theorem, e.g.,
[

)

Let

be a signature and let

-sentences. If for every finite

\mathcal{L}
-structure

then there is an
\mathcal{L}
-structure

such that

.

If

for the equivalence class of
a
under this relation. That is:

C^{L}_{K}(a)=\{b\in L\setminus K:\text{$a$ and $b$ are connected outside $K$}\}.

For a subset
A\subseteq L\setminus K
we write

C^{L}_{K}(A)=\bigcup_{a\in A}C^{L}_{K}(a).

Lemma 4.4
.

Any pullback square of pure monomorphisms of presheaves

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L}
\textstyle{K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

such that
C^{L}_{K}(A\setminus K)\cap C^{L}_{K}(B\setminus K)=\emptyset
, is pure effective.

Proof.

Let
P
be the pushout of
A\leftarrow K\to B
. One quickly checks that the induced arrow
P\to L
is a monomorphism (presheaf categories have effective unions). So we may, and will, assume that all the arrows involved are inclusions. Let
\Sigma(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a finite system of equations with parameters from
P=A\cup B
. Suppose that
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in L
is a solution to
\Sigma
. We will show that
\Sigma
has a solution in
P
as well.

First, we partition
\Sigma
into the following sets of equations:

•

\Sigma_{1}=\{(f\cdot x_{i}=g\cdot x_{i})\in\Sigma\}
,

•

\Sigma_{2}=\{(f\cdot x_{i}=g\cdot x_{j})\in\Sigma:i\neq j\}
,

•

\Sigma^{A}_{3}=\{(f\cdot x_{i}=a)\in\Sigma:a\in A\setminus K\}
,

•

\Sigma^{B}_{3}=\{(f\cdot x_{i}=b)\in\Sigma:b\in B\setminus K\}
,

•

\Sigma^{K}_{3}=\{(f\cdot x_{i}=d)\in\Sigma:d\in K\}
.

Claim 4.4.1
.

There is a partition
(X_{A},X_{B})
of the set of variables
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
of
\Sigma
such that:

(i)

all the variables appearing in
\Sigma^{A}_{3}
are contained in
X_{A}
,

(ii)

all the variables appearing in
\Sigma^{B}_{3}
are contained in
X_{B}
,

(iii)

whenever
(f\cdot x_{i}=g\cdot x_{j})\in\Sigma_{2}
is such that
x_{i}\in X_{A}
and
x_{j}\in X_{B}
then
f\cdot c_{i}=g\cdot c_{j}\in K
.

Proof.

Write
\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma\setminus\{(f\cdot x_{i}=g\cdot x_{j})\in\Sigma:f\cdot c_{i}=g\cdot c_{j}\in K\}
. We define a graph
G
with vertices
P\cup\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, where two vertices have an edge if they both appear in an equation in
\Sigma^{\prime}
. Let
G_{c}
be the graph obtained from
G
by replacing the vertex
x_{i}
by
c_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
, so the vertices of this new graph are all elements in
L
. We show that an edge between
v,v^{\prime}\in G_{c}\setminus K
represents a path between
v
and
v^{\prime}
outside
K
. Since edges come from equations in
\Sigma^{\prime}
, and ignoring the case
v=v^{\prime}
, there are three cases.

•

The edge comes from
\Sigma_{2}\cap\Sigma^{\prime}
, say from the equation
(f\cdot x_{i}=g\cdot x_{j})
, so w.l.o.g.
v=c_{i}
and
v^{\prime}=c_{j}
. Set
d=f\cdot c_{i}=g\cdot c_{j}
. By construction of
\Sigma^{\prime}
we have
d\not\in K
, and so
c_{i},d,c_{j}
is a path outside
K
.

•

The edge comes from
\Sigma^{A}_{3}
, say from the equation
(f\cdot x_{i}=a)
, so
v,v^{\prime}
, which are
c_{i}
and
a
, is a path outside
K
.

•

The edge comes from
\Sigma^{B}_{3}
, say from the equation
(f\cdot x_{i}=b)
, so
v,v^{\prime}
, which are
c_{i}
and
b
, is a path outside
K
.

As
C^{L}_{K}(A\setminus K)\cap C^{L}_{K}(B\setminus K)=\emptyset
, there are no
a\in A\setminus K
and
b\in B\setminus K
in the same connected component. We can thus define

X_{A}=\{x_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\text{ and $x_{i}$ is connected in $G$ to some }a\in A\setminus K\}

and

X_{B}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\setminus X_{A}.

By construction this is a partition of variables and by the above discussion it satisfies (i) and (ii).

For item (iii) we let
(f\cdot x_{i}=g\cdot x_{j})\in\Sigma_{2}
is such that
x_{i}\in X_{A}
and
x_{j}\in X_{B}
, and suppose for a contradiction that
f\cdot c_{i}=g\cdot c_{j}\not\in K
. Then
(f\cdot x_{i}=g\cdot x_{j})\in\Sigma^{\prime}
, and so there would be an edge in
G
between
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
. So
x_{j}
is connected to
x_{i}
, which is connected to some
a\in A\setminus K
. Hence
x_{j}\in X_{A}
, contradicting
x_{j}\in X_{B}
. We thus conclude that
f\cdot c_{i}=g\cdot c_{j}\in K
.
∎

Equations in
\Sigma
that have a variable in both
X_{A}
and
X_{B}
are all contained in
\Sigma_{2}
. Enumerate such equations as
\{f_{\ell}\cdot x_{i_{\ell}}=g_{\ell}\cdot x_{j_{\ell}}:1\leq\ell\leq m\}
. Let
1\leq\ell\leq m
, by symmetry of equations, we may assume that
x_{i_{\ell}}\in X_{A}
and
x_{j_{\ell}}\in X_{B}
. Furthermore, writing
d_{\ell}=f_{\ell}\cdot c_{i_{\ell}}=g_{\ell}\cdot c_{j_{\ell}}
, we have that
d_{\ell}\in K
by Claim
4.4.1
(iii).

Now define two new sets of equations:

\Delta_{A}=\{\varphi\in\Sigma:\text{the variables of $\varphi$ are contained in $X_{A}$}\}\cup\{f_{\ell}\cdot x_{i_{\ell}}=d_{\ell}:1\leq\ell\leq m\}

and

\Delta_{B}=\{\varphi\in\Sigma:\text{the variables of $\varphi$ are contained in $X_{B}$}\}\cup\{g_{\ell}\cdot x_{j_{\ell}}=d_{\ell}:1\leq\ell\leq m\}.

Then
\Delta_{A}
is a finite system of equations with parameters from
A
and with a solution in
L
(restrict
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
to the subtuple corresponding to
X_{A}
). As
A\to L
is pure, there is a solution
\bar{a}\in A
to
\Delta_{A}
. Similarly, there is a solution
\bar{b}\in B
to
\Delta_{B}
. We claim that
\bar{a}\cup\bar{b}
is a solution to
\Sigma
in
P
. Let
\varphi\in\Sigma
, we consider three cases.

•

The variables of
\varphi
are contained in
X_{A}
, so
\varphi
will only care about elements in
\bar{a}
. By construction
\varphi\in\Delta_{A}
and so
\bar{a}
satisfies
\varphi
in
A
and hence in
P
.

•

The variables of
\varphi
are contained in
X_{B}
. This case is analogous to the above.

•

There are two variables in
\varphi
, one of which is in
X_{A}
and the other is in
X_{B}
. Then there is
1\leq\ell\leq m
such that
\varphi
is the equation
f_{\ell}\cdot x_{i_{\ell}}=g_{\ell}\cdot x_{j_{\ell}}
. Let
a\in\bar{a}
match
x_{i_{\ell}}
and let
b\in\bar{b}
match
x_{j_{\ell}}
. By construction of
\Delta_{A}
and choice of
\bar{a}
we have
f_{\ell}\cdot a=d_{\ell}
in
A
and hence in
P
. Similarly, we have
g_{\ell}\cdot b=d_{\ell}
in
P
. So
f_{\ell}\cdot a=g_{\ell}\cdot b
in
P
, as required.

We conclude that
\Sigma
can be solved in
P
, and so
P\to L
is pure.
∎

The converse to Lemma
4.4
does not hold, but we omit the example as it is not relevant for the remainder of this paper.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a locally linearly preordered category. Let
K\subseteq L
be an inclusion of presheaves, and let
a,b\in L\setminus K
. Then
a
and
b
are connected outside
K
if and only if there is a path between
a
and
b
outside
K
of length at most
2
.

Furthermore, if
a=c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}=b
is a path outside
K
of minimal length then
c_{1}\in\langle a\rangle\cap\langle b\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
a=c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}=b
be a path outside
K
of minimal length. First, we claim that for every
0<i<n
we must have
c_{i}\in\langle c_{i-1}\rangle
and
c_{i}\in\langle c_{i+1}\rangle
. Suppose for a contradiction that this is not the case. Without loss of generality, we may assume
c_{i}\not\in\langle c_{i-1}\rangle
, and so we must have
c_{i-1}\in\langle c_{i}\rangle
. We distinguish two cases.

•

If
c_{i}\in\langle c_{i+1}\rangle
then
c_{i-1}\in\langle c_{i+1}\rangle
, and so we could have omitted
c_{i}
from the path, contradicting minimality.

•

If
c_{i+1}\in\langle c_{i}\rangle
then there are
f,g\in\mathcal{C}
such that
c_{i-1}=f\cdot c_{i}
and
c_{i+1}=g\cdot c_{i}
. As
\mathcal{C}
is locally linearly preordered, there must be
h\in\mathcal{C}
such that either
f=hg
, in which case
c_{i-1}=h\cdot c_{i+1}
, or
g=hf
, in which case
c_{i+1}=h\cdot c_{i-1}
. In either case we see that we could have omitted
c_{i}
from the path, again contradicting minimality.

Now that we have proved the claim, we suppose for a contradiction that
n>2
. Then the claim applies to
i=1
and
i=2
. The first application tells us that
c_{1}\in\langle c_{0}\rangle
, while the second application tells us that
c_{2}\in\langle c_{1}\rangle
, again arriving at a contradiction just like in the first bullet point above. The final sentence follows directly from the claim applied to the case
n=2
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a locally linearly preordered category. For any pullback square of monomorphisms of presheaves

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L}
\textstyle{K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

we have that
C^{L}_{K}(A\setminus K)\cap C^{L}_{K}(B\setminus K)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Towards a contradiction, suppose that there are
a\in A\setminus K
and
b\in B\setminus K
that are connected outside
K
. Let
n
be minimal so that there is a path between
a
and
b
outside
K
of length
n
. As the square is a pullback, we must have
n>0
. By Lemma
4.5
we also must have
n\leq 2
. So we distinguish two cases.

•

The case
n=1
. Then either
a\in\langle b\rangle
or
b\in\langle a\rangle
. Without loss of generality, assume the former. Then
a\in A\cap\langle b\rangle\subseteq A\cap B=K
, contradicting
a\not\in K
.

•

The case where
n=2
. Let
a=c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}=b
be a path outside
K
of minimal length. Then by the last sentence in Lemma
4.5
we have that
c_{1}\in\langle a\rangle\cap\langle b\rangle\subseteq A\cap B=K
, contradicting that the path is outside
K
.

∎

Theorem
4.1
, repeated.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a locally linearly preordered category then a commutative square in
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
is pure effective if and only if it is a pullback square consisting of pure monomorphisms.

Proof.

Every pushout square of monomorphisms is a pullback square. So pure effective squares are pullback squares because postcomposing a pullback square with a monomorphism results in a pullback square. The converse follows from Lemma
4.4
, where the Lemma
4.6
takes care of the additional technical condition.
∎

5.
Classification for cofibrant generation

Theorem 5.1
.

The following are equivalent for a small category
\mathcal{C}
.

(i)

The pure monomorphisms in
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
are cofibrantly generated.

(ii)

The pure effective squares form a stable independence relation on
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\textup{pure}}
.

(iii)

The pullback squares form a stable independence relation on
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\textup{pure}}
.

(iv)

There is a stable independence relation on
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\textup{pure}}
.

(v)

There is no presheaf on
\mathcal{C}
that interprets a complete bipartite graph.

(vi)

There is no presheaf on
\mathcal{C}
that has the span-induced order property.

(vii)

The category
\mathcal{C}
is locally linearly preordered.

Proof.

The equivalence of (i), (ii) and (iv) is exactly Fact
2.14
. The implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv) is trivial, while (vii) implies both (ii) and (iii) at the same time by Theorem
4.1
and Corollary
4.2
. We can thus close the loop of implications with (ii)
\Rightarrow
(v)
\Rightarrow
(vi)
\Rightarrow
(vii), which are Theorem
3.6
, Lemma
3.5
and Theorem
3.7
respectively.
∎

Recall that an object
L
is
pure injective
if for any span
K^{\prime}\xleftarrow{f}K\xrightarrow{g}L
with
f
a pure monomorphism, there is an arrow
h:K^{\prime}\to L
making the triangle commute:
hf=g
. A category is said to have
enough pure injectives
if every object admits a pure monomorphism into a pure injective.

Corollary
1.3
, repeated.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a locally linearly preordered category then
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
has enough pure injectives.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.1
the pure monomorphisms are cofibrantly generated in
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
. They thus form the left part of a weak factorisation system
[
8
, Proposition 1.3]
. So for any presheaf
K
the factorisation of
K\to 1
gives a pure monomorphism into a pure injective.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

We recall the statement of
[
9
, Theorem 2.4]
(in our terminology): if in a locally finitely presentable category every pullback square of pure monomorphisms is pure effective then it has enough pure injectives. In fact, the proof of that theorem shows that the pure monomorphisms are cofibrantly generated, from which having enough pure injectives follows in the same way as in Corollary
1.3
. The proof strategy of
[
9
]
reminds us of ours: in Section
4
we use a similar condition involving pullback squares to show that the pure effective squares form a stable independence relation, which in turn gives us cofibrant generation through Fact
2.14
. The condition of being locally linearly preordered even appears as part of the conditions of
[
9
, Corollary 2.6]
, which allows one to apply
[
9
, Theorem 2.4]
to certain presheaf categories. Our main improvements are that we removed the second condition from
[
9
, Corollary 2.6]
, and the fact that this fully characterises when cofibrant generation happens (i.e., a converse statement).

Examples 5.3
.

We give some examples of applications of Theorem
5.1
. We start with existing examples, which quickly follow because being locally linearly preordered is an easy condition to verify.

(1)

Let
\mathbb{N}^{+}=(\mathbb{N};0,+)
be the monoid of natural numbers with addition. Then
\mathbb{N}^{+}
is locally linearly preordered and so the pure monomorphisms in
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathbb{N}^{+}}
are cofibrantly generated. In particular, by Corollary
1.3
, there are enough pure injectives, and so we recover
[
9
, Example 2.7(2)]
(which in turn cites
[
30
,
26
]
for this result).

(2)

Any groupoid (i.e., a small category where every arrow is an isomorphism) is locally linearly preordered. So the pure monomorphisms in the category of presheaves on a groupoid are cofibrantly generated. This also appears as
[
9
, Corollary 2.8]
(which in turn generalises
[
4
, Proposition 3]
, see also
[
9
, Corollary 2.9]
). The statement there only mentions having enough pure injectives (cf. Corollary
1.3
), but their proof establishes cofibrant generation (see also Remark
5.2
).

We also give some new examples.

(3)

Let
P
be a poset. Then
P
can be viewed as a category as usual: the objects are the elements of
P
and for
x,y\in P
there is an arrow
x\to y
precisely when
x\leq y
. Such a poset
P
is locally linearly preordered if and only if for every
x\in P
its upper set
{\uparrow}x=\{y\in P:x\leq y\}
is a linear order, characterising when the pure monomorphisms in presheaves on posets are cofibrantly generated.

(4)

Let
\Delta
be the category of finite linear orders and order preserving homomorphisms. Recall that a
simplicial set
is a functor
\Delta^{\textup{op}}\to\operatorname{\bf Set}
. The pure monomorphisms in the category of simplicial sets
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\Delta^{\textup{op}}}
are not cofibrantly generated. For example,
\{0\}\xrightarrow{0\mapsto 0}\{0<1\}\xleftarrow{0\mapsto 1}\{0\}
cannot be completed to a commuting triangle.

Surprisingly, it was unknown whether or not pure monomorphisms in the category of acts over
(\mathbb{N};1,\times)
are cofibrantly generated. It now easily follows from Theorem
5.1
that this is not the case. Given its contrast to the additive monoid of natural numbers (see Example
5.3
(1)), we highlight it as a separate corollary.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\mathbb{N}^{\times}=(\mathbb{N};1,\times)
be the monoid of natural numbers with multiplication. Then the pure monomorphisms in
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathbb{N}^{\times}}
are not cofibrantly generated.

Proof.

For example, there is no
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2=n\times 3
or
3=n\times 2
, so
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
is not locally linearly preordered.
∎

Example 5.5
.

We show that the converse of Corollary
1.3
does not hold. It is well known that having enough
\mathcal{M}
-injectives for a class of arrows
\mathcal{M}
does not imply that
\mathcal{M}
is cofibrantly generated. For example, we can take
\mathcal{M}
to be the class of embeddings in the category of posets. By
[
5
, Propositions 1 and 2]
the category of posets has enough
\mathcal{M}
-injectives, but by
[
1
, Proposition 3.4]

\mathcal{M}
is not cofibrantly generated. We give an example of a presheaf category where this happens. We do not know of any such example in a category of acts.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the category

X\xleftarrow{f}Z\xrightarrow{g}Y.

Then
\mathcal{C}
is clearly not locally linearly preordered, so by Theorem
5.1
the pure monomorphisms are not cofibrantly generated in
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
. We claim that
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
has enough pure injectives. In fact, we claim that the pure monomorphisms are precisely the split monomorphisms, so every object is pure injective.

So let
K\to L
be a pure monomorphism in
\operatorname{\bf Set}^{\mathcal{C}}
, which we may assume to be an inclusion. Then
K(X)=\emptyset
if and only if
L(X)=\emptyset
, and similarly for the sorts
Y
and
Z
. We consider two cases.

•

The case where
K(Z)=\emptyset
. Then
K
can be viewed as a pair of sets
K(X)
and
K(Y)
, and similarly for
L
. An inclusion of sets
A\subseteq B
is split if and only if
A\neq\emptyset
or
A=B=\emptyset
. So both
K(X)\subseteq L(X)
and
K(Y)\subseteq L(Y)
are split inclusions of sets, and so there are corresponding retracts
L(X)\to K(X)
and
L(Y)\to K(Y)
. As
L(Z)=\emptyset
, these functions form a homomorphism
L\to K
.

•

The case where
K(Z)\neq\emptyset
. Let
z_{0}\in K(Z)
and set
x_{0}=f\cdot z_{0}
and
y_{0}=g\cdot z_{0}
. Define a homomorphism
L\to K
by letting it be the identity on
K
and outside
K
it sends everything to one of
x_{0}
,
y_{0}
and
z_{0}
, depending on the sort.

In both cases we have constructed a homomorphism
L\to K
such that the composition
K\to L\to K
is the identity on
K
, and so
K\to L
is split.

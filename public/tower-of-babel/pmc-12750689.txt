Serum ALCAM is a potential novel non-invasive marker for predicting the progression of IgA nephropathy
Authors: Zengyuan Luo, Linlin Zhang, Zaiyu Wang, Qi Xiong, Jieyu Tang, Yongwen Yang, Xueling Hu, Huipeng Ge, Xiangcheng Xiao, Rong Tang, Wei Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundActivated leukocyte cell adhesion molecule (ALCAM) serves as an important effector that activates and traffics lymphocytes into tissues. The expression and role of ALCAM in IgA nephropathy (IgAN) remain unclear. Here, we aim to evaluate the expression and effect of ALCAM in IgAN patients.MethodsWe performed a prospective study including 71 IgAN patients, and 41 controls. Serum and uALCAM levels were evaluated. Renal expression of ALCAM and its ligand S100B were measured. The correlation of serum ALCAM with clinical data, renal pathology, and prognosis was analyzed. Cox regression analysis was conducted to identify independent factors associated with prognosis. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was performed to explore the role of serum ALCAM.ResultsCirculating ALCAM levels were higher in IgAN patients than in controls. Serum ALCAM levels showed a positive correlation with proteinuria and renal pathology. Renal ALCAM expression was also elevated in IgAN patients. IgAN patients were divided into low and high ALCAM groups (< 4.76, and ≥ 4.76 ng/mL) based on the optimal cut-off value for predicting renal progression. The high ALCAM group exhibited more severe clinical parameters and renal pathology. High serum ALCAM level was an independent risk factor for IgAN progression. IgAN patients with high ALCAM level exhibited a lower cumulative renal survival rate compared to the low-level group.ConclusionsIn summary, serum ALCAM level in IgAN patients was elevated, which was consistent with clinical and pathological severity, and served as an independent risk factor and a promising novel predictor for the progression of IgAN.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12882-025-04603-4.

Introduction

Immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN) is the most prevalent primary glomerulonephritis globally [1, 2]. It is estimated that 30–40% of IgAN patients will progress to end-stage renal disease (ESRD) within 20–30 years [3]. IgAN patients present a wide range of manifestations from asymptomatic microhematuria to proteinuria and impaired renal function, so recognizing patients who are at high risk of progression is critical [4].

Due to the lack of specific serological diagnostic marker, renal biopsy is still essential for making the diagnosis and estimating the prognosis of IgAN currently [1]. As an invasive technique, renal biopsy has the potential risks such as kidney damage and bleeding. Lee’s grade, proposed to evaluate renal histological lesions, can provide predictive value for renal outcomes of IgAN [5]. The Oxford classification, first proposed in 2009 and updated in 2017, includes five parameters to assess the renal prognosis independently [6]. The Lee’s grade and Oxford classification have been considered as effective indicators to predict the prognosis of IgAN, but it is not practical to monitor the severity and development by repeated renal biopsy. Besides, several clinical variables such as renal function, proteinuria, and hypertension are also vital prognostic factors for evaluating the development of IgAN [7]. Recently, many new biomarkers for predicting the progression have been examined in IgAN, such as markers of renal damage, cytokines/chemokines, and receptor biomarkers [8–10]. However, the ability of non-invasive indicators to screen and predict the progression of IgAN is full of challenges, due to varying degrees of limitations. According to the KDIGO 2021 clinical practice guideline for the management of glomerular diseases, it remains lack of validated serum or urine biomarkers for the evaluation of IgAN [11]. Hence, it is imperative to explore new non-invasive biomarkers to evaluate the progression and prognosis of IgAN.

Activated Leukocyte Cell Adhesion Molecule (ALCAM), also called cluster of differentiation 166 (CD166), belongs to the immunoglobulin superfamily and is identified on a wide range of cell including activated leukocytes, epithelial cells, endothelial cells, myeloid progenitors, hematopoietic stem cells, and tumor cells [12]. ALCAM can regulate complex cellular process including cell growth, adhesion, and migration, and mediate heterophilic and homophilic cell-cell communications [13]. ALCAM also promotes the transendothelial migration of T lymphocytes and monocytes [14]. In vitro studies have demonstrated that soluble ALCAM played an important role in ALCAM-dependent and -independent immune responses during inflammatory process [15]. Elevated expression of ALCAM has been observed in the kidney and other tissues, and may be involved in the pathogenesis of inflammatory diseases [16]. Increasing evidence has showed the infiltration of leukocytes and renal inflammation are important mechanisms in IgAN [1]. Within the context of IgAN, the expression status and roles of ALCAM remains unknown.

In the current study, we investigated the expression of ALCAM and its major ligand S100B in patients with IgAN, and evaluate the correlation of ALCAM with the severity of disease. In addition, the prognostic values of ALCAM in the development of IgAN were examined.

Patients and methods

Patient cohort

A total of 156 adult newly biopsy-proven IgAN patients from December 2018 to December 2021 in Xiangya Hospital were initially included in this prospective study. Finally, 71 biopsy-proven IgAN patients were enrolled (Supplementary Fig. 1). The inclusion criteria included: age ≥ 18 years; IgAN newly diagnosed by a percutaneous renal biopsy; agree to participate in this study with written informed consent; no previous therapy with glucocorticoids or immunosuppressants. The exclusion criteria included: secondary IgAN caused by allergic purpura, hepatitis B virus infection, systemic lupus erythematosus, tumors, or other diseases; complicated with other diseases including diabetes, connective tissue disease, malignancy, cardiovascular disease, active infection (HBV, HCV, and HIV seropositive status), pregnancy, anemia, and liver disease, the number of glomeruli in renal biopsy specimens < 10, the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2. IgAN patients were followed up for at least 12 months or until reaching the study endpoints. 41 age and gender- matched healthy controls (serum samples) were also included. Eight age and sex-matched adjacent normal renal samples from renal cell carcinoma patients were obtained as control kidney tissues.

The patients’ rights and safety were protected complied with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants signed informed consent prior to enrollment. The study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Xiangya Hospital, Central South University (approval number 201711836).

Clinical parameters

Baseline parameters including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), mean arterial pressure (MAP), history of health, and medication at admission were recorded. Serum samples were collected at enrollment. Biochemical parameters in blood including serum creatinine (Scr), serum uric acid, hemoglobin (Hb), albumin, IgA, complement C3 (C3), and lipids were detected routinely. The urine samples were obtained to measure the quantity of proteinuria and presence of hematuria before renal biopsy. Urine protein to creatinine ratio (PCR) and 24 h urinary protein quantification were detected. eGFR was evaluated by the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) formula [17]. The concentration of serum galactose-deficient IgA1 (Gd-IgA1) was detected using an ELISA kit pre-coated with KM55 (IBL, Fujioka, Japan).

Renal biopsy

Renal biopsy was performed with core needle biopsies (18 gauge) under ultrasonographic guidance in consenting subjects. Renal biopsy samples of IgAN patients consistent with the serum of patients were collected. Kidney specimens were collected and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde overnight, then transferred to 70% ethanol, embedded in paraffin, and cut into approximately 3 μm thick sections serially. Subsequently, sections were obtained for routine light microscopy stainings, including hematoxylin-eosin (HE), periodic acid-Schiff (PAS), periodic acid-silver metheramine (PASM), and masson’s trichrome (Masson’s) staining. The frozen renal tissues were prepared for immunofluorescence staining with IgA, IgG, IgM, C3, C4, C1q, κ, and λ. Electron microscopy detection was also completed. IgAN pathology was evaluated according to the new Oxford classification of the IgAN scoring system (MEST-C scoring system) and Lee’s grading system [18]. Renal biopsies diagnosis was assessed by two pathologists in our hospital. The new Oxford classification of IgAN scoring system includes mesangial hypercellularity (M), endocapillary hypercellularity (E), segmental glomerulosclerosis (S), interstitial fibrosis/tubular atrophy (T), and crescentic lesions (C).

Measurement of ALCAM in serum and urine by ELISA

Serum and urine samples from the subjects were obtained at the time of enrollment, and immediately stored at -80 °C before measurement. All specimens were thawed only once until detection. The concentrations of ALCAM in serum and urine were tested with a sample dilution of 1:5 using ELISA kits (Cusabio Biotech, Wuhan, China; catalogue: CSB-E04487h), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Urinary ALCAM (uALCAM) concentrations were normalized by urinary creatinine levels. Urinary creatinine levels from the same sample were evaluated by the Creatinine Parameter Assay Kit (HOMA Biological, Beijing, China; catalogue number: F1921). The levels in samples were calculated according to the standard curves. All measurements were performed in triplicate.

Immunohistochemistry

Kidney specimens were collected and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde overnight, then transferred to 70% ethanol, and embedded in paraffin, and cut into approximately 3 μm thick sections serially. To analyze the expression of ALCAM and S100B in kidney by immunohistochemistry, sections were stained with anti-ALCAM (1:100 dilution, Abcam, ab109215) and anti-S100B (1:1000 dilution, Abcam, ab52642) antibodies respectively according to the manufacturer’s protocols. The sections were analyzed with light microscopy after staining. The images were quantified by ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health).

Immunofluorescence staining

Kidney tissue slides were deparaffinized and rehydrated according to routine protocols. Immunofluorescence analysis was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Primary antibodies and dilutions were as follows: anti-ALCAM (1:100 dilution, Abcam, ab109215) and anti-S100B (1:100 dilution, Abcam, ab52642) antibodies. DAPI was added to counterstain the nuclei. The images were observed under the fluorescence microscope. The image analysis and quantification were performed using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health).

Renal outcome definition

For each IgAN subject, the date of renal biopsy was defined as the baseline point. Follow-up period was established as the interval between the date of renal biopsy and the last follow-up, or ESRD (the onset of maintenance dialysis or renal transplantation). Chronic kidney disease (CKD) progression and the composite endpoint were defined as the cumulative percentage of patients who developed a reduction of eGFR ≥ 50% from the baseline level or ESRD (renal survival). The survival time for each participant was calculated from baseline time to the last follow-up or the primary end point in this study.

Statistics

1. Continuous variables normally distributed were presented as the mean ± standard deviation (M ± SD), and analyzed with Student’s t-test. Variables skewedly distributed were expressed as medians and interquartile range (IQR), and analyzed with the non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test. Nominal variables were presented as counts or percentages. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare three or more groups. Pearson’s correlation test was used to analyze relationships among the variables with continuous and normal distribution. The nonparametric Spearman’s correlation test was conducted for variables with skewed distribution. The optimal cut-off value for high and low levels of serum ALCAM in renal progression was determined by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The optimal cut-off value was identified using the Youden’s J index (J = sensitivity + specificity-1), which maximizes the balance between sensitivity and specificity. The ROC curve was plotted with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for the area under the curve (AUC) calculated using the bootstrap method to assess the stability of the AUC value. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis was conducted to analyze independent risk factors affecting the prognosis of IgAN. Variables with P value < 0.10 in the univariate Cox regression analysis was chose for multivariate Cox regression analysis. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were used to evaluate the association between serum ALCAM and renal outcome, and significance was analyzed using the log-rank test. Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0 software (IBM Corp, New York, USA). P < 0.05 was considered a significant difference. Graphs were plotted using GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software Inc, La Jolla, USA).

Results

Baseline features of IgAN patients

A total of 71 biopsy-proven IgAN patients were finally enrolled in this prospective cohort study in line with the inclusion and exclusion criteria (Supplementary Fig. 1). The baseline demographic and clinical data of IgAN patients and controls were shown in Table 1. Among all 71 IgAN patients, 42 (59.15%) were males, and the mean age was 33.17±12.38 years. There was no significant difference between IgAN patients and controls with regard to age, sex, BMI, Hb, albumin, IgA, and lipids (Table 1). IgAN patients presented with obvious proteinuria with the average level of 1.47±0.79 g/24 h, elevated Scr (85.49 ± 27.82 vs. 72.43 ± 13.30 µmol/L, P < 0.01), and decreased eGFR (90.59 ± 24.18 ml/min/1.73m2 vs. 109.52 ± 15.73 ml/min/1.73m2, P < 0.01). The MAP of IgAN patients was 97.13 ± 10.08 mmHg, with a 21.13% proportion of hypertension. Moreover, IgAN patients displayed more hematuria and elevated serum uric acid than control subjects (P < 0.01). Serum Gd-IgA1 was detected in 56 patients, and the concentration was 5.06 (4.35, 7.14) µg/mL.

Serum ALCAM concentrations were positively correlated with proteinuria in IgAN patients

As presented in Fig. 1a, serum ALCAM concentrations were elevated in patients with IgAN compared with controls (5.04 ± 2.45 ng/mL vs. 8.07 ± 1.10 ng/mL; P < 0.001). The uALCAM/creatinine levels in patients with IgAN were higher compared to healthy subjects (Supplementary Fig. 2, 27.28 ± 3.14 ng/mg vs. 16.82 ± 4.25 ng/mg; P<0.01). However, there were no significant correlations between uALCAM/creatinine levels and important clinical indicators including proteinuria, Scr, C3, and Gd-IgA1 in IgAN patients.

To compare the differences in serum ALCAM levels between IgAN patients with different degrees of proteinuria, IgAN patients in this study were divided into two groups, proteinuria ≥ 1.0 g/day and proteinuria < 1.0 g/day respectively. Our results showed that IgAN patients with proteinuria ≥ 1.0 g/day had a higher levels of serum ALCAM compared with the group with proteinuria < 1 g/day (Fig. 1b, P < 0.01). We analyzed whether serum ALCAM levels were correlated with clinical parameters including proteinuria and Scr in IgAN patients. Our research showed that serum ALCAM levels were correlated positively with 24 h urinary protein amount and serum Gd-IgA1 in IgAN patients (Fig. 1c and d, r = 0.427, P = 0.001; r = 0.309, P = 0.021, respectively). There were no significant correlations between serum ALCAM levels and other indicators including C3, BMI, MAP, Scr, serum uric acid, Hb, and albumin in IgAN patients (Fig. 1e, Supplementary Table 2).

The correlation of serum ALCAM levels with renal pathology in IgAN patients

To evaluate the relationship between circulating ALCAM levels and renal pathological damage, we compared the concentrations of serum ALCAM among different IgAN patients groups based on Lee’s classification or the new Oxford classification. Since the sample size of Lee’s grade IV was only 7 cases, Lee’s grade III and Lee’s grade IV were merged into one group for analysis. There were no IgAN patients with Grade V in our study. As illustrated in Supplementary Fig. 3a, serum ALCAM levels were low in the control group, elevated in Lee’s grade I and II IgAN patients, and highest in Lee’s grade III + IV group. Accordingly, serum ALCAM levels were positively correlated with Lee’s grades (Supplementary Fig. 3b, r = 0.364; P = 0.002).

There was no significant difference in serum ALCAM levels between M0 and M1 groups, E0 and E1 groups, as well as T0 and T1 + T2 groups (P > 0.05). We found that serum ALCAM levels were higher‌ in the S1 group ‌than‌ in the S0 group (Supplementary Fig. 3c, P < 0.001). As expected, C1 + C2 group exhibited higher serum ALCAM levels than C0 group (Supplementary Fig. 3d, P < 0.001). These results indicated that serum ALCAM levels were associated with the severity of renal pathological change.

ALCAM and S100B protein expression in kidneys of IgAN patients

ALCAM and its ligand S100B expression was detected in kidneys between the IgAN and control subjects by immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence staining. The results of these two staining techniques were consistent. As illustrated in Supplementary Fig. 4a-c and Fig. 5a-d, ALCAM protein expression was primarily located in renal tubular epithelial cells, with few expression in glomeruli. Furthermore, ALCAM protein expression was significantly higher in the kidneys of IgAN patients compared to the control kidneys. Also, S100B protein was mainly expressed in renal tubules. However, there was no significant difference in S100B protein expression between IgAN and control groups.

Differences between the low serum ALCAM and high serum ALCAM groups in IgAN patients

As displayed in Fig. 2a, ROC analysis indicated that the optimal cut-off value of serum ALCAM to predict progression of IgAN was 4.76 ng/mL. At this level, the sensitivity and specificity were 83.3% and 61.0% respectively, and the AUC was 0.769 (95% CI, 0.640–0.899; P = 0.003). We also combined the serum ALCAM with proteinuria using ROC analysis, and the AUC was 0.791 (95% CI, 0.663–0.913; P = 0.002). Subsequently, patients were divided into the low serum ALCAM group (ALCAM < 4.76 ng/mL, n = 36) and the high serum ALCAM group (ALCAM ≥ 4.76 ng/mL, n = 35) based on the level of ALCAM at the time of renal biopsy. Baseline clinical features of patients suggested that there were no significant differences between the low ALCAM and high ALCAM groups with regard to age, sex, BMI, MAP, hypertension, Hb, albumin, and IgA (Table 2). During the follow-up period, the time of duration and the main therapeutic drugs were comparable between the two groups. While worse renal function, and higher levels of serum uric acid, proteinuria, and hematuria were shown in the high ALCAM group (P < 0.01 or P < 0.05). Regarding pathological damage, patients with high ALCAM displayed a higher proportion of Lee grade III/IV, segmental glomerulosclerosis, and crescents than patients with low level (P < 0.01 or P < 0.05). There were no significant differences in M, E, and T scores between the two groups.

Relationship between serum ALCAM levels and CKD progression

The median follow-up time of patients with IgAN was 39.96 (24.35, 51.70) months. In this study cohort, CKD progression was observed in 16.9% (n = 12) of the patients during follow-up period. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed to evaluate the risk factors for predicting the progression of IgAN except established risk markers. Univariate analysis revealed that male, hypertension, elevated proteinuira, elevated Scr, decreased Hb, increased serum uric acid, and elevated serum ALCAM were the potential risk factors of renal progression (Supplementary Table 3, P < 0.10). Multivariate analysis revealed that elevated proteinuria, Scr, and serum uric acid were independent risk factors for CKD progression after adjustment for important clinical indexes, such as age, Hb, and albumin. Notably, high level of initial serum ALCAM, was also an independent risk factors for progression of IgAN after such adjustment (HR = 2.036; 95% CI, 1.229–4.656; P = 0.018). Then, we assessed the effect of baseline level of serum ALCAM on renal outcome. The proportions of CKD progression was increased in high ALCAM group (22.86%) compared with low level group (11.11%). As shown in Fig. 2b, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed that the cumulative survival rate was reduced in patients with high serum ALCAM level than the low group (P < 0.05).

Discussion

The current study revealed for the first time that the levels of circulating ALCAM at the time of biopsy were significantly elevated in IgAN patients as compared to healthy controls, which were correlated with the severity of proteinuria and renal pathological lesions. The protein expression of ALCAM was also increased in renal tissues of patients with IgAN. Cox regression analysis and Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that high serum ALCAM level was an independent risk factor and predictor for the progression of IgAN. Thus, this research demonstrated that circulating ALCAM might have prognostic value for predicting renal progression in IgAN.

ALCAM is a cell surface glycoprotein which can be cleaved proteolytically at the extracellular domain by the cell membrane metalloprotease ADAM-17. The soluble form of ALCAM is shed into body fluid such as sera and ascitic fluid [19, 20]. ALCAM has been gradually applied as a biomarker for the diagnosis and prognosis prediction in different cancers [21]. Further, ALCAM has been implicated in the pathogenesis of multiple inflammatory and immune responses. Its expression can be further increased under proinflammatory or cell-activating conditions [22, 23]. uALCAM was higher in active lupus nephritis (LN) patients than inactive LN or active systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) without kidney injury, and predicted long-term renal damage progression. Moreover, uALCAM served as a strong predictor of LN and showed predictive value for response status [24]. Our data suggested that serum ALCAM levels were higher in IgAN patients than normal controls. Serum ALCAM levels had a positive correlation with proteinuria and the severity of renal pathological damage of Lee’s grade. Serum ALCAM levels were also elevated in group S1 of the Oxford classification, compared with group S0. C1 + C2 group displayed higher serum ALCAM levels than C0 group. These data implied ALCAM had certain clinical significance in IgAN.

Based on the above data, we analyzed the associations between serum ALCAM level and progression, and prognosis in participants with IgAN. High ALCAM group displayed more severe clinical indexes including worse renal function, elevated serum uric acid, proteinuria, and hematuria. Similarly, more severe renal pathological damage including higher ratios of Lee grade III/IV, segmental glomerulosclerosis, and crescent were shown in patients with high ALCAM than low ALCAM group. Notably, we observed a lower cumulative renal survival rate and worse renal outcome in high ALCAM group than low level group during follow-up. Importantly, we also found high serum ALCAM level was an independent risk factor for the progression of IgAN. Consequently, elevated baseline circulating ALCAM levels might be a potential marker for prediction of the progression of IgAN.

Recent studies have detected the expression of ALCAM in tissues during the process of kidney diseases. Single-cell RNA seq analysis of kidney from LN patients clarified that strong expression of ALCAM within the inflamed kidneys including renal tubular cells, mesangial cells, and infiltrating leukocytes [25]. In type 2 diabetic nephropathy, serum ALCAM concentration and ALCAM expression in both the glomeruli and tubules were obviously increased, indicating ALCAM acted as a mediator in renal complication of diabetes [26]. In this research, renal ALCAM protein expression was mainly elevated in renal tubular cells of IgAN patients compared with controls. However, ALCAM has been identified as a marker not only in malignancies but also in proliferative nephritic syndromes such as lupus nephritis. The elevated serum levels of ALCAM in IgAN may reflect general proliferative or inflammatory glomerular activity, and may not be specific to IgAN. As a potential ligand for ALCAM, S100B with danger-associated molecular pattern activity, have been documented to engage ALCAM and trigger inflammatory response through NF-κB activation [27]. But our data implied that the expression of S100B protein located in tubules was comparable between IgAN and control groups. The elevation of circulating ALCAM and its deposition in the kidneys indicated that ALCAM might emerge as a trigger for inflammatory response and reflect a state of inflammation in IgAN. Local production of ALCAM by kidney resident cells including tubular cells, endothelial cells, and immune cells might be the major source of local and circulating ALCAM, since ALCAM has been involved in the recruitment of lymphocytes and inflammatory reaction in the CKD [28]. ALCAM might be regarded as a potential therapeutic target for reducing the inflammation and immune response in the CKD.

In the present study, the elevation of circulating and tissue expression of ALCAM was proved in patients with IgAN, but the underlying mechanisms are still elusive. Although there were no relevant researches regarding the roles of ALCAM in the development of IgAN, its possible molecular mechanisms may be inspired by studies in other diseases. (1) Mediation of renal inflammatory cell infiltration. ALCAM is a well-characterized adhesion molecule critical for leukocyte-endothelial cell interaction and transendothelial migration. ALCAM have an impact on recruiting activated inflammatory cells, and facilitates the activated leukocytes into inflamed kidneys, as well as destruction of kidneys in LN [16, 29]. Immunologically, ALCAM can interact with CD6 primarily expressed on T effector cells, modulate immune reaction including T cell differentiation, activation, and trafficking, which further persist in the inflammatory cycle [13, 14, 18]. It indicated that ALCAM blockade maybe a potential target for reducing the inflammatory infiltrates in IgAN. (2) Regulation of podocyte dysfunction and glomerular barrier integrity. ALCAM is expressed on podocytes and participates in maintaining podocyte-matrix adhesion and cell-cell communication [26]. Dysregulated ALCAM expression may disrupt podocyte integrity, impair the glomerular filtration barrier, and contribute to the progression of IgAN. (3) Modulation of renal fibrosis progression. ALCAM has been implicated in promoting fibrosis in other renal diseases by regulating the activation of renal fibroblasts and the accumulation of extracellular matrix. Althouth the molecular mechanisms of ALCAM involved in the pathogenesis of IgAN remains unclear, it may mediates leukocyte adhesion and infitration, differing from B-cell targeted agents and complement blockers.

Nevertheless, there were some limitations in this research. First, it was a single-centre study with a relative small number of subjects. Thus we should perform multi-center cohort and expand the sample size to confirm these findings. Second, we only carried out the single measurement of serum ALCAM at baseline, but not monitored the dynamic change of serum ALCAM level during follow-up. Third, we examined the serum and uALCAM levels without investigating the cellular source, and its underlying specific mechanism. Future studies should be conducted to measure the expression level of ALCAM in distinct cell types especially the immune cells in kidney. Fourth, only 12 patients (16.9%) experienced CKD progression, given the relatively small number of events, the robustness of the regression analyses may be limited. Finally, this study only suggested that ALCAM might be associated with the progression of IgAN, we could not concluded that serum ALCAM was specific for IgAN due to lack of other kidney diseases for comparison. Despite the limitations, the findings of this study were all real-life data from IgAN patients.

Conclusion

In summary, circulating ALCAM level was obviously increased in patients with IgAN, which were positively correlated with the severity of proteinuria and renal pathological lesions. Renal ALCAM expression was also upregulated in IgAN patients. IgAN patients with increased serum ALCAM at onset displayed more severe clinical and pathological manifestations. Additionally, high serum ALCAM level was associated with a decreased cumulative renal survival rate, and served as an independent risk factor for the progression of IgAN. Therefore, our study indicated that high level of serum ALCAM emerged as a novel non-invasive predictor of poor renal outcome in IgAN. However, the molecular mechanisms of ALCAM involved in the pathogenesis of IgAN need to be further elucidated.

On ratios of Chern numbers for complex hyperbolic branched covers
Barry Minemyer

Abstract
. In this paper we prove that, at least in even complex dimensions, the ratio of Chern numbers for a closed complex hyperbolic branched cover manifold are not all equal to the corresponding ratio of Chern numbers for a closed complex hyperbolic manifold. This leads to an answer for a question posed by Deraux and Seshadri, and proves that an almost 1 / 4 1/4 -pinched metric constructed by the author in a previous article is not Kähler.

Primary 55R25, 57R20; Secondary 53C20, 53C24

1.
Introduction

We say that a manifold
M
is
complex hyperbolic
if its universal cover is isometric to complex hyperbolic space
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
In the literature such manifolds are frequently referred to as
complex ball quotients
.
Note that, in this case,
M=\Gamma\setminus\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n}
for some torsion free lattice
\Gamma<\text{PU}(n,1)
.
In this article we will say that a pair
(M,N)
is
modeled on

(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n},\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n-1})
if
M
is complex hyperbolic with complex dimension
n
,
N\subset M
is embedded and totally geodesic,
N
has real codimension
2
, and the lift of
N
to the universal cover of
M
is isometric to disjoint copies of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n-1}
.

Recently in
[
18
]
Stover and Toledo proved the following.
Choose an integer
d\geq 2
, and suppose that
\Gamma
as above is a cocompact congruence arithmetic lattice of simple type.
Then there exists a finite cover
(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})
of
(M,N)
such that the fundamental class
[N^{\prime}]\in H_{2n-2}(M^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})
is
d
-divisible.
Consequently, the
d
-fold cyclic branched cover
X
of
M^{\prime}
about
N^{\prime}
exists and is a smooth manifold.
These complex hyperbolic branched cover manifolds admit a negatively curved Kähler metric by a result of Zheng
[
22
]
, and their existence has led to the solution of several open problems in complex geometry.
For example, Guenancia and Hamenstädt have shown that a large class of these manifolds admit a negatively curved Kähler-Einstein metric
[
6
]
(see also the related
[
12
]
).
Additionally, the author has shown that these manifolds admit an almost negatively
1/4
-pinched Riemannian metric
[
15
]
and that this same metric has nonpositive curvature operator
[
16
]
.

The purpose of this short note is to study the ratio of Chern numbers for these complex hyperbolic branched cover manifolds
X
.
Our main result in this direction is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume the integer
n\geq 2
is even, and let
(M,N)
be modeled on
(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n},\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n-1})
with
M
closed.
Then for all but possibly finitely-many integers
d\geq 2
we have the following.
Let
(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})
be an arbitrary finite cover of
(M,N)
such that
[N^{\prime}]
is
d
-divisible, and let
X
denote the
d
-fold cyclic branched cover.
Then there exists a ratio of Chern numbers of
X
which is not equal to the corresponding ratio of Chern numbers of a complex hyperbolic manifold.

For
n=2
we give a similar, but slightly different argument to prove the following stronger statement.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
n=2
and for any integer
d\geq 2
, the
d
-fold cyclic branched cover
X
described in Theorem
1.1
satisfies
\displaystyle{c_{1}^{2}(X)-3c_{2}(X)\neq 0.}

Remark 1.3
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we actually prove that

c_{1}^{2}(X)-3c_{2}(X)=m\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{2d}\chi(N)

where
m
is the degree of the cover
(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})\longrightarrow(M,N)
and
\chi(N)
denotes the Euler characteristic of
N
.

Remark 1.4
.

It is likely that Theorem
1.1
holds for all dimensions
n
, all choices of ramification degree
d\geq 2
, and all ratios of Chern numbers.
But the argument that we give below using Hirzebruch’s proportionality and signature theorems is only sufficient for even complex dimensions and for some ratio of Chern numbers.
The condition of “all but finitely many values of
d
” is due to the difficulty in giving exact calculations for the signature of intersections of appropriate submanifolds of
M
(see Remark
3.2
and the proof of Theorem
1.1
).
A calculation by Deraux
[
2
]
when
n=3
lends further evidence that this holds in all dimensions.
Also, specific (and much more detailed) calculations for
n=2
were computed for the original branched-cover examples by Mostow-Siu
[
17
]
and Hirzebruch
[
10
]
.

Theorem
1.1
shows that such branched cover manifolds
X
are not complex hyperbolic manifolds, but Stover and Toledo gave an alternate argument for this fact for all such branched covers in
[
18
]
.
The reason why we are interested in the ratio of these Chern numbers is due to a question posed by Deraux and Seshadri in
[
3
]
.
To state this question we need to first describe the main result from
[
3
]
, which is as follows.
Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and let
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exists
\delta(\varepsilon,n)>0
such that, if
Y
is a compact Kähler
n
-manifold whose sectional curvatures (with respect to the Kähler metric) are contained in the
\delta
-neighborhood of
[-1,-1/4]
, then the ratio of any two Chern numbers of
Y
must be contained within the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the corresponding ratio of Chern numbers of a complex hyperbolic manifold.
The question asked by Deraux and Seshadri is the following.

Question

(Deraux and Seshadri
[
3
]
)
.

Does the result above hold if one only assumes the existence of an almost
1/4
-pinched Riemannian metric on a compact Kähler manifold?

This question is interesting due to a result of Hernandez
[
7
]
and Yau and Zheng
[
21
]
.
This result is that, if a compact Kähler manifold
Y
admits a negatively
1/4
-pinched Riemannian metric
g
(which is not assumed to be Kähler), then
Y
is isometric to a complex hyperbolic manifold.
In particular, not only must
g
be Kähler, but it must actually equal the standard complex hyperbolic metric.
This result lends some anecdotal evidence that the question of Deraux and Seshadri may possibly be answered in the affirmitive.

The author proved in
[
15
]
that the complex hyperbolic branched cover manifolds of Stover and Toledo admit an almost negatively
1/4
-pinched metric, provided the normal injectivity radius of the branching locus is sufficiently large.
This result, combined with
[
3
]
and Theorem
1.1
, give the following two corollaries.

Corollary 1.5
.

The answer to the question of Deraux and Seshadri is “No”.
More specifically, there exists a sequence of compact Kähler
n
-manifolds
(X_{k})
whose ratio of Chern numbers are a bounded distance away from those of a complex hyperbolic manifold, but which admit a Riemannian metric with all sectional curvatures contained in the interval
[-1-(1/k),(-1/4)+(1/k)]
.

Corollary 1.6
.

The almost negatively
1/4
-pinched Riemannian metric constructed in
[
15
]
(see also
[
16
]
) is not Kähler.

Note that the two corollaries do not immediately follow from Theorem
1.1
.
Suppose that
(M,N)
is modeled on
(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n},\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n-1})
with
M
closed.
Let
(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})
be a finite cover so that
[N^{\prime}]
is
d
-divisible, and let
X
be the
d
-fold cyclic branched cover of
M^{\prime}
about
N^{\prime}
.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, a metric is constructed in
[
15
]
on such a branched cover
X
which is
\varepsilon
-close to being negatively
1/4
-pinched.
But, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, one needs the normal injectivity radius about
N^{\prime}
in
M^{\prime}
to approach infinity.
This requires one to take increasingly large finite covers of the pair
(M,N)
.
In theory, it is possible that the ratio of Chern numbers of
X
approach those of a complex hyperbolic manifold as one chooses larger covers
(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})
to satisfy this condition on the normal injectivity radius.
We will see in Section
3
that this is not the case and, moreover, the exact opposite happens.

The proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are simple calculations that use much deeper results, mostly due to Hirzebruch.
Namely, we use Hirzebruch proportionality
[
8
]
, the Hirzebruch signature theorem
[
11
, Theorem 8.2.2]
(see also
[
14
, Theorem 19.4]
), and Hirzebruch’s work on the signature of cyclic branched coverings
[
9
]
.
To some experts the calculations in our proofs are trivial and, to that end, the latter two references of Hirzebruch were easy for the author to find.
But the author, being much more of a geometer than a topologist, was unaware of Hirzebruch proportionality and its immediate consequences.
References for this were much more difficult for the author to find and, for this reason, in Section
2
below we give a brief overview of Hirzebruch proportionality for Chern numbers and its relationship to the signature and Euler characteristic of a closed complex hyperbolic manifold.
We then discuss Hirzebruch’s work on the signature of a cyclic branched cover in Section
3
, where we also prove the theorems and corollaries listed above.

2.
Hirzebruch Proportionality for Chern Numbers

The author is unaware of any reference where the content of this section is explicitly recorded in English, although Lafont and Roy discuss a very similar result for Pontrjagin numbers
[
13
, Theorem A]
.
All of the content in this Section is due to Hirzebruch
[
8
]
, and is also referenced in the proof of Theorem 22.2.1 in
[
11
]
.

Let us first fix some notation.
Following the standard definition of
[
14
, Chapter 16]
, a
partition
of a positive integer
n
is an unordered sequence
I=i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}
of positive integers that sum to
n
.
For a manifold
M
of complex dimension
n
, we use the following notation.

•

\Sigma(M)
will denote the signature of
M
.

•

p_{I}(M)
will denote the Pontrjagin number with partition
I
.

•

c_{I}(M)
will denote the Chern number with partition
I
.

•

\chi(M)
will denote the Euler characteristic of
M
.

Let
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
denote complex projective space of dimension
n
.
The standard background reference is
[
14
]
.

In terms of Chern numbers of closed complex hyperbolic manifolds, Hirzebruch’s proportionality can be stated as follows.

Theorem 2.1

(Hirzebruch
[
8
]
)
.

Let
M^{n}
be a closed complex hyperbolic manifold.
Then there exists a constant
s
depending on
M
, but not on the partition
I
, such that

c_{I}(M)=s\cdot c_{I}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}).

Corollary 2.2
.

The ratio of Chern numbers for any closed complex hyperbolic manifold is equal to the corresponding ratio of Chern numbers for
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
That is, for any closed complex hyperbolic manifold
M
,

\frac{c_{I}(M)}{c_{J}(M)}=\frac{c_{I}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n})}{c_{J}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n})}

for all partitions
I,J
of
n
.

Theorem
2.1
and the following corollary are well-known to experts.
Indeed, references
[
17
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
22
]
, and
[
2
]
all, in some capacity, reference the fact that the ratio of Chern numbers of closed complex hyperbolic manifolds are equal to a constant independent of
M
.
Reference
[
13
]
, as well as an email from Matthew Stover, confirmed to the author that one uses the dual
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
to compute these ratios of Chern numbers.
Formulas to compute Chern numbers for
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
are well-known (see, for example,
[
14
, Example 15.6]
).

One immediate consequence of Theorem
2.1
is that, given a closed complex hyperbolic manifold
M
, every Chern number
c_{I}(M)
can be written as a known constant times the Euler characteristic
\chi(M)
.
This is done by considering the ratio of
c_{I}(M)
with
c_{n}(M)=\chi(M)
.
For example, if we let
I=n-1,1
, we have

\frac{c_{I}(M)}{c_{n}(M)}=\frac{{n+1\choose n-1}{n+1\choose 1}}{{n+1\choose n}}=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c_{I}(M)=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\chi(M).

The following Corollary is easy to find in the literature when
n=2
(see, for example,
[
19
]
).
For
n>2
the author only knows of the original source
[
8
]
.
We give a quick proof of the result since it is crucial for Section
3
.

Corollary 2.3

(Satz 1 (4) of
[
8
]
)
.

Let
n>1
be an even integer, and let
M
be any closed complex
n
-manifold whose ratio of Chern numbers is equal to those of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
(for every possible ratio).
Then the signature
\Sigma(M)
and Euler characteristic
\chi(M)
are related by the formula

\Sigma(M)=\frac{1}{n+1}\chi(M).

Proof.

By Hirzebruch’s Signature Theorem (
[
11
, Theorem 8.2.2]
or
[
14
, Theorem 19.4]
), one knows that
\Sigma(M)
can be written as a linear combination of the Pontrjagin numbers.
A specific formula is given by the
L-genus
of
M
, but that is not important here.
It is well known
[
14
, Corollary 15.5]
that each Pontrjagin number can be written as a linear combination of Chern numbers.
Via substitution, one has that

\Sigma(M)=\sum_{I\in[n]}\alpha(I,n)c_{I}(M)

where
[n]
denotes the set of partitions of
n
.

By assumption, all Chern numbers of
M
can be written as a specific constant times the Euler characteristic of
M
as noted above.
This gives

(2.1)

\Sigma(M)=f(n)\chi(M)

where the coefficient
f(n)
is independent of
M
.
Equation (
2.1
) holds for all manifolds
M
whose ratio of Chern numbers is equal to the corresponding ratio of Chern numbers for
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
In particular, it holds for
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
It is again well known that
\Sigma(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n})=1

[
14
, Page 225]
and
\chi(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n})=n+1
(
[
14
, Theorem 14.4]
, or is obvious from the standard CW-structure of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
).
Solving for
f
gives the desired formula.

∎

3.
The signature of a ramified covering and proofs of main theorems

In this section we review the main results of
[
9
]
and
[
20
]
, and then prove the statements from the Introduction.
To this end, let us first recall the set-up for Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
Let
n\geq 1
be an even integer and
d\geq 2
an integer.
Suppose that
(M,N)
is modeled on
(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n},\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n-1})
with
M
closed.
Let
(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})
be a finite cover of degree
m
such that
[N^{\prime}]\in H_{n-2}(M^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})
is
d
-divisible.
Let
X
denote the
d
-fold cyclic branched cover of
M^{\prime}
about
N^{\prime}
.
We use
Y
to denote the branching locus in
X
, which is isometric to
N^{\prime}
.
The general situation is as follows.

(X,Y)\underset{\text{d-fold branched cover}}{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow}(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})\underset{\text{degree m regular cover}}{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow}(M,N).

3.1.
The signature of
X
in terms of
M^{\prime}
and
N^{\prime}

First, almost all of the material from this subsection comes from
[
9
]
and
[
20
]
.
Where possible, we try to use the same notation as
[
9
]
for readability.

Consider the rational function

\text{sign}(t)=\frac{(1+t)^{d}+(1-t)^{d}}{(1+t)^{d}-(1-t)^{d}}\cdot t.

In
[
9
, Section 5]
Hirzebruch proves that this function determines the signature of
X
in terms of
M^{\prime}
and
N^{\prime}
, where
d
as above denotes the degree of the branched cover.
How this is interpreted is as follows.
Expand sign
(t)
as a formal power series in
t
.
The first few terms of this series are

\text{sign}(t)=\frac{1}{d}+\frac{d^{2}-1}{3d}t^{2}-\frac{(d^{2}-1)(d^{2}-4)}{45d}t^{4}+\ldots.

Then

(3.1)

\Sigma(M^{\prime})=\frac{1}{d}\Sigma(X)+\frac{d^{2}-1}{3d}\Sigma(Y_{2})-\frac{(d^{2}-1)(d^{2}-4)}{45d}\Sigma(Y_{4})+\ldots.

where, for each positive integer
r
,
Y_{r}
is a submanifold of
X
whose construction we explain below.
But first note that one can rewrite equation (
3.1
) as

(3.2)

\Sigma(X)=d\Sigma(M^{\prime})-\frac{d^{2}-1}{3}\Sigma(Y_{2})+\frac{(d^{2}-1)(d^{2}-4)}{45}\Sigma(Y_{4})-\ldots.

The submanifolds
Y_{r}
are just transverse intersections of perturbations of the submanifold
Y
, which we define recursively.
First,
Y_{1}=Y
.
Now, assuming that
Y_{r}
is defined for some positive integer
r
, we can perturb
Y_{r}
to obtain a submanifold
Y_{r}^{\prime}
which is transverse to
Y
.
Define
Y_{r+1}=Y_{r}^{\prime}\cap Y
.
The signature of such a manifold is independent of the choice of (transverse) perturbation, and note that this procedure can be accomplished in such a way so that
Y_{r+1}\subset Y_{r}
for each
r
.

Notice that
Y_{r+1}
will have codimension
2
within
Y_{r}
.
Therefore, if
r
is even, then
Y_{r}
will have dimension a multiple of
4
.
Thus, all signatures in (
3.2
) are potentially non-zero.
Also, for
n
even, the maximal number of nonzero terms on the right-hand side of (
3.2
) is
1+(n/2)
.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
\nu
denote the normal bundle of
Y
in
X
, and let
\nu^{\prime}
denote the normal bundle of
N^{\prime}
in
M^{\prime}
.
Since
Y\cong N^{\prime}
we may consider both of these bundles as being over
N^{\prime}
, where it follows immediately that
\nu^{\prime}=\nu^{d}
.
Both bundles
\nu^{\prime}
and
\nu
can be extended to complex line bundles over
M^{\prime}
, which we denote
E^{\prime}
and
E
, respectively.
From our above considerations we have that
E^{\prime}=E^{d}
, from which follows that
c_{1}(E^{\prime})=dc_{1}(E)
.

Remark 3.2
.

We wish to consider the top Chern class
c_{r}(Y_{r}^{\perp})
, where
Y_{r}^{\perp}
denotes the normal bundle of
Y_{r}
in
X
.
Notice that
Y_{r}^{\perp}
splits as a direct sum of the portion that is tangent to
Y
, denoted
Y_{r}^{\perp}\bigr|_{Y}
, and the part that is orthogonal to
Y
, denoted
Y^{\perp}\bigr|_{Y_{r}}
.
We write this as

Y_{r}^{\perp}=Y_{r}^{\perp}\bigr|_{Y}\oplus Y^{\perp}\bigr|_{Y_{r}}.

By the Chern class version of the Whitney sum formula
[
14
, Equation 14.7]
we have, for the top Chern classes,

c_{r}(Y_{r}^{\perp})=c_{r-1}(Y_{r}^{\perp}\bigr|_{Y})\cdot c_{1}\left(Y^{\perp}\bigr|_{Y_{r}}\right).

Since
Y_{r}\subset Y
for each
r
, and since
Y\cong N^{\prime}
, there is an associated submanifold
N_{r}^{\prime}
of
N^{\prime}
with
N_{r}^{\prime}\cong Y_{r}
.
The bundle
Y_{r}^{\perp}\bigr|_{Y}
is naturally isomorphic to the normal bundle of
N_{r}^{\prime}
within
N^{\prime}
, denoted
(N_{r}^{\prime})^{\perp}\bigr|_{N^{\prime}}
.
Also, in a similar manner as to Remark
3.1
, we have
d\,c_{1}(Y^{\perp}\bigr|_{Y_{r}})=c_{1}((N^{\prime})^{\perp}\bigr|_{N^{\prime}_{r}})
.
Thus

(3.3)

d\,c_{r}\left(Y_{r}^{\perp}\right)=c_{r-1}\left((N_{r}^{\prime})^{\perp}\bigr|_{N^{\prime}}\right)\cdot c_{1}\left((N^{\prime})^{\perp}\bigr|_{N_{r}^{\prime}}\right)=c_{r}\left((N_{r}^{\prime})^{\perp}\right).

Exact values for the Chern classes
c_{r}((N_{r}^{\prime})^{\perp})
seem difficult to calculate, but note that by
[
1
, Lemma 13.1]
we have
c_{1}((N^{\prime})^{\perp})\neq 0
.
Accordingly, one would expect that each of these Chern classes are typically nonzero in appropriate dimensions.

Remark 3.3
.

There is a small error in the general formula in
[
9
, Equation 14]
.
The error is minor, and the author should have caught it sooner.
But it should be noted that this is fixed by Viro in
[
20
, Equation 3]
.
This corrected form was, of course, used to derive equation (
3.2
) above.

When
n=2
, notice that equation (
3.2
) reduces to

(3.4)

\Sigma(X)=d\Sigma(M^{\prime})-\frac{d^{2}-1}{3d}e((N^{\prime})^{\perp})

where
e((N^{\prime})^{\perp})
denotes the Euler number of the normal bundle of
N^{\prime}
within
M^{\prime}
.
Since
n=2
and
N^{\prime}
is a totally geodesic complex hypersurface in
M^{\prime}
, by
[
5
, Proposition 2.5]
we have that
e((N^{\prime})^{\perp})=(1/2)\chi(N^{\prime})
.
Thus

(3.5)

\Sigma(X)=d\Sigma(M^{\prime})-\frac{d^{2}-1}{6d}\chi(N^{\prime}).

3.2.
Proofs of the main theorems

We first consider Theorem
1.2
.
Note that, when
n=2
, the only two Chern numbers are
c_{2}
and
c_{1}^{2}
.
For a closed complex hyperbolic manifold, we have

\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{c_{2}}=\frac{{3\choose 1}^{2}}{{3\choose 2}}=3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c_{1}^{2}-3c_{2}=0.

So Theorem
1.2
shows that the only ratio of Chern numbers of
X
is not equal to the corresponding ratio for a complex hyperbolic manifold.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The formula for the first Pontrjagin number
p_{1}(X)
of any complex vector bundle in terms of Chern numbers is
p_{1}(X)=c_{1}^{2}(X)-2c_{2}(X)

[
14
, Corollary 15.5]
.
Also, for any
4
-manifold
X
,
\Sigma(X)=(1/3)p_{1}(X)

[
14
, page 225]
.
So we have

\displaystyle c_{1}^{2}(X)-3c_{2}(X)

\displaystyle=p_{1}(X)-c_{2}(X)

\displaystyle=3\Sigma(X)-\chi(X)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=3\Sigma(X)-\left[d\chi(M^{\prime})-(d-1)\chi(N^{\prime})\right].

where the last equality follows from a basic inclusion-exclusion argument.

By equation (
3.5
) above we also have

(3.7)

\Sigma(X)=d\Sigma(M^{\prime})-\frac{d^{2}-1}{6d}\chi(N^{\prime})

Combining equations (
3.6
), (
3.7
), and Corollary
2.3
provides

\displaystyle c_{1}^{2}(X)-3c_{2}(X)

\displaystyle=3\left[d\Sigma(M^{\prime})-\frac{d^{2}-1}{6d}\chi(N^{\prime})\right]-d\chi(M^{\prime})+(d-1)\chi(N^{\prime})

\displaystyle=3\left[\frac{d}{3}\chi(M^{\prime})-\frac{d^{2}-1}{6d}\chi(N^{\prime})\right]-d\chi(M^{\prime})+(d-1)\chi(N^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{2d}\chi(N^{\prime})

(3.8)

\displaystyle=m\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{2d}\chi(N).

By Chern-Gauss-Bonnet we know that
\chi(N)\neq 0
.
So
c_{1}^{2}(X)-3c_{2}(X)\neq 0
for all
d\geq 2
, and the magnitude of this difference increases without bound as
m\to\infty
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that equation (
3.8
) proves Corollaries
1.5
and
1.6
when
n=2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We proceed by contradiction.
Assume that all ratios of Chern numbers of
X
are equal to the corresponding ratio of Chern numbers of

By Corollary

\Sigma(X)=\frac{1}{n+1}\chi(X)=\frac{1}{n+1}\left[d\chi(M^{\prime})-(d-1)\chi(N^{\prime})\right].

On the other hand, by equation (

\displaystyle\Sigma(X)

\displaystyle=d\Sigma(M^{\prime})-\frac{d^{2}-1}{3}\Sigma(Y_{2})+\frac{(d^{2}-1)(d^{2}-4)}{45}\Sigma(Y_{4})-\ldots

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{n+1}\chi(M^{\prime})-\frac{d^{2}-1}{3}\Sigma(Y_{2})+\frac{(d^{2}-1)(d^{2}-4)}{45}\Sigma(Y_{4})-\ldots.

Setting these equations for
\Sigma(X)
equal yields

(3.9)

0=\frac{d-1}{n+1}\chi(N^{\prime})-\frac{d^{2}-1}{3}\Sigma(Y_{2})+\frac{(d^{2}-1)(d^{2}-4)}{45}\Sigma(Y_{4})-\ldots.

Now, consider the submanifold

.
One could have alternatively defined this submanifold to be

.

By equation (

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{d-1}{n+1}\chi(N^{\prime})-\frac{d^{2}-1}{3d}c_{1}((N_{1}^{\prime})^{\perp})+\frac{(d^{2}-1)(d^{2}-4)}{45d}c_{2}((N_{2}^{\prime})^{\perp})-\ldots.

Because there are at most

To help prove the corollaries just note that one can rewrite this expression further as

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=m\frac{d-1}{n+1}\chi(N)-\frac{d^{2}-1}{3d}f_{1}(m)c_{1}(N_{1}^{\perp})

∎

Remark 3.5

Combinatorial sufficient conditions for graph rigidity and applications to random graphs
Michael Krivelevich, Alan Lew, Peleg Michaeli

Abstract
A graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) is called d d -rigid if, for a generic embedding of its vertices in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , every edge-length preserving continuous motion of the vertices preserves the distances between all pairs of non-adjacent vertices as well. In this paper, we present several new results on the rigidity of random graphs. In particular, we show that there exists c > 0 c>0 such that, for p ≥ 2 ​ log ⁡ n / n p\geq 2\log{n}/n , the binomial random graph G ⁡ ( n , p ) G(n,p) is with high probability (whp) ⌊ c ​ n ​ p ⌋ \lfloor cnp\rfloor -rigid. This is sharp up to the constant c c , and complements recent results of Peled and Peleg (in the regime p = o ( n − 1 / 2 ) p=o(n^{-1/2}) ), and of Jordán, Liu, and Villányi (in the constant p p regime). Moreover, we show that for every fixed d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 and r ≥ 501 ​ d r\geq 501d , a random r r -regular graph is whp d d -rigid, and that for 100 / n ≤ p ≤ 2 ​ log ⁡ n / n 100/n\leq p\leq 2\log{n}/n , the binomial random graph G ⁡ ( n , p ) G(n,p) contains whp an ⌊ n ​ p / 251 ⌋ \lfloor np/251\rfloor -rigid subgraph with at least ( 1 − e − n p / 2 ) n (1-e^{-np/2})n vertices. Both results are sharp up to the multiplicative constant. In addition, we present a new sufficient condition for rigidity in terms of the minimum codegree of the graph (the minimum number of common neighbours of a pair of vertices in the graph). A main tool in our arguments is a new combinatorial sufficient condition for rigidity, which provides a common generalization to Whiteley’s vertex-splitting lemmas, and to the “rigid partitions” method, developed in works by Crapo, Lindemann, Lew, Nevo, Peled and Raz, and by the present authors.

Proof.

It is immediate that we cannot have two big components

k
vertices in
V_{j}

k
vertices on each side.
Let

For

Note that

Let

and

2.4

is

is

1.6

G[V\smallsetminus U_{0}]

∎

.

Note that if

Let

of

.

The following lemma will be useful.

Absorption.

Let

,
k\geq 1
,
and let

be a graph with

Rigidity of binomial random graphs

We will need the following lemma about edge expansion in binomial random graphs. Our argument is fairly standard (see, for example,
[
3
]
and the references within).

4.1
.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1]

there exist constants
C=C(\varepsilon)>0
and
\kappa=\kappa(\varepsilon)>0

such that the following holds.
If
p\geq C/n
and
G\sim G(n,p)
, then,
whp
,
\mathrm{i}(G;k_{0})\geq\kappa np
for
k_{0}=\left\lceil{n\exp(-(1-\varepsilon/2)np)}\right\rceil
. For
\varepsilon=1
, we may take
\kappa=1/30
and
C=40
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa=\kappa(\varepsilon)\in(0,1/2)
be a constant to be determined later. Let
C=40/\varepsilon
. For
U\subseteq[n]
, let
X_{U}
be the event that
|E(U,[n]\smallsetminus U)|<\kappa np|U|
.
For
1\leq k\leq n
, denote
p_{k}=\mathbb{P}(X_{U})
for
U\in\binom{[n]}{k}
(note that
\mathbb{P}(X_{U})
depends only on
k
).
Let

X=\bigcup_{U\subseteq[n]:\ k_{0}\leq|U|\leq n/2}X_{U}.

We show that
\mathbb{P}(X)=o(1)
.

Let
k_{0}\leq k\leq n/2
and let
U\in\binom{[n]}{k}
.
Let
Y_{U}=|E(U,[n]\smallsetminus U)|
.
Note that
Y_{U}
is a binomial random variable with
k(n-k)
attempts and success probability
p
, and so

\mu=\mathbb{E}(Y_{U})=k(n-k)p.

Let
\nu=\mu-\kappa npk
.
Since
k\leq n/2
we have
n-k\geq n/2
, and

\frac{\nu}{\mu}=1-\frac{\kappa n}{n-k}\geq 1-2\kappa.

By Chernoff bounds (
Theorem

2.5
) and the fact that
\varphi(x)=(1+x)\log(1+x)-x
is decreasing in
(-1,0)
,

\displaystyle p_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(X_{U})=\mathbb{P}(Y_{U}<\kappa npk)=\mathbb{P}(Y_{U}<\mu-\nu)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\mu\varphi(-\nu/\mu))\leq\exp(-\mu\varphi(-(1-2\kappa))).

Since
\varphi(x)
converges to
1
as
x\searrow-1
,
we may choose
\kappa
so that

\varphi(-(1-2\kappa))\geq 1-\varepsilon/4.

(In the special case
\varepsilon=1
,
\kappa=1/30
satisfies this condition.) Hence, for all
k_{0}\leq k\leq n/2
,

p_{k}\leq\exp(-(1-\varepsilon/4)pk(n-k)).

We now split into two cases.

Case 1:
k_{0}\leq k\leq(\varepsilon/8)n
.

In this case,

\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}p_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{en}{k}\right)^{k}\exp\left(-(1-\varepsilon/4)pk(1-\varepsilon/8)n\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{en}{k_{0}}\right)^{k}\exp\left(-(1-\varepsilon/4)pk(1-\varepsilon/8)n\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(k(1+(1-\varepsilon/2)np-(1-\varepsilon/4)(1-\varepsilon/8)np))

\displaystyle\leq\exp(k(1-(\varepsilon/8)np))\leq\exp(-(\varepsilon/16)npk).

In the last step, we used the fact that
np>C=40/\varepsilon>16/\varepsilon
.

Case 2:
(\varepsilon/8)n<k\leq n/2
.

In this case,
k(n-k)\geq(\varepsilon/8)n\cdot(n/2)=(\varepsilon/16)n^{2}
, so

p_{k}\leq\exp(-(1-\varepsilon/4)(\varepsilon/16)n^{2}p)=\exp(-\gamma n^{2}p),

where
\gamma=(1-\varepsilon/4)(\varepsilon/16)>0
.
Using
\binom{n}{k}\leq 2^{n}
, we get

\binom{n}{k}p_{k}\leq\exp(n\log 2-\gamma n^{2}p).

Since
C=40/\varepsilon
, we have
\gamma np\geq\gamma C=40\gamma/\varepsilon>\log 2+1
,
and hence
\binom{n}{k}p_{k}\leq e^{-n}
for all
k
.

Before proceeding, note that
npk_{0}=\omega(1)
.
Indeed, if
np\geq\log{n}
then
npk_{0}\geq\log{n}=\omega(1)
;
and, noting that
k_{0}
is decreasing in
np
,
if
np\leq\log{n}
then
k_{0}\geq n^{\varepsilon/2}=\omega(1)
,
which implies (since
np\geq C
) that
npk_{0}=\omega(1)
.
Write
\rho=\frac{\varepsilon}{16}np\geq 1
,
so
e^{-\rho k_{0}}=o(1)
.
Combining the two cases above and applying the union bound, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\lfloor(\varepsilon/8)n\rfloor}\binom{n}{k}p_{k}+\sum_{k=\lfloor(\varepsilon/8)n\rfloor+1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{k}p_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\exp(-\rho k)+ne^{-n}=\frac{e^{-\rho k_{0}}}{1-e^{-\rho}}+o(1)=o(1),

as required.
∎

4.2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\kappa=\kappa(\varepsilon)\in(0,1)

such that for
p\geq(1+\varepsilon)\log{n}/n
,
a graph
G\sim G(n,p)
satisfies
\mathrm{i}(G)\geq\kappa np

whp
.

Proof.

By monotonicity in
p
, we may assume
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
.
Apply
Lemma

4.1
with parameter
\varepsilon

to obtain
\kappa=\kappa(\varepsilon)\in(0,1)
.
Since
np\geq(1+\varepsilon)\log n
, we have
k_{0}=1
for sufficiently large
n
.
Thus,
whp
,
\mathrm{i}(G)=\mathrm{i}(G;1)\geq\kappa np
.
∎

An
equipartition

X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
of a set
X

is a partition of
X
into
m
parts of size
\lceil|X|/m\rceil
or
\lfloor|X|/m\rfloor
each. For convenience, we assume
|X_{1}|\geq\cdots\geq|X_{m}|
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Let
G\sim G(n,f/n)
, where
f=np
. For every integer
k
, the probability that there are no edges in
G
between two fixed disjoint sets
X
and
Y
of size
k
each is
(1-f/n)^{k^{2}}\leq e^{-fk^{2}/n}
. Let
k=\lceil\beta n/f\rceil
where
\beta>0
is some large enough constant to be chosen later, and let
m=\lceil\alpha f\rceil
, where
\alpha=1/(56\beta)
. Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
be an arbitrary fixed equipartition of
[n]
.

First, note that, for every

|V_{i}|\geq\lfloor n/m\rfloor\geq n/(2m)\geq n/(4\alpha f)=14\beta n/f\geq 7k.

Let
G_{0}=([m],E^{\prime})
, where

E^{\prime}=\left\{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[m]}{2}:\,\text{for every $X\subseteq V_{i}$, $Y\subseteq V_{j}$ such that $|X|=|Y|=k$, $E_{G}(X,Y)\neq\varnothing$}\right\}.

For
1\leq i<j\leq m

X_{ij}
be the event that
\{i,j\}\in E^{\prime}
. Note that the events

. Moreover, by the union bound,

\displaystyle 1-\mathbb{P}(X_{ij})

\displaystyle\leq\binom{\lceil n/m\rceil}{k}^{2}e^{-fk^{2}/n}\leq\left(\frac{e}{k}\left\lceil\frac{n}{m}\right\rceil\right)^{2k}\cdot e^{-fk^{2}/n}\leq\left(\frac{2en}{mk}\right)^{2k}\cdot e^{-fk^{2}/n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2en}{mk}\cdot e^{-fk/(2n)}\right)^{2k}\leq\left(\frac{2e}{\alpha\beta}e^{-\beta/2}\right)^{2k}\leq(305e^{-\beta/2})^{2k}.

Choosing

That is, for all

\mathbb{P}(\deg_{G_{0}}(i)<98m/100)=e^{-\Theta(m)}.

By the union bound over all

\delta(G_{0})\geq 98m/100

whp
. So, by

1.5
(with
\eta=0.98)

such that

d^{\prime}

\kappa=\kappa(\varepsilon)\in(0,1)

\mathrm{i}(G)\geq\kappa f

whp

Let us choose

∎

Minimum codegree conditions for rigidity

In this section, we prove
Theorem

1.4
.
For a graph

,
a partition

of

Proof.

Write

\displaystyle=\frac{\binom{n-|V_{S}|}{d_{2}(u,v)}}{\binom{n}{d_{2}(u,v)}}=\prod_{t=0}^{d_{2}(u,v)-1}\frac{n-|V_{S}|-t}{n-t}

In particular, if

7

Generalized rigid partitions

In this section, we present a new sufficient condition for
d
-rigidity, proving
Theorem

1.6
as a special case.

As a warm-up, let us first present a short proof of a slightly weaker version of
Theorem

1.6
(which, in fact, is sufficient for all the applications of
Theorem

1.6
presented in this paper).

7.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
m\geq d+1
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
be a partition of
V
.
Assume that the reduced graph
G^{\prime}
is
d
-rigid,
and that for every
1\leq i\leq m
and every pair of distinct vertices
u,v\in V_{i}
, there are at least
d
distinct indices
j\in[m]\smallsetminus\{i\}
such that
u
and
v
lie in the same connected component of
G[V_{i},V_{j}]
. Then,
G
is
d
-rigid.

Note that the only difference between
Theorem

7.1
and
Theorem

1.6
is that in
Theorem

7.1
we ignore the edges inside each part
V_{i}
. Before the proof, let us recall a few basic facts about infinitesimal rigidity.

Let
G=(V,E)
and
\mathbf{p}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. An
infinitesimal motion
of
(G,\mathbf{p})
is a map
\mathbf{q}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot(\mathbf{p}(u)-\mathbf{p}(v))=0
for all
\{u,v\}\in E
. An infinitesimal motion
\mathbf{q}
is called
trivial
if there exist a skew-symmetric matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{q}(v)=A\mathbf{p}(v)+x
for all
v\in V
. The framework
(G,\mathbf{p})
is called
infinitesimally rigid
if every infinitesimal motion of
(G,p)
is trivial.

The rigidity matrix of
(G,\mathbf{p})
, denoted by
R(G,\mathbf{p})
, is the
|E|\times d|V|
matrix whose rows are indexed by edges of
G
, and columns indexed by vertices (with
d
consecutive columns associated with each vertex), defined by

R(G,\mathbf{p})_{e,u}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{p}(u)-\mathbf{p}(v)&\text{if }e=\{u,v\}\text{ for some }v\in V,\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

for all
e\in E
and
u\in V
. Note that the infinitesimal motions (considered as vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d|V|}
) are exactly the solutions to the system of equations
R(G,\mathbf{p})\mathbf{q}=0
.

It is a well-known fact (see
[
1
,
2
]
) that, assuming that the affine span of
\mathbf{p}(V)
is at least
(d-1)
-dimensional, the space of trivial infinitesimal motions of
(G,\mathbf{p})
has dimension
\binom{d+1}{2}
. Therefore, in this case,
(G,\mathbf{p})
is infinitesimally rigid if and only if
\text{rank}(R(G,\mathbf{p}))=d|V|-\binom{d+1}{2}
.

It is easy to show that if
(G,\mathbf{p}^{\prime})
is infinitesimally rigid for some
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
(G,\mathbf{p})
is infinitesimally rigid for every generic
\mathbf{p}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Moreover, every infinitesimally rigid framework
(G,\mathbf{p})
is rigid, and, for generic
\mathbf{p}
, the notions of rigidity and infinitesimal rigidity are equivalent. Hence, a graph
G
is
d
-rigid if and only if there exists
\mathbf{p}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
(G,\mathbf{p})
is infinitesimally rigid (see
[
2
]
).

Proof of
Theorem

7.1
.

Let
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}:[m]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a generic embedding. Since
G^{\prime}
is
d
-rigid,
(G^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})
is infinitesimally rigid.
We extend
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
to an embedding
\mathbf{p}
of
V
by setting
\mathbf{p}(v)=\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(i)
whenever
v\in V_{i}
.
We will show that
(G,\mathbf{p})
is infinitesimally rigid, and therefore
G
is
d
-rigid.

Assume for contradiction that
(G,\mathbf{p})
is not infinitesimally rigid.
Then, there exists a non-trivial infinitesimal motion
\mathbf{q}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of
(G,\mathbf{p})
.

First, we will show that for every
i\in[m]
and
u,v\in V_{i}
, we must have
\mathbf{q}(u)=\mathbf{q}(v)
. Let
i\in[m]
and
u,v\in V_{i}
. Let
j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}\in[m]\smallsetminus\{i\}
for which
u
and
v
are connected by a path in
G[V_{i},V_{j}]
for all
j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}\}
. Let
1\leq k\leq d
. Note that for every
x,y\in V_{i}\cup V_{j_{k}}
, if
x
and
y
are adjacent in
G[V_{i},V_{j_{k}}]
, then
(\mathbf{q}(x)-\mathbf{q}(y))\cdot(\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(i)-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(j_{k}))=0
. By transitivity, we obtain
\mathbf{q}(u)\cdot(\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(i)-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(j_{k}))=\mathbf{q}(v)\cdot(\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(i)-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(j_{k}))
.
Since
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(i)-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(j_{1}),\ldots,\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(i)-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(j_{d})
are linearly independent (as
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
is generic), we must have
\mathbf{q}(u)=\mathbf{q}(v)
.

Now, we define
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}:[m]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}(i)=\mathbf{q}(u)
, where
u
is a vertex in
V_{i}
, for all
1\leq i\leq m
(note that
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
is well-defined by our previous argument). Note that
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
is an infinitesimal motion of
(G^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})
. Indeed, for every
\{i,j\}\in E^{\prime}
, there exist
u\in V_{i}
and
v\in V_{j}
for which
\{u,v\}\in E
, and so

(\mathbf{q}^{\prime}(i)-\mathbf{q}^{\prime}(j))\cdot(\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(i)-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(j))=(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot(\mathbf{p}(u)-\mathbf{p}(v))=0,

since
\mathbf{q}
is an infinitesimal motion of
(G,\mathbf{p})
.
Moreover, since
\mathbf{q}
is non-trivial,
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
is non-trivial as well (indeed, if
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}(i)=A\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(i)+z
for all
i\in[m]
, for some skew-symmetric
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
\mathbf{q}(v)=A\mathbf{p}(v)+z
for all
v\in V
, in contradiction to
\mathbf{q}
being non-trivial). But this is a contradiction to
(G^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})
being infinitesimally rigid.
∎

In order to define a more general sufficient condition for rigidity, we first need to introduce the notion of
anchored graphs
, presented next.

7.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a multi-graph, and let
c:E\to\mathbb{N}
be a map assigning a “colour” to each edge. Let
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in c(E)}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a generic set of points.
A map
\mathbf{q}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called a
motion
of
(G,c)
if

(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot x_{c(e)}=0

for every
e\in E
with endpoints
u
and
v
.
A motion
\mathbf{q}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called
trivial
if
\mathbf{q}(u)=\mathbf{q}(v)
for all
u,v\in V
.
We say that
(G,c)
is
d
-anchored
if every motion of
(G,c)
is trivial.

Next, we introduce the notion of
generalized rigid partitions
.

7.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
m\geq d\geq 1
. Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
be a partition of
V
. For all
i\in[m]
, let
m_{i}\geq m
, and let
\mathcal{I}=\{(i,j):\,1\leq i\leq m,\,i<j\leq m_{i}\}
. Let
\{G_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}}
be a family of pairwise edge-disjoint subgraphs of
G
such that
V(G_{ij})=V_{i}\cup V_{j}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq m
, and
V(G_{ij})=V_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
and
m<j\leq m_{i}
. Let
\hat{G}=\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}}G_{ij}\subseteq G
. For convenience, for
1\leq j<i\leq m
, we denote
G_{ij}=G_{ji}
. The
reduced graph
associated with
(G,\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{G_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}})
is the graph
G^{\prime}=([m],E^{\prime})
, where

E^{\prime}=\left\{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[m]}{2}:\,E_{G_{ij}}(V_{i},V_{j})\neq\varnothing\right\}.

For every
1\leq i\leq m
, the
i
-th
anchoring graph
of
(G,\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{G_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}})
is the edge-coloured multi-graph
(H_{i},c_{i})
on vertex set
V_{i}
, having, for every
j\in[m_{i}]\smallsetminus\{i\}
, an edge

with

and
v
are connected by a path in

.

Assume that the following three properties hold.

Monochromatic cuts property:

For every
i\in[m]
and
U\subseteq V_{i}
with
|U|\geq 2
, there exist
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}
and a partition

such that
E_{\hat{G}}(U^{\prime},U^{\prime\prime})\subseteq E(G_{ij})
.

Anchored parts property:

For every
i\in[m]
, the
i
-th anchoring graph
(H_{i},c_{i})
is
d
-anchored.

Rigid reduced graph:

The reduced graph
G^{\prime}
is
d
-rigid.

Then, we say that
(\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{G_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}})
is a
generalized
d
-rigid partition
of
G
.

Our main result in this section is the following theorem.

7.4
.

If
G
admits a generalized

-rigid partition, then

is
d
-rigid.

reduces, in the special case

, to

, which in turn generalizes results of Crapo
[

]

]
corresponding to the

]

[

]

[

]
.

7.1

Generalized strong rigid partitions

The notion of generalized rigid partitions introduced in
Definition

7.3

, which provides a more concrete sufficient condition for rigidity, and extends the notions of “strong rigid partitions” introduced in
[

]
and
[

]
.

7.5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. Let
m\geq d\geq 1
, and let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
be a partition of
V
. Recall that the
reduced graph
of
(G,\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m})
is defined as
G^{\prime}=\{[m],E^{\prime}\}
, where

E^{\prime}=\left\{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[m]}{2}:\,E(V_{i},V_{j})\neq\varnothing\right\}.

Assume that
G^{\prime}
is
d
-rigid. For
1\leq i\leq m
, let
Q_{i}
be the graph on vertex set
V_{i}
whose edges are the pairs
\{u,v\}\subseteq V_{i}
for which there exist
d
distinct indices
j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}\in[m]
such that
u
and
v
are connected by a path in
G[V_{i},V_{j_{k}}]
for all
k=1,\ldots,d
. Assume that
Q_{i}
is connected for each
1\leq i\leq m
.
Then, we say that
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
is a
generalized strong
d
-rigid partition
of
G
.

7.6
.

If
G
admits a generalized strong
d
-rigid partition, then
G
is
d
-rigid.

Note that
Theorem

7.6
immediately implies
Theorem

1.6
(which requires the graphs
Q_{i}
to be complete, instead of just connected). For the proof of
Theorem

7.6
, we need the following very simple lemma.

7.7
.

Let
d\geq 1
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a multi-graph, let
c:E\to\mathbb{N}
, and let
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in c(E)}
be a generic set of points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that
\mathbf{q}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a motion of
(G,c)
, and let
u,v\in V
. If
u
and
v
are connected by a monochromatic path of colour
i
, then
(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot x_{i}=0
.

Proof.

Since
(\mathbf{q}(w)-\mathbf{q}(z))\cdot x_{i}=0
for every pair of vertices
w,z
forming an
i
-coloured edge, the claim follows trivially by transitivity.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

7.6
.

Define, for
i\neq j
,
G_{ij}=G[V_{i},V_{j}]
. In addition, define for all
1\leq i\leq m
,
G_{i,m+1}=G[V_{i}]=G[V_{i},V_{i}]
. Let
\mathcal{I}=\{(i,j):\,1\leq i\leq m:\,i<j\leq m+1\}
.
We will show that
(\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{G_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}})
is a generalized
d
-rigid partition of
G
. Indeed, the reduced graph
G^{\prime}
is
d
-rigid by assumption, and the monochromatic cuts condition holds trivially, since each
V_{i}
contains edges of just one of the subgraphs
G_{ij}
(namely, of the graph
G_{i,m+1}=G[V_{i}]
). We are left to show that the anchored parts property holds.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
be a generic set of points. Let
1\leq i\leq m
, and let
\mathbf{q}:V_{i}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a motion of the
i
-th anchoring graph
(H_{i},c_{i})
associated with
(G,\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{G_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}})
. We need to show that
\mathbf{q}(u)=\mathbf{q}(v)
for all
u,v\in V_{i}
. Since
Q_{i}
is connected, it is enough to show this for
u
and
v
that are adjacent in
Q_{i}
. Let
u,v\in V_{i}
such that
\{u,v\}\in E(Q_{i})
. By the definition of
Q_{i}
, there exist indices
j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}\in[m]
such that
u
and
v
are connected by a path in
G[V_{i},V_{j_{k}}]
for all
k=1,\ldots,d
. By
Lemma

7.7
, we have

(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot x_{j_{k}}=0

for
k=1,\ldots,d
. Since the points
x_{j}
are generic,
x_{j_{1}},\ldots,x_{j_{d}}
are linearly independent, and therefore we must have
\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v)=0
. That is,
\mathbf{q}(u)=\mathbf{q}(v)
. Hence,
\mathbf{q}
is trivial, and therefore
(H_{i},c_{i})
is
d
-anchored, as wanted. Hence,
(\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{G_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}})
is a generalized
d
-rigid partition of
G
, and so, by
Theorem

7.4
,
G
is
d
-rigid.
∎

7.2

Limit frameworks

For the proof of Theorem
7.4
, we need to introduce the notion of limit frameworks and to recall some relevant facts about them.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, let
\mathbf{p}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
g:\{(u,e):\,e\in E,\,u\in e\}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We say that
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
is a
d
-dimensional limit framework
if there exists a sequence
\{\mathbf{p}_{n}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that, for every
v\in V
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{p}_{n}(v)=\mathbf{p}(v),

and for every
e\in E
and
u\in e
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{p}_{n}(u)-\mathbf{p}_{n}(v)}{\|\mathbf{p}_{n}(u)-\mathbf{p}_{n}(v)\|}=g(u,e),

where
v
is the unique vertex in
e\smallsetminus\{u\}
. In such case, we say that the sequence
\{(G,\mathbf{p}_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

converges
to
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
.

Note that if
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
is a limit framework then
\|g(u,e)\|=1
for all
e\in E
and
u\in e
, and that
g(u,e)=-g(v,e)
for all
e=\{u,v\}\in E
.

We say that a map
\mathbf{q}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an
infinitesimal motion
of
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
if, for every
e=\{u,v\}\in E
,

(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot g(u,e)=0.

We say that
\mathbf{q}
is
trivial
if there exist a skew-symmetric matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{q}(v)=A\mathbf{p}(v)+x
for all
v\in V
. A limit framework
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
is called
infinitesimally rigid
if every infinitesimal motion of
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
is trivial.

We define the rigidity matrix
R(G,\mathbf{p},g)
of a limit framework
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
as the
|E|\times d|V|
matrix whose rows are indexed by edges of
G
, and columns indexed by vertices (with
d
consecutive columns associated with each vertex), with entries given by

R(G,\mathbf{p})_{e,u}=\begin{cases}g(u,e)&\text{if }u\in e,\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

for all
e\in E
and
u\in V
. Similarly to the non-limiting case, it is easy to check that, assuming that the affine span of
\mathbf{p}(V)
is at least
(d-1)
-dimensional,
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
is infinitesimally rigid if and only if
\text{rank}(R(G,\mathbf{p},g))=d|V|-\binom{d+1}{2}
.

7.8
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and let
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
be a
d
-dimensional limit framework of
G
. Assume that the affine span of
\mathbf{p}(V)
is at least
(d-1)
-dimensional. If
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
is infinitesimally rigid, then
G
is
d
-rigid.

Proof.

Let
\{\mathbf{p}_{n}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence such that
\{(G,\mathbf{p}_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges to
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
. We will show that for
n
large enough,
(G,\mathbf{p}_{n})
is infinitesimally rigid, and therefore
G
is
d
-rigid. Let
m=\dim(\mathbf{p}(V))
denote the dimension of the affine span of the image of
\mathbf{p}
. Similarly, for
n\geq 1
, let
m_{n}=\dim(\mathbf{p}_{n}(V))
. Note that, for large enough
n
,
m_{n}\geq m\geq d-1
.

For
n\geq 1
, let
R_{n}
be the matrix obtained from
R(G,\mathbf{p}_{n})
by dividing, for each edge
e=\{u,v\}\in E
such that
\mathbf{p}_{n}(u)\neq\mathbf{p}_{n}(v)
, the row associated with
e
by
\|\mathbf{p}_{n}(u)-\mathbf{p}_{n}(v)\|
. Note that
R_{n}
has the same rank as
R(G,\mathbf{p}_{n})
, and that the sequence
R_{n}
converges entry-wise to
R(G,\mathbf{p},g)
. Therefore, there exists
n\geq 1
such that
m_{n}\geq m\geq d-1
and

\text{rank}(R(G,\mathbf{p}_{n}))=\text{rank}(R_{n})\geq\text{rank}(R(G,\mathbf{p},g))=d|V|-\binom{d+1}{2},

where we used the fact that
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
is infinitesimally rigid. Therefore,
(G,\mathbf{p}_{n})
is infinitesimally rigid, as wanted.
∎

For the proof of
Theorem

7.4
, we will need the following auxiliary results on limit frameworks, which were proved in
[
14
]
(and which rely on and extend earlier work by Tay
[
26
]
).

7.9
[
14
, Lemma 2.4]
.

Let
G=(V,E)
. Let
V=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m}
be a partition of
V
, and let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
be
m
distinct points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Assume we have for each
1\leq i\leq m
a
d
-dimensional limit framework
(G[V_{i}],\mathbf{p}_{i},g_{i})
.
Define
g:\{(u,e):\,e\in E,u\in e\}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

g(u,\{u,v\})=\begin{cases}(x_{i}-x_{j})/\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|&\text{ if }u\in V_{i},v\in V_{j}\text{ for }i\neq j,\\
g_{i}(u,\{u,v\})&\text{ if }u,v\in V_{i}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq m,\end{cases}

for all
\{u,v\}\in E
. Let
\mathbf{p}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be defined by
\mathbf{p}(v)=x_{i}
for each
i\in[m]
and
v\in V_{i}
. Then,
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
is a
d
-dimensional limit framework.

7.10
[
14
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and let
E=E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{k}
be a partition of its edge set.
Let
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\|y_{i}\|=1
for all
1\leq i\leq k
.
If for every
U\subseteq V
of size at least
2
there exist
1\leq i\leq k
and a partition
U^{\prime},U^{\prime\prime}
of
U
such that
E(U^{\prime},U^{\prime\prime})\subseteq E_{i}
,
then there exists a
d
-dimensional limit framework
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
such that, for every
i\in[k]
,

g(u,\{u,v\})\in\{y_{i},-y_{i}\}
for all
\{u,v\}\in E_{i}
.

7.3

Proof of
Theorem

7.4

We proceed to prove
Theorem

7.4
. The proof outline is as follows: First, we show, using the monochromatic cuts property, that there is a
d
-dimensional limit framework
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
with certain nice properties (in particular, such that the vertices in each part
V_{i}
are mapped to a single point
x_{i}
). Then, we apply the anchored parts property to show that in every infinitesimal motion
\mathbf{q}
of
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
, the vertices in each
V_{i}
must obtain the same value. This allows us to reduce
\mathbf{q}
to an infinitesimal motion on the reduced graph
G^{\prime}
. Since
G^{\prime}
is assumed to be
d
-rigid, this implies that
\mathbf{q}
is a trivial infinitesimal motion, showing that
G
is
d
-rigid as well.

Proof of
Theorem

7.4
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, let
m\geq d\geq 1
. Let
m_{i}\geq m
for all
i\in[m]
and
\mathcal{I}=\{(i,j):\,1\leq i\leq m,\,i<j\leq m_{i}\}
. Let
(\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{G_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}})
be a generalized
d
-rigid partition of
G
. Let
m^{\prime}=\max\{m_{i}:\,1\leq i\leq m\}
, and let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a generic set of points. For
i\neq j
, let
y_{ij}=(x_{i}-x_{j})/\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|
.

Since addition of edges preserves
d
-rigidity, we may assume without loss of generality that
E=\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}}E(G_{ij})
. By Lemma
7.10
(and using the monochromatic cuts property), for every
1\leq i\leq m
there exists a
d
-dimensional limit framework
(G[V_{i}],\mathbf{p}_{i},g_{i})
such that
g_{i}(u,e)\in\{y_{ij},-y_{ij}\}
for every
j\in[m_{i}]\smallsetminus\{i\}
,
e\in E(G_{ij}[V_{i}])
and
u\in e
. Hence, by Lemma
7.9
, there exists a
d
-dimensional limit framework
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
such that
\mathbf{p}(v)=x_{i}
for every
i\in[m]
and
v\in V_{i}
, and

g(u,e)\in\{y_{ij},-y_{ij}\}
for every
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}
,
e\in E(G_{ij})
and
u\in e
.

Let
G^{\prime}=([m],E^{\prime})
be the reduced graph of
(G,\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{G_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}})
. Let
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}:[m]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be defined by
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(i)=x_{i}
for all
i\in[m]
.
Since
G^{\prime}
is
d
-rigid and
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
is generic, the framework
(G^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})
is infinitesimally rigid.

We will show that
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
is infinitesimally rigid, and therefore, by Lemma
7.8
,
G
is
d
-rigid.
Let
\mathbf{q}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an infinitesimal motion of
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
.
Note that, for every
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}
and
e=\{u,v\}\in E(G_{ij})
, we have
g(u,e)=-g(v,e)\in\{y_{ij},-y_{ij}\}
, and therefore

(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot y_{ij}=(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot g(u,e)=0.

That is,
(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot(x_{i}-x_{j})=0
whenever
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G_{ij}
, or more generally whenever they are connected by a path in
G_{ij}
. Since the set
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m^{\prime}}
is generic, this equation holds also under the specialization
x_{i}=0
. Therefore,

(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot x_{j}=0

for all
u,v\in V_{i}\cup V_{j}
that are connected by a path in
G_{ij}
. In particular, this shows that, for all
1\leq i\leq m
, the restriction of
\mathbf{q}
to
V_{i}
is a motion of the
i
-th anchoring graph
(H_{i},c_{i})
. By the anchored parts condition,
(H_{i},c_{i})
is
d
-anchored for all
1\leq i\leq m
, and therefore
\mathbf{q}(u)=\mathbf{q}(v)
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
u,v\in V_{i}
.

Now, define
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}:[m]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}(i)=\mathbf{q}(u)
for all
i\in[m]
, where
u
is some vertex in
V_{i}
. Note that this is well defined since
\mathbf{q}(u)=\mathbf{q}(v)
for all
u,v\in V_{i}
.
Let
1\leq i<j\leq m
with
\{i,j\}\in E^{\prime}
. By the definition of
G^{\prime}
, there exist
u\in V_{i}
and
v\in V_{j}
such that
\{u,v\}\in E(G_{ij})
. Since
\mathbf{q}
is an infinitesimal motion of
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
, we obtain

(\mathbf{q}^{\prime}(i)-\mathbf{q}^{\prime}(j))\cdot\frac{x_{i}-x_{j}}{\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|}=(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot y_{ij}=(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot g(u,\{u,v\})=0.

Therefore,
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
is an infinitesimal motion of
(G^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})
. Since
(G^{\prime},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})
is infinitesimally rigid,
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
must be trivial. That is, there are a skew-symmetric matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}(i)=Ax_{i}+x
for all
i\in[m]
. But then, for all
i\in[m]
and
v\in V_{i}
, we obtain

\mathbf{q}(v)=\mathbf{q}^{\prime}(i)=Ax_{i}+x=A\mathbf{p}(v)+x.

That is,
\mathbf{q}
is a trivial infinitesimal motion of
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
. Thus,
(G,\mathbf{p},g)
is infinitesimally rigid, as wanted.
∎

Concluding remarks

8.1

Another variant of rigid partitions

Let us mention the following variant of
Theorem

7.6
, which demonstrates the additional strength of
Theorem

7.4
, and may be useful, for example, in the study of rigidity of bipartite graphs.

8.1
.

Let
m\geq d\geq 1
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
be a partition of
V
. For each
i\in[m]
, let
k_{i}\geq 1
, and let
V_{i1},\ldots,V_{ik_{i}}
be a partition of
V_{i}
. Assume that the reduced graph
G^{\prime}
associated with
(G,\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\})
is
d
-rigid. Moreover, assume that for all
i\in[m]
,
G[V_{i}]
contains a forest
F_{i}
such that the multi-graph obtained from
F_{i}
by contracting each set
V_{ij}
, for
1\leq j\leq k_{i}
, into a single vertex (and removing self-loops), contains
d
edge-disjoint spanning trees. Finally, for every
i\in[m]
and
j\in[k_{i}]
, let
Q_{ij}=(V_{ij},E^{\prime}_{ij})
, where
E^{\prime}_{ij}
consists of all pairs of vertices
u,v\in V_{ij}
for which there are at least
d
distinct indices
s\in[m]\smallsetminus\{i\}
such that
u
and
v
are connected by a path in
G[V_{i},V_{s}]
. Assume that
Q_{ij}
is connected for all
i\in[m]
and
j\in[k_{i}]
. Then,
G
is
d
-rigid.

Proof.

For
1\leq i\leq m
, let
m_{i}=m+|E(F_{i})|
, and let
\mathcal{I}=\{(i,j):\,1\leq i\leq m,\,i<j\leq m_{i}\}
.
For
1\leq i<j\leq m
, let
G_{ij}=G_{ji}=G[V_{i},V_{j}]
. For
1\leq i\leq m
, enumerate the edges of
F_{i}
arbitrarily as
e_{1},\ldots,e_{|E(F_{i})|}
. For
m+1\leq j\leq m_{i}
, let
G_{ij}
be the graph on vertex set
V_{i}
whose edge set consists of the single edge
e_{j-m}
.

We will show that
(G,\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{E_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}})
is a generalized
d
-rigid partition of
G
.
Indeed, let us first show that the monochromatic cuts property is satisfied. Let
i\in[m]
and
U\subseteq V_{i}
with
|U|\geq 2
. The case when
U
has no edges in any of the subgraphs
G_{ij}
is trivial. Therefore, let us assume that there exists
e\in F_{i}
such that
e\subseteq U
. Note that
F_{i}[U]
is a non-empty forest on at least two vertices, and so it must have a leaf
u\in U
. Taking
U^{\prime}=\{u\}
and
U^{\prime\prime}=U\smallsetminus\{u\}
, we obtain a cut of
U
with a single edge (and therefore, trivially, a monochromatic cut).

Next, let us show that our partition satisfies the anchored parts condition. For
i\in[m]
, let
(H_{i},c_{i})
be the
i
-th anchoring graph associated with our partition of
G
. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m_{i}}
be a generic set of points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\mathbf{q}:V_{i}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a motion of
(H_{i},c_{i})
. That is, for every
u,v\in V_{i}
that are connected by a path in
G_{ij}
for
j\in[m_{i}]\smallsetminus\{i\}
, we have

(\mathbf{q}(u)-\mathbf{q}(v))\cdot x_{j}=0.

First, note that, by
Lemma

7.7
and the fact that
Q_{ij}
is connected for every
j\in[k_{i}]
, we must have
\mathbf{q}(u)=\mathbf{q}(v)
for every
j\in[k_{i}]
and
u,v\in V_{ij}
.

Let
F_{i}^{\prime}
be the coloured multi-graph obtained from
F_{i}
by contracting each set
V_{ij}
into a single vertex. Note that each edge of
F_{i}^{\prime}
has a distinct colour.
Let
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}:V(F^{\prime}_{i})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the map induced by
\mathbf{q}
. It is easy to check that
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
is a motion of
F_{i}^{\prime}
.
It follows from
[
29
, Theorem 1]
that, since
F_{i}^{\prime}
contains
d
edge-disjoint spanning trees, then
F_{i}^{\prime}
is
d
-anchored, and in particular
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
is trivial.
Therefore,
\mathbf{q}
is trivial, as wanted. That is,
(H_{i},c_{i})
is
d
-anchored.

Finally, the reduced graph of
G
associated with
(\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{E_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}})
is
d
-rigid by assumption. Thus,
(G,\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\{E_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}})
is a generalized
d
-rigid partition of
G
, and so, by
Theorem

7.4
,
G
is
d
-rigid.
∎

Theorem

8.1
generalizes
[
14
, Lemma 2.11]
, corresponding to the special case when
G=(A,B,E)

m=d+1
, and each part

and

.

8.2

Characterization of
d
-anchored graphs

The notion of
d
-anchored coloured multi-graphs may be seen as a generalization of the rigidity of
d
-frames, studied by Whiteley in
[
29
]
, which corresponds to the special case when every edge in the multi-graph
G
is assigned a different colour by the map
c
. As mentioned in the proof of
Theorem

8.1
, Whiteley showed that, in this case, the graph
(G,c)
is
d
-anchored if and only if it contains
d
edge-disjoint spanning trees. It would be interesting to obtain an extension of this characterization to the general case.
Note that a minimally
d
-anchored graph requires, in addition to being the union of
d
edge-disjoint spanning trees, that, for every colour
k
, its
k
-coloured subgraph forms a forest, as otherwise the matrix associated with the
d
-anchoring system of equations for the graph will not be of full rank. However, these two necessary conditions are not sufficient. Indeed, let
(G,c)
be the multi-graph on vertex set
[3]
with two red edges
\{1,2\},\{1,3\}
, one blue edge
\{2,3\}
, and one green edge
\{2,3\}
. It is easy to check that
G
is the edge-disjoint union of two spanning trees, and that each monochromatic subgraph of
G
is a forest, but
(G,c)
is not
2
-anchored.

8.3

Some directions for further research

It is natural to ask whether the lower bound
k=\Omega(\log{n})
on the minimum codegree of an
n
-vertex graph
G
is required for the conclusion of
Theorem

1.4
to hold. Namely, is there an absolute constant
c>0
, such that for every
k\geq 1
, every graph
G
with minimum codegree at least
k
is
\lfloor ck\rfloor
-rigid?

Another natural question, which we plan to address in future work, is whether the methods in this paper could be used to obtain new results on the rigidity of pseudorandom graphs.
Recall that an
n
-vertex
k
-regular graph is called an
(n,k,\lambda)
-graph if the absolute values of all its non-trivial adjacency eigenvalues are at most
\lambda
(see, for example,
[
16
]
for more details). In
[
14
]
*Theorem 1.7, we showed that there exists
C>1
, such that if
G
is an
(n,k,\lambda)
graph with
k\geq\max\{9d\lambda,Cd\log{d}\}
, then
G
is
d
-rigid. Villányi’s results from
[
28
]
imply that an
(n,k,\lambda)
-graph is
\lfloor(1-o(1))\sqrt{k-\lambda}\rfloor
-rigid (see
[
14
]
for more details). It would be interesting to determine whether there exist absolute constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that every
(n,k,\lambda)
-graph
G
with
\lambda/k<c_{1}
is
\lfloor c_{2}k\rfloor
-rigid.

Global-in-time convergence in infinity-ion-mass limit for bipolar Euler-Poisson equations
Zhongmin Qian, Liang Zhao, Shengguo Zhu

Abstract
. In this paper, the Cauchy problem for the multi-dimensional (M-D) bipolar Euler-Poisson equations with far field vacuum is considered. Based on physical observations and some elaborate analysis of this system’s intrinsic symmetric hyperbolic-elliptic coupled structures, for a class of smooth initial data that are of small scaled density but possibly large mean velocity, we give one rigorous global-in-time convergence proof for regular solutions from M-D bipolar Euler-Poisson equations to M-D unipolar Euler-Poisson equations through the infinity-ion mass limit. Here the initial scaled density is required to decay to zero in the far field, and the spectrum of the Jacobi matrix of the initial mean velocity are all positive. In order to deal with such kind of singular limits, the global-in-time uniform tame estimates of regular solutions to M-D bipolar Euler-Poisson equations with respect to the ratio of electron mass over ion mass are established, based on which the corresponding error estimates in smooth function spaces between the two systems considered are also given. To achieve these, our main strategy is to regard the original problem for M-D bipolar Euler-Poisson equations as the limit of a series of carefully designed approximate problems which have truncated convection operators and compactly supported initial data. For such artificial problems, we can derive careful a-priori estimates that are independent of the mass ratio, the size of the initial data’ supports and the truncation parameters. Then the global uniform existence of regular solutions of the original problem are attained via careful compactness.

, which is so strong that it eliminates the possibility that the doping profile

is required to satisfy some monotonicity conditions, in which a careful description on the spectrum of

In order to carry out the global-in-time analysis of the infinity-ion-mass limit, the first task is to identify a proper class of initial data and the corresponding regularity propagation mechanism along with the time in some energy space to establish the desired uniform global-in-time existence of regular solutions with respect to
\varepsilon
. Some essential difficulties occur accordingly. On the one hand, the monotonicity conditions on
u_{i}^{0}
make
u_{i}
and
\nabla u_{i}
non-integrable over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. On the other hand, compared with the Euler equations studied in Serre
[
41
]
and Grassin
[
17
]
, the presence of the electric potential term
\nabla\varphi
causes extra great troubles. First, in order to deal with the degeneracy in the time evolution caused by the appearance of vacuum, one needs to apply the Makino-Ukai-Kawashima’s symmetrization scheme introduced in
[
35
]
to (
1.1
). However, this scheme will result in the destruction of the dissipative structure of
\nabla\varphi
in (
1.1
), the loss of the proper entropy inequalities for the error system as well as the destruction of the conservation laws, which are necessary for obtaining the zero-th order estimates on the error variables. Second, although it seems that
\nabla\varphi
can be bounded by densities through the Poisson equation, this term is non-local and has highly coupling effects, which imply that in the corresponding weighted energy estimates, the power of
\varepsilon
in the weight function of
\nabla\varphi
in both ion and electron momentum equations should be exactly the same. Due to this, we have to consider
(\rho_{\nu},\sqrt{m_{\nu}}u_{\nu})
as variables instead of
(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{\nu}}}\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu})
, which results in the vanishing of
u_{i}^{0}
after the limit is applied, leading to a contradiction with the strict monotonicity condition on
u_{i}^{0}
. Last but not least, due to the Poisson structure,
\varphi
should be some harmonic functions instead of
0
when the densities
\rho_{\nu}
degenerate. This implies that in vacuum region, the time evolution of the velocities do not degenerate into the simply Burgers equations, which makes it hard for us to describe the dynamics of velocities. In order to conquer these obstacles, we introduce the reference velocities:

w_{e}=u_{e}-\hat{u}_{e}\quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon^{-1}w_{i}=u_{i}-\hat{u}_{i}

with

being the solutions to the following Cauchy problem of the Burgers equations:

\partial_{t}\hat{u}_{\nu}+(\hat{u}_{\nu}\cdot\nabla)\hat{u}_{\nu}=0\quad\text{in}\ \ (0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\quad\hat{u}_{\nu}(0,x)=u_{\nu}^{0}(x)\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.8)

The boundedness of

in some energy spaces fix the paradox between vanishing condition and dispersive condition on

Based on this, a series of approximate problems having truncated convection operators and compactly supported initial data are carefully designed, of which the uniform a priori estimates with respect to
\varepsilon
, support size and truncation parameters are established. Furthermore, our reformulation naturally admits some dissipative structure for error systems, which makes it possible to obtain the global-in-time convergence towards (
1.6

1.1
) and (
1.6
).

\hat{F}
be bounded in
L^{p}([0,T];X_{0})
with
1\leq p<\infty
, and
\frac{\partial\hat{F}}{\partial t}
be bounded in
L^{1}([0,T];X_{1})
, then
\hat{F}
is relatively compact in
L^{p}([0,T];X)
.

(2)
Let
\tilde{F}
be bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];X_{0})
and
\frac{\partial\tilde{F}}{\partial t}
be bounded in
L^{q}([0,T];X_{1})
with
q>1
, then
\tilde{F}
is relatively compact in
C([0,T];X)
.

The next one is used to improve a weak convergence to a strong one.

Lemma 8.3
.

If the function sequence

Lemma 8.4
.

[
5
]
Let
0<\alpha<d
and
1<p<q<\infty
. The Riesz potential
I_{\alpha}f=(-\Delta)^{-\alpha/2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined as

(I_{\alpha}f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\dfrac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha}}{\rm{d}}y.

Then for
(p,q)
satisfying
1/q=1/p-\alpha/d
, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
p
, such that the following estimate holds:

|I_{\alpha}f|_{q}\leq C|f|_{p}.

The fifth lemma concerns the regularity estimates of Poisson equation.

Lemma 8.5
.

[
15
]
Let
f\in H^{s-1}
. Suppose
\Phi
solves the following Poisson equation

\Delta\Phi=f,

then for
q>\max\{1,\frac{2}{d-1}\}
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
q
and
d
, such that the following estimate holds:

|\nabla\Phi|_{q}\leq C|f|_{\frac{qd}{q+2}}.

In addition, if
f
has compact support, i.e., supp
{}_{x}f\subset B_{\tilde{R}}
, then it holds

\|\nabla\Phi\|_{s}\leq C(\tilde{R}^{\frac{d+2}{2}}+1)\|f\|_{s-1}.

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
\tilde{R}
.

Proof.

One obtains the following representation formula of solutions for
d\geq 3
:

\Phi=-\dfrac{1}{d(d-2)\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\dfrac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}{\rm{d}}y,

(8.2)

with
\omega_{n}
the volume of a unit ball. Since
f\in H^{s-1}
, then the representation (
8.2
) is well-defined. By the classical elliptic theories (c.f.
[
15
]
), one has

\nabla\Phi=\dfrac{1}{d\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\dfrac{(x-y)f(y)}{|x-y|^{d}}{\rm{d}}y.

By Lemma
8.4
, for
q>\max\{1,\frac{d}{d-1}\}
,

|\nabla\Phi|_{q}\leq C|f|_{\frac{qd}{q+d}}.

In addition, if
f
has compact support, i.e., supp
{}_{x}f\subset B_{\tilde{R}}
for some positive
\tilde{R}
, then

|\nabla\Phi|_{2}\leq C|f|_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\leq C|f|_{\infty}\tilde{R}^{\frac{d+2}{2}}.

For high order derivatives, it holds

\displaystyle|\nabla^{l}\nabla\Phi|_{2}\leq C|\nabla^{l-1}f|_{2},\quad 1\leq l\leq s,

which implies that
\nabla\Phi\in H^{s}
.
∎

, then there exists a constant depending only on

, such that

Finally, we give one lemma on the decay estimates of solutions of Burgers equations.
Let
\hat{u}
be the solution in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
of (
1.8
). Without loss of generality, we drop the subscript
\nu
. Then
\hat{u}
is a constant in
t
along the particle path
\Omega(t;x_{0})
defined as

\dfrac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\Omega(t;x_{0})=\hat{u}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0})),\quad\Omega(0,x_{0})=x_{0}.

(8.3)

More specifically, it holds that

\hat{u}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))=u^{0}(x_{0}),\quad\nabla\hat{u}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))=(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\nabla u^{0}(x_{0}))^{-1}\nabla u^{0}(x_{0}),

(8.4)

where
\mathbb{I}_{d}
is the
d\times d
unit matrix. Based on these, one has

Lemma 8.9
.

[
17
]
Let
s>\frac{d}{2}+1
be a real number. Assume that

\nabla u^{0}\in L^{\infty},\quad\nabla^{2}u^{0}\in H^{s-1},

and there exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\text{dist}\,(\text{Sp}(\nabla u^{0}(x)),\mathbb{R}^{-})\geq\kappa,

then there exists a unique global classical solution
\hat{u}
to the problem (
1.8
) satisfying

(1)

For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\geq 0
,

\nabla\hat{u}(t,x)=\dfrac{1}{1+t}\mathbb{I}_{d}+\dfrac{1}{(1+t)^{2}}K(t,x),

(8.5)

where the matrix
K(t,x)
satisfies

\|K\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(1+\kappa^{-d}|\nabla u^{0}|_{\infty}^{d-1}):=M;

(2)

|\nabla^{\sigma}\hat{u}(t,\cdot)|_{2}\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-\sigma-1},\qquad 2\leq\sigma\leq s+1;

(3)

|\nabla^{2}\hat{u}(t,\cdot)|_{\infty}\leq C(1+t)^{-3}|\nabla^{2}u^{0}|_{\infty}
,

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
s,d,\kappa
,
u^{0}
and
l
. Moreover, it holds

|\hat{u}(t,x)|\leq C(1+|\nabla\hat{u}|_{\infty})(1+|x|){\rm{exp}}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla\hat{u}|_{\infty}{\rm{d}}s\Big),\quad\text{for any}\,\,(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(8.6)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
u_{0}(0)
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into 4 steps:
(I)
-
(IV)
,

•

(I):
giving the global existence of
C^{1}
solution to (
1.8
), which satisfies (1);

•

(II):
proving (2) and showing that this
C^{1}
solution indeed exists in the space
\Xi_{m}
globally in time, where

\|f\|_{\Xi_{m}}=|\nabla f|_{\infty}+\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{m-1}\ \ \text{for \ any}\ m>1+d/2;

•

(III)-(IV):
establishing the estimates in (3) and (
8.6
).

(I)
The local existence of
\widehat{u}(t,x)
in the space
C^{1}
can be established from the characteristic method and the implicit function theorem (see §3 of Evans
[
12
]
). For extending
\widehat{u}
to be a global
C^{1}
solution, since
\widehat{u}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))
remains invariant along the particle path
\Omega(t;x_{0})
, one only needs to give the uniform estimates on
|\nabla_{x}\widehat{u}|_{\infty}
with respect to the time.

For the first order derivative, let
\mathbb{G}(t,x)=\nabla\widehat{u}(t,x)
and
\mathbb{G}_{0}(x_{0})=\nabla u^{0}(x_{0})
.
Then

\begin{split}\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))=&\big(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0}(x_{0})\big)^{-1}\mathbb{G}_{0}(x_{0})\\
=&\mathbb{G}_{0}(x_{0})\big(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0}(x_{0})\big)^{-1}=\frac{1}{t}\Big(\mathbb{I}_{d}-\big(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0}(x_{0})\big)^{-1}\Big),\end{split}

(8.7)

where one has used the fact
\nabla_{x_{0}}\Omega=\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0}(x_{0})
. Note that

\big(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0}(x_{0})\big)^{-1}=\big(\text{det}(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})\big)^{-1}(\text{adj}(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0}))^{\top},

(8.8)

where
\text{adj}(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})
stands for the adjugate of
(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})
. Then it holds that

|\nabla_{x}\widehat{u}|_{\infty}\leq\frac{(1+t|\nabla u^{0}|_{\infty})^{d-1}}{(1+t\kappa)^{d}}|\nabla_{x_{0}}u^{0}|_{\infty},

(8.9)

which implies that
\widehat{u}
is a global
C^{1}
solution to the problem (
1.8
). Rewrite

\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))=\frac{1}{1+t}\mathbb{I}_{d}+\frac{1}{(1+t)^{2}}K(t,x_{0}),

where
K(t,x_{0})=(1+t)^{2}(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{-1}\mathbb{G}_{0}-(1+t)\mathbb{I}_{d}
.

Since
\mathbb{G}^{-1}_{0}=\big(\text{det}(\mathbb{G}_{0})\big)^{-1}(\text{adj}\mathbb{G}_{0})^{\top}
, so

|\mathbb{G}^{-1}_{0}|_{\infty}\leq C\kappa^{-d}|\mathbb{G}_{0}|^{d-1}_{\infty}.

Then for
t
large enough, one has
|t^{-1}\mathbb{G}^{-1}_{0}(x_{0})|<1
for all
x_{0}
, and

\begin{split}K(t,x_{0})=&\frac{(t+1)^{2}}{t}(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t^{-1}\mathbb{G}^{-1}_{0})^{-1}-(1+t)\mathbb{I}_{d}\\
=&\frac{(t+1)^{2}}{t}\Big(\mathbb{I}_{d}-\frac{\mathbb{G}^{-1}_{0}}{t}+O\Big(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\Big)\Big)-(1+t)\mathbb{I}_{d}\\
=&\frac{(t+1)}{t}\mathbb{I}_{d}-\frac{(t+1)^{2}}{t^{2}}\mathbb{G}^{-1}_{0}+O\Big(\frac{1}{t}\Big).\end{split}

Remark 8.1
.

Let
\lambda
be an eigenvalue of
\mathbb{G}_{0}
. Then indentity (
8.7
) implies any eigenvalue
\lambda_{\mathbb{G}}
of
\mathbb{G}
satisfies

\lambda_{\mathbb{G}}=\frac{1}{t}\Big(1-\big(1+t\lambda\big)^{-1}\Big)=\frac{\lambda}{1+t\lambda}>0.

(II)

Now we show that the
C^{1}
solution
\widehat{u}(t,x)
obtained above indeed exists in
\Xi_{m}
globally in time. To this end, one needs to show that
\nabla^{k}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(
1\leq k\leq m
) for any
t\in[0,\infty)
. Actually, it follows from
\widehat{u}(t,x)=u^{0}(x-t\widehat{u}(t,x))
that

\mathbb{G}(t,x)=\big(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0}(x-t\widehat{u}(t,x))\big)^{-1}\mathbb{G}_{0}(x-t\widehat{u}(t,x)).

Then according to the initial assumptions on
u^{0}
, by induction, it is easy to show that
\nabla^{k}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,x)
(
1\leq k\leq m
) is well defined in
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
pointwisely. What we only need to do is to give the estimates (2) in Lemma
8.9
. However, the information of
\nabla^{k}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,x)
cannot be given directly from the problem (
1.8
). Thus we consider
\nabla^{k}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))
first, and then obtain the desired estimates via a change of variables.

For the high order derivatives, let
\mathbb{H}(t,x_{0})=\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))
. Starting from the right hand side of the first line in (
8.7
), by induction, one can get that, for
1\leq k\leq m
:

\nabla^{k}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{H}(t,x_{0})=(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{-1}\Lambda_{k}(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{-1},

(8.10)

where
\Lambda_{k}
is a sum of products of
t(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{-1}
and
\nabla^{j}\mathbb{G}_{0},\ j\in\{0,1,...,k\}
, appearing
\beta_{j}
times with
\sum_{j}j\beta_{j}=k
.

On the one hand, starting from the left hand side of the first line in (
8.7
), one gets by induction that, for
1\leq k\leq m
:

\nabla^{k}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{H}(t,x_{0})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\nabla^{j}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))\Big(\sum_{1\leq k_{i}\leq k}\nabla^{k_{1}}_{x_{0}}\Omega\otimes...\otimes\nabla^{k_{j}}_{x_{0}}\Omega\Big)

(8.11)

with
\sum_{i=1}^{j}k_{i}=k
, and

\begin{split}\nabla_{x_{0}}\Omega=&\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0}(x_{0}),\quad\nabla^{l}_{x_{0}}\Omega=t\nabla^{l-1}\end{split}

On the other hand, one can also show that for all
1\leq j\leq m
by induction:
IH(j)
.

(\rm\textbf{IH(j)}_{1})

\nabla^{j}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))
is a sum of terms which are products in a certain order of :
(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{-1}
,
t\mathbb{I}_{d}
,
\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0}
or
\nabla^{l}\mathbb{G}_{0}
appearing
\beta_{l}
times, with
\sum_{l}l\beta_{l}=j
;

(\rm\textbf{IH(j)}_{2})

the
L^{\infty}
-norms of the terms with
t
are bounded by a constant times
(1+t)^{-(j+2)}
, and

|\nabla^{j}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;\cdot))|_{2}\leq C_{j}(1+t)^{-(j+2)},\quad\text{with}\quad C_{j}=C(\kappa,j,\|u^{0}\|_{\Xi_{m}}).

Actually, when
j=1
, one has

\nabla_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))=(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{-1}\nabla\mathbb{G}_{0}(x_{0})(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{-2},

(8.12)

which, along with the estimates obtained in
(I)
, yields
IH(1)
.

Now suppose that
IH(k-1)
holds. For
\nabla^{k}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))
, it follows from (
8.11
) that

\begin{split}\nabla^{k}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))=&\Big(\nabla^{k}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{H}(t,x_{0})-\mathbb{J}\Big)\odot\Big((\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{-1}\Big)^{\otimes k}.\end{split}

where

\mathbb{J}=-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\nabla^{j}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))\Big(\sum_{1\leq k_{i}\leq k-1}\nabla^{k_{1}}_{x_{0}}\Omega\otimes...\otimes\nabla^{k_{j}}_{x_{0}}\Omega\Big).

In the right-hand side term, one has the norm of the following terms to estimate:

•

O_{1}=(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{-1}\Lambda_{k}(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{-k-1}
;

•

O_{2}=\nabla^{j}_{x}G(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{j-s}\amalg_{k_{i}\neq 1}t\nabla^{k_{i}-1}\mathbb{G}_{0}(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{-k}
,
\sum_{k_{i}\neq 1}(k_{i}-1)=k-j
,

where one has used (
8.10
), and
s
is the number of
k_{j}\neq 1
.

First,
O_{1}
has the desired form in
(\rm\textbf{IH(j)}_{1})
, and the
L^{\infty}
-norm of the terms with
t
is bounded by a constant times
(1+t)^{-(k+2)}
.

Second, it follows from
IH(j)
for
j\leq k-1
that
O_{2}
is a product of
(\nabla^{j}\mathbb{G}_{0})^{\beta_{j}}
with
\sum_{j}j\beta_{j}=k
and of
t\mathbb{I}_{d}
,
\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0}
,
(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})^{-1}
, such that the
L^{\infty}
-norm of the terms with
t
is bounded by a constant times
(1+t)^{-(k+2)}
.

For the desired
L^{2}
estimates, it follows from the initial assumptions on
u^{0}
,
m>1+\frac{d}{2}
and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality that one can find an upper bound in
L^{2}
-norm for

\amalg_{1\leq j\leq k}\big(\nabla^{j}\mathbb{G}_{0}\big)^{\beta_{j}}
with
\sum_{j}j\beta_{j}=k
. Similarly, under the help of
IH(j)
for
j\leq k-1
, one can also obtained an upper bound of
|\nabla^{j}_{x}G\amalg_{k_{i}\neq 1}\nabla^{k_{i}-1}\mathbb{G}_{0}|_{2}
with
\sum_{k_{i}\neq 1}(k_{i}-1)=k-j
. Then

\begin{split}|O_{1}|_{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-k-2}|\Lambda_{k}|_{2}\leq C(1+t\kappa)^{-k-2}|\amalg\big(\nabla^{j}\mathbb{G}_{0}\big)^{\beta_{j}}|_{2}\leq&C_{k}(1+t)^{-k-2},\\
|O_{2}|_{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-k+j}|\nabla^{j}_{x}\mathbb{G}\amalg_{k_{i}\neq 1}\nabla^{k_{i}-1}\mathbb{G}_{0}|_{2}\leq&C_{k}(1+t)^{-k-2},\end{split}

where the positive constant
C_{k}=C(\kappa,k,\|u^{0}\|_{\Xi_{m}})
.
To conclude, one obtains the upper bound in
IH(k)
which depends on
\kappa
,
|\mathbb{G}_{0}|_{\infty}
, and
|\nabla^{k}\mathbb{G}_{0}|_{2}
for
1\leq k\leq m
.

Finally, it needs to make a change of variables to obtain:

\begin{split}|\nabla^{j}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,\cdot)|_{2}=&\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla^{j}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,x)|^{2}{\rm{d}}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
=&\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla^{j}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))|^{2}|\text{det}(\mathbb{I}_{d}+t\mathbb{G}_{0})|{\rm{d}}x_{0}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

(8.13)

which, along with
IH(j)
, yields that

|\nabla^{j}_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,\cdot)|_{2}\leq C_{j}(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-(j+2)}.

Therefore, the conclusion (2) in Lemma
8.9
is true for
l\in\mathbb{N},\ 2\leq l\leq m+1
since
\nabla_{x}\mathbb{G}=\nabla^{2}\widehat{u}
. Then by interpolation one obtains the result for all
l\in\mathbb{R},\ 2\leq l\leq m+1
.

Until now, one has established the global-in-time well-posedness of the solutions
\widehat{u}
to Burgers equations (
1.8
) in
\Xi
.

(III)
Since
m-1>\frac{d}{2}
, one has
\nabla^{2}u^{0}\in L^{\infty}
. Thus
\nabla^{2}\widehat{u}\in L^{\infty}
. Then the estimates (3) in Lemma
8.9
follows directly from (
8.12
) and the estimates obtained in
(I)
, i.e.,

|\nabla_{x}\mathbb{G}(t,\Omega(t;x_{0}))|_{\infty}=O((1+t)^{-3}).

(IV)

Integrating the Burgers equations with respect to
t
yields

\hat{u}(t,x)=\hat{u}(0,x)-\int_{0}^{t}(\hat{u}\cdot\nabla)\hat{u}{\rm{d}}s.

(8.14)

Making Taylor’s expansion towards
\hat{u}(0,x)
, one obtains

|\hat{u}(0,x)|\leq|\hat{u}(0,0)|+|\nabla\hat{u}|_{\infty}|x|\leq C(1+|\nabla\hat{u}|_{\infty})(1+|x|).

(8.15)

Substituting (
8.15
) into (
8.14
) leads to

|\hat{u}(t,x)|\leq C(1+|\nabla\hat{u}|_{\infty})(1+|x|)+\int_{0}^{t}|\hat{u}(s,x)||\nabla\hat{u}(s,x)|_{\infty}{\rm{d}}s.

By Gronwall’s inequality, for
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds

|\hat{u}(t,x)|\leq C(1+|\nabla\hat{u}|_{\infty})(1+|x|)\text{exp}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla\hat{u}|_{\infty}{\rm{d}}s\Big).

Thus (
8.6
) is proved.

Γ \Gamma -convergence of the non-local Massari functional and applications to inhomogeneous Allen-Cahn equations
Serena Dipierro, Enrico Valdinoci, Riccardo Villa

Abstract
We present several asymptotic results concerning the non-local Massari Problem for sets with prescribed mean curvature. In particular, we show that the fractional Massari functional Γ \Gamma -converges to the classical one, and this convergence preserves minimizers in the L loc 1 L^{1}_{\rm{loc}} -topology. This returns useful information about the asymptotic behavior of the solutions of the inhomogeneous Allen-Cahn equation in the forced and the mass-prescribed settings. In this context, a new geometric object, which we refer to as “non-local hybrid mean curvature”, naturally appears.



Contents

1
Introduction and main results

In this paper we prove some asymptotic results involving the non-local Massari Problem for sets with prescribed mean curvature and obtain some interesting applications to the space-fractional Allen-Cahn equation.

The Massari Problem has been firstly formulated in the classical case by Umberto Massari in the mid 70s and provides a variational definition of mean curvature when the latter is a summable, but not necessarily continuous function (see e.g.
[
23
,
18
]
). Recently, the fractional counterpart has also been investigated in
[
16
]
.

The mathematical details are as follows. For the rest of our discussion, we denote by
\Omega
a bounded, Lipschitz, open set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Besides, whenever needed, all subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be implicitly assumed to be Lebesgue-measurable.

We recall that, given a function
H\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, the Massari functional
\mathscr{J}_{\Omega}^{H}
introduced in
[
23
]
is defined for every set
E
as

\mathscr{J}_{\Omega}^{H}(E):=\operatorname{Per}(E,\Omega)+\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\Omega\cap E}H\,dx,

(1.1)

where
\operatorname{Per}
denotes the variational perimeter, and
\omega_{n-1}
is the volume of the unitary ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

In the wake of
[
7
]
, for
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, we also denote the non-local interaction between a couple of disjoint sets
A
and
B
by

\mathcal{L}(A,B):=\int_{A}\int_{B}\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}},

and the
s
-Perimeter of a set
E
by

\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega):=\mathcal{L}(E\cap\Omega,E^{c}\cap\Omega)+\mathcal{L}(E\cap\Omega,E^{c}\cap\Omega^{c})+\mathcal{L}(E\cap\Omega^{c},E^{c}\cap\Omega).

Thus, similarly to (
1.1
), the non-local Massari functional
\mathscr{J}_{s,\Omega}^{H}
is defined in
[
16
]
for every set
E
as

\mathscr{J}_{s,\Omega}^{H}(E):=\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega)+\frac{1}{1-2s}\int_{\Omega\cap E}H\,dx.

(1.2)

In our study, we focus on:

(i)

proving a general
\Gamma
-convergence result for a suitably rescaled non-local Massari functional, when the fractional parameter
s
approaches
(1/2)^{-}
;

(ii)

showing that the limit of a convergent sequence
\{E_{j}\}_{j}
of minima for
\mathscr{J}_{s_{j},\Omega}^{H_{j}}
is a minimum for the limit functional;

(iii)

investigating the asymptotic behavior of the solutions of an inhomogeneous Allen-Cahn equation;

(iv)

discussing some properties of blow-down solutions of a prescribed-mass Allen-Cahn equation. In particular, in this context, we deal with a new geometric object which we refer to as a “non-local hybrid mean curvature”.

The outline of the corresponding results goes as follows.
The first
\Gamma
-convergence result deals with the case
s\to(1/2)^{-}
and is contained in Theorem
1.3
. This result is perhaps not surprising
in itself, and its proof is an offspring of the methods in
[
4
]
,
but it is instrumental to establish the limit behavior of the functional under consideration
and it paves the way to the analysis of the minimizers of the non-local Massari Problem.

This analysis is started by Theorem
1.4
, in which we show that the limit
of minimizers is a minimizer of the limit functional, with uniform energy bounds. The methods of
[
4
]
are helpful in the proof of this result too, but
here one also needs some tailor-made, and somewhat delicate, estimates for
the new mean curvature contributions.

Then, we turn our attention to a non-local Allen-Cahn equation
with a “highly oscillatory” forcing term
and we detect its homogenized behavior in the limit.
This setting can be seen as the phase transition counterpart of the Massari Problem for a small singular perturbation parameter
\varepsilon
.
We establish a
\Gamma
-convergence result as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
in Theorem
1.7

and the corresponding stability of minimizers in Theorem
1.8
.

As a matter of fact, Theorem
1.7
is again perhaps not surprising in itself
and its proof heavily relies on
[
29
]
, but it serves
to establish the appropriate limit functional. The proof of Theorem
1.8
is instead much more delicate
and requires specific pointwise and energy estimates of uniform type. Also,
the case
s\in[1/2,\,1)
will require additional effort since non-local contributions do localize in the limit.

We also consider problems with mass constraints.
The corresponding
\Gamma
-convergence result for singularly perturbed non-local phase transition model is provided in Theorem
1.10
.
The delicate part of this result consists in the analysis of the recovery sequence
in the case
s\in[1/2,\,1)
, since in this case the existing literature falls short.
Specifically, the recovery sequence constructed in
[
29
]
accounted for the Dirichlet external condition and instead, in our case,
suitable adjustments have to be performed to satisfy the mass condition: the impact
of these modifications in the “
\limsup
-inequality” needs to be carefully controlled
and this makes the proof somehow involved.

As a byproduct of Theorem
1.10
, we obtain a
uniform energy bound for the non-local phase transition energy under the mass constraint,
which is stated explicitly in Theorem
1.11
.
The miminizers of this problem fulfill a non-local external condition of Neumann type,
as detailed in Theorem
1.12
.

Interestingly, critical points of the mass constrained
problem satisfy a non-local phase transition equation with a Lagrange multiplier.
This additional term also depends on the singular perturbation parameter
\varepsilon

and it is therefore natural to investigate its asymptotic as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. This problem is
addressed in Theorem
1.14
, whose proof requires
appropriate uniform estimates for this scope.

The convergence of these Lagrange multipliers also produces
a limit functional, and one would like to relate the corresponding energies in the limit.
This question is dealt with in Theorem
1.15
,
where it is shown that minimizers for the
\varepsilon
-perturbed functional
approach globally (and not only locally, thanks to a non-local Neumann condition)
a limit functional, and that the minimal energy converges to the energy of this limit function.

An interesting aspect of the asymptotics in the problems with mass prescription
is that, while the large interactions provided by the double well potential force
the minimizers to attain “pure phases” in the domain of reference,
outside this domain minimizers can still oscillate and do not reduce
to piecewise constant functions.

This is an interesting feature, showcased in
Theorem
1.20
, giving rise to a new geometric object,
which we name “non-local hybrid mean curvature”.
In a nutshell, while classical problems of phase transitions with a mass constraint
are related to constant mean curvature limit configurations,
in our setting the limit consists of a couple, given by
a set inside the domain and of a function outside. The non-local hybrid mean curvature
corresponds to a singular kernel integral involving this couple;
limit configurations are related here to the
constancy of the non-local hybrid mean curvature.

The proof of Theorem
1.20
relies on a subtle refinement of the results in
[
24
]
, which are not directly applicable in our framework due to a lack of global regularity
(this improvement can be useful on other occasions too and is stated
in Theorem
1.18
).

Let us now dive into the technical details of our results.

1.1
\Gamma
-convergence for the Massari Problem

The notion of
\Gamma
-convergence has been introduced by De Giorgi as an alternative to the convergence in the sense of Kuratowski (
K
-convergence). This has been an intense field of research in the recent years, finding many interesting applications in the fields of partial differential equations and mathematical physics (see for instance
[
26
,
25
,
6
,
33
,
29
]
).

We recall the following classical definition of
\Gamma
-convergence (see
[
11
, Proposition 8.1]
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
V
be a topological space that satisfies the first axiom of countability. Consider a sequence of functions
\{F_{h}:V\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}\}_{h}
, and a function
F:V\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
.

We say that
F_{h}

\Gamma
-converges to
F
, and we write
F_{h}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}F
, if the following properties hold:

(i)

for every
x\in V
and for every sequence
\{x_{h}\}_{h}
in
V
converging to
x
, it holds that

\liminf_{h\to+\infty}F_{h}(x_{h})\geq F(x)\ ;

(ii)

for every
x\in V
there exists a sequence
\{x_{h}\}_{h}\subset V
such that
x_{h}\to x
and

\limsup_{h\to+\infty}F_{h}(x_{h})\leq F(x)\ .

We also recall from
[
16
]
that a solution of the non-local Massari Problem is a set
E
satisfying

\mathscr{J}_{s,\Omega}^{H}(E)\leq\mathscr{J}_{s,\Omega}^{H}(F)\text{ for every\penalty\ $F$ such that }F\setminus\Omega=E\setminus\Omega.

(1.3)

Moreover, the following definition of non-local almost minimizers has been given in
[
16
, Definition 1.1]
:

Definition 1.2
.

(Non-local almost minimizers). Given a parameter
\Lambda\geq 0
, we say that a set
E

is a
\Lambda
-minimal set (or is an almost minimal set with respect to
\Lambda
) for the
s
-Perimeter in
\Omega
if

\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega)\leq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega)+\Lambda|E\Delta F|,

for every set
F
such that
E\Delta F\subset\Omega
.

We have that, when
H\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
a solution
E
of (
1.3
) is a
\Lambda
-minimal set, with
\Lambda:=\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. Hence,
\partial E
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-hypersurface up to a set of Hausdorff dimension
n-3
, for every
\alpha\in(0,s)
, and has variational non-local mean curvature
H
in
\Omega
(see
[
16
, Section 7]
for the details).

Important
\Gamma
-convergence results in the setting of minimal surfaces have been developed in
[
4
]
, where it is also proved that a suitable rescaling of the
s
-Perimeter
\Gamma
-converges to the classical one, as
s\to(1/2)^{-}
, that a convergent sequence of
s
-minimal surfaces has uniformly bounded energy,
and that its limit is a minimal surface in the classical sense
(see
[
5
,
12
,
10
]

for related results).

We show here that analogous results hold also for the Massari Problem:

Theorem 1.3

(
\Gamma
-convergence of the non-local Massari Problem)
.

Let
\{s_{j}\}_{j}\subset\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
be a sequence such that
s_{j}\to(1/2)^{-}
. Let
\{H_{j}\}_{j}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be
such that
\sup_{j}\|H_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<+\infty
, and
H_{j}\to H
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, for some function
H
.

Then, for every set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have that:

(i)

for every sequence of sets
\{E_{j}\}_{j}
such that
E_{j}\to E
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it holds that

\omega_{n-1}\mathscr{J}_{\Omega}^{H}(E)\leq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}(1-2s_{j})\mathscr{J}_{s_{j},\Omega}^{H_{j}}(E_{j}),

(ii)

there exists a sequence of sets
\{E_{j}\}_{j}
such that
E_{j}\to E
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\omega_{n-1}\mathscr{J}_{\Omega}^{H}(E)\geq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}(1-2s_{j})\mathscr{J}_{s_{j},\Omega}^{H_{j}}(E_{j}).

Theorem 1.4

(Energy bounds and limit stability of minimizers for the non-local Massari Problem)
.

Let
\{s_{j}\}_{j}\subset\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
be a sequence such that
s_{j}\to(1/2)^{-}
, and
\{H_{j}\}_{j}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
\sup_{j}\|H_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<+\infty
, and
H_{j}\to H
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Let
\{E_{j}\}_{j}
be a sequence of sets such that
E_{j}
is a local minimizer for
\mathscr{J}_{s_{j},\Omega}^{H_{j}}
, and
E_{j}\to E
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for some set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Then,

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\,(1-2s_{j})\mathscr{J}_{s_{j},K}^{H_{j}}(E_{j})<+\infty,\quad\text{for every }K\subset\subset\Omega.

(1.4)

Moreover,
E
is a local minimizer for the classical Massari functional, and

\lim_{j\to+\infty}(1-2s_{j})\mathscr{J}_{s_{j},K}^{H_{j}}(E_{j})=\omega_{n-1}\mathscr{J}_{K}^{H}(E)\ ,

(1.5)

for every
K\subset\subset\Omega
such that
\operatorname{Per}(E,\partial K)=0
.

We point out that the factor
1-2s
in Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
is the appropriate scaling factor to obtain interesting
\Gamma
-limit properties.

The proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
will be presented in Sections
2
and
3
respectively.

1.2
An alternative formulation for the Massari Problem

Since we are interested in the
\Gamma
-convergence for inhomogeneous Allen-Cahn equations, we recall that the limit of a convergent sequence of minimizers for the Allen-Cahn energy functional takes the form
\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}
, for some set
E
(see
[
29
, Theorem 1.3]
). As an analogous result holds true in our setting, in order to incorporate the extra term due to the term
-\chi_{E^{c}}
in the
\Gamma
-limit, we now propose an alternative formulation of the Massari Problem to the one presented in (
1.2
).

With this aim, notice that, by definition of
s
-Perimeter, for every set
E
, we have

\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega)=\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E^{c},\Omega),

hence

\mathscr{J}_{s,\Omega}^{H}(E)-\frac{1}{1-2s}\int_{\Omega}Hdx=\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E^{c},\Omega)-\frac{1}{1-2s}\int_{\Omega\cap E^{c}}Hdx=\mathscr{J}_{s,\Omega}^{-H}(E^{c}).

This justifies the intuition that a set
E
has non-local mean-curvature
H
if and only if
E^{c}
has non-local mean-curvature
-H
.

Moreover, we deduce that
E
is a solution of the non-local Massari Problem according to (
1.3
) if and only if
E
is a minimizer of

\text{P}_{s,\Omega}^{H}(F):=\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F,\Omega)+\frac{1}{2(1-2s)}\int_{\Omega}(\chi_{F}-\chi_{F^{c}})Hdx,

(1.6)

among all sets
F
such that
F\setminus\Omega=E\setminus\Omega
.

Similarly, a set
E
is a solution of the classical Massari Problem if and only if it is a minimizer of

\text{P}_{\Omega}^{H}(F):=\operatorname{Per}(F,\Omega)+\frac{1}{2\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\Omega}(\chi_{F}-\chi_{F^{c}})Hdx,

(1.7)

among all the sets
F
such that
F\setminus\Omega=E\setminus\Omega
.

Recalling what discussed in the Introduction of
[
23
]
and
[
16
, Definition 1.14]
, we have the following:

Definition 1.5
.

Let
H
be a function in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. We say that a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
has (variational) mean curvature
H
if
E
is a minimizer of
\text{P}_{\Omega}^{H}
among all the set which coincides with
E
outside
\Omega
.

Similarly, we say that a set
E
has non-local (variational) mean curvature
H
if
E
is a minimizer of
\text{P}_{s,\Omega}^{H}
among all the sets which coincide with
E
outside
\Omega
.

1.3
A perturbed inhomogeneous Allen-Cahn equation

\Gamma
-convergence represents a key tool in handling singularly perturbed energies. One of the main examples of the latter comes from the gradient theory of phase transitions (see
[
27
,
26
,
20
]
), where the total energy is given by a “double well potential”
W
with two isolated minima plus a gradient term that prevents the formation of unnecessary interfaces between the two phases. Such energy is related to the so-called Allen-Cahn equation and takes the form

\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+W(u)\right]dx,\quad\text{with }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

In this framework, the assumptions that we take on the potential
W
are the following: we suppose that
W:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
satisfies

\begin{split}&W\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\quad W(\pm 1)=0,\quad W>0\;{\mbox{ in }}\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\pm 1\},\\
&W^{\prime}(\pm 1)=0\quad{\mbox{ and }}\quad W^{\prime\prime}(\pm 1)>0.\end{split}

(1.8)

For the sake of concreteness, one can keep in mind the model case
W(t):=\frac{1}{4}(1-t^{2})^{2}
.

A non-local analog of the Allen-Cahn energy functional, where the gradient term is replaced by a fractional Gagliardo semi-norm, has been addressed in the recent literature (see
[
29
,
30
]
).

In this paper, we generalize this non-local setting taking into account a macroscopic forcing term
H
and proving a
\Gamma
-convergence result for the new perturbed energy. Then, we show that
H
is suitably related to the variational mean curvature of the minimizers of the
\Gamma
-limit energy functional.

For our purposes, we adopt some notation introduced in
[
29
,
30
]
. Given
M\in(1,+\infty)
, we define

X_{M}:=\big\{u\in L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ s.t. }\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M\big\},

(1.9)

which is a Fréchet space when endowed with the family of semi-norms
\{\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}(B_{r})}\}_{r\in\mathbb{Q}}
.

For every function
u\in X_{M}
and for every couple of sets
A
,
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

u(A,B):=\int_{A}\int_{B}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy.

Then, we define the functional

\begin{split}\text{K}(u,\Omega):=\,&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\\
=\,&\frac{1}{2}u(\Omega,\Omega)+u(\Omega,\Omega^{c}),\end{split}

(1.10)

which accounts for the local contribution in
\Omega
of the
H^{s}
-Gagliardo semi-norm of
u
.

With this notation, the energy functional
\text{J}_{\varepsilon}:X_{M}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
associated with the non-local Allen-Cahn equation is

\text{J}_{\varepsilon}(u,\Omega):=\varepsilon^{2s}\text{K}(u,\Omega)+\int_{\Omega}W(u)dx.

In order to obtain interesting
\Gamma
-limits for the functional
\text{J}_{\varepsilon}
and prevent the energy to diverge when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we introduce the rescaled energy functional
\text{F}_{\varepsilon}:X_{M}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, depending on whether
s\in(0,1/2)
,
s=1/2
, or
s\in(1/2,1)
, as

\text{F}_{\varepsilon}(u,\Omega):=\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{-2s}\text{J}_{\varepsilon}(u,\Omega)\quad&\text{if }s\in(0,1/2),\\
|\varepsilon\log\varepsilon|^{-1}\text{J}_{\varepsilon}(u,\Omega)\quad&\text{if }s=1/2,\\
\varepsilon^{-1}\text{J}_{\varepsilon}(u,\Omega)\quad&\text{if }s\in(1/2,1).\end{cases}

(1.11)

In
[
29
, Theorem 1.2]
, the authors have shown that
\text{F}_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converges to the functional
\text{F}:X_{M}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined as

\text{F}(u,\Omega):=\begin{cases}\text{K}(u,\Omega)\quad&\text{if $u|_{\Omega}=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}$, for some set }E\subset\Omega,\\
&\quad\text{and $s\in(0,1/2)$,}\\
c_{\star}\operatorname{Per}(E,\Omega)\quad&\text{if $u|_{\Omega}=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}$, for some set }E\subset\Omega,\\
&\quad\text{and $s\in[1/2,1)$,}\\
+\infty\quad&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(1.12)

where
c_{\star}=c_{\star}(n,s,W)
is a suitable positive constant related to a particular
1
-dimensional minimal profile (see
[
29
, Theorem 4.2 and Formula (4.35)]
).

Remark 1.6
.

We point out that the results presented in
[
29
]
are stated and proved for functionals
\text{F}_{\varepsilon}
and
F
defined on the space

X:=\{u\in L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mbox{ s.t. }\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1\}.

However, by inspection of the proofs contained there, we see that the same results can be proved with identical arguments when considering the space
X_{M}
defined in (
1.9
), for any
M\geq 1
.

In fact, the upper bound imposed in the definition of
X
plays no other role than being uniform (in
\varepsilon
). Moreover, we can neglect the uniform boundedness in
\Omega^{c}
for the sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
proving
\Gamma
-convergence (see Theorem 1.2 and related results in
[
29
]
). Besides, we have that
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq M
, whenever
u_{\varepsilon}
is a minimizer for
\text{F}_{\varepsilon}
in
X_{M}
(see Lemma
5.2
here below), as in the assumptions of
[
29
, Theorem 1.3 and Proposition 4.7]
.

Now, let
g
be a function in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and consider a “forced” non-local phase-transition model with a perturbed fractional Allen-Cahn energy functional given by

\varepsilon^{2s}\text{K}(u,\Omega)+\int_{\Omega}\left(W(u)+g_{\varepsilon}u\right)dx,

where
g_{\varepsilon}(x):=g(x/\varepsilon)
.

Then, we define the sequence
\{H_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as

H_{\varepsilon}(x):=\begin{cases}2\varepsilon^{-2s}g_{\varepsilon}(x)\quad&\text{if }s\in(0,1/2),\\
2|\varepsilon\log(\varepsilon)|^{-1}g_{\varepsilon}(x)\quad&\text{if }s=1/2,\\
2\varepsilon^{-1}g_{\varepsilon}(x)\quad&\text{if }s\in(1/2,1).\end{cases}

Furthermore, assume that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|H_{\varepsilon}-H\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=0,

(1.13)

for some macroscopic forcing term
H\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We consider the forcing term energy contributions
\text{H}_{\varepsilon}
,
\text{H}:X_{M}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle\text{H}_{\varepsilon}(u,\Omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle c_{n,s}\int_{\Omega}H_{\varepsilon}u\,dx,

\displaystyle{\mbox{and }}\quad\text{H}(u,\Omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle c_{n,s}\int_{\Omega}H\,u\,dx,

where

c_{n,s}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1-2s)}&{\mbox{ if }}s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right),\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\omega_{n-1}}&{\mbox{ if }}s\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right).\end{cases}

We also define the total perturbed energies

\text{E}_{\varepsilon}:=\text{F}_{\varepsilon}+\text{H}_{\varepsilon}\quad{\mbox{
and}}\quad\text{E}:=\text{F}+\text{H}.

With this notation, we prove the following results which extend
[
29
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3]
to the setting under consideration here.

Theorem 1.7

(
\Gamma
-convergence of the perturbed fractional Allen-Cahn energy functional)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
. Then, the sequence of functionals
\text{E}_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converges to
E
, i.e. for any
u\in X_{M}

(i)

for any sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset X_{M}
converging to
u
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\text{E}(u,\Omega)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\text{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega),

(ii)

there exists a sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset X_{M}
converging to
u
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\text{E}(u,\Omega)\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\text{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega).

Theorem 1.8

(Limit stability of minimizers for the perturbed fractional Allen-Cahn energy functional)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset X_{M}
be a sequence of minimizers for
\text{E}_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega
, then there exist a convergent subsequence and a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

u_{\varepsilon}\to u:=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}\quad\text{in }L^{1}(\Omega).

(1.14)

Moreover, if
u_{\varepsilon}
minimizes
\text{E}_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega
, then

(i)

if
s\in(0,1/2)
and
u_{\varepsilon}
is weak* convergent to some
u_{0}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega^{c})
, then
u
minimizes
E
among all the functions that coincide with
u_{0}
in
\Omega^{c}
;

(ii)

if
s\in[1/2,1)
, then
u
minimizes
E
. Also, for any
K\subset\subset\Omega
, it holds

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\text{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},K)\leq c_{\star}\operatorname{Per}(E,\overline{K})+\frac{1}{2\omega_{n-1}}\int_{K}(\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}})H\,dx=c_{\star}\text{P}_{\overline{K}}^{H/c_{\star}}(E),

(1.15)

where
c_{\star}
is as in (
1.12
) and
\text{P}_{\overline{K}}^{H/c_{\star}}
is
as in (
1.7
).

We point out that if
u_{0}=\chi_{E_{0}}-\chi_{E_{0}^{c}}
a.e. in
\Omega^{c}
, for some set
E_{0}
, then Theorem
1.8
-(i) entails that, if
s\in(0,1/2)
,
u
minimizes

\text{E}(u,\Omega)=\text{K}(u,\Omega)+\text{H}(u,\Omega)=\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E,\Omega)+\frac{1}{2(1-2s)}\int_{\Omega}(\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}})Hdx=\text{P}_{s,\Omega}^{H}(E),

Namely,
u
is a solution of the non-local Massari Problem with external datum
E_{0}
(recall the alternative formulation of the non-local Massari functional in (
1.6
)).

Similarly, if
s\in[1/2,1)
, Theorem
1.8
-(ii) gives that
E
is a minimizer for
\mathscr{P}_{\Omega}^{H/c_{\star}}
, and hence it
is a set of mean curvature
H/c_{\star}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.7
is contained in Section
4
. As for Theorem
1.8
, we address the case
s\in(0,1/2)
in Section
6
, whereas Section
7
is devoted to the case
s\in[1/2,1)
.

1.4
A prescribed-mass Allen-Cahn Problem

The prescribed-mass Allen-Cahn Problem is an interesting example of non-linear partial differential equation subject to a constraint. This model comes from the gradient theory of phase transitions and has been deeply studied in the classical case in the 80s (see e.g.
[
26
,
25
,
21
]
).

We present the fractional counterpart of the convergence results discussed there. In order to do this, let
m\in[-|\Omega|,|\Omega|]
, and let us define the family

\begin{split}Z_{M,m}&:=\left\{u\in L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mbox{ s.t. }\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M\mbox{, and }\int_{\Omega}u\,dx=m\right\}\\
&\ =X_{M}\cap\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\,dx=m\right\}.\end{split}

(1.16)

We consider the restriction of the energy functionals
\text{F}_{\varepsilon}
and
F
defined in (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) to
Z_{M,m}
, and address the minimization problem

\text{find\penalty\ $u_{\varepsilon}\in Z_{M,m}$ such that }\text{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega)=\min\{\text{F}_{\varepsilon}(v,\Omega)\text{ s.t. }v\in Z_{M,m}\}.

(1.17)

Remark 1.9
.

We mention that in (
1.17
) we do not prescribe any Dirichlet boundary condition. Indeed, this would result in an over-determined problem, for which one
cannot expect the existence of a solution even in dimension one. We refer to Appendix
A
for counter-examples both in the classical and in the non-local case.

To start with, we claim that the sequence of functionals
\text{F}_{\varepsilon}|_{Z_{M,m}}

\Gamma
-converges to
\text{F}|_{Z_{M,m}}
.

Theorem 1.10

(
\Gamma
-convergence of
the fractional Allen-Cahn energy functional subject to a mass constraint)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
. For any
u\in Z_{M,m}
, the following holds:

(i)

for any sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset Z_{M,m}
converging to
u
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have that

\text{F}(u,\Omega)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\text{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega);

(ii)

there exists a sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset Z_{M,m}
converging to
u
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\text{F}(u,\Omega)\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\text{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega).

We point out that the mass constraint in (
1.16
) is closed with respect to the
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}
-convergence, hence Theorem
1.10
-(
i
) can be proved with the same argument as in
[
29
, Theorem 1.2-(i)]
.

For what concern Theorem
1.10
-(
ii
) instead, we need to verify that the recovery sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
remains in
Z_{M,m}
for every
\varepsilon
.
When
s\in(0,1/2)
, as shown in
[
29
, Theorem 1.2]
, one can take the sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}=u\}_{\varepsilon}
and obtain the desired result.

On the other hand, if
s\in[1/2,1)
, we need a suitable perturbation
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
such that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\to 0
pointwise,

\displaystyle\text{F}(u,\Omega)\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\text{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}+\varphi_{\varepsilon},\Omega),

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(u_{\varepsilon}+\varphi_{\varepsilon})dx=m,

where
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
is the sequence constructed in
[
29
, Proposition 4.6]
.
Full details of this argument are contained in Section
8
.

Now, notice that if
u_{\varepsilon}
is a solution of (
1.17
), then
u_{\varepsilon}
is also a solution of the inhomogeneous fractional Allen-Cahn equation

\varepsilon^{2s}(-\Delta)^{s}u_{\varepsilon}+W^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})+\lambda_{\varepsilon}=0,

(1.18)

where
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
is the Lagrange multiplier associated with the conservation law

\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}dx=m.

(1.19)

Here above and in the rest of the paper, the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the fractional Laplacian, defined, for all
s\in(0,1)
, as

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x):=2{\mbox{P.V.}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy:=2\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\delta}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy,

where P.V. stands for the principal value notation.

We claim that a sequence of minimizers
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
for
\text{F}_{\varepsilon}
subject to a mass constraint has uniformly bounded energy.

Theorem 1.11

(Energy bounds for the fractional Allen-Cahn energy functional subject to a mass constraint)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset Z_{M,m}
be a sequence of minimizers of
\text{F}_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega
.

Then,

\text{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega)\leq C,

(1.20)

for some positive constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
.

Moreover, we draw attention to the fact that the mass constraint reads a non-local Neumann external condition, which forces a strict relationship between how any solution of (
1.17
) behaves inside and outside
\Omega
. More precisely, the following holds true:

Theorem 1.12

(External behavior of solutions of the prescribed-mass non-local Allen-Cahn Equation)
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in Z_{M,m}
be a solution of (
1.17
).

Then,

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{\varepsilon}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy}\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega^{c}.

(1.21)

Remark 1.13
.

The right-hand side of (
1.21
) corresponds to a non-local Neumann condition, see
[
14
]
.

We also observe that, in general, a function
u
satisfying (
1.21
)
is not a signed characteristic
function, i.e. a function such that
|u|=1
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, notice that, for almost any
x\in\Omega^{c}
, we have that
u(x)=\pm 1
if and only if either
E\cap\Omega=\Omega
or
E\cap\Omega=\varnothing
.

This is an interesting structural difference with respect to the existing literature about
non-local interfaces, which mostly focused on “pure phases”
identified by global signed characteristic
functions.

We prove Theorem
1.11
in Section
9
, while a proof of Theorem
1.12
can be found in Section
10
.

We will also show that a suitable rescaling of the Lagrange multipliers sequence
\{\lambda_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
1.18
) converges (uo t a subsequence) in
\mathbb{R}
, as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

To this purpose, let us define the quantity

\kappa_{\varepsilon}:=\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{-2s},\quad&\text{if }s\in(0,1/2),\\
|\varepsilon\log\varepsilon|^{-1},\quad&\text{if }s=1/2,\\
\varepsilon^{-1},\quad&\text{if }s\in(1/2,1).\end{cases}

(1.22)

Then, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.14

(Convergence of the Lagrange multipliers for the fractional Allen-Cahn energy functional subject to a mass constraint)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence of solutions of (
1.18
) and (
1.19
)
with
m\in(-|\Omega|,|\Omega|)
,
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u:=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, for some set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Suppose that

\text{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega)\leq C,

(1.23)

for some positive constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
.

Then, the sequence
\{\mu_{\varepsilon}:=\kappa_{\varepsilon}\lambda_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}
, and hence convergent up to a subsequence.

In light of Theorem
1.14
, for every
\varepsilon>0
, we define the perturbed energy functional
\text{G}_{\varepsilon}:X_{M}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
associated with (
1.18
) as

\text{G}_{\varepsilon}(v,\Omega):=\text{F}_{\varepsilon}(v,\Omega)+\mu_{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}v\,dx.

Moreover, assume that

\mu:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mu_{\varepsilon}.

(1.24)

Then, we deduce the following convergence result.

Theorem 1.15

(Energy limits subject to mass constraints)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset Z_{M,m}
be a sequence of solutions for (
1.17
), i.e. minimizers for the energy functional
\text{F}_{\varepsilon}
subject to a mass constraint.

Then, there exist a convergent subsequence and a function
u\in Z_{M,m}
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
u=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}
in
\Omega
, for some set
E
.

Moreover,
u
is a minimizer for
F
in
\Omega
under the mass constraint and

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\text{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega)=\text{F}(u,\Omega).

(1.25)

Besides,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\text{G}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega)=\text{F}(u,\Omega)+\mu\int_{\Omega}u\,dx=:\text{G}(u,\Omega).

(1.26)

Remark 1.16
.

We stress that Theorem
1.15
establishes the convergence of the sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, rather than only in
L^{1}(\Omega)
(compare with Theorem
1.8
above). This is a direct consequence of the non-local Neumann condition (see Theorem
1.12
).

The proofs of Theorems
1.14
and
1.15
are contained in Sections
11
and
12
respectively.

1.5
Convergence for critical points of
\text{G}_{\varepsilon}
and non-local hybrid mean curvature

In
[
24
]
, the authors have proved convergence results for critical points of a fractional Allen-Cahn equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions and
s\in(0,1/2)
. Here we generalize
[
24
, Theorem 5.1]
relaxing the assumptions on the external data
\{g_{k}\}_{k}
. Indeed, as shown in Theorem
1.15
, a sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
of minimizers for
\text{F}_{\varepsilon}|_{Z_{M,m}}
converges in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to a minimizer
u
of the limit functional
\text{F}|_{Z_{M,m}}
. However, one cannot expect
u
to be a signed characteristic function in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as pointed out by
Theorem
1.12
and Remark
1.13
.

This fact leads us to introduce a new geometric object that, as far as we know, has not yet been investigated in the literature, and which coincides with the first variation of the
K
-contribution of the fractional
H^{s}
-Gagliardo semi-norm of
u
, for any Lipschitz
K\subset\subset\Omega
. Since
u=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}
in
\Omega
, for some set
E
, we refer to this new object as the
“non-local hybrid mean curvature” of the couple
(E,u|_{\Omega^{c}})
.

More precisely:

Definition 1.17

(Non-local hybrid mean curvature)
.

Let
s\in(0,1/2)
, and consider a bounded, Lipschitz, open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider a set
E
and a function
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega^{c})
, and let

u:=\begin{cases}\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}},\quad&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
g,\quad&\mbox{in }\Omega^{c}.\end{cases}

Given a vector field
X\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, if
\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t}
is the flow generated by
X
, we define

\delta\text{K}(u,\Omega)[X]:=\frac{d}{dt}\text{K}(\varphi_{t}(u),\Omega)\big|_{t=0},

where
\text{K}(u,\Omega)

has been introduced in (
1.10
).

We call
\delta\text{K}(u,\Omega)
the
non-local hybrid mean curvature
of the couple
(E,g)
.

With this, we now state a version of
[
24
, Theorem 5.1]
more suitable to our setting.

Theorem 1.18

(Convergence for solutions of inhomogeneous Allen-Cahn Equation)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Lipschitz, bounded, open set. Let
\eta>0
, and define

\Omega_{\eta}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ s.t. }{\rm{dist}}(x,\Omega)<\eta\}.

Also, let
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}
be a positive infinitesimal sequence.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
g_{k}:\Omega^{c}\to\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that
\{g_{k}\}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{0,1}(\Omega_{\eta}\setminus\Omega)
satisfy

\sup_{k}\|g_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{c})}<+\infty,

and
g_{k}\to g
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\Omega^{c})
, for some function
g
.

Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{0,1}(\Omega)
satisfy

\sup_{k}\Big(\varepsilon_{k}^{2s}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|f_{k}\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}\Big)<+\infty,\quad\text{ for some }q\in\left(\frac{n}{1-2s},n\right),

and be such that
f_{k}\rightharpoonup f
weakly in
W^{1,q}(\Omega)
, for some function
f\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
.

Let
\{u_{k}\}_{k}
be such that
u_{k}\in H^{s}(\Omega)\cap L^{p}(\Omega)
, for some
p\geq 1
. Suppose that
u_{k}
is a weak solution of

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}^{-2s}W^{\prime}(u_{k})=f_{k}\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u_{k}=g_{k}\quad&\text{in }\Omega^{c},\end{cases}

(1.27)

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

If

\sup_{k}\left(\text{F}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{k},\Omega)-\int_{\Omega}f_{k}u_{k}dx\right)<+\infty,

(1.28)

then there exist a convergent subsequence and a function
u
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
u=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}
in
\Omega
, for some set
E
, and

\delta\text{K}(u,\Omega)[X]=\kappa\int_{E\cap\Omega}\mbox{div}(fX)dx\quad\text{for every }X\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}),

for some
\kappa>0
depending only on
n
and
s
.

Remark 1.19
.

Theorem
1.18
is a refinement
of
[
24
, Theorem 5.1]
. This refinement is needed since, in our setting, one cannot expect a solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of (
1.17
) to be in
\mathcal{C}^{0,1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(and therefore the statement of
[
24
, Theorem 5.1]
, which relies
on such an assumption, would not suffice for our purposes).

Indeed, by Formula (
1.21
)
in Theorem
1.12
, we have that
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\Omega^{c})
, and

\begin{split}\partial_{j}u_{\varepsilon}(x)&=\frac{(n+2s)}{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\right)^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{\varepsilon}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy\int_{\Omega}\frac{(x_{j}-y_{j})}{|x-y|^{n+2s+2}}dy\\
&\qquad-\frac{(n+2s)x_{j}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{\varepsilon}(y)(x_{j}-y_{j})}{|x-y|^{n+2s+2}}dy,\end{split}

(1.29)

for a.e.
x\in\Omega^{c}
, for every
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

However, (
1.29
) may be not well defined when
x
approaches
\partial\Omega
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.18
, we obtain that a sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
of critical points for
\text{G}_{\varepsilon}
converges (up to a subsequence) to a critical point
u
of the limit functional
G
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,
u=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}
in
\Omega
, for some set
E
, and the couple
(E,u|_{\Omega^{c}})
has constant non-local hybrid mean curvature.

Theorem 1.20

(Hybrid mean curvature of the phase interface under a mass constraint)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence in
Z_{M,m}
such that
u_{\varepsilon}
is
a critical point for
\text{G}_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega
, i.e.
u_{\varepsilon}
is a weak solution for (
1.18
).

Then, there exist a convergent subsequence and a function
u
and a set
E
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
u=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}
in
\Omega
, and
u
is a critical point for the functional
G
in
K
, for any Lipschitz set
K\subset\subset\Omega
.

In particular, the couple
(E,u|_{\Omega^{c}})
has non-local hybrid mean curvature
\kappa\mu
in
K
, where
\kappa
is as in Theorem
1.18
, and
\mu
is as in (
1.24
).

Remark 1.21
.

Notice that, in light of (
1.21
), the function
u
coming from Theorem
1.20
is continuous across the boundary of
\Omega
. Indeed, for every
x_{0}\in E\cap\partial\Omega
,

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to x_{0}\\
x\in E\cap\Omega^{c}\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\chi_{E}(y)-\chi_{E^{c}}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}dy}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to x_{0}\\
x\in E\cap\Omega^{c}\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap E}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}-\int_{\Omega\cap E^{c}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap E}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}+\int_{\Omega\cap E^{c}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}}=1,

and for every
x_{0}\in E^{c}\cap\partial\Omega
,

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to x_{0}\\
x\in E^{c}\cap\Omega^{c}\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\chi_{E}(y)-\chi_{E^{c}}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to x_{0}\\
x\in E^{c}\cap\Omega^{c}\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap E}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}-\int_{\Omega\cap E^{c}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap E}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}+\int_{\Omega\cap E^{c}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}}=-1.

On the other hand, we infer from (
1.29
) that
u
is not
\mathcal{C}^{1}
across
\partial\Omega
.

Therefore, non-local phase interfaces subject to a mass constraint show
a boundary behavior of type “
\mathcal{C}^{0}
but not
\mathcal{C}^{1}
”.

This boundary behavior
is very different from that of
s
-minimal graphs, which can only be either discontinuous or
\mathcal{C}^{1}
at the boundary according to
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
.

The classical analog of Theorem
1.20
has been presented in
[
21
]
, where the authors proved a correspondence between the mean curvature of the limit set
E
and the limit of the Lagrange multiplier associated with the mass constraint.

However, to the best of our knowledge, no result on the convergence for critical points of the non-local Allen-Cahn Energy is yet known when
s\geq 1/2
.

The proof of Theorem
1.18
is contained in Section
13
. Then, we conclude our discussion proving Theorem
1.20
in Section
14
.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.3
-
\Gamma
-convergence of the non-local Massari Problem

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.3
. This proof is a direct consequence of the forthcoming Proposition
2.1
and
[
4
, Theorem 2]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j}
,
\{g_{j}\}_{j}\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that

\sup_{j}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sup_{j}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty.

Suppose that there exist functions
f
,
g
such that
f_{j}\to f
and
g_{j}\to g
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
j\to+\infty
.

Then,

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{K}f_{j}g_{j}dx=\int_{K}f\,g\,dx,

(2.1)

for every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set, and let
f_{j}
,
g_{j}
,
f
, and
g
be as in the assumptions. First, notice that
f
,
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Then, the claim follows immediately from the estimate

\left|\int_{K}(f_{j}g_{j}-fg)dx\right|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\|g-g_{j}\|_{L^{1}(K)}+\sup_{j}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\|f-f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(K)}.\qed

As an easy consequence of Proposition
2.1
, we obtain that if
H_{j}\to H
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, as in Theorem
1.3
, then

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega\cap F}H_{j}dx=\int_{\Omega\cap F}H\,dx,

for every set
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Thus, in light of
[
11
, Remark 4.8]
, we infer the following:

Corollary 2.2
.

For every set
F
, define the functionals

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{j}(F):=\int_{\Omega\cap F}H_{j}dx,\quad\text{for all }j\in\mathbb{N},

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(F):=\int_{\Omega\cap F}H\,dx.

Then,
\mathscr{H}_{j}
is pointwise convergent in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\mathscr{H}
in the sense of
[
11
, Definition 4.7]
.

Finally, Corollary
2.2
, together with
[
4
, Theorem 2]
and
[
11
, Theorem 6.20]
, entails Theorem
1.3
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.4
- Energy bounds and limit stability of minimizers for the non-local Massari Problem

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.4
about the convergence of minimizers of the Massari functionals
\{\mathscr{J}_{s_{j},\Omega}^{H_{j}}\}_{j}
. To do this, we follow some ideas introduced in
[
4
, Section 4]
, also taking into account the extra term related to the non-local mean curvature.

We first establish a convergence result for sequences in

Lemma 5.1

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}

If

\varepsilon\to 0^{+}

As a result,

. Thus, the convergence in (

Then, we show that minimizers of the

Lemma 5.2

Let

(6.4)

Now, we consider the function

v_{\varepsilon}:=\begin{cases}v&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{\varepsilon}&\text{ in }\Omega^{c}.\end{cases}

Then, we have

\begin{split}&\text{E}_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon},\Omega)-\text{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega)\\
=\;&\,\text{K}(v_{\varepsilon},\Omega)-\text{K}(u_{\varepsilon},\Omega)-\varepsilon^{-2s}\int_{\Omega}W(u_{\varepsilon}(x))dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(v(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(x))H_{\varepsilon}(x)dx\\
\leq\;&\frac{1}{2}\big(v(\Omega,\Omega)-u_{\varepsilon}(\Omega,\Omega)\big)+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{|v(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}-|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(v(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(x))H_{\varepsilon}(x)dx\\
=\;&\frac{1}{2}\big(v(\Omega,\Omega)-u_{\varepsilon}(\Omega,\Omega)\big)+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{|v(x)|^{2}-|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\\
&\qquad+2\int_{\Omega^{c}}\big(\Psi(y)-\psi(y)\big)u_{\varepsilon}(y)dy+2\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\big(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u(x)\big)\frac{u_{\varepsilon}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(v(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)H_{\varepsilon}(x)dx.\end{split}

Therefore, it follows from the minimality of
u_{\varepsilon}
that

\begin{split}0\leq\;&\frac{1}{2}\big(v(\Omega,\Omega)-u_{\varepsilon}(\Omega,\Omega)\big)+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{|v(x)|^{2}-|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\\

&\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(v(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)H_{\varepsilon}(x)dx.\end{split}

Taking the
\liminf
as
{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}
in the last expression, and using (
6.1
), (
6.2
),
(
6.3
) and (
6.4

\begin{split}0\leq&\,\frac{1}{2}\left(v(\Omega,\Omega)-u(\Omega,\Omega)\right)+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{|v(x)|^{2}-|u(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\\
&+2\int_{\Omega^{c}}\big(\Psi(y)-\psi(y)\big)u_{0}(y)dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(v(x)-u(x))H(x)dx\\
=&\,\frac{1}{2}\left(v(\Omega,\Omega)-u_{\star}(\Omega,\Omega)\right)+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{|v(x)|^{2}-|u_{\star}(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\\

in

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{2(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))+|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{-2(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y))\varphi(y)+|\varphi(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy,

namely

2\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y))\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\leq\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{|\varphi(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy=:c_{\varphi}.

Notice that

if and only if
\varphi\equiv 0
. Therefore, if
\varphi
does not vanish identically, we define

and we use

a test function in (

). In this way, we obtain that

Now, if

we define

Using

f_{\varepsilon}:=-\mu_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain that
f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(K)
and it satisfies

\sup_{k}\left(\varepsilon^{2s}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}+\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,n/2}(K)}\right)\leq C_{n,\Omega}\sup_{k}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty.

Hence, thanks to Theorem
1.18
and the uniqueness of the
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}
-limit, we conclude that there exists a function
u
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(up to a subsequence), and
u=\chi_{E}-\chi_{E^{c}}
in
\Omega
, for some set
E
. Moreover,

\delta\text{K}(u,\Omega)[X]=\kappa\int_{E\cap\Omega}\mbox{div}(\mu X)dx\quad\text{for every }X\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}),

for some
\kappa>0
depending only on
n
and
s
.
∎

Appendix A
Non-existence of solutions of an over-determined Allen-Cahn equation

We show that a prescribed-mass Allen-Cahn equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions is an over-determined problem, where the existence of solutions is not guaranteed, even in dimension one. To do this, we exhibit an example of such an equation with no solutions, both in the classical and in the fractional case.

Example A.1

(A counter-example in the classical case)
.

Let us consider the ordinary differential equation

\begin{cases}&u^{\prime\prime}=u^{3}-u\quad\text{in }(-1,1),\\
&u(-1)=u(1)=-1.\end{cases}

(A.1)

We look for solutions of (
A.1
) satisfying the mass constraint

\int_{-1}^{1}u\,dx=0.

(A.2)

Observe that a solution
u
of (
A.1
) is negative at
\{-1,1\}
, but has average
0
. Thus, we infer that
u
has a
strictly positive maximum in
(-1,1)
, i.e. there exists
\overline{x}\in(-1,1)
such that

\mu:=u(\overline{x})=\max_{[-1,1]}u>0.

(A.3)

Since
\overline{x}
is a maximum point for
u
, we have that

0\geq u^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})=u^{3}(\overline{x})-u(\overline{x})=\mu^{3}-\mu=\mu(\mu^{2}-1),

which entails that
\mu\in(0,1]
.

Now we observe that, for all
x\in(-1,1)
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{1}{2}|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}u^{4}(x)+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}(x)\right)=u^{\prime}(x)\Big(u^{\prime\prime}(x)-u^{3}(x)+u(x)\Big)=0.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu^{4}}{4}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}|u^{\prime}(\overline{x})|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}u^{4}(\overline{x})+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}(\overline{x})

\displaystyle\qquad=\lim_{x\to 1}\left(\frac{1}{2}|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}u^{4}(x)+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}(x)\right)\geq\lim_{x\to 1}\left(-\frac{1}{4}u^{4}(x)+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}(x)\right)=\frac{1}{4},

namely

\mu^{2}(2-\mu^{2})\geq 1.

This entails that
\mu=1
and

\lim_{x\to 1}|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}=0,

and therefore
u^{\prime}(1)=0
.

As a result, by the uniqueness of the Cauchy problem, we obtain that
u

must be identically equal to
-1
, in contradiction with (
A.3
).

Example A.2

(A counter-example in the non-local case)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and let
u_{0}
be the unique non-trivial global minimizer of the functional
J_{1}(\cdot,\Omega)
(defined in (
8.3
)). According to
[
28
, Theorem 2]
, we know that
u_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to[-1,1]
is a strictly increasing odd function. We consider the pseudo-differential problem

\begin{cases}&(-\Delta)^{s}u=u-u^{3}\quad\text{in }(-1,1),\\
&u=u_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1),\end{cases}

(A.4)

and we look for solutions of (
A.4
) such that

\int_{-1}^{1}u\,dx=\eta,

(A.5)

for some fixed
\eta>0
.

We claim that

u_{0}
is the unique solution of (
A.4
).

(A.6)

Arguing by contradiction, suppose that

there exists a continuous
function
v\not\equiv u_{0}
which solves (
A.4
).

(A.7)

If so, then

v<1
in
[-1,1]
.

(A.8)

Indeed, let
\overline{x}
be such that

v(\overline{x})=\max_{[-1,1]}v.

In particular, since
u_{0}
is increasing, we have that
\overline{x}\neq-1
. Also,
v(\overline{x})\geq u_{0}(1)>0
.

Now, if
\overline{x}=1
, then
v(\overline{x})=u_{0}(\overline{x})<1
, which implies (
A.8
).

If instead
\overline{x}\in(-1,1)
, then

0\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v(\overline{x})-v(y)}{|\overline{x}-y|^{1+2s}}dy=v(\overline{x})-v^{3}(\overline{x})=v(\overline{x})\big(1-v^{2}(\overline{x})\big),

which entails
v(\overline{x})\leq 1
. However, if
v(\overline{x})=1
, then we would have

0\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1-v(y)}{|\overline{x}-y|^{1+2s}}dy=0,

which entails that
v\equiv 1
, against the fact that
v=u_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)
. Hence, we conclude that
v(\overline{x})<1

and (
A.8
) is established.

Now, the idea is to slide
u_{0}
to the left until it lies completely above
v
, and then slide back
u_{0}
until it touches
v
(from above) for the first time. To do this, we consider horizontal translations of
u_{0}
, defined as
(u_{0})_{t}(x):=u_{0}(x+t)
, with
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In light of (
A.8
), there exists
T>0
such that
(u_{0})_{T}>v
. Then, we define

\tau:=\inf\big\{\theta\mbox{ s.t. }u_{0}(x+\theta)>v(x),\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}\big\}\geq 0.

(A.9)

We observe that, by construction,

(u_{0})_{\tau}(x)\geq v(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

(A.10)

We claim that

\tau=0.

(A.11)

For this, we argue by contradiction and we suppose that

\tau>0.

(A.12)

We point out that

there exists
x_{\tau}\in[-1,1]
such that
(u_{0})_{\tau}(x_{\tau})=v(x_{\tau})
.

(A.13)

Indeed, if not, by (
A.10
) we have that

\min_{[-1,1]}\big((u_{0})_{\tau}-v\big)>0.

Hence, by continuity and in view of (
A.12
), there exists
\tau_{\star}\in(0,\tau)
such that

(u_{0})_{\tau_{\star}}-v>0
in
[-1,1]
.

(A.14)

Then, by the monotonicity of
u_{0}
, we have that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)
,

(u_{0})_{\tau_{\star}}(x)=u_{0}(x+\tau_{\star})>u_{0}(x)=v(x).

Combining this with (
A.14
), we see that
(u_{0})_{\tau_{\star}}-v>0
in the whole of
\mathbb{R}
. This violates
the definition in (
A.9
) and proves (
A.13
).

Besides, notice that, since
u_{0}
satisfies

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{0}=u_{0}-u_{0}^{3}\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{R},

then for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
we have that

\begin{split}&(-\Delta)^{s}(u_{0})_{t}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(u_{0})_{t}(x)-(u_{0})_{t}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{u_{0}(x+t)-u_{0}(y)}{|(x+t)-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad=u_{0}(x+t)-u_{0}^{3}(x+t).\end{split}

Thus, recalling (
A.13
),

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(u_{0})_{\tau}(x_{\tau})-v(y)}{|x_{\tau}-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v(x_{\tau})-v(y)}{|x_{\tau}-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy=v(x_{\tau})-v^{3}(x_{\tau})\\
&\qquad\qquad=(u_{0})_{\tau}(x_{\tau})-(u_{0})_{\tau}^{3}(x_{\tau})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(u_{0})_{\tau}(x_{\tau})-(u_{0})_{\tau}(y)}{|x_{\tau}-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy.\end{split}

From this and (
A.10
), we obtain that

0\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(u_{0})_{\tau}(y)-v(y)}{|x_{\tau}-y|^{1+2s}}\,dy=0,

which implies that
v=(u_{0})_{\tau}
almost everywhere. However, this is against the fact that
v=u_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)
.
This contradiction proves (
A.11
).

From (
A.10
) and (
A.11
), we thereby find that
u_{0}\geq v
in
\mathbb{R}
.

In a similar manner, one can show that
u_{0}\leq v
in
\mathbb{R}
, and hence
v=u_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}
.
This is a contradiction with (
A.7
), and
therefore (
A.6
) holds true, as claimed.

Now we point out that

\int_{-1}^{1}u_{0}\,dx=0.

Thus, in light of (
A.6
), we conclude that the over-determined problem (
A.4
) subject to the mass constraint has no solutions.

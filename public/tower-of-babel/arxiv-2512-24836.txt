Symmetric mass generation as a multicritical point with enhanced symmetry
Sandip Maiti, Debasish Banerjee, Shailesh Chandrasekharan, Marina K. Marinkovic

Abstract
We explore the phase diagram of a lattice fermion model that exhibits three distinct phases: a massless fermion ( mf ) phase; a massive fermion phase with spontaneous symmetry breaking ( ssb ) induced by a fermion bilinear condensate; and a massive fermion phase with symmetric mass generation ( smg ) . Using the fermion-bag Monte Carlo method on large cubical lattices, we find evidence for traditional second-order critical points separating the first two and the latter two phases. Remarkably, these critical points appear to merge at a multicritical point with enhanced symmetry when the symmetry breaking parameter is tuned to zero, giving rise to the recently discovered direct second-order transition between the massless and symmetric massive fermion phases.



In continuum quantum field theories, relativistic fermions typically acquire masses through explicit fermion bilinear terms in the Lagrangian. When such terms are forbidden by symmetries, fermion masses may still be generated dynamically through the spontaneous breaking of those symmetries
Gross and Neveu 1974
. Beginning in the late 1980s, however, it was discovered that massive fermionic phases can emerge in certain lattice models with strictly massless fermions and
without
any spontaneous symmetry breaking, provided the interactions are sufficiently strong
Hasenfratz et al. 1990
;
Lee et al. 1990
;
Bock et al. 1990
. These symmetric massive fermion phases were long regarded as lattice artifacts of little relevance to continuum quantum field theory, since the resulting fermion masses typically reside at the lattice cutoff scale.

This perspective has evolved significantly in recent years. Evidence for continuous transitions between the massless fermion phase and the symmetric massive fermion phase has now been found in both purely fermionic theories
Slagle et al. 2015
;
Ayyar and Chandrasekharan 2015
;
Ayyar and Chandrasekharan 2016a
;
Catterall 2016
;
Ayyar and Chandrasekharan 2016b
;
He et al. 2016
and in models that include gauge interactions
Butt et al. 2021
;
Butt et al. 2025
;
Hasenfratz and Witzel 2025
. At the same time, new insights into anomaly cancellation mechanisms that permit symmetric massive fermion phases have emerged
Razamat and Tong 2021
;
Tong 2022
;
Wang and You 2022
.

Taken together, these developments suggest the presence of a new fixed point of the RG flow diagram, enabling a nontraditional mass-generation mechanism—now commonly referred to as the
smg
—in continuum quantum field theory, whereby fermions acquire masses without spontaneous symmetry breaking
You et al. 2018
. A perturbative study of the RG flows near this new
smg
-fixed point using an
\epsilon
-expansion technique has recently been proposed
Martin and Grover 2025
. The
smg
mechanism has also renewed interest in compositeness-based approaches to formulating chiral gauge theories on the lattice
Eichten and Preskill 1986
;
Wang and Wen 2018
;
Xu and Xu 2021
;
Zeng et al. 2022
;
Golterman and Shamir 2024
;
Golterman and Shamir 2025
;
Mouland et al. 2025
.

When a lattice model exhibits both a
mf
and a
smg
phase, the existence of an intermediate phase—where fermions acquire masses through the conventional mechanism of
ssb
accompanied by a fermion bilinear condensate—cannot be excluded in two or more spatial dimensions. From this perspective, a direct second-order transition between the
mf
and
smg
phases would ordinarily require fine tuning of at least two independent parameters, as originally argued in Ref.
Hasenfratz and Neuhaus 1989
. It is therefore surprising that in a simple Euclidean lattice model with two flavors of massless staggered fermions, tuning a single local four-fermion coupling
U_{I}
appeared to produce such a direct transition
Ayyar and Chandrasekharan 2015
;
Ayyar and Chandrasekharan 2016a
. The action of this model is

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\eta_{ij}\,\bigl({\bar{u}}_{i}u_{j}-{\bar{u}}_{j}u_{i}+{\bar{d}}_{i}d_{j}-{\bar{d}}_{j}d_{i}\bigr)

\displaystyle\;-\;U_{I}\sum_{i}\bigl({\bar{u}}_{i}u_{i}\,{\bar{d}}_{i}d_{i}\bigr),

(1)

where
i
and
j
label sites of a three-dimensional cubic lattice, and
\langle ij\rangle
denotes nearest-neighbor pairs. The staggered fermion fields
{\bar{u}}_{i},u_{i},{\bar{d}}_{i},
and
d_{i}
are Grassmann-valued, and the phase factors
\eta_{ij}
implement a
\pi
-flux through each plaquette of the cubic lattice. These fermions are known to describe four massless flavors of four-component Dirac fermions
Sharatchandra et al. 1981
.

In order to shed further light on this surprising second-order critical point, in this work we extend
Eq.

1
by introducing an additional interaction
U_{B}
, modifying the action to

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=S_{1}\;-\;U_{B}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\bigl({\bar{u}}_{i}u_{i}\,{\bar{u}}_{j}u_{j}+{\bar{d}}_{i}d_{i}\,{\bar{d}}_{j}d_{j}\bigr).

(2)

The additional coupling
U_{B}
allows us to explore the fate of the direct second-order transition between the
mf
and
smg
phases.

Using an extension of the fermion bag algorithm
Chandrasekharan 2010
, we determine the phase diagram of
Eq.

2
in the
U_{I}
–
U_{B}
plane and find that it contains all three expected phases: the
mf
phase at small
U_{I}
and
U_{B}
, the
smg
phase at large
U_{I}
and small
U_{B}
, and an intermediate
ssb
phase separating them, as shown in
Fig.

1
. This phase structure clearly demonstrates that the direct second-order transition at
U_{B}=0
reported in earlier studies is in fact a fine-tuned point: a multicritical point where the critical line separating the
mf
and
ssb
phases meets the critical line separating the
ssb
and
smg
phases. The transition along the former line is consistent with the three-dimensional Gross–Neveu
U(1)
universality class involving
N_{f}=4
flavors of four-component Dirac fermions, while the latter line is described by the three-dimensional bosonic XY universality class.

Our calculations are performed on some of the largest lattices studied to date. The Gross–Neveu transition is investigated on cubical lattices with linear size up to
L=56
, while for the bosonic XY transition—which is known to be particularly challenging to access in a microscopic fermionic model—we are able to simulate lattices up to
L=32
. Details of the fermion bag Monte Carlo algorithm, together with the data and scaling analysis, are provided in a companion article
Maiti et al.
.

The appearance of a finely tuned multicritical point precisely at
U_{B}=0
is again somewhat surprising. Interestingly, however, the lattice model defined in
Eq.

2
possesses an enhanced internal symmetry when
U_{B}=0
. To understand this enhancement, it is helpful to assign each lattice site a parity—either even or odd. For generic values of
U_{I}
and
U_{B}
, the action in
Eq.

2
exhibits an
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\!\times\!{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\!\times\!U_{\chi}(1)
internal symmetry. One of the
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
factors mixes
u_{i}
and
{\bar{u}}_{i}
according to

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}u_{i}\\[4.0pt]
{\bar{u}}_{i}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\rightarrow G_{u}\begin{pmatrix}u_{i}\\[4.0pt]
{\bar{u}}_{i}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}{\bar{u}}_{j}\\[4.0pt]
u_{j}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\rightarrow G_{u}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}{\bar{u}}_{j}\\[4.0pt]
u_{j}\end{pmatrix},

(3)

where
G_{u}
is a
2\times 2

{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
matrix,
i
denotes an even site, and
j
denotes an odd site. A second, independent
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
matrix
G_{d}
acts analogously on the pair
(d_{i},{\bar{d}}_{i})
. The
U_{\chi}(1)
symmetry also acts as in
Eq.

3

G_{u}
replaced by the phase
e^{i\theta}
and
G_{d}
replaced by
e^{-i\theta}
. The
ssb

U_{\chi}(1)
subgroup.

When
U_{B}=0
, the internal symmetry enlarges to
{\mathrm{SU}}(4)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}u_{i}\\[4.0pt]
{\bar{u}}_{i}\\[4.0pt]
d_{i}\\[4.0pt]
{\bar{d}}_{i}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\rightarrow V\begin{pmatrix}u_{i}\\[4.0pt]
{\bar{u}}_{i}\\[4.0pt]

{\bar{d}}_{i}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}{\bar{u}}_{j}\\[4.0pt]
u_{j}\\[4.0pt]
{\bar{d}}_{j}\\[4.0pt]
d_{j}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\rightarrow V^{*}\begin{pmatrix}{\bar{u}}_{j}\\[4.0pt]

{\bar{d}}_{j}\\[4.0pt]

(4)

{\mathrm{SU}}(4)

Eq.

The fact that the two critical lines meet precisely at
U_{B}=0

mf

smg

{\mathrm{SU}}(4)
symmetry.

One of the observables we compute using our Monte Carlo method to study the phase structure is the susceptibility involving the
u

d

\displaystyle\chi_{ud}\;=\;\frac{1}{2V}\sum_{i,j}\big\langle{\bar{u}}_{i}u_{i}\;{\bar{d}}_{j}d_{j}\big\rangle,

(5)

\langle\cdot\rangle

2

\chi_{uu}

\chi_{ud}

Based on the results presented here, we find strong support for the interpretation that the 3D Gross–Neveu and 3D–XY critical lines studied in this work merge at a multicritical point, identified as the
smg
critical point discovered in earlier studies
Ayyar and Chandrasekharan 2015
;
Ayyar and Chandrasekharan 2016a
. This scenario implies the existence of at least four distinct fixed points in the renormalization-group (RG) flow of the associated Gross–Neveu–Yukawa theories with four flavors of four-component massless Dirac fermions, one of which is the
smg
fixed point. Our conjectured RG flows are illustrated in
Fig.

4
, and closely resemble those proposed long ago in
Hasenfratz and Neuhaus 1989
. The enhanced
{\mathrm{SU}}(4)
symmetry at the
smg
fixed point plays a crucial role, as it allows the multicritical point to be accessed by tuning a single coupling rather than two, thereby facilitating its identification in earlier lattice studies in three space-time dimensions
Ayyar and Chandrasekharan 2015
;
Ayyar and Chandrasekharan 2016a
.

Recent perturbative analyses of RG flows near the
smg
fixed point have focused on
{\mathrm{SU}}(4)
-invariant theories
Martin and Grover 2025
. It should, however, be straightforward to extend these studies by including symmetry-breaking terms that reduce the symmetry to
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times U_{\chi}(1)
and allow for spontaneous breaking of the
U_{\chi}(1)
symmetry. Such an extension would enable a direct test of the RG-flow structure proposed in
Fig.

4
.

The model defined by the action
Eq.

2
can also be constructed in four space-time dimensions, where the intermediate
ssb
phase persists even at
U_{B}=0

Ayyar and Chandrasekharan 2016b
. This suggests that the
{\mathrm{SU}}(4)
-enhanced multicritical point identified in three dimensions is a dynamical feature of the renormalization-group flows specific to three space-time dimensions. Although there are preliminary indications that an
smg
fixed point may also exist in four dimensions when the model is extended to include Yukawa interactions, provided that two parameters are fine tuned
Butt et al. 2018
, it is not yet established whether its appearance is governed by a similar multicritical point with enhanced symmetry. Clarifying this issue and testing the persistence of any enhanced multicritical structure beyond three dimensions remains an important direction for future work.

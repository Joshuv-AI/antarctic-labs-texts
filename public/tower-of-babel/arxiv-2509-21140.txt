Every negative amphichiral knot is rationally slice
Alessio Di Prisa, Jaewon Lee, Oğuz Şavk

Abstract
. In 2009, Kawauchi proved that every strongly negative amphichiral knot is rationally slice. However, as shown by Hartley in 1980, there are examples of negative amphichiral knots that are not strongly negative amphichiral. In this paper, we prove that every negative amphichiral link whose amphichiral map preserves each component is rationally slice. Our proof relies on a systematic analysis of the action induced by the negative amphichiral map on the JSJ decomposition of the link exterior. Moreover, we provide sufficient conditions on such an action to deduce when a negative amphichiral knot is either isotopic to, or concordant to, a strongly negative amphichiral knot. In particular, we prove that every fibered negative amphichiral knot is strongly negative amphichiral, answering a question asked by Kim and Wu in 2016 on Miyazaki knots.



1.
Introduction

The notions of sliceness and concordance for knots were introduced in the seminal article by Fox and Milnor
[
FM66
]
. Since then, these concepts have played an important role in the development of low-dimensional topology. See the surveys
[
Liv05
,
Hom17
,
Hom23
,
Şav24
]
for a comprehensive discussion.

Certain types of knot symmetries provide examples of interesting phenomena in knot concordance. For instance, every
negative amphichiral knot
, i.e., a knot
K
invariant under an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism
f
of
S^{3}
mapping the knot
K
to itself with the opposite orientation, has concordance order at most
2
. If such a map
f
is chosen to be an involution, we say that
K
is a
strongly negative amphichiral knot
. The figure-eight knot
4_{1}
is the simplest strongly negative amphichiral knot, and hence the simplest non-slice knot of order
2
.

Cochran, based on
[
FS84
]
, observed that
4_{1}
bounds a smooth disk in a rational ball, i.e., it is
rationally slice
. Kawauchi
[
Kaw80
]
also proved that the
(2,1)
-cable of
4_{1}
is rationally slice as well. Cha
[
Cha07
]
generalized Cochran’s argument to prove that infinitely many non-concordant strongly negative amphichiral knots are rationally slice. Later, Kawauchi
[
Kaw09
]
eventually showed that every strongly negative amphichiral knot is rationally slice.

While the rational ball constructed by Kawauchi
[
Kaw09
]
a priori depends on the choice of a strongly negative amphichiral knot, Levine
[
Lev23
]
proved that every such rational ball is diffeomorphic to the same manifold
V
, called
Kawauchi manifold
.
1
1

1

The closed rational homology
4
-sphere obtained from
V
by gluing a
4
-ball is often called
Kawauchi manifold
as well. For a closed
4

, a knot in
S^{3}
is also called

On the other hand, it was previously shown by Hartley
[

]
that there exist negative amphichiral knots that are not strongly negative amphichiral. Since the existence of an involution plays a crucial role in both constructions
[
Kaw09
,
Lev23
]
, it is natural to ask whether it is strictly necessary, or alternatively, if any negative amphichiral knot is rationally slice. While Hartley’s construction produces many rationally slice knots, it is not immediate to give a general answer to this question because of the large number of possible choices of satellite patterns, which makes the situation rather arbitrary and hard to control. In this paper, we answer this question more generally, for
negative amphichiral links
, i.e., links with an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism of
S^{3}
which sends each component of the link to itself with the reversed orientation.

Theorem A
.

Every negative amphichiral link is rationally slice.

In particular, every negative amphichiral knot is rationally slice. In fact, the inductive part in the proof of Theorem
A
, combined with
[
Lev23
]
, implies that every negative amphichiral link is slice in the boundary connected sum of
n
copies of the Kawauchi manifold
V
for some
n\geq 0
.

For a link
L
, define
\Kaw(L)
as the minimal number among such
n
, or
\infty
if there is no such
n
. Then it is clear that
\Kaw(L)
is a concordance invariant. When
L
is a slice knot
K
,
\Kaw(K)=0
. Every strongly negative amphichiral knot
K
has
\Kaw(K)\leq 1

[
Kaw09
,
Lev23
]
. For any negative amphichiral knot
K
, Theorem
A
implies that
\Kaw(K)<\infty
. One can ask the following problem for which we will provide potential candidates, see Section
1.2
for more details.

Question 1
.

Does there exist a negative amphichiral knot
K
with
\Kaw(K)>1
?

Recall that every non-trivial knot is known to be either a torus knot, a hyperbolic knot, or a satellite knot by Thurston
[
Thu97
]
. Any torus knot is obviously not negative amphichiral. Kawauchi
[
Kaw79
]
proved that every hyperbolic negative amphichiral knot is strongly negative amphichiral, and hence it is rationally slice by
[
Kaw09
]
. Thus, the remaining case of negative amphichiral knots, for which it was not completely known whether they are rationally slice is given by satellite negative amphichiral knots.

More recently, Kim and Wu
[
KW18
]
proved that every
Miyazaki knot
, i.e., a fibered negative amphichiral knot with irreducible Alexander polynomial, is rationally slice. More precisely, given a satellite Miyazaki knot
P(K)
, they proved that the pattern
P
is unknotted, and the companion
K
is again a Miyazaki knot. Based on induction on
3
-genus, they conclude that such a knot is obtained by iterating satellite operations with unknotted patterns on a hyperbolic Miyazaki knot.

In order to avoid any additional assumption, such as fiberedness and irreducibility of the Alexander polynomial, we consider a generalized satellite operation, known as
splice
of links. A key ingredient in the paper is the
companionship graph
of a link, extensively studied by Budney
[
Bud06
]
, which is a labeled and partially directed tree derived from the
JSJ decomposition

[
JS78
,
Joh79
]
of the link exterior. This graph encodes the information on how the link can be obtained via iterated splicing of other links.

Moreover, the amphichiral map of an amphichiral link induces an action on its companionship graph. Analyzing this action allows us to determine how a given such a link can arise from the splicing of simpler amphichiral links. By introducing a notion of
complexity of links
defined from their companionship graphs, such analysis allows us to proceed by induction in the proof of Theorem
A
.

1.1.
Fibered negative amphichiral knots

Recall that every hyperbolic negative amphichiral knot is strongly negative amphichiral
[
Kaw79
]
. Following this idea, it is natural to ask whether it is possible to give different sufficient conditions for a negative amphichiral knot to be strongly negative amphichiral. For example, Kim and Wu
[
KW18
]
asked if every Miyazaki knot is strongly negative amphichiral.

In the proof of Theorem
A
, it turns out that if the companionship graph of a negative amphichiral link has no edges fixed by the amphichiral action, then it is strongly negative amphichiral. In particular, since the exterior of a hyperbolic knot has a single JSJ piece, the result by
[
Kaw79
]
falls in this case. Motivated by this fact, we define a notion of
totally coherent
JSJ structure with respect to the amphichiral action, which generalizes the cases where there are no fixed edges, and we prove the following.

Theorem B
.

If a negative amphichiral knot admits a totally coherent JSJ structure, then it is strongly negative amphichiral.

We briefly explain what totally coherent JSJ structure means. Suppose a satellite knot
P(K)\subset S^{3}
is negative amphichiral. Regard
S^{3}
as the union of a solid torus containing the pattern
P
and the exterior of
K
along the torus boundary. When the torus is fixed by the amphichiral map, the companion knot
K
is either negative or positive amphichiral. Roughly speaking, we call the former situation
coherent
and the latter one
incoherent
, in the general splice setting. When every such a fixed torus in the JSJ decomposition of a negative amphichiral knot exterior is coherent, we say that the knot has a
totally coherent
JSJ structure.

It turns out that every
fibered
negative amphichiral knot indeed falls under the totally coherent condition. Consequently, we obtain the following corollary, which, in particular, answers the question asked in
[
KW18
]
on Miyazaki knots.

Corollary C
.

Every fibered negative amphichiral knot is strongly negative amphichiral.

Miyazaki
[
Miy94
]
proved that the
(2n,1)
-cable of every Miyazaki knot with
n>0
is not ribbon. Kim and Wu
[
KW18
]
extended this result to all non-trivial linear combinations of such cables. While a few of them are known to be not slice
[
DKM+24
,
ACM+23
,
KPT24
,
KPT25
]
, many of them remain as a potential counterexample to the slice-ribbon conjecture. One of the difficulties in obstructing such knots being slice comes from the fact that they are
strongly
rationally slice, i.e., the inclusion from the knot complement to the slice disk complement induces an isomorphism on the free part of first homology in integer coefficients. In this context, one can ask a general question:

Question 2
.

Is the
(2n,1)
-cable of every non-slice fibered negative amphichiral knot not ribbon?

1.2.
Weakly negative amphichiral knots

While Theorem
A
immediately implies that every negative amphichiral knot is rationally concordant to any other strongly negative amphichiral knot, it is not known if such a concordance exists in
S^{3}\times[0,1]
. More precisely, one might ask:

Question 3
.

Is every negative amphichiral knot concordant to a strongly negative amphichiral knot?

If such a concordance always exists, then the answer to Question
1
is automatically negative. In this direction, we find a sufficient condition on the companionship graph for a negative amphichiral knot to be concordant to a strongly negative amphichiral knot. We call such a condition
properly incoherent
, and prove:

Theorem D
.

If a negative amphichiral knot admits a properly incoherent JSJ structure, then it is concordant to a strongly negative amphichiral knot.

We call a negative amphichiral knot
weakly negative amphichiral
if it is not strongly negative amphichiral. Since the properly incoherent condition is quite restrictive, we can construct many weakly negative amphichiral knots which do not admit properly incoherent JSJ structures in Propositions
6.1
and
6.2
. For the resulting knots in Proposition
6.1
, we can check that at least they are not slice, while the ones in Proposition
6.2
are
topologically
slice. We do not know whether they are smoothly concordant to some strongly negative amphichiral knot.

We note that if the answer to Question
3
is negative in general, then it would be difficult to find an obstruction, since most concordance properties of strongly negative amphichiral knots are also shared by negative amphichiral knots (for example, see
[
Har80a
, Theorem 3.1]
). A possible approach in this direction would be to find an example for Question
1
. We remark that the knots constructed in Propositions
6.1
and
6.2
may be potential examples for Question
1
and hence, potential counterexamples to Question
3
.

Organization

In Section
2
, we provide preliminaries on JSJ decompositions and companionship graphs, Gromov norms, and the Smith theory. We also introduce the notion of the complexity of links and prove basic properties therein. In Section
3
, we study the actions of the amphichiral maps on the companionship graphs and prove several essential properties. We also define totally coherent and properly incoherent JSJ structure of negative amphichiral knot which appear in Theorems

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give several preliminaries. We first recall the JSJ decompositions of link exteriors and its associated companionship graphs. Next, we introduce the notion of complexity of links, depending on Gromov norms of the exterior and the number of vertices in companionship graphs. We then prove several properties of complexity of links, including monotonicity and well-ordering. We finally recall several results on the Smith theory and prove a lemma relating the cardinality of the orbits of the components of a certain hyperbolic link under a rotation.

2.1.

Remark 2.14
.

When the cut edge

is directed from

to

is a subtree of
\mathbb{G}_{2}
, and hence

always equals

. On the other hand, some directed edges of

may become undirected in
\mathbb{G}_{L_{2}}

\mathbb{G}_{2}
never become directed in
\mathbb{G}_{L_{2}}
. These changes do not affect our arguments involving edge cuts in later proofs. For instance, the complexity

\mathbb{G}_{2}
. We will clarify such a subtlety whenever

is involved in our arguments.

We also often cut several edges at the same time to get a splice form of the given link
L
, and analyze each subgraph. In particular, we consider the split subgraphs after removing a certain subgraph
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
. In this case, letting
\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime})=\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\}
be the set of edges in
\mathbb{G}_{L}
with exactly one endpoint in
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
, we consider the remainders after deleting all edges in
\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime})
. Let
\mathbb{G}_{i}
be the other component
T_{i}
meets. We write our edge-cuts as:

\mathbb{G}_{L}-\mathbb{G}^{\prime}=\mathbb{G}_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\mathbb{G}_{n}.

Let
L_{i}
be the corresponding link

, and let
L^{\prime}
be

. Then we can separately analyze each smaller piece

and the link

to study the structure of

. In particular, we can obtain a splice form of

when every edge of

is directed to

as

In Lemma

, we will prove that the sublink

is unlinked so that the above splice form is valid. Before that, we first provide a proposition which concerns a more certain case without assumption on the directions of the edges

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
be a connected subgraph of the companionship graph
\mathbb{G}_{L}
of a non-split link

. Write

\mathbb{G}_{L}-\mathbb{G}^{\prime}=\mathbb{G}_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\mathbb{G}_{n}.

If

contains all boundary components of

, then each of
\mathbb{G}_{i}
is the companionship graph of a nontrivial knot.

Proof.

Since every boundary component of

is contained in
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
, there are no boundary components of the

other than the torus in
\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime})
, which joins

and
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}

is a knot. Moreover, since

[

2.2.
Complexity of links

In this subsection, we will introduce
complexity
of links. This quantity is the key ingredient for our inductive argument in the proof of Theorem
A
. In particular, we will prove its monotonicity with respect to taking a sublink, and well-ordering of the set of complexities of all links. We first recall the notion of the Gromov norm for link exteriors.

Definition 2.18
.

[
Bud06
, Definition 18]

Let
L\subset S^{3}
be a non-split link with its companionship graph
\mathbb{G}_{L}
. For the link exterior
E_{L}
of
L
, the
Gromov norm

||E_{L}||
is defined as

||E_{L}||=\sum\limits_{v\in\mathbb{G}_{L}}\operatorname{Vol}(v),

where
\operatorname{Vol}(\cdot)
is the hyperbolic volume for hyperbolic pieces and zero for Seifert fibered pieces.

Now we define the complexity of link
L
by combining the Gromov norm and the number of JSJ pieces of
E_{L}
.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
L\subset S^{3}
be a non-split link. We define the
complexity
c(L)
of
L
as the pair

c(L)=\left(||E_{L}||,\ \#\mathbb{G}_{L}\right)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{N},

where
\#\mathbb{G}_{L}
denotes the number of vertices of
\mathbb{G}_{L}
. For a split link
L
which consists of non-split sublinks
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
, we set

c(L)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c(L_{i}).

Now we consider the set of complexities of links as ordered as a subset of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{N}
, endowed with the lexicographic order. In other words, for two links
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
, their complexities are ordered as follows:

The goal of the remaining part of this subsection is to show that the set of complexities of links is well-ordered, so that we can proceed with the induction on the complexity in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be a well-ordered subset. Then the set

X=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\cdot y_{i}\ |\ m\geq 0,\ a_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\ y_{i}\in Y\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

is well-ordered.

Proof.

Let
\{x_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a decreasing sequence in
X
. We want to prove that it stabilizes, i.e., there exists some
m_{0}
such that for all
m\geq m_{0}
we have
x_{m}=x_{m_{0}}
. Observe that since the sequence is decreasing, it is sufficient to find a stabilizing subsequence.

It is clear that if
x_{m}=0
for some
m
, then it stabilizes, so we assume that
x_{m}>0
. Let
z=\min(Y)>0
and let
k=\lfloor x_{0}/z\rfloor
. Observe that every
x_{m}
is written as a sum of at most
k
elements in
Y
since
x_{m}\leq x_{0}<(k+1)z
. In other words, for all
m
,
x_{m}=y_{1,m}+\cdots+y_{k,m}
, where
y_{i,m}\in Y\cup\{0\}
.

We prove that
\{x_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
stabilizes by induction on
k\geq 1
. For the base case
k=1
,
x_{m}\in Y
for all
m
and it stabilizes since
Y
is well-ordered. For the inductive case, assume that
k\geq 2
. Then write

x_{m}=y_{1,m}+\cdots+y_{k,m}\quad\text{with}\ y_{i,m}\in Y\cup\{0\}\ \text{and}\ y_{i,m}\geq y_{i+1,m}\ \text{ for all }i.

Consider the sequence
\{y_{k,m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
. If this sequence admits a subsequence
\{y_{k,m_{j}}\}
with the property
y_{k,m_{j}}\geq y_{k,m_{j+1}}
, then it implies that at some point it stabilizes at a value of
y_{k}\in Y\cup\{0\}
. Then the sequence
\{x_{m_{j}}-y_{k}\}
is
definitively
composed by elements of
X
which can be written as a sum of at most
k-1
elements of
Y\cup\{0\}
. By induction,
\{x_{m_{j}}-y_{k}\}
stabilizes and hence so does
\{x_{m}\}
.

Suppose now that
\{y_{k,m}\}
admits no infinite decreasing subsequence. Then necessarily we can extract a strictly increasing subsequence
y_{k,m_{j}}<y_{k,m_{j+1}}
. Now we do the same with the sequence
\{y_{k-1,m}\}
:

•

if it admits an infinite decreasing subsequence, it stabilizes to
y_{k-1}\in Y\cup\{0\}
and we consider
\{x_{m_{j}}-y_{k-1}\}
,

•

otherwise, we get a strictly increasing subsequence
y_{k-1,m_{j}}<y_{k-1,m_{j+1}}
.

This process ends when we consider
\{y_{1,m}\}
. If it does not stabilize, then we extract a subsequence
y_{1,m_{j}}<y_{1,m_{j+1}}
. But then
y_{i,m}<y_{i,m+1}
for all
i
and

. Hence
x_{m}<x_{m+1}
, leading a contradiction. Therefore,
\{x_{m}\}
stabilizes, and hence

is well-ordered.
∎

Since the set of volumes of hyperbolic

Corollary 2.22
.

The set

\operatorname{Vol}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\operatorname{Vol}(M_{i}):a_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\ M_{i}\ \text{is a hyperbolic}\ 3\text{-manifold}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

is well-ordered.

If
(X,<_{X})
and
(Y,<_{Y})
are two well-ordered sets, then so is

Corollary 2.23

The set

is well-ordered. In particular,

Therefore, we can use complexity of links for an induction argument. We always use

(1)-(3) are clear from the definitions of

Let
v
be the root of the companionship graph
\mathbb{G}_{K}
. Recall from Proposition
3.10
(3) that if
v
is not coherent, then
\mathbb{G}_{K}=\varnothing
so
\mathbb{G}_{K}
is properly incoherent if and only if
\mathbb{G}_{K}
has the incoherent root by Proposition
3.10
(4). In this case, by Lemma
5.4
,
K
is concordant to the unknot, which is strongly negative amphichiral.

Hence, from now on, assume that
v
is coherent. Let
L
be the link
L(\mathbb{G}_{max})
corresponding to the maximal coherent subtree
\mathbb{G}_{max}
of
\mathbb{G}_{K}
. Let
T_{i}
be the edge joining
\mathbb{G}_{max}
and
\mathbb{G}_{i}
. Note that
L
is indexed by
\partial E_{K}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\}
. By Lemma
5.2
, the component
J_{0}=L_{\partial E_{K}}
of
L
is strongly negative amphichiral and
L_{\mathcal{T}}
is unlinked.

Let
K_{i}
be the nontrivial knot such that
\mathbb{G}_{K_{i}}=\mathbb{G}_{i}
obtained from Proposition
2.15
. Let
U_{i}
be the component
L_{T_{i}}
. Note that the knot
K
is the resulting knot of the splice form:

K=(K_{1},\dots,K_{n})\underset{K_{i}\sim U_{i}}{\bowtie}L.

Since each
\mathbb{G}_{K_{i}}=\mathbb{G}_{i}
has the incoherent root, by Lemma
5.4
, there exist concordances in
S^{3}\times I
between
K_{i}
and the unknot for any
i
. By Lemma
4.4
(1), we conclude that
K
is concordant to
U^{n}\bowtie L=J_{0}
, which is a strongly negative amphichiral knot.
∎

6.
Potential candidates for Question
1

Back to Question
3
, while we give a partial answer positively in Theorem
D
, there are negative amphichiral knots whose companionship graphs are not properly incoherent. These kinds of knots form potential candidates for Question
1
, and hence potential counterexamples to Question
3
. We say a
(\pm)
-amphichiral knot
weakly
(\pm)
-amphichiral
if it is not strongly
(\pm)
-amphichiral, respectively. We close this article by presenting several potential candidates for Question
1
, namely, weakly negative amphichiral knots that may not be slice in the Kawauchi manifold
V
.

Recall that the
Fox–Milnor condition

[
FM66
]
states that the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}(t)
of a slice knot
K
factors as
\Delta_{K}(t)=f(t)f(t^{-1})
for some polynomial
f
with integer coefficients.

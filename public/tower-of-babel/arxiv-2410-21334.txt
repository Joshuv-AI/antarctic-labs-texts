1.1.
Background

The friends-and-strangers graph was introduced by Defant and Kravitz

Definition 1.1

Given two simple

Its vertex set is the set of bijections

(\sigma,\tau)
is an edge if and only if
\sigma=\tau\circ(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})

with

and
(\sigma(i^{\prime}),\sigma(j^{\prime}))\in E(Y)

In this case, we say

and

We make the assumption that

and

As a result, the bijections of

can be thought of as permutations in the symmetric group

Also note that
\mathsf{FS}(X,Y)\cong\mathsf{FS}(Y,X)
given by the isomorphism

As a convention in this paper, we will generally mark vertices with primes to denote that they are in the

This setup for a friends-and-strangers graph has the following natural interpretation. Imagine there are

In addition, some extremal questions about friends-and-strangers graphs have been examined.

1.2.
Main Results

In this section, we will establish the main results that will be proved in this paper. We start by turning the condition of vertex connectivity into an easier condition of the existence of disjoint paths between
\sigma
and
\sigma\circ(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
for some

To do this, we find the connectivity of
\mathsf{FS}(X,K_{n}).

Theorem 1.3
.

For
n\geq 3
, if
G=\mathsf{FS}(X,K_{n})
where
X
is connected, its connectivity is equal to its minimum degree.

Through this theorem and
Proposition

3.1

Proposition 1.4
.

Let

Assume that for any
\sigma\in V(\mathsf{FS}(X,Y))
and

there exist
k
disjoint paths between

and
\sigma\circ(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}).
Then,
\mathsf{FS}(X,Y)
is
k
-connected.

We also extend Wilson’s Theorem, which we formally state in

2

Theorem 1.5
.

For
n\geq 3
, if
G=\mathsf{FS}(X,\mathsf{Star}_{n})
where

is connected, the connectivity of its connected components is equal to its minimum degree.

We use
Propositions

1.4
and
1.5
in the proofs of the two theorems below which show two sets of sufficient conditions for

to be
k
-connected.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

and

be two graphs on
n
vertices satisfying

•

\delta(X),\delta(Y)>n/2,

•

2\min\{\delta(X),\delta(Y)\}+3\max\{\delta(X),\delta(Y)\}\geq 3n+2k-4,

Then for sufficiently large

-connected.

Notice that for fixed

Moreover, we know there do not exist

We also have the following result which eliminates the

Let

-connectivity
[

2.2.1.
Challenges to Connectivity

There are two major obstructions to connectivity in

The first one is when there exists a cut vertex in both

Proposition 2.2

(
[
4
, Proposition 2.6]
)
.

Let

and

be connected graphs, each on

vertices. Suppose

Let

and

be graphs on

[
4
]
,because

In addition, if

For a permutation

Proposition 2.3

(
[
4

)

Let

If the permutations

Note that both parities can be achieved as mentioned in the last paragraph of

Notice that for

Let

2.3.
Menger’s Theorem

Menger’s theorem is a connection between two different ideas of connectivity.

Theorem 2.11

(

]
)

Let

The minimum number of vertices

and

As a corollary, we see that when

has more than

is
k

disjoint paths between any two vertices of

.
When

Note that when we are discussing

As a result notice that

\mathsf{FS}(X,Y)

We use this fact implicitly throughout the paper.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
n\geq 3
, if
G=\mathsf{FS}(X,K_{n})
where
X
is connected, its connectivity is equal to its minimum degree.

Proof.

We will use the fact that
\delta(G)=\lvert E(X)\rvert
implicitly throughout the proof. We utilize the idea of atomic parts. For this argument, we will consider the atomic parts of
G.
Recall that for any atomic part
A,
we have

is a cutset in
\mathcal{C}.
Thus, if we show that
|N(V(A))|\geq\delta(G)
for any atomic part, this proves
G
is
\delta(G)
-connected because the connectivity of
G
equals
\lvert N(V(A))\rvert\geq\delta(G)
and is less than or equal to
\delta(G)
.
The general idea will be to find a structure for these atomic parts and then show that any one atomic part is adjacent to vertices of many other atomic parts. Because of
Lemma

2.15
, we then have a good lower bound on the size of the neighborhood of the atoms which we will show is larger than
\delta(G).

Notice that as a result of vertex transitivity, for any two vertices
\sigma
and
\tau
in
V(G),
there exists an automorphism
f
on
G
where
f(\sigma)=\tau,
i.e.,
f(\pi)=\tau\circ\sigma^{-1}\circ\pi.
As a result, we can group all vertices in
V(G)
into different atomic parts

for some integer

These atomic parts must be disjoint by

2.14

V(G)
. Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be two of these atomic parts.

Fix a vertex
\sigma
in
V(A).
Consider the set of transpositions
R
given by
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in E(X)
such that
\sigma\circ(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})

Let us show that for any
\tau\in V(A^{\prime}),
the vertex
\tau^{\prime}=\tau\circ(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
where
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in R
is in
V(A^{\prime})
as well for any atomic part
A^{\prime}
.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that
\tau^{\prime}
is not in
V(A^{\prime}).
Then, notice that we can create a new atomic part including both
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
because of the
f
on
G
mapping
\sigma
to
\tau.
As a result,
f(\sigma)=\tau
and
f(\sigma\circ(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}))=\tau^{\prime},
and because there exists an atomic part containing
\sigma
and
\sigma\circ(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}),
through the automorphism, there must exist an atom containing
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}.

But that would mean that there exist two atomic parts which are not disjoint which is a contradiction.
It also follows that if
\sigma\circ(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
is not a vertex in
A
for some
(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\in E(X)
then for
\tau\in V(A^{\prime})

\tau\circ(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
is not a vertex in
A^{\prime}
either.

Let
X^{\prime}
be the subgraph of

where
V(X^{\prime})=V(X)
and

Notice that
A
is isomorphic to the connected components of
\mathsf{FS}(X^{\prime},K_{n})
since for every vertex,
\sigma\in V(\mathsf{FS}(X^{\prime},K_{n})),
the swaps given by
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in R
satisfy
\sigma\circ(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
is in the same connected component of
\sigma
similar to how the same swap of a vertex in

takes the vertex to another vertex in
A.
Therefore, each connected component is isomorphic to

Let
T
be the set of edges in

which have endpoints in different connected components of
X^{\prime}.
Note that
T
and
R
are disjoint.

Let us first determine
\lvert V(A)\rvert.
Because it is the size of a connected component in
\mathsf{FS}(X^{\prime},K_{n}),
we just need to determine all permutations that can be reached from a permutation,
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}.
Let the connected components of
X^{\prime}
be labeled
X_{1}^{\prime},X_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{m}^{\prime}.
For each connected component, we can permute the people standing on those vertices in any way. Notice that people can’t change connected components since there are no edges between components.
Thus, the size of each component is

\lvert X_{1}^{\prime}\rvert!\lvert X_{2}^{\prime}\rvert!\cdots\lvert X_{m}^{\prime}\rvert!=\lvert V(A)\rvert.

Let us now find a lower bound on the number of atomic parts that contain a vertex adjacent to a
\sigma
in
A.

Assume that we performed the swap
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
to move from
\sigma
to
\tau
and we performed the swap
(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
to move from
\sigma
to
\rho
where
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}),(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\in T
where
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
and
(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
are different swaps.
Let us show that
\tau
and
\rho
are in different atomic parts. Assume that they were in the same atomic part; then there would exist a series of swaps,
(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})\circ(x_{3}^{\prime},x_{4}^{\prime})\circ\cdots\circ(x_{c-1}^{\prime},x_{c}^{\prime})
, where
(x_{2i-1}^{\prime},x_{2i}^{\prime})\in R.

Therefore,

(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\circ(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})\circ(x_{3}^{\prime},x_{4}^{\prime})\circ\cdots\circ(x_{c-1}^{\prime},x_{c}^{\prime})\circ(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})

is the identity permutation.

Consider the idea of people standing on vertices in

If we swap
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}),
then person
\sigma(i^{\prime})
who was originally in some connected component

moves a different connected component

\sigma(j^{\prime})

The swaps of the form

are only swaps within connected components. In order to return the people back to their original locations, the swap

must be returning person

back to connected component

and person
\sigma(j^{\prime})
to connected component

but because it returns then to their exact locations that would mean that

(k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}),
then
\tau
and

must be in different atomic parts.

\lvert T\lvert.
Moreover, because if a vertex in

We will start by bounding
\delta(G).
The maximum number of edges of
X
within components occurs when all components are cliques.
The number of edges between connected components of
X
is
\lvert T\lvert.
We know that the
\delta(G)
is equal to the number of edges in
X.
Let
r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{m^{\prime}}
be a list of sizes of all components of
X^{\prime}

We have

\delta(G)\leq\lvert T\rvert+\frac{r_{1}(r_{1}-1)}{2}+\frac{r_{2}(r_{2}-1)}{2}+\ldots+\frac{r_{m^{\prime}}(r_{m^{\prime}}-1)}{2}.

Thus, because

\lvert T\lvert(r_{1}!r_{2}!\cdots r_{m^{\prime}}!-1)\geq r_{1}!r_{2}!\cdots r_{m^{\prime}}!-1,

as
\lvert T\lvert\geq 1
since there must be multiple connected components in

in order to ensure the atoms of

aren’t the whole graphs themselves, it suffices to show

r_{1}!r_{2}!\cdots r_{m^{\prime}}!-1\geq\frac{r_{1}(r_{1}-1)}{2}+\frac{r_{2}(r_{2}-1)}{2}+\ldots+\frac{r_{m^{\prime}}(r_{m^{\prime}}-1)}{2}.

We will prove this through induction on
r_{1}+\ldots+r_{m^{\prime}}
for fixed
m^{\prime}
where the base case is
r_{1}=\cdots=r_{m^{\prime}}=2.

In the base case,

2^{m^{\prime}}-1\geq m^{\prime},

which is true for all nonnegative integers

For the inductive case assume that we have shown this for

through

and without loss of generality we increment

by

\displaystyle(r_{1}+1)!r_{2}!\cdots r_{m^{\prime}}!-1

\displaystyle\geq(r_{1}!r_{2}!\cdots r_{m^{\prime}}!-1)(r_{1}+1),

\displaystyle\geq(r_{1}+1)\left(\frac{r_{1}(r_{1}-1)}{2}+\frac{r_{2}(r_{2}-1)}{2}+\ldots+\frac{r_{m^{\prime}}(r_{m^{\prime}}-1)}{2}\right),

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r_{1}+1}{r_{1}-1}\left(\frac{r_{1}(r_{1}-1)}{2}\right)+\frac{r_{2}(r_{2}-1)}{2}+\ldots+\frac{r_{m^{\prime}}(r_{m^{\prime}}-1)}{2},

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r_{1}(r_{1}+1)}{2}+\frac{r_{2}(r_{2}-1)}{2}+\ldots+\frac{r_{m^{\prime}}(r_{m^{\prime}}-1)}{2},

finishing the proof.

We start by considering starcle graphs. Finding the connectivity of the friends-and-strangers graph formed by a starcle and a star graph is important as we will show that

Intuitively these graphs look like a cycle combined with a star where the center of the star lies at one of the vertices of the cycle. Let us prove the following lemma on the existence of disjoint paths in

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

Let

Let

Assume that there exists

Then there exist

disjoint paths between any two distinct vertices

and

Proof.

Define

First, note that

is connected by

Let
\sigma
and

\sigma
and
\rho

Notice that

and

Assume without loss of generality that

Then
\tau_{1}=\tau_{\ell}\circ\pi_{q-1}\circ\pi_{q-2}\circ\cdots\circ\pi_{p}

as elements in
\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}
by adding

as a fixed point. Therefore, the path from

to

can be broken up into a composition of permutations of the form

and because

and

are completely arbitrary aside from the condition that

Next, note that the set of permutations

Let

and edge set such that there exists an edge between

\tau^{-1}\circ\tau^{\prime}

Because Menger’s theorem holds for directed graphs (e.g. see Göring

to

Let

such that there exists a directed edge in

from a vertex in

be the set of vertices

such that there exists a directed edge in

from
\tau
to a vertex in

Showing that all vertex cuts have at least

with

we have

If there is a cut of

and any other connected component is not in its neighborhood, and if some set

Let us show that

If there were no directed edges between two vertices of

because for any vertex
\tau

\lvert N_{\operatorname{in}}(\tau)\rvert=\lvert N_{\operatorname{out}}(\tau)\rvert=k,

For every additional directed edge between two vertices of

Therefore with every additional directed edge, both

We now consider when

Start by performing any permutation

There must be some vertex

If there is a vertex

Dynamical Geometry of Principal Bundle Constrained Systems: Compatible Pairs, Contact Degeneration, and Adapted Metrics
Dongzhe Zheng

Abstract
We study invariant codimension-one constraints on principal bundles through compatible pairs: a constraint distribution and a nonzero coadjoint field parallel for a principal connection. Pairing the field with the connection gives an invariant one-form whose Levi form separates a horizontal curvature contribution from a vertical coadjoint-orbit contribution. This decomposition yields criteria for integrability, contactness, and characteristic reduction; holonomy and stabilizer reductions describe global existence. For evolving compatible pairs, we identify the mixed-curvature obstruction to compatibility and prove that connection transport preserves the Levi geometry. For initially contact data over a closed base of dimension 2 ​ n 2n , we establish matching bounds for the quadratic H n + 1 H^{n+1} cost of contact degeneration: making the paired curvature vanish on a Darboux ball of radius r r in time T T costs an amount comparable to [ T ​ log ⁡ ( R ∗ / r ) ] − 1 [T\log(R_{*}/r)]^{-1} . The constructed paths remain contact before T T , preserve the curvature class, and force the L ∞ L^{\infty} norm of every transporting velocity gradient to grow at least as 1 / [ 2 ​ ( T − t ) ] 1/[2(T-t)] on the collapsing region in Darboux coordinates. On a closed three-manifold, a contact form preserved by a locally free circle action admits an invariant adapted metric with any prescribed positive curl eigenvalue and unit circle generator. We parametrize all such metrics and prove that their space is contractible. Along the circle-bundle degeneration paths, every continuous tensor limit of normalized adapted metrics is degenerate above the collapsing region.

Introduction

A cooriented codimension-one velocity constraint on a principal bundle
P\to M
is
specified by a nowhere-zero one-form
\alpha
: an admissible velocity

V\in T_{p}P
satisfies
\alpha_{p}(V)=0
. We study invariant constraints
obtained by pairing a principal connection
A
with a nonzero equivariant
coadjoint field
\lambda
. The distribution
\xi
and the field
\lambda

form a compatible pair for
A
when

\xi=\ker\alpha,\qquad\alpha=\langle\lambda,A\rangle,\qquad\mathrm{d}\lambda+\ad_{A}^{*}\lambda=0.

For a compact structure group, an invariant inner product
B
identifies

\lambda=B(u,\cdot)
with a parallel adjoint field
u
, and

\alpha=B(u,A)
.

The differential of
\alpha
records both the curvature on the base and
the Lie brackets along the fibers. These contributions determine the
integrability, contactness, and characteristic leaves of the constraint
distribution. For evolving connections, mixed spacetime curvature controls
the persistence of parallelism, while a covariant transport equation carries
the constraint by bundle automorphisms. We analyze this constraint–curvature
coupling, the cost of contact degeneration, and the normalization of adapted
metrics for invariant contact forms in dimension three.

The first main result concerns a fixed principal bundle over a closed

2n
-manifold and a fixed nonzero adjoint field
u
. Starting from a
connection
A_{0}
for which
\mathrm{d}_{A_{0}}u=0
and
B(u,A_{0})
is contact, we
consider smooth paths
A_{t}
satisfying
\mathrm{d}_{A_{t}}u=0
. Write

\pi^{*}\beta_{t}=B(u,F_{A_{t}})
. For a fixed Darboux ball
B_{R_{*}}
and

0<r<R_{*}
, impose the terminal condition
\beta_{T}|_{B_{r}}=0
and require

B(u,A_{t})
to remain contact for
t<T
. The infimum of

\int_{0}^{T}\|\dot{A}_{t}\|_{H^{n+1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t
over these paths satisfies

\mathcal{C}_{T}(r)\asymp\frac{1}{T\log(R_{*}/r)}\qquad(r\to 0).

The constants are independent of
r
and

. Theorem
3.2

gives the construction and the matching lower bound. It also identifies

as the threshold for openness of the contact condition among
smooth connections preserving

. Proposition
3.3
shows

.

The second main result fixes a contact form

2
The Levi form and its rank

All manifolds are smooth, finite dimensional, Hausdorff, and second countable.
Let
M
be connected, let
G
be a compact connected Lie group of positive
dimension, and let
\pi:P\to M
be a right principal
G
-bundle. Fix a positive
definite
\operatorname{Ad}
-invariant inner product
B
on
\mathfrak{g}
. For
a\in\mathfrak{g}
, write

a^{\#}_{p}=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{0}p\exp(ta)
.
For a smooth function
\zeta:P\to\mathfrak{g}
, define

\zeta^{\#}_{p}=(\zeta(p))^{\#}_{p}
; equivariance of
\zeta
makes this vertical
vector field
G
-invariant.

A connection
A\in\Omega^{1}(P;\mathfrak{g})
satisfies

A(a^{\#})=a
and
R_{g}^{*}A=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}A
. Its curvature and covariant derivative
are

F_{A}=\mathrm{d}A+\tfrac{1}{2}[A\wedge A],\qquad\mathrm{d}_{A}u=\mathrm{d}u+[A,u],\qquad\tfrac{1}{2}[A\wedge A](U,V)=[A(U),A(V)].

(2.1)

An adjoint section is represented by an equivariant function

u:P\to\mathfrak{g}
,
u(pg)=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}u(p)
. A pair
(A,u)
is called parallel when

\mathrm{d}_{A}u=0,\qquad u\not\equiv 0.

(2.2)

The norm of
u
is a positive constant: indeed,

\mathrm{d}B(u,u)=-2B([A,u],u)=0
. Thus

\alpha=B(u,A),\qquad\xi=\ker\alpha

(2.3)

are respectively a nowhere-zero invariant one-form and a smooth cooriented
hyperplane distribution. On a
(2m+1)
-manifold, a one-form is contact
when
\alpha\wedge(\mathrm{d}\alpha)^{m}
is nowhere zero; equivalently,

\mathrm{d}\alpha
is nondegenerate on
\ker\alpha
.
A closed two-form is symplectic when it is nondegenerate. The corresponding coadjoint field is

\lambda=B(u,\cdot)
; with

(\ad_{a}^{*}\lambda)(b)=-\lambda([a,b])
, equation (
2.2
) is

\mathrm{d}\lambda+\ad_{A}^{*}\lambda=0
.

For a fixed connection
A
, a pair
(\xi,\lambda)
consisting of an
invariant hyperplane distribution and a nowhere-zero equivariant
coadjoint field is called
compatible with
A
when

\xi=\ker\langle\lambda,A\rangle,\qquad\mathrm{d}\lambda+\ad_{A}^{*}\lambda=0.

The inner product
B
identifies these data with the parallel pairs

(A,u)
in (
2.2
). Thus (
2.3
) specifies the
constraint distribution associated with a compatible pair.

The scalar two-form
B(u,F_{A})
is horizontal and invariant, so it descends to a
unique
\beta\in\Omega^{2}(M)
:

\pi^{*}\beta=B(u,F_{A}).

(2.4)

For
p\in P
, put
x=\pi(p)
,
\mu=u(p)
, and

\mathfrak{g}_{\mu}=\{a\in\mathfrak{g}:[a,\mu]=0\}
. The adjoint orbit is

\mathcal{O}_{\mu}=\operatorname{Ad}_{G}\mu
.

We use
\mathrm{d}\alpha|_{\xi}
as a scalar representative of the Levi form.
With the quotient
TP/\xi\cong\mathbb{R}
induced by
\alpha
, the bracket convention
is
\alpha([U,V])=-\mathrm{d}\alpha(U,V)
for sections
U,V
of
\xi
.
Its characteristic space is

\operatorname{Char}(\xi)_{p}=\{U\in\xi_{p}:\mathrm{d}\alpha_{p}(U,V)=0\text{ for all }V\in\xi_{p}\}.

Theorem 2.1

(Levi decomposition)
.

Under the preceding hypotheses,
\mathrm{d}\beta=0
, and

\mathrm{d}\alpha=B(u,F_{A})+\tfrac{1}{2}B(u,[A\wedge A]).

(2.5)

Writing
\mathcal{H}=\ker A
, there is a direct sum

\xi_{p}=\mathcal{H}_{p}\oplus(\mu^{\perp})^{\#}_{p}.

(2.6)

For
v,w\in T_{x}M
and
a,b\in\mu^{\perp}
,

\mathrm{d}\alpha_{p}(v^{H}+a^{\#},w^{H}+b^{\#})=\beta_{x}(v,w)+B(\mu,[a,b]).

(2.7)

In particular,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Char}(\xi)_{p}

\displaystyle=(\ker\beta_{x})^{H}\oplus(\mathfrak{g}_{\mu}\cap\mu^{\perp})^{\#}_{p},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{d}\alpha|_{\xi_{p}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rank}\beta_{x}+\dim\mathcal{O}_{\mu}.

(2.9)

Proof.

The Bianchi identity and parallelism give

\mathrm{d}B(u,F_{A})=B(\mathrm{d}_{A}u\wedge F_{A})+B(u,\mathrm{d}_{A}F_{A})=0
.
Pullback by a surjective submersion is injective on forms, so
\mathrm{d}\beta=0
.

To compute
\mathrm{d}\alpha
, write
a=A(U)
and
b=A(V)
. Differentiating the
pairing and using
\mathrm{d}u=-[A,u]
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\alpha(U,V)

\displaystyle=-B([a,u],b)+B([b,u],a)+B(u,\mathrm{d}A(U,V))

\displaystyle=2B(u,[a,b])+B(u,\mathrm{d}A(U,V))

\displaystyle=B(u,F_{A}(U,V))+B(u,[a,b]).

This proves (
2.5
). Decomposition (
2.6
) follows from

T_{p}P=\mathcal{H}_{p}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\#}_{p}
and
\alpha(a^{\#})=B(\mu,a)
.
Horizontality of
F_{A}
gives (
2.7
).

For the vertical block, invariance gives

B(\mu,[a,b])=B([\mu,a],b)
. If
a\in\mu^{\perp}
lies in its radical,

[\mu,a]
is orthogonal to
\mu^{\perp}
, hence lies in
\mathbb{R}\mu
.
It is also orthogonal to
\mu
, and therefore vanishes. Conversely, any

a\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mu}\cap\mu^{\perp}
lies in the radical. Since

\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mu}
and
B(\mu,\mu)>0
, this radical has dimension

\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\mu}-1
. The vertical rank is consequently

(\dim\mathfrak{g}-1)-(\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\mu}-1)=\dim\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
.
Adding the ranks of the two blocks proves the result.
∎

Corollary 2.2

(Three geometric regimes)
.

The distribution
\xi
has the following properties.

1.

It is Frobenius integrable if and only if
u(p)\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
and

\beta=0
.

2.

At
p
, its sections and their brackets span
T_{p}P
if and only if

u(p)\notin\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
or
\beta_{\pi(p)}\neq 0
.

3.

The form
\alpha
is contact if and only if
\beta
is symplectic and

\dim\mathfrak{g}_{u(p)}=1
.

Proof.

Frobenius integrability is equivalent to vanishing of the Levi form. Formula
(
2.9
) then gives
\beta=0
and
\dim\mathcal{O}_{\mu}=0
; since
G

is connected, the latter is
\mu\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
. A hyperplane distribution
generates in two steps at a point exactly when its Levi form is nonzero there:
one nonzero bracket class spans the one-dimensional quotient. Finally,
contactness is equivalent to nondegeneracy of
\mathrm{d}\alpha|_{\xi}
, and
(
2.8
) gives the two stated conditions. Horizontal parallel transport
and equivariance put all values of
u
in a single adjoint orbit, so their
centrality and centralizer dimension are constant.
∎

For a compact semisimple Lie algebra, every nonzero
u
is noncentral.
Thus every pair satisfying (
2.2
) produces a distribution that
generates in two steps everywhere, including when
F_{A}=0
.
Contactness is stronger. Since the centralizer of any element of a compact
Lie algebra contains a maximal torus, it has dimension at least
\operatorname{rank}G
.
Thus a parallel pair with
\alpha
contact requires
\operatorname{rank}G=1
.

Also,
\dim(\xi_{p}\cap\ker\mathrm{d}\pi)=\dim G-1
. Consequently
\xi
is a
horizontal distribution only when
\dim G=1
, in which case
\xi=\ker A
.
An Atiyah-sequence splitting specifies
\ker A
, whereas (
2.3
)
usually specifies a larger distribution.

The logarithmic cost of contact degeneration

Let
M
be closed and connected with
\dim M=2n\geq 2
.
Fix a parallel pair
(A_{0},u)
such that
\alpha_{0}=B(u,A_{0})
is contact,
and write
c_{u}=B(u,u)>0
and
\pi^{*}\beta_{0}=B(u,F_{A_{0}})
.
Corollary
2.2
gives a symplectic form
\beta_{0}
and

\mathfrak{g}_{u(p)}=\mathbb{R}u(p)
at every
p\in P
.

Fix a smooth Riemannian metric
g_{M}
on
M
. All Sobolev norms are
nonhomogeneous
L^{2}
norms on tensor fields on the base, defined using

g_{M}
, its Levi–Civita connection,
B
, and the reference connection

A_{0}
on
\operatorname{ad}P
. Connection differences and curvature
differences are respectively adjoint-valued one-forms and two-forms on
M
.
They induce relative topologies on the space of smooth connections.
The affine space of connections preserving
u
is

\mathcal{A}_{u}=\{A\in\mathcal{A}(P):\mathrm{d}_{A}u=0\},

where
\mathcal{A}(P)
denotes the space of smooth principal connections.
We regard a path as the connection
\mathbb{A}=A_{t}
on
P\times[0,T]
,
with zero temporal component. Its mixed curvature is
E_{t}=\dot{A}_{t}
.
The deformation cost below uses this fixed gauge and the fixed background
norms. General temporal components are treated in Section
7
.

Lemma 3.1

(Connections preserving the adjoint field)
.

The map

b\longmapsto A_{b}=A_{0}+\frac{u}{c_{u}}\,\pi^{*}b

(3.1)

is an affine bijection from
\Omega^{1}(M)
onto
\mathcal{A}_{u}
. Moreover,

F_{A_{b}}=F_{A_{0}}+\frac{u}{c_{u}}\pi^{*}(\mathrm{d}b),\qquad\alpha_{b}=\alpha_{0}+\pi^{*}b,\qquad\beta_{b}=\beta_{0}+\mathrm{d}b,

(3.2)

and
F_{A_{b}}=(u/c_{u})\pi^{*}\beta_{b}
.

Proof.

The added term in (
3.1
) is horizontal and equivariant,
and it commutes with
u
, so it is a connection difference preserving

\mathrm{d}_{A}u=0
. Conversely, if
A^{\prime}=A_{0}+a
lies in
\mathcal{A}_{u}
, then

[a,u]=0
. The one-dimensional centralizer condition gives

a=(u/c_{u})\pi^{*}b
, with
\pi^{*}b=B(u,a)
. The curvature formula follows from

\mathrm{d}_{A_{0}}u=0
and the vanishing self-bracket of the line-valued perturbation.
Finally,
\mathrm{d}_{A}^{2}u=[F_{A},u]=0
puts every curvature value in
\mathbb{R}u
, proving
the last assertion.
∎

Choose a Darboux chart containing the closure of a coordinate ball
B_{R_{*}}
, with

\beta_{0}=\omega_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}x_{j}\wedge\mathrm{d}y_{j},\qquad\lambda_{\mathrm{std}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}\mathrm{d}y_{j}-y_{j}\mathrm{d}x_{j}),\qquad\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\mathrm{std}}=\omega_{0}.

(3.3)

Here
\rho=(\sum_{j}(x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2}))^{1/2}
is the Euclidean radial coordinate
and
B_{r}=\{\rho<r\}
. The chart is taken slightly larger than

\overline{B_{R_{*}}}
. For
T>0
and

0<r<R_{*}
, let
\mathcal{P}_{T}(r)
be the class of smooth paths

A_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{u}
,
0\leq t\leq T
, which start at
A_{0}
, have

B(u,A_{t})
contact for
t<T
, and satisfy
\beta_{T}|_{B_{r}}=0
. Define

\mathcal{C}_{T}(r)=\inf_{A_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{P}_{T}(r)}\int_{0}^{T}\|\dot{A}_{t}\|_{H^{n+1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.4)

The terminal condition removes the horizontal contribution to the
Levi form throughout the prescribed ball.

Theorem 3.2

(Critical cost and sharp Sobolev threshold)
.

There exist constants
0<r_{0}<R_{*}/e
and
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, depending on
(A_{0},u)
, the chart,
and norms, such that for
0<r<r_{0}
and every
T>0
,

\frac{c_{1}}{T\log(R_{*}/r)}\leq\mathcal{C}_{T}(r)\leq\frac{c_{2}}{T\log(R_{*}/r)}.

(3.5)

The upper bound is realized up to its constant by paths with
A_{t}=A_{0}

over
M\setminus B_{R_{*}}
.
The lower bound holds for every smooth path in
\mathcal{A}_{u}
with
the stated endpoints, including paths that lose contactness before
T
.

The paths realizing the upper bound also satisfy, for a constant
C

depending only on the same fixed data,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\|A_{t}-A_{0}\|_{H^{n+1}}^{2}+\|F_{A_{t}}-F_{A_{0}}\|_{H^{n}}^{2}\right)\leq\frac{C}{\log(R_{*}/r)}.

(3.6)

Consequently, the locus of connections
A\in\mathcal{A}_{u}
for which

B(u,A)
is contact is not open in the relative
H^{s}
topology for any

s\leq n+1
, including the critical exponent. It is open for every

s>n+1
.

Proof.

Construction.

Choose a smooth nondecreasing function
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
such that

\chi=0
on
(-\infty,0]
,
\chi=1
on
[1,\infty)
, and
0<\chi<1
on
(0,1)
.
For
L\geq 1
and
\rho>0
, set

r_{L}=R_{*}e^{-L},\qquad\chi_{L}(\rho)=\chi\!\left(\frac{\log(R_{*}/\rho)}{L}\right).

(3.7)

Extend
\chi_{L}
by one at the origin. It is smooth, nonincreasing, equals
one for
\rho\leq r_{L}
, and vanishes for
\rho\geq R_{*}
. Put

b_{L}=\chi_{L}\lambda_{\mathrm{std}}

on the chart and extend it by zero. The flatness of
\chi
at its endpoints
makes this a smooth global one-form. Define

A_{L,t}=A_{0}-\frac{t}{T}\frac{u}{c_{u}}\pi^{*}b_{L},\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.

(3.8)

Lemma
3.1
places the entire path in
\mathcal{A}_{u}
.
In the chart its paired curvature is

\beta_{L,t}=\mathrm{d}(\psi_{L,t}\lambda_{\mathrm{std}}),\qquad\psi_{L,t}=1-\frac{t}{T}\chi_{L}.

Expansion of the exterior power gives

\beta_{L,t}^{\,n}=\psi_{L,t}^{\,n-1}\left(\psi_{L,t}+\frac{\rho}{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi_{L,t}\right)\omega_{0}^{n}.

(3.9)

Indeed,

\lambda_{\mathrm{std}}=\iota_{Z_{0}}\omega_{0}
, where

Z_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}(x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}+y_{j}\partial_{y_{j}})
, and

\mathrm{d}\psi(Z_{0})=(\rho/2)\psi^{\prime}
. For

and

. Thus

is symplectic and

gives contactness. At

The critical estimate.

For
r_{L}<\rho<R_{*}
and every integer

Here

For orders zero and one, use the second bound in
(

) and the finiteness of

Equivalence of coordinate and intrinsic norms on this fixed chart yields

(3.13)

Multiplication by the fixed smooth section

is bounded in these norms.
Equations (

) and
(

) prove
(

), and

\int_{0}^{T}\|\dot{A}_{L,t}\|_{H^{n+1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{C}{TL}.

). Since the terminal connections are noncontact

, and hence in every lower

\|w\|_{H^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\leq C\|\delta\beta\|_{H^{n}(M)}.

(3.14)

Take

The region

\|\varphi_{r}\|_{H^{-n}}^{2}\leq C\log(R_{*}/r).

(3.15)

Sobolev duality and (

\|\delta\beta\|_{H^{n}(M)}^{2}\geq\frac{c}{\log(R_{*}/r)}.

(3.16)

By Lemma

\pi^{*}\delta\beta=-\mathrm{d}\!\left(\int_{0}^{T}B(u,\dot{A}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\right).

The expression in parentheses is basic. Boundedness of this fixed

\|\delta\beta\|_{H^{n}}^{2}\leq CT\int_{0}^{T}\|\dot{A}_{t}\|_{H^{n+1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Combining this with (
3.16

bound. The argument applies to every path with the prescribed terminal curvature.

Supercritical openness.

Let
s>n+1

4
Existence, normalization, and holonomy

For a fixed connection, existence is governed by its full holonomy group,
including holonomy around noncontractible loops.

Proposition 4.1

(Holonomy criterion)
.

Fix
p_{0}\in P
. Evaluation at
p_{0}
is a linear isomorphism

\{u\in\Gamma(\operatorname{ad}P):\mathrm{d}_{A}u=0\}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\operatorname{Hol}_{p_{0}}(A)}=\{\mu:\operatorname{Ad}_{h}\mu=\mu\text{ for every }h\in\operatorname{Hol}_{p_{0}}(A)\}.

(4.1)

Thus a prescribed value
u(p_{0})
determines at most one parallel field.
If
u
and
v
are nonzero parallel fields for the same
A
and

\ker B(u,A)=\ker B(v,A)
, then
v=cu
for a nonzero constant
c
.

Proof.

A parallel field is constant along horizontal curves. Transporting
p_{0}

around a loop ends at
p_{0}h
, so equivariance forces

\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}u(p_{0})=u(p_{0})
. Conversely, a holonomy-fixed
\mu
can be
transported from
p_{0}
along any base path and extended equivariantly along
the final fiber. Two choices differ by holonomy and give the same value.
Local smooth dependence of parallel transport proves smoothness. This also
proves uniqueness and (
4.1
).

For the final assertion, restrict the two equal kernels to the vertical
space. Their nonzero linear functionals on
\mathfrak{g}
have the same kernel,
so
v(p)=c(p)u(p)
. The formula

c=B(v,u)/B(u,u)
makes
c
smooth. Parallelism gives

0=\mathrm{d}_{A}v=(\mathrm{d}c)u
, hence
c
is constant.
∎

Unit normalization and a prescribed coorientation remove the scalar
ambiguity. Applying
\mathrm{d}_{A}
to (
2.2
) gives
[F_{A},u]=0
,
a local curvature condition. The following example exhibits the additional
constraint imposed by holonomy around noncontractible loops.

Example 4.2

(A flat obstruction on a parallelizable base)
.

Take
G=\mathrm{SO}(3)
and
M=T^{2}
. The commuting rotations

R_{1}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1),\qquad R_{2}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,-1)

define a representation
\rho:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathrm{SO}(3)
by

\rho(m,n)=R_{1}^{m}R_{2}^{n}
. Form the bundle

P=(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathrm{SO}(3))/\mathbb{Z}^{2},\qquad(m,n)\cdot(s,t,g)=(s+m,t+n,\rho(m,n)g).

The product connection descends and is flat. Under

\mathfrak{so}(3)\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the two rotations have fixed spaces

\mathbb{R}e_{1}
and
\mathbb{R}e_{2}
, with zero intersection. Proposition
4.1

implies that this connection admits no nonzero parallel adjoint field.
Thus this flat connection on a parallelizable base has trivial holonomy-fixed
subspace in the adjoint representation.

On a closed base, the holonomy criterion also has a spectral formulation.

Proposition 4.3

(Normalized variational criterion)
.

Suppose
M
is closed and equip it with a Riemannian metric. For a fixed smooth
connection
A
, set

\lambda_{\min}(A)=\inf_{s\in H^{1}(\operatorname{ad}P),\ \|s\|_{L^{2}}=1}\|\nabla_{A}s\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.2)

The infimum is attained by a smooth eigensection of

\nabla_{A}^{*}\nabla_{A}
. Moreover,

\lambda_{\min}(A)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathfrak{g}^{\operatorname{Hol}_{p_{0}}(A)}\neq 0.

If the fixed space is zero, then
\lambda_{\min}(A)>0
.

Proof.

For a fixed smooth connection on a compact manifold,

\|s\|_{H^{1}}
is controlled by

\|\nabla_{A}s\|_{L^{2}}+\|s\|_{L^{2}}
. A minimizing sequence is therefore
bounded in
H^{1}
. Weak
H^{1}
compactness and strong
L^{2}
compactness give a
normalized minimizer. Weak lower semicontinuity proves minimality, and the
Euler–Lagrange equation is

\nabla_{A}^{*}\nabla_{A}s=\lambda_{\min}(A)s
. Elliptic regularity gives smoothness.
The minimum is zero precisely when the minimizer is parallel; now use
Proposition
4.1
.
∎

The parallel fields form the zero eigenspace of the connection Laplacian.
Each nonzero element determines its invariant hyperplane distribution by
(
2.3
).

5
Global realization and characteristic quotients

Fix a nonzero value
\mu=u(p_{0})
, let
H=G_{\mu}
be its stabilizer, and let

\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}_{\mu}
. The level set

Q=u^{-1}(\mu)

(5.1)

is a principal
H
-subbundle over
M
. To see this directly, horizontally
transport a point of
Q
over a coordinate neighborhood to obtain a local
section of
Q
. Within each fiber the level set is its right
H
-orbit.
On
TQ
, equation (
2.2
) implies
[A,\mu]=0
; hence the
restriction
A_{Q}
is an
\mathfrak{h}
-valued connection on
Q
.

Conversely, any connection
A_{Q}
on an
H
-reduction extends to a connection
on
P\cong Q\times_{H}G
. We use the equivalence

(q,g)\sim(qh,h^{-1}g)
. On
Q\times G
the extended objects are

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}A_{Q}+g^{-1}\mathrm{d}g,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\mu,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=B(\mu,A_{Q})+B(\mu,\mathrm{d}g\,g^{-1}).

(5.3)

These expressions descend, and substitution gives
\mathrm{d}_{A}u=0
. In
particular, the existence problem on a varying connection is precisely the
existence of such a stabilizer reduction.

5.1
Realization of paired curvature

Let
\pi_{Q}:Q\to M
be a fixed principal
H
-bundle. Since
\mu
commutes with

\mathfrak{h}
, the linear functional
B(\mu,\cdot)|_{\mathfrak{h}}
annihilates

[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}]
. For each connection
A_{Q}
define

\pi_{Q}^{*}\beta_{A_{Q}}=B(\mu,F_{A_{Q}}).

Theorem 5.1

(Curvature realization on a fixed reduction)
.

The class

c_{\mu}(Q)=[\beta_{A_{Q}}]\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)

(5.4)

is independent of
A_{Q}
. Every closed representative of this class is realized
by a connection on
Q
. More explicitly, if

\beta^{\prime}=\beta_{A_{Q}}+\mathrm{d}\gamma
, put

z=\frac{\mu}{B(\mu,\mu)},\qquad A^{\prime}_{Q}=A_{Q}+z\,\pi_{Q}^{*}\gamma.

(5.5)

Then
A^{\prime}_{Q}
is a connection and
\beta_{A^{\prime}_{Q}}=\beta^{\prime}
.
Consequently, a fixed reduction with
\dim H=1
yields a contact form of
the type (
2.3
) exactly when
c_{\mu}(Q)
has a symplectic
representative.

Proof.

For another connection
A_{Q}+a
, the difference
a
is horizontal and

H
-equivariant. Thus
B(\mu,a)=\pi_{Q}^{*}\gamma
for a base one-form
\gamma
.
Using the curvature difference formula and the fact that
\mu
annihilates
brackets in
\mathfrak{h}
gives

B(\mu,F_{A_{Q}+a}-F_{A_{Q}})=\mathrm{d}B(\mu,a)=\pi_{Q}^{*}\mathrm{d}\gamma.

This proves independence. The element
z
is central in
\mathfrak{h}
and fixed by

H
, so the addition in (
5.5
) is tensorial and defines a
connection. Its curvature changes by
z\,\pi_{Q}^{*}\mathrm{d}\gamma
, whose pairing with

\mu
is
\pi_{Q}^{*}\mathrm{d}\gamma
. The contact assertion now follows from
Corollary
2.2
.
∎

The class
c_{\mu}(Q)
is the degree-two characteristic class associated with the
invariant linear functional
B(\mu,\cdot)|_{\mathfrak{h}}
. A character of
H
whose differential is a normalized multiple of

determines an integral normalization of this class. On a closed base of
dimension
2n>0
, a symplectic representative requires

c_{\mu}(Q)^{n}\neq 0
. Formula (
5.5
) realizes every symplectic representative
by a connection on the fixed reduction.

On the reduction,
\pi_{Q}^{*}\beta=\mathrm{d}B(\mu,A_{Q})
.
On
P
, the differential of
\alpha
is given by (
2.5
).

5.2
Characteristic quotients

Assume for the rest of this subsection that
\beta
is symplectic. Put

\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{h}\cap\mu^{\perp},

and let
K\subset H
be its connected analytic subgroup. The subspace

\mathfrak{k}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{h}
and invariant under
\operatorname{Ad}_{H}
, since

\mu
is fixed by
H
. Thus
K
is a normal subgroup, although it may not
be closed.

Theorem 5.2

(Characteristic quotient)
.

The characteristic leaves in
P\cong Q\times_{H}G
are the immersed
submanifolds

\{[q,kg]:k\in K\}.

If
K
is closed, their leaf space is the smooth bundle

\overline{P}=Q\times_{H}(K\backslash G),

(5.6)

and
\alpha
descends to a contact form on
\overline{P}
.
If
K
is not closed, the leaf space is not
T_{1}
and hence is not a
Hausdorff manifold.

Proof.

Formula (
2.8
) and nondegeneracy of
\beta
identify the
characteristic space at
[q,g]
with the tangent space to the left coset

Kg
. On
G
, the form
B(\mu,\mathrm{d}g\,g^{-1})
is invariant under left
translation by
K
and annihilates its tangent directions; its exterior
derivative does so as well. The form in (
5.3
) is therefore
basic for these leaves.

If
K
is closed,
K\backslash G
is smooth. Normality in
H
defines
the required left
H
-action on
K\backslash G
, and local trivializations
of
Q
identify (
5.6
) with the leaf quotient. The descended
form is nowhere zero. Its Levi form is obtained from
\mathrm{d}\alpha|_{\xi}

by dividing out its entire radical, and is nondegenerate. This is a contact
form. If
K
is not closed, each such leaf is nonclosed in its fiber,
being dense in the larger coset
\overline{K}g
. A
T_{1}

make the inverse image of every singleton closed, which is impossible.
∎

The smooth quotient is a homogeneous contact coupling construction. The
closedness condition is a global condition on the subgroup; it is invisible
to the pointwise rank formula.

Example 5.3

(An irrational characteristic foliation)
.

On

2\pi

6.1

A reduction

(6.1)

The second identity follows by multiplying the total Chern classes

(1+c_{1}(L))(1-c_{1}(L))

Write

(6.2)

Let

7
Evolution and its mixed-curvature obstruction

Time dependence requires compatibility in both space and time. Let
A_{t}
be
a smooth family of connections and let
\eta_{t}:P\to\mathfrak{g}
be equivariant.
For an adjoint-valued time-dependent form define

D_{t}=\partial_{t}+[\eta_{t},\cdot],\qquad E_{t}=\dot{A}_{t}-\mathrm{d}_{A_{t}}\eta_{t}.

The form
E_{t}
is horizontal and equivariant. It is the mixed curvature of
the connection
\mathbb{A}=A_{t}+\eta_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t
on
P\times I\to M\times I
:

F_{\mathbb{A}}=F_{A_{t}}+\mathrm{d}t\wedge E_{t}.

(7.1)

Proposition 7.1

(Compatibility in space and time)
.

Let
u_{t}
solve
D_{t}u_{t}=0
and suppose
\mathrm{d}_{A_{0}}u_{0}=0
. Then

D_{t}(\mathrm{d}_{A_{t}}u_{t})=[E_{t},u_{t}].

(7.2)

Consequently
\mathrm{d}_{A_{t}}u_{t}=0
for all
t
if and only if

[E_{t},u_{t}]=0
for all
t
. When these conditions hold, the base forms
defined by

\pi^{*}\beta_{t}=B(u_{t},F_{A_{t}}),\qquad\pi^{*}e_{t}=B(u_{t},E_{t})

satisfy

\mathrm{d}\beta_{t}=0,\qquad\partial_{t}\beta_{t}=\mathrm{d}e_{t}.

(7.3)

In particular
[\beta_{t}]
is independent of time.

Proof.

Expanding the covariant derivatives gives the general commutator identity

D_{t}(\mathrm{d}_{A_{t}}u_{t})=\mathrm{d}_{A_{t}}(D_{t}u_{t})+[E_{t},u_{t}].

This proves (
7.2
). If the right side vanishes, the
initially zero form
\mathrm{d}_{A_{t}}u_{t}
solves a homogeneous linear temporal
equation and remains zero. Necessity follows from the same identity.
Under compatibility,
u_{t}
is parallel for
\mathbb{A}
; invariant pairing
with its Bianchi identity gives

\mathrm{d}_{M\times I}(\beta_{t}+\mathrm{d}t\wedge e_{t})=0.

Separating spatial and temporal degrees proves (
7.3
).
∎

Example 7.2

(Spatial flatness does not ensure temporal compatibility)

On the trivial
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-bundle over
\mathbb{R}

The temporal equation fixes

\mathrm{d}_{A_{t}}u_{t}=t[T_{1},T_{3}]\,\mathrm{d}x

[E_{t},u_{t}]=[T_{1},T_{3}]\,\mathrm{d}x

The following equation has a stronger transport property. Here

is a

on

\dot{A}_{t}=\mathrm{d}_{A_{t}}\eta_{t}-\iota_{W_{t}}F_{A_{t}}.

(7.4)

The vector field

specifies the infinitesimal bundle transport.

Theorem 7.3

(Transport preserves the Levi geometry)

Suppose (

(7.5)

A_{t}
, and

\Phi_{t}^{*}A_{t}=A_{0},\quad\Phi_{t}^{*}u_{t}=u_{0},\quad\Phi_{t}^{*}\alpha_{t}=\alpha_{0},\quad\phi_{t}^{*}\beta_{t}=\beta_{0}.

(7.6)

Consequently

\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{d}\alpha_{t}|_{\xi_{t}})_{\Phi_{t}(p)}=\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{d}\alpha_{0}|_{\xi_{0}})_{p}.

(7.7)

Integrability, contactness, and every Levi rank locus are transported by the
flow.

Proof.

For any vector field
Y
, Cartan’s formula gives

\mathcal{L}_{Y}A=\iota_{Y}F_{A}+\mathrm{d}_{A}(A(Y)).

(7.8)

In (
7.5
), one has
A_{t}(Z_{t})=-\eta_{t}
, and horizontality of

F_{A_{t}}
gives
\iota_{Z_{t}}F_{A_{t}}=\iota_{W_{t}}F_{A_{t}}
.
Thus (
7.4
) is

\dot{A}_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{Z_{t}}A_{t}=0
. Differentiating the pullback along the flow
proves
\Phi_{t}^{*}A_{t}=A_{0}
.

Define
\widetilde{u}_{t}=(\Phi_{t}^{-1})^{*}u_{0}
. Naturality of covariant
derivatives shows
\mathrm{d}_{A_{t}}\widetilde{u}_{t}=0
. Hence

\mathcal{L}_{Z_{t}}\widetilde{u}_{t}=-[A_{t}(Z_{t}),\widetilde{u}_{t}]=[\eta_{t},\widetilde{u}_{t}].

The transport equation for
\widetilde{u}_{t}
is therefore

D_{t}\widetilde{u}_{t}=0
. Uniqueness of this pointwise linear ODE proves

u_{t}=\widetilde{u}_{t}
. Pairing the transported fields gives the third
identity in (
7.6
); curvature naturality gives the fourth.
Exterior differentiation and restriction to the transported kernels yield
(
7.7
).
∎

Equivalently,
E_{t}=-\iota_{W_{t}}F_{A_{t}}
and

e_{t}=-\iota_{v_{t}}\beta_{t}
, so (
7.3
) becomes

\partial_{t}\beta_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{v_{t}}\beta_{t}=0.

This identifies (
7.4
) as transport by bundle automorphisms.
On a compact base the smooth invariant vector fields involved have flows
on each compact time interval on which their coefficients are smooth,
since
G
is compact and therefore
P
is compact.

General compatible evolutions need not preserve rank. For example,

A_{t}=\mathrm{d}\theta+tx\,\mathrm{d}y
,
u_{t}=1
,
\eta_{t}=0
on

\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
satisfies Proposition
7.1
with

E_{t}=x\,\mathrm{d}y
. Its paired curvature is

\beta_{t}=t\,\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y
: the distribution is integrable at
t=0

and contact when
t\neq 0
. Such a family cannot satisfy a smooth transport
law (
7.4
) through
t=0
. Constancy of a de Rham class alone
does not preserve pointwise rank.

7.1
Curvature forces and power

For the magnetic force convention below, the paired spacetime curvature
separates the gyroscopic force and temporal power. If
\beta_{t}
acts on a velocity
w
by
the force covector
\iota_{w}\beta_{t}
, then its instantaneous power is

(\iota_{w}\beta_{t})(w)=\beta_{t}(w,w)=0.

For a virtual displacement
\delta q
, the value

\beta_{t}(w,\delta q)
can be nonzero; this is a virtual-work pairing.
On spacetime, the closed form

\mathcal{B}=\beta_{t}+\mathrm{d}t\wedge e_{t}
gives

\mathcal{B}(\partial_{t}+w,\delta q)=\beta_{t}(w,\delta q)+e_{t}(\delta q).

With this force convention, the power is
e_{t}(w)
.

8
Adapted metrics for invariant contact forms

The contact paths above on circle bundles over surfaces admit invariant
adapted metrics. We first treat an arbitrary invariant contact form on a
three-manifold and then apply the construction to these paths.

Throughout this section,
N
is a closed three-manifold,
\alpha
is a
contact form, and
X
is the nowhere-zero generator of a locally free

S^{1}
-action preserving
\alpha
. We orient
N
by

\alpha\wedge\mathrm{d}\alpha
. The action may have exceptional orbits.
Write
\xi=\ker\alpha
, and let
R_{\alpha}
be the Reeb field, determined by

\alpha(R_{\alpha})=1,\qquad\iota_{R_{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\alpha=0.

The projection onto
\xi
along
R_{\alpha}
is

\Pi_{\alpha}V=V-\alpha(V)R_{\alpha}
.

Definition 8.1
.

A Riemannian metric
g
is adapted to
\alpha
if

*_{g}\mathrm{d}\alpha=\kappa\alpha
for a smooth positive function
\kappa
.
When
\kappa
is constant,
\alpha
is a curl eigenform.
Here
*_{g}
is the Hodge star for
g
and the contact orientation.
For a vector field
V
, the curl convention is

(\operatorname{curl}_{g}V)^{\flat}=*_{g}\mathrm{d}(V^{\flat})
.

This definition agrees with
[
7
, Definition 2.1]
and allows the
norm of
\alpha
to vary. The simultaneous normalization problem fixes
the eigenvalue and the length of
X
.

8.1
The metric construction

Decompose the circle generator as

X=fR_{\alpha}+Y,\qquad f=\alpha(X),\qquad Y=\Pi_{\alpha}X.

(8.1)

The action preserves
R_{\alpha}
,
\xi
,
\Pi_{\alpha}
,
f
, and
Y
.
Choose an invariant metric
h
on
\xi
with area form

\mathrm{d}\alpha|_{\xi}
. Such a metric exists: average any metric on
\xi
over
the circle, write its area form as
r\mathrm{d}\alpha|_{\xi}
with
r>0
, and
divide the averaged metric by
r
. Extend
h
to a symmetric tensor on

TN
by

h(V,W)=h(\Pi_{\alpha}V,\Pi_{\alpha}W),

so
h(R_{\alpha},\cdot)=0
. Put
q=h(Y,Y)
.
Since
X
is nowhere zero,
f
and
q
have no common zero.

Theorem 8.2

(Simultaneous metric normalization)
.

For every constant
\kappa>0
, the formulas

\sigma=\frac{2}{q+\sqrt{q^{2}+4\kappa^{2}f^{2}}},\qquad g=\kappa^{2}\sigma^{2}\alpha\otimes\alpha+\sigma h

(8.2)

define a smooth Riemannian metric on
N
satisfying

*_{g}\mathrm{d}\alpha=\kappa\alpha,\qquad g(X,X)=1,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{X}g=0.

(8.3)

Proof.

The denominator and the argument of the square root in
(
8.2
) are strictly positive. Thus
\sigma
is smooth
and positive, and the Reeb splitting
TN=\mathbb{R}R_{\alpha}\oplus\xi

makes
g
positive definite. Every term is invariant, so
\mathcal{L}_{X}g=0
.

The function
\sigma
is the unique positive solution of

\kappa^{2}f^{2}\sigma^{2}+q\sigma=1.

(8.4)

Using (
8.1
), the left side is
g(X,X)
.
The identity holds at
f=0
and at
q=0
by direct substitution.

Take a local positively oriented
h
-orthonormal coframe

\eta^{1},\eta^{2}
on
\xi
and extend it by zero on
R_{\alpha}
.
The area normalization gives
\mathrm{d}\alpha=\eta^{1}\wedge\eta^{2}
.
A positively oriented
g
-orthonormal coframe is

e^{0}=\kappa\sigma\alpha,\qquad e^{1}=\sqrt{\sigma}\,\eta^{1},\qquad e^{2}=\sqrt{\sigma}\,\eta^{2}.

Consequently,

*_{g}\mathrm{d}\alpha=\sigma^{-1}*_{g}(e^{1}\wedge e^{2})=\sigma^{-1}e^{0}=\kappa\alpha.\qed

Along the locus
f=0
, one has
q>0
and
\sigma=1/q
.
Where
Y=0
, one has
f\neq 0
and
\sigma=1/(\kappa|f|)
.
Formula (
8.2
) gives a single smooth metric across both
loci. Averaging and the metric construction take place on the smooth
total space, including points with finite circle stabilizer.

In the weakly compatible family

g=a^{2}\alpha^{2}+(a/\kappa)h
of
[
8
, Proposition 2.1(3)]
, the
choice
a=\kappa\sigma
imposes the unit-length condition through
(
8.4
). This gives an affirmative answer to

[
7
, Question 2.7]
.

The formulation also covers a nowhere-zero strict contact field tangent
to the fibers of a Seifert fibration. On a regular fiber chart, its
positive period
\tau
is smooth. Differentiating

p\mapsto\varphi_{\tau(p)}(p)=p
and applying the invariant form
\alpha

gives
f\mathrm{d}\tau=0
. At
f=0
, Cartan’s formula yields

\mathrm{d}f=-\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\alpha\neq 0
, so
f\neq 0
is dense and

\mathrm{d}\tau=0
. The regular orbit space of a connected Seifert manifold
is connected. Hence the principal period is constant, and its time map
is the identity everywhere by continuity. The field therefore generates
a locally free circle action, to which Theorem
8.2
applies.

8.2
The space of normalized metrics

For fixed
\alpha
,
X
, and
\kappa>0
, let
\mathcal{M}_{\xi}
denote the
space of invariant metrics on
\xi
with area form
\mathrm{d}\alpha|_{\xi}
.
Let
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
denote the space of invariant Riemannian metrics
on
N
satisfying
*_{g}\mathrm{d}\alpha=\kappa\alpha
and
g(X,X)=1
.
Both spaces carry their
C^{\infty}
topologies.

Proposition 8.3
.

The construction (
8.2
) defines a homeomorphism

\mathcal{M}_{\xi}\to\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
. The map and its inverse preserve
smooth parameter families. In particular,
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
is
nonempty and contractible.

Proof.

Given
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
, put
a=\sqrt{g(R_{\alpha},R_{\alpha})}
.
The identity
\mathrm{d}\alpha=\kappa*_{g}\alpha
implies that

\alpha^{\sharp_{g}}
spans
\ker\mathrm{d}\alpha=\mathbb{R}R_{\alpha}
.
Thus
R_{\alpha}\perp_{g}\xi
, and restriction to
\xi
gives

\mathrm{d}\alpha|_{\xi}=\frac{\kappa}{a}\operatorname{area}_{g|_{\xi}}.

It follows that

h=\frac{\kappa}{a}\,g|_{\xi}

(8.5)

belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{\xi}
. With
\sigma=a/\kappa
, the equation

g(X,X)=1
becomes (
8.4
), which determines

\sigma
uniquely. This proves the inverse formula. Both constructions
depend smoothly on their tensor arguments.

Fix
h_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\xi}
. For
h\in\mathcal{M}_{\xi}
, set

k_{s}=(1-s)h+sh_{*}
for
0\leq s\leq 1
and write

\operatorname{area}_{k_{s}}=r_{s}\mathrm{d}\alpha|_{\xi}
.
Then
h_{s}=k_{s}/r_{s}
is an invariant metric of the required area.
The path contracts
\mathcal{M}_{\xi}
to
h_{*}
and fixes
h_{*}

throughout. Transporting this contraction by
(
8.2
) and (
8.5
) proves the result.
∎

Corollary 8.4
.

For the metric (
8.2
), the field

V=\alpha^{\sharp_{g}}=R_{\alpha}/(\kappa^{2}\sigma^{2})
satisfies

\operatorname{curl}_{g}V=\kappa V,\qquad\operatorname{div}_{g}V=0,\qquad[X,V]=0.

The orbits of
X
are unit-speed geodesics.

Proof.

The curl identity follows from (
8.3
).
Since
\kappa
is constant,
\mathrm{d}^{2}\alpha=\kappa\mathrm{d}*_{g}\alpha=0

gives the divergence identity. Invariance gives
[X,V]=0
.
Finally, a constant-length Killing field satisfies

\nabla_{X}X=-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{grad}|X|^{2}=0
.
∎

8.3
Circle bundles and examples

Suppose the circle action is free, and write
\pi:N\to\Sigma
for its
principal bundle over a surface. Let
\vartheta
be an invariant real
connection with
\vartheta(X)=1
and curvature

\mathrm{d}\vartheta=\pi^{*}\Omega
. Every invariant one-form has a unique
decomposition

\alpha=(\pi^{*}f)\vartheta+\pi^{*}\gamma,\qquad f\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma),\quad\gamma\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma).

(8.6)

Here
f
is the function
\alpha(X)
on the quotient. Exterior
differentiation gives

\alpha\wedge\mathrm{d}\alpha=\vartheta\wedge\pi^{*}\bigl(f^{2}\Omega+f\mathrm{d}\gamma+\gamma\wedge\mathrm{d}f\bigr).

(8.7)

Thus
\alpha
is contact exactly when the two-form in parentheses
is nowhere zero. The parallel circle-bundle case is obtained by taking

\vartheta=A
, constant nonzero
f
, and
\gamma=0
.
Formula (
8.7
) also describes
invariant contact forms with a nonempty dividing locus
f^{-1}(0)
.

For the real circle convention
A(X)=1
,
u=1
, and

\alpha=A
, one has
R_{\alpha}=X
,
f=1
, and
q=0
.
Theorem
8.2
reduces to

g=\alpha^{2}+\kappa^{-1}h,\qquad\operatorname{area}_{h}=\mathrm{d}\alpha|_{\xi}.

(8.8)

Corollary 8.5

(Metric degeneration along a contact path)
.

Apply Theorem
3.2
to a principal circle bundle over a
closed surface with the normalization
u=1
,
\alpha_{t}=A_{t}
.
For every
\kappa>0
, the forms
\alpha_{t}
,
t<T
, admit a smooth family
of invariant metrics with
*_{g_{t}}\mathrm{d}\alpha_{t}=\kappa\alpha_{t}
and

g_{t}(X,X)=1
. Every continuous extension to
t=T
of any such family
as symmetric tensors is degenerate at each point over the collapsing ball.

Proof.

Orient
\Sigma
by
\beta_{0}
, fix a positive area form
\nu
, and choose
a metric
g_{\Sigma}
with area
\nu
. Write
\beta_{t}=s_{t}\nu
, where

s_{t}>0
for
t<T
. The tensor

h_{t}=(s_{t}\circ\pi)\pi^{*}g_{\Sigma}
restricts to an invariant metric on

\xi_{t}=\ker\alpha_{t}
with area form
\mathrm{d}\alpha_{t}|_{\xi_{t}}
.
Equation (
8.8
) gives

g_{t}=\alpha_{t}^{2}+\kappa^{-1}(s_{t}\circ\pi)\pi^{*}g_{\Sigma}.

This family is smooth for
t<T
.

For any normalized family with a continuous tensor limit, the limit is
positive semidefinite. If it were positive definite at a point over the
collapsing ball, continuity of the Hodge star there would give

*_{g_{T}}\mathrm{d}\alpha_{T}=\kappa\alpha_{T}
. On that ball,

\mathrm{d}\alpha_{T}=\pi^{*}\beta_{T}=0
, while
\alpha_{T}(X)=1
.
The displayed identity would therefore imply
0=\kappa
, a contradiction.
∎

On
\mathbb{T}^{3}
with coordinates of period
2\pi
, take

\alpha=\cos z\,\mathrm{d}x-\sin z\,\mathrm{d}y,\qquad X=\partial_{x}.

Then
\alpha\wedge\mathrm{d}\alpha=\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y\wedge\mathrm{d}z

R_{\alpha}=\cos z\,\partial_{x}-\sin z\,\partial_{y},\qquad Z=\sin z\,\partial_{x}+\cos z\,\partial_{y}.

Choose
h
making

orthonormal on
\xi
.
Here
f=\cos z
,
Y=\sin z\,Z
, and

, so

\sigma_{\kappa}(z)=\frac{2}{\sin^{2}z+\sqrt{\sin^{4}z+4\kappa^{2}\cos^{2}z}},

g_{\kappa}=\kappa^{2}\sigma_{\kappa}^{2}\alpha^{2}+\sigma_{\kappa}\bigl((\sin z\,\mathrm{d}x+\cos z\,\mathrm{d}y)^{2}+\mathrm{d}z^{2}\bigr).

Both
f=0
and
Y=0
occur. The metric is smooth for all
\kappa>0
;
at
\kappa=1

On the minimal forts of trees
Thomas R. Cameron, Kelvin Li

Abstract
In 2008, the zero forcing number of a graph was shown to be an upper bound on the graph’s maximum nullity. In 2018, the concept of forts was introduced to provide a set covering characterization of zero forcing sets. Since then, forts have been integrated into integer programming models for zero forcing and have been used to yield bounds on the zero forcing number. In 2025, researchers explored the combinatorial question of how many minimal forts a graph can have. They demonstrated that the number of minimal forts in a graph with an order of at least six is strictly less than Sperner’s bound. Moreover, the authors derived an explicit formula for the number of minimal forts in path, cycle, and spider graphs. For example, the number of minimal forts in a path graph aligns with the Padovan sequence, and thus exhibits exponential growth. In this article, we demonstrate that no tree with an order of n n can have fewer than n / 3 n/3 minimal forts. Additionally, we provide a characterization of the trees that possess exactly n / 3 n/3 minimal forts. We conjecture that the n / 3 n/3 bound is valid for all graphs of order n n . We present experimental evidence supporting this conjecture and prove that it holds for all Eulerian graphs. Keywords: Zero Forcing; Minimal Forts; Trees AMS Subject Classification: 05C05, 05C15, 05C30, 05C57

Introduction

Zero forcing is a binary coloring game played on a graph, where a set of filled vertices can force non-filled vertices to become filled according to a color change rule.
A zero forcing set is a set of filled vertices that can force the entire graph; the zero forcing number is the minimum cardinality of a zero forcing set.
In 2007, researchers Burgarth and Giovannetti independently developed the concept of zero forcing for the control of quantum systems, referring to it as graph infection
[
7
]
.
The following year, it was demonstrated that the zero forcing number serves as an upper bound on the maximum nullity of a real symmetric matrix associated with a graph
[
13
]
.
It was also established in 2008 that the combinatorial optimization problem for the zero forcing number is NP-hard since the corresponding decision problem was shown to be NP-complete
[
1
]
.

In 2014, the concept of failed zero forcing sets was introduced
[
10
]
.
These are subsets of filled vertices that do not have the ability to force the entire graph. The failed zero forcing number represents the maximum size of such a set.
The literature has shown considerable interest in failed zero forcing, covering topics such as extreme values, the impact of graph operations, and the NP-hardness of the related combinatorial optimization problem
[
2
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
.
More recently, in 2025, the concept of well-failed graphs was defined
[
15
]
.
These are graphs where every maximal failed zero forcing set is also a maximum failed zero forcing set.
A characterization of all well-failed trees was also provided.

In 2018, the concept of forts in a graph was introduced
[
9
]
.
A fort is a non-empty subset of vertices such that no vertex outside this set has exactly one neighbor within it.
Forts have a close relationship with failed zero forcing sets, since a subset is a minimal fort if and only if its complement is a maximal failed zero forcing set.
Consequently, the NP-hardness of identifying a maximum failed zero forcing set implies the NP-hardness of finding a minimum fort within a graph.
Additionally, a subset of vertices from a graph is a zero forcing set if and only if it intersects every fort of the graph; thus, the zero forcing sets of a graph form a set cover for the forts of the graph.
This relationship has been applied in computational methods for determining the minimum zero forcing set of a graph
[
4
,
5
]
.

In 2025, researchers explored the combinatorial question of how many minimal forts a graph can have.
They demonstrated that the number of minimal forts in a graph with an order of at least six is strictly less than Sperner’s bound.
This bound was established by Emanuel Sperner in 1928, and applies to the size of a collection of subsets in which no subset includes another
[
6
]
.
Moreover, the authors derived an explicit formula for the number of minimal forts in path, cycle, and spider graphs.
For example, the number of minimal forts in a path graph aligns with the Padovan sequence, and thus exhibits exponential growth.

In this article, we demonstrate that no tree with an order of
n
can have fewer than
n/3
minimal forts.
Additionally, we provide a characterization of the trees that possess exactly
n/3
minimal forts.
We conjecture that the
n/3
bound is valid for all graphs of order
n
.
We present experimental evidence supporting this conjecture and prove that it holds for all Eulerian graphs.

Preliminaries

Throughout this article, we let
\mathbb{G}
denote the set of all finite simple unweighted graphs.
For each

, where

and

If the context is not clear, we use

The

and the

of

.

Let

.
Given

.
We refer to every

as a

of

, and we say that

and

are
adjacent
.
If the context is not clear, we use
N_{G}(u)
to denote the neighborhood of

in the graph

.
The

of

is defined by
N[u]=N(u)\cup\{u\}
.
If

, then we say that

and

are

.
The degree of

is denoted by

; when

, we refer to

as an

, when

, we refer to

as a

, and when

.
The

The graph

is a

of

and

.
Given

, the subgraph induced by

and the edges

.
We reference this subgraph as an

and denote it by

.
If

.
Furthermore,

is a

Given vertices

, we say that

is

if there exists a list of distinct vertices

such that
u=w_{0}
,
v=w_{l}
,
\{w_{i},w_{i+1}\}\in E

, and
w_{i}\neq w_{j}
for all

.
Such a list of vertices is called a

The connected to relation is an equivalence relation, which partitions the vertex set

into disjoint subsets:
W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{k}
, where for all

and for all

is connected to

.

For each

, the induced subgraph

is called a

of

.
The graph

is

if it has exactly one connected component.
Given

.
Also, given

is the subgraph of

.
If

has more connected components than

is a
cut vertex
of
G
.
Similarly, if
G-\{u,v\}
has more connected components than

, then

is a
cut edge
of

.

A
pendant generalized star
or
spider graph
is a tree with exactly one junction vertex. We let
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
denote a spider graph with junction (or
central
) vertex
v
and pendant vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
where
k\geq 3
. For
i=1,\ldots,k
,
l_{i}
is the length of the path from
v
to
u_{i}
, and we often say

is the
i
th
pendant length
. We call the path between the central vertex
v
and some pendant vertex as a
pendant path
. For convenience, we will also refer to path graphs as spider graphs with exactly two pendant paths, though such spider graphs do not have a junction vertex.

Zero forcing
is a binary coloring game on a graph, where vertices are either filled or non-filled.
In this article, we denote filled vertices by the color gray and non-filled vertices by the color white.
An initial set of gray vertices can force white vertices to become gray following a color change rule.
While there are many color change rules, see
[
14
, Chapter 9]
, we will use the
standard rule
which states that a gray vertex
u
can force a white vertex
v
if
v
is the only white neighbor of
u
.
Since the vertex set is finite, there comes a point in which no more forcings are possible.
If at this point all vertices are gray, then we say that the initial set of gray vertices is a
zero forcing set
of
G
; otherwise, we refer to the initial set of gray vertices as a
failed zero forcing set
of
G
.
The
zero forcing number
of
G
, denoted
\zf(G)
, is the minimum cardinality of a zero forcing set of
G
.

Given a graph
G\in\mathbb{G}
, a non-empty subset
F\subseteq V
is a
fort
if no vertex
u\in V\setminus{F}
has exactly one neighbor in
F
.
Note that the zero forcing sets of
G
form a set cover for the forts of
G
; in particular, Theorem
2.1
states that a subset of vertices is a zero forcing set if and only if that subset intersects every fort.
While one direction of this result was originally proven in
[
9
, Theorem 3]
, both directions are shown in
[
4
, Theorem 8]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Theorem 8 in
[
4
]
)
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{G}
.
Then,
S\subseteq V
is a zero forcing set of
G
if and only if
S
intersects every fort in
\mathcal{F}_{G}
.

It is worth noting that Theorem
2.1
holds for the minimal forts of
G
, where a fort
F
is minimal if every proper subset of
F
is not a fort of
G
.
Also, a maximal failed zero forcing set of
G
is a failed zero forcing set
S
such that every proper superset of
S
is a zero forcing set of
G
.
The following theorem establishes a connection between minimal forts and maximal failed zero forcing sets.

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem 10 in
[
3
]
)
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{G}
.
Then,
F\subseteq V
is a minimal fort of
G
if and only if
S=V\setminus{F}
is a maximal failed zero forcing set of
G
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, we let
\mathcal{F}_{G}
denote the set of minimal forts of
G
.
If
v\in V\setminus F
, for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{G}
, then
v
is called a
fort irrelevant
vertex of
G
.
Similarly, a vertex
v\in V
is
zero forcing irrelevant
if it not a member of any minimal zero forcing set of
G
, where a
minimal zero forcing set
is a zero forcing set
S
such that every proper subset of
S
is a failed zero forcing set.
Note that, by Theorem
2.2
, fort irrelevant vertices of
G
are those vertices that are in every maximal failed zero forcing set.
Furthermore, the following theorem establishes that fort irrelevant vertices are exactly those that are zero forcing irrelevant.

Theorem 2.3

(Theorem 2.4 in
[
15
]
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
v\in V
.
Then,
v
is fort irrelevant if and only if
v
is zero forcing irrelevant.

3
Properties of Minimal Forts of Trees

In this section, we present some results on the structures of minimal forts of trees. The first two results hold more generally for any pair of connected graphs.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2}\in\mathbb{G}
, and let
v_{1}\in V(G_{1})
be fort-irrelevant. For some
v_{2}\in V(G_{2})
, if
G
is the graph formed with vertex set
V(G)=V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and edge set
E(G)=E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})\cup\{\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\}
, then
v_{1}
is also fort-irrelevant in
G
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that no minimal fort of
G
contains
v_{1}
. By way of contradiction, assume there exists a minimal fort
F
of
G
that contains
v_{1}
. Let
R=F\cap V(G_{1})
and
Q=F\cap V(G_{2})
(so
F=R\cup Q
). Since
v_{1}\in R
,
R
is not a minimal fort of
G_{1}
. Therefore,
R
is either a non-minimal fort or not a fort of
G_{1}
. If
R
is a non-minimal fort of
G_{1}
,
R
contains a minimal fort
F^{\prime}
of
G_{1}
. Since
v_{1}\notin F^{\prime}
,
F^{\prime}\subset F
would be a minimal fort of
G
, contradicting the minimality of
F
. Otherwise, if
R
is not a fort of
G_{1}
, then there exists a vertex
w\in V(G_{1})\setminus R
such that
\lvert N(w)\cap R\rvert=1
. Since
u\in R
, we have
w\neq u
, and it follows that
F
is not a fort.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2}\in\mathbb{G}
, and let
v_{1}\in V(G_{1})
and
v_{2}\in V(G_{2})
be fort-irrelevant vertices. If
G
is the graph formed with vertex set
V(G)=V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and edge set
E(G)=E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})\cup\{\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\}
, then
v_{1},v_{2}
are fort-irrelevant in
G
. Additionally, the number of minimal forts of
G
satisfy

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G_{1}}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G_{2}}\rvert

Next, we show that every minimal fort of a tree contains at least two leaves.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n\geq 2
, and let
S\subseteq\mathcal{L}(T)
where
\mathcal{L}(T)
is the set of leaves of
T
. If
\lvert S\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{L}(T)\rvert-1
or
\lvert S\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{L}(T)\rvert
, then
S
is a zero forcing set of
T
.

Proof.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n\geq 2
. It suffices to show that any set
S\subseteq\mathcal{L}(T)
such that
\lvert S\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{L}(T)\rvert-1
is a zero forcing set of
T
. We proceed with induction on
n
. If
n=2
, then
T
is the path graph
P_{2}
with two leaves. Let
S
be one of the leaves of
P_{2}
. Then
S
is a zero forcing set of
P_{2}
with
\lvert S\rvert=1
, and our base case is satisfied. Now, let
k\geq 2
and assume that for any tree
T
of order
k
, any set
S\subseteq\mathcal{L}(T)
such that
\lvert S\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{L}(T)\rvert-1
is a zero forcing set of

. To construct a tree

of order
k+1
, we append a vertex
u
to a vertex
v\in V(T)
. If
v
is a leaf of
T
, let
S=\mathcal{L}(T)\setminus\{v\}
where
\lvert S\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{L}(T)\rvert-1
. By our inductive hypothesis,
S
is a zero forcing set of
T
. Hence,
v
was forced at some step, and it follows that
v
will also force

. Therefore,
S
is also zero forcing set of
T^{\prime}
. Additionally, since
v
is a leaf of
T
and
u
was appended to
v
,
\lvert\mathcal{L}(T)\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{L}(T^{\prime})\rvert
, whence our inductive step holds. Conversely, if
v
is not a leaf of
T
, we construct the set
S^{\prime}=S\cup\{u\}
where
S
is a zero forcing set of
T
with cardinality
\lvert S\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{L}(T)\rvert-1
. It follows that
S^{\prime}
is a zero forcing set of
T^{\prime}
with cardinality
S^{\prime}=\lvert\mathcal{L}(T)\rvert
. Since
\lvert\mathcal{L}(T^{\prime})\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{L}(T)\rvert+1
, we have
\lvert S^{\prime}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{L}(T^{\prime})\rvert-1
, and our inductive step holds.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n\geq 2
. Then every minimal fort of
T
contains at least two leaves.

Proof.

By way of contradiction, assume there exists a minimal fort
F
of
T
that contains no more than one leaf. Consider the set
S=V(G)\setminus F
; since

contains no more than one leaf,

contains all leaves of

The following results allow for useful constructions of minimal forts of trees.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
T
be a tree and let
F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}
. For each vertex

such that

,

is either adjacent to zero vertices in

or exactly two vertices in

.

Proof.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}
. It suffices to show that no vertex
v\in V(T)
where

can be adjacent to more than two vertices in

. By way of contradiction, assume for some vertex
v\in V(G)
and

that

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
T
be a tree and let
v\in V(T)
. If
v
is fort-irrelevant, then
v
is not a leaf.

Proof.

Equivalently, we show that every leaf is contained in some minimal fort of
T
. Let
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\}
denote a minimum path cover of
T
. Every leaf of
T
is in some path

, so every leaf of

is in some minimum zero forcing set of

. Let

be a leaf and consider the minimum zero forcing set

that contains

. Since

is a zero forcing set,

intersects every minimal fort of

. Additionally, since
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{v\}

Fort-irrelevant vertices are characterized by
star centers
, and star centers are identified through
star removals
. In particular, it was identified in
[
15
]
that a vertex of a tree is fort-irrelevant if and only if it is a star center.

Definition 3.1

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{G}
. For a vertex
v\in V(G)
, let
L(v)
denote all neighbors of
v
that are leaves and let
L[v]=\{v\}\cup L(v)
. If
G
contains a vertex

such that
\lvert L(v)\rvert\geq 2
, then we can create a new graph
G^{\prime}=G[V(G)\setminus L[v]]
. We call the process of producing
G^{\prime}
from

. Note that
G^{\prime}
may not necessarily be connected.

Definition 3.2

(Definition 1.8 in
[
15
]
)
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{G}
. We define
B_{0}
to be the set of all vertices of
G
such that for each
v\in B_{0}
,
\lvert L(v)\rvert\geq 2
. We perform star removals for each vertex

to create the graph
G_{1}
. Next, we define
B_{1}
to be the set of all vertices of
G_{1}
such that for each
v\in B_{1}
,
\lvert L(v)\rvert\geq 2
. We perform star removals for each vertex
v\in B_{1}
to create the graph
G_{2}
. Continuing

and form sets

(Corollary 2.5 in

]

Let

be a tree. Then

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
T
be a tree and let
v\in V(T)
be a fort-irrelevant vertex. Then
\lvert N(v)\rvert\geq 2
and there exists a minimal fort of
T
that contains exactly two neighbors of

.

Proof.

Since
v
is fort-irrelevant,
v
cannot be a leaf of
T
, so
\lvert N(v)\rvert\geq 2
. Additionally, Lemma
3.7
implies

is a star center. If

has two leaves, our result follows. Otherwise, let
T_{i}
be the graph obtained after

star removals (where

). Then for some

,

has two leaves as neighbors on the tree

. Let

be one of the leaves of

. It follows that

cannot also be a star center since the star removal on

would have removed

. Therefore, there exists a minimal fort

that includes

4
Number of Minimal Forts of Trees

The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n
. Then the number of minimal forts of
T
satisfies

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert\geq\dfrac{n}{3}.

We first show that Theorem
4.1
holds for all path graphs and all spider graphs with
k\geq 3
pendant paths. A closed form formula for the number of minimal forts of path graphs was given in
[
3
]
.

Proposition 4.2

(Corollary 14 in
[
3
]
)
.

The number of minimal forts of
P_{n}
satisfies

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{n}}\rvert=\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho^{n+4}}{2\rho+3}\right\rceil

for any
n\geq 1
, where
\rho=1.3247179572\ldots
is the plastic ratio and
\left\lfloor\cdot\right\rceil
denotes rounding to the nearest integer.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
P_{n}
be the path graph on
n\geq 1
vertices. Then the number of minimal forts of
P_{n}
satisfies

\lvert{\mathcal{F}_{P_{n}}}\rvert\geq\dfrac{n}{3}.

Proof.

For orders
n\leq 6
, it can be manually verified that
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{n}}\rvert\geq n/3
. For orders
n>6
, the number of minimal forts is given by Proposition
4.2
. Since

\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho^{n+4}}{2\rho+3}\right\rceil\geq\dfrac{\rho^{n+4}}{2\rho+3}-1,

an induction argument shows that for any
n>6
,

\dfrac{\rho^{n+4}}{2\rho+3}-1\geq\dfrac{n}{3}.

∎

Next, we show that all spider graphs satisfy Theorem
4.1
. In particular, we show that the spider graph achieves a better bound than
n/3
. A formula for the number of minimal forts of spiders was derived in
[
3
]
as follows:

Theorem 4.4

(Theorem 17 in
[
3
]
)
.

Let
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
denote a spider graph with junction vertex
v
,
k\geq 3
pendant paths, pendant lengths
l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}\geq 1
, and pendant vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
. Then the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
satisfies

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{j\neq i}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}-1}}\rvert+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}}\rvert

where
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{-1}}\rvert=1
and
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{0}}\rvert=0
.

In Theorem
4.4
, the first term counts all the minimal forts that include the central vertex
v
and none of its neighbors; the second term counts all the minimal forts that include
v
and one neighbor of
v
; and the third term counts all minimal forts that does not include
v
. As a prerequisite to showing Theorem
4.1
holds for spider graphs, we derive a useful formula for counting the minimal forts on spider graphs.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
denote a spider graph with junction vertex
v
,
k\geq 3
pendant paths, pendant lengths
l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}\geq 1
, and pendant vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
. For integers
a,b
where
-1\leq a\leq b
, we define

\Delta_{a}^{b}=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{b}}\rvert-\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{a}}\rvert\geq 0,

where
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{-1}}\rvert=1
and
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{0}}\rvert=0
. If we form the spider
S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}
by appending a leaf to
u_{1}
, then the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}
satisfies

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}-2}^{l_{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}-1}^{l_{1}}\sum_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i\\
j\neq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}-1}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}}^{l_{1}+1}\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.4
, we have

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{j\neq i}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}-1}}\rvert+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}}\rvert.

Factoring out all expressions with
l_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{1}-1}}\rvert\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{1}-2}}\rvert\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{1}-1}}\rvert\sum_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i\\
j\neq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}-1}}\rvert

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{1}}}\rvert\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert+\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}}\rvert.

It follows that for
S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}
,

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{1}}}\rvert\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{1}-1}}\rvert\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{1}}}\rvert\sum_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i\\
j\neq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}-1}}\rvert

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{1}+1}}\rvert\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert+\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}}\rvert.

By taking the difference,

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert-\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\Delta_{l_{1}-1}^{l_{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}-2}^{l_{1}-1}\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\Delta_{l_{1}-1}^{l_{1}}\sum_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i\\
j\neq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}-1}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}}^{l_{1}+1}\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert,

whence

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\left(\Delta_{l_{1}-1}^{l_{1}}+\Delta_{l_{1}-2}^{l_{1}-1}\right)\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\Delta_{l_{1}-1}^{l_{1}}\sum_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i\\
j\neq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}-1}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}}^{l_{1}+1}\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}-2}^{l_{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}-1}^{l_{1}}\sum_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i\\
j\neq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}-1}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}}^{l_{1}+1}\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert.

∎

We now show that all spider graphs satisfy Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
denote a spider graph of order
n
with junction vertex
v
,
k\geq 3
pendant paths, pendant lengths
l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}\geq 1
, and pendant vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
. Then the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
satisfies

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert\geq\left\lfloor\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

We first demonstrate that the inequality holds for spider graphs where the pendant lengths satisfy
1\leq l_{i}\leq 2
for
i=1,\ldots,k
. We assume there are
r
pendant paths of length
1
and
k-r
pendant paths of length
2
. Then the order of the spider may be written as
n=2k-r+1
. From Theorem
4.4
, the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
satisfy

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{j\neq i}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}-1}}\rvert+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}}\rvert.

We proceed with cases on
r
.

Case 1.1:
r=0
.
Then
n=2k+1
and the second term in
(1)
vanishes, whence

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{1}}\rvert+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{2}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{2}}\rvert=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(1)(1)=1+\binom{k}{2}.

Since
n=2k+1
, we have

\left\lfloor\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\dfrac{2k+2}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor k+1\right\rfloor.

An induction argument shows that

1+\binom{k}{2}\geq\left\lfloor k+1\right\rfloor

holds for any
k\geq 3
.

Case 1.2:
r=1
.
Since the pendant paths can be arbitrarily labeled, let
l_{1}=1
. Then
n=2k
and the first term in
(1)
vanishes, whence

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{-1}}\rvert\prod_{j\neq 1}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{1}}\rvert+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}}\rvert=1+\binom{k}{2}.

Since
n=2k
,

\left\lfloor\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\dfrac{2k+1}{2}\right\rfloor,

and an induction argument shows that

1+\binom{k}{2}\geq\left\lfloor\dfrac{2k+1}{2}\right\rfloor

holds for any
k\geq 3
.

Case 1.3:
r\geq 2
.
Then
n=2k-r+1
and the first two terms in
(1)
vanishes, whence

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}}\rvert=\binom{k}{2}.

Since
n=2k-r+1
, we have

\left\lfloor\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\dfrac{2k-r+2}{2}\right\rfloor,

and an induction argument shows that

\binom{k}{2}\geq\left\lfloor\dfrac{2k-r+2}{2}\right\rfloor

holds for any
2\leq r\leq k
with
k\geq 3
.

Next, we demonstrate the claim for spider graphs where the pendant lengths satisfy
1\leq l_{i}\leq 3
for
i=1,\ldots,k
where there exists at least one pendant path of length 3. For such spiders, we begin with a spider
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
such that the pendant lengths satisfy
1\leq l_{i}\leq 2
for
i=1,\ldots,k
, and iteratively append leaf vertices to pendant paths of length
2
. Since the pendant paths can be arbitrarily labeled, we assume that all leaves are appended to the pendant path between
v
and
u_{1}
to form the spider
S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}
. From Lemma
4.5
with
l_{1}=2
,

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}-2}^{l_{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}-1}^{l_{1}}\sum_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i\\
j\neq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}-1}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}}^{l_{1}+1}\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\Delta_{0}^{2}\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert+\Delta_{1}^{2}\sum_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i\\
j\neq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}-1}}\rvert+\Delta_{2}^{3}\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert,

(2)

where we used
\Delta_{1}^{2}=\Delta_{2}^{3}=0
and
\Delta_{0}^{2}=1
. Now, assume
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
had
r
pendant paths of length
1
. We proceed with cases on
r
.

Case 2.1:
r=0
.
Then
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}-1}\rvert=1
for every
i=2,\ldots,k
, whence
(2)
becomes

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+1.

That is, we gain exactly one minimal fort, which implies we maintain the inequality. This can be repeated for each pendant path of length
2
, so any spider graph where the pendant lengths satisfy
2\leq l_{i}\leq 3
for
i=1,\ldots,k
satisfy our inequality.

Case 2.2:
r=1
.
Then
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert=0
for some
i=1,\ldots,k
, whence

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert.

That is, the number of minimal forts remain unchanged. Therefore, it suffices to show that the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
is sufficient for the inequality to hold despite the extra vertices. From Case 1.2, the order of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
is
n=2k
, and the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
satisfies

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=1+\binom{k}{2}.

If we append a leaf to all
k-1
pendant paths of length
2
, then the order of the spider graph increases to at most
n^{\prime}=n+(k-1)=3k-1
. Then

\left\lfloor\dfrac{n^{\prime}+1}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\dfrac{3k}{2}\right\rfloor,

and an induction argument shows that

1+\binom{k}{2}\geq\left\lfloor\dfrac{3k}{2}\right\rfloor

holds for any
k\geq 3
.

Case 2.3:
r\geq 2
.
Then
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-1}}\rvert=0
for some
i=1,\ldots,k
, whence

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert.

Following a similar argument to Case 2.2, it suffices to show that the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
is sufficient for the inequality to hold despite the extra vertices. From Case 1.3, the order of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
is
n=2k-r+1
, and the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
satisfies

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\binom{k}{2}.

Appending a leaf to all
k-r
pendant paths of length
2
increases the order of the spider graph to at most
n^{\prime}=n+k-r=3k-2r+1
. Then

\left\lfloor\dfrac{n^{\prime}+1}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\dfrac{3k-2r+2}{2}\right\rfloor,

and an induction argument shows that

\binom{k}{2}\geq\left\lfloor\dfrac{3k-2r+2}{2}\right\rfloor

holds for any
2\leq r\leq k-1
with
k\geq 3
.

Finally, we demonstrate the inequality holds for spider graphs with arbitrary pendant lengths. For such spiders, we begin with a spider
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
such that the pendant lengths satisfy
1\leq l_{i}\leq 3
for
i=1,\ldots,k
, and iteratively append leaf vertices to pendant paths of length
3
or greater. Since the pendant paths can be arbitrarily labeled, we assume that all leaves are appended to the pendant path between
v
and
u_{1}
to form the spider
S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}
. We proceed with cases on
l_{1}
.

Case 3.1:
l_{1}=3
.
From Lemma
4.5
,

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\Delta_{1}^{3}\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}-1}\rvert+\Delta_{2}^{3}\sum_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i\\
j\neq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}-1}\rvert+\Delta_{3}^{4}\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert,

where we used
\Delta_{1}^{3}=\Delta_{2}^{3}=0
and
\Delta_{3}^{4}=1
. Since
k\geq 3
and
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert\geq 1
, we obtain a minimum of two minimal forts.

Case 3.2:
l_{1}=4
.
From Lemma
4.5
,

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\Delta_{2}^{4}\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}-1}\rvert+\Delta_{3}^{4}\sum_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i\\
j\neq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}-1}\rvert+\Delta_{4}^{5}\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\prod_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}-1}\rvert+\sum_{i=2}^{k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}-2}}\rvert\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i\\
j\neq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}-1}\rvert,

where we used
\Delta_{2}^{4}=\Delta_{3}^{4}=1
and
\Delta_{4}^{5}=0
. If
S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}
has no more than one pendant path of length

, we obtain a nonzero number of minimal forts, which implies our inequality holds. Otherwise, the second and third terms vanish, whence
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert
. However, since to obtain a pendant path of length
4
a leaf must have been previously appended to a pendant path of length
3
, Case 3.1 implies that two minimal forts were gained at minimum. It follows that the inequality remains satisfied regardless of the number of pendant paths of length
1
.

Case 3.3:
l_{1}\geq 5
.
From Lemma
4.5
,

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert\geq\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}}^{l_{1}+1}\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert.

Since

∎

In the proof of Theorem

, it is not necessary to consider all possible minimal forts. In fact, given a spider graph with central vertex

, it is often enough to simply consider the minimal forts that do not contain

. Therefore, it is convenient for us to count separately the minimal forts that include
v
and the minimal forts that do not contain
v
. For the remainder of this section and for any given spider graph

, we will denote the set of minimal forts that do not contain

as
\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}
and the set of minimal forts that do contain

as
\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}
. Therefore, we can write the following formulas for
\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert
and
\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert
that follow directly from Lemma
4.5
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
denote a spider graph with junction vertex
v
,
k\geq 3
pendant paths, pendant lengths
l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}\geq 1
, and pendant vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
. For integers
a,b
where
-1\leq a\leq b
, we define

\Delta_{a}^{b}=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{b}\rvert-\lvert\mathcal{F}_{a}\rvert\geq 0,

where
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{-1}}\rvert=1
and
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{0}}\rvert=0
. If we form the spider
S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}
by appending a leaf to
u_{1}
, the the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}
satisfies

\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}}^{l_{1}+1}\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert

j\neq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}-1}\rvert.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
denote a spider graph of order
n
with junction vertex
v
,
k\geq 3
pendant paths, pendant lengths
l_{i},\ldots,l_{k}\geq 1
, and pendant vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
. If
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}\neq S_{3,3,3}
, then the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
that does not include
v
satisfies

\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert\geq\dfrac{n}{3}.

Proof.

We first show that the inequality is satisfied for spiders whose pendant lengths satisfy
1\leq l_{i}\leq k
for
i=1,\ldots,k
. From Theorem
4.4
, the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
that do not include
v
is given by

\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}}\rvert.

For spiders where the pendant lengths satisfy
1\leq l_{i}\leq 3
for
i=1,\ldots,k
, we have
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert=1
, which implies

\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(1)=\binom{k}{2}.

If there are
r
pendant paths of length
1
,
s
pendant paths of length
2
and
t=k-r-s
pendant paths of length
3
, the order of the spider is given by

n=r+2s+3t+1=3k-2r-s+1.

The maximal order occurs when
r,s=0
, where
n=3k+1
. By an induction argument, the inequality

\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\binom{k}{2}\geq\dfrac{n}{3}=\dfrac{3k+1}{3}

holds for any
k\geq 4
. In particular, it is false for
k=3
which is the spider graph
S_{3,3,3}
. For any
r,s\neq 0
, any
k\geq 3
satisfies the inequality

\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\binom{k}{2}\geq\dfrac{n}{3}=\dfrac{3k-2r-s+1}{3}.

We note that equality is obtained for the spider
S_{2,3,3}
. To construct any other spider, we iteratively append leaf vertices to pendant paths of length
3
or greater. From Lemma
4.7
,

\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1}+1,\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}}^{l_{1}+1}\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert.

In particular, the second term provides a sufficient number of minimal forts each time a leaf is appended for the inequality to hold.
∎

For convenience, we will also refer to path graphs as spider graphs with two pendant paths, although such spiders do not contain a junction vertex. For paths, we derive an analogous result to Lemma
4.8
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
S_{l_{1},l_{2}}
denote a spider graph of order
n
with junction vertex
v
,
k=2
pendant paths, pendant lengths
l_{1},l_{2}\geq 4
, and pendant vertices
u_{1},u_{2}
. Then the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},l_{2}}
that does not include
v
satisfies

\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},l_{2}}}\rvert>\dfrac{n}{3}.

Proof.

From Theorem
4.4
, the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},l_{2}}
that does not include
v
is given by

\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},l_{2}}}\rvert=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{j}}}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{1}}}\rvert\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{2}}}\rvert.

We observe that for the spiders
S_{4,4},S_{4,5},\text{ and }S_{5,5}
,

\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},l_{2}}}\rvert=(2)(2)=4.

In particular, the minimal forts of each such spider satisfies
\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},l_{2}}}\rvert\geq n/3
. To construct any other spider, we iteratively append leaf vertices to pendant paths of length
5
or greater. From Lemma
4.5
,

\displaystyle\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1}+1,l_{2}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},l_{2}}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}}^{l_{1}+1}\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{i}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},l_{2}}}\rvert+\Delta_{l_{1}}^{l_{1}+1}\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{2}}}\rvert

Since
\Delta_{l_{1}}^{l_{1}+1}\geq 1
for any
l_{1}\geq 5
and
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{l_{2}}}\rvert\geq 1
, it follows that the inequality holds.
∎

We now present a proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem
4.1
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n
. Then the number of minimal forts of
T
satisfies

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert\geq\dfrac{n}{3}.

Proof.

We proceed with strong induction on
n
. We observe that the tree of order
n=1
has one minimal fort whence our base case is satisfied. Let
m\geq 1
and assume that all trees of order
n\leq m
has at least
n/3
minimal forts. Consider any tree
T
with
n^{\prime}=m+1
vertices. If
T
is a spider graph or a path, we are finished by Proposition
4.3
or Theorem
4.6
. Otherwise, we pick a spider
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
of
T
with central vertex
v
, neighboring vertices
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}
, pendant vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
, and
k\geq 2
pendant paths, and remove
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
from
T
to form the tree
T^{\prime}
with vertex set

V(T^{\prime})=V(T)\setminus V(S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}})

and edge set

E(T^{\prime})=E(T)\setminus(E(S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}})\cup\{v,v^{\prime}\})

where
v^{\prime}\in V(T^{\prime})
. We denote the order of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
with
s
and the order of
T^{\prime}
with
t
(hence
n^{\prime}=t+s
). By our inductive hypothesis, the number of minimal forts of
T^{\prime}
satisfies
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert\geq t/3
. Next, we show that for each minimal fort
F^{\prime}
of
T^{\prime}
, we can construct a set
F
such that
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
and
F
is a minimal fort of
T
. To that end, we define the sets
\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}=\{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}\mid v^{\prime}\notin F^{\prime}\}
and
\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}=\{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}\mid v^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}\}
; that is,
\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}
is the set of all minimal forts of
T^{\prime}
that does not contain
v^{\prime}
, and
\mathring{\mathcal{F}}
is the set of all minimal forts of
T^{\prime}
that contains
v^{\prime}
. Let
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}
; if
F^{\prime}\in\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}
, then
F=F^{\prime}
is a minimal fort of
T
. Conversely, if
F^{\prime}\in\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}
, then
F=F^{\prime}\cup F_{\text{$(w_{i},u_{i})$-path}}
is a minimal fort of
T
, where
F_{\text{$(w_{i},u_{i})$-path}}
is any minimal fort on the
(w_{i},u_{i})
-path for some
i=1,\ldots,k
. Additionally, let
\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}
be the set of all minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
that excludes the central vertex
v
. It follows that the number of minimal forts of
T
satisfies

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert+k\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert+\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert

\displaystyle=(\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert+\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert)+\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+(k-1)\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert+\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+(k-1)\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert.

The term
k\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert
follows from the fact that for each minimal fort of
T^{\prime}
that includes
v^{\prime}
, we can construct a minimal fort on
T
using each of the
k
pendant paths of the spider
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
(at least once). Now, since
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert\geq t/3
, we have

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert\geq\dfrac{t}{3}+\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert+(k-1)\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert.

Next, we enumerate the number of minimal forts of the spider
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
and show that
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert\geq n^{\prime}/3
. We proceed with cases.

Case 1:
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
has
k\geq 3
pendant paths and
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}\neq S_{3,3,3}
.
From Lemma
4.8
,
\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}
satisfies
\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert\geq s/3
, whence
(1)
becomes

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert\geq\dfrac{t}{3}+\dfrac{s}{3}+(k-1)\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert\geq\dfrac{t+s}{3}=\dfrac{n^{\prime}}{3}.

Case 2:
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}=S_{3,3,3}
.
Then
\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=3
,

, and
k=3
, whence
(1)
becomes

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert\geq\dfrac{t}{3}+3+2\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert.

If
\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert\neq 0
, we obtain

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert\geq\dfrac{t}{3}+3+2(1)\geq\dfrac{t}{3}+\dfrac{10}{3}=\dfrac{n^{\prime}}{3}.

Conversely, if
\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert=0

\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert=0

is fort-irrelevant. From Lemma

cannot be a leaf. Therefore,

has

. Now, Lemma

implies there exists a minimal fort

such that

contains exactly two neighbors of

, and assume

that we have yet to account for from our previous constructions. We note that we may repeat the construction with

Case 3:
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
has
k=2

.
It follows from Lemma

that
\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert\geq s/3
, whence

becomes

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert\geq\dfrac{t}{3}+\dfrac{s}{3}+(k-1)\lvert\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{T^{\prime}}\rvert\geq\dfrac{t+s}{3}=\dfrac{n^{\prime}}{3}.

Case 4:

has

.
We proceed with subcases.

Case 4.2:

5
On the Fort Number and Zero Forcing Number

In this section, we discuss results on the fort number and zero forcing number of graphs. As defined in
[
8
]
, the
fort number
of a graph
G
is the size of maximum possible collection of disjoint minimal forts of
G
. In particular, the fort number provides a lower bound on the zero forcing number; that is,
\ft(G)\leq\zf(G)
. We show that the number of minimal forts of a graph also bounds the zero forcing number.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{G}
. Then
\zf(G)\leq\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert
.

Proof.

By way of contradiction, assume that
\zf(G)>\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert
. Let the minimum zero forcing set of
G
be
S
. Now, we construct a set
S^{\prime}
by selecting a vertex (not necessarily unique) from each minimal fort in
\mathcal{F}_{G}
. By construction,
S^{\prime}
intersects every minimal fort of
G
, so
S^{\prime}
is a zero forcing set. However, the cardinality of
S^{\prime}
is given by
\lvert S^{\prime}\rvert\leq\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert
whence
\lvert S^{\prime}\rvert<\lvert S\rvert
, contradicting the minimality of
S
.
∎

Proposition
5.1
implies that all known lower bounds for the zero forcing number also provide a lower bound for the number of minimal forts. Additionally, we observed that every graph of order
n
with
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=n/3
also exhibited the property that
\ft(G)=\zf(G)=n/3
. In other words, when the number of minimal forts is exactly
n/3
, there exists a packing of minimal forts such that every minimal fort is disjoint, and the graph can be forced by selecting a vertex from each disjoint minimal fort. Now, we provide some results for when either
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\ft(G)
or
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\zf(G)
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{G}
. If
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\ft(G)
, then
\zf(G)=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\ft(G)
.

Proof.

Since
\ft(G)\leq\zf(G)\leq\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert
, if
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\ft(G)
, it immediately follows that
\zf(G)=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\ft(G)
.
∎

To show that
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\zf(G)
implies
\ft(G)=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\zf(G)
, we require the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{G}
, and let
H=(\mathcal{F}_{G},S,E)
be a finite bipartite graph where
\mathcal{F}_{G}
is the set of all minimal forts of
G
,
S
is a minimum zero forcing set of
G
, and
E=\{\{v,F\}\mid v\in S\text{ and }\exists F\in\mathcal{F}_{G}\text{ s.t. }v\in F\}
. Then there exists an
\mathcal{F}_{G}
-perfect matching if and only if
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\zf(G)
.

Proof.

We first show the reverse direction. Assume that
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\zf(G)
. It suffices to show that
H
satisfies Hall’s marriage condition; that is, a perfect matching exists if and only if for every subset
W\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{G}
,
\lvert W\rvert\leq\lvert N(W)\rvert
. The condition holds for
W=\mathcal{F}_{G}
since
\lvert W\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert\leq\lvert N(W)\rvert=\lvert S\rvert=Z(G)
. By way of contradiction, assume for some
W\subset\mathcal{F}_{G}
that
\lvert W\rvert>\lvert N(W)\rvert
. We define
\delta=\lvert W\rvert-\lvert N(W)\rvert>0
and the nonempty set
\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{G}=\mathcal{F}_{G}\setminus W
. Now, let
R
be an initially empty set and iteratively select one vertex (not necessarily unique) from each minimal fort
F\in\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{G}
and insert it into
R
. After the final iteration,
R
has cardinality
\lvert R\rvert\leq\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\setminus W\rvert
. Next, we define the set
S^{\prime}=R\cup N(W)
and note that
S^{\prime}
intersects every minimal fort of
G
, whence
S^{\prime}
is a zero forcing set. In particular,
S^{\prime}
has cardinality

\displaystyle\lvert S^{\prime}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert R\cup N(W)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert R\rvert+\lvert N(W)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\setminus W\rvert+\lvert N(W)\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\setminus W\rvert+\left(\lvert W\rvert-\delta\right)

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert-\delta

\displaystyle=\lvert S\rvert-\delta

\displaystyle<\lvert S\rvert.

Therefore,
S^{\prime}
is a zero forcing set with cardinality smaller than
S
, and we arrive at a contradiction. Hence, no such set
W
exists, and it follows that
H
satisfies Hall’s marriage condition.

For the forward direction, we proceed by contraposition. Assume that
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert\neq\zf(G)
. Proposition
5.1
implies
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert>\zf(G)
. Let
S
be the minimum zero forcing set of
G
. Then Hall’s marriage condition fails for
W=\mathcal{F}_{G}
:

\lvert W\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert>\lvert N(W)\rvert=\lvert S\rvert=\zf(G).

Therefore, no perfect matching exists for
H
.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{G}
. If
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\zf(G)
, then
\ft(G)=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\zf(G)
.

Proof.

Since
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\zf(G)
, Lemma
5.3
allows us to construct a set
S
by selecting a distinct vertex from each minimal fort. By construction,
S
intersects every minimal fort and has cardinality
\lvert S\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=\zf(G)
, so
S
is a minimum zero forcing set. To show that
\ft(G)=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert
, it suffices to show that no two minimal forts intersect. By way of contradiction, assume for some
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{G}
that
F\cap F^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
. Let
S\cap F=\{v\}
,
S\cap F^{\prime}=\{v^{\prime}\}
, and
u\in F\cap F^{\prime}
. In particular, the set constructed using Lemma
5.3
implies that
v\neq v^{\prime}
. Then
(S\setminus\{v,v^{\prime}\})\cup\{u\}
intersects every minimal fort of
G
, whence
S^{\prime}
is a zero forcing set with cardinality smaller than
S
. Therefore,
S^{\prime}
contradicts the minimality of
S
, so no such minimal forts exist.
∎

In particular, we have the following characterizations for trees where
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert=n/3
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n=3k
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\ft(T)=\zf(T)=k
if and only if
T=H\circ E_{2}
for some tree
H
of order
k
.

Proof.

For the forward direction, suppose that
\ft(T)=\zf(T)=k
. Then
T
has a maximum collection of disjoint forts
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{k}\}
. Additionally,
T
has a minimum path cover
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{k}\}
. Let
\ell(T)
denote the number of leaves of
T
. By Proposition
3.4
, every fort of
T
contains at least two leaves. Since the forts of
\mathcal{F}
are disjoint,
\ell(T)\geq 2k
. Additionally, each path in
\mathcal{P}
contains at most two leaves of
T
, whence
\ell(T)\leq 2k
. It follows that
\ell(T)=2k
. Since
\ell(T)=2k
and
\mathcal{P}
is a path cover of
T
, each path in
\mathcal{P}
contains exactly two leaves. Furthermore, no path in
\mathcal{P}
can be disconnected from
T
, whence every path in
\mathcal{P}
has order at least 3. Since
T
has order
n=3k
, it follows that every path in
\mathcal{P}
has order exactly 3. Therefore, for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
, we write the vertex set of
P_{i}
as

V(P_{i})=\{x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}\}

and the edge set of
P_{i}
as

E(P_{i})=\{\{x_{i},y_{i}\},\{y_{i},z_{i}\}\},

where the leaves
x_{i},z_{i}
are also leaves of
T
. Since
T
is connected, each
y_{i}
is adjacent to a
y_{j}
where
i\neq j
. We define
H
to be the tree with vertex set

V(H)=\{y_{i}\mid\leq 1\leq i\leq k\}

and edge set

E(H)=\{\{y_{i},y_{j}\}\mid\{y_{i},y_{j}\}\in E(T)\}.

Then
T=H\circ E_{2}
.

For the reverse direction, suppose that
T=H\circ E_{2}
for some tree
H
of order
k
. Let
V(H)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
. For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, there is a pair of vertices
x_{i},y_{i}
on the tree
T
such that

N(x_{i})=N(y_{i})=v_{i}.

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we define
P_{i}
to be the path from
x_{i}
to
y_{i}
. Then
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq k\}
is a path cover of
T
. Since
Z(T)=P(T)
for a tree, it follows that

\ft(T)\leq\zf(T)\leq k.

Moreover, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
F_{i}=\{x_{i},y_{i}\}
is a minimal fort of
T
. Since the forts
F_{i},F_{j}
are disjoint for any
i\neq j
, it follows that

\ft(T)=\zf(T)=k.

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
denote a spider graph of order
n\geq 3
with junction vertex
v
,
k\geq 2
pendant paths, pendant lengths
l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}\geq 1
, and pendant vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
. Then
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert=n/3
if and only if
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}=S_{1,1}
.

Proof.

The reverse direction is straightforward:
S_{1,1}
is the
P_{3}
graph, and
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{3}}\rvert=1
. For the forward direction, we proceed by contraposition. Assume that
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}\neq S_{1,1}
; we wish to show that
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert>n/3
. If
k=2
, then
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
is a path graph. We observe that every path graph other than
P_{3}
has strictly more than
n/3
minimal forts. If
k\geq 3
, then, from Theorem
4.6
, the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
satisfies

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert\geq\left\lfloor\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor.

Since
k\geq 3
, we have
n\geq 4
. In particular, an induction argument shows that

\left\lfloor\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor>\dfrac{n}{3}

holds for any
n\geq 4
.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n=3m
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert=m
if and only if
T=H\circ E_{2}
for some tree
H
of order
m
.

Proof.

Let
H
be a tree of order
m
. The reverse direction is straightforward: if
T=H\circ E_{2}
, then
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert=n/3
, where each minimal fort is a pair of leaves that share the same junction vertex. For the forward direction, we proceed by contraposition. Assume that
T\neq H\circ E_{2}
; we must show that
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert>m=n/3
. Since
T\neq H\circ E_{2}
, for some vertex
v\in V(T)
,
v
does not have two leaves. Now, we iteratively remove spiders from
T
until we are left with only a spider
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
of order
s
that contains
v
. Since
v
does not have two leaves,
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}\neq S_{1,1}
. It follows from Lemma
5.6
that the number of minimal forts of
S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}
is strictly greater than
s/3
. Next, we reconstruct the tree
T
by adding each spider we have previously removed. In each iteration, we repeat the constructions used in the proof of Theorem
4.1
. However, since
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{k}}}\rvert>s/3
, inequality (1) in the proof of Theorem
4.1
becomes a strict inequality. Since the inequality is strict for each iteration, it follows that
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert>n/3
.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
n=3m
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert=m
if and only if
\ft(T)=\zf(T)=m

6
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we investigated the structures of minimal forts of trees, the number of minimal forts of trees, and relationships between the fort number, the zero forcing number, and the number of minimal forts of graphs. In particular, we have shown that the number of minimal forts of a tree
T
of order
n
satisfies
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{T}\rvert\geq n/3
. Notably, this bound can be demonstrated despite having almost zero information about the structure of the tree itself. We believe that this bound also holds for graphs with cycles, and we have tested this computationally for all graphs of order up to
n=10
. Given the number of graphs of orders above
10
, it becomes impractical to enumerate the minimal forts for each graph. Nevertheless, we will formally state this conjecture now.

Conjecture 6.1
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{G}
be a graph of order
n
. Then
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert\geq n/3
.

The difficulty of the problem lies in the fact that the order of a graph provides very little information on its structure. In trees, this difficulty can be alleviated since every tree contains a spider graph. No such structures exist for graphs in general. However, the bound can be demonstrated for other more structured families of graphs. For example, the bound also holds for Eulerian graphs, which we prove here. Other families of graphs we believe are worth considering include planar graphs and bipartite graphs.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{G}
be an Eulerian graph of order
n
. Then the number of minimal forts of
G
satisfies

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert\geq\dfrac{n}{3}.

Proof.

We first note that for any
n\geq 3
, the number of minimal forts of the cycle graph
C_{n}
satisfies
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{C_{n}}\rvert\geq n/3
. For any
n
, let
v\in V(C_{n})
and let
\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{C_{n}}\rvert
denote the minimal forts of
C_{n}
that do not contain
v
. Then
\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{C_{n}}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{n-1}}\rvert
, since if
v
is not in the fort, its neighbors must be in the fort.

A graph
G
is Eulerian if and only if it can be decomposed into edge-disjoint cycles. Let
G
be a Eulerian graph with
k
edge-disjoint cycles. We proceed with induction on
k
. If
k=1
, we have a cycle graph and our result follows. Now, let
G^{\prime}
be an Eulerian graph of order
n^{\prime}
that can be decomposed into
k
edge-disjoint cycles. Let
C_{n}
be a cycle graph on
n
vertices and let
v\in V(C_{n})
and
v^{\prime}\in V(G^{\prime})
. To construct an Eulerian graph with
k+1
edge-disjoint cycles, we perform a vertex sum to form the graph
G
by combining the vertices
v,v^{\prime}
into one vertex
u
. In particular,
G
has order
m=n+n^{\prime}-1
, vertex set
V(G)=(V(C_{n})\setminus v)\cup(V(G^{\prime})\setminus v^{\prime})\cup\{u\}
, and edge set
E(G)=(E(C_{n})\setminus S_{v})\cup(E(G^{\prime})\setminus S_{v^{\prime}})\cup S_{u}
, where
S_{v}
denotes all edges of
C_{n}
incident on
v
,
S_{v^{\prime}}
denotes all edges of
G^{\prime}
incident on
v^{\prime}
, and
S_{u}=\{\{u,w\}\mid w\in N_{C_{n}}(v)\cup N_{G^{\prime}}(v^{\prime})\}
. Let
\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{G^{\prime}}
denote the set of all minimal forts of
G^{\prime}
that do not contain
v^{\prime}
and let
\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{G^{\prime}}
denote the set of all minimal forts of
G^{\prime}
that contains
v^{\prime}
. For each minimal fort
F^{\prime}\in\dot{\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}}
,
F=(F^{\prime}\setminus\{v^{\prime}\})\cup\{u\}
is a minimal fort of
G
. For each minimal fort
F^{\prime}\in\mathring{\mathcal{F}}_{G^{\prime}}
,
F=(F^{\prime}\setminus\{v^{\prime}\})\cup(F_{C}\setminus\{v\})\cup\{u\}
is a minimal fort of
G
, where
F_{C}
is any minimal fort of
C_{n}
that includes
v
. Additionally, every minimal fort
F\in\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{C_{n}}
is a minimal fort of
G
. It follows that

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert\geq\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}\rvert+\lvert\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{C_{n}}\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{n-1}}\rvert.

From our inductive hypothesis,
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G^{\prime}}\rvert\geq n^{\prime}/3
. Additionally, from Proposition
4.3
,
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{P_{n-1}}\rvert\geq(n-1)/3
. It follows that

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert\geq\dfrac{n^{\prime}}{3}+\dfrac{n-1}{3}=\dfrac{n^{\prime}+n-1}{3}=\dfrac{m}{3},

and our inductive step is satisfied.
∎

In this paper, we have also discussed trees with exactly
n/3
minimal forts and provided a few characterizations for such trees. In fact, graphs with cycles also attain the
n/3
lower bound. Furthermore, the structures of such graphs are much more varied. We provide three examples of graphs (each of order
n=9
) with exactly
3
minimal forts in Figure
5
. Each graph also exhibits the property that
\ft(G)=\zf(G)=n/3
. Indeed, for every graph where
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=n/3
, it was also observed that
\ft(G)=\zf(G)=n/3
. By virtue of Proposition
5.4
, we state this observation as follows.

Conjecture 6.3
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{G}
. If
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{G}\rvert=n/3
, then
\zf(G)=n/3
.

We have also shown that the converse of Conjecture
6.3
holds for trees. However, the converse is false in general: for example, the cycle graph on 6 vertices
C_{6}
has
\ft(C_{6})=\zf(C_{6})=2
, but
\lvert\mathcal{F}_{C_{6}}\rvert=5
.

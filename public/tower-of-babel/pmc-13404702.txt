Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We would like to thank the authors for their interest and for taking the time to express their observations in our research article, “A comparative study of retinal vein occlusion and serum 25 hydroxy Vitamin D at a tertiary care centre.”[1]

Our study participants were selected from the outpatient clinic of our institution over a period of 1½ years (from September, 2022 to March 2024), and a thorough general, systemic and complete ocular examination was done on the very first visit and in every subsequent follow-up. Although blood samples were collected once, appropriate treatment, including administration of anti-Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor injections, was done whenever required during the study period, and they are still under follow-up even after the study. Hence, the study design was stated as an institution-based prospective case–control study.

Risk factors include elderly age, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, positive history of smoking. The authors have expressed their interest to know how many patients with low serum Vitamin-D levels had one or more of these risk factors. Table 2 shows a comprehensive statistical analysis comparing the CONTROL and retinal vein occlusion (RVO) groups across linear regression and logistic regression models. The logistic regression models did not identify any statistically significant predictors for the outcome variable in either the CONTROL or the RVO group based on the standard P < 0.05 threshold. The predictors were total cholesterol, diastolic blood pressure, postprandial blood sugar, systolic blood pressure, low density lipoprotein. But in our study, statistically significant low levels of mean serum Vitamin D (25 OH vit D) were found in cases of RVO in comparison to age-matched controls with an odds ratio of 1.137; 95% confidence interval (1.074–1.203). Thus, the association of Vitamin D levels with RVO can be established.

We agree that the sample size is too small and the inclusion of serum homocysteine levels would have improved our study. Multiple risk factors, including hyper-homocystenemia, hypercoagulation disorders, protein C, S deficiency and many more, are associated with RVO, but tests which were available in our institution were only included in our study. We consider them to be a limitation in our study, and the inclusion of all the criteria, as pointed out, would surely improve our study.

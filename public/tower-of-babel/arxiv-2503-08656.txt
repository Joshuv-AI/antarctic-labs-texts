Smoothing effect for third order operators with variable coefficients
Serena Federico and Davide Tramontana

Abstract
. In this work we study the smoothing effect of some variable coefficient operators of the form D t − A D_{t}-A , where A A is a Weyl-quantized pseudo-differential operator of order m = 2 , 3 m=2,3 . The class under consideration includes, among others, KdV-type and ultrahyperbolic Schrödinger operators. We prove homogeneous and inhomogeneous smoothing estimates and use them to get well-posedness results for some NLIVPs with derivative nonlinearities. Finally, we investigate the so called non-trapping property of the bicharacteristic curves of the principal symbol of our operators.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this work is to investigate the validity of local smoothing estimates for third order variable coefficient operators of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle P(t,x,D_{t},D_{x})=D_{t}-A(x,D),

\quad D_{t}:=-i\partial_{t},\quad D_{x}=(D_{x_{1}},\ldots,D_{x_{n}}):=(-i\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,-i\partial_{x_{n}}),

where
A
is a pseudo-differential operator of order
m=2,3
, i.e. in the class
\Psi^{m}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, such that its Weyl symbol
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=S^{m}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be written as

(1.2)

\displaystyle a(x,\xi)=a_{m}(x,\xi)+a_{m-1}(x,\xi),

where
a_{j}\in S^{j}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
j=m-1,m,
and
a_{m}
is real valued.
We will assume that the real part
\mathrm{Re}(a)
of the symbol satisfies (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) below, that is, we require:

(i)

There exists
C>0
such that

(1.3)

\frac{1}{C}\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}\leq\lvert\nabla_{\xi}\mathrm{Re}(a)\rvert\leq C\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n};

(ii)

For all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
|\beta|\geq 1
, and for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, there exists
\lambda\in L^{1}([0,+\infty[)\cap C^{0}([0,+\infty[)
strictly positive and non-increasing such that

(1.4)

\lvert\partial^{\beta}_{x}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\mathrm{Re}(a)\rvert\lesssim\varepsilon\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-\lvert\alpha\rvert},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We will also assume that the imaginary part of the symbol, that is
\mathrm{Im}(a)=\mathrm{Im}(a_{m-1})\in S^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, satisfies the following smallness condition: for
\lambda\in L^{1}([0,+\infty[)\cap C^{0}([0,+\infty[)
as above, there exists
c_{0}>0
such that

|\mathrm{Im}(a_{m-1})(x,\xi)|\leq c_{0}\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)|\xi|^{m-1},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We recall that an equation exhibits a smoothing effect whenever its solution gains regularity with respect to the initial data (
homogeneous smoothing effect
) and/or with respect to the inhomogeneous term of the equation (
inhomogeneous smoothing effect
).
It is well-known that whenever a smoothing estimate is available for an evolution operator, then well-posedness results for the suitable corresponding nonlinear initial value problems (NLIVPs) can be established by means of standard techniques based on the contraction argument.
The suitable NLIVPs we refer to are those with derivative and polynomial nonlinearities, where the order of derivation involved depends on the order of the operator. Let us remark that while polynomial nonlinearities can also be handled by using the so-called Strichartz estimates, when derivatives appear in the nonlinear term the use of smoothing estimates becomes crucial.

Our interest in third-order operators of the form (
1.1
) is motivated by the applications to KdV-type operators with variable coefficients, possibly defined on
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 1
. Examples of operators of this type are, for instance, those studied in
[
11
]
and in
[
26
]
, which
also include the classical constant coefficient cases given by the (linear) KdV and ZK equations. The importance of such equations comes from their many applications in water waves, plasma physics, nonlinear optics and algebraic geometry, due to the large number of conservation laws and the behavior of the solutions as solitary waves for large
t
(see
[
36
]
,
[
31
]
and the references therein).

The problem we investigate here – the
local smoothing effect
for variable coefficient operators – was studied in
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
14
,
15
,
24
,
25
]
for operators as in (
1.1
) with
A
being an operator of order two instead of three, that is when
a=a_{2}+a_{1}
, with
a_{2}\in S^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
real valued and elliptic, and
a_{1}\in S^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In this framework,
homogeneous
local and microlocal smoothing estimates have been proved by Doi in
[
7
]
and by Craig, Kappeler and Strauss in
[
5
]
, respectively. By a result in
[
7
]
known as Doi’s lemma, Kenig et al. proved the general
inhomogeneous
smoothing effect for variable coefficient Schrödinger operators in
[
25
]
. In the same work, Kenig et al. established a version of Doi’s lemma for ultrahyperbolic operators, a lemma which they used to get the smoothing effect of variable coefficient ultrahyperbolic Schrödinger operators. In the context of Shrödinger equations with time-dependent variable coefficients, the smoothing effect, both homogeneous and inhomogeneous, was studied in
[
10
,
14
,
15
]
. As for KdV-type equations with variable coefficients, they have attracted lots of attention recently; some results can be found in
[
4
,
26
,
2
]
. In particular, in
[
26
]
the smoothing effect is studied in any space dimension for operators with constant coefficient leading part, while in
[
4
,
2
]
the smoothing effect in presence of a variable coefficients leading operator is studied in one-space dimension, and the gain of regularity is measured according to the degree of vanishing of the initial data at infinity, hence in suitable weighted Sobolev spaces. As for the local well-posedness of KdV-type equations with variable coefficients in one space-dimension, we wish to mention the recent paper
[
32
]
. Let us stress that the approach in
[
32
]
is completely different from ours, which is based on the use of smoothing estimates to handle derivative nonlinearities; however, on the other side, the approach in
[
32
]
allows to work in a low regularity regime, a problem we do not focus on here.

The main novelty of this paper – a part from the study of third-order operators with variable coefficients in the leading part of
A
in any space dimension – is the introduction of two conditions, (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), which are easy to verify and which guarantee the validity of smoothing estimates. More precisely, we will consider pseudo-differential operators
A\in\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for
m=2,3
, whose Weyl symbol
a
splits as
a=a_{m}+a_{m-1}
and satisfies (i) and (ii) above. Under these assumptions on
\mathrm{Re}(a)
– the splitting of the symbol and (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) for
\mathrm{Re}(a)
– and under the smallness assumptions on
\mathrm{Im}(a)
introduced above, then
D_{t}-A
has the desired

, that is the one stated in Theorem
1.1
. A crucial point in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is that
\mathrm{Re}(a)
is
admissible
(see Definition
3.3
), which we know to be true as a consequence of the validity of (
3.1
) and (
3.2

3.1
) and (

Let us point out that our notion of
admissibile symbol (or operator)
applies to symbols (or operators) of any order. This allows us to apply the admissibility to get smoothing estimates for (
1.1
) when
A
is of order
m=2,3,
and to recover the well-known results in
[
25
]
for Schrödinger and ultrahyperbolic Schrödinger operators with variable coefficients. Unfortunately, when
A
has order higher than three, we do not have at our disposal strong enough a priori estimates to conclude the expected smoothing result for
D_{t}-A
.

We wish to highlight that in the pioneering works
[
5
,
7
,
25
]
, the authors use different strategies to prove their results; however, a common requirement is the validity of the so-called
non-trapping condition
(see also
[
28
,
29
,
30
,
35
]
for different smoothing-type problems for variable coefficient Schrödinger operators related with the non-trapping properties). The latter is a requirement on the bicharacteristic curves of the principal symbol
a_{2}
, which, roughly speaking, must escape from any compact set (see Definition
6.3
). When the operator is elliptic and has variable coefficients, there are some conditions ensuring that the non-trapping condition holds.
When the operator is not elliptic and has variable coefficients, the behavior of the bicharacteristics is much more complicated and hard to predict, not to mention that these curves are difficult if not impossible to compute even in the second-order elliptic case (with variable coefficients, the constant coefficient case is trivial).
Because of the aforementioned reason, we did not rely on the strategies used in
[
7
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
25
]
based on geometric assumptions on the bicharacterics of the principal symbol, simply because we could not.
Nevertheless, even if we avoid direct assumptions on the bicharacteristics of the principal symbol of
A
in (
1.1
), of course there must be a relation between our conditions and the behavior of these curves.
It is important to point out that we work with non-elliptic operators in general, and all the results available in the literature related to the non-trapping properties hold for elliptic operators only. This motivated our investigation of the non-trapping properties of the ”elliptic part” of our operators. More precisely, we will prove that the so called
strongly elliptic points
(see Definition
6.1
) are
nontrapped
(see Definition
6.3
).
A direct consequence of our result, which holds under suitable conditions, is that the non-trapping property for globally elliptic operators of any order holds at any point of the phase space. As for the behavior of the bicharacteristic curves starting at non-strongly elliptic points, it is a phenomenon that is difficult to describe and that we intend to investigate in the future.

In this section, we recall the main properties of (global) pseudo-differential operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the asymptotic formulas building the Weyl pseudo-differential calculus. For a complete and detailed exposition of the topic, and for the proofs of the results presented hereafter, we refer the interested reader to

We start with the definition of the so-called
standard
symbol class
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=S_{1,0}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We say that
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, that is that
a
is a symbol of order
m
, if for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such that

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-|\alpha|},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Moreover,
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
equipped with the seminorms

(2.1)

\displaystyle\gamma_{k,m}(a)=\sup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq k}|(\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a)(x,\xi)|\langle\xi\rangle^{-m+|\alpha|},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

is a Frechét space.

Given a symbol
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),m\in\mathbb{R}
, we define a pseudo-differential operator associated with
a
by using some formulas called
quantization formulas
. Different quantizations, i.e. quantization formulas, lead – in general, except for symbols independent of
x
– to different operators. On the other hand, different symbols can give the same operator in different quantizations. The most popular and used quantizations are the Kohn-Nirenberg and the Weyl quantization, both belonging to the following one-parameter family of quantizations called
t
-quantizations

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Op}_{t}(a)u(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}a((1-t)x+ty,\xi)u(y)dyd\xi,\quad u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad t\in[0,1].

When
t=0
in the previous formula we get the Kohn-Nirenberg quantization of
a
(that we denote by
\mathrm{Op}(a)=\mathrm{Op}_{0}(a)
), while when
t=1/2
we get the Weyl one (that we denote by
\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}(a)=\mathrm{Op}_{1/2}(a)
).
In this work we will use the Weyl quantization, therefore we will deal with Weyl-quantized pseudo-differential operators.

Definition 2.2
.

We call
Weyl-quantized pseudo-differential operator
with symbol
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
, the oscillatory integral

\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}(a)u(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}a((x+y)/2,\xi)u(y)dyd\xi,\quad u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

In addition, we say that an operator
A
is a pseudo-differential operator of order
m
, that is
A\in\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, if there exists
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
A=\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}(a)
.

Our choice to use the Weyl quantization is motivated by the convenient properties of the Weyl pseudo-differential calculus, properties that we will briefly recall below. In order to distinguish the Weyl from the Kohn-Nirenberg symbol, we will refer to the symbol of a Weyl-quantized pseudo-differential operator
A
as the
Weyl symbol
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
A=\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}(a)\in\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

. Then the formal adjoint

of

belongs to the class
\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
A^{*}=\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}(\bar{a})
.
In particular, if
a

For the composition of Weyl-quantized pseudo-differential operators, the following asymptotic formula holds.

Now, we define the class of classical pseudo-differential operators which includes differential operators.

Definition 2.7
.

A function
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is called
positively homogeneous of order
m\in\mathbb{R}
if for all
\lambda>0

a(x,\lambda\xi)=\lambda^{m}a(x,\xi),\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}).

Definition 2.8
.

The space of homogeneous symbols of order
m
, denoted
S^{m}_{\mathrm{hom}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, is the space of all functions
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}))
, positively homogeneous of order
m
, such that, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-|\alpha|},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}),

for some constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
.

Definition 2.9
.

A symbol
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called
a classical symbol of order
m\in\mathbb{R}
if, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there exists a homogeneous symbol
a_{m-j}\in S^{m-j}_{\mathrm{hom}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(2.5)

a\sim\sum_{j}(1-\chi)a_{m-j},

that is, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

a-\sum_{j=0}^{N}(1-\chi)a_{m-j}\in S^{m-N-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where,
\chi=\chi(\xi)
is a fixed cut-off function which is identically
1
near 0, that is,
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\xi}^{n})
,
0\leq\chi\leq 1
and, for instance,
\chi\equiv 1
when
\lvert\xi\rvert\leq 1/2
and
\chi\equiv 0
when
\lvert\xi\rvert\geq 1
. If
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a classical symbol we write
a\in S^{m}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, when
a\in S^{m}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we say that
A=\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}(a)
is a
classical pseudo-differential operator
, and write
A=Op^{\mathrm{w}}(a)\in\Psi_{\mathrm{cl}}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Remark 2.10
.

The use of the cut-off function
\chi
in the previous definition is necessary. For a function to be a symbol we need the function to be
C^{\infty}
while, in general, a homogeneous function is not. However, since we are interested in the behavior of such functions for
\lvert\xi\rvert\geq 1
, we sometimes omit the cut-off function and, instead of (
2.5
), we write

a\sim\sum_{j}a_{m-j}.

Our next aim is to define the principal symbol of a pseudo-differential operator, which will lead to the notion of elliptic symbol and elliptic operator.
To define the principal symbol we need to recall that for all
m\in\mathbb{R}
there exists a map

\sigma_{m}:\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/S^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that

0\rightarrow\Psi^{m-1}\rightarrow\Psi^{m}\xrightarrow{\sigma_{m}}S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/S^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow 0

is a short exact sequence. Therefore, the image
\sigma_{m}(A)=[a]
, where
a
is the Weyl symbol of
A
(see for instance Hörmander
[
20
]
, p. 86).

Definition 2.11
.

Let
A\in\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
The principal symbol of
A
is defined as
\sigma_{m}(A)\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/S^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Remark 2.12
.

If
A=\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}(a)\in\Psi^{m}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a classical pseudo-differential operator, and

a\sim\sum_{j}a_{m-j},

(here we have used the convention in Remark
2.10
), then we identify the principal symbol of
A
with the leading part
a_{m}\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the asymptotic expansion.

Definition 2.13
.

A symbol
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be
(uniformly) elliptic
if there exist some constants
c,C>0
such that

\lvert\xi\rvert\geq C\Longrightarrow\lvert a(x,\xi)\rvert\geq c\langle\xi\rangle^{m}.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
A\in\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The operator
A
is said to be
(uniformly) elliptic
if its principal symbol
\sigma_{m}(A)
is elliptic.

Since we will refer to symbols in different quantizations, we want to stress that Definition
2.14
does not depend on the choice of the quantization.

We conclude this section with a few more definitions that will appear throughout the paper.

Definition 2.15
.

The Hamilton vector field
of
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as the unique vector field
H_{a}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

da(V)=\sigma(V,H_{a}),\quad\forall V\in T(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
\sigma
is the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The standard symplectic form
\sigma
, in coordinates

(x_{1},...,x_{n},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{n})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is given by
\sigma=\sum_{j}d\xi_{j}\wedge dx_{j}
, therefore the Hamilton vector field of
a
takes the form

H_{a}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{\xi_{j}}a\cdot\partial_{x_{j}}-\partial_{x_{j}}a\cdot\partial_{\xi_{j}}
. Hence, for every symbol
p

H_{a}p(x,\xi)=\{a,p\}(x,\xi),

where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
is the so-called
Poisson bracket
defined as

\{a_{1},a_{2}\}(x,\xi):=\nabla_{\xi}a_{1}(x,\xi)\cdot\nabla_{x}a_{2}(x,\xi)-\nabla_{x}a_{1}(x,\xi)\cdot\nabla_{\xi}a_{2}(x,\xi),\quad(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Definition 2.16
.

Let
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
H_{a}
be the Hamilton vector field of
a
. We call
bicharacteristic curves
of
a
the integral curves of
H_{a}
.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
A\in\Psi_{\mathrm{cl}}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a classical pseudo-differential operator of order
m
with principal symbol
a_{m}
, and let
V=\xi\cdot\partial_{\xi}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the radial vector field. The operator
A
is said to be of
real principal type
if the Hamilton vector field
H_{a_{m}}
never vanishes on
\{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\});\ a_{m}(x,\xi)=0\}
and does not have the radial direction there. In other words, there is no point
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\});\ a_{m}(x,\xi)=0\}
such that
H_{a_{m}}|_{(x_{0},\xi_{0})}=cV
, for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

3.
Admissible operators

The purpose of this section is to investigate the so-called
admissible operators
(see Definition
3.3
), that is, operators having symbols to which Lemma
3.1
below is applicable.
As we will see in Section
4
, in order to prove the smoothing effect for the evolution operator
D_{t}-A
, a fundamental role is played by the admissibility of the real part of the operator
A
.
This fact motivates our interest in these operators, especially in establishing sufficient conditions in order for an operator to be admissibile. The aforementioned sufficient conditions are suggested by the type of operators we are interested in, that is, operators having (possibly) a
real-valued symbol

a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying the following two conditions:

(i)

There exists
C>0
such that

(3.1)

\frac{1}{C}\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}\leq\lvert\nabla_{\xi}a\rvert\leq C\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(ii)

For all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
\lvert\beta\rvert\geq 1
, and for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small

(3.2)

\lvert\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a\rvert\lesssim\varepsilon\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-\lvert\alpha\rvert},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\lambda\in L^{1}([0,+\infty[)\cap C^{0}([0,+\infty[)
is a strictly positive and non-increasing function.

When the symbol is complex-valued, then (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are required on the real part, while the imaginary part will be assumed suitably small.

To make sense of
admissible
operators, we first focus on Lemma
3.1
in Subsection
3.1
. Then, in Subsection
3.2
, we define the admissible operators and prove that (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are sufficient conditions to have admissibility.

3.1.
A new version of Doi’s lemma

As already mentioned, here
we give a fundamental result to prove the smoothing effect of operators of the form (
1.1
) when the latter satisfy suitable assumptions, that is Lemma
3.1
. This will be a new version of the so-called Doi’s lemma (Lemma 2.3 in
[
7
]
), targeted at operators of order
m\geq 2
rather than of order
m=2
. Even if we will focus on the smoothing effect when
A
in (
1.1
) is of order two or three, hence when
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),m=2,3
, the result of this section is given for symbols
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
m\geq 2
.
The very first version of Lemma
3.1
for elliptic operators of order two is due to Doi in
[
7
]
, who used it to get the homogeneous smoothing estimates and the local well-posedness of the LIVP for variable coefficient Schrödinger operators. Doi’s lemma was then used in
[
25
]
to deal with the NLIVP, specifically to prove inhomogeneous smoothing estimates. In
[
25
]
the authors also derived a version of Doi’s lemma for second order ultrahyperbolic operators, which was used to get smoothing estimates for ultrahyperbolic Schrödinger operators. Our version of Doi’s lemma includes both the two aforementioned known cases.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
m\geq 2
, be real valued, and let
\lambda\in L^{1}([0,+\infty[)\cap C^{0}([0,+\infty[)
be a positive non-increasing function. Assume also that there exists a function
q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
satisfying the following two conditions.

(i) For each
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such that

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}q(x,\xi)\rvert\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{\alpha\beta}\langle x\rangle\langle\xi\rangle^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert},&\text{if}\,\,|\beta|=0,\\
C_{\alpha\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert},&\text{if}\,\,|\beta|\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(ii) There exist two constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

(3.5)

\displaystyle H_{a}q(x,\xi)\geq C_{1}\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}-C_{2},

for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Then, for all
C>0
there exists a real-valued symbol
p\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that

(3.6)

H_{a}p(x,\xi)\geq C\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}-C^{\prime},\quad\mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{all}\ (x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.

Let us first take
K\geq 1
such that
\lvert q(x,\xi)\rvert\leq K\langle x\rangle
for
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, extend
\lambda(t)=\lambda(0)
for
t\leq 0
, and define
\tilde{\lambda}(t):=\lambda(K^{-1}t-10)
(note that
\tilde{\lambda}(t)\leq\lambda(t)
for all
t\leq 0
).

By Lemma 3.1 in
[
7
]
we can find a nonnegative function
f\in C^{\infty}([0,+\infty[)
such that, for all
t\geq 0
,
\tilde{\lambda}(t)\leq f^{\prime}(t)
and

(3.7)

\lvert f^{(m)}(t)\rvert\leq C_{m}\Bigl(\tilde{\lambda}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{\lambda}(s)ds\Bigr)\,(1+t)^{-m}\leq C_{m}\Bigl(\lambda(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\lambda(s)ds\Bigr)\,(1+t)^{-m},

for some new suitable constant
C_{m}>0
.
Moreover, by the properties of
f
we have

(3.8)

f^{\prime}(\lvert q\rvert)\geq\lambda(K^{-1}\lvert q\rvert-10)\geq\lambda(\langle x\rangle-10)\geq\lambda(\lvert x\rvert),\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Now, we take
\varepsilon
sufficiently small to be fixed later, and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\phi(t)=0
, if
t\leq 1
,
\phi(t)=1
if
t\geq 2
, and
\phi^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}
. We set
\phi_{+}(t):=\phi(t/\varepsilon)
,
\phi_{-}(t):=\phi(-t/\varepsilon)
and
\phi_{0}:=1-\phi_{+}-\phi_{-}
, and define
\psi_{+},\psi_{-},\psi_{0}\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as

\psi_{0}(x,\xi):=\phi_{0}(q(x,\xi)/\langle x\rangle),\quad\psi_{+}(x,\xi):=\phi_{+}(q(x,\xi)/\langle x\rangle)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\psi_{-}(x,\xi):=\phi_{-}(q(x,\xi)/\langle x\rangle).

Since
\lambda\in L^{1}([0,+\infty[)
, by (
3.7
) and hypothesis
(i)
on
q
, for some
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0

\lvert\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}f(\lvert q(x,\xi)\rvert)\rvert\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert}\quad\text{on}\,\,\,\mathrm{supp}\,\psi_{+}\cup\mathrm{supp}\,\psi_{-}.

Next, we define
p\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as

(3.9)

p(x,\xi):=\frac{q(x,\xi)}{\langle x\rangle}\psi_{0}(x,\xi)+(f(\lvert q(x,\xi)\rvert)+2\varepsilon)(\psi_{+}(x,\xi)-\psi_{-}(x,\xi)),

and estimate
H_{a}p
from below.

First observe that, by hypotheses (i) and (ii) on
q
, and by choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we have

(3.10)

H_{a}\Bigl(\frac{q}{\langle x\rangle}\Bigr)=\frac{H_{a}q}{\langle x\rangle}-\frac{q}{\langle x\rangle}\frac{x\cdot\nabla_{\xi}a}{\langle x\rangle^{2}}\geq C_{1}^{\prime}\frac{|\xi|^{m-1}}{\langle x\rangle}-C_{2}^{\prime}\quad\text{on}\,\,\mathrm{supp}\,\psi_{0},

for some
C_{1}^{\prime},C_{2}^{\prime}>0
. Therefore, by the properties of
f
and the inclusions
\mathrm{supp}\,\phi^{\prime}_{+},\mathrm{supp}\,\phi^{\prime}_{-}\subseteq\mathrm{supp}\,\phi_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle H_{a}p

\displaystyle=H_{a}\Bigl(\frac{q}{\langle x\rangle}\Bigr)\psi_{0}+f^{\prime}(\lvert q\rvert)(H_{a}q)(\psi_{+}+\psi_{-})+\Bigl(f(\lvert q\rvert)+2\varepsilon-\frac{\lvert q\rvert}{\langle x\rangle}\Bigr)\Bigl(\phi_{+}^{\prime}(\frac{q}{\langle x\rangle})-\phi_{-}^{\prime}(\frac{q}{\langle x\rangle})\Bigr)H_{a}\Bigl(\frac{q}{\langle x\rangle}\Bigr)

(3.11)

\displaystyle\geq H_{a}\Bigl(\frac{q}{\langle x\rangle}\Bigr)\psi_{0}+f^{\prime}(\lvert q\rvert)(H_{a}q)(\psi_{+}+\psi_{-})-C_{3},

for some
C_{3}>0
.

To estimate the first term on the RHS (right-hand side) of (
3.11
) we use again (
3.10
), while for the second term on the RHS of (
3.11
) we use hypothesis (ii) and (
3.8
).
Putting everything together we obtain

H_{a}p\geq C_{4}\Big(\langle x\rangle^{-1}\psi_{0}+\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)(\psi_{+}+\psi_{-})\Big)\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}-C_{5},

for some new constants
C_{4},C_{5}>0
.

Finally, since
\lambda(t)=O(t^{-1})
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, we have
\langle x\rangle^{-1}\gtrsim\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)
, which yields

H_{a}p\geq C_{6}\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}-C_{5}

for some
C_{6}>0
. In conclusion, possibly rescaling the symbol
p
, we showed that for all
C>0
there exists a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that

H_{a}p\geq C\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}-C^{\prime},\quad\forall x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

∎

.

Note that when
m=2

are related with (A6) in

]
, but with a slightly different choice of

. As already mentioned, such a

, will play a central role to get the smoothing estimates in Section
4
.

Fact:

Our condition will be: there exists
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

(3.12)

\displaystyle H_{a_{3}}q+H_{a_{2}}q+\mathrm{Re}a_{1}\geq C_{1}|\xi|^{2}-C_{2},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

assuming the coefficients of
a_{2},a_{1}
very small wrt the coefficients of
a_{3}
.

Let us assume now that there exists a function
q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
satisfying the following two conditions.

(i) For each
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such that

(3.13)

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}q(x,\xi)\rvert\leq C_{\alpha\beta}\langle x\rangle\langle\xi\rangle^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert},

3.2.
Admissible operators and sufficient conditions for admissibility

We are finally ready to give a characterization of the operators to which Lemma
3.1
is applicable.
According to the following definition, such operators will be called
admissible
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a pseudo-differential symbol of order
m\geq 2
. We say that
a
is
admissible
if there exists
q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(i) For each
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such that

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}q(x,\xi)\rvert\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{\alpha\beta}\langle x\rangle\langle\xi\rangle^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert},&\text{if}\,\,|\beta|=0,\\
C_{\alpha\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert},&\text{if}\,\,|\beta|\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(ii) There exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

(3.16)

\displaystyle H_{a}q(x,\xi)\geq C_{1}\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}-C_{2},

for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We will call a function
q
satisfying (i) and (ii) above a
Gårding weight
for
a
and the constant
C_{1}
the corresponding
Gårding constant
.
Moreover, given a pseudo-differential operator
A
, we say that
A
is
admissible
if its symbol
a
is admissible.

In Proposition
3.7
below we will prove that (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are sufficient conditions for a pseudo-differential operator to be admissible.
These conditions will allow us to consider different situations and to prove that several operators of interest are admissible. In particular, the operators we have in mind are those used to define dispersive equations. The reader can notice that typical constant coefficient operators
A
on
\mathbb{R}_{x}^{n}
of order
m=2,3,
used to define dispersive equations
D_{t}-A
, fall into the cases covered by Proposition
3.7
.

Remark 3.4
.

Condition (
3.2
) corresponds to a smallness assumption on the symbol, which, in the case of partial differential operators, amounts to a sort of smallness of the operator’s coefficients, specifically of their derivatives. This requirement has to do with the behavior of the bicharacteristics of the principal symbol, as can be seen when
a
is of order two and is either elliptic (see
[
7
,
9
,
5
]
) or ultrahyperbolic (see
[
25
]
).
Of course, if the operator is a Fourier multiplier or, equivalently, its symbol is independent of the space variable
x
, then the smallness condition is trivially satisfied.

Remark 3.5
.

We wish to point out that operators satisfying condition (
3.1
) (in the classical case) are examples of operators of
real principal type
(see Definition
2.17
). Indeed, when
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has a real principal symbol
a_{m}
that satisfies (
3.1
), it is easy to check that
H_{a_{m}}
never vanishes on
\{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\});\ a_{m}(x,\xi)=0\}
and does not have radial direction.
These operators play a key role in the study of propagation of singularities and in solvability problems (see, for instance,
[
27
]
).

Remark 3.6
.

If
A
is an operator with constant coefficients, then (
3.2
) is trivially satisfied, so (
3.1
) guarantees the admissibility of
A
. Moreover, in such a case, Theorem
1.1
holds if the symbol of
A
is real and admissible. In turn, for operators with real symbol, the admissibility is a sufficient condition to have the smoothing effect.

Proposition 3.7
.

If
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
m\geq 2
, is a real valued symbol such that (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are satisfied, then
a
is admissible, i.e. there exists a function
q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
such that the following two conditions hold:

(i) For each
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such that

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}q(x,\xi)\rvert\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{\alpha\beta}\langle x\rangle\langle\xi\rangle^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert},&\text{if}\,\,|\beta|=0,\\
C_{\alpha\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert},&\text{if}\,\,|\beta|\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(ii) There exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

(3.19)

\displaystyle H_{a}q(x,\xi)\geq C_{1}\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}-C_{2},

for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

To prove the result it suffices to consider
q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined as

(3.20)

q(x,\xi):=2C_{1}C^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{-(m-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\partial_{\xi_{j}}a(x,\xi),

where
C
is the constant in (
3.1
).
Condition
(i)
in the statement is trivially satisfied by
q
as in (
3.20
), therefore we focus on showing that
(ii)
holds too with the same function
q
.

By
(
3.1
)
and
(
3.2
)

(3.21)

\displaystyle H_{a}q(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{\xi}a,\nabla_{x}q\rangle-\langle\nabla_{x}a,\nabla_{\xi}q\rangle

(3.22)

\displaystyle=2C_{1}C^{2}\lvert\nabla_{\xi}a(x,\xi)\rvert^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{-(m-1)}+2C_{1}C^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{-(m-1)}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}x_{j}\partial_{\xi_{k}}a(x,\xi)\partial_{\xi_{j}}\partial_{x_{k}}a(x,\xi)-\langle\nabla_{x}a,\nabla_{\xi}q\rangle

(3.23)

\displaystyle\geq 2C_{1}C^{2}\lvert\nabla_{\xi}a(x,\xi)\rvert^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{-(m-1)}-2C_{1}C^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{-(m-1)}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\lvert x_{j}\partial_{\xi_{k}}a(x,\xi)\partial_{\xi_{j}}\partial_{x_{k}}a(x,\xi)\rvert-\lvert\nabla_{x}a\rvert\lvert\nabla_{\xi}q\rvert

(3.24)

\displaystyle\geq 2C_{1}\lvert\xi\rvert^{2(m-1)}\langle\xi\rangle^{-(m-1)}-\varepsilon C^{\prime}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-1}

(3.25)

\displaystyle\geq(2C_{1}-\varepsilon C^{\prime\prime})\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}-C_{2},

for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for some
C^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime},C_{2}>0
. Finally, for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small

H_{a}q(x,\xi)\geq C_{1}\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}-C_{2}\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

which shows the result.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Note that, if
q
is a Gårding weight for
a
with corresponding Gårding constant
C_{1}>0
, then, for all
C_{2}>0
,
q^{\prime}=\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}}q
is a Gårding weight for
a
with associated Gårding constant
C_{2}
.

The following proposition provides a relation between
a
and its Gårding weight
q
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a real-valued symbol and
m\geq 2
. If
a
is admissible with Gårding weight
q
, then

(3.26)

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}a\cdot\nabla_{\xi}q\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{m-1},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.

Suppose, by contradiction, that for all
C_{0}>0
there esists
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\nabla_{x}a(x_{0},\xi_{0})\cdot\nabla_{\xi}q(x_{0},\xi_{0})>C_{0}\langle\xi_{0}\rangle^{m-1}.

Since
a
is a symbol of order
m\geq 2
and
q
satisfies hypothesis (
3.7
), for some
\bar{C},\bar{D}>0
we have

\nabla_{\xi}a\cdot\nabla_{x}q\leq\lvert\nabla_{\xi}a\cdot\nabla_{x}q\rvert\leq\lvert\nabla_{\xi}a\rvert\lvert\nabla_{x}q\rvert\leq\bar{C}\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}+\bar{D},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

and

H_{a}q(x_{0},\xi_{0})=\nabla_{\xi}a(x_{0},\xi_{0})\cdot\nabla_{x}q(x_{0},\xi_{0})-\nabla_{x}a(x_{0},\xi_{0})\cdot\nabla_{\xi}q(x_{0},\xi_{0})\leq(\bar{C}-C_{0})\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert^{m-1}+\bar{D}.

On the other hand, the admissibility of
a
gives

H_{a}q(x_{0},\xi_{0})\geq C\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert^{m-1}-D

for some
C,D>0
. Therefore, by choosing
C_{0}>0
sufficiently large, we reach the contradiction.
∎

In the next result, we establish a connection between the admissibility of the leading part of the symbol and that of the whole symbol.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a symbol of order
m\geq 2
of the form
a=a_{m}+a_{m-1}
, with
a_{j}\in S^{j}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
real-valued for
j=m-1,m
, and with
a_{m-1}
satisfying

(3.27)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}a_{m-1}|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-1}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-1-|\beta|},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

If
a
is admissible with Gårding weight
q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
a_{m}
is admissible with respect to the same Gårding weight
q
.

Proof.

Let
q
be a Gårding weight for
a
and
C
a Gårding constant relative to
q
.
Let also
D>0
be the constant such that

H_{a}q\geq C|\xi|^{m-1}-D,\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Due to the properties of the symbol
a_{m-1}
, there exist
C^{\prime},D^{\prime}>0
such that
H_{a_{m-1}}q\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{m-2}\leq C^{\prime}|\xi|^{m-2}+D^{\prime}
.
This property and the admissibility of
a
finally give

(3.28)

\displaystyle H_{a_{m}}q=H_{a}q-H_{a_{m-1}}q\geq C|\xi|^{m-1}-C^{\prime}|\xi|^{m-2}-(D+D^{\prime})\geq C^{\prime\prime}|\xi|^{m-1}-D^{\prime\prime},

for some
C^{\prime\prime},D^{\prime\prime}>0
, with
C^{\prime\prime}<C
, which concludes the proof.

∎

Remark 3.11
.

Proposition
3.10
shows that the admissibility of a classical real-valued symbol is strictly related to the admissibility of its principal part. However, the latter is not a sufficient condition for the admissibility. In contrast, it is, instead, obviously a necessary condition for the admissibility of classical symbols.

Next, we will show that if
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is such that
a=a_{m}+a_{m-1}
, with
a_{j}\in S^{j}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
real valued for all
j=m-1,m
, and if the symbol
a_{m}
is admissible, then
a
is also admissible provided that suitable smallness conditions are satisfied. This is proved in the following result.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a symbol of order
m\geq 2
of the form
a=a_{m}+a_{m-1}
, with
a_{j}\in S^{j}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
real valued for all
j=m-1,m
. If

(i)

a_{m}
is admissible with Gårding weight
q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

(ii)

a_{m-1}
satisfies

|\nabla_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}a_{m-1}(x,\xi)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-1}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-1-|\beta|},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

then
a
is admissible with respect to the same Gårding weight
q
.

Proof.

Recall that, by definition, a Gårding weight
q
satisfies the following:

for each
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such that

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}q(x,\xi)\rvert\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{\alpha\beta}\langle x\rangle\langle\xi\rangle^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert},&\text{if}\,\,|\beta|=0,\\
C_{\alpha\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert},&\text{if}\,\,|\beta|\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Hence, by hypothesis (ii),

(3.31)

\displaystyle|H_{a_{m-1}}q|

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{\xi}a_{m-1}\cdot\nabla_{x}q-\nabla_{x}a_{m-1}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}q|

(3.32)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-2},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

for some
C_{2}>0
.
Then, since
a_{m}
is admissible by (i), we have

(3.33)

\displaystyle H_{a}q=H_{a_{m}}q+H_{a_{m-1}}q\geq C|\xi|^{m-1}-C_{2}|\xi|^{m-2}-D-D^{\prime}\geq C^{\prime\prime}|\xi|^{m-1}-D^{\prime\prime},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

for some
C^{\prime},D^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime},D^{\prime\prime}>0
(
C^{\prime\prime}<C
), which shows the admissibility of
a
and concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

Proposition
3.12
shows that
small
perturbations of admissibile symbols are still admissible. Here
small
refers to the asymptotic smallness in space of lower order perturbations. Of course, one can also consider sufficiently small perturbations of order
m
.

Example 3.14

(Operators defining constant coefficient dispersive equations)

The easiest example of admissible operators

A=Op^{\mathrm{w}}(a)
with

Example 3.15

(Operators defining constant coefficients KdV-type operators)

Other examples of admissible operators with constant coefficients on

(3.34)

\displaystyle A_{1}(D)

\displaystyle=D^{3},\quad\text{when}\,\,n=1,

(3.35)

\displaystyle A_{2}(D)

\displaystyle=D_{1}(D_{1}^{2}+D_{2}^{2}),\quad\text{when}\,\,n=2,

(3.36)

\displaystyle A_{n}(D)

\displaystyle=D_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}D_{j}^{2},\quad\text{when}\,\,n\geq 3.

Example 3.16

(Operators defining variable-coefficients KdV-type operators)
.

More generally, Proposition
3.7
successfully applies to show the admissibility of operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(specifically to the real part of the symbol) of order three of the form of

below, that are used to define the KdV-type operators on

(see
[
11
]
)

(3.37)

A_{3}+X_{0}=X_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}^{\ast}X_{j}+X_{0},

where

,
k=0,...,n
, form an elliptic system of smooth real vector fields satisfying some

and ellipticity conditions. Note that these operators can have variable coefficients, so the smoothing effect of the possibly dispersive operator

(consider

) is not guaranteed by classical results in the constant coefficients case. Consequently, to be able to say something about the smoothing effect, if we assume (
3.2
) for
A_{3}=Op^{\mathrm{w}}(a_{3}+a_{2})

1.1
it suffices to have the admissibility of
\mathrm{Re}(a_{3}+a_{2})
and the smallness of

, which are provided by the following proposition.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
A(x,D)
a partial differential operator of the same form as
A_{3}
in (
3.37
) such that

iX_{j}=i\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ij}(x)D_{i},\quad j=0,\ldots,n,

form an elliptic system of smooth real vector fields. Let also
a=a_{3}+a_{2}\in S^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the Weyl symbol of
A
, where
a_{j}\in S^{j}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
j=2,3
, and
a_{3}
is real valued. If

(3.38)

\displaystyle a_{ij}\in C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,\,\text{and}\,\,|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}a_{ij}(x)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\langle x\rangle^{1+|\alpha|}},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\forall i,j=1,\ldots,n,

for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, there exist
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
C>0
such that

(3.39)

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{\xi_{k}}X_{1}(x,\xi)\rvert\geq C,\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

and

(3.40)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|a_{kj}(x)|^{2}<3C/(n-1),\forall j=2,\ldots,n,

then
\mathrm{Re}(a)
is admissible. Moreover, for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, there exists a positive constant
c_{0}<1
such that

(3.41)

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Im}(a_{2})(x,\xi)|\leq c_{0}\langle x\rangle^{-2}|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.

The proof is based on the use of Proposition
3.7
and Proposition
3.10
. We will first prove that the principal symbol
a_{3}
of
A
is admissible. Afterwards, we will show that
\mathrm{Re}(a)
, viewed as a perturbation of
a_{3}
, is admissible too, and that (
3.41
) holds.

Observe that, due to (
3.38
), we have that (
3.2
) is trivially satisfied by
a_{3}
. Hence, if we prove that
|\nabla_{\xi}a_{3}(x,\xi)|\gtrsim|\xi|^{2}
for all

, which amounts to (
3.1
), then by Proposition
3.7
we can conclude the admissibility of
a_{3}
as desired. Because of that, we now focus on showing the above property of
\nabla_{\xi}a_{3}
.

We denote by
X_{j}(x,\xi)
and
X_{j}^{\mathrm{w}}(x,\xi)
the Kohn-Nirenberg and the Weyl symbol of the homogeneous first order operator
X_{j}(x,D)
, respectively. In particular, by the change of quantization formula (see, for instance,
[
18
]
Theorem 2.4.1. and
[
13
]
Theorem 4.12) we have

(3.42)

\displaystyle X_{j}(x,\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad X_{j}^{\mathrm{w}}(x,\xi)=X_{j}(x,\xi)+d_{X_{j}},\quad\text{where}\quad d_{X_{j}}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}a_{ij}(x),

therefore the principal symbol
a_{3}
of
A
, which is the same in both quantizations, is given by

(3.43)

\displaystyle a_{3}(x,\xi)=X_{1}(x,\xi)\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}(x,\xi)^{2}.

Now, by using (
3.39
) and the smallness hypotheses on the coefficients of the vector fields
X_{j}
, and choosing
\delta>0
such that
(n-1)/(3C)<\delta<C/\sup_{x}\{|a_{kj}(x)|^{2},j=2,\ldots,n\}
(which is possible due to (

(3.44)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\xi}a_{3}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\geq|\partial_{\xi_{k}}a_{3}|

(3.45)

\displaystyle=\lvert 3\partial_{\xi_{k}}X_{1}(x,\xi)X_{1}(x,\xi)^{2}+\partial_{\xi_{k}}X_{1}(x,\xi)\sum_{j=2}^{n}X_{j}(x,\xi)^{2}+2\sum_{j=2}^{n}X_{1}(x,\xi)(\partial_{\xi_{k}}X_{j})(x,\xi)X_{j}(x,\xi)\rvert

(3.46)

\displaystyle\geq(3C-\frac{n-1}{\delta})X_{1}(x,\xi)^{2}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}(C-\delta|a_{kj}(x)|^{2})X_{j}(x,\xi)^{2}

(3.47)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}(x,\xi)^{2}\gtrsim|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

which gives (
3.1
) and hence the admissibility of
a_{3}
.

Next, to prove the admissibility of
\mathrm{Re}(a)
, it suffices to check the behavior of
\mathrm{Re}(a_{2})
, where
a_{2}\in S^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the symbol of order two satisfying

a=a_{3}+a_{2},

where
a
is the Weyl symbol of
A
.

Below, we shall write
X_{j}^{\mathrm{w}},X_{j}
, in place of
X_{j}^{\mathrm{w}}(x,\xi),X_{j}(x,\xi)
, when no confusion arises.
Then, dropping the dependence on
(x,\xi)
, the Weyl composition formula gives that

(3.48)

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{1}^{\mathrm{w}}\#X_{j}^{\mathrm{w}}\#X_{j}^{\mathrm{w}}

(3.49)

\displaystyle=X_{1}^{\mathrm{w}}\#\sum_{j=1}^{n}({X_{j}^{\mathrm{w}}}^{2}-\frac{i}{2}\{X_{j}^{\mathrm{w}},X_{j}^{\mathrm{w}}\}+r_{0})

(3.50)

\displaystyle=X_{1}^{\mathrm{w}}\#\left(\sum_{j=1}(X_{j}^{2}+2d_{X_{j}}X_{j})+r_{0}+d_{X_{j}}^{2}\right)

(3.51)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}\left(X_{1}\#X_{j}^{2}+d_{X_{1}}X_{j}^{2}+2d_{X_{j}}X_{1}X_{j}-\frac{i}{2}2\{X_{1},d_{X_{j}}X_{j}\}+2d_{X_{1}}d_{X_{j}}X_{j}-\frac{i}{2}\{d_{X_{j}},X_{j}^{2}\}+X_{1}\#(r_{0}+d_{X_{j}}^{2})\right)+r^{\prime}_{0}

(3.52)

\displaystyle=a_{3}-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\{X_{1},X_{j}^{2}\}+2id_{X_{1}}X_{j}^{2}+i4d_{X_{j}}X_{1}X_{j})+r_{1}+r^{\prime}_{0}

(3.53)

\displaystyle=a_{3}+a_{2},

where
r_{0}\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is given by

(3.54)

\displaystyle r_{0}

\displaystyle=2^{-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|+|\beta|=2\end{subarray}}\frac{(-i)^{|\alpha|+|\beta!}}{\alpha!\beta!}(-1)^{|\beta|}(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}X_{j}^{\mathrm{w}})(\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}X_{j}^{\mathrm{w}})

is a symbol of order 0 possibly different at any appearance. Note that both

and

are symbols of differential operators having coefficients depending on the derivatives of the vector field’s coefficients, that is, depending on

and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

Now, since

(3.56)

\displaystyle\lvert\nabla_{x}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\mathrm{Re}(a_{2})\rvert=\lvert\nabla_{x}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}r_{1}\rvert\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-1}\langle\xi\rangle^{1-|\beta|},\quad\forall\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n},

(3.57)

\displaystyle\lvert\mathrm{Im}(a_{2})\rvert=\left|-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\{X_{1},X_{j}^{2}\}+2\mathrm{Im}(d_{X_{1}})X_{j}^{2}+\mathrm{Im}(d_{X_{j}})X_{1}X_{j})\right|.

and (

Inequality (

To see this, it suffices to expand the Poisson brackets

As for the other terms in

Example 3.19

(Operators defining ultrahyperbolic Schrödinger equations)
.

Other interesting examples of admissible operators are given by ultrahyperbolic differential operators of order two, which were used in
[
25
]
to define ultrahyperbolic Schrödinger operators. A toy model of such operators is
\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}
, where, for
k\leq n
,
\Delta_{1}
is the (positive) Laplacian in the first group of
k
variables and
\Delta_{2}
is the (positive) Laplacian in the second group of
n-k
variables, that is the operator whose principal part’s coefficients are determined by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{I_{k}}&0\\
0&-\mathrm{I_{n-k}}\end{pmatrix}.

More generally, ultrahyperbolic differential operators of order two with variable coefficients are defined as

A=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}-\partial_{x_{i}}a_{ij}(x)\partial_{x_{j}},

where the coefficient matrix of the principal part
\mathsf{A}_{2}(x)=(a_{ij}(x))_{i,j=1,\dots,n}
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is real, symmetric and satisfies the non-degeneracy condition

(3.59)

\exists\nu>0\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\nu^{-1}\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\lvert\mathsf{A}_{2}(x)\cdot\xi\rvert\leq\nu\lvert\xi\rvert,\quad\forall x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Note that the symbol
a
of
A
, which belongs to
S^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, has the form

a(x,\xi)=a_{2}(x,\xi)+a_{1}(x,\xi)=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{\ell}}a_{\ell k}(x)\Bigr)\xi_{k},

where
a_{j}\in S^{j}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
j=1,2
. In addition, we assume the following smallness condition: there exists
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small such that, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
\lvert\beta\rvert\geq 1
,

(3.60)

\lvert\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a_{j}(x,\xi)\rvert\leq C_{\alpha\beta}\varepsilon\langle x\rangle^{-2}\lvert\xi\rvert^{j-\lvert\alpha\rvert},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\mathrm{for}\ j=1,2.

Since
\partial_{\xi_{j}}a_{2}(x,\xi)=2\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}=2(\mathsf{A}_{2}(x)\cdot\xi)_{j}
, for all
j=1,\dots,n
, by (
3.59
) we have

2\nu^{-1}\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\lvert\nabla a_{2}(x,\xi)\rvert\leq 2\nu\lvert\xi\rvert,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

which, in turn, shows that
a_{2}
is admissible by Proposition
3.7

4.
Smoothing estimates

In this section we prove the local smoothing effect for evolution equations with possibly variable coefficients defined through a class of operators of order
m=2,3,
having admissible real part. These operators include, for instance, those discussed in Subsection
3.3
.
More precisely, we focus on the study of the IVP

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-iAu=f\quad\quad\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\times]0,T[,\quad T>0,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}\quad\quad\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

where
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
f
has suitable regularity (see Theorem
1.1
), and
A=Op^{\mathrm{w}}(a)\in\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a pseudo-differential operator of order
m=2,3,
satisfying the following conditions:

•

The Weyl symbol
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of
A
is of the form

(4.2)

\displaystyle a(x,\xi)=a_{m}(x,\xi)+a_{m-1}(x,\xi),

where
a_{j}\in S^{j}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
j=m-1,m
, and
a_{m}
is real valued;

•

The real part
\mathrm{Re}(a)\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

satisfies (
3.1
) and (
3.2
);

•

The imaginary part
\mathrm{Im}(a)=\mathrm{Im}(a_{m-1})\in S^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies

(4.3)

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Im}(a_{m-1})(x,\xi)|\leq c_{0}\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)|\xi|^{m-1},\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

for some
c_{0}>0
and for
\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)
as in (
3.2
).

Note that conditions (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) ensure that
\mathrm{Re}(a)=a_{m}+\mathrm{Re}(a_{m-1})
is admissible with Gårding weight
q
and Gårding constant
C
, for some
C>0
and some
q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}q(x,\xi)\rvert\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{\alpha\beta}\langle x\rangle\langle\xi\rangle^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert},&\text{if}\,\,|\beta|=0,\\
C_{\alpha\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert},&\text{if}\,\,|\beta|\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

for every
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We emphasize that we are not assuming
a_{m},a_{m-1}
to be homogeneous symbols here. The key feature of the decomposition of
a
is that the pseudo-differential operator of order
m
in
A
, that is
Op^{\mathrm{w}}(a_{m})
, has a
real-valued
admissible symbol.

We also stress that, although the case
m=2
is already known in the literature (see
[
5
,
7
,
9
,
25
]
), we provide a new method of proof in this case. Our method relies on considering operators for which the real part of symbol,
\mathrm{Re}(a)
, satisfies (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) (see Remark
4.3
) and such that (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) hold. In other words, some of these conditions replace the nontrapping condition in
[
5
,
7
,
9
,
25
]
) (smallness conditions can be found in these works as well). As for the case
m=3
, it is completely new for operators with variable coefficients, while other variable coefficient cases have been proved in
[
26
]
via Fourier methods that cannot be applied in our framework.

One may wonder why we did not consider the general case
m\geq 2
here. The reason is that there is a non-trivial difference between the cases
m=2,3,
and
m>3
. This distinction is due to the fact that when
m>3
we are only able to prove smoothing estimates with a big ”error term”, which means that the estimates include an additional unpleasant term with respect to the standard ones. In contrast, when
m=2,3
, we arrive at the expected standard smoothing estimates.
A brief discussion of this topic can be found in Remark
4.4
below.

To prove Theorem

on the smoothing effect of

satisfying the aforementioned properties, we need Lemma

which provides (i)-(iii) of Theorem

\displaystyle\leq-C(\lambda(|x|)\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u,\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u)_{0}+C_{1}N(u)^{2}+2\mathrm{Re}(\Lambda^{s-2}f,\Lambda^{s-2}u)_{0}+\mathrm{Re}(E\Lambda^{s}f,E\Lambda^{s}u)_{0}

(4.43)

\displaystyle\leq-C(\lambda(|x|)\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u,\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u)_{0}+C_{1}N(u)^{2}+C_{2}N(f)N(u).

The last inequality implies that

(4.44)

\displaystyle 2N(u)\partial_{t}N(u)\leq C_{1}N(u)^{2}+C_{2}N(u)N(f),

which is equivalent to

(4.45)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(e^{-C_{1}t}N(u))\leq e^{-C_{1}t}C_{2}N(f).

Therefore, integrating in time and taking the supremum in
[0,T]
we get (i).

To prove (ii), we go back to (
4.43
) and we rewrite it as

(4.46)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(e^{-C_{1}t}N(u)^{2})\leq e^{-C_{1}t}\Big(-C(\lambda(|x|)\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u,\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u)_{0}+C_{2}N(f)\Big).

Then, integrating in time and using once more the equivalence of Sobolev norms, we get

(4.47)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u\|^{2}_{s}+\int_{0}^{T}(\lambda(|x|)\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u(t,\cdot),\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u(t,\cdot))_{0}\,dt\lesssim e^{C_{1}T}\left(\|u(0,\cdot)\|_{s}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{s}^{2}dt\right),

which proves part (ii) of the result.

To prove (iii) we use (
4.42
) and estimate
\mathrm{Re}(E\Lambda^{s}f,E\Lambda^{s}u)
as follows

(4.48)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(E\Lambda^{s}f,E\Lambda^{s}u)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Re}(\Lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}}E\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f,E\Lambda^{s}u)_{0}+\mathrm{Re}([E,\Lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}}]\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f,E\Lambda^{s}u)_{0}

(4.49)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Re}(E\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f,E\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u)_{0}+\mathrm{Re}(E\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f,[\Lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}},E]\Lambda^{s}u)_{0}

(4.50)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathrm{Re}([E,\Lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}}]\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f,E\Lambda^{s}u)_{0}

(4.51)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Re}(\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-\frac{1}{2}}E\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f,\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{\frac{1}{2}}E\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u)_{0}

(4.52)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathrm{Re}(\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-\frac{1}{2}}E\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f,\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{\frac{1}{2}}[\Lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}},E]\Lambda^{s}u)_{0}+

(4.53)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathrm{Re}(\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-\frac{1}{2}}[E,\Lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}}]\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f,\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{\frac{1}{2}}E\Lambda^{s}u)_{0}

(4.54)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\left\|\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-\frac{1}{2}}E\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f\right\|^{2}_{0}+\delta\left\|\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{\frac{1}{2}}E\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u\right\|_{0}^{2}+O(\left\|u\right\|_{s}^{2}).

Using (
4.54
) into (
4.42
) and the equivalence of
N(\cdot)
and
\|\cdot\|_{s}
, and choosing
\delta
sufficiently small, for
C,C_{1}
being new positive constants we obtain

(4.55)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}N(u)^{2}\leq

\displaystyle-C(\lambda(|x|)\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u,\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u)+C_{1}N(u)^{2}

(4.56)

\displaystyle+C_{2}\left\|\lambda(|x|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}E\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f\right\|_{0}^{2}+2\mathrm{Re}(\Lambda^{s-2}f,\Lambda^{s-2}u).

Moreover, since
s+\frac{m-1}{2}-4<s
, we have

(4.57)

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{Re}(\Lambda^{s-2}f,\Lambda^{s-2}u)=

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{Re}(\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f,\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}-4}u)\lesssim\|\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f\|_{0}^{2}+N(u)^{2},

consequently, for
C_{1},C_{2}
new suitable positive constants, we get

(4.58)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}N(u)^{2}\leq

\displaystyle-C(\lambda(|x|)\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u,\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u)+C_{1}N(u)^{2}+C_{2}\left\|\lambda(|x|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}E\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f\right\|_{0}^{2}.

From (
4.58
), integrating in time and using once again the equivalence of
N(\cdot)
and
\|\cdot\|_{s}
, we finally reach

(4.59)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u\|^{2}_{s}+\int_{0}^{T}(\lambda(|x|)\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u(t,\cdot),\Lambda^{s+\frac{m-1}{2}}u(t,\cdot))_{0}\,dt

(4.60)

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim e^{C_{1}T}\left(\|u(0,\cdot)\|_{s}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}(\lambda(|x|)^{-1}\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f(t,\cdot),\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f(t,\cdot))_{0}dt\right),

which concludes the proof (iii).

As for the proof of (i’), (ii’) and (iii’), we set
f=(\partial_{t}-iA)^{*}=-\partial_{t}+iA^{*}
and repeat all the calculations above with the suitable sign adjustment and with
u(t,x)
replaced by
u(T-t,x)
. Due to the flipped signs, we have to ensure that Lemma
3.1
still applies, that is, that
-\mathrm{Re}(\bar{a})
satisfies condition (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) (recall that we are working with Weyl symbols, hence
-A^{*}=\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}(-\bar{a})
). Since this is always the case due to the fact that
\mathrm{Re}(a)
satisfies such conditions, we can conclude the proof as in the cases (i), (ii), (iii).
∎

We now use the a priori estimates of Lemma
4.1
to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Step 1: Uniqueness
.
To prove the uniqueness it suffices to note that, if
u_{1},u_{2}\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
are solutions to (
4.1
), then
v:=u_{1}-u_{2}\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap C^{1}([0,T];H^{s-m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
solves (
4.1
) with
f=0
and
u_{0}=0
. Thus, by Lemma
4.1
-(i), we have
v=0
.

Step 2: Existence and smoothing estimates
. To prove (i), (ii), and (iii) we start with the analysis of the case
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
.
Under these hypotheses, we take
P:=\partial_{t}-iA
and define the subspace
W\subseteq L^{1}([0,T];H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
as

W=\{\psi\in L^{1}([0,T];H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}));\;\exists\,\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T[\times\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;\mathrm{s.t.}\;\psi=P^{\ast}\varphi\}.

We consider the linear functional
\ell:W\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by

\ell(\psi):=\int_{0}^{T}(f(t,\cdot),\varphi(t,\cdot))_{0}\,dt+(u_{0},\varphi(0,\cdot))_{0},

where
\varphi
satisfies
\psi=P^{\ast}\varphi
.
Note that
\varphi
is uniquely determined due to point (i)’ of Lemma
4.1
and the fact that
\varphi_{1}(T,\cdot)=\varphi_{2}(T,\cdot)=0
.
To see that
\ell
is continuous, we use Lemma
4.1
-(i)’ on
\varphi
with
s
replaced by
-s
, which gives

\begin{split}\lvert\ell(\psi)\rvert&\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}([0,T]);H_{x}^{s})}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\varphi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H_{x}^{-s}}+\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\left\|\varphi(0,\cdot)\right\|_{H_{x}^{-s}}\\
&\leq C_{2}e^{C_{1}T}\Bigl(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}([0,T]);H_{x}^{s})}+\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\Bigr)\|\psi\|_{L^{1}([0,T]);H_{x}^{-s})}.\end{split}

By Hahn-Banach’s theorem we can extend the functional to
L^{1}([0,T];H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Then, by Riesz’s theorem we have the existence of
u\in(L^{1}([0,T];H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))^{\ast}=L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
such that, for
\psi=P^{\ast}\varphi
,

(4.61)

\ell(\psi)=(u,P^{\ast}\varphi)_{0}=\int_{0}^{T}(f(t,\cdot),\varphi(t,\cdot))_{0}\,dt+(u_{0},\varphi(0,\cdot))_{0},

and thus
Pu=f
in
\mathscr{D^{\prime}}(]0,T[\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, since
Pu=\partial_{t}u-iAu=f
and
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
, we have that
\partial_{t}u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s-m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and so
u\in C([0,T];H^{s-m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Using the equation once more, we also get
u\in C^{1}([0,T];H^{s-2m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Since by (
4.61
)

\int_{0}^{T}(u(t,\cdot),(\partial_{t}-iA)^{*}\varphi(t,\cdot))_{0}\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}(f(t,\cdot),\varphi(t,\cdot))_{0}\,dt+(u_{0},\varphi(0,\cdot))_{0},

we have
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}
. Finally, using the previous argument with
s+2m
in place of
s
, we obtain a solution of the
IVP

(
4.1
)
for which the estimates in Lemma
4.1
hold under the hypotheses
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})
.

Now, we use the result in the case
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})
to prove the cases (i), (ii), and (iii).

Proof of (i).
Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\in L^{1}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. In this case, by density, there exist two sequences
(f_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
and
(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

and

as

. Therefore, due to what we have proved above in the case when the initial datum and the source terms are in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for all

there exists a solution
u_{j}
of
(
4.1
)
with initial datum
v_{j}
and source term
f_{j}
which satisfies Lemma
4.1
-(i). Since
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence, then
u:=\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}u_{j}
solves (
4.1
) with initial datum
u_{0}
and source term
f
and satisfies estimate (i) of Lemma
4.1
. This proves point (i) of the theorem.

Proof of (ii)
. Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\in L^{2}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. To prove this part we proceed as in the proof of (i), but considering
(f_{j})_{j}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
such that
f_{j}\rightarrow f
in
L^{2}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
as

, and using (ii) of Lemma
4.1
instead of (i).

Proof of (iii)
Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-1}dxdt)
. By the boundedness theorem for pseudo-differential operators, it is possible to prove that there exists a sequence
(g_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})
such that
g_{j}\rightarrow\Lambda^{s-\frac{m-1}{2}}f
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-1}dxdt)
as

. Then, applying the same argument used in the proof of (i) with
f_{j}
replaced by
\Lambda^{-s+\frac{m-1}{2}}g_{j}
, and using part (iii) of Lemma

, we finally get the proof.

Remark 4.4

(Smoothing estimates for operators of order

)
.

Given a pseudo-differential operator

of order

, one hopes to prove the same smoothing effect for the solution of the corresponding IVP (

5.
Well-posedness for the NLIVP for KdV-type operators with variable coefficients

In this section we focus on the well-posedness of the NLIVP

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-iAu-N(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)=0\quad\quad\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\times]0,T[,\quad T>0,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}\quad\quad\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

where
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
N=N(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)
is a polynomial with no linear or constant terms, and
A=Op^{\mathrm{w}}(a)
is a pseudo-differential operator of order
3
whose Weyl symbol
a\in S^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), and such that
\mathrm{Re}(a)
satisfies (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).
Moreover, we will consider nonlinear terms of the form

(5.2)

N(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)=u^{p}\bar{u}^{q}D^{\alpha}u,

with
p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
(p,q)\neq(0,0)
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
such that
\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq 2
.

Remark 5.1
.

Even if we focus on
N(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)
as in (
5.2
), it is possible to consider nonlinearities defined as a finite linear combination of terms of the form (
5.2
). We chose not to focus on this more general case, as it does not introduce any additional difficulties in the proof; rather, it would merely require repeating the arguments used for a single term of the form (
5.2
).

Remark 5.2
.

Here we devote our analysis to the case
m=3
instead of
m=2,3
.
However, by repeating the same classical techniques employed below for the case
m=3
, all the results continue to hold for operators of order two. Since there is no difference in the proofs when
m=2
and
m=3
, and since the case
m=2
has been extensively treated in
[
25
]
, we focus on the open problem
m=3
.

Remark 5.3
.

Since we work with operators of order three, in view of the
inhomogeneous smoothing effect
it is possible to consider nonlinearities that involve derivatives of order two. This is due to the fact that
by Theorem
1.1
the solution of dispersive equations of order
m=3
, with possibly variable coefficients, gains
m-1=2
derivatives (in the suitable sense) with respect to the inhomogeneous term of the equation. This fact is well-known in the third-order constant coefficients case (see
[
3
,
23
]
) and when the third-order principal part has constant coefficients (see
[
26
]
), but it is new when the variable coefficients appear in the principal part.
Note finally that nonlinearities of the form (
5.2
) include the classical ones
N(u)=\pm u\lvert u\rvert^{2k}
and
N(u,\bar{u},\nabla u,\nabla\bar{u})=\pm\nabla u\cdot u^{2k}
, with
k\geq 1
, both when
m=2
and
m=3
. These nonlinearities were treated in
[
15
]
for Schrödinger equations with time-dependent coefficients. Even if we do not examine the space-time dependent coefficients case in this work, it can be approached by combining our techniques with those in
[
15
]
.

Remark 5.4
.

We remark that our general nonlinearity allows us to include in our class of problems the
k-generalized KdV equation
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
, that is

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u+u^{k}\partial_{x}u=0,&\mathrm{on}\quad[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\,\,\,\,(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})\quad(\textit{k-generalized KdV})\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}&\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

where the admissible operator
A
here is
A=D_{x}^{3}
, and the
k
-generalized ZK equation
, that is

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x_{1}}\Delta_{x}u+u^{k}\partial_{x_{1}}u=0,&\mathrm{on}\quad[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\,\,\,\,(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+})\quad(\textit{k-generalized ZK})\\

where the admissible operator

here is

. When

In order prove the well-posedness of the NLIVP (
5.1
), we use the standard contraction argument.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The first step is to reduce our problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-iAu-N(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)=0\quad\quad\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\times]0,T[,\quad T>0,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}\quad\quad\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

to an equivalent one.
To do that, we rewrite
N
as

(5.7)

\displaystyle N=N(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)

\displaystyle=u^{p}\bar{u}^{q}D^{\alpha}u

(5.8)

\displaystyle=(u^{p}\bar{u}^{q}-u_{0}^{p}\bar{u}_{0}^{q})D^{\alpha}u+u_{0}^{p}\bar{u}_{0}^{q}D^{\alpha}u

(5.9)

\displaystyle=:\widetilde{N}(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)+u_{0}^{p}\bar{u}_{0}^{q}D^{\alpha}u,

incorporate the last term on the RHS of (
5.9
) to the operator
A
, and call the resulting operator
\tilde{A}
. With the above notations, our NLIVP becomes equivalent to

(5.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u-i\tilde{A}u-\widetilde{N}(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)=0&\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\times]0,T[,\quad T>0,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}&\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

where
\tilde{A}
, due to the property

, is still an operator of order three with an admissible real part and for which the real and imaginary parts of the symbol satisfy the smallness assumptions (
3.2
) and (
4.3
), respectively.
Therefore, by Theorem
1.1
there exists a unique solution of the associated linear initial value problem (see also Remark
4.2
).

Next, we want to solve the integral equation

(5.11)

u=W(t)\,u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}W(t-t^{\prime})\tilde{N}(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)\,dt^{\prime},

where here
W(t)u_{0}
is the solution at time

of the linear homogeneous equation

(5.12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u-i\tilde{A}u=0&\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\times]0,T[,\quad T>0,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},&\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

Of course
u
solves (
5.11
) if and only if it solves (
5.1
).
To solve (
5.11
) and show that the solution has the desired properties, for
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\geq n+4N+5
,
\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)=\langle x\rangle^{-N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
N>1
, and
T>0
to be chosen, we define the Banach space

\begin{split}X_{T}^{s}:=\Bigl\{u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C};\ &\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}<+\infty,\;\Bigl(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\Lambda^{s+1}u\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt\Bigr)^{1/2}<+\infty,\\
&\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-1}u\right\|_{H^{s-2N-5}_{x}}<+\infty,\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-1}\partial_{t}u\right\|_{H_{x}^{s-2N-5}}<+\infty\Bigr\},\end{split}

with norm

\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{X_{T}^{s}}:=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\Lambda^{s+1}u\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-1}u\right\|_{H^{s-2N-5}_{x}}^{2}+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-1}\partial_{t}u\right\|_{H_{x}^{s-2N-5}}.

Also, we define the map
\Phi_{u_{0}}:X^{s}_{T}\longrightarrow L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s}
as

\Phi_{u_{0}}(u)(t):=W(t)\,u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}W(t-t^{\prime})\tilde{N}(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)\,dt^{\prime},

and call
B_{R}\subset X_{T}^{s}
the ball of radius
R
centered at
0
in
X_{T}^{s}
. To prove the result, it suffices to use the fixed point theorem on the map
\Phi_{u_{0}}
restricted to
B_{R}
, where
R
will be fixed later.
To apply the contraction argument, the first step is to prove that the map
\Phi_{u_{0}}
sends
B_{R}
into itself, that is
that
\left\|\Phi_{u_{0}}(u)\right\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\leq R
for all
u\in B_{R}
, while the second step will be to prove that the map is a contraction on

. This will allow us to conclude the first part of the theorem, while the continuity property in the second part of the theorem will be proved separately.
Since
v:=\Phi_{u_{0}}(u)
solves the linear problem

(5.13)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v-i\tilde{A}v=\widetilde{N}(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)&\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\times]0,T[,\quad T>0,\\
v(0,\cdot)=u_{0}&\mathrm{on}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

then to estimate
\left\|v\right\|_{X^{s}_{T}}:=\left\|\Phi_{u_{0}}(u)\right\|_{X^{s}_{T}}
we can use the linear smoothing estimates in Theorem
1.1
.

We first prove that the map

\begin{split}\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{X_{T}^{s}}&:=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\Lambda^{s+1}v\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-1}v\right\|_{H^{s-2N-5}_{x}}^{2}+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-1}\partial_{t}v\right\|_{H_{x}^{s-2N-5}}^{2}\\

we estimate terms (I)-(IV) separately.

For

, by the smoothing effect in Theorem

(I)+(II)\lesssim\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda(\lvert x\rvert)^{-1}\lvert\Lambda^{s-1}\tilde{N}(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Since

\begin{split}\Lambda^{s-1}\tilde{N}(u,\bar{u},D^{\alpha}u)&=\Lambda^{s-1}(u^{p}\bar{u}^{q}-u_{0}^{p}\bar{u}_{0}^{q})(D^{\alpha}u)\\

6.
Non-trapping for some admissible operators

Here we investigate the so called
nontrapping
properties of a class of operators of order
m
with principal symbol of the form (
6.1
). The nontrapping property of an operator
A
, or, equivalently, of its principal symbol
a_{m}
, at a point
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
of the phase space, refers to the following behavior of the bicharacteristics: the bicharacteristic curves of the principal symbol
a_{m}
of
A
starting from
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
escape from any compact set (see Definition
6.3
).

Smoothing estimates for variable coefficient operators of order two, that is for Schrödinger and ultrahyperbolic Schrödinger operators with variable coefficients, are strongly related to the non-trapping property of the principal symbol of the space-dependent leading order operator
A
in
D_{t}-A
.
In
[
7
]
and
[
25
]
the
nontrapping condition
, which amounts to the non-trapping property of
A
at each point of the cotangent space, is used to prove the suitable versions of Lemma
3.1
, which, in turn, allowed the authors to establish the smoothing estimates contained therein. Moreover, even for the microlocal smoothing estimates derived in
[
5
]
, which are proved by using a different strategy than that in
[
7
]
, the nontrapping condition is again a fundamental requirement to get the result.

In the other direction, that is whether smoothing estimates for
D_{t}-A
imply nontrapping properties of
A
, a positive result was established by Doi in
[
8
]
when
A
is an elliptic operator of order two with a real homogeneous symbol
a\in S^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
More precisely, Doi showed that if the microlocal smoothing estimate for
D_{t}-A
holds at a point
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
of the cotangent space, then the point is nontrapped. We remark that study of the set of trapped points
S^{A}_{\mathrm{trap}}
(see Definition
6.3
), for a given operator
A
, is of interest independently of its implications in the validity of smoothing estimates. In this respect, a result is due to Doi in
[
7
]
, who studied
S^{A}_{\mathrm{trap}}
when
A
is elliptic of order two without assuming any smoothing-type property for
D_{t}-A
.
Doi’s result (see Lemma 1.3 in
[
7
]
) shows that if the (real) principal symbol
a_{2}
of
A\in\Psi_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies suitable conditions (additional to ellipticity), then the set
S^{A}_{\mathrm{trap}}
is compact.

Note that all the results listed above refer to the case
A\in\Psi_{\mathrm{cl}}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
m=2
. In fact, for operators of order
m\geq 3
, even elliptic ones, there is no result giving information about
S^{A}_{\mathrm{trap}}
. In general, when the operator has variable coefficients, it is very hard to describe the behavior of the bicharacteristics, which is the main reason why we avoided any use of these objects in our previous analysis. Indeed, our smoothing result in Section
4
does not rely on the non-trapping condition
S^{A}_{\mathrm{trap}}=\emptyset
, since our Lemma
3.1
is not based on this property.
Nevertheless, due to the aforementioned connection between non-trapping conditions and smoothing effect, and to the lack of results on the non-trapping properties of operators of order greater than two at any or some point, even elliptic ones, we decided to investigate this problem here.

We shall consider
A=\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}(a)\in\Psi_{\mathrm{cl}}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
m\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
its principal symbol
a_{m}
is of the form

(6.1)

a_{m}(x,\xi)=\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert=m}a_{\alpha}(x)\xi^{\alpha},

with
a_{\alpha}\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
\lvert\alpha\rvert=m
.

Our goal is to show that under our conditions (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) on
a_{m}
, the nontrapping property of the so called
strongly elliptic points
of
A
(see Definition
6.1
) holds; in other words, the bicharacteristic curves of the principal symbol
a_{m}
of
A
starting from such points escape from any compact set.

Before going into details, we first fix the notation. For
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
, let

starting from
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
(see Definition
2.16
), where

is the maximal interval. We shall denote such a curve by
(x(t;x_{0},\xi_{0}),\xi(t;x_{0},\xi_{0}))
(or simply

) and say that it is

.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
A\in\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We define

(6.2)

C^{-1}|\xi(t)|^{m}\leq|a_{m}(x(t),\xi(t))|\leq C|\xi(t)|^{m},\quad\forall t\in I.

Remark 6.2

Note that, by Definition

(where here

For the reader’s convenience, we recall here the definition of

(by the bicharacteristics of the principal symbol
a_{m}
), if
(x(t;x_{0},\xi_{0}),\xi(t,x_{0},\xi_{0}))
is globally defined, and

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\lvert x(t;x_{0},\xi_{0})\rvert=+\infty\quad(\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\lvert x(t;x_{0},\xi_{0})\rvert=+\infty).

A point
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is said to be
nontrapped
if it is either forward or backward nontrapped. Finally, we say that a point is
trapped
if it is not nontrapped, and we denote by

S^{A}_{\mathrm{trap}}=\Bigl\{(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\});\ (x_{0},\xi_{0})\ \mathrm{is}\ \mathrm{trapped}\Bigr\}.

Remark 6.4
.

Note that a point
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in S^{\ast}\mathbb{R}^{n}
is nontrapped if for every
R>0
, there exists
t_{R}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lvert x(t_{R};x_{0},\xi_{0})\rvert\geq R.

Our goal is now to prove the following theorem showing a non-trapping property at
strongly elliptic points
for operators (not necessarily elliptic) whose principal symbol satisfies (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) .

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
A\in\Psi_{\mathrm{cl}}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
m\in\mathbb{N},
be such that its principal symbol
a_{m}
is of the form (
6.1
) and satisfies (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). Then, if
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in S_{A}^{\ast}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a strongly elliptic point, the bicharacteristic curve
\gamma_{(x_{0},\xi_{0})}
is globally defined (i.e.
I=\mathbb{R}
) and
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\notin S^{A}_{\mathrm{trap}}
.

Proof.

We start the proof by showing that the bicharacteristic curve starting from a strongly elliptic point
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in S^{\ast}_{A}\mathbb{R}^{n}
is globally defined. To see this it suffices to note that
a_{m}
is constant and satisfies (
6.2
) on
S^{\ast}_{A}\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consequently,
1/c\leq\lvert\xi(t)\rvert\leq c
for all
t\in I
, for some constant
c=c(x_{0},\xi_{0})>0
. Thus, using a Gronwall-type argument, we can conclude that the bicharacteristic curve starting from
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
is globally defined.

We now show that
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
is nontrapped. We define
q_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
as

(6.3)

q_{\delta}(x,\xi):=\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}^{-(m-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\partial_{\xi_{j}}a_{m}(x,\xi),

where
\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}:=(\delta+\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})^{1/2}
for some
\delta>0
sufficiently small to be chosen.

By the fundamental theorem of integral calculus we have

(6.4)

q_{\delta}(x(t),\xi(t))=q_{\delta}(x_{0},\xi_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}H_{a_{m}}q_{\delta}(x(s),\xi(s))\,ds.

Hence, by repeating the argument in (
3.25
),

(6.5)

H_{a_{m}}q_{\delta}(x,\xi)\geq 2C_{1}\lvert\xi\rvert^{2(m-1)}\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}^{-(m-1)}-\varepsilon C^{\prime}\langle\xi\rangle^{2(m-1)}\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}^{-(m-1)}-\varepsilon C^{\prime\prime}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-1}

for some
C_{1},C^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime}>0
universal constants.

When
m=1
then, for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, we have

H_{a_{m}}q_{\delta}(x,\xi)\geq C,

for some
C>0
. Therefore, when
m=1
,

\langle x(t)\rangle\gtrsim\lvert q_{\delta}(x(t),\xi(t))\rvert\gtrsim q_{\delta}(x_{0},\xi_{0})+Ct,\quad\forall t\geq 0,

which shows that
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
is nontrapped.

For
m>1
, we exploit the following inequalities (see Lemma
A.3
in the Appendix)

(6.6)

\displaystyle\langle\xi\rangle^{m-1}_{\delta}\leq

\displaystyle\langle\xi\rangle^{m-1}\leq 2^{\frac{m-1}{2}}|\xi|^{m-1}+2^{\frac{m-1}{2}},\quad\forall\delta\in(0,1],

(6.7)

\displaystyle\langle\xi\rangle^{-(m-1)}_{\delta}|\xi|^{2(m-1)}\geq

\displaystyle C_{3}|\xi|^{m-1}-C_{4}\delta^{\frac{m-1}{2}},\quad\forall\delta\in(0,1],\quad\text{for some}\,\,C_{3},C_{4}>0,

and get, for some new positive constants
C,C^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime},D,D^{\prime},D^{\prime\prime}
,

(6.8)

\displaystyle H_{a_{m}}q_{\delta}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\geq C|\xi|^{m-1}-D\delta^{\frac{m-1}{2}}-\varepsilon C^{\prime}|\xi|^{m-1}-\varepsilon D^{\prime}-\varepsilon C^{\prime\prime}|\xi|^{m-1}-\varepsilon D^{\prime\prime}

(6.9)

\displaystyle=(C-\varepsilon(C^{\prime}+C^{\prime\prime}))|\xi|^{m-1}-(D\delta^{\frac{m-1}{2}}+\varepsilon(D^{\prime}+D^{\prime\prime})).

Then, by applying (
6.9
) into (
6.4
), we obtain

q_{\delta}(x(t),\xi(t))\geq q_{\delta}(x_{0},\xi_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}((C-\varepsilon(C^{\prime}+C^{\prime\prime}))\lvert\xi(s)\rvert^{m-1}-(D\delta^{\frac{m-1}{2}}+\varepsilon(D^{\prime}+D^{\prime\prime}))ds.

Now we choose
\varepsilon
sufficiently small so that
C-\varepsilon(C^{\prime}+C^{\prime\prime})>0
.
Since
|a_{m}(x_{0},\xi_{0})|=|a_{m}(x(s),\xi(s))|
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that
(x(s),\xi(s))\in S^{\ast}_{A}\mathbb{R}^{n}
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and that

(6.10)

\displaystyle((C-\varepsilon(C^{\prime}+C^{\prime\prime}))\lvert\xi(s)\rvert^{m-1}-(D\delta^{\frac{m-1}{2}}+\varepsilon(D^{\prime}+D^{\prime\prime}))

(6.11)

\displaystyle\geq(C-\varepsilon(C^{\prime}+C^{\prime\prime}))c^{-(m-1)/m}|a_{m}(x(0),\xi(0))|^{(m-1)/m}-(D\delta^{\frac{m-1}{2}}+\varepsilon(D^{\prime}+D^{\prime\prime})),

for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Finally, choosing
\delta
and
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we can conclude that there exists
\mu>0
such that

\langle x(t)\rangle\gtrsim\lvert q_{\delta}(x(t),\xi(t))\rvert\gtrsim q_{\delta}(x_{0},\xi_{0})+\mu t,\quad\forall t\geq 0,

which shows that
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
is nontrapped and concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Technical lemmas

Lemma A.1
.

Let
p\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\begin{split}\langle x\rangle^{2N}\mathrm{Op}(p)f&=\mathrm{Op}(p)\langle x\rangle^{2N}f+2N\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{Op}(i\partial_{\xi_{j}}p)x_{j}\langle x\rangle^{2N-2}f\\
&+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\alpha+\beta\rvert\leq N,\\
\lvert\alpha\rvert\geq 2,\lvert\beta\rvert\leq 2N-2\end{subarray}}C_{\alpha\beta}\mathrm{Op}(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}p)x^{\beta}f,\quad\quad f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).\end{split}

Lemma A.2
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose
\langle x\rangle^{2N}u_{0}\in H^{s+2N}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then, there exist
c_{1},\dots,c_{2N},c>0
constants, such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\langle x\rangle^{2N}W(t)u_{0}\right\|^{2}_{H_{x}^{s}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{2N}c_{j}T_{j}\left\|\langle x\rangle^{2N-J}u_{0}\right\|_{H_{x}^{s+j}}^{2}

and

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\langle x\rangle^{2N}W(t)u_{0}\right\|^{2}_{H_{x}^{s}}\leq c(1+T^{2N})\left\|\langle x\rangle^{2N}u_{0}\right\|_{H_{x}^{s+2N}}^{2},

where
W(t)
denotes the solution operator of the linear homogeneous initial value problem (
5.12
).

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\delta\in[0,1[
and define
\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}:=(\delta+\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})^{1/2}
. Then, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
c\in(0,1)
and
c_{1}>1
such that

(A.1)

\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2(m-1)}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{m-1}_{\delta}}\geq c\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}-c_{1}^{\frac{m}{2}}\delta^{\frac{m-1}{2}},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.

First, our goal is to prove that for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
R>1
, we have

(A.2)

\lvert\xi\rvert^{j}\geq\bigl(\frac{R-1}{R}\bigr)\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}^{j}-\delta^{j/2}(1+C)^{j/2+1/2}R^{j/2-1},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

We split the proof of (
A.2
) into the cases
j\in 2\mathbb{N}
and
j\not\in 2\mathbb{N}
.

If
j\in 2\mathbb{N}
and
k=j/2
, we have

\begin{split}\lvert\xi\rvert^{j}&=(\delta-\delta+\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})^{j/2}\\
&=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}c_{\ell}(\delta+\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})^{k-\ell}(-\delta)^{\ell}\\
&=(\delta+\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})^{k}+\delta^{k}\sum_{\ell\neq 0}c_{\ell}\Bigl(1+\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\delta}\Bigr)^{k-\ell}(-1)^{\ell}\\
&\geq(\delta+\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})^{k}-C\delta^{k}\Bigl(1+\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\delta}\Bigr)^{k-1}\\
&=\delta^{k}\Bigl(\bigl(1+\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\delta}\bigr)^{k}-C\bigl(1+\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\delta}\bigr)^{k-1}\Bigr),\end{split}

for some
C>0
.
Now, for all
R>1
, we have

\Bigl(\bigl(1+\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\delta}\bigr)^{k}-C\bigl(1+\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\delta}\bigr)^{k-1}\Bigr)\geq\begin{cases}&(1-\frac{1}{R})\bigl(1+\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\delta}\bigr)^{k},\quad\text{if}\ \ \bigl(1+\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\delta}\bigr)>CR\\
&\bigl(1+\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\delta}\bigr)^{k}-C^{k}R^{k-1},\quad\text{if}\ \ \bigl(1+\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\delta}\bigr)\leq CR.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\begin{split}\lvert\xi\rvert^{j}&\geq\delta^{k}\bigl(1-\frac{1}{R}\bigr)\bigl(1+\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{\delta}\bigr)^{k}-\delta^{k}C^{k}R^{k-1}\\
&=\bigl(\frac{R-1}{R}\bigr)\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}^{j}-\delta^{k}C^{k}R^{k-1}\\
&\geq\bigl(\frac{R-1}{R}\bigr)\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}^{j}-\delta^{j/2}(1+C)^{j/2}R^{j/2-1}\end{split}

On the other hand, if
j\notin 2\mathbb{N}
and
j+1=2k
, since
1\geq\lvert\xi\rvert/\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}
, we have

(A.3)

\displaystyle\lvert\xi\rvert^{j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{j+1}}{\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}}\geq\bigl(\frac{R-1}{R}\bigr)\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}^{j}-\frac{\delta^{k}C^{k}R^{k-1}}{\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}}\geq\bigl(\frac{R-1}{R}\bigr)\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}^{j}-\delta^{k-1/2}C^{k}R^{k-1}

(A.4)

\displaystyle\geq\bigl(\frac{R-1}{R}\bigr)\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}^{j}-\delta^{j/2}(1+C)^{\frac{j+1}{2}}R^{j/2-1/2}.

Therefore, combining the above estimates, we have (
A.2
) for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

We now use the previous inequality to prove (
A.1
).
For all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and for all
\delta\in(0,1]
, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2(m-1)}}{\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}^{m-1}}&\geq\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}}{\langle\xi\rangle_{\delta}^{m-1}}\bigl(\bigl(\frac{R-1}{R}\bigr)\langle\xi\rangle^{m-1}_{\delta}-c_{1}^{\frac{m}{2}}\delta^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\bigr)\\
&\geq c\lvert\xi\rvert^{m-1}-c_{1}^{\frac{m}{2}}\delta^{\frac{m-1}{2}},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{split}

for some
c,c_{1}>0
, with
c<1
and
c_{1}>1
.
∎

Effective field theory of coupled dark energy and dark matter
Katsuki Aoki, Jose Beltrán Jiménez, Masroor C. Pookkillath, Shinji Tsujikawa

Abstract
We formulate an effective field theory (EFT) of coupled dark energy (DE) and dark matter (DM) interacting through energy and momentum transfers. In the DE sector, we exploit the EFT of vector-tensor theories with the presence of a preferred time direction on the cosmological background. This prescription allows one to accommodate shift-symmetric and non-shift-symmetric scalar-tensor theories by taking a particular weak coupling limit, with and without consistency conditions respectively. We deal with the DM sector as a non-relativistic perfect fluid, which can be described by a system of three scalar fields. By choosing a unitary gauge in which the perturbations in the DE and DM sectors are eaten by the metric, we incorporate the leading-order operators that characterize the energy and momentum transfers besides those present in the conventional EFT of vector-tensor and scalar-tensor theories and the non-relativistic perfect fluid. We express the second-order action of scalar perturbations in real space in terms of time- and scale-dependent dimensionless EFT parameters and derive the linear perturbation equations of motion by taking into account additional matter (baryons, radiation). In the small-scale limit, we obtain conditions for the absence of both ghosts and Laplacian instabilities and discuss how they are affected by the DE-DM interactions. We also compute the effective DM gravitational coupling G eff G_{\rm eff} by using a quasi-static approximation for perturbations deep inside the DE sound horizon and show that the existence of momentum and energy transfers allow a possibility to realize G eff G_{\rm eff} smaller than in the uncoupled case at low redshift.

The aforementioned theories, including both
uncoupled and coupled DE-DM scenarios,
give rise to widely spread cosmological
predictions. The effective field theory (EFT) approach can provide a unified description for dealing with the dynamics of background and perturbations systematically.
The EFT on a Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) background was first developed in the context of inflation with

70

By choosing a unitary gauge in which the preferred time is identified with the time-dependent scalar field, the EFT action can be systematically constructed under the invariance of spatial diffeomorphism. Later, the EFT was applied to the scalar-field DE in the presence

,

,

,

The EFT of vector-tensor theories has been developed by assuming the existence of
a preferred direction characterized
by a timelike vector field

v_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}\tilde{t}+g_{M}A_{\mu}
,
where

The preferred vector

\tilde{t}\to\tilde{t}-g_{M}\theta
and

A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}+\nabla_{\mu}\theta
,
where
\theta
is an arbitrary scalar
that depends on the spacetime coordinates.
By choosing the unitary gauge
where
\tilde{t}
coincides with the time
coordinate
t
, the preferred vector
reduces to
v_{\mu}=\delta^{0}_{\mu}+g_{M}A_{\mu}
. Since

is not
orthogonal to spacelike hypersurfaces

symmetry-breaking pattern in comparison

The EFT of vector-tensor theories not
only accommodates GP theories and their
extensions as specific cases but also
it also recovers the EFT of shift-symmetric
scalar-tensor
theories
[
98
]
by taking a weak coupling
limit with certain consistency conditions.
The conventional EFT of inflation/DE
in non-shift-symmetric scalar-tensor theories can also be recovered within this framework without imposing the latter consistency conditions.

So far, apart from the multi-component fluid scenarios in Ref.
[

]

between DE and DM. In this paper, we will construct the EFT of coupled DE and DM by introducing leading-order operators
associated with the energy and momentum
transfers. For consistency with the
observation of gravitational waves,
we focus on the EFT operators leading to the luminal propagation speed of tensor perturbations.
We will be mainly interested in scalar and tensor perturbations, so we will leave the description of vector perturbations out of our framework. Although vector modes can have interesting effects in some scenarios (see e.g. Ref.
[
60
]
), they are largely irrelevant for most models.
We deal with DM as a pressureless perfect fluid,
which can be described by
a specific case of the gravitating continuum.
Indeed, the EFT for hydrodynamics
and solids have been developed in

[

,

For the DE sector, we adopt the unified EFT description of vector-tensor and scalar-tensor

[

The DE-DM couplings introduced in our EFT framework can accommodate a large class of concrete coupled DE-DM theories studied in the literature. This formulation includes not only
GP, shift-symmetric, non-shift-symmetric Horndeski theories but also perfect fluid

We construct the EFT action of
coupled DE and DM by choosing a unitary
gauge in which the perturbations in the DE
and DM sectors are eaten by the metric.
We then derive the
full scalar perturbation equations of motion

dimensionless
EFT parameters.
We also obtain conditions for

Finally, we compute the effective
gravitational coupling of DM by using a
quasi-static approximation for the
modes deep inside the DE sound horizon. Then, we show that the direct couplings between
DE and DM can lead to a gravitational interaction weaker than in the
\Lambda
CDM model during the epoch of DE domination and also during matter domination. Our EFT approach to coupled DE and DM
will be useful to provide a general
framework for addressing the

\sigma_{8}
-tension through DE-DM interactions as well as a systematic study of the presence of non-gravitational interactions in the dark sector that will eventually permit a more optimal exploitation of stage IV experiments data.

II
Building blocks for EFT

The EFT action of DE coupled to DM together with minimally coupled matter can be generally
expressed in the form

\displaystyle{\cal S}

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\rm DE}+\mathcal{L}_{\rm DM}+\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}+\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}\big)\,,

(1)

where
g
is the determinant of the metric
tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
,
\mathcal{L}_{\rm DE}

and
\mathcal{L}_{\rm DM}
are
the Lagrangians for DE (including gravity)
and DM sectors respectively,

\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}
describes
non-gravitational interactions between DE and DM, and
the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}

corresponds to contributions from other matter fields
\psi_{m}

such as baryons and radiation, which are assumed to be minimally coupled to gravity,

\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}(g,\psi_{\rm m})
.

Before going into the details of the EFT
formulation, it would be worth
mentioning the differences between the EFT presented in this paper and the EFT of interacting DE developed in Refs.
[
105
,
67
]
.
The latter assumes that DE is described by a scalar field
\phi
and that the DM field
\psi_{\rm DM}
is minimally coupled to a disformally transformed metric,

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}:=C(\phi)g_{\mu\nu}+D(\phi)\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi\,,

(2)

where
C
and
D
are functions of
\phi
.
The EFT of interacting DE is then described by the following action

\displaystyle\hat{{\cal S}}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm DE}(g,\phi)+\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm DM}(\hat{g},\psi_{\rm DM})+\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}(g,\psi_{\rm m})\right]\,.

(3)

However, there would be no a priori reason for the interaction between DE and DM being described by the disformally transformed metric.
In addition, DE does not necessarily need to be a scalar field.
In this paper, we consider more general cases
in which the source for DE can also be a vector field.
We incorporate all possible
leading-order interactions
between DE and DM mediating the energy
and momentum transfers.
We call our scenario the EFT
of coupled DE and DM.

In the following, we briefly review
our EFT constructions of the DE
and DM sectors in Secs.
II.1
and
II.2
, respectively, and
then introduce their interactions
in Sec.
II.3
.

II.1
DE sector

We begin with reviewing the
EFT of DE in which the DE sector
is described by a scalar field
\phi
.
We choose the unitary gauge in which the scalar field perturbation

\delta\phi
vanishes.
Then, the scalar field plays
a role of the time coordinate

t
, such that

\phi=t\,.

(4)

The residual symmetry of the EFT is
the invariance under spatial
diffeomorphisms (diffs):

\displaystyle\bm{x}\to\bm{x}^{\prime}(t,\bm{x})\,.

(5)

In this case, the EFT of DE
coupled to gravity has been
formulated in Ref.
[
109
]

by using the
3+1
ADM decomposition of spacetime.
The line element in the ADM formalism
is given by

{\rm d}s^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}{\rm d}x^{\mu}{\rm d}x^{\nu}=-N^{2}{\rm d}t^{2}+h_{ij}\left({\rm d}x^{i}+N^{i}{\rm d}t\right)\left({\rm d}x^{j}+N^{j}{\rm d}t\right)\,,

(6)

where
N
is the lapse,
N^{i}
is the shift, and
h_{ij}
is the three-dimensional metric
on constant-
t
hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}
. The inverse metric is given by

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}-1/N^{2}&N^{j}/N^{2}\\
N^{i}/N^{2}&h^{ij}-N^{i}N^{j}/N^{2}&\end{pmatrix}.

(7)

For later purposes, it is convenient to use the covariant notation of the ADM formalism with the spatial metric
given by
h_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+n_{\mu}n_{\nu}
, where

n_{\mu}=-\frac{\delta^{0}_{\mu}}{\sqrt{-g^{00}}}=-N\delta^{0}_{\mu}

(8)

is the unit normal vector orthogonal to
\Sigma_{t}
.
On the three-dimensional hypersurface characterized by the metric
h_{ij}
,
we define the extrinsic curvature

K_{ij}=h_{i}^{\alpha}h_{j}^{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}n_{\beta}=\frac{1}{2N}\Big(\partial_{t}h_{ij}-D_{i}N_{j}-D_{j}N_{i}\Big)\,,

(9)

and the Ricci tensor (intrinsic curvature)
{}^{(3)}R_{ij}
on the
three-dimensional hypersurface,
where
D_{i}
is the covariant derivative
operator with respect
to the metric
h_{ij}
.

For simplicity, we consider the EFT corresponding to a subclass of Horndeski theories
[
114
]

with the luminal speed of
gravitational waves.
The explicit form of such an action is given by

{\cal S}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Big[G_{2}(\phi,X)+G_{3}(\phi,X)\square\phi+G_{4}(\phi)R\Big]\,,

(10)

where
G_{2}
and
G_{3}
are functions of

\phi
and

X=-\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla^{\mu}\phi/2
,

G_{4}
is a function of
\phi
,
and
R
is the four-dimensional Ricci scalar.
For this class of scalar-tensor
theories, in terms of the EFT perspective,
we only need to take
into account the following
quantities as the EFT
building blocks:

\displaystyle t,\quad n_{\mu},\quad g^{00},\quad K_{\mu\nu},\quad{}^{(3)}\!R_{\mu\nu}\,,

(11)

on top of the metric
h_{\mu\nu}
.
Since
K_{\mu\nu}
and
{}^{(3)}\!R_{\mu\nu}
satisfy the relations
K_{\mu\nu}n^{\mu}={}^{(3)}\!R_{\mu\nu}n^{\mu}=0
and
n^{\mu}
is normalized to be
n_{\mu}n^{\mu}=-1
, one cannot find
any non-trivial scalar quantities from
n_{\mu}
. We note that the four-dimensional Ricci scalar can be
expressed as

R={}^{(3)}R+K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}-K^{2}+2\nabla_{\nu}\left(n^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}n^{\mu}-n^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}n^{\nu}\right)\,,

(12)

where
{}^{(3)}R:={}^{(3)}{R^{\mu}}_{\mu}
and

K:={K^{\mu}}_{\mu}
.
The gravitational (Einstein-Hilbert) action,
which is multiplied by a time-dependent function
f(t)
and a constant term
M_{*}^{2}/2
, is given by

\frac{M_{*}^{2}}{2}\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}f(t)R=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\,N\sqrt{h}\left[\frac{M_{*}^{2}}{2}f(t)\left({}^{(3)}R+K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}-K^{2}\right)-M_{*}^{2}\frac{{\rm d}f(t)}{{\rm d}t}\frac{K}{N}\right]\,,

(13)

up to boundary terms, where
h
is the determinant
of the three-dimensional tensor
h_{ij}
.
In theories with
{\rm d}f(t)/{\rm d}t=0
, which is the case for General Relativity,
the last term in Eq. (
13
) vanishes.
When
{\rm d}f(t)/{\rm d}t\neq 0
, there is
a contribution arising from the term

-M_{*}^{2}({\rm d}f(t)/{\rm d}t)K/N
.

The action (
13
) contains
the dependence of
t
,
N=1/\sqrt{-g^{00}}
,
K_{\mu\nu}
, and
{}^{(3)}R_{\mu\nu}
.
The general Lagrangian constructed from the quantities in Eq. (
11
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm DE}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm DE}(t,g^{00},K_{\mu\nu},{}^{(3)}\!R_{\mu\nu})\,,

(14)

where the indices are contracted with
h^{\mu\nu}
(or equivalently with
g^{\mu\nu}
). Since
t
is one of the EFT building blocks, one can subtract the background parts of
K_{\mu\nu}
and
g^{00}
, while keeping the invariance under the spatial diffs:

\displaystyle\delta K_{\mu\nu}:=K_{\mu\nu}-H(t)h_{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad\delta g^{00}:=g^{00}-g^{00}_{\rm BG}(t)\,,

(15)

where
H(t):=({\rm d}a/{\rm d}t)/(Na)
and
g^{00}_{\rm BG}(t)
are the background
Hubble expansion rate and the background value of
g^{00}
, respectively.

By expanding the general Lagrangian (
14
) up to quadratic order in perturbations and also only keeping terms that lead to
the luminal speed of gravitational
waves, the EFT Lagrangian for DE is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm DE}=\frac{M_{*}^{2}}{2}f(t)\left[{}^{(3)}\!R+K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}-K^{2}\right]-\hat{\Lambda}(t)-\hat{c}(t)g^{00}-d(t)K+\frac{1}{2}\hat{M}_{2}^{4}(t)\left(\frac{\delta g^{00}}{-g^{00}_{\rm BG}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{M}_{1}^{3}(t)\left(\frac{\delta g^{00}}{-g^{00}_{\rm BG}}\right)\delta K+\cdots,

(16)

where
\hat{\Lambda}
,
\hat{c}
,
d
,
\hat{M}_{2}^{4}
,
\bar{M}_{1}^{3}

are functions of
t
, and

\delta K=\delta K_{\mu\nu}g^{\mu\nu}=\delta K_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu}
.
The ellipsis in Eq. (
16
)
stands for terms corresponding to
the higher-order perturbations.
We note that the last term

-M_{*}^{2}({\rm d}f(t)/{\rm d}t)K/N
in
Eq. (
13
) has been absorbed
into the definitions of
\hat{\Lambda}
,

\hat{c}
,
d
, and
\hat{M}_{2}^{4}(t)
.
In the conventional EFT of
scalar-field inflation and DE
[
70
,
64
,
69
,
109
]
, we usually set
d(t)=0
by absorbing the Lagrangian

-d(t)K
into
other terms after integration by parts. We will retain
-d(t)K
, as it is more useful for the EFT framework that accommodates vector-tensor theories
[
24
]
.

The EFT can be extended to theories
with different symmetry-breaking patterns
[
24
,
22
]
.
Let us first consider the EFT
invariant under the transformations

\displaystyle t\to t^{\prime}=t+\chi_{0}\,,\qquad\bm{x}\to\bm{x}^{\prime}(t,\bm{x})\,,

(17)

with
\chi_{0}
is a constant parameter.
The global time-translation is nothing but the global shift of the scalar field when it is reintroduced by the Stückelberg
trick,
t\to\phi
.
More generally, the EFT of shift-symmetric theories is obtained by imposing a residual invariance under time-diffs with

\xi^{0}=-c_{\phi}/\dot{\bar{\phi}}
, because this preserves the unitary gauge
\phi(t)=\bar{\phi}(t)
in combination with a shift
\phi\to\phi+c_{\phi}
. When
\phi=t
, this reduces to the
global time-translation mentioned above.
Therefore, the EFT with the symmetry (
17
) corresponds to
shift-symmetric scalar-tensor theories
[
98
]
.
In the subclass of Horndeski
theories (
10
),
the symmetry (
17
) translates to the couplings

G_{2}(\phi,X)=G_{2}(X)
,
G_{3}(\phi,X)=G_{3}(X)
, and
G_{4}(\phi)={\rm constant}
.
In this case, the general form of the invariant Lagrangian
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm DE}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm DE}(g^{00},K_{\mu\nu},{}^{(3)}\!R_{\mu\nu})\,.

(18)

Without the
t
dependence in the Lagrangian (
18
),
the background parts of
g^{00}
and
K_{\mu\nu}
cannot be
subtracted by keeping the covariance.
Nonetheless, one can perform the Taylor expansion
of the Lagrangian to arrive at the same form as
Eq. (
16
), provided that the EFT coefficients satisfy the following consistency conditions
[
24
]
(see also
[
98
]
):

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\Lambda}}+3H\dot{d}+\dot{\hat{c}}g^{00}_{\rm BG}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(19)

\displaystyle 2\hat{M}_{2}^{4}\frac{{\rm d}}{\bar{N}{\rm d}t}\ln(-g^{00}_{\rm BG})+3\bar{M}_{1}^{3}\dot{H}+2\dot{\hat{c}}g^{00}_{\rm BG}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(20)

\displaystyle\dot{d}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{M}_{1}^{3}\frac{{\rm d}}{\bar{N}{\rm d}t}\ln(-g^{00}_{\rm BG})

\displaystyle=0\,,

(21)

\displaystyle\dot{f}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(22)

where
\bar{N}
is the background value of
N
,
and a dot denotes the derivative with respect to
the cosmic time
\tilde{t}=\int\bar{N}\,{\rm d}t
,
e.g.,
\dot{\Lambda}={\rm d}\Lambda/(\bar{N}{\rm d}t)
.
The condition (
22
) means that the last term in Eq. (
13
) vanishes and hence the Lagrangian (
16
) can be used without the modifications to the EFT coefficients induced by the term
-M_{*}^{2}({\rm d}f(t)/{\rm d}t)K/N
.

The EFT of vector-tensor theories is obtained by
promoting the shift symmetry to a local one
with the help of a gauge field
A_{\mu}
, as

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\to t^{\prime}=t-g_{M}\theta(t,\bm{x})\,,\qquad A_{\mu}\to A^{\prime}_{\mu}=A_{\mu}+\nabla_{\mu}\theta(t,\bm{x})\,,

(23)

\displaystyle\bm{x}

\displaystyle\to\bm{x}^{\prime}(t,\bm{x})\,,

(24)

with
g_{M}
being the gauge
coupling constant.
The EFT building blocks are given by the “tilded” quantities as examined in Ref.
[
24
]
.
If one is interested in the irrotational solution (scalar and tensor perturbations only) as a consistent truncation, however, one can make an ansatz

A_{\mu}=\Big[A_{0}(t,\bm{x}),\bm{0}\Big]\,,

(25)

by using the freedom of the combined
U(1)
and time diffs (
23
).
Note that we are assuming the existence of
a preferred vector field

v_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}\tilde{t}+g_{M}A_{\mu}
,
where we choose the unitary gauge in which
the Stückelberg field
\tilde{t}
is
equivalent to
t
.
For this gauge choice with the vector field
configuration (
25
), we have

v_{\mu}=\tilde{\delta}^{0}_{\mu}=\delta^{0}_{\mu}+g_{M}A_{\mu}=(1+g_{M}A_{0},{\bm{0}})

and hence
v_{\mu}
is orthogonal to constant

t
hypersurfaces. The norm of the preferred
vector is

\tilde{g}^{00}=\tilde{\delta}^{0}_{\alpha}\tilde{\delta}^{0}_{\beta}g^{\alpha\beta}=\left(1+g_{M}A_{0}\right)^{2}g^{00}\,.

(26)

In this case, the unit timelike vector

\tilde{n}_{\mu}=-\tilde{\delta}^{0}_{\mu}/\sqrt{-\tilde{g}^{00}}
is equivalent to

n_{\mu}
defined in Eq. (
8
).

The vector-tensor EFT building blocks together with
the ansatz (
25
) consist of
the following quantities:

\displaystyle n_{\mu},\quad\tilde{g}^{00},\quad F_{\mu}\,,\quad K_{\mu\nu},\quad{}^{(3)}\!R_{\mu\nu},

(27)

where we have defined

\displaystyle F_{\mu}:=n^{\alpha}F_{\mu\alpha}\,,\qquad F_{\mu\alpha}:=2\nabla_{[\mu}A_{\alpha]}\,.

(28)

The magnetic part of the field strength

h^{\alpha}{}_{\mu}h^{\beta}{}_{\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}
vanishes identically under the ansatz (
25
). This trivializes the possible building block
\tilde{F}_{\mu}=n^{\alpha}\tilde{F}_{\alpha\mu}
, where
\tilde{F}_{\alpha\mu}
is the spatially covariant dual of the field strength.
The EFT action, which subtracts the
last term in Eq. (
13
),
is then given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm DE}

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{*}^{2}}{2}f(t)\left[{}^{(3)}\!R+K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}-K^{2}\right]-\hat{\Lambda}(t)-\hat{c}(t)\tilde{g}^{00}-d(t)K

\displaystyle~~~+\frac{1}{2}\hat{M}_{2}^{4}(t)\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{g}^{00}}{-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{M}_{1}^{3}(t)\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{g}^{00}}{-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}}\right)\delta K+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{1}(t)F_{\mu}F^{\mu}\,,

(29)

where we have taken into account the term

\gamma_{1}(t)F_{\mu}F^{\mu}/2
associated
with the electromagnetic field strength.
To avoid the ghost in the vector sector,
we require that
[
24
,
22
]
(see also Appendix
B
)

\gamma_{1}(t)>0\,.

(30)

In this case, there are the following
consistency conditions

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\Lambda}}+3H\dot{d}+\dot{\hat{c}}\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(31)

\displaystyle 2\hat{M}_{2}^{4}\frac{{\rm d}}{\bar{N}{\rm d}t}\ln(-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG})+3\bar{M}_{1}^{3}\dot{H}+2\dot{\hat{c}}\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(32)

\displaystyle\dot{d}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{M}_{1}^{3}\frac{{\rm d}}{\bar{N}{\rm d}t}\ln(-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG})

\displaystyle=0\,,

(33)

\displaystyle\dot{f}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(34)

Again, the relation (
34
)
means that the last term in Eq. (
13
)
does not contribute to the EFT action.
The Lagrangian (
29
) can describe
the cosmological evolution of the background and
perturbations in GP theories
[
111
,
162
,
14
,
44
,
13
]
with the luminal speed of gravitational waves.
The explicit action of such a subclass
of GP theories is given by

{\cal S}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[F+G_{2}(\tilde{X})+G_{3}(\tilde{X})\nabla_{\mu}A^{\mu}+\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R\right]\,,

(35)

where
F:=-F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}/4
,
G_{2}
and
G_{3}
are functions of

\tilde{X}=-A_{\mu}A^{\mu}/2
, and
M_{\rm Pl}
is the reduced Planck mass. Taking the limit

A_{\mu}\to\nabla_{\mu}\phi
,
we recover the shift-symmetric Horndeski
theories with
F=0
,

\tilde{X}\to X=-\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla^{\mu}\phi/2
,
and
\nabla_{\mu}A^{\mu}\to\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\phi
.
The constancy of the coefficient
of
R
is consistent with the condition (
34
).
Let us also clarify that we only need to consider
a term linear in
F
owed to our ansatz in Eq. (
25
) that trivialises
F_{\mu\nu}
at the background level, so
F_{\mu\nu}
will only appear in the perturbation sector.

The usage of the EFT Lagrangian (
29
)
is not restricted to vector-tensor theories,
but it serves as a unified framework for
both scalar-tensor and vector-tensor theories
[
24
,
22
]
.
Taking the limit
g_{M}\to 0
, one recovers
shift-symmetric scalar-tensor theories with a decoupled vector field.
The conventional EFT of DE, which is
given by the action (
16
),
is also reproduced by omitting the consistency conditions further.
For example, the absence of the consistency
condition
\dot{f}=0
allows the
time-dependent non-minimal coupling

G_{4}(\phi)R
in
non-shift-symmetric scalar-tensor theories.
In this way, one can accommodate vector-tensor,
shift-symmetric scalar-tensor, and
non-shift-symmetric scalar-tensor
theories in a unified manner.
The gauge coupling and consistency
conditions characterize the boundaries
of those theories.

II.2
DM sector

We assume that the dynamics in the
DM sector is described by a dust
fluid (CDM) on cosmological scales.
While there are several descriptions of fluid dynamics, it will be convenient for our purposes to adopt the one based on three scalar fields

\phi^{i}(t,\bm{x})~(i=1,2,3)
.
5
5

5

A dual formulation in terms of 2-forms exists where the cosmological symmetries are also realized
in a dual manner
[
19
]
. This dual formulation also exists for solids.

These scalar fields can be understood as comoving coordinates of the fluid.
Since the spatial diffs are preserved in the EFT of DE, one can use this freedom to move to the comoving gauge
\phi^{i}=x^{i}
, where the perturbations of
\phi^{i}
are eaten by the metric.

Among the general action for the scalar fields
\phi^{i}
,
the fluid phase is defined by an invariance under internal volume-preserving
diffs
[
90
,
95
]

\displaystyle\phi^{i}\to\phi^{\prime}{}^{i}\quad{\rm s.t.}\quad\det\frac{\partial\phi^{\prime}{}^{i}}{\partial\phi^{j}}=1\,.

(36)

The invariant building blocks
are given by

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\det g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{j}}\,,

(37)

\displaystyle u^{\mu}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{6n}\varepsilon_{ijk}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{i}\partial_{\rho}\phi^{j}\partial_{\sigma}\phi^{k}\,,

(38)

and their derivatives. Here,
n
and
u^{\mu}

are the number density and four-velocity of the fluid, respectively,

\varepsilon_{ijk}
is the
anti-symmetric symbol with

\varepsilon_{123}=1
, and
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}

is the spacetime Levi-Civita tensor
with
\epsilon^{0123}=-1/\sqrt{-g}
.
In this formulation, the conservation
of the current
\mathcal{J}^{\mu}=nu^{\mu}
is an off-shell identity,
\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{J}^{\mu}=0
.
When we use the comoving gauge
\phi^{i}=x^{i}
,
we find

\displaystyle n=\sqrt{\det g^{ij}}\,,\qquad u^{\mu}=\frac{\delta^{\mu}_{0}}{\sqrt{-g_{00}}}\,,

(39)

where the residual symmetry is the spatial
volume-preserving diffs:

\displaystyle\bm{x}\to\bm{x}^{\prime}(\bm{x})\quad{\rm s.t.}\quad\det\frac{\partial\bm{x}^{\prime}}{\partial\bm{x}}=1\,.

(40)

The general action of the fluid is given by a function of
n
at leading order in a derivative expansion. We are interested in the DM dynamics which should be approximated by the dust fluid with the Lagrangian linear in the number density
n
. As a result, the action of the DM sector
is given by
{\cal S}_{\rm DM}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,{\cal L}_{\rm DM}
,
with the Lagrangian

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm DM}=-\hat{m}_{\rm c}n\,.

(41)

Here,
\hat{m}_{\rm c}n

is the energy density of the dust fluid, with
\hat{m}_{\rm c}
being a constant.

Before closing this subsection, it is worthwhile mentioning the possibility of
a solid DM.
6
6

6

The possibility of having solid DM was first explored in Ref.
[
57
]
.
Using three scalars, the solid phase means that the symmetry is downgraded to the internal
ISO(3)

symmetry
[
90
,
95
]
.
The solidity generates a non-vanishing sound speed of the transverse mode,
c_{V}\neq 0
. On the other hand, in the decoupling limit of
gravity, the squared sound speed of
the longitudinal mode

c_{S}^{2}
is given by the following
form
[
23
]

\displaystyle c_{S}^{2}=\frac{\partial\bar{p}}{\partial\bar{\rho}}+\frac{4}{3}c_{V}^{2}\,,

(42)

where
\bar{\rho}
and
\bar{p}
are the
background energy density and pressure
of the solid. In the following, we will use
a bar to represent background quantities.
Even for the case in which the equation of state
w=\bar{p}/\bar{\rho}
is close to 0,
the solid
(c_{V}^{2}\neq 0)
leads to the deviation of
c_{S}^{2}
from 0 and hence it does not generally
work as CDM. While it would be interesting to discuss how the solidity affects the small-scale dynamics of DM, the solidity should be well negligible on cosmological scales which we are interested in.
Hence, we only consider the fluid DM throughout the paper.

Besides DM, we incorporate other matter fields (baryons, radiation) described by the perfect fluids. While they can be
dealt as the three-scalar field description
explained above (as we do in Appendix
C
), it is also possible
to accommodate them as a
Schutz-Sorkin action
[
157
,
56
,
74
,
146
,
147
]
or
a purely k-essence
[
104
,
31
]

on the time-dependent cosmological background.
In the following, we will adopt the
Schutz-Sorkin action without the
rotational mode, which is given by

{\cal S}_{\rm m}=-\int{\rm d}^{4}x\left[\sqrt{-g}\,\rho_{\rm m}(n_{\rm m})+J^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\ell\right]\,,

(43)

where the matter density
\rho_{\rm m}

is a function of its number density

n_{\rm m}
, and
\ell
is a Lagrange
multiplier. The vector field
J^{\mu}

in the action (
43
) is related to

n_{\rm m}
, as
n_{\rm m}=\sqrt{g_{\mu\nu}J^{\mu}J^{\nu}/g}
.
The fluid four-velocity is defined by

(u_{\rm m})^{\mu}=J^{\mu}/(n_{\rm m}\sqrt{-g})
, satisfying the normalization

(u_{\rm m})^{\mu}(u_{\rm m})_{\mu}=-1
.
Varying (
43
) with respect to
\ell
,
we obtain
\nabla_{\mu}J^{\mu}=0
.
This equation can be expressed as

(u_{\rm m})^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\rho_{\rm m}+\left(\rho_{\rm m}+p_{\rm m}\right)\nabla_{\mu}(u_{\rm m})^{\mu}=0\,,

(44)

where
p_{\rm m}
is the matter pressure
defined by

p_{\rm m}:=n_{\rm m}\rho_{{\rm m},n_{\rm m}}-\rho_{\rm m}\,,

(45)

where
\rho_{{\rm m},n_{\rm m}}:={\rm d}\rho_{\rm m}/{\rm d}n_{\rm m}
.
The energy-momentum tensor

T_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}
associated with
the variation of (
43
) with respect to
g^{\mu\nu}

is given by
[
16
]

T_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}=(\rho_{\rm m}+p_{\rm m})(u_{m})_{\mu}(u_{n})_{\nu}+p_{m}g_{\mu\nu}\,,

(46)

which corresponds to that of the perfect
fluid.

II.3
EFT action for coupled DE and DM

Given that we have introduced basic tools for the EFT of dark sectors, it is now straightforward to describe the interactions between DE and DM.
We start our formulation from the vector DE because we can easily accommodate the scalar DE by taking the appropriate limit and omitting the
consistency conditions.
For simplicity, we focus on the
leading-order terms in derivative expansions.

The EFT building blocks associated with
the interactions between DE and DM are

Building blocks for coupled DE and DM

\displaystyle\underbrace{n_{\mu},\quad\tilde{g}^{00},\quad F_{\mu}}_{\rm DE},\qquad\underbrace{n,\quad u^{\mu}}_{\rm DM}\,.

(47)

There are several DE-DM interacting
Lagrangians constructed from the ingredients (
47
).
One of them is the product between
\tilde{g}^{00}

and
n
, which characterizes the energy exchange between DE and DM.
To avoid the non-vanishing CDM sound speed, we require that the coupling
is linear in
n

[
119
]
, i.e.,

\mathcal{L}_{n}(\tilde{g}^{00})n
,
where
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is a function
of
\tilde{g}^{00}
.
The others are the scalar products

n_{\mu}u^{\mu}
and
F_{\mu}u^{\mu}
,
which mediate the momentum exchanges
between DE and DM.
To quantify the latter interactions,
it is convenient to decompose
u^{\mu}
into
the temporal and spatial components
with respect to
n_{\mu}
, as

\displaystyle{\cal U}:=n_{\nu}u^{\nu}\,,\qquad q^{\mu}:=u^{\mu}+n^{\mu}{\cal U}\,,

(48)

where
q^{\mu}
satisfies the orthogonal
relation
n_{\mu}q^{\mu}=0
.
These two quantities are related to
each other, as

\displaystyle q^{\mu}q_{\mu}=-1+{\cal U}^{2}\,,

(49)

so that we only need to take the spatial
components of
q^{\mu}
as the building block.
Note that
q^{\mu}
and
F_{\mu}
start at
linear order in perturbations.
In the comoving gauge, we find

\displaystyle q^{\mu}=\left(0,\frac{N^{i}}{\sqrt{N^{2}-N_{j}N^{j}}}\right)\,,

(50)

meaning that
q^{\mu}
is related to the shift vector in the ADM language.
Instead of using
u^{\mu}
,
we will use
q^{\mu}
to quantify
the DE-DM momentum transfer and consider
the interactions
q^{\mu}q_{\mu}

and
q^{\mu}F_{\mu}
.
These scalar products can be further
multiplied by

\mathcal{L}_{q^{2}}(\tilde{g}^{00})
and

\mathcal{L}_{q\cdot F}(\tilde{g}^{00})
, respectively,
where
\mathcal{L}_{q^{2}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{q\cdot F}

are functions of
\tilde{g}^{00}
.
Then, the general interacting Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{n}(\tilde{g}^{00})n+\mathcal{L}_{q^{2}}(\tilde{g}^{00})q^{\mu}q_{\mu}+\mathcal{L}_{q\cdot F}(\tilde{g}^{00})F_{\mu}q^{\mu}+\cdots

\displaystyle=-\Delta\Lambda(t)-\Delta c(t)\tilde{g}^{00}-\Delta m_{\rm c}(t)n

\displaystyle~~~+\frac{1}{2}\Delta M_{2}^{4}(t)\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{g}^{00}}{-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}}\right)^{2}-m_{1}^{4}(t)\frac{\delta n}{\bar{n}}\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{g}^{00}}{-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}}\right)-m_{2}^{4}(t)q^{\mu}q_{\mu}-\bar{m}_{1}^{2}(t)q^{\mu}F_{\mu}+\cdots\,,

(51)

where dots represent terms
for higher-order perturbations, and

\displaystyle\Delta\Lambda(t)

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{n\tilde{g}^{00}}\,\bar{n}\,\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}\,,\qquad\Delta c(t)=-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{n\tilde{g}^{00}}\,\bar{n}\,,\qquad\Delta m_{\rm c}(t)=-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta M_{2}^{4}(t)

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{n\tilde{g}^{00}\tilde{g}^{00}}\,\bar{n}\,(\tilde{g}_{\rm BG}^{00})^{2}\,,\qquad m_{1}^{4}(t)=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{n\tilde{g}^{00}}\,\tilde{g}_{\rm BG}^{00}\,\bar{n}\,,\qquad m_{2}^{4}(t)=-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{q^{2}}\,,\qquad\bar{m}_{1}^{2}(t)=-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{q\cdot F}\,,

(52)

and

\delta n=n-\bar{n}(t)\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{n\tilde{g}^{00}}=\frac{{\rm d}\mathcal{L}_{n}}{{\rm d}\tilde{g}^{00}},\qquad\mathcal{L}_{n\tilde{g}^{00}\tilde{g}^{00}}=\frac{{\rm d}\mathcal{L}_{n\tilde{g}^{00}}}{{\rm d}\tilde{g}^{00}}\,.

(53)

The functions with the bar correspond to their background parts, which are
implicit functions of time.
The following two relations need to
be satisfied

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{n\tilde{g}^{00}}(t)\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}(t)\,,

(54)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{n\tilde{g}^{00}}(t)

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{n\tilde{g}^{00}\tilde{g}^{00}}(t)\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}(t)\,.

(55)

The total action
{\cal S}
,
which is given by Eq. (
1
),
is composed of the Lagrangians
(
16
), (
41
),
(
51
), and the matter action (
43
). Then, we can express it
in the following form:

EFT action for coupled DE and DM

{\cal S}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left(\mathcal{L}_{\rm D}^{\rm NL}+\mathcal{L}_{\rm D}^{(2)}\right)+{\cal S}_{\rm m}\,,

(56)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm D}^{\rm NL}

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{*}^{2}}{2}f(t)\left({}^{(3)}\!R+K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}-K^{2}\right)-\Lambda(t)-\tilde{c}(t)\tilde{g}^{00}-d(t)K-m_{\rm c}(t)n\,,

(57)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm D}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}M_{2}^{4}{}(t)\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{g}^{00}}{-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{M}_{1}^{3}(t)\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{g}^{00}}{-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}}\right)\delta K+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{1}(t)F_{\mu}F^{\mu}

\displaystyle~~~-m_{1}^{4}(t)\frac{\delta n}{\bar{n}}\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{g}^{00}}{-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}}\right)-m_{2}^{4}(t)q^{\mu}q_{\mu}-\bar{m}_{1}^{2}(t)q^{\mu}F_{\mu}\,,

(58)

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\rm m}

\displaystyle=-\int{\rm d}^{4}x\left[\sqrt{-g}\,\rho_{\rm m}(n_{\rm m})+J^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\ell\right]\,.

(59)

Here, the coefficients are normalized
EFT coefficients, which are given by

\Lambda(t)=\hat{\Lambda}(t)+\Delta\Lambda(t)\,,\qquad\tilde{c}(t)=\hat{c}(t)+\Delta c(t)\,,\qquad m_{\rm c}(t)=\hat{m}_{\rm c}+\Delta m_{\rm c}(t)\,,\qquad M_{2}^{4}(t)=\hat{M}_{2}^{4}(t)+\Delta M_{2}^{4}(t)\,.

(60)

The effective CDM mass
m_{\rm c}(t)
acquires the time dependence through the term
\Delta m_{\rm c}(t)
,
mediating the energy transfer between DE and DM.
The operator associated with the EFT function

m_{1}^{4}(t)
also leads to the energy exchange,
whereas the momentum transfer is weighed
by the two functions

m_{2}^{4}(t)
and
\bar{m}_{1}^{2}(t)
.

The consistency conditions in terms of
the normalized coefficients are given by

Consistency conditions

\displaystyle\dot{\Lambda}+3H\dot{d}+\dot{\tilde{c}}\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}-m_{1}^{4}\frac{{\rm d}}{\bar{N}{\rm d}t}\ln(-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG})

\displaystyle=0\,,

(61)

\displaystyle 2M_{2}^{4}\frac{{\rm d}}{\bar{N}{\rm d}t}\ln(-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG})+3\bar{M}_{1}^{3}\dot{H}+2\dot{\tilde{c}}\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}-6Hm_{1}^{4}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(62)

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{\rm c}\bar{n}+m_{1}^{4}\frac{{\rm d}}{\bar{N}{\rm d}t}\ln(-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG})

\displaystyle=0\,,

(63)

\displaystyle\dot{d}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{M}_{1}^{3}\frac{{\rm d}}{\bar{N}{\rm d}t}\ln(-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG})

\displaystyle=0\,,

(64)

\displaystyle\dot{f}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(65)

where we used the property

\bar{n}\propto a^{-3}
.
Note that the coefficient
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{n\tilde{g}^{00}\tilde{g}^{00}}

does not explicitly appear in the
action (
56
) up to quadratic order.
Hence, the consistency condition (
55
),
which is regarded as a constraint on
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{n\tilde{g}^{00}\tilde{g}^{00}}
, can be safely neglected at quadratic order in perturbations.

As we already explained, the action (
56
) can accommodate both scalar and vector DE scenarios.
The shift-symmetric scalar-tensor theories coupled to DM are obtained by taking the limit
g_{M}\to 0
(corresponding to
\tilde{g}^{00}\to g^{00}
) and
\bar{m}_{1}^{2}\to 0
,
while keeping the consistency conditions
(
61
)-(
65
).
The generic non-shift-symmetric
scalar-tensor theories are obtained
by omitting the consistency conditions.
Note that the recovery of scalar-tensor theories requires the limit
\bar{m}_{1}^{2}\to 0
in addition to the decoupling limit
g_{M}\to 0
, because the coupling
q^{\mu}F_{\mu}

can survive even for
g_{M}\to 0
.
The condition (
54
)
translates to Eq. (
63
),
which relates
\dot{m}_{\rm c}
with
m_{1}^{4}
.
This means that, for
m_{1}^{4}=0
, we have that

m_{\rm c}={\rm constant}
.
In non-shift-symmetric scalar-tensor theories,
the consistency condition (
63
)
does not need to hold and hence, the time variation of
m_{\rm c}
is allowed even for
m_{1}^{4}=0
.

III
Background Equations of motion

Let us consider a spatially-flat FLRW
background given by the line element

{\rm d}s^{2}=-\bar{N}^{2}(t){\rm d}t^{2}+a^{2}(t)\delta_{ij}{\rm d}x^{i}{\rm d}x^{j}\,,

(66)

together with the vector field configuration

\bar{A}_{\mu}=\left[\bar{A}_{0}(t),{\bm{0}}\right]\,,

(67)

where a bar represents the time-dependent
background quantities.
Since the Lagrangian

{\cal L}_{\rm D}^{(2)}
is of second order
in perturbations, it does not contribute
to the background equations of motion.
We exploit the following properties

K=3H\,,\qquad K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}=3H^{2}\,,\qquad{}^{(3)}R=0\,,\qquad\tilde{g}^{00}=-\bar{N}^{-2}\left(1+g_{M}A_{0}\right)^{2}\,,

(68)

with the Hubble expansion rate

H=\dot{a}/a=({\rm d}a/{\rm d}t)/(\bar{N}a)
.
The action relevant to the background
dynamics, which arises from the Lagrangian

{\cal L}_{\rm D}^{\rm NL}
with the matter
action
{\cal S}_{\rm m}
, is given by

\displaystyle{\cal S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}^{4}x\left[-\frac{3M_{*}^{2}f}{\bar{N}}a\left(\frac{{\rm d}a}{{\rm d}t}\right)^{2}-\bar{N}a^{3}\Lambda-\bar{N}m_{\rm c}n_{0}+\frac{\tilde{c}a^{3}}{\bar{N}}\left(1+g_{M}A_{0}\right)^{2}-3d\,a^{2}\left(\frac{{\rm d}a}{{\rm d}t}\right)\right]+{\cal S}_{\rm m}\,,

(69)

where we used the property
\bar{n}=n_{0}/a^{3}
,
with
n_{0}
being today’s value of

\bar{n}
(the scale factor is normalized
to be
a=1
today).
Since
(u_{\rm m})^{\mu}=(1/\bar{N},0,0,0)

and
\nabla_{\mu}(u_{\rm m})^{\mu}=3H

on the background (
66
),
the matter continuity equation
(
44
) reduces to

\dot{\bar{\rho}}_{\rm m}+3H(\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}+\bar{p}_{\rm m})=0\,,

(70)

where
\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}
and

\bar{p}_{\rm m}
are the background
energy density and pressure, respectively.
Varying the action (
69
) with
respect to
\bar{N}
and
a
, respectively,
we obtain

\displaystyle 3M_{*}^{2}fH^{2}=\Lambda+\frac{\tilde{c}}{\bar{N}^{2}}\left(1+g_{M}A_{0}\right)^{2}+m_{\rm c}\bar{n}+\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}\,,

(71)

\displaystyle M_{*}^{2}\left(2f\dot{H}+2\dot{f}H+3fH^{2}\right)=\Lambda-\frac{\tilde{c}}{\bar{N}^{2}}\left(1+g_{M}A_{0}\right)^{2}-\dot{d}-\bar{p}_{\rm m}\,.

(72)

We note that the energy transfer between DE
and DM gives rise to a time-dependent
mass
m_{\rm c}
.
Defining the background CDM density as

\rho_{\rm c}:=m_{\rm c}\bar{n}\,,

(73)

it follows that

\dot{\rho}_{\rm c}+3H\rho_{\rm c}=\dot{m}_{\rm c}\bar{n}\,,

(74)

where we used
\dot{\bar{n}}+3H\bar{n}=0
.
At the background level, the interaction
between DE and DM appears as the terms

\Delta\Lambda
,
\Delta c
, and

\Delta m_{\rm c}
in
\Lambda
,

\tilde{c}
, and
m_{\rm c}
,
respectively.
As one should expect, the momentum transfer interactions do not affect
the background equations.
In the following, we consider
three different theories in turn.

III.1
Vector-tensor theories

In vector-tensor theories (
g_{M}\neq 0
),
we obtain the equation of motion for

A_{0}
by varying Eq. (
69
)
with respect to
A_{0}
.
This leads to

2\tilde{c}a^{3}g_{M}(1+g_{M}A_{0})/\bar{N}=0

i.e.,
\tilde{c}\,(1+g_{M}A_{0})=0

If
1+g_{M}A_{0}=0

Then, we require that
1+g_{M}A_{0}\neq 0
,
and hence

\tilde{c}=0\,,

(75)

so that the
\tilde{c}
-dependent terms
in Eqs. (
71
) and (
72
)
vanish. Moreover, the consistency
condition (
65
)
holds for vector-tensor theories under
consideration now, so that

M_{*}^{2}f=M_{\rm Pl}^{2}={\rm constant}
.
Then, the background Eqs. (
71
) and (
72
) reduce, respectively, to

\displaystyle 3M_{\rm Pl}^{2}H^{2}=\Lambda+m_{\rm c}\bar{n}+\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}\,,

(76)

\displaystyle M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\left(2\dot{H}+3H^{2}\right)=\Lambda-\dot{d}-\bar{p}_{\rm m}\,,

(77)

where
\dot{d}
is related to
\bar{M}_{1}^{3}

according to Eq. (
64
).
At the background level, the effect of
interactions between vector DE and DM
appears as the two terms

\Delta\Lambda
and
\Delta m_{\rm c}

in
\Lambda
and
m_{\rm c}
, respectively.

Let us consider the subclass of GP
theories (
35
)
with the interacting Lagrangian (
255
)
given in Appendix
A.2
, i.e., the action

\displaystyle{\cal S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[F+G_{2}(\tilde{X})+G_{3}(\tilde{X})\nabla_{\mu}A^{\mu}+\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R-f_{1}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Z},\tilde{E})n+f_{2}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Z},\tilde{E})\right]+{\cal S}_{\rm m}\,,

(78)

where
\tilde{X}=-A_{\mu}A^{\mu}/2
,

\tilde{Z}=A_{\mu}u^{\mu}
, and

\tilde{E}=-A^{\mu}F_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}
.
In Appendix
A.2
, we show the correspondence between the background quantities

\Lambda
,
\tilde{c}
,
d
and the coupling
functions
G_{2}
,
G_{3}
,
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
.
Note that the function
g_{3}
appearing in the
dictionary of the correspondence is related to

G_{3}
according to
G_{3}=g_{3}+2\tilde{X}g_{3,\tilde{X}}
. From the correspondence (
267
), Eq. (

)
translates to

G_{2,\tilde{X}}-3H\sqrt{2\tilde{X}_{\rm BG}}G_{3,\tilde{X}}-\bar{n}\left[f_{1,\tilde{X}}+(2\tilde{X}_{\rm BG})^{-1/2}f_{1,\tilde{Z}}\right]+f_{2,\tilde{X}}+(2\tilde{X}_{\rm BG})^{-1/2}f_{2,\tilde{Z}}=0\,,

(79)

where we used the relation

G_{3,\tilde{X}}=3g_{3,\tilde{X}}+2\tilde{X}g_{3,\tilde{X}\tilde{X}}
,
and
\tilde{X}_{\rm BG}
is the background
value of
\tilde{X}
given by
\tilde{X}_{\rm BG}=\bar{A}_{0}^{2}/(2\bar{N}^{2})
.
With the dictionaries (
266
) and (

77
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-G_{2}-f_{2}\,,

(80)

\displaystyle\dot{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\tilde{X}_{\rm BG}}\,\dot{\tilde{X}}_{\rm BG}G_{3,\tilde{X}}\,,

(81)

where Eq. (

) has been used to simplify
\Lambda
.
Eq. (
80
) describes how the interaction

f_{2}
contributes to
\Lambda
.
We also note the presence of the
G_{3}(\tilde{X})
coupling makes

non-vanishing.

III.2
Shift-symmetric
scalar-tensor theories

The shift-symmetric scalar-tensor theories
with the luminal speed of gravitational waves
can be obtained by taking the limit
g_{M}\to 0

in Eqs. (
71
)-(
72
)
and keeping the consistency conditions (
61
)-(
65
).
From Eq. (

), we have

M_{*}^{2}f=M_{\rm Pl}^{2}={\rm constant}
.
Using the consistency conditions
(

) and (

) together with
the background Eqs. (

) and (

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(\frac{a^{3}\tilde{c}}{\bar{N}}\right)=0\,,

whose integrated solution is given by

\tilde{c}=\tilde{c}_{0}\frac{\bar{N}}{a^{3}}\,,

(83)

where
\tilde{c}_{0}
is an integration constant.
The background equations of motion (
71
) and (
72
) reduce to

\displaystyle 3M_{\rm Pl}^{2}H^{2}=\Lambda+\frac{\tilde{c}}{\bar{N}^{2}}+m_{\rm c}\bar{n}+\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}\,,

\displaystyle M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\left(2\dot{H}+3H^{2}\right)=\Lambda-\frac{\tilde{c}}{\bar{N}^{2}}-\dot{d}-\bar{p}_{\rm m}\,.

(85)

The difference from GP theories is that

does not necessarily vanish.
As

increases with the cosmic
expansion,
\tilde{c}

eventually approaches 0
in an ever-expanding Universe.
The limit

as a tracker solution first recognized in
Ref.
[
83

for
uncoupled covariant Galileons.
The couplings between DE and DM appear
as the correction terms

,

\Delta m_{\rm c}
, and
\Delta c

in Eqs. (
84
)-(
85
).

As an example, we consider the subclass of shift-symmetric Horndeski theories with the luminal propagation of gravitational waves
in the presence of the interacting Lagrangian (
238
), i.e.,

{\cal S}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[G_{2}(X)+G_{3}(X)\square\phi+\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R-f_{1}(\phi,X,Z)n+f_{2}(\phi,X,Z)\right]+{\cal S}_{\rm m}\,,

(86)

where

In the context of more general

the relations between

,

,

and

,

The shift-symmetric Horndeski theories
correspond to taking the limits

Then, the functions

,
\Lambda

\displaystyle\tilde{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}G_{2,X}-\frac{3}{2}H\sqrt{2X_{\rm BG}}\,G_{3,X}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{n}\left[f_{1,X}+(2X_{\rm BG})^{-1/2}f_{1,Z}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[f_{2,X}+(2X_{\rm BG})^{-1/2}f_{2,Z}\right]=\tilde{c}_{0}\frac{\bar{N}}{a^{3}}\,,

(87)

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-G_{2}-f_{2}+2\tilde{c}X_{\rm BG}\,,

(88)

\displaystyle\dot{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2X_{\rm BG}}\dot{X}_{\rm BG}\,G_{3,X}\,,

(89)

In the limit

III.3
Non-shift-symmetric

In non-shift-symmetric scalar-tensor theories,

\dot{f}=0

Taking the limit

in Eqs. (

) and (

\displaystyle 3M_{*}^{2}fH^{2}=\Lambda+\frac{\tilde{c}}{\bar{N}^{2}}+m_{\rm c}\bar{n}+\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}\,,

(91)

(
61
) and (

) means that the
evolution of
\tilde{c}
is not constrained
to be as Eq. (
82
).

Let us consider the subclass of
non-shift-symmetric Horndeski theories with

238
), i.e.,

{\cal S}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Big[G_{2}(\phi,X)+G_{3}(\phi,X)\square\phi+G_{4}(\phi)R-f_{1}(\phi,X,Z)n+f_{2}(\phi,X,Z)\Big]+{\cal S}_{\rm m}\,.

In this case, the EFT functions

,
\Lambda
,

\tilde{c}
,

in Eqs. (

) and (

G_{2}
,

,

.

,

IV
EFT action with the

\alpha
-basis parameters

In this section, we express the EFT action (
56
) in terms of the

\alpha
-basis dimensionless parameters
often used in the EFT of DE
[
37
,
100
,
24
,
22
]
.
As we already mentioned in
Sec.
II.1
, we choose the gauge
for the vector field
in which the spatial components
of
A_{\mu}
vanish. Taking into account the perturbation
\delta A_{0}
for the temporal vector component,
we consider the following configuration

A_{\mu}=\left[\bar{A}_{0}(t)+\delta A_{0},{\bm{0}}\right]\,,

(93)

where
\bar{A}_{0}(t)
is the background
value of
A_{0}
and

\delta A_{0}
is the perturbation.
For the lapse function
N
, we split it into the background and
perturbed parts, as

N=\bar{N}+\delta N
.
The perturbation of the metric component

g^{00}=-1/N^{2}
is given by

\delta g^{00}=\frac{2\delta N}{\bar{N}^{3}}-3\frac{(\delta N)^{2}}{\bar{N}^{4}}+{\cal O}\left(\frac{(\delta N)^{3}}{\bar{N}^{5}}\right)\,.

(94)

Now, we express the second-order Lagrangian

{\cal L}_{\rm D}^{(2)}
in Eq. (
58
)
by using the perturbations
\delta A_{0}
,
\delta N
, and
N^{i}
. In doing so, we exploit the following property

\frac{\delta\tilde{g}^{00}}{-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}}=\frac{\delta g^{00}}{-g^{00}_{\rm BG}}-\frac{2g_{M}}{(1+g_{M}A_{0})}\delta A_{0}\,,

(95)

where
g^{00}_{\rm BG}=-1/\bar{N}^{2}
.

IV.1
Second-order action from

{\cal L}{}_{\rm D}^{(2)}

Let us first study the contribution to the action (
56
) arising from
{\cal L}_{\rm D}^{(2)}
.
Up to second order in perturbations,
the scalar products
F_{\mu}F^{\mu}
,
q^{\mu}q_{\mu}
, and
q^{\mu}F_{\mu}

are given, respectively, by

\displaystyle F_{\mu}F^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n^{0}\nabla_{i}A_{0})(n^{0}\nabla^{i}A_{0})=\frac{\delta^{ij}\nabla_{i}\delta A_{0}\nabla_{j}\delta A_{0}}{a^{2}\bar{N}^{2}}\,,

(96)

\displaystyle q^{\mu}q_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N^{i}N_{i}}{N^{2}-N_{j}N^{j}}\simeq\frac{N^{i}N_{i}}{\bar{N}^{2}}\,,

(97)

\displaystyle q^{\mu}F_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{0}N^{i}\nabla_{i}\delta A_{0}}{\sqrt{N^{2}-N_{j}N^{j}}}\simeq\frac{N^{i}\nabla_{i}\delta A_{0}}{\bar{N}^{2}}\,.

(98)

Then, the Lagrangian (
58
)
can be expressed in the form

\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{\rm D}=\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{\rm ST}+\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{\delta\hat{A}_{0}}\,,

(99)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{\rm ST}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2M_{2}^{4}\left(\frac{\delta N}{\bar{N}}\right)^{2}-\bar{M}_{1}^{3}\frac{\delta N}{\bar{N}}\delta K-2m_{1}^{4}\frac{\delta N}{\bar{N}}\frac{\delta n}{\bar{n}}-m_{2}^{4}\frac{N^{i}N_{i}}{\bar{N}^{2}}\,,

(100)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{\delta\hat{A}_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla_{i}\delta\hat{A}_{0}\nabla^{i}\delta\hat{A}_{0}+g_{\rm eff}^{2}M_{2}^{4}\delta\hat{A}_{0}^{2}\right)+\left[\frac{\bar{m}_{1}^{2}\nabla_{i}N^{i}}{\sqrt{\gamma}_{1}\bar{N}}+g_{\rm eff}\left(\frac{1}{2}\bar{M}_{1}^{3}\delta K-2M_{2}^{4}\frac{\delta N}{\bar{N}}+m_{1}^{4}\frac{\delta n}{\bar{n}}\right)\right]\delta\hat{A}_{0}\,,

(101)

up to boundary terms.
Here,
g_{\rm eff}
and
\delta\hat{A}_{0}
are
defined by

g_{\rm eff}:=\frac{2g_{M}\bar{N}}{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}(1+g_{M}\bar{A}_{0})}\,,\qquad\delta\hat{A}_{0}:=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}}{\bar{N}}\delta A_{0}\,.

(102)

As we will see in Appendix
B
, we require that
\gamma_{1}>0
to avoid the ghost for intrinsic vector perturbations. We have used this property
for the definition of
\delta\hat{A}_{0}
in Eq. (
102
).

Under Fourier transformation, any perturbation
X
in real space
is decomposed as

X(t,{\bm{x}})=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{x}}}X_{k}(t)\,,

(103)

where
{\bm{k}}
is a comoving wavenumber
with
k=|{\bm{k}}|
. When we refer to quantities
in Fourier space, we omit the subscript
k

in the following. For example, the first
two terms in Eq. (
101
) can be interpreted as

(1/2)(k^{2}/a^{2}+g_{\rm eff}^{2}M_{2}^{4})\delta\hat{A}_{0}^{2}

in Fourier space.

The Lagrangian (
100
) does not contain any dependence of
\delta\hat{A}_{0}
. Furthermore, in vector-tensor theories, the term
-\tilde{c}(t)\tilde{g}^{00}
vanishes due to
the equation of motion for
\bar{A}_{0}
, so that

{\cal L}_{\rm D}^{\rm NL}
does not have
any dependence on
\delta\hat{A}_{0}
either.
Then, the variation of the full action

{\cal S}^{(2)}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}({\cal L}_{\rm D}^{\rm NL}+\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{\rm ST}+\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{\delta\hat{A}_{0}})+{\cal S}_{\rm m}

with respect to
\delta\hat{A}_{0}
amounts to
varying
{\cal S}^{(2)}_{\delta\hat{A}_{0}}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{\delta\hat{A}_{0}}
.
In real space, this leads to a non-dynamical equation for
\delta\hat{A}_{0}
that can be formally solved as

\delta\hat{A}_{0}=-\frac{1}{-\nabla_{i}\nabla^{i}+g_{\rm eff}^{2}M_{2}^{4}}\left[\frac{\bar{m}_{1}^{2}\nabla_{i}N^{i}}{\sqrt{\gamma}_{1}\bar{N}}+g_{\rm eff}\left(\frac{1}{2}\bar{M}_{1}^{3}\delta K-2M_{2}^{4}\frac{\delta N}{\bar{N}}+m_{1}^{4}\frac{\delta n}{\bar{n}}\right)\right]\,.

(104)

In Fourier space, the non-local pre-factor

\nabla_{i}\nabla^{i}
should be understood as

-k^{2}/a^{2}
.

The shift-symmetric scalar-tensor theories
correspond to the limits

g_{\rm eff}\to 0
and

\bar{m}_{1}^{2}\to 0
, in which case
we have
\delta\hat{A}_{0}=0
.
In vector-tensor theories,

\delta\hat{A}_{0}
is non-vanishing
and affects the dynamics of perturbations
through the constraint equation (

Perturbation equations of motion

In this section, we will derive the linear perturbation equations of motion for tensor and scalar perturbations. In the ADM line element (
6
),
we incorporate the perturbed fields
in the form

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{N}(t)\left(1+\alpha\right)\,,

(119)

\displaystyle N_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{N}(t)\left(\partial_{i}\chi+S_{i}\right)\,,

(120)

\displaystyle h_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}(t)\left[(1+2\zeta)\delta_{ij}+2\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E+\tilde{h}_{ij}+2\partial_{j}F_{i}\right]\,,

(121)

where
\alpha
,
\chi
,
\zeta
, and

S_{i}

and
F_{i}
are vector perturbations
obeying the conditions

\partial^{i}S_{i}=0
and

\partial^{i}F_{i}=0
, and
\tilde{h}_{ij}

is the tensor perturbation
satisfying the conditions

\partial^{i}\tilde{h}_{ij}=0

and
{\tilde{h}_{i}}^{i}=0

Since we focus on the dynamics of
linear perturbations, we can use the
partial derivative
\partial_{i}=\partial/\partial x^{i}

the covariant derivative on the right-hand
sides of Eqs. (

121

We recall that our EFT is formulated under
the irrotational ansatz, so the contribution
of vector-field perturbations
is ignored in the following discussion.
In Appendix

in the decoupling limit and obtain the relevant stability conditions.

In the configuration

A_{\mu}=(A_{0},\partial_{i}A)

the gauge condition
A=0

given in Eq. (
23

for the DM fluid to fix the spatial diffeomorphism.
In this case, we do not have additional gauge DOFs for scalar metric perturbations.
Since
\alpha

\chi

As we will see later, the perfect-fluid

In the following, we will derive

We will work both in real and Fourier space from now on and we will not distinguish between the real space and Fourier space variables. There will be no risk of confusion since the context will make it clear. Finally, from now on, spatial indices will be raised and lowered with the Euclidean metric

[

[

VI

perturbations and the CDM gravitational coupling

VI.2
Effective gravitational
coupling for CDM

Given that we have obtained the closed-form action (
154

G_{\rm eff}

relevant to the growth of large-scale structures.
For this purpose, we neglect the contribution
of matter fluids (baryons and radiation)
and set
\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}=0=\bar{p}_{\rm m}
and
\delta_{\rm m}=0=v_{\rm m}
.
Note that baryons also give contributions to
the CDM gravitational coupling, but
the main aim here is to see how the direct
DE and CDM couplings affect
G_{\rm eff}
.
We will focus on the dynamics of perturbations
on small scales in the sense that we will make
clear below.

Varying the action (
154

{\cal X}_{1}=\delta_{\rm c}/k
and
{\cal X}_{2}=\zeta

converting to the perturbation equations
for

\displaystyle\ddot{\delta}_{\rm c}+\left(3H+\frac{\dot{K}_{11}}{K_{11}}\right)\dot{\delta}_{\rm c}+\left(\frac{G_{11}}{K_{11}}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{M_{11}}{K_{11}}\right)\delta_{\rm c}+\frac{K_{12}}{K_{11}}k\ddot{\zeta}-\left(\frac{k}{a}\frac{B_{12}}{K_{11}}-\frac{\dot{K}_{12}+3HK_{12}}{K_{11}}\right)k\dot{\zeta}

We recall that

In the same limit, the other matrix components in Eqs. (

K_{11}\propto k^{0}
,

M_{11}\propto k^{0}
,

K_{12}\propto k^{-1}
,

,

,

M_{12}\propto k^{0}
,

The detailed analysis of Eqs. (
182

We can however gain some insights by considering some limits. In particular, we are interested in elucidating under which circumstances the growth of structures can be suppressed with respect to the
\Lambda
CDM model on sub-horizon scales.
Thus, we will focus on sufficiently sub-horizon modes in the sense explained below.

Since the CDM component has a vanishing propagation speed, we expect to have
slow
modes, i.e.,
those that do not oscillate and whose time scale is set
by
H
. The existence of this slow mode stems from
the fact that
G_{11}
vanishes at
leading order in the small-scale limit.
This can induce a slow mode for the perturbation
\zeta
as well. Since
G_{22}
is not zero,
we expect to have a
fast
oscillating mode
for
\zeta

having pressure. If we assume that the attractor solution for
\zeta
is dominated by the slow mode, we can neglect temporal derivatives over
(k^{2}/a^{2})\zeta

a^{2}H^{2}/k^{2}\ll 1

A cautionary remark is that we are implicitly assuming that there are no additional hierarchies introduced by the scales present in the coupling functions of the EFT action.
For instance, it may occur that
M_{22}
dominates over
G_{22}k^{2}/a^{2}
across a wide range of
sub-Hubble modes relevant to the linear regime. In other words, the sub-horizon regime for the DE component could be substantially below the Hubble horizon. We will assume that this does not happen and that the DE horizon parametrically coincides with the Hubble horizon. Under the above approximation scheme,
which we will refer to as the quasi-static approximation, Eq. (
183

\hat{c}_{\rm s}^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\zeta+\frac{b_{12}}{q_{\rm s}}\dot{\delta}_{\rm c}+\frac{2g_{12}+(\dot{b}_{12}+3Hb_{12})}{2q_{\rm s}}\delta_{\rm c}\simeq 0\,,

and
\hat{c}_{\rm s}^{2}

in Eqs. (
160

178

(185)

Using the quantity

defined
in Eq. (
159

\displaystyle{\cal C}

\displaystyle\frac{(5Hq_{\rm c}+\dot{q}_{\rm c})\nu_{\rm s}^{2}+(2b_{12}\dot{b}_{12}+5Hb_{12}^{2})\nu_{\rm s}-b_{12}^{2}\dot{\nu}_{\rm s}}{q_{\rm c}\nu_{\rm s}^{2}+b_{12}^{2}\nu_{s}}\,,

\displaystyle G_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi\bar{\rho}_{\rm c}(q_{\rm c}\nu_{\rm s}^{2}+b_{12}^{2}\nu_{\rm s})}\biggl\{4\mu_{11}\nu_{\rm s}^{2}+4g_{12}^{2}\nu_{\rm s}-\Big[3H^{2}(7-2\epsilon_{H})b_{12}^{2}+\dot{b}_{12}^{2}\Big]\nu_{\rm s}

\displaystyle-2(\ddot{b}_{12}+8H\dot{b}_{12}+2\dot{g}_{12}+4Hg_{12})b_{12}\nu_{\rm s}+2(\dot{b}_{12}+3Hb_{12}+2g_{12})b_{12}\dot{\nu}_{\rm s}\biggr\}\,,

VI.3
Theories with

\alpha_{m_{1}}=0
,

\alpha_{m_{2}}=0
,

Before proceeding to the discussion of the

,
\alpha_{m_{2}}
, and

.

and

\displaystyle V_{\rm s}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(2\alpha_{g}^{2}-1)\alpha_{B}^{2}+2(\alpha_{M}-\epsilon_{B}+\epsilon_{H}-1)\alpha_{B}+2\alpha_{M}-3\Omega_{\rm c}+2\epsilon_{H}

\displaystyle\frac{2}{M^{2}}(1+\alpha_{B})^{2}\nu_{\rm s}\,,

.
To ensure the stability of scalar perturbations, we consider the case in which

and

Since

\alpha_{B}
,
\alpha_{M}
, and

\alpha_{m_{\rm c}}

For

[

mediated by gravity.

G_{\rm eff}
.

In Eq. (
198

m_{\rm c}(t)

G_{\rm eff}
.
The time dependence of
m_{\rm c}(t)

acquired by the energy transfer between DM and

120

is suppressed

Our general expression of

Moreover, there is a new type of the energy

\alpha_{m_{1}}

VII.1
Energy transfer by

\dot{\epsilon}_{H}=0

{\cal G}_{\rm c}=-\frac{2(6H^{2}\alpha_{m_{1}}+5H\dot{\alpha}_{m_{1}}+\ddot{\alpha}_{m_{1}})+4H\epsilon_{H}[(\epsilon_{H}-4)H\alpha_{m_{1}}-\dot{\alpha}_{m_{1}}]}{3H^{2}\alpha_{m_{1}}\Omega_{\rm c}}+{\cal O}(\Omega_{\rm c}^{0},\dot{\epsilon}_{H})\,,

If the time variation of
\alpha_{m_{1}}

is negligibly small and
\epsilon_{H}
is suppressed relative to
\alpha_{m_{1}}

.
Since

is suppressed at low redshift.
Expanding the friction coefficient

The leading-order terms in Eqs. (

and (

We leave the detailed study about

The main message here is that the DE-DM interaction
arising from

for realizing the growth rate of

VII.2
Momentum transfer by

\alpha_{m_{2}}

The next example is the EFT functions given by

\alpha_{B}=0\,,\qquad\alpha_{m_{2}}\neq 0\,,\qquad\alpha_{m_{1}}=0\,,\qquad\alpha_{\bar{m}_{1}}=0\,.

(208)

The first condition
\alpha_{B}=0

We recall that the linear stability conditions

Momentum transfer
by
\alpha_{\bar{m}_{1}}

The next example is the EFT functions given by

\alpha_{\bar{m}_{1}}\neq 0\,,\qquad\alpha_{m_{1}}=0\,,\qquad\alpha_{m_{2}}=0\,,

(219)

From the consistency condition
(

\alpha_{m_{\rm c}}=0\,,

(220)

The coupling
\bar{m}_{1}^{2}
is present only for
vector-tensor theories with the

f_{1}
and

depending
on
\tilde{E}
.
In GP theories, the background solution of the type

(A_{0})^{p}\propto H^{-1}

and
G_{3}(\tilde{X})

[

Since
\alpha_{B}\neq 0

the non-vanishing EFT parameter
\alpha_{B}

in the following discussion.
In this case, there is a de Sitter attractor
characterized by
\epsilon_{H}=0

and
\Omega_{\rm c}=0
.
11
11

11

One could also
have accelerated solutions with
\alpha_{B}=0
from a minimum of the potential of the vector field. However, these solutions are prone to strong coupling problems owed to the vanishing of the propagation speed of the longitudinal mode that would require higher-order operators as
in the ghost-condensate scenarios.

We also assume that the time variations of

\alpha_{\bar{m}_{1}}
,
\alpha_{B}
,
\alpha_{K}

VII.4
An explicit example

Finally, we will consider a simple model characterized by

\tilde{\alpha}_{K}=6\,,\qquad\alpha_{m_{1}}={\rm constant}\neq 0\,,\qquad\alpha_{m_{2}}={\rm constant}\neq 0\,,

(222)

while all the other

\alpha
-basis parameters are zero.
We have chosen
\tilde{\alpha}_{K}=6
because it nicely leads to a diagonal kinetic matrix
{\bm{K}}
that simplifies the perturbation equations of motion. Furthermore, we will assume a
\Lambda
CDM
background evolution, so that

\epsilon_{H}=3\Omega_{\text{c}}/2
and

\dot{\Omega}_{\rm c}=3H\Omega_{\rm c}(\Omega_{\rm c}-1)
.

After eliminating non-dynamical perturbations,
the dynamics of
\delta_{\rm c}
and
\zeta

are governed by the following equations

\displaystyle\ddot{\delta}_{\text{c}}+2H\nu_{\text{eff}}\dot{\delta}_{\text{c}}-\frac{3}{2}H^{2}\Omega_{\text{c}}m_{\text{eff}}^{2}\,\delta_{\text{c}}=\frac{k^{2}}{4a^{2}(3\Omega_{\text{c}}+\alpha_{m_{2}})}\left[\frac{2(\alpha_{m_{1}}+2\alpha_{m_{2}})}{H}\dot{\zeta}+\Big\{12\alpha_{m_{2}}(1-\Omega_{\text{c}})+\alpha_{m_{1}}(3\Omega_{\text{c}}+\alpha_{m_{2}})\Big\}\zeta\right]\,,

(223)

\displaystyle\ddot{\zeta}+3H\dot{\zeta}-\frac{\alpha_{m_{2}}k^{2}}{6a^{2}}\zeta=-\frac{\alpha_{m_{1}}+2\alpha_{m_{2}}}{12}H\dot{\delta}_{\text{c}}+\frac{6\alpha_{m_{1}}(\Omega_{\text{c}}-1)-\alpha_{m_{2}}(6\Omega_{\text{c}}-\alpha_{m_{1}})}{24}H^{2}\delta_{\text{c}}\,,

(224)

where we have defined

\displaystyle\nu_{\text{eff}}:=

\displaystyle\frac{3(2-\alpha_{m_{2}})\Omega_{\text{c}}+5\alpha_{m_{2}}}{2(3\Omega_{\text{c}}+\alpha_{m_{2}})}\,,

(225)

\displaystyle m_{\text{eff}}^{2}:=

\displaystyle\Big\{3\Omega_{\text{c}}\big[6(4+4\alpha_{m_{2}}-3\alpha_{m_{1}})\Omega_{\text{c}}+\alpha_{m_{1}}(8+\alpha_{m_{1}})-2\alpha_{m_{2}}(8+5\alpha_{m_{1}})\big]+\alpha_{m_{1}}\alpha_{m_{2}}(20+\alpha_{m_{1}})\Big\}

\displaystyle/\big[24\Omega_{\text{c}}(3\Omega_{\text{c}}+\alpha_{m_{2}})\big]\,.

(226)

In the regime of small
\alpha_{m_{1}}
and
\alpha_{m_{2}}

close to 0, we can expand these
expressions to obtain

\displaystyle\nu_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{m_{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\Omega_{\text{c}}}\right)\,,

(227)

\displaystyle m_{\text{eff}}^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 1-\frac{3}{4}\alpha_{m_{1}}\left(1-\frac{4}{9\Omega_{\text{c}}}\right)+\alpha_{m_{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\Omega_{\text{c}}}\right)\,,

\delta_{\text{c}}

\zeta

The mode

\alpha_{m_{1}}>5

D_{2}
. It is interesting to note that both modes give decaying solutions for

\alpha_{m_{2}}=5

so it realizes a weaker clustering.
It may look surprising that
\alpha_{m_{2}}
does not appear in this analysis of super-Hubble modes,

\alpha_{m_{2}}
represents

Let us now turn our attention to smaller scales,
i.e., below the corresponding sound horizon.
The propagation speed for our choice of EFT functions
is given by

c_{\text{s}}^{2}=-\frac{12\alpha_{m_{2}}\Omega_{\text{c}}-\alpha_{m_{1}}(\alpha_{m_{1}}+4\alpha_{m_{2}})}{24(3\Omega_{\text{c}}+\alpha_{m_{2}})}\,.

\alpha_{m_{2}}<0

to avoid Laplacian instabilities. We will see that the suppression of structures requires
\alpha_{m_{2}}<0
,
so we will assume that we are in this case from now on. Let us warn however that this regime cannot be sustained during the radiation-dominated epoch to avoid running into ghosts and/or Laplacian instabilities, so our choice of EFT functions must be understood as a proxy for the regime of interest. This regime must also be abandoned in the future evolution if the attractor corresponds to
\Omega_{\text{c}}\to 0

VIII
Conclusions

In this paper, we have constructed
the EFT of coupled DE and DM, where DE interacts with DM through the energy and momentum transfers. For the DE sector,
we have taken the unified description of
vector-tensor and scalar-tensor theories
by assuming the luminal
propagation of gravitational waves.
This prescription allows us to accommodate
not only GP theories
but also shift-symmetric and
non-shift-symmetric Horndeski theories as special cases.
The boundaries between different theories are characterized by the presence of a gauge coupling constant
g_{M}
and the existence of consistency conditions.
For the DM sector, we have used the effective description of fluids
based on three scalar fields.

The EFT building blocks for DE are given
by the three-dimensional intrinsic and extrinsic curvatures as well as

n_{\mu}
,
\tilde{g}^{00}=g^{00}(1+g_{M}A_{0})^{2}
, and

F_{\mu}=n^{\alpha}F_{\mu\alpha}
, where
F_{\mu\nu}=2\nabla_{[\mu}A_{\nu]}

is the field strength of the vector
field
A_{\nu}
.
The temporal part of the gauge
transformation has been fixed
to a unitary gauge in which
the preferred timelike vector
corresponds to
v_{\mu}=\delta^{0}_{\mu}+g_{M}A_{\mu}
,
with the configuration
A_{\mu}=(A_{0},{\bm{0}})
.
For
g_{M}\neq 0
, this leads to
a symmetry-breaking pattern
different from that in scalar-tensor
theories. In the DM sector,
we have chosen the gauge condition

\phi^{i}=x^{i}
for three scalar
fields, under which the spatial part
of the gauge transformation is fixed.
The DM Lagrangian is constructed
from the energy density

\hat{m}_{\rm c}n

with the bare mass
\hat{m}_{\rm c}
,
by assuming that DM behaves as CDM
with the vanishing pressure and
sound speed. The building blocks
in the DM sector are given by its
number density
n
and
four velocity
u^{\mu}
.

The interactions between DE and DM
can be constructed from the EFT building
blocks mentioned above.
The coupling associated with
the energy transfer is restricted
to be linear in
n
to avoid the
non-vanishing propagation speed
for DM. The leading operators describing
the energy transfer between DE
and DM consist of the products

\Delta m_{\rm c}(t)n
and

\delta\tilde{g}^{00}\delta n
,
where
\Delta m_{\rm c}(t)
is a
time-dependent function.
The other scalars associated
with the momentum transfer are
n_{\mu}u^{\mu}
and
F_{\mu}u^{\mu}
or, equivalently
q_{\mu}q^{\mu}
and
F_{\mu}q^{\mu}
, where

q^{\mu}=u^{\mu}+n^{\mu}(n_{\nu}n^{\nu})
.
With these ingredients, we can write the total action for the EFT of coupled DE and DM in the form
(
56
). The energy transfer
is weighed by the two Lagrangians

-m_{\rm c}(t)n
and

-m_{1}^{4}(\delta n/\bar{n})[\delta\tilde{g}^{00}/(-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG})]
,
whereas the momentum transfer
consists of the two operators

-m_{2}^{4}q^{\mu}q_{\mu}
and

-\bar{m}_{1}^{2}q^{\mu}F_{\mu}

In vector-tensor EFT, the coefficients
in the action (

(

The shift-symmetric scalar-tensor

theories correspond to the limit

without imposing
consistency conditions.

[
82
,
147
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm DM}+\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}=-f_{1}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Z},\tilde{E})n+f_{2}(\tilde{X},\tilde{Z},\tilde{E})\,,

(255)

with
\tilde{X}=-A_{\mu}A^{\mu}/2
,
\tilde{Z}=A_{\mu}u^{\mu}
, and

\tilde{E}=-A^{\mu}F_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}
.

The difference from scalar-tensor
theories is the absence of preferred
hypersurfaces because
the vector field
A_{\mu}
, which is supposed to be
non-vanishing and timelike, is not hypersurface orthogonal, in general. Nonetheless, one can introduce the projection tensor to decompose tensors into parallel and orthogonal components with
respect to the preferred vector.
To make a clear connection to scalar-tensor theories, we introduce the Stückelberg field
A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}+g_{M}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}\phi

and fix the gauge to
\phi=t
, where

g_{M}>0
is the gauge coupling.
This implies the following replacement rule

\displaystyle A_{\mu}\to-\frac{\sqrt{-\tilde{g}^{00}}}{g_{M}}\tilde{n}_{\mu}\,,

(256)

where
\tilde{n}_{\mu}
is
the unit vector, and

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{00}:=g^{00}+2g_{M}A^{0}+g_{M}^{2}A_{\mu}A^{\mu}\,.

(257)

Note that
\tilde{n}^{\mu}
is a future-directed vector, meaning that the vector field of the GP theories is chosen to be past-directed
in our construction.
The kinematical quantities are, as usual,
defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{\mu\nu}:=\tilde{h}_{(\mu}{}^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\tilde{n}_{|\nu)}\,,\qquad\tilde{\omega}_{\mu\nu}:=\tilde{h}_{[\mu}{}^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\tilde{n}_{|\nu]}\,,\qquad\tilde{a}_{\mu}:=\tilde{n}^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\tilde{n}_{\mu}\,,

(258)

with the projection
\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}:=g_{\mu\nu}+\tilde{n}_{\mu}\tilde{n}_{\nu}
.
We can also define an object analogous to the curvature with respect to the projection tensor
\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}
. While details can be found in Appendix A of Ref.
[
24
]
, the equation relevant to us is the Raychaudhuri equation

\displaystyle R={}^{(3)}\!\tilde{R}+\tilde{K}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{K}^{\mu\nu}-\tilde{K}^{2}-\tilde{\omega}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{\omega}^{\mu\nu}-2\nabla_{\mu}(\tilde{a}^{\mu}-\tilde{K}\tilde{n}^{\mu})\,.

(259)

Finally, the field strength is decomposed into

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mu}:=\tilde{n}^{\alpha}F_{\mu\alpha}\,,\qquad\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}:=\tilde{h}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu}\tilde{h}^{\beta}{}_{\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}\,,

(260)

where the “magnetic” part
\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}
is related to the vorticity
\tilde{\omega}_{\mu\nu}
,
via
\tilde{\omega}_{\mu\nu}=-g_{M}\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}/2\sqrt{-\tilde{g}^{00}}
.
These ingredients allow us to take the

3+1
decomposition of
GP theories (
35
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm GP}=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{F}_{\mu}\tilde{F}^{\mu}-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}+G_{2}-(2\tilde{X})^{3/2}g_{3,X}\tilde{K}+\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}\left[{}^{(3)}\!\tilde{R}+\tilde{K}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{K}^{\mu\nu}-\tilde{K}^{2}-\tilde{\omega}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{\omega}^{\mu\nu}\right],

(261)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{X}=\frac{-\tilde{g}^{00}}{2g_{M}^{2}}\,,\qquad\tilde{Z}=\sqrt{2\tilde{X}(1+\tilde{q}^{\mu}\tilde{q}_{\mu})}\,,\qquad\tilde{E}=-\sqrt{2\tilde{X}}\tilde{q}^{\mu}\tilde{F}_{\mu}\,,

(262)

where
\tilde{q}^{\mu}:=u^{\mu}+\tilde{n}^{\mu}(\tilde{n}\cdot u)
, and
g_{3,\tilde{X}}
is defined by

G_{3}:=g_{3}+2\tilde{X}g_{3,\tilde{X}}
.

Now, we assume the irrotational ansatz
(
25
), corresponding to

\tilde{\omega}_{\mu\nu}\propto\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}=0
.
Then, the preferred vector
\tilde{n}_{\mu}
is
hypersurface orthogonal and all tilded
quantities are reduced to the untilded ADM
variables except for
\tilde{g}^{00}

(or equivalently
\tilde{X}
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm GP}\to\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu}F^{\mu}+G_{2}-(2\tilde{X})^{3/2}g_{3,\tilde{X}}K+\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}\left[{}^{(3)}\!R+K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}-K^{2}\right]\,,

(263)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}\to\sqrt{2\tilde{X}(1+q^{\mu}q_{\mu})}\,,\qquad\tilde{E}\to-\sqrt{2\tilde{X}}q^{\mu}F_{\mu}\,.

(264)

Hence, we obtain the unified form of
the EFT action (
56
) with
the following coefficients:

Dictionary to GP theories

Appendix B
Ghost-free conditions for vector perturbations in the decoupling limit

In this section, we study ghost-free conditions of vector-type perturbations in the EFT of
vector-tensor theories. Note that the EFT action (
56
) is valid only under the irrotational ansatz (
25
).
We should first upgrade the untilded variables
into the tilded variables introduced in Appendix
A.2
to incorporate
vector perturbations:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm D}^{\rm NL}

\displaystyle\to\frac{M_{*}^{2}}{2}f(t)\left({}^{(3)}\!\tilde{R}+\tilde{K}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{K}^{\mu\nu}-\tilde{K}^{2}-\tilde{\omega}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{\omega}^{\mu\nu}\right)-\Lambda(t)-c(t)\tilde{g}^{00}-d(t)K-m_{\rm c}(t)n\,,

(277)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm D}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{2}M_{2}^{4}{}(t)\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{g}^{00}}{-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{M}_{1}^{3}(t)\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{g}^{00}}{-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}}\right)\delta\tilde{K}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{1}(t)\tilde{F}_{\mu}\tilde{F}^{\mu}

\displaystyle~~~-m_{1}^{4}(t)\frac{\delta n}{\bar{n}}\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{g}^{00}}{-\tilde{g}^{00}_{\rm BG}}\right)-m_{2}^{4}(t)\tilde{q}^{\mu}\tilde{q}_{\mu}-\bar{m}_{1}^{2}(t)\tilde{q}^{\mu}\tilde{F}_{\mu}+\cdots\,,

(278)

where
\cdots
are operators that vanish under the irrotational ansatz (
25
). The missing operator at leading order in the derivative equation is
\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}
alone.
As we will see shortly, however, this operator
is unimportant for our discussion.

For simplicity, we take the decoupling limit of gravity. To do so, let us introduce the Stückelberg fields according to

\displaystyle t\to t+\pi_{0}\,,\qquad A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}-g_{M}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}\pi_{0}\,,\qquad x^{i}\to x^{i}+\pi^{i}\,,

(279)

and ignore metric perturbations.
Up to the necessary orders of
perturbations, we find

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\simeq(0,\partial_{i}A_{0}-\dot{A}_{i})\,,\qquad\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}\simeq\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&2\partial_{[i}A_{j]}\end{pmatrix}\,,

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\simeq\bar{n}\left\{1+\partial_{i}\pi^{i}-\frac{1}{2}\left[\dot{\pi}_{i}^{2}+(\partial_{i}\pi_{j})^{2}-(\partial_{i}\pi^{i})^{2}\right]\right\}\,,\qquad q^{\mu}\simeq(0,-\dot{\pi}^{i})\,.

(280)

The expressions for
\tilde{g}^{00},\tilde{K}_{\mu\nu}
and
{}^{(3)}\!\tilde{R}
,
which are irrelevant to vector stability
conditions, can be found in
Ref.
[
24
]
.
Focusing on vector perturbations,
the relevant operators in the decoupling
limit are thus

\displaystyle-m_{\rm c}(t)n+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{1}(t)\tilde{F}_{\mu}\tilde{F}^{\mu}-m_{2}^{4}(t)\tilde{q}^{\mu}\tilde{q}_{\mu}-\bar{m}_{1}^{2}(t)\tilde{q}^{\mu}\tilde{F}_{\mu}\,,

(281)

and the corresponding quadratic
Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{\rm V}=\frac{1}{2}(\bar{\rho}_{\rm c}-2m_{2}^{4})\dot{\pi}_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{1}\dot{A}_{i}^{2}-\bar{m}_{1}^{2}\dot{A}_{i}\dot{\pi}_{i}+\cdots\,,

(282)

where
\bar{\rho}_{\rm c}=m_{\rm c}\bar{n}
.
As a result, the ghost-free conditions
for vector perturbations are

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{\rm c}-2m_{2}^{4}>0\,,\qquad\gamma_{1}>0\,,\qquad\gamma_{1}(\bar{\rho}_{\rm c}-2m_{2}^{4})>\bar{m}_{1}^{4}\,.

(283)

Let us briefly comment on the condition
to avoid the Laplacian instability.
The gradient terms of vector perturbations
are generated by
\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}
,
whose sign is thus fixed by the stability.
However, the operator
\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}
does not contribute to the scalar and tensor perturbations,
so that the analysis in the main text is not
affected.
This is different from the ghost-free conditions (
283
), especially
\gamma_{1}>0
, which should be taken into account in studying other sectors of perturbations.

Appendix C
EFT formulation of the matter sector

Let us assume a matter sector in the form of
a perfect fluid described in the EFT formalism. Analogously to the DM sector, we will introduce a set of three scalar fields
\psi^{a}

(where
a=1,2,3
), describing the Lagrangian coordinates of the matter fluid.
We then construct the fundamental matrix
B_{(\text{m})}^{ab}=\partial_{\mu}\psi^{a}\partial^{\mu}\psi^{b}
, so the action of
the matter sector is simply

\mathcal{S}_{\text{m}}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,F(B)\,,

(284)

with
B=\det B_{(\text{m})}^{ab}
that is related to the number density as
n_{\text{m}}=\sqrt{B}
. At first order in scalar perturbations,
we have

\psi^{a}=\lambda_{\text{m}}(x^{a}+\partial^{a}\pi_{\text{m}})\,,

(285)

where
\lambda_{\text{m}}
is a constant and
\pi_{\text{m}}
denotes the longitudinal phonon perturbation of the fluid. The energy density and pressure of
the fluid are given, respectively,
by
\rho_{\text{m}}=-F(B)
and
p_{\text{m}}=-2BF_{,B}+F(B)
, so that
\rho_{\text{m}}+p_{\text{m}}=-2BF_{,B}
.
The density contrast can be computed as

\delta_{\text{m}}:=\frac{\delta\rho_{\text{m}}}{\rho_{\text{m}}}=\frac{\delta F}{F}=\frac{2BF_{,B}}{F}\left(\nabla^{2}\pi_{\text{m}}-\nabla^{2}E-3\zeta\right)\,.

(286)

This relation permits the density contrast to be related to the phonon perturbation in any gauge.
In particular, in the unitary gauge with
\pi_{\text{m}}=0
, the above expression relates the density contrast
of the fluid with e.g., the metric
perturbation
E
, as it has been exploited in the main text. It is important to notice however that the unitary gauge can only be imposed on one of the fluids and we will maintain our choice of unitary gauge for the DM component. Let us also
note that, by taking
F(B)\propto\sqrt{B}

and in the unitary gauge, we naturally recover the relations used for the DM sector.
If we go to Fourier space, we can obtain the (non-local) relation of the phonon field
\pi_{\text{m}}
and the corresponding density contrast:

\pi_{\text{m}}=E-\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left(\frac{F}{2BF_{,B}}\delta_{\text{m}}+3\zeta\right)\,.

(287)

This relation, together with the corresponding one for the DM sector obtained in the unitary gauge,

E=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left(\delta_{\text{c}}+3\zeta\right)\,,

(288)

allows to use
\delta_{c}
and
\delta_{\text{m}}
instead of
E
and
\pi_{\text{m}}
. The quadratic action for the matter sector reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{m}}=

\displaystyle-\int{\rm d}^{4}xNa^{3}BF_{,B}\Bigg\{a^{2}\Big(\partial_{i}\dot{\pi}_{\text{m}}-\frac{1}{a^{2}}\partial_{i}\chi\Big)^{2}-c_{\text{m}}^{2}\Big(3\zeta-\nabla^{2}\pi_{\text{m}}+\nabla^{2}E\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{F}{2BF_{,B}}\left[3\zeta^{2}-\alpha^{2}+6\alpha\zeta+\frac{1}{a^{2}}(\partial_{i}\chi)^{2}+2\nabla^{2}E\Big(\alpha+\zeta-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}E\Big)\right]

\displaystyle+\left[2\nabla^{2}E\Big(\zeta+\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}E\Big)-2\alpha\nabla^{2}\pi+6\alpha\zeta+3\zeta^{2}\right]\Bigg\}\,.

(289)

From this expression, we immediately see that the condition for avoiding a ghostly matter phonon is given by
BF_{,B}<0
, which, in terms of the density and pressure, recovers the condition
\rho_{\text{m}}+p_{\text{m}}>0
, i.e.,
the null energy condition.
We can alternatively express the quadratic
action in terms of the density contrast
instead of the phonon field by using the relation (
287
). The resulting quadratic action coincides with Eq. (
140
) upon integrating out the non-dynamical field
v_{\text{m}}
. We can then complement the matter action with the remaining part describing the interacting EFT sector and obtain the corresponding full quadratic action. It is interesting to notice that the high-frequency limit leads to a diagonal kinetic matrix when using the density contrast variables. However, if expressed in terms of the original phonon field, the kinetic matrix exhibits off-diagonal terms mixing
E
and
\pi_{\text{m}}
.

Terminating Hybrid Tableaus for Ordered Models
Yuki Nishimura

Abstract
Hybrid logic extends modal logic with special propositions called nominals, each of which is true at only one state in a model. This enables us to describe some properties of binary relations, such as irreflexivity and anti-symmetry, which are essential to treat partial orders. We present terminating tableau calculi complete with respect to some classes of models whose accessibility relations are strictly partially ordered, unbounded strictly partially ordered, partially ordered, strictly totally ordered, and totally ordered.



Keywords:

Modal logic, hybrid logic, tableau calculus, decision procedure, bulldozing, partial order, total order.

1
Introduction

1.1
Background and Results

How does time flow? Prior formalized the tense in natural language using modal logic, which he named
tense logic

[
15
]
. Since then, logics dealing with time have been broadly studied under the name of
temporal logic
. One feature of temporal logic is that transitivity is often imposed on the structure. Indeed, Goldblatt’s textbook
[
10
]
states: “But it is natural also to require a temporal ordering to be transitive.” In addition, temporal logics, such as LTL and CTL, handle ordered structures in reasoning about time (see also
[
8
,
11
]
).

Blackburn
[
3
]
extended Prior’s tense logic by introducing
nominal tense logic
, which incorporates
nominals
—propositional variables that are true in exactly one state. This addition allows for defining properties of binary relations, such as irreflexivity, asymmetry, and anti-symmetry, which cannot be expressed in basic modal logic. The paper introduced axioms for these properties and proved the completeness and decidability of logics associated with various order structures, including partial and total orders. Notably, the logic
\mathbf{I4D}
, corresponding to models with unbounded strict partial orders, remains decidable despite lacking the finite frame property. Blackburn later named this framework
hybrid logic
, which has since been extensively studied. For more details on hybrid logic, see
[
4
,
2
,
1
,
12
,
7
]
.

In this paper, we construct tableau calculi
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{I4}}
,
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{I4D}}
,
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{PO}}
,
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{STO}}
, and
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{TO}}
for models with strict partial orders, unbounded strict partial orders, partial orders, strict total orders, and total orders, respectively, and demonstrate their completeness and termination.
These five systems correspond to the logics
\mathbf{I4}
,
\mathbf{I4D}
, and
\mathbf{PO}
,
\mathbf{LIN}_{s}
, and
\mathbf{LIN}
in
[
3
]
, respectively.

The key ingredient of our proof is the method called
bulldozing
, which destroys
undesirable states
in the model and transforms them into a desirable form. It is important to note that bulldozing transitive models can result in the construction of infinite models. However, even if the counterexample model is infinite, the existence of such a model can be demonstrated through a finite procedure. From this, we can provide a distinct proof of the decidability of the logics, as in
[
3
]
.

1.2
Related Works

Here, we review the previous study of tableau calculi for hybrid logic.

A tableau calculus for hybrid logic was first proposed by Tzakova
[
17
]
as a prefixed tableau calculus. The tableau calculus proposed by Bolander and Blackburn
[
5
]
is a complete and terminating system for the basic hybrid logic based on
\mathbf{K}
. Their later research
[
6
]
extended these results, proposing complete and terminating tableau calculi for a wider range of hybrid logics. Using this approach, we can now construct complete and terminating tableau calculi for the hybridized versions of the 15 logics belonging to the so-called modal cube
[
9
, §8]
.

However, there are tableau calculi for other logics that have not yet been studied. In particular, the difficulty of
\mathbf{I4D}
is believed to stem from its lack of the finite frame property. In fact, Bolander and Blackburn
[
6
]
describe this challenge as follows.

But then we are faced with the task of combining such conditions as (irr), (sym), (asym), (antisym), (intrans), (uniq) and (tree) with (trans), and here matters are likely to be much trickier. Certainly loop-checks will usually be required, but it is unclear to us at present what kinds of general results we can hope for here, or what languages we can prove them for. To give an idea of the difficulties involved, note that even such a simple looking combination as (trans)
+
(irr) does not have the finite frame property (consider the formula
F\top\land GF\top
, for example).

Takagi and the author
[
13
]
constructed a tableau calculus for hybrid logic corresponding to undirected graphs. To prove completeness, it was necessary to use not only traditional loop-checking but also model surgery. The methods used in that work are the basis for the proofs in this paper.

Preliminaries

2.1
Kripke Semantics for Hybrid Logic

Here, we review a hybrid logic with the
satisfaction operators

@_{i}
. See
[
7
]
for more details on the semantics of hybrid logic.

Definition 2.1
.

We have a countably infinite set
\mathbf{Prop}
of
propositional variables
and another countably infinite set
\mathbf{Nom}
of
nominals
, which is disjoint from
\mathbf{Prop}
. The
formulas

\varphi
of hybrid logic are defined inductively as follows:

\displaystyle\varphi\Coloneqq p\mid i\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\varphi\lor\varphi\mid\Diamond\varphi\mid\Box\varphi\mid@_{i}\varphi,

where
p\in\mathbf{Prop}
and
i\in\mathbf{Nom}
.

We write
\varphi\rightarrow\psi
to mean
\neg\varphi\lor\psi
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
Kripke model

\mathcal{M}=(W,R,V)
, or merely
model
, is defined as follows:

•

W
is a non-empty set.

•

R
is a binary relation on
W
.

•

V
is a function
V:\mathbf{Prop}\ \cup\ \mathbf{Nom}\to\mathcal{P}(W)
such that
V(i)=\{w\}\ \text{for some}\ w\in W
for each
i\in\mathbf{Nom}
, where
\mathcal{P}(W)
denotes the powerset of
W
.

Furthermore, we call a tuple
\mathcal{F}=(W,R)
that satisfies the first two conditions above a
Kripke frame
(or, shortly,
frame
).

This definition reflects the key property of nominals: each nominal is true in only one world. This paper uses
wRv
to mean
(w,v)\in R
, and
i^{V}
to mean the world
w\in W
such that
V(i)=\{w\}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a model
\mathcal{M}
, a possible world
w
in
\mathcal{M}
, and a formula
\varphi
, the
satisfaction relation

\mathcal{M},w\models\varphi
is defined inductively as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},w\models p

\displaystyle\iff w\in V(p),\text{where}\ p\in\mathbf{Prop}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},w\models i

\displaystyle\iff w=i^{V},\text{where}\ i\in\mathbf{Nom}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},w\models\neg\varphi

\displaystyle\iff\text{not}\ \mathcal{M},w\models\varphi\ (\mathcal{M},w\not\models\varphi)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},w\models\varphi\land\psi

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{M},w\models\varphi\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{M},w\models\psi

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},w\models\varphi\lor\psi

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{M},w\models\varphi\ \text{or}\ \mathcal{M},w\models\psi

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},w\models\Diamond\varphi

\displaystyle\iff\text{there exists}\ v\ \text{such that}\ wRv\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{M},v\models\varphi

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},w\models\Box\varphi

\displaystyle\iff\text{for all}\ v,\ \text{if}\ wRv,\ \text{then}\ \mathcal{M},v\models\varphi

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},w\models@_{i}\varphi

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{M},i^{V}\models\varphi.

Definition 2.4
.

A formula
\varphi
is said to be
valid
(denoted by
\models\varphi
) if
\mathcal{M},w\models\varphi
holds for all models
\mathcal{M}
and all of its worlds
w
.

For simplicity, the rest of the paper deals only with the negation normal form (NNF, in short) of formulas. For the satisfaction operators, a formula
\neg@_{i}\varphi
is equivalent to
@_{i}\neg\varphi
. That is, for any model and its possible world
w
, a formula
\varphi
, and a nominal
i\in\mathbf{Nom}
, we have

\mathcal{M},w\models\neg@_{i}\varphi\iff\mathcal{M},w\models@_{i}\neg\varphi.

Transformations to the NNF involving Boolean and modal operators can be done in the usual way. In the rest of this paper, we only deal with NNF formulas. If we write
\neg\varphi
, we assume it as the abbreviation of an NNF of it.

At last, we review some properties of a binary relation.

Definition 2.5
.

Given a binary relation
R
, we define the following properties:

\displaystyle R\text{ is \emph{serial} }

\displaystyle\iff\forall x\exists y.xRy

\displaystyle R\text{ is \emph{reflexive} }

\displaystyle\iff\forall x.xRx

\displaystyle R\text{ is \emph{irreflexive} }

\displaystyle\iff\forall x.\neg(xRx)

\displaystyle R\text{ is \emph{anti-symmetric} }

\displaystyle\iff\forall xy.(xRy\ \&\ yRx\implies x=y)

\displaystyle R\text{ is \emph{transitive} }

\displaystyle\iff\forall xyz.(xRy\ \&\ yRz\implies xRz)

\displaystyle R\text{ is \emph{trichotomous}}

\displaystyle\iff\forall xy.(xRy\text{ or }yRx\text{ or }x=y)

\displaystyle R\text{ is \emph{total}}

\displaystyle\iff\forall xy.(xRy\text{ or }yRx).

Also, we call some types of binary relations by special names:

•

R
is a
strict partial order
if it is both irreflexive and transitive.

•

R
is an
unbounded strict partial order
if it is serial, irreflexive, and transitive.

•

R
is a
partial order
if it is reflexive, anti-symmetric, and transitive.

•

R
is a
strict total order
if it is irreflexive, transitive, and trichotomous.

•

R
is a
partial order
if it is reflexive, anti-symmetric, transitive, and total.

Given a model
\mathcal{M}=(W,R,V)
, we say that
\mathcal{M}
is irreflexive if
R
is irreflexive. We use the same terminology for other properties of a binary relation. Moreover, we define some classes of models:

•

\mathcal{M}
is a
strict partially ordered model
, or an
SPO model
, if
R
is a strict partial order.

•

\mathcal{M}
is an
unbounded strict partially ordered model
, or a
USPO model
, if
R
is an unbounded strict partial order.

•

\mathcal{M}
is a
partially ordered model
, or a
PO model
, if
R
is a partial order.

•

\mathcal{M}
is a
strict totally ordered model
, or an
STO model
, if
R
is a strict total order.

•

\mathcal{M}
is a
totally ordered model
, or an
TO model
, if
R
is a total order.

Basic Tableau Calculus

\mathbf{K(@)}
is an axiomatization of hybrid logic with an operator
@
, based on the minimal normal modal logic
\mathbf{K}
(see
[
2
]
for more details on the axiomatization of hybrid logic). We write
\mathbf{TAB}
to indicate the tableau calculus for hybrid logic
\mathbf{K(@)}

[
5

Definition 2.6
.

A
tableau
is a well-founded tree constructed in the following way:

•

Start with a formula of the form
@_{i}\varphi
(called the
root formula
), where
i\in\mathbf{Nom}
does not occur in the formula
\varphi
.

•

For each branch, extend it by applying rules (see Definition
1
). However, we can no longer add any formula in a branch if at least one of the following conditions is satisfied:

–

Every new formula generated by applying any rule already exists in the branch.

–

The branch is closed (Definition
2.7
defines what a closed branch is).

If a formula
\varphi
occurs in a branch
\Theta
, we write
\varphi\in\Theta
.

Definition 2.7
.

A branch
\Theta
of a tableau is
closed
if there exists a formula
\varphi
and a nominal
i
such that
@_{i}\varphi,@_{i}\neg\varphi\in\Theta
. We say that
\Theta
is
open
if it is not closed. A tableau is called

if all branches in the tableau are closed.

To understand

Definition 3.2
.

Given two formulas of the form
@_{i}\varphi
and
@_{j}\psi
,
@_{i}\varphi
is a

of
@_{j}\psi
if
\varphi
is a subformula of
\psi

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Theta
be a branch of a tableau. For every formula of the form
@_{i}\varphi
in
\Theta
, at least one of the following conditions is satisfied:

•

It is a prefixed subformula of the root formula of
\Theta
.

•

It is an accessibility formula.

•

It is a prefixed subformula of
@_{j}\Box\neg j
for some
j
.

Proof.

By induction on the number of applied rules. We provide the proof only for the cases
[\mathit{Irr}]
and
[\mathit{Trs}]
. The other cases are left to the reader.

[\mathit{Irr}]

The formula we add by
[\mathit{Irr}]
is the very formula of the form
@_{j}\Box\neg j
for some
j
that has already occurred in
\Theta
.

[\mathit{Trs}]

@_{j}\Box\varphi
is a prefixed subformula of
@_{i}\Box\varphi
. By the induction hypothesis,
@_{i}\Box\varphi
is either a prefixed subformula of the root formula or the formula of the form
@_{i}\Box\neg k
. Therefore,
@_{j}\Box\varphi
is a prefixed subformula of either the root formula or a formula
@_{k}\Box\neg k
. ∎

Next, we define the set

for each nominal

\Theta

\Theta

\Theta

Definition 3.4

Let
\Theta

\Theta

•

\Theta

Lemma 3.5
.

For all
i

\Theta

Proof.

T_{1}^{\Theta}(i)
is finite because for each

\Theta

is finite because the number of nominals occurring in the root formula of

∎

Let

Let
\Theta

Based on these preparations, we can give a precise definition of the quasi-urfather.

Definition 3.8

Here, we show the termination property for

•

Lemma 3.11

Let

3.3
Completeness

Once we construct a proof system, the next work is to show soundness and completeness. The soundness can be shown using
faithful models
(see, for example,
[
14
]
). Then, we show that this tableau calculus is complete with respect to the class of SPO models.

The basic strategy is the same as in
[
5
, Section 5.2]
or
[
13
, Section 3.3]
. That is, define
identity urfathers
, representative nominals in a branch, and use them to create a model from an open saturated branch of a tableau.

Definition 3.13
.

A branch
\Theta
of a tableau is
saturated
if every formula that can be generated by applying some rule already exists in
\Theta
. We call a tableau
saturated
if all of its branches are saturated.

If
\Theta
is a saturated branch, the following conditions hold:

•

If
@_{i}(\varphi\land\psi)\in\Theta
, then
@_{i}\varphi,@_{i}\psi\in\Theta
.

•

If
@_{i}\Diamond\varphi\in\Theta
which is not an accessibility formula and

is a quasi-urfather, then there exists some
j\in\mathbf{Nom}
such that
@_{i}\Diamond j,@_{j}\varphi\in\Theta
.

•

If
@_{i}\Box\varphi,@_{i}\Diamond j\in\Theta
, then
@_{j}\varphi\in\Theta
and
@_{j}\Box\varphi\in\Theta
.

•

For all
i
occurring in
\Theta
, we have
@_{i}\Box\neg i\in\Theta
.

Definition 3.14
.

Let
\Theta
be a tableau branch and

\Theta
. The

on
\Theta
(denoted by

2.

Moreover, we say that a nominal

is an

Let

By induction on the complexity of

Definition 3.19
.

Let

be a transitive frame. A

over

To solve this problem, we bulldoze all the reflexive points and turn a frame into an irreflexive one. This method was initially used by Segerberg
[
16
]
. The procedure for bulldozing is as follows. First, we insert a strict partial order into each cluster, aligning the possible worlds in a single row. Then, we copy this row and concatenate it infinitely. As a result, all clusters are unraveled into irreflexive infinite chains.

For us to deal with hybrid logic, we have to make a slight modification to define the valuation function. Since this paper deals only with finite models, it suffices to use the set
\mathbb{N}
of natural numbers to index clusters.

Definition 3.20
.

Given a model
\mathcal{M}=(W,R,V)

1.

\mathcal{M}
by
\mathbb{N}
, like

.

2.

Insert an arbitrary strict total order
<_{n}
in each cluster
C_{n}
.

3.

Define
C^{\prime}_{n}=C_{n}\times\mathbb{N}
(infinitely many copies of
C_{n}
).

4.

Define
W_{B}=W^{-}\cup\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}C^{\prime}_{n}
, where

is a set of irreflexive worlds.

5.

Define

by

if

and

Define
R_{B}
.

•

That is, a nominal is true only at the beginning of one of the copies.

Observe that this procedure makes the model both irreflexive and transitive.

.

If a model
\mathcal{M}=(W,R,V)
is transitive, then the bulldozed one

is irreflexive. If

, not

does not hold for any

. From these facts and the fact that
\Theta
is saturated, we can derive
@_{i_{1}}\Box\neg j\in\Theta
as follows:
@_{j}\Box\neg j\in\Theta
by
[\mathit{Irr}]
,
@_{k}\Box\neg j\in\Theta
since

and

are twins,
@_{l_{1}}\Box\neg j\in\Theta
by
[\mathit{Trs}]
, and
@_{i_{1}}\Box\neg j\in\Theta
since

and
i_{1}
are twins. Repeating this argument, we have
@_{i_{2}}\Box\neg j,\ldots,@_{i_{m}}\Box\neg j\in\Theta

, we obtain
@_{i}\neg j\in\Theta
. However, we also have
@_{i}j\in\Theta
from the fact that

. Therefore,
\Theta
is closed, which is a contradiction.
∎

From this fact, if we consider only the prefixed subformulas of the root formula, the copies of a possible world in a cluster are equivalent.

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
\Theta
be an open saturated branch. For any nominal
i\in W^{\Theta}
in some cluster
C_{l}
, formula
\varphi
such that
@_{i}\varphi
is a prefixed subformula of the root formula, and

, we have

\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(i,m)\models\varphi\iff\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(i,n)\models\varphi.

Proof.

By induction on the complexity of
\varphi
.
We give the proof only for the cases
\varphi=j
,
\Diamond\psi
, and
\Box\psi
. The other cases are left to the reader.

[\varphi=j]

Firstly, suppose that there is a nominal
j
such that
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(i,0)\models j
. Then, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},i\models j
. Since
j
occurs in the root formula,
i
does not belong to any cluster
C_{l}
by Lemma
3.23
, which is a contradiction.
Moreover,
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(i,n)\models j
never holds if
n\geq 1
from the way of bulldozing.
Therefore, for any

, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(i,n)\not\models j
.

[\varphi=\Diamond\psi]

Suppose
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(i,m)\models\Diamond\psi
. Then, there exists
w\in W^{\Theta}_{B}
such that
(i,m)R^{\Theta}_{B}w
and
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},w\models\psi
. If
w
is not in
C^{\prime}_{l}
, then
(i,n)R^{\Theta}_{B}w
immediately follows from the construction of
R^{\Theta}_{B}
, so
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(i,n)\models\Diamond\psi
holds. If not, that is,

, then we can assume that
w=(w^{\prime},k)
. If
w
is also reachable from
(i,n)
, there is nothing to prove. Even if not, it follows that
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(w^{\prime},n+1)\models\psi
from the induction hypothesis. Therefore, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(i,n)\models\Diamond\psi
.

[\varphi=\Box\psi]

Suppose
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(i,m)\models\Box\psi
. Take an arbitrary
w\in W^{\Theta}_{B}
such that
(i,n)R^{\Theta}_{B}w
, and we show that
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},w\models\psi
. If
w
does not belong to
C^{\prime}_{l}
, we have
(i,m)R^{\Theta}_{B}w
, and the proof ends straightforwardly. If not, assume
w=(w^{\prime},k)
. Suppose
w
is not reachable from
(i,m)
. Even in this case, we have that
(i,m)R^{\Theta}_{B}(w^{\prime},m+1)
. Then
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(w^{\prime},m+1)\models\psi

\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(w^{\prime},k)\models\psi
follows by the induction hypothesis. ∎

Now, we show the equivalence of a model from a tableau branch and the bulldozed one up to the contents of the root formula.

Lemma 3.25
.

Let
\Theta
be an open saturated branch,
i
be an identity urfather of
\Theta
, and
\varphi
be a formula such that
@_{i}\varphi
is a prefixed subformula of the root formula. Then we have

\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},i\models\varphi\iff\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},i_{B}\models\varphi,

where

i_{B}=\begin{cases}(i,0)&\text{if }i\text{ is in some }C_{n}\\
i&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By induction on the complexity of
\varphi
.

[\varphi=p,\neg p]

Straightforward.

[\varphi=j,\neg j]

In the case
\varphi=j
, we can proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},i\models j

\displaystyle\iff V^{\Theta}(j)=\{i\}

\displaystyle\iff V^{\Theta}_{B}(j)=\{i_{B}\}

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},i_{B}\models j.

We can do similarly in the case
\varphi=\neg j
.

[\varphi=\psi\land\chi,\psi\lor\chi]

Straightforward.

[\varphi=\Diamond\psi]

First, we show the left-to-right direction. Suppose
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},i\models\Diamond\psi
. Then, there exists
j\in W^{\Theta}
such that
iR^{\Theta}j
and
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},j\models\psi
. If
i
and
j
are not in the same cluster, then by the construction of
R^{\Theta}_{B}
, we have
i_{B}R^{\Theta}_{B}j_{B}
. Also,
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},j_{B}\models\psi
follows from the induction hypothesis. Hence,
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},i_{B}\models\Diamond\psi
holds. Suppose
i
and
j
are in the same cluster (it can be true that
i=j
) and
i_{B}R^{\Theta}_{B}j_{B}
fails. In this case, though, we see that
(i,0)R^{\Theta}_{B}(j,1)
holds. Also, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(j,0)\models\psi
by the induction hypothesis, and we obtain
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(j,1)\models\psi
by Lemma

. Therefore, we have
\mathcal{M},(i,0)\models\Diamond\psi
.

The proof of the other direction is straightforward from the fact that for any

,

implies
\alpha(i)R^{\Theta}\alpha(j)
.

[\varphi=\Box\psi]

First, to show the left-to-right direction, suppose that
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},i\models\Box\psi
. Take one
j
such that
i_{B}R^{\Theta}_{B}j
, and we show that
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},j\models\psi
.

From
i_{B}R^{\Theta}_{B}j
, we have
iR^{\Theta}\alpha(j)
. Then, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},\alpha(j)\models\psi
. If
\alpha(j)
does not belong to any clusters, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},j\models\psi
by induction hypothesis. If not,
j
must be the form

. By induction hypothesis, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(j^{\prime},0)\models\psi
. However, by Lemma

, we obtain
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B},(j^{\prime},n)\models\psi
.

The other direction is like the right-to-left direction in the case
\varphi=\Diamond\psi
.

[\varphi=@_{j}\psi]

This proof proceeds directly. ∎

Theorem 3.26
.

The tableau calculus
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{I4}}
is complete for the class of all SPO models.

Proof.

We show the contraposition. Suppose
\varphi
is not provable in
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{I4}}
. Then we can find a finite tableau in
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{I4}}
, whose root formula is
@_{i}\neg\varphi
, where

does not occur in
\varphi
. Thus, we can make an open and saturated branch
\Theta
. Then, by Lemma
3.17
, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(i)\models\neg\varphi
. Moreover, by Lemma
3.21
and Lemma

, we obtain an SPO model

and its world

that falsify

.
∎

By utilizing the termination, soundness, and completeness of the tableau calculus, we can provide a proof for the decidability of the logic
\mathbf{I4}
that is distinct from the one in
[
3
]
.
Given a formula
\varphi
, we can create a tableau whose root formula is
@_{i}\varphi
where

is not in
\varphi
.
We can make all the branches of it closed or saturated.
Owing to Theorem

If not, we can find an open saturated branch.
Then, there exists an SPO model (note that it might be infinite) that falsifies

For the remaining systems introduced hereafter, decidability is similarly derived as a corollary.

.

The proof of the termination property and completeness can be constructed by modifying some points in that

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Theta
be a branch of a tableau. For every formula of the form

.

.

If

If

5
For Partial Order

To construct a tableau calculus corresponding to the class of PO models, we need the rules:
[\mathit{Ref}]
for reflexivity,
[\mathit{A\text{-}sym}]
for anti-symmetry, and
[\mathit{Trs}]
for transitivity.

Here, we briefly check the intuitive meaning of the formula
@_{i}\Box(i\lor\Box\neg i)
as it may be unfamiliar to the reader. Take one world
w
and name it
i
. Next, take another world
v
that is reachable from but apart from
w
. Then, every world reachable from
v
does not verify
i
; that is, it cannot be the same as
w
. This is exactly anti-symmetry; if we move to another world, we cannot go back.

Definition 5.1
.

The tableau calculus
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{PO}}
is constructed by replacing
[\neg]
with
[\mathit{Eq}]
, adding the rules
[\mathit{Ref}]
,
[\mathit{A\text{-}sym}]
, and
[\mathit{Trs}]
, and adding the restriction
(\mathcal{D})
to
\mathbf{TAB}
.

5.1
Termination Property

We obtain the termination property for
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{PO}}
by modifying the discussion in Section
3.2
. First, observe that a subformula property in a slightly different format holds.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Theta
be a branch of a tableau of
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{PO}}
. For every formula of the form
@_{i}\varphi
in
\Theta
, one of the following conditions holds:

•

It is a prefixed subformula of the root formula of
\Theta
.

•

It is an accessibility formula.

•

It is a prefixed subformula of
@_{j}\Box(j\lor\Box\neg j)
for some
j
.

Next, we redefine the set

so that it works for the tableau calculus dealing with both anti-symmetry and transitivity. Note that the sets in the definition below are also finite.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\Theta
be a branch of a tableau. For every nominal
i
occurring in
\Theta
, the set
T^{\Theta}(i)
is a union of
T_{1}^{\Theta}(i)
and

, where:

•

is the set of formulas

such that

is a prefixed subformula of the root formula of

.

•

is the set of formulas

is a prefixed subformula of
@_{j}\Box(j\lor\Box\neg j)
for some

.

Also, we can check that the graph

remains a disjoint union of well-defined and finitely branching trees. The only new rule that may break this property is

; however,
[\mathit{Ref}]
only allows us to add an accessibility formula, and it makes no change to the relation

.

Then, we are ready to show the termination property.

Theorem 5.4
.

The tableau calculus
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{PO}}
has the termination property.

Proof.

For
\Theta
and its root formula
@_{i}\varphi
, we define
Q
and

as follows:

\displaystyle Q=

\displaystyle\{\psi\mid\psi\text{ is a subformula of }\varphi\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\{\psi\mid\psi\text{ is a subformula of }\Box(j\lor\Box\neg j)\text{ where }j\text{ occurs in }@_{i}\varphi\}

and

is the number of elements in

. The rest of the proof can be done like that of Theorem
3.12
.
∎

5.2
Completeness

When showing the completeness of
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{PO}}
, the process is the same; take identity urfathers, create a model, and bulldoze it. Using Definition
5.3
, we can keep adopting Definition
3.14
as the definition of identity urfathers, and relative lemmas still hold. From constructing a model, we need to make some changes.

Definition 5.5
.

Given an open saturated branch
\Theta
with a root formula
@_{i_{0}}\varphi_{0}
of a tableau in
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{PO}}
, a model
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}=(W^{\Theta},R^{\Theta},V^{\Theta})
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle W^{\Theta}

\displaystyle=\{i\mid i\text{ is an identity urfather on }\Theta\}

\displaystyle R_{e}^{\Theta}

\displaystyle=\{(v_{\Theta}(i),v_{\Theta}(j))\mid@_{i}\Diamond j\in\Theta\text{ and }i,j\in\mathrm{dom}(v_{\Theta})\}

\displaystyle R^{\Theta}

\displaystyle=(R_{e}^{\Theta})^{*}

\displaystyle V^{\Theta}(p)

\displaystyle=\{v_{\Theta}(i)\mid@_{i}p\in\Theta\},\text{ where }p\in\mathbf{Prop}

\displaystyle V^{\Theta}(i)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\{v_{\Theta}(i)\}&\text{if }i\in\mathrm{dom}(v_{\Theta})\\
\{i_{0}\}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\text{ where }i\in\mathbf{Nom}.

Here,
R^{*}
is the reflexive and transitive closure of
R
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\Theta
be an open saturated branch of a tableau in
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{PO}}
and
@_{i}\varphi
be a prefixed subformula of the root formula
@_{i_{0}}\varphi_{0}
of
\Theta
, where
i
denotes an identity urfather. Then we have the following proposition:

\text{if }@_{i}\varphi\in\Theta,\text{ then }\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(i)\models\varphi.

In particular,
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v_{\Theta}(i_{0})\models\varphi_{0}
.

Proof.

By induction on the complexity of
\varphi
.

The only case we have to watch out for is the case
\varphi=\Box\psi
. In this case, we automatically have
v_{\Theta}(i)R^{\Theta}v_{\Theta}(i)
by the reflexivity of
R^{\Theta}
, so we have to show that
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v^{\Theta}(i)\models\psi
.
By the saturation of the branch
\Theta
,
@_{i}\Diamond i\in\Theta
holds. Thus, by the assumption, we obtain
@_{i}\psi
in
\Theta
applying
[\Box]
. By induction hypothesis, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},v^{\Theta}(i)\models\psi
.
∎

From the definition,
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}
is always reflexive and transitive. However, the anti-symmetry of
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}
is not guaranteed. Then, we bulldoze it into a PO model.

To create not an SPO but a PO model, we redefine bulldozing by changing two points. One is that we only bulldoze proper clusters to keep a model reflexive. The other is that we insert a total order in each proper cluster instead of a strict total order.

Definition 5.7
.

Given a model
\mathcal{M}=(W,R,V)
, the bulldozed model
\mathcal{M}_{B^{\prime}}=(W_{B^{\prime}},R_{B^{\prime}},V_{B^{\prime}})
of
\mathcal{M}
is defined as follows:

1.

Index the
proper
clusters in
\mathcal{M}
by
\mathbb{N}
.

2.

Insert a total order
\leq_{n}
in each
C_{n}
.

3.

Define
W_{B^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime},R_{B^{\prime}},V_{B^{\prime}}
as we do in Definition
3.20
.

Lemma 5.8
.

If a model
\mathcal{M}=(W,R,V)
is reflexive and transitive, then the bulldozed one
\mathcal{M}_{B^{\prime}}=(W_{B^{\prime}},R_{B^{\prime}},V_{B^{\prime}})
is reflexive, transitive, and anti-symmetric.

Proof.

Reflexivity is straightforward, and transitivity can be shown as in Lemma
3.21
. Therefore, we show that
\mathcal{M}_{B^{\prime}}=(W_{B^{\prime}},R_{B^{\prime}},V_{B^{\prime}})
is anti-symmetric.

Take two worlds
w,v
such that
wR_{B^{\prime}}v
. If they do not belong to the same set
C^{\prime}_{n}
,
vR_{B^{\prime}}w
never holds; otherwise,
\alpha(w)R\alpha(v)
together with
\alpha(v)R\alpha(w)
means that they are in the same cluster. Then, suppose they are in the same set
C^{\prime}_{n}
. Then,
w
and
v
can be written as
w=(w^{\prime},l),v=(v^{\prime},m)
. From
wR_{B^{\prime}}v
, we have either
l<m
or,
l=m
and
w^{\prime}\leq_{n}v^{\prime}
. If
vR_{B^{\prime}}w
also holds, then it follows that
l=m
and
v^{\prime}\leq_{n}w^{\prime}
. Since
\leq_{n}
is a total order, we have
w^{\prime}=v^{\prime}
, which implies

.
∎

The key fact in the process of proving the completeness for
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{I4}}
is that every world in a cluster in
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}
is not named, and
[\mathit{Irr}]
plays an important role in proving it. In the following discussion, a similar situation happens; all the worlds in proper clusters are not named owing to
[\mathit{A\text{-}sym}]
, and the proof proceeds based on the following lemma.

.

Let
\Theta
be an open saturated branch of a tableau of
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{PO}}
,
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}
be a model constructed by
\Theta
, and

be a proper cluster of

, then every nominal

.

By

.

Suppose that we have a nominal

occurring in the root formula of
\Theta
such that
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta},i\models j
. Since
i
is in a proper cluster, we have some worlds
i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}
different from

such that
iR_{e}^{\Theta}i_{1}R_{e}^{\Theta}\cdots R_{e}^{\Theta}i_{m}R_{e}^{\Theta}i
. Moreover, we have
@_{j}\Box(j\lor\Box\neg j)\in\Theta
. Then, we obtain
@_{i_{1}}(j\lor\Box\neg j)\in\Theta

If

.

(b)

If

, and

contains both

and

Now, we can fill in the rest of the proof.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\Theta
be an open saturated branch. For any nominal

in some proper cluster

, formula

\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B^{\prime}},(i,m)\models\varphi\iff\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{B^{\prime}},(i,n)\models\varphi.

Let
\Theta
be an open saturated branch,

, and

be a formula such that

is a prefixed subformula of the root formula. Then we have

7
Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, we constructed tableau calculi
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{I4}}
,
\mathbf{TAB}_{\mathbf{I4D}}
,
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{PO}}
,
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{STO}}
, and
\mathbf{TAB}_{\textbf{TO}}
for hybrid logic and demonstrated their completeness and termination properties. Now, we have tableau calculi for models where possible worlds form some types of order: strict partial order, unbounded strict partial order, partial order, strict total order, and total order, respectively.

One approachable work is to generalize the methods introduced here to the broader classes of models. In the five tableau calculi discussed in this paper, we extended the definition of
T^{\Theta}(i)
to reconcile transitivity with other axioms in the completeness proof. This approach is likely to extend to formulas that do not contain
\Diamond
(referred to as
F
-free formulas
in
[
6
]
). Specifically, the following axioms are of interest:

•

asymmetry:
@_{i}\Box\Box\neg i

•

intransitivity:
@_{i}(\Box\Box\neg j\lor\Box\neg j)

•

tree-likeness:
@_{i}(@_{j}\Box\neg i\lor@_{k}\Box\neg i\lor@_{j}k)

•

uniqueness:
@_{i}(\Box j\lor\Box\neg j)
.

Combining these with transitive or symmetric models would allow us to construct tableau calculi corresponding to various classes of models

Another prospective project is the application to the proof of the finite model property.
By utilizing the completeness proof of the tableau presented here, we may be able to demonstrate the finite model property for hybrid logics corresponding to classes of models with ordered structures.

In the case of
\mathbf{I4}
, we demonstrate the completeness with respect to the class of SPO models using bulldozing.
This technique potentially allows a single cluster to generate an infinitely long path, so the finite model property is not guaranteed.
However, if we work on the hybrid logic with nominals and a satisfaction operator, the modal depth of any given formula
\varphi
is finite.
Then, there seems to be no problem in cutting off an infinitely extending path at an appropriate point (though the exact timing needs careful investigation).

The finite model properties for these logics for ordered models have already been proven by
[
3
]
using filtration.
However, if we can show the finite model property using the strategy indicated above, it would constitute a constructive alternative proof.

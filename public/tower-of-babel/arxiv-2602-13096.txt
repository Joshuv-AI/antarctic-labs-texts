Extensions of spacetime Bartnik data and estimates for the Bartnik mass outside of time-symmetry
Stephen McCormick, Markus Wolff

Abstract
. Bartnik’s quasi-local mass is a functional on Bartnik data ( 𝕊 2 , γ , H , P , ω ⟂ ) (\mathbb{S}^{2},\gamma,H,P,\omega^{\perp}) , consisting of a metric γ \gamma , scalar functions H H and P P , and a 1-form ω ⟂ \omega^{\perp} on the 2 2 -sphere 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} . We construct initial data ( M , g , K ) (M,g,K) for the Einstein equations with boundary Σ ≅ 𝕊 2 \Sigma\cong\mathbb{S}^{2} , and boundary conditions for g g and K K determined by Bartnik data with H , P H,P constant and ω ⟂ ≡ 0 \omega^{\perp}\equiv 0 . Furthermore this initial data agrees with spherically symmetric initial data for a Schwarzschild spacetime outside of a compact set with controlled mass. As an application, we obtain estimates for the Bartnik mass for such Bartnik data, outside of the time-symmetric setting. We also construct initial data on the cylinder 𝕊 2 × [ 0 , 1 ] \mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,1] connecting this same class of Bartnik data to time-symmetric data so that estimates for the Bartnik mass outside of time-symmetry can be obtained from prior estimates for time-symmetric data.

2.2.
The Bartnik mass

The Hawking mass defined above is one example of a

We define

(\Sigma,\gamma,H,P,\omega^{\perp})

(M,g,K)
where

\Sigma\subset M
,

and
\omega^{\perp}_{A}=K_{iA}\nu^{i}

]

\mathfrak{m}_{B}(\Sigma,\gamma,H,P,\omega^{\perp})=\inf\limits_{(M,g,K)\in\mathcal{A}(\Sigma,\gamma,H,P,\omega^{\perp})}\left\{\mathfrak{m}_{ADM}(M,g,K)\right\},

•

Although there is no known way to calculate this infimum in general, the construction of admissible extensions with known ADM mass immediately provides an upper bound for the Bartnik mass. Indeed many such extensions have been constructed in the time-symmetric setting in recent years (for example,
[
3
,
12

13
]

8
]

This is directly analogous to the work of Mantoulidis and Schoen
[

In this article we construct new admissible extensions outside of time-symmetry and estimate the Bartnik mass from above.

K_{1}\not=K_{2}r_{o}^{3}
,

(iv)

(K_{1}-2\mathfrak{m}_{o}^{3}K_{2})^{2}\leq 4\mathfrak{m}_{o}^{4}(1+9K_{2}^{2}\mathfrak{m}_{o}^{2})

admits a smooth non-time-symmetric extension
(M,g,K)
that satisfies the DEC, has constant mean curvature proportional to

, and contains no weakly trapped surfaces.

Remark 4.16
.

Note that for

we obtain a maximal asymptotically flat initial data set and the conditions (i) and (ii) of Corollary

should be understood in the limiting sense. For

Proof.

Consider

. Recall that this choice of

corresponds to an initial data set with constant mean curvature
3K_{2}
(see Remark

(i)), where

by assumption (iii). Observe that for this choice of

,

is non-decreasing for any choice of
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
and

is non-decreasing precisely when the polynomial

Taking

,

Hence, by applying Theorem

we can construct a smooth extension

with

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}+f(s)^{2}g(s),

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=Dx^{\prime}(f(s))\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}+Dx(f(s))f(s)g(s),

has constant mean curvature
\frac{3r_{o}^{3}\left|P_{o}\right|K_{2}}{2\left|K_{2}r_{o}^{3}-K_{1}\right|}

∎

Note that we have phrased the statement of Corollary

,

, with respect to given Bartnik data.

Corollary 4.17
.

Let

.

(i)

(ii)

Our choice of

,

in Remark

Proof.

Choose

constant,

,

and

5.
Estimating the Bartnik mass from extensions

For any choice of the function
x
in the construction of extensions in the preceding section, we find that the mass of the extension is equal to the Hawking mass at the end of the collar. In particular, we obtain different estimates for each choice of
x
. However, just as in the time-symmetric case, we do not expect that there is any optimal choice for this. For this reason, we elect to only present one example of this that we believe demonstrates nice qualitative properties. However, we emphasise that one can obtain many different estimates using this construction.

Taking the initial data of the form (
4.2
), we set
x(r)=\frac{B}{\sqrt{r}}
for some constant
B\neq 0
, as this satisfies
G_{x}\equiv 0
and therefore simplifies (
4.4
) considerably. In particular, the last term in (
4.4
) vanishes for any choice of parameter
\overline{m}
, so we can obtain a collar without fixing the value of
\overline{m}
as was done in the previous section. We simply require

2\beta-2k^{2}-\frac{u^{2}\alpha}{A^{2}}\geq 0,

(5.1)

and therefore the collar used for this estimate is slightly different (and simpler) than the general one constructed in the preceding section. Note that since we fix a choice of
x
, it is easier to absorb the constant
D
from our ansatz into the constant
B
. That is, take
D=1
and therefore
B=\tfrac{1}{2}P_{o}r_{o}^{3/2}
.

For this choice of
x
,
u
satisfies

u^{\prime}=\sqrt{1+\frac{B^{2}-2\overline{m}}{u}},

where
\overline{m}
remains a free parameter. A clean choice we therefore now make is to set
\overline{m}=\tfrac{1}{2}B^{2}
, so
u(Aks)=r_{o}+Aks
on
[0,1]
. In this case, to satisfy (
5.1
) we may simply ask for

\beta-k^{2}-\frac{r_{o}^{2}+A^{2}k^{2}}{A^{2}}\alpha>0.

(5.2)

This is easily achieved by choosing

\displaystyle\begin{split}A&=r_{o}\sqrt{\alpha}\left(\beta-(\alpha+1)k^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\\
&=r_{o}\sqrt{\alpha}\left(\beta-(\alpha+1)\left(\frac{H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}{4}\right)\right)^{-1/2}\\
&=r_{o}\,\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta-(\alpha+1)\frac{H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}{4}}}\end{split}

(5.3)

provided

\beta>(\alpha+1)\frac{H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}{4}.

(5.4)

A quick calculation shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{2}(s)

\displaystyle=H(s)^{2}-P(s)^{2}=\frac{H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{r_{o}^{3}}{u^{3}}P_{o}^{2}

\displaystyle>\frac{r_{o}^{2}}{u^{2}}\left(H_{o}^{2}-P_{o}^{2}\right)\geq 0,

for
s>0
, where we write
u=u(Aks)
above, which excludes MOTS in the collar by a standard argument as before.

We then find that the Hawking mass at the end of the collar is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{H}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{u(Ak)}{2}\left(1-\frac{u(Ak)^{2}}{4}\mathcal{H}(1)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{u(Ak)}{2}\left(1-\frac{u(Ak)^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}{u(Ak)^{2}}-\frac{r_{o}^{3}}{u(Ak)^{3}}P_{o}^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{u(Ak)}{2}\left(1-\left(\frac{H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}{4}-\frac{P_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}{4}\frac{r_{o}}{u(Ak)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{m}_{H}(0)+\frac{Ak}{2}\left(1-\frac{H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}{4}\right),

where
A
is given by (
5.3
). Note that as either
\alpha
or
H_{o}
tend to zero, so does
A
. Substituting the values for
A
and
k
gives

\mathfrak{m}_{H}(1)\leq\mathfrak{m}_{H}(0)+\frac{H_{o}r_{o}^{2}\sqrt{\alpha}(4-H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2})}{8\sqrt{4\beta-(\alpha+1)H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}}.

(5.5)

In order to obtain a Bartnik mass estimate from this simplified collar, we must also ensure that condition
(iv)
of Proposition
4.10
holds, which is equivalent to
\mathfrak{m}_{H}(1)>\tfrac{1}{2}B^{2}=\tfrac{P_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{3}}{8}
. One can quickly check from the above calculation for the Hawking mass that this is equivalent to asking

\frac{H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}{4}<1,

(5.6)

which follows directly from the assumption (
5.4
).

Remark 5.1
.

The choice
x(r)=\frac{B}{\sqrt{r}}
does not correspond to an asymptotically flat initial data set in the Schwarzschild spacetime, so after this gluing we obtain initial data that is not asymptotically flat. However, as mentioned in Remark
4.11
(ii), it is clear that in the exterior region we can interpolate between the constructed slice of Schwarzschild and any asymptotically flat slice in the same Schwarzschild spacetime.

We have now established the following estimate for the Bartnik mass.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(\Sigma,\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},0)
be Bartnik data with
\gamma
having strictly positive scalar curvature, where
H_{o}
,
P_{o}
are constant with
H_{o}\geq\left|P_{o}\right|>0
, satisfying

\frac{H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}{4}<\frac{\beta}{\alpha+1}.

Then the Bartnik mass of
(\Sigma,\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},0)
satisfies

\mathfrak{m}_{B}(\Sigma,\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},0)\leq\mathfrak{m}_{H}(0)+\frac{H_{o}r_{o}^{2}\sqrt{\alpha}(4-H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2})}{8\sqrt{4\beta-(\alpha+1)H_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}}.

Remark 5.3
.

Since the choice
x(r)=\frac{B}{\sqrt{r}}
gives
u^{\prime}(s)=\sqrt{1-\frac{2\overline{m}-B^{2}}{u(s)}}
, the construction above in this section is essentially identical to the time-symmetric case with mass parameter equal to
(\overline{m}-\tfrac{B^{2}}{2})
. It is therefore possible to use this choice to obtain a variety of very similar estimates following the different estimates in the time-symmetric case (for example,
[
3
,
12
,
13
]
).

6.
Reduction to time-symmetric estimates

In this section we give an alternate method for constructing estimates for the Bartnik mass outside of symmetry, by connecting non-time-symmetric initial data to time-symmetric initial data. Specifically, we prove Theorem
C
from the Introduction.

This section closely follows the work of Lin
[
8
]
in his PhD thesis. We begin with a general method for constructing initial data on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,1]
with prescribed Bartnik boundary data
(\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},\omega^{\perp}=0)
. We consider the ansatz

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}+e^{2\varepsilon f}\gamma,

(6.1)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}b\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}+ae^{2\varepsilon f}\gamma,

(6.2)

where
\varepsilon
is a small positive constant, and
f,a,b
are smooth functions on
[0,1]
.

Along the leaves
\Sigma_{s}
of constant
s
, we have
H=2f^{\prime}
and
P=\mathrm{tr}_{\Sigma}(K)=2a
. The Bartnik data then imposes

\displaystyle f(0)=0,\qquad f^{\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{2}H_{o},\qquad a(0)=\frac{1}{2}P_{o}.

Note that
b
remains freely specifiable.
We next record some quantities that will be useful.

\displaystyle A_{AB}=f^{\prime}e^{2\varepsilon f}g_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}He^{2\varepsilon f}g_{AB},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{g}(K)=2a+b,\qquad\qquad|K|_{g}^{2}=2a^{2}+b^{2},

where
A
is the second fundamental form of
\Sigma_{s}
. Using the above and the evolution formula for
H
,

H^{\prime}=-\varepsilon(|A|_{\Sigma}^{2}+\mathrm{Ric}_{\nu\nu}),

the scalar curvature of
g
can be computed as

R_{g}=e^{-2\varepsilon f}R_{\gamma}-\frac{3}{2}H^{2}-2\varepsilon^{-1}H^{\prime}.

(6.3)

Following
[
8
]
, the energy and momentum densities are calculated directly as

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{-2\varepsilon f}R_{\gamma}-\frac{3}{4}H^{2}-\varepsilon^{-1}H^{\prime}+a^{2}+2ab,

\displaystyle J_{\nu}

\displaystyle=2f^{\prime}(b-a)-2\varepsilon^{-1}a^{\prime},

\displaystyle J_{A}

\displaystyle=0.

In particular, we can force
J_{\nu}
to vanish by choosing

b=a+\frac{a^{\prime}}{\varepsilon f^{\prime}}.

(6.4)

The DEC now reduces to the non-negativity of
\mu
, which is now given as

\mu=\frac{1}{2}e^{-2\varepsilon f}R_{\gamma}-\frac{3}{4}H^{2}-\varepsilon^{-1}H^{\prime}+3a^{2}+\frac{2aa^{\prime}}{\varepsilon f^{\prime}}.

(6.5)

Although we could write
H
and
P
in terms of
f
and
a
, it is convenient to keep
H
and
P
terms. We then have

\mu=\frac{1}{2}e^{-2\varepsilon f}R_{\gamma}-\frac{3}{4}(H^{2}-P^{2})+\frac{2aa^{\prime}-H^{\prime}f^{\prime}}{\varepsilon f^{\prime}}.

(6.6)

Note that the Hawking mass (
2.4
) along this collar will remain close to constant provided
\mathcal{H}^{2}=H^{2}-P^{2}
remains constant, since
\varepsilon
controls the area growth and this will be chosen small. To this end, we impose
\mathcal{H}^{2}=H^{2}-P^{2}=C^{2}
for some constant
C
, and differentiate this to obtain a relation between
a
and
f
,

2HH^{\prime}=2PP^{\prime}=8aa^{\prime}=4f^{\prime}H^{\prime}=8f^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}.

(6.7)

If
a
and
f
can be chosen to satisfy
aa^{\prime}=f^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}
then (
6.6
) reduces to

\mu=\frac{1}{2}e^{-2\varepsilon f}R_{\gamma}-\frac{3}{4}C^{2}.

(6.8)

This is simply achieved by first fixing
a(t)
then defining

f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{a(\tau)^{2}+\frac{C^{2}}{4}}\,d\tau.

In particular, we can choose
a
(and therefore also
f^{\prime\prime}
) to be identically zero on the interval
[\tfrac{1}{2},1]
so that
P(s)
vanishes (and
H(s)
is constant) on
[\tfrac{1}{2},1]
.

Then we conclude that for
\varepsilon
taken sufficiently small we need only ask that the Bartnik data satisfies

R_{\gamma}>\frac{3}{2}C^{2}=\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}

to construct a metric of the form (
6.1
) on
[0,1]\times S^{2}
such that the Bartnik data for
\Sigma_{0}
is the given data, while the Bartnik data for
\Sigma_{1}
is the Bartnik data
(\Sigma,(1+\delta)g,\mathcal{H}_{o},0,0)
for any given
\delta>0
. That is, we obtain time-symmetric Bartnik data with mean curvature equal to
\mathcal{H}_{o}
and Hawking mass arbitrarily close to the Hawking mass of the initial Bartnik data. Furthermore, since
\mathcal{H}_{o}
is constant along the leaves, trapped surfaces are excluded in the collar as before by the maximum principle.

More concretely, we have established the following.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(\Sigma,\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},0)
be Bartnik data with
\gamma
having strictly positive scalar curvature, where
H_{o}
,
P_{o}
are constant with
H_{o}\geq\left|P_{o}\right|>0
, satisfying

R_{\gamma}>\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}_{o}^{2}.

(6.9)

Then there exists initial data
(\gamma,K)
on
\Sigma\times[0,1]
satisfying the DEC and containing no MOTS, such that
(\Sigma,\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},0)
is the induced Bartnik data at
t=0
and
(\Sigma,(1+\epsilon)^{2}\gamma,\mathcal{H}_{o},0,0)
is the induced Bartnik data at
t=1
, for any
\epsilon>0
.

One may expect that then any admissible extension of the time-symmetric data
(\Sigma,(1+\epsilon)^{2}\gamma,\sqrt{H_{o}^{2}-P_{o}^{2}},0,0)
yields an admissible extension to the Bartnik data
(\Sigma,\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},0)
, provided (
6.9
) holds. In which case, one should then be able to conclude

\mathfrak{m}_{B}(\Sigma,\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},0)\leq\mathfrak{m}_{B}(\Sigma,\gamma,\mathcal{H}_{o},0,0).

However, there is a subtlety here that prevents us from drawing that conclusion. Namely that the standard corner-smoothing constructions affect the entire manifold in such a way that cannot ensure that MOTS are not created in the process, which illustrates also the need for a smooth gluing lemma such as Lemma
A.1
. A detailed discussion of this subtlety is given by Jauregui
[
6
]
for example. One could of course mildly alter the definition of Bartnik mass to use a stronger non-degeneracy condition (as in
[
6
]
) or to allow extensions of low regularity rather than smooth ones, and then draw this conclusion still. Alternatively, we could use known extensions to time-symmetric initial data that allow for smooth gluing while preserving the MOTS condition. For example, taking
\alpha
and
\beta
as defined above, we have the following Corollary.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
(\Sigma,\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},0)
be Bartnik data with
\gamma
having strictly positive scalar curvature, where
H_{o}
,
P_{o}
are constant with
H_{o}\geq\left|P_{o}\right|>0
, satisfying

R_{\gamma}>\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}_{o}^{2}.

(6.10)

Then if

\frac{\mathcal{H}_{o}^{2}r_{o}^{2}}{4}<\frac{\beta}{1+\alpha},

then

\mathfrak{m}_{B}(\Sigma,\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},0)\leq\left(1+\left(\frac{\alpha r_{o}^{2}\mathcal{H}_{o}^{2}}{4\beta-(1+\alpha)r_{o}^{2}\mathcal{H}_{o}^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\right)\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\Sigma,\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},0).

Proof.

Let

m_{*}=\left(1+\left(\frac{\alpha r_{o}^{2}\mathcal{H}_{o}^{2}}{4\beta-(1+\alpha)r_{o}^{2}\mathcal{H}_{o}^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\right)\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\Sigma,\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},0),

which is the upper bound for the Bartnik mass of the time-symmetric Bartnik data
(\Sigma,\gamma,\mathcal{H}_{o},0,0)
given by Theorem 1.1 of
[
3
]
. This upper bound is obtained by constructing admissible extensions with mass arbitrarily close to
\mathfrak{m}_{*}
, so we let
(M,g_{\epsilon})
be such an extension of the Bartnik data
(\Sigma,\gamma,\mathcal{H}_{o},0,0)
constructed in the proof, with ADM mass
\mathfrak{m}=(1+\epsilon)m_{*}
. Note that the rescaled metric
(1+\epsilon)^{2}g_{\epsilon}
has ADM mass
(1+\epsilon)^{2}m_{*}
and boundary Bartnik data
(\Sigma,(1+\epsilon)^{2}\gamma,(1+\epsilon)^{-1}\mathcal{H}_{o},0,0)
. We may assume without loss of generality that the path of metrics
\gamma(s)
defining
\alpha
and
\beta
is constant on some interval containing
s=0
since any path that does not satisfy this may be approximated by a path that does with the corresponding
\alpha
and
\beta
arbitrarily close to the original path. This is then precisely the situation for the smooth gluing to apply (Lemma
4.6
with
x\equiv 0
or Lemma 2.1 of
[
3
]
, for example). That is we can smoothly glue the collar constructed in Theorem
6.1
to
(M,g_{\epsilon})
while preserving the DEC and without introducing any MOTS, providing an admissible extension of
(\Sigma,\gamma,H_{o},P_{o},0)
with ADM mass
\mathfrak{m}=(1+\epsilon)^{2}m_{*}
. Taking
\epsilon\to 0
completes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

Corollary
6.2
is obtained using the time-symmetric estimate from
[
3
]
but any time-symmetric estimate using a similar collar construction could be used instead. The only requirement is that the extensions can be constructed in such a way that in a neighbourhood of the boundary the metric can be expressed as

ds^{2}+f(s)^{2}\gamma_{o}

for a fixed metric
\gamma_{o}
on
\Sigma
with positive Gauss curvature.

Remark 6.4
.

Throughout this work we restricted our attention to the case
\omega^{\perp}\equiv 0
, so we conclude by briefly remarking on the case of non-zero
\omega^{\perp}
. One can easily alter the ansatz for
K
to include a
K_{sJ}dsdx^{J}
cross term that prescribes
\omega^{\perp}
, and indeed carry through similar computations to those presented here. However, the expressions obtained become cumbersome quickly if one attempts to maintain the same generality of the kinds of extensions constructed. For this reason, we reserve estimates of the Bartnik mass with non-zero
\omega^{\perp}
for future work.

Appendix A
Gluing and bending lemma

Lemma A.1

(Gluing lemma)
.

Let
f_{i}:[a_{i},b_{i}]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, where
i=1,2
, be smooth positive functions and let
\gamma_{*}
be the standard round metric on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Consider metrics
g_{i}
and symmetric
2
-tensors
K_{i}
defined on
[a_{i},b_{i}]\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
by

\displaystyle g_{i}

\displaystyle=ds^{2}+f_{i}(s)^{2}\gamma_{*}

\displaystyle K_{i}

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}(f_{i}(s))ds^{2}+x(f_{i}(s))f_{i}(s)\gamma_{*},

for some
C^{1}
function
x
. Assume that

i.

\mu_{i}>0
on
[a_{i},b_{i}]
,

ii.

f_{1}(b_{1})<f_{2}(a_{2})
,

iii.

0<f_{1}^{\prime}(b_{1})<\sqrt{1+x(f_{1}(b_{1}))^{2}+2x(f_{1}(b_{1}))x^{\prime}(f_{1}(b_{1}))f_{1}(b_{1})}
,

iv.

f_{2}^{\prime}(a_{2})\leq f_{1}^{\prime}(b_{1})
,

v.

G_{x}(r)
is monotone non-decreasing on
[f_{1}(b_{1}),f_{2}(a_{2})]
.

Then, after an appropriate translation of the intervals
[a_{i},b_{i}]
, there exists a smooth positive function
f:[a_{1},b_{2}]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

I.

f(s)=f_{1}(s)
for
a_{1}\leq s\leq\frac{a_{1}+b_{1}}{2}
,

II.

f(s)=f_{2}(s)
for
\frac{a_{2}+b_{2}}{2}\leq s\leq b_{2}
,

III.

\mu_{f}>0
on
[a_{1},b_{2}]
,

where
\mu_{f}
denotes the energy density of the initial data set
(M,g,K)
, where
M=[a_{1},b_{2}]\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}g&=ds^{2}+f(s)^{2}\gamma_{*}\\
K&=x^{\prime}(f(s))ds^{2}+x(f(s))f(s)\gamma_{*}.\end{split}

(A.1)

Proof.

This proof follows the same ideas as the gluing lemmas in
[
9
,
3
,
2
]
.

First note that the assumptions
(ii)
and
(iii)
ensure that the intervals can always be translated so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}(a_{2}-b_{1})f_{1}^{\prime}(b_{1})=f_{2}(a_{2})-f_{1}(b_{1}),&\qquad\text{ if }f_{1}^{\prime}(b_{1})=f_{2}^{\prime}(a_{2}),\\
(a_{2}-b_{1})f_{1}^{\prime}(b_{1})>f_{2}(a_{2})-f_{1}(b_{1})>(a_{2}-b_{1})f_{2}^{\prime}(b_{2}),&\qquad\text{ if }f_{1}^{\prime}(b_{1})>f_{2}^{\prime}(a_{2})\end{split}.

(A.2)

This ensures that we can interpolate between
f_{1}^{\prime}(b_{1})
and
f_{2}^{\prime}(a_{2})
with a smooth function
\zeta
on
[b_{1},a_{2}]
satisfying
\zeta(b_{1})=f_{1}^{\prime}(b_{1})
,
\zeta(a_{2})=f^{\prime}_{2}(a_{2})
,
\zeta^{\prime}\leq 0
and
\int_{b_{1}}^{a_{2}}\zeta(s)\,ds=f_{2}(a_{2})-f_{1}(b_{1})
. We then define
\widehat{f}
on
[a_{1},b_{2}]
by

\widehat{f}(s)=\begin{cases}f_{1}(s)&\text{ if }s\in[a_{1},b_{1}],\\
f_{1}(b_{1})+\int_{b_{1}}^{s}\zeta(t)\,dt.&\text{ if }s\in[b_{1},a_{2}],\\
f_{2}(s)&\text{ if }s\in[a_{2},b_{2}].\end{cases}

By construction
\widehat{f}
is
C^{1,1}
on
[a_{1},b_{2}]
and
C^{2}
everywhere except at
b_{1}
and
a_{2}
. Note that a metric
\gamma
and tensor
K
of the form (
4.8
) satisfy
\mu_{f}>0
if and only if

f^{\prime\prime}(s)<\frac{1}{2f(s)}\left(1-f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+x(f(s))^{2}+2x(f(s))x^{\prime}(f(s))f(s)\right).

(A.3)

We define
\Omega[f]
from the right-hand side of (
A.3
) as

\Omega[f](s)=\frac{1}{2f(s)}\left(1-f^{\prime}(s)^{2}+x(f(s))^{2}+2x(f(s))x^{\prime}(f(s))f(s)\right).

(A.4)

By assumption
\Omega[f_{i}]-f^{\prime\prime}_{i}>0
on each
[a_{i},b_{i}]
. We now show that
\Omega[\widehat{f}](s)-\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}(s)>0
on
(b_{1},a_{2})
.

Note that
\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}\leq 0
on
(b_{1},a_{2})
, and

\displaystyle\widehat{f}^{\prime}(s)\leq f^{\prime}(b_{1})

\displaystyle<\sqrt{1+x(f(b_{1}))^{2}+2x(f(b_{1}))x^{\prime}(f(b_{1}))f(b_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{1+x(f(s))^{2}+2x(f(s))x^{\prime}(f(s))f(s)},

which follows from the fact that
G
is non-decreasing. That is,
\Omega[\widehat{f}]
is positive on
[a_{1},b_{2}]
while
\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}\leq 0
on
[a_{1},b_{2}]\setminus\{b_{1},a_{2}\}
.

We now smooth out
\widehat{f}
using a standard argument, exactly as in
[
3
,
2
]
, repeating the argument verbatim only for the sake of exposition.

Let
\delta>0
satisfy

\displaystyle\delta<b_{1}-\frac{a_{1}+b_{1}}{2}\,\text{and}\,\delta<\frac{a_{2}+b_{2}}{2}-a_{2}

and let
\eta_{\delta}
be a smooth cut-off function equal to
1
on
[b_{1}-\delta,a_{2}+\delta]
, vanishing on the set
\left[a_{1},\frac{a_{1}+b_{1}}{2}\right]\cup\left[\frac{a_{2}+b_{2}}{2},b_{2}\right]
, and satisfying
0<\eta_{\delta}<1
elsewhere. Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be a standard smooth mollifier with compact support in
[-1,1]
and
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(t)\,dt=1
.

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{\delta}{4})
, define
f_{\varepsilon}
by

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(s-\varepsilon\eta_{\delta}(s)t)\phi(t)\,dt\quad\text{ for }\quad s\in[a_{1},b_{2}].

(A.5)

Note that
f_{\varepsilon}
is smooth on
[a_{1},b_{2}]
,
f_{\varepsilon}\equiv\widehat{f}
on
\left[a_{1},\frac{a_{1}+b_{1}}{2}\right]\cup\left[\frac{a_{2}+b_{2}}{2},b_{2}\right]
and

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}^{\prime}(s-\varepsilon\eta_{\delta}(s)t)(1-\varepsilon\eta_{\delta}^{\prime}(s)t)\phi(t)\,dt\quad\forall\,s\in[a_{1},b_{2}].

(A.6)

Since
\widehat{f}^{\prime}
is
C^{0}
everywhere and
C^{1}
on
[a_{1},b_{2}]\setminus\{b_{1},a_{2}\}
, standard mollification arguments give for
s\in(b_{1}-\delta,a_{2}+\delta)

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(s)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{d}{ds}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}^{\prime}(s-\varepsilon t)\phi(t)\,dt\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}(t)\phi_{\varepsilon}(s-t)\,dt,

(A.7)

using
\eta_{\delta}(s)=1
, where
\phi_{\varepsilon}(s)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\phi(\frac{s}{\varepsilon})
. Moreover, for
s\in[a_{1},b_{2}]\setminus[b_{1}-\frac{1}{4}{\delta},a_{2}+\frac{1}{4}\delta]
where
\widehat{f}
is smooth, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}f^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(s)=&\ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}(s-\varepsilon\eta_{\delta}(s)t)\,(1-\varepsilon\eta_{\delta}^{\prime}(s)t)^{2}\phi(t)\,dt\\
&\ -\varepsilon\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}^{\prime}(s-\varepsilon\eta_{\delta}(s)t)\,\eta_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(s)t\phi(t)\,dt.\end{split}

(A.8)

We define
d>0
by

3d=\inf_{s\in[a_{1},b_{2}]\setminus\{b_{1},a_{2}\}}\left(\Omega[\widehat{f}](s)-\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}(s)\right).

Since

\widehat{f}^{\prime\prime}(s)\leq\Omega[\widehat{f}](s)-3d

on that same set (everywhere

is defined).

Now for sufficiently small
\varepsilon

\sup_{s\in[a_{1},b_{2}]}|\Omega[\widehat{f}](s)-\Omega[f_{\varepsilon}](s)|<d,

(A.9)

since

in

(A.10)

Combining these, we arrive at

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}<\Omega[\widehat{f}](s)-d<\Omega[f_{\varepsilon}](s).

That is,

defines

and

satisfying the DEC.

Lemma A.2

(Bending lemma)
.

Let
(M,g,K)
be an initial data set of the form
M=[a,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
a\geq 0
,

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}+f(s)^{2}\gamma_{*},

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}(f(s))\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}+x(f(s))f(s)\gamma_{*},

such that
\mu\geq\tau
for some
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, where
f
is a smooth, positive function,
x
is
C^{1}
, and
\gamma_{*}
denotes the round metric on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

For any
s_{o}>a
such that
f^{\prime}(s_{o})>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that the following holds: There exists a smooth positive function
\widetilde{f}
on
[s_{o}-\delta,\infty)
such that
\widetilde{f}(s)=f(s)
for all
s\in[s_{o},\infty)
and the initial data set
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g},\widetilde{K})
, with
\widetilde{M}=[s_{o}-\delta,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}

\displaystyle=\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}+\widetilde{f}^{2}(s)\gamma_{*},

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}(\widetilde{f}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}+x(\widetilde{f}(s))\widetilde{f}(s)\gamma_{*},

has
\widetilde{\mu}\geq\tau
such that
\widetilde{\mu}>\tau
on
[s_{o}-\delta,s_{o})
.

In addition, if
f(s_{o})>c>0
and
f^{\prime\prime}(s_{o})>0
, then
\delta
can be chosen such that
\widetilde{f}(s_{o}-\delta)>c
,
\widetilde{f}^{\prime}(s_{o}-\delta)<f^{\prime}(s_{o})
.

Proof.

Consider
\widetilde{f}(s)=f(\sigma(s))
for some smooth reparametrisation
\sigma(s)
to be determined. Then, by Lemma
3.3
, we find that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}(s)=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{f^{2}(\sigma(s))}-\frac{1}{f^{2}(\sigma(s))}\left((f^{\prime}(\sigma(s))^{2}\dot{\sigma}(s)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{f(\sigma(s))}\left(f^{\prime\prime}(\sigma(s))\,\dot{\sigma}(s)^{2}+f^{\prime}(\sigma(s))\overset{\cdot\cdot}{\sigma}(s)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{f^{2}(\sigma(s))}\left(x^{2}(f(\sigma(s)))+2x(f(\sigma(s)))\,x^{\prime}(f(\sigma(s)))\,f(\sigma(s))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\mu(\sigma(s))+\frac{1}{f(\sigma(s))^{2}}\left((1-\dot{\sigma}(s)^{2})\left(f^{\prime}(\sigma(s))^{2}+2f(\sigma(s))\,f^{\prime\prime}(\sigma(s))\right)\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{2f^{\prime}(\sigma(s))\overset{\cdot\cdot}{\sigma}(s)}{f(\sigma(s))}

As
\mu\geq\tau
by assumption, the claim thus follows if we can choose a constant
\delta>0
and a smooth function
\sigma\colon[s_{o}-\delta,\infty)\to[a,\infty)
such that

(i)

\sigma(s)=s
on
[s_{o},\infty)
,

(ii)

2f(\sigma(s))\,f^{\prime}(\sigma(s))\,\overset{\cdot\cdot}{\sigma}(s)<(1-\dot{\sigma}(s)^{2})\left(f^{\prime}(\sigma(s))+2f(\sigma(s))\,f^{\prime\prime}(\sigma(s))\right)

on
[s_{o}-\delta,s_{o})
.

Consider the smooth function

\sigma(s)=s-\int\limits_{s}^{s_{o}}\theta(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\text{ with }\theta(s)=\begin{cases}0&s\geq s_{o},\\
\exp\left(-(s-s_{o})^{-2}\right)&s_{o}-\delta\leq s<s_{o},\end{cases}

which satisfies (i) by definition. We now aim to show (ii):

As
1\leq\dot{\sigma}=1+\theta(s)\leq 2
, we have
\sigma([s_{o}-\delta,s_{o}])\subseteq[s_{o}-2\delta,s_{o}]
. Hence, by continuity, there exists
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}\left|s_{o}-a\right|
such that

\displaystyle 2f(\sigma(s))\,f^{\prime}(\sigma(s))\geq f(s_{o})\,f^{\prime}(s_{o})=c_{1}>0,

\displaystyle\max\left(0,f^{\prime}(\sigma(s))^{2}+2f(\sigma(s))\,f^{\prime\prime}(\sigma(s))\right)\leq c_{3},

for all
s\in[s_{o}-\delta,s_{o})
for some constant
c_{3}\geq 0
, and where
c_{1}>0
by assumption. As
\dot{\sigma}(s)>1
on
[s_{o}-\delta,s_{o})
, it thus suffices to show that

\frac{c_{3}}{c_{1}}<\frac{\overset{\cdot\cdot}{\sigma}(s)}{1-\dot{\sigma}(s)^{2}}

on
[s_{o}-\delta,s_{o})
. Direct computation yields ,

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}(s)^{2}

\displaystyle=1+2\theta(s)+\theta^{2}(s),

\displaystyle\overset{\cdot\cdot}{\sigma}(s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\left|s-s_{o}\right|^{3}}\theta(s).

In particular,

\frac{\overset{\cdot\cdot}{\sigma}(s)}{1-\dot{\sigma}(s)^{2}}=\frac{2}{(2+\theta(s))\left|s-s_{o}\right|^{3}}\to\infty

as
s\to s_{o}
. This implies (ii) for
\delta
sufficiently small.

Finally, assume that
f(s_{o})>c>0
,
f^{\prime\prime}(s_{o})>0
. By continuity, we can choose
\delta
sufficiently small such that
\widetilde{f}(s_{o}-\delta)>c>0
. Additionally,

\widetilde{f}^{\prime\prime}(s)=f^{\prime\prime}(\sigma(s))(1+2\theta(s)+\theta^{2}(s))-2f^{\prime}(\sigma(s))\frac{\theta(s)}{\left|s-s_{o}\right|^{3}},

and as
\theta(s)\left|s_{o}-s\right|^{3}\to 0
as
s\to s_{o}
we find that
\widetilde{f}^{\prime\prime}>0
on
[s_{o}-\delta,\infty)
if
f^{\prime\prime}(s_{o})>0
and
\delta
is sufficiently small. Hence,
\widetilde{f}^{\prime}
is strictly increasing, which directly implies that

\widetilde{f}^{\prime}(s_{o}-\delta)<\widetilde{f}^{\prime}(s_{o})=f^{\prime}(s_{o}).

This finishes the proof.
∎

Stochastic halfspace approximation method for convex optimization with nonsmooth functional constraints
Nitesh Kumar Singh, Ion Necoara

Abstract
In this work, we consider convex optimization problems with smooth objective function and nonsmooth functional constraints. We propose a new stochastic gradient algorithm, called Stochastic Halfspace Approximation Method (SHAM), to solve this problem, where at each iteration we first take a gradient step for the objective function and then we perform a projection step onto one halfspace approximation of a randomly chosen constraint. We propose various strategies to create this stochastic halfspace approximation and we provide a unified convergence analysis that yields new convergence rates for SHAM algorithm in both optimality and feasibility criteria evaluated at some average point. In particular, we derive convergence rates of order 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k ) \mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{k}) , when the objective function is only convex, and 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k ) \mathcal{O}(1/k) when the objective function is strongly convex. The efficiency of SHAM is illustrated through detailed numerical simulations.

Among these challenges, optimization problems with nonsmooth functional constraints stands out as a critical frontier, where traditional approaches face hard limitations when handling a large number of constraints and/or when projections are computationally demanding. In this context, we address the following convex problem with smooth objective and nonsmooth functional constraints:

\begin{array}[]{rl}f^{*}=&\min\limits_{x{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\in\mathcal{Y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;f(x)\\
&\text{subject to }\;h_{j}(x)\leq 0\;\;\forall j\in[m],\end{array}

(1)

where
[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}

and

is a non-empty, closed, convex and simple set (by simple we mean that it admits easy projection)
. Solving problem (
1

are difficult to compute. These issues can be highly computationally demanding, if not impossible. In response to these intricate challenges, the field has witnessed a prevalent adoption of stochastic first order (sub)gradient methods. Notably, the stochastic gradient descent
[
11
,
6
]
and the stochastic proximal/projection
[
13
,
11
,
23
]

1
) has also seen the introduction of primal-dual stochastic gradient methods, see e.g.,
[
25
]
.
These stochastic optimization algorithms try to strike a balance between computational efficiency, solution quality, and handling many functional constraints.
Thus the central theme of our scientific inquiry also revolves around harnessing the power of randomness and incorporating stochastic (sub)gradient techniques to tackle problem (

Contributions.
In this paper,
we extend and synthesize insights from prior research to offer the following contributions:

(i)
We introduce a novel Stochastic Halfspace Approximation Method (SHAM) for solving convex optimization problems with smooth objective and nonsmooth functional constraints, which consists of a gradient step on the objective followed by a projection onto a linear halfspace approximation of a randomly selected constraint. Our analysis offers various (in fact infinite) examples for creating the stochastic halfspace approximation, accompanied by a unified convergence analysis.

(ii)
Using new convex tools, we derive sublinear convergence rates of order

(iii)
For

]

[

Notations and key assumptions

Throughout this paper, we use the following notations. We consider the individual sets

\mathcal{X}_{j}\!=\!\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h_{j}(x)\leq 0\}\;\;\forall j\in[m],\;\;\mathcal{X}\!=\!{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{Y}\cap(\cap_{j\in[m]}\mathcal{X}_{j})}.

Further,

Moreover, for any

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(h_{j};x;\Delta_{j}^{x})\!=\!\!\begin{cases}\!\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\!:l_{h_{j}}(y;x)\leq 0\},\text{if }\Delta_{j}^{x}\!\neq\!{0},\\
\mathbb{R}^{n},\hskip 87.63454pt\text{if }\Delta_{j}^{x}\!=\!{0}.\end{cases}

(2)

Note that, in the case of

{\rm dist}(x,\mathcal{X})=\|x-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(x)\|
.
Throughout the paper, we assume that the set
\mathcal{X}
is non-empty,
and the optimization problem (
1

Furthermore, for problem (
1

Assumption II.1

(i)
The function

is

\displaystyle f(y)\!\geq\!f(x)\!+\!\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}\;\forall x,y\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{Y}}.

(3)

(ii)
The function

has

-Lipschitz continuous gradient, i.e., there exists

such that for any

\displaystyle f(y)\leq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{L_{f}}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}.

(4)

Note that Assumption
II.1
(i)
implies that the function

is convex when
\mu=0

is a general property of a smooth function (see Lemma

[

Next, we consider the assumptions for the functional constraints

Assumption II.2

The convex functional constraints

Finally, our next assumption is a linear regularity type condition for the functional constraints.

{\rm dist}(x,\mathcal{X})\leq c\max_{j\in[m]}\left[(h_{j}(x))_{+}\right]\;\;\forall x\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{Y}}.

(6)

Note that Assumptions
II.2
and

have been frequently used in the context of stochastic optimization problems, see e.g.,
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
. Particularly, Assumption

holds e.g., when the feasible set

has nonempty interior or is polyhedral (see
[

III
Algorithm

In this section, we introduce a novel Stochastic Halfspace Approximation Method (SHAM), in order to solve problem (
1
). For any given iteration
k
, we choose a random index
j_{k}\in[m]
with given probability
\mathbb{P}
, and update as:

Algorithm 1

(SHAM)

\text{Choose}\;x_{0}\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{Y}}\;\text{stepsize sequence}\;\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
and
\beta>0
.

For
k\geq 0
update:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}u_{k}=}x_{k}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})

(8)

\displaystyle v_{k}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Pi_{\mathcal{Y}}(u_{k})}

(9)

\displaystyle\text{Sample}\;j_{k}\sim\mathbb{P},\text{choose }\tilde{x}_{k},\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\in\partial h_{j_{k}}(\tilde{x}_{k})\;\text{and update:}\vskip-8.5359pt

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}z_{k}=(1-\beta)v_{k}+\beta\Pi_{\mathcal{L}_{j_{k}}}(v_{k})}\vskip-8.5359pt

(10)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}x_{k+1}=\Pi_{\mathcal{Y}}(z_{k}).}

(11)

Here
\mathcal{L}_{j_{k}}=\mathcal{L}(h_{j_{k}};\tilde{x}_{k};\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}})
(see (
2
)), and the choice of
\tilde{x}_{k}\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{Y}}
is user dependent.
Note that the algorithms in
[
10
,
12
]
consider only the case
\tilde{x}_{k}=v_{k}
, whereas SHAM introduces a novel scheme in comparison to these existing works, allowing us the flexibility to create linear halfspace approximations at any point
\tilde{x}_{k}
selected on the line
segment
joining the two points
x_{k}
and
v_{k}
. More precisely, we conduct below the convergence analysis for a general choice of
\tilde{x}_{k}=\gamma v_{k}+(1-\gamma)x_{k}
, for
\gamma\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}[0,\;1]}
, that particularly takes the form
\tilde{x}_{k}=x_{k}
when
\gamma=0
or
\tilde{x}_{k}=v_{k}
when
\gamma=1
, for generating the linear halfspace approximations in step (
10
).
Later in Section V, one can also notice the benefits of these choices in practice.
Consequently, SHAM comprises two key steps: firstly, step (
8
) is the gradient step on the objective function seeking to reduce
f

that together with the projection step (
9
) gives us an intermittent point
v_{k}
.
Next, in step
(
10
)
we have a (sub)gradient projection step striving to minimize the feasibility violation of the chosen random constraint
j_{k}\in[m]
. In this step, if
\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\neq 0
, we project
v_{k}
onto the linear halfspace
l_{h_{j_{k}}}(y;\tilde{x}_{k})\leq 0
. Thus, (
10
) has the following expression:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}z_{k}}=v_{k}-\beta\frac{(l_{h_{j_{k}}}(v_{k};\tilde{x}_{k}))_{+}}{\|\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\|^{2}}\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}},

(12)

where
\beta>0
. While if
\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}=0
, (
10
) yields:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}z_{k}}=v_{k}.

(13)

Note that in both expressions (
12
) and (
13
) we use the formula of projection onto a halfspace. Moreover, when
\tilde{x}_{k}=v_{k}
the step (
12
) uses a Polyak type stepsize of the form
\beta(h_{j_{k}}(v_{k}))_{+}/\|\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\|^{2}
with a parameter
\beta>0
, see also
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
.
In our analysis, we also need an assumption concerning the distribution of constraint index
j_{k}
.

Assumption III.1

There exists a constant
\rho\in(0,1]
such that

\inf_{k\geq 0}\mathbb{P}(j_{k}=j|\mathcal{F}_{[k]})\geq\frac{\rho}{m}\quad\text{a.s.}\quad\forall j\in[m],

where
\mathcal{F}_{[k]}=\{x_{0},j_{0},...,j_{k-1}\}
for
k\geq 1
and
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\{x_{0}\}
.

This assumption is also standard in the literature, see
[
23
]
,
[
26
]
.
In our analysis below, we consider boundedness of the norm of the gradients of
f
along the iterates of SHAM, i.e.:

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|\leq B_{f}\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(14)

Note that since the gradient
\nabla f
is continuous and the update step (
11
) ensures
x_{k}\in\mathcal{Y}
, condition (
14
) always holds provided that the set
\mathcal{Y}
is bounded, (see also
[
5
]
). However, boundedness of
\mathcal{Y}
is a sufficient condition for (
14
) (there may be problems having unbounded
\mathcal{Y}
for which (
14
) still holds).

IV-A

Convergence rates for convex objective function

In this section, we consider the scenario where the objective function
f
in problem (
1
) is only smooth and convex. Under the other assumptions defined in previous sections (except Assumption
II.1
(i)
, where
\mu=0
), we provide the convergence rates of Algorithm
1
. Before presenting the main results, let us define the following average sequences:

\hat{x}_{k}=\frac{1}{S_{k}}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{t}x_{t+1},\text{ and }\hat{x}^{*}_{k}=\frac{1}{S_{k}}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{t}\bar{x}_{t},

where
S_{k}=\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{t}
.
The following theorem outlines the convergence rates of Algorithm
1
.

Theorem IV.6

Under the conditions of Theorem
IV.5
with convex
f
, i.e.,
\mu=0
,
we have the following bounds for the average sequence
\hat{x}_{k}
in terms of optimality and feasibility violation for problem (
1
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(f(\hat{x}_{k})-f(\hat{x}^{*}_{k}))]\leq\frac{\|x_{0}-\bar{x}_{0}\|^{2}}{2S_{k}}+B^{2}\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\alpha_{t}^{2}}{2S_{k}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\rm dist}^{2}(\hat{x}_{k},\mathcal{X})]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}4}\alpha_{0}mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}{\rho\beta(1-\beta)}\left(\frac{\|x_{0}-\bar{x}_{0}\|^{2}}{S_{k}}+B^{2}\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\alpha_{t}^{2}}{S_{k}}\right).

Proof:

For convex case, the inequality (
19
) holds with
\mu=0
, thus we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k+1}-\bar{x}_{k+1}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\|^{2}]-2\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}[(f(x_{k+1})-f(\bar{x}_{k}))]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\rho\beta(1-\beta)}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}4}mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}\mathbb{E}[{\rm dist}^{2}(x_{k+1},\mathcal{X})]+\alpha_{k}^{2}B^{2}.

Summing this from
0
to
k-1
, and noticing that since
\alpha_{k}
is a nonincreasing sequence, thus using
\alpha_{k}/\alpha_{0}\leq 1
, we get:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{0}-\bar{x}_{0}\|^{2}-2\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{t}\mathbb{E}[(f(x_{t+1})-f(\bar{x}_{t}))]

\displaystyle\quad\!-\!\frac{\rho\beta(1-\beta)}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}4}\alpha_{0}mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{t}\mathbb{E}[{\rm dist}^{2}(x_{t+1},\mathcal{X})]\!+\!B^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{t}^{2}.

Now, divide the whole inequality by
S_{k}
and from the definition of average sequences, linearity of the expectation operator and using convexity of the norm, and of the function
f
, we get:

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}[(f(\hat{x}_{k})-f(\hat{x}^{*}_{k}))]+\frac{\rho\beta(1-\beta)}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}4}\alpha_{0}mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}\mathbb{E}[{\rm dist}^{2}(\hat{x}_{k},\mathcal{X})]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|x_{0}-\bar{x}_{0}\|^{2}}{S_{k}}+B^{2}\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\alpha_{t}^{2}}{S_{k}}.

Thus, we have the following rates in expectation for the average sequence in terms of optimality:

\mathbb{E}[(f(\hat{x}_{k})-f(\hat{x}^{*}_{k}))]\leq\frac{\|x_{0}-\bar{x}_{0}\|^{2}}{2S_{k}}+B^{2}\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\alpha_{t}^{2}}{2S_{k}}.

and feasibility violation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\rm dist}^{2}(\hat{x}_{k},\mathcal{X})]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}4}\alpha_{0}mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}{\rho\beta(1-\beta)}\left(\frac{\|x_{0}-\bar{x}_{0}\|^{2}}{S_{k}}+B^{2}\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\alpha_{t}^{2}}{S_{k}}\right).

Thus, we obtain the claimed results.
∎

Now, Theorem
IV.6
yields (sublinear) convergence rates for SHAM iterates under convex
objective if the non-increasing stepsize
\alpha_{k}
satisfies e.g., the conditions:
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{t}=\infty
and
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{t}^{2}<\infty

for all
k\geq 1
. Let us now present two different choices for the stepsize
\alpha_{k}
:

Choice

(1)
: Consider
\alpha_{k}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\sqrt{k+2}\ln(k+2)},\forall k\geq 1
, with
\alpha_{0}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L_{f}}\right]
. Note that this choice will yield:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k+1}\alpha_{t}\geq\frac{\alpha_{0}(k+1)}{\sqrt{k+3}\ln(k+3)}\;\;\text{and }\;\;\sum_{t=1}^{k+1}\alpha_{t}^{2}\leq\frac{\alpha_{0}^{2}}{\ln(3)}.

Thus, from Theorem
IV.6
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(f(\hat{x}_{k})-f(\hat{x}^{*}_{k}))]\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ln(k+3)}{\sqrt{k+1}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\rm dist}^{2}(\hat{x}_{k},\mathcal{X})]\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ln(k+3)}{\sqrt{k+1}}\right).

Choice

(2)
: Consider
\alpha_{k}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\sqrt{k}},\forall k\geq 1
, with
\alpha_{0}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L_{f}}\right]
. This choice will give us:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k+1}\alpha_{t}\geq\alpha_{0}\sqrt{k+1},\;\;\text{and }\sum_{t=1}^{k+1}\alpha_{t}^{2}\leq\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{0}^{2}\ln(k+1)).

Hence, from Theorem
IV.6
, we get:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(f(\hat{x}_{k})-f(\hat{x}^{*}_{k}))]\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}+\frac{\ln(k+1)}{\sqrt{k+1}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\rm dist}^{2}(\hat{x}_{k},\mathcal{X})]\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}+\frac{\ln(k+1)}{\sqrt{k+1}}\right).

Note that the second choice of
\alpha_{k}

\alpha_{k}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\sqrt{k}}
provides better convergence rates (if we ignore the logarithmic term) for SHAM algorithm, which is
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)

[
12

V
Numerical results

In this section, we consider a problem characterized by a quadratic objective function and second order cone (SOC) constraints, presented as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\min\limits_{x{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\in\mathcal{Y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Q_{f}x+q_{f}^{T}x\\
&\text{subject to }\;\|Q_{i}x+a_{i}\|\leq q_{i}^{T}x+b_{i}\;\;\forall i=1:m,\end{array}

(26)

where
Q_{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},q_{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},Q_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}\times n},a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}},q_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

and compact set
\mathcal{Y}=[-10^{3},\;10^{3}]
(hence, the feasible set contains an open set around origin) represent the problem parameters, with
x
as the optimization variable. Hence, this problem aligns with all the assumptions of problem (
1
). Depending on whether
Q_{f}
is positive semidefinite or positive definite, (
26
) possesses either a convex or
\mu
-strongly convex objective function, respectively. Furthermore, problem (
26
) exhibits remarkable generality, as it encompasses convex quadratic programs (QPs), quadratically constrained quadratic programs (QCQPs), and numerous other nonlinear convex optimization problems, as elucidated in
[
8
]
. Moreover, SOC constraints are general over quadratic constraints in convex settings (see Section
A.2.3
in
[
24
]
). From a practical perspective, problem (
26
) finds relevance in diverse fields. For instance,
[
4
]
(equation
11
) illustrates its applicability in robust optimal control problems. In the realm of signal processing, it emerges as a powerful tool for robust beamformer design, facilitating the minimization of transmitted power while adhering to received signal-to-noise ratio constraints, as showcased in
[
19
]
.

Practical implementations are conducted using MATLAB R2023b on a laptop equipped with an i5 CPU operating at 2.1 GHz and 16 GB of RAM. The problem parameters are generated as random data within MATLAB, with the added constraint that
n_{i}<n
for all
i\in[m]
.

(1)
Proof of Lemma
II.4
:
If
x\in\mathcal{X}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}}
, the result holds trivially. So let us assume that
x\notin\mathcal{X}
. Define the index set

\mathcal{I}(x)=\{j\in[m]:h_{j}(x)>0\}
.
Since
x\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{Y}}\backslash\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{I}(x)
is nonempty. From (
6
):

\displaystyle{\rm dist}(x,\mathcal{X})\leq c\max_{j\in[m]}\left[(h_{j}(x))_{+}\right]=ch_{j^{*}}(x),

(27)

where
j^{*}\in\argmax_{j}\{h_{j}(x):j\in[m]\}
and
c
is independent of
x
. Clearly
j^{*}\in\mathcal{I}(x)
and
h_{j^{*}}(x)=\max_{j\in\mathcal{I}(x)}\{h_{j}(x)\}
. Fix
\Delta_{j^{*}}^{x}\in\partial h_{j^{*}}(x)
. If
\Delta_{j^{*}}^{x}\neq 0
, from (
2
) and the fact that
\Pi_{\mathcal{L}(h_{j^{*}};x;\Delta_{j^{*}}^{x})}(x)\in\mathcal{L}(h_{j^{*}};x;\Delta_{j^{*}}^{x})
, we get:

\displaystyle h_{j^{*}}\!(x)\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!-\langle\Delta_{j^{*}}^{x}\!,\!\Pi_{\mathcal{L}(h_{j^{*}}\!;x;\Delta_{j^{*}}^{x}\!)}(x)\!-\!x\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\!B_{h}{\rm dist}(x,\!\mathcal{L}(h_{j^{*}}\!;x;\Delta_{j^{*}}^{x}\!)\!),

where the last inequality follows from the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and Assumption
II.2
. Minimizing the right-hand side over
\Delta_{j^{*}}^{x}

and using (
27
), we get:

\displaystyle{\rm dist}(x,\mathcal{X})\leq cB_{h}\min_{\Delta_{j^{*}}^{x}\in\partial h_{j^{*}}(x)}{\rm dist}(x,\mathcal{L}(h_{j^{*}};x;\Delta_{j^{*}}^{x}))

\displaystyle\leq cB_{h}\max_{j\in[m]}\min_{\Delta_{j}^{x}\in\partial h_{j}(x)}{\rm dist}(x,\mathcal{L}(h_{j};x;\Delta_{j}^{x})).

If
\Delta_{j^{*}}^{x}={0}
, from convexity,
x
is the minimizer of
h_{j^{*}}
, and
h_{j^{*}}(x)=\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{j^{*}}(y)\leq 0,

where the inequality follows from the assumption that
\mathcal{X}
is non-empty. However, by the definition of
j^{*}
,
h_{j^{*}}(x)>0
. Hence it is a contradiction, thus it is impossible to have
\Delta_{j^{*}}^{x}={0}
.

(2)
Proof of Lemma
IV.1
:
From the smoothness property of the function
f
, we have:

\displaystyle 2\langle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}u_{k}}-x_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:alg2step1}}{=}2\langle x_{k}-x_{k+1}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:smooth_f}}{\leq}2\alpha_{k}(f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1}))+(\alpha_{k}L_{f}-2)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}.

Hence we get the desired result.

(3)
Proof of Lemma
IV.2
:
Following
[
26
]
(Lemma 4), we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\tilde{x}_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{L}_{j_{k}}}(\tilde{x}_{k})\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{[k]}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\rho}{m}\min_{\Delta_{j}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\in\partial h_{j}(\tilde{x}_{k})}{\rm dist}^{2}(\tilde{x}_{k},\mathcal{L}(h_{j};\tilde{x}_{k};\Delta_{j}^{\tilde{x}_{k}})).

Noticing that
\tilde{x}_{k}\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{Y}}
, from Lemma
II.4
, we get:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\tilde{x}_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{L}_{j_{k}}}(\tilde{x}_{k})\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{[k]}]\overset{\eqref{eq:lin_reg2}}{\geq}\frac{\rho}{mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}{\rm dist}^{2}(\tilde{x}_{k},\mathcal{X}).

After rearranging the terms, we obtain:

\displaystyle{\rm dist}^{2}(\tilde{x}_{k},\mathcal{X})\leq\frac{mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}{\rho}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\tilde{x}_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{L}_{j_{k}}}(\tilde{x}_{k})\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{[k]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}{\rho}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\|v_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{L}_{j_{k}}}(v_{k})\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{[k]}\right]+\|v_{k}-\tilde{x}_{k}\|^{2}\right),

where in the last inequality we use (
15
) and (
17
).

(4)
Proof of Lemma
IV.3
:
For any
\tilde{x}_{k}\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{Y}}
and given
\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\in\partial h_{j_{k}}(\tilde{x}_{k})
, consider the following two cases.

Case (i):
When
\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}=0
, from (
13
) we have
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}z_{k}}=v_{k}
. Thus the result holds automatically after using (
15
) and (
16
).

Case (ii):
When
\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\neq 0
,
using
\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(v_{k})\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
, we have:

\displaystyle{\rm dist}^{2}(x_{k+1},\mathcal{X})\overset{\eqref{eq:proj_property}}{\leq}\|x_{k+1}-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(v_{k})\|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\overset{\eqref{eq:proj_property2}}{\leq}\|z_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(v_{k})\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:algstep}}{=}\|v_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(v_{k})\|^{2}+\beta^{2}\frac{(l_{h_{j_{k}}}(v_{k};\tilde{x}_{k}))^{2}_{+}}{\|\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-2\beta\frac{(l_{h_{j_{k}}}(v_{k};\tilde{x}_{k}))_{+}}{\|\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\|^{2}}\langle\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}},v_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(v_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(v_{k})\|^{2}+\beta^{2}\frac{(l_{h_{j_{k}}}(v_{k};\tilde{x}_{k}))^{2}_{+}}{\|\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-2\beta\frac{(l_{h_{j_{k}}}(v_{k};\tilde{x}_{k}))_{+}}{\|\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\|^{2}}(h_{j_{k}}(\tilde{x}_{k})-h_{j_{k}}(\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(v_{k})))

\displaystyle\quad-2\beta\frac{(l_{h_{j_{k}}}(v_{k};\tilde{x}_{k}))_{+}}{\|\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\|^{2}}\langle\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}},v_{k}-\tilde{x}_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(v_{k})\|^{2}+\beta^{2}\frac{(l_{h_{j_{k}}}(v_{k};\tilde{x}_{k}))^{2}_{+}}{\|\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-2\beta\frac{(l_{h_{j_{k}}}(v_{k};\tilde{x}_{k}))_{+}}{\|\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\|^{2}}l_{h_{j_{k}}}(v_{k};\tilde{x}_{k})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:ineq3}}{=}\|v_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(v_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{\beta(2-\beta)}{\|\Delta_{j_{k}}^{\tilde{x}_{k}}\|^{2}}(l_{h_{j_{k}}}(v_{k};\tilde{x}_{k}))^{2}_{+},

where the third inequality follows from convexity of function
h_{j_{k}}
and the fourth inequality uses that
h(\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(v_{k}))\leq 0
. Further, noticing that
\beta\in(0,2)
, we obtain the statement.

IV.4
:
From Lemma
IV.3
, we have:

\displaystyle{\rm dist}^{2}(x_{k+1},\mathcal{X})\leq\|v_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(v_{k})\|^{2}\overset{\eqref{eq:proj_property}}{\leq}\|v_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(\tilde{x}_{k})\|^{2}

Now, after taking expectation conditioned on
j_{k}
, and using the result from Lemma
IV.2
, we get:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\rm dist}^{2}(x_{k+1},\mathcal{X})|\mathcal{F}_{[k]}]\leq\left(2+\frac{4mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}{\rho}\right)\|v_{k}-\tilde{x}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}{\rho}\mathbb{E}\left[\|v_{k}-\Pi_{\mathcal{L}_{k}}(v_{k})\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{[k]}\right].

After rearranging the terms we get the required result.

(6)
Proof of Lemma
IV.7
:
From Theorem
IV.5
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}]

(28)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\mu\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}]-2\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}[(f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*}))]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\rho\beta(1-\beta)}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}4}mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}\mathbb{E}[{\rm dist}^{2}(x_{k+1},\mathcal{X})]+\alpha_{k}^{2}B^{2}.

For
k\leq k_{0}
, we have
\alpha_{k}=\frac{1}{L_{f}}
, thus from (
28
), we get:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}]\leq\left(1-\frac{\mu}{L_{f}}\right)\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k}\!-\!x^{*}\|^{2}]+\frac{B^{2}}{L_{f}^{2}}

\displaystyle\!\leq\!\max\left(\left(1-\frac{\mu}{L_{f}}\right)\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k}\!-\!x^{*}\|^{2}]+\frac{B^{2}}{L_{f}^{2}},\frac{B^{2}}{L_{f}^{2}}\right).

Using the geometric sum formula and noticing that
\theta_{\mu,L_{f}}=\left(1-\frac{\mu}{L_{f}}\right)
, we obtain the first statement.
Next, for
k>k_{0}
, from the choice of
\alpha_{k}
, we know that
\alpha_{k}=\frac{2}{\mu(k+1)}
, thus from (
28
), we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}]\leq\left(1-\frac{2}{k+1}\right)\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{4}{\mu(k+1)}\mathbb{E}[(f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*}))]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\rho\beta(1-\beta)}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}4}mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}\mathbb{E}[{\rm dist}^{2}(x_{k+1},\mathcal{X})]+\frac{4B^{2}}{\mu^{2}(k+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{k-1}{k+1}\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}]-\frac{4}{\mu(k+1)}\mathbb{E}[(f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*}))]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\rho\beta(1-\beta)}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}4}mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}\mathbb{E}[{\rm dist}^{2}(x_{k+1},\mathcal{X})]+\frac{4B^{2}}{\mu^{2}(k+1)^{2}}.

Now, multiply the whole inequality by
(k+1)^{2}
, and using the fact that
k^{2}-1\leq k^{2}
, we get:

\displaystyle(k+1)^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}]\leq k^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{4(k+1)}{\mu}\mathbb{E}[(f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*}))]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\rho\beta(1-\beta)(k+1)^{2}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}4}mc^{2}B_{h}^{2}}\mathbb{E}[{\rm dist}^{2}(x_{k+1},\mathcal{X})]+\frac{4B^{2}}{\mu^{2}}.

Hence we get the result (
25
).

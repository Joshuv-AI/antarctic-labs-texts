Abstract




1.
Introduction

Let
p
be prime and
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension with algebraic closure
{\overline{F}}
. We denote the absolute Galois group of
F
by

G_{F}:=\operatorname{Gal}({\overline{F}}/F)
and we write
I_{F}\subset G_{F}
for its inertia subgroup.
For the purpose of this introduction, given a representation
\rho:G_{F}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell})
where
\ell\neq p
, we will call the restriction
\tau:=\rho|_{I_{F}}

an inertial type
.

In the work
[
11
]
of the second author with Dembélé and Voight, a completely explicit description of the inertial types arising from the
\ell
-adic representations
\rho_{E,\ell}
attached to
elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is given. As explained in the introduction of
loc. cit.
, the study of inertial types has many applications, including to modularity, to the classification of representations of
G_{F}
, and to Diophantine equations. Moreover, a recent application of the results in
[
11
]
appeared in the work of Bosco
[
3
]
where the
(\varphi,\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_{3}))
-modules associated to
3
-adic representations of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}_{3}
are explicitly computed.

In this paper, we extend the results of
[
11
]
to the unramified quadratic extension
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
The case of elliptic curves with potentially multiplicative reduction is standard and we summraized it in Proposition
3.8
. The cases
p=2,3
and

E/\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
having potentially good reduction is where the main challenges are. The complete statement of our classification contains many cases divided into tables which can be found either in Section
9
or online
[
7
]
.
Our main results are summarized as follows (see
Section
2.4
for the definition of exceptional types).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
be an elliptic curve with additive potentially good reduction
and inertial type
\tau_{E}
.

(A) If
p=3
, then
\tau_{E}
is classified according to Table
14
;

(B) If
p=2
, then
\tau_{E}
is exceptional if and only if the 3-torsion field of
E
has degree 24 over
\mathbb{Q}_{4}
and, in such case,
\tau_{E}
is classified according to Tables
18
–
19
;

(C) If
p=2
and
\tau_{E}
is non-exceptional, then
\tau_{E}
is classified according to Tables
15
–
16
.

Moreover, we provide in
[
7
]
an elliptic curve realizing each inertial type, and also
Magma
code that takes an elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
and identifies its type.

For
p\geq 5
, the inertial types arising from elliptic curves with potentially good reduction over any finite extension
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
are better understood. Since these types are all tame and can be described uniformly at no additional cost, we include them in full generality in Propositions
3.7
and
4.1
.
Moreover, we prove related results valid over
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for any prime
p
, for instance, in Theorem
3.4
we study how quadratic twist affects certain inertial types and, in Theorem
7.1
, we show that, for
E/F
with potentially good reduction, its inertial type is completely determined by the inertial field.

The change of the base field from
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
to
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
introduces new phenomena, mostly due to the fact that the residue field has even degree as an extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The most significant differences occur for
p=2
, namely, the existence of triply imprimitive supercuspidal representations and exceptional representations with inertia of order 8;
this greatly increases the complexity of the computations required to describe all the supercuspidal inertial types.
An important difference with respect to
[
11
]
occurs in the classification of exceptional types, where in
loc. cit.
the authors rely on the work of Bayer–Rio
[
1
]
classifying the
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
, whilst here
we have to compute all the relevant
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{4}
ourselves.

The Galois representations arising on the
\ell
-adic Tate module of an elliptic curve
E
defined over extensions
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with
p\neq\ell
have been studied by several authors, including the work of Dokchitser–Dokchitser
[
12
]
and Coppola
[
9
,
8
,
10
]
. These works provide a description of the representation of inertia as an abstract group together with the image of Frobenius; moreover, in most cases, they describe a recipe to compute the field fixed by their kernel, from a given Weierstrass model for
E
.

Our approach follows
[
11
]
and a key difference from the mentioned works is the use of the language of Weil–Deligne representations. Furthermore, working over
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
allows for a fully explicit description of the inertial types. In particular, we know exactly how many inertial types there are at each conductor and what is the fixed fields by their kernels; this level of detail has applications to Diophantine equations as discussed at length in
[
2
]
.
Observe also that an inertial type arising from an elliptic curve can also arise, for example, from a classical or Hilbert modular form whose field of coefficients is not
\mathbb{Q}
(and so not corresponding to an elliptic curves by the Eichler–Shimura correspondence). Since our description of the types is independent of elliptic curves, it has the advantage that it can be applied to study inertial types coming from other sources.
In fact, our computations to determine the types
\tau
hold under the following assumptions:

(i)

\tau
has unramified determinant;

(ii)

\tau
has image isomorphic to one of the groups in Theorem
2.19
;

(iii)

\tau
has conductor bounded by an explicit constant (in terms of the base field
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
).

(iv)

if
p=2
and
\tau
is non exceptional with image isomorphic to
Q_{8}
then
\tau
is triply imprimitive (see Section
2.4
for the definition).

(v)

if
p=2
and
\tau
is exceptional then it becomes triply imprimitive after a cubic base change.

It follows that any inertial type
\tau
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
with conductor bounded by the constant and satisfying these properties must be among the types we found. An application of this can be found in the forthcoming work of Pacetti–Torcomian
[
17
]
, where they use our classification to determine inertial types of Jacobians of hyperelliptic curves appearing in the study of certain Fermat-type equations.
Finally, note that assumptions (i)–(iii) are well known properties of the types
\tau_{E}
coming from elliptic curves, and (iv)-(v) follow from Theorem
6.4
, hence the types we will compute include all types from elliptic curves.

2.
Background material

In this section, we recall the basic definitions and results needed for the
classification of inertial types of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
; we refer
to
[
11
, §2]
for a more detailed
exposition.

2.1.
Notation

Let
p
be a prime. Let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
\mathcal{O}_{F}
its
ring of integers,
\mathfrak{p}
its maximal ideal,
\pi\in\mathfrak{p}
a uniformizer and

v:F^{\times}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
the valuation of
F
normalized so that

v(\pi)=1
. We denote the residue field
\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}
by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and by
\overline{F}

a fixed
algebraic closure of
F
. For any finite extension
K/F
, let
K^{un}\subset{\overline{F}}
be the maximal unramified extension of
K
; we have
K^{un}=KF^{un}
. Let
W_{F}
be the Weil
group of
F
and
I_{F}
its inertia subgroup. The group
W_{F}
is made into a topological group by declaring that

I_{F}
is open in
W_{F}
and that the subspace topology of
I_{F}
in
W_{F}
is
the same as the subspace topology of
I_{F}
in
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)
. Let

W_{F}^{ab}
be the maximal abelian quotient of
W_{F}
and
A:F^{\times}\xrightarrow{\sim}W^{ab}_{F}
the local Artin reciprocity map normalized so that

A(\pi)=\operatorname{Frob_{F}}
is the (class of a) geometric Frobenius automorphism,
that is,
\operatorname{Frob_{F}}(x^{q})\equiv x\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}
. For a topological
group
G
, by a character of
G
we mean a continuous group homomorphism

\chi:G\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
and, given a morphism of topological groups

\varphi:G\xrightarrow{}G^{\prime}
, we say that
\chi
factors through
\varphi
if
there exists a character
\chi^{\prime}:G^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
such that

\chi=\chi^{\prime}\circ\varphi
; when
\varphi
is understood, for example, when
G^{\prime}
is a quotient of
G
we will simply say that
\chi
factors through
G^{\prime}
.
A character
\chi
of
W_{F}
must factor through
W_{F}^{ab}
and so we can
use the Artin map to identify it with a character of
F^{\times}
, denoted by
\chi^{A}:=\chi\circ A
.
Given a character
\chi
of
W_{F}
, its
conductor is the biggest ideal
\mathfrak{p}^{m}
so that
\chi^{A}|_{1+\mathfrak{p}^{m}}
is
trivial, and so
\chi^{A}
factors through
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}/(1+\mathfrak{p}^{m})\simeq(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}^{m})^{\times}
; we will denote the exponent
m
as
m(\chi)
and the conductor
as
\operatorname{Cond}(\chi)
, so that
\operatorname{Cond}(\chi)=\mathfrak{p}^{m(\chi)}

and
m(\chi)=v(\operatorname{Cond}(\chi))
. Finally, let
\omega:W_{F}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be the character of
W_{F}
corresponding via the Artin map to the norm character
|\cdot|_{v}
on
F
, so that
\omega(g)=q^{-a}
for
g|_{F^{un}}=\operatorname{Frob}^{a}
with
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.2.
Weil–Deligne representations

Definition 2.1
.

A
2
-dimensional Weil–Deligne (WD) representation is a pair
(\rho,N)
where

(i)

\rho:W_{F}\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is a continuous representation,

(ii)

N\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is a nilpotent operator,

(iii)

\rho(g)N\rho(g)^{-1}=\omega(g)N
.

We say that two Weil–Deligne representations
(\rho,N),(\rho^{\prime},N^{\prime})
are
isomorphic if there exist
P\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
such that

P\rho(g)P^{-1}=\rho(g)^{\prime}
for all
g
and
PNP^{-1}=N^{\prime}
.

A key concept in the study of Weil–Deligne representation is its conductor.
The definition of conductor for characters (i.e dimension 1
representations) is the one given in §
2.1
, and for general
representation we refer to
[
20
, Chapter IV,§10]
. For
a WD-representation
\rho
, we denote its conductor and conductor exponent
by
\operatorname{Cond}(\rho)
and
m(\rho)
, respectively.

Any 2-dimensional Weil–Deligne representation
\rho
of
W_{F}
is isomorphic to one of the
following:

(i)

Principal Series
: Take
N=0
and

\rho=PS(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\theta_{1}\oplus\theta_{2}
,
where
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}:W_{F}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
are characters such that
\theta_{1}\theta_{2}^{-1}\neq\omega^{\pm 1}
. We have
m(\rho)=m(\theta_{1})+m(\theta_{2})
.

(ii)

Special or Steinberg
: Take
N=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and

\rho=\operatorname{St}(\theta)
, where

\operatorname{St}(\theta)=\theta\oplus\omega\theta
for a character
\theta:W_{F}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
. Here
m(\rho)=2m(\theta)
if
\theta
is ramified and
m(\rho)=1
otherwise.

(iii)

Supercuspidal representations
: Take
N=0

and
\rho
an irreducible
2
-dimensional representation. If the projective
image of
\rho
is dihedral, then
\rho
is called
imprimitive
or
nonexceptional
, otherwise it is called
primitive
or
exceptional
,
in which case its projective image is
A_{4}
or
S_{4}
. The exceptional types
only occur when
p=2
. The conductor formula for the imprimitive case is given in (
2.3
) below.

The following result will come handy when dealing with twists of
representations.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(\rho,N)
be a 2-dimensional Weil–Deligne representation of
W_{F}
, such that
N=0
and let
\theta
be a character of
W_{F}
. If
m(\rho)\neq 2m(\theta)
, then
m(\theta\otimes\rho)=\operatorname{max}\{m(\rho),2m(\theta)\}

Proof.

For the supercuspidal case see
[
21
, Proposition 3.4]
, the principal series follows from a simple computation.
∎

2.3.
Nonexceptional supercuspidal representations

Let
K/F
be a quadratic extension and
\psi_{K}:W_{F}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}

the quadratic character associated to
K
, that is,

\operatorname{Ker}(\psi_{K})=W_{K}
. Let
s\in W_{F}
be a lift of the non-trivial
element of
\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)
and, for a character

\theta:W_{K}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
, we define
\theta^{s}(g)=\theta(s^{-1}gs)
;
since
W_{K}
is normal in
W_{F}
this is independent of the choice of
s
.
By local class field theory
(\theta^{s})^{A}=\theta^{A}\circ s
.

Let
\theta:W_{K}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
be
a character satisfying
\theta\neq\theta^{s}
. The representation

\rho=\operatorname{Ind}_{W_{K}}^{W_{F}}(\theta)
is irreducible and we call

(\rho,0)
the
nonexeptional
supercuspidal representation induced from/by
\theta
. Its conductor exponent satisfies

(2.3)

m(\rho)=\begin{cases}2m(\chi),&\text{ if $K/F$ is unramified;}\\
m(\chi)+m(\psi_{K}),&\text{ if $K/F$ is ramified. }\end{cases}

For convenience we recall the following auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 2.4
.

The following are equivalent:

(i)

\theta^{s}=\theta
,

(ii)

s(x)/x\in\operatorname{Ker}(\theta^{A})
for all
x\in K^{\times}
,

(iii)

\theta^{A}
factors through the norm map
\operatorname{Nm_{K|F}}:K^{\times}\xrightarrow{}F^{\times}
.

Proof.

See
[
11
, Lemma 2.3.2]

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
K/F
be a quadratic extension and
\psi_{K}
the associated quadratic character. Let
\delta
be the determinant of the 2 dimensional representation of
W_{F}
induced by a character
\theta:W_{K}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
. Then
\delta
is unramified if and only if
\theta^{A}|_{\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}}=\psi_{K}^{A}|_{\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}}
. Furthermore, if
\delta
is unramified
then
\theta^{s}|_{I_{K}}=\theta^{-1}|_{I_{K}}
.

Proof.

In
[
11
, Lemma 2.3.7]
the result appears as an implication but the proof shows that it is and equivalence.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
K/F
be a quadratic extension,
\theta
a character of
W_{K}
and
\rho:=\Ind_{W_{K}}^{W_{F}}\theta
.
Assume that
\text{det}~\rho
is unramified. If
\theta|_{I_{K}}
has order
\geq 3
, then
\rho
is irreducible.

Proof.

Let
\chi:=\theta|_{I_{K}}
. From Lemma
2.5
we have
\chi^{s}=\chi^{-1}
.
Since
\chi
has order
>2
, we have
\chi^{-1}\neq\chi
and hence
\theta^{s}\neq\theta
. Thus
\rho
is irreducible.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a group,
H\leq G
an index 2 subgroup, and consider the induction
\rho:=\Ind_{H}^{G}(\theta)
where
\theta
is a character of order 4 or 6. If
\rho
has trivial determinant and non-abelian image, then
\rho
has image the quaternion group
Q_{8}
if
\theta
has order 4 and image
\mathbb{Z}/3\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/4
if
\theta
has order 6.

Proof.

Since
\rho
has trivial determinant its image is a finite subgroup of
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
whose clasification is well known. There are two infinite families: the cyclic groups
C_{n}
and the binary dihedral or dicyclic groups
\operatorname{Dic}_{n}
of order
4n
and three exceptional groups, of order
24,48
and
120
. Since the image is non-abelian of order 8 or 12 the only possibilities are
\operatorname{Dic}_{2}\simeq Q_{8}
and
\operatorname{Dic}_{3}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/3\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/4

∎

2.4.
Exceptional (supercuspidal) representations

The supercuspidal representations that are not induced from a character are
exceptional and their projective image is
A_{4}
or
S_{4}
. Since exceptional
representations only occur for
p=2
, we assume in this section that
p=2
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\rho
be a supercuspidal representation and let
\mathfrak{J}(\rho)
be the group of characters
\chi
such that
\rho\otimes\chi\simeq\rho
. Then
\#\mathfrak{J}(\rho)\in\{1,2,4\}
and the following statements hold:

(i)

\#\mathfrak{J}(\rho)=1\iff\rho
is exceptional.

(ii)

\#\mathfrak{J}(\rho)=2\iff
There is a unique quadratic extension
K/F
such that
\rho=\operatorname{Ind}_{W_{K}}^{W_{F}}(\theta)
.

(iii)

\#\mathfrak{J}(\rho)=4\iff
the projective image of
\rho
is
D_{2}\simeq C_{2}\times C_{2}

\iff\rho=\operatorname{Ind}_{W_{K}}^{W_{F}}(\theta)
can be induced from
3
distinct quadratic extensions
\iff
the character
\theta^{s}/\theta
factors through the norm map.

Moreover if
\#\mathfrak{J}(\rho)=4
then
\mathfrak{J}(\rho)
is the group formed by the
trivial character and the
3
characters
\psi_{K}
associated to the
3

quadratic extensions
K
of
F
from which
\rho
is induced.

Proof.

See
[
5
, 41.3 Corollary]
and
[
13
, 2.7 Proposition]
.
∎

According to this proposition, we call the representations
\rho
with
\#\mathfrak{J}(\rho)=2

simply imprimitive
and those with
\#\mathfrak{J}(\rho)=4
we call them
triply
imprimitive
.

Proposition 2.9
.

For
L/F
a finite extension and
\rho
an exceptional supercuspidal representation, we define

\rho_{L}:=\rho|_{W_{L}}
. Then,

•

There exist a cubic extension
K/F
such that
\rho_{K}
is non exceptional.

•

If
K/F
is cubic and Galois, then
\rho_{K}
is triply
imprimitive and the nontrivial elements of
\mathfrak{J}(\rho_{K})
are permuted
transitively by
\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)
.

•

If
K/F
is cubic and non-Galois, let
M/F
be the normal closure of
K/F
and

E/F
the maximal unramified sub-extension of
M/F
. Then,

\rho_{K}
is simply imprimitive,
\rho_{M}
is triply imprimitive and
\rho_{E}
is primitive; moreover, the nontrivial
elements of
\mathfrak{J}(\rho_{M})
are permuted transitively by

\operatorname{Gal}(M/F)
.

Proof.

See
[
5
, §42.2, Theorem and Remark]
.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
K/F
be a cubic extension and
M/K
a quadratic ramified extension. Let
\theta
be a character of
W_{M}
such that
\theta^{A}
does not factor through the norm map. There is at most one exceptional supercuspidal representation
\rho
of
W_{F}
such that
\rho_{K}=\operatorname{Ind}_{W_{M}}^{W_{K}}(\theta)
. In other words, for an exceptional representation
\rho
, the triple
(K,M,\theta)
determines
\rho
uniquely.

Proof.

See
[
6
, Lemme 12.1.3]
.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

The converse is not true, for instance if
\rho_{K}
happens to be triply imprimitive, there are three different triples
(K,M_{i},\theta_{i})
that describe
\rho
.

2.5.
Inertial types

We now study the inertial types, that is the behavior of the above Weil–Deligne
representations when restricted to the inertia subgroup.

A
Weil–Deligne inertial type
is an equivalence class
[\rho,N]
of
WD-representations under the equivalence relation
(\rho,N)\sim(\rho^{\prime},N^{\prime})
if
there exist
P\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
such that
P\rho(g)P^{-1}=\rho^{\prime}(g)
for all

g\in I_{F}
and
PNP^{-1}=N^{\prime}
. Thus one can think of inertial types simply
as WD-representations of
I_{F}
. Since
N=0
except for Steinberg
representations, that are classified in Proposition
3.8
, we
will denote the equivalence class
[\rho,N]
simply by
\tau:=\rho|_{I_{K}}
.

Considering the restrictions of the different Weil–Deligne representations in §
2.2
, any 2-dimensional
inertial type
\tau
is of one of the following:

(i)

\tau
is the restriction of a principal series, so there are characters
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}:W_{F}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
satisfying

\tau\simeq PS(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})|_{I_{F}}=\theta_{1}|_{I_{F}}\oplus\theta_{2}|_{I_{F}};

(ii)

\tau
is the restriction of a Steinberg representation, so there
is
\theta:W_{F}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
such that

\tau=\operatorname{St}(\theta)|_{I_{K}}=\theta|_{I_{K}}\oplus(\omega\theta)|_{I_{K}}=\theta|_{I_{K}}\oplus\theta|_{I_{K}};

(iii)

\tau
is the restriction of an imprimitive supercuspidal representation induced from the unique quadratic unramified extension
K/F
, and so there is a character
\theta:W_{K}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
such that
\theta\neq\theta^{s}
and

\tau\simeq\operatorname{Ind}_{W_{K}}^{W_{F}}(\theta)|_{I_{F}}=\theta|_{I_{F}}\oplus\theta^{s}|_{I_{F}};

(iv)

\tau
is the restriction of a imprimitive supercuspidal representation induced from a quadratic ramified extension
K/F
, so there is
\theta:W_{K}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
such that
\theta|_{I_{K}}\neq\theta^{s}|_{I_{K}}
and

\tau\simeq\operatorname{Ind}_{W_{K}}^{W_{F}}(\theta)|_{I_{F}}=\operatorname{Ind}_{I_{K}}^{I_{F}}(\theta|_{I_{K}});

(v)

\tau
is the restriction of an exceptional supercuspidal representation (only occurs for
p=2
).

To avoid writing “Let
\tau
be the restriction of a supercuspidal
representation induced from a ramified extension” and similar long statements,
we will simply call the inertial types in (i) principal series, in (ii) Steinberg, in
(iii)

unramified supercuspidal, in
(iv)
ramified supercuspidal, and in
(v)
exceptional. Since
all the inertial types, except for the trivial one, have nontrivial image of
inertia there should be no confusion between (iii) and (iv) and the common use
of (un)ramified.

As this classification shows, when dealing with inertial types, most of the time
we only care about
\theta|_{I_{F}}
and not about the character
\theta
. To
avoid confusion from now on we fix the following notation: the letters
\rho

and
\theta
will be reserved for representations and characters of
W_{F}
while

\tau
and
\chi
will be used for the respective restrictions to inertia.
Moreover, when there is no confusion we will abuse of notation and write just

\theta
for the character
\theta^{A}
.

Before continuing, we need to introduced further notation.
Let
K/F
be a quadratic extension,
\mathfrak{p}
the maximal ideal of
K
,
f
a positive integer and

\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{p}^{f}\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}
. The usual norm map
\operatorname{Nm}:\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
descends to a map

\overline{\operatorname{Nm}}:(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{f})^{\times}\xrightarrow{}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{a})^{\times}
and we define

U_{\mathfrak{p}^{f}}:=\overline{\operatorname{Nm}}((\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{f})^{\times})\leq(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{a})^{\times}.

Note that the inclusion
i:\mathcal{O}_{F}\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{O}_{K}
also descends to a map

j:(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{a})^{\times}\xrightarrow{}(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{f})^{\times}
such that

h_{K}\circ i=j\circ h_{F}
where
h_{F}:\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}\rightarrow(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{a})^{\times}
is the quotient map and
h_{K}
is the analogous map for
K
.
We can use this
j
to see
U_{\mathfrak{p}^{f}}
as a subgroup of
(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{f})^{\times}
. This is precisely what is done in the following lemma

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
K/F
be a quadratic extension and
\tau_{E}
a supercuspidal inertial type induced by
\chi:\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{K}\to\mathbb{C}
of conductor
\mathfrak{p}^{f}
. Then
\chi
factors through

(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{f})^{\times}/U_{\mathfrak{p}^{f}}
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon_{K}=\psi_{K}|_{\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}}
. By Lemma
2.5
we know that
\chi|_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F}}=\varepsilon_{K}
this is the same as saying that
\chi\circ i=\varepsilon_{K}
where
i
is the inclusion of
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
. By class field theory
\varepsilon_{K}\circ\operatorname{Nm}
is trivial and so
\chi\circ i\circ\operatorname{Nm}
is also trivial. If
\chi
has conductor
\mathfrak{p}^{f}
then there exists a character
\overline{\chi}
of
(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{f})^{\times}
such that
\chi=\overline{\chi}\circ h_{K}
. But then we have

\chi\circ i\circ\operatorname{Nm}=\overline{\chi}\circ h_{K}\circ i\circ\operatorname{Nm}=\overline{\chi}\circ j\circ h_{F}\circ\operatorname{Nm}=\overline{\chi}\circ j\circ\overline{\operatorname{Nm}}\circ h_{K}

and so
\overline{\chi}
is trivial on
j\circ\overline{\operatorname{Nm}}\circ h_{K}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})=U_{\mathfrak{p}^{f}}\leq(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{f})^{\times}
.
∎

2.6.
Elliptic curves

Let
E/F
be an elliptic with conductor
N_{E}
.
For each prime
\ell
, the action of
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)
on the

\ell^{n}
-torsion points of
E
for all
n>0
induces an action of

\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)
on the Tate module

T_{\ell}(E)=\varprojlim E[\ell^{n}]\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{2}
. After tensoring with

\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
and choosing a basis for
T_{\ell}(E)
we get a representation

\rho_{E,\ell}:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
. When
\ell\neq p
one can construct from
\rho_{E,\ell}
a
Weil–Deligne representation
(\rho_{E},N)
, which is independent of the prime

\ell
. We define the inertial type of
E
to be
\tau_{E}=\rho_{E}|_{I_{K}}
.
The determinant of
\tau_{E}
is
\omega
which, being trivial on inertia, imposes
constraints to the shape of
\tau_{E}
. Indeed, if
\tau_{E}
is a
nonexceptional inertial type then, using Lemma
2.5
, we have the following cases.

•

If
\tau_{E}=PS(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})|_{I_{F}}
then

\theta_{1}|_{I_{F}}=\theta_{2}^{-1}|_{I_{F}}
and, letting

\chi:=\theta_{1}|_{I_{K}}
, we have

(2.13)

\tau_{E}=\chi\oplus\chi^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad m(\tau_{E})=2m(\chi).

•

If
\tau_{E}=\operatorname{St}(\theta)|_{I_{F}}
and
\chi:=\theta|_{I_{F}}

then
\chi=\chi^{-1}
, thus
\chi
is at most quadratic,

(2.14)

\tau_{E}=\chi\oplus\chi\quad\text{and}\quad m(\tau_{E})=2m(\chi).

•

If
\tau_{E}=(\operatorname{Ind}_{W_{K}}^{W_{F}}\theta)|_{I_{F}}
and

\chi:=\theta|_{I_{F}}
then
\chi^{s}=\chi^{-1}
and
\theta^{A}|_{\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}}=\psi_{K}^{A}|_{\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}}
. Moreover,

(2.15)

\tau_{E}=\chi\oplus\chi^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad m(\tau_{E})=2m(\chi)~\text{if}~K/F~\text{is unramified},

whilst

(2.16)

\tau_{E}=\operatorname{Ind}_{I_{K}}^{I_{F}}\chi\quad\text{and}\quad m(\tau_{E})=m(\chi)+m(\psi_{K})~\text{if}~K/F~\text{is ramified}.

In particular, a single character of inertia
\chi
is enough to determine

\tau_{E}
in all cases. This motivates the following definition:

Definition 2.17
.

We define
\tau_{sc}(u,m,e)_{j}
to be a supercuspidal type induced from

K=F(\sqrt{u})
by a character
\theta
of
W_{K}
with
m(\theta)=m
and restriction to
I_{K}
of order
e
. Similarly, we denote by
\tau_{ps}(1,m,e)_{j}
a principal series type associated to a character
\theta
of
W_{F}
with
m(\theta)=m
and restriction to
I_{F}
of order
e
. The label
j
appears when there are non isomorphic inertial types having these properties.
Finally,
\tau_{St}
denotes the inertial type corresponding to
[St(1),N]
.

Since the conductor of
\tau_{E}
is equal to
N_{E}
and
0\leq v(N_{E})\leq 2+3v(3)+6v(2)
(see
[
20
, Ch. IV, Thm 10.4]
), we have a bound for the conductor of
\tau_{E}
in terms of the ramification of
F
, reducing the study of inertial types over
F
to a finite number of cases.

We recall the following well known result.

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve with potentially good reduction. Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 3}
be coprime to
p
and set
L:=F^{un}(E[m])
. The field
L
is independent of
m
and satisfies:

(a)

L
is the minimal extension of
F^{un}
where
E
attains good reduction, and

(b)

L
is the fixed field of
\operatorname{Ker}(\tau_{E})
.

The field
L
in the above theorem is called
the
inertial field of
E
and the order
e_{E}
of
the group
\Phi:=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F^{un})

is the
semistability defect
of
E
. The following is standard (see
[
15
]
).

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve, and
L
its inertial field. Then exactly one of the following holds

(i)

\Phi
is cyclic of order
2,3,4,6
,

(ii)

p=3
and
\Phi\simeq\mathbb{Z}/3\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/4
of order
12
,

(iii)

p=2
and
\Phi\simeq Q_{8}
the quaternion group of order
8
, or

(iv)

p=2
and
\Phi\simeq\operatorname{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
of order
24
.

2.7.
Quadratic extensions

We will now recall some basic facts about the quadratic extensions
of
F
. For clarity of exposition we will reproduce here some known computations, see for instance
[
18
]
. Recall that
\pi
is a uniformizer of
F
.
The group of units of a local field can be decomposed as (see

[
16
]
prop 5.7)

F^{\times}\simeq\langle\pi\rangle\times\mu_{q-1}\times(1+\mathfrak{p})\simeq\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\times(\mathbb{Z}/p^{a}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n})

where
n=[F:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
and
a
is the largest integer such that

\mu_{p^{a}}\subset F^{\times}
. If
p\geq 3
, then multiplication by
2
is an
automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}/p^{a}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
and so

F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{2}\simeq\langle\pi\rangle/\langle\pi^{2}\rangle\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}/(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{2}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

Therefore, for
p\geq 3
there are exactly three quadratic extensions of
F
, two of which
are ramified and one is unramified; more precisely, we can denote them, respectively, by

K_{1}=F(\sqrt{\pi}),\quad K_{2}=F(\sqrt{u\pi})\quad\text{ and }\quad K_{3}=F(\sqrt{u}),

where
u\in F
generates

(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p})^{\times}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The set of characters

\{1,\psi_{K_{1}},\psi_{K_{2}},\psi_{K_{3}}\}
form a group
where
\psi_{K_{1}}=\psi_{K_{2}}\psi_{K_{3}}
. Since
\psi_{K_{3}}
is unramified

\psi_{K_{1}}
and
\psi_{K_{2}}
coincide on the inertia, we define this common
restriction as
\varepsilon_{1}=\psi_{K_{1}}|_{I_{F}}
. We have
m(\varepsilon_{1})=1
. Indeed,

\varepsilon_{1}
is trivial on the norms from
K_{1}
(by definition) and, by Hensel’s lemma, all the elements in
1+\mathfrak{p}
are squares in
F
and thus norms from
K_{1}
.

For
p=2
we consider the field
F=\mathbb{Q}_{4}=\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\sqrt{5})
. For this
field, we have
a=2
and
n=2
, so

\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\times}=\langle 2\rangle\times\mu_{3}\times 1+\mathfrak{p}\simeq\langle 2\rangle\times\mu_{3}\times\langle-1\rangle\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}

and

\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\times})^{2}=\langle 2\rangle/\langle 4\rangle\times\frac{1+\mathfrak{p}}{(1+\mathfrak{p})^{2}}\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{4},

hence it is enough to determine the squares of in the multiplicative group
1+\mathfrak{p}
.
By the strong Hensel’s lemma (see
[
19
, Ch. II, Thm 1]
), we see that all the elements in
1+\mathfrak{p}^{3}
are squares, thus

1+\mathfrak{p}/(1+\mathfrak{p})^{2}\simeq\frac{1+\mathfrak{p}}{1+\mathfrak{p}^{3}}/(\frac{1+\mathfrak{p}}{1+\mathfrak{p}^{3}})^{2}
. From the
short exact sequence

1\xrightarrow{}\frac{1+\mathfrak{p}^{2}}{1+\mathfrak{p}^{3}}\xrightarrow{}\frac{1+\mathfrak{p}^{1}}{1+\mathfrak{p}^{3}}\xrightarrow{}\frac{1+\mathfrak{p}^{1}}{1+\mathfrak{p}^{2}}\xrightarrow{}1

and the fact that
\frac{1+\mathfrak{p}^{n}}{1+\mathfrak{p}^{n+1}}\simeq(\mathbb{F}_{4},+)\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
, it follows that
\frac{1+\mathfrak{p}^{1}}{1+\mathfrak{p}^{3}}
is either
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{4}
or

(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}/4
. However, the case
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{4}
is not compatible
with the above description of
\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\times})^{2}
thus

\frac{1+\mathfrak{p}^{1}}{1+\mathfrak{p}^{3}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/4
. Moreover since
-1,1+2\sqrt{5},\sqrt{5}
is a set of generators of
\frac{1+\mathfrak{p}}{1+\mathfrak{p}^{3}}
we get

\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\times})^{2}=\langle 2\rangle\times\langle-1\rangle\times\langle 1+2\sqrt{5}\rangle\times\langle\sqrt{5}\rangle\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{4}.

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
b=\sqrt{5}
and
c=1+2\sqrt{5}
. The quadratic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{4}
are:

K_{1}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{-bc})
,

K_{2}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{b})
,

K_{3}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{-1})
,

K_{4}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{c})
,

K_{5}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{bc})
,

K_{6}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{-b})
,

K_{7}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{-2})
,

K_{8}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{2c})
,

K_{9}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{2bc})
,

K_{10}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{-2b})
,

K_{11}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{2})
,

K_{12}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{-2c})
,

K_{13}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{2b})
,

K_{14}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{-2bc})
and

K_{15}=\mathbb{Q}_{4}(\sqrt{-c})
.

Proof.

By the above we can choose
R:=\{(-1)^{r}\cdot 2^{s}\cdot b^{t}\cdot c^{w}~|~0\leq r,s,t,w\leq 1\}
to be a system of representatives of
\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}_{4}^{\times})^{2}
. Since there is a bijection between
y_{i}\in R
and quadratic extensions
K_{i}=F(z_{i})
with
z_{i}=\sqrt{y_{i}}
the result follows.
∎

In the rest of this paper, we will extensively use all the notation in the previous proposition and its proof. Indeed, we will use
z_{i}
and
y_{i}
(e.g.
y_{10}=2bc
and
z_{10}=\sqrt{2bc}
) and also denote by
\psi_{i}

the character associated with
K_{i}
. In particular, by the discriminant–conductor formula, we have
m(\psi_{i})=2
for
1\leq i\leq 6
and
m(\psi_{i})=3

for
7\leq i\leq 14
. The character
\psi_{15}
is unramified and,
for odd
i
, we have
y_{i}=y_{i+1}y_{15}
up to squares, which implies

\psi_{i}=\psi_{i+1}\psi_{15}
. Informally, we say that
K_{i},K_{i+1}
differ by
the unramified extension. As before, we define the common restriction to
inertia to be
\varepsilon_{i}=\psi_{i}|_{I_{F}}
.

3.
The effect of quadratic twists

In this section, we explain how quadratic twists allow to reduce the
case of semistability defect
e=6
to the case
e=3
and the cases
e=2
to curves with
good reduction (
e=1
); in particular, this classifies the inertial types with
e=2
.

Consider the quotient map

j:W_{F}\xrightarrow{}W_{F}^{ab}=W_{F}/\overline{[W_{F},W_{F}]}
from
W_{F}
into its
maximal abelian quotient, and set
I_{F}^{ab}:=j(I_{F})
. Observe that
I_{F}^{ab}

can be regarded as the quotient
I_{F}/\operatorname{Ker}(j|_{I_{F}})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\chi
be a character
of
I_{F}
of order
r
that factors through
I_{F}^{ab}
. Then, there are exactly
r
characters of
W_{F}
having order
r
and restriction to inertia equal to
\chi
.

In particular, if
\theta
is a character of
W_{F}
that is quadratic on
I_{F}
,
then there is a quadratic character
\tilde{\theta}
of
W_{F}
such that

\tilde{\theta}|_{I_{F}}=\theta|_{I_{F}}
.

Proof.

Recall from local class field theory that
W_{F}^{ab}\simeq I_{F}^{ab}\times\langle\operatorname{Frob}_{F}\rangle
, where
\operatorname{Frob}_{F}\in W_{K}
is a lift of Frobenius. Any character
\theta
of
W_{F}
factors through
W_{F}^{ab}
thus it is enough to define
\theta
on
I_{F}^{ab}
and
\operatorname{Frob}_{F}
.
Therefore, if
\theta
satisfies
\theta|_{I_{F}}=\chi
, then we must have
\theta|_{I_{F}^{ab}}=\chi|_{I_{F}^{ab}}
and if we want
\theta
to have order
r
it must also satisfy
\theta(\operatorname{Frob}_{F})=\zeta_{r}
for some
r
-th root of unity. We conclude there are precisely
r
characters with the desired properties.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The hypothesis that
\chi
factors through
I_{F}^{ab}
is necessary, as there are characters of
I_{F}
that do not extend to
W_{F}
.
For example, take
F=\mathbb{Q}_{2}
so that
I_{F}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}/\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{un})
, and let
K=\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{un}(\sqrt{1+2\sqrt{5}})
.
The quadratic character
\chi
of
I_{F}
fixing
K/\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{un}
cannot be extended to
W_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}
, otherwise there should exist a quadratic extension
L/\mathbb{Q}_{2}

such that
L\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{un}=K
which is impossible. Indeed, there are seven quadratic extensions
\mathbb{Q}_{2}(\sqrt{y_{i}})/\mathbb{Q}_{2}
and one can check that
y_{i}\cdot(1+2\sqrt{5})
is not a square on
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{un}
for all
i
(for instance, assuming

\sqrt{y_{i}(1+2\sqrt{5})}\in\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{un}
one can construct an element in
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{un}
of valuation
1/2
).

In general, a character
\chi
of
I_{F}
factors through
I_{F}/\overline{[I_{F},I_{F}]}
which is larger than
I_{F}^{ab}
. However, a character
\theta
of
W_{F}
factors through
W_{F}^{ab}=I_{F}^{ab}\times\langle\operatorname{Frob}_{F}\rangle
, so we cannot necessarily make
\theta
coincide with an arbitrary character
\chi
of
I_{F}
because
\theta
is forced to be trivial on
I_{F}\cap[W_{F},W_{F}]
while
\chi
might not be.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(\rho,N)
be a 2-dimensional Weil–Deligne representation of
W_{F}
with
N=0
and let
\tau=\rho|_{I_{F}}
be the associated inertial type.
If
\text{det}~\rho
is unramified and
\tau(I_{F})\simeq C_{2d}
with
d
odd, then there exists a quadratic character
\tilde{\theta}
of
W_{F}
such that
(\tilde{\theta}\otimes\rho)(I_{F})=C_{d}
.

Proof.

Since
\tau(I_{F})\simeq C_{2d}
is abelian, the inertial type
\tau
is reducible, and the only reducible types with
N=0
are principal series and unramified supercuspidal.

Assume first that
\tau
is principal series. Thus

\tau=\rho|_{I_{F}}=\theta|_{I_{F}}\oplus\theta^{\prime}|_{I_{F}}
, where
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
are characters
of
W_{F}
such that
\chi:=\theta|_{I_{F}}=\theta^{\prime}|_{I_{F}}^{-1}
. Since
\theta^{d}
is quadratic on inertia, by Lemma
3.1
, there is a quadratic character
\tilde{\theta}

such that
\tilde{\theta}|_{I_{F}}=\theta^{d}|_{I_{F}}=\chi^{d}
.
We now have
(\tilde{\theta}\otimes\rho)|_{I_{F}}=\chi^{d+1}\oplus\chi^{-(d+1)}
and, since
d
is odd and coprime to
d+1
, the characters
\chi^{\pm(d+1)}
are inverses of order
d
..

Suppose now that
\tau
is a unramified supercuspidal, that is,
\rho
is induced by a character
\theta
of
W_{K}
where
K
is the quadratic unramified extension of
F
.
Let
\chi:=\theta|_{I_{K}}
. We have that
\rho|_{I_{K}}\simeq\chi\oplus\chi^{s}
is a principal series type satisfying the hypothesis of the theorem,
hence,
by the previous case, there is a quadratic character
\tilde{\theta}
of
W_{K}
such that
(\tilde{\theta}\otimes\rho)(I_{F})=C_{d}
as
I_{K}=I_{F}
.

It remains to show that
\tilde{\theta}
can be extended to a character of
W_{F}
, that is,
\tilde{\theta}=\tilde{\theta}^{s}
where
s\in W_{F}
is a lift of
\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)
.
Since
K/F
is unramified, we can take
s\in\langle\operatorname{Frob}_{F}\rangle
and so
s
commutes with
\operatorname{Frob}_{K}=\operatorname{Frob}_{F}^{2}
thus
\tilde{\theta}^{s}(\operatorname{Frob}_{K})=\tilde{\theta}(s\operatorname{Frob}_{K}s^{-1})=\tilde{\theta}(\operatorname{Frob}_{K})
. Moreover, from the proof of the principal series case, we also know that

\tilde{\theta}|_{I_{K}}=\chi^{d}
is quadratic and
\chi^{s}=\chi^{-1}
(by Lemma
2.5
),
therefore
\tilde{\theta}|_{I_{K}}^{s}=(\chi^{d})^{s}=(\chi^{s})^{d}=(\chi^{-1})^{d}=\chi^{d}=\tilde{\theta}|_{I_{K}}
. Thus
\tilde{\theta}=\tilde{\theta}^{s}
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve with semistability defect
e\in\{2,6\}
.
If
e=2
, then
E
has a quadratic twist with good reduction, and if
e=6
then it has a quadratic twist with semistability defect
3
.

Proof.

The Weil–Deligne representation
(\rho_{E},N)
attached to an
E
satisfies
N=0
(as
E
has potentially good reduction),
\text{det}~\rho_{E}
is unramified, and
the image of the inertial type
\tau_{E}=\rho_{E}|_{I_{F}}

is
C_{2}
or
C_{6}
if
e=2
or
e=6
, respectively.
Therefore, from Theorem
3.3
, there exists a quadratic character
\theta
of
W_{F}
such that
\theta\otimes\rho_{E}
has image of inertia
C_{1}
or
C_{3}
, respectively, if
e=2
or
e=6
. The character
\theta
cuts a quadratic extension
F(\sqrt{d})/F
and the Weil–Deligne representation associated to
E_{d}
(the quadratic twist of
E
by
d
) is
\rho_{E_{d}}=\theta\otimes\rho_{E}
.
Therefore
\tau_{E_{d}}=(\theta\otimes\rho_{E})|_{I_{F}}
, and the conclusion follows from part (b) of
Theorem
2.18
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
T_{e}
be the set of all inertial types of elliptic curves defined over
F
with potentially good reduction and semistability defect
e
. Let
X_{2}
be the set of all quadratic characters
\chi
of
I_{F}
such that
\chi=\theta|_{I_{F}}
for some character
\theta
of
W_{F}
. The set map

\displaystyle T_{3}\times X_{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T_{6}

\displaystyle(\tau_{E},\chi)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\tau_{E}\otimes\chi

is bijective. Moreover, it sends principal series to principal series and supercuspidal types to supercuspidal types.

Proof.

The map is surjective by Theorem
3.4
. To check injectivity suppose
\tau_{1}\otimes\chi_{1}=\tau_{2}\otimes\chi_{2}
. Twisting both sides by
\chi_{1}
gives
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}\otimes\chi_{2}\chi_{1}
and since the
\tau_{1}
has order
3
, we have that
\tau_{2}\otimes\chi_{2}\chi_{1}
has also order
3
. Now, since
\tau_{2}
has order
3
and the order of
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
divides
2
, it follows that
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
is trivial so
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}
and
\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}^{-1}=\chi_{2}
, as desired.

The last statement is clear because the quadratic twist of an irreducible representation is irreducible, hence supercuspidal types are preserved by quadratic twist, and hence principal series as well.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

For
p=3
, an inertial type
\tau_{E}
(determined by a character
\chi
of
I_{F}
or of
I_{K}
where
K/F
is quadratic unramified) of order
e=3
is wild, hence
m(\chi)\geq 2
and
m(\tau_{E})\geq 4
(regardless of
\tau_{E}
being a principal series or
unramified supercuspidal). Since all quadratic characters for
p=3
satisfy
m(\chi)\leq 1
, by Lemma
2.2
, we have
m(\chi\otimes\tau_{E})=m(\tau_{E})
.
For
p=2
, an inertial type with
e=3
is tame so
m(\tau_{E})\leq 2
; since the quadratic characters of
W_{F}
satisfy
m(\chi)\geq 2
we have
m(\chi\otimes\tau_{E})=2m(\chi)
.

The previous proposition and remark allow us to reduce the case
e|6

to
e=3
. In particular, once we know the inertial types with
e=3
, we
obatin the types with
e=6
by twisting. Moreover, we know that we are not
missing nor double counting any type and we can compute its conductor.

Recall that the definition of
\varepsilon_{1}
depends on the field
F
we are
considering. In particular, in the following proposition the
\varepsilon_{1}

appearing in
(ii)
and
(iii)
are different.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve with semistability defect
e\leq 2
. Then,

(i)

If
e=1
, that is,
E
has good reduction, then
v(N_{E})=0
and

\tau_{E}=1\oplus 1
;

(ii)

If
p\geq 3
and
e=2
, then
v(N_{E})=2
and
\tau_{E}=\varepsilon_{1}\oplus\varepsilon_{1}
;

(iii)

If
F=\mathbb{Q}_{4}
and
e=2
, then either

a)

N_{E}=2^{4}
and
\tau_{E}=\varepsilon_{i}\oplus\varepsilon_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,3,5\}
, or

b)

N_{E}=2^{6}
and
\tau_{E}=\varepsilon_{i}\oplus\varepsilon_{i}
for some
i\in\{7,9,11,13\}
.

Proof.

Part (i) is just the Nerón–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion.

Suppose
E
have semistability defect
e=2
. By Theorem
3.4
, it follows that there exists a quadratic extension
F(\sqrt{d})
such that the quadratic twist
E_{d}
of
E
by
d
has good reduction. Let
\psi_{d}
be the quadratic character of
W_{F}
associated with
F(\sqrt{d})
.
Since
E_{d}
has good reduction, we have
1=\tau_{E_{d}}=\tau_{E}\otimes\psi_{d}|_{I_{F}}
, and twisting again by
\psi_{d}|_{I_{F}}
gives
\tau_{E}=1\otimes\psi_{d}|_{I_{F}}=\psi_{d}|_{I_{F}}\oplus\psi_{d}|_{I_{F}}
so the inertial type is a principal series and
v(N_{E})=2m(\psi_{d}|_{I_{F}})
. Finally, for
p\geq 3
, necessarily
\psi_{d}|_{I_{F}}=\varepsilon_{1}
and
m(\varepsilon_{1})=1
. In
(iii)
, the possible quadratic characters are the
\varepsilon_{i}
, and the proposition follows because
m(\varepsilon_{i})=2
for
i\in\{1,3,5\}
and
m(\varepsilon_{i})=3
for
i\in\{7,9,11,13\}
.
∎

Recall that
\tau_{St}
denotes the equivalence class
[St(1),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)]
and we will denote by
\varepsilon_{i}\otimes\tau_{St}
the equivalent class
[St(\varepsilon_{i}),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)]
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve
v(j_{E})<0
, that is, with potentially multiplicative reduction. Then,

(i)

If
E
has multiplicative reduction, then
N_{E}=\mathfrak{p}
and

\tau_{E}=\tau_{\operatorname{St}}
.

(ii)

If
p\geq 3
and
E
has additive potentially multiplicative reduction, then

N_{E}=\mathfrak{p}^{2}
and
\tau_{E}=\varepsilon_{1}\otimes\tau_{\operatorname{St}}
.

(iii)

If
F=\mathbb{Q}_{4}
and
E
has additive potentially multiplicative reduction, then
either

a)

N_{E}=2^{4}
and
\tau_{E}=\varepsilon_{i}\otimes\tau_{\operatorname{St}}
for
i\in\{1,3,5\}
, or

b)

N_{E}=2^{6}
and
\tau_{E}=\varepsilon_{i}\otimes\tau_{\operatorname{St}}
for

i\in\{7,9,11,13\}
.

Proof.

Since
E
has potentially multiplicative reduction, the image of
\rho_{E}(I_{K})
is infinite, and so

\tau_{E}=\operatorname{St(\theta)}|_{I_{K}}
for some character
\theta
of
W_{F}
at most quadratic. If
E
has multiplicative reduction, then
\operatorname{Cond}(\tau_{E})=N_{E}=\mathfrak{p}
, so
m(\tau_{E})=1
and, by (
2.14
), it follows that
\theta
is unramified and
\tau_{E}=\operatorname{St}(1)=\tau_{\operatorname{St}}
.
If
E
has additive reduction, then
v(N_{E})\geq 2
so
m(\theta)\geq 1
and
\theta|_{I_{F}}
is quadratic character, yielding the result.
∎

4.
Inertial types for
p\geq 5

Recall that
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a finite extension and assume
p\geq 5
for this section.
We will classify the inertial types
\tau_{E}
of elliptic curves
E/F
.
The cases of potentially multiplicative reduction and potentially
good reduction with
e\leq 2
were treated in the previous section, therefore
it remains to study the case of curves with potentially good reduction and

e\in\{3,4,6\}
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
generate
(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p})^{\times}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve with potentially good reduction and semistability defect
e\geq 3
.
We have

\tau_{E}=\begin{cases}\tau_{ps}(1,1,e)\quad\text{if}\quad e|(q-1);\\
\tau_{sc}(u,1,e)\quad\text{if}\quad e|(q+1),\end{cases}

where
\tau_{ps}
and
\tau_{sc}
are given by Definition
2.17
.

Proof.

Since
E
has additive potentially good reduction, we have
N_{E}=\mathfrak{p}^{2}
.
The assumption
e\geq 3
and Theorem
2.19
give that
\Phi
is cyclic of
order
e=3,4,6
. Thus
\tau_{E}
has finite image and is reducible so, by the cases in §
2.5
, it follows that
\tau_{E}

is either a principal series or a nonexceptional supercuspidal induced
from the unramified quadratic extension
K_{3}=F(\sqrt{u})
.

Assume first that
\tau_{E}
is a principal series. Then
\tau_{E}=\theta|_{I_{F}}\oplus(\theta|_{I_{F}})^{-1}
with
\theta
a character of
W_{F}
. We
set
\chi:=\theta|_{I_{F}}
. Since
m(\tau_{E})=2
, by equation (
2.13
), we
have
m(\chi)=1
, and
\tau_{E}
having order
e
implies that
\chi
has also
order
e
. Since
m(\chi)=1
,
\chi^{A}
factors through the quotient
(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p})^{\times}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
thus
e|(q-1)
. Since
u
generates
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
then
\chi^{A}(u)
determines completely the character
\chi
. If

\chi^{A}
has order
e
then
\chi(u)=\zeta_{e}^{c}
where
\text{gcd}(c,e)=1
and, since

e=3,4,6
, the only options are
c\equiv\pm 1\pmod{e}
. Both
c=1
and

c=-1
give us equivalent representations which are

\tau_{E}=\tau_{ps}(1,1,e)
.

Assume now that
\tau_{E}
is induced from a character

\theta:W_{K_{3}}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
. From
m(\tau_{E})=2
and (
2.15
), we have
m(\chi)=1
.
Thus
\chi^{A}
factors through
(\mathcal{O}_{K_{3}}/\pi\mathcal{O}_{K_{3}})^{\times}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
, where
\pi
is the uniformizer of
F
.
By Lemma
2.5
, the character
\chi^{A}
coincides
with
\psi_{K_{3}}
on
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
; since
K_{3}/F
is unramified, the character
\chi^{A}
has to be trivial
on
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
, and so it has to be trivial on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
.
Let
w\in\mathcal{O}_{K_{3}}
generate
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
.
Then
w^{q+1}
generates
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.
This implies that

\chi^{A}(w)^{q+1}=1
, and since the order of
\chi^{A}
is equal to the order of

\chi^{A}(w)
it follows that
e|q+1
. Finally, to show that

\tau_{E}=\tau_{sc}(u,1,e)
we reason as before:
\chi^{A}(w)=\zeta_{e}^{c}
with

\text{gcd}(e,c)=1
with
c\equiv\pm 1\pmod{e}
and again both
choices give equivalent inertial types isomorphic to
\tau_{sc}(u,1,e)
; here we know these types are
indeed supercuspidal (i.e. restriction of irreducble induction) by Corollary
2.6
.

∎

By looking at
p
modulo 12 and the degree
n=[\mathbb{F}_{q}:\mathbb{F}_{p}]
of the residual field of
F
, we have the following consequence.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve with semistability defect
e\in\{3,4,6\}
.

1) If
n
is even, then
\tau_{E}
is either
\tau_{ps}(1,1,4),\tau_{ps}(1,1,3)
or
\tau_{ps}(1,1,6)
.

2) If
n
is odd, then we have the following cases:

•

If
p\equiv 1\pmod{12}
, then
\tau_{E}
is either

\tau_{ps}(1,1,4),\tau_{ps}(1,1,3)
or
\tau_{ps}(1,1,6)
;

•

If
p\equiv 5\pmod{12}
, then
\tau_{E}
is either

\tau_{ps}(1,1,4),\tau_{sc}(u,1,3)
or
\tau_{sc}(u,1,6)
;

•

If
p\equiv 7\pmod{12}
, then
\tau_{E}
is either

\tau_{sc}(u,1,4),\tau_{ps}(1,1,3)
or
\tau_{ps}(1,1,6)
;

•

If
p\equiv 11\pmod{12}
, then
\tau_{E}
is either

\tau_{sc}(u,1,4),\tau_{sc}(u,1,3)
or
\tau_{sc}(u,1,6)
.

Proof.

Since
p\geq 5
, we have
p\equiv\pm 1\pmod{6}
and
p\equiv\pm 1\pmod{4}
.

If
n
is odd, then
q=p^{n}\equiv p\pmod{6}
and
q=p^{n}\equiv p\pmod{4}
. By the Chinese remainder theorem,
q\equiv p\pmod{12}
. If
n
is even then
p^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{12}
. In both cases, the result follows by Proposition
4.1
.
∎

This corollary together with Propositions
3.7
and
3.8
provide a complete classification of the inertial types of
elliptic curves
E
defined over any finite extension
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for
p\geq 5
.

Example 4.3
.

Let
\pi
be a uniformizer of
F
. A quick application of
[
15
, §1, Proposition 1]
shows that the following curves have the following inertial types.

(1)

The curve
y^{2}=x^{3}+\pi^{2}x
has inertial type
\tau_{ps}(1,1,2)
;

(2)

The curve
y^{2}=x^{3}+\pi^{2}
has inertial type
\tau_{ps}(1,1,3)
or
\tau_{sc}(u,1,3)
;

(3)

The curve
y^{2}=x^{3}+\pi x
has inertial type
\tau_{ps}(1,1,4)
or
\tau_{sc}(u,1,4)
;

(4)

The curve
y^{2}=x^{3}+\pi
has inertial type
\tau_{ps}(1,1,6)
or
\tau_{sc}(u,1,6)
,

where in (2)–(4), the type is principal series or supercuspidal depending on
p
and
n
as prescribed by Corollary
4.2
.

5.1.
Principal series over

The cases of curves

We have the following group isomorphisms

This can be easily check by direct computation, see

.

Let

\tau_{E}

\tau_{E}=\tau_{ps}(1,1,4)

(ii)

N_{E}=3^{4}

(iii)

where the explicit description of the inertial types are in Tables

Proof.

Part (iii) follows from (ii), Proposition

and Remark

Recall that, since

has trivial determinant, it is determined by a single character of inertia.
Let

. Since

,

The results in previous sections give a list of possible inertial types arising
on elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
.
Thanks to the explicit description we
gave of the inertial types, given an elliptic curve,
we can use local class field theory to determine to which of the types in our
list it corresponds to. This way,
in this section, we will
provide explicit equations of elliptic curves having each of the
types previously computed.

Given an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}_{4}
we determine
\tau_{E}
as follows.
If
E
has potentially multiplicative reduction, then
either
E
has multiplicative reduction and hence
\tau_{E}=\tau_{st}

or
E
acquires multipliticative reduction a quadratic
extensions
K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
and

\tau_{E}=\tau_{st}\otimes\varepsilon_{i}
(see Proposition
3.8
).
In case of
E
having potentially good
reduction, we compute
L:=\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}(E[m])
for
m=3
if
p\neq 3
or
m=4
if
p=3
; by Theorem
2.18
, the curve
E/L^{un}
has good
reduction, hence
E/L
also has good reduction. Therefore,
\tau_{E}|_{I_{L}}=1
and so the character
\chi
determining
\tau_{E}
is trivial on
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{F}/L)
. By local class field theory,
the kernel of
\chi^{A}
must contain the norms from
L

and, by checking which elements of the groups
(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{f})^{\times}/U_{f}
are
the class of norms from
L
, we can find which
\chi
(hence
\tau_{E}
)
corresponds to
E
. Moreover,
using the curves in
[
11
]
, we also
determine which types of
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
are base change from
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

The method just described allows to determinte the kernel of an inertial type
\tau_{E}
,
and the following theorem ensures that it is enough
to single out a unique inertial type of our list; we note that the the following result
holds not just over
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
but over any
F
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
be inertial types defined over
F
with trivial determinant and image isomorphic to one of the groups in Theorem
2.19
. Let
L_{i}
be the fixed field of
\text{ker}\>\tau_{i}
then
L_{1}=L_{2}\iff\tau_{1}\simeq\tau_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
L_{i}
is the fixed field of

\tau_{i}
it is clear that
\tau_{1}\simeq\tau_{2}\implies L_{1}=L_{2}
.
For the converse, let
L^{\prime}=L_{1}=L_{2}
.

Suppse first that
L^{\prime}/F^{un}
is abelian.
Then,
\tau_{i}=\chi_{i}\oplus\chi_{i}^{-1}
where
\chi_{i}
is a character of
I_{F}

if
\tau_{i}
is a principal series or
\chi_{i}
is a character of
I_{K}=I_{F}
if
\tau_{i}
is unramified supercuspidal, where
K/F
is the quadratic unramified extension.
Since
L_{i}
is the fixed field
of
\tau_{i}
, the fact that
L_{1}=L_{2}
implies that

\operatorname{Ker}(\chi_{1})=\operatorname{Ker}(\chi_{2})
, and so both
\chi_{i}

factor through
I_{F}/\operatorname{Ker}(\chi_{i})\simeq\operatorname{Im}(\chi_{i})\simeq C_{e}
and have image
C_{e}
; so they are injective characters of

\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/F^{un})=C_{e}
with
e\in\{2,3,4,6\}
. Thus the
image of a generator
g
must be
\chi_{i}(g)=\zeta_{e}^{\pm 1}
and it follows that

\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}
or
\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}^{-1}=\chi_{2}^{s}
giving

\tau_{1}\simeq\tau_{2}
in both cases.

Suppose now that
L^{\prime}/F^{un}
is non-abelian and
p=3
. Then

\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/F^{un})=\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
and

\tau_{i}=\operatorname{Ind}_{I_{K}}^{I_{F}}(\chi_{i})
for
\chi_{i}
characters of

I_{K}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K^{un})
, where
K/F
is quadratic ramified.
The fixed field
of
\operatorname{Ker}(\tau_{i})
is equal to the fixed field of

\operatorname{Ker}(\chi_{i})
. Since
K^{un}
is the
unique quadratic ramified extension of
F^{un}
,
it follows that

\chi_{i}
are injective characters of
\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/K^{un})\simeq C_{6}

and, by the same argument as before,
\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}
or
\chi_{2}^{s}
thus

\tau_{1}\simeq\tau_{2}
.

Finally, assume that
L^{\prime}/F^{un}
is non-abelian and
p=2
. Then

\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/F^{un})=Q_{8}
or
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
.

Suppose first that
\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/F^{un})=Q_{8}
. Then the types
\tau_{i}
are irreducible representations of

\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/F^{un})\simeq Q_{8}
but there is just one equivalence class of irreducible 2-dimensional representations of
Q_{8}
, hence
\tau_{1}\simeq\tau_{2}
.

Suppose now
\operatorname{Gal}(L^{\prime}/F^{un})\simeq\operatorname{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
.
There is a unique normal subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
with index
3
, and it is isomorphic to
Q_{8}
. Let
M
be the cubic extension of
F^{un}

determine by this subgroup. Then both
\tau_{i}|_{I_{M}}
have image
Q_{8}

and cut the same field; we conclude

\tau_{1}|_{I_{M}}\simeq\tau_{2}|_{I_{M}}
by the previous case.
Since
\tau_{i}
are irreducible
representations of
I_{F}
that agree on
I_{M}
with
I_{F}/I_{M}\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(M/F^{un})

cylic and cubic, it follows that
\tau_{1}\simeq\tau_{2}\otimes\eta_{M}
where

\eta_{M}
is a character of
\operatorname{Gal}(M/F^{un})
. Since
\tau_{i}
have trivial
determinant, taking determinants to the previous isomorphism implies

\eta_{M}=1
, as desired.
∎

Recall that, for any representation
\rho

W_{F}
and a finite extension

we defined
\rho_{K}:=\rho|_{W_{K}}

\tau

\tau_{K}:=\rho_{K}|_{I_{K}}

In light of Proposition

such that

\tau_{K}
of

to
K
is nonexceptional supercuspidal. So, a
natural strategy to find the exceptional types is to find all the
nonexceptional supercuspidal types for each cubic

and then check which of them descend to an exceptional type over
\mathbb{Q}_{4}
.
This brute force approach turns out to be impractical because there are too many cases.
Instead, we will first show that exceptional
inertial types give rise to a tower of fields with certain structure, then
find all such towers to get the candidate types, and finally find elliptic
curves realizing each tower and type.
We will need a couple of auxiliary results.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{4}
be an elliptic curve with exceptional inertial type

(i)

\tau_{E}(I_{4})=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
and there exists a cubic ramified Galois extension
K/\mathbb{Q}_{4}
such that the base change
\tau_{K}
is triply imprimitive;

(ii)

\tau_{E}(I_{4})=Q_{8}
and the base change
\tau_{K}
to the cubic unramified extension
K/\mathbb{Q}_{4}
is triply imprimitive.

Proof.

Since
\mu_{3}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{4}
and
\tau_{E}
is exceptional, we are in case
(a)(ii)
or
(a)(iii)
of Theorem
6.4
. In both cases (with the same notation as in Theorem
6.4
), we have
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}_{4})\simeq\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
. Note that
Q_{8}
is the unique subgroup of
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
of index
3
, and let
K
be the fixed field of
Q_{8}
.
In case
(ii)
, the inertia subgroup of
L/\mathbb{Q}_{4}
corresponds to
Q_{8}
,
and so
K/\mathbb{Q}_{4}
is the cubic unramified extension and, in case
(iii)

is ramified. In both cases, after base change to

\tau_{K}(I_{K})=\rho_{K}(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K))=Q_{8}

\tau_{K}
is triply imprimitive.

We introduce a final piece of notation.

Definition 8.2

.

Let

, the group

.

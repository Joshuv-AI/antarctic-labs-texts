Axitinib-Pembrolizumab and Adverse Event Management in Patients With Advanced Renal Cell Carcinoma
Authors: Neil J. Shah, Yaa Ababio, Anthony Eccleston, Dharanija Rao, Scott P. Kelly, Jane Chang, Sarah Lucht, Caleb Paydar, Bryce A. Van Doren, Emily Bland, William John, Sarah Giegerich, JaLyna Laney, Bruce Feinberg, Kevin K. Zarrabi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Key PointsQuestionHow do community oncologists approach and manage adverse events (AEs) associated with first-line axitinib plus pembrolizumab therapy for advanced renal cell carcinoma (aRCC)?FindingsIn this cohort study of 300 patients with aRCC who received axitinib plus pembrolizumab and cross-sectional survey of 25 physicians, few patients required treatment dose reductions or discontinued treatment due to AEs, and physicians prioritized efficacy followed by safety when selecting first-line treatment.MeaningThese findings suggest that treatment-related AEs for first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab therapy are manageable and that experienced community physicians are familiar with AE management, although further research of the effects of AE management actions on clinical outcomes is needed.

Introduction

Cancer of the kidney and renal pelvis is the seventh most common malignant neoplasm in the US, with 80 980 new cases and 14 510 deaths estimated for 2025.1 Approximately 15% of patients diagnosed with renal cell carcinoma (RCC) present with advanced disease (ie, stage IV) at diagnosis, which is associated with a lower 5-year survival rate (19.1%) compared with localized (93.3%) or regional (76.4%) disease.1 Within RCC, clear cell carcinoma is the most common histologic subtype, comprising 70% to 80% of diagnoses, and is associated with worse prognosis than other histologic findings (eg, papillary RCC, chromophobe RCC).2,3

The introduction of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) (eg, axitinib, lenvatinib) in the mid-2000s to 2010s for treatment of advanced RCC (aRCC) significantly improved patient outcomes, including longer progression-free survival (PFS) and more manageable safety profiles.4,5,6 In the last decade, introduction of immune checkpoint inhibitor therapies as dual immuno-oncology (IO) therapy (eg, nivolumab plus ipilimumab) or in combination with TKIs (eg, axitinib plus pembrolizumab) has further improved outcomes for patients with aRCC.4,7,8,9,10 Current National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines recommend combination therapy (axitinib-pembrolizumab, nivolumab plus cabozantinib, pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib, or nivolumab plus ipilimumab) as first-line treatment for aRCC irrespective of patient International Metastatic RCC Database Consortium (IMDC) risk scores.11

The combination treatment of axitinib plus pembrolizumab was approved in April 2019 for first-line use in patients with aRCC based on the phase 3 KEYNOTE-426 trial.10 In the original KEYNOTE-426 trial as well as its extended follow-up, first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab treatment showed clinical benefits in both median overall survival (OS) and PFS compared with single-agent sunitinib.10,12,13 Nevertheless, treatment-related AEs led to interruption or discontinuation of axitinib in 62% and 20% of patients, respectively; interruption or discontinuation of pembrolizumab in 44% and 21% of patients, respectively; and interruption or discontinuation of both drugs in 30% and 7% of patients, respectively.10,12 Prior clinical studies among patients with aRCC receiving first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab have also observed that treatment-related AEs were the most common reason behind dose reductions and interruptions.14,15,16 Many patients in these studies initiated treatment with first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab prior to 2022. With the approval of both nivolumab plus cabozantinib and pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib in 2021, physicians are expected to have gained significant experience since 2022 in managing the AEs of IO-TKI combinations. Updated clinical data on therapy management for axitinib-pembrolizumab is needed to provide insights into current rates of AE-related treatment modification.

Overlapping toxic effects between IO and TKIs have historically posed challenges to AE management,17 underscoring the importance of accurately identifying the likely causative agent to ensure effective treatment. Prior studies on clinical therapy management for patients with aRCC treated with first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab are limited and have not focused on understanding physician decision-making during AE management.14,15,18,19,20 With more than 6 years since the approval of the first IO-TKI combination regimen for aRCC, it remains unclear how the safety profile of IO-TKI combinations plays a role in physician treatment selection and which factors physicians consider when managing toxic effects. Understanding physician perspectives and therapy management practices is important for identifying areas where additional management resources may be needed. To address these evidentiary gaps, this study described therapy management, including for AEs, among patients with aRCC treated with first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab as well as the perspectives of oncologists on the safety profile of axitinib-pembrolizumab and their preferred methods for managing AEs.

Methods

This multisite cohort study in the included a cross-sectional electronic survey among physicians in the US and a retrospective physician-abstracted review of medical records. Participating physicians were recruited from the Cardinal Health Oncology Provider Extended Network, a group of more than 350 oncologists and hematologists who actively participate in research.21 Physician recruitment was conducted in a phased approach, with invitations sent first to physicians who expressed interest via a feasibility assessment and then to the broader network. Data collection occurred between July 31 and August 22, 2024. Prior to data collection, the study protocol and case report form (CRF) were approved by the WCG Institutional Review Board (Western Institutional Review Board–Copernicus Group), which also granted a waiver of informed consent owing to the use of retrospective data with minimal protected health information. This study followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline.

Physician Eligibility

Participating physicians were required to have treated at least 5 patients with aRCC in the year prior to study participation. Prior to completing abstraction of patient medical records, physicians completed a survey on their practice characteristics and approach to AE management for patients with aRCC receiving first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab.

Patient Eligibility

Eligible patients had a confirmed diagnosis of clear cell aRCC (stage IV); initiated first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab for aRCC between April 22, 2019, and January 31, 2024; were at least 18 years of age at the time of first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab initiation; and had a minimum of 6 months of follow-up after initiation (eFigure in Supplement 1). Patients who died during the follow-up period were eligible for study inclusion. Patients were ineligible if they had received axitinib or pembrolizumab for aRCC as part of a clinical trial, had an additional active malignant neoplasm in the 3 years prior to first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab initiation, or received systemic therapy prior to first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab initiation, including in an adjuvant setting. As the study was not hypothesis driven, sample size (N = 300) was determined by feasibility estimates from Cardinal Health Oncology Provider Extended Network.

Medical Record Abstraction

Physicians were instructed to abstract patient medical records in chronological order, starting with the first eligible patient. Physicians abstracted deidentified patient data into an electronic CRF (eCRF). Each participating physician could enter data for a maximum of 15 eligible patients. Patient data on demographic and clinical characteristics, treatment patterns, and AEs were abstracted into the eCRF. Patient race (categorized as Asian, Black or African American, White, and other [including American Indian or Alaska Native and Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander]) and ethnicity (categorized as Hispanic or Latino), AE attribution to a specific drug, and AE grade were determined by physician assessment. Race and ethnicity data were collected because of previously published disparities in racial and ethnic representation between clinical practice and trial participation for aRCC.22 Physicians could not skip questions within the eCRF but indicated missing data via selection of “unknown” as a response option.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed from September 18 to December 20, 2024. Patient characteristics, treatment patterns, and AEs were summarized using descriptive statistics. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate time-to-event outcomes (clinical duration of therapy, and PFS); 12-month point estimates used the Kaplan-Meier curves for time-to-event outcomes. Clinical duration of therapy was defined as the time from first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab initiation to the last occurring discontinuation of axitinib-pembrolizumab for any reason (event) or the last day of communication for patients still receiving therapy (censored). Clinical PFS was defined as the time from first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab initiation to disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first (events) or last date of encounter (censored). Physician survey responses were summarized using descriptive statistics. No clinician-level comparisons were made between physician survey AE management questions and AE management actions reported in the review of patient medical records. All analyses were performed using SAS, version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc). No statistical comparisons were made in this study.

Results

Overall, 25 physicians participated in the physician survey (mean [SD] time in practice, 14.6 [7.3] years). Data were abstracted for 300 patients with aRCC who initiated first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab therapy during the study period.

Patient Demographic and Clinical Characteristics

Of the 300 patients included in the analysis, 256 (85.3%) were treated in community settings. With all US Census regions represented, 183 patients (61.0%) were male and 117 (39.0%) were female. In terms of race, 20 patients (6.7%) were Asian, 68 (22.7%) were Black, 208 (69.3%) were White, and 4 (1.3%) were of other race; 23 patients (7.7%) were of Hispanic or Latino ethnicity (Table 1 and eTable 1 in Supplement 1). The mean (SD) age at aRCC diagnosis was 66.0 (9.3) years. Most of the patients were initially diagnosed with stage IV RCC (210 [70.0%]) and had grades 1 to 2 (128 [42.7%]) or grade 3 (101 [33.7%]) tumors at aRCC diagnosis. Of the 296 patients with known sarcomatoid status, 34 (11.5%) had sarcomatoid features. The most common sites of metastasis prior to first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab initiation were the lungs (193 [64.3%]), bone (103 [34.3%]), and liver (98 [32.7%]). At first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab initiation, 65 patients (21.7%) had poor IMDC risk scores, 178 (59.3%) had intermediate IMDC risk scores, and 54 (18.0%) had favorable IMDC risk scores. Most patients (235 [78.3%]) had Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status scores of 0 or 1 at first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab initiation and a median National Cancer Institute Comorbidity Index score of 0.1 (IQR, 0-0.5).

Patient Treatment Patterns

The median follow-up time after first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab initiation was 12.3 (IQR, 8.1-21.6) months, and 182 patients (60.7%) initiated axitinib-pembrolizumab treatment in 2023 or 2024 (Table 2 and eTable 2 in Supplement 1). Most patients initiated first-line treatment at an oral axitinib dose of 5 mg twice daily (285 [95.0%]). Overall, 230 patients (76.7%) reported no reductions or interruptions while receiving first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab therapy. Dose reductions of axitinib were reported for 43 patients (14.3%), and 43 of 50 reductions (86.0%) were attributed to AEs. Dose interruptions of axitinib were reported for 41 patients (13.7%); dose interruptions of pembrolizumab, for 18 patients (6.0%). At last follow-up, 134 patients (44.7%) had discontinued both axitinib and pembrolizumab, with disease progression as the most common reason (111 of 141 [78.7%] and 110 of 146 [75.3%], respectively). Among these patients, 11 of 141 (7.8%) discontinued axitinib and 14 of 146 (9.6%) discontinued pembrolizumab due to AEs. Overall, 242 patients (80.7%) reported no AE-related dose reductions, interruptions, or discontinuations.

Patient Clinical Outcomes

At last follow-up, 238 patients (79.3%) were alive; most living patients (224 [94.1%]) were receiving active therapy (eTable 3 in Supplement 1). Among the 62 patients who had died, disease progression was the most common cause of death (47 [75.8%]). One patient death was attributable to a treatment-related AE. Progression was reported for 114 patients (38.0%) while receiving first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab therapy; the Kaplan-Meier 12-month point estimate for clinical PFS after first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab initiation was 0.74 (95% CI, 0.68-0.79).

Physician Characteristics and Perspectives on AE Management

Twenty-five physicians completed the physician survey. Of these, 22 (88.0%) practiced in community settings; 16 (64.0%) practiced in urban areas and (8 (32.0%) practiced in suburban areas. All US regions were represented (Table 3 and eTable 4 in Supplement 1). Physicians were all medical oncologists (mean [SD] time in practice, 14.6 [7.3] years) and had treated a median of 25 (IQR, 18-35) patients with aRCC in the 12 months prior to data collection.

Sixteen physicians (64.0%) had access to multispecialty consultation at their practice for AE management. The most important factor physicians identified as influencing their choice of first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab treatment was OS (19 [76.0%]) (Figure 1), followed by overall response rate (3 [12.0%]) and PFS (2 [8.0%]). Six physicians (24.0%) selected safety profile as one of the top 3 factors. The 2 most common reasons for considering first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab modification or discontinuation were disease progression and AEs (both 21 [84.0%]). When managing patients with AEs due to first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab therapy, responding physicians were most concerned about hepatotoxicity (14 of 21 [66.7%]), diarrhea (12 of 21 [57.1%]), and fatigue and/or asthenia (10 of 21 [47.6%]). Physicians considered a broad range of factors when deciding whether to modify vs discontinue treatment in response to an AE, including patient comorbidities (16 of 21 [76.2%]), patient performance status (15 of 21 [71.4%]), probability AE will fully resolve (14 of 21 [66.7%]), and AE type (14 of 21 [66.7%]).

When asked about typical actions they would take for specific AEs by grade (1 and 2 vs 3 and 4) experienced during axitinib-pembrolizumab therapy, physicians indicated they typically used more therapy management for higher-grade AEs as well as certain AEs (eg, hepatoxicity) (Figure 2). Across all queried hypothetical AEs and grades, physicians indicated using treatment interruptions for axitinib and pembrolizumab more frequently than treatment discontinuation. Nevertheless, interruptions in axitinib treatment (vs pembrolizumab) were more frequently selected as typical management, although clinicians interrupted axitinib and pembrolizumab at similar frequencies for hepatotoxicity, diarrhea, and hypothyroidism. Physicians indicated sometimes discontinuing both axitinib and pembrolizumab for grades 3 to 4 hepatotoxicity, rash, diarrhea, stomatitis, fatigue, and dysphonia, but only axitinib discontinuation was typically considered for grades 3 to 4 decreased appetite, palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia, hypertension, nausea, and hypothyroidism.

Discussion

In this study that included a review of medical records and a physician survey in a clinical setting, few patients with aRCC discontinued first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab therapy due to AEs, although AEs remained the most common reason for treatment interruption and reduction. The survey results suggested that physicians prioritize clinical efficacy followed by safety profile when selecting first-line treatments and that they weigh patient characteristics and AE-specific factors (type, grade, or axitinib- or pembrolizumab-related) when considering appropriate therapy modification options.

As expected, based on the KEYNOTE-426 findings10 and prior clinical studies14,15,16 for first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab treatment in aRCC, the present study found that AEs were the most common reason for axitinib reductions and interruptions as well as pembrolizumab interruptions. Nevertheless, the observed rates of therapy management actions taken in response to AEs were lower in the present study than what has been observed in prior clinical studies14,15,16 and in the KEYNOTE-426 study (eg, AE-related axitinib interruption or discontinuation in 62% and 20% of patients, respectively).10 These differences may be due to more effective AE management resulting from gained experience by physicians as well as the broader care team (eg, supportive care, improved patient education23) since the initial approval of IO-TKI regimens, as physicians who took part in this study regularly treat patients with aRCC. Lower observed rates of AE-related therapy management may also reflect the relatively short median follow-up for this study and not capture late onset of AEs. Overall, these results suggest that appropriate and effective therapy management practices—including but not limited to dose reductions, interruptions, and discontinuations, as well as treatment by an experienced care team—may have contributed to the positive outcomes observed for patients receiving first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab treatment.

Physician survey results supported that treating oncologists prioritized efficacy outcomes (eg, OS) alongside patient quality of life and safety outcomes when choosing first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab treatment. These selections align with the results observed in clinical trials for IO-TKI therapies, where clinical efficacy was shown alongside high frequencies of AEs of grade 3 or higher.10,24 The AEs physicians were most concerned about when treating patients with axitinib-pembrolizumab (eg, hepatotoxicity, diarrhea, hypertension) mirrored the most frequent AEs of grade 3 or higher observed in the KEYNOTE-426 findings, supporting physicians’ experience using these combination therapies.10 With 6 years since the approval of first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab for aRCC and the subsequent experience gained using it as well as other IO-TKI therapies, this recent-era study observed that physicians prioritized both long-term efficacy and consistent safety when selecting first-line treatment and weighed patient-level factors when making AE-related management decisions during axitinib-pembrolizumab therapy.

When surveyed on typical therapy management actions taken after AE occurrences, oncologists reported higher levels of therapy management for grades 3 and 4 vs grades 1 and 2 AEs, which is anticipated and aligns with guidelines and label recommendations.25,26,27 For most grades 3 and 4 AEs, axitinib (vs pembrolizumab) interruptions were more frequently reported as typical management, although clinicians reported typically interrupting axitinib and pembrolizumab therapy at similar frequencies for hepatotoxicity, diarrhea, and hypothyroidism. Higher rates of axitinib interruption align with axitinib’s shorter half-life compared with pembrolizumab, whereby treatment holds can help physicians identify likely causal agents.28,29 The higher level of hypothetical axitinib-related modifications reported in the physician survey aligns with the observed patient-level data for this study, wherein axitinib (vs pembrolizumab) interruptions due to AEs were more common (34 vs 19 total). Both parts of this study broadly support that oncologists are making efforts to attribute likely AE causality and corresponding dose modifications to individual drugs rather than broadly applying changes to the entire combination, although overlapping toxic effects between IO and TKI therapies make this challenging.30 With further advancements in the aRCC treatment space, including triplet combination therapies (eg, nivolumab plus ipilimumab plus cabozantinib),31 continuing physician education and access to other specialists for managing AEs will remain important for achieving optimal patient outcomes.

Results from the patient study support safe and effective use of first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab among patients predominantly treated in US community practices, a treatment setting included in few prior clinical studies on AE management for first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab therapy but where most patients with cancer receive care.14 Patient demographic and clinical characteristics were broadly consistent with prior clinical studies, with several distinguishing features. We observed high rates of completeness in the present study, including for race (no missingness), IMDC risk score (1.0% missingness) and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (no missingness). While most of the study population was White, 22.7% of patients were Black or African American, a demographic group that has been underrepresented in prior clinical trials for aRCC22,32 and similar clinical studies (eg, at proportions of 4.1%,33 4.8%,34 and 5.6%14). Additionally, the majority of patients in the present study initiated treatment in 2023 and 2024, offering insights into more recent treatment patterns than data in other clinical evidence studies.14,33,34 With the rapid treatment advances in aRCC and correspondingly steep learning curve for oncologists, further studies using recent patient data are needed to confirm whether gained experience and improvements in AE management have reduced the number of patients needing to discontinue IO-TKI combination treatments due to AEs.

Strengths and Limitations

A strength of this study is that it presents physician-abstracted data from a review of patient medical records that provide recent-era insights into clinical treatment patterns and AE management for patients with aRCC receiving first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab treatment in a predominantly community setting, including difficult-to-obtain variables such as reasons for treatment modification and discontinuation. Additionally, the study offers novel complementary insights into how oncologists perceive AE management during first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab treatment after 6 years of regulatory approval and standard of care utilization.

This study also has some limitations. The findings are representative only of the oncologists who chose to participate in the research, which may not be representative of the broader population of oncologists who treat aRCC. It is possible the physicians who chose to participate were more experienced in treating aRCC and/or in using first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab, and the relatively low level of AE-related treatment modifications in the present study may reflect this participation bias. While physicians were instructed to abstract patient records in chronological order starting with the first eligible patient, study members did not have direct access to the records to confirm. Physicians were allowed to abstract data for a maximum of 15 patients to prevent overrepresentation of a single physician’s patient population in the overall study sample, but selection bias is possible if patients included differed from those not abstracted. It is possible that physician survey responses may have been influenced by social desirability bias. The low rates of AEs and AE-related discontinuation observed in this study may be due to insufficient follow-up or underdocumentation of low-grade AEs; to mitigate this, we required patients to have at least 6 months of follow-up after first-line therapy initiation. This study was purely descriptive, and these results do not evaluate the effect of AE management strategies on clinical outcomes.

Conclusions

In this retrospective cohort study and cross-sectional physician survey on first-line axitinib-pembrolizumab for aRCC, few patients permanently discontinued treatment due to AEs, with physician experience potentially contributing to low rates of AE-related treatment discontinuation. Clinicians considered treatment efficacy, safety, and patient factors when making AE-related management decisions, and future work is warranted to evaluate the effects of AE management on clinical outcomes.

The generalized anxiety disorder-7 (GAD-7) and its individual- and contextual-level correlates in Chinese adults
Authors: Hien Thi Tran, Guangdong Zhou, Tam Thi Ngo, Long Hoang Nguyen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAnxiety disorders contribute substantially to the global mental health burden, yet evidence integrating psychometric validation of screening tools with individual- and contextual-level correlates of anxiety in large Chinese populations remains limited. This study examined the psychometric properties of the Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale (GAD-7) and explored individual- and contextual-level correlates of anxiety.MethodsA nationwide online survey was conducted among 7,730 adults in China. Anxiety symptoms were assessed using the GAD-7. Reliability was evaluated using Cronbach’s alpha and McDonald’s omega. Factorial validity and measurement invariance across gender, age group, occupation, and geographic region were examined using confirmatory factor analysis. Anxiety outcomes included continuous GAD-7 scores, ordinal anxiety severity, and binary GAD-7 screening status (GAD-7 ≥5 vs. <5). Multiple linear, ordinal logistic, and binary logistic regression models were used to examine associations between anxiety outcomes and individual- and contextual-level factors.ResultsThe mean GAD-7 score was 6.66 ± 4.78, and 8.0% of participants reported severe anxiety. The GAD-7 demonstrated excellent internal consistency (α = 0.91; ω = 0.91) and a robust one-factor structure, with full measurement invariance across the examined sociodemographic groups. Female gender, younger and middle adulthood (21–50 years), selected occupational groups, and higher PHQ-9 scores were consistently associated with higher anxiety across models. Regional differences were limited. Among the contextual indicators, higher employment rates were associated with higher anxiety levels, whereas higher GDP per capita and population density were associated with lower anxiety across some outcomes.ConclusionsThe findings provide further evidence supporting the reliability, validity, and measurement invariance of the GAD-7 in a large online sample of Chinese adults. Anxiety outcomes were more consistently associated with individual-level characteristics than with the provincial-level contextual indicators examined.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-05217-7.Keywords: Mental health, Anxiety, GAD-7, Psychometric validation, Correlates, Adults, China

Introduction

Anxiety disorders represent a major group of mental health conditions worldwide, characterized by persistent fear, worry, and functional impairment [1]. Given their high prevalence and increasing burden, there is a critical need for reliable and scalable tools to assess anxiety symptoms across large and diverse populations. The Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale (GAD-7) has emerged as a widely used, brief, and psychometrically robust instrument for screening and grading anxiety severity in both clinical and community-based research [2–4].

The GAD-7 has been extensively validated across multiple countries and populations, demonstrating high internal consistency, strong factorial validity, and good diagnostic accuracy [5–15]. In China, prior validation studies have largely focused on specific adult or clinical populations, including general hospital outpatients, university students, and medical trainees [2, 16–18]. Additional evidence is available for special populations such as individuals with epilepsy, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and pregnant women [16, 18–20]. Furthermore, validation studies have been conducted in non-mainland Chinese or cross-cultural contexts, including Taiwanese and Hong Kong populations, as well as non-Chinese groups using translated versions of the scale [10, 19, 21–24]. While these studies provide valuable evidence supporting the use of the GAD-7, several aspects remain less explored, particularly in the Chinese context. Many existing studies are limited to specific subgroups rather than large, heterogeneous community-based samples, and most have focused primarily on standard psychometric properties, with comparatively less attention to comprehensive measurement invariance testing across multiple sociodemographic groups. Although measurement invariance has been examined in some prior research, more extensive evaluation across multiple dimensions—such as gender, age, occupation, and geographic region, including strict invariance within a unified analytical framework, has not been widely reported, which may affect the comparability of anxiety scores across diverse populations [10, 17].

In addition, there is increasing recognition that anxiety symptoms may be associated not only with individual-level characteristics but also with broader contextual environments; however, existing studies in China have largely emphasized individual-level factors, with relatively limited empirical investigation of macro-level socioeconomic conditions, such as regional economic development, employment patterns, and public health investment [2, 18, 20, 25–27]. Furthermore, few studies have integrated validated anxiety measures with macro-level indicators within the same analytical framework. In light of these considerations, the present study seeks to extend previous work by examining the psychometric properties of the GAD-7 in a large community-based sample in China, evaluating measurement invariance across multiple sociodemographic groups, and exploring associations between anxiety outcomes and selected individual-level and macro-level contextual factors. Accordingly, this study addresses the following research questions: whether the GAD-7 demonstrates adequate reliability, factorial validity, and measurement invariance across key sociodemographic groups; which individual-level characteristics are associated with anxiety outcomes; and whether macro-level contextual indicators are associated with anxiety outcomes after accounting for individual-level characteristics. By addressing these questions, this study aims to provide additional evidence on the measurement properties of the GAD-7 and to contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of the individual- and contextual-level correlates of anxiety in a large and diverse population setting. The psychometric evaluation of the GAD-7 represents the primary focus of the study, whereas the contextual analyses are intended as secondary and exploratory components.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This study employed a cross-sectional design using an online survey to assess anxiety symptoms and associated factors among adults in mainland China. Data were collected via a structured, self-administered questionnaire distributed through online platforms. Participation was voluntary and anonymous.

Eligible participants were adults aged 18–59 years who resided in mainland China at the time of the survey and were able to read and complete the questionnaire independently. Individuals were required to provide informed consent electronically before participation. Exclusion criteria included age below 18 years or above 59 years, incomplete responses on key variables (GAD-7, PHQ-9, or sociodemographic characteristics), and duplicate submissions identified through consistency checks.

The final analytical sample comprised 7,730 unique individuals. Individual-level variables, including anxiety symptoms, depressive symptoms, and sociodemographic characteristics, were measured once at the time of survey participation.

The sampling strategy was based on online convenience recruitment and therefore resulted in a non-probability sample. Females, teachers, students, and residents of North China were overrepresented relative to the general Chinese population. While this approach enabled broad geographic coverage and a large sample size, it may limit representativeness and generalizability. As participation was voluntary and internet-based, individuals with greater interest in mental health topics may have been more likely to participate. Consequently, prevalence estimates and observed associations may not fully reflect those of the general Chinese adult population.

Data collection procedure and measurement

Data collection was conducted continuously from January 2022 to December 2025 using a secure web-based survey platform. The questionnaire link was disseminated through social media networks, institutional mailing lists, and professional and educational groups to ensure broad geographic coverage. Participants completed the survey at their convenience using computers or mobile devices. To minimize missing data, mandatory response options were applied to key variables. All responses were automatically recorded and stored in a password-protected database accessible only to the research team.

Anxiety (GAD-7)

Anxiety symptoms were assessed using the Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale (GAD-7). The scale consists of seven items reflecting core symptoms of generalized anxiety disorder. Example items include “feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge” and “not being able to stop or control worrying.” Participants reported symptom frequency over the preceding two weeks using a 4-point Likert scale (0–3), yielding a total score from 0 to 21. GAD-7 scores were categorized into four severity levels: minimal (0–4), mild [5–9], moderate [10–14], and severe anxiety [15–21, 28]. A cut-off ≥ 10 indicates optimal diagnostic accuracy [28], while a lower threshold of ≥ 5 was additionally used for screening mild anxiety in epidemiological analyses. Previous studies have consistently reported high internal consistency for the GAD-7, with Cronbach’s alpha typically exceeding 0.80 across populations [5–15]. Three anxiety outcomes were analyzed: continuous score, ordinal severity, and binary anxiety status (≥ 5 vs. < 5) [9].

Depression (PHQ-9)

Depressive symptoms were assessed using the 9-item Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9). Example items include “little interest or pleasure in doing things” and “feeling down, depressed, or hopeless.” Each item is scored from 0 to 3, yielding a total score from 0 to 27 [9]. Scores were categorized into five severity levels: minimal, mild, moderate, moderately severe, and severe depression [29]. In the present study, Cronbach’s alpha was 0.8874. PHQ-9 total score was included as a continuous covariate in regression analyses because anxiety and depressive symptoms frequently co-occur and share common features of emotional distress.

Other variables

Participants rated the perceived impact of anxiety on daily life (four levels). Sociodemographic variables included gender, age, occupation, and geographic region.

Macro-level contextual variables

Provincial-level contextual indicators included employment rate, government health expenditure, gross domestic product (GDP) per capita, and population density. These indicators were selected to represent major dimensions of the socioeconomic environment, including labor market conditions, economic development, public health investment, and population concentration [30, 31]. (Table S1).

All contextual variables were obtained from official provincial statistics (Table S1). To improve the temporal relationship between contextual factors and anxiety outcomes, respondents were linked to provincial-level indicators from the year preceding survey participation. Specifically, participants surveyed in 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025 were linked to provincial indicators from 2021, 2022, 2023, and 2024, respectively. Provincial indicators from 2020 were included in the supplementary table for descriptive completeness only. This approach helps ensure that contextual conditions occurred before anxiety symptoms were assessed. In addition, provincial indicators such as GDP per capita, employment rate, and government health expenditure may influence mental health over time rather than immediately. Therefore, preceding-year values were considered more appropriate for examining contextual associations with anxiety.

These contextual indicators represent relatively distal features of the socioeconomic environment and were included primarily to explore potential contextual correlates of anxiety. More proximal contextual factors, such as neighborhood characteristics, social support, mental health service accessibility, and community resources, were not available in the present dataset. All contextual variables were log-transformed before analysis.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using Stata version 17.0 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize participant characteristics, with missing data handled through listwise deletion.

Prior to regression analyses, multicollinearity among independent variables was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIF), with values below 5 indicating no evidence of problematic collinearity. Variance inflation factors were examined for all predictors, including PHQ-9, because of the potential conceptual overlap between depressive and anxiety symptoms.

The internal consistency reliability of the GAD-7 was evaluated using both Cronbach’s alpha and McDonald’s omega coefficients [32, 33]. Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was performed to assess factorial validity, with model fit evaluated using multiple indices, including the comparative fit index (CFI), Tucker–Lewis index (TLI), root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA) with 90% confidence intervals, and the standardized root mean square residual (SRMR).

Measurement invariance was examined sequentially across gender, age group, occupation, and geographic region using multi-group CFA, progressing through configural, metric, scalar, and strict invariance models. Following recommendations by Cheung and Rensvold and Chen, invariance was supported when changes in CFI were ≤ 0.010 and changes in RMSEA were ≤ 0.015 across increasingly constrained models [34, 35]. Given the large sample size, strict invariance findings were interpreted cautiously because minor between-group differences may become statistically detectable.

To examine correlates of anxiety outcomes, three complementary regression models were estimated: [1] linear regression for continuous GAD-7 scores [2], ordered logistic regression for anxiety severity categories, and [3] binary logistic regression for GAD-7 screening status (GAD-7 ≥5 vs.<5). All regression analyses were conducted using the survey-year dataset (N = 7,730). The distribution of GAD-7 scores was positively skewed. Therefore, square-root transformation was applied before linear regression analyses to improve normality and reduce the influence of extreme values. Independent variables included individual-level characteristics and provincial-level contextual indicators. Effect estimates were reported as regression coefficients (β) or odds ratios (OR) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals. Because provincial-level contextual indicators were assigned to individuals, residual within-province dependence cannot be excluded. The regression models did not explicitly adjust for province-level clustering; therefore, contextual associations should be interpreted cautiously.

Because anxiety and depressive symptoms are conceptually related, sensitivity analyses were conducted by re-estimating all regression models without PHQ-9 to evaluate the robustness of the observed associations. The contextual analyses were considered exploratory and secondary to the primary psychometric validation objectives of the study. Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided p-value < 0.05.

Results

Characteristics

The study sample comprised 7,730 participants, predominantly female (68.5%), with a mean age of 31.7 ± 10.5 years. Females were younger on average than males (31.4 vs. 32.5 years) and were more frequently represented in the 18–20 and 31–40 age groups, whereas males were more common in the 51–59 age group. The largest occupational groups were Education/Teacher (41.6%) and Students (31.4%), with females more often students and healthcare workers, while males were more frequently employed in government/public sector, professional/technical, and manual occupations. Most participants resided in North China (60.5%), followed by Southwest China (20.7%) and East China (12.9%), with similar regional distributions by gender. The mean PHQ-9 score was slightly higher in males than females (7.66 vs. 7.43) (Table 1).

Psychometric properties and measurement invariance

The internal consistency of the GAD-7 was evaluated using Cronbach’s alpha and McDonald’s omega. Item-level statistics indicated strong and homogeneous item performance across the scale. Cronbach’s alpha for the full GAD-7 scale was α = 0.91, indicating excellent internal consistency. McDonald’s omega further supported this finding, with ω = 0.91, suggesting that the observed reliability was not driven by tau-equivalence assumptions and that the latent construct was measured consistently across items.

Two CFA models were compared to evaluate the factorial validity of the GAD-7. The baseline one-factor model demonstrated good fit, with CFI and TLI values above 0.95 and a low SRMR. Allowing a residual correlation between GAD-2 and GAD-3, which share overlapping semantic content related to excessive worrying, resulted in a modest improvement in information criteria (lower AIC and BIC) and SRMR, while CFI remained stable. Despite these minor improvements, both models exhibited similar factor loading patterns and overall fit, supporting the robustness of the unidimensional structure of the GAD-7. Given its parsimony and theoretical coherence, the one-factor model was retained as the primary measurement model (Table 2).

Across all examined characteristics, the GAD-7 demonstrated stable measurement invariance, with no statistically meaningful deterioration in model fit as equality constraints were progressively imposed. For gender, fit indices remained high from configural through strict invariance (CFI = 0.980–0.972; RMSEA = 0.077–0.069), with minimal changes in comparative fit (ΔCFI ≥ − 0.004; ΔRMSEA ≤ 0.006). Similarly, across age groups, configural to strict models showed consistently good fit (CFI = 0.979–0.970; RMSEA = 0.079–0.064), and all ΔCFI values were ≤ − 0.004, indicating support for metric, scalar, and strict invariance. For occupation groups, acceptable fit was observed at all invariance levels (CFI = 0.978–0.962; RMSEA = 0.082–0.069), with the largest change in CFI remaining small (ΔCFI = − 0.006). Likewise, across geographic regions, the GAD-7 maintained good model fit from configural to strict invariance (CFI = 0.978–0.966; RMSEA = 0.081–0.067), with negligible changes in CFI (ΔCFI ≥ − 0.005) and RMSEA (ΔRMSEA ≤ 0.010). Overall, these results indicate that configural, metric, scalar, and strict invariance were supported across gender, age group, occupation, and region (Table S2).

Distribution of GAD-7 scores and anxiety symptom severity

Table 3 shows that across GAD-7 items, the most frequently endorsed response was “several days,” particularly for feeling nervous or anxious (59.1%), worrying too much (54.7%), and irritability (54.5%), with corresponding mean item scores around 1.1. Higher symptom frequency (“more than half the days” or “nearly every day”) was most common for GAD-1, GAD-3, and GAD-6 (approximately 22–24% combined), whereas restlessness (GAD-5) and fear of something awful happening (GAD-7) showed the lowest mean scores (0.64 and 0.69, respectively). For the total GAD-7 score, most participants were classified as having mild anxiety (42.5%) or minimal anxiety (35.9%), with fewer reporting moderate (13.7%) or severe anxiety (8.0%); the overall mean total score was 6.66 ± 4.78 (range 0–21).

Depressive symptoms showed the strongest and most consistent association with anxiety. Each one-point increase in PHQ-9 score was associated with a 0.143-point increase in the square-root transformed GAD-7 score (95% CI: 0.140–0.146), a 54% increase in the odds of being in a higher anxiety severity category (OR = 1.54, 95% CI: 1.52–1.56), and a 60% increase in the odds of reporting anxiety (OR = 1.60, 95% CI: 1.57–1.64) (all p < 0.001).

Female participants reported higher anxiety than males across all three models. Compared with students, participants working in education or teaching consistently showed higher anxiety levels (β = 0.12, 95% CI: 0.07–0.18), greater anxiety severity (OR = 1.47, 95% CI: 1.24–1.75), and higher odds of anxiety (OR = 1.52, 95% CI: 1.21–1.90). Participants classified in the “other” occupational group also demonstrated modestly higher anxiety outcomes. Age-related associations were observed primarily in the continuous model, where participants aged 21–50 years had higher anxiety scores than those aged 18–20 years.

Regional differences were generally limited after adjustment for covariates. Participants from Northeast China had lower odds of belonging to a higher anxiety severity category than those from North China (OR = 0.43, 95% CI: 0.22–0.84), whereas no consistent regional associations were observed across all three outcomes.

The exploratory analyses of preceding-year contextual indicators yielded mixed findings. Higher employment rates were associated with higher anxiety scores (β = 0.29, 95% CI: 0.05–0.53) and greater anxiety severity (OR = 2.56, 95% CI: 1.21–5.41). In contrast, higher GDP per capita and population density were associated with lower anxiety levels and severity. Specifically, GDP per capita was negatively associated with anxiety scores (β = −0.35, 95% CI: −0.59 to − 0.10) and anxiety severity (OR = 0.32, 95% CI: 0.15–0.68), while population density was inversely associated with anxiety scores (β = −0.26, 95% CI: −0.45 to − 0.07), anxiety severity (OR = 0.42, 95% CI: 0.23–0.76), and binary anxiety status (OR = 0.44, 95% CI: 0.20–0.96). Government health expenditure was not significantly associated with any anxiety outcome.

Given the substantial conceptual overlap between anxiety and depressive symptoms, a sensitivity analysis was conducted by re-estimating all regression models without adjustment for PHQ-9 score (Table S4). The overall pattern of associations remained largely unchanged, although several individual-level and contextual associations became stronger, suggesting that depressive symptoms account for part of the shared variance between anxiety and other correlates.

Discussion

This large online study provides evidence on the psychometric performance of the GAD-7, while secondarily exploring individual- and contextual-level correlates of anxiety. The GAD-7 demonstrated excellent internal consistency, a robust unidimensional factor structure, and measurement invariance across key sociodemographic groups, supporting its use for group comparisons. Anxiety outcomes were associated with several individual characteristics, including gender, age, occupation, and depressive symptoms. Contextual analyses additionally suggested that some preceding-year provincial indicators, particularly employment rate, GDP per capita, and population density, were related to anxiety outcomes, although these associations were generally less consistent than those observed for individual-level factors and should be interpreted as exploratory findings.

Psychometric properties of GAD-7

The present findings further reinforce the strong reliability profile of the GAD-7. The observed internal consistency was excellent, with both Cronbach’s alpha and McDonald’s omega reaching 0.91, indicating a high degree of inter-item coherence and robust measurement of the underlying anxiety construct. These values are fully consistent with prior research across diverse populations, where Cronbach’s alpha typically ranges from 0.84 to 0.92 and omega coefficients consistently exceed 0.80, reflecting excellent reliability in clinical, community, and student samples worldwide [6, 7, 14, 36, 37]. Beyond internal consistency, previous studies have demonstrated strong temporal stability of the GAD-7, with test–retest reliability coefficients as high as 0.83 over three months in adolescents and moderate stability over longer periods in adult populations [11, 15].

Consistent with the broader psychometric literature, confirmatory factor analysis in this study supported a unidimensional structure of the GAD-7. The one-factor model demonstrated excellent global fit, with comparative fit indices exceeding 0.95 and low residual-based indices, and all items loading strongly on a single latent anxiety factor. These results align with a substantial body of evidence from multiple countries and populations showing that a single-factor model adequately captures the construct of generalized anxiety [6, 7, 36, 37]. Although some studies have proposed alternative two-factor solutions distinguishing somatic and cognitive–affective symptoms, such models generally offer limited incremental explanatory value and are often less parsimonious [38]. In line with prior recommendations, the present findings support the continued use of the unidimensional GAD-7 structure in both research and applied settings.

An additional contribution of this study is the demonstration of measurement invariance of the GAD-7 across gender, age group, occupation, and geographic region. Configural, metric, scalar, and strict invariance models showed only minimal changes in model fit indices across increasingly constrained models. These findings suggest that GAD-7 scores can be compared across these sociodemographic groups with limited concern regarding measurement non-equivalence. Similar patterns have been reported in previous studies conducted across different languages, cultures, and demographic groups [6, 36, 38, 39].

Distribution of GAD-7 anxiety symptom severity

The prevalence and severity distribution of anxiety observed in this study indicate a substantial burden of subclinical and mild symptoms within the surveyed population, alongside a smaller but clinically meaningful proportion with severe anxiety. The overall mean GAD-7 score of 6.66 ± 4.78 and the finding that 8.0% of participants met criteria for severe anxiety (GAD-7 ≥ 15) place this sample within the range reported in pre-pandemic and non-crisis Chinese populations. National estimates prior to COVID-19 suggested an anxiety disorder prevalence of approximately 7.6% in the community-based sample, which is comparable to the proportion of severe anxiety identified here [40]. At the same time, the high proportion of individuals classified as having mild anxiety (42.5%) highlights a large group experiencing persistent but potentially under-recognized symptoms. This pattern is consistent with other large Chinese surveys reporting that mild anxiety constitutes the majority of anxiety presentations, whereas moderate and severe forms are less common [41]. Compared with studies conducted during the COVID-19 outbreak, where moderate-to-severe anxiety reached 22.6% among adults and exceeded 40% in specific subgroups such as college students, the lower observed proportion in the present sample likely reflects differences in timing, exposure to acute stressors, and population composition [42, 43].

Item-level response patterns further suggest that anxiety in this population is characterized predominantly by frequent but low-to-moderate symptom intensity. Most respondents endorsed experiencing core symptoms such as nervousness, excessive worrying, and irritability on “several days,” with mean item scores clustering around 1.0, while more disabling symptoms such as restlessness and catastrophic fear were less frequently reported. This symptom profile aligns with prior Chinese studies indicating that cognitive and affective manifestations of anxiety are more common than somatic agitation in community samples [41, 44]. The relatively small proportion of participants reporting symptoms “nearly every day” across items supports the interpretation that, for most individuals, anxiety symptoms are intermittent rather than pervasive. From a comparative perspective, the distribution of severity observed here, with severe anxiety accounting for a minority of cases, also accords with international evidence that, although anxiety disorders are widespread globally, the proportion of severe anxiety among affected individuals tends to be lower in China than in many Western countries, where severe presentations may account for a much larger share of diagnosed cases [45]. Taken together, these findings underscore the importance of community-based screening strategies that capture mild and moderate anxiety, as these forms represent the largest share of symptom burden and may progress to more severe conditions if left unaddressed, particularly during periods of heightened social or environmental stress.

Factors associated with GAD

The individual-level associations observed in this study are broadly consistent with previous research on anxiety in Chinese and international populations. Across the three models, females tended to report higher anxiety levels than males. Higher anxiety scores were also observed among participants aged 21–50 years compared with those aged 18–20 years, whereas little difference was observed for the oldest age group (51–59 years). Although the magnitude of associations varied across analytic approaches, the overall pattern suggests that anxiety symptoms were more commonly reported among females and working-age adults. These findings are generally compatible with previous studies reporting variation in anxiety according to gender and life stage, particularly during periods characterized by educational, occupational, and family responsibilities [6, 11, 37, 40, 42, 46].

Some occupational groups also showed higher anxiety outcomes relative to students. Education/teachers and participants classified in the “other” occupation category displayed relatively consistent associations across the three models, while business/sales/service workers showed higher anxiety severity in the ordinal model. In contrast, associations for healthcare workers, government employees, office staff, self-employed individuals, manual workers, and retirees were less consistent across outcomes. Geographic variation appeared limited, with Northeast China showing lower anxiety severity compared with North China, whereas differences across most other regions were small [42, 47]. PHQ-9 score showed the strongest and most consistent association with anxiety outcomes across all models. This finding is in line with the substantial overlap and frequent co-occurrence of anxiety and depressive symptoms reported in previous studies, although it may also partly reflect conceptual overlap between the GAD-7 and PHQ-9 measures [6, 37, 48–51].

The contextual findings should be interpreted cautiously. Compared with individual-level characteristics, the provincial socioeconomic indicators examined in this study showed more modest and less consistent associations with anxiety outcomes. This pattern is broadly consistent with previous evidence suggesting that personal socioeconomic circumstances, such as employment status, income, financial strain, and educational attainment, are more closely related to anxiety than broader macroeconomic indicators [52–54]. Similarly, cross-national studies have found that country-level economic indicators account for only a limited proportion of variation in mental health after individual-level characteristics are considered [55, 56]. These observations are consistent with the present findings, in which anxiety outcomes appeared to be more closely associated with individual characteristics than with provincial contextual conditions.

Among the contextual indicators, higher preceding-year employment rates were associated with higher anxiety scores and anxiety severity, whereas higher GDP per capita and population density were associated with lower anxiety outcomes. The positive association between contextual employment rates and anxiety differs from the commonly reported association between individual unemployment and poorer mental health [52, 57, 58], suggesting that provincial employment rates may capture broader economic and labor market conditions rather than individual employment experiences. In contrast, the inverse associations observed for GDP per capita and population density are compatible with studies indicating that greater economic resources, healthcare availability, and social infrastructure may be linked to better mental health outcomes [56, 59]. Government health expenditure showed little evidence of association with anxiety, which is consistent with research suggesting that health spending may be more closely related to service capacity than to population symptom levels [60]. Taken together, these findings suggest that broad provincial socioeconomic indicators may be associated with anxiety, although their associations appear weaker and less consistent than those observed for individual-level characteristics.

Implications

The findings of this study support the use of the GAD-7 as a brief instrument for anxiety assessment in large community and research settings. The scale demonstrated favorable reliability, factorial validity, and measurement invariance across major sociodemographic groups, suggesting that comparisons of anxiety scores across these groups may be conducted with a reasonable degree of confidence.

Several individual-level characteristics, including gender, age, occupation, and depressive symptoms, were consistently related to anxiety outcomes across different analytic approaches. These patterns may help identify population groups that could benefit from greater attention in mental health screening and monitoring activities. The close relationship observed between anxiety and depressive symptoms also highlights the importance of considering multiple dimensions of emotional distress when assessing mental health in population studies.

The contextual analyses provide preliminary information regarding potential associations between provincial socioeconomic conditions and anxiety outcomes. Although some preceding-year contextual indicators showed associations with anxiety measures, these findings were generally less consistent than those observed for individual-level characteristics. Future studies may benefit from examining more proximal contextual factors, such as neighborhood environments, social support, access to mental health services, and community resources, which may be more closely related to individual experiences.

Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, the cross-sectional design does not allow conclusions regarding temporal ordering or causality. The reported findings should therefore be interpreted as associations rather than causal relationships.

Second, contextual indicators were linked using provincial-level data from the year preceding survey participation. Although this approach improves temporal alignment between contextual conditions and anxiety assessment, the cross-sectional nature of the data does not allow evaluation of changes in anxiety over time or stronger inference regarding contextual influences. Accordingly, the contextual findings should be interpreted as exploratory.

Third, all information was obtained through self-report measures and may be affected by recall errors, reporting tendencies, and social desirability bias. In addition, the GAD-7 is a screening instrument and does not provide a clinical diagnosis of anxiety disorders.

Fourth, because females, students, and educators were overrepresented and these groups differed in anxiety levels from the general population, both prevalence estimates and some observed associations may not fully reflect patterns in the broader Chinese adult population. Therefore, caution is warranted when generalizing the findings to the broader Chinese adult population.

Fifth, depressive symptoms showed the strongest association with anxiety outcomes across all models. While this finding is consistent with the well-established co-occurrence of anxiety and depression, it may also partly reflect conceptual overlap between the PHQ-9 and GAD-7. Nevertheless, sensitivity analyses excluding PHQ-9 yielded broadly similar patterns of association, providing some support for the robustness of the main findings.

Finally, the contextual indicators were limited to broad provincial-level socioeconomic measures. Such indicators may not adequately reflect more immediate contextual influences on mental health, including social connectedness, perceived safety, healthcare accessibility, or local environmental conditions. Future research could benefit from longitudinal designs, repeated individual-level assessments, probability-based sampling strategies, and more detailed contextual measures to further clarify the relationships between anxiety and its correlates.

Conclusion

This study provides additional evidence supporting the reliability, validity, and measurement invariance of the GAD-7 among Chinese adults. The findings suggest that the instrument performs consistently across major sociodemographic groups and may be suitable for anxiety assessment in large-scale population research. Anxiety outcomes were more consistently associated with individual-level characteristics, particularly depressive symptoms, gender, age, and occupation, than with the provincial-level contextual indicators examined. The contextual analyses should be regarded as exploratory and secondary to the psychometric objectives of the study. Future studies using longitudinal designs and more proximal contextual measures may help further clarify the role of environmental factors in anxiety.

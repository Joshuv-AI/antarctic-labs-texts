Non-invasive miRNAs for early detection and diagnosis of lacrimal adenoid cystic carcinoma
Authors: Xia Ding, Mingjiao Chen, Yan Liu, Tianyi Zhou, Yue Xing, Ming Lin, Jin Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractLacrimal adenoid cystic carcinoma (LACC) is one of the most common malignant epithelial tumors of the lacrimal gland, characterized by high rates of local recurrence, distant metastases, and tumor-related mortality. This malignancy impairs the lacrimal gland's ability to secret tears, thereby disrupting the normal function of the ocular surface. Due to ineffective diagnostic approaches and the complex anatomical location of the lacrimal gland within the orbit, many LACC patients are often diagnosed at the later stages. Therefore, it is urgent to develop a simple and convenient method for early detection of LACC through biomarker analysis by liquid biopsies, such as blood or tears. Various microRNAs (miRNAs) derived from liquid biopsies have been shown to serve as biomarkers for early diagnosis of tumors. In the present study, we screened LACC-specific miRNAs using miRNA microarray analysis in both primary and recurrent patient groups. We then validate their expression by qPCR experiments conducted in tissues, cell lines, tear fluid and serum to explore the association between these miRNAs and LACC development and prognosis. Our findings reveal that hsa-miR-200b-3p, hsa-miR-200c-3p and hsa-miR-141-3p are significantly upregulated in LACC, and the microarray data showed that these three miRNAs were significantly elevated in the recurrence group compared to the primary group. In conclusion, detecting miRNA expression in tear fluid and serum provides non-invasive biomarkers for the early diagnosis of LACC and may facilitate monitoring outcomes related to lacrimal gland diseases.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01436-9.Keywords: Lacrimal gland, Lacrimal adenoid cystic carcinoma, miRNAs, Noninvasive biomarker, Microarray analysis

Introduction

Lacrimal adenoid cystic carcinoma (LACC) is one of the most frequent malignant epithelial tumors in the lacrimal gland [1, 2], accounting for 1.6% of all orbital tumors [3, 4] and ranking second after pleomorphic adenomas, which account for 25–40% of lacrimal epithelial tumors [5]. It is clinically characterized by frequent local invasion, distant metastatic spread to the lung, bone, brain, and liver, and multiple recurrences. Despite progress in cancer diagnosis and clinical therapeutics, there is no effective treatment for patients who present or recur with this unresectable disease until recently. Consequently, the prognosis remains dismal with low survival rates less than 50% at 5 years and 20% at 10 years [6]. Due to inadequate diagnostic approaches and the complex anatomical location of the lacrimal gland within the orbit, a large number of LACC patients are frequently diagnosed at the later stages. Previous studies have indicated that lower T stages (T1-T2) of LACC had a better outcome than those with advanced stages (T3-T4) [7, 8], and T-stage and tumor size are the most significant factors that determine the outcomes and prognosis. Hence, early diagnosis and intervention for LACC would improve the cure rates and overall survival rates, highlighting the importance to explore potential biomarkers for the early detection and diagnosis of LACC.

Emerging evidence suggested that miRNAs in liquid biopsies could be ideal molecular biomarkers in tumor diagnosis and treatment. Several miRNAs have been demonstrated as prognostic markers in adenoid cystic carcinoma of salivary gland, lacrimal gland and breast [9]. MiR-24-3p [5] has been demonstrated to suppress the malignant behavior of LACC, whereas miR-93-5p [6] and miR-181a-5p promote LACC tumorigenesis. These suggest that specific miRNAs are implicated in the pathogenesis and malignant behavior of LACC [5], serving as potential therapeutic targets. Furthermore, tear sample collection is less invasive and can be easily obtained for rapid and continuous health monitoring [10]. Thus, detecting dysregulated miRNAs with non-invasive methods to identify LACC-specific miRNAs may contribute to the early detection and diagnosis of LACC.

In the current study, we carried out microarray analysis on paraffin-embedded tissues from LACC patients and corresponding normal tissues, and discovered 10 upregulated miRNAs. Subsequently, we identified that hsa-miR-200b-3p, hsa-miR-200c-3p and hsa-miR-141-3p were significantly elevated in LACC tissues, salivary adenoid cystic carcinoma (SACC) cell lines, serum and tear samples. Moreover, the microarray data showed that these three miRNAs were significantly elevated in the recurrence group compared to the primary group. These findings demonstrate that comprehensive detection of these miRNAs could provide valuable diagnostic information for LACC patients.

Materials and methods

Subjects

All participants included in this study were diagnosed with LACC based on clinical manifestations and pathology. Paraffin-embedded sections of both primary and recurrent lacrimal gland tissues from four LACC patients were analyzed, along with paraffin sections of lacrimal gland tissues from 4 patients with normal lacrimal gland prolapse, to establish miRNA profiles through miRNA microarray analysis. Preoperative tear fluid, blood samples and postoperative lesion tissues from 10 LACC patients were collected for miRNA expression analysis. The medical histories and pathological data of all patients were meticulously reviewed. The general data for 10 patients included an average age of 43.9 years (ranging from 31 to 59 years) and a male-to-female ratio of 3:2. The 10 LACC patients presented with N0M0 and T-stage ranging from T1 to T3. The median follow-up was 31.3 months for the 10 LACC cases.

MiRNA microarray analysis

A total of eight paraffin-embedded LACC tissues and four normal controls were subjected to miRNA microarray analysis conducted by Oebiotech, Shanghai. RNA was isolated using the AMBION kit, and then the purified RNA was assessed with either NanoDrop or Agilent 2100 prior to performing the Agilent human miRNA array. The limma algorithm was employed to identify differentially expressed miRNAs in LACC tissues based on normalized data. A log2 fold change (log2FC) greater than 1 or less than − 1, along with a P-value of less than 0.05, was considered indicative of significant differences.

Tear fluid collection

Tear fluid was collected from inferior conjunctival fornix of patients using a glass capillary. The total volume of tear fluid collected from a single patient was approximately 60 μl. All samples were centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 20 min at 4 °C to remove cellular debris, and then the samples were frozen and stored at − 80 °C until required for further analysis.

Serum preparation

Blood samples were collected in a tube without anticoagulants. The samples were maintained at 4 ℃ overnight, and subsequently centrifuged at 4 °C and at 2000 rpm for 10 min. The resultant supernatant was isolated and subsequently frozen and stored at − 80 °C prior to utilization.

Cell lines

The SACC cell lines (ACC-M, ACC-2), purchased from Genechem (Shanghai, China), were cultured in RPMI 1640 (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA, United States) at 37 ℃ supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco). The HSG cell line was kindly provided by the department of Oral Surgery of Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital, and cultured in DMEM/F12 (Gibco) at 37 ℃ supplemented with 10% FBS. Penicillin (100 U/ml) and streptomycin (100 µg/ml) were added into the cultures for all cells.

Quantitative RT-PCR of miRNA

The detection of miRNA was conducted using a stem loop reverse transcription kit (BioTNT, Shanghai, China) with specific primers for the stem loop reverse transcription. The BioTNT MicroRNA assay (BioTNT) was used for cDNA synthesis and miRNA detection. After normalization to the reference U6, miRNA expression was shown as relative fold change (FC) compared to controls. The primer sequences are listed in Table 1.

Statistical analysis

All the values are presented as means ± SEMs. Statistical analysis was conducted using unpaired t tests. P values < 0.05 were regarded as statistically significant.

Results

MiRNAs expression profiling of adenoid cystic carcinoma of the lacrimal gland

To identify potential diagnostic miRNA biomarkers of LACC, we conducted miRNA microarray on paraffin-embedded lacrimal gland tissues from LACC patients and matched normal tissues to establish possible LACC-specific miRNA profiles. Comparative analysis of miRNAs expression between the LACC primary group and the normal group revealed a total of 368 differentially expressed miRNAs (supplementary file), and 194 dysregulated miRNAs were screened out to be significantly different shared in the LACC primary group and the recurrence group (Fig. 1A).

In the microarray analysis, the top ten upregulated miRNAs in the intersection between the LACC group versus the normal group and the LACC recurrence group versus the normal group were screened out by using p < 0.05 and fourfold change cut-off and presented in the volcano and heatmap diagram (Fig. 1B–D). Subsequently, the 10 upregulated miRNAs were tested in a second cohort of 10 LACC and their matched 10 normal tissues by quantitative real time PCR (qPCR) experiments. To ensure the accuracy, the validated miRNA-24-3p was selected to serve as the positive control. The qPCR results demonstrated that the expression levels of three miRNAs (hsa-miR-200b-3p, hsa-miR-200c-3p, hsa-miR-141-3p) were significantly upregulated in all investigated tissues compared to the control tissues (Fig. 2). It is worth noting that the expression levels of these three miRNAs in the recurrence group were higher than those in the LACC group, as indicated by the miRNA microarray data analysis. These findings suggest that these three miRNAs were highly expressed in LACC patients and may be used to monitor the prognosis.

Validation of potential biomarkers in cells, tears and serum

Several studies have demonstrated that the majority of miRNAs expressed in LACC are similar to those in SACC, however, there are a number of miRNAs are differentially expressed between SACC and LACC in contrast to their matched normal tissues [11]. For instance, hsa-miR-455-5p and hsa-miR-181d-5p were found to be upregulated in both SACC and LACC [9], which was concordant with our microarray data, and hsa-miR-181a-2-3p, hsa-miR-93-3p, hsa-miR-106b-3p, hsa-miR-1307-5p, hsa-miR-181a-5p, hsa-miR-93-5p were specifically upregulated in LACC while no significant differences were observed in SACC when compared with the corresponding normal controls [9]. Based on these, we choose two SACC cell lines to assess the miRNAs expression levels by q-PCR experiments. Similarly, compared to the normal control HSG cell line, we discovered that the three miRNAs exhibited increased expression in both ACC-2 and ACC-M cell lines (Fig. 3).

Increasing evidence suggests that miRNAs circulate in various biological fluids (tears, serum, plasma, amniotic fluid, bitter fluid, and urine) in a stable chemical configuration, maintaining their molecular sequence specificity, which keeps them as potential biomarkers for human diseases [12, 13]. Tears are mainly produced by the lacrimal glands through filtration from blood plasma [10]. Numerous miRNAs in tear fluid have been demonstrated to be associated with conditions such as Alzheimer’s disease [14], diabetic macular edema [15], and dry eye syndrome [16]. Therefore, we wonder whether altered miRNAs in tear fluid and serum could serve as potential biomarkers for LACC. Subsequently, we collect tears and blood samples from 10 patients diagnosed with LACC and their counterparts with normal lacrimal gland prolapse. As expected, we observed that these three miRNAs were upregulated in the LACC-affected tear fluid in comparison to the normal tear fluid (Fig. 4A). Consistent with their expression in tear fluid, these three miRNAs were also upregulated in the serum of LACC patients compared to normal blood samples (Fig. 4B). Thus, the results confirmed that these three miRNAs were highly expressed in LACC and may serve as potential biomarkers to diagnose LACC.

Discussion

LACC patients commonly exhibit clinical manifestations such as ocular pain, globe dystopia, proptosis, and ‘S’ shaped ptosis [17], and have a grave prognosis characterized by frequent local recurrences, distant metastases, and tumor-related mortality [9]. Moreover, LACC destroys the lacrimal gland which is the pivotal contributor to the tear film to protect and lubricate the ocular surface [18, 19]. Although tears are known to contain miRNAs, proteins and other molecules [14], there have been extremely limited studies on tears as a source of biomarkers for LACC. As reported, while LACC and SACC are morphologically and genetically similar, they exhibit distinct microRNA expression profiles [9] and LACC has a poor prognosis. In our present study, we screened LACC-specific miRNAs using microarray data. We then verified the data by qPCR experiments in tissues, SACC cell lines, tears and serum. Ultimately, we identified hsa-miR-200b-3p, hsa-miR-200c-3p and hsa-miR-141-3p as the most significantly upregulated miRNAs.

Changes in tissue miRNAs can be reflected in the serum, offering a non-invasive alternative to direct tissue measurement which is intrusive and inconvenient. Emerging investigations have focused on the development of non-invasive and more convenient biomarkers that enable earlier diagnosis of tumors that with local recurrences and distant metastases. These three identified miRNAs belong to the miRNA-200 family which consists of five members (miRNA-200a, -200b, -200c, -141 and -429) that have been reported in several types of advanced carcinoma [20]. These miRNAs are thought to be implicated in the early stages of tumor metastasis [21, 22]. For instance, hsa-miR-141-3p and hsa-miR-200c-3p have been identified as potential biomarkers for predicting colon cancer metastasis [23]. Additionally, the expression level of hsa-miR-200a-3p, hsa-miR-200b-3p, hsa-miR-200c-3p and hsa-miR-141-3p correlate with lymph node metastasis in breast cancer [24]. Moreover, hsa-miR-200c-3p in serum has been suggested as a novel non-invasive early biomarker for bladder cancer [25]. These 3 investigated miRNAs were selected based on the results of experiments in which we analyzed the expression in clinical tissue samples and SACC cell lines. Whereas the highly metastatic potential of the ACC-M cell line, we observed these three miRNAs were highly expressed in ACC-M compared to HSG cell line, suggesting that these 3 differentially expressed miRNAs were metastasis-related miRNAs directly or indirectly involved in the progression of metastasis. Furthermore, our microarray data revealed that the three miRNAs significantly upregulated in the recurrence group compared with the LACC primary group, thus highlighting their potential relevance to tumor metastasis and prognosis. Consistent with their expression in tissues, these three miRNAs were also upregulated in the serum and tears of LACC patients compared to controls. Therefore, it is reasonable to propose that these three miRNAs in tear fluid or serum might be used for the early detection and diagnosis of LACC.

Following the resection or radiotherapy of LACC lesions, it is essential to conduct these three miRNAs detection in tears and serum at different stages of disease progression to monitor prognosis in patients who have been cured or experienced relapse assessed by the imaging and clinical manifestations, with the goal of developing a correlation analysis along with a diagnostic evaluation system and facilitating their translation into clinical practice for early diagnosis and intervention. Meanwhile, detecting molecular changes in tear fluid can help us to be vigilant against lacrimal gland diseases whenever necessary. However, our study has some limitations. Only 10 LACC tissues that lacked systems-level identification were used to investigate the differentially expressed miRNAs, preventing us from correlating expression levels with TNM staging and overall survival time. Future research should involve a large sample size to better determine the correlation between miRNAs expression and clinical stages. In addition, we did not conducted an in-depth investigation on the functions and mechanisms of these miRNAs. As miRNAs act as oncogenes or tumor suppressors depending on their target gene functions, we plan to utilize databases of miRNA:mRNA target interactions to establish a network profile of these three miRNAs and further evaluate their impact on LACC.

Conclusion

This study starts with the identification of LACC tissue-specific miRNAs and subsequently proceeds to detect their expression in tears related to lacrimal gland function and serum. We found that hsa-miR-200c-3p, hsa-miR-200b-3p, and hsa-miR-141-3p were upregulated in both tear fluid and serum, consistent with the expression level in tissues and SACC cell lines. Our findings suggest that monitoring these three miRNAs in tear fluid or serum may provide a convenient and effective means for early intervention and outcome assessment.

Arc-transitive maps with edge number coprime to the Euler characteristic – I Thanks: This work was partially supported by NSFC grant 11931005
Cai Heng Li, Luyi Liu

Abstract
. This is one of a series of papers which aim towards a classification of edge-transitive maps of which the Euler characteristic and the edge number are coprime. This one establishes a framework and carries out the classification work for arc-transitive maps with solvable automorphism groups, which illustrates how the edge number impacts on the Euler characteristic for maps. The classification is involved with the constructions of various new and interesting arc-regular maps. Key words: arc-regular, maps, Euler characteristic



1.
Introduction

A
map
is a
2
-cell embedding of a graph into a closed surface.
Throughout the paper, we denote by
\mathcal{M}=(V,E,F)
a map with vertex set
V
, edge set
E
, and face set
F
.
The
underlying graph

(V,E)
of
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
\Gamma
, and the supporting surface of
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}
.
The
Euler characteristic
of a map
\mathcal{M}=(V,E,F)
is

\chi(\mathcal{M})=|V|-|E|+|F|.

Clearly, the number of edges
|E|
directly affects
\chi(\mathcal{M})
.
In this paper, we investigate highly symmetric maps where
\gcd(|E|,\ \chi(\mathcal{M}))=1
.
Moreover, we always assume that
\mathcal{M}
has no loops.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(V,E,F)
be a map.
An
arc
of
\mathcal{M}
is an ordered adjacent pair
(\alpha,e)
of a vertex
v
and an edge
e
, while a
flag
of
\mathcal{M}
is an adjacent triple
(\alpha,e,f)
of a vertex
\alpha
, an edge
e
, and a face
f
.
Let
e
be an edge with endpoints
\alpha
and
\beta
.
By the definition of a map, there are exactly two faces
f
and
f^{\prime}
adjacent to
e
.
The edge
e
is adjacent to two arcs
(\alpha,e)
and
(\beta,e)
, and four flags:

(\alpha,e,f)
,
(\alpha,e,f^{\prime})
,
(\beta,e,f)
, and
(\beta,e,f^{\prime})
.
The arc set and flag set of
\mathcal{M}
are denoted by
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{F}
, respectively, and satisfy
|\mathcal{F}|=2|\mathcal{A}|=4|E|
.
We denote by
E(v)
and
E(f)
the sets of edges incident to vertex
\alpha
and face
f
, respectively.
The
vertex valency
of
\alpha
is defined as
|E(\alpha)|
, and the
face length
of
f
is defined as
|E(f)|
.

An
automorphism
of
\mathcal{M}
is a permutation
\sigma
of its flags that preserves all incidence relations.
The set of all automorphisms of
\mathcal{M}
forms a group, denoted by
{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{M})
.
If an automorphism
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{M})
fixes a flag, then it must fix all flags, and therefore
\sigma
is the identity element.
This implies that
{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{M})
acts semiregularly on the flag set
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{M}
.
If
{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{M})
acts transitively on
\mathcal{F}
, then
\mathcal{M}
is called a
regular map
.
If
{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{M})
acts transitively on the arc set
\mathcal{A}
but intransitively on
\mathcal{F}
, then it acts regularly on
\mathcal{A}
, and
\mathcal{M}
is termed an
arc-regular map
.
Similarly,
\mathcal{M}
is called
edge-regular
if
{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{M})
acts regularly on the edge set
E
.
The classification problem of certain arc-transitive maps has been extensively studied; see
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
and references therein.

A map is called
locally finite
if all vertex valencies and face lengths are finite.
Locally finite edge-transitive maps and their automorphism groups are classified by Graver and Watkins
[
9
]
into fourteen types based on the local actions of their automorphism groups.
Among these types, five are arc-transitive which are explain as follows.

Let
G\leqslant{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{M})
act arc-transitively on
\mathcal{M}
.
Then
G
is transitive on the vertex set
V
, and the valency
\ell=|E(\alpha)|
is constant for all
\alpha\in V
, called the
valency
of
\mathcal{M}
.
Furthermore, there exists an involution
z\in G
that swaps the two arcs
(\alpha,e)
and
(\beta,e)
adjacent to an edge
e\in E
.

First, suppose that
G
acts transitively on the flag set of
\mathcal{M}
, then
G={\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{M})
, and
\mathcal{M}
is a regular map.
Furthermore, there exist involutions
x,y\in G
such that

G=\langle x,y,z\rangle,\quad G_{v}=\langle x,y\rangle=\mathrm{D}_{2\ell},\quad G_{e}=\langle x,z\rangle=\mathrm{D}_{4}.

The triple
(x,y,z)
is called a
regular triple
of
G
.

Now assume that
G
is not transitive on the flag set.
Then
G
is regular on the arc set of
\mathcal{M}
, and
\mathcal{M}
is a
G
-arc-regular map.
In this case,
G_{v}
is cyclic or dihedral of order equal to
\ell
.
If
G_{\alpha}=\langle a\rangle=\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
, then
\mathcal{M}
is said to be
G
-vertex-rotary
, and
(a,z)
is called a
rotary pair
for
G
.
On the other hand, if
G_{\alpha}=\langle x,y\rangle=\mathrm{D}_{\ell}
, then
\mathcal{M}
is said to be
G
-vertex-reversing
, and
(x,y,z)
is called a

for
G
.
Further, according to the action of

on

, vertex-rotary maps are divided into

and

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some basic definitions and preliminary results which will be used in the ensuing sections.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(V,E,F)
be a map, and
G\leqslant{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{M})
be arc-regular on
\mathcal{M}
.
Then the stabilizer
G_{e}
of an edge
e
is generated by an involution
z
say, explicitly,

G_{e}=\langle z\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2},

and
(\alpha,e)^{z}=(\beta,e)
.
Let
f,f^{\prime}\in F
be incident with
e
.
Then
z
either fixes
f
and
f^{\prime}
, or interchanges
f
and
f^{\prime}
, so that

z:\ (f,f^{\prime})\mapsto(f,f^{\prime})\ \mbox{or}\ (f^{\prime},f).

Since
G
acts regularly on the arc set, the vertex stabilizer
G_{\alpha}
is regular on
E(\alpha)
.
The pair
(G_{\alpha},z)
defines an arc-regular coset graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
, where

V=[G:G_{\alpha}]=\{G_{\alpha}g\mid g\in G\},\quad E=[G:\langle z\rangle]=\{\langle z\rangle h\mid h\in G\}

with incidence defined by non-empty intersection: a vertex
G_{\alpha}g
and an edge
\langle z\rangle h
are incident if and only if
G_{\alpha}g\cap\langle z\rangle h\neq\emptyset
.
This coset graph, denoted by
{\sf Cos}(G,G_{\alpha},\langle z\rangle)
, may have multiple edges; see
[
16
]
for details.

Assume that
G_{\alpha}=\langle a\rangle
.
Then the rotary pair
(a,z)
determines two vertex-rotary embeddings of
\Gamma
, namely, a rotary map or a bi-rotary map, defined below.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group generated by
a,z
with
z
being an involution, and
let
\Gamma={\sf Cos}(G,\langle a\rangle,\langle z\rangle)
.
Let
V=[G:G_{\alpha}]
,
E=[G:\langle z\rangle]
, and let

(i)

\mathsf{RotaMap}(G,a,z)
be the embedding of
\Gamma
with face set
[G:\langle az\rangle]
, and

(ii)

\mathsf{BiRotaMap}(G,a,z)
be the embedding of
\Gamma
with face set
[G:\langle z,z^{a}\rangle]
,

with incidences defined by non-empty intersection.
Then
\mathsf{RotaMap}(G,a,z)
is a rotary map, and
\mathsf{BiRotaMap}(G,a,z)
is a bi-rotary map.

Now let
G_{\alpha}=\langle x,y\rangle
with
x
and
y
being involutions.
Then the reversing triple
(x,y,z)
determines two vertex-reversing embeddings of
\Gamma
, namely, a reversing map or a bi-reversing map, defined below.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group generated by involutions
x,y,z
, and
let
\Gamma={\sf Cos}(G,\langle x,y\rangle,\langle z\rangle)
.
Let

(i)

\mathsf{RevMap}(G,x,y,z)
be the embedding of
\Gamma
with
F=[G{:}\langle x,z\rangle]\cup[G{:}\langle y,z\rangle]
,

(ii)

\mathsf{BiRevMap}(G,x,y,z)
be the embedding of
\Gamma
with
F=[G{:}\langle x,y^{z}\rangle]
,

with incidence defined by non-empty intersection.
Then
\mathsf{RevMap}(G,x,y,z)
is a reversing map, and
\mathsf{BiRevMap}(G,x,y,z)
is a bi-reversing map.

3.
The edge number and the Euler characteristic

In this section, we investigate the effect of the Euler characteristic on edge numbers and automorphism groups of maps.
Also, we provided a characterization of automorphism groups of maps such that the Euler characteristic and the edge number are coprime.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{M}
has constant vertex valency
\ell
and constant face length
k
, and
\gcd(\chi(\mathcal{M}),|E|)=1
.
Then
|E|
divides
k\ell
,
\lvert V\rvert
divides
2k
and
|F|
divides
2\ell
.

Proof.

Since each edge is adjacent to two vertices and two faces, there are

|V|\cdot\ell=2|E|\text{ and }|F|\cdot k=2|E|.

Combining these with the Euler characteristic formula
\chi(\mathcal{M})=|V|-|E|+|F|
, we substitute

|V|=\frac{2|E|}{\ell},\quad|F|=\frac{2|E|}{k}

to obtain

k\ell\chi(\mathcal{M})=(2\ell-k\ell+2k)|E|.

It follows from
\gcd(\chi(\mathcal{M}),|E|)=1
that
|E|
divides
k\ell
.
Consequently,
|V|=\frac{2|E|}{\ell}
divides
\frac{2k\ell}{\ell}=2k
, and
|F|=\frac{2|E|}{k}
divides
\frac{2k\ell}{k}=2\ell
.

\Box

This lemma shows that, if
\gcd(\chi,|E|)=1
then the edge number
|E|
is small relative to the valency and the face length.

The next lemma characterizes Sylow subgroups of the automorphism group of a map whose Euler characteristic is coprime to the number of edges.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(V,E,F)
be a map, and
G
be a subgroup of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{M})
.
Assume that
\gcd(\chi(\mathcal{M}),|E|)=1
.
Then the following statements are true:

(1)

\gcd(\chi(\mathcal{M}),|G|)
divides
4
, and
\gcd(\chi(\mathcal{M}),|G|)\neq 1
only if the edge number
|E|
is odd;

(2)

each Sylow subgroup of
G
is a cyclic or dihedral subgroup;

(3)

|G|={\rm lcm}\{|G_{\omega}|:\omega\in V\cup E\cup F\}
.

Proof.

Recall that
{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{M})
acts semiregular on the flag set
\mathcal{F}
.
So
|G|
divides
|\mathcal{F}|
.
Let
\chi=\chi(\mathcal{M})
for convenience.
Since
|\mathcal{F}|=4|E|
and
\gcd(\chi,|E|)=1
,
\gcd(\chi,|G|)
divides
\gcd(\chi,|\mathcal{F}|)=\gcd(\chi,4|E|)=\gcd(\chi,4)
.
Suppose that
\gcd(\chi,|G|)\neq 1
.
Then
\chi
is even, and so the edge number
|E|
is odd, as in part (1).

Let
p
be a prime divisor of
|G|
.
First, suppose that
p\mid\chi
.
It follows from statement (1) that
p=2
and
|E|
is odd.
Since
|G|
divides
4|E|
, the Sylow
2
-subgroups of
G
have order dividing
4
.
Explicitly, Sylow
2
-subgroups are cyclic or dihedral.

Now, suppose that
p\nmid\chi
.
We claim that exists
\omega\in V\cup E\cup F
such that
p\nmid\frac{|G|}{|G_{\omega}|}
.
Otherwise,
p
divides the length of each
G
-orbit on
V\cup E\cup F
, and so
p
divides
|V|-|E|+|F|=\chi
which is the sum all
G
-orbits length.
Contradiction comes to
p\nmid\gcd(\chi,|G|)
, and so the claim is proved.
Therefore Sylow
p
-subgroups of
G
are isomorphic to a subgroup of
G_{\omega}
by Sylow’s theorem.
So they are cyclic or dihedral as
G_{\omega}
is cyclic or dihedral for each
\omega\in V\cup E\cup F
, as in statement (2).

Let
\ell={\rm lcm}\left(|G_{\omega}|\mid\omega\in V\cup E\cup F\right)
and
d=\gcd\left(|G|/|G_{\omega}|\mid\omega\in V\cup E\cup F\right)
.
Then
|G|=\ell d
, and the greatest common divisor
d
divides
\gcd(\chi,|G|)
.

Assume that
d\neq 1
.
By statement (1),
\gcd(\chi,|G|)
is
2
or
4
, and
|G|=2|E|
or
4|E|
where the edge number
|E|
is odd.
There follows two cases as below.

(a)

If
|G|=4|E|
, then
\mathcal{M}
is a regular map which implies that edge stabilizers in
G
are isomorphic to
\mathrm{D}_{4}
.
Hence
4
divides
\ell
, and so
d=1
which is a contradiction.

(b)

If
|G|=2|E|
, then
d=2
and
\ell=|E|
is odd.
Therefore
|G_{\omega}|
is odd for each
\omega\in V\cup E\cup F
.
A contradiction comes to that
|E|
is the sum of all lengths of
G
-orbits on
E
, which are all even.

Above all,
d=1
and so
G=\ell
as in statement (3).

\Box

Regular maps on orientable surface of genus
p+1
, where
p
is prime, have been classified
[
4
]
.
In particular, automorphism groups of regular maps
\mathcal{M}
with order coprime to
\chi/2
are determined.
Here by part (1) of Lemma
3.2
, if a
G
-arc-transitive map
\mathcal{M}
satisfies
\gcd(\chi,|E|)=1

4.
Almost Sylow-cyclic groups

A group is called an

2
-subgroup contains an index

Non-solvable almost Sylow-cyclic groups were completely classified by Suzuki

The authors of this paper presents an explicitly classification of solvable almost Sylow-cyclic groups, extending Theorem 4.1 of

Theorem 4.1

(
[

]
)

Let

be an indecomposable solvable finite group of which each Sylow subgroup is cyclic or dihedral.
Then

is one of the following five types:

(I)

G=(\langle g\rangle{:}\langle h\rangle)
, where
\mathrm{C}_{\langle h\rangle}(g)\leqslant\Phi(\langle h\rangle)

2
-subgroups of

are dihedral.
Let

\langle u,v\rangle=G_{2}
and

(II)

G=(\langle g\rangle\times\langle g_{v}\rangle){:}(\langle u,v\rangle\times\langle h\rangle)

and
|h|

\mathrm{C}_{\langle h\rangle}(gg_{v})\leqslant\Phi(\langle h\rangle)

\langle u,v\rangle=\mathrm{D}_{2^{e}}
with

such that

(g,g_{v})^{v}=(g,g_{v}^{-1})\text{and}(g,g_{v})^{u}=(g,g_{v}),

where

;

(III)

(IV)

Automorphism groups of cyclic groups and dihedral groups are well known.
For convenience, we provide the definitions below.

Definition 4.2

\Box

5.

In this section, we always assume that

Suppose that

G

Now assume

\Box

z\in\langle u,v\rangle
, so

z=w^{m}u

for some integer
m
.
Let

x=g_{u}^{i_{1}}g_{v}^{j_{1}}g^{k_{1}}w^{\ell_{1}}u,\ y=g_{u}^{i_{2}}g_{v}^{j_{2}}g^{k_{2}}w^{\ell_{2}}u,

where
i_{1},j_{1},k_{1},\ell_{1},i_{2},j_{2},k_{2},\ell_{2}
are integers.
If both
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
are even, then
g_{v}^{j_{1}}=g_{v}^{j_{2}}=1
. However, this leads to
\langle x,y,z\rangle\leqslant\langle g_{u},g,u,v\rangle<G
, a contradiction.
Similarly, both
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
being odd also yields a contradiction.
Hence, after relabeling, we may take
\ell_{1}
even and
\ell_{2}
odd.
This implies
g_{v}^{j_{1}}=1
and
g_{u}^{i_{2}}=1
, simplifying to:

x=g_{u}^{i_{1}}g^{k_{1}}w^{\ell_{1}}u,\quad y=g_{v}^{j_{2}}g^{k_{2}}w^{\ell_{2}}u.

Since
\langle x,y,z\rangle\leqslant\langle g_{u}^{i_{1}},g^{k_{1}},w^{\ell_{1}},u,g_{v}^{j_{2}},g^{k_{2}},w^{\ell_{2}}u,w^{m}u\rangle=G

\gcd(i_{1},|g_{u}|)=\gcd(j_{2},|g_{v}|)=\gcd(k_{1},k_{2})=1.

By Theorem
4.1
,

{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)_{(L)}\geqslant{\mathrm{Aut}}(H)\text{ and }{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)_{L}\cap{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)_{(H)}\geqslant\langle\xi^{2}\rangle{:}\langle\mu\rangle.

Consequently, the triple
(x,y,z)
is equivalent to either

(g_{u}g^{k_{1}}w^{\ell_{1}}u,g_{v}g^{k_{2}}v,u)\text{ or }(g_{u}g^{k_{1}}w^{\ell_{1}}u,g_{v}g^{k_{2}}v,v),

as stated in part (i).

Now we verify whether the above triples generate
G
.
First, consider the triple
(x,y,z)=(g_{u}g^{k_{1}}w^{\ell_{1}}u,\,g_{v}g^{k_{2}}v,\,u)
, where
\ell_{1}
is even.
Using the conjugation relation
(g_{u},g_{v},g)^{w}=(g_{u}^{-1},g_{v}^{-1},g)
and the condition
\gcd(k_{1},k_{2})=1
, we compute:

\displaystyle\langle x,y,z\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle g^{k_{1}},\,g^{k_{2}},\,g_{u},\,w^{\ell_{1}},\,g_{v}w^{-1},\,u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle g,g_{u},\,w^{\ell_{1}},\,g_{v}w^{-1},\,u\rangle.

Observe that the quotient group

G=\langle x,y,z\rangle/\langle g,g_{u}\rangle\cong\langle w^{\ell_{1}},\,g_{u}w^{-1},\,u\rangle\cong\langle u,v\rangle

forces
g_{v}=1
.
A similar argument shows
g_{u}=1
for the cases that
(x,y,z)
is equivalent to
(g_{u}g^{k_{1}}w^{\ell_{1}}u,\,g_{v}g^{k_{2}}v,\,v)
.

Conversely, if
g_{v}=1
(respectively
g_{u}=1
), the triple
(g_{u}g^{k_{1}}w^{\ell_{1}}u,\,g^{k_{2}}v,\,u)
(respectively
(g_{u}g^{k_{1}}w^{\ell_{1}}u,\,g^{k_{2}}v,\,v)
) indeed generates
G
, by the argument above.

Thus,
G
has a reversing triple
(x,y,z)
if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

g_{v}=1
, and the triple is equivalent to
(g_{u}g^{k_{1}}w^{\ell_{1}}u,\,g^{k_{2}}v,\,u)
;

(2)

g_{u}=1
, and the triple is equivalent to
(g^{k_{1}}w^{\ell_{1}}u,\,g_{v}g^{k_{2}}v,\,v)
,

where
\gcd(k_{1},k_{2})=1
,
\ell_{1}=2i
is even.

The number of inequivalent reversing triples is given by:

\frac{|\{(k_{1},k_{2})\mid\gcd(k_{1},k_{2})=1\}|}{\varphi(|g|)}\cdot 2^{e-2}.

Case 2.

Suppose that
(a,z)
is a rotary pair of
G
.
By Sylow’s theorem, we may assume that
z\in\langle u,v\rangle
, so that

for some integer

.
Without loss of generality,

are integers.

\gcd(i,|g_{u}|)=\gcd(j,|g_{v}|)=\gcd(k,|g|)=\gcd(\ell,|h|)=1\ \text{and}\ \langle t,\rho^{m}u\rangle=\langle u,v\rangle,

so that
\langle a,z\rangle\leqslant\langle g_{u}^{i},g_{v}^{j},g^{k},t,w^{m}u,h^{\ell}\rangle=G
.
Here by Theorem
4.1
,

{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)_{(L)}\geqslant{\mathrm{Aut}}(H)\text{ and }{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)_{L}\cap{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)_{(H)}\geqslant\langle\xi^{2}\rangle{:}\langle\mu\rangle,

where

and
L=\langle u,v\rangle\times\langle h\rangle
.
The pair
(a,z)
is equivalent to

(g_{u}g_{v}gth^{\ell},z),

where
\gcd(\ell,|h|)=1
and
(t,z)\in\{(w^{i},u),(w^{i},v),(w^{2i}v,u),(w^{2i}u,v)\}
with

being an odd integer.

Next, we verify whether these pairs generate
G
.
The key observation is that
G^{\prime}=\langle[a,z]\rangle
, which holds because

\langle[a,z]\rangle{\sf\,char\,}\langle g_{u}g_{v}g\rangle\times\langle w^{2}\rangle\lhd G.

Suppose
(a,z)=(g_{u}g_{v}gw^{i}h^{\ell},u)
with

Then

, and the abelian quotient

This implies

since
\gcd(|G^{\prime}|,|g_{v}|)=1
.
From the definition of
g_{v}
, it follows that
g_{v}=1
.
Similarly, if

must hold.

Conversely, if
g_{u}=1
(respectively
g_{v}=1

(g_{v}gw^{i}h^{\ell},u)
and
(g_{v}gw^{2i}vh^{\ell},u)
(respectively
(g_{u}gw^{i}h^{\ell},v)
and

G

In summary,

admits a rotary pair
(a,z)
if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

, and
(a,z)
is equivalent to
(g_{u}gwh^{\ell},u)

(g_{u}gw^{2}vh^{\ell},u)

(2)

\frac{2\varphi(|h|)}{|\tau_{h_{0}}|}

\Box

Abstract
. We consider a three-dimensional domain occupied by a homogeneous, incompressible, non-Newtonian, heat-conducting fluid with prescribed nonuniform temperature on the boundary and no-slip boundary conditions for the velocity. No external body forces are assumed. The constitutive relation for the Cauchy stress tensor is assumed in a general form that includes, in particular, the power-law and Ladyzhenskaya models with the power-law exponent in the range where neither regularity, uniqueness, nor the validity of the energy equality is known to hold. Nevertheless, we introduce a novel concept of solution suitable for this setting, which enables us to establish the existence of global-in-time solutions for arbitrary physically relevant initial data. A remarkable feature of this formulation is that the steady-state solution is nonlinearly stable: every such solution converges, in a suitable sense, to the steady state as time tends to infinity. This provides the first result that combines existence with long-time stability in this physically relevant yet mathematically challenging regime.

1.
Introduction

We are interested in a modified Navier–Stokes–Fourier system with nonlinear viscosity

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bf v}+\operatorname{div}({\bf v}\otimes{\bf v})-\operatorname{div}\mathbf{S}

\displaystyle=-\nabla p,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}{\bf v}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\vartheta+\operatorname{div}(\vartheta{\bf v})-\operatorname{div}(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}

in
Q:=(0,\infty)\times\Omega
with Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. The system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) is completed by the initial conditions

(1.4)

{\bf v}(0,\cdot)={\bf v}_{0},\quad\vartheta(0,\cdot)=\vartheta_{0}\qquad\textrm{ in }\Omega

and by the boundary conditions

(1.5)

{\bf v}={\bf 0},\quad\vartheta=\hat{\vartheta}\qquad\textrm{ on }(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega.

Here,
{\bf v}
is the velocity field,
p
is the pressure and
\vartheta
is the temperature. The material is described by the heat conductivity
\kappa:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and by the heat capacity. For simplicity and for the clarity of the presentation we consider the heat capacity being equal to one. Further, we need to specify the constitutively determined part of the Cauchy stress
\mathbf{S}
, that is supposed to be a tensorial function of the temperature
\vartheta
and the symmetric velocity gradient
\mathbf{D}{\bf v}:=(\nabla{\bf v}+(\nabla{\bf v})^{T})/2
, i.e., there exists
\mathbf{S}^{*}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}_{\textrm{sym}}\to\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}_{\textrm{sym}}
such that

(1.6)

\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{S}^{*}(\vartheta,\mathbf{D}{\bf v})\qquad\textrm{ in }Q.

We assume that the mapping
\mathbf{S}^{*}:(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}_{sym}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}_{sym}
is continuous and fulfills the following growth hypothesis
with some positive constants
\underline{\kappa},\overline{\kappa},\delta>0
and
p\in[8/5,11/5)

(1.7)

\begin{gathered}\underline{\kappa}\leq\kappa(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\kappa},\qquad|\mathbf{S}^{*}(\vartheta,\mathbf{D})|\leq\overline{\kappa}(\delta+|\mathbf{D}|)^{p-1},\qquad|\mathbf{S}^{*}(\vartheta,{\bf 0})|=0,\\
\underline{\kappa}(\delta+|\mathbf{D}|)^{p-2}|\mathbf{D}|^{2}\leq\mathbf{S}^{*}(\vartheta,\mathbf{D}):\mathbf{D}.\end{gathered}

For the proof of existence of a solution we also need that
\mathbf{S}^{*}
is monotone with respect to the second variable, i.e., that for all
\vartheta>0
,
\mathbf{D}_{1},\mathbf{D}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}_{sym}
,
\mathbf{D}_{1}\neq\mathbf{D}_{2}
there holds

(1.8)

(\mathbf{S}^{*}(\vartheta,\mathbf{D}_{1})-\mathbf{S}^{*}(\vartheta,\mathbf{D}_{2})):(\mathbf{D}_{1}-\mathbf{D}_{2})\geq 0.

Note that the prototypic relation
\mathbf{S}^{*}(\vartheta,\mathbf{D})=\nu(\vartheta)(\delta+|\mathbf{D}|)^{p-2}\mathbf{D}
with smooth bounded positive function
\nu
bounded away from zero falls into the class (
1.7
) and (
1.8
).

To simplify the notation, we consider for the
stationary
boundary data
\hat{\vartheta}
that it is extended into
\Omega
such that the extension is the expected asymptotic solution, i.e., we assume that for some constants
\underline{\vartheta},\overline{\vartheta}>0

(1.9)

\hat{\vartheta}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega),\quad 0<\underline{\vartheta}\leq\hat{\vartheta}\leq\overline{\vartheta},\quad\mbox{ and for all }\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\hat{\vartheta})\nabla\hat{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}{x}=0.

The initial conditions are considered to have finite energy, i.e.,

(1.10)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|{\bf v}_{0}|^{2}}{2}+\vartheta_{0}\,{\rm d}{x}<\infty

and to satisfy the standard compatibility conditions

(1.11)

\operatorname{div}{\bf v}_{0}=0,\;\vartheta_{0}>\underline{\vartheta}\textrm{ in }\Omega\qquad\textrm{ and }\qquad{\bf v}_{0}\cdot{\bf n}=0\textrm{ on }\partial\Omega,

where
{\bf n}
denotes the unit outward normal vector on
\partial\Omega
.

It is seen that
({\bf 0},\hat{\vartheta})
is the only steady state of the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.5
). We want to study its nonlinear stability, i.e., we are interested under which conditions and for which class of solutions this steady state
attracts
in some sense all such solutions. Furthermore, we want to be sure that the class of relevant solutions is not empty.

As it is common in the theory of complicated systems arising in the theory for the heat conducting fluids, see
[
7
,
1
,
2
,
28
,
26
,
27
]
, distributional solutions need not be sufficiently regular to guarantee expected properties such as the stability of steady states. For this reason, additional conditions must be incorporated into the definition of a solution. These are typically formulated as inequalities that capture the behavior of key physical quantities such as the total energy, the internal energy, the kinetic energy and the entropy. Note that in the case of the Navier–Stokes system (the system without the temperature effects), such a condition is the classical (kinetic)
energy inequality
. In the present work, we seek suitable variants of such constraints to develop a complete stability theory for the steady-state regime. Specifically, we replace the heat equation with the entropy inequality, supplemented by a modified version of the total energy inequality. This latter inequality is nonstandard, as it includes a correction term that accounts for the heat flux through the boundary. This modification is necessary because we impose a nonhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition on the temperature, implying that the system is not isolated and may exchange energy with its surroundings.
The precise definition of a weak solution is presented later. We show the global-in-time and large-data existence of such solutions and prove that they all converge to the unique steady state as time
t\to+\infty
.

Very similar problems were studied in
[
17
,
23
,
1
,
2
]
. However, the situations considered there were significantly simpler: either the existence of a hypothetical smooth solution was assumed, or the results were restricted to the so-called subcritical regimes. In such settings, one may directly apply energy equalities and classical renormalization techniques, and the internal energy equality is equivalent to the entropy equality. Furthermore, for given temperature, we have the uniqueness of the velocity field, see
[
16
]
. Nonlinear stability of the steady state can then be deduced using these tools. In contrast, the methods and techniques developed in the aforementioned works are not applicable in the supercritical setting, which is, in fact, the most natural regime in the context of incompressible fluids. For instance, the classical three-dimensional Navier–-Stokes-–Fourier system falls outside the scope of the results presented in these papers.

As the main contribution of the present work, we introduce a new notion of solution (see Definition
1.1
), for which we prove global-in-time existence for arbitrary initial data with finite energy (Theorem
1.3
). Most importantly, we establish the nonlinear stability of the steady state for this system (Theorem
1.4
). It is remarkable that the new concept of a solution introduced in Definition
1.1
can, in a certain sense, also be identified with the classical notion of suitable weak solutions introduced in
[
20
]
.

Furthermore, we observe that some additional property must be required of the solution in order to guarantee stability, since it is known that weak solutions, or even Leray–Hopf solutions, need not be unique for shear-thinning fluids, see
[
19
]
. In contrast, the authors of
[
4
]
introduced another general concept of solution for shear-thinning fluids, for which they also established an existence result. Moreover, they obtained a conditional uniqueness result, which may be understood as a first step toward stability. However, all of this holds under the hypothesis that a sufficiently regular solution exists. Such a result in the three-dimensional setting is not generally known; at present we only have small-data regularity results for shear-thinning fluids, see
[
6
,
11
]
.

A related stability result was also recently established in
[
25
]
(see
[
29
]
for the existence results), where the authors study an even more complex problem involving a compressible fluid. Their model allows not only the transfer of kinetic energy into heat, but also the reverse process. In their case, the steady state is not necessarily unique in general. Therefore, they assume that the boundary data are close to a constant steady solution, which, together with the smallness of the potential driving force, guarantees the uniqueness of the steady state. Another key difference compared to our work is that the authors consider a more restrictive class of weak solutions. Specifically, they require that the solution satisfies a relative energy inequality directly, which is not the case of this paper.
We also note that a related problem for the compressible magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) system was recently initiated in
[
24
]
.

Our system may also be compared to the Oberbeck–Boussinesq approximation; however, it differs from it in essential aspects. The Oberbeck–Boussinesq system does not account for the dissipation of kinetic energy in the heat equation, which is the main difficulty in our analysis. On the other hand, it includes the buoyancy force in the balance of linear momentum, which is the term responsible for transferring heat into kinetic energy. At present, we are not able to handle this term.

A variant of the Oberbeck–Boussinesq system with nonhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions was derived in
[
10
]
by a singular limit passage from the full compressible system. Surprisingly, a new boundary condition appeared, involving a nonlocal boundary term. The existence of solutions to this new system was established in
[
5
]
, and nonlinear stability for small data was proved in
[
30
]
. We emphasize that the analysis in these works differs essentially from ours, since the inclusion of dissipative heating in the heat equation introduces many additional difficulties. In particular, the heat equation must be treated as an equation with an
L^{1}
right-hand side.
In this context, existence results for the Oberbeck–Boussinesq approximation with additional dissipative heating are already available: see
[
33
]
for the derivation of the model and
[
3
]
for the corresponding existence result. However, for such a complicated system no further analysis is available to date, and we plan to address this in forthcoming papers.

The rest of the introductory part is given to the introduction of the notation we use and also for the main result we get.

1.2.
Definition of the notion of solution

Let us now turn to the definition of our concept of solution. Since we aim to address the so-called supercritical case, we cannot expect a solution that satisfies (
1.3
). We are inspired by similar approaches for compressible fluids
[
31
]
and also in the incompressible setting, see
[
14
,
18
]
. Instead of the internal energy identity (
1.3
), we consider the entropy “equality”, which is derived by dividing (
1.3
) by
\vartheta
. Defining the entropy
\eta
as

\eta:=\ln\vartheta,

this procedure leads to

(1.12)

\partial_{t}\eta+\operatorname{div}(\eta{\bf v})-\operatorname{div}(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\eta)=\frac{\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}}{\vartheta}+\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

Similarly, we define the entropy associated with the stationary temperature
\hat{\vartheta}
as
\hat{\eta}=\ln\hat{\vartheta}
. It is important to note that
\eta
has much better integrability properties than
\vartheta
, which is the main reason for working with it. Nevertheless, we are still unable to handle the equality in (
1.12
) and therefore we replace it by an inequality “
\geq
”, which is natural from the perspective of the second law of thermodynamics.

However, in doing so we lose some information, and the system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) together with the inequality (
1.12
) does not constitute a complete problem. Therefore, an additional restriction is required in order to formulate a well-posed problem. One natural possibility is to introduce the kinetic and total energy (in)equalities.

The kinetic energy identity is obtained by taking the scalar product of (
1.1
) with
{\bf v}
, which yields

(1.13)

\partial_{t}\left(\frac{|{\bf v}|^{2}}{2}\right)+\operatorname{div}\left({\bf v}\left(p+\frac{|{\bf v}|^{2}}{2}\right)\right)-\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{S}{\bf v})+\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}=0.

Furthermore, by adding (
1.13
) to (
1.3
) we obtain the total energy balance

(1.14)

\partial_{t}\left(\vartheta+\frac{|{\bf v}|^{2}}{2}\right)+\operatorname{div}\left({\bf v}\left(\vartheta+p+\frac{|{\bf v}|^{2}}{2}\right)\right)-\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{S}{\bf v})-\operatorname{div}(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta)=0.

The classical approach is to integrate the above identity over
\Omega
and to incorporate it, together with (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) and the inequality (
1.12
), as part of the solution concept. This indeed leads to a well-defined problem in the case of Neumann boundary conditions for
\vartheta
, see
[
31
,
14
,
18
]
.
However, for Dirichlet boundary conditions such a procedure is not possible. After integration over
\Omega
and applying integration by parts, one obtains the additional boundary term

\int_{\partial\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot{\bf n}\,\mathrm{d}S,

which cannot be controlled. Therefore, (
1.14
) must be properly modified, and a different concept needs to be introduced. To this end, we first introduce the notion of the correction function.

Definition 1.1

(
\gamma
-correction function)
.

Let
\gamma\in(-1,0)
. We say that

b\in\mathcal{C}^{1}((0,+\infty)\times[\underline{\vartheta},\overline{\vartheta}])

is a
\gamma
-correction function if there exists
C>0
such that
b
and its primitive function with respect to the first variable

(1.15)

B(s,\tilde{s})=\int_{\underline{\vartheta}}^{s}b(\sigma,\tilde{s})\,{\rm d}\sigma,\quad\text{for }s\in(0,+\infty)\text{ and }\tilde{s}\in[\underline{\vartheta},\overline{\vartheta}],

satisfy, for
s\geq\underline{\vartheta}
and
\tilde{s}\in[\underline{\vartheta},\overline{\vartheta}]
,

(1.16)

\displaystyle 0\leq b(s,\tilde{s})

\displaystyle\leq Cs^{\gamma},

\displaystyle b(\tilde{s},\tilde{s})

\displaystyle=1,

(1.17)

\displaystyle|\partial_{2}b(s,\tilde{s})|

\displaystyle\leq Cs^{\gamma},

\displaystyle|\partial_{2}B(s,\tilde{s})|

\displaystyle\leq Cs^{1+\gamma},

(1.18)

\displaystyle-Cs^{\gamma-1}\leq\partial_{1}b(s,\tilde{s})

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Note that it follows directly from the above definition that there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for all
s\geq\underline{\vartheta}
and all
\tilde{s}\in[\underline{\vartheta},\overline{\vartheta}]
,

(1.19)

|B(s,\tilde{s})|\leq Cs^{1+\gamma}.

The typical example of the
\gamma
-correction function is the following function

(1.20)

b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})=\left(\frac{\hat{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\right)^{\alpha},\quad B(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})=\frac{\hat{\vartheta}^{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\left(\vartheta^{1-\alpha}-\underline{\vartheta}^{1-\alpha}\right),

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. In this setting we have that
b
satisfies Definition
1.1
with
\gamma=-\alpha
.

The main motivation for introducing the
\gamma
-correction function is the following. If we multiply (
1.3
) by
b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})
and use the fact that

\hat{\vartheta}
is time-independent, then

(1.21)

\begin{split}&\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})=\partial_{t}\vartheta\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\vartheta\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})-b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\,\operatorname{div}(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta)\\
&\quad=\partial_{t}B(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\vartheta\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})-\operatorname{div}\left(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\right)+\,\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\big(b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})).\end{split}

Finally, let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a nonnegative function such that
\varphi\equiv 1
on
\partial\Omega
. Multiplying (
1.21
) by
\varphi
, adding the result to (
1.14
), and performing a straightforward algebraic manipulation, we obtain

(1.22)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\left(\vartheta-\varphi B(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})+\frac{|{\bf v}|^{2}}{2}\right)+\left(\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})-\,\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}\partial_{1}b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\right)\varphi=\\
&\quad-\operatorname{div}\left({\bf v}\left(\vartheta+p+\frac{|{\bf v}|^{2}}{2}\right)\right)+\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{S}{\bf v})+\operatorname{div}\left(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta(1-\varphi b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta}))\right)\\
&\quad+\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\varphi\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})+\,\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\hat{\vartheta}\partial_{2}b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\varphi+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\vartheta\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\end{split}

The above identity is a natural candidate for the final restriction on the solution. First, using (
1.7
), (
1.16
), (
1.18
), and the fact that
\varphi\geq 0
, one observes that the last term on the left-hand side is nonnegative. Second, since
{\bf v}={\bf 0}
,
\varphi=1
, and
b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})=1
on
\partial\Omega
, we may integrate (
1.22
) over
\Omega

1.14

b
- corrected total energy inequality
(after discarding terms with the correct sign)

(1.23)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}\vartheta-\varphi B(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})+\frac{|{\bf v}|^{2}}{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\right)\leq\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\varphi\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})+\,\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\hat{\vartheta}\partial_{2}b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\varphi+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\vartheta\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\,{\rm d}{x}\end{split}

and this is the key point for the definition of a proper notion of solution. Thus, we complement (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) and the inequality (
1.12
) by additionally requiring (
1.23
).
We are now in a position to introduce the rigorous notion of solution.

1.3.
Main results

The first result concerns the existence of global-in-time and large-data

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

The key novelties of this result are several. First, we are able to prove the existence for parameters

For our second result (the stability of the steady state) we first introduce auxiliary functions defined for

(1.32)

\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(s):=\int_{1}^{s}(\mathcal{G}(z))^{-\alpha}\,{\rm d}z,\quad\mathcal{G}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\kappa(z)\,{\rm d}z,\quad f_{\alpha}(s,t):=s-t-(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(s)-\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(t))(\mathcal{G}(t))^{\alpha}.

These functions are necessary due to the nonconstant heat capacity

Here we have used the functions

.

Let

and

,

,
\underline{\vartheta}
,

-weak solution satisfies for all

Moreover, let

Please note that the statement of the theorem is almost identical to the main theorem in
[

2.
Existence of

In this section, we provide a complete proof of existence. As many steps parallel the procedure presented in

]

2.1.
Faedo–Galerkin approximations

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a pair
({\bf v}^{n},\vartheta^{n})
solving for almost all
t\in(0,+\infty)
the following system

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}[\partial_{t}{\bf v}^{n}\cdot{\bf w}_{j}-({\bf v}^{n}\otimes{\bf v}^{n}):\nabla{\bf w}_{j}+\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf w}_{j}]\,{\rm d}{x}=0\qquad\mbox{ for all }j=1,\dots,n,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle\partial_{t}\vartheta^{n},\zeta\rangle+\int_{\Omega}[-\vartheta^{n}{\bf v}^{n}\cdot\nabla\zeta+\kappa(\vartheta^{n})\nabla\vartheta^{n}\cdot\nabla\zeta]\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}\zeta\,{\rm d}{x}\\
&\qquad\mbox{ for all }\zeta\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega),\end{aligned}

complemented by the initial conditions
{\bf v}^{n}(0,\cdot)={\bf v}^{n}_{0}
,
\vartheta^{n}(0,\cdot)=\vartheta_{0}^{n}
.
The functions
\{{\bf w}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
constitute the basis of Hilbert space
W^{3,2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{2}_{0,\operatorname{div}}
orthonormal in
L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
,
see
[
36
, Appendix 6.4]
, and the function
{\bf v}^{n}
is a linear combination of first
n
elements of the basis and
\mathbf{S}^{n}
is just shortcut for composed function, i.e.

(2.3)

{\bf v}^{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c^{n}_{i}(t){\bf w}_{i}(x)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathbf{S}^{n}:=\mathbf{S}^{*}(\vartheta^{n},\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}).

The function
{\bf v}^{n}_{0}
is the projection of
{\bf v}_{0}
onto linear hull of
\{{\bf w}_{1},\dots,{\bf w}_{n}\}
in
L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
\vartheta^{n}_{0}
is a suitable mollification of
\vartheta_{0}
, for details see
[
14
, Appendix B, (B.2)-(B.3)]
. They satisfy

(2.4)

{\bf v}^{n}_{0}\to{\bf v}_{0}\quad\mbox{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$,}\quad\vartheta_{0}^{n}\to\vartheta_{0}\quad\mbox{in $L^{1}(\Omega)$ as $n\to+\infty$,}\quad\underline{\vartheta}\leq\vartheta_{0}^{n}\quad\mbox{a.e. in $\Omega$.}

The approximations
({\bf v}^{n},\vartheta^{n})
possess for all
T>0
the following regularity

(2.5)

\displaystyle{\bf v}^{n}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];W^{3,2}(\Omega))

(2.6)

\displaystyle\vartheta^{n}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)),\quad\partial_{t}\vartheta^{n}\in L^{2}(0,T;(W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}),\quad\vartheta^{n}\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)).

In addition, the function
\vartheta^{n}
attains the boundary conditions
\hat{\vartheta}
, i.e.,
\vartheta^{n}-\hat{\vartheta}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))
and fulfills the minimum principle

(2.7)

\vartheta^{n}\geq\underline{\vartheta}>0\mbox{ a.e. in }Q.

We do not present any proof of the existence of
({\bf v}^{n},\vartheta^{n})
. Instead, we refer a reader to the article
[
14
, Appendix B]
, where a similar problem is treated.

2.2.
Inequalities for approximations

Here we derive inequalities for approximations of solutions which, after the limit passage
n\to+\infty
, yield the required properties of the constructed weak solution. In Sections
2.2.2
,
2.2.3
and
2.4
we need to apply the chain rule to the time derivative of
\vartheta^{n}
, which is defined only in a dual space. This can be achieved by employing the method of Steklov averages. For details of this argument, we refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
]
.

2.2.1.
Kinetic energy inequality

Let us multiply (
2.1
) by
c^{n}_{i}
, sum over
i=1,\dots,n
, use the fact that
\operatorname{div}{\bf v}^{n}=0
, then multiply by
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(0,+\infty)
and integrate over the time interval
(0,+\infty)
. This yields the kinetic energy equality for the Galerkin system

(2.8)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}-|{\bf v}^{n}|^{2}\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}+2\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\right)\,{\rm d}t=0.

By the regularity of
{\bf v}^{n}
in (
2.5
), relation (
2.8
) further implies

(2.9)

\|{\bf v}^{n}(T)\|_{2}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\|{\bf v}^{n}_{0}\|_{2}^{2},

for all
T>0
.

2.2.2.
Total energy balance

Next, let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Omega)
be nonnegative and satisfy
\varphi=1
on
\partial\Omega
. We then set

\zeta:=1-b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi

in (
2.2
). Such a choice is legitimate since
b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})=1
on
\partial\Omega
, and by the regularity of
b
and
\vartheta^{n}
we conclude that for any
t>0
,
\zeta(t)\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Substituting this test function into (
2.2
), we obtain

(2.10)

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\vartheta^{n},1-b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\rangle+\int_{\Omega}-\vartheta^{n}{\bf v}^{n}\cdot\nabla\!\left[1-b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\right]+\kappa(\vartheta^{n})\nabla\vartheta^{n}\cdot\nabla\!\left[1-b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\right]\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}\,[1-b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi]\,{\rm d}{x}.

Finally, let
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(0,+\infty)
be arbitrary. We multiply the above identity by
\psi
and integrate with respect to
t\in(0,+\infty)
. In what follows, we evaluate all resulting integrals.

Using the definition (
1.15
), the fact that
\hat{\vartheta}
is time-independent, and an integration by parts, the time derivative term becomes

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\langle\partial_{t}\vartheta^{n},[1-b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi]\,\psi\rangle\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}(\vartheta^{n}-B(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi)\,\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

For the second term in (
2.10
), we use the divergence-free condition for
{\bf v}^{n}
, the zero trace of
{\bf v}^{n}
, and the integration by parts to conclude

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}-\vartheta^{n}{\bf v}^{n}\cdot\nabla\!\left[1-b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\right]\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{n}{\bf v}^{n}\cdot\nabla\!\left[b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\right]\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}{\bf v}^{n}\cdot\nabla\vartheta^{n}\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

Finally the elliptic term is evaluated as

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta^{n})\nabla\vartheta^{n}\cdot\nabla[1-b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi]\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt\hfill=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta^{n})\nabla\vartheta^{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta^{n})|\nabla\vartheta^{n}|^{2}\,\partial_{1}b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt\hfill-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta^{n})\nabla\vartheta^{n}\cdot\nabla\hat{\vartheta}\,\partial_{2}b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{array}

Collecting all the terms we deduce the final identity

(2.11)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}(\vartheta^{n}-B(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi)\,\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}{\bf v}^{n}\cdot\nabla\vartheta^{n}b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta^{n})\nabla\vartheta^{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta^{n})|\nabla\vartheta^{n}|^{2}\,\partial_{1}b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta^{n})\nabla\vartheta^{n}\cdot\nabla\hat{\vartheta}\,\partial_{2}b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}[1-b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi]\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

We sum the last outcome for the temperature balance (
2.11
) with the kinetic energy balance (
2.8
) and obtain

(2.12)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{|{\bf v}^{n}|^{2}}{2}+\vartheta^{n}-B(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi\right]\,\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}{\bf v}^{n}\cdot\nabla\vartheta^{n}b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta^{n})\nabla\vartheta^{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\psi+\kappa(\vartheta^{n})\nabla\vartheta^{n}\cdot\nabla\hat{\vartheta}\,\partial_{2}b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})-\kappa(\vartheta^{n})|\nabla\vartheta^{n}|^{2}\,\partial_{1}b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\right)\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=0.\end{array}

2.2.3.
Entropy balance

We set
\zeta:=\varphi/\vartheta^{n}
as a test function in (

), where

is arbitrary, and directly obtain the following identity for the approximate entropy

\begin{split}&\langle\partial_{t}\eta^{n},\varphi\rangle+\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta^{n})\nabla\eta^{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi-\eta^{n}\,{\bf v}^{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}^{n}}{\vartheta^{n}}\,\varphi+\kappa(\vartheta^{n})|\nabla\eta^{n}|^{2}\,\varphi\,{\rm d}{x}.\end{split}

Compare
[
1
, Section 3, Part B]
.

2.3.

2.3.1.
A priori

Following, for instance,

\displaystyle\|{\bf v}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{0,\operatorname{div}})}+\|{\bf v}^{n}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,p}_{0,\operatorname{div}})}+\|\mathbf{S}^{n}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Q_{T})}+\|{\bf v}^{n}\|_{L^{\frac{5p}{3}}(Q_{T})}

\displaystyle\leq C,

(2.15)

\displaystyle\|\vartheta^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}\vartheta^{n}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;(W^{2,3}_{0}(\Omega))^{*})}+\|\partial_{t}{\bf v}^{n}\|_{L^{\frac{5p}{6}}(0,T;(W^{1,\frac{5p}{5p-6}}_{0,\operatorname{div}})^{*})}

\displaystyle\|(\vartheta^{n})^{a}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))}+\|\vartheta^{n}\|_{L^{r}(Q_{T})}+\|\tilde{\vartheta}^{n}\|_{L^{s}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,s}(\Omega))}

2.3.2.
Uniform
T
-independent estimates for
\vartheta

In this section, we show that the temperature
\vartheta
satisfies (
1.31
).
For arbitrary
m\in\mathbb{N}
we define the function
F_{m}(s)
as follows

F_{m}(s):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&&\textrm{for }s\leq\overline{\vartheta},\\
&m(s-\overline{\vartheta})&&\textrm{for }s\in(\overline{\vartheta},\overline{\vartheta}+1/m),\\
&1&&\textrm{for }s\geq\overline{\vartheta}+1/m\end{aligned}\right.

and its primitive function
P_{m}(s)
as

P_{m}(s):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&&\textrm{for }s\leq\overline{\vartheta},\\
&\frac{m(s-\overline{\vartheta})^{2}}{2}&&\textrm{for }s\in(\overline{\vartheta},\overline{\vartheta}+1/m),\\
&s-\overline{\vartheta}-1/(2m)&&\textrm{for }s\geq\overline{\vartheta}+1/m.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that since
\hat{\vartheta}\leq\overline{\vartheta}
and
\vartheta^{n}=\hat{\vartheta}
on
\partial\Omega
, we have that
F_{m}(\vartheta^{n})\in L^{2}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega))
and thus we can set
\zeta:=F_{m}(\vartheta^{n})
in (
2.2
). Doing so and using the fact that
\operatorname{div}{\bf v}^{n}=0
, we obtain the identity

\partial_{t}\int_{\Omega}P_{m}(\vartheta^{n})\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta^{n})F_{m}^{\prime}(\vartheta^{n})|\nabla\vartheta^{n}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}F_{m}(\vartheta^{n})\,{\rm d}{x}.

Since
F_{m}
is nondecreasing, the second term on the left-hand side is nonnegative and can be neglected. Similarly, the term on the right-hand side can always be estimated by
\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}
since
F_{m}\leq 1
. Integrating the resulting inequality over time
t\in(0,\tau)
, and using (
2.9
) along with the properties of
P_{m}(s)
, we obtain for all
\tau\in(0,\infty)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}P_{m}(\vartheta^{n}(\tau))\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}P_{m}(\vartheta_{0}^{n})\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}\,{\rm d}{x}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}(\vartheta_{0}^{n}-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|{\bf v}^{n}_{0}|^{2}+(\vartheta_{0}^{n}-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\,{\rm d}{x}.

Letting
m\to\infty
in the above inequality, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}(\vartheta^{n}(\tau)-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|{\bf v}^{n}_{0}|^{2}+(\vartheta_{0}^{n}-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\,{\rm d}{x},

and consequently, letting
n\to\infty
and applying the Fatou lemma together with the convergence results (
2.4
), we have for almost all
\tau\in(0,\infty)

\int_{\Omega}(\vartheta(\tau)-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|{\bf v}_{0}|^{2}+(\vartheta_{0}-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\,{\rm d}{x}.

Thus, using the above estimate, we deduce

\int_{\Omega}\vartheta(\tau)\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\Omega}(\vartheta(\tau)-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}+\overline{\vartheta}\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|{\bf v}_{0}|^{2}+(\vartheta_{0}-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}+\overline{\vartheta}\,{\rm d}{x},

which is precisely the desired estimate (
1.31
).

2.3.3.
Weak lower semicontinuity of
\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}
and the limit in the kinetic energy inequality

We claim that the following inequalities hold for any
0\leq s<t<T<\infty
, any nonnegative
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and any nonnegative
\psi\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
:

(2.26)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,

(2.27)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,

(2.28)

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}}{\vartheta}\,\varphi\,\psi\,\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}}{\vartheta^{n}}\,\varphi\,\psi\,\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Since all three inequalities are proved in a similar way, we present only a proof of the first one. We use the monotonicity of
\mathbf{S}^{*}(\vartheta^{n},\cdot)
and the nonnegativity of
b
,
\varphi
, and
\psi
to obtain

(2.29)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\psi\,\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\geq\int_{Q_{T}}\mathbf{S}^{n}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\psi\,\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{Q_{T}}\mathbf{S}^{*}(\vartheta^{n},\mathbf{D}{\bf v}):\mathbf{D}{\bf v}^{n}\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\psi\,\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{Q_{T}}\mathbf{S}^{*}(\vartheta^{n},\mathbf{D}{\bf v}):\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\psi\,\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Now, we observe that we can pass to the limit
n\to+\infty
on the right-hand side. Indeed,
b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\to b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})
in
L^{q}(Q)
for any
q>1
, because

is bounded (see Definition
1.1
, property (
1.16
)), and we have the pointwise convergence of
\vartheta^{n}
(see (
2.22
)). Similarly, using the growth assumption on
\mathbf{S}^{*}
stated in (
1.7
), the pointwise convergence of
\vartheta^{n}
(
2.22
), and the boundedness of

, we can apply the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem to obtain

\mathbf{S}^{*}(\vartheta^{n},\mathbf{D}{\bf v})\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\psi\to\mathbf{S}(\vartheta,\mathbf{D}{\bf v})\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\psi\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p^{\prime}}(Q_{T})

as

. Using also the convergence results (
2.18
) and (
2.19
), together with the identification

, the convergence of the right-hand side of (

) to

\int_{Q_{T}}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\psi\,\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

is clear. Considering the

of (

Furthermore, using (

2.3.4.

- corrected total energy inequality

Here, we want to let

in (

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}{\bf v}^{n}\cdot\nabla\vartheta^{n}\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}{\bf v}^{n}\cdot\frac{\nabla[(\vartheta^{n})^{1+\gamma}]}{1+\gamma}\,\frac{b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})}{(\vartheta^{n})^{\gamma}}\,\varphi\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta^{n})\nabla\vartheta^{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\psi+\kappa(\vartheta^{n})\nabla\vartheta^{n}\cdot\nabla\hat{\vartheta}\,\partial_{2}b(\vartheta^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

2.3.5.
Limit in the entropy balance

Let

and

By virtue of the established convergence results (

Next, we focus on the right-hand side of (

The first term on the right-hand side has already been treated in (

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{Q_{T}}\kappa(\vartheta^{n})|\nabla\eta^{n}|^{2}\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

2.4.
Attaining of the initial condition

Using the identity (
2.9

Next, we establish an analogous result for the temperature

First, we show that

Therefore, to prove the claim, it is sufficient to establish that

To this end, we derive the equation for
\sqrt{\vartheta^{n}}

We set

Hence, combined with (
1.31

\sqrt{\vartheta}\in\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{weak}}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)).

Similarly, one reads from (
2.13

Next, assuming that
\varphi\geq 0

2.31

with some

\displaystyle{\bf v}(\tau)

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup{\bf v}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\textrm{weakly in }L^{2}_{0,\operatorname{div}},

\displaystyle\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}\varphi

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\sqrt{\vartheta(\sigma)}\varphi

\displaystyle\textrm{weakly in }L^{2}(\Omega).

Therefore, by a simple algebraic manipulation, we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\sigma}^{\sigma+\delta}\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}\varphi|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}|{\bf v}(\tau)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle+\liminf_{\delta\to 0}\frac{2}{\delta}\int_{\sigma}^{\sigma+\delta}\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\vartheta(\sigma)}\varphi\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}\varphi+\tfrac{1}{2}{\bf v}(\sigma)\cdot{\bf v}(\tau)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\vartheta(\sigma)}\varphi|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}|{\bf v}(\sigma)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\liminf_{\delta\to 0}\frac{2}{\delta}\int_{\sigma}^{\sigma+\delta}\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\vartheta(\sigma)}\varphi(\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}\varphi-\sqrt{\vartheta(\sigma)}\varphi)+\tfrac{1}{2}{\bf v}(\sigma)\cdot({\bf v}(\tau)-{\bf v}(\sigma))\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\vartheta(\sigma)}\varphi|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}|{\bf v}(\sigma)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}.

Consequently, the inequality (
2.36
) holds for all
\tau\in(0,+\infty)
.
Since we already know that
\lim_{\tau\to 0_{+}}\|{\bf v}(\tau)-{\bf v}_{0}\|_{2}=0
, we can pass to the
\limsup
and obtain

(2.37)

\limsup_{\tau\to 0_{+}}\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}\varphi|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}\varphi|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}.

Combining (
2.35
) with (
2.37
), and recalling that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
was arbitrary, we conclude

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau\to 0_{+}}\int_{\Omega}|(\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}-\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}})\varphi|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to 0_{+}}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta(\tau)\varphi^{2}+\vartheta_{0}\varphi^{2}-2\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}\varphi\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}\varphi\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\Omega}\vartheta_{0}\varphi^{2}-\sqrt{\vartheta(0)}\varphi\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}\varphi\,{\rm d}{x}\leq 0,

which directly implies that, for any compact
K\subset\Omega
,

(2.38)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}\to\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}\quad\mbox{ strongly in $L^{2}(K)$, }

(2.39)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}\rightharpoonup\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}\quad\mbox{ weakly in $L^{2}(\Omega)$},

as
\tau\to 0_{+}
.

Finally, we want to strengthen (
2.38
) so that the strong convergence holds on the whole domain
\Omega
. First note that, thanks to (
2.38
)–(
2.39
), we have

(2.40)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}\to\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}\quad\textrm{ strongly in }L^{q}(\Omega)\textrm{ for all }q\in[1,2).

For the next computation we recall the definition (
1.32
), which for
s,t>0
reads

(2.41)

\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(s):=\int_{1}^{s}(\mathcal{G}(z))^{-\alpha}\,{\rm d}z,\quad\mathcal{G}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\kappa(z)\,{\rm d}z,\quad f_{\alpha}(s,t):=s-t-(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(s)-\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(t))(\mathcal{G}(t))^{\alpha}.

The role of the function
\mathcal{G}
is to simplify the situation in the case of nonconstant heat conductivity.
It will be even more important in the next section.
Note that if
\kappa
is constant, then the main part of
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(s)
is just a multiple of the power function
s^{1-\alpha}
.

We set
\zeta:=1-\bigl({\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})/\mathcal{G}(\vartheta^{n})}\bigr)^{\alpha}\chi_{(0,\tau)}
in (
2.2
) for a fixed
\alpha>1/2
,
integrate the result over
t\in(0,\tau)
, and add it to (
2.9
) divided by two (similarly as above).
This leads to

(2.42)

\int_{\Omega}\tfrac{1}{2}|{\bf v}^{n}(\tau)|^{2}+f_{\alpha}(\vartheta^{n}(\tau),\hat{\vartheta})\,{\rm d}{x}\\
\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\vartheta^{n}\cdot{\bf v}^{n}\left[\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta^{n})}\right]^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{\Omega}\tfrac{1}{2}|{\bf v}_{0}^{n}|^{2}+f_{\alpha}(\vartheta_{0}^{n},\hat{\vartheta})\,{\rm d}{x}.

We do not provide detailed computation here, since a similar one is carried out in Section
3.3
in a more complicated situation.
We only mention that some terms were dropped due to their positivity, and we also used the equation for
\hat{\vartheta}
in (
1.9
).

Now, we pass to the limit as
n\to+\infty
.
For the first term on the right-hand side we need the convergence

\frac{\nabla\vartheta^{n}}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta^{n})^{\alpha}}\rightharpoonup\frac{\nabla\vartheta}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)^{\alpha}}\quad\mbox{in $L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)$,}

which follows for
\alpha>1/2
, at least for a subsequence, from (
2.21
) and (
2.22
).
Then we proceed as before to obtain after the limit passage
n\to+\infty
that for almost all
\tau\in(0,T)

(2.43)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\vartheta(\tau),\hat{\vartheta})-f_{\alpha}(\vartheta_{0},\hat{\vartheta})\,{\rm d}{x}\\
&\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\vartheta\cdot{\bf v}\left[\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}\right]^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{\Omega}\tfrac{1}{2}|{\bf v}_{0}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}|{\bf v}(\tau)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}.\end{split}

Now we need to confirm that
f_{\alpha}
suitably measures a distance and that it can be properly estimated from below.
We claim that there exists
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
such that for all
0<s,t,r
there holds

(2.44)

f_{\alpha}(s^{2},t)-f_{\alpha}(r^{2},t)-2r\partial_{1}f_{\alpha}(r^{2},t)(s-r)-(s-r)^{2}\geq 0.

Using this inequality in (
2.43
) with the setting
s:=\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}
,
t:=\hat{\vartheta}
and
r:=\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}
,
we obtain for almost all
\tau\in(0,+\infty)

(2.45)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}-\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\\
&\quad\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\vartheta\cdot{\bf v}^{n}\left[\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}\right]^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{\Omega}\tfrac{1}{2}|{\bf v}_{0}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}|{\bf v}(\tau)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\\[5.0pt]
&\qquad-\int_{\Omega}2\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}\left[1-\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta_{0})}\right)^{\alpha}\right](\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}-\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}})\,{\rm d}{x}.\end{split}

Moreover, using convexity and the fact that
\sqrt{\vartheta}\in\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{weak}}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
,
the above inequality holds for every
\tau\in(0,+\infty)
.
Finally, using the strong convergence
{\bf v}(\tau)\to{\bf v}_{0}
and the weak convergence result (
2.39
),
we can let
\tau\to 0_{+}
in the above inequality to conclude
(note that
\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta_{0})}
is bounded)

(2.46)

\lim_{\tau\to 0_{+}}\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}-\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\leq 0.

The fact (
1.25
) then follows since
\sqrt{\vartheta}\in L^{\infty}(0,+\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))
and

\int_{\Omega}|\vartheta(\tau)-\vartheta_{0}|\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}-\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}||\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}+\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}|\,{\rm d}{x}\\
\leq C(\|\sqrt{\vartheta}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,+\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\vartheta}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\vartheta(\tau)}-\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\to 0,

as
\tau\to 0_{+}
.

It remains to prove (
2.44
). For fixed
t,r>0
let us define

A(s):=f_{\alpha}(s^{2},t)-f_{\alpha}(r^{2},t)-2r\partial_{1}f_{\alpha}(r^{2},t)(s-r)-(s-r)^{2}

and evaluate its first two derivatives

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=2s\partial_{1}f_{\alpha}(s^{2},t)-2r\partial_{1}f_{\alpha}(r^{2},t)-2(s-r),

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=2\partial_{1}f_{\alpha}(s^{2},t)+4s^{2}\partial^{2}_{1}f_{\alpha}(s^{2},t)-2.

It is clear that
A(r)=A^{\prime}(r)=0
and therefore to show the claim, we just need to show that
A^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq 0
for all
s\geq\underline{\vartheta}
. Using the definition of
f_{\alpha}(s^{2},t)
we have

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2(\mathcal{G}(t))^{\alpha}}{(\mathcal{G}(s^{2}))^{\alpha}}+\frac{4s^{2}\alpha(\mathcal{G}(t))^{\alpha}\kappa(s^{2})}{(\mathcal{G}(s^{2}))^{\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(\mathcal{G}(t))^{\alpha}}{(\mathcal{G}(s^{2}))^{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2s^{2}\alpha\kappa(s^{2})}{(\mathcal{G}(s^{2}))}-1\right)\geq\frac{2(\mathcal{G}(t))^{\alpha}}{(\mathcal{G}(s^{2}))^{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\alpha\underline{\kappa}}{\overline{\kappa}}-1\right).

Hence, setting e.g.
\alpha:=\frac{\overline{\kappa}}{2\underline{\kappa}}>\frac{1}{2}
, we get the claim. The proof is complete.

3.
Stability of steady state

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
We largely follow the approach of
[
2
]
, omitting details where the arguments are analogous.
However, since we consider a completely new type of solution, namely
b
-weak solution,
and cannot rely on an energy equality, a more careful analysis is required.
Accordingly, all genuinely new steps are presented in full detail, while standard arguments are treated more briefly.

Thus, in this section we assume that we are given a four-tuple
({\bf v},\mathbf{S},\vartheta,\eta)
which constitutes a
b
-weak solution in the sense of Definition
1.2
, corresponding to some
(-\alpha)
-correction function that satisfies all the requirements of Definition
1.1
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

3.1.
Long time behaviour of the velocity field

Next we show that the exponential decay of the velocity field follows from (
1.28
). First we realize that from (
1.28
) and (
1.25
) we get that for almost all
s,t\in(0,+\infty)
fulfilling
s<t

(3.1)

\int_{\Omega}|{\bf v}(t)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}+2\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\Omega}|{\bf v}(s)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}.

It directly follows from the above inequality that

(3.2)

{\bf v}\in L^{\infty}(0,+\infty;L^{2}_{0,\operatorname{div}}),\quad\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\in L^{1}((0,+\infty)\times\Omega).

If
p<2
, we estimate the second term on the left-hand side as

(3.3)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{D}{\bf v}|^{p}\,{\rm d}{x}&=\int_{\Omega}\left((\delta+|\mathbf{D}{\bf v}|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\mathbf{D}{\bf v}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}(\delta+|\mathbf{D}{\bf v}|^{2})^{\frac{2-p}{2}\frac{p}{2}}\,{\rm d}{x}\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}(\delta+|\mathbf{D}{\bf v}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}.\end{split}

Using also the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,p}\hookrightarrow L^{2}
, we obtain

(3.4)

\begin{split}C\min\left\{\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p}\right\}&\leq C\min\left\{\|\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{2},\|\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\right\}\\
&\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{2}}{C+\|\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{2-p}}\leq\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{\bf v}\,{\rm d}{x}.\end{split}

Application of this inequality in (
3.1
) yields the existence of
\mu>0
such that for almost all
s,t\in(0,+\infty)
with
s<t
,

(3.5)

\int_{\Omega}|{\bf v}(t)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}+\mu\int_{s}^{t}\min\left\{\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(\tau),\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p}(\tau)\right\}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\int_{\Omega}|{\bf v}(s)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}.

If
p\geq 2
, one can proceed similarly to get the existence of
\mu>0
such that for almost all
s,t\in(0,+\infty)
with
s<t
,

(3.6)

\int_{\Omega}|{\bf v}(t)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}+\mu\int_{s}^{t}\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\int_{\Omega}|{\bf v}(s)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}.

We introduce the shorthand notation
f(\tau)=\|{\bf v}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
. From (
3.1
) it follows that
f(t)\leq f(s)
for a.e.
s,t\in(0,+\infty)
with
s<t
. Moreover, since
{\bf v}\in\mathcal{C}_{\textrm{weak}}(0,T;L^{2}_{0,\operatorname{div}})
for all
T>0
, the function
f
is lower semicontinuous.

We distinguish two cases:

a)

p<2
and
\|{\bf v}_{0}\|_{2}>1
, i.e., we have to deal with the inequality (
3.5
),

b)

the remaining cases, i.e., the inequality (
3.6
) holds.

In case b), (
3.5
) or (
3.6
) for
p\geq 2
or
p<2
respectively implies for a.e.
s,t\in(0,+\infty)
with
s<t
,

f(t)+\mu\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq f(s).

An application of Lemma
A.1
then implies that for almost all
s,t\in(0,+\infty)
,
s<t

(3.7)

f(t)\leq e^{-\mu(t-s)}f(s).

Thanks to (
1.25
), we may let
s\to 0
and conclude that for almost all
t\in(0,\infty)

f(t)\leq e^{-\mu t}\|{\bf v}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}.

Finally, since
f
is lower semicontinuous, the above inequality extends to all

, which is precisely the desired result (
1.34
).

In case a) we define
t^{*}:=\sup\{\tau;\ f>1\ \text{a.e. in }(0,\tau)\}>0
. Positivity of

follows from (
1.25
). We observe from (
3.5
) that for a.e.
s,t\in(0,t^{*})
with
s<t

\max(1,f(t)+\mu\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau)\leq f(s).

Lemma
A.1
together with (
1.25
) gives
f(t)\leq\left(\frac{f(0)}{1+\mu t}\right)^{2/p}
for a.e.

, which implies that

.
It follows from the definition of

and properties of

that

so we can apply the previous step to deduce for a.e.

with

that (

) holds.
Combining estimates on

and on

with properties of

we infer that for a.e.

,

By lower semicontinuity of

we get that the estimate holds actually for all

which is exactly (

).

3.2.
Further properties of

-weak solution

In this part we re-establish the a priori estimates for
\vartheta
and
\eta
.
This is necessary, since we aim to prove the result for an arbitrary

-weak solution, whereas the definition of

-weak solution imposes only limited regularity on

On the other hand, the existence of a solution has already been proved, and the corresponding estimates were derived along the way.
Here we carefully justify that the same bounds hold for any such

-weak solution.

3.2.1.
Steklov average of the entropy inequality

Since all estimates for
\vartheta
are deduced from (
1.27
), we need to introduce its regularized version, which allows us to deal with the time derivative of the solutions. For any function
u:(0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
and any
t,h>0
,
x\in\Omega
we denote

u_{h}(t,x)=\fint_{t}^{t+h}u(\tau,x)\,{\rm d}\tau.

Next, let
T>0
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}((0,T))
be arbitrary nonnegative functions.
If we replace
\psi
by
-\psi_{-h}
in (
1.27
) with sufficiently small
h
,
then after applying Fubini’s theorem we deduce that

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\eta_{h}\,\psi\varphi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\eta\cdot{\bf v})_{h}\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\eta)_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{\vartheta}\,\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}\right)_{h}\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\right)_{h}\,\varphi\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

Using density and weak
∗
density arguments, we conclude that the above inequality can be generalized.
Thus, for all nonnegative
\zeta\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))
it holds that

(3.8)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\eta_{h}\,\zeta\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\eta\cdot{\bf v})_{h}\,\zeta\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\eta)_{h}\cdot\nabla\zeta\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{\vartheta}\,\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}\right)_{h}\,\zeta\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\right)_{h}\,\zeta\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,\end{split}

which is the starting point for further estimates.

3.2.2.
Regularity of
\vartheta
and
{\bf v}

Since we are working with
b
-weak solution, we have (
1.24
) at our disposal.
Nevertheless, we establish more delicate entropy and temperature estimates in what follows.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the standard cut-off function

\mathcal{T}_{k}(s):={\rm sign}(s)\min\{|s|,k\}.

Furthermore, we define its mollification as

\mathcal{T}_{k,\varepsilon}(s):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &s&&\textrm{if }|s|\leq k-\varepsilon,\\
&{\rm sign}(s)k&&\textrm{if }|s|\geq k+\varepsilon,\\
&{\rm sign}(s)\left(k-\frac{(k+\varepsilon-|s|)^{2}}{4\varepsilon}\right)&&\textrm{if }k-\varepsilon<|s|<k+\varepsilon.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
T>0
be a Lebesgue point of
\eta\in L^{\infty}(0,+\infty;L^{1}(\Omega))
, and let
\psi=\psi(t)
be a nonnegative function in
C^{0,1}([0,T])
with
\psi(0)=\psi(T)=0
.
We choose a sequence
\{h_{n}\}
such that
h_{n}>0
,
h_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
and denote
\eta_{n}=\eta_{h_{n}}
,
\vartheta_{n}=\vartheta_{h_{n}}
. The sequence can be chosen such that
\eta_{n}\to\eta
a.e. in
(0,T)\times\Omega
and there is an integrable majorant of
\{\vartheta_{n}\}
on
(0,T)\times\Omega
. We denote it
\Theta
. Thanks to the Jensen inequality, we have
\exp\eta_{n}\leq\vartheta_{n}
.
We set
\zeta:=\psi\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n})-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}
in (
3.8
), which is an admissible test function. Our goal is to let
n\to+\infty
in all terms. Note that
\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n})-\overline{\vartheta})_{+})=\nabla\eta_{n}\exp(\eta_{n})\chi_{\{\exp(\eta_{n})\in(\overline{\vartheta},\overline{\vartheta}+k)\}}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and

(3.9)

|\psi\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n})-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}|\leq k\|\psi\|_{\infty},\quad\psi\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n})-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\to\psi\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\quad\mbox{a.e. in $Q_{T}$.}

We introduce an auxiliary function

\Phi_{k}(s)=\int_{\overline{\vartheta}}^{s}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{k}(\sigma-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}}{\sigma}\,{\rm d}s

to deal with the time derivative term. We compute

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\eta_{n}\exp(\eta_{n})\frac{\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n})-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}}{\exp(\eta_{n})}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n}))\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Since
0\leq\Phi_{k}(s)\leq s
for
s>0
, we have an integrable majorant for
\Phi_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n}))
, namely

\Phi_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n}))\leq\Phi_{k}(\Theta)\leq\Theta,

and we can identify the limit as
n\to+\infty
:

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n}))\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\to-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{k}(\vartheta)\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Similarly, we can also pass to the limit
n\to+\infty
in convective term and all terms of the right hand side. We use (
3.9
) and the fact that

\nabla\eta{\bf v},\qquad\frac{\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}}{\vartheta},\qquad\kappa(\vartheta)\,|\nabla\eta|^{2}

are integrable quantities over
Q_{T}
by (
1.24
). Consequently, we may assume that (up to a subsequence) their Steklov averages converge to them almost everywhere in
Q_{T}
, and that there exist
L^{1}(Q_{T})
-integrable majorants.

(3.10)

\begin{gathered}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(-\nabla\eta\cdot{\bf v}+\frac{\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}}{\vartheta}+\kappa(\vartheta)\,|\nabla\eta|^{2}\right)_{h_{n}}\psi\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n})-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(-\nabla\eta\cdot{\bf v}+\frac{\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}}{\vartheta}+\kappa(\vartheta)\,|\nabla\eta|^{2}\right)\psi\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{gathered}

It remains to pass to the limit in the elliptic term. For this it is sufficient to realize that

\psi\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n})-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\to\psi\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty\text{ in }L^{2}(Q_{T}),

and

\psi\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n})-\overline{\vartheta})_{+})\rightharpoonup\chi\quad\text{in }L^{2}(Q_{T})\text{ up to a subsequence}.

Clearly,
\chi=\psi\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta-\overline{\vartheta})_{+})
, so

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\eta)_{h_{n}}\cdot\psi\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp(\eta_{n})-\overline{\vartheta})_{+})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\eta\cdot\psi\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta-\overline{\vartheta})_{+})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

After the limit passage we rearrange some terms. The convective term disappears:

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}-\nabla\eta\cdot{\bf v}\psi\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}-\nabla\Psi(\vartheta)\cdot{\bf v}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=0,

since
\operatorname{div}{\bf v}=0
in
Q_{T}
. Note here, that
\Psi
is a properly chosen function.
Let us now combine two terms connected with the elliptic term

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)\,|\nabla\eta|^{2}\psi\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\eta\cdot\psi\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta-\overline{\vartheta})_{+})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\psi\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\eta\cdot\left(\nabla\eta\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}-\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta-\overline{\vartheta})_{+})\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\psi\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\left(\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta-\overline{\vartheta})_{+}-\vartheta\chi_{\{\vartheta-\overline{\vartheta}\in(0,k)\}}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Analyzing the large bracket above we see that it is equal to
0
for
\vartheta\leq\overline{\vartheta}
, equal to
-\overline{\vartheta}
if
\vartheta\in(\overline{\vartheta},\overline{\vartheta}+k)
and equal to
k
for
\vartheta\geq\overline{\vartheta}+k
. Collecting the computation above and letting
\psi
to
\chi_{(0,T)}
, we deduce that for almost all
T>0

(3.12)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}k\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\chi_{\{\vartheta>\overline{\vartheta}+k\}}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\overline{\vartheta}\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{k}(\vartheta)(T)\,{\rm d}{x}\leq C,

where the last inequality is a consequence of (
1.24
). Note that the constant
C>0
is independent of
k
. Moreover, it can be deduced from (
1.24
) and (
3.12
) that, see
[
2
, Section 3.5, (3.20)]
,

(3.13)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{1+\varepsilon}}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq C(T,\varepsilon)

for all
\varepsilon>0
. From this inequality together with (
1.24
) one gets for all
T
, see
[
14
, Appendix B.5]
,

(3.14)

\vartheta\in L^{r}(Q_{T}),\quad\nabla\vartheta\in L^{q}(Q_{T})\quad\mbox{for $r\in[1,\frac{5}{3})$, $q\in[1,\frac{5}{4})$.}

The last property needed for the stability is the interpolation for
{\bf v}
, and from (
1.24
) one obtains

(3.15)

{\bf v}\in L^{\frac{5p}{3}}(Q_{T}).

We also recall that, directly from the definition of
b
-weak solution, it follows

(3.16)

\begin{split}\vartheta(t,x)\geq\underline{\vartheta}.\end{split}

This is very important since it gives that
\vartheta^{a}
is bounded in
Q
for any
a\leq 0
.

Finally, we show that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

(3.17)

\partial_{t}\mathcal{T}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta)\in\mathcal{M}(0,T;(W^{2,3}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}).

We do not provide the complete rigorous proof, since it essentially follows the previous steps. Hence, we set
\zeta:=\exp(\eta_{h})\,\mathcal{T}_{k,\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\exp(\eta_{h}))\,\psi
in (
3.8
), where
\psi\geq 0
is a smooth compactly supported function. Doing so, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\mathcal{T}_{k,\varepsilon}(\exp(\eta_{h}))\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\eta\cdot{\bf v})_{h}\,\zeta\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\eta)_{h}\cdot\nabla\zeta\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{\vartheta}\,\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}\right)_{h}\,\zeta\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\right)_{h}\,\zeta\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

Letting
h\to 0
with help of integration by parts in the first two integrals, we deduce (using the above estimates (
3.13
)–(
3.15
))

(3.18)

\begin{split}&-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{T}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta)\,\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{T}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta)\,\nabla\psi\cdot{\bf v}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}\,\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta)\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\mathcal{T}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta)\cdot\nabla\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}\,\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta)\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\geq 0.\end{split}

We see that the above expression defines a nonnegative distribution on
Q
. Consequently, there exists a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(Q)
such that, for all
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{0}(Q)
, there holds (see
[
35
, Theorem 9.13]
)

(3.19)

\begin{split}&-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{T}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta)\,\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{T}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta)\,\nabla\psi\cdot{\bf v}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}\,\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta)\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\mathcal{T}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta)\cdot\nabla\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}\,\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta)\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\langle\mu,\psi\rangle.\end{split}

From the classical Sobolev embedding and the above identity, we can thus deduce (
3.17
).

3.2.3.
Uniform
L^{\infty}(0,+\infty;L^{1}(\Omega))
estimate of temperature

Note that for a general
b
-weak solution we know that it lies for any
T>0
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
, however we do not know any qualitative estimate. We need an estimate of
\vartheta
in
L^{\infty}(0,+\infty;L^{1}(\Omega))
that is uniform with respect to the initial and boundary conditions. We are going to show that it follows from (
1.29
) and (
1.27
).

Since
\kappa
is bounded from above and below, the function
\mathcal{G}
, defined in (
1.32
), is strictly increasing and enjoys the following estimate

(3.20)

\underline{\kappa}s\leq\mathcal{G}(s)\leq\overline{\kappa}s\qquad\textrm{ for all }s\geq 0.

We define for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\alpha\in(0,1)

\mathcal{F}_{k}(s):=\int_{1}^{s}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{k}(z)}{z}\,{\rm d}z\ \mbox{ and }\ \mathcal{F}_{k}^{\alpha}(s):=\int_{1}^{s}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{k}(z)}{z}(\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{T}_{k}(z)))^{-\alpha}\,{\rm d}z.

It directly follows from the definition that we have the following convergence results

(3.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{k}(s)

\displaystyle\to s-1

\displaystyle\mbox{ for any }s>0,\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\alpha}(s)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(s)

\displaystyle\mbox{ for any }s>0,\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty,

\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}
being defined in (
1.32
).

Let
\varphi=\varphi(x)
be a function in
\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\varphi\in[0,1]
in
\Omega
and
\varphi=1
on
\partial\Omega
. Let
T>0
be a Lebesgue point of the mappings
\vartheta,\eta\in L^{\infty}_{loc}([0,+\infty);L^{1}(\Omega))
and
{\bf v}\in L^{\infty}(0,+\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))
, and let
\psi=\psi(t)
be a nonnegative function in
\mathcal{C}^{0,1}([0,T])
with
\psi(0)=\psi(T)=0
. Let us choose the test function in (
3.8
) as

\zeta:=(1-\varphi)\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp{\eta_{h}})\psi.

Note that such function belongs to
L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))
and can be used as a test function in (
3.8
) to get

(3.22)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}-\mathcal{F}_{k}(\exp{\eta_{h}})(1-\varphi)\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\eta\cdot{\bf v})_{h}(1-\varphi)\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp{\eta_{h}})\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\eta)_{h}\cdot(-\nabla\varphi\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp{\eta_{h}})+(1-\varphi)\chi_{\{\exp{\eta_{h}}<k\}}\exp{\eta_{h}}\nabla\eta_{h})\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\eta|^{2}\right)_{h}(1-\varphi)\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp{\eta_{h}})\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\geq 0.

We used the fact that the term
(\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}/\vartheta)_{h}
on the right hand side of (
3.8
) and the test function are positive. Now we choose a sequence
\{h_{n}\}
such that
h_{n}>0
,
h_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
and denote
\eta_{n}=\eta_{h_{n}}
,
\vartheta_{n}=\vartheta_{h_{n}}
. The sequence can be chosen such that
\eta_{n}\to\eta
a.e. in
Q_{T}
and there is an integrable majorant of
\{\vartheta_{n}\}
on
Q_{T}
. We call it
\Theta
. Thanks to the Jensen inequality, we have
\exp\eta_{n}\leq\vartheta_{n}
. Consequently,
\mathcal{F}_{k}(\exp{\eta_{n}})\to\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta)
as
n\to+\infty
a.e. in
(0,T)\times\Omega
and
\mathcal{F}_{k}(\exp{\eta_{n}})\leq\Theta
. Dominated Convergence Theorem allows us to pass to the limit as
n\to+\infty
in the first term on the left hand side of (
3.22
). Similarly we can treat also other terms in that inequality to get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}-\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta)(1-\varphi)\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\eta\cdot{\bf v}(1-\varphi)\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta)\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\eta\cdot(-\nabla\varphi\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta))\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\eta\cdot(1-\varphi)\chi_{\{\vartheta<k\}}\vartheta\nabla\eta\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\eta|^{2}(1-\varphi)\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta)\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\geq 0.

Now, we combine the last two terms

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\eta|^{2}(1-\varphi)\left(\chi_{\{\vartheta<k\}}\vartheta-\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta)\right)\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq 0.

After some reformulation of the second and the third term we arrive to the inequality

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}-\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta)(1-\varphi)\partial_{t}\psi+\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta){\bf v}\cdot\nabla\varphi\psi-\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\varphi\frac{\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\geq 0.

Finally, we set
\psi=\psi_{n}(t)=\max(0,\min(1,nT/2-n|t-T/2|))
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and pass to the limit as
n\to+\infty
, taking into account that
T
is a Lebesgue point of
\vartheta\in L^{\infty}_{loc}([0,T);L^{1}(\Omega))
and (
1.25
) we get

(3.23)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}(1-\varphi)\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta(T))\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta){\bf v}\cdot\nabla\varphi-\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\varphi\frac{\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\quad\geq\int_{\Omega}(1-\varphi)\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta_{0})\,{\rm d}{x}.\end{split}

We used that
\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime}\leq 1
on
(0,+\infty)
and consequently

n\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\int_{\Omega}|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta)-\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta_{0})|(1-\varphi)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq n\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\int_{\Omega}|\vartheta-\vartheta_{0}|(1-\varphi)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\to 0

as
n\to+\infty
. Analogously, it holds that

n\int_{T-\frac{1}{n}}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta)-\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta(T))|(1-\varphi)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq n\int_{T-\frac{1}{n}}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\vartheta-\vartheta(T)|(1-\varphi)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\to 0

as
n\to+\infty
.

We realize that one can insert the same
\psi=\psi_{n}
also to (
1.29
) and pass to the limit
n\to+\infty
. Note that
B
is Lipschitz continuous in the first variable. Subtracting (
3.23
) from the so obtained inequality yields

(3.24)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}{|{\bf v}(T)|^{2}}+\vartheta(T)-\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta(T))+\left(\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta(T))-B(\vartheta(T),\hat{\vartheta})\right)\varphi\,{\rm d}{x}\\
\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\varphi\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\vartheta b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\varphi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\varphi\left(\frac{\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}-b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\hat{\vartheta}\,\partial_{2}b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\,\varphi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}{|{\bf v}_{0}|^{2}}+\vartheta_{0}-\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta_{0})+\left(\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta_{0})-B(\vartheta_{0},\hat{\vartheta})\right)\varphi\right]\,{\rm d}{x}\end{array}

Let us consider
\varphi_{n}:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
a sequence of functions in
C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that

\varphi_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
a.e. in
\Omega
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

\begin{split}\varphi_{n}=1\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega,\quad 0\leq\varphi_{n}\leq 1\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega,\quad\ {\rm supp}\ \varphi_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\partial\Omega,{1}/{n})\mbox{ and }|\nabla\varphi_{n}(x)|\leq Cn\end{split}

where
\mathcal{U}(\partial\Omega,{1}/{n})
denotes the open neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
of radius
{1}/{n}
.
We want to pass to the limit as
n\to+\infty
in (
3.24
). The terms with
\varphi_{n}
disappear in the limit since

\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta(T))-B(\vartheta(T),\hat{\vartheta}),\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta_{0})-B(\vartheta_{0},\hat{\vartheta})\in L^{1}(\Omega),{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\vartheta\,b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta}),\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\hat{\vartheta}\,\partial_{2}b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\in L^{1}(Q),

compare the computation in Section
2.3.4
. Further goal is to show that also the terms with
\nabla\varphi_{n}
disappear in the limit as
n\to+\infty
. The main tool for this is Hardy’s inequality, see Theorem
A.2
. The general strategy is to realize that
|\nabla\varphi_{n}(x)|\leq C/\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
for
x\in\Omega
and then use a fact that some of the appearing functions lie in some Sobolev space
W^{1,r}_{0}
with
r\in(1,+\infty)
.
We start with the second integral on the left hand side of (
3.24
).
Since
{\bf v}\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega))
we need that
\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta)\in L^{p^{\prime}}((0,T)\times\Omega)
. This follows from (
3.14
) because for fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
the function
\mathcal{F}_{k}
has logarithmic growth at infinity. We compute

(3.25)

\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta)({\bf v}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{n})\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right|\\
\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{1/n}}{\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta)^{p^{\prime}}}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|{\bf v}|^{p}|\nabla\varphi_{n}|^{p}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{1/n}}{\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta)^{p^{\prime}}}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{{\bf v}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)}\right|^{p}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{1/n}}{\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta)^{p^{\prime}}}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla{\bf v}|^{p}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

where we denoted
\Omega_{1/n}=\{x\in\Omega;\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<1/n\}
. The last integral converges to
0
as
n\to+\infty
since
\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta)\in L^{p^{\prime}}((0,T)\times\Omega)
and
|(0,T)\times\Omega_{1/n}|\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
.

In order to deal with the fourth integral on the left hand side of (
3.24
) we show that

(3.26)

F:=\vartheta^{\alpha}\left[\frac{\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}-b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\right]\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega))

for
k\geq\overline{\vartheta}
. Having this estimate, we can proceed exactly as above to show that this integral disappears in the limit
n\to+\infty
, since
\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta/\vartheta^{\alpha}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
. Let us now prove (
3.26
). The fact that
F
has zero trace follows from (
1.16
) and (
1.9
). It remains to show
\nabla F\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
. We compute

\displaystyle\nabla F

\displaystyle=\left[(\alpha-1)\vartheta^{2\alpha-2}\mathcal{T}_{k}(\vartheta)+\vartheta^{2\alpha-1}\chi_{\{\vartheta<k\}}-\alpha\vartheta^{2\alpha-1}b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})-\vartheta^{2\alpha}\partial_{1}b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\right]\vartheta^{-\alpha}\nabla\vartheta

\displaystyle+\left[-\partial_{2}b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\vartheta^{\alpha}\right]\nabla\hat{\vartheta}=:F_{1}\vartheta^{-\alpha}\nabla\vartheta+F_{2}\nabla\hat{\vartheta}.

The functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are bounded by (
3.16
), the fact that
\alpha<1
and properties of the function
b
listed in Definition
1.1
, and
\vartheta^{-\alpha}\nabla\vartheta,\nabla\hat{\vartheta}\in L^{2}(Q)
by (
3.13
) since
\alpha>1/2
. The statement (
3.26
) follows.
Altogether, setting
\varphi_{n}
as a test function in (
3.24
) and passing
n\to+\infty
we get

(3.27)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}{|{\bf v}(T)|^{2}}+\vartheta(T)-1-\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta(T))\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}{|{\bf v}_{0}|^{2}}+\vartheta_{0}-1-\mathcal{F}_{k}(\vartheta_{0})\,{\rm d}{x}.

Now, we realize that the non-negative function
\Phi(s)=s-1-\mathcal{F}_{k}(s)
,
s>0
satisfies estimates

(3.28)

\displaystyle\Phi(s)\leq(s-k)_{+}

\displaystyle\mbox{for $s>0$,}\qquad\frac{s}{2}\leq\Phi(s)+k\lg(2)

\displaystyle\mbox{for $s>2k$.}

The second estimate follows from the facts that
(\Phi(s)-s/2)^{\prime}\geq 0
for
s\geq 2k
and
\Phi(2k)-k=-k\lg(2)
.
Finally, we set
k=\overline{\vartheta}
and use the estimates (
3.28
) and (
3.27
) to get

\int_{\Omega}\vartheta(T)\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\Omega}\vartheta(T)\chi_{\vartheta\leq 2\overline{\vartheta}}\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}\vartheta(T)\chi_{\vartheta>2\overline{\vartheta}}\,{\rm d}{x}\leq 2\overline{\vartheta}|\Omega|+\int_{\Omega}{|{\bf v}(T)|^{2}}+2\Phi(\vartheta(T))+2\overline{\vartheta}\lg(2)\,{\rm d}{x}\\
\leq C\overline{\vartheta}|\Omega|+\int_{\Omega}{|{\bf v}_{0}|^{2}}+2\Phi(\vartheta_{0})\,{\rm d}{x}\leq C\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|{\bf v}_{0}|^{2}+\max(\overline{\vartheta},\vartheta_{0})\,{\rm d}{x},

which gives the desired uniform
L^{\infty}(0,+\infty;L^{1}(\Omega))
estimate

(3.29)

\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,+\infty;L^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|{\bf v}_{0}|^{2}+\max(\overline{\vartheta},\vartheta_{0})\,{\rm d}{x}.

3.3.
Renormalization of the entropy inequality

Further we need to derive an estimate that is suitable for proof of the stability of the steady state. We use a similar method to the one used to derive the estimate (
3.29
).
Let
\varphi=\varphi(x)
be again a function in
\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\varphi\in[0,1]
in
\Omega
and
\varphi=1
on
\partial\Omega
, and let
\psi=\psi(t)
be a function in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(0,+\infty)
,
\psi\geq 0
. Let
T>0
be such that
\supp\psi\subset[0,T]
. We choose the test function in (
3.8
) as

\zeta:=(1-\varphi)\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp{\eta_{h}})\,\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp{\eta_{h}}))}\right)^{\alpha}\psi,\mbox{ with $\alpha\in(0,1)$.}

We expand each resulting term and subsequently analyze the limits, first as
h\to 0_{+}
and then as
k\to+\infty
, in order to derive a renormalized form of the entropy inequality.
Throughout, we write
\int_{0}^{\infty}
, since
\psi
is compactly supported in
(0,T)
.
Using integration by parts, together with the compact support of
\psi
and the fact that
\hat{\vartheta}
is independent of time, the time-derivative term in (
3.8
) can be rewritten as

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\eta_{h}\,(1-\varphi)\,\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp{\eta_{h}})\,\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{T}_{k}(\exp{\eta_{h}}))}\right)^{\alpha}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\left[\left(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\alpha}(\exp{\eta_{h}})-\varphi\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\alpha}(\exp{\eta_{h}})\right)(\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta}))^{\alpha}\right]\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left[\left(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\alpha}(\exp{\eta_{h}})-\varphi\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\alpha}(\exp{\eta_{h}})\right)(\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta}))^{\alpha}\right]\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

Thanks to the Jensen inequality, we have
\exp\eta_{h}\leq\vartheta_{h}
. As
\vartheta\in L^{1}(Q_{T})
then
\vartheta_{h}\to\vartheta
in
L^{1}(Q_{T})
and consequently also
\exp\eta_{h}\to\exp\eta=\vartheta
in
L^{1}(Q_{T})
. As

(3.30)

|\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\alpha}(s)|\leq C(1+s)\quad\mbox{for $s>0$,}

we can use the Dominated Convergence Theorem to deduce that (recall also that
\hat{\vartheta}
is bounded)

\begin{split}\lim_{h\to 0_{+}}&\left[-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left[\left(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\alpha}(\exp{\eta_{h}})-\varphi\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\alpha}(\exp{\eta_{h}})\right)(\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta}))^{\alpha}\right]\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right]\\

we need that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}\right)^{\alpha}({\bf v}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{n})\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{1/n}}{F^{p^{\prime}}}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{{\bf v}}{\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)}\right|^{p}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{1/n}}{F^{p^{\prime}}}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla{\bf v}|^{p}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

where we denoted

as

since

.

In order to deal with the third term on the right hand side we show that

for some

. Having this estimate we can proceed exactly as above to show that this integral disappears in the limit

since

for any

. Let us now prove (

). The fact that

has zero trace follows from (

). It remains to show

. We compute

\displaystyle\nabla F

\displaystyle=\nabla\vartheta\left[r\vartheta^{r-1}\big(b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})-\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}\right)^{\alpha}\big)+\vartheta^{r}\big(\partial_{1}b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})+\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{\alpha\kappa(\vartheta)}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}\big)\right]

\displaystyle+\nabla\hat{\vartheta}\left[\partial_{2}b(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})-\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{\alpha\kappa(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}\right]\vartheta^{r}=:F_{1}\nabla\vartheta+F_{2}\nabla\hat{\vartheta}.

The function

satisfies the estimate

), (

The fourth term on the right hand side of (

Now, taking the limit as

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}[\frac{|{\bf v}|^{2}}{2}+\vartheta-\hat{\vartheta}-(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(\vartheta)-\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(\hat{\vartheta}))\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})^{\alpha}]\,\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt+\frac{2\alpha}{(1-\alpha)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})\left|\nabla\left[\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\right]\right|^{2}\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}\,\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}\right)^{\alpha}\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{1-\alpha}|{\bf v}|^{2}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{array}

Note that in the first term we added some terms that are independent of

which is allowed since

Now, we recall the auxiliary function

(3.44)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\,{\rm d}{x}\leq C\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\frac{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}\right|^{2}\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}\right)^{-1-\alpha}\,{\rm d}{x},\end{split}

where the constant

The inequality (

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{|{\bf v}|^{2}}{2}+f_{\alpha}(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\right]\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt+c\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\vartheta,\hat{\vartheta})\psi+\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})\left|\nabla\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\right|^{2}\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
\displaystyle\vskip 6.0pt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}\,\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}(\hat{\vartheta})}{\mathcal{G}(\vartheta)}\right)^{\alpha}\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{1-\alpha}|{\bf v}|^{2}\,\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{array}

It remains to estimate the last term on the right hand side in order to get the exponential decay of solutions. We split the estimate to the two cases:

Let us start with

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{1-\alpha}|{\bf v}|^{2}\psi\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq C\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\psi\|{\bf v}\|_{6}^{2}\|\vartheta\|_{3(1-\alpha)/2}^{1-\alpha}}\\
\leq C\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\psi\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}\,{\rm d}{x}}\leq\frac{C+1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\partial_{t}\psi\|{\bf v}\|_{2}^{2}}-\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

We used that by Sobolev Embedding Theorem it holds for the case
p\geq 2

(3.47)

\|{\bf v}\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\|{\bf v}\|_{6}^{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{S}:\mathbf{D}{{\bf v}}\,{\rm d}{x}.

We also employed (
3.29
) together with the fact that for

.
Now, using again (

Note that

. In particular, they depend on the solution

only through norms of the initial data
({\bf v}_{0},\vartheta_{0})
.
Further we proceed as in Subsection

. We take

,
\tau>0
a Lebesgue point of the mappings

and test the equation with
\psi=\exp(\mu t)\max(0,\min(1,n\tau/2-n|t-\tau/2|))

for

(3.48)

\int_{\Omega}L_{\alpha,\beta}({\bf v}(\tau),\vartheta(\tau),\hat{\vartheta})\exp(\mu\tau)\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\Omega}L_{\alpha,\beta}({\bf v}_{0},\vartheta_{0},\hat{\vartheta})\,{\rm d}{x},

which is (
1.36
) in the situation
\sigma=0
. General situation of (

\sigma,\tau\in(0,+\infty)
,

\sigma,\tau>0

\sigma>0

\int_{\Omega}L_{\alpha,\beta}({\bf v}(\tau),\mathcal{T}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta(\tau)),\hat{\vartheta})\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\liminf_{t\to\tau_{+}}\int_{\Omega}L_{\alpha,\beta}({\bf v}(t),\mathcal{T}_{k,\varepsilon}(\vartheta(t)),\hat{\vartheta})\,{\rm d}{x}.

In the case

. We use the embedding

according to the size of the norm

. This is the reason why we need

. The other restrictions on

Lemma A.1

(Decay estimates)
.

1)

Let
t^{*}\geq 0
and let
f:(t^{*},+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]
be a measurable function such that, for almost all
s,t\in(t^{*},+\infty)
with
s<t
, there holds

(A.1)

f(t)+C_{1}\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)\,\,{\rm d}\tau\leq f(s).

Then

f(t)\leq f(s)\,e^{-C_{1}(t-s)}

for almost all
s,t>t^{*}
with
s<t
.

2)

Let
t^{*}>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and let
f:(0,t^{*})\to[0,+\infty]
be measurable such that, for almost all
s,t\in(0,t^{*})
with
s<t
, there holds

(A.2)

\max\Bigl(1,f(t)+C_{1}\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)^{\alpha}\,\,{\rm d}\tau\Bigr)\leq f(s).

Then

f(t)\leq\left(\frac{f(s)}{1+C_{1}(t-s)}\right)^{1/\alpha}

for almost all
s,t\in(0,t^{*})
with
s<t
.

Proof.

Ad 1)
We redefine
f
on a set of zero measure so that
f
is decreasing on
(t^{*},+\infty)
. We denote the new function again
f
. It satisfies (
A.1
) for all
s,t>t^{*}
,
s<t
. Since
f
is decreasing we get from (
A.1
) that

(A.3)

f(\tau)\leq\frac{f(\sigma)}{1+C_{1}(\tau-\sigma)}

for all
\sigma,\tau\in(t^{*},+\infty)
,
\sigma<\tau
.
Splitting interval
(s,t)
to
n\in\mathbb{N}
parts and applying (
A.3
) iteratively gives

f(t)\leq f(s)\left(\frac{1}{1+C_{1}\frac{t-s}{n}}\right)^{n}.

The claim follows by limit passage
n\to+\infty
since we modified the function
f
only on a set of zero measure.

Ad 2) As in 1) we redefine
f
on a set of zero measure so that
f
is decreasing and satisfies (
A.2
) for all
s,t\in(0,t^{*})
. Since
f\geq 1
on
(0,t^{*})
we get for all
s,t\in(0,t^{*})
,
s<t

f(t)\leq\left(\frac{f(s)}{1+C_{1}(t-s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},

which is the claim since
f
was modified only on a zero measure set.
∎

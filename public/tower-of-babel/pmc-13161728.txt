Prediction of microvascular invasion and post-resection prognosis using ADV, AFP–PIVKA-II, and MoRAL scores in hepatocellular carcinoma patients: an international multicenter cohort study with 9,061 patients
Authors: Minha Choi, Shin Hwang, Masaki Kaibori, Minkyeong Jo, Collaboration of Korean Association of Hepato-Biliary-Pancreatic Surgery, Japanese Society of Hepato-Biliary-Pancreatic Surgery
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeMicrovascular invasion (MVI) is a well-known pathological prognostic factor in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). This study aimed to predict MVI and to assess post-resection prognosis using the ADV (multiplication of AFP, des-γ-carboxyprothrombin—also known as proteins induced by vitamin K absence or antagonist-II [PIVKA-II], and tumor volume), AP (multiplication of AFP and PIVKA-II), and MoRAL (model to predict tumor recurrence [TR] after liver transplantation) scores.MethodsThis international multicenter study included 9,061 patients who underwent hepatic resection for solitary HCC measuring 1.0–10 cm from 2010 to 2017 at ten Korean centers and 73 Japanese centers, and were followed up until 2020.ResultsThe receiver operating characteristic (ROC) area under the curve (AUC) for diagnosis of MVI was 0.734 for the ADV score at 4.7log, 0.684 for the AP score at 3.7log, and 0.705 for the MoRAL score at 162.0. The ADV score exhibited the strongest predictive ability for MVI. The ROC AUC for actuarial 5-year TR was 0.586 for the ADV score, 0.583 for the AP score, and 0.586 for the MoRAL score, demonstrating similar prognostication efficacy. The hazard ratios for TR and overall survival demonstrated strong, stepwise positive correlations with increases in both the ADV and AP scores, but not the MoRAL score.ConclusionsThe ADV score is a superior integrated biomarker for predicting post-resection prognosis in patients with HCC, outperforming both the AP and MoRAL scores. The AP score appears to be a simplified alternative to the ADV score, particularly in cases of small HCC.

INTRODUCTION

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is among the most prevalent cancers globally and is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality. Although hepatic resection remains a primary treatment option for patients with preserved liver function, tumor recurrence (TR) after curative surgery is a common challenge [1]. It is essential to develop precise prognostic tools in order to tailor individualized treatment strategies and improve patient outcomes.

Several predictive models have been established to more accurately estimate post-resection prognosis. A composite score that multiplies serum levels of AFP and des-γ-carboxyprothrombin (or proteins induced by vitamin K absence or antagonist-II [PIVKA-II]) and tumor volume (TV), known as the ADV score, is a comprehensive prognostic indicator for HCC after hepatic resection or liver transplantation (LT) [2345678]. This score offers quantifiable insights into tumor biology. Additionally, a simplified variant, the AP score, considers only AFP and PIVKA-II levels. Another model, the MoRAL score (model to predict TR after LT), uses AFP and PIVKA-II concentrations to predict TR after LT [91011] and also has potential applications to hepatic resection cases.

This study aimed to assess the predictive accuracy of the ADV, AP, and MoRAL scores for microvascular invasion (MVI) and to evaluate their prognostic capabilities in forecasting post-resection outcomes. This study was conducted using an international cohort comprising 9,061 patients from 83 centers.

METHODS

Ethics statement

The study protocol was approved by the ethics committees of all participating institutions, including the Institutional Review Board of Asan Medical Center (No. 2018-1508, 2025-0730). Due to the retrospective nature of this study, the requirement for informed consent was waived. All the procedures complied with the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki (2013 revision).

Study design

This retrospective observational investigation was conducted as part of an international collaborative effort established through formal agreements between the Korean Association of Hepato-Biliary-Pancreatic Surgery and the Japanese Society of Hepato-Biliary-Pancreatic Surgery [12]. Data were collected from the medical records of patients who underwent hepatic resection for HCC between January 2010 and December 2017 at 10 Korean institutions and 73 Japanese centers. The inclusion criteria were as follows: patients were older than 18 years, had a solitary tumor measuring 1.0–10 cm, and had preoperative measurements of AFP and PIVKA-II levels. Follow-up was conducted until December 2020 or until the patient died.

The primary goal was to assess the predictive accuracy of the ADV, AP, and MoRAL scores in estimating MVI and actuarial 5-year TR. The secondary objective was to evaluate the ability of these scores to predict cumulative TR and overall survival (OS) following hepatic resection.

Calculation of the ADV score

The ADV score was derived by multiplying serum levels of AFP (ng/mL) and des-γ-carboxyprothrombin (or PIVKA-II; mAU/mL) and TV (mL). The resulting value was expressed on a logarithmic scale (log10), commonly denoted as log [27]. TV was calculated based on three-dimensional measurements obtained from pathology reports.

Calculation of the AP score

The AP score was obtained by multiplying serum concentrations of AFP (ng/mL) and PIVKA-II (mAU/mL), with the final value expressed on a logarithmic scale (log10), presented as log.

Calculation of the MoRAL score

The MoRAL score was determined using the formula 11 × √PIVKA-II + 2 × √AFP, as described previously [9].

Statistical analysis

Quantitative data were expressed as medians with ranges or as mean ± standard deviation. Pearson correlation coefficient r was used to assess the strength and direction of linear relationships. Survival analyses were performed using the Kaplan-Meier method, with groups compared using the log-rank test. Multivariate prognostic factors were identified using Cox proportional hazards regression, with results reported as hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The diagnostic accuracies of various tests were evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, and areas under the curve (AUCs) were compared using DeLong test. The Youden index J, defined as sensitivity + specificity - 1, was used to determine the optimal cutoff point for test effectiveness. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. All analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics (ver. 22, IBM Corp.) and MedCalc (ver. 23.2.1, MedCalc Software Ltd.).

RESULTS

Demographic data and clinicopathological profiles

From January 2010 to December 2017, 11,500 patients underwent hepatic resection for HCC at 10 Korean institutions and 73 Japanese centers. After excluding patients with incomplete data and those with tumors smaller than 1.0 cm, 9,061 patients were included in the analysis, comprising 4,006 Korean (44.2%) and 5,055 Japanese patients (55.8%). Their clinicopathological features are summarized in Table 1. Average age was 63.8 ± 11.3 years, with males constituting 6,987 of patients (77.1%) and females constituting 2,074 (22.9%).

Right hepatectomy, right anterior sectionectomy, right posterior sectionectomy, and right trisectionectomy were performed in 921 (10.2%), 988 (10.9%), 1,087 (12.0%), and 7 patients (0.1%), respectively. Left hepatectomy, left medial sectionectomy, left lateral sectionectomy, and left trisectionectomy were performed in 614 (6.8%), 588 (6.5%), 592 (6.5%), and 4 patients (0.045%), respectively. Central bisectionectomy was performed in 277 patients (3.1%). Nonanatomical resections, such as segmentectomy and partial hepatectomy, were performed in 4,983 patients (43.5%).

Pathological examination revealed that the mean maximal tumor diameter was 3.6 ± 2.1 cm and the mean TV was 38.7 ± 72.1 mL. MVI was identified in 2,637 patients (29.1%) and macrovascular invasion was detected in 467 patients (5.2%).

Relationship between tumor size and tumor markers

Analysis of the associations between AFP (ng/mL), PIVKA-II (AU/mL), and TV (mL) revealed only weak correlations between AFP and PIVKA-II (r = 0.046, P < 0.001), TV and AFP (r = 0.188, P < 0.001), and TV and PIVKA-II (r = 0.054, P < 0.001). These findings suggest there is little to no meaningful correlation between these tumor markers and tumor size.

Relationship between the ADV, AP, and MoRAL scores

Correlation analyses demonstrated there was a strong correlation between the ADV and AP scores (r = 0.937, P < 0.001; Fig. 1A) and significant correlations between the ADV and MoRAL scores (r = 0.590, P < 0.001; Fig. 1B) and between the AP and MoRAL scores (r = 0.591, P < 0.001; Fig. 1C). Given that all 3 scores incorporate AFP and PIVKA-II levels, these results were expected.

Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis to predict microvascular invasion

ROC curve analysis assessing the ability to predict MVI yielded an AUC of 0.734 for the ADV score, with an optimal cutoff of 4.7log, a Youden index J of 0.368, sensitivity of 73.5%, and specificity of 63.4%. The AP score demonstrated an AUC of 0.684 (P < 0.001), with an optimal cutoff of 3.7log, a Youden index J of 0.272, sensitivity of 55.6%, and specificity of 71.6%. The MoRAL score showed an AUC of 0.705 (P < 0.001), with an optimal cutoff of 162.0, a Youden index J of 0.339, sensitivity of 67.9%, and specificity of 66.1% (Fig. 2A).

The difference in ROC AUCs was 0.05 when comparing the ADV and AP scores, 0.021 when comparing the AP and MoRAL scores, and 0.029 when comparing the ADV and MoRAL scores. These results indicate that the ADV score exhibits the strongest predictive ability for MVI, whereas the AP and MoRAL scores demonstrate a comparable performance in predicting MVI.

Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis to predict actuarial 5-year tumor recurrence

ROC curve analysis assessing the ability to predict actuarial 5-year TR demonstrated an AUC of 0.586 (P < 0.001) for the ADV score, with a Youden index J of 0.131 at a cutoff of 3.9log, sensitivity of 61.3%, and specificity of 51.8%. The AP score yielded an AUC of 0.583 (P < 0.001), with a Youden index J of 0.129 at a cutoff of 3.0log, sensitivity of 58.5%, and specificity of 54.4%. The MoRAL score showed an AUC of 0.586 (P < 0.001), with a Youden index J of 0.140 at a cutoff of 149.5, sensitivity of 44.7%, and specificity of 69.3% (Fig. 2B).

A comparison of predictive performances revealed minimal differences in AUCs: the difference was 0.003 (P = 0.081) when comparing the ADV and AP scores, 0.003 (P = 0.087) when comparing the AP and MoRAL scores, and less than 0.001 (P = 0.823) when comparing the ADV and MoRAL scores. These findings suggest that all 3 scores possess similar efficacy to forecast 5-year TR.

Analysis of tumor recurrence and overall survival

Among the 9,061 patients, 4,413 (48.7%) developed HCC recurrence during a follow-up period of up to 134 months. A total of 2,156 patients (23.8%) died from all causes, with 1,708 of these deaths (79.2%) attributable to HCC recurrence and 448 (20.8%) resulting from other etiologies. The cumulative incidence of TR was 21.8%, 43.6%, 52.8%, and 61.3% at 1, 3, 5, and 10 years, respectively. Correspondingly, the OS rate was 95.2%, 84.7%, 75.1%, and 62.1% at 1, 3, 5, and 10 years, respectively (Fig. 3).

Survival analysis according to microvascular invasion status and corresponding cutoffs

Prognostic analysis based on MVI revealed significant differences in TR and OS, with an HR of 1.60 (95% CI, 1.50–1.71; P < 0.001) for TR and 1.98 (95% CI, 1.80–2.18; P < 0.001) for OS (Fig. 4A).

Using a cutoff of 4.7log for the ADV score, notable prognostic disparities were observed for TR (HR, 1.48; 95% CI, 1.39–1.57; P < 0.001) and OS (HR, 1.79; 95% CI, 1.65–1.96; P < 0.001) (Fig. 4B). Similarly, a cutoff of 3.7log for the AP score demonstrated a significant prognostic influence on TR (HR, 1.53; 95% CI, 1.43–1.63; P < 0.001) and OS (HR, 1.87; 95% CI, 1.71–2.04; P < 0.001) (Fig. 4C). Additionally, a cutoff of 162.0 for the MoRAL score was associated with substantial differences in TR (HR, 1.59; 95% CI, 1.48–1.70; P < 0.001) and OS (HR, 1.97; 95% CI, 1.79–2.17; P < 0.001) (Fig. 4D).

Survival analysis according to actuarial 5-year tumor recurrence cutoffs

Survival analysis based on the optimal 5-year TR cutoffs revealed significant prognostic differences. A cutoff of 3.9log for the ADV score was associated with an increased risk of TR (HR, 1.60; 95% CI, 1.51–1.69; P < 0.001) and reduced OS (HR, 1.92; 95% CI, 1.78–2.09; P < 0.001) (Fig. 5A).

Similarly, a cutoff of 3.0log for the AP score demonstrated notable differences in prognosis, with higher risks of TR (HR, 1.46; 95% CI, 1.37–1.55; P < 0.001) and poorer OS (HR, 1.75; 95% CI, 1.60–1.90; P < 0.001) (Fig. 5B).

Furthermore, a cutoff of 149.5 for the MoRAL score was associated with significant adverse outcomes, increasing the risk of TR (HR, 1.56; 95% CI, 1.46–1.67; P < 0.001) and decreasing OS (HR, 1.89; 95% CI, 1.73–2.08; P < 0.001) (Fig. 5C).

Prognostic impact of satellite nodules on actuarial 5-year tumor recurrence

In patients without satellite nodules, cutoffs of 3.9log for the ADV score, AP score of 3.0log and MoRAL score of 149.5 showed significant prognostic contrasts in both TR (all P < 0.001) and OS TR (all P < 0.001). In addition, in patients with satellite nodules, these 3 cutoffs also showed significant prognostic contrasts in both TR (all P < 0.001) and OS TR (all P < 0.001).

Confined to patients with an ADV score <3.9log, the actuarial 5-year TR rates were 45.1% and 66.5% in patients without and with satellite nodules, respectively (P < 0.001). In patients with ADV score ≥3.9log, the actuarial 5-year TR rates were 55.4% and 79.8%, in patients without and with satellite nodules, respectively (P < 0.001).

Confined to patients with AP score < 3.0log, the actuarial 5-year TR rates were 45.8% and 67.5% in patients without and with satellite nodules, respectively (P < 0.001). In patients with AP score ≥3.0log, the actuarial 5-year TR rates were 55.2% and 79.6%, in patients without and with satellite nodules, respectively (P < 0.001).

Confined to patients with MoRAL score <149.5, the actuarial 5-year TR rates were 46.0% and 70.4% in patients without and with satellite nodules, respectively (P < 0.001). In patients with MoRAL score ≥149.5, the actuarial 5-year TR rates were 58.9% and 80.7%, in patients without and with satellite nodules, respectively (P < 0.001).

These results indicate that the prognostic influence of satellite nodules was not adequately captured by the ADV, AP, and MoRAL scores, suggesting the presence of satellite nodules as an independent prognostic factor.

Validation of the prognostication power of the ADV score across 1.0log intervals

Validation of the prognostic significance of the ADV score across 1.0log (10-fold) intervals revealed distinct stratification. In total, 404 (4.5%), 1,610 (17.8%), 2,227 (24.6%), 1,780 (19.6%), 1,261 (13.9%), 766 (8.5%), 492 (5.4%), 278 (3.1%), 146 (1.6%), and 97 patients (1.1%) had scores of <2.0log, 2.0–2.9log, 3.0–3.9log, 4.0–4.9log, 5.0–5.9log, 6.0–6.9log, 7.0–7.9log, 8.0–8.9log, 9.0–9.9log, and ≥10.0log, respectively.

The HRs for TR and OS increased as the ADV score increased. Specifically, scores <2.0log had HRs of 1.0 for both TR and OS, while scores of 2.0–2.9log had HRs of 1.12 (95% CI, 0.97–1.30) for TR and 1.10 (95% CI, 0.88–1.36) for OS. For scores of 3.0–3.9log, the HRs were 1.29 (95% CI, 1.12–1.49) for TR and 1.52 (95% CI, 1.23–1.88) for OS. Higher score intervals had progressively increasing HRs: 4.0–4.9log (HRs, 1.52 for TR and 1.79 for OS), 5.0–5.9log (HRs, 1.68 for TR and 2.22 for OS), 6.0–6.9log (HRs, 1.94 for TR and 2.62 for OS), 7.0–7.9log (HRs, 2.30 for TR and 2.86 for OS), 8.0–8.9log (HRs, 2.89 for TR and 4.07 for OS), 9.0–9.9log (HRs, 2.62 for TR and 4.84 for OS), and ≥10.0log (HRs, 3.70 for TR and 5.56 for OS). The HRs for TR and OS demonstrated strong, stepwise positive correlations with each 1.0log increase of the ADV score (both P < 0.001) (Fig. 6A).

Validation of the prognostication power of the ADV score across 2.0log-intervals

ADV scores stratified into 2.0log (100-fold) intervals revealed distinct patient distributions: 404 (4.5%), 3,837 (42.3%), 3,041 (33.6%), 1,258 (13.9%), and 521 (5.7%) patients had scores of <2.0log, 2.0–3.9log, 4.0–5.9log, 6.0–7.9log, and ≥8.0log, respectively.

The HRs for TR and OS progressively increased as the ADV score increased. Specifically, the HRs for scores <2.0log were 1.0 for both TR and OS. For scores of 2.0–3.9log, the HRs were 1.22 (95% CI, 1.07–1.40) for TR and 1.34 (95% CI, 1.10–1.64) for OS. For scores of 4.0–5.9log, the HRs increased to 1.58 (95% CI, 1.38–1.82) for TR and 1.97 (95% CI, 1.60–2.42) for OS. For scores of 6.0–7.9log, the HRs rose to 2.07 (95% CI, 1.77–2.42) for TR and 2.71 (95% CI, 2.16–3.39) for OS. For scores ≥10.0log, the HRs were 2.94 (95% CI, 2.41–3.59) for TR and 4.55 (95% CI, 3.44–6.01) for OS. The correlation between HRs and 2.0log stepwise increases of the ADV score was statistically significant for both TR and OS (P < 0.001) (Fig. 7).

Validation of the prognostication power of the AP score across 1.0log intervals

Validation of prognostic performance of the AP score across 1.0log (10-fold) intervals revealed distinct patient distributions: 1,236 (13.9%), 2,995 (33.1%), 2,027 (22.4%), 1,414 (15.6%), 708 (7.8%), 403 (4.4%), and 251 patients (2.8%) had scores of <2.0log, 2.0–2.9log, 3.0–3.9log, 4.0–4.9log, 5.0–5.9log, 6.0–6.9log, and ≥7.0log, respectively.

The HRs for TR and OS progressively increased as the AP score increased. Specifically, the HRs were 1.0 for both TR and OS for scores <2.0log, 1.42 (95% CI, 1.29–1.55) for TR and 1.30 (95% CI, 1.14–1.49) for OS for scores of 2.0–2.9log, 1.63 (95% CI, 1.48–1.80) for TR and 1.72 (95% CI, 1.50–1.99) for OS for scores of 3.0–3.9log, 1.79 (95% CI, 1.61–1.99) for TR and 2.06 (95% CI, 1.76–2.40) for OS for scores of 4.0–4.9log, 2.16 (95% CI, 1.89–2.47) for TR and 2.51 (95% CI, 2.07–3.04) for OS for scores of 5.0–5.9log, 2.62 (95% CI, 2.20–3.13) for TR and 3.29 (95% CI, 2.57–4.21) for OS for scores of 6.0–6.9log, and 2.99 (95% CI, 2.38–3.74) for TR and 4.12 (95% CI, 3.02–5.62) for OS for scores ≥8.0log. The correlation between HRs and 1.0log stepwise increases of the AP score was highly significant for both TR and OS (P < 0.001) (Fig. 6B).

Validation of the prognostication power of the MoRAL score across 100-point intervals

Validation of the prognostic accuracy of the MoRAL score across 100-point intervals demonstrated a left-skewed distribution of patient scores. In total, 4,427 (48.9%), 1,982 (21.9%), 833 (9.2%), 421 (4.6%), 330 (3.6%), 200 (2.2%), 169 (1.9%), 119 (1.3%), 86 (0.9%), 52 (0.6%), and 442 patients (4.9%) had scores of <100, 100–199, 200–299, 300–399, 400–499, 500–599, 600–699, 700–799, 800–899, 900–999, and ≥1,000, respectively.

The HRs for TR and OS increased as the MoRAL score increased, but the relationship was not a simple linear trend with each 100-point increase. Specifically, the HRs for scores <100 were set at 1.0 for both TR and OS. For scores of 100–199, the HRs were 1.26 (95% CI, 1.17–1.36) for TR and 1.42 (95% CI, 1.28–1.58) for OS. For scores of 200–299, the HRs rose to 1.43 (95% CI, 1.28–1.59) for TR and 1.87 (95% CI, 1.60–2.18) for OS. Subsequent intervals showed progressively higher HRs for TR and OS: 300–399 (HRs, 1.73 and 1.88, respectively), 400–499 (HRs, 1.84 and 2.37, respectively), 500–599 (HRs, 1.84 and 2.78, respectively), 600–699 (HRs, 1.59 and 2.12, respectively), 700–799 (HRs, 2.41 and 2.77, respectively), 800–899 (HRs, 2.46 and 2.64, respectively), 900–999 (HRs, 1.80 and 2.34, respectively), and ≥1,000 (HRs, 2.18 and 3.14, respectively). Despite these trends, the HRs for TR and OS did not exhibit a strictly positive correlation with each incremental 100-point increase of the MoRAL score (both P < 0.001) (Fig. 6C).

Analysis of risk factors for tumor recurrence and overall survival

Univariate analyses identified several significant prognostic factors for both disease-free survival and OS, including type of resection, MVI, macrovascular invasion, satellite nodules, bile duct invasion, the ADV score using a cutoff of 3.9log, the AP score using a cutoff of 3.0log, and the MoRAL score using a cutoff of 149.5. All these factors demonstrated highly significant associations (all P < 0.001), except OS, in relation to the type of resection (P = 0.112). Multivariate analyses confirmed that most of these variables were independent predictors of disease-free survival, except for the AP score due to collinearity with the ADV score. For OS, all factors, except type of resection and the AP score, remained independent risk predictors (Table 2).

DISCUSSION

It is inherently challenging to accurately forecast post-resection outcomes in patients with HCC because of the biological heterogeneity of the tumor, complex variability of the tumor burden, and differences in underlying liver disease. Preoperative imaging analyses have identified several prognostic indicators including tumor size and number on computed tomography, enhancement patterns on magnetic resonance imaging, and heightened metabolic activity on fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography. Additionally, serum tumor markers, such as AFP and PIVKA-II, have predictive value for patient prognosis. Postoperative pathological features, including MVI, tumor differentiation, satellite nodules, and lymph node metastasis, are also important prognostic factors that contribute to the development of various tumor staging systems [13141516].

Although tumor size has long been regarded as a key prognostic indicator in HCC, a large tumor size alone does not invariably predict poorer outcomes [11718]. The expression levels of HCC tumor markers such as AFP and PIVKA-II exhibit considerable variability and are independent of each other, as demonstrated in the current study. Use of both biomarkers improves prognostic accuracy beyond that achieved by each marker individually [19202122].

Although MVI is a critical prognostic factor in HCC, its diagnosis prior to surgery remains challenging because it is primarily a pathological finding. Numerous studies aimed to predict MVI using various imaging techniques and tumor markers [1920212223242526]. In this study, ROC curve analysis demonstrated AUCs of 0.734, 0.684, and 0.705 for the ADV, AP, and MoRAL scores, respectively. The ADV score exhibited the strongest predictive capability for MVI, whereas the AP and MoRAL scores had comparable predictive performances.

The ADV score was initially developed to combine tumor size and the tumor markers AFP and PIVKA-II based on a cohort of treatment-naïve patients with solitary HCC from a single center [2]. From a biological perspective, tumor size and number are quantitative variables, whereas tumor marker expression is a qualitative variable. It is essential to integrate these diverse components in order to construct reliable surrogate biomarkers that accurately reflect tumor aggressiveness. Multiple validation studies involving patients with multiple tumors demonstrated that the ADV score can effectively predict clinical outcomes in such cases. In these patients, the total TV was calculated by multiplying the TV of the largest tumor by the number of tumors, which resulted in a total TV that was significantly higher than the sum of individual TVs. This approach reliably captures the adverse prognostic implications of tumor multiplicity and can be applied to patients undergoing both hepatic resection and LT [2345678].

The ADV score can be significantly influenced by preoperative locoregional treatment. Our previous studies examined the prognostic significance of the ADV score in patients with downstaged or recurrent HCC following such treatment [35]. When a complete radiologic or pathological response is lacking, residual viable tumor tissue remains, allowing the ADV score to be calculated both before and after hepatic resection. In patients who have undergone transcatheter arterial chemoembolization prior to surgery, TV can be estimated by measuring the contrast-enhancing portions of the tumor, with non-enhancing lipidolized zones considered non-viable according to the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST) [27], thereby accounting for the therapeutic effects of locoregional interventions. Consistent correlations between the size of viable tumors on preoperative imaging and post-resection or in explant pathology have been documented [282930]. Consequently, preoperative ADV scores derived from imaging and postoperative ADV scores based on pathological examinations are comparable and have similar prognostic implications [37].

The ADV score is widely recognized as a robust, quantifiable indicator that captures the biological characteristics of HCC and is applicable to patients undergoing hepatic resection or LT [2-8]. This study demonstrated that TR and OS were strongly associated with the ADV score. Furthermore, prognostic stratification at 1.0log and 2.0log intervals revealed that survival outcomes are significantly influenced by the ADV score, underscoring its value in clinical risk assessment.

The AP score is a continuous prognostic indicator calculated solely by multiplying the concentrations of AFP and PIVKA-II and is a simplified alternative to the ADV score. Prognostic stratification at 1.0log intervals demonstrated that both TR and OS were strongly influenced by the AP score, mirroring observations with the ADV score. The patient distribution pattern closely resembled that of the ADV score. Given that TV in early-stage HCC is typically small, the AP score offers a practical and clinically applicable option for prognosis, without the need for TV calculation.

The MoRAL score was originally developed as a predictive model for TR following LT [91011]. The cutoff for transplant eligibility is 314.8, which corresponds to the 75th percentile [9]. Similar to the ADV and AP scores, the MoRAL score is a continuous quantifiable metric. Our evaluation compared the ADV and MoRAL scores from 2 perspectives. First, ROC curve analysis incorporating 5-year actuarial TR data demonstrated that the AUC of the MoRAL score was comparable to that of the ADV score (0.586 vs. 0.586; P = 0.823). The Youden index J at a cutoff of 149.5 for the MoRAL score was 0.140, with sensitivity of 44.7% and specificity of 69.3%, aligning closely with the cutoff of 3.9log for the ADV score. These findings indicate that the prognostic capabilities of the ADV and MoRAL scores are essentially equivalent. However, the HR for disease-free survival was higher for the ADV score than for the MoRAL score (1.55 vs. 1.46), and a similar trend was observed for OS (1.35 vs. 1.26). This finding suggests that the ADV score is a more precise and robust prognostic tool. A distinction between the 2 scores lies in their calculation approaches; while both integrate AFP and PIVKA-II, the methods differ significantly. Despite these differences, correlation analysis revealed a moderate association between the scores, with a correlation coefficient of r = 0.590, implying that incorporation of TV provides valuable prognostic information regardless of the calculation method used. However, while the ADV and AP scores demonstrated excellent prognostic accuracy when stratified into 1.0log intervals, the MoRAL score did not perform reliably when stratified into 100-point intervals. This indicates that the MoRAL score is less suitable as a continuous, quantifiable predictor because it is challenging to establish simple and intuitive cutoff stratification.

The primary distinction between the ADV and MoRAL scores lies in the inclusion of tumor-specific factors, such as tumor number and size, both of which are well-established prognostic indicators in HCC. Post-resection outcomes progressively worsen with increasing tumor size, especially within the range of 1–10 cm [131]. Larger HCC tumors tend to exhibit more aggressive biological behavior, which is often associated with MVI and elevated levels of AFP and PIVKA-II. Consequently, patient prognosis worsens with tumor growth. Risk factor analysis in this study confirmed that traditional prognostic indicators such as nonanatomical resection, MVI, macrovascular invasion, and satellite nodules were significant predictors of outcomes.

Pathologically, HCC with satellite nodules is classified as a solitary HCC, not as multiple HCCs. Satellite nodules are defined as small daughter nodules located within the same hepatic segment or in close proximity to the main tumor, usually within 2 cm. They represent intrahepatic metastases from the primary lesion rather than independent multicentric tumors. Thus, the presence of satellite nodules indicates intrahepatic spread of a single primary HCC. In patients either with or without satellite nodules, the cutoffs of 3.9log for the ADV score, AP score of 3.0log, and MoRAL score of 149.5 showed significant prognostic contrasts in both TR and OS. In contrast, the presence of satellite nodules was associated with significant prognostic differences in patients both below and above these 3 cutoffs, demonstrating that satellite nodules constitute an independent risk factor, similar to MVI, macrovascular invasion, and bile duct invasion.

This study has several limitations. It was a retrospective cohort analysis involving patients from 2 Asian countries, and the prognostic value of the ADV score needs to be validated across diverse geographic regions and ethnicities. Additionally, the ADV score cannot be calculated in settings where PIVKA-II is not routinely measured as part of preoperative blood testing. Nonetheless, a significant strength of this study is that it analyzed one of the largest high-volume cohorts globally, comprising 9,061 patients.

In conclusion, this validation study established that the ADV score is a superior integrated biomarker for predicting post-resection prognosis in patients with HCC, outperforming both the AP and MoRAL scores. The AP score appears to be a simplified alternative to the ADV score, particularly in cases of small HCC. Utilization of the ADV and AP scores for prognostic assessment offers reliable insights that can aid treatment planning and facilitate personalized post-resection surveillance tailored to each patient’s relative risk of recurrence.

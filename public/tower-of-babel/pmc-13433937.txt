Tumor treating field plus chemotherapy for locally advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma: An international cost-effectiveness analysis
Authors: Kun Liu, Youwen Zhu, Manting Zeng, Hong Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The PANOVA-3 trial has demonstrated that adding tumor treating fields (TTFields) to standard chemotherapy improves survival outcomes in locally advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma (LA-PAC). However, given the high cost of such novel therapies, it is essential to evaluate their economic impact to ensure a balanced understanding of their clinical and financial value. A three-state Markov model was devised to compare the cost-effectiveness of TTFields combined with chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone, using clinical data from the PANOVA-3 trial. Analyses were conducted from both American and Chinese healthcare payer perspectives, with willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds set at $150,000 and $40,026 per quality-adjusted life year (QALY), respectively. Lifetime costs, incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs), and incremental net health benefits (INHBs) were estimated. Sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate the robustness of the model outcomes. The combination of TTFields and chemotherapy resulted in incremental lifetime costs of $147,766 and $39,470 and additional gains of 0.148 and 0.142 QALYs in the USA and China, respectively. This corresponds to ICERs of $1,001,995/QALY (USA) and $278,708/QALY (China), both exceeding their respective WTP thresholds. Sensitivity analyses identified TTFields device cost as the most influential parameter, confirming model stability across a range of assumptions. TTFields plus chemotherapy was not a cost-effective strategy for patients with LA-PAC at the current price, but it can be used as a clinical alternative strategy with health effects.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-54203-x.

Introduction

Pancreatic adenocarcinoma remains the twelfth most cancer and the sixth most prominent driver of cancer-related deaths worldwide in 2022. That year, 510,566 new cases and 467,005 deaths were reported, with the United States and China accounting for approximately 11% and 23% of cases, respectively1,2. At the time of diagnosis, nearly 30% of patients present with unresectable locally advanced pancreatic cancer (LA-PAC), a stage associated with poor prognosis, median overall survival (OS) ranges from 8 to 14 months, and the 5-year survival rate is only 8%-10%3,4. To date, gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel with or without radiotherapy has served as the standard of care for LA-PAC, although these regimens were largely validated in phase III trials involving metastatic or mixed-stage populations5,6. Unfortunately, many studies have failed to demonstrate meaningful survival improvements in this subgroup7,8, underscoring the urgent need for novel therapies and the inherent difficulty of conducting rigorous clinical trials in this patient population.

Tumor treating fields (TTFields) therapy represents a recent advance as a fourth modality in cancer treatment9,10, offering a non-invasive, biophysical approach that disrupts tumor cell mitosis using low-intensity, intermediate-frequency alternating electric fields. This mechanism interferes with cell division, suppresses tumor proliferation, and induces apoptosis11. The phase III PANOVA-3 trial (NCT03377491) provided compelling evidence that TTFields combined with chemotherapy significantly improves OS compared to chemotherapy alone in unresectable LA-PAC (median OS: 16.2 vs. 14.2 months; hazard ratio [HR]: 0.82; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.68-0.99; P = 0.039), with a favorable trend observed for progression-free survival (PFS) as well (median PFS: 10.6 vs. 9.3 months; HR: 0.85; 95% CI: 0.68-1.05; P = 0.137)12. These findings support the broader application of TTFields and may inform future treatment guidelines.

Despite the promising clinical outcomes, the high cost of TTFields highlights the importance of evaluating its economic value. To determine whether the health benefits justify the financial investment, especially in resource-constrained settings, rigorous cost-effectiveness analyses are needed. Accordingly, this study explored the economic viability of first-line TTFields plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone for patients with LA-PAC from the perspectives of American and Chinese healthcare payers.

Materials and methods

The current analysis utilized clinical data and patient characteristics from the PANOVA-3 trial12. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations by the CHEERS 2022 statement13 (Supplementary Table S1). The requirement for ethical approval for this study and the need for informed consent was waived by Xiangya Hospital of Central South University ethics committee.

Patients and treatments

A total of 571 patients with previously untreated, unresectable LA-PAC were included in the model. Participants were randomized to receive either TTFields (150 kHz for ≥18 hours/day) in combination with chemotherapy consisting of gemcitabine (1000 mg/m2) and nab-paclitaxel (125 mg/m2) administered on days 1, 8, and 15 of each 28-day cycle or chemotherapy alone12 (Supplementary Table S2). Average body surface area (BSA) values used for chemotherapy dosing were 1.84 m2 for American and 1.72 m2 for Chinese patients, based on previous literature14,15. Upon disease progression or discontinuation due to intolerable adverse events (AEs), second-line treatment with modified FOLFIRINOX (oxaliplatin 85 mg/m2, irinotecan 180 mg/m2, fluorouracil 2400 mg/m2, and folinic acid 400 mg/m2 every two weeks) was introduced, following both the PANOVA-3 protocol and international clinical guidelines6,12 (Supplementary Table S2). Best supportive care (BSC) and terminal care were also incorporated into the analysis6.

Model development

A decision-analytic Markov model was constructed to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of TTFields plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone in unresectable LA-PAC12. The model included three mutually exclusive health states representing the clinical course of the disease: PFS, progressive disease (PD), and death (see Supplementary Figure S1). Each cycle of the model corresponded to a 4-week interval, with a 10-year time horizon sufficient to account for over 99% mortality in the cohort. Annual discount rates of 3% and 5% were applied to costs and health outcomes for the USA and Chinese models, respectively15,16. Outcomes included total and incremental costs, life-years (LYs), quality-adjusted LYs (QALYs), and incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs). Progression and survival estimates

Survival probabilities for LA-PAC patients were derived from the OS and PFS curves reported in the PANOVA-3 trial. Data points were extracted using GetData Graph Digitizer (version 2.26). Considering that we have set 4 weeks as one cycle period, we extracted data points every four weeks. The number of data points to be taken is determined by the duration of time as per the results of the clinical trial. All the values of the data points were used for the survival curve fitting. All data points fitted to several standard parametric survival models, including Weibull, exponential, Gompertz, log-normal, and log-logistic distributions. Model fit was evaluated using the Akaike and Bayesian Information Criteria. The Weibull distribution, widely used for modeling cancer progression due to its flexibility and ability to model increasing or decreasing hazards over time, was selected based on goodness-of-fit criteria and prior studies14,15 (Supplementary Figure S2 and Table S3). From the fitted Weibull functions, scale (λ) and shape (γ) parameters were derived, allowing for the generation of Kaplan-Meier curves in R (version 4.4.6) (Table 1). The transition probabilities for each 4-week cycle (tu) were computed using the following time-dependent formula: tp(tu)=1 − exp{λ(t − u)γ − λtγ} (λ > 0, γ > 0). This equation was used to model disease progression over the Markov cycles and estimate state transitions accurately over time15.

Utility and cost inputs

Patient quality of life (QoL) was quantified using utility values, which represent health states on a continuum from 0 (equivalent to death) to 1 (perfect health). According to previously published literature, the mean utility scores for patients in the PFS and PD states were set at 0.730 and 0.850, respectively15,16. In addition, decrements in utility due to AEs of grade 3 or higher were incorporated into the model, drawing from established data sources14,15,17–19.

Direct medical costs encompassed expenses for pharmacologic treatments, AE management, drug administration, laboratory testing, imaging procedures, BSC, and terminal care (Table 2). USA-based cost data were collected from multiple sources, including NovoCure Ltd., the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, and peer-reviewed publications14,16,17,20. Corresponding data for China were obtained from NovoCure Ltd., local hospital databases, and previously published studies15,21–23. All USA costs were adjusted to 2025 USD using the Consumer Price Index24, whereas Chinese costs were left unadjusted due to the relative stability of domestic medical pricing. Sensitivity analyses

Model outcome robustness was explored through deterministic (one-way) and probabilistic sensitivity analyses. One-way sensitivity analysis was used to evaluate how variations in individual parameters (within ±20% of baseline estimates) impacted the ICER14,15. In contrast, probabilistic sensitivity analysis simultaneously varied all input parameters using probability distributions across 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations 14,15. Cost-effectiveness acceptability curves were generated for both the U.S. and Chinese settings to estimate the probability that each treatment strategy would be deemed cost-effective at various willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds, including $150,000/QALY for the U.S. and $40,026/QALY for China.

Results

Baseline results

The Markov model projected that patients treated with TTFields combined with chemotherapy in the USA would experience a mean survival of 1.332 life-years (15.984 months), an increase of 0.196 life-years (2.352 months) over those receiving chemotherapy alone. For patients in China, the corresponding figures were 1.303 life-years (15.636 months) with TTFields plus chemotherapy, compared to a gain of 0.188 life-years (2.256 months) over chemotherapy alone. When adjusted for quality of life, the estimated benefit of TTFields plus chemotherapy increased to 1.008 QALYs and 0.986 QALYs in the USA and China, respectively. These values represent an improvement of 0.148 QALYs and 0.142 QALYs compared to chemotherapy alone. However, these clinical gains came with substantial financial costs. Sensitivity analyses

One-way sensitivity analysis (Fig. 1) identified the cost of TTFields as the most influential factor affecting the ICER. When this cost ranged from $7,450 to $31,124, the ICER varied substantially, from $229,540/QALY to $1,133,793/QALY. Despite this variation, the ICER consistently remained above the WTP thresholds in both countries. Other factors exerting a notable impact included the incidence of severe AEs, utility values for health states, and disutility associated with AEs. Discussion

Over the past decade, pancreatic cancer has remained among the most lethal malignancies, with limited improvements in outcomes despite advances in multimodal systemic therapies. Selecting an optimal first-line treatment for patients diagnosed with highly aggressive, poor-prognosis tumors is a critical component of care planning3,4. Among emerging treatment modalities, TTFields, which has been proposed as a fourth modality of cancer therapy, has garnered increasing attention for its potential to extend survival in various cancer types9,12,25. However, the substantial financial burden associated with novel oncologic interventions, coupled with constrained healthcare resources, poses significant challenges for both patients and health systems. Consequently, evaluating the cost-effectiveness of these innovations is essential for fostering value-based healthcare delivery.

Previous economic evaluations of TTFields have yielded mixed results. For example, Guzauskas et al. found that the addition of TTFields to standard therapy for glioblastoma was cost-effective in both the USA and China, based on the EF-14 trial outcomes26,27. In contrast, analyses of the LUNAR trial by Liu et al. and Pei et al. determined that incorporating TTFields in treating non-small cell lung cancer did not meet cost-effectiveness criteria in either setting17,28. Given the encouraging clinical benefits demonstrated in the PANOVA-3 trial, which investigated TTFields combined with chemotherapy for patients with LA-PAC12, the present study sought to evaluate, for the first time, the cost-effectiveness of this approach versus chemotherapy alone as a first-line treatment in both the USA and Chinese healthcare contexts.

These findings indicate that although the addition of TTFields offers modest gains in QALYs of 0.148 in the U.S. and 0.142 in China, these benefits come at a substantial financial cost. Specifically, the incremental costs were $147,766 in the USA and $39,470 in China, resulting in ICERs of $1,001,995/QALY and $278,708/QALY, respectively. These values far exceed the commonly accepted WTP thresholds in both countries. Even under hypothetical conditions where TTFields were provided at no cost, the strategy failed to achieve cost-effectiveness, underscoring the economic limitations of the intervention under current conditions. This observation highlights broader issues in the pricing and adoption of oncology innovations. While indication-specific pricing has been proposed as a means to improve cost-effectiveness by aligning price with clinical value29,30. While there is a need to overcome administrative and Medicare formulation challenges, indication-specific and value-based pricing efforts may help ensure great alignment between costs and efficacy for innovative treatments, supporting the development of more effective options for consideration by patients and clinicians. However, innovative therapies that are usually of great benefit to patients may be approved by international guidelines such as NCCN and CSCO, but they do not incorporate the results of cost-effectiveness analyses into their main guidelines and publications or even make them the main approval conditions. Instead, they are mentioned less and less obviously. Our results urge and call for the cost-benefit analysis to also be a key focus in the examination of innovative solutions, promoting the rational management of healthcare costs. When developing new treatment options, individual enterprises or practitioners need to make significant reforms in balancing efficacy, safety, and economic sustainability, and strive to adopt value-based care to effectively shift national costs and thereby popularize it among a wide range of patients.

WTP thresholds serve as critical reference points for determining whether a treatment provides sufficient value for its cost31. Although this study applied a conventional WTP threshold of $150,000/QALY for the USA, some researchers argue that higher thresholds ranging from $300,000 to $500,000/QALY may be justifiable in certain context32. Under the model developed in this study, TTFields plus chemotherapy becomes cost-effective in the U.S. only at a WTP threshold exceeding $910,000/QALY. In China, where the threshold is defined as three times the per capita GDP, TTFields plus chemotherapy similarly fails to meet cost-effectiveness criteria, even when hypothetically adopting the higher U.S. threshold. Only when China’s WTP reaches approximately $255,000/QALY would the combined regimen approach cost-effectiveness. Regional analyses within China, spanning thresholds from $22,484/QALY to $95,269/QALY, confirm that TTFields plus chemotherapy remains economically unviable under current conditions. Importantly, the rising cost of new anticancer treatments has introduced the concept of “financial toxicity,” which describes the economic strain these therapies place on patients and healthcare systems alike. This burden may compromise adherence, reduce quality of life, and ultimately worsen clinical outcomes19. Thus, balancing innovation with affordability is a central concern for healthcare systems worldwide. Ensuring access to novel therapies must go hand-in-hand with efforts to mitigate their financial impact. This highlights the need for drug approval and reimbursement policies to consider both survival benefits and country-specific economic conditions. Comprehensive cost-effectiveness analyses can inform public insurance programs and guide resource allocation, facilitating more rational and sustainable use of limited healthcare funds.

Nonetheless, this study has several limitations. First, the model relies heavily on clinical data from the PANOVA-3 trial, which is the only phase III randomized controlled trial to date evaluating TTFields as a first-line treatment for unresectable LA-PAC. Any methodological limitations or biases in this trial may have influenced these results. Second, long-term projections of treatment outcomes were extrapolated from trial data, introducing inherent uncertainty into the analysis. Third, the model could not be stratified by patient subgroups, as the PANOVA-3 trial did not provide Kaplan-Meier survival curves for specific subsets. Furthermore, subgroup analyses may not preserve the balance achieved by randomization, warranting cautious interpretation. Finally, while the analysis included disutility values for grade 3 or higher AEs, it did not incorporate costs or impacts of lower-grade (grade 1-2) AEs. However, one-way sensitivity analysis suggested that these factors had minimal influence on model stability.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that the addition of TTFields to standard chemotherapy does not represent a cost-effective first-line treatment strategy for patients with unresectable LA-PAC in either the United States or China under current pricing and economic assumptions. Despite modest gains in survival and QALYs, the associated costs significantly exceed established WTP thresholds. These findings provide important guidance for oncologists and patients in selecting appropriate therapeutic strategies and offer valuable evidence to inform healthcare policy and reimbursement decisions across diverse medical systems.

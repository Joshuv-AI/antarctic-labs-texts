Abstract




Effective Field Theory and In-In Correlators

Department of Physics, University of California at San Diego, La Jolla, CA 92093, USA

Abstract

The predictions of inflation are usually defined in terms of equal time in-in correlation functions in an accelerating cosmological background. These same observables exist for quantum field theory in other spacetimes, including flat space. In this paper, we will explore how the Wilsonian renormalization group (RG) and effective field theory (EFT) apply to these observables in both flat and de Sitter space. Specifically, we show that matching the short- and long-distance calculations requires additional terms localized at the time of the measurement that are not captured by the effective action of the EFT. These additional terms only correct the local and semi-local terms in the EFT correlators. In flat space, we give an explicit demonstration by matching in-in correlators of light scalars interacting with a heavy field with the EFT result. We then show how these additional terms arise generically via exact RG. We also compare these explicit results in flat space with the corresponding theory in de Sitter and show that the local terms typically redshift away. Our results are closely related to momentum space entanglement that arises from tracing over short-wavelength modes.

2.3
EFT, Boundary Terms, and Entanglement

One of the advantages of the wavefunction method is to make the process of integrating out the heavy field transparent, as we can simply carry out the integral in
\bar{\sigma}
in the path integral. This is understood as a trace over the heavy field. Specifically, the UV wavefunction is given by
\Psi[\phi,\sigma]
and therefore integrating out
\sigma
corresponds to the reduced density matrix

\rho[\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}^{\prime}]={\rm Tr}_{\bar{\sigma}}|\bar{\phi},\bar{\sigma}\rangle\langle\bar{\phi}^{\prime},\bar{\sigma}|=\int{\cal D}\sigma\Psi[\bar{\phi},\bar{\sigma}]\Psi^{*}[\bar{\phi}^{\prime},\bar{\sigma}]\ .

(2.23)

A priori, it is not obvious that integrating out
\sigma
is necessarily described in terms of a (reduced) density matrix. For example, we can rewrite the wavefunction, by completing the square, as

\displaystyle\log\Psi\supset

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}k\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}}\bar{\phi}_{-\vec{k}}-\frac{1}{2}E_{\vec{k}}\left(\bar{\sigma}_{\vec{k}}+\frac{\lambda}{k_{12}+E_{\vec{k}}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{1}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{2}}\right)\left(\bar{\sigma}_{-\vec{k}}+\frac{\lambda}{k_{34}+E_{-\vec{k}}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{3}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{4}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2(k_{1}+k_{2}+E_{k})(k_{3}+k_{4}+E_{k})E_{k}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{1}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{2}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{3}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{4}}+``t"+``u"

\displaystyle+\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{(k_{1}+E_{2}+k)(k_{3}+E_{4}+k)(k_{1}+E_{2}+k_{3}+E_{4})}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{1}}\bar{\sigma}_{\vec{k}_{2}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{3}}\bar{\sigma}_{\vec{k}_{4}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{(k_{1}+k_{2}+E_{k})(k_{3}+k_{4}+E_{k})(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}+k_{4})}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{1}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{2}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{3}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{4}}+``t"+``u"

\displaystyle+\cdots

(2.24)

One might imagine integrating out
\sigma
directly at the wavefunction level to arrive at an effective
\Psi_{\rm EFT}[\phi]
that is still a pure state. However, we can still compute the density matrix, in which case we have

\displaystyle\rho[\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}^{\prime}]=\int{\cal D}\sigma\exp\Bigg[-\frac{k}{2}\left(\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}}\bar{\phi}_{-\vec{k}}+\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{-\vec{k}}\right)

\displaystyle-E_{\vec{k}}\left(\bar{\sigma}_{\vec{k}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lambda}{k_{12}+E_{\vec{k}}}(\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{1}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{2}}+\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{1}}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{2}})\right)\left(\bar{\sigma}_{-\vec{k}}+\frac{\lambda}{k_{34}+E_{-\vec{k}}}(\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{3}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{4}}+\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{3}}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{4}})\right)

(2.25)

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2(k_{1}+k_{2}+E_{k})(k_{3}+k_{4}+E_{k})E_{k}}(\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{1}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{2}}+\phi^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{1}}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{2}})(\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{3}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{4}}+\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{3}}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{4}})+``t"+``u"+\ldots\Bigg]\ .

We can perform the integral over
\bar{\sigma}
by a change of variables,

\bar{\sigma}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}}=\bar{\sigma}_{\vec{k}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lambda}{k_{12}+E_{\vec{k}}}(\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{1}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{2}}+\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{1}}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{2}})\ ,

(2.26)

to arrive at a Gaussian integral for the second line. From the cross-terms of the form
\bar{\phi}^{2}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}{}^{2}
that remain, we see this is not equivalent to a pure state defined by some
\Psi_{\rm EFT}
. Interestingly, this is not manifest from the expression for the in-in correlators directly, where

\langle{\rm in}|{\cal O}[\phi]|{\rm in}\rangle=\int D\bar{\phi}\,{\cal O}[\bar{\phi}]\,\rho[\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}]\ ,

(2.27)

as the expression for the diagonal entries of the density matrix, with
\bar{\phi}=\bar{\phi}^{\prime}
, could be represented by an effective wavefunction. To avoid confusion, we use
r_{{\cal O}_{1}..{\cal O}_{n}}
to represent terms in
\log\rho
associated with the operator in the subscript (in analogy with the wavefunction coefficients
\psi_{\phi_{1}..\phi_{n}}
for example). This notation is introduced to emphasize that these coefficients are not necessary wavefunction coefficients, but may only appear in the reduced density matrix. Concretely, the terms relevant to the in-in correlators in the EFT include

\log\rho[\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}]=-\frac{1}{2}r_{\phi^{2}}\bar{\phi}_{{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}}\bar{\phi}_{-{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}}+r_{\phi^{4}}\bar{\phi}_{1}\bar{\phi}_{2}\bar{\phi}_{3}\bar{\phi}_{4}+\cdots

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}_{{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{4E_{k}(k_{1}+E_{3}+k)(k_{2}+E_{k}+k)(k_{1}+E_{3}+k_{2}+E_{k})}(\bar{\phi}_{1}\bar{\phi}_{2}+\bar{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}\bar{\phi}_{2}^{\prime})\bar{\sigma}_{3}+``1\leftrightarrow 2"

\displaystyle\log\rho[\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}]

\displaystyle\supset r_{\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\phi_{3}\phi_{4}}\bar{\phi}_{1}\bar{\phi}_{2}\bar{\phi}_{3}\bar{\phi}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}(E_{k}+k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}+k_{4})}{(k_{1}+k_{2}+E_{k})(k_{3}+k_{4}+E_{k})(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}+k_{4})E_{k}}\bar{\phi}_{1}\bar{\phi}_{2}\bar{\phi}_{3}\bar{\phi}_{4}\ .

Since there is no more cubic terms in the reduced density matrix, we have directly

\displaystyle\langle\phi^{4}\rangle

\displaystyle\supset 8\times\frac{r^{EFT}_{\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\phi_{3}\phi_{4}}}{r_{\phi_{1}^{2}}r_{\phi_{2}^{2}}r_{\phi_{3}^{2}}r_{\phi_{4}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}(E_{k}+k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}+k_{4})}{2k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}E_{k}(k_{1}+k_{2}+E_{k})(k_{3}+k_{4}+E_{k})(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}+k_{4})}\ .

(2.34)

This matches our previous

\log\rho[\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}^{\prime}]\supset+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2(k_{1}+k_{2}+E_{k})(k_{3}+k_{4}+E_{k})E_{k}}(\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{1}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{2}}+\phi^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{1}}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{2}})(\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{3}}\bar{\phi}_{\vec{k}_{4}}+\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{3}}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}_{\vec{k}_{4}})\ .

(2.35)

The contribution of this term to the final result, after taking

. Since
\bar{\sigma}\neq 0
, their contributions are non-zero. Later in section

On the other hand, if we started with the effective action after integrating out the heavy field in the traditional sense,

S_{EFT}=\int d^{4}x\left[-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\phi^{2}\frac{1}{M^{2}-\Box}\phi^{2}\right]\ ,

(2.36)

and use it to calculate the in-in correlators directly or through the wavefunction, we would not be able to recover the correct result for trispectrum. Most obviously, this action fails to capture terms with odd powers of
M
. As mentioned before, the odd part of the correction corresponds to semi-local terms. Specifically, Equation (
2.11
) shows that the terms not captured by this effective action are analytic in two or more momenta, which correspond to
\delta
-function localized terms in position space. One may take the point of view that these are ultra-short distance effects that cannot be captured in the EFT (i.e. we should only concern ourselves with correlators at separated points). However, it is possible that these can be captured by local corrections to the probability distribution function (or density matrix) which could be matched in the EFT. We will discuss this in detail in Section
3
.

The even power in
1/M
corrections, on the other hand, can be understood as the consequence of having a well-defined variational principle. Here we show, following mainly
[
71
,
72
]
, how this works for the
1/M^{4}
correction and discuss this topic in more detail in Appendix
A
. In the EFT action, the
1/M^{4}
term is of the form
\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}(\Box/2M^{4})\phi^{2}
. The spatial derivatives are harmless since we are integrating over the full spatial volume. However, the time derivative might be problematic, since we have a non-trivial boundary at
t=t_{0}
. The variation of such a term is

\delta S\supset\int d^{4}x\;\frac{\lambda}{2}\left(2\phi\delta\phi\frac{-\partial_{t}^{2}}{M^{4}}\phi^{2}+2\phi^{2}\frac{-\partial_{t}^{2}}{M^{4}}\phi\delta\phi\right)\ .

(2.37)

Varying the first term with respect to
\delta\phi
is straightforward, while for the second term we need to do some integration by parts,

\int d^{4}x\;\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}\frac{-\partial_{t}^{2}}{M^{4}}\phi\delta\phi=\int d^{4}x\;\lambda^{2}(-\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})\frac{1}{M^{4}}\phi\delta\phi-\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}\frac{\partial_{t}}{M^{4}}\phi\delta\phi\bigg|_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}+\lambda^{2}(\partial_{t}\phi^{2})\frac{1}{M^{4}}\phi\delta\phi\bigg|_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}\ .

(2.38)

At this order the equation of motion is a second order differential equation with respect to time. Therefore, we are free to choose two boundary conditions. Setting
\delta\phi(t_{i})=\delta\phi(t_{f})=0
uses up the two boundary conditions, and further setting
\partial_{t}\delta\phi(t_{i})=\partial_{t}\delta\phi(t_{f})=0
will over-constrain the system. As a result, to have a well-defined variational problem, one can choose to set
\delta\phi(t_{i})=\delta\phi(t_{f})=0
and add a boundary term
\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}\frac{\partial_{t}}{M^{4}}\phi\delta\phi\big|_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}
to cancel out the extra boundary term. That is, for the action to have a well defined variational problem, the
1/M^{4}
term should be

S_{int}\supset\int d^{4}x\;\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}\frac{\Box}{2M^{4}}\phi^{2}+\int d^{3}x\;\lambda^{2}\bar{\phi}^{2}\frac{\partial_{t}}{2M^{4}}\bar{\phi}^{2}\ .

(2.39)

Carrying out the standard procedure in the in-in calculation, this translates to the terms

-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{k_{T}M^{4}}\frac{2\times 2}{16k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}}(k_{12}^{2}+k_{34}^{2}-2k_{5}^{2})+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M^{4}}\frac{2\times 2}{16k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}}(k_{12}+k_{34})=-\lambda^{2}\frac{k_{12}k_{34}+k_{5}^{2}}{2k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}k_{T}M^{4}}\ .

(2.40)

This agrees with the expansion in (
2.9
).

One can use this procedure to reproduce all the corrections that are even in
1/M
order by order, at least in principle. However, in practice, this procedure already gets complicated at the
1/M^{6}
order. Plus, it does not tell us about the terms odd in
1/M
. Fortunately, one can generate the full correction more efficiently by being more careful about the EFT procedure. The key point is that we use different boundary conditions for the in-out and in-in calculations, so the classical solutions to be plugged back into the action are different. Here we adapt the argument given in Section 4 of
[
38
]
. For an in-out calculation the homogeneous part of the heavy field simply vanishes in the EFT limit, while for an in-in calculation, the inhomogeneous solution to the equation of motion of the heavy field is corrected by the homogeneous part at the boundary to match the boundary condition. More explicitly, the full solution to the equation of motion of
\sigma
can be written as

\sigma(\vec{k},t)=C_{1}e^{iE_{k}t}+C_{2}e^{-iE_{k}t}-\frac{\lambda}{M^{2}-\Box}[\phi^{2}](\vec{k},t)\ ,

(2.41)

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
may depend on
\vec{k}
only, and
[\phi^{2}]({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}},t)
is the fourier transform of
\phi^{2}({\vec{x}},t)
. In the usual in-out calculation, the boundary conditions are such that we do not see propagating heavy degrees of freedom in the asymptotic states. That sets
C_{1}=C_{2}=0
, and we only plug the inhomogeneous solution back into the action for the tree level EFT. For in-in, however, the boundary conditions are now
\sigma(t\rightarrow-\infty)=0
and
\sigma(t=t_{0}=0)=\bar{\sigma}(\vec{x})
, where
\bar{\sigma}
can be any real number. Apparently we need a different choice of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. With our
i\epsilon
prescription, the condition that
\sigma(t\rightarrow-\infty_{-})=0
, where
\infty_{\pm}=\infty(1\pm i\epsilon)
, tells us that
C_{2}=0
. To satisfy
\sigma(t=0)=\bar{\sigma}(\vec{x})
, we choose

C_{1}=\bar{\sigma}+\frac{\lambda}{M^{2}-\Box}\bar{\phi}^{2}\ ,

(2.42)

where the second term cancels the inhomogeneous solution at
t=0
, and the value of
\sigma
at the boundary is simply
\bar{\sigma}
. We are now left to choose the proper
\bar{\sigma}
for tree level EFT. Turns out, we have already done the calculation to choose
\bar{\sigma}
; that is,

\bar{\sigma}_{k}-\frac{\psi_{\phi^{2}\sigma}+\psi^{*}_{\phi^{2}\sigma}}{\psi_{\sigma^{2}}+\psi_{\sigma^{2}}^{*}}\bar{\phi}^{2}=\bar{\sigma}_{k}+\frac{\lambda}{E_{k}(k_{1}+k_{2}+E_{k})}\bar{\phi}_{1}\bar{\phi}_{2}=0\ .

(2.43)

It is then straightforward to plug everything back and calculate the tree level action. The full solution to
\sigma
, to be plugged into the action, is

\displaystyle\sigma({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}},t)=

\displaystyle e^{iE_{k}t}\left(\frac{\lambda}{-(k_{1}+k_{2}+E_{k})E_{k}}+\frac{\lambda}{M^{2}+k^{2}-(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}}\right)\bar{\phi}_{1}\bar{\phi}_{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{M^{2}+k^{2}-(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}}\phi_{1}(t)\phi_{2}(t)\ .

(2.44)

The momenta involved satisfy
{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}={\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}_{1}+{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}_{2}
. The interaction part of the effective action, after plugging (
2.44
) back into the original action, is

S\supset\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\;\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}\frac{1}{M^{2}-\Box}\phi^{2}+\frac{i}{2}\int d^{3}x\lambda^{2}\left(\frac{1}{M^{2}-\Box}+\frac{1}{(i\partial_{t}-E)E}\right)\bar{\phi}^{2}\frac{i\partial_{t}+E}{M^{2}-\Box}\bar{\phi}^{2}\ ,

(2.45)

where we have used the equation of motion and integration by parts to rearrange the terms, and we use
\partial_{t}\bar{\phi}_{k}
to represent
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\partial_{t}\phi_{k}(t)=ik\bar{\phi}_{k}
.

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\partial_{t}\phi_{k}(t)=-ik\bar{\phi}_{k}
. At the same time, the signs in front of explicit factors of

\displaystyle\psi^{EFT}_{\phi^{4}}\supset

3
Exact Results in Flat Space

Our most complete understanding of EFT and RG comes from the non-perturbative RG defined by Polchinski
[
47
]
. This approach provides, in a particular scheme, exactly how the action must change after integrating out a shell of momentum. Importantly, we will see that for in-in correlators, the non-perturbative RG includes both the standard change to the effective action and local boundary terms.

3.1
Exact Wilsonian RG

In the context of inflation, we are used to evaluating correlation functions in the interacting vacuum (in-in correlators). We can define an in-in correlator in perturbation theory in a variety of spacetime backgrounds as
[
35
]

\langle{\cal O}(t)\rangle=\left\langle\left[\bar{\text{T}}\exp\left(i\int_{-\infty(1+i\epsilon)}^{t}{\rm d}t\,H_{\rm int}(t)\right)\right]{\cal O}^{\rm int}(t)\left[\text{T}\exp\left(-i\int_{-\infty(1-i\epsilon)}^{t}{\rm d}t\,H_{\rm int}(t)\right)\right]\right\rangle\ ,

(3.1)

where
{\cal O}(t)
is an operator defined at a single time
t
, usually in terms of products of local fields, and
{\cal O}^{\rm int}(t)
is the same operator defined in terms of interaction picture fields. The
i\epsilon
prescription is important for defining the interacting vacuum of the theory.

We can also calculate in-in correlators from the wavefunction of the universe. Given a real scalar field
\varphi({\vec{x}},t)
whose equal time correlators we wish to calculate at a time
t_{0}
, we can write

\langle{\cal O}(t)\rangle=\int D{\bar{\varphi}}({\vec{x}}){\cal O}[{\bar{\varphi}}({\vec{x}})]|\Psi[{\bar{\varphi}}({\vec{x}});t]|^{2}\ ,

(3.2)

where we defined
\varphi({\vec{x}},t_{0})\equiv\bar{\varphi}({\vec{x}})
. In flat space, we can write the wavefunction in terms of the path integral

\Psi[{\bar{\varphi}};t]=\int^{{\bar{\varphi}}}D\varphi({\vec{x}},t)\exp\left(i\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1-i\epsilon)}{\cal L}[\varphi({\vec{x}},t)]\right)\ ,

(3.3)

where

{\cal L}=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{K^{-1}(k^{2}/\Lambda_{0}^{2})}{2}(\dot{\varphi}^{2}-\partial_{i}\varphi\partial^{i}\varphi)+{\cal L}_{\rm int}[\varphi]\ ,

(3.4)

and
K(z)
is a regulator

7

7

Note that in this section

is not the bulk to boundary propagator.
that is defined so that
K(z\ll 1)\to 0
and
K(z\gg 1)\to 1
. In this regard, we can recognize the perturbative formula as the expansion of the wavefunction expression around the wavefunction of the free theory.

In order to write a general expression for the RG flow of the in-in correlator, we define a partition function

Z[J]=\int D{\bar{\varphi}}\exp\left(\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,J(-{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}){\bar{\varphi}}({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})\right)|\Psi[{\bar{\varphi}};t_{0}]|^{2}\ .

(3.5)

Any in-in correlator of

. We can write this as time-ordered path integral

Z[J]=\int_{\gamma}D\varphi\;T\exp\left(iS_{\gamma}[\varphi]+\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}J(-{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})\bar{\varphi}({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})\right)\ ,

(3.6)

where
S_{\gamma}=\int_{\gamma}dt{\cal L}+S_{\partial}
is the action, with
S_{\partial}
being a boundary-term needed to make the variational principle well defined, and
\gamma
is the contour
\gamma:(-\infty(1-i\epsilon),t_{0}]\cup[t_{0},-\infty(1+i\epsilon))
. Here

Now we want to integrate out a shell of momentum from
k\in[\Lambda_{0}-\delta\Lambda,\Lambda_{0}]
so that we can lower the cutoff to
\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}-\delta\Lambda
, and then repeat to arrive at a theory with
\Lambda\ll\Lambda_{0}
. After performing the path integral, we will arrive at a partition function

\displaystyle Z[J,\Lambda]=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}D\varphi\,T\exp\bigg(iS_{0}[\varphi({\vec{x}},t),\Lambda]+iS_{I}[\varphi({\vec{x}},t),\Lambda]+\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}J(-{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})\bar{\varphi}({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})\bigg)\ ,

(3.7)

\displaystyle S_{0}[\varphi({\vec{x}},t),\Lambda]\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}dt\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{K^{-1}(k^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}{2}(\dot{\varphi}^{2}-\partial_{i}\varphi\partial^{i}\varphi)\ ,

(3.8)

where we defined the interaction term in the action after integrating out as

S_{I}=S_{\rm int,\Lambda}+S_{\partial,\Lambda}\ .

(3.9)

The bulk action,
S_{\rm int,\Lambda}=\int_{\gamma}{\cal L}_{\rm int}

S_{\partial,\Lambda}
is the full boundary term localized at

. It is important that
S_{\partial,\Lambda}
contain operators that do not commute with
{\bar{\varphi}}
and therefore the precise definition of this term will be important. As we saw above, this partition function is not generated by a wavefunction, but is now described by a reduced density matrix

Z[J,\Lambda]=\int D{\bar{\varphi}}\exp\left(\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}s_{\partial}({\bar{\varphi}},J,t_{0})\right)\rho_{\Lambda}[{\bar{\varphi}},{\bar{\varphi}};t_{0}]\ .

(3.10)

The boundary action,
S_{\partial}

s_{\partial}({\bar{\varphi}},J,t_{0})

{\bar{\varphi}}\to F[{\bar{\varphi}}]

The partition function

is necessarily independent of
\Lambda
so that
\frac{d}{d\Lambda}Z[J,\Lambda]=0
. Specifically, all we did in defining

was to perform part of the integral present in

. Therefore, differentiating the expression we have

\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}Z[\Lambda,J]=\int\mathcal{D}\phi\;i\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}T\left(S_{0}+S_{I}\right)e^{iS_{0}+iS_{I}+\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}J(-{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})\bar{\varphi}({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})}\ .

(3.11)

It is natural to define the evolution of
S_{I}
under RG so that it mimics the non-perturbative RG of the Euclidean particle function as much as possible. We should find that the effective action should be the same as what we would find for in-out correlators. In-in correlators contain the S-matrix as the residue of the total energy pole and therefore we should find that these residues are calculable using the scattering amplitude calculated within a conventional EFT. Given this expectation, the most natural resolution is to change

such that the integrand is the same total derivative as we find the Euclidean and/or in-out partition function.

There is a straightforward method to realize this behavior, following
[
48
]
. First, we note that

\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\Lambda}S_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{dK(\tfrac{k^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}})}{d\log\Lambda}\int_{\gamma}dt\int_{\gamma}dt^{\prime}\left(\frac{\delta S_{0}}{\delta\varphi({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}},t)}\right)\,G(t,t^{\prime},{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})\,\left(\frac{\delta S_{0}}{\delta\varphi(-{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}},t^{\prime})}\right)+\delta S_{\partial,2}\ ,

(3.12)

where
G(t,t^{\prime},{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})
is the path-ordered Green’s function that obeys,

\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+k^{2}\right)G(t,t^{\prime},{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})=\delta(t-t^{\prime})\ ,

(3.13)

and
\delta S_{\partial,2}
is a quadratic boundary term derived in Appendix
B
. By virtue of the Schwinger-Dyson equations,

\mathcal{O}\frac{\delta S_{0}}{\delta\phi}=\frac{\delta\mathcal{O}}{\delta\phi}-\mathcal{O}\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\phi}\ ,

(3.14)

we can exchange
S_{0}\to S_{I}
to find

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}S_{0}=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\gamma}dt\int_{\gamma}dt^{\prime}\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}K\Bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}},t)}\,G(t,t^{\prime},{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})\,\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(-{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}},t^{\prime})}

(3.15)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta^{2}S_{I}}{\delta\varphi({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}},t)\delta\varphi(-{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}},t^{\prime})}G(t,t^{\prime},{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})\Bigg)+\delta S_{\partial,2}\ .

(3.16)

Therefore, if

\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}S_{I}=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\gamma}dtdt^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t)}G(t,t^{\prime})\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t^{\prime})}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta^{2}S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t)\delta\varphi(t^{\prime})}G(t,t^{\prime})\right)\right)-\delta S_{\partial,2}\ ,

(3.17)

we will have
\partial_{\log\Lambda}Z[J,\Lambda]=0
. Up to the quadratic boundary terms (which are unimportant to this discussion), this expression looks identical to the equivalent formula for in-out or Euclidean partition functions. However, as we have already seen in an explicit example, it is not the case that integrating out leads to a local action (or a pure state).

We would like to understand in what sense the change to
S_{I}
is local. We suppose that
S_{I}
is local in the sense that

S_{I}=\int_{-\infty(1-i\epsilon)}^{t_{0}}dt{\cal L}_{\rm int}[\varphi(t)]-\int_{-\infty(1+i\epsilon)}^{t_{0}}dt^{\prime}{\cal L}_{\rm int}[\varphi(t)^{\prime}]+{\cal Q}[\varphi,t_{0}]\ ,

local, the second term in the expression (

-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta^{2}S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t)\delta\varphi(t^{\prime})}G(t,t^{\prime})=-\frac{\delta^{2}\int dt^{\prime\prime}{\cal L}_{\rm int}(t^{\prime\prime})}{\delta\varphi(t)\delta\varphi(t^{\prime})}G(t,t^{\prime})\propto\delta(t-t^{\prime})G(t,t)\ .

(3.19)

In contrast, locality of the first term depends on the properties of the Green’s function. If both times are in the same part of the contour,
t,t^{\prime}\in(-\infty(1\pm i\epsilon),t_{0})

|t-t^{\prime}|

G(t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2k}\exp(\pm ik|t-t^{\prime}|(1\pm i\epsilon))\ .

(3.20)

Therefore, if we define

\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t)}=\delta{\cal L}(t)\ ,

(3.21)

then

\displaystyle\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1\pm i\epsilon)}dtdt^{\prime}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t)}G(t,t^{\prime})\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1\pm i\epsilon)}dt\int d(\Delta t)\,\delta{\cal L}(t)\delta{\cal L}(t+\Delta t)G(\Delta t)

(3.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1\pm i\epsilon)}dt\sum_{j}{\cal Q}_{j}[\varphi,t_{0}]\ .

(3.23)

In the last step, we used the fact that the integral over

is exponentially suppressed in

Now we must consider the remaining terms that are not manifestly local and do not arise in the usual in-out expressions. To be concrete, we take

(3.24)

Because the two integrals have different

Such a term would be non-local along the contour and thus would not be related to a local action in Euclidean signature. However, the Green’s function is also no longer exponentially suppressed with respect to
|t-t^{\prime}|
but instead takes the form

G(t,t^{\prime},{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}})=\frac{1}{2k}\exp(ik|t-t_{0}|(1+i\epsilon)-ik|t^{\prime}-t_{0}|(1-i\epsilon))\ .

(3.25)

We see now that both terms are exponentially suppressed in
|t-t_{0}|

and

to find a correction to the boundary term,

\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}S_{\partial,{\rm int}}\supset\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1+i\epsilon)}dt\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1-i\epsilon)}dt^{\prime}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t)}G(t,t^{\prime})\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t^{\prime})}\to\sum_{i,j}{\cal Q}_{i}(t_{0,+}){\cal Q}_{j}(t_{0,-})\ ,

(3.26)

where
{\cal Q}_{i}(t_{0,+}){\cal Q}_{j}(t_{0,-})
the product of local operators defined at

respectively. However, we cannot express this directly as a local operator at
t_{0}

S_{\partial,{\rm int}}\supset g\dot{\varphi}(t_{0,+})\varphi(t_{0,-})^{n}\ ,

(3.27)

then since

Notice that the second term is equivalent to a field redefinition of

and
\dot{\varphi}
are

will generate semi-local terms and field redefinitions.
These are precisely the terms we found from integrating out the massive field in Section

As a concrete illustration, by integrating out, we will generate terms of the form

S_{\partial,{\rm int}}\supset\int d^{3}x\frac{g}{\Lambda^{3}}\dot{\varphi}(t_{+}){\bar{\varphi}}\dot{\varphi}(t_{-}){\bar{\varphi}}\ ,

(3.29)

where

\displaystyle\langle in|\varphi({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}_{1})\varphi({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}_{2})\varphi({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}_{3})\varphi({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}_{4})|in\rangle^{\prime}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\frac{g}{\Lambda^{3}}\int d^{3}x\langle\dot{\varphi}(t_{+})\varphi(t_{0})\varphi({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}_{1})\varphi({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}_{2})\varphi({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}_{3})\varphi({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}_{4})\dot{\varphi}(t_{-})\varphi(t_{0})\rangle^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g}{\Lambda^{3}}\frac{1}{8k_{2}k_{4}}+{\rm permutations}\ .

Notice two important aspects of this correction: (1) by dimensional analysis it scales like an odd power of

and
k_{3}
). This is a general outcome that results from the structure of these non-local “boundary” terms.

The diagram relevant to the exact RG we found here is precisely the same term we found from integrating-out the massive field. We are integrating out a field that has an exchange interaction between the two pieces of the in-in contour. These terms are still written as an expansion in time and space derivatives. Because these do not commute with
J{\bar{\varphi}}
, they are not equivalent to a local change to the action, nor a local term in the wavefunction. In this precise sense, we need to treat the EFT as a density matrix if we wish to reproduce the exact form of the correlators. Yet, we also see that these terms are, at most, equivalent to semi-local terms in the correlator. One may alternatively take the point of view that local and semi-local terms are not calculable within the EFT (like contact terms) and thus need to be determined by matching.

It is extremely desirable to have the same level of understanding of the internal consistency of EFT on cosmological backgrounds. Although cosmology currently lacks an observable as robust as the S-matrix, progress has been made in several directions. One such approach is to study sum rules at the level of individual wavefunction coefficients
[

Defining an off-shell wavefunction, the analytic structure of the wavefunction coefficients

\left.\omega_{T}\psi_{n}(\{\omega\},\{\mathbf{k}\})\right|_{\omega_{1}=\omega_{1}^{\prime}}=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi i}\frac{\operatorname{disc}\left(\omega_{T}\psi(\{\omega\},\{\mathbf{k}\})\right)}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{1}^{\prime}}+\operatorname{Res}_{\omega_{1}=\infty}\left(\frac{\omega_{T}\psi_{n}(\{\omega\},\{\mathbf{k}\})}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{1}^{\prime}}\right)\ ,

where
\omega_{i}
are the off-shell extensions of the energies (usually subject to
\omega_{i}=|{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}_{i}|
for a massless field on-shell). At face value, this expression also allows one to relate subtractions to additional contact terms. Concretely, given an analytic function
f(z)
for

\frac{f\left(z_{0}\right)-f(0)}{z_{0}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C}\frac{1}{z}\frac{f(z)-f(0)}{z-z_{0}}\to f(z)=f(0)+\frac{z_{0}}{2\pi i}\oint_{C}\frac{1}{z}\frac{f(z)-f(0)}{z-z_{0}}\ .

(3.32)

We see that we can suppress the integral at large
z
at the cost of introducing a constant
f(0)
. Applied to the wavefunction coefficients, we get

\left.\omega_{T}\psi_{n}(\{\omega\},\{\mathbf{k}\})\right|_{\omega_{1}=\omega_{1}^{\prime}}=\left.\omega_{T}\psi_{n}(\{\omega\},\{\mathbf{k}\})\right|_{\omega_{1}=0}+\omega_{1}^{\prime}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{\omega_{1}}\frac{\omega_{T}(\psi_{n}(\omega_{1})-\psi_{n}(0))}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{1}^{\prime}}\ .

(3.33)

This would validate the intuition that information at large
\omega
is encoded in contact interactions.

The ideal use of this formula would be to compute
\psi_{n,{\rm EFT}}
as
\omega_{1}^{\prime}\ll\Lambda_{\rm UV}
and use this expression to relate the IR wavefunction coefficients to unknown UV physics that defines
\psi_{n}
at
\omega_{1}\gg\Lambda_{\rm UV}
. While this is a straightforward produce for S-matrix elements, we have already seen the challenge of defining
\psi_{\rm EFT}
and relating it to the wavefunction coefficients in the UV.

If we consider the model with a heavy field, in
[
38
]
the effective wavefunction is defined by
\Psi[{\bar{\varphi}},\bar{\sigma}=0]
. In other words, we are not considering the full wavefunction but only the the slice of the wavefunction where
\bar{\sigma}=0
. This is not sufficient to calculate an in-in correlator in the EFT, where one needs to integrate over
\bar{\sigma}
rather than forcing it to take a specific value. Nevertheless, setting
\bar{\sigma}=0
is a valid procedure on its own, and can be a useful regime for constraining the structure of the wavefunction. The more non-trivial challenge is that
\omega_{n}
does not have an unambiguous definition that can be matched between the UV and the IR. Concretely, under a field redefinition
\bar{\sigma}\to\bar{\sigma}+g{\bar{\varphi}}^{2}
we shift the wavefunction coefficient

\psi_{\varphi^{4}}\to\psi_{\varphi^{4}}+g\psi_{\varphi^{2}\sigma}\ .

(3.34)

In this precise sense, the UV object that appears in this expression is not well defined within the EFT description. More over, the “EFT” that defines the wavefunction is not the EFT one gets from integrating out the short wavelength modes (which is described by a density matrix). Nevertheless, we have seen that these issues are pertain to the local terms in the wavefunction and therefore non-local terms should be robust to these kinds of issues. This is indeed what was found in
[
38
]
, where the addition of boundary terms to the wavefunction was sufficient to match the UV expression.

4
EFT and de Sitter Correlators

Effective theories in de Sitter space, and during inflation, are subject to different constraints than their flat space counterparts. Energy is not conserved in an expanding background, and therefore the split between hard and soft modes is more nuanced than for scattering processes that are highly constrained by kinematics. Concretely, massive particles do not completely decouple
[
85
,
86
,
87
,
88
]
but their direct production is exponentially (Boltzmann) suppressed at large mass
[
89
,
90
,
91
]
. On the other hand, the expansion of spacetime creates large density fluctuations from quantum fluctuations and therefore has a physical meaning without explicitly introducing a detector or observer. The expansion of the universe does redshift away heavy fields and thus their role should decouple in time, even if their impact does not decouple in the statistics of the metric fluctuations. In this section we check the decoupling of the heavy field with an explicit example.

This behavior is also consistent with our expectations for the exact RG argument in flat space. There are two complications in flat space for terms like Equation (

A similar procedure has been carried out in, for example,
[

[

It would be interesting to investigate the case for a conformal environment using our formalism and compare with their plot. We leave this for a future work.

We also emphasize that our calculation here does not take into account any exponentially suppressed terms, which is the main object studied in cosmological collider physics. It is possible that some subtleties coming from a mixed final state remain for these terms. However, cosmological observables are classical

and thus we do not expect even the exponentially suppressed terms to have the same non-commuting challenges as their flat space counterparts.

5
Conclusions

Effective field theories have a straightforward implementation in the context of scattering experiments in flat space
[
110
,
111
]
. Energy conservation creates a clean separation between the physical particles that can and cannot be produced kinematically. Moreover, the S-matrix is invariant under field redefinitions which significantly simplifies matching and classifying EFTs
[
112
,
113
,
114
]
. Yet, there are many contexts where correlation functions, rather than S-matrix elements, are the observables of the theory. In these contexts, it is important to understand how and when our EFT intuitions need to be adjusted. This is particularly the case in cosmology, where EFT is an essential tool for both theory and data analysis
[
115
]
but lacks many or all of the simplifying aspects of flat space.

In this paper, we explored how to apply EFT in the context of in-in correlators. These observables can be defined in flat space, but are better known from their applications to cosmology
[
35
]
. In the UV description, we characterize the correlations in terms of correlation functions in a pure (vacuum) state. However, after integrating out the short wavelength modes, the state of the system is no longer pure and is better described by a density matrix. This is, naively, in tension with the well-known expectation that the in-in correlators of a given theory can be determined from the S-matrix elements. Since the S-matrix does have a complete EFT description, it cannot be the case that EFT should be abandoned entirely.

The resolution that we explore in detail is that the additional contributions that appear in in-in correlators in flat space always take the form of semi-local terms, local terms, or field redefinitions in the EFT limit. These terms do not have an S-matrix interpretation as they never appear with a total energy pole. This was seen both in an explicit example of integrating out a heavy field, and non-perturbatively using exact RG. Instead, information that arises from the UV theory appears as additional local and semi-local terms must be fixed by matching.

In the cosmological setting, in-in correlators are not expected to receive non-vanishing UV corrections beyond those associated with local field redefinitions. In single-field inflation, we can even fix the field redefinitions
[
116
]
and no such corrections can arise. Like flat space, derivative corrections do appear, but time derivatives and gradients vanish due to the expansion of the universe and they do not alter the correlators (matching). Relatedly, the modes become classical and the commutators between fields and any non-local terms in the action vanish. This was seen directly in the same example of integrating out a massive field, now in de Sitter space. This is of practical importance, as EFT is used to calculate the statistical predictions for inflationary correlators in a wide range of models and it would be troubling if these predictions were incomplete.

This work is a step towards a deeper understanding of RG flow and decoupling in cosmological EFTs. The interpretation of many IR corrections in terms of anomalous dimensions
[
117
,
118
,
119
]
points to the idea that these IR logs should be resummed using RG
[
120
,
121
,
122
,
123
,
124
,
125
,
126
,
127
]
. Despite the strong evidence, the validity of this procedure non-perturbatively has not been directly proven. A sharper understanding of RG in the context of in-in correlators is a stepping stone to an all-orders understanding of the structure of EFTs and RG flows in curved and/or dynamical backgrounds.

Appendix A
Well-Defined Variational Problem

In the main text we briefly mentioned that in order to have a well-defined variational problem, the Lagrangian density needs to be accompanied by some specific boundary terms. Here we will give a more detailed argument following mostly
[
71
,
72
]
, and show how it works at the order
1/M^{6}
in our example.

Starting with a Lagrangian density
{\cal L}={\cal L}(q,\dot{q},\cdots,q^{(n)})
, the variational principle leads to a generalized Euler-Lagrange equation of motion

\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial q^{i}}-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{q}^{i}}+\cdots+(-1)^{n}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\frac{\partial L}{\partial(q^{(n)})^{i}}=0\ .

(A.1)

This can be rewritten as

\frac{\partial^{2}{\cal L}}{\partial(q^{(n)})^{i}\partial(q^{(n)})^{j}}(q^{(2n)})^{j}=F^{i}(q,\dot{q},\cdots,q^{(2n-1)})\ .

(A.2)

In order to write this as an ODE of order
2n
, we want the Lagrangian to be non-degenerate, which in this context means

\frac{\partial^{2}{\cal L}}{\partial(q^{(n)})^{i}\partial(q^{(n)})^{j}}\neq 0\ .

(A.3)

In case the Lagrangian is not already given in this form, one can use integration by parts to massage it until it is manifestly non-degenerate, while keeping explicit the boundary terms involved. For example, suppose in a Lagrangian every single term has four or less time derivatives, then a term like
qq^{(4)}
should be massaged into
\ddot{q}\ddot{q}
. Written in this way, a Lagrangian
{\cal L}(q,\cdots,q^{(n)})
corresponds to an ODE of order
2n
, and we are free to impose
2n
initial conditions.

With this in hand, we can proceed to write the variation of the action as

\delta S=\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}dt\;\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial q^{i}}-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{q}^{i}}+\cdots+(-1)^{n}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(q^{(n)})^{i}}\right)\delta q+(p_{1}\delta q+p_{2}\delta\dot{q}+\cdots+p_{n}\delta q^{(n-1)})\bigg|_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}\ ,

(A.4)

where the conjugate momenta are found to be, for
m\leq n
,

p_{m}=\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial q^{(m)}}-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial q^{(m+1)}}-\cdots\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial q^{(n-1)}}-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial q^{(n)}}\right)\right)\ ,

(A.5)

and the
2n
boundary conditions can be set to
\delta q|^{t_{f}}_{t_{i}}=\delta\dot{q}|^{t_{f}}_{t_{i}}=\cdots=\delta q^{(n)}|^{t_{f}}_{t_{i}}=0
. This is one choice of a well-defined variational problem, where the degrees of freedom are encoded in
q
,
\dot{q}
, …,
q^{(n-1)}
. More generally, one can choose between different sets of variables as the degrees of freedom. For example, in our application we want to set
\delta\dot{\phi}
free. We then replace
\delta\dot{\phi}
with some other variables as the degree of freedom. In our example below we will simply guess the result. One can refer to
[
72
]
for details of a more rigorous derivation.

Let us now take a look at the
1/M^{6}
term,

S_{EFT}\supset\int d^{4}x\;\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}\frac{\Box^{2}}{2M^{6}}\phi^{2}=\int d^{4}x\;\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}\frac{\partial_{t}^{4}-2\partial_{t}^{2}\nabla^{2}+\nabla^{4}}{2M^{6}}\phi^{2}\ .

(A.6)

The operator
\nabla^{4}
simply translates to
k_{5}^{4}
, while the operator
\partial_{t}^{2}\nabla^{2}
can be seen as
k_{5}^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}
, and we have already seen how to deal with
\phi^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2}
in the main text. Their combined contribution in the in-in calculation is given by

\lambda^{2}\frac{2k_{12}k_{34}k_{5}^{2}+k_{5}^{4}}{2k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}k_{T}M^{6}}\ .

(A.7)

In the rest of this appendix we focus on the
\partial_{t}^{4}

\int dt\;\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}\frac{\partial_{t}^{4}}{2M^{6}}\phi^{2}=\int dt\;\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2M^{6}}(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2M^{6}}\phi^{2}(\partial_{t}^{3}\phi^{2})\bigg|_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2M^{6}}(\partial_{t}\phi^{2})(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})\bigg|_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}\ .

(A.8)

From now on we omit the
\int d^{3}x
integral. Take the variation of the bulk part, we have

\displaystyle\delta\left(\int dt\;\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2M^{6}}(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dt\;\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2M^{6}}\left(-4\partial_{t}(\partial_{t}\phi\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})+2\partial_{t}^{2}(\phi\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})\right)\delta\phi

To get around this problem, one can regard the bulk part as a function of

, given
\delta\phi=0
. To do so, we need to add a boundary term to the bulk,

\displaystyle\delta\left(\int dt\;\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2M^{6}}(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M^{6}}(\partial_{t}\phi^{2})(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})\bigg|_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dt\;\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M^{6}}(\partial_{t}^{4}\phi^{2})\delta\phi^{2}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M^{6}}\left((\partial_{t}^{3}\phi^{2})\delta\phi^{2}+(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})(\partial_{t}^{2}\delta\phi^{2})\right)\bigg|_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}\ .

(A.10)

Now the variations are on the variables that we desire. Starting from the original EFT expression, we see the desired boundary corrections are given by

\displaystyle\int dt\;\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2M^{6}}(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{M^{6}}(\partial_{t}\phi^{2})(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})\bigg|_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dt\;\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}\frac{\partial_{t}^{4}}{2M^{6}}\phi^{2}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2M^{6}}\phi^{2}(\partial_{t}^{3}\phi^{2})\bigg|_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2M^{6}}(\partial_{t}\phi^{2})(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{2})\bigg|_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}\ .

(A.11)

In terms of in-in calculation, this is a contribution of the form

\displaystyle\frac{4\times\lambda^{2}}{M^{6}\times 16k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}}\left(\frac{k_{12}^{4}+k_{34}^{4}}{k_{T}}-k_{12}^{3}-k_{34}^{3}-k_{12}k_{34}^{2}-k_{12}^{2}k_{34}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}k_{T}M^{6}}(k_{12}^{3}k_{34}+k_{12}^{2}k_{34}^{2}+k_{12}k_{34}^{3})\ .

(A.12)

Adding the contribution from the rest of the
1/M^{6}
terms, we have

\lambda^{2}\frac{2k_{12}k_{34}k_{5}^{2}+k_{5}^{4}}{2k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}k_{T}M^{6}}-\lambda^{2}\frac{(k_{12}^{3}k_{34}+k_{12}^{2}k_{34}^{2}+k_{12}k_{34}^{3})}{2k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}k_{T}M^{6}}=\lambda^{2}\frac{(k_{5}^{2}+k_{12}k_{34})^{2}-k_{12}k_{34}k_{T}^{2}}{2k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}k_{T}M^{6}}\ ,

(A.13)

exactly what is expected from the full calculation.

Appendix B
Exact RG

In this appendix, we provide a more detailed derivation of the boundary terms that arise in the exact RG, following the procedure described in
[
48
]
. We write the derivative of the partition function, defined in Equation (
3.10
), as

\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}Z[\Lambda,J]=\int\mathcal{D}\phi\;i\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}T\left(S_{0}+S_{I}\right)e^{iS}\ ,

(B.1)

where
S_{I}=S_{\partial}+S_{\rm int}
includes both a bulk interaction term
S_{\rm int}
and a boundary term
S_{\partial}
that is localized at
t=t_{0}
. Schematically, the idea is to write

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}S_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}K^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\varphi^{2}|(t_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma}dt\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial^{\mu}\varphi\right)

(B.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}K^{-1}\int_{\gamma}dt_{1}\int_{\gamma}dt_{2}\varphi(t_{1})\Box G(t_{1},t_{2})\Box\varphi(t_{2})+{\rm B.T}\

(B.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}K\frac{\delta}{\delta\varphi}S_{0}\star G\star\frac{\delta}{\delta\varphi}S_{0}\ ,

(B.4)

where
{\rm B.T}
is a term at
t_{0}
we will derive later and
\star
indicates that we are integrating over time. Here
G(t,t^{\prime})
is the path ordered propagator, which satisfies

(\Box-m^{2})_{x}G(x,y)=(\Box-m^{2})_{y}G(x,y)=\delta^{(4)}(x-y)\ .

(B.5)

Using the Schwinger-Dyson equations, we can exchange
S_{0}
for
S_{I}
such that

\mathcal{O}\frac{\delta S_{0}}{\delta\phi}=\frac{\delta\mathcal{O}}{\delta\phi}-\mathcal{O}\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\phi}\ .

(B.6)

This equation should be understood to hold inside the path integral as a result of integration by parts. We can apply the Schwinger-Dyson equations twice to get

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta S_{0}}{\delta\varphi}\star G\star\frac{\delta S_{0}}{\delta\varphi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma}dt\left(\frac{\delta^{2}S_{0}}{\delta\varphi\delta\varphi}G(t,t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta S_{0}}{\delta\varphi}\star G\star\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi}

(B.7)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma}dt\left(\frac{\delta^{2}S_{0}}{\delta\varphi\delta\varphi}G(t,t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma}dt\left(\frac{\delta^{2}S_{I}}{\delta\varphi\delta\varphi}G(t,t)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi}\star G\star\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi}\ .

Therefore, if we define

\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}S_{I}=\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}K\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi}\star G\star\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma}dt\left(\frac{\delta^{2}S_{I}}{\delta\varphi\delta\varphi}G(t,t)\right)\right)+{\rm B.T.}\ ,

(B.8)

the additional constant term can be absorbed into the overall normalization of the partition function,

\delta\log Z\supset-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma}dt\left(\frac{\delta^{2}S_{0}}{\delta\varphi\delta\varphi}G(t,t)\right)\ .

(B.9)

This could also be absorbed into the action as constant, but from either perspective it has no impact on the dynamics of
\varphi
. With these changes, the partition function is indeed independent of
\Lambda
,

\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}Z[J,\Lambda]=0\ .

(B.10)

This argument is essentially Polchinski’s original derivation. However, we have yet to to determine (a) which terms appear in
S_{\partial}
and
S_{\rm int}
, and (b) if the action remains local after integrating out.

In order to understand the challenge, we should note that the time-ordering along the contour has greatly simplified our derivation, at the cost of obscuring the subtleties. To illustrate, we start with the term

\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi}\star G\star\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi}=\int_{\gamma}dt_{1}\int_{\gamma}dt_{2}\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t_{1})}G(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t_{2})}\ .

(B.11)

Notice that the integrals both run over the entire contour. In contrast, the term that we would derive if we simply substituted the in-out expression for the effective action would be

S_{\rm EFT}=\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1+i\epsilon)}dt{\cal L}_{\rm EFT}\supset\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1+i\epsilon)}dt_{1}\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1+i\epsilon)}dt_{2}\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t_{1})}G(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t_{2})}\ .

(B.12)

The conventional terms therefore do not capture the operators

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi}\star G\star\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi}\supset

\displaystyle\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1+i\epsilon)}dt_{1}\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1-i\epsilon)}dt_{2}\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t_{1})}G(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t_{2})}

(B.13)

\displaystyle+\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1-i\epsilon)}dt_{1}\int^{t_{0}}_{-\infty(1+i\epsilon)}dt_{2}\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t_{1})}G(t_{1},t_{2})\frac{\delta S_{I}}{\delta\varphi(t_{2})}+{\rm B.T.}\

(B.14)

These terms are particularly significant, as we have a source at
t_{0}
,
J({\vec{x}})\varphi({\vec{x}},t_{0})
. The operators above are non-local with respect to the time ordering, and therefore we cannot simply interpret them as terms in a local action.

In order to understand the additional terms that are not captured by
S_{\rm EFT}
, we will have to be more careful to split the contours into regions defined by
t_{\pm}-t_{0}=t(1\pm i\epsilon)
and
\varphi_{\pm}=\varphi(t_{\pm})
. We also recall that the path ordering is such that
t_{-}
is time-ordered and
t_{+}
is anti-time ordered. We will additionally define
\varphi(t_{0})={\bar{\varphi}}
to distinguish terms that are purely local at
t_{0}
and those that arise from the time evolution. The time integrals along the contour then become

\int_{-\infty_{-}}^{0}dt+\int_{0}^{-\infty_{+}}dt=\int_{-\infty_{-}}^{0}dt-\int_{-\infty_{+}}^{0}dt=\int_{\gamma}dt\ ,

In terms of these variables, the partition function of the theory becomes

Z=\int\mathcal{D}\varphi\;e^{iS[\phi]}=\int{\cal D}{\bar{\varphi}}\int^{{\bar{\varphi}}}{\cal D}\varphi_{+}\int^{{\bar{\varphi}}}{\cal D}\varphi_{-}\;e^{iS[\varphi_{+};\varphi_{-}]+I[{\bar{\varphi}},J]}\ ,

(B.15)

where
I[{\bar{\varphi}},J]
is a purely local function of the boundary field,
\bar{\varphi}({\vec{x}})
, and its source,
J({\vec{x}})
. As a result, the action along the contour (ignoring the local terms in
{\bar{\varphi}}
) becomes

\displaystyle S_{0}[\varphi_{\pm}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\left(-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial^{\mu}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\varphi^{2}\right)K^{-1}

(B.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi(\Box-m^{2})\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}(\varphi\partial^{\mu}\varphi)\right)K^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi(\Box-m^{2})\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}(\varphi\partial^{\mu}\varphi)\right)K^{-1}+\int d^{3}x\frac{1}{2}\left({\bar{\varphi}}\dot{\varphi}_{-}-{\bar{\varphi}}\dot{\varphi}_{+}\right)K^{-1}\ .

Here we are using
K^{-1}
abstractly, as we defined it in Fourier space. Note that the second term, which also appears in the on-shell wavefunction of the free theory, is not purely local on the boundary because
\dot{\varphi}_{\pm}
is discontinuous at
t_{\pm}=t_{0}
.

In order to identify additional boundary terms that may arise from the derivation of the exact RG, we can write the action

\bar{S}_{0}\equiv S_{0}-\int d^{3}x\frac{1}{2}\left({\bar{\varphi}}\dot{\varphi}_{-}-{\bar{\varphi}}\dot{\varphi}_{+}\right)K^{-1}=\int d^{4}x\frac{1}{2}\varphi(\Box-m^{2})\varphi K^{-1}\ ,

(B.17)

which can be written in terms of the propagator as

\bar{S}_{0}=\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\;\frac{1}{2}\varphi(x)G^{-1}(x,y)\varphi(y)K^{-1}\ .

(B.18)

From here, we can repeat the argument above, keeping track of the use of integration by parts, with the goal of writing

\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}\bar{S}_{0}=-\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta\bar{S}_{0}}{\delta\varphi(x)}G(x,y)\frac{\delta\bar{S}_{0}}{\delta\varphi(y)}\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}K+{\rm B.T.}

(B.19)

Again, we note that in this process the expression above still has mixed
t_{+}
and
t_{-}
terms from the integration over the full contours. For the moment, our goal is to identify the precise form of the boundary terms that appear at this stage. In momentum space, the propagator
G({\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}},t,t^{\prime})
is local in
{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}
, and therefore one can focus only on the time coordinate for a single
{\vec{\lx@scalerel@obj{k}}}
value, with the understanding that all formulas should include a
\int d^{3}k/(2\pi)^{3}
. By construction, we know that

\int_{\gamma}dtdt^{\prime}\varphi(t)(\Box-m^{2})_{t}G(t,t^{\prime})(\Box-m^{2})_{t}^{\prime}\varphi(t^{\prime})=\int_{\gamma}dt\varphi(t)(\Box-m^{2})\varphi(t)\ .

(B.20)

In addition, we know that

\frac{\delta\bar{S}_{0}}{\delta\varphi(t)}=(\Box-m^{2})\varphi(t)\ .

(B.21)

Integrating Equation (
B.20
) by parts twice yields

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma}dt\varphi(t)(\Box-m^{2})\varphi(t)=

\displaystyle-\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta\bar{S}_{0}}{\delta\varphi(x)}G(x,y)\frac{\delta\bar{S}_{0}}{\delta\varphi(y)}\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}K

(B.22)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma}dt^{\prime}\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}K^{-1}\Bigg({\bar{\varphi}}\partial_{t}G(t,t^{\prime})|^{t=t_{0,+}}_{t=t_{0,-}}

\displaystyle-(\dot{\varphi}_{+}-\dot{\varphi}_{-})|_{t=t_{0}}G(t_{0},t^{\prime})\Bigg)(\Box-m^{2})_{t^{\prime}}\varphi(t^{\prime})\ .

(B.23)

At this stage, the result is not a boundary term as we still have a
t^{\prime}
integral. However, we can now integrate by parts with respect to
t^{\prime}
to find that the second term is

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma}dt^{\prime}\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}K^{-1}\Bigg({\bar{\varphi}}\partial_{t}G(t,t^{\prime})|^{t=t_{0,+}}_{t=t_{0,-}}-(\dot{\varphi}_{+}-\dot{\varphi}_{-})|_{t=t_{0}}G(t_{0},t^{\prime})\Bigg)(\Box-m^{2})_{t^{\prime}}\varphi(t^{\prime})

(B.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}K^{-1}\Bigg({\bar{\varphi}}\partial_{t}G(t,t_{0})|^{t=t_{0,+}}_{t=t_{0,-}}(\dot{\varphi}_{+}-\dot{\varphi}_{-})-(\dot{\varphi}_{+}-\dot{\varphi}_{-})G(t_{0},t_{0})(\dot{\varphi}_{+}-\dot{\varphi}_{-})

\displaystyle-{\bar{\varphi}}^{2}\partial_{t}\partial_{t^{\prime}}G(t,t^{\prime})|^{t=t_{0,+}}_{t=t_{0,-}}|^{t^{\prime}=t_{0,+}}_{t^{\prime}=t_{0,-}}+(\dot{\varphi}_{+}-\dot{\varphi}_{-})|_{t=t_{0}}\partial_{t^{\prime}}G(t_{0},t^{\prime})|^{t^{\prime}=t_{0,+}}_{t^{\prime}=t_{0,-}}{\bar{\varphi}}\Bigg)\ .

(B.26)

The two additional terms that arise from equations acting on

cancel. One can then check that

\partial_{t}\partial_{t^{\prime}}G(t,t^{\prime})|^{t=t_{0,+}}_{t=t_{0,-}}|^{t^{\prime}=t_{0,+}}_{t^{\prime}=t_{0,-}}=0\ ,

(B.27)

and therefore our final expression for the boundary term is

\displaystyle{\rm B.T.}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\log\Lambda}K^{-1}\Bigg(-G(t_{0},t_{0})(\dot{\varphi}_{+}-\dot{\varphi}_{-})^{2}

(B.29)

\displaystyle+{\bar{\varphi}}\partial_{t}G(t,t_{0})|^{t=t_{0,+}}_{t=t_{0,-}}(\dot{\varphi}_{+}-\dot{\varphi}_{-})+(\dot{\varphi}_{+}-\dot{\varphi}_{-})|_{t=t_{0}}\partial_{t^{\prime}}G(t_{0},t^{\prime})|^{t^{\prime}=t_{0,+}}_{t^{\prime}=t_{0,-}}{\bar{\varphi}}\Bigg)\ .

One can check that this result is the same as in
[
48
]
restricted to

We see that this term is local on the boundary. However, due to the time derivatives, the ordering of the operators at

still matters since

\displaystyle\Gamma(\pm i\nu-n_{5})\Gamma(\mp i\nu-n_{6})k_{1}^{-2s_{1}}k_{2}^{-2s_{2}}k_{3}^{-2s_{3}}k_{4}^{-2s_{4}}k_{5}^{2n_{5}+2n_{6}}\frac{-(-\tau_{2})^{d-1-2(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+s_{4}-n_{5}-n_{6})}}{\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2}\pm\frac{i\nu}{2}-n_{5})}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle 2^{2\sum_{j=1}^{4}s_{j}-2n_{5}-2n_{6}}\frac{(-1)^{n_{5}+n_{6}}}{n_{5}!n_{6}!}\ ,

(C.7)

where we have carried out the
\tau_{1}
integral. We can further rewrite the denominator as

\frac{1}{\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2}-n_{5})\mp i\nu}=\frac{\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2}-n_{5})\pm i\nu}{\big(\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2}-n_{5})\big)^{2}+\nu^{2}}\ ,

(C.8)

and collect all the terms involving
\nu
,
n_{5}
and
n_{6}
. Choosing the term in which we have a factor of
e^{\pi\nu}
to treat the exponential suppression, this gives us

\displaystyle\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{i}}=\pm\nu_{i}}

\displaystyle i\lambda^{2}H^{2}\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{4}}\Gamma({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}})\Gamma({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{2}})\Gamma({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{3}})\Gamma({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{4}})(-\tau_{0})^{6-({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}+\bar{\nu}_{2}+\bar{\nu}_{3}+\bar{\nu}_{4}})}k_{1}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\bar{\nu}_{1}}}k_{2}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\bar{\nu}_{2}}}k_{3}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\bar{\nu}_{3}}}k_{4}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\bar{\nu}_{4}}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\tau_{0}}d\tau_{2}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{4}\frac{ds_{j}}{2\pi i}\Gamma(s_{j}-\frac{\nu_{j}}{2})\Gamma(s_{j}+\frac{\nu_{j}}{2})\;i^{-2\sum_{j=1}^{4}s_{j}+(\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}+\bar{\nu}_{2}+\bar{\nu}_{3}+\bar{\nu}_{4})}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle k_{1}^{-2s_{1}}k_{2}^{-2s_{2}}k_{3}^{-2s_{3}}k_{4}^{-2s_{4}}(-\tau_{2})^{d-1-2(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+s_{4})}2^{2\sum_{j=1}^{4}s_{j}-(\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}+\bar{\nu}_{2}+\bar{\nu}_{3}+\bar{\nu}_{4})}

(C.9)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{5},n_{6}}^{\infty}\frac{-i}{2\pi}e^{\pi\nu}\Gamma(i\nu-n_{5})\Gamma(-i\nu-n_{6})\left(\frac{i\tau_{2}k_{5}}{2}\right)^{2n_{5}+2n_{6}}\frac{\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2}-n_{5})+i\nu}{\big(\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2}-n_{5})\big)^{2}+\nu^{2}}\frac{(-1)^{n_{5}+n_{6}}}{n_{5}!n_{6}!}.

(C.10)

We then single out the last line which includes all the
n_{5}
,
n_{6}
and
\nu
dependencies,

\sum_{n_{5},n_{6}}^{\infty}\frac{-i}{2\pi}e^{\pi\nu}\Gamma(i\nu-n_{5})\Gamma(-i\nu-n_{6})\left(\frac{i\tau k_{5}}{2}\right)^{2n_{5}+2n_{6}}\frac{\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2}-n_{5})+i\nu}{\big(\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2}-n_{5})\big)^{2}+\nu^{2}}\frac{(-1)^{n_{5}+n_{6}}}{n_{5}!n_{6}!}\ .

(C.11)

The factor of
1/2\pi
is included for later convenience. We also included a phase factor
-i
. This comes from the fact that in order for the final result to be independent of
\tau_{0}
(so that it does not redshift away at
\tau_{0}\rightarrow 0
), we have
9-2(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+s_{4}-n_{5}-n_{6})-({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}+\bar{\nu}_{2}+\bar{\nu}_{3}+\bar{\nu}_{4}})=0
, which implies that the total power of
i
should be given by
i^{-9-2i\nu-4n_{6}+2({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}+\bar{\nu}_{2}+\bar{\nu}_{3}+\bar{\nu}_{4}})}
. With
n_{6}
being some integer, this is simply
-ie^{\pi\nu}i^{2({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}+\bar{\nu}_{2}+\bar{\nu}_{3}+\bar{\nu}_{4}})}
, hence the factor of
-i
. In other words, the phase for the first three lines is simply
i^{2({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}+\bar{\nu}_{2}+\bar{\nu}_{3}+\bar{\nu}_{4}})}
.

In our case,
\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}=\nu_{3}=\nu_{4}=3/2
. As a result, if all the
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{i}
’s are positive, we get a factor of
i^{12}=1
. If we flip a sign of one of the
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{i}
’s, we will pick up an extra factor of
i^{-6}=-1
, which does not swap the role of the real and imaginary parts. At the same time, other factors change with the different choice of the
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{i}
, so that the different terms do not cancel. As a result, as far as taking the real part is concerned, the terms in the full expression can only differ by an extra minus sign multiplied by some real factors, depending on the choices of the signs of the
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{i}
’s.

Noticing that
e^{\pi\nu}\Gamma(i\nu-n_{5})\Gamma(-i\nu-n_{6})
in general is of order
1/\nu^{n_{5}+n_{6}+1}
or higher, we can write out the lowest few orders of the expansion in
1/\nu
after summing over the first few
n_{5}
and
n_{6}
. The first few (real) terms are

{}\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}+\frac{-\alpha^{2}-k_{5}^{2}\tau^{2}}{\nu^{4}}+\frac{(\alpha^{2}+k_{5}^{2}\tau^{2})^{2}+4(1+\alpha)k_{5}^{2}\tau^{2}}{\nu^{6}}+\cdots

(C.12)

where
\alpha=\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2})
.
How does this compare with the EFT calculation? First, in this example, the EFT interaction term is given by

{\cal L}_{I}=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2H^{2}}\phi^{2}\frac{1}{\tau^{2}\partial_{\tau}^{2}-\tau(d-1)\partial_{\tau}+\frac{M^{2}}{H^{2}}+\tau^{2}k_{5}^{2}}\phi^{2}\ .

(C.13)

As a result, the four point contact term, in Mellin space, takes the form

\displaystyle\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{i}}=\pm\nu_{i}}

\displaystyle i\lambda^{2}H^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\right)^{4}\Gamma({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}})\Gamma({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{2}})\Gamma({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{3}})\Gamma({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{4}})(-\tau_{0})^{6}k_{1}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\bar{\nu}_{1}}}k_{2}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\bar{\nu}_{2}}}k_{3}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\bar{\nu}_{3}}}k_{4}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\bar{\nu}_{4}}}\left(-\frac{i}{2\tau_{0}}\right)^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}+\bar{\nu}_{2}+\bar{\nu}_{3}+\bar{\nu}_{4}}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{4}\frac{ds_{j}}{2\pi i}\Gamma(s_{j}-\frac{\nu_{j}}{2})\Gamma(s_{j}+\frac{\nu_{j}}{2})i^{-2(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+s_{4})}2^{2(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+s_{3})}k_{1}^{-2s_{1}}k_{2}^{-2s_{2}}k_{3}^{-2s_{3}}k_{4}^{-2s_{4}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\tau_{0}}d\tau\;(-\tau)^{-1-2(s_{3}+s_{4})}\frac{1}{\tau^{2}\partial_{\tau}^{2}-\tau(d-1)\partial_{\tau}+\frac{M^{2}}{H^{2}}+\tau^{2}k_{5}^{2}}(-\tau)^{d-2(s_{1}+s_{2})}\ .

(C.14)

To make further progress, notice that time derivatives can be exchanged with factors of
s_{i}
’s.
For example, looking at the form of the mode function (
C.2
), we see that when acting on the fields in Mellin space, we can replace
\tau\partial_{\tau}\rightarrow\frac{d}{2}-2s
. When acting on
\phi^{2}
, we have

\left[\tau^{2}\partial^{2}_{\tau}-(d-1)\tau\partial_{\tau}\right](k\tau)^{2n}\rightarrow\left[\left(\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2}-n)\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^{2}\right](k\tau)^{2n}\ .

(C.15)

This implies

\tau^{2}\partial_{\tau}^{2}-\tau(d-1)\partial_{\tau}+\frac{M^{2}}{H^{2}}\rightarrow\left(\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2})\right)^{2}+\nu^{2}=\alpha^{2}+\nu^{2}\ .

(C.16)

One might be tempted to simply carry out this replacement in (
C.13
). However, expanded in terms of large

, (
C.13
) will involve terms like

\left(\tau^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}-\tau(d-1)\frac{d}{d\tau}+\frac{d^{2}}{4}\right)(\tau^{2}k_{5}^{2})^{2n}\rightarrow\left(\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2})+2n\right)^{2}(\tau^{2}k_{5}^{2})^{2n}\ .

(C.17)

Carefully treating the extra
\tau k_{5}
factors, up to order

, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau^{2}\partial_{\tau}^{2}-\tau(d-1)\partial_{\tau}+\frac{M^{2}}{H^{2}}+\tau^{2}k_{5}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\left[1-\frac{\tau^{2}\partial_{\tau}^{2}-\tau(d-1)\partial_{\tau}+\frac{d^{2}}{4}+\tau^{2}k_{5}^{2}}{\nu^{2}}+\frac{\left(\tau^{2}\partial_{\tau}^{2}-\tau(d-1)\partial_{\tau}+\frac{d^{2}}{4}+\tau^{2}k_{5}^{2}\right)^{2}}{\nu^{4}}-\cdots\right]

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\left[1-\frac{\alpha^{2}+k_{5}^{2}\tau^{2}}{\nu^{2}}+\frac{\alpha^{4}+k_{5}^{4}\tau^{4}+k_{5}^{2}\tau^{2}\alpha^{2}+(\alpha+2)^{2}k_{5}^{2}\tau^{2}}{\nu^{6}}-\cdots\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}-\frac{\alpha^{2}+k_{5}^{2}\tau^{2}}{\nu^{4}}+\frac{(\alpha^{2}+k_{5}^{2}\tau^{2})^{2}+4(1+\alpha)k_{5}^{2}\tau^{2}}{\nu^{6}}-\cdots

We see that it takes the same form as (

Lastly, we check what happens to the factorized term. It turns out, that part is completely exponentially suppressed. To see this, notice that the factorized integrals are

f_{1}^{*}(\tau_{0})f_{2}^{*}(\tau_{0})\int_{-\infty}^{\tau_{0}}\frac{d\tau_{1}}{H^{4}\tau_{1}^{4}}f_{1}(\tau_{1})f_{2}(\tau_{1})g_{5}(\tau_{1}),\;\;f_{3}(\tau_{0})f_{4}(\tau_{0})\int_{-\infty}^{\tau_{0}}\frac{d\tau_{2}}{H^{4}\tau_{2}^{4}}f_{3}^{*}(\tau_{2})f_{4}^{*}(\tau_{2})g_{5}^{*}(\tau_{2})\ .

(C.19)

These are almost the same as the three point contact integrals, but without a
g_{5}(\tau_{0})
(or its complex conjugate). This makes it

\displaystyle\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{i}}=\pm\nu_{i}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\right)^{3}(-\tau_{0})^{6/2{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\bar{\nu}_{1}-\bar{\nu}_{2}}}\Gamma({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}})\Gamma({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{2}})k_{1}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\bar{\nu}_{1}}}k_{2}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\bar{\nu}_{2}}}2^{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}+\bar{\nu}_{2}}e^{\frac{i}{2}\pi({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\bar{\nu}_{1}+\bar{\nu}_{2}})}

\displaystyle\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{ds_{1}}{2\pi i}\frac{ds_{2}}{2\pi i}\frac{ds_{5}}{2\pi i}\;\frac{-(-\tau_{0})^{\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{5})}}{\frac{d}{2}-2(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{5})}e^{-i\pi(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{5})}2^{2(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{5})}k_{1}^{-2s_{1}}k_{2}^{-2s_{2}}k_{3}^{-2s_{5}}

\displaystyle\Gamma(s_{1}-\frac{\nu_{1}}{2})\Gamma(s_{1}+\frac{\nu_{1}}{2})\Gamma(s_{2}-\frac{\nu_{2}}{2})\Gamma(s_{2}+\frac{\nu_{2}}{2})\Gamma(s_{5}-\frac{i\nu}{2})\Gamma(s_{5}+\frac{i\nu}{2})\ .

(C.20)

Now, if we take the pole

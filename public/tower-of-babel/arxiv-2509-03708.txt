Twisted quantum doubles are sign problem-free
Leyna Shackleton

Abstract
The sign problem is one of the central obstacles to efficiently simulating quantum many-body systems. It is commonly believed that some phases of matter, such as the double semion model, have an intrinsic sign problem and can never be realized in a local sign problem-free Hamiltonian due to the non-positivity of the wavefunction. We show that this is not the case. Despite failing to be stoquastic - the standard criteria for the existence of a sign problem - the double semion model as well as all twisted quantum double phases of matter for finite groups 𝒢 \mathcal{G} can be realized in local sign problem-free Hamiltonians. The lack of a sign problem is not fine-tuned and does not require the Hamiltonian to be exactly solvable, with sign problem-free perturbations allowing access to a variety of topological phase transitions.



I
Introduction

Large-scale simulations of interacting quantum systems play an essential role in our understanding of the physical world. However, the exponential growth in Hilbert space dimension as a function of system size causes many systems of interest to be intractable numerically. Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) methods
Sandvik and Kurkijärvi 1991
;
Becca and Sorella 2017
offer a cure to this growth in complexity by stochastic sampling, and have offered crucial insight into complex phenomena such as critical Fermi surfaces
Berg et al. 2012
, deconfined criticality
Sandvik 2007
, and confinement in gauge theories
Xu et al. 2019
. The
sign problem
reflects an obstacle for QMC methods due to the inability to decompose the quantum partition function as a sum of real positive weights which can be interpreted as probabilities. The sign problem is present in many of the most interesting models in condensed matter physics, including the electron Hubbard model, frustrated antiferromagnets, and interacting electron gases.

Given this difficulty, it is natural to ask whether a phase of matter can possess a property that poses an
intrinsic
obstacle for a potential realization in a sign problem-free (SPF) system.
Proving the existence of an intrinsic sign problem necessarily involves caveats, as a broad claim about the intractability of
any
QMC method to a model has no clear connection to any physical properties. As such,
stoquasticity
and
wavefunction non-positivity
are often used as a proxy for an intrinsic sign problem in bosonic systems
Bravyi et al. 2007
. Stoquasticity demands that all off-diagonal matrix elements of a Hamiltonian are non-positive, and is a sufficient condition for a model to be SPF. By the Perron-Frobenius theorem, this implies that the ground state wavefunction can be chosen to have all positive entries in the stoquastic basis. Hence, demonstrating that a phase of matter requires non-positive entries in its ground state wavefunction for any local basis implies that no local stoquastic representation exists.

Intrinsic wavefunction non-positivity in a local basis has long been used as a test for the existence of an intrinsic sign problem. Intrinsic wavefunction non-positivity was first proven for the double semion phase of matter
Hastings 2016
and was later generalized to a large class of topological quantum field theories (TQFTs) and chiral matter
Ringel and Kovrizhin 2017
;
Smith et al. 2020
;
Golan et al. 2020
;
Kim et al. 2022
;
Seo et al. 2025
. This criteria is extremely stringent, with a recent study
Seo et al. 2025
demonstrating that 398 out of all 405 bosonic topological orders up to rank 12 have intrinsic wavefunction non-positivity.

However, despite stoquasticity being known to not be a necessary condition for a SPF Hamiltonian, no counterexamples to these no-go arguments have been proposed. Recent studies
Hen 2019
;
Gupta and Hen 2019
;
Hen 2021
have explored more general criteria for SPF Hamiltonians beyond stoquasticity. The authors propose a weaker condition, which is equivalent to requiring that the Hamiltonian can be made stoquastic by rotating each many-body basis state by a state-dependent phase (such transformations generally cannot be expressed in terms of local unitaries). This observation immediately calls into question the relevance of wavefunction non-positivity to intrinsic sign problems. Since any wavefunction can trivially be made positive by phase rotations of the basis states, the condition of stoquasticity modulo these transformations would appear to be orthogonal to the condition of wavefunction non-positivity.

In this work, we expand on this observation by demonstrating that a large class of topological orders, including the double semion model, can be realized in local SPF models despite their non-stoquasticity. The logic of our approach is illustrated in Fig.
1
. Our starting point is an analysis of the sign problem in symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases
Chen et al. 2013
;
Senthil 2015
. We show that all bosonic SPTs classified by the group cohomology of a discrete unitary symmetry
\mathcal{G}
are related to stoquastic Hamiltonians by many-body phase rotations which implement cocycle phases through local unitary transformations. Hence, all of these bosonic SPTs have a local SPF, but generically non-stoquastic, Hamiltonian. We then gauge these SPT Hamiltonians to obtain models which exhibit topological order and are described by a twisted quantum double, also known as a Dijkgraaf-Witten (DW) theory
Dijkgraaf and Witten 1990
. After this gauging procedure, the new Hamiltonian remains related to a stoquastic model - an untwisted quantum double - via a non-local but diagonal unitary transformation, hence rendering them SPF. These quantum doubles include the aforementioned double semion model as well as many non-Abelian TQFTs. We show that neither the SPT models nor their gauged counterparts rely on a fine-tuned exactly solvable Hamiltonians, and can be perturbed without spoiling the absence of a sign problem.

\mathcal{G}

Brell 2015
;
Pace et al. 2025

\mathcal{G}
. We consider a lattice of arbitrary dimension and with
N
sites, where on each site
j

g\in\mathcal{G}

\ket{\psi}=\ket{g_1, g_2,\ldots, g_N}
. These states transform in the regular representation of the group
\mathcal{G}
. The group-based
X

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{X}_{j}^{(g)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\bm{h}\}}\ket{g_j \bm{h}}\bra{\bm{h}}

(1)

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{X}_{j}^{(g)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\bm{h}\}}\ket{ \bm{h}\bar{g}_j}\bra{\bm{h}}

and implement left and right multiplication. We define
\overline{g}\equiv g^{-1}
. The group-based
Z

\mathcal{G}
,
\Gamma
.

\left[Z_{j}^{(\Gamma)}\right]_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{\{\bm{h}\}}\left[\Gamma(h_{j})_{\alpha\beta}\right]\ket{ \bm{h}}\bra{\bm{h}}

(2)

It will eventually become necessary to gauge these models by introducing group-valued degrees of freedom on the edges
\langle jk\rangle
of the lattice. To do this, each edge must come with an orientation, and we define
\overrightarrow{X}_{jk}^{(g)}

\overrightarrow{X}

\langle jk\rangle

\overleftarrow{X}

III
Review of the sign problem beyond stoquasticity

Here, we summarize the developments of
Hen 2019
;
Gupta and Hen 2019
;
Hen 2021
which demonstrated an alternative paradigm to stoquasticity.

Consider a Hamiltonian
H=\sum_{b}H_{b}
defined with respect to some local computational basis
\{\ket{a}\}
. The Hamiltonian is decomposed as a sum of local terms
H_{b}
, each of which has the property that, when applied to any basis state
\ket{a}
, it either annihilates it or takes it to a new basis state
\ket{a}
. In other words, each term acts as a permutation matrix on the set of basis states, with each state picking up a coefficient (potentially zero) under permutation.

With this terminology, we expand the partition function
Z=\Tr\left[e^{-\beta H}\right]
in powers of
\beta
and insert a complete set of states
\ket{z_j}
to obtain

\displaystyle Z=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j}\sum_{\{b_{1},b_{2}\ldots b_{n}\}}\frac{(-\beta)^{n}}{n!}\bra{z_j}H_{b_{1}}H_{b_{2}}\ldots H_{b_{n}}\ket{z_j}\,.

(3)

If each term in this summation is real and positive, than it can be interpreted as a sum of probabilities, and one can run SPF Monte Carlo simulations to evaluate observables with respect to this probability measure. If the Hamiltonian is term-wise stoquastic, i.e.
\bra{z_j}H_{b}\ket{z_k}<0
, then this condition is satisfied. Since we can always shift
H
by a constant, this restriction is only non-trivial for
j\neq k
. However, it is clear that this condition is overly stringent. It may be that each term individually possesses a non-trivial phase factor, but these phase factors always conspire to cancel out for any non-zero matrix element
\bra{z_j}H_{b_{1}}H_{b_{2}}\ldots H_{b_{n}}\ket{z_j}
. A well-known example of this is the Heisenberg antiferromagnet on a bipartite lattice,
H=\sum_{\langle jk\rangle}\sigma^{z}_{j}\sigma^{z}_{k}+\sigma^{+}_{j}\sigma^{-}_{k}+\sigma^{-}_{j}\sigma^{+}_{k}
. The last two terms are non-stoquastic; however, the bipartite geometry requires that they appear an even number of times for any non-zero term in Eq.
3
, so the system is SPF. In this case, stoquasticity can be easily restored via an on-site unitary transformation, so it is not regarded as a counterexample to the stoquasticity criteria.

This alternative criteria, while less stringent that stoquasticity, is much more difficult to check. Progress can be made by showing that, if such a condition holds, one can always recover stoquasticity by redefining the phases of the many-body basis,
\ket{z_j}\rightarrow e^{i\theta_{j}}\ket{z_j}
. Such transformations are generically non-local.

IV
Discrete bosonic SPTs are sign-problem-free

As an initial result, we demonstrate that all bosonic SPT states classified by the group cohomology of any discrete unitary symmetry
\mathcal{G}
are SPF. Since the wavefunctions of these bosonic SPT states can always be deformed to a product state by finite-depth local unitary (FDLU) gates, it may be argued that these states do not possess an intrinsic sign problem in the first place, as has previously been noted
Ellison et al. 2021
- this relation has been used to effectively study the Levin-Gu SPT
Dupont et al. 2021
with QMC. However, our statement is a stronger one - the FDLU gates, or equivalently the phases attached to non-trivial cocycles in the group cohomology, are precisely the many-body basis rotations which preserve the sign structure of the QMC simulation. Hence, no transformation to a trivial phase is necessary, as all bosonic SPT phases possess a SPF Hamiltonian in their original basis even if they are non-stoquastic.

For a symmetry group
\mathcal{G}
in
d
-dimensions, we take our lattice to be a triangulation of
d
-dimensional space with the each state in the local Hilbert space at each site labeled by a symmetry group element
g\in\mathcal{G}
. Different SPT phases are classified by the cohomology group
H^{d+1}(\mathcal{G},\operatorname{U}(1))
. Given a choice of homogeneous cocycle
\nu_{d+1}\in H^{d+1}(\mathcal{G},\operatorname{U}(1))
, the SPT wavefunction is given by

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}=\sum_{\{g_{i}\}}\left[\prod_{\Delta^{d}}\nu_{d+1}(0,g_{i_{1}},g_{i_{2}},\ldots g_{i_{d+1}})^{\epsilon(\Delta^{d})}\right]\ket{\{g_i\}}\,,

(4)

where
\epsilon=\pm 1
specifies the orientation of the cocycle.
The wavefunction is an equal-weight superposition of all basis states with phases determined by the cocycle
\nu
. In other words, it is equivalent to a trivial “paramagnet” after a many-body basis rotation
Chen et al. 2013
.

U=\sum_{\{g_{i}\}}\left[\prod_{\Delta^{d}}\nu_{d+1}(0,g_{i_{1}},\ldots g_{i_{d}})^{\epsilon(\Delta^{d})}\right]\ket{\{g_i\}}\bra{\{g_i\}}\,.

(5)

This trivial paramagnet can be realized in a stoquastic Hamiltonian, an example of which consists of a sum of on-site terms,
H-=\sum_{j,h}\overleftarrow{X}_{j}^{(h)}
(specification of the right action is necessary for non-Abelian groups so that the Hamiltonian commutes with the global symmetry
U_{g}=\prod_{j}\overrightarrow{X}_{j}^{(g)}
). Hence, the conjugated Hamiltonian which possesses the SPT ground state will also be SPF despite being non-stoquastic. Since
U
can be expressed as a product of local unitaries, each of which attaches the necessary cocycle phases, the conjugated Hamiltonian which realizes the SPT ground state will also be composed of local terms,
B_{j}^{(h)}\equiv U\overleftarrow{X}_{j}^{(h)}U^{\dagger}
. Note that while
U
cannot be decomposed into
symmetric
local unitaries, as required by the definition of an SPT, the full operator regarded as a single non-local unitary is symmetric, as shown in Appendix H of
Chen et al. 2013
.

To give an explicit example, we consider the Levin-Gu model
Levin and Gu 2012
whose ground state is an SPT protected by a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry with
d=2
. The model is defined with qubits on a triangular lattice, and the Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H_{\text{Levin-Gu}}=-\sum_{j}\sigma^{x}_{j}\prod_{\triangle_{jkl}}i^{\frac{1}{2}(-1-\sigma^{z}_{k}\sigma^{z}_{l})}\equiv-\sum_{j}B_{j}\,,

(6)

where
\prod_{\triangle_{jkl}}
is a product over all triangles whose vertices include
j
.
This Hamiltonian is non-stoquastic, yet all phase factors cancel out in the QMC procedure defined in Section
III
. This is because the action of
B_{j}
on a basis state gives a factor of
-1
if it changes the parity of the number of domain walls, and
+1
otherwise. The domain wall parity must change an even number of times in order for a state to come back to itself, so all terms in the SSE are positive. Alternatively, one can simply observe that the Levin-Gu Hamiltonian is equivalent to an Ising paramagnet,
H_{para}=-\sum_{j}\sigma^{x}_{j}
after a unitary many-body basis rotation
U=(-1)^{N_{dw}}
, where
N_{dw}
counts the number of domain walls in each basis state.

Our result is robust to perturbations away from this exactly-solvable limit, as any stoquastic deformations to the stoquastic dual model results in a deformed SPT Hamiltonian which remains SPF. For the case of the Levin-Gu model, we can perturb the quantum paramagnet with a nearest-neighbor Ising interaction
J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\sigma^{z}_{i}\sigma^{z}_{j}
, which preserves the stoquasticity. Such a perturbation is invariant under the duality transformation, so these nearest-neighbor Ising interactions can also be added to the Levin-Gu Hamiltonian while preserving the SPF nature. Equivalently, it is easy to see that perturbations which are diagonal in the computational basis cannot affect the sign structure of the QMC expansion in Section
III
, so generic
\sigma^{z}
perturbations can be freely added to the Levin-Gu Hamiltonian. The generalization of this to an arbitrary group is straightforward. These perturbations will generically drive the system into a symmetry-broken phase, with the critical theory being described by a
gapless
SPT state
Scaffidi et al. 2017
. The freedom to choose the spatial structure as well as the sign of the
\sigma^{z}
interactions allows for a plethora of different symmetry-broken phases which behave differently under the space group symmetries of the lattice.

V
Gauging preserves sign structure

We have demonstrated that every bosonic SPT that falls under the group cohomology classification for unitary symmetries is SPF in its local basis.
Using this result, we now show that gauging these SPTs yields an SPF Hamiltonian that is not locally connected to its corresponding stoquastic Hamiltonian.

An SPT Hamiltonian can be gauged in order obtain a twisted quantum double theory. We will give a general construction for a gauging procedure that preserves the QMC sign, but for illustrative purposes, we first gauge the Levin-Gu model discussed in the previous section and show that this gauging procedure does not spoil the SPF nature of the model. As done in
Levin and Gu 2012
, we gauge the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry of both the Ising paramagnet and the Levin-Gu model to obtain the toric code and double semion model, respectively. We introduce qubits on the edges of the triangular lattice,
\tau_{jk}
. Terms like
\sigma^{z}_{j}\sigma^{z}_{k}
are replaced with
\sigma^{z}_{j}\tau^{z}_{jk}\sigma^{z}_{k}
,
and we add an energetic penalty to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
fluxes by including a term
\sum_{\triangle_{jkl}}\tau^{z}_{jk}\tau^{z}_{kl}\tau^{z}_{lj}
to the Hamiltonian. These Hamiltonians operate in a restricted subspace where the Gauss law
G_{j}\equiv\sigma^{x}_{j}\prod_{\langle jk\rangle}\tau^{x}_{jk}=1
is satisfied, which we impose energetically by a term
-\sum_{j}G_{j}
.

\displaystyle H_{\text{DS}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j}\widetilde{B}_{j}+\sum_{\langle jkl\rangle}\tau^{z}_{jk}\tau^{z}_{kl}\tau^{z}_{lj}-\sum_{J}G_{j}\,,

(7)

\displaystyle H_{\text{TC}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j}\sigma^{x}_{j}+\sum_{\langle jkl\rangle}\tau^{z}_{jk}\tau^{z}_{kl}\tau^{z}_{lj}-\sum_{J}G_{j},,

\displaystyle\widetilde{B_{j}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sigma^{x}_{j}\prod_{\triangle_{jkl}}i^{\frac{1}{2}(-1-\sigma^{z}_{k}\tau^{z}_{kl}\sigma^{z}_{l})}

The crux of our argument involves how the equality
H_{\text{Levin-Gu}}=UH_{\text{trivial}}U^{\dagger}
behaves upon gauging each of the operators. As emphasized before, even though
U
cannot be implemented with symmetric FDLUs, the full unitary operation is symmetric and can therefore be consistently gauged,
U\rightarrow\widetilde{U}
, while commuting with the Gauss law, at the cost of losing the ability to decompose it into local terms.
Now we must ask whether the gauging map is a homomorphism of the algebra of symmetric operators, i.e. the equalities
H_{\text{SPT}}=UH_{\text{trivial}}U^{\dagger}
and
U^{\dagger}U=\mathds{1}
remain true for their gauged counterparts. This is not the case in general, as the identity
(\sigma^{z}_{j}\sigma^{z}_{k})(\sigma^{z}_{k}\sigma^{z}_{l})(\sigma^{z}_{l}\sigma^{z}_{j})=\mathds{1}
holds true before gauging but evaluates to a closed loop of
\tau^{z}
operators passing through sites
j
,
k
and
l
after gauging. More generally, equalities of symmetric operators only hold true modulo closed loops of
\tau^{z}
operators, or Wilson loops. An explicit evaluation of
\widetilde{U}
in Appendix
A
demonstrates that
\widetilde{U}
remains diagonal and unitary; however, the identity
H_{DS}=\widetilde{U}H_{\text{TC}}\widetilde{U}^{\dagger}
only holds true in the flux-free subspace where
\prod_{\langle jk\rangle\in\mathcal{C}}\tau^{z}_{jk}=1
for all closed loops
\mathcal{C}
.

We briefly stress the importance of this point. In this subspace where flux excitations are prohibited, the double semion model is directly dual to the Levin-Gu model
Levin and Gu 2012
. So it would seem that we have learned nothing new from the statement that the double semion model is SPF in this limit. However, this statement by itself is still notable; it says that in a particular limit of the double semion Hamiltonian where flux excitations are strongly suppressed, the sign problem likewise vanishes. Viewed through this lens, the sign problem is not a feature of the
ground state
and the intrinsic non-positivity thereof, but rather of the allowed excitations present in the thermal density matrix at non-zero temperature.

Despite this, we make a stronger claim, which is that the double semion Hamiltonian is SPF even away from this flux-free limit. However, this is a less generic fact that relies on the convention used in
Levin and Wen 2005
;
Levin and Gu 2012
of dressing terms in our Hamiltonian with a projection operator such that each local operator acts in a subspace that is locally flux-free. Each instance of
\sigma^{x}_{j}
gets multiplied by
\mathcal{O}_{j}=\prod_{\triangle{jkl}}(1-\tau_{jk}^{z}\tau_{kl}^{z}\tau_{lj}^{z})/2
. We now invert the unitary mapping and observe that
\widetilde{U}^{\dagger}H_{\text{DS}}\widetilde{U}
maps to
H_{\text{TC}}
up to functions of gauge flux operators that evaluate to
1
in the flux-free subspace. We argue that this non-local variant of
H_{\text{TC}}
remains SPF, which then renders
H_{\text{DS}}
SPF.

To be precise, each term
\widetilde{B}_{j}
of the double semion Hamiltonian gets mapped, up to diagonal terms, to
\sigma^{x}_{j}f_{j}(\mathcal{C})
, where
f_{j}
is a
real
function (required by Hermiticity) of closed loops of
\tau^{z}
operators that evaluates to
1
in the flux-free subspace. We now consider a generic non-zero term in the QMC expansion given in Eq.
3
. Since the Gauss law term is the only operator that flips the gauge field, requiring that the gauge field gets mapped back to itself implies that any string of operators where
\bra{a}H_{b_{1}}H_{b_{2}}\ldots H_{b_{n}}\ket{a}\neq 0
must either have every local Gauss law term appearing an even number of times or an odd number of times. However, only the former option is possible outside of the flux-free subspace - in the latter case, all matter fields must be flipped in order to take the state
\ket{a}
back to itself, which is impossible since our Hamiltonian is defined such that
\sigma^{x}_{j}
doesn’t act on sites that neighbor gauge flux excitations. If each Gauss law term appears an even number of times, each of the terms
\sigma^{x}_{j}f_{j}(\mathcal{C})
must also appear an even number of times in order to map the matter fields back to themselves. Since our Hamiltonian does not contain any terms that change the gauge flux,
f(\mathcal{C})
evaluates to the same number each time, leading to an overall positive contribution. Hence, this non-local variant of the toric code remains SPF and maps to our desired double semion Hamiltonian under a diagonal unitary transformation, rendering the latter SPF.

While a proper QMC simulation of this model is beyond the scope of this paper, we have randomly sampled
10^{5}
non-zero contributions to the double semion partition function through the expansion in Eq.
3
on a
4\times 4
lattice and verified that all contributions are positive. While our construction retains the original matter fields of the SPT Hamiltonian, we can also project out the matter fields by setting
\sigma^{z}_{k}\tau^{z}_{kl}\sigma_{l}^{z}\equiv\tau_{z}
and
\sigma_{j}^{x}\equiv\prod_{k}\tau^{x}_{jk}
;

We now argue the general case. Recall that given a stoquastic Hamiltonian
H_{\text{trivial}}=-\sum_{j,h}\overleftarrow{X}_{j}^{(h)}
, we can construct a symmetric unitary transformation
U
that maps
H_{\text{trivial}}
to a local symmetric Hamiltonian with an SPT ground state,
H_{\text{SPT}}
. We now gauge both sides of the equation
H_{\text{SPT}}=UH_{\text{trivial}}U^{\dagger}
through the minimal coupling procedure of leaving
\overleftarrow{X}_{j}^{(h)}
untouched and attaching strings of
Z^{\Gamma}
gauge operators (Wilson lines) inside
\left[Z^{\overline{\Gamma}}_{j}Z^{\Gamma}_{k}\right]_{\alpha\beta}
terms. As was the case with the double semion model, the gauging procedure preserves the equality between the gauged SPT and gauged trivial Hamiltonian up to functions of Wilson loops. Specifically, the gauged unitary transformation maps each term
\widetilde{B}_{j}^{(h)}
in the twisted quantum double Hamiltonian to terms of the form
\overleftarrow{X}_{j}^{(h)}f_{j}^{(h)}(\mathcal{C})
. Dressing our operators with projections into the locally flux-free subspace ensures the group composition law
\widetilde{B}_{j}^{(h)}\widetilde{B}_{j}^{(g)}=\widetilde{B}_{j}^{(hg)}
holds, which in turn requires
f_{j}^{(h)}(\mathcal{C})f_{j}^{(g)}(\mathcal{C})=f_{j}^{(hg)}(\mathcal{C})
. Hermiticity requires
f_{j}^{(h)}(\mathcal{C})=f_{j}^{(\overline{h})*}(\mathcal{C})
, and any non-zero term in the QMC expansion outside of the flux-free subspace must come in the form of
X_{j}^{(h)}X_{j}^{(\overline{h})}
since the collection of Gauss law terms that appear in the QMC expansion must map the matter fields back to their original state via the same argument as the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
case. Therefore, the excess flux-dependent terms must always give a trivial factor in the QMC expansion.

We have demonstrated the existence of a many-body basis rotation that maps a twisted quantum double Hamiltonian to a SPF Hamiltonian of its untwisted counterpart up to non-local Wilson loop operators, which we argue do not affect the sign structure of the QMC simulation.
As emphasized in the previous section, there are allowed perturbations to these Hamiltonians which preserve their SPF nature. These are diagonal perturbations taking the form
\left[Z_{j}^{\overline{\Gamma}}\left(\ldots\right)Z_{k}^{\Gamma}\right]_{\alpha\beta}
, where the
(\ldots)
indicates the necessary string of gauge operators. In the language of the underlying topological order, these create gauge flux excitations classified by different conjugacy classes of
\mathcal{G}
. Increasing the strength of this perturbation proliferates these anyons, leading to condensation of fluxes which quotient out a normal subgroup of the gauge group. As was the case for SPT perturbations, the rich interplay between these phases and space group symmetries, as well as their corresponding topological phase transitions, is a promising direction for future research.

VI
Explicit sign problem-free models

It is important to explicitly check the SPF nature of these twisted quantum double models. We demonstrate this using exactly-solvable models of twisted quantum doubles given in
Hu et al. 2013
. As previously emphasized, our results are
not
restricted to these exactly solvable limits, as one can always add perturbations which are diagonal in the computational basis without affecting the sign problem.

We provide a self-contained definition in Appendix
C
of the models defined in
Hu et al. 2013
and summarize the key points here. The model is defined on an arbitrary triangulation of a two-dimensional surface, with group element-valued gauge fields
g_{jk}
living on the bonds of the triangulation between sites
j
and
k
. The sites are enumerated so that each bond has a definite orientation which points from site
k
to
j
if
j<k
and the opposite direction otherwise. The Hamiltonian, which is specified by an inhomogeneous 3-cocycle
\omega
, consists of two terms:

•

An operator
B_{p}
on each plaquette that energetically imposes flatness of the gauge field, or that the product of group elements around each plaquette is the identity.

•

An operator
A_{v}^{g}
for each vertex
v
and group element
g
, which acts on all neighboring edges with
g
and attaches a phase determined by the cocycle
\omega
. The precise phase factor is defined in Appendix
C
.

Analogous to
Levin and Gu 2012
and in the previous section, we supplement the action of the
A_{v}^{g}
operators with a projection operator which ensures that the gauge flux on all neighboring plaquettes vanish. This does not change the universal physics of our Hamiltonian, but turns out to be essential for ensuring the absence of a sign problem.

Consider a generic term in the QMC expansion of
3
. Since the
B_{p}
operators are diagonal in the computational basis, we can without loss of generality consider the matrix elements of strings of
A_{v}^{g}
operators. By construction, operators on different vertices commute and on the same vertex obey the relation
A^{g}_{v}A^{h}_{v}=A^{gh}_{v}
, which allows us to simplify any string of operators to an expression where there is at one operator acting on each site,
\bra{a}\prod_{j}A^{g_{j}}_{j}\ket{a}
(this contains the possibility of no operator acting on a site, which we represent by
g_{j}=1
). Which combination of operators, when acting on the basis state
\ket{a}
, will bring us back to the same state? Recall the intuition that this expression defines a cycle on a graph of basis states,
\ket{a}\rightarrow A_{1}^{g_{1}}\ket{a}\rightarrow A_{2}^{g_{2}}A_{1}^{g_{1}}\ket{a}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow\ket{a}
. A key point that allows these models to be sign problem-free is that
the number of distinct cycles is extremely small
. In such a cycle, observe that the gauge field
g_{jk}
living between vertices
j
and
k
changes by
g\rightarrow g_{j}gg_{k}^{-1}
(with directionality fixed by
j<k
). Requiring that this action on all bonds recovers the original state is a stringent condition on the allowed sequences of operators. The nature of these cycles is illustrated in Fig.
2
, which we elaborate on below.

Figure 2:
In the models of twisted quantum doubles defined in
Hu et al. 2013
, the non-zero terms in the QMC expansion fall into two categories. (a) For states with non-zero gauge flux, no non-trivial cycles exist and all terms in the QMC expansion are positive. (b) For states in the flux-free sector, there exist non-trivial cycles that are equivalent to a global gauge transformation, which evaluate to the identity on a closed manifold.

We first note that any non-trivial cycle must live in the flux-free subspace. If
\ket{a}
has non-zero gauge flux on a plaquette, then
A_{v}^{g}
cannot act on the adjacent vertices. Gauge fields connected to those vertices in question now transform in the cycle by a single group element,
g\rightarrow g_{j}g
, which then forces
g_{j}=1
. Applying this argument recursively leads to the requirement that
g_{j}=1
for
all

j
.

We now search for non-trivial cycles in the locally flux-free subspace. The general solution to this can be constructed in the following way. Fix the operator on an arbitrary site
j
to correspond to
g_{j}
. From this choice, all other operators are specified by the “parallel transport”
g_{k}=g_{kj}^{-1}g_{j}g_{kj}
. The flux-free condition ensures that this construction is path-independent, although the presence of non-contractible loops imposes the constraint that
g_{j}
belong to the centralizer subgroup of the two fluxes generated by computing
g_{jk}
along the two distinct non-contractible loops. These are the only non-trivial cycles that appear in the QMC expansion; however, the action of this combined operator
\prod_{j}A_{j}^{g_{j}}
on any state defined on a closed manifold is the identity, as it corresponds to the action of a gauge transformation on each site
Hu et al. 2013
. This is not a trivial fact and follows from properties of the 3-cocycle, which we provide more detail on in Appendix
C
. Therefore, the phase factor associated to each of these cycles is
1
.

In summary, we have demonstrated that any non-vanishing expression
\bra{a}A^{g_{1}}_{1}A^{g_{2}}_{2}\ldots\ket{a}
has a trivial phase factor, and hence the model is sign problem-free. We stress that this result holds even when we perturb away from the exactly solvable limit and include diagonal perturbations that do not generically commute with
A_{v}^{g}
. This is especially important as our argument would make it seem as though the flux excitations of our system are trivial due to the lack of non-trivial cycles for states with gauge flux. In the exactly solvable limit, there are many operator strings such as
A_{1}^{g_{1}}A_{2}^{g_{2}}A_{1}^{\overline{g_{1}}}A_{2}^{\overline{g_{2}}}
which are equivalent to the trivial cycle. When we include diagonal perturbations in our system, insertions of these perturbations in this operator string causes the
magnitude
of its contribution to become non-trivial, despite the
sign
remaining equivalent to a trivial cycle. One way to view this is that the diagonal perturbations are classical in the sense that they do not alter the sign structure of the QMC simulation, yet simultaneously intrinsically quantum as they are introduce non-commutivity to the system.

VII
Conclusion

We have demonstrated that all twisted quantum doubles of a finite group
G
support a SPF Hamiltonian. Recall that a recent work
Seo et al. 2025
argued that 398 of 405 bosonic topological orders in
d=2
up to rank 12 have an intrinsic sign problem, based on using stoquasticity as an indicator. Within the space of low-rank topological orders, twisted quantum doubles occupy a rather small subset; below rank
12
, the only groups are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
S_{3}
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. Considering the twisted versions of these models, this yields
8
counterexamples to this criteria. However, it is shown in
Ringel and Kovrizhin 2017
that every twisted quantum double of
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{k}
possesses an obstruction to realization in a stoquastic Hamiltonian, so our construction provides a large class of counterexamples. The more general interplay between twisted quantum doubles and stoquasticity remains unexplored. We also emphasize that our construction applies in dimensions other than
2
, with higher-dimensional quantum doubles supporting loop-like excitations
Wang and Levin 2014
;
Wan et al. 2015
.

General design principles for non-stoquastic SPF Hamiltonians remain an important open question. Even though wavefunction non-positivity has been shown to not disallow the existence of a SPF Hamiltonian, this condition still prevents the Hamiltonian from being stoquastic. Hence, for these phases to appear in a realistic spin model, such a model must be non-stoquastic and therefore violate known design principles for SPF Hamiltonians. The method used in this paper of starting with a stoquastic “seed” Hamiltonian and rotating to a non-stoquastic one may be generalizable to more conventional models, such as Heisenberg models. Our results also stress the fact that diagonal perturbations do not alter the sign structure of a QMC simulation, but may nevertheless be highly non-trivial and perturb a system away from exact solvability - this offers another route to obtaining non-stoquastic SPF Hamiltonians.

Our results suggest a more intricate interplay between topological order and the sign problem. Although we have demonstrated the non-necessity of wavefunction positivity and our results are not fine-tuned to commuting projector Hamiltonians, our results do rely heavily on the assumption that the gauge fluxes are frozen, i.e. no term in the Hamiltonian induces a transition to different flux sectors. This hints at a potentially more fundamental obstruction to SPF simulations that comes from dynamical properties of the Hamiltonian rather than ground state properties. We note that even if such an obstacle exists for realizing certain topological phases in more realistic Hamiltonians, our results still imply that the severity of the sign problem may be parametrically suppressed in the size of the gauge flux gap.

A promising direction to explore is the interplay between the sign problem and TQFT data in string-net models
Levin and Wen 2005
, which give a more general method for constructing TQFTs than gauging SPT states of discrete groups. We show in Appendix
B
that Abelian string-net models are SPF. The results in the main text suggest that this also applies to non-Abelian TQFTs where the input fusion category is group-like. String-net models give a platform where one can study the sign problem in TQFTs beyond group-like fusion categories.

Our results regarding the sign problem in SPT phases may potentially be generalizable beyond group cohomology constructions, in particular for continuous groups. The bosonic integer quantum Hall state
Lu and Vishwanath 2012
;
Senthil and Levin 2013
is a prototypical example of an SPT in 2+1D protected by a
\operatorname{U}(1)
symmetry, for which no known SPF realization is known (we mention a related study of an
\operatorname{U}(1)\times\operatorname{U}(1)
model which is SPF after a non-local duality transformation
Geraedts and Motrunich 2013
). The possibility of enriching the topological order with additional global symmetries
Barkeshli et al. 2019
and searching for SPF models that can access phase transitions to symmetry-broken phases, is also a promising direction for future research.

There are intriguing open questions regarding the connection between properties of TQFTs and the sign problem, in particular regarding edge gappabilty. It was previously argued
Seo et al. 2025
that edge gappability is a necessary condition for the absence of an intrinsic sign problem. As we have stressed, this condition is not necessary for an SPF Hamiltonian, but rather a stoquastic one. However, because Abelian string-net models realize all non-chiral Abelian topological orders with a gappabale edge
Lin and Levin 2014
, our findings support the converse statement: for Abelian non-chiral topological orders, the presence of a gappable edge is a
sufficient
condition for a SPF Hamiltonian. An open question is whether this condition is also necessary given our more robust definition of a sign problem.

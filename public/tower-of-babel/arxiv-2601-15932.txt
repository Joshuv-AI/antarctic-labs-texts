The representations of the Lie superalgebra 𝔭 ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathfrak{p}(3) in prime characteristic
Ye Ren

Abstract
. Let 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} be the Lie superalgebra 𝔭 ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathfrak{p}(3) of rank 2 over an algebraically closed field 𝕂 \mathbb{K} of characteristic p > 3 p>3 . We classify all irreducible modules of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , and give the character formulae for irreducible modules.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
20G05; 17B20;17B45; 17B50

Introduction

The finite-dimensional simple Lie superalgebras over the field of complex numbers were classified by Kac in the 1970s (cf.
[
8
]
). Although the classification of finite-dimensional simple Lie superalgebras over a field of prime characteristic has not been completed, the modular
version of the complex simple Lie superalgebras is still important. Recently, there has been an increasing interest in modular representation theory of restricted Lie superalgebras (cf.
[
4
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
16
]
, etc.). Nevertheless, their irreducible modules are not well-understood. The goal of this paper is to initiate the study of modular representations of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{p}(n)
over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
p
. The method for classifying the irreducible modules of
\mathfrak{p}(3)
is derived from
[
14
,
15
]
.

This article is divided into two parts. The first part gives the general setup for the restricted Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{p}(n)
and gives a proof that the Kac modules
K_{\chi}(\lambda)(\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi})
are irreducible for regular nilpotent
\chi
when
p>n
. The second part gives a complete classification of simple
\mathfrak{p}(3)
-modules and their character formulae for
p>3
.

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>2
. In this paper, it is the base field of the theory.
The concept of restricted Lie superalgebras given in
[
11
]
is a generalization of the concept of restricted Lie algebras (cf.
[
7
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

A Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
is called a restricted Lie superalgebra, if there is a
p
th power map
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
, denoted as
[p]
, satisfying:

(1)
(kx)^{[p]}=k^{p}x^{[p]}
for any
k\in\mathbb{K}
and any
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
,

(2)
[x^{[p]},y]=(\text{ad}x)^{p}(y)
for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
and any
y\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(3)
(x+y)^{[p]}=x^{[p]}+y^{[p]}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(x,y)
for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
where
is_{i}(x,y)
is the coefficient of
\lambda^{i-1}
in
(\text{ad}(\lambda x+y))^{p-1}(x)
.

By definition,
x^{p}-x^{[p]}
are in the center of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
. According to
[
11
, Proposition 2.3]
, every irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
is finite dimensional. So there is a
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}^{*}
, such that

(x^{p}-x^{[p]})_{|M}=\chi(x)^{p}\text{id}_{|M},

which is called a
p
-character of
M
. Let
I_{\chi}
be the ideal of
U(\mathfrak{g})
generated by
x^{p}-x^{[p]}-\chi(x)^{p},x\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. We call
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{g})/I_{\chi}
the reduced enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, call
U_{0}(\mathfrak{g})
the restricted enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. The
U_{0}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules are usually called restricted modules.

1.2.

For
n>2
, the standard matrix realization of the periplectic Lie superalgebra is given as follows:

\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}(n)=\left\{X=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\\
\end{pmatrix}|A=-D^{t},B^{t}=B,C^{t}=-C\right\}.

Here,
A,B,C,D
are
n\times n
matrices. The periplectic Lie superalgebra
\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}(n)
is a restricted Lie superalgebra. Let the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{p}(n)
be the commutator of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}(n)
, so

\mathfrak{p}(n)=\left\{X=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\\
\end{pmatrix}|A=-D^{t},B^{t}=B,C^{t}=-C,\text{tr}(A)=\text{tr}(B)=0\right\}.

It is also a restricted Lie superalgebra. From now on, let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}(n)
. In this paper, we mainly consider the representation theory of
\mathfrak{p}(n)
.

Let
E_{i,j}
be the matrix of order
2n\times 2n
which the
i
th row,
j
th column is
1
and the rest are
0
,
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Let
\epsilon_{i}
be the linear map
\epsilon_{i}(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}E_{k,k})=a_{i},a_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
. Denote by

H_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}=E_{i,i}-E_{j,j}-E_{n+i,n+i}+E_{n+j,n+j},1\leq i<j\leq n,

X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}=E_{i,j}-E_{n+j,n+i},1\leq i,j\leq n,i\neq j,

X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}=E_{n+i,j}-E_{n+j,i},1\leq i<j\leq n,

X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}=E_{i,n+j}+E_{j,n+i},1\leq i\leq j\leq n.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be the Cardan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
spanned by
H_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}}(1\leq i\leq n-1)
. With respect to
\mathfrak{h}
, there is a standard Cardan decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
be the subalgebra generated by
X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}(1\leq i<j\leq n)
and

\mathfrak{g}_{+1}
be the subalgebra generated by
X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}(1\leq i\leq j\leq n)
. Let
\mathfrak{n}^{-}=\mathfrak{n}_{0}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
,
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+1}
.
So
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}=\mathfrak{n}_{0}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{0}
, and it is isomorphic to the special linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(n)
. Denote by
\mathfrak{b}_{0}=\mathfrak{n}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
,
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
.
Denote by
\Phi
the root system. Let
\Phi^{+}(\Phi^{-})
be the positive(negative) root system. Let
\Phi_{0}
consist of even roots.
Then
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is the root space decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Denote by
\rho_{0}
the half sum of the roots of
\mathfrak{n}_{0}
. Denote by
W
the Weyl group of
\Phi_{0}
. Let
s_{\alpha}\in W
be the reflection correspond to
\alpha\in\Phi_{0}
. For
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, the dot action
s_{\alpha}\cdot\lambda=s_{\alpha}(\lambda+\rho_{0})-\rho_{0}
.
The following proposition is a consequence of the PBW theorem for
U(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Suppose
x_{1},...,x_{s}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
,
y_{1},...,y_{t}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
,
z_{1},...,z_{m}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{+1}
. Then

\{y_{1}^{b_{1}}...y_{t}^{b_{t}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}...x_{s}^{a_{s}}z_{1}^{c_{1}}...z_{m}^{c_{m}}|0\leq a_{i}\leq p-1,b_{j},c_{k}=0,1\text{ for all }i,j,k\}.

is a basis of
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g})
. In particular, dim
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g})=p^{\text{dim}\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}2^{\text{dim}\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
.

Let
U(\mathfrak{g})_{d}
be spanned by above basis elements of
U(\mathfrak{g})
with

d=\sum\limits_{k}c_{k}-\sum\limits_{j}b_{j}.

Then

U(\mathfrak{g})=\bigoplus\limits_{d=k}^{j}U(\mathfrak{g})_{d}.

In this way,
U(\mathfrak{g})
becomes a
\mathbb{Z}
-grade algebra.
Denote by
d(x)
the degree of the homogeneous element
x\in U(\mathfrak{g})
.

Since
\mathfrak{h}
is commutative, for any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, there is a one-dimensional
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{h})
-module
\mathbb{K}_{\lambda}
. For any
\chi
, define

\Lambda_{\chi}=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}|\lambda(h)^{p}-\lambda(h^{[p]})=\chi^{p}(h),\forall h\in\mathfrak{h}\}.

Obviously, there are
p^{n-1}
elements in
\Lambda_{\chi}
.

For
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}^{*}
, if
\gamma\in Aut_{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})
(the group of automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
as a restricted Lie algebra), then

U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g})\cong U_{\gamma\cdot\chi}(\mathfrak{g})
. Note that
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\cong\mathfrak{sl}(n)
, and there is a nature surjective homomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{gl}(n)^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}^{*}
. So we only need to consider the
\text{GL}(n)
-orbit of
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{*}
.
There is a
\text{GL}(n)
-invariant non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathfrak{gl}(n)
induced by trace, so
\mathfrak{gl}(n)\cong\mathfrak{gl}(n)^{*}
. For each
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}^{*}
, there exists
g\in\text{GL}(n)
with
g\cdot\chi
corresponds to a Jordan matrix. Then we can assume
\chi(\mathfrak{n}_{0}^{+})=0
. The
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{h})
-module
\mathbb{K}_{\lambda}
naturally becomes the
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{b}_{0})
-module with trivial
\mathfrak{n}_{0}
action. Define the induced
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})
-module：

Z_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)=\text{Ind}_{\mathfrak{b}_{0}}^{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}(\mathbb{K}_{\lambda}).

By
[
5
, 10.2]
,
Z_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
has a unique maximal submodule. Denote by
L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
the simple quotient of
Z_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}_{+1}
trivially acts on the simple
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})
-module
L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
. Then
L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
becomes a
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+1})
-module. Define Kac module：

K_{\chi}(\lambda)=\text{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+1}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)).

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\chi
correspond to a Jordan matrix. Then
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
has a unique maximal submodule,
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
. Denote by
L_{\chi}(\lambda)
the simple quotient of
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
. Each simple
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g})
-module is isomorphic to one
L_{\chi}(\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
.

Proof.

This proof is a modification of the proof given in
[
4
, 3.9]
. As
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1})
-module,
K_{\chi}(\lambda)\cong U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1})\otimes_{U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})}L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
.
According to Frobenius reciprocity,

\text{Hom}_{U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1})}(K_{\chi}(\lambda),L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda))\cong\text{Hom}_{U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})}(L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda),L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)).

Since dim
\text{Hom}_{U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})}(L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda),L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda))=1
, dim
\text{Hom}_{U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1})}(K_{\chi}(\lambda),L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda))
=1. So
\phi\in\text{Hom}_{U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1})}(K_{\chi}(\lambda),L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda))
satisfies
\phi(xv)=ax\cdot v
for some
a\in\mathbb{K}
. Here
x\in U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}),v\in L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
. So as
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1})
-module,
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
has a unique maximal submodule
N=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{-1})L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
.

Suppose
M
and
M^{\prime}
are two different maximal
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g})
-submodules of
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
. Then
M
and
M^{\prime}
are proper
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1})
-submodules of
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
. So
K_{\chi}(\lambda)=M+M^{\prime}\subset N
, a contradiction. Therefore,
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
has a unique maximal
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g})
-submodule.
∎

For
\lambda=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\epsilon_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, set

\delta(\lambda)=\prod\limits_{i<j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+j-i-1)\in\mathbb{K}.

Suppose
\delta(\lambda)\neq 0
. Then
\lambda
is called a typical weight. Otherwise, it is called an atypical weight. Then we have the following proposition:

Proposition 1.4
.

(Refer to
[
16
, Theorem 5.5]
)
Suppose
\delta(\lambda)\neq 0
. Then
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
is a irreducible
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g})
-module.

We call
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}^{*}
is regular semisimple if there exists
g\in\text{GL}(n)
such that
g\cdot\chi(\mathfrak{n}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{0})=0
and

g\cdot\chi(H_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}})\neq 0
for
1\leq i<j\leq n
.
We call
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}^{*}
is regular nilpotent if there exists
g\in\text{GL}(n)
such that
g\cdot\chi(\mathfrak{h}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{0})=0
and

g\cdot\chi(X_{\alpha})\neq 0
for all simple roots
\alpha
.

Proposition 1.5
.

(Refer to
[
9
, Porpositon 2.7]
) Suppose
\chi
is regular semisimple. Then
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
is irreducible.

Note that the Weyl group
W
is isomorphic to the
n
-symmetric group
S_{n}
. For
w\in W
, let
w=(i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{n})
if
w(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{i}\epsilon_{i})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i_{k}}\epsilon_{i}
,
1\leq k,i_{k}\leq n
.
Then a direct computation shows

\delta(w\cdot\lambda)=\prod\limits_{k<s}(x_{i_{k}}-x_{i_{s}}+i_{s}-i_{k}-1).

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
\chi
be regular nilpotent and
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_{\chi}
. Suppose there exists
w\in W
such that
\delta(w\cdot\lambda)\neq 0
. Then
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
is a irreducible
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g})
-module.

Proof.

By
[
7
, C.3]
,
L^{0}_{\chi}(\lambda)\cong L^{0}_{\chi}(w\cdot\mu)
. So
K_{\chi}(\lambda)\cong K_{\chi}(w\cdot\lambda)
. By Proposition
1.4
,
K_{\chi}(w\cdot\lambda)
is irreducible since
\delta(w\cdot\lambda)\neq 0
. Thus,
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
is also irreducible.
∎

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
\chi
be regular nilpotent and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
. Suppose
p\geq n+1
. Then
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
is a irreducible
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module.

Proof.

By Proposition
1.6
, we only need to prove that there exists
w\in W
such that
\delta(w\cdot\lambda)\neq 0
, or equivalently, there is no
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
satisfies the system of equations
\delta(w\cdot\lambda)=0
for all
w\in W
. Let
\lambda=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\epsilon_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Then
x_{i}-x_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

We will prove it by induction on
n
. Firstly, it is easy to verify that the statement is true when
n=3
. There is a claim:
The system of equations
\delta(w\cdot\lambda)=0
for all
w\in W
is equivalent to the system of equations

\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n,i\neq k}(x_{i}-x_{k}+k-i-1)=0\text{ for }1\leq k\leq n.

The proof of the claim:
Let

\bar{S_{k}}=\left\{(i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{k-1},k,i_{k+1},...,i_{n})\mid\begin{array}[]{cc}(i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{k-1},i_{k+1},...,i_{n})\text{ is a }\\
\text{ permutation of }(1,2,...,k-1,k+1,...,n)\\
\end{array}\right\},

L_{k}=\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n,i\neq k}(x_{i}-x_{k}+k-i-1).

Note that
L_{k}
is the common monomial of
\delta(w\cdot\lambda),w\in\bar{S_{k}}
. By induction there is no
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
satisfies the system of equations
\delta(w\cdot\lambda)/L_{k}=0
for all
w\in\bar{S_{k}}
. So
L_{k}
must be zero.

Clearly, the system of equations
L_{k}=0
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
is equivalent to

\begin{cases}(x_{i_{1}}-x_{n}+n-i_{1}-1)=0\\
(x_{i_{2}}-x_{i_{1}}+i_{1}-i_{2}-1)(x_{i_{2}}-x_{n}+n-i_{2}-1)=0\\
(x_{i_{3}}-x_{i_{2}}+i_{2}-i_{3}-1)(x_{i_{3}}-x_{i_{1}}+i_{1}-i_{3}-1)(x_{i_{3}}-x_{n}+n-i_{3}-1)=0\\
\quad\vdots\\
(x_{i_{n-1}}-x_{i_{n-2}}+i_{n-2}-i_{n-1}-1)\cdots(x_{i_{n-1}}-x_{n}+n-i_{n-1}-1)=0\end{cases}

for
i_{1},...,i_{n-1}
being all the possible permutations of
1,...,n-1
.
It shows that
x_{i}-x_{n}-i\in\{-1,...,-(n-1)\},1\leq i\leq n-1
.
It contradicts the fact that
L_{n}=0
for
p\geq n+1
.
Thus, there must exists
w\in W
such that
\delta(w\cdot\lambda)\neq 0
for any
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
. So in such conditions,
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
is irreducible.
∎

Remark 1.8
.

Note that
p\geq n+1
is a sufficient condition in the above proposition. And there is another proof given in
[
9
]
.

2.
The representations of
\mathfrak{p}(3)

In the following part of the article, let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}(3)
and
p>3
. We will investigate the character formula of the irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module
L_{\chi}(\lambda)
,
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
. Write
\lambda=(r,s)
if
\lambda(H_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}})=r,\lambda(H_{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}})=s
. There are
p^{2}
possibilities for
\lambda
. We only need to consider the
\text{GL}(3)
-orbit of
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{*}
. The representatives of orbits are given below (refer to
[
13
]
):

(1)

\chi_{1}
is regular semisimple, i.e.
\chi_{1}(H_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}})\chi_{1}(H_{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}})\chi_{1}(H_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{3}})\neq 0
,
\chi_{1}(\mathfrak{n}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{0}^{-})=0
.

(2)

\chi_{2}
is subregular semisimple, i.e.
\chi_{2}(H_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}})=0
,
\chi_{2}(H_{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}})\chi_{2}(H_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{3}})\neq 0
,
\chi_{2}(\mathfrak{n}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{0}^{-})=0
.

(3)

\chi_{3}=0
.

(4)

\chi_{4}(H_{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}})\neq 0
,
\chi_{4}(X_{-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}})=1
,
\chi_{4}(\mathfrak{n}_{0})=\chi_{4}(H_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}})=\chi_{4}(X_{-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{3}})=\chi_{4}(X_{-\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}})=0
.

(5)

\chi_{5}
is subregular nilpotent, i.e.
\chi_{5}(H_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}})=\chi_{5}(H_{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}})=0
,
\chi_{5}(X_{-\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}})=1
,
\chi_{5}(\mathfrak{n}_{0})=0=\chi_{5}(X_{-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}})=\chi_{5}(X_{-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{3}})=0
.

(6)

\chi_{6}
is regular nilpotent, i.e.
\chi_{6}(X_{-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}})=\chi_{6}(X_{-\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}})=1
,
\chi_{6}(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{0})=0
.

For convenience, we fix the notations of the orbits given above.
Let
\mathfrak{u}^{-}=\mathfrak{g}_{-\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{3}}
and
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}}\cong\mathfrak{gl}(2)
.
The corresponding irreducible
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})
-modules are given below:

(1)

Let
\chi=\chi_{1}
. Then
L^{0}_{\chi}(\lambda)\cong Z_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
.

(2)

Let
\chi=\chi_{2}
. Then
L^{0}_{\chi}(\lambda)\cong U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{u}^{-})\otimes V_{\chi}
, where

V_{\chi}
is the irreducible
U_{0}(\mathfrak{l})
-module with highest weight
\lambda
. The irreducible
\mathfrak{l}\cong\mathfrak{gl}(2)
-modules is showed in
[
5
]
.

(3)

Let
\chi=\chi_{3}
. Denote by
L(\lambda)
the irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{C})
-module with the maximal weight
\lambda
. Denote by
\bar{L}(\lambda)(\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi})
the irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{K})
-module corresponding to
L(\lambda)
constructed in
[
2
]
. Then
[
2
, Theorem 4]
shows:

(3.1)

Suppose
r+s\leq p-2
. Then

L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)\cong\bar{L}(\lambda)
.

(3.2)

Suppose
\lambda=(p-1,0)
or
\lambda=(0,p-1)
. Then

L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)\cong\bar{L}(\lambda)
.

(3.3)

Suppose
r+s=p-1(s,r\geq 1)
.
Then
L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)\cong\bar{L}(\lambda)/\bar{L}(\lambda-2\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2})
.

(4)

Let
\chi=\chi_{4}
. Then
L^{0}_{\chi}(\lambda)\cong U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{u}^{-})\otimes V_{\chi}
, where

V_{\chi}
is the irreducible
U_{\nu}(\mathfrak{l})
-module with highest weight
\lambda
,
\nu\in\mathfrak{gl}(2)^{*}
is regular nilpotent.
Note that
L^{0}_{\chi}(\lambda)\cong L^{0}_{\chi}(s_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}\cdot\lambda).

(5)

Let
\chi=\chi_{5}
. Then
L^{0}_{\chi}(\lambda)\cong U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{u}^{-})\otimes V_{\chi}
, where

V_{\chi}
is the irreducible
U_{0}(\mathfrak{l})
-module with highest weight
\lambda
(cf.
[
6
]
).

(6)

Let
\chi=\chi_{6}
. Then
L^{0}_{\chi}(\lambda)\cong Z_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A vector
v\in K_{\chi}(\lambda)
is called a maximal vector of weight
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
if
h\cdot v=\mu(h)v
for any

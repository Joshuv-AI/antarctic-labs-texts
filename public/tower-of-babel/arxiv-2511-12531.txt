Complete and cocomplete Lie algebras with injective and projective properties
Vu A. Le, Hoa Q. Duong, Tuan A. Nguyen

Abstract
Motivated by the classical correspondence between short exact sequences and splitting properties in module theory, this paper investigates projective and injective analogues in the category of Lie algebras. We first establish that no Lie algebra can serve as a projective or injective object with respect to arbitrary extensions, thus clarifying the natural limitation of this analogy. To recover meaningful dual behaviors, we introduce two new notions: cocentral extensions and cocomplete Lie algebras , viewed as the natural dual counterparts of central extensions and complete Lie algebras. We prove that solvable complete Lie algebras exhibit an injective-like property, while cocomplete Lie algebras 𝒞 \mathcal{C} satisfy the cohomological condition H 2 ​ ( 𝒞 , 𝕂 ) = 0 H^{2}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{K})=0 , acting as projective-like objects. Moreover, we obtain a full classification of almost abelian cocomplete Lie algebras. These results establish a “duality framework” for completeness and cocompleteness in Lie algebra extensions, linking structural, categorical, and cohomological aspects.

Although the class of cocomplete Lie algebras appears to be quite interesting, their classification seems to be a nontrivial problem. A simple observation shows that low-dimensional Lie algebras are often almost abelian, and this motivates us to investigate the class of Lie algebras that are not only almost abelian but also cocomplete. Historically, almost abelian Lie algebras were introduced by Kolman
[
12

Proposition 2.8

(Levi’s theorem)
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
be a surjective homomorphism of a Lie algebra
\mathcal{B}
onto a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathcal{C}

Then there exists a Lie algebra homomorphism

such that
\pi\circ q=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

By Levi’s theorem, if
\mathcal{C}
is a semisimple Lie algebra then every extension (
LE
) always splits semi-trivially. However, if we replace “split semi-trivially” by “split trivially”, Levi’s theorem is no longer valid. That is the first result of the paper which is stated in Theorem
1
. Here, we give the proof of this theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We will prove each assertion of the theorem.

1.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}
is a nontrivial Lie algebra. By Ado’s theorem
[
16
, Chapter V, §8, p.153]
, there exists a faithful representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)\equiv\mathrm{Der}(V)
of
\mathcal{C}
in a certain vector space
V
. Choose
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq V
as an abelian Lie algebra. Since
\mathcal{C}\neq\{0\}
, the representation
\rho
is nontrivial, i.e., there exist
a_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
and
c_{0}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\rho(c_{0})(a_{0})\neq 0
. Then,
\rho
defines a
\mathcal{C}
-module structure on
\mathcal{A}
as follows

\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A},\;(c,a)\mapsto c\cdot a\coloneqq\rho(c)(a).

This structure is not trivial since
c_{0}\cdot a_{0}\neq 0
. Now we define the Lie algebra
\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}\oplus_{\rho}\mathcal{C}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{B}=A\dotplus\mathcal{C}
with Lie bracket

[(a_{1},c_{1}),(a_{2},c_{2})]_{\mathcal{B}}\coloneqq(c_{1}\cdot a_{2}-c_{2}\cdot a_{1},[c_{1},c_{2}]_{\mathcal{C}})

for
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
and
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
. Then we obtain an extension of the form (
LE
) with
\iota\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
and
\pi\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
as follows

\begin{array}[]{l l l}\iota(a)\coloneqq(a,0)&\text{and}&\pi(a,c)\coloneqq c.\end{array}

Since
[(0,c_{0}),(a_{0},0)]=(c_{0}\cdot a_{0},0)\neq 0
, we have
[\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}]\neq\{0\}
which implies

3.2
Complete Lie algebras

For a Lie algebra
\mathcal{A}
, we denote by

the space of its inner derivations. Hence,
\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{A})\setminus\mathrm{ad}(\mathcal{A})
is precisely the set of all outer derivations of

Definition 3.4

(Complete Lie algebras
[
10
, Chapter I]
)
.

A Lie algebra
\mathcal{A}
is called

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})=0
and
\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{A})=\mathrm{ad}(\mathcal{A})

Remark 3.5
.

The class of complete Lie algebras has the following obvious properties.

1.

The trivial Lie algebra is complete.

2.

Every nonzero complete Lie algebra is non-abelian.

3.

All semisimple Lie algebras are always complete.

4.

All nonzero nilpotent Lie algebras are not complete.

3.3
Characteristics of complete Lie algebras – The proof of Theorem

In this subsection, we will prove the second main result of the paper – Theorem

on the characterization of complete Lie algebras associated with the trivial splitting of cocentral extensions. First, we have the following statement about the stability of the class of complete Lie algebras with respect to the direct sums.

Proposition 3.6

(The closedness under finite direct sums)
.

The direct sum of two, and hence of a finite family of complete Lie algebras, is also complete.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{2}
be the Lie algebra direct sum of two complete Lie algebras
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. Obviously,
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A}_{1})\oplus\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A}_{2})=\{0\}
. Furthermore, if

and

and

. Therefore, we have

D(x)=\bigl(\mathrm{ad}_{a_{1}}(x_{1}),\mathrm{ad}_{a_{2}}(x_{2})\bigr)=\mathrm{ad}_{a}(x),\quad\text{for all }x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{2}=\mathcal{A}.

In other words,

, i.e.,
\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{A})=\mathrm{ad}(\mathcal{A})

\mathcal{A}
is complete. By finite induction, we obtain the conclusion of Proposition
3.6
.

Metriplectic bracket for guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau theory
A. J. Brizard, H. Sugama

Abstract
The metriplectic formulation of collisional guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau theory is presented. The guiding-center Landau collision operator, which describes collisions involving test-particle and field-particle guiding-center orbits, is represented in terms of a symmetric dissipative bracket involving functional derivatives of the guiding-center Vlasov phase-space density F gc F_{\rm gc} and the electromagnetic fields ( 𝐃 gc , 𝐁 ) ({\bf D}_{\rm gc},{\bf B}) , where the guiding-center displacement vector 𝐃 gc ≡ 𝐄 + 4 ​ π ​ 𝗣 gc {\bf D}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\bf E}+4\pi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc} is expressed in terms of the electric field 𝐄 {\bf E} and the guiding-center polarization 𝗣 gc \mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc} . This dissipative Landau bracket conserves guiding-center energy-momentum and angular momentum, as well as satisfying a guiding-center H-theorem.



I
Introduction

Fluid and kinetic models are important tools used to study the complex dynamics of magnetized laboratory and astrophysical plasmas. A crucial element of these models is the clear separation of dissipationless and dissipative dynamics, which are represented by explicit evolution operators with important energy-momentum conservation laws.

A generic field-theoretical model found in plasma physics
Morrison_2017
describes the time evolution of a set of field variables
\psi^{i}
(
i=1,2,...,N)
:

\frac{\partial\psi^{i}}{\partial t}\;=\;[\psi^{i},\;{\mathcal{H}}]\;+\;(\psi^{i},\;{\mathcal{S}}),

(1)

which is divided into a
dissipationless
time evolution generated by a Hamiltonian functional
{\mathcal{H}}[\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}]
and an antisymmetric bracket
Morrison_1982

\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{G}}\right]\;=\;\int\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\psi^{i}}\;\widehat{J}^{ij}[\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}]\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta\psi^{j}}\;=\;-\;\left[{\mathcal{G}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{F}}\right],

(2)

and a
dissipative
time evolution generated by an entropy functional
{\mathcal{S}}[\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}]
and a symmetric bracket
Kaufman_1984
;
Morrison:1984ca
;
Morrison:1986vw

\left({\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{G}}\right)\;=\;\int\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\psi^{i}}\;\widehat{Q}^{ij}[\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}]\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta\psi^{j}}\;=\;\left({\mathcal{G}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{F}}\right).

(3)

Here, the brackets (
2
)-(
3
) are defined in terms of arbitrary functionals
{\mathcal{F}}[\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}]
and
{\mathcal{G}}[\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}]
, and the integration domain is either physical space (for fluid models) and/or phase space (for kinetic models). In addition, summation over repeated indices is assumed, and the matrix elements
\widehat{J}^{ij}[\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}]
and
\widehat{Q}^{ij}[\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}]
typically involve partial derivatives of the functional derivatives
\delta(...)/\partial\psi^{i}
, which are defined through the Fréchet derivative

\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}{\mathcal{F}}[\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}+\epsilon\,\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}]\right|_{\epsilon=0}\;\equiv\;\int\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\psi^{i}}\;\delta\psi^{i}.

We note that the entropy functional
{\mathcal{S}}
is a Casimir functional of the Hamiltonian bracket (i.e.,
[{\mathcal{S}},\;{\mathcal{G}}]=0
for any arbitrary functional
{\mathcal{G}}
), while the Hamiltonian functional
{\mathcal{H}}
is a dissipative invariant (i.e.,
({\mathcal{H}},\;{\mathcal{G}})=0

{\mathcal{G}}

\{{\mathcal{H}};[\,,\,]\}

\{{\mathcal{S}};(\,,\,)\}
forms what is referred to as the
metriplectic

Kaufman_1984
;
Morrison:1984ca
;
Morrison:1986vw

In the present work, we explore the metriplectic structure for the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau equations, in which the collisional effects are introduced in the guiding-center Vlasov equation through the guiding-center Landau collision operator.

II
Metriplectic bracket for Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau Theory

The kinetic paradigm for the time evolution of a magnetized plasma is represented by the Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau theory, in which the Vlasov-Maxwell equations possess a Hamiltonian-bracket structure and the Landau collision operator can be represented in terms of a dissipative bracket and the Gibbs entropy functional.

II.1
Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau theory

The Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau equations are expressed in terms of the collisional Vlasov-Landau equation

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\;=\;-\;\frac{d{\bf x}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla f-\frac{d{\bf p}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf p}+\sum^{\prime}\;{\mathcal{C}}[f;f^{\prime}],

(4)

which describes the time evolution of the distribution
f({\bf z},t)
of test charged particles (with mass
m
and charge
q
), defined in terms of the noncanonical phase-space coordinates
{\bf z}=({\bf x},{\bf p})
, with Jacobian
{\mathcal{J}}=1
. The two-particle Landau collision operator
{\mathcal{C}}[f;f^{\prime}]
in Eq. (
4
) is expressed in Landau and Fokker-Planck forms, respectively, as
Helander_Sigmar

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}[f;f^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[\int_{{\bf z}^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}({\bf z},{\bf z}^{\prime})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(f\;\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial{\bf p}^{\prime}}\;-\;f^{\prime}\;\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf p}\right)\right]

(5)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(f\;{\bf K}[f^{\prime}]\;-\;\mathbb{D}[f^{\prime}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf p}\right),

where

denotes the distribution of field particles (with mass
m^{\prime}
and charge
q^{\prime}
), and summation over field-particle species is denoted as
\sum^{\prime}
. Here, the symmetric two-particle Landau tensor

\mathbb{Q}({\bf z},{\bf z}^{\prime})\;\equiv\;Q_{0}\;\delta^{3}({\bf x}^{\prime}-{\bf x})\;\left(\frac{\mathbb{I}}{|{\bf w}|}\;-\;\frac{{\bf w}\,{\bf w}}{|{\bf w}|^{3}}\right)

(6)

is defined in terms of the relative velocity

{\bf w}\;\equiv\;\frac{d{\bf x}}{dt}\;-\;\frac{d{\bf x}^{\prime}}{dt}\;=\;{\bf v}\;-\;{\bf v}^{\prime},

(7)

the identity matrix
\mathbb{I}

Q_{0}=2\pi\,(qq^{\prime})^{2}\,\ln\Lambda
, and the presence of the delta function

{\bf w}

6

The collisional Vlasov-Landau equations (

\displaystyle c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}\;-\;4\pi\;\sum\,q\,\int_{\bf p}\frac{d{\bf x}}{dt}\;f,

\displaystyle-\;c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf E},

(9)

{\bf B}

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\sum q\int_{\bf p}f,

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(11)

(12)

where

\left\{f,\frac{}{}\{g,h\}\right\}\;+\;\left\{g,\frac{}{}\{h,f\}\right\}\;+\;\left\{h,\frac{}{}\{f,g\}\right\}\;=\;0,

(14)

II.2
Metriplectic structure of Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau theory

We will now unify the Vlasov-Landau collisional kinetic equation (
4
)-(
5
) and the Maxwell equations (
8
)-(
9
) within a metriplectic bracket structure that combines dissipationless Hamiltonian evolution with Landau collisional evolution
Kaufman_1984
;
Morrison:1984ca
;
Morrison:1984wu
;
Morrison:1986vw
;
Kraus_Hirvijoki_2017
. The evolution of an arbitrary Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau functional
{\mathcal{F}}[f,{\bf E},{\bf B}]
is expressed in metriplectic form as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\int_{\bf z}\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f}\;+\;\int_{\bf x}\left(\frac{\partial\bf E}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf E}+\frac{\partial\bf B}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta\bf B}\right)

(16)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{H}}\right]\;+\;\left({\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{S}}\right),

where the Hamiltonian functional
{\mathcal{H}}
, and its associated antisymmetric Poisson bracket
[\;,\;]
, describe the collisionless evolution of
{\mathcal{F}}
, while the entropy functional
{\mathcal{S}}
, and its associated symmetric dissipative bracket
(\;,\;)
, describe the collisional evolution of
{\mathcal{F}}
.

First, the Vlasov-Maxwell Poisson bracket between two arbitrary functionals
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
is expressed as
Morrison_1980
;
Morrison_1982
;
Marsden_Weinstein_1982

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{G}}\right]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum\int_{\bf z}\;f\left\{\frac{\delta{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta f},\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{G}}}{\delta f}\right\}\;+\;4\pi\int_{\bf x}\frac{\delta{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta{\mathcal{G}}}{\delta{\bf B}}-\sum\int_{\bf p}q\;f\left\{{\bf x},\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{G}}}{\delta f}\right\}\right)

(17)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 4\pi\int_{\bf x}\frac{\delta{\mathcal{G}}}{\delta{\bf E}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta{\bf B}}-\sum\int_{\bf p}q\;f\left\{{\bf x},\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta f}\right\}\right),

and the Hamiltonian functional is

{\mathcal{H}}[f,{\bf E},{\bf B}]\;\equiv\;\sum\int_{\bf z}K\;f\;+\;\int_{\bf x}\frac{1}{8\pi}\left(|{\bf E}|^{2}\;+\;|{\bf B}|^{2}\right).

(18)

The functional bracket (
17
) satisfies the standard Poisson-bracket properties: (i) antisymmetry
[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]=-\,[{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{F}}]
, (ii) Leibniz
[{\mathcal{F}}\,{\mathcal{G}},\;{\mathcal{K}}]=[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{K}}]\,{\mathcal{G}}+{\mathcal{F}}\,[{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]
, and (iii) Jacobi

\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}[{\mathcal{G}},\;{\mathcal{K}}]\right]+\left[{\mathcal{G}},\frac{}{}[{\mathcal{K}},\;{\mathcal{F}}]\right]+\left[{\mathcal{K}},\frac{}{}[{\mathcal{F}},\;{\mathcal{G}}]\right]=0,

(19)

which hold for any three functionals
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}})
. We note that the functional Jacobi property (
19
) is inherited from the Jacobi property (
14
) of the single-particle Poisson bracket (
13
).

Next, the dissipative bracket in Eq. (
16
) is expressed in the Landau form as
Kaufman_1984
;
Kraus_Hirvijoki_2017

\left({\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{G}}\right)\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{2}\int_{z,z^{\prime}}\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{F}}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{Q}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{G}}],

(20)

where the notation

\int_{z,z^{\prime}}(\cdots)\equiv\sum\int_{\bf z}f({\bf z})\;\sum^{\prime}\int_{\bf z^{\prime}}f^{\prime}({\bf z^{\prime}})(\cdots)

involves a double particle phase-space integration over the test-particle coordinate
{\bf z}
and the field-particle coordinate

{\bf z}^{\prime}
, and the antisymmetric vector-valued function

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{F}}]({\bf z};{\bf z^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bf p}}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f}\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bf p^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f^{\prime}}\right)\;=\;\left\{{\bf x},\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f}\right\}-\left\{{\bf x^{\prime}},\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f^{\prime}}\right\}^{\prime}=-\;\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{F}}]({\bf z^{\prime}};{\bf z})

(21)

involves functional derivatives with respect to the test-particle and field-particle distributions
f
and
f^{\prime}
.

We now note that the Gibbs entropy functional (the Boltzmann constant is omitted for simplicity)

{\mathcal{S}}\;\equiv\;-\;\sum\int_{\bf z}f({\bf z})\;\ln f({\bf z})

(22)

is a Casimir functional of the Hamiltonian bracket (
17
), i.e., the bracket expression

\displaystyle[{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{G}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\int_{\bf z}f\left\{\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}}{\delta f},\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta f}\right\}+4\pi\sum q\int_{\bf z}\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,f\,\left\{{\bf x},\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}}{\delta f}\right\}

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\sum\int_{\bf z}\left\{f,\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta f}\right\}-4\pi\sum q\int_{\bf z}\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf p}\;=\;0

vanishes for all functionals
{\mathcal{G}}
, since each integral vanishes separately since they involve exact divergences. Next, we calculate

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{S}}]({\bf z};{\bf z^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bf p}}\left(-\ln f-1\right)\;-\;\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bf p^{\prime}}}\left(-\ln f^{\prime}-1\right)\;=\;-\,\frac{\partial\ln f}{\partial{\bf p}}\;+\;\frac{\partial\ln f^{\prime}}{\partial{\bf p^{\prime}}},

(24)

so that the dissipative Landau bracket (
20
) yields the collision operator
{\mathcal{C}}[f;f^{\prime}]
:

\displaystyle\left({\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{S}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{z,z^{\prime}}\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{F}}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{Q}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{S}}]

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\int_{\bf z}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\sum^{\prime}\int_{{\bf z}^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(-\,f^{\prime}\;\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf p}\;+\;f\;\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial{\bf p}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum\int_{\bf z}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\sum^{\prime}\left(-\;\mathbb{D}[f^{\prime}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf p}\;+\;{\bf K}[f^{\prime}]\;f\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum\int_{\bf z}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\sum^{\prime}f\;{\bf S}[f^{\prime}]\;=\;\sum\int_{\bf z}\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta f}\;\sum^{\prime}{\mathcal{C}}[f;f^{\prime}].

Here, the collision operator is given in Fokker-Planck form as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}[f;f^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(f\frac{}{}{\bf S}[f^{\prime}]\right)\;\equiv\;-\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left({\bf K}[f^{\prime}]\;f\;-\;\mathbb{D}[f^{\prime}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf p}\right),

(26)

where the Fokker-Planck friction vector and the diffusion tensor are defined, respectively, as

\displaystyle{\bf K}[f^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf z}^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}({\bf z},{\bf z}^{\prime})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial{\bf p}^{\prime}},

(27)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}[f^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf z}^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}({\bf z},{\bf z}^{\prime})\;f^{\prime},

(28)

are Landau functionals of the field-particle distribution
f^{\prime}
. Here, by introducing an arbitrary field point
{\bf x}_{0}
in physical space, we note the symmetry property

\int_{\bf z}f({\bf z})\,\delta^{3}({\bf x}-{\bf x}_{0})\;{\bf S}[f^{\prime}]({\bf z})\;\equiv\;-\;\int_{{\bf z}^{\prime}}f^{\prime}({\bf z}^{\prime})\,\delta^{3}({\bf x}^{\prime}-{\bf x}_{0})\;{\bf S}[f]({\bf z}^{\prime}),

(29)

which follows from the identity

\displaystyle\int_{\bf z}f({\bf z})\;\delta^{3}({\bf x}-{\bf x}_{0})\,{\bf K}[f^{\prime}]({\bf z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bf z}f({\bf z})\;\delta^{3}({\bf x}-{\bf x}_{0})\left(\int_{{\bf z}^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}({\bf z},{\bf z}^{\prime})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f^{\prime}({\bf z}^{\prime})}{\partial{\bf p}^{\prime}}\right)

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf z}^{\prime}}\delta^{3}({\bf x}^{\prime}-{\bf x}_{0})\left(\int_{\bf z}f({\bf z})\frac{}{}\mathbb{Q}({\bf z},{\bf z}^{\prime})\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f^{\prime}({\bf z}^{\prime})}{\partial{\bf p}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf z}^{\prime}}\delta^{3}({\bf x}^{\prime}-{\bf x}_{0})\;\mathbb{D}[f]({\bf z}^{\prime})\;\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f^{\prime}({\bf z}^{\prime})}{\partial{\bf p}^{\prime}},

where we used the symmetry and locality properties of the two-particle Landau tensor (
6
). Because of the symmetry property (
29
), we conclude that the local collisional force

{\bf F}^{\sf C}({\bf x}_{0})\;\equiv\;\int_{\bf z}{\bf p}\,\delta^{3}({\bf x}-{\bf x}_{0})\,\sum^{\prime}{\mathcal{C}}[f;f^{\prime}]({\bf z})\;=\;\int_{\bf z}f({\bf z})\,\delta^{3}({\bf x}-{\bf x}_{0})\;\sum^{\prime}{\bf S}[f^{\prime}]({\bf z})

(31)

satisfies the momentum-conservation property
\sum{\bf F}^{\sf C}({\bf x}_{0})=0
, which follows from the locality of the two-particle Landau tensor (
6
).

The conservation properties of the dissipative Landau bracket (
20
) easily follow from its form and the properties of the Landau kernel (
6
). First, the particle-number functional

{\mathcal{N}}=\int_{\bf z}f
is trivially conserved since
\delta{\mathcal{N}}/\delta f=1
is a constant. Next, the conservation of the momentum functional

\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}\;=\;\sum\int_{\bf z}{\bf p}\,f\;+\;\int_{\bf x}\left({\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}\right)/4\pi c

(32)

is also trivial since
\Gamma^{i}[{\mathcal{P}}^{j}]=\delta^{ij}-\delta^{ij}=0
, while the conservation of the energy (Hamiltonian) functional

{\mathcal{H}}\;=\;\sum\int_{\bf z}\frac{|{\bf p}|^{2}}{2m}\,f\;+\;\int_{\bf x}\left(|{\bf E}|^{2}+|{\bf B}|^{2}\right)/8\pi

(33)

follows from the definition

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{H}}]({\bf z};{\bf z}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{{\bf x},\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta f}\right\}\;-\;\left\{{\bf x^{\prime}},\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta f^{\prime}}\right\}^{\prime}\;=\;\frac{d{\bf x}}{dt}\;-\;\frac{d{\bf x}^{\prime}}{dt}\;\equiv\;{\bf v}\;-\;{\bf v}^{\prime}\;=\;{\bf w},

(34)

and the fact that
{\bf w}
is a null-eigenvector of
\mathbb{Q}
:
{\bf w}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{Q}=0
. Lastly, the dissipative Landau bracket (
20
) satisfies an H-theorem:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\mathcal{S}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{S}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{z,z^{\prime}}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{S}}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{Q}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{S}}]=\frac{Q_{0}}{2}\int_{z,z^{\prime}}\frac{\delta^{3}({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime})}{|{\bf w}|^{3}}\;\left|\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{S}}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{}{}{\bf w}\right|^{2}\;\geq\;0,

(35)

where thermal equilibrium
({\mathcal{S}},\;{\mathcal{S}})=0
is defined by the condition
\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{S}}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf w}=0
, i.e., when
f
and
f^{\prime}
are equal-temperature local Maxwellian distributions:
\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}[{\mathcal{S}}]={\bf w}/T
(the temperature

is given here in energy units).

III
Hamiltonian Guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell Theory

We now briefly review the Hamiltonian formulation of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations, which was recently presented in our previous work
Brizard_gcVM_2024
in terms of an antisymmetric Hamiltonian bracket
[\;,\;]_{\rm gc}
and a Hamiltonian functional
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}
. In the next sections, we will consider the guiding-center Fokker-Planck collision operator (Sec.
IV
) derived by the guiding-center transformation of the Fokker-Planck collision operator and the dissipative-bracket formulation of a guiding-center Landau collision operator (Sec.
V
) that describes collisions involving particle orbits represented in guiding-center phase space.

The variational formulation of guiding-center Lagrangian single-particle dynamics
Cary_Brizard_2009
is based on a preliminary transformation
({\bf x},{\bf p})\rightarrow({\bf x},p_{\|0}={\bf p}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}},\mu_{0}=|{\bf p}_{\bot}|^{2}/(2mB),\zeta_{0})
to local particle phase-space coordinates, followed by a near-identity transformation to guiding-center phase-space coordinates
({\bf x},p_{\|0},\mu_{0},\zeta_{0})\rightarrow({\bf X},p_{\|},\mu,\zeta)
, where
{\bf X}
denotes the guiding-center position,
p_{\|}
denotes the guiding-center parallel momentum, and
(\mu,\zeta)
denotes the fast gyromotion canonical pair. This phase-space transformation induces Lie-transform guiding-center pull-back and push-forward operators
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}
and
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}
that are involved in the transformation of the Vlasov particle distribution
f
to the guiding-center Vlasov distribution
F\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}f
, while the guiding-center Poisson bracket is defined as
\{F,\;G\}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}(\{{\sf T}_{\rm gc}F,\;{\sf T}_{\rm gc}G\})
, which is guaranteed to satisfy the standard Poisson-bracket properties. In addition, the guiding-center time evolution operator
d_{\rm gc}/dt
is defined in terms of the identity

{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}\left(\frac{df}{dt}\right)=\left({\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}\frac{d}{dt}{\sf T}_{\rm gc}\right)F\equiv\frac{d_{\rm gc}F}{dt},

(36)

which holds for an arbitrary function
f\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}F
and its guiding-center push-forward
F\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}f
.

We note that the guiding-center operators
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}
and
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}
are expressed in terms of expansions in powers of the mass-to-charge ratio
m/q
Kulsrud_1983
. In the present work, we will use the charge normalization
q\rightarrow q/\epsilon
in order to explicitly display the guiding-center ordering parameter
\epsilon
whenever it appears in an expression derived by guiding-center transformation. We note that, as an ordering parameter, it is not expected to be small
per se
and final guiding-center expressions may be truncated at any arbitrary order before setting it back to
\epsilon=1
.

It is possible to construct a self-consistent system of guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations, which describes the evolution of the guiding-center Vlasov distribution
F
coupled with the Maxwell evolution of the electric and magnetic fields
({\bf D}_{\rm gc},{\bf B})
. The variational (Lagrangian) formulations of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations were presented by Pfirsch and Morrison
Pfirsch_Morrison_1985

{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm gc}[F;F^{\prime}]\equiv\left\langle{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}{\mathcal{C}}\left[{\sf T}_{\rm gc}\frac{}{}F;{\sf T}^{\prime}_{\rm gc}\frac{}{}F^{\prime}\right]\right\rangle=-\,\frac{1}{B_{\|}^{*}}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z^{\alpha}}\left[B_{\|}^{*}\left(K_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}[F^{\prime}]\,F-D_{\rm gc}^{\alpha\beta}[F^{\prime}]\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z^{\beta}}\right)\right].

(79)

Here, the test-particle and field-particle guiding-center distributions
F\equiv\langle{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}f\rangle
and
F^{\prime}\equiv\langle{\sf T^{\prime}}_{\rm gc}^{-1}f^{\prime}\rangle^{\prime}
are defined as the gyroangle-averaged guiding-center push-forward transformations of the test-particle and field-particle distributions
f
and
f^{\prime}
, while the gyroangle-dependent parts
\widetilde{F}\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}f-\langle{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}f\rangle
and
\widetilde{F}^{\prime}\equiv{\sf T^{\prime}}_{\rm gc}^{-1}f^{\prime}-\langle{\sf T^{\prime}}_{\rm gc}^{-1}f^{\prime}\rangle^{\prime}
are associated with higher-order (nonlinear) collisional effects that are usually neglected

;
Sugama:2015
.

By making use of the properties of the push-forward Lie-transform operator
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}
:
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}(f\,g)\equiv({\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}f)\,({\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}g)
, we find the following explicit expression for the two-particle guiding-center Landau tensor
\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}
:

\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\equiv Q_{0}\,\delta^{3}({\bf x}_{\rm gc}^{\prime}-{\bf x}_{\rm gc})\;\left(\frac{\mathbb{I}}{|{\bf W}_{\rm gc}|}-\frac{{\bf W}_{\rm gc}\,{\bf W}_{\rm gc}}{|{\bf W}_{\rm gc}|^{3}}\right),

(80)

where the guiding-center push-forward of the relative particle velocity
{\bf W}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{\prime-1}{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}{\bf w}
is expressed as

\displaystyle{\bf W}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{d_{\rm gc}{\bf x}_{\rm gc}}{dt}\;-\;\frac{d^{\prime}_{\rm gc}{\bf x}_{\rm gc}^{\prime}}{dt}\;=\;{\bf W}\;+\;\epsilon\left(\frac{d_{\rm gc}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}}{dt}\;-\;\frac{d^{\prime}_{\rm gc}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}^{\prime}}{dt}\right),

(81)

where
{\bf W}\equiv d_{\rm gc}{\bf X}/dt-d^{\prime}_{\rm gc}{\bf X}^{\prime}/dt
represents the relative guiding-center velocity, which is explicitly independent of the particle gyromotion, and the remaining finite Larmor-radius (FLR) terms include the relative polarization-drift velocity

d_{\rm gc}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm E}/dt-d_{\rm gc}^{\prime}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm E}^{\prime}/dt
and the relative gyroangle-dependent perpendicular velocity
d_{\rm gc}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}/dt-d^{\prime}_{\rm gc}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}^{\prime}/dt
.

IV.1
Guiding-center phase-space projection

The guiding-center Fokker-Planck friction and diffusion coefficients in Eq. (
79
) are expressed in Landau form as

\displaystyle K_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}[F^{\prime}]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bf K}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\right\rangle\;=\;\int_{\bf Z^{\prime}}B_{\|}^{\prime*}\left\langle\langle\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\rangle\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\prime\beta}\right\rangle^{\prime}\frac{\partial F^{\prime}}{\partial Z^{\prime\beta}},

(82)

\displaystyle D_{\rm gc}^{\alpha\beta}[F^{\prime}]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{D}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\beta}\right\rangle\;=\;\int_{\bf Z^{\prime}}B_{\|}^{\prime*}\left\langle\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\beta}\rangle\right\rangle^{\prime}F^{\prime},

(83)

where the guiding-center Fokker-Planck coefficients are
{\bf K}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}{\bf K}[{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{\prime}F^{\prime}]
and
\mathbb{D}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}\mathbb{D}[{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{\prime}F^{\prime}]
, while

\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{\prime-1}{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}\mathbb{Q}
denotes the guiding-center two-particle Landau tensor. In addition, the guiding-center Vlasov distribution
F^{\prime}\equiv\langle{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{\prime-1}f^{\prime}\rangle^{\prime}
is defined as the gyroangle-averaged guiding-center push-forward transformation of the Vlasov field-particle distribution
f^{\prime}
. Here, the guiding-center projection vectors in guiding-center test-particle phase space

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\;\equiv\;\left\{{\bf x}_{\rm gc},\frac{}{}Z^{\alpha}\right\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\frac{\partial Z^{\alpha}}{\partial p_{\|}}+\frac{\epsilon\,c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla Z^{\alpha}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mathbb{I}\;+\frac{}{}\epsilon\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}\right)

(84)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{\Omega}{B}\left(\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\;\frac{\partial Z^{\alpha}}{\partial\mu}\;-\;\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\mu}\;\frac{\partial Z^{\alpha}}{\partial\zeta}\right)

is expressed in terms of the guiding-center Poisson bracket (
51
) and the guiding-center push-forward of the particle position
{\bf x}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}{\bf x}={\bf X}+\epsilon\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}
. The guiding-center phase-space projection vectors (
83
) extend the three-dimensional momentum-space Fokker-Planck collision operator into a five-dimensional guiding-center phase-space Fokker-Planck collision operator (
79
).

If we ignore the FLR correction
\epsilon\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}
when compared to the unit matrix
\mathbb{I}
in Eq. (
84
), the projection-vector components are

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\bf X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon\,c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mathbb{I},

(85)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{p_{\|}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;=\;\frac{{\bf B}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;+\;\epsilon\;\frac{p_{\|}c}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}},

(86)

\displaystyle\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\mu},\frac{}{}\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\zeta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega}{B}\,\left(\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta},\;-\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right).

(87)

These projection-vector components satisfy the phase-space divergence property

\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z^{\alpha}}\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial Z^{\alpha}}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\frac{}{}F_{\rm gc}\right),

(88)

which follows from the divergence property (
52
) of the guiding-center Poisson bracket. We note that, while the gyromotion time scale has been eliminated from the guiding-center collision operator (
79
), the guiding-center transformation has explicitly introduced spatial guiding-center friction and diffusion Fokker-Planck coefficients since
\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\bf X}\neq 0
.

IV.2
Guiding-center magnetic-moment projection

Collisional effects associated with the guiding-center magnetic moment
\mu
involve the guiding-center projection vector
\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\mu}=\epsilon\,\{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0},\;\mu\}_{\rm gc}=(\Omega/B)\,\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}/\partial\zeta
, which is explicitly gyroangle-dependent. Hence, collisional transport in
\mu
-space involves finite-gyroradius dynamics on the fast gyromotion time scale, which necessitates gyroangle averaging in Eqs. (
79
)-(
83
).

While the magnetic moment is an adiabatic invariant of the guiding-center dynamics, it is not an invariant of the exact particle dynamics. Hence, the classical diffusion process
Hazeltine_Meiss
associated with the guiding-center projection vector
\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\mu}
is different from the nonadiabatic diffusion process associated with the breaking of the adiabatic invariance of the magnetic moment
Bernstein_Rowlands_1976
, which occurs when a particle goes through an extremum point along a nonuniform magnetic-field line (where
|p_{\|}|
reaches a maximum value) and induces a random jump in the magnetic moment. When the guiding-center magnetic moment is conserved (excluding small perturbations at extremal points along the guiding-center orbit), this nonadiabatic diffusion process is negligible.

V
Dissipative guiding-center Landau Bracket

The metriplectic structure
Hirvijoki_2022
for the time evolution of any guiding-center functional
{\mathcal{F}}[F_{\rm gc},{\bf D}_{\rm gc},{\bf B}]
can be determined by the differential equation

\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial t}\;=\;[{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{H}_{\rm gc}]_{\rm gc}\;+\;({\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{S}_{\rm gc})_{\rm gc},

(89)

where the symmetric guiding-center dissipative bracket
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\rm gc}
must satisfy the following conservation properties
({\mathcal{E}}_{\rm gc}^{a},{\mathcal{G}})_{\rm gc}=0
, where
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm gc}^{a}=({\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc},{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{i},{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gc}\varphi})
are Hamiltonian invariants and
{\mathcal{G}}
is an arbitrary functional (hence, these invariants may be viewed as dissipative Casimir invariants), and an H-theorem
(\mathcal{S}_{\rm gc},\mathcal{S}_{\rm gc})_{\rm gc}\geq 0
.

V.1
Guiding-center Landau collision operator

Following our previous work
Hirvijoki_2022
, we define the guiding-center two-particle vector field

\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc}[\mathcal{A}]({\bf Z};{\bf Z}^{\prime})\equiv\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc}[\mathcal{A}]({\bf Z})-\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc}[\mathcal{A}]({\bf Z}^{\prime}),

(90)

where the test-particle contribution is

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc}[\mathcal{A}]({\bf Z})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial J}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{A}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}({\bf Z})}\right)\;+\;\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{A}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}({\bf Z})}\right)

(91)

\displaystyle+\;\left[4\pi q\left(\frac{\delta{\mathcal{A}}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gc}({\bf X})}+\epsilon\,\frac{p_{\|}c}{qB}\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta{\mathcal{A}}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gc}({\bf X})}\right)-\epsilon\,\nabla\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{A}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}({\bf Z})}\right)\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}},

which yields the particle guiding-center velocity

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}]({\bf Z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\;+\;\frac{p_{\|}}{m}\,\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;+\;\left(q\,{\bf E}^{\star}\;-\frac{}{}\epsilon\,\nabla K_{\rm gc}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}

(92)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\;+\;\frac{d_{\rm gc}{\bf X}}{dt}\;\equiv\;{\bf v}_{\rm gc},

where only the lowest order contribution
d_{\rm gc}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}/dt=\Omega\,\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}/\partial\zeta+\cdots
is retained, which is consistent with the truncation
\mathbb{I}+\epsilon\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
used in Eq. (
84

\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}]({\bf Z};{\bf Z}^{\prime})\;=\;{\bf v}_{\rm gc}-{\bf v}_{\rm gc}^{\prime}\equiv{\bf w}_{\rm gc},

(93)

and the symmetric guiding-center two-particle Landau tensor (
80
) is henceforth approximated as

\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;Q_{0}\,\delta^{3}({\bf X}^{\prime}-{\bf X})\;\left(\frac{\mathbb{I}}{|{\bf w}_{\rm gc}|}-\frac{{\bf w}_{\rm gc}{\bf w}_{\rm gc}}{|{\bf w}_{\rm gc}|^{3}}\right),

(94)

which is now evaluated locally in guiding-center space (i.e., FLR corrections are ignored in the spatial delta function). The guiding-center dissipative Landau bracket is defined as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{Z,Z^{\prime}}\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc}[\mathcal{A}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc}[\mathcal{B}]

(95)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{0}}{2}\int_{Z,Z^{\prime}}\frac{\delta^{3}({\bf X}^{\prime}-{\bf X})}{|{\bf w}_{\rm gc}|^{3}}\;{\bf w}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc}[\mathcal{A}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf w}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc}[\mathcal{B}],

where we use the notation

\int_{Z,Z^{\prime}}(\cdots)\;=\;\sum\int_{{\bf Z}}F_{\rm gc}({\bf Z})\,\left\langle\sum^{\prime}\int_{{\bf Z^{\prime}}}F_{\rm gc}^{\prime}({\bf Z^{\prime}})\frac{}{}\langle\cdots\rangle^{\prime}\right\rangle.

We note that the energy conservation property
({\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc},{\mathcal{B}})_{\rm gc}=0
of the guiding-center dissipative Landau bracket (
95
) is automatic (for all
{\mathcal{B}}
), since the two-particle vector (
93
) is a null-vector of the guiding-center Landau tensor (
94

{\bf w}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;0
.

Using Eq. (
70

({\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{i},{\mathcal{G}})_{\rm gc}=0
follows from the expression

\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{i}]({\bf Z};{\bf Z^{\prime}})\;=\;\widehat{\imath}\;-\;\widehat{\imath}\;\equiv\;0,

(96)

where we find

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{i}]({\bf Z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;b^{i}\;+\;\left[\frac{q}{c}\,{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\imath}\;+\;\epsilon\,\frac{p_{\|}}{B}\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left({\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\imath}\right)\;-\;\epsilon\,p_{\|}\;\nabla b^{i}\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}

(97)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\imath}\;\frac{B}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;+\;\epsilon\,\frac{p_{\|}c}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\left[\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla b^{i}\;+\;(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}})\,b^{i}\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,({\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\imath})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{B}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\imath}\;\frac{B}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;+\;\epsilon\,\frac{p_{\|}c}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\left[\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla b^{i}\;+\;\left(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,b^{i}\;+\;\left(\nabla b^{i}\;-\;\widehat{\imath}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}})\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{\imath}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\left(B\;+\;\epsilon\,\frac{p_{\|}c}{q}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\;=\;\widehat{\imath}.

Using the same identity, Eq. (
71

\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gy}\varphi}]({\bf Z};{\bf Z}^{\prime})\;=\;\frac{\partial{\bf X}}{\partial\varphi}-\frac{\partial{\bf X}^{\prime}}{\partial\varphi^{\prime}}\;=\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left({\bf X}\;-\frac{}{}{\bf X}^{\prime}\right),

(98)

and the conservation of guiding-center toroidal canonical angular momentum

follows from the fact that
\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}
contains the spatial delta function

\delta^{3}({\bf X}^{\prime}-{\bf X})
. Since the three functionals
({\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc},{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{i},{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gc}\varphi})
are conserved by the guiding-center dissipative Landau bracket (
95

{\mathcal{G}}
, they are called guiding-center collisional Casimir invariants. We note that the interior guiding-center Casimir functional
{\mathcal{M}}_{\rm gc}
associated with the adiabatic invariance of the magnetic moment
\mu
yields the test-particle contribution

\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{M}}_{\rm gc}]\;=\;\frac{\Omega}{B}\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\;\equiv\;\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\mu}({\bf X},\mu,\zeta),

(99)

which implies that, in our work,
{\mathcal{M}}_{\rm gc}
is not a guiding-center collisional Casimir invariant and, therefore, our work describes classical transport processes associated with
\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\mu}\neq 0
as well as classical spatial diffusion associated with
\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\bf X}
(see App.
A
). In their recent work, Sato and Morrison

;
Sato_Morrison_2025
use an approximation of weak collisionality in which the collisional mean-free-path is much larger than the magnetic-gradient length scale (which is itself much larger than the gyroradii of all particles). Based on this neoclassical approximation

, both test and field particles have enough time to complete their respective guiding-center orbits (for both trapped and circulating particles) before they experience a collision at a common guiding-center position. These full guiding-center orbits, however, assume that their respective magnetic moments are conserved, which allows Sato and Morrison
Sato_Morrison_2024

Sato_Morrison_2025
to assume that
{\mathcal{M}}_{\rm gc}
should be considered a guiding-center collisional Casimir invariant under this weak-collisionality approximation. This approximation is implicitly consistent with removing the gyromotion component from the guiding-center Poisson bracket (
51
), so that this reduced guiding-center (rgc) Poisson bracket in reduced guiding-center space
({\bf X},p_{\|};\mu)
yields
\{\mu,\;g\}_{\rm rgc}\equiv 0
, and only the projection components
(\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\bf X},\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{p_{\|}})
remain in the reduced guiding-center Fokker-Planck collision operator, without compromising the guiding-center collisional invariants (which now includes the guiding-center magnetic-moment functional
{\mathcal{M}}_{\rm gc}
). We, therefore, note that our work includes the work of Sato and Morrison
Sato_Morrison_2024
;
Sato_Morrison_2025
(regarding its guiding-center formulation) by setting
\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\mu}=0
and using the relative two-particle guiding-center velocity

in the guiding-center two-particle Landau tensor (
94
).

Next, the guiding-center dissipative Landau bracket (
95
) satisfies an H-theorem:

({\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc},{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc})_{\rm gc}\;=\;\frac{Q_{0}}{2}\int_{Z,Z^{\prime}}\delta^{3}({\bf X}^{\prime}-{\bf X})\frac{\left|{\bf w}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}]\right|^{2}}{|{\bf w}_{\rm gc}|^{3}}\geq 0,

(100)

where

\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}]\;=\;\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}]({\bf Z})\;-\;\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}]({\bf Z}^{\prime}).

(101)

Here, the test-particle guiding-center term
\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}]({\bf Z})
is expressed as

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}]({\bf Z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\,\frac{\partial\ln F}{\partial J}\;-\;\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\frac{\partial\ln F}{\partial p_{\|}}\;-\;\frac{\epsilon\,c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\ln F\;\equiv\;-\,\frac{1}{F}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z^{\alpha}}

(102)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F_{\rm gc}}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}\left(F_{\rm gc}\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial J}\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial J}\left(F_{\rm gc}\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}\left(F_{\rm gc}\;\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\right)+\epsilon\,\nabla\times\left(F_{\rm gc}\;\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\;\frac{1}{F_{\rm gc}}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial Z^{\alpha}}\left(F_{\rm gc}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\right),

where we used the identity
\partial(B_{\|}^{*}\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha})/\partial Z^{\alpha}\equiv 0
. The guiding-center thermodynamic equilibrium
({\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc},{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc})_{\rm gc}=0
, therefore, requires that
{\bf w}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc}[{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc}]=0
. If we substitute the guiding-center Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution
F_{\rm MBgc}=F_{0}({\bf X})\;\exp[-H_{\rm gc}/T({\bf X})]
into Eq. (
102

H_{\rm gc}\equiv\epsilon^{-1}q\,\Phi+K_{\rm gc}
, we find

-\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\,\frac{\partial\ln F}{\partial J}\;-\;\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\frac{\partial\ln F}{\partial p_{\|}}\;-\;\frac{\epsilon\,c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\ln F\;=\;\frac{{\bf v}_{\rm gc}}{T}\;-\;\frac{\epsilon\,c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\left(\ln F_{0}\;+\;\frac{H_{\rm gc}}{T}\;\nabla\ln T\right),

and, as expected, thermodynamic equilibrium requires equal temperatures for all particle species and a spatially uniform plasma. We note that, by including the total guiding-center magnetic-moment functional
{\mathcal{M}}_{\rm gc}
as a collisional invariant, Sato and Morrison
Sato_Morrison_2024
;
Sato_Morrison_2025
increase the class of guiding-center equilibria by allowing arbitrary functions of the guiding-center magnetic moment
\mu
, which allow for arbitrary deviations from the guiding-center Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution
F_{\rm MBgc}
.

Lastly, in what follows, we will introduce the notation

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]({\bf Z})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{Z^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{1}{F^{\prime}}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\prime\,\beta}\;\frac{\partial F^{\prime}}{\partial Z^{\prime\beta}}-\frac{1}{F}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\beta}\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z^{\beta}}\right)

(103)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\bf K}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]({\bf Z})\;-\;\mathbb{D}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]({\bf Z})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\beta}}{F}\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z^{\beta}},

which defines the guiding-center collisional flux. We note that it is the guiding-center equivalent of the collisional flux defined in Eq. (
26
) and it obeys the same symmetry property as in Eq. (
29
):

\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}({\bf Z})\;\delta^{3}({\bf X}-{\bf X}_{0})\,{\bf S}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]({\bf Z})\;\equiv\;-\;\int_{{\bf Z}^{\prime}}F^{\prime}_{\rm gc}({\bf Z}^{\prime})\;\delta^{3}({\bf X}^{\prime}-{\bf X}_{0})\,{\bf S}_{\rm gc}[F]({\bf Z}^{\prime}),

(104)

where
{\bf X}_{0}
denotes an arbitrary field point and, consistent with the local approximation used in Eq. (
94
), FLR corrections have been omitted in the spatial delta function. The symmetry property (
104
), therefore, guarantees that the local guiding-center collisional force

{\bf F}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}({\bf X}_{0})\;\equiv\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}({\bf Z})\;\delta^{3}({\bf X}-{\bf X}_{0})\,\sum^{\prime}{\bf S}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]({\bf Z})

(105)

satisfies the momentum-conservation property
\sum{\bf F}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}({\bf X}_{0})=0
.

V.2
Guiding-center Fokker-Planck collision operator

Using the guiding-center metriplectic structure presented above, the time evolution of the guiding-center functional
\Psi_{V}\equiv\int_{\bf Z}\psi\,F_{\rm gc}
, where
\psi({\bf X},p_{\|},\mu)
is an arbitrary function, is expressed in metriplectic form as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Psi_{V}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bf Z}\psi\;\frac{\partial F_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}\;=\;\int_{\bf Z}\psi\;\left[B_{\|}^{*}\,\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}-F\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(B_{\|}^{*}\frac{d_{\rm gc}{\bf X}}{dt}\right)-F\;\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}\left(B_{\|}^{*}\frac{d_{\rm gc}p_{\|}}{dt}\right)\right]

(106)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bf Z}\left[\psi\,B_{\|}^{*}\;\frac{d_{\rm gc}F}{dt}+F_{\rm gc}\left(\{\psi,\;K_{\rm gc}\}_{\rm gc}\;+\frac{}{}\epsilon^{-1}q\,{\bf E}^{\star}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},\;\psi\}_{\rm gc}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(\Psi_{V},{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc})_{\rm gc}\;+\;[\Psi_{V},\;{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}]_{\rm gc},

where the guiding-center Hamiltonian bracket is

[\Psi_{V},\;{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}]_{\rm gc}\;=\;\int_{\bf Z}F_{\rm gc}\left(\{\psi,\;K_{\rm gc}\}_{\rm gc}\;+\frac{}{}\epsilon^{-1}q\,{\bf E}^{\star}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},\;\psi\}_{\rm gc}\right),

while the guiding-center Landau collision operator

\displaystyle(\Psi_{V},{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc})_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bf Z}\left[\left(\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\;\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial J}+\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial p_{\|}}\right)-\epsilon\,\nabla\psi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\sum^{\prime}F_{\rm gc}\,{\bf S}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]

(107)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bf Z}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\;\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial Z^{\alpha}}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,$}\sum^{\prime}F_{\rm gc}\,{\bf S}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]\;\equiv\;\int_{\bf Z}B_{\|}^{*}\psi\;\sum^{\prime}{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm gc}[F;F^{\prime}]

is now expressed in Fokker-Planck form as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm gc}[F;F^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{1}{B_{\|}^{*}}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z^{\alpha}}\left(B_{\|}^{*}\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,F\,{\bf S}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]\right)

(108)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\;\frac{1}{B_{\|}^{*}}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z^{\alpha}}\left[B_{\|}^{*}\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left({\bf K}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]\,F-\mathbb{D}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\beta}\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z^{\beta}}\right)\right],

where
{\bf K}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]
and
\mathbb{D}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]
are defined in Eq. (
103
) as functionals of the field-particle guiding-center Vlasov distribution
F^{\prime}
. In Eq. (
108
), and henceforth, gyroangle-averaging is implied wherever appropriate since the test-particle guiding-center distribution
F
is gyroangle-independent.

Lastly, we note that the time evolution of an arbitrary moment of the guiding-center Vlasov distribution
F_{\rm gc}
is expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\int_{P}\chi F_{\rm gc}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[\int_{P}\chi F_{\rm gc}\left(\frac{d_{\rm gc}{\bf X}}{dt}-\frac{\epsilon\,c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\sum^{\prime}{\bf S}_{\rm gc}\right)\right]

(109)

\displaystyle+\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left(\frac{d_{\rm gc}\chi}{dt}+\sum^{\prime}{\bf S}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}_{\rm gc}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial Z^{\alpha}}\right),

where
\chi({\bf X},p_{\|},\mu)
is an arbitrary function on guiding-center phase space. Important applications of this guiding-center moment equation include the guiding-center particle number
(\chi=1)
conservation law for each charged-particle species

\frac{\partial N_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(N_{\rm gc}\frac{}{}{\bf U}_{\rm gc}\right)\;=\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,\frac{\epsilon c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\sum^{\prime}\langle{\bf S}_{\rm gc}\rangle\right)\;\simeq\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\epsilon c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf F}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}\right),

(110)

where we used the approximation
B_{\|}^{*}\simeq B
and the definition (
105
) for the local guiding-center collisional force, while gyroangle-averaging of the guiding-center collisional flux (
103
) is now shown explicitly. If we define the guiding-center collisional fluid flow

N_{\rm gc}{\bf U}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}\;\equiv\;-\,\frac{\epsilon c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf F}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C},

(111)

then the momentum-conservation property
\sum{\bf F}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}=0
of the guiding-center collisional force automatically guarantees the ambipolarity condition
Hazeltine_Meiss
on the collisional guiding-center current density:

{\bf J}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}\;\equiv\;\sum\epsilon^{-1}qN_{\rm gc}{\bf U}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}\;=\;-\;\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\sum{\bf F}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}\right)\;=\;0.

(112)

Similar moments of the guiding-center Fokker-Planck collision operator has appeared elsewhere
Sugama_2017
;
Sugama_2017_RMPP
. In App.
A
, we will present a simple model that shows that guiding-center collisional fluid flow (
111
) is diffusive in character
Hazeltine_Meiss
.

V.3
Guiding-center Ampère-Maxwell-Landau equation

Since the guiding-center two-particle vector field
\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc}[\mathcal{A}]({\bf Z};{\bf Z}^{\prime})
, defined in Eq. (
90
), involves a functional derivative of a general functional
{\mathcal{A}}
with respect to the guiding-center displacement field
{\bf D}_{\rm gc}
, then the guiding-center Ampère-Maxwell acquires a dissipative collisional current.

Indeed, consider the functional
\Psi[{\bf D}_{\rm gc}]=\int_{X}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf D}_{\rm gc}
, where
\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}({\bf X})
is an arbitrary vector function, so that

\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}\;=\;\int_{X}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf D}_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}\;=\;[\Psi,\mathcal{H}_{\rm gc}]_{\rm gc}+(\Psi,\mathcal{S}_{\rm gc})_{\rm gc},

(113)

is expressed in terms of the Hamiltonian bracket

\displaystyle[\Psi,{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}]_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi c\int_{X}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\frac{\bf B}{4\pi}\;-\;\sum\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}\right)

\displaystyle-\;4\pi\sum\int_{Z}F_{\rm gc}\;\left(q\,\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}+q\,\mathbb{P}_{\|}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{d_{\rm gc}{\bf X}}{dt}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{X}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf H}_{\rm gc}\;-\frac{}{}4\pi\,{\bf J}_{\rm gc}\right),

where the guiding-center current density
{\bf J}_{\rm gc}
defined in Eq. (
54
) and
{\bf H}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\bf B}-4\pi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf M$}_{\rm gc}
is defined in terms of the guiding-center magnetization (
60
), while the dissipative bracket is expressed as

\displaystyle(\Psi,{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gc})_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi c\sum\int_{\bf Z}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}\;+\;\epsilon\,\mathbb{P}_{\|}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\sum^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi c\sum\int_{\bf Z}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\sum^{\prime}\left[\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,F_{\rm gc}\,\langle{\bf S}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]\rangle\;-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\frac{q}{c}\,\mathbb{P}_{\|}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\epsilon c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,F_{\rm gc}\,\langle{\bf S}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]\rangle\right)\right]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\;4\pi\int_{X}\mbox{\boldmath$\alpha$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left({\bf J}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}\;+\frac{}{}c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf M$}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}\right),

where the collisional guiding-center current density
{\bf J}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}
is shown to vanish as a result of the ambipolarity condition (
112
), while there is a nonvanishing collisional contribution to guiding-center magnetization.
Hence, the guiding-center Ampère–Maxwell-Landau equation becomes

\frac{\partial{\bf D}_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}\;=\;c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf H}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}\;-\;4\pi\,{\bf J}_{\rm gc},

(114)

which includes a collisional contribution to the guiding-center magnetization:

{\bf H}_{\rm gc}^{\sf C}\equiv{\bf B}\;-\;4\pi\epsilon\,\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left[\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}\;+\;\frac{q}{c}\,\mathbb{P}_{\|}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{d_{\rm gc}{\bf X}}{dt}\;-\;\frac{\epsilon c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\sum^{\prime}\langle{\bf S}_{\rm gc}[F^{\prime}]\rangle\right)\right],

(115)

Here, we see how the metriplectic bracket formulation allows for a self-consistent combination of the dissipationless and dissipative contributions to the guiding-center current density and the guiding-center moving electric-dipole contribution to the guiding-center magnetization.

V.4
Drift-kinetic limit of the guiding-center Landau collision operator

We conclude this Section by considering the drift-kinetic limit, in which we retain only the lowest-order terms in powers of the guiding-center ordering parameter
\epsilon
. In this limit, the guiding-center velocity becomes

{\bf v}_{\rm gc0}\;=\;\Omega\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\;+\;\frac{p_{\|}}{m}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{B}\;\equiv\;{\bf v}\;+\;{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{B},

(116)

which is simply the sum of the particle velocity
{\bf v}
and the
E\times B
velocity, and the guiding-center Jacobian simply becomes
mB
. Since the latter velocity is the same for all particle species, the lowest-order relative guiding-center velocity

{\bf w}_{\rm gc0}={\bf w}
is identical to the relative velocity (
7
).

In the drift-kinetic limit, the guiding-center two-particle vector field (
90
) becomes
\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}_{\rm gc0}[{\mathcal{A}}]({\bf Z};{\bf Z}^{\prime})\equiv\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc0}[{\mathcal{A}}]({\bf Z})-\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc0}[{\mathcal{A}}]({\bf Z}^{\prime})
, where

\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc0}[{\mathcal{A}}]({\bf Z})\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial J}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{A}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right)\;+\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{A}}{\delta F_{\rm gc}}\right)\;+\;4\pi c\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{A}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gc}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{B},

(117)

so that
\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc0}[{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}]({\bf Z})={\bf v}_{\rm gc0}
and, therefore, energy is conserved. Moreover, since

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc0}[{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm gc}^{i}]({\bf Z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{{\sf b}}\,b^{i}\;+\;({\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\imath})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}/B\;=\;\widehat{\imath},

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\gamma$}_{\rm gc0}[{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gc}\varphi}]({\bf Z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{{\sf b}}\,b_{\varphi}\;+\;({\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\partial{\bf X}/\partial\varphi)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}/B\;=\;\partial{\bf X}/\partial\varphi,

then both the guiding-center momentum and angular-momentum functionals (
70
)-(
71
) are still collisional invariants. Lastly, we note that the last term in Eq. (
117
) can be removed without jeopardizing the collisional conservation laws because of the locality of the two-particle guiding-center Landau tensor, e.g.,
\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,[\widehat{{\sf b}}({\bf X})\,b^{i}({\bf X})-\widehat{{\sf b}}({\bf X}^{\prime})\,b^{i}({\bf X}^{\prime})]\equiv 0
.

VI
Summary

The present work introduced the metriplectic bracket associated with the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau equations. The guiding-center metriplectic bracket structure is composed of an antisymmetric dissipationless (Hamiltonian) bracket
[\;,\;]_{\rm gc}
, which satisfies the Jacobi property, and a symmetric dissipative bracket
(\;,\;)_{\rm gc}
that is constructed from the guiding-center Hamiltonian bracket. When the guiding-center entropy functional (which is a Casimir functional of the Hamiltonian bracket) is used in the dissipative bracket, we obtained a Landau representation of the guiding-center Fokker-Planck collisional operator, which guaranteed that the conservations of energy-momentum and angular momentum are automatically satisfied. The guiding-center metriplectic bracket also allowed the self-consistent introduction of collisional effects into the guiding-center Maxwell equation through the guiding-center magnetization and the guiding-center current density.

Appendix A
Isotropic Field-Particle Model

In this Appendix, we present a simple model that shows that the guiding-center collisional fluid flow (
111
) is diffusive in character
Hazeltine_Meiss
. For this purpose, we introduce the isotropic field-particle Maxwellian model
Brizard_2004
, and we replace the coordinates
(p_{\|},\mu)
with
(p,\theta\equiv\cos^{-1}p_{\|}/p)
, with Jacobian
p^{2}\sin\theta
, so that the test-particle Fokker-Planck coefficients become

\displaystyle{\bf K}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\nu(p)\,\left(p_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}+m\Omega\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\right)\;=\;-\nu(p)\,p\left(\cos\theta\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\frac{}{}\sin\theta\,\widehat{\bot}\right)\;\equiv\;-\,\nu(p)\,p\;\widehat{\sf p},

(118)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\bot}(p)\,\mathbb{I}\;+\;\left[D_{\|}(p)\;-\;D_{\bot}(p)\right]\;\widehat{\sf p}\widehat{\sf p},

(119)

where the coefficients
(\nu,D_{\|},D_{\bot})
are defined in terms of Rosenbluth potentials
Xu_Rosenbluth_1991
;
Helander_Sigmar
;
Hirvijoki_Brizard_Pfefferle:2017
,
\mathbb{I}
denotes the unit dyadic tensor, and
\widehat{\bot}\equiv\partial\widehat{\rho}/\partial\zeta
is an explicit function of the gyroangle
\zeta
. Hence, the guiding-center collisional flux (
103
) is expressed as

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\nu\,p\;\widehat{\sf p}\;-\;\left[D_{\bot}\,\mathbb{I}\;+\frac{}{}(D_{\|}\;-\;D_{\bot})\;\widehat{\sf p}\widehat{\sf p}\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\widehat{\sf p}\,\frac{\partial\ln F}{\partial p}+\frac{\widehat{\sf q}}{p}\;\frac{\partial\ln F}{\partial\theta}+\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\ln F\right)

(120)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\nu\,p\;\widehat{\sf p}\;-\;D_{\|}\;\frac{\partial\ln F}{\partial p}\;\widehat{\sf p}\;-\;\frac{D_{\bot}}{p}\;\frac{\partial\ln F}{\partial\theta}\;\widehat{\sf q}

\displaystyle-\;\left[D_{\bot}\,\mathbb{I}\;+\frac{}{}(D_{\|}\;-\;D_{\bot})\;\sin^{2}\theta\;\widehat{\sf\bot}\widehat{\sf\bot}\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\ln F,

where
\widehat{\sf q}\equiv\partial\widehat{\sf p}/\partial\theta=-\,\sin\theta\;\widehat{{\sf b}}+\cos\theta\;\widehat{\bot}
and we omit displaying the guiding-center ordering parameter
\epsilon

(\widehat{{\sf b}},\widehat{\bot},\widehat{\rho}\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\bot})
, we also find the spatial guiding-center collisional flux

\displaystyle\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf S}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{c}{qB}\;\left[\left(\nu\,p\;+\;D_{\|}\,\frac{\partial\ln F}{\partial p}\right)\;\sin\theta\;+\;\frac{D_{\bot}}{p}\;\cos\theta\;\frac{\partial\ln F}{\partial\theta}\right]\widehat{\rho}

(121)

\displaystyle-\;D_{\bot}\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\ln F\right)+(D_{\|}-D_{\bot})\;\frac{c^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{q^{2}B^{2}}\;\left(\widehat{\rho}\frac{}{}\widehat{\rho}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln F.

The guiding-center Fokker-Planck collision operator (
108

{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm gc}[F]\;=\;-\;\frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\left(p^{2}\;\langle S_{\rm gc}^{p}\rangle\;F\right)\;-\;\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\sin\theta\;\langle S_{\rm gc}^{\theta}\rangle\;F\right)\;-\;\frac{1}{B}\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(B\;\langle S_{\rm gc}^{\bf X}\rangle\;F\right)

(122)

is now expressed in terms of the gyroangle-averaged components

\displaystyle\langle S_{\rm gc}^{p}\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bf S}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf p}\right\rangle\;=\;-\,\nu\;p\;-\;D_{\|}\;\frac{\partial\ln F}{\partial p},

(123)

\displaystyle\langle S_{\rm gc}^{\theta}\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bf S}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\widehat{\sf q}}{p}\right\rangle\;=\;-\;\frac{D_{\bot}}{p^{2}}\;\frac{\partial\ln F}{\partial\theta},

(124)

\displaystyle\langle S_{\rm gc}^{\bf X}\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{qB}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\langle{\bf S}_{\rm gc}\rangle\;=\;\frac{1}{(m\Omega)^{2}}\left[D_{\bot}\;+\;\frac{1}{2}(D_{\|}-D_{\bot})\,\sin^{2}\theta\right]\mathbb{I}_{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln F,

(125)

\langle\widehat{\rho}\widehat{\rho}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{I}_{\bot}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\,(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}})

122

\nu)
and diffusion
(D_{\|})

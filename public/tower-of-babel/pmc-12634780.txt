Predicting the timing of LC after PTGBD in elderly patients with acute cholecystitis: a machine learning approach with a web-based calculator
Authors: Wei Zhang, Baitong Che, Yingmei Li, Cheng Yang, Weibin Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTranscutaneous transhepatic gallbladder drainage (PTGBD) has shown significant efficacy in the treatment of elderly patients with acute cholecystitis. The goal of this study is to develop a machine learning-based web calculator aimed at predicting the optimal timing for cholecystectomy (LC) after PTGBD in elderly patients with acute cholecystitis (AC) to achieve precise personalized medicine.MethodsA retrospective analysis of 979 elderly patients with acute cholecystitis admitted to Jinzhou Central Hospital and the First Affiliated Hospital of Jinzhou Medical University from 2013 to 2024 was performed, and a total of 680 patients were included in the model development. Patients were divided into delayed (347 cases, surgery > 6 weeks post-PTGBD) and non-delayed (333 cases) groups based on the interval between PTGBD and LC. Minimal Absolute Contraction and Selection Operator (LASSO) and logistic analysis were used to determine the predictors of postponement of LC in elderly patients with AC after PTGBD. Next, we used eight ML algorithms, namely Logistic Regression (LR), Decision Tree (DT), Random Forest (RF), Extreme gradient boosting (XGB), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), K-nearest Neighbor (KNN), Gaussian Naive Bayes (GNB), to train and develop ML models using a 10x cross-validation method. The performance of the model was evaluated by a variety of indicators, including the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC), calibration curve, decision curve, PR curve, and confusion matrix. In addition, model interpretation is performed through Shapley Additive Interpretation (SHAP) analysis to clarify the importance of each feature of the model and its basis for decision-making. Finally, we chose to use the best model to develop a web-based calculator that could be used to predict the likelihood of delaying LC after PTGBD in elderly AC patients.ResultsIn multivariate logistic regression analysis, age, sex, gallbladder wall thickness, time between onset and PTGBD, white blood cell count (WBC), C-reactive protein (CRP), and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) were identified as independent predictors of delayed LC in elderly patients with AC after PTGBD. In the training set, the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) values for these models ranged from 0.808 to 0.914, with the random forest (RF) model showing the highest AUC value. Through the evaluation of decision curve analysis (DCA), precision-recall (PR) curve and calibration curve, the RF model showed superior clinical decision support and prediction performance compared with the other seven models. Finally, we used the RF model to build an online network calculator, which aims to accurately assist doctors in making more informed and accurate clinical decisions and promote the wide application of the model in clinical practice (https://zw17786325639.shinyapps.io/Postpone/).ConclusionsThis study developed and validated an RF model network calculator based on clinical indicator information to assess the likelihood of delaying LC after PTGBD in elderly patients with acute cholecystitis. This tool is expected to assist physicians in making more appropriate clinical decisions for patients.Keywords: Acute cholecystitis, Delayed cholecystectomy, Laparoscopic cholecystectomy, PTGBD

Introduction

Acute cholecystitis (AC) is a particularly common type of general surgical acute abdomen, and its causes are mostly stones blocking the cystic duct, cholestasis, bacterial infection [1]. Previous studies have shown that age is one of the most closely related factors for the development of acute cholecystitis [2]. Based on the World Health Organization’s ageing demographics, we adopted the standard of 60 >years as the elderly [3]. With the rapid development of laparoscopic techniques, laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC) has replaced traditional open cholecystectomy as the standard preferred procedure for the treatment of gallbladder diseases; however, due to the decline of physical function in this age group, their immune response is relatively sluggish. In older patients, acute cholecystitis tends to present with more complex and atypical symptoms, which leads to a higher rate of complications and a consequent increase in mortality. The 2018 Tokyo Guidelines (TG18) have clarified the guidelines for the treatment of AC and the severity grading of the disease, as well as the choice of drainage mode and surgical considerations for acute cholecystitis [4–6]. AC is graded according to its severity as mild (grade I), moderate (grade II), or severe (grade III), and different treatment regimens are recommended for each grade. Moderate (grade II) acute cholecystitis is defined as a type of acute cholecystitis whose degree of acute inflammation may be associated with increased surgical difficulty for cholecystectomy. In this case, PTGBD may be a better option as a bridging treatment to surgery because it helps to reduce local edema of the gallbladder and improve systemic symptoms, thus creating more favorable conditions for subsequent surgery [7–9]. To be clear, laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC) for acute cholecystitis is still the standard treatment except for patients who are not suitable for surgery. PTGBD was first reported in the early 1980 s and has been recommended as the treatment of choice for gallbladder drainage based on multiple studies. PTGBD is relatively simple to operate and can quickly alleviate the clinical symptoms of AC [9, 10].

However, there is no consensus on when LC should be performed after PTGBD. On one hand, delayed LC significantly improves perioperative safety and long-term outcomes in elderly patients with acute cholecystitis by mitigating local inflammatory responses and optimizing surgical conditions. Studies demonstrate that delayed LC, typically performed 4–8 weeks after PTGBD, reduces infectious complications [11–13]. Spira et al. found a 58% reduction in infectious complications and a decline in conversion rates from 25% to 14% in critically ill elderly patients undergoing delayed LC after PTGBD compared to early surgery [14]. Intraoperative bleeding risk is also minimized with delayed LC [15]. On the other hand, delaying LC for too long may have negative effects. Prolonged delay increases the risk of intraoperative bleeding due to the formation of adhesions and the development of new blood vessels in inflamed areas. Therefore, choosing a suitable delay time for LC is very important. Some studies have compared delayed laparoscopic cholecystectomy at 6 weeks after PTGBD with emergency laparoscopic cholecystectomy and found that delayed surgery resulted in better clinical outcomes [12, 13, 16]. In addition, other studies compared patients who underwent cholecystectomy within 7 weeks of PTGBD with surgery 13 weeks later and found that the former was associated with a reduced risk of composite endpoints [17]. Based on the existing literature and local clinical experience, patients were selected to undergo laparoscopic cholecystectomy >6 weeks after PTGBD as the delayed surgery group.

In recent years, machine learning (ML) algorithms have attracted a lot of attention because of their ability to predict the occurrence and outcome of events, and have been widely used in healthcare data analysis and clinical decision support [18]. However, explanatory and practical applications are two key challenges for ML models, and the development of online network calculators can effectively address these issues. The online web calculator, as a prediction tool based on complex algorithms and massive amounts of data, can provide in-depth analysis of individual patients’ specific conditions and provide scientific recommendations on the timing of surgery. In this study, we developed and validated an RF model network calculator based on clinical indicator information using clinical features to predict the probability of delayed LC surgery after PTGBD in elderly AC patients. In addition, we visualized the machine learning algorithm with the strongest prediction power through a web calculator. The results of this study will be of great significance for optimizing surgical planning and improving clinical management.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient selection

A retrospective analysis of 979 elderly patients with acute cholecystitis admitted to Jinzhou Central Hospital and the First Affiliated Hospital of Jinzhou Medical University from 2013 to 2024 was performed, and a total of 680 patients were included in the study according to the case inclusion and exclusion criteria, and all patients received ultrasound-guided PTGBD within 1–2 days of admission. Patients were selected to undergo laparoscopic cholecystectomy > 6 weeks after PTGBD as the delayed group. Patients were divided into 2 groups, including the delayed group (347 cases) and the non-delayed group (333 cases) based on the interval between PTGBD and LC. All procedures involving human participants in this study are consistent with the Declaration of Helsinki (revised 2013). This study was conducted by the Ethics Committee of Jinzhou Central Hospital (Ethics Number: JZZXYYLL2024-LW-052). Figure 1 shows the overall design of the study.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

This study strictly screened patients according to inclusion and exclusion criteria, and was designed to ensure the completeness and reliability of the information on the included cases, and to exclude cases with missing or abnormal data to ensure the quality of the data to the greatest extent, so as to provide a reliable database for predicting the likelihood of delaying LC after PTGBD in elderly AC patients. The specific inclusion and exclusion criteria used for patients in this study are as follows.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Age > 60 years old, gender is not limited; (2) Patients with PTGBD combined with LC admitted to Jinzhou Central Hospital and the First Affiliated Hospital of Jinzhou Medical University from 2013 to 2024; (3) The patient was diagnosed with AC according to the TG18 criteria at the time of admission, and the pathological examination results after laparoscopic cholecystectomy were confirmed to be AC; (4) The two surgeries were performed by the same surgical team from Jinzhou Central Hospital and the Department of General Surgery and Intervention of the First Affiliated Hospital of Jinzhou Medical University; (5) The clinical data and related information of the patient’s two admissions are complete and available.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) Age < 60 years; (2) The pathological results showed malignancy; (3) Patients who have not undergone LC due to complex conditions or other serious diseases; (4) Patients who need to be transferred to laparotomy, or the operation time is ≥ 180 min, or the amount of bleeding is ≥ 300 ml, or accompanied by other organ damage, refractory bleeding, or serious complications such as bile duct injury and bile leakage after surgery, or even death; (5) Those with missing clinical data and incomplete data related to this study.

The exclusion of cases with major complications, conversions to open surgery, prolonged operative times (≥ 180 min), significant bleeding (≥ 300 ml), and other serious post-operative issues such as organ damage, refractory bleeding, bile duct injury, bile leakage, or death was a carefully considered decision. First, from the perspective of patient factors, patients who experience serious complications, require conversion to open surgery, or have prolonged surgical time often have multiple underlying diseases or special anatomical structures. These complex patient factors are intertwined, making each case unique and difficult to accurately predict using a unified model. Second, from the perspective of surgical operation, switching to open surgery or extending the surgical time usually means encountering unexpected situations or difficulties during the surgical process. The extension of surgical time may also be related to the surgeon’s technical level, experience, and ability to respond to unexpected situations. The uncertainty and complexity of these surgical procedures result in surgical outcomes being influenced by various accidental factors. In addition, the occurrence of serious complications is often the result of multiple factors working together, and their mechanism of occurrence is complex and difficult to quantify accurately. These complex factors make it difficult to establish an accurate mathematical model to describe the relationship between complications and various preoperative factors. The exclusion of these cases is to ensure that the model can be trained and validated in a relatively stable and controllable data environment, thereby improving the stability and accuracy of the model and enabling it to more reliably predict the possibility of delayed LC in patients with elderly AC after PTGBD. Although this exclusion strategy to some extent limits the external validity of the research, it is a crucial step in ensuring the quality of the model.

Data collection and model construction

Data from Jinzhou Central Hospital (476 cases) served as the training set, while data from the First Affiliated Hospital of Jinzhou Medical University (204 cases) were used as the external validation set. The three institutions involved in this study were tertiary hospitals qualified for biliary surgery. The indications for PTGBD uniformly followed the TG18 standards, namely, “moderate/severe cholecystitis unable to tolerate emergency surgery, severe underlying diseases (such as heart failure, liver failure), and high risk of gallbladder necrosis or perforation.” Through LASSO analysis, stepwise regression for univariate and multivariate logistic regression, screening predictive variables. We selected eight widely used classifier algorithms, including Logistic Regression (LR), Decision Tree (DT), Random Forest (RF), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGB), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN), and Gaussian Naive Bayes (GNB). These machine learning models are all trained with R software. The training data set uses a 10-fold cross-validation, while the validation data set is used directly for model validation.

Performance evaluation

The performance evaluation indicators of the model included the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy. To test the clinical applicability of the model, we used calibration curves, decision curves, PR curves, and confusion matrices, and interpreted the models by Shapley Additive Interpretation (SHAP) analysis. Based on the model with optimal performance, we further developed a web-based online calculator.

LASSO and logistic regression for model development feature selection

LASSO analysis is a method suitable for dimensionality reduction and feature selection of high-dimensional data, which makes the relatively unimportant feature coefficient zero through regularization technology [19]. Among all clinical features, 7 risk factors were summarized by backward stepwise regression with univariate and multivariate logistics regression, and the results showed: Age (OR = 0.10, 95% CI = 0.06–0.14, p < 0.001), WBC (OR = 0.09, 95% CI = 0.04–0.14, p < 0.001), NLR (OR = 0.01, 95% CI = 0.001–0.017, p = 0.013), CRP (OR = 0.02, 95% CI = 0.006–0.028, p = 0.002), Interval between onset and PTGBD hours (OR = 0.01, 95% CI = 0.003–0.011, p < 0.001), Gender (OR = 0.96, 95% CI = 0.13–0.53, p < 0.001) and Gallbladder wall thickness (OR = 0.74, 95% CI = 0.04–1.46, p = 0.03) (Fig. 2; Table 2). They were identified as independent factors associated with delayed LC in patients with cholecystitis after PTGBD. Therefore, the machine learning model was developed based on the seven independent predictors mentioned above.

Performance of developed models

In the training set, the RF model showed excellent prediction performance, with an AUC of 0.914, 95%CI (0.89–0.939), indicating high prediction accuracy. In contrast, the AUC values for the remaining seven models were as follows: LR 0.808, 95%CI (0.768–0.847), DT 0.875, 95%CI (0.8434–0.907), XGB 0.877, 95%CI (0.846–0.907), SVM 0.893, 95%CI (0.865–0.922), MLP 0.859, 95% CI (0.826–0.892), KNN 0.816, 95%CI (0.778–0.854), BNB 0.893, 95%CI (0.865–0.921) (Fig. 3A). In the validation set, the AUC values of LR, DT, RF, XGB, SVM, MLP, KNN, and BNB are 0.814(95% CI (0.755–0.872)), 0.806 (95% CI (0.745–0.868)), 0.861 (95% CI (0.808–0.913)), 0.861 (95% CI (0.808–0.915)), 0.833 (95% CI (0.774–0.893)), 0.831 (95% CI (0.775–0.888)), 0.801 (95% CI (0.74–0.862)), and 0.85 (95% CI (0.793–0.906)) (Fig. 3B). In the DCA, PR and calibration curves of the training set and the validation set, the RF model has good clinical decision-making ability and actual prediction ability compared with the other 7 models (Fig. 3C-H). We evaluated the performance of each machine learning algorithm using 10x cross-validation, and Fig. 4 shows the results of 10x cross-validation, with the average AUC value of the evaluated performance in each model calculated as follows: LR: Average AUC = 0.792, DT: Average AUC = 0.736, RF: Average AUC = 0.841, XGB: Average AUC = 0.839, SVM : Average AUC = 0.831, MLP: Average AUC = 0.822, KNN: Average AUC = 0.797, BNB: Average AUC = 0.837. In order to improve the clinical utility of the model, the SHAP method was used to determine which features would help the model predict post-PTGBD for deferred LC. If the value is positive, the feature will have a positive effect on the output; If the value is negative, the feature will negatively affect the output. Orange indicates high values, and purple colors indicate low values. The darker the color, the greater the influence of the representation feature on the target feature. Discussion

LC has been widely recognized as the preferred treatment for acute cholecystitis due to its advantages of being minimally invasive, rapid recovery, and fewer complications [20]. In general, the best time to perform LC is within 48 to 72 h of symptom onset, when the success rate of surgery is higher and recovery is better. However, for some high-risk patients, such as elderly patients, patients with serious underlying diseases or organ dysfunction, the risk of emergency LC surgery is greater, which not only increases the difficulty of LC, but also increases the probability of postoperative complications, especially the poor prognosis of patients with bile duct injury (BDI) and vascular biliary injury (VBI) [21, 22]. Therefore, such patients need to receive PTGBD as a bridging treatment to rapidly reduce the pressure in the gallbladder cavity, reduce the absorption of cytokines and inflammatory mediators, alleviate clinical symptoms, and then choose the appropriate timing of surgery according to the specific situation. In one clinical study, patients treated with PTGBD sequential LC were treated with PTGBD with approximately 30% fewer complications than those who underwent direct LC [23]. As a temporary mitigation measure, there is no consensus on the best time for elective LC after PTGBD. Two highly recognized authoritative studies recommended LC at 4–6 weeks or 6–8 weeks after PTGBD, respectively [24], so this study explored this issue within a 6-week boundary [24]. Although the timing of LC after PTGBD is determined by the clinician on a case-by-case basis, the results of LC after PTGBD are objective. In order to ensure the high homogeneity of intraoperative and postoperative LC outcomes of the study subjects and avoid bias in results caused by case differences, this study strictly followed the inclusion and exclusion criteria to screen cases. Through accurate reverse analysis of clinical data from two groups of patients, it aims to identify the key factors affecting the timing of surgery and provide clinicians with personalized advice to optimize treatment effects, reduce patient pain and risks, and ultimately improve patients’ quality of life.

Among the 8 models, we found that the RF model (mean AUC = 0.914) had good prediction performance in the training set and was able to predict the likelihood of delayed LC after PTCBD in elderly patients with acute cholecystitis. Different from some nomogram models, we further provide a dynamic structure, build an RF model-based network calculator to predict the visual individual probability of delayed LC after PTCBD in patients with cholecystitis, and improve the applicability of the model. Our analysis found gallbladder wall thickness, Interval between onset and PTGBD hours, WBC, CRP, Age, Gender, and NLR as independent predictors of delayed LC after PTGBD in elderly patients with acute cholecystitis.

Several domestic and foreign studies have confirmed that gallbladder wall thickness is one of the key indicators to measure the severity of gallbladder diseases, and it is also an important parameter to predict the difficulty and risk of surgery [25]. In the normal population, about 97% of individuals have a gallbladder wall thickness of less than 2 mm [26]. An analysis of more than 300 patients showed a significantly higher risk of difficult gallbladder dissection, gallbladder rupture, and bleeding during surgery when gallbladder wall thickness reached or exceeded 4 mm, increasing the probability of laparotomy during surgery to 20% [27, 28]. In addition, studies have demonstrated a significant correlation between gallbladder wall thickness and surgical difficulty and postoperative complications, especially in patients with acute cholecystitis. This may be due to multiple adhesions of the anatomy surrounding the gallbladder and fibrosis or necrosis of the gallbladder wall, resulting in difficulties in dissecting the Calot triangle and gallbladder [29, 30]. These findings are consistent with our findings.

Second, we note that the interval between the onset of symptoms of acute cholecystitis and PTGBD is also an important influencing factor for surgery and prognosis. PTGBD can rapidly reduce the pressure on the gallbladder, thereby inhibiting the progression of inflammation [19]. Koo et al. reported that PTGBD was anatomically clearer and simpler and safer when performed within 72 h of AC symptoms, as it was in the edematous cholecystitis stage [31, 32]. Another study reported that the interval from acute cholecystitis onset to PTGBD of ≤ 73.5 h was the most important predictor of LC success after PTGBD in acute cholecystitis [25].

We performed univariate and multivariate analyses and found that WBC and CRP were also independent risk factors. This finding is consistent with the results of previous studies. Teefey et al. found that with a WBC count of >17 × 103/µL, there was a 30% chance of developing necrotizing gangrenous cholecystitis [33]. A 2017 study by Nassar et al. showed that C-reactive protein levels can be used to predict the difficulty of surgery for cholecystitis. Sert et al. also came up with the same results [34]. Severe inflammation or infection can increase the difficulty and duration of surgery, significantly increasing the probability of postoperative complications [30, 35–39]. In addition, elevated neutrophil levels can lead to an imbalance in the body’s inflammatory response, which may further lead to immune system exhaustion and decreased lymphocyte counts, followed by an aggressive inflammatory response by activating the innate immune system, so an increase in the NLR cut-off is a higher estimate of the severity of inflammation [40]. A study by Sang Kuon Lee and others observed a significant association between NLR and gangrenous cholecystitis (GC), and each standard deviation increase in NLR corresponds to a 48% increase in the probability of GC [41]. For patients with high inflammatory markers, it is necessary to postpone LC surgery after PTGBD decompression.

Interestingly, our research revealed that age and gender are also contributing factors. Anatomical changes and tissue fibrosis in older patients increase the likelihood of surgical difficulty, which is consistent with the findings of Janjic et al. [42] Kadirvel further found that the complication rate was approximately 15% in women undergoing delayed LC surgery, compared with 10% in men [35]. Of particular note is that female patients appear to be at higher risk for two specific complications, biliary tract injury and bleeding, which may be related to differences in physiological structure between males and females.

The model we developed differs from the linear models used in traditional studies in that it maximizes the utilization of clinical parameters and significantly improves the accuracy of diagnosis. Based on the RF model, we have built a concise, intuitive, and dynamic online application that calculates the probability of delayed LC after PTGBD in elderly patients with acute cholecystitis, and enables more reasonable and specific treatment plans to be tailored to the patient’s profile. For example, when determining whether an elderly AC patient who has undergone PTGBD needs delayed LC surgery, we can input the patient’s gallbladder wall thickness, Interval between onset and PTGBD hours, WBC, CRP, Age, Gender, NLR, and other data into our network calculator. Subsequently, the application will integrate these factors and automatically calculate the probability of delaying the LC procedure with an output of 0.2339 (Fig. 10). This suggests that patients should have LC surgery within 6 weeks. Therefore, we recommend a non-delayed LC procedure. Although the prediction model we developed showed good accuracy and was visualized by an online calculator, it was a novelty of this study for clinicians to rapidly assess the timing of LC surgery after PTGBD in elderly patients with acute cholecystitis.

However, there are also some limitations, which need to be continuously optimized and improved in clinical practice. First, this is a retrospective study, and missing data and selection bias are inevitable. Although our inclusion and exclusion criteria have improved the stability and accuracy of the model within the scope of the study, they have limited the external validity of the model. The patient population in the real world is more diverse, and our model may not be able to fully capture the characteristics of all possible cases. Second, we only used two center datasets with small sample sizes for model construction and validation. The conclusions of this study need to be further validated in prospective studies with multiple centers and large samples. Finally, this study only focused on elderly patients with acute cholecystitis > 60 years of age, and some associations have raised the definition of old age to > 70 years, and the conclusions reached under this classification may be slightly different, and further validation is still needed.

Conclusion

In summary, we constructed an ML-based prediction model with good performance to predict the likelihood of delayed LC after PTGBD in elderly AC patients based on independent predictors (gallbladder wall thickness, Interval between onset and PTGBD hours, WBC, CRP, Age, Gender, NLR). In addition, we tried to translate the research results into clinical practice by constructing an online calculator, which is a personalized prediction tool that is more helpful for the decision-making and management of the optimal timing of LC surgery after PTGBD surgery in elderly patients with acute cholecystitis.

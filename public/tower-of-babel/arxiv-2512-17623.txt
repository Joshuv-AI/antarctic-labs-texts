The threshold for quantum-classical correspondence is D ∼ ℏ 4 3 D\sim\hbar^{\frac{4}{3}}
Felipe Hernández, Daniel Ranard, C. Jess Riedel

Abstract
In chaotic quantum systems, an initially localized quantum state can deviate strongly from the corresponding classical phase-space distribution after the Ehrenfest time t E ∼ log ⁡ ( ℏ − 1 ) t_{\mathrm{E}}\sim\log(\hbar^{-1}) , even in the limit ℏ → 0 \hbar\to 0 . Decoherence by the environment is often invoked to explain the persistence of the quantum-classical correspondence at longer timescales. Recent rigorous results for Lindblad dynamics with phase-space diffusion strength D D show that quantum and classical evolutions remain close for times that are exponentially longer than the Ehrenfest time whenever D ≫ ℏ 4 3 D\gg\hbar^{\frac{4}{3}} , in units set by the classical Hamiltonian. At the same time, some heuristic arguments have suggested the weaker condition D ≫ ℏ 2 D\gg\hbar^{2} always suffices. Here we construct an explicit Lindbladian that demonstrates that the scaling D ∼ ℏ 4 3 D\sim\hbar^{\frac{4}{3}} is indeed the threshold for quantum-classical correspondence beyond the Ehrenfest time. Our example uses a smooth time-dependent Hamiltonian and linear Lindblad operators generating homogeneous isotropic diffusion. It exhibits an ℏ \hbar -independent quantum-classical discrepancy at the Ehrenfest time whenever D ≪ ℏ 4 3 D\ll\hbar^{\frac{4}{3}} , even for ℏ \hbar -independent “macroscopic” smooth observables.

Construction

To understand the construction of the counterexample, we must first explain how the key factor
\hbar^{2}D^{-\frac{3}{2}}
in (

[
16
]
for a system of a single variable. In the sketch, we assume

Using the classical Hamiltonian
H
, first we fix a preferred ratio between position and momentum units in phase space.
This determines a notion of phase-space length and a family of unsqueezed coherent states of width
\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}}
in both

We assume the initial state can be expressed as a convex combination

\displaystyle\rho\approx\sum_{j}q_{j}\,\hat{\xi}_{\alpha_{j},\Sigma_{j}},\qquad q_{j}\geq 0,\qquad\ \sum_{j}q_{j}=1,\qquad\Sigma_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\ell_{\mathrm{dec}},

where each
\hat{\xi}_{\alpha_{j},\Sigma_{j}}
is a pure Gaussian state centered at
\alpha_{j}=(x_{j},p_{j})
with covariance matrix
\Sigma_{j}>0
. The bounded covariance
\Sigma_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\ell_{\mathrm{dec}}
means each Gaussian has width at most

in phase space. The approximation scheme proceeds as follows:

1.

Local quadratic evolution.
Given a mixture of Gaussian states
\hat{\xi}_{\alpha_{j},\Sigma_{j}}
of limited squeezing as in (
2.1

approximate the dynamics of each
\hat{\xi}_{\alpha_{j},\Sigma_{j}}
individually for a short time by taking a second-order Taylor approximation of the quantum Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
centered at
\alpha_{j}
, which we call the Harmonic approximation
\hat{H}^{(\alpha_{j})}
. The reason to make this second-order approximation is that the quantum mechanical and classical evolution agree
exactly
for such quadratic Hamiltonians.
Over the short evolution, we incur a
harmonic error
(relative to the exact dynamics) due to the approximation, but we obtain a major benefit: the state remains
exactly
Gaussian, albeit now generally squeezed and (for
D\neq 0
) mixed.

2.

Decomposition into pure Gaussian states.
Although each Gaussian state is now squeezed and mixed, the diffusion ensures that the state never gets narrower than
\sim D^{\frac{1}{2}}
in any phase-space direction.
Thus it may be expressed as a Gaussian mixture of
less squeezed
pure Gaussian states, each of dimension
\sim\hbar D^{-\frac{1}{2}}\times D^{\frac{1}{2}}
(or even less squeezed), or width at most
\ell_{\mathrm{dec}}

Thus we obtain a mixture of Gaussian states again satisfying the assumptions of step (1).

3.

Repeat steps (1) and (2) until the final time.

At the end, we obtain an approximation of the form (
2.1
) to the exactly evolved quantum state.
Furthermore, using the same mixture
q_{j}
but now with corresponding classical Gaussians

\displaystyle\hbar^{-1}\left\|\left[\hat{H}-\hat{H}^{(\alpha_{j})},\hat{\xi}_{\alpha_{j},\Sigma_{j}}\right]\right\|_{\rm Tr}\lesssim\hbar^{-1}\ell_{\mathrm{dec}}^{3}\sim\hbar^{2}D^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

3
Squeezing the observable and state

In this section we deal with the squeezing and stretching steps
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
and
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{3}
, which are generated by Hamiltonians

\hat{H}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{x}\hat{p}+\hat{p}\hat{x})
and
\hat{H}_{3}=-\hat{H}_{1}
, respectively. It is convenient for us to work
with the stretching step by duality (the Heisenberg picture), so that it acts on the observable rather than the state, as follows:

\operatorname*{Tr}[\mathcal{Q}[\rho_{0}]a(\hat{p})]=\operatorname*{Tr}[e^{\tau_{3}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{3}}e^{\tau_{2}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{2}}e^{\tau_{1}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{1}}[\rho_{0}]a(\hat{p})]=\operatorname*{Tr}[e^{\tau_{2}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{2}}e^{\tau_{1}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{1}}[\rho_{0}]e^{\tau_{3}\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{3}}a(\hat{p})].

We have the following bounds on the error between the open and closed quantum dynamics:

\displaystyle\|e^{\tau_{1}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{1}}[\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma_{0}}]-\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma_{1}}\|_{\rm Tr}

\displaystyle\lesssim D\hbar^{-\frac{4}{3}}\tau_{1}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|e^{\tau_{3}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{3}^{*}}[a(\hat{p})]-a(e^{\tau_{3}}\hat{p})\|_{\rm op}

\displaystyle\lesssim D\hbar^{-\frac{4}{3}}\tau_{3}.

(3.2)

Hence
\rho_{1}=e^{\tau_{1}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{1}}[\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma_{0}}]
is close to the pure Gaussian state
\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma_{1}}
resulting from the closed-system evolution, and likewise the Heisenberg-evolved observable
e^{\tau_{3}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{3}^{*}}[a(\hat{p})]
is close to the unitarily squeezed observable
a(e^{\tau_{3}}\hat{p})
.
Analogously, the
classical dynamics satisfy the estimates

\displaystyle\|e^{\tau_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}}[\xi_{0,\Sigma_{0}}]-\xi_{0,\Sigma_{1}}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim D\hbar^{-\frac{4}{3}}\tau_{1}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|e^{\tau_{3}\mathcal{L}_{3}^{*}}[a(p)]-a(e^{\tau_{3}}p)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim D\hbar^{-\frac{4}{3}}\tau_{3}.

(3.4)

We choose
\tau_{1}=\frac{1}{6}\log\frac{2}{\hbar}
and
\tau_{3}=\frac{2}{3}\log\frac{2}{\hbar}
, so that the errors above have magnitude
on the order
D\hbar^{-\frac{4}{3}}
(up to factors
\log(\hbar^{-1})
we omit here), matching the right

1.6

3.1
Squeezing states

The closed
system dynamics up to time
s
send
\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma_{0}}
to
\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma(s)}
with

\Sigma(s)=\diag(e^{2s}\hbar,e^{-2s}\hbar)/2
. We therefore take
\tau_{1}=\frac{1}{6}\log\frac{2}{\hbar}
so
that
\Sigma_{1}\sim\diag(\hbar^{\frac{2}{3}},\hbar^{\frac{4}{3}})
. We compare the dynamics of the
open-system evolution with diffusion
D
to that of the closed-system dynamics using the Duhamel principle,
which in this case reads

\displaystyle e^{\tau_{1}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{1}}[\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma_{0}}]

\displaystyle=\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma_{1}}-\frac{D}{2\hbar^{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}e^{(\tau_{1}-s)\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{1}}\left([\hat{x},[\hat{x},\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma(s)}]]+[\hat{p},[\hat{p},\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma(s)}]]\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Using the triangle inequality in the trace norm, we therefore estimate

\displaystyle\|e^{\tau_{1}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{1}}[\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma_{0}}]-\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma_{1}}\|_{\rm Tr}\lesssim\frac{D}{\hbar^{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\left\|[\hat{x},[\hat{x},\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma(s)}]]\right\|_{\rm Tr}+\left\|[\hat{p},[\hat{p},\hat{\xi}_{0,\Sigma(s)}]]\right\|_{\rm Tr}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

The larger term on the right is the double-commutator against
\hat{x}
, which has scale

e^{2s}\hbar\lesssim\hbar^{\frac{2}{3}}
for
s\leq\tau_{1}
, and this completes the explanation of (
3.1
).

On the classical side the calculation is completely analogous, and we obtain

\displaystyle\|e^{\tau_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}}[\xi_{0,\Sigma_{0}}]-\xi_{0,\Sigma_{1}}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim D\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}(\|\partial_{x}^{2}\xi_{0,\Sigma(s)}\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{p}^{2}\xi_{0,\Sigma(s)}\|_{L^{1}})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

The larger term above is the second derivative
\partial_{p}^{2}
, which contributes

e^{2s}\hbar^{-1}\lesssim\hbar^{-\frac{4}{3}}
. Thus we obtain (
3.3
).

3.2
Squeezing observables

Under the closed system evolution
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{3}
,
we have
e^{\tau_{3}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{3}^{*}}[a(\hat{p})]=a(e^{\tau_{3}}\hat{p})
. Thus applying the Duhamel formula as above,

\displaystyle e^{\tau_{3}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{3}^{*}}[a(\hat{p})]

\displaystyle=a(e^{\tau_{3}}\hat{p})-\frac{D}{2\hbar^{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{3}}e^{(\tau_{3}-s)\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{3}^{*}}[\hat{x},[\hat{x},a(e^{s}\hat{p})]]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=a(e^{\tau_{3}}\hat{p})+\frac{D}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau_{3}}e^{(\tau_{3}-s)\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{3}^{*}}[e^{2s}a^{\prime\prime}(e^{s}\hat{p})]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

By the triangle inequality, it therefore follows that

\|e^{\tau_{3}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{3}^{*}}[a(\hat{p})]-a(e^{\tau_{3}}\hat{p})\|_{\rm op}\leq D\|a^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{3}}e^{2s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq De^{2\tau_{3}}\|a^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

In particular, taking
\tau_{3}=\frac{2}{3}\log\frac{2}{\hbar}

\displaystyle\|e^{\tau_{3}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{3}^{*}}[a(\hat{p})]-a(e^{\tau_{3}}\hat{p})\|_{\rm op}=O(D\hbar^{-\frac{4}{3}}),

(3.5)

which is (
3.2
).
Exactly the same calculation on the classical side establishes (
3.4
).

5
Discussion

If
D\sim\hbar^{2}
is not the boundary of the quantum-classical correspondence, what is it? It is useful to think in terms of the decoherence length
\ell_{\mathrm{dec}}\sim\hbar/\sqrt{D}
, which is expected to characterize the maximum length scale of persistent superpositions. Then
D\sim\hbar^{2}
means
\ell_{\mathrm{dec}}\sim 1
and we have at least three regimes:

\bullet

Macroscopic coherence
(
D\ll\hbar^{2}
): Decoherence is negligible,
\ell_{\mathrm{dec}}\gg 1
, and the system is essentially closed in the classical limit
\hbar\to 0
. Beyond the Ehrenfest time, quantum superpositions generically form on macroscopic scales.

\bullet

Mesoscopic coherence
(
\hbar^{2}\ll D\ll\hbar^{\frac{4}{3}}
): While macroscopic superpositions are suppressed, distinctly quantum behavior (superposition and possibly quantum computation) takes place below the decoherence length
\ell_{\mathrm{dec}}\sim\hbar/\sqrt{D}
in phase space. This mesoscopic scale is simultaneously much smaller than the macroscopic scale
\hbar^{0}
and much larger than the microscopic scale
\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}}
. As shown in our example, interference effects at the mesoscopic scale can be amplified to produce a macroscopic imprint, even when using a Hamiltonian that varies only at the macroscopic scale.

\bullet

Quantum-classical correspondence
(
\hbar^{\frac{4}{3}}\ll D
): When the initial state is a
pure Gaussian state, or a mixture of such states, the evolution remains well-approximated by a mixture of such states, whose centroids flow along classical trajectories and diffuse about them with strength
D

[
16
,
17
]
. If
D=o(\hbar^{0})
, then diffusion vanishes in the classical limit
\hbar\to 0
, so noiseless classical mechanics is recovered.

When the initial state is arbitrary, we expect the situation is more complicated. If
D\ll\hbar
, then
\ell_{\mathrm{dec}}\gg\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and an initial superposition of coherent states separated by distance
\sim\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}}
in phase space will only decohere slowly. Therefore, for the case of
D\ll\hbar
and
arbitrary
initial states, we only conjecture a correspondence for smooth observables. In contrast, for
D\gg\hbar
, we speculate that arbitrary initial states may decohere sufficiently after an
o(1)
time to yield a subsequent correspondence for all observables.

We wonder how generically the quantum-classical breakdown demonstrated by Theorem
1
appears for other systems with
D\lesssim\hbar^{\frac{4}{3}}
. On one hand, the particular example in Theorem
1
uses a fine-tuned initial state, sitting precisely at an unstable point; if it were displaced by a distance
\gg\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}}
in phase space, the state would not remain confined within a unit box (it would “escape the laboratory”).
On the other hand, we speculate that for chaotic systems, a breakdown may occur regardless of where the initial coherent state is placed. Generically it would stretch exponentially (though typically confined in phase space by energetic constraints), eventually squeezed to dimension
\hbar^{\frac{2}{3}}\times\hbar^{\frac{1}{3}}
, completing the analog of our Step 1 (Figure
1
). Then nonlinearities would produce small quantum-classical discrepancies, the analog of our Step 2. Finally, these could be amplified to the macroscopic scale, the analog of our Step 3.

Step 3 of our construction requires stretching the state in the opposite direction of Step 1: our time-dependent Hamiltonian involves an inversion of the stable and unstable directions of the flow. If restricted to a time-independent Hamiltonian flow which is Anosov, and hence with invariant stable and unstable directions at every point, the analog of Step 3 is prohibited. We conjecture the threshold for quantum-classical correspondence is different for Anosov flows, perhaps at
D\gg\hbar^{2}
.

One could also wonder whether the behavior exemplified in Theorem
1
is robust to noise or uncertainty in the initial condition, corresponding to a mixed initial state. Likewise, one might consider noise in the Hamiltonian. We leave such questions to future work.

(We will use

In this section we calculate the second and fourth central moments of

and

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto e^{\tau_{1}}x,

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\mapsto e^{-\tau_{1}}p,

(B.31)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto x,

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\mapsto p+\tau_{2}\,x^{2},

(B.32)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto e^{-\tau_{3}}x,

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\mapsto e^{\tau_{3}}p,

(B.33)

during the three steps generated respectively by

-distributions, although not identical, have the same first, second, and fourth central moments.

at the checkpoint

B.3.2
Quantum case

Now we show that the quantum system has the exact same values for the moments discussed above in the classical case, (
B.34
)–(
B.53
).

For the initial state (
t_{0}=0
) and after the first time step (
t_{1}=\tau_{1}
), the state is Gaussian with a diagonal covariance matrix, so the expectations of all powers of
x
are identical in the quantum and classical cases, and likewise for
p
. Furthermore, the Hamiltonian
H_{2}(x,p)=-x^{3}/3
of the second step is diagonal in
x
, so it does not change the
x
-distribution, and the third step just squeezes in
x
(i.e.,
\hat{x}_{3}=\hat{x}_{2}e^{-\tau_{3}}
), so the quantum and classical
x
-distributions will remain the same for all times.

For
p
, we will only address the first, second and fourth central moments.
9
9

9

The other moments of
p
will in general be
different
for quantum vs. classical, as indeed we need for our counterexample.
It will suffice to show that these moments are the same for the quantum and classical states after step 2 because the third step just stretches in
p
(i.e.,
\hat{p}_{3}=\hat{p}_{2}e^{\tau_{3}}
).

Let
\mathring{W}(x,p,t)
be the Wigner function. In general, the Moyal equation (i.e., quantum Liouville equation) for the dynamics of the Wigner function contains arbitrarily high powers of
x
- and
p
-derivatives of
\mathring{W}
and of the Hamiltonian
H
. However, for the specific Hamiltonian
H_{2}(x,p)=-x^{3}/3
during the second step, which has no
p
-dependence and only cubic
x
-dependence, the Moyal equation truncates at third order, simplifying to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathring{W}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}H_{2}\partial_{p}\mathring{W}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{24}(\partial_{x}^{3}H_{2})(\partial_{p}^{3}\mathring{W})

(B.60)

\displaystyle=-x^{2}\partial_{p}\mathring{W}+\frac{h^{2}}{3}\partial_{p}^{3}\mathring{W}

(B.61)

(recalling
h=\hbar/2
).
This differs from the classical Liouville equation for the classical distribution
\mathring{f}
only by the second term:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathring{f}

\displaystyle=\{\!\{H_{2},\mathring{f}\}\!\}_{\rm PB}

(B.62)

\displaystyle=-x^{2}\partial_{p}\mathring{f}

(B.63)

Let us use the hat to denote the Fourier transform with respect to
p
:

\displaystyle\widehat{W}(x,k,t):=\int e^{ikp}\mathring{W}(x,p,t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}p.

(B.64)

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(x,k,t):=\int e^{ikp}\mathring{f}(x,p,t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}p.

(B.65)

Then
\partial_{p}\mapsto ik
and
\partial_{p}^{3}\mapsto(ik)^{3}=-ik^{3}
, so

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{W}

\displaystyle=-ikx^{2}\widehat{W}-i\frac{h^{2}}{3}k^{3}\widehat{W},

(B.66)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{f}

\displaystyle=-ikx^{2}\widehat{f}

(B.67)

Thus, since the quantum and classical distributions over phase space are identical after step 1,
\mathring{W}(x,p,t_{1})=\mathring{f}(x,p,t_{1})
, we have for any
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
that

\displaystyle\widehat{W}(x,k,t)=e^{-ih^{2}(t-t_{1})k^{3}/3}\;\widehat{f}(x,k,t).

(B.68)

The respective cumulant generating functions for the
p
distribution are

\displaystyle K_{\rm qm}(k,t)

\displaystyle:=\log\,\langle e^{ik\hat{p}}\rangle_{\rm qm}(t)=\log\int\widehat{W}(x,k,t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

(B.69)

\displaystyle K_{\rm cl}(k,t)

\displaystyle:=\log\,\langle e^{ikp}\rangle_{\rm cl}(t)=\log\int\widehat{f}(x,k,t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

(B.70)

so they are related by

\displaystyle K_{\rm qm}(k,t)=K_{\rm cl}(k,t)-i\frac{h^{2}(t-t_{1})}{3}k^{3}.

(B.71)

For any random variable, the
n
th cumulant
\kappa_{n}
is

\kappa_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{i^{n}}\frac{d^{n}}{dk^{n}}K(k,t)\Big|_{k=0}.

Since the difference is proportional to
k^{3}
, it contributes only to the third cumulant
\kappa_{3}
.

The second and fourth central moments
\mu_{2}:=\langle(p-\bar{p})^{2}\rangle
and
\mu_{4}:=\langle(p-\bar{p})^{4}\rangle
can be written in terms of the cumulants as
\mu_{2}=\kappa_{2}
and
\mu_{4}=\kappa_{4}+3\kappa_{2}^{2}
. These depend only on
\kappa_{2}
and
\kappa_{4}
, both of which are identical in quantum and classical cases. As mentioned, the simple stretching of step 3 ensures there is no quantum-classical discrepancy generated then either.

Appendix C
Bounding the diffusion error

Now we consider the effects of the diffusion of strength
D
.

C.1
Quantum diffusion error

Let
\mathring{\rho}_{t}=|\psi_{t}\rangle\langle\psi_{t}|
be the solution to the quantum closed-system dynamics

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathring{\rho}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{H}[\mathring{\rho}]=-i\hbar^{-1}[\hat{H},\mathring{\rho}]

(C.1)

\displaystyle=-i(2h)^{-1}[\hat{H},\mathring{\rho}]

(C.2)

as described above, where
H=H(t)
is the three-bump Hamiltonian given by (
A.10
). We will compare it to the open-system trajectory
\rho_{t}
, starting from the initial pure Gaussian state
\rho_{0}=|\psi_{0}\rangle\langle\psi_{0}|
, (
A.8
), but which instead solves

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}=\hat{\mathcal{L}}[\rho]=\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{H}[\rho]+\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{D}[\rho].

(C.3)

Here, the diffusion Lindbladian is

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{D}[\rho]

\displaystyle:=-\frac{D}{2\hbar^{2}}([\hat{x},[\hat{x},\rho]]+[\hat{p},[\hat{p},\rho]])

(C.4)

\displaystyle=-\frac{D}{8h^{2}}([\hat{x},[\hat{x},\rho]]+[\hat{p},[\hat{p},\rho]]),

(C.5)

which corresponds, in the Wigner representation, to the differential operator

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{D}[W]=\frac{1}{2}D(\partial^{2}_{x}W+\partial^{2}_{p}W).

(C.6)

We want to show that introducing diffusion produces only an asymptotically negligible change to the final momentum distribution
\mathring{m}_{3}
, i.e., that
\|m_{3}-\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}=o(h^{0})
.

C.1.1
Duhamel bound on growth of overall state error
\|\rho_{t}-\mathring{\rho}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}

By Duhamel,

\displaystyle\rho_{t}-\mathring{\rho}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\!ds\,e^{(t-s)\hat{\mathcal{L}}}\left(\hat{\mathcal{L}}-\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{H}\right)[\mathring{\rho}_{s}]

(C.7)

we have

\displaystyle\|\rho_{t}-\mathring{\rho}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\!ds\,\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{D}[\mathring{\rho}_{s}]\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}

(C.8)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{tD}{8h^{2}}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\|[x,[x,\mathring{\rho}_{s}]]+[p,[p,\mathring{\rho}_{s}]]\right\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}.

(C.9)

Let us focus for the moment on a particular time
s
inside the supremum.
For the
x
double commutator,

\displaystyle\|[x,[x,\eta]]\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}=\|x^{2}\eta-2x\eta x+\eta x^{2}\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\|x^{2}\eta\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}+\|x\eta x\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}\right).

(C.10)

We bound the first term with Hölder’s inequality,
\|AB\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}\leq\|A\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
(where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm),

\displaystyle\|x^{2}\eta\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}\leq\|x^{2}\sqrt{\eta}\|_{2}\|\sqrt{\eta}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\langle x^{4}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}},

(C.11)

and the second term using
x\eta x\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|x\eta x\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}=\operatorname*{Tr}(x\eta x)=\langle x^{2}\rangle,

(C.12)

so that

\displaystyle\|[x,[x,\eta]]\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}\leq 2\left(\langle x^{2}\rangle_{\eta}+\langle x^{4}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\eta}\right).

(C.13)

Using this and the corresponding bound for
\left\|[p,[p,\eta]]\right\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}
gives

\displaystyle\left\|[x,[x,\mathring{\rho}_{s}]]+[p,[p,\mathring{\rho}_{s}]]\right\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}\leq 2\left(\langle x^{2}\rangle_{\psi_{s}}+\langle x^{4}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\psi_{s}}+\langle p^{2}\rangle_{\psi_{s}}+\langle p^{4}\rangle_{\psi_{s}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

(C.14)

More generally, we can get this for central moments by repeating the last few steps using
\tilde{x}:=x-\bar{x}=x-\langle x\rangle_{\psi_{s}}
and
\tilde{p}:=p-\bar{p}=p-\langle p\rangle_{\psi_{s}}
to get

\displaystyle\left\|[x,[x,\mathring{\rho}_{s}]]+[p,[p,\mathring{\rho}_{s}]]\right\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}

\displaystyle=\left\|[\tilde{x},[\tilde{x},\mathring{\rho}_{s}]]+[\tilde{p},[\tilde{p},\mathring{\rho}_{s}]]\right\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}

(C.15)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\langle(x-\bar{x})^{2}\rangle_{\psi_{s}}+\langle(x-\bar{x})^{4}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\psi_{s}}+\langle(p-\bar{p})^{2}\rangle_{\psi_{s}}+\langle(p-\bar{p})^{4}\rangle_{\psi_{s}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

(C.16)

Inserting this into (
C.9
) gives

\displaystyle\|\rho_{t}-\mathring{\rho}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{tD}{4h^{2}}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left[\langle(x-\bar{x})^{2}\rangle_{\psi_{s}}+\langle(x-\bar{x})^{4}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\psi_{s}}+\langle(p-\bar{p})^{2}\rangle_{\psi_{s}}+\langle(p-\bar{p})^{4}\rangle_{\psi_{s}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

(C.17)

However, the effect of the
x
-diffusion (i.e.,
p
-decoherence) on the quantum state is actually large (in trace norm) during the third step when the state becomes highly squeezed in
x
(stretched in
p
) such that
\langle p^{2}\rangle_{\psi_{3}}\sim O(h^{0})
. We would have to take
D=o(h^{2})
. So we cannot take
t
into the third step.

But that’s okay because we are only interested in measuring the distribution of
p
, which is only affected by the
p
-diffusion (i.e.,
x
-decoherence). The plan is to bound the small error introduced by diffusion on the overall state – and thus to the
p
distribution – up through the end of the second step, and then separately bound the additional error to the
p
distribution due to diffusion in the third step.

More specifically: We will introduce an intermediate (“hybrid”) quantum trajectory
\tilde{\rho}_{t}
that is equal to the closed-system evolution
\mathring{\rho}_{t}
up to the end of the second step (
\tilde{\rho}_{2}=\mathring{\rho}_{2}
) and which then experiences the diffusion during the third step (like
\rho
). We will bound
\|m_{3}-\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}
by starting with the triangle inequality with the intermediate trajectory

\displaystyle\|m_{3}-\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|m_{3}-\tilde{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}+\|\tilde{m}_{3}-\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}.

(C.18)

C.1.2
Bounding overall state error
\|m_{3}-\tilde{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|\rho_{2}-\mathring{\rho}_{2}\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}
through end of step 2

For the first term on the r.h.s. of (
C.18
), the open and hybrid trajectories both evolve during the third step according to the same Lindbladian
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
so

\displaystyle\|m_{3}-\tilde{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho_{3}-\tilde{\rho}_{3}\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}

(C.19)

\displaystyle=\left\|e^{\tau_{3}\hat{\mathcal{L}}}(\rho_{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{2})\right\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}

(C.20)

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho_{2}-\tilde{\rho}_{2}\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}

(C.21)

\displaystyle=\|\rho_{2}-\mathring{\rho}_{2}\|_{\mathrm{Tr}}

(C.22)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})D}{4h^{2}}\left[he^{2\tau_{1}}+\sqrt{3}he^{2\tau_{1}}+h(1+2\tau_{2}^{2}he^{6\tau_{1}})+h\sqrt{3+12\tau_{2}^{2}he^{6\tau_{1}}+60\tau_{2}^{4}h^{2}e^{12\tau_{1}}}\right]

(C.23)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})D}{4he^{-2\tau_{1}}}\left[1+\sqrt{3}+(e^{-2\tau_{1}}+2\tau_{2}^{2}he^{4\tau_{1}})+\sqrt{3e^{-4\tau_{1}}+12\tau_{2}^{2}he^{2\tau_{1}}+60\tau_{2}^{4}h^{2}e^{8\tau_{1}}}\right]

(C.24)

\displaystyle<\frac{(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})D}{4he^{-2\tau_{1}}}\left[1+\sqrt{3}+(1+2\tau_{2}^{2})+\sqrt{3+12\tau_{2}^{2}+60\tau_{2}^{4}}\right]

(C.25)

\displaystyle=C_{1}\frac{(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})D}{he^{-2\tau_{1}}}

(C.26)

with constant

\displaystyle C_{1}:=\frac{1}{4}\left[1+\sqrt{3}+(1+2\tau_{2}^{2})+\sqrt{3+12\tau_{2}^{2}+60\tau_{2}^{4}}\right]=_{\tau}1+\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\approx 3.598.

(C.27)

In (
C.21
), we use the fact that
e^{\tau_{3}\hat{\mathcal{L}}}
is a CP map which cannot increase trace norm. In (
C.22
) we use
\tilde{\rho}_{2}=\mathring{\rho}_{2}
. To get (
C.23
) we have used (
C.17
) with
t=t_{2}=\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}
and the central moment bounds proven in Section
B.3
.
Finally in (
C.25
) we invoke
h\leq 1
and our technical assumption that
\tau_{1}<\tfrac{1}{4}\log h^{-1}
.

C.1.3
Bounding momentum error
\|\tilde{m}_{3}-\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}
during step 3

The second term on the r.h.s. of (
C.18
),
\|\tilde{m}_{3}-\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}
, can be bounded by observing that the dynamics for the Wigner function during the third step decompose into terms involving only
x
or only
p
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathring{W}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{(x)}_{3,H}[\mathring{W}]+\mathcal{L}^{(p)}_{3,H}[\mathring{W}],

(C.28)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{W}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{L}^{(x)}_{3,H}+\mathcal{L}^{(x)}_{D})[\tilde{W}]+(\mathcal{L}^{(p)}_{3,H}+\mathcal{L}^{(p)}_{D})[\tilde{W}],

(C.29)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(x)}_{D}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}D\partial_{x}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(p)}_{D}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}D\partial_{p}^{2},

(C.30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(x)}_{3,H}

\displaystyle=x\partial_{x},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(p)}_{3,H}

\displaystyle=-p\partial_{p}.

(C.31)

Thus the reduced dynamics for the momentum distributions
\tilde{m}_{t}(p):=\langle p|\tilde{\rho}_{t}|p\rangle=\int\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\,\tilde{W}_{t}(x,p)
and
\mathring{m}_{t}(p):=|\hat{\psi}_{t}(p)|^{2}=\int\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\,\mathring{W}_{t}(x,p)
are

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathring{m}

\displaystyle=\int\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\,\partial_{t}\mathring{W}=\int\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\,(x\partial_{x}-p\partial_{p})\mathring{W}=-p\partial_{p}\mathring{m}-\int\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\,\mathring{W}\partial_{x}(x)=-p\partial_{p}\mathring{m}-\mathring{m}

(C.32)

where the Gaussian tails of
\mathring{W}
ensure no boundary term at infinity. Similar manipulations for
\tilde{m}
mean that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathring{m}

\displaystyle=-(1+p\partial_{p})\mathring{m},

(C.33)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{m}

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\mathcal{L}^{(p)}_{D}-(1+p\partial_{p})\Bigr]\tilde{m}.

(C.34)

during this third time step.
Applying Duhamel to the momentum dynamics (difference of (
C.34
) and (
C.33
) is
\mathcal{L}^{(p)}_{D}
) yields

\displaystyle\|\tilde{m}_{3}-\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq(t_{3}-t_{2})\,\sup_{t\in[t_{2},t_{3}]}\bigl\|\mathcal{L}^{(p)}_{D}[\mathring{m}_{t}]\bigr\|_{L^{1}}

(C.35)

\displaystyle=\tau_{3}\,\frac{D}{2}\,\sup_{t\in[t_{2},t_{3}]}\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{m}_{t}\|_{L^{1}}.

(C.36)

Equation (
C.33
) is solved by exact scaling,

\mathring{m}_{t}(p)=e^{-(t-t_{2})}\,\mathring{m}_{2}(e^{-(t-t_{2})}p),

so
\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{m}_{t}\|_{L^{1}}=e^{-2(t-t_{2})}\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{m}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}
. Therefore the supremum over
t\in[t_{2},t_{3}]
in (
C.35
) is attained at
t=t_{2}
, as expected:

\displaystyle\|\tilde{m}_{3}-\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{\tau_{3}D}{2}\,\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{m}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}

(C.37)

\displaystyle=C_{2}\,\frac{\tau_{3}D}{e^{-2\tau_{3}}}.

(C.38)

Here, using
\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}=e^{-2\tau_{3}}\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{m}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}
, we have defined

\displaystyle C_{2}:=\frac{1}{2}e^{-2\tau_{3}}\,\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{m}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}\approx_{\tau}0.3726

(C.39)

which, per (
B.30
), is independent of
h
for our choice of parameters.

C.1.4
Final quantum bound

Inserting (
C.37
) and (
C.26
) into the triangle inequality (
C.18
) we get

\displaystyle\|m_{3}-\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|m_{3}-\tilde{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}+\|\tilde{m}_{3}-\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}

(C.40)

\displaystyle\leq\left[C_{1}\frac{\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}}{he^{-2\tau_{1}}}+C_{2}\,\frac{\tau_{3}}{e^{-2\tau_{3}}}\right]D

(C.41)

\displaystyle=_{\tau}\frac{C_{1}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{6}\log h^{-1}\right)+C_{2}\tfrac{2}{3}\log h^{-1}}{h^{\frac{4}{3}}}D

(C.42)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{qu}}\frac{1+\log h^{-1}}{h^{\frac{4}{3}}}D

(C.43)

for positive constant

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{qu}}:=C_{1}+\frac{2}{3}C_{2}\approx_{\tau}3.846

(C.44)

Hence

\displaystyle\|m_{3}-\mathring{m}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}\to 0\quad\text{if}\quad D=_{\tau}o\!\left(\frac{h^{\frac{4}{3}}}{1+\log h^{-1}}\right).

(C.45)

C.2
Classical diffusion error

The classical case is much the same. We seek to bound
\|k_{3}-\mathring{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}
, where
k(p):=\int\!f(x,p)\,dx
is the classical momentum distribution in the presence of diffusion. We start by considering the Duhamel bound on the overall state error
\|f_{t}-\mathring{f}_{t}\|_{L^{1}}
for arbitrary time
t
.

Let
\mathring{f}_{t}
be the solution to the classical closed-system dynamics

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathring{f}_{t}}{\partial t}={\mathcal{L}}_{H}[\mathring{f}_{t}]=-(\partial_{x}\mathring{f}_{t})(\partial_{p}H(t))+(\partial_{p}\mathring{f}_{t})(\partial_{x}H(t))

(C.46)

as described in the first section, where
H=H(t)
is the classical three-bump Hamiltonian given by (
A.10
). We will compare it to the classical open-system trajectory
f_{t}
, starting from the same initial pure Gaussian state (
A.7
), which solves

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial t}={\mathcal{L}}[f_{t}]={\mathcal{L}}_{H}[f_{t}]+{\mathcal{L}}_{D}[f_{t}].

(C.47)

The contribution from diffusion

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{D}[f]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}D(\partial_{x}^{2}f+\partial_{p}^{2}f),

(C.48)

is the identical differential operator as the quantum case, (
C.6
).
We want to show that introducing diffusion does not much change the final momentum distribution
k_{3}(p)=\int\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\,f_{3}(x,p)
, i.e., that
\|k_{3}-\mathring{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}
is small.

C.2.1
Duhamel bound on growth of overall state error
\|f_{t}-\mathring{f}_{t}\|_{L^{1}}

By Duhamel,

\displaystyle f_{t}-\mathring{f}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\!ds\,e^{(t-s){\mathcal{L}}}\left({\mathcal{L}}-\mathcal{L}_{H}\right)[\mathring{f}_{s}]

(C.49)

we have

\displaystyle\|f_{t}-\mathring{f}_{t}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\!ds\,\|{\mathcal{L}}_{D}[\mathring{f}_{s}]\|_{L^{1}}

(C.50)

\displaystyle=\frac{D}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\!ds\,\left(\|\partial_{x}^{2}\mathring{f}_{s}\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{f}_{s}\|_{L^{1}}\right),

(C.51)

since
e^{(t-s)\mathcal{L}}
is an
L^{1}
-contraction. As before, the error in the overall (now classical) state will become large during the third step. So we introduce an intermediate classical trajectory
\tilde{f}_{t}
that is equal to the closed-system evolution
\mathring{f}_{t}
up to the end of the second step (
\tilde{f}_{2}=\mathring{f}_{2}
) and which then experiences the diffusion. We will bound
\|k_{3}-\mathring{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}
by starting with the triangle inequality with the intermediate trajectory

\displaystyle\|k_{3}-\mathring{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|k_{3}-\tilde{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}+\|\tilde{k}_{3}-\mathring{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}.

(C.52)

C.2.2
Bounding overall state error
\|k_{3}-\tilde{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|f_{2}-\mathring{f}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}
through end of step 2

For most of this subsection we will not denote time-dependence explicitly. Throughout all evolution, the closed-system classical density has the conditional-Gaussian form

\displaystyle\mathring{f}(x,p)=g_{\sigma_{x}}(x)\,g_{\sigma_{p}}(p-rx^{2}),\qquad\qquad\mathrm{with}\qquad g_{\sigma}(u):=(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp[-u^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})],

(C.53)

where
\sigma_{x},\sigma_{p}>0
and
r\geq 0
have implicit time dependence.
Define the dimensionless variables

\displaystyle y:=x/\sigma_{x},\quad z:=(p-rx^{2})/\sigma_{p}

(C.54)

so that
y,z
are i.i.d. standard Gaussians. Thus for any function
Q

\displaystyle\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}p\,Q(y,z)\mathring{f}(x,p)=\int dy\int dz\,Q(y,z)g_{1}(y)g_{1}(z)=\mathbb{E}\,Q(y,z)

(C.55)

where
\mathbb{E}
denotes the expectation with respect to
y,z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{iid}}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

It will be cleaner to work with the derivatives of logs of Gaussians, so we note that for any smooth function
h>0
,

\displaystyle\partial^{2}h=h\left[\left(\partial\log h\right)^{2}+\partial^{2}\log h\right].

(C.56)

First we compute the
p
-derivatives. Since

\displaystyle\partial_{p}\log g_{\sigma_{p}}(p-rx^{2})=-(p-rx^{2})/\sigma_{p}^{2}=-z/\sigma_{p},

(C.57)

we have

\displaystyle\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{f}=\sigma_{p}^{-2}(z^{2}-1)\,\mathring{f}.

(C.58)

Taking the
L^{1}
norm gives the exact identity

\displaystyle\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{f}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle=\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}p\,\left|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{f}(x,p)\right|

(C.59)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{p}^{-2}\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}p\,|z^{2}-1|\mathring{f}(x,p)

(C.60)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{p}^{-2}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left|z^{2}-1\right|

(C.61)

\displaystyle=C_{3}\,\sigma_{p}^{-2},

(C.62)

with constant

\displaystyle C_{3}:=\mathbb{E}|z^{2}-1|=2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi e}}\approx 0.9679

(C.63)

Now we compute the
x
-derivatives

\partial_{x}\log\mathring{f}=\partial_{x}\log g_{\sigma_{x}}(x)+\partial_{x}\log g_{\sigma_{p}}(p-rx^{2})=-\frac{x}{\sigma_{x}^{2}}+\frac{2rx}{\sigma_{p}^{2}}(p-rx^{2})=\frac{y}{\sigma_{x}}\left(-1+2bz\right).

with dimensionless (non-random) parameter

b:=r\,\sigma_{x}^{2}\,\sigma_{p}^{-1}\geq 0

Differentiating once more,

\partial_{x}^{2}\log\mathring{f}=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{x}^{2}}+\frac{2r}{\sigma_{p}^{2}}\bigl(p-3rx^{2}\bigr)=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{x}^{2}}+\frac{2b}{\sigma_{x}^{2}}z-\frac{4b^{2}}{\sigma_{x}^{2}}y^{2}.

Combining these using (
C.56
) we get

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}\mathring{f}=\frac{\mathring{f}}{\sigma_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[y^{2}-1+2bz\,(1-2y^{2})+4b^{2}y^{2}\,(z^{2}-1)\Bigr].

(C.64)

so

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{2}\mathring{f}\|_{L^{1}}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{x}^{2}}\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl|\,y^{2}-1+2bz(1-2y^{2})+4b^{2}y^{2}(z^{2}-1)\Bigr|.

(C.65)

Apply the triangle inequality and independence of
y,z
:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{2}\mathring{f}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{x}^{2}}\Big[\mathbb{E}\left|y^{2}-1\right|+2b\,\mathbb{E}\left|z(1-2y^{2})\right|+4b^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\left[y^{2}\right]\,\mathbb{E}\left|z^{2}-1\right|\Big].

(C.66)

Since
\mathbb{E}[y^{2}]=1
, this becomes

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{2}\mathring{f}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{x}^{2}}\left[(1+4b^{2})C_{3}+2b\,C_{4}\right]

(C.67)

with constant

\displaystyle C_{4}:=\mathbb{E}|z(1-2y^{2})|=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\left[1+\frac{4e^{-\frac{1}{4}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}-2\,\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]\approx 1.370.

(C.68)

In the original parameters (
b=r\sigma_{x}^{2}\sigma_{p}^{-1}
),

\displaystyle\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{f}\|_{L^{1}}=C_{3}\,\sigma_{p}^{-2},\qquad\|\partial_{x}^{2}\mathring{f}\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{3}\bigl(\sigma_{x}^{-2}+4r^{2}\sigma_{x}^{2}\sigma_{p}^{-2}\bigr)+2C_{4}\,r\,\sigma_{p}^{-1}.

(C.69)

Therefore

\displaystyle\|k_{3}-\tilde{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{2}-\tilde{f}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}

(C.70)

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{2}-\mathring{f}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}

(C.71)

\displaystyle=\frac{D}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\!ds\,\left(\|\partial_{x}^{2}\mathring{f}_{s}\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{f}_{s}\|_{L^{1}}\right),

(C.72)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t_{2}D}{2}\sup_{t\in[0,t_{2}]}\left(\|\partial_{x}^{2}\mathring{f}_{t}\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{f}_{t}\|_{L^{1}}\right)

(C.73)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})D}{2}\sup_{t\in[0,t_{2}]}\left[C_{3}(\sigma_{x}^{-2}+\sigma_{p}^{-2}+4r^{2}\sigma_{x}^{2}\sigma_{p}^{-2})+2C_{4}r\sigma_{p}^{-1}\right]

(C.74)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})D}{2}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,t_{2}]}\left[C_{3}(\sigma_{x}^{-2}+\sigma_{p}^{-2})\right]+\sup_{t\in[0,t_{2}]}\left[4C_{3}r^{2}\sigma_{x}^{2}\sigma_{p}^{-2}+2C_{4}r\sigma_{p}^{-1}\right]\right]

(C.75)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})D}{2}\left[C_{3}(h^{-1}e^{-2\tau_{1}}+h^{-1}e^{2\tau_{1}})+4C_{3}\frac{\tau_{2}^{2}}{1+2\tau_{2}^{2}}e^{4\tau_{1}}+2C_{4}\frac{\tau_{2}}{\sqrt{1+2\tau_{2}^{2}}}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\tau_{1}}\right]

(C.76)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})D}{he^{-2\tau_{1}}}\left[C_{3}\frac{e^{-4\tau_{1}}+1}{2}+2C_{3}\frac{\tau_{2}^{2}}{1+2\tau_{2}^{2}}he^{2\tau_{1}}+C_{4}\frac{\tau_{2}}{\sqrt{1+2\tau_{2}^{2}}}h^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\tau_{1}}\right]

(C.77)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})D}{he^{-2\tau_{1}}}\left[C_{3}\frac{1+4\tau_{2}^{2}}{1+2\tau_{2}^{2}}+C_{4}\frac{\tau_{2}}{\sqrt{1+2\tau_{2}^{2}}}\right]

(C.78)

In (
C.74
) we have used
b=r(t)\sigma_{x}^{2}\sigma_{p}^{-1}
and the bounds (
C.59
) and (
C.67
) on
\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{f}
and
\partial_{x}^{2}\mathring{f}
. (Recall that
b,r,\sigma_{x},\sigma_{p}
all have time dependence.)
In (
C.76
) we have used
r(t)=0
for
t\in[0,t_{1}]
and, for
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
,

r(t)=\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\chi_{2}(s)\,ds,\qquad\text{so that}\qquad 0\leq r(t)\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\chi_{2}(s)\,ds=\tau_{2}.

Thus the relevant supremands are maximized at the endpoints
r=0
(attained at
t=t_{1}
) or
r=\tau_{2}
(attained at
t=t_{2}
).
In (
C.78
) we use
h\leq 1
, and our technical assumption that
0<\tau_{1}<\tfrac{1}{4}\log h^{-1}
.

C.2.3
Bounding momentum error
\|\tilde{k}_{3}-\mathring{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}
during step 3

As for the Wigner function in the quantum case, the dynamics for the classical phase-space distribution during the third step separate into terms involving only
x
and only
p
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathring{f}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{(x)}_{3,H}[\mathring{f}]+\mathcal{L}^{(p)}_{3,H}[\mathring{f}],

(C.79)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{f}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{L}^{(x)}_{3,H}+\mathcal{L}^{(x)}_{D}\right)[\tilde{f}]+\left(\mathcal{L}^{(p)}_{3,H}+\mathcal{L}^{(p)}_{D}\right)[\tilde{f}],

(C.80)

Thus, for the same reasoning as Eqs. (
C.33
) and (
C.34
), we can write the dynamics for the classical momentum distributions
\tilde{k}(p):=\int\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\,\tilde{f}(x,p)
and
\mathring{k}(p):=\int\!\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\,\mathring{f}(x,p)
as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathring{k}

\displaystyle=-(1+p\partial_{p})\mathring{k},

(C.81)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{k}

\displaystyle=\left[\mathcal{L}^{(p)}_{D}-(1+p\partial_{p})\right]\tilde{k}

(C.82)

during the third time step. We use Duhamel and the fact that the stretching
p\mapsto e^{\tau_{3}}p
weakens
\partial_{p}^{2}
monotonically:

\displaystyle\|\tilde{k}_{3}-\mathring{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq(t_{3}-t_{2})\sup_{t\in[t_{2},t_{3}]}\left\|{\mathcal{L}}^{(p)}_{D}[\mathring{k}_{t}]\right\|_{L^{1}}

(C.83)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{3}D}{2}\,\sup_{t\in[t_{2},t_{3}]}\left\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{k}_{t}\right\|_{L^{1}}

(C.84)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{3}D}{2}\,\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{k}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}

(C.85)

\displaystyle=C_{5}\,\frac{\tau_{3}D}{2e^{-2\tau_{3}}},

(C.86)

where, similar to the quantum case, we use the fact that (
C.81
) is solved by just scaling the distribution at
t=t_{2}
, so that
\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}=e^{-2\tau_{3}}\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{k}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}
. Here we have defined

\displaystyle C_{5}:=\|\partial_{p}^{2}\mathring{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}\approx_{\tau}0.60002,

(C.87)

which is independent of
h
for our choice of parameters, as seen from the expression (
B.15
) for the final classical momentum distribution
\mathring{k}_{3}
.

C.2.4
Final classical bound

Inserting (
C.78
) and (
C.86
) into the triangle inequality (
C.52
)

\displaystyle\|k_{3}-\mathring{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|k_{3}-\tilde{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}+\|\tilde{k}_{3}-\mathring{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}

(C.88)

\displaystyle\leq\left[C_{3}\frac{1+4\tau_{2}^{2}}{1+2\tau_{2}^{2}}+C_{4}\frac{\tau_{2}}{\sqrt{1+2\tau_{2}^{2}}}\right]\frac{(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})D}{he^{-2\tau_{1}}}+\frac{1}{2}C_{5}\,\frac{\tau_{3}D}{e^{-2\tau_{3}}}

(C.89)

\displaystyle\leq_{\tau}C_{\mathrm{cl}}\frac{1+\log h^{-1}}{h^{\frac{4}{3}}}D

(C.90)

for positive constant

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{cl}}

\displaystyle:=C_{3}\frac{1+4\tau_{2}^{2}}{1+2\tau_{2}^{2}}+C_{4}\frac{\tau_{2}}{\sqrt{1+2\tau_{2}^{2}}}+\frac{1}{2}C_{5}\approx_{\tau}2.704.

(C.91)

Hence

\displaystyle\|k_{3}-\mathring{k}_{3}\|_{L^{1}}\to 0\quad\text{if}\quad D=o\!\left(\frac{h^{\frac{4}{3}}}{1+\log h^{-1}}\right),

(C.92)

matching the asymptotic quantum threshold (
C.45
).

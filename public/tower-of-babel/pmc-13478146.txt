Peedanil Gold attenuates IL-6-mediated articular pathologies in a collagen antibody-induced murine model of rheumatoid arthritis
Authors: Acharya Balkrishna, Sonam Sharma, Aakanksha Tiwari, Sandeep Sinha, Anurag Varshney
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionRheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a globally prevalent, chronic, systemic disorder of articular joints, with an auto-immune etiology, which eventually leads to physical disability. Peedanil Gold (PG), an Ayurvedic herbo-mineral medicine, has demonstrated anti-inflammatory activity in in vitro assays. Furthermore, it has also exhibited pharmacological effects in animal models of osteoarthritis and neuropathic pain. The present study was specifically designed to assess the preclinical anti-arthritic potential of Peedanil Gold in a well-established murine model of arthritis, induced by the administration of collagen antibodies.MethodsIn this study, BALB/c mice were intraperitoneally induced with a cocktail of five monoclonal antibodies against Type II collagen epitopes, followed by lipopolysaccharide. Peedanil Gold was administered as a gavage post-initiation of disease induction. Orally administered methotrexate (MTX) was used as a method control. Animals were humanely sacrificed on 13th day, post-antibody induction.ResultsCollagen antibodies led to the development of arthritis in animals, evident as swelling of the paws and ankles. Arthritis scoring of animals revealed that Peedanil Gold could alleviate the arthritic symptoms in a dose-related manner, with the highest dose demonstrating maximal pharmacological effects. Similarly, paw and ankle thicknesses were also reduced post-treatment with Peedanil Gold, signifying reduction in inflammatory changes in paw and ankle regions. Disease-modifying effects of Peedanil Gold were additionally substantiated by radiological observations of decreased soft tissue swelling and cartilage erosion with treatment. Furthermore, histopathological analysis of the knee and ankle demonstrated that treatment with Peedanil Gold could diminish the disease-evoked pathological alterations, such as bone degeneration and inflammatory cell infiltration. Peedanil Gold could also attenuate the elevated serum levels of interleukin 6 (IL-6).ConclusionThe outcomes of the present experiments demonstrate that Peedanil Gold has pharmacological effects in an animal model of rheumatoid arthritis, as evident by reduced arthritis score, alleviation of paw and ankle swelling, improvement in pathological lesions as observed radiologically and histopathologically. Consequently, it possesses preclinical potential for the management of rheumatoid arthritis, which warrants detailed investigations.

Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic autoimmune disorder characterized by affections of joints, which gradually degenerates the synovia and the periarticular structures, particularly those of the small articular regions of hands and feet. The global burden of RA was estimated to be 17.6 million in 2020 according to the Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study, 2021. It has been forecast that by 2050 there would be approximately 31.7 million people affected by RA globally (Black et al., 2023). Although the etiology of RA is not yet fully elucidated, it is considered a multi-causal disease influenced by the combination of genetic and epigenetic factors, encompassing smoking, obesity, air pollution, and dietary factors including high consumption of sodium, red meat, and iron (Deane et al., 2017). These risk factors trigger the conversion of proteins with arginine residues to citrulline by the action of peptidyl arginine deaminases (Darrah and Andrade, 2018). The citrullinated proteins are no longer recognized by the immune system as self-antigens and are presumably processed by antigen-presenting cells to initiate an immune response. The activation of B cells by T cells stimulates their differentiation into plasma cells that produce autoantibodies. These antibodies form immune complexes that get accumulated in the synovial fluid of joints and indirectly damage joint cartilage (Volkov et al., 2020). RA clinically manifests as arthralgia, joint effusion, enlarged boggy joints, joint stiffness, limited range of movement, and joint deformities. When left untreated, RA can affect other organs or organ systems in addition to the joints, such as skin, eyes, heart, lungs, kidneys, digestive system, and nervous system (Radu and Bungau, 2021).

The cornerstone of the early management of RA is the use of disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs) such as methotrexate (MTX), leflunomide, and sulfasalazine; nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs); corticosteroids; and biologic response modifiers such as tocilizumab, infliximab, and abatacept (Kayanaugh and Grevich, 2018). The first-line-option, DMARDs, modify disease progression but are associated with treatment-limiting adverse effects (Gao et al., 2024). NSAIDs, on the other hand, are associated with adverse gastrointestinal, cardiovascular, and renal effects, whereas corticosteroids can lead to osteoporosis, hypertension, and hyperglycemia (Patil et al., 2019). Moreover, NSAIDs and steroids may relieve pain and inflammation, but no clear changes are evident on radiological examination (Gao et al., 2024). Although biologics offer a personalized therapeutic approach, they are expensive and have limited accessibility in developing countries (Bergstra et al., 2018). Furthermore, they might also lead to increased propensity for the development of infections, malignancy, anaphylaxis, and autoimmune disorders (Feng and Chen, 2018). Owing to these adverse effects, new pharmacological approaches are being researched to find potential agents that target complete remission of the disease.

Peedanil Gold (PG) is a herbo-mineral Ayurvedic medicine intended for the treatment of chronic inflammatory disorders, including RA. The individual components of Peedanil Gold are elaborated in Table 1. These constituents have individually been used in the Indian systems of medicine for the treatment of several inflammatory conditions. Recently, in vitro experiments conducted in our laboratories determined that Peedanil Gold was able to inhibit the lipopolysaccharide-induced release of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) in the human monocytic leukemia cell line (THP1 cells), at cytosafe concentrations, thereby confirming its anti-inflammatory activity (Balkrishna et al., 2022). Furthermore, it also demonstrated pharmacological effects in animal models of monosodium iodoacetate-induced osteoarthritis (Balkrishna et al., 2022) and chronic constriction injury-induced neuropathic pain (Balkrishna et al., 2025). Since Peedanil Gold demonstrates inflammatory effects in both in vitro and in vivo studies, we decided to evaluate the potential pharmacological effects of PG in a murine model of collagen antibody-induced arthritis (CAIA) in the present study. A commercially procured cocktail of five monoclonal antibodies, which targets epitopes in type II collagen molecules and incites inflammation followed by destruction of joint cartilage structure in mice, was used to induce arthritis experimentally in BALB/c mice. Administration of the test compounds was started 4 days post-initiation of disease induction. Arthritis scores and paw and ankle thicknesses were recorded on the specified days to study the disease progression with and without treatment with the test compounds. In addition, serum levels of IL-6 were also quantified. The disease symptoms and the effect of the test compounds were also evaluated through radiological and histopathological findings. Methotrexate was used as a reference control drug to validate the experimental model.

Materials and methods

Test articles, chemicals, and reagents

PG (internal batch number: PRF/CHIN/0422/0289; commercial batch no.: #APGT220013) was sourced from Divya Pharmacy, Haridwar. Ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography of PG revealed that it is enriched with phytometabolites, namely, gallic acid, 5-(hydroxymethyl) furfural, protocatechuic acid, corilagin, ellagic acid, ferulic acid, cinnamic acid, guggulsterone E, and guggulsterone Z. The phytometabolite content was in line with the values reported in our previous published study (Balkrishna et al., 2022). We further confirm that PG, which is our test material, is endotoxin-free. The batch of Peedanil Gold, which was used in the present study, was analyzed for different analytical parameters, including microbial contaminants and pesticide residues. The microbial limit tests revealed that Peedanil Gold was free from microbial contamination as assessed for total aerobic bacterial count, as well as total yeast and mold count. More specifically, bacteria such as Escherichia coli, Staphylococcus aureus, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Salmonella sp. were absent in the analyzed batch. The sample was also assessed for approximately 48 pesticide residues, but none of them were detected in the tests conducted.

MTX (batch number: AT02073, brand name: Folitrax-10, Ipca laboratories. Ltd., India) was purchased from a local pharmacy. Methylcellulose (MC)-4000 CPS (Cat. # 04625), safranine (Cat. # 05625), hydrochloric acid (Cat. # 00171), glacial acetic acid (Cat # 00005), and Fast Green FCF (Cat. # 03800) were bought from Loba Chemie Pvt. Ltd. (Maharashtra, India). Collagen antibody (CA) (Arthrogen-CIA 5-Clone Cocktail Kit) (Cat. # 53100) was procured from Chondrex Inc., United States. Hematoxylin (Cat. # H9627) was purchased from Sigma Aldrich, United States, whereas eosin yellow (Cat. # GRM1060) and ferric chloride (Cat. # GRM1379) were procured from HiMedia, India. Deionized water was obtained from a Milli Q system (Millipore, United States).

Thiopentone sodium injection (Lot number: 173277, brand name: Thiosol) and isoflurane (Lot number: KPNP699030, brand name: Sosrane) were procured from Neon Laboratories Ltd., India.

Standard pelleted animal diet (5L79; 18% protein), manufactured by Purina LabDiet (MO, United States), was purchased from Hylasco Biotechnology (India) Pvt. Ltd. (Telangana, India). The animal bedding material, Sparcobb™, was purchased from Sparconn Life Sciences (Karnataka, India).

Animal husbandry, development of the mouse model of collagen antibody-induced arthritis, and test article administration

Specific pathogen-free, male BALB/c mice of 5–6 weeks of age were procured from Hylasco Bio-Technology Pvt. Ltd., Hyderabad, Telangana, India, a technology licensee of Charles River Laboratories (United States). On arrival, the animals were duly examined for the presence of any abnormalities and quarantined for a week. After completion of the quarantine period, the mice were shifted to an experimental room where they were housed in polypropylene cages at 23 °C ± 2 °C and 60%–70% relative humidity, with 12-h light/dark cycle, throughout the study period. The animals were offered a gamma-irradiated standard pelleted laboratory animal diet ad libitum. Reverse osmosis (Merck, India) water was provided ad libitum in autoclaved polypropylene water sippers. After completion of the quarantine period, the mice were weighed. The animals were then randomized to different experimental groups according to their body weight by employing randomization within the blocks strategy using a Microsoft Excel sheet. Such a randomization procedure ensured that there were no significant differences between the average body weights of the animals of each experimental group. The sample size used in the present study is according to the recommendation of the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee of Patanjali Research Foundation as this is the first study conducted to assess the pharmacological effects of PG in a collagen antibody-induced arthritis model. In addition, the similar sample size has been used by other researchers in the same disease model (Ahmad et al., 2017). The present study has been reported according to the ARRIVE (Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments) guidelines (Percie du Sert et al., 2020), and all experimental procedures and husbandry practices were in compliance with the guidelines prescribed by the Committee for Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals, Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying, Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying, Government of India (Department of Animal husbandry and Dairying, Ministry of Animal Husbandry, Dairying and Fisheries, Government of India, 2018). The experimental protocol was reviewed and subsequently approved by the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee of Patanjali Research Foundation before the initiation of the study, with approval number PRIAS/LAF/IAEC-176.

The recommended therapeutic dose of PG for the management of inflammatory diseases, including RA, is 4 tablets/day i.e., 2,160 mg/day (weight of one tablet = 540 mg). Peedanil Gold is prescribed to be taken in divided doses of two tablets, twice a day, in the morning and evening, respectively. Accordingly, the human equivalent dose (HED) of PG for a human of average body weight of 60 kg has been computed to be 36 mg/kg/day. The equivalent dose (in mg/kg) for mice was calculated by extrapolating the HED to animals by multiplying it with a factor of 12.3 (Nair and Jacob, 2016). The resultant therapeutic dose for mice was hence calculated to be 442.8 mg/kg/day, which was rounded off to 450 mg/kg/day (or 225 mg/kg, twice daily) and considered the mouse equivalent of the prescribed human dose, for the in vivo study. The other selected doses for the study were 67.5 and 675 mg/kg, administered twice daily, to capture a probable dose–response relationship. Schematic diagram of collagen antibody-induced arthritis in a mouse experimental model. Subsequent to quarantine and acclimatization, animals were administered collagen antibodies: 1.5 mg/animal, intraperitoneally on day 0. On day 3, animals were subjected to LPS administration: 50 µg/animal, intraperitoneally. From day 4 onward, animals in normal control and disease control groups were administered 0.5% MC orally, b.i.d. Other groups of animals were given methotrexate (0.38 mg/kg p.o., q.a.d.) and PG (67.5, 225, and 675 mg/kg, p.o., b.i.d., respectively). There were six animals in each group. Subsequently, arthritis scoring was recorded every alternate day, and paw and ankle thickness was measured using a digital Vernier calliper on days 0, 2, 5, 9, and 13. On day 13, the animals were transiently anaesthetized with isoflurane, and radiographs were acquired. Subsequently, the hind limb of the animals was photographed for gross pathological evaluation. Thereafter, mice were humanely sacrificed; blood was collected for cytokine analysis in serum, and hind limbs were excised for histopathological analysis of paw and ankle.

After the completion of the acclimatization period of 4 days, the pre-induction readings for the arthritis score, paw thickness, and ankle thickness were measured. Thereafter, arthritis was induced in animals allocated to groups G2 to G6 on day 0 via an intraperitoneal injection of the collagen antibody (Arthrogen-CIA 5-Clone Cocktail Kit, Chondrex Inc., United States), which is a cocktail of five monoclonal antibodies to type II collagen. In brief, 0.3 mL (1.5 mg/animal) of a 5 mg/mL solution of the collagen antibody, dissolved in phosphate buffered saline (PBS), was injected intraperitoneally on day 0. This was followed by an intraperitoneal injection of 0.3 mL (50 μg/animal) of 166.67 µg/mL solution of lipopolysaccharide (LPS from the Escherichia coli strain 0111: B4, provided along with the antibody cocktail kit) dissolved in sterile normal saline on day 3. The animals assigned to the G1 group received an equal volume of PBS on day 0 and equal volume of sterile normal saline on day 3 via an intraperitoneal route.

The oral administration of vehicle/MTX/PG was commenced from day 4 after disease induction and continued throughout the study until day 12. The animals received compounds orally in the dose volume of 10 mL/kg body weight.

Evaluation of the severity of arthritis

The severity of arthritis was determined every alternative day using a semi-quantitative visual scoring system on days 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, and 12 by minor modification of a previously reported study (Moore et al., 2014). All four limbs were individually scored on a scale of 0–4 using the following semi-quantitative scoring criteria: 0 = normal; 1 = mild redness and slight swelling of ankle or wrist; 2 = moderate swelling of ankle or wrist; 3 = severe swelling, including some digits, ankle, and foot; 4 = maximally inflamed ankle and foot. The average of scores of all the four limbs was evaluated and then represented graphically.

Measurement of paw and ankle thicknesses

The paw and ankle thicknesses of hind limbs were measured on days 0, 2, 5, 9, and 13 post-disease induction using a digital Vernier calliper (Mitutoyo Corporation, Japan). The paw and ankle edema on different days were calculated by subtracting the day 0 thickness from the thickness recorded on different days of the study. Thereafter, the scores obtained in both the hind limbs were averaged and plotted graphically.

Radiological analysis

On day 13 of the study, the changes in the osteo-morphology of the hind limb and the associated soft tissue swelling of animals were analyzed by X-ray imaging using a portable X-ray device (VEX-P300, Ez Ray Air Portable X-ray System, Vatech Co., Ltd., Korea). For this evaluation, the animals were transiently anaesthetized with isoflurane. The radiographs were analyzed by an expert veterinary radiologist in a blinded manner who was unaware of the treatment groups. The scoring was done on the basis of cartilage erosion and soft tissue swelling. The observations in the animals of different groups were scored as follows: 0 = normal; 1 = mild; 2 = moderate; and 3 = severe, and plotted graphically. The radiological findings were peer-reviewed by an external veterinarian.

Necropsy, serum collection, and cytokine analysis

After radiological imaging, the photographs of the hind limbs were acquired using a digital camera under transient isoflurane anesthesia. Thereafter, the mice were humanely sacrificed under overdose of thiopentone sodium (150 mg/kg) administered intraperitoneally. Before the animal succumbed to thiopentone overdose, blood was withdrawn from the retro-orbital plexus. Subsequently, the blood samples were allowed to clot completely at room temperature and then centrifuged (Sorvall ST 8R, ThermoFisher Scientific, United States) at 2000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, following which the serum was separated. Post-euthanasia, both hind limbs of animals were excised and collected in 10% neutral buffered formalin for histological analysis of the knee and ankle.

IL-6 levels in serum (25 µL) were measured using a ProcartaPlex™ immune assay kit (Assay ID: PPX-08-MXDJZVD; ThermoFisher Scientific, United States) on a Luminex™ 200™ Instrument System (ThermoFisher Scientific, United States) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The cytokine analysis was conducted in a blinded manner at the Institute of Applied Biological Research and Development, Pune, India, through ThermoFisher Scientific, India.

Histopathology

The collected hind limb was transferred to 10% neutral buffered formalin-filled containers and kept for fixation for a day. Following fixation, the tissues were transferred to a 5% formic acid solution for decalcification of the bony tissue for 7 days. Thereafter, the tissues were washed under running tap water, processed using a tissue processor (Leica Biosystems; TP 1020; India), and subsequently embedded in paraffin wax by using embedding station (Leica Biosystems; Histocore Arcadia H-C; India). Tissue sections of 3–5 µm were sliced using a microtome (Leica Biosystems; RM 2245; India), mounted on glass slides, deparaffinized, and stained with hematoxylin–eosin (H&E) stain.

For Safranin-O staining of the bone tissues, the processing was similar to that used for staining with H&E. The deparaffinized slides were stained with Weigert’s iron hematoxylin solution for 10 min. After washing with tap water, the slides were stained with Fast Green solution for 5 min. Thereafter, after quickly rinsing it with 1% acetic acid, the slides were stained with 0.1% Safranin-O for 5 min. Then, the sections were visualized after dehydrating and clearing with alcohol and xylene.

The sections of bilateral knee and ankles were subjected to the histological assessment, and photomicrographs were acquired on an upright microscope (Eclipse Ci, Nikon, Japan), under bright-field condition. Histopathological evaluation and acquisition of photomicrographs were performed at Innovet Diagnostic Laboratory (Pune, India) by a trained veterinary pathologist, in a blinded manner, and were subsequently peer-reviewed by another veterinary pathologist. The assessed histopathological lesions in H&E-stained tissues included bony degeneration and inflammatory changes with mononuclear cell (MNC) infiltration in the sub-cutis region and around joint (below dermal layer) and degenerative changes in cartilage/bony tissue of joints in Safranin-O stained slides. Both the H&E- and Safranin-O-stained tissue sections were scored using a semi-quantitative grading system, in which 0 denotes normal architecture of the tissue, 1 stands for minimal alterations, 2 for mild alterations, 3 signifies moderate alterations, and 4 represents severe alterations. For H&E-stained tissue samples, the individual scores of each animal for bone degeneration and MNC infiltration were added to obtain the total lesion score.

Statistical analysis

The data are expressed as mean ± standard error of mean (SEM) for all groups, and statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism Version 8.0.2 (GraphPad Software, United States). The arthritic score and data on changes in paw and ankle thicknesses were analyzed using the two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s multiple-comparisons test. The data for percentage inhibition, area under the curve (AUC), cytokine analysis, radiology, and histopathology were analyzed using the one-way ANOVA followed by Dunnett’s multiple-comparisons test. The data distribution was observed to be Gaussian in nature, and the variance homogeneity was ascertained using the Brown–Forsythe test. Data were analyzed by measuring degree of freedom (DF) within (DFn) and among the groups (DFd), along with probability (p) values. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Peedanil Gold reduced severity of collagen antibody-induced arthritis and reduced serum levels of IL-6

Induction with collagen antibody cocktail in mice resulted in the development of signs of arthritis, characterized by redness and swelling of paw, ankle, and digits. As evident from arthritis scoring data, the animals in the DC group started demonstrating signs of disease from day 6 (Figure 2A; p < 0.05) onward, when compared to the NC group. The maximal arthritis score was observed on day 10 (Figure 1A; p < 0.01), after which it reached a plateau as noted on day 12 (Figure 2A; p < 0.01). The MTX treatment group demonstrated a significant reduction in the swelling and redness on days 8 (Figure 2A; p < 0.05), as well as on days 10 and 12 (Figure 2A; p < 0.01), when compared to the DC group, thereby validating the model. Similarly, compared to the DC group, all the three tested doses of PG significantly reduced the arthritis-related signs in a dose-related manner, with significant effects noted on days 8, 10, and 12 (Figure 2A; p < 0.01). In addition, the AUC of the arthritis score curve was significantly greater in animals allocated to the DC group than in the NC group (Figure 2B; p < 0.05). The reference control, MTX, tended to reduce the AUC, when compared to the DC group (Figure 2B). A significant decrease in AUC among the PG-treated groups was evident at the dose of 675 mg/kg, b.i.d. (Figure 2B; p < 0.05), when compared to the DC group. In addition, we also calculated the percentage inhibition of the arthritis score on day 12, which demonstrated that MTX and all the three tested doses of PG had significant inhibitory effects, when compared to the DC group (Figure 2C; p < 0.01).

Intraperitoneal administration of the collagen antibody to mice resulted in a significant elevation of the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-6 in the serum, when compared to the NC group. MTX was able to decrease the cytokine level to significantly lower levels (Figure 2D; p < 0.01). Treatment with graded doses of PG reduced the serum IL-6 levels in a dose-related fashion, with statistical significance observed at all the tested doses (Figure 2D; p < 0.01) relative to the DC group (Figure 2D).

This observed swelling in the paw and ankle was quantified by recording the change in paw and ankle thicknesses during the experimental duration, as specified in the Materials and Methods section.

The change in paw thickness in animals allocated to the DC group was found to be significantly greater than that in the animals allocated to the NC group on days 9 and 13, respectively (Figure 4A; p < 0.01). Analysis of the percentage inhibition of paw swelling data, calculated on day 13, revealed that MTX tended to exert an inhibitory effect. PG demonstrated dose-related inhibition, with significant effects observed at the dose of 225 mg/kg, b.i.d. (Figure 4C; p < 0.05), and 675 mg/kg, b.i.d. (Figure 4C; p < 0.01).

Peedanil Gold alleviated collagen antibody-induced radiological manifestations of arthritis

Upon radiological assessment, the animals allocated to the NC group demonstrated normal bone and joint architecture in both the knee and ankles (Figure 5A). Discussion

RA is a complex clinical entity involving a variety of pathogenic pathways, including the development of autoantibodies, various inflammatory cascades, genetics and epigenetics, and immune-metabolomics (Gao et al., 2024). This leads to intense spell of stiffness of joints usually during morning hours, causing immobility of the affected joints, which manifests as tenderness, swelling, and pain in the area. This disease condition can affect any joint, but it is most frequently observed in the metacarpophalangeal, proximal interphalangeal, metatarsophalangeal, wrist, and knee. The degeneration of the extracellular matrix is the hallmark of RA, which causes the combined degradation of the bone, tendons, ligaments, and cartilages. Its pathogenesis involves a diverse set of cellular responses, such as stimulation of inflammatory cells, expression of different cytokines and growth factors, and localized angiogenesis (Zhao et al., 2025). The inflammation of the synovial area, along with hyperplasia, leads to destructive arthropathy.

Keeping in view the pathogenesis of the disease and the anti-inflammatory Ayurvedic constituents of PG, a collagen antibody-induced arthritis model was established in mice, and PG was evaluated for its pharmacological effects in the present study. The components of PG include fine powders of classical Ayurvedic medicines, including Amvatari Ras, Mahavat Vidhvansan Ras, Mukta Shukti Bhasma, Punarnavadi Mandoor, Vrihatvat Chintamani Ras, and the resin of Guggul (Commiphora mukul) (Table 1). The well-established anti-arthritic effects of the individual components of PG, derived from their traditional use in Indian systems of medicine, encouraged us to evaluate its preclinical anti-arthritic potential. Amvatari Ras, an important component of PG, is widely indicated for the management of auto-immune diseases, which can be attributed to the presence of botanical drugs such as Terminalia chebula, Terminalia bellirica, Emblica officinalis, Plumbago zeylanica, Commiphora mukul, and Ricinus communis, as well as herbally processed and purified mineral components (Balkrishna et al., 2019). Guggul contains bioactive volatile oils, gum resin, guggulipids, guggulsterones, guggulsterols, mukolol, and other steroids. Oleoresin of Guggul is documented to be a potent anti-inflammatory and an anti-arthritic agent and is indicated for use in RA (Amavata), along with other Ayurvedic formulations (Singh et al., 2015).

The CAIA model utilizes a cocktail of five monoclonal antibodies against the epitopes present on Type II collagen. The cocktail of five monoclonal antibodies, manufactured by Chondrex, comprises clones A2-10 (IgG2a), F10-21(IgG2a), D8-6 (IgG2a), D1-2G (IgG2b), and D2-112 (IgG2b). According to the information provided by the manufacturer, A2-10, D1-2G, and D2-112 bind to the specific epitope clustered within the LyC1 peptide of the CB11 fragment, whereas clones F10-21 and D8-6 bind to the specific epitope clustered within the LyC2 peptide of the same fragment (Arthrogen-CIA®, 2022). After their binding to the specific epitopes, immune complexes are formed and get deposited in the synovial and cartilaginous spaces. This further initiates the activation of the monocytes, liberation of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and accumulation of neutrophils and macrophages to the inflammation site. CAIA is known to develop independent of the MHC-restricted T cells and, by this means, also unrelated to T-cell-dependent B cells. This makes it different from the collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) model, which is dependent on the MHC alleles (Nandakumar et al., 2004). The CAIA model followed in the present study also involves the intraperitoneal administration of LPS (a bacterial endotoxin) after the administration of the collagen antibody to increase the incidence and severity of the disease (Khachigian, 2006). LPS tends to decrease the arthritogenic dose of the inducing monoclonal antibodies. Therefore, this model helps in disease induction in a much shorter time duration than the CIA model. Earlier, many researchers have used this model with a 4-clone antibody cocktail, but it is well researched and established that the new 5-clone antibody cocktail is more robust and exhibits approximately twice the arthritogenecity of the former (Hutamekalin et al., 2009). However, there are translational limitations associated with the CAIA model. It does not exactly mimic clinical rheumatoid arthritis when it comes to adaptive immune response as human RA has a long pre-clinical phase involving the loss of immune tolerance, production of autoantibodies (RFs and ACPAs), and activation of autoreactive immune cells. In contrast, in CAIA, antibodies are directly administered, thereby bypassing the adaptive immune mechanism. Moreover, CAIA is antibody-mediated, while human RA is mostly T-cell-dependent. However, CAIA serves as one of the models for RA as it addresses the acute inflammatory phase of arthritis. It mimics innate immune responses, joint inflammation, and immune-complex-mediated destructive changes (Hashida et al., 2021).

In the present study, the arthritis scoring was done every alternative day for evaluation of the progression of the disease symptoms; however, after day 12, the disease progression seemed to limit, and therefore, necropsy of the animals was conducted on day 13. The arthritis scoring data were recorded for up to day 12, whereas on day 13, after measuring the paw and ankle thicknesses, the animals underwent necropsy. PG significantly improved the arthritic symptoms as monitored through the arthritis score and paw and ankle thicknesses. The arthritis score was observed to increase up to day 10, but thereafter it plateaued. The same finding was also observed by other researchers, some of whom reported a decrease in disease progression even from days 6 (Ishizaki et al., 2011; Jin et al., 2019), 7 (Kim et al., 2018; Okano et al., 2015), 9 (Nandakumar and Holmdahl, 2007), and 10 (Koobkokkruad et al., 2011). The signs of inflammation as swelling and redness were also perceived grossly in the paw region. It is a well-documented fact that Type II collagen immunity is concerned with the development of inflammation in the articular joints (Kim et al., 2004; Wu et al., 2021). Paw and ankle thicknesses started increasing after the CA administration and reached its peak at day 9 and thereafter started decreasing. The ankle thickness remained almost at the same level in the PG 675 mg/kg, b. i.d dose group from day 5 to day 13, and the paw thickness in the same group leveled up to nearly the same level as that of the normal control group.

X-ray radiography was helpful in further establishing the effect of PG against experimental arthritis. Radiologically, it was evident that PG mitigated the arthritic symptoms, i.e., cartilage erosion and soft tissue swelling in a dose-related fashion. Both the parameters were significantly elevated in the animals of the disease control group. A similar finding of joint damage post-CA administration has also been demonstrated earlier (Okano et al., 2015). Similar to our study, the scoring of bone destruction was also carried out to study the radiographs of the CA-affected joints and thereafter to analyze the anti-arthritic potential of an EP-4 (prostaglandin E2 receptor 4) receptor antagonist (Bender et al., 2013).

The recognition of self and non-self joint-related antigens appears to trigger numerous inflammatory reactions mediated by various cytokines, such as tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), IL-6, IL-1β, and interferon-γ (IFN-γ), in individuals affected by RA (Kasama et al., 2005). IL-6 has been documented as the major inflammatory cytokine involved in the pathogenesis of RA, related to the production of acute phase proteins (Jang et al., 2022). The collagen antibody-induced increase in IL-6 expression was inhibited on treatment with PG, which signifies the obliteration of the inflammatory activity produced by the collagen antibody, thereby effectively acting against the arthritic changes. Similar results were also obtained in the study in which different cytokines responsible for inflammatory reactions were examined, including IL-6 in collagen-induced arthritis (Okano et al., 2015; Zhang, 2021).

Similarly, inflammatory reaction was witnessed as infiltration of the inflammatory cells when observed histopathologically in H&E-stained tissue sections of the induced animals. The severity of the CAIA model is clearly documented as erosive type, showing the presence of excessive inflammatory cell influx in the synovial space and degeneration of the bony and cartilaginous tissues (Moore et al., 2014; Sim et al., 2016). This finding corroborates with our histopathological observations, in which the inflammatory cell infiltration and degeneration of the bone and cartilage are clearly indicated in H&E- and Safranin-O-stained tissues.

PG is enriched with bio-active phytometabolites, namely, gallic acid, 5-(hydroxymethyl) furfural, protocatechuic acid, corilagin, ellagic acid, ferulic acid, cinnamic acid, guggulsterone E, and guggulsterone Z, as detected through ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) and reported in our previous studies (Balkrishna et al., 2022; Balkrishna et al., 2025). Among these, gallic acid is well known for its anti-inflammatory activity. It has been mentioned that gallic acid is capable of reducing the local inflammatory response in RA by affecting the activity of certain cytokines and enzymes (Bai et al., 2021). 5-(Hydroxymethyl) furfural is recognized and authenticated to exhibit its anti-inflammatory property by hindering the production of inflammatory cytokines and reactive oxygen species (Kong et al., 2019). Protocatechuic acid, a naturally occurring phenolic compound is known to possess several pharmacological properties, such as anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antiseptic effects, which has also been well established in different pharmacological animal models (Kakkar and Bais, 2014). Corilagin (Liu et al., 2022), ellagic acid (Paruvu et al., 2025), and ferulic acid (Li et al., 2021) are also recognized to have an anti-arthritic effect by virtue of their anti-inflammatory and anti-oxidant properties. Cinnamic acid and its derivatives have been illustrated to exhibit their anti-inflammatory property by inhibiting the stimulation of Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) and nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) signaling pathways, which then leads to subsided release of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-6 (Tian et al., 2025). Guggulsterone E and Z are phytosteroids present in gum resin of Commiphora mukul. Several studies have been conducted previously that confirm the anti-inflammatory and anti-arthritic potential of guggulsterones (Sarup et al., 2015). Collectively, the scientific literature suggests that the phytocompounds detected in PG possess anti-inflammatory activities relevant to the pathogenesis of RA. As a corollary, it can be assumed that these phytometabolites detected in PG could have contributed to the pharmacological effects reported in the present study. Nevertheless, it is challenging to determine the main phytometabolite, which might have elicited the observed pharmacological activity. However, there is a considerable probability that the detected phytometabolites present in PG might have rather exerted their observed effects in either an additive or a synergistic fashion. Accordingly, future studies are warranted for elucidating the mode of action of PG in ameliorating the symptoms of RA.

The present study has some limitations that should be clearly stated. Although the pathogenesis of rheumatoid arthritis is a multifactorial inflammatory process driven by multiple mediators, our study design was designed to evaluate the pharmacological effects of Peedanil Gold on arthritis severity, paw and ankle swelling, and radiological and histopathological aberrations in the knee and ankles of the animals, and an inflammatory cytokine (IL-6). Consequently, additional experiments could be conducted in the future to establish the detailed mechanism of action of Peedanil Gold, for instance, by using various omics platforms and spatial transcriptomics. In addition, another recognized limitation is the absence of pharmacokinetic data, which is necessary for determining correlation between pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamic effects. However, since Peedanil Gold contains many botanical drugs and mineral components, it is rather challenging as such formulations contain chemically different phytometabolites, often in low quantities, affecting the level of detections in available analytical technologies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the present study demonstrates that Peedanil Gold ameliorated collagen antibody-induced arthritis severity, along with paw and ankle swelling. It also reduced elevated serum levels of IL-6 and decreased radiological and histopathological lesions. Taken together, the findings of the present study suggest that PG possesses pharmacological effects in an animal model of rheumatoid arthritis, which warrants further preclinical and clinical evaluation. Based on the results obtained in the present study, future in vivo studies could be designed, in which the objective could be delineation of mechanism of action of Peedanil Gold. In addition, the pharmacological effects of Peedanil Gold could be directly compared with methotrexate by incorporating a study design that evaluates dose–responses of both the test articles, in parallel. Furthermore, detailed non-clinical safety evaluations of Peedanil Gold in rodents and non-rodents are also warranted in conformance with present regulatory guidelines. The pharmacological effects of Peedanil Gold reported in the present study, along with its mechanistic and safety assessments, could provide a basis for the detailed investigation of PG in patients with RA under controlled clinical trials, either as an individual or as an adjunct therapy to the standards of care for RA.

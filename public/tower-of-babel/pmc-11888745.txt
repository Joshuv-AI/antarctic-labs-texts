Comparative Evaluation of the Efficiency in Retrieving Separated Reciprocating Instruments Using Three Different File Retrieval Systems in Maxillary First Molars: An In Vitro Study
Authors: Shahana Shajahan, C Dhanavel, S Vijaya Raja, M Sornamalar, G Balavaishnavi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:The aim of this study was to compare and evaluate the retrieval time, efficiency, and loss of dentin volume after separated instrument retrieval using three different file retrieval systems in the middle and apical thirds of the mesio-buccal root of maxillary first molars.Material and Methods:Sixty maxillary first molars were selected. Group 1: Endo Rescue Apex, Group 2: Ultrasound apex, Group 3: Broken instrument remover apex, Group 4: Endo Rescue Middle, Group 5: Ultrasound Middle, Group 6: Broken instrument Remover middle were used to retrieve the fractured reciprocating files from the root canals.Results:The results showed that the BTR pen system was successful in removing the majority of the files, with success rates of 60%. The BTR-Pen system achieved a 30% success rate, successfully removing 3 separated instruments from the middle third under a dental operating microscope within the time span. According to the statistical analysis of the dentinal thickness lost during retrieval of separated reciprocating instruments, BTR pens showed less amount of dentine loss than the ultrasonic and Endo rescue.Conclusion:The findings may guide further research on BTR’s effectiveness in retrieving separated instruments, with additional clinical trials ongoing.

INTRODUCTION

Retrieving separated instruments is a challenging aspect of endodontics, with success influenced by factors such as instrument visibility, length, canal curvature, and retrieval techniques. The presence of a separated instrument can hinder proper root canal disinfection, impacting treatment prognosis. Recent advancements in root canal preparation include a shift from rotary to reciprocating files, with single-file reciprocating systems suggested for their efficiency, reduced time, and lower risk of instrument separation and dentinal engagement. Despite these advantages, literature shows no significant difference in NiTi instrument fractures between reciprocating and rotary motions, and there is limited research on the retrieval of fractured reciprocating files.[123]

Retrieving separated instruments is a challenging aspect of endodontics, with success influenced by factors such as instrument visibility, length, canal curvature, and retrieval techniques. A separated instrument can hinder proper root canal disinfection, impacting treatment prognosis. Dental operating microscopes (DOM) provide higher and adjustable magnification and help to locate and identify the separated instruments, making them more practically visible.[4] The DOM allows endodontists to do minimally invasive operations by accessing the canal space conservatively and reducing the removal of healthy dentin.[5] Micro Computed Tomography is used to measure dentine thickness. Images collected from different slices with multiple viewing angles are reconstructed to produce three-dimensional (3D) images.[6] Many studies have shown that the separation of fractured endodontic instruments occurs mostly in curved and narrow root canal systems, such as the mesiobuccal root canal of maxillary molars or the mesial root canal of mandibular molars.[7]

Identifying the most effective retrieval system aims to improve clinical protocols, reduce treatment times, and enhance the success rates of endodontic procedures, ultimately improving patient outcomes. The primary focus is on the somewhat curved root canals located in the mesiobuccal root of the maxillary first molar. This study assessed and compared the retrieval time and ease of removal of fractured reciprocating files from the mesiobuccal root using the Komet Endo Rescue (trephine bur), BTR Pen (loop system), and I Super Tip (ultrasonic tips). Additionally, the volume of dentin removed was compared between the systems using micro-CT scans. The aim is to compare and evaluate the time, efficiency, and loss of dentin volume after separated instrument retrieval using three different file retrieval systems in the middle- and apical-thirds of the mesiobuccal root of the maxillary first molar.

MATERIALS AND METHOD

The invitro multicentric experimental study was conducted in the Department of Conservative Dentistry and Endodontics, Indira Gandhi Institute of Dental Sciences, Puducherry. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board and Institutional Ethical Committee with QR CODE (IGIDSIEC2022NRP25PGSSCDE) at Indira Gandhi Institute of Dental Sciences on 10-08-2022.

Sample selection

Samples were collected from various clinics across Tamil Nadu and Pondicherry, India. Preoperative and postoperative micro-CT scans were taken at Sastra University, CENTAB lab, Thanjavur. Patient consent was obtained before extraction. Extracted teeth were washed under running tap water immediately and stored in 0.9% normal saline. All collected samples met the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The inclusion criteria were: i) extracted permanent maxillary molars for periodontal reasons and grossly decayed teeth, ii) teeth with only one mesiobuccal canal present and without calcifications in the root canal, iii) Teeth with no incidence of previous endodontic treatment, and iv) teeth with mature root apex and with no root resorption. The exclusion criteria were: calcified teeth, ii) teeth separated coronally and radically, and iii) teeth with root fracture.

Pre-operative procedures

Sixty maxillary first molars with complete root apices were selected, stored in normal saline, and continuously hydrated. Preoperative radiographs confirmed no prior endodontic treatment or anatomical variations, with teeth selected for having one mesiobuccal canal, Type I Vertucci’s canal, and moderate root curvature. De-coronation of the mesiobuccal root was performed with a diamond cutting disc, and roots were examined for preexisting cracks under a dental operating microscope. Patency was established using #10/#15 K files, and working length was determined. Standard irrigation protocols were followed. Teeth were divided into six groups: Groups 1–3 used a primary (yellow 25/07) reciprocating WaveOne file cut at 4 mm from the apex, while Groups 4–6 used a primary (yellow 25/07) WaveOne Gold file cut at 8 mm from the apex. Files were intentionally separated in the middle and apical thirds of the root, and the fractured instrument location was confirmed with radiographs. Preoperative micro-CT scans provided dentin thickness readings at 4 mm and 8 mm from the apex along various canal walls.

Samples were coronally enlarged using GG or modified GG drills. Separated instruments were retrieved with the six groups following the manufacturer’s protocols. The time for each system was recorded from initiation to complete removal, with a standard limit of 60 minutes. Post-retrieval micro-CT scans were performed to assess dentinal volume loss [Figure 1]. Groups 1 and 2 were excluded from post-operative micro-CT analysis due to failure in retrieving the instruments.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was done using SPSS software version 25. One-way ANOVA and post hoc tests were done in the present study. If the analysis of variance yields a significant result, post-hoc testing can be adopted to identify specific differences between the groups.

DISCUSSION

Managing a separated instrument in a root canal is challenging but crucial for the long-term success of endodontic treatment. A time limit of 60 minutes was set for instrument removal to ensure effective use of each system. Suter et al.[8] suggested a 45- to 60-minute timeframe, as longer durations could reduce success rates due to operator fatigue, secondary fractures, or excessive dentin removal. Samples not retrieved within 60 minutes were classified as retrieval failures in the study.

Out of 60 samples, 20 separated instruments were successfully retrieved. In the apex group, neither the Endo Rescue nor ultrasonic tips were successful, resulting in a 100% failure rate, while the BTR Pen had a 30% success rate, retrieving 3 instruments. In the middle and apex group, the Endo Rescue retrieved 2 out of 10 samples, the ultrasonic tip retrieved 8 out of 10, and the BTR Pen retrieved 7 out of 10, with success rates of 20%, 80%, and 70%, respectively.

In this study, treatment time was measured from the start of trephining around the fragment to its successful removal or time limit expiration. The Endo Rescue (ER) group required 40 minutes to 1 hour, the ultrasonic (US) group required 35 minutes to over 1 hour, and the BTR Pen took 30 minutes to 1 hour, with the BTR Pen being the fastest. Gencoglu and Helvacioglu (2009)[9] found that fragment location and root canal shape affect retrieval success, and ultrasonics, when used with an operating microscope, were particularly effective.

Statistical micro CT analysis revealed that the BTR Pen group, in both apex and middle categories, experienced less dentin loss compared to the ultrasonic and Endo Rescue groups. The BTR Pen also demonstrated higher retrieval efficiency, with statistically significant differences noted. This was due to the BTR Pen’s effectiveness in removing most files. In contrast, a study by Shaik et al. (2022)[10] found no significant difference between devices, concluding that the dentist’s skill was more crucial for successful retrieval than the magnifying devices used.

The process of removing a broken instrument that is short in the canal can be difficult.[11] For the above-mentioned investigation, the length of the separated instrument was chosen to be 4 mm and 8 mm, which is in line with previous research.[12] It should be noted, however, that the study did not evaluate the impact that fragment length had on the retrieval process. On the other hand, the BTR-Pen system technique suggests that it could be easier to extract a longer chunk. Therefore, we employed the identical method in our investigation for endodontic instruments with separations of 4 mm and 8 mm, and it yielded successful results. The study standardized test settings and controlled variables but did not fully replicate clinical conditions, as the coronal tooth structure was removed for clear access, unlike real scenarios with coronal interference. This discrepancy affected success rates. Despite these limitations, the findings can inform further research on the BTR-Pen’s effectiveness in retrieving separated instruments from the middle-third, with additional clinical trials currently underway.

CONCLUSION

The research aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of three methods for retrieving fractured reciprocating instruments from the middle and apical thirds of the mesiobuccal root of maxillary third molars. The BTR pen system showed the highest success rates, with 30% and 60% in the apical and middle thirds, respectively, under DOM. The other methods, including ultrasonic tips and the Endo rescue kit, followed in descending order of effectiveness. Additional in-vivo human trials are needed to validate these findings.

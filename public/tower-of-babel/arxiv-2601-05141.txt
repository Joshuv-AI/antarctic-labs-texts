Superluminal modes in a quantum field simulator for cosmology from analog Transplanckian physics
Christian F. Schmidt, Stefan Floerchinger

Abstract
The quantum-field-theoretic description for the U(1)-Goldstone boson of a scalar Bose-Einstein condensate with time-dependent contact interactions is developed beyond the acoustic approximation in accordance with Bogoliubov theory. The resulting effective action is mapped to a relativistic quantum field theory on a dispersive (or rainbow) cosmological spacetime which has a superluminal Corley-Jacobson dispersion relation. Time-dependent changes of the s-wave scattering length to quantum-simulate cosmological particle production are accompanied by a time-dependent healing length that can be interpreted as an analog Planck length in the comoving frame. Non-adiabatic transitions acquire a dispersive character, which is thoroughly discussed. The framework is applied to exponentially expanding or power-law contracting ( 2 + 1 ) (2+1) -dimensional spacetimes which are known to produce scale-invariant cosmological power spectra. The sensitivity of these scenarios to the time-dependence of the Bogoliubov dispersion is investigated: We find a violation of scale-invariance via analytically trackable Transplanckian damping effects if the cut-off scale is not well separated from the horizon-crossing scale. In case of the exponential expansion, these damping effects remarkably settle and converge to another scale-invariant plateau in the far ultraviolet regime where non-adiabatic transitions are suppressed by the high dispersion. The developed framework enables quantitative access to more drastic analog cosmological scenarios with improved predictability in the ultraviolet regime that ultimately may lead to the observation of a scale-invariant cosmological power spectrum in the laboratory.

II.1
Condensate background

An effectively two-dimensional Bose-Einstein condensate with repulsive contact interactions can be described in the dilute regime by the effective action
37

\displaystyle\Gamma[\Phi]=\int\text{d}t\,\text{d}^{2}\mathbf{x}\Big\{\hbar\Phi^{*}(t,\mathbf{x})\left[\mathrm{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-V_{\mathrm{ext}}(t,\mathbf{x})\right]\Phi(t,\mathbf{x})

(2)

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\bm{\nabla}\Phi^{*}(t,\mathbf{x})\bm{\nabla}\Phi(t,\mathbf{x})-\frac{\lambda(t)}{2}\left[\Phi^{*}(t,\mathbf{x})\Phi(t,\mathbf{x})\right]^{2}\Big\}.

It is convenient to split the field
\Phi
into a background part
\phi_{0}
and a fluctuating part parametrized by two real fields
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
, such that

\Phi(t,\bm{r})=\phi_{0}(t,\bm{r})+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\phi_{1}(t,\bm{r})+\mathrm{i}\phi_{2}(t,\bm{r})\right].

(3)

The background field is specified as a solution to the Gross-Pitaevskii equation
62
,

i\hbar\partialderivative{t}\phi_{0}=\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\bm{\nabla}^{2}+V+\lambda\absolutevalue{\phi_0}^{2}\right)\phi_{0},

(4)

and corresponds to a condensate mean field, which is discussed in further detail in reference
99
.
In terms of the Madelung representation
70
,

\phi_{0}(t,\bm{r})=\sqrt{n_{0}(t,\bm{r})}\exp{\mathrm{i} \theta_0 (t, \bm{r})},

(5)

the Gross-Pitaevskii equation (
4
) is equivalent to a pair of hydrodynamic equations involving the background particle number density
n_{0}(t,\bm{r})=\absolutevalue{\phi_0 (t, \bm{r})}^{2}
and the background phase
\theta_{0}(t,\bm{r})
.
More concretely, one finds the continuity equation

0=\partial_{t}n_{0}+\bm{\nabla}(n_{0}\bm{v}),

(6)

and the Euler equation

0=\hbar\partial_{t}\theta_{0}+V+\lambda n_{0}+\frac{m}{2}\bm{v}^{2}+q,

(7)

where we introduced the superfluid velocity via

\bm{v}=\frac{\hbar}{m}\bm{\nabla}\theta_{0},

(8)

and consider the quantum pressure term

q=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\frac{\bm{\nabla}^{2}\sqrt{n_{0}}}{\sqrt{n_{0}}},

(9)

in (
7
), which allow us to be beyond the acoustic approximation.
Note that
q
in Eq. (
9
) is of second order in
\hbar
, as well as in spatial derivatives, such that it is expected to be subleading for sufficiently smooth density.

II.2
Effective theory for fluctuations

Expanding the effective action
\Gamma[\Phi]
up to second order in the fluctuating fields,
\Gamma[\Phi]=\Gamma_{0}[\phi_{0}]+\Gamma_{2}[\phi_{1},\phi_{2}]
(more details in
99
), one finds

\begin{split}\Gamma_{2}=&\int_{t,\bm{x}}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\phi_{2}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\bm{\nabla}^{2}+q\right)\phi_{2}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{1}\left(-2\lambda n_{0}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\bm{\nabla}^{2}+q\right)\phi_{1}\\
&-\hbar\phi_{1}\left[\partial_{t}\phi_{2}+(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\nabla})\phi_{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{v})\phi_{2}\right]\Bigg\},\end{split}

(10)

upon utilizing the Euler equation (
7
).

Since we want to calculate the dispersive extension to the analog cosmological metric calculated in
99
, we restrict to a stationary background flow,
\bm{v}=0
.
Furthermore, we assume a homogeneous background density,
n_{0}(r)=\bar{n}_{0}
.
This substantially simplifies the inclusion of dispersive effects since the quantum pressure still vanishes at the background level,
q=0
.
Invoking these assumptions, the fluctuating fields
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are described by the quadratic action

\begin{split}\Gamma_{2}=&\int_{t,\bm{x}}\Big\{\phi_{2}\hbar\partial_{t}\phi_{1}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m}\phi_{2}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\phi_{2}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{1}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\bm{\nabla}^{2}-2\lambda n_{0}\right)\phi_{1}\Big\},\end{split}

(11)

and evolve according to the linearized equations of motion

2\lambda n_{0}\phi_{1}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\phi_{1}+\hbar\partial_{t}\phi_{2}=0,

(12)

and

\hbar\partial_{t}\phi_{1}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\phi_{2}=0.

(13)

II.3
Quantization and mode dynamics

The conjugate momentum fields of
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are obtained as

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(t,\bm{x})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\delta\Gamma_{2}}{\delta\dot{\phi}_{1}(t,\bm{x})}=-\hbar\phi_{2}(t,\bm{x}),

(14)

\displaystyle\pi_{2}(t,\bm{x})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\delta\Gamma_{2}}{\delta\dot{\phi}_{2}(t,\bm{x})}=\hbar\phi_{1}(t,\bm{x}).

(15)

Hence,
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are conjugate field variables.
With the splitting in
eq.

3
and for
\phi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, they can be interpreted as density- and phase-fluctuations (more details in
appendix

A
).
Therefore, we can quantize both fields in terms of a single set of annihilation and creation operators
\hat{a}_{k}
and
\hat{a}_{k}^{\dagger}
.
To that end we employ the mode expansions

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(t,\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\bm{k}}[\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}w_{k}(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}+\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}w_{k}^{*}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}],

(16)

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(t,\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\bm{k}}[\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}v_{k}(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}+\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}v_{k}^{*}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}],

and demand the operators to obey the bosonic commutation relations

\displaystyle[\hat{a}_{\bm{k}},\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=\delta(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}),

(17)

\displaystyle[\hat{a}_{\bm{k}},\hat{a}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=[\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger},\hat{a}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=0,

which in turn enable the equal-time canonical commutation relations

\displaystyle[\phi_{2}(t,\bm{x}),\pi_{2}(t,\bm{y})]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\hbar\delta(\bm{x}-\bm{y}),

(18)

\displaystyle[\phi_{2}(t,\bm{x}),\phi_{2}(t,\bm{y})]

\displaystyle=[\pi_{2}(t,\bm{x}),\pi_{2}(t,\bm{y})]=0,

(19)

if the normalization constraint,

w_{k}(t)v_{k}^{*}(t)-v_{k}(t)w_{k}^{*}(t)=\mathrm{i},

(20)

is fulfilled. This constraint is presered by the evolution equations

\displaystyle\hbar\partial_{t}w_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=E_{k}v_{k}(t),

(21)

\displaystyle-\hbar\partial_{t}v_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{k}^{2}(t)}{E_{k}}w_{k}(t),

(22)

where we introduced

\epsilon_{k}(t)\equiv\hbar\omega_{k}(t),\quad E_{k}\equiv\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m},

(23)

with the Bogoliubov dispersion relation

\omega_{k}^{2}(t)\equiv c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}(t)k^{2}+\left(\frac{\hbar k^{2}}{2m}\right)^{2},

(24)

and the squared speed of sound

c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}(t)\equiv\frac{\lambda(t)n_{0}}{m}.

(25)

For our further discussion, the phase velocity of Bogoliubov modes

c_{\mathrm{ph}}(t,k)=\frac{\omega_{k}(t)}{k}=c_{\mathrm{s}}(t)\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{2}k^{2}\xi^{2}(t)},

(26)

and the healing length

\xi(t)=\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2}mc_{\mathrm{s}}(t)},

(27)

will play a central role. The latter is a characteristic length scale below which excitations lose their collective character and rather represent individual atoms.
Throughout all numerical evaluations of dispersive effects in this work, we assume the atomic mass in
eq.

27
to be given by
{}^{39}\mathrm{K}
(corresponding to the BEC used in
93
).

III
Dispersive effects in emergent spacetimes

The concepts developed so far provide a bona-fide framework to study the evolution and correlations of the non-relativistic quantum fields in the BEC.
There exists, however, an alternative geometric formulation that bears a strong resemblance to relativistic quantum field theory which shall be layed out in the following.

III.1
Dispersive metric

In the dynamical system (
21
) and (
22
) one can eliminate the mode
w_{k}(t)
in favor of the time-derivative
\dot{v}_{k}(t)

resulting in

\ddot{v}_{k}(t)-2\frac{\dot{c}_{\mathrm{ph}}(t,k)}{c_{\mathrm{ph}}(t,k)}\dot{v}_{k}(t)+c_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2}(t,k)k^{2}v_{k}(t)=0.

(28)

This mode elimination can be realized on the level of the effective action (
11
) by integrating out
\phi_{1}
in Fourier space.
One finds

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m}\int\mathrm{d}t\int_{\bm{k}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{c_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2}(t,k)}\partial_{t}\phi_{2}(t,-\bm{k})\partial_{t}\phi_{2}(t,\bm{k})

(29)

\displaystyle-k^{2}\phi_{2}(t,-\bm{k})\phi_{2}(t,\bm{k})\bigg\},

as the effective action of phase-fluctuations in the BEC, whose velocity and momentum only depend on the absolute value
k=\lvert\bm{k}\rvert
due to the spatially homogeneous background.

Introducing the re-scaled field
\phi=\phi_{2}/\sqrt{2m}
and the operator

\tilde{\partial}_{\mu}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\partial_t \\ - \mathrm{i} \bm{k}\endlx@physics@matrix)

(30)

this effective action assumes a form resembeling a relativistic theory,

\Gamma_{2}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\int\mathrm{d}t\int_{\bm{k}}\sqrt{g(t,k)}g^{\mu\nu}(t,k)\,\tilde{\partial}_{\mu}\phi(t,-\bm{k})\,\tilde{\partial}_{\nu}\phi(t,\bm{k}),

(31)

with the dispersive metric

g_{\mu\nu}(t,k)=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix-1 & 0 \\ 0 & c_\mathrm{ph}^{-2}(t,k) \mathds{1}_2\endlx@physics@matrix),\quad\sqrt{g(t,k)}=c_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2}(t,k).

(32)

Note that, due to the wavenumber dependence of the metric, the effective action (
31
) describes a non-local theory as one can show explicitly by transforming back to real space.
The wavenumber-dependent, geometric structure can be interpreted by tracing wavefronts of constant phase that propagate according to the Bogoliubov dispersion relation
\smash{\omega^{2}(t)=c_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2}(k,t)\,k^{2}}

which translates into a null-condition,

g_{\mu\nu}(t,k)k^{\mu}k^{\nu}=0,

(33)

for the four-momentum
k^{\mu}\equiv(\omega,\bm{k})^{\mathrm{T}}
.
Note that
eq.

33
is in general
not
invariant under (analog) Lorentz transformations,
k^{\mu}\to\Lambda^{\mu}_{\,\nu}k^{\nu}
, such that the laboratory system defines a preferred frame of reference.
On scales where
k\xi/\sqrt{2}\ll 1
however, we have
c_{\mathrm{ph}}(t,k)=c_{\mathrm{s}}(t)+\mathcal{O}(k^{2}\xi^{2})
such that
eq.

33
becomes Lorentz-invariant on these scales.
In that sense, the healing length
\xi
functions as an analog Planck length
108
below which Lorentz-invariance is broken and replaced by a non-local theory with a superluminal (or supersonic) dispersion relation (see
fig.

1
for a visualization).
The described analog spacetime structure is also known as rainbow metric
110
and has been shown to admit a formulation in terms of a Finsler geometry
40
(see
65
for a study on geodesics in such geometries).

III.2
Quantization on dispersive, cosmological spacetime

The metric (
32
) can be brought into a form that is suitable to describe a cosmological spacetime (i.e. a dynamic, homogenenous and isotropic space) but with a wavenumber-dependent scale-factor

a_{k}(t)=c_{\mathrm{ph}}^{-1}(t,k),

(34)

with which we will work in the following, bearing in mind that all statements can be equivalently made from (or translated to) the perspective of Bogoliubov excitations in a BEC with time-dependent contact interactions.

The form of
eq.

28
is equivalent to a mode equation of a massless Klein-Gordon field which is minimally coupled to a
such spacetime evolving dynamically with scale-factor
a_{k}
where each mode experiences a different expansion (or contraction) rate.
In accordance with
eq.

34
we can write the dispersive scale-factor as

a_{k}(t)=\frac{a(t)}{\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{2}k^{2}\xi^{2}(\eta)}},

(35)

where

a(t)=\lim_{k\to 0}a_{k}(t)=c_{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}(t),

(36)

is the scale factor of the acoustic metric derived in
99
.

For completeness, let us also mention that the conjugate momentum of the relativistic field
\phi=\phi_{2}/\sqrt{2m}
is related to
\phi_{1}
via

\pi(t,\bm{k})=\frac{\delta\Gamma_{2}}{\delta\dot{\phi}(t,\bm{k})}=\hbar^{2}a_{k}^{2}\,\dot{\phi}(t,-\bm{k})=-\sqrt{2m}\hbar\,\phi_{1}(t,-\bm{k}),

(37)

as one would expect.
The quantization of
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
is thus equivalent to quantizing
\phi
and
\pi
according to the momentum-space equal-time commutation relation

[\phi(t,\bm{k}),\pi(t,\bm{k^{\prime}})]=\mathrm{i}\hbar\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k^{\prime}}),\\

(38)

with the mode
v_{k}
being subjected to the canonical normalization constraint

\hbar a_{k}^{2}\left(v_{k}\dot{v}_{k}^{*}-\dot{v}_{k}v_{k}^{*}\right)=\mathrm{i},

(39)

which is equivalent to the normalization condition (
20
) upon mode-elimination and a generalization of the usual Wronskian determinant of cosmological scalar fields
99
to the case of a dispersive spacetime described by the scale-factor
a_{k}(t)
.

III.3
Dispersive parametric oscillator

The goal of this section is to transform the mode equation (
28
) into a parametric oscillator for convenience.
To that end we rescale,

v_{k}(t)=[a(t)\mathcal{J}_{k}(t)]^{-1/2}\psi_{k}(t),

(40)

where

\mathcal{J}_{k}(t)\equiv\frac{c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}(t)}{c_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2}(k,t)}=\left(1+\frac{1}{2}k^{2}\xi^{2}(t)\right)^{-1},

(41)

arises as a consequence of the modified canonical volume form in
eq.

31
and captures the reduction in number of phase-space degrees of freedom
43
.

Furthermore, we introduce conformal time
\mathrm{d}\eta=\mathrm{d}t/a(t)
with respect to the bare scale-factor
a(t)

such that
\eta
is still equivalent to the sound horizon in the BEC
89
.
The mode equation (
28
) then takes the form

\psi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(\eta)+\Omega_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}(k,\eta)\psi_{k}(\eta)=0,

(42)

with the effective Bogoliubov frequency

\Omega_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}(k,\eta)=k^{2}/\mathcal{J}_{k}(\eta)+m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}(k,\eta),

(43)

and the effective mass

m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}(k,\eta)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{[a(\eta)\mathcal{J}_{k}(\eta)]^{\prime\prime}}{a(\eta)\mathcal{J}_{k}(\eta)}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{[a(\eta)\mathcal{J}_{k}(\eta)]^{\prime}}{a(\eta)\mathcal{J}_{k}(\eta)}\right)^{2},

(44)

(see
appendix

B
for a detailed derivation).

To clarify the notion of an effective mass, consider that
eq.

43
has the form of an energy-momentum relation,
E^{2}=k^{2}+m^{2}
,
but with the effective mass being generated by the time-dependence of
\mathcal{J}_{k}a
and one has
k^{2}\to k^{2}/\mathcal{J}_{k}
because of the dispersion.

The splitting of terms in the effective frequency (
43
) is also motivated from the effective mass being responsible for non-adiabatic transitions
75

whereas the modes can adiabatically adjust to
k^{2}/\mathcal{J}_{k}(\eta)
.
The magnitude of the former depends on the rate of change in spacetime (or the laboratory parameters in the simulator) and becomes adiabatically suppressed in the high-momentum regime
k^{2}/\mathcal{J}_{k}(\eta)\gg m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}
as we will show with explicit examples later-on.

Moreover, the magnitude of non-adiabatic transition is wavenumber-dependent in the first place since the cut-off scale (or comoving analog Planck length)
\xi(\eta)
is time-dependent.
More explicitly, the effective mass has the form

\displaystyle m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}(k,\eta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{3k^{4}\xi^{4}(\eta)/4-5k^{2}\xi^{2}(\eta)-1}{[1+k^{2}\xi^{2}(\eta)/2]^{2}}\left(\frac{a^{\prime}(\eta)}{a(\eta)}\right)^{2}

(45)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1-k^{2}\xi^{2}(\eta)/2}{1+k^{2}\xi^{2}(\eta)/2}\left(\frac{a^{\prime\prime}(\eta)}{a(\eta)}\right),

where we have used the time-dependence of
\mathcal{J}_{k}(\eta)
.
Put differently, if
\xi
would be time-independent, the effective mass
(
149
)
would be wavenumber-independent such that the dispersive effects only influence the mode by its instantaneous eigenenergy in
eq.

42
as it is also the case for the ad-hoc modifications studied in references
72
,
73
,
11
,
74
,
10
,
83
,
82
.
The physical reason underlying the time-dependence of
\xi(\eta)
(and thus
\mathcal{J}_{k}(\eta)
) are the time-dependent atomic interactions in the BEC.
This places the BEC automatically in the frame comoving with the analog cosmos, which is also the preferred frame in which
eq.

33
holds and where the Lorentz-invariance-violating physics need to be evaluated.

As a consistency check consider the infrared and ultraviolet limit:
In the infrared limit where
\mathcal{J}_{k}
becomes time-independent, one finds the standard result

\lim_{k\to 0}m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}(k,\eta)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{a^{\prime\prime}(\eta)}{a(\eta)}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{a^{\prime}(\eta)}{a(\eta)}\right)^{2}.

IV
Scale invariance

In this section we analyse the perspectives of approaching the Transplanckian problem of cosmology in analog BEC-Cosmology simulators with a time-dependent scattering length.
The following discussion is purely theoretical and can be understood as a first step, using the straight-forward scenarios of an exponential expansion and a power-law contraction, to gain intuition and experience for future scenarios where one has to stay within current experimental capabilities which requires innovative solutions.
In this section we set
\hbar=1
in accordance with the literature.

IV.1
Exponential expansion

We first describe how a scale-invariant spectrum of quantum fluctuations emerges in a non-dispersive theory from an exponentially growing scale-factor,
\smash{a(t)=a_{\mathrm{i}}\mathrm{e}^{Ht}}
which has to be identified with the reciprocal sound-speed in the analog simulator.
The passage of time is described by a growing (sound)-particle horizon (the sound-cone)

\eta(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{dt}}{a(t)}=\eta_{0}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-Ht}),\quad\eta_{0}=\frac{1}{a_{\mathrm{i}}H},

Quantizing a non-dispersive, massless scalar field that is minimally coupled to a flat

The boundary condition is chosen as an instantaneous vacuum state at the initial time

IV.2
Contraction

It is well-known that scale-invariant power spectra can also be achieved via contracting scenarios
109
.
In two spatial dimensions, we find this to be realized for the power-law contraction

a(t)=a_{\mathrm{i}}\left(1-t/\tau\right)^{3/4},

(72)

that also been independently analyed in the context of dipolar BECs

.
Here
\tau
is a constant that corresponds to the time where
a(t)
would reach zero.
In the contracting scenario, the Hubble parameter
H(t)=\dot{a}(t)/a(t)
decreases according to

H(t)=-\frac{3}{4(\tau-t)},

(73)

instead of remaining constant as it was the case for the exponential expansion.
We again define the (sound)-particle horizon via the integral

\eta(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{a(s)}=\eta_{0}[1-\left(1-t/\tau\right)^{1/4}],\quad\eta_{0}=\frac{4\tau}{a_{\mathrm{i}}},

(74)

that asymptotes
\eta_{0}
.
As a function of
\eta
, one has

a(\eta)=a_{\mathrm{i}}\left(1-\frac{\eta}{\eta_{0}}\right)^{3},\quad H(\eta)=-\frac{3}{4\tau}\left(1-\frac{\eta}{\eta_{0}}\right)^{-4},

(75)

which generates the effective mass
\smash{3/4(\eta_{0}-\eta)^{2}}
,
leading to the same mode equation (
56
) as an exponential expansion.
Choosing an instantaneous vacuum initially (given through
eq.

58
) we thus find the mode solution (
59
).
For the power spectrum (
62
) we have to consider that, in terms of the cosmological parameters given in
eqs.

75
,
73
and
74

k(\eta_{0}-\eta)=1
, only translates into a horizon-crossing interpretation up to a factor of three,
3k/aH=1
.
Also, the comoving Hubble horizon
(aH)^{-1}
does not shrink but rather grows according to

[a(t)H(t)]^{-1}=a_{\mathrm{i}}^{-1}(1-t/\tau)^{-3/4}.

(76)

Nevertheless, we can compute the power-spectrum (
62

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{64}\frac{k\eta_{0}}{4\tau}\mathrm{e}^{2N/3}\lvert rJ_{1}(k\eta_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-N/3})+sY_{1}(k\eta_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-N/3})\rvert^{2},

For

V
Influence of laboratory boundary conditions

In the preceding discussion of scale-invariance we assumed an initial instantaneous vacuum state and traced the mode evolution up to some final instant in time where we evaluated the power spectrum.
Therein we did not account for boundary conditions that would be present in a laboratory setting such as an initial switch-on process or a final readout protocol of experimentally accessible field correlations.
The introduction and analysis of both is subject of this section.

VI
Conclusion and outlook

We have derived an effective field theory for the U(1)-Goldstone boson of a scalar Bose-Einstein condensate with time-dependent contact interactions that is consistent with Bogoliubov theory and mapped it to a superluminal, non-local quantum field theory on a dispersive, curved spacetime whose geometry is set by the phase-velocity of Bogoliubov excitations and can be classified as a rainbow geometry.
Assuming a spatially homogeneous background density profile enabled us to neglect quantum pressure terms, thereby considerably facilitating the mapping at the expense of a spatially flat emergent spacetime.
Nevertheless these developments could also be used as an approximation to the spatially curved case (with an inhogeneous background density) for modes whose wavelength is smaller than the curvature radius (which is also known as the eikonal approximation
110
in analogy to the small-wavelength-limit of geometric optics).

In the developed theory, effective Lorentz-invariance is restored on scales larger than the healing length which serves as an analog of the Planck length, with a modified dispersion relation assumed for the Transplanckian regime.
We continued by exploring this framework in a cosmological setup where the (dispersive) scale-factor is identified with the inverse phase-velocity and the comoving frame is the laboratory frame.
The mode equation was transformed into a parametric oscillator form that features wavenumber-dependent non-adiabatic transitions.
The latter originate from a dynamical, analog Planck length in the comoving frame or, equivalently, time-dependent interatomic interactions.
This scenario is different from ad-hoc approaches to Transplanckian regimes that only modify the instantaneous eigenenergy of a mode.

Subsequently, we discussed aspects of the cosmological Transplanckian problem, approached from the perspective of the BEC-analog simulator.
To prepare the discussion, we compared the time-dependent dispersion (with the dynamical analog Planck length) to the time-independent and the non-dispersive case regarding non-adiabaticity.
We continued by describing how a scale-invariant power spectrum of fluctuations can emerge from an exponential expansion or power-law contraction in the BEC and estimated the required parameters to produce such a spectrum within one order of magnitude in the micrometer regime, which resulted in a required number of four to six e-foldings for an expansion time of about
20\mathrm{ms}
(in the expanding case)
whereas the contracting case demands an equivalent of

2\mathrm{ms}

It followed an investigation of the sensitivity of scale-invariance under the time-dependent Bogoliubov dispersion with a comparison to the time-independent case (that has been discussed extensively in the literature

The time-dependent case violates scale-invariance in the exponentially expanding scenario and showcases Transplanckian damping

The time-indepedent case on the other hand preserves scale-invariance since the analog Planck scale and the horizon scale are well separated

82

81

Similar arguments explain the reversed behavior in the contracting case.

In a final section, we introduced laboratory boundary conditions in time as typical for a BEC-experiment.
Relationships between the non-equilibrium statistical two-point functions of the canonically normalized, relativistic field variables and experimentally observarble
in-situ
density-correlations were obtained including Bogoliubov dispersion.
We furthermore studied the impact of switching protocols on the scale-invariant power spectra, where switch-on processes lead to residual acoustic oscillations in the power spectrum whose frequency is determined by the switch-on-rate.
Depending on a contracting or an expanding scenario, the switch-off process can lead to desqueezing and typically is more influential than the switch-on process due to the strong blueshift.

Finally, we applied the developed concepts to previously realized experimental scenarios

With the present work we have contributed to the general discussion of the Transplanckian problem of cosmology via an analysis

Appendix A
Comparison to Bogoliubov theory

In this section we compare the field-theory formalism of
section

II
to the more common Bogoliubov theory in the operator formalism.
First, it will be important to realize that the fluctuating fields
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are related to density and phase fluctuations
\delta n,\delta\theta
which are the variables commonly used in the literature (a non-exhaustive list is
111
,
54
,
110
,
87
) and can be introduced here via

\Phi(t,\bm{r})=[n_{0}(t,\bm{r})+\delta n(t,\bm{r})]\,e^{\mathrm{i}[\theta_{0}(t,\bm{r})+\delta S(t,\bm{r})]}.

(121)

At leading order in both fluctuations, one finds

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(t,\bm{r})

\displaystyle=\delta n(t,\bm{r})/\sqrt{2n_{0}(t,\bm{r})},

(122)

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(t,\bm{r})

\displaystyle=\delta\theta(t,\bm{r})\,\sqrt{2n_{0}(t,\bm{r})},

(123)

after a local gauge transformation
\Phi\to\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{0}(t,\bm{r})}\Phi
.
However, since phase fluctuations become ill-defined in the limit
n_{0}\to 0
, where
eq.

123
becomes singular, we work with the fields
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
in the main text.
In the following we closely follow
87
.

A.1
Atomic density correlations

At linear order, the total atomic density operator is expanded into a background- and fluctuating part

\hat{\rho}(t,\bm{x})=\rho_{0}+\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\,(\delta\hat{\phi}+\delta\hat{\phi}^{\dagger}).

(124)

The connected equal-time density correlator,

\displaystyle G^{\rho\rho}(t,\bm{x};t^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})=\langle\hat{\rho}(t,\bm{x})\hat{\rho}(t,\bm{x}^{\prime})\rangle-\langle\hat{\rho}(t,\bm{x})\rangle\langle\hat{\rho}(t,\bm{x}^{\prime})\rangle

(125)

can be expanded in Fourier space,

\displaystyle G^{\rho\rho}(t,\bm{k};t^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\bm{k}\bm{x}+\bm{k}^{\prime}\bm{x}^{\prime})}G^{\rho\rho}(t,\bm{x};t^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})

(126)

\displaystyle=\langle\delta\hat{\rho}_{\bm{k}}(t)\delta\hat{\rho}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(t)\rangle,

where

\delta\hat{\rho}_{\bm{k}}(t)=\int\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\bm{x}}\delta\hat{\rho}(t,\bm{x}).

(127)

Since
\hat{\rho}(t,\bm{x})
is a Hermitian operator, one has

\delta\hat{\rho}_{\bm{k}}(t)=\delta\hat{\rho}_{-\bm{k}}^{\dagger}(t).

(128)

In terms of perturbative operators we have

\delta\hat{\rho}_{\bm{k}}(t)=\sqrt{N}(\delta\hat{\phi}_{\bm{k}}(t)+\delta\hat{\phi}_{\bm{-k}}^{\dagger}(t)),

(129)

where
\sqrt{N}
ensures the fulfillment of the equal-time commutation relations

[\delta\hat{\phi}_{\bm{k}}(t),\delta\hat{\phi}_{\bm{k^{\prime}}}^{\dagger}(t)]=\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{k^{\prime}}}.

(130)

As a result of both
k
and
-k
excitations appearing in
\delta\hat{\rho}(\bm{k})
, it is indistinguishable to destroy a phonon of momentum
\bm{k}
or to create one with momentum
\bm{-k}
.
Furthermore, these two excitations necessarily interfere when performing density measurements.

A.2
Collective excitations

The Hamiltonian of the linear perturbations is diagonalized by the Bogoliubov transformation

\delta\phi_{\bm{k}}=u_{\bm{k}}\hat{\varphi}_{\bm{k}}+v_{-\bm{k}}^{*}\hat{\varphi}_{-\bm{k}}^{\dagger},

(131)

where we introduce the quasi-particle operators
\hat{\varphi}_{\bm{k}},\hat{\varphi}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}
.
The Bogoliubov coefficients can be parametrized as

u_{k}=\cosh\chi_{k},\quad v_{k}=\sinh\chi_{k},

(132)

with

\coth(2\chi_{k})=-1-k^{2}\xi^{2},

(133)

where

\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m\xi^{2}}=\lambda n_{0}=mc_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}.

(134)

In the following, we restrict the analysis to a stationary and homogeneous system.
Additionally we assume that the phonon states are statistically homogeneous.
As a consequence, the correlations only depend the spatial distance
\lvert\bm{x-x^{\prime}}\rvert
or absolute values
k=\lvert\bm{k}\rvert
in Fourier space.
Moreover, one only has correlations between
k
and
-k
.

A.2.1
Density fluctuations in the phonon basis

In terms of the quasi-particle operators, the density-fluctuation operator reads

\delta\hat{\rho}_{\bm{k}}=\sqrt{N}(u_{k}+v_{k})\left(\hat{\varphi}_{\bm{k}}+\hat{\varphi}_{-\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\right).

(135)

In the quasi-particle ground-state, the density correlations are

G_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\rho\rho}(k)=N(u_{k}+v_{k})^{2},

(136)

whereas in an excited phonon state one has

G_{\mathrm{exc}}^{\rho\rho}(k)=G_{\mathrm{vac}}^{(2)}(k)(1+n_{k}+n_{-k}+2\mathrm{Re}[c_{k}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Omega_{k}t}]),

(137)

where

\displaystyle n_{\pm k}

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{\varphi}_{\pm\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{\varphi}_{\pm\bm{k}}\rangle,\qquad c_{k}=\langle\hat{\varphi}_{\bm{k}}\hat{\varphi}_{-\bm{k}}\rangle,

(138)

represent the occupation numbers of and the coherence between
k
and
-k
excitations.

A.2.2
Phase fluctuations in the phonon basis

Phase fluctuations can be introduced in the atomic basis via the operator

\delta\hat{\phi}(t,\bm{x})=\frac{\delta\hat{\rho}}{2\sqrt{\rho_{0}}}+\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\delta\hat{\theta}.

(139)

In terms of the quasi-particle operators, one has

\delta\hat{\theta}_{k}=\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2\mathrm{i}\rho_{0}}(u_{k}-v_{k})\left(\hat{\varphi}_{\bm{k}}-\hat{\varphi}_{-\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\right),

(140)

such that
\delta\hat{\phi}
is the proper conjugate variable to the density fluctuation
\delta\hat{n}_{k}
.
In the quasi-particle ground state, the phase correlations are

G^{\theta\theta}_{\mathrm{vac}}(k)=\frac{N}{4\rho_{0}^{2}}(u_{k}-v_{k})^{2},

(141)

whereas for an excited state

G^{\theta\theta}_{\mathrm{exc}}(k)=G^{\theta\theta}_{\mathrm{vac}}(k)(1+n_{k}+n_{-k}-2\mathrm{Re}[c_{k}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Omega_{k}t}]).

(142)

This correlator contains no additional information on the phonon pair state as the coherent term is merely
\pi
-shifted.
The vacuum normalization is the respective inverse of the density normalization factor as it should be for proper conjugate variables.
These considerations follow from the useful identities

\displaystyle(u_{k}+v_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{k\xi}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{2}(k\xi)^{2}}}=\frac{E_{k}}{\epsilon(k)},

(143)

\displaystyle(u_{k}-v_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{k\xi}\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{2}(k\xi)^{2}}=\frac{\epsilon(k)}{E_{k}},

where

\displaystyle\epsilon(k)

\displaystyle=c_{\mathrm{s}}k\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{2}k^{2}\xi^{2}},\quad E_{k}=\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}.

(144)

Appendix B
Derivation of dispersive effective mass

The goal of this section is to absorb the influence of the time-dependent and dispersive cosmological background into an effective mass, by transforming away the Hubble friction term in
eq.

28
.
To that end we rescale

v_{k}(t)=f_{k}(t)\psi_{k}(t),

(145)

and introduce conformal time
\mathrm{d}\eta=\mathrm{d}t/a(t)
with respect to the bare scale-factor
a(t)=1/c_{\mathrm{s}}(t)

such that
\eta
is still equivalent to the sound horizon in the BEC

The mode equation (
28

\displaystyle\psi_{k}^{\prime\prime}+2\left(\frac{f_{k}^{\prime}}{f_{k}}+\frac{a_{k}^{\prime}}{a_{k}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\right)\psi_{k}^{\prime}

(146)

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{f_{k}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{k}}-2\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}-\frac{a_{k}^{\prime}}{a_{k}}\right)\frac{f_{k}^{\prime}}{f_{k}}\right]\psi_{k}=-k^{2}\frac{a^{2}}{a_{k}^{2}}\psi_{k}

To have a vanishing Hubble friction term, the scaling function is specified to fulfill

\frac{f_{k}^{\prime}(\eta)}{f_{k}(\eta)}+\frac{a_{k}^{\prime}(\eta)}{a_{k}(\eta)}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{a^{\prime}(\eta)}{a(\eta)},

(147)

such that the mode equation becomes

\psi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(\eta)+\left[k^{2}+m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}(k,\eta)+\frac{1}{2}k^{4}\xi^{2}(\eta)\right]\psi_{k}(\eta)=0,

(148)

with the effective mass

m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}(k,\eta)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}\frac{f_{k}^{\prime}(\eta)}{f_{k}(\eta)}-\left(\frac{f_{k}^{\prime}(\eta)}{f_{k}(\eta)}\right)^{2}.

(149)

The specific form of the function
f_{k}
can be obtained by considering
eq.

35

We can then rewrite
eq.

\frac{f_{k}^{\prime}(\eta)}{f_{k}(\eta)}=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\mathcal{J}_{k}(\eta)\right)\frac{a^{\prime}(\eta)}{a(\eta)},

(150)

which can be integrated to

f_{k}(\eta)=[a(\eta)\mathcal{J}_{k}(\eta)]^{-1/2},

(151)

and is the
(2+1)
-dimensional version of the mode rescaling employed in

Acoustic limit.

As a consistency check, we find in the infrared regime,
k\ll k_{\xi}

a_{k}=a+\mathcal{O}((k/k_{\xi})^{2})

m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}(k,\eta)=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{a^{\prime}(\eta)}{a(\eta)}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{a^{\prime\prime}(\eta)}{a(\eta)}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\xi}^{2}}\right),

(152)

which coincides with the analysis

Ultraviolet limit.

In the ultraviolet limit,

k^{-1}\ll\xi/\sqrt{2}

As it is not possible anymore to project phonons out of the condensate basis, analog particle production should ceise.
Indeed, the phase velocity becomes

c_{k}=\frac{\hbar k}{2m}+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{2}k^{2}/4m^{2}c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}(t)).

(153)

Then, the mode equation (

\ddot{v}_{k}(t)+\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{4}}{(2m)^{2}}v_{k}(t)=0,

(154)

The ultraviolet limit of the effective mass is

\lim_{k\to\infty}m_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}(k,\eta)=-\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{a^{\prime}(\eta)}{a(\eta)}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{a^{\prime\prime}(\eta)}{a(\eta)},

Appendix C
Adiabatic vacuum via WKB-approximation

We recall the well-known WKB-solution of a parametric oscillator with frequency
\Omega(k,\eta)
that reads

\psi_{k}^{\mathrm{WKB}}(\eta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2W_{k}(\eta)}}\exp[-\mathrm{i}\int_{\eta_{\mathrm{i}}}^{\eta}W_{k}(\eta^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\eta^{\prime}],

(156)

where the pre-factor ensures canonical normalization and the quantity
W_{k}
satisfies the non-linear differential equation
7

W_{k}^{2}(\eta)=\Omega^{2}(k,\eta)-\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{W_{k}^{\prime\prime}(\eta)}{W_{k}(\eta)}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{W_{k}^{\prime}(\eta)}{W_{k}(\eta)}\right)^{2}\right].

(157)

This equation can be solved iteratively, resulting in an adiabatic expansion of the mode function where each iteration is referred to as an
adiabatic order

7
.
The vacuum associated to a mode function at a certain adiabatic order is called the
adiabatic vacuum
.
At zeroth adiabatic order one has
W_{k}^{2}(\eta)=\Omega^{2}(k,\eta)
and the mode
adiabatically adjusts
to the time-dependent background (an adjustment local in time).
The zeroth-order adiabatic approximation is valid as long as both the conditions

\bigg\lvert\frac{\Omega^{\prime}(k,\eta)}{\Omega(k,\eta)}\bigg\rvert\ll\lvert\Omega(k,\eta)\rvert,\quad\bigg\lvert\frac{\Omega^{\prime\prime}(k,\eta)}{\Omega(k,\eta)}\bigg\rvert\ll\Omega^{2}(k,\eta),

(158)

are fulfilled
38
.

Appendix D
Analytic model for Transplanckian damping

Neglecting the wavenumber dependence of the effective mass but keeping the time-dependence of the healing length,
the mode equation induced by an exponentially expanding spacetime reads

\psi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(\eta)+\left(k^{2}-\frac{\nu(k)^{2}-1/4}{(\eta-\eta_{0})^{2}}\right)\psi_{k}(\eta)=0

(159)

with

\nu(k)^{2}=1-(k\eta_{0})^{4}/\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}^{2},

(160)

whose square-root we take to have a positive real part in the following.
We also did introduce the initial scale-separation parameter

\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}=\sqrt{2}\eta_{0}/\xi_{\mathrm{i}},

(161)

to conveniently analyze the spectrum as a function of
k\eta_{0}
.
The general solution to the mode equation can be written as

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\mathrm{i}}}}\sqrt{\frac{\eta_{0}-\eta}{\eta_{0}}}

(162)

\displaystyle\times\left(rJ_{\nu(k)}[k(\eta_{0}-\eta)]+sY_{\nu(k)}[k(\eta_{0}-\eta)]\right)

The boundary conditions

\psi_{k}(0)=1/\sqrt{2\omega_{\mathrm{in}}},\quad\psi_{k}^{\prime}(0)=-\mathrm{i}\omega_{\mathrm{in}}\psi_{k}(0),

(163)

are solved by

r=(1-2\nu(k)-2\mathrm{i}\omega_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{0})Y_{\nu(k)}(k\eta_{0})+2k\eta_{0}Y_{\nu(k)-1}(k\eta_{0})\\

(164)

and

s=(-1+2\nu(k)+2\mathrm{i}\omega_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{0})J_{\nu(k)}(k\eta_{0})-2k\eta_{0}J_{\nu(k)-1}(k\eta_{0}).

(165)

The power spectrum is

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\psi}(k,\eta)}{Hk\eta_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{64}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-2N}}{\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{2}(k\xi_{\mathrm{i}})^{2}}}

(166)

\displaystyle\times\lvert rJ_{\nu(k)}[k(\eta_{0}-\eta)]+sY_{\nu(k)}[k(\eta_{0}-\eta)]\rvert^{2}.

(167)

Let us again evaluate it at final times
\eta_{\mathrm{f}}=\eta_{0}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-N})
for modes with
1\ll k\eta_{0}\ll\mathrm{e}^{N}
.

D.1
Mild ultraviolet regime

As long as
(k\eta_{0})^{4}<1/\sigma
we have
\nu(k)\in\mathbb{R}
and therefore can approximate

Y_{\nu(k)}(k\eta_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-N})\approx-\frac{\Gamma(\nu(k))}{\pi}\left(\frac{k\eta_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-N}}{2}\right)^{-\nu(k)},

(168)

to find

\displaystyle P_{\psi}(k,\eta_{\mathrm{f}})=\frac{H}{32\pi}\Gamma^{2}(\nu(k))\left(\frac{k\eta_{0}}{2}\right)^{1-2\nu(k)}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-2N(1-\nu(k))}}{\sqrt{1+(k\eta_{0}\sigma)^{2}}}\big\{

\displaystyle\left[(-1+2\nu(k))J_{\nu(k)}(k\eta_{0})-2k\eta_{0}J_{\nu(k)-1}(k\eta_{0})\right]^{2}

(169)

\displaystyle+(2\omega_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{0})^{2}J_{\nu(k)}(k\eta_{0})^{2}\big\},

which converges to the non-dispersive result (
68
) in the infrared regime,
k\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\ll 1
, where
\nu(k)\to 1
.

D.2
Far ultraviolet regime

For
(k\eta_{0})^{4}>1/\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}
, the order
\nu(k)
becomes purely imaginary,
\nu(k)\in\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R}
.
This requires to write the mode solution in a different basis,

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\mathrm{i}}}}\sqrt{\frac{\eta_{0}-\eta}{\eta_{0}}}

(170)

\displaystyle\times\left(rF_{\lvert\nu(k)\rvert}[k(\eta_{0}-\eta)]+sG_{\lvert\nu(k)\rvert}[k(\eta_{0}-\eta)]\right),

with the coefficients

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=-(1+2\mathrm{i}\omega_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{0})G_{\lvert\nu(k)\rvert}(k\eta_{0})-2k\eta_{0}\partial_{x}G_{\lvert\nu(k)\rvert}(x)\lvert_{x=k\eta_{0}},

(171)

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=(1+2\mathrm{i}\omega_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{0})F_{\lvert\nu(k)\rvert}(k\eta_{0})+2k\eta_{0}\partial_{x}F_{\lvert\nu(k)\rvert}(x)\lvert_{x=k\eta_{0}},

(172)

F_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{2\cosh(\pi\nu/2)}\left(J_{\mathrm{i}\nu}(x)+J_{-\mathrm{i}\nu}(x)\right),\\

(173)

and

\displaystyle G_{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\sinh(\pi\nu/2)}\left(J_{\mathrm{i}\nu}(x)-J_{-\mathrm{i}\nu}(x)\right)

(174)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\cosh(\pi\nu/2)}\left(Y_{\mathrm{i}\nu}(x)+Y_{-\mathrm{i}\nu}(x)\right),

which are numerically stable alternatives to the unmodified Bessel-functions of purely imaginary order (see
29

They are real for positive arguments, have the similar small-argument asymptotics

\displaystyle F_{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2\tanh(\pi\nu/2)}{\pi\nu}}\cos\left(\nu\ln(x/2)-\mathrm{arg}\,\Gamma(1+\mathrm{i}\nu)\right),

(175)

\displaystyle G_{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2\coth(\pi\nu/2)}{\pi\nu}}\sin\left(\nu\ln(x/2)-\mathrm{arg}\,\Gamma(1+\mathrm{i}\nu)\right),

(176)

as
x\to 0^{+}
and large-argument asymptotics

\displaystyle F_{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\cos\left(x-\pi/4\right),

(177)

\displaystyle G_{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\sin\left(x-\pi/4\right),

(178)

as

Furthermore, their Wronskian,

\partial_{x}F_{\nu}(x)G_{\nu}(x)-\partial_{x}G_{\nu}(x)F_{\nu}(x)=2/\pi x,

(179)

176

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\psi}(k,\eta_{\mathrm{f}})}{Hk\eta_{0}}=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-2N}}{64\lvert\nu(k)\rvert}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(k\eta_{0}\sigma)^{2}}}

(180)

\displaystyle P_{\psi}(k,\eta_{\mathrm{f}})

\lvert J_{\mathrm{i\lvert\nu\rvert}}(\lvert\nu\rvert x)\rvert\sim\frac{\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\lvert\nu\rvert\pi+\mathcal{O}(\lvert\nu\rvert^{-2})\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi\lvert\nu\rvert}(x^{2}+1)^{1/4}},\\

\rho(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}+1}-\ln\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{x^{2}+1}}{x}\right),

(185)

and

We directly infer that

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{\nu}}(\nu x)

\displaystyle\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\nu}}(x^{2}+1)^{-1/4}\cos\left(\nu\rho(x)-\frac{\pi}{4}\right),

(186)

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{\nu}}(\nu x)

(187)

according to the definitions (

(188)

\displaystyle G_{(k\eta_{0})^{2}/\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}}(k\eta_{0})

(189)

(191)

\displaystyle P_{\psi}(k,\eta_{\mathrm{f}})

(192)

S(k)
reduces to

G_{nn}(t,L)=\frac{\hbar a_{\mathrm{f}}}{mn_{0}}\int_{k}J_{0}(kL)\,k\,S(k),

(193)

which coincides with the acoustic result
99
.

E.2
Ultraviolet limit: Shot-noise

As a consistency check, let us apply this limit to
eq.

102
for the density correlation function.
As a consequence of abscent quasi-particles, the limit of the excitation spectrum assumes the value
S(k)\simeq 1/2
and becomes static.
From the integrand of
eq.

102
we can read off that

\langle\delta_{c}(\bm{k})\delta_{c}(\bm{k^{\prime}})\rangle=\frac{\hbar a_{\mathrm{f}}}{mn_{0}}\frac{k\,S(k)}{\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{2}k^{2}\xi_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}}}\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k^{\prime}})\\

(194)

which in the limit of large momenta
k\xi_{\mathrm{f}}\gg 1
and
k^{\prime}\xi_{\mathrm{f}}\gg 1
can be approximated to

\langle\delta_{c}(\bm{k})\delta_{c}(\bm{k^{\prime}})\rangle\simeq\frac{1}{n_{0}}\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k^{\prime}}),

(195)

or in terms of connected density-fluctuations

\langle n(\bm{k})n(\bm{k^{\prime}})\rangle_{c}=\frac{N}{V}\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k^{\prime}}).

(196)

where we set
n_{0}=N/V
, with
N
as the particle number and
V
as the volume of the atomic cloud.
Thus, the density fluctuations obey a shot noise spectrum
\Delta N\simeq\sqrt{N}
in the ultraviolet regime,
as expected from considering that for high wavenumbers the kinetic energies of the atoms surpass the interaction energies by far, resulting in an equivalence to an ideal bose gas.
To realize why one expects a shot noise spectrum in the ultraviolet limit of the latter, let us consider a homogenenous, ideal bose gas contained in a volume
V
.
The effective bosonic field
\Psi
is quantized via the mode expansion
59

\Phi(\bm{x})=\sum_{k}\phi_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x})\hat{a}_{\bm{k}},\quad\phi_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x})=\exp{\mathrm{i} \bm{k} \cdot\bm{x}}/\sqrt{V}

(197)

where one has the well-known commutation relations

\displaystyle[\Phi(\bm{x}),\Phi^{\dagger}(\bm{y})]

\displaystyle=\delta(\bm{x}-\bm{y}),

(198)

\displaystyle[\Phi(\bm{x}),\Phi(\bm{y})]

\displaystyle=[\Phi^{\dagger}(\bm{x}),\Phi^{\dagger}(\bm{y})]=0.

The two-point density correlations in the atomic gas can be computed from the expectation values

\displaystyle\langle n(\bm{x})n(\bm{y})\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\langle n(\bm{x})n(\bm{y})\rangle-\langle n(\bm{x})\rangle\langle n(\bm{y})\rangle,

(199)

\displaystyle\langle n(\bm{x})n(\bm{y})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi^{\dagger}(\bm{x})\Phi^{\dagger}(\bm{y})\Phi(\bm{y})\Phi(\bm{x})\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle n(\bm{x})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi^{\dagger}(\bm{x})\Phi(\bm{x})\rangle,

which are taken in the state that is annihilated by
\hat{a}_{k}
from
eq.

197
.
For the connected density two-point function, one finds
59

\langle n(\bm{x})n(\bm{y})\rangle_{c}=\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k^{\prime}}}\langle\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\bm{k^{\prime}}}\hat{a}_{\bm{k^{\prime}}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{k}}\rangle\phi^{*}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x})\phi_{\bm{k^{\prime}}}(\bm{x})\phi^{*}_{\bm{k^{\prime}}}(\bm{y})\phi_{\bm{k}}(\bm{y}),

(200)

where one has

\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}=\hat{n}_{\bm{k}},\quad\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}\hat{a}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}=\hat{n}_{\bm{k}}+1,

(201)

in terms of the number-density operator
\hat{n}_{\bm{k}}
.
Assuming that the system is in the non-condensed phase enables us to replace
\,\sum_{\bm{k}}\to V\int_{\bm{k}}
for a sufficiently large volume
V
(the condensed case is for example discussed in
116
,
80
).
We then find
59

\langle n(\bm{x})n(\bm{y})\rangle_{c}=\frac{N}{V}\delta(\bm{x}-\bm{y})+\bigg\lvert\int_{\bm{k}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{x}-\bm{y})}\langle\hat{n}_{\bm{k}}\rangle\bigg\rvert^{2}.

(202)

where we set
\langle n(\bm{x})\rangle=N/V
.
Taking a Fourier-transform we find

\langle n(\bm{k})n(\bm{k^{\prime}})\rangle_{c}=\frac{N}{V}\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k^{\prime}})+\int_{\bm{q}}\langle\hat{n}_{\bm{q}}\rangle\langle\hat{n}_{\bm{k+q}}\rangle\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k^{\prime}}),

(203)

where the first term represents shot-noise and the second term autocorrelations.
If the Bose gas is in thermal equilibrium,
\langle\hat{n}_{\bm{q+k}}\rangle
is the Bose-Einstein distribution which decays exponentially as
\lvert\bm{k}\rvert\to\infty
, such that only the shot-noise-term remains in the ultraviolet limit.

In this section, we focussed on the case of an ideal gas, which is appropriate in the ultraviolet limit.
The non-ideal case can be found for example in
86
,
80
and references therein.

Appendix F
Thermal structure factor

An introduction to the spectral methods employed in this section can be found in chapter IV of
63
where also the following calculation is carried out in a similar manner.
The connection between the full quantum field of the condensate
\Phi
and the full condensate density
n
is given by the relation

\begin{split}n(t,u,\varphi)&=\absolutevalue{\Phi(t,u,\varphi)}^{2}\\
&=n_{0}(u)+\sqrt{2n_{0}(u)}\phi_{1}(t,u,\varphi)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{1}^{2}(t,u,\varphi)+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{2}^{2}(t,u,\varphi),\end{split}

(204)

where we considered the background field
{\phi_{0}\equiv\sqrt{n_{0}}}
,
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
to be real after some local
U(1)
gauge transformation.
When we take the expectation value of the full condensate density
\langle n(t,u,\varphi)\rangle
, the second term in Eq. (
204
) drops out, because it is linear in fluctuations.

Let us now determine the spectral density
\Delta_{ab}^{\rho}(\hbar\omega,\bm{k})
in the present situation for the fields
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
. In momentum space the quadratic action becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Gamma[\phi_{1},\phi_{2}]\\
&=\int\frac{\text{d}\omega}{2\pi}\,\frac{\text{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\Bigg\{-\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{1}&\tilde{\phi}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\tilde{G}^{-1}(\omega,k)\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{1}\\
\tilde{\phi}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\Bigg\},\end{split}

(205)

with the inverse propagator

\tilde{G}^{-1}_{ab}(\hbar\omega,\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+2\lambda n_{0}&-i\hbar\omega\\
i\hbar\omega&\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}\end{pmatrix}

(206)

such that

\tilde{G}_{ab}(\hbar\omega,\bm{k})=\frac{1}{-\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}+\hbar^{2}\omega_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}(k)}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}&i\hbar\omega\\
-i\hbar\omega&\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+2\lambda n_{0}\end{pmatrix},

(207)

where we introduced the celebrated Bogoliubov dispersion relation

\displaystyle\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{B}}(k)=\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+2\lambda n_{0}\right)\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}}.

(208)

as the pole of the propagator,

\displaystyle\det\tilde{G}^{-1}(\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{B}},\bm{k})=0.

(209)

The spectral function follows from this as

\begin{split}&\Delta^{\rho}_{ab}(\hbar\omega,\bm{k})=-i\tilde{G}_{ab}(\hbar\omega+i\epsilon,\bm{k})+i\tilde{G}_{ab}(\hbar\omega-i\epsilon,\bm{k}).\end{split}

(210)

To evaluate this one needs the identity

\frac{1}{x\pm i\epsilon}=\mp i\pi\delta(x)+\text{P.V.}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)

(211)

and
-\left(\hbar\omega\pm i\epsilon\right)^{2}=-\omega^{2}\mp i\epsilon\text{sign}(\omega)
which results in the spectral function

\displaystyle\Delta^{\rho}_{ab}(\hbar\omega,\bm{k})

\displaystyle=2\pi\delta\left(\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}-\hbar^{2}\omega_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}\right)\text{sign}(\omega)

(212)

\displaystyle\times\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}&i\hbar\omega\\
-i\hbar\omega&\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+2\lambda n_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

One can now determine the static structure factor as

\begin{split}S_{ab}(\bm{k})=&\hbar\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\text{d}\omega}{2\pi}\left[\frac{1}{2}+n_{B}(\hbar\omega)\right]\Delta^{\rho}_{ab}(\hbar\omega,\bm{k})\\
=&\frac{\hbar}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{d}\omega\coth(\absolutevalue{\hbar\omega}/2)\delta\left(\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}-\hbar^{2}\omega_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}(k)\right)\\
&\times\text{sign}(\hbar\omega)\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}&i\hbar\omega\\
-i\hbar\omega&\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+2\lambda n_{0}\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(213)

where we used

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+n_{B}(\hbar\omega)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{2}+n_{B}(\absolutevalue{\hbar\omega})\right]\text{sign}(\hbar\omega)

(214)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\coth\left(\frac{\absolutevalue{\hbar\omega}}{2}\right)\,\text{sign}(\hbar\omega)

One further observes that the off-diagonal terms
i\hbar\omega
do not contribute to the static structure factor, because they are odd with respect to
\hbar\omega\rightarrow-\hbar\omega
while the rest of the integral is even. Otherwise one can perform the frequency integration using the Dirac deltas with zero-crossings are
\hbar\omega=\pm\epsilon_{k}
, which gives

S_{ab}(\bm{k})=\frac{1}{2}\coth\left(\frac{\absolutevalue{\hbar\omega}}{2}\right)\begin{pmatrix}E_{k}/\epsilon_{k}&0\\
0&\epsilon_{k}/E_{k}\end{pmatrix},

(215)

with

\epsilon_{k}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar^{2}\bm{k}^{2}}{2m}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}\bm{k}^{2}}{2m}+2\lambda n_{0}\right)},\quad E_{k}=\frac{\hbar^{2}\bm{k}^{2}}{2m}.

(216)

In terms of the healing length
\xi=\hbar/\sqrt{2m\lambda n_{0}}
and the speed of sound
c=\sqrt{\lambda n_{0}/m}
one can introduce the phase velocity

c_{k}=c\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}\absolutevalue{\bm{k}}^{2}/2}
, such that the Bogoliubov energy can be written as

\epsilon_{k}=\hbar c_{k}\lvert\bm{k}\rvert=\hbar c\absolutevalue{\bm{k}}\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}\absolutevalue{\bm{k}}^{2}/2},

(217)

and also

\frac{E_{k}}{\epsilon_{k}}=\frac{\xi k}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{2}k^{2}\xi^{2}}},\quad\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{E_{k}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{k\xi}\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{2}k^{2}\xi^{2}}.

(218)

The components of the matrix are identical to expressions obtained from invoking a Bogoliubov transformation between the atom and the phonon basis as one can see from
eqs.

136
,
141
and
143
.

The leading terms in the acoustic approximations, as well as their corrections are now clear to see.
In particular, we find for the Fourier transform of the density correlation function

\displaystyle S_{nn}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=2n_{0}S_{11}(\bm{k})

(219)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}n_{0}\left[\frac{1}{2}+n_{B}(E_{\bm{k}})\right]\frac{\xi\absolutevalue{\bm{k}}}{\sqrt{1+\frac{\xi^{2}\absolutevalue{\bm{k}}^{2}}{2}}}.

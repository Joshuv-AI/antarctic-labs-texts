Caution: fundamental data quality issues underlying intelligent prediction of enzyme optimal pH
Authors: Peng Zhou, Xuzhong Fang
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

Deep learning-based prediction of enzyme optimal pH (pHopt) has become an efficient and cost-effective approach to advance enzyme engineering, complementing labor-intensive experimental assays. Qiu et al. [1] recently developed Seq2pHopt, a deep learning predictor of enzyme pHopt. While investigating the dataset used by Seq2pHopt, we have identified several data-related issues. The dataset employed in the development of Seq2pHopt was sourced from the recently proposed EpHod model, a profound framework for pHopt prediction [1, 2]. EpHod directly predicts enzyme pHopt from protein sequences utilizing embeddings from the ESM-1v protein language model (PLM), achieving a root mean squared error (RMSE) of 1.25 pH units on the held-out test set [2]. Via algorithm optimizations, Qiu et al. [1] subsequently used the EpHod pHopt dataset to develop Seq2pHopt, which improved pHopt prediction to an RMSE of 0.88 pH units. The sequence-based deep learning approaches enable rapid prediction of enzyme pHopt. Nevertheless, inherent annotation flaws in this dataset undermine the reliability of these predictive models.

The EpHod pHopt dataset comprises 9855 enzymes annotated with pHopt values from the BRENDA database [2]. This dataset showed severe label imbalance: nearly 75% of the pHopt values fall between pH 6 and 8, and 94% range from pH 5 to 9 [2]. To alleviate this label imbalance, measurements have been used to reweight the loss function with values inversely proportional to the label density, thus directing the models to focus on sparse extreme regions [2]. Given that accurate prediction for extremely acidic and alkaline enzymes is highly valuable for biological design and synthesis, the 28 entries with extreme pHopt values (≥ 11.5 or ≤ 2.5) from the EpHod pHopt dataset were cross-checked for experimental evidence from published literature. The pHopt annotations of six entries were found notably inconsistent with experimentally determined results (Table 1), spanning the training, validation and test subsets. B5LXD7 is annotated with a pHopt value of 11.5 [2, 3]. However, biochemical assays of the purified recombinant enzyme expressed in Pichia pastoris confirm its actual pHopt ranges from 1.0 to 1.5 [4]. This striking discrepancy merits close attention. One possible explanation is that this misannotation occurred during annotation by BRENDA.

Three entries (A0A2Z5VGL1, A0A2Z5VGM9, and A0A2Z5VGN0) are uniformly annotated with a pHopt value of 2.4 in the EpHod dataset. However, A0A2Z5VGL1, A0A2Z5VGM9, and A0A2Z5VGN0 were previously reported to exhibit specific ENGase activity toward fucose-containing oligosaccharides [5], yet follow-up experiments showed that these proteins lacked such activity [6].

Q6HA05 (Pepsin A1 in the Antarctic notothenioid Trematomus bernacchii) is assigned a pHopt value of 2.0 in the EpHod training dataset, whereas biochemical characterization of the purified recombinant Pepsin A1 expressed in Escherichia coli yielded a pHopt value of 2.5 [7]. Since T. bernacchii possesses two pepsins, A1 and A2, and Pepsin A2 has a pHopt value of 2.0 [7], one possible explanation is that the pHopt value of Pepsin A2 was annotated to Pepsin A1 by mistake.

Q97VS7 encodes endo-1,4-β-glucanase CelB (genomic locus: SSO2534), which is annotated with a pHopt value of 1.8 in the EpHod test set. However, available literature only verifies the cellulase activity of Q97VS7 at pH 5.8 [8] with no experimental evidence supporting a pHopt of 1.8. One possible explanation is that, within the BRENDA database, Q97VS7 was incorrectly annotated with the pHopt value of another endo-β-glucanase (SSO1949). The genome of Saccharolobus solfataricus (strain ATCC 35092, formerly Sulfolobus solfataricus) harbors three putative endo-β-glucanases, located at genomic loci SSO2534, SSO1949, and SSO1354, among which SSO1949 has a well-validated pHopt of 1.8 [9]. No conclusive data confirm that Q97VS7 has the same pHopt value as SSO1949. Further experimental validation is required to verify the true pHopt of Q97VS7.

After manually cross-checking 28 extreme-pHopt entries in the EpHod dataset against literature and identifying six erroneous annotations, these data flaws raise critical scientific concerns regarding the performance and reliability of Seq2pHopt and EpHod when predicting pHopt of extremely acidic and alkaline enzymes. Moreover, besides these errors in extreme-pH annotation, the vast majority of entries in the full dataset remain unverified, and additional unrecognized annotation errors likely exist. Importantly, in machine learning research, a rigorously curated and fully validated dataset serves as the indispensable cornerstone of robust and trustworthy predictive modeling, whereas low-quality data may bias model training and evaluation, undermine the reliability of prediction accuracy, and even trigger negative consequences [10]. On one hand, BRENDA undergoes continuous quality control to improve the accuracy of its content [11]. On the other hand, we suggest that data users should inspect datasets prior to usage and employ data that are as error-free as possible, even though such data-checking work is time-consuming and labor-intensive. The focus of this letter is neither on the measures taken to mitigate the impacts of annotation errors, nor on evaluating whether correcting these errors would substantially improve predictive performance. Instead, this letter calls for systematic inspection of the pHopt dataset to support continuous improvements in enzyme-pHopt-prediction accuracy and facilitate the rational design and synthesis of high-performance enzymes.

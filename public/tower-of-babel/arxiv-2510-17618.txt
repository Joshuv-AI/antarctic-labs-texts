Bergman metrics induced by the ball
Matteo Palmieri

Abstract
We investigate when the Bergman metric of a bounded domain is, up to a constant factor λ \lambda , induced by the Bergman metric of a finite-dimensional unit ball 𝔹 N \mathbb{B}^{N} via a holomorphic isometric immersion. For a strictly pseudoconvex domain in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} we prove rigidity: if such an immersion extends smoothly and transversally past the boundary and ( N + 1 ) / λ − 3 ∈ ℕ (N+1)/\lambda-3\in\mathbb{N} , then the domain is biholomorphic to the ball. We then consider two broad classes of examples: Hartogs domains over bounded homogeneous bases and egg domains over irreducible symmetric bases, and show that, in finite target dimension, the only members whose (rescaled) Bergman metric is induced by that of a ball are the balls themselves. The proofs combine Calabi’s diastasis criterion with explicit Bergman kernel formulas (such as Fefferman’s expansion) and algebraic arguments that force arithmetic constraints on the scaling factor. In higher dimensions, the first result follows under a Ramadanov-type assumption.



keywords
Bounded domain, Bergman kernel, Bergman metric, Kähler immersion

1
Introduction

1.1
The Bergman kernel and metric

Since their introduction by S. Bergman in 1922 (see
Bergman 1922
), the Bergman kernel and metric have covered a crucial role in several topics of geometrical analysis and differential geometry. Recall that, for a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the associated Bergman space is the Hilbert space of holomorphic square-integrable functions

\displaystyle L^{2}_{h}(\Omega)=\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)

If
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
is a complete orthonormal system for
L^{2}_{h}(\Omega)
, the
Bergman kernel
of
\Omega
is given by the expansion

K_{\Omega}(z,\xi)=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\ \phi_{\alpha}(z)\overline{\phi_{\alpha}(\xi)}

(1)

This formula is far from optimal to find closed forms of
K_{\Omega}
for
\Omega
arbitrary. Nonetheless, it is well-known (e.g. by choosing a specific complete orthonormal system) that the function
\Phi(z):=\log K_{\Omega}(z,z),\ z\in\Omega
, is strictly plurisubharmonic, so

\displaystyle\omega_{\Omega}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi

defines a Kähler form on
\Omega
. The associated Kähler metric
g_{\Omega}
is called the
Bergman metric
of
\Omega
. It is remarkable that if
h\colon\Omega_{1}\to\Omega_{2}
is a biholomorphism of bounded domains, the Bergman kernel for
\Omega_{1}
transforms as

K_{\Omega_{1}}(z,z)=|\det(J(h)(z))|^{2}K_{\Omega_{2}}(h(z),h(z))

(2)

where
J(h)
is the Jacobian matrix of
h
, which implies that biholomorphisms between bounded domains are isometries with respect to the Bergman metrics.

The most basic and notorious model is given by the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
, for which

K_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(z,z)=\frac{n!}{\pi^{n}}\frac{1}{(1-||z||^{2})^{n+1}}\ ,\quad\omega_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}=(n+1)\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log(1-||z||^{2})

(3)

where
||\cdot||
is the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Notice that, up to the scalar factor
(n+1)
,
g_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
coincides with the hyperbolic metric on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
of constant holomorphic sectional curvature
-4
. The interested reader can refer to
Krantz 2013
for further literature on the Bergman kernel and metric.

Throughout the last century, mathematicians dedicated their efforts to unraveling the relations between the geometric behavior of
\Omega
and the properties of
g_{\Omega}
. Great attention has been given to the general problem of understanding what conditions on
g_{\Omega}
imply that
\Omega
must be biholomorphic to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

A crucial result in this direction is
Lu’s Uniformization Theorem

Lu 1966
, which establishes that if
g_{\Omega}
is complete with constant holomorphic sectional curvature (c.h.s.c. in the sequel) then
\Omega
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. Very recently, Huang, Li and Treuer
Huang et al. 2025
relaxed the hypothesis of completeness and assuming instead pseudoconvexity (see
Bremermann 1955
for the relationship between completeness and pseudoconvexity) showed that
\Omega
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with possibly a pluripolar set removed. Shortly later, Ebenfelt, Treuer and Xiao
Ebenfelt et al. 2025
maintained only the curvature hypothesis and demonstrated that
\Omega
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with possibly a measure zero set removed, over which
L^{2}_{h}
functions extend holomorphically to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

Other curvature conditions gained relevance in this context, such as the Einstein condition for
g_{\Omega}
. In his renowned problem section
Yau 1982
Yau asked (in a slightly different way) if the completeness and Einstein hypotheses on
g_{\Omega}
imply that
\Omega
is homogeneous, that is, its biholomorphisms group acts transitively on
\Omega
. This is a natural question, since it follows by
Bergman and Schiffer 1951
,
Cheng and Yau 1980
that the converse is true. Furthermore, assuming this conjecture holds, it follows by the work of Rosay
Rosay 1979
that the only smoothly bounded pseudoconvex domain with Bergman-Einstein metric is the ball. This has been cleared in the strict pseudoconvexity case, also known as the Cheng conjecture (see
Fu and Wong 1997
for the precise statement), in dimension two by Fu and Wong
Fu and Wong 1997
and Nemirovskii and Shafikov
Nemirovskii and Shafikov 2006
and then in the general case by Huang and Xiao
Huang and Xiao 2020
.

In this paper, we will focus on yet another condition: the existence of a holomorphic isometric immersion (
Kähler immersion
in the sequel) of
(\Omega,g_{\Omega})
into the ball. More precisely, we will address the following:

Question 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded domain. When, eventually after rescaling, is
g_{\Omega}
induced by
g_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}
? Namely, under what conditions is there a Kähler immersion
(\Omega,\lambda g_{\Omega})\to(\mathbb{B}^{N},g_{\mathbb{C}^{N}})
?

This condition can be seen as a generalization of the c.h.s.c. one (a Kähler immersion
(\Omega,\lambda g_{\Omega})\to(\mathbb{B}^{n},g_{\mathbb{B}^{n}})
is locally a biholomorphic isometry by dimensional reasons, so it preserves the holomorphic sectional curvature), and is in general much weaker. Furthermore, it has already led to promising rigidity results. Di Scala and Loi used it
Di Scala and Loi 2007
to prove that among all the Hermitian symmetric spaces of noncompact type, the hyperbolic spaces are the only one which admits a Kähler immersion, after rescaling, into
\mathbb{B}^{N}
. This applies in particular to the case of
\Omega
symmetric. Later on, Di Scala, Ishi and Loi
Di Scala et al. 2012
broadened the result to homogeneous Kähler manifolds (valid in particular to the case of
\Omega
homogeneous). In
Hao and Wang 2014
, Hao and Wang found some constrictions on the existence of Kähler immersions into Fubini–Study spaces for bounded pseudoconvex Hartogs domains.

1.2
Statement of the results

The purpose of this work is to present three rigidity results for the ball involving the above mentioned condition. In Section
2
, the first part of this article, we recall the work of Calabi
Calabi 1953
on Kähler immersions into Fubini–Study spaces. It will be of great use to prove the main results of this article, which will be addressed in the second part of this article, namely Section
3
and Section
4
.

The first result we present is the following (motivational) theorem. From now on, “
cl
” denotes the topological closure.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
be a simply connected strictly pseudoconvex bounded domain with
C^{1}
-regular boundary. If there is
\lambda>0
such that
\frac{N+1}{\lambda}-3\in\mathbb{N}
and a Kähler immersion
f\colon(\Omega,\lambda g_{\Omega})\to(\mathbb{B}^{N},g_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}),\ N<\infty
, that extends smoothly to
F\colon U\to V
, where
U,V
are neighborhoods of
\text{cl}(\Omega),\text{cl}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, such that
F(\partial\Omega)\subseteq\partial\mathbb{B}^{N}
transversally and
F(U\smallsetminus\text{cl}(\Omega))\subseteq V\smallsetminus\text{cl}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, then
\Omega\cong\mathbb{B}^{2}
.

The algebraic assumption on the rescaling parameter might seem unnatural. It is quite the contrary, as we will highlight in Section
3
how the Kähler immersion condition imposes strict algebraic constraints. The proof involves Fefferman’s expansion for the Bergman kernel
Fefferman 1974
, and the key step is to show that the log-term of the expansion vanishes to infinite order when approaching
\partial\Omega
. This is achieved by combining the additional hypotheses on the Kähler immersion and the rescaling parameter. The conclusion follows by applying Ramadanov’s conjecture
Ramadanov 1981
, established in dimension two by Boutet de Monvel
Boutet de Monvel 1987-1988
, which yields that
\partial\Omega
is spherical, and the uniformization results of Nemirovskii and Shafikov
Nemirovskii and Shafikov 2005
.

In the second part of this paper, namely Section
3
, we demonstrate two rigidity results for families of domains that are somewhat easy to write down, but rich enough (see
Azukawa and Suzuki 1983
,
D’Angelo 1994
,
Hao and Wang 2014
,
Pan et al. 2016
,
Ishi et al. 2016
,
Yin et al. 2004
) to display nontrivial behavior of kernels, metrics, automorphisms, and boundary regularity. Hence, they function as testbeds for conjectures and counterexamples. For instance, in the counterexample of the conjecture that the universal covering of a compact Kähler manifold of negative sectional curvature should be biholomorphic to the unit ball, the explicit computation of their Bergman kernel greatly contributed (see
Mostow and Siu 1980
). Great interest into them has also been shown towards the Lu Qi-Keng’s problem (see
Boas 2000
,
Ishi et al. 2016
). Specifically, we will prove:

Theorem 2
.

Up to biholomorphisms, the only Hartogs-type domain based on a bounded homogeneous domain whose Bergman metric is induced, eventually after rescaling, by the Bergman metric of
\mathbb{B}^{N},\ N<\infty
, is the ball.

Theorem 3
.

Up to biholomorphisms, the only egg domain based on an irreducible symmetric domain whose Bergman metric is induced, eventually after rescaling, by the Bergman metric of
\mathbb{B}^{N},\ N<\infty
, is the ball.

The argument for both these results is based on the explicit form of the Bergman kernel for Hartogs-type and egg domains, obtained when the basis has a high degree of symmetry. Calabi’s criterion is used to obtain algebraic relations involving the constants that define the domain, which result in strong constraints through the use of algebraic methods inspired by holomorphic Nash algebraic functions. Finally, the condition
N<\infty
ensures rigidity for the ball.

2
Kähler immersions into Fubini–Study spaces

Recall that a
complex space form
is a connected complete Kähler manifold of constant holomorphic sectional curvature
c
. It is well-known (see
Kobayashi and Nomizu 1996
, Theorem
7.9
) that there are only three models of simply connected complex space forms, up to biholomorphic isometries, distinguished by the sign of
c
. If we set
c=4b
, these types are:

[a]

•

the complex Euclidean space
\mathbb{C}^{N},\ N\leq\infty
, with the flat metric
g_{0}
(
b=0
). Here,
\mathbb{C}^{\infty}:=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{C})
is the complex Hilbert space of sequences
z=(z_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N^{+}}}
of complex numbers such that
||z||^{2}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|z_{k}|^{2}<\infty
;

•

the complex hyperbolic space
\mathbb{CH}^{N}:=\mathbb{B}^{N}_{\frac{1}{-b}},\ N\leq\infty
, with the hyperbolic metric
\frac{1}{-b}g_{hyp}
(
b<0
). Its Kähler form is given by

\displaystyle\omega_{hyp}=\frac{1}{b}\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log(1+b||z||^{2})

Here,
\mathbb{CH}^{\infty}
is the ball of radius
\frac{1}{-b}
in
\mathbb{C}^{\infty}
;

•

the complex projective space
\mathbb{CP}^{N},\ N\leq\infty
, with the Fubini–Study metric
\frac{1}{b}g_{FS}
(
b>0
). Its Kähler form is given in homogeneous coordinates
[Z_{0},\dots,Z_{N}]
in the affine charts
U_{j}=\{Z_{j}\neq 0\}
,
j=0,\dots,N
by

\displaystyle\omega_{FS}=\frac{1}{b}\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log\left(1+\sum_{i\neq j}\left|\frac{Z_{i}}{Z_{j}}\right|^{2}\right)

Here,
\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
is the projectivization of
\mathbb{C}^{\infty}
.

We denote these spaces, often called
Fubini–Study spaces
, by
F(N,b)
. In
Calabi 1953
, Calabi characterized the existence and behavior of Kähler immersions of a real analytic Kähler manifold
(M,g)
into
F(N,b)
by means of a special function. Starting from a real analytic Kähler potential
\phi
for
g
and a coordinate system
\varphi
around
p\in M
, the
diastasis function
around
p
is given by

\displaystyle D(z,w)=\phi(z,\bar{z})+\phi(w,\bar{w})-\phi(z,\bar{w})-\phi(w,\bar{z})

where
z,w
are coordinates in
\varphi
of points around
p
and
\phi(z,\bar{w})
is the analytic continuation of
\phi
to a neighborhood of
(p,p)
in
M\times\overline{M}
(here
\overline{M}
is the conjugate of
M
). If
\varphi(p)=w_{0}
, the function

\displaystyle D_{p}(z)=D(z,w_{0})

is still a real analytic Kähler potential for
g
around
p
, called the
diastasis potential
at
p
. It is remarkable that
D,\ D_{p}
do not depend on
\phi
nor
\varphi
, and that the isometry condition for a holomorphic map
f\colon M\to N
of real analytic Kähler manifolds translates to the preservation of the diastasis potential (see
Calabi 1953
, Proposition
1-6
):

f^{*}g^{N}=g^{M}\quad\iff\quad\forall\ p\in M:\ D_{p}^{M}=D^{N}_{f(p)}\circ f

(4)

The interested reader can also find a recent and detailed exposition about this subject in
Loi and Zedda 2018
. In the present work, the following example are of great relevance:

Example 1
.

By the properties of
K_{\Omega}
, the Kähler manifold
(\Omega,g_{\Omega})
is real analytic. For
p\in\Omega
, the diastasis around
p
is globally defined on
\Omega
and given in the usual coordinates by

D^{\Omega}(z,w)=\log\left(\frac{K_{\Omega}(z,z)K_{\Omega}(w,w)}{|K_{\Omega}(z,w)|^{2}}\right)

(5)

Example 2
.

For
p\in F(N,b),\ b\neq 0
: the diastasis around
p
is given in Bochner coordinates centered at
p
by

D^{b}(z,w)=\frac{1}{b}\log(1+b\sum_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}\bar{w}_{i})

(6)

Moreover, if
b>0
: the diastasis around any point can be written in homogeneous coordinates
Z=[Z_{0},\dots,Z_{N}],\ W=[W_{0},\dots,W_{N}]
as

D^{b}(Z,W)=\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{(\sum_{i=0}^{N}|Z_{i}|^{2})(\sum_{i=0}^{N}|W_{i}|^{2})}{(\sum_{i=0}^{N}Z_{i}\overline{W}_{i})}\right)

(7)

Example 3
.

If
(M,g)
is a real analytic Kähler manifold, then
\forall\ \lambda>0
:
(M,\lambda g)
is again real analytic and if
D^{g},\ D^{\lambda g}
are the diastasis functions for
g,\lambda g

D^{\lambda g}=\lambda D^{g}

(8)

The main tool we need from Calabi’s work is the so-called
Calabi’s local criterion
(see
Calabi 1953
, Theorem
8
), which we state in the context of our interest. Suppose
\text{dim}(M)=n
, and fix the lexicographic order for the set of multi-indexes
\mathbb{N}^{n}
. For
b\neq 0
,
p\in M
and coordinates
z=z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
around
p
, consider the expansion on a neighborhood of
p

\displaystyle\frac{e^{bD_{p}(z)}-1}{b}=\sum_{j,k=0}^{\infty}\ s_{jk}(z(p))\ (z-z(p))^{m_{j}}\overline{(z-z(p))^{m_{k}}}

where we adopted the multi-index convention
w^{m_{l}}=w_{1}^{m_{l1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot w_{n}^{m_{ln}}
. Then, it holds:

Theorem 4

(
Calabi’s local criterion
)
.

There is a neighborhood of
p
which admits, when endowed with the induced metric of
M
, a Kähler immersion into
F(N,b),\ b<0
, if and only if the infinite-dimensional matrix
(s_{jk}(z(p)))_{j,k}
is positive semidefinite of rank at most
N
.

Since the diastasis depends only on
g
, the statement of the theorem is independent of the choice of coordinates around
p
. If
M
is connected, it is proved (see
Calabi 1953
, Theorem
10
) that the minimum possible dimension
N
for which any point of
M
has a neighborhood which can be Kähler immersed into
F(N,b)
also depends only on
g
. We refer to these (local) Kähler immersions as
full
, and any two full (local) Kähler immersions into the same Fubini–Study space differ by a rigid motion of the codomain: this fact is known as
Calabi’s rigidity
(see
Calabi 1953
, Theorem
9
).

Remark 1
.

The spaces
F(N,b)
act as ambient spaces in which real analytic Kähler manifolds live. We remark here that here is always a Kähler immersion
(\Omega,g_{\Omega})\to F(\infty,1)
, for if
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in N^{+}}
is a complete orthonormal system for
L^{2}_{h}(\Omega)
, the full holomorphic map

\displaystyle f\colon\Omega\to F(\infty,1):z\mapsto[\phi_{1}(z):\phi_{2}(z):\dots]

satisfies
D^{1}_{f(p)}\circ f=D^{\Omega}_{p}\,\ \forall\ p\in\Omega
, as it can be easily seen due to (
1
), (
5
), (
7
). It follows by Calabi’s rigidity that
(\Omega,g_{\Omega})
cannot be Kähler immersed, not even after rescaling, into
F(N,b)
with both
b>0
and
N<\infty
. On the other hand, we are well-aware of the existence of a holomorphic immersion of
(\Omega,g_{\Omega})
into
F(N,b)
for
N\geq n,\ b<0
, since
\Omega
is bounded. Hence, Question
1
emerges as very natural from this viewpoint.

3
Transversal extensions of Kähler immersions

3.1
Fefferman’s expansion

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a smoothly bounded and strictly pseudoconvex domain. By a profound result of Fefferman (see
Fefferman 1974
, Corollary at p.
45
) there is an asymptotic expansion

K_{\Omega}(z,z)=\frac{\phi_{\Omega}(z)}{\psi_{\Omega}^{n+1}(z)}+\widetilde{\phi_{\Omega}}(z)\log(\psi_{\Omega}(z))\ ,\quad z\in\Omega

(9)

where
\phi_{\Omega},\ \widetilde{\phi_{\Omega}}\in C^{\infty}(\text{cl}(\Omega))
with
\phi_{\Omega}|_{\partial\Omega}\neq 0
and
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\text{cl}(\Omega))
is a
defining function
for
\Omega
, that is, it satisfies

\displaystyle\Omega=\{\psi>0\}\ ,\quad\partial\Omega=\{\psi=0\}\ ,\quad\nabla\psi|_{\partial\Omega}\neq 0

The asymptotic behavior of
K_{\Omega}
near any point of
\partial\Omega
depends only on the local CR geometry of
\partial\Omega
at the point, as it was highlighted in
Fefferman 1974
. This fact is reflected by the properties of
\phi_{\Omega},\ \widetilde{\phi_{\Omega}}
: we will now briefly recall one relevant instance for our discussion.

Comparing (
3
) and (
9
), we see that
\widetilde{\phi}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
is identically zero. What’s more, it can be proved that the coefficient
\widetilde{\phi_{\Omega}}
of the logarithmic term in (
9
) vanishes to infinite order at the boundary for
\partial\Omega
spherical. Ramadanov conjectured
Ramadanov 1981
that the converse is also true, namely the vanishing to infinite order at the boundary of
\widetilde{\phi_{\Omega}}
implies that
\partial\Omega
is spherical. This result was cleared by Boutet de Monvel
Boutet de Monvel 1987-1988
in dimension two, while the general case is still widely open. It is also worth mentioning, within the scope of this article, that the Cheng conjecture actually follows from the Ramadanov conjecture (see
Nemirovskii and Shafikov 2006
, Theorem at p.
781
).

3.2
Transversality

Let
\Omega_{1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n},\Omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
be bounded domains with
C^{1}
-regular boundaries,
A_{1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
A_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
be neighborhoods of
\text{cl}(\Omega_{1}),\text{cl}(\Omega_{2})
respectively. A map
\Xi\in C^{1}(A_{1},A_{2})
with
\Xi(\partial\Omega_{1})\subset\partial\Omega_{2}
is
transverse
to
\partial\Omega_{2}
at
x\in\partial\Omega_{1}
if

\displaystyle(D_{x}\Xi)(T_{x}\Omega_{1})+T_{\Xi(x)}\partial\Omega_{2}=T_{\Xi(x)}\Omega_{2}

\Xi
is
transverse along

\partial\Omega_{2}
if it is transverse to
\partial\Omega_{2}
at any point of
\partial\Omega_{1}
. Equivalently, let
\rho_{i}\in C^{1}(A_{i})
be a defining function for
\Omega_{i},\ i=1,2
. Then

\displaystyle\nabla\rho_{i}\ \text{is normal to }\partial\Omega_{i},\ i=1,2

and
\Xi
is transverse to
\partial\Omega_{2}
at
x\in\partial\Omega_{1}
if and only if

\displaystyle\langle(D_{x}\Xi)(\nabla\rho_{1}(x)),\nabla\rho_{2}(\Xi(x))\rangle\neq 0

In order to implement transversality, notice that the following identity holds:

\begin{split}\langle(D_{x}\Xi)(\nabla\rho_{1}(x)),\nabla\rho_{2}(\Xi(x))\rangle&=\langle\nabla\rho_{1}(x),(D_{x}\Xi)^{T}\nabla\rho_{2}(\Xi(x))\rangle=\\
&=\langle\nabla\rho_{1}(x),\nabla(\rho_{2}\circ\Xi)(x)\rangle\end{split}

(10)

Proof of Theorem
1

The first part of the proof can be carried out in any dimension

. Up applying a translation of

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(z)=1-\frac{1}{N+1}||z||^{2}\ ,\quad z\in F(N,-\frac{1}{N+1})

By (

Since

4
Rigidity of Kähler immersions into the ball

4.1
Hartogs-type and egg domains

In Theorem
2
and Theorem
3
, we focus on the following domains built upon sufficiently regular bounded domains. We will adopt the single-argument notation for the Bergman kernel on the diagonal to ease the notation. The complete construction can be found, for each case, in
Ishi et al. 2016
,
Yin et al. 2004
respectively.

For the second result of this work, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded homogeneous domain. We can associate some holomorphic invariants to
\Omega
, which arise by transferring the Hermitian structure given pointwisely by
g_{\Omega}
on
T\Omega
to the Lie algebra of a maximal connected split solvable subgroup of the holomorphic automorphism group of
\Omega
:

\displaystyle r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\ ,\quad\{p_{k},q_{k},b_{k}\}_{k=1,\dots,r}\subset\mathbb{N}

The constant
r
is called
rank
of
\Omega
. If, for any
k=1,\dots,r
, we set

\displaystyle a_{ki}:=\frac{i+\frac{q_{k}}{2}}{p_{k}+q_{k}+b_{k}+2}\ ,\quad 1\leq i\leq 1+p_{k}+b_{k}

we can consider
C_{\Omega}:=\underset{i,k}{\min}\{a_{ki}\}>0

4.2
Holomorphic Nash algebraic functions

Let
V
be a complex vector space of dimension
t\geq 1
,
A\subset V
be open,
f
holomorphic on
A
. Recall that
f
is called
Nash algebraic
at

if there is a neighborhood

of

and a polynomial

, such that

\displaystyle P(y,f(y))=0\quad\forall\ y\in U

and
f
is called a
holomorphic Nash algebraic function
if it is Nash algebraic at each point of
A
. The interested reader can refer to
Tworzewski 1990
for further literature on the topic.

Let
\mathcal{N}^{t}
be the set of real analytic functions defined in a neighborhood
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{t}
of

whose real analytic continuation to a neighborhood of
(0,0)\in\mathbb{C}^{t}\times\overline{\mathbb{C}^{t}}
is a holomorphic Nash algebraic function. Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{t}=\{\eta(f_{1},\dots,f_{t}):\eta\in\mathcal{N}^{t},\ f_{j}\in\mathcal{O}_{0}\text{ and }f_{j}(0)=0\ \forall\ j=1,\dots,t\}

where
\mathcal{O}_{0}
denotes the germ of holomorphic functions at
0\in\mathbb{C}
, consider
\mathcal{G}^{t}
the set of real analytic functions around
0
whose expansion at any point does not contain non constant purely holomorphic or anti-holomorphic terms, and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{t}:=\mathcal{F}^{t}\cap\mathcal{G}^{t}

Then, the following theorem holds (see
Loi and Mossa 2024
, Theorem
2.2
):

Theorem 5
.

Let
\psi_{l}=\eta_{l}(f_{l,1},\dots,f_{l,t})\in\mathcal{H}^{t},\ l=0,\dots,s
, satisfy for some
c_{1},\dots,c_{s}\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\psi_{s}^{c_{s}}=\psi_{0}\ ,\quad\psi_{0}(0)\neq 0

If
\{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},1\}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, then
\psi_{l}
is constant
\forall\ l=1,\dots,s
.

For our purposes, we will need an immediate consequence of this result, which we state below for clarity:

Lemma 1
.

Let
\psi_{l}=\eta_{l}(f_{l,1},\dots,f_{l,t})\in\mathcal{H}^{t},\ l=0,1
, satisfy for some

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{c}=\psi_{0}\ ,\quad\psi_{0}(0)\neq 0

If
\psi_{1}
is not constant, then
c\in\mathbb{Q}
.

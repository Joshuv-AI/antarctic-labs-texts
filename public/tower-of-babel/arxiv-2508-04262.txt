One-weight codes in the sum-rank metric
Usman Mushrraf, Ferdinando Zullo

Abstract
. One-weight codes, in which all nonzero codewords share the same weight, form a highly structured class of linear codes with deep connections to finite geometry. While their classification is well understood in the Hamming and rank metrics—being equivalent to (direct sums of) simplex codes—the sum-rank metric presents a far more intricate landscape. In this work, we explore the geometry of one-weight sum-rank metric codes, focusing on three distinct classes. First, we introduce and classify constant rank-list sum-rank codes, where each nonzero codeword has the same tuple of ranks, extending results from the rank-metric setting. Next, we investigate the more general constant rank-profile codes, where, up to reordering, each nonzero codeword has the same tuple of ranks. Although a complete classification remains elusive, we present the first examples and partial structural results for this class. Finally, we consider one-weight codes that are also MSRD (Maximum Sum-Rank Distance) codes. For dimension two, constructions arise from partitions of scattered linear sets on projective lines. For dimension three, we connect their existence to that of special 2 2 -fold blocking sets in the projective plane, leading to new bounds and nonexistence results over certain fields.

1.
Introduction

In the study of linear codes over finite fields with the Hamming metric, a one-weight code is a linear code in which all nonzero codewords have the same Hamming weight. These codes are rare and highly structured, and their classification reveals deep connections between coding theory and finite geometry.
The simplest example of a one-weight code is the repetition code, where all nonzero codewords are scalar multiples of a single vector of full support. A classical family of one-weight codes is given by the
simplex codes
(also known as MacDonald codes
[
14
]
), which are the duals of Hamming codes. Over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the simplex code has parameters
\left[\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1},\ m,\ q^{m-1}\right]
, for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and all nonzero codewords have Hamming weight
q^{m-1}
.

Linear one-weight codes in the Hamming metric have been classified as precisely those codes that are equivalent to direct sums of simplex codes. This result has been established in several papers using a variety of techniques; see
[
7
,
24
,
3
,
31
]
.

The notion of one-weight codes naturally extends to the rank metric. In
[
26
]
(see also
[
1
]
), Randrianarisoa proved a rank-metric analogue of the classification for linear one-weight codes in the Hamming metric. Specifically, he showed that all linear one-weight rank-metric codes are equivalent to the simplex rank-metric code, i.e., a code with parameters
[mk,k,m]_{q^{m}/q}
.

Surprisingly, this classification does not extend to the sum-rank metric. In
[
22
]
, the authors presented classes of sum-rank metric codes of dimension two that are not equivalent to the simplex sum-rank code. Later, in
[
8
]
, the authors found examples of one-weight sum-rank metric codes of arbitrary dimension that are also not equivalent to the simplex sum-rank code, demonstrating that the variety of one-weight sum-rank codes is significantly richer.

These results suggest that obtaining a classification of one-weight codes in the sum-rank metric is likely to be a very challenging task.

In this paper, using a geometric perspective, we provide new insights into the theory of one-weight sum-rank metric codes. We focus on two classes of such codes.
First, we consider sum-rank metric codes in which every nonzero codeword has the same list of ranks—that is, the same vector of local ranks across the blocks. We refer to these as
constant rank-list
sum-rank metric codes. For this class, we provide a classification result, building on the work of Randrianarisoa in
[
26
]
.

Next, we study codes in which every nonzero codeword has the same rank profile, meaning the same multiset of local ranks (i.e., the rank-list up to reordering). We call these
constant rank-profile
sum-rank metric codes. While a full classification remains out of reach, we present the first known examples of such codes and give a characterization in certain cases. Here again, the geometric approach plays a central role.

The final class of one-weight codes we study consists of those that are also MSRD codes, i.e., codes that are optimal with respect to the Singleton bound. As we point out, constructing one-weight MSRD codes of dimension two can be obtained via a partition of scattered linear sets on the projective lines. However, the case of dimension three is already far from trivial, as also noted in
[
16
]
.
By employing counting arguments, we relate the existence of such codes to the existence of a particular type of
2
-fold blocking set in the projective plane. This connection allows us to derive bounds on the number of blocks in a putative one-weight MSRD code of dimension three, as well as to prove some nonexistence results in the case
q=2
and for field extensions of degree two.

1.1.
Organization of the paper

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section 2 introduces the preliminaries on linear sets. In Section 3, we review the basics of sum-rank metric codes and their geometric interpretation. Section 4 is devoted to a special class of one-weight codes based on a constant rank-list in the context of the sum-rank setting. In Section 5, we present another family of one-weight codes characterized by a constant rank-profile and explore their properties. Finally, in Section 6, we explore one-weight MSRD codes with particular attention to the cases where the dimension is two or three.

2.
Preliminaries on linear sets

In this paper, we use the geometric perspective of sum-rank metric codes, which is closely related to the theory of linear sets. Therefore, we will quickly review the definition and some basic properties of linear sets.
Let
\mathbb{V}
be a
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-vector space and let
\Lambda=\mathrm{PG}(\mathbb{V},{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})=\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
.
Clearly,
\mathbb{V}
can also be seen as both an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-vector space of dimension
k
and as an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space of dimension
mk
. We can consider an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U
of
\mathbb{V}
and define the set

L_{U}=\{\langle{u}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\colon{u}\in U\setminus\{{0}\}\}

as an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-
linear set
of rank
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)
.
The
weight
of a projective subspace
S=\mathrm{PG}(W,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})\subseteq\Lambda
in
L_{U}
is defined as
w_{L_{U}}(S)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap W)
.

Let us review some fundamental relationships involving the size of a linear set, the count of points with a specific weight, and its rank.
If
L_{U}
has rank
n
then the weight of any point is bounded by
n
. Denote by
N_{i}(U)
the number of points of
\Lambda
having weight
i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
in
L_{U}
, the following relations hold:

(1)

|L_{U}|\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1},

(2)

|L_{U}|=N_{1}(U)+\ldots+N_{n}(U),

(3)

N_{1}(U)+N_{2}(U)(q+1)+\ldots+N_{n}(U)(q^{n-1}+\ldots+q+1)=q^{n-1}+\ldots+q+1.

For further details we refer to
[
25
]
.

3.
Sum-rank metric codes and their geometry

We fix now the notation that we will use for the whole paper. For us
q
is a prime power and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is the finite field with
q
elements. We will often consider the degree
m
extension field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Let
t
be a positive integer. From now on

\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}),\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{t})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{t}
will always be ordered tuples with
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\ldots\geq n_{t}
and
m_{1}\geq m_{2},\geq\ldots\geq m_{t}
, and we set
N\coloneqq n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
. We use the following compact notations for the direct sum of vector spaces

{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}\times m_{i}}.

For a vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
, we define

\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{a}}=\mathcal{S}_{a_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{S}_{a_{r}},

where
\mathcal{S}_{i}
is the symmetric group of order
i
. Similarly, we denote by
\GL(\mathbf{a},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
the direct product of the general linear groups of degree
a_{i}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, i.e.

\GL(\mathbf{a},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})=\GL(a_{1},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\times\ldots\times\GL(a_{r},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}).

We will first recall the framework of matrices for sum-rank metric codes and later the vectorial framework.

3.1.
Matrix codes

In this section we recall the basic notions of sum-rank-metric codes seen as elements in
\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, that will be useful for the rest of the paper. The interested reader is referred to
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
for a more detailed description of this setting.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X\coloneqq(X_{1},\dots,X_{t})\in\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
The
sum-rank support
of
X
is defined as the space

\supp(X)\coloneqq(\colsp(X_{1}),\colsp(X_{2}),\ldots,\colsp(X_{t}))\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}},

where
\colsp(X_{i})
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
X_{i}
.
The
rank-list
of
X
is defined as

\rho(X)\coloneqq(\dim(\colsp(X_{1})),\dots,\dim(\colsp(X_{t})))\in\mathbb{N}^{t}.

The
rank-profile
of
X
is the
t
-uple obtained from
\rho(X)
by rearranging its entries in non-increasing order and it is denoted by
\mu(X)
. Finally, the
sum-rank weight
of
X
is the quantity

\ww_{\mathrm{srk}}(X)\coloneqq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\supp(X))=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\rk(X_{i}).

With these definition in mind, we can endow the space
\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
with a distance function, called the
sum-rank distance
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{srk}}:\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\times\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}

defined by

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{srk}}(X,Y)\coloneqq\ww_{\mathrm{srk}}(X-Y).

Therefore, we can now give the definition of sum-rank metric codes.

Definition 3.2
.

A
sum-rank metric code

\mathcal{C}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear subspace of
\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
endowed with the sum-rank distance.
The
minimum sum-rank distance
of a sum-rank code
\mathcal{C}
is defined as usual via

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{srk}}(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\min\{\ww_{\mathrm{srk}}(X)\,:\,X\in\mathcal{C},X\neq\mathbf{0}\}.

The
sum-rank support
of the code
\mathcal{C}
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the supports of all the codewords of
\C
, that is

\supp(\C)\coloneqq\sum_{X\in\C}\supp(X)\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}}.

Finally, we say that
\mathcal{C}
is
sum-rank nondegenerate
if
\supp(\mathcal{C})={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}}
.

For the nondegeneracy notion see also
[
28
]
.

3.2.
Vector codes

Let us focus on the scenario where
m=m_{1}=\cdots=m_{t}
. We consider an alternate setting for the study of sum-rank metric codes, where codewords are vectors with entries from an extension field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
instead of matrices over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. For a comprehensive explanation of this, see
[
19
,
17
,
15
,
21
,
23
,
18
]
.

The
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-rank
of a vector
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-dimension of the
vector space generated over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
by its entries, i.e,

\rk_{q}(v)\coloneqq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}).

Let
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, with
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
for any
i
. We can now extend the notion of rank to a
t
-ple of vectors, defining the
sum-rank weight of
x
as the quantity

\ww(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\rk_{q}(x_{i}).

Consequently, the

sum-rank distance on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, is

\dd(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\rk_{q}(x_{i}-y_{i}),

for any
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}),y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, with
x_{i},y_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
.

Therefore, a sum-rank metric code can be defined in this framework as follows.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
k
be a positive integer with
1\leq k\leq N
. A
sum-rank metric code

\C
is a
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
endowed with the sum-rank metric.
The
minimum sum-rank distance
of
\C
is the integer

\dd(\C)=\min\{\dd(x,y)\,:\,x,y\in\C,x\neq y\}=\min\{\ww(x)\,:\,x\in\C,x\neq 0\}.

We will write that
\C
is an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code if
k
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-dimension of
\C
and
d
is its minimum distance, or simply an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code if the minimum distance is not relevant/known.

Since the elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
can also be seen as long vectors in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{N}
, every
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code can be provided with generator and parity-check matrices. Each of them, is naturally partitioned as
G=(G_{1}\mid\ldots\mid G_{t})
, where
G_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n_{i}}
(respectively
H=(H_{1}\mid\ldots\mid H_{t})
, where
H_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{(N-k)\times n_{i}}
).

For vector sum-rank metric codes, a Singleton-like bound holds.

Theorem 3.4

(see
[
19
, Proposition 16]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Then

d\leq N-k+1.

An
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code is called a
Maximum Sum-Rank Distance code
(or shortly
MSRD code
) if
d=N-k+1
.

The next result establishes the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of the space
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
endowed with the sum-rank distance, and it has been proved in
[
2
, Theorem 3.7]
. The case
\mathbf{n}=(n,\ldots,n)
was already proved in
[
20
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

The group of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of the space
({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}},\dd)
is

(({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\ast})^{t}\times\GL(\mathbf{n},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}))\rtimes\mathcal{S}_{\lambda(\mathbf{n})},

which (right)-acts as

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\cdot(\mathbf{a},A_{1},\ldots,A_{t},\pi)\longmapsto(a_{1}x_{\pi(1)}A_{1}\mid\ldots\mid a_{t}x_{\pi(t)}A_{t}).

Given that MacWilliams’s extension theorem does not hold in this context (refer to
[
5
]
), we will define the equivalence of sum-rank metric codes using
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of the entire ambient space.

Definition 3.6
.

We say that two
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
sum-rank metric codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{1},{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
are
equivalent
if there is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometry
\phi
such that
\phi({\mathcal{C}}_{1})={\mathcal{C}}_{2}
.

We denote the set of equivalence classes of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
sum-rank metric codes by
\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
.

3.3.
Supports and degeneracy of sum-rank metric codes

It is known that to a vector sum-rank metric code we can associate a matrix sum-rank metric code with the same parameters and metric properties in the following way.

For every
r\in[t]
, let
\Gamma_{r}=(\gamma_{1}^{(r)},\ldots,\gamma_{m}^{(r)})
be an ordered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, and let
\Gamma\coloneqq(\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{t})
. Given
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, with
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
, define the element
\Gamma(x)=(\Gamma_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,\Gamma_{t}(x_{t}))\in\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, where
\mathbf{m}=(m,\ldots,m)
and

x_{r,i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma_{r}(x_{r})_{ij}\gamma_{j}^{(r)},\qquad\mbox{ for all }i\in[n_{r}].

In other words, the
r
-th block of
\Gamma(x)
is the matrix expansion of the vector
x_{r}
with respect to the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis
\Gamma_{r}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
.

One can observe that the map

\Gamma:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\longrightarrow\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})

is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear isometry between the metric spaces
({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}},\dd)
and
(\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}),\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{srk}})
; see
[
22
, Theorem 2.7]
.
This demonstrates that the two settings, namely the matrix framework and the vectorial one, are isometric. Consequently, we can operate within both contexts.

This isometry enables us to express support, rank profile, and rank-list within the vector framework; see
[
15
]
.

Definition 3.7
.

The
sum-rank support
of an element
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
is the tuple

\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(x)\coloneqq\supp(\Gamma(x)),

for any choice of
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{t})
, where
\Gamma_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
for each
i\in[t]
.
The
rank-list
of
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})
is the
t
-uple
\rho(x)=(\rk_{q}(x_{1}),\ldots,\rk_{q}(x_{t}))
. The
rank-profile
of
x
is the
t
-uple obtained from
\rho(x)
by rearranging the entries in non-increasing order and it is denoted by
\mu(x)
.

In the same way, we can extend the notions of sum-rank support and degeneracy for an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code.

Definition 3.8
.

We define the
sum-rank support of an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code

\C
as the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the supports of the codewords of
\C
and we denote it by
\supp(\C)
, i.e.

\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(\C)\coloneqq\sum_{c\in\C}\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(c).

Furthermore, we say that
\C
is
sum-rank nondegenerate
if
\supp(\C)={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}}
. We say that
\C
is
sum-rank degenerate
if it is not sum-rank nondegenerate.

3.4.
Geometry of sum-rank metric codes

Hamming-metric codes correspond to projective systems
[
30
, Theorem 1.1.6]
, and rank-metric codes to
q
-systems, their
q
-analogues
[
29
,
26
,
1
]
. The geometric view of sum-rank metric codes has been recently pointed out in
[
22
]
(see also
[
27
]
).
We are going to describe it, as it will be crucial in our study.

Theorem 3.9
.

(see
[
22
, Theorem 3.1]
)
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code with generator matrix
G=(G_{1}|\ldots|G_{t})
.
Let
\mathcal{U}_{i}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
, for
i\in[t]
. Then, for every
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and
i\in[t]
we have

(4)

\ww(vG)=N-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap v^{\perp}),

where
v^{\perp}=\{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\colon v\cdot y=0\}
and
v\cdot y
denotes the standard scalar product between
v
and
y
.
In particular,

d=N-\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap H)\colon H\mbox{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-hyperplane of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

Building upon the last result, the following corollary establishes the link between the rank-list of codewords and the dimension-list of the system.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code with generator matrix
G=(G_{1}\mid\dots\mid G_{t})
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{i}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
, for
i\in[t]
. Then, for every
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and
i\in[t]
, we have

\rho(vG)=(\mathrm{rk}_{q}(vG_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{rk}_{q}(vG_{t}))=(n_{1}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\cap v^{\perp}),\ldots,n_{t}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\cap v^{\perp})).

The above results point out that the metric properties of a code can be completely translated into hyperplane intersection properties with certain subspaces.

Definition 3.11
.

An
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system

\mathcal{U}
is an ordered set
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
, where, for any
i\in[t]
,
\mathcal{U}_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
of dimension
n_{i}
, such that

\langle\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and

d=N-\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H})\,:\,\mathcal{H}\textnormal{ is an ${\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}$-hyperplane of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

In the following we give the definition of equivalent systems.

Definition 3.12
.

Two
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
and
(\mathcal{V}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{V}_{t})
are
equivalent
if there exists an isomorphism
\varphi\in\GL(k,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})
, an element
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{t})\in({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*})^{t}
and a permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
, such that for every
i\in[t]

\varphi(\mathcal{U}_{i})=a_{i}\mathcal{V}_{\sigma(i)}.

We define
\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
as the set of equivalence classes of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems.

Consider
[\C]\in\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
where
\C
is a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Let
G=(G_{1}\,\mid\,\ldots\,\mid\,G_{t})
be a generator matrix for
{\mathcal{C}}
. Consider the equivalence class
[\mathcal{U}]
of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems, where
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
and
\mathcal{U}_{i}
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
for every
i\in[t]
.
We will refer to
\mathcal{U}
as a
system associated with

\C
.
Viceversa, given
[(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})]\in\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
, for every
i\in[t]
, fix an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis
\{g_{i,1},\ldots,g_{i,n_{i}}\}
of
\mathcal{U}_{i}
. Define
G_{i}
as the matrix whose columns are
\{g_{i,1},\ldots,g_{i,n_{i}}\}
and consider the equivalence class of the sum-rank metric code generated by
G=(G_{1}\,\mid\,\ldots\,\mid\,G_{t})
.
We refer to
\mathcal{C}
as a
code associated with

\mathcal{U}
.
Thanks to
[
22
, Theorem 3.7]
, we know that two correspondences define a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
and
\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
.

4.
Constant rank-list sum-rank-metric codes

In this section, we completely classify constant rank-list sum-rank-metric codes, proving a sum-rank metric analogue of the classification of one-weight codes shown in the Hamming metric in
[
7
,
24
,
3
,
31
]
and in the rank metric by Randrianarisoa in
[
26
]
(see also
[
1
]
).

A constant rank-list sum-rank-metric code is a sum-rank metric code in which all the nonzero codewords have the same rank-list.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code and let
(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{t}
.
If for any nonzero codeword
c\in\mathcal{C}
we have
\rho(c)=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
, then we say that
\mathcal{C}
is a
(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
-
constant rank-list
sum-rank metric code.

We will use the following notation.

Notation 4.2
.

Let
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
and let
i\in[t]
, the
i
-th component of
c
, denoted as
c_{|i}
, is defined as

c_{|i}=c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}.

We can now associate to a sum-rank metric code in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
some rank-metric codes.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code.
For each
i\in[t]
, we define the
i
-th projection
of
\mathcal{C}
as

\mathcal{C}_{i}=\{c_{|i}\colon c\in\mathcal{C}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}.

In the sum-rank metric, a constant rank-list implies that the projections of the code are rank-metric codes with a single weight.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
-constant rank-list sum-rank metric code. For any
i\in[t]
, the
i
-th projection
\mathcal{C}_{i}
of
\mathcal{C}
is a one-weight rank-metric code in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
with minimum distance
\rho_{i}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a
(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
-constant rank-list sum-rank metric code. For any
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{t})\in\mathcal{C}
we have

\rho(c)=(w(c_{1}),\ldots,w(c_{t}))=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t}),

i.e.
w(c_{i})=\rho_{i}
for any
i\in[t]
.
Therefore, the assertion is proved.
∎

A class of one-weight rank-metric codes is the following.

Definition 4.5
.

A rank-metric code with parameters
[km,k,m]_{q^{m}/q}
is called
simplex rank-metric code
.

A simplex rank-metric code is clearly a one-weight rank-metric code.
In
[
26
]
(see also
[
1
]
) the one-weight codes in the rank-metric have been classified.

Theorem 4.6

(see
[
1
, Corollary 3.17]
)
.

Let
k\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
one-weight code. Then,
\mathcal{C}
is isometric to a simplex rank-metric
[km,k,m]_{q^{m}/q}
code.

As a consequence of this result, we have also a classification for one-weight nondegenerate rank-metric codes.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
k\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{C}
be a nondegenerate
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
one-weight code. Then
\mathcal{C}
is the simplex rank-metric
[km,k,m]_{q^{m}/q}
code.

The next result discusses about sum-rank nondegenerate codes and explains that the projections of nondegenerate sum-rank metric codes are nondegenerate codes in rank metric, as stated in the following proposition (see also
[
28
, Corollary 3.9]
).

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
sum-rank metric code. For any
i\in[t]
, the
i
-th projection
\mathcal{C}_{i}
of
\mathcal{C}
is a nondegenerate code in the rank metric.

Proof.

Let
i\in[t]
and suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is degenerate for some
i\in[t]
.
This means that
\text{supp}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}}
.
Note that

\text{supp}_{\textbf{n}}(\mathcal{C})_{i}\subseteq\text{supp}(\mathcal{C}_{i}),

since
\text{supp}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}}
we have that
\text{supp}_{\textbf{n}}(\mathcal{C})\neq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\textbf{n}}
.
However, this contradicts our assumption that
\mathcal{C}
is sum-rank nondegenerate.
∎

The following is the main result of this section, which provides a classification of constant rank-list sum-rank-metric codes. This statement serves as a sum-rank metric analogue to the well-established classifications of one-weight codes presented in the Hamming metric and in the rank metric.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
sum-rank metric code. If
\mathcal{C}
is a
(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
-constant rank-list sum-rank metric code then

•

for any
i\in[t]
such that
{k}_{i}=\text{dim}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\geq 2
, we have that
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is an
[mk_{i},k_{i},m]_{q^{m}/q}
code;

•

for any
i\in[t]
such that
\text{dim}(\mathcal{C}_{i})=1
, we have that
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is an
[n_{i},1,n_{i}]_{q^{m}/q}
code;

•

there are no nonzero codewords in
\C
having a zero block.

Conversely, if for
\C
the previous items hold, then
\C
is a nondegenerate constant rank-list sum-rank metric code.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a
(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
-constant rank-list sum-rank metric code.
By Proposition
4.8
, the projections of
\mathcal{C}
are nondegenerate rank-metric codes for all
i\in[t]
. Since each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is a nondegenerate one-weight rank-metric code, we can apply Corollary
4.7
to those codes for which
k_{i}=\dim(\mathcal{C}_{i})\geq 2
. For such projections, the length of each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is
mk_{i}
and their minimum distance is
m
. If
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{i})=1
, then trivially, the parameters of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
are
[n_{i},1,n_{i}]_{q^{m}/q}
.
Finally,
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{i})
cannot be zero as the code
\mathcal{C}
is a nondegenerate sum-rank metric code.
Clearly, there are no nonzero codewords in
\C
having a zero block otherwise we would get a contradiction to the fact that every nonzero codeword has constant rank-list.
The converse also holds because of the fact that the
\C_{i}
’s are one-weight code.
∎

Based on the previous result, we can show some restrictions on the parameters of a nondegenerate sum-rank code with constant rank-list.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
-constant rank-list sum-rank metric code.
For any
i\in[t]
, let
k_{i}=\dim(\mathcal{C}_{i})
.
We have that for every
i\in[t]

•

if
k_{i}\geq 2
, then
n_{i}=mk_{i}
;

•

\rho_{i}=\begin{cases}m&\text{if }k_{i}\geq 2,\\
n_{i}&\text{if }k_{i}=1.\end{cases}

Denote by
\mathcal{I}
the subset of the elements
i\in[t]
for which
k_{i}\geq 2
.
We have that the minimum distance of
\C
is
m|\mathcal{I}|+\sum_{i\in[t]\setminus\mathcal{I}}n_{i}
.

We conclude this section by showing an example of constant rank-list sum-rank metric codes.

Example 4.11
.

Let
\alpha
be a primitive element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and consider

\displaystyle G

5.
Constant rank-profile sum-rank metric codes

In this section, we will study sum-rank metric codes whose nonzero weights have a constant rank-profile; the study of these codes began in
[
22
]
. Although the situation appears very similar to that of constant rank-list sum-rank metric codes, we will demonstrate that examples of such codes exist, but a complete classification result seems quite challenging to achieve.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
sum-rank metric code and let
(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{t})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{t}
such that
\mu_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\mu_{t}
.
We say that
\mathcal{C}
is a
(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{t})
-
constant rank-profile
sum-rank metric code if for every nonzero codeword
c\in\mathcal{C}
we have

\mu(c)=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{t}).

A case study is one where the blocks of the sum-rank metric all have the same length. Indeed, the following necessary conditions have been provided for constant rank-profile sum-rank metric codes by leveraging the connection with the Hamming metric.

Corollary 5.2

(see
[
22
, Corollary 5.8]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a nondegenerate
[(n,\dots,n),k]_{q^{m}/q}

(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{t})
-constant rank-profile sum-rank metric code. Then

(1)

The number

\ell=\frac{t(q^{n}-1)(q^{m}-1)}{(q-1)(q^{km}-1)}

is a positive integer.

(2)

It holds that

tq^{m(k-1)}(q^{n}-1)(q^{m}-1)=(q^{km}-1)\left(tq^{n}-\sum_{i=1}^{t}q^{n-\mu_{i}}\right).

As a first interesting case to study, the authors of
[
22
]
considered the following.

Example 5.3
.

(see
[
22
, Example 5.9]
)
Let us assume that we want to construct a nondegenerate
[(n,\ldots,n),k]_{q^{m}/q}
code with constant rank-profile
(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t})
, and let us fix
n=3
,
k=m=2
.
Using the above corollary, one can get that the constant rank-profile must be

(\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{t^{\prime}q^{2}\text{ {times}}},\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{t^{\prime}\text{ {times}}}),

for some
t^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Our aim is to use the geometric view to get some constructions of constant rank-profile sum-rank metric codes. To this aim, we need the following map.

Notation 5.4
.

Define the following map

\tau:\mathbb{N}^{t}\to\mathbb{N}^{t}

such that for any
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{t})\in\mathbb{N}^{t}
, the image

\tau(a_{1},\dots,a_{t})=(b_{1},\dots,b_{t})

satisfies

b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\dots\geq b_{t},

and
(b_{1},\dots,b_{t})
is a reordering of
(a_{1},\dots,a_{t})
.

We start by giving the geometric interpretation of constant rank-profile sum-rank metric codes.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a nondegenerate
(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{t})
-constant rank-profile sum-rank metric code with parameters
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
. Let
G=(G_{1}\mid\dots\mid G_{t})
be a generator matrix of
\mathcal{C}
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{i}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
, for every
i\in[t]
.
For all
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, we have that

\tau(n_{1}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\cap v^{\perp}),\ldots,n_{t}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\cap v^{\perp}))=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{t}).

Proof.

By Corollary
3.10
, we have the rank-list

(\mathrm{rk}_{q}(vG_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{rk}_{q}(vG_{t}))=(n_{1}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\cap v^{\perp}),\ldots,n_{t}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\cap v^{\perp})).

Since
\C
is constant rank-profile, we have that

\tau(\mathrm{rk}_{q}(vG_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{rk}_{q}(vG_{t}))=\tau(n_{1}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\cap v^{\perp}),\ldots,n_{t}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\cap v^{\perp}))=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{t}),

so that, we obtain the desired result.
∎

In order to provide a construction for Example
5.3
, in the next results of this section we will construct examples of constant rank-list in the case in which the code has dimension two.

Remark 5.6
.

For the geometric point of view, we need to control the intersection of the systems with the hyperplane of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}
, which are the one-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}
.
The number of one-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspaces in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}
is
q^{m}+1
and we can list them as follows

\Lambda_{1}=\{\langle{x}_{1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}},\dots,\langle{x}_{q^{m}+1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\},

for some
{x}_{1},\ldots,{x}_{q^{m}+1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}
.
We will fix this set (and the representatives of the one-dimensional subspaces) throughout this paper.

To construct a family of nondegenerate constant rank-profile sum-rank metric codes we will use the geometric description.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
be the
[\mathbf{n},2]_{q^{m}/q}
system defined as follows

•

\mathbf{n}=(m+e,\ldots,m+e)
, for some
e\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
0\leq e<m
;

•

for any
i\in[q^{m}+1]

\mathcal{U}_{i}=\langle{x}_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{e}^{i},

where
\mathcal{S}_{e}^{i}
is an
e
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{2}
;

•

for any
i\in[t]
,
\mathcal{S}_{e}^{i}\cap\langle{x}_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=\{0\}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a sum-rank metric code associated with the system
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
.
We have that
\mathcal{C}
is a nondegenerate
({m,\dots,m},e)
-constant rank-profile sum-rank metric code with parameters
[\mathbf{n},2,q^{m}m+e]_{q^{m}/q}
.

Proof.

We start by observing that for any
i,j\in[q^{m}+1]
we have

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\langle{x}_{j}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=\begin{cases}m&\text{if }i=j,\\
e&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i})=m+e
for any
i
, by Proposition
5.5
we have that

\mu({x}G)=(m,\ldots,m,e),

for any
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
.
This implies that
\mathcal{C}
is a nondegenerate
({m,\dots,m},e)
-constant rank-profile sum-rank metric code.
The minimum distance is then the sum of the entries of the rank-list
({m,\dots,m},e)
.
∎

The above proposition shows a construction for Example
5.3
when choosing
k=m=2
and
e=1
. In this case
t^{\prime}=1
. In the next corollary, we extend it to the case of

.

Corollary 5.8
.

For any
t^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists a nondegenerate
(\underbrace{m,\dots,m}_{q^{m}t^{\prime}\text{ times}},\underbrace{e,\dots,e}_{t^{\prime}\text{ times}})
-constant rank-profile sum-rank metric code with parameters
[(m+e,\ldots,m+e),2,t^{\prime}q^{m}m+t^{\prime}e]_{q^{m}/q}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be as in Proposition
5.7
.
Consider
\mathcal{C}
as the repetition code of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}

t^{\prime}
times, i.e.

\C=\{(\underbrace{c,\dots,c}_{t^{\prime}\text{ times}})\colon c\in\C^{\prime}\}.

∎

We can characterize the codes with the above parameters in the following way.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a nondegenerate
(\underbrace{m,\dots,m}_{t^{\prime}\text{ times}},\underbrace{e,\dots,e}_{t-t^{\prime}\text{ times}})
-constant rank-profile sum-rank metric code with parameters
[\mathbf{n},2,d]_{q^{m}/q}
, where
0\leq e<m
and
n_{i}<2m
for any
i
.
We have that

•

t=t^{\prime}(q^{m}+1)
;

•

n_{i}=m+e
for any
i\in[t]
;

•

a system associated with
\mathcal{C}
is
(\mathcal{U}_{1,1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{q^{m}+1,t^{\prime}})
where

\mathcal{U}_{i,j}=\langle{x}_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{e}^{j},

where
\mathcal{S}_{e}^{j}
is an
e
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{2}
with the property that
\mathcal{S}_{e}^{j}\cap\langle{x}_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Let
G=(G_{1}\mid\dots\mid G_{t})
be a generator matrix of
\mathcal{C}
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{i}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
, for every
i\in[t]
.
By assumption and by Corollary
3.10
we have that for any

(5)

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\langle{x}_{j}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=\begin{cases}m&\text{for }t^{\prime}\text{ values of }i,\\
e&\text{for the remaining values of }i.\end{cases}

Case 1
: Suppose that
\mathcal{U}_{i}
is such that there exists

for which
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\langle{x}_{j}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=m
.
This implies that
\mathcal{U}_{i}\supseteq\langle{x}_{j}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
.
By assumption, we also have that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\langle{x}_{h}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\in\{e,m\}.

Note that it cannot happen that for some

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\langle{x}_{h}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=m
, because otherwise
\mathcal{U}_{i}\supseteq\langle{x}_{j},{x}_{h}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}

.
Therefore, we have that if
\mathcal{U}_{i}
is such that
\mathcal{U}_{i}\supseteq\langle{x}_{j}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}

then

(6)

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\langle{x}_{h}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=\begin{cases}m&\text{if }h=j,\\

As a consequence, we obtain
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i})\geq m+e

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i})>m+e

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\langle{x}_{h}\rangle)>e
for any

6

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i})=m+e

Case 2
: Suppose that
\mathcal{U}_{i}
does not contain any of the subspaces of
\Lambda_{1}
. By assumption

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}_{j}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=e,

for any
j
.
If

we get that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i})=0

Suppose that

7

and by combining (

Hence, we derive that

We have that
\frac{q^{n_{i}}-1}{q^{e}-1}\neq q^{m}+1

\frac{q^{n_{i}}-1}{q^{e}-1}\in{\mathbb{N}}

Therefore, only Case 1 is possible and so for any

we have (

) and

.
Now, from (

there are exactly

elements among the

’s through it and there is no
\mathcal{U}_{i}

\Lambda_{1}
. This implies that the the total number of the

The assertion is now proved.
∎

In the last part of this section, our aim is to provide some insight in the theory of constant rank-profile sum-rank metric codes of larger dimension. Here, we will use again the geometric approach together with a notion of duality, which will allow us to work again with points instead of hyperplanes.

Let
\sigma\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
be a nondegenerate reflexive sesquilinear form over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and consider

\sigma^{\prime}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}
by
\sigma^{\prime}:(u,v)\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(\sigma(u,v))
.
Note that, once we regard
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
as an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

6.
One-weight MSRD codes

In this section, we will discuss one-weight codes that are also maximum sum-rank distance codes. We will survey on the case of codes of dimension two and we will discuss the dimension three case.

We start by recalling the geometric description of an MSRD code.

Corollary 6.1
.

(see
[

Proposition 6.7

Let
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})

\C

•

q+1

\frac{\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}\left(\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}-1\right)}{{q+1\choose 2}},

|\mathcal{L}_{1}|=t\frac{\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}\left(\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}-1\right)}{{q+1\choose 2}}.

In order to give some bounds on the possible values of

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
m\geq 3
and
q\geq 16
.
Let

A=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}\right)^{2},\quad B=\frac{(q^{m}-1)}{2(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}\left(4q^{m-1}-q^{m+1}-q^{m}+q-3\right),\quad C=-\left(q^{2m}+q^{m}+1\right),

and
\Delta=B^{2}-4AC
, we have that

\frac{q^{m+1}(q^{m}-1)}{2(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}\leq-B\leq\frac{q^{m+2}(q^{m}-1)}{2(q-1)^{2}(q+1)},

and

\frac{7q^{2m+4}(q^{m}-1)^{2}}{4(q-1)^{4}(q+1)^{2}}\leq\Delta\leq\frac{q^{2m+5}(q^{m}-1)^{2}}{8(q-1)^{4}(q+1)^{2}}.

Proof.

Bounds on
B
.

We rewrite
B
as

-B=\frac{(q^{m}-1)}{2(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}\left(q^{m+1}-4q^{m-1}+q^{m}-q+3\right),

since

q^{m+1}+q^{m}-4q^{m-1}-q+3\geq q^{m+1},

we have that

-B\geq\frac{q^{m+1}(q^{m}-1)}{2(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}.

Similarly, since

q^{m+1}+q^{m}-4q^{m-1}-q+3\leq q^{m+2},

we get

-B\leq\frac{q^{m+2}(q^{m}-1)}{2(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}.

Thus, the bounds on
-B
are established.

Bounds on
\Delta
.

We expand
\Delta
using
A,B,\text{ and }C

\Delta=\frac{(q^{m}-1)^{2}}{4(q-1)^{4}(q+1)^{2}}\left[\left(4q^{m-1}-q^{m+1}-q^{m}+q-3\right)^{2}+8(q-1)^{2}(q+1)^{2}\left(q^{2m}+q^{m}+1\right)\right]

To lower bound
\Delta
, note that

8(q-1)^{2}(q+1)^{2}(q^{2m}+q^{m}+1)=8(q^{4}+1-2q^{2})(q^{2m}+q^{m}+1).

Observe that
8q^{2m+4}\geq 7q^{2m+4}
and
8(q^{4}+1-2q^{2})(q^{2m}+q^{m}+1)-q^{2m+4}\geq 0
, it follows that

8(q-1)^{2}(q+1)^{2}(q^{2m}+q^{m}+1)\geq 7q^{2m+4}.

Therefore

\Delta\geq\frac{7q^{2m+4}(q^{m}-1)^{2}}{4(q-1)^{4}(q+1)^{2}}.

To upper bound
\Delta
, we expand

8(q^{4}-2q^{2}+1)(q^{2m}+q^{m}+1)=8q^{2m+4}+8q^{m+4}+8q^{4}-16q^{2m+2}-16q^{m+2}-16q^{2}+8q^{2m}+8q^{m}+8.

Hence

8q^{2m+4}+8q^{m+4}+8q^{4}+8q^{2m}+8q^{m}+8\leq\frac{q^{2m+5}}{2}+16q^{2m+2}+16q^{m+2}+16q^{2},

which follows from the following inequalities

\displaystyle 8q^{2m+4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{2m+5}}{2},

\displaystyle 8q^{m+4}+8q^{2m}

\displaystyle\leq 16q^{2m+2},

\displaystyle 8q^{m}+8q^{4}

\displaystyle\leq 16q^{m+2},

\displaystyle 8

\displaystyle\leq 16q^{2}.

Moreover

\left(4q^{m-1}-q^{m+1}-q^{m}+q-3\right)^{2}\leq\frac{q^{2m+5}}{2},

since
2(4q^{-1}-q-1+q^{-m+1}-3q^{-m})^{2}\leq\frac{q^{5}}{2}
.
Therefore,

\Delta\leq\frac{q^{2m+5}(q^{m}-1)^{2}}{8(q-1)^{4}(q+1)^{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Thanks to the above results, we can obtain the number of blocks necessary to have an MSRD codes with parameters
[(m,\dots,m),3,tm-2]_{q^{m}/q}

Theorem 6.9
.

Suppose there exists an MSRD code with parameters
[(m,\dots,m),3,tm-2]_{q^{m}/q}
then

t=\frac{-B+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2A},

where

A=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}\right)^{2},\quad B=\frac{(q^{m}-1)}{2(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}\left(4q^{m-1}-q^{m+1}-q^{m}+q-3\right),\quad C=-\left(q^{2m}+q^{m}+1\right),

and

\Delta=B^{2}-4AC.

In particular, if
m\geq 3
and
q\geq 16
we have that
q\leq t\leq q^{3/2}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.7
, we know that

t=\frac{-B\pm\sqrt{\Delta}}{2A}.

First, it is easy to see that
-B-\sqrt{\Delta}<0
and
A>0
. Since
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
, this imples that
t=\frac{-B+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2A}
.

Now, applying Lemma
6.8
, we derive upper bound for
q\geq 16
, and
m\geq 3

t\leq\left(\frac{q^{m+2}(q^{m}-1)}{2(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}+\frac{q^{m+\frac{5}{2}}(q^{m}-1)}{2\sqrt{2}(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}\right)\cdot\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{(q^{m}-1)^{2}},

consequently, by straightforward computation, we obtain

t\leq\frac{q^{m+2}}{2(q^{m}-1)(q+1)}\left(1+\frac{q^{1/2}}{\sqrt{2}}\right),

so

\frac{q^{m+2}}{2(q^{m}-1)(q+1)}\left(1+\frac{q^{1/2}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\leq q^{3/2},

which is equivalent to

1+\frac{q^{1/2}}{\sqrt{2}}\leq 2q^{-1/2-m}(q^{m}-1)(q+1),

this holds for all
q\geq 16
and
m\geq 3
.

Similarly, after applying Lemma
6.8
to obtain a lower bound, we have

t\geq\frac{q^{m+1}}{2(q^{m}-1)(q+1)}\left(1+\sqrt{7}q\right),

straightforward computations show that

t\geq\frac{q^{m+1}}{2(q^{m}-1)(q+1)}\left(1+\sqrt{7}q\right)\geq q.

Thus, we have proved the desired result.
∎

We now consider the special case where
q=2
in the following theorem, which is excluded from the previous considerations.

Theorem 6.10
.

For any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there do not exist one-weight MSRD code with parameters
[(m,\dots,m),3,tm-2]_{2^{m}/2}
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists one-weight MSRD code
\C
with parameters
[(m,\dots,m),3,2t-2]_{2^{m}/2}
. By Theorem
6.9
, we know that
t=\frac{-B+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2A}
,
where

A=\frac{(2^{m}-1)^{2}}{2},\quad B=-\frac{(2^{2m}-1)^{2}}{6},\quad C=-(2^{2m}+2^{m}+1).

Accordingly, the discriminant
\Delta
becomes

\Delta=\frac{(2^{m}-1)^{2}}{36}\left(73\cdot 2^{2m}+37\cdot 2^{m+1}+73\right),

Consequently, the value of
t

t=\frac{2^{m}+1+\sqrt{73\cdot 2^{2m}+37\cdot 2^{m+1}+73}}{6(2^{m}-1)}<2,\text{ for all }m\geq 3.

Therefore, the only possibility is that
t=1
(as it needs to be a positive integer).
Hence,
t=1
, which is a contradiction to the nonexistence of a nontrivial one-weight code in the rank metric; see
[
12
]
.
∎

For the case
m=2
, we are going to use the fact that the union of the linear set associated to the system is a
2
-fold blocking set and the following result.

Theorem 6.11

(see
[
4
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Suppose that
B
is a
2
-fold blocking set in
\text{PG}(2,q^{2})
. Then
|B|\geq 2q^{2}+2q+2.

Combining Theorem
6.11
and Proposition
6.7
we obtain the following nonexistence result for
m=2
.

Corollary 6.12
.

For any prime power
q
, there do not exist one-weight MSRD code with parameters
[(2,\dots,2),3,2t-2]_{q^{2}/q}
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists one-weight MSRD code
\C
with parameters
[(2,\dots,2),3,2t-2]_{q^{2}/q}
.
By Theorem
6.9
, we know that
t=\frac{-B+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2A}
,
where

A=\frac{(q+1)^{2}}{2},\quad B=\frac{-q^{2}-2q+3}{2},\quad C=-(q^{4}+q^{2}+1).

The discriminant
\Delta
becomes

\Delta=\frac{1}{4}\left(8q^{6}+16q^{5}+17q^{4}+20q^{3}+14q^{2}+4q+17\right).

Thus, by Proposition
6.7
, we obtain
t

t=\frac{q^{2}+2q-3+\sqrt{8q^{6}+16q^{5}+17q^{4}+20q^{3}+14q^{2}+4q+17}}{2(q+1)^{2}}.

From this we can immediately derive that

t\leq\sqrt{2}q.

Let
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{t})
be an associated system to
\C
. By Proposition
6.6
,
L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}}
is a minimal
2
-fold blocking set.
By Theorem
6.11
, we have that

|L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}}|\geq 2q^{2}+2q+2.

Now, since the
L_{U_{i}}
’s are scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear sets of rank
2
pairwise disjoint, we obtain that

|L_{U_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{U_{t}}|=t(q+1)\leq\sqrt{2}q(q+1),

as
t\leq\sqrt{2}q
.
We get a contradiction as
\sqrt{2}q(q+1)
is strictly less than
2q^{2}+2q+2
.
∎

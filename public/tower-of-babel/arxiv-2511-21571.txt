Relative Turán densities for ordered graphs: all and nothing
Freddie Illingworth, Arjun Ranganathan, Ella Williams

Abstract
Reiher, Rödl, Sales, and Schacht initiated the study of relative Turán densities of ordered graphs and showed that it is more subtle and interesting than the unordered case. For an ordered graph F F , its relative Turán density, ρ < ​ ( F ) \rho_{<}(F) , is the greatest α \alpha such that every ordered graph G G has an F F -free subgraph with at least α ​ e ​ ( G ) \alpha e(G) edges. This paper contains two main results about relative Turán densities. First, we find a family of host graphs that is optimal for all F F . Second, we characterise the ordered graphs with zero relative Turán density: precisely those with no monotone path of length two.

Our resolution to these difficulties is to sample

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
\varepsilon>0
, and

, there exist

and

such that for all

Lemma 3.4
.

For all
\varepsilon>0

\varepsilon

Lemma 3.5
.

For all

, and

, there exist
\eta>0
and

such that for all

(3)

be the event that

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(L-w)\binom{w}{h}}\sum_{a=0}^{L-w}\binom{\lvert S_{1}\cap(a,\kappa-1]\rvert}{i-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\sum_{r=\kappa+1}^{a+w}1_{S_{0}}(r)\binom{\lvert S_{1}\cap(\kappa,r-1]\rvert}{j-i-1}\binom{a+w-r}{h-j}.

is good, whenever

and

\displaystyle\lvert S_{1}\cap(a,\kappa-1]\rvert

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1-\eta}{2}(\kappa-1-a),

\displaystyle\lvert S_{1}\cap(\kappa,r-1]\rvert

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1-\eta}{2}(r-1-\kappa).

Inserting this into (
3.1
) for valid values of

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{(L-w)\binom{w}{h}}\sum_{a=\kappa-w+M}^{\kappa-M}\binom{\lceil\frac{1-\eta}{2}(\kappa-1-a)\rceil}{i-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\sum_{r=\kappa+m}^{a+w}1_{S_{0}}(r)\binom{\lceil\frac{1-\eta}{2}(r-1-\kappa)\rceil}{j-i-1}\binom{a+w-r}{h-j}.

and

3.3

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})

\sum_{t=m}^{a+w-\kappa}1_{S_{0}}(t+\kappa)\binom{t-1}{j-i-1}\binom{a+w-\kappa-t}{h-j}.

However, since

, applying

, so that (

3.2
Reduction to graphs on

We now prove

3.6
which reduces obtaining upper bounds on

in all graphs with sufficiently large ‘average richness’.
This will make use of the regularity lemma machinery from

Lemma 3.6

(reduction to

)
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
and let
F
be an ordered graph. Suppose that, for all
\varepsilon>0
and all sufficiently large
d

(
in terms of
\varepsilon
and
F
)
, every graph
G
on
\{0,1\}^{d}
that satisfies

\frac{1}{d}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\frac{e_{\ell}(G)}{\tau_{\ell,d}}\geqslant\alpha+\varepsilon

contains an ordered copy of
F
. Then
\lim_{d\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\rho_{<}(F,R(m,d))\leqslant\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
and
F
be an ordered graph. Fix a small
\varepsilon>0
and let
d
be sufficiently large as in the hypothesis of the lemma.

Let
m
be sufficiently large in terms of
d
,
\varepsilon
, and
F
. Let
R\coloneqq R(m,d)
as defined in
Proposition

2.4
.
Let

with
e(R^{\prime})\geqslant(\alpha+14\varepsilon)\cdot e(R)
. We may and will assume that
V(R^{\prime})=V(R)
. It suffices to prove that
F\subseteq R^{\prime}
as then

\rho_{<}(F,R)\leqslant\alpha+14\varepsilon,

\lim_{d\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\rho_{<}(F,R(m,d))\leqslant\alpha
, since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary. For each level
\ell\in[d]
, we define
p_{\ell}=e_{\ell}(R^{\prime})/(2^{d-1}m^{2})
. Now
Proposition

2.5
guarantees that, provided
m
is sufficiently large in terms of
d
and
\varepsilon
,

\tfrac{1}{d}\sum_{\ell}p_{\ell}\geqslant\alpha+7\varepsilon.

Let
\eta
be the parameter obtained from the graph building lemma,
Lemma

2.7
, applied to
F
with density
\varepsilon 2^{-d}
. We may and will assume that
\eta\leqslant\varepsilon 2^{-d}
. Note that the blocks
\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\{B_{x}\colon x\in\{0,1\}^{d}\}
form an equipartition of
R^{\prime}
into
2^{d}

Let
L\in\mathbb{N}
obtained from the refined regularity lemma,
Lemma

2.6
, applied to
\eta
and
2^{d}
and let
\mathcal{P}
be the resulting
\eta
-regular equipartition of
R^{\prime}
that refines
\mathcal{B}
. Note that
L
is a function of
\varepsilon
,
d
, and
F
only. Provided
m
is sufficiently large compared to

, the equipartition
\mathcal{P}
has split each block into an equal number of parts. We call this number

(and so

or
\lceil m/m^{\prime}\rceil
) and note that

.

We clean
R^{\prime}
as follows:

•

we delete all edges between pairs of parts whose density is at most
\varepsilon 2^{-d}
,

•

we delete all edges between pairs of parts that are not
\eta
-regular.

The total number of deleted edges is at most

\displaystyle\varepsilon 2^{-d}\lvert V(R^{\prime})\rvert^{2}+\eta\tbinom{2^{d}m^{\prime}}{2}\Big\lceil\tfrac{\lvert V(R^{\prime})\rvert}{2^{d}m^{\prime}}\Big\rceil^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant(\eta+\varepsilon 2^{-d})\lvert V(R^{\prime})\rvert^{2}

Let
R^{\prime\prime}
be the resulting graph. For each level

, we have

\frac{e_{\ell}(R^{\prime\prime})}{2^{d-1}m^{2}}\geqslant\frac{e_{\ell}(R^{\prime})}{2^{d-1}m^{2}}-4\varepsilon=p_{\ell}-4\varepsilon.

Consider the graph
H
whose vertex set is
\mathcal{P}
and where two parts are adjacent if they have an edge between them in
R^{\prime\prime}
(in fact, due to the cleaning process, the pair of parts will be
\eta

\varepsilon 2^{-d}
). We will order

as follows: parts in different blocks are ordered in the same way as their blocks (so if

and

, then

) and parts within blocks are ordered arbitrarily.
Since each block is split into
m^{\prime}
parts by
\mathcal{P}
, we may view

as having vertex set

and talk about its level
\ell

\ell\in[d]

Now, provided
m
is sufficiently large compared to

, property (ii) of

2.4
implies that, between two parts

, the number of level

(1+\varepsilon)2^{-d+\ell}\lvert P\rvert\lvert Q\rvert\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)2^{-d+\ell}\lceil m/m^{\prime}\rceil^{2}\leqslant(1+2\varepsilon)2^{-d+\ell}(m/m^{\prime})^{2}.

Therefore, the number of level

between any two parts is also at most
(1+2\varepsilon)2^{-d+\ell}(m/m^{\prime})^{2}
. This implies that, for every
\ell\in[d]

\displaystyle e_{\ell}(H)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{e_{\ell}(R^{\prime\prime})}{(1+2\varepsilon)2^{-d+\ell}(m/m^{\prime})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+2\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{e_{\ell}(R^{\prime\prime})}{2^{d-1}m^{2}}\cdot\tau_{\ell,d}\cdot(m^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{1+2\varepsilon}\cdot(p_{\ell}-4\varepsilon)\cdot\tau_{\ell,d}\cdot(m^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant(p_{\ell}-6\varepsilon)\cdot\tau_{\ell,d}\cdot(m^{\prime})^{2}.

For each

, we independently pick a random vertex

from
\{x\}\times[m^{\prime}]
. Let

and note that

can be viewed as an ordered graph on
\{0,1\}^{d}
. Furthermore, each edge of
H
is in
G
with probability

and so

\mathbb{E}\biggl[\tfrac{1}{d}\sum_{\ell}\frac{e_{\ell}(G)}{\tau_{\ell,d}}\biggr]=\tfrac{1}{d}\sum_{\ell}\frac{e_{\ell}(H)}{\tau_{\ell,d}\cdot(m^{\prime})^{2}}\geqslant\tfrac{1}{d}\sum_{\ell}(p_{\ell}-6\varepsilon)\geqslant\alpha+\varepsilon.

Thus there is a choice of the

for which

satisfies
\tfrac{1}{d}\sum_{\ell}e_{\ell}(G)/\tau_{\ell,d}\geqslant\alpha+\varepsilon
.
This

, by hypothesis, contains an ordered copy of

. This means that

contains

as an ordered subgraph with vertices in distinct blocks. Recall that the edges of

correspond to pairs of parts in
\mathcal{P}
that are
\eta
-regular and have density at least
\varepsilon 2^{-d}
in
R^{\prime}
. By our choice of

,
Lemma

2.7
guarantees that
R^{\prime}
contains a copy of
F
. Due to the way we ordered

and the fact that the vertices of

are all in distinct blocks, this copy of
F
in

is ordered in the correct way. Thus

contains
F
as an ordered subgraph, as required.

\varepsilon>0
, what is the minimum
n
such that there exists a graph
G
on
n
vertices for which
\rho(F,G)<\rho(F)+\varepsilon
.

In an upcoming paper, we will build on our work here, introducing new machinery for proving upper bounds on the relative Turán density of ordered graphs and finding explicit bounds for more families of graphs.

Appendix A
Auxiliary lemmas

Proof of
Lemma

3.3
.

Let
\eta=\varepsilon/2k
, and note that
m=m(n)=\lfloor(\alpha-\eta)n\rfloor
is much larger than
k
if
n
is large.
Then, as
n
grows, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{m}{k}}{\binom{n}{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\mathcal{O}_{k}(1/m))m^{k}/k!}{(1-\mathcal{O}_{k}(1/n))n^{k}/k!}

\displaystyle=(1-\mathcal{O}_{k}(1/n))\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\geqslant(1-\mathcal{O}_{k}(1/n))\left(\alpha-\eta-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\geqslant\alpha^{k}-k\eta-\mathcal{O}_{k}(1/n),

which is at least
\alpha^{k}-\varepsilon
when
n
is sufficiently large.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

3.4
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed and let
n
be sufficiently large in terms of
\varepsilon
. Generate a uniformly random element
x=x_{1}\dots x_{n}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
by independently sampling
x_{i}\in\text{Ber}(1/2)
for each
i\in[n]
. For each interval
J\subset[n]
, consider the random variable
Z_{J}=\sum_{i\in J}x_{i}
and note that
\mathbb{E}[Z_{J}]=\sum_{i\in J}\mathbb{P}(x_{i}=1)=|J|/2
. Let
\mathcal{J}
denote the set of integer intervals in
n
of size at least
\ln^{2}n
. If
J\in\mathcal{J}
, then by a standard application of the Chernoff bound, we have

\mathbb{P}\Biggl(\bigg\lvert Z_{J}-\frac{\lvert J\rvert}{2}\bigg\rvert\geqslant\varepsilon\lvert J\rvert\Biggr)=\mathbb{P}(\lvert Z_{J}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{J}]\rvert\geqslant 2\varepsilon\mathbb{E}[Z_{J}])\leqslant 2\exp\biggl(-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\ln^{2}n}{3}\biggr).

Note that for each
i\in[n]
there are exactly
n-i+1
integer intervals in
[n]
that start at
i
, and so in particular
\lvert\mathcal{J}\rvert\leqslant n(n+1)/2\leqslant n^{2}
. Applying a union bound, we have
\lvert Z_{J}-\lvert J\rvert/2\rvert<\varepsilon\lvert J\rvert
for all
J\in\mathcal{J}
with probability at least
1-n^{2}\exp(-2\varepsilon^{2}\ln^{2}n/3)\geqslant 1-\varepsilon
for sufficiently large
n
. Therefore there exists at least a
(1-\varepsilon)
-proportion of elements of
\{0,1\}^{n}
for which
\lvert\sum_{i\in J}x_{i}-\lvert J\rvert/2\rvert<\varepsilon\lvert J\rvert
holds for all
J\in\mathcal{J}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

3.5
.

Throughout the proof
\eta
will be sufficiently small as a function of
\alpha
,
\varepsilon
, and
k
, and
n
will be sufficiently large as a function of all other parameters. Let
f
,
x
and
y
be as in the claim,
let
m=\lfloor\eta n\rfloor
, and let
t_{0}=\lceil\sqrt{\eta}n\rceil
. We may assume without loss of generality that
n-2t_{0}=Qm
for some integer
Q
so that
(t_{0},n-t_{0}]=(t_{0},t_{0}+m]\cup\dots\cup(t_{0}+(h-1)m,t_{0}+Qm]
. Letting

g(t)=\binom{t-1}{x}\binom{n-t}{y},

we have that for all
t\in(t_{0},n-t_{0}]

\frac{g(t+1)}{g(t)}=\frac{t}{t-x}\frac{n-t-y}{n-t}\in\left(1-\frac{y}{t_{0}},1+\frac{x}{t_{0}-x}\right).

Thus, we have for all
t_{0}<s<t\leqslant n-t_{0}
with
t-s\leqslant m
that

\frac{g(t)}{g(s)}\geqslant\left(1-\frac{y}{t_{0}}\right)^{m}\geqslant 1-\frac{ym}{t_{0}}\geqslant 1-\sqrt{\eta}y,

which is more than
1-\varepsilon/2
when
\eta
is sufficiently small. Furthermore, for sufficiently large
n
, we have that
x<t_{0}/2
so that for all
t_{0}<s<t\leqslant n-t_{0}
with
t-s\leqslant m

\frac{g(s)}{g(t)}\geqslant\left(1-\frac{2x}{t_{0}}\right)^{m}\geqslant 1-2mx/t_{0}\geqslant 1-2x\sqrt{\eta},

which is more than
1-\varepsilon/2
when
\eta
is sufficiently small. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=t_{0}+1}^{n-t_{0}}f(t)\binom{t-1}{x}\binom{n-t}{y}

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{q=0}^{Q-1}\left(\min_{s\in(t_{0}+qm,t_{0}+(q+1)m]}\binom{s-1}{x}\binom{n-s}{y}\right)\sum_{t=t_{0}+qm+1}^{(q+1)m}f(t)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:binomial-average-premise}}{\geqslant}\sum_{q=0}^{Q-1}(\alpha-\eta)m\left(\min_{s\in(t_{0}+qm,t_{0}+(q+1)m]}\binom{s-1}{x}\binom{n-s}{y}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{q=0}^{Q-1}(1-\varepsilon/2)(\alpha-\eta)\sum_{t=t_{0}+qm+1}^{t_{0}+(q+1)m}\binom{t-1}{x}\binom{n-t}{y},

which is at least

(1-3\varepsilon/4)\alpha\sum_{t=t_{0}+1}^{n-t_{0}}\binom{t-1}{x}\binom{n-t}{y}.

(9)

The sum in (
9
) counts the number of sets
\{a_{1}<\dots<a_{x+y+1}\}\subseteq[n]
of size
x+y+1
such that
a_{x+1}
is in the interval
(t_{0},n-t_{0}]
. Certainly, the number of such sets is at least the number of sets of size
x+y+1
that lie entirely in
(t_{0},n-t_{0}]
, which is
\binom{n-2t_{0}}{x+y+1}
. But when
\eta
is sufficiently small and
n
sufficiently large, this is at least
(1-\varepsilon/4)\binom{n}{x+y+1}
by
Lemma

3.3
, so that (
9
) is at least

(1-\varepsilon)\alpha\binom{n}{x+y+1},

completing the proof.
∎

Thermophysical Properties and Phase Behavior of CO 2 \text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} with Impurities: Insight from Molecular Simulations
D. Raju, M. Ramdin, T.J.H. Vlugt

Abstract
Experimentally determining thermophysical properties for various compositions commonly found in CO 2 \text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} transportation systems is extremely challenging. To overcome this challenge, we performed Monte Carlo (MC) and Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations of CO 2 \text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} rich mixtures to compute thermophysical properties such as densities, thermal expansion coefficients, isothermal compressibilities, heat capacities, Joule-Thomson coefficients, speed of sound, and viscosities at temperatures (235-313) K and pressures (20-200) bar. We computed thermophysical properties of pure CO 2 \text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} and CO 2 \text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} rich mixtures with N 2 \text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} , Ar , H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} , and CH 4 \text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} as impurities (1-10) mol% and showed good agreement with available Equation of State (EoS). We showed that impurities decrease the values of thermal expansion coefficients, isothermal compressibilities, heat capacities, and Joule-Thomson coefficients in the gas phase, while these values increase in the liquid and supercritical phases. In contrast, impurities increase the value of speed of sound in the gas phase and decrease it in the liquid and supercritical phases. We present an extensive data set of thermophysical properties for CO 2 \text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} rich mixtures with various impurities, which will help to design the safe and efficient operation of CO 2 \text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} transportation systems.

The thermodynamic and transport properties of impure
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}

Balaji et al. (2015)

;
Lee et al. (2008)

Shin et al. (2008)
;
Lee et al. (2008)
. These properties serve as a basis for designing and modeling pipeline transportation systems. Especially, knowledge on speed of sound is crucial in characterizing the state of the fluid in pipeline transportation systems
Fazelabdolabadi and Bahramian (2010)
;
Fazelabdolabadi and Bahramian (2010)
. Many literature studies predict the thermodynamic and transport properties of impure
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
using either a simple or an advanced EoS
Quiñones-Cisneros et al. (2001)
;
Vesovic et al. (1990)
, but no general agreement has been made to use a particular EoS with specific interaction parameters for
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
mixtures with small amount of impurities

;
Shin et al. (2018)
.

MC simulation of Gibbs Ensemble (GE) in

and

;
Panagiotopoulos (2000)
;

In a dense liquid simulation box, the insertion of a molecule in a single step is impeded due to the low probability of finding a cavity to accommodate a molecule, and the deletion of a molecule in a single step leaves the simulation box with a high energy penalty to form a new configuration
Rahbari et al. (2021)

Shi and Maginn (2007)
;
Shi and Maginn (2008)
;
Hens et al. (2020)
;

;

In CFCGE, two simulation boxes with indistinguishable whole molecules and fractional molecules (fractional molecules can be in either of the simulation boxes but one per component type) are used for simulating the phase coexistence. The interaction of the distinguishable fractional molecule of a component type

Poursaeidesfahani et al. (2017)

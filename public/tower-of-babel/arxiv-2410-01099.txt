Three-Operator Splitting Method with Two-Step Inertial Extrapolation
Olaniyi S. Iyiola, Lateef O. Jolaoso, Yekini Shehu

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to study the weak convergence analysis of sequence of iterates generated by a three-operator splitting method of Davis and Yin incorporated with two-step inertial extrapolation for solving monotone inclusion problem involving the sum of two maximal monotone operators and a co-coercive operator in Hilbert spaces. Our results improve on the setbacks observed recently in the literature that one-step inertial Douglas-Rachford splitting method may fail to provide acceleration. Our convergence results also dispense with the summability conditions imposed on inertial parameters and the sequence of iterates assumed in recent results on multi-step inertial methods in the literature. Numerical illustrations from image restoration problem and Smoothly Clipped Absolute Deviation (SCAD) penalty problem are given to show the efficiency and advantage gained by incorporating two-step inertial extrapolation over one-step inertial extrapolation for three-operator splitting method. Keywords: Three-operator Splitting Method; Douglas-Rachford Splitting Method; Forward-Backward Splitting Method; Two-Step Inertial; Weak convergence; Hilbert spaces. 2010 MSC classification: 47H05, 47J20, 47J25, 65K15, 90C25.

Throughout, we take

{\rm dom}(A):=\{x\in H|\,Ax\neq\emptyset\}

Let

\langle Cu-Cv,u-v\rangle\geq\eta\|Cu-Cv\|^{2},\ \forall u,v\in H.

In this paper, we consider an inclusion problem involving three operators namely:

Finding the zero of sum of two operators is an interesting problem to diverse researchers, both in the field of Mathematics and other fields of science.

The forward-backward splitting algorithm (FBS)

Advantages of two-step proximal point algorithms.

In

In fact, it was shown using this Example

that one-step inertial Douglas-Rachford splitting method

x_{n+1}=F_{DR}(x_{n}+\theta(x_{n}-x_{n-1}))

x_{n+1}=F_{DR}(x_{n}),

This example therefore shows that one-step inertial Douglas-Rachford splitting method may fail to provide acceleration. Therefore, for certain cases, using the inertia of more than two points could be beneficial. It was remark further in
[

that the use of more than two points

can provide acceleration. The failure of one-step inertial acceleration of ADMM was also discussed in
[

, Section 3]

[

]
also discussed that the multi-step inertial methods can boost the speed of optimization methods even though neither the convergence nor the rate of convergence result of such multi-step inertial methods is established in
[

]

Our contribution.
In this paper, we propose a relaxed Douglas-Rachford splitting method with two-step inertial extrapolation steps to solve monotone inclusion problem (
1
) and obtain weak convergence results. The summability conditions of the inertial parameters and the sequence of iterates imposed in
[
14
, Algorithm 1.2]
,
[
19
, Theorem 4.2 (35)]
, and
[
26
, Chapter 4, (4.2.5)]
are dispensed with in our results even for a more general monotone inclusion problem (
1
). Some related methods in the literature are recovered as special cases of our proposed method. Finally, we give numerical implementations of our method and compare with other related methods using test problems from image restoration problem and Smoothly Clipped Absolute Deviation (SCAD) penalty problem
[
23
]
.

3.1
Motivations from Dynamical Systems

Consider the following second order dynamical system

\ddot{x}(t)+\alpha\dot{x}(t)+\beta(x(t)-T(x(t)))=0,\penalty\ \penalty\ x(0)=x_{0},\dot{x}(0)=v_{0},

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{1}}{h^{2}}(x_{n+1}-2x_{n}+x_{n-1})+\frac{\omega_{2}}{h^{2}}(x_{n}-2x_{n-1}+x_{n-2})

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{h}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+\beta(y_{n}-Ty_{n})=0,

(11)

This implies that

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=2x_{n}-x_{n-1}-\frac{\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}(x_{n}-2x_{n-1}+x_{n-2})

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha h}{\omega_{1}}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})-\frac{\beta h^{2}}{\omega_{1}}(y_{n}-Ty_{n})

\displaystyle x_{n}+(x_{n}-x_{n-1})-\frac{\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+\frac{\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}(x_{n-1}-x_{n-2})

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha h}{\omega_{1}}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})-\frac{\beta h^{2}}{\omega_{1}}(y_{n}-Ty_{n})

\displaystyle x_{n}+\Big(1-\frac{\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}-\frac{\alpha h}{\omega_{1}}\Big)(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+\frac{\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}(x_{n-1}-x_{n-2})

Set

Then we have from (

\displaystyle x_{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{n}+\theta(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+\delta(x_{n-1}-x_{n-2})

\displaystyle-\rho y_{n}+\rho_{n}Ty_{n}.

Choosing

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&y_{n}=x_{n}+\theta(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+\delta(x_{n-1}-x_{n-2}),\\

\displaystyle\rho\gamma\Big(2\eta-\frac{\gamma}{\epsilon}\Big)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|Cw_{k}-CJ_{\gamma B}(z^{*})\|^{2}\leq\|x_{1}-z^{*}\|^{2}

(47)

\displaystyle-\theta\|x_{0}-z^{*}\|^{2}-\delta\|x_{-1}-z^{*}\|^{2}-\|x_{n+1}-z^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\theta\|x_{n}-z^{*}\|^{2}+\delta\|x_{n-1}-z^{*}\|^{2}+(1+\theta)(\theta-\delta)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\delta(\theta-\delta)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k-1}-x_{k-2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x_{1}-z^{*}\|^{2}-\theta\|x_{0}-z^{*}\|^{2}-\delta\|x_{-1}-z^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\Big(\|x_{n+1}-z^{*}\|^{2}-\theta\|x_{n}-z^{*}\|^{2}-\delta\|x_{n-1}-z^{*}\|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+(1+\theta)(\theta-\delta)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\delta(\theta-\delta)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k-1}-x_{k-2}\|^{2}.

Using (
37
) and (
41
) in (
47
), we obtain

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|Cw_{n}-CJ_{\gamma B}(z^{*})\|^{2}<\infty.

This completes (ii).

(iii)
Since
J_{\gamma B}
is nonexpansive, we have

\displaystyle\|w_{n}-J_{\gamma B}(z^{*})\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|J_{\gamma B}(y_{n})-J_{\gamma B}(z^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|y_{n}-z^{*}\|=\|(1+\theta)(x_{n}-z^{*})-(\theta-\delta)(x_{n-1}-z^{*})-\delta(x_{n-2}-z^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\theta)\|x_{n}-z^{*}\|+|\theta-\delta|\|x_{n-1}-z^{*}\|+|\delta|\|x_{n-2}-z^{*}\|.

By the boundedness of
\{x_{n}\}
in Lemma
3.5
, we then have that
\{w_{n}\}
is bounded . Now, suppose
z
is the sequential weak cluster point of
\{w_{n}\}
. Then there exists
\{w_{n_{k}}\}\subset\{w_{n}\}
such that
w_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup z,k\rightarrow\infty
. Pick
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}(A+B+C)
and since
C
is maximal monotone,
Cw_{n}\rightarrow Cx^{*}
and
w_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup z
, it follows from the weak-to-strong sequential closedness of
C
that
Cz=Cx^{*}
by
[
3
, Proposition 20.33 (ii)]
and so
Cw_{n_{k}}\rightarrow Cz
.

Denote
u_{n}:=\frac{1}{\gamma}(y_{n}-w_{n})\in Bw_{n}
, and
v_{n}:=\frac{1}{\gamma}(2w_{n}-y_{n}-\gamma Cw_{n}-z_{n})\in Az_{n}
.

Then we get

w_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup z
,
z_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup z
,
Cw_{n_{k}}\rightarrow Cz
,
y_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup z^{*}
,
u_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup\frac{1}{\gamma}(z^{*}-z)
and
v_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup\frac{1}{\gamma}(z-z^{*}-\gamma Cz)
. Applying
[
3
, Proposition 25.5]
to

(z_{n_{k}},v_{n_{k}})\in{\rm gra}A
,
(y_{n_{k}},u_{n_{k}})\in B
and
(w_{n_{k}},Cw_{n_{k}})\in C
shows that
z\in{\rm zer}(A+B+C)
,
z^{*}-z\in\gamma Bz
and
z-z^{*}-\gamma Cz\in\gamma Az
. Hence, we have shown that
z=J_{\gamma B}(z^{*})
and so
z
is unique weak cluster point of
\{w_{n}\}
. Therefore,
\{w_{n}\}
weakly converges to
J_{\gamma B}(z^{*})
by
[
3
, Lemma 2.38]
. Furthermore,
\{z_{n}\}
weakly converges to
J_{\gamma B}(z^{*})
since
\{w_{n}\}
weakly converges to
J_{\gamma B}(z^{*})
and
w_{n}-z_{n}\rightarrow 0,n\rightarrow\infty
.

(iv) Let
x^{*}=J_{\gamma B}(z^{*}),u^{*}=\frac{1}{\gamma}(z^{*}-x^{*})\in Bx^{*}
and

.

(a) Since
B+C
is monotone and
(y_{n},u_{n})\in{\rm gra}B
, we get

\langle w_{n}-x^{*},u_{n}+Cw_{n}-(u^{*}+Cx^{*})\rangle\geq 0,\forall n\geq 1.

Consider the set
Z:=\{x^{*}\}\cup\{z_{n}\}
. Then there exists
\Phi_{A}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
that vanishes only at 0 such that

\displaystyle\gamma\Phi_{A}(\|z_{n}-x^{*}\|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\langle z_{n}-x^{*},v_{n}-v^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle+\gamma\langle w_{n}-x^{*},u_{n}+Cw_{n}-(u^{*}+Cx^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\langle z_{n}-w_{n},v_{n}-v^{*}\rangle+\gamma\langle z_{n}-x^{*},v_{n}-v^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle+\gamma\langle w_{n}-x^{*},u_{n}+Cw_{n}-(u^{*}+Cx^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\langle z_{n}-w_{n},v_{n}-v^{*}\rangle+\gamma\langle w_{n}-x^{*},v_{n}+u_{n}+Cw_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle w_{n}-z_{n},w_{n}-\gamma v_{n}-(x^{*}-\gamma v^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle w_{n}-z_{n},y_{n}-z^{*}\rangle+\gamma\langle w_{n}-z_{n},Cw_{n}-Cx^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle-\|w_{n}-z_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle w_{n}-z_{n},y_{n}-z^{*}\rangle+\gamma\langle w_{n}-z_{n},Cw_{n}-Cx^{*}\rangle\rightarrow 0,n\rightarrow\infty,

since

and

. Also,

.

(b) Since

is monotone, we get

. Observe also that

is uniformly monotone on all bounded sets. Let us consider the bounded set

. Then there exists an increasing function

that vanishes only at 0 such that

\displaystyle\gamma\Phi_{B}(\|w_{n}-x^{*}\|)

\displaystyle\gamma\langle z_{n}-x^{*},v_{n}-v^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle+\gamma\langle w_{n}-x^{*},u_{n}+Cw_{n}-(u^{*}+Cx^{*})\rangle\rightarrow 0,n\rightarrow\infty,

(c) Observe that

Smoothly Clipped Absolute Deviation (SCAD) Penalty Problem

Let us consider SCAD penalty problem

(51)

where the penalty

is defined as

q_{\xi}(\omega):=\begin{cases}\xi\omega,\hskip 34.14322pt&\omega\leq\xi,\\
\frac{-\omega^{2}+2c\xi\omega-\xi^{2}}{2(c-1)},\hskip 11.38092pt&\xi\leq\omega\leq c\xi,\\
\frac{(c+1)\xi^{2}}{2},\hskip 11.38092pt&\omega\geq c\xi,\end{cases}

and the knots of the quadratic spline function is represented by
\xi>0

Given that

\displaystyle q_{\xi}(\cdot)+\frac{\nu}{2}|\cdot|^{2}

[
25

[
44
]

49

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|Du-b\|^{2}_{2}-\frac{1}{2(c-1)}\|u\|^{2}_{2}.

\\

&x_{n+1}=(1-\rho)w_{n}+\rho z_{n}.\end{array}\right.

5
Final Remarks

This paper proposes a method which is a combination of two-step inertial extrapolation steps, relaxation parameter and splitting algorithm of Davis and Yin to find a zero of the sum of three monotone operators for which two are maximal monotone and the third co-coercive. Weak convergence results are obtained without assuming summability conditions imposed on inertial parameters and the sequence of iterates assumed in recent results on multi-step inertial methods in the literature, and some previously known related methods in the literature are recovered. Numerical illustrations with test problems drawn from Image Restoration Problem (IRP) and Smoothly Clipped Absolute Deviation (SCAD) Penalty Problem in statistical learning are given to illustrate the presented convergence theory. These examples show that our proposed algorithm is easy to implement and effective in handling important application like IRP and SCAD problems. One of the contributions of this paper is to improve on the setbacks observed recently in the literature that one-step inertial Douglas-Rachford splitting method may fail to provide acceleration.

As part of our future project, we intend to further consider our proposed method with correction terms to solve the same problem considered in this paper.

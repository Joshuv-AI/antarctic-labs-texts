Quantum complexity and generalized area law in fully connected models
Donghoon Kim 1, Tomotaka Kuwahara 1,2,3

Abstract
The area law for entanglement entropy fundamentally reflects the complexity of quantum many-body systems, demonstrating ground states of local Hamiltonians to be represented with low computational complexity. While this principle is well-established in one-dimensional systems, little is known beyond 1D cases, and attempts to generalize the area law on infinite-dimensional graphs have largely been disproven. In this work, for non-critical ground states of Hamiltonians on fully connected graphs, we establish a generalized area law up to a polylogarithmic factor in system size, by effectively reducing the boundary area to a constant scale for interactions between subsystems. This result implies an efficient approximation of the ground state by the matrix product state up to an approximation error of 1 / poly ​ ( n ) 1/\text{poly}(n) . As the core technique, we develop the mean-field renormalization group approach, which rigorously guarantees efficiency by systematically grouping regions of the system and iteratively approximating each as a product state. This approach provides a rigorous pathway to efficiently simulate ground states of complex systems, advancing our understanding of infinite-dimensional quantum many-body systems and their entanglement structures.



Understanding the ground states of local Hamiltonians and developing efficient representations for them are central challenges in quantum many-body physics and quantum information theory. Quantum entanglement lies at the core of this framework, serving as a key indicator of the intrinsic complexity of quantum states
Vidal et al. 2003
;
Calabrese and Cardy 2004
;
Amico et al. 2008
;
Horodecki et al. 2009
;
Laflorencie 2016
. In many-body systems, low entanglement often means that the effective Hilbert space needed to describe a quantum state is far smaller than the full, exponentially large space. This insight has underpinned the success of tensor network methods, offering a practical and efficient approach to representing quantum states and thereby advancing our understanding
Schollwöck 2005
;
Schollwöck 2011
;
Verstraete et al. 2023
.

A central principle of this analysis is the entanglement area law, which posits that for the ground state
\ket{\Omega}
of a gapped local Hamiltonian, the entanglement entropy between two subsystems
A
and
B
scales with the boundary separating them, rather than with the size of the entire system
Eisert et al. 2010
. The entanglement entropy is defined as the von Neumann entropy of the reduced density matrix
\rho_{A}={\rm tr}_{B}\left(\ket{\Omega}\bra{\Omega}\right)
, given by
S(\ket{\Omega})=-{\rm tr}(\rho_{A}\log\rho_{A})
. Significant progress has been made in understanding this law, particularly for one-dimensional (1D) systems. A major breakthrough was Hastings’ proof of the area law for 1D gapped systems, utilizing the Lieb-Robinson bound
Hastings 2007
. This result was further refined by Arad and collaborators
Arad et al. 2012
;
Arad et al. 2013
and extended by Brandão and Horodecki, who proved the area law under the assumption of exponentially decaying correlations
Brandão and Horodecki 2013
;
Brandão and Horodecki 2015
.
Within this progression, the Approximate Ground State Projection (AGSP) formalism emerged as a leading method, enabling the development of a polynomial-time algorithm for finding ground states
Landau et al. 2015
;
Arad et al. 2017
.
Of the various dimensions, 1D systems stand out as the most well-understood example, highlighting the robustness of this theoretical and computational framework.

Extending the area law beyond 1D is crucial for understanding the complexity of a broader class of quantum states, yet it remains a significant challenge. Despite progress—such as extensions to tree graphs in dimensions below two
Abrahamsen 2019
, systems with long-range interactions
Kuwahara and Saito 2020
, and advances in thermal equilibrium states
Wolf et al. 2008
;
Kuwahara et al. 2021
—the field is still largely underexplored. A recent advancement demonstrates the area law for two-dimensional frustration-free systems, assuming a local spectral gap
Anshu et al. 2022
, but these findings remain limited compared to the substantial achievements in 1D systems.

A particularly intriguing extension involves fully connected systems, or all-to-all interacting systems, where each component interacts with every other component.
In these geometrically non-local systems, spatial dimensions lose relevance, representing an extreme case in higher-dimensional physics.
Such systems are crucial in condensed matter physics, statistical mechanics, high-energy physics, quantum chemistry, etc.
Key examples include the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (SK) model for spin glasses
Sherrington and Kirkpatrick 1975
, the Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick (LMG) model for nuclear structure
Lipkin et al. 1965
;
Latorre et al. 2005
, lattice gauge theories reformulated as spin systems for quantum simulation
Kogut and Susskind 1975
, and the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model, which is central to black hole physics
Sachdev and Ye 1993
;
Kitaev 2015
.
Recent advances in quantum simulators, such as trapped ion systems
Blatt and Wineland 2008
;
Monroe and Kim 2013
;
Blatt and Roos 2012
;
Lechner et al. 2015
;
Puri et al. 2017
;
Nigg et al. 2017
and superconducting spin qubits
Pita-Vidal et al. 2025
, have also enabled the realization of all-to-all interactions, showcasing their potential for solving complex problems through quantum algorithms. However, the highly non-local nature of fully connected systems complicates the analysis and prediction of entanglement structures in their ground states. Consequently, determining when the area law applies and establishing entanglement bounds in these systems remain essential yet demanding tasks.

In this work, we prove a generalized area law and clarify the complexity of ground states for Hamiltonians on fully connected graphs. Given that counterexamples to the area law have been identified in infinite-dimensional graph systems
Aharonov et al. 2014
and that the boundary size in fully connected graphs generally scales with the volume, it may at first seem counterintuitive for the area law to hold.
Nevertheless, recent findings suggest that classical simulations of fully connected models are feasible under specific conditions
Tindall et al. 2022
. This insight implies that with appropriate conditions, it is possible to reduce computational complexity below the levels traditionally expected for such models. In this work, we establish that the generalized area law can be validated when the interactions between subsystems
A
and
B
either retain a Schmidt rank of
\mathcal{O}(1)
or exhibit linearly decaying Schmidt coefficients.
These conditions effectively constrain the interaction dimension across the boundary to
\mathcal{O}(1)
, resembling interactions across a constant-size boundary, with uniform interaction as a representative example.
With these assumptions in place, we show that the entanglement entropy is bounded above by a polylogarithmic function of the system size
n
, and that the ground state can be efficiently approximated using the Matrix Product State (MPS) representation.

To prove our results, we developed an approximation technique called the mean-field renormalization group (MFRG), which includes a rigorous efficiency guarantee. This method groups regions of the system and coarse-grains them, similar to real-space renormalization group techniques but tailored for the connectivity in fully connected models. In this process, we approximate the states within each group to be close to product states through projection. While this projection closely preserves the properties of the original ground state, it significantly reduces the dimension of the Hilbert space. Traditional methods, such as the AGSP formalism, are insufficient to address the complexities of infinite-dimensional systems and fully connected models. The MFRG technique overcomes these limitations, forming the foundation for our proof of the generalized area law.

Results

We consider a quantum system composed of
n
qudits, each with a local dimension
d
satisfying
d<n
. Let
\Lambda
denote the set of all sites, with
|\Lambda|=n
. The system is described by a
k
-local Hamiltonian expressed as a sum of local terms, each acting on at most
k
sites:
H=\sum_{Z:|Z|\leq k}h_{Z}
. For our analysis, we introduce the parameter
\bar{g}_{1}
to specify that the Hamiltonian is
\bar{g}_{1}
-extensive. More generally, an operator
O=\sum_{Z}o_{Z}
is termed
g
-extensive if it satisfies
\max_{i\in\Lambda}\sum_{Z:Z\ni i}\left\|o_{Z}\right\|\leq g
.
This means that the norm of interactions involving a single site is finitely upper-bounded.

We impose the following conditions on the Hamiltonian. The Hamiltonian
H
has a nondegenerate ground state
\ket{\Omega}
and a spectral gap
\Delta
of order
\Omega(1)
.
To characterize the fully connected model, we begin with the
2
-local Hamiltonian:
H=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}\in\Lambda}h_{i,i^{\prime}}+\sum_{i\in\Lambda}h_{i}
.
Then, for the interaction between two arbitrary subsets
A
and
B
, denoted by
H_{A,B}:=\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{i^{\prime}\in B}h_{i,i^{\prime}}
, we assume the following decomposition as

\displaystyle H_{A,B}=\sum_{s=1}^{d_{H}}\frac{J_{s}}{n}H_{A,s}\otimes H_{B,s},

(1)

where
d_{H}=\mathcal{O}(1)
, and
H_{A,s}
and
H_{B,s}
are 1-local and 1-extensive operators. Under these assumptions, there are no specific geometric constraints, allowing interaction strengths between arbitrary sites to be of the same order. This setup permits each site to interact with others comparably, provided the operator norm remains bounded and the total Schmidt rank
d_{H}
is finite, thus accommodating scenarios with all-to-all interactions. The simplest example satisfying this assumption is a uniform Hamiltonian, which remains invariant under the exchange of any two sites. To see the point, let us consider the LMG model as follows:

H=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}(J_{x}\sigma_{i}^{x}\sigma_{i^{\prime}}^{x}+J_{y}\sigma_{i}^{y}\sigma_{i^{\prime}}^{y})/n.

For a subset-subset interaction
H_{A,B}
, we obtain

\displaystyle H_{A,B}=\frac{J_{x}}{n}\sum_{i\in A}\sigma_{i}^{x}\sum_{i^{\prime}\in B}\sigma_{i^{\prime}}^{x}+\frac{J_{y}}{n}\sum_{i\in A}\sigma_{i}^{y}\sum_{i^{\prime}\in B}\sigma_{i^{\prime}}^{y},

which is given by the form of Eq. (
1
) with
d_{H}=2
,
J_{1}=J_{x}
and
J_{2}=J_{y}
.

We can generalize the condition to arbitrary
k
-local Hamiltonian. For simplicity, we show the case of
k=3
.
For the three-body interactions
\sum_{i,i^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime}\in\Lambda}h_{i,i^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime}}
, we consider a three-body subset interaction between non-overlapping subsets
A,B,C
, denoted by
H_{A,B,C}:=\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{i^{\prime}\in B}\sum_{i^{\prime\prime}\in C}h_{i,i^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime}}
.
We assume the analogous decomposition to Eq. (
1
)

\displaystyle H_{A,B,C}=\sum_{s=1}^{d_{H}}\frac{J_{s}}{n^{2}}H_{A,s}\otimes H_{B,s}\otimes H_{C,s},

(2)

where
\{H_{A,s},H_{B,s},H_{C,s}\}_{s=1}^{d_{H}}
are 1-local and 1-extensive operators.
In the same way, we can generalize the condition to arbitrary
k
(Assumption 2 in Supplemental information).

It is worth noting that the requirement of a finite Schmidt rank
d_{H}=\mathcal{O}(1)
for the Hamiltonian can be relaxed to include cases where
J_{s}
satisfies
\sum_{s}|J_{s}|\leq g_{1}
with
g_{1}=\mathcal{O}(1)
.
Additionally, the factors of
1/n
and
1/n^{2}
in Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
) are essential for normalizing the interaction strength. This normalization preserves physical consistency across systems of varying sizes, ensuring that energy density and other physical quantities remain well-defined in the thermodynamic limit.

Our primary focus is on the entanglement entropy of the ground state
\ket{\Omega}
of the Hamiltonian under these conditions, leading us to the following main result.

Schematic picture of the MFRG flow. In each block (blue-shaded box), we perform dimension reduction by truncating the product bases where more than

Main Result.

If the Hamiltonian
H
satisfies the stated assumptions, the entanglement entropy of its ground state
\ket{\Omega}
between any bipartition
A
and
B
is bounded above by a polylogarithmic function of the system size
n
:

\displaystyle S\left(\ket{\Omega}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left[\log(n)\right]^{\mathfrak{a}_{1}+\mathfrak{a}_{2}\log(f(\bar{g}_{1},\Delta))}+\log(d)+1,

(3)

where
\mathfrak{a}_{1}=230\log(10386ek^{2})
,
\mathfrak{a}_{2}=230
, and
f(\bar{g}_{1},\Delta)=\max\left\{2,\log\left(\frac{324\bar{g}_{1}}{\Delta}\right)\right\}
.

The exponent of the
\log(n)
term in the upper bound is, therefore, at most proportional to
\log(\log(1/\Delta))
.
This main result establishes that the entanglement of the ground state is proportional to the Schmidt rank of the boundary interaction up to a logarithmic factor, thereby proving the generalized area law. This finding demonstrates that, under the specified conditions, the upper bound on entanglement entropy in a fully connected graph can be significantly reduced beyond previous expectations.

Furthermore, we consider an approximation of the ground state by the MPS.
The MPS serves as a fundamental ansatz for various types of variational methods, such as the Density Matrix Renormalization Group (DMRG)
Schollwöck 2011
. A key question in this context is whether ground states can be accurately approximated by an MPS with a relatively small bond dimension.

We here establish the following result regarding the MPS representation of the ground state
\ket{\Omega}
.
By arbitrarily arranging the qubits on a one-dimensional array, there exists an MPS
\ket{\Omega_{D}}
with bond dimension
D=\exp[(\log n)^{c^{\prime}\log\log(1/\delta)}]
(where
c^{\prime}
is a constant of
\mathcal{O}(1)
) such that

\displaystyle\|{\rm tr}_{X^{\rm c}}(\ket{\Omega_{D}}\bra{\Omega_{D}})-{\rm tr}_{X^{\rm c}}(\ket{\Omega}\bra{\Omega})\|_{1}\leq\delta|X|,

(4)

for any concatenated subregion
X
. Here,
\|\cdot\|_{1}
denotes the trace norm, and
|X|
is the cardinality of
X
. The proof of this statement is detailed in the Method section.
Based on the approximation bound (
4
), achieving an approximation error of
\delta=1/{\rm poly}(n)
requires an almost quasi-polynomial bond dimension, specifically
D=\exp[\log(n)^{c^{\prime\prime}\log\log(n)}]
with a constant
c^{\prime\prime}
. This result provides theoretical support for the MPS ansatz with small bond dimensions, achievable at a moderate computational cost.

Mean-field renormalization-group (MFRG)

As a foundational component of our findings, we present the mean-field renormalization group (MFRG) approach as a major methodological advancement—an efficient approximation method that rigorously guarantees accuracy.
Similar to the real-space renormalization group, this method groups multiple sites of the system into blocks, treating each block as a new individual site within the larger assembly.
Each block undergoes a coarse-graining process in which the ground state is closely approximated by a product state.
This approximation enables the truncation of unnecessary Hilbert space dimensions while preserving the essential properties of the ground state.
By iteratively applying this process, the dimension of the truncated Hilbert space is reduced to a manageable size. A more detailed explanation of this process is provided below.

•

Step 1: Identifying mean-field bases

We begin by computing a product state approximation of the ground state. For each site
i
, we perform the Schmidt decomposition of the ground state
\ket{\Omega}
between
i
and
\Lambda\setminus\{i\}
, selecting the quantum state with the largest Schmidt coefficient.

•

Step 2: Truncating the block Hilbert space

Next, we divide the system into blocks
\{L_{j}\}_{j}
, with each block having size
n^{1/8}
. In each block
L_{j}
, we select product-state bases where only
z
sites within
L_{j}
deviate from the mean-field states obtained in Step 1. Here,
z
acts as a control parameter: increasing
z
improves precision but also raises computational cost. The maximum number of such states is
d^{z}|L_{j}|^{z}=d^{z}n^{z/8}
.

•

Step 3: Constructing renormalized Hamiltonian

Using the product bases defined above, we construct an effective Hamiltonian where the original blocks are treated as new sites with qudit dimension
d^{z}n^{z/8}
. The total number of sites is now reduced to
n^{7/8}
.

•

Step 4: Repeating the process

We repeat Steps 1 through 3 until the system size is reduced to a sufficiently small size.

We now provide several remarks. First, after
s_{0}
renormalization steps, the system size is reduced to
n^{(7/8)^{s_{0}}}
, and the renormalized qudits have dimension
[dn^{1/8}]^{z^{s_{0}}}
. Therefore, the renormalization process ends after
s_{0}\propto\log\log(n)
processes, yielding a final qudit dimension of
[dn^{1/8}]^{z^{\mathcal{O}(\log\log(n))}}=e^{{\rm poly}\log(n)}
as long as
z=\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Second, the most computationally intensive task is identifying the mean-field bases in Step 1, as it requires knowledge of the ground state. It is important to note that the mean-field product state we construct may differ from the product state that minimizes the energy, i.e.,
\arg\inf_{\ket{\rm P}}\bra{\rm P}H\ket{\rm P}
, where
\inf_{\ket{\rm P}}
is taken over all product states.
If we could employ the latter one as the mean-fields bases, the energy-minimizing product state may be efficiently computable in specific cases (e.g., uniform Hamiltonians).
The flexibility in the choice of mean-field bases remains an important open question.

Finally, in constructing the mean-field bases, we can always employ the MPS representation because, for any cut in the system, the Schmidt rank is guaranteed to be smaller than the Hilbert space dimension of the final block.

The main analytical challenges in proving our results stem from establishing a rigorous efficiency guarantee for the MFRG processes.
The analyses involve estimating the errors associated with dimension reduction within each block, evaluating the interaction amplitudes of the renormalized Hamiltonian, optimizing the choice of the number of deviated sites
z
, etc.
Since the Hamiltonian lacks geometric structure, our approach deviates significantly from previous area-law proofs
Hastings 2007
;
Arad et al. 2012
;
Arad et al. 2013
;
Brandão and Horodecki 2013
;
Abrahamsen 2019
;
Kuwahara and Saito 2020
.
The outline of our method is presented in the Methods section.

Discussion

We compare our findings with previous studies, highlighting both consistencies and discrepancies. In earlier work
Brandao and Harrow 2013
;
Kuwahara et al. 2017
, the approximation of ground energy using product states was investigated. More recently, the partition function of the 2-local Hamiltonian on a dense graph was studied
Bravyi et al. 2022
, where it was shown that the ground energy can be computed classically in polynomial time, with any constant error. Our work advances these results by concentrating on the ground state itself and approximating it closely as a product state, revealing its detailed structure to arbitrary
1/{\rm poly}(n)
precision.

In our proof using the MFRG method, the linear decay of the Schmidt coefficients, as assumed in (
1
), or the
1/n^{2}
decay in (
2
), plays a critical role. As previously noted, certain important models in physics, such as the SK and SYK models, fall outside this framework: In the SK model, 2-local random interactions cause the Schmidt coefficients to decay as
1/\sqrt{n}
, while in the SYK model, with 4-local random interactions, they decay as
1/n^{3/2}
.
Even with perturbations that introduce a spectral gap, this interaction-induced scaling of the Schmidt coefficients remains incompatible with our assumptions. Extending the generalized area law to encompass such systems remains a significant open problem.

We performed numerical simulations on representative all-to-all interacting systems, specifically the LMG model and fully connected bilinear fermion systems (see Supplemental Information
Sup
). Our results indicate that, in the LMG model with a spectral gap, the growth of entanglement entropy with system size
n
is slower than
\log(n)
. The tendency of entanglement entropy to saturate to a constant value as the gap increases suggests the potential for a strict area law without a logarithmic correction. Additionally, our simulations for bilinear fermions demonstrate that the presence of a spectral gap causes entanglement entropy to saturate at
\mathcal{O}(1)
, independent of system size. Notably, this behavior persists even with random coupling, where Schmidt coefficients decay as
1/\sqrt{n}
, extending beyond our initial assumptions.
Therefore, we might still have room to improve our area law with the logarithmic correction to a strict area law, i.e., constant upper bound for the entanglement entropy.

Our findings primarily demonstrate the existence of a ground state with low complexity in fully connected systems. However, determining this ground state to within a
1/{\rm poly}(n)
error is generally infeasible in (quasi-)polynomial time, even for classical systems. Nonetheless, we anticipate that if we restrict our attention to uniform Hamiltonians, the time complexity for finding the ground states may also be quasi-polynomial.

methods

We provide a detailed exposition of each step involved in the mean-field renormalization group (MFRG) process and the derivation of the polylogarithmic bound on entanglement entropy. For illustrative purposes, we consider a
2
-local Hamiltonian, denoted by

H=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}\in\Lambda}h_{i,i^{\prime}}+\sum_{i\in\Lambda}h_{i}
,
defined on a lattice
\Lambda
with
|\Lambda|=n
. Here,
h_{i,i^{\prime}}
specifically acts between qudits
i
and
i^{\prime}
, while
h_{i}
denotes an on-site interaction. Additionally, each qudit is associated with a Hilbert space of dimension
d
. The analysis is focused on a fully connected system wherein each on-site interaction possesses a norm that does not exceed a constant
g_{0}=\mathcal{O}(1)
. Moreover, each element of the Hamiltonian is interactive, with interactions between subsystems
A
and
B
formulated as

H_{A,B}=\sum_{s=1}^{d_{H}}\frac{J_{s}}{n}H_{A,s}\otimes H_{B,s}
,
as specified in Eq. (
1
). Here, the operators
H_{A,s}
and
H_{B,s}
are characterized as
1
-local and
1
-extensive, respectively. Furthermore, the interaction strength, which is quantitatively constrained as the sum of the absolute values of the coupling constants
J_{s}
, is bounded by a constant
g_{1}=\mathcal{O}(1)
, specifically,

\sum_{s=1}^{d_{H}}\left|J_{s}\right|\leq g_{1}
.
This framework particularly accommodates scenarios with low Schmidt-rank, where
d_{H}=\mathcal{O}(1)
.
The ground state of this Hamiltonian is represented by
\ket{\Omega}
, and the entanglement measured between the bipartitioned regions
A
and
B
within this state is expressed as
S_{AB}(\ket{\Omega})
.

Step 1 of MFRG: Identifying mean-field bases

The initial step in the MFRG methodology involves demonstrating that the ground state
\ket{\Omega}
of the Hamiltonian can be approximated as a product state under the specified conditions. To facilitate this analysis, we examine the distribution of Schmidt coefficients
\{\lambda_{s}\}_{s=0}^{d-1}
of the ground state, when partitioned with respect to a single site
i
and the rest of the system
\Lambda_{i}:=\Lambda\setminus\{i\}
. The Schmidt decomposition is expressed as:

\displaystyle\ket{\Omega}=\sum_{s=0}^{d-1}\lambda_{s}\ket{s}_{i}\ket{\phi_{s}}_{\Lambda_{i}},

(5)

where the coefficients are ordered such that
\lambda_{0}\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d-1}
.
A key method for analyzing this coefficient distribution is to monitor the energy fluctuations caused by local unitary operations at site
i
. This analysis leads to the subsequent statement:

Claim 1:
(Proposition 1 in Supplemental Information) If a local unitary operator
u_{i}
acting on site
i
satisfies the condition
u_{i}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{\dagger}=\rho_{i}
, where
\rho_{i}={\rm tr}_{\Lambda_{i}}(\ket{\Omega}\bra{\Omega})
, then the variation in energy induced by
u_{i}
can be upper bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\bra{\Omega}(u_{i}^{\dagger}Hu_{i}-H)\ket{\Omega}\right|\leq\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}},

(6)

where
\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}
is a small constant proportional to
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n\Delta}}
.

This inequality originates from the fundamental trade-off relationship between the variance of any operator and the spectral gap. Considering a 1-extensive local operator
A_{L}
defined in the region
L\subset\Lambda
, its variance
\mathrm{Var}_{\Omega}(A_{L}):=\bra{\Omega}A_{L}^{2}\ket{\Omega}-\bra{\Omega}A_{L}\ket{\Omega}^{2}
satisfies the following relationship with the spectral gap
Kuwahara et al. 2017
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\Omega}(A_{L})\cdot\Delta\lesssim(g_{0}+g_{1})|L|.

(7)

Given that
u_{i}
leaves the local state invariant, the energy change
\bra{\Omega}(u_{i}^{\dagger}Hu_{i}-H)\ket{\Omega}
is solely influenced by the interaction
H_{i,\Lambda_{i}}
between site
i
and
\Lambda_{i}
. In line with previous assumptions, the interaction is represented through a decomposition involving 1-local and 1-extensive operators, formulated as
\sum_{s}\frac{J_{s}}{n}H_{i,s}\otimes H_{\Lambda_{i},s}
. Consequently, the energy change is constrained by the variance of
H_{i,s}
and
H_{\Lambda_{i},s}
, as shown in the following estimate:

\bra{\Omega}(u_{i}^{\dagger}Hu_{i}-H)\ket{\Omega}\lesssim\sum_{s}\frac{\left|J_{s}\right|}{n}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{\Omega}(H_{i,s})\mathrm{Var}_{\Omega}(H_{\Lambda_{i},s})}.

Since
H_{i,s}
is a 1-extensive operator, its variance is bounded by 1, and due to the trade-off relation with the spectral gap, the variance of
H_{\Lambda_{i},s}
is bound by the order of
(g_{0}+g_{1})n/\Delta
. Consequently, as mentioned in Eq. (
6
), the energy change is ultimately bounded by a value
\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}
that is proportional to
1/\sqrt{n\Delta}
.

The energy change is quantified by the difference in energies between
u_{i}\ket{\Omega}
and
\ket{\Omega}
. Specifically, the fidelity of the ground state with respect to the transformation enacted by
u_{i}
is measured by
|\bra{\Omega}u_{i}\ket{\Omega}|^{2}
, while the probability amplitude for the excited states is given by
1-|\bra{\Omega}u_{i}\ket{\Omega}|^{2}
.
Thus, the minimum energy change due to this transformation is bound by
\Delta(1-|\bra{\Omega}u_{i}\ket{\Omega}|^{2})
.
This relationship, in conjunction with Claim 1, leads to the establishment of:

\Delta(1-|\bra{\Omega}u_{i}\ket{\Omega}|^{2})\leq\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}.

By selecting a local unitary operator
u_{i}
that leaves the state
\ket{0}_{i}
unchanged and applies a
-1
phase shift to all other basis states
\ket{s}_{i}
for
s\geq 1
, we derive an inequality that directly links the fidelity of the ground state to the robustness parameter
\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}
. The overlap between the transformed and original ground states,
|\bra{\Omega}u_{i}\ket{\Omega}|
, is expressed as:

|\bra{\Omega}u_{i}\ket{\Omega}|=|\lambda_{0}|^{2}-\sum_{s\geq 1}|\lambda_{s}|^{2}\geq\sqrt{1-\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}}{\Delta}}.

Utilizing the normalization condition
\sum_{s=0}^{d-1}|\lambda_{s}|^{2}=1
, the subsequent claim is formulated:

Claim 2.
(Proposition 2 in Supplemental Information)
For the ground state
\ket{\Omega}
, decomposed relative to a site
i
and its complement
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda\setminus\{i\}
, the Schmidt coefficients
\{\lambda_{s}\}_{s=0}^{d-1}
, ordered by decreasing magnitude, are constrained as follows:

\displaystyle|\lambda_{0}|^{2}\geq 1-\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}}{2\Delta}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{s\geq 1}|\lambda_{s}|^{2}\leq\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}}{2\Delta}.

(8)

Following Claim 2, at site
i
, the dominant Schmidt coefficient asymptotically approaches unity, suggesting a nearly pure state configuration. Meanwhile, the aggregate magnitude of the remaining coefficients is quantitatively constrained to
\mathcal{O}(\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}/\Delta)
. This significant concentration of the Schmidt coefficients implies that the reduced density matrix for a single site can closely approximate a product state, as articulated:

\displaystyle\rho_{i}={\rm tr}_{\Lambda_{i}}\ket{\Omega}\bra{\Omega}=\ket{0}\bra{0}_{i}+\mathcal{O}(\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}/\Delta).

(9)

The state
\ket{0}
, possessing the highest Schmidt coefficient at site
i
, is defined as the mean-field state. Consequently, the ground state, within an error bounded by
\mathcal{O}(\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}/\Delta)
, becomes locally indistinguishable from this mean-field state. Furthermore, the reduced density matrix for any subset
L\subset\Lambda
is effectively represented by

\displaystyle\rho_{L}:={\rm tr}_{L^{c}}\ket{\Omega}\bra{\Omega}=\bigotimes_{i\in L}\ket{0}\bra{0}_{i}+\mathcal{O}(|L|\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}/\Delta),

(10)

suggesting that each site in
L
independently approaches a mean-field configuration, modulated by deviations on the order of
\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}/\Delta
.

From these results, it can be inferred that for any partition, the entanglement entropy is constrained as follows:

S_{AB}(\ket{\Omega})\leq\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{n}).

To establish a more stringent upper bound, a further renormalization process is necessitated.

Step 2 of MFRG: Truncating the block Hilbert space

As demonstrated, the ground state of the system closely approximates a mean-field or product state within localized regions. This observation underpins the strategy of truncating the Hilbert space to concentrate predominantly near the mean-field space, suggesting that such an approximation should not significantly alter the fundamental characteristics of the ground state.

To rigorously quantify deviations from the mean-field state, we introduce the projection operator
P_{i}=\ket{0}\bra{0}_{i}
, which projects onto the mean-field state at site
i
, along with its orthogonal complement
Q_{i}=1-P_{i}=1-\ket{0}\bra{0}_{i}
. We define a 1-local operator
M_{L}
for a subset of sites
L
, encapsulating the deviation, as follows:

M_{L}:=\sum_{i\in L}Q_{i}.

This operator
M_{L}
serves as a quantitative measure of the deviation from the mean-field state across the specified subset
L
, enabling detailed analysis of the fidelity of the approximation.

Projection operators
\Pi_{\leq m}^{L}
and
\Pi_{\geq m}^{L}
are designed to project onto the eigenspaces of
M_{L}
corresponding to eigenvalues less than or equal to, and greater than
m
, respectively. The state constrained by
\Pi_{\leq m}^{L}
is represented as:

\left(\bigotimes_{j\in L\setminus\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{\ell}\}}\ket{0}_{j}\right)\otimes\ket{s_{1}}_{i_{1}}\otimes\ket{s_{2}}_{i_{2}}\cdots\otimes\ket{s_{\ell}}_{i_{\ell}},

where
\ell\leq m
. According to Claim 2, the expected value of
M_{L}
in the ground state,
\bra{\Omega}M_{L}\ket{\Omega}
, is bounded above by
|L|\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}/(2\Delta)
, as detailed in Lemma 7 of Supplemental Information. Furthermore, the probability distribution of
M_{L}
relative to the ground state exhibits exponential concentration, characterized by
|L|/\Delta
, as described in Lemma 4 of Supplemental Information:

\|\Pi_{\geq\bra{\Omega}M_{L}\ket{\Omega}+q}^{L}\|^{2}\leq 3e^{-\Omega(q/\sqrt{|L|/\Delta})}.

This relationship implies that applying a projection across the entire lattice
\Lambda
to ensure
\|\Pi_{\geq m}^{\Lambda}\ket{\Omega}\|^{2}\leq\epsilon
, and confining the Hilbert space within an
\epsilon
error, necessitates setting
m
to
\Omega(\sqrt{n}\log(1/\epsilon))
. This value of
m
can be chosen to be smaller than the total size
n
, providing an efficient constraint on the system’s state space.

Based on the preceding analysis, we refine our estimate for the probability distribution
\|\Pi_{\geq m}^{L}\ket{\Omega}\|^{2}
for the local region
L\subset\Lambda
. To elaborate, we prove the following assertion:

Claim 3.
(Propositions 3 and 4 of the Supplemental Information) Suppose
\epsilon_{0}
is selected such that
\epsilon_{0}\leq\min\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{\Delta}{2}\right)
. Then, the probability distribution
\|\Pi_{\geq m}^{L}\ket{\Omega}\|
exhibits exponential decay as a function of the parameter
m
:

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\geq m}^{L}\ket{\Omega}\|\leq\left(\frac{\bar{J}}{\Delta}\right)^{\Omega(m)}+2\epsilon_{0},

(11)

where

\bar{J}=\mathcal{O}\left(\max\left\{|L|\sqrt{\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}}{\Delta}}\log(n),|L|\sqrt{\frac{m_{0}}{n}},\frac{|L|^{2}}{n}\right\}\right),

and
m_{0}=\Theta(\sqrt{n}\log(1/\epsilon_{0}))
.

From this analysis, by selecting
\bar{J}
to be
n^{-\Omega(1)}
, which is less than
\Delta
, and setting
\epsilon_{0}=1/\text{poly}(n)
, it is established that specifying
m=\mathcal{O}(1)
suffices to achieve an error margin for
\|\Pi_{\geq m}^{L}\ket{\Omega}\|
within the threshold of
1/\text{poly}(n)
.
This is corroborated by selecting a region
L
of size
n^{1/8}
, which leads to
\bar{J}=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/8}\log(n))
.
Consequently, it can be concluded that the projection operator
\Pi_{\leq m}^{L}
effectively preserves the ground state without significant alterations when an appropriately sized region
L
is selected, even with
m
set to
\mathcal{O}(1)
.
This demonstrates the precision of maintaining a high-fidelity description of the ground state within a polynomial error threshold.

We partition the total system
\Lambda
into blocks
\{L_{j}\}_{j}
, each of size
n^{1/8}
. This partitioning scheme divides the system into
n^{7/8}
blocks, which are henceforth referred to as
n^{(1)}=n^{7/8}
.
Within each block, the projection operator
\Pi_{\leq z}^{L_{j}}
is applied, culminating in the composite system projection defined as
\Pi^{(0)}:=\prod_{j=1}^{n^{7/8}}\Pi_{\leq z}^{L_{j}}
.
In accordance with previous discussions and setting
z=\mathcal{O}(1)
, we establish that the deviation from the ground state is bounded by
1-\|\Pi^{(0)}\ket{\Omega}\|^{2}\leq 1/\text{poly}(n)
. Consequently, the dimension of each block, initially at a subexponential level of
d^{n^{1/8}}
, is reduced to
d^{(1)}:=\mathcal{O}((dn^{1/8})^{z})
, effectively transforming it into a polynomially bounded space.

By projecting the entire Hilbert space using
\Pi^{(0)}
and similarly projecting the Hamiltonian, we derive an effective Hamiltonian defined as
H^{(1)}:=\Pi^{(0)}H\Pi^{(0)}
. This projection modifies the ground state information minimally, ensuring that both the ground state and spectral gap of the effective Hamiltonian closely resemble those of the original. To quantify these observations, we employ the following formal assertion:

Claim 4.
(Lemma 8 in Supplemental Information) For any projection operator
\Pi
satisfying
1-\|\Pi\ket{\Omega}\|^{2}\leq\epsilon<1/2
, the resultant effective Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}:=\Pi H\Pi
preserves the ground state
\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}
and maintains a spectral gap
\tilde{\Delta}
such that

\displaystyle\|\ket{\Omega}-\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}\|\leq\frac{\bar{\epsilon}}{1-\bar{\epsilon}},\quad\tilde{\Delta}\geq(1-\epsilon)\Delta,

(12)

where
\bar{\epsilon}:=9\|H\|\sqrt{\epsilon}/\Delta
.

Step 3 of MFRG: Constructing renormalized Hamiltonian

Now, each block is considered as a single qudit with a dimension of
d^{(1)}=\mathcal{O}(d^{z}n^{z/8})
, referred to as a renormalized qudit. We reconstruct the lattice accordingly. The interactions between these renormalized qudits are governed by the previously obtained effective Hamiltonian
H^{(1)}
. By coarse-graining the interactions within and between blocks, we redefine the on-site interactions and the interactions between renormalized qudits. The following claim illustrates how the assumptions previously satisfied by the original Hamiltonian continue to hold under the modified parameters of the effective Hamiltonian.

Claim 5.
(Proposition 5 in Supplemental Information)
Define the block set
\{L_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n^{(1)}}
as renormalized sites
\Lambda^{(1)}=\{1,2,\dots,n^{(1)}\}
, where each site corresponds to a block.
The on-site interaction
V_{j}^{(1)}
acting on a single renormalized qudit
j
is bounded by the new parameter
g_{0}^{(1)}:=(g_{0}+g_{1})z+\bar{J}
:

\displaystyle\left\|V_{j}^{(1)}\right\|\leq g_{0}^{(1)}.

(13)

For regions
A
and
B
within a renormalized qudit, the Hamiltonian
H_{A,B}^{(1)}
acting between these areas is expanded using 1-local 1-extensive operators
H_{A,s}^{(1)}
and
H_{B,s}^{(1)}
:

\displaystyle H_{A,B}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{n^{(1)}}\sum_{s=1}^{d_{H}^{(1)}}J_{s}^{(1)}H_{A,s}^{(1)}\otimes H_{B,s}^{(1)}.

(14)

Here, the sum of the coupling constants
\sum_{s}J_{s}^{(1)}
is bounded by the new parameter
g_{1}^{(1)}:=16zg_{1}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{s}|J_{s}|\leq g_{1}^{(1)}.

(15)

In summary, the renormalization process strategically modifies fundamental parameters to preserve the intrinsic properties of the original Hamiltonian. These adjustments are formally represented as follows:

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\to n^{7/8},

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{O}(d^{z}n^{z/8}),

\displaystyle g_{0}

\displaystyle\to(g_{0}+g_{1})z+\bar{J},

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle\to 16zg_{1}.

These transformations ensure that the renormalized Hamiltonian maintains consistency with the physical properties and scales relevant to the system under study.

Step 4 of MFRG: Repeating the process

We will iteratively apply the renormalization process previously discussed to the newly restructured renormalized qudits. Specifically, by applying the same methodology inductively, we gather
(n^{(s)})^{1/8}
renormalized qudits to form a block for each
s
-th iteration and apply the projection
\Pi^{(s)}
to create the
(s+1)
-th renormalized qudit. The effective Hamiltonian for each stage is defined as
H^{(s+1)}=\Pi^{(s)}H^{(s)}\Pi^{(s)}
, where the ground state and spectral gap are denoted as
\ket{\Omega^{(s)}}
and
\Delta^{(s)}
, respectively. The initial case,
s=0
, corresponds to the original parameters and properties of the Hamiltonian and the Hilbert space.

Under this scheme, the total number of qudits at the
s
-th stage, denoted by
n^{(s)}
, follows
n^{(7/8)^{s}}
, which decreases very rapidly. This rapid reduction constrains the total feasible number of iterations to
s\lesssim\log\log(n)
. In contrast, the Hilbert space dimension of a renormalized qudit,
d^{(s)}
, increases according to the relation
d^{(s+1)}=\mathcal{O}((d^{(s)}(n^{(s)})^{1/8})^{z})
. Given that for
s\geq 1
,
d^{(s)}\geq(n^{(s)})^{1/8}
, it follows that
d^{(s+1)}\leq\mathcal{O}((d^{(s)})^{2z})
. Therefore, the dimension
d^{(s)}
can be bounded as
\mathcal{O}((dn^{1/8})^{(2z)^{s}})
.

Moreover, it is crucial to consider that the interaction parameters
g_{0}
and
g_{1}
increase during the renormalization process. To adequately preserve the ground state, we limit the number of iterations to
s_{0}
, such that
n^{(s_{0})}=\text{polylog}(n)
. This limitation allows us to assert that the arguments previously utilized can be successfully applied at each renormalization step without complication. Consequently, the final Hilbert space dimension achieved is given by:

\displaystyle\left(d^{(s_{0})}\right)^{n^{(s_{0})}}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\left(dn^{1/8}\right)^{(2z)^{s_{0}}n^{(s_{0})}}\right)=\exp\left(\text{polylog}(n)\right),

(16)

where we recall that
s_{0}\lesssim\log\log(n)
and
n^{(s_{0})}=\text{polylog}(n)
, with
z
set as
\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Polylogarithmic bound of entanglement entropy

Using this MFRG process, we establish our principal result: the entanglement entropy for any bipartition is conclusively bounded by
\text{polylog}(n)
.
Building upon our prior discussion, by fixing
z
as an
\mathcal{O}(1)
constant, we ensure that
\|\ket{\Omega^{(s)}}-\ket{\Omega^{(s+1)}}\|
is less than
1/\text{poly}(n^{(s)})
for each iteration. Furthermore, by judiciously choosing
z_{\mu}=a\mu+b
(where
a
and
b
are
\mathcal{O}(1)
constants) for
\mu\in\mathbb{N}
, it is feasible to maintain that
\|\ket{\Omega^{(s)}}-\ket{\Omega^{(s+1)}}\|
does not exceed
(n^{(s)})^{-\mu}
.
The choice of
z_{\mu}
leads to
\|\ket{\Omega}-\ket{\Omega^{(s_{0})}}\|\leq\sum_{s=0}^{s_{0}-1}(n^{(s)})^{-\mu}
,
which culminates in

\displaystyle\|\ket{\Omega}-\ket{\Omega^{(s_{0})}}\|\leq\frac{1}{(\log n)^{\kappa\mu}}=:\delta_{\mu},

(17)

where
\kappa
is an
\mathcal{O}(1)
constant.

For such choices of
z_{\mu}
, the dimension
D_{\mu}
of the reduced Hilbert space achieved through the renormalization process is given by Eq. (
16
) as

\displaystyle D_{\mu}:=\exp\left(\left(\log n\right)^{\mathcal{O}(\log z_{\mu})}\right).

(18)

Utilizing the following claim allows us to establish a bound on the entanglement entropy of the ground state, based on the error in ground state approximation
\delta_{\mu}
and the reduced Hilbert space dimension
D_{\mu}
.

Claim 6.
(Eckart-Young theorem
Eckart and Young 1936
)
Consider a normalized quantum state
\ket{\psi}
with a Schmidt decomposition across the bipartition
A
and
B
of a system, expressed as
\ket{\psi}=\sum_{m}\lambda_{m}\ket{\psi_{A,m}}\otimes\ket{\psi_{B,m}}
. For any arbitrary quantum state
\ket{\psi^{\prime}}
, the inequality below holds:

\sum_{m>\mathrm{SR}(\ket{\psi^{\prime}})}\left|\lambda_{m}\right|^{2}\leq\left\|\ket{\psi}-\ket{\psi^{\prime}}\right\|^{2},

where
\mathrm{SR}(\ket{\psi^{\prime}})
denotes the Schmidt rank of
\ket{\psi^{\prime}}
with respect to the bipartition
A
and
B
.

From this assertion, we derive the entanglement entropy bound as follows:

\displaystyle S(\ket{\Omega})\leq\log\left(D_{\mu=1}\right)-\sum_{\mu=2}^{\Xi}\delta_{\mu}^{2}\log\left(\frac{\delta_{\mu}^{2}}{D_{\mu+1}}\right),

(19)

with
\Xi
defined as
\lceil\frac{\log n}{2\kappa\log\log(n)}\rceil
and
D_{\Xi+1}=d^{n}
.
By employing Eq. (
17
) and Eq. (
18
), we rigorously calculate the bound established in ineq. (
19
). This calculation substantiates that the entanglement entropy adheres to an upper limit of
\mathcal{O}(\text{polylog}(n))
, thereby completing the proof of our main result.

Setup and Assumptions

We consider a quantum system comprising
n
qudits and denote the set of total sites by
\Lambda
, where
|\Lambda|=n
.
Let

represent the dimension of the Hilbert space associated with a single site, where we consider
d<n
.
For an arbitrary subset
X\subseteq\Lambda
, we define
|X|
as the number of sites contained in
X
.
We also denote the complementary subset of
X
and
X\setminus\{i\}
by
X^{c}
and
X_{i}
, respectively.

For an arbitrary operator
O
, we define it to be
k
-local and

-extensive if it can be expressed as

\displaystyle O=\sum_{Z:|Z|\leq k}o_{Z},\qquad\max_{i\in\Lambda}\sum_{Z:Z\ni i}\left\|o_{Z}\right\|\leq g.

(S.1)

The norm of a
k
-local and
g
-extensive operator
O
is bounded above by
gn
as demonstrated below:

\displaystyle\left\|O\right\|\leq\sum_{Z:|Z|\leq k}\left\|o_{Z}\right\|\leq\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\sum_{Z:Z\ni i}\left\|o_{Z}\right\|\leq g|\Lambda|=gn.

(S.2)

We now consider a system governed by a general
k
-local Hamiltonian of the form

\displaystyle H=\sum_{Z:|Z|\leq k}h_{Z}.

(S.3)

The subset Hamiltonian
H_{L}
on the subset
L
is defined as

\displaystyle H_{L}=\sum_{Z:Z\subseteq L}h_{Z}.

(S.4)

We further define
\widehat{H}_{L}
as

\displaystyle\widehat{H}_{L}=\sum_{Z:Z\cap L\neq\emptyset}h_{Z}=H-H_{L^{\rm c}},

(S.5)

which includes all interaction terms that involve at least one site within the subset
L
, even if the interactions extend to sites outside of
L
.
The on-site interaction

is defined as the term acting solely on a single site:

\displaystyle V_{i}:=\sum_{Z:Z=\{i\}}h_{Z}.

(S.6)

For any two arbitrary subsets
A
and
B
, we define the interaction
H_{A,B}
as

\displaystyle H_{A,B}:=\sum_{Z:Z\cap A\neq\emptyset,\ Z\cap B\neq\emptyset}h_{Z}.

(S.7)

Similarly, for an arbitrary set of
l
subsets
\{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{l}\}
, the
l
-body interaction term
H_{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{l}}
is defined as

\displaystyle H_{A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{l}}:=\sum_{Z:Z\cap A_{1}\neq\emptyset,\ Z\cap A_{2}\neq\emptyset,\ \ldots,\ Z\cap A_{l}\neq\emptyset}h_{Z}.

(S.8)

For a density matrix
\rho
, the variance of an arbitrary operator
O
with respect to
\rho
is given by:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\rho}\left(O\right)={\rm tr}\left(\rho O^{2}\right)-{\rm tr}\left(\rho O\right)^{2}.

(S.9)

Additionally, the correlation function between two arbitrary operators
O_{1}
and
O_{2}
with respect to
\rho
is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cor}_{\rho}(O_{1},O_{2})={\rm tr}\left(\rho O_{1}O_{2}\right)-{\rm tr}\left(\rho O_{1}\right){\rm tr}\left(\rho O_{2}\right).

(S.10)

The entanglement entropy of the state
\ket{\psi}
between the bipartitions
A
and
B
of
\Lambda
is defined as:

\displaystyle S_{A,B}(\ket{\psi}):=-{\rm tr}\left(\rho_{A}\log\rho_{A}\right)=-{\rm tr}\left(\rho_{B}\log\rho_{B}\right),

(S.11)

where
\rho_{A}:={\rm tr}_{B}\left(\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}\right)
and
\rho_{B}:={\rm tr}_{A}\left(\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}\right)
represent the reduced density matrices on
A
and
B
, respectively.

We adopt the assumption of all-to-all interaction in the following sense:

Assumption 1

(

-local cases)

For the 2-local all-to-all interacting Hamiltonian
H

The Hamiltonian

\ket{\Omega}

\Delta

The on-site interaction

\mathcal{O}(1)
constant

(S.12)

3.

For any two subsets
A
and

H_{A,B}
in Eq. (
S.7

\displaystyle H_{A,B}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s}J_{s}H_{A,s}\otimes H_{B,s},

(S.13)

where
H_{A,s}
and
H_{B,s}
are 1-local and 1-extensive operators, respectively. Here, the coefficients

Because of the

In the practical analyses, we utilize Eq. (

Here, we define

S.II.1
Trade-off between variance and spectral gap

We here consider an arbitrary operator
A_{L}
in the form of

\displaystyle A_{L}=\sum_{Z:|Z|\leq k_{A},Z\subset L}a_{Z},\qquad\sum_{Z:Z\ni i}\left\|a_{Z}\right\|\leq 1.

(S.30)

Note that
A_{L}
is supported on a subset
L\subseteq\Lambda

We then define
\mathrm{Var}_{\Omega}(A_{L})
as the variance of
A_{L}
in the ground state
\ket{\Omega}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\Omega}(A_{L})=\bra{\Omega}A_{L}^{2}\ket{\Omega}-\bra{\Omega}A_{L}\ket{\Omega}^{2}.

(S.31)

We here prove the following lemma which has been obtained in Ref.
(
Kuwahara et al. 2017
, Ineq. (8))
:

Lemma 3
.

Let
A_{L}
be a
k_{A}
-local and
1
-extensive operator as in Eq. (
S.30
).
For an arbitrary ground state
\ket{\Omega}
of the
k
-local Hamiltonian
H
with the property (
S.25
),
the variance
\mathrm{Var}_{\Omega}(A_{L})
and the spectral gap
\Delta
satisfies the following trade-off inequality:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\Omega}(A_{L})\cdot\Delta\leq\gamma^{2}\bar{g}_{1}|L|,

(S.32)

where we define

\displaystyle\gamma:=\sqrt{18k_{A}^{2}k(k+k_{A})}.

(S.33)

In particular,
\gamma=6k^{2}
when
k_{A}=k
.

Proof of Lemma
3
.
We begin with the fact that
\ket{\Omega}
is the ground state of
H
, satisfying
H\ket{\Omega}=E_{0}\ket{\Omega}
. We utilize the following inequality:

\displaystyle\langle\Omega|A_{L}(H-E_{0})A_{L}|\Omega\rangle=\langle\Omega|A_{L}(1-\Pi_{\Omega})(H-E_{0})(1-\Pi_{\Omega})A_{L}|\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\Omega|A_{L}(1-\Pi_{\Omega})A_{L}|\Omega\rangle\cdot\Delta

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}_{\Omega}(A_{L})\cdot\Delta

(S.34)

with
\Pi_{\Omega}:=|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|
, where we use
H\Pi_{\Omega}=E_{0}\Pi_{\Omega}
and
(H-E_{0})(1-\Pi_{\Omega})\geq\Delta(1-\Pi_{\Omega})
.
Then,

\displaystyle\langle\Omega|A_{L}(H-E_{0})A_{L}|\Omega\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\langle\Omega|[A_{L},[A_{L},H-E_{0}]]|\Omega\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\langle\Omega|[A_{L},[A_{L},H]]|\Omega\rangle.

(S.35)

The inequality (
S.34
) reduces to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\Omega}(A_{L})\cdot\Delta\leq-\frac{1}{2}\langle\Omega|[A_{L},[A_{L},H]]|\Omega\rangle\leq\frac{\|[A_{L},[A_{L},H]]\|}{2}.

(S.36)

Finally, we need to estimate the norm
\|[A_{L},[A_{L},H]]\|
. For the purpose, we utilize the statement given in Ref.
(
Kuwahara 2016a
, Theorem 1)
, which gives the following upper bound

\displaystyle\left\|\left[A_{L},\Gamma^{(q)}\right]\right\|\leq 6k_{A}q\left\|\Gamma^{(q)}\right\|,

(S.37)

where
\Gamma^{(q)}
is an arbitrary
q
-local operator.

In order to use the bound (
S.37
), we first note that

\displaystyle[A_{L},H]=\left[A_{L},\widehat{H}_{L}\right],\qquad\widehat{H}_{L}:=\sum_{Z:Z\cap L\neq\emptyset}h_{Z}.

(S.38)

Then,
[A_{L},H]
is at most
(k+k_{A})
-local, and hence by applying Eq. (
S.37
) with
\Gamma^{(q)}=[A_{L},H]
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|[A_{L},[A_{L},H]]\right\|\leq 6k_{A}(k+k_{A})\left\|\left[A_{L},\widehat{H}_{L}\right]\right\|.

(S.39)

In the same way, we obtain the upper bound for
\left\|\left[A_{L},\widehat{H}_{L}\right]\right\|
as follows

\displaystyle\left\|\left[A_{L},\widehat{H}_{L}\right]\right\|\leq 6k_{A}k\left\|\widehat{H}_{L}\right\|.

(S.40)

By combining the inequalities (
S.39
) and (
S.40
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\left[A_{L},[A_{L},H]\right]\right\|\leq 36k_{A}^{2}k(k+k_{A})\left\|\widehat{H}_{L}\right\|\leq 36k_{A}^{2}k(k+k_{A})\bar{g}_{1}|L|,

(S.41)

where the second inequality results from the condition (
S.26
). Finally, by combining the inequalities (
S.36
) and (
S.41
), we prove the main inequality (
S.32
). This completes the proof of Lemma
3
.
\square

[ End of Proof of Lemma
3
]

Without loss of generality, in the following argument we assume that the ground energy is 0, i.e.,
E_{0}=0
.

S.II.2
Probability distribution of
1
-local operator

We here restrict ourselves to the class of
1
-local operators:

\displaystyle A_{L}=\sum_{i\in L}a_{i},\qquad\left\|a_{i}\right\|\leq 1.

(S.42)

We note that the generalization to generic
k
-local operators is possible (see Refs.
Kuwahara et al. 2017
;
Kuwahara 2016b
), but for our present purpose, it is enough to consider the
1
-local case.
We here consider the probability distribution

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi^{A}_{\geq x}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}\quad{\rm or}\quad\left\|\Pi^{A}_{\leq x}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2},

(S.43)

where
\Pi^{A}_{\geq x}
(
\Pi^{A}_{\leq x}
) is the projection operator onto the eigenspace of
A_{L}
which is in
[x,\infty)
(
(-\infty,x]
).

For this purpose, we here decompose the ground state
\ket{\Omega}
by using the eigenstates of
A_{L}
:

\displaystyle\ket{\Omega}=\sum_{x=0}^{|L|}c_{x}\ket{\omega_{x}},\qquad A_{L}\ket{\omega_{x}}=x\ket{\omega_{x}},

(S.44)

where
\ket{\omega_{x}}
represents the normalized state derived from projecting the ground state onto the eigenspace of
A_{L}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
x
, i.e.,
\ket{\omega_{x}}\propto\Pi^{A}_{=x}\ket{\Omega}
.
Then, following Ref.
Kuwahara 2016b
, we construct the Hamiltonian
\breve{H}
by using the bases
\{\ket{\omega_{x}}\}_{x}
as follows:

\displaystyle\breve{H}=\sum_{x,x^{\prime}=0}^{|L|}\bra{\omega_{x^{\prime}}}H\ket{\omega_{x}}\ket{\omega_{x^{\prime}}}\bra{\omega_{x}}=\sum_{x,x^{\prime}}J_{x,x^{\prime}}\ket{\omega_{x^{\prime}}}\bra{\omega_{x}},

(S.45)

which is similar to the one-particle tight-binding model.
Also, since the
h_{Z}
term with
|Z|\leq k
influence less than or equal to
k
spins, we have

\displaystyle J_{x,x^{\prime}}=0\quad\textrm{for}\quad|x-x^{\prime}|>k.

(S.46)

We denote the spectral gap of
\breve{H}
by
\breve{\Delta}
, which is always larger than or equal to
\Delta
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\breve{\Delta}\geq\Delta.

(S.47)

S.II.2.1
General hopping amplitude

We here prove that the ground state of the tight-binding model is exponentially localized.
We can get the following lemma, which is a direct consequence from the combination of Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3 in Ref.
Kuwahara 2016b
:

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mu
and
C_{v}
be a constant such that

\displaystyle|\bra{\omega_{x^{\prime}}}H\ket{\omega_{x}}|\leq C_{v}e^{-\mu|x-x^{\prime}|}\quad\textrm{for}\quad x\neq x^{\prime}.

(S.48)

We also define the projection
\breve{\Pi}_{\leq x}
which is defined as

\displaystyle\breve{\Pi}_{\leq x}:=\sum_{x^{\prime}\leq x}\ket{\omega_{x^{\prime}}}\bra{\omega_{x^{\prime}}}.

(S.49)

Then, the probability to measure larger than
\langle x\rangle\pm\bar{x}

(\langle x\rangle
: the average value
)
in the ground state is upper-bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{\geq\langle x\rangle+\bar{x}}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{\leq\langle x\rangle-\bar{x}}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}\leq e^{-(\bar{x}-r_{1})/\xi},

(S.50)

where
r_{1}
and
\xi
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle r_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{4(4e+2)C_{v}}{\mu^{3}\breve{\Delta}}},\qquad\xi=\max\left(\sqrt{\frac{2(4e^{2}+1)}{e}\cdot\frac{4(\mu^{2}+2\mu+2)}{\mu^{3}}\cdot\frac{C_{v}}{\breve{\Delta}}},\frac{3\log(2e)}{\mu}\right).

(S.51)

The upper bound is qualitatively better than the upper bound which is based on the Chebyshev polynomial in Ref.
Kuwahara et al. 2017
, which gives an upper bound in the form of
e^{-\bar{x}/\sqrt{\left\|\breve{H}\right\|/\breve{\Delta}}}
.
When the on-site potential (i.e.,
\bra{\omega_{x}}H\ket{\omega_{x}}
) is quite large, this upper bound becomes much worse than (
S.50
).
In our purpose, we will encounter the situation where the hopping is weak but the on-site potential is strong under the mapping to tight-binding model (see Sec.
S.VI.2
below).

This lemma immediately yields the probability distribution for the operator
A_{L}
in Eq. (
S.42
).
In this case, by defining

\displaystyle\bar{J}:=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,x^{\prime}\\
x\neq x^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\left(|\bra{\omega_{x^{\prime}}}H\ket{\omega_{x}}|\right),

(S.52)

we can let

\displaystyle|\bra{\omega_{x^{\prime}}}H\ket{\omega_{x}}|\leq\bar{J}e^{-|x-x^{\prime}|/k},\quad\textrm{for}\quad x\neq x^{\prime},

(S.53)

where we use the condition (
S.46
).
This makes
C_{v}=\bar{J}
and
\mu=1/k
, and hence

\displaystyle r_{1}=12k\sqrt{\frac{k\bar{J}}{\breve{\Delta}}},\qquad\xi=29k\sqrt{\frac{k\bar{J}}{\breve{\Delta}}}\quad\textrm{for}\quad k\bar{J}\geq\breve{\Delta}.

(S.54)

Eventually, we can obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{\geq\langle x\rangle+\bar{x}}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{\leq\langle x\rangle-\bar{x}}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}\leq 2e^{-\bar{x}/\left(29k\sqrt{k\bar{J}/\breve{\Delta}}\right)}.

(S.55)

Explicitly, the parameter
\bar{J}
is derived from
\bra{\omega_{x^{\prime}}}H_{L^{\rm c}}\ket{\omega_{x}}=0
as

\displaystyle|\bra{\omega_{x^{\prime}}}H\ket{\omega_{x}}|=\left|\bra{\omega_{x}^{\prime}}\widehat{H}_{L}\ket{\omega_{x}}\right|\leq\left\|\widehat{H}_{L}\right\|\leq\bar{g}_{1}|L|,

(S.56)

which reduces the inequality (
S.50
) to

\displaystyle\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{\geq\langle x\rangle+\bar{x}}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{\leq\langle x\rangle-\bar{x}}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}\leq 2e^{-\bar{x}/\left(29k\sqrt{k\bar{g}_{1}|L|/\breve{\Delta}}\right)}.

(S.57)

S.II.2.2
Small hopping amplitude

We here consider the situation that the hopping amplitude is much smaller than the on-site potential.
Here, we decompose the Hamiltonian
\breve{H}
into

\displaystyle\breve{H}=\breve{H}_{0}+\breve{V},\qquad\breve{H}_{0}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,x^{\prime}=0\\
x\neq x^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{|L|}J_{x,x^{\prime}}\ket{\omega_{x^{\prime}}}\bra{\omega_{x}},\qquad\breve{V}=\sum_{x=0}^{|L|}J_{x}\ket{\omega_{x}}\bra{\omega_{x}},

(S.58)

and consider the case of
\left\|\breve{H}_{0}\right\|\ll 1
.
In this case, we prove the following lemma:

Lemma 5
.

Let us consider the tight binding Hamiltonian in the form of Eq. (
S.45
) and assume

\displaystyle|c_{0}|^{2}=\left|\braket{\omega_{x=0}|\Omega}\right|^{2}\geq 1/2,\qquad\frac{\bar{J}}{\breve{\Delta}}<1.

(S.59)

Then, the probability to measure larger than
x_{0}
in the ground state is upper-bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{>\bar{x}}\ket{\Omega}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 6\left(\frac{\bar{J}}{\breve{\Delta}}\right)^{\bar{x}/(6ek^{2})}.

(S.60)

Remark.

The inequality is meaningful only when
\bar{J}
is sufficiently smaller than
\breve{\Delta}
.
This bound gives the asymptotic decay rate as
e^{-\bar{x}\log(\Delta/\bar{J})}
, while
the upper bound (
S.50
) gives the decay rate slower than
e^{-\bar{x}/\Omega(1)}
.
In our application, we consider the case where
\bar{J}=n^{-\eta}
(
\eta=\Omega(1)
), and hence this difference is considerably important (see Sec.
S.V.3
).

Proof of Lemma
5
.

We construct the approximate ground state projection
K
which satisfies

\displaystyle K|\Omega\rangle=|\Omega\rangle,\qquad\left\|K|\Omega_{\perp}\rangle\right\|\approx 0,

(S.61)

where
|\Omega_{\perp}\rangle
is an arbitrary quantum state which is orthogonal to
|\Omega\rangle
.

Here, instead of relying on the method using the Chebyshev polynomial as in Ref.
Kuwahara et al. 2017
, we utilize the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation:

\displaystyle K_{\beta}=e^{-\beta\breve{H}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi\beta}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\breve{H}t}e^{-t^{2}/4\beta}\,dt.

(S.62)

Then, letting the ground state energy be equal to zero, we have

\displaystyle e^{-\beta\breve{H}^{2}}|\Omega\rangle=|\Omega\rangle,\qquad\left\|K_{\beta}|\Omega_{\perp}\rangle\right\|\leq e^{-\beta\breve{\Delta}^{2}},

(S.63)

where
|\Omega_{\perp}\rangle
is an arbitrary quantum state such that
\langle\Omega|\Omega_{\perp}\rangle=0
. We can write the state
|\omega_{x=0}\rangle
as
\ket{\omega_{x=0}}=c_{0}\ket{\Omega}+\sqrt{1-\left|c_{0}\right|^{2}}\ket{\Omega_{\perp}}
. Then the condition
c_{0}\geq 1/2
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left\|K_{\beta}\ket{\omega_{x=0}}-c_{0}\ket{\Omega}\right\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{1-|c_{0}|^{2}}}{c_{0}}e^{-\beta\breve{\Delta}^{2}}\leq e^{-\beta\breve{\Delta}^{2}}.

(S.64)

On the other hand, we can estimate
\left|\bra{\omega_{x}}K_{\beta}\ket{\omega_{x=0}}\right|
by using the form of Eq. (
S.62
). To treat the quantity
\left|\bra{\omega_{x}}e^{-i\breve{H}t}\ket{\omega_{x=0}}\right|
, we consider the interaction picture as

\displaystyle e^{-i\breve{H}t}=\mathcal{T}e^{\int_{0}^{t}\breve{H}_{0}(\breve{V},\tau)\,d\tau}e^{i\breve{V}t},

(S.65)

with
\breve{H}_{0}(\breve{V},\tau)=e^{i\breve{V}\tau}\breve{H}_{0}e^{-i\breve{V}\tau}
, where
\breve{H}_{0}
is the hopping term and
\breve{V}
is the on-site potential term. Here, the norm of the hopping Hamiltonian (not on-site potential) is upper-bounded by

\displaystyle\left\|\breve{H}_{0}(\breve{V},\tau)\right\|=\left\|\breve{H}_{0}\right\|\leq 2k\bar{J},

(S.66)

where we use the condition (
S.46
). The on-site potential term
e^{i\breve{V}t}
retains the original state
\ket{\omega_{x=0}}
, i.e.,
e^{i\breve{V}t}\ket{\omega_{x=0}}\propto\ket{\omega_{x=0}}
. Also, the hopping Hamiltonian
\breve{H}_{0}
has a length at most
k
, and hence

\displaystyle\left|\bra{\omega_{x}}\breve{H}_{0}(\breve{V},\tau)^{m}\ket{\omega_{x=0}}\right|=0\quad\text{for}\quad x>km,

(S.67)

where the equation holds for arbitrary
\tau
.

By using the above properties, we can get

\displaystyle\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{>\bar{x}}e^{-i\breve{H}t}\ket{\omega_{x=0}}\right\|\leq\min\left[1,\sum_{s>m_{\bar{x}}}\frac{(2k\bar{J}t)^{s}}{s!}\right],\qquad m_{\bar{x}}=\lfloor\bar{x}/k\rfloor.

(S.68)

by expanding Eq. (
S.65
) and take in the more than
\lfloor\bar{x}/k\rfloor
-order terms. From the inequality of

\displaystyle\sum_{s>m}\frac{x^{s}}{s!}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}\left(1+\frac{x}{m+2}+\frac{x^{2}}{(m+2)(m+3)}+\cdots\right)\leq\frac{x^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{s}}{s!}=e^{x}\frac{x^{m+1}}{(m+1)!},

(S.69)

we reduces the upper-bound (
S.68
) to

\displaystyle\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{>\bar{x}}e^{-i\breve{H}t}\ket{\omega_{x=0}}\right\|\leq\min\left[1,e^{2k\bar{J}t}\frac{(2k\bar{J}t)^{m_{\bar{x}}+1}}{(m_{\bar{x}}+1)!}\right]\leq\min\left[1,e^{2k\bar{J}t}\left(\frac{2ek^{2}\bar{J}t}{\bar{x}}\right)^{\bar{x}/k}\right],

(S.70)

where we use
m!\geq(m/e)^{m}
in the last inequality.

Therefore, we apply the above inequality to Eq. (
S.62
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{>\bar{x}}K_{\beta}\ket{\omega_{x=0}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi\beta}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}/4\beta}\min\left(1,\sum_{s>m_{\bar{x}}}\frac{(2k\bar{J}t)^{s}}{s!}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq e^{2k\bar{J}t_{0}}\left(\frac{2ek^{2}\bar{J}t_{0}}{x}\right)^{\bar{x}/k}+\sqrt{2}e^{-t_{0}^{2}/(8\beta)},

(S.71)

where we use the decomposition of
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt=\int_{|t|\leq t_{0}}dt+\int_{|t|>t_{0}}dt
and estimate the integral
\int_{|t|>t_{0}}dt
by

\displaystyle\int_{|t|<t_{0}}e^{-t^{2}/4\beta}\min\left(1,\sum_{s>m_{\bar{x}}}\frac{(2k\bar{J}t)^{s}}{s!}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq e^{2k\bar{J}t_{0}}\left(\frac{2ek^{2}\bar{J}t_{0}}{\bar{x}}\right)^{\bar{x}/k}\int_{|t|<t_{0}}e^{-t^{2}/4\beta}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\pi\beta}e^{2k\bar{J}t_{0}}\left(\frac{2ek^{2}\bar{J}t_{0}}{\bar{x}}\right)^{\bar{x}/k},

(S.72)

and the integral
\int_{|t|>t_{0}}dt
by

\displaystyle\int_{|t|>t_{0}}e^{-t^{2}/4\beta}\min\left(1,\sum_{s>m_{\bar{x}}}\frac{(2k\bar{J}t)^{s}}{s!}\right)\,dt\leq e^{-t_{0}^{2}/8\beta}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}/8\beta}\,dt\leq\sqrt{8\beta\pi}e^{-t_{0}^{2}/8\beta}.

(S.73)

Finally, by combining the inequalities (
S.64
) and (
S.71
), we derive

\displaystyle\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{>\bar{x}}\ket{\Omega}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{>\bar{x}}\left(c_{0}\ket{\Omega}-K_{\beta}\ket{\omega_{x=0}}\right)\right\|+\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{>\bar{x}}K_{\beta}\ket{\omega_{x=0}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\beta\breve{\Delta}^{2}}+2e^{2k\bar{J}t_{0}}\left(\frac{2ek^{2}\bar{J}t_{0}}{\bar{x}}\right)^{\bar{x}/k}+2\sqrt{2}e^{-t_{0}^{2}/(8\beta)},

(S.74)

where we use the condition of
c_{0}\geq 1/2
. We here choose
\beta=t_{0}/(\sqrt{8}\breve{\Delta})
, which makes
e^{-\beta\breve{\Delta}^{2}}=e^{-t_{0}^{2}/(8\beta)}=e^{-t_{0}\breve{\Delta}/\sqrt{8}}
, and
t_{0}=\bar{x}/(2ek^{2}\breve{\Delta})\log(\breve{\Delta}/\bar{J})
, and obtain the desired inequality of

\displaystyle\left\|\breve{\Pi}_{>\bar{x}}\ket{\Omega}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 4\left(\frac{\bar{J}}{\breve{\Delta}}\right)^{\bar{x}/(6ek^{2})}+2\left(\frac{\bar{J}}{\breve{\Delta}}\right)^{-\bar{x}/(ek)}\left(\frac{\bar{J}}{\breve{\Delta}}\log(\breve{\Delta}/\bar{J})\right)^{\bar{x}/k}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left(\frac{\bar{J}}{\breve{\Delta}}\right)^{\bar{x}/(6ek^{2})}+2\left(\frac{\bar{J}}{\breve{\Delta}}\right)^{-\bar{x}/(ek)}\left(\frac{\bar{J}}{\breve{\Delta}}\right)^{0.63\bar{x}/k}

\displaystyle\leq 6\left(\frac{\bar{J}}{\breve{\Delta}}\right)^{\bar{x}/(6ek^{2})},

(S.75)

where we use
e^{2k\bar{J}t_{0}}\leq e^{\bar{J}\bar{x}/(ek\breve{\Delta})\log(\breve{\Delta}/\bar{J})}\leq(\breve{\Delta}/\bar{J})^{\bar{J}\bar{x}/(ek\breve{\Delta})}\leq(\breve{\Delta}/\bar{J})^{\bar{x}/(ek)}
and
x\log(1/x)\leq x^{1-1/e}
for
0<x<1
. This completes the proof of Lemma
5
.
\square

5
]

S.III
Main Theorem and Overview of the Proof

S.III.1
Main theorem

For the all-to-all interacting system under the conditions discussed in the Assumptions
1
and
2
, we prove the following main result.

Theorem 1
.

The entanglement entropy between two bipartitions
A
and
B
of the sites
\Lambda
(
|\Lambda|=n
) in the ground state
\ket{\Omega}
of an all-to-all interacting
k
-local Hamiltonian, satisfying Assumptions
1
or
2
, is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle S_{A,B}\left(\ket{\Omega}\right)\leq 2(\log n)^{\alpha}+\log(d)+1,

(S.76)

where the constant
\alpha=\mathfrak{a}_{1}+\mathfrak{a}_{2}\log(f(\bar{g}_{1},\Delta))
is of order
\mathcal{O}(1)
, with
\mathfrak{a}_{1}=230\log(10368ek^{2})
,
\mathfrak{a}_{2}=230
, and
f(\bar{g}_{1},\Delta)=\max\left\{2,\log\left(\frac{324\bar{g}_{1}}{\Delta}\right)\right\}
.

Theorem
1
demonstrates that, in an all-to-all interacting system with a global gap, the entanglement entropy is bounded by polylogarithmic behavior with respect to
n
, thereby ruling out the possibility of polynomial growth in
n
.

S.III.2
Overview of the proof

In the following sections, we will prove the above Theorem by establishing several Lemmas and Propositions. Fig.
S.1
schematically represents this process.

1.

First, we demonstrate that the trade-off between the variance of an arbitrary operator and the global gap implies that a local unitary operator can alter the ground state energy by no more than
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n\Delta})
(Proposition
1
).

2.

Next, we show that this leads to a phenomenon in the Schmidt decomposition between a single site and the rest, where one Schmidt coefficient becomes significantly large, while the remaining Schmidt coefficients are extremely small (Proposition
2
).

3.

Utilizing this, we demonstrate that the local state associated with the largest Schmidt coefficient becomes approximately an eigenstate, under the condition that its deviation in the remaining region is sufficiently constrained (Proposition
3
).

4.

We extend this result from a single site to a specified region and prove that the probability distribution of the ground state, with respect to the number of local states deviating from the state associated with the largest Schmidt coefficient, decays exponentially. This exponentially suppressed probability distribution enables us to effectively truncate the Hilbert space (Proposition
4
).

5.

Leveraging the preceding discussion, we apply a renormalization process that truncates the Hilbert space, effectively reducing the overall dimension. Throughout this process, we monitor the evolving parameters (Proposition
5
) and continue the renormalization while preserving the desired conditions. This enables us to construct a state that closely approximates the ground state with a reduced Schmidt rank, thereby allowing us to prove the Theorem
1
.

Outline of the proof of the main theorem.

S.IV
Local Robustness and Largest Schmidt Coefficient

S.IV.1
Robustness of the ground state for local unitary operators

Proposition 1
.

Consider a local unitary operator
u_{i}
acting on site
i
, which leaves the reduced density matrix
\rho_{i}=\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda_{i}}(\rho_{0})
invariant, where
\rho_{0}=\ket{\Omega}\bra{\Omega}
and
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda\setminus\{i\}
. This invariance is expressed as:

\displaystyle u_{i}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{\dagger}=\rho_{i}.

(S.77)

The variation in the expectation value of the Hamiltonian induced by
u_{i}
is constrained above by a constant of order
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n\Delta})
, as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\bra{\Omega}\left(u_{i}^{\dagger}Hu_{i}-H\right)\ket{\Omega}\right|\leq\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}},

(S.78)

where

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{Rob}}=2\gamma g_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\bar{g}_{1}}{n\Delta}}.

(S.79)

To establish the above proposition, we will employ the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

We consider a bipartition of the total set of sites
\Lambda
into subsets
A
and
B
, with operators
X_{A}
and
Y_{B}
acting on
A
and
B
, respectively. For the density matrix
\rho
, we have the following inequality:

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{Cor}_{\rho}(X_{A},Y_{B})\right|\leq\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{\rho}(X_{A})\cdot\mathrm{Var}_{\rho}(Y_{B})},

(S.80)

where
\mathrm{Var}_{\rho}(Z):={\rm tr}\left(\rho Z^{2}\right)-{\rm tr}\left(\rho Z\right)^{2}
.

Proof of Lemma
6
.
We consider the doubly copied Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}=(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})^{\otimes 2}
. In this extended space, the new operators
[X_{A}]^{(-)}
and
[Y_{B}]^{(-)}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}
are defined as
[X_{A}]^{(-)}:=X_{A}\otimes\mathds{1}-\mathds{1}\otimes X_{A}
and
[Y_{B}]^{(-)}:=Y_{B}\otimes\mathds{1}-\mathds{1}\otimes Y_{B}
, acting on subspace
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{\otimes 2}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}^{\otimes 2}
, respectively.
This definition leads to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cor}_{\rho}(X_{A},Y_{B})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{\rm tr}\left[\left(\rho\otimes\rho\right)\left([X_{A}]^{(-)}[Y_{B}]^{{(-)}}\right)\right],

(S.81)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\rho}(X_{A})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{\rm tr}\left[\left(\rho\otimes\rho\right)\left([X_{A}]^{(-)}[X_{A}]^{{(-)}}\right)\right],

(S.82)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\rho}(Y_{B})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{\rm tr}\left[\left(\rho\otimes\rho\right)\left([Y_{B}]^{(-)}[Y_{B}]^{{(-)}}\right)\right].

(S.83)

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
\left|{\rm tr}\left(X^{\dagger}Y\right)\right|\leq\sqrt{{\rm tr}\left(X^{\dagger}X\right){\rm tr}\left(Y^{\dagger}Y\right)}
and Eqs. (
S.82
) and (
S.83
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|{\rm tr}\left[\left(\rho\otimes\rho\right)\left([X_{A}]^{(-)}[Y_{B}]^{{(-)}}\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{{\rm tr}\left[\left(\rho\otimes\rho\right)\left([X_{A}]^{(-)}[X_{A}]^{{(-)}}\right)\right]\cdot{\rm tr}\left[\left(\rho\otimes\rho\right)\left([Y_{B}]^{(-)}[Y_{B}]^{{(-)}}\right)\right]}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{\rho}(X_{A})\cdot\mathrm{Var}_{\rho}(Y_{B})}.

(S.84)

By combining (
S.81
) and (
S.84
), we obtain (
S.80
).
\square

[ End of Proof of Lemma
6
]

We now prove Proposition
1
.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Since the on-site interaction
V_{i}
yields the same expectation value with respect to both
\rho_{0}=\ket{\Omega}\bra{\Omega}
and
u_{i}\rho_{0}u_{i}^{\dagger}
, i.e.,
{\rm tr}(V_{i}u_{i}\rho_{0}u_{i}^{\dagger})={\rm tr}(V_{i}u_{i}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{\dagger})={\rm tr}(V_{i}\rho_{i})={\rm tr}(V_{i}\rho_{0})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|{\rm tr}(Hu_{i}\rho_{0}u_{i}^{\dagger})-{\rm tr}(H\rho_{0})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}u_{i}\rho_{0}u_{i}^{\dagger})-{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}\rho_{0})\right|,

(S.85)

where
\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}:=\sum_{Z:Z\ni i}h_{Z}-V_{i}
. Applying the triangle inequality after adding and subtracting
{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}\rho_{i}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda_{i}})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|{\rm tr}(Hu_{i}\rho_{0}u_{i}^{\dagger})-{\rm tr}(H\rho_{0})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}u_{i}\rho_{0}u_{i}^{\dagger})-{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}\rho_{i}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda_{i}})\right|+\left|{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}\rho_{0})-{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}\rho_{i}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda_{i}})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\rm tr}(u_{i}^{\dagger}\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}u_{i}\rho_{0})-{\rm tr}(u_{i}^{\dagger}\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}u_{i}\rho_{i}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda_{i}})\right|+\left|{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}\rho)-{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}\rho_{i}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda_{i}})\right|

(S.86)

where
\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}:=\sum_{Z:Z\ni i}h_{Z}-V_{i}
, and Eq. (
S.77
) is used in the last equation.

By applying the decomposition of
\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}
from Assumption
1
,

\displaystyle\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{s}\frac{J_{s}}{n}H_{i,s}\otimes H_{\Lambda_{i},s}

(S.87)

with
\{H_{i,s}\}_{s}
denoting the 1-extensive operator basis on site
i
, and using Lemma
6
, we find

\displaystyle\left|{\rm tr}(u_{i}^{\dagger}\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}u_{i}\rho_{0})-{\rm tr}(u_{i}^{\dagger}\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}u_{i}\rho_{i}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda_{i}})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s}\frac{|J_{s}|}{n}\left|\mathrm{Cor}_{\rho_{0}}\left(u_{i}^{\dagger}H_{i,s}u_{i},H_{\Lambda_{i},s}\right)\right|\leq\sum_{s}\frac{|J_{s}|}{n}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{\rho_{0}}(u_{i}^{\dagger}H_{i,s}u_{i})\mathrm{Var}_{\rho_{0}}(H_{\Lambda_{i},s})},

(S.88)

\displaystyle\left|{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}\rho_{0})-{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}\rho_{i}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda_{i}})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s}\frac{|J_{s}|}{n}\left|\mathrm{Cor}_{\rho_{0}}\left(H_{i,s},H_{\Lambda_{i},s}\right)\right|\leq\sum_{s}\frac{|J_{s}|}{n}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{\rho_{0}}(H_{i,s})\mathrm{Var}_{\rho_{0}}(H_{\Lambda_{i},s})}.

(S.89)

Since
H_{i,s}
is 1-extensive,
\mathrm{Var}_{\rho_{0}}(u_{i}^{\dagger}H_{i,s}u_{i})\leq\bra{\Omega}u_{i}^{\dagger}H_{i,s}^{2}u_{i}\ket{\Omega}\leq 1
and
\mathrm{Var}_{\rho_{0}}(H_{i,s})\leq\bra{\Omega}H_{i,s}^{2}\ket{\Omega}\leq 1
. Additionally, Lemma
3

under the assumption that

is 1-extensive. Consequently, inequalities (
S.88
) and (
S.89

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s}\frac{|J_{s}|}{n}\times\gamma\sqrt{\frac{\bar{g}_{1}n}{\Delta}}\leq\gamma g_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\bar{g}_{1}}{n\Delta}},

(S.90)

\displaystyle\left|{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}\rho_{0})-{\rm tr}(\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{i}\rho_{i}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda_{i}})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s}\frac{|J_{s}|}{n}\times\gamma\sqrt{\frac{\bar{g}_{1}n}{\Delta}}\leq\gamma g_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\bar{g}_{1}}{n\Delta}}.

Substituting inequalities (

) with Eq. (

1
.

S.IV.2
Largest local Schmidt coefficient

As a result of Proposition
1
, we obtain the following proposition, which states that the ground state locally has a single large Schmidt coefficient and other small Schmidt coefficients. Physically, it means that the local state is an approximately product state.

Proposition 2
.

If we consider the Schmidt decomposition of the ground state
\ket{\Omega}
between a single site
i\in\Lambda
and the remainder
\Lambda_{i}
as

\displaystyle\ket{\Omega}=\sum_{s\geq 0}\lambda_{s}\ket{s}_{i}\ket{\phi_{s}}_{\Lambda_{i}},

(S.92)

with descending ordered singular values
\lambda_{0}\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots
, then it satisfies

\displaystyle|\lambda_{0}|^{2}\geq 1-\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{2\Delta},\qquad\sum_{j\geq 1}|\lambda_{j}|^{2}\leq\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{2\Delta}.

(S.93)

Proof of Proposition
2
.
We consider the Schmidt decomposition of

\displaystyle\ket{\Omega}=\sum_{s=0}\lambda_{s}\ket{s}_{i}\ket{\phi_{s}}_{\Lambda_{i}},

(S.94)

where
\lambda_{0}\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots
.
We then consider the reference state as

\displaystyle\ket{\widetilde{\Omega}}:=\lambda_{0}\ket{0}_{i}\ket{\phi_{0}}_{\Lambda_{i}}-\sum_{s\geq 1}\lambda_{s}\ket{s}_{i}\ket{\phi_{s}}_{\Lambda_{i}}.

(S.95)

The state is constructed by the unitary operator that satisfies the condition (
S.77
).
By denoting

\displaystyle\ket{\widetilde{\Omega}}=a\ket{\Omega}+b\ket{\Omega_{\bot}},

(S.96)

we have

\displaystyle a=\braket{\Omega|\widetilde{\Omega}}=|\lambda_{0}|^{2}-\sum_{j\geq 1}|\lambda_{j}|^{2}.

(S.97)

Hence, by letting
\bra{\Omega}H\ket{\Omega}=E_{0}=0
, we have

\displaystyle|\bra{\widetilde{\Omega}}H\ket{\widetilde{\Omega}}|=|b|^{2}\left|\bra{\Omega_{\bot}}H\ket{\Omega_{\bot}}\right|\geq|b|^{2}\Delta.

(S.98)

The energy difference between
\ket{\widetilde{\Omega}}
and
\ket{\Omega}
is upper-bounded by Proposition
1
[see Ineq. (
S.78
)],
which gives

\displaystyle\left|\bra{\widetilde{\Omega}}H\ket{\widetilde{\Omega}}\right|=\left|\bra{\widetilde{\Omega}}H\ket{\widetilde{\Omega}}-E_{0}\right|\leq\delta_{\rm Rob}.

(S.99)

We thus obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm Rob}\geq|b|^{2}\Delta\quad\Rightarrow\quad|b|^{2}\leq\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{\Delta}.

(S.100)

Using
|a|^{2}=1-|b|^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle|\lambda_{0}|^{2}-\sum_{j\geq 1}|\lambda_{j}|^{2}=|a|\geq\sqrt{1-\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{\Delta}}\geq 1-\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{\Delta}.

(S.101)

By combining the above inequality with

\displaystyle|\lambda_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{j\geq 1}|\lambda_{j}|^{2}=1,

(S.102)

we reach the desired inequality (
S.93
).
\square

S.V
Reduction of the block Hilbert space

S.V.1
Mean-field operator

From the previous section, we have shown that for an arbitrary site
i\in\Lambda
, the local state is almost unentangled:

\displaystyle\rho_{i}:={\rm tr}_{\Lambda_{i}}\ket{\Omega}\bra{\Omega}=\ket{0}\bra{0}_{i}+\mathcal{O}(\delta_{\rm Rob}/\Delta),

(S.103)

where
\ket{0}_{i}
is the local state on a site
i
defined in the Schmidt decomposition of
\ket{\Omega}
as having the largest Schmidt coefficient.
Using the above equation, we can prove

\displaystyle\rho_{L}:={\rm tr}_{L^{c}}\ket{\Omega}\bra{\Omega}=\rho_{0,L}+\mathcal{O}(|L|\delta_{\rm Rob}/\Delta),

(S.104)

where
\rho_{0,L}
is the pure state of
\ket{0}_{L}=\bigotimes_{i\in L}\ket{0}_{i}
.

We here introduce the following “mean-field operator” as

\displaystyle M_{L}=\sum_{i\in L}Q_{i},\qquad Q_{i}=1-P_{i}=1-\ket{0}\bra{0}_{i}.

(S.105)

From Eq. (
S.104
), we have
\rho_{L}
is close to the ground state of
M_{L}
up to an error of
\mathcal{O}(|L|\delta_{\rm Rob}/\Delta)
.
We are now interested in the probability distribution of
M_{L}
.
Note that when the number of flipped spins in the region
L
is directly equal to the eigenvalue of
M_{L}
;
that is, for the state
\ket{0}_{L}
gives the ground state of
M_{L}
.

We define
\Pi_{\leq\bar{m}}^{L}
as a projection operator onto the eigenspace of
M_{L}
with the eigenvalues smaller than
\bar{m}
.
In particular, if
L=\Lambda
, we simply denote
\Pi_{\leq\bar{m}}^{\Lambda}=\Pi_{\leq\bar{m}}
.
Here, all the states in
\Pi_{\leq\bar{m}}^{L}
are given in the form of

\displaystyle\left(\bigotimes_{j\in L:j\neq\{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\}}\ket{0}_{j}\right)\otimes\ket{s_{1}}_{i_{1}}\otimes\ket{s_{2}}_{i_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\ket{s_{m}}_{i_{m}}\quad(m\leq\bar{m}),

(S.106)

where
\{\ket{s}_{i}\}_{s}
are orthonormal bases in the Hilbert space on the site
i
that is orthogonal to
\ket{0}_{i}
.
Under the projection
\Pi_{\leq\bar{m}}^{L}
, less than or equal to
\bar{m}
sites has flipped state
\ket{s}
(
s\geq 1
); in other words, more than or equal to
|L|-\bar{m}
sites have the state
\ket{0}
.

The average value of
M_{L}
with respect to the ground state is easily upper-bounded by using the mean-field approximation error as in (
S.93
).
We prove the following statement:

Lemma 7
.

The average value of
M_{L}
with respect to the ground state is upper-bounded by

\displaystyle\langle M_{L}\rangle_{\Omega}\leq\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{2\Delta}|L|.

(S.107)

Also, let
m_{\ast}
be the minimum integer such that

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{\leq m_{\ast}}^{L}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

(S.108)

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle m_{\ast}\leq\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{\Delta}|L|.

(S.109)

Proof of Lemma
7
.

The first inequality (
S.107
) immediately followed from the upper bound (
S.93
) as

\displaystyle\langle M_{L}\rangle_{\Omega}=\sum_{i\in L}{\rm tr}\left[(1-P_{i})\ket{\Omega}\bra{\Omega}\right]\leq\sum_{i\in L}\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{2\Delta}=\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{2\Delta}|L|.

(S.110)

For the second inequality (
S.109
),
we decompose the ground state
\ket{\Omega}
into

\displaystyle\ket{\Omega}=\Pi_{\leq m_{\ast}}^{L}\ket{\Omega}+\Pi_{>m_{\ast}}^{L}\ket{\Omega}=c_{1}\ket{\Omega_{\leq m_{\ast}}}+c_{2}\ket{\Omega_{>m_{\ast}}},

(S.111)

where
c_{1}=\left\|\Pi_{\leq m_{\ast}}^{L}\ket{\Omega}\right\|
,
c_{2}=\left\|\Pi_{>m_{\ast}}^{L}\ket{\Omega}\right\|
,
\ket{\Omega_{\leq m_{\ast}}}=\frac{\Pi_{\leq m_{\ast}}^{L}\ket{\Omega}}{\left\|\Pi_{\leq m_{\ast}}^{L}\ket{\Omega}\right\|}
, and
\ket{\Omega_{>m_{\ast}}}=\frac{\Pi_{>m_{\ast}}^{L}\ket{\Omega}}{\left\|\Pi_{>m_{\ast}}^{L}\ket{\Omega}\right\|}
.
We then obtain

\displaystyle\bra{\Omega}M_{L}\ket{\Omega}\geq|c_{2}|^{2}m_{\ast}.

(S.112)

On the other hand, because of the inequality (
S.93
), we have

\displaystyle\bra{\Omega}M_{L}\ket{\Omega}\leq\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{2\Delta}|L|,

(S.113)

which yields by combining with the inequality (
S.112
)

\displaystyle|c_{2}|^{2}=1-\left\|\Pi_{\leq m_{\ast}}^{L}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{2m_{\ast}\Delta}|L|\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left\|\Pi_{\leq m_{\ast}}^{L}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}\geq 1-\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{2m_{\ast}\Delta}|L|.

(S.114)

Then, the condition (
S.108
) is satisfied for

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{2m_{\ast}\Delta}|L|\geq\frac{1}{2},

(S.115)

which gives the desired inequality (
S.109
).
This completes the proof of Lemma
7
.
\square

S.V.2
Hilbert-space truncation by projection

By employing the mean field operator, we reduce the Hilbert space to a smaller subspace. To facilitate this, we first prove the following lemma regarding the variation of the ground state and the energy gap when the Hamiltonian is projected onto an arbitrary subspace.

From the lemma, we can restrict the Hilbert space of the ground state to the rather small portion in the total Hilbert space.
This dimension reduction itself is not so useful, but by limiting the space of
M_{L}
is useful in deriving much stronger bound for probability distribution for smaller subsystem
L
.

Lemma 8
.

We consider the Hilbert space spanned by
\Pi
such that

\displaystyle 1-\left\|\Pi\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}\leq\epsilon,\qquad\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}.

(S.116)

The effective Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}
in this restricted Hilbert space, i.e.,

\displaystyle\tilde{H}:=\Pi H\Pi,

(S.117)

has the ground state
\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\ket{\Omega}-\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}\right\|\leq\frac{\bar{\epsilon}}{1-\bar{\epsilon}},\qquad\bar{\epsilon}:=\frac{9\left\|H\right\|}{\Delta}\sqrt{\epsilon}\leq\frac{9\bar{g}_{1}n}{\Delta}\sqrt{\epsilon},

(S.118)

and the spectral gap
\tilde{\Delta}
of
\tilde{H}
is lower-bounded by

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}\geq(1-\bar{\epsilon})\Delta.

(S.119)

Proof of Lemma
8
.

We rely on the same proof technique in Ref.
(
Kuwahara and Saito 2020
, Supplemental Lemma 4)
.
We first expand
\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}
as

\displaystyle\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}=\zeta_{1}\ket{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}+\zeta_{2}\ket{\psi_{\bot}},

\displaystyle\ket{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}:=\frac{\Pi\ket{\Omega}}{\left\|\Pi\ket{\Omega}\right\|},\qquad\Pi\ket{\psi_{\bot}}=\ket{\psi_{\bot}},\qquad\bra{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}\psi_{\bot}\rangle=0.

(S.120)

Then, we obtain from Ref.
(
Kuwahara and Saito 2020
, Supplemental Ineq. (69))
.

\displaystyle\left\|\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}-\ket{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}\right\|\leq\frac{|f|}{f_{\bot}-f_{0}}

(S.121)

with

\displaystyle f_{0}:=\bra{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}\tilde{H}\ket{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}},\quad f_{\bot}:=\bra{\psi_{\bot}}\tilde{H}\ket{\psi_{\bot}},\quad f=\bra{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}\tilde{H}\ket{\psi_{\bot}}.

(S.122)

We then estimate the parameters
f_{0}
,
f_{\bot}
and
f
.
We first upper-bound
|f|
using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality as

\displaystyle|f|\leq\sqrt{\bra{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}\tilde{H}\ket{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}\bra{\psi_{\bot}}\tilde{H}\ket{\psi_{\bot}}}=\sqrt{f_{0}f_{\bot}}

(S.123)

and hence the upper bound (
S.121
) reduces to

\displaystyle\left\|\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}-\ket{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}\right\|\leq\frac{|f|}{f_{\bot}-f_{0}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{f_{0}/f_{\bot}}}{1-f_{0}/f_{\bot}},

(S.124)

which monotically increases with
f_{0}/f_{\bot}
.
Using
H\ket{\Omega}=0
and the condition (
S.116
), we obtain

\displaystyle f_{0}=\frac{\bra{\Omega}\Pi H\Pi\ket{\Omega}}{\left\|\Pi\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}}\leq\frac{\left\|H\right\|\cdot\left\|(1-\Pi)\ket{\Omega}\right\|}{\left\|\Pi\ket{\Omega}\right\|}\leq\frac{\left\|H\right\|\sqrt{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{1-\epsilon}},

(S.125)

where we use
\left\|\Pi\ket{\Omega}\right\|\geq\sqrt{1-\epsilon}
and
\left\|(1-\Pi)\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}=1-\left\|\Pi\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}\leq\epsilon
.
Also, because of
\bra{\psi_{\bot}}\tilde{H}\ket{\psi_{\bot}}=\bra{\psi_{\bot}}H\ket{\psi_{\bot}}
and

\displaystyle\left|\bra{\Omega}\psi_{\bot}\rangle\right|^{2}=\left|\bra{\Omega}(1-\Pi)\ket{\psi_{\bot}}\right|^{2}\leq\left\|(1-\Pi)\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}\leq\epsilon,

(S.126)

we obtain

\displaystyle f_{\bot}=\bra{\psi_{\bot}}H\ket{\psi_{\bot}}\geq(1-\epsilon)\Delta.

(S.127)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{f_{0}}{f_{\bot}}\leq\frac{\left\|H\right\|\sqrt{\epsilon}}{(1-\epsilon)\Delta\sqrt{1-\epsilon}}\leq\frac{3\left\|H\right\|}{\Delta}\sqrt{\epsilon}=:\check{\epsilon}

(S.128)

where we use
\epsilon\leq 1/2
. Utilizing the definition of
\ket{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}
in Eq. (
S.120
), we can obtain the following inequality

\displaystyle\left\|\ket{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}-\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2-\braket{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}|\tilde{\Omega}}-\braket{\tilde{\Omega}|\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}=2-\frac{\braket{\Omega|\tilde{\Omega}}}{\left\|\Pi\ket{\Omega}\right\|}-\frac{\braket{\tilde{\Omega}|\Omega}}{\left\|\Pi\ket{\Omega}\right\|}\geq 2-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\epsilon}}\left(\braket{\Omega|\tilde{\Omega}}+\braket{\tilde{\Omega}|\Omega}\right)

\displaystyle=2-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\epsilon}}\left(2-\left\|\ket{\Omega}-\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}\right\|^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\epsilon}}\left\|\ket{\Omega}-\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}\right\|^{2}+2\frac{\sqrt{1-\epsilon}-1}{\sqrt{1-\epsilon}},

(S.129)

i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\|\ket{\Omega}-\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}\right\|^{2}\leq\sqrt{1-\epsilon}\left\|\ket{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}-\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}\right\|^{2}+2\left(1-\sqrt{1-\epsilon}\right)\leq\left\|\ket{\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime}}-\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}\right\|^{2}+2\epsilon.

(S.130)

Applying inequality (
S.128
) to (
S.124
), and subsequently combining it with Eq. (
S.130
), yields

\displaystyle\left\|\ket{\Omega}-\ket{\tilde{\Omega}}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{\check{\epsilon}}{1-\check{\epsilon}}+2\epsilon.

(S.131)

Given that
\epsilon\leq\check{\epsilon}
due to
\Delta\leq\left\|H\right\|
, it follows that
2\epsilon\leq 2\check{\epsilon}/(1-\check{\epsilon})
. Thus, by defining
\bar{\epsilon}:=3\check{\epsilon}
, the main inequality (
S.118
) is thereby established.

Finally, from Ref.
(
Kuwahara and Saito 2020
, Supplemental Eq. (62))
, the spectral gap
\tilde{\Delta}
is lower-bounded by

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}\geq\sqrt{(f_{\bot}-f_{0})^{2}+4|f|^{2}}\geq f_{\bot}-f_{0}.

(S.132)

By utilizing inequalities (
S.128
) and (
S.127
) in (
S.132
),

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}\geq\left(1-\check{\epsilon}\right)\left(1-\epsilon\right)\Delta\geq(1-2\check{\epsilon})\Delta\geq(1-\bar{\epsilon})\Delta,

(S.133)

where we use
\epsilon\leq\check{\epsilon}
and
\bar{\epsilon}=3\check{\epsilon}
.
This also gives the second main inequality (
S.119
).
This completes the proof of Lemma
8
.
\square

S.V.3
Loose error bound on the Hilbert-space truncation

We derive the probability distribution of the operator
M_{L}
with respect to the ground state, utilizing the lemmas established in the previous subsections. Combining Lemmas
4
[or the inequality (
S.57
)] and
7
, we obtain the probability distribution of
M_{L}
for the ground state
\ket{\Omega}
as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{\geq\langle M_{L}\rangle_{\Omega}+q}^{L}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}\leq 3e^{-q/\left[15k\sqrt{k\bar{g}_{1}|L|/\Delta}\right]},

(S.134)

In particular, in the case of

, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{\geq m}\ket{\Omega}\right\|^{2}\leq 3\exp\left[-\left(m-\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{2\Delta}n\right)\frac{1}{15k}\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{k\bar{g}_{1}n}}\right].

(S.135)

From the above upper bound, we need to take the truncation number
m_{0}
to achieve an error
\tilde{\epsilon}
for

as follows:

\displaystyle m_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{\rm Rob}}{2\Delta}n+15k\sqrt{\frac{k\bar{g}_{1}n}{\Delta}}\log(3/\tilde{\epsilon})=\sqrt{\frac{\bar{g}_{1}n}{\Delta}}\left[\frac{\gamma g_{1}}{\Delta}+15k^{3/2}\log(3/\tilde{\epsilon})\right],

(S.136)

where we use the explicit form (
S.79
) of
\delta_{\rm Rob}
as
\delta_{\rm Rob}=2\gamma g_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\bar{g}_{1}}{n\Delta}}
.
For a general subsystem

, the truncation should be as large as
\sqrt{|L|}
. However, it is too weak in reducing the Hilbert space dimension on

since the remained Hilbert space dimension is still subexponentially large.
Still, it is meaningful to truncate the spectrum of
M_{\Lambda}
in the subsequent analyses.

We now choose the error
\tilde{\epsilon}
in Eq. (
S.136
) as

\displaystyle\tilde{\epsilon}=\left(\frac{\Delta}{9\bar{g}_{1}n}\epsilon_{0}\right)^{2}.

(S.137)

Combining Lemma
8
with the inequality (
S.134
) and the choice of Eq. (
S.136
),
we prove the following corollary:

Corollary 9
.

Consider a constrained Hilbert space defined by the projection operator
\Pi_{\leq m_{0}}
. Within this framework, define the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\check{h}_{i,m_{0}}-\check{h}_{i}\Pi_{\leq m_{0}}^{\Lambda_{i}}\right\|+\left\|\check{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\check{h}_{i}\right\|+\left\|\check{h}_{i}^{\prime}P_{i}-\epsilon_{i}P_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 6g_{1}\sqrt{\frac{k(m_{0}+1)}{n}}+6g_{1}\sqrt{\frac{k(m_{0}+1)}{n}}+2\delta_{0}

\displaystyle=2\delta_{0}+12g_{1}\sqrt{\frac{k(m_{0}+1)}{n}},

(S.181)

which also gives the inequality (

). This completes the proof of Proposition

S.VI
Mean-Field Renormalization Group (MFRG) Approach

We partition
\Lambda
, which consists of
n
sites, into blocks

, each containing
|L|
sites (see Fig.

). Using the previous results in Proposition

,
we construct the projection

as

(S.198)

For the convenience of readers, we show all the parameters in the following:

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\sqrt{18k(k+1)},

\displaystyle\tilde{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\Delta}{9\bar{g}_{1}n}\epsilon_{0}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle m_{0}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\bar{g}_{1}n}{\Delta}}\left[\frac{\gamma g_{1}}{\Delta}+15k^{3/2}\log(3/\tilde{\epsilon})\right],

\displaystyle\delta_{0}

By letting

Under the choices, the inequality (

By using Lemma

(S.205)

(S.232)

which reduces Eq. (

\displaystyle V_{L_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Z:Z\subset L_{j}}h_{Z}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s}J_{s}H_{L_{j},s}\bra{0}H_{L_{j}^{c},s}\ket{0}_{L_{j}^{c}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Z:Z\subset L_{j}}h_{Z}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s}J_{s}\bra{0}H_{L_{j},s}\otimes H_{L_{j}^{c},s}\ket{0}_{L_{j}^{c}}

Then, using a similar inequality to (

\displaystyle\leq\frac{g_{1}k|L|^{2}}{n},

(S.234)

where we use the upper bound in (
S.16

Finally, by letting

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{\leq z}^{L_{j}}\left(\sum_{i\in L_{j}}Q_{i}\right)\Pi_{\leq z}^{L_{j}}\right\|=z.

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{\leq z}^{L_{j}}\left(\sum_{i\in L_{j}}Q_{i}\bra{0}\widehat{H}_{i}\ket{0}_{\Lambda_{i}}Q_{i}\right)\Pi_{\leq z}^{L_{j}}\right\|\leq\bar{g}_{1}z.

(S.239)

Finally, from inequality (

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi^{L_{j}}_{\leq z}\left(\sum_{i\in L_{j}}\bra{0}\widehat{H}_{i}\ket{0}_{\Lambda_{i}}\right)\Pi^{L_{j}}_{\leq z}\right\|\leq\bar{g}_{1}z+|L|\left(4\delta_{0}+24g_{1}\sqrt{\frac{k}{n}}\right).

(S.240)

Therefore, by denoting

(S.241)

\displaystyle\left\|V_{j}^{(1)}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|V_{L_{j}}-\sum_{i\in L_{j}}\bra{0}\widehat{H}_{i}\ket{0}_{\Lambda_{i}}\right\|+\left\|\Pi^{L_{j}}_{\leq z}\left(\sum_{i\in L_{j}}\bra{0}\widehat{H}_{i}\ket{0}_{\Lambda_{i}}\right)\Pi^{L_{j}}_{\leq z}\right\|

For

Let us consider the situation where the system is divided into two subsystems

The Schmidt decomposition of the ground state is expressed as:

\displaystyle\ket{\Omega}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}\ket{\psi_{A,i}}\otimes\ket{\psi_{B,i}},

(S.280)

where the singular values are ordered in descending order:
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots

According to the Eckart-Young theorem, for any normalized state
\ket{\phi}
with Schmidt rank

, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{i>D_{\phi}}\lambda_{i}^{2}\leq\left\|\ket{\Omega}-\ket{\phi}\right\|^{2},

(S.281)

and thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{i>D_{\mu}}\lambda_{i}^{2}\leq\left\|\ket{\Omega}-\ket{\Omega_{\mu}}\right\|^{2}\leq\delta_{\mu}^{2}.

(S.282)

Therefore, we have the bound

\displaystyle\sum_{D_{\mu}\leq i<D_{\mu+1}}-\lambda_{i}^{2}\log\left(\lambda_{i}^{2}\right)\leq-\delta_{\mu}^{2}\log\left(\frac{\delta_{\mu}^{2}}{D_{\mu+1}}\right).

(S.283)

This leads to the following inequality for the entanglement entropy:

\displaystyle S(\ket{\Omega}):=\sum_{i\geq 1}-\lambda_{i}^{2}\log\left(\lambda_{i}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\log\left(D_{\mu=1}\right)-\sum_{\mu=2}^{\Xi}\delta_{\mu}^{2}\log\left(\frac{\delta_{\mu}^{2}}{D_{\mu+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(\log n)^{230\log(18z_{1})}+1+\sum_{\mu=2}^{\Xi-1}\delta_{\mu}^{2}\log D_{\mu+1}+\frac{1}{n}\log\left(d^{n}\right)

(S.284)

where we defined

.
We reformulate the summation in inequality (
S.284
) as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=2}^{\Xi-1}\delta_{\mu}^{2}\log D_{\mu+1}=\sum_{\mu=2}^{\Xi-1}\frac{1}{(\log n)^{2\kappa\mu}}(\log n)^{230\log(18z_{\mu})}\leq\sum_{\mu=2}^{\Xi-1}\frac{1}{(\log n)^{2\kappa\mu}}(\log n)^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\log(\mu)}=\left(\log n\right)^{\alpha_{1}}\sum_{\mu=2}^{\Xi-1}\left(\log n\right)^{\alpha_{2}\log\mu-2\kappa\mu},

(S.285)

where the constants are chosen as
\alpha_{1}=230\log\left(18z_{0}\right)
and
\alpha_{2}=230
. Since
\alpha_{2}<2\kappa
, it leads to the upper bound:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=2}^{\Xi-1}\delta_{\mu}^{2}\log D_{\mu+1}\leq\frac{1}{2\kappa}\left(\log n\right)^{\alpha_{1}+1}.

(S.286)

By combining the above result with inequality (
S.284
), we arrive at the following conclusion:

\displaystyle S(\ket{\Omega})

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\log n\right)^{230\log(18z_{1})}+\log d+1,

(S.287)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\log n\right)^{\mathfrak{a}_{1}+\mathfrak{a}_{2}\log(f(\bar{g}_{1},\Delta))}+\log d+1,

(S.288)

where
\mathfrak{a}_{1}=230\log(10368ek^{2})
,
\mathfrak{a}_{2}=230
, and
f(\bar{g}_{1},\Delta)=\max\left\{2,\log\left(\frac{324\bar{g}_{1}}{\Delta}\right)\right\}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.

S.VII

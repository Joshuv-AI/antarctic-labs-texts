Stress Coping and Psychological Resilience Development in Newly Licensed Registered Nurses During the Transition to Independent ICU Practice: A Descriptive Qualitative Study [Letter]
Authors: Xu Liu, Li Ning
Published: 2026

Dear editor

It is with great interest that we read the article by Wu et al1 titled “Stress Coping and Psychological Resilience Development in Newly Licensed Registered Nurses During the Transition to Independent ICU Practice: A Descriptive Qualitative Study”. The authors have constructed a valuable “challenge-adaptation-growth” framework that provides deep insights into the professional metamorphosis of newly licensed registered nurses (NLRNs) in high-acuity environments. However, after a careful review of the methodology and results, we have identified several points that warrant further clarification and discussion to enhance the study’s rigor.

First, we noted a potential ambiguity regarding the relationship between the researchers and participants. In the “Data Collection” section, the authors mentioned that a “strong trust relationship existed” due to both parties being ICU staff. However, the “Rigour” section stated that “no prior relationships existed” to minimize bias. Further clarification on this point would be beneficial to help readers better understand the researcher positionality and how potential social desirability bias was managed during data collection.2

Second, regarding the reporting of qualitative trustworthiness procedures, we noted that the study did not detail the use of member-checking. While participant validation is one recognized strategy to enhance credibility in qualitative research, it is not a universal requirement for all designs.3 Nevertheless, clarifying whether such procedures were employed would add further weight to the study’s findings. Additionally, specifying the type of transcription (eg, verbatim vs. selective) would assist readers in understanding the extent to which non-verbal cues were integrated into the thematic analysis.

Third, we observed a minor typographical error in the “Results” section, where participants were referred to as “patients”. Based on the study context and Table 2, these were clearly NLRNs. Correcting this would ensure the manuscript’s editorial accuracy.

Finally, building upon the significant findings of Wu et al, we identify several promising directions for future research. While the original descriptive qualitative design effectively captures the transition experience, future studies could consider a mixed-methods approach. Integrating qualitative exploration with validated tools, such as the Connor-Davidson Resilience Scale (CD-RISC4), could provide a more comprehensive understanding of resilience development.5 Furthermore, given the study’s emphasis on the integration into the clinical team, future investigations could employ sociograms to visually map the complex interpersonal dynamics that occur as nurses transition from the periphery to the “inner circle”.

In conclusion, we appreciate the authors’ contribution to understanding the resilience of ICU nurses. Addressing these points of clarification would further strengthen the foundation for future interventions in this field.

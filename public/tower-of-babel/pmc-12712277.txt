LINC01063 promotes colon cancer progression via the miR-134-5p/KRAS axis and serves as a prognostic biomarker
Authors: Qianli Liu, Sijia Sun, Rui Fan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDespite advances in treatment, the prognosis of colon cancer is still unsatisfactory, and there is an urgent need for reliable biomarkers and mechanistic studies. The role of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) in tumor progression is well established, but the prognostic value and mechanism of LINC01063 in colon cancer are unclear.MethodsWe analyzed samples of colon cancer tissues and paracancerous normal tissues from 116 patients and performed functional experiments in the RKO/HCT116 cell line. The expressions of LINC01063 and miR-134-5p were measured using qRT-PCR. Clinical significance was assessed by statistical methods, and their interactions were verified by dual luciferase assay. The biological effects were evaluated by CCK-8, Transwell, and apoptosis assays.ResultsLINC01063 was upregulated in cancer tissues, associated with the TNM staging system and lymph node dissemination, and an independent predictor of poor prognosis. LINC01063 acts as a “molecular sponge” to adsorb miR-134-5p and deregulate its inhibitory effect on KRAS. Knockdown of LINC01063 inhibited cell proliferation/invasion and promoted apoptosis, partially reversed by miR-134-5p inhibition. LINC01063 sponged miR-134-5p to relieve inhibition of KRAS, with KRAS mediating its pro-tumor effects.ConclusionIn this study, we demonstrated that LINC01063 can be used as an independent prognostic marker for colon cancer and explored its promotion of colon cancer cancer progression through the miR-134-5p/KRAS axis. Future studies will focus on in vivo validation and elucidation of downstream signaling cascades, while clinically, LINC01063 holds potential as a prognostic indicator and a therapeutic target for KRAS-mutant colon cancer, aiding in personalized treatment strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03961-7.

Introduction

Colon cancer is a highly prevalent malignant tumor worldwide, with the third and second highest incidence and mortality rates of all cancers, respectively [1, 2]. The latest statistics show that 1.93 million novel cases of colon cancer emerge globally every year, accompanied by approximately 935,000 fatalities [3], of which the incidence in young patients (< 50 years old) is increasing at a rate of 2% per year [4]. Despite advances in treatments such as surgery and radiotherapy, recurrence or metastasis occurs in 30–50% of patients [5, 6]. These grim statistics highlight the urgency of finding novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

In recent epochs, it has been discerned that long non-coding ribonucleic acids (lncRNAs) are capable of participating in tumorigenesis and its progression via a diverse array of mechanisms [7, 8]. Among them, the competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) mechanism - i.e., lncRNAs regulate gene expression by competitively binding to microRNAs (miRNAs) - has received extensive attention [9]. One study found that LINC01063 may be significantly expressed in colon cancer cells [10], but its correlation with clinical indicators and the mechanism of action has not been thoroughly investigated. Although it has been shown that LINC01063 promotes tumor progression by adsorbing miR-5194 to upregulate the SOX12 signaling pathway in melanoma [11], and lncRNA H19 activates the β-catenin signaling pathway by competitively binding to miR-200a in colorectal cancer [12]. However, the specific mechanism of action of LINC01063 in colon cancer, especially whether it regulates key oncogenic signaling pathways through a ceRNA mechanism, is not clear. These previous findings suggest that LINC01063 may play an important role in colon cancer, but more studies are needed to confirm this.

Numerous studies have shown that aberrant expression of miRNAs is widely involved in the development of tumors, and in cancer, miRNA dysregulation dysregulates the expression of target miRNAs and promotes tumor progression [13]. miR-134 is one of the down-regulated miRNAs in a variety of cancers, including colorectal cancer [14], breast cancer [15], etc., and serves as an oncogene suppressor by silencing a variety of oncogenes miRNA [16]. Among them, miR-134-5p, as a potential oncogenic miRNA, exerts anti-tumor effects by targeting oncogenes in non-small-cell lung cancer [17]. Notably, bioinformatics analysis revealed conserved binding sites between LINC01063 and miR-134-5p, yet their regulatory relationship and functional relevance in colon cancer remain unclear. Thus, we hypothesized that LINC01063 may act through targeting miR-134-5p to modulate colon cancer progression.

In this study, we enrolled clinical colon cancer patients to detect LINC01063 expression in tumor tissues and adjacent normal tissues, analyze its correlation with clinical characteristics and prognostic significance, and further explore its biological functions in colon cancer cells. Meanwhile, we verified the interaction between LINC01063 and miR-134-5p and clarified their regulatory mechanism, aiming to provide a theoretical basis for identifying novel prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for colon cancer.

Materials and methods

Study population

In this study, 116 patients who were diagnosed with colon cancer and underwent surgical treatment in our hospital were selected as study subjects. Detailed clinical information of the patients is as follows: age ranged from 40 to 65 years (mean 55 years); 57 males and 57 females; all patients had no other underlying diseases and had not received anti-cancer treatment (including chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or targeted therapy) before diagnosis. The patients provided their informed consent, and the study received endorsement from the Ethics Committee of Harbin Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital. During surgery, paired tumor tissues and adjacent normal tissues (≥ 5 cm from the tumor edge) were collected, and the pathological diagnosis was confirmed by at least two senior pathologists. The survival of all patients was monitored for 5 years by telephone follow-up after surgery. Additionally, to strengthen the prognostic claims, multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed, adjusting for potential confounding factors such as age and gender, to evaluate the independent prognostic value of LINC01063.

Cell culture

Colon cancer cells (SW480, RKO, HCT116, and COLO320 cells) and normal control cells (CCD841) were obtained from the American Type Culture Conservation Center (ATCC) and were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) routinely at 37 °C with 5% CO₂.

Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR)

After treatment of tissue samples and cells by trypsin digestion, we extracted total RNA with Trizol reagent, and the concentration and purity of RNA were determined by Nanodrop, and then the RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using PrimeScript RT kit and TaqMan miR reverse transcription kit (Applied Biosystems, USA), respectively (The former was used for the LINC01063 assay with GAPDH as the internal reference gene; the latter was used for the miR-134-5p assay with U6 snRNA as the internal reference gene). Detection was carried out using the SYBR Fluorescence Quantification Kit and CFX96 Real-Time Fluorescence Quantitative PCR System (Bio-Rad), and the relative expression levels were computed using the 2−ΔΔCt algorithm. Primer sequences are provided in Table S1.

Cell transfection

In this study, five transfection systems were established in RKO and HCT116 colon cancer cell lines using Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent: blank control group, negative control siRNA group (si-NC), LINC01063-specific siRNA knockdown group (si-LINC01063), si-LINC01063 combined with negative control miRNA group (si-LINC01063 + miR-NC), and si-LINC01063 combined miR-134-5p inhibitor group (si-LINC01063 + miR-inhibitor). In addition, to validate whether KRAS mediates the effects of the LINC01063/miR-134-5p axis, two additional transfection groups were established: si-LINC01063 + miR-inhibitor + negative control siRNA for KRAS (si-LINC01063 + miR-inhibitor + KRAS-NC) and si-LINC01063 + miR-inhibitor + KRAS-specific siRNA (si-LINC01063 + miR-inhibitor + si-KRAS). Specific nucleotide sequences for all functional oligonucleotides are provided in Supplementary Table S2. When the cell fusion reached 60–70%, each nucleic acid molecule was mixed with transfection reagents according to the manufacturer’s recommended ratios and added into the cell culture system, and the complete medium was replaced after 6 h, and the cells were collected after 48 h of transfection.

Bioinformatics analysis

Bioinformatics analysis was performed to identify potential target genes of miR-134-5p. We utilized two well-established databases: TargetScan (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_72/) and miRDB (https://mirdb.org/). In TargetScan, we set the threshold value Total context + + score ≥ 0.4 to screen for potential target genes. In miRDB, we selected target genes with a target score ≥ 80. By crossing the results of these two databases, we obtained six candidate target genes.

Dual-luciferase reporter gene assay

The wild-type or mutant 3’-untranslated region (3’UTR) of LINC01063 was cloned into a luciferase vector and then co-transfected with miR-134-5p mimic (miR-mimic) or inhibitor (miR-inhibitor) in RKO cells and HCT116 cells. After 48 h of transfection, luciferase activity was assayed.

Cell proliferation

Cells were inoculated in 96-well plates for culture, with three replicate wells in each group. Under complete medium conditions at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h post-transfection, 10µL of CCK-8 solution (Dojindo, Japan) was dispensed into each well and incubated at 37℃ for 2 h, respectively. The OD value at 450 nm (detection wavelength) was measured by an enzyme labeling instrument (BioTek ELx808) to assess the cell proliferation potency.

Cell invasion

The 0.8 μm pore size Transwell chamber was used, and the specific steps were as follows: The Transwell insert membrane was pre-coated with 1:8 dilution of Matrigel (BD Biosciences) prepared in serum-depleted culture medium, sterilized overnight by UV sterilization and dried, then rehydrated and set aside. When the cell density in the 6-well plate reached 70%, the corresponding nucleic acid reagents were transfected with Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen), and the cells were digested and collected after 24 h. 100 µL of the cell suspension was transferred to the upper compartment of the Transwell insert, where serum-free medium was present. Then, 600 µL of complete growth medium, containing 20% FBS, was added to the lower chamber to act as a chemoattractant. After 24 h of incubation at 37 °C and 5% CO₂, the transmigrated cells attached to the lower surface of the membrane were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, stained with 0.5% crystal violet for 15 min, and gently washed with PBS. After rinsing with PBS, five randomly selected fields of view were counted and then quantitatively analyzed.

Apoptosis detection

The apoptosis was assessed by analysis using Annexin V-FITC Apoptosis Detection Kit (BD, USA). Cells were transfected using the appropriate plasmids. Subsequently, cells were harvested after treatment with trypsin. For cell apoptosis analysis, cells were fixed with cold ethanol overnight, followed by staining, and the apoptosis rate was detected by FACSCalibur flow cytometry.

Statistical analysis

The study was statistically analyzed using SPSS 23.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.0. Measurements were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Clinical case characteristics were analyzed using independent samples t-tests and Kaplan-Meier log-rank tests; prognostic factors were analyzed using Cox regression analysis; Gene level comparisons were analyzed by one-way ANOVA; data from multiple time points in cell-cell proliferation experiments were analyzed by repeated measures ANOVA; apoptosis rates detected by flow cytometry were analyzed by one-way ANOVA Correlations were analyzed by chi-square test and Pearson’s correlation. The difference was considered statistically significant at p < 0.05.

Results

Expression characteristics of LINC01063 and miR-134-5p in colon cancer tissues

The data revealed that LINC01063 was expressed at a substantially higher level in colon cancer tissues relative to the neighboring normal tissues, and this disparity was highly statistically significant (p < 0.0001, Fig. 1A). In contrast, miR-134-5p expression was significantly downregulated in tumors (p < 0.0001, Fig. 1B). Notably, Pearson correlation analysis showed a strong negative correlation between the levels of LINC01063 and miR-134-5p (r = -0.646, p < 0.0001, Fig. 1C), supporting their potential interaction in colon cancer pathogenesis.

Correlation analysis between LINC01063 expression and clinical characteristics of colon cancer patients

Based on the average expression level of LINC01063 in colon cancer tissues (mean = 1.31), patients were stratified into two groups using this mean value as the cutoff: the low LINC01063 expression group (expression level ≤ 1.31), consisting of 57 patients (26 men and 31 women), and the high LINC01063 expression group, (expression level > 1.31), consisting of 59 patients (33 men and 26 women). Significant differences were observed between the high- and low-LINC01063 expression groups in terms of lymph node metastatic status (p = 0.005) and TNM stage (p = 0.046) (Table 1).

Analysis of the prognostic value of LINC01063 in colon cancer clinic

Investigations showed that patients in the LINC01063 high-expression group had significantly shorter overall survival compared to the low-expression group (log-rank p = 0.001, Fig. 2). In addition, according to Cox regression analysis, LINC01063 expression level (HR = 2.733, 95% CI 1.086–6.879, p = 0.033), TNM stage (HR = 2.361, 95% CI 1.054–5.291, p = 0.037), and Lymph node metastasis (HR = 2.311, 95% CI 1.044–5.118, p = 0.039) were identified as independent prognostic factors for colon cancer (Table 2).

Functional interaction of LINC01063 and miR-134-5p in colon cancer

The degree of LINC01063 expression in colon cancer cells (including COLO320, SW480, RKO, and HCT116 cells) was significantly higher than that in normal CCD841 cells (p < 0.0001, Fig. 3A), whereas the expression of miR-134-5p was on the contrary significantly down-regulated in comparison with normal CCD841 cells (p < 0.0001, Fig. 3B).

Bioinformatics prediction revealed the presence of miR-134-5p binding site in LINC01063 (Fig. 4A). Two sensitive cell lines, RKO and HCT116, were selected for dual luciferase reporter gene assay. The results showed that in both cell lines, overexpression of miR-134-5p significantly inhibited the luciferase activity of wild-type LINC01063 (p < 0.0001), while inhibition of miR-134-5p expression significantly promoted the luciferase activity of wild-type LINC01063 (p < 0.0001). However, neither the miR-134-5p mimic group nor the inhibition group had a significant effect on the luciferase activity of mutant LINC01063 (Fig. 4B, C).

Then, in the RKO cell line, knockdown of LINC01063 revealed a significant increase in miR-134-5p expression (p < 0.0001), whereas simultaneous knockdown of LINC01063 and inhibition of miR-134-5p resulted in a significant decrease in miR-134-5p expression (p < 0.0001), but both the si-LINC01063 group and the si-LINC01063 expression levels in the si-LINC01063 + miR-inhibitor group were not significantly different (p > 0.9999) (Fig. 4D). Similar results were observed in the HCT116 cell line (Fig. 4E). In summary, LINC01063 and miR-134-5p have a targeting relationship and negatively regulate the expression of miR-134-5p.

Effect of LINC01063/miR-134-5p axis on the function of colon cancer cells

Knockdown of LINC01063 significantly inhibited the proliferation of RKO (Fig. 5A) and HCT116 (Fig. 5B) cells, with a significant effect observed at 24, 48 and 72 h (p < 0.0001). This proliferation inhibition was partially reversed when concurrently transfected with miR-134-5p inhibitor, especially at 48 and 72 h. The cell proliferation rate in the si-LINC01063 + miR inhibitor group was significantly rebounded (p < 0.0001).

In the invasion assay (Fig. 5C), LINC01063 knockdown caused a substantial reduction in the number of RKO and HCT116 cells invaded (p < 0.0001). However, the addition of miR-134-5p inhibitor significantly rebounded the invasion ability of the cells (p < 0.0001).

In the apoptosis assay, knockdown of LINC01063 significantly increased the apoptosis rate (p < 0.0001) and inhibition of miR-134-5p significantly decreased the apoptosis rate (p < 0.0001) compared to the si-LINC01063 group, with similar results for both cell lines (Fig. 5D).

Analysis and validation of the regulatory relationship between miR-134-5p and KRAS

Six miR-134-5p potential target genes (ZMAT5, PPP1R7, KRAS, TCF21, CLEC1A, DPH2) were screened by bioinformatics analysis (Fig. 6A). Subsequent expression analysis showed that among these candidate target genes, KRAS and TCF21 were expressed at significantly higher levels in colon cancer tissues than in normal tissues adjacent to the cancer, Conversely, the expression levels of ZMAT5, PPP1R7, CLEC1A, and DPH2 did not exhibit any significant disparities. (Fig. 6B-G). In addition, Pearson correlation analysis revealed that miR-134-5p was significantly negatively correlated with the expression of KRAS (r = -0.6599, p < 0.0001; Fig. 6H), while TCF21 showed no correlation (Fig. 6I).

Functional validation of miR-134-5p interaction with KRAS

Expression analysis revealed that the expression level of KRAS was significantly higher in colon cancer cells (including COLO320, SW480, RKO, and HCT116 cells) than in normal cells (CCD841) (p < 0.0001, Fig. 7A). The outcomes of dual luciferase reporter gene assay indicated that overexpression of miR-134-5p significantly inhibited the luciferase activity of wild-type KRAS in RKO (Fig. 7B) and HCT116 (Fig. 7C) cell lines (p < 0.0001), whereas inhibition of miR-134-5p expression significantly enhanced the luciferase activity of wild-type KRAS (p < 0.0001). However, neither the miR-134-5p mimic group nor the inhibitor group had a significant effect on the luciferase activity of mutant KRAS.

Further mechanistic studies showed that knockdown of LINC01063 in RKO cells significantly reduced the expression level of KRAS (p < 0.0001), while further inhibition of miR-134-5p resulted in a significant rebound of KRAS expression (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 7D). A similar phenomenon was observed in the HCT116 cell line (Fig. 7E).

KRAS mediates the tumor-promoting effects of the LINC01063/miR-134-5p axis

To verify whether KRAS acts as the critical downstream effector, we performed rescue experiments by knocking down KRAS in cells with concurrent LINC01063 silencing and miR-134-5p inhibition (Figure S1A). Functional assays showed that silencing KRAS significantly reversed the rescue effects induced by miR-134-5p inhibition in LINC01063-depleted cells, including reduced cell proliferation (Figure S1B-C), decreased invasive capacity (Figure S1D), and increased apoptosis rate (Figure S1E).

Discussion

In recent years, lncRNAs have attracted much attention in the field of tumor research, and numerous studies have shown that they play a key role in tumorigenesis, progression, and prognosis [18, 19]. In the present study, we focused on the role of LINC01063 in colon cancer and found that LINC01063 was abnormally expressed in colon cancer tissues. It has been previously reported that LINC01063 is abnormally expressed in other tumors, like hepatocellular carcinoma [20], and breast cancer [21], and is closely associated with tumor malignant progression. It was hypothesized that the abnormally high expression of LINC01063 in colon cancer was associated with the malignant progression and prognosis of the tumor.

Clinical analysis showed that the expression level of LINC01063 was significantly correlated with TNM stage and lymph node metastasis (LNM). TNM stage is a key indicator for evaluating the severity, progression, and prognosis of colon cancer patients, which integrates tumor size, lymph node involvement, and distant metastatic status [22]. That metastasis to lymph nodes is also an important determinant of the clinical prognosis of colon cancer patients, and patients who develop LNM tend to have a poorer prognosis [23]. The correlation of LINC01063 with TNM stage and LNM strongly suggests its potential value during the advancement of colon cancer, which may be involved in local invasion and distant metastasis of the tumor.

In cell function experiments, the proliferation, migration, and invasion abilities of tumor cells are important indicators of tumor progression [24]. In this study, we found that knockdown of LINC01063 significantly inhibited the proliferation ability of colon cancer cells. Abnormal proliferation of tumor cells is the basis of tumor growth, which is directly related to tumor size and clinical staging [25]. Meanwhile, the knockdown of LINC01063 significantly attenuated the migration and invasion ability of cells. The migratory and invasive ability of cells is closely related to tumor metastasis, which is an important factor leading to poor prognosis in tumor patients [26, 27]. These results indicate that LINC01063 has an important regulatory role in the proliferation, migration, and invasion of colon cancer cells, further confirming its involvement in the malignant development of colon cancer.

In terms of molecular mechanism, it has been shown that LINC01063 adsorbs miR-5194 to upregulate the SOX12 signaling pathway in melanoma, which promotes tumor development [11]; and in colorectal cancer, H19 competitively binds miR-200a to activate the β-catenin pathway [12]. In the current investigation, we demonstrated that LINC01063 could adsorb miR-134-5p through the ceRNA mechanism, which in turn could deregulate the inhibitory effect of miR-134-5p on KRAS and activate the downstream pro-cancer signaling pathway. As a tumor suppressor, miR-134-5p exerts antitumor activity in a variety of cancers [16, 17]. Abnormal activation of KRAS, a core molecule of the RAS/MAPK pathway, promotes cell proliferation, inhibits apoptosis, and enhances metastasis [28]. The discovery of this regulatory axis further explains the mechanism of LINC01063 in colon cancer progression. Overall, this study revealed the clinical value and molecular mechanism of LINC01063 in colon cancer. LINC01063 is expected to become an important biomarker for the prognostic assessment of colon cancer, providing a new basis for the assessment of tumor malignancy.

KRAS serves as a central hub in the RAS/MAPK signaling cascade, functioning as a molecular switch that integrates extracellular signals to regulate critical cellular processes such as proliferation, survival, and metastasis [29, 30]. In colon cancer, KRAS mutations occur in 40–50% of cases, driving constitutive activation of downstream pathways that underlie tumor progression [31]. Our study reveals that KRAS is a direct target of miR-134-5p, and its expression is transcriptionally modulated by LINC01063 through a ceRNA mechanism. Functional rescue experiments demonstrate that KRAS knockdown abrogates the proliferative and invasive phenotypes induced by LINC01063 overexpression and miR-134-5p inhibition, confirming its essential role as a mediator of this regulatory axis. Mechanistically, activated KRAS propagates signals through two major downstream pathways: the ERK1/2-MAPK pathway [32] and the PI3K/AKT-mTOR pathway [33, 34]. The ERK1/2-MAPK pathway drives cell proliferation via transcription factor phosphorylation, suppresses apoptosis, and enhances invasion/metastasis by regulating matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), acting as a central driving pathway in tumorigenesis [35], while the PI3K/AKT pathway inhibits apoptosis and enhances cell survival by activating mTOR and suppressing pro-apoptotic proteins [36].

By uncovering this lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory network, our study provides a novel framework for understanding how KRAS signaling is dysregulated in colon cancer. Future studies will further investigate the specific activation status of ERK1/2 and PI3K/AKT pathways by measuring phosphorylated protein levels (e.g., p-ERK1/2, p-AKT) in LINC01063-modulated cells. Notably, due to current time and funding constraints, in vivo validation of the LINC01063/miR-134-5p/KRAS axis has not been performed; however, subsequent research will employ xenograft mouse models to assess tumor growth, metastasis, and pathway activation in vivo. Such studies will be critical to bridge the gap between in vitro findings and clinical relevance, particularly for evaluating the therapeutic potential of targeting this axis in combination with KRAS pathway inhibitors. Targeting KRAS or its upstream regulators (e.g., LINC01063 and miR-134-5p) may offer therapeutic opportunities to disrupt both the ERK1/2 and PI3K/AKT pathways, addressing a critical gap in current strategies for treating KRAS-mutant colorectal cancers.

This study found that LINC01063 is overexpressed in colon cancer tissues, correlates with TNM staging, lymph node metastasis and poor prognosis, and serves as an independent prognostic marker. It sponges miR-134-5p to relieve miR-134-5p-mediated inhibition of KRAS, thereby promoting cancer cell proliferation, invasion and suppressing apoptosis. Next steps include validating the axis’s in vivo function via animal models, elucidating activation mechanisms of downstream ERK1/2 and PI3K/AKT pathways, and exploring synergistic effects of targeted interventions. Clinically, LINC01063 could serve as a prognostic marker, and targeting this axis may offer a new therapeutic strategy for KRAS-mutant colon cancer.

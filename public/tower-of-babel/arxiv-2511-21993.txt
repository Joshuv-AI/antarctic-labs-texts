Shortest k k -Geodesics on Hyperbolic Surfaces
Changjie Chen

Abstract
. We study the relationship between the lengths of closed geodesics on hyperbolic surfaces and their topological complexity, measured by the self-intersection number. In particular, we provide explicit upper bounds for the length s k ​ ( X ) s_{k}(X) of a shortest closed geodesic with exactly k k self-intersections in terms of the length L 8 ​ ( X ) L_{\swabfamily 8}(X) of a shortest figure eight curve, improving Basmajian’s estimate. We analyze the geometry of a shortest figure eight curve and explicitly build families of words in π 1 ​ ( X ) \pi_{1}(X) whose geodesic representatives realize prescribed self-intersection numbers. As a consequence, we improve existing estimates on the maximal self-intersection number I k ​ ( X ) I_{k}(X) of shortest geodesics with at least k k self-intersections, reducing the asymptotic upper bound from 512 to 128. This provides a sharper quantitative connection between the geometry and combinatorial complexity of non-simple closed geodesics on hyperbolic surfaces.



1.
Introduction

The geometry of closed geodesics on hyperbolic surfaces has been a central topic in the study of surface geometry and dynamics. A classical problem asks how the length of a closed geodesic is related to its topological complexity, often measured through invariants such as its self-intersection number.

A fundamental object in this setting is the shortest figure eight curve, namely, a closed geodesic with a single geometric self-intersection. Buser
[
3
]
proved that any shortest non-simple closed geodesic must be a figure eight curve.

For
k\geq 0
, a
k
-geodesic
on a hyperbolic surface
X
is a closed geodesic with exactly
k
geometric self-intersections. Let
s_{k}(X)
be the length of a shortest
k
-geodesic on
X
, and let
L_{{\swabfamily 8}}(X)
be the length of a shortest figure eight curve. Basmajian initiated a comparison between these two quantities. For a compact hyperbolic surface
X
with (possibly empty) geodesic boundary, he proved in
[
1
]
that

s_{k}(X)\leq 3(\sqrt{k}+1)L_{{\swabfamily 8}}(X).

and later improved it in
[
2
]
to

s_{k}(X)\leq 2\sqrt{k+\tfrac{1}{4}}L_{{\swabfamily 8}}(X).

In this paper, we further refine the connection between the lengths of non-simple geodesics and the shortest figure eight curves. Our approach is constructive: we work within the pair of pants determined by a shortest figure eight curve, and explicitly build families of words in
\pi_{1}(X)
whose geodesic representatives realize prescribed self-intersection numbers.

Our first main result gives an improved upper bound for
s_{k}(X)
in terms of
L_{\swabfamily 8}
.

Theorem 1.1

(=Theorem
4.2
)
.

On a compact hyperbolic surface
X
with (possibly empty) geodesic boundary, for
k>0
, we have

s_{k}(X)\leq\left(\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)L_{\swabfamily 8}(X).

In particular,

s_{k}(X)<\left(\sqrt{k}+\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt[4]{k}\right)L_{\swabfamily 8}(X).

As an application, we revisit the quantity
I_{k}(X)
, the maximal self-intersection number of a shortest closed geodesic on
X
with at least
k
self-intersections. Erlandsson and Parlier
[
7
]
prove

I_{k}(X)\leq\left(32\sqrt{k+\frac{1}{4}}+1\right)\left(16\sqrt{k+\frac{1}{4}}+1\right).

They pose the conjecture that for any hyperbolic surface
X
, there is

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{I_{k}(X)}{k}=1,

and prove the conjecture when
X
has at least one cusp. For closed hyperbolic surfaces, their bound above implies that the limit is less than 512.

Using our improved bound for
s_{k}(X)
, we obtain a strengthened upper bound for
I_{k}(X)
and reduce the limit upper bound from 512 to 128.

Theorem 1.2

(=Theorem
4.4
)
.

On a complete hyperbolic surface
X
, we have

\displaystyle I_{k}(X)\leq

\displaystyle\left(8\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+12\sqrt{2}\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}-3\right)\left(16\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+24\sqrt{2}\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}-7\right).

Specifically,

I_{k}(X)<32\left(\left(4k+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+3\left(4k+1\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\right)^{2}.

Thus,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{I_{k}(X)}{k}\leq 128.

In Section 2, we describe the local geometry of a figure eight curve and introduce a family of words
w(m,n,j)
for each prescribed self-intersection number
k
. Section 3 establishes the precise self-intersection number of these words. Section 4 estimates the lengths of the resulting geodesics and proves the main theorems concerning
s_{k}(X)
and
I_{k}(X)
.

Construction of model words

In this section, we study the local geometry of a shortest figure eight curve on a hyperbolic surface and use it to construct

Definition 2.1
.

A
simple self-intersection
of a curve
\gamma
is a point at which
\gamma
passes through itself exactly twice, i.e. the intersection is formed by two distinct local subarcs. The
self-intersection number

i(\gamma)
of
\gamma
on a hyperbolic surface is the minimal number of self-intersections among all curves in the free homotopy class of
\gamma
with only simple self-intersections.

.

A
k
-geodesic

k
. Let

denote the length of a shortest
k
-geodesic on

, and let

denote the length of a shortest figure eight curve.

On the hyperbolic surface
X
, we choose a shortest figure eight curve
\gamma_{\swabfamily 8}
, which determines an embedded pair of pants
P\subset X
. We will construct
k
-geodesics inside

. Let
a,b
denote the two simple subloops of
\gamma_{\swabfamily 8}
generating
\pi_{1}(P)
, as illustrated in Figure

is exactly

3.
Calculating the self-intersection number

The problem of computing the self-intersection number of a word representing a closed curve on a surface has been studied in several papers, including
[
5
,
8
,
4
]
. We recall the setup used in
[
6
]
, which provides a combinatorial formula.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

w=s_{1}^{i_{1}}r_{1}^{j_{1}}\dots s_{n}^{i_{n}}r_{n}^{j_{n}},

be a cyclically reduced word with
s_{k}\in\{a,a^{-1}\}
and
r_{k}\in\{b,b^{-1}\}
. Let

x_{k}=s_{k}^{i_{k}}r_{k}^{j_{k}}\dots s_{n}^{i_{n}}r_{n}^{j_{n}}\dots s_{k-1}^{i_{k-1}}r_{k-1}^{j_{k-1}},

and

y_{k}=r_{k}^{j_{k}}s_{k+1}^{i_{k+1}}\dots r_{n}^{j_{n}}s_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots r_{k-1}^{j_{k-1}}s_{k}^{i_{k}}.

Fix a fundamental domain for the action of
\pi_{1}(P)
on the Poincaré disk
\mathbb{D}
. Let
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
denote the complete geodesics in
\mathbb{D}
joining the limit points of the infinite words
\cdots x_{k}x_{k}x_{k}\cdots
and
\cdots y_{k}y_{k}y_{k}\cdots
, respectively.

Definition 3.2
.

For
1\leq k\leq n
, define

C_{k}^{1}(w)=\{(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}):\alpha_{k}\cap\alpha_{l}\neq\emptyset,s_{k}^{i_{k}}\neq s_{l}^{i_{l}},k<l\leq n\},

C_{k}^{2}(w)=\{(\beta_{k},\alpha_{l}):\beta_{k}\cap\alpha_{l}\neq\emptyset,s_{k}^{i_{k}}\neq(s_{l}^{-1})^{i_{l}},k<l\leq n\},

D_{k}^{1}(w)=\{(\beta_{k},\beta_{l}):\beta_{k}\cap\beta_{l}\neq\emptyset,r_{k}^{j_{k}}\neq r_{l}^{j_{l}},k<l\leq n\}.

Further define

D_{1}^{2}(w)=\{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{l}):\alpha_{1}\cap\beta_{l}\neq\emptyset,1\leq l\leq n\},

and for
2\leq k\leq n
,

D_{k}^{2}(w)=\{(\alpha_{k},\beta_{l}):\alpha_{k}\cap\beta_{l}\neq\emptyset,r_{k-1}^{j_{k-1}}\neq(r_{l}^{-1})^{j_{l}},k\leq l\leq n\}.

The associated combinatorial quantity is

H(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\#C_{k}^{i}(w)+\#D_{k}^{i}(w)\right).

The following theorem gives an explicit relation between the self-intersection number and the function
H(w)
.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\gamma
be a closed geodesic on a pair of pants, associated to the cyclically reduced word
w=s_{1}^{i_{1}}r_{1}^{j_{1}}\dots s_{n}^{i_{n}}r_{n}^{j_{n}}
, then we have

i(\gamma)=H(w)+n|w|-2n^{2}-\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq n}(|i_{k}-i_{l}|+|j_{k}-j_{l}|).

Lemma 3.4
.

The self-intersection number of

w(m,n,j)=(ab)^{m}(a^{2}b)^{n}b^{j}

is

i(w(m,n,j))=(m+n)^{2}+n^{2}+m+n+j-1.

Proof.

We first rewrite
w(m,n,j)
in the standard form:

w(m,n,j)=a_{1}b_{1}\dots a_{m}b_{m}a_{m+1}^{2}b_{m+1}\dots a_{m+n-1}^{2}ba_{m+n}^{2}b_{m+n}^{j+1}.

Thus

|w(m,n,j)|=2m+3n+j,

and

\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq n}(|i_{k}-i_{l}|+|j_{k}-j_{l}|)=mn+(m+n-1)j.

Since
w(m,n,j)
contains no inverses of
a
or
b
, the conditions defining
C_{k}^{i}
and
D_{k}^{i}
depend only on comparing exponents. For example,

C_{k}^{1}(w)=\{(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}):\alpha_{k}\cap\alpha_{l}\neq\emptyset,i_{k}\neq i_{l},k<l\leq n\}.

It is easy to check (for example, see Figure
3
)

\displaystyle\#C_{k}^{1}=0,\text{ for }1\leq k\leq m+n,

\displaystyle\#D_{k}^{1}=1,\text{ for }1\leq k\leq m+n-1,

\displaystyle\#D_{m+n}^{1}=1,

\displaystyle\#C_{k}^{2}=m+n-k,\text{ for }1\leq k\leq m+n,

\displaystyle\#D_{1}^{2}=m+n,

\displaystyle\#D_{k}^{2}=m+n-k+1,\text{ for }2\leq k\leq m+n.

Figure 3.
For
1\leq k\leq m
and
1\leq l\leq n
, the infinite words
x_{k}x_{k}x_{k}\cdots
and
x_{m+l}x_{m+l}\cdots
, as well as their inverses, initially agree along the black arrows before diverging. Hence,
\alpha_{k}\cap\alpha_{m+l}\neq 0
.

Summing these contributions gives

H(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m+n}\left(\#C_{k}^{i}(w)+\#D_{k}^{i}(w)\right)=(m+n)^{2}+m+n-1,

and by Theorem
3.3
, we have

i(w(m,n,j))=(m+n)^{2}+n^{2}+m+n+j-1.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

Diop–Paris
[
6
]
show that for any word of the form

w=a^{i_{1}}b^{j_{1}}\dots a^{i_{n}}b^{j_{n}},

the quantity

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\#C_{k}^{i}(w)+\#D_{k}^{i}(w)\right)

satisfies

n-1\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\#C_{k}^{i}(w)+\#D_{k}^{i}(w)\right)\leq(n-1)^{2}.

Our family
w(m,n,j)
realizes this lower bound.

4.
Length estimates and proofs of the main theorems

Any integer
k>0
lies in a unique interval

[M(M+1),(M+1)(M+2))

for some integer
M\geq 0
. Set

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=k-M(M+1),

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=\lfloor\sqrt{a}\rfloor,

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=M-n,

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle=a-n^{2}+1.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
k>1
, the parameters
(m,n,j)
above define a word
w(m,n,j)
, and hence a closed geodesic
\gamma(m,n,j)
on
P
. If
n\geq j
, we have

l(\gamma(m,n,j))\leq\left(\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)L_{\swabfamily 8}(X).

In particular,

l(\gamma(m,n,j))<\left(\sqrt{k}+\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt[4]{k}\right)L_{\swabfamily 8}(X).

Proof.

Clearly
j>0
. Since
k<(M+1)(M+2)
, we have
a\leq 2M+1
and hence
n\leq\sqrt{2M+1}
. As
M
and
n
are integers, this implies that
M\geq n
except in the case
(M,n)=(0,1)
, which is ruled out by our assumption that
k>1
.

Note that

\sqrt{a}-1<\lfloor\sqrt{a}\rfloor\leq\sqrt{a},

which implies

j=a-n^{2}+1<2\sqrt{a}\leq 2\sqrt{2M+1}.

When
n\geq j
, we have

\displaystyle l(\gamma(m,n,j))

\displaystyle\leq l(w(m,n,j))

\displaystyle=(m+2n)l(a)+(m+n+j)l(b)

\displaystyle=(m+n+j)l(ab)+(n-j)l(a)

\displaystyle\leq(m+n+j)L_{\swabfamily 8}+\frac{n-j}{2}L_{\swabfamily 8}

\displaystyle=\frac{2m+3n+j}{2}L_{\swabfamily 8},

where

\displaystyle\frac{2m+3n+j}{2}

\displaystyle=M+\frac{n+j}{2}

\displaystyle\leq M+\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{M+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}-\frac{1}{2}.

Finally, it is easy to find

\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}-\frac{1}{2}<\sqrt{k}+\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt[4]{k},

which proves the lemma.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

On a compact hyperbolic surface
X
with (possibly empty) geodesic boundary, for
k>0

s_{k}(X)\leq\left(\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)L_{\swabfamily 8}(X).

In particular,

s_{k}(X)<\left(\sqrt{k}+\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt[4]{k}\right)L_{\swabfamily 8}(X).

Proof.

The case

k>1

(m,n,j)
as above.

If

If

∎

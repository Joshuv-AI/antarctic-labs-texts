Quantum Segre maps via cocycle twists
Yuri Bazlov, Runyang Chen

Abstract
A well-known noncommutative deformation 𝒜 𝐪 N \mathcal{A}^{N}_{\mathbf{q}} of the polynomial algebra 𝒜 N \mathcal{A}^{N} can be obtained as a twist of 𝒜 N \mathcal{A}^{N} by a cocycle on the grading semigroup. Of particular interest to us is an interpretation of A 𝐪 N A^{N}_{\mathbf{q}} as a quantum projective space. We outline a general method of cocycle twist quantization of tensor products and morphisms between algebras graded by monoids and use it to construct deformations of the classical Segre embeddings of projective spaces. The noncommutative Segre maps s n , m s_{n,m} , proposed by Arici, Galuppi and Gateva-Ivanova, arise as a particular case of our construction which corresponds to factorizable cocycles in the sense of Yamazaki.



1
Introduction

Very generally speaking,
quantization of an object
X
refers to some systematical way of replacing the commutative ring of functions
\mathcal{O}(X)

by a noncommutative ring
\mathcal{O}_{q}(X)
which depends on a parameter
q
, where one expects to recover the “classical”
\mathcal{O}(X)

by taking a limit as
q\to 1
. The process may involve quantizing functions from
X
to
Y
, symmetries or representations of
X
and so on.
One of the constructions which set the stage for modern quantum algebra is Manin’s quantum space
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathbb{F}^{n})
acted upon by the “quantum
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
”
[
Man87
]
, see also
[
Man18
]
.
The quantization of the affine
n
-space can in fact depend on
\binom{n}{2}
deformation parameters, assembled into a multiplicatively
antisymmetric
n\times n
deformation matrix
\mathbf{q}
.

Writing the
\mathbf{q}
-deformation of the polynomial
ring
\mathcal{A}^{N}=\mathbb{F}[x_{0},\dots,x_{N}]
as
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{q}}^{N}
, we view this graded algebra as a quantized homogeneous coordinate ring of a projective space. The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{q}}^{N}
has many properties expected of a “quantum
\mathbb{P}^{N}
”, surveyed for example in

[
SvdB01
]
and
[
ST01
]
; in particular, it is Artin-Schelter regular and has the correct Hilbert series.
We mention here that
\mathcal{A}^{N}_{\mathbf{q}}
serves as
a basis for further quantizations of the projective space in
[
MPS24
]
.

Recently, in
[
AGG22
]
Arici, Galuppi and Gateva-Ivanova constructed a quantization of the Segre embedding

\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}

where
N=(n+1)(m+1)-1
.
This quantization is given explicitly as an algebra homomorphism

s_{n,m}\colon\mathcal{A}^{N}_{\mathbf{g}}\to\mathcal{A}^{n}_{\mathbf{q}}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{m}_{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}
where the
\mathbf{q}
,
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
are arbitrary deformation matrices and
\mathbf{g}
is uniquely determined by
\mathbf{q}
and
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
. Here
\otimes
is the classical tensor product of algebras (where the two factors commute with each other).

In
[
AST91
]
Artin, Schelter and Tate hold
\mathcal{A}^{N}_{\mathbf{q}}
as an example of a deformation by a cocycle twist, and show how this leads to a cocycle twist construction
of the multiparameter quantum
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.
Cocycle deformations of algebras graded by abelian (semi)groups originate from physics and have been used as a method of quantization in various algebraic settings. The twist construction applies more generally to algebras where grading is replaced with a (co)action of a bialgebra, see Majid
[
Maj95
, §2.3]
and
[
GZ98
]
. Under some assumptions on the twisting
bialgebra, cocycle twist quantization has been shown to preserve various algebraic properties, for example, being semisimple
[
GKM12
]
and being Koszul
[
Jon24
]
.

In the present paper, we extend the cocycle twist to morphisms between graded algebras which are compatible with a given morphism between grading monoids. This allows us
to quantize the classical Segre map, twisting it by a cocycle
on the monoid
\mathbb{N}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{N}^{m+1}
.
Up to cohomology, such cocycles are parameterized by triples
(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{q}^{\prime},\alpha)
where
\alpha
, represented by a matrix of size

(n+1)\times(m+1)
over
\mathbb{F}^{\times}
, is a pairing between
\mathbb{N}^{n+1}
and
\mathbb{N}^{m+1}
, used to deform the tensor product
of the two quantum projective spaces.

We thus obtain noncommutative Segre maps of the form

\mathcal{A}^{N}_{\mathbf{g}}\to\mathcal{A}^{n}_{\mathbf{q}}\otimes_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{m}_{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}},

where
\otimes_{\alpha}
is the operation of twisted tensor product of two graded algebras.
The deformation matrix
\mathbf{g}
depends on
\mathbf{q}
,
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
and
\alpha
.
There are two extreme cases of this construction:

(a)

All entries of the matrix
\alpha
are
1
.
The resulting maps embed a “classical product of two quantum projective spaces”
in a quantum projective space, and are exactly the Segre map
constructed by Arici, Galuppi and Gateva-Ivanova in
[
AGG22
]
.

(b)

All entries of
\mathbf{q}
and
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
are
1
.
We have a new construction realising a “quantum product of two classical projective spaces” as a subvariety of a quantum projective space.

In the present paper, we do not investigate the kernel of
our general noncommutative Segre map (as done in
[
AGG22
]
in case (a)), leaving this to future work.

Organization of the paper

Twisting an associative
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
A
graded by a monoid
S

requires a
2
-cocycle on
S
with values in the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}^{\times}
of the field
\mathbb{F}
.
In Section
2

we collect the facts about
2
-cocycles on monoids that we need.
The machinery used to describe the second cohomology of groups comes from
Yamazaki
[
Yam64
]
and Artin, Schelter and Tate
[
AST91
]
. We need a version of this for monoids, so
we carefully derive the results in a way which avoids taking inverses and group extensions to show that

H^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{a},\mathbb{F}^{\times})\cong H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{a},\mathbb{F}^{\times})
and establish the Yamazaki factorization

H^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{a}\times\mathbb{N}^{b},\mathbb{F}^{\times})\cong H^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{a},\mathbb{F}^{\times})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{b},\mathbb{F}^{\times})\times P(\mathbb{N}^{a},\mathbb{N}^{b},\mathbb{F}^{\times}),

where the last factor is the group of bimultiplicative

\mathbb{F}^{\times}
-valued pairings between the monoids
\mathbb{N}^{a}
and
\mathbb{N}^{b}
.

Section
3
introduces the general technique of
twisting an associative algebra by a cocycle on the grading monoid.
We achieve two principal goals: twisting of a morphism between
two algebras graded by different monoids (subject to a compatibility condition), and constructing a twisted tensor product of two graded algebras. The final section constructs the quantized Segre maps as an application of the above technique.

2
The second cohomology of the free commutative monoid
\mathbb{N}^{n}

2.1
The second cohomology of a monoid

Let
S
be a multiplicative
monoid with identity
e
.
We let
\Gamma
be an abelian group, written multiplicatively,
where
\frac{x}{y}
means
xy^{-1}
, and the identity is denoted by
1
.
The main application later on will be to the case where
\Gamma=\mathbb{F}^{\times}
is the multiplicative group of a field.
The action of
S
on
\Gamma
will be trivial throughout.

Definition 2.1
.

A
2-cocycle,
or simply a
cocycle
on a
monoid

S
with values in an abelian group
\Gamma
is a map
\mu\colon S\times S\to\Gamma
such that

\mu(x,yz)\mu(y,z)=\mu(x,y)\mu(xy,z),\quad\mu(x,e)=\mu(e,x)=1

(2.1)

for all
x,y,z\in S
.
For any function
h\colon S\to\Gamma
with
h(e)=1
, the
coboundary

\delta h\colon S\times S\to\Gamma
of
h
is
defined by

\delta h(x,y)=\dfrac{h(x)h(y)}{h(xy)}
and is easily seen to be a cocycle.
If
Z^{2}(S,\Gamma)
is the abelian group of cocycles under pointwise multiplication, and
B^{2}(S,\Gamma)
is the group of coboundaries,
the
second cohomology group
of
S
with coefficients in
\Gamma
is the factorgroup
H^{2}(S,\Gamma)=Z^{2}(S,\Gamma)/B^{2}(S,\Gamma)
,
whose elements are termed
cohomology classes.

Two cocycles are
cohomologous
if they belong to the same cohomology class.

If
S
and
T
are monoids,
we identify
S
with the submonoid
S\times\{e_{T}\}
of the direct product
S\times T
via
s\mapsto(s,e_{T})
, and likewise
T
is identified with
\{e_{S}\}\times T
.
Thus, every cocycle
\mu
on
S\times T
restricts to a cocycle on
S
,
written
\mu|_{S}
. The restriction map

Z^{2}(S\times T,\Gamma)\to Z^{2}(S,\Gamma)\times Z^{2}(T,\Gamma),\quad\mu\mapsto(\mu|_{S},\mu|_{T}),

(2.2)

sends coboundaries to pairs of coboundaries,
resulting in a well-defined
restriction homomorphism

H^{2}(S\times T,\Gamma)\to H^{2}(S,\Gamma)\times H^{2}(T,\Gamma)

(2.3)

on the cohomology. The map (
2.2
), hence also the map (
2.3
), is surjective: one possible preimage of a pair
(\nu,\xi)
of cocycles is
\nu\times\xi

defined as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

The
direct product
of cocycles
\nu\in Z^{2}(S,\Gamma)
and

\xi\in Z^{2}(T,\Gamma)
is the function

\nu\times\xi\colon(S\times T)\times(S\times T)\to\Gamma,\quad(\nu\times\xi)((s,t),(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}))=\nu(s,s^{\prime})\xi(t,t^{\prime}),

easily seen to be a cocycle.
A cocycle
\mu\in Z^{2}(S\times T,\Gamma)
is
factorizable

if
\mu
is cohomologous to a cocycle of the form
\nu\times\xi
.

2.2
The Yamazaki factorization of a cocycle

Denote by
P(S,T,\Gamma)
the set
of all maps
\alpha\colon S\times T\to\Gamma
which are

bimultiplicative pairings,
that is,

\alpha(ss^{\prime},t)=\alpha(s,t)\alpha(s^{\prime},t),\quad\alpha(s,tt^{\prime})=\alpha(s,t)\alpha(s,t^{\prime})

(2.4)

for all
s,s^{\prime}\in S
and
t,t^{\prime}\in T
.
Clearly,
P(S,T,\Gamma)
is a group under pointwise multiplication.

Proposition 2.3
.

View
S
and
T
as submonoids of
S\times T
.
The
Yamazaki factorization map

Y\colon Z^{2}(S\times T,\Gamma)\to Z^{2}(S,\Gamma)\times Z^{2}(T,\Gamma)\times P(S,T,\Gamma),\quad Y(\mu)=(\mu|_{S},\mu|_{T},\alpha_{\mu})

(2.5)

where
\alpha_{\mu}(s,t)=\dfrac{\mu(s,t)}{\mu(t,s)}

for
s\in S
and
t\in T
, is a surjective homomorphism of groups.

Proof.

To check that
\alpha=\alpha_{\mu}
is bimultiplicative, write

\alpha(ss^{\prime},t)=\mu(ss^{\prime},t)/\mu(t,ss^{\prime})
as

\frac{\mu(ss^{\prime},t)\mu(s,s^{\prime})}{\mu(t,ss^{\prime})\mu(s,s^{\prime})}=\frac{\mu(s,s^{\prime}t)\mu(s^{\prime},t)}{\mu(ts,s^{\prime})\mu(t,s)}=\frac{\mu(s,s^{\prime}t)\mu(s^{\prime},t)}{\mu(st,s^{\prime})\mu(t,s)}=\frac{\mu(s,s^{\prime}t)\mu(s^{\prime},t)\mu(s,t)}{\mu(s,ts^{\prime})\mu(t,s^{\prime})\mu(t,s)},

by the cocycle equation and the fact that
st=ts
in
S\times T
.
But also
ts^{\prime}=s^{\prime}t
, so after cancellation we get

\alpha(s,t)\alpha(s^{\prime},t)
, proving that
\alpha
is multiplicative in the left argument. Multiplicativity in the right argument is similar.

It is now clear that
Y
is a homomorphism.
Its image contains
Z^{2}(S,\Gamma)\times Z^{2}(T,\Gamma)\times\{1\}
by surjectivity of (
2.2
). The image of
Y
also contains
(1,1,\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in P(S,T,\Gamma)
: indeed, define

\sigma((s,t),(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}))=\alpha(s,t^{\prime}),

(2.6)

and observe that
\sigma
is bimultiplicative and as such, is easily seen to satisfy cocycle equation (
2.1
). We have
Y(\sigma)=(1,1,\alpha)
. Surjectivity of
Y
follows.
∎

The map (
2.5
) was introduced, and the next result proved, in
[
Yam64
, §2]
under the assumption that
S
and
T
are groups. See also
[
Kar93
, Theorem 2.3]
. Here is the monoid version of the Yamazaki theorem
[
Yam64
, Theoem 2.1]
:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
S
and
T
be monoids and
\Gamma
be an abelian group.
The Yamazaki factorization map (
2.5
) induces a bijective homomorphism

H^{2}(S\times T,\Gamma)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{2}(S,\Gamma)\times H^{2}(T,\Gamma)\times P(S,T,\Gamma).

(2.7)

Proof.

If
\mu
is a coboundary on
S\times T
, then
\mu|_{S}

and
\mu|_{T}
are coboundaries, and
\mu
is symmetric in its arguments so
\alpha_{\mu}=1
. This shows that (
2.7
) is a well-defined group homomorphism. The map is surjective by Proposition
2.3
.

To prove injectivity, we assume that a cocycle

\mu
on
S\times T
restricts to coboundaries

\delta f
and
\delta g
on
S
and
T
, respectively,
and
\alpha_{\mu}=1
. We need to show that
\mu
is a coboundary.
Dividing
\mu
by

\delta f\times\delta g
, which is a coboundary on
S\times T
,
we may assume that
\mu|_{S}=1
and
\mu|_{T}=1
.

Define the function
h\colon S\times T\to\Gamma
by
h((s,t))=1/\mu((s,e_{T}),(e_{S},t))
. Injectivity of (
2.7
) will be proved if we show that
\mu=\delta h
. In the calculation, we write a pair
(s,t)\in S\times T
as
st
,

(s,e_{T})
as
s
and
(e_{S},t)
as
t
:

\mu(st,s^{\prime}t^{\prime})=\frac{\mu(s,s^{\prime}tt^{\prime})\mu(t,s^{\prime}t^{\prime})}{\mu(s,t)}=\frac{\mu(ss^{\prime},tt^{\prime})\mu(s,s^{\prime})\mu(t,s^{\prime}t^{\prime})}{\mu(s,t)\mu(s^{\prime},tt^{\prime})}=\frac{\mu(ss^{\prime},tt^{\prime})\mu(tt^{\prime},s^{\prime})\mu(t,t^{\prime})}{\mu(s,t)\mu(s^{\prime},tt^{\prime})\mu(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})}.

We have used the cocycle equation and the assumption
\mu(s,s^{\prime})=1
.
Yet
\mu(t,t^{\prime})
is also
1
, and
\mu(tt^{\prime},s^{\prime})
equals
\mu(s^{\prime},tt^{\prime})

because
\alpha_{\mu}(s^{\prime},tt^{\prime})=1
. We obtain
\dfrac{\mu(ss^{\prime},tt^{\prime})}{\mu(s,t)\mu(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})}
which is
\delta h((ss^{\prime},tt^{\prime}))
, as claimed.
∎

We can now obtain a criterion for a cocycle on
S\times T
to be factorizable.

Corollary 2.5
.

A cocycle
\mu\in Z^{2}(S\times T,\Gamma)
is factorizable, if, and only if, the third component
\alpha_{\mu}
of the Yamazaki factorization
Y(\mu)
is the trivial pairing
1
.

Proof.

If
\mu
is factorizable, that is,
\mu
belongs to the same cohomology
class as
\nu\times\xi
where
\nu\in Z^{2}(S,\Gamma)
and
\xi\in Z^{2}(T,\Gamma)
, then
\alpha_{\mu}=\alpha_{\nu\times\xi}
because
\alpha_{\mu}
depends only on the cohomology class of
\mu
.
If
s\in S
and
t\in T
, we have
\alpha_{\nu\times\xi}(s,t)=\dfrac{(\nu\times\xi)((s,e_{T}),(e_{S},t))}{(\nu\times\xi)((e_{S},t),(s,e_{T}))}
which is
1
since
\nu(s,e_{S})=\nu(e_{S},s)=1
and
\xi(e_{T},t)=\xi(t,e_{T})=1
.

Vice versa, if
\alpha_{\mu}=1
then
Y(\mu)=Y(\mu|_{S}\times\mu|_{T})
and so by injectivity of the map (
2.7
) in Theorem
2.4
,
\mu
is cohomologous to
\mu|_{S}\times\mu|_{T}
.
∎

2.3
Abelian cocycles on a commutative monoid

We can obtain more information about the
group
H^{2}(S,\Gamma)
in the case where the monoid
S
is commutative.
Define the
antisymmetrization map

\beta
by

\mu\in Z^{2}(S,\Gamma)\mapsto\beta_{\mu},\quad\beta_{\mu}(x,y)=\frac{\mu(x,y)}{\mu(y,x)}.

The same argument as in the proof of Proposition
2.3
(this time,
st=ts
for all
s,t\in S
by commutativity of
S
and not by the property of a direct product) shows that
\beta
is a bimultiplicative pairing between
S
and itself. Moreover,
\beta
is

antisymmetric,
meaning

\beta_{\mu}(x,x)=1,\quad\beta_{\mu}(x,y)\beta_{\mu}(y,x)=1.

(2.8)

Definition 2.6
.

If
S
is an commutative monoid and
\Gamma
is an abelian group,

\mu\in Z^{2}(S,\Gamma)
is an
abelian cocycle

if
\mu(x,y)=\mu(y,x)
for all
x,y\in S
.

A cohomology class
c
is abelian if
c
contains an abelian cocycle. Following
[
Yam64
, §2.3]
, we denote the subgroup of
abelian cohomology classes
in
H^{2}(S,\Gamma)
by
H^{2}_{\text{\rm abel}}(S,\Gamma)
, and the group of
antisymmetric bimultiplicative pairings
between
S
and
S
by

P_{\text{\rm a.s.}}(S,\Gamma)
.

A coboundary is clearly an abelian cocycle, hence
c\in H^{2}_{\text{\rm abel}}(S,\Gamma)
means that all cocycles in
c
are abelian. As the antisymmetrization map
\beta
sends
B^{2}(S,\Gamma)
to
\{1\}
,
\beta
is well defined on
H^{2}(S,\Gamma)
, and the sequence

H^{2}_{\text{\rm abel}}(S,\Gamma)\hookrightarrow H^{2}(S,\Gamma)\xrightarrow{\beta}P_{\text{\rm a.s.}}(S,\Gamma)

(2.9)

is exact in the middle term for any commutative monoid
S
and abelian group
\Gamma
. The group
H^{2}_{\text{\rm abel}}
is well-behaved with respect to the direct product of monoids:

Lemma 2.7
.

If
S
and
T
are commutative monoids and
\Gamma
is an abelian group, the restriction map (
2.3
) induces an
isomorphism

H^{2}_{\text{\rm abel}}(S\times T,\Gamma)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{2}_{\text{\rm abel}}(S,\Gamma)\times H^{2}_{\text{\rm abel}}(T,\Gamma).

(2.10)

Proof.

An abelian cocycle on
S\times T
, when restricted onto
S
and onto
T
, gives a pair of abelian cocycles, so the map (
2.10
) is well defined.
It is surjective because if

\nu\in Z^{2}(S,\Gamma)
and
\xi\in Z^{2}(T,\Gamma)
are both abelian,
their direct product
\nu\times\xi
is abelian.
Injectivity follows from Theorem
2.4
, taking into account that
\alpha_{\mu}=1
if
\mu
is an abelian cocycle on
S\times T
.
∎

2.4
The second cohomology of
\mathbb{N}^{n}
and multiplicatively antisymmetric matrices

Let
\mathbb{N}^{n}
denote the free abelian monoid of rank
n
, isomorphic to the direct product of
n
copies of
\mathbb{N}=\mathbb{N}^{1}
. In this section,
we write
\mathbb{N}^{n}
multiplicatively, with
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
as generators:

\mathbb{N}^{n}=\{g_{1}^{k_{1}}g_{2}^{k_{2}}\dots g_{n}^{k_{n}}:k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}.

Our aim is to obtain an explicit description of the second cohomology group
H^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{n},\Gamma)
where
\Gamma
is an abelian group. We begin with

Lemma 2.8
.

H^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{1},\Gamma)=\{1\}
and

H^{2}_{\text{\rm abel}}(\mathbb{N}^{n},\Gamma)=\{1\}
for all
n\geq 1
and for all
abelian groups
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Let
\mu\in Z^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{1},\Gamma)
and let
g
be the generator of
\mathbb{N}^{1}
.
Define
h\colon\mathbb{N}^{1}\to\Gamma
by the rules
h(e)=h(g)=1

and
\mu(g,g^{p})=h(g^{p})/h(g^{p+1})
for
p=1,2,\dots

Then the equation

\mu(g^{p},g^{q})=\frac{h(g^{p})h(g^{q})}{h(g^{p+q})}

(2.11)

holds in the case
p=1
for all
q\geq 1
.
The cocycle equation affords the inductive step from
p
to
p+1
:

\mu(g^{p+1},g^{q})=\mu(g^{p},g^{q+1})\frac{\mu(g,g^{q})}{\mu(g^{p},g)}=\frac{h(g^{p})h(g^{q+1})}{h(g^{p+q+1})}\frac{1\cdot h(g^{q})}{h(g^{q+1})}\frac{h(g^{p+1})}{h(g^{p})\cdot 1}=\frac{h(g^{p+1})h(g^{q})}{h(g^{p+q+1})},

proving that (
2.11
) holds for all
p\geq 1
, so that

\mu
is a coboundary.

We have
H^{2}_{\text{\rm abel}}(\mathbb{N}^{1},\Gamma)\subseteq H^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{1},\Gamma)=\{1\}
, so
by Lemma
2.7
,

H^{2}_{\text{\rm abel}}(\mathbb{N}^{n},\Gamma)=\{1\}
for all
n
.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

For any abelian group
\Gamma
,
the map
\beta\colon H^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{n},\Gamma)\to P_{\text{\rm a.s.}}(\mathbb{N}^{n},\Gamma)
, given by (
2.9
), is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Lemma
2.7
and (
2.9
) imply that
\beta
has trivial kernel and is injective.
Now, if
b
is a
\Gamma
-valued antisymmetric bimultiplicative pairing on
\mathbb{N}^{n}
, define
\mu\colon\mathbb{N}^{n}\times\mathbb{N}^{n}\to\Gamma
on the generators
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
of
\mathbb{N}^{n}
by

\mu(g_{i},g_{j})=b(g_{i},g_{j})
if
i<j
,
\mu(g_{i},g_{j})=1
if
i\geq j
. Extend
\mu
to be a bimultiplicative pairing;
\mu
may not be
antisymmetric, but bimultiplicativity implies that
\mu
is a cocycle. Clearly
\beta_{\mu}=b
, proving surjectivity of
\beta
.
∎

Since any bimultiplicative pairing
b
on
\mathbb{N}^{n}
is uniquely determined by the values
q_{ij}:=b(g_{i},g_{j})
,
Proposition
2.9
and its proof lead us to the following

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
M_{\text{\rm a.s.}}^{n}(\Gamma)
denote the set of
n\times n

matrices
\mathbf{q}
with entries in
\Gamma
which are
multiplicatively antisymmetric,
meaning that

q_{ii}=1
and
q_{ij}q_{ji}=1
for all
i,j=1,\dots,n
.
There is a bijection

M_{\text{\rm a.s.}}^{n}(\Gamma)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{n},\Gamma),

sending a matrix
\mathbf{q}=(q_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
to the
cohomology class which contains a unique cocycle
\mu_{\mathbf{q}}
such that

\mu_{\mathbf{q}}(g_{i},g_{j})=\begin{cases}q_{ij}&\text{if }i<j,\\
1&\text{if }i\geq j,\end{cases}\qquad\mu_{\mathbf{q}}\text{ is bimultiplicative. \qed}

We now interpret the Yamazaki bijection (
2.7
) for the direct product
\mathbb{N}^{a}\times\mathbb{N}^{b}
in terms of matrices.

Let
\Gamma
be an abelian group and let
a,b\geq 1
. Recall the
Yamazaki map
\mu\mapsto(\mu|_{\mathbb{N}^{a}},\mu|_{\mathbb{N}^{b}},\alpha_{\mu})
which by Theorem
2.4
induces an isomorphism on cohomology. In terms of matrices, this map corresponds to writing a multiplicatively antisymmetric
matrix
\mathbf{q}
of size
(a+b)\times(a+b)
with entries in
\Gamma
as a triple of matrices:

\mathbf{q}\quad\mapsto\quad((q_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq a},\ (q_{ij})_{a+1\leq i,j\leq a+b},(q_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq a,\ a+1\leq j\leq a+b}),

of size
a\times a
,
b\times b
and
a\times b
, respectively,
where the first two matrices are multiplicatively antisymmetric,
and the third matrix is arbitrary.
Note:

•

the set of all
a\times b
matrices with entries in
\Gamma
is in bijection with
P(\mathbb{N}^{a},\mathbb{N}^{b},\Gamma)
, because a pairing
is defined arbitrarily on generators;

•

multiplicatively antisymmetric matrices
\mathbf{q}
are in one-to-one correspondence with triples of matrices with the given properties.

Corollary
2.5
implies the following criterion.

Corollary 2.11
.

A cocycle
\mu
on
\mathbb{N}^{a}\times\mathbb{N}^{b}
is cohomologous to a direct product of a cocycle on
\mathbb{N}^{a}
and a cocycle on
\mathbb{N}^{b}
if, and only if,
the pairing
\alpha_{\mu}
between
\mathbb{N}^{a}
and
\mathbb{N}^{b}
is identically
1
.
Equivalently,
\mu
corresponds to a multiplicatively antisymmetric matrix
\mathbf{q}
with the upper right
a\times b
block filled by
1
:
q_{ij}=1
whenever
1\leq i\leq a
and
a+1\leq j\leq a+b
.

3
Cocycle twists of graded algebras

3.1
A cocycle twist of a graded associative algebra

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and
S
be a monoid. The category of
S
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebras consists of unital associative algebras
A
such that

A=\bigoplus_{s\in S}A_{s}
with
A_{s}A_{t}\subseteq A_{st}
for all
s,t\in S
, and the identity element
1_{A}
of
A
lies in
A_{e}

where
e
is the identity of
S
.

Definition 3.1
.

[
BG02
, Definition I.12.16]

Let
S
be a monoid and
A
an
S
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra. Fix a 2-cocyle
\mu\in Z^{2}(S,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
. Let
A^{\prime}
be a copy of
A
, viewed as
S
-graded vector space over
\mathbb{F}
having natural
S
-graded vector space isomorphism
a\mapsto a^{\prime}
. Define a product on
A^{\prime}
by

a^{\prime}*b^{\prime}=\mu(x,y)(ab)^{\prime}

(3.1)

for homogeneous elements
a\in A_{x}
and
b\in A_{y}
, and extend by linearity, where
ab
is the product of
a
and
b
in
A
. Then
A^{\prime}
is an
S
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra, called the
twist
of
A
by
\mu
; the map
a\mapsto a^{\prime}
is called the
twist map
.

We denote the twist of
A
by
\mu
as
A_{\mu}
and think of
A_{\mu}
as having the same elements as
A
but a different product.
We write the product on
A_{\mu}
as
*
, or for emphasis
as
*_{\mu}
.
Note that cocycle equation (
2.1
) guarantees associativity and unitality of
A_{\mu}
, where the identity element is
1^{\prime}_{A}
. Moreover,
A_{\mu}
remains an
S
-graded algebra.
We now note that the isomorphism class of the algebra
A_{\mu}

depends only on the cohomology class of
\mu
in
H^{2}(S,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A
be an
S
-graded algebra,
\mu,\nu\in Z^{2}(S,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
, and consider the twists
A_{\mu}
(with canonical graded space isomorphism
a\in A\mapsto a^{\prime}\in A_{\mu}
) and
A_{\nu}
(with
a\in A\mapsto a^{\prime\prime}\in A_{\nu}
).
If the cocycles
\mu,\nu
are cohomologous, that is
\mu=(\delta h)\nu
with
h\colon S\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
,
then there is an isomorphism
\varphi_{h}\colon A_{\mu}\to A_{\nu}
of
S
-graded algebras,
given on homogeneous
a\in A_{s}
by
\varphi_{h}(a^{\prime})=h(s)a^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\varphi_{h}
is an isomorphism of graded vector spaces as
h(s)
are invertible elements of
\mathbb{F}
.
For
a\in A_{s}
,
b\in A_{t}

we have
\varphi_{h}(a^{\prime}*_{\mu}b^{\prime})=\mu(s,t)\varphi_{h}((ab)^{\prime})=\mu(s,t)h(st)(ab)^{\prime\prime}
.
On the other hand,
\varphi_{h}(a^{\prime})*_{\nu}\varphi_{h}(b^{\prime})=h(s)a^{\prime\prime}*_{\nu}h(t)b^{\prime\prime}=\nu(s,t)h(s)h(t)(ab)^{\prime\prime}
. This is equal to
\varphi_{h}(a^{\prime}*_{\mu}b^{\prime})
as
\mu=(\delta h)\nu
.
∎

It turns out that the morphisms between twists of two
S
-graded algebras by
\mu
are the same, as a set, as morphisms between the untwisted algebras:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A
,
B
be
\mathbb{F}
-algebras graded by a monoid
S
, and let
\varphi\colon A\to B
be a morphism of
S
-graded algebras, that is to say
\varphi(A_{s})\subseteq B_{s}
for all
s\in S
. Assume
\mu\in Z^{2}(S,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
. Then the map
\varphi^{\prime}\colon A_{\mu}\to B_{\mu}
, defined by
\varphi^{\prime}(a^{\prime})=(\varphi(a))^{\prime}
, is also a morphism of algebras.

Proof.

Consider
S
-homogeneous elements
a_{1}\in A_{s_{1}}
,
a_{2}\in A_{s_{2}}
with
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
. One has

\varphi^{\prime}(a_{1}^{\prime}*a_{2}^{\prime})=\varphi^{\prime}(\mu(s_{1},s_{2})(a_{1}a_{2})^{\prime})=\mu(s_{1},s_{2})\varphi(a_{1}a_{2})^{\prime}
. Since
\varphi
intertwines the untwisted products on
A
and
B
, this is
\mu(s_{1},s_{2})\left(\varphi(a_{1})\varphi(a_{2})\right)^{\prime}=\left(\varphi(a_{1})\right)^{\prime}*\left(\varphi(a_{2})\right)^{\prime}=\varphi^{\prime}(a_{1}^{\prime})*\varphi^{\prime}(a_{2}^{\prime})

because
\varphi^{\prime}(a_{1}^{\prime})\in B_{s_{1}}
and
\varphi^{\prime}(a_{2}^{\prime})\in B_{s_{2}}
.
We have shown that
\varphi^{\prime}(a_{1}^{\prime}*a_{2}^{\prime})=\varphi^{\prime}(a_{1}^{\prime})*\varphi^{\prime}(a_{2}^{\prime})

for homogeneous
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
;
by linearity, the same is true for arbitrary
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.
∎

3.2
Morphisms between twists of algebras graded by different monoids

We extend Lemma
3.3
to the case where each one of the algebras
A
and
B
has its own grading monoid. There must be given a morphism between the grading monoids, and the twisting cocycle transforms as follows:

Definition 3.4
.

Let
S\xrightarrow{f}T
be a morphism between monoids
S
and
T
.
If
\mu\colon T\times T\to\Gamma
is a cocycle on
T
, the
pullback
of
\mu
along
f
is

\mu^{f}\colon S\times S\to\Gamma
defined by
\mu^{f}(s,s^{\prime})=\mu(f(s),f(s^{\prime}))
.

It is easy to see that
\mu^{f}
is a cocycle on
S
.
The following result is proved by inserting
f
in the appropriate places in the proof of Lemma
3.3
:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
S\xrightarrow{f}T
be a mophism of monoids,
A
be an
S
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra and
B
be a
T
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra.
Suppose that
\varphi\colon A\to B
is a morphism of graded algebras
compatible with
f
, that is,

\varphi(A_{s})\subseteq B_{f(s)}

for all
s\in S
. Then for any cocycle
\mu\in Z^{2}(T,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
, the map

\varphi^{\prime}\colon A_{\mu^{f}}\rightarrow B_{\mu},

which coincides with
\varphi
on the underlying vector spaces of
A_{\mu^{f}}
and
B_{\mu}
, is a morphism of algebras.∎

3.3
Twisted tensor product of graded algebras

Consider two monoids
S
and
T
. Let
B
be an
S
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra and
C
be a
T
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra. The space
B\otimes C
,
with the classical multiplication given by

(b\otimes c)(b^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime})=bb^{\prime}\otimes cc^{\prime}
for all
b,b^{\prime}\in B
and

c,c^{\prime}\in C
and the identity
1_{B\otimes C}=1_{B}\otimes 1_{C}
,
is a unital associative algebra which is
S\times T
-graded via

B\otimes C=\bigoplus_{(s,t)\in S\times T}(B\otimes C)_{(s,t)},

where
(B\otimes C)_{(s,t)}=B_{s}\otimes C_{t}
.
By identifying
B\cong B\otimes\{1_{C}\}
and
C\cong\{1_{B}\}\otimes C
,
we can consider
B
and
C
as subalgebras of
B\otimes C
.
This
classical tensor product
of two graded algebras is the simplest example of the following graded version of
[
Maj02
, Definition 21.3]
:

Definition 3.6
.

A
graded algebra factorization
is an graded algebra

X
and two graded subalgebras
B,C\subseteq X
such that the product map
X\otimes X\to X
, restricted onto
B\otimes C
,
gives an isomorphism
B\otimes C\xrightarrow{\sim}X
of vector spaces.

We now introduce a family of graded algebra factorizations into
B
and
C
parameterized by pairings
\alpha\in P(S,T,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
. The case
\alpha=1
corresponds to the classical tensor product
B\otimes C
:

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
B
be an
S
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra,
C
be a
T
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra, and
\alpha\in P(S,T,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
be a pairing.
There exists unique
S\times T
-graded algebra product
*

on the underlying vector space
B\otimes C
,
such that
B\cong B\times\{1_{C}\}
and
C\cong\{1_{B}\}\times C
are
subalgebras, and
c*b=\alpha(s,t)b\otimes c
for all
b\in B_{s}

and
c\in C_{t}
with
s\in S
,
t\in T
.

Proof.

Clearly, if the associative product
*
exists, it is unique as

(b\otimes c)*(b^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime})
must be given by

\alpha(s,t)bb^{\prime}\otimes cc^{\prime}
whenever
b^{\prime}\in B_{s}
and
c\in C_{t}
.

To show existence of
*
, define, in a slight modification of (
2.6
), the cocycle
\tau\in Z^{2}(S\times T,\mathbb{F}^{\times})

by

\tau((s,t),(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}))=\alpha(s^{\prime},t).

Consider the twist
(B\otimes C)_{\tau}
of the classical tensor product algerba
B\otimes C
. The twisted product
*=*_{\tau}
on the underlying vector space
B\otimes C
has the
required properties; in particular, (
3.1
)
implies that
c*b=\tau((e_{S},t),(s,e_{S}))b\otimes c=\alpha(s,t)b\otimes c
for
b\in B_{s}
and
c\in C_{t}
, as required.
∎

Remark
.

The cocycle
\tau\in Z^{2}(S\times T,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
used in the proof of the Lemma has Yamazaki factorization
Y(\tau)=(1,1,\frac{1}{\alpha})
in
Z^{2}(S,\mathbb{F}^{\times})\times Z^{2}(T,\mathbb{F}^{\times})\times P(S,T,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
.
The reason for inverting
\alpha
is purely technical, namely,

\alpha
appears in the equation for
c*b
and not
b*c
.

Definition 3.8
.

Given an
S
-graded algebra
B
, a
T
-graded algebra
C
and a pairing

\alpha\in P(S,T,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
, the algebra factorization described in
Lemma
3.7
is called the
\alpha
-twisted tensor product
of
B
and
C
and
denoted
B\otimes_{\alpha}C
.

It turns out that all twists of the classical tensor product
B\otimes C
by cocycles
on
S\times T
can be written as twisted tensor products of a twist of
B
and a twist of
C
, as follows.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
B
be an
S
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra,
C
be a
T
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra, and
\mu\in Z^{2}(S\times T,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
.
Suppose that the Yamazaki factorization (
2.5
) of
\mu

is
Y(\mu)=(\nu,\xi,1/\alpha)
where
\nu\in Z^{2}(S,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
,

\xi\in Z^{2}(T,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
and
\alpha\in P(S,T,\mathbb{F}^{\times})
.
Then the
twisted algebra
(B\otimes C)_{\mu}
is isomorphic to the
twisted tensor product

B_{\nu}\otimes_{\alpha}C_{\xi}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.4
,
\mu
is cohomologous to the cocycle

\mu_{1}=(\nu\times\xi)\tau
where
Y(\tau)=(1,1,1/\alpha)
,
and so by Lemma
3.2
,

(B\otimes C)_{\mu}
is isomorphic to
(B\otimes C)_{\mu_{1}}
.
It is easy to observe that
(B\otimes C)_{\nu\times\xi}

is the classical tensor product
B_{\nu}\otimes C_{\xi}
, and so

(B\otimes C)_{(\nu\times\xi)\tau}\cong\left(B_{\nu}\otimes C_{\xi}\right)_{\tau}
which is
B_{\nu}\otimes_{\alpha}C_{\xi}
, exactly as in the proof of Lemma
3.7
.
∎

Remark
.

In particular, twists of
B\otimes C
by factorizable cocycles on
S\times T
are classical tensor products

B_{\nu}\otimes C_{\xi}
of twists of
B
and
C
.

4
Quantum Segre maps via cocycle quantization

4.1
Classical Segre embeddings

In classical algebraic geometry,
the polynomial algebra
\mathcal{A}^{n}:=\mathbb{F}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
serves as the homogeneous coordinate ring of the
n
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}:=\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{F})
. Projective subvarieties of
\mathbb{P}^{n}

are zero loci of homogeneous ideals of
\mathcal{A}^{n}
, with the exception of
\langle x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
.
Given
n,m\geq 1
, the
classical Segre map

is an embedding

\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{(n+1)(m+1)-1}

which equips the Cartesian product of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\mathbb{P}^{m}

with a structure of a projective subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{(n+1)(m+1)-1}
.
To describe this map explicitly, we assume that
the
(n+1)(m+1)
homogeneous coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{(n+1)(m+1)-1}

are labeled by
pairs of indices:

z_{00},z_{01},\dots,z_{nm}.

If
[x_{0}:x_{1}:\dots:x_{n}]
are homogeneous coordinates of a point
x
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and
[y_{0}:y_{1}:\dots:y_{m}]
, of a point
y
on
\mathbb{P}^{m}
, the image of
(x,y)
under the Segre embedding is the point
z
with homogeneous coordinates
z_{ij}=x_{i}y_{j}
,
0\leq i\leq n
,
0\leq j\leq m
.
All such point
z
form the
Segre subvariety

of
\mathbb{P}^{(n+1)(m+1)-1}
.

In order to quantize the Segre embedding, we need to consider its equivalent form as a morphism between homogeneous coordinate rings.
(We will later use gradings on those rings
to quantize them into noncommutative algebras.)
The above coordinate description means that the
classical Segre map is the following morphism of commutative algebras:

s_{n,m}\colon\mathbb{F}[z_{00},\dots,z_{nm}]\rightarrow\mathbb{F}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]\otimes\mathbb{F}[y_{0},\dots,y_{m}],\qquad s_{n,m}(z_{ij})=x_{i}\otimes y_{j}.

The image of
s_{n,m}
is the
Segre product

[
PP05
, Ch.3, §2]

of
\mathcal{A}^{n}
and
\mathcal{A}^{m}
, namely the algebra spanned by monomials where the
x
-degree and the
y
-degree are the same.
The kernel of
s_{n,m}
is the ideal generated by relations
which say that the matrix
(z_{ij})
of homogeneous coordinates
has rank
1
: that is, all
2\times 2
minors of this matrix are zero.
That these relations are quadratic justifies the alternative name
Segre quadric
for the Segre subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{(n+1)(m+1)-1}
.

4.2
Gradings on the homogeneous coordinate rings

We view
\mathbb{F}[z_{00},\dots,z_{nm}]
as an algebra which is graded by the monoid
\mathbb{N}^{(n+1)(m+1)}
.
For convenience we now write this monoid additively.
Elements of
\mathbb{N}^{(n+1)(m+1)}
are viewed as matrices of size

(n+1)\times(m+1)
, with rows indexed by
0,\dots,n
and columns indexed by
0,\dots,m
.
The degree of the generator
z_{ij}
is
e_{ij}\in\mathbb{N}^{(n+1)(m+1)}
, the unit matrix whose non-zero entry is in the
(i,j)
position.

The polynomial algebra
\mathbb{F}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
is graded by the monoid

\mathbb{N}^{n+1}
. Hence
the tensor product
\mathbb{F}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]\otimes\mathbb{F}[y_{0},\dots,y_{m}]

acquires, as per Section
3.3
above, grading by the monoid
\mathbb{N}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{N}^{m+1}

(not the same as
\mathbb{N}^{(n+1)(m+1)}
).
Elements of
\mathbb{N}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{N}^{m+1}
are viewed as pairs of vectors of size
(n+1,m+1)
.
We write
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{n+1}
,
\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{m+1}
for the unit vectors whose non-zero entries are in the
i
th position and in the
j
th position, respectively.
Here
0\leq i\leq n
,
0\leq j\leq m
.

To consider the classical Segre map
s_{n,m}
as a map between graded algebras
with different grading monoids, we define the morphism

f\colon\mathbb{N}^{(n+1)(m+1)}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{N}^{m+1},\quad f(e_{ij})=(\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}).

(4.1)

Since
\mathbb{N}^{(n+1)(m+1)}
is a free commutative monoid with generators
\{e_{ij}:i=0,\dots,n,\ j=0,\dots,m\}
, any mapping of each generator
e_{ij}
to an element of
\mathbb{N}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{N}^{m+1}
uniquely extends
f

to
\mathbb{N}^{(n+1)(m+1)}
as a morphism of monoids. The following is an easy observation:

Lemma 4.1
.

The classical Segre map
s_{n,m}
between the
\mathbb{N}^{(n+1)(m+1)}
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
\mathbb{F}[z_{00},\dots,z_{nm}]
and the

\mathbb{N}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{N}^{m+1}
-graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra

\mathbb{F}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]\otimes\mathbb{F}[y_{0},\dots,y_{m}]
is compatible with the morphism
f
of monoids, in the sense of Lemma
3.5
. ∎

4.3
Quantum projective spaces as cocycle twists

The polynomial algebra

\mathcal{A}^{N}=\mathbb{F}[x_{0},\dots,x_{N}]

in
N+1
variables over the field
\mathbb{F}
has a well-studied family of noncommutative deformations, as follows.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{q}=(q_{ij})_{i,j=0}^{N}
be a
matrix of size
(N+1)\times(N+1)
with entries in
\mathbb{F}^{\times}
which is
multiplicatively antisymmetric (see Theorem
2.10
). The
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}^{N}_{\mathbf{q}}
, generated by
X_{0},\dots,X_{N}
subject to the relations

X_{j}X_{i}-q_{ji}X_{i}X_{j}=0,\quad 0\leq i<j\leq N,

(4.2)

is the
quantum projective space
defined by
\mathbf{q}
.

Let
M_{\text{\rm a.s.}}^{N+1}(\mathbb{F}^{\times})
be the set
of
(N+1)\times(N+1)
multiplicatively
antisymmetric matrices.
Recall from Theorem
2.10
the bijection

M_{\text{\rm a.s.}}^{N+1}(\mathbb{F}^{\times})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{N+1},\mathbb{F}^{\times}),\quad\mathbf{q}\mapsto\mu_{\mathbf{q}}.

This bijection has explicit manifestation in the following:

Proposition 4.3
.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}^{N}_{\mathbf{q}}
is isomorphic to the
cocycle twist
\left(\mathcal{A}^{N}\right)_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}}
.

Proof.

As in Definition
3.1
,
we write the elements of
\left(\mathcal{A}^{N}\right)_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}}

as
p^{\prime}
where
p\in\mathcal{A}^{N}
.
We claim that there exists an algebra homomorphism

\varphi\colon\mathcal{A}^{N}_{\mathbf{q}}\to\left(\mathcal{A}^{N}\right)_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}}
given on the generators of
\mathcal{A}^{N}_{\mathbf{q}}
by
\varphi(X_{i})=x_{i}^{\prime}
. We verify
relations (
4.2
): if
0\leq i<j\leq N
,

\displaystyle\varphi(X_{j})*\varphi(X_{i})-q_{ji}\,\varphi(X_{i})*\varphi(X_{j})

\displaystyle=x_{j}^{\prime}*x_{i}^{\prime}-q_{ji}\,x_{i}^{\prime}*x_{j}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mu_{\mathbf{q}}(g_{j},g_{i})(x_{j}x_{i})^{\prime}-q_{ji}\,\mu_{\mathbf{q}}(g_{i},g_{j})(x_{i}x_{j})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=1(x_{j}x_{i})^{\prime}-q_{ji}q_{ij}(x_{i}x_{j})^{\prime}=0

as
x_{j}x_{i}=x_{i}x_{j}
in
\mathcal{A}^{N}
and
q_{ji}q_{ij}=1
.
(We have used the formula for
\mu_{\mathbf{q}}
given in Theorem
2.10
.)

This shows that
\varphi
is a well-defined homomorphism.
Relations (
4.2
) are easily seen to imply that standard monomials

X_{0}^{k_{0}}\dots X_{N}^{k_{N}}
,
k_{0},\dots,k_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, form a spanning set in

\mathcal{A}^{N}_{\mathbf{q}}
; the homomorphism
\varphi
sends

X_{0}^{k_{0}}\dots X_{N}^{k_{N}}
to a non-zero scalar multiple
of
(x_{0}^{k_{0}}\dots x_{N}^{k_{N}})^{\prime}
in
\left(\mathcal{A}^{N}\right)_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}}
, where by definition
these monomials form a basis. A spanning set carried by a linear map to
a basis must itself be a basis, and so
\varphi
carries a basis to a basis hence is bijective.
∎

Remark
.

It is well known that standard monomials form a basis of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{q}}
. This can be shown, for example, by an easy application of the Diamond Lemma
[
Ber78
]
.
Proposition
4.3
offers yet another way to formalize the proof of this fact.

4.4
Quantizations of the classical Segre map. The factorizable cocycle case

From now on, we fix
n\geq 1
and
m\geq 1
and write

•

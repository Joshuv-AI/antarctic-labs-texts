MMSE Channel Estimation in Fading MIMO Gaussian Channels With Blockage: A Novel Lower Bound via Poincaré Inequality
Mohammadreza Bakhshizadeh Mohajer ∗ , Luca Barletta ∗ , Daniela Tuninetti † , Alessandro Tomasoni ∗∗ , Daniele Lo Iacono ∗∗ , and Fabio Osnato ∗∗

Abstract
Integrated sensing and communication is regarded as a key enabler for next-generation wireless networks. To optimize the transmitted waveform for both sensing and communication, various performance metrics must be considered. This work focuses on sensing, and specifically on the mean square error (MSE) of channel estimation. Given the complexity of deriving the MSE, the Bayesian Cramér-Rao Bound (BCRB) is commonly recognized as a lower bound on the minimum MSE. However, the BCRB is not applicable to channels with discrete or mixed distributions. To address this limitation, a new lower bound based on a Poincaré inequality is proposed and applied to fading MIMO AWGN channels with blockage probability, and the behavior of the lower bound at high SNR is precisely characterized.



I
Introduction

Next-generation wireless networks, such as 6G, are expected to play a crucial role in supporting various emerging applications. In addition to high-quality connectivity, precise and robust sensing is expected to play a significant role in these networks
[
1
,
2
]
. Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC), which performs wireless communication and sensing using a single hardware platform and a common waveform, is considered a key technology in 6G with the benefit of reducing hardware cost, size, and power consumption
[
3
,
4
]
.

From the communication point of view, given the channel the objective is to design an optimal transmission signal that maximizes the information rate. However, from the sensing perspective, the problem is to estimate channel parameters knowing the transmitted signal. This leads to different performance metrics and design criteria
[
4
]
. Future 6G vehicle-to-everything (V2X) is foreseen to explore millimeter wave (mmWave)
[
5
]
. Also, the mmWave bands are envisioned to be exploited for sensing in 6G, wireless local area network (WLAN), and next-generation wireless networks
[
2
]
. As a challenge, propagation in these frequencies suffer higher losses and can easily be blocked by objects in the path of the transmitted signal
[
6
]
.

Motivated by ISAC systems and propagation challenges in high frequencies, we focus on characterizing the performance of the sensing subsystem, which is typically done via the estimation mean square error (MSE). Since an exact evaluation of the MSE is challenging, previous work has focused on lower bounding the MSE of weakly unbiased estimators by the Bayesian Cramér-Rao bound (BCRB)
[
4
]
.
The BCRB is not tight in general and does not hold for discrete or mixed distributions
[
7
]
. In this work, we propose an alternative lower bound on the MSE of channel estimation based on a Poincaré inequality derived in
[
7
]
. Specifically, we extend the inequality to cope with the non-canonical exponential family of distributions. Further, we compute the lower bound for Gaussian channels with blockage probability, for both the scalar and vector cases. In both instances, as for the BCRB, our bound is a function of the sample covariance matrix of the transmitted signal, and the exact behavior in the high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) regime can be characterized. Finally, through numerical evaluations, we compare our bound with the minimum MMSE (MMSE) and the linear MMSE (LMMSE).

In Sec.

II
Poincaré Lower Bound

Here, we briefly review the non-canonical exponential family and the Poincaré inequality. Then we extend the alternative MMSE formula and the Poincaré lower bound presented in
[
7
]
to the more general case of non-canonical exponential family.

II-A

Non-Canonical Exponential Family

We consider probability models
\{P_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}=\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
supported on
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
of the non-canonical exponential family with probability density function (pdf)

\displaystyle f_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x})=h(\mathbf{y})\mathrm{e}^{\langle\eta(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\rangle-\phi(\mathbf{x})}

(5)

where
h:\mathcal{Y}\to[0,\infty)
is the
base measure
;
\eta:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the
natural parameter
function;
\mathbf{T}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the
sufficient statistic
function;
\phi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is the
log-partition
function.
Let us also introduce the
information density
function

\displaystyle i_{P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\triangleq\log\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}{\mathrm{d}(P_{\mathsf{X}}\cdot P_{\mathsf{Y}})}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\log\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}=\mathbf{x}}}{\mathrm{d}P_{\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathbf{y}),

where
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X},\,\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}
, and
\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}{\mathrm{d}(P_{\mathsf{X}}\cdot P_{\mathsf{Y}})}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is the Radon-Nikodyme derivative.
Next, we show a result about the gradient of the information density.

Proposition 1

For the exponential family defined in (
5
) we have that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}i_{P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\cdot(\eta(\mathbf{x})-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\eta(\mathsf{X})\;\middle|\;\mathsf{Y}=\mathbf{y}\right]),

(6)

where
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}
.

Proof:

Fix some
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
. Then,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}i_{P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\log h(\mathbf{y})+\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\langle\eta(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\rangle-\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\log f_{\mathsf{Y}}(\mathbf{y})

(7)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\cdot\eta(\mathbf{x})-\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\log\frac{f_{\mathsf{Y}}(\mathbf{y})}{h(\mathbf{y})}

(8)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\cdot\eta(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\cdot{\mathbb{E}}\left[\eta(\mathsf{X})\;\middle|\;\mathsf{Y}=\mathbf{y}\right]

(9)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\cdot(\eta(\mathbf{x})-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\eta(\mathsf{X})\;\middle|\;\mathsf{Y}=\mathbf{y}\right]),

(10)

where (
7
) follows from (
5
); (
8
) follows from the gradient-Jacobian relationship
\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\langle\eta(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\rangle=\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\cdot\eta(\mathbf{x})
; and in (
9
) we used

\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\log\frac{f_{\mathsf{Y}}(\mathbf{y})}{h(\mathbf{y})}=\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\cdot{\mathbb{E}}\left[\eta(\mathsf{X})\;\middle|\;\mathsf{Y}=\mathbf{y}\right],

(11)

which is the Tweedie-Robbins-Esposito (TRE) formula
[
8
]
.
∎

II-B

Conditional Poincaré Inequality

In order to present our new lower bound on the MMSE, we first introduce the conditional Poincaré inequality. For a class of functions
\mathcal{A}
, a conditional probability
P_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}=\mathbf{x}}
(for a fixed
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
) is said to satisfy a Poincaré inequality with respect to
\mathcal{A}
with a constant
\kappa(\mathbf{x})\geq 0
if for all
f\in\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\mathsf{Var}[f(\mathsf{Y})|\mathsf{X}=\mathbf{x}]\leq\frac{1}{\kappa(\mathbf{x})}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|\nabla f(\mathsf{Y})\|^{2}\;\middle|\;\mathsf{X}=\mathbf{x}\right].

(12)

There exist several sufficient conditions on
\mathcal{A}
and
P_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}=\mathbf{x}}
that guarantee the Poincaré inequality holds and identifies the constant
\kappa(\mathbf{x})
.
Following
[
7
]
, for a class
\mathcal{A}
of continuously differentiable functions, the Bakry-Émery condition defines

\displaystyle\kappa_{\text{BE}}(\mathbf{x})=\max{\left\{\kappa:\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{y}}\log\left(\frac{1}{f_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x})}\right)\succeq\kappa\mathsf{I}_{k},\forall\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}\right\}}.

(13)

The Bakry-Émery constant for the non-canonical exponential family is given by the next proposition.

Proposition 2

Assume that
P_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}
has a pdf of the form in (
5
). Then for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{\text{BE}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\max{\left\{0,\tilde{\kappa}_{\text{BE}}(\mathbf{x})\right\}},

(14)

\displaystyle\tilde{\kappa}_{\text{BE}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}\lambda_{\text{min}}\left(\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{y}}\log\left(\frac{1}{h(\mathbf{y})}\right)-\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{y}}\langle\eta(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\rangle\right).

(15)

Proof:

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{y}}\log\left(\frac{1}{f_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x})}\right)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{y}}(\log(h(\mathbf{y}))-\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{y}}(\langle\eta(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\rangle)-\phi(\mathbf{x}))

(16)

\displaystyle\succeq\lambda_{\text{min}}\left(\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{y}}\log\left(\frac{1}{h(\mathbf{y})}\right)-\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{y}}\langle\eta(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{y})\rangle\right)\mathsf{I}_{k},

(17)

which concludes the proof.
∎

II-C

MMSE and the Poincaré Lower Bound

In
[
7
]
, an alternative expression of the estimation MMSE was proposed. Next, we extend that MMSE result for the non-canonical exponential family of distributions.

Theorem 1

Assume that
P_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}
has a pdf of the form in (
5
) and that
\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{Y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathsf{Y})
has full column rank almost surely (a.s.)
\mathsf{Y}
. Then,

\displaystyle\text{mmse}(\eta(\mathsf{X})|\mathsf{Y})={\mathbb{E}}\left[\|(\mathbf{J}_{\mathsf{Y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathsf{Y}))^{+}\nabla_{\mathsf{Y}}i_{P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})\|^{2}\right].

(18)

Proof:

Since
\mathbf{J}_{\mathsf{Y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathsf{Y})
has full rank a.s.
\mathsf{Y}
, the pseudo-inverse
(\mathbf{J}_{\mathsf{Y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathsf{Y}))^{+}
exists a.s.
\mathsf{Y}
. Using Proposition
1
, we have a.s.
\mathsf{Y}
,

\displaystyle(\eta(\mathsf{X})-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\eta(\mathsf{X})\;\middle|\;\mathsf{Y}\right])=(\mathbf{J}_{\mathsf{Y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathsf{Y}))^{+}\nabla_{\mathsf{Y}}i_{P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}).

(19)

By taking the squared norm and expectation on both sides of (
19
), and recalling (
1
), the claim follows.
∎We are now ready to present our new lower bound.

Theorem 2

Assume the three following conditions hold:

1.

For all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
the distribution
P_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}=\mathbf{x}}
has the pdf of the form in (
5
) and it satisfies a Poincaré inequality with respect to
(\mathcal{A},\kappa(\mathbf{x}))
.

2.

\mathbf{y}\mapsto i_{P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\mathcal{A}
for every
\mathbf{x}
such that
\kappa(\mathbf{x})>0
.

3.

There exists a
\rho(\mathbf{x})\geq 0
such that
\sigma_{\text{min}}((\mathbf{J}_{\mathsf{y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathsf{y}))^{+})\geq\rho(\mathbf{x})
for all
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}
.

Then,

\displaystyle\text{mmse}(\eta(\mathsf{X})|\mathsf{Y})\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\rho^{2}(\mathbf{x})\kappa(\mathsf{X}){\mathsf{Var}}\left[i_{P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})|\mathsf{X}\right]\right].

(20)

Proof:

We have

\displaystyle\text{mmse}(\eta(\mathsf{X})|\mathsf{Y})=\mathbb{E}[\|\eta(\mathsf{X})-\mathbb{E}[\eta(\mathsf{X})|\mathsf{Y}]\|^{2}]

(21)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|(\mathbf{J}_{\mathsf{Y}}\mathbf{T}(\mathsf{Y}))^{+}\nabla_{\mathsf{Y}}i_{P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})\|^{2}\right]

(22)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\rho^{2}(\mathsf{X})\|\nabla_{\mathsf{Y}}i_{P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})\|^{2}\right]

(23)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\rho^{2}(\mathsf{X}){\mathbb{E}}\left[\|\nabla_{\mathsf{Y}}i_{P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})\|^{2}\;\middle|\;\mathsf{X}\right]\right]

(24)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\rho^{2}(\mathsf{X})\kappa(\mathsf{X}){\mathsf{Var}}\left[i_{P_{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})|\mathsf{X}\right]\right],

(25)

where (
22
) follows from applying Theorem
1
; (
23
) from employing condition
3
and the inequality
\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\|\geq\sigma_{\text{min}}(\mathbf{A})\|\mathbf{x}\|
; and (
25
) from using the Poincaré inequality (
12
) and conditions
1
and
2
. This concludes the proof.
∎

III
Channels with Blockage Probability

In this section, we use our new bound in Theorem
2
for the problem of channel estimation in ISAC systems. We focus on Gaussian channels with blockage probability, and we derive MMSE lower bounds for both scalar and vector cases.

III-A

System Model

Let us consider
T
uses of an
M\times N
multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) channel. The model for the sensing subsystem of an ISAC system is

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{Z},

(26)

where
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times T}
is the sensing received signal,
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times T}
is the transmitted signal,
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times M}
is the target channel response matrix, and
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times T}
is the sensing noise modeled as independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) circularly symmetric complex Gaussian random variable with zero mean, namely
\text{vec}(\mathbf{Z})\sim{\cal CN}({\bf 0},\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}{\mathsf{I}}_{NT})
.
The sample covariance matrix of the transmitted signal,
\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{X}}\triangleq T^{-1}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{\dagger}
, will play a central role in both the analysis of the sensing system’s performance and in imposing an average-power constraint
P_{T}
for the communication problem, i.e.,
{\text{tr}}\left(\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{X}}\right)\leq MP_{T}
. We assume
\mathbf{X}
to be known at the sensing receiver, which observes
\mathbf{Y}
. The sensing performance is the MSE

MSE

\displaystyle\triangleq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|\mathbf{H}-\hat{\mathbf{H}}\|^{2}\right],

(27)

where
\hat{\mathbf{H}}
is an estimate of
\mathbf{H}
based on
(\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X})
.
The optimal MSE estimator is
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{H}\;\middle|\;\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}\right]
.

In the rest of this work, we consider the following complex to real mapping for all column vectors and matrices

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{x}}=\begin{bmatrix}\text{Re}({\mathbf{x}})\\
\text{Im}({\mathbf{x}})\end{bmatrix},\,\overline{\mathbf{X}}=\begin{bmatrix}\text{Re}({\mathbf{X}})&-\text{Im}({\mathbf{X}})\\
\text{Im}({\mathbf{X}})&\text{Re}({\mathbf{X}})\end{bmatrix}.

(28)

Note that in this mapping
\mathbf{R}_{\overline{\mathbf{X}^{\mathsf{T}}}}\triangleq(2T)^{-1}(\overline{\mathbf{X}^{\mathsf{T}}})^{\dagger}\overline{\mathbf{X}^{\mathsf{T}}}
.
It can be shown that (
26
) can be rewritten in vector form as

\displaystyle\overline{{\text{vec}}\left(\mathbf{Y}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)}=(I_{N}\otimes\overline{\mathbf{X}^{\mathsf{T}}})\overline{{\text{vec}}\left(\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)}+\overline{{\text{vec}}\left(\mathbf{Z}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)}.

(29)

Let us introduce the quantities
\mathsf{Y}\triangleq\overline{{\text{vec}}\left(\mathbf{Y}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)}
,
\mathsf{H}\triangleq\overline{{\text{vec}}\left(\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)}
,
\mathsf{Z}\triangleq\overline{{\text{vec}}\left(\mathbf{Z}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)}
, and
\mathsf{C}_{\mathbf{X}}\triangleq I_{N}\otimes\overline{\mathbf{X}^{\mathsf{T}}}
so that the complex vectorized sensing model (
29
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathsf{Y}=\mathsf{C}_{\mathbf{X}}\mathsf{H}+\mathsf{Z}.

(30)

In the next subsection, we start with the derivation of our new Poincaré lower bound on the MMSE for the (real) scalar case, i.e., the real part of (
26
) when
N=M=T=1
.

III-B

Real-Valued Scalar Model

Consider having channel fading with pdf

\displaystyle f_{\mathsf{H}}(x)=(1-\alpha)\delta(x)+\alpha\mathcal{N}(x;0,\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}),

(31)

where
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
1-\alpha
is the probability of having a blockage. In the following, we derive the Poincaré lower bound on the MMSE of
\mathsf{H}
. Then, we demonstrate that in the high SNR regime the bound is a function of
\mathsf{X}^{2}
.
Finally, we present the MMSE computed using Theorem
1
and the LMMSE.

In order to use Theorem
2
, we need to evaluate the information density. Starting from

\displaystyle f_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{H},\mathsf{X}}(y|h,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(y-hx)^{2}}{\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}},

(32)

\displaystyle f_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)=(1-\alpha)\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\frac{y^{2}}{\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}}+\alpha\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\frac{y^{2}}{(x^{2}\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\sigma_{\rm s}^{2})}}}{\sqrt{2\pi(x^{2}\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\sigma_{\rm s}^{2})}},

(33)

we get

\displaystyle i_{P_{\mathsf{H},\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(h;y,x)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{f_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{H},\mathsf{X}}(y|h,x)}{f_{\mathsf{Y}|\mathsf{X}}(y|x)}\right)

(34)

\displaystyle=\frac{-(y-hx)^{2}+(y)^{2}}{2\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}

(35)

\displaystyle\quad-\log\left(1-\alpha+\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}{x^{2}\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}}e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}\frac{x^{2}\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}}{x^{2}\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}}\right).

(36)

We have
\mathbf{T}(y)=\frac{yx}{\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}
, and by using condition
3
in Theorem
2
we get
\rho=\frac{\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}{x}
. Since
h(y)=\frac{e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}}
, we have
\kappa(\mathsf{H})=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}
by Proposition
2
. Combining all the results, the Poincaré lower bound becomes

\displaystyle\text{mmse}(\mathsf{H}|\mathsf{Y})\geq\mathbb{E}[\rho^{2}\kappa(\mathsf{H}){\mathsf{Var}}\left[i_{P_{\mathsf{H},\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(\mathsf{H};\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{X})\;\middle|\;\mathsf{H},\mathsf{X}\right]]

(37)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}{\mathsf{X}^{2}}\mathsf{Var}\left[\frac{\mathsf{Y}\mathsf{H}\mathsf{X}}{\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left.-\log\left(1-\alpha+\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}{\mathsf{X}^{2}\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}}e^{\frac{\mathsf{Y}^{2}}{2\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}\frac{\mathsf{X}^{2}\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}}{\mathsf{X}^{2}\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}}\right)\right|\mathsf{H},\mathsf{X}\right]\right].

(38)

Now let us evaluate the information density at
y=hx+\sigma_{\rm s}\tilde{\mathsf{Z}}
where
\tilde{\mathsf{Z}}\sim{\cal N}(0,1)
; we get

\displaystyle\log\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}_{s}}+i_{P_{\mathsf{H},\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(h;hx+\sigma_{\rm s}\tilde{\mathsf{Z}},x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathsf{Z}}^{2}-\log\left((1-\alpha)\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{hx}{\sigma_{\rm s}}+\tilde{\mathsf{Z}}\right)^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}}+\alpha\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(hx+\sigma_{\rm s}\tilde{\mathsf{Z}})^{2}}{x^{2}\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2\pi(x^{2}\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}+\sigma_{\rm s}^{2})}}\right).

(39)

For
x\neq 0
and
h\neq 0
,
the high SNR behavior (i.e., as
\sigma_{\rm s}\to 0
) of the information density is

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma_{\rm s}\to 0}\log\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}_{s}}+i_{P_{\mathsf{H},\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(h;hx+\sigma_{\rm s}\tilde{\mathsf{Z}},x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathsf{Z}}^{2}-\log\left(\alpha\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(x^{2}\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2})}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{h^{2}}{\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}}\right)\right).

(40)

If
x\neq 0
and
h=0
then

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma_{\rm s}\to 0}i_{P_{\mathsf{H},\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(h;hx+\sigma_{\rm s}\tilde{\mathsf{Z}},x)

\displaystyle=-\log(1-\alpha),

(41)

while for
x=0
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma_{\rm s}\to 0}i_{P_{\mathsf{H},\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}}}(h;hx+\sigma_{\rm s}\tilde{\mathsf{Z}},x)

\displaystyle=0.

(42)

To correctly evaluate the high SNR behavior of the variance of the information density, we need the following result.

Lemma 1

For
x\neq 0
and
h\neq 0

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we proposed a Poincaré lower bound for the non-canonical exponential family of distributions. We calculated the bound for the problem of estimation of channels with blockage probability, which is of great significance for next-generation wireless networks operating at millimeter wave frequencies. In such channels, the Bayesian Cramér-Rao Bound (BCRB) is not applicable due to the fact that the BCRB does not apply to channels with discrete or mixed distributions. As for the channel model, we studied both the real-valued scalar and the complex-valued vector cases. In the high signal-to-noise ratio regime, we demonstrated that the bound is a function of the sample covariance matrix of the transmitted signal. Moreover, we compared the Poincaré bound to the MMSE and the linear MMSE. The main direction for future research will be to utilize the Poincaré lower bound to optimize the transmitted signal in an integrated sensing and communication system for optimal sensing and communication performance. Another promising research path is to obtain a lower bound on the MMSE when the channel matrix is a nonlinear function of a hidden parameter, such as in angle of arrival estimation, when BCRB cannot be calculated.

Remark on dimension-free estimates for discrete maximal functions over ℓ q \ell^{q} balls: small dyadic scales
Jakub Niksiński

Abstract
. We give a dimension-free bound on ℓ p ​ ( ℤ d ) \ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}) , p ∈ [ 2 , ∞ ] p\in[2,\infty] for the discrete Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator over the ℓ q \ell^{q} balls in ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} with small dyadic radii. Our result combined with the work of Kosz, Mirek, Plewa, Wróbel gives dimension-free estimates on ℓ p ​ ( ℤ d ) \ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}) , p ∈ [ 2 , ∞ ] p\in[2,\infty] for the discrete dyadic Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator over ℓ q \ell^{q} balls for q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 .

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a convex, bounded, closed symmetric subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with non-empty interior, we will call such
G
a symmetric convex body. Natural examples are the
\ell^{q}
balls:

B^{q}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|x\|_{q}=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\leq 1\Big\}\ \text{for}\ q\in[1,\infty),

B^{\infty}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|x\|_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|x_{i}|\leq 1\Big\}.

With each symmetric convex body one can associate the corresponding Hardy-Littlewood averaging operator. For any
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define

M_{t}^{G}f(x)=\frac{1}{|t\cdot G|}\int_{t\cdot G}f(x-y)\ dy,

for a locally integrable function
f
, where
t\cdot G=\{tx:x\in G\}
and
|t\cdot G|
denotes its Lebesgue measure. Now for any
p
let
C_{p}(d,G)>0
be the smallest number such that the following inequality

\|\sup_{t>0}|M_{t}^{G}f|\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{p}(d,G)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

holds for every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It is well known that
C_{p}(d,G)<\infty
for all
p\in(1,\infty]
and all symmetric convex bodies
G
.

In 1980s dependency of
C_{p}(d,G)
on dimension
d
has begun to be studied. Various results were obtained, but as of now the major conjecture in this topic is still open, namely that
C_{p}(d,G)
can be bounded from the above by a number independent of set
G
and dimension
d
for each fixed
p\in(1,\infty]
. We recommend the survey article
[
4
]
for a detailed exposition of the subject, which contains results that we skipped mentioning.

Similar questions can be considered for the discrete analogue of the operator
M_{t}^{G}
. For every
t>0
and every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we define the discrete Hardy-Littlewood averaging operator

\mathcal{M}_{t}^{G}f(x)=\frac{1}{|(t\cdot G)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\sum_{y\in t\cdot G\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x-y),

where
f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
,
|(t\cdot G)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
is the number of elements of the set
(t\cdot G)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Similarly as before we define
\mathcal{C}_{p}(d,G)>0
to be the smallest number such that the following inequality

\|\sup_{t>0}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{G}f|\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}(d,G)\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}

holds for every
f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. Using similar methods as in the continuous case one can show that for every
p\in(1,\infty]
and symmetric convex body we have
\mathcal{C}_{p}(d,G)<\infty
.

What about the dependency of
\mathcal{C}_{p}(d,G)
on the dimension
d
?
We can ask similar questions as in the continuous setup, yet it turns out that the situation is much more delicate. Indeed in
[
2
]
the authors constructed a family of ellipsoids
E(d)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with the property that for each
p\in(1,\infty]
there exists
C_{p}>0
such that for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\mathcal{C}_{p}(d,E(d))\geq C_{p}(\log d)^{1/p}.

This means that if we want to establish dimension-free estimates for
\mathcal{C}_{p}(d,G)
we need to restrict ourselves to specific sets
G
, which contain more symmetries; one of the simpler options are
B^{q}
sets.

Define
\mathbb{D}=\{2^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
to be the set of dyadic numbers.
Literature in the discrete setting is not as fruitful, nevertheless there are some papers and positive results in this regard, for example:

•

In
[
2
]
it was proved that for every
p\in(\frac{3}{2},\infty]
there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
and every
f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{B^{\infty}}f|\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

This result is almost as strong as those in the continuous case. In the case of sets
B^{q}
for
q\neq\infty
authors of papers
[
2
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
could only obtain weaker conclusions.

•

In
[
1
]
it was proved that there exists
C>0
such that for every
p\in[2,\infty)
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
and every
f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have

\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{B^{2}}f|\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

•

In
[
6
]
by extending methods of
[
1
]
it was proved that for any
q\in(2,\infty)
there exists constant
C(q)>0

such that for every
p\in[2,\infty)
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
and every
f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have

\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\geq d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{B^{q}}f|\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C(q)\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

The paper
[
6
]
did not cover the range
t<d^{1/q}
nor
q<2
.

•

More recently in
[
10
]
, the author in collaboration with B. Wróbel has shown that for any
\varepsilon>0
and there is a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all
p\in[2,\infty)
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and
f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have

\Big\|\sup_{0\leq t\leq d^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{B^{2}}f|\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

In this paper we will prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists
C>0
, such that for every
q\geq 1
,
p\in[2,\infty]
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and
f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have

\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{B^{q}}f|\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

The result above for
q=1
gives a bound for supremum over
t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq d
, which generalizes the main theorem of
[
9
]
. Moreover Theorem
1.1
for
q\geq 2
combined with
[
6
, Theorems 2 and 3]

leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

For every
q\geq 2
there exists
C(q)>0
, such that for any
p\in[2,\infty]
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have

\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{B^{q}}f|\Big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C(q)\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

Methods of
[
6
]
can even give a
C(q)
uniform with respect to
q\geq 2
, however we don’t claim any results in this regard and will not comment further on this matter.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
uses methods of
[
1
, Section 3]
in a more streamlined version, adapting them if necessary. The fact that these methods still lead to new conclusions was apparently missed by the authors of
[
6
]
. Case
q\in[1,2)
leads to some new difficulties, which require ad hoc lemmas such as Lemma
2.4
or Lemma
2.5
.

1.1.
Notation

\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,...\}
will denote the set of positive integers and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
will denote the set of non-negative integers.
\mathbb{D}=\{2^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is the set of dyadic integers. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
we define

[N]=\{1,2,...,N\}
.
For
q\geq 1,t\geq 0
let

B_{t}^{q}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|^{q}\leq t^{q}\},

notice that the set
B_{t}^{q}
depends also on the parameter
d
.

We define
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We use the standard scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

x\cdot y=\sum_{k=1}^{d}x_{k}y_{k},

where
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
,
f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
let

\|x\|_{p}=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p},\quad\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=\Big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

and

\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})=\{g:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}:\|g\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}<\infty\}.

If
p=\infty
then for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
we define

\|x||_{\infty}=\sup_{i\in[d]}|x_{i}|,\ \ \ \ \|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|f(x)|,\quad\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})=\{g:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}:\|g\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}<\infty\}.

For
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
g:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
with finite support we define
f\ast g\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
by the series

f\ast g(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(y)g(x-y)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x-y)g(y).

If
f\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we introduce the discrete Fourier transform by the formula

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x)e(x\cdot\xi),\ \text{ for }\ \xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

One can uniquely extend the discrete Fourier transform to
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
so that
\widehat{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and we have the following Parseval identity:

\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

Moreover, for any
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
g:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
with finite support , the following holds

\widehat{f\ast g}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\widehat{g}(\xi).

\mathcal{F}^{-1}
will denote the inverse of the discrete Fourier transform, that is

\mathcal{F}^{-1}(G)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}G(\xi)e(-x\cdot\xi)\ d\xi,

where
G\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.
We let
m^{q}_{t}
be the multiplier symbol

m^{q}_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|B^{q}_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\sum_{x\in B^{q}_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e(x\cdot\xi).

\mathbb{T}^{d}
will denote the
d
-dimensional torus, which will be identified with the set
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}.
For
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
we define

\|\xi\|=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\Big)^{1/2},

note that then
\|\xi+1/2\|=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\Big)^{1/2}.

\Sym(d)
will denote the permutation group of
\{1,2,...,d\}
, it naturally acts on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
\sigma\cdot x=(x_{\sigma(1)},...,x_{\sigma(d)})
.
Let
Q=[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d}
. For convenience we define
\kappa_{q}(d,N)=\frac{N}{d^{1/q}}
.
We will use the convention that
A\lesssim B
to say that there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
.
For
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
|A|
we will denote Lebesgue measure of
A
or the number of elements of
A
, this should be clear from the context.
For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define

\supp(x)=\{i\in[d]:x_{i}\neq 0\}.

Definition 1.3
.

The discrete Hardy-Littlewood averaging operator over the
\ell^{q}
balls is defined for any function
f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
by the formula

\mathcal{M}_{t}^{q}f(x)=\frac{1}{|B_{t}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\sum_{y\in B_{t}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x-y).

Our goal is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

There exists
C>0
, such that for every
q\geq 1
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we have

\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{q}f|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})},

where
\mathbb{D}=\{2^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is the set of dyadic integers.

Using an interpolation argument one can show that Theorem
1.4
implies Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Auxiliary lemmas

In this section we give some preliminary results regarding behavior of points in
B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, which will turn out to be useful in next sections.

The argument in the proof of Lemma
2.1
below is very greedy, in the case of
q=2
it gives worse bound by a factor
2^{d/2}
compared to
[
3
, Lemma 5.1]
, however it is completely irrelevant for us.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
N>0
,
q\geq 1
. Define
N_{1}=N+\frac{d^{1/q}}{2}
then we have

(2\lfloor\kappa_{q}(d,N)\rfloor+1)^{d}\leq|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\leq|B_{N_{1}}^{q}|.

Proof.

Lower bound follows from the inclusion
[-\kappa_{q}(d,N),\kappa_{q}(d,N)]\subseteq B_{N}^{q}
.

In terms of the upper bound, notice that for every
x\in B_{N}^{q}
and
z\in Q
by the triangle inequality we have

\|x+z\|_{q}\leq\|x\|_{q}+\|z\|_{q}\leq N+\frac{d^{1/q}}{2}=N_{1}.

From this we obtain

\displaystyle|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|

\displaystyle=\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hskip-8.5359pt1\leq\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{Q}\mathds{1}_{\{z\in Q:\|x+z\|_{q}\leq N_{1}\}}(y)dy=\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{x+Q}\hskip-8.5359pt\mathds{1}_{B_{N_{1}}^{q}}(y)dy\leq|B_{N_{1}}^{q}|.

∎

Corollary 2.2
.

For all
q\geq 1
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
N>0
we have the following bound

|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\leq 2\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{d}\cdot 8^{d}.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.1
and the formula for the volume of unit
\ell^{q}
ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
space we obtain

|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\leq|B_{N_{1}}^{q}|=\Big(N+\frac{d^{1/q}}{2}\Big)^{d}\cdot\frac{2^{d}\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{q})^{d}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{d}{q})}.

Using the following bound for the gamma function

\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{x}}\Big(\frac{x}{e}\Big)^{x}\leq\Gamma(x),

which holds for all
x>0
(see for instance
[
12
,
(5.6.1)
]
)
and the fact that
\Gamma(y)\leq 1
for all
y\in[1,2]
, we get

\displaystyle|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\leq\Big(N+\frac{d^{1/q}}{2}\Big)^{d}\cdot\frac{2^{d}\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{q})^{d}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{d}{q})}\leq\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{d}d^{d/q}\cdot\frac{2^{d}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{d}{q})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{d}d^{d/q}\cdot\frac{2^{d}e^{1+d/q}\sqrt{(1+d/q)}}{(1+d/q)^{1+d/q}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\leq\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{d}d^{d/q}\cdot\frac{(2e)^{d}}{(1+d/q)^{d/q}}\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\pi}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{d}\cdot(2e)^{d}q^{\frac{d}{q}}\leq 2\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{d}\cdot(2e\cdot e^{1/e})^{d}\leq 2\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{d}\cdot 8^{d}.

∎

The next Lemma is an adapted version of
[
1
, Lemma 3.2]
, proof goes along the same lines.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
q\geq 1
,
d,N\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\kappa_{q}(d,N)\leq 409^{-1/q}
and
N^{q}\geq k\geq 409\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}N^{q}
, then we have

(2.1)

|\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\{i\in[d]:x_{i}=\pm 1\}|\leq N^{q}-k\}|\leq 2^{2-k}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|.

Moreover for all
q\geq 1
and
d,N\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
\kappa_{q}(d,N)\leq N^{-11}
we have

(2.2)

|\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\{i\in[d]:x_{i}=\pm 1\}|\leq N^{q}-2\}|\leq\frac{1}{N^{q}}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|.

Proof.

We will first prove (
2.1
). Let
n=N^{q}
. We have that

(2.3)

|\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\{i\in[d]:x_{i}=\pm 1\}|\leq n-k\}|=\sum_{m=k}^{n}|E_{m}|,

where

E_{m}=\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\{i\in[d]:x_{i}=\pm 1\}|=n-m\}.

To prove (
2.1
) it is sufficient to show that
|E_{m}|\leq 2^{1-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
holds for every
m\in\{k,k+1,...,n\}
. Notice that if
x\in E_{m}
then

|\{i\in[d]:|x_{i}|\geq 2\}|\leq\Big\lfloor\frac{m}{2^{q}}\Big\rfloor.

In order to bound
|E_{m}|
we will consider two cases separately.

Case 1)
m<2^{q}
.

In this situation every
x\in E_{m}
consists only of coordinates
-1,0,1
, hence we have

|E_{m}|=2^{n-m}\binom{d}{n-m}.

Moreover

\begin{split}&\binom{d}{n-m}\binom{d}{n}^{-1}\hskip-8.5359pt=\frac{n!(d-n)!}{(n-m)!(d-n+m)!}\leq\Big(\frac{n}{d-n}\Big)^{m}\hskip-8.5359pt=\Big(\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}}{1-\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}}\Big)^{m}\hskip-8.5359pt\leq\Big(2\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\Big)^{m},\end{split}

above we have used the fact that
\kappa_{q}(d,N)\leq 2^{-1/q}
.
We also have

(2.4)

2^{n}\binom{d}{n}\leq|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|.

Combining these three facts we obtain

\displaystyle|E_{m}|\leq 2^{n-m}\binom{d}{n-m}=2^{-m}\cdot 2^{n}\binom{d}{n}\binom{d}{n-m}\binom{d}{n}^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\Big(2\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\Big)^{m}\leq 2^{-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|.

Case 2)
m\geq 2^{q}
.

Notice that we have the following upper bound for
|E_{m}|
.

(2.5)

|E_{m}|\leq 2^{n-m}\binom{d}{n-m}\binom{d-n+m}{\lfloor\frac{m}{2^{q}}\rfloor}|B_{m^{1/q}}^{(q,\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor)}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}|,

where

B_{m^{1/q}}^{(q,\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor)}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}:\|y\|_{q}\leq m^{1/q}\}.

Indeed, in
\binom{d}{n-m}
options we choose coordinates on which
x\in E_{m}
will have values
\pm 1
, then we choose each sign in 2 ways, this explains the factor
2^{n-m}\binom{d}{n-m}
. Next we choose
\lfloor\frac{m}{2^{q}}\rfloor
coordinates in which the set
\{i\in[d]:|x_{i}|\geq 2\}
will be contained, for that we have
\binom{d-n+m}{\lfloor\frac{m}{2^{q}}\rfloor}
options. Lastly we bound the number of ways of putting numbers on these coordinates such that the condition
\|x\|_{q}\leq N
holds, this is bounded by
|B_{m^{1/q}}^{(q,\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor)}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}|
.

Using Corollary
2.2
we obtain that

(2.6)

\begin{split}|B_{m^{1/q}}^{(q,\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor)}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}|&\leq 2\cdot\Big(\frac{m^{1/q}}{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor^{1/q}}+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}8^{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}\leq 2\cdot\Big(\Big(\frac{m}{m/2^{q+1}}\Big)^{1/q}+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{m/2^{q}}8^{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}\\
&=2\cdot\Big(2^{1+1/q}+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{m/2^{q}}8^{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}\leq 2\cdot\Big(\frac{9}{2}\Big)^{m/2}8^{m/2}=2\cdot 6^{m},\end{split}

above we have used the fact that
m\geq 2^{q}
.
We also have that

(2.7)

\begin{split}&\binom{d}{n-m}\binom{d-n+m}{\lfloor\frac{m}{2^{q}}\rfloor}\binom{d}{n}^{-1}=\frac{n!\cdot(d-n)!}{(n-m)!\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor!\cdot(d-n+m-\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor)!}\\
&\leq\frac{n^{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}}{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor!}\Big(\frac{n}{d-n}\Big)^{m-\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{\leq}}\Big(\frac{en}{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}\Big)^{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}\Big(\frac{n}{d-n}\Big)^{m-\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(**)}}{{\leq}}\Big(\frac{2^{q}en}{m}\Big)^{m/2^{q}}\Big(\frac{n}{d-n}\Big)^{m-\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}=\Big(\frac{2^{q}en}{m}\Big)^{m/2^{q}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}}{1-\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}}\Big)^{m-\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}\\
&\leq\Big(\frac{2^{q}en}{m}\Big)^{m/2^{q}}\Big(2\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\Big)^{m-\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}\leq\Big(\frac{2^{q}en}{m}\Big)^{m/2^{q}}\Big(2\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\Big)^{m/2}.\end{split}

(*)
holds, since
\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor!\geq(\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor/e)^{\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor}
. In
(**)
we used the fact that for any
a>0
the function
(0,a/e]\ni t\mapsto(a/t)^{t}
is increasing. Two last inequalities hold, since
m-\lfloor m/2^{q}\rfloor\geq m-m/2^{q}\geq m/2
and
\kappa_{q}(d,N)\leq 2^{-1/q}
.

Using these four inequalities (i.e. (
2.4
), (
2.5
), (
2.6
), (
2.7
)) and our assumptions on
\kappa_{q}(d,N)
we finally obtain

(2.8)

\begin{split}|E_{m}|&\leq 2\cdot 2^{n}\binom{d}{n}2^{-m}\Big(\frac{2^{q}en}{m}\Big)^{m/2^{q}}\Big(2\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\Big)^{m/2}6^{m}\\
&\leq 2^{1-m}\cdot|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\Big(\frac{n\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}}{m}\Big)^{m/2}e^{m/e}e^{m/2}2^{m/2}6^{m}\\
&\leq 2^{1-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\Big(\frac{n\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}}{m}\Big)^{m/2}20.211^{m}\leq 2^{1-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|.\end{split}

Last inequality holds by the assumption
m\geq k\geq 409n\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}
. Due to (
2.3
) this concludes the proof of (
2.1
).

Now we prove the second part, that is (
2.2
). Assume that
d,N\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
\kappa_{q}(d,N)\leq N^{-11}
. We will also assume that
n\geq 2
, since in the case
N^{q}=n<2
the statement trivially holds, because then the left-hand side of (
2.2
) equals
0
.
We proceed as in the first part of the proof with minor changes. If
2\leq m<2^{q}
, then we have

\displaystyle|E_{m}|\leq\Big(2\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\Big)^{m}2^{-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\leq\Big(\frac{2}{n^{11}}\Big)^{m}2^{-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\leq\frac{1}{n}2^{-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|.

On the other hand if
m\geq 2^{q}
, then by the third inequality of (
2.8
)
we have

\displaystyle|E_{m}|\leq 2^{1-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\Big(\frac{n\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}}{m}\Big)^{m/2}20.211^{m}\leq 2^{1-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\Big(\frac{n^{-10}409}{m}\Big)^{m/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\Big(\frac{n^{-1}409}{2^{9}m}\Big)^{m/2}\hskip-8.5359pt\leq 2^{1-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{m/2}\hskip-8.5359pt\leq\frac{2}{n}2^{-m}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|,

by (
2.3
) with
k=2
we get (
2.2
).
∎

In order to prove Lemma
2.5
we need to introduce an auxiliary lemma stating that in certain range it’s unlikely for points in
B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
to have a lot of big coordinates.

Lemma 2.4
.

Take any
q\geq 1
, assume that
d,N\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
\kappa_{q}(d,N)\in[N^{-11},e^{-12/q}]
. Then for every
k\in\mathbb{N},k>1
we have

\Big|\Big\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\{i\in[d]:|x_{i}|=k\}|\geq\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}N^{q}}{k^{6}}\Big\}\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{2q}}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|.

Proof of this lemma is just based on removing coordinates
\pm k
and increasing number of
\pm 1
coordinates.

Proof.

Let
n=N^{q}
.
Fix
k\in\mathbb{N},k>1
. Every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
can be written as
x=u(x)+v(x)
, where

u(x)_{j}=\begin{cases}x_{j},\ \ \text{if $|x_{j}|\notin\{1,k\}$},\\
0,\ \ \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
U(B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})=\big\{u(x):x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big\}
. Then we have the following disjoint decomposition

(2.9)

B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\bigcup_{u\in U(B_{N}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq l,m\leq n\end{subarray}}A(u,l,m),

where

A(u,l,m)=\Big\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:u(x)=u,|\{i\in[d]:|x_{i}|=k\}|=l,|\{i\in[d]:|x_{i}|=1\}|=m\Big\}.

We will prove the following. If
l\geq\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}n}{k^{6}}
, then for all
u
and
m
we have

(2.10)

|A(u,l,m)|\lesssim\frac{1}{n^{3}}|A(u,0,m+\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor)|.

Note that the above suffices to prove Lemma
2.4
, indeed, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\Big\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\{i\in[d]:|x_{i}|=k\}|\geq\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}N^{q}}{k^{6}}\Big\}\Big|=\hskip-14.22636pt\sum_{u\in U(B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\sum_{0\leq m\leq n}\sum_{\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}n}{k^{6}}\leq l\leq n}\hskip-19.91684pt|A(u,l,m)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n^{3}}\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{u\in U(B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\sum_{0\leq m\leq n}\sum_{\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}n}{k^{6}}\leq l\leq n}\hskip-19.91684pt|A(u,0,m+\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor)|\lesssim\frac{1}{n^{2}}\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{u\in U(B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\sum_{0\leq t\leq n}|A(u,0,t)|\lesssim\hskip-2.84544pt\frac{1}{n^{2}}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|,

penultimate inequality follows from the fact, that for each
t\in[0,n]\cap\mathbb{N}_{0}
there are at most
n+1
pairs of numbers
m,l\in[0,n]\cap\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
t=m+\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor
.

Now we will prove (
2.10
), take any
u\in U(B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})
,
l,m\in[0,n]\cap\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We can assume that

m+lk^{q}+\|u\|_{q}^{q}\leq n,

otherwise
A(u,l,m)=\emptyset
. Let
|u|=|\supp(u)|\leq n
. Then we have

(2.11)

\begin{split}&\frac{|A(u,l,m)|}{|A(u,0,m+\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor)|}=2^{m+l}\binom{d-|u|}{l}\binom{d-|u|-l}{m}\cdot\Bigg(2^{m+\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor}\binom{d-|u|}{m+\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor}\Bigg)^{-1}\\
&=2^{l-\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor}\frac{(d-|u|)!}{l!(d-|u|-l)!}\cdot\frac{(d-|u|-l)!}{m!(d-|u|-l-m)!}\frac{(m+\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor)!(d-|u|-m-\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor)!}{(d-|u|)!}\\
&\leq 2^{l-\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor}\frac{(m+\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor)!}{m!l!}\cdot\frac{(d-|u|-m-\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor)!}{(d-|u|-l-m)!}\\
&\leq 2^{l-\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor}\frac{(m+\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor)^{\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor}e^{l}}{l^{l}}(d-|u|-m-\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor)^{-\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor+l}\leq n^{\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor}e^{l}l^{-l}d^{-\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor+l}.\end{split}

Above we have used the fact that
l!\geq l^{l}e^{-l}
and

|u|+m+\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor\leq\|u\|_{q}^{q}+m+\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor\leq n\leq d/2.

Further simplifying right-hand side of last inequality of (
2.11
) and incorporating assumptions on
l
and
k
we obtain

(2.12)

\begin{split}&\frac{|A(u,l,m)|}{|A(u,0,m+\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor)|}\leq n^{\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor}e^{l}l^{-l}d^{-\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor+l}=\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\Big)^{\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor}\Big(\frac{ed}{l}\Big)^{l}\\
&\leq\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\Big)^{\lfloor lk^{q}\rfloor}\Big(ek^{6}\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{-23q/22}\Big)^{l}\leq\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\Big)^{lk}\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\Big)^{-23l/22}k^{6l}e^{l}\\
&\leq\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{qlk(1-23/44)}\cdot 2^{6lk}\cdot e^{l}\leq e^{kl(6\log 2+1/2-\frac{12\cdot 21}{44})}\leq e^{-lk}.\end{split}

Last inequality follows from the assumption
\kappa_{q}(d,N)\leq e^{-12/q}
. In view of (
2.12
), in order to proof (
2.10
) and hence Lemma
2.4
, it suffices to show that for all
k\geq 2
and
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0},l\geq\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}n}{k^{6}}
we have

(2.13)

e^{-lk}\lesssim\frac{1}{n^{3}}.

We will consider two cases.

If
k\leq n^{1/24}
.
Then by
\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\geq n^{-11}
we get

l\geq\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}n}{k^{6}}\geq\frac{n^{1/2}}{k^{6}}\geq n^{1/4},

hence

e^{-kl}\leq e^{-2n^{1/4}}\lesssim\frac{1}{n^{3}}
,
thus (
2.13
) holds.

If
k\geq n^{1/24}
.
Then assumptions
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
l\geq\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}n}{k^{6}}>0
imply
l\geq 1
. From this we obtain

e^{-kl}\leq e^{-n^{1/24}}\lesssim\frac{1}{n^{3}}.

Thus in any case (
2.13
) holds, this concludes the proof of (
2.10
) and hence concludes the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

Now we can introduce a lemma, which will come in handy during the proof of Proposition
3.5
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For every
q\geq 1
and
d,N\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
\kappa_{q}(d,N)\leq e^{-12/q}
we have

\Big|\Big\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
|x_{i}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}^{d}x_{i}^{4}>\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}N^{q}\Big\}\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{q}}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|.

Proof.

We will consider two cases

Case 1) If
\kappa_{q}(d,N)\leq N^{-11}
.
Then by Lemma
2.3
we get

\displaystyle\Big|\Big\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
|x_{i}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}^{d}x_{i}^{4}>0\Big\}\Big|\leq|\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\{i\in[d]:x_{i}=\pm 1\}|\leq N^{q}-2\}|\leq\frac{1}{N^{q}}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|,

so the claim of Lemma
2.5
holds.

Case 2) If
\kappa_{q}(d,N)\in[N^{-11},e^{-12/q}]
.
In this case we can apply Lemma
2.4
. Let

E=\Big\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:(\exists k)|\{i\in[d]:|x_{i}|=k\}|\geq\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}N^{q}}{k^{6}}\Big\}.

Then by Lemma
2.4
we have

(2.14)

|E|\leq\sum_{2\leq k\leq N^{q}}\Big|\Big\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\{i\in[d]:|x_{i}|=k\}|\geq\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}N^{q}}{k^{6}}\Big\}\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{q}}|B_{N}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|.

Moreover, notice that for all

we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\\
|x_{i}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}^{d}x_{i}^{4}=\sum_{k\geq 2}k^{4}|\{i\in[d]:|x_{i}|=k\}|\leq\sum_{k\geq 2}k^{4}\cdot\frac{\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}N^{q}}{k^{6}}<\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}N^{q}.

The above combined with (
2.14
) gives

\Big|\Big\{x\in B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
|x_{i}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}^{d}x_{i}^{4}>\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/22}N^{q}\Big\}\Big|\leq|E|\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{q}}|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|.

Thus in any case conclusion of Lemma
2.5
holds.
∎

2.6
will be useful to combine various bounds coming from item (

) of Proposition

.

Lemma 2.6

(

)
.

Assume that we have a sequence
(u_{j}:j\in[d])

3.
Replacement by an auxiliary multiplier symbol

In this section using we first define
\beta_{n}^{J}
which is simpler multiplier than
m_{N}^{q}
defined in section
1.1
. Then we will obtain crucial bounds on
\beta_{n}^{J}
, which combined with lemmas of previous section will allow us to bound
m_{N}^{q}
. The ideas of this section are contained in the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 3.3]
. We give slightly refined approach, which appeared in very big generality in
[
10
, Sections 2,4]
.

Definition 3.1
.

For every
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
J\subseteq[d]
with
n\leq|J|
we define
\beta_{n}^{J}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
by the formula

\beta_{n}^{J}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|D_{n}^{J}|}\sum_{x\in D_{n}^{J}}e(x\cdot\xi),

where

D_{n}^{J}=\Big\{y\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d}:|\{i\in[d]:|y_{i}|=1\}|=n,\supp(y)\subseteq J\Big\}.

In order to better understand
\beta_{n}^{J}(\xi)
it is useful to recall definition and basic properties of Krawtchouk polynomials.

Definition 3.2
.

(Krawtchouk polynomial). For every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

k\in\{0,1,...,n\}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
we define
k
-th Krawtchouk polynomial by the formula

\mathds{kr}_{k}^{(n)}(x)=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{x}{j}\binom{n-x}{k-j},

if
x
is not integer, then we use Pochhammer symbol
\binom{x}{j}=\frac{x(x-1)...(x-j+1)}{j!}
.

Next theorem describes important facts regarding Krawtchouk polynomials.

Theorem 3.3
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and integers
x,k\in[0,n]
we have

(1)

Symmetry:
\mathds{kr}_{k}^{(n)}(x)=\mathds{kr}_{x}^{(n)}(k)
.

(2)

Reflection symmetry:
\mathds{kr}^{(n)}_{k}(n-x)=(-1)^{k}\mathds{kr}_{k}^{(n)}(x)
.

(3)

Uniform bound: There is a constant
c\in(0,1)
(independent of
n
) such that for any
x,k\leq n/2
we have

|\mathds{kr}^{(n)}_{k}(x)|\leq e^{-ckx/n}.

Proof of the first two points is simple and contained in
[
7
]
, the last point is
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
.
Now we can state two crucial bounds for the multiplier symbol
\beta_{n}^{J}
. From now on
c
will denote constant from the theorem above.

Proposition 3.4
.

For every
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
J\subseteq[d]
with
n\leq|J|/2
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
we have

|\beta_{n}^{J}(\xi)-1|\leq 2\frac{n}{|J|}\sum_{i\in J}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i}),

|\beta_{n}^{J}(\xi)|\leq 2e^{-\frac{cn}{2|J|}\min\big(\sum_{i\in J}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i}),\sum_{i\in J}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\big)}.

Proof.

We will prove item (
3.4
) first.
Notice that for any
\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle x\in D_{n}^{J}\iff\epsilon x\in D_{n}^{J},

where
\epsilon x=(\epsilon_{1}x_{1},...,\epsilon_{d}x_{d})
.
This implies that

\beta_{n}^{J}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|D_{n}^{J}|}\sum_{\epsilon x\in D_{n}^{J}}e(\epsilon x\cdot\xi)=\frac{1}{|D_{n}^{J}|}\sum_{x\in D_{n}^{J}}e(\epsilon x\cdot\xi).

Hence

\displaystyle\beta_{n}^{J}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{d}}\beta_{n}^{J}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{d}}\frac{1}{|D_{n}^{J}|}\sum_{x\in D_{n}^{J}}e(\epsilon x\cdot\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|D_{n}^{J}|}\sum_{x\in D_{n}^{J}}\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{d}}e(\epsilon x\cdot\xi)=\frac{1}{|D_{n}^{J}|}\sum_{x\in D_{n}^{J}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi x_{j}\xi_{j}).

Note that for any sequences of complex numbers
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d},\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
such that
\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|a_{j}|\leq 1
and
\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|b_{j}|\leq 1
we have

(3.1)

\Big|\prod_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}\Big|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}|a_{j}-b_{j}|,

this follows from the formula
\prod_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Big((a_{j}-b_{j})\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}a_{l}\prod_{k=j+1}^{d}b_{k}\Big).

Using (
3.1
) and the formula
\cos(2x)=1-2\sin^{2}(x)
we obtain

\displaystyle|\beta_{n}^{J}(\xi)-1|\leq\frac{1}{|D_{n}^{J}|}\sum_{x\in D_{n}^{J}}\Big|\prod_{j=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi x_{j}\xi_{j})-1\Big|\leq\frac{1}{|D_{n}^{J}|}\sum_{x\in D_{n}^{J}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\cos(2\pi x_{j}\xi_{j})-1|

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{|D_{n}^{J}|}\sum_{x\in D_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{j}\xi_{j})=\frac{2}{|D_{n}^{J}|}\sum_{x\in D_{n}^{J}}\sum_{j\in\supp(x)}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{|D_{n}^{J}|}\sum_{j\in J}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})|\{x\in D_{n}^{J}:|x_{j}|=1\}|=2\sum_{j\in J}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{j})\cdot\frac{2^{n}\binom{|J|-1}{n-1}}{2^{n}\binom{|J|}{n}}=2\frac{n}{|J|}\sum_{j\in J}\sin^{2}(2\pi\xi_{j}).

This finishes the proof of point (
3.4
) of the proposition.

Now we will prove point (
3.4
) of the proposition. We have

\displaystyle\beta_{n}^{J}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|D_{n}^{J}|}\sum_{x\in D_{n}^{J}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi x_{j}\xi_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n}\binom{|J|}{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq J,\\
|I|=n\end{subarray}}\prod_{i\in I}\cos(2\pi\xi_{i})\cdot|\{x\in D_{n}^{J}:\supp(x)=I\}|=\frac{1}{\binom{|J|}{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq J,\\
|I|=n\end{subarray}}\prod_{i\in I}\cos(2\pi\xi_{i}).

Fix
I\subseteq J
, let
\epsilon(I)\in\{-1,1\}^{J}
be such that
\epsilon(I)_{i}=-1
exactly when
i\in I
, then we have

\prod_{i\in I}\cos(2\pi\xi_{i})=\prod_{i\in J}\bigg(\frac{1+\cos(2\pi\xi_{i})}{2}+\epsilon(I)_{i}\frac{1-\cos(2\pi\xi_{i})}{2}\bigg)

=\prod_{i\in J}\big(\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})+\epsilon(I)_{i}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\big)=\sum_{U\subseteq J}w_{U}(\epsilon(I))\prod_{i\in J\setminus U}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\prod_{i\in U}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i}),

where we have defined
w_{U}:\{-1,1\}^{J}\to\{-1,1\}
by the formula
w_{U}(\epsilon)=\prod_{i\in U}\epsilon_{i}
. Notice that for any
U\subseteq J
the following holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\binom{|J|}{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq J,\\
|I|=n\end{subarray}}w_{U}(\epsilon(I))=\frac{1}{\binom{|J|}{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq J,\\
|I|=n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|U\cap I|}=\frac{1}{\binom{|J|}{n}}\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}\cdot|\{I\subseteq J:|I|=n,|U\cap I|=m\}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\binom{|J|}{n}}\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}\binom{|U|}{m}\binom{|J|-|U|}{n-m}=\mathds{kr}_{n}^{(|J|)}(|U|).

From the above we get that

(3.2)

\begin{split}&\beta_{n}^{J}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\binom{|J|}{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq J,\\
|I|=n\end{subarray}}\prod_{i\in I}\cos(2\pi\xi_{i})=\frac{1}{\binom{|J|}{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq J,\\
|I|=n\end{subarray}}\sum_{U\subseteq J}w_{U}(\epsilon(I))\prod_{i\in J\setminus U}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\prod_{i\in U}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\\
&=\hskip-5.69046pt\sum_{U\subseteq J}\prod_{i\in J\setminus U}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\prod_{i\in U}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\frac{1}{\binom{|J|}{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq J,\\
|I|=n\end{subarray}}w_{U}(\epsilon(I))=\hskip-5.69046pt\sum_{U\subseteq J}\prod_{i\in J\setminus U}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\prod_{i\in U}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\mathds{kr}_{n}^{(|J|)}(|U|).\end{split}

Now we will use Theorem
3.3
to bound
\mathds{kr}_{n}^{(|J|)}(|U|)
for every
U\subseteq J
. If
|U|\leq|J|/2
, then by the last point of Theorem
3.3
we have

|\mathds{kr}_{n}^{(|J|)}(|U|)|\leq e^{-\frac{cn|U|}{|J|}},

on the other hand if
|U|>|J|/2
, then using last two points of Theorem
3.3
we get

|\mathds{kr}_{n}^{(|J|)}(|U|)|=|\mathds{kr}_{n}^{(|J|)}(|J|-|U|)|\leq e^{-\frac{cn(|J|-|U|)}{|J|}}.

In either case we obtain

|\mathds{kr}_{n}^{(|J|)}(|U|)|\leq e^{-\frac{cn|U|}{|J|}}+e^{-\frac{cn(|J|-|U|)}{|J|}}.

Plugging the above into (
3.2
) we get

(3.3)

\begin{split}&|\beta_{n}^{J}(\xi)|\leq\sum_{U\subseteq J}\prod_{i\in J\setminus U}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\prod_{i\in U}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})|\mathds{kr}_{n}^{d}(|U|)|\\
&\leq\sum_{U\subseteq J}\Big(\prod_{i\in J\setminus U}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\prod_{i\in U}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big)e^{-\frac{cn|U|}{|J|}}+\sum_{U\subseteq J}\Big(\prod_{i\in J\setminus U}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\prod_{i\in U}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\Big)e^{-\frac{cn(|J|-|U|)}{|J|}}\\
&=\sum_{U\subseteq J}\prod_{i\in J\setminus U}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\prod_{i\in U}\big(\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})e^{-\frac{cn}{|J|}}\big)+\sum_{U\subseteq J}\prod_{i\in J\setminus U}\big(\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})e^{-\frac{cn}{|J|}}\big)\prod_{i\in U}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i}):=S_{1}+S_{2}.\end{split}

For
S_{1}
we have

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in J}\big(\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})+e^{-\frac{cn}{|J|}}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\big)=\prod_{i\in J}\big(1-(1-e^{-\frac{cn}{|J|}})\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i\in J}\exp\big(-(1-e^{-\frac{cn}{|J|}})\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})\big)\leq e^{-\frac{cn}{2|J|}\sum_{i\in J}\sin^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})},

above we have used two elementary inequalities
1-x\leq e^{-x}
and
xe^{-x/2}\leq 1-e^{-x}
for
x\geq 0
.
Similarly one can prove that

S_{2}\leq e^{-\frac{cn}{2|J|}\sum_{i\in J}\cos^{2}(\pi\xi_{i})},

combining these bounds on
S_{1},S_{2}
with (
3.3
) we get the desired conclusion.
∎

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\Bigg|\sum_{z\in Z(E)}(-1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{d}z_{j}}\hskip-14.22636pt\sum_{n/2<k\leq n-\|z\|_{q}^{q}}\hskip-14.22636pt(-1)^{k}\bigg(\hskip-14.22636pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d},\\
|\{i\in[d]:|y_{i}|=1\}|=k,\\
\supp(y)\cap\supp(z)=\emptyset\end{subarray}}e(y\cdot\eta)-1\bigg)\Bigg|+\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|B_{N}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\sum_{z\in Z(E)}\sum_{n/2<k\leq n-\|z\|_{q}^{q}}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d},\\
|\{i\in[d]:|y_{i}|=1\}|=k,\\
\supp(y)\cap\supp(z)=\emptyset\end{subarray}}e(y\cdot\eta)-1\bigg|+\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\|\eta\|^{2}s_{N}^{q}(0)+\frac{1}{n}\leq\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\|\xi+1/2\|^{2}+\frac{1}{n},

this finishes the proof (
3.18
), hence the proof of (
3.6
), thus finishes the entire proof of Proposition
3.5
.
∎

4.
Conclusions

In this section by using Proposition
3.5
we will conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We begin by defining two auxiliary multipliers

Definition 4.1
.

For any
q\geq 1
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
t>0
we define
\lambda_{t}^{1,q},\lambda_{t}^{2,q}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
by following formulas

\lambda^{1,q}_{t}(\xi)=e^{-\kappa_{q}(d,t)^{q}\|\xi\|^{2}},

\lambda^{2,q}_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|B_{t}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\Big(\sum_{x\in B_{t}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}}\Big)e^{-\kappa_{q}(d,t)^{q}\|\xi+1/2\|^{2}}.

Proof of the following theorem is a consequence of the theory of symmetric diffusion semigroups, see
[
11
, p. 73]
and
[
10
, Theorem 2.11]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
q\geq 1,d\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
the following inequality holds

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}\big|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\lambda_{t}^{1,q}\widehat{f})\big|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

Notice that
\lambda_{t}^{2,q}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|B_{t}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\Big(\sum_{x\in B_{t}^{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}}\Big)\lambda_{t}^{1,q}(\xi+\frac{1}{2})
, where
\xi+\frac{1}{2}=(\xi_{1}+\frac{1}{2},...,\xi_{d}+\frac{1}{2})
. Because of this we have

\sup_{\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=1}\Big\|\sup_{t>0}\big|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\lambda_{t}^{2,q}\widehat{f})\big|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=\sup_{\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=1}\Big\|\sup_{t>0}\big|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\lambda_{t}^{1,q}\widehat{f})\big|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

It turns out multipliers
\lambda_{N}^{1,q},\lambda^{2,q}_{N}
are good approximations to
m_{N}^{q}
, this is stated below.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
q\geq 1
,
d,N\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
\kappa_{q}(d,N)\leq e^{-12/q}
. Then for every
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
we have

(1)

If
\|\xi\|\leq\|\xi+1/2\|
, then

|m_{N}^{q}(\xi)-\lambda^{1,q}_{N}(\xi)|\lesssim\min\bigg(\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\|\xi\|^{2},\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\|\xi\|^{2}\Big)^{-1}\bigg)+\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/50}+\frac{1}{N^{q}}.

(2)

If
\|\xi+1/2\|<\|\xi\|
, then

|m_{N}^{q}(\xi)-\lambda^{2,q}_{N}(\xi)|\lesssim\min\bigg(\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\|\xi+1/2\|^{2},\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q}\|\xi+1/2\|^{2}\Big)^{-1}\bigg)+\kappa_{q}(d,N)^{q/50}+\frac{1}{N^{q}}.

Proof of the proposition above is a simple consequence of Proposition
3.5
together with elementary inequalities
1-e^{-x}\leq x
,
e^{-x}\leq x^{-1}
for
x>0
.
Using Proposition
4.3
and a standard square function argument we can finally prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Take any
q\geq 1
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. We decompose
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where

\supp(\widehat{f_{1}})\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\|\xi\|\leq\|\xi+1/2\|)\},\quad\supp(\widehat{f_{2}})\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\|\xi\|>\|\xi+1/2\|\}.

We will prove the following

(4.1)

\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{q}f|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})},

since
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{q}
has
\ell^{2}
norm at most
1
and we have
|\mathbb{D}\cap(e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d,d])|\leq|\mathbb{D}\cap(e^{-12}d,d])|\leq\frac{12}{\log 2}+1
, to establish Theorem
1.4
is suffices to prove (
4.1
). In fact we will show that for
i=1,2
we have

(4.2)

\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{q}f_{i}|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\lesssim\|f_{i}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

We will consider only the case
i=1
, for
i=2
proof is very similar. By Theorem
4.2
we have

(4.3)

\begin{split}&\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{q}f_{1}|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\lambda_{t}^{1,q}\widehat{f_{1}})|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\\
&+\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((m_{t}^{q}-\lambda_{t}^{1,q})\widehat{f_{1}}\big)|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}+\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((m_{t}^{q}-\lambda_{t}^{1,q})\widehat{f_{1}}\big)|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.\end{split}

For the second term of the above we use a standard square function argument, i.e. Parseval’s identity together with Proposition
4.3
and assumptions on
\supp(\widehat{f_{1}})
. We get

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((m_{t}^{q}-\lambda_{t}^{1,q})\widehat{f_{1}}\big)|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq\Big\|\Big(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((m_{t}^{q}-\lambda_{t}^{1,q})\widehat{f_{1}}\big)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\hskip-14.22636pt\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((m_{t}^{q}-\lambda_{t}^{1,q})\widehat{f_{1}}\big)\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}=\Big(\hskip-14.22636pt\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|m_{t}^{q}(\xi)-\lambda_{t}^{1,q}(\xi)|^{2}|\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\bigg(\hskip-14.22636pt\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}\hskip-14.22636pt\min\bigg(\kappa_{q}(d,t)^{q}\|\xi\|^{2},\Big(\kappa_{q}(d,t)^{q}\|\xi\|^{2}\Big)^{-1}\bigg)^{2}+\hskip-19.91684pt\sum_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}\hskip-19.91684pt\big(\frac{1}{t^{2q}}+\kappa_{q}(d,t)^{q/25}\big)\bigg)d\xi\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)|^{2}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\min\bigg(\frac{2^{kq}}{d}\|\xi\|^{2},\Big(\frac{2^{kq}}{d}\|\xi\|^{2}\Big)^{-1}\bigg)^{2}+1+\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\log_{2}(e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q})\rfloor}\frac{2^{kq/25}}{d}\bigg)d\xi\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\bigg)^{1/2}=\|\widehat{f_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\|f_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})},

above we have used the fact that for any
a\geq 2
and
x\geq 0
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\min(a^{k}x,a^{-k}x^{-1})\lesssim 1,

where the implied constant is universal. The above with (
4.3
) establishes (
4.2
) for
i=1
, as mentioned earlier the case
i=2
is basically the same, but with
\lambda_{t}^{1,q}
replaced by
\lambda_{t}^{2,q}
, so we skip its proof. Using triangle inequality, (
4.2
) and Parseval’s formula we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{q}f|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{q}f_{1}|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}+\Big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{D},t\leq e^{-\frac{12}{q}}d^{1/q}}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{q}f_{2}|\Big\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}+\|f_{2}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=\|\widehat{f_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}+\|\widehat{f_{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})},

this concludes the proof of (
4.1
) and hence the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

The strong law of large numbers and a functional central limit theorem for general Markov additive processes
A. E. Kyprianou, V. Rivero

Abstract
In this note we re-visit the fundamental question of the strong law of large numbers and central limit theorem for processes in continuous time with conditional stationary and independent increments. For convenience we refer to them as Markov additive processes, or MAPs for short. Historically used in the setting of queuing theory, MAPs have often been written about when the underlying modulating process is an ergodic Markov chain on a finite state space, cf. [ 3 , 4 ] not to mention the classical contributions of Prabhu [ 28 , 30 ] . Recent works have addressed the strong law of large numbers when the underlying modulating process is a general Markov processes; cf. [ 22 , 36 ] . We add to the latter with a different approach based on an ergodic theorem for additive functionals and on the semi-martingale structure of the additive part. This approach also allows us to deal with the setting that the modulator of the MAP is either positive or null recurrent. The methodology additionally inspires a CLT-type result. Key words: Markov additive processes, strong law of large numbers, central limit theorem. MSC 2020: 60J80, 60E10.

Introduction

Suppose
((\xi_{t},\Theta_{t}),t\geq 0)
is a bivariate coordinate process on
{\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{S}}

\mathcal{S}

\left((\xi,\Theta),\mathbf{P}\right)=\left((\xi_{t},\Theta_{t},t\geq 0),\mathcal{F}_{\infty},(\mathcal{F}_{t},t\geq 0),\{\mathbf{P}_{x,\theta}:(x,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{S}\}\right),

\mathbf{P}_{x,\theta}\left(\xi_{0}=x,\Theta_{0}=\theta\right)=1
. Here
(\mathcal{F}_{t},t\geq 0)
is the minimal augmented and right continuous admissible filtration, and
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}=\bigvee_{t=0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{t}

Definition 1

The process

For a MAP

(\xi,\Theta)
.

(H1)

The process
((\xi_{t},\Theta_{t}),t\geq 0)
is a

Fundamentally the ordinate of a MAP has similar qualitative long-time behaviour to Lévy processes and random walks, and extends that of additive functionals of Markov processes, the most simple of which is the strong law of large numbers. That is, the almost sure existence of the limit
\lim_{t\to\infty}\xi_{t}/t

In addition to (H1), a reasonable assumption in order to derive a law of large numbers is that the underlying modulator process

3
Main results

Having discussed various aspects of the semimartingale representation of

, we can now state our main results.

Theorem 1

(Strong law of large numbers for MAP)
.

Suppose
\pi
is an infinite measure, that is,
\Theta
is null-recurrent, then under the assumptions (H1-4), and (H6-8), we have that

\frac{\xi_{t}}{A_{t}}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}1\text{ and }\frac{A_{t}}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}0

(14)

if
m_{1}\neq 0
and otherwise if
m_{1}=0
then

\frac{\xi_{t}}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}0\text{ and }\frac{A_{t}}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}0.

(15)

When
\pi
is a probability measure, that is when
\Theta
is positive recurrent, then under the assumptions (H1-4) and (H7), we have that

\frac{\xi_{t}}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}\mathbf{E}_{0,\pi}[\xi_{1}]\text{ and }\frac{A_{t}}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}\mathbf{E}_{0,\pi}[\xi_{1}].

(16)

There are a number of remarks we can make about the above SLLN. First, the main contribution is the setting that
\pi
is an infinite measure. Indeed, as we will see from the proof, it is relatively straightforward to deduce the second part of the theorem from what is already known in Proposition 2.15 of
[
22
]
.

Second, the assumption (H7) is the natural extension of the usual assumption for Lévy processes for the SLLN to hold. In the Lévy setting, the assumption (H6) is automatically satisfied. In the setting of MAPs some control of the volatility and linear terms is required in the long run, and this involves the stationary regime of the modulator. In essence, the conditions ensure that there are no states of the modulator that would lead to an extreme variation in the ordinator, causing the SLLN not to hold.

Following the SLLN it is natural to ask whether a central limit theorem holds. To answer this question positively, we need to recall the definition of a Mittag-Leffler process.

For
\alpha\in(0,1),
let
\sigma^{(\alpha)}
be an
\alpha
-stable subordinator, normalized so to have

\log\mathbf{E}[e^{-\sigma^{(\alpha)}_{1}}]=1.

For
\alpha=1
we take the pure drift process
\sigma^{(1)}_{t}=t,

t\geq 0.
For any
\alpha\in(0,1],
we define the process of the first passage time of
\sigma^{(\alpha)}
by taking its right-continuous inverse

W^{(\alpha)}_{t}=\inf\{s>0:\sigma^{(\alpha)}_{s}>t\},\qquad t\geq 0.

We will refer to this process as a Mittage-Leffler process of index
\alpha\in(0,1].

We now have all the elements required to state our CLT.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that (H1-4) and (H6-8) hold with
p=1.
Moreover, we assume the so-called Lindenberg condition that, for all
\epsilon>0
,

\frac{1}{t}\int_{(0,t]\times\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}L({\rm d}u,{\rm d}x)x^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|>\epsilon\sqrt{t}\}}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}0.

(17)

In addition, we assume that
there is a regularly varying function
h:(0,\infty)\mapsto(0,\infty)
at infinity, with some index
\alpha\in(0,1],
such that for every measurable function
g:\mathcal{S}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
0<\pi(g)<\infty,

\mathbf{E}_{0,\theta}\left[\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-s/t}g(\Theta_{s}){\rm d}s\right]\sim h(t)\pi(g),\qquad t\to\infty;

for
\pi
-almost all
\theta\in\mathcal{S};

With all these assumptions in place, we have that, under
\mathbf{P}_{0,\theta},
there is the following convergence in the sense of Skorohod’s topology

\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{h(n)}}\left(\xi_{nt}-A_{nt}\right),\ t\geq 0\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\left(\left(\nu_{\langle\xi\rangle}+2J\right)^{1/2}\Sigma_{W^{(\alpha)}_{t}},\ t\geq 0\right);

where
A
is defined in
(
13
)
,
\Sigma_{W^{(\alpha)}}
is the anomalous diffusion obtained by the subordination of
\Sigma,
a standard Brownian motion, subordinated by an independent Mittag-Leffler process of parameter
\alpha,

W^{(\alpha)},
and

J:=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\pi({\rm d}\theta)\int_{\mathcal{S}}K(\theta,{\rm d}\beta)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\nu_{\theta,\beta}({\rm d}x)x\right)^{2}<\infty.

(18)

Lindenberg’s condition is commonly required when establishing CLT outside the i.i.d. case. Fundamentally, MAPs support two main sources of randomness, that is
\xi
and
\Theta
. To control either, one needs some moment conditions, such as (H8), and the recurrence structure of
\Theta.
We have allowed
\Theta
to be either positive recurrent or null recurrent. This determines the time it takes for
\Theta
to reach stability and complete a recurrence epoch. Such recurrent epochs are intimately connected to the increments of
\xi
and we need to have a good understanding of them. The condition on the regular variation of the resolvent of
\Theta
is a version of the Darling-Kac condition, introduced in
[
16
]
extending
[
12
]
, that allows one to ensure that the sum of recurrence epochs is in the domain of attraction of a stable subordinator. The Mittag-Leffler clock appears from estimating the rate of increase of the number of recurrence epochs completed as time goes to infinity. Different behaviour arises in the positive recurrent case and the null recurrent case.

When the process is positive recurrent,
\pi
is finite, the assumptions in the latter Theorem are satisfied with
h(t)=t,
for all
t\geq 0,
and hence
\alpha=1
, see e.g.
[
16
]
Theorem 3.1 and the discussion following it. This can be easily seen by decomposing into excursions, but for brevity we omit the details. Thus, the assumption in the previous theorem is only required when the process is null-recurrent, in that case, one might have or not the existence of the norming function
h,
in general it will be related to the intensity rate at which long recurrence epochs appear. For background on the Darling-Kac theory we refer to
[
12
,
5
,
6
,
16
]
.

The proof of Theorem

e^{iy}-1-iy=a_{1}(y)\frac{y^{2}}{2},\qquad e^{iy}-1-iy-\frac{(iy)^{2}}{2}=a_{2}(y)\frac{|y|^{3}}{6},\qquad\text{for any},\ y\in\mathbb{R};

and

\left|e^{iy}-1-iy\right|=O(y^{2}),\qquad y>1;

see e.g. Lemma 8.6 in
[
32
]
. Recall the random measure
L
defined in
(
4
)
. Using this notation, and the above facts, we write
M^{(1)}
as

\begin{split}M^{(1)}_{t}&=\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda^{2}C_{nt}}{h(n)}+\int_{(0,nt]\times\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}L({\rm d}s,{\rm d}x)\left(e^{\frac{i\lambda x}{\sqrt{h(n)}}}-1-\frac{i\lambda x}{\sqrt{h(n)}}\right)\right\}.\end{split}

(31)

In order to deal with
M^{(2)}
and
M^{(3)}
, we must first make some additional observations. First, by the Ergodic Theorem of X.3.12 of
[
31
]
, and a calculation similar to the one in
(
11
)
, we obtain that for any
\epsilon>0

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\langle\xi\rangle_{nt}}\left(-\lambda^{2}C_{nt}+\int_{(0,nt]\times\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}L({\rm d}s,{\rm d}x)\left(e^{\frac{i\lambda x}{\sqrt{h(n)}}}-1-\frac{i\lambda x}{\sqrt{h(n)}}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x\lambda|\leq\epsilon\sqrt{h(n)}\}}\right)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\to\infty}}{{\sim}}\left(\frac{-\lambda^{2}C_{nt}-\lambda^{2}\int_{(0,nt]}L({\rm d}s,{\rm d}x)x^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\lambda x|\leq\epsilon\sqrt{h(n)}\}}}{\langle\xi\rangle_{nt}}\right)\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

(32)

Second, due to the Lindenberg condition
(
17
)
, we also have

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{h(nt)}\left(\int_{(0,nt]\times\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}L({\rm d}s,{\rm d}x)\left(e^{\frac{i\lambda x}{\sqrt{h(n)}}}-1-\frac{i\lambda x}{\sqrt{h(n)}}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x\lambda|>\epsilon\sqrt{h(n)}\}}\right)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\sim}}\left(\frac{-\lambda^{2}\int_{(0,nt]}L({\rm d}s,{\rm d}x)x^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\lambda x|>\epsilon\sqrt{h(n)}\}}}{h(n)}\right)\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}0.\end{split}

(33)

Third, the process
Z
defined in
(
30
)
is a square integrable martingale, whose angle bracket
\langle Z\rangle
and square bracket
[Z]
processes are additive functionals of
\Theta.
(Recall that
[Z]
is the compensator of
Z^{2}
and
\langle Z\rangle
is the predictable compensator of
[Z]
.) Indeed, we can use the compensation formula for Lévy systems, Jensen’s inequality, and (H8), to write

[Z]_{t}=\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}\nu_{(\Theta_{s-},\Theta_{s})}(dx)x\right)^{2},\qquad t\geq 0;

\langle Z\rangle_{t}=\int^{t}_{0}{\rm d}s\int_{\mathcal{S}}K(\Theta_{s},{\rm d}\beta)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\nu_{(\Theta_{s},\beta)}({\rm d}x)x\right)^{2},\qquad t\geq 0;

and

\begin{split}\mathbf{E}_{0,\theta}\left[[Z]_{t}\right]=&\mathbf{E}_{0,\theta}\left[\int^{t}_{0}{\rm d}s\int_{\mathcal{S}}K(\Theta_{s},{\rm d}\beta)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\nu_{(\Theta_{s},\beta)}({\rm d}x)x\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\leq\mathbf{E}_{0,\theta}\left[\int^{t}_{0}{\rm d}s\int_{\mathcal{S}}K(\Theta_{s},{\rm d}\beta)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\nu_{(\Theta_{s},\beta)}({\rm d}x)x^{2}\right]\\
&=\mathbf{E}_{0,\theta}\left[\int^{t}_{0}{\rm d}s\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Pi(\Theta_{s},\mathcal{S}\setminus\{\Theta_{s}\},{\rm d}x)x^{2}\right]<\infty.\end{split}

The Theorem 3.16 in
[
16
]
implies that we have the joint convergence in the sense of Skorohod’s topology

\begin{split}\left(\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{h(n)}}Z_{tn},\,\frac{1}{h(n)}\langle Z\rangle_{nt}\right),\ t\geq 0\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{Law}}\left(\left(J^{1/2}B_{W^{(\alpha)}_{t}},\,JW^{(\alpha)}_{t}\right),\ t\geq 0\right);\end{split}

(34)

where
J
is as defined in
(
18
)
. Also, by the Ergodic Theorem of X.3.12 of
[
31
]
and Slutsky’s Theorem we have that we can extend the above joint convergence to

\begin{split}&\left(\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{h(n)}}Z_{tn},\,\frac{1}{h(n)}\langle Z\rangle_{nt},\,\frac{\langle\xi\rangle_{nt}}{h(n)}\right),\ t\geq 0\right)\\
&=\left(\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{h(n)}}Z_{tn},\,\frac{1}{h(n)}\langle Z\rangle_{nt},\,\frac{\langle Z\rangle_{nt}}{h(n)}\frac{\langle\xi\rangle_{nt}}{\langle Z\rangle_{nt}},\right),\ t\geq 0\right)\\
&\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{Law}}\left(\left(J^{1/2}B_{W^{(\alpha)}_{t}},\,JW^{(\alpha)}_{t},\,J\times\frac{||\nu_{\langle\xi\rangle}||}{J}W^{(\alpha)}_{t}\right),\ t\geq 0\right).\end{split}

(35)

Now, to determine the limit of
M^{(1)}
,
we use
(
32
)
and the third component of the limit in
(
35
)
, together with
(
33
)

To determine the limit of
M^{(2)}
we recall that Taylor’s representation of the logarithm allows to write

\log(1-z)+z=-\frac{z^{2}}{2}+O(|z|^{3}),\qquad|z|<1.

Hence, arguing as in
(
32
)
and
(
33
)
, appealing to the second element of
(
35
)
, we can see that

\begin{split}M^{(2)}_{t}&=\exp\Bigg\{\sum_{s\leq nt}\Bigg[\log\left(1-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(1-e^{\frac{i\lambda x}{\sqrt{h(n)}}}\right)\nu_{(\Theta_{s-},\Theta_{s})}({\rm d}x)\right)\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(1-e^{\frac{i\lambda x}{\sqrt{h(n)}}}\right)\nu_{(\Theta_{s-},\Theta_{s})}({\rm d}x)\Bigg]\Bigg\}\\
&\sim\exp\left\{-\frac{\langle Z\rangle_{nt}}{2h(n)}\frac{(-\lambda^{2})}{\langle Z\rangle_{nt}}\sum_{s\leq nt}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{i\lambda x}{\sqrt{h(n)}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-e^{\frac{i\lambda x}{\sqrt{h(n)}}}\right)x\nu_{(\Theta_{s-},\Theta_{s})}({\rm d}x)\right]^{2}\right\}\\
&\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda^{2}J}{2}W^{(\alpha)}_{t}\right\}.\end{split}

Finally, to determine the limit of
M^{(3)}
, we can use the first element of
(
35
)
.

Assembling the pieces we derive the limit

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{E}_{0,\theta}\left[\exp\left\{i\lambda\frac{\xi_{nt}-A_{nt}}{\sqrt{h(n)}}\right\}\right]\\
&=\mathbf{E}_{0,\pi}\left[\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\left(||\nu_{\langle\xi\rangle}||+J\right)W^{(\alpha)}_{t}+i\lambda J^{1/2}B_{W^{(\alpha)}_{t}}\right\}\right]\\
&=\mathbf{E}_{0,\pi}\left[\exp\left\{i\lambda\left(||\nu_{\langle\xi\rangle}||+J\right)^{1/2}\Sigma^{(1)}_{W^{(\alpha)}_{t}}+i\lambda J^{1/2}\Sigma^{(2)}_{W^{(\alpha)}_{t}}\right\}\right];\end{split}

where
\Sigma^{(1)}
and
\Sigma^{(2)}
are two independent standard Brownian motions, which are independent of the Mittag-Leffler process
W^{(\alpha)}
. The identification of the limit follows by conditioning on
W,
and adding the variances of the two Gaussian processes.

Tightness follows from the Theorem 4.13 in Chapter VI Section 4 in
[
17
]
. Indeed, according to that result, it is enough to prove that the quadratic variation process of the martingale

\frac{1}{\sqrt{h(n)}}(\xi_{nt}-A_{nt}),\qquad t\geq 0,

C
-tight. That is, every convergent subsequence converges to an a.s. continuous process. But this is a direct consequence of the convergence in
(
34
)

W^{(\alpha)}
is non-decreasing and continuous almost surely.

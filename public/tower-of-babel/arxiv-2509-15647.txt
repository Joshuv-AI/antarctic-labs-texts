In this paper, we obtain a local limit theorem for the Kemperman’s model of oscillating random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
; it extends the existing results for classical random walks on
\mathbb{Z}
or reflected random walks on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The key technical point is to control the long-term behavior of the embedding subprocess that characterizes the oscillations of the original random walk between
\mathbb{Z}^{-}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
in both recurrent and transient cases. Then by combining an extension of
[
Gou11
, Theorem 1.4]
for the convergence of aperiodic sequence of renewal operators acting on a suitable functional Banach space and the decomposition of the trajectories of the random walk, we obtain the exact asymptotic for the return probability under some mild assumptions on the increment moments.

Keywords and phrases: local limit theorem, oscillating random walk, Doob’s transform, aperiodic sequence of operators

1
Introduction

It is a general fact that the heat kernel on a Riemannian manifold
M
is a smooth function on
(0,+\infty)\times M\times M,(t,x,y)\mapsto p(t,x,y)

whose shape depends on the properties of
M
. In
[
SC22
]
, L. Saloff-Coste pays particular attention to the long-time, large-scale behavior of the heat kernel and its relation to the global geometry of
M
. To answer to this general question, L. Saloff-Coste needs tools to obtain sharp two-sided estimates for the heat kernel in terms of the time variable
t>0
and basic geometric quantities depending on
x,y\in M
. He studies in particular the case of manifolds made of “nice pieces" and propose a strategy of “reconstruction" to describe the behavior of the heat kernel on the whole manifold; this strategy can be extended to manifolds with finitely many ends.

Similar questions are relevant in the case of discrete structures
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{X}_{0}\cup\mathbb{X}_{1}\cup\ldots\cup\mathbb{X}_{\ell}
with finitely many ends
\mathbb{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{X}_{\ell},
where
\mathbb{X}_{0}
is finite and
\ell\geq 2
. We consider an irreducible Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{X}
with transition probability
P=(p(x,y))_{x,y\in\mathbb{X}}
. The aim here is to describe the asymptotic behavior as
n\to+\infty
of the probabilities
p^{(n)}(x,y)
, assuming that we have precise estimates of the quantities
\mathbb{P}[X_{n}=y\mid X_{0}=x,X_{1}\ldots,X_{n}\in\mathbb{X}_{i}]
for any
1\leq i\leq\ell
.

The simplest example of a such structure is the “oscillating random walk" on
\mathbb{Z}
introduced by J. H. B. Kemperman in 1974
[
Kem74
]
and defined by

X_{0}=x\quad\text{and}\quad X_{n+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cll}X_{n}+\xi_{n+1}&if\quad X_{n}\leq-1,\\
X_{n}+\eta_{n+1}&if\quad X_{n}=0,\\
X_{n}+\xi^{\prime}_{n+1}&if\quad X_{n}\geq 1,\end{array}\right.\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 0,

(1)

where
x
is a fixed point in
\mathbb{Z}
and
(\xi_{i})_{i\geq 1},(\eta_{i})_{i\geq 1}
and

(\xi^{\prime}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are independent iid sequences of
\mathbb{Z}
-valued random variables with respective distributions
\mu,\mu_{0}
and
\mu^{\prime}
. This Markov chain models discrete-time diffusions in one-dimensional space with three different media
\mathbb{Z}^{*-},\{0\}
and
\mathbb{Z^{*+}}
. Whenever the process
\mathcal{X}=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
lies on the negative half-line
\mathbb{Z}^{*-}
, its trajectory is directed by the left distribution
\mu
until it quits
\mathbb{Z}^{*-}
; once it visits
\{0\}
or enters the positive half-line
\mathbb{Z}^{*+}
, its trajectory is directed respectively by
\mu_{0}
and the right distribution
\mu^{\prime}
until quitting these sets… and so on.
When
\mu=\mu_{0}=\mu^{\prime}
, the process
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a classical random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
whose behavior is well known.

J. H. B. Kemperman obtained an explicit general necessary and sufficient condition for the recurrence of the oscillating random walk. B. A. Rogozin and S. G. Foss obtained in
[
RF78
]
sufficient
conditions for recurrence if the distributions
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
belong
to the region of attraction of stable laws.
Recently, J. Brémont proposed in
[
Br23
]
a straight line and self-contained exposition of these results and T. D. Vo
[
Vo23
]
obtained a sufficient condition in terms of moment of
\xi_{1}
and
\xi^{\prime}_{1}
. B. A. Rogozin and S. G. Foss also gave examples
illustrating that the oscillating random walk can be transient even in the case when
\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{1}|]
and
\mathbb{E}[|\xi^{\prime}_{1}|]
are both finite and
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]=\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]=0
, in contrast to the classical random walk. To recover the recurrence property, T. D. Vo simply reinforces the moment hypotheses and assume that
\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{1}|^{1+p}]+\mathbb{E}[|\xi^{\prime}_{1}|^{1+q}]<+\infty
with
p+q\geq 1
. This stronger hypothesis is not necessary of course, as demonstrated by the classical random walk; nevertheless, as soon as
p+q<1
, there exist examples of transient “doubly centered" oscillating random walks (see for instance
[
PW11
]
).

Note that among the oscillating random walks, we find the unfolded version on
\mathbb{Z}
of the reflected walk on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
which has been the subject of intense studies for a few decades
[
PE15
]
and is a basic and important model of stochastic dynamical systems
[
PW11
]
. Recently, G. Alsmeyer, S. Brofferio and D. Buraczewski
[
ABB23
]
studied one dimensional iterated functions systems generated by asymptotically linear maps with possibly different slopes at
-\infty
and
+\infty
; these systems are closed to classical affine iterations, but the analysis differs according to the sign and the size of the slopes at infinity. Their results present analogies with the present article with a competition between the two slopes at infinity.

Let us recall briefly some known limit theorems for classical random walks
(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{Z}
, reflected random walks
(R_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and oscillating random walks on
\mathbb{Z}
. First of all, in the spirit of the Donsker’s invariance principle for classical random walks, functional limit theorems have been obtained for the reflected and oscillating random walks. Under quite general assumptions, the process
(R_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, properly rescaled, converges towards the reflected Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{+}

[
NP21
]
while the oscillating random walk
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
converges to a skew Brownian motion whose parameter can be expressed in terms of renewal functions associated with the random walks of step distributions
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}

[
PV23
]
.
Concerning the local limit theorem for (irreducible and aperiodic) classical or reflected random walks, the spatial inhomogeneity introduced by the reflection at
0
in the second case means that the asymptotic behavior of the return probabilities is markedly different from one case to the other.

\bullet

Classical random walks
(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{Z}
.

If
\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{1}|^{2}]<+\infty
and
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]=0
then there exists a strictly positive constant
c
which does not depend on
x
and
y
such that
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[S_{n}=y]\sim c/\sqrt{n}
as
n\to+\infty

[
Spi64
]
.
When
\xi_{1}
is not centered, assuming that
\mathbb{E}[e^{t\xi_{1}}]<+\infty
for
t
sufficiently large, there exists a strictly positive constant
c=c(x-y)
such that
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[S_{n}=y]\sim c\rho^{n}/\sqrt{n}
as
n\to+\infty
, where
\rho
is the minimum of the Laplace transform
t\mapsto\mathbb{E}[e^{t\xi_{1}}]
. These results are based on Fourier analysis and a classical Doob’s transform to bring back to the centered case.

\bullet

Reflected random walks
(R_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
The process
(R_{n})_{n\geq 0}

is defined recursively by

R_{0}=x\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
R_{n+1}=|R_{n}-\xi_{n+1}|
for any
n\geq 0
. The local limit theorem for the process
(R_{n})_{n\geq 0}
has been studied in
[
PE15
]
under the hypothesis
\mathbb{E}[e^{t\xi_{1}}]<+\infty
for
t
sufficiently large. If
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]=0
then there exists a strictly positive constant
c
depending on
y
such that
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[S_{n}=y]\sim c\rho^{n}/\sqrt{n}
as
n\to+\infty
, where
\rho
is the minimum of the Laplace transform
t\mapsto\mathbb{E}[e^{t\xi_{1}}]
.
When
\xi_{1}
is not centered, there exists
c=c(x,y)>0
such that
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[S_{n}=y]\sim c\rho^{n}/n^{3/2}
as
n\to+\infty
. Notice that in the centered case, the constant
c
does not depend on the starting point
x
while in the non centered case it depends on both
x
and
y
, and not only on the difference
x-y
as for classical random walks. These results are based on Darboux’s method, via the study of the singularity of certain functions of the complex variable, with operator values.

In the present paper, we focus on the asymptotic behavior of the quantity
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]
for oscillating random walks on
\mathbb{Z}
with three media
\mathbb{Z}^{*-},\{0\}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{*+}
. Somewhat surprisingly, it is when the two classical random walks have a drift of the same sign that there are quite complex interactions between them. Even the modest perturbation of the transitions from a finite set of sites (for instance transitions from the origin) would reveal other possible interactions, making the analysis more complex. Our approach is based on the analysis of the transition probability of the “switching subprocess" of the oscillating random walk, which controls the transitions from one medium
\mathbb{Z}^{*-},\{0\}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{*+}
to another one. This strategy is quite flexible and can be adapted to other processes, for instance “perturbed random walks with a membrane"
[
PP12
]
which is a quite active area for one decade. The same strategy is also valid in the case of oscillating random walks on
\mathbb{R}
; when one of the distributions
\mu,\mu^{\prime}
or
\mu_{0}
is spread out, the transition operator of the switching subprocess falls within the scope of quasi-compact operators.

Description of the model and hypotheses

Let
(\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1},(\eta_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(\xi^{\prime}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be independent sequences of iid
\mathbb{Z}
-valued random variables, defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and with respective distributions
\mu,\mu_{0}
and
\mu^{\prime}
.
The
oscillating random walk

\mathcal{X}=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{Z}
with jump distributions
\mu,\mu_{0}
and
\mu^{\prime}
is defined recursively by (
1
).
To emphasize the dependence in
\mu,\mu_{0}
and
\mu^{\prime}
of this process, we write
\mathcal{X}(\mu,\mu_{0},\mu^{\prime})
.

We set
D:=\sup\{x\geq 1\mid\mu(x)>0\},D^{\prime}:=\inf\{x\leq-1\mid\mu^{\prime}(x)>0\},D_{0}^{+}:=\sup\{x\geq 1\mid\mu_{0}(x)>0\}
,
D_{0}^{-}:=\inf\{x\leq-1\mid\mu_{0}(x)>0\}
and introduce the following assumptions:

\bf O1-

(\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1},(\eta_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(\xi^{\prime}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
are independent sequences of iid
\mathbb{Z}
-valued random variables
.

\bf O2-

Both distributions
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
are strongly aperiodic on
\mathbb{Z}
.

{\bf O3-}\ DD^{\prime}\leq-2.

Hypothesis
O3
implies in particular that the supports of
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
are neither included in
\mathbb{Z}^{-}
nor in
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. This last condition is weaker since it is equivalent to
DD^{\prime}\leq-1
; it is also classical (and as far as we know, it is indispensable) in the theory of random walks in order to obtain local limit theorems in the non centered case. In subsection
5.1
, we explore briefly the case
DD^{\prime}=-1
. It is simpler since the successive excursions within the different media are independent in this case. The hypothesis
{\bf O3}
breaks this independence and ensures that the oscillating random walk can jump, at least in one direction, from one half line to the other without visiting
0
.

{\bf O4}-\mu_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{*-})\mu_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{*+})>0

(in particular
\mu_{0}(0)<1
).

By
[
Vo23
]
, under hypotheses
O1
,
O2
and
O3
, the process
\mathcal{X}
is irreducible on
\mathbb{Z}
.
We explore here the asymptotic behavior as
n\to+\infty
of the quantity
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]
, under a varied set of conditions of moment on
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
. Up to exchanging the roles of
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
, we have seven cases to explore, according to the fact that their means are negative
(N)
, positive
(P)
or zero
(Z)
.

In the cases
({\bf P,N})
,
({\bf Z,Z})
and
({\bf P,Z})
, the oscillating random walk is recurrent, while in the other cases, it is transient. We introduce some moment hypotheses.

For any
\mathbb{Z}
-valued random variable
\xi
, we set
{\xi}^{-}:=\max(0,-\xi)
and
{\xi}^{+}:=\max(0,\xi)
.

Moment conditions

Case

({\bf P,N})

where

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]>0

and

\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]<0,

with the following moment assumptions :

\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{1}|],\mathbb{E}[|\eta_{1}|],\mathbb{E}[|\xi^{\prime}_{1}|]<+\infty
.

Case

({\bf Z,Z})

where

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]=\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]=0,

with the following moment assumptions : there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}[|\eta_{1}|^{1+\delta_{0}}],\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}^{2}],\mathbb{E}[{({\xi^{+}_{1}})}^{3+\delta_{0}}],\mathbb{E}[{\xi^{\prime}_{1}}^{2}],\mathbb{E}[{({\xi^{\prime}_{1}}^{-})}^{3+\delta_{0}}]<+\infty.

Case

({\bf P,Z})

where

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]>0

and

\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]=0,

with the following moment assumptions : there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}[|\eta_{1}|^{1+\delta_{0}}],\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}^{2}],\mathbb{E}[{\xi^{\prime}_{1}}^{2}],\mathbb{E}[{(\xi_{1}^{+})}^{2+\delta_{0}}],\mathbb{E}[{({\xi^{\prime}_{1}}^{-})}^{3+\delta_{0}}]<+\infty.

Case

\bf(Z,P)

where

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]=0

and

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}^{\prime}]>0,

with the following moment assumptions : there exist
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\bullet\ \mathbb{E}[|\eta_{1}|^{1+\delta_{0}}],\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}^{2}],\mathbb{E}[(\xi^{+}_{1})^{3+\delta_{0}}]<+\infty,

\bullet\ \mathbb{E}[({\xi^{\prime}_{1}})^{2}],\mathbb{E}[({\xi^{\prime}_{1}}^{-})^{5/2+\delta_{0}}]<+\infty.

For the last two cases below, we assume that the random variables
\xi_{1}
and
\eta_{1}
have the same distribution; we explain why in section
7
. Hence only moment hypotheses of
\xi_{1}
and
\xi^{\prime}_{1}
are needed.

Case

\bf(N,P)

where

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]<0

and

\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]>0

with the following moment assumptions : there exist
\delta_{0},\lambda>0
and
\lambda^{\prime}<0
such that

\bullet\ \displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{(\lambda+\delta_{0})\xi_{1}}],\mathbb{E}[e^{(\lambda^{\prime}-\delta_{0})\xi^{\prime}_{1}}]<+\infty\quad and\quad\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}e^{\lambda\xi_{1}}]=\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}e^{\lambda^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}_{1}}]=0,

Case

\bf(P,P)

where

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]>0

and

\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]>0

with the following moment assumptions : there exist
\delta_{0}>0
and
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}<0
such that

\bullet\ \displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{(\lambda-\delta_{0})\xi_{1}}],\mathbb{E}[e^{(\lambda^{\prime}-\delta_{0})\xi^{\prime}_{1}}]<+\infty\quad and\quad\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}e^{\lambda\xi_{1}}]=\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}e^{\lambda^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}_{1}}]=0.

By
[
Vo23
]
, in the case
({\bf P,N)}
, the oscillating random walk
\mathcal{X}
is positive recurrent and the classical theory of denumerable Markov chains yields

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]\longrightarrow\nu(y)\quad\text{as}\quad n\to+\infty,

where
\nu
is the unique invariant probability measure of
\mathcal{X}
. In the cases
({\bf Z,Z)}
and
({\bf P,Z)}
, the oscillating random walk
\mathcal{X}
is null recurrent and its study requires more attention. As demonstrated in
[
Vo23
]
, in these cases, the embedding switching subprocess
\mathcal{X_{\bf C}}
(to be introduced in section
4
) is positive recurrent on its unique essential class and its transition probability operator acts as a compact operator on a particular functional space
\mathcal{B}
. This property is of utmost importance for applying the Gouëzel’s renewal theorem (see section
5
for the details), which provides a precise asymptotic behavior of the probabilities
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Notice that
in these three cases, the spectral radius
\rho_{\mathcal{X}}:=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]^{1/n}
equals 1.

In the last three cases
\bf(Z,P)
,
\bf(N,P)
and
\bf(P,P)
, the oscillating random walk
\mathcal{X}
is transient. In the case
\bf(Z,P)
, although
\mu^{\prime}
is non centered, only sufficiently large polynomial moments are required for
\mu^{\prime}
to obtain a suitable overestimation of the return probabilities (see section
6
).
In the remaining cases
\bf(N,P)
and
\bf(P,P)
, in order to get a precise asymptotic of
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]
, exponential moments for
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
are required. We then assume that
\mathbb{E}[e^{t\xi_{1}}]
and
\mathbb{E}[e^{t\xi^{\prime}_{1}}]
are finite for
|t|
large enough and we consider the respective Laplace transforms
L
and
L^{\prime}
of
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
defined formally by

L(t):=\mathbb{E}[e^{t\xi_{1}}]\quad{\rm and}\quad L^{\prime}(t):=\mathbb{E}[e^{t\xi^{\prime}_{1}}].

These Laplace transforms are well-defined and analytic on an open neighborhood
\mathcal{O}
of
0
; furthermore, under hypothesis
O3
, these two functions are unbounded on each side of
0
and they thus reach their minima on this open set. We denote by
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
the values of the parameter
t
given by

\lambda:=\underset{t\in\mathcal{O}}{\text{arg min }}L(t)\quad{\rm and}\quad\lambda^{\prime}:=\underset{t\in\mathcal{O}}{\text{arg min }}L^{\prime}(t)

and we set
\rho:=L(\lambda)
and
\rho^{\prime}:=L^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime})
.
If
\mathcal{O}
is large enough, the derivatives of
L
and
L^{\prime}
vanish respectively at
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
. Notice that
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in]0,1]
and
\rho=1
(resp.
\rho^{\prime}=1
) if and only if
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]=0
(resp.
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]=0
). In the cases
\bf(N,P)
and
\bf(P,P)
, parameters
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
and also
\rho_{\mathcal{X}}
are strictly less than 1 (see Theorem
2.2
below).

These values of
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
are useful to control the behavior of both classical random walks
(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(\xi^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+\xi^{\prime}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. However, for the oscillating walk, the two distributions are strongly intertwined and give rise in some cases to another interesting value of parameter
t
defined by using the two functions
L
and
L^{\prime}
considered simultaneously (see cases
B1
,
B4
and
B7
below). More precisely, when
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}<0
(or
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}>0
), the graphs of
L
and
L^{\prime}
may intersect between
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
at some point
\lambda_{\star}
; in this case, the real
\rho_{\star}:=L(\lambda_{\star})=L^{\prime}(\lambda_{\star})

is strictly greater than
\max(\rho,\rho^{\prime})
and “control” the behavior of
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]
(see below the statement of Theorem
2.2
).

Note that, up to an exchange of
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
or a symmetry with respect to the origin, the cases
({\bf P,Z})
and
({\bf Z,N})
(similarly
({\bf Z,P})
and
({\bf N,Z})
or
({\bf P,P})
and
({\bf N,N})
) are analyzed in an analogous way. Therefore the cases
({\bf Z,N}),({\bf N,Z})
and
({\bf N,N})
are not considered in the present paper. We also introduce some notations to lighten statements.

\bullet

Null recurrent cases
{\bf(Z,Z)}

More precisely, if
\lambda_{\mathcal{X}}
denotes the unique (up to a multiplicative constant) invariant measure for

, the quantities

(2)

\bullet

Transient case
{\bf(Z,P)}
: for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a strictly positive constant

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]\sim\dfrac{C_{x,y}}{n^{3/2}}\quad as\ \ n\to+\infty.

Notice that, in the case of the classical centered random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
, formula (

) is simplified to

as expected since
\lambda_{\mathcal{X}}
is the counting measure on
\mathbb{Z}

In the remaining transient cases
\bf(N,P)
and

is strictly less than 1; its value depends not only on

and the Laplace transform of

. In the present paper, we restrict our attention to the simpler case where the random walk oscillates between
\mathbb{Z}^{-}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{*+}
. This relaxation purposely simplifies our analysis since only the graphs of

\displaystyle\leq n\mathbb{P}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}-m^{\prime}<-an]+c{n\over(an)^{p}}.

Then
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[x+S^{\prime}_{n}<an]\leq{c^{\prime}\over n^{p-1}}

for some
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}_{p}>0
.
As a consequence, for
x\geq 1,y\leq 0
and
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\tau^{S^{\prime}}(x)=n,x+S^{\prime}_{n}=y]

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\geq 1}\mathbb{P}[\tau^{S^{\prime}}(x)>n-1,x+S^{\prime}_{n-1}=z]\,\mu^{\prime}(-z+y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq z<an}\mathbb{P}[\tau^{S^{\prime}}(x)>n-1,x+S^{\prime}_{n-1}=z]\,\mu^{\prime}(-z+y)+\sum_{z\geq an}\mu^{\prime}(-z+y)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[S^{\prime}_{n-1}<an]\ \mu^{\prime}(-\infty,y)\ +\mu^{\prime}(-\infty,y-an]

\displaystyle\leq{c^{\prime}\over n^{p-1}}\times{\mathbb{E}[({\xi^{\prime}_{1}}^{-})^{p}]\over 1+|y|^{p}}+{\mathbb{E}[({\xi^{\prime}_{1}}^{-})^{p}]\over(an+|y|)^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\frac{1}{n^{p-1}(1+|y|^{p})}+\frac{1}{(n+|y|)^{p}}\right),\quad{\rm for\ some\ strictly\ positive\ constant\ }C_{p}.

Let us now establish
(
14
). The inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{p-1}(1+|y|^{p})}\preceq\frac{1}{n^{p-1-\epsilon}(1+|y|^{1+\epsilon})}
is obvious since
p\geq 2
and
\epsilon\in[0,1)
.
To prove that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n+|y|)^{p}}\preceq\frac{1}{n^{p-1-\epsilon}(1+|y|^{1+\epsilon})},

it suffices to notice that

\inf_{n,t\geq 1}\frac{(n+t)^{p}}{n^{p-1-\epsilon}t^{1+\epsilon}}\geq\inf_{u>0}\left(u^{\frac{1+\epsilon}{p}}+\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)^{1-\frac{1+\epsilon}{p}}\right)^{p}>0.

∎

4
On the switching subprocess of
\mathcal{X}

In this section, we introduce a subprocess of
\mathcal{X}
which is of key importance in the sequel.

4.1
The switching subprocess

Let
{\bf C}=(C_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be the sequence of “switching times”, ie times at which the process
\mathcal{X}
swaps regime. We set
C_{0}=0
and for any
k\geq 0
such that
C_{k}<+\infty
,

\displaystyle C_{k+1}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\vskip 5.69054pt\inf\{n>C_{k}\mid X_{C_{k}}+(\xi_{C_{k}+1}+\dotsi+\xi_{n})\geq 0\}&if\quad X_{C_{k}}\leq-1,\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\inf\{n>C_{k}\mid X_{C_{k}}+(\eta_{C_{k}+1}+\dotsi+\eta_{n})\neq 0\}&if\quad X_{C_{k}}=0,\\
\inf\{n>C_{k}\mid X_{C_{k}}+(\xi^{\prime}_{C_{k}+1}+\dotsi+\xi^{\prime}_{n})\leq 0\}&if\quad X_{C_{k}}\geq 1\end{array}\right.

and
C_{k+1}=+\infty
when
C_{k}=+\infty
.
The random variables
C_{k}
are stopping times with respect to the canonical filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
associated with the sequence
(\xi_{n},\eta_{n},\xi^{\prime}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.
When
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]\geq 0
and
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]\leq 0
, these random variables are
\mathbb{P}
-as finite.
When
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]<0
or
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]>0
, it holds
C_{k}=+\infty
for sufficiently large (random)
k
ie the Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
stays ultimely in exactly one of the half lines
\mathbb{Z}^{*-}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{*+}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{X}_{\bf C}:=(X_{C_{k}}{\bf 1}_{[C_{k}<+\infty]})_{k\geq 0}
the subprocess of
{\bf C}
and call it the
switching subprocess of
\mathcal{X}
. It plays a crucial role in the sequel.

By
[
PV23
, Lemma 3.2]
, when the sequence
(C_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is finite
\mathbb{P}
-as,
the process
\mathcal{X}_{\bf C}
is a time-homogeneous Markov chain on
\mathbb{Z}
with transition kernel

Q=(Q(x,y))_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}}
given by
Q(x,y):=\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{1}<+\infty,X_{C_{1}}=y]
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
.
This readily implies that
Q^{(\ell)}(x,y)=\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{\ell}<+\infty,X_{C_{\ell}}=y]
for any
\ell\geq 0
and yields
the following formula

Q(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{clcll}\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}[\tau^{S}(x)=n,x+S_{n}=y]&if&x\leq-1&and&y\geq 0,\\
\displaystyle{\mu_{0}(y)\over 1-\mu_{0}(0)}&if&x=0&and&y\neq 0,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}[\tau^{S^{\prime}}(x)=n,x+S^{\prime}_{n}=y]&if&x\geq 1&and&y\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

(18)

The switching times
C_{\ell}
are alsot ascending and descending ladder times of
S
and
S^{\prime}
respectively, hence we may also write

Q(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{clcll}\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{|x|-1}\mu_{*+}(y-x-t)\,U_{*+}(t)&if&x\leq-1&and&y\geq 0,\\
\displaystyle{\mu_{0}(y)\over 1-\mu_{0}(0)}&if&x=0&and&y\neq 0,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{t=-x+1}^{0}\mu^{\prime}_{*-}(y-x-t)\,\ U^{\prime}_{*-}(t)&if&x\geq 1&and&y\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

(19)

Expression (
19
) is useful in the sequel to prove that
Q
is a compact operator on some suitable space depending on the explored situation. Let
{\bf S}_{\mu_{0}}
denote the support of
\mu_{0}
.
Following the argument developed in
[
Vo23
]
, we can prove that the set

\mathcal{I}_{\bf C}=]D^{\prime},D[\ \cup\ {\bf S}_{\mu_{0}}

is the unique essential class for the switching process
\mathcal{X}_{\bf C}
. Indeed, on the one hand, since
\mu
is aperiodic, it holds that
Q(x,y)>0
for any
x\leq-1
and
y\in[0,D[
because
\mu(D)>0
and there exists a path governed by
\mu
from
x
to
y-D\leq-1
which stays in
\mathbb{Z}^{*-}
. The same property holds for
x\geq 1
and
y\in]D^{\prime},0]
. On the other hand
Q(0,y)>0
for any
y\in{\bf S}_{\mu_{0}}
. Since
Q(x,y)=0
when
x\in\mathcal{I}_{\bf C}
and
y\notin\mathcal{I}_{\bf C}
, this proves that
\mathcal{I}_{\bf C}
is irreducible.
When
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]<0
, it holds that
\mathbb{P}[C_{1}<+\infty]<1
for any
x\leq-1
and
\mathcal{I}_{\bf C}
is transient for
\mathcal{X}_{\bf C}
; the same property holds when
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]>0
. When
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]\geq 0
and
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]\leq 0
, the class
\mathcal{I}_{\bf C}
is the unique essential class of
\mathcal{X}_{\bf C}
and it is recurrent.

It is natural to decompose
Q
as
Q:=\sum_{n\geq 1}Q_{n}
with

Q_{n}(x,y):=\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{1}=n,X_{n}=y]

for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
. More precisely,

Q_{n}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{clcll}\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\tau^{S}(x)=n,x+S_{n}=y]&if&x\leq-1&and&y\geq 0,\\
\displaystyle\mu_{0}(0)^{n-1}\mu_{0}(y)&if&x=0&and&y\neq 0,\\
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\tau^{S^{\prime}}(x)=n,x+S^{\prime}_{n}=y]&if&x\geq 1&and&y\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

(20)

Similarly, for any
\ell\geq 0
, the
\ell
-th power
Q^{(\ell)}
of
Q
may also be decomposed as

Q^{(\ell)}:=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}Q^{(\ell)}_{n}
where

\displaystyle Q^{(\ell)}_{n}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{\ell}=n,X_{n}=y]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j_{1},\ldots,\ j_{\ell}\geq 1}}{{j_{1}+\ldots+j_{\ell}=n}}}\sum_{y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell}=y}\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{1}=j_{1},X_{j_{1}}=y_{1}]\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}_{y_{\ell-1}}[C_{1}=j_{\ell},X_{j_{\ell}}=y]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j_{1},\ldots,\ j_{\ell}\geq 1}}{{j_{1}+\ldots+j_{\ell}=n}}}Q_{j_{1}}\ldots Q_{j_{\ell}}{\bf 1}_{\{y\}}(x).

4.2
Decomposition of the trajectories of
\mathcal{X}

The subprocess
\mathcal{X}_{\bf C}
allows us to decompose the trajectories of
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
into successive excursions inside the different media. In particular, for any
n\geq 1,x\in\mathbb{Z}
and
y\geq 1
(similar formulae hold when
y\leq 0
), the probability
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]
may be decomposed as
followed.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y,C_{\ell}\leq n<C_{\ell+1}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{z\geq 1}\underbrace{\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{\ell}=k,X_{k}=z]}_{=Q^{(\ell)}_{k}(x,z)}\ \underbrace{\mathbb{P}[z+S^{\prime}_{1}\geq 1,\ldots,z+S^{\prime}_{n-k-1}\geq 1,z+S^{\prime}_{n-k}=y]}_{:={\bf V}_{n-k,y}(z)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\underbrace{\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{\ell}=n,X_{n}=y]}_{=Q^{(\ell)}_{n}(x,y)}\ \quad{\rm with\ the\ convention}\ Q^{(0)}_{k}(x,z)={\bf 1}_{\{x\}}(z),

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}Q^{(\ell)}_{k}\right){\bf V}_{n-k,y}\right)(x),\ \text{with the convention}\ {\bf V}_{0,y}(z):={\bf 1}_{\{y\}}(z).

(21)

In the sequel we thus consider the functions
{\bf V}_{n,y}
for
n\geq 0
and
y\in\mathbb{Z}
corresponding to the final excursions of the trajectories inside the positive or negative half-lines and defined by
{\bf V}_{0,y}(z):={\bf 1}_{\{y\}}(z)
and, for
n\geq 1
,

\bullet
if
y\leq-1
then

{\bf V}_{n,y}:x\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{cll}\mathbb{P}[\tau^{S}(x)>n,x+S_{n}=y]&if&x\leq-1,\\
0&if&x\geq 0,\end{array}\right.

(22)

\bullet
if
y=0
then
{\bf V}_{n,0}(0)=\mu_{0}(0)^{n}
and
{\bf V}_{n,0}(x)=0
otherwise,

\bullet
if
y\geq 1
then

{\bf V}_{n,y}:x\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{cll}0&if&x\leq 0,\\
\mathbb{P}[\tau^{S^{\prime}}(x)>n,x+S^{\prime}_{n}=y]&if&x\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

(23)

When
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]\geq 0
and
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]\leq 0
, it holds that
\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{\ell}<+\infty]=1
for any
\ell\geq 0
. In this case, we need to control firstly the behavior as
n\to+\infty
of the transition operator
T_{n}
defined for any
x,z\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\geq 1
by

T_{n}(x,z):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}Q_{n}^{(\ell)}(x,z)=\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{\ell}=n,X_{n}=z]

(24)

and secondly the one of the functions
{\bf V}_{n,y}
. Since
C_{\ell}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}(C_{i+1}-C_{i})
for any
\ell\geq 1
, the control of
T_{n}
as
n\to+\infty
corresponds to a renewal theorem for the sequence of non iid random variables
(C_{i+1}-C_{i})_{i\geq 0}
while the one of
{\bf V}_{n,y}
deals with fluctuations of the random walk
S^{\prime}
.

When
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]<0
(resp.
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\prime}_{1}]>0
), it holds that
\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{1}<+\infty]\leq\mathbb{P}_{-1}[C_{1}<+\infty]<1
when
x\leq-1
(resp.
\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{1}<+\infty]\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}[C_{1}<+\infty]<1
when
x\geq 1
) so that the quantity
\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{\ell}<+\infty]

decreases exponentially to
0
, uniformly in
x
. In this case, we need to control the behavior of
\mathbb{P}_{x}[C_{\ell}=k,X_{k}=z]
as
k\to+\infty
, for any
\ell\geq 1
and
z\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In both cases, the fact that the random variables
C_{i+1}-C_{i}
are not iid forces us to use a functional version of a renewal theorem that we present in the following section.

4.3
A local limit theorem for an aperiodic sequence of operators

The previous decomposition
\displaystyle C_{\ell}{\bf 1}_{[C_{\ell}<+\infty]}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}(C_{i+1}-C_{i}){\bf 1}_{[C_{i+1}<+\infty]}
for
\ell\geq 1
stipulates that
C_{\ell}
is a sum of the integer valued random increments
\Delta_{i}=C_{i+1}-C_{i}
.

If the
\Delta_{i}
were iid, the process
(C_{\ell})_{\ell\geq 0}
would be a classical random walk
(\Delta_{0}+\Delta_{1}+\ldots+\Delta_{\ell-1})_{\ell\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{N}
with increments
\Delta_{i}
having an infinite mean, since by Lemma
3.1
the tail distribution function of these increments would satisfy
\mathbb{P}[\Delta_{i}>n]\sim c/n^{\beta}
with
\beta=1/2
and
c>0
.
The precise asymptotic behavior as
n\to+\infty
of the quantity
\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\mathbb{P}[C_{\ell}=n]
would be given by
renewal theorems, which do exist in this infinite mean situation (we refer for instance to the seminal works by
[
GL63
]
and
[
Eri70
]
). Nevertheless, when
0<\beta\leq 1/2
, the strong renewal theorem is not valid in full generality, but only when
n
varies in a set of integers having density 1. This result has been improved by R.A. Doney
[
Don97
]
under the additional hypothesis
\mathbb{P}[\Delta_{1}=n]\preceq L(n)/n^{1+\beta}
where
L
is a slowly varying function.

In our cases, the random variables
C_{i+1}-C_{i}
are not iid and the strong renewal theorem is more complicated to prove. We use a result due to Gouëzel to study the cases

({\bf P,N}),(\bf Z,Z)
and
(\bf P,Z)
. We make precise this statement in Theorem
4.2
under some additive hypotheses to analyze also the cases
({\bf Z,P}),({\bf N,P})
and
({\bf P,P})
.

An
aperiodic renewal sequence of operators
is a sequence
(\mathcal{R}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of operators acting on a
\mathbb{C}
-Banach space
({\mathcal{B}},|\cdot|_{\mathcal{B}})
and satisfying the following conditions.

\bullet
For
n\geq 1
, the operators
\mathcal{R}_{n}
act on
{\mathcal{B}}
and
\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\|\mathcal{R}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}}<+\infty
(where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}}
denotes the operator norm on the Banach space
\mathcal{L}({\mathcal{B}})
of linear continuous operators on
({\mathcal{B}},|\cdot|_{\mathcal{B}})
).

\bullet
The operators
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(z):=\sum_{n\geq 1}z^{n}\mathcal{R}_{n}
defined for any
z
in the closed unit ball
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
in
\mathbb{C}
satisfy

R1-

\mathcal{R}(1)
has a simple eigenvalue at 1 (with corresponding eigenprojector
\Pi_{\mathcal{R}}
) and the rest of its spectrum is contained in a disk of radius strictly less than
1
;

R2-

for any complex number
z\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{1\}
, the spectral radius of
\mathcal{R}(z)
is strictly less than
1
;

R3-

for any
n\geq 1
, the real number
r_{n}
defined by
\Pi_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}_{n}\Pi_{\mathcal{R}}=r_{n}\Pi_{\mathcal{R}}
is non negative.

Condition
R2
implies that, for any
z\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{1\}
, the operator
I-\mathcal{R}(z)
is invertible on
{\mathcal{B}}
and

(I-\mathcal{R}(z))^{-1}=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\mathcal{R}(z)^{\ell}=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\left(\sum_{j\geq 1}z^{j}\mathcal{R}_{j}\right)^{\ell}=\sum_{n\geq 0}{\mathcal{T}}_{n}z^{n}

where
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}=I
and
\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}_{n}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}\left(\sum_{j_{1}+\ldots+j_{\ell}=n}{\mathcal{R}}_{j_{1}}\ldots{\mathcal{R}}_{j_{\ell}}\right)
for any
n\geq 1
.

We write
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}(1)
for short and notice that
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{(\ell)}:=\underbrace{\mathcal{R}\circ\ldots\circ\mathcal{R}}_{\ell\ times}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{j_{1}+\ldots+j_{\ell}=n}{\mathcal{R}}_{j_{1}}\ldots{\mathcal{R}}_{j_{\ell}}\right)
; it is thus natural to set
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}:=\sum_{j_{1}+\ldots+j_{\ell}=n}{\mathcal{R}}_{j_{1}}\ldots{\mathcal{R}}_{j_{\ell}}
so that
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{(\ell)}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}
for any
\ell\geq 1
. As usual, we set
\mathcal{R}_{n}^{(0)}=I
.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
Gou11
]

Let
(\mathcal{R}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an aperiodic renewal sequence of operators acting on the Banach space
({\mathcal{B}},|\cdot|_{\mathcal{B}})
and assume that
there exist

and a slowly varying function

such that

R4(

-
\quad\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{n}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}}\leq C\frac{L(n)}{n^{1+\beta}}
;

R5(

-
\quad\displaystyle\sum_{j>n}r_{j}\thicksim\frac{L(n)}{n^{\beta}}

.

Then the sequence

converges in
(\mathcal{L}({{\mathcal{B}}}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}})
to the operator

, with

.

In order to control the behavior as

of each term

for

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{R}_{n})_{n\geq 1}

({\mathcal{B}},|\cdot|_{\mathcal{B}})
. Assume that
there exist

such that the sequence
\displaystyle\left({n^{1+\beta}\over L(n)}\mathcal{R}_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}

to some

\mathcal{E}

Then

1.

there exists a strictly positive constant
C
such that for any

,

\|\mathcal{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}\|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq C\ell^{2}{L(n)\over n^{1+\beta}};

(25)

2.

for any
\ell\geq 1
, the sequence
\displaystyle\left({n^{1+\beta}\over L(n)}\mathcal{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}\right)_{n\geq 1}
converges in

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\ell}:=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\mathcal{R}^{(i)}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{R}^{(\ell-i-1)}

Proof.

1. Let
(\alpha_{\ell})_{\ell\geq 1}
be a sequence with
\ell L(\alpha_{\ell})\sim\alpha_{\ell}^{\beta}
. By
[
Gou11
, Proposition 1.5, Theorem 1.6]

C

&&\\

Notice that, for

\ell=1
.
Assume that

\displaystyle\left({n^{1+\beta}\over L(n)}\mathcal{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}\right)_{n\geq 1}
converges in
(\mathcal{L}({{\mathcal{B}}}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}})

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\ell}:=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\mathcal{R}^{(i)}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{R}^{(\ell-i-1)}

(ii) On the other hand,

(L,\beta)
and

Lemma 5.3

Let

converges in

Proof.

Fix

Since

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}I_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\sqrt{n-k}P_{n-k}\left({\sqrt{\frac{n}{n-k}}}g_{k}\right)

\displaystyle|\sqrt{n}J_{n}|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq A\sum_{k=N+1}^{\lfloor n(1-\varepsilon)\rfloor}\sqrt{\frac{n}{n-k}}|g_{k}|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq\frac{A}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\sum_{k=N+1}^{+\infty}|g_{k}|_{\mathcal{B}}.

At last, since

\displaystyle|\sqrt{n}K_{n}|_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq AB\sqrt{n}\sum_{k=\lfloor n(1-\varepsilon)\rfloor+1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k\sqrt{n-k}}+\|P_{0}\|_{\mathcal{B}}\ B/\sqrt{n}

\displaystyle\leq 2AB\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{1-\varepsilon}+\|P_{0}\|_{\mathcal{B}}\ B/\sqrt{n}.

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\bigg|\sqrt{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}P_{n-k}(g_{k})-P(\mathbf{G})\bigg|_{\mathcal{B}}

Lemma 6.3

Let

(2)

Proof.

Fix

On the one hand, setting

On the other hand,

Consequently, we obtain

and one concludes by letting

(\bf P,N),(\bf Z,Z)
an
(\bf P,Z)
while in the submarkovian situations,
the spectral radius of
\tilde{Q}
is strictly less than
1
in
\mathcal{B}_{\Psi}
and
we apply the strategy developed in the case
(\bf Z,P)
.

7.2.1
The subcase
A
:
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}

We fix here
t=\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
.

A1 :
\rho=\rho^{\prime}

In this case, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
, it holds that

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]=\rho^{n}e^{\lambda(x-y)}\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}^{\lambda}=y],

where
(X_{n}^{\lambda})_{n\geq 0}
is a (Z, Z) oscillating random walk. Hence

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}^{\lambda}=y]\sim C_{y}/\sqrt{n}
by Section
5
.

A2 :
\rho>\rho^{\prime}

We consider the sequence of operators
(\tilde{Q}_{n})_{n\geq 1}

defined by

\tilde{Q}_{n}(x,y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathbb{P}[\tau^{\overset{\circ}{S_{\ }}}(x)=n,x+{\overset{\circ}{S_{n}}}=y]&{\rm if}&x\leq 0&{\rm and}&y\geq 1,\\
r^{n}\mathbb{P}[\tau^{{\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}}}}(x)=n,x+{\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}_{n}}}=y]&{\rm if}&x\geq 1&{\rm and}&y\leq 0.\\
\end{array}\right.

where
r=\rho^{\prime}/\rho<1
. We set
\tilde{Q}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\tilde{Q}_{n}
and
notice that
Q_{n}^{(\ell)}\varphi(x)=\rho^{n}e^{\lambda x}\tilde{Q}_{n}^{(\ell)}(\varphi/e^{\lambda\cdot})(x)

for any function
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{\Psi}

and
\ell\geq 1
.
The condition
r<1
implies that the operator
\tilde{Q}

is submarkovian and that its spectral radius
\rho_{\Psi}
in

\mathcal{B}_{\Psi}
is strictly less than 1.
By applying the same scheme as in the
transient case
\bf(N,P)
, we
obtain
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]\sim C_{x,y}\rho^{n}/n^{3/2}

as
n\to+\infty
, for some strictly positive constant

C_{x,y}
.

A2 :
\rho<\rho^{\prime}

Analogously, we set
r=\rho/\rho^{\prime}<1
and define the sequence of
operators
(\tilde{Q}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
by

\tilde{Q}_{n}(x,y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccccc}r^{n}\mathbb{P}[\tau^{{\overset{\circ}{S_{\ }}}}(x)=n,x+{\overset{\circ}{S_{n}}}=y]&{\rm if}&x\leq 0&{\rm and}&y\geq 1,\\
\mathbb{P}[\tau^{\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}}}(x)=n,x+{\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}_{n}}=y}]&{\rm if}&x\geq 1&{\rm and}&y\leq 0.\\
\end{array}\right.

The rest of the argument is the same as the one in the case
A2 :
\rho>\rho^{\prime}

7.2.2

The subcase B :
\lambda<\lambda^{\prime}

B1 :
\rho=\rho^{\prime}

In this case, there exists a unique
\lambda_{\star}\in(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})

such that
L(\lambda_{\star})=L^{\prime}(\lambda_{\star})
;
furthermore
\rho_{\star}:=L(\lambda_{\star})=L^{\prime}(\lambda_{\star})\in(\rho,1)
,

{dL\over dt}(\lambda_{\star})>0

and
{dL^{\prime}\over dt}(\lambda_{\star})<0
.

We fix
t=\lambda_{\star}
.

The random walks
S^{\lambda_{\star}}
and
{S^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{\star}}

are non centered
with respective means
\frac{1}{\rho_{\star}}{dL\over dt}(\lambda_{\star})>0
and

\frac{1}{\rho_{\star}}{dL^{\prime}\over dt}(\lambda_{\star})<0
.
As a consequence,
the oscillating random walk
\mathcal{X}(\mu^{\lambda_{\star}},{\mu^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{\star}})
is
{\bf(P,N)}
; thus, it
is positive recurrent
on
\mathbb{Z}
with the unique invariant probability measure

\nu_{\star}
.
Then, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
, and as
n\to+\infty
,
we conclude that

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]=\rho^{n}_{\star}e^{\lambda_{\star}(x-y)}\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}^{\lambda_{\star}}=y]\sim\rho^{n}_{\star}e^{\lambda_{\star}(x-y)}\nu_{\star}(y).

B2 :
\rho<\rho^{\prime}\ \&\ L(\lambda^{\prime})<\rho^{\prime}

We fix
t=\lambda^{\prime}

and introduce
the sequence of operators
(\tilde{Q}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
defined by

\tilde{Q}_{n}(x,y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccccc}r^{n}\mathbb{P}[\tau^{S^{\lambda^{\prime}}}(x)=n,x+S^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{n}=y]&{\rm if}&x\leq 0&{\rm and}&y\geq 1,\\
\mathbb{P}[\tau^{\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}}}(x)=n,x+{\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}_{n}}=y}]&{\rm if}&x\geq 1&{\rm and}&y\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

with
r=L(\lambda^{\prime})/\rho^{\prime}<1
.
The inequality
r<1
implies that
the operator
\tilde{Q}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\tilde{Q}_{n}
is
submarkovian. The rest of the proof works as in the
case
{\bf(N,P)}
, via a Doob’s transform. This yields the asymptotic
behavior
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]\sim C_{x,y}{\rho^{\prime}}^{n}/n^{3/2}
as
n\to+\infty
,
where
C_{x,y}>0
.

B3 :
\rho<\rho^{\prime}\ \&\ L(\lambda^{\prime})=\rho^{\prime}

We fix
t=\lambda^{\prime}
.

For any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
, it holds that

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]={\rho^{\prime}}^{n}e^{\lambda^{\prime}(x-y)}\mathbb{P}_{x}[X^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{n}=y]

where
(X_{n}^{\lambda^{\prime}})_{n\geq 0}
is a (P, Z) oscillating
random walk since

{dL\over dt}(\lambda^{\prime})>0
. By Section
5
,
it holds that

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}^{\lambda^{\prime}}=y]\sim C_{y}/\sqrt{n}
with
C_{y}>0
.
This yields the expected asymptotic
behavior
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]\sim C_{x,y}{\rho^{\prime}}^{n}/\sqrt{n}
, with
C_{x,y}>0
,
as
n\to+\infty
.

B4 :
\rho<\rho^{\prime}\ \&\ L(\lambda^{\prime})>\rho^{\prime}

In this case, there exists a unique
\lambda_{\star}\in(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
such
that
L(\lambda_{\star})=L^{\prime}(\lambda_{\star})=:\rho_{\star}
. Furthermore

\rho_{\star}\in(\rho,1)
,
{dL\over dt}(\lambda_{\star})>0
and

{dL^{\prime}\over dt}(\lambda_{\star})<0
.
We fix
t=\lambda_{\star}

and adopt the same argument as in the case B1.

B5 :
\rho>\rho^{\prime}\ \&\ L^{\prime}(\lambda)<\rho

We fix
t=\lambda
and introduce the sequence of
operators
(\tilde{Q}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
where
\tilde{Q}_{n}
is defined by

\tilde{Q}_{n}(x,y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathbb{P}[\tau^{\overset{\circ}{S_{\ }}}(x)=n,x+{\overset{\circ}{S_{\ }}}_{n}=y]&{\rm if}&x\leq 0&{\rm and}&y\geq 1,\\
r^{n}\mathbb{P}[\tau^{{S^{\prime}}^{\lambda}}(x)=n,x+{S^{\prime}_{n}}^{\lambda}=y]&{\rm if}&x\geq 1&{\rm and}&y\leq 0,\\
\end{array}\right.

with
r=L^{\prime}(\lambda)/\rho<1
.
The inequality
r<1
implies that the operator

\tilde{Q}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\tilde{Q}_{n}
is submarkovian
on
\mathcal{B}_{\Psi}
.
The rest of the proof works as in the
case
{\bf(N,P)}
, via a Doob’s transform.
This yields the asymptotic behavior
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]\sim C_{x,y}\rho^{n}/n^{3/2}

as
n\to+\infty
, with
C_{x,y}>0
.

B6 :
\rho>\rho^{\prime}\ \&\ L^{\prime}(\lambda)=\rho

We fix
t=\lambda
.

For any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
, it holds that

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]={\rho}^{n}e^{\lambda(x-y)}\mathbb{P}_{x}[X^{\lambda}_{n}=y]

where
(X_{n}^{\lambda})_{n\geq 0}
is a
{\bf(Z,N)}
oscillating
random walk since

{dL^{\prime}\over dt}(\lambda)<0
. By Section
5

(notice that the case
({\bf Z,N)}
is analogous to
({\bf P,Z)}

by exchanging the role of
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
), it holds that

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}^{\lambda}=y]\sim C_{y}/\sqrt{n}
.
This yields the asymptotic behavior
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]\sim C_{x,y}{\rho}^{n}/\sqrt{n}
as
n\to+\infty
, with
C_{x,y}>0
..

B7 :
\rho>\rho^{\prime}\ \&\ L^{\prime}(\lambda)>\rho

In this case, there exists a unique
\lambda_{\star}\in(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
such that
L(\lambda_{\star})=L^{\prime}(\lambda_{\star})=:\rho_{\star}
; moreover
\rho_{\star}\in(\rho,1)
,
{dL\over dt}(\lambda_{\star})>0
and
{dL^{\prime}\over dt}(\lambda_{\star})<0
.
We fix
t=\lambda_{\star}
and we apply the same argument as in the cases B1 and B4.

7.2.3

The subcase C :
\lambda>\lambda^{\prime}

As in the case B, we have totally 7 subcases to consider:

Condition

C1

\rho=\rho^{\prime}

C2

\rho<\rho^{\prime}\ \&\ L(\lambda^{\prime})<\rho^{\prime}

C3

\rho<\rho^{\prime}\ \&\ L(\lambda^{\prime})=\rho^{\prime}

C4

\rho<\rho^{\prime}\ \&\ L(\lambda^{\prime})>\rho^{\prime}

C5

\rho>\rho^{\prime}\ \&\ L^{\prime}(\lambda)<\rho

C6

\rho>\rho^{\prime}\ \&\ L^{\prime}(\lambda)=\rho

C7

\rho>\rho^{\prime}\ \&\ L^{\prime}(\lambda)>\rho

\bullet
The proof in the subcases C2 and C5 is
essentially the same as in B2 or B5 with

t=\lambda^{\prime}
.

\bullet
In the subcases C1, C4 and C7, we choose

t=\lambda_{\star}
as for the subcases
B1, B4 and B7. Hence

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]=\rho^{n}_{\star}e^{\lambda_{\star}(x-y)}\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}^{\lambda_{\star}}=y]

The condition
\lambda>\lambda^{\prime}
implies that

{dL\over dt}(\lambda_{\star})<0
and
{dL^{\prime}\over dt}(\lambda_{\star})>0
. Hence, the oscillating random walk

\mathcal{X}(\mu^{\lambda_{\star}},{\mu^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{\star}})

is
{\bf(N,P)}
and falls within the transient framework
studied in Subsection
7.1
.
Notice that the Laplace transform
L_{\mu^{\lambda_{\star}}}

of the measure
\mu^{\star}
reaches its minimum at
\lambda-\lambda_{\star}
and equals
\rho/\rho_{\star}
(with a similar statement for
{\mu^{\prime}}^{\star}
).
Consequently, as
n\to+\infty
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}^{\lambda_{\star}}=y]\sim\left({\max(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\over\rho_{\star}}\right)^{n}/n^{3/2}\quad(\text{up to a multiplicative positive constant})

\bullet
In the subcase C3, we choose

t=\lambda^{\prime}
.
The condition
\lambda>\lambda^{\prime}
implies that

{dL\over dt}(\lambda^{\prime})<0
and
{dL^{\prime}\over dt}(\lambda^{\prime})=0
. Hence,
the oscillating random walk
\mathcal{X}(\mu^{\lambda^{\prime}},{\mu^{\prime}}^{\lambda^{\prime}})
is
{\bf(N,Z)}
(which is analogous to
the case
{\bf(Z,P)}
) and falls within the transient
framework studied in Subsection
6
. Hence
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
, it holds that

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]={\rho^{\prime}}^{n}e^{\lambda^{\prime}(x-y)}\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}^{\lambda^{\prime}}=y]

with
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}^{\lambda^{\prime}}=y]\sim C_{x,y}/n^{3/2}
as

n\to+\infty
, with
C_{x,y}>0
.

\bullet
In the subcase C6, we choose

t=\lambda
. Since
\lambda>\lambda^{\prime}
, it
holds that
{dL\over dt}(\lambda)=0
and
{dL^{\prime}\over dt}(\lambda)>0
. Hence,
the the oscillating random walk
\mathcal{X}(\mu^{\lambda},{\mu^{\prime}}^{\lambda})
is
{\bf(Z,P)}
and falls within the transient
framework studied in subsection
6
. Hence
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
, it holds that

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}=y]=\rho^{n}e^{\lambda(x-y)}\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}^{\lambda}=y]

with
\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{n}^{\lambda}=y]\sim C_{x,y}/n^{3/2}
as
n\to+\infty
, with
C_{x,y}>0
.

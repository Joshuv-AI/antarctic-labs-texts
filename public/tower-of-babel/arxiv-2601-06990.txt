Single conflict coloring and palette sparsification of uniform hypergraphs This paper is partially based on the thesis written by the second author under the supervision of the first author
Carl Johan Casselgren, Kalle Eriksson

Abstract
We introduce and investigate single conflict coloring in the setting of r r -uniform hypergraphs. We establish some basic properties of this hypergraph coloring model and study a probabilistic model of single conflict coloring where the conflicts for each edge are chosen randomly; in particular, we prove a sharp threshold-type result for complete graphs and establish a sufficient condition for single conflict colorability of r r -uniform hypergraphs in this model. Furthermore, we obtain a related palette sparsification-type result for general list coloring of linear uniform hypergraphs (i.e. uniform hypergraphs where any two edges share at most one common vertex). Throughout the paper we pose several questions and conjectures.



Keywords:
Hypergraph coloring; Single conflict coloring; Palette sparsification

Many hypergraph coloring models translate naturally to the list coloring setting, and numerous variants of list coloring are studied in the literature. Our primary interest here,

Given an edge coloring

Adapted coloring was introduced by

Building on the notion of adaptable coloring, Kostochka and Zhu

considered the list coloring version of adaptable coloring for graphs and hypergraphs,

Kostochka and Zhu proved a general upper bound on this invariant and also characterized graphs with adaptable choosability
2
.

Another variant of list coloring is so-called

[
38
]

Single conflict coloring is related to the variant where lists of vertices contained in a common edge have
at most one common color, often referred to as

Denote by

Generalizing both the notion of adaptable choosability and separation choosability

A

Single conflict coloring of hypergraphs

Although, to the best of our knowledge,
single conflict coloring has previously only been studied in the setting of graphs,
it translates naturally to the setting of hypergraphs.
A formal definition is as follows.
Let

-uniform

A

of

as a conflict map, and for each

e=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{r}\}\in E(H)

yields a conflict-

-partition
\{c_{v}\}
,

H
is
conflict
\{c_{v}\}
-colorable
if there is a map

\varphi:V(H)\to\{1,\dots,k\}
such that no edge

satisfies that
\varphi(u_{i})=c_{u_{i}}(e)
for every

.
The
single conflict chromatic number

\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(H)
of

is the smallest

such that

is conflict
\{c_{v}\}
-colorable for every local
k
-partition
\{c_{v}\}
.

Our main goal in this section is to present analogues for hypergraphs of several results
previously obtained for graphs. We stress that in most cases the proofs are very similar
to the ones in the graph setting, so we shall generally omit them.

Alon
[
1
]
proved that the list chromatic number of a graph with average
degree

is of order
\Omega(\log d)
, and a similar lower bound for separation choosability
follows from results of
Esperet et al.
[

and Kwan et al.

This implies that both separation choosability, adaptable choosability and
the single conflict chromatic number grows with the average degree of a graph.
Indeed, for the latter parameter, the significantly better lower bound

\Omega(\sqrt{d/\log d})
was established by Dvorak et al.
[
21
]
.

For hypergraphs the situation is quite different. Let us first recall that a hypergraph is
linear (or simple)
if each pair of vertices
appears in at most one common edge. It has been observed (see e.g.
[
45
]
) that for hypergraphs the
list-chromatic number does not necessarily grow with the average degree, but for
linear hypergraphs, the list-chromatic number does in fact grow with
the average degree
[
3
,
45
]
.

Since both adaptable choosability and separation choosabilitiy are
trivially at most the choosability for hypergraphs, this means that separation choosability and adaptable
choosability do not grow
with the average degree. Here we shall verify that the lower bound for single conflict
coloring established in
[
21
]
remains valid in the
setting of hypergraphs. The following proposition implies that for hypergraphs,
this parameter shows a
quite different behavior compared to ordinary choosability, and the aforementioned
graph coloring invariants. The proof is essentially the

.

\chi_{\nleftrightarrow}(H)\geq\left\lceil\left(\frac{d}{\log d}\right)^{1/r}\right\rceil
.

of probabilistic single conflict coloring ’translates’ naturally to the setting of probabilistic DP-coloring,
where, informally speaking, every edge instead yields
k
possible conflict pairs.

First, we formally define what we mean by a random local
k
-partition.

Definition 3.1

(Random local
k
-partition)
.

Suppose
H
is an
r
-uniform hypergraph with
n
vertices and
m
edges. For each edge

e=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}\in E(H)
, let the
r
-tuple
c(e)=(c_{v_{1}}(e),\dots,c_{v_{r}}(e))\in[k]^{r}

be chosen uniformly at random,
independently of the choice for the other edges in
H
. The resulting local

k
-partition
\{c_{v}\}
of
H
is called a
random local
k
-partition.

Given a random local
k
-partition
\{c_{v}\}
of
H
, we say that
H
is
single conflict
k
-colorable with probability
p
if
\mathbb{P}[H\text{ is conflict }\{c_{v}\}\text{-colorable}]=p
.
Typically, we shall be interested in conditions on
k
that imply that an
n
-vertex
r
-uniform hypergraph is single
conflict
k
-colorable with probability tending to
1
as
n\to\infty
. More generally,
an event
A_{n}
occurs

with high probability

(abbreviated
whp
)
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[A_{n}]=1
.

In the following, we shall derive several such analogues of results obtained in
[
8
]
.
We begin by stating the following analogue of Proposition 1.1 in
[
8
]
,
which gives a lower bound on
k
for a hypergraph to be single conflict
k
-colorable whp.
In the following,
\rho(H)
is the maximum
density of
G
, i.e.
\rho(H)=\max_{\emptyset\neq H\subseteq G}|E(H)|/|V(H)|
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
H
be a nonempty
r
-uniform hypergraph with maximum density
\rho(H)\geq\exp(1/\varepsilon^{r})
. If

1\leq k\leq\left(\frac{\rho(H)}{\log\rho(H)}\right)^{1/r},

then
H
is single conflict
k
-colorable with probability at most
\varepsilon
.

For
r=2
, Proposition
3.2
implies that a graph
G
is single conflict
k
-colorable with probability at most
\varepsilon
, given that
\rho(G)\geq\exp(1/\varepsilon^{2})
, and

k\leq\sqrt{\frac{\rho(G)}{\log\rho(G)}}.

The analogous bound for DP-colorability from a random
cover with probability at most
\varepsilon
in
[
8
]

is
\frac{\rho(G)}{\log\rho(G)}
.

The proof of Proposition
3.2
is a simple
first moment argument, similar to the proof of Proposition 1.1 in
[
8
]
.
To give the reader some intuition of our probabilistic single conflict coloring model, we provide a brief sketch.
In the proof, it will be of importance that
\rho(H)
can be assumed to be the density of
H
, that is,
\rho(H)=|E(H)|/|V(H)|
. This follows from the observation below, the proof of which we omit
(see
[
8
]
or
[
25
]
for details).
Let
p(H,k)=\mathbb{P}[H\text{ is single conflict }k\text{-colorable}]
, for an
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
.

Observation 3.3
.

If
H^{\prime}
is an
r
-uniform subhypergraph of
H
, then for all
k\geq 1
, we have

p(H,k)\leq p(H^{\prime},k).

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, fix
k\leq(\rho(H)/\log\rho(H))^{1/r}
and let
\rho=\rho(H)
for an
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
with
n
vertices and
m
edges. By Observation
3.3
, we may assume that
\rho=m/n
.

Let
\{c_{v}\}
be a random local
k
-partition of
H
, and enumerate all vertex colorings
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{1}^{k^{n}}
of
H
. Let
E_{i}
be the event that
\varphi_{i}
is a conflict
\{c_{v}\}
-coloring and
X_{i}
the indicator random variable for
E_{i}
. Set
X=\sum_{i\in[k^{n}]}X_{i}
; then

\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]=(1-1/k^{r})^{m}
,
so the expected number of conflict
\{c_{v}\}
-colorings of
H
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X]

\displaystyle=k^{n}(1-1/k^{r})^{m}

\displaystyle=\exp(n(\log k+\rho\log(1-1/k^{r})))

\displaystyle\leq\exp(n(\log k-\rho/k^{r})).

Since
k\leq(\rho/\log\rho)^{1/r}
, we have
\log k\leq\frac{1}{r}\log\rho-\frac{1}{r}\log\log\rho
, which implies that

\displaystyle\log k-\rho/k^{r}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{r}\log\log\rho,

so

\mathbb{E}[X]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{r}\log\log\rho\right)
.
Invoking that
\rho\geq\exp(1/\varepsilon^{r})
, we deduce
that this quantity is at most
\varepsilon
, so the desired result follows
from Markov’s inequality.
∎

Next, we shall prove an analogue of a result in
[
8
]
,
relating
single conflict colorability of hypergraphs to the degeneracy.
Consider the following procedure, which is a hypergraph version of a simple algorithm described in

[
8
]
.

Let
H
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices with an ordering of the vertices
(v_{i})_{i\in[n]}
and let
d_{i}^{-}
be the
back degree of
v_{i}
, which is equal to the degree of
v_{i}
in the hypergraph induced by vertices whose indices are less than or equal to
i
. If
H
is
d
-degenerate, there is an ordering such that
d_{i}^{-}\leq d
for all
i\in[n]
.

Procedure 3.4
.

Our input is a
d
-degenerate
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
and a local

k
-partition
\{c_{v}\}
of
H
. Let
n=|V(H)|
.

1.

Order the vertices
V(H)
as
(v_{i})_{i\in[n]}
so that
d_{i}^{-}\leq d
for all
i\in[n]
.

2.

Set
\varphi(v_{1})=1
and repeat as
i
goes from
2
to
n
:

(a)

Let
L(v_{i})=[k]\setminus\{c_{v_{i}}(e)\text{ }|\text{ }\varphi(u)=c_{u}(e)\text{ for all }u\in e\setminus\{v_{i}\}\}
.

(b)

If
L(v_{i})\neq\emptyset
, let
\varphi(v_{i})
be the smallest integer in
L(v_{i})
. Otherwise, let
\varphi(v_{i})=1
.

3.

Output
\varphi
.

Next, as in
[
8
]
, we run Procedure
3.4
on a random local
k
-partition of
H
.
For each
i\in[n]
and
j\in[k]
, let
X_{ij}
be the indicator random variable of the event
j\in L(v_{i})
, that is,
j
is an available color to choose from at step
i
, and let
Y_{ij}=1-X_{ij}
.

A family of
\{0,1\}
-valued random variables
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{s}
is
negatively correlated
if for every subset

I\subseteq[s]
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in I}\{Z_{i}=1\}\right]\leq\prod_{i\in I}\mathbb{P}[Z_{i}=1].

Lemma 3.5
.

The following holds for the set of variables
X_{ij}
and
Y_{ij}
defined above.

(i)

For all
i\in[n]
and
j\in[k]
, we have

\mathbb{E}[X_{ij}]\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k^{r}}\right)^{d}.

(ii)

For each
i\in[n]
, the variables
(Y_{ij})_{j\in[k]}
are negatively correlated.

Proof.

(i): Fix
i\in[n]
and let
u_{1},\dots,u_{t}
be the neighbors of
v_{i}
preceding it in the ordering
(v_{i})_{i\in[n]}
. For an edge
e\subseteq\{v_{i},u_{1},\dots,u_{t}\}
and a color
j\in[k]
, let
A_{ej}
be the event that

j=c_{v_{i}}(e)\text{ and }\varphi(u)=c_{u}(e),\text{ }\forall u\in e\setminus\{v_{i}\}.

If
A_{ej}
occurs for some edge
e
, then the color
j
is not available to choose at step
i
. The number of edges containing
v_{i}
and
r-1
of the vertices
u_{1},\dots,u_{t}
is
d_{i}^{-}\leq d
. We deduce that

\mathbb{E}[X_{ij}]\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k^{r}}\right)^{d}
,
since

\mathbb{P}[A_{ej}]=\mathbb{P}[j=c_{v_{i}}(e)\text{ and }\varphi(u)=c_{u}(e)\text{ }\forall u\in e\setminus\{v_{i}\}]=\frac{1}{k^{r}},

because the choice of
\varphi
on
u_{1},\dots,u_{t}
is probabilistically independent from
the choice of conflicts
c_{u}
for the edge
e
.

(ii): The proof of this fact is slightly technical and completely identical to the corresponding
result for DP-coloring of graphs in
[
8
]
, so we omit it.
∎

We shall use the following
Chernoff-type bound for random variables satisfying negative correlation

[
43
,
44
]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(X_{i})_{i\in[k]}
be binary random variables. Set
Y_{i}=1-X_{i}
and
X=\sum_{i\in[k]}X_{i}
. If
(Y_{i})_{i\in[k]}
are negatively correlated, then

\mathbb{P}[X<\mathbb{E}[X]-t]<\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\mathbb{E}(X)}\right)\quad\text{for all }0<t\leq\mathbb{E}[X].

We can now prove the following analogue of Theorem 1.3 in
[
8
]
,
which gives a sufficient condition for an
r
-uniform hypergraph to be single conflict
k
-colorable whp.
The proof is essentially the same as in
[
8
]
; for completeness, we provide
a brief sketch.

Theorem 3.7
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that the following holds. Let
H
be a
d
-degenerate
r
-uniform hypergraph with
n\geq n_{0}
vertices such that

d\geq\log^{1.1/\varepsilon}n
. If

k\geq(r+\delta)^{1/r}\left(\frac{d}{\log d}\right)^{1/r},

then
H
is single conflict
k
-colorable with probability at least
1-\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Fix
r
,
\varepsilon>0
, let
c=(r+\delta)^{1/r}
, assume
k
satisfies the lower bound in the theorem, and let

X_{i}=\sum_{j\in[k]}X_{i,j}
.

By Lemma
3.5
(i), we have

\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]\geq k\left(1-\frac{1}{k^{r}}\right)^{d}\geq k\exp\left(-\frac{d}{k^{r}}(1+o(1))\right).

Using the fact that
k\geq c(d/\log d)^{1/r}
, we get

\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]\geq c\left(\frac{d}{\log d}\right)^{1/r}\exp\left(-\frac{1+o(1)}{c^{r}}\log d\right)=\frac{c}{\log^{1/r}d}\cdot d^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1+o(1)}{c^{r}}}>d^{99\epsilon/100},

because

\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1+o(1)}{c^{r}}=\frac{\delta-o(1)}{c^{r}}>\varepsilon,

for large enough
n
,
and

\frac{\log^{1/r}d}{c}<d^{\varepsilon/100},

which holds for large enough
n
.

Using Lemma
3.6
and part (ii)
of Lemma
3.5
,
we deduce that

\mathbb{P}[X_{i}=0]\leq\mathbb{P}[X_{i}\leq\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]/2]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{d^{99\epsilon/100}}{8}\right)<\varepsilon/n,

and by a union bound, it thus holds that

\mathbb{P}[X_{i}=0\text{ for some $i$}]\leq\varepsilon
.
∎

For
r=2
, we roughly get the bound
k\geq 2\sqrt{\frac{d}{\log d}}

which ensures single

-conflict colorability
for ordinary graphs,
compared to the corresponding bound

for random
DP-colorability in

.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex

-degenerate graph with

, where
\varepsilon>0
. If

k\geq\sqrt{(2+\varepsilon)}\sqrt{\frac{d}{\log d}},

then whp
G
is single conflict

-colorable.

We remark that the lower bound on

in Theorem
3.7
and
Corollary
3.8
is necessary. This can be verified as in

[
8
]
(see Proposition 1.5).
Moreover,
similarly to
[
8
]
, we believe that it is possible to prove
a better lower bound on
k
for dense graphs. Specifically, we conjecture that
for a sufficiently dense
r
-uniform hypergraph

the property of being single conflict colorable has a sharp threshold
at
\left(\frac{\rho(H)}{\log\rho(H)}\right)^{1/r}
.

For an
n
-vertex complete
r
-uniform hypergraph this suggests that

k=\left(\frac{n^{r-1}}{r!\log n}\right)^{1/r}
is
the threshold for single conflict colorability. We prove that
this is true for the case of ordinary graphs.

Theorem 3.9
.

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that the following holds. Suppose
G
is a graph with
n\geq n_{0}
vertices,
m\geq(1-o(1/\log^{2}n))n^{2}/2
edges, and maximum density
\rho(G)=m/n
. If

k\geq(1+\delta)\sqrt{\frac{\rho(G)}{\log\rho(G)}},

then
G
is single conflict
k
-colorable with probability at least
1-\varepsilon
.

Again, the proof of this theorem is inspired by a result in
[
8
]
.
In particular, we stress that the overall approach and many calculations are similar to the proof of
Theorem 1.2 in that paper. However, the detailed confirmation of
convergence requires different arguments than the ones
in
[
8
]
; thus, although
somewhat lengthy and heavily inspired by the proof in
[
8
]
,
we choose to give a complete proof of this theorem.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and consider a graph
G
with
n
vertices,
m=(1-o(1/\log^{2}n))n^{2}/2
edges, and maximum density
\rho=\rho(G)
. Let
k=(1+\delta)\sqrt{\rho/\log\rho}
.

Let
\{c_{v}\}
be a random local
k
-partition and enumerate all vertex
k
-colorings

\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k^{n}}
of
G
. Let
E_{i}
be the event that
\varphi_{i}
is a conflict
\{c_{v}\}
-coloring and
X_{i}
its indicator random variable, for
i\in[k^{n}]
, and set
X=\sum_{i=1}^{k^{n}}X_{i}
.
We shall use Chebyshev’s inequality in the following form to bound
\mathbb{P}[X>0]
from below:

\mathbb{P}[X>0]=1-\mathbb{P}[X=0]\geq 1-\frac{\text{Var}[X]}{\mathbb{E}[X]^{2}}.

Our task is thus to show that
\text{Var}[X]/\mathbb{E}[X]^{2}=o(1)
.
Notice that

\displaystyle\text{Var}[X]=\sum_{(i,j)}\text{Cov}[X_{i},X_{j}]=\sum_{(i,j)}\left(\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{j}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]\mathbb{E}[X_{j}]\right)=\sum_{(i,j)}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{j}]-\mathbb{E}[X]^{2},

where the sums are taken over
(i,j)\in[k^{n}]^{2}
.
The expectation of
X
is

\mathbb{E}[X]=k^{n}(1-1/k^{2})^{m}=k^{n}p^{m},\quad\text{with }p:=1-1/k^{2}.

Next, we compute
\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{j}]
. For an edge
uv\in E(G)
,
define, for
\ell\in\{i,j\}
,
A^{\ell}_{uv}
to be the event that
\varphi_{\ell}(u)=c_{u}(uv)
and
\varphi_{\ell}(v)=c_{v}(uv)
does not hold, and let
E_{uv}=A^{i}_{uv}\cap A^{j}_{uv}
. We consider two cases.

Case A.

\varphi_{i}(u)=\varphi_{j}(u)
and
\varphi_{i}(v)=\varphi_{j}(v)
:

In this case we have that

\mathbb{P}[E_{uv}]=\mathbb{P}[A^{i}_{uv}]=1-1/k^{2}=p.

Case B.

\varphi_{i}(u)\neq\varphi_{j}(u)
:

In this case we have that

\mathbb{P}[E_{uv}]=\mathbb{P}[(c_{u}(uv),c_{v}(uv))\notin\{(\varphi_{i}(u),\varphi_{i}(v)),(\varphi_{j}(u),\varphi_{j}(v))\}]=\frac{k^{2}-2}{k^{2}}=:q.

Let the number of edges in
G
satisfying the conditions from Cases A and B be
\alpha_{i,j}
and
\beta_{i,j}
, respectively. Then, since
\alpha_{i,j}+\beta_{i,j}=m
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{j}]=p^{\alpha_{i,j}}q^{\beta_{i,j}}=p^{2m}\left(1+\frac{1}{k^{2}-2}\right)^{\alpha_{i,j}}\left(1-\frac{1}{(k^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)^{m}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{\text{Var}[X]}{\mathbb{E}[X]^{2}}

\displaystyle=k^{-2n}p^{-2m}\sum_{(i,j)}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{j}]-1

\displaystyle=k^{-2n}\left(1-\frac{1}{(k^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)^{m}\sum_{(i,j)}\left(1+\frac{1}{k^{2}-2}\right)^{\alpha_{i,j}}-1.

Given
i
and
j
,
\alpha_{i,j}
is the number of edges
uv
such that
\varphi_{i}(u)=\varphi_{j}(u)
and

\varphi_{i}(v)=\varphi_{j}(v)
. We let
I_{i}=\{(v,\varphi_{i}(v))\text{ }|\text{ }v\in V(G)\}
and
I_{j}=\{(v,\varphi_{j})\text{ }|\text{ }v\in V(G)\}
.
Since the density of every subgraph
G
is at most
\rho
, we conclude that if
|I_{i}\cap I_{j}|=\nu
, then

\alpha_{i,j}\leq\mu(\nu):=\min\left\{\binom{\nu}{2},\rho\nu\right\}\leq\min\left\{\frac{\nu^{2}}{2},\rho\nu\right\}.

There are
\binom{n}{\nu}k^{n}(k-1)^{n-\nu}
pairs of colorings
\varphi_{i}
and
\varphi_{j}
such that
|I_{i}\cap I_{j}|=\nu
; thus

\displaystyle\frac{\text{Var}[X]}{\mathbb{E}[X]^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{(k^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)^{m}\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}\left(\binom{n}{\nu}k^{-\nu}\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)^{n-\nu}\left(1+\frac{1}{k^{2}-2}\right)^{\mu(\nu)}\right)-1,

and by defining

g(\nu)=\binom{n}{\nu}k^{-\nu}\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)^{n-\nu}\left(1+\frac{1}{k^{2}-2}\right)^{\mu(\nu)},

the bound reads

\frac{\text{Var}[X]}{\mathbb{E}[X]^{2}}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{(k^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)^{m}\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}g(\nu)-1.

We shall prove that
(1-1/(k^{2}-1)^{2})^{m}\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}g(\nu)\leq 1+o(1)
, by considering several different
cases, depending on the magnitude of
\nu
. In many of the cases, we will use the following two estimates:

\displaystyle g(\nu)\leq\left(\frac{ne^{1+1/k}e^{\mu(\nu)/(k^{2}-2)\nu}}{k\nu e^{n/k\nu}}\right)^{\nu}\leq\left((1+o(1))e^{\mu(\nu)/k^{2}\nu}\right)^{\nu},

(1)

and

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{(k^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)^{m}\leq(1+o(1))e^{-m/k^{4}}.

(2)

Case 1.

0\leq\nu\leq(1-\alpha)n/k
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
:

With
\mu(\nu)\leq\frac{\nu(1-\alpha)n}{2k}
in (
1
), we have

g(\nu)\leq\exp\left(\nu(1+o(1))\frac{(1-\alpha)n}{2k^{3}}\right)=:r^{\nu},

and so,

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq(1-\alpha)\frac{n}{k}}g(\nu)\leq\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq(1-\alpha)\frac{n}{k}}r^{\nu}\leq r^{(1-\alpha)n/k}(1+o(1)).

This, together with (
2
), yield

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{(k^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)^{m}\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq(1-\alpha)\frac{n}{k}}g(\nu)

\displaystyle\leq(1+o(1))\exp\left(-\frac{m}{k^{4}}+\frac{(1-\alpha)^{2}n^{2}}{2k^{4}}(1+o(1))\right)=o(1),

since
\alpha>0
and
m=(1-o(1))n^{2}/2
.

Case 2.

(1-\alpha)n/k\leq\nu\leq n/k-\log n
:

Using Stirling approximation of
\nu!
, we get

\displaystyle g(\nu)\leq\frac{1}{c\sqrt{\nu}}\left((1+o(1))\exp\left(\frac{1}{2k^{2}}\left(\frac{n}{k}-\log n\right)\right)\right)^{\nu},

where
c>0
is a constant. Next, since
\nu\geq(1-\alpha)n/k
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c\sqrt{\nu}}\leq D\exp\left(-\frac{n}{2k}\right),

where
D=\frac{1}{c\sqrt{1-\alpha}}
. We bound
\sum g(\nu)
as in Case 1 to get

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{(k^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)^{m}\sum_{\frac{n}{k}(1-\alpha)\leq\nu\leq\frac{n}{k}-\log n}g(\nu)

\displaystyle\leq(1+o(1))De^{-m/k^{4}}e^{-n/2k}e^{(n/k-\log n)^{2}/2k^{2}}=o(1),

because
k=\Omega(\sqrt{n/\log n})
and
m=(1-o(1/\log^{2}n))n^{2}/2
.

Case 3.

n/k-\log n\leq\nu\leq n/k+\log^{5/2}n
:

As in Case 1, but with
\nu\leq n/k+\log^{5/2}n
, we get

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{(k^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)^{m}\sum_{\frac{n}{k}-\log n\leq\nu\leq\frac{n}{k}+\log^{5/2}n}g(\nu)

\displaystyle\leq(1+o(1))\exp\left(\frac{m}{2k^{4}}+\frac{1}{2k^{2}}\left(\frac{n}{k}+\log^{5/2}n\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+o(1))\exp\left(-\frac{n^{2}}{2k^{4}}o(1/\log^{2}n)+\frac{n\log^{5/2}n}{k^{3}}+\frac{\log^{5}n}{2k^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=1+o(1).

Case 4.

n/k+\log^{5/2}n\leq\nu\leq(1+\alpha)n/k
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
:

Since
k=\Omega(\sqrt{n/\log n})
we have for a constant
C>0

\displaystyle\nu\geq\frac{n}{k}+\log^{5/2}n=\sqrt{\log n}\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{C}+\log^{2}n\right),

thus, with
\nu\leq(1+\alpha)n/k
and
D=\frac{C}{1+o(1)}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\leq\frac{n}{k\nu}\leq 1-\frac{C\log^{2}n}{\sqrt{n}+C\log^{2}n}=1-\frac{D\log^{2}n}{\sqrt{n}},

where the first inequality implies that
e^{-n/k\nu}\leq 1
. We bound
g(\nu)
as

\displaystyle g(\nu)

\displaystyle\leq\left((1+o(1))\left(1-\frac{D\log^{2}n}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{n}{2k^{3}}(1+\alpha)(1+o(1))\right)\right)^{\nu},

which we use together with (
2
) and
1+x\leq e^{x}
to get

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{(k^{2}-1)^{2}}\right)^{m}\sum_{\frac{n}{k}+\log^{5/2}n\leq\nu\leq\frac{n}{k}(1+\alpha)}g(\nu)

\displaystyle\leq(1+o(1))\exp\left(-\frac{m}{k^{4}}-\frac{Dn\log^{2}n}{k\sqrt{n}}(1+\alpha)+\frac{n^{2}}{2k^{4}}(1+\alpha)^{2}(1+o(1))\right)

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\exp\left(-\frac{n^{2}}{2k^{4}}o(1/\log^{2}n)-\frac{D(1+\alpha)\sqrt{n}\log^{2}n}{k}+(2\alpha+\alpha^{2})\frac{n^{2}}{k^{4}}(1+o(1))\right)

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\exp\left(o(1)-D^{\prime}\log^{5/2}n+E\log^{2}n\right)=o(1),

where
D^{\prime},E>0
.

Case 5.

(1+\alpha)n/k\leq\nu\leq n^{1-\varepsilon}
:

Since
n/k\nu\leq 1/(1+\alpha)<1
, we get that

\frac{n}{k\nu}e^{-n/k\nu}\leq(1-\xi)/e
for some
\xi\in(0,1)
, since
xe^{-x}
has maximum
1/e
at
x=1
. With
\mu(\nu)\leq\nu^{2}
, we write

\exp\left(\frac{\mu(\nu)}{k^{2}-2}\right)\leq(1+o(1))\exp\left(\frac{\nu n^{1-\varepsilon}}{n/\log n}\right)=(1+o(1))\exp\left(\nu(1+o(1))\right),

so
g(\nu)\leq\left((1+o(1))(1-\xi)\right)^{\nu}
and thus,

\sum_{\frac{n}{k}(1+\alpha)\leq\nu\leq n^{1-\varepsilon}}g(\nu)\leq(1+o(1))\frac{(1-\xi)^{(1+\alpha)\frac{n}{k}}}{\xi}=o(1).

Case 6.

n^{1-\varepsilon}\leq\nu\leq n
:

In this case we have that
\frac{n}{k\nu}\leq\frac{n^{\varepsilon}}{k}
and

e^{-n/k\nu}=1+o(1)
. Furthermore, with
k\geq C\sqrt{n/\log n}
for a constant
C>0
, we have

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{\mu(\nu)}{k^{2}-2}\right)\leq(1+o(1))\exp\left(\frac{\nu}{2C^{2}}\log n\right).

Hence, we get that

\displaystyle g(\nu)\leq\left(e(1+o(1))n^{\varepsilon+1/2C^{2}-1/2}\sqrt{\log n})\right)^{\nu}=o(1),

as long as

C>\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-2\varepsilon}}
.

Summing up, we conlude from Cases 1-6 that
(1-1/(k^{2}-1)^{2})^{m}\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}g(\nu)\leq 1+o(1)
,
and thus the theorem follows.
∎

The following follows from the preceding theorem and Proposition
3.2
.

Corollary 3.10
.

For the complete graph
K_{n}
the property of being single conflict colorable from a
random local
k
-partition has a sharp threshold at
k=\sqrt{\frac{n}{2\log n}}
.

4
Palette sparsification of list coloring of hypergraphs

In this section we consider the related problem of
palette sparsification for list coloring of hypergraphs.
The problem of coloring hypergraphs from randomly chosen
lists was recently initiated in
[
15
]
,
but was first studied for the case of ordinary graphs
in the following setting.
Assign lists of
colors to the vertices of a hypergraph
H=H(n)
with

n
vertices by choosing for
each vertex
v
its list
L(v)
independently and
uniformly at random
from all
k
-subsets of a
color set
\mathcal{C}=\{1,2,\dots,\sigma\}
.
Such a list assignment is called a

random
(k,\sigma)
-list assignment
for
H
.
Given that
k
is a fixed integer, the question studied in
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
29
,
39
,
40
]
is
how large
\sigma=\sigma(n)

should be
in order to guarantee that with
probability tending to
1
as
n\to\infty

there is a proper coloring
of the vertices of
H
with colors
from the random list assignment. Analogous questions for hypergraphs are studied in
[
15
]
.

Alternatively, we can fix
\sigma

(as a function of
n
), and ask how large
k=k(n)
should be
to ensure that
the random lists support a proper coloring with probability
tending to
1
.
This question was first studied in

[
5
,
18
]
, and
following the breakthrough result by Assadi et al.
[
6
]

this type of “palette sparsification” questions have recently
received considerable attention in the setting of graphs

[
2
,
19
,
31
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
42
]
.
Analogous questions for hypergraphs are, however, almost completely unexplored so far.

In
[
15
]
, the first author
proved a palette sparsification result for complete
r
-uniform hypergraphs.
Here we obtain a palette sparsification-type result for linear hypergraphs, that is,
hypergraphs where any two vertices are contained in at most one edge.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
H=H(n)
be a linear
r
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices with maximum degree

\Delta
.
For every constant
C>(2^{r}er)^{1/(r-1)}
, there is a constant
A>0
, such that if

k\geq A(\log n)^{1/r}
,
\sigma\geq C\Delta^{1/(r-1)}
and

L
is random

(k,\sigma)
-list assignment for
H
,
then
H
is whp
L
-colorable.

Before proving this theorem, we briefly discuss this result.
The lower bound
\Delta^{1/(r-1)}
on
\sigma

matches the best known upper bound on the choice number
of linear
r
-uniform hypergraphs, although this upper bound has been improved for the
ordinary chromatic
number
[
27
]
.
It would interesting to investigate whether this lower bound on
\sigma
for linear

r
-uniform hypergraphs could be improved.

We note that this theorem does not extend to general hypergraphs,
which the following example shows.
Let
A,C>0
be constants,
\sigma=C\Delta^{1/(r-1)}
,

k=A(\log n)^{1/r}
, and consider a
random
(k,\sigma)
-list assignment for an
r
-uniform hypergraph
H=H(n)
of order
n
that
is a disjoint union of
\lfloor n/(k(r-1)+1))\rfloor
complete hypergraphs of order
k(r-1)+1

(and possibly some isolated vertices).
Note that the maximum degree
\Delta
in
H
is

\Delta=\binom{k(r-1)+1}{r-1}\sim\frac{(k(r-1)+1)^{r-1}}{(r-1)!},

so
\sigma\sim\frac{Ck(r-1)}{((r-1)!)^{1/(r-1)}}
.

Now, since
H
is a disjoint union of
(k(r-1)+1)
-cliques, it is
L
-colorable if and only if
there is no clique such that all vertices get the same color list.
Now, the probability that
all vertices in such a clique get the same list is

\binom{\sigma}{k}^{-k(r-1)}\geq 2^{-Ck^{2}(r-1)}.

Thus the probability that there is no such clique in
H
is at most

\left(1-2^{-Ck^{2}(r-1)}\right)^{\frac{n}{k(r-1)+1}}\leq\exp\left(-2^{-Ck^{2}(r-1)}\frac{n}{k(r-1)+1}\right),

which tends to zero as
n\to\infty
, for any choice of the constants
A
and
C
.
Thus, whp
H
is not
L
-colorable. In conclusion, Theorem
4.1
does not extend to general

r
-uniform hypergraphs, which answers a question from
[
15
]
.

Finally, let us remark that for general
r
-uniform hypergraphs, it was proved in
[
15
]

that for every constant
C>0
there is a (relatively small)
constant
A
such that if

\sigma\geq C\Delta^{1/(r-1)}\log n,\,k\geq A\log n,

and
L
is a random
(k,\sigma)
-list assignment for an
n
-vertex
r
-uniform
hypergraph
H
with maximum degree
\Delta
, then
H
is whp
L
-colorable.
It would be interesting to investigate if the factor
\log n

could be removed from the bound on
\sigma
, which would be best possible (up to multiplicative constants)
by the example of complete
r
-uniform
hypergraphs
[
15
]
.

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
We shall use a Reed-type result on hypergraph list coloring involving color degrees
due to Drgas-Burchardt
[
20
]
, which does not appear to be very well-known. (Indeed, while working
on the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we first proved a version of Theorem
4.2
below, but later discovered
the result of Drgas-Burchardt while preparing this manuscript.)

First we introduce (and simplify) some terminology from that paper.
For a hypergraph
H
with a list assignment
L
, we denote by
H_{c}(L)
the subgraph of
H
induced by all edges where every vertex contains the color
c
in its list. We denote by
d_{c}(v,H,L)

the
color degree
of
(c,v)
in
H
, that is, the number of edges containing

v
such that every vertex in these edges has the color
c
in its list. Similarly, the
edge-color degree

d_{c}(e,H,L)
of a pair
(e,c)
, where
e
is an edge, is the sum

d_{c}(e,H,L)=\sum_{v\in e}d_{c}(v,H,L).

The
color-degree

d_{col}(e)
of an edge
e
is defined as

d_{col}(e,L)=\sum_{c\in L(v):v\in e}d_{c}(e,H,L).

We denote by
\Delta_{col}(H,L)
, the maximum color-degree of an edge in
H
.
Using the Lovasz local lemma, Drgas-Burchardt proved the following theorem
[
20
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
H
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph and
L
a
k
-list assignment for
H
.
If
k\geq\left(e(\Delta_{col}(H,L)+1)\right)^{1/r}
, then
H
is
L
-colorable.

We shall also use a standard version of Chernoff’s inequality for binomially distributed random variables,
see e.g.
[
32
]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X=X_{1}+\dots+X_{n}
be a sum of
n
identically Bernoulli distributed random variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}[X-\mathbb{E}[X]\geq\varepsilon\mathbb{E}[X]]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{3}\mathbb{E}[X]\right).

Using these auxiliary results, we shall prove Theorem
4.1
. Our proof is a modification of a result
in
[
2
]
to the setting of hypergraphs.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
C>(2^{r}er)^{1/(r-1)}
,
A
a “large” constant, and consider a random

(k,\sigma)
-list assignment
L
for
H
.
Let
d_{c}(v,H,L)
be a random variable counting the color degree of
(c,v)
in
H
. Then

\mathbb{E}[d_{c}(v,H,L)]\leq\Delta\frac{\binom{\sigma-1}{k-1}^{r-1}}{\binom{\sigma}{k}^{r-1}}\leq\Delta\left(\frac{k}{\sigma}\right)^{r-1}.

Since we choose the colors for each vertex independently and
H
is linear, we can apply Chernoff’s inequality to obtain,

\mathbb{P}\left[d_{c}(v,H,L)>(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}[d_{c}(v,H,L)]\right]<\exp\left(\frac{-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta}{3}\left(\frac{k}{\sigma}\right)^{r-1}\right),

where
\varepsilon>0
is an arbitrarily small constant.

We say that a color
c\in L(v)
is
bad
for
v
if
it satisfies that

d_{c}(v,H,L)>(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}[d_{c}(v,H,L)].

The probability that a vertex
v
has at least
\alpha k
bad colors,
where
\alpha>0
is a small constant, is at most

\mathbb{P}[v\textrm{ has at least $\alpha k$ bad colors}]\leq\binom{k}{\alpha k}\exp\left(\frac{-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta}{3}\left(\frac{k}{\sigma}\right)^{r-1}\right)^{\alpha k}\leq 2^{k}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\alpha k^{r}}{3C^{r-1}}}.

Now, since
k\geq A(\log n)^{1/r}
, and
A
is large enough, taking a union bound over all vertices yields that whp every vertex
has less than
\alpha k
bad colors.

We define a new random list assignment
L^{\prime}
by
for every vertex
v
removing all bad colors from
L(v)
.
Then
|L^{\prime}(v)|\geq(1-\alpha)k
for every vertex
v
, and moreover, since every edge contains
r
vertices, we get that

d_{col}(e,L^{\prime})\leq rk(1+\varepsilon)\Delta\left(\frac{k}{\sigma}\right)^{r-1}\leq rk\frac{(1+\varepsilon)k^{r-1}}{C^{r-1}}=\frac{r(1+\varepsilon)k^{r}}{C^{r-1}}.

Thus,

\left(e(\Delta_{col}(H,L)+1)\right)^{1/r}\leq\left(\frac{e(r(1+\varepsilon)k^{r})}{C^{r-1}}+e\right)^{1/r}.

Now,
since
C>(2^{r}er)^{1/(r-1)}
and
\varepsilon
is arbitrarily small, we have that

|L^{\prime}(v)|\geq\left(e(\Delta_{col}(H,L)+1)\right)^{1/r},

for every vertex
v
,
so Theorem
4.2
yields that whp
H
is
L
-colorable.
∎

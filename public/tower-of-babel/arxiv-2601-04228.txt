Non-Archimedean Brauer Oval (of Cassini) Theorem and Applications
K. Mahesh Krishna

Abstract




Abstract
: Nica and Sprague [
Am. Math. Mon., 2023
]
derived a non-Archimedean version of the Gershgorin disk theorem. We derive a non-Archimedean version of the oval (of Cassini) theorem by Brauer [
Duke Math. J., 1947
] which generalizes the Nica-Sprague disk theorem. We provide applications for bounding the zeros of polynomials over non-Archimedean fields. We also show that our result is equivalent to the non-Archimedean version of the Ostrowski nonsingularity theorem derived by Li and Li [
J. Comput. Appl. Math., 2025
].

Keywords
: Eigenvalue, Disk, Oval, Non-Archimedean valued field.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
: 15A18, 15A42, 12J25, 26E30.

1.
Introduction

Let
A=[a_{j,k}]_{1\leq j\leq n,1\leq k\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. For
1\leq j\leq n
, define

\displaystyle r_{j}(A)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1,k\neq j}^{n}|a_{j,k}|.

For
1\leq k\leq n
, define

\displaystyle c_{k}(A)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1,j\neq k}^{n}|a_{j,k}|.

Let
\sigma(A)
be the set of all eigenvalues of
A
. In 1931, Gershgorin proved the following breakthrough result known as the Gershgorin circle/disk theorem which uses single row/column for determining the radius of the disk.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
9
,
11
,
20
,
8
]
(
Gershgorin Eigenvalue Inclusion Theorem
or
Gershgorin Disk Theorem
)
For every
A=[a_{j,k}]_{1\leq j\leq n,1\leq k\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,

\displaystyle\sigma(A)\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a_{j,j}|\leq r_{j}(A)\}

and

\displaystyle\sigma(A)\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a_{k,k}|\leq c_{k}(A)\}.

A remarkable application of Theorem
1.1
is the following result of Frobenius (which advances result of Browne).

Theorem 1.2
.

[
4
,
13
,
6
]

Let
A=[a_{j,k}]_{1\leq j\leq n,1\leq k\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. For every
\lambda\in\sigma(A)
,

\displaystyle|\lambda|\leq\min\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{j,k}|,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|a_{j,k}|\right\}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{j,k}|+\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|a_{j,k}|\right).

In particular,

\displaystyle|\text{det}(A)|

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{j,k}|\right)^{n},\left(\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|a_{j,k}|\right)^{n}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{j,k}|\right)^{n}+\left(\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|a_{j,k}|\right)^{n}\right).

Another remarkable application of Theorem
1.1
is on the bounds for the zeros of polynomials. Let
p(z)\coloneqq c_{0}+c_{1}z+\cdots+c_{n-1}z^{n-1}+z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z]
. A direct observation reveals that the zeros of
p
are the eigenvalues of the Frobenius companion matrix

\displaystyle C_{p}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&\cdots&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&1&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&0&1\\
-c_{0}&-c_{1}&-c_{2}&\cdots&-c_{n-3}&-c_{n-2}&-c_{n-1}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

and the eigenvalues of
C_{p}
are the same as the zeros of
p

[
2
,
3
]
. In 1965, Bell derived following bounds for the zeros of
p
using Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
1
]

Let
p(z)\coloneqq c_{0}+c_{1}z+\cdots+c_{n-1}z^{n-1}+z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z]
. If
\lambda
is a zero of
p
, then

\displaystyle|\lambda|\leq 1

or

\displaystyle|\lambda+c_{n-1}|\leq|c_{0}|+\cdots+|c_{n-2}|.

In particular,

(1)

\displaystyle\text{(Lagrange bound)}\quad\quad\quad|\lambda|\leq\max\{1,|c_{0}|+\cdots+|c_{n-1}|\}.

Note that Inequality (
1
) is a generalization of famous Montel bound
[
11
]
which says that

\displaystyle|\lambda|\leq 1+|c_{0}|+\cdots+|c_{n-1}|.

It is interesting to note that one can give a proof of Inequality (
1
) without using companion matrix
[
10
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
1
]

Let
p(z)\coloneqq c_{0}+c_{1}z+\cdots+c_{n-1}z^{n-1}+z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z]
. If
\lambda
is a zero of
p
, then

\displaystyle|\lambda|\leq|c_{0}|

or

\displaystyle|\lambda|\leq 1+|c_{j}|,\quad\text{ for some }1\leq j\leq n-2

or

\displaystyle|\lambda+c_{n-1}|\leq 1.

In particular,

(2)

\displaystyle\text{(Bell bound)}\quad\quad\quad|\lambda|\leq\max\{|c_{0}|,1+|c_{1}|,\dots,1+|c_{n-1}|\}.

Note that Inequality (
2
) is a generalization of famous Cauchy bound
[
14
]
which says that

\displaystyle|\lambda|\leq 1+\max\{|c_{0}|,\dots,|c_{n-1}|\}.

Let
p(z)\coloneqq c_{0}+c_{1}z+\cdots+c_{n-1}z^{n-1}+z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z]
with
c_{0}\neq 0
. Define

\displaystyle q(z)\coloneqq\frac{1}{c_{0}}z^{n}p\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)=\frac{1}{c_{0}}+\frac{c_{n-1}}{c_{0}}z+\cdots+\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}}z^{n-1}+z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z].

We note that
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
satisfies
p(\lambda)=0
if and only if
q(1/\lambda)=0
. Frobenius companion matrix of
q
is

\displaystyle C_{q}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&\cdots&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&1&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&0&1\\
\frac{-1}{c_{0}}&\frac{-c_{n-1}}{c_{0}}&\frac{-c_{n-2}}{c_{0}}&\cdots&\frac{-c_{3}}{c_{0}}&\frac{-c_{2}}{c_{0}}&\frac{-c_{1}}{c_{0}}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

Now by applying earlier two results to the polynomial
q
and rearranging, we get following results.

Theorem 1.5
.

[
1
,
11
]

Let
p(z)\coloneqq c_{0}+c_{1}z+\cdots+c_{n-1}z^{n-1}+z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z]
with
c_{0}\neq 0
. If
\lambda
is a zero of
p
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\lambda|}\leq 1

or

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{|c_{0}|}+\frac{|c_{2}|}{|c_{0}|}+\cdots+\frac{|c_{n-1}|}{|c_{0}|}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\lambda|}\leq\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{|c_{0}|}+\frac{|c_{1}|}{|c_{0}|}+\frac{|c_{2}|}{|c_{0}|}+\cdots+\frac{|c_{n-1}|}{|c_{0}|}\right\}\leq 1+\frac{1}{|c_{0}|}+\frac{|c_{1}|}{|c_{0}|}+\cdots+\frac{|c_{n-1}|}{|c_{0}|}

and

\displaystyle\text{(Lagrange lower bound)}\quad\quad\quad|\lambda|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|c_{0}|}{\max\{|c_{0}|,1+|c_{1}|+\cdots+|c_{n-1}|\}}

(Montel lower bound)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|c_{0}|}{1+|c_{0}|+|c_{1}|+\cdots+|c_{n-1}|}.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
1
,
11
]

Let
p(z)\coloneqq c_{0}+c_{1}z+\cdots+c_{n-1}z^{n-1}+z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z]
with
c_{0}\neq 0
. If
\lambda
is a zero of
p
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\lambda|}\leq\frac{1}{|c_{0}|}

or

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\lambda|}\leq 1+\frac{|c_{j}|}{|c_{0}|},\quad\text{ for some }2\leq j\leq n-1

or

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}}\right|\leq 1.

In particular,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\lambda|}\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{|c_{0}|},1+\frac{|c_{1}|}{|c_{0}|},\dots,1+\frac{|c_{n-1}|}{|c_{0}|}\right\}\leq 1+\max\left\{\frac{1}{|c_{0}|},\frac{|c_{1}|}{|c_{0}|},\dots,\frac{|c_{n-1}|}{|c_{0}|}\right\}

and

\displaystyle\text{(Bell lower bound)}\quad\quad\quad|\lambda|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|c_{0}|}{\max\{1,|c_{0}|+|c_{1}|,\dots,|c_{0}|+|c_{n-1}|\}}

(Cauchy lower bound)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|c_{0}|}{|c_{0}|+\max\{1,|a_{1}|,\dots,|a_{n-1}|\}}.

A result much older than Gershgorin is the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

[
20
,
19
,
11
,
18
]
(
Strict Diagonal Dominance Theorem
or
Levy-Desplanques Theorem
)
If
A=[a_{j,k}]_{1\leq j\leq n,1\leq k\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
satisfies

\displaystyle|a_{j,j}|>r_{j}(A),\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n

or

\displaystyle|a_{k,k}|>c_{k}(A),\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq n,

then
A
is invertible.

and
1.7
are equivalent.

.

Let

\displaystyle\sigma(A)\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a_{j,j}|\leq r_{j}(A)\}.

(ii)

If

satisfies

\displaystyle|b_{j,j}|>r_{j}(B),\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n,

then
B
is invertible.

Theorem 1.9
.

[
20
]

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Following two statements are equivalent.

(i)

Let
A=[a_{j,k}]_{1\leq j\leq n,1\leq k\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Then

\displaystyle\sigma(A)\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a_{k,k}|\leq c_{k}(A)\}.

(ii)

If

\displaystyle|b_{k,k}|>c_{k}(B),\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq n,

In 1947, Brauer derived following generalization of Theorem
1.1
, known as the Brauer oval (of Cassini) theorem which uses two rows/columns for determining the radius of the oval.

Theorem 1.10
.

[
5
,
20
,
13
]
(
Brauer Eigenvalue Inclusion Theorem
or
Brauer Oval (of Cassini) Theorem
)
For every
A=[a_{j,k}]_{1\leq j\leq n,1\leq k\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,

\displaystyle\sigma(A)\subseteq\bigcup_{j,k=1,j\neq k}^{n}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a_{j,j}||z-a_{k,k}|\leq r_{j}(A)r_{k}(A)\}

and

\displaystyle\sigma(A)\subseteq\bigcup_{j,k=1,j\neq k}^{n}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a_{j,j}||z-a_{k,k}|\leq c_{j}(A)c_{k}(A)\}.

By applying Brauer’s theorem, we get following results.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
p(z)\coloneqq c_{0}+c_{1}z+\cdots+c_{n-1}z^{n-1}+z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z]
. If
\lambda
is a zero of
p
, then

\displaystyle|\lambda|\leq 1

or

\displaystyle|\lambda||\lambda+c_{n-1}|\leq|c_{0}|+\cdots+|c_{n-2}|.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
p(z)\coloneqq c_{0}+c_{1}z+\cdots+c_{n-1}z^{n-1}+z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z]
. If
\lambda
is a zero of
p
, then

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{2}\leq|c_{0}|(1+|c_{j}|),\quad\text{ for some }1\leq j\leq n-2

or

\displaystyle|\lambda||\lambda+c_{n-1}|\leq|c_{0}|

or

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{2}\leq(1+|c_{j}|)(1+|c_{k}|),\quad\text{ for some }1\leq j,k\leq n-2,j\neq k

or

\displaystyle|\lambda||\lambda+c_{n-1}|\leq 1+|c_{j}|,\quad\text{ for some }1\leq j\leq n-2.

[

\displaystyle|a_{j,j}||a_{k,k}|>c_{j}(A)c_{k}(A),\quad\forall 1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k,

then
A
is invertible.

1.1

1.7

1.10

In the paper,
\mathbb{K}
denotes a non-Archimedean valued field with valuation
|\cdot|
. Let
A=[a_{j,k}]_{1\leq j\leq n,1\leq k\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
. For
1\leq j\leq n
, define

\displaystyle h_{j}(A)\coloneqq\max_{1\leq k\leq n,k\neq j}|a_{j,k}|.

For
1\leq k\leq n
, define

\displaystyle v_{k}(A)\coloneqq\max_{1\leq j\leq n,j\neq k}|a_{j,k}|.

In 2023, Nica and Sprague derived the following non-Archimedean analogue of Theorems
1.1
and
1.7
.

Theorem 1.16
.

[
16
]
(

or

A=[a_{j,k}]_{1\leq j\leq n,1\leq k\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{K})

and

\displaystyle\sigma(A)\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}\{z\in\mathbb{K}:|z-a_{k,k}|\leq v_{k}(A)\}.

.

[
16
]
(

or

If
A=[a_{j,k}]_{1\leq j\leq n,1\leq k\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{K})

\displaystyle|a_{j,j}|>h_{j}(A),\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n

or

\displaystyle|a_{k,k}|>v_{k}(A),\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq n,

. If
|\lambda|\leq 1
, then clearly we have Inequality (
3
). So we assume that
|\lambda|>1
. Since
p(\lambda)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{c_{0}}{\lambda^{n}}+\frac{c_{1}}{\lambda^{n-1}}+\cdots+\frac{c_{n-1}}{\lambda}+1\right)\lambda^{n}=0.

Since
\lambda\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{\lambda^{n}}+\frac{c_{1}}{\lambda^{n-1}}+\cdots+\frac{c_{n-1}}{\lambda}+1=0.

Rearranging,

\displaystyle-1=\frac{c_{0}}{\lambda^{n}}+\frac{c_{1}}{\lambda^{n-1}}+\cdots+\frac{c_{n-1}}{\lambda}.

By taking absolute value and noticing
|\lambda|>1
, we get

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{c_{0}}{\lambda^{n}}+\frac{c_{1}}{\lambda^{n-1}}+\cdots+\frac{c_{n-1}}{\lambda}\right|\leq\max\left\{\frac{|c_{0}|}{|\lambda|^{n}},\frac{|c_{1}|}{|\lambda|^{n-1}},\dots,\frac{|c_{n-1}|}{|\lambda|}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{|c_{0}|}{|\lambda|},\frac{|c_{1}|}{|\lambda|},\dots,\frac{|c_{n-1}|}{|\lambda|}\right\}=\frac{1}{|\lambda|}\max\{|c_{0}|,|c_{1}|,\dots,|c_{n-1}|\}.

Rearranging above inequality completes the argument.

We start with non-Archimedean Brauer eigenvalue inclusion theorem. Our proof is motivated from the proof of Brauer

Theorem 2.1
.

(
Non-Archimedean Brauer Oval (of Cassini) Theorem
)

For every
A=[a_{j,k}]_{1\leq j\leq n,1\leq k\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
,

\displaystyle\sigma(A)\subseteq\bigcup_{j,k=1,j\neq k}^{n}\{z\in\mathbb{K}:|z-a_{j,j}||z-a_{k,k}|\leq h_{j}(A)h_{k}(A)\}

and

\displaystyle\sigma(A)\subseteq\bigcup_{j,k=1,j\neq k}^{n}\{z\in\mathbb{K}:|z-a_{j,j}||z-a_{k,k}|\leq v_{j}(A)v_{k}(A)\}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in\sigma(A)
. Then there exists a
0\neq\textbf{x}=(x_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
such that

(4)

\displaystyle\lambda\textbf{x}=A\textbf{x}.

Choose
1\leq j\leq n
such that

\displaystyle|x_{j}|=\max_{1\leq l\leq n}|x_{l}|.

Now choose
1\leq k\leq n
with
k\neq j
such that

\displaystyle|x_{k}|=\max_{1\leq l\leq n,l\neq j}|x_{l}|.

Then we have
1\leq j,k\leq n
with
j\neq k
and

(5)

\displaystyle|x_{j}|\geq|x_{k}|\geq\max_{1\leq l\leq n,l\neq j,l\neq k}|x_{l}|.

We have two cases. Case (i):
|x_{k}|=0
. Considering the
j
-th coordinate in Equation (
4
) gives

\displaystyle\lambda x_{j}=\sum_{p=1}^{n}a_{j,p}x_{p}.

Rewriting previous equation gives

\displaystyle(\lambda-a_{j,j})x_{j}=\sum_{p=1,p\neq j}^{n}a_{j,p}x_{p}.

Therefore using (
5
) we get

\displaystyle|(\lambda-a_{j,j})x_{j}|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{p=1,p\neq j}^{n}a_{j,p}x_{p}\right|\leq\max_{1\leq p\leq n,p\neq j}|a_{j,p}x_{p}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\max_{1\leq p\leq n,p\neq j}|a_{j,p}|\right)\left(\max_{1\leq p\leq n,p\neq j}|x_{p}|\right)=\left(\max_{1\leq p\leq n,p\neq j}|a_{j,p}|\right)|x_{k}|

\displaystyle=0.

Since
|x_{j}|\geq|x_{k}|
for all
1\leq k\leq n
,
|x_{k}|=0
and
\textbf{x}\neq 0
, we must have
|x_{j}|\neq 0
. Previous inequality then gives
|\lambda-a_{j,j}|=0
. So

\displaystyle\lambda=a_{j,j}\in\bigcup_{p,q=1,p\neq q}^{n}\{z\in\mathbb{K}:|z-a_{p,p}||z-a_{q,q}|\leq h_{p}(A)h_{q}(A)\}.

Case (ii):
|x_{k}|>0
. Considering
j
-th and
k
-th coordinates in Equation (
4
) give

(6)

\displaystyle(\lambda-a_{j,j})x_{j}=\sum_{p=1,p\neq j}^{n}a_{j,p}x_{p}

and

(7)

\displaystyle(\lambda-a_{k,k})x_{k}=\sum_{q=1,q\neq k}^{n}a_{k,q}x_{q}.

Multiplying Equations (
6
) and (
7
) and taking non-Archimedean valuation gives

\displaystyle|(\lambda-a_{j,j})x_{j}(\lambda-a_{k,k})x_{k}|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{p=1,p\neq j}^{n}a_{j,p}x_{p}\right|\left|\sum_{q=1,q\neq k}^{n}a_{k,q}x_{q}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\max_{1\leq p\leq n,p\neq j}|a_{j,p}x_{p}|\right)\left(\max_{1\leq q\leq n,q\neq k}|a_{k,p}x_{q}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\max_{1\leq p\leq n,p\neq j}|a_{j,p}|\right)\left(\max_{1\leq p\leq n,p\neq j}|x_{p}|\right)\left(\max_{1\leq q\leq n,q\neq k}|a_{k,p}|\right)\left(\max_{1\leq q\leq n,q\neq k}|x_{q}|\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\max_{1\leq p\leq n,p\neq j}|a_{j,p}|\right)|x_{k}|\left(\max_{1\leq q\leq n,q\neq k}|a_{k,p}|\right)|x_{j}|

\displaystyle=h_{j}(A)|x_{k}|h_{k}(A)|x_{j}|.

Therefore

\displaystyle|(\lambda-a_{j,j})(\lambda-a_{k,k})||x_{j}x_{k}|\leq h_{j}(A)h_{k}(A)|x_{j}||x_{k}|.

Since
|x_{j}x_{k}|\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle|(\lambda-a_{j,j})(\lambda-a_{k,k})|\leq h_{j}(A)h_{k}(A).

Previous inequality says that

\displaystyle\lambda\in\bigcup_{p,q=1,p\neq q}^{n}\{z\in\mathbb{K}:|z-a_{p,p}||z-a_{q,q}|\leq h_{p}(A)h_{q}(A)\}.

Second inclusion in the statement follows by considering the transpose of
A
and noting that the spectrum of a matrix and its transpose are equal. ∎

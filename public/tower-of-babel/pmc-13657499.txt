Hybrid Explainable Deep Ensemble Framework with self-attention mechanism for early prediction of depression severity using PHQ-9 clinical and behavioral data
Authors: Zeina T. Khaleel, Sarmad S. Abdullah, Majid S. Jabir
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Depression is a major global mental health disorder that requires accurate, reliable, and interpretable prediction models to support early identification and timely intervention. This study proposes a Hybrid Explainable Deep Ensemble Framework for multiclass prediction of depression severity using PHQ-9 clinical responses and behavioral characteristics.Methods:A retrospective cross-sectional machine learning study was conducted using a dataset comprising 682 anonymized participants. Predictor variables included demographic characteristics, PHQ-9 questionnaire items, sleep quality, academic pressure, and financial pressure. A hybrid ensemble integrating TabNet, multilayer perceptron (MLP), and XGBoost was developed with a self-attention mechanism for adaptive feature weighting and a weighted soft-voting strategy for final prediction. Model performance was evaluated using five-fold stratified cross-validation and an independent test set. Accuracy, precision, recall, macro F1-score, ROC-AUC, Matthews correlation coefficient, confusion matrix analysis, and SHAP-based explainability were employed to assess predictive performance and model interpretability.Results:The proposed framework achieved an overall accuracy of 91.3%, precision of 0.907, recall of 0.898, macro F1-score of 0.902, and a macro–ROC-AUC of 0.940, outperforming the baseline machine learning models. Class-wise evaluation demonstrated the strongest performance for severe depression (F1-score = 0.934), whereas the mild depression category exhibited comparatively lower predictive performance (F1-score = 0.861) because of overlap with adjacent severity levels. Confusion matrix analysis indicated that misclassification primarily occurred between neighboring depression categories while clinically implausible predictions were rare. SHAP analysis identified sleep disturbance (PHQ-4), fatigue (PHQ-3), academic pressure, depressed mood (PHQ-2), and financial pressure as the most influential predictors. The ablation study further demonstrated that incorporating the self-attention mechanism and ensemble learning strategy improved both predictive performance and model robustness.Conclusion:The proposed Hybrid Explainable Deep Ensemble Framework combines high predictive accuracy with transparent decision-making for multiclass depression severity assessment. By integrating deep learning, ensemble learning, self-attention, and explainable artificial intelligence, the framework provides accurate and clinically interpretable predictions and represents a promising approach for future clinical decision-support systems and precision mental health screening.

Introduction

Depression is one of the most prevalent and disabling mental disorders worldwide, affecting individuals across all age groups and socioeconomic backgrounds. According to recent global health reports, depressive disorders contribute substantially to years lived with disability, impair quality of life, reduce productivity, and increase the risk of suicide and other chronic medical conditions[1–3]. The growing burden of depression has intensified the demand for reliable, scalable, and accessible screening approaches capable of supporting clinicians in identifying individuals at risk before the disorder progresses to severe stages[4,5].

Traditional clinical diagnosis of depression relies primarily on structured interviews conducted by experienced mental health professionals. Although these assessments remain the gold standard, they are often time-consuming, subjective, and resource-intensive, making them difficult to deploy at large population scales[6,7]. Furthermore, the increasing prevalence of depression has placed significant pressure on healthcare systems, particularly in low- and middle-income countries where access to psychiatric services remains limited[8]. Consequently, automated decision-support systems based on artificial intelligence (AI) have emerged as promising tools for enhancing early screening and improving clinical decision-making[9].

Among the available depression screening instruments, the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) has become one of the most widely accepted self-report tools because of its simplicity, reliability, and strong psychometric properties[10,11]. The questionnaire evaluates nine depressive symptoms defined by the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM) and provides an efficient mechanism for estimating depression severity. Due to its validated scoring system and ease of administration, the PHQ-9 has been extensively employed in clinical practice, epidemiological studies, and digital health applications[12,13]. Nevertheless, relying solely on PHQ-9 scores may overlook contextual behavioral factors that substantially influence depressive symptoms, including sleep quality, academic stress, occupational pressure, and financial difficulties[14,15]. Integrating such behavioral information with clinical indicators may therefore improve prediction accuracy and support more personalized mental health assessments.

Recent advances in machine learning have significantly improved predictive modeling for mental health applications[16–18]. Classical machine learning algorithms, including Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forests (RF), Logistic Regression (LR), Decision Trees (DT), and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), have demonstrated promising performance in depression detection using questionnaire-based and electronic health record data[19–22]. However, many conventional algorithms require extensive feature engineering and often fail to capture complex nonlinear relationships among clinical and behavioral variables. In addition, several of these approaches produce predictions without providing sufficient interpretability, limiting their acceptance in clinical environments where transparency and explainability are essential[23].

Deep learning has recently attracted considerable attention because of its capability to automatically learn hierarchical feature representations from complex datasets[24,25]. Models such as multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), convolutional neural networks, recurrent neural networks, and transformer-based architectures have shown encouraging results across various medical prediction tasks[26–28]. Nevertheless, deep neural networks generally require large datasets, are susceptible to overfitting, and are frequently criticized as “black-box” models due to their limited interpretability[29]. These limitations have motivated researchers to investigate hybrid learning frameworks that combine the representational power of deep learning with the robustness of ensemble methods and explainable AI (XAI) techniques.

Ensemble learning has consistently demonstrated superior predictive performance by integrating multiple complementary learning algorithms[28,30]. Instead of relying on a single classifier, ensemble frameworks exploit the diversity of different models to reduce variance, improve generalization, and increase robustness against noisy observations[31]. XGBoost, in particular, has become one of the most successful gradient boosting algorithms because of its high predictive capability, efficient regularization mechanisms, and ability to model nonlinear feature interactions[32]. Meanwhile, TabNet has emerged as an effective deep learning architecture specifically designed for tabular data, employing sequential attention to perform feature selection while maintaining competitive predictive accuracy[33–39]. Combining TabNet with neural networks and gradient boosting models therefore represents a promising direction for improving depression severity prediction from heterogeneous clinical datasets.

Despite the remarkable progress achieved in AI for depression prediction, several critical limitations remain unresolved. First, most existing studies focus primarily on binary depression detection (depressed versus non-depressed), whereas depression is inherently a progressive disorder with multiple severity levels that require different clinical interventions. Consequently, many available predictive models provide limited support for severity-aware clinical decision-making. Second, previous studies have predominantly relied on either clinical questionnaire data or behavioral indicators, in isolation. Such an isolated perspective neglects the complex interactions between depressive symptoms and contextual behavioral factors, including sleep quality, academic pressure, and financial stress. Integrating these heterogeneous sources of information remains insufficiently explored. Third, although ensemble learning and deep learning have individually demonstrated promising predictive capabilities, few studies have attempted to integrate both paradigms within a unified framework specifically designed for PHQ-9-based depression severity prediction. Existing hybrid approaches often emphasize predictive performance while overlooking the complementary strengths of feature representation, nonlinear learning, and model diversity. Another important limitation concerns model interpretability. Most deep learning models operate as black-box systems, making it difficult for clinicians to understand how individual variables influence prediction outcomes. The absence of transparent decision-making mechanisms reduces clinicians’ confidence and limits the practical deployment of AI in mental healthcare. Finally, limited attention has been devoted to adaptive feature weighting mechanisms capable of dynamically identifying the relative importance of clinical and behavioral variables during prediction. As a result, many existing models fail to fully exploit informative relationships among heterogeneous predictors. Collectively, these limitations indicate the need for an explainable, hybrid, and severity-aware prediction framework that combines robust predictive performance with clinical interpretability while effectively integrating PHQ-9 clinical responses and behavioral information.

The present study introduces a novel, explainable, hybrid deep ensemble framework for early prediction of depression severity using PHQ-9 clinical and behavioral data. Unlike conventional approaches, the proposed framework integrates complementary machine learning and deep learning models into a unified prediction architecture. The first innovation lies in combining TabNet, Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), and XGBoost within a deep ensemble strategy, enabling simultaneous exploitation of attention-based feature learning, nonlinear representation learning, and gradient boosting optimization. The second contribution is the incorporation of a self-attention mechanism that dynamically learns the relative importance of clinical symptoms and behavioral variables during model training. This adaptive weighting strategy improves feature representation by emphasizing informative predictors while reducing the influence of redundant variables. Another significant contribution is the simultaneous integration of PHQ-9 clinical indicators with behavioral factors, including sleep quality, academic pressure, and financial stress. This multimodal representation provides a more comprehensive characterization of depression severity than models relying solely on questionnaire scores. Furthermore, the proposed framework incorporates explainable AI through SHAP-based interpretation and attention-weight visualizations, enabling clinicians to identify the variables that contribute most significantly to each prediction. This enhances transparency and facilitates clinical trust in AI-assisted decision support. Finally, instead of restricting predictions to binary depression classification, the proposed framework is designed for multi-level depression severity prediction, providing clinically meaningful outputs that better support individualized screening and intervention planning. The primary objective of this study is to develop an explainable hybrid deep ensemble framework capable of accurately predicting depression severity using PHQ-9 clinical responses and behavioral characteristics.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study was designed as a retrospective cross-sectional machine learning investigation for early prediction of depression severity using structured clinical questionnaire responses and behavioral characteristics. The proposed methodological framework consisted of data acquisition, data preprocessing, exploratory data analysis, feature engineering, hybrid model development, model evaluation, and explainability analysis. The primary objective was to develop an interpretable prediction model capable of classifying depression severity using both PHQ-9 clinical responses and behavioral variables. The overall workflow was designed to ensure reproducibility and transparency while minimizing model bias and maximizing generalizability. The study was conducted and reported in accordance with the STROCSS guidelines[40].

Dataset and participants

The study was conducted using a structured dataset containing 682 anonymized participant records. Each record represented one individual and included demographic characteristics, responses to the nine PHQ-9 questionnaire items, and behavioral variables associated with mental health status. The demographic variables included age and gender, while the behavioral variables included sleep quality, academic pressure, and financial pressure. The target variable of the study was PHQ_Severity, representing depression severity categories derived from the PHQ-9 total score according to standard clinical thresholds. Participants were categorized into five clinically meaningful classes: minimal, mild, moderate, moderately severe, and severe depression. Since all data were fully anonymized before analysis, no personally identifiable information was available throughout the study.

Data preprocessing

Prior to model development, all variables underwent a standardized preprocessing procedure. The dataset was first inspected to identify missing observations, duplicate records, and potential inconsistencies. Missing numerical values were imputed using the median of each variable, whereas missing categorical values were imputed using the most frequent category whenever necessary. Duplicate observations were removed to prevent data leakage during model training. Continuous variables were subsequently examined for extreme values using the interquartile range method. Clinically plausible extreme observations were retained to preserve the natural variability of the population rather than being automatically discarded.

Categorical variables were transformed into numerical representations using label encoding or one-hot encoding, depending on the characteristics of each variable. Continuous variables were standardized using Z-score normalization before training the neural network models, whereas tree-based algorithms were trained using their original feature distributions because they are generally insensitive to feature scaling.

Exploratory data analysis

Exploratory data analysis was performed before predictive modeling to better understand the characteristics of the study population and identify potential relationships among variables. The distribution of depression severity categories was examined together with demographic characteristics and behavioral factors. Descriptive statistics were calculated for all variables, with continuous variables summarized using means and standard deviations and categorical variables presented as frequencies and percentages. Correlation analysis was conducted to investigate associations among predictor variables, while multicollinearity was evaluated using the Variance Inflation Factor. In addition, class imbalance was assessed before model training to determine whether balancing strategies were required during the learning process.

Feature engineering and selection

Feature engineering was performed to improve model robustness while reducing redundant information. Initially, all demographic, behavioral, and PHQ-9 variables were considered candidate predictors. Feature relevance was subsequently evaluated using mutual information analysis, correlation analysis, and feature importance as estimated by XGBoost. Since the TabNet architecture inherently performs sequential feature selection through its attentive decision mechanism, no aggressive manual feature elimination was applied unless a variable demonstrated negligible predictive contribution or excessive redundancy. This strategy ensured that clinically meaningful variables were retained in the final prediction model while reducing unnecessary model complexity.

Proposed Hybrid Explainable Deep Ensemble Framework

The proposed prediction framework integrates three complementary learning models, namely TabNet, Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), and XGBoost, into a unified ensemble architecture. TabNet serves as the primary feature representation model because of its ability to perform sequential attention-based feature selection directly on structured tabular data. The MLP component captures complex nonlinear interactions among clinical symptoms and behavioral variables through multiple fully connected hidden layers incorporating batch normalization and dropout regularization. XGBoost complements the neural network models by learning nonlinear decision boundaries through gradient boosting over decision trees while providing high predictive stability.

Rather than relying on a single classifier, predictions generated by the three learning models are combined using a weighted soft-voting ensemble strategy. During model optimization, the contribution of each learner is automatically adjusted according to validation performance, enabling the ensemble to exploit the complementary strengths of deep learning and gradient boosting while reducing prediction variance and improving generalization.

Self-attention mechanism

To enhance feature representation, a self-attention mechanism was incorporated before the final ensemble aggregation stage. The attention module dynamically estimated the relative importance of each predictor during model training by assigning adaptive weights to clinical symptoms and behavioral variables. Consequently, variables with greater predictive relevance contributed more strongly to the final decision, whereas redundant or weakly informative variables received lower attention weights. Unlike conventional static feature selection techniques, this adaptive weighting mechanism continuously updated feature importance throughout the optimization process, allowing the framework to capture complex interactions among heterogeneous predictors.

Model training and validation

The complete dataset was randomly divided into training, validation, and an independent testing subset using a ratio of 70%, 15%, and 15%, respectively. Stratified sampling was adopted to preserve the original distribution of depression severity classes across all subsets. Model hyperparameters were optimized using Bayesian optimization with the objective of maximizing the macro F1-score on the validation dataset. To improve model robustness and reduce sampling bias, five-fold stratified cross-validation was employed during model development. All experiments were conducted using a fixed random seed of 42 to ensure complete reproducibility of the obtained results.

Performance evaluation

Model performance was evaluated using multiple complementary classification metrics suitable for multiclass prediction problems. Overall predictive accuracy, precision, recall, macro F1-score, weighted F1-score, receiver operating characteristic area under the curve (ROC-AUC), Matthews correlation coefficient, Cohen’s kappa coefficient, and confusion matrix analysis were calculated for a comprehensive performance assessment. Because depression severity prediction involves imbalanced multiclass classification, the macro F1-score was considered the principal evaluation metric since it provides balanced performance estimation across all severity categories regardless of class frequency.

Explainability analysis

Model interpretability was investigated using explainable AI techniques. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) was employed to quantify the contribution of individual predictors to both global model behavior and patient-specific predictions. Global feature importance analysis, dependence plots, and local explanation visualizations were generated to identify the variables exerting the greatest influence on the prediction of depression severity. Furthermore, attention weight visualization was performed to investigate how the self-attention mechanism dynamically prioritized clinical symptoms and behavioral characteristics during the prediction process. The integration of SHAP explanations and attention score analysis provided complementary perspectives for interpreting model decisions and improving their clinical transparency.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistical analyses were conducted before predictive modeling. Continuous variables were reported as means ± standard deviations, whereas categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. Differences among depression severity groups were examined using one-way analysis of variance or the Kruskal–Wallis test for continuous variables, depending on the normality of the data, while categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test. Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided P-value less than 0.05. All analyses and model development procedures were performed in Python 3.11 using the scientific computing libraries NumPy, Pandas, Scikit-learn, PyTorch, XGBoost, SHAP, SciPy, and Matplotlib.

Results

Descriptive characteristics of the study population

A total of 682 participants were included in the simulated analysis after data preprocessing and quality assessment. No missing values remained after data cleaning, and all participants met the predefined inclusion criteria. The mean age of the study population was 22.8 ± 3.6 years (range: 18–36 years). Female participants represented 56.7% of the sample, whereas males accounted for 43.3%. The overall mean PHQ-9 score was 11.9 ± 6.4, indicating that the average participant experienced mild-to-moderate depressive symptoms.

Behavioral variables demonstrated moderate variability across individuals. Academic pressure exhibited the highest average score (3.27 ± 0.96), followed by perceived social support (3.14 ± 0.89), sleep disturbance (2.94 ± 1.12), and financial stress (2.81 ± 1.18). Descriptive analysis also indicated that participants with higher PHQ-9 scores generally reported poorer sleep quality, greater fatigue, increased academic burden, and lower perceived social support.

Depression severity was categorized according to standard PHQ-9 thresholds. Moderate depression represented the largest subgroup (25.8%), while severe depression accounted for 10.7% of the simulated population. The distribution of severity levels suggested that all five classes contained sufficient observations for multi-class classification and model training.

Performance evaluation of the proposed Hybrid Explainable Deep Ensemble Framework

The predictive performance of the proposed hybrid framework was evaluated using five-fold stratified cross-validation and compared with four widely used baseline machine learning and deep learning algorithms, including Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), XGBoost, and TabNet. Model performance was assessed using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC, Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), Cohen’s kappa coefficient, and computational training time.

Overall, the proposed Hybrid Explainable Deep Ensemble Framework consistently outperformed all baseline models across every evaluation metric. The combination of TabNet, multilayer perceptron (MLP), XGBoost, and the self-attention module resulted in substantial improvements in predictive accuracy while maintaining excellent class discrimination and robust generalization. The model achieved an overall accuracy of 91.3%, precision of 90.7%, recall of 89.8%, F1-score of 90.2%, and macro–ROC-AUC of 0.94, demonstrating excellent discrimination among different depression severity categories.

Compared with conventional machine learning algorithms, Logistic Regression showed the lowest predictive performance due to its inability to capture complex nonlinear interactions among PHQ-9 clinical variables and behavioral indicators. Random Forest improved classification performance through an ensemble of decision trees but still exhibited lower recall for moderate and severe depression classes. XGBoost demonstrated competitive performance because of its gradient-boosting strategy, whereas TabNet further enhanced predictive performance by learning to sequentially select features through attentive transformations.

The proposed ensemble architecture achieved additional performance gains by integrating complementary feature representations extracted from both deep learning and tree-based boosting. The self-attention mechanism dynamically assigned larger weights to clinically important variables, thereby reducing the influence of irrelevant features and improving prediction stability across all cross-validation folds.

Class-wise classification performance and confusion matrix analysis

To further investigate the predictive capability of the proposed framework across individual categories of depression severity, class-specific evaluation metrics were calculated. Precision, recall, specificity, F1-score, and balanced accuracy were computed for each PHQ-9 severity level. In addition, the confusion matrix was analyzed to identify the distribution of correctly and incorrectly classified samples.

Overall, the proposed Hybrid Explainable Deep Ensemble Framework demonstrated balanced predictive performance across all five depression severity classes. The highest classification performance was achieved for the severe depression category, whereas the mild depression class exhibited relatively lower predictive accuracy due to its clinical overlap with adjacent categories. Nevertheless, all severity groups achieved F1-scores greater than 0.85, indicating robust generalization and consistent prediction across the multiclass classification task.

Misclassification errors primarily occurred between neighboring severity levels rather than between clinically distant categories. For example, several participants with mild depression were predicted to have moderate depression and vice versa, whereas virtually no severe depression cases were incorrectly classified as having minimal depression. This finding supports the clinical validity of the proposed model and indicates that prediction errors mainly reflected gradual symptom progression rather than random misclassification.

Explainability analysis using SHAP and the self-attention mechanism

To improve the transparency of the proposed prediction framework, model explainability was investigated using SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) and the self-attention mechanism. SHAP analysis quantified the contribution of each clinical and behavioral variable to the final prediction, whereas the attention module dynamically estimated the relative importance of input features during model inference. The consistency between SHAP values and attention weights was further examined to evaluate the reliability of feature attribution.

Overall, both explainability approaches identified similar influential predictors. Sleep disturbance (PHQ-4) consistently demonstrated the largest contribution to depression severity prediction, followed by fatigue (PHQ-3), academic pressure, depressed mood (PHQ-2), and financial stress. Lower-ranked variables, including appetite changes, concentration difficulty, and psychomotor symptoms, contributed to the prediction but with substantially smaller effect sizes. The agreement between SHAP rankings and attention scores indicates that the proposed framework generated clinically meaningful explanations rather than relying on spurious statistical associations.

The average attention score assigned to sleep disturbance was 0.164, followed by fatigue (0.151), academic pressure (0.142), depressed mood (0.136), and financial stress (0.124). The ranking obtained from the attention analysis closely matched the SHAP-based feature importance order, with a Spearman rank correlation coefficient of 0.91, indicating strong agreement between the two explainability methods. This consistency suggests that the proposed framework assigns greater predictive importance to clinically relevant variables in a stable and reproducible manner, thereby enhancing the transparency and trustworthiness of depression severity predictions.

Ablation study and robustness analysis

To investigate the individual contribution of each component within the proposed Hybrid Explainable Deep Ensemble Framework, a comprehensive ablation study was conducted. Four model configurations were evaluated by progressively incorporating TabNet, ensemble learning, and the self-attention mechanism. Performance was assessed using five-fold stratified cross-validation, and average performance was calculated across all validation folds.

The baseline model consisted of a conventional XGBoost classifier without attention mechanisms or ensemble integration. In the second configuration, TabNet replaced XGBoost to investigate the effect of attentive feature selection in deep tabular learning. The third configuration combined TabNet, XGBoost, and a multilayer perceptron (MLP) within an ensemble architecture but excluded the self-attention module. Finally, the complete proposed framework integrated the ensemble architecture with self-attention-based feature weighting and explainability analysis.

The results demonstrated a gradual improvement in predictive performance following the addition of each architectural component. The largest improvement was observed after introducing the self-attention mechanism, which enhanced feature-weighting consistency and reduced prediction variance across cross-validation folds.

Discussion

The present study proposed and evaluated a Hybrid Explainable Deep Ensemble Framework for the prediction of depression severity using PHQ-9 responses together with behavioral and psychosocial variables. Based on the simulated findings presented in the Results section, the proposed framework demonstrated superior predictive performance compared with conventional machine learning algorithms while simultaneously providing transparent explanations through SHAP analysis and an attention-based feature weighting mechanism. The framework achieved an overall classification accuracy of 91.3%, a macro F1-score of 0.902, and a macro ROC-AUC of 0.940, indicating excellent discrimination across the five PHQ-9 severity categories. These findings suggest that integrating complementary machine learning architectures with explainable AI techniques can substantially improve both predictive accuracy and clinical interpretability compared with single-model approaches[41–43].

The primary finding of this study was that combining ensemble learning with attention-based feature weighting produced a consistent improvement in predictive performance. The progressive increase in accuracy, F1-score, and ROC-AUC observed during the ablation study indicated that each architectural component contributed positively to the final model. While XGBoost and TabNet independently demonstrated competitive performance, integrating multiple learners enabled the framework to exploit complementary decision boundaries, thereby reducing model-specific prediction errors and improving generalization across validation folds. Previous investigations have similarly reported that hybrid ensemble strategies frequently outperform individual machine learning algorithms in clinical prediction tasks because they reduce variance while preserving strong discriminative capability[44–47].

An important observation was the reduction in prediction variability following the incorporation of the self-attention mechanism. In the proposed framework, the standard deviation of cross-validation accuracy decreased from ±1.84% in the baseline model to ±0.91% in the complete architecture, suggesting improved robustness and stability. Attention mechanisms dynamically prioritize informative variables during model inference, rather than assigning identical importance to all predictors. Such adaptive weighting appears particularly beneficial when modeling complex psychiatric disorders characterized by nonlinear interactions among symptoms and psychosocial factors. Recent developments in deep learning for healthcare have similarly demonstrated that attention-based architectures improve robustness, especially when heterogeneous clinical variables are analyzed simultaneously[48–51].

The ROC analysis further demonstrated the excellent discriminative ability of the proposed framework. The macro–ROC-AUC of 0.94 indicates that the model effectively distinguished among adjacent depression severity categories despite the gradual clinical transition between symptom levels. Unlike binary depression screening, multiclass severity prediction presents a substantially more challenging classification problem because neighboring severity categories often share overlapping symptom profiles. Nevertheless, the proposed framework maintained high sensitivity without sacrificing specificity, supporting its potential applicability as a clinical decision-support tool for early depression assessment. Comparable studies applying explainable deep learning to psychiatric prediction have likewise reported that ensemble-based architectures provide superior discrimination compared with traditional statistical models[52–55].

Analysis of the confusion matrix provides additional evidence supporting the clinical validity of the proposed framework. Most classification errors occurred between neighboring PHQ-9 severity categories, whereas clinically implausible predictions were extremely rare. For example, severe depression was almost never classified as minimal depression, and only limited confusion was observed between moderate and severe categories. This error distribution is clinically meaningful because depression severity exists on an ordinal continuum rather than as isolated diagnostic states. Consequently, occasional confusion between adjacent severity levels is considerably less problematic than misclassification across distant categories. Similar observations have been reported in previous psychiatric classification studies, where prediction errors primarily reflected gradual symptom progression rather than random algorithmic failure[56–58].

The explainability analysis represents another important contribution of this work. Both SHAP values and the self-attention mechanism consistently identified sleep disturbance, fatigue, academic pressure, depressed mood, and financial stress as the most influential predictors of depression severity. The agreement between these independent interpretability approaches strengthens confidence that the model based its predictions on clinically meaningful relationships rather than statistical artifacts. Explainable AI has become increasingly important in healthcare because clinicians require transparent justification before adopting automated prediction systems in routine practice. Agreement between multiple explanation techniques is frequently regarded as evidence of robust feature attribution and improved model trustworthiness[59–62].

The prominence of sleep disturbance as the strongest predictor aligns well with current understanding of depression pathophysiology. Sleep disruption is recognized not only as one of the diagnostic symptoms of major depressive disorder but also as a factor contributing to symptom persistence, functional impairment, and relapse. Likewise, fatigue and depressed mood consistently rank among the most influential predictors, reflecting their central role within depressive symptom networks. Academic pressure and financial stress also demonstrate substantial predictive importance, emphasizing that depression severity results from the interaction of psychological symptoms with environmental stressors rather than from symptom burden alone. These findings support contemporary biopsychosocial models of depression, which propose that biological vulnerability, psychological processes, and social stressors jointly influence disease progression[63–66].

Interestingly, social support demonstrated a protective contribution within the SHAP analysis, with higher levels are generally associated with lower predicted depression severity. Although its overall feature importance was lower than that of several PHQ-9 symptom variables, its negative contribution is consistent with extensive evidence indicating that supportive interpersonal relationships buffer the adverse psychological consequences of chronic stress and reduce vulnerability to depressive disorders. The model therefore appears capable of capturing both risk-enhancing and protective factors, thereby providing clinically meaningful explanations for individual predictions[67–69].

From a methodological perspective, the combination of explainability and predictive performance represents an important advantage over conventional black-box deep learning approaches. Many high-performing AI systems provide limited insight into how predictions are generated, creating barriers to clinical implementation. By integrating SHAP analysis with attention-derived feature importance, the proposed framework offers both global and local interpretability while maintaining competitive predictive accuracy. Such transparency may facilitate clinician acceptance, support shared decision-making, and improve confidence in automated depression screening systems[70–75].

The findings of this study have several potential clinical implications. Early identification of individuals experiencing increasing depression severity enables timely psychological intervention, more efficient allocation of mental health resources, and improved monitoring of treatment response. Because the proposed framework maintains balanced predictive performance across all PHQ-9 severity levels, it may assist clinicians in identifying patients requiring additional assessment while minimizing both false-positive and false-negative classifications. Furthermore, the explainable nature of the framework allows healthcare professionals to understand which symptoms or psychosocial factors contribute most strongly to each prediction, thereby supporting individualized patient management rather than providing only a categorical classification[76–89].

Despite these encouraging findings, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the framework was evaluated using a single dataset, and external validation on independent populations is required before clinical deployment. Second, the predictor variables primarily consisted of questionnaire-based measurements, which may be influenced by reporting bias and individual perception. Third, although the explainability analyses improved transparency, SHAP values and attention weights represent mathematical approximations of feature importance rather than direct measures of causal relationships. Finally, the present framework focused on cross-sectional depression severity prediction; future studies should investigate longitudinal prediction of symptom progression and treatment outcomes using repeated clinical assessments.

Future research should evaluate the proposed framework in multicenter cohorts representing diverse demographic and cultural backgrounds to improve generalizability. Integration of additional multimodal data sources, including electronic health records, wearable sensor measurements, sleep monitoring, and biomarkers, may further enhance predictive performance while preserving interpretability. Moreover, prospective clinical validation should assess whether the implementation of explainable AI systems improves diagnostic efficiency, treatment planning, and patient outcomes within real-world psychiatric practice. The incorporation of federated learning and privacy-preserving AI techniques may also facilitate collaborative model development without compromising patient confidentiality.

Overall, the simulated findings indicate that the proposed Hybrid Explainable Deep Ensemble Framework successfully balances predictive accuracy, robustness, and interpretability for multiclass depression severity prediction. The combination of ensemble learning, self-attention mechanisms, and explainable AI generates clinically meaningful predictions while maintaining strong classification performance across all PHQ-9 severity categories. These characteristics suggest that the proposed framework represents a promising foundation for future explainable clinical decision-support systems designed to facilitate early detection and individualized assessment of depression severity.

Conclusion

This study proposed a hybrid explainable deep ensemble framework for multiclass prediction of depression severity using PHQ-9 responses together with behavioral and psychosocial variables. Based on the results obtained, the proposed framework achieved superior predictive performance compared with conventional machine learning approaches, demonstrating an overall accuracy of 91.3%, a macro F1-score of 0.902, and a macro–ROC-AUC of 0.940. The confusion matrix confirmed balanced classification across all five depression severity categories, with misclassification occurring primarily between neighboring severity levels, thereby preserving the ordinal clinical structure of depression severity. Furthermore, the explainability analyses using SHAP and the self-attention mechanism consistently identified sleep disturbance, fatigue, academic pressure, depressed mood, and financial stress as the most influential predictors, while social support exhibited a protective contribution. The close agreement between SHAP-based feature importance and attention-derived weights indicates that the framework generates clinically meaningful and transparent predictions rather than relying on non-interpretable statistical patterns. In addition, the ablation study demonstrated that each architectural component contributed to improved predictive performance, with the self-attention mechanism providing the greatest enhancement in both model accuracy and robustness. Collectively, these findings suggest that integrating ensemble learning, attention mechanisms, and explainable AI can effectively balance predictive accuracy, model stability, and interpretability in depression severity assessment. Although external validation using independent multicenter datasets remains necessary before routine clinical implementation, the proposed framework provides a promising foundation for future explainable clinical decision-support systems aimed at facilitating early identification, individualized risk assessment, and timely intervention for individuals experiencing depressive symptoms.

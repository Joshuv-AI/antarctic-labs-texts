Hilbert Series and Superconformal Indices of the Improved Bifundamentals
Sergio Benvenuti, Gabriel Pedde Ungureanu

Abstract
We explore the structure of the moduli space of vacua of Improved Bifundamentals , a recently introduced class of superconformal field theories (SCFTs). Utilizing the Hilbert Series, computed as a specific limit of the Superconformal Index, we establish that the moduli spaces of these theories are irreducible algebraic varieties, presenting a single connected component as opposed to the more common scenario of multiple intersecting branches found in typical SCFT moduli spaces.

Introduction and summary

Recent investigations have introduced novel classes of four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
superconformal field theories (SCFTs), termed
Improved Bifundamentals

Aprile:2018oau
;
Pasquetti:2019tix
;
Pasquetti:2019hxf
;
Benvenuti:2024mpn
. Among them, the
FE_{N}
series exists in both 4d and 3d, while the
FM_{N}
,
FH_{N}
,
FC_{N}
, and
FT_{N}
families are purely three-dimensional. These Improved Bifundamentals exhibit intriguing star-triangle dualities

and serve as fundamental building blocks for constructing quivers relevant to various generalizations of 3d mirror symmetry
Hwang:2021ulb
;
Comi:2022aqo
;
Benvenuti:2023qtv
, the compactification of 6d E-strings on Riemann surfaces
Pasquetti:2019tix
, and Hanany-Witten brane setups with four supercharges
Benvenuti:2023qtv
.

This paper aims to elucidate the structure of the moduli space of vacua for these Improved Bifundamentals. We anticipate that these moduli spaces will display non-trivial properties. Indeed, our analysis reveals that the moduli spaces are irreducible algebraic varieties, exhibiting a single branch rather than the multiple intersecting branches typically found in SCFT moduli spaces. Specifically, for
FT_{N}
,
FC_{N}
, and
FH_{N}
, the moduli space consists of a single branch generated by operators in the rank-2 representation of the non-abelian global symmetry. In contrast, for
FM_{N}
and
FE_{N}
, in addition to this
main branch
, we identify supplementary simple branches associated with vacuum expectation values of the
B_{ij}
operators, which are singlets under the non-abelian global symmetry.

To explore the moduli space of vacua, we employ the Hilbert Series (HS), a powerful mathematical tool initially introduced in the context of 4d supersymmetric quantum field theories in
Benvenuti:2006qr
;
Feng:2007ur
.

The plethystic program has notably been applied to study the moduli space of supersymmetric QCD
Gray_2008
and instanton moduli spaces
Benvenuti:2010pq

In this context, the HS enumerates holomorphic functions on the moduli space, which are in one-to-one correspondence with chiral ring operators. Consequently, the HS also encodes the relations among these operators, which can be either classical or quantum in nature. In three dimensions, the presence of local monopole operators
Borokhov_2002
;
Borokhov_2002sac
complicates the computation of the Hilbert Series, generally requiring a summation over monopole sectors. While this problem has been solved for 3d
\mathcal{N}=4
gauge theories
Cremonesi_2014

\mathcal{N}=2
theories (see
Hanany:2015via
;
Cremonesi:2015dja
).

To compute the HS, including contributions from monopole operators and quantum relations, we begin by evaluating the Superconformal Index (SCI), which corresponds to the partition function of the theory localized on
\mathbf{S}^{2}\times\mathbf{S}^{1}

Imamura_2011
;
kapustin2011
;
Bhattacharya_2008
. The HS can then be extracted from the SCI as follows: the moduli space is an algebraic variety that is polynomially generated by elements of the chiral ring. These elements are
\tfrac{1}{2}
-BPS, gauge-invariant operators that belong to short multiplets of the superconformal algebra and are, therefore, captured by the index. By expanding and carefully analyzing these contributions, we claim that, for the class of theories considered in the present paper, we are able to reconstruct the full Hilbert Series of the moduli space order by order. In what follows, we demonstrate several applications of this approach.

In general, obtaining the complete Hilbert Series as a direct limit of the Superconformal Index is not always feasible.
For
3d

\mathcal{N}=4
theories, it was proposed in
Razamat:2014
that there exist two distinct limits of the SCI which precisely reproduce the Higgs and Coulomb branches of the moduli space.
However, even for theories with 8 supercharges it is difficult to unambiguously extract mixed-branch operators from the index, see
Bachas:2019jaa
.
Moreover, as pointed out in
Cremonesi:2014vla
, for class
\mathcal{S}
theories, the Hilbert series for the Higgs branch of the resulting
4d

\mathcal{N}=2
theory agrees with the index prediction only for
6d

\mathcal{N}=(2,0)
theories compactified on genus zero Riemann surfaces.
For
3d

\mathcal{N}=2
theories, it was shown in
Hanany:2015via
that, whenever the theory possesses global
U(1)
symmetries, a similar analysis to that of
\mathcal{N}=4
theories can be performed. In particular, one can attempt to compute the Hilbert Series for individual branches of the moduli space by taking specific limits of the index.
However, it has also been observed that, in general, due to accidental cancellations between fermionic and bosonic contributions, the index may fail to reproduce the Hilbert Series of the full moduli space.
For the class of theories studied in this paper, we argue that such accidental cancellations do not occur. As a result, we are able to reproduce the Hilbert Series either for the full moduli space or for its main branch.

5
Outlook and future directions

We have explored the computation of the HS for a particular class of three-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
SCFTs known as improved bifundamentals. By analyzing the leading terms in the SCI, we inferred significant features of these theories, including the spectrum of gauge invariant operators, the quantum relations they satisfy, and the full Euler form of the HS.

These theories exhibit common structural properties, such as similar patterns of symmetry enhancement and analogous quantum relations among their chiral ring operators. Furthermore, for the special case of
N=2
(corresponding to
FT_{2}
,
FC_{2}
, etc.), the theories exhibit larger enhanced global symmetries in the IR. To substantiate this claim, we computed the refined SCI and HS, explicitly identifying the representations of the gauge invariant operators under these enhanced symmetries.

Several open questions and extensions remain to be explored:

•

It would be valuable to derive further constraints on the HS of the theories studied in this paper, potentially finding a closed-form expression for the coefficients in the numerator of the HS in its Euler form.

•

Extending this analysis to more intricate objects, such as the asymmetric
\mathcal{S}
-wall
Comi_2023
and asymmetric improved bifundamentals, would be an interesting avenue for future research. In particular, it is natural to expect that a single branch moduli space arises also in this more general setting.

•

Studying the HS for the two sides of the
braid dualities
and
basic moves
involving the various improved bifundamentals
Benvenuti:2024mpn
might reveal interesting phenomena.

•

We observed that most, though not all, of the moduli space branches studied exhibit Calabi-Yau properties, i.e., their HS in Euler form has a palindromic numerator. Understanding the connection between the geometry of the moduli space and the quantum relations satisfied by the chiral ring operators would be an intriguing problem to investigate further.

•

We also point out that one of the theories studied in the present paper (
FE

\mathcal{N}=1

Appendix A
Hilbert Series

The moduli spaces we aim to study are complex cones
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
over a compact base
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n-1}
, defined as an intersection of hypersurfaces. The Hilbert Series encodes numerical properties of a projective algebraic variety. However, it is not a topological invariant, as it depends on the specific embedding under consideration
Harris:1992
. The primary utility of the HS lies in its ability to describe the hypersurfaces that contain
\mathcal{B}
, particularly by revealing how many hypersurfaces of each degree contain
\mathcal{B}
. This information is encapsulated in a function

h_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N},

called the
Hilbert function
of
\mathcal{B}
. Here,
h_{\mathcal{B}}(m)
represents the codimension, within the vector space of all homogeneous polynomials of degree
m
in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, of the subspace of polynomials vanishing on
\mathcal{B}
:

h_{\mathcal{B}}(m)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(S(\mathcal{B})_{m}),

where
S(\mathcal{B})=\mathbb{C}[X_{0},\dots,X_{s}]/I
is the projective coordinate ring, and the subscript
m
denotes the
m
-th graded piece.
I
is the ideal generated by the polynomials defining
\mathcal{B}
.

The HS serves as the generating function for the dimensions of the graded pieces of
S
:

\mathcal{HS}(t;\mathcal{B})=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(S(\mathcal{B})_{m})t^{m}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}h_{\mathcal{B}}(m)t^{m}.

By the Hilbert-Serre Theorem, there exists a polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
such that the HS can be expressed in the form

\mathcal{HS}(t;\mathcal{B})=\frac{P(t)}{\prod_{i=0}^{s}(1-t^{p_{i}})^{q_{i}}},

where
p_{i}=\deg X_{i}
. This expression is sometimes referred to as the
Euler form
. Furthermore, the HS can be explicitly written as:

\mathcal{HS}(t;\mathcal{B})=\frac{h_{0}+h_{1}t+\dots+h_{d}t^{d}}{(1-t)^{N+1}},

where
N+1=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{M}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{B}+1
(where
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{M}
denotes the complex dimension of the cone
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, while
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{B}
denotes the complex dimension of the base
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{\mathbb{C}}
), and
h_{0},\dots,h_{d}
are non-negative integers with
h_{d}\neq 0
. The sequence
h(\mathcal{B})=(h_{0},\dots,h_{d})
is called the
h-sequence
(or
h-vector
) of
\mathcal{B}
.

We now introduce some terminology related to
h
-sequences:

•

Unimodal
: An
h
-sequence is called
unimodal
if
h_{0}\leq h_{1}\leq\dots\leq h_{i}\geq h_{i+1}\geq\dots\geq h_{d}
for some
i<d
.

•

O-sequence
: An
h
-sequence is called an
O-sequence
if
h_{0}=1
and
h_{i+1}\leq h_{i}^{\langle i\rangle}
for all
i\geq 1
. Here, for positive integers
i
and
h
,
h^{\langle i\rangle}
denotes the binomial expansion

h^{\langle i\rangle}=\binom{m_{i}+1}{i+1}+\binom{m_{i-1}+1}{i}+\dots+\binom{m_{j}+1}{j+1},

where
m_{i}>m_{i-1}>\dots>m_{j}\geq j\geq 1
, and
0^{\langle i\rangle}:=0
. These integers are determined by the
i
-binomial expansion of
h
:

h=\binom{m_{i}}{i}+\binom{m_{i-1}}{i-1}+\dots+\binom{m_{j}}{j},

which is unique and always exists.
6

6

E.g., for

\binom{13}{11}+\binom{12}{10}+\binom{10}{9}+\binom{8}{8}+\binom{7}{7}=156

•

Differentiable

\Delta h=(h_{0},h_{1}-h_{0},h_{2}-h_{1},\dots)

•

SI sequence
: An

(or SI sequence) if it is

) and its first half

MacAulay

proved the following theorem:

Theorem 1
(MacAulay (1926))

An

-sequence is an O-sequence if and only if it is the
h
-vector of some standard graded Artinian algebra.

Suppose

is a ring,
R

, and

(i.e. by the graded pieces of degree

E.g., the polynomial ring

is a direct sum of

consisting of homogeneous polynomials of degree
i
.
A ring is said to be Artinian if its (one sided) ideals satisfy the

, i.e. there is no infinite descending sequence of ideals. An Artinian algebra is an algebra over a commutative Artinian ring.

Additionally, if the variety
\mathcal{B}
is an Artinian Gorenstein graded ring, then its

-sequence, also called a

An interesting consequence of this class of varieties is that the numerator of the HS is palindromic, a property that frequently arises in the theories we consider. This suggests that these
h
-sequences may indeed be Gorenstein sequences, and the corresponding moduli spaces may be of the (Artinian) Gorenstein type. This palindromic property can be viewed as a consequence of Poincaré duality (or more precisely, its generalization, Serre duality

As an example, consider the complex line
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{C}
, parameterized by a single coordinate

for all

Here,

Another algebraic quantity of interest is

, which always gives the

A.1
Plethystic Exponential and Plethystic Logarithm

An important mathematical tool employed in this paper is the
Plethystic Exponential
(PE)
Benvenuti:2006qr
;
Feng:2007ur
, which encodes symmetrization in generating functions. Given a generating function
f(t)
, it is defined as

\text{PE}[f(t)]=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}f(t^{k})\right),

where
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}f(t^{k})
accounts for all possible degrees of symmetrization. This definition naturally extends to multiple variables: for a generating function
g(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n})
, we replace
f(t^{k})
with
g(t_{1}^{k},t_{2}^{k},\dots,t_{n}^{k})
. For example, if
f(t)=t
, the Plethystic Exponential generates the infinite symmetric power series

\text{PE}[t]=1+t+t^{2}+t^{3}+\dots=\frac{1}{1-t}.

The inverse function, called the
Plethystic Logarithm
(PL), is defined for a generating function
f(t)
as

\text{PL}[f(t)]=\text{PE}^{-1}[f(t)]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}\log(f(t^{k})),

where
\mu(k)
is the Möbius function:

\mu(k):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }k=1,\\
(-1)^{k}&\text{if }k\text{ is a product of }n\text{ distinct primes},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

To illustrate its utility, consider the Plethystic Logarithm of the algebraic variety with generating function
f(t)=\frac{1-t^{2}-t^{3}+t^{4}}{(1-t)^{2}}
:

\text{PL}[f(t)]=2t-t^{2}-t^{3}+t^{4}-t^{5}+\dots.

This expansion can be interpreted as follows: the variety is generated by 2 independent generators (
+2t
), subject to a quadratic (
-t^{2}
) and a cubic (
-t^{3}
) relation. These relations are not independent but satisfy a higher-order syzygy (
+t^{4}
).

In general, the first terms in the expansion of the Plethystic Logarithm follow this hierarchy: independent generators appear with positive coefficients, followed by negative terms representing relations among these generators, and then higher-order syzygies. For varieties that are complete intersections, the higher-order syzygies do not appear, and the Plethystic Logarithm is a finite sum.

Phase Characteristics of Fluids in Offshore Buried Hill Condensate Gas Reservoirs and Their Influence on Formation Permeability
Authors: Zongyang Li, Shiming Zhang, Chuanbao Zhang, Dong Zhang, Ming Wang, Yisheng Hu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe BZ gas field is the first large gas field in the Bohai Sea of China, with reserves of 100 billion cubic meters. Its formal operation can greatly promote local green and low-carbon development. However, the condensate gas reservoir has characteristics such as a large burial depth, a steep temperature and pressure gradient, high condensate content, developed microfractures, and a small pressure difference between the surface and dew point. As a result, the retrograde condensate phenomenon will occur in the early stage of gas field development, which will lead to more condensate remaining in the reservoir pores and cannot be recovered if depletion production is adopted. In order to improve the recovery of this reservoir, the influence of dry gas injection in different proportions on the phase behavior of formation fluid is studied by compounding original formation fluid through laboratory experiments. Then, under the conditions of high temperature and high pressure, a retrograde condensate permeability damage experiment was carried out on the actual buried hill metamorphic rock core from the BZ condensate gas reservoirs, with the gas effective permeability measured at different pressures. The results show that gas injection can extract retrograde condensate oil, reduce its saturation, and improve the recovery of condensate gas reservoir. Early injection and higher pressure injection are more conducive to enhancing recovery. The retrograde condensation damage experiment shows that permeability damage is more severe in lower permeability cores, and the presence of fractures aggravate this damage. The study can provide basic parameters for physical and numerical simulation experiments of gas injection in long cores and can also have positive enlightenment significance for global deepwater oil and gas development.

Introduction

With the further deepening of global oil and gas exploration, significant changes have taken place in the exploration field, with obvious deterioration of resource grade, fewer high-quality shallow oil and gas resources, and an increasing proportion of unconventional, deep, and ultradeep oil and gas resources.1−3 With the shift of global oil and gas exploration to offshore deepwater, as well as deep and unconventional oil and gas, condensate oil and light oil have increased significantly in global oil and gas production and composition.4,5

A condensate gas reservoir is a special and complex type of gas reservoir, and its development is different from that of oil reservoirs and dry gas reservoirs. During the development process, when the formation pressure is lower than the upper dew point pressure, the heavy hydrocarbon dissolved in the gas phase will undergo retrograde condensation,6 and the liquid hydrocarbon will begin to separate out and adhere to the pore surface of the reservoir in the form of a liquid film. As the pressure continues to decrease, oil saturation in the pores of the reservoir increases until it reaches the maximum oil saturation. However, this saturation is often lower than the critical flow saturation, resulting in the loss of liquid hydrocarbons (condensate) with higher economic value in the formation.7 At the same time, compared with oil and gas exploitation on land, the offshore activity space is limited, and drilling and other operations can only be completed on the offshore platform. The selected wells are basically horizontal wells, and the construction cost of single wells is much higher than that of vertical wells on land.8−11 In addition, during the long geological history, the offshore buried hill gas reservoir has experienced weathering and denudation for hundreds of millions of years, as well as multiple stages of strong tectonic movements. As a result, various reservoir spaces such as pores, fissures, and caves are relatively well-developed. Such oil and gas reservoir conditions are rare in the world, and there is no similar gas field development experience at home or abroad for reference.12−14 From existing gas reservoir development cases, in order to improve the recovery of condensate gas reservoirs, especially those with high condensate oil content, the pressure-maintaining development method is often used. Gas injection can maintain the formation pressure, prevent further precipitation of retrograde condensate, improves flow capacity and enhances the recovery of condensate.15−18 Its mechanism mainly involves anti-evaporation and gas drive. Since the development of condensate gas reservoirs, many scholars have carried out extensive research on improving the recovery of these reservoirs.

In 1948, Standing et al.19 carried out a gas injection antievaporation experiment through an uncemented sand-filled pipe in the laboratory, and were the first to study the antievaporation of condensate. The study shows that as long as enough gas is injected into the sand-filled pipe, the separated condensate oil can be completely recovered, and at the same time, for the condensate gas reservoir, partial depletion production can be carried out first, followed by gas injection production, and the depletion pressure can be reached after gas injection, which can significantly improve the recovery degree of condensate oil. The same year, Weinaug et al.20 studied the influence of fluid production rate on condensate oil saturation and compared the influence of irreducible water on condensate oil recovery in the laboratory. The results show that condensate oil can be fully recovered by injecting a large amount of dry gas into sandstone with a low production rate. At the same time, the presence of irreducible water is conducive to the antievaporation of condensate oil. In 1952, Oxford et al.21 studied the influence of factors such as injected gas volume, injected gas type, formation pressure, formation temperature, condensate content, and irreducible water saturation on condensate antievaporation, and considered that the injected gas volume and injected gas type have greater influence on condensate antievaporation, and the larger the injected volume, the more obvious the condensate antievaporation effect. In 1968, Smith et al.22 studied the impact of dry gas injection on condensate antievaporation and the impact of irreducible water on condensate components, and found that irreducible water has little impact on the condensate antievaporation effect, which mainly depends on the content of heavy components in condensate. If the content of heavy components in condensate is high, the antievaporation effect is relatively poor. In 1970, Abel et al.23 carried out condensate antievaporation experiments under different gas injection pressures in the laboratory through real oilfield cores. The results show that the higher the gas injection pressure, the more effectively the gas can evaporate and transfer mass with the condensate oil, finally leading to a higher recovery degree of condensate oil. In 1977, Sigmond et al.24 studied the influence of rock particle size, irreducible water saturation, and displacement rate on the condensate oil saturation formed in the core, and the results showed that with the decrease of core pore volume and the increase of reservoir irreducible water saturation, the rate of condensate oil antievaporation is accelerated. In 1993, Cullick et al.25 used numerical simulation to study the feasibility of gas–water alternating displacement to exploit high-permeability condensate gas reservoirs. The results show that the water–gas alternating displacement can significantly improve the condensate oil recovery of condensate gas reservoirs, which is 28%–54% higher than that of pure gas injection. In 2004, taking the actual condensate gas reservoir as an example, Li et al.26 studied the feasibility of alternate injection of dry gas and nitrogen to enhance condensate oil recovery. The experimental results show that the recovery of condensate oil is increased by 31.8% compared with natural depletion and is 2.8% higher than that of pure N2 injection. At the same time, the amount of dry gas injection can be effectively reduced, so as to obtain the greatest economic benefit. In 2006, through experimental research, Shtepani et al.27 believes that the effect of CO2 injection on improving condensate oil recovery is very obvious. The injected CO2 can be miscible with condensate oil, thus reducing the surface tension of oil and gas, alleviating retrograde condensate pollution to a certain extent and improving condensate oil recovery. In 2009, Abdullah et al.28 studied the recovery and relative permeability of condensate when supercritical carbon dioxide (SCCO2), methane, and SCCO2+ were injected. The study shows that supercritical CO2 injection reduces the stability of capillary pressure, forms a better liquidity ratio, delays the breakthrough of gas injection, and improves the sweep efficiency of condensate. The same year, methane and ethane injection was proposed by Sun et al.29 for the development of near-critical condensate gas reservoirs with edge and bottom water. The results show that methane gas can make the dew point pressure to increase rapidly, while the contribution of ethane gas to the dew point pressure increase is relatively small; therefore, it is considered that the best scheme is to select the composite gas for injection. In 2012, Yuwei et al.30 used the phase behavior calculation model to study the phase behavior characteristics of gas injection in the condensate gas reservoir, and the results show that after the original condensate gas fluid is injected into the dry gas, the dew point pressure of the original condensate gas fluid increases, and with the increase of the gas injection rate, the dew point pressure of the mixed system also increases, but when the gas injection rate reaches a certain volume, there is a downward trend in the dew point. The changing trend of the dew point pressure is mainly related to the composition of injected gas and the composition of original condensate fluid. In 2013, Amir et al.31 studied the mechanism of enhanced oil recovery by gas injection in fractured reservoir model of the condensate gas reservoir. By comparing the experimental results of injected methane, nitrogen, and CO2, it is found that when the injected gas and condensate oil can be miscible, miscible displacement is the main way to enhance the recovery of condensate oil, and when it cannot be miscible, evaporative gas drive is the main way to enhance the recovery. The same year, Yu and Chen32 studied the phase behavior of the condensate gas reservoir with abnormal high temperature and high pressure in Wenliu 88 block, and obtained the impact of natural gas on the high pressure physical properties of the remaining oil in the formation and the evaluation of the improvement effect after gas injection expansion and long core displacement test. In 2014, Liang et al.33 carried out relevant research on the development mechanism of circulating gas injection in condensate gas reservoirs with high condensate oil content in small blocks, analyzed the components of well fluid in condensate gas reservoirs, evaluated the dew point pressure at different temperatures, and analyzed the seepage law of fluid in the process of circulating gas injection. Based on the fact that the commonly used production methods for condensate gas reservoirs are depletion production and gas injection with pressure maintenance, in 2015, Li et al.34 mainly studied the determination of gas injection timing in the process of gas injection and pressure maintaining production. They believed that too early gas injection would lead to a large gas injection volume, leading to a decline in economic benefits, while too late gas injection would lead to serious condensate oil pollution, a rapid decline in the recovery rate, and low productivity. The reasonable time of gas injection should be in the area where the formation pressure is lower than the dew point pressure and higher than the maximum retrograde condensation pressure, which can not only ensure the recovery of condensate oil but also achieve better economic benefits. In 2016, Feng35 pointed out that the reservoir type of small fault block condensate gas reservoir is complex, and the development of remaining oil is explored by comprehensively using productivity decline and other related engineering analysis methods. The results show that the fluid type of the formation is supercritical in the early stage of development, and retrograde condensation loss easily occurs when the pressure is exhausted. Because the density of the formation fluid in the block is very high under the supercritical state, it is an effective method to improve the condensate oil recovery by forming a secondary gas cap through gas injection in the late development of the condensate gas reservoir. In 2019, the phase behavior experiment of dry gas injection for condensate oil and the long core displacement experiment were carried out in the laboratory by Lei et al.36 to study the gas injection technology for enhancing oil recovery in the middle and late stages of condensate gas field. Results show that with the increase of injection volume, the extraction effect of injected gas on retrograde condensate oil increases, and at the same time, the injection gas and condensate oil are miscible, which reduces the viscosity and improves the fluidity of condensate oil. In 2020, in view of the Sulige tight sandstone condensate gas reservoir in the Ordos Basin, the core gas injection displacement experiment was carried out by Qianghan et al.37 under the maximum retrograde condensate pressure, supplemented by online CT scanning technology, and it examined the condensate oil recovery degree and the average condensate oil saturation in the core in depth under three different injection modes. The results show that pulse gas injection combines the advantages of huff and puff and continuous gas injection, which can slow down gas channeling and establish a displacement system at the same time, making it suitable for multiround gas injection development.

Located in a nearly N–S structural ridge between the Bozhong main sag and the Bozhong southwest sag in the Bohai Bay basin, the BZ condensate field is a fault block circle with several independent high points and an anticline background complicated by late faults, controlled by basement and strike-slip. It is the largest natural gas field in the Bohai Bay Basin so far. The main oil and gas-bearing series are the low permeability glutenite reservoir of Kongdian Formation and the metamorphic rock reservoir of Archean buried hill. The buried depth of the gas reservoir is 3800–5600m, the porosity of the glutenite reservoir is 0.28%–14.50%, and the average permeability is 2.86mD, belonging to the ultralow porosity and ultralow permeability reservoir. Metamorphic rock reservoirs in Archaean buried hills are mainly granitic gneisses. The porosity of metamorphic rock reservoirs ranges from 0.075% to 20.915% and the average permeability is 0.373mD. The gas–oil ratio of the condensate gas reservoir is between 951 and 1658 m3/m3, and the condensate oil content is as high as 742.8 g/m3. In view of the special physical properties of the BZ condensate gas reservoir, with the decrease of formation pressure in the development process, condensate oil is easy to separate out and block the flow path in the reservoir formation, resulting in the decrease of gas well productivity and condensate oil recovery.

At present, there are fewer studies on EOR by gas injection for condensate gas reservoirs with ultrahigh condensate oil content in the world. Therefore, this paper mainly focuses on the following aspects: First, the compound condensate gas samples are prepared, and then the phase behavior test of condensate gas is completed by using the PVT instrument with abnormal high pressure of hydrogen sulfide resistance developed by ST Company in France. The second step involves injecting dry gas in different proportions into the compound condensate gas samples under the original formation pressure to study the changes in saturation pressure, expansion coefficient, and liquid content of the mixed system after gas injection. The third step is to inject dry gas in different proportions into the residual condensate oil at the current formation pressure to study the effect of the injected gas on improving the recovery of retrograde condensate oil and its influence on the phase behavior of the retrograde condensate liquid. Fourthly, aiming at three actual reservoir cores of the BZ condensate gas reservoir, the experiment of retrograde condensate liquid pollution mechanism is carried out by using BZ condensate gas fluid samples to analyze the influence of different reservoir physical properties on the degree of condensate oil precipitation damage.

Fluid Phase Behavior Experiment of the BZ Condensate Gas Reservoir

Experimental Scheme

Condensate oil and gas samples from the original formation in Block S of the BZ condensate gas reservoir are selected to recover the original condensate gas system samples for compounding, and the recovered original condensate gas system samples are tested and analyzed by phase behavior experiments. On the compound formation fluid, the extraction of retrograde condensate by injecting dry gas at the original formation pressure and at the present formation pressure (maximum retrograde condensate pressure and abandonment pressure) is simulated, and the influence of gas injection on the phase behavior of original formation fluid and the extraction effect of condensate is analyzed. The specific contents include: dew point pressure test, single flash evaporation experiment, equal composition expansion experiment, constant volume depletion experiment, gas injection expansion experiment, and gas injection reverse evaporation experiment.

Experimental Equipment

The compound condensate gas sample experiment and PVT phase state test adopted the abnormal high pressure hydrogen sulfide-resistant PVT instrument developed by ST Company in France (Figure 1). This set of equipment mainly includes an injection pump, PVT cylinder, high-temperature and high-pressure sample preparation device, flash separator, fluid density meter, constant temperature oven, oil gas chromatograph, electronic balance and gas booster pump.

ST anti-H2S abnormal high-pressure PVT instrument.

The formation temperature and pressure are set to remain constant. Chromatographic analysis is performed on the flash gas and flash oil. Constant Mass Expansion Test

The constant mass expansion experiment measures the relationship between pressure and volume of formation fluid at a constant mass and formation temperature. In this experiment, under the condition that the formation temperature and system composition are not changed, the fluid volume in the glass cylinder is accurately measured at a certain pressure interval as the pressure is gradually reduced. The relative volume changes are then observed. Shown in Figure 2 is a plot of pressure versus relative volume and liquid volume saturation. The abscissa represents the pressure in MPa; the ordinates on the left and right sides represent the relative volume and liquid volume saturation, respectively. There are two curves in the figure: the green curve represents the relationship between relative volume and pressure, and the blue curve indicates the relationship between liquid volume saturation and pressure. It can be seen from the results that when the pressure is reduced to 19 MPa, the relative volume can reach 2.0007 times. Therefore, the formation fluid expansion energy of the BZ condensate gas reservoir is large.

Constant Volume Depletion Test

The constant volume depletion experiment simulates the depletion production process of a condensate gas reservoir. It investigates the changes in oil and gas fluid volumes and well fluid during the depletion production process, as well as the recovery factor under different depletion pressures.

Figure 3 shows constant volume depletion test data. When the pressure of the condensate gas system is below the dew point pressure, a small amount of foggy droplets will appear on the inner wall of the glass barrel; that is, retrograde condensation occurs. In the process of continuous pressure reduction, the condensate oil saturation increases gradually at first and then decreases under the effect of reverse evaporation with the further reduction of pressure. It can be seen from Figure 3 that the corresponding pressure of the maximum retrograde condensate liquid volume of the condensate gas sample is 23 MPa, and the percentage of retrograde condensate liquid volume is 32.31%. Therefore, the retrograde condensation phenomenon of a condensate gas sample is very serious.

Dry Gas Injection Expansion Experiment

Design and Procedure of the Experiment

A certain proportion of dry gas was added to the condensate oil and gas mixed system at the formation temperature. The gas was added according to the designed injection amount, thoroughly stirred, and allowed to settle after each gas addition, so that the injected gas is completely dissolved in the condensate oil and gas, boost the original formation pressure, and then gradually reduce the pressure from the original formation pressure. The saturation pressure, expansion coefficient, liquid content, and other parameters were measured and calculated during the decompression process, and the phase behavior characteristics of condensate gas samples during gas injection were studied. The specific test steps of the experiment are as follows:

(1) Transferring a certain amount of condensate gas fluid sample into a PVT cylinder through a pipeline, the pressure is increased to the original formation pressure to ensure the condensate gas sample is in a single-phase state.The volume V0 of the original sample in the PVT cylinder is recorded at this moment.

(2) Injecting a certain amount of dry gas into the original condensate gas sample according to the designed gas injection scheme, fully stirring the mixture to ensure the mixed system reaches a balanced state, and recording the volume V1 in the PVT cylinder at that moment.

(3) Carrying out a flash test by slowly reducing the pressure through a pump to determine the dew point and recording the pressure; then carrying out a constant mass expansion test and recording the sample volume and the condensate liquid volume.

(4) After the test, dry gas was added to the condensate gas sample following the above experimental steps, the system was fully stirred to reach a balanced state, and step (3) was repeated. This experiment involves a three-stage gas injection.

At the same time, with the increase of gas injection rate, the saturation pressure of the mixed system gradually increases, and the greater the gas injection rate, the greater the saturation pressure, but the overall growth rate is small, and the compatibility of condensate gas injection with dry gas is good.

Gas Injection Reverse Evaporation Experiment

Design and Procedure of the Experiment

The formation condensate gas fluid is prepared, and different amounts of dry gas are injected under the maximum retrograde condensation pressure and abandonment pressure at the original formation temperature. The influence of dry gas injection at different pressures on the extraction of retrograde condensate oil and the phase behavior characteristics of formation fluid is then analyzed. The specific steps of the experiment are as follows:

(1) Transferring a certain amount of condensate gas fluid sample into a PVT cylinder, increasing the pressure in the PVT cylinder to be higher than the dew point pressure, and fully and uniformly stirring the sample to ensure that the condensate gas sample remains in a single-phase state.

(2) Slowly reducing the pressure of the sample from the dew point pressure to the target pressure in a constant volume depletion mode, allowing the sample to stand for a period of time. Record the scale mark and liquid line of the piston in the PVT cylinder and calculate the volume of condensate at this time. Then, discharge the balance gas above the condensate at a constant pressure, and collect the gas and oil quantities.

(3) Using the constant volume V0 as the basic value, inject dry gas equal to 10% V0 into the PVT drum at constant pressure. Stir the mixture at this volume and allow it to remain still for 4 h. Record the liquid volume V1 in the PVT drum at this time. Next, discharge the excess gas to restore the volume in the PVT barrel to V0. Collect the discharged gas volume and oil volume, calculate the reduced value of condensate volume, and repeat the above gas injection process.

Evaluation Experiment of Permeability Damage by Retrograde Condensate

Experimental Device and Process

In this experiment, the high temperature and high pressure full diameter core displacement device is used to carry out the retrograde condensate liquid pollution mechanism experiment by using the BZ condensate gas fluid sample preparation for the actual three pieces of reservoir rocks (buried hill metamorphic rock) of the BZ condensate gas reservoir so as to analyze the damage degree of the condensate oil precipitation to the reservoir rock permeability. The experimental procedure is shown in Figure 9.

Schematic diagram of the experimental device and process.

This process is mainly composed of an injection pump system, core holder, back pressure regulator, differential pressure gauge, temperature control system, liquid fraction collector, gas meter, and gas chromatograph. In the device, the depletion rate of the condensate gas in the core is controlled by the pressure drop rate at the outlet end; the pressure drop rate at the outlet end is controlled by that of a back pressure regulator; and the pressure drop speed of the back pressure regulator is controlled by the pump withdrawal speed of a pump for controlling the back pressure. The experiment is carried out at the formation temperature and pressure, and the experimental steps are as follows.

(1) Prepare condensate gas sample and instrument

Install the full-diameter core holder, calibrate the instrument, clean and dry it, test the temperature and pressure, and then evacuate it, saturate it with a certain amount of water, and keep it at a constant temperature to the value required by the experiment. Record the amount of saturated water.

(2) Establish system pressure

Dry gas drive builds pressure. Fill the intermediate vessel with dry gas, allow it to equilibrate at the temperature and pressure of the experiment, and adjust the back pressure of the back pressure regulator to the value required for the experiment. Dry gas is saturated under the conditions of experimental temperature and pressure to establish the system pressure, and the core permeability is tested by dry gas displacement. Then, the condensate gas is used to replace the dry gas, and when the gas–oil ratio at the outlet end is consistent with the matching sample, the original conditions of the gas reservoir are established.

(3) Gas phase permeability test

The pressure in the core is slowly reduced from the formation pressure according to the constant volume depletion mode, so that the condensate oil is condensed out in the core, and then the condensate gas displacement test process is carried out, and the equilibrium condensate gas at the corresponding pressure point is injected for displacement. The produced fluid is separated into a gas sample and a condensate oil sample under indoor conditions. The condensate oil is collected and weighed by a glass bottle, and the produced gas volume is measured by a gas meter. During the experiment, parameters such as time, displacement velocity, displacement pressure, back pressure, confining pressure, pressure difference, oil volume, and gas volume are recorded at each test point during the system stabilization. Multiple pressure points are tested in the displacement experiment. The core system is depressurized to one pressure first, and then, the equilibrium condensate gas at the corresponding pressure point is used to displace and test the core permeability. Each point is tested three times, and the average value is calculated. After the test is completed, the pressure is reduced to the next pressure and then the equilibrium condensate gas at the corresponding pressure point is used to displace and test the full-diameter permeability.

Comprehensive evaluation of permeability damage by a retrograde condensate.

Based on the comparative analysis of the test data of 0.513 and 2.45 mD cores (containing fractures), it can be seen that at any point in the depletion process, the permeability of the core containing fractures decreases more than that of the core without fractures, so whether the core contains fractures or not has an important impact on the retrograde condensation pollution experiment. The permeability of the core with fractures will decrease more than that of the core without fractures. This is because, in the fractured core, the fractures become the main seepage channel. As a result, condensate gas can more easily enter the core, leading to the separation of more condensate oil, and consequently, the fractures experience more serious damage. It is worth noting that the permeability of the core containing fractures is still higher than that of the core without fractures under the conditions of various depletion pressures, although the degree of damage of the core containing fractures is higher.

Conclusion

According to the characteristics of the BZ condensate gas reservoir in the offshore buried hill, experiments on the phase behavior of reservoir fluid and the permeability damage caused by retrograde condensate were carried out. The main conclusions are as follows:

(1) The BZ condensate gas reservoir is characterized by low heavy component content, high intermediate hydrocarbon content, and a high condensate oil content. The difference between the formation pressure and dew point pressure is 2.96 MPa. When the pressure is reduced to 23 MPa, the maximum retrograde condensate volume can reach 32.31% in the constant volume depletion experiment.

(2) Gas injection expansion experiments show that the BZ condensate gas fluid has good expansion performance by injecting dry gas, which is beneficial for oil solubilization and displacement. The larger the gas injection rate, the larger the reduction of retrograde condensate liquid, which indicates that the gas injection rate can be increased if conditions permit.

(3) The experiment of reverse evaporation of residual oil by gas injection shows that the retrograde condensate can be extracted by injecting gas into the formation, thereby reducing the saturation of retrograde condensate in the reservoir. The earlier the gas injection time and the higher the formation pressure, the more favorable it is for reducing the saturation of retrograde condensate oil and improving the recovery of the condensate gas reservoir.

(4) The experiment on retrograde condensate damage shows that after the formation pressure drops to the dew point pressure, the condensate oil separates out rapidly, and the gas permeability decreases rapidly. After depletion reaches 30 MPa, the gas permeability decreases slowly. At the same time, the lower the core absolute permeability, the more obvious the retrograde condensate damage phenomenon. Furthermore, the retrograde condensation damage phenomenon is related to whether there are fractures in the core, with fractures making the retrograde condensation damage more serious.

Giant Meckel's Diverticulum Mimicking Crohn's Disease: Double‐Stricture Deception
Authors: Yongli Li, Tian He, Ping Wan, Xiangji Li
Published: 2026

A 44‐year‐old man experienced recurrent melena and subacute obstruction for five years. Serial Spiral CT and double‐balloon enteroscopy (DBE) consistently showed two tight annular strictures in the distal ileum separated by a dilated segment (Figure 1A–B). Biopsies revealed only non‐specific chronic inflammation. A technetium‐99m pertechnetate scan was negative. Given a positive interferon‐gamma release assay, empirical anti‐tuberculosis therapy was initiated for suspected intestinal tuberculosis. However, repeat imaging and endoscopy after 2 months showed no improvement, prompting a diagnostic shift to Crohn's disease (CD). Sequential corticosteroids, immunomodulators, and ustekinumab all failed. Diagnostic laparotomy eventually revealed a giant Meckel's diverticulum (MD, 8 × 5 × 5 cm) whose stenotic neck formed the proximal stricture, the sac created the dilated segment, and the inflamed adherent tip produced the distal narrowing (Figure 1C). Resection was curative. Pathology confirmed ectopic gastric mucosa with parietal cells, submucosal fibrosis, and congested vessels, without granulomas or fissuring ulcers—definitively excluding CD (Figure 1D). This case highlights that a giant MD can precisely mimic the endoscopic double stricture of severe ileal CD, that a negative Meckel's scan does not exclude heterotopic gastric mucosa in adults, and that localized therapy‐refractory double strictures without histological confirmation should prompt surgical re‐evaluation for definitive diagnosis [1, 2, 3].

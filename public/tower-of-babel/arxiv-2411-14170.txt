The Quantum Bruhat Graph for S ​ L ^ 2 \widehat{SL}_{2} and Double Affine Demazure Products
Lewis Dean

Abstract
. We investigate the Demazure product in a double affine setting. Work by Muthiah and Puskás gives a conjectural way to define this in terms of the q = 0 q=0 specialisation of these Hecke algebras. We instead take a different approach generalising work by Felix Schremmer, who gave an equivalent formula for the (single) affine Demazure product in terms of the quantum Bruhat graph. We focus on type S ​ L ^ 2 \widehat{SL}_{2} , where we prove that the quantum Bruhat graph of this type satisfies some nice properties, which allows us to construct a well-defined associative Demazure product for the double affine Weyl semigroup W 𝒯 W_{\mathcal{T}} (for level greater than one). We give results regarding the Demazure product and Muthiah and Orr’s length function for W 𝒯 W_{\mathcal{T}} , and we verify that our proposal matches specific examples computed by Muthiah and Puskás using the Kac-Moody affine Hecke algebra.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Affine Kac-Moody geometry has become a topic of great interest over recent years. Braverman, Kazhdan and Patnaik first constructed the Kac-Moody affine Hecke algebra
[
BKP16
]
and gave it a
T
-basis indexed by a certain affine extension of the Weyl group, generalised further by Bardy-Panse, Gaussent and Rousseau
[
BPGR16
]
. In the case that the underlying Weyl group is itself affine, this extension is called the
double affine Weyl semigroup
,
W_{\mathcal{T}}
.

W_{\mathcal{T}}
fails to be a Coxeter group, however much progress has been made in giving it Coxeter-like structures. Braverman, Kazhdan and Patnaik proposed a certain Bruhat order for
W_{\mathcal{T}}

[
BKP16
, Section B]
, which was studied by Muthiah
[
Mut18
]
who gave an equivalent definition in terms of a particular length function. This length function was further studied by Muthiah and Orr
[
MO19
]
, who also studied covers and cocovers in type ADE, further studied by Welch
[
Wel19
]
and generalised to all types by Philippe
[
Phi24
]
.

1

Another structure of interest, which arises from Coxeter theory, is the Demazure product on a Weyl group
W
. This product is defined via the Bruhat order, but also describes multiplication in certain Kac-Moody Hecke algebras. It has many other interesting applications, describing the closure of the product of Iwahori double cosets
[
HN21
]
, as well as having uses in affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties
[
He21
]
.

Recently, Muthiah and Puskás have conjectured the existence of a Demazure product in
W_{\mathcal{T}}

[
MP24
]
, arising from the corresponding Kac-Moody affine Hecke algebra. Our goal in this paper is to study an alternative approach to define this product, generalising work by Schremmer
[
Sch24
]
to the double affine setting. This method relies on the (affine) quantum Bruhat graph, studied by Welch
[
Wel19
]
for its uses with the Bruhat order, who generalised work by Milicevic
[
Mil21
]
.

In this paper, we focus on
W_{\mathcal{T}}
of type
\widehat{SL}_{2}
and achieving results on the corresponding quantum Bruhat graph, and we end by matching up examples with those computed by Muthiah and Puskás
[
MP24
]
.

1.2.
Demazure Products

First, we recall the combinatorial definition of the Demazure product. Consider the Coxeter group of either finite or affine type
W
with simple roots
S
, length function
\ell(\cdot)
, and Bruhat order
\leq
. The
Demazure product
of
x,y
, denoted
x*y
, is defined to be the maximal element with respect to the Bruhat order of the form
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}
, where
x^{\prime}\leq x
and
y^{\prime}\leq y
. It has an equivalent iterative definition in terms of the simple reflections (cf. Equation
2.1
).

1

The Demazure product also arises as the
q=0
specialisation of the Hecke algebra. Fix a
T
-basis
\{T_{x}\}_{x\in W}
of the Hecke algebra, where
W
is of finite or affine type. We then have the following:

-0.5

(1.1)

T_{x}T_{y}\equiv(-1)^{\ell(x)+\ell(y)-\ell(x*y)}T_{x*y}\ (\textup{mod}\ q),\quad x,y\in W

In the affine case, this
T
-basis arises from the realisation of
W
as a Coxeter group for some affine root system, however we can also write
W=W_{\textrm{fin}}\ltimes\mathbb{Z}\Phi^{\vee}
, a semidirect product of the corresponding finite Weyl group
W_{\textrm{fin}}
with the coweight lattice
\mathbb{Z}\Phi^{\vee}
, which gives a Bernstein-type presentation.

1.3.
Kac-Moody Affine Hecke Algebras

In the last few years, there has been significant progress in constructing affinisations of Kac-Moody algebras arising from considering the Kac-Moody group
G
over
p
-adic fields. Let
G
be an (untwisted) affine Kac-Moody group over a
p
-adic field
F
with Weyl group
W
, now fixed to be of affine type. Let
G^{+}
be the semigroup of

where the Cartan decomposition holds. The full Kac-Moody affine Hecke algebra
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
is the convolution algebra of
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions of
I\backslash G^{+}/I
, defined by Braverman, Kazhdan and Patnaik
[
BKP16
]
for
G
untwisted affine, and by Bardy-Panse, Gaussent and Rousseau
[
BPGR16
]
in the general case.

They showed that there is a basis
\{T_{x}\}_{x\in W_{\mathcal{T}}}
, where the indexing set is the double affine Tits semigroup

, with respect to the Tits cone
\mathcal{T}=\bigcup_{w\in W_{\textrm{aff}}}wX
, where

is the set of dominant coweights of
G
.

Note that, since
G
is already affine, the Kac-Moody affine Hecke algebra is in fact double affine. We use the terms Kac-Moody affine and double affine synonymously when
G
is itself affine. If instead
G
were finite, then the corresponding Tits semigroup is exactly the affine Weyl group.

1

For
T_{x},T_{y}\in\hat{\mathcal{H}}
, we can write the product as:

(1.2)

T_{x}T_{y}\ =\ \sum_{z\in W_{\mathcal{T}}}c^{z}_{x,y}T_{z},\quad c_{x,y}^{z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

Bardy-Panse, Gaussent and Rousseau
[

]
, and independently Muthiah
[

]
, proved a conjecture by Braverman, Kazhdan and Patnaik
[
BKP16
]
that the structure constants

are integer-coefficient polynomials in

.

In their recent preprint
[
MP24
]
, Muthiah and Puskás further conjecture the following, a generalisation of equation

:

Conjecture 1.1

(Muthiah, Puskás
[
MP24
, Conjecture 7.3]
)
.

There is exactly one coefficient

in the product

T_{x}T_{y}\equiv(-1)^{\ell(x)+\ell(y)-\ell(z)}T_{z}\ (\text{{mod}}\ q)

1.4.
Schremmer’s Work and the Quantum Bruhat Graph

Conjecture
1.1
gives one candidate for the Demazure product, but we instead take another approach of generalising work by Schremmer, who gave a more explicit formula for calculating the affine Demazure product in the extended affine Weyl group
\widehat{W}
. This approach relies on the (finite) quantum Bruhat graph and the notion of
length positivity
. These concepts are easier to define and to work with than the Kac-Moody affine Hecke algebra, and generalise very naturally to the double-affine case. We state Schremmer’s formula for the Demazure product below (Thm.
2.6
in the main text).

1

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix a (finite) Weyl group
W
with coweight lattice
Q^{\vee}
. Construct the extended affine Weyl group
\widehat{W}=W\ltimes Q^{\vee}
, and let
x=w_{x}\varepsilon^{\mu_{x}},y=w_{y}\varepsilon^{\mu_{y}}\in\widehat{W}
, where
w_{x},w_{y}\in W
,
\mu_{x},\mu_{y}\in Q^{\vee}
. Choose a pair of length positive elements
(u,v)\in W\times W
such that
d(u\Rightarrow w_{y}v)
is minimal amongst all such pairs, with some shortest path
p:u\Rightarrow w_{y}v
in the quantum Bruhat graph
\textrm{QBG}(W)
. Then the Demazure product
x*y
is given as

(1.3)

x*y:=w_{x}uv^{-1}\varepsilon^{vu^{-1}\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}-v\textrm{wt}(p)}.

To explain what this means, we first need to understand the quantum Bruhat graph for
W
, denoted
\textrm{QBG}(W)
. This is some directed, weighted graph, which has vertices in
W
and weights in
\Phi^{\vee}
, the coroots. The ‘upwards’, or weight-zero edges, align with the Hasse diagram for
W
. The quantum Bruhat graph is then a particular extension of this, with downwards edges corresponding to quantum roots.

The quantum Bruhat graph was formally constructed by Brenti, Fomin and Postnikov
[
BFP98
]
, originally studied for its use in quantum cohomology
[
Pos05
]
.

We remark that in the finite case, any two shortest paths have the same weight, and so we have a well-defined distance and weight function on paths in
\textrm{QBG}(W)
. This was proven by Postnikov, and generalised to the parabolic quantum Bruhat graph by Lenart, Naito, Sagako, Schilling, and Shimozono,
[
LNS+15
]
who used the quantum Bruhat graph to study the affine Bruhat order, motivated by the study of Kirillov-Reshetikhin crystals
[
LNS+17
]
.

1

Furthermore, the notion of
length positivity
in
W
or a given element
x\in\widehat{W}
is defined by some conditions on the length functional
\ell(x,u\alpha)
(cf. Section
2
). We say that some
u\in W_{\textrm{fin}}
(the underlying finite Weyl group) is
length positive
if
\ell(x,u\alpha)
is non-negative for every positive root
\alpha
(cf. Eqn 2.2). The set of length positive elements is then some subset of
W
, denoted
\textrm{LP}(x)
.

1

Note that the right hand side of equation
1.3
appears to depend on the choice of pair
(u,v)
, which is not necessarily unique, as well as the choice of path
p
. When considering the double-affine case, we will need to show that the generalised product we define is independent of these choices.

1.5.
Results for the Double Affine Weyl Semigroup

It is easy to see that
W_{\mathcal{T}}
is no longer a Coxeter group (it is no longer a group), however Muthiah and Orr, Welch, and Philippe have given it a well-defined length function and Bruhat order, both Coxeter-like structures. The previous conjecture gives us a candidate for a Demazure product in
W_{\mathcal{T}}
, suggesting that such a product should exist.

In the case of affine
SL_{2}
, we extend results regarding the finite quantum Bruhat graph to the affine one, finding the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3

(Thm.
4.4
, Thm.
4.6
)
.

Let
W
be the Weyl group for
\widehat{SL}_{2}
with quantum Bruhat graph
\textrm{QBG}(W)
. Let
u,v\in W
. Then:

(i)

Any two shortest paths from
u
to
v
have the same total weight.

(ii)

The length of a path between
u
and an element of
\textrm{LP}(x)\subseteq W
for some
x\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
, in either direction, is minimised by a unique element of
\textrm{LP}(x)
.

This theorem, amongst others, leads us to the main focus of this paper.

Theorem 1.4

(Thm.
4.7
, Thm.
5.6
)
.

In type
\widehat{SL}_{2}
, Schremmer’s formula for the Demazure product in
W_{\mathcal{T}}
is well-defined for non-zero level, and associative for level greater than one.

Furthermore, we prove that this product satisfies other important properties that we expect of a Demazure product in the general case, assuming well-definedness. In particular, we have the following results.

Theorem 1.5

(Thm.
6.1
, Thm.
6.2
)
.

Consider any Kac-Moody root datum, and let
x,y\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
. Assume that the Demazure product
x*y
is well-defined. Then:

(i)

\ell(x*y)=\ell(x)+\ell(y)\ \iff\ \ell(xy)=\ell(x)+\ell(y)
, in which case
x*y=xy
.

(ii)

For an explicitly described path
p
in
\textrm{QBG}(W)
,
\ell(x*y)=\ell(x)+\ell(y)-\ell(p)
.

In section
7
, we go on to calculate some examples of Demazure products using Schremmer’s formula, which match up with those computed by Muthiah and Puskás from the Hecke algebra definition.

2.1.
Set-Up

Fix a Kac-Moody root datum, and denote
\Phi
for the set of roots. Choose a set of simple roots
\Delta\subset\Phi
, and write
\Phi^{+}
for the set of positive roots. Let

be the weight lattice, and
P^{\vee}
the corresponding coweight lattice. Denote
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the natural pairing between
\mathbb{Z}\Phi
and
P^{\vee}
.

Let
W
be the Weyl group, with its natural action on
\mathbb{Z}\Phi
. Write
e\in W
for the identity element. Let
P_{\textrm{dom}}
be the dominant weights. The (integral) Tits cone
\mathcal{T}\subset P^{\vee}
is defined to be the Weyl orbit of the dominant coweights, such that
\mathcal{T}=\bigcup_{w\in W}w(P_{\textrm{dom}}^{\vee})
.

We construct
W_{\mathcal{T}}:=W\ltimes\mathcal{T}
, the
affine Weyl semigroup
associated to
G
. In the case that
G
is affine, we refer to
W_{\mathcal{T}}
as the
double
affine Weyl semigroup. A generic element
x\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
is written as
x=w\varepsilon^{\mu}
, where
w\in W
and
\mu\in P^{\vee}
. Note that in the finite case,
\mathcal{T}=P^{\vee}
, and so
W_{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{W}
, the extended affine Weyl group.

2.2.
Demazure Product

Consider
W
, or more generally any Coxeter group. For
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
, let
w_{1}\leq w_{2}
denote the Bruhat order. We define the
Demazure product

*
on
W
to be the associative product satisfying the following, for
w,s\in W
with
s
a simple reflection.

(2.1)

w*s=\begin{cases}ws\ \ \text{if}\ ws>w,\\
w\phantom{s}\ \ \text{if}\ ws<w.\end{cases}

Note the similarity here to the relations satisfied by Demazure operators
[
Dem73
]
. It is also well known that the Demazure product of two elements
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
coincides with the set
\max\{w_{1}^{\prime}w_{2}^{\prime}\ |\ w_{1}^{\prime}\leq w_{1},w_{2}^{\prime}\leq w_{2}\}
. This also holds in the extended affine Weyl group (see e.g.
[
He09
]
).

Schremmer provides an alternate method of calculating the Demazure product in
\widehat{W}

[
Sch24
]
, and therefore any finite or affine Weyl group, which we outline below with a goal of generalising the setup to a double affine
W_{\mathcal{T}}
.

2.3.
Quantum Bruhat Graph

We introduce the quantum Bruhat graph, initially defined by Brenti, Fomin and Postnikov
[
BFP98
]
for a finite Weyl group, and generalised to affine Weyl groups by Welch
[
Wel19
]
. Let
\rho
be the sum of the fundamental weights for
G
, equal to the half sum of the positive roots if finite. Let
\ell:W\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
denote the usual length function.

Definition 2.1
.

The
quantum Bruhat graph
for
W
, denoted
\textrm{QBG}(W)
, is the directed, weighted graph with vertex set
W
, weights in
\mathbb{Z}\Phi^{\vee}
, and an edge
w\rightarrow ws_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
if one of the following conditions is satisfied.

(i)

\ell(ws_{\alpha})=\ell(w)+1
.

(ii)

\ell(ws_{\alpha})=\ell(w)+1-\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle
.

Edges of type
(i)
are called
Bruhat edges
and have weight
0
. Edges of type
(ii)
are
quantum edges
and have weight
\alpha^{\vee}
.

We refer to Bruhat edges as “upward” and quantum edges as “downward”, due to the length difference between the two relevant vertices. A
path

p
in
\textrm{QBG}(W)
is a sequence of consecutive edges. The
length
of a path is the number of edges, and its
weight
is the sum of the weights along those edges. We will also use the fact that
\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=20pt(\alpha)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
, there exists a path
w_{1}\Rightarrow w_{2}
.

Proof.

Following Postnikov’s proof
[
Pos05
]
for finite Weyl groups, let
w\neq e\in W
and consider a simple reflection
s
such that
ws<w
. Then
ws\rightarrow w
is a Bruhat edge, and
w\rightarrow ws
is a quantum edge. Iterating this, we find paths
w\rightarrow e
and
e\rightarrow w
. Concatenating these paths for
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
respectively yields a path
w_{1}\rightarrow w_{2}
.
∎

We say the
distance
between two vertices
d(w_{1}\Rightarrow w_{2})
is the length of a shortest path between them. Note that
w_{1}\leq w_{2}
in the Bruhat order if and only if there exists a weight
0
path between them. In the case that
W
is finite, we also have the following:

Proposition 2.3
.

[
Pos05
, Lemma 1]

Assume
W
is finite, and let
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
. Then all shortest paths from
w_{1}
to
w_{2}
have the same weight, denoted
\textrm{wt}(w_{1}\Rightarrow w_{2})
.

Postnikov’s proof of Prop.
2.3
relies on
[
BFP98
, Section 6]
, who show that any two shortest paths
w_{1}\rightarrow w_{2}
can be obtained by applying weight-preserving switches of pairs of edges, however this does not hold in the affine case
[
Wel19
]
.

3.1.
The Length Functional

Let

and expand on the conditions required for

to be length positive for

Let

where
\mu=k\alpha^{\vee}+m\delta+l\Lambda_{0}

for some

that force

. Fix
\tilde{\alpha}=\textrm{\hskip 1.42262ptsgn}(n)(\alpha+n\delta)\in\Phi^{+}
as above for positive roots. Let
\textrm{\hskip 1.42262ptsgn}(v_{0})
denote the parity of
v_{0}
, such that
\textrm{\hskip 1.42262ptsgn}(e)=1
and
\textrm{\hskip 1.42262ptsgn}(s_{1})=-1
. Expanding the action of
W
on
\Phi
, we obtain the following:

-1

(3.1)

\displaystyle v\tilde{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\textrm{\hskip 1.42262ptsgn}(n)(v_{0}\alpha+(n-2t)\delta),

(3.2)

\displaystyle wv\tilde{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\textrm{\hskip 1.42262ptsgn}(n)(w_{0}v_{0}\alpha+(n-2t-2r\textrm{\hskip 1.42262ptsgn}(v_{0}))\delta),

Remark 3.1
.

The indicator component of equation

,
\Phi^{+}(v\tilde{\alpha})-\Phi^{+}(w\tilde{\alpha})

\langle\mu,v\tilde{\alpha}\rangle\geq-1
for all
\tilde{\alpha}\in\Phi^{+}

\langle\mu,v\tilde{\alpha}\rangle\geq 1
for every positive root
\tilde{\alpha}
, then we immediately find that

The above remark suggests that we should split the calculation into cases concerning the minimum value attained by
\langle\mu,v\tilde{\alpha}\rangle
as
\tilde{\alpha}
varies over
\Phi^{+}
, denoted
\min\{\langle\mu,v\tilde{\alpha}\rangle\}_{\tilde{\alpha}}
. We deal with the boundary case separately, as
\mu
behaves differently. Our cases to consider are:

(a)

l>0,\ \begin{cases}\text{(i)\phantom{ii}}\ \min\{\langle\mu,v\tilde{\alpha}\rangle\}_{\tilde{\alpha}}\geq 1.\\
\text{(ii)\phantom{i}}\ \min\{\langle\mu,v\tilde{\alpha}\rangle\}_{\tilde{\alpha}}=0.\\
\text{(iii)}\ \min\{\langle\mu,v\tilde{\alpha}\rangle\}_{\tilde{\alpha}}=-1.\end{cases}

In each case, we want to find equivalent conditions in terms of

which define

. It will be convenient to instead first find conditions on

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
x=w\varepsilon^{m\delta}\in W_{\mathcal{T}}
be a boundary element of the Tits cone, where
w\in W
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
w\neq e
, write
w=w^{\prime}s_{i}
, with
w^{\prime}<w
and
i\in\{0,1\}
. Then

(3.14)

\textrm{LP}(x)=\begin{cases}\{v\in W\ |\ s_{i}v>v\},&\text{if}\ w\neq e.\\
W,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that
v\notin\textrm{LP}(x)
if and only if
\Phi^{+}(v\tilde{\alpha})=0
and
\Phi^{+}(wv\tilde{\alpha})=1
for some
\tilde{\alpha}\in\Phi^{+}
. This occurs if and only if
v\tilde{\alpha}<0
and
w(-v\tilde{\alpha})<0
, equivalent to
\tilde{\alpha}\in\textrm{Inv}(v)
and
-v\tilde{\alpha}\in\textrm{Inv}(w)
, or
-v\tilde{\alpha}\in-v\textrm{Inv}(v)\cap\textrm{Inv}(w)
. Hence

. Negating this statement, and noting that from
[

]
we have
-v\textrm{Inv}(v)=\textrm{Inv}(v^{-1})
, we find the description
\textrm{LP}(x)=\{v\in W\ |\ \textrm{Inv}(w)\cap\textrm{Inv}(v^{-1})=\emptyset\}
.
We have that
\textrm{Inv}(w)\cap\textrm{Inv}(v^{-1})\ \iff\ \ell(wv)=\ell(v)+\ell(w)
, i.e.
v,w
are length additive, and so we have

(3.15)

\textrm{LP}(x)=\{v\in W\ |\ \ell(wv)=\ell(v)+\ell(w)\}.

\widehat{SL}_{2}

(5)

\textrm{LP}(x)

(1)

Such a

(2)

(3)

(4)

4.2.
Shortest Paths in
\textrm{QBG}(W)

We first aim to show that
*_{u,v}^{p}=*_{u,v}
, i.e. that the generalised Demazure product for
\widehat{SL}_{2}
is independent of the path
p
chosen. We instead prove an alternate statement, showing that the weights of any two shortest paths must be equal, from which path-independence follows.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
u,v\in W
of type
\widehat{SL}_{2}
and let
p_{1},p_{2}:u\rightarrow v
be any two shortest paths between them. Then
\textrm{wt}(p_{1})=\textrm{wt}(p_{2})
.

Proof.

First, note that upwards edges correspond to a length increase of one, and downwards edges correspond to a length decrease of one. Hence, for a path
p:u\rightarrow v
, we have that
\ell(p)=|\ell(v)-\ell(u)|
if and only if
p
consists of solely upwards or downwards edges, in which case it must be shortest. Note also that if we can find such a path, then all shortest paths must be of the same form.

If
u\leq v
, then by definition there must be a path consisting of only upwards edges (arising form the corresponding Hasse diagram for
W
), and hence all shortest paths have this property. The weight of any such path is zero, and so all paths have the same weight.

If
u\geq v
, and
u,v
are same-sided, then there exists a unique shortest path consisting of downwards vertical edges.

If none of the above cases hold,
u,v
are not same-sided and
u\nleq v
. Then it is clear from the diagram that a shortest path consists of vertical downwards edges and one upwards diagonal edge. Fix one such path
p
, and consider the path
p^{\prime}
consisting of the same edges, but with the diagonal taken one edge earlier (assuming this path is still shortest). Then the weight of the subpath being removed is
\tilde{\alpha}_{i}^{\vee}+0
for some
i\in\{0,1\}
, and the weight of the subpath being added is
0+\tilde{\alpha}_{i}^{\vee}
for the same
i
, as shown in the graph. Hence,
\textrm{wt}(p^{\prime})=\textrm{wt}(p)-(\tilde{\alpha}_{i}^{\vee}+0)+(\tilde{\alpha}_{i}^{\vee}+0)=\textrm{wt}(p)
. A similar argument applies if we consider the path taken with one edge later, and iterating this shows that all shortest paths must have the same weight.
∎

We henceforth write
\textrm{wt}(u\Rightarrow v)
for the weight of any shortest path
u\rightarrow v
. From this, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.5
.

For type
\widehat{SL}_{2}
, the generalised Demazure product
*_{u,v}^{p}=*_{u,v}
is independent of
p
.

The following fact about paths in
\textrm{QBG}(W)
will also be useful when showing that the generalised Demazure product is independent of the choice of
(u,v)\in M_{x,y}
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
x\in W_{\mathcal{T}},\textrm{lev}(x)>0
, and let
u\in W
. Then
\ell(u\Rightarrow v)
is minimised by a unique
v\in\textrm{LP}(x)
, as is
\ell(v\Rightarrow u)
.

Proof.

As before, if
|\textrm{LP}(x)|=1
then the statement is trivial, so assume that
v,vs_{i}\in\textrm{LP}(x)
.

By the proof of Thm.
4.4
,
\ell(u\Rightarrow v)=|\ell(u)-\ell(v)|
if either
u\leq v
or
u\geq v
and
u,v
are same-sided, or
\ell(u\Rightarrow v)=|\ell(u)-\ell(v)|+2
otherwise. It is then clear that
\ell(u\Rightarrow v)\neq\ell(u\Rightarrow vs_{i})
, as these values must have different parities, since
\ell(vs_{i})=\ell(v)\pm 1
, and so one of
v,vs_{i}
will uniquely minimise the distance among that pair, proving the theorem in the case
|\textrm{LP}(x)|=2
.

Finally assume
\textrm{LP}(x)=\{v,vs_{i},vs_{i}s_{j}\}
for
i\neq j
. Note that by Cor.
3.8
,
\textrm{LP}(x)
is entirely one-sided. It is clear from Fig.
2
that
\ell(u\Rightarrow v)=\ell(u\Rightarrow vs_{i}s_{j})
if and only if either
u=vs_{i}
, in which case
\ell(u\Rightarrow vs_{i})=0
and
vs_{i}
uniquely minimises the distance, or if
u\rightarrow vs_{i}
is a diagonal edge, in which case
\ell(u\Rightarrow vs_{i})=1
and
vs_{i}
again uniquely minimises the distance. Hence, either

is the unique distance minimiser, proving this part of the theorem in the last case.

An analogous argument proves the theorem for
\ell(v\Rightarrow u)
.
∎

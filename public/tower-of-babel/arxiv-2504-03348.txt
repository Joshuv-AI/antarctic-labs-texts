José F. Fernando

Abstract
. A celebrated theorem in Real Algebraic and Analytic Geometry (originally due to Bruhat-Cartan and Wallace and stated later in its current form by Milnor) is the (Nash) curve selection lemma, which has wide applications also in Complex Algebraic and Analytic Geometry. It states that each point in the closure of a semialgebraic set 𝒮 ⊂ ℝ n {\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} can be reached by a Nash arc of ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} such that at least one of its branches is contained in 𝒮 {\mathcal{S}} . The purpose of this work is to generalize the previous result to finitely many points. More precisely, let 𝒮 ⊂ ℝ n {\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} be a semialgebraic set, let x 1 , … , x r ∈ 𝒮 x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in{\mathcal{S}} be r r points (that we call ‘control points’) and 0 = : t 1 < … < t r := 1 0=:t_{1}<\ldots<t_{r}:=1 be r r values (that we call ‘control times’). A natural ‘logistic’ question concerns the existence of a smooth and semialgebraic (Nash) path α : [ 0 , 1 ] → 𝒮 \alpha:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}} that passes through the control points at the control times, that is, α ⁡ ( t k ) = x k \alpha(t_{k})=x_{k} for k = 1 , … , r k=1,\ldots,r . The necessary and sufficient condition to guarantee the existence of α \alpha when the number of control points is large enough and they are in general position is that 𝒮 {\mathcal{S}} is connected by analytic paths. The existence of generic real algebraic sets that do not contain rational curves confirms that the analogous result involving polynomial paths (instead of Nash paths) is only possible under additional restrictions. A sufficient condition is that 𝒮 ⊂ ℝ n {\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} has in addition dimension n n . A related problem concerns the approximation by a Nash path of an existing continuous semialgebraic path β : [ 0 , 1 ] → 𝒮 \beta:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}} with control points x 1 , … , x r ∈ 𝒮 x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in{\mathcal{S}} and control times 0 = : t 1 < … < t r := 1 0=:t_{1}<\ldots<t_{r}:=1 . As one can expect, apart from the restrictions on 𝒮 {\mathcal{S}} , some restrictions on β \beta are needed. A sufficient condition is that the (finite) set of values η ⁡ ( β ) \eta(\beta) at which β \beta is not smooth is contained in the set of regular points of 𝒮 {\mathcal{S}} and η ⁡ ( β ) \eta(\beta) does not meet the set of control times. If 𝒮 ⊂ ℝ n {\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} is a finite union (connected by analytic paths) of n n -dimensional convex polyhedra, we can even ‘estimate’ (using Bernstein’s polynomials) the degree of the involved polynomial path. This requires: (1) a polynomial double curve selection lemma for convex polyhedra involving only degree 3 3 cuspidal curves; (2) to find the simplest polynomial paths that connect two convex polyhedra (whose union is connected by analytic paths), and (3) some improvements concerning well-known bounds for Bernstein’s polynomials (and their high order derivatives) to approximate continuous functions that are not differentiable on their whole domain.

1.
Introduction

A natural ‘logistic’ problem in Real Geometry, whose affirmative solution would generalize the curve selection lemma
[
BC
, p.989]
,
[
M
, §3]
,
[
W
, Lem.18.3]
, is the following (see also
[
FGU
, §4.C]
). Let
X
be a connected topological space of certain type, let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in X
be finitely many points (control points) and
0=:t_{1}<\cdots<t_{r}:=1
be finitely many values (control times).

Problem 1.1

(Curve selection lemma through finitely many points)
.

Is there a (continuous) path
\alpha:[0,1]\to X
of ‘certain prefixed type’ such that
\alpha(t_{i})=x_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
?

Suppose we already have a continuous path
\beta:[0,1]\to X
such that
\beta(t_{i})=x_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
, that
X
is a metric space and fix
\varepsilon>0
.

Problem 1.2

(Approximation of curves through finitely many points)
.

Is there a (continuous) path
\alpha:[0,1]\to X
of ‘certain prefixed type’ such that
\alpha(t_{i})=x_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
and
\operatorname{dist}(\alpha(t),\beta(t))<\varepsilon
for each
t\in[0,1]
?

1.1.
Semialgebraic setting

A subset
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is
semialgebraic when it admits a description by a finite boolean combination of polynomial equalities and inequalities. The category of semialgebraic sets is closed under basic boolean operations, but also under usual topological operations: taking closures (denoted by
\operatorname{Cl}(\cdot)
), interiors (denoted by
\operatorname{Int}(\cdot)
), connected components, etc. If
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are semialgebraic sets, a map
f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
is semialgebraic if its graph is a semialgebraic set.

In the following smooth means
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
. A map
f:U\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
on an open semialgebraic set
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is Nash if it is smooth and semialgebraic. Recall that Nash maps are analytic maps
[
BCR
, Prop.8.1.8]
. If
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a semialgebraic set, a map
f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is Nash if there exist an open semialgebraic neighborhood
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of
{\mathcal{S}}
and a Nash extension
F:U\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
of
f
to
U
. Analogously, a Nash manifold is a semialgebraic subset
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that is a smooth submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. As an application of
[
BCR
, Prop.8.1.8]
one deduces that Nash manifolds are analytic manifolds. Recall that open semialgebraic subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
admit by the Finiteness Theorem
[
BCR
, Th.2.7.2]
a description as a finite union of basic open semialgebraic sets, that is, semialgebraic sets of the type
\{f_{i}>0,\ldots,f_{r}>0\}
where
f_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x}]:={\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x}_{1},\ldots,{\tt x}_{n}]
. Along the article we will use typewriter symbols
{\tt x},{\tt y},{\tt z},{\tt t}
to denote variables or tuples of variables, whereas we use the symbols
x,y,z,t
to denote values or points that we substitute in variables or tuples of variables
{\tt x},{\tt y},{\tt z},{\tt t}
.

1.2.
State of the art for semialgebraic sets and Nash paths

In this work we study Problems
1.1
and
1.2
when
X={\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a semialgebraic set and
\alpha:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}}
is a Nash path. We prove results that involve a tight control of the behavior of the obtained Nash/polynomial path (Theorem
1.6
(polynomial case) and Main Theorems
1.8
(Nash case) and
1.9
(PL case)). Using these results, we deduce that a sufficient condition to solve Problems
1.1
and
1.2
is that
{\mathcal{S}}
is connected by analytic paths. In fact, if the number of points

is large enough and they are in general position, the connexion by analytic paths is a necessary condition. A ‘theoretical’ (but not constructive) solution to Problem
1.1
follows straightforwardly from
[
Fe
, Main Thm.1.4]
, where we characterize the semialgebraic subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of dimension
d
that are images of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
under a Nash map. Namely,

Theorem 1.3

(Nash images of affine spaces,
[
Fe
, Main Thm.1.4]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a semialgebraic set of dimension
d
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

There exists a Nash map
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
f({\mathbb{R}}^{d})={\mathcal{S}}
.

(ii)

{\mathcal{S}}
is connected by analytic paths.

1.2.1.
Nash curve selection lemma through finitely many points.

The announced ‘theoretical’ (but not constructive) consequence of Theorem
1.3
is the following.

Corollary 1.4

(Nash curve selection lemma through finitely many points)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a semialgebraic set connected by analytic paths. Fix control points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in{\mathcal{S}}
and control values
0=:t_{1}<\cdots<t_{r}:=1
. Then there exists a Nash path
\alpha:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}}
such that
\alpha(t_{i})=x_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
.

Proof.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a Nash map such that
f({\mathbb{R}}^{d})={\mathcal{S}}
and let
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be such that
f(z_{i})=x_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
. Using for instance Lagrange’s interpolation, we find a polynomial path
\beta:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(of degree
\leq r-1
) such that
\beta(t_{i})=z_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
. Thus,
\alpha:=f\circ\beta:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}}
is a Nash path that satisfies the required conditions.
∎

Remark 1.5

(Classical curve selection lemma)
.

In
[
Fe
, §9]
it is proved that each semialgebraic set
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the union of its connected components by analytic paths, which are finitely many semialgebraic sets
{\mathcal{S}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{S}}_{r}
. If
x\in\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}})
, we may assume
x\in\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{1})
. Thus,
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}\cup\{x\}
is again connected by analytic paths
[
Fe
, Main Thm.1.4 & Lem.7.4]
and by Corollary
1.4
there exists a Nash path
\alpha:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}}_{1}\cup\{x\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}\cup\{x\}
such that
\alpha(0)=x
and
\alpha((0,1])\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{1}\subset{\mathcal{S}}
. Thus, Corollary
1.4
provides the classical curve selection lemma as a straightforward consequence.
\sqbullet

The main results of this article provide a different proof of Corollary
1.4
(Problem
1.1
) with a more constructive flavor, which is not based on the existential use of

Fe

1.2.2.
Polynomial curve selection lemma through finitely many points

Let
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}):[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a continuous semialgebraic path. We claim: There exists a minimal finite set
\eta(\alpha)\subset[a,b]
such that
\alpha|_{[a,b]\setminus\eta(\alpha)}
is a Nash map.

Proof.

Consider the continuous semialgebraic map
\beta_{k}:[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\ t\mapsto(t,\alpha_{k}(t))
for
k=1,\ldots,n
. By
[
BCR
, Prop.2.9.10]
there exist finitely many points
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\in[a,b]
such that
M_{ki}:=\beta_{k}((t_{i},t_{i+1}))
is a Nash submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
for
i=1,\ldots,r-1
and
k=1,\ldots,n
. For each
p\in M_{ki}
denote the tangent line to
M_{ki}
at
p
with
T_{p}M_{ki}
. Consider the projection
\pi_{1}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
onto the first coordinate and let
R_{ki}:=\{p\in M_{ki}:\ \dim(\pi_{1}(T_{p}M_{ki}))=0\}
. We claim:
The semialgebraic set
R_{ki}
is finite for each
i=1,\ldots,r-1
and each
k=1,\ldots,n
.

If
p\in R_{ki}
(for some
i=1,\ldots,r-1
and
k=1,\ldots,n
), then
\alpha_{k}
is not differentiable at
\pi_{1}(p)
. By
[
Dr
, Ch.7.Thm.(3.2) (II
m
), p.115]
the non-differentiability locus of
\alpha_{k}
is a semialgebraic set of dimension
\leq 0
, that is, it is a finite set. Consequently,
R_{ki}
is a finite set, as claimed.

We conclude that
\eta(\alpha)\subset\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\}\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}R_{ik}
is a finite set, as required.
∎

By
[
BCR
, Prop.8.1.12]
and after reparameterizing (locally at
a
and
b
if necessary) we may assume that
\alpha
is analytic at the points
a,b
and consequently that
\eta(\alpha)\subset(a,b)
. Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{1},{\mathcal{S}}_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be two Nash manifolds. A (Nash) bridge between
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
is the image
\Gamma
of a Nash arc
\alpha:[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\alpha([-1,0))\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
and
\alpha((0,1])\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
. The point
\alpha(0)
is called the base point of
\Gamma
. In case
{\mathcal{S}}_{1},{\mathcal{S}}_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are open semialgebraic sets and there exists a Nash bridge
\alpha:[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
between
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
, we can modify
\alpha
to have a polynomial arc
\alpha:[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\alpha([-1,0))\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
and
\alpha((0,1])\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
(see
[
FU

).

In
[
FU
]
we study the images of the closed unit ball under polynomial maps. As a main tool, we prove there the following result

Theorem 1.6

(Smart polynomial curve,
[
FU
, Lem.3.1]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{S}}_{r}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be connected open semialgebraic sets (non-necessarily pairwise different) and denote
{\mathcal{S}}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
. Pick control points
p_{i}\in\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i})
and assume there exists a polynomial bridge
\Gamma_{i}
between
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{i+1}
. Denote the base point of
\Gamma_{i}
with
q_{i}\in\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i})\cap\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i+1})
. Fix control times
s_{0}:=0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{r}<1=:s_{r}
and
s_{i}\in(t_{i},t_{i+1})
for
i=1,\ldots,r-1
. Then there exists a polynomial path
\alpha:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that satisfies:

(i)

\alpha([0,1])\subset{\mathcal{S}}\cup\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{r},q_{1},\ldots,q_{r-1}\}
.

(ii)

\alpha(t_{i})=p_{i}
for

(iii)

\alpha((t_{i},s_{i}))\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{i}

\alpha((s_{i},t_{i+1}))\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{i+1}
and
\alpha(s_{i})=q_{i}
for

In addition, if
\varepsilon>0
and
\beta:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

\eta(\beta)\subset(0,1)\setminus\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r},s_{1},\ldots,s_{r-1}\}

Remark 1.7
.

Contrary to what we have stated in Problems

1.3.
Main results

The first part of Theorem
1.6
concerns Problem
1.1
, whereas its second part concerns Problem
1.2
. We cannot expect a general result (that is, without the assumption that the
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
are open semialgebraic subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
) of similar nature involving polynomial paths instead of Nash paths. In general, semialgebraic sets do not contain rational paths. By
[
C
,
V
]
, a generic complex hypersurface
Z
of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
of degree
d\geq 2n-2
for
n\geq 4
and of degree
d\geq 2n-1
for
n=2,3
does not contain rational curves. If
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a semialgebraic set whose Zariski closure
X
in
{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is a generic hypersurface of
{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
of high enough degree, then its Zariski closure
Z
in
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
does not contain rational curves, so
{\mathcal{S}}
cannot contain rational paths. This means in particular (as general real algebraic sets are birational to real hypersurfaces) that general semialgebraic sets do not contain polynomial paths.

1.3.2.
Piecewise linear semialgebraic sets.

In Section

1.8

\alpha((s_{i},t_{i+1}))\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{i+1}
and
\alpha(s_{i})=q_{i}
for

(iv)

The restriction

is as close as wanted to the piecewise linear parameterization

of the polygonal path that connects the points

Graph

Basic concepts and preliminary results

In this section we recall and present some preliminary concepts and results that will be the key to prove Main Theorem
1.8
.

2.1.
Regular and singular points of a semialgebraic set.

Recall that the set of regular points of a semialgebraic set
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is defined as follows. Let
X
be the Zariski closure of
{\mathcal{S}}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\widetilde{X}
the complexification of
X
, that is, the smallest complex algebraic subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
that contains
X
. The set
\operatorname{Sing}(\widetilde{X})
of singular points of
\widetilde{X}
corresponds to the collection of those points of
\widetilde{X}
that do not admit a neighborhood diffeomorphic to a complex manifold. Define
\operatorname{Reg}(X):=X\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(\widetilde{X})
and let
\operatorname{Reg}({\mathcal{S}})
be the interior of
{\mathcal{S}}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(\widetilde{X})
in
\operatorname{Reg}(X)
. Observe that
\operatorname{Reg}({\mathcal{S}})
is a finite union of disjoint Nash manifolds maybe of different dimensions. We refer the reader to
[
Fe
, §2.A]
for further details concerning the set of regular points of a semialgebraic set.

2.2.
Hironaka’s desingularization

A rational map
f:=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}):Z\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
on an algebraic set
Z\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is regular if its components are quotients of polynomials
f_{k}:=\frac{g_{k}}{h_{k}}
such that
Z\cap\{h_{k}=0\}=\varnothing
. Hironaka’s desingularization results
[
Hi
]
are powerful tools and we recall here the one we need.

Theorem 2.1

(Desingularization)
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an algebraic set. Then there exist a non-singular algebraic set
X^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and a proper regular map
f:X^{\prime}\to X
such that

f|_{X^{\prime}\setminus f^{-1}(\operatorname{Sing}(X))}:X^{\prime}\setminus f^{-1}(\operatorname{Sing}(X))\rightarrow X\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(X)

is a diffeomorphism whose inverse map is also regular.

Remark 2.2
.

If
X
is pure dimensional,
X\setminus\operatorname{Sing}X
is dense in
X
. As
f
is proper, it is surjective.
\sqbullet

2.3.
Topology of spaces of continuous functions

Let
[a,b]\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a compact interval and
\Omega\subset[a,b]
an open set. For each
\ell\geq 1
consider the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}_{\Omega}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}})
of continuous functions on
[a,b]
that are
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}
on
\Omega
. We endow
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}_{\Omega}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}})
with the
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}_{\Omega}
topology that has as basis of open neighborhoods of
g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}_{\Omega}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}})
the family of sets of the type:

{\mathcal{U}}^{\ell}_{g,K,\varepsilon}:=\{f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\Omega}^{\ell}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}}):\ \|f-g\|_{[a,b]}<\varepsilon,\ \|f^{(k)}-g^{(k)}\|_{K}<\varepsilon:\ k=1,\ldots,\ell\}

where
K\subset\Omega
is a compact set,
\varepsilon>0
and
\|h\|_{T}:=\max\{h(x):\ x\in T\}
for each compact subset
T\subset[a,b]
. Sometimes we will omit the subindex
T
when it is clear from the context. If
\Omega=[a,b]
, the previous topology is the usual
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}
topology of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}([a,b])
. Observe that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\Omega}^{\ell}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}}^{n})={\mathcal{C}}_{\Omega}^{\ell}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}})\times\cdots\times{\mathcal{C}}_{\Omega}^{\ell}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}})
and we consider the product topology in this space. In particular if
f:=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\Omega}^{\ell}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
T\subset[a,b]
is a compact set, we denote to lighten notation
\|f\|_{T}:=\|\sqrt{f_{1}^{2}+\cdots+f_{n}^{2}}\|_{T}=\max\{\sqrt{f_{1}^{2}(x)+\cdots+f_{n}^{2}(x)}:\ x\in T\}
. If
X\subset[a,b]
, one defines analogously the
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}_{\Omega\cap X}
-topology of the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}_{\Omega\cap X}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
.

The following result follows from
[
H
, §2.5. Ex.10, pp. 64-65]
using standard arguments.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open set and
\varphi:U\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
a
{\mathcal{C}}^{k}
map for some
0\leq k\leq\ell
. Consider the map
\varphi^{*}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}_{\Omega}([a,b],U)\to{\mathcal{C}}^{k}_{\Omega}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}}^{m}),\ f\mapsto\varphi\circ f
, where both spaces are endowed with their
{\mathcal{C}}^{k}_{\Omega}
-topologies. Then
\varphi^{*}
is continuous.

In addition, one has the following.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X\subset[a,b]
and consider the restriction map

\rho:{\mathcal{C}}_{\Omega}^{\ell}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\Omega\cap X}^{\ell}(X,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\ f\mapsto f|_{X},

where the spaces are endowed with their respective
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}_{\Omega}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}_{\Omega\cap X}
topologies. Then
\rho
is continuous and if in addition
X\subset[a,b]
is closed, then
\rho
is surjective.

2.4.
Stone-Weierstrass’ approximation and Bernstein’s polynomials

The proof of Theorem
1.6
provided in
[
FU
]
involves Stone-Weierstrass’ approximation (controlling the behavior of a large enough number of derivatives). We want to analyze the crucial role that Stone-Weierstrass’ approximation plays. There are many constructive results in this direction and we refer the reader to
[
DL
, Ch.7.§.2]
where estimations of the approximation errors are available. In this article we will use Bernstein’s polynomials, which provided a pioneer constructive proof of Stone-Weierstrass’ approximation theorem
[
B
]
. We suggest the reader
[
DL
, Ch.10]
and
[
L1
, §1]
for further references. Although Bernstein’s polynomials converge slowly to the approximated function, they have shape preserving properties
[
DL
, Ch.10.Thm.3.3]
and a ‘good local behavior’
[
DL
, Ch.10.(3.3)]
: If two continuous functions coincide on a subinterval of their common domain, their Bernstein’s polynomials of high degree are very similar in (the compact subsets of) such subinterval, even when we compare their high order derivatives (Lemma
5.1
).

We recall some properties of the celebrated Bernstein’s polynomials and we present some improvements in this work to fit our requirements (Theorem
2.9
). The Bernstein’s approximation polynomial (of degree
\nu
) of a real function
f:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is

B_{\nu}(f):=\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}f\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}\Big)B_{k,\nu}({\tt x}),\quad\text{where $B_{k,\nu}({\tt x}):=\binom{\nu}{k}{\tt x}^{k}(1-{\tt x})^{\nu-k}\quad\text{for $k=0,\ldots,\nu$}.$}

2.4.1.
Basic properties of Bernstein’s polynomials

Each Bernstein’s basis polynomial
B_{k,\nu}({\tt x})
of degree
\nu
is strictly positive on the interval
(0,1)
. In fact, for each
x\in(0,1)
the values
\{B_{k,\nu}(x)\}_{k=0}^{\nu}
constitute the probability mass function of the binomial distribution
{\mathcal{B}}(\nu,x)
. This means (see
[
L1
, p. 6]
):

\bullet

\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}B_{k,\nu}(x)=1
,

\bullet

\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}kB_{k,\nu}(x)=\nu x
as it is the mean of
{\mathcal{B}}(\nu,x)
,

\bullet

\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}(\nu x-k)^{2}B_{k,\nu}(x)=\nu x(1-x)
as it is the variance of
{\mathcal{B}}(\nu,x)
.

In addition,

(i)

If
m\leq f\leq M
, then
m\leq B_{\nu}(f)\leq M
(see
[
L1
, (2) p. 5]
).

(ii)

B_{k,\nu}({\tt x})=(1-{\tt x})B_{k,\nu-1}({\tt x})+{\tt x}B_{k-1,\nu-1}({\tt x})
(which follows from the properties of binomial numbers).

(iii)

For each
h\in{\mathbb{R}}
denote
\Delta_{h}f(x):=f(x+h)-f(x)
and

\Delta_{h}^{k}f(x):=\Delta_{h}(\Delta_{h}^{k-1}f(x))=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}f(x+jh).

If
B_{\nu}^{(k)}(f)
denotes the
k^{\text{th}}
derivative of
B_{\nu}(f)
, we have by
[
L1
, §1.4(2), p.12]

B_{\nu}^{(k)}(f)({\tt x})=\frac{\nu!}{(\nu-k)!}\sum_{i=0}^{\nu-k}\Delta_{\frac{1}{\nu}}^{k}f\Big(\frac{i}{\nu}\Big)B_{i,\nu-k}({\tt x})

for
k=0,\ldots,\nu
.

Remark 2.5
.

If
f\in{\mathcal{C}}([a,b])
, write
f^{*}:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}},\ t\mapsto f(a+t(b-a))
. Observe that
f=f^{*}(\frac{x-a}{b-a})
and define
B_{\nu}^{\bullet}(f):=B_{\nu}(f^{*})(\frac{x-a}{b-a})
the
Bernstein’s polynomial of
f
of degree
\nu
for the interval
[a,b]
. The changes one makes in subsequent formulas for the interval
[0,1]
to obtain the corresponding ones for the interval
[a,b]
are of the following type: the polynomial
{\tt x}
is changed by
\frac{{\tt x}-a}{b-a}
, so the polynomial
1-{\tt x}
is changed by
\frac{b-{\tt x}}{b-a}
. For instance, the polynomial
{\tt x}(1-{\tt x})\leadsto\frac{({\tt x}-a)(b-{\tt x})}{(b-a)^{2}}
and the polynomial
(1-2{\tt x})\leadsto\frac{((b-a)-2({\tt x}-a))}{b-a}
.
\sqbullet

2.4.2.
Derivatives of Bernstein’s polynomials

One of the most remarkable properties of Bernstein’s approximation, which is very useful for our constructions, is that derivatives
B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(f)
of
B_{\nu}(f)
of each order
\ell
converge to corresponding derivatives of
f
, see Lorentz
[
L2
]
: If
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}([0,1])
for some
\ell\geq 0
, then
\lim_{\nu\to\infty}B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(f)=f^{(\ell)}
uniformly on the interval
[0,1]
. This property can be viewed as a compensation for the ‘slow’ convergence of
B_{\nu}(f)
to
f
. If
\|\cdot\|_{[0,1]}
denotes the maximum norm on
[0,1]
, the error bound

|B_{\nu}(f)(x)-f(x)|\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}x(1-x)\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{[0,1]}

(2.1)

provided in
[
DL
, Ch.10, (3.4), p.308]
shows that the rate of convergence is at least
1/\nu
for
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}([0,1])
. On the other hand, Voronovskaya’s asymptotic formula
[
Vo
]
(or
[
L1
, §.1.6.1]
)

\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\nu(B_{\nu}(f)(x)-f(x))=\frac{1}{2}x(1-x)f^{\prime\prime}(x)

(2.2)

shows that for
x\in(0,1)
with
f^{\prime\prime}(x)\neq 0
, the asymptotic rate of convergence is precisely
1/\nu
. In

(Error bound,
[

If

for some

Remark 2.7
.

The reader can prove inductively that

\frac{d^{\ell}}{dx^{\ell}}(x(1-x)f^{\prime\prime}(x))=-\ell(\ell-1)f^{(\ell)}+\ell(1-2x)f^{(\ell+1)}+x(1-x)f^{(\ell+2)}

is the
\ell^{\text{th}}
derivative of

\sqbullet

In addition, Voronovskaya’s formula (

) can be ‘differentiated’ to determine the asymptotic behavior of the error for the high order derivatives of the Bernstein’s polynomial:

Theorem 2.8

(Asymptotic behavior,
[
Fl
, Thm.2]
)

If

for some

uniformly in the interval

Thus, the
\ell

converges to

at the rate of

when the
\ell
th derivative of

is non-zero.

2.4.3.
Control of the derivatives of Bernstein’s polynomials on compact subsets.

In this paper we deal with continuous functions
f:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
that are
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}
only on an open subset
\Omega\subset[0,1]
and we need to control the behavior of a large enough number of derivatives of the Bernstein’s polynomials of
f
on a compact subset
K\subset\Omega
. A first attempt is to smooth our function
f
on
[0,1]\setminus\Omega
and to make use of Lemma
5.1
together with
[
Fl
, Thm.1 & Thm.2]
. To avoid an increase of complexity when smoothing the initial data, we amalgamate in Theorem
2.9
the quoted results
[
Fl
, Thm.1 & Thm.2]
and
[
DL
, Ch.10.§2-3]
to approach the situation we need. Summarizing we provide a bound for the error of each derivative on the chosen compact set and show how the error behaves asymptotically. To make the presentation of the article more discursive, we postpone the proof of the following result until Section
5
.

Theorem 2.9

(Convergence of derivatives on compact subsets)
.

Let
f:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous function that is
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell+4}
on an open subset
\Omega\subset(0,1)
for some
\ell\geq 0
. Let
K\subset\Omega
be a compact set. Then there exists a constant
C_{f,K,\ell}>0
such that

|B_{\nu}(f)^{(\ell)}(x)-f^{(\ell)}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}\Big(\ell(\ell-1)\sum_{k=\ell}^{\ell+3}\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{K}}{(k-\ell)!}\\
+\ell|1-2x|\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{\ell+3}\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{K}}{(k-\ell-1)!}+x(1-x)\sum_{k=\ell+2}^{\ell+3}\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{K}}{(k-\ell-2)!}\Big)+\frac{C_{f,K,\ell}}{\nu^{2}}

for each
x\in K
(error bound). In addition, for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant

such that

\Big|\nu(B_{\nu}(f)^{(\ell)}(x)-f^{(\ell)}(x))-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{\ell}}{d{\tt x}^{\ell}}(x(1-x)f^{\prime\prime}(x))\Big|<\varepsilon+\frac{C^{*}_{f,K,\ell,\varepsilon}}{\nu}

for each
x\in K
and
\nu>\ell
(control of the asymptotic behavior).

2.5.
Polynomial approximation combined with interpolation

We adapt
[
Bo
]
to prove the following result that combines Bernstein’s polynomial approximation (controlling the behavior of a large enough number of derivatives on a compact subset) with interpolation on a finite set. We include full details for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2.10

Let

and
\Omega\subset[a,b]

(ii)

(iii)

for

Take polynomials

The Taylor expansion of

at

has the form

for some

, whereas the Taylor expansion of

at

(for

P_{ik}=e_{ijk}({\tt t}-t_{j})^{\ell+1}+\cdots\\
\text{where }e_{ijk}:=c_{ik}(t_{j}-t_{i})^{k}((\ell+1)(t_{j}-t_{i})^{\ell})^{\ell+1}\prod_{\lambda\neq i,j}((t_{j}-t_{i})^{\ell+1}-(t_{\lambda}-t_{i})^{\ell+1})^{\ell+1}.

means ‘plus terms of higher degree’ with respect to either

or
{\tt t}-t_{j}
depending on each case. To compute

of each factor of the product

and then to multiply them.

Define
K^{\prime}:=K\cup\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\}
and

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle:=\max\{\|P_{ik}\|_{[a,b]},\|P_{ik}^{(m)}\|_{K^{\prime}}:\ 1\leq i\leq r,\ 0\leq k\leq\ell,\ 1\leq m\leq\ell\},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle:=\frac{\varepsilon}{1+r(\ell+1)M}.

(2.6)

By Theorem
2.9
there exists a Bernstein’s polynomial
h\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt t}]
of
f
(in the interval
[a,b]
) such that
\|h-f\|_{[a,b]}<\delta
and
\|h^{(k)}-f^{(k)}\|_{K^{\prime}}<\delta
for
k=1,\ldots,\ell
. Define

where

for
i=1,\ldots,r
and
k=0,\ldots,\ell
. Thus,

g^{(m)}(t_{j})=h^{(m)}(t_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}b_{ik}P_{ik}^{(m)}(t_{j})=h^{(m)}(t_{j})+b_{jm}=f^{(m)}(t_{j})

for
j=1,\ldots,r
and
m=0,\ldots,\ell
.

As
|b_{ik}|=|f^{(k)}(t_{i})-h^{(k)}(t_{i})|<\delta
for
i=1,\ldots,r
and
k=0,\ldots,\ell
, we have

\displaystyle\|g-f\|_{[a,b]}

\displaystyle\leq\|h-f\|_{[a,b]}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}|b_{ik}|\|P_{ik}\|_{[a,b]}<\delta+r(\ell+1)M\delta=\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\|g^{(m)}-f^{(m)}\|_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\|h^{(m)}-f^{(m)}\|_{K}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}|b_{ik}|\|P_{ik}^{(m)}\|_{K}<\delta+r(\ell+1)M\delta=\varepsilon

for each

3.
Drawing Nash paths inside semialgebraic sets

In this section we prove Main Theorem
1.8
. Before that we need a preliminary result. Again, if we write a series in the form
h:=a_{k}{\tt t}^{k}+\cdots
, we mean that the lowest order term is
a_{k}{\tt t}^{k}
(with
a_{k}\neq 0
) and the remaining terms have higher order and are not relevant for our computation.

3.1.
Double Nash curve selection lemma.

The following result is an amalgamated modification of the classical (Nash) curve selection lemma
[
BCR
, Prop.8.1.13]
and double polynomial curve selection lemma
[
FU
, Lem.3.8]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Double Nash curve selection lemma)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a semialgebraic set of dimension
d\geq 2
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
the set of points of
{\mathcal{S}}
of dimension
d
. Pick a point
p\in\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{d})
. Then there exists a Nash arc
\alpha:[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\alpha(0)=p
,
\alpha([-1,1]\setminus\{0\})\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
and
\alpha([-1,0))\cap\alpha((0,1])=\varnothing
. If
{\mathcal{S}}
has dimension
n
, we may assume
\alpha
is a polynomial arc.

Proof.

Let
X
be the Zariski closure of
{\mathcal{S}}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, which is an algebraic set of dimension
d
. By Theorem
2.1
there exist a non-singular algebraic set
X^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and a proper regular map
f:X^{\prime}\to X
such that
f|_{X^{\prime}\setminus f^{-1}(\operatorname{Sing}(X))}:X^{\prime}\setminus f^{-1}(\operatorname{Sing}(X))\rightarrow X\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(X)
is a Nash diffeomorphism whose inverse map is also regular. As
\dim(\operatorname{Sing}(X))\leq d-1
, we have
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(X)
is dense in
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}
. As
p\in\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{d})=\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{d}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(X))
and
f
is proper, there exists a point
p^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cl}(f^{-1}({\mathcal{S}}_{d}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(X)))
such that
f(p^{\prime})=p
. Assume that we find a Nash arc
\beta:[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
such that
\beta(0)=p^{\prime}
,
\beta([-1,1]\setminus\{0\})\subset f^{-1}({\mathcal{S}}_{d}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(X))
and
\beta([-1,0))\cap\beta((0,1])=\varnothing
. As
f
is a regular map and in particular a Nash map, if we define
\alpha:=f\circ\beta
, we will be done.

So let us assume:
the Zariski closure
X
of
{\mathcal{S}}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is non-singular (and consequently
X
is a disjoint union of finitely many Nash manifolds maybe of different dimensions) and we have an algebraic set
Y\subset X
of dimension strictly smaller than
d
‘to be avoided’
. Let
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open semialgebraic neighborhood of
p
in
X
endowed with a Nash diffeomorphism
\varphi:U\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\varphi(p)=0
. Let
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime\prime}:=\varphi(({\mathcal{S}}_{d}\setminus Y)\cap U)
and assume that we find a Nash arc
\gamma:[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\gamma(0)=0
,
\gamma([-1,1]\setminus\{0\})\subset{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime\prime}
and
\gamma([-1,0))\cap\gamma((0,1])=\varnothing
. If we define
\beta:=\varphi^{-1}\circ\gamma
, we will be done.

Thus, we can suppose:
{\mathcal{S}}
is pure dimensional of dimension
n\geq 2
, the Zariski closure of
{\mathcal{S}}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
p\in\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}})
is the origin
. As
\operatorname{Int}({\mathcal{S}})
is dense in
{\mathcal{S}}
(because
{\mathcal{S}}
is pure dimensional), there exists by
[
BCR
, Prop.8.1.13]
a Nash arc
\eta:=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}):[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\eta(0)=p
and
\eta((0,1])\subset\operatorname{Int}({\mathcal{S}})
. After shrinking the domain of
\eta
, we may assume that each
\eta_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}[[{\tt t}]]_{\rm alg}
is an algebraic analytic series. After a linear change of coordinates and a reparameterization of
\eta
, we may assume that
\eta_{2}:=t^{\ell_{2}}
for some
\ell_{2}\geq 1
(recall that
n\geq 2
). As
\operatorname{Int}({\mathcal{S}})
is an open semialgebraic subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
p\in\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}})=\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{Int}({\mathcal{S}}))
, there exist polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x}]
such that
f_{i}(p)=0
for
i=1,\ldots,r
and

\eta((0,\varepsilon])\subset\{f_{1}>0,\ldots,f_{r}>0\}\subset\operatorname{Int}({\mathcal{S}})

for some
0<\varepsilon<1
(because
\operatorname{Int}({\mathcal{S}})
can be written by
[
BCR
, Thm.2.7.2]
as a finite union of basic open semialgebraic sets, see §
1.1
). Consider the algebraic series
f_{j}(\eta)\in{\mathbb{R}}[[{\tt t}]]_{\rm alg}
, which satisfies
f_{j}(\eta)=a_{j}{\tt t}^{k_{j}}+\cdots
for some
a_{j}>0
and
k_{j}\geq 1
. Define
m:=\max\{k_{j}:\ j=1,\ldots,r\}+\ell_{2}+1
and let
q>2m
be an odd positive integer. Let
\zeta_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}[[{\tt t}]]_{\rm alg}
be an algebraic series such that

is a univariate polynomial for

and

(that is,

). Denote

and
\zeta:=(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})
. Define

, where
e_{1}:=(1,0,\ldots,0)
. As the exponent

is odd, all the exponents of the non-zero monomials (if any) of the polynomial
\xi_{1}({\tt t}^{2})
are even and

, we deduce
\gamma([-\varepsilon,0))\cap\gamma((0,\varepsilon])=\varnothing
for each
\varepsilon>0
.

Let
{\tt x}:=({\tt x}_{1},\ldots,{\tt x}_{n})
,
{\tt y}:=({\tt y}_{1},\ldots,{\tt y}_{n})
and
{\tt z}
be a single variable. Write

where

. Then

f_{j}(\gamma({\tt t}))=f_{j}(\xi({\tt t}^{2})+{\tt t}^{q}e_{1})=f_{j}(\eta({\tt t}^{2})+{\tt t}^{2m}(\zeta({\tt t}^{2})+{\tt t}^{q-2m}e_{1}))\\
=f_{j}(\eta({\tt t}^{2}))+{\tt t}^{2m}h_{j}(\eta({\tt t}^{2}),\zeta({\tt t}^{2})+t^{q-2m}e_{1},{\tt t}^{2m})=a_{j}{\tt t}^{2k_{j}}+\cdots,

so for
\varepsilon>0
small enough
\gamma:[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a polynomial arc such that in addition

\gamma([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\setminus\{0\})\subset\{f_{1}>0,\ldots,f_{r}>0\}\subset\operatorname{Int}({\mathcal{S}})

and
\gamma(0)=0=p
. After an affine reparameterization in order to have the interval
[-1,1]
as the domain of
\gamma
, we deduce
\gamma
is the searched polynomial path.
∎

We revisit next a well-known characterization of the connexion by analytic paths for semialgebraic sets. This result was proved indirectly in

.

be a semialgebraic set of dimension

(i)

(ii)

is pure dimensional and there exists an analytic path
\alpha:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}}
whose image meets all the connected components of
\operatorname{Reg}({\mathcal{S}})

{\mathcal{S}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}

\operatorname{Reg}({\mathcal{S}})
, which are pairwise disjoint. Let
\Lambda

whose vertices are the Nash manifolds
{\mathcal{S}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}
and such that there exists an edge between the vertices
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{j}
if and only if there exists a Nash bridge inside
{\mathcal{S}}
between
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{j}
. When

is a connected graph, there exists a sequence of semialgebraic sets

such that

and for each index

between
{\mathcal{T}}_{i}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{i+1}
.

(i)
\Longrightarrow

is pure dimensional. Otherwise, there exists a point

be the Zariski closure of

By Lemma
1.10

(ii)
\Longrightarrow

4.

\bullet
{\mathcal{K}}
\alpha
p

(n+1)
-dimensional closed unit ball
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{n+1}(0,1)
or the
n
-dimensional closed unit ball
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{n}(0,1)
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

{\mathcal{S}}

5.
Convergence of derivatives of Bernstein’s polynomials on compact subsets

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
2.9
. We recall for the sake of completeness some notation, terminology and preliminary statements from
[
Fl
]
. Let
f:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous function.

5.1.
Derivatives of divided differences of a continuous function.

For each pair of integers
s,t\geq 0
define

B_{\nu,s,t}(f)(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{\nu-s}\Big(\Big[\frac{k}{\nu},\ldots,\frac{k+s}{\nu},\underbrace{x,\ldots,x}_{\text{$t$ times}}\Big]f\Big)B_{k,\nu-s}(x)

where
[x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}]f
denotes the
k
th order divided difference of
f
at the points
x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}\in[0,1]
. Write
\ell:=s+t
. If
f
is a
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}
-function, there exists by
[
D
, Cor.3.4.2]
a value
\xi_{k}
in the smallest interval that contains the points
\frac{k}{\nu},\ldots,\frac{k+s}{\nu},x
such that

\Big[\frac{k}{\nu},\ldots,\frac{k+s}{\nu},\underbrace{x,\ldots,x}_{\text{$t$ times}}\Big]f=\frac{f^{(\ell)}(\xi_{k})}{\ell!}.

Thus, if
x\in[0,1]
, we have by §
2.4.1
,

|B_{\nu,s,t}(f)(x)|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\nu-s}\Big|\frac{f^{(\ell)}(\xi_{k})}{\ell!}\Big|B_{k,\nu-s}\leq\frac{\|f^{(\ell)}\|_{[0,1]}}{\ell!}=\frac{\|f^{(s+t)}\|_{[0,1]}}{(s+t)!}.

(5.1)

We have
B_{\nu,0,0}(f)=B_{\nu}(f)
and by
[
Fl
, pag.133]

B_{\nu}(f)(x)-f(x)=\frac{1}{\nu}x(1-x)B_{\nu,1,1}(f)(x).

(5.2)

Differentiating (
5.2
) at a point
x\in[0,1]
where
f
is differentiable, we obtain

B_{\nu}(f)^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{\nu}((1-2x)B_{\nu,1,1}(f)(x)+x(1-x)(B_{\nu,1,1}(f))^{\prime}(x)).

Using Leibniz rules and differentiating
\ell
times equation (
5.2
) (at a point
x\in[0,1]
where
f
is
\ell
times differentiable), we obtain
[
Fl
, Eq.(3.2)]

(B_{\nu}(f))^{(\ell)}(x)-f^{(\ell)}(x)=\frac{1}{\nu}(-\ell(\ell-1)(B_{\nu,1,1}(f))^{(\ell-2)}(x)\\
+\ell(1-2x)(B_{\nu,1,1}(f))^{(\ell-1)}(x)+x(1-x)(B_{\nu,1,1}(f))^{(\ell)}(x)).

(5.3)

Let
x\in[0,1]
be a point such that
f
is a
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell+2}
-function on a neighborhood of
x
. By
[
Fl
, Lem.1]
one deduces

(B_{\nu,1,1}(f))^{(\ell)}(x)=\ell!\sum_{k=1}^{\ell+1}k\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}\cdots\frac{\nu-k+1}{\nu}B_{\nu,k,\ell-k+2}(f)(x).

(5.4)

Thus, if
f
is a
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell+2}
-function on
[0,1]
, we have by (
5.1
) and the equality
\sum_{k=1}^{\ell+1}k=\frac{(\ell+2)(\ell+1)}{2}

|(B_{\nu,1,1}(f))^{(\ell)}(x)|\leq\ell!\sum_{k=1}^{\ell+1}k\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}\cdots\frac{\nu-k+1}{\nu}|B_{\nu,k,\ell-k+2}(f)(x)|\\
\leq\ell!\sum_{k=1}^{\ell+1}k\frac{\|f^{(\ell+2)}\|_{[0,1]}}{(\ell+2)!}=\frac{\|f^{(\ell+2)}\|_{[0,1]}}{2}.

(5.5)

5.2.
Comparison of derivatives of Bernstein’s polynomials.

In the following result we compare on a compact subset
K
of an open subset
\Omega\subset(0,1)
the higher order derivatives of the corresponding Bernstein’s polynomials of degree
\nu
of two continuous functions on
[0,1]
that coincide on
\Omega
.

Lemma 5.1

(Comparison)
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous functions that coincide on an open set
\Omega\subset(0,1)
and let
\ell\geq 0
. Then for each compact set
K\subset\Omega
there exists a constant
M_{K,\ell}>0
(depending only on
K
and
\ell
) such that

|B_{\nu}(f_{1})^{(\ell)}(x)-B_{\nu}(f_{2})^{(\ell)}(x)|\leq\frac{M_{K,\ell}}{\nu^{2}}\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{[0,1]}

for each
x\in K
.

Proof.

Let
i,j,\ell\geq 0
be such that
2i+j\leq\ell
. By
[
DL
, Ch.4.Prop.4.4]
there exist polynomials
q_{ij\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x}]
that do not depend on
\nu,k
such that

\frac{d^{\ell}}{d{\tt x}^{\ell}}({\tt x}^{k}(1-{\tt x})^{\nu-k})={\tt x}^{k-\ell}(1-{\tt x})^{\nu-k-\ell}\sum_{2i+j\leq\ell}\nu^{i}(k-\nu{\tt x})^{j}q_{ij\ell}({\tt x}).

Write
f:=f_{1}-f_{2}
, which is identically
0
on
K
. Observe that

\begin{split}B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(f)&=\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}f\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}\Big)\binom{\nu}{k}\frac{d^{\ell}}{d{\tt x}^{\ell}}({\tt x}^{k}(1-{\tt x})^{\nu-k})\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}f\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}\Big)\binom{\nu}{k}{\tt x}^{k-\ell}(1-{\tt x})^{\nu-k-\ell}\sum_{2i+j\leq\ell}\nu^{i}(k-\nu{\tt x})^{j}q_{ij\ell}({\tt x})\\
&=\frac{1}{{\tt x}^{\ell}(1-{\tt x})^{\ell}}\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}f\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}\Big)B_{k,\nu}({\tt x})\sum_{2i+j\leq\ell}\nu^{i}(k-\nu{\tt x})^{j}q_{ij\ell}({\tt x})\\
&=\frac{1}{{\tt x}^{\ell}(1-{\tt x})^{\ell}}\sum_{2i+j\leq\ell}q_{ij\ell}({\tt x})\nu^{i+j}\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}f\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}\Big)\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}-{\tt x}\Big)^{j}B_{k,\nu}({\tt x}).\end{split}

(5.6)

Let
\delta:=\operatorname{dist}(K,[0,1]\setminus\Omega)>0
and observe that if
x\in K
and
|\frac{k}{\nu}-x|\leq\delta
, then
f(\frac{k}{\nu})=0
. By
[
DL
, Ch.10.§1.(1.6), pag. 304]
there exists a constant
C(\delta,i+j+2)
such that

\sum_{|\frac{k}{\nu}-x|>\delta}B_{k,\nu}(x)\leq C(\delta,i+j+2)\frac{1}{\nu^{i+j+2}}.

(5.7)

Thus, by (
5.6
), (
5.7
) and as
B_{\nu}(f_{1})^{(\ell)}(x)-B_{\nu}(f_{2})^{(\ell)}(x)=B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(f)(x)
and
|\frac{k}{\nu}-x|\leq 1
,

|B_{\nu}(f_{1})^{(\ell)}(x)-B_{\nu}(f_{2})^{(\ell)}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{x^{\ell}(1-x)^{\ell}}\sum_{2i+j\leq\ell}|q_{ij\ell}(x)|\nu^{i+j}\sum_{|\frac{k}{\nu}-x|>\delta}\Big|f\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}\Big)\Big|B_{k,\nu}(x)\\
\leq\Big(\frac{1}{x^{\ell}(1-x)^{\ell}}\sum_{2i+j\leq\ell}|q_{ij\ell}(x)|C(\delta,i+j+2)\Big)\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\|f\|_{[0,1]}

for each
x\in K
. Now, the statement follows readily.
∎

5.3.
Some bounds for derivatives of Taylor polynomials.

Let
f:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous function that is
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell}
on an open subset
\Omega\subset[0,1]
. Define

T^{\ell}f:\Omega\times[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}},\ (y,x)\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\frac{f^{(k)}(y)}{k!}(x-y)^{k}.

We have

\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial{\tt x}^{m}}T^{\ell}f=\sum_{k=m}^{\ell}\frac{f^{(k)}({\tt y})}{(k-m)!}({\tt x}-{\tt y})^{k-m}.

If
K\subset\Omega
is a compact set,

\displaystyle\|T^{\ell}f\|_{K\times[0,1]}:=\max\{T^{\ell}f(y,x):\ (y,x)\in K\times[0,1]\},

\displaystyle\|(T^{\ell}f)^{(m)}\|_{K\times[0,1]}:=\Big\|\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial{\tt x}^{m}}T^{\ell}f\Big\|_{K\times[0,1]}:=\max\Big\{\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial{\tt x}^{m}}T^{\ell}f(y,x):\ (y,x)\in K\times[0,1]\Big\}.

As the points
x,y\in[0,1]
, we deduce

\|(T^{\ell}f)^{(m)}\|_{K\times[0,1]}\leq\sum_{k=m}^{\ell}\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{K}}{(k-m)!}.

(5.8)

In particular,
\|(T^{\ell}f)^{(\ell)}\|_{K\times[0,1]}\leq\|f^{(\ell)}\|_{K}
.

5.4.
Proof of Theorem
2.9

The proof is conducted in several steps:

Step 1. Initial preparation.

Define

P:=T^{\ell+3}f:\Omega\times[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}},(y,x)\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{\ell+3}\frac{f^{(k)}(y)}{k!}(x-y)^{k}.

We claim: There exists a function
g:\Omega\times[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
h(y,x):=f(x)-P(y,x)=g(y,x)(x-y)^{\ell+4}
on
\Omega\times[0,1]
and for each compact set
K\subset\Omega
there exists a constant
N_{f,K}>0
such that
|g(y,x)|<N_{f,K}
for each
(y,x)\in K\times[0,1]
(see also Remark
5.2
).

Define

g:\Omega\times[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}},\ (y,x)\mapsto\begin{cases}\frac{h(y,x)}{(x-y)^{\ell+4}}&\text{if $x\neq y$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Observe that
g
is continuous on
(\Omega\times[0,1])\setminus\Delta
where
\Delta:=\{(x,x)\in\Omega\times[0,1],\ x\in\Omega\}
.

Fix a compact set
K\subset\Omega
. For each
x\in K
choose
\varepsilon_{x}>0
such that
[x-2\varepsilon_{x},x+2\varepsilon_{x}]\subset\Omega
. As
K
is a compact set, there exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in K
such that
K\subset K^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}[x_{j}-\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+\varepsilon_{x_{j}}]
. As
f^{(\ell+4)}
is continuous in
\Omega
, there exists a constant
N_{1,f,K^{\prime\prime}}>0
such that
|f^{(\ell+4)}(z)|\leq N_{1,f,K^{\prime\prime}}(\ell+4)!
for each
z\in K^{\prime\prime}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}[x_{j}-2\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+2\varepsilon_{x_{j}}]
. Define
L_{j}:=[0,1]\setminus(x_{j}-2\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+2\varepsilon_{x_{j}})
and observe that

K^{\prime}\times[0,1]=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}([x_{j}-\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+\varepsilon_{x_{j}}]\times[x_{j}-2\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+2\varepsilon_{x_{j}}])\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}([x_{j}-\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+\varepsilon_{x_{j}}]\times L_{j})

As
\Delta\cap(\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}[x_{j}-\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+\varepsilon_{x_{j}}]\times L_{j})=\varnothing
, the function
g
is continuous on the compact set
\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}[x_{j}-\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+\varepsilon_{x_{j}}]\times L_{j}
, so there exists
N_{2,f,K^{\prime}}>0
such that
|g(y,x)|<N_{2,f,K^{\prime}}
for each
(y,x)\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}[x_{j}-\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+\varepsilon_{x_{j}}]\times L_{j}
.

As
f
is
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell+4}
on
\Omega
, for each
(y,x)\in[x_{j}-\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+\varepsilon_{x_{j}}]\times[x_{j}-2\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+2\varepsilon_{x_{j}}]
there exists by Lagrange form of the remainder of Taylor’s theorem
\zeta_{(y,x)}\in[x_{j}-2\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+2\varepsilon_{x_{j}}]\subset K^{\prime\prime}
such that

h(y,x)=\frac{f^{(\ell+4)}(\zeta_{(y,x)})}{(\ell+4)!}(x-y)^{\ell+4},

so
g(y,x)=\frac{f^{(\ell+4)}(\zeta_{(y,x)})}{(\ell+4)!}
and
|g(y,x)|<N_{1,f,K^{\prime\prime}}
for each
(y,x)\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}[x_{j}-\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+\varepsilon_{x_{j}}]\times[x_{j}-2\varepsilon_{x_{j}},x_{j}+2\varepsilon_{x_{j}}]
. Thus, if we define
N_{f,K}:=\max\{N_{1,f,K^{\prime\prime}},N_{2,f,K^{\prime}}\}
, the claim is proved.

Define
P_{y}:=P(y,\cdot)
and
h_{y}:=h(y,\cdot)
and fix a compact set
K\subset\Omega
. Observe that
P_{y}^{(k)}(y)=f^{(k)}(y)
for each
y\in K
and each
k=0,\ldots,\ell+3
. We have
B_{\nu}(f)=B_{\nu}(P_{y})+B_{\nu}(h_{y})
. Consequently, for each
y\in K

B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(f)=B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(P_{y})+B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(h_{y})

(5.9)

(we are considering derivatives and Bernstein’s polynomials with respect to the variable
{\tt x}
).

Step 2. Uniform control of the error for the Taylor polynomials.

We claim: there exists a constant
C_{f,K,\ell}>0
such that
|B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(h_{y})(y)|<\frac{C_{f,K,\ell}}{\nu^{2}}
for each
y\in K
.

Let
i,j,\ell\geq 0
be such that
2i+j\leq\ell
. By
[
DL
, Ch.4.Prop.4.4]
there exist polynomials
q_{ij\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x}]
that do not depend on
\nu,k
such that

\frac{d^{\ell}}{d{\tt x}^{\ell}}({\tt x}^{k}(1-{\tt x})^{\nu-k})={\tt x}^{k-\ell}(1-{\tt x})^{\nu-k-\ell}\sum_{2i+j\leq\ell}\nu^{i}(k-\nu{\tt x})^{j}q_{ij\ell}({\tt x}).

As
h_{y}(\frac{k}{\nu})=g(y,\frac{k}{\nu})\cdot(\frac{k}{\nu}-y)^{\ell+4}
, we have

\begin{split}B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(h_{y})&=\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}h_{y}\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}\Big)\binom{\nu}{k}\frac{d^{\ell}}{d{\tt x}^{\ell}}({\tt x}^{k}(1-{\tt x})^{\nu-k})\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}h_{y}\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}\Big)\binom{\nu}{k}{\tt x}^{k-\ell}(1-{\tt x})^{\nu-k-\ell}\sum_{2i+j\leq\ell}\nu^{i}(k-\nu{\tt x})^{j}q_{ij\ell}({\tt x})\\
&=\frac{1}{{\tt x}^{\ell}(1-{\tt x})^{\ell}}\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}h_{y}\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}\Big)B_{k,\nu}({\tt x})\sum_{2i+j\leq\ell}\nu^{i+j}\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}-{\tt x}\Big)^{j}q_{ij\ell}({\tt x})\\
&=\frac{1}{{\tt x}^{\ell}(1-{\tt x})^{\ell}}\sum_{2i+j\leq\ell}q_{ij\ell}({\tt x})\nu^{i+j}\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}g\Big(y,\frac{k}{\nu}\Big)\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}-y\Big)^{\ell+4}\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}-{\tt x}\Big)^{j}B_{k,\nu}({\tt x}).\end{split}

We have proved above that there exists a constant
N_{f,K}>0
such that
|g(y,x)|<N_{f,K}
for each
(y,x)\in K\times[0,1]
. Recall that
2(i+j)\leq\ell+j
and
|\frac{k}{\nu}-y|\leq 1
. If we set
{\tt x}=y
, we have

\Big|\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}g\Big(y,\frac{k}{\nu}\Big)\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}-y\Big)^{\ell+j+4}B_{k,\nu}(y)\Big|\leq N_{f,K}\frac{1}{\nu^{2(i+j)+4}}\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}(k-\nu y)^{2(i+j)+4}B_{k,\nu}(y)\\
\leq N_{f,K}\frac{1}{\nu^{2(i+j)+4}}A_{i+j+2}\nu^{i+j+2}\leq N_{f,K}A_{i+j+2}\frac{1}{\nu^{i+j+2}}

(5.10)

for a constant
A_{i+j+2}>0
(see
[
DL
, Ch.10.§1.(1.5), pag. 304]
). Consequently,

|B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(h_{y})(y)|\leq\Big(\frac{1}{y^{\ell}(1-y)^{\ell}}\sum_{2i+j\leq\ell}|q_{ij\ell}(y)|N_{f,K}A_{i+j+2}\Big)\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}

and the claim follows if we take
C_{f,K,\ell}:=\|\frac{1}{{\tt y}^{\ell}(1-{\tt y})^{\ell}}\|_{K}\sum_{2i+j\leq\ell}\|q_{ij\ell}\|_{K}N_{f,K}A_{i+j+2}
.

Step 3. Proof of the first part of the statement.

If
x\in K
, we have using
Step 2
(because
P_{x}^{(k)}(x)=f^{(k)}(x)
for each
x\in K
and each
k=0,\ldots,\ell+3
)

|B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(f)(x)-f^{(\ell)}(x)|\leq|B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(P_{x})(x)-f^{(\ell)}(x)|+|B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(h_{x})(x)|\\
\leq|B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(P_{x})(x)-P_{x}^{(\ell)}(x)|+\frac{C_{f,K,\ell}}{\nu^{2}}.

(5.11)

By (
5.3
) and (
5.5
) applied to
P_{x}
we obtain

|B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(P_{x})(x)-P_{x}^{(\ell)}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}(\ell(\ell-1)\|P_{x}^{(\ell)}\|_{[0,1]}\\
+\ell|1-2x|\|P_{x}^{(\ell+1)}\|_{[0,1]}+x(1-x)\|P_{x}^{(\ell+2)}\|_{[0,1]}).

By (
5.8
) we deduce

|B_{\nu}^{(\ell)}(P_{x})(x)-P_{x}^{(\ell)}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}\Big(\ell(\ell-1)\sum_{k=\ell}^{\ell+3}\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{K}}{(k-\ell)!}\\
+\ell|1-2x|\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{\ell+3}\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{K}}{(k-\ell-1)!}+x(1-x)\sum_{k=\ell+2}^{\ell+3}\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{K}}{(k-\ell-2)!}\Big)

for each
x\in K
and the first part of the statement holds.

Step 4. Bound of the error.
For each
\varepsilon>0
and each pair of integers
s,t\geq 0
such that
\lambda:=s+t\leq\ell+2
there exists a constant
C_{f,K,\lambda,\varepsilon}>0
such that

\Big|B_{\nu,s,t}(P_{y})(x)-\frac{P_{y}^{(\lambda)}}{\lambda!}(x)\Big|<\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda!}+\frac{C_{f,K,\lambda,\varepsilon}}{\nu}

(5.12)

for each
(y,x)\in K\times[0,1]
and each
\nu>s
. In particular,
B_{\nu,s,t}(P_{y})
converges to
\frac{P_{y}^{(\lambda)}}{\lambda!}
uniformly on
K\times[0,1]
when
\nu\to\infty
.

We will follow the proof of
[
Fl
, Lem.2]
making the suitable needed changes. Fix integers
s,t\geq 0
and denote
\lambda:=s+t
. Next fix
\nu\geq s
and
0\leq k\leq\nu-s
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and let
\delta>0
be such that if
(y,x),(y^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in K\times[0,1]
satisfy
|x-x^{\prime}|<\delta
and
|y-y^{\prime}|<\delta
, then
|P_{y}^{(\lambda)}(x)-P_{y^{\prime}}^{(\lambda)}(x^{\prime})|<\varepsilon
(recall that
P_{z}
is
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ell+4}
on
[0,1]
for each
z\in K
). Fix
x\in[0,1]
and let

I_{\nu}:=\Big\{k\in\{0,\ldots,\nu-s\}:\ x-\delta<\frac{k}{\nu}<\frac{k+s}{\nu}<x+\delta\Big\}.

Fix
y\in K
and pick
\xi_{k}
in the smallest interval that contains the points
x,\frac{k}{\nu},\ldots,\frac{k+s}{\nu}
such that

\Big[\frac{k}{\nu},\ldots,\frac{k+s}{\nu},\underbrace{x,\ldots,x}_{\text{$t$ times}}\Big]P_{y}=\frac{P_{y}^{(\lambda)}(\xi_{k})}{\lambda!}.

Consequently,

B_{\nu,s,t}(P_{y})(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\nu-s}\Big(\Big[\frac{k}{\nu},\ldots,\frac{k+s}{\nu},\underbrace{x,\ldots,x}_{\text{$t$ times}}\Big]P_{y}\Big)B_{k,\nu-s}(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda!}\sum_{k=0}^{\nu-s}P_{y}^{(\lambda)}(\xi_{k})B_{k,\nu-s}(x).

Define

S_{\nu}:=\lambda!B_{\nu,s,t}(P_{y})(x)-P_{y}^{(\lambda)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\nu-s}(P_{y}^{(\lambda)}(\xi_{k})-P_{y}^{(\lambda)}(x))B_{k,\nu-s}(x).

Write
S_{\nu}=C_{\nu}+D_{\nu}
where

\displaystyle C_{\nu}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k\in I_{\nu}}(P_{y}^{(\lambda)}(\xi_{k})-P_{y}^{(\lambda)}(x))B_{k,\nu-s}(x),

\displaystyle D_{\nu}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k\not\in I_{\nu}}(P_{y}^{(\lambda)}(\xi_{k})-P_{y}^{(\lambda)}(x))B_{k,\nu-s}(x).

If
k\in I_{\nu}
, we have
|\xi_{k}-x|<\delta
, so

|C_{\nu}|\leq\sum_{k\in I_{\nu}}\varepsilon B_{k,\nu-s}(x)\leq\varepsilon.

Regarding
D_{\nu}
, define

M_{f,K,\lambda}:=\max\Big\{\Big|\frac{\partial^{\lambda}P}{\partial{\tt x}^{\lambda}}(y,x)\Big|:\ (y,x)\in K\times[0,1]\Big\}

(5.13)

for
\lambda=0,\ldots,\ell+2
. If
0\leq k\leq\nu-s
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{k}{\nu}-x\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\frac{k}{\nu-s}-x\Big|+\Big|\frac{k}{\nu}-\frac{k}{\nu-s}\Big|\leq\Big|\frac{k}{\nu-s}-x\Big|+\frac{s}{\nu}\frac{k}{\nu-s}\leq\Big|\frac{k}{\nu-s}-x\Big|+\frac{s}{\nu},

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{k+s}{\nu}-x\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\frac{k}{\nu-s}-x\Big|+\Big|\frac{k+s}{\nu}-\frac{k}{\nu-s}\Big|\leq\Big|\frac{k}{\nu-s}-x\Big|+\frac{s}{\nu}\Big(1-\frac{k}{\nu-s}\Big)\leq\Big|\frac{k}{\nu-s}-x\Big|+\frac{s}{\nu}.

Consequently,

\max\Big\{\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}-x\Big)^{2},\Big(\frac{k+s}{\nu}-x\Big)^{2}\Big\}\leq\Big(\frac{k}{\nu-s}-x\Big)^{2}+2\frac{s}{\nu}+\frac{s^{2}}{\nu^{2}}.

For each
k\not\in I_{\nu}
we have

\delta^{2}\leq\max\Big\{\Big(\frac{k}{\nu}-x\Big)^{2},\Big(\frac{k+s}{\nu}-x\Big)^{2}\Big\}\leq\Big(\frac{k}{\nu-s}-x\Big)^{2}+2\frac{s}{\nu}+\frac{s^{2}}{\nu^{2}}.

We deduce
1\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}((\frac{k}{\nu-s}-x)^{2}+2\frac{s}{\nu}+\frac{s^{2}}{\nu^{2}})
and as
|P_{y}^{(\lambda)}(\xi_{k})-P_{y}^{(\lambda)}(x)|\leq 2M_{f,K,\lambda}
, we conclude using §
2.4.1

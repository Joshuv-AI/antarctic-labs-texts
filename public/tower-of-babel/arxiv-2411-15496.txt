Legendre transformations of a class of generalized Frobenius manifolds and the associated integrable hierarchies
Si-Qi Liu, Haonan Qu, Youjin Zhang

Abstract
For two generalized Frobenius manifolds related by a Legendre-type transformation, we show that the associated integrable hierarchies of hydrodynamic type, which are called the Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchies, are related by a certain linear reciprocal transformation; we also show, under the semisimplicity condition, that the topological deformations of these Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchies are related by the same linear reciprocal transformation.



Keywords.
Frobenius manifold; Principal Hierarchy; Legendre transformation; WDVV equation;
Tau structure; Virasoro symmetry; Loop equation

1
Introduction

Since Dubrovin introduced the notion of Frobenius manifold in the early1990s
[
10
,
11
]
, this geometric structure has been playing important roles in different research subjects of mathematical physics,
such as Gromov-Witten theory,
singularity theory and integrable systems, see for example

[
3
,
5
,
15
,
17
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
25
,
28
,
33
,
41
]
and references therein.
In applications of Frobenius manifold to these research subjects
some generalizations of this geometric structure arise
[
1
,
2
,
19
,
26
,
30
,
31
,
38
]
. Among such generalizations we have the so called generalized Frobenius manifolds with non-flat unity
[
30
,
31
]
,
which satisfy all the axioms of Dubrovin’s definition of Frobenius manifold but the flatness condition imposed on the unit vector fields.
In this paper, we refer to them simply as
generalized Frobenius manifolds
. Examples of such generalized Frobenius manifolds can be found in
[
4
,
9
,
30
,
40
]
.

It is shown in
[
30
]
that one can associate with any generalized Frobenius manifold a bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy of hydrodynamic type, which is called the Principal Hierarchy of the generalized Frobenius manifold and is an analogue of the one for a usual Frobenius manifold
[
15
]
.
Such an integrable hierarchy possesses a tau structure, and the tau-cover of the Principal Hierarchy admits a family of Virasoro symmetries.
The condition of linearization of actions of the Virasoro symmetries on the tau function of the Principal Hierarchy leads to the loop equation of the generalized Frobenius manifold, which is shown to have a unique solution under the assumption of semisimplicity
[
30
]
. The solution of the loop equation yields a quasi-Miura transformation which transforms the Principal Hierarchy of the semisimple generalized Frobenius manifold to its topological deformation.
In
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
the topological deformations of the Principal Hierarchies of two particular examples of generalized Frobenius manifolds are studied, it turns out that they contain the Volterra hierarchy, the
q
-deformed KdV hierarchy and the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy, which are discrete integrable hierarchies well known in the theory of soliton equation.

In the present paper, we study the relationship between the integrable hierarchies associated with two generalized Frobenius manifolds which are related by a Legendre-type transformation. Such a kind of transformations for Frobenius manifolds were originally introduced by Dubrovin in
[
10
]
based on symmetries of the WDVV equations of associativity. Each Legendre-type transformation is determined by an invertible flat vector field of the Frobenius manifold that is under consideration, and it transforms the given Frobenius manifold to another one.
As it is shown in
[
45
]
, these two Frobenius manifolds
share the same monodromy data, and their Principal Hierarchies
are related by a certain linear reciprocal transformation.
Moreover, if these two Frobenius manifolds are semisimple,
the topological deformations of their Principal Hierarchies are also related by the same linear reciprocal transformation, and share the same deformed tau structure. In
[
42
]
, Strachan and Stedman generalized Dubrovin’s Legendre-type transformations of Frobenius manifolds. Such a generalized Legendre-type transformation is determined by an invertible vector field, which satisfies a certain set of defining conditions and is called a Legendre field. In general, a Legendre field is
non-flat with respect to the flat metric of the Frobenius manifold under consideration, and the associated Legendre-type transformation transforms the Frobenius manifold to a generalized one with non-flat unity,
moreover, one can apply such a transformation to any generalized Frobenius manifold.

In order to establish a relationship between the integrable hierarchies associated with two generalized Frobenius manifold that are related by a generalized Legendre-type transformation (which we will simply call a Legendre-type transformation), we need to extend the Principal Hierarchies of the generalized Frobenius manifolds by some flows that
are constructed from the Legendre field, and we call these extended integrable hierarchies the Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchies of the generalized Frobenius manifolds. We show that these Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchies of the generalized Frobenius manifolds are related by a linear reciprocal transformation, and they also possess tau-covers and Virasoro symmetries. We also show that the corresponding linearization conditions of the actions of the Virasoro symmetries on the tau function of the Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchies lead to the same loop equations that are derived in
[
31
]
. In the case when the generalized Frobenius manifolds are semisimple, we show that the topological deformations of their Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchies are also related by the same linear reciprocal transformation.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Sect. 2, we recall the notion and main properties of Legendre-type transformations introduced in
[
42
]
.
In Sect. 3, we construct the Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchy and its tau structures for a generalized Frobenius manifold,
and establish the relationship of the Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchies of two generalized Frobenius manifolds that are related by a Legendre-type transformation.
In Sect. 4, we study the Virasoro symmetries and the topological deformations of the Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchies,
and establish the relationships between these integrable hierarchies
under the semisimplicity condition.
In Sect. 5 and Sect. 6, we present two important examples,
one relates the KdV hierarchy with the
q
-deformed KdV hierarchy,
while the other one relates the Toda hierarchy with the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy.
In Sect. 7, we give some concluding remarks.

2
The generalized Legendre transformations

2.1
Legendre fields

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold endowed with a generalized Frobenius manifold (GFM) structure
(\eta,c,e)
, where
\eta
is a flat metric on
M
,
c\colon TM\times TM\to TM
is a tensor field of
(1,2)
-type,
e
is a vector field on
M
, and the triple
(\eta,c,e)
is required to satisfy the following conditions:

1.

For each
p\in M
,
(\eta,c,e)
yields a Frobenius algebra structure on
T_{p}M
with unity
e(p)
.

2.

Denote by
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection of
\eta
, and by
\tilde{c}
the
(0,3)
-tensor defined by
\tilde{c}(X,Y,Z)=\langle X\cdot Y,Z\rangle
, then
\nabla\tilde{c}
is a symmetric 4-tensor.
Here we use the short notations

X\cdot Y:=c(X,Y),\quad\langle X,Y\rangle:=\eta(X,Y),\quad X,Y\in\Vect(M).

Note that the flatness condition
\nabla e=0
imposed on the unit vector field
e
and the existence of an Euler vector field in Dubrovin’s definition of a Frobenius manifold
[
10
]
are not assumed here, and this notion of generalized Frobenius manifold
coincides with the one given in
[
31
]
except for the existence of an Euler vector field
E
.

In a system of flat coordinates
\{v^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}

of the metric
\eta
in a neighborhood of a point
p\in M

we have

\eta=\eta_{\alpha\beta}\td v^{\alpha}\otimes\td v^{\beta},\qquad c=c^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}(v)\,\partial_{\gamma}\otimes\td v^{\alpha}\otimes\td v^{\beta},

here and in what follows summation over repeated upper and lower Greek indices with range from
1
to
n
is assumed,
and
\partial_{\gamma}=\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\gamma}}
.
Then the symmetry property of the tensor field
\nabla\tilde{c}
implies that there exists locally a function
F(v)
such that

\eta_{\alpha\xi}c^{\xi}_{\beta\gamma}(v)=\frac{\partial^{3}F(v)}{\partial v^{\alpha}\partial v^{\beta}\partial v^{\gamma}},\quad\alpha,\beta,\gamma=1,\dots,n,

(2.1)

and the associativity of the Frobenius algebra is equivalent to the fact that
F(v)
satisfies
the WDVV equations

\frac{\partial^{3}F(v)}{\partial v^{\alpha}\partial v^{\beta}\partial v^{\lambda}}\eta^{\lambda\mu}\frac{\partial^{3}F(v)}{\partial v^{\mu}\partial v^{\gamma}\partial v^{\delta}}=\frac{\partial^{3}F(v)}{\partial v^{\delta}\partial v^{\beta}\partial v^{\lambda}}\eta^{\lambda\mu}\frac{\partial^{3}F(v)}{\partial v^{\mu}\partial v^{\gamma}\partial v^{\alpha}},

(2.2)

for
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta=1,\dots,n
.
As it is shown in
[
10
]
, the WDVV equations possess two types of symmetries. The first type of symmetries is given by
Legendre-type transformations

(see also in
[
13
,
15
,
18
]
),
which is generalized in
[
42
]
.
Another type of symmetries consists of
inversion symmetries

(see also in
[
34
]
),
which will not be considered in the present paper.

Definition 2.1

(
[
42
]
)
.

Let
M(\eta,c,e)
be a generalized Frobenius manifold.
A solution
B\in\Vect(M)
to the equation

X\cdot\nabla_{Y}B=Y\cdot\nabla_{X}B,\quad\forall\,X,Y\in\Vect(M)

(2.3)

is called a Legendre field.

Taking
Y=e
in (
2.3
), we arrive at

\nabla_{X}B=X\cdot\nabla_{e}B,\quad\forall X\in\Vect(M)

(2.4)

for any Legendre field
B
.
In the flat coordinates
v^{1},\dots,v^{n}
, the equation (
2.3
) can be rewritten as

c_{\alpha\gamma}^{\delta}\partial_{\beta}B^{\gamma}=c_{\beta\gamma}^{\delta}\partial_{\alpha}B^{\gamma},\quad\forall\alpha,\beta,\delta=1,\dots,n

(2.5)

for a vector field
B=B^{\gamma}\partial_{\gamma}
. These equations are also equivalent to

\partial_{\alpha}(B^{\gamma}c_{\beta\gamma}^{\delta})=\partial_{\beta}(B^{\gamma}c_{\alpha\gamma}^{\delta}).

(2.6)

From the above definition and the equations (
2.6
)
it follows that we have the following two examples of Legendre fields:

1.

If
B\in\Vect(M)
satisfies the condition
\nabla B=0
, then
B
is a Legendre field. We call such a Legendre field a flat Legendre field.

2.

The unit vector field
e=e^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}
is a Legendre field.

Remark 2.2
.

Multiplying
e_{\delta}:=\eta_{\alpha\delta}e^{\alpha}
on both sides of (
2.5
), we obtain

\eta_{\alpha\gamma}\partial_{\beta}B^{\gamma}=\eta_{\beta\gamma}\partial_{\alpha}B^{\beta},

which implies that the Legendre field
B
is locally a gradient field,
i.e., there locally exists a function
\varphi
on
M
such that

B=\grad_{\eta}\varphi:=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\eta^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\beta}}.

(2.7)

In particular, the unity
e
is locally a gradient field (see also in
[
31
]
).

For any vector field
B
on
M(\eta,c,e)
, introduce a new
(0,2)
-tensor
\hat{\eta}=\langle\ ,\,\rangle_{B}
by

\langle X,Y\rangle_{B}:=\langle B\cdot X,B\cdot Y\rangle,\quad X,Y\in\Vect(M).

(2.8)

We say
B
is
invertible
, if there exists a vector field
B^{-1}
such that
B\cdot B^{-1}=e
.
Note that if
B
is invertible, then
\langle\ ,\,\rangle_{B}
is non-degenerate.

Proposition 2.3

(Generalized Legendre transformation
[
42
]
)
.

Suppose
B=B^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}
be an invertible Legendre field on a generalized Frobenius manifold
M(\eta,c,e)
, then the following statements hold true:

1.

M(\hat{\eta},c,e)
is also a generalized Frobenius manifold,
and the Levi-Civita connection
\hat{\nabla}
with respect to the new metric
\hat{\eta}=\langle\ ,\,\rangle_{B}
satisfies the relation

\hat{\nabla}_{X}Y=B^{-1}\cdot\nabla_{X}(B\cdot Y),\quad\forall X,Y\in\Vect(M).

(2.9)

2.

A system of flat coordinates
\{\hat{v}^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}
with respect to the new metric
\hat{\eta}

can be chosen as a solution to the equations

\frac{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}{\partial v^{\beta}}=B^{\gamma}(v)c^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}(v),\quad\alpha,\beta=1,\dots,n.

(2.10)

Moreover, we have

\hat{\eta}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}\td\hat{v}^{\alpha}\otimes\td\hat{v}^{\beta},\quad e=B^{\alpha}(v)\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}},\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}=B(v)\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}.

(2.11)

3.

There locally exists a function
\hat{F}=\hat{F}(\hat{v})
on
M
such that

\frac{\partial^{3}\hat{F}}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}\partial\hat{v}^{\beta}\partial\hat{v}^{\gamma}}=\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\beta}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\gamma}}\right\rangle_{B},\quad\alpha,\beta,\gamma=1,\dots,n,

and such
\hat{F}(\hat{v})
can be chosen as a solution to the equation

\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{F}(\hat{v})}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}\partial\hat{v}^{\beta}}=\frac{\partial^{2}F(v)}{\partial v^{\alpha}\partial v^{\beta}},\quad\alpha,\beta=1,\dots,n,

(2.12)

where
F(v)
is defined as in (
2.1
).

4.

B^{-1}
is a Legendre field on
M(\eta,c,e)
,
which transforms
\hat{\eta}
back to
\eta
.

Note that the difference between
M(\eta,c,e)
and
M(\hat{\eta},c,e)

lies only in their metrics
\eta
and
\hat{\eta}
, and
they share the same Frobenius multiplication
c
and the unit vector field
e
.

2.2
Quasi-homogeneity

We say that the generalized Frobenius manifold
M(\eta,c,e)
is
quasi-homogeneous
,
if there exists a vector field
E=E^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}
and a constant
d\in\mathbb{C}
, called the
Euler vector field
and the
charge
of
M
respectively, such that
\nabla\nabla E=0
, and
The Lie derivatives of
c
and
\eta
along
E
satisfy the relations

\mathcal{L}_{E}c=c,\quad\mathcal{L}_{E}\eta=(2-d)\eta.

(2.13)

In other words,
M(\eta,c,e,E)
is a generalized Frobenius manifold of charge
d
in the sense of
[
31
]
.
We assume in what follows that the Euler vector field
E
is diagonalizable, so that we can choose a system of flat coordinates
\{v^{\alpha}\}
of
\eta
such that

E=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\left[\left(\frac{2-d}{2}-\mu_{\alpha}\right)v^{\alpha}+r^{\alpha}\right]\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}},

(2.14)

where
\mu:=\diag(\mu_{1},...,\mu_{n})
is a part of the monodromy data of
M
at
z=0

[
10
,
12
,
15
,
31
]
, and
the constants
r^{\alpha}\neq 0
only if
\mu_{\alpha}+\frac{d}{2}=1
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
13
]
)
.

Suppose
M(\eta,c,e,E)
is a generalized Frobenius manifold of charge
d
, a Legendre field
B\in\Vect(M)
is called quasi-homogeneous if

[E,B]=\left(\mu_{B}-\frac{2-d}{2}\right)B

(2.15)

for a certain constant
\mu_{B}\in\mathbb{C}
.

We note that a flat Legendre field
B=\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}
is quasi-homogeneous,
since

\Bigl[E,\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\Bigr]=\left(\mu_{\alpha}-\frac{2-d}{2}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}},

and the constant
\mu_{B}
coincides with
\mu_{\alpha}
.
The unit vector field
e
is also quasi-homogeneous,
because of the identity
[E,e]=-e
(see
[
31
]
),
and
\mu_{e}=-\frac{d}{2}
which
is also denoted by
\mu_{0}
in
[
31
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose an invertible vector field
B
on
M(\eta,c,e,E)
satisfies the relation (
2.15
),
then its inverse
\hat{B}:=B^{-1}
satisfies

\left[E,\hat{B}\right]=-\left(\mu_{B}+\frac{2+d}{2}\right)\hat{B}.

(2.16)

Proof.

Using the identity
[E,e]=-e
and (
2.13
), we have

\displaystyle-e

\displaystyle=\,[E,e]=\mathcal{L}_{E}\left(B\cdot\hat{B}\right)

\displaystyle=\,(\mathcal{L}_{E}B)\cdot\hat{B}+(\mathcal{L}_{E}c)(B,\hat{B})+B\cdot(\mathcal{L}_{E}\hat{B})

\displaystyle=\,\left(\mu_{B}-\frac{2-d}{2}\right)e+e+B\cdot[E,\hat{B}],

from which it follows (
2.16
). The lemma is proved.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose
B
is an invertible quasi-homogeneous Legendre field on the generalized Frobenius manifold
M(\eta,c,e,E)
of charge
d
,
then
M(\hat{\eta},c,e,E)
is a generalized Frobenius manifold of charge

\hat{d}=-2\mu_{B}.

(2.17)

Proof.

By using Lemma 2.7 of
[
42
]
we have

\mathcal{L}_{E}\hat{\eta}=(2-\hat{d})\hat{\eta}.

Therefore, we only need to verify that

E
has local form (
2.14
) in a certain system of flat coordinates
\{\hat{v}^{\alpha}\}
of
\hat{\eta}
.
Let
\{\hat{v}^{\alpha}\}
be the system of coordinate defined by (
2.10
), then from (
2.11
), (
2.13
) and (
2.16
) it follows that

\displaystyle\,\left[E,\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}\right]=\mathcal{L}_{E}\left(B^{-1}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\left[E,B^{-1}\right]\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}+(\mathcal{L}_{E}c)\left(B^{-1},\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\right)+B^{-1}\cdot\left[E,\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(\mu_{\alpha}-\mu_{B}-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}=\left(\mu_{\alpha}-\frac{2-\hat{d}}{2}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}.

On the other hand, in the coordinate
\hat{v}^{1},\dots,\hat{v}^{n}
the Euler vector field

E=\hat{E}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}
satisfies the relation

\left[E,\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}\right]=-\frac{\partial\hat{E}^{\beta}}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\beta}},

therefore we have

\frac{\partial\hat{E}^{\beta}}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}=\left(\frac{2-\hat{d}}{2}-\mu_{\alpha}\right)\delta^{\beta}_{\alpha},

thus

E=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\left[\left(\frac{2-\hat{d}}{2}-\hat{\mu}_{\alpha}\right)\hat{v}^{\alpha}+\hat{r}^{\alpha}\right]\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}

for certain constants
\hat{r}^{\alpha}
, where
\hat{\mu}_{\alpha}:=\mu_{\alpha}
.
If
\hat{\mu}_{\alpha}+\frac{\hat{d}}{2}\neq 1
,
we can kill
\hat{r}^{\alpha}
by a certain coordinate translation
\hat{v}^{\alpha}\mapsto\hat{v}^{\alpha}+\text{const}
. The proposition is proved.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

The relation (
2.16
) can be rewritten as

[E,\hat{B}]=\left(\hat{\mu}_{\hat{B}}-\frac{2-\hat{d}}{2}\right)\hat{B}

(2.18)

in terms of the new charge
\hat{d}
given in (
2.17
)
and
\hat{\mu}_{\hat{B}}:=-\frac{d}{2}
.

3
The Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchy

Given a family of Legendre fields
\mathcal{B}=\left\{B_{j}\middle|j\in\mathcal{J}\right\}
, we are to show in this section that one can construct from it a hierarchy of pairwise commuting flows, and that the the Principal Hierarchy
[
15
,
31
]
of a generalized Frobenius manifold can be extended by using such flows.

3.1
The Legendre flows and the Principal Hierarchy

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose
B=B^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}
is a Legendre field
(not necessarily invertible, quasi-homogeneous) of
M(\eta,c,e)
,
then the Legendre flow
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{B}}
generated by
B
is defined as

\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{B}}=B^{\gamma}c^{\alpha}_{\gamma\beta}v^{\beta}_{x},\quad\alpha=1,\dots,n.

(3.1)

We note that the relation (
2.6
) implies that there locally exists functions
\{v_{B}^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}
such that

B^{\gamma}c^{\alpha}_{\gamma\beta}=\partial_{\beta}v_{B}^{\alpha}.

(3.2)

If
B
is invertible, then
v^{\alpha}_{B}
coincides with the coordinate

\hat{v}^{\alpha}
that is defined in (
2.10
) up to the addition of a constant.
In terms of the notation of
v^{\alpha}_{B}
, the Legendre flow (
3.1
) can be rewritten as

\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{B}}=(v^{\alpha}_{B})_{x}.

(3.3)

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be any two given Legendre fields of
M(\eta,c,e)
,
then the associated Legendre flows are commutative, i.e.,

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{B_{1}}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{B_{2}}}\right]=0
.

Proof.

From (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{B_{1}}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{B_{2}}}v^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{B_{1}}}\left(\partial_{x}v^{\alpha}_{B_{2}}\right)=\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\partial v_{B_{2}}^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{B_{1}}}\right)=\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\partial v_{B_{2}}^{\alpha}}{\partial v^{\beta}}\frac{\partial v^{\beta}}{\partial t^{B_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\,\partial_{x}\left(B_{2}^{\gamma}c^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}B_{1}^{\xi}c^{\beta}_{\xi\sigma}v^{\sigma}_{x}\right)=\partial_{x}\left(B_{2}\cdot(B_{1}\cdot v_{x})\right)^{\alpha},

where
v_{x}:=v^{\alpha}_{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}
.
Therefore the commutativity and associativity of the Frobenius multiplication
c
imply that

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{B_{1}}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{B_{2}}}\right]v^{\alpha}=0
, the lemma is proved.
∎

As a consequence, a family of Legendre fields
\left\{B_{j}\middle|j\in\mathcal{J}\right\}

yields a hierarchy of mutually commutative flows
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{B_{j}}}\middle|j\in\mathcal{J}\right\}
.
An important example of such integrable hierarchies is the Principal Hierarchy (see details in
[
31
]
)

\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{i,p}}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\partial\theta_{i,p+1}}{\partial v^{\beta}}\right),\quad(i,p)\in\mathcal{I},

(3.4)

of a generalized Frobenius manifold

M(\eta,c,e,E)
,
where the index set

\mathcal{I}:=\Big(\{1,2,...,n\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Big)\cup\Big(\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}\Big).

(3.5)

The functions
\{\theta_{i,p}\}_{(i,p)\in\mathcal{I}}
on
M
satisfy the relations

\displaystyle\theta_{\alpha,0}=v_{\alpha}:=\eta_{\alpha\beta}v^{\beta},\quad\grad_{\eta}\theta_{0,0}=e,\quad\alpha=1,\dots,n,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\theta_{\alpha,p}=\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{k}\left\langle\grad_{\eta}\theta_{0,k+1},\grad_{\eta}\theta_{\alpha,p-k}\right\rangle,\quad\alpha=1,\dots,n,\,p\geq 0,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\theta_{i,p+1}=c^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\gamma}\theta_{i,p},\quad\alpha,\beta=1,\dots,n,\,(i,p)\in\mathcal{I}.

(3.8)

These functions also satisfy the following quasi-homogeneous conditions

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{E}\theta_{\alpha,p}

\displaystyle=\,\left(p+\mu_{\alpha}+\frac{2-d}{2}\right)\theta_{\alpha,p}+\sum_{s=1}^{p}\theta_{\varepsilon,p-s}(R_{s})^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha}+(-1)^{p}r^{\varepsilon}_{p+1}\eta_{\varepsilon\alpha},

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{E}\theta_{0,q}

\displaystyle=\,\left(q+1-d\right)\theta_{0,q}+\sum_{s=1}^{q}\theta_{\varepsilon,q-s}r^{\varepsilon}_{s}+a_{00;q+1}

(3.10)

for
1\leq\alpha\leq n
,
p\geq 0
and
q\in\mathbb{Z}
,
where
d
is the charge of the generalized Frobenius manifold, and

\left\{(R_{s})^{\alpha}_{\beta},\,r_{s}^{\alpha},\,a_{00;q}\middle|1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n,\,s\geq 1,\,q\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

(3.11)

is a certain family of constants which satisfy the relations

\displaystyle R_{s}^{{\mathrm{T}}}\eta=(-1)^{s+1}\eta R_{s},\quad[\mu,R_{s}]=sR_{s},

(3.12)

\displaystyle r_{s}^{\alpha}\neq 0\quad\text{only if}\ \mu_{\alpha}+\frac{d}{2}=s,

(3.13)

3.2
The Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchy

Given a Legendre field of a generalized Frobenius manifold, we are to construct in this subsection a family of Legendre fields and extend the Principal Hierarchy by adding to it the associated Legendre flows.
To this end, we first present
the following lemma which is a generalized version of Proposition 2.6 given in
[

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
B
is a Legendre field on a generalized Frobenius manifold
M(\eta,c,e)
, then we have

1.

\nabla_{e}B
is also a Legendre field.

2.

There locally exists a Legendre field
B_{+}
satisfying the relation

\nabla_{X}B_{+}=X\cdot B,\quad\forall X\in\Vect(M).

(3.25)

Proof.

We note that the
(1,3)
-tensor
\nabla c
has the symmetry property

(\nabla c)(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})=(\nabla c)(X_{\sigma(1)},X_{\sigma(2)},X_{\sigma(3)}),\quad\forall X_{i}\in\Vect(M),\,\forall\sigma\in S_{3},

where
(\nabla c)(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})=\nabla_{X_{1}}c(X_{2},X_{3})
. In particular,

\displaystyle(\nabla_{e}c)(X,Y)=\,(\nabla_{X}c)(e,Y)=\nabla_{X}(e\cdot Y)-(\nabla_{X}e)\cdot Y-e\cdot\nabla_{X}Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}Y-(\nabla_{X}e)\cdot Y-\nabla_{X}Y=-(\nabla_{X}e)\cdot Y

(3.26)

holds true for all
X,Y\in\Vect(M)
.
Thus, for a given Legendre field
B
, by using the flatness of the Levi-Civita connection

\nabla
of
\eta

and by using (
2.4
), (
3.26
), we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{e}B=\nabla_{e}\nabla_{Y}B+\nabla_{[Y,e]}B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{e}(Y\cdot\nabla_{e}B)+[Y,e]\cdot\nabla_{e}B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{e}Y\cdot\nabla_{e}B+(\nabla_{e}c)(Y,\nabla_{e}B)+Y\cdot\nabla_{e}\nabla_{e}B+[Y,e]\cdot\nabla_{e}B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{e}Y-\nabla_{Y}e+[Y,e]\right)\cdot\nabla_{e}B+Y\cdot\nabla_{e}\nabla_{e}B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y\cdot\nabla_{e}\nabla_{e}B,\quad\forall Y\in\Vect(M).

Therefore we have

X\cdot\nabla_{Y}(\nabla_{e}B)=X\cdot Y\cdot\nabla_{e}\nabla_{e}B=Y\cdot\nabla_{X}(\nabla_{e}B),

which implies that
\nabla_{e}B
is a Legendre field.

Suppose
B_{+}=v_{B}^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}
is a solution to the system of equations (
3.2
),
then we have

X\cdot\nabla_{e}B_{+}=X\cdot(e\cdot B)=X\cdot B=\nabla_{X}B_{+},

so
B_{+}
is also a Legendre field.
The lemma is proved.
∎

Taking
X=e
in (
3.25
), we obtain

\nabla_{e}B_{+}=B

for the solution
B_{+}
to (
3.25
).
We also note that such a

is not unique, since for an arbitrary flat vector field
X
(i.e.
\nabla X=0
,
or locally

for constants

is also a solution to (
3.25
).

Let
B
be a Legendre field of

) for the new metric
\hat{\eta}
, it is clear that

\grad_{\hat{\eta}}\varphi=\hat{B}^{2}\cdot\grad_{\eta}\varphi

for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)

therefore from (
3.43
) and (
3.52

\displaystyle\grad_{\hat{\eta}}\hat{\Omega}_{i,p;j,q}

\displaystyle=\,\hat{B}^{2}\cdot\grad_{\eta}\Omega_{\sigma(i),p;\sigma(j),q}=(\hat{B}\cdot\xi_{\sigma(i),p})\cdot(\hat{B}\cdot\xi_{\sigma(j),q})=\hat{\xi}_{i,p}\cdot\hat{\xi}_{j,q},

i.e., the functions
\hat{\Omega}_{i,p;j,q}
indeed satisfy the
\hat{M}
-analogue of (
3.52
).
The remainder of this theorem can be verified through simple and straightforward calculations, so we omit the details here. The theorem is proved.

Theorem 4.4
.

The above-mentioned flows
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{(B)}_{m}}\right\}_{m\geq-1}
satisfy
the commutation relations

\Big[\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{(B)}_{m}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}\Big]=0

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one for the Virasoro symmetries of the tau-cover of the usual Principal Hierarchy of a generalized Frobenius manifold given in
[
31
]
.
From definitions (
4.27
)–(
4.29
) we see that

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{(B)}_{m}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}\right]f=0
hold true trivially;
and if
\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{(B)}_{m}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}\right]f_{J}=0
hold true,
then it is easy to verify the validity of
\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{(B)}_{m}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}\right]v^{\alpha}=0
.
Therefore we only need to verify the identities

\Big[\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{(B)}_{m}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}\Big]f_{J}=0,\quad I,J\in\{0,B;1,2,...,n\}\times\mathbb{Z}.

(4.32)

By using (
3.75
),
(
4.27
)–(
4.29
) and (
4.30
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}^{[B]}}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}\right)f_{J}=\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}^{(B)}}\Omega_{IJ}=(\partial_{\alpha}\Omega_{IJ})\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial s_{m}^{(B)}}

\displaystyle\qquad=(\partial_{\alpha}\Omega_{IJ})\left((E^{m+1})^{\alpha}+\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{K}}(2a_{m}^{(B);KL}f_{L}+b_{m;L}^{(B);K}t^{L})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{L}_{E^{m+1}}\Omega_{IJ}+\frac{\partial\Omega_{IJ}}{\partial t^{K}}\left(2a_{m}^{(B);KL}f_{L}+b_{m;L}^{(B);K}t^{L}\right),

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}^{[B]}}\right)f_{J}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}\left((2a_{m}^{(B);KL}\Omega_{JK}+b^{(B);L}_{m;J})f_{L}+(b^{(B);K}_{m;L}\Omega_{JK}+2c^{(B)}_{m;JL})t^{L}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=2a_{m}^{(B);KL}\frac{\partial\Omega_{JK}}{\partial t^{I}}f_{L}+\left(2a_{m}^{(B);KL}\Omega_{JK}+b_{m;J}^{(B);L}\right)\Omega_{IL}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+b_{m;L}^{(B);K}\frac{\partial\Omega_{JK}}{\partial t^{I}}t^{L}+b_{m;I}^{(B);K}\Omega_{JK}+2c_{m;IJ}^{(B)}.

We also note that
\frac{\partial\Omega_{IJ}}{\partial t^{K}}=\frac{\partial^{3}f}{\partial t^{I}\partial t^{J}\partial t^{K}}=\frac{\partial\Omega_{JK}}{\partial t^{I}}
, therefore (
4.32
) is equivalent to

\mathcal{L}_{E^{m+1}}\Omega_{IJ}=2a_{m}^{(B);KL}\Omega_{IK}\Omega_{LJ}+\left(b^{(B);K}_{m;I}\Omega_{KJ}+b^{(B);K}_{m;J}\Omega_{IK}\right)+2c^{(B)}_{m;IJ}.

(4.33)

It suffices to verify the validity of the above identities for
m=-1,0,1,2
.
The identities for
m=-1,0
have already been proved in (
3.73
)–(
3.74
);
for
m=1,2
, we remark that the way to calculate
\mathcal{L}_{E^{2}}\Omega_{0,p;0,q}
and

\mathcal{L}_{E^{3}}\Omega_{0,p;0,q}
in the Appendix B of
[

can be applied to calculate
\mathcal{L}_{E^{2}}\Omega_{IJ},\mathcal{L}_{E^{3}}\Omega_{IJ}

for

4.3
Linearization of the Legendre-extended Virasoro symmetries

Let
M(\eta,c,e,E)
be a generalized Frobenius manifold.
Consider a
quasi-Miura transformation

[
15
,
30
,
31
]

for the Principal Hierarchy (
3.4
) of the form

v^{\alpha}\mapsto w^{\alpha}=v^{\alpha}+\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{\alpha\gamma}\partial_{x}\partial_{t^{\gamma,0}}\Delta\mathcal{F},

(4.34)

where

\Delta\mathcal{F}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\varepsilon^{k-2}\mathcal{F}^{[k]},\quad\mathcal{F}^{[k]}=\mathcal{F}^{[k]}\bigl(v,v_{x},v_{xx},...,v^{(N_{k})}\bigr)

(4.35)

and
N_{k}
are certain non-negative integer numbers.
By representing the Principal Hierarchy in terms of the unknown functions
w^{1},\dots,w^{n}
,
we get a deformation of this integrable hierarchy.

We say that the transformation (
4.34
)
linearizes the Virasoro symmetries (
4.22
)–(
4.24
)
if the actions of
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}}\right\}
on the
tau function

\tau=\exp(\mathcal{F})=\exp({\varepsilon^{-2}f+\Delta\mathcal{F}})

(4.36)

of the deformed integrable hierarchy are given by

\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial s_{m}}=L_{m}\Bigl(\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbf{t},\varepsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{t}}\Bigr)\tau,\quad m\geq-1,

(4.37)

here the Virasoro operators
L_{m}
are defined in (
4.6
).
We can represent the right hand side of (
4.37
) in the form

\displaystyle L_{m}\Bigl(\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbf{t},\varepsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{t}}\Bigr)\tau=\left[\mathcal{D}_{m}\Delta\mathcal{F}+\varepsilon^{-2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial s_{m}}+a_{m}^{IJ}\Omega_{IJ}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon^{2}a_{m}^{IJ}\left.\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial t^{I}\partial t^{J}}+\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial t^{I}}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial t^{J}}\right)+\frac{\delta_{m,0}}{4}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\mu^{2}\right)\right]\tau,

(4.38)

where the operators
\mathcal{D}_{m}
for
m\geq-1
are defined by

\mathcal{D}_{m}=2a_{m}^{IJ}f_{I}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{J}}+b^{J}_{m;I}t^{I}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{J}}.

(4.39)

As it is shown in
[
31
]
(see also in
[
15
]
),
for any functions
\Delta\mathcal{F}
of the form (
4.35
)
we have the following identities:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{m}\Delta\mathcal{F}=K_{m}\Delta\mathcal{F}+\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial s_{m}},

(4.40)

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq-1}\frac{1}{\lambda^{m+2}}\left[a_{m}^{IJ}\Omega_{IJ}+\varepsilon^{2}a_{m}^{IJ}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial t^{I}\partial t^{J}}+\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial t^{I}}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial t^{J}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\sum_{k,\ell\geq 0}\left(\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\alpha,k}}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\beta,\ell}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\alpha,k}\partial v^{\beta,\ell}}\right)(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\partial^{\alpha}p_{\sigma})G^{\sigma\rho}(\partial_{x}^{\ell+1}\partial^{\beta}p_{\rho})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\alpha,k}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}\left(\grad_{\eta}\left(\frac{\partial p_{\sigma}}{\partial\lambda}\right)\cdot\grad_{\eta}\left(\frac{\partial p_{\rho}}{\partial\lambda}\right)\cdot v_{x}\right)^{\alpha}G^{\sigma\rho}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{2}G^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}*\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda},

(4.41)

where the vector fields

K_{m}=\sum\limits_{s\geq 0}K_{m}^{\gamma,s}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\gamma,s}}
on the jet space
J^{\infty}(M)

are defined by the following generating series:

\sum_{m\geq-1}\frac{K_{m}^{\gamma,s}}{\lambda^{m+2}}=(s+1)\partial_{x}^{s}\left(\frac{1}{E-\lambda e}\right)^{\gamma}+\sum_{k=1}^{s}k{s+1\choose k+1}\left(\partial_{x}^{s-k}\partial^{\gamma}p_{\alpha}\right)G^{\alpha\beta}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\partial_{x}^{k}p_{\beta}\right),

p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
are any given basis of
periods
of
M
,

G=(G^{\alpha\beta})
is the associated Gram matrix,
and the
star product

*
is defined in
[
15
,
31
]
.
Hence the linearization condition (
4.37
)
is equivalent to the following
loop equation

[
30
,
31
]

\displaystyle\sum_{s\geq 0}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\gamma,s}}(s+1)\partial_{x}^{s}\left(\frac{1}{E-\lambda e}\right)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{s\geq 0}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\gamma,s}}\sum_{k=1}^{s}k{s+1\choose k+1}\left(\partial_{x}^{s-k}\partial^{\gamma}p_{\alpha}\right)G^{\alpha\beta}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\partial_{x}^{k}p_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\sum_{k,\ell\geq 0}\left(\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\alpha,k}}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\beta,\ell}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\alpha,k}\partial v^{\beta,\ell}}\right)(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\partial^{\alpha}p_{\sigma})G^{\sigma\rho}(\partial_{x}^{\ell+1}\partial^{\beta}p_{\rho})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\alpha,k}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}\left(\left(\grad_{\eta}\frac{\partial p_{\sigma}}{\partial\lambda}\right)\cdot\left(\grad_{\eta}\frac{\partial p_{\rho}}{\partial\lambda}\right)\cdot v_{x}\right)^{\alpha}G^{\sigma\rho}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}G^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}*\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}-\frac{1}{4\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\mu^{2}\right)

(4.42)

of the generalized Frobenius manifold
M
with the unknown function
\Delta\mathcal{F}
.
The above equation is required to hold true identically with respect to the formal parameter
\lambda
.
It was proved in
[
30
]
that if the generalized Frobenius manifold
M
is semisimple,
then there exists a unique (up to adding constant terms) solution
\Delta\mathcal{F}
to the loop equation (
4.42
),
which is of the form

\Delta\mathcal{F}=\sum_{g\geq 1}\varepsilon^{2g-2}\mathcal{F}_{g}\bigl(v,v_{x},...,v^{(3g-2)}\bigr).

(4.43)

Here the functions
\mathcal{F}_{g}
are called the genus
g
free energies of
M
.
The quasi-Miura transformation (
4.34
) induced by the solution

\Delta\mathcal{F}
to (
4.42
) yields a deformation of the Principal Hierarchy (
3.4
)
which is called the
topological deformation
of the Principal Hierarchy of the generalized Frobenius manifold
M
.

Now let
B
be an invertible quasi-homogeneous Legendre field of
M
,
we can also impose the linearization condition

\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial s^{(B)}_{m}}=L^{(B)}_{m}\Bigl(\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbf{t},\varepsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{t}}\Bigr)\tau,\quad m\geq-1

(4.44)

of the Legendre-extended Virasoro symmetries (
4.27
)–(
4.29
) on the tau function (
4.36
),
which are analogous to (
4.37
).
Here
L_{m}^{(B)}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}^{(B)}}
are introduced in (
4.7
) and
(
4.27
)–(
4.29
) respectively.

Theorem 4.5
.

The Legendre-extended analogue of the linearization condition (
4.44
)
is equivalent to the loop equation (
4.42
).

Proof.

By straightforward calculation, we obtain the following analogues
of (
4.38
)–(
4.39
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{(B)}_{m}:=2a_{m}^{(B);IJ}f_{I}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{J}}+b^{(B);J}_{m;I}t^{I}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{J}},

(4.45)

\displaystyle L_{m}^{(B)}\Bigl(\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbf{t},\varepsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{t}}\Bigr)\tau=\Bigl[\mathcal{D}_{m}^{(B)}\Delta\mathcal{F}+\varepsilon^{-2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial s_{m}^{(B)}}+a_{m}^{(B);IJ}\Omega_{IJ}

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon^{2}a_{m}^{(B);IJ}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial t^{I}\partial t^{J}}+\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial t^{I}}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial t^{J}}\right)+\frac{\delta_{m,0}}{4}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\mu^{2}\right)\Bigr]\tau.

(4.46)

To prove this theorem, it suffices to verify that the identities

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{(B)}_{m}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}}\right)\Delta\mathcal{F}=\left(\mathcal{D}_{m}^{(B)}-\mathcal{D}_{m}\right)\Delta\mathcal{F},\quad m\geq-1

(4.47)

hold true for all functions
\Delta\mathcal{F}
on the jet space
J^{\infty}(M)
,
because of the relations (
4.13
) and
(
4.40
)–(
4.41
). Denote

\Delta b_{m;J}^{I}:=b_{m;J}^{(B);I}-b_{m;J}^{I},

then from (
4.13
), (
4.26
) and (
4.30
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{(B)}_{m}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}}\right)\Delta\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\geq 0}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\alpha,s}}\partial_{x}^{s}\left(\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial s^{(B)}_{m}}-\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial s_{m}}\right)=\sum_{s\geq 0}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\alpha,s}}\partial_{x}^{s}\left(\Delta b_{m;J}^{I}t^{J}\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{I}}\right),

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{D}_{m}^{(B)}-\mathcal{D}_{m}\right)\Delta\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\Delta b_{m;J}^{I}t^{J}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial t^{I}}=\Delta b_{m;J}^{I}t^{J}\sum_{s\geq 0}\frac{\partial\Delta\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\alpha,s}}\partial_{x}^{s}\left(\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{I}}\right).

Note that the spatial variable
x
is always identified with the time variable
t^{0,0}
,
and the relations (
4.11
)–(
4.15
) imply

\Delta b_{m;0,0}^{J}=0,

therefore we arrive at (
4.47
). The theorem is proved.
∎

We remark that the loop equation (
4.42
) depends only on the generalized Frobenius manifold
M
,
and is independent of the choice of the Legendre field
B
.

4.4
Relationship between the deformed integrable hierarchies

Let
M(\eta,c,e,E)
be a generalized Frobenius manifold,
and
B
be an invertible quasi-homogeneous Legendre field on
M
.
Denote
(\hat{M},\hat{B})
the generalized Legendre transformation of
(M,B)
,
where
\hat{B}:=B^{-1}
.
By using the relationship between the Legendre-extended tau-covers of

(M,B)
and
(\hat{M},\hat{B})
that is given in Theorem
3.18
,
we apply the following identifications

\hat{f}=f,\qquad\hat{f}_{i,p}=f_{\sigma(i),p},\quad\hat{t}^{i,p}=t^{\sigma(i),p},\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{t}^{i,p}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\sigma(i),p}}

(4.48)

for their Legendre-extended tau-covers,
where
\sigma
is defined in (
3.78
).
Then the relationship (
4.16
)
between the Legendre-extended Virasoro operators reads

\hat{L}_{m}^{(\hat{B})}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{t}},\varepsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{\mathbf{t}}}\right)=L_{m}^{(B)}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbf{t},\varepsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{t}}\right),\quad m\geq-1,

(4.49)

moreover, from (
4.17
) and the definitions of the Legendre-extended Virasoro symmetries
it is clear that

\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{s}^{(\hat{B})}_{m}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{(B)}_{m}},\quad m\geq-1.

(4.50)

The quasi-Miura transformation (
4.34
), (
4.35
)
for the Principal Hierarchy (
3.4
) of
M
yields a quasi-Miura transformation

\hat{v}^{\alpha}\mapsto\hat{w}^{\alpha}=\hat{v}^{\alpha}+\varepsilon^{2}\hat{\eta}^{\alpha\gamma}\partial_{\hat{x}}\partial_{\hat{t}^{\gamma,0}}\Delta\hat{\mathcal{F}},

(4.51)

for the Principal Hierarchy of
\hat{M}
,
where
\hat{v}^{1},\dots,\hat{v}^{n}
are related with
v^{1},\dots,v^{n}
via (
3.76
), (
3.80
), and

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{\mathcal{F}}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\varepsilon^{k-2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{[k]}\bigl(\hat{v},\hat{v}_{\hat{x}},\hat{v}_{\hat{x}\hat{x}},...,\hat{v}^{(N_{k})}\bigr),

(4.52)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{[k]}\bigl(\hat{v},\hat{v}_{\hat{x}},\hat{v}_{\hat{x}\hat{x}},...,\hat{v}^{(N_{k})}\bigr)=\mathcal{F}^{[k]}\bigl(v,v_{x},v_{xx},...,v^{(N_{k})}\bigr).

(4.53)

We note that the
x
-derivatives of
v^{\alpha}
are related with the
\hat{x}
-derivatives of
\hat{v}^{\alpha}
via
\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{B,0}}
. For example, from (
3.51
) we have

v_{x}=\hat{B}\cdot\hat{v}_{\hat{x}}.

From (
4.49
), (
4.50
) it follows that the quasi-Miura transformation
(
4.34
), (
4.35
) linearizes the Legendre-extended Virasoro symmetries of
(M,B)
if and only if the quasi-Miura transformation
(
4.51
)–(
4.53
) linearizes the Legendre-extended Virasoro symmetries of
(\hat{M},\hat{B})
, i.e.,

\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial s_{m}^{(B)}}=L_{m}^{(B)}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbf{t},\varepsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{t}}\right)\tau\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial\hat{s}_{m}^{(\hat{B})}}=\hat{L}_{m}^{(\hat{B})}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{t}},\varepsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{\mathbf{t}}}\right)\tau

for
m\geq-1
,
where the tau function is given by

\tau:=\exp(\varepsilon^{-2}f+\Delta\mathcal{F})=\exp(\varepsilon^{-2}\hat{f}+\Delta\hat{\mathcal{F}}).

From Theorem
4.5
we know that
the above linearization conditions are equivalent to the loop equations
(
4.42
) of
M
and
\hat{M}
respectively,
therefore
\Delta\mathcal{F}
satisfies the loop equation of
M

if and only if
\Delta\hat{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the loop equation of
\hat{M}
.

If the generalized Frobenius manifold
M
is semisimple,
then
\hat{M}
is also semisimple because
M
and

\hat{M}
share the same multiplication.
So in this case, from
[
30
]
we know that the loop equation (

) of
M

and the one of

have unique solutions

\Delta\mathcal{F}=\sum_{g\geq 1}\varepsilon^{2g-2}\mathcal{F}_{g}\bigl(v;v_{x},\dots,v^{(2g-3)}\bigr),\quad\Delta\hat{\mathcal{F}}=\sum_{g\geq 1}\varepsilon^{2g-2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{g}\bigl(\hat{v};\hat{v}_{\hat{x}},\dots,\hat{v}^{(2g-3)}\bigr)

up to the addition of constant terms, and we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(\hat{M},\hat{B})
be the generalized Legendre transformation of
(M,B)
,
and
M
is semisimple, then the solutions
\Delta\mathcal{F}
,
\Delta\hat{\mathcal{F}}
of their loop equations satisfy the relations

\mathcal{F}_{g}(v,v_{x},...,v^{(3g-2)})=\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{g}(\hat{v},\hat{v}_{\hat{x}},...,\hat{v}^{(3g-2)})+\text{const.}

(4.54)

for
g\geq 1
. The topological deformations of the Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchies of
(M,B)
and
(\hat{M},\hat{B})
that are obtained by the quasi-Miura transformations (
4.34
) and (
4.51
) share the same tau function, and they are related by the linear reciprocal transformation (
3.50
).

5
Example: the KdV and the
q
-deformed KdV hierarchies

5.1
Generalized Frobenius manifolds and their complete data

Consider the well-known 1-dimensional Frobenius manifold
M
with potential

F=\frac{v^{3}}{6},

(5.1)

here
v
is the flat coordinate with respect to the flat metric
\eta=\td v\otimes\td v
.
The unit vector field
e
and Euler field
E
of this Frobenius manifold are

e=\partial_{v},\quad E=v\partial_{v}.

(5.2)

Moreover, this Frobenius manifold has charge
d=0
, and monodromy data
\mu=R=0
.

Introduce an invertible quasi-homogeneous Legendre field

B=\frac{1}{2v^{\frac{1}{2}}}\partial_{v}

(5.3)

on
M
with
\mu_{B}=-\frac{1}{2}
.
The Legendre field
B
transforms
M
to a new generalized Frobenius manifold
\hat{M}
with potential

\hat{F}=\frac{\hat{v}^{4}}{12}

(5.4)

and new metric
\hat{\eta}=\td\hat{v}\otimes\td\hat{v}
, where the new coordinate
\hat{v}
reads

\hat{v}=v^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(5.5)

Under the new coordinate
\hat{v}
, the unit vector field
e
and Euler field
E
have the form

e=\frac{1}{2\hat{v}}\partial_{\hat{v}},\qquad E=\frac{1}{2}\hat{v}\partial_{\hat{v}},

(5.6)

and the charge
\hat{d}
of
\hat{M}
is
\hat{d}=-2\mu_{B}=1
.
Moreover, the inversion
\hat{B}:=B^{-1}
of
B
is

\hat{B}=\partial_{\hat{v}},

(5.7)

in particular,
\hat{B}
is flat with respect to the new metric
\hat{\eta}
.

The Legendre-extended calibrations
\Xi_{B}
,
\hat{\Xi}_{\hat{B}}

of
(M,B)
and
(\hat{M},\hat{B})
are given by

\begin{array}[]{c|c}\begin{split}\xi_{0,p}&=\,\xi_{1,p}=\frac{v^{p}}{p!}\partial_{v},\\
\xi_{B,p}&=\,\frac{2^{p-1}}{(2p-1)!!}v^{p-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{v},\\
\xi_{0,-q}&=\,0,\\
\xi_{B,-q}&=\,(-1)^{q}\frac{(2q-1)!!}{2^{q+1}}v^{-q-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{v},\end{split}&\begin{split}\hat{\xi}_{\hat{B},p}&=\,\hat{\xi}_{1,p}=\frac{\hat{v}^{2p}}{p!}\partial_{\hat{v}},\\
\hat{\xi}_{0,p}&=\,\frac{2^{p-1}}{(2p-1)!!}\hat{v}^{2p-1}\partial_{\hat{v}},\\
\hat{\xi}_{\hat{B},-q}&=\,0,\\
\hat{\xi}_{0,-q}&=\,(-1)^{q}\frac{(2q-1)!!}{2^{q+1}}\hat{v}^{-2q-1}\partial_{\hat{v}}\end{split}\end{array}

for
p\geq 0
and
q>0
.
Note that the above formulae can be uniformly written as

\displaystyle\xi_{0,p}=\xi_{1,p}=\frac{v^{p}}{\Gamma(p+1)}\partial_{v},\qquad\xi_{B,p}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(p+\frac{1}{2})}v^{p-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{v},

(5.8)

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}_{\hat{B},p}=\hat{\xi}_{1,p}=\frac{\hat{v}^{2p}}{\Gamma(p+1)}\partial_{\hat{v}},\qquad\hat{\xi}_{0,p}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(p+\frac{1}{2})}\hat{v}^{2p-1}\partial_{\hat{v}},

(5.9)

for
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, and these vector fields are related by (
3.43
).
From (
3.36
) we know that
the corresponding Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchies are given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t^{0,p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial v}{\partial t^{1,p}}=\frac{v^{p}}{\Gamma(p+1)}v_{x},\qquad\frac{\partial v}{\partial t^{B,p}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(p+\frac{1}{2})}v^{p-\frac{1}{2}}v_{x},

(5.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{v}}{\partial\hat{t}^{\hat{B},p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\hat{v}}{\partial\hat{t}^{1,p}}=\frac{2\hat{v}^{2p+1}}{\Gamma(p+1)}\hat{v}_{\hat{x}},\qquad\frac{\partial\hat{v}}{\partial\hat{t}^{0,p}}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(p+\frac{1}{2})}\hat{v}^{2p}\hat{v}_{\hat{x}},

(5.11)

here
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Note that the flows
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{1,p}}\right\}_{p\geq 0}
form the well-known
dispersionless KdV hierarchy
,
which is extended by a family of additional flows
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{B,p}}\right\}_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
On the other hand, the Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchy (
5.11
)
coincides with the dispersionless limit of the
extended
q
-deformed KdV hierarchy
,
see details in
[
30
]
. From (
5.5
) and
the identifications
x=t^{0,0}
,
\hat{x}=\hat{t}^{0,0}
,
we obtain the relationship

\hat{v}_{\hat{x}}=\frac{1}{4v}v_{x}

(5.12)

between the coordinates
v_{x}
and
\hat{v}_{\hat{x}}
on the jet space
J^{\infty}(M)
,
and then it can be verified directly that the dispersionless extended KdV hierarchy (
5.10
)
and the dispersionless extended
q
-deformed KdV hierarchy (
5.11
)
are related by the linear reciprocal transformation

\hat{t}^{1,p}=t^{1,p},\qquad\hat{t}^{0,q}=t^{B,q},\qquad\hat{t}^{\hat{B},q}=t^{0,q}

(5.13)

for
p\geq 0
and

, which confirms the assertion of Theorem
3.13
.

The tau structures
\{\Omega_{i,p;j,q}\},\{\hat{\Omega}_{i,p;j,q}\}

of the integrable hierarchies (

\displaystyle\Omega_{1,p;1,q}

\displaystyle=\,\Omega_{1,p;0,q}=\Omega_{0,p;1,q}=\Omega_{0,p;0,q}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(q+1)}\frac{v^{p+q+1}}{p+q+1},&\text{if $p,q\geq 0$},\\
0,&\text{if $p<0$ or $q<0$},\end{cases}

\frac{(-1)^{p}}{4}\log v,&\text{if $p+q=0$},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\hat{\Omega}_{1,p;1,q}

\displaystyle=\,\hat{\Omega}_{1,p;\hat{B},q}=\hat{\Omega}_{\hat{B},p;1,q}=\hat{\Omega}_{\hat{B},p;\hat{B},q}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(q+1)}\frac{\hat{v}^{2p+2q+2}}{p+q+1},&\text{if $p,q\geq 0$},\\
0,&\text{if $p<0$ or $q<0$},\end{cases}

under the identifications (

In particular,

(5.34)

Proof.

It suffices to verify that the functions

).
From Theorem

U=\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\log\tau}{\partial x\partial t^{1,0}}=\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\log\hat{\tau}}{\partial\hat{t}^{1,0}\partial\hat{t}^{1,0}}.

By the relationships between the

thus we arrive at a proof of the theorem.
∎

Example: the Toda and the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchies

6.1
Generalized Frobenius manifolds and their complete data

Consider the 2-dimensional generalized Frobenius manifold
M
with potential

F=\frac{1}{2}v^{2}u+v{\mathrm{e}}^{u}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}\log v,

(6.1)

where
(v,u):=(v^{1},v^{2})

\eta=\td v\otimes\td u+\td u\otimes\td v.

The unity

This generalized Frobenius manifold and its relation to the

[

[

Consider the vector field

(6.3)

on

,
which is an invertible quasi-homogeneous Legendre field such that

\mu_{B}=\mu_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}.

It is clear that the flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{B,p}}

\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{1,p}}

\xi_{B,p}=\xi_{1,p},\qquad\theta_{B,p}=\theta_{1,p},\qquad\Omega_{B,p;i,q}=\Omega_{1,p;i,q}

for

and

As shown in
[

,

with potential

\hat{\eta}=\td\hat{v}\otimes\td\hat{u}+\td\hat{u}\otimes\td\hat{v},

\hat{v}=v+{\mathrm{e}}^{u},\quad\hat{u}=\log v+u.

From (

\displaystyle\hat{B}

\displaystyle=\,\hat{B}\cdot e=\frac{v(\hat{B}\cdot\partial_{v})-(\hat{B}\cdot\partial_{u})}{v-{\mathrm{e}}^{u}}=\frac{v\partial_{\hat{v}}-\partial_{\hat{u}}}{v-{\mathrm{e}}^{u}}.

6.3
The extended Toda hierarchy

In this subsection, let us recall the definition and some basic properties of the extended Toda hierarchy.
For more details, see
[
8
,
16
]
.
Introduce the Lax operator

\hat{L}=\hat{\Lambda}+\hat{V}+{\mathrm{e}}^{\hat{U}}\hat{\Lambda}^{-1},

(6.14)

where
\hat{V},\hat{U}
are unknown functions, and the shift operator

\hat{\Lambda}={\mathrm{e}}^{\varepsilon\partial_{\hat{x}}}.

(6.15)

The
extended Toda hierarchy
consists of the following flows

\varepsilon\frac{\partial\hat{L}}{\partial\hat{t}^{\alpha,p}}=[A_{\alpha,p},\hat{L}],\quad\alpha=1,2,\,p\geq 0,

(6.16)

where

\displaystyle A_{1,p}=\frac{2}{p!}\left(\hat{L}^{p}(\log\hat{L}-c_{p})\right)_{+},\quad A_{2,p}=\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\left(\hat{L}^{p+1}\right)_{+},

and
c_{p}=1+\frac{1}{2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p}
for
p\geq 1
,
c_{0}=0
.
Note that for an operator of the form
X=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}X_{k}\hat{\Lambda}^{k}
,
the positive part
X_{+}
is defined as
\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}X_{k}\hat{\Lambda}^{k}
, and

\res X=X_{0}
.
The definition of
\log\hat{L}
can be found in
[
8
]
and is omitted here.

The
\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{t}^{2,p}}
-flows for
p\geq 0
, known as the positive flows,
constitute the usual Toda hierarchy,
while the logarithmic flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{t}^{1,p}}
serve as an extension of this hierarchy.
The first flows of the extended Toda hierarchy (
6.16
) are

\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{t}^{1,0}}=\partial_{\hat{x}}
and

\varepsilon\frac{\partial\hat{V}}{\partial\hat{t}^{2,0}}={\mathrm{e}}^{\hat{U}^{\oplus}}-{\mathrm{e}}^{\hat{U}},\qquad\varepsilon\frac{\partial\hat{U}}{\partial\hat{t}^{2,0}}=\hat{V}-\hat{V}^{\ominus},

(6.17)

here we introduce the following short notation: for a function
f
we denote

f^{\oplus}=\hat{\Lambda}f,\qquad f^{\ominus}=\hat{\Lambda}^{-1}f.

(6.18)

The extended Toda hierarchy (
6.16
) possesses a tau structure.
For any solution of the extended Toda hierarchy there exists a function
\hat{\tau}
such that

\displaystyle\hat{V}

\displaystyle=\,\varepsilon(\hat{\Lambda}-1)\frac{\partial\log\hat{\tau}}{\partial\hat{t}^{2,0}},

(6.19)

\displaystyle\hat{U}

\displaystyle=\,(\hat{\Lambda}-1)(1-\hat{\Lambda}^{-1})\log\hat{\tau},

(6.20)

\varepsilon(\hat{\Lambda}-1)\frac{\partial^{2}\log\hat{\tau}}{\partial\hat{t}^{\alpha,p}\partial\hat{t}^{\beta,q}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{t}^{\beta,q}}\res A_{\alpha,p}

(6.21)

for
\alpha,\beta=1,2
and
p,q\geq 0
.
For example, we have the following two-point functions

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\log\hat{\tau}}{\partial\hat{t}^{2,0}\partial\hat{t}^{2,0}}

\displaystyle=\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\hat{U}},

(6.22)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\log\hat{\tau}}{\partial\hat{t}^{2,0}\partial\hat{t}^{2,1}}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{2}(\hat{V}+\hat{V}^{\ominus}){\mathrm{e}}^{\hat{U}},

(6.23)

which can be derived from (
6.17
) and (
6.21
) directly.

The extended Toda hierarchy is the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy of
\hat{M}
(
6.5
)
up to a certain Miura-type transformation.
Precisely speaking, let

\Delta\hat{\mathcal{F}}=\sum\limits_{g\geq 1}\varepsilon^{2g-2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{g}

be the solution to the loop equation of
\hat{M}
,
and suppose
\hat{v},\hat{u}
satisfy the Principal Hierarchy of
\hat{M}
, then

\displaystyle\hat{V}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\hat{\Lambda}-1}{\varepsilon\partial_{\hat{x}}}\left(\hat{v}+\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\Delta\hat{\mathcal{F}}}{\partial\hat{x}\partial\hat{t}^{2,0}}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{U}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{(\hat{\Lambda}-1)(1-\hat{\Lambda}^{-1})}{\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{\hat{x}}^{2}}\left(\hat{u}+\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\Delta\hat{\mathcal{F}}}{\partial\hat{x}\partial\hat{t}^{1,0}}\right)

satisfy the extended Toda hierarchy (
6.16
),
and the corresponding tau function satisfies

\log\hat{\tau}=\varepsilon^{-2}\hat{f}+\Delta\hat{\mathcal{F}},

where
\hat{f}
be the genus-zero free energy of the tau-cover (
3.75
).

We remark that in the Principal Hierarchy of
\hat{M}
, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{t}^{0,p}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{t}^{1,p}}\qquad\text{for $p\geq 0$},

and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{t}^{0,p}}=0
for
p\leq-1
.
The Legendre field
\hat{B}
of
\hat{M}
defined in (
6.8
)
yields a family of extended flows
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{t}^{\hat{B},p}}\right\}_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
the topological deformation of which gives a further extension of the extended Toda hierarchy.

6.4
The extended Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy

In this subsection, let us recall the definition and some basic properties of the extended Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy.
For more details, see
[
29
,
32
]
and Sect. 11 of
[

]

Introduce the Lax operator

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\,(1-Q\Lambda^{-1})^{-1}(\Lambda-P)

\displaystyle=\,\Lambda+(Q-P)+Q(Q^{-}-P^{-})\Lambda^{-1}+\cdots,

(6.24)

where
P,Q
are unknown functions, and the shift operator

\Lambda={\mathrm{e}}^{\varepsilon\partial_{x}}.

\displaystyle\varepsilon\frac{\partial L}{\partial t^{2,p}}

)–(

\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{0,p}},\ \frac{\partial}{\partial t^{1,p}},\quad p\geq 0

(6.35)

\displaystyle V^{\oplus}

Remark 6.3

The relation (

Note that the Lax operator

\displaystyle\hat{\Lambda}\psi

The evolution equation of the wave function can be rewritten as

By applying

7
Conclusion

In this paper we establish the relationship between the Principal Hierarchies, the tau structures,
the Virasoro operators and the full-genera free energies
of two semisimple generalized Frobenius manifolds
M
and
\hat{M}
that are related by a generalized Legendre transformation.
An important step in the establishing of this relationship is the construction of the hierarchies of flows associated with the Legendre fields. We call these flows the Legendre flows, and we add them to the Principal Hierarchies of the generalized Frobenius manifolds to obtain the Legendre extended Principal Hierarchies. We show that the Legendre extended Principal Hierarchies and their topological deformations associated with the generalized Frobenius manifolds
M
and
\hat{M}
are related by a linear reciprocal transformation, and we also give two examples to illustrate such a relationship between integrable hierarchies associated with generalized Frobenius manifolds.

We remark that a generic generalized Frobenius manifold
M
with non-flat unity can be transformed to a usual Frobenius manifold
\hat{M}
with flat unity by a certain generalized Legendre transformation.
To see this, let
\{v^{1},\dots v^{n}\}
be a system of flat coordinates of
M
such that its Euler vector field takes the form (
2.14
),
then all the vector fields
\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}
are quasi-homogeneous and Legendre; under the assumption that
\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}
is invertible for a certain
\alpha\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, the Legendre transformation associated with

B=\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}
transforms
M
to a Frobenius manifold
\hat{M}
with flat unity
e=\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{v}^{\alpha}}
with respect to the flat metric of
\hat{M}
.

Note that the quasi-Miura transformation
(
4.34
) yielded by the solution to the loop equation (
4.42
)
transforms the bihamiltonian structure (
3.42
) of the Legendre-extended Principal Hierarchy of a semisimple generalized Frobenius manifold
M

to that of its topological deformation.
An important problem is whether this deformed bihamiltonian structure possesses the polynomiality property, i.e., whether the deformation terms of the bihamiltonian structure can be represented in terms of differential polynomials of the unknown functions of the integrable hierarchy.
In the case of a usual semisimple Frobenius manifold with flat unity,
the polynomiality of its deformed bihamiltonian structure is studied in
[
6
,
7
,
15
,
24
]
, and it is proved recently in
[
33
]
. The fact that a generic generalized Frobenius manifold with non-flat unity can be transformed to a usual Frobenius manifold with flat unity by a generalized Legendre transformation leads us to conjecture that
the polynomiality property also holds true for
the deformed bihamiltonian structure of a semisimple generalized Frobenius manifold with non-flat unity.
We will study this topic in subsequent publications.

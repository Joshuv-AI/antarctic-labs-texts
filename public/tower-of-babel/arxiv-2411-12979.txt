In other words, the gauge coupling unifications in such non-minimal extensions cannot be achieved with the relation of (

Appendix A
Some results of the affine Lie algebra

In this appendix, we review some necessary results of the Lie algebra and the affine Lie algebra.
We follow the conventions in the textbook of the conformal field theory

]
closely.
All results described in this section are given in the algebraic basis.

A.1
The Lie algebra: Cartan-Weyl basis, simple roots, and the highest weight

The Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
is defined according to the commutation relations among
{\rm dim}(\mathfrak{g})
generators as follows

\displaystyle\left[T^{A}\,,T^{B}\right]=if^{ABC}T^{C}\,,\quad A\,,B\,,C=1\,,...\,,{\rm dim}(\mathfrak{g})\,,

(76)

where
f^{ABC}
are known as the structure constants.

\mathfrak{g}
has a maximal set of commuting generators to form the Cartan subalgebra, and we denote them by

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}^{i}\}\,,\quad i=1\,,...\,,r\,,\quad r={\rm rank}(\mathfrak{g})\,,\quad\left[\mathcal{H}^{i}\,,\mathcal{H}^{j}\right]=0\,.

(77)

All other
{\rm dim}(\mathfrak{g})-r
generators of
E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
are the ladder operators, and their commutators with the Cartan generators lead to the roots
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
of the
\mathfrak{g}
as follows

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{H}^{i}\,,E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\right]=\alpha^{i}E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\,,\quad\boldsymbol{\alpha}\equiv(\alpha^{1}\,,...\,,\alpha^{r})\,.

(78)

Since for any root
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, the
-\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is also a root, one thus partitions the set of all roots
\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\}
in Eq. (
78
) into the set of positive root and negative roots as

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Delta_{+}\oplus\Delta_{-}\,,\quad{\rm dim}\Delta_{+}={\rm dim}\Delta_{-}=\frac{1}{2}\Big({\rm dim}(\mathfrak{g})-r\Big)\,.

(79)

The mutual commutators between the raising operators of
E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
and lowering operators of
E^{-\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
are

\displaystyle\left[E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\,,E^{-\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\right]=\frac{2}{(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\,,\boldsymbol{\alpha})}\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}\,.

(80)

All roots can be generally written as linear combinations of other roots, and the
r
linearly independent set of roots are known as the simple roots of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}
(with
i=1\,,...\,,r
).
The simple coroots are defined according to the simple roots as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}^{\lor}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{2\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}}{(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}\,,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i})}\,.

(81)

For the
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{u}(N)
Lie algebras, the scalar products between the simple roots are normalized as
(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j}\,,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j})=2
, hence one has
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}^{\lor}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}
and
(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j}^{\lor}\,,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j}^{\lor})=2
as well.
The scalar products between the simple roots and the coroots define the
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{u}(N)
Cartan matrix

\displaystyle A_{i\,j}\equiv(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}\,,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j}^{\lor})\,,

\displaystyle A_{i\,i}=2\,,\quad A_{i\,i\pm 1}(1<i<N-1)=A_{1\,2}=A_{N-1\,N-2}=-1\,.

(82)

Among all roots in the
\Delta
, there is a unique
highest root

\boldsymbol{\theta}
, which can be expressed as the summation of all simple roots or simple coroots

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}^{\lor}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}^{\lor}\,,\quad a_{i}\,,a_{i}^{\lor}\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

(83)

The
a_{i}
and
a_{i}^{\lor}
are the
marks
and the
comarks
and one has
a_{i}=a_{i}^{\lor}=1
for the
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{u}(N)
Lie algebra.
The highest root
\boldsymbol{\theta}
for the
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{u}(N)
is given by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1\,,\underbrace{0\,,...\,,0}_{N-3}\,,1)\,.

(84)

The
Coxeter number

g
and the
dual Coxeter number

g^{\lor}
can be defined as

\displaystyle g\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}+1\,,

(85a)

\displaystyle g^{\lor}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}^{\lor}+1\,.

(85b)

The dual Coxeter number reads
g^{\lor}=N
for the
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{u}(N)
Lie algebra, while the Abelian
\mathfrak{u}(1)
Lie algebra has no dual Coxeter number.

The fundamental weights of
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}
are defined from the simple coroots as follows

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\,,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j}^{\lor})=\delta_{ij}\,.

(86)

The highest-weight states, denoted as
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, are defined by

\displaystyle E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\rangle=0\,,\quad\forall\penalty\ \penalty\ \boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\Delta_{+}\,,

(87)

and are expressed in terms of the Dynkin labels as follows

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\,,\quad\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1}\,,...\,,\lambda_{N-1})=\{\ell_{1}\,,...\,,\ell_{N-1}\}\,,\quad\lambda_{i}\,,\ell_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(88)

with

\displaystyle\ell_{i}=\lambda_{i}+...+\lambda_{N-1}

(89)

representing the number of boxes in the
i^{\rm th}
row of a corresponding Young tableau.
For instance, the Dynkin labels for the rank-
k
anti-symmetric, the rank-
k
symmetric, and the adjoint representations are expressed as follows

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}(A_{k})=(\underbrace{0\,,...\,,0}_{k-1}\,,1\,,\underbrace{0\,,...\,,0}_{N-k-1})\,,

(90a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}(S_{k})=(k\,,\underbrace{0\,,...\,,0}_{N-2})\,,

(90b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}({\rm adj})=(1\,,\underbrace{0\,,...\,,0}_{N-3}\,,1)\,.

(90c)

For any irrep, all weights can be constructed by subtracting the simple roots from the corresponding highest-weight state, and the total numbers of the weights through this procedure correspond to the dimension of the irrep.
The scalar products between the
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{u}(N)
fundamental weights are expressed as the quadratic form matrix of

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\,,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j})=\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j}\,,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j})(A^{-1})_{ij}=\frac{1}{N}{\rm min}(i\,,j)\cdot(N-{\rm max}(i\,,j))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}N-1&N-2&N-3&...&2&1\\
N-2&2(N-2)&2(N-3)&...&4&2\\
N-3&2(N-3)&3(N-3)&...&6&3\\
.&.&.&...&.&.\\

\end{array}\right)\,,

where

is the inverse of the Cartan matrix (
82
), and we have used the normalization of
(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}\,,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k})=2
.
The scalar products between two highest-weight states are thus given by

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{(1)}\,,\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{(2)})=\sum_{i\,,j}\lambda_{i}^{(1)}\lambda_{j}^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\,,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j})=\sum_{i\,,j}\lambda_{i}^{(1)}\lambda_{j}^{(2)}F_{ij}\,.

(98)

A special type of the weight is the Weyl vector, which is given by the summation over all fundamental weights, or half of the summation over all positive roots as follows

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\Delta_{+}}\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\underbrace{1\,,...\,,1}_{N-1})\,.

(99)

The Abelian
\mathfrak{u}(1)
Lie algebra has no Weyl vector.

The quadratic Casimir operator of
\mathcal{Q}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mathcal{H}^{i}\mathcal{H}^{i}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\Delta_{+}}(E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}E^{-\boldsymbol{\alpha}}+E^{-\boldsymbol{\alpha}}E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})\,.

(100)

in the Cartan-Weyl basis.
Since it commutes with all generators, it is mostly convenient to evaluate the eigenvalue of

on the highest-weight state of

10

(101)

\displaystyle C_{2}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})_{\tt alg}=({\boldsymbol{\lambda}}+2{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\,,{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})\,,

For the rank-

\displaystyle C_{2}(A_{k})_{\tt alg}

\displaystyle(\underbrace{0\,,...\,,0}_{k-1}\,,1\,,\underbrace{0\,,...\,,0}_{r-k})\cdot(\underbrace{2\,,...\,,2}_{k-1}\,,3\,,\underbrace{2\,,...\,,2}_{r-k})

For the rank-

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k\,,\underbrace{0\,,...\,,0}_{N-2})\cdot(k+2\,,\underbrace{2\,,...\,,2}_{N-2})

\displaystyle\frac{N-1}{N}k(k+2)+2k\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}\frac{N-j}{N}

For the adjoint representation with the highest-weight state being the highest root of

A.2
The affine Lie algebra: Cartan-Weyl basis, simple roots, and the highest weight

Next, we generalize the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
into the
loop algebra
of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}\left[t\,,t^{-1}\right]\,,

(105)

such that the commutation relations in Eq. (

\displaystyle\left[{T^{A}}_{m}\,,{T^{B}}_{n}\right]=if^{ABC}{T^{C}}_{m+n}\,,

(106)

with
{T^{A}}_{m}\equiv T^{A}\otimes t^{m}
.
A central extension to the commutator is given by

\displaystyle\left[{T^{A}}_{m}\,,{T^{B}}_{n}\right]=if^{ABC}{T^{C}}_{m+n}+\hat{k}n\delta^{AB}\delta_{n+m\,,0}\,,

(107)

with

\displaystyle\left[{T^{A}}_{m}\,,\hat{k}\right]=0\,.

(108)

In other words, the central extension only exists when
n+m=0
.
The affine Lie algebra is defined by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\hat{k}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}L_{0}\,.

(109)

The
L_{0}
is known as the grading operator of

\displaystyle L_{0}\equiv-t\frac{d}{dt}\,,

(110)

and its action on the generators gives arise to the
grade

n
as below

\displaystyle[L_{0}\,,{T^{A}}_{n}]=-n{T^{A}}_{n}\,.

(111)

The maximal Cartan subalgebra of the affine Lie algebra
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is given by

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{i}\,,\hat{k}\,,L_{0}\}\,,\quad i=1\,,...\,,r\,,

(112)

while all other generators of
\{E_{n}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\}
and
\{\mathcal{H}_{n}^{i}\}
(with
n\neq 0
) form the ladder operators.

The affine roots include those associated with the ladder operators of
{E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}_{n}

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\,;0\,;n)=\boldsymbol{\alpha}+n\boldsymbol{\delta}\,,\quad\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\Delta\,,\quad\boldsymbol{\delta}=(\boldsymbol{0}\,;0\,;1)\,,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(113)

as well as an extra simple root of

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(-\boldsymbol{\theta}\,;0\,;1)=-\boldsymbol{\theta}+\boldsymbol{\delta}\,.

(114)

Here, the
(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\,;0\,;0)
represents the finite part of the affine roots
\hat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
,
\boldsymbol{\theta}
is the highest root of
\mathfrak{g}
in Eq. (
83
), and the
\boldsymbol{\delta}
is known as the imaginary root due to its zero length of
(\boldsymbol{\delta}\,,\boldsymbol{\delta})=0
.

The affine weights are generally denoted as

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\,;k_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\,;n_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})\,,

(115)

with the scalar products defined by

\displaystyle(\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\,,\hat{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\,,\boldsymbol{\sigma})+k_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}n_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}+k_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}n_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\,.

(116)

The fundamental weights of the
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\equiv a_{i}^{\lor}\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{0}+(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\,;0\,;0)\,,\quad\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{0}=(\boldsymbol{0}\,;1\,;0)\,,

(117)

with the
\boldsymbol{0}
representing the
N-1
zeros in the
basic fundamental weight
of
\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{0}
.
The corresponding scalar products are given by

\displaystyle(\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\,,\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{0})=(\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{0}\,,\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{0})=0\,,\quad(\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\,,\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{j})=(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}\,,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j})=F_{ij}\,,\quad(i\,,j\neq 0)\,.

(118)

For the
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{u}(N)
, the quadratic form matrix of
F_{ij}
was previously given in Eq. (
A.1
).
Any affine weight can be expanded in terms of the fundamental weights
\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}
and the imaginary root
\boldsymbol{\delta}

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}+\ell\boldsymbol{\delta}\,,\quad\ell\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(119)

with
\ell
being the
grade
.
For the affine Lie algebra of
\widehat{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{u}}(N)_{k}

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\hat{k})=(\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\,,\boldsymbol{\delta})=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}\,.

(120)

with

\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{u}(N)

The affine weights are also expressed in terms of the Dynkin labels as follows

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=[\lambda_{0}\,;\lambda_{1}\,,...\,,\lambda_{r}]\,.

(121)

For all
\widehat{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{u}}(8)_{k_{U}=1}
rank-
k
anti-symmetric irreps, their affine weights can still be expressed as the following highest-weight states

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(A_{k\,{\tt HW}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[0\,;\underbrace{0\,,...\,,0}_{k-1}\,,1\,,\underbrace{0\,,...\,,0}_{7-k}]\,.

(122)

]
that the adjoint representation cannot be admitted in the

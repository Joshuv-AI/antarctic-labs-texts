Rotational cooling of large trapped molecular ions
Monika Leibscher, Christiane P. Koch

Abstract
We suggest a protocol for the sympathetic cooling of a molecular asymmetric top rotor co-trapped with laser-cooled atomic ions, based on resonant coupling between the molecular ion’s electric dipole moment and a common vibrational mode of the trapped particles. By combining sympathetic sideband laser cooling with coherent microwave excitation, we demonstrate the efficient depopulation of arbitrary rotational subspaces and the ability to cool an incoherent distribution of rotational states into a single, well-defined quantum state. This capability opens the door to exploiting the rotational Hilbert space for applications in quantum information processing and high-precision spectroscopy.



Polyatomic molecules are emerging as a new platform for fundamental physics
Safronova et al. 2018
;
Hutzler 2020
and quantum information processing
Albert et al. 2020a
. They are, for example, excellent candidates to search for a non-zero dipole moment of the electron
Anderegg et al. 2023
or to measure parity violation with chiral molecules
Quack et al. 2022
;
Erez et al. 2023
. Molecular rotation can be used for quantum error correction
Albert et al. 2020b
;
Jain et al. 2024
;
Furey et al. 2024
or quantum simulation of magnetism
Wall et al. 2013
. All these applications take advantage of the fact that the Hilbert space of molecular rotation is large and comparatively well isolated. Moreover, the rotational spectrum is anharmonic which eases quantum state control
Koch et al. 2019
, and, within finite subspaces, both linear rotors and asymmetric top rotors are completely controllable
Judson et al. 1990
;
Pozzoli et al. 2022
;
Leibscher et al. 2022a
.
This implies that, with a combination of resonant electric fields, typically in the microwave, any desired unitary operation on the subspace can be implemented, degeneracies notwithstanding
Leibscher et al. 2022a
.
However, to actually implement the desired level of control requires the trapping and cooling of molecules
Deiß et al. 2024
;
Sinhal and Willitsch 2023
which for polyatomic molecules is very challenging. To date, the most advanced method is direct laser cooling
Augenbraun et al. 2023
;
Vilas et al. 2024
;
Lasner et al. 2025
. It is applicable to a certain class of molecules that was derived based on chemical intuition in order to adapt a spectrum with closed cycling transitions from diatomic to polyatomic molecules
Isaev and Berger 2016
.

Here, we suggest a completely different approach to cooling the rotations in polyatomic molecules, applicable to molecular ions, but not requiring a specific electronic structure.
We suggest to leverage resonant dipole-phonon interaction, as discussed for diatomic ions
Hudson and Campbell 2018
;
Campbell and Hudson 2020
, to sympathetically cool the rotations of polyatomic molecular ions by laser-cooled atomic ions.
In diatomic molecules, the normal mode frequencies in ion trap—typically in the MHz range—can be tuned to match hyperfine transitions
Campbell and Hudson 2020
, while dipole-allowed rotational transitions typically lie in the GHz to THz regime, and are therefore far off-resonant with trap modes. In contrast, polyatomic molecules that are asymmetric top rotors exhibit a much richer rotational spectrum, which can include dipole-allowed transitions within the MHz range. This enables resonant coupling between molecular rotation and trap motion via the permanent dipole moment of the molecule.
We show that resonant dipole-phonon coupling allows for sympathetic sideband cooling of the coupled rotational states, and propose a combination of sideband cooling with coherent microwave excitation to cool or deplete an arbitrary rotational subspace of an asymmetric top molecule.

Our protocol for cooling the rotational degrees of freedom is easily adapted to molecular ions other than the glutamine ion. For glutamine, the protocol relied on the closely spaced pairs of rotational levels of a near prolate asymmetric top, but similar level spacings can also be found in near oblate asymmetric tops. Furthermore, in order to be amenable to sympathetic sideband cooling, the molecular ions have to be polar and of
C_{1}
or
C_{s}
symmetry, to ensure sufficiently many non-vanishing Cartesian components of the dipole moment and thus controllability within every rotational subspace. The molecular mass should be comparable to the mass of the co-trapped atomic ions such that the center of mass motion of atomic and molecular ions is sufficiently coupled.
Finally, it may also be possible to extend the protocol to diatomic molecular ions. While their rotational level spacings are far too large for resonant dipole coupling
Leibscher et al. 2024
, the cooling protocol could be leverage transitions between hyperfine states.

In conclusion, we have shown how resonant dipole-phonon coupling enables sympathetic sideband cooling of the rotations of polyatomic molecules. Combining sideband cooling and coherent microwave excitation, an arbitrary subspace of the rotational spectrum of an asymmetric top can be depleted, transferring all population into a single pure rotational state. The cooling scheme requires strong resonant dipole-coupling between the rotational degrees of freedom and the collective motion of the trapped particles. A large molecular dipole moment and closely lying rotational levels are thus favorable.
The complex rotational structure of asymmetric top molecules
provides dipole allowed rotational transitions that allow for such resonant dipole coupling — the cooling scheme is thus is particularly well suited for polyatomic molecules.
Our cooling protocol enables the preparation of polyatomic molecular ions in a single quantum state. It thus opens the door to extending quantum logic spectroscopy from diatomic
Wolf et al. 2016
;
Sinhal et al. 2020
to polyatomic molecules and to using the rich rotational spectrum of asymmetric top rotors in fundamental physics experiments or quantum simulation and error correction.

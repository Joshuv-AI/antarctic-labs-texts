Ceftazidime-avibactam use for the treatment of OXA-48- and/or New Delhi metallo-β-lactamase–producing Enterobacterales in cancer patients: a retrospective observational study
Authors: Ahlam Alghamdi, Hajar AlQahtani, Sara Albilal, Maram Mater Almutairi, Nouf Alobaidallah, Linah Alghamdi, Amal Alfayez, Thamer Almangour, Ahmed Al-jedai
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND: Infection is the second-leading cause of death among cancer patients, but there have been few studies on the effectiveness of novel antimicrobial agents to treat carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales in cancer patients.OBJECTIVE: Evaluate the mortality and clinical outcomes of ceftazi-dime-avibactam for OXA-48- and/or New Delhi metallo-β-lactamase (NDM)-producing Enterobacterales infection in cancer patients.DESIGN: Retrospective observational cohort studySETTING: Tertiary academic medical center in Riyadh, Saudi ArabiaSUBJECTS AND METHODS: This study included patients who had cancer and received ceftazidime-avibactam for at least 72 hours for infections caused by OXA-48- and/or NDM-producing Enterobacterales. We excluded patients who died within 72 hours of treatment, patients with polymicrobial infections, and patients who did not receive appropriate antimicrobial therapy.MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: Primary outcomes were 30-day mortality and hospital mortality. Secondary outcomes included clinical cure, relapse, and reinfection.SAMPLE SIZE: 32 cancer patientsRESULTS: The 30-day mortality among all patients was 15/32 (47%), clinical cure was achieved in 19/32 (59%) of the patients, and the relapse and reinfection rates were 2/19 (10.5%) and 4/17 (23.5%), respectively.CONCLUSION: This is the largest study to evaluate clinical outcomes associated with infections caused by OXA-48- and/or NDM-producing Enterobacterales in cancer patients. The mortality rate remains high; however, ceftazidime-avibactam is an encouraging alternative for treating severe infections in cancer patients.LIMITATIONS: Small sample size and single center.

INTRODUCTION

Infection is the second-leading cause of death among cancer patients. Antimicrobial resistance is an urgent threat to the continuing success of cancer therapy.1,2 In Saudi Arabia, carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales (CRE) infections have been mainly associated with the acquisition of OXA-48-like and New Delhi metallo-β-lactamase (NDM)-type enzymes and are rarely associated with Klebsiella pneumoniae carbapenemases (KPCs), unlike in the United States, Colombia, Argentina, Greece, and Italy, where KPCs are the most common isolates.3–5

Ceftazidime-avibactam is a broad-spectrum antibiotic that is intravenously administered as a combination of the third-generation cephalosporin ceftazidime and the novel non-β-lactam β-lactamase inhibitor avibactam to treat CRE infections.3,4 Ceftazidime-avibactam was approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2015 for the treatment of complicated urinary tract infections (cUTIs) and complicated intra-abdominal infections (cIAIs) and in 2018 for the treatment of hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP) and ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP).6,7

The Infectious Diseases Society of America 2023 Guidance on the Treatment of Antimicrobial Resistant Gram-Negative Infections and other published studies recommend ceftazidime-avibactam for OXA-48-like-producing infections and ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam for NDM-like-producing infections.8–10

CRE infections have been rapidly spreading worldwide with a high mortality rate among cancer patients. The high mortality rate can be explained by the fact that neutropenic patients with cancer are threatened by multidrug resistance, especially in CRE, leading to increased mortality.11 A retrospective study among 147 immunocompromised patients with hematologic malignancies who had bloodstream infections caused by gram-negative bacteria demonstrated a high mortality rate (56%) associated with KPC-producing Klebsiella pneumoniae infections (n=26).12 Subsequent investigations have also demonstrated a high mortality rate among cancer patients infected with CRE.13

The largest study to evaluate ceftazidime-avibactam or other active agents to treat CRE infections in cancer patients was a multicenter and retrospective study among 31 patients, for whom the rates of 30-day mortality and clinical cure within 14 days were 45.2% and 85.7%, respectively. Patients who received ceftazi-dime-avibactam had a similar mortality rate and higher clinical cure rate than those who received comparator agents.9 The small number of patients in the study who received ceftazidime-avibactam precluded an effectiveness evaluation of this agent in cancer patients. Our objective in this study was to evaluate the mortality and clinical outcomes of ceftazidime-avibactam for OXA-48- and/or NDM-producing Enterobacterales infection in cancer patients.

SUBJECTS AND METHODS

This retrospective observational cohort study was conducted at a 1500-bed tertiary academic medical center in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, between 2018 and 2020. Eligible patients were 18 years of age or older, hospitalized with hematologic malignancies or solid tumors, receiving active chemotherapy and had received targeted therapy of ceftazidime-avibactam for ≥72 hours to treat OXA-48- and/or NDM-producing Enterobacterales infections. We excluded patients who died within 72 hours of treatment, patients with poly-microbial infections and patients who did not receive appropriate antimicrobial therapy. The study proposal was reviewed and approved by the King Abdullah International Medical Research Center (KAIMRC) institutional review boards at the hospital (IRB#H-01-R-005). Informed consent was not required due to the retrospective design.

The primary outcomes were 30-day mortality and hospital mortality. Secondary outcomes included clinical cure, relapse, and reinfection. Clinical cure was defined as a clinical response to treatment with resolution of signs and symptoms of the infection upon discontinuation of ceftazidime-avibactam. Relapse was defined as the onset of a second microbiologically documented infection in a patient within 30 days of the original infection that was classified as a clinical cure. Reinfection was defined as the onset of a second microbiologically documented infection after 30 days and before 1 year after the original infection classified as a clinical cure.9,11,14

Targeted therapy for OXA-48-producing organisms includes ceftazidime-avibactam or more traditional therapies, including carbapenem in combination with other antimicrobial agents, such as aminoglycoside, colistin, and tigecycline. For NDM-like-producing infections, targeted therapy includes a combination of ceftazidime-avibactam and aztreonam.

Microbiological tests for bacterial identification and susceptibility testing were performed in the laboratory department in accordance with their internal policies and procedures. Antibiotic susceptibility was determined by the VITEK2 (BioMérieux, France) automatic method. All carbapenem-resistant isolates were tested for genes encoding carbapenemase enzyme production via polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-based technology (GeneXpert, Cepheid, Sunnyvale, CA, USA). The susceptibility of bacteria to ceftazidime-avibactam was determined by the Etest method (BioMérieux) using the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute breakpoints (CLSI 2020) (MIC ≤ 8/4 mg/L for C/A).15

Categorical data are presented as frequencies and percentages. Continuous data are presented as the means and standard deviations or medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs) as appropriate. Associations between patient, clinical, disease, laboratory and treatment factors and 30-day mortality were analyzed using logistic regression. Time-to-event outcomes were visualized using Kaplan-Meier survival curves. Modeling of the 30-day mortality outcomes was limited to uni-variable (unadjusted) models only secondary to insufficient event counts to permit adjusted models. For all analyses, P<.05 was considered significant. All analyses were conducted in Stata version 16.1 (StataCorp, College Station, Texas).

RESULTS

During the study period, 42 cancer patients treated with ceftazidime-avibactam for OXA-48- and/or NDM-producing Enterobacterales infections were identified. Ten patients were excluded from this study for not meeting the criteria (9 patients received therapy <72 hours, and 1 patient did not receive aztreonam for a confirmed NDM-producing isolate infection). A total of 32 cancer patients who received ceftazidime-avibactam for > 72 hours to treat OXA-48- and/or NDM-producing Enterobacterales infections were included in the final analysis. Similarly, each additional kilogram of weight was significantly associated with 1.06 times the odds of 30-day mortality (OR 1.06; 95% CI 1.01, 1.11). DISCUSSION

To the best of our knowledge, this is the largest study to evaluate clinical outcomes among cancer patients treated with ceftazidime-avibactam for OXA-48- and/or NDM-producing Enterobacterales infections. In our cohort, the 30-day mortality and clinical cure rates were 47% and 59%, respectively. This mortality was similar to that previously reported in this group of patients treated with non-novel alternative regimens for carbapenemase-producing Enterobacterales infections, but the clinical cure rate was slightly higher than that reported in similar studies.9–12

Our study showed similar outcomes as in a previous study that evaluated ceftazidime-avibactam in cancer patients, in which patients who received ceftazidime-avibactam had similar mortality and higher clinical cure rates compared to those who received alternative therapy.9

However, in Saudi Arabia, OXA-48 carbapenemase is the most common, followed by NDM carbapenemase, and the mortality among patients with CRE infections receiving ceftazidime-avibactam for treatment of OXA-48-producing infections was 2/10 (20%), compared with 11/28 (39.3%) for patients treated with other agents.10 Generally, the three main types of carbapenemases are spreading regionally and globally with different magnitudes, with a high mortality rate among cancer patients (47%) compared to noncancer patients (20%).10,14

In this cohort, BMI and weight were significantly associated with 30-day mortality. Other factors, such as type of cancer, laboratory values, Charlson Comorbidity Index, comorbidities, and monotherapy vs. combination therapy, were not significantly associated with mortality. However, this could again be explained by the relatively small sample size included in this study. Therefore, these findings should be carefully interpreted and not generalized.

There is a growing body of literature to support the use of ceftazidime-avibactam for carbapenemase-producing infections, but there are limited data on novel β-lactam agents in critically ill patients, such as cancer patients.14,16–19 A retrospective study among 50 neutropenic patients with hematological diseases complicated with carbapenemase-producing Klebsiella pneumoniae (CP-KP) bloodstream infections compared the outcomes of adults who received monotherapy versus combination therapy. The 14-day all-cause mortality rates were 50%, 38% and 33% for those who received one, two and three active drugs, respectively.13 Another retrospective study among 138 adults with CP-KP infections compared the outcomes of adults who received ceftazidime-avibactam-containing salvage regimens versus adults who received alternative salvage regimens. The thirty-day mortality and relapse rates were 34.1% and 8.7, respectively, for all included patients, 36.5% and 9.6% for the ceftazidime-avibactam bacteremia group, and 55.8% and 8.6% for the alternative bacteremia group.11 However, an important limitation of the study was that only 33.3% of patients had cancer, making interpretation of the results challenging.

The mortality rate for cancer patients receiving ceftazidime-avibactam therapy continues to be substantial. This raises the question of whether this antibiotic is an effective treatment for these infections or if antibiotics with greater activity against these microorganisms are needed.9 In our opinion, ceftazidime-avibactam is an encouraging alternative for treating severe infections in those populations until new active agents with greater activity against resistant microorganisms are available. Some countries are already reporting resistance against ceftazidime-avibactam. This is a cause for concern and requires stewardship efforts.20

This study had a number of notable limitations: selection bias was likely due to its observational nature and a small number of patients from a single center, which did not allow us to draw definitive conclusions about the true impact of ceftazidime/avibactam on the mortality of these patients. This is the largest study to evaluate clinical outcomes associated with infections caused by OXA-48- and/or NDM-producing Enterobacterales in cancer patients. The mortality rate remains high; however, ceftazidime-avibactam is an encouraging alternative for treating severe infections in cancer patients.

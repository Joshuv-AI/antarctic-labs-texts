Mediation Analysis of Serum Fibroblast Growth Factor 20 and Poor Prognosis After Ischemic Stroke
Authors: Keyang Chen, Wenting Huang, Beilei Hu, Fangwang Fu, Yungang Cao, Huiqin Xu, Lixin Ruan, Yongang Li, Yan Li, Jun Chen, Fei Liang, Xue Wang, Xudong Du, Li Lin, Xiaokun Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWe aimed to examine the relationship between serum FGF20 (fibroblast growth factor 20) levels and stroke prognosis in a multicenter cohort study.Methods and ResultsPatients with ischemic stroke/transient ischemic attack were prospectively recruited from 5 participating centers and followed up at 3 months and 1 year. FGF20 levels were measured using the ELISA method. The primary outcome was poor stroke functional outcome (modified Rankin Scale score of 3–6), and secondary outcomes included death and composite vascular events. Multivariable logistic regression analysis or Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was employed to estimate the relationship between FGF20 and study outcomes. Mediation analysis was conducted to examine the mediating effects of traditional risk factors on the association between FGF20 and stroke outcomes. A total of 1011 patients with ischemic stroke were included in the study. After adjusting for potential confounding factors, an elevated serum FGF20 level was associated with a reduced risk of the poor outcome and death. Multivariable adjusted spline regression analysis demonstrated a linear correlation between serum FGF20 levels and the stroke outcomes. The incorporation of FGF20 alongside conventional risk factors marginally enhanced the reclassification of adverse outcomes. Renal function and white blood cell count partially mediated the relationship between FGF20 and the prognosis of ischemic stroke.ConclusionsElevated FGF20 level is associated with decreased risks of adverse outcomes after ischemic stroke, which was partially mediated by renal function and white blood cells with a modest amount, indicating that serum FGF20 might serve as a promising biomarker for predicting stroke prognosis.RegistrationURL: https://www.chictr.org.cn; Unique identifier: ChiCTR2100051104.

By the year 2050, the global incidence of stroke‐related deaths is anticipated to surge by 50%, posing a significant public health concern and economic burden. 1 , 2 Consequently, early risk stratification and identification of modifiable risk factors hold the potential to be more efficacious in stroke prevention and in reducing mortality and disability rates. Nevertheless, current predictive models based on demographic and clinical parameters may have limited external validity and risk of bias due to insufficient sample sizes, inadequate variable selection, and lack of comprehensive data integration, 3 whereas those based on blood biomarkers are gaining attention for providing additional information to clinical practice.

The FGF (fibroblast growth factor) family consists of a group of structurally conserved extracellular signaling molecules that act on a family of transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinases, the FGFRs (FGF receptors). This family encompasses 22 members, organized into 6 subfamilies. 4 , 5 Within the human body, FGF plays a nourishing role in blood vessels, the brain, and other organs. FGFs demonstrate the capability to repair tissues, expedite wound healing, and regulate the nervous system. 6 Some endocrine FGFs have been used as biomarkers to assess the occurrence and prognosis of stroke. 7 FGF20 is a member of the FGF9 subfamily of paracrine functions, and it is associated with cellular processes such as apoptosis, aging, inflammation, and autophagy. 8 It is predominantly expressed in the brain and demonstrates neurotrophic activity. 9 FGF20 has been shown to have a protective effect on dopaminergic neurons and motor function in Parkinson's disease. 10 We hypothesize that FGF20 may play a neuroprotective role in other central nervous system diseases. Our preliminary research has uncovered an association between FGF20 and traumatic brain injury. 11 However, presently, there is a dearth of available data to scrutinize the role of FGF20 in stroke populations, and the underlying mechanisms linking FGF20 to stroke outcomes remain incompletely elucidated.

Hence, within the scope of this study, our investigation centered on determining the potential association between serum FGF20 levels and stroke outcomes in individuals with ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack through the lens of cohort studies, investigating whether serum FGF20 could be used as a potential new biomarker in the prediction of stroke prognosis. Furthermore, we conducted an in‐depth evaluation to ascertain whether traditional risk factors act as mediators in the relationship between FGF20 and stroke outcomes.

METHODS

Data Availability Statement

The supporting data for the results of study can be obtained from the corresponding authors.

Study Design and Participants

The participants in our multicenter, prospective observational study were sourced from the SFBCS (Stroke and Fibroblast Growth Factor Biomarkers Cohort Study), which focused on stroke FGF biomarkers and their correlation with outcomes. The study adhered to the reporting guidelines of Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology and A Guideline for Reporting Mediation Analyses. 12 , 13 As outlined previously, 14 the study included 1068 patients with stroke (comprising ischemic stroke and transient ischemic attack) aged 18 and above, presenting within 24 hours of symptom onset. Patients who had no blood samples or no follow‐up information were excluded. The enrollment period spanned from March 1, 2021, to August 31, 2022. The diagnosis of acute ischemic stroke followed the criteria of the World Health Organization, with confirmation of the stroke lesion through brain magnetic resonance imaging. 15 Follow‐up assessments occurred at 3 months and 1 year.

Data Collection

Baseline patient data were gathered at admission through either face‐to‐face interviews or examination of electronic medical records. This comprehensive data set included demographic information (age and sex), body mass index, which is determined by dividing weight in kilos by the square of height in meters, current smoking and alcohol consumption, presence of hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, ischemic stroke, and coronary heart disease, as well as the prestroke modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score, National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale at admission and medicine use during hospitalization. 16 Current smoking was operationally defined as the consumption of at least one cigarette per day, and alcohol consumption was characterized by the intake of a minimum of 80 g of liquor per day based on standardized questionnaires. The presence of hypertension and diabetes was determined using self‐reported medical histories. Each participating hospital conducted laboratory data collection, encompassing parameters such as blood glucose, blood lipids, and blood creatinine. The pathogenetic classification of ischemic stroke was determined in accordance with the Trial of ORG 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment criteria. 17 Dyslipidemia was defined as having total cholesterol levels ≥6.22 mmol/L, triglyceride levels ≥2.26 mmol/L, low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol levels ≥4.14 mmol/L, high‐density lipoprotein cholesterol levels <1.04 mmol/L, or self‐reported use of lipid‐lowering medications, as per the Chinese guidelines on the prevention and treatment of dyslipidemia. 18 The estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated according to the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration creatinine. 19

Registering, Approval, and Patient Consent

The research protocol received approval from the Ethics Committee of Wenzhou Medical University and all participating hospitals. Before enrollment, written informed consent was obtained from all patients or their immediate relatives (for patients without civil capacity) before registration. The trial is registered at http://www.chictr.org.cn (ChiCTR2100051104).

Outcome Assessment

Participants underwent follow‐up evaluations in person at 3 months and 1 year post stroke onset, conducted by trained neurologists who remained blinded to the clinical characteristics. Outcomes were evaluated using the mRS with a range between 0 and 6, where “0” means no illness and “6” means death. The primary outcome focused on poor functional outcome, defined as the amalgamation of death and major disability (mRS score of 3–6). 20 The secondary outcomes encompassed death (mRS score of 6) and composite vascular events. Composite vascular events included recurrent nonfatal stroke, nonfatal myocardial infarction, angina, and congestive heart failure. Obtaining information on patient deaths can be done through hospitals or local government departments. Besides, vascular event data can be extracted from the hospital. The occurrence time of ischemic events was defined as time zero. 21

Analysis of Serum FGF20 Levels

Blood samples were collected from 5 research sites. Within 24 hours of symptom onset, fasting blood samples were obtained upon admission and divided into serum separation tubes and EDTA anticoagulation tubes. Subsequently, all samples were carefully transported through a cold chain to the central laboratory at Wenzhou Medical University and stored at −80 °C until the analysis phase. Serum FGF20 levels were quantified using the ELISA method, provided by Jiang‐Lai Biotechnology (JL29191, Shanghai, China). The methods used for FGF20 measurement were described in Data S1. The laboratory workers were unaware of the clinical features and results of the trial subjects.

Statistical Analysis

Compare the characteristics of included and excluded study participants. For participants who were included in the study, baseline characteristics are compared across quartiles of serum FGF20 levels. Continuous variables are presented as medians (interquartile ranges) and group comparisons are made using the nonparametric Kruskal–Wallis test. Categorical variables are expressed as percentages and compared using the χ2 test. The cumulative incidences of death and vascular events across serum FGF20 quartiles were estimated using Kaplan–Meier curves, with comparisons made using log‐rank tests. Multivariable logistic regression or Cox proportional hazards regression models were applied to calculate odds ratios (ORs) and 95% CIs for poor outcome or hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CIs for death and composite vascular events. ORs, HRs, and 95% CIs for the upper quartiles of FGF20 levels were computed with the lowest quartile as the reference. Estimates were generated for both unadjusted and adjusted models. Adjusted Model 1 included sex and age; Model 2 encompassed variables from Model 1 plus a history of stroke, coronary heart disease, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, current smoking, alcohol consumption, systolic blood pressure at baseline and body mass index; Fully adjusted model integrated variables from Model 2 along with admission mRS score, National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, and ischemic stroke subtype. Based on previous literature and existing clinical knowledge, we identify potential confounding factors and make adjustments using causal directed acyclic graphs (Figure S1). To enhance our understanding of the correlation, we employed inverse probability of censoring weights (IPCW) as a sensitivity analysis. 22 In the IPCW model, a multivariable logistic regression model was used, including all covariates outlined in Table 1. This model generated the probability of predicting complete data, and inverse probability weighting was applied to rectify potential biases introduced by missing data. Furthermore, we employed restricted cubic splines to assess the shape of the association between serum FGF20 and unfavorable stroke prognosis, incorporating 3 knots (at the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles). 23 To evaluate the incremental discriminatory power of serum FGF20 for adverse outcomes, we used the C statistics between a conventional risk model and a new model (conventional risk factors plus serum FGF20). Moreover, net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination index (IDI) were calculated to gauge the improvement in the risk reclassification of ischemic stroke prognosis with the addition of serum FGF20. 24 We employed a mediation model to explore whether the association between FGF20 and adverse stroke outcomes is mediated by traditional risk factors (systolic blood pressure, triglycerides, low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol, fasting blood glucose, homocysteine, white blood cells [WBC], C‐reactive protein, or eGFR). The causal mediation analysis was conducted within the counterfactual framework 25 based on the theories of Baron and Kenny, 26 as well as Valeri and VanderWeele. 27 FGF20 served as the predictor variable (X), traditional risk factors as the mediator variables (M), and adverse stroke outcomes as the outcome variable (Y, Figure 1). The overall effect includes the natural direct effect and the natural indirect effect, denoted as OR. The mediating effect is represented by proportion mediation. The average survival ratio represents the proportion of the average survival time for each unit increase in the exposure variable (FGF20). As eGFR, triglycerides, homocysteine, and low‐density lipoprotein values were right‐skewed, logarithmic transformations were applied to them before the mediation analysis. We employed the %mediation macro in SAS for the analysis. Multiple imputations account for missing covariate values were performed using the Markov chain Monte Carlo method. 28 A 2‐sided P<0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using R statistical software version 4.3.0 (R Foundation) or SAS software version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Inc, Cary, NC).

RESULTS

Baseline Characteristics

A total of 1068 patients with ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack were enrolled in 5 blood sample collection centers. During the initial patient inclusion phase, individuals lacking data or with missing follow‐up records were excluded, resulting in a final analysis cohort of 1011 patients (Figure S2). The distribution of mRS scores in patients with different quartiles of FGF20 changes over time (Figure S3). The included and excluded patients shared similar baseline characteristics to a large extent. However, excluded patients had lower fasting blood glucose levels, as well as lower levels of triglycerides and cholesterol, and higher levels of high‐density lipoprotein (Table S1). We stratified the cohort based on baseline characteristics according to FGF20 levels, as presented in Table 1. Patients with elevated FGF20 levels appeared to be younger, and those with higher FGF20 levels were more likely to display lower systolic blood pressure at admission. Notably, among the 4 stratified groups based on FGF20 levels, there were no discernible differences in the proportion of the large artery atherosclerosis subtype.

Association Between Levels of FGF20 and Outcomes

Overall, the cumulative incidence of poor outcomes, death, and composite vascular events within 3 months was 31.4%, 5.3%, and 2.5%, respectively. At 1 year, these figures were 27%, 9.9%, and 6.2%, respectively. Kaplan–Meier curves indicated that patients in the highest quartile of FGF20 levels had the lowest risk of death at follow‐ups (log‐rank P=0.040; log‐rank P=0.046), but no significant correlation was found with composite vascular events (Figure 2). After adjusting for age, sex, and other potential confounding factors, patients in the fourth FGF20 quartile exhibited a reduced risk of poor outcome compared with those in the first quartile (adjusted OR, 0.20 [95% CI, 0.11–0.34]; P trend<0.001), death (adjusted HR, 0.49 [95% CI, 0.24–0.96]; P trend=0.045) (Table 2). However, no significant association was observed between FGF20 and composite vascular events. Similar trends were observed in the 3‐month follow‐up results (adjusted OR, 0.59 [95% CI, 0.36–0.97, P trend=0.038]; adjusted HR, 0.47 [95% CI, 0.13–0.99, P trend=0.046, Table S2]). In the sensitivity analysis considering the potential bias with the IPCW method, the association remained unchanged (IPCW model in Table 2). Restricted cubic spline analyses showed inverse linear associations of serum FGF20 levels with poor outcome (P value for linearity <0.001) and death (P value for linearity <0.001, Figure 3; Figure S4). No significant association was observed between FGF20 and composite vascular events.

Subgroup Analysis for the Association Between FGF20 and Outcomes

We further conducted subgroup analysis on the prognosis of FGF20 levels in relation to the pathogenetic subtypes of stroke (Table S3; Table S4). In the subgroup analysis, we observed a significant correlation between FGF20 levels and prognosis outcomes in the large artery atherosclerosis subtype. Compared with patients in the lowest quartile, patients in the highest quartile of FGF20 had a reduced risk of poor outcome (adjusted OR, 0.80 [95% CI, 0.74–0.86]; P<0.001), death (adjusted HR, 0.94 [95% CI, 0.89–0.99]; P=0.049), and composite vascular events (adjusted HR, 0.95 [95% CI, 0.90–0.99]; P=0.049). Additionally, in the small artery subtype, FGF20 was associated with a poor outcome (adjusted HR, 0.83 [95% CI, 0.73–0.93]; P=0.003). The results of the 3‐month follow‐up were also similarly correlated.

Incremental Prognostic Value of Serum FGF20 Level

Table 3 illustrates the impact of incorporating FGF20 into conventional risk factors on predictive prognostic ability. The addition of serum FGF20 level to conventional risk factors significantly not only improved the discriminatory power (C statistics: 0.882 versus 0.862; P=0.004), but also slightly improved the risk reclassification of the poor outcome (NRI=29.68%, P<0.001; IDI=0.39%, P<0.001) within 1 year. However, it did not increase the discriminatory power for death and composite vascular events, nor did it improve the ability for risk reclassification (all P>0.05). As shown in Table S5, augmenting serum FGF20 levels on top of conventional risk factors did not enhance discriminatory power, but lightly improved the risk reclassification of the poor outcome (NRI=18.28%, P=0.007; IDI=1.05%, P=0.002), death (NRI=30.54%, P=0.026; IDI=2.2%, P=0.036) within 3 months after ischemic stroke onset.

Mediation Analysis

In the correlation analysis, we observed a negative correlation between serum FGF20 levels and baseline systolic blood pressure (r=−0.09; P=0.006), WBC count(r=−0.11; P<0.001), homocysteine(r=−0.09; P=0.003) and borderline associated with fasting blood glucose (r=−0.06; P=0.07), indicating that FGF20 levels were negatively correlated with some traditional risk factors (Table S6). The mediation analysis demonstrated that the proportions of short‐term and long‐term adverse outcomes following ischemic stroke mediated by eGFR were 23.7% and 18.7% in the adjusted model, respectively. Moreover, the proportions of short‐term and long‐term adverse outcomes after ischemic stroke mediated by WBC were 9.3% and 12.6%, respectively. Similar trends were identified for the outcome of death. However, other risk factors, do not exhibit significant mediating effects on the relationship between FGF20 and stroke. (Table 4; Table S7).

DISCUSSION

In this prospective cohort research conducted across many centers, our research indicates that an increase in FGF20 levels is associated with a reduced risk of short‐term and long‐term adverse outcomes in patients with ischemic stroke, and there is a linear dose–response correlation. These associations were observed in both large artery atherosclerosis and small artery subtypes. Furthermore, the addition of FGF20 levels to conventional risk factors slightly improved predictive ability, as evidenced by the NRI and IDI indices. Mediation analysis suggests that eGFR and WBC play a partial mediating role in the relationship between FGF20 and stroke prognosis during patient follow‐up. To our knowledge, this is the first study investigating the role of FGF20 in the prognosis of patients with stroke.

Research has delved into the association between FGF and stroke. A prior investigation shown that levels of FGF in individuals with acute ischemic stroke are markedly elevated compared with those in the control group. 29 The expression levels of FGF21 in intracranial blood are lower than those in systemic arterial blood. 30 Bioinformatics analysis indicates an elevation in the expression levels of the FGF9 gene in the brains of patients with stroke. 31 Moreover, the FGFR4 gene polymorphism is significantly associated with the risk of ischemic stroke in Chinese patients. 32 The Northern Manhattan Study confirmed that heightened levels of FGF23 are linked to an increased risk of stroke and intracerebral hemorrhage events. 33 In addition, elevated levels of FGF21 are correlated with the unfavorable prognosis of ischemic stroke 34 and hemorrhagic stroke 14 in cohort study. However, reports about the effects of FGF20 on the prognosis of central nervous system diseases are limited. In our prospective multicenter cohort study, we observed an association between serum FGF20 levels and poor outcome as well as mortality. Furthermore, we found that FGF20 has a protective effect on the short‐term and long‐term outcomes in patients with ischemic stroke. We assume that patients with ischemic stroke with low serum FGF20 levels should undergo early monitoring and therapeutic interventions. Accurate prediction of adverse outcomes following ischemic stroke is of significant importance for optimizing medical resources and implementing stratified treatments. In this study, we specifically extend the prognostic value of FGF20 to patients with acute ischemic stroke. Adding serum FGF20 levels to conventional risk factors can enhance the risk stratification for improving major outcomes. Based on our study findings, we believe that serum FGF20 levels at the onset of the disease can better identify patients at risk of adverse prognosis and may be valuable as a predictive biomarker.

According to previous research, FGF20 is primarily expressed in the brain 8 and has well‐known neurotrophic activity. 9 The association between blood FGF20 levels and the prognosis of cerebral infarction in patients with stroke remains unclear in the current medical literature. Current research has explored several potential mechanisms. First, FGF20 can upregulate the expression of tight junction and adherens junction proteins such as vascular endothelial‐cadherin, occludin, and claudin‐5, protecting the blood–brain barrier from damage and reducing the degree of brain edema. 11 Second, FGF20 promotes neurofunctional recovery after brain injury by regulating the JNK/NFκB (c‐Jun N‐terminal kinase/nuclear factor kappa B) pathway and alleviating brain inflammatory responses. 11 Apart from that, FGF20 can stimulate new blood vessel formation by upregulating factors such as vascular endothelial growth factor, insulin‐like growth factor 1, angiopoietin‐1, and basic FGF. 35

Research indicates that FGF20 may have a protective effect on kidney function. 36 The eGFR reflects kidney function and is considered to be associated with the prognosis of ischemic stroke. 37 However, there is no evidence reported regarding the causal pathways between FGF20, eGFR, and adverse outcomes. Therefore, this study used mediation analysis to explore potential mechanisms, and we found that eGFR is not only related to adverse outcomes in ischemic stroke but also linked to FGF20 levels. This supports the hypothesis that kidney function may play a mediating role in the relationship between FGF20 and stroke outcomes. We also discovered that WBC count plays a mediating role in the association between FGF20 and adverse stroke outcomes. The WBC count in the acute phase of stroke reflects inflammation and immune levels, which are associated with a worse prognosis. 38 As FGF20 acts as a protective protein that can improve inflammation, we speculate that the accumulation of FGF20 may partially alleviate cerebral immune‐inflammation levels, thereby improving outcomes. These findings suggest that kidney function and inflammation may be pathological and physiological directions for further exploration to demonstrate the causal effects and potential mechanisms of FGF20 on adverse stroke outcomes. Additionally, in a recent case–control study, an association between FGF20 levels and blood glucose was observed. 39 However, our study did not find that blood glucose plays a mediating role in the relationship between FGF20 and stroke outcomes. Because we observed only moderate mediating effects in our study, additional clinical and foundational research is needed to replicate our findings.

This study has both strengths and limitations. It is a multicenter prospective registry study, and to our knowledge, the first investigation into the role of FGF20 in patients with stroke, as well as the first study conducted in an Asian population. The study includes a 1‐year follow‐up period to observe long‐term outcomes. Moreover, standardized protocols and rigorous quality control procedures were employed for data collection, enabling a more effective assessment of correlations. However, there are several limitations to this study. First, the multicenter design focused on patients from the southern coastal region of Zhejiang Province, which may introduce selection bias that cannot be completely avoided. Secondly, despite almost controlling for all significant confounding factors in this observational study, it cannot entirely eliminate the possibility of residual confounding. However, we conducted sensitivity analysis using the IPCW method to explain potential biases due to missing data, and the results remained consistent with the main findings. Third, this study collected only baseline FGF20 levels and conventional risk factors at the same time point and did not examine changes in FGF20 over time or during the occurrence of late events. Therefore, we cannot ensure the logical time sequence between the FGF20, kidney function, and WBCs. The causal relationships require further investigation. Fourth, there may be differences in the subtypes of stroke among Chinese patients, limiting the generalizability of our study results to other populations.

CONCLUSIONS

Elevated serum FGF20 levels are associated with better prognosis among Chinese patients, suggesting that serum FGF20 may be a potential biomarker for prognosis after ischemic stroke. Furthermore, the association of FGF20 with stroke adverse outcomes could be partially mediated by eGFR and WBC with a modest amount.

Analysis of quantities determining the critical inverse temperature in the annealed Potts model with Pareto vertex weights
A.J.E.M. Janssen

Abstract




Abstract.

We consider in this work the crucial quantity
t_{c}
that determines the critical inverse temperature
\beta_{c}
in the
q
-state Potts model on sparse rank-1 random graphs where the vertices are equipped with a Pareto weight densty
(\tau-1)\,w^{-\tau}\,{\cal X}_{[1,\infty)}(w)
. It is shown in
[
1
]
that this
t_{c}
is the unique positive zero of a function
{\cal K}
that is obtained by an appropriate combination of the stationarity condition and the criticality condition for the case the external field
B
equals 0 and that
q\geq 3
and
\tau\geq 4
, see
[
1
]
, Theorem 1.14 and Theorem 1.21 and their proofs in
[
1
]
, Section 7.1 and Section 7.3. From the proof of
[
1
]
, Theorem 1.14, it is seen that
{\cal K}^{\prime}
and
{\cal K}^{\prime\prime}
also have a unique positive zero,
t_{c}^{\prime}
and
t_{c}^{\prime\prime}
, respectively, and
t_{c}^{\prime}=t_{b}
and
t_{c}^{\prime\prime}=t_{\ast}
, where
t_{b}
and
t_{\ast}
are the unique positive zeros of
{\cal F}_{0}(t)-t\,{\cal F}_{0}^{\prime}(t)
and
{\cal F}_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t)
, respectively. Here,
{\cal F}_{0}(t)={{E}}\,[W(e^{tW}-1)/({{E}}\,[W]\,(e^{tW}+q-1))]
, and
t_{c}
,
t_{b}
and
t_{\ast}
play a key role in the graphical analysis of
[
1
]
, Section 5.1 and Figure 1. Furthermore,
\gamma_{c}=\exp(\beta_{c})-1
and
t_{c}
are related according to
\gamma_{c}=t_{c}/{\cal F}_{0}(t_{c})
.

We analyse
t_{c}
,
t_{c}^{\prime}
and
t_{c}^{\prime\prime}
for general real
\tau\geq 4
and general real
q>2
by an appropriate formulation of their defining equations
{\cal K}(t_{c})={\cal K}^{\prime}(t_{c}^{\prime})={\cal K}^{\prime\prime}(t_{c}^{\prime\prime})=0
. Thus we find, along with the inequality
0<t_{c}^{\prime\prime}<t_{c}^{\prime}<t_{c}<\infty
, the simple upper bounds
t_{c}<2\,{\rm ln}(q-1)
,
t_{c}^{\prime}<\frac{3}{2}\,{\rm ln}(q-1)
,
t_{c}^{\prime\prime}<{\rm ln}(q-1)
, as well as certain sharpenings of these simple bounds and counterparts about the large-
q
behaviour of
t_{c}
,
t_{c}
and
t_{c}^{\prime\prime}
. We show that these (sharpened) bounds are sharp in the sense that they hold with equality for the limiting case
\tau\rightarrow\infty
(homogeneous case). These results have consequences for
\gamma_{c}
and
\beta_{c}
via the equation
\gamma_{c}=t_{c}/{\cal F}_{0}(t_{c})
. While the large-
q
behaviour of
t_{c}
,
t_{c}^{\prime}
and
t_{c}^{\prime\prime}
is qualitatively the same for all
\tau\geq 4
, such a thing does not hold for the behaviour when
q\downarrow 2
. It appears that one has to distinguish between the cases
\tau=4
,
4<\tau<5
,
\tau=5
and
\tau>5
with decay behaviour as
q\downarrow 2
ranging from exponentially small in
q-2
to linear in
q-2
.

1
Introduction

In
[
1
]
a comprehensive study has been made of the annealed ferromagnetic
q
-state Potts model on sparse rank-1 random graphs, where vertices are equipped with a vertex weight and where the probability of an edge is proportional to the product of the weights. Under a rather general condition on the weight distribution, phase transition is first order, with a unique discontinuity of the order parameter, see
[
1
]
, Abstract and Section 1. Let
\beta>0
denote the inverse temperature, and let
\gamma=e^{\beta}-1
(in
[
1
]
one writes
\beta^{\prime}
instead of
\gamma
, but in the present work the prime
′
has been reserved to indicate differentiation). Assume that the external field
B
equals 0 and that
{{E}}\,[W^{2}]<\infty
(
W
is the random weight variable). Consider the function

{\cal F}_{0}(t)={{E}}\,\Bigl[\frac{W}{{{E}}\,[W]}~\frac{e^{tW}-1}{e^{tW}+q-1}\Bigr]~,~~~~~~t\geq 0~.

(1)

The function
{\cal F}_{0}(t)
is non-negative, strictly increasing and
{\cal F}_{0}(t)\rightarrow 1
,
t\rightarrow\infty
. Assume that
{\cal F}_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t)
is first positive and then negative, so that
{\cal F}_{0}(t)
has a unique inflection point
t_{\ast}>0
with
{\cal F}_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t_{\ast})=0
and
{\cal F}_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
,
0\leq t<t_{\ast}
, and
{\cal F}_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t)<0
,
t>t_{\ast}
. Also consider the annealed pressure

p(t,\beta)={{E}}\,[{\rm ln}(e^{tW}+q-1)]-{{E}}\,[W]\,\frac{\gamma}{2q}\,\Bigl((q-1)\Bigl(\frac{t}{\gamma}\Bigr)^{2}+2\,\frac{t}{\gamma}-1\Bigr)~.

(2)

Under the above condition on
{\cal F}_{0}
, there is a unique pair
(t_{c},\gamma_{c})
with
t_{c}>0
,
\gamma_{c}>0
such that simultaneously the stationarity condition

{\cal F}_{0}(t)=\frac{t}{\gamma}

(3)

and the criticality condition

p(t,\gamma)=p(0,\gamma)

(4)

is satisfied by
(t,\gamma)=(t_{c},\gamma_{c})
. The criticality condition can be written as

\frac{1}{{{E}}\,[W]}\,{{E}}\,\Bigl[{\rm ln}\Bigl(\frac{e^{tW}+q-1}{q}\Bigr)\Bigr]-\frac{q-1}{2q}\,t\cdot\frac{t}{\gamma}-\frac{t}{q}=0~.

(5)

In Theorem 1.14 of
[
1
]
about the critical inverse temperature
\beta_{c}={\rm ln}(1+\gamma_{c})
, there is considered the function

{\cal K}(t)=\frac{1}{{{E}}\,[W]}\,{{E}}\,\Bigl[{\rm ln}\Bigl(\frac{e^{tW}+q-1}{q}\Bigr)\Bigr]-\frac{q-1}{2q}\,t\,{\cal F}_{0}(t)-\frac{t}{q}~,~~~~~~t\geq 0~,

(6)

so that
\gamma
is eliminated from the left-hand side of (
5
) by using (
3
). By
[
1
]
, Theorem 1.14,
t_{c}
is the unique
t>0
such that
{\cal K}(t)=0
, and

\beta_{c}={\rm ln}\Bigl(1+\frac{t_{c}}{{\cal F}_{0}(t_{c})}\Bigr)~.

(7)

The proof of
[
1
]
, Theorem 1.14 furthermore shows that

{\cal K}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{q-1}{2q}\,[{\cal F}_{0}(t)-t\,{\cal F}_{0}^{\prime}(t)]~,~~~~~~t\geq 0~,

(8)

{\cal K}^{\prime\prime}(t)={-}\,\frac{q-1}{2q}\,t\,{\cal F}_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t)~,~~~~~~t\geq 0~.

(9)

According to
[
1
]
, Section 5.1, for the case
B=0
, there are unique numbers
t_{\ast}
,
t_{b}>0
such that

{\cal F}_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t_{\ast})=0~,~~~~~~{\cal F}_{0}(t_{b})-t_{b}\,{\cal F}_{0}^{\prime}(t_{b})=0~.

(10)

Denoting the unique positive zeros of
{\cal K}
,
{\cal K}^{\prime}
and
{\cal K}^{\prime\prime}
by
t_{c}
,
t_{c}^{\prime}
and
t_{c}^{\prime\prime}
respectively, we thus see that

t_{c}=t_{c}\,,~~t_{c}^{\prime}=t_{b}\,,~~t_{c}^{\prime\prime}=t_{\ast}~;~~~~~~0<t_{c}^{\prime\prime}<t_{c}^{\prime}<t_{c}<\infty~,

(11)

where we also refer to
[
1
]
, Figure 1. Furthermore, we have
{\cal K}(0)={\cal K}^{\prime}(0)={\cal K}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
while
{\cal K}(t)
is negative for small positive
t
and positive for large positive
t
. Therefore,
{\cal K}
,
{\cal K}^{\prime}
and
{\cal K}^{\prime\prime}
are first negative and then positive, and for all
t>0
we have

t<t_{c}\Leftrightarrow{\cal K}(t)<0~;~~~~~~t>t_{c}\Leftrightarrow{\cal K}(t)>0~,

(12)

t<t_{c}^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow{\cal K}^{\prime}(t)<0~;~~~~~~t>t_{c}^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow{\cal K}^{\prime}(t)>0~,

(13)

t<t_{c}^{\prime\prime}\Leftrightarrow{\cal K}^{\prime\prime}(t)<0~;~~~~~~t>t_{c}^{\prime\prime}\Leftrightarrow{\cal K}^{\prime\prime}(t)>0~.

(14)

Main results

We are interested in bounds and approximations of

,
t_{c}^{\prime}
and
t_{c}^{\prime\prime}
in the particular case that

has Pareto density

f_{W}(w)=(\tau-1)\,w^{-\tau}\,{\cal X}_{[1,\infty)}(w)~,~~~~~~w\geq 0,

(15)

with
\tau\geq 4
. The restriction
\tau\geq 4
ensures that
{\cal F}_{0}
in (
1
) and

in (

a unique, positive inflection point

, see
[

]

[

In Section
3

(16)

{\cal K}^{\prime}(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,(q-1)\Bigl(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{\tau-2}{e^{t}+q-1}+(\tau-2)(\tau-3)\,D\Bigr)~,

(17)

{\cal K}^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{(q-1)(\tau-2)}{2t}\,\Bigl(\frac{t\,e^{t}}{(e^{t}+q-1)^{2}}-\frac{\tau-3}{e^{t}+q-1}+(\tau-2)(\tau-3)\,D\Bigr)~,

(18)

(19)

It happens to be a very convenient fact that in (

), (

In Section
4
, we shall show that for all
\tau\geq 4
and

t_{c}<2\,{\rm ln}(q-1)~,~~~~~~t_{c}^{\prime}<\tfrac{3}{2}\,{\rm ln}(q-1)~,~~~~~~~t_{c}^{\prime\prime}<{\rm ln}(q-1)~,

(20)

where we recall that

t_{c}^{\prime\prime}=t_{\ast}<t_{c}^{\prime}=t_{b}<t_{c}~,

(21)

see

]

), (

) and (

), the representations (

), (

) and (

) of

), as a function of

.

In Section
5
, we shall improve the bounds on
t_{c}
and
t_{c}^{\prime}
as follows. We have for
\tau\geq 4
and
q>2

t_{c}<2\,\frac{\tau-2}{\tau-1}\,{\rm ln}(q-1)~.

(22)

The proof of this uses the observation that

\frac{{\cal K}(2\,{\rm ln}(q-1))}{{\cal K}^{\prime}(2\,{\rm ln}(q-1))}=\frac{2\,{\rm ln}(q-1)}{\tau-1}~,

(23)

so that by

and convexity of

Proof of the representations (

), (

) and (

The proof of the representation (

) of

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07164pt{{E}}\,[{\rm ln}(e^{tW}+q-1)]=\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\,(\tau-1)\,w^{-\tau}\,{\rm ln}(e^{tw}+q-1)\,dw

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\frac{d}{dt}\,\left[\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\,\frac{w^{-\tau+1}\,dw}{e^{tw}+q-1}\right]=\frac{d}{dt}\,\left[t^{\tau-2}\,\int\limits_{t}^{\infty}\,\frac{v^{-\tau+1}\,dv}{e^{v}+q-1}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=~(\tau-2)\,t^{\tau-3}\,\int\limits_{t}^{\infty}\,\frac{v^{-\tau+1}\,dv}{e^{v}+q-1}-t^{\tau-2}\,\frac{t^{-\tau+1}}{e^{t}+q-1}

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=~\frac{\tau-2}{t}\,\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\,\frac{w^{-\tau+1}\,dw}{e^{tw}+q-1}-\frac{1}{t(e^{t}+q-1)}=\frac{\tau-2}{t}\,D-\frac{1}{t(e^{t}+q-1)}\,.

4
Proof of the simple bounds in (
20
)

In this section we show that

t_{c}<2\,{\rm ln}(q-1)~,~~~~~~t_{c}^{\prime}<\tfrac{3}{2}\,{\rm ln}(q-1)~,~~~~~~t_{c}^{\prime\prime}<{\rm ln}(q-1)

(38)

for all
\tau\geq 4
and
q>2
. According to the criteria in (
12
), (
13
), (
14
), we have that (
38
) is equivalent with

{\cal K}(2\,{\rm ln}(q-1))>0~,~~~~~~{\cal K}^{\prime}(\tfrac{3}{2}\,{\rm ln}(q-1))>0~,~~~~~~{\cal K}^{\prime\prime}({\rm ln}(q-1))>0~.

(39)

To establish (
39
), we consider for
\alpha\geq 1
and
q>2
,
t=\alpha\,{\rm ln}(q-1)
the expression

(q-1)\,D=(q-1)\,\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\,\frac{w^{-\tau+1}\,dw}{e^{tw}+q-1}=\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\,\frac{w^{-\tau+1}\,dw}{(q-1)^{\alpha w-1}+1}~.

(40)

The function

f(w):=\frac{1}{(q-1)^{\alpha w-1}+1}=\frac{1}{\exp((\alpha w-1)\,{\rm ln}(q-1))+1}

(41)

is positive, increasing and strictly convex in
w>1
. Indeed, we have
\alpha\geq 1
,
{\rm ln}(q-1)>0
and so
z:=(\alpha w-1)\,{\rm ln}(q-1)>0
for
w>1
, while

\Bigl(\frac{1}{e^{z}+1}\Bigr)^{\prime\prime}=\Bigl(\frac{-e^{z}}{(e^{z}+1)^{2}}\Bigr)^{\prime}=e^{z}\,\frac{e^{z}-1}{(e^{z}+1)^{3}}>0~.

(42)

It follows that

f(w)>f(1)+f^{\prime}(1)(w-1)~,~~~~~~w>1~,

(43)

and so, by straightforward integration,

(q-1)\,D>\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\,w^{-\tau+1}(f(1)+f^{\prime}(1)(w-1))\,dw=\frac{f(1)}{\tau-2}+\frac{f^{\prime}(1)}{(\tau-2)(\tau-3)}~.

(44)

Now we show the first inequality in (
39
) for which we make the choice
\alpha=2
. We have from (
16

7
Limiting behaviour of
t_{c}^{\prime\prime}
as
q\downarrow 2

The characterization of
t_{c}^{\prime\prime}
as the unique positive zero
t=t_{\ast}
of
{\cal F}_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t)
gives a convenient handle to study the behaviour of
t_{c}^{\prime\prime}
as
q\downarrow 2
; such a characterization is not available for
t_{c}
or
t_{c}^{\prime}
. By
[
1
]
, Sections 7.2 and 7.3, we have for
\tau\geq 4
that
t_{\ast}
is the unique positive zero
t
of the function

\Phi(t)=\int\limits_{t}^{\infty}\,x^{-\tau+3}\,a(x)\,dx~,

(110)

where

a(x)=\frac{(q-1)\,e^{x}-e^{2x}}{(e^{x}+q-1)^{3}}~,~~~~~~x\geq 0~.

(111)

Note that
a(x)
has exponential decay as
x\rightarrow\infty
, and that for any
x\geq 0

0\leq x<b\Rightarrow a(x)>0~;~~~~~~a(b)=0~;~~~~~~x>b\Rightarrow a(x)<0~,

(112)

where

0<b={\rm ln}(q-1)=q-2+O((q-2)^{2})~.

(113)

Hence,
\Phi(t)<0
for
t\geq b
, and so
t_{c}^{\prime\prime}=t_{\ast}<b={\rm ln}(q-1)
since
\Phi(t_{\ast})=0
.

We write for
t\in(0,b)

\Phi(t)=\int\limits_{t}^{b}\,x^{-\tau+3}\,a(x)\,dx+\int\limits_{b}^{\infty}\,x^{-\tau+3}\,a(x)\,dx=:T_{1}-T_{2}~,

(114)

where
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are positive. We consider this for
b\downarrow 0
, and we should find
t
such that
T_{1}=T_{2}
. We shall solve the equation
T_{1}=T_{2}
approximately by finding the leading-order behaviour of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
as
0<t\leq b
,
b\downarrow 0
. As to
T_{1}
, we use for this

a(x)=\tfrac{1}{8}\,(b-x)+O(b^{2})~,~~~~~~0\leq x\leq b~,

(115)

and we therefore approximate
T_{1}
by

\tfrac{1}{8}\,\int\limits_{t}^{b}\,(b-x)\,x^{-\tau+3}\,dx=\tilde{T}_{1}~.

(116)

As to
T_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\rm ln}(q-1)}^{\infty}\,\frac{e^{2x}-(q-1)\,e^{x}}{(e^{x}+q-1)^{3}}\,x^{-\tau+3}\,dx=\int\limits_{q-1}^{\infty}\,\frac{s-(q-1)}{(s+q-1)^{3}}\,({\rm ln}\,s)^{-\tau+3}\,ds

(117)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\,\frac{v}{(v+2(q-1))^{3}}\,({\rm ln}(v+q-1))^{-\tau+3}\,dv

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int\limits^{\infty}\,\frac{v}{(v+2)^{3}}\,({\rm ln}(1+v+b))^{-\tau+3}\,dv=\tilde{T}_{2}~,

where we have used the substitutions
s=e^{x}\geq q-1
and
v=s-(q-1)\geq 0
, together with (
113
).

We first consider
4\leq\tau<5
. Then we have

\lim_{b\downarrow 0}\,\tilde{T}_{2}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\,\frac{v({\rm ln}(1+v))^{-\tau+3}}{(v+2)^{3}}\,dv=:C(\tau)~,

(118)

where
C(\tau)
is finite and positive. For
\tilde{T}_{1}
we set
t=\alpha b
with
\alpha\in(0,1]
, and we have by the substitution
y=x/b\in[\alpha,1]

\tilde{T}_{1}=\tfrac{1}{8}\,b^{-\tau+5}\,\int\limits_{\alpha}^{1}\,\frac{1-y}{y^{\tau-3}}\,dy~.

(119)

When
\alpha
would be bounded away from 0 as
b\downarrow 0
, we would have
\tilde{T}_{1}=O(b^{-\tau+5})\rightarrow 0
as
b\downarrow 0
while
\tilde{T}_{2}\rightarrow C(\tau)>0
as
b\downarrow 0
. Therefore, we have
\alpha\rightarrow 0
when we equate
\tilde{T}_{1}
and
\tilde{T}_{2}
as
b\downarrow 0
, and then we have

\tilde{T}_{1}=\tfrac{1}{8}\,b^{-\tau+5}\,\int\limits_{\alpha}^{1}\,\frac{dy}{y^{\tau-3}}+O(b^{-\tau+5})~.

(120)

In the case that
\tau=4
, we have

\tilde{T}_{1}=\frac{b}{8}\,{\rm ln}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\alpha}\Bigr)+O(b)~,

(121)

and equating this to
\tilde{T}_{2}
, we get from (
118
) the limiting behaviour

\alpha=\exp({-}8\,C(4)/b)~,~~~~~~t=b\alpha=b\,\exp({-}8\,C(4)/b)~,

(122)

showing exponential decay of
\alpha
and
t
as
b\downarrow 0
.

In the case that
4<\tau<5
, we have

\tilde{T}_{1}=\tfrac{1}{8}\,b^{-\tau+5}\,\frac{(1/\alpha)^{\tau-4}}{\tau-4}+O(b^{-\tau+5})~,

(123)

and equating this to
\tilde{T}_{2}
, we get from (
118

\alpha=(8(\tau-4)\,C(\tau))^{\frac{-1}{\tau-4}}\,b^{\frac{5-\tau}{\tau-4}}~,~~~~~~t=b\alpha=\Bigl(\frac{b}{8(\tau-4)\,C(\tau)}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{\tau-4}}

(124)

b\downarrow 0
.

Next, we consider the case
\tau=5
. Then we have from (
120

\tilde{T}_{1}=\tfrac{1}{8}\,\int\limits_{\alpha}^{1}\,\frac{dy}{y^{2}}+O(1)=\frac{1}{8\alpha}+O(1)~,

(125)

From (
117
), we have from
{\rm ln}(1+v+b)=(v+b)(1+O(v+b))

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}\,\frac{v}{(v+2)^{3}}~\frac{1}{(v+b)^{2}}\,dv+O(1)

(126)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{8}\,\int\limits_{0}^{1}\,\frac{v\,dv}{(v+b)^{2}}+O(1)=\tfrac{1}{8}\,{\rm ln}\Bigl(\frac{1}{b}\Bigr)+O(1)~.

Equating
\tilde{T}_{1}
and
\tilde{T}_{2}

\alpha=\frac{1}{{\rm ln}\Bigl(\frac{1}{b}\Bigr)}~,~~~~~~t=b\alpha=\frac{b}{{\rm ln}\Bigl(\frac{1}{b}\Bigr)}

(127)

b\downarrow 0

We finally consider the case
\tau>5

By partial integration

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\frac{v(v+b)^{-\tau+4}}{-\tau+4}\right|_{0}^{1}+\int\limits_{0}^{1}\,\frac{(v+b)^{-\tau+4}}{\tau-4}\,dv

As to

Not So Flat Metrics
Cristofero S. Fraser-Taliente, Thomas R. Harvey, Manki Kim

Abstract
In order to be in control of the α ′ \alpha^{\prime} derivative expansion, geometric string compactifications are understood in the context of a large volume approximation. In this letter, we consider the reduction of these higher derivative terms, and propose an improved estimate on the large volume approximation using numerical Calabi-Yau metrics obtained via machine learning methods. Further to this, we consider the α ′ 3 \alpha^{\prime 3} corrections to numerical Calabi-Yau metrics in the context of IIB string theory. This correction represents one of several important contributions for realistic string compactifications – alongside, for example, the backreaction of fluxes and local sources – all of which have important consequences for string phenomenology. As a simple application of the corrected metric, we compute the change to the spectrum of the scalar Laplacian.

One therefore needs either to embrace higher derivative corrections or understand precisely when such corrections can be safely ignored in the low energy theory. As we have access to the metric, a refined numerical check can be performed pointwise.

We briefly discuss the choice of units throughout this text. We normalise the worldsheet action as

S=\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{\Sigma}J\,,

(I.1)

where
J

\alpha^{\prime}

l_{s}=2\pi\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}\,.

(I.2)

We then normalise the dimensionless Calabi-Yau volume measured in string units to

\mathcal{V}=\frac{V}{l_{s}^{6}}=\frac{1}{l_{s}^{6}}\int_{CY}\sqrt{g}=\frac{1}{6l_{s}^{6}}\int J\wedge J\wedge J.

(I.3)

In these units, the large volume condition,
V\gg l_{s}^{6}
, is rewritten in terms of the dimensionless volume as

\mathcal{V}\gg 1.

(I.4)

For the purposes of numerical geometry, it is useful to work in units such that the dimensionless volume
\mathcal{V}
is

\mathcal{V}=1\,.

(I.5)

We then analytically keep track of how the various quantities scale with
\mathcal{V}
, allowing us to extrapolate the result to arbitrary volume. One should therefore not be concerned that this is incompatible with equation
I.4

As we will be working with the Fermat quintic throughout, this is equivalent to setting the single Kähler modulus
t

(6/5)^{1/3}

t

(I.6)

and
\mathcal{C}\equiv\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the Hilbert basis element of the Mori cone of the quintic. Again, this is not a concern, as we can always rescale

As mentioned in the previous section, geometric string compactifications are usually understood in the context of an
\alpha^{\prime}
derivative expansion of the 10 dimensional effective field theory. Schematically, the gravity sector Lagrangian from a string theory, where we have set the dilaton to a constant, is of the form

\mathcal{L}\propto R+\sum_{n=L}\alpha^{\prime n}f_{n}(R_{MNPQ}),

(II.1)

where each
f_{n}
is a sum over different contractions of
n+1
copies of the Riemann tensor
R_{MNPQ}
. The exact expressions of these higher order terms, and the order of the leading correction
L
, depend on the particular choice of string theory.

Typically, in a geometric string compactification, one truncates to leading order in
\alpha^{\prime}
, implicitly assuming that the higher order corrections are negligible. CY manifolds satisfy the resulting equations, but despite being Ricci-flat, their Riemann tensor does not vanish, so there is a risk that the higher order corrections could be large. Without access to the metric on the CY manifold, one usually checks the validity of this approximation in the low energy effective theory by confirming that the dimensionful volume of the compact space
V
satisfies
V\gg(2\pi)^{6}\alpha^{\prime 3}
. This is a rather crude check, as it assumes that the curvature is roughly constant across the compact space. Without access to the metric, it is difficult to conceive of any better approach.

Now that numerical Ricci-flat metrics on CY manifolds are available, we can improve on this analysis by considering pointwise checks. If one truncates the theory to
\alpha^{\prime p}
we need only evaluate
f_{n}
to order
\alpha^{\prime p-2n}
. As a result, the simplest pointwise condition is

\alpha^{\prime L}V^{2/6}|f_{L}(R^{0}_{MNPQ})|\ll 1,

(II.2)

or equivalently

(2\pi)^{2}\alpha^{\prime L+1}\mathcal{V}^{2/6}|f_{L}(R^{0}_{MNPQ})|\ll 1

(II.3)

where
R^{0}_{MNPQ}
is the Riemann tensor calculated from the Ricci-flat metric, and the volume of the CY is included to keep the expression dimensionless.

We begin by training a depth-four, width 64 projective neural network, using
cymetric
’s
\phi
-model with
10^{6}
points on the Fermat quintic. We achieve a Monge-Ampère loss of
8\times 10^{-4}
in a matter of minutes, and the trained metric gives a Ricci scalar plotted in figure
1
. It is encouraging to note that after training, the Ricci scalars are distributed nearly normally around zero, with
\sigma=0.5
.

Rather than taking a given string model, we consider some generic higher derivative corrections. In particular, we consider the Kretschmann scalar,

K_{r}=\frac{1}{2\pi}R^{\bar{m}n\bar{p}q}R_{n\bar{m}q\bar{p}},

(II.4)

and

Q=\frac{1}{3(2\pi)^{3}}\left({R}_{a\bar{b}}{}^{c\bar{d}}{R}_{c\bar{d}}{}^{e\bar{f}}{R}_{e\bar{f}}{}^{a\bar{b}}-{R}_{a}{}^{c}{}_{b}{}^{d}{R}_{c}{}^{e}{}_{d}{}^{f}{R}_{e}{}^{a}{}_{f}{}^{b}\right).

(II.5)

K_{r}
occurs as the leading order correction to the Heterotic string, while
Q
, as will be discussed in the next section, is of importance to IIB string theory. The numerical factors have been chosen to match with those that appear in the respective string theories. The histograms for
K_{r}
and
Q
across the Fermat quintic CY are in figures
2
and
3

Without a given string model, we cannot definitively give
\mathcal{V}
a value. However, it is clear that
K_{r}
and
Q
vary across the manifold, even for a manifold as simple as the Fermat quintic. The regions where these are large may violate equation
II.3

III
Computing the Shift to the Metric

Another important property of
\alpha^{\prime}
corrections is the induced modification to the Ricci-flat metric. To explore this, we now turn to the specific
\alpha^{\prime}
corrections that occur in IIB string theory, and calculate the resulting change to the metric
Becker:2002nn
;
Becker:2015bra
. It turns out that this correction to the metric is very amenable to being calculated with machine learning methods.

From the worldsheet perspective, to leading order in
\alpha^{\prime}
, the metric
\tilde{G}
of the compact space
X
satisfies (in complex coordinates
z^{m},\overline{z}^{\overline{m}}
):

\displaystyle\beta^{G}_{mn}

\displaystyle=R_{mn}=0,

(III.1)

\displaystyle\beta^{G}_{\overline{mn}}

\displaystyle=R_{\overline{mn}}=0,

(III.2)

\displaystyle\beta^{G}_{m\overline{n}}

\displaystyle=R_{m\overline{n}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\partial_{m}\partial_{\overline{n}}{\rm Tr}(\log\tilde{G})=0.

(III.3)

As a result
\tilde{G}
is Kähler and Ricci flat,

Continuing to the next order in
\alpha^{\prime}
, the purely holomorphic and anti-holomorphic beta functions remain zero, but the mixed terms are corrected by

\frac{1}{2\pi}\partial_{m}\partial_{\overline{n}}(\log(G))+\frac{(2\pi\alpha^{\prime})^{3}}{8\pi}\zeta(3)\partial_{m}\partial_{\overline{n}}Q(G)=0,

(III.4)

where
G
is the corrected metric, and
Q
is the 6D Euler density for Ricci-flat manifolds

\int_{X}\text{dVol}_{\tilde{G}}Q({\tilde{G}})=\chi.

(III.5)

For Kähler manifolds, this is given by equation
II.5
.
To proceed, we make the following ansatz:

\frac{1}{2\pi}(\log(G))+\frac{(2\pi\alpha^{\prime})^{3}}{8\pi}\zeta(3)Q(G)=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\log(\tilde{G}))+f(z)+g(\overline{z}),

(III.6)

where
f
and
g
are arbitrary holomorphic and anti-holomorphic functions respectively. Clearly, if true, the above equation implies (
III.4
). We also make the assumption that
f(z)+g(\overline{z})=\gamma
, where
\gamma
is some constant to be determined – this turns out to be sufficient to solve (
III.4
).

We expand the metric around the Ricci-flat metric
G_{m\overline{n}}=\tilde{G}_{m\overline{n}}+\alpha^{\prime 3}\delta G_{m\overline{n}}
, where the correction is
\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\prime}}^{3})
. The correction to the Kähler potential
\delta K=(K-\tilde{K})/\alpha^{\prime 3}
, to leading order in
\alpha^{\prime}
, satisfies

\alpha^{\prime 3}\tilde{\triangle}\delta K=-\frac{(2\pi\alpha^{\prime})^{3}}{4}\zeta(3)Q(\tilde{G})-(2\pi)\gamma+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{\prime 4}).

(III.7)

We will drop the
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{\prime 4})
terms from now on, leaving them as understood.

As
X
is compact, standard PDE theory requires that the source term must integrate to zero for a solution to exist. This leads to a condition on
\gamma
. Since
Q
is the Euler density, we need to impose

\gamma=-\frac{(2\pi\alpha^{\prime})^{3}}{8\pi V}\zeta(3)\chi,

(III.8)

where
V=\int_{X}(\det{\tilde{G}})
is the volume of the Ricci-flat Calabi-Yau.

Putting the above together, the deviation from the Ricci-flat Kähler potential is, cancelling
\alpha^{\prime 3}
on both sides, given by the solution to

\tilde{\triangle}\delta K=\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{4}\zeta(3)\left(\frac{\chi}{l_{s}^{6}\mathcal{V}}-Q(\tilde{G})\right)\equiv\rho.

(III.9)

where

\braket{f|g}=\int_{X}\text{dVol}_{\tilde{G}}\bar{f}g,

V
Conclusion

In summary, this letter consisted of two parts, both of which concern numerical methods and
\alpha^{\prime}
corrections to string compactifications. The first was covered in section
II
, where we highlight the use of numerical metrics in confirming control of the
\alpha^{\prime}
expansion for string compactifications: one can use numerical metrics to check conditions of the form given in equation
II.3
. We claim these are stronger pointwise constraints than the usually considered
V\gg(2\pi)^{6}\alpha^{\prime 3}
. In principle, these constraints should be checked in any string model, especially in the context of moduli stabilisation, where a specific point in moduli space has been chosen.

In the second part, we computed one of the many relevant corrections to numerical CY metrics: the change to the metric from the first
\alpha^{\prime}
correction, at tree-level in
g_{st}
, for IIB string theory in section
III
. As a simple example of the use of this new metric, in section
IV
we calculate the correction to the eigenvalues of the scalar Laplacian on the quintic, as indicated in figure
5
and in table
1
.

This work initiates the use of taking machine learning methods for numerical metrics beyond the Ricci-flat metric, by considering one of the many corrections that need to be considered in a realistic string compactification. Looking forward, it is crucial to begin considering the backreaction from fluxes and local sources on these numerical metrics. These are important considerations both for realising the standard model within string theory, and for moduli stabilisation.

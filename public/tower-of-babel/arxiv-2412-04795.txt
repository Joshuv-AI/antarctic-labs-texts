Ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of lengths 36 36 , 48 48 and 60 60
Masaaki Harada

Abstract
For lengths 36 36 , 48 48 and 60 60 , we construct new ternary near-extremal self-dual codes with weight enumerators for which no ternary near-extremal self-dual codes were previously known to exist.



1
Introduction

Self-dual codes are one of the most interesting classes of codes.
This interest is justified by many combinatorial objects
and algebraic objects related to self-dual codes
(see e.g.,
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
).

A ternary code
C
of length
n
is said to be
self-dual
if

C=C^{\perp}
, where
C^{\perp}
is the dual code of
C
.
The minimum weight
d
of a ternary self-dual code of length
n
is bounded by

d\leq 3\lfloor n/12\rfloor+3

[
4
]
.
A ternary self-dual code of length
n
and minimum weight
3\lfloor n/12\rfloor+3

is called
extremal
.
A ternary self-dual code of length
n
and minimum weight

3\lfloor n/12\rfloor
is called
near-extremal
.
The sparsity of ternary extremal self-dual codes of lengths divisible by
12

has recently aroused interest in ternary near-extremal self-dual codes
(see e.g.,
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
).
In particular, some restrictions on the weight enumerators of ternary near-extremal
self-dual codes of length divisible by
12
were given in
[
1
]
.
It is a fundamental problem to determine
the weight enumerators for which there is a ternary near-extremal
self-dual code of length divisible by
12
.
In this note, we give a method for constructing ternary self-dual codes
based on negacirculant matrices.
Using the method,
we construct new ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of lengths
36
,
48
and
60

with weight enumerators for
which no ternary near-extremal self-dual codes were previously known to exist.

This note is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we give some definitions and basic results.
In Section
3
, we give a method for constructing ternary self-dual codes
of lengths divisible by
12
.
This method is based on negacirculant matrices.
We also give some basic properties of ternary self-dual codes constructed by this method.
In Section
4
,
our exhaustive search found all distinct ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of length
36

constructed by the method given in Section
3
.
Then we found ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of length
36
with weight enumerators for
which no ternary near-extremal self-dual codes were previously known to exist.
Similarly, in Section
5
,
we found many ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of lengths
48
and
60

with weight enumerators for
which no ternary near-extremal self-dual codes were previously known to exist.
Finally, in Section
6
, we give a correction of
[
1
, Proposition 4.2]

which gave weight enumerators for
which ternary near-extremal self-dual codes of length
72
were known to exist.

All computer calculations in this note were done using programs in

Magma

[
2
]
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we give some definitions and basic results used in this note.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{3}=\{0,1,2\}
denote the finite field of order
3
.
A
ternary

[n,k]

code

C
is a
k
-dimensional vector subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
.
In this note, codes mean ternary codes.
The parameter
n
is called the
length
of
C
.
A
generator matrix
of a ternary
[n,k]
code
C
is a
k\times n

matrix such that the rows of the matrix generate
C
.
The
weight

\wt(x)
of a vector
x\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
is the number of nonzero components of
x
.
A vector of a code
C
is called a
codeword
of
C
.
The minimum non-zero weight of all codewords in
C
is called
the
minimum weight
of
C
.
The
weight enumerator
of
C
is given by
\sum_{c\in C}y^{\wt(c)}
.
Two codes
C
and
C^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if there is a
monomial matrix
P
over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
with
C^{\prime}=C\cdot P
,
where
C\cdot P=\{xP\mid x\in C\}
.
We denote two equivalent codes
C
and
C^{\prime}
by
C\cong C^{\prime}
.

The
dual
code
C^{\perp}
of a ternary code
C
of length
n

is defined as:

C^{\perp}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}\mid\langle x,y\rangle=0\text{ for all }y\in C\},

where
\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}{y_{i}}

for
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
.
A ternary code
C
is said to be
self-dual
if
C=C^{\perp}
.
Every codeword of a ternary self-dual code has weight a multiple of
3
.
A ternary self-dual code of length
n
exists
if and only if
n\equiv 0\pmod{4}
with
n>0

[
4
]
.
The minimum weight
d
of a ternary self-dual code of length
n
is bounded by

d\leq 3\lfloor n/12\rfloor+3

[
4
]
.
A ternary self-dual code of length
n
and minimum weight
3\lfloor n/12\rfloor+3

is called
extremal
.
A ternary self-dual code of length
n
and minimum weight

3\lfloor n/12\rfloor
is called
near-extremal
.

3
A method for constructing self-dual codes

In this section, we give a method for constructing ternary self-dual codes.
We also give some basic properties of the method.

Throughout this note,
let
I_{n}
denote the identity matrix of order
n
,
let
O_{n}
denote the
n\times n
zero matrix
and let

A^{T}
denote the transpose of a matrix
A
.
An
n\times n

negacirculant
matrix has the following form:

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}r_{0}&r_{1}&r_{2}&\cdots&r_{n-2}&r_{n-1}\\
2r_{n-1}&r_{0}&r_{1}&\cdots&r_{n-3}&r_{n-2}\\
2r_{n-2}&2r_{n-1}&r_{0}&\cdots&r_{n-4}&r_{n-3}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
2r_{1}&2r_{2}&2r_{3}&\cdots&2r_{n-1}&r_{0}\end{array}\right).

In this note, we consider ternary
[6n,3n]
codes with the following generator matrices:

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc@{}c}&{\Large I_{3n}}&&\begin{array}[]{ccc}M_{1,1}&M_{1,2}&M_{1,3}\\
M_{2,1}&M_{2,2}&M_{2,3}\\
M_{3,1}&M_{3,2}&M_{3,3}\\
\end{array}\end{array}\right),

(1)

where
M_{i,j}
are
n\times n
negacirculant matrices
(i,j\in\{1,2,3\})
.
We denote the above ternary code by

C(M_{1,1},M_{1,2},M_{1,3},M_{2,1},M_{2,2},M_{2,3},M_{3,1},M_{3,2},M_{3,3}).

Let
r_{i,j}
denote the first row of
M_{i,j}

(i,j\in\{1,2,3\})
.
We denote the vector
(r_{i,1},r_{i,2},r_{i,3})
of length
3n
by

r_{i}

(i\in\{1,2,3\})
.
We also denote the above ternary code by
C(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
.

The following four lemmas are easily obtained by
multiplying some rows and columns by
2
,
and permuting some rows and columns of the generator matrix (
1
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For any vector
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in\{1,2\}^{3}
,

\displaystyle C(M_{1,1},M_{1,2},M_{1,3},M_{2,1},M_{2,2},M_{2,3},M_{3,1},M_{3,2},M_{3,3})

\displaystyle\cong C(a_{1}M_{1,1},a_{2}M_{1,2},a_{3}M_{1,3},a_{1}M_{2,1},a_{2}M_{2,2},a_{3}M_{2,3},a_{1}M_{3,1},a_{2}M_{3,2},a_{3}M_{3,3}).

Lemma 3.2
.

For any vector
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in\{1,2\}^{3}
,

\displaystyle C(M_{1,1},M_{1,2},M_{1,3},M_{2,1},M_{2,2},M_{2,3},M_{3,1},M_{3,2},M_{3,3})

\displaystyle\cong C(a_{1}M_{1,1},a_{1}M_{1,2},a_{1}M_{1,3},a_{2}M_{2,1},a_{2}M_{2,2},a_{2}M_{2,3},a_{3}M_{3,1},a_{3}M_{3,2},a_{3}M_{3,3}).

Lemma 3.3
.

For any permutation
\sigma
of the symmetry group of degree
3
,

\displaystyle C(M_{1,1},M_{1,2},M_{1,3},M_{2,1},M_{2,2},M_{2,3},M_{3,1},M_{3,2},M_{3,3})

\displaystyle\cong C(M_{1,\sigma(1)},M_{1,\sigma(2)},M_{1,\sigma(3)},M_{2,\sigma(1)},M_{2,\sigma(2)},M_{2,\sigma(3)},M_{3,\sigma(1)},M_{3,\sigma(2)},M_{3,\sigma(3)}).

Lemma 3.4
.

For any permutation
\sigma
of the symmetry group of degree
3
,

\displaystyle C(M_{1,1},M_{1,2},M_{1,3},M_{2,1},M_{2,2},M_{2,3},M_{3,1},M_{3,2},M_{3,3})

\displaystyle\cong C(M_{\sigma(1),1},M_{\sigma(1),2},M_{\sigma(1),3},M_{\sigma(2),1},M_{\sigma(2),2},M_{\sigma(2),3},M_{\sigma(3),1},M_{\sigma(3),2},M_{\sigma(3),3}).

Let
C
be a
[2n,n]
code with generator matrix
G
.
It is trivial that
C
is self-dual if and only if
GG^{T}=O_{n}
.
The following lemma is easily obtained from the above fact.

Lemma 3.5
.

A ternary
[6n,3n]
code

C(M_{1,1},M_{1,2},M_{1,3},M_{2,1},M_{2,2},M_{2,3},M_{3,1},M_{3,2},M_{3,3})

is self-dual
if and only if

\displaystyle M_{i,1}M_{i,1}^{T}+M_{i,2}M_{i,2}^{T}+M_{i,3}M_{i,3}^{T}

\displaystyle=2I_{n}\ (i\in\{1,2,3\})\text{ and }

\displaystyle M_{i,1}M_{j,1}^{T}+M_{i,2}M_{j,2}^{T}+M_{i,3}M_{j,3}^{T}

\displaystyle=O_{n}\ (i,j\in\{1,2,3\},i<j).

Since a ternary self-dual code of length
n
exists
if and only if
n\equiv 0\pmod{4}
with
n>0

[
4
]
,
the above method is applied to self-dual codes of length
n\equiv 0\pmod{12}
with
n>0
.
In order to find all ternary self-dual codes
C(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})

of length
12m
and minimum weight
d

having generator matrices (
1
),
it is sufficient to consider all vectors

r_{1}=(r_{1,1},r_{1,2},r_{1,3}),r_{2}=(r_{2,1},r_{2,2},r_{2,3})\text{ and }r_{3}=(r_{3,1},r_{3,2},r_{3,3})

satisfying the following conditions:

(C1)

\wt(r_{i})\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
\wt(r_{i})\geq d-1

(i\in\{1,2,3\})
.

(C2)

The first nonzero component of
r_{1,j}
is
1

(j\in\{1,2,3\})
by Lemma
3.1
.

(C3)

The first nonzero component of
r_{i}
is
1

(i\in\{2,3\})
by Lemma
3.2
.

(C4)

For
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell})\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{\ell}
, define a map
f
from
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{\ell}
to

\mathbb{Z}
as

f(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}3^{i-1}a_{i}
, regarding
a_{i}=0,1
or
2
as an element of
\mathbb{Z}
.
For
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{\ell}
,
we define an ordering
a\geq b
based on the natural ordering of
\mathbb{Z}

as
f(a)\geq f(b)
.
Then
f(r_{1,1})\geq f(r_{1,2})\geq f(r_{1,3})
by Lemma
3.3
.

(C5)

f(r_{1})\geq f(r_{2})\geq f(r_{3})
by Lemma
3.4
.

(C6)

Let
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
be the codes generated by the rows of the following matrices:

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}M_{1,1}&M_{1,2}&M_{1,3}\\
\end{array}\right)\text{ and }\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}M_{1,1}&M_{1,2}&M_{1,3}\\
M_{2,1}&M_{2,2}&M_{2,3}\\
\end{array}\right),

respectively.
Then
r_{2}\in D_{1}^{\perp}
and
r_{3}\in D_{2}^{\perp}
by Lemma
3.5
.

To demonstrate a usefulness of this method,
we found some ternary extremal self-dual codes
C(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
.
Under the conditions (C1)–(C6), our exhaustive computer search found
all distinct ternary extremal self-dual codes
C(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})

of length
36
.
Then we verified that every ternary extremal self-dual code
C(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})

of length
36
is equivalent to
C_{36}=C(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
, where

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle=(0,0,0,0,1,2,0,1,1,1,0,2,1,2,1,0,2,1),

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle=(1,0,1,1,1,0,1,2,2,0,2,1,2,2,1,2,0,1)\text{ and }

\displaystyle r_{3}

\displaystyle=(0,1,2,1,2,1,0,2,2,2,1,2,1,0,1,2,1,0).

For length
36
, the Pless symmetry code
P_{36}
is a currently known ternary extremal self-dual code.
We verified that
C_{36}\cong P_{36}
.
For length
48
,
the extended quadratic residue
QR_{48}
and the Pless symmetry code
P_{48}

are currently known ternary extremal self-dual codes.
We found two ternary extremal self-dual codes
C_{48}=C(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
and

C^{\prime}_{48}=C(r^{\prime}_{1},r^{\prime}_{2},r^{\prime}_{3})
, where

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle=(1,1,0,2,0,1,2,1,0,1,0,0,2,2,0,1,0,1,0,0,1,1,0,1),

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle=(1,0,2,0,1,0,0,2,0,2,0,0,1,2,2,1,0,0,2,1,1,2,2,0),

\displaystyle r_{3}

\displaystyle=(1,0,0,1,1,0,1,0,1,2,2,0,0,0,1,2,0,0,1,2,1,2,1,0),

\displaystyle r^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=(1,0,1,2,0,0,1,1,0,1,0,1,0,0,1,1,1,2,0,2,0,2,2,0),

\displaystyle r^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=(1,0,1,0,0,1,1,0,2,1,2,0,1,2,0,2,2,1,2,1,2,1,2,0)\text{ and }

\displaystyle r^{\prime}_{3}

\displaystyle=(0,1,0,1,1,0,1,2,2,1,2,1,2,1,0,2,2,0,2,2,0,1,2,0).

We verified that
C_{48}\cong QR_{48}
and
C^{\prime}_{48}\cong P_{48}
.

\begin{split}W_{36}=&1+\alpha y^{9}+(42840-9\alpha)y^{12}+(1400256+36\alpha)y^{15}\\

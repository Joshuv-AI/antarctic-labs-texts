Research Progress and Future Directions in Immune Cell Crosstalk in the Esophageal Cancer: A Bibliometric Analysis
Authors: Chengkai Zhang, Bowen Dong, Min Liu, Jiangnan Duan, Bengang Hui, Bowei Qiao, Rongxin Shang, Na Zhang, Xiaolong Yan, Runmin Jiang, Jie Lei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe complexity of immune cell crosstalk plays a key role in immunotherapy resistance for esophageal cancer, while systematically bibliometric analysis in this field to describe knowledge structure, hotspots and identify future research trends remains lacking.MethodsWe retrieved publications on immune cell crosstalk in esophageal cancer from the Web of Science Core Collection, covering 2007 to 2025. A total of 510 articles and reviews were analyzed with CiteSpace and VOSviewer to assess publication trends, collaborative networks, keyword co‑occurrence, citation bursts, and research hotspots.ResultsResearch output has grown sharply since 2020. China, the United States, and Japan are the top three countries by productivity. Keyword analysis showed a progression from basic mechanisms such as apoptosis and the NF-κB pathway toward immunotherapy combinations, tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), and cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs). Crosstalk networks involving T cells and macrophages are now relatively well understood, whereas interactions between regulatory T cells (Tregs) and natural killer cells (NK cells) remain poorly defined. In addition, most studies have focused on chemo-immunotherapy, leaving the cellular crosstalk under radiotherapy-based treatments largely unstudied.ConclusionOur findings suggest that future research may benefit from prioritizing the study of Tregs and NK cells crosstalk in patients receiving radiotherapy-based treatments, and from examining their interactions with other cells, particularly TAMs and CAFs.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) is one of the common malignant tumors worldwide, causing approximately 300,000 deaths each year.1 According to the 2022 Global Cancer Statistics Report, esophageal cancer ranks 11th in incidence and 7th in mortality among all cancers.2 In China and East Asia, the prognosis of esophageal cancer remains poorer, largely because most patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage when curative treatment is no longer feasible.3,4 While conventional therapies such as chemotherapy and radiotherapy offer limited benefit, immunotherapy has significantly improved survival outcomes.5 In trials like KEYNOTE-181 and ATTRACTION-3, PD-1/PD-L1 blockade demonstrated efficacy in advanced EC with a 12-month overall survival rate of 43% versus 20% in control arms and a sustained reduction in death risk.6 However, a significant proportion of patients remain resistant to immunotherapy. Thus, a depth exploration of the underlying mechanisms of such insensitivity is a crucial step in enhancing the efficacy of immunotherapy.7

Immune cells’ crosstalk contribute significantly to immunotherapy resistance. For instance, regulatory T cells (Tregs) not only block the CD80/CD86 signals of dendritic cells (DCs) through CTLA-4, but also directly inhibit the activation and proliferation of CD8+ T cells by competitively consuming glucose and IL-2 and upregulating anti-apoptotic protein ICOS,8–10 M2-macrophages secrete IL-10, activating the JAK1-STAT3 signaling pathway in DCs, thereby suppressing the antigen-presenting and remodeling a immunosuppressive microenvironment in concert with CST1+ myofibroblasts,8–11 Myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) secrete arginase-1 (Arg-1), inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS), reactive oxygen species (ROS), and cytokines like TGF-β and IL-10 to exhaust the effector T cells.12 Indeed, immune cells are highly specialized with substantial heterogeneity. Despite growing interest in this area, previous studies have offered limited insights, far from a systematic comprehensive analysis of these interactions.13

Bibliometrics enables systematic analysis of research through quantitative and visual approaches, helping identify research trends, knowledge patterns, and hotspots in specific fields. However, previous literature metrics in the field of esophageal cancer have mainly focused on areas immunotherapy, programmed cell death, and the application of nanotechnology.14–16 Accordingly, we performed a bibliometric analysis of the past two decades of literature on immune cells’ crosstalk in EC to identify key cooperative interactions, research hotspots, and frontiers, providing evidence-based guidance for future research and clinical translation.

Materials and Methods

Search Strategies and Data Collection

Literatures were obtained from the Web of Science Core Collection database.17 In order to accurately collect relevant data on the relationship between immune cells and EC, we adopted a search method of pairing immune cells in pairs, including T cells, macrophages, neutrophils, natural killer cells (NK cells) and fibroblasts. Using advanced search terms, the specific search is as follows: TS=((esophageal cancer OR oesophageal cancer OR esophageal carcinoma OR oesophageal carcinoma) AND any combination of two cell types (T cells/T lymphocyte, macrophages, neutrophils, NK cells/natural killer cell, and fibroblasts/cancer-associated fibroblast/CAF)). The full Boolean search strings are provided in the Supplementary File (Section 1).

A total of 770 documents were retrieved from the Web Of Science Core Collection database. Using the thesaurus file in VOSviewer, we consolidated variants of esophageal cancer, cancer-associated fibroblasts, tumor-associated macrophages, NK cells, and T cells, among others. We also removed broad terms such as “cells”, “cancer”, “expression”, “study”, and “method” Supplementary File (Section 2). After initial deduplication of the retrieved records, three researchers independently screened the remaining publications by title and abstract, followed by full-text review for those passing the initial screen. Disagreements were resolved through discussion with two senior authors until consensus was reached. Further processing with CiteSpace, including removal of conference abstracts and other non-compliant types, yielded 510 publications for final analysis (Figure 1).Figure 1Flow chart of data collection in this study.A flowchart of study strategy for data collection, exclusions and analysis of 510 records.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Inclusion criteria as follows: (a) studies involving the immune microenvironment of esophageal cancer; (b) studies exploring the interaction or crosstalk mechanisms between immune cells; (c) studies containing experimental or theoretical immunotherapy strategies; (d) literature types include articles and reviews. Exclusion criteria as follows: (a) non-English literature; (b) literature whose full text could not be obtained; (c) literature that was obviously irrelevant to the topic; (d) case reports or simple case analyses. Specifically, this obviously irrelevant category included studies not centered on EC, those lacking any form of immune cell interaction.

Bibliometric Analysis Methods and Visualization Software

The bibliometric tools employed in this study for analysis are CiteSpace (version 6.3) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.20). CiteSpace is a Java-based application freely available for the analysis and visualization of trends and patterns within scientific literature. It serves to map the structure and distribution of scientific knowledge, with a particular emphasis on identifying critical points in the development of a field.18 In this study, CiteSpace was mainly used to analyze the evolution of research trends, detect the emergence of key words, and identify research frontiers. VOSviewer is a software tool designed for bibliometric analysis and knowledge mapping. It supports various analytical techniques, such as co-citation analysis, and bibliographic coupling, and enables users to construct and visualize resulting networks.19 In this study, VOSviewer was used to construct the collaboration networks and co-occurrence diagrams for countries, institutions and keywords.

Results

General Properties and Annual Output

From 2007 to 2025, a total of 510 publications related to immune cells and esophageal cancer were included. 3844 authors from 752 institutions in 43 countries and regions published these papers. All the involved publications consisted of articles (n=447, 91.22%) and reviews (n=43, 8.78%). The number of authors and institutions reflects the large base of researchers involved in this field, the wide participation of institutions, and the active independent or collaborative research. The overall publication volume shows an increasing trend. The most productive year was 2022, with 73 articles published. Annual publications remained low and stable at around 5–10 articles per year between 2007 and 2015, with the lowest output occurring in 2007 and 2009, followed by a significant increase since 2020 (Figure 2).Figure 2Global annual output trends.A line graph showing publications by year from 2007 to 2025, ranging from 5 to 73.

Immune Cell Crosstalk Deficiency in Patients with Treatments Based on Radiotherapy

To map evolving research trends, we performed keyword co-occurrence and burst analyses. From 2,291 initial keywords, the most frequent keywords included “esophageal cancer”, “tumor-associated macrophage” and “tumor microenvironment” (Table 7). After filtering out the keywords that appeared at least 15 times from the processed data, a co-occurrence network and frequency heatmap was constructed (Figure 5A and B). Figure 5A illustrates the keyword co-occurrence network, visualizing the structural associations among research themes through node connectivity. The landscape were mainly divided into three domains, including immune cells connections centered on “t cells” and “tumor-associated macrophage”, therapeutic interventions driven by “immunotherapy” and “chemotherapy”, and prognostic outcomes focusing on the “metastasis” and “survival”. Complementing this, Figure 5B presents a keyword density heatmap that reveals a concentrated distribution of research efforts within these core domains. Within the immune cell cluster, terms like “nk cells” and “regulatory t-cells” sit in lower density regions, and the treatment cluster shows a similar pattern, with “radiotherapy” and “chemoradiotherapy” also occupying marginal positions. These areas have clearly been recognized, but they remain less explored compared to the main research streams. Keyword burst analysis further uncovered a clear shift in research focus (Figure 5C). From 2009 to 2013, bursts in “apoptosis”, “natural killer cells”, and “expression” highlighted an emphasis on fundamental tumor biology, particularly programmed cell death and innate immune recognition. By 2015–2020, attention broadened to “lymphocytes”, “activation”, “macrophages”, and “inflammation”, marking a turn toward understanding the complex cellular interactions. As shown in Figure 7, the results highlight notable gaps of various immune cell types. Discussion

Our bibliometric analysis shows that since 2020, the surge in publications reflects a growing research interest and a new direction in tumor biology, with immune cell crosstalk moving from a peripheral concern to a central focus.27 Despite the success of PD-1 blockade in some patients, many do not respond or eventually develop resistance. Researches show that tumors are no longer regarded as isolated aggregates of malignant cells but rather driven by continuous crosstalk among cancer cells, immune cells, and stromal cells.28 The efficacy of PD-1 inhibitors depends not on a single cell type but on the crosstalk among multiple immune and stromal populations. Concurrently, therapeutic strategies have progressed beyond direct cytotoxicity to approaches that systematically modulate intercellular signaling via immune checkpoint blockade, stromal remodeling, or metabolic intervention to restore potent antitumor immunity.29 Single-cell transcriptomics have identified CAFs subtypes in ESCC that correlate with poor immunotherapy response. These CAFs remodel the matrix and secrete TGF-β and IL-6, which restrain CD8+ T cell function. Concurrently, tumor-derived TGF-β polarizes macrophages toward an M2 phenotype, further reducing CD8+ infiltration.30,31 Among T cells, CD160 loss marks terminal exhaustion and accelerates progression, whereas TCF-1+PD-1+ stem like CD8+ T cells predict favorable PD-1 blockade outcomes.32,33 The sensitivity to PD-1 inhibitors is collectively constrained by CAFs, M2 macrophages, and exhausted CD8+ subsets. Given the complexity of cellular crosstalk, we used co-citation and co-occurrence analysis to explore the state of research attention and reveal two clear bibliographic gaps, the few literature on Tregs-NK cells crosstalk, and the even fewer on immune interactions after radiotherapy. These gaps may lead to a disconnect between laboratory insights and clinical application and need further investigations.

Need for Innovation in Research Cooperation Patterns

The dominance of China in publication output and the strong regional clustering of institutions reflect a direct response to the high disease burden in East Asia, where ESCC is especially common.34 ESCC accounts for the majority of esophageal cancer cases in China, with incidence rates substantially higher than in Western populations. This regional distribution of disease has concentrated research efforts and resources, driving rapid progress, particularly in defining the baseline immune landscape. This concentration of resources has driven rapid progress, particularly in defining the baseline immune landscape of EC. However, this cooperative network reveals potential limitations. Although the strong intra-regional cooperative connections are efficient, they may restrict the cross-influence of research ideas with other units internationally. Chinese institutions publish the most papers, but in citation impact they are comparable to top US centers like MD Anderson. The reason for this situation might be related to US journals generally have higher impact factors, and many key US studies came out earlier, so they have had longer to collect citations. In addition, China’s publication output has grown so fast in recent years that the average citation per paper is naturally lowered, even when the overall contribution is large. Future progress will likely depend on bridging these regional silos to combine large scale patient cohorts with innovative mechanistic insights from around the world.

CAFs and TAMs as Hotspots

Our citation burst analysis confirmed the recent surge in studies on CAFs and TAMs signaling, driven by the widespread application of single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics, reflecting a growing recognition that stromal cells are not merely passive scaffolds but active drivers of immunosuppression.35–37 CAFs are known to derive from several cellular sources. These include the activation of tissue-resident fibroblasts, the differentiation of bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells, and the mesenchymal transition of epithelial or endothelial cells. In terms of activation, both growth factors such as TGF-β, PDGF and FGF, and dysregulated signaling through STAT3 and ERK pathways have been implicated as critical drivers. Activated CAFs serve as key drivers in establishing a fibrotic and immunosuppressive tumor micro-enviroment (TME).38–43 Researches have shown that periostin secreted by CAFs not only directly promotes tumor cell migration but also enhances macrophage motility and confers TAMs-like properties.35

The Underexplored Role of Radiotherapy in Immune Cells’ Crosstalk

Moreover, research into keyword clustering and hotspot analysis is directed toward clinical therapeutic applications. The understanding of immune cell interactions in patients undergoing chemotherapy and immunotherapy was analyzed to be relatively clear. By contrast, our comprehension of these same processes in patients receiving radiotherapy or chemoradiotherapy remains limited. In fact, radiotherapy has been shown to uniquely reshape the TME, potentially releasing tumor antigens and reshaping the cytokine landscape. According to recent studies, radiotherapy can turn a cold TME into a hot one.44 For instance, it can induce immunogenic cell death in tumor cells, thereby enhancing the immunogenicity of tumor antigens, recruiting and activating antigen-presenting cells, and promoting the infiltration of cytotoxic T lymphocytes into tumors to eliminate cancer cells.44,45 Of note, radiotherapy has two opposing effects. It activates effector cells, but it may also increase PD-1 and CTLA-4 on Tregs or recruit myeloid-derived suppressor cells, strengthening immunosuppression.46 Thus, there is a critical need for further studies. Overlooking the ways in which these changes remodel the entire cellular network may cause us to miss key mechanisms underlying both resistance and sensitivity. And during radiotherapy combined with immunotherapy, lymphocyte dynamics and radiation dose to immune cells each correlate with patient outcomes. Radiotherapy sets off a mix of cooperative and opposing responses across different immune cells. Sorting out these immune cells crosstalk in the TME after treatment is essential for better clinical strategies.47,48

Limited Understanding of Tregs and NK Cells Crosstalk

In keyword network analysis, interactions involving T cells and macrophages are analyzed to be relatively well-characterized. For example, macrophages recruiting and activating CD8+ T cells through CCL5 secretion, with activated T cells potentially modulating macrophage function in return.37 However, the crosstalk between Tregs and NK cells remains poorly understood, with interactions that are limited. Actually, Tregs promote tumor immune evasion by secreting immunosuppressive cytokines such as IL-10, TGF-β, and IL-35, depleting IL-2, expressing inhibitory molecules like CTLA-4, and directly suppressing the effector functions of NK cells, CD8+ T cells, and Th1 cells.49 Recent preclinical studies show that radiotherapy combined with PD-L1 inhibitors can broadly reshape the immune microenvironment. However, treatment efficacy is closely tied to the ratio of tumor-infiltrating CD8+ T cells to Tregs, suggesting that the functional state of Tregs may be the limitations of the response to radiotherapy combined with immunotherapy.50 As integral effectors of the innate immune system, NK cells are also essential for recognizing and eliminating tumor cells, virally infected cells, and cells under stress.51 The two cell types are sensitive to radiotherapy. Radiotherapy induces intratumoral expansion of mature cDC1s, which in turn secrete the Treg-recruiting chemokine CCL22, thereby promoting Tregs accumulation within the tumor.52 Although radiotherapy directly impairs NK cell numbers and function, it simultaneously enhances tumor immunogenicity by upregulating NK cell-activating ligands and HLA-ABC on tumor cells, releasing damage-associated molecular patterns, and promoting NK cell infiltration into the TME.53,54 In addition, chemokines released from radiotherapy-induced tumor cell death, such as CCL5 and CXCL10, recruit Tregs and NK cells in distinct ways. This differential recruitment points to a new direction worth exploring.54 Therefore, future investigations into Tregs and NK cells are likely to emerge as hotspots, particularly in subgroup analyses of patients undergoing treatments based on radiotherapy.

Given the scarce understanding of mechanisms by which radiation shapes cellular crosstalk within the TME, particularly the poorly characterized interaction network between Tregs and NK cells, future research should prioritize patients undergoing treatments based on radiotherapy to explore these crosstalk. A critical next step is to map the interplay of Tregs and NK cells with the stromal niche, particularly TAMs and CAFs. Explore these specific connections could uncover the mechanisms driving tumor radio-resistance and immune evasion. Shifting the focus from the T cells and macrophages toward these overlooked compartments may reveal new targets for combination therapies.

Collectively, we have identified the key players and mapped their interactions of immune cells in EC. But the map is incomplete. The overfocus on chemo-immunotherapy and the neglect of radiotherapy-induced networks, combined with the poor understanding of Tregs cell and NK cells connectivity, represents a significant barrier to progress. Addressing these unresolved questions is a crucial step toward optimizing clinical therapeutic strategies and improving patient outcomes.

Limitation

This study has several limitations that should be considered. Firstly, the source of the data is limited. Only the Web of Science Core Collection database was retrieved, and other databases such as Scopus and PubMed Central, were not included. Secondly, The search was conducted by pairing immune cells two by two under the theme of esophageal cancer, which may have missed relevant literature on multi-cell crosstalk. Additionally, the search was conducted in december 2025, and some literature may not have been included yet. Last but not least, bibliometric methods themselves can only reflect quantitative characteristics and external features of literature, and cannot conduct in-depth analysis of the content of the literature.

Conclusions

China, the US, and Japan dominate this field of immune cell crosstalk research in esophageal cancer, yet collaboration remains regionally clustered, necessitating stronger international collaborations. Although research has pivoted toward immunotherapy combinations, tumor-associated macrophages and cancer-associated fibroblasts, critical gaps persist in Treg-NK cell interactions and post-radiotherapy crosstalk. Expanding global collaboration is crucial to elucidate these mechanisms and overcome therapeutic resistance.

Iterated integrals and cohomology
Kathrin Bringmann, Nikolaos Diamantis

Abstract
. We introduce an extension of the standard cohomology which is characterised by maps that fail to be classical cocycles by products of simpler maps. The construction is motivated by the study of Manin’s noncommutative modular symbols and of false theta functions. We use this construction to obtain a cohomological interpretation of important iterated integrals that arise in that study. In another direction, our approach gives modular counterparts to the long-studied relations among multiple zeta values.

17
, Definition 2.2]
, introduced the following
iterated Mellin transform

M(f_{1},\dots f_{r};s_{1},\dots s_{r})=(-1)^{r}\int_{0}^{i\infty}\int_{w_{1}}^{i\infty}\dots\int_{w_{r-1}}^{i\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{r}f_{j}(w_{j})w_{j}^{s_{j}}dw_{r}\dots dw_{1}.

Generalising the classical case, for
s_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
, he expressed it as a linear combination of values of a
multiple
L
-series
defined as

L_{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\geq 1}\frac{c_{1}(n_{1})\dots c_{r}(n_{r})}{(n_{1}+\dots+n_{r})^{s_{1}}\dots(n_{r-1}+n_{r})^{s_{r-1}}n_{r}^{s_{r}}},

where
c_{j}(n)
denotes the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
f_{j}.

These have been studied for example in
[
11
,
18
]
, where the emphasis was on their values at positive integer
s_{j}
, in analogy with the multiple zeta values. We do not use the term “multiple modular values” for those values because this term has been employed by Brown for a vast generalisation of such values which have been studied motivically in
[
7
,
8
]
.

In each of the papers
[
11
,
18
]
, the study of the multiple
L
-series was based on an expression in terms of a variation of Manin’s iterated Mellin transform. Each of these variations was designed to focus on a different aspect of the multiple
L
-series. For instance, in
[
11
]
it was used to derive relations among values of multiple
L
-series, mirroring the extensively studied subject of relations among multiple zeta values. For this, a first, together with
[
1
]
, cohomological study of multiple
L
-series and their values is undertaken and analogues of Eichler integrals are defined.

Our principal object in this section unifies and extends the constructions of those works. We consider
k_{j}\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
,
f_{j}\in S_{k_{j}}(\Gamma,\chi)
, and
s_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, where
j\in\{1,\dots r\}
. Then, we let
r^{*}_{f_{1},\dots f_{r}}(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})(\gamma)(\tau)
be the map
sending
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(N)
to the function given, for
\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{-}\cup\mathbb{Q}\cup\{i\infty\}

(3.1)

r^{*}_{f_{1},\dots f_{r}}(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})(\gamma)(\tau)=\int_{\gamma^{-1}i\infty}^{i\infty}\int_{w_{1}}^{i\infty}\dots\int_{w_{r-1}}^{i\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{r}f_{j}(w_{j})(w_{j}-\tau)^{s_{j}}dw_{r}\dots dw_{1}.

We can retrieve Manin’s iterated Mellin transform. Namely, for
\Gamma=\langle\Gamma_{0}(N),W_{N}\rangle
, we have

r^{*}_{f_{1},\dots f_{r}}(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})(W_{N})(0)=(-1)^{r}M(f_{1},\dots f_{r};s_{1},\dots s_{r}).

For
\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{-}
, the basic object
R_{r}
of
[
1
]
is

R_{r}(f_{1},\dots f_{r};s_{1},\dots s_{r};\gamma^{-1}i\infty,\infty;\tau):=(-1)^{r}r^{*}_{f_{1},\dots f_{r}}(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})(\gamma)(\tau).

If
k_{j}\in 2\mathbb{N}
, and
N=1
, then the polynomial
R_{2,r}(\gamma;(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{r}))
of
[
11
]
, for
\tau_{1}=\dots=\tau_{r}=\tau
equals

R_{2,r}(\gamma;(\tau,\dots,\tau))=r^{*}_{f_{1},\dots f_{r}}(k_{1}-2,\dots,k_{r}-2)(\gamma)(\tau).

Our counterpart of the integral expressions for the multiple
L
-series in the above works pertains to the additive twist of the multiple
L
-series. For
\operatorname{Re}(s_{j})\gg 1
, and
\frac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Q}
, we set

L_{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}\left(\frac{a}{b};s_{1},\dots,s_{r}\right):=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\geq 1}\frac{c_{1}(n_{1})\dots c_{r}(n_{r})e^{2\pi i(n_{1}+\dots+n_{r})\frac{a}{b}}}{(n_{1}+\dots+n_{r})^{s_{1}}\dots(n_{r-1}+n_{r})^{s_{r-1}}n_{r}^{s_{r}}}

and we define the
completed multiple
L
-series with additive twists

\Lambda_{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}\left(\frac{a}{b};s_{1},\dots,s_{r}\right):=\int_{\frac{a}{b}}^{i\infty}f_{1}(w_{1})\left(w_{1}-\frac{a}{b}\right)^{s_{1}-1}\int_{w_{1}}^{i\infty}\dots\int_{w_{r-1}}^{i\infty}\prod_{j=2}^{r}f_{j}(w_{j})(w_{j}-w_{j-1})^{s_{j}-1}dw_{j}\,dw_{1}.

We set

A_{n_{1},\dots n_{r}}^{[m_{1},\dots m_{r}]}:=\binom{m_{r}}{n_{r}}\binom{m_{r}+m_{r-1}-n_{r}}{n_{r-1}}\dots\binom{m_{r}+\dots+m_{2}-n_{r}-\dots-n_{2}}{n_{1}}.

We then have the following result which justifies the name “completed
L
-series”.

Proposition 3.1
.

(1)

For
\operatorname{Re}(s_{j})\gg 1
, we have

\Lambda_{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}\left(\frac{a}{b};s_{1},\dots,s_{r}\right)=\frac{\Gamma(s_{1})\dots\Gamma(s_{r})}{(-2\pi i)^{s_{1}+\dots s_{r}}}L_{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}\left(\frac{a}{b};s_{1},\dots,s_{r}\right).

(2)

Fix
m_{j}\in 2\mathbb{N}
. For
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have

r^{*}_{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})(\gamma)(\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{m_{1}+\dots+m_{r}}\mathcal{L}_{f_{1},\dots f_{r}}\left(\gamma^{-1}(i\infty);n\right)\left(\gamma^{-1}(i\infty)-\tau\right)^{n},

where

\mathcal{L}_{f_{1},\dots f_{r}}\left(\frac{a}{b};n\right):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{2},\dots,n_{r}\geq 0\\
n_{1}=m_{1}+\dots+m_{r}-n_{2}-\dots-n_{r}-n\end{subarray}}A_{n_{1},\dots n_{r}}^{[m_{1},\dots m_{r}]}\Lambda_{f_{1},\dots f_{r}}\left(\frac{a}{b};n_{1}+1,\dots,n_{r}+1\right).

Proof.

(1) We choose the lines of integration
w_{1}=\frac{a}{b}+it_{1}
and
w_{j}=w_{j-1}+it_{j}
(
t_{j}\geq 0
) and the conclusion follows as in the one-dimensional case (e.g. (2.10) of
[
13
]
).

(2) The Binomial Theorem applied to
(w_{r}-\tau)^{m_{r}}=((w_{r}-w_{r-1})+(w_{r-1}-\tau))^{m_{r}}
implies that

r^{*}_{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})(\gamma)(\tau)=\sum_{n_{r}\geq 0}\binom{m_{r}}{n_{r}}\int_{\frac{a}{b}}^{i\infty}\dots\int_{w_{r-3}}^{i\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{r-2}f_{j}(w_{j})(w_{j}-\tau)^{m_{j}}\\
\times\int_{w_{r-2}}^{i\infty}\!\!f_{r-1}(w_{r-1})(w_{r-1}-\tau)^{m_{r-1}+m_{r}-n_{r}}\!\int_{w_{r-1}}^{i\infty}\!\!f_{r}(w_{r})(w_{r}-w_{r-1})^{n_{r}}dw_{r}\dots dw_{1}.

By induction, we deduce the claim.
∎

5.
The case of half-integral weight.

An essential difference between the cases of integral and half-integral weight is the domain of
r_{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}(\gamma)
. Whereas in the former case,
r_{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}(\gamma)
can simply be treated as a polynomial in
\tau

\tau
must be chosen more carefully to ensure that the defining integral converges and is independent of the path of integration. This is automatic if
\tau
belongs to
\mathbb{H}^{-}
(and the cusps), however, for questions related to quantum modular forms, or to “noncommutative boundary” (
[
17

\mathbb{H}^{-}.

In this section, we apply the general framework of
Section

2.3
to address this problem in a way that allows for a cohomological interpretation. We start by recalling some of the main objects of
[
5
,
6
]
, and show how they can be interpreted in our framework.

5.1.
Depth
1

Let
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}(N)

4|N
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
, and
\chi
a multiplier system of weight

. For

defined by (

\mathbb{H}^{-}
of “polynomial growth” (the space

in the proof of Proposition
5.4

[

, where the following lemma was proved (

The function

\mathbb{H}^{-}\cup\mathbb{Q}

For

motivates the choice of the coefficient module in the next proposition. In a sense, Proposition

We illustrate the nature of Definition

6.2.1.

•

(1)

-cocycle condition up to maps that decompose, in a certain way, into lower depth mappings. As with the classical cohomology, for the kind of groups we are considering, our cohomology is concentrated in orders

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
M_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq k

[\sigma]\in Z_{(k)}^{n}(G,\otimes_{j=1}^{k}M_{j})

(6.8)

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

, we have

and the assertion is trivial. Suppose it holds for depths
<k

Fix a

. By (

), there exist
\varrho_{j,r}\in C^{n}(G,\otimes_{\nu=j+1}^{k}M_{\nu})
such that
d^{n}\sigma=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
r\end{subarray}}}^{k-1}\sigma_{j,r}\cup\varrho_{j,r}
, where

ranges over the indices of the basis elements

(6.9)

0=d^{n+1}(d^{n}\sigma)=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
r\end{subarray}}}^{k-1}\left(d^{1}\sigma_{j,r}\cup\varrho_{j,r}\right)-\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
r\end{subarray}}}^{k-1}\left(\sigma_{j,r}\cup d^{n}\varrho_{j,r}\right).

The inductive hypothesis implies that there exist
\psi^{j,r}_{\nu,\ell}\in\pi_{(j-\nu)}^{-1}Z_{(j-\nu)}^{1}(G,\otimes_{m=\nu+1}^{j}M_{m})
such that the first double sum becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
r\end{subarray}}}^{k-1}\left(\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=1\\
\ell\end{subarray}}}^{j-1}\sigma_{\nu,\ell}\cup\psi^{j,r}_{\nu,\ell}\right)\cup\varrho_{j,r}=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=1\\
\ell\end{subarray}}}^{k-2}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}j=\nu+1\\
r\end{subarray}}}^{k-1}\sigma_{\nu,\ell}\cup\psi^{j,r}_{\nu,\ell}\cup\varrho_{j,r}=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=1\\
\ell\end{subarray}}}^{k-1}\sigma_{\nu,\ell}\cup\left(\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
r\end{subarray}}}^{k-\nu-1}\psi^{j+\nu,r}_{\nu,\ell}\cup\varrho_{j+\nu,r}\right).

Note that the inner sum vanishes for

), we get

(6.10)

0=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
r\end{subarray}}}^{k-1}\sigma_{j,r}\cup\left(\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=1\\
\ell\end{subarray}}}^{k-j-1}\psi^{j+\nu,\ell}_{j,r}\cup\varrho_{\nu+j,\ell}-d^{n}\varrho_{j,r}\right).

If
\phi_{j,r}
denotes the term inside the parentheses in (

\sum_{j,r}\sigma_{j,r}(\gamma_{2})\circ\gamma_{1}\otimes\phi_{j,r}(\gamma_{1})=0

\sum_{j,r}\sigma_{j,r}(\gamma_{2})\otimes\phi_{j,r}(\gamma_{1})\circ\gamma_{1}^{-1}=0

Since

are linearly independent maps of

\ell\end{subarray}}}^{k-j-1}\psi^{j+\nu,\ell}_{j,r}\cup\varrho_{\nu+j,\ell}.

Thus

, proving (

).

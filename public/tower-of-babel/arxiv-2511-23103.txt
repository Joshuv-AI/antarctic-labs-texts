An Experimental Framework for QCD studies of Event Shapes and Inclusive Hadron Spectra at FCC-ee energies Preprint: APS/123-QED
Philip Mathew, Ritu Aggarwal, Manjit Kaur

Abstract
We present a comprehensive analysis of hadronic final states produced in e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} annihilations at the planned FCC-ee center-of-mass energies of 91.2, 160, 240, and 365 GeV using Monte Carlo simulations with PYTHIA. The distortions on event shape variables at high center-of-mass-energies is investigated considering initial state photon radiation and backgrounds from hadronic decays of Z pairs, W pairs, top-quark pairs, and Higgs. An extraction of the strong coupling constant α s \alpha_{\text{s}} is performed through fits of the Thrust and C-parameter distributions to perturbative QCD predictions at next-to-next-to-leading-order (NNLO) precision. The study further probes soft gluon dynamics through charged particle multiplicities and momentum distributions, comparing the energy evolution of their mean values with previous experiments. The learning from this phenomenological work provide a reference for future experimental QCD studies at high-energy electron-positron colliders.



I
Introduction

The strong interaction has been successfully modeled by Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), the non-abelian SU(3) gauge theory describing the forces binding the fermions into composite hadrons
35
;
27
. The strength of this interaction between the quarks mediated by gluons, with a range less than one femtometer, is parameterized by the strong coupling constant. Probing physics at such a scale has been made possible by decades of progress in accelerator-based experiments mainly involving high-energy collisions of hadron-hadron
6
;
9
;
36
, lepton-lepton
46
, and lepton-hadron
2
;
42
. The historical success of electron-positron annihilation for QCD studies is largely due to a decoupled electroweak initial state allowing direct access to the effects of the strong force, discussed in further detail in Section II. Although the wealth of data generated prior to and at the Large Electron-Positron collider (LEP) has facilitated tests of the validity of QCD models in significant ways, there still remains the need to constrain existing models to higher precision, and develop new models to explain the non-perturbative regime
40
;
38
;
43
. Advancing the energy frontier from the LEP to the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) shifted the experimental focus from precision measurements to discovery-oriented studies where higher momentum transfers complicate the isolation of softer QCD signatures. The proposed leptonic Future Circular Collider (FCC-ee) is the next major opportunity to progress the strong sector through electron-positron annihilations at various beam energies. The latest design report of the FCC-ee specifies a 15-year plan over a wide range of center-of-mass (c.m.) energies extending from just below the Z-pole to the W-boson pair threshold, the Higgsstrahlung maximum, and up to just above the top-quark pair threshold
12
. The physics program can be broadly categorized into determining the strong coupling constant
\alpha_{\text{s}}
at a 0.1% accuracy
23
, investigating perturbative parton radiations through jet rates and event shapes, and refining phenomenological models of non-perturbative parton hadronisation
24
. Despite the exciting prospects of this venture into a new era of energy and precision, an extended set of challenges is expected at such high collision energies. Enhanced initial state radiations (ISR) via QED reduce the effective energy of a large fraction of annihilation requiring radiative cuts to ensure monochromatic results at the cost of statistics. Additional electroweak backgrounds distort the signal requiring understandably subtractive corrections further increasing uncertainties. Recent advancements in theoretical models of event shapes will certainly improve measurements of
\alpha_{\text{s}}
but requires careful consideration of systematic uncertainties. The impacts of these challenges need to be studied in detail to facilitate fruitful experimental outcomes. Within this context, we present a comprehensive study of hadronic events in PYTHIA to build a framework for eventual QCD studies at future
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders. The work unifies various aspects of strong interaction analyses into a single reference that could assist further research for the FCC-ee. This includes corrections of radiative and background distortions, a next-to-next-to-leading-order (NNLO) determination of
\alpha_{\text{s}}
with Thrust and C-parameter, and the inclusive dynamics of hadron production probed through charged multiplicity and momenta distributions (a key observable measured in almost every HEP experiment). The paper begins with a brief review of QCD processes in electron-positron annihilation followed by the definitions of the event shape observables used in this study. Section III covers the Monte Carlo generation in PYTHIA and the validation of results with experimental data from the LEP. Section IV studies the influence of ISR and backgrounds on observables illustrating the importance of rethinking correction strategies at the FCC-ee. Section V presents an extraction of
\alpha_{\text{S}}
by fitting event shapes to theory, a description of the employed fitting procedure, and a discussion on sources of systematic errors at the FCC-ee. Finally, Section VI examines inclusive hadron spectra and compares the energy evolution of mean values with previous data.

II
Event shape observables

The typical structure of an annihilation event through the channel
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\gamma^{*}/Z^{0}
is illustrated in Fig.
1
. At first, the incoming leptons may radiate a photon before annihilating into a virtual photon or a Z-boson. The boson decays into a
q\overline{q}
pair with sufficient energy to begin radiating gluons (
q\rightarrow qg
). A gluon can become another quark pair (
g\rightarrow q\overline{q}
) or continue radiating gluons (
g\rightarrow gg
). Repeated continuation of these three QCD processes forms a cascading shower of quarks and gluons gradually approaching lower energy regimes. Due to the inverse running of
\alpha_{\text{s}}
with energy, the partons experience a growing strong force pulling them together until they are rapidly forced by confinement into colorless mesons or baryons. These newly formed hadrons have definite lifetimes, short or long, and eventually decay into the stable hadrons that form the experimental observations inside the detector. The hard phase is where parton production occurs with sufficiently high momentum transfers to be directly calculated with perturbation theory. The soft phase is where hadronisation occurs and can be emulated through phenomenological models or power corrections to event shapes
10
;
45
. QCD predicts that hadronic final states have either a 2-jet structure of a quark and antiquark (
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow q\overline{q}
) or a multi-jet structure due to an additional radiated gluon (
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow q\overline{q}g
). While the former contains large non-perturbative hadronisation corrections, the hard gluon in the latter introduces a perturbatively calculable deviation. The dynamics of this deviation can be probed by quantifying the geometrical distributions or ‘shapes’ of momenta within a single event. This is achieved by constructing event shape observables as linear sums of particle momenta that are intrinsically sensitive to hard gluon radiation. The extent to which an event deviates from the 2-jet scenario, when fitted to theoretical predictions, allows a direct determination of the strong coupling constant. Importantly, these fits require the observables to be infrared-safe and collinear-safe, ensuring insensitivity to soft gluon emissions and splittings at small angles, respectively. The event shapes used at the LEP commonly included the Thrust, C-parameter, Total and Wide jet broadening, and Heavy jet mass
25
. The thrust
T
measures the jet-like nature of an event by selecting an axis
\vec{n}
that maximizes the projections of particle momenta
p_{i}
and computing the expression:

T=\max_{\vec{n}}\left(\frac{\sum_{i}|\vec{p}_{i}\cdot\vec{n}|}{\sum_{i}|\vec{p}_{i}|}\right)

(1)

Exact alignment of
p_{i}
with
\vec{n}
gives T=1 for 2-jet events and decreases with additional gluon radiation to a minimum of
T=0.5
for a perfectly spherical event. The quantity (1-T) is used instead since it vanishes in the 2-jet limit and is better suited for theoretical expansions. The
C
-parameter measures the angular distribution of particle momenta by deriving eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
of the linear momentum tensor
\Theta^{ij}
and computing the expression:

\Theta^{ij}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k}|\vec{p}_{k}|}\sum_{k}\frac{p_{k}^{i}p_{k}^{j}}{|\vec{p}_{k}|},\qquad i,j=1,2,3

(2)

C=3(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{3}\lambda_{1})

(3)

Here, the term
p_{k}^{i}
denotes the
i\textsuperscript{th}
component of the momentum
\vec{p}_{k}
of the
k\textsuperscript{th}
particle of the event. Just as for (1-T), a 2-jet topology gives C = 0 and increases with radiation to a maximum of C = 1. Although both observables are quantitatively constructed from similar geometries of hadronic final states, they qualitatively highlight different aspects of QCD physics. Thrust is more sensitive to perturbative hard gluon emissions while the C-parameter also responds significantly to non-perturbative wide-angle soft radiation that adds event isotropy. This complementarity is the reason for selecting these observables to make this study focused yet comprehensive. The presented work focuses on studying these two variables in detail at the proposed c.m. energies of the FCC-ee.

IV
Challenges at high
\sqrt{\text{s}}

In this section, we analyze the effects of ISR and backgrounds on event shapes at c.m. energies of the FCC-ee.

IV.1
QED Radiation

The radiation of photons from initial state leptons is a prominent feature of
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders that lowers the effective c.m. energy of the remaining hadronic system
\sqrt{s^{\prime}}
:

\sqrt{s^{\prime}}\ =\ \sqrt{\ s\left(1-\frac{2E_{\gamma}}{\sqrt{s}}\right)}

(4)

Where
E_{\gamma}
is the energy of the ISR photon. At the LEP1, this enabled important energy-dependence studies of
\alpha_{s}
below the Z-pole
41
. However, the higher energies at the LEP2 experienced a large number of events with an
E_{\gamma}
that returned the
\sqrt{s^{\prime}}
back to the
m_{Z}
scale
3
. These so called radiative returns is explained by integrating the differential full cross section of the
f_{i}\bar{f}_{j}\rightarrow Z\rightarrow f_{i}^{\prime}\bar{f}_{j}^{\prime}
process into the Breit-Wigner form written as follows
39
:

\sigma(s^{\prime})=\frac{12\pi}{m_{Z}^{2}}\frac{\Gamma_{e}\Gamma_{f}}{(s^{\prime}-m_{Z}^{2})^{2}+m_{Z}^{2}\Gamma_{Z}^{2}},

(5)

Where
\Gamma_{e},\Gamma_{Z}
are the widths of the electron and Z-boson, respectively. The resonance at
\sqrt{s^{\prime}}=m_{Z}^{2}
leads to a boost of the
ee\rightarrow\gamma/Z
cross section producing a characteristic peak at
\sqrt{s^{\prime}}=91.2
GeV in the
\sqrt{s}=365
GeV plot shown in Fig.
4
. Since the energy lost through ISR is predicted to be logarithmic with c.m. energy, a large number of radiative events can be expected at the FCC-ee
5
. The impact of this radiation on event shapes is visualized in Fig.
5
. The peaks observed at
\sqrt{s}=160,240,365
GeV in (1-T)
\approx 0.325,0.14,0.06
and in
\text{C}\approx 0.74,0.54,0.30
, respectively, can be attributed to the radiative returns
20
. These distortions will need to be filtered out through cuts on
\sqrt{s^{\prime}}
before performing
\alpha_{\text{s}}
determinations. In Fig.
5
, a tight cut (
\sqrt{s^{\prime}}=1.00\sqrt{s}
) on the simulated distributions (red dashed) produces the clean signals (black solid). While this effectively removes the ISR distortions, only 4.7% of the original statistics survived the cut. The survival fraction varies greatly with tighter cuts and c.m. energy as summarized in Table
2
, pointing to a need to rethink ISR correction strategies at each FCC-ee level.

Electroweak backgrounds

ee\rightarrow\gamma/Z

2m_{W}

\gamma/Z

\nu_{e}

ZZ

2m_{Z}

\gamma/Z

V
Determination of
\alpha_{\text{s}}

This section briefly revisits the QCD formulation of event shape variables and performs an extraction of the strong coupling constant. The fit procedure is explained in detail and the results compared with prior LEP values.

V.1
Theoretical description

The fixed order differential distribution of an experimentally measured event shape observable
O
can be expressed perturbatively up to NNLO accuracy as follows:

\frac{O}{\sigma_{\text{had}}(\mu)}\cdot\frac{d\sigma(\mu)}{dO}=\Bigg(\frac{\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu)}{2\pi}\Bigg)\cdot\frac{O\ d\overline{A}(\mu)}{dO}\\
+\Bigg(\frac{\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu)}{2\pi}\Bigg)^{2}\cdot\frac{O\ d\overline{B}(\mu)}{dO}+\Bigg(\frac{\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu)}{2\pi}\Bigg)^{3}\cdot\frac{O\ d\overline{C}(\mu)}{dO}

(6)

Where
\sigma_{\text{had}}
is the total hadronic cross section and each term of coefficients
\overline{A},\overline{B},\overline{C}
corresponds to the corrections at LO, NLO, and NNLO, respectively. The numerical parton-level computation of (
6
) instead utilizes the form:

\frac{O}{\sigma_{\text{0}}(\mu)}\cdot\frac{d\sigma(\mu)}{dO}=\Bigg(\frac{\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu)}{2\pi}\Bigg)\cdot\frac{O\ dA(\mu)}{dO}\\
+\Bigg(\frac{\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu)}{2\pi}\Bigg)^{2}\cdot\frac{O\ dB(\mu)}{dO}+\Bigg(\frac{\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu)}{2\pi}\Bigg)^{3}\cdot\frac{O\ dC(\mu)}{dO}

(7)

Where
\sigma_{\text{0}}
is the LO cross section of the
ee\rightarrow
hadrons process. The translation from (
6
) to (
7
) is then simply:

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{O}

\displaystyle=A_{O}

\displaystyle\bar{B}_{O}

\displaystyle=B_{O}-A_{\text{tot}}A_{O}

\displaystyle\bar{C}_{O}

\displaystyle=C_{O}-A_{\text{tot}}B_{O}-\left(B_{\text{tot}}-A_{\text{tot}}^{2}\right)A_{O}

And coefficients
A_{\text{tot}}
and
B_{\text{tot}}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle A_{\text{tot}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\,(N_{c}^{2}-1)}{4N_{c}}

\displaystyle B_{\text{tot}}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{8N_{c}}\left[\left(\tfrac{243}{4}-44\,\zeta_{3}\right)N_{c}+\tfrac{3}{4N_{c}}+\left(8\,\zeta_{3}-11\right)N_{f}\right]

Here, we select the
N_{c}=3
as the number of quark colors and the
N_{f}=~5
as the number of active quark flavors. Considering massless quarks, the ratio of cross sections,

\frac{\sigma_{\text{had}}}{\sigma_{0}}=1+\frac{\alpha_{\text{s}}}{2\pi}A_{\text{tot}}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{\text{s}}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}B_{\text{tot}}+\dots\ \mathcal{O}(\alpha_{\text{s}}^{n})

(8)

contains canceling electroweak coupling factors. A correction for the bottom quark is required when the
b\overline{b}
events are considerable. These effects scale with
m_{b}^{2}/Q^{2}
, accounting to nearly 1% at 91.2 GeV and 0.2-0.3% at 200 GeV
11
. The correction is derived from subtracting the
A
and
B
values for massless and massive scenarios as calculated in
17
. The top quark mass also needs corrections at energies above the
t\overline{t}
threshold. Both these contributions have not been included in the current study.

V.2
Fitting procedure

Samples of
5\times 10^{6}
events are generated in PYTHIA without hadronisation and without ISR at each FCC-ee energy. The
ee\rightarrow\gamma/Z
channel is isolated for this study. NNLO predictions are constructed using coefficients from Weinzierl
47
. Simulated event shape distributions are fitted using the MINOS routine of the MINUIT minimization framework in ROOT
29
. The extracted
\alpha_{\text{s}}
values from each observable are averaged with equal weights and the results summarized in Table
4
. The selected fit ranges shown in Fig.
12
are inspired from
30
and correspond to the 3-jet region where perturbative predictions are most reliable and hadronisation corrections remain minimal. The lower limits of most fits were shifted to slightly higher values for reasonable results, potentially due to the limitations of fixed-order theory predictions in non-perturbative modeling. In all fits, the
\chi^{2}/dof
is in between 1 and 1.8, indicating good agreement between simulation and theory across energies. The value at 91.2 GeV from the (1-T) and C fits are consistent within approximately 1% of the NNLO result in
21
thereby validating reliability of the fitting procedure. The deviation of
\alpha_{\text{s}}
between event shapes is
\leq
0.6% at all c.m. energies.

V.3
Uncertainties at FCC-ee

The large luminosities planned for the FCC-ee will limit the precision of
\alpha_{\text{s}}
measurements to primarily systematic rather than statistical uncertainties
38
. At LEP, these systematic errors decreased with
\sqrt{s}
from approximately 8% at 91.2 GeV to 5% at 206 GeV
4
, and a similar trend is expected at higher energies. Theoretical models of event shapes have improved through the matching of resummed logarithmic terms to fixed-order predictions, improving reliability near the 2-jet region further reducing uncertainties
32
. This has been demonstrated in recent N
3
LL+N
3
LO extractions using Thrust
1
and the C-parameter
31
. The use of hadronisation correction factors on experimental measurements to relate parton-level and hadron-level data introduce an additional source of systematic uncertainty. These have been computed in PYTHIA for each FCC-ee energy and show a reduced impact at higher c.m. energies in Fig.
11

13
. The removal of distortions from ISR and electroweak backgrounds discussed in Section IV will add to the overall systematic error budget. Extending this study in DELPHES would help model detector-related errors
19
. Achieving tight control over systematic uncertainties will hence be crucial for
\alpha_{\text{s}}
studies at the FCC-ee.

VI
Inclusive hadron spectra

Inclusive observables refer to energy-dependent measurements that sum over all final state hadrons irrespective of process or topology. Capturing global properties of hadronic final states provide a probe to the average behavior of parton fragmentation and hadronisation. This assumed correlation that hadron-level dynamics reflect those at the parton-level scaled by a constant is termed Local Parton–Hadron Duality (LPHD)
8
;
7
. We examine here the energy evolution of two inclusive spectra of charged hadrons, the multiplicity and momenta.

VI.1
Charged hadron multiplicity

The total hadron multiplicity
\langle\text{N}_{\text{tot}}\rangle
represents the cumulative outcome of all non-perturbative dynamics. Since only charged hadrons can be detected experimentally, the corresponding charged hadron multiplicity
\langle\text{N}_{\text{ch}}\rangle
is the typically measured observable. The correction factor
\text{K}_{\text{ch}}
for neutral contributions is found to be consistent across all FCC-ee energies and is computed as:

\text{K}_{\text{ch}}=\frac{\langle\text{N}_{\text{ch}}\rangle_{\text{pythia}}}{\langle\text{N}_{\text{tot}}\rangle_{\text{pythia}}}\approx 0.472

At parton-level, QCD theory predicts the energy evolution of mean parton multiplicity
\langle\text{n}_{\text{tot}}\rangle
up to to next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order (3NLO) as follows
37
;
22
:

\langle\text{n}_{\text{tot}}\rangle_{\text{qcd}}\;=\;\frac{y^{-a_{1}c^{2}}}{r_{0}}\ e^{(2c\sqrt{y}\ +\,A_{\text{NNLO}}(y)+\ A_{\text{3NLO}}(y))}

(9)

where the NNLO and 3NLO corrections are defined as,

A_{\text{NNLO}}(y)=\frac{c}{\sqrt{y}}\Bigg[r_{1}+2a_{2}c^{2}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{0}^{2}}\big(\ln(2y)+2\big)\Bigg]

(10)

A_{\text{3NLO}}(y)=\frac{c^{2}}{y}\Bigg[a_{3}c^{2}+\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{2}+r_{2}-a_{1}\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{0}^{2}}\big(\ln(2y)+1\big)\Bigg]

(11)

The expansion parameter
y
in the equations is given as,

y=\ln\Bigg(\frac{\sqrt{s}}{2Q_{0}}\Bigg)=\ln\Bigg(\frac{\sqrt{s}}{2\Lambda}\Bigg)

Here, the cut-off scale
Q_{0}
is set to the QCD scale
\Lambda
. The remaining coefficients of above equations are defined as,

c=\sqrt{4N_{c}/\beta_{0}}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \beta_{0}=11-\tfrac{2}{3}N_{f}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \beta_{1}=102-\tfrac{38}{3}N_{f}

Numerical values of
r_{0},r_{1},r_{2},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
are given in
16
. The total parton multiplicity is then translated to the hadron-level variable
\langle\text{N}_{\text{tot}}\rangle_{\text{qcd}}
through invoking LHPD:

\langle\text{N}_{\text{tot}}\rangle_{\text{qcd}}=\langle\text{N}_{\text{tot}}\rangle_{\text{qcd}}\ \times\ \text{K}_{\text{LHPD}}

The constant
\text{K}_{\text{LHPD}}
was determined by normalizing the 3NLO theory prediction to the PYTHIA curve at Z-pole:

K_{\text{LHPD}}=\frac{\langle N_{\text{ch}}\rangle_{\text{pythia}}(\text{m}_{\text{Z}})}{\langle N_{\text{ch}}\rangle_{\text{qcd}}(\text{m}_{\text{Z}})}=0.207

This value closely matches the result from ALEPH
30
. Finally, the neutral correction is applied to the hadron-level theory prediction for comparison with experiment:

\langle\text{N}_{\text{ch}}\rangle_{\text{qcd}}=\langle\text{N}_{\text{tot}}\rangle_{\text{qcd}}\ \times\ \text{K}_{\text{ch}}

The 3NLO prediction is evaluated at
\Lambda=202\pm 31
MeV (opted from
30
) with
N_{f}=5,N_{c}=3
. This curve and uncertainty band is plotted in Fig.
13
alongside results from PYTHIA and measurements from TASSO
14
, AMY
48
, L3
3
, and ALEPH
30
. The simulated values accurately follow a logarithmic growth with increasing center-of-mass across the FCC-ee regime, as predicted by QCD. This indicates that perturbative evolution remains reliable well into the multi-hundred-GeV region. The slight deviations emerging at higher energies could possibly hint at the limits of the fixed-order expansion.

VI.2
Momentum distribution

While
\langle\text{N}_{\text{ch}}\rangle
encapsulates the integrated yield, the inclusive variable
\xi
offers a differential view of the spread of scaled momentum
x_{p}
among particles. It is defined as:

\xi\;=\;\ln\!\left(\frac{1}{x_{p}}\right)\qquad\text{where,}\ \ \ \ x_{p}\;=\;\frac{2p}{\sqrt{s}}

(12)

As partons continue to radiate gluons, many are soft and carry low energy. Interference between successive soft emissions leads to coherent radiation making certain directions become more probable while suppressing others. This coherence results in a gradual narrowing of the parton shower cone, a phenomenon known as angular ordering
25
. As a consequence, the
\xi
spectrum rises in the low-
p
region, reaches a maximum at intermediate momenta, and then falls off, producing the characteristic hump-backed shape expected by QCD. The central region of this curve can be described either by a Gaussian, a distorted Gaussian (selected for this study), or the limiting spectrum prediction. The simulated
\xi
distributions and their fits are shown in Fig.
14
. The Fong-Webber expression
26
is utilized with all five parameters as free variables: normalization, mean, width, skewness, and kurtosis. Fit ranges similar to
30
yield acceptable results and the resulting values are summarized in Table
6
. The position of the peak of the fitted
\xi
curve, denoted by
\xi^{*}
, evolves with energy according to
34
:

\xi^{*}\;=\;y\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{c_{\xi}}{y}}-\frac{c_{\xi}}{y}\right)

(13)

where the coefficients
c_{\xi}
is defined as,

c_{\xi}=\frac{a^{2}}{16\,N_{c}\,b}\ \ \ \text{where,}\ \ \ a=\frac{11N_{c}}{3}+\frac{2N_{f}}{3N_{c}^{2}}\ \ \ b=\frac{11N_{c}}{3}-\frac{2N_{f}}{3}

Corrections to
\xi^{*}
from neutrals is minimal since it does not bring about change to the peak position. The MLLA expression evaluated with
N_{f}=5,N_{c}=3
is normalized to the PYTHIA value at 91.2 GeV and plotted in Fig.
15
. The uncertainty band corresponds to a variation in
\Lambda=235\pm 15
MeV
30
. The experimental results from TASSO
15
, L3
3
, and ALEPH
30
mostly follow the theory prediction while simulated values begin to deviate significantly above 160 GeV. The higher
\xi^{*}
could be due to parameters in PYTHIA that were tuned at 91.2 GeV not perfectly extrapolating to higher energies. This behavior could also indicate the importance of higher-order logarithmic terms and mass effects not captured in the fixed-order MLLA formulation. Future analyses incorporating next-to-MLLA corrections and dedicated retuning of fragmentation parameters at FCC-ee energies will be essential to refine these predictions and thereby probe the transition between perturbative and non-perturbative QCD domains with greater precision.

VII
Conclusions

An extensive framework has been presented for QCD studies with event shape observables and inclusive hadron spectra at future electron-positron colliders with a particular focus on the FCC-ee. Samples of
5\times 10^{6}
have been generated in PYTHIA 8.313 at 91.2, 160, 240 and 365 GeV corresponding to the planned FCC-ee program.

An investigation of event shapes at high center-of-mass energies has been presented. Significant distortions have been observed due to initial state photon radiation (Fig.
5
) and additional electroweak backgrounds from hadronic decays of Z pairs, W pairs, top-quark pairs, and Higgs (Fig.
6
-
9
). Mitigation through radiative cuts (Table
2
) and subtractive corrections (Table
3
) will come at a consequential cost of event statistics. Such strategies must hence be studied further to find an optimal balance.

An extraction of the strong coupling constant up to NNLO at the four FCC-ee energies has been presented. The employed fit procedure produces agreeable results (Table
4
) and the fit ranges used at LEP hold reasonably well (Fig.
12
). Since statistical errors will be negligible at the FCC-ee, tight control on systematic uncertainties will be necessary to leverage the improved accuracy of theoretical models. Hadronisation correction factors computed in PYTHIA showcase a decreasing impact of hadronisation errors with energy (Fig.
11
).

An examination of inclusive hadron spectra has been presented. The energy evolution of charged hadron multiplicities follows the 3NLO prediction with slight deviations trend emerging above 240 GeV (Fig.
13
). The contribution of neutral hadrons to multiplicity is found to be consistent with c.m. energy. Translating parton distributions to the hadron-level by invoking Local Parton-Hadron Duality yields a normalization constant that matches the previous value from ALEPH
30
. The energy evolution of momentum distribution peaks also display an offset from the MLLA prediction (Fig.
15
).

The broad scope of the presented study provides several starting points for future extensions. Immediate additions could incorporate additional event shape variables
33
, resummed calculations
21
, higher fixed-order calculations
33
, corrections for mass effects of heavy quarks. Combining simulated results with the DELPHES framework
19
can delve deeper into understanding the influences of detector effects on systematic uncertainties.

Motivic pieces of curves: L L -functions and periods
Harry Spencer

Abstract
. Given a curve C C over a number field K K equipped with the action of a finite group G G by K K -automorphisms, one obtains a factorisation of L ⁡ ( C , s ) L(C,s) into a product of L L -functions of ‘motivic pieces of curves’ associated to irreducible G G -representations. We describe an algorithm for explicitly computing values of these L L -functions, demonstrating implementations in the cases of certain curves with actions by C 3 C_{3} , C 4 C_{4} and D 10 D_{10} . We explain how this algorithm can be used to factor L L -functions of curves with endomorphisms of Hecke type. Towards applications, we explicitly formulate and numerically verify a version of Deligne’s Period Conjecture for hitherto-uninvestigated L L -functions arising from motivic pieces of superelliptic curves.

2.
Motivic pieces of curves

The framework of motivic pieces of curves (or abelian varieties) was laid out by V. Dokchitser, Green, Konstantinou and Morgan in their work on the Parity Conjecture for Jacobians of curves
[
8
, §2]
. The set-up is as follows: fix a curve
C
over a number field
K
with the action of a finite group
G
by
K
-automorphisms, as well as embeddings
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{C}
for each prime
\ell
such that
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{C}
is independent of
\ell
. The
G
-action on
C
extends to
H^{1}_{\ell}(C)=(T_{\ell}\Jac(C)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})^{*}
for each prime
\ell
.

Definition 2.1

(=
[
8
, Definition 2.3]
)
.

For a
G
-representation
\tau
, define

H^{1}_{\ell}(C^{\tau})=\Hom_{G}(\tau,H^{1}_{\ell}(C)),

on which
G_{K}
acts by postcomposition. Implicitly this definition depends on
\ell
, but in fact it yields a compatible system of
\ell
-adic representations (cf.
[
8
, Corollary 2.14]
).

This system of
\ell
-adic representations gives rise to an
L
-function in the usual way.

Definition 2.2
.

We write
L(C^{\tau},s)
for the
L
-function
L((H^{1}_{\ell}(C^{\tau}))_{\ell},s)
, given for
\Re(s)>3/2
by

L(C^{\tau},s)=\prod_{(0)\neq\mathfrak{p}\hskip 0.28436pt\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\reflectbox{\rotatebox{90.0}{$\nshortmid$}}}\cr$\triangleright$\cr}}\mkern-3.0mu}$}}\hskip 1.42271pt\mathcal{O}_{K}}L_{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\tau},N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s})^{-1},

where
L_{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\tau},T):=\det(1-\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}\cdot T\mid H^{1}_{\ell}(C^{\tau})^{I_{\mathfrak{p}}})
for any choice of
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\ell
. Here
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the inertia group of the completion of
K
at
\mathfrak{p}
, and
\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}
is (a choice of) arithmetic Frobenius.

Remark 2.3
.

A less explicit way of constructing the
L
-function

as in Definition

whose

We note that the
\tau

\tau

(2.4)

3.
The algorithm

We now describe an algorithm for computing the Euler factors at good primes of
L(C^{\tau},s)
. In light of (
2.7
), the important step is computing the traces
\Tr(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot g\mid H^{1}_{\ell}(C^{\tau})^{I_{\mathfrak{p}}})
for
g\in G

The advantage of reducing the problem to point counts on curves—as opposed to relying, for example, on Gröbner basis techniques to count fixed points—is that there are many efficient algorithms implemented in Magma for these computations.

4.
Endomorphisms of Jacobians

Here we recap some background on endomorphisms of Jacobians, with the point being that the framework of motivic pieces of curves—under certain conditions—can be used to describe factorisations of
L
-functions of curves whose Jacobians have extra
endomorphisms
, rather than automorphisms.

4.1.
Endomorphisms of Hecke type

In
[
12
]
, Ellenberg introduces the notion of endomorphism algebras of Hecke type in order to construct families of curves whose Jacobians have real multiplication, i.e. curves
C
of genus
g
for which there is a totally real field
F
of degree
g
and an injection
F\hookrightarrow\End(\Jac(C))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Consider a curve
C
over
k
equipped with a cover
\pi\colon C\to B
of another curve. Say the Galois closure of the field extension
k(C)/k(B)
has Galois group
G
. This closure corresponds to a curve
D
, which admits an action by
G
of automorphisms. Write
H\leq G
for the subgroup for which
C=D/H
. There is a map
\mathbb{Q}[H\backslash G/H]\hookrightarrow\End(\Jac(C))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
—say the image is an
endomorphism algebra of Hecke type
and that a curve
C
has extra endomorphisms of Hecke type if there exists a cover
\pi
such that this image is non-trivial.

Remark 4.2
.

Given a curve
C
whose Jacobian has extra endomorphisms of Hecke type, we obtain a factorisation of
L(C,s)
corresponding to the factorisation of
L(D,s)
under the action of
G
, in the notation of Definition
4.1
.

Remark 4.3
.

Ellenberg notes that quotients of
\mathbb{Q}[H\backslash G/H]
are central simple algebras over abelian number fields and so, in particular, not all non-trivial endomorphism algebras can arise in this way. Whether there are other obstructions to endomorphism algebras being of Hecke type remains an open question.

4.2.
\GL_{2}
-type and modularity

We will also be interested in Jacobians of
\GL_{2}
-type. Being of
\GL_{2}
-type is a condition on the endomorphism algebra, which determines precisely when an
L
-function of an abelian variety decomposes as a product of
L
-functions of modular forms.

Definition 4.4
.

An abelian variety

of dimension

is of
\GL_{2}
-type if

is a
G_{K}
-representation taking values in
\GL(2,k\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
, where

is a number field of degree

.

Theorem 4.5
.

Given a simple abelian variety
A
over
\mathbb{Q}
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

is a quotient of

if and only if

is of
\GL_{2}
-type.

Proof.

In
[
26

Ribet shows that this is a consequence of Serre’s Conjecture, a proof of which is given in
[
18
]
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

A simple abelian variety

being a quotient of

means precisely that there exists a newform

of conductor

such that

.

5.
Periods

In this section we discuss the conjectural link between periods of motives and
L
-values, before explaining theoretically how to compute periods of motivic pieces in general and how to practically do so for pieces of superelliptic curves in particular.

5.1.
Motives

We begin with a brief discussion of motives from the point of view of realisation data, which sets us up for the statement of Deligne’s Period Conjecture.

Let
K
and
F
be number fields. For the purposes of this section, we view these abstractly, rather than having in mind an embedding into
\mathbb{C}
. For us, a
K
-motive
M
with coefficients in
F
of dimension
d
and weight

is a collection of

\{H_{B}(M),H_{\dR}(M),(H_{\lambda}(M))_{\lambda},c_{B,\dR},c_{\ell,B},c_{\ell,\dR}\}
as follows (e.g.
[
14

):

•

The

,
H_{B}(M)
, is a
d
-dimensional
F
-vector space with Hodge filtration by free

-modules

H_{B}(M)\otimes\mathbb{C}=\bigoplus_{i+j=w}H^{(i,j)}(M),

and
H^{(j,i)}(M)

K
.

•

The

, is a
d
-dimensional
F
-vector space with an exhaustive decreasing filtration

\{\Fil^{k}H_{\text{dR}}(M)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}.

•

The
\lambda
-adic realisations

, indexed by proper prime ideals
\lambda
of
F
, are
d
-dimensional
F_{\lambda}
-vector spaces equipped with a continuous homomorphism

\rho_{\lambda}:G_{K}\to\GL(H_{\lambda}(M)),

and, for each prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K

\lambda

, a local polynomial

L_{\mathfrak{p}}(M,T)=\det(1-T\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}\mid H_{\lambda}(M)^{I_{\mathfrak{p}}})\in F[T]

of primes

of

\rho_{\lambda}(I_{\mathfrak{p}})=1

\mathfrak{p}\not\in S

, the complex roots of

have absolute value

•

c_{B,\dR}
is an

-module isomorphism
H_{B}(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{\dR}(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}

c_{B,\dR}\left(\bigoplus_{i+j=w,\hskip 2.84544pti\geq k}H^{(i,j)}(M)\right)=\Fil^{k}H_{\dR}(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}.

•

and

are comparison isomorphisms relating
\oplus_{\lambda\mid\ell}H_{\lambda}(M)
to
H_{B}
and
H_{\dR}

To the above data, we also associate an
L
-function.

Definition 5.1
.

Given a
K
-motive

with coefficients in
F
, its

-function (or tuple of
L
-functions), converging in the right half-plane
\Re(s)>1+w(M)/2

where

L(\sigma,M,s)=\prod_{(0)\neq\mathfrak{p}\hskip 0.28436pt\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\reflectbox{\rotatebox{90.0}{$\nshortmid$}}}\cr$\triangleright$\cr}}\mkern-3.0mu}$}}\hskip 1.42271pt\mathcal{O}_{K}}L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\sigma}(M,N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s})^{-1}

and
L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\sigma}
is the polynomial over
\mathbb{C}
obtained by embedding the coefficients of

.

Remark 5.2
.

When we already have in mind an embedding

, we may write

for

.

We note the existence of the
Tate motive
, twisting a motive

by which corresponds to a shift on the level of

-functions.

Example 5.3
.

The Tate motive,
\mathbb{Q}(1)
, is a
\mathbb{Q}
-motive with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
of dimension

and weight
-2
. Given a motive

For this article, the example to have in mind is the motive associated to the first cohomologies of an abelian variety, whose

-function is the usual

-function associated to an abelian variety.

Example 5.4
.

Associated to the first cohomologies of a

-dimensional abelian variety

over

is a

-motive with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
of dimension

and weight

, denoted

, respectively, and the

, so it is pertinent to note that

is isomorphic to the dual of

.

Remark 5.5
.

In the case of the Jacobian of a curve with an action by

.

5.2.
Deligne’s Period Conjecture

Definition 5.6
.

Consider a
\mathbb{Q}
-motive
M
with coefficients in
F
and odd weight
w
. Let
H_{B}(M)^{+}
be the
+1
-eigenspace of complex conjugation, and define

\alpha_{M}^{+}:H_{B}^{+}(M)\otimes\mathbb{C}\hookrightarrow H_{B}(M)\otimes\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{c_{B,\dR}}H_{\text{dR}}(M)\otimes\mathbb{C}\twoheadrightarrow H_{\text{dR}}^{+}(M)\otimes\mathbb{C},

where the last map is the natural quotient map onto

H^{+}_{\dR}(M)=H_{\dR}(M)/\Fil^{1+\lfloor w(M)/2\rfloor}H_{\dR}(M).

In the case that
\alpha_{M}^{+}
is an isomorphism, denote by
c^{+}(M,0)\in(F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C})^{\times}
a representative of the class of
\det(\alpha^{+}_{M})
in
(F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C})^{\times}/F^{\times}
, computed with respect to
F
-bases of
H_{B}
and
H_{\dR}
. We view this as a tuple
(c^{+}(\sigma,M,0))_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{F}}\in(\mathbb{C}^{\times}/F^{\times})^{\Sigma_{F}}
indexed by the embeddings of
F
. Moreover, we write
c^{+}(M,s_{0})
for
c^{+}(M(s_{0}),0)
and call this the
Deligne period
of
M
at

.

Remark 5.7
.

There is an analogous definition in the case that
w
is even, but for our purposes this is not required.

As mentioned above, Deligne conjectures that these periods describe the ‘irrational parts’ of
L
-values. To state this, we require one further definition.

Definition 5.8

(Critical value)
.

Given a
K
-motive
M
with coefficients in
F
, say

is a critical value for

if neither
L_{\infty}(M,s)
nor
L_{\infty}(M^{*}(1),s)
has a pole at
s_{0}
.

Conjecture 5.9

(=
[
4
, Conjecture 2.8]
)
.

Let
M
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-motive with coefficients in
F
and odd weight, and let
s_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
be a critical value for
M
. Then
\alpha_{M}^{+}
is an isomorphism and there exists
\mathcal{L}(M,s_{0})\in F
such that

(5.10)

L(M,s_{0})=c^{+}(M,s_{0})\cdot\mathcal{L}(M,s_{0}).

Remark 5.11
.

Conjecture
5.9
depends implicitly on the conjectural analytic continuation of
L(M,s)
. We take this as a standing assumption, and in practice use Magma to numerically verify the functional equation before computing any
L
-values.

Conjecture
5.9
extends to motives over any number field via Weil restriction, under which
L
-functions are invariant.

Example 5.12
.

Fix an abelian variety over a number field,
A/K
. (Alternatively, a curve
C/K
, seeing as
C
and
\Jac(C)
have the same first cohomologies.) BSD implicitly predicts

(5.13)

\frac{L(A,1)}{|\Delta_{K}|^{\dim A/2}\prod_{\nu\mid\infty}|\Omega_{A^{\nu}}|}\in\mathbb{Q}.

Here, this product is over the infinite places of
K
and
\Omega_{A^{\nu}}
is defined as follows: fix a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis
\mathcal{W}
for the holomorphic differentials
H^{0}(C,\Omega^{1}_{C})
and, for a given
\nu
, choose a basis
\Gamma_{\nu}
for the singular homology group
H_{1}(A^{\nu}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
such that, if
\nu
is a real place, then there exists some
\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}\subset\Gamma_{\nu}
which is a basis for
H_{1}(A^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
.

(5.14)

\Omega_{A^{\nu}}=\begin{cases}\det\left(\int_{\gamma}\omega\right)_{\omega\in\mathcal{W},\gamma\in\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}}&\text{ if }\nu\text{ is a real place}\\
\det\left(\int_{\gamma}\omega\right)_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}\cup\overline{\mathcal{W}},\gamma\in\Gamma_{\nu}}&\text{ if }\nu\text{ is a complex place.}\end{cases}

That this is consistent with Conjecture
5.9
can be seen from the formulation of BSD for abelian varieties over number fields in
[
15
]
, applying restriction of scalars and referring to
[
4

—the key observation being that

is isomorphic to the dual of

, as mentioned in Example
5.4
.

We give a brief explanation of the provenance of the period term in (
5.13
) from Deligne’s perspective, focussing for simplicity on the case that
K
is totally real. Firstly, we must pass to the restriction of scalars
R=\Res_{K/\mathbb{Q}}A
, which is an abelian variety over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
H_{B}^{+}(h^{1}(R))
has dual
H_{1}(R(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
, which we identify with
\prod_{\nu\mid\infty}H_{1}(A^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
, using
R(\mathbb{R})=A(K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R})\cong\prod_{\nu}A^{\nu}(\mathbb{R})
. We take a basis
\Gamma_{\nu}^{+}
for each place as above. On the other hand, for a basis
\{k_{i}\}
for
K/\mathbb{Q}
,
H^{+}_{\dR}(h^{1}(R))=H^{0}(R,\Omega^{1}_{R})
has basis
\{k_{i}\cdot\omega\mid\omega\in H^{0}(A,\Omega^{1}_{A}),i\in\{1,\ldots,[K:\mathbb{Q}]\}\}
.

Labelling the infinite places of
K
as
\nu_{j}
, the period matrix of
R
is now made up of
\dim A\times\dim A
blocks with the
(i,j)
-th block given by

\left(\nu_{j}(k_{i})\int_{\gamma}\omega\right)_{\omega\in\mathcal{W},\gamma\in\Gamma_{j}}=\nu_{j}(k_{i})\cdot P_{j},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ where }\hskip 5.69046ptP_{j}=\left(\int_{\gamma}\omega\right)_{\omega\in\mathcal{W},\gamma\in\Gamma_{j}},

using the classical result that
\omega\mapsto[\gamma\mapsto\int_{\gamma}\omega]
defines an isomorphism from de Rham cohomology to the dual of singular cohomology. In particular, this matrix is equal to

\left(\left(\nu_{j}(k_{i})\right)_{i,j}\otimes I_{g_{C}}\right)\cdot\text{diag}(P_{j})_{j},

where
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product. The determinant of
\left(\nu_{j}(k_{i})\right)_{i,j}\otimes I_{g_{C}}
—up to rationals—is
(\sqrt{\Delta_{K/\mathbb{Q}}})^{g_{C}}
by definition of the discriminant.

If
A
has coefficients in
F
, to compute the period
c^{+}(h^{1}(A),1)\in(F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C})^{\times}
as in (
5.10
), one must then choose

5.14
) with respect to these bases. The effect of forgetting this additional structure is to take the norm map

, in which case our periods and

5.13
) from Conjecture
5.9
.

We now return to the context of motivic pieces of curves. Given

,

,
\tau
as usual, write
\hat{\tau}
for the rational representation
\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\tau)/\mathbb{Q})}\sigma(\tau)
. Recall from Remark
2.3
that the piece
C^{\hat{\tau}}
corresponds to the motive of an abelian variety,
A_{\hat{\tau}}
, a quotient of
\Jac(C)
. This abelian variety has coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}(\tau)
, with the corresponding
L
-values being associated to the pieces
C^{\tau}
under different embeddings
\mathbb{Q}(\tau)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
or, equivalently, the Galois conjugate pieces under a fixed embedding. Applying Conjecture
5.9
to
A_{\hat{\tau}}
then gives a prediction for the
L
-values
L(C^{\sigma(\tau)},1)
.

Because the
\mathbb{Q}(\tau)
-coefficients are coming from the
G
-action, this is equivalent to taking the determinant of
\tau
-part of the period map from
H^{0}(\Res_{K/\mathbb{Q}}C,\Omega^{1}_{\Res_{K/\mathbb{Q}}C})
to the dual of
H_{1}(\Res_{K/\mathbb{Q}}C(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
, with different embeddings
\mathbb{Q}(\tau)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
corresponding to the periods of Galois-conjugate pieces.

5.3.
Superelliptic curves

Molin and Neurohr explicitly describe methods to compute period matrices associated to superelliptic curves over
\mathbb{C}
to high precision in
[
25
]
, with implementations of their work available at
[
24
]
. The methods they use do not give the full
2g\times 2g
-matrices as in the complex case of (
5.14
), but rather the
g\times 2g
-matrix

(5.15)

P_{\mathcal{W},\Gamma}=\left(\int_{\gamma}\omega\right)_{\omega\in\mathcal{W},\gamma\in\Gamma}

for specific choices
\mathcal{W}
and
\Gamma
of bases of
H^{0}(C,\Omega^{1}_{C})
and
H_{1}(C,\mathbb{Z})
, respectively. We explain how to extract periods of motivic pieces of superelliptic curves from their work.

Firstly, we fix a superelliptic curve
C/K:y^{m}=f(x)
and an embedding
\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, where
f
is separable and
K
is a number field containing the
m
-th roots of unity. Write
C_{\sigma}
for the complex curve corresponding to this embedding, and take
n=\deg f
,
\delta=\gcd(m,n)
. We consider the group of automorphisms
G\cong C_{m}
generated by
\alpha:y\mapsto\zeta_{m}\cdot y
.

Proposition 5.16

(=
[
25
, Proposition 3.8]
)
.

The set

(5.17)

\mathcal{W}=\left\{\omega_{i,j}:=\frac{x^{i-1}\emph{d}x}{y^{j}}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1,\hskip 5.69046pt1\leq j\leq m-1,\hskip 5.69046pt-mi+jn-\delta\geq 0\right\}

forms a basis for
H^{0}(C,\Omega^{1}_{C})
.

We will write
\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}=\{\sigma(\omega)\mid\omega\in\mathcal{W}\}
. The action of
G
on
H^{0}(C_{\sigma},\Omega^{1}_{C_{\sigma}})
is thus clear;
\alpha
acts on
\omega_{i,j}
by
\sigma(\zeta_{m})^{-j}
and
\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}
is already an eigenbasis for
\alpha
. The story for singular homology is less straightforward. To construct a basis for
H_{1}(C_{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
, for each pair
(a,b)
of branch points of the rational map
x
, a branch
y_{a,b}
of
C
is defined (see
[
25
, p. 6]
for details) and hence oriented paths

(5.18)

\gamma_{[a,b]}^{(\ell)}=\{(x,\sigma(\zeta_{m})^{\ell}\cdot y_{a,b}(x))\mid x\in[a,b]\},\hskip 14.22636pt\ell\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}

are obtained. Here
[a,b]
is the oriented line segment joining
a
and
b
in the complex plane. We obtain loops

\gamma_{a,b}^{(\ell)}=\gamma_{[a,b]}^{(\ell)}\cup\gamma_{[b,a]}^{(\ell)}

for each pair of branch points. Write
X
for the complete graph whose vertices are branch points of
x
, and
E
for the edges of any choice of spanning tree of
X
. Writing
\gamma_{e}^{(\ell)}
for
\gamma_{a,b}^{(\ell)}
, where
e
is the edge between vertices
a,b\in X
, we are now able to describe generators for
H_{1}(C_{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
.

Theorem 5.19

(=
[
25
, Theorem 3.6]
)
.

The set

(5.20)

\Gamma_{\sigma}=\{\gamma_{e}^{(\ell)}\mid 0\leq\ell<m-1,e\in E\}

forms a generating set for
H_{1}(C_{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
.

Remark 5.21
.

In general,
\Gamma_{\sigma}
is not a basis for
H_{1}(C_{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})
, although this is the case when
\delta=1
.

Remark 5.22
.

The reason for the strict inequality in (
5.20
) is that
\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\gamma_{e}^{(\ell)}=1
in
H_{1}(C_{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})=\pi_{1}(C_{\sigma})^{\text{ab}}
.

Molin and Neurohr then describe and implement a method for computing
\int_{\gamma}\omega
for
\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\sigma}
. We explain how to use their implementation to compute Deligne periods in this setting, writing
R=\Res_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})/\mathbb{Q}}C
.

Firstly, note that the graph
X
as above exists independently of any embedding
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and select a set
E
of edges of a spanning tree. For each such embedding
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})}
, we see from (
5.18
) that
\Gamma_{\sigma,E}:=\{\gamma_{e}^{(0)}\mid e\in E\}
generates
H_{1}(C_{\sigma},\mathbb{Q})
as a
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
-vector space. We will write
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma}\subseteq\Gamma_{\sigma,E}
for a
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
-basis of
H^{1}(C_{\sigma},\mathbb{Q})
. Taking the restriction of scalars and writing
\Sigma_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})}^{+}
for a set of coset representatives of
\Sigma_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})}
modulo complex conjugation—corresponding to infinite places of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
—we see that

\cup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})}^{+}}\{\gamma\mid\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma}\}

is a
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
-basis for
H_{1}(R(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Q})
after identifying with
\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})}^{+}}H_{1}(C_{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})
. We note also that
H^{0}(R,\Omega^{1}_{R})
has
\cup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})}}\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}
as a
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
-basis. Therefore, we wish to compute the determinant of the period map

\text{diag}\left(\left(\int_{\gamma}\omega\right)_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}\cup\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{\sigma},\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma}}\right)_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})}^{+}}.

To take the determinant of the
\tau
-part of this map, write
\mathcal{W}_{\sigma,\tau}
for
\{\omega\mid\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}\cup\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{\sigma},\alpha(\omega)/\omega=\tau(\alpha)\}
,
\pi_{\tau}
for the
\tau
-projector in
\mathbb{C}[G]
, and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma,\tau}\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma}
for a subset such that
\{\pi_{\tau}(\gamma)\mid\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma,\tau}\}
generates the
\tau
-component of
H_{1}(C_{\sigma},\mathbb{Q})
. We need to compute the determinants of the matrices

\left(\int_{\pi_{\tau}(\gamma)}\omega\right)_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{\sigma,\tau},\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma,\tau}}=\left(\int_{\gamma}\omega\right)_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{\sigma,\tau},\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma,\tau}}

as
\sigma
varies over
\Sigma_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})}^{+}
, where equality holds here because the comparison isomorphism is
G
-equivariant. We are viewing
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma,\tau}
as an element of
H_{1}(R(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z})
, on which complex conjugation acts trivially. In this context, we may replace
\int_{\gamma}\overline{\omega}
by
\overline{\int_{\gamma}\omega}
. We therefore compute our Deligne periods as

(5.23)

\Omega_{C^{\sigma(\tau)}}=\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})}^{+}}\det\begin{pmatrix}P_{\sigma,\tau}\\
\hline\cr\vskip-11.38092pt\\
\overline{P_{\overline{\sigma},\tau}}\end{pmatrix},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ where }\hskip 5.69046ptP_{\sigma,\tau}=\left(\int_{\gamma}\omega\right)_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}\cap\mathcal{W}_{\sigma,\tau},\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma,\tau}}.

Remark 5.24
.

Note that there is no discriminant term appearing here as in Example
5.12
. One could state Conjecture
5.25
for superelliptic curves over finite extensions
K
of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
, in which case an analogous term involving the relative discriminant
\Delta_{K/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})}
would appear. In practice, working over proper extensions of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
is computationally out-of-reach, so we are content with this simpler formulation.

Finally, Conjecture
5.9
predicts:

Conjecture 5.25
.

Let
C/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m}):y^{m}=f(x)
be as above, choose
\tau
a character of
C_{m}
. Fix an embedding
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. There exists
\mathcal{L}(C^{\tau})\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
such that, for
\sigma\in\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\tau)/\mathbb{Q})
,

(5.26)

L(C^{\sigma(\tau)},1)=\Omega_{C^{\sigma(\tau)}}\cdot\sigma(\mathcal{L}(C^{\tau})).

In the sequel we combine
[
24
]
with our implementations of Algorithm
3.1
to numerically verify Conjecture
5.25
for the motivic pieces of several superelliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})
and
\mathbb{Q}(i)
.

6.
Computations

6.1.
Exponent three

Here we present some computations of
L
-functions,
L
-values and periods for superelliptic curves
C/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3}):y^{3}=f(x)
with
\deg f=4
and
f
is separable. Such curves have genus
3
and are known as Picard curves.

Example 6.1
.

Consider the Picard curve

(6.2)

C/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3}):\hskip 5.69046pty^{3}=x^{4}+x^{3}+\zeta_{3}x^{2}+\zeta_{3}-2

and write
\tau
for a non-trivial character of
C_{3}
. We use an implementation of Algorithm
3.1
(see
[
28
,
Superelliptic_Exp3.m
]
) to compute Dirichlet series coefficients
L(C^{\tau},s)
, with the caveat being that we guess the correct Euler factor at the unique prime
\pi
above
3
and the conductor exponent at
3
from the conjectural functional equation. To guess the conductor, we assume that the bound

(6.3)

v_{\pi}(\mathcal{N}(C))\leq v_{\pi}(\Delta)

is satisfied, thereby reducing to a finite check. Note that (
6.3
) is not known to hold in this setting, but is generally expected to.

Taking
L_{\pi}(C^{\tau},s)=1
and
v_{\pi}(N(C^{\tau}))=12
, we find that

seems to satisfy its functional equation—the output of Magma’s
CFENew

15

10^{-11}

100,000

L
-functions of the pieces, we note that Magma estimates that a number of coefficients of the order
10^{9}
would be needed to naïvely verify the Hasse–Weil Conjecture for
C
to this same precision.

We compute
L(C^{\tau},1)\approx 1.84609002978320\ldots-i\cdot 1.54470301185825\ldots\neq 0

5.25
. We obtain the value
\Omega_{C^{\tau}}=-38.6473408677\ldots-i\cdot 105.727015294\ldots

5.23
) via the method described in §

, and compute

(6.4)

\frac{L(C^{\tau},1)}{\Omega_{C^{\tau}}}\approx-0.0185185185185\ldots+i\cdot 0.0106916716517\ldots\approx\frac{\zeta_{3}-1}{81}.

The error in the latter approximation is approximately
10^{-13}

81^{-6}
and so vastly smaller than one would expect from an approximation with denominator of this size, and roughly to the same precision to which we computed the
L
-function.

Because Galois acts as complex multiplication on
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})

and
\Omega_{C^{\overline{\tau}}}=\overline{\Omega_{C^{\tau}}}

5.25
would automatically follow if (

) were a precise equality.

Remark 6.5
.

The algorithm described in
[
21
]
allows one to provably compute the conductor exponent at
3
, so in principle one needn’t guess the conductor here. In practice this is implemented only for curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, making guess-work the easier option, although there is no theoretical obstacle to extending the implementation to number fields.

Example 6.6
.

Now consider the curve

(6.7)

C/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3}):\hskip 5.69046pty^{3}=x^{4}-3x^{3}+(1+\zeta_{3})x.

Using the same methods and notation as in Example

where the error in the latter approximation is again smaller than the precision to which our

Remark 6.9
.

In both examples we have taken Picard curves
C:y^{3}=f(x)
where the coefficients of

\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})

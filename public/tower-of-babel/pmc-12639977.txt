A clinical prediction model for postoperative delirium following surgical treatment of femoral fractures in older adult patients: development and validation study
Authors: Xue Liu, Xinye Song, Nan Chen, Mengmeng Bai, Yong Luan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivePostoperative delirium (POD) represents a significant postoperative complication in older adult patients undergoing femoral fracture surgery, associated with substantial mortality, extended hospitalization, and heightened healthcare expenditure. This study aimed to develop and validate a clinical model to predict POD in older adult patients with femoral fractures, facilitating early risk identification and targeted interventions.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed patients aged ≥ 65 years who underwent femoral surgery at the First Affiliated Hospital of Dalian Medical University from June 2017 to June 2023. Demographic, laboratory, and perioperative data were collected, with POD as the primary outcome. After multivariable logistic regression identified independent predictors, which were incorporated into a nomogram. The cohort was divided into training (70%) and validation (30%) sets. Model performance was assessed by area under the ROC curve (AUC), Hosmer–Lemeshow(HL) test, calibration plots, and decision curve analysis (DCA). The multivariable analysis identified only three independent predictors of postoperative delirium: tranexamic acid use, preoperative glucose levels, and preoperative lymphocyte counts.ResultsAmong 2,029 patients, POD incidence was 23.8%. Multiple factors were associated with POD in univariable analysis, including history of cerebral infarction, laboratory abnormalities, and perioperative factors. However, multivariable analysis identified only three independent predictors. Notably, tranexamic acid use (adjusted OR = 0.32, 95% CI: 0.24–0.44) and higher preoperative lymphocyte counts (adjusted OR = 0.60, 95% CI: 0.48–0.75) were protective, while elevated preoperative blood glucose increased risk (adjusted OR = 1.41, 95% CI: 1.35–1.49). The model showed strong discrimination (AUC 0.85 training, 0.80 validation) and good calibration (test HL P = 0.385). DCA indicated significant net clinical benefit within probability thresholds of 0.2–0.6. Sensitivity and specificity at the optimal cut-off point (0.257) were 82.4% (95% CI: 80.0%-85.0%) and 75.0% (95% CI: 71.0%-80.0%), respectively.ConclusionThis validated model, integrating multidimensional perioperative factors, enables individualized risk stratification for POD in older adult femoral fracture patients. Key predictors include tranexamic acid use, preoperative glucose, and preoperative lymphocyte counts, supporting early identification and preventive strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12871-025-03465-5.Keywords: Aged, Femoral Fractures, Orthopedic Procedures, Delirium, Neuropsychological Tests, Clinical Prediction Rule

Introduction

With the acceleration of the global aging process, the incidence of geriatric femoral fractures has been increasing annually [1–4]. According to a 2019 study, the annual incidence rate of femoral fractures in older adults aged 65 years and older is approximately 124 per 100,000, while in China, this figure is 135 per 100,000 [1]. The incidence of postoperative delirium (POD) following femoral surgery in older adult patients varies widely across studies, with reported rates typically ranging from 15% to 50% [2, 3]. Research has shown that postoperative delirium not only affects patients’ postoperative recovery but may also lead to long - term cognitive decline and reduced quality of life [4, 5]. Geriatric femoral surgery typically includes procedures such as femoral neck fracture repair and hip arthroplasty, aiming to restore function at the fracture site and promote early mobility in patients. Anesthesia, postoperative pain management, and other perioperative factors during the surgical process can contribute to the occurrence of POD. Furthermore, research indicates that surgical trauma may induce delirium through mechanisms such as neuroinflammation and neurotransmitter imbalance [6]. In addition, comorbidities, pre - operative cognitive status, and mental health conditions of older adult patients are also closely associated with the occurrence of POD [7, 8].

The risk factors for POD following femoral surgery in older adult patients are complex and multifactorial. A systematic review and meta-analysis involving 3,555 older adult patients aged 65 and older revealed no significant difference in the incidence of POD between regional anesthesia (RA) and general anesthesia (GA) [9]. Another prospective observational study found that pre-existing sleep disorders were independently associated with POD, and postoperative admission to the ICU was also a risk factor [10]. Additionally, studies have indicated that factors such as age, male sex, preoperative erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), and postoperative pain scores are associated with the incidence of POD in older adult patients. For example, advanced age is significantly associated with increased risk of developing delirium [11]. The pathogenesis of postoperative delirium (POD) involves multiple interdependent mechanisms, with neuroinflammation emerging as a critical pathway. A study has demonstrated that preoperative corticosteroid administration effectively ameliorates the inflammatory response induced by surgery, thereby reducing the incidence and severity of cognitive complications. This landmark study provided compelling evidence for the inflammatory hypothesis of POD, showing that dexamethasone reduced the risk of early cognitive decline after cardiac surgery while significantly attenuating the release of inflammatory markers, including C-reactive protein and interleukin-6 [12]. Although existing literature has identified multiple risk factors, the underlying mechanisms and interactions among these factors remain unclear, underscoring the pressing need for further high-quality research to elucidate this issue. Recent expert consensus on intensive care big data applications strongly recommends the development of machine learning-based prediction models for early identification of patients at risk for various postoperative complications, emphasizing the value of multidimensional clinical data in improving risk stratification [13]. According to this consensus, prediction models should follow standardized development and validation processes with clear evaluation metrics for clinical implementation. This approach enhances not only the model’s statistical rigor but also its practical utility in clinical settings.

This study aims to develop a clinical prediction model to evaluate the association between geriatric femoral surgery and POD. We will enroll a specific number of older adult patients and systematically analyze preoperative and intraoperative multidimensional clinical data to identify key risk factors influencing POD and provide empirical support for clinical practice. By focusing on factors available before and during surgery, our model aims to enable early risk stratification when preventive interventions can be most effective. The research findings will provide critical evidence for perioperative management in older adult patients, assisting in the development of personalized prevention and intervention strategies to improve postoperative outcomes and quality of life.

Study methods

Inclusion criteria and exclusion criteria

This study was a retrospective cohort study, and the enrolled patients were all treated at the First Affiliated Hospital of Dalian Medical University from June 2017 to June 2023. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Dalian Medical University (Ethics Review No.: [PJ-KS-KY-2025-233]). Eligible patients were those aged ≥ 65 years who underwent femoral fracture surgery under general anesthesia at our institution during the study period. Inclusion Criteria: (1) older adult patients aged over 65 years with a clinical diagnosis of femoral fracture; (2) Patients who underwent closed reduction and intramedullary nailing, open reduction and internal fixation of the femur, or artificial femoral head replacement under GA; (3) American Society of Anesthesiologists Physical Status Classification System(ASA): Class I-III; (4) Patients with complete clinical and imaging data. Exclusion Criteria: (1) patients with preoperative delirium; (2) patients with psychiatric disorders that impair effective communication; (3) patients with concurrent traumatic brain injury, dementia, intellectual disability, or other psychiatric disorders; (4) patients requiring postoperative transfer to the intensive care unit (ICU) for critical care; (5) patients with terminal illnesses (e.g., malignancies) who voluntarily declined treatment; (6) patients who received regional anesthesia instead of general anesthesia. The data were collected through the hospital’s electronic medical record system and inpatient record system to ensure data integrity and accuracy. Due to the retrospective cohort of the study, all patient information was fully anonymized. Patient data were processed in strict compliance with medical ethics guidelines and privacy protection principles to ensure ethical adherence and data security throughout the research process.

Surgery and anesthesia

This retrospective analysis exclusively evaluated patients who underwent femoral fracture surgical repair under general anesthesia.

Surgical procedures were standardized based on fracture type

Surgical interventions were methodically executed based on fracture classification protocols. Patients with femoral neck fractures received either closed reduction with internal fixation using cannulated screws or artificial femoral head replacement utilizing biological or cemented prostheses. For intertrochanteric fractures, the standard protocol involved closed reduction followed by intramedullary fixation with proximal femoral nail anti-rotation (PFNA) devices. All procedures were exclusively performed by attending-level orthopedic surgeons possessing a minimum of five years’ specialized experience in geriatric fracture management, thereby ensuring consistent surgical quality and adherence to established clinical protocols.

Anesthesia management

Our standardized anesthetic protocol incorporated propofol for induction, sevoflurane with remifentanil infusion for maintenance, and cisatracurium for neuromuscular blockade, complemented by multimodal analgesia. Notably, regional anesthetic techniques were not incorporated into standard practice during the study period, thereby ensuring methodological consistency in our assessment of postoperative delirium risk factors in this clinical population receiving exclusively general anesthesia.

Variables and definitions

POD is a serious postoperative complication of the central nervous system (CNS), characterized by altered consciousness, cognitive dysfunction, and disrupted sleep-wake cycle after surgery [14, 15]. POD can also increase the incidence of other postoperative complications, delay treatment, and lead to increased healthcare costs, placing a significant burden on patients and their families. POD was assessed in all surgical patients by a uniformly trained team at our hospital using the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) [16], the internationally standardized diagnostic tool, on postoperative days 1 to 5. The CAM criteria are as follows: (1) acute onset with fluctuating course; (2) inattention; (3) altered level of consciousness; (4) disorganized thinking. Delirium was diagnosed if both criteria (1) and (2) were met, along with either criterion (3) or (4). Patients were monitored daily for delirium occurrence and categorized into delirium or non-delirium groups based on these assessments.

Exposure variables were recorded in the database as binary variables (yes/no) to facilitate statistical analysis. The outcome variable was the development and prediction of a clinical predictive model, constructed using multivariable logistic regression analysis. Model performance was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and the area under the curve (AUC). Covalriates include: (1) Demographic and clinical data: age, sex, underlying diseases, comorbidities; (2) Perioperative variables: American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status classification, anesthesia type, surgical duration, intraoperative blood loss, intraoperative blood transfusion (yes/no), and intraoperative medications; (3) Preoperative laboratory indices: hemoglobin, white blood cells, lymphocytes, albumin, total bilirubin, preoperative blood glucose, C-reactive protein (CRP), and prothrombin time. These variables were selected based on prior literature and clinical expert consensus. Missing data were addressed using multiple imputation methods to minimize bias and enhance the validity of statistical analyses. The proportion of missing data was reported in detail, and its potential impact on study outcomes was evaluated through completeness analysis.

Statistical methods

The source data were cleaned, and missing values were evaluated. For variables with a relatively low proportion of missing values, multiple imputation was used to reduce bias. SPSS 26.0 and R 4.0 were employed for multivariable logistic regression analysis and nomogram construction. Continuous variables were described as the mean ± standard deviation or the median (interquartile range) and were analyzed using the t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were described as frequencies and percentages and were analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. In the univariate analysis, variables with P < 0.10 and clinically significant factors were included in the multivariable logistic regression model. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed using the forward stepwise selection method (likelihood ratio test) to identify independent predictors of POD. In the forward selection process, variables with P < 0.10 in univariable analysis were initially considered for iterative addition. This approach balances parsimony and predictive accuracy, particularly suitable for reducing model complexity when handling multiple potential predictors. The regression coefficients, odds ratios (ORs), and their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported to identify potential independent influencing factors.

In terms of model construction, all patients were randomly stratified into training and validation sets at a 7:3 ratio. ROC and AUC were compared between the training and validation sets to evaluate discriminative ability. HL test and calibration curves were used to assess the goodness-of-fit and calibration of the model, while decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate its clinical utility across different risk thresholds. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, and likelihood ratios with corresponding 95% confidence intervals were calculated to enhance clinical interpretability of the model. A two-tailed P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic differences

Figure 1 shows the patient enrollment flowchart. A total of 2,029 patients were enrolled in the study, categorized into a delirium group (n = 482) and a non-delirium group (n = 1,547) (Fig. 1.) Table 1 shows the demographic and clinical characteristics of patients with and without POD. Compared to the non-delirium group, the delirium group exhibited significantly elevated levels of INR (P = 0.006), preoperative glucose (P < 0.001), CRP (P = 0.003), and neutrophil count (P < 0.001). Conversely, serum potassium (P = 0.022), magnesium (P = 0.022), hemoglobin (P = 0.020), preoperative lymphocyte count (P < 0.001), and eosinophil percentage (P = 0.002) were significantly reduced in the delirium group. The delirium group had a significantly lower intraoperative tranexamic acid administration rate compared to the non-delirium group (P < 0.001), while the incidence of cerebral infarction was significantly higher (P = 0.036). No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups in demographic characteristics, chronic comorbidities, perioperative factors, or medication use (e.g., age: P = 0.692; gender: P = 0.057; length of stay: P = 0.168) (Table 1).

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses

Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses identified intraoperative tranexamic acid (TXA) administration, preoperative lymphocyte count, and preoperative glucose levels as independent predictors of POD. Univariable analysis revealed that TXA administration was associated with a significantly reduced risk of POD (OR = 0.21, 95% CI: 0.16–0.28, P < 0.001), whereas elevated glucose levels (OR = 1.47, 95% CI: 1.39–1.55, P < 0.001) and decreased preoperative lymphocyte counts (OR = 0.50, 95% CI: 0.41–0.62, P < 0.001) were associated with an increased risk of POD. Multivariable analysis further confirmed that TXA administration was associated with a 68% reduction in the risk of POD (adjusted OR = 0.32, 95% CI: 0.24–0.44, P < 0.001). Additionally, for every 1-unit increase in lymphocyte count, the risk of POD decreased by 40% (adjusted OR = 0.60, 95% CI: 0.48–0.75, P < 0.001), whereas for every 1-unit increase in glucose level, the risk increased by 41% (adjusted OR = 1.41, 95% CI: 1.35–1.49, P < 0.001) (Table 2).

Development and validation of a clinical prediction model

In this study, a retrospective cohort analysis of 2,029 older adult patients aged 65 years or older who underwent femoral surgery was conducted to develop a clinical prediction model for POD. Multivariable logistic regression analysis identified intraoperative use of TXA, preoperative blood glucose levels, and preoperative lymphocyte count as independent risk factors. A nomogram was constructed to visually illustrate the impact of these factors on the risk of POD.The model demonstrated favorable predictive performance, with AUCs of 0.85 in the training set and 0.80 in the validation set, providing an effective clinical tool for personalized risk assessment and targeted intervention for POD in older adult patients (Figs. 2 and 3) (Table 3).

To enhance clinical utility, we determined the optimal cutoff threshold using the Youden Index method. A nomogram score of 105 points (corresponding to a predicted probability of 25.7%) was identified as the optimal threshold for classifying patients at high risk for POD. At this threshold, the model demonstrated a sensitivity of 82.4% (95% CI: 80.0–85.0%), specificity of 75.0% (95% CI: 71.0–80.0%), positive predictive value of 92.2% (95% CI: 90.0–93.0%), and negative predictive value of 56.0% (95% CI: 51.0–61.0%) in the validation cohort.

How to use the nomogram

To use this nomogram, locate the patient’s value for each variable on the corresponding axis and draw a vertical line up to the ‘Points’ line to determine the points associated with that value. Sum all points and locate this total on the “Total Points” axis. Draw a vertical line down from this point to find the predicted probability of postoperative delirium on the “Risk of POD” axis. For example, a patient with preoperative lymphocyte count of 1.0 × 10^9/L (80 points), preoperative blood glucose of 7.0 mmol/L (18 points), and intraoperative tranexamic acid use (0 points) would have a total score of 98 points, corresponding to approximately 20% risk of POD.

Risk classification

(1)Low risk: <0.257 probability (total points < 105);(2)High risk: >0.257 probability (total points > 105).

Clinical calibration assessment

The calibration curves demonstrated good agreement between predicted and observed probabilities within the observed probability range (0.2–0.4) for both the training and validation sets, with most points closely following the ideal diagonal line. However, it should be noted that our model did not predict probabilities in the extreme ranges (< 0.2 or > 0.8), limiting our ability to assess calibration across the full probability spectrum.

Specifically, the HL test showed a χ² value of 8.506 (P = 0.385) in the training set and 6.673 (P = 0.57) in the validation set, indicating no statistically significant lack of fit and suggesting that the model did not exhibit systematic overestimation or underestimation. The Bias-corrected curve and Apparent curve were nearly identical, demonstrating minimal overfitting and stable predictive performance across both small and large sample sizes. Additionally, in the low probability range (< 0.2) and high probability range (> 0.8), the curves of both the training and validation sets closely aligned with the Ideal curve, further confirming the model’s accuracy in extreme risk stratification (Figs. 4 and 5).

Clinical utility assessment

The DCA revealed that in both the training and validation sets, the prediction model yielded substantial clinical net benefit across risk thresholds ranging from 0 to 0.8. Specifically, across risk thresholds of 0.2 to 0.6, the model demonstrated maximum clinical net benefit, indicating its ability to provide reliable clinical decision support for clinicians.This finding highlights the potential for flexible threshold adjustment in clinical practice, tailored to individual patient characteristics, to optimize postoperative management strategies and reduce the incidence of delirium (Figs. 6 and 7).

Discussion

This study utilized a retrospective cohort design to evaluate a clinical prediction model for POD in patients aged 65 years or older undergoing femoral surgery. A total of 2,029 patients were included, and multivariable logistic regression analysis identified several independent risk factors, including intraoperative TXA administration, preoperative blood glucose levels, and preoperative lymphocyte counts. The prediction model developed based on these factors demonstrates favorable predictive performance. Further evaluation using the HL test and DCA confirmed the model’s effectiveness in clinical calibration and utility. This prediction model provides a robust basis for assessing POD risk in older adult patients undergoing femoral surgery, enabling the development of personalized prevention and intervention strategies to improve patient outcomes.

Our study demonstrates a significant association between intraoperative TXA administration and reduced incidence of POD in older adult patients undergoing femoral surgery. Compared with existing evidence, these findings propose TXA as a potential pharmacological strategy for POD mitigation in this high-risk population. Mechanistic exploration suggests that TXA may exert its effects through multimodal pathways, including modulation of inflammatory cascades, preservation of blood-brain barrier integrity, and attenuation of hyperfibrinolysis-induced cerebral microemboli. Biochemical analyses revealed that TXA administration modulates inflammatory responses, as reflected by significant changes in key immunoinflammatory biomarkers. Clinical studies have shown that TXA reduces plasma levels of tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), interleukin-10 (IL-10), and interleukin-6 (IL-6) within 2 h of administration. Concurrently, TXA upregulates CD83—a critical immunostimulatory dendritic cell marker—while downregulating immunosuppressive biomarkers such as programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) and latency-associated peptide (LAP) [17]. Another study demonstrated that a 2 - year long - term, low - dose treatment with TXA in mice improved age - related declines in memory and learning capacity. This was achieved by suppressing plasmin production, brain levels of interleukin − 1β (IL − 1β) and TNF - α, and microglial activation [18]. The reduced POD incidence in older adult patients with TXA administration may be attributed to its potential physiological mechanisms.First, TXA may mitigate the risk of POD through anti-inflammatory mechanisms. Research has shown that delirium severity correlates with elevated postoperative interleukin-8 (IL-8) and IL-10 levels (both P < 0.05), with TXA administration significantly reducing these cytokine concentrations [19]. Furthermore, evidence from clinical studies confirms a strong association between postoperative inflammatory cascades and delirium development [20], suggesting that TXA-mediated attenuation of pro-inflammatory cytokine release may underlie its protective effects against POD.Second, regarding surgical stress, operative tissue injury triggers a systemic inflammatory cascade involving the release of cytokines (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α, IL-10) and activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis, driving leukocyte migration to injured sites via chemotaxis. Excessive inflammation disrupts wound healing and contributes to complications such as postoperative pain, fatigue, atrial fibrillation, acute kidney injury, and cognitive dysfunction [21]. TXA mitigates these adverse effects by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine release, thereby improving clinical outcomes and potentially lowering the risk of POD.Studies have demonstrated that perioperative administration of TXA in adult cardiac surgery patients significantly reduces the concentrations of IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α, and neutrophils. These findings indicate that TXA possesses anti-inflammatory properties and mitigates perioperative inflammation in this population [22]. Furthermore, our results are consistent with these observations.Third, TXA is a conventional antifibrinolytic agent. Fibrinolysis is recognized as a hallmark of the systemic inflammatory response [23], and plasmin inhibition can mitigate certain ischemic events [24]. Additionally, perioperative blood transfusion has been identified as a risk factor for increased infection risk [25], while inflammation itself influences coagulation initiation and propagation [26]. TXA has been shown to reduce perioperative blood loss and transfusion requirements in surgical patients [27–29], thereby further decreasing the incidence of infection. The anti-inflammatory effects of TXA may represent one of the mechanisms by which it reduces the risk of POD in older adult patients undergoing femoral surgery.This apparent discrepancy can be explained by several factors: Our findings revealed a paradoxical relationship: despite similar intraoperative blood loss between groups, TXA was administered significantly less frequently to patients who developed POD. This suggests TXA may possess neuroprotective effects beyond hemostasis. Research indicates TXA exhibits direct anti-inflammatory properties by inhibiting plasmin-mediated inflammatory cascades, enhancing blood-brain barrier integrity, and potentially mitigating systemic inflammatory responses through reduction of damage-associated molecular pattern release. These mechanisms may explain the inverse correlation between TXA administration and POD incidence despite similar hemorrhagic profiles. This relationship warrants further investigation, particularly as postoperative anemia remains a recognized risk factor for cognitive dysfunction.

Furthermore, our findings demonstrate that decreased preoperative lymphocyte counts are significantly associated with the development of POD, with each 1 × 109/L decrement corresponding to a 1.6-fold elevation in delirium risk (OR 1.6 per 1 × 109/L decrease, 95% CI [1.2–2.1], P < 0.01).Lymphocytes, as essential cellular components of the immune system, play a critical role in modulating immune responses. Alterations in preoperative lymphocyte counts may reflect the body’s adaptive neuroinflammatory response to surgical stress. During surgery, the body typically undergoes significant stress, leading to the release of inflammatory mediators that affect both lymphocyte function and count. A decrease in lymphocyte count may in turn contribute to excessive activation of the inflammatory response. A recent systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrated that elevated preoperative neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) is significantly associated with increased risk of POD, underscoring the pivotal role of neuroinflammatory mechanisms in delirium pathogenesis [30]. Emerging evidence demonstrates that an elevated monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR) is independently associated with an increased risk of POD, further supporting the pivotal role of neuroimmune crosstalk in delirium pathogenesis [31]. Additional research has indicated that the occurrence of POD is closely associated with neuroinflammation. A decrease in lymphocytes may represent an adaptive response to this inflammatory state, yet it may also contribute to further damage to the nervous system [32]. Additionally, studies have demonstrated that elevated preoperative NLR may serve as a risk factor for POD, potentially mediating its pathogenesis through increased P-tau/T-tau levels [33]. Our multivariable analysis further revealed a significant association between preoperative lymphocyte count and the risk of POD. Therefore, a decrease in preoperative lymphocyte count may not only serve as a marker of the postoperative inflammatory response but also play a critical role in the pathogenesis of POD. Future research should aim to elucidate the specific mechanisms by which lymphocytes contribute to the development of POD and explore strategies to reduce its incidence by modulating lymphocyte function. These investigations may provide novel clinical intervention strategies to improve the prognosis of POD in older adult patients.

The association between perioperative blood glucose levels and POD has emerged as a critical focus in perioperative medicine research. Our study revealed that preoperative hyperglycemia (defined as blood glucose >180 mg/dL) was independently associated with a 45% increased risk of POD, a finding that aligns with and extends the current understanding of neurovascular coupling impairment in delirium pathogenesis.Emerging evidence from a prospective cohort study of geriatric hip fracture patients demonstrated a dose-dependent relationship between preoperative hyperglycemia and the risk of POD, with each 1 mmol/L increment in fasting preoperative blood glucose associated with a 6% increase in delirium risk (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR] 1.06, 95% CI 1.02–1.10, p = 0.003) [34]. This relationship was further validated in a multicenter registry analysis involving 25,843 non-cardiac surgical patients, where sustained perioperative hyperglycemia (mean glucose >8.3 mmol/L) independently predicted a 35% higher incidence of POD (aHR 1.35, 95% CI 1.18–1.56, p < 0.001), even after adjusting for surgical complexity and comorbidities [35]. A deeper investigation into the potential mechanisms by which hyperglycemia contributes to POD indicates an association with blood-brain barrier (BBB) dysfunction. The BBB, as the brain’s first line of defense, strictly regulates the passage of substances to maintain cerebral homeostasis. Cerebral microvascular endothelial cells play a critical role in preserving the integrity of tight junctions and the physiological functions of the BBB [36]. Consequently, the survival or apoptosis of these endothelial cells directly affects BBB function and vascular stability.Recent studies have demonstrated that hyperglycemia significantly impairs the survival of cerebral microvascular endothelial cells, thereby disrupting CNS function, leading to neuronal injury and neurodegenerative changes, which ultimately contribute to the development of POD [37, 38]. The potential mechanisms by which hyperglycemia induces POD may also be closely associated with neuroinflammation and oxidative stress. Poor preoperative glycemic control, dyslipidemia, and vascular injury can all result in neuronal damage, causing structural and functional brain alterations that precipitate cognitive decline and ultimately contribute to POD [39]. Recent studies have revealed that hyperglycemia disrupts neuroprotective mechanisms by inhibiting the expression and activity of hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF), resulting in damage to neurons and glial cells in the brain. This leads to abnormalities in neural conduction and synaptic connectivity, exacerbating postoperative cerebral hypoxia and contributing to the development of POD [40]. This mechanism provides an explanation for the increased susceptibility to POD in patients with hyperglycemia.

This study developed a clinical prediction model for older adult patients undergoing femoral surgery, with the following clinical implications: First, the model provides a personalized risk assessment tool for POD, facilitating early identification of high-risk patients. Second, it integrates multiple clinical and biochemical indicators, enhancing the accuracy and comprehensiveness of predictions. Finally, the model offers a scientific basis for formulating personalized prevention and intervention strategies. The strengths of this study lie in its robust study design and comprehensive data analysis. Using the HL test and DCA, we validated the calibration of the model and demonstrated its utility in clinical practice. The model provides the greatest clinical benefit when the threshold probability is set between 0.2 and 0.6. The protocol begins with a comprehensive preoperative assessment of the patient’s glucose level (mmol/L) and lymphocyte count (×10⁹/L), followed by risk calculation through the application of these values to an established nomogram. We propose that patients with scores ≥ 105 points could benefit from enhanced preventive interventions, including perioperative cognitive monitoring, glycemic control prior to surgical intervention, and early intraoperative administration of TXA (in the absence of contraindications) However, clinicians should view this threshold as a guide rather than an absolute rule, considering the continuous nature of risk and the trade-off between sensitivity and specificity.

Limitations

Despite the significant progress achieved in this study, certain limitations remain. Future research should further investigate the underlying mechanisms of these factors and conduct multicenter clinical studies to optimize clinical management strategies. The homogeneity of general anesthesia protocols without postoperative ICU admission in our study, while strengthening internal validity, constrains the applicability of our findings to alternative anesthetic approaches. Despite institutional standardization, our retrospective design could not account for potential cognitive effects arising from variations in specific anesthetic agents and dosages. Prospective research examining our predictive model across diverse anesthetic techniques would elucidate the relationship between anesthesia methodology and identified postoperative delirium risk factors. Meanwhile, The calibration curves show the relatively narrow range of predicted probabilities limits our ability to assess model calibration at the extremes of the risk spectrum, which should be addressed in future external validation studies with potentially more heterogeneous populations.

Conclusions

The POD prediction model developed in this study integrates key indicators, including TXA, preoperative blood glucose levels, and preoperative lymphocyte count, revealing the complex associations of these factors in the occurrence of POD among older adult patients undergoing femoral surgery. This model aims to provide an important theoretical foundation and practical guidance for precision medicine in clinical practice.

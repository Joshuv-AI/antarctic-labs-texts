Elliptic and Pseudo-Parabolic Gradient System Arising from Anisotropic Image-Denoising with Orientation Adaptation
Naotaka Ukai

Abstract




\footcomment

AMS Subject Classification:
35K70, 35K59, 35K61, 35J62.

Keywords: Elliptic equation, Pseudo-parabolic equation, Image-denoising, Orientation adaptation, Nonconvex energy functional, Well-posedness

Abstract.

In this paper, we consider a coupled system of nonlinear elliptic and pseudo-parabolic PDEs arising in anisotropic monochrome image-denoising with an orientation adaptation. This system is motivated by the minimization of a nonconvex energy functional. This study focuses on the treatment of the initial data for the orientation variable. Previous models have not provided explicit procedures or clarified a natural and convincing method for its determination. To resolve this issue, we propose a formulation in which the time derivative of the orientation variable is removed. This enables the initial value to be computed automatically within the equation. This formulation weakens the energy-dissipation property and introduces new challenges in constructing a stable minimization process. Consequently, a different approach from previous works is required. In light of the above, this paper aims to establish the well-posedness and energy-dissipation for our system. The proofs are based on a time-discretization method. The proposed time-discrete scheme determines the initial orientation data and ensures consistency with the continuous model. These results guarantee the stability and effectiveness of the proposed method for numerical implementation and provide a method for determining the initial data of orientation in the time-discretization scheme.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following elliptic and pseudo-parabolic system, denoted by (S).

(S):

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\kappa\mathit{\Delta}\alpha+\nabla\gamma(R(\alpha)\nabla u)\cdot R(\alpha+{\textstyle\frac{\pi}{2}})\nabla u\ni 0\mbox{ in $Q$,}\\[4.30554pt]
\alpha=0\mbox{ on $\Sigma$,}\end{cases}

(0.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}\bigl(\,{{}^{\top}R}(\alpha)\nabla\gamma(R(\alpha)\nabla u)+\nu|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+\mu\nabla\partial_{t}u\,\bigr)\\
\hskip 17.22217pt+\lambda(u-u_{\mathrm{org}})\ni 0\mbox{ in $Q$,}\\[4.30554pt]
u=\partial_{t}u=0\mbox{ on $\Sigma$, ~~ $u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)$, $x\in\Omega$.}\end{cases}

(0.2)

This system is motivated by minimization process of the following energy-functional:

\displaystyle E:[\alpha,u]\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\mapsto E(\alpha,u):=\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\alpha|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\nu}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\gamma(R(\alpha)\nabla u)\,dx+\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u-u_{\mathrm{org}}|^{2}\,dx\in[0,\infty).

(0.3)

In this context,
T\in(0,\infty)
is fixed, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
. The set
Q:=(0,T)\times\Omega
is the space-time cylindrical domain, and
\Sigma:=(0,T)\times\Gamma
is its lateral boundary. The coefficients
\kappa
,
\mu
,
\nu
, and
\lambda
are fixed positive constants, and
p>2
is a fixed real exponent.

The energy functional
E
is based on the energy proposed by Berkels et al.
[
5
]
, which was formulated for high-precision denoising of gray-scale images with orientation adaptation. A similar formulation of this energy is also adopted in
[
2
]
, where theoretical results concerning the well-posedness (i.e., existence, uniqueness, and continuous dependence on initial data) of the derived pseudo-parabolic system are established.

The original study
[
5
]
focuses on specialized image data in which characteristic polygonal patterns frequently appear, such as in satellite photographs of urban areas. In this model, the unknown
u
represents the gray-scale intensity distribution over a monochrome image region
\Omega
. To capture the local orientation of structural features, a fixed anisotropy function
\gamma
and an additional unknown variable
\alpha
are introduced. The anisotropy function
\gamma
is constructed as a non-Euclidean norm, whose unit closed ball, called Wulff shape, is designed to match the target polygonal structure. The variable
\alpha
represents the orientation of the anisotropy of polygonal structure.

A central idea in
[
5
]
is the introduction of an additional unknown parameter
\alpha
, which enables the adaptation of the orientation of polygonal patterns. The effectiveness of this mechanism has been confirmed through numerical experiments presented in their paper.

On the other hand, the energies considered in
[
5
,
2
]
are nonconvex, and minimal uniqueness of minimizers is not guaranteed. Therefore, in the mathematical analysis of the associated minimization problem, the estimate of the initial condition for the corresponding minimization schemes plays an important role, since the quality of the output strongly depends on the initial data. For the image data
u
, it is reasonable and natural to set the given data
u_{\mathrm{org}}
as the initial condition. In contrast, for the orientation variable
\alpha
, it is quite difficult to obtain reliable reference data. To date, however, the concrete procedure for specifying such an estimated initial condition has not been clarified, and no convincing or natural method has been explicitly provided in the previous works, including
[
5
,
2
]
.

In this paper, we aim to address this issue by developing a method that enables the orientation
\alpha
to be computed automatically within the equation. As a possible approach, we consider the system (S), which is derived from the following evolution equation:

\displaystyle-\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
(I+\mu(-\mathit{\Delta}_{0}))u^{\prime}(t)\end{matrix}\right]=\frac{\delta}{\delta[\alpha,u]}E(\alpha(t),u(t))

(0.4)

in
[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
, for
t\in(0,T)
.

(0.5)

obtained by removing the time derivative term of
\alpha
from the model proposed in
[
2
]
. Here,
u^{\prime}
denotes the time derivative of
u
,
\frac{\delta}{\delta[\alpha,u]}E
represents the variation of the energy functional, and
-\mathit{\Delta}_{0}
denotes the maximal monotone operator associated with the Laplacian under homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.

In the system (
0.4
), the absence of the time derivative of
\alpha
has the advantage that the initial data of
\alpha
is automatically determined by the system itself. However, this simultaneously weakens the driving force of energy dissipation, and consequently it is expected to be more challenging to construct a stable minimization process based on the system (
0.4
).

In view of these, we set the goal of this paper to establish the following Main Theorem:

Main Theorem:
Well-posedness and energy-dissipation for the system (S).

The main theorem of this paper is proved by means of a time-discretization method. The time-discretization scheme adopted here is also intended as a proposal for a numerical computation scheme. Although it is possible to use other time-discretization schemes to prove the main theorem, those schemes do not specify the concrete procedure for computing the initial data of
\alpha
. In contrast, the scheme proposed here clearly specifies how to determine the initial data of
\alpha
. This feature constitutes a significant advantage from the viewpoint of implementation.

Outline.
Section 1 provides the necessary preliminaries. Based on these, the main theorem is stated in Section 2. In order to prove them, Section 3 introduces a time-discretization scheme for the system (S) and prepares several auxiliary results. Finally, using the results of Section 3, the main theorem is proved in Section 4.

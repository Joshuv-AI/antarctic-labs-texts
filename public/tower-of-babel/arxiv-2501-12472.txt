On Polyconvexity and Almgren Uniform Ellipticity With Respect to Polyhedral Test Pairs
Maciej Lesniak

Abstract
We study anisotropic geometric energy functionals defined on a class of k k -dimensional surfaces in a Euclidean space. The classical notion of ellipticity, coming from Almgren, for such functionals is investigated. We prove a variant of a recent result of De Rosa, Lei, and Young and show that uniform ellipticity of an anisotropic energy functional with respect to real polyhedral chains implies uniform polyconvexity of the integrand.



1
Introduction

Let
\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
denote the Grassmannian of oriented
k
-planes in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
. Given
a continuous integrand
F:\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)\to(0,\infty)
, one defines
anisotropic
energy functional

\Phi_{F}:\mathbf{P}_{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\to\mathbf{R}
, acting on
k
-dimensional
polyhedral chains (see §
4
for relevant definitions)
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
by

\Phi_{F}(T)=\int F(\vec{T}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\|T\|(x)\quad\text{for }T\in\mathbf{P}_{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n}),

where
\|T\|
is a Radon measure over
\mathbf{R}^{n}
and
\vec{T}
is a
\|T\|
-measurable
function with values in
\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
.

Assuming
F
is even (i.e.
F(-\xi)=F(\xi)
for
\xi\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
) we may consider

F
to be defined on the unoriented Grassmannian and then the functional
\Phi_{F}
can be
extended to act on varifolds by setting
\Phi_{F}(V)=\int F(T)\,\mathrm{d}V(x,T)
for
a
k
-dimensional varifold
V
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
; see
[
All72
]
. Almgren studied the
regularity of varifolds which are local minimisers of
\Phi_{F}
and proved that they are
almost everywhere regular, provided
F
is
uniformly elliptic
(abbreviated AUE)

[
Alm68
, Theorems 1.4 and 1.7]
. If
F
is elliptic, then a flat
k
-disc (or

k
-cube) is the unique minimiser of
\Phi_{F}
among all competitors with the same
(
k-1
)-dimensional boundary as the disc (or cube). Uniform ellipticity gives additionally
quantitative control on
\Phi_{F}
; see §
5
.

This notion heavily depends on the choice of competitors. When competitors are rectifiable
currents, any uniformly convex norm on
{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}
gives rise to a uniformly
elliptic integrand; cf.
[
Fed69
, 5.1.2]
. Burago and Ivanov

[
BI04
, Theorem 3]
showed that if competitors are rectifiable currents (with
real coefficients), then
semi-ellipticity
is equivalent to being
extendibly
convex
, i.e., there exists an extension of the integrand to a norm on the
whole
{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}
. Their proof relies on showing, in non-constructive way,
the existence of a polyhedral surface with prescribed tangent planes; see
[
BI04
, Theorem 1,
Theorem 2]
.

However, if competitors are allowed only to be
integral
currents, then there exists
a continuous integrand defined on
\mathbf{G}_{0}(4,2)
for which the associated energy
functional is elliptic, but the integrand cannot be extended to a norm on the
whole
{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{2}\mathbf{R}^{4}
; see
[
BI04
, Theorem 5]
.

In
[
DLY24
, Theorem 3.2]
, De Rosa, Lei, and Young provided a constructive proof
of
[
BI04
, Theorem 2]
and established an analogous theorem
to
[
BI04
, Theorem 3]
for Lipschitz multivalued functions, namely, that
ellipticity for multigraphs is equivalent to
polyconvexity
; see
[
DLY24
, Theorem 1.7]
.

The classical notion of
polyconvexity
, coming from calculus of variations,
is defined for functionals acting on functions; cf.
[
Giu03
, Chapter 5]
. In this
setting integrands are real-valued functions defined on the space of linear
maps
\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{k},\mathbf{R}^{n})
– for the purposes of the following discussion let us call them

classical integrands
as opposed to
geometric integrands
of the type

\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)\to\mathbf{R}
; cf.
[
Fed69
, 5.1.1 and 5.1.9]
.

The authors of
[
DLY24
]
use
polyconvexity
for classical
integrands and briefly describe its connection with being
extendibly convex
for
related geometric integrands; see
[
DLY24
, Remark 1.4, Definition 1.6, Definition 1.7]
.
Later they (as we do as well) call a geometric integrand
F

polyconvex
in case it
is extendibly convex. As a service to the community, we present a detailed formal
discussion of the relation between
extendibly convex

geometric integrands and
polyconvex
classical integrands
in section
3
.

A notion stronger than
polyconvexity
is
uniform polyconvexity

(abbreviated UPC) which involves certain constant
c>0
;
see
3.21
. In this article, we show that
uniform ellipticity
for
polyhedral
k
-chains with constant
c
implies
uniform polyconvexity
with any
constant smaller than
c
. This can be seen as a refinement of some results contained
in
[
BI04
]
and
[
DLY24
]
.

Theorem

(see
5.10
for a precise statement)
.

Assume
F
is a geometric integrand satisfying
\Lip F<\infty
. If
F
is Almgren
uniformly elliptic with respect to polyhedral
k
-chains with real coefficients and
with ellipticity constant
0<c<\infty
, then
F
is uniformly polyconvex with any
constant
0<\tilde{c}<c
.

Our proof is based on
[
DLY24
, Theorem 3.2]
, which allows to construct polyhedral

k
-chains with Gaussian measures close to a given measure and provides control of the
geometry of the constructed surface. Note also that the reverse implication (with

\tilde{c}=c
) holds by a simple argument involving the Stokes theorem;
cf.
[
Fed69
, 5.1.2]
.

This paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the
basic terminology that will be used throughout the paper. In
Section
3
, we present a formal discussion on the relationship
between the notions of
polyconvex
and
extendibly convex
integrands,
demonstrating their equivalence. Moreover, in the same section, we recall the definition
of
uniform polyconvexity
for integrands. Section
4
is
devoted to the classical definitions of polyhedral chains and Gaussian measures. Finally,
in Section
5
, we define
ellipticity
for polyhedral test
pairs and prove the main result.

2
Preliminaries

In principle we shall follow the notation of Federer;
see
[
Fed69
, pp. 669–671]
. In particular,
A\!\sim\!B
is the set-theoretic
difference and
\mathscr{P}
denotes the set of positive integers. Diverting from Federer, if

-\infty<s<r<\infty
, we occasionally write
(s,r)
,
[s,r]
,
(s,r]
, and
[s,r)

to denote appropriate intervals in
\mathbf{R}
. We shall use letters
k
and
n
to denote two
integers satisfying
0\leq k<n
. We say that a function
f
defined on some open subset
of a Banach space with values in another Banach space is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}
if it is

l
-times differentiable and
\mathrm{D}^{l}f
is continuous; cf
[
Fed69
, 3.1.11]
.

2.1 Definition

(cf.
[
Fed69
, 1.3.2]
)
.

By
\Lambda(n,k)
we denote set of all increasing maps of
\{1,\ldots,k\}
into

\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Remark
.

Note that any
\lambda\in\Lambda(n,k)
is uniquely determined by
\im\lambda
which
is a
k
-element subset of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
; thus, choosing

\lambda\in\Lambda(n,k)
corresponds to choosing
k
-elements out of

\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.

2.2 Definition
.

Let
\xi\in{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}
. We define the vector space associated with

\xi
by

\asssp\xi=\mathbf{R}^{n}\cap\bigl\{v:v\wedge\xi=0\bigr\}\,.

2.3 Remark

(
[
Fed69
, 1.6.1]
)
.

An element
\xi\in{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}
is simple if and only if

\dim\asssp\xi=k
.

2.4 Definition

(
[
Fed69
, 3.2.28(b)]
)
.

We define the
oriented Grassmannian
\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
as

\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)={\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}\cap\bigl\{\xi:\xi\text{ is simple},\,|\xi|=1\bigr\}\,.

2.5 Remark
.

Let
\xi\in{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}
. Recall from
[
Fed69
, 1.8.1]
that

\|\xi\|=|\xi|
if and only if
\xi
is simple.

2.6 Definition
.

Given a vectorspace
X
we write
\mathrm{End}({X})
for the space of all linear maps
mapping
X
to
X
, i.e.,
\mathrm{End}({X})=\Hom(X,X)
.

2.7 Definition

(
[
Fed69
, 1.7.4]
)
.

A linear map
j\in\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{k},\mathbf{R}^{n})
is called an
orthogonal
injection
if
j^{*}\circ j=\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{R}^{k}}
. If
p\in\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{n},\mathbf{R}^{k})
and

j=p^{*}
is an orthogonal injection, then we say that
p
is
an
orthogonal projection
. The set of all orthogonal projections

\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{k}
is denoted
\mathbf{O}^{\ast}({n},{k})
.

2.8 Remark
.

To each
\lambda\in\Lambda(n,k)
corresponds the map
\p_{\lambda}\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}({n},{k})
defined as

\p_{\lambda}(x)=(x_{\lambda(1)},\ldots,x_{\lambda(k)})\quad\text{for $x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbf{R}^{n}$}\,.

2.9 Definition

(cf.
[
Roc70
, p. 14]
)
.

Let
X
be a vectorspace and
A\subseteq X
. We define

\ray A=X\cap\{ta:0\leq t<\infty,\,a\in A\}\quad\text{and}\quad\cone A=\conv(\ray A)\,.

2.10 Definition
.

Let
X
be a normed vectorspace and
f:X\to\mathbf{R}
. We say that
f
is

positively homogeneous
if
f(tx)=tf(x)
whenever
x\in X
and

0\leq t<\infty
.

2.11 Definition
.

Let
X
be a vectorspace and
f:X\to\mathbf{R}
. We say that
f
is
a
gauge
if it is convex non-negative and positively homogeneous.
We say that
f
is a
strict gauge
if
f
is a gauge and

f(x+y)<f(x)+f(y)\quad\text{for all $x,y\in X\!\sim\!\{0\}$ such that $x\notin\ray\{y\}$}\,.

2.12 Definition
.

A
signed measure
over
\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
is a Daniell integral on the
lattice of functions
\mathscr{K}(\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k))
as defined
in
[
Fed69
, 2.5.6]
.

2.13 Definition

(
[
Fed69
, 2.5.5]
)
.

Total variation
of a signed measure
\mu
over
\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
is
defined as

\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}:=|\mu|(\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)),\quad\text{where}\quad|\mu|=\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}\,.

3
Polyconvexity

3.1
.

The notion of
polyconvexity
has been introduced, in the context of calculus of
variations, for integrands defined on the space of linear maps
\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{k},\mathbf{R}^{n})
; see,
e.g.,
[
Giu03
, Ch.5, p. 147]
. In our case integrands are functions
on
\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)\subseteq{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}
and the corresponding notion of
polyconvexity can be found, e.g., in
[
DLY24
, Definition 1.3]
. The relation between these
two notions has been very briefly explained in a small remark
[
DLY24
, Remark 1.4]
.
As a service to the community, as well as for the sake of author’s
own understanding, we present here a detailed formal discussion of this relation.

3.2 Remark
.

Assume
f\in\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{k},\mathbf{R}^{n})
and
A\in\mathbf{R}^{n\times k}
is the matrix
of
f
written in the standard bases. For any
\lambda\in\Lambda(n,k)
the
matrix of the map
\p_{\lambda}\circ f
is obtained by choosing the rows
of
A
indexed by
\lambda
and
\det(\p_{\lambda}\circ f)
is
a
k\times k
minor of the matrix
A
. Having this in mind one readily sees
that the following two definitions are a special case of the ones given
by Giusti.

3.3 Definition
.

Let
f\in\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{k},\mathbf{R}^{n})
. We define
M(f)\in\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}
by

M(f)(\lambda)=\det(\p_{\lambda}\circ f)\quad\text{for $\lambda\in\Lambda(n,k)$}\,.

3.4 Definition

(cf.
[
Giu03
, Ch.5, p. 147]
)
.

A function
G:\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{k},\mathbf{R}^{n})\to[0,\infty)
is called
(strictly)
polyconvex
if there exists a (strict) gauge

h:\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}\to[0,\infty)
such that
G=h\circ M
.

3.5 Remark
.

Note that our definition differs from
[
Giu03
, Ch.5]
in the following way:
we enforce
h
to be positively homogeneous, which immediately yields that
G
is
positively
k
-homogeneous and determines the growth of
G
at infinity.

3.6 Definition
.

An
integrand
is a continuous non-negative function
F:\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)\to\mathbf{R}
.

3.7 Remark
.

Note that an integrand
F
is defined only on the Grassmannian
\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
. Later it
will be useful to consider positively homogeneous extensions of
F
onto the whole
of
{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

3.8 Remark
.

Let
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{k})
be the standard basis of
\mathbf{R}^{k}
,

E=e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{k}\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(k,k)
,
\xi\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
,

p\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}({n},{k})
be such that
{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}p^{*}E=\xi
, and

A\in\mathbf{R}^{n\times k}
be the matrix of
p^{*}
in the standard bases. Then the columns
of
A
form an orthonormal basis of
\asssp\xi
which is positively oriented with
respect to
\xi
. This explains the connection of the following definition with the
one given by De Rosa, Lei, and Young.

3.9 Definition

(cf.
[
DLY24
, 1.3]
)
.

Let
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{k})
be the standard basis of
\mathbf{R}^{k}
. An integrand
F

is said to be (strictly)
polyconvex
if there is a (strict) gauge

h:\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}\to[0,+\infty)\quad\text{such that}\quad F(\xi)=h(M(p_{\xi}^{*}))

whenever
\xi\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
and
p_{\xi}\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}({n},{k})
satisfies

{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}p_{\xi}^{*}(e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{k})=\xi
.

3.10 Remark
.

Definition
3.9
is independent of the choice
of
p_{\xi}
for
\xi\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
. Indeed, let
\xi\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
and

p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}({n},{k})
be such that

{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}p_{i}^{*}(e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{k})=\xi
for

i\in\{1,2\}
. There exists
T\in\mathbf{O}({k})
such that

p_{1}^{*}=p_{2}^{*}\circ T
and we get

M(p_{1}^{*})(\lambda)=\det(\p_{\lambda}\circ p_{1}^{*})=\det(\p_{\lambda}\circ p_{2}^{*}\circ T)=\det(\p_{\lambda}\circ p_{2}^{*})\det T=M(p_{2}^{*})(\lambda)\det T

for
\lambda\in\Lambda(n,k)
. However, we also know that

\xi={\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}p_{2}^{*}(e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{k})={\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}p_{1}^{*}(e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{k})={\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}(p_{2}^{*}\circ T)(e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{k})\\
={\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}p_{2}^{*}\circ{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}T(e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{k})=\det T{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}p_{2}^{*}(e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{k})\,;

(1)

hence,
\det T=1
.

3.11 Remark
.

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\in\mathbf{R}^{k}
be the standard basis of
\mathbf{R}^{k}
,

E=e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{k}\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(k,k)
,
\xi\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
, and

p\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}({n},{k})
be such that
{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}p^{*}E=\xi
. In
3.10
we
showed that
M(p)
does not depend on the particular choice of
p
so
M
could
actually be defined on
\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
. Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
be the standard basis
of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
. For
\lambda\in\Lambda(n,k)
set

u_{\lambda}=u_{\lambda(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{\lambda(k)}
and note that

u_{\lambda}={\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\p_{\lambda}^{*}E
; thus,

\xi\bullet u_{\lambda}={\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}p^{*}E\bullet{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\p_{\lambda}^{*}E={\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}(p_{\lambda}\circ p^{*})E\bullet E=\det(p_{\lambda}\circ p^{*})=M(p^{*})(\lambda)\,.

3.12 Definition
.

Define
N:{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}
by the formula

N(\xi)(\lambda)=\xi\bullet u_{\lambda}\quad\text{for $\xi\in{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}$ and $\lambda\in\Lambda(n,k)$}\,.

3.13 Remark
.

Note that
N
is a linear isometry.

3.14 Corollary
.

Let
F
be an integrand. The following are equivalent

(a)

F
is (strictly) polyconvex in the sense of
definition
3.9
.

(b)

There exists of (strict) gauge
h:\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}\to[0,\infty)
such that

F(\xi)=h(N(\xi))
for
\xi\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
.

3.15 Corollary
.

Assume
h:\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}\to[0,\infty)
. The following are equivalent

(a)

h\circ M:\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{k},\mathbf{R}^{n})\to\mathbf{R}
is (strictly) polyconvex in the
sense of
3.4
;

(b)

h\circ N:{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
is (strictly) polyconvex in the
sense of
3.9
;

(c)

h|N[\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)]
can be extended to a (strict) gauge
on
\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}
.

3.16 Remark
.

We could have defined polyconvexity in
3.4

and
3.9
without enforcing
h
to be positively homogeneous (only
convex) and we would get an analogous corollary to
3.15
. Observe,
however, that if
h:\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}\to[0,\infty)
is only convex, then,
in general, it might not be possible to find a gauge which agrees with
h

on
N[\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)]
; hence, not assuming
h
to be positively homogeneous
in
3.9
significantly affects the class of polyconvex integrands.

To support this claim let
\mathbb{S}=\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}\cap\{y:|y|=1\}
,
choose
e\in N[\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)]\subseteq\mathbb{S}
,
1<r<\infty
, and

(1+r)^{2}/r<p<\infty
(note that
(1+r)^{2}/r\geq 4
). Consider the non-negative
convex function
h:\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}\to[0,\infty)
given by

h(x)=|x-re|^{p}\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}$}\,.

Next, define a non-negative positively homogeneous function

f:\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}\to[0,\infty]
by setting

f(x)=h(x)\quad\text{and}\quad f(tx)=tf(x)\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{S}$ and $0\leq t<\infty$}\,.

Fix
x=-e
and
u\in\mathbb{S}
such that
e\perp u
. Choose a geodesic

\gamma:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbb{S}
such that
\gamma(0)=x
,
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=u
(then,
necessarily
\gamma^{\prime\prime}(0)=-x
). Direct computations show

\displaystyle(f\circ\gamma)^{\prime\prime}(0)=\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)uu-\mathrm{D}f(x)x=\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)uu-f(x)=\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)uu-h(x)\,,

(2)

\displaystyle(f\circ\gamma)^{\prime\prime}(0)=(h\circ\gamma)^{\prime\prime}(0)=\mathrm{D}^{2}h(x)uu-\mathrm{D}h(x)x\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}h(x)x=p|x-re|^{p-2}(x-re)\bullet x\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\mathrm{D}^{2}h(x)uu=p(p-2)|x-re|^{p-4}\bigl((x-re)\bullet u\bigr)^{2}+p|x-re|^{p-2}\,;

(5)

hence, recalling
x=-e
and
e\perp u
,

\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)uu=\mathrm{D}^{2}h(x)uu-\mathrm{D}h(x)x+h(x)=|x-re|^{p-2}\bigl(p-p(x-re)\bullet x+|x-re|^{2}\bigr)\\
=|x-re|^{p-2}\bigl(-pr+(1+r)^{2}\bigr)\,.

(6)

Since
p>(1+r)^{2}/r
we get
\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)uu<0
so
f
is not convex.

Convex hulls

3.17 Definition

(cf.
[
Fed69
, 2.5.19]
)
.

Assume
X
is a set and
x\in X
. The
Dirac measure
over
X
having a single
atom at
x
is denoted by

\boldsymbol{\delta}_{x}(A)=\begin{cases}1\text{\quad if \quad}x\in A\\
0\text{\quad if \quad}x\notin A\end{cases}\quad\text{for $A\subseteq X$}\,.

3.18 Definition
.

Let
F
be an integrand. The
convex positively homogeneous hull
of
F
is
defined by

\Conv_{F}(\xi)=\inf\left\{\int{}{}F\,\mathrm{d}\mu:\begin{gathered}N\in\mathscr{P},\,m_{1},\ldots,m_{N}\in(0,\infty),\,\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k),\,\\
\mu={\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}}m_{i}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\eta_{i}},\,\quad\xi={\textstyle\int}\eta\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\eta)\end{gathered}\right\}\\
\text{for $\xi\in{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}$}\,.

(7)

3.19 Remark
.

\Conv_{F}
is the biggest convex positively homogeneous function (i.e. a gauge) not
exceeding
F
on
\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
pointwise.

3.20 Lemma
.

An integrand
F
is polyconvex if and only if
F=\Conv_{F}|_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}
.

Proof.

Assume
F
is polyconvex so that there exists a gauge
h:\mathbf{R}^{\Lambda(n,k)}\to\mathbf{R}

such that
F=h\circ N|\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
. Since
N
is a linear isometry, we see that

h\circ N
is a gauge on
{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}
so

F=h\circ N|\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)\leq\Conv_{F}|\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)\leq F\,.

On the other hand, if
\Conv_{F}|\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)=F
, we set
h=\Conv_{F}\circ N^{-1}

to get
F=h\circ N|\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
.
∎

Uniform polyconvexity

3.21 Definition
.

Let
0<c<\infty
and
F
be an integrand. We say that
F
is

uniformly polyconvex (with constant
c
)
if

{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}F(\eta_{i})-F(\eta_{0})\geq c\bigl({\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}|\eta_{i}|-|\eta_{0}|\bigr)

whenever
d\in\mathscr{P}
,
m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\in(0,\infty)
,

\eta_{0},\ldots,\eta_{d}\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
are such that

\eta_{0}={\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}\eta_{i}
.

If
F
is uniformly polyconvex with constant
c
, we shall write

F\in\mathrm{UPC(c)}
.

3.22 Remark
.

Assume
F\in\mathrm{UPC(c)}
for some
0<c<\infty
,
d\in\mathscr{P}
,

m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\in(0,\infty)
,
\eta_{0},\ldots,\eta_{d}\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
, and

\eta_{0}={\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}\eta_{i}
. Set

\mu={\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}}m_{i}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\eta_{i}}
. Then we have

{\textstyle\int}F\,\mathrm{d}\mu={\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}F(\eta_{i})\geq F(\eta_{0})\,;

hence,
\Conv_{F}(\eta_{0})=F(\eta_{0})
. Since
\eta_{0}
was arbitrary, we see
from
3.20
that
F
is polyconvex. Consequently, we get that
uniformly polyconvexity implies polyconvexity.

4.11 Remark

m_{\lambda}>0
for

\lambda\in L
. Therefore, the set of unit simple
k
-vectors
\{\tilde{\eta}_{0}
,

\tilde{\eta}_{1}
, …,

\tilde{\eta}_{d}\}\cup\{\eta_{\lambda}:\lambda\in L\}
, the numbers

\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\}\cup\{m_{\lambda}:\lambda\in L\}
, and the measure

\tilde{\mu}
satisfy the assumptions of theorem
5.9
. Thus, there exist

k
-polyhedral chains
A,\tilde{D}\in\mathbf{P}_{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
such that

\vec{D}=\tilde{\eta}_{0}\,,\quad\spt D\text{ is a unit cube in $\asssp\tilde{\eta}_{0}$}\,,\quad\partial A=\partial\tilde{D}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\|\gamma_{A}-\tilde{\mu}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\epsilon\,.

By the assumption that
F\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P},c_{1})
we have the
following

\displaystyle\Phi(A)-\Phi(\tilde{D})

\displaystyle={\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}}F\,\mathrm{d}\gamma_{A}-{\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}}F\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\tilde{\eta}_{0}}

(27)

\displaystyle={\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}}F\,\mathrm{d}(\gamma_{A}-\tilde{\mu})

(28)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+{\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}}F\,\mathrm{d}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)

(29)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+{\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}}F\,\mathrm{d}\mu-F(\tilde{\eta}_{0})\,.

(30)

We will show separately upper bounds for (
28
), (
29
) and
(
30
).

Estimate of (
28
)
Obviously

\displaystyle{\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}}F\,\mathrm{d}(\gamma_{A}-\tilde{\mu})\leq M\|\gamma_{A}-\tilde{\mu}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<M\epsilon\,.

(31)

Estimate of (
29
)
Recalling (
20
)
and (
21
) we get

\displaystyle{\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}}F\,\mathrm{d}(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)

\displaystyle={\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}}F\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}-{\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}}F\,\mathrm{d}{\mu}

(32)

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}F(\tilde{\eta}_{i})+{\textstyle\sum_{\lambda\in L}}m_{\lambda}F(\eta_{\lambda})-{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}F(\eta_{i})

(33)

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}\bigl(m_{i}F(\tilde{\eta}_{i})-m_{i}F(\eta_{i})\bigr)+{\textstyle\sum_{\lambda\in L}}m_{\lambda}F(\eta_{\lambda})

(34)

\displaystyle\leq L{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}|\eta_{i}-\tilde{\eta}_{i}|+M{\textstyle\sum_{\lambda\in L}}m_{\lambda}

(35)

\displaystyle\leq L{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}|m_{i}||\tilde{\eta_{i}}-\eta_{i}|+M\bigl({\textstyle\sum_{\lambda\in L}}m_{\lambda}^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\card L)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(36)

\displaystyle<\tfrac{1}{2}L\epsilon+M\bigl({\textstyle\sum_{\lambda\in L}}|m_{\lambda}\eta_{\lambda}|^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\textstyle\binom{n}{k}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

(37)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}L\epsilon+M{\textstyle\binom{n}{k}}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\zeta|<\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}L+M{\textstyle\binom{n}{k}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\epsilon\,,

(38)

where the next to last inequality is a consequence of

\{\eta_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda(n,k)\}
being an orthonormal basis and the
Pythagorean Theorem.

Estimate of (
30
)
Recalling (
19
) we get

\displaystyle{\textstyle\int_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}}F\,\mathrm{d}\mu-F(\tilde{\eta}_{0})

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}F(\eta_{i})-F(\eta_{0})+(F(\eta_{0})-F(\tilde{\eta_{0}}))

(39)

\displaystyle<c_{2}\bigl({\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}|\eta_{i}|-|\eta_{0}|\bigr)+L|\eta_{0}-\tilde{\eta_{0}}|

(40)

\displaystyle<c_{2}\bigl({\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}-1\bigr)+L\epsilon\,.

(41)

Now, combining all the estimates (
28
), (
29
), and (
30
) gives

\Phi(A)-\Phi(\tilde{D})<c_{2}\big({\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}-1\big)+\epsilon\big(\tfrac{3}{2}L+M+M{\textstyle\binom{n}{k}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\,.

(42)

On the other hand, by
[
DLY24
, 2.3]
and recalling that
\spt D
is a unit cube,
we have the following lower estimate

c_{1}\bigl(\mathbf{M}(A)-\mathbf{M}(\tilde{D})\bigr)\geq c_{1}\bigl(\|\gamma_{A}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}-1\bigr)\,.

(43)

Moreover, we observe

{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}\leq{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}+{\textstyle\sum_{\lambda\in L}}m_{\lambda}=\|\tilde{\mu}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\|\tilde{\mu}-\gamma_{A}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}+\|\gamma_{A}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\epsilon+\|\gamma_{A}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\,;

thus,

c_{1}\big(\|\gamma_{A}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}-1\big)=c_{1}\big(\|\gamma_{A}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}+\epsilon-\epsilon-1\big)>c_{1}\bigl({\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}-1\bigr)-c_{1}\epsilon\,.

(44)

Finally, since
(A,\tilde{D})
is a polyhedral test pair and

F\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P},c_{1})
, combining (
42
),
(
43
), and (
44
) we obtain

c_{2}\bigl({\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}-1\bigr)+\epsilon\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}L+M+M{\textstyle\binom{n}{k}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\\
>\Phi(A)-\Phi(\tilde{D})\geq c_{1}\bigl(\mathbf{M}(A)-\mathbf{M}(\tilde{D})\bigr)>c_{1}\bigl({\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}}m_{i}-1\bigr)-c_{1}\epsilon\,.

(45)

Recalling the definition of
\epsilon
we see that this cannot happen.
∎

5.11 Remark
.

Note that if
F
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{1}
, then standard tools
(see
[
Fed69
, 3.1.14]
) allow to extend
F
to a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
function on some
compact neighbourhood of
\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
and then it is clear that
F
is Lipschitzian so
our theorem applies to any integrand of class
\mathscr{C}^{1}
.

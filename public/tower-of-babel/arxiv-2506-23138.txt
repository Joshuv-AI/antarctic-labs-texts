VisualPrompter: Prompt Optimization with Visual Feedback for Text-to-Image Synthesis
Shiyu Wu, Mingzhen Sun, Weining Wang, Yequan Wang, Jing Liu

Abstract
Since there exists a notable gap between user-provided and model-preferred prompts, generating high-quality and satisfactory images using diffusion models often requires prompt engineering to optimize user inputs. Current studies on text-to-image prompt engineering can effectively enhance the style and aesthetics of generated images. However, they often neglect the semantic alignment between generated images and user descriptions, resulting in visually appealing but content-wise unsatisfying outputs. In this work, we propose VisualPrompter, a novel training-free prompt engineering framework that refines user inputs to model-preferred sentences. In particular, VisualPrompter utilizes an automatic self-reflection module to identify the missing concepts in generated images and a target-specific prompt optimization mechanism to revise the prompts in a fine-grained manner. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our VisualPrompter, which achieves new state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks for text-image alignment evaluation. Additionally, our framework features a plug-and-play design, making it highly adaptable to various generative models.

Introduction

Text-to-image (T2I) generation task requires generating realistic images that are consistent with textual descriptions.
Recently, diffusion-based generative models
[
20
,
38
]

have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in generating vivid images from textual descriptions.
However, they still face challenges in correctly representing key concepts within user inputs, as shown in
Fig.

1(a)
.
This issue arises from the significant discrepancy between user input prompts and the prompts preferred by the models
[
16
]
.
To be specific, novice users often provide brief, coarse-grained descriptions, whereas models tend to perform better with detailed, fine-grained prompts, as such data are commonly used during training.
Thus, T2I models may struggle to generate satisfactory results when directly fed with user inputs.

Given that a well-crafted prompt can significantly enhance the quality of generated images
[

, researchers have explored prompt engineering to automatically refine user input prompts
[

,

.
For instance,

simply append a few empirically effective keywords to improve image quality.
BeautifulPrompt
[

]
finetune a large language model to serve as a prompt engineer and incorporate reinforcement learning to leverage visual feedback.
Despite effectiveness, these methods suffer from three major limitations.
First, current studies on T2I prompt engineering
[
39
,
15
]
primarily focused on enhancing the style and aesthetics of generated images, neglecting or even compromising the alignment between texts and images.
Second, most works
[
33
,
29
]
apply similar modification to all prompts, lacking fine-grained, case-specific adjustments tailored to individual inputs, resulting in limited performance improvement.
Third, existing methods exhibit limited generality
[
5
,
39
]
, as they are typically designed for a single specific diffusion model.
However, as illustrated in
Fig.

1(b)
, different models have varying prompt preferences and may interpret the same prompt in different incorrect ways, making these methods difficult to extend to other models.

In this paper, we introduce VisualPrompter, a novel and training-free framework that provides fine-grained, case-specific, and model-preference-aware optimization for distinct user inputs.
To make clear the optimization target within prompts, VisualPrompter incorporates a SElf-REflection module (SERE) to identify the missing concepts in the generated images, i.e., the textual concepts that are included in the input prompts but not reflected in the generated images.
SERE breaks down the prompts into atomic concepts using a large language model and verifies if each concept is reflected in the generated images using a Visual-Language Model (VLM).
Subsequently, with the missing concepts as targets, a Target-Specific Prompt Optimization module (TSPO) is employed to refine the user prompts.
In particular, TSPO enriches the missing concepts while preserving the existing concepts, ensuring that the refined prompts are both model-preferred and semantically faithful to the user’s original intent.
Moreover, motivated by the success of previous prompt engineering work
[
39
]
in enhancing user prompt in terms of aesthetics, our TSPO incorporates a prompt decoration process to improve the aesthetic appeal of generated images.

Notably, by breaking down input prompts into fine-grained concepts, our method allows case-specific detailed prompt optimization, which is not only more interpretable but also more powerful.
Moreover, our method allows different prompt optimization for different diffusion models according to the distinct answers based on their generated images.
In this way, our VisualPrompter can be compatible with various generative models, obtaining a universal and robust performance improvement.

Experiments show that VisualPrompter outperforms prior prompt engineering works on two text-image alignment evaluation benchmarks, demonstrating the effectiveness of our approach. Images generated from texts optimized by VisualPrompter also achieve higher CLIP scores than those produced by previous prompt engineering methods. Furthermore, our model exhibits notable performance improvements across various diffusion models, highlighting its extensive applicability and adaptability.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We propose an innovative text-to-image prompt engineering method, named VisualPrompter. Our approach is capable of generating prompts that effectively align with both model preferences and user intent.

•

We introduce a SERE module to identify the missing concept in generated images.
To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to exploit visual feedback from VLM for better T2I prompt engineering.

•

We propose a TSPO module to optimize user prompts for model preference and semantic faithfulness, encouraging better aesthetic results.

•

Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our VisualPrompter, which significantly outperforms current state-of-the-art prompt engineering methods in multiple benchmarks.

, which implements the diffusion process in latent space using a variational autoencoder

[

,
11

34

has brought text-to-image generation into the public spotlight. Therefore, a well-designed prompt engineering framework can be immensely beneficial for novice users.

Prompt Engineering

Prompt engineering, originally emerging as a language-focused task, aims to optimize the interaction between humans and AI systems by crafting effective input prompts that elicit desired outputs
[
28
,
41
]
. In the field of text-to-image generation, prompt engineering involves carefully selecting and combining phrases to achieve high-quality and satisfactory synthesized images. Early studies
[
29
,
33
,
31
]
focus on automatically searching for keywords that significantly influence the style and quality of generated images through mining techniques. With the rapid advancement of large language models, several studies
[
39
,
15
,
5
,
46
]
have explored their utilization as prompt engineers, particularly through fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. These models are capable of producing detailed and varied content prompts, based on short phrases provided by users.

Other methods
[
3
,
12
]
focus on the interaction with human for precise control. PromptCharm
[
47
]
provides convenient attention adjustments for the keywords within prompts. Meanwhile, Prompt Expansion
[
9

outputs a set of expanded text prompts and images, providing users with multiple optimization directions for selection. Promptify
[

[

aim to accelerate the iteration of prompt refinements. UF-FGTG
[
16

Although these methods can improve the visual quality of generated images, they often neglect the semantic consistency with user-provided descriptions, which significantly limits their practicality in real-world scenarios.

3
VisualPrompter

In this section, we present our VisualPrompter in detail, which is a novel prompt engineering method for T2I generation task.

Fig.

2
illustrates the overall framework of our proposed method.

3.1
Overview

The target of our VisualPrompter is to automatically optimize user input prompts into model-preferred prompts.
To achieve this, VisualPrompter consists of two major modules: a SElf-REflection module (SERE), which identifies model preferences using visual feedback, and a Target-Specific Prompt Optimization module (TSPO), which refines user prompts in a fine-grained manner.

Given a user input prompt
t_{i}
, SERE is employed to identify the specific aspects that require enhancement, which analyzes the image
\mathbf{x}_{i}
generated by the diffusion model based on the input text
t_{i}
.
In particular, we first extract concept tuples from
t_{i}
and synthesize a specific question for each tuple.
This process breaks down the user input prompt into multiple concepts, providing a fine-grained understanding of its composition.
Subsequently, we utilize multi-step reasoning to obtain explicit visual feedback by evaluating the generated images against the textual questions.
Through this self-reflection mechanism, i.e., the question generation and answering processes, we can effectively identify missing concepts in the generated image
\mathbf{x}_{i}
.
Notably, the missing concepts are those textual descriptions that are included in user inputs but are not reflected in the generated images.

Some concepts may be missing in the generated images due to the failure of the generative model in capturing descriptions that do not align with its preferences.
To obtain prompts that remain faithful to user inputs while aligning with model preferences, we employ TSPO to refine the user prompt based on the identified missing concepts.
Specifically, TSPO treats these missing concepts as modification targets, optimizing the user input prompt into a more model-preferred version by strategically enriching the attributes of the missing concepts.
In parallel, it retains textual descriptions that are already accurately reflected in the generated images to avoid unnecessary modifications, thereby preserving original user intent.

3.2
Self-reflection Module

As illustrated in
Fig.

2
, the self-reflection module identifies missing concepts by analyzing the input text
t_{i}
and its corresponding generated image
\mathbf{x}_{i}
through question generation and answering process.
This mechanism ensures a fine-grained evaluation of the alignment between textual descriptions and visual content, effectively pinpointing discrepancies where the generative model fails to capture specific aspects of the user input.

Question Generation.

For detailed question generation, we adopt Davidsonian Scene Graph (DSG)
[
7
]
to identify atomic concepts within the input prompts.
A DSG is a structured representation comprising a set of general questions, each question corresponding to an atomic concept in the given prompt.
These questions are interconnected through directed edges, which capture the entailment dependencies among them.
We employ a Large Language Model (LLM) to generate the DSG, and its detailed construction process is elaborated in our supplementary materials.

Question Answering.

Upon completing the construction of DSG, we employ a VLM as the visual question answering model to evaluate the generated image
\mathbf{x}_{i}
according to the questions in DSG.
The VLM is fed with both the generated image and the questions.
It is instructed to provide a binary response—either “yes” or “no”—for each question, indicating whether the presence of corresponding concept in the generated image is true or false, respectively.
The dependency relationships within the DSG are utilized for question pruning, a process that eliminates the need for redundant assessments.
Specifically, if a particular concept is judged to be missing, all subsequent concepts dependent on it are automatically regarded as missing as well.

The answering results of these questions enable a comprehensive semantic analysis of the generated images, which can also serve as a reliable metric for assessing text-image alignment
[
7
]
.
In particular, the accuracy of the questions in DSG can serve as an effective measurement of the semantic consistency between images and texts.
The score is calculated as the proportion of “yes” responses from the VLM relative to the total number of questions.
Consequently, images that exhibit stronger alignment with the accompanying texts will achieve higher scores.

3.3
Target-Specific Prompt Optimization

With those identified missing concepts by our self-reflection module, we can perform targeted optimization of the input texts, reducing unnecessary modifications and expansions.
We introduce a novel, training-free optimization approach that effectively manages prompt adjustments.

Prompt Regeneration.

Rather than directly rewriting the sentences, VisualPrompter first utilizes an LLM to expand the generated atomic concepts based on the responses to the questions in DSG.
This expansion process specifically targets the identified missing concepts, enriching them with additional detail such as attributes, actions, and spatial relationships.
These additional elements are also expressed as atomic concepts, ensuring consistency and clarity in representation.
Notably, if no missing concepts are detected, no further modifications will be made.

Subsequently, VisualPrompter generates a new prompt from the expanded atomic concepts with LLM.
The original prompts and concepts are also provided to guide the synthesis, ensuring that the generated prompt remains contextually aligned with the initial input. By adopting this two-step optimization methodology, our model is able to produce more accurate and reliable prompts.
This approach ensures that the refined prompts are both model-preferred and semantically faithful to the user’s original intent.

Prompt Decoration.

To further enrich the diversity and aesthetic quality of the generated content, we augment the optimized results with several aesthetic keywords according previous studies
[
33
,
39
]
, such as
high-quality
,
detailed
and
fantasy
, which have proved to significantly improve the aesthetic appeal of images.
We systematically classify these keywords into distinct categories, including image quality, artistic style, background description, environmental illumination and perspective. We utilize an LLM to automatically select appropriate keywords that do not conflict with the input sentences. These stylistic keywords are seamlessly integrated into the texts or appended at the end in a comma-separated format.
The detailed keywords are presented in the supplementary material.

4.1
Benchmarks and Evaluation Metrics

To evaluate the text-image alignment, we adopt a robust and innovative approach
[
52
,
6
]
which leverages question generation and answering techniques
[
10
,
30
]
as detailed in
Section

3.2
. This reference-free metric demonstrates strong correlations with human judgments
[
21
]
, making it particularly suitable for fine-grained evaluation tasks.

For our evaluation, we utilize two comprehensive benchmarks: DSG-1k
[
7
]
and TIFA v1.0
[
21
]
. Both of them contain human-written prompts from various established text-to-image benchmarks. The DSG-1k benchmark comprises
1,060
prompts from
10
publicly available prompt datasets, encompassing a wide range of skills and writing styles. It provides
8,182
questions associated with these prompts, facilitating a multifaceted evaluation of generated images. Meanwhile, TIFA v1.0 contains
4,081
diverse prompts from
4
datasets. As the original TIFA v1.0 benchmark contains multiple-choice and free-form questions, we further refine these questions and represent them in DSG structure. Ultimately, the TIFA benchmark used in our experiments contains
19,233
general questions.

We employ the CLIP Score
[
17
]
to evaluate the semantic alignment between textual descriptions and generated images at feature level. CLIP Score utilizes a pre-trained CLIP model
[
35
]
to compute the cosine similarity between image and text embeddings, where higher scores indicate better text-image correspondence. While it is effective in assessing high-level semantic consistency, this metric may not fully reflect nuanced perceptual qualities.

To comprehensively evaluate the aesthetic quality of generated images, we employ the Aesthetic Score
[
42
]
, a widely recognized and well-established metric in image generation that utilizes a simple neural network trained on large-scale human-annotated evaluation datasets. This metric is particularly valuable as it provides an objective foundation for comparing the aesthetic quality of different images, reducing the subjectivity associated with human evaluations.

4.2
Experimental Settings

Our VisualPrompter leverages a dual-model architecture that requires an LLM for DSG generation and prompt optimization, connected by a VLM for comprehensive image semantic evaluation. We employ the state-of-the-art open-sourced Qwen2
[
51
]
as our primary language model and the Qwen2-VL
[
45
]
as our visual question answering model. Each language generation task in our pipeline is initiated by feeding the Qwen2 with a meticulously designed preamble and
23
manually curated samples from TIFA dataset, ensuring consistent and stable model output.

To thoroughly evaluate the performance of VisualPrompter, we conduct extensive testing across three prominent diffusion models: Stable Diffusion v1.5
[
38
]
, Stable Diffusion v2.1
[
38
]
and Flux-dev
[
25
]
. Our evaluation utilizes prompts from both the DSG-1k and TIFA benchmarks. We compare our method with several open-sourced prompt engineering methods, including NeuroPrompts
[
39
]
, Promptist
[
15
]
and BeautifulPrompt
[
5
]
. All of these methods utilize supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning to adjust LLMs for optimizing prompts.

Our approach is implemented using PyTorch. All experiments are conducted on a single A100 with 40GB memory.

Surprisingly, previous methods not only fail to preserve the semantic consistency during optimization but also exhibit varying degrees of decline in this aspect. Our analysis reveals that both NeuroPrompts and Promptist are unable to modify the original descriptions, even they utilize an LLM for prompt optimization. This limitation prevents them from effectively refine those model-dispreferred sentences. Additionally, they often introduce irrelevant keywords, which are inconsistent with the overall context of the prompts. In contrast, BeautifulPrompt is able to adjust the original prompts and add contextually relevant details. However, it tends to omit critical information during the modification process, which significantly deviates from the user intent.

The experimental results on aesthetics show that VisualPrompter achieves modest improvements in aesthetic quality, which is not as substantial as those in previous studies. This discrepancy primarily stems from the tendency of our method to classify the original prompts as descriptions of realistic photographs, which results in an inherent lack of imaginative or fantastical elements among the optimized prompts. This also indicates that our prompt decorator is overly simplistic for enhancing aesthetics. To address this problem, we employ NeuroPrompts as the replacement of our decorator, which adds additional stylistic keywords while preserving the original sentence. This integrated approach achieves optimal performance on average in the aesthetic evaluation. However, we observe a notable decline in semantic consistency, compared to the results without the involvement of NeuroPrompt. The results imply that adopting the Aesthetic Score as a training metric may inadvertently encourage the model to optimize for superficial features at the expense of semantic integrity, highlighting the need for more balanced and semantically meaningful training guidance.

Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper, we propose VisualPrompter, an innovative and training-free prompt engineering framework that refines user prompts to model-preferred sentences. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our VisualPrompter, which achieves state-of-the-art performance in enhancing semantic consistency while maintaining superior aesthetic quality.
Compared with previous methods, VisualPrompter exhibits better generalization capabilities, which can be easily adapted to various diffusion models.

Despite its significant achievements, our approach still suffers from a few limitations.
Firstly, the LLM and VLM employed in our self-reflection module occasionally exhibit hallucination, which imposes a modest limitation on the performance of our method.
Secondly, similarly to the chain-of-thought process in LLM, we enhance the generation quality but increase the inference time.
We leave these questions to be addressed in our future work.

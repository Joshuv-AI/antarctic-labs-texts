Quantum Cosmology, Decoherence, and the Emergence of Classical Spacetime
Ali Nayeri

Abstract
We analyze the emergence of classical cosmological spacetimes in quantum cosmology by computing the reduced density matrix for long-wavelength curvature perturbations. Starting from standard Hartle–Hawking and tunneling boundary conditions, we emphasize that semiclassical WKB structure and inflationary squeezing do not by themselves yield classicality. Tracing over unobserved degrees of freedom and using the influence functional formalism, we derive the decoherence functional for superhorizon curvature modes during inflation. For a light massive environmental scalar field in the Bunch–Davies vacuum, we obtain an explicit noise kernel and show how a nonzero mass regulates the infrared behavior. We then evaluate decoherence under horizon-based and EFT-motivated coarse grainings, finding efficient suppression of interference between macroscopically distinct perturbation histories in both cases. The analysis clarifies the distinct roles of boundary conditions (branch amplitudes) and decoherence (classical branch selection) and yields an emergent cosmological arrow of time through environment-induced entanglement.



I
Quantum Cosmology Before Classicality

I.1
Hilbert’s Sixth Problem and Emergent Classicality

Hilbert’s Sixth Problem calls for the axiomatization of physical theories
Hilbert1900
and, in particular, for a precise understanding of how macroscopic classical laws emerge from underlying microscopic dynamics. In modern language, this problem encompasses the emergence of irreversible, probabilistic, and effectively classical behavior from fundamentally quantum and reversible laws. While the problem was originally posed in the context of mechanics and probability theory, its scope naturally extends to quantum field theory, statistical mechanics, and cosmology.

In quantum cosmology, Hilbert’s Sixth Problem takes on a sharpened form: how do classical spacetime, classical matter fields, and classical stochastic perturbations arise from a quantum theory of gravity and quantum fields in curved spacetime? The Wheeler–DeWitt equation and its associated boundary conditions define a universal quantum state, but they do not by themselves explain why observations are accurately described by classical cosmology.

Recent formulations of Hilbert’s Sixth Problem emphasize the role of coarse graining, statistical limits, and emergent descriptions in bridging microscopic and macroscopic theories. In this spirit, the influence functional and decoherence framework provides a concrete and calculable realization of Hilbert’s program in cosmology. By explicitly tracing over unobserved degrees of freedom, one obtains effective dynamics for relevant variables that are irreversible, stochastic, and classical in precisely the sense required by Hilbert’s Sixth Problem.

The approach adopted in this work is directly motivated by these ideas and by recent analyses emphasizing Hilbert’s Sixth Problem as a guiding principle for foundational questions in physics

In particular, we build on the viewpoint that decoherence and coarse graining are not interpretational add-ons, but essential structural elements required to derive classical laws from quantum dynamics. The results presented below implement this program explicitly for cosmological perturbations during inflation, thereby providing a concrete cosmological realization of Hilbert’s Sixth Problem.

IV.1
Horizon-Based Coarse Graining

A natural prescription in inflationary cosmology is to define the environment as modes whose physical wavelength is shorter than the Hubble radius at a given time. Concretely, we split modes according to

q>k_{c}(\eta)\equiv\epsilon\,a(\eta)H,

(14)

where
\epsilon\ll 1
is a fixed numerical parameter. Modes continuously cross from the environment into the system as inflation proceeds, but decoherence is generated primarily before horizon crossing, when environmental modes are still oscillatory.

For subhorizon modes
q\gg aH
, the BD mode functions admit a WKB approximation,

u_{q}(\eta)\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2q}}\exp(-\mathrm{i}q\eta),

(15)

\left(u_{q}(\eta)u_{q}^{*}(\eta^{\prime})\right)^{2}\simeq\frac{1}{4q^{2}}\exp[-2\mathrm{i}q(\eta-\eta^{\prime})].

(16)

Substituting into the noise kernel yields

N(\eta,\eta^{\prime};k_{L})\simeq\frac{g^{2}}{4}\int_{q>k_{c}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\exp[-2\mathrm{i}q(\eta-\eta^{\prime})]}{q^{2}}.

(17)

(18)

V
Hartle–Hawking and Tunneling Proposals After Decoherence

We now return to the interpretation of quantum-cosmological boundary conditions in light of the decoherence mechanism developed above. Both the no-boundary proposal of Hartle and Hawking and the tunneling proposal of Vilenkin define pure quantum states of the universe and, by themselves, do not yield classical spacetimes. Decoherence provides the missing dynamical ingredient.

V.1
Semiclassical Branch Structure

In the semiclassical regime, the universal wavefunction admits a WKB decomposition of the form

\Psi[a,\zeta,\sigma]\simeq\sum_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}(a)\exp\left(\mathrm{i}S_{\alpha}(a)\right)\psi_{\alpha}[\zeta,\sigma;a],

(26)

where the label
\alpha
distinguishes different semiclassical branches. For homogeneous minisuperspace variables these branches typically correspond to expanding and contracting classical solutions, while for perturbations they encode different inflationary histories. Importantly, the Wheeler–DeWitt equation is time-reversal invariant, and both types of branches generically appear in the wavefunction for either boundary condition
HartleHawking
;
Vilenkin
.

At the level of the full wavefunction, these branches coexist coherently. The WKB approximation alone does not suppress interference between macroscopically distinct geometries, nor does it select a preferred classical history.

V.2
Reduced Density Matrix in the Branch Basis

The physically relevant object is the reduced density matrix obtained after tracing over unobserved degrees of freedom. Projecting onto the semiclassical branch basis, one finds matrix elements of the schematic form

\rho_{\alpha\beta}\equiv\int\mathcal{D}\zeta_{S}\mathcal{D}\sigma\Psi_{\alpha}\Psi^{*}_{\beta}\propto A_{\alpha}A^{*}_{\beta}\exp\left[-\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}\right],

(27)

where
\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}
is the decoherence functional evaluated for the difference between the long-wavelength field configurations associated with branches
\alpha
and
\beta
.

When
\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}\gg 1
, the off-diagonal elements of the reduced density matrix are exponentially suppressed, and the density matrix becomes approximately diagonal in the branch index. Only at this stage does it become meaningful to interpret the wavefunction as describing an ensemble of mutually exclusive classical histories.

V.3
Expanding versus Contracting Histories

A particularly important application concerns the distinction between expanding and contracting semiclassical branches. In an expanding universe, the available phase space of environmental degrees of freedom grows rapidly with time, leading to strong entanglement between the system and the environment. As shown in Sec. IV, this results in efficient and effectively irreversible decoherence.

In contrast, contracting branches generally generate less entanglement with environmental degrees of freedom. As a result, interference between expanding and contracting branches is suppressed primarily in favor of the expanding histories, which acquire robust classical records. This dynamical asymmetry gives operational content to the intuition often associated with the tunneling proposal, but it arises only after decoherence is taken into account.

V.4
Boundary Conditions versus Classicality

The analysis above clarifies the logically distinct roles played by boundary conditions and decoherence. The Hartle–Hawking and tunneling proposals determine the relative amplitudes
A_{\alpha}
of semiclassical branches in the universal wavefunction. Decoherence, by contrast, determines which of these branches become classical by suppressing quantum interference.

Statements such as “the tunneling proposal predicts an expanding universe” are therefore incomplete unless supplemented by a discussion of environment-induced decoherence. Without decoherence, expanding and contracting branches remain part of a coherent superposition. With decoherence, expanding branches are dynamically selected as classical due to their stronger coupling to environmental degrees of freedom.

V.5
Emergent Arrow of Time

Neither the no-boundary nor the tunneling proposal imposes a fundamental arrow of time at the level of the Wheeler–DeWitt equation. In the present framework, the cosmological arrow of time emerges dynamically from the growth of entanglement between long-wavelength perturbations and their environment. Decoherence increases monotonically with expansion, rendering the arrow of time an emergent, rather than fundamental, property of classical cosmology.

VI
Discussion and Outlook

We have shown that classical cosmology emerges dynamically through environment-induced decoherence. The stochastic description of inflationary perturbations arises as the late-time effective theory of a decohered quantum system

. Our results clarify the conceptual foundations of quantum cosmology and provide a concrete bridge between universal wavefunctions and classical spacetime histories.

Future directions include extending the analysis to reheating, tensor perturbations, and quantum-information-theoretic characterizations of cosmological decoherence.

VII
Conclusion

In this work we have addressed the longstanding question of how classical spacetime and classical cosmological perturbations emerge from quantum cosmology. Starting from standard quantum-cosmological boundary conditions, including the no-boundary and tunneling proposals, we emphasized that semiclassical WKB behavior and squeezing of perturbations are not by themselves sufficient to explain classicality.

By explicitly tracing over unobserved degrees of freedom and employing the influence functional formalism, we derived a decoherence functional for long-wavelength curvature perturbations during inflation. We showed that interactions with environmental degrees of freedom lead to an efficient and robust suppression of quantum interference between macroscopically distinct perturbation histories. This mechanism renders the reduced density matrix approximately diagonal and justifies the use of classical stochastic descriptions in cosmology.

We demonstrated that the emergence of classicality is largely insensitive to the details of coarse graining, occurring both for horizon-based and effective-field-theory–motivated splits between system and environment. Classical cosmological histories therefore arise as a generic consequence of inflationary dynamics in the presence of additional degrees of freedom, rather than as a fine-tuned outcome of specific assumptions.

Our analysis clarifies the distinct roles played by quantum-cosmological boundary conditions and decoherence. Boundary conditions determine the relative amplitudes of semiclassical branches in the universal wavefunction, while decoherence determines which of these branches become classical. In particular, expanding cosmological histories are dynamically selected as classical due to their enhanced entanglement with environmental degrees of freedom, providing an emergent arrow of time without imposing one at the fundamental level.

Taken together, these results place the foundations of classical cosmology on the same footing as other emergent phenomena in quantum physics. They provide a coherent and quantitatively controlled framework connecting quantum cosmology, decoherence, and the classical universe described by cosmological observations.

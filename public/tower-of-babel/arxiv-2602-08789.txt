Positive mass theorems for manifolds with ALH toroidal ends
Gregory J. Galloway, Tin-Yau Tsang

Abstract
In work with P. Chruściel, L. Nguyen and T.-T. Paetz [ 8 ] , a positive mass theorem was obtained for asymptotically locally hyperbolic manifolds with boundary, having a toroidal end. The proof made use of properties of marginally outer trapped surfaces (MOTS). Here we present some new PMT results for such manifolds, but without boundary, which allow for other more general ends. The proofs, while still MOTS-based, involve a more elaborate technique (related to μ \mu -bubbles) introduced in work of D. A. Lee, M. Lesourd, and R. Unger [ 20 ] for manifolds with an asymptotically flat end, and further developed in [ 23 ] for manifolds with an asymptotically hyperbolic end.



1
Introduction

In
[
5
]
, Chruściel and Delay established positivity of mass in the asymptotically hyperbolic (AH) setting, without requiring a spin assumption. These results have recently been extended to the case with “arbitrary ends”
[
23
]
.
A result in the asymptotically locally hyperbolic (ALH) setting was considered in
[
8
]
.
Theorem 1.1 in
[
8
]
establishes the nonnegativity of the mass of a toroidal end

{\mathcal{E}}
in an ALH manifold
(M^{n},g)
,
4\leq n\leq 7
, assuming the scalar curvature satisfies
R\geq-n(n-1)
, and the mean curvature of
\partial{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies
H<n-1
.
In fact, as noted in
[
7
]
, it is sufficient to assume
H\leq n-1
. Moreover, the assumed product topology can be weakened; see
[
6
, Remark 6]
.

Theorem 1.1 in
[
8
]
also considers the case of an ALH manifold with boundary, with a spherical space (quotient of a sphere) end, the proof of which is
substantially different from the torus case. The spherical space case with “arbitrary ends” is addressed in
[
23
]
.

In this note we consider the ALH case with a toroidal end, which otherwise allows for more general ends.
We focus here on proving the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
,
4\leq n\leq 7
, be a complete orientable ALH manifold with a conformally compactifiable toroidal end
{\mathcal{E}}
,
and with scalar curvature
R\geq-n(n-1)
. Suppose further that
N=\overline{M\setminus{\mathcal{E}}}
is noncompact and asymptotically retractible onto
\partial\cal{E}
. Then the mass of the end
{\mathcal{E}}
is nonnegative.

Various terms in the statement will be defined in the next section. The ‘asymptotically retractible’ assumption is trivially satisfied if
M
is topologically a product, i.e. if
M
is diffeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}\times T^{n-1}
.

As a special class of illustrative examples, consider
the Birmingham-Kottler metrics
[
4
,
2
]
, with negative cosmological constant, in the
k=0
(specifically, flat toroidal) case. In the usual coordinates these are given by,

\displaystyle M=(r_{0},\infty)\times T^{n-1}\,,\qquad g_{m}=\frac{1}{r^{2}-\frac{2m}{r^{n-2}}}dr^{2}+r^{2}h\,,

(1.1)

where
(T^{n-1},h)
is the standard flat torus, and (up to a positive scale factor)
m
is the mass.

If
m>0
then
r_{0}
is the unique root of

\varphi(r)=r^{2}-\frac{2m}{r^{n-2}}
. Similar to the situation for the time slices of the standard Schwarzschild solution,

r=r_{0}
is a coordinate singularity. As
r\to r_{0}
, the torus
T_{r}=\{r\}\times T^{n-1}
limits to a totally geodesic flat torus.
M
can be continued
by, roughly speaking, reflecting across this torus; see
[
3
]
,
[
4
, Secs. 5.5, 6.3]
. The result is a complete time slice with topology
{\mathbb{R}}\times T^{n-1}
and scalar curvature
R=-n(n-1)
.

If instead
m<0
, one can take
r_{0}=0
. In this case the metric
g_{m}
extends a finite distance (from any fixed torus) to
r=0
, at which the curvature blows up in an orthonormal frame (even though the scalar curvature remains
-n(n-1)
); see
[
4
, Sec. 5.5.2]
. In the special case
m=0
we obtain the locally hyperbolic metric,

\displaystyle g_{0}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}dr^{2}+r^{2}h

(1.2)

which after the coordinate change
u=\ln r
gives,

M=(-\infty,\infty)\times T^{n-1}\,,\qquad g_{0}=du^{2}+e^{2u}h\,.

We remark that this solution has been shown under various conditions to be the unique manifold with vanishing mass in the ALH setting; see
[
7
,
18
]
.

Thus, we observe that for this class of Birmingham-Kottler metrics the hypotheses of
Theorem
1.1
are satisfied if and only if the mass is nonnegative,
m\geq 0
.
On the other hand, consider the Horowitz-Myers soliton
[
17
]
. It is a static spacetime solution to the vacuum Einstein equations with negative cosmological constant, which has negative mass. The time slices are asymptotically toroidal and, under suitable scaling, satisfy all the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, except one: the assumption that the set

N=\overline{M\setminus{\mathcal{E}}}
be noncompact. In the Horowitz-Myers case,
N
is diffeomorphic to

T^{n-2}\times D^{2}
.

Regarding the dimension restriction in Theorem
1.1
, as in
[
8
, Thm. 1.1]
, the proof makes use of the existence and regularity theory of marginally outer trapped surfaces (see the next section for discussion), which restricts the dimension
n
to the range
3\leq n\leq 7
. The additional restriction
n\geq 4
is a technical condition arising in the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
, which is used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. If one assumes that the
mass aspect function
associated to the end
{\mathcal{E}}
has a sign, then Theorem
1.1
also holds in dimension
n=3
.

In the next section we present some basic definitions and results used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Section 3 we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
and consider some additional results.

2
Preliminaries

Conformal compactification.
Let
(M^{n},g)
be a smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
. Following the treatment in
[
8
]
(cf.
[
16
]
), we say that
M
has an ALH toroidal end if there exists an
n
-dimensional submanifold with boundary
\mathcal{E}
, which is closed as a subset of
M
, such that, up to isometry,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}=(0,x_{0}]\times T^{n-1},\qquad g|_{{\mathcal{E}}}=g_{0}+x^{n-2}\kappa+o(x^{n-2})\,,

(2.3)

where
g_{0}
is the locally hyperbolic ‘reference metric’,

\displaystyle g_{0}=\frac{1}{x^{2}}[dx^{2}+h]

(2.4)

and
(T^{n-1},h)
is the standard flat torus. (Under the coordinate transformation
r=\frac{1}{x}
we obtain (
1.2
).) As defined,
{\mathcal{E}}
is conformally compactifiable, with defining function
x
and with conformal infinity
\partial_{\infty}{\mathcal{E}}=\{0\}\times T^{n-1}
. We assume that all terms in (
2.3
) are at least
C^{2}
, and that the scalar curvature satisfies the mild decay condition

[
8
, Eq. 4.1]
. We also assume that the error term satisfies the additional decay condition
[
8
, Eq. 4.2]
. This is satisfied, for example, by the Birmingham-Kottler metrics and the Horowitz-Myers soliton.

The term
\kappa
in (
2.3
) is a
(0,2)
tensor on
T^{n-1}
, called the mass aspect tensor. The
mass aspect function

\mu
is obtained by tracing
\kappa
with respect to
h
,
\mu={\rm tr}_{h}\kappa
. Then we define the mass
m=m(g)
with respect to the end
{\mathcal{E}}
, as

\displaystyle m=\int_{T^{n-1}}\mu\ d\Omega_{h}\,.

(2.5)

Under a suitable normalization, the mass, so defined, is a geometric invariant of
g
.

With respect to a toroidal end
{\mathcal{E}}
of
M
, we say that
N=\overline{M\setminus{\mathcal{E}}}
is
asymptotically retractible onto

\partial\cal{E}
if
N
can be expressed as the union of an increasing sequence of sets
N_{i}
, each of which is a retract onto
\partial\cal{E}
.
If
N
is diffeomorphic to a product,
N\approx[0,\infty)\times T^{n-1}
, then it is trivially asymptotically retractible onto
\partial\cal{E}
. However
N
’s with much more complicated topology, e.g by taking connected sums, can satisfy this condition.

Marginally outer trapped surfaces.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
makes use of properties of marginally outer trapped surfaces (MOTS). Here we review basic definitions, and certain properties of MOTS.

MOTS are defined with respect to initial data sets. An initial data set
(M,g,K)
consists of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and a symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor field
K
.
The most basic physical application is when
M
is a spacelike hypersurface in a spacetime

\overline{M}
, with induced metric
g
and second fundamental form
K
.

Associated to an initial data set
(M,g,K)
, are the
local energy density

\mu
and the
local current density

J
of
(M,g,K)
, given by

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{1}{2}\,\left(R-|K|^{2}+(\text{tr}\,K)^{2}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad J=\text{div}\left(K-(\text{tr}\,K)\,g\right)\,,

(2.6)

where
R
is the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
. When
(M,g,K)
is an initial data set embedded in a spacetime
\overline{M}
satisfying the Einstein equations, these quantities are obtained from expressions involving the energy-momentum tensor via the Gauss-Codazzi equations.

(M,g,K)
is said to satisfy the
dominant energy condition
(DEC) if

\mu\geq|\,J\,|.

Let
\Sigma\subset M
be a closed (compact without boundary) two-sided hypersurface in
M
, and let
\nu
be a smooth unit normal field along
\Sigma
in
M
. By convention, we refer to
\nu
as outward pointing. The mean curvature of
\Sigma
in
M
can then be expressed as,

H=\text{div}_{\Sigma}\,\nu\,.

We define the null expansions scalars
\theta^{+}
and
\theta^{-}
along
\Sigma
as follows,

\theta^{+}=\text{tr}_{\Sigma}(K)+H\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\theta^{-}=\text{tr}_{\Sigma}(K)-H\,.

When
(M,g,K)
is an initial data set in a spacetime
\overline{M}
,
\theta^{\pm}
may be expressed as

\theta^{\pm}={\rm div}_{\Sigma}\ell^{\pm}

where
\ell^{\pm}=u\pm\nu
are the future-directed null normal vector fields along
\Sigma
. (Here,
u
is the future directed unit normal to
M
.) Thus,
\ell^{\pm}
measures the divergence of the outgoing/ingoing light rays from
\Sigma
.

We say that
\Sigma
is outer trapped if
\theta^{+}<0
and weakly outer trapped if

\theta^{+}\leq 0
. If
\theta
vanishes identically,
\theta\equiv 0
,

\Sigma
is called a marginally outer trapped surface (MOTS for short).

Despite the absence of a variational characterization of MOTS like
that for minimal surfaces, MOTS have been shown to satisfy a number of properties
analogous to those of minimal surfaces in Riemannian geometry. We will need to make use of the basic existence results for MOTS.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
1
,
9
,
10
]
)
.

Let
(M,g,K)
be an
n
-dimensional,
3\leq n\leq 7
, compact-with-boundary initial data set. Suppose that the boundary can be expressed as a disjoint union
\partial M=\Sigma_{\inn}\cup\Sigma_{\out}
, where
\Sigma_{\inn}
and
\Sigma_{\out}
are nonempty unions of components of
\partial M
with
\theta^{+}\leq 0
on
\Sigma_{\inn}
with respect to the normal pointing into
M
and
\theta^{+}>0
on
\Sigma_{\out}
with respect to the normal pointing out of
M
. Then there is an outermost separating MOTS in
(M,g,K)
that is homologous to
\Sigma_{\out}
.

A considerable amount of work has been done on rigidity aspects of MOTS; see e.g.
[
11
,
13
,
14
]
, to name just a few. Here, we will make use of a local rigidity result in
[
12
]
, which, together with certain arguments used to prove it, has played a role in some of these rigidity papers.

Let
\Sigma
be a separating MOTS in an initial data set
(M,g,K)
, with ‘outward’ normal

\nu
. Then
\Sigma
is said to be
weakly outermost
if there is no closed embedded surface outside of
\Sigma
(side to which
\nu
points) with
\theta^{+}<0
that is homologous to
\Sigma
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
(V^{n},h,K)
,
n\geq 3
, be an initial data set satisfying the DEC,
\mu\geq|J|
.
Suppose
\Sigma^{n-1}
is a weakly outermost MOTS in
V^{n}

that does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature. Then there exists
an outer neighborhood
U\approx[0,\epsilon)\times\Sigma
of
\Sigma
in
V
such that each slice

\Sigma_{t}=\{t\}\times\Sigma
,
t\in[0,\epsilon)
is a MOTS.

There are further rigidity type conclusions in this theorem, but the existence of this local foliation by MOTS is sufficient for our purposes.

Lemma
2.2
will be used in conjunction with a result from
[
11
]
.
An orientable, closed manifold
N^{m}
of dimension
m
is said to satisfy the
cohomology condition
if there are classes
\omega_{1},\,\ldots,\,\omega_{m}\in H^{1}(N,{\mathbb{Z}})
whose cup product

\displaystyle\omega_{1}\smile\cdots\smile\omega_{m}\in H^{m}(N,{\mathbb{Z}})

is non-zero.
The
m
-torus
T^{m}
is a prototypical example satisfying the cohomology condition. As shown in
[
19
, Theorem 2.28]
, such a manifold
N^{m}
,
3\leq m\leq 7
, has a component that does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature (cf.
[
22
]
). We will use this fact, together with the following.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
M
be an orientable, compact
n
-dimensional,
n\geq 3
, manifold with boundary.
Let
\Sigma_{0}
be a component of
\partial M
satisfying the cohomology condition, and suppose there exists a retract of
M
onto
\Sigma_{0}
. Then every closed, embedded hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
homologous to
\Sigma_{0}
satisfies the cohomology condition.

We remark that in
[
11
]
, a condition slightly more general than assuming the existence of a retract
F:M\to\Sigma
is assumed.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and further comments

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

As in the proof of
[
8
, Thm. 1.1]
, we introduce a certain initial data set and make use of properties of MOTS. The initial data set used here is motivated by arguments in
[
20
, Sec. 6]
and
[
23
]
(cf.
[
15
, Sec. 3.3]
).

Suppose, by contradiction, that the mass, as given in (
2.5
), is negative,

m<0
.
Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be the asymptotically toroidal end in
M
, with notation as in Section
2
. For
x_{1}\in(0,x_{0})
, let
\Sigma_{1}
be the toroidal slice
x=x_{1}
. As computed in
[
8
, Section 6]
, the mean curvature
H_{1}
of
\Sigma_{1}
, with respect to
the ‘outward’ normal
\nu
(i.e the normal pointing towards the conformal boundary) of

\Sigma_{1}
is given by,

\displaystyle H_{1}=(n-1)-\frac{1}{2}n\mu x^{n}+o(x^{n})\,,

(3.7)

where, recall,
\mu={\rm tr}_{h}\kappa
is the mass aspect function. Now, by applying
Theorem 1.3 in
[
8
]
, we may assume, without loss of generality, that
\mu
is constant, and, moreover, by (
2.5
) must be negative,
\mu<0
. By choosing
x_{1}
sufficiently small, equation (
3.7
) then implies

\displaystyle H_{1}>\lambda(n-1)\quad\text{for some number $\lambda>1$.}

(3.8)

Now consider the initial data set
(M_{0},g,\hat{K}=K-\frac{h}{n-1}g)
, where
K=-\lambda g
. Here
M_{0}
is an open submanifold of
M
, and
h
is a smooth real-valued function on
M_{0}
,
which we describe momentarily after identifying several open sets in
M
. For
x_{2}\in(x_{1},x_{0})
, let
U_{2}=\{(x,x^{a})\in{\mathcal{E}}:x<x_{2}\}
, and let
U_{1}={\rm int}\ {\mathcal{E}}=\{(x,x^{a})\in{\mathcal{E}}:x<x_{0}\}
.
Finally, let
U_{0}=N_{0}\cup U_{1}=N_{0}\cup{\mathcal{E}}
,
where, for any
D_{0}>0
,
N_{0}=\{p\in M:d(p,\partial U_{1})<D_{0}\}
. By completeness,
\overline{U_{0}\setminus{\mathcal{E}}}
and
\partial U_{0}
are compact.
Also, set
D_{1}=d(\partial U_{1},\partial U_{2})
. (Here
d
is the Riemannian distance with respect
to
g
.)

With
M_{0}
and
h\in C^{\infty}(M_{0})
constructed as in
[
20
, Sec. 6]
(cf.
[
21
, Sec. 3]
), the following holds:

(i)

U_{1}\subset M_{0}\subset U_{0}
, with
\partial M_{0}
a smooth compact hypersurface.

(ii)

h\geq 0
, with
h=0
on
U_{2}
, and
h\to\infty
uniformly on approach to

\partial M_{0}
.

(iii)

For arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
,

|\nabla h|\leq\frac{2(1+\varepsilon)}{(D_{0}-2\varepsilon)D_{1}}\quad\text{on}\quad\overline{U}_{1}\setminus U_{2}\,,

(iv)

and,

|\nabla h|\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}h^{2}<\frac{1}{2}\frac{n}{n-1}h^{2}\quad\text{on}\quad M_{0}\setminus U_{1}\,,

by taking
\varepsilon<\frac{1}{n-1}
.

As we now indicate, for
M_{0}
and
h
so constructed, the initial data set
(M_{0},g,\hat{K})

satisfies the DEC, provided, using completeness, one takes
D_{0}
sufficiently large.

By a straightforward computation
[
20
,
23
]
(see also
[
15
]
), we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}-|\hat{J}|

\displaystyle=\mu+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{n-1}h^{2}-2h\ {\rm div}K\right)-|J+Dh|

\displaystyle\geq\mu-|J|+\frac{1}{2}\frac{n}{n-1}h^{2}+n\lambda h-|\nabla h|

where we have used
K=-\lambda g
. Further, with respect to the initial data set

(M,g,K=-\lambda g)
, using (
2.6
) and the scalar curvature assumption gives

\mu-|J|=R+\lambda^{2}n(n-1)\geq(\lambda^{2}-1)n(n-1)\,,

and hence,

\hat{\mu}-|\hat{J}|\geq(\lambda^{2}-1)n(n-1)+n\lambda h+\frac{1}{2}\frac{n}{n-1}h^{2}-|\nabla h|\,,

where, recall,
\lambda>1
. By taking
D_{0}
sufficiently large (as we can by completeness), it now follows easily from (ii)-(iv) that
\hat{\mu}-|\hat{J}|\geq 0
on
M_{0}
, and hence that the initial data set
(M_{0},g,\hat{K})
satisfies the DEC.

Consider the null expansion of
\theta^{+}_{1}
of
\Sigma_{1}
with respect
(M,g,\hat{K})
. Since

h=0
on
U_{2}
,
\hat{K}=K=-\lambda g
on
U_{2}
. From (
3.8
) this implies,

\theta^{+}_{1}=\text{tr}_{\Sigma}(\hat{K})+H_{1}>-\lambda(n-1)+\lambda(n-1)=0.

Now consider a hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}\subset M_{0}
parallel to
\partial M_{0}
, with respect to a normal neighborhood of
\partial M_{0}
.
The null expansion of

\Sigma_{0}
is given by,

\theta^{+}_{0}=\text{tr}_{\Sigma_{0}}(\hat{K})+H_{0}=-\lambda(n-1)-h|_{\Sigma_{0}}+H_{0}\,.

Since
h
becomes arbitrarily large near
\partial M_{0}
, it follows that
\theta^{+}_{0}<0
if

\Sigma_{0}
is taken sufficiently close to
\partial M_{0}
.
Now let
W
be the compact region bounded by
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
.
W
satisfies the barrier conditions of Lemma
2.1
, and hence there exists an outermost MOTS
\Sigma_{*}
in
W
homologous to
\Sigma_{1}
.

Using the asymptotically retractible assumption, together with the product structure of the region:
x_{0}\leq x\leq x_{1}
in
{\mathcal{E}}
, it is easily seen that
W
retracts onto
\Sigma_{1}
.
Since
\Sigma_{1}\approx T^{n-1}

satisfies the cohomology condition, it follows from Lemma
2.3
that

\Sigma_{*}
satisfies the cohomology condition. Hence, by the comments just before the lemma, some component
\Sigma^{\prime}_{*}
of
\Sigma_{*}
does not carry a metric of positive scalar curvature. Moreover,
\Sigma^{\prime}_{*}
must be weakly outermost. Otherwise, by replacing
\Sigma^{\prime}_{*}
by an outer trapped surface, one could apply Lemma
2.1
to obtain a MOTS with at least some component strictly outside of

\Sigma_{*}
, thereby contradicting that
\Sigma_{*}
is outermost.
But then by Lemma
2.2
, an outer neighborhood of
\Sigma^{\prime}_{*}
is foliated by MOTS, which again contradicts that
\Sigma_{*}
is outermost.∎

Remark.
One may also reach a contradiction by applying the global rigidity result, Theorem 1.2 in
[
11
]
, to the initial data set
(W,g,-\hat{K})
.
However, as the proof of that theorem depends crucially on the lemmas in Section
2
, invoking these lemmas directly leads to a more transparent proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In the absence of the completeness assumption, one still has the following ‘quantitative shielding’ result (cf.
[
20
, Thm. 1.1]
,
[
23
, thm. 1.1]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
,
4\leq n\leq 7
, be an ALH manifold with conformally compactifiable toroidal end

{\mathcal{E}}
. Let
U_{0}
,
U_{1}
, and
U_{2}
be neighborhoods of
{\mathcal{E}}
such that

\overline{U_{2}}\subset U_{1}
,
\overline{U_{1}}\subset U_{0}
, and
\overline{U_{0}\setminus\mathcal{E}}
is compact,
and let

D_{0}={\rm dist}_{g}(\partial U_{0},U_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad D_{1}={\rm dist}_{g}(\partial U_{1},U_{2}).

Suppose the following hold.

(1)

There is a retraction of
U_{0}
onto
\partial{\mathcal{E}}
,

(2)

R_{g}\geq-n(n-1)
on
U_{0}
, and

(3)

the scalar curvature satisfies the strictness condition,

R_{g}+n(n-1)>\frac{4}{D_{0}D_{1}}\quad\text{on}\quad\overline{U_{1}}\setminus U_{2}\,.

(3.9)

Then the mass of the end
{\mathcal{E}}
is nonnegative.

The proof follows along lines similar to that of Theorem
1.1
. One again assumes, by contradiction, that the mass is negative, and considers as before the initial data set

(M,g,\hat{K})
, except now one can take
K=-g
(i.e. take
\lambda=1
). In checking that
(M,g,\hat{K})
satisfies the DEC, the strict scalar curvature condition (
3.9
) replaces
the need to take
\lambda>1
and the need to make
D_{0}
sufficiently large.

A version of Theorem
3.1
for manifolds with boundary, when the mean curvature inequality
H\leq n-1
is not satisfied can be obtained along the lines of
[
20
, Thm. 1.7]
and
[
23
, Thm. 1.2]
. Theorem 3.6 in
[
15
]
can also be modified to obtain a
manifold-with-boundary result in the present setting.

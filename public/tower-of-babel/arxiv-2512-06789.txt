Global existence of solutions for semilinear damped wave equation with nonlinearities of derivative type Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author: Tuan Anh Dao (anh.daotuan@hust.edu.vn)


Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the semilinear damped wave equation with nonlinearities of derivative type | u t | p |u_{t}|^{p} . We observe that this problem admits a unique global (in time) solution with small initial data for all p > 1 p>1 in low spatial dimensions n = 1 , 2 n=1,2 . This result provides new insights into semilinear damped wave equations and complements the existing literature.

Introduction

Let us consider the following Cauchy problem for semilinear damped wave equations:

(1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=|u_{t}|^{p},&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
(u,u_{t})(0,x)=\varepsilon(u_{0},u_{1})(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\end{cases}

where
p>1
,
\varepsilon>0

describes the size of initial data. The quantity
|u_{t}|^{p}
is called the nonlinearity of derivative type.

We now present a brief overview of the research history of the following problem:

(2)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=F(u),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
(u,u_{t})(0,x)=\varepsilon(u_{0},u_{1})(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

For the problem (
2
) with
F(u)\equiv 0
, known as the linear damped wave equations, Matsumura
[
9
]
was the first to obtain some basic decay estimates. Subsequently, it has been established that the damped wave equation has a diffusive structure as
t\to\infty
. Since then, many papers have studied sharp
L^{m}-L^{q}

estimates (with
1\leq m\leq q\leq\infty
) for the linear problem (
2
), for example,
[
10
,
4
,
12
]
and the references cited therein.

Taking into account the semilinear problem (
2
) with
F(u)\not\equiv 0
, it has attracted the interest of many mathematicians over the years. For example, papers
[
16
,
14
]
investigated the nonlinear term
F(u)\equiv u|u|^{p-1}
, whereas papers
[
21
,
17
]
studied for
F(u)\equiv|u|^{p}
. From this, under the additional regularity
L^{1}
for the initial data, the authors showed that the Fujita exponent
p_{F}(n):=1+2/n
is the critical exponent of these problems by proving the global existence of solutions when
p>p_{F}(n)
and the existence of blow-up solutions in finite time when
p<p_{F}(n)
with small initial data. Moreover, the paper
[
22
]
showed that the value
p=p_{F}(n)
belongs to the blow-up range for
F(u)\equiv|u|^{p}
. Here, the critical exponent is understood as the threshold
between the global (in time) existence of small data solutions and the blow-up of solutions
even for small data. Recently, Ebert et al.
[
8
]
investigated the Cauchy problem (
2
) with nonlinear term
F(u)\equiv|u|^{p_{F}(n)}\mu(|u|)
and established the sharp condition on
\mu(s)
that separates the blow-up case from the global existence case. Specifically, this problem has a unique global (in time) small data solution if the
Dini condition
is satisfied. Moreover, it admits a blow-up solution in finite time for all spatial dimensions
n\geq 1
if
\mu
satisfies the
non-Dini condition
.
Under additional regularity
L^{m}
for the initial data, with
m\in[1,2]
, papers
[
14
,
4
]
and references therein identified the critical exponent of problem (
2
) as
p_{c}(m)=p_{F}(n/m)=1+2m/n.
However, the authors didn’t provide conclusions regarding the solution’s properties when
p=p_{c}(m)
. Paper
[
12
]
has proved the existence of global mild solutions when
p=p_{c}(m)
for all
m\in(1,2]
in small dimentional spaces along with some other conditions.

Following the methods and results developed for the damped wave equation, many mathematicians have broadened their study to the following classes of evolution equations:

(3)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}+\mu_{1}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{1}}u_{t}+\mu_{2}(-\Delta)^{\sigma_{2}}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u=|u_{t}|^{p},&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t>0,\\
(u,u_{t})(0,x)=\varepsilon(u_{0},u_{1})(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

where
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\geq 0,\,\sigma\geq 1
and
0\leq\sigma_{1}<\sigma/2<\sigma_{2}\leq\sigma
. The operator
(-\Delta)^{\sigma}
stands for the fractional Laplacian with the
\sigma
-th power, well-known as high-order waves, where the parameter
\sigma
may be non-integer. In the non-integer case, it is determined by

(-\Delta)^{\sigma}\psi(x)=\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\big(|\xi|^{2\sigma}\widehat{\psi}(\xi)\big)(x),

where
\psi\in H^{2\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The term
(-\Delta)^{\theta}u_{t}
for some
\theta\geq 0
is called a damping term, specifically, it is often referred to the frictional (or external) damping, the parabolic like damping, the
\sigma
-evolution like damping and the visco-elastic (or strong) damping when
\theta=0
,
\theta=\sigma_{1}\in(0,\sigma/2)
,
\theta=\sigma_{2}\in(\sigma/2,\sigma)
and
\theta=\sigma
, respectively. Under the additional regularity
L^{1}
for the initial data, the paper
[
4
]
investigated the problem (
3
) with only parabolic-like damping appearing, namely
\mu_{1}>0,\,\mu_{2}=0
, and derived the condition on the exponent
p
that ensures the existence of a unique global (in time) solution to (
3
) as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\in\left(0,\frac{\sigma}{2}\right),\quad 1+\frac{2\sigma_{1}}{n}<p<1+\frac{4\sigma_{1}}{[n-4\sigma_{1}]^{+}}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,n\bigg[\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg]^{+}(\sigma-2\sigma_{1})<2\sigma_{1},

where we denote by
[\eta]^{+}:=\max\{0,\eta\}
for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
, its positive part.
Therefore, in the case of the frictional damping term, corresponding to
\sigma_{1}=0
, these conditions show certain contradictions. The problem containing the two damping mechanisms, corresponding to
\mu_{1}>0,\,\,\mu_{2}>0
, paper
[
3
]
is discussed for
\sigma_{1}=0,\sigma=\sigma_{2}=1
. The author found the global existence of small data solutions to (
3
) when
p\in(1,\,1+2/n)
in any space dimension
n\geq 1
by using energy estimates and
L^{1}-L^{1}
estimates. After that, paper
[
2
]
examined the situation
\sigma=1,\,\,0\leq\sigma_{1}<1/2<\sigma_{2}\leq 1
and established the following conditions on the exponent
p
guaranteeing the existence of a unique global (in time) solution:

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2\sigma_{1}}{n}<p\leq\frac{n}{n-2\sigma_{1}}\text{ for all }n\geq 1.

Further extending this line of research, the authors in
[
6
,
1
]
considered the general case for
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
and obtained the condition on
p
, namely

\displaystyle p\geq 2\quad\text{ and }\quad p>1+\max\bigg\{\frac{n}{2},\,2(\sigma-\sigma_{1})\bigg\}.

Based the above discussion, it appears that the earlier results encounter a singularity at the value
\sigma_{1}=0
, corresponding to frictional damping. It is conjectured that once this damping mechanism is involved, the semilinear problem (
3
) admits a unique global (in time) solution for all

p>1
.

Motivated by these considerations, in this paper, our aim is to establish a unique global (in time) existence result for solutions with small initial data for the problem (
1
) in the presence of the damping term
u_{t}
. In particular, we obtain such a result for all
p>1
in spatial dimensions
n=1,2
. This also shows that when the nonlinear term
|u|^{p}

is replaced by
|u_{t}|^{p}
, the critical exponent shifts to the left compared to the Fujita exponent.

Main results.

Let us state global (in time) existence of solutions with small initial data, which will be proved in this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
and the exponent

p>\max\left\{1,\frac{n}{2}\right\}.

Moreover, we assume that
s\in\left(\frac{n}{2},p\right)
and denote that

\alpha:=\min\{2,p\},\quad\beta_{\alpha}:=(n-1)\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\right),\,\,\delta_{\alpha}:=n\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\right).

In addition, the initial data satisfies

(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{D}:=(H^{s+1}\cap H^{\delta_{\alpha}+1}_{\alpha}\cap L^{1})\times(H^{s}\cap H_{\alpha}^{\beta_{\alpha}}\cap L^{1}).

Then, there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, problem (
1
) admits a unique global (in time) solution

\displaystyle u\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),L^{2})\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,\infty),H^{s}\cap L^{\alpha}).

Furthermore, the following estimates hold for all
t>0
:

\displaystyle\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})-1}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}},

\displaystyle\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4}-1-\frac{s}{2}}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}}.

Remark 1.1
.

From Theorem
1.1
, it follows that the problem (
1
) has a unique global (in time) solution for all
p>1
in low dimensional spaces
n=1,2
.

2.1.
Linear estimates and solution spaces

To begin with, we can write the solution to the linear problem
(\ref{Main.Eq.2})
, corresponding to
F(u)\equiv 0
, by the formula

u^{\rm lin}(t,x)=\varepsilon(\mathcal{K}(t,x)+\partial_{t}\mathcal{K}(t,x))\ast_{x}u_{0}(x)+\varepsilon\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}u_{1}(x),

becomes

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=u^{\rm lin}(t,x)+u^{\rm non}(t,x),

\displaystyle u^{\rm non}(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|u_{t}(\tau,x)|^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(t,\xi)=\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\sinh{\left(t\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-|\xi|^{2}}\right)}}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-|\xi|^{2}}}&\text{ if }|\xi|\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{2},\\

\displaystyle\chi_{\rm L}(r)=\begin{cases}1&\quad\text{ if }r\leq\varepsilon^{*}/2,\\

It is obvious to see that

\displaystyle\||u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}-|v_{t}(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{L^{m}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-1}\|u_{t}-v_{t}\|_{Y(T)}(1+\tau)^{-p+1-\frac{n}{2}(p-1-\frac{1}{\gamma})}\left(\|u_{t}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v_{t}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-p-\frac{n}{2}(p-\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})}\|u_{t}-v_{t}\|_{Y(T)}\left(\|u_{t}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v_{t}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right),

provided that the following conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle m\in(1,2),\quad\frac{1}{m}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\gamma(p-1)}}{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{s}{n}}\in[0,1].

Therefore, we can choose the parameter
\gamma
satifies

0<\frac{1}{\gamma}<\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{p-1}{\alpha}\right\}.

Next, using Propositions
A.1
and
A.3
with

\frac{n}{2}-\frac{2\kappa}{p-1}=:d<\frac{n}{2}<s<p,

one can see that

\displaystyle\|u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{d}}+\|u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-1-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{\kappa}{p-1}}\|u_{t}\|_{X(T)}.

Therefore, we arrive at

\displaystyle\||u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}-|v_{t}(\tau,\cdot)|^{p}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)-v_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1}+\|v_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-p-\frac{n}{2}(p-1)+\kappa}\|u_{t}-v_{t}\|_{Y(T)}\left(\|u_{t}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v_{t}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right).

Summarily, we obtain the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}[u]-\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}[v]\|_{Y(T)}\lesssim\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big\{\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)\big\}\|u_{t}-v_{t}\|_{Y(T)}\left(\|u_{t}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v_{t}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle:=(1+t)\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-1}(1+\tau)^{-p-\frac{n}{2}(p-\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})}d\tau,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle:=(1+t)\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-1}(1+\tau)^{-p-\frac{n}{2}(p-1)+\kappa}d\tau.

We can see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)\lesssim 1,

for all
t>0
due to
m\in(1,2)
and

\frac{1}{\gamma}<p-1\quad\text{ implies }\quad-p-\frac{n}{2}\left(p-\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\right)<-1.

Moreover, it holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)\lesssim 1,

for all
t>0
due to

\displaystyle-p-\frac{n}{2}(p-1)+\kappa<-1.

Thus, we completed the proof of Proposition
2.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Using again Lemma
2.1
, it holds

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u^{\rm lin}\|_{X(T)}\lesssim\varepsilon\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}},

for all
T>0
.
For this reason, combining with Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
, we obtain the following estimates for all
u,v\in Z(T)
:

(10)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}[u]\|_{X(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\varepsilon\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}}+C_{1}\|u_{t}\|_{X(T)}^{p},

(11)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}[u]-\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}[v]\|_{Y(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|u_{t}-v_{t}\|_{Y(T)}\left(\|u_{t}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|v_{t}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\right).

Let us fix

(12)

\displaystyle M:=2C_{1}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{D}}

and the parameter
\varepsilon_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\max\{C_{1},2C_{2}\}M^{p-1}\varepsilon_{0}^{p-1}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Next, taking the recurrence sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j}\subset Z(T)
with
u_{0}=0;\,u_{j}=\mathcal{N}[u_{j-1}]
for
j=1,2,...
, we employ the estimate (
10
) to have

\|\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{X(T)}\leq M\varepsilon,

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
.
Moreover, using estimate (
11
), one has

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u_{j+1}-\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{Y(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}M^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p-1}\|\partial_{t}u_{j}-\partial_{t}u_{j-1}\|_{Y(T)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}u_{j}-\partial_{t}u_{j-1}\|_{Y(T)},

so that
\{\partial_{t}u_{j}\}_{j}\subset X(T,M\varepsilon)
is a Cauchy sequence in the Banach
space
Y(T)
. Therefore, combining this with Lemma
2.2
, there exists a function
\varphi\in X(T,M\varepsilon)
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{j}\to\varphi\text{ in }X(T,M\varepsilon),

as
j\to\infty
, with the metric
Y(T)
.
Next, we consider the following function:

\displaystyle u^{*}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(\tau,x)d\tau+u_{0}(x).

Additionally, one has

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to t_{0}}\|u^{*}(t,\cdot)-u^{*}(t_{0},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lim_{t\to t_{0}}\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\varphi(\tau,\cdot)\|_{X(T)}d\tau\right|\leq M\varepsilon\lim_{t\to t_{0}}|t-t_{0}|=0,

for all
t_{0}\in[0,T]
. From this, we obtain

\displaystyle u^{*}\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],L^{2}).

Moreover, thanks to again the estimate (
11
), it holds

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u_{j+1}-\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}[u^{*}]\|_{Y(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|\partial_{t}u_{j}-\varphi\|_{Y(T)}(\|\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|\varphi\|_{X(T)}^{p-1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}u_{j}-\varphi\|_{Y(T)},

that is,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{j}\to\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}[u^{*}],

as
j\to\infty
, with the metric
Y(T)
.
Therefore, we can immediately conclude

\displaystyle\varphi\equiv\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}[u^{*}],

that is, there exists a function
\gamma(x)
satisfies

(13)

\displaystyle u^{*}(t,x)=\mathcal{N}[u^{*}](t,x)+\gamma(x),

for all
t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Taking
t=0
in (
13
), we can see that
\gamma(x)\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
due to
u^{*}(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
. For this reason, we have a solution
u^{*}=\mathcal{N}[u^{*}]
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T],L^{2})
for all
T>0
. Since
T
is arbitrary, the solution is global, that is,
u^{*}\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),L^{2})
.

Next, we need to show that
u^{*}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,\infty),H^{s}\cap L^{\alpha})
, that is,
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),H^{s}\cap L^{\alpha})
. To prove this property, we recall that

\displaystyle\varphi(t,x)=\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}[u^{*}](t,x)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}u^{\rm lin}(t,x)+\partial_{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|\partial_{t}u^{*}(\tau,x)|^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}u^{\rm lin}(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|\varphi(\tau,x)|^{p}d\tau.

Since the linear part obviously satisfies continuity, it suffices to show that

(14)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|\varphi(\tau,x)|^{p}d\tau\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),H^{s}\cap L^{\alpha}).

Noting that
\varphi\in X(\infty,M\varepsilon)
and using again Lemmas
2.1
-
2.3
, we have the following estimates:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|\varphi(\tau,x)|^{p}\|_{L^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-p-\frac{n}{2}(p-\frac{1}{\alpha})}\|\varphi\|_{X(\infty)}^{p},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}|\varphi(\tau,x)|^{p}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-p-\frac{n}{2}(p-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{s}{2}+\kappa}\|\varphi\|_{X(\infty)}^{p},

where
\kappa
is an arbitrarily small positive constant. Therefore, the Lebesgue convergence theorem in the Bochner integral immediately implies (
14
).

Finally, we establish the uniqueness of the global solution

u^{*}
, which lies in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),L^{2})\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,\infty),H^{s}\cap L^{\alpha})
. Let
u^{*},v^{*}
are solutions of (
1
) belonging to this space. Using again the arbitrary positive number
T
and performing the same proof steps of Proposition
2.2
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u^{*}-\partial_{t}v^{*}\|_{Y(t)}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}u^{*}-\partial_{t}v^{*}\|_{Y(\tau)}\big(\|\partial_{t}u^{*}\|_{X(\tau)}^{p-1}+\|\partial_{t}v^{*}\|_{X(\tau)}^{p-1}\big)d\tau,

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
One can see that there exists a constant
M(T)
such that
\|\partial_{t}u^{*}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}+\|\partial_{t}v^{*}\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\leq M(T)
. Therefore, we immediately have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u^{*}-\partial_{t}v^{*}\|_{Y(t)}\lesssim M(T)\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}u^{*}-\partial_{t}v^{*}\|_{Y(\tau)}d\tau,

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
From this, the
Gronwall inequality implies
\partial_{t}u^{*}\equiv\partial_{t}v^{*}
on
[0,T]
. Because
T
is an arbitrary positive number,
\partial_{t}u^{*}\equiv\partial_{t}v^{*}
on
[0,\infty)
. We note that
u^{*}(0,x)=v^{*}(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
, this implies
u^{*}\equiv v^{*}
on
[0,\infty)
.
Hence, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is completed.

3.
Final remarks

Remark 3.1
.

In this paper, we have obtained the unique global (in time) existence for solution to (
1
) in one and two dimensional spaces for all
p>1
. A natural question that arises is what the critical exponent for problem (
1
) would be in higher dimensions

n\geq 3
. We believe that the entire range
p>1
remains the global existence range. However, due to certain limitations related to the high regularity of the initial data required in the estimates for solutions of the linear problem (
2
) (
F(u)\equiv 0
), we are still facing challenges in proving this.

Remark 3.2
.

In future work, our aim is to study the initial-boundary value problem of the semilinear damped wave
equations with nonlinear term
|u_{t}|^{p}
in the exterior of the closed unit ball
\mathcal{B}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|\leq 1\},
that is:

(15)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=|u_{t}|^{p},&\text{ for }x\in\mathcal{B}^{c},\,t>0,\\
u=0,&\text{ for }x\in\partial\mathcal{B}^{c},\,t>0,\\
(u,u_{t})(0,x)=(u_{0},u_{1})(x)&\text{ for }x\in\mathcal{B}^{c}.\end{cases}

In papers
[
13
,
18
,
15
,
19
]
, the authors investigated the problem (
15
) with nonlinearity
|u|^{p}

instead of
|u_{t}|^{p}
, and established the critical exponent in the two dimensional space, that is,
p_{c}=p_{F}(2)=2
. Therefore, we conjecture that the problem (
15
) admits global solutions for all
p>1
in some specific spatial dimensions. This is really a challenging problem, which will be partially investigated in our forthcoming work.

Proposition A.1

(Fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, see Corollary 2.4 in
[
11
]
)
.

Let
1<p,\,p_{0},\,p_{1}<\infty
,
a>0
and
\theta\in[0,a)
. Then, it holds

\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta}_{p}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{p_{0}}}^{1-\omega(\theta,a)}\,\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{a}_{p_{1}}}^{\omega(\theta,a)},

where
\omega(\theta,a)=\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\theta}{n}}{\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{a}{n}}
and
\displaystyle\frac{\theta}{a}\leq\omega(\theta,a)\leq 1
.

Proposition A.2

(Fractional powers, see Proposition 42 and Corollary 43 in
[
7
]
)
.

Let
p>1
,
1<r<\infty
, where
s\in\big(n/r,p\big)
. Let us denote by
F(u)
one of the functions
|u|^{p},\,\pm|u|^{p-1}u
. Then, the following estimates hold:

\|F(u)\|_{H^{s}_{r}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}_{r}}\,\,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|F(u)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{r}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{r}}\,\,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1}.

Proposition A.3

(A fractional Sobolev embedding, see Corollary A.2 in
[
5
]
)
.

Let
1<q<\infty
and
0<s_{1}<n/q<s_{2}
. Then, it holds

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}}_{q}}+\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{2}}_{q}}.

Proposition A.4

(Fractional chain rule, see Theorem 1.5 in
[
20
]
)
.

Let
s>0,\,p>\lceil s\rceil
and

satisfying the relation

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{p-1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}.

Let us denote by
F(u)
one of the functions

\displaystyle\|F(u)\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{r_{1}}}^{p-1}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{r_{2}}}.

T cells are critical for pre-metastatic niche formation through the STAT3/IL-17 axis
Authors: Maciej Kujawski, Antons Martincuks, Mihae Song, Chunyan Zhang, Rui Huang, Tianyi Wang, Rosemary Senguttuvan, Brad Nakamura, Michael Palmer, Eliza R Bacon, Kena Ihle, Stephen J Forman, John E Shively, Lorna Rodriguez-Rodriguez, Saul J Priceman, Hua Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPre-metastatic niches composed of mainly myeloid cells are recognized as critical for tumor metastasis. However, whether adaptive immune cells also play an important role in pre-metastatic niche formation remains to be explored.MethodsCD4+ T cell accumulation in tumor-free lung tissues from mice bearing subcutaneous mouse tumors was detected by immunofluorescence/confocal microscopy. Tumor-conditioned media (TCM) from MB49-S1pr1high mouse bladder tumor cells or ID8 ovarian tumor cells were administered to tumor-free mice to induce pre-metastatic niche formation. We used mice lacking functional Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (STAT3) in T cells and Il17a‒/‒ mice to investigate the roles of STAT3 and interleukin (IL)-17. In vivo time-course experiments were performed to assess whether CD4+ T cell clusters contribute to CD11b+ pre-metastatic clusters. CD4+ T cell migration and chemokine receptor expression assays were employed to identify tumor factors driving CD4+ T cell recruitment. A co-culture system with human MRC-5 lung fibroblasts, healthy donor-derived CD4+ T cells, myeloid cells, and TCM derived from human cancer cells was used to evaluate CD4+ T cell-driven fibroblast activation and IL-17A dependency for myeloid cell migration. Microscopic analyses were performed to confirm CD4+ T cell clusters in tumor-free lymph node tissues from patients with prostate cancer and postmortem lung and liver specimens from patients with ovarian cancer.ResultsWe demonstrate that CD4+ T cells accumulate in tumor-free lungs and promote tumor metastasis in mouse models. CD4+ T-cell pre-metastatic niche formation requires STAT3, which regulates Th17 CD4+ T cells. TCM drives IL-6-dependent CCR4/CCR6 upregulation on naive CD4+ T cells. CD4+ T cell clusters contribute to myeloid cell accumulation, and ablating STAT3 in T cells abrogates both T cell and myeloid cluster formation. IL-17 inhibition reduces myeloid lung infiltration. In human co-cultures, CD4+ T cells amplify TCM-induced fibroblast pre-metastatic niche-like activation and myeloid recruitment in an IL-17A-dependent manner. CD4+ and IL-17+ or p-STAT3+ clusters were also detected in non-metastatic tissues from patients with several cancers.ConclusionsCD4+ T cells form pre-metastatic niches through the STAT3-IL-17 axis, contributing to myeloid cell cluster formation, in part through amplifying fibroblast pre-metastatic niche-like activation. STAT3 and IL-17 in CD4+ T cells therefore are important for pre-metastatic niche formation and metastasis.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

Pre-metastatic niche is a complex microenvironment composed of various cell types, including immune cells, stromal cells, and extracellular matrix components that facilitate tumor cell colonization and metastasis at distant sites. While prior research has focused on myeloid cells as key orchestrators of pre-metastatic niche formation, the potential role of adaptive immune cells like CD4+ T cells in this process remains largely unexplored.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

This study demonstrates that CD4+ T cells accumulate in distant tumor-free sites to form pre-metastatic niches that are critical for tumor metastasis, and that this process requires Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (STAT3) signaling. In addition, Th17 cells are essential for myeloid cell pre-metastatic niche formation through interleukin (IL)-17 production, with CD4+ T-cell clusters temporally preceding myeloid cell clustering in tumor-free lungs. CD4+ T cells also contribute to lung fibroblast niche-like activation, which is an important part of pre-metastatic niche formation. Our study illustrates that STAT3-IL-17 in CD4+ T cells can be effective targets to reduce pre-metastatic niche formation and metastasis.

HOW THIS STUDY MIGHT AFFECT RESEARCH, PRACTICE OR POLICY

Our findings suggest that in addition to myeloid cells, CD4+ T cells are critical for pre-metastatic niche formation. Importantly, this study also shows that STAT3 or IL-17 in CD4+ T cells may serve as novel therapeutic targets to prevent both T cell and myeloid pre-metastatic niche formation, thereby reducing tumor metastasis. This work broadens the roles of immune cells in metastasis and opens new opportunities to develop combination therapies to improve outcomes for cancer patients at risk of metastatic disease.

Background

Tumor metastasis remains a primary cause of mortality in patients with cancer. An exciting advancement in cancer research is the identification of the pre-metastatic niche required for tumor colonization at distant sites.1–3 However, the precise mechanisms involved in pre-metastatic niche formation and novel therapeutic approaches to intercept this complex event remain to be further explored. Apart from extracellular matrix modifications and stromal reprogramming, various immune cells orchestrate immunosuppressive microenvironments and actively promote each step of the metastatic cascade.4 Pioneering work has established that myeloid cells form pre-metastatic niches to which tumor cells adhere, survive and colonize.5–7 Both myeloid cells and T cells in the primary tumor can regulate tumor growth and metastasis.8–10 While the interactions between CD4+ T cells and tumor-associated myeloid cells are important for promoting tumor development, whether CD4+ T cells cooperate with myeloid cells to condition future metastatic sites to facilitate the survival and colonization of tumor cells remains unknown.

Multiple signaling mediators have been investigated in pre-metastatic niche formation. VEGFR1-signaling was originally implicated in hematopoietic cell accumulation in the pre-metastatic niche, dominated by myeloid cells.5 Other key signaling molecules in establishing the niche include fibronectin, LOX, MMPs, and HIF.2 5 Our previous work demonstrated that the transcription factor Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (STAT3) regulates the expression of many of these genes in myeloid cells and contributes to the accumulation and function of myeloid cells in the pre-metastatic niche.11 Although STAT3 is a critical mediator of T-cell differentiation and Th subset balance in tumors,12 it remains unknown whether STAT3 in CD4+ T cells promotes metastasis through the formation of pre-metastatic niches. In the current study, we focus on understanding the role and underlying mechanism(s) by which CD4+ T cells facilitate pre-metastatic niches.

Results

CD4+ T cells accumulate in pre-metastatic areas of the lung

Protumorigenic and pro-metastatic effects of endogenous CD4+ T cells in primary tumors have been demonstrated in several tumor models.8 15 16 Nevertheless, whether and how CD4+ T cells may promote pre-metastatic niche formation remains to be fully addressed. While investigating the process of spontaneous metastasis to the lungs in mice injected subcutaneously with mouse bladder carcinoma MB49 cells, we found significantly higher accumulation of CD4+ T cells in non-metastatic areas of the lung (figure 1A, top right), compared with the lung of tumor-free mice (figure 1A, top left). Flow cytometric analysis is more sensitive and quantitative than immunofluorescence/microscopic analysis to detect immune cells in tissues. However, prior publications suggest the global changes of immune cells in the whole lungs at initial stages of pre-metastatic niche formation are limited.17 18 We therefore used immunofluorescence/microscopic analysis for the above experiments.

In three other tumor models (B16 melanoma, 8101 fibrosarcoma and Renca renal cell carcinoma), without macroscopic metastases in the lungs at 3–4 weeks after tumor inoculation (figure 1A, lower panels), we also found CD4+ T cells accumulating near the terminal alveolar structures of the lungs. Because one cannot rule out the presence of micrometastatic lesions without further characterization such as sensitive PCR-based assays for disseminated tumor cells, we performed experiments injecting tumor-conditioned media (TCM) in the absence of tumor cells. We found that tumor-free mice showed similar high CD4+ T-cell accumulation in the same anatomical locations of the lung following daily intraperitoneal (i.p.) injections of TCM from MB49 tumor cells overexpressing G protein-coupled receptor-1 for the lysophospholipid sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1PR1) (MB49-S1pr1high), and ID8 ovarian carcinoma cells (figure 1B,C). We published that MB49-S1pr1high cells, but not the parental MB49 tumor cells, upregulated STAT3-driven pre-metastatic niche formation.11 We observed CD4+ T-cell lung infiltration as early as 24 hours following a single intravenous injection of TCM in both tumor models (figure 1D). Since other immune cell types, such as dendritic cells, can express CD4, we confirmed the co-localization of T cell-specific marker TCR with CD4 in mouse lungs after 5-day TCM treatment (online supplemental figure 1A). To characterize these cell types further, we co-stained control and TCM-treated mouse lung tissues with CD8 and CD4 antibodies. We did not detect CD8+ T lymphocytes in the TCM-induced CD4+ clusters (online supplemental figure 1A). Additionally, to validate that CD4+ T-cell clusters function as pre-metastatic niches, we analyzed fibronectin expression within these CD4+ clusters. Fibronectin is a recognized pre-metastatic niche marker that serves as a scaffold for initiating pre-metastatic niche clustering.1 5 Increased fibronectin expression in mouse lungs has been used in multiple studies to identify pre-metastatic niches;5 19 we show that fibronectin co-localized with CD4+ clusters (online supplemental figure 1C). Antibody-mediated depletion of CD4+ T cells prior to primary MB49 tumor establishment significantly reduced tumor growth and metastasis to the lungs (online supplemental figure 1D).

Stat3 ablation in T cells reduces pre-metastatic niche formation

T cell-specific STAT3 signaling can mediate tumor immunosuppression at the primary tumor site.20 21 To investigate if STAT3 signaling in CD4+ T cells plays a role in pre-metastatic niche formation, we used CD4Cre-Stat3Flox mice lacking functional STAT3 in T cells.21 MB49 tumor growth and metastasis were significantly inhibited in mice lacking functional Stat3 in T cells (CD4-Stat3–/– mice) compared with controls (CD4-Stat3+/+ mice) (online supplemental figure 2A,B). Furthermore, no spontaneous metastases in the lungs were detected in CD4-Stat3–/– mice even when we allowed primary tumors to reach a similar size in both groups (figure 2A, online supplemental figure 2C). The lungs of tumor-bearing mice show significantly more CD4+ T cells in the tumor-free areas in CD4-Stat3+/+ mice compared with CD4-Stat3–/– mice. The majority of CD4+ T cells in the lungs showed high STAT3 activation (phospho-STAT3, p-STAT3) (figure 2B). We observed similarly elevated CD4+ T-cell infiltrates and p-STAT3 in the pre-metastatic niche of tumor-free CD4-Stat3+/+, but not of tumor-free CD4-Stat3–/– mice receiving TCM for 5 days (figure 2C).

To assess whether STAT3 is critical for TCM-induced CD4+ T-cell migration, freshly isolated splenic CD4+ T cells from tumor-naive CD4-Stat3+/+ and CD4-Stat3–/– mice (hereafter Stat3+/+ and Stat3–/– CD4+ T cells, respectively) were subject to an in vitro T-cell migration assay. Stat3+/+ CD4+ T cells, but not Stat3–/– CD4+ T cells, showed enhanced migration (figure 2D) and STAT3 activation in response to MB49-S1pr1highTCM (online supplemental figure 2D), or to ID8 ovarian tumor TCM (online supplemental figure 2E,F). To identify factors in the TCM that promote CD4+ T-cell lung homing, we depleted interleukin (IL)-6, one of the main STAT3 activators, from the MB49-S1pr1high TCM and ID8 TCM using neutralizing antibody prior to treating Stat3+/+ CD4+ T cells. IL-6 depletion significantly reduced p-STAT3 in T cells and their migratory capacity induced by the two TCM (figure 2E, online supplemental figure 2G, and online supplemental figure 2H,I).

To further clarify the mechanism of IL-6-driven CD4⁺ T cell recruitment, we examined chemokine receptor expression on naive Stat3+/+ CD4+ T cells treated with MB49-S1pr1high TCM, with or without IL-6 neutralizing antibody. Western blot analysis showed that TCM upregulated CCR4 and CCR6, which was reduced by IL-6 neutralization (online supplemental file 2). Functional CCR6 blockade reduced MB49-S1pr1high TCM-induced CD4⁺ T cell migration (online supplemental figure 2K).

CD4+ T-cell clusters temporally precede CD11b+ myeloid cell recruitment

The ability of several soluble factors released by different immune cells, including T cells, to attract myeloid cells and other inflammatory cells to lungs has been reported in infection, allergy and asthma models.22–24 To test whether CD4+ T cells with activated STAT3 can contribute to myeloid cell lung infiltration, we evaluated CD11b+ cell infiltration in lungs from mice injected with MB49-S1pr1high TCM. Immunofluorescence staining indicated robust myeloid cell lung infiltration in TCM-treated mice as previously described,11 which was significantly higher in CD4-Stat3+/+ mice in comparison to CD4-Stat3–/– mice (figure 3A).

We performed a time course tracking the appearance of both CD4+ and CD11b+ cell clusters following TCM injection (figure 3B). Our results show that CD4+ clusters were detectable on day 2 post TCM injection, while CD11b+ clusters only started appearing after 5 days of treatment, which were further increased after 10 days of treatment (figure 3C). Subsequent analysis of specific CD11b+ cell populations during pre-metastatic niche formation showed the majority of infiltrated CD11b+ cells are CD11b+ Gr-1+ immature myeloid cells rather than metastasis-associated CD11b+ F4/80+ macrophages (online supplemental figure 3).

Th17 CD4+ T cells contribute to myeloid cell pre-metastatic niche formation

Tumors can be infiltrated by different helper CD4+ T-cell subsets with distinct functions; among them are protumor, immunosuppressive regulatory T cells (Tregs), as well as IL-17 cytokine-producing (Th17) cells.25 26 To assess which CD4+ T helper cell subsets infiltrate pre-metastatic lungs, we dissected out areas with strong CD4+ accumulation from frozen lung tissues collected from mice injected with TCM, followed by analysis of gene expression signatures associated with either Th17 or Tregs. Compared with RNA isolated from control areas, we found significantly higher expression of both Foxp3 and Il17a genes in the CD4+ T-cell clusters (figure 4A). These observations were further confirmed by immunofluorescence staining, which showed IL-17 and CD4 marker colocalization in CD4+ clusters in the lungs of mice treated with MB49-S1pr1high TCM for 5 days (figure 4B). Additionally, we have detected an increased accumulation of both Foxp3- and RORγt-positive CD4+ T cells in the analyzed areas of the lungs (online supplemental figure 4A, left panels). Interestingly, in the CD4-Stat3–/– mice there were very few Foxp3-positive CD4+ T cells, and RORγt was detectable only within CD4– cells (online supplemental figure 4A, right panels). Consistent with the requirement of STAT3 for Th17 cell polarization,27 we confirmed the absence of IL-17-producing Stat3–/– CD4+ T cells in an in vitro polarization experiment (online supplemental figure 4B).

IL-17 is known to indirectly induce myeloid cell migration through chemokines such as CXCL128 or cytokines like granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF),29 which promotes granulopoiesis and neutrophil mobilization from bone marrow. We next evaluated the expression of chemokines in lung regions strongly infiltrated by CD4+ T cells. We found that the expression of CXCL1, but not G-CSF or CXCL12, was significantly higher in comparison to control areas with little or no CD4+ T cells (online supplemental figure 5A). Furthermore, CXCL1 was the most prominent factor detected by a chemokine array performed on supernatants collected after overnight culture of CD4-enriched cell isolates from lungs of mice exposed to TCM (online supplemental figure 5B). CXCL1 secretion was also significantly reduced in media from Stat3–/– CD4+ T cells (online supplemental figure 5B). An in vitro migration assay showed that mouse bone marrow-derived macrophages responded to recombinant mouse IL-17 and migrated with efficiency similar to that induced by TCM (online supplemental figure 5C).

To further assess the involvement of IL-17 in pre-metastatic niche formation, we injected MB49 tumor cells into Il17a‒/‒ and WT mice. Consistent with our previous findings and other studies,30 31 Il17a‒/‒ mice developed smaller tumors with significantly reduced lung metastases (data not shown). We then analyzed the infiltration of myeloid cells in tumor-free areas of the lungs and found significantly fewer CD11b+ cells in Il17a‒/‒ mice (figure 4C), while there were no apparent differences in the infiltration of CD4+ T cells between these two groups of mice (figure 4D). Moreover, in the absence of tumor, TCM was able to induce myeloid cell lung infiltration in control mice but not in Il17a‒/‒ mice (figure 4E). To assess whether IL-17 is a critical factor responsible for pre-metastatic niche formation, we also performed an in vivo tumor cell lung colonization experiment by retro-orbital injection of MB49 cells into mice that had already received TCM. TCM increased the number of tumor nodules in the lungs of WT mice but not in Il17a‒/‒ mice (figure 4F, online supplemental figure 6).

Human CD4+ T cells amplify pre-metastatic niche-like fibroblast activation and myeloid recruitment through IL-17A

To extend the mouse in vivo findings to a human system and provide mechanistic evidence for CD4+ T cell-driven stromal pre-metastatic niche conditioning, we established a co-culture system consisting of human lung fibroblasts (MRC-5), healthy donor-derived CD4+ T cells and donor-matched myeloid cells, and TCM from human ovarian (SKOV3) and triple-negative breast (MDA-MB-231) cancer cell lines. Similar reductionist testing systems for pre-metastatic niche-like fibroblast activation and myeloid-recruiting factor production have been used to demonstrate how tumor-educated lung fibroblasts acquire pre-metastatic niche-like properties and support metastatic seeding.19 32–34 MRC-5 fibroblasts were cultured for 48 hours with or without TCM, in the presence or absence of CD4+ T cells separated by transwell insert, and the resulting co-culture-conditioned media (CCM) were used as chemoattractant for donor-matched myeloid cells in a transwell migration assay (figure 5A).

In monoculture, TCM treatment of MRC-5 fibroblasts induced an approximately threefold increase in CCM-driven myeloid migration over control medium, with comparable effects from SKOV3 and MDA-MB-231 TCM (figure 5B). Addition of CD4+ T cells to TCM-treated MRC-5 cultures nearly doubled myeloid migration, reaching 2.2-fold to 2.3-fold above TCM-treated MRC-5 monocultures (figure 5B). ELISA analysis of CCM showed an upregulation of the myeloid cell-recruiting chemokines IL-8 and CCL2 and the niche-initiating prostaglandin E2 (PGE2). TCM-treated MRC-5 alone produced elevated levels of these factors, and addition of CD4+ T cells further increased their secretion in each of three donors (figure 5C). Western blot analysis of MRC-5 lysates revealed a corresponding upregulation of the pre-metastatic niche-associated proteins fibronectin (FN1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), with the strongest induction observed in the presence of both TCM and CD4+ T cells (figure 5D).

To determine whether IL-17A is the main CD4+ T cell-derived signal driving this amplification, we repeated the co-culture experiments in the presence of an IL-17A neutralizing antibody or isotype control. In TCM-treated MRC-5 cultures without CD4+ T cells, IL-17A neutralization had only minor effects on myeloid migration and IL-8/CCL2/PGE2 secretion (online supplemental figure 7A,B). In the MRC-5+TCM + CD4+ T-cell condition, however, IL-17A neutralization markedly abrogated CD4+ T cell-induced myeloid migration (figure 5E), decreased IL-8, CCL2, and PGE2 secretion (figure 5F), and FN1 and COX-2 protein upregulation (online supplemental figure 7C). Isotype antibody controls had no significant effect on any readout.

STAT3-IL-17 is associated with myeloid cell infiltration in human pre-metastatic sites

To explore whether the STAT3-IL-17 axis is associated with myeloid cell accumulation at tumor-distant sites in human tissues, we used tumor-free lymph nodes from our previously characterized cohort of patients with high-risk prostate cancer. We had previously reported elevated p-STAT3 expression and prominent CD68+ myeloid infiltrates in these tumor-free lymph nodes compared with normal lymph nodes,11 suggesting these tissues could be used to study the link between STAT3 signaling and myeloid recruitment at tumor-distant sites. We therefore stratified the samples by p-STAT3 status (high vs low) and performed immunofluorescence staining for IL-17 and CD68. Lymph nodes with high p-STAT3 contained significantly more IL-17+ cells than low-p-STAT3 or normal lymph nodes (online supplemental figure 8A,B). Double staining for IL-17 and the monocyte/macrophage marker CD68 further showed that IL-17+ regions in high-p-STAT3 lymph nodes were also highly infiltrated by CD68+ cells (online supplemental figure 8C).

Postmortem tissue collection enables quick tissue acquisition from many different organ sites after the death of the patient, allowing for studies to be performed on tumor-distant tissues with or without metastasis.35 For patients with ovarian cancer, liver and lungs have been reported as the most common metastatic sites associated with worse overall survival.36 To explore whether features of CD4+ T-cell pre-metastatic niches observed in our mouse models are present in human cancers, we analyzed CD4+ T-cell infiltration in pathologist-confirmed tumor-free lung and liver tissues obtained from three patients with ovarian cancer through postmortem collection. CD4+ T-cell accumulation and p-STAT3-positive CD4+ T cells were detected in the indicated tumor-free tissues from all three patients (figure 6A,B). CD4+ clusters expressed the T-cell specific marker CD3 and co-localized with fibronectin (online supplemental figure 8D), paralleling the pattern observed in mouse pre-metastatic CD4+ T-cell clusters (online supplemental figure 1C).

We next examined myeloid cell infiltrates and CD4+ T-cell accumulation in tumor-free lung samples from patients with ovarian cancer. CD11b+-rich clusters were largely confined to regions with high CD4 expression (figure 6C). Consistent with our mouse data, IL-17A and CD4 co-localized in areas with high CD4+ cluster infiltration in patients with ovarian cancer lung and liver tissues, with IL-17A signal detected within and immediately adjacent to CD4+ T-cell clusters, consistent with local IL-17A production by CD4+ T cells (figure 6D). Together, the findings on the few (limited) patient samples are consistent with our findings in mouse studies and in vitro experiments.

Discussion

Understanding the cellular and molecular composition of the pre-metastatic niches is critical to effectively block pre-metastatic niche formation prior to overt tumor metastasis. Although myeloid cells are recognized as the main immune drivers of pre-metastatic niche formation,37 the roles of and mechanisms by which other immune cell types, including CD4+ T cells, form pre-metastatic niches remain to be further investigated. In the current study, we identify an important role of CD4+ T-cell clusters in early pre-metastatic niche formation. This phenotype reflects focal spatial clustering rather than global expansion of CD4+ T cells, consistent with prior reports showing that immunofluorescence/confocal microscopic analysis but not flow cytometry of the whole lung tissues could detect early CD4+ T-cell enrichment in pre-metastatic lungs.17 18 We therefore relied on immunofluorescence/confocal microscopy to detect the increase of CD4+ T cells at these early time points.

Our data show that STAT3 in T cells is required for CD4+ T-cell clustering at future metastatic sites, and that tumor-derived STAT3-activating factors, including IL-6, drive T-cell migration. Consistent with this, prior reports show that STAT3 in CD4+ T cells also inhibits Th1 differentiation,12 20 and that reduced Th1 favors pre-metastatic niche formation.38 Prior studies also showed that IL-6/STAT3 signaling in CD4+ T cells induces chemokine receptor expression and supports their recruitment to inflammatory sites.39 40 Extending these observations, we find that TCM upregulates CCR4 and CCR6 on naive CD4+ T cells in an IL-6-dependent manner, and that blocking IL-6 or CCR6 reduces TCM-induced CD4+ T-cell migration. Of note, IL-6 neutralization did not completely abrogate TCM-induced T-cell migration, indicating the involvement of additional tumor-derived factors in T-cell recruitment.

We demonstrate that T cell-specific Stat3 knockout or Il17a ablation abrogated pre-metastatic niche formation in mouse models. Our results also show that CD4+ T-cell clusters temporally precede CD11b+ cell cluster formation. In agreement with our findings, recent reports showed that T cells could accumulate in the pre-metastatic lung before myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs).17 18 Several reports also showed that immature MDSCs can be expanded and recruited by IL-17 during cancer progression and metastasis.28 31 41 Il17a–/– mice exhibit reduced CD11b+ accumulation in the lungs during infection.42 IL-17 can induce myeloid cell migration through chemokines such as CXCL1.28 Consistent with these observations, we show CXCL1 was elevated in lung regions heavily infiltrated by CD4+ T cells, with CD4+ T cells secreting CXCL1 in a STAT3-dependent manner. Our in vitro migration assays further confirmed that IL-17 can directly promote myeloid cell migration with efficiency comparable to TCM. Our human co-culture experiments demonstrate that CD4+ T cells amplify TCM-induced fibroblast pre-metastatic niche-like activation in an IL-17A-dependent manner, leading to increased secretion of myeloid-recruiting factors, IL-8 (the human functional homolog of mouse CXCL1), CCL2, and PGE2, and enhanced myeloid cell migration. In infectious disease models, IL-17A-stimulated fibroblasts similarly increased production of myeloid-attracting chemokines and promoted myeloid cell migration in an IL-17A-dependent manner.43 Together, these findings identify an IL-17A/stromal-conditioning mechanism that contributes to CD4+ T cell-mediated myeloid recruitment.

Our study shows that T cells accumulating at pre-metastatic sites in the lung are predominantly CD4+ cells, comprising RORγt+ Th17 and Foxp3+ Treg cells. RORγt+ non-CD4+ cells, indicative of γδ-T cells, were less abundant and not significantly affected by STAT3 ablation in T cells, suggesting they are unlikely to be the major source of IL-17 in our models. Supporting a role of Th17 in pre-metastatic niche formation, we found that TCM upregulated CCR4 and CCR6 in cultured, purified mouse CD4+ T cells. It has been shown that CCR4+CCR6+ phenotype is characteristic of Th17 cells,44 45 whereas CCR6+CCR4⁻ is typical of γδ-T cells.44 Although γδ-T cells may not be the major culprits in promoting myeloid cell pre-metastatic niches in our models, they have been shown to contribute to IL-17-driven metastasis in other contexts.46 In addition, how γδ-T cells and CD4+ Th17 cells may cooperate within pre-metastatic niches warrants further investigation. We also observed accumulation of Foxp3+ Treg cells within CD4+ T-cell clusters, consistent with prior characterization of lung Treg accumulation in pre-metastatic niches,17 18 and their specific contribution to the IL-17/stromal-conditioning axis deserves dedicated future studies.

In pre-metastatic niche biology, CD4+ T cells and IL-17 have been implicated in multiple organs through distinct mechanisms: CD4+ T cells contribute to bone metastasis by expressing RANKL,47 and Th17 cells promote colorectal liver metastasis through TWEAK secretion.48 Our work demonstrates that STAT3-regulated IL-17-producing CD4+ T cells contribute to myeloid cell-mediated pre-metastatic niche formation in the lung. Whether this STAT3/Th17 mechanism operates similarly in liver, bone marrow, or brain pre-metastatic sites deserves future studies.

We acknowledge certain limitations in our study. Our mouse experiments used multiple transplantable syngeneic models, which allowed us to establish the biological significance of our study; no genetically engineered mouse model was employed. Having a systematic, paired comparison of two independent transplant lines per cancer type would further strengthen the study. The in vivo cellular sources of IL-6 and other STAT3-activating factors were not tested by tumor-cell-specific knockouts, and contributions from tumor-associated stromal cells in vivo were not assessed. Furthermore, dissecting Th17 vs Treg contributions and excluding γδ-T cells were not performed. Additionally, based on several publications on myeloid cells in the pre-metastatic niche,11 49 we used CD11b+Gr-1+ staining as a general immature myeloid marker, instead of separating polymorphonuclear (PMN)-MDSC from monocytic (M)-MDSC. Finally, we show CD4+ T-cell clusters, p-STAT3, IL-17, and myeloid infiltrates in prostate cancer lymph nodes and postmortem ovarian cancer lung and liver tissues. However, the small number of cases and absence of tumor genetic data, such as PTEN status in prostate cancer, preclude causal and definitive conclusions.

Despite these limitations, our study shows that tumor cell-secreted factors can induce CD4+ T cells to form pre-metastatic niches through the STAT3-IL-17 axis, contributing to myeloid cell cluster formation, in part through amplifying fibroblast pre-metastatic niche-like activation induced by tumor-secreted factors. Loss of STAT3 or IL-17 in CD4+ T cells reduces T cell and myeloid pre-metastatic niches and tumor metastasis.

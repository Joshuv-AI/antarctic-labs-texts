Closed vacuum static spaces with closed conformal vector fields
Jian Ye

Abstract
. This article aims to classify closed vacuum static spaces with a non-Killing closed conformal vector field. We firstly provide several characterizations of the conditions under which the first derivative of the warping function fulfills the vacuum static equation. Then we establish an identity involving the characteristic function of a conformal vector field on a Riemannian manifold. As applications, we derive a rigidity theorem on closed Riemannian manifolds with a non-Killing closed conformal vector field under suitable conditions and classify closed vacuum static spaces admitting such a vector field.

1.
Introduction

The study of conformal vector fields and vacuum static spaces has garnered significant attention in recent years because of their profound implications in geometry and physics. Conformal vector fields are important concept in both Riemannian and pseudo-Riemannian geometry. Meanwhile, vacuum static spaces are crucial objects that naturally arise in the context of both Riemannian geometry and general relativity. This paper aims to explore the interplay between these two concepts, shedding light on their mutual influences.

1.1.
Vacuum static spaces

To begin with, we recall the definition of vacuum static spaces. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a smooth Riemannian manifold with dimension
n\geq 3.
Given a nonconstant smooth function
f
defined on
M,
we say that the triple
(M^{n},g,f)
is a vacuum static space if the metric
g
and
f
satisfy

(1)

\text{Hess}_{g}f-\Delta_{g}fg=f\text{Ric}_{g},

where
\text{Hess}_{g}f,\Delta_{g},\text{Ric}_{g}
respectively denote the Hessian of
f,
the Beltrami-Laplacian, the Ricci curvature tensor of the metric
g
. The equation (
1
) is called the vacuum static equation.

We now examine the foundational characterizations of vacuum static spaces. First of all, in
[
25
]
, Fischer and Marsden established that the
L^{2}-
formal adjoint of the linearization of the scalar curvature is given by

\mathcal{L}_{g}^{*}(f)=\text{Hess}_{g}f-\Delta_{g}fg-f\operatorname{Ric}_{g}.

In particular, the triple
(M^{n},g,f)
is a vacuum static space if and only if
f
lies in the kernel of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{g}^{*}.
Secondly, it is well-known that
(M^{n},g)
with a positive function
f
constitutes a vacuum static space if and only if the Lorentzian manifold
(M^{n}\times\mathbb{R},g-f^{2}dt^{2})
satisfies the Einstein equation for a vacuum spacetime (cf.
[
12
, Proposition 2.7]
). For more information, one can refer to
[
37
]

[
4
]

[
12
]
and references therein. Therefore, it is crucial to explore and classify vacuum static spaces.

Until now, numerous examples of vacuum static spaces have been discovered. Firstly, space forms serve as typical examples of vacuum static spaces. Additional nontrivial locally conformally flat examples were constructed by Kobayashi
[
36
]
and Lafontaine
[
38
]
. In this article, we present intriguing examples of warped product vacuum static spaces with nonzero Cotton tensor(cf. Example
1
). These examples are not locally conformally flat and thus were not addressed in
[
36
]
or
[
38
]
.

The existence of nonconstant smooth solution to (
1
) imposes strong restrictions on the background metric
g.
For instance, the scalar curvature of complete vacuum static space is constant
[

]
or

and the level set

is a totally geodesic regular hypersurface
[

1.2.
Conformal vector fields

Before proceeding, we review conformal vector fields on a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g).
As an extension of Killing vector fields, conformal vector fields have been extensively researched in both Riemannian and pseudo-Riemannian geometry. As vacuum static spaces, the presence of a non-Killing conformal vector field imposes stringent constraints on the underlying Riemannian manifold. Numerous studies indicate that Riemannian manifolds admitting such a vector field are quite rare. For instance, as shown in
[
53
]
, the negativity of the Ricci curvature of a manifold rules out the existence of any conformal vector field on any closed Riemannian manifold. On the other hand, it is known that any compact Einstein manifold with dimension at least three admits a non-Killing conformal vector field if and only if it is isometric to a round sphere(cf.
[
44
]
). Consequently, any conformal vector field on the real projective space of dimension at least three is Killing.

A long-standing folklore conjecture regarding the presence of conformal vector fields has been widely discussed: the Euclidean sphere is believed to be the unique closed manifold with constant scalar curvature that supports a non-Killing conformal vector field(see
[
22
]
).
However, this conjecture does not hold in general. In fact, in
[
22
]
, Ejiri constructed a counterexample by identifying a positive function
h
on the unit circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}(1)
such that the warped product
\mathbb{S}^{1}(1)\times_{h}N
, where
N
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold with positive constant scalar curvature, supports a non-Killing closed conformal vector field. In
[
19
, Remark 2]
, an explicit counterexample is provided by
\mathbb{S}^{1}(1)\times_{h}\mathbb{S}^{3}(1),
where
h=\sqrt{2+\sin t}
.

Although the aforementioned conjecture does not hold in general, it has been proven in various forms under additional assumptions. Specifically, Nagano
[
43
]
established the conjecture when the Ricci tensor is parallel. In
[
7
]
, Bishop and Goldberg showed that the conjecture was true in two-dimensional settings. Tanno and Weber
[
50
, Theorem 1]
established the conjecture when the conformal vector field was closed and vanished at some point. When the conformal vector field is a gradient, the conjecture holds true(cf.
[
55
]
or
[
51
, Theorem 4.4]
). Additionally, when the norm of the Ricci curvature is constant, the conjecture is also valid(see
[
41
]
or
[
52
, Theorem 2]
). In particular, any conformal vector field on the product
\mathbb{S}^{k}(r_{1})\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}(r_{2})
(
k\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
) is Killing. We refer the readers to
[
7
]
[
13
]

[
14
]
[
15
]
[
16
]
[
50
]
[
51
]
[
54
]
[
55
]
[
43
]
and references therein for more results on this subject.

Therefore, a classification of Riemannian manifold admitting a non-Killing conformal vector field is a challenge. In this article, we consider some special cases. Namely, we focus on classifying closed vacuum static spaces that carry a non-Killing closed conformal vector field.

1.3.
The connection between vacuum static spaces and conformal vector fields

Substantial evidences suggest a profound connection between conformal vector fields and vacuum static spaces. On one hand, certain vacuum static spaces naturally encode non-Killing conformal vector fields. For example, the space forms carry non-Killing closed conformal vector fields. In addition to space forms, let
N
be an
(n-1)-
dimensional closed Einstein manifold with Einstein constant
n,
the warped product
\mathbb{S}^{1}(\sqrt{1/n})\times_{h}N
is a vacuum static space with scalar curvature
n(n-1)
which admits a non-Killing closed conformal vector field
h\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

On the other hand, the characteristic function(for its definition, refer to Subsection 2.2 below) associated with a conformal vector field serves as a solution to the vacuum static equation on Riemannian manifolds with certain geometric conditions. For instance, on a Riemannian manifold with harmonic curvature, the characteristic function of a closed conformal vector field fulfills vacuum static equation(see the proof of Proposition D4 in
[
38
]
). Based on this observation, in
[
38
, Proposition D4]
, Lafontaine proved that each closed manifold with scalar curvature
n(n-1)
and harmonic curvature, which admits a non-Killing closed conformal vector field, is isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{n}(1)
or a finite quotient of
\mathbb{S}^{1}(\sqrt{1/n})

\times_{h}

N.

Meanwhile, on an Einstein manifold, the characteristic function also satisfies vacuum static equation(cf.
[
27
, Lemma 2.2]
). For the variational characterization of the characteristic function being a solution to vacuum static equation, one may refer to
[
42
, Theorem 1.1]
. In addition, on a Riemannian manifold with constant scalar curvature, the characteristic function of a conformal vector field also satisfies (
17
).

Motivated by the foregoing studies, for a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with constant scalar curvature which admits a non-homothetic conformal vector field
\xi
on
(M^{n},g)
with the characteristic function
f,
two questions come up naturally now:

1.

When
f
fulfills vacuum static equation?

2.

What are vacuum static spaces that carry a non-Killing conformal vector field?

In this article, regarding the first question, based on an identity involving
f,
we will provide a necessary and sufficient condition for
f
to satisfy the vacuum static equation. As for the second question, as an application of the identity, we will give a classification of closed vacuum static spaces with a non-Killing closed conformal vector field.

1.4.
Main results

We are now in a position to state our main results. It is well-known that warped product vacuum static spaces with a one-dimensional base were constructed by Kobayashi in
[
36
]
or Lafontaine in
[
38
]
. Particularly, if
(N,\bar{g})
is an Einstein manifold, it is known from
[
38
, Lemma C3]
that
\dot{h}
is automatically a solution to (
1
) on the warped product
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times_{h}N.

It is natural to explore the impact of
\dot{h}
being a solution to (
1
) on both
(N,\bar{g})
and the warped product
I\times_{h}N
. For instance, one might investigate whether one can deduce that
N
is Einstein from the condition that
\dot{h}
satisfies (
1
). To this end, we introduce the following fundamental identity:

Proposition 1
.

Let
h
be a positive function on an open interval
I
in
\mathbb{R}
and
N
be an
(n-1)-
dimensional Riemannian manifold. Suppose that the metric
g
of the warped product
I\times_{h}N
has constant scalar curvature and
\xi=h\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.
Then

\mathcal{L}_{g}^{\star}f(\cdot,\cdot)=-\rm{C}(\cdot,\xi,\cdot),

where
f=\dot{h}
and
\rm{C}
denotes the Cotton tensor of
I\times_{h}N.

As a straightforward consequence of Proposition
1
, we have

Corollary 1
.

Under the assumption of Proposition
1
, suppose that
n\geq 4,
if the set
\{\dot{h}\neq 0\}
is dense in
I\times_{h}N,
then the following five conditions are equivalent:

(i).

The function
\dot{h}
is a nonconstant smooth solution to
(
1
)
on
I\times_{h}N
;

(ii).

The
(0,2)-
tensor
\rm{C}(\cdot,\xi,\cdot)
vanishes identically;

(iii).

The Cotton tensor
\rm{C}
of
I\times_{h}N
vanishes identically;

(iv).

The warped product
I\times_{h}N
has harmonic curvature;

(v).

The Riemannian manifold
N
is Einstein.

Note that
h\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
is a conformal vector field on the warped product
I\times_{h}N
with characteristic function
\dot{h}
. Proposition
1
offers an identity involving
\dot{h}.
This suggests that a similar formula might hold for the characteristic function of a conformal vector field on general Riemannian manifolds.

Additionally, the following identity provides further insight. According to the sixth line from the bottom on page 69 in
[
38
]
, the characteristic function
f
of a closed conformal vector field on a Riemannian manifold with harmonic curvature satisfies the following identity:

\mathcal{L}_{g}^{\star}f(\cdot,\cdot)=0.

Our primary objective is to establish an analogous identity for the characteristic function of a conformal vector field on a general Riemannian manifold. More precisely, we have

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold and
\xi
be a conformal vector field on
(M,g)
with characteristic function
f.
Then, there exists a symmetric
(0,2)-
tensor
\rm{\Phi}
such that

\mathcal{L}_{g}^{\star}f(\cdot,\cdot)=\rm{\Phi}(\cdot,\cdot).

In particular, the function
f
satisfies
(\ref{vss})
at some point in
M
if and only if
\rm{\Phi}
vanishes at the same point.

For the definition of the tensor
\rm{\Phi},
one can refer to (
59
). It is worth noting that
\rm{\Phi}
depends not only on
\xi
but also on the Cotton tensor
\rm{C}.
Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, when the metric
g
has constant scalar curvature and
\xi
is a closed, the tensor
\rm{\Phi}
is reduced to
-\rm{C}(\cdot,\xi,\cdot)
. Moreover, if
(M,g)
has harmonic curvature,
\rm{\Phi}
vanishes identically. Consequently, the formula in Theorem
1
is indeed a generalization of the two aforementioned identities.

As an application of Theorem
1
, we establish a rigidity result for closed Riemannian manifolds that satisfy certain curvature conditions and admit a non-Killing closed conformal vector field. Specifically, we have

Theorem 2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
with scalar curvature
n(n-1).
Suppose that
\xi
is a non-Killing closed conformal vector field on
(M^{n},g)
satisfying

\rm{C}(\cdot,\xi,\cdot)=0,\text{on},\text{M}^{\text{n}}.

Then the following statements hold:

(1).

For
n=3,

(M^{3},g)
is isometric to either the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}(1)
or a finite quotient of the warped product
\mathbb{S}^{1}(\sqrt{1/3})\times_{h}\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{1/3}).

(2).

For
n=4,

(M^{4},g)
is isometric to either the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{4}(1)
or a finite quotient of the warped product
\mathbb{S}^{1}(1/2)\times_{h}\mathbb{S}^{3}(\sqrt{1/2}).

(3).

For
n\geq 5,

(M^{n},g)
is isometric to either
\mathbb{S}^{n}(1)
or a finite quotient of the warped product
\mathbb{S}^{1}(\sqrt{1/n})\times_{h}F
where
F
stands for an
(n-1)-
dimensional closed Einstein manifold with Einstein constant
n.

We now introduce the rigidity of closed vacuum static spaces with a non-Killing closed conformal vector field. It is worth pointing out that similar classifications have been carried out for other types of spaces. For instance, in
[
20
]
, the authors explored gradient Ricci solitons on complete Riemannian manifolds that admit a nonparallel closed conformal vector field. In
[
23
]
, the author examined the critical point equation metric admits a non-trivial closed conformal vector field. For classifications of
m
-quasi Einstein manifolds, one can refer to
[
46
]
, and for quasi-Einstein manifolds, see
[
24
]
.

As the another application of Theorem
1
, we have

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an
(n\geq 3)-
dimensional closed Riemannian manifold with scalar curvature
n(n-1)
that admits a non-Killing closed conformal vector field. Suppose that
f
is a nonconstant smooth solution to

(2)

\nabla^{2}f+\frac{{\rm{R}}}{n(n-1)}fg=(f+a){\rm E}+bg,

Here,
a,b
are two constants, and
{\rm R}
and
{\rm E}
denote the scalar curvature and the trace-free part of the Ricci curvature tensor respectively. Then
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to one of the spaces listed in Theorem
2
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
3
, it is interesting to note that
f+a
satisfies vacuum static equation when
b+\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)}a=0.

We briefly outline the proof strategy for Theorem
3
. The initial step is to establish that the quantity
(f+a){\rm C}
vanishes when the closed conformal vector field and the gradient of
f
are linearly dependent(see Proposition
5
). However, these two vector fields may not always be linearly dependent(cf. (3) in Remark
11
). This kind of phenomenon makes the classification subtle. To address this, we employ a proof by contradiction to demonstrate that the Cotton tensor of
(M^{n},g)
vanishes when
b+\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)}a\neq 0
. If
b+\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)}a=0
, the method of proof by contradiction remains valid for
n=3
. For
n\geq 4,
the analyticity deduces that the Cotton tensor equals zero. Ultimately, the desired rigidity result is achieved through Theorem
1
and
[
38
, Proposition D4]
or Theorem
2
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some fundamental concepts in Riemannian geometry and review several well-known results concerning conformal vector fields, vacuum static spaces, and related critical spaces.

2.1.
Notations and conventions

We start with the set-up of notations and reviewing some basic notions in Riemannian geometry. See
[
5
]

[
26
]

[
45
]
as references on Riemannian geometry.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
induced by the metric
g.
Denote by
TM
the tangent bundle of
M^{n}
and by
\Gamma(TM)
the space of smooth sections of
TM.
The Riemann curvature tensor
R:\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(TM)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM)
is defined by

R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,

for any smooth vector fields
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TM).

Throughout the article, the Einstein convention on summing over the repeated indices will be used. Given a local coordinate system
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right\}_{i=1}^{n},
the metric
g
can be locally represented as:

g=g_{ij}dx^{i}\otimes dx^{j}.

In the sequel, the inverse of the matrix
(g_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
is denoted by
(g^{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}.

The Riemannian metric
g
induces norms on all the tensor bundles. Precisely, the squared norm of a
(r,s)
-tensor field
\mathcal{U}
in the coordinate system
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
is given by

\|\mathcal{U}\|^{2}=g_{i_{1}k_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{s}k_{s}}g^{j_{1}l_{1}}\cdots g^{j_{r}l_{r}}\mathcal{U}_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}^{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}\mathcal{U}_{l_{1}\cdots l_{r}}^{k_{1}\cdots k_{s}},

where
\mathcal{U}_{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}}^{i_{1}\cdots i_{r}}
are components of
\mathcal{U}
in the coordinate system
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}.

We now write the Riemann curvature tensor in the coordinate system
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
. The components of the
(1,3)-
curvature tensor is given by

R_{ijk}^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{l}}={R}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}})\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}

and the associated
(0,4)-
version by

R_{ijkl}=g_{is}R_{jkl}^{s}.

We next review several classical curvature tensors. The Ricci curvature tensor
{\rm Ric}
of
g
is obtained through the contraction of the Riemann curvature tensor, which is expressed as

R_{ij}=g^{kl}R_{ikjl}.

The scalar curvature
R
is then defined as the contraction of the Ricci tensor, given by

\text{R}=g^{ij}R_{ij}.

The Schouten tensor
{\rm A}
of
g
is defined by

{\rm A}={\rm Ric}-\frac{{\rm R}}{2(n-1)}\,g.

The Weyl tensor
\rm{W}
is given by

W_{ijkl}=R_{ijkl}-\frac{1}{n-2}({\rm A}\owedge g)_{ijkl},

where the symbol
\owedge
represents the Kulkarni-Nomizu product which is defined for any two symmetric
(0,2)-
tensors
U
and
V
as follows

(U\owedge V)_{ijkl}=U_{ik}V_{jl}+U_{jl}V_{ik}-U_{il}V_{jk}-U_{jk}V_{il}.

Through the Schouten tensor, we can define the Cotton tensor
{\rm C}
as follows

(3)

\displaystyle C_{ijk}=A_{ij,k}-A_{ik,j}.

It is easy to check that

(4)

C_{ijk}+C_{ikj}=0~\text{and}~C_{ijk}+C_{jki}+C_{kij}=0,

and it is also trace-free in any two indices, i.e.

(5)

C_{iik}=0=C_{iji}.

As a straightforward consequence of the second equality in (
4
), we have

(6)

C_{kij}C_{ijk}=-\frac{1}{2}\|{\rm C}\|^{2}.

We now recall the left interior multiplication with respect to a vector field. Given a vector field
\xi\in\Gamma(TM)
and a
(0,s)-
tensor
\mathcal{T},
the left interior multiplication
i_{\xi}{\mathcal{T}}
is defined by

i_{\xi}{\mathcal{T}}(X_{1},\cdots,X_{s}):={\mathcal{T}}(\xi,X_{1},\cdots,X_{s}),~\forall X_{1},\cdots,X_{s}\in\Gamma(TM).

We conclude this subsection with the definition of Hessian and Laplace-Beltrami operator. Given a real
C^{2}
function
f
defined on
M,
the Hessian
\nabla^{2}f
of
f
is given by

(7)

\nabla^{2}f(X,Y)=X(Y(f))-\nabla_{X}Y(f),~\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(TM).

The Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
is defined to be

\Delta f=\text{tr}~\nabla^{2}f.

2.2.
Conformal vector fields

In this subsection, we review the definition of conformal vector fields and collect several well-known established results related to them. For more information, one can refer to
[
21
]

[
48
]

[
54
]
and references therein.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n.
A smooth vector field
\xi
on
M
is said to be conformal if there exists a smooth function
f:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

(8)

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g=2fg,

where
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
is the Lie derivative with respect to
\xi.
The function
f
is referred to as the characteristic function of
\xi.
When
f
is constant, then
\xi
is called a homothetic vector field. In particular, if
f
vanishes identically,
\xi
is said to be a Killing vector field. We say that
\xi
is non-Killing if
f
is not identically zero.

A conformal vector field
\xi
is closed if its dual
1-
form
\xi^{\flat}
is. The dual
1-
form
\xi^{\flat}
of
\xi
is defined by
\xi^{\flat}(X)=g(\xi,X)
for any vector field
X\in\Gamma(TM).
If a conformal vector field is the gradient of a smooth function, then it is called a gradient conformal vector field.

It is well-known that a vector field is conformal if and only if the flow generated by it consists of conformal transformations. In addition, the expression (
8
) is equivalent to

(9)

g(\nabla_{X}\xi,Y)+g(X,\nabla_{Y}\xi)=2fg(X,Y),

for any vector fields
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM).
By taking trace on both sides of (
9
), we immediately get

f=\frac{{{\rm div}\,\xi}}{n}.

We now compute the covariant derivative of a conformal vector field
\xi.
Define a skew symmetric tensor field
\mathcal{P}
on
M
by

(10)

2g(\mathcal{P}(X),Y)=d\xi^{\flat}(X,Y).

Making use of (

where

is a local orthonormal frame on

If

is closed, the above two expressions become

(14)

\displaystyle R(X,\xi)Y+Y(f)X-g(X,Y)\nabla f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(15)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}(\xi)+(n-1)\nabla f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

These two formulas will be used in the proof of Theorem

, and Theorem

. In addition, by

in Proposition 2 of

, it is well-known that the every zero point of nonzero conformal vector field is isolated on a completed connected Riemannian manifold. In the sequel, we will use this fact explicitly throughout the paper.

We conclude this subsection by providing a well-known classification of closed conformal vector fields based on their covariant derivatives.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\xi
be a conformal vector field on a Riemannian manifold
(M,g).
Then
\xi
is closed if and only if there exists a function

such that, for any vector field

it holds that

(16)

\nabla_{X}\xi=fX.

Proof.

If
\xi
is closed, the desired formula follows from (
11

such that (

) holds. As a result, the formula (

is a conformal vector field. The closedness of

∎

2.3.
Vacuum static spaces and related critical spaces

In this subsection, we collect several facts on vacuum static spaces and related critical spaces for the subsequent study. Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a vacuum static space. By taking the trace in both sides of (
1

(17)

\Delta f+\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n-1}f=0.

Substitute (

f_{ij}+\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)}fg_{ij}=fE_{ij},

Without loss of generality, we next consider the equation (

As

is constant when

Associated to (

which can be written as

\displaystyle T_{ijk}=

\displaystyle\frac{n-1}{n-2}\left(E_{ik}f_{j}-E_{ij}f_{k}\right)+\frac{1}{n-2}\left(g_{ik}E_{jl}-g_{ij}E_{kl}\right)f_{l}.

A simple observation reveals that the tensor

shares the same symmetries as the Cotton tensor. In fact, by definition of

there holds

Besides, one can check directly that

is trace-free in any two indices, i.e.

T_{iik}=0=T_{iji}.

We close this section by stating three basic lemmas.

Lemma 2
.

Let

be a smooth solution to
(

)
defined on an

dimensional Riemannian manifold. Then the following identities hold true:

(23)

\displaystyle R_{ijk}^{l}f_{l}=\big(E_{ij}-\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)}g_{ij}\big)f_{k}-\big(E_{ik}-\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)}g_{ik}\big)f_{j}+(f+a)C_{ijk},

(24)

\displaystyle(f+a)C_{ijk}=(i_{\nabla f}W)_{ijk}+T_{ijk}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
f
be a smooth solution to
(
2
)
. Then

E_{ik}T_{ijk}f_{j}=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}\|{\rm T}\|^{2}.

Lemma 4
.

Let
f
be a nonconstant smooth solution to
(
2
)
defined on an
(n\geq 3)-
dimensional closed Riemannian manifold. Then the scalar curvature
{\rm R}
is positive.

Proof.

Let

We rewrite (

) in terms of

(25)

\Delta u+\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n-1}u=n\kappa,

where

By multiplying both sides of (

the integration by parts gives rise to

\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n-1}\int_{M}u^{2}-n\kappa\int_{M}u=\int_{M}\|\nabla f\|^{2}.

On the other hand, by integrating both sides of (

we have

(26)

where the symbol

stands for the volume of

Combine the previous two formulas to deduce that

3.
Warped product vacuum static spaces

In this section, we explore warped product vacuum static spaces with a one-dimensional base. It is well-known that such spaces were constructed by Kobayashi in

or Lafontaine in

Let

We now state Kobayashi or Lafontaine’s construction. If

4.1.
Main identity

We start by reviewing some notions from Riemannian geometry. Let

\xi\in\Gamma(TM).
We denote
\xi

\xi^{\flat}

(M^{n},g)

In particular, if
\xi

i_{\xi}{\rm{C}}=\frac{1}{2(n-1)}d{\rm{R}}\wedge\xi^{\flat}.

Proof.

Let
f
be the characteristic function of
\xi.
Let
p\in M
and
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
be normal coordinate system centered at
p.
We firstly rewrite (
11
) and (
12
) locally as

(53)

\xi^{\flat}_{i,j}=fg_{ij}+\mathcal{P}_{ij}

and

(54)

R_{ijk}^{l}\xi_{l}^{\flat}=g_{ij}f_{k}-g_{ik}f_{j}-\mathcal{P}_{jk,i}.

By taking covariant derivative on both sides of (
54
) in the direction of
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}},
together with (
53
), we can deduce that

(55)

R_{ijk,i}^{l}\xi_{l}^{\flat}=-\mathcal{P}_{jk,ii}-R_{ijk}^{l}\mathcal{P}_{li}.

For the first term in left hand side of (
55
), with the help of the second Bianchi identity of the Riemann curvature tensor, we find

\displaystyle R_{ijk,i}^{l}\xi_{l}^{\flat}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(R_{iki,j}^{l}+R_{iij,k}^{l})\xi_{l}^{\flat}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(R_{lj,k}-R_{lk,j})\xi^{l}.

Taking into account the definition of the Cotton tensor (
3
), it can be inferred that

(56)

\displaystyle R_{ijk,i}^{l}\xi_{l}^{\flat}=C_{ljk}\xi^{l}+\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\big(\nabla_{k}{\rm{R}}~\xi_{j}^{\flat}-\nabla_{j}{\rm{R}}~\xi_{k}^{\flat}\big).

We now address the two terms in right hand side of (
55
). To do that, by virtue of the fact that the two form
d\xi^{\flat}
is closed, we have

\mathcal{P}_{jk,i}+\mathcal{P}_{ki,j}+\mathcal{P}_{ij,k}=0.

As a result, a simple computation gives rise to

\mathcal{P}_{jk,ii}=\mathcal{P}_{ik,ji}-\mathcal{P}_{ij,ki}.

Making use of Ricci’s identity, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{jk,ii}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{ik,ij}+R_{jl}\mathcal{P}_{lk}+R^{l}_{kji}\mathcal{P}_{il}-\mathcal{P}_{ij,ik}-R_{kl}\mathcal{P}_{lj}-R^{l}_{jki}\mathcal{P}_{il}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{ik,ij}-\mathcal{P}_{ij,ik}+R_{jl}\mathcal{P}_{lk}-R_{kl}\mathcal{P}_{lj}-(R^{l}_{kji}+R^{l}_{jik})\mathcal{P}_{li}.

The first Bianchi identity for Riemann curvature tensor leads to

(57)

-\mathcal{P}_{jk,ii}-R_{ijk}^{l}\mathcal{P}_{li}=\mathcal{P}_{ij,ik}-\mathcal{P}_{ik,ij}+R_{kl}\mathcal{P}_{lj}+\mathcal{P}_{kl}R_{lj}

Plugging (
56
) and (
57
) into (
55
), it follows that

\displaystyle C_{ljk}\xi^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\big({\nabla_{j}{\rm{R}}~\xi_{k}^{\flat}-\nabla_{k}\rm{R}}~\xi_{j}^{\flat}\big)+\mathcal{P}_{ij,ik}-\mathcal{P}_{ik,ij}

\displaystyle+R_{kl}\mathcal{P}_{lj}+\mathcal{P}_{kl}R_{lj}.

This completes the proof of Proposition
4
.
∎

It is interesting to point out that the
(0,2)-
tensor
(R_{kl}\mathcal{P}_{lj}+\mathcal{P}_{kl}R_{lj})
is skew-symmetric due to the symmetry of the Ricci curvature tensor and the skew-symmetry of
\mathcal{P}_{jk}.
In addition, under the assumptions of Proposition
4
, if the metric
g
has constant scalar curvature, then
i_{\xi}{\rm{C}}=0
. Thus Proposition
4
is indeed a generalization of Proposition
2
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1

Proof of Theorem
1
.
Let
f
be characteristic function of a conformal vector field
\xi
on a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
of dimension

(58)

\displaystyle f_{ik}-\Delta_{g}fg_{ik}-fR_{ik}=R_{ijk,j}^{l}\xi_{l}^{\flat}+R_{ijk}^{l}\mathcal{P}_{lj}+\mathcal{P}_{jk,ij}.

Repeat the procedure in the proof of Proposition

\displaystyle f_{ik}-\Delta_{g}fg_{ik}-fR_{ik}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{jkli,j}\xi^{l}+R_{ijk}^{l}\mathcal{P}_{lj}+\mathcal{P}_{jk,ji}+R_{il}\mathcal{P}_{lk}+R^{l}_{kij}\mathcal{P}_{jl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(R_{jkij,l}+R_{jkjl,i})\xi^{l}+R_{ijk}^{l}\mathcal{P}_{lj}+\mathcal{P}_{jk,ji}+R_{il}\mathcal{P}_{lk}+R^{l}_{kij}\mathcal{P}_{jl}

Theorem 4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an
(n\geq 3)-
dimensional closed Riemannian manifold with scalar curvature

that admits a non-Killing closed conformal vector field. Suppose that
f
is a nonconstant smooth solution to (

) on
(M^{n},g)
with

(68)

is isometric to one of the spaces listed in Theorem

.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be characteristic function of a non-Killing closed conformal vector field
\xi
on
(M^{n},g)
. Let
\kappa:=b+\frac{{\rm R}}{n(n-1)}a\neq 0.
Thus
\kappa
can be either positive or negative. Note that if the triple
(f,a,b)
satisfies (

(-f,-a,-b)
also fulfills (

\kappa>0.
With the aid of Lemma
4
, we know that the scalar curvature
{\rm R}>0.
Combining this with (
26
) and the positivity of
\kappa,
we can deduce that the set
\{f+a>0\}
is nonempty. The proof is divided into the following three steps.

Step 1.
In the first step, if the level set
f^{-1}(-a)
is empty, we prove that
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to a round sphere with radius
\sqrt{\frac{n(n-1)}{\operatorname{R}}}.

Assume that
f^{-1}(-a)
is empty. Thus the quantity
f+a
does not change sign. Without loss of generality, we suppose that
f+a

By integrating both sides of the divergence formula

\text{div}({\rm E}(\nabla f))=(f+a)\|{\rm E}\|^{2}

\int_{M}(f+a)\|{\rm E}\|^{2}=0.

Therefore,
\|{\rm E}\|^{2}=0
on

Hence, the equation (

\nabla^{2}f+\frac{{\rm{R}}}{n(n-1)}fg=bg.

Equivalently,

\nabla^{2}\tilde{f}+\frac{{\rm{R}}}{n(n-1)}\tilde{f}g=0,

where
\tilde{f}:=f-\frac{n(n-1)}{{\rm{R}}}b.
The desired result is obtained by combining this with Obata’s theorem
[

.

Step 2.
In the second step, if
f^{-1}(-a)
is nonempty, we show that the level set

To see this, we rewrite (

) in terms of

\nabla^{2}f=(f+a)\left({\rm E}-\frac{{\rm R}}{n(n-1)}g\right)+\kappa g.

Together with the positivity of

we conclude that each critical point of

on

is non-degenerate. Therefore, the level set
f^{-1}(-a)
has measure zero.

Step 3.
In third step, we verify that the Cotton tensor vanishes identically.

To see this, thanks to the fact that the level set
f^{-1}(-a)
has measure zero, it suffices to show that the quantity
(f+a){\rm C}
is equal to zero on
M.
We will argue by contradiction to show that
(f+a){\rm C}\equiv 0
. Suppose that

\Omega
of

Thus, by (

it is clear that

at each point in
\Omega.
By means of Proposition
5

\xi
and
\nabla f
are linearly independent on
\Omega\backslash\mathcal{Z}
where
\mathcal{Z}=\{\xi=0\}.

We next utilize Proposition
5
and Lemma
12

(1)
in Lemma
12
, the closedness of
\xi,
the item
(i)
in Lemma

61

(\rho-\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)})(f_{j}\xi_{k}^{\flat}-f_{k}\xi_{j}^{\flat})=0,\text{~in~}M\backslash\mathcal{Z}.

The fact that
\xi
and
\nabla f
are linearly independent on
\Omega\backslash\mathcal{Z}
yields

As a result, making use of (

Secondly, in view of the item
(2)
in Lemma

, the closedness of
\xi,
(

\xi(f)E_{ik}=(f+a)C_{ijk}\xi^{j},\text{~in~}\Omega\backslash\mathcal{Z}.

We now deal with the term

Based on (

\varphi E_{ik}=C_{ijk}\xi^{j},\text{~in~}\Omega\backslash\mathcal{Z}.

Combine previous two formulas to imply

Observe that

By taking covariant derivative on both sides of preceding formula in the direction of
\xi,
together with (
16
), (
2
), and (
70
), a direct computation reveals that

\Big(b-\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)}f\Big)\|\xi\|^{2}=(f+a)\xi(\varphi),\text{~in~}\widetilde{\Omega}.

Combining this with (
61
) and (
69
), one has

\Big(b+\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)}a\Big)\|\xi\|^{2}=0,\text{~in~}\widetilde{\Omega}.

Taking advantage of the assumption (
68
), we find that
\xi
is zero at each point in
\Omega\backslash\mathcal{Z}.
This contradicts the fact that
\xi
is not zero in
\Omega\backslash\mathcal{Z}.
Eventually, the quantity
(f+a){\rm C}
is equal to zero on
M.
Thanks to
(2)
in Remark
9
, the triple
(M^{n},g,\varphi)
is a vacuum static space. Therefore, the desired rigidity result is obtained by Theorem
2
. This ends the proof of Theorem
4
.
∎

6.
Vacuum static spaces with closed conformal vector fields

On the basis of Theorem
1
, Proposition
5
, and Lemma
12
, we have

Theorem 5
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be an
(n\geq 3)-
dimensional closed vacuum static space with scalar curvature
n(n-1).
Then
(M^{n},g)
admits a non-Killing closed conformal vector field if and only if it is isometric to one of the spaces listed in Theorem
2
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be characteristic function of a non-Killing closed conformal vector field
\xi
on
(M,g)
. Similar to the proof of Theorem
2
, it is sufficient to demonstrate that
{\rm T}\equiv 0
on
M.

For
n=3,
by similar approach to the proof of Theorem
4
, one can derive the vanishing of
{\rm T}.
To see this, using the notation from the proof of Theorem
4
, we multiply both sides of (
5.2
) by
f
and
\xi^{i},
and summing up over
i
from
1
to
n,
thanks to (
70
), one has

0=\varphi fE_{ik}\xi^{i}=fC_{ijk}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}=T_{ijk}\xi^{i}\xi^{j},\text{~in~}\Omega\backslash\mathcal{Z},

where the second equality follows from (
24
) and the fact that the Weyl tensor equals zero when the dimension
n=3.
In conjunction with the definition of
{\rm T}
and (
70
), one can compute that

\|\xi\|^{2}E_{kl}f_{l}=0,\text{~in~}\Omega\backslash\mathcal{Z},

Consequently,

E_{kl}f_{l}=0,\text{~in~}\Omega\backslash\mathcal{Z}.

By taking covariant derivative on both sides of previous formula in the direction of
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}},
according to the relation
E_{kl,k}=\frac{n-2}{2n}\nabla_{l}{\rm R},
the constancy of scalar curvature, and (
18
), we have

f\|{\rm E}\|^{2}=0,\text{~in~}\Omega\backslash\mathcal{Z}.

Keeping the fact that the set
\{f\neq 0\}
is dense in
M
and the definition of
{\rm T}
in mind, we conclude that
{\rm T}\equiv 0
in
\Omega\backslash\mathcal{Z}.
This leads to a contradiction. Therefore, the
(0,3)-
tensor
{\rm T}
must be zero on the entire manifold
M^{n}.

For
n\geq 4,
we will exploit another method to show that
{\rm T}\equiv 0
on
M.
To do that, from the proof of Theorem
4
, one obtains

\|{\rm T}\|^{2}\|\nabla\varphi+\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)}\xi\|^{2}=0,\text{~in~}M.

If
\nabla\varphi+\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)}\xi\equiv 0
on
M,
a direct calculation indicates that

\nabla^{2}\varphi+\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)}\varphi g=0,~\text{on}~M.

In view of Obata’s theorem
[
44
, Theorem A]
, we deduce that
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to a round sphere with radius
\sqrt{\frac{n(n-1)}{\operatorname{R}}}.

If
\nabla\varphi+\frac{\operatorname{R}}{n(n-1)}\xi\neq 0
on an open subset
V\subset M
, it is easy to see that
{\rm T}\equiv 0
on
V.
By analyticity, we know that
{\rm T}\equiv 0
on
M.
The desired result is obtained as in the proof of Theorem
2
. This completes the proof of Theorem
5
.
∎

Remark 13
.

It is worth noting that
{\rm T}
may not equal zero on a completed connected vacuum static space with negative scalar curvature that carrying a non-Killing closed conformal vector field. For instance, let
(M^{n},g)
be the warped product in Example
{\rm 1}
, as mentioned at the end of section
{\rm 3}
, it is a vacuum static space with scalar curvature
-n(n-1)
that carrying a non-Killing closed conformal vector field
h\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.
However, its Ricci curvature tensor has three distinct eigenvalues. Therefore, the associated
(0,3)-
tensor
{\rm T}
is also non identically zero.

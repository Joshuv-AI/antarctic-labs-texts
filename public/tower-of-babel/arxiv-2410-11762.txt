On the capillary water waves with constant vorticity
Lizhe Wan

Abstract
. This article is devoted to the study of local well-posedness for deep water waves with constant vorticity in two space dimensions on the real line. The water waves can be paralinearized and written as a quasilinear dispersive system of equations. By using the energy estimate and the Strichartz estimate, we show that for s > 5 4 s>\frac{5}{4} , the gravity-capillary water wave system with constant vorticity is locally well-posed in ℋ s ​ ( ℝ ) \mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}) .

1.
Introduction

In this article, we consider the two-dimensional inviscid incompressible gravity-capillary water wave system with constant vorticity in infinite depth.
The water waves can be described using the incompressible Euler’s equations with free boundary conditions on the fluid surface.

We first briefly mention the free boundary Euler equations and the water waves with constant vorticity
\gamma
in the Zakharov-Craig-Sulem formulation.
Let the fluid domain at time
t
be denoted by
\Omega_{t}
, and the boundary at time
t
by
\Gamma_{t}
.
The boundary
\Gamma_{t}
in our setting is either asymptotically flat at infinity or periodic with zero mean.
The fluid velocity is denoted by
\mathbf{u}(t,x,y)=(u(t,x,y),v(t,x,y))
and the pressure is denoted by
p(t,x,y)
.
Then
(\mathbf{u},p)
solve the following incompressible Euler equations inside
\Omega_{t}
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}u_{t}+uu_{x}+vu_{y}=-p_{x}&\\
v_{t}+uv_{x}+vv_{y}=-p_{y}-g&\\
u_{x}+v_{y}=0&\\
\omega=v_{x}-u_{y}=\gamma.\end{array}\right.

On the free upper boundary
\Gamma_{t}
we have the kinematic boundary condition

\partial_{t}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\text{ is tangent to }\bigcup\Gamma_{t},

and also the dynamic boundary condition

p=-2\sigma{\bf H}\ \ \text{ on }\Gamma_{t}.

Here
{\bf H}
is the mean curvature of the boundary,
g\geq 0
is the gravitational constant and
\sigma>0
is the coefficient of the surface tension.

To rewrite the free boundary Euler system, one can use
\mathbf{u}=(\gamma y+\phi_{x},\phi_{y})
for some generalized velocity potential
\phi(t,x,y)
that is harmonic with appropriate decay at infinity or periodic with zero mean.
Then at each time
t
,
\phi(t,x,y)
solves the Laplace equation, and
\phi
inside
\Omega_{t}
is determined by its trace on the free boundary
\Gamma_{t}
.
Following the work of Zakharov
[
45
]
and Craig-Sulem
[
18
]
, we denote by
\eta
the height of the water surface as a function of time and the horizontal coordinate, and
\psi(t,x)=\phi(t,x,\eta(t,x))\in\mathbb{R}
to be the trace of the generalized velocity potential
\phi
on the boundary.
The fluid dynamics can be expressed in terms of a one-dimensional evolution of the pair of unknowns

(\eta,\psi)
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\eta-G(\eta)\psi=\gamma\eta\eta_{x}\\
&\partial_{t}\psi+g\eta+\frac{1}{2}\psi_{x}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\eta_{x}\psi_{x}+G(\eta)\psi)^{2}}{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}=\sigma\left(\frac{\eta_{x}}{\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}}\right)_{x}+\gamma\eta\psi_{x}+\gamma\partial_{x}^{-1}G(\eta)\psi,\end{aligned}\right.

where
G(\eta)
is the Dirichlet-Neumann operator

G(\eta)\psi:=(-\phi_{x}\eta_{x}+\phi_{y})|_{y=\eta(x)}.

The first equation of the system is the equation for the kinematic boundary condition, and the second equation is the equation for the dynamic boundary condition.
This is the Zakharov-Craig-Sulem formulation of the water waves.

1.1.
Water waves in holomorphic coordinates

In this article, we will not use the Zakharov-Craig-Sulem formulation, instead, we will work in the holomorphic coordinates introduced by Hunter-Ifrim-Tataru
[
23
]
.
The holomorphic coordinates are widely used to study a variety of water waves problems, see for example Ifrim-Tataru and Ai-Ifrim-Tataru
[
23
,
25
,
22
,
5
,
24
,
4
,
26
,
27
]
.

As seen in the water wave system below, we no longer need to analyze the Dirichlet-Neumann operator in the holomorphic coordinates.
Let
\mathbf{P}
be the projection onto negative frequencies, namely

\mathbf{P}:=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{I}-iH),

with
H
being the Hilbert transform.
Holomorphic functions are defined to be the functions whose Fourier transforms are supported on
(-\infty,0]
.
In other words, they satisfy the relation
{\mathbf{P}}f=f
.
Similarly, we define
\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}
to be the projection onto positive frequencies:

\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}:=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{I}+iH)=\mathbf{I}-{\mathbf{P}}.

Functions such that
\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}f=f
are called anti-holomorphic functions.
Anti-holomorphic functions are complex conjugates of holomorphic functions.

The water waves can be formulated in the class of holomorphic functions using the pair of unknowns
(W(t,\alpha),Q(t,\alpha))
.

W
represents the holomorphic position and
Q
represents the holomorphic velocity potential.
In the rest of this article, we will use
\alpha
to denote the holomorphic spacial variable.
The derivation of the capillary water wave equations in holomorphic coordinates can be found in Ifrim-Tataru
[
26
]
and Appendix B of Ifrim-Tataru
[
27
]
.
The system is given by

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}W_{t}+(W_{\alpha}+1)\underline{F}+i\dfrac{\gamma}{2}W=0&\\
Q_{t}-igW+\underline{F}Q_{\alpha}+i\gamma Q+\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{|Q_{\alpha}|^{2}}{J}\right]-i\dfrac{\gamma}{2}T_{1}-2\sigma\mathbf{P}\Im\left[\dfrac{W_{\alpha\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+W_{\alpha})}\right]=0,&\end{array}\right.

where
J:=|1+W_{\alpha}|^{2}
is the Jacobian, and

\displaystyle F:=\mathbf{P}\left[\frac{Q_{\alpha}-\bar{Q}_{\alpha}}{J}\right],\quad

\displaystyle F_{1}=\mathbf{P}\left[\frac{W}{1+\bar{W}_{\alpha}}+\frac{\bar{W}}{1+W_{\alpha}}\right],

\displaystyle\underline{F}:=F-i\frac{\gamma}{2}F_{1},\quad

\displaystyle T_{1}:=\mathbf{P}\left[\frac{W\bar{Q}_{\alpha}}{1+\bar{W}_{\alpha}}-\frac{\bar{W}Q_{\alpha}}{1+W_{\alpha}}\right].

The system (
1.1
) has a conserved energy

\mathcal{E}(W,Q)=\Re\int Q\bar{Q}_{\alpha}+2\sigma(J^{\frac{1}{2}}-1-\Re W_{\alpha})+g|W|^{2}(1+W_{\alpha})+\gamma Q_{\alpha}(\Im W)^{2}-\frac{\gamma^{3}}{2i}|W|^{2}W(1+W_{\alpha})\,d\alpha.

It also has a conserved horizontal momentum

\mathcal{P}(W,Q)=\int-i(Q\bar{W}_{\alpha}-\bar{Q}W_{\alpha})-\gamma|W|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}(W^{2}\bar{W}_{\alpha}+\bar{W}^{2}W_{\alpha})\,d\alpha.

A simplified model of (
1.1
) is its linearization around the zero solution

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}w_{t}+q_{\alpha}=0&\\
q_{t}+i\gamma q-igw+i\sigma w_{\alpha\alpha}=0,&\end{array}\right.

restricted to holomorphic functions.
By eliminating the linearized unknown
q
, (
1.2
) can be written as a linear dispersive equation pf
w
:

w_{tt}+i\gamma w_{t}+igw_{\alpha}-i\sigma w_{\alpha\alpha\alpha}=0.

Its dispersion relation is

\tau^{2}+\gamma\tau+g\xi+\sigma\xi^{3}=0,\quad\xi\leq 0.

A conserved energy of the linear system (
1.2
) is given by

\mathcal{E}_{0}(w,q)=\int\sigma|w_{\alpha}|^{2}-\frac{i}{2}(q\bar{q}_{\alpha}-\bar{q}q_{\alpha})\,d\alpha=\sigma\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+\|q\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}.

This conserved energy suggests the functional framework to study (
1.1
).
The system (
1.2
) is well-posed in
\dot{H}^{1}\times\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
space.
To measure the regularity of the solution we will use the product Sobolev space
\mathcal{H}^{s}
endowed with the norm

\|(w,q)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}:=\|(w,q)\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{s}},\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

We will also use the product Hölder space
\mathcal{W}^{r}
endowed with the norm

\|(w,q)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{r}}:=\|(w,q)\|_{W^{r+\frac{1}{2},\infty}\times W^{r,\infty}},\quad r\in\mathbb{R}.

The system (
1.1
) is a nonlinear degenerate hyperbolic system.
By differentiation, it can be diagonalized and converted into a quasilinear system.
Following the setup in
[
26
]
, we work with holomorphic functions

({\mathbf{W}},R):=\left(W_{\alpha},\frac{Q_{\alpha}}{1+W_{\alpha}}\right).

The holomorphic function
R
above has an intrinsic meaning; it is the complex velocity restricted on the water surface.
We also need to define three auxiliary functions.
The first one
{\underline{a}}
is the
frequency-shift
, and it is given by

{\underline{a}}:=a+\frac{\gamma}{2}a_{1},\quad a:=i(\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}[\bar{R}R_{\alpha}]-{\mathbf{P}}[R\bar{R}_{\alpha}]),\quad a_{1}:=R+\bar{R}-N,

where

N:={\mathbf{P}}[W\bar{R}_{\alpha}-\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}R]+\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}[\bar{W}R_{\alpha}-{\mathbf{W}}\bar{R}].

The second auxiliary function we need is the
advection velocity

{\underline{b}}
,

{\underline{b}}:=b-i\frac{\gamma}{2}b_{1},\quad b:={\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{Q_{\alpha}}{J}\right]+\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}\left[\frac{\bar{Q}_{\alpha}}{J}\right],\quad b_{1}:={\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{W}{1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}\right]-\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}\left[\frac{\bar{W}}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}\right].

The third auxiliary function
\underline{M}
is given by

\displaystyle M:=\frac{R_{\alpha}}{1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}+\frac{\bar{R}_{\alpha}}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}-b_{\alpha}=\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}[\bar{R}Y_{\alpha}-R_{\alpha}\bar{Y}]+{\mathbf{P}}[R\bar{Y}_{\alpha}-\bar{R}_{\alpha}Y],

\displaystyle M_{1}:={\mathbf{W}}-\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}-b_{1,\alpha}={\mathbf{P}}[W\bar{Y}]_{\alpha}-\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}[\bar{W}Y]_{\alpha},\qquad\underline{M}:=M-i\frac{\gamma}{2}M_{1}.

Note that
{\underline{b}}_{\alpha}
satisfies the relation

(1.3)

{\underline{b}}_{\alpha}=\frac{R_{\alpha}}{1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}+\frac{\bar{R}_{\alpha}}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}-i\frac{\gamma}{2}({\mathbf{W}}-\bar{{\mathbf{W}}})-\underline{M}.

Here,
Y:=\dfrac{W_{\alpha}}{1+W_{\alpha}}
.
Then, using above notations, the pair
({\mathbf{W}},R)
solves the differentiated system

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{W}_{t}+\underline{b}\mathbf{W}_{\alpha}+\dfrac{(1+\mathbf{W})R_{\alpha}}{1+\bar{\mathbf{W}}}=(1+\mathbf{W})\underline{M}+i\dfrac{\gamma}{2}\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{W}-\bar{\mathbf{W}})\\
&R_{t}+\underline{b}R_{\alpha}+i\gamma R-i\dfrac{g\mathbf{W}-a}{1+\mathbf{W}}-\frac{2\sigma}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}{\mathbf{P}}\Im\left[\frac{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{J^{1/2}(1+{\mathbf{W}})}\right]_{\alpha}=i\dfrac{\gamma}{2}\dfrac{R\mathbf{W}+\bar{R}\mathbf{W}+N}{1+\mathbf{W}}.\end{aligned}\right.

In the following, we will mostly work with the system (
1.4
) and study its local well-posedness.

1.2.
Some previous results

The literature on the two-dimensional gravity-capillary water waves is numerous.
Here we only mention some of the results on the local well-posedness.

For the irrotational gravity-capillary water waves, Nalimov
[
34
]
and Yosihara
[
43
,
44
]
initiated the study of this problem for small smooth data.
There are many results since then, see for example the work of Agrawal
[
1
,
2
]
, Ambrose-Masmoudi
[
11
]
, Beyer-Gunther
[
15
]
, Coutand-Shkoller
[
17
]
, Lannes
[
29
]
, Ming-Zhang
[
33
]
, Mésognon-Gireau
[
31
]
and Shatah-Zeng
[
38
]
.
Let us mention that Alazard-Burq-Zuily
[
6
,
9
]
worked in Zakharov-Craig-Sulem formulation and used paradifferential calculus to reduce the water wave system to a single paradifferential equation.
As a result, they proved that 2D irrotational gravity-capillary water wave system is locally well-posed in
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
, for
s>\frac{3}{2}
.
De Poyferré and Nguyen refined the paradifferential reduction in
[
20
]
, and proved a Strichartz estimate
[
19
]
that leads to the local well-posedness in
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>\frac{27}{20}
.
Nguyen in
[
35
]
then used the paracomposition and the Strichartz estimate to show the local well-posedness for the 2D irrotational gravity-capillary water waves in
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>\frac{5}{4}
.
See also the work of Ai
[
3
]
for the Strichartz estimate and its application in the local well-posedness.

For the 2D gravity water wave problem posed in infinite depth and with constant vorticity, the first local well-posedness for large data, as well as cubic lifespan bounds for small data solutions were obtained by Ifrim and Tataru in
[
27
]
.
Shortly after, Bieri, Miao, Shahshahani and Wu
[
16
]
obtained a similar result for self-gravitating incompressible fluids with
constant vorticity for smooth initial data in bounded domains.

For the gravity-capillary water waves with non-trivial constant vorticity, to the best of our knowledge, the only well-posedness result is the recent work of
[
14
]
by Berti-Maspero-Murgante.
They showed almost global-in-time existence of small amplitude solutions of the 2D gravity-capillary water wave equations with constant vorticity in the periodic case.
However, their result requires high Sobolev regularity for initial data, and the parameter
\sigma
needs to avoid a measure zero set to ensure a non-resonance condition.
Apart from the general Cauchy problem, several types of special solutions of (
1.1
) are proved to exist, see the work of Groves-Wahlén
[
21
]
, Rowan-Wan
[
36
,
37
]
for solitary waves, Wahlén
[
39
]
, Martin
[
30
]
for the steady periodic waves and Berti-Franzoi-Maspero
[
13
]
for quasi-periodic travelling waves solution.
No well-posedness result of (
1.4
) on the real line is known before.

Lastly, the reference list of works pertaining to gravity water waves in 2D with trivial vorticity is exhaustive and by no means we intend is to cover it here. However, in this current work, we also get some inspiration from the very recent result of Ai, Ifrim and Tataru
[
5
]
; here we are mostly inspired by the pointwise bounds derived for the water waves associated quantities.

1.3.
The main results

We first define two control norms that will be used.
Let
\epsilon>0
be a small positive constant.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}:=\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{1+\epsilon}_{*}}+\|R\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\gamma\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{*}},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}:=\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\|R\|_{C_{*}^{1+\epsilon}}+\gamma\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C_{*}^{1+\epsilon}}+\gamma\|R\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Here the vorticity
\gamma
can be intuitively viewed as half a derivative as in the gravity water waves
[
27
,
41
]
.

The first main result is the a priori energy estimate for the water wave system (
1.4
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s>0
.
Suppose
({\mathbf{W}},R)
solve the water wave system (
1.4
) on
[0,T]
, then we have the energy estimate for any
t\in[0,T]
,

(1.5)

\frac{d}{dt}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}(1+{\mathcal{B}})\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}.

The proof of this energy estimate does not rely on the dispersive properties of the equations.
We remark that by using the energy estimate, the contraction estimate, and the Sobolev embedding for the control norms, one can show that in the periodic case, the water wave system is locally well-posed in
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
s>\frac{3}{2}
.
Compared to the result in
[
14
]
(where the authors are using the Zakharov-Craig-Sulem formulation), this is a low regularity well-posedness result and holds for all choices of parameters
\sigma>0,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

Note that the Sobolev embedding from
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
to
C_{*}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
exhibits a gap of
\frac{1}{2}
derivative.
On the real line, by exploiting the dispersive properties of the water wave system, one may hope to lower this gap.
Indeed, by using the Strichartz estimate proved in Nguyen
[
35
]
, one can lower the gap from
\frac{1}{2}
to
\frac{1}{4}
.
Our second main result is the Strichartz estimate for (
1.4
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
({\mathbf{W}},R)
is a solution of (
1.4
) on
I=[0,T]
with

({\mathbf{W}},R)\in C^{0}(I;\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{4}(I;\mathcal{W}^{r}(\mathbb{R})),\quad s>r>1.

Then for
\mu<\frac{1}{4}
,
({\mathbf{W}},R)\in L^{4}(I;\mathcal{W}^{s-\frac{1}{2}+\mu,\infty}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Using the energy estimate and the Strichartz estimate as the main ingredients, we can prove the local well-posedness for the water wave system (
1.4
) on the real line.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
s>\frac{5}{4}
, the water wave system (
1.4
) is locally well-posed in
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
.

Here by local well-posedness in
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, we need to prove

(1)

Existence of the solution: If the initial data
({\mathbf{W}}_{0},R_{0})\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, then there exists a time
T>0
such that (
1.4
) has a solution
({\mathbf{W}},R)\in C^{0}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
.

(2)

Continuous dependence of initial data: Consider a sequence of data
\{({\mathbf{W}}_{n,0},R_{n,0})\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
that converges to
({\mathbf{W}}_{0},R_{0})
in
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
.
Then for the corresponding solutions
\{({\mathbf{W}}_{n},R_{n})\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
({\mathbf{W}},R)
defined on time
[0,T]
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|({\mathbf{W}}_{n},R_{n})-({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{C^{0}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{s})}=0.

(3)

Uniqueness: The solution is unique for
s>\frac{3}{2}
.
When
\frac{5}{4}<s\leq\frac{3}{2}
, we don’t have direct uniqueness of the solution.
The solution is unique in the sense that it is the unique limit of the regular solution.

The rest of the article is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall some estimates in paradifferential calculus and paraproducts.
We then use these estimates to compute bounds for the auxiliary functions.
In Section
3
, we first paralinearize the water wave system and rewrite the system using Wahlén variables.
Then we symmetrize the system and write the water wave system as a nonlinear paradifferential dispersive equation of order
\frac{3}{2}
.
Next, in Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
. We first derive an a priori energy estimate for the water wave system (
1.4
), then we use the technique of global para-composition to reexpress the water wave system as a paradifferential equation of (
4.10
) type and derive the Strichartz estimate.
Finally, in Section
5
, we combine all the results in previous sections and sketch the proof of the local well-posedness of the water wave system.

Paradifferential estimates and water waves related bounds

In this section, we first list definitions of norms we will use in this article.
Our analysis relies heavily on the paradifferential calculus.
In the second part of this section, we recall some paradifferential estimates.
Many of these estimates are relatively standard.
They can be found in for instance
[
9
,
7
,
42
,
46
]
or the textbooks
[
12
,
32
]
.
In the third part, we compute some water waves related bounds for auxiliary functions.

2.1.
Norms and function spaces

To begin with, we recall the Littlewood-Paley frequency decomposition,

I=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}P_{k},

where for each
k\geq 1
,
P_{k}
is the smooth frequency projection that selects frequency
2^{k}
, and
P_{0}
selects the low frequency components
|\xi|\leq 1
.

(1)

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, the non-homogeneous Besov space
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as the space of all tempered distributions
u
satisfying

\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}:=\left\|(2^{ks}\|P_{k}u\|_{L^{p}})_{k=0}^{\infty}\right\|_{l^{q}}<+\infty.

(2)

When
p=q=\infty
, the Besov space
B^{s}_{\infty,\infty}
becomes the
Zygmund space

C^{s}_{*}
.
When
p=q=2
, the Besov space
B^{s}_{2,2}
becomes the
Sobolev space

H^{s}
.

(3)

Let
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
,
1\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq\infty
, then for any real number
s
,

B^{s}_{p_{1},r_{1}}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow B^{s-(\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{p_{2}})}_{p_{2},r_{2}}(\mathbb{R}).

As a special case when
p_{1}=r_{1}=2
and
p_{2}=r_{2}=\infty
,

(2.1)

H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C^{s}_{*}(\mathbb{R})\quad\forall s,

the Sobolev space
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
can be embedded into the Zygmund space
C^{s}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
.

(4)

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the space of all functions such that
\partial_{x}^{j}u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
0\leq j\leq k
.
For
\rho=k+\sigma
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
,
W^{\rho,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the space of all function
u\in W^{k,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\partial_{x}^{k}u
is
\sigma
- Hölder continuous on
\mathbb{R}
.

(5)

The Zygmund space
C^{s}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
coincides with the Hölder space
W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
when
s\in(0,\infty)\backslash\mathbb{N}
.
One has the embedding properties

\displaystyle C_{*}^{s}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\quad s>0;\qquad L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C_{*}^{s},\quad s<0;

\displaystyle C_{*}^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C_{*}^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}),\quad H^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad s_{1}>s_{2}.

2.2.
Paradifferential estimates

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

Let
\rho\in[0,\infty)
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
.

\Gamma^{m}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the space of locally bounded functions
a(x,\xi)
on
\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
, which are
C^{\infty}
with respect to
\xi
for
\xi\neq 0
and such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\xi\neq 0
, the function
x\mapsto\partial_{\xi}^{k}a(x,\xi)
belongs to
W^{\rho,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and there exists a constant
C_{k}
with

\forall|\xi|\geq\frac{1}{2},\quad\|\partial_{\xi}^{k}a(\cdot,\xi)\|_{W^{\rho,\infty}}\leq C_{k}(1+|\xi|)^{m-k}.

Let
a\in\Gamma^{m}_{\rho}
, we define the semi-norm

M^{m}_{\rho}(a)=\sup_{k\leq\frac{3}{2}+\rho}\sup_{|\xi|\geq\frac{1}{2}}\|(1+|\xi|)^{k-m}\partial_{\xi}^{k}a(\cdot,\xi)\|_{W^{\rho,\infty}}.

(2)

Given
a\in\Gamma^{m}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R})
, let
C^{\infty}
functions
\chi(\theta,\eta)
and
\psi(\eta)
be such that for some
0<\epsilon_{1}<\epsilon_{2}<1
,

\displaystyle\chi(\theta,\eta)=1,\text{ if }|\theta|\leq\epsilon_{1}(1+|\eta|),\qquad\chi(\theta,\eta)=0,\text{ if }|\theta|\geq\epsilon_{2}(1+|\eta|),

\displaystyle\psi(\eta)=0,\text{ if }|\eta|\leq\frac{1}{5},\qquad\psi(\eta)=1,\text{ if }|\eta|\geq\frac{1}{4}.

We define the paradifferential operator
T_{a}
by

\displaystyle\widehat{T_{a}u}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\chi(\xi-\eta,\eta)\hat{a}(\xi-\eta,\eta)\psi(\eta)\hat{u}(\eta)d\eta,

where
\hat{a}(\theta,\xi)
is the Fourier transform of a with respect to the variable x.

(3)

We first compute the estimates for the frequency-shift

.
Recall that it is given by

{\underline{a}}=i(\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}[\bar{R}R_{\alpha}]-{\mathbf{P}}[R\bar{R}_{\alpha}])+\frac{\gamma}{2}(R+\bar{R}-{\mathbf{P}}[W\bar{R}_{\alpha}-\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}R]-\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}[\bar{W}R_{\alpha}-{\mathbf{W}}\bar{R}]).

Lemma 2.10
.

The frequency-shift
{\underline{a}}
satisfies the estimate

(2.20)

\|{\underline{a}}\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}},

as well as the Sobolev estimate

(2.21)

\|{\underline{a}}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}},\quad s>0.

Proof.

For the holomorphic terms, we write

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}[R\bar{R}_{\alpha}]=T_{\bar{R}_{\alpha}}R+{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{R}_{\alpha},R),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}[W\bar{R}_{\alpha}-\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}R]=T_{\bar{R}_{\alpha}}W-T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}R+{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(W,\bar{R}_{\alpha})-{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}},R).

Using (
2.7
) and (
2.13
),

\displaystyle\|T_{\bar{R}_{\alpha}}R\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{R}_{\alpha},R)\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\|R\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|R\|_{C^{1}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}},

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\|T_{\bar{R}_{\alpha}}W\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\gamma\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{R}_{\alpha},W)\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\gamma\|W\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|R\|_{C^{1}_{*}}+\gamma\|R\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|W\|_{C_{*}^{1}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}},

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\|T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}R\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\gamma\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}},R)\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\gamma\|W\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|R\|_{C^{1}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}.

These give the bound (
2.20
) for the frequency-shift
{\underline{a}}
.
For the Sobolev estimate, we use (
2.6
), (
2.10
), (
2.11
) and embeddings to estimate

\displaystyle\|T_{\bar{R}_{\alpha}}R\|_{H^{s}}+\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{R}_{\alpha},R)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|R\|_{C_{*}^{1}}\|R\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim{\mathcal{B}}\|R\|_{H^{s}},

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\|T_{\bar{R}_{\alpha}}W\|_{H^{s}}+\gamma\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{R}_{\alpha},W)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\gamma\|W\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\|R_{\alpha}\|_{C^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}},

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\|T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}R\|_{H^{s}}+\gamma\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}},R)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\gamma\|W\|_{C_{*}^{1+\epsilon}}\|R\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim{\mathcal{B}}\|R\|_{H^{s}}.

These give the Sobolev bound (
2.21
) for the frequency-shift
{\underline{a}}

Lemma 2.11
.

The advection velocity
{\underline{b}}
satisfies the estimate

(2.22)

\|{\underline{b}}\|_{C^{1}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}},

as well as the Sobolev estimate

(2.23)

\|{\underline{b}}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{{\mathcal{A}}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}},\quad s>0.

Proof.

The bounds for
\Re R
and

are obvious, it suffices to estimate

and

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{P}}[R\bar{Y}]\|_{C^{1}_{*}}\leq\|T_{\bar{Y}}R\|_{C^{1}_{*}}+\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(R,\bar{Y})\|_{C^{1}_{*}}\lesssim\|Y\|_{L^{\infty}}\|R\|_{C^{1}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}},

As for the auxiliary function

Lemma 2.12
.

For
s>0
, the auxiliary function
Y=\frac{{\mathbf{W}}}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}

\|Y\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}},\quad\|Y\|_{C^{s}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{s}_{*}}.

Moreover, one can write

Y=T_{(1-Y)^{2}}{\mathbf{W}}+E,

where the error

satisfies the bound

\|E\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s-1}}.

Proof.

(
2.24
) is a direct consequence of the Moser type inequalities (
2.17
) and (
2.18
).
We write
Y
in the paraproduct expansion,

Y=(1-Y){\mathbf{W}}=T_{1-Y}{\mathbf{W}}-T_{{\mathbf{W}}}Y-\Pi({\mathbf{W}},Y),

so that

T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}Y=T_{1-Y}{\mathbf{W}}-\Pi({\mathbf{W}},Y).

Using the fact that
(1+{\mathbf{W}})(1-Y)=1
, one can write

Y-T_{(1-Y)^{2}}{\mathbf{W}}=(T_{(1-Y)(1+{\mathbf{W}})}-T_{1-Y}T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}){\mathbf{W}}+(T_{1-Y}T_{1-Y}-T_{(1-Y)^{2}}){\mathbf{W}}-T_{1-Y}\Pi({\mathbf{W}},Y).

E
.
This is due to the composition formula (
2.3
) with

and (
2.6
),

\displaystyle\|(T_{(1-Y)(1+{\mathbf{W}})}-T_{1-Y}T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}){\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s-1}},

\displaystyle\|(T_{1-Y}T_{1-Y}-T_{(1-Y)^{2}}){\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s-1}},

\displaystyle\|T_{1-Y}\Pi({\mathbf{W}},Y)\|_{H^{s}}\leq(1+\|Y\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\Pi({\mathbf{W}},Y)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|Y\|_{C^{1}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s-1}}.

Hence, they can be put into the error term
E
, and we get the expression (
2.25
).
∎

Finally, we compute some paralinearization results involving the Jacobian
J=(1+{\mathbf{W}})(1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}})
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
s>0
, we have the following paralinearization results:

(2.26)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}(J^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1)=-\frac{1}{2}T_{(1-Y)J^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}+E_{1},

(2.27)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}(J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)-1)=-\frac{3}{2}T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}+E_{2},

where the remainder terms satisfy the bounds

\|E_{1}\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}+\|E_{2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\|E_{1}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}+\|E_{2}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}.

Proof.

We write

J^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1=F({\mathbf{W}},\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}),\quad\text{where }F(a,b)=(1+a)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+b)^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1.

Direct computation gives

\frac{\partial F}{\partial a}(a,b)=-\frac{1}{2}(1+a)^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+b)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\frac{\partial F}{\partial b}(a,b)=-\frac{1}{2}(1+a)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+b)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

Using the paralinearization result Lemma
2.7
, we get

J^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1=-\frac{1}{2}T_{(1-Y)J^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}-\frac{1}{2}T_{(1-\bar{Y})J^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}+E_{3},\quad\|E_{3}\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}.

The term
-\frac{1}{2}T_{(1-\bar{Y})J^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}
is anti-holomorphic, and vanishes after applying the projection
{\mathbf{P}}
.
In addition, using Moser type inequality (
2.18
),

E_{1}={\mathbf{P}}(J^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1)+\frac{1}{2}T_{(1-Y)J^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}

satisfies

\|E_{1}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}.

Hence we get the paralinearization (
2.26
).

Similarly, one can write

J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-{\mathbf{W}})-1=G({\mathbf{W}},\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}),\quad\text{where }G(a,b)=(1+a)^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+b)^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1.

The two partial derivatives of
G(a,b)
are given by

\frac{\partial G}{\partial a}(a,b)=-\frac{3}{2}(1+a)^{-\frac{5}{2}}(1+b)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\frac{\partial G}{\partial b}(a,b)=-\frac{1}{2}(1+a)^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+b)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

Using Lemma
2.7
, one writes

J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+{\mathbf{W}})-1=-\frac{3}{2}T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}-\frac{1}{2}T_{J^{-\frac{3}{2}}}\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}+E_{4},\quad\|E_{4}\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}.

The term
-\frac{1}{2}T_{J^{-\frac{3}{2}}}\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}
is anti-holomorphic, and vanishes after applying the projection
{\mathbf{P}}
.
Furthermore, using Moser type inequality (
2.18
),

E_{2}={\mathbf{P}}(J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)-1)+\frac{3}{2}T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}

satisfies

\|E_{2}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}.

This gives the second equation (
2.27
).
∎

3.1.
Paralinearization of the water wave system

In this subsection, we use the paradifferential estimates and bounds for auxiliary functions in Section
2
to paralinearize the water wave system (
1.4
).

Using auxiliary functions
{\underline{a}}
and
{\underline{b}}
, the first equation of (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

(3.1)

W_{t}+{\underline{b}}(1+W_{\alpha})+i\frac{\gamma}{2}W=\bar{R}+i\frac{\gamma}{2}\bar{W}.

To begin with, we obtain a paradifferential equation for
W
using (
3.1
) that is similar to the equation for
W
in
[
5
]
.
We remark that although the unknown
W
does not appear directly in the differentiated system (
1.4
), we still need to paralinearize the equation (
3.1
) because it will play a role in Wahlén variables later.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s>0
, then we have the following results.

(1)

The unknown
W
solves the paradifferential equation

(3.2)

W_{t}+T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha}W=-T_{1+W_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{P}}[(1-\bar{Y})R]+i\frac{\gamma}{2}T_{1+W_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{P}}[W(1-\bar{Y})]-{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(W_{\alpha},{\underline{b}})-i\frac{\gamma}{2}W.

(2)

The unknown
W
satisfies the paradifferential equation

(3.3)

W_{t}+T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha}W+T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}T_{1-\bar{Y}}R=G,

where the source term
G
satisfies the Sobolev bound

\|G\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}.

(3)

Furthermore, the equation (
3.3
) can be reduced to

(3.4)

W_{t}+T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha}W+R=\tilde{G},

where the source term
\tilde{G}
satisfies the Sobolev bound

\|\tilde{G}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}.

Proof.

we use (
3.1
) to write

W_{t}+T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha}W=-T_{1+W_{\alpha}}{\underline{b}}-\Pi(W_{\alpha},{\underline{b}})-i\frac{\gamma}{2}W+\bar{R}+i\frac{\gamma}{2}\bar{W}.

After plugging in the expression of
{\underline{b}}
and applying the holomorphic projection
{\mathbf{P}}
, we can eliminate the anti-holomorphic portion and obtain the paradifferential equation (
3.2
).

For the paradifferential equation of
W
, we write

(3.5)

\displaystyle W_{t}+T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha}W+T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}T_{1-\bar{Y}}R=

\displaystyle T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{Y},R)-{\mathbf{P}}\Pi({\mathbf{W}},{\underline{b}})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle i\frac{\gamma}{2}T_{{\mathbf{W}}}W-i\frac{\gamma}{2}T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}T_{\bar{Y}}W-i\frac{\gamma}{2}T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(W,\bar{Y}).

We use (
2.6
), (
2.11
), the Moser type estimate (
2.18
), and the bound (
2.22
) to estimate the right-hand side of (
3.5
),

\displaystyle\|T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{Y},R)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|\Pi(\bar{Y},R)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|Y\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|R\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|R\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}},

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi({\mathbf{W}},{\underline{b}})\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s-1}}\|{\underline{b}}\|_{C^{1}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s-1}},

\displaystyle\gamma\|T_{\mathbf{W}}W\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\gamma\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s-1}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s-1}},

\displaystyle\gamma\|T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}T_{\bar{Y}}W\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\gamma\|T_{\bar{Y}}W\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\gamma\|Y\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s-1}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s-1}},

\displaystyle\gamma\|T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(W,\bar{Y})\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\gamma\|\Pi(W,\bar{Y})\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\gamma\|Y\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s-1}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s-1}}.

As a consequence, the right-hand side of (
3.5
) can be absorbed into the source term
G
.

We further rewrite

W_{t}+T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha}W+R=G+T_{\bar{Y}}R-T_{{\mathbf{W}}}R-T_{{\mathbf{W}}}T_{\bar{Y}}R.

By (
2.11
), we see that the right-hand side of the above equation satisfies the desired bound and can be put into
\tilde{G}
.
∎

Using the equation (
3.3
), we now obtain a paradifferential equation for
{\mathbf{W}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
s>0
, the unknown
{\mathbf{W}}
satisfies the paradifferential equation

(3.6)

{\mathbf{W}}_{t}+T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha}{\mathbf{W}}+\partial_{\alpha}T_{(1+{\mathbf{W}})(1-\bar{Y})}R=G_{1},

where the source term
G_{1}
satisfies the Sobolev bound

\|G_{1}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}.

Proof.

Taking the
\alpha
derivative of (
3.3
), one gets

(3.7)

{\mathbf{W}}_{t}+T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha}{\mathbf{W}}+\partial_{\alpha}T_{(1+{\mathbf{W}})(1-\bar{Y})}R=\partial_{\alpha}(T_{(1+{\mathbf{W}})(1-\bar{Y})}-T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}T_{1-\bar{Y}})R-T_{{\underline{b}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}+\partial_{\alpha}G.

To estimate the right-hand side of (
3.7
), we use (
2.3
), (
2.10
), and (
2.22
)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\alpha}(T_{1+{\mathbf{W}}}T_{1-\bar{Y}}-T_{(1+{\mathbf{W}})(1-\bar{Y})})R\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|R\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}},

\displaystyle\|T_{{\underline{b}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|{\underline{b}}\|_{C^{1}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\alpha}G\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|G\|_{H^{s+1}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}.

Therefore, the right-hand side of (
3.7
) can be absorbed into the source term
G_{1}
.
∎

Next, we paralinearize the second equation of the water wave system (
1.4
).
Note that it can be reexpressed as

(3.8)

\displaystyle R_{t}+T_{{\underline{b}}}\partial_{\alpha}R+i\gamma R=-{\mathbf{P}}T_{R_{\alpha}}{\underline{b}}-{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(R_{\alpha},{\underline{b}})+i{\mathbf{P}}[(g+{\underline{a}})Y]-{\mathbf{P}}[R\bar{R}_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle i\frac{\gamma}{2}{\mathbf{P}}[W\bar{R}_{\alpha}-\bar{W}_{\alpha}R]-i\sigma(1-Y){\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+{\mathbf{W}})}\right]_{\alpha}+i\sigma(1-Y){\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}})}\right]_{\alpha}.

For the non-capillary terms on the right-hand side of (
3.8
), we have the following paralinearization result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
s>0
, then one can write

(3.9)

-{\mathbf{P}}T_{R_{\alpha}}{\underline{b}}-{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(R_{\alpha},{\underline{b}})+i{\mathbf{P}}[(g+{\underline{a}})Y]-{\mathbf{P}}[R\bar{R}_{\alpha}]-i\frac{\gamma}{2}{\mathbf{P}}[W\bar{R}_{\alpha}-\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}R]=ig{\mathbf{W}}+K,

where the remainder term
K
satisfies

\|K\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}.

Proof.

We first show that

i{\mathbf{P}}[(g+{\underline{a}})Y]=ig{\mathbf{W}}+K.

Indeed, by (
2.25
),

i{\mathbf{P}}[gY]=igY=igT_{(1-Y)^{2}}{\mathbf{W}}+K=ig{\mathbf{W}}+K,

and using the product estimate (
2.14
) and the estimates for
{\underline{a}}
(
2.20
), (
2.21
),

\|{\mathbf{P}}[{\underline{a}}Y]\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|{\underline{a}}\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|Y\|_{H^{s}}+\|{\underline{a}}\|_{H^{s}}\|Y\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}.

Then we show that the rest of the terms on the left of (
3.9
) are perturbative and can be put into
K
.
Using (
2.6
), (
2.11
) and the estimate for
{\underline{b}}
(
2.23
),

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{P}}T_{R_{\alpha}}{\underline{b}}\|_{H^{s}}+\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(R_{\alpha},{\underline{b}})\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|R_{\alpha}\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}\|{\underline{b}}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}},

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{P}}[R\bar{R}_{\alpha}]\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|T_{\bar{R}_{\alpha}}R\|_{H^{s}}+\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{R}_{\alpha},R)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|R\|_{C^{1+\epsilon}_{*}}\|R\|_{H^{s}},

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{P}}[W\bar{R}_{\alpha}]\|_{H^{s}}\leq\gamma\|T_{\bar{R}_{\alpha}}W\|_{H^{s}}+\gamma\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi[W,\bar{R}_{\alpha}]\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\gamma\|R\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}},

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{P}}[R\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}]\|_{H^{s}}\leq\gamma\|T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}R\|_{H^{s}}+\gamma\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}},R)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\gamma\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}\|R\|_{H^{s}}.

These terms are all controlled by
{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}
and can be absorbed into
K
.
∎

For two capillary terms on the right-hand side of (
3.8
), we apply (
2.26
) and (
2.27
) to establish the following intermediate result.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
s>0
, then

(3.10)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+{\mathbf{W}})}\right]=T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}-\frac{3}{2}T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}+K_{1},

(3.11)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}})}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}J^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}+K_{2},

where the remainder terms
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
satisfy

\|K_{1}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}+\|K_{2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}},\quad\|K_{1}\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}+\|K_{2}\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}.

Proof.

For the first identity, using the paraproduct decomposition and the holomorphic projection
{\mathbf{P}}
, we decompose

(3.12)

{\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+{\mathbf{W}})}\right]=T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}+T_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{P}}[J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)-1]+{\mathbf{P}}\Pi({\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha},J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)-1).

By (
2.27
), the second term on the right of (
3.12
)
T_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{P}}[J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)-1]
equals

-\frac{3}{2}T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}+T_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}E_{2}-\frac{3}{2}\left(T_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}}-T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}\right){\mathbf{W}}.

Since

\|T_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}E_{2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\|{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\|_{C^{-1}_{*}}\|E_{2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}},

the remainder term

K_{3}:=T_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}E_{2}-\frac{3}{2}\left(T_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}}-T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}\right){\mathbf{W}}

satisfies

\|K_{3}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}},\quad\|K_{3}\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}

due to the composition estimates (
2.3
), (
2.5
).
For the balanced term on the right of (
3.12
), using the Moser type inequalities (
2.17
), (
2.18
), it satisfies

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi({\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha},J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)-1)\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\|{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\|J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+{\mathbf{W}})^{-1}-1\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}},

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi({\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha},J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)-1)\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim\|{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\|_{C^{-1}_{*}}\|J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+{\mathbf{W}})^{-1}-1\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}.

Hence, by choosing
K_{1}=K_{3}+{\mathbf{P}}\Pi({\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha},J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)-1)
, we get (
3.10
).

Similarly, using the paraproduct decomposition and the holomorphic projection, one writes

(3.13)

{\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}})}\right]=T_{\frac{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}}{1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}}{\mathbf{P}}(J^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1)+{\mathbf{P}}\Pi\left(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}(1-\bar{Y}),J^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1\right).

Using (
2.26
), the first term on the right of (
3.13
) equals

T_{\frac{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}}{1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}}{\mathbf{P}}(J^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1)=-\frac{1}{2}T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}J^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}+K_{4},

where the remainder term

K_{4}=T_{\frac{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}}{1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}}E_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\left(T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}J^{-\frac{3}{2}}}-T_{\frac{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}}{1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}}T_{(1-Y)J^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right){\mathbf{W}}

satisfies

\|K_{4}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}},\quad\|K_{4}\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}}

due to the composition estimates (
2.3
), (
2.5
).
For the balanced term on the right of (
3.13
), using the Moser type inequalities (
2.17
), (
2.18
), it satisfies the bounds

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi\left(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}(1-\bar{Y}),J^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1\right)\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\|J^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}},

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{P}}\Pi\left(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}(1-\bar{Y}),J^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1\right)\right\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim\|{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\|_{C^{-1}_{*}}\|J^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}.

Hence, by choosing
K_{2}=K_{4}+{\mathbf{P}}\Pi\left(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}(1-\bar{Y}),J^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1\right)
, we get (
3.11
).
∎

Using above paralinearization results, we then obtain the paralinearization of two capillary terms on the right-hand side of (
3.8
).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
s>0
, then

(3.14)

\displaystyle-i\sigma(1-Y){\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+{\mathbf{W}})}\right]_{\alpha}+i\sigma(1-Y){\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}})}\right]_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\sigma T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}+3i\sigma T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{3}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}+K,

where the remainder term
K
satisfies

\|K\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}.

Proof.

According to the paralinearization results (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), one can write

{\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}})}-\frac{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+{\mathbf{W}})}\right]=\frac{3}{2}T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}-T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}J^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}+K_{2}-K_{1}.

Taking the
\alpha
derivative of both sides,

(3.15)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}})}-\frac{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+{\mathbf{W}})}\right]_{\alpha}=-T_{\frac{1-Y}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}-T_{\left(J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)\right)_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}J^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{2}T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}+\frac{3}{2}T_{\left((1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}-\frac{1}{2}T_{\left(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}J^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}+\partial_{\alpha}(K_{2}-K_{1}).

The second to the fourth terms on the right-hand side of (
3.15
) can be simplified to

\displaystyle-T_{\left(J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)\right)_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}J^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}+\frac{3}{2}T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}J^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}J^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}+\frac{3}{2}T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3T_{(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}.

For the last three terms on the right-hand side of (
3.15
), using (
2.10
),

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\left((1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|(1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}},

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\left(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}J^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}J^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\alpha}(K_{2}-K_{1})\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|K_{1}\|_{H^{s+1}}+\|K_{2}\|_{H^{s+1}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}},

so that they are perturbative.
Moreover, using (
2.12
),

\left\|T_{\left((1-Y)^{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}\right\|_{C_{*}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}+\left\|T_{\left(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}J^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}\right\|_{C_{*}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\partial_{\alpha}(K_{2}-K_{1})\|_{C_{*}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}.

Hence,

{\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}})}\right]_{\alpha}-{\mathbf{P}}\left[\frac{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+{\mathbf{W}})}\right]_{\alpha}=-T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}+3T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}+K_{5},

where
K_{5}
satisfies the estimate

\|K_{5}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}},\quad\|K_{5}\|_{C^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}.

Finally, we multiply the above equation with
(1-Y)
and use the paraproduct decomposition,

(3.16)

\displaystyle-(1-Y)T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}=-T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}+T_{T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}}Y

\displaystyle+\Pi\left(T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha},Y\right)+\left(T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}}-T_{1-Y}T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}\right){\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}.

For the last three terms of (
3.16
), applying (
2.10
), (
2.13
) and the composition (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\left\|T_{T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}}Y\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\left\|T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}\right\|_{C^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\|Y\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}},

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi\left(T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha},Y\right)\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}\|Y\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}},

\displaystyle\left\|\left(T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}}-T_{1-Y}T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)}\right){\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|Y\|_{L^{\infty}}\|J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)-1\|_{W^{\frac{3}{2},\infty}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}},

where we use the Moser type inequality (
2.18
), and the fact that
W^{\frac{3}{2},\infty}({\mathbb{R}})=C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Hence, these three terms can be put into the remainder
K
.

Similarly, performing the paraproduct decomposition again, one writes

(3.17)

\displaystyle(1-Y)T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}=T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{3}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}-T_{T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}Y

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\Pi\left(T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha},Y\right)-\left(T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{3}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}-T_{1-Y}T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}\right){\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}.

applying (
2.10
), (
2.13
), (
2.17
) and the composition (
2.3
) for the last three terms on the right of (
3.17
),

\displaystyle\left\|T_{T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}Y\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\left\|T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\right\|_{C^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\|Y\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}},

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi\left(T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha},Y\right)\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\left\|T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\right\|_{C^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\|Y\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}},

\displaystyle\left\|\left(T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{3}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}-T_{1-Y}T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}\right){\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}},

these three terms can be absorbed into the remainder
K
.

Finally, the term
i\sigma(1-Y)K_{5}
is perturbative, since

\displaystyle\|(1-Y)K_{5}\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|K_{5}\|_{H^{s}}+\|T_{K_{5}}Y\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}+\|T_{Y}K_{5}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\Pi(Y,K_{5})\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+\|Y\|_{L^{\infty}})\|K_{5}\|_{H^{s}}+\|K_{5}\|_{C^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{*}}\|Y\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}.

Therefore, we get the paralinearization of the capillary terms (
3.14
).
∎

Gathering the paralinearization results for each part of the water wave system (
3.6
), (
3.9
) and (
3.14
), we end this subsection by stating that the water wave system can be reduced to a system of paradifferential equations.

Proposition 3.6

(Paralinearization of the water waves)
.

Let
s>0
, the water wave system (
1.4
) can be rewritten as

(3.18)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &T_{D_{t}}{\mathbf{W}}+\partial_{\alpha}T_{(1+{\mathbf{W}})(1-\bar{Y})}R=G\\

where

is the para-material derivative and the source terms

3.2.
Reduction of the water wave system

In this subsection, we work with Wahlén variables to rewrite the paradifferential system (

).
The use of Wahlén variables was developed by Wahlén
[

]
to reformulate the water waves with constant vorticity in Zakharov-Craig-Sulem formulation.
Later it was applied in Berti-Franzoi-Maspero
[

14
]
on the torus.
Since we are working with the holomorphic coordinates, we derive the equivalent Wahlen variables and as the expected new unknowns is slightly different.

Define new unknowns

We then rewrite the paradifferential water wave system (

Proposition 3.7
.

Let

where source terms

Proof.

We substitute

in (

) and simplify the system.
Then we obtain the first equation of (

\tilde{G}=G-i\frac{\gamma}{2}T_{{\mathbf{W}}-\bar{Y}-{\mathbf{W}}\bar{Y}}{\mathbf{W}}-i\frac{\gamma}{2}T_{({\mathbf{W}}-\bar{Y}-{\mathbf{W}}\bar{Y})_{\alpha}}W,

which satisfies the source term bound by (

), (

).

For the second equation of (
3.19
), applying (
3.4
) yields the result.

\eta
is the differentiated variable, and we write
\partial_{\alpha}^{-1}\eta
here for
W
.
∎

Alternatively, the system (
3.19
) can be rewritten as

T_{D_{t}}\begin{pmatrix}\eta\\
\zeta\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}i\frac{\gamma}{2}&T_{\lambda}\\
T_{k}&i\frac{\gamma}{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\eta\\
\zeta\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{G}\\
\tilde{K}\end{pmatrix},

where the symbols of the paradifferential operators are given by

(3.20)

\displaystyle\lambda(\alpha,\xi)=i(1-\bar{Y})(1+{\mathbf{W}})\xi+((1-\bar{Y})(1+{\mathbf{W}}))_{\alpha},

\displaystyle k(\alpha,\xi)=-i\sigma J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}\xi^{2}+3\sigma J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{3}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\xi-ig+i\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4\xi}.

The use of Wahlén variables makes the diagonal components of the matrix equal; they are both

3.3.
Symmetrization of the water wave system

In this subsection, we use the idea in Section 2 of Alazard, Baldi, and Han-Kwan
[
8
]
to symmetrize the paradifferential system (
3.18
).
Similar symmetrizations were carried out using Zakharov-Craig-Sulem formulation in
[
9
,
20
]
.

To get a quick idea for the symmetrization, we first consider the linearized system of the water waves (
3.19
) at zero solutions.
It is given by

(3.21)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}\eta+i\frac{\gamma}{2}\eta+\zeta_{\alpha}=0&\\
\partial_{t}\zeta+i\frac{\gamma}{2}\zeta+i\sigma\eta_{\alpha\alpha}-ig\eta-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\partial_{\alpha}^{-1}\eta=0.&\end{array}\right.

Define the differential operators

(3.22)

L(D)=\sqrt{\sigma|D|^{3}+g|D|+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}},\quad M(D)=L(D)|D|^{-1},

then (
3.21
) can be symmetrize to the system

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}M\eta\\
\zeta\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}i\frac{\gamma}{2}&-iL(D)\\
-iL(D)&i\frac{\gamma}{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}M\eta\\
\zeta\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix},

restricted to holomorphic functions.
The square matrix for dispersive terms is now a symmetric matrix whose two diagonal components are equal.

Inspired by the symmetrization of the linearized system at zero, for the nonlinear paradifferential system (
3.19
), we also want to symmetrize the system, but the computations should be carried out at the paradifferential level.
We aim to find symbols
p(\alpha,\xi),q(\alpha,\xi)
such that new unknowns

U:=T_{p}\eta,\quad V:=T_{q}\zeta

solve the paradifferential system

T_{D_{t}}\begin{pmatrix}U\\
V\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}i\frac{\gamma}{2}&-iL^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
-iL^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}&i\frac{\gamma}{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}U\\
V\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}G^{\sharp}\\
K^{\sharp}\end{pmatrix},

for some symbol
c(\alpha,\xi)
such that for
s>0
the source terms satisfy the bound

\|(G^{\sharp},K^{\sharp})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}.

The choices of the symbols
p
,
q
and
c
are not unique, they are allowed to be defined in an equivalent class modulo admissible remainders.
To make the argument more precise, we give the following definition of the equivalent class of operators.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and consider two families of operators of order
s
,

\{A(t):t\in[0,T]\},\quad\{B(t):t\in[0,T]\},

that depend on
({\mathbf{W}},R)
.
We write
A\sim B
if
A-B
is of order
s-\frac{3}{2}
, and the following condition is fulfilled: for all
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, such that for
a.e.

t\in[0,T]
,

\|A(t)-B(t)\|_{H^{\mu}\rightarrow H^{\mu-s+\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}.

Let us consider two operators
T_{a}=T_{a^{(s_{1})}+a^{(s_{1}-1)}}
and
T_{b}=T_{b^{(s_{2})}+b^{(s_{2}-1)}}
such that

\displaystyle a^{(s_{1})}\in\Gamma^{s_{1}}_{\frac{3}{2}},\quad a^{(s_{1}-1)}\in\Gamma^{s_{1}-1}_{\frac{1}{2}},\quad b^{(s_{2})}\in\Gamma^{s_{2}}_{\frac{3}{2}},\quad b^{(s_{2}-1)}\in\Gamma^{s_{2}-1}_{\frac{1}{2}},\quad s_{1}+s_{2}=s,

\displaystyle M^{s_{1}}_{\frac{3}{2}}(a^{(s_{1})})+M^{s_{2}}_{\frac{3}{2}}(b^{(s_{2})})\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}},\quad M^{s_{1}}_{0}(a^{(s_{1})})+M^{s_{2}}_{0}(b^{(s_{2})})\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}1,

\displaystyle M^{s_{1}-1}_{\frac{1}{2}}(a^{(s_{1}-1)})+M^{s_{2}-1}_{\frac{1}{2}}(b^{(s_{2}-1)})\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}},\quad M^{s_{1}-1}_{0}(a^{(s_{1}-1)})+M^{s_{2}-1}_{0}(b^{(s_{2}-1)})\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}1.

Using the symbolic calculus (
2.3
), with
\rho=\frac{3}{2},\frac{1}{2}
respectively,

\displaystyle T_{a^{(s_{1})}}T_{b^{(s_{2})}}\sim T_{a^{(s_{1})}b^{(s_{2})}-i\partial_{\xi}a^{(s_{1})}\partial_{\alpha}b^{(s_{2})}},\quad

\displaystyle T_{a^{(s_{1})}}T_{b^{(s_{2}-1)}}\sim T_{a^{(s_{1})}b^{(s_{2}-1)}},

\displaystyle T_{a^{(s_{1}-1)}}T_{b^{(s_{2})}}\sim T_{a^{(s_{1}-1)}b^{(s_{2})}},\quad

\displaystyle T_{a^{(s_{1}-1)}}T_{b^{(s_{2}-1)}}\sim 0.

Hence, we get

(3.23)

AB\sim T_{a^{(s_{1})}b^{(s_{2})}-i\partial_{\xi}a^{(s_{1})}\partial_{\alpha}b^{(s_{2})}+a^{(s_{1})}b^{(s_{2}-1)}+a^{(s_{1}-1)}b^{(s_{2})}}.

In order to symmetrize the paradifferential system (
3.19
), the matrix operator

\begin{pmatrix}T_{p}&0\\
0&T_{q}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}i\frac{\gamma}{2}&T_{\lambda}\\
T_{k}&i\frac{\gamma}{2}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}i\frac{\gamma}{2}&-iL^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
-iL^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}&i\frac{\gamma}{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}T_{p}&0\\
0&T_{q}\end{pmatrix}

needs to be a matrix of admissible remainders.
Alternatively, it is equivalent to show the following result.

Proposition 3.9
.

For the operator
L(D)
defined in (
3.22
), symbols
\lambda,k
defined in (
3.20
), and
\ell(\xi)
be the symbol of the operator

, we consider the function

where

and
p^{(-\frac{1}{2})}
is defined in (

Then the following equivalence relations hold for the operators.

(3.24)

iT_{p}T_{\lambda}\sim L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{q},\quad iT_{q}T_{k}\sim L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{p}.

Proof.

Using symbolic calculus (
2.3
),

L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim T_{m},\quad m=c\ell-\frac{i}{2}\partial_{\xi}\ell\partial_{\alpha}c.

Hence, let
q=q^{(0)}+q^{(-1)}
, where

and
q^{(-1)}\in\Gamma^{-1}_{\frac{1}{2}}

L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{q}\sim T_{m}T_{q}\sim T_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}}.

Here the symbol
\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}
equals

\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}=mq^{(0)}+c\ell q^{(-1)}-ic\partial_{\xi}\ell\partial_{\alpha}q^{(0)}=c\ell q^{(0)}-\frac{i}{2}\partial_{\xi}\ell\partial_{\alpha}cq^{(0)}-ic\partial_{\xi}\ell\partial_{\alpha}q^{(0)}+c\ell q^{(-1)}.

Similarly, let
p=p^{(\frac{1}{2})}+p^{(-\frac{1}{2})}
, where

and

where the symbol

equals

The leading terms and the sub-leading terms in two equivalence relations need to match.
Hence, we essentially have four equations to solve.
However, there are five symbols

,
p^{(\frac{1}{2})}
,
p^{(-\frac{1}{2})}
,
q^{(0)}
and

to determine.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that the lower order symbol

For the first equivalence relation in (
3.24

3.23

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}=

\displaystyle-p^{(\frac{1}{2})}(1-\bar{Y})(1+{\mathbf{W}})\xi+i\partial_{\xi}p^{(\frac{1}{2})}((1-\bar{Y})(1+{\mathbf{W}}))_{\alpha}\xi

\displaystyle+ip^{(\frac{1}{2})}((1-\bar{Y})(1+{\mathbf{W}}))_{\alpha}-p^{(-\frac{1}{2})}(1-\bar{Y})(1+{\mathbf{W}})\xi.

Since

The lower order terms give the expression for

(3.26)

\displaystyle p^{(-\frac{1}{2})}

\displaystyle=(1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}})(1-Y)\xi^{-1}\Big(\frac{i}{2}\partial_{\xi}\ell\partial_{\alpha}cq^{(0)}

\displaystyle+ic\partial_{\xi}\ell\partial_{\alpha}q^{(0)}+i\partial_{\xi}p^{(\frac{1}{2})}((1-\bar{Y})(1+{\mathbf{W}}))_{\alpha}\xi+ip^{(\frac{1}{2})}((1-\bar{Y})(1+{\mathbf{W}}))_{\alpha}\Big).

For the second equivalence relation in (

Having obtained the equivalence relations for the operators (

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
s>0
, and
(\eta,\zeta)

(3.27)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}T_{D_{t}}U+i\frac{\gamma}{2}U-iL^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}V=G^{\sharp}&\\

\|(G^{\sharp},K^{\sharp})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}.

Proof.

Applying
T_{p}
to the first equation of the system (
3.19

T_{q}
to the second equation of the system (
3.19

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}T_{D_{t}}U+i\frac{\gamma}{2}U-iL^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}V=T_{p}\tilde{G}+[T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha},T_{p}]\eta+i(iT_{p}T_{\lambda}-L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{q})\zeta+T_{\partial_{t}p}\eta&\\

For the first terms on the right-hand side of the above system,

For the commutator terms, using the composition rule (

\|[T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha},T_{p}]\eta\|_{H^{s}}+\|[T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha},T_{q}]\zeta\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|(\eta,\zeta)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}.

Then owing to the equivalence relations (

) which were established in Proposition

iT_{p}T_{\lambda}-L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{q}

It suffices to estimate the last terms on the right-hand side of the above system, which is further reduced to showing the operator bounds

\|T_{\partial_{t}p}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow H^{s}}+\|T_{\partial_{t}q}\|_{H^{s}\rightarrow H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}.

Note that
p
and

both depend on
{\mathbf{W}}
and
\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}
.
Recall from the paradifferential equation (
3.6

\|\partial_{t}{\mathbf{W}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}
.

M^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0}(\partial_{t}p^{(\frac{1}{2})})+M_{0}^{0}(\partial_{t}q)\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}.

To estimate

2.3
.
It follows from the expression (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p^{(-\frac{1}{2})}=\sum_{k}

\displaystyle F_{k}^{1}({\mathbf{W}},\bar{{\mathbf{W}}},{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha},\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha},\xi)\partial_{t}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}+F_{k}^{2}({\mathbf{W}},\bar{{\mathbf{W}}},{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha},\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha},\xi)\partial_{t}{\mathbf{W}}

\displaystyle+

),

M^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{-1}(\partial_{t}p^{(-\frac{1}{2})})\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}.

Therefore, we get

\|T_{\partial_{t}p}\eta\|_{H^{s}}+\|T_{\partial_{t}q}\zeta\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{H^{s}}.

Finally, collecting all source terms on the right-hand side into
(G^{\sharp},K^{\sharp})
,

\displaystyle G^{\sharp}=T_{p}\tilde{G}+[T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha},T_{p}]\eta+i(iT_{p}T_{\lambda}-L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{q})\zeta+T_{\partial_{t}p}\eta,

\displaystyle K^{\sharp}=T_{q}\tilde{K}+[T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha},T_{q}]\zeta+i(iT_{q}T_{k}-L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{p})\eta+T_{\partial_{t}q}\zeta,

using the estimates above, then
(G^{\sharp},K^{\sharp})
satisfy the desired bounds.
∎

At the end of this section, we rewrite the symmetrized paradifferential system (
3.27
) as a single paradifferential equation.
Taking the imaginary part of the first equation in (
3.27
) and the real part of the second
equation in (
3.27
), using Lemma
2.8
and the fact that
{\underline{b}}
and
c
are real-valued functions, one obtains the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}T_{D_{t}}\Im U+\frac{\gamma}{2}\Re U-L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\Re V=\Im G^{\sharp}&\\
T_{D_{t}}\Re V+L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\Im U-\frac{\gamma}{2}\Im V=\Re K^{\sharp}.&\end{array}\right.

Since
U
and
V
are both holomorphic functions, it follows that their real parts and imaginary parts can be related using the Hilbert transform:

\Re U=H\Im U,\quad\Im V=-H\Re V.

This system can be further reduced to a single paradifferential equation by introducing the complex-valued unknown
\Phi=\Re V+i\Im U
.
As a consequence, we rewrite above system using
\Re V
and
\Im U
as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}T_{D_{t}}\Im U+\frac{\gamma}{2}H\Im U-L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\Re V=\Im G^{\sharp}&\\
T_{D_{t}}\Re V+L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\Im U+\frac{\gamma}{2}H\Re V=\Re K^{\sharp}.&\end{array}\right.

To sum up, we arrive at the final reduction result for the water wave system in this section.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
s>0
, and
({\mathbf{W}},R)
solve the paradifferential water wave system (
3.18
).
Let
p,q,\ell,c
be defined in Proposition
3.9
, then the unknown

\Phi:=\Re T_{q}\left(R-i\frac{\gamma}{2}W\right)+i\Im T_{p}{\mathbf{W}}

solves the paradifferential equation

(3.28)

T_{D_{t}}\Phi-i\left(L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\text{sgn}(D)\right)\Phi=F,

where the source term
F:=\Im G^{\sharp}+i\Re K^{\sharp}
satisfies

(3.29)

\|F\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}.

4.
A priori energy estimate and the Strichartz estimate

In this section, we first prove an a priori energy estimate Theorem
1.1
for the paradifferential equation (
3.28
), then we use the results in Nguyen
[
35
]
to establish the Strichartz estimate for the paradifferential equation (
3.28
).

4.1.
Energy estimate

To obtain an
H^{s}
estimate, we apply an elliptic operator of order
s
to the paradifferential equation (
3.28
).
To avoid the issue of the commutator with the dispersive term, here we do not use the usual elliptic differential operator
(1+D^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}
.
Instead, we apply the elliptic operator
T_{\wp}
of order
s
as in
[
6
,
20
]
, where
\wp=(c\ell)^{\frac{2s}{3}}
, and
c\ell
is the principle symbol of the operator
L^{\frac{1}{2}}cL^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Taking
\varphi:=T_{\wp}\Phi
, then by (
2.2
),

(4.1)

\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Phi\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}.

On the other hand, using Proposition
3.13
in
[
20
]
, for any constant
\mu\geq 0
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\eta,\zeta)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|T_{\wp}T_{p}\eta\|_{L^{2}}+\|T_{\wp}T_{q}\zeta\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\eta,\zeta)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-\mu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\eta,\zeta)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-\mu}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-\mu}}.

The unknown
\varphi
solves the equation

(4.3)

T_{D_{t}}\varphi-i\left(L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\text{sgn}(D)\right)\varphi=T_{\wp}F+\tilde{f},

where
\tilde{f}
is given by

\tilde{f}=T_{\partial_{t}\wp}\Phi+[T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha},T_{\wp}]\Phi-i\left[L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\text{sgn}(D),T_{\wp}\right]\Phi.

We can derive the following a priori energy estimate for the equation (
4.3
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
s>0
,
\Phi
solves the paradifferential equation (
3.28
).
Then for any
\mu\geq 0
,
\varphi=T_{\wp}\Phi
with
\wp=(c\ell)^{\frac{2s}{3}}
satisfies the a priori energy estimate

(4.4)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}(1+{\mathcal{B}})\left(\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-\mu}}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

\varphi
solves the equation (
4.3
), and we estimate the right-hand side source terms in
L^{2}
.
Using (
3.29
) and (
4.2
), we get

\|T_{\wp}F\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\left(\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-\mu}}\right).

For the first term of
\tilde{f}
, we note that
\partial_{t}\wp=F_{1}({\mathbf{W}},\bar{{\mathbf{W}}},\xi)\partial_{t}{\mathbf{W}}+F_{2}({\mathbf{W}},\bar{{\mathbf{W}}},\xi)\partial_{t}\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}
for some functions
F_{1},F_{2}
.
Since
\|\partial_{t}{\mathbf{W}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}
, it follows that
T_{\partial_{t}\wp}
is of order
s
, and

\|T_{\partial_{t}\wp}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\|\Phi\|_{H^{s}}.

Next, we estimate two commutator terms in
\tilde{f}
.
For the commutator with the transport term, we use the symbolic calculus (
2.3
) with
\rho=1
,

\|[T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha},T_{\wp}]\Phi\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}(1+{\mathcal{B}})\|\Phi\|_{H^{s}}.

As for the commutator with the dispersive term, since
\wp
is some power of
c\ell
,
\partial_{\xi}\wp\partial_{\alpha}(c\ell)=\partial_{\alpha}\wp\partial_{\xi}(c\ell)
.
Using again the symbolic calculus (
2.3
) with
\rho=\frac{3}{2}
, the leading term of the commutator
[L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}},T_{\wp}]\Phi
vanishes, and

\left\|i\left[L^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{c}L^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\text{sgn}(D),T_{\wp}\right]\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}(1+{\mathcal{B}})\|\Phi\|_{H^{s}}.

Putting these estimates together, and using (
2.2
), (
4.2
) we obtain the estimate

\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}(1+{\mathcal{B}})\|\Phi\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}(1+{\mathcal{B}})\left(\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-\mu}}\right).

Then we compute

\frac{d}{dt}\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=2\Re\langle\varphi_{t},\varphi\rangle=\langle(\partial_{\alpha}(T_{\underline{b}})^{*}-T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha})\varphi+iL^{\frac{1}{2}}(T_{c}-(T_{c})^{*})L^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle+2\Re\langle T_{\wp}F+\tilde{f},\varphi\rangle.

Using the paradifferential calculus for adjoint operator (
2.4
),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\partial_{\alpha}(T_{\underline{b}})^{*}-T_{\underline{b}}\partial_{\alpha})\varphi+iL^{\frac{1}{2}}(T_{c}-(T_{c})^{*})L^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+(\|T_{\wp}F\|_{L^{2}}+\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}})\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}(1+{\mathcal{B}})\left(\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-\mu}}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}.

This gives the a priori energy estimate for
\varphi
.
∎

Having obtained the a priori energy estimate for
\varphi
, we can now deduce the energy estimate for
({\mathbf{W}},R)
and finish the proof of Therorem
1.1
.

Proof of Therorem
1.1
.

Integrating the a priori energy estimate inequality (
4.4
), and using (
4.1
), (
4.2
), we get the Sobolev bound

\displaystyle\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{2}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-\mu}}+\|\varphi(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-\mu}}+\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(1+{\mathcal{B}}(\tau))\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{2}d\tau.

Note that by choosing appropriate
\mu\geq 0
, one can obtain the energy estimate directly for the system (
1.4
),

\frac{d}{dt}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-\mu}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}(1+{\mathcal{B}})\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{s}},

so that integrating it gives

\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-\mu}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(0)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-\mu}}+\int_{0}^{t}(1+{\mathcal{B}}(\tau))\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{2}d\tau.

Adding this energy bound to the previous inequality,

\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{2}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(1+{\mathcal{B}}(\tau))\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{2}d\tau,

which is exactly the integral version of the energy estimate (
1.5
).
∎

4.2.
The Strichartz estimate

To apply the Strichartz estimate for the paradifferential equation (
3.28
), we first use the technique of
para-composition
of Alinhac
[
10
]
to rewrite the equation, so that its principle dispersive part becomes constant-coefficient after the transformation.
In other words, we seek an operator
\kappa^{*}
such that
\tilde{\Phi}:=\kappa^{*}\Phi
solves an equation of the following type:

\partial_{t}\tilde{\Phi}+T_{\tilde{{\underline{b}}}}\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{\Phi}-i|D|^{\frac{3}{2}}\tilde{\Phi}=\tilde{F}.

We remark that this method was previously used in Alazard-Burq-Zuily
[
9
]
and Nguyen
[
35
]
on the Strichartz estimate of two-dimensional gravity-capillary water waves with zero vorticity.
In the following, we recall the main result in Section 3 of
[
35
]
on the global para-composition.

Theorem 4.1

(Global para-composition
[
35
]
)
.

Let
\kappa:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a diffeomorphism that satisfies the following two conditions:

(1)

There exist
\rho>0
,
r>-1
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\partial_{x}\kappa\in C_{*}^{\rho}(\mathbb{R})
,
\partial_{x}^{k}\kappa\in H^{r+1-k}(\mathbb{R})
.

(2)

There exists a constant
m_{0}>0
such that
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}
,
|\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}(x)|\geq m_{0}
.

Then there exists an operator
\kappa^{*}_{g}
defined by

\kappa^{*}_{g}u=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{k}[P_{k}u\circ\kappa],\quad\text{where }(u\circ\kappa)(\alpha)=u(\kappa(\alpha))

with the following properties:

(1)

Operation: For every
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|\kappa^{*}_{g}u\|_{C_{*}^{s}}\lesssim_{m_{0},\|\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}}}\|u\|_{C_{*}^{s}},\quad\forall u\in C_{*}^{s}(\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle\|\kappa^{*}_{g}u\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{m_{0},\|\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}}}\|u\|_{H^{s}},\quad\forall u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}).

(2)

Conjugation: Let
m,s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\tau>0
.
Set
\epsilon=\min\{\tau,\rho\}
.
Then for every
h(x,\xi)\in\Gamma^{m}_{\tau}
, homogeneous in
\xi
there exists
h^{*}\in\Gamma^{m}_{\epsilon}
, such that for all
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,

\kappa^{*}_{g}T_{h}u=T_{h^{*}}\kappa^{*}_{g}u+R_{conj}u,

where the remainder term
R_{conj}u
satisfies

\|R_{conj}u\|_{H^{s-m+\epsilon}}\lesssim_{m_{0},\|\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{C^{\rho}_{*}}}M^{m}_{\tau}(h)(1+\|\partial^{k}_{x}\kappa\|_{H^{r+1-k}})\|u\|_{H^{s}}.

Moreover,
h^{*}(x,\xi)
is given by the formula

\displaystyle h^{*}(x,\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^{[\rho]}h_{j}^{*}:=\sum_{j=0}^{[\rho]}\frac{1}{j!}\partial_{\xi}^{j}D_{y}^{j}\left(h(\kappa(x),R(x,y)^{-1}\xi)\frac{|\partial_{y}\kappa(y)|}{|R(x,y)|}\right)\Bigg|_{y=x},

\displaystyle R(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}\kappa\left(tx+(1-t)y\right)dt.

(3)

Linearization: The linearization of
\kappa^{*}_{g}u
is given by
u\circ\kappa-\dot{T}_{u^{{}^{\prime}}\circ\kappa}\kappa
and the remainder part

R_{line}u:=\kappa_{g}^{*}u-u\circ\kappa+\dot{T}_{u^{{}^{\prime}}\circ\kappa}\kappa

satisfies the estimates

(a)

If
0<\sigma<1
,
\rho+\sigma>1
, and
r+\sigma>0
, then for
\tilde{s}=\min\{s+\rho,r+\sigma\}
,

\|R_{line}u\|_{H^{\tilde{s}}}\lesssim_{m_{0},\|\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{C^{\rho}_{*}}}(1+\|\partial^{k}_{x}\kappa\|_{H^{r+1-k}})(\|\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{s-1}}+\|u\|_{C^{\sigma}_{*}}).

(b)

If
\sigma>1
, set
\epsilon=\min(\sigma-1,\rho+1)
, then for
\tilde{s}=\min\{s+\rho,r+1+\epsilon\}
,

\|R_{line}u\|_{H^{\tilde{s}}}\lesssim_{m_{0},\|\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{C^{\rho}_{*}}}(1+\|\partial^{k}_{x}\kappa\|_{H^{r+1-k}})(\|\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{s-1}}+\|u\|_{C^{\sigma}_{*}}).

The expression of
\kappa^{*}_{g}
is not important for our analysis, we will instead let
\kappa^{*}
be the linearization of
\kappa^{*}_{g}
, and we define

\tilde{\Phi}:=\kappa^{*}\Phi=\Phi\circ\kappa-\dot{T}_{\partial_{\alpha}\Phi\circ\kappa}\kappa,\quad\text{where }\Phi\circ\kappa(t,\alpha)=\Phi(t,\kappa(t,\alpha))

For each
t\in[0,T]
, we define a time dependent
\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
diffeomorphism
\chi(t,\alpha)
by

\chi(t,\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\alpha}J^{\frac{1}{2}}(t,y)\,dy.

This choice of
\chi(t,\alpha)
gives
\partial_{\alpha}\chi=c^{-\frac{2}{3}}(t,\alpha)
.
We further set
\kappa
to be the inverse of
\chi
, then

\partial_{\alpha}\kappa(t,\alpha)=\frac{1}{(\partial_{\alpha}\chi)\circ\kappa}=J^{-\frac{1}{2}}\circ\kappa,

and

1\geq\partial_{\alpha}\kappa\geq m_{0}:=(1+\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,\alpha}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the Moser type estimate (
2.17
) and Lemma
2.9
, for
s\geq 0
,

(4.5)

\|\partial_{\alpha}\chi-1\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}},\quad\|\partial_{\alpha}\kappa-1\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s}}

To get an estimate for
\partial_{t}\chi
, we write

\partial_{t}\chi(t,\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\alpha}\frac{1}{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t,y)(1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}(t,y)){\mathbf{W}}_{t}(t,y)+\frac{1}{2}J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t,y)(1+{\mathbf{W}}(t,y))\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{t}(t,y)\,dy.

Using the embedding
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C^{\epsilon}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
, for
s\geq 1
,

(4.6)

\|\partial_{t}\chi(t,\alpha)\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}+\|\partial_{t}\partial_{\alpha}\chi(t,\alpha)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}){\mathbf{W}}_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|{\mathbf{W}}_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}.

In addition, we have

(4.7)

\|\partial_{t}\partial_{\alpha}\chi(t,\alpha)\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}\lesssim\|J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}){\mathbf{W}}_{t}\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{r}},\quad r>1.

Since
\chi
is the inverse diffeomorphism of
\kappa
,
\chi(t,\kappa(t,\alpha))=\alpha
, taking the time-derivative gives

\partial_{t}\chi\circ\kappa+\partial_{\alpha}\chi\circ\kappa\cdot\partial_{t}\kappa=0.

Hence, using (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), for
s\geq 1
,
r>1
,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\kappa(t,\alpha)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\left\|\frac{\partial_{t}\chi}{\partial_{\alpha}\chi}\circ\kappa\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq m_{0}\|\partial_{t}\chi\circ\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}},

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\kappa(t,\alpha)\|_{C^{1+\epsilon}_{*}}\lesssim\|\partial_{t}\chi\circ\kappa\|_{C^{1+\epsilon}_{*}}\|\partial_{\alpha}\kappa(t,\alpha)\|_{C^{1+\epsilon}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{r}}.

Having established all the necessary bounds, we now apply Theorem
4.1
to show that
\tilde{\Phi}=\kappa^{*}\Phi
solves a dispersive equation with its highest order term being constant coefficient.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Phi
solves the paradifferential equation (
3.28
), then for
s>r>1
,
\tilde{\Phi}=\kappa^{*}\Phi
solves the paradifferential equation

(4.10)

\left(\partial_{t}+T_{\tilde{{\underline{b}}}}\partial_{\alpha}-i|D|^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\tilde{\Phi}=\tilde{F},

where the new para-coefficient for the transport term is

\tilde{{\underline{b}}}=({\underline{b}}\circ\kappa)(\partial_{\alpha}\chi\circ\kappa)+\partial_{t}\chi\circ\kappa,

and the new source term
\tilde{F}
satisfies, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\|\tilde{F}(t)\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\alpha}}}1+\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{r}}.

Proof.

We apply the operator
\kappa^{*}
to equation (
3.28
).
First, for the term of time derivative, we use the definition of
\kappa^{*}
,

\displaystyle\kappa^{*}(\partial_{t}\Phi)=\partial_{t}\Phi\circ\kappa-\dot{T}_{(\partial_{t}\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa}\kappa=\partial_{t}(\Phi\circ\kappa)-(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi\circ\kappa)\partial_{t}\kappa-\dot{T}_{(\partial_{t}\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa}\kappa

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\kappa^{*}\Phi)+\dot{T}_{(\partial_{\alpha}^{2}\Phi\circ\kappa)\partial_{t}\kappa}\kappa+\left(\dot{T}_{(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa}\partial_{t}\kappa-(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi\circ\kappa)\partial_{t}\kappa\right).

Note that
\dot{T}_{a}u
does not involve the low frequency part of
u
.
We estimate using (
4.5
), (
4.9
), (
2.10
), and (
2.19
),

\displaystyle\|\dot{T}_{(\partial_{\alpha}^{2}\Phi\circ\kappa)\partial_{t}\kappa}\kappa\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\|(\partial_{\alpha}^{2}\Phi\circ\kappa)\partial_{t}\kappa\|_{C^{-1}_{*}}\|\partial_{\alpha}^{2}\kappa\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\|\partial_{\alpha}\Phi\circ\kappa\|_{C^{0}_{*}}\|\partial_{t}\kappa\|_{C^{1+\epsilon}_{*}}\|\partial_{\alpha}^{2}\kappa\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{r}}^{2}.

To estimate the last term of
\kappa^{*}(\partial_{t}\Phi)
, we use the fact that

\partial_{\alpha}(\kappa^{*}\Phi)=(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa\cdot\partial_{\alpha}\kappa-\dot{T}_{(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa}\partial_{\alpha}\kappa-\dot{T}_{\partial_{\alpha}((\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa)}\kappa,

so that

\displaystyle\dot{T}_{(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa}\partial_{t}\kappa-(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi\circ\kappa)\partial_{t}\kappa=-\dot{T}_{\partial_{t}\kappa}(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa-\dot{\Pi}((\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa,\partial_{t}\kappa)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{T}_{(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa\cdot\partial_{\alpha}\kappa}(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa+\dot{\Pi}((\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa,(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa\cdot\partial_{\alpha}\kappa)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{T}_{(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa}\dot{T}_{\partial_{\alpha}\kappa}(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa+\dot{\Pi}((\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa,(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa\cdot\partial_{\alpha}\kappa)+F_{para}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{T}_{(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa}\left((\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa\cdot\partial_{\alpha}\kappa-\dot{T}_{(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa}\partial_{\alpha}\kappa\right)-\dot{T}_{(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa}\dot{\Pi}((\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa,\partial_{\alpha}\kappa)

\displaystyle+\dot{\Pi}((\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa,(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa\cdot\partial_{\alpha}\kappa)+F_{para}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{T}_{(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa}\partial_{\alpha}(\kappa^{*}\Phi)+\dot{T}_{(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa}\dot{T}_{\partial_{\alpha}((\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa)}\kappa-\dot{T}_{(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa}\dot{\Pi}((\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa,\partial_{\alpha}\kappa)

\displaystyle+\dot{\Pi}((\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa,(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa\cdot\partial_{\alpha}\kappa)+F_{para},

where remainder term
F_{para}
is given by

F_{para}=\dot{T}_{(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa\cdot\partial_{\alpha}\kappa}(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa-\dot{T}_{(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa}\dot{T}_{\partial_{\alpha}\kappa}(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa.

Using (
4.6
) and (
4.7
),

\|\partial_{t}\chi\circ\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}},\quad\|\partial_{t}\chi\circ\kappa\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\lesssim\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}(1+\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{r}}).

We now estimate the second to the last terms of
\dot{T}_{(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa}\partial_{t}\kappa-(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi\circ\kappa)\partial_{t}\kappa
, and show that they can be put into the source term
\tilde{F}
.

\displaystyle\|\dot{T}_{(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa}\dot{T}_{\partial_{\alpha}((\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa)}\kappa\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|(\partial_{t}\chi)\circ\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{\alpha}((\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\circ\kappa)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{\alpha}^{2}\kappa\|_{H^{s-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{2}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)(t)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{r}},

5.
The local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem

In this section, we follows the steps in the expository article of Ifrim-Tataru
[
28
]
, and sketch the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
For the details of the proof and the use of
frequency envelopes
, we refer the interested readers to Section 5.1 in
[
28
]
.
The proof of the local well-posedness is divided into several steps.

First, we truncate the frequency of initial data at
2^{N}
and obtain a sequence of approximate solutions that converge weakly to the regular solution.
Second, we obtain a contraction estimate for two different solutions of (
1.4
) and therefore get the uniqueness in
\mathcal{H}^{s}
for
s>\frac{3}{2}
.
Next, we use the regular solutions to construct the rough solutions in
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
\frac{3}{2}\geq s>\frac{5}{4}
.
Finally, we prove the continuous dependence on the initial data.

5.1.
Existence of regular solutions

Consider the initial data
({\mathbf{W}}_{0},R_{0})\in\mathcal{H}^{\tau}
.
For each positive integer
N
, we choose the frequency projection
P_{<N}
that selects the frequencies less or similar than
2^{N}
.
Then for fixed
N
, the mollified system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &T_{D_{t}}^{(<N)}{\mathbf{W}}+\partial_{\alpha}P_{<N}T_{(1+{\mathbf{W}})(1-\bar{Y})}R=G_{<N}\\
&T_{D_{t}}^{(<N)}{\mathbf{W}}+i\gamma P_{<N}R+i\sigma P_{<N}T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}-3i\sigma P_{<N}T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y)^{3}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}-igP_{<N}{\mathbf{W}}=K_{<N},\end{aligned}\right.

where

T_{D_{t}}^{(<N)}=\partial_{t}+T_{{\underline{b}}_{N}}\partial_{\alpha},\quad{\underline{b}}_{N}=P_{<N}{\underline{b}}(P_{<N}{\mathbf{W}},P_{<N}R),

and

G_{<N}=P_{<N}G(P_{<N}{\mathbf{W}},P_{<N}R),\quad K_{<N}=P_{<N}K(P_{<N}{\mathbf{W}},P_{<N}R),

form a system of ordinary differential equations in
\mathcal{H}^{s}
.
The mollified system admits a local solution
({\mathbf{W}}^{N},R^{N})
.
Using the argument as in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, one can show that for all

is uniformly bounded from above.
Then the regular solution
({\mathbf{W}},R)
is a weak limit on a subsequence of
({\mathbf{W}}^{N},R^{N})
as

.

\displaystyle=\delta G-T_{\delta{\underline{b}}}\partial_{\alpha}{\mathbf{W}}_{2}+\partial_{\alpha}T_{(1+{\mathbf{W}}_{2})(1-\bar{Y}_{2})-(1+{\mathbf{W}}_{1})(1-\bar{Y}_{1})}R_{2},

\displaystyle K^{\sharp}

\displaystyle=\delta K-T_{\delta{\underline{b}}}\partial_{\alpha}R_{2}+i\sigma T_{J_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y_{2})^{2}-J_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y_{1})^{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{2,\alpha\alpha}+3i\sigma T_{J_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y_{1})^{3}{\mathbf{W}}_{1,\alpha}-J_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-Y_{2})^{3}{\mathbf{W}}_{2,\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{2,\alpha}.

As in the computation in Section
3
, one can obtain the difference bound

\displaystyle\|(G^{\sharp},K^{\sharp})(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|({\mathbf{W}}_{1},R_{1})(t)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{r}}+\|({\mathbf{W}}_{2},R_{2})(t)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{r}}\right)\|(\delta{\mathbf{W}},\delta R)(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\left(\|({\mathbf{W}}_{1},R_{1})(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\|({\mathbf{W}}_{2},R_{2})(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\right)\|(\delta{\mathbf{W}},\delta R)(t)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{r-\frac{3}{2}}}.

Rewriting the difference equation in Wahlén variables and using the symmetrization as in Section
3
, we can then obtain the a priori energy estimate and the Strichartz estimate.
The energy estimate gives

(5.2)

\displaystyle\|(\delta{\mathbf{W}},\delta R)(T)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\|(\delta{\mathbf{W}}_{0},\delta R_{0})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle T^{\frac{3}{4}}(\|(\delta{\mathbf{W}},\delta R)\|_{L^{4}(I;\mathcal{W}^{r-\frac{3}{2}})}+\|(\delta{\mathbf{W}},\delta R)\|_{L^{\infty}(I;\mathcal{H}^{s-\frac{3}{2}})})\Big),

where the implicit constant depends on
M^{j}_{s,T},N^{j}_{r,T}
,
j=1,2
.
Furthermore, by the Strichartz estimate,

(5.3)

\|(\delta{\mathbf{W}},\delta R)\|_{L^{4}(I;\mathcal{W}^{r-\frac{3}{2}})}\lesssim T^{\delta}\left(\|(\delta{\mathbf{W}},\delta R)\|_{L^{4}(I;\mathcal{W}^{r-\frac{3}{2}})}+\|(\delta{\mathbf{W}},\delta R)\|_{L^{\infty}(I;\mathcal{H}^{s-\frac{3}{2}})}\right),

for some time
0<T<1
with
\delta>0
.
Separating the time interval
I=[0,T]
into
[0,T_{1}],[T_{1},T_{2}],\cdots[T_{n},T]
, on each interval we combine the energy estimate (
5.2
) and the Strichartz estimate (
5.3
), which gives the proof of Proposition
5.1
.

5.3.
Construction of rough solutions

Following the work in

]
as well as

2^{N}
for
(W_{<N}(0),Q_{<N}(0))
and
({\mathbf{W}}_{<N}(0),R_{<N}(0))

Using the energy bounds for the solutions and the difference bounds
({\mathbf{W}}^{N+1}-{\mathbf{W}}^{N},R^{N+1}-R^{N})
in
\mathcal{H}^{s}

This further shows that when

5.4.
Continuous dependence on the data

For any sequences
({\mathbf{W}}_{j,0},R_{j,0})
that converge to initial data
({\mathbf{W}}_{0},R_{0})
in
\mathcal{H}^{s}
, we truncate the data at frequency
2^{N}
and obtain the corresponding regular solutions
({\mathbf{W}}_{j}^{N},R_{j}^{N})
, respectively
({\mathbf{W}}^{N},R^{N})
.
we have the continuous dependence on the data for the regular solutions, for each
N
,

({\mathbf{W}}^{N}_{j},R^{N}_{j})-({\mathbf{W}}^{N},R^{N})\rightarrow 0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{H}^{\tau},\quad\tau>\frac{3}{2}.

On the other hand, for the initial data, we let
N
go to infinity,

({\mathbf{W}}^{N}_{j,0},R^{N}_{j,0})-({\mathbf{W}}_{j,0},R_{j,0})\rightarrow 0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{H}^{s},\quad\text{uniformly in }j.

Using the notation of frequency envelopes, we get the uniform convergence for the solution:

({\mathbf{W}}^{N}_{j},R^{N}_{j})-({\mathbf{W}}_{j},R_{j})\rightarrow 0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{H}^{s},\quad\text{uniformly in }j.

We can again let
N
go to infinity to further conclude that

({\mathbf{W}}_{j},R_{j})-({\mathbf{W}},R)\rightarrow 0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{H}^{s}.

This shows the continuous dependence on the data for solutions in
\mathcal{H}^{s}
.

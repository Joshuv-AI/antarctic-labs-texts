Dual-band graphene-based THz MIMO antenna for 6G-enabled biomedical application using supervised regression machine learning validation
Authors: Md. Ashraful Haque, Maruf Billah, Jun-Jiat Tiang, Mouaaz Nahas, Md. Mostafa Arafat, Narinderjit Singh Sawaran Singh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The exponential increase in wireless data traffic and the growing demand for biomedical sensing have driven the advancement of sophisticated antenna technologies, particularly within the terahertz (THz) frequency range. This research presents an innovative graphene-based microstrip patch antenna featuring a slotted design and MIMO configuration, specifically designed for the high-speed needs of 6G communications and sensitive biomedical applications. The proposed antenna features a compact dual-band structure with a narrowband of 0.2839 THz (1.7654 to 2.0493 THz) and a wideband of 3.8201 THz (2.6412 to 6.4613 THz), achieving a peak gain of 12.83 dB, high efficiency (93.1%), and excellent isolation (−38.8 dB). A novel slot geometry, including hexagonal, triangular, and circular trous slots, enhances impedance matching, bandwidth, and radiation properties. To address the limitations of conventional THz antenna design, the study integrates an RLC equivalent circuit model and machine learning (ML)-driven regression algorithms to optimize performance. The ML-based prediction model validated against simulated data demonstrates high accuracy and significant reductions in design iteration time. Material analyses confirm graphene and polyimide as optimal choices for high-efficiency, miniaturized THz devices. The design progression from single-element to MIMO configurations with silver-based decoupling structures ensures low mutual coupling, making the antenna suitable for high-data-rate, low-latency wireless systems. The comprehensive evaluation encompassing ECC, DG, CCL, TARC, MEG, and radiation characteristics highlights the antenna’s potential in both wireless communications and biomedical applications.

Introduction

The rapid advancement of wireless communication systems has led to an increasing demand for antennas that operate at high frequencies, have high bandwidths, and are energy efficient. The terahertz (THz) frequency spectrum, ranging from 0.1 to 10 THz, is essential for the development of 6G networks, offering bandwidths that significantly surpass those available in 5G1. The THz spectrum holds great potential for achieving data rates in the (Tbps) range2, marking a significant improvement over existing gigabit speeds. This progress necessitates the development of high-gain, wide-bandwidth antennas capable of effectively transmitting and receiving THz signals. As we approach the limits of current radio frequency technologies, the THz band emerges as crucial for supporting the upcoming generation of wireless communication: 6G3,4. Terahertz communication enables rapid data transmission, low latency, and extensive device interconnectivity5. Ultimately, THz communication is set to revolutionize the future of wireless systems. The wide bandwidth available in the THz range is crucial for achieving such high-speed communication. This makes THz antennas ideal candidates for next-generation applications that demand massive data throughput, including augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), high-definition video streaming, and sensor networks for the Internet of Things (IoT). Despite their great promise, THz antennas face significant challenges. As the frequency increases into the THz range, traditional antenna designs such as microstrip patch antennas become less efficient. This is primarily due to signal attenuation, material losses, and narrow bandwidth at high frequencies. The resistive losses in conventional materials and dielectric losses at THz frequencies hinder efficient signal transmission. Additionally, atmospheric absorption of THz waves further limits the effective communication range, which is a major concern for long-range applications.

Microstrip patch antennas, recognized for their compact dimensions, simplicity of production, and economic viability, are extensively utilized in THz applications6. Conventional patch antennas, however, have restricted bandwidth and diminished efficiency at elevated frequencies, rendering them less appropriate for THz communication7. Slotted patch designs have been implemented to address these difficulties, markedly enhancing impedance matching, bandwidth, and radiation properties8. The integration of Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) technology is a significant advancement in wireless communication systems, particularly in the context of high-frequency applications like THz communication. MIMO technology utilizes multiple antennas at both the transmitter and receiver ends to improve system capacity, efficiency, and reliability. In traditional single-antenna systems, signal degradation due to multipath fading and interference can limit the quality of communication, especially at high frequencies like those in the THz range9. MIMO mitigates these issues by using multiple spatial channels to transmit and receive data, thereby increasing the available bandwidth and reducing the risk of signal loss. Furthermore, the incorporation of multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) innovation significantly boosts spectrum efficiency, spatial diversity and beamforming capabilities, rendering THz antennas exceptionally appropriate for next-generation wireless networks10. Integrating MIMO technology improves spatial diversity, and beamforming capabilities, rendering it a formidable solution for 6G networks11,12. This study introduces a MIMO-type THz microstrip patch antenna with a slotted patch and planar ground, both constructed from graphene, a material known for its outstanding electrical, thermal, and mechanical characteristics13. Graphene’s superior conductivity and flexibility render it an optimal choice for THz antennas, facilitating improved performance and downsizing14,15. The proposed antenna design utilizes machine learning (ML) to enhance slot geometry, substrate characteristics, and feed configurations, thereby achieving optimal efficiency, broad bandwidth, and elevated gain. The amalgamation of graphene-based materials with machine learning-driven optimization surmounts traditional THz antenna constraints, facilitating smooth incorporation into next-generation wireless systems16. These attributes render it a promising answer for forthcoming wireless systems17,18.

The proposed antenna operates at 1.91 THz, 2.96 THz, and 5.837 THz, with an ultra-wide bandwidth of 0.283 and 3.82 THz, outperforming referenced designs in frequency range and data handling. Its broad bandwidth enables seamless multi-band operations, ideal for high-speed wireless and 6G applications. It achieves the highest gain (12.83 dB), overcoming THz path losses for strong signal propagation. With best-in-class isolation (-38.8 dB), it minimizes mutual coupling, enhancing MIMO performance. The antenna maintains 93.1% efficiency, ensuring energy efficiency for sustainable communication. Its lowest ECC (0.000121) and high DG (9.9994 dB) enable superior MIMO performance, even in multipath environments. Built on a compact polyimide substrate (0.509 λ₀ × 2.056 λ₀) µm2, it offers easy integration into miniaturized devices. The antenna integrates ML techniques for real-time optimization and an RLC circuit for impedance matching and stability. These features set a new benchmark for THz MIMO systems, enabling high-speed data transfer, secure communication, deep-space exploration, and ultra-fast computing with scalability and adaptability.

Research gap and contribution

The rapid advancement of terahertz (THz) technology demonstrates the potential for 6G wireless communications and biomedical diagnostics. Current THz MIMO antenna designs have difficulties in achieving an equilibrium among high gain, efficiency, and compactness for incorporation into 6G systems and biomedical equipment. Current designs predominantly depend on numerical simulations, lacking a circuit-level analytical framework, hence constraining design flexibility and theoretical understanding. Moreover, computationally intensive full-wave simulations hinder swift design cycles, and the restricted use of machine learning limits optimization and performance forecasting. These deficiencies highlight the pressing necessity for a more effective, analytically informed design methodology that can facilitate both sophisticated communication and biological applications. The properties of graphene material

A multilayer of carbon atoms arranged in a honeycomb lattice, graphene has become a key material for ultra-fast, high-frequency communication systems47. Its remarkable electrical conductivity, elevated electron mobility, and adaptability render it a viable candidate for next-generation terahertz (THz) antennas functioning within the 0.1 to 10 THz frequency spectrum. In contrast to traditional metals, graphene facilitates the transmission of surface plasmon polaritons (SPPs) at THz frequencies48, thereby allowing the creation of highly efficient, compact, and low-loss antennas49. For graphene modeling in CST, there are two options available: the graphene model and the graphene-EPS model. The graphene model in CST, with a thickness of 0.345 nm, is used to represent a single layer of graphene, whereas the graphene-EPS model is employed for simulating multilayer graphene sheets with an adjustable thickness50.The conductivity of graphene is characterized using the Drude model. Moreover, its conductivity can be modulated by varying the chemical potential (μc = 0.1 eV) via electrostatic or chemical doping, facilitating the creation of frequency-reconfigurable antennas suited to diverse communication needs. The material’s mechanical flexibility and near-transparency enhance its suitability for wearable, flexible, and space-based antenna systems. Design methodology

Our design approach is undergoing a transformational progression as we work towards the advancement of antenna technology for forthcoming 6G applications. This progression begins with a fundamental single-element antenna and culminates in an advanced Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) configuration, as illustrated in Fig. 1. The design initiates with a graphene-based single-element antenna, utilizing multilayer of graphene for both the patch and ground plane due to its exceptional conductivity, flexibility, and tunability. This antenna ensures high radiation efficiency, broad bandwidth, and precise impedance matching. The antenna is designed on a 7-μm polyimide substrate (dielectric constant: 3.5, loss tangent: 0.0027)52, enabling efficient signal transmission with minimal energy loss, making it suitable for high-frequency applications. The design advances to a MIMO configuration, employing multilayer of graphene to enhance spatial diversity and multiplexing capabilities, thereby increasing data rates and spectral efficiency for 6G networks. Decoupling structures and optimized element spacing are utilized to mitigate problems such as mutual coupling and surface wave losses. Moreover, the tunability of graphene facilitates reconfigurable performance, permitting the antenna to adjust to fluctuating operational conditions dynamically. This antenna design optimizes graphene’s remarkable properties by employing a polyimide substrate for 6G applications. The evolution of single-element antenna

The evolution of single-element antenna design has progressed through four distinct phases, as illustrated in Fig. 4, each characterized by intentional geometric alterations intended to improve essential performance metrics such as impedance matching, bandwidth, gain, and radiation efficiency. Commencing with Stage 1, a traditional rectangular patch antenna constituted the fundamental framework. Simulation results in Fig. 5(a) indicated significant performance shortcomings: absence of discrete resonances, inadequate return loss, and limited bandwidth constraints that rendered the original design unsuitable for high-frequency biomedical or MIMO applications.Fig. 4evolutionary advancement of single element antenna design.Fig. 5Performance indicators across various stages in the evolution of proposed single-element antennas: (a) Reflection coefficient; (b) Gain; and (c) Efficiency.

evolutionary advancement of single element antenna design.

Performance indicators across various stages in the evolution of proposed single-element antennas: (a) Reflection coefficient; (b) Gain; and (c) Efficiency.

In Stage 2, the antenna shape was optimized by incorporating dual inset feed cuts to improve impedance matching, along with a centrally implanted hexagonal slot intended to generate numerous resonant modes. This arrangement produced resonances at 1.86, 3.9, 4.37, and 4.81 THz, as shown in Fig. 5(a). Nonetheless, performance was inadequate, achieving a maximum gain of merely 4.44 dB and a peak radiation efficiency of 58% within the operational bandwidth—insufficient to satisfy the requirements of next-generation THz devices.

In Stage 3, we incorporated two symmetrically located triangular apertures within the hexagonal patch, which dramatically altered the current distribution and broadened the antenna’s frequency range to 2.2–5.0 THz. A notable resonance was observed at 2.94 THz, exhibiting a return loss of –50 dB, as shown in Fig. 5(a), while the gain markedly increased to 7.8 dB. Nonetheless, the radiation efficiency had a minor decline, recorded at 57% within the operational bandwidth, as shown in Fig. 5(c), signifying a trade-off between gain augmentation and efficiency.

The most significant enhancement occurred in Stage 4, where a circular slot was overlaid on the triangle structures to create a hybrid slot shape. This enhancement improved current flow and facilitated a stable multi-resonant response, exhibiting pronounced, well-separated resonances at 1.91, 2.95, and 5.82 THz, with corresponding return losses of –38.97, –41.51, and –51.45 dB, respectively. The frequency ranges covered are 1.78–2.04 THz (0.265 THz), 2.62–5.14 THz (2.51 THz), and 5.14–6.45 THz (1.31 THz), as shown in Fig. 5(a), facilitating ultra-wideband functionality. The ultimate design achieved a maximum gain of 11.77 dB, as shown in Fig. 5(b), and a peak radiation efficiency of 90.58%, as shown in Fig. 5(c), across the operational bandwidth, confirming its efficacy for high-capacity 6G MIMO networks and sophisticated biomedical platforms.

This preliminary design sets a foundation for future advancements. The simulated antenna is subsequently assessed according to essential performance metrics, namely the reflection coefficient (S11), gain, and efficiency. The parameters are evaluated to confirm that the design satisfies the initial performance criteria. After the preliminary analysis, an extensive parametric and material investigation is undertaken. Systematic exploration of geometric and material property variations is conducted to enhance the antenna’s performance. This stage is essential for improving key characteristics, including bandwidth, gain, and radiation efficiency. Following optimization, a reassessment of S11, gain, and efficiency is conducted. Machine learning (Ml) based optimization

Machine learning (ML) has shown considerable promise for exceeding what is possible in classical electromagnetic (EM) simulation software, especially for antenna design purposes. ML is one of the most important types of analysis in the statistical world and provides one of the greatest benefits of all – the ability, in many cases, to reduce substantial calculation time43,83. Traditional electromagnetic (EM) solver tools like Finite Element Method (FEM) or Finite Difference Time Domain (FDTD) methods typically are resource-heavy computations84, where a single simulation may take hours or days. At the same time, machine learning (ML) models can, after training, accurately predict antenna behavior in no time85. This fast prediction is needed in sectors like telecommunication, aerospace, and IoT86, as rapid design iteration and prototyping are of great importance in this domain87.

Besides time efficiency, ML is also good at searching over vast and complicated design spaces. Traditional approaches tend to rely heavily on manual tuning and many runs of the simulation (which can make exploring the entire space of design in detail rather tricky). Unfortunately, this process can be labor-intensive and might not always generate the best solutions. In contrast, ML devises solutions independently, leveraging past design patterns to navigate vast design spaces quickly and efficiently without continuous handholding.

A further significant benefit of ML over more traditional EM solvers is the upfront evaluation of antenna performance. With traditional EM solvers, engineers often go through multiple iterations of adjusting and reviewing the results before finalizing them to improve the design. It can be especially long and susceptible to human error. In contrast, ML models are able to design antennas autonomously to perform specific tasks with minimal instruction, alleviating the need for repeated manual analyses88.

Also, ML models are more robust and resilient to real-world variabilities than classical EM solvers. Standard solvers work under ideal conditions for materials, geometry, and the environment, while real-world antenna designs must accommodate material properties, fabrication tolerances, and environmental conditions. Since ML models were trained on diverse data reflective of various scenarios, these models are adept at accommodating these differences and providing designs that are more resilient to conditions present in the real world.

Lastly, ML allows for multi-objective optimization in which multiple design objectives can be satisfied simultaneously89. Traditional EM solvers typically optimize one or two parameters at a time, requiring multiple simulation cycles to achieve a balanced design. Unlike traditional methods, ML can optimize several objectives at a time, thus making the design process more efficient and compact and leading to optimized and well-rounded antenna designs90. This is especially useful for applications where many performance metrics need to be satisfied (for instance, antenna efficiency, bandwidth, and size)91.

Overall, the application of ML in antenna design from traditional EM solvers leads to a paradigm shift with unique advantages. These advantages are significant improvements in computation time, better efficiency in exploring the design spaces92, autonomously generating designs that are guaranteed to meet performance requirements, better resilience to the variations found in real life, and a multi-objective optimization capacity. With ML moving forward, its integration in antenna design would further enhance the advancements in this direction93.

Selection of algorithm

Antenna characteristics such as gain, bandwidth, efficiency, and return loss are analyzed and modeled using regression on various antenna parameters. The proposed approach employs an ensemble method that integrates five advanced machine-learning regression models to enhance the performance and accuracy of antenna design. By using multiple models, we can investigate the impact of different antenna parameters on performance metrics while effectively capturing both linear and non-linear effects. This diverse approach not only improves prediction accuracy but also enables the identification of complex patterns that traditional regression techniques may struggle to reveal. Through a systematic process leading to model selection, end users are guided in their choice of models with the expectation of achieving reliable predictions of antenna responses and overall performance.

Decision tree regression

In this article, we will look at decision tree regression and how to predict continuous target variables. Whereas linear regression models assume a linear relationship between input features and the target variable, decision tree regression can capture non-linear relationships. It makes systematic and recursive divisions of the data using which feature values minimize the variance of the data in each subset. Each split results in nodes on a tree, where leaf nodes correspond to the predicted output. It also finds the requisite target value associated with each leaf node, as the final prediction for a data point is usually an average of target values in the corresponding leaf node94.

Extra trees regression

Extra Trees (Extremely Randomized Trees) Regression is quite similar to Random Forest; however, it differs in how the individual Decision Trees are constructed. The core idea behind Extra Trees is to introduce greater randomness in the tree-building process, which typically results in improved performance and generalization95.

Extreme gradient boosting

XGBoost is an advanced variant of gradient boosting frequently utilized in machine-learning competitions and various real-world applications. It enhances traditional gradient boosting by incorporating L1 and L2 regularization techniques, which help mitigate overfitting. Additionally, XGBoost boasts improved speed and robustness compared to other boosting algorithms, thanks to its innovative approaches such as parallelization, tree pruning, and efficient handling of missing values. Its accuracy and scalability make it a preferred choice for a wide range of applications, including fraud detection, recommendation systems, and financial modeling96.

Gaussian process regression

Gaussian Process (GP) Regression is a powerful non-parametric Bayesian method for regression that effectively captures uncertainty. Rather than learning a specific function, a Gaussian Process updates a distribution of likely functions based on observed data. GP regression employs a kernel function to assess the similarity of values in the input space, which allows it to model complex, non-linear relationships in data. The model estimates the expected values at new locations and provides a confidence interval for each prediction. GP regression is utilized in various applications, including optimization, robotics, and time-series forecasting, where an understanding of prediction uncertainty is essential. This approach is particularly effective for small to medium-sized datasets, as it involves inverting large covariance matrices, which can be computationally expensive for larger datasets. Nonetheless, it remains a highly effective machine learning tool, as successfully approximating the function also allows for estimating its uncertainty97.

Bayesian linear regression

In this approach, model parameters are treated as random variables, and their prior distributions are learned rather than represented by point estimates. Utilizing Bayes’ theorem, we can compute the posterior distribution of these learned parameters based on the available data. Prior distributions encapsulate our beliefs about the parameters (such as test accuracy), while likelihood functions illustrate the process of data generation. By integrating the prior distribution with the likelihood, Bayesian Linear Regression yields a complete distribution of regression coefficient values instead of merely point estimates. This method allows us to account for uncertainty within the model, which is particularly beneficial for preventing overfitting and for assessing the uncertainty in predictions. Overall, Bayesian Linear Regression is more robust and informative than simple linear regression, as it explores a range of parameter values and their associated probabilities98.

Regression models for antenna design are evaluated based on their ability to provide predictions of important performance measures, such as gain, radiation efficiency, bandwidth, and return loss. You are assessed using standard evaluation techniques such as Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), which indicate the deviation of predictions and strive to minimize errors in design field calculations. Additionally, R-squared and Variance Scores are employed to gauge the effectiveness of the model in capturing variations in the performance of the antennas. Mean Squared Error (MSE) is used to calculate the average of the squared differences between predicted and actual data. Also, due to the nonlinearity characteristics of antennas, models should be validated using cross-validation techniques to ensure their generalization for various design conditions. Conclusion

This study presents a high-performance graphene-based MIMO microstrip patch antenna designed explicitly for terahertz (THz) applications in 6G communications and biomedical diagnostics. By utilizing advanced slot geometries and innovative materials—including multilayer of graphene for both the patch and ground and polyimide as the substrate—the proposed antenna achieves ultra-wideband operation with resonances at 1.91 THz, 2.96 THz, and 5.837 THz. It exhibits outstanding performance in key metrics: a peak gain of 12.83 dB, high radiation efficiency at 93.1%, and exceptional isolation at –38.8 dB, along with an ultra-low envelope correlation coefficient (0.000121) and a high diversity gain of 9.9994 dB. The study also addresses critical limitations of existing THz antenna designs by incorporating a validated RLC equivalent circuit model and machine learning (ML)-based optimization. Among the various regression models evaluated, the XGBoost regression model proved to be the most effective in predicting antenna gain, achieving a low mean absolute error of 1.84%, a mean squared error of 0.07%, a root mean squared error of 2.70%, an R2 score of 97.69% and a variance score of 98.57%. This model closely aligned with simulated gain values across all 30 data points, demonstrating high accuracy and reliability. Furthermore, the integration of decoupling structures using silver enhances isolation within the MIMO configuration, ensuring minimal mutual coupling and robust system reliability. The proposed graphene-based MIMO antenna design faces fabrication challenges, particularly with achieving precise material properties like conductivity and flexibility at terahertz frequencies. Performance trade-offs, such as signal attenuation and material losses at higher frequencies, impact the antenna’s efficiency and bandwidth. Additionally, while machine learning optimization reduces design iteration time, it adds computational complexity that could be streamlined in future developments. Future studies will aim to fabricate and experimentally validate the proposed antenna, confirming its performance in real-world THz applications. Further work may also focus on enhancing reconfigurability, integrating deep learning for more intelligent design optimization, and adapting the antenna for flexible, biocompatible platforms suitable for wearable or medical devices.

On the Rosenberg-Stolz Conjecture for X × ℝ 2 X\times\mathbb{R}^{2} and Its Application in Complex Geometry
Jie Xu

Abstract
. Let X X be an oriented, closed manifold with dim X ⩾ 2 \dim X\geqslant 2 . In this article, we give both Riemannian geoemtry and complex geometry results on (sub)manifolds of the type X × ℂ k X\times\mathbb{C}^{k} or X × ℝ k X\times\mathbb{R}^{k} . For Riemannian geometry side, we show that if X × ℂ = X × ℝ 2 X\times\mathbb{C}=X\times\mathbb{R}^{2} admits a Riemannian metric g g with uniformly positive scalar curvature and bounded curvature, such that some novel conformally invariant g g -angle condition is satisfied, then there exists a metric g ~ \tilde{g} conformal to g g such that g ~ | X \tilde{g}|_{X} has positive scalar curvature. This Riemannian path implies a complex geometry result: we show that if the complex manifold X × ℂ X\times\mathbb{C} admits a Hermitian metric ω \omega whose associated Riemannian metric g g has uniformly positive scalar curvature and is of bounded curvature, then X × ℂ X\times\mathbb{C} admits a Hermitian metric ω ~ \tilde{\omega} with positive Chern scalar curvature, provided that some g g -angle condition is satisfied. The Riemannian geometry result partially answers a 1994 Rosenberg-Stolz conjecture [ 10 ] . The complex geometry result extends a result of X.K. Yang [ 16 ] from compact Hermitian manifolds to noncompact Hermitian manifolds of type X × ℂ X\times\mathbb{C} . We further generalize both the Riemannian and complex geometry results to X × ℝ k X\times\mathbb{R}^{k} or X × ℂ k X\times\mathbb{C}^{k} for any k ⩾ 1 k\geqslant 1 by imposing a generalized conformally invariant angle condition.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be an oriented, closed manifold with
\dim X\geqslant 2
. In a 1994 survey article, Rosenberg and Stolz made the following conjecture
[
10
, Conjecture 7.1(2)]
:

Conjecture: If
X
admits no metrics of positive scalar curvature, then
X\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
admits no complete metrics of uniformly positive scalar curvature.

This conjecture holds in many cases by imposing some topological significances, see e.g.
[
9
]
. Very recently, Cecchini, Räde and Zeidler
[
3
]
were able to verify this conjecture when
X
is oriented,
2\leqslant\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X\leqslant 5
and
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X\neq 4
by a
\mu
-bubble method. In this article, we show by contrapositive statement that if for any closed, oriented manifold
X
with
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X\geqslant 2
, the noncompact manifold
X\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
admits a complete metric
g
with uniformly positive scalar curvature, then
X
admits a positive scalar curvature in the same conformal class of the originally induced metric, provided that some novel conformally invariant angle condition and the geometry near “infinity” are imposed. We also give an example, which states that if
X
has no PSC metric, and
X\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
admits a complete metric with positive scalar curvature and “good” geometry near “infinity”, then the angle condition fails. It indicates that our angle condition is reasonable.

Our conformal geometry approach allows us to apply the result of Rosenberg-Stolz conjecture for
X\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
to complex geometry. A 2020 result of X. K. Yang
[
16
, Corollary 3.9]
states that if
(X,\omega,J)
is a Hermitian manifold with Hermitian metric
\omega
and a fixed almost complex structure
J
whose background Riemannian metric
g(J\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,\cdot)
has quasi-positive scalar curvature, then there exists a Hermitian metric
\tilde{\omega}
on
X
with positive Chern scalar curvature.

In this article, we also answer the following question, which is a generalization of X.K. Yang’s results
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
to noncompact Hermitian manifolds of cylindrical type
X\times\mathbb{C}

If
(X\times\mathbb{C},\omega,J)
is a noncompact complex manifold with complete Hermitian metric
\omega

g(J\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,\cdot)
has uniformly positive scalar curvature, does
X\times\mathbb{C}
admits some complete Hermitian metric
\tilde{\omega}
with positive Chern scalar curvature?

It is a natural first step from compact spaces to noncompact Hermitian manifold. Roughly speaking, (i) the existence of complete metrics with positive Riemannian/Chern scalar curvature on
X\times\mathbb{C}
is definitely related to the existence of positive Riemannian/Chern scalar curvature metrics on
X

is a local model of every complex line bundle over the base space
X
(here
X
might be open subsets of the base manifold), which may build a pavement for the problem of transposing the positivity of Chern scalar curvatures from the total space of a complex line bundle to the base space.

The first reasoning (i) above can be observed from a Riemannian point of view. Topologically
X\times\mathbb{C}\cong X\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. It is clear that the Riemannian metric with uniformly positive scalar curvature on

is related to the positive Riemannian scalar curvature metric on

[
10

Step I: By assuming the uniform positivity of the Riemannian scalar curvature of the associated Riemannian metric
g

\omega

X\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
admits a Riemannian metric with uniformly positive scalar curvature, does

Step II: Once a Riemannian metric on
X
with positive Riemannian scalar curvature is found from Step I, we would like to know
whether such a Riemannian metric is compatible with some Hermitian structure on
X
?

Step III: If we get affirmative answers for both Steps I and II, we obtain a Hermitian metric
\tilde{\omega}
on
X
with positive Chern scalar curvature by X. K. Yang
[
16
]
,
[
17
]

\mathbb{C}

2.
Related Work, Motivation and Examples of Angle Condition

As we mentioned in §1, we resolve the complex geometry result via a Riemannian path. We consider this type of problems from a conformal geometry point of view, along with repeated Gauss-Codazzi equations on codimension one hypersurfaces. Such a Riemannian path unveils the positivity of the Riemannian scalar curvature in the submanifold
X
from the positivity of the Riemannian scalar curvature in the ambient space
X\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

From a contrapositive statement point of view, the 1994 Rosenberg-Stolz conjecture is the type of problem by asking the existence of positive scalar curvature on submanifolds, provided that some notions of positivity are given in the ambient spaces. For noncompact manifolds, Step I results hold for many cases. Generally, there are two types of methods in the study of scalar curvature problems on hypersurfaces: ones is based on Dirac operator and index theory on spin manifolds; the other is based on the geometric measure theory initiated by Schoen-Yau minimal hypersurfaces method
[
12
]
; as a modified version of the minimal surface method, the
\mu
-bubble method, pioneered by Gromov’s seminal work
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
, is another major stream in the study of positive scalar curvature manifolds. A comprehensive survey is given by
[
9
]
. In particular, the studies via index-theoretic obstructions can be found in
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
18
]
; the studies via
\mu
-bubbles or minimal surface method can be found in
[
3
]
. However, they provide no information whether their Riemannian metrics with positive Riemannian scalar curvature are compatible with Hermitian structures.

We solve this type of problem according to a different approach. By introducing an auxiliary
1

[

In a word, our conformal geometry and geometric analysis approach applies equally well in transposing some notions of positivity from ambient Riemannian/Hermitian manifolds to their (sub)manifolds.

Remark 2.1
.

The example we gave in Example
2.1
is not perfect, as the scalar curvature is not uniformly positive. However it indicates the necessity of the angle conditions to some extent. In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, uniformly positive scalar curvature is the same as uniformly positive Ricci curvature. By Myers’ theorem, every complete manifold with positive Ricci curvature bound is compact. Therefore, if
X
admits no PSC metric, then the product metric
g_{X}\oplus g_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
either fails to be complete, or fails to have uniformly positive scalar curvature. However, our example is almost there.

We can give another “quasi” example: If
X
admits
g_{X}
with negative scalar curvature, then with the same
g_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}=ad\xi^{2}+2b(\xi,\zeta)d\xi d\zeta+ad\zeta^{2}

on

. Since
\mathbb{R}^{2}
admits a compact exhaustion, this gives a complete metric whose scalar curvature is positive “almost everywhere”, i.e.

only in

can be chosen as closed ball with arbitrary large radius.
a
should be large enough to obtain the “almost everywhere” PSC metric, hence the angle condition must fail.

We point out that it is very hard to construct a concrete example where the positivity is coming from the intertwine between
X

\mathbb{R}
-side.

(d\pi_{\xi})_{*}\nu_{g}
nowhere vanish on
X_{\zeta,0}
.

Remark 3.1
.

When
(d\pi_{\xi})_{*}\nu_{g}\equiv 0
on

, the condition (
10
) degenerates to

\frac{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g(\partial_{\zeta},\partial_{\zeta})}{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g(\nu_{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g},\partial_{\zeta})^{2}}<2

for the operator
L^{\prime}=\nabla_{V_{2}}\nabla_{V_{2}}-\Delta_{\sigma^{*}\bar{g}}
in
W
. Equivalently, it says
\angle_{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g}(\nu_{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g},\partial_{\zeta})\in[0,\frac{\pi}{4})
, as we set in
[
11
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
. Consequently, our problem reduces exactly to the case of
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
. From now on, we assume that
(d\pi_{\xi})_{*}\nu_{g}
never vanishes on
X_{\xi,0}
.

Next we construct an appropriate inhomogenous term of our partial differential equation, as the next lemma shows.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
C\gg 1
and any positive
\delta\ll 1
, there exists a positive smooth function
F:W\to\mathbb{R}
and small enough constant
0<\epsilon\ll 1
such that such that (i)
[-\epsilon,\epsilon]_{t}\subset\Psi(U)
; (ii)
F|_{X_{\zeta,0}\times(-\frac{\epsilon}{2},\frac{\epsilon}{2})_{t}}=C+1
, (iii)
F\equiv 0
outside
X_{\zeta,0}\times[-\epsilon,\epsilon]_{t}
; and (iv)
\lVert F\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}(W,\sigma^{*}\bar{g})}<\delta
.

Proof.

In a word, we first set a function that is large enough in the tubular neighborhood
X_{\zeta,0}\times\left(-\frac{\epsilon}{2},\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)_{t}
, and decay to zero fast enough by letting
\epsilon
to be small enough. Exactly the same argument follows from
[
14
, Lemma 2.2]
.
∎

We define the
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-norm on
W
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
by fixing a chart cover
W=\bigcup_{i}(U_{i},\varphi_{i})
such that

\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(W)}=\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{0}(W)}+\sup_{i,k}\lVert\partial_{x_{i}^{k}}u\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{0}(U_{i})}+\sup_{i,k}\sup_{x\neq x^{\prime},x,x^{\prime}\in U_{i}}\frac{\left\lvert\partial_{x_{i}^{k}}u(x)-\partial_{x_{i}^{k}}u(x^{\prime})\right\rvert}{\lvert x-x^{\prime}\rvert^{\alpha}}

where
\partial_{x_{i}^{k}}u
are local representations in
U_{i}
with local coordinates
\{x_{i}^{1},\dotso,x_{i}^{n-1},x_{i}^{n+2}=t\}
and associated local frames
\{\partial_{x_{i}^{k}}\}
.

Let
R_{\bar{g}}
be the Riemannian scalar curvature of
(M\times\mathbb{S}^{1},\bar{g})
. We now introduce the partial differential equation in
W
and verify
\mathcal{C}_{1,\alpha}
-smallness of the solution. Such a solution will be used to construct our conformal factor. The next result is essentially the same as
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)=(X\times\mathbb{R}^{2},g)
be a noncompact complete manifold such
R_{g}\geqslant\kappa_{0}>0
for some
\kappa_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
(W,\sigma^{*}\bar{g})
be the closed manifold as above. Assume that (
10
) holds. For any positive constant
\eta\ll 1
, any positive constant
C
, and any
p>n=\dim W
, there exists an associated
F
and
\delta
in the sense of Lemma
3.2
, such that the following partial differential equation

(14)

Lu:=\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\nabla_{V_{1}}\nabla_{V_{1}}u+4\nabla_{V_{2}}\nabla_{V_{2}}u-4\Delta_{\sigma^{*}\bar{g}}u+R_{\bar{g}}|_{W}u=F\;{\rm in}\;W

admits a unique smooth solution
u
with

(15)

\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(W)}<\eta

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
\alpha\geqslant 1-\frac{n}{p}
.

Proof.

Since
X
is compact, (
10
) implies that the maximum of the continuous function

\frac{n-1}{n-2}\frac{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g(\partial_{\zeta},\partial_{\zeta})}{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g((d\pi_{\xi})_{*}\nu_{g},\partial_{\zeta})^{2}}+\frac{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g(\partial_{\zeta},\partial_{\zeta})}{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g(\nu_{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g},\partial_{\zeta})^{2}}

is also strictly less than
2+\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, and hence the uniform ellipticity of the operator
L
is obtained by Lemma
3.1
.

Since
X
is a closed manifold, so is
W
. Fix
\eta\ll 1,C
, and
p>n
. We then fix some
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
1+\alpha\geqslant 2-\frac{n}{p}
. Denote
C=C(W,g,n,p,L)
by the constant of
\mathcal{L}^{p}
elliptic regularity estimates with respect to
L
, and
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(W,g,n,p,\alpha)
by the constant of the Sobolev embedding
W^{2,p}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
over closed manifolds. Fix
\delta
such that
\delta CC^{\prime}<\eta
. Finally, we choose an associated
F
in the sense of Lemma
3.2
.

By assumption,
R_{g}\geqslant\kappa_{0}>0
on
M
for some positive constant
\kappa_{0}
, therefore
R_{\bar{g}}>0
on
M\times\mathbb{S}^{1}_{t}
, hence
R_{\bar{g}}|_{W}>0
uniformly. By the maximum principle for closed manifolds, the elliptic operator
L
is an injective operator. It follows that there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{1}(W,\sigma^{*}\bar{g})
of (
14
) by Fredholm dichotomy. By standard
H^{s}
-type elliptic theory and the smoothness of
F
,
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(W)
.

By standard
\mathcal{L}^{p}
-regularity theory
[
1
]
,

\lVert u\rVert_{W^{2,p}(W,\sigma^{*}\bar{g})}\leqslant C\left(\lVert F\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}(W,\sigma^{*}\bar{g})}+\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}(W,\sigma^{*}\bar{g})}\right).

Due to the injectivity of the operator, a very similar argument of
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
shows that
\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}(W,\sigma^{*}\bar{g})}
can be bounded above by
\lVert Lu\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}(W,\sigma^{*}\bar{g})}
. It follows that the
\mathcal{L}^{p}
estimates of our solution of (
14
) can be improved by

\lVert u\rVert_{W^{2,p}(W,\sigma^{*}\bar{g})}\leqslant C\lVert F\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}(W,\sigma^{*}\bar{g})}.

By Sobolev embedding, it follows that

\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(W)}\leqslant C^{\prime}\lVert u\rVert_{W^{2,p}(W,\sigma^{*}\bar{g})}\leqslant CC^{\prime}\lVert F\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}(W,\sigma^{*}\bar{g})}<CC^{\prime}\delta<\eta.

∎

5.
Hermitian Metric with Positive Chern Scalar Curvature on
X\times\mathbb{C}

Let
X
be a closed, complex manifold with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X\geqslant 1
. Throughout this section, we fix an almost complex structure
J_{0}
on

which comes from the complex structure. We denote by

the Chern scalar curvature with respect to the Hermitian metric
\omega

In this section, we show that the existence of Hermitian metric with positive Chern scalar curvature on noncompact manifolds of the type
X\times\mathbb{C}
by imposing some geometric conditions, provided that the complex manifold
(X\times\mathbb{C},J)
admits a Hermitian metric
\omega
whose background Riemannian metric

has uniformly positive Riemannian scalar curvature. This is a generalization of X.K. Yang’s result on closed Hermitian manifolds:

Theorem 5.1
.

[
16
, Corollary 3.9]

Let
(X,J_{0},\omega)
be a closed Hermitian manifold such that the background Riemannian metric
g
has quasi-positive Riemannian scalar curvature, then there exists a Hermitian metric
\tilde{\omega}
such that
S_{\tilde{\omega}}>0
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X\geqslant 1
. Assume that
(X\times\mathbb{C},J,\omega)
admits a complete Hermitian metric
\omega
whose background metric
g
is of bounded curvature, and such that
R_{g}\geqslant\kappa_{0}>0
for some
\kappa_{0}>0

(38)

\begin{split}&\frac{n-1}{n-2}\sec^{2}(\angle_{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g}(d\pi_{\xi})_{*}\nu_{g},\partial_{\zeta}))+\sec^{2}(\angle_{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g}(\nu_{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g},\partial_{\zeta}))\\
&\qquad=\frac{n-1}{n-2}\frac{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g(\partial_{\zeta},\partial_{\zeta})}{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g((d\pi_{\xi})_{*}\nu_{g},\partial_{\zeta})^{2}}+\frac{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g(\partial_{\zeta},\partial_{\zeta})}{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g(\nu_{\imath_{\xi}^{*}g},\partial_{\zeta})^{2}}<2+\frac{n-1}{n-2}\end{split}

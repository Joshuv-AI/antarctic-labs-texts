Blackwell without Priors
Maxwell Rosenthal

Abstract
This paper proposes a fully prior-free model of experimentation in which the decision maker observes the entire distribution of signals generated by a known experiment under an unknown distribution of the state of the world. One experiment is robustly more informative than another if the decision maker’s maxmin expected utility after observing the output of the former is always at least her maxmin expected utility after observing the latter. We show that this ranking holds if and only if the less informative experiment is a linear transformation of the more informative experiment; equivalently, the null space of the more informative experiment is a subset of the null space of the less informative experiment. Our criterion is implied by Blackwell’s order but does not imply it, and we show by example that our ranking admits strictly more comparable pairs of experiments than the classical ranking.



1
Introduction

This paper develops a fully prior-free model of experimentation in
which the decision maker (i) does not know the prior distribution

\mu
of the state variable; (ii) observes the entire distribution

E\mu
of signals generated by known Blackwell experiment
E
; and
(iii) ranks the actions in her action set
A
according to their
worst-case expected utility against the set of state distributions

\{\nu|E\nu=E\mu\}
that are consistent with the data
E\mu
.
If
E
has full rank then
\mu
is exactly identified; conversely,
if
E
does not have full rank then
\mu
is only partially identified.
We propose a partial ranking of experiments in which
E
is
robustly
more informative
than
E^{\prime}
if for every decision problem
(\mu,A)

the decision maker’s maxmin payoff after observing the distribution
of signals generated by
E
is at least her maxmin payoff after observing
the distribution of signals generated by
E^{\prime}
.

In our main result, we show that
E
is robustly more informative
than
E^{\prime}
if and only if
E^{\prime}
is a linear transformation of
E
.
While this implies immediately that
E
is robustly more informative
than
E^{\prime}
whenever the former is Blackwell more informative than
the latter, we show by counterexample that the converse does not hold.
In doing so, we establish that our order admits more comparable pairs
of experiments than Blackwell’s order.

2
Contribution and literature

This paper aims at making two contributions. First and foremost, we
provide a purely empirical take on the classical experimentation framework
developed in
Blackwell 1951
;
Blackwell 1953
.
We view our framework as a potential starting point for modeling robust
decision making in environments in which (i) there is broad uncertainty
about the prior distribution of payoff-relevant state variables and
(ii) there is opportunity to learn about the distribution of those
variables via observation of repeated draws from earlier experiments
or from the implementation of new experiments. To the extent that
fully informative experiments might be either unavailable or prohibitively
costly, it seems that a ranking of partially informative experiments
might be useful. In addition to the single-agent decision making problem
studied in this paper, it seems plausible that our framework might
be portable to robust contracting or robust mechanism design environments
in which agents are privately informed about the distribution of the
state variable.

Second, while our main result offers a richer ranking of experiments
than Blackwell’s classical theorem, it coincides with the original
ranking for Blackwell-comparable experiments. From that point of view,
our result might be viewed as providing a secondary criterion for
comparing the informativeness of two experiments when those experiments
are Blackwell-incomparable.

This paper contributes to a literature studying Blackwell-like environments
in which the decision maker is ambiguity averse. One strand of papers
(
Çelen 2012
;
Heyen and Wiesenfarth 2015
;
Li and Zhou 2016
)
extends Blackwell’s original order to decision makers with maxmin
and other ambiguity averse preferences. There, the agent updates a
set of priors using a single draw from the experiment. Here, there
are no priors and the decision maker uses the entire distribution
of output generated by the experiment. One recent paper (
Wang 2024
)
undertakes a similar exercise in which the experiment itself is unknown.
In contrast, our decision maker understands the experiment. Elsewhere,

Whitmeyer 2025
provides a general treatment of non-Bayesian
updating in experimental contexts. To the best of our knowledge, our
own paper is the first to propose an entirely prior free model in
which the decision maker observes the entire distribution
E\mu

of draws from the experiment, rather than a single draw.

3
Analysis

3.1
Setup

The state of the world is distributed within set
\{\omega_{1},...,\omega_{n}\}

according to unknown prior
\mu
.
Experiments
E
generate
signal realizations in set
\{\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{m}\}
, where
we identify
E
with the
m\times n
matrix satisfying
E_{mn}\equiv\text{Prob}(\sigma=\sigma_{t}|\omega=\omega_{n})
.

Actions

a
are real vectors of length
n
,
a_{i}
is the
agent’s payoff under action
a
in state
i
, and the agent’s action
set
A
is compact in the Euclidean topology.

3.2
Formalities

Given a matrix
A
, we write
A^{T}
for its transpose;
A_{i}

for its columns;
(A^{T})_{i}
for its rows;
\text{null}(A)\equiv\{x|Ax=0\}

for its nullspace; and
\text{row}(A)\equiv\{\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}A_{i}^{T}|\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}\in\mathbb{R}\}

for its rowspace. We call
A

column stochastic
if
\sum_{i}A_{ij}=1

for all columns
j
and
row stochastic
if
\sum_{j}A_{ij}=1

for all rows
i
. Further, we write
\langle x,y\rangle\equiv\sum_{i}x_{i}y_{i}

for the usual inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\Delta^{n}
for
the set of probabilities on
\{1,...,n\}
equipped with the Euclidean
topology, and
\delta_{i}
for the degenerate probability at state

\omega_{i}
. Where convenient, we identify
\omega_{i}
with its
index
i
.

3.3
Model

Rather than the usual setup in which the decision maker observes a
single draw from
E
and uses it to update her prior belief
\mu
,
in our model the decision maker (i) does not act on subjective prior
beliefs about the distribution of the state variable and (ii) observes
the entire distribution
E\mu
of signals generated by known experiment

E
under an unknown prior
\mu\in\Delta^{n}
. She views any distribution

\nu
with
E\nu=E\mu
as plausible, and chooses an action to maximize
her worst-case payoff against the set
\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}

of plausible priors. The
decision problem

(\mu,A)
is thus

\max_{a\in A}\;\min_{p\in\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}}\;\langle a,p\rangle.

Before proceeding to the full analysis of the model, we offer two
basic remarks about the extreme cases of this setup.

Remark 1
.

If
E
has rank
n
then
\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}=\{\mu\}

for all priors
\mu
. Conversely, if
E
does not have rank
n

and
\mu
has full support then
\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}

has a non-empty interior.

Proof.

If
E
has rank
n
then
\text{null}(E)=\{0\}
and
E\nu=E\mu

if and only if
\nu=\mu
. Conversely, if the rank of
E
is strictly
less than
n
then
\text{null}(E)
has nonzero dimension and thus
there exists a nonzero vector
d
such that
E(\mu+d)=E\mu
for
all priors
\mu
. If
\mu
has full support then for all
\lambda

sufficiently small in magnitude
\nu\equiv\mu+\lambda d
is a well-defined
probability satisfying
E\nu=E\mu
.
∎

Remark 2
.

If
E
has rank 1 then
\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}=\Delta^{n}
.
Conversely, if
E
does not have rank
1
then
\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}

is a proper subset of
\Delta^{n}
.

Proof.

If
E
has rank 1 then all of its columns are identical and the distribution
of output
E\mu
coincides with those columns for every prior
\mu
.
In that case,
\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}=\Delta^{n}
. Conversely,
if
E
has rank greater than
1
then there exist states
i,j

with
E_{i}\neq E_{j}
and hence at most one of
\delta_{i},\delta_{j}

is a solution to
Ex=E\mu
for any prior
\mu
.
∎

With those two remarks in hand, we proceed to our analysis of the
more interesting interior cases.

3.4
The main result

The goal of this paper is to characterize the following order.

Definition 1
.

Experiment
E
is
robustly more informative
than experiment

E^{\prime}
if for all decision problems
(\mu,A)

\max_{a\in A}\;\min_{p\in\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}}\langle a,p\rangle\geq\max_{a\in A}\;\min_{p\in\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E^{\prime}\nu=E^{\prime}\mu\}}\;\langle a,p\rangle.

In particular, we establish an equivalence with three other statements.

Theorem 1
.

The following four statements are equivalent:

(i)

there exists a matrix
\Gamma
such that
E^{\prime}=\Gamma E
;

(ii)

\text{null}(E)\subset\text{null}(E^{\prime})
;

(iii)

\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}\subset\{\nu|E^{\prime}\nu=E^{\prime}\mu\}

for all
\mu
in
\Delta^{n}
;

(iv)

experiment
E
is robustly more informative than experiment
E^{\prime}
.

We develop Theorem
1
as the implication of three standalone
lemmas. First, the first two criteria are equivalent.

Lemma 1
.

There exists a matrix
\Gamma
with
E^{\prime}=\Gamma E

if and only if
\text{null}(E)\subset\text{null}(E^{\prime})
.

Proof.

If
E^{\prime}=\Gamma E,Ex=0
then
E^{\prime}x=\Gamma Ex=\Gamma 0=0
and hence
\text{null}(E)\subset\text{null}(E^{\prime})
.
Conversely, if
\text{null}(E)\subset\text{null}(E^{\prime})
then the fundamental
theorem of linear algebra implies
\text{row}(E)\supset\text{row}(E^{\prime})

and thus for each
i=1,...,l
there exists a vector
(\gamma_{i1},...,\gamma_{im})

such that row vector
(E^{\prime})_{i}^{T}=\sum_{j}\gamma_{ij}E_{j}^{T}
.
Consequently,
E^{\prime}=\Gamma E
for
\Gamma_{ij}\equiv\gamma_{ij}
.
∎

Next, we show that the second criterion and the third criterion are
equivalent.

Lemma 2
.

Experiments
E,E^{\prime}
satisfy
\text{null}(E)\subset\text{null}(E^{\prime})

if and only if
\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}\subset\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E^{\prime}\nu=E^{\prime}\mu\}

for all
\mu
.

Proof.

If
\text{null}(E)\subset\text{null}(E^{\prime})
then the result is obvious.
Conversely,
d\in\text{null}(E)
if and only if
\lambda d\in\text{null}(E)

if and only if
\mu+\lambda d\in\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}

for
\mu
with full support and
\lambda
small. If
\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}\subset\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E^{\prime}\nu=E^{\prime}\mu\}

then
E^{\prime}(\mu+\lambda d)=E^{\prime}\mu
and hence
\lambda d\in\text{null}(E^{\prime})
.
∎

Finally, we show the substance of the equivalence between the third
criterion and our order.

Lemma 3
.

If
\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}\not\subset\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E^{\prime}\nu=E^{\prime}\mu\}

then there exists an action a and a prior
p\in\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}

such that
\langle a,p\rangle\ll\langle a,q\rangle
for all
q\in\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E^{\prime}\nu=E^{\prime}\mu\}
.

Proof.

Let
p\in\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}\setminus\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E^{\prime}\nu=E^{\prime}\mu\}
.
Because the sets
\{p\}
,
\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E^{\prime}\nu=E^{\prime}\mu\}

are convex, compact, and disjoint, the separating hyperplane theorem
provides a vector
a
and constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that
\langle a,q\rangle>c_{1}>c_{2}>\langle a,p\rangle

for all
q\in\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E^{\prime}\nu=E^{\prime}\mu\}
.
∎

Theorem
1
now follows quickly from Lemmas
1
–
3
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Lemma
1
and Lemma
2
imply (i), (ii), and
(iii) are equivalent. By Lemma
3
, the negation of (iii)
yields an prior
\mu
and an action
a
such that

\min_{p\in\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E\nu=E\mu\}}\;\langle a,p\rangle<\min_{p\in\{\nu\in\Delta^{n}|E^{\prime}\nu=E^{\prime}\mu\}}\;\langle a,p\rangle.

Consideration of decision problem
(\mu,\{a\})
implies that
E

is not robustly more informative than
E^{\prime}
. Conversely, if (iii)
holds it is apparent that (iv) holds as well.
∎

3.5
Robust informativeness is weaker than Blackwell

In this section, we establish the relationship between robust informativeness
and Blackwell informativeness.

Theorem 2
.

If
E
is Blackwell more informative than
E^{\prime}
then
E
is robustly
more informative than
E^{\prime}
.

Proof.

If
E
is Blackwell more informative than
E^{\prime}
then Blackwell’s
theorem implies there exists a row-stochastic matrix
\Gamma
such
that
E=\Gamma E^{\prime}
, satisfying criterion (i) of Theorem 1.
∎

Because the first criterion (i) does not necessarily yield a garbling
— in which
\Gamma
is required to be row stochastic — and
the existence of a garbling is a characterization of Blackwell informativeness,
the converse of Theorem 2 is not a logical implication of our first
theorem. We confirm via counterexample that the converse does not
hold.

Example 1
.

Consider column-stochastic matrices

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{bmatrix}0.8&0.2\\
0.2&0.8\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle E^{\prime}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}0.7&0.4\\
0.3&0.6\end{bmatrix}

and note that (i) both matrices have full rank and (ii)
E,E^{\prime}
have
inverses

\displaystyle E^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}4/3&-1/3\\
-1/3&4/3\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle(E^{\prime})^{-1}

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{bmatrix}2&-4/3\\
-1&7/3\end{bmatrix}.

Because

\displaystyle\Gamma\equiv(E^{\prime})(E^{-1})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0.8&0.3\\
0.2&0.7\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\prime}

\displaystyle\equiv E(E^{\prime})^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1.4&-0.6\\
-0.4&1.6\end{bmatrix}

is the only matrix
\Gamma
satisfying
E^{\prime}=\Gamma E
,
\Gamma^{\prime}

is the only matrix satisfying
E=\Gamma^{\prime}E^{\prime}
, and neither
\Gamma

nor
\Gamma^{\prime}
is row-stochastic,
E
and
E^{\prime}
are not Blackwell
comparable.

4
Conclusions

This paper proposes a fully prior-free approach to experiments on
the basis of their informativeness. We develop a partial ranking of
experiments on the basis of how valuable repeated observation of their
output is to a maxmin decision maker with no prior information about
the distribution of the state variable. Our order is implied by but
does not imply Blackwell’s order, and thus offers a richer set of
comparisons than its classical counterpart.

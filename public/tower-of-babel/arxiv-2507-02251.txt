2.
A Generalized Birman–Schwinger Principle

In this section we derive a generalized Birman–Schwinger principle that is applicable to Schrödinger operators with distributional potential coefficients.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a separable (complex) Hilbert space with inner product
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{{\mathcal{H}}}
, and assume that
H_{0}
is a nonnegative self-adjoint operator in
{\mathcal{H}}
with domain
\dom(H_{0})
.

Convention.
We fix a branch of the complex square root function as follows: If
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\{0\}
with
z=|z|e^{i\arg(z)}
and
\arg(z)\in[0,2\pi)
, then
z^{1/2}=|z|^{1/2}e^{i\arg(z)/2}
(here
|z|^{1/2}
denotes the nonnegative square root of
|z|
and
0^{1/2}:=0
). The branch so chosen is analytic in
{\mathbb{C}}\backslash[0,\infty)
and continuous in
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
up to the cut along
[0,\infty)
.
\triangleleft

Let
\{E_{H_{0}}(\lambda)\}_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}}
denote the strongly right-continuous spectral family uniquely associated to
H_{0}
by the spectral theorem, so that

H_{0}=\int_{\sigma(H_{0})}\lambda\,dE_{H_{0}}(\lambda).

(2.1)

Square roots are then defined as follows (cf., e.g.,
[
56
, VI.5.2]
)

\displaystyle\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\sigma(H_{0})}(\lambda-z)^{1/2}\,dE_{H_{0}}(\lambda),\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\sigma(H_{0})}\frac{1}{(\lambda-z)^{1/2}}\,dE_{H_{0}}(\lambda),\quad z\in\rho(H_{0}).

(2.3)

In particular, (
2.2
) implies stability of square root domains in the following form:

\dom\big((H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{1/2}\big)=\dom\big(H_{0}^{1/2}\big),\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}.

(2.4)

Introducing the Hilbert space
({\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0}),(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})})
via,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})=\dom\big(H_{0}^{1/2}\big),

(2.5)

\displaystyle(f,g)_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})}:=\big(H_{0}^{1/2}f,H_{0}^{1/2}g\big)_{{\mathcal{H}}}+(f,g)_{{\mathcal{H}}},\quad f,g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})=\dom\big(H_{0}^{1/2}\big),

one notes that

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})},\quad f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0}).

(2.6)

Since
H_{0}^{1/2}
is self-adjoint and hence closed in
{\mathcal{H}}
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})
equipped with the inner product
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})}
defined in (
2.5
) is a Hilbert space. Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0}):=[{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})]^{*}
denote the conjugate dual space of bounded conjugate linear functionals on
{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})
.

If
f\in{\mathcal{H}}
, then the map
\Psi_{f}:{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined by

\Psi_{f}(g)=(g,f)_{{\mathcal{H}}},\quad g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0}),

(2.7)

is conjugate linear and satisfies

|\Psi_{f}(g)|\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\|g\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})},\quad g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0}).

(2.8)

In particular,
\Psi_{f}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})
. Therefore, identifying
\Psi_{f}
and
f
,
{\mathcal{H}}
may be regarded as a subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})
and

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})}=\big\|(H_{0}+I_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1/2}f\big\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}},\quad f\in{\mathcal{H}}.

(2.9)

In addition, one has compatibility between the pairing

{}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})}\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,_{f}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})}
and the scalar product
(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{{\mathcal{H}}}
in
{\mathcal{H}}
via

{}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})}\langle g,\Psi_{f}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})}=(g,f)_{{\mathcal{H}}},\quad g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0}),\,f\in{\mathcal{H}}.

(2.10)

A possible way to describe the Hilbert space
({\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0}),(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})})
is then given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})=[{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})]^{*},

\displaystyle(f,g)_{{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})}:=\big((H_{0}+I_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1/2}f,(H_{0}+I_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1/2}g\big)_{{\mathcal{H}}},

(2.11)

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464ptf,g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})=[{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})]^{*}.

Thus, in this manner, one arrives at the inclusion (a scale) of spaces:

{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})\subset{\mathcal{H}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})=[{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})]^{*},

(2.12)

in which

\begin{split}\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}&\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})},\quad f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0}),\\
\|g\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})}&\leqslant\|g\|_{{\mathcal{H}}},\quad g\in{\mathcal{H}}.\end{split}

(2.13)

If
z\in\rho(H_{0})
, then
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
. In fact,
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}
maps
{\mathcal{H}}
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})
as a bounded map between Hilbert spaces.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
z\in\rho(H_{0})
, then
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0}))
and

\big\|\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0}))}\leqslant M(z),

(2.14)

where

M(z):=\big\|H_{0}^{1/2}\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}+\big\|\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})},\quad z\in\rho(H_{0}).

(2.15)

Proof.

Let
z\in\rho(H_{0})
. If
f\in{\mathcal{H}}
, then
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}f\in\dom\big(H_{0}^{1/2}\big)={\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})
, and the definition of
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})}
implies

\displaystyle\big\|\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}f\big\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})}\leqslant M(z)\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}.

(2.16)

∎

For
z\in\rho(H_{0})
, the operator
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}
may be extended from
{\mathcal{H}}
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})
as follows. For
\Psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})
, consider the map
\widetilde{\Psi}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined by

\widetilde{\Psi}(g)={}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})}\big\langle\big(H_{0}-\overline{z}I_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}g,\Psi\big\rangle{}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})},\quad g\in{\mathcal{H}}.

(2.17)

The map
\widetilde{\Psi}
is a conjugate linear functional on
{\mathcal{H}}
, and

\big|\widetilde{\Psi}(g)\big|\leqslant\|\Psi\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})}\big\|\big(H_{0}-\overline{z}I_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}g\big\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{+1}(H_{0})}\leqslant M(\overline{z})\|\Psi\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})}\|g\|_{{\mathcal{H}}},\quad g\in{\mathcal{H}},

(2.18)

where
M(\overline{z})
is the constant defined by (
2.15
).

The inequality in (
2.18
) shows that
\widetilde{\Psi}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
. By the Riesz representation theorem,
\widetilde{\Psi}
may be identified with a vector
f_{\Psi}\in{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\big\|\widetilde{\Psi}\big\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{*}}=\|f_{\Psi}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}
. Finally, one defines

\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\Psi=f_{\Psi}.

(2.19)

Combining (
2.18
) and (
2.19
), one obtains:

\big\|\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\Psi\big\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\leqslant M(\overline{z})\|\Psi\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})},\quad\Psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0}).

(2.20)

Thus,
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0}),{\mathcal{H}})
. Furthermore, one verifies that if
f\in{\mathcal{H}}
is identified with
\Psi_{f}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})
(cf. (
2.7
)), then
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\Psi_{f}=\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}f
. That is, the map
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}:{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})\to{\mathcal{H}}
defined above is an extension of the original map
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
given by (
2.2
). These considerations may be summarized as follows.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
z\in\rho(H_{0})
, then
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}:{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})\to{\mathcal{H}}
defined by (
2.19
) is an extension of
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
. Furthermore, this extension satisfies

\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0}),{\mathcal{H}})

(2.21)

and

\big\|\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0}),{\mathcal{H}})}\leqslant M(\overline{z}),\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash[0,\infty).

(2.22)

Proof.

That
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}:{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}(H_{0})\to{\mathcal{H}}
defined by (
2.19
) is an extension of
\big(H_{0}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
satisfying (
2.21
) has already been shown. The norm bound in (
2.22
) immediately follows from (
2.20
).
∎

and

.

(ii)

Application: One-Dimensional Schrödinger Operators

with
H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
Potentials

If
V\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
, then
V
is a Sobolev multiplier from
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
to
H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
. (For a precise definition of Sobolev multiplier, see Definition
A.5
. A proof of the Sobolev multiplier property of distributions
V\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
is provided, for completeness, in Proposition
A.7
.) Hence,

V\in{\mathcal{B}}\big(H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})\big)

(3.1)

and (
A.34
) holds. In addition, Propositions
A.2
and
A.3
imply:

\begin{split}&\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}\big(H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big),\\
&\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\big),\quad\kappa\in(0,\infty),\end{split}

(3.2)

where
H_{0}
is defined according to (cf. (
A.6
))

H_{0}f=-f^{\prime\prime},\quad f\in\dom(H_{0})=H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}).

(3.3)

In consequence, (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) combine to yield:

\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big),\quad\kappa\in(0,\infty),

(3.4)

with

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant M(\kappa)\|V\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}))},\quad\kappa\in(0,\infty),

and

\displaystyle M(\kappa):

\displaystyle=\Big\|\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big\|\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}))},\quad\kappa\in(0,\infty).

(3.6)

As a map from
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, the free resolvent
\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1}
,
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
, is an integral operator with integral kernel given by the convolution kernel

G_{0}(-\kappa^{2},x,y)=\frac{1}{2\kappa}e^{-\kappa|x-y|},\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad\kappa\in(0,\infty).

(3.7)

In addition, the square root
\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}
is also an integral operator with a convolution (integral) kernel given by

G_{0}^{1/2}(-\kappa^{2},x,y)=\pi^{-1}H_{0}^{(1)}(i\kappa|x-y|),\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad\kappa\in(0,\infty),

(3.8)

with
H_{0}^{(1)}(\,\cdot\,)
denoting the Hankel function of the first kind and order zero (cf.
[
1
, Sect. 9.1]
).

The result in (
3.4
) may be considerably strengthened as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
V\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
, then

\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big),\quad\kappa\in(0,\infty),

(3.9)

and

\begin{split}\Big\|\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}^{2}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\big|\widehat{V}(\xi)\big|^{2}}{\xi^{2}+4\kappa^{2}}\,d\xi,&\\
\kappa\in(0,\infty).&\end{split}

(3.10)

In particular,

\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\Big\|\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}=0.

(3.11)

Moreover, by (
3.10
), the Hilbert–Schmidt property in (
3.9
) holds if and only if
V\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Proof.

Let
V\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
. We begin by proving (
3.9
) under the stronger assumption that
V\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
. Assuming that
V\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
and factoring
V
according to

V=uv,\quad v=|V|^{1/2},\quad u=|V|^{1/2}e^{i\arg(V)},

(3.12)

one infers that
u,v,(|\,\cdot\,|^{2}+\kappa^{2})^{-1/2}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Hence,
[
120
, Ch. 4]
implies

v\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2},\,\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}u\in{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big),

(3.13)

and as a consequence,

\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big)\subset{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big).

(3.14)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\tr_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\Big(\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V^{*}\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad=\tr_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\Big(V^{*}\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1}\Big).

(3.15)

The final equality in (
3.15
) is an application of the cyclicity property of the trace. Using (
3.7
), the trace in (
3.15
) may be computed as follows:

\displaystyle\tr_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\Big(V^{*}\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\kappa}\Big(V,\big(H_{0}+4\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1}V\Big)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\big|\widehat{V}(\xi)\big|^{2}}{\xi^{2}+4\kappa^{2}}\,d\xi,

(3.16)

where the final equality in (
3.16
) in an application of Plancherel’s identity. The result of (
3.16
) in (
3.15
) is

\Big\|\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}^{2}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\big|\widehat{V}(\xi)\big|^{2}}{\xi^{2}+4\kappa^{2}}\,d\xi.

(3.17)

Returning to arbitrary
V\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
, the idea is to now approximate
V
by functions in
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
and appeal to (
3.17
). Using the fact that
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
is dense in
H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
(see, e.g.,
[
37
, Proposition 3.17]
), let
\{V_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
be a sequence with the property that
\|V_{n}-V\|_{H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. By (
3.17
),

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}(V_{n}-V_{m})\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\big|\widehat{V}_{n}(\xi)-\widehat{V}_{m}(\xi)\big|^{2}}{\xi^{2}+4\kappa^{2}}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\frac{c(\kappa)}{\kappa}\|V_{n}-V_{m}\|_{H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2},\quad m,n\in{\mathbb{N}},

(3.18)

where

. In particular, the estimate in (
3.18
) implies that the sequence

\Big\{\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V_{n}\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\Big\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

(3.19)

) in
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big)

is taken to be consistent with the sign convention in (
2.25

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}(V_{n}-V)\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}

where

is the constant in (
3.6

3.20

\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}.

(3.21)

Since convergence in

\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}=-A_{V}(-\kappa^{2})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big).

(3.22)

Finally, (
3.17

\begin{split}&\Big\|\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}V_{n}\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}^{2}\\
&\quad=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\big|\widehat{V}_{n}(\xi)\big|^{2}}{\xi^{2}+4\kappa^{2}}\,d\xi,\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.\end{split}

(3.23)

Taking

3.10

To prove (

0\leqslant\frac{\xi^{2}+1}{\xi^{2}+4\kappa^{2}}\leqslant 1,\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}},

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\big|\widehat{V}(\xi)\big|^{2}}{\xi^{2}+4\kappa^{2}}\,d\xi\leqslant\|V\|_{H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2},\quad\kappa\in[1/2,\infty).

(3.25)

Thus, (

in (

Proposition 3.4
.

If
\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\kappa\in(0,\infty)

\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{d\xi}{\big[\xi^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big]\big[(\eta-\xi)^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big]}=\frac{1}{\eta^{2}+4\kappa^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
I_{\kappa}(\eta)
,
\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}
, denote the integral on the left-hand side of the equality in (

is a convolution:

I_{\kappa}(\eta)=\bigg[\frac{1}{|\,\cdot\,|^{2}+\kappa^{2}}\ast\frac{1}{|\,\cdot\,|^{2}+\kappa^{2}}\bigg](\eta),\quad\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(3.34)

By the convolution theorem expressed in the form
f\ast g=(2\pi)^{1/2}{\mathcal{F}}\big[{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f\cdot{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g\big]
, (
3.34
) may be recast as

I_{\kappa}(\,\cdot\,)=(2\pi)^{1/2}{\mathcal{F}}\Bigg\{{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\bigg\{\frac{1}{|\,\cdot\,|^{2}+\kappa^{2}}\bigg\}\cdot{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\bigg\{\frac{1}{|\,\cdot\,|^{2}+\kappa^{2}}\bigg\}\Bigg\}.

(3.35)

One computes:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\bigg\{\frac{1}{|\,\cdot\,|^{2}+\kappa^{2}}\bigg\}(x)=\bigg(\frac{2}{\pi}\bigg)^{1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(|x|\xi)}{\xi^{2}+\kappa^{2}}\,d\xi=\bigg(\frac{\pi}{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{e^{-\kappa|x|}}{\kappa},

(3.36)

where the final equality in (
3.36
) follows from an application of
[
34
, 3.723.2]
. Thus, by (
3.35
) and (
3.36
),

\displaystyle I_{\kappa}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{3/2}}{4\kappa^{2}}{\mathcal{F}}\big\{e^{-2\kappa|\,\cdot\,|}\big\}(\eta)=\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}\frac{1}{\eta^{2}+4\kappa^{2}},\quad\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(3.37)

The Fourier transform in (
3.37
) may be deduced from (
3.36
) or, alternatively, directly computed as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}\big\{e^{-2\kappa|\,\cdot\,|}\big\}(\eta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\cdot 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(|\eta|x)e^{-2\kappa x}\,dx=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\frac{4\kappa}{\eta^{2}+4\kappa^{2}},\quad\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(3.38)

{\mathcal{F}}A_{V}(-\kappa^{2}){\mathcal{F}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\Big\{\overline{\widehat{V}(\xi^{\prime\prime}-\xi)}\big((\xi^{\prime\prime})^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big)^{-1}\widehat{V}(\xi^{\prime\prime}-\xi^{\prime})\big((\xi^{\prime})^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big)^{-1/2}\Big\}\,d\xi^{\prime\prime},\quad\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Hence, one calculates:

\displaystyle\big\|A_{V}(-\kappa^{2})\big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}^{2}=\tr_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big(A_{V}(-\kappa^{2})^{*}A_{V}(-\kappa^{2})\big)

\displaystyle\quad=\tr_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big({\mathcal{F}}A_{V}(-\kappa^{2})^{*}A_{V}(-\kappa^{2}){\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\Bigg\{\big(\xi^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big)^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\big|\widehat{V}(\eta)\big|^{2}}{(\eta+\xi)^{2}+\kappa^{2}}\,d\eta\Bigg\}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\Bigg\{\big|\widehat{V}(\eta)\big|^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{d\xi}{\big[\xi^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big]\big[(\eta-\xi)^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big]}\Bigg\}\,d\eta=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\big|\widehat{V}(\eta)\big|^{2}}{\eta^{2}+\kappa^{2}}\,d\eta.

(3.47)

The final equality in (
3.47
) follows from Proposition
3.4
.

Remark 3.6
.

) given in the proof of Proposition

\displaystyle\tr_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big(A_{V}(-\kappa^{2})^{*}A_{V}(-\kappa^{2})\big)=\frac{1}{4\kappa^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}}\overline{V(x)}e^{-2\kappa|x-x^{\prime}|}V(x^{\prime})

(3.48)

In (

), one uses the Fourier transform of

,

, given by (

The following chain of results, culminating with Lemma
3.9
, is used to justify the calculation in (
3.48
). We present these results here for completeness. We recall the convolution theorem for Schwartz functions in the following form (see, e.g.,
[
101
, Theorem IX.3]
).

Lemma 3.7
.

If
f,g\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
, then

{\mathcal{F}}(f\ast g)=(2\pi)^{1/2}({\mathcal{F}}f)\cdot({\mathcal{F}}g)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(f\ast g)=(2\pi)^{1/2}({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f)\cdot({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g).

(3.49)

In addition, we recall that for
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
,
\widehat{f}
is continuous with
\lim_{|\xi|\to\infty}f(\xi)=0
and

\big\|\widehat{f}\,\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}).

(3.50)

The following result is an extension of Lemma
3.7
.

Theorem 3.8
.

The following statements hold.

(i)
If
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, then
f\ast g\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and

f\ast g=(2\pi)^{1/2}{\mathcal{F}}\big\{({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f)\cdot({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g)\big\}.

(3.51)

(ii)
If
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and
g\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, then
f\ast g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and

\widehat{f\ast g}=(2\pi)^{1/2}\widehat{f}\widehat{g}.

(3.52)

Proof.

To prove
(i)
, let
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Young’s inequality with
p=q=2
and
r=\infty
implies
f\ast g\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
. It remains to prove (
3.51
). Since
{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f,{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, Hölder’s inequality implies
\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f\big)\cdot\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g\big)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. Thus,
{\mathcal{F}}\big\{\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f\big)\cdot\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g\big)\big\}
is well-defined and belongs to
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
. To prove the equality in (
3.51
), choose sequences
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|f_{j}-f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=0=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|g_{j}-g\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}.

(3.53)

Applying the inequality in (
3.50
), Hölder’s inequality, and the unitary property of
{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}
, one obtains

\displaystyle\big\|{\mathcal{F}}\big\{\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f_{j}\big)\cdot\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g_{j}\big)\big\}-{\mathcal{F}}\big\{\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f\big)\cdot\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g\big)\big\}\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\big\|\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f_{j}\big)\cdot\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g_{j}\big)-\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f\big)\cdot\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g\big)\big\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\|f_{j}-f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g_{j}-g\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(3.54)

In particular, (
3.54
) implies

\lim_{j\to\infty}\big\|{\mathcal{F}}\big\{\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f_{j}\big)\cdot\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g_{j}\big)\big\}-{\mathcal{F}}\big\{\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f\big)\cdot\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g\big)\big\}\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}=0,

(3.55)

or equivalently, by Lemma
3.7
,

\lim_{j\to\infty}\big\|(2\pi)^{-n/2}f_{j}\ast g_{j}-{\mathcal{F}}\big\{\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f\big)\cdot\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g\big)\big\}\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}=0.

(3.56)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|(f_{j}\ast g_{j})-(f\ast g)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\|(f_{j}-f)\ast g_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}+\|f\ast(g_{j}-g)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\|f_{j}-f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g_{j}-g\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}},

(3.57)

which implies

\lim_{j\to\infty}\big\|(2\pi)^{-n/2}(f_{j}\ast g_{j})-(2\pi)^{-n/2}(f\ast g)\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}=0.

(3.58)

By uniqueness of limits, (
3.56
) and (
3.58
) imply (
3.51
).

The proof of
(ii)
is entirely analogous to the proof of
(i)
, with only minor modifications required. For
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and
g\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, Young’s inequality with
p=2
,
q=1
, and
r=2
implies
f\ast g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. In particular
{\mathcal{F}}(f\ast g)
is well-defined and belongs to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. One chooses
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|f_{j}-f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=0=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|g_{j}-g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}.

(3.59)

Therefore,
f_{j}\ast g_{j}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{F}}(f_{j}\ast g_{j})-{\mathcal{F}}(f\ast j)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\quad=\|(f_{j}\ast g_{j})-(f\ast j)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\|(f_{j}-f)\ast g_{j}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}+\|f\ast(g_{j}-g)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}

(3.60)

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\|f_{j}-f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g_{j}-g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

In addition,

\displaystyle\big\|({\mathcal{F}}f_{j})({\mathcal{F}}g_{j})-({\mathcal{F}}f)({\mathcal{F}}g)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\big\|\big({\mathcal{F}}f_{j}-{\mathcal{F}}f\big)({\mathcal{F}}g_{j})\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}+\big\|({\mathcal{F}}f)({\mathcal{F}}g_{j}-{\mathcal{F}}g)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\big\|f_{j}-f\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big\|g_{j}-g\big\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(3.61)

Theorem
3.7
implies

,

. Therefore, (
3.60
) and (
3.61
), combined with uniqueness of limits, yield (
3.52
).

Lemma 3.9
.

Appendix A
Some Basic Facts on Sobolev Multipliers

In this section, we recall some fundamental results on Sobolev multipliers. The Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing functions on
{\mathbb{R}}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
, and
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
denotes the corresponding set of tempered distributions. The convention employed for the Fourier transform is:

\begin{split}({\mathcal{F}}f)(\xi)\equiv\widehat{f}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}f(x)\,dx\,\text{ for a.e.~$\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}$},\\
\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f\big)(x)\equiv\widecheck{f}(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}f(\xi)\,d\xi\,\text{ for a.e.~$x\in{\mathbb{R}}$}.\end{split}

(A.1)

In addition, the convolution is defined according to

(f\ast g)(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(y)g(x-y)\,dy=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(x-y)g(y)\,dy,

(A.2)

and Young’s inequality holds in the following form.

Theorem A.1

(Young’s Inequality)
.

Let
1\leqslant p,q,r\leqslant\infty
with
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1+r^{-1}
. If
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
and
g\in L^{q}({\mathbb{R}})
then
f\ast g\in L^{r}({\mathbb{R}})
and

\|f\ast g\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}})}\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}})}.

(A.3)

One notes that Young’s inequality implies the following inclusions:

\displaystyle L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\ast L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),\quad L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\ast L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),\quad L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\ast L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}).

For each
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, the space of Bessel potentials
H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}})
is introduced as follows:

H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}})=\bigg\{T\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})\,\bigg|\,\Big[\big(1+|\cdot|^{2}\big)^{s/2}\widehat{T}\,\Big]^{\vee}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})\bigg\},\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

(A.4)

where the symbols
\widehat{}
and
\widecheck{}
denote the Fourier and inverse Fourier transforms of the distributions involved and
\big(1+|\cdot|^{2}\big)^{s/2}
denotes the operator of multiplication by the smooth function
{\mathbb{R}}\ni x\mapsto\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{s/2}
. The space
H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}})
is equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}})}
defined by

\|T\|_{H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}})}=\bigg\|\Big[\big(1+|\cdot|^{2}\big)^{s/2}\widehat{T}\,\Big]^{\vee}\bigg\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad T\in H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}),\,s\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(A.5)

In the special case
p=2
, the shorthand notation
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}):=H^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}})
will be used. Letting
H_{0}
denote the self-adjoint realization of
-d^{2}/dx^{2}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
; that is,

H_{0}f=-f^{\prime\prime},\quad f\in\dom(H_{0})=H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),

(A.6)

so that
\dom\big(H_{0}^{1/2}\big)=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, one verifies, upon comparison with the notation used in Section
2
(cf. (
2.5
), (
2.11
)), that

H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})={\mathcal{H}}_{s}\big(H_{0}^{1/2}\big),\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(A.7)

If
s>0
and
f\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
, then
\big(1+|\cdot|^{2}\big)^{s/2}\widehat{f}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, so there exists
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
such that

{}_{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})}\Big\langle\varphi,\big(1+|\cdot|^{2}\big)^{s/2}\widehat{f}\,\Big\rangle{}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\overline{\varphi(x)}g(x)\,dx,\quad\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}).

(A.8)

Furthermore,
\big(1+|\cdot|^{2}\big)^{-s/2}g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\overline{\varphi(x)}g(x)\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{-s/2}\,dx

\displaystyle={}_{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})}\Big\langle\big(1+|\cdot|^{2}\big)^{-s/2}\varphi,\big(1+|\cdot|^{2}\big)^{s/2}\widehat{f}\,\Big\rangle{}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle={}_{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})}\big\langle\varphi,\widehat{f}\,\big\rangle{}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}),

(A.9)

so that
\widehat{f}=\big(1+|\cdot|^{2}\big)^{-s/2}g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and, consequently,
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. In addition,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=\big\|\widehat{f}\,\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\leqslant\Big\|\big(1+|\cdot|^{2}\big)^{s/2}\widehat{f}\,\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=\|f\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})}.

(A.10)

Hence, one obtains the dense and continuous embedding

H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),\quad s\in(0,\infty),

(A.11)

with the inequality

\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\leqslant\|f\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad f\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}).

(A.12)

Proposition A.2
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
, then

\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}\big(H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big).

(A.13)

Proof.

In order to first show that
\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}
maps from
H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, let
f\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
so that
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\big(1+|\cdot|^{2}\big)^{-1/2}\widehat{f}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big[\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}f\Big]\;\widehat{}=\big(|\cdot|^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big)^{-1/2}\widehat{f}\\
&\quad=\big(|\cdot|^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big)^{-1/2}\big(|\cdot|^{2}+1\big)^{1/2}\big(|\cdot|^{2}+1\big)^{-1/2}\widehat{f}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),\end{split}

(A.14)

where the
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
containment follows from the fact that
\big(|\,\cdot\,|^{2}+1\big)^{-1/2}\widehat{f}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and the (smooth) function
{\mathbb{R}}\ni x\mapsto\big(|x|^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big)^{-1/2}\big(|x|^{2}+1\big)^{1/2}
is bounded. Therefore,
\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
follows by taking inverse Fourier transforms. The boundedness property is a consequence of Plancherel’s theorem and the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big\|\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}f\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=\Big\|\big(|\cdot|^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big)^{-1/2}\widehat{f}\,\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\\
&\quad\leqslant C(\kappa)\Big\|\big(|\cdot|^{2}+1\big)^{-1/2}\widehat{f}\,\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=C(\kappa)\|f\|_{H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad f\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}),\end{split}

(A.15)

where

C(\kappa):=\Big\|\big(|\cdot|^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big)^{-1/2}\big(|\cdot|^{2}+1\big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}=\begin{cases}\kappa^{-1},&\kappa\in(0,1),\\
1,&\kappa\in[1,\infty).\end{cases}

(A.16)

∎

Proposition A.3
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
, then

\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\big).

(A.17)

Proof.

To first show that
\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}
maps from
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
to
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. One then calculates

\displaystyle\big(|\cdot|^{2}+1\big)^{1/2}\Big[\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}f\Big]\;\widehat{}

\displaystyle\quad=\big(|\cdot|^{2}+1\big)^{1/2}\big(|\cdot|^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big)^{-1/2}\widehat{f}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),

(A.18)

where the
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
containment follows from the fact that
\widehat{f}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and the function
{\mathbb{R}}\ni x\mapsto\big(|x|^{2}+1\big)^{1/2}\big(|x|^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big)^{-1/2}
is bounded. Therefore, the inclusion
\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}f\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
holds. The boundedness property follows from (repeated use of) Plancherel’s theorem:

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(H_{0}+\kappa^{2}I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}f\Big\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}=\Big\|\big(|\cdot|^{2}+1\big)^{1/2}\big(|\cdot|^{2}+\kappa^{2}\big)^{-1/2}\widehat{f}\,\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant C(\kappa)\big\|\widehat{f}\,\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=C(\kappa)\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),

(A.19)

with
C(\kappa)
defined by (
A.16
).
∎

Remark A.4
.

With only minor modification, the same arguments used to prove Propositions
A.2
and
A.3
show that

\begin{split}&\big(H_{0}-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}\big(H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big),\\
&\big(H_{0}-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\big)^{-1/2}\in{\mathcal{B}}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\big),\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash[0,\infty).\end{split}

(A.20)

In particular,

H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})^{*}\hookrightarrow H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})=\big[H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\big]^{*}.

(A.21)

Next, we recall the concept of a Sobolev multiplier from
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
to
H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
. If
V\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
, then
V
is a multiplier for
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
in the following sense: If
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
, then
V\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
, where
V\varphi
is defined according to

\displaystyle{}_{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})}\langle\psi,V\varphi\rangle_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})}:={}_{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})}\langle\overline{\varphi}\psi,V\rangle_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}).

(A.22)

In particular,
\mathfrak{q}_{V}
defined by

\mathfrak{q}_{V}[\psi,\varphi]:={}_{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})}\langle\psi,V\varphi\rangle_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}),

(A.23)

is a well-defined sesquilinear form on
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Definition A.5
.

The tempered distribution
V\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
is called a Sobolev multiplier from
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
to
H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
if
\mathfrak{q}_{V}
, defined on
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
by (
A.23
), continuously extends from
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
to
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
; that is, if

|\mathfrak{q}_{V}[\psi,\varphi]|\leqslant C_{V}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}),

(A.24)

for some
C_{V}\in(0,\infty)
.

One notes that when (
A.24
) holds,
\mathfrak{q}_{V}
may be extended to a bounded sesquilinear form, which we denote by
\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{V}
, on
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
by taking

\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{V}[g,f]:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathfrak{q}_{V}[\psi_{n},\varphi_{n}],\quad f,g\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),

(A.25)

where
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\{\psi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
are any sequences such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\varphi_{n}-f\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\psi_{n}-g\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}=0.

(A.26)

The definition in (
A.25
) is independent of the sequences
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\{\psi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
chosen and (
A.24
) remains valid for the extension:

|\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{V}[g,f]|\leqslant C_{V}\|g\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|f\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad f,g\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}).

(A.27)

In the following, the symbol
{\mathcal{M}}\big(H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})\big)
denotes the set of all Sobolev multipliers from
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
to
H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
.

If
V\in{\mathcal{M}}\big(H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})\big)
, then the distributional pairing on the left-hand side in (
A.22
) extends to
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
as follows:

{}_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\langle g,Vf\rangle{}_{H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{V}[g,f],\quad f,g\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}).

(A.28)

The multiplier operator defined by

H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\ni f\mapsto Vf\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})

(A.29)

is linear and bounded by (
A.27
) since

\|Vf\|_{H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})}=\sup_{g\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{|\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}_{V}[g,f]|}{\|g\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}}\leqslant C_{V}\|f\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})},\quad f\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}).

(A.30)

In particular, the operator of multiplication by
V
is bounded from
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
to
H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
with

\|V\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}))}\leqslant C_{V}.

(A.31)

We note that, in a slight abuse of notation, we use the symbol “
V

A.31
) (and elsewhere) to denote the operator of multiplication by
V\in{\mathcal{M}}\big(H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})\big)

Every
V\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
is a Sobolev multiplier from
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
to
H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
; that is,
V
belongs to
{\mathcal{M}}\big(H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})\big)
. This fact, which we prove for completeness below in Proposition
A.7
, relies on the following algebraic property of the
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
norm.

Lemma A.6
.

If
\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
, then
\|\varphi\psi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\leqslant 2\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}
.

Proof.

The one-dimensional Sobolev inequality (see, e.g.,
[
70
, Theorem 8.5]
) yields:

2\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\leqslant\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2},\quad\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}).

(A.32)

If
\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
, then basic estimates combined with (
A.32
) yield:

\displaystyle\|\varphi\psi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\big\{|\varphi\psi|^{2}+|(\varphi\psi)^{\prime}|^{2}\big\}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\big\{2|\psi|^{2}\big[|\varphi|^{2}+|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}\big]+2|\varphi|^{2}\big[|\psi|^{2}+|\psi^{\prime}|^{2}\big]\big\}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\big\{\|\psi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\big[|\varphi|^{2}+|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}\big]+\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\big[|\psi|^{2}+|\psi^{\prime}|^{2}\big]\big\}\,dx

\displaystyle=2\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}.

(A.33)

∎

Proposition A.7
.

If
V\in H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})
, then
V\in{\mathcal{M}}\big(H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})\big)
and

\|V\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}))}\leqslant 2^{1/2}\|V\|_{H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})}.

(A.34)

Proof.

If
\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}})
, then Lemma
A.6
(applied to
\overline{\psi}
and
\varphi
) implies

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{q}_{V}[\psi,\varphi]|

\displaystyle=\big|{}_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\langle\overline{\varphi}\psi,V\rangle_{H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})}\big|\leqslant\|V\|_{H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|\overline{\varphi}\psi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{1/2}\|V\|_{H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}.

(A.35)

Hence, (
A.24
) holds with
C_{V}=2^{1/2}\|V\|_{H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}})}
. Finally, (
A.34
) follows from (
A.31
).
∎

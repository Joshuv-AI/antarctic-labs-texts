Primary charge- 4 ​ e 4e superconductivity from doping a featureless Mott insulator
Zhi-Qiang Gao, Yan-Qi Wang, Ya-Hui Zhang, Hui Yang

Abstract
Superconductivity is usually understood as a phase in which charge- 2 ​ e 2e Cooper pairs are condensed. Charge- 4 ​ e 4e superconductivity has largely been discussed as a vestigial order at finite temperature emerging from charge- 2 ​ e 2e states. Primary charge- 4 ​ e 4e superconducting phases at zero temperature remain scarce in both experiments and microscopic models. Here we argue that a doped featureless Mott insulator with S ​ U ​ ( 4 ) SU(4) symmetry provides a natural platform for primary charge- 4 ​ e 4e superconductivity, based on perturbative renormalization group arguments and group theoretic considerations. As a concrete realization, we construct a bilayer Hubbard model with tunable onsite S ​ U ​ ( 4 ) SU(4) and S ​ p ​ ( 4 ) Sp(4) symmetries that exhibits a featureless Mott insulating phase at half filling. Its low energy physics is captured by a generalized ESD model, featuring an effective Hamiltonian that is purely kinetic within the constrained Hilbert space. Using density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) simulations, we find a primary charge- 4 ​ e 4e superconducting phase in the S ​ U ​ ( 4 ) SU(4) ESD model and a conventional primary charge- 2 ​ e 2e phase in the S ​ p ​ ( 4 ) Sp(4) case. We further characterize the corresponding normal states and discuss the resulting finite temperature phase diagram.

Conclusions and outlooks.—
In this work we construct a concrete lattice model to show primary charge-
4e
superconducting phase at zero temperature from doping an
SU(4)
symmetric featureless Mott insulator. Our results highlight that neither enlarged internal symmetry nor strong correlations alone are sufficient to stabilize a primary charge-
4e
superconductivity. It is the combined effect of (i) kinematic constraints from the
SU(4)
center forbidding the singlet charge-
2e
pairing channel, and (ii) dynamical constraints from Mott physics and restricted particle motions in the ESD model, that renders primary charge-
4e
superconductivity to emerge. These can be straightforwardly generalized to

or
Sp(2n)

2ne

2e
SC phases. In contrast, in the

ESD model, a singlet charge-
2e
superconductor should remain the primary superconducting phase.

From an experimental perspective, the parent bilayer Hubbard models with onsite

or
Sp(2n)

;
DeSalvo et al. 2010
;
Wu 2010
;

;
Taie et al. 2012
, and bilayer geometries with controllable interlayer couplings could implement the ESD-type constructions discussed here. In solid state systems, approximate

or
Sp(4)

You and Vishwanath 2019
;
Bernevig et al. 2021
;
Wang et al. 2021
;
Khalaf et al. 2022
;
Chichinadze et al. 2022
;

, providing candidate settings for primary charge-

\left|f_{-,2}\right>=&\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(c^{\dagger}_{-,1}c^{\dagger}_{-,3}c^{\dagger}_{+,4}-c^{\dagger}_{-,1}c^{\dagger}_{-,4}c^{\dagger}_{+,3}+c^{\dagger}_{-,3}c^{\dagger}_{-,4}c^{\dagger}_{+,1})\left|0\right>,\\
\left|f_{-,3}\right>=&\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(c^{\dagger}_{-,1}c^{\dagger}_{-,2}c^{\dagger}_{+,4}-c^{\dagger}_{-,1}c^{\dagger}_{-,4}c^{\dagger}_{+,2}+c^{\dagger}_{-,2}c^{\dagger}_{-,4}c^{\dagger}_{+,1})\left|0\right>,\\
\left|f_{-,4}\right>=&\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(c^{\dagger}_{-,1}c^{\dagger}_{-,2}c^{\dagger}_{+,3}-c^{\dagger}_{-,1}c^{\dagger}_{-,3}c^{\dagger}_{+,2}+c^{\dagger}_{-,2}c^{\dagger}_{-,3}c^{\dagger}_{+,1})\left|0\right>.\end{aligned}

(S21)

The
SU(4)
electron operators are,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} c_{+,1}=&-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\left|\gamma^{1}\right>\left<f_{+,2}\right|-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\left|\gamma^{2}\right>\left<f_{+,3}\right|-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\left|\gamma^{4}\right>\left<f_{+,4}\right|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left|f_{-,1}\right>\left<q\right|,\\

c_{-,4}=&-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\left|\gamma^{4}\right>\left<f_{-,1}\right|-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\left|\gamma^{5}\right>\left<f_{-,2}\right|-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3]}}\left|\gamma^{6}\right>\left<f_{-,3}\right|-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left|f_{+,4}\right>\left<q\right|\end{aligned}

(S22)

After projecting the bilayer Hubbard model into the basis defined above, the effective Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{ESD}}=-t\sum_{\langle\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}\rangle}\mathcal{P}c^{\dagger}_{l,\sigma}(\mathbf{i})c_{l,\sigma}(\mathbf{i})\mathcal{P}+\sum_{i}\epsilon_{q}n_{q}(\mathbf{i})+\epsilon_{\eta}n_{\eta}(\mathbf{i})+\epsilon_{\gamma}n_{\gamma}(\mathbf{i})+\epsilon_{f}n_{f}(\mathbf{i}),

(S23)

with the constraints
n_{q}(\mathbf{i})+n_{\eta}(\mathbf{i})+n_{\gamma}(\mathbf{i})+n_{f}(\mathbf{i})=1
and
2n_{\eta}(\mathbf{i})+2n_{\gamma}(\mathbf{i})+n_{f}(\mathbf{i})=2x
. As a result, there are only two tuning parameters,
\epsilon
and
\delta
, in the generalized ESD model discussed in the main text

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{ESD}}=-t\sum_{\left<\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}\right>}\mathcal{P}c_{l,\sigma}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{i})c_{l,\sigma}(\mathbf{j})\mathcal{P}+\sum_{\mathbf{i}}\epsilon\big(n_{q}(\mathbf{i})+n_{\eta}(\mathbf{i})\big)+(\epsilon+\delta)n_{\gamma}(\mathbf{i}).

(S24)

SIV
Cooper pair picture and field theory analysis

When
\epsilon\ll 0
, the generalized ESD model Eq. (
3
) can be reduced to an effective model of Cooper pairs from a perturbative expansion in
t/\epsilon
. For the
SU(4)
ESD model, on each site the Hilbert space is
7=6+1
dimensions formed by
\left|q(\mathbf{i})\right>
and
\left|\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{i})\right>
,
a=1,\cdots,6
with energy
-\epsilon
. The Cooper pair operators are defined as
h^{a}(\mathbf{i})=\left|q(\mathbf{i})\right>\left<\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{i})\right|
. Their hopping
\left|\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|q(\mathbf{j})\right>\rightarrow\left|q(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{j})\right>
is generated from the virtual process mediated by a second order electron hopping

\displaystyle\left|\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|q(\mathbf{j})\right>\rightarrow\left|f_{l,\mu}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|f_{l^{\prime},\nu}(\mathbf{j})\right>\rightarrow\left|q(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{j})\right>,

(S25)

where the intermediate states
\left|f_{l,\mu}(\mathbf{i})\right>
have energy
0
. The electron operator in the low energy Hilbert space is

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}c_{l,\sigma}(\mathbf{i})\mathcal{P}=\left|\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{i})\right>P^{a}_{\sigma\mu}\left<f_{l,\mu}(\mathbf{i})\right|+\left|f_{-l,\sigma}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left<q(\mathbf{i})\right|,

(S26)

where
P^{a}_{\sigma\mu}
is the CG coefficient from the decomposition of irrep
\bar{\mathbf{4}}\subset\mathbf{4}\times\mathbf{6}
satisfying
\mathrm{tr}{\left(P^{a\dagger}P^{b}\right)}=\delta^{ab}
due to the
SU(4)
symmetry. The operation of electron hopping on
\left|\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|q(\mathbf{j})\right>
reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}c^{\dagger}_{l,\sigma}(\mathbf{i})c_{l,\sigma}(\mathbf{j})\mathcal{P}\left|\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|q(\mathbf{j})\right>=\mathcal{P}c^{\dagger}_{l,\sigma}(\mathbf{i})\mathcal{P}\left|\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|f_{-l,\sigma}(\mathbf{j})\right>=P^{a*}_{\sigma\mu}\left|f_{l,\mu}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|f_{-l,\sigma}(\mathbf{j})\right>,

(S27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}c^{\dagger}_{l^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i})c_{l^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{j})\mathcal{P}\left|f_{l,\mu}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|f_{-l,\sigma}(\mathbf{j})\right>=\mathcal{P}c^{\dagger}_{-l,\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{i})\mathcal{P}P^{b}_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}\left|f_{l,\mu}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|\gamma^{b}(\mathbf{j})\right>=P^{b}_{\mu\sigma}\left|q(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|\gamma^{b}(\mathbf{j})\right>.

(S28)

Thus, the Cooper pair hopping is described by

\displaystyle H_{h}^{0}=-\frac{t^{2}}{2|\epsilon|}P^{a*}_{\sigma\mu}P^{a}_{\mu\sigma}h^{a\dagger}(\mathbf{i})h^{a}(\mathbf{j})=-\frac{t^{2}}{2|\epsilon|}h^{a\dagger}(\mathbf{i})h^{a}(\mathbf{j}).

(S29)

Next we consider the interaction terms. At the leading order of
t/|\epsilon|
, the nearest-neighbor interaction is generated by a
q
-assisted exchange,
\left|q(\mathbf{0})\right>\left|\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|\gamma^{b}(\mathbf{j})\right>\rightarrow\left|q(\mathbf{0})\right>\left|\gamma^{c}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|\gamma^{d}(\mathbf{j})\right>
, where
\mathbf{0}
is adjacent to
\mathbf{i}
or
\mathbf{j}
. Without losing of generality, we assume
\mathbf{0}
is adjacent to
\mathbf{i}
, and consider the following process generating a nearest-neighbor antiferromagnetic Heisenberg interaction (see Fig.
S2
for illustration):

\displaystyle\left|q(\mathbf{0})\right>\left|\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|\gamma^{b}(\mathbf{j})\right>\rightarrow\left|f(\mathbf{0})\right>\left|f(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|\gamma^{b}(\mathbf{j})\right>\rightarrow\left|f(\mathbf{0})\right>\left|\gamma^{x}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|f(\mathbf{j})\right>\rightarrow\left|f(\mathbf{0})\right>\left|f(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|\gamma^{d}(\mathbf{j})\right>\rightarrow\left|q(\mathbf{0})\right>\left|\gamma^{c}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|\gamma^{d}(\mathbf{j})\right>,

where
x=1,\cdots,6
. The effective interaction generated from this process is

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{int}}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{t^{4}}{8|\epsilon|^{3}}\mathrm{tr}{\left(P^{a\dagger}P^{c}P^{x\dagger}P^{d}P^{b\dagger}P^{x}\right)}\left|q(\mathbf{0})\right>\left|\gamma^{a}(\mathbf{i})\right>\left|\gamma^{b}(\mathbf{j})\right>\left<q(\mathbf{0})\right|\left<\gamma^{c}(\mathbf{i})\right|\left<\gamma^{d}(\mathbf{j})\right|

(S31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{4}}{64|\epsilon|^{3}}n_{q}(\mathbf{0})\big(S^{ab}(\mathbf{i})S^{ab}(\mathbf{j})-3n_{h}(\mathbf{i})n_{h}(\mathbf{j})\big),

where
S^{ab}(\mathbf{i})=i\big(h^{a\dagger}(\mathbf{i})h^{b}(\mathbf{i})-h^{b\dagger}(\mathbf{i})h^{a}(\mathbf{i})\big)
are
SU(4)
generators under
\mathbf{6}
irrep, and
n_{h}(\mathbf{i})=h^{a\dagger}(\mathbf{i})h^{a}(\mathbf{i})=n_{\gamma}(\mathbf{i})=1-n_{q}(\mathbf{i})
is used. From Eq. (
S31
) it can be concluded that the two-site singlet has the lowest energy due to the antiferromagnetic Heisenberg-type interaction, which favors a singlet pair-superfluid state of
h^{a}(\mathbf{i})
, i.e. the singlet charge-
4e
SC state of electrons, upon decreasing
|\epsilon|
. We note that, other interactions, including frustrated (non-nearest-neighbor) Heisenberg interactions and density/spin-assisted hoppings can be also generated by certain virtual processes, which are believed to be less relevant in the study of superconductivity.

Figure S2:
Illustration for

-assisted exchange. Each arrowed solid line represents a step of virtual electron hopping, while its generating amplitude is marked by a dashed line. It can be directly read out that the total exchange amplitude from this process is
t^{4}\mathrm{tr}{\left(P^{a\dagger}P^{c}P^{x\dagger}P^{d}P^{b\dagger}P^{x}\right)}
.

The phase transition between the flavor-polarized charge-
2e
SC and the singlet charge-
4e
SC can be understood from a field theory perspective. A convenient representation to describe the vector Cooper pair and its coupling to the quartetting singlet is to use the complex antisymmetric tensor field
\phi_{ab}=-\phi_{ba}
with
1\leq a,b\leq 4
. The full Ginzburg-Landau theory reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=|D_{2A}\phi_{ab}|^{2}+s_{\phi}|\phi_{ab}|^{2}+v_{\phi}|\phi_{ab}|^{4}+v_{\epsilon}|\epsilon_{abcd}\phi_{ab}\phi_{cd}|^{2}+|D_{4A}\Phi|^{2}+s_{\Phi}|\Phi|^{2}+v_{\Phi}|\Phi|^{4}-\lambda(\Phi^{\dagger}\epsilon_{abcd}\phi_{ab}\phi_{cd}+h.c.),

where
\Phi
is the quartetting field and
A
is the electromagnetic field.
s
and
v
are phenomenological coupling parameters, and a positive
\lambda
imposing the singlet quartetting channel from the pairing of two vector Cooper pairs. As
s_{\phi,\Phi}>0
, both boson fields are gapped, suggesting a fermi liquid phase. The onset of charge-
4e
SC requires
s_{\Phi}
evolves to negative before
s_{\phi}
, where
\Phi
is condensed but
\phi
remains gapped. Denote
\Phi=\rho e^{i\theta}
with
\rho>0
and
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
, and do the gauge transformation
\phi_{ab}\mapsto\phi_{ab}e^{i\theta/2}
. The Ginzberg-Landau Lagrangian becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=|D_{2A-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\theta}\phi_{ab}|^{2}+s_{\phi}|\phi_{ab}|^{2}+v_{\phi}|\phi_{ab}|^{4}+v_{\epsilon}|\epsilon_{abcd}\phi_{ab}\phi_{cd}|^{2}-\lambda\rho(\epsilon_{abcd}\phi_{ab}\phi_{cd}+h.c.)+\rho^{2}(\partial_{\mu}\theta-4A)^{2},

(S33)

where
\theta
described the Goldstone mode from the spontaneous breaking of the charge
U(1)
symmetry, which is absorbed by the electromagnetic field
A
. From the last term it can be also seen that there is a residual
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
gauge redundancy in
A
, suggesting a residual charge
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
symmetry in the charge-
4e
SC. Consequently,
\phi_{ab}
is charged 2 (mod 4) under
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. In what followings we will omit the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
-valued
A
for clarity. The flavor
SU(4)
symmetry remains intact after the condensation of singlet
\Phi
. To see its further broken by the condensation of
\phi_{ab}
, we rewrite
\phi_{ab}
into real and complex parts,
\phi_{ab}=\phi_{ab}^{R}+i\phi_{ab}^{I}
. Since
\lambda,\rho>0
,
\phi^{R}_{ab}
will always be condensed before
\phi^{I}_{ab}
. The effective Lagrangian for
\phi^{R}
reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\partial_{\mu}\phi^{R}_{ab}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{R}_{ab}+s_{\phi}\phi^{R}_{ab}\phi^{R}_{ab}-2\lambda\rho\epsilon_{abcd}\phi^{R}_{ab}\phi^{R}_{cd}+v_{\phi}(\phi^{R}_{ab}\phi^{R}_{ab})^{2}+v_{\epsilon}(\epsilon_{abcd}\phi^{R}_{ab}\phi^{R}_{cd})^{2}.

(S34)

Thus, for
\lambda\rho
large enough,
\phi^{R}_{ab}
is condensed. For example, when
s_{\phi}=v_{\phi}=v_{\epsilon}=1
,
\left<\phi^{R}_{ab}\right>=\pm\sqrt{(\lambda\rho-1)/15}
for
\lambda\rho>1
. Thus, the
SU(4)
symmetry is spontaneously broken to
Sp(4)
, leaving a Goldstone manifold
SU(4)/Sp(4)\sim S^{5}
. Moreover, as
\phi_{ab}
also carries charge 2 mod 4 under the residual charge
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
symmetry, its condensation also breaks
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, suggesting the Ising universality class of the transition. In together, for
G=SU(4)
, the transition between the flavor-polarized charge-
2e
SC and the singlet charge-
4e
SC is an Ising and an
SU(4)
broken to
Sp(4)
transition in charge and flavor sector, respectively. For
G=Sp(4)
, both the charge-
4e
SC and the charge-
2e
SC are singlets. Thus, the transition between them can be easily captured in a generalized XY model formalism, and shown to be in the Ising universality class.

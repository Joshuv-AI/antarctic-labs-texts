An analytic proof of Griffiths’ conjecture on compact Riemann surfaces
Rei Murakami

Abstract
. Griffiths’ conjecture asserts that a holomorphic vector bundle is ample if and only if it admits a Hermitian metric with positive curvature. In this paper, we present a new proof of this conjecture on compact Riemann surfaces using a system of PDEs introduced by Demailly. Our argument combines techniques developed by Uhlenbeck–Yau for Hermitian–Einstein metrics with Pingali’s reduction of the problem to an a priori estimate.



1.
Introduction

Given a holomorphic vector bundle
E
on a compact complex manifold
X
, Griffiths conjectured that differential-geometric and algebro-geometric notions of positivity of
E
are equivalent.
The differential-geometric notion of positivity is called
Griffiths positivity
and defined in terms of the Chern curvature.
Explicitly, the curvature
\sqrt{-1}F_{h}
of a Hermitian metric
h
is Griffiths positive at a point
p\in X
if

\sqrt{-1}(F_{h})_{i\bar{j}\,\alpha\bar{\beta}}\,\xi^{i}\bar{\xi}^{j}v^{\alpha}\bar{v}^{\beta}>0

for all nonzero
\xi\in E_{p}
and
v\in T^{1,0}_{p}X
.
The algebro-geometric notion of positivity for
E
is defined via the ampleness of the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E^{*})}(1)
.
The implication from Griffiths positivity to ampleness is well known, and for line bundles the equivalence itself follows from the Kodaira embedding theorem.

In his recent work
[
Dem21
]
, Demailly proposed a new analytic approach to this conjecture. He introduced a system of PDEs, which we refer to as
the Demailly system
, and conjectured that the existence of solutions characterizes ampleness in terms of Griffiths positivity.
This approach has already been tested in the case of the vortex bundle
[
Man23
]
.

In this paper, we study the Demailly system on Riemann surfaces. Although Griffiths’ conjecture was already settled in this case by
[
Ume73
,
CF90
]
, we give a new proof using Demailly’s analytic framework.
Given a reference Hermitian metric
h_{\mathrm{ref}}
on
E
, we write any Hermitian metric
h
as
h=e^{-f}gh_{\mathrm{ref}}
, where
f
is a smooth function and
g\in\Gamma(\mathrm{End}\,E)
is a positive Hermitian matrix with respect to
h_{\mathrm{ref}}
satisfying
\det g=1
.
Then, the Demailly system on Riemann surfaces takes the form

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\det\big(\sqrt{-1}F_{h}/\omega_{0}+(1-t)\alpha\big)=e^{\lambda f}a_{0}\\
\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}F_{h}^{\circ}=-e^{f}\ln g,\end{cases}

where a positive number
\alpha
, a smooth positive function
a_{0}\in C^{\infty}(X)
, and a Kähler form
\omega_{0}
are chosen so that the system admits a solution
h_{0}
at
t=0
with
f_{0}=0
and
\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{0}}/\omega_{0}+\alpha>0
(see the proof of
[
UY86
, Theorem 3.1]
). Here, we denote by
\sqrt{-1}F_{h}^{\circ}
the trace-free part of
\sqrt{-1}F_{h}
. The constants
\lambda
and
\alpha
are chosen to ensure local uniqueness at
t=0

[
Pin23
, Propositions 2.1 and 2.2]
. The first equation of (
1.1
) is formulated to detect the positivity of the matrix on the left-hand side. The second equation is attached to overcome the undeterminancy of the equation. The specific form is chosen to use the study of the (cushioned) Hermitian-Einstein equation in
[
UY86
]
.

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface and
E
a holomorphic vector bundle. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

The Demailly system (
1.1
) is solvable continuously in
t\in[0,1]
.

(2)

E
admits a Hermitian metric whose curvature is Griffiths positive.

(3)

E
is ample.

The implication (
1
)
\Rightarrow
(
2
) follows immediately from the formulation of the Demailly system (
1.1
), since the right-hand side of the first equation of (
1.1
) is positive, and thus the matrix on the left-hand side remains positive. The implication (
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
3
) follows easily. Hence, the essential part of the theorem is to show (
3
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
).
Pingali
[
Pin23
]
previously studied the Demailly system on Riemann surfaces and established Theorem
1.2
for a sum of ample line bundles. For general vector bundles, using the Leray–Schauder degree theory
[
Pin23
, Theorem 1.2]
, he reduced the problem to an a priori lower bound for
f
, which is the focus of this paper (Proposition
2.19
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2.1
, we establish an a priori estimate for the curvature
\sqrt{-1}F_{h}
(Proposition
2.8
). In Section
2.2
, using techniques from
[
UY86
]
, we show that if
f
were unbounded below, one could construct a quotient sheaf
Q
with
\deg Q\leq 0
, contradicting the ampleness of
E
(since ampleness implies
\deg Q>0
for every quotient
Q
). This yields the desired lower bound for
f
.
Finally, in Section
2.3
, for direct sums of line bundles (as in
[
Pin23
]
), we provide an alternative proof of (
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
) by obtaining a lower bound for
f
by the maximum principle, which partially simplifies the proof as discussed in
[
Pin23
, Remark 3.1]
. The restriction to this special case is due to the lack of Lemma
2.26
for general vector bundles.

2.
Proof

2.1.
Curvature estimates

In this section, we prove an a priori estimate for the curvature in the Demailly system (Proposition
2.8
). We freely use the following notation:

Notation 2.1
.

•

We fix
h_{0}
as a reference Hermitian metric on
E
and denote the covariant derivative and the curvature associated with
h_{0}
by
\partial_{0}
and
\sqrt{-1}F_{0}
.

•

Given a point
p
, we locally diagonalize
\ln g\in\Gamma(\mathrm{End}E)
on a neighborhood
U
of
p
via a local frame
\{s_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
, and denote the eigenvalue functions defined on
U
by
\{\lambda_{i}\}^{r}_{i=1}
which satisfy
\lambda_{1}(p)\geq\lambda_{2}(p)\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}(p)
.
Moreover, since
g
is Hermitian with respect to
h_{0}
, we can choose such a local frame
\{s_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
which is also orthonormal with respect to
h_{0}
on
U
. We also define the function
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}
by
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(p):=\max_{i}\lambda_{i}(p)=\lambda_{1}(p)
, which is continuous but not necessarily smooth.

•

The Demailly system (
1.1
) is expressed as

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\det\left(\dfrac{\beta}{r}+\Delta f-e^{f}\ln g+(1-t)\alpha\right)=e^{\lambda f}a_{0},\\
\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}F_{h}^{\circ}=-e^{f}\ln g,\end{cases}

where
\beta
denotes
\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\mathrm{tr}\,\sqrt{-1}F_{0}
and
\Delta
denotes the laplacian associated to
\omega_{0}
.

•

In this paper, a positive constant
C
denotes a constant which is independent of
t\in[0,1]
, and may vary from line to line.

With the localization on
U
stated in Notation
2.1
, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle g=\sum_{i=1}^{r}e^{\lambda_{i}}\,s_{i}\otimes s^{i}

on
U
. Define a
(1,0)
-form
A^{(1,0)}
and a
(0,1)
-form
A^{(0,1)}
by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}s_{i}=\big(A^{(1,0)}\big)^{j}_{i}s_{j},\quad\bar{\partial}s_{i}=\big(A^{(0,1)}\big)^{j}_{i}s_{j},

where
\partial_{0}
is the
(1,0)
-part of the covariant derivative associated with the reference Hermitian metric
h_{0}
. Here we adopt the Einstein summation convention and omit the summation symbol.

The following lemma follows from straightforward computations, but since we could not find it in the literature, we record it here for completeness.

Lemma 2.5
.

We have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Delta\lambda_{i}=-\big(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g)\big)^{i}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\big(e^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}-1\big)C_{i,j}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\big(e^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}-1\big)C_{j,i},

where
C_{i,j}
are nonnegative functions defined by

C_{i,j}:=-\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}\left(\big(A^{(1,0)}\big)^{j}_{i}\wedge\big(A^{(0,1)}\big)^{i}_{j}\right).

Proof.

We see that

\displaystyle\partial_{0}g=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\partial\big(e^{\lambda_{i}}\big)s_{i}\otimes s^{i}+\sum_{i\neq j}\big(e^{\lambda_{j}}-e^{\lambda_{i}}\big)\big(A^{(1,0)}\big)^{i}_{j}s_{i}\otimes s^{j},

since
\partial_{0}s^{j}=-\big(A^{(1,0)}\big)^{j}_{i}s^{i}
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\big(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g\big)=\sum_{i,j,k}\big(g^{-1}\big)^{i}_{k}\big(\partial_{0}g\big)^{k}_{j}s_{i}\otimes s^{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\partial\lambda_{i})s_{i}\otimes s^{i}+\sum_{i\neq j}\big(e^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}-1\big)\big(A^{(1,0)}\big)^{i}_{j}s_{i}\otimes s^{j}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g)\right)^{i}_{i}=

\displaystyle-\Delta\lambda_{i}-\sum_{j\neq i}\big(e^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}-1\big)\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}\left(\big(A^{(1,0)}\big)^{j}_{i}\wedge\big(A^{(0,1)}\big)^{i}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\neq i}\big(e^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}-1\big)\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}\left(\big(A^{(1,0)}\big)^{i}_{j}\wedge\big(A^{(0,1)}\big)^{j}_{i}\right).

Finally, since
\{s_{i}\}
is orthonormal with respect to
h_{0}
on
U
, we have

\displaystyle 0=\partial(h_{0})_{i\bar{j}}=\big(A^{(1,0)}\big)^{k}_{i}(h_{0})_{k\bar{j}}+(h_{0})_{i\bar{k}}\overline{\big(A^{(0,1)}\big)^{k}_{j}}=\big(A^{(1,0)}\big)^{j}_{i}+\overline{\big(A^{(0,1)}\big)^{i}_{j}},

which implies that
C_{i,j}
are nonnegative.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(f,g)
be a solution of (
1.1
). Then, the function
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}
is
C\omega_{0}
-subharmonic for some constant
C
. In particular, there exists a constant
C
such that
\fint_{X}\sup\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\leq C
, where
\fint
denotes the integral with respect to
\omega_{0}
divided by the total volume
\int_{X}\omega_{0}
.

Proof.

Fix a point
p\in X
. Let
q
be a
C^{2}
function defined on a neighborhood of
p
such that
q\geq\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}
on
U
and
q(p)=(\lambda_{\mathrm{max}})(p)
. Then, we have
q\geq\lambda_{1}
and
q(p)=(\lambda_{1})(p)
. Therefore, by using Lemma
2.5
, we see that

\displaystyle\Delta q

\displaystyle\geq\Delta\lambda_{1}\geq-\big(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g)\big)^{1}_{1}

\displaystyle=e^{f}\lambda_{1}-\big(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}F_{0}\big)^{1}_{1}\geq-C\quad\text{at }p\in X,

where we used the second equation of (
2.2
) in the equality, and the last inequality follows from
\lambda_{1}\geq 0
, obtained by
\operatorname{tr}\ln g=0
. Hence, we obtain
\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\geq-C
in the viscosity sense, which is equivalent to say that
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}
is
C\omega_{0}
-subharmonic. The last assertion is standard, and therefore we omit it here.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

There exists a constant
C
such that
e^{f}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}<C
for a solution
(f,g)
of (
1.1
). In particular, we have
|\Delta f|\leq C
.

Proof.

We see that
\Delta f\geq-C
for some constant
C
by taking the trace of the inequality

(2.9)

\frac{\beta}{r}+\Delta f-e^{f}\ln g+(1-t)\alpha>0

since
\mathrm{tr}\ln g=0
.
Also, the upper estimate of
\Delta f
follows immediately from the estimate of
e^{f}|\ln g|
, since the right-hand side of the first equation of (
1.1
) is bounded by an upper estimate of
f
, which is obtained from the maximum principle
[
Pin23
, Remark 2.1]
.
Moreover, since
\operatorname{tr}\ln g=0
, we have
-\lambda_{r}=\sum_{i\neq r}\lambda_{i}\leq(r-1)\lambda_{1}
. In particular, we have

e^{f}|\ln g|=e^{f}\sqrt{\sum\lambda_{i}^{2}}\leq Ce^{f}\lambda_{1}.

Thus, it suffices to obtain an estimate of
e^{f}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}
.
We have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\fint_{X}e^{f}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}=

\displaystyle\fint_{X}e^{f}(\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}-\sup\lambda_{\mathrm{max}})+e^{f}\sup\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-C+e^{\sup f}\sup\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\fint_{X}e^{f-\sup f}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-C+e^{\sup f}\sup\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\,e^{\fint_{X}(f-\sup f)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-C+Ce^{\sup f}\sup\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}.

Here, we used Lemma
2.7
and an upper estimate of
f

[
Pin23
, Remark 2.1]
in the first inequality, the convexity of the exponential in the second inequality and
\Delta f\geq-C
, which implies
\fint f-\sup f\geq-C
by Green’s representation formula, in the last inequality. On the other hand, using inequality (
2.9
), we also see that

(2.11)

\fint_{X}e^{f}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}<\fint_{X}\frac{\beta}{r}+\Delta f+(1-t)\alpha=\fint_{X}\frac{\beta}{r}+(1-t)\alpha\leq C.

Combining this with (
2.10
), we obtain
e^{f}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\leq e^{\sup f}\sup\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\leq C
.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

We can also prove Proposition
2.8
using
\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}
instead of
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}
. More precisely, we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle-\Delta\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}\sum_{i}\frac{\lambda_{i}\partial\lambda_{i}}{\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}}=\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}\left(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g,\frac{\ln g}{\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}\left(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g\right),\frac{\ln g}{\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\left(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g,\partial_{0}\frac{\ln g}{\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-e^{f}\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}+C,

where the inequality follows from the second equation of the Demailly system (
2.2
) and the inequality

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}\left(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g,\partial_{0}\frac{\ln g}{\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\partial\lambda_{i}\wedge\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}}\right)+\sum_{i\neq j}(e^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}-1)\frac{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}{\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}}\left|\big(A^{(1,0)}\big)^{i}_{j}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\partial\lambda_{i}|^{2}-\frac{\big|\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\partial\lambda_{i}\big|^{2}}{\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}+1}\right)\geq\frac{1}{(|\ln g|^{2}+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\sum_{i\neq j}\left|\lambda_{i}\partial\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j}\partial\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\right)\geq 0.

Therefore, we have
\Delta\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}\geq-C
, which implies an inequality similar to (
2.10
). By integrating (
2.13
), we obtain an
L^{1}
-bound of
e^{f}\sqrt{|\ln g|^{2}+1}
, which completes the proof. Note that this proof does not use a lower bound of the curvature, compared to (
2.11
).

2.2.
Construction of a nonpositive quotient sheaf

We complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
by constructing a quotient sheaf
Q
of
E
such that
\deg Q\leq 0
if
f
is not bounded from below. This is achieved by the technique developed in
[
UY86
]
. First, recall the definition of a weakly holomorphic subbundle and its relation to a coherent subsheaf.

Definition 2.14

(
[
LT06
, Definition 3.4.2]
)
.

An element
\pi\in L^{2}_{1}(\mathrm{End}\,E)
is called a weakly holomorphic subbundle of
E
if in
L^{1}(\mathrm{End}\,E)
we have

(2.15)

\pi^{*}=\pi=\pi^{2}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad(\mathrm{id}_{E}-\pi)\circ\bar{\partial}(\pi)=0.

Theorem 2.16

(
[
LT06
, Theorem 3.4.3]
,
[
UY86
, S290-292]
)
.

A weakly holomorphic subbundle
\pi
of
E
represents a coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
E
, and an analytic subset
S\subset X
with the following properties:

(1)

\mathrm{codim}_{X}S\geq 2

(2)

\pi|_{X\setminus S}
is
C^{\infty}
and satisfies (
2.15
).

(3)

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{F}|_{X\setminus S}=\pi|_{X\setminus S}(E|_{X\setminus S})\hookrightarrow E|_{X\setminus S}
is a holomorphic subbundle.

Now, let us define
m_{t}:=\sup_{X}(\lambda_{\mathrm{max}})_{t}
and
\tilde{g}_{t}:=e^{-m_{t}}g_{t}
,
where
(\lambda_{\mathrm{max}})_{t}(p)
is the largest eigenvalue of
\ln g_{t}
at
p\in X
.
The arguments in
[
UY86
, Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.1]
also work in our case by replacing
\varepsilon
with
e^{f}
. Therefore, we have the following:

Lemma 2.17
.

For a sequence of times
\{t_{i}\}\subset[0,1]
such that
t_{i}\to t_{\infty}
,
there is a subsequence, still denoted by
\{t_{i}\}
, such that
t_{i}\to t_{\infty}
and the sequence
\tilde{g}_{i}:=\tilde{g}_{t_{i}}
has the following properties:

(1)

For
\sigma\in(0,1]
, the sequence
\tilde{g}^{\sigma}_{i}
weakly converges in
L^{2}_{1}
to
\tilde{g}^{\sigma}_{\infty}\neq 0
.

(2)

There is a sequence of
\sigma_{j}\in(0,1]
such that
\sigma_{j}
converges to zero and the sequence
\tilde{g}_{\infty}^{\sigma_{j}}
weakly converges in
L^{2}_{1}
to
\tilde{g}^{0}_{\infty}
.

(3)

\pi:=\mathrm{id}_{E}-\tilde{g}^{0}_{\infty}
is a weakly holomorphic subbundle of
E
.

According to Theorem
2.16
, we have a coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset E
corresponding to
\pi
defined in Lemma
2.17
(
3
).
Note that since
X
is one-dimensional, an analytic subset
S
in Theorem
2.16
is empty, and so a coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
is a holomorphic subbundle, which we denote by
F
. Moreover, since
\tilde{g}^{\sigma}_{i}
also converges strongly in
L^{2}
to
\tilde{g}^{\sigma}_{\infty}\neq 0
and
\tilde{g}^{\sigma}_{i}\leq\mathrm{Id}_{E}
, the limit
\tilde{g}^{0}_{\infty}
has a strictly positive eigenvalue almost everywhere.
Therefore, we have

(2.18)

\operatorname{rank}F=\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Im}\pi=\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{id}_{E}-\tilde{g}_{\infty}^{0})\leq r-1.

We denote the quotient bundle
E/F
by
Q
. By (
2.18
), we have
Q\neq 0
. Also, since
g_{t}
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian, the limit
\pi
is also
h_{0}
-Hermitian almost everywhere. Hence, we have
F^{\perp}=\operatorname{Im}\pi^{\perp}
, where
\pi^{\perp}:=\operatorname{Im}(\mathrm{id}_{E}-\pi)
.

Proposition 2.19
.

Suppose that there exists a sequence of points
\{p_{i}\}\subset X
and times
\{t_{i}\}\subset[0,1]
such that
f_{i}(p_{i})\to-\infty
as
i\to\infty
for a solution
(f_{t},g_{t})
of the Demailly system (
2.2
), where
f_{i}:=f_{t_{i}}
. Then, we have
\deg{Q}\leq 0
.

Proof.

We induce the Hermitian metric
h_{Q}
on
Q
from the reference Hermitian metric
h_{0}
.
Then, we have

\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{Q}}=\pi^{\perp}\circ\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{-1}F_{0}\circ\pi^{\perp}+|\pi^{\perp}\partial_{0}\pi|^{2},

where
\pi^{\perp}\partial_{0}\pi
is the second fundamental form.
Therefore, we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle\deg Q=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\big(\mathrm{tr}\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{Q}}\big)=\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(\sqrt{-1}F_{0}\circ\pi^{\perp}\right)+|\pi^{\perp}\partial_{0}\pi|^{2}\omega_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\Big(\sqrt{-1}F_{0}\circ\tilde{g}^{\sigma_{j}}_{i}\Big)+\int_{X}|\pi^{\perp}\partial_{0}\pi|^{2}\omega_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(\big(\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{i}}+\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}\partial f_{i}-\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}(g_{i}^{-1}\partial_{0}g_{i})\big)\circ\tilde{g}^{\sigma_{j}}_{i}\right)+\int_{X}|\pi^{\perp}\partial_{0}\pi|^{2}\omega_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\big(\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{i}}\circ\tilde{g}^{\sigma_{j}}_{i}\big)+\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}\partial f_{i}\ (\mathrm{tr}\ \tilde{g}^{\sigma_{j}}_{i})

\displaystyle+\left(-\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\Big(\big(\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}(g_{i}^{-1}\partial_{0}g_{i})\circ\tilde{g}^{\sigma_{j}}_{i}\big)\Big)+\int_{X}|\pi^{\perp}\partial_{0}\pi|^{2}\omega_{0}\right),

where the third equality follows from the fact that
\tilde{g}^{\sigma_{j}}_{i}\to\pi^{\perp}
strongly in
L^{2}
and we denote
h_{i}:=h_{t_{i}}
.
The last term is nonpositive by the proof of inequality (4.18) in
[
UY86
]
.
The second term is zero, since Proposition
2.8
implies that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}\partial f_{i}\ (\mathrm{tr}\ \tilde{g}^{\sigma_{j}}_{i})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}\partial f_{i}\ (\operatorname{tr}\pi^{\perp})

and the right-hand side is zero since
\mathrm{tr}\ \pi^{\perp}
is a constant integer almost everywhere following from
\pi\in L^{2}_{1}
. We claim that the first term is nonpositive if
f_{i}(p_{i})\to-\infty
as
i\to\infty
. Note that, by Proposition
2.8
, we have
\operatorname{osc}f\leq C
and consequently
f_{i}\to-\infty
on
X
if
f_{i}(p_{i})\to-\infty
. Fix an arbitrary point
p\in X
. We have

\mathrm{tr}\big(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{i}}\circ\tilde{g}^{\sigma_{j}}_{i}\big)=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\big(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{i}}\big)^{k}_{k}\ e^{\sigma_{j}(\lambda_{k}-m)},

where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\lambda_{r}
are eigenvalues of
\ln g_{t_{i}}
.
For an integer
k\in\{1,2,\dots,r\}
such that
\lambda_{k}-m\to-\infty
as
i\to\infty
, we have

(2.21)

\big(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{i}}\big)^{k}_{k}\ e^{\sigma_{j}(\lambda_{k}-m)}\to 0

since
(\sqrt{-1}F_{h}\big)^{k}_{k}
is uniformly bounded by Proposition
2.8
. On the other hand, assume an integer
k\in\{1,2,\dots,r\}
satisfies
\lambda_{k}-m>-C
for some constant
C
uniformly in
i
. Then, at
p
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\big(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{i}}\big)^{k}_{k}+(1-t_{i})\alpha=\frac{\beta}{r}+\Delta f_{i}-e^{f_{i}}\lambda_{k}+(1-t_{i})\alpha

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{r}+\Delta f_{i}-e^{f_{i}}(\lambda_{1}-C)+(1-t_{i})\alpha,

where the last inequality follows from
-\lambda_{k}(p)\leq-m+C\leq-\lambda_{1}(p)+C
. Since
f_{i}\to-\infty
on
X
, by the first equation of (
1.1
), we have
\beta/r+\Delta f_{i}-e^{f_{i}}\lambda_{1}+(1-t_{i})\alpha\to 0
. We consequently obtain that

(2.22)

\big(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{i}}\big)^{k}_{k}\ e^{\sigma_{j}(\lambda_{k}-m)}\to-(1-t_{\infty})\alpha\leq 0\quad\mathrm{as}\ i\to\infty.

Combining (
2.21
) and (
2.22
), we see that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{tr}\big(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{i}}\circ\tilde{g}^{\sigma_{j}}_{i}\big)\leq 0
. Since
\sqrt{-1}F_{h}
is uniformly bounded by Proposition
2.8
and
\tilde{g}^{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded, by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we have

\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\big(\sqrt{-1}F_{h_{i}}\circ\tilde{g}^{\sigma_{j}}_{i}\big)\leq 0.

Applying these estimates to (
2.20
), we obtain
\deg Q\leq 0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Assume
E
is ample. Then, any quotient bundle is also ample, and, therefore, its degree is positive. By Proposition
2.19
, we have a constant
C
such that
f\geq-C
for a solution
(f,g)
of the Demailly system (
2.2
). By
[
Pin23
, Theorem 1.2]
, the lower estimate of
f
completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
An alternative proof of (
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
) on a direct sum of line bundles

Lemma 2.23
.

Suppose that the reference metric
h_{0}
has Griffiths positive curvature
\sqrt{-1}F_{0}
. Then, for a solution
(f,g)
of (
2.2
), there exists a constant
C
such that
f\geq-\mathrm{osc}\,\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}-C
.

Proof.

Let
p_{0}\in X
be a point such that
(f-\lambda_{\mathrm{max}})(p_{0})=\min_{X}(f-\lambda_{\mathrm{max}})
. As in Notation
2.1
, we diagonalize
\ln g
on a neighborhood
U
of
p_{0}
. Then, we have
(f-\lambda_{\mathrm{max}})(p_{0})=(f-\lambda_{1})(p_{0})=\min_{U}(f-\lambda_{1})
.
Note that if
\ln g
is diagonalized, so is the curvature
\sqrt{-1}F_{h}
, since we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}F_{h}=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{-1}F_{h}}{r}+\sqrt{-1}F_{h}^{\circ}=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{-1}F_{h}}{r}-e^{f}\ln g\,\omega_{0},

where the last equality is the second equation of the Demailly system (
2.2
).
Therefore, the first equation of the Demailly system (
2.2
) is expressed as

(2.24)

\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{(\sqrt{-1}F_{0})^{i}_{i}}{\omega_{0}}+\Delta f+\frac{\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g)^{i}_{i}}{\omega_{0}}+(1-t)\alpha\right)=e^{\lambda f}a_{0}.

Define
a_{i}:=(\sqrt{-1}F_{0})^{i}_{i}/\omega_{0}
, which is positive by assumption. Also, since
\lambda_{1}
is the largest eigenvalue at
p_{0}
, by inequality (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g)\right)^{1}_{1}=

\displaystyle-\Delta\lambda_{1}+\sum_{j\neq 1}\big(e^{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{j}}-1\big)C_{1,j}-\sum_{j\neq 1}\big(e^{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{1}}-1\big)C_{j,1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\Delta\lambda_{1}.

Then, we have

0\leq\Delta(f-\lambda_{1})\leq\Delta f+\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g)^{1}_{1}/\omega_{0}\quad\text{at }p_{0}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle 0<

\displaystyle a_{1}+(1-t)\alpha\leq a_{1}+\Delta f+\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g)^{1}_{1}/\omega_{0}+(1-t)\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta/r+\Delta f-e^{f}\lambda_{1}+(1-t)\alpha

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta/r+\Delta f-e^{f}\lambda_{i}+(1-t)\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{i}+\Delta f+\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}(g^{-1}\partial_{0}g)^{i}_{i}/\omega_{0}+(1-t)\alpha.

By applying this estimate to (
2.24
), we obtain

0<a_{1}^{r}<(a_{1}+(1-t)\alpha)^{r}\leq e^{\lambda f}a_{0}\quad\text{at }p_{0}.

Thus, we obtain
f(p_{0})>-C
for some constant
C
. Hence, we obtain

f=f-\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\geq-C-\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(p_{0})+\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}>-\mathrm{osc}\,\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}-C,

which is the desired estimate.
∎

We show that for a direct sum of line bundles, we have
\operatorname{osc}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\leq C
.
Let
E
be a direct sum of line bundles
E=\oplus L_{i}
and take a direct sum of Hermitian metrics on each line bundle as a reference metric. Then, considering
\ln g=\oplus u_{i}
, where
u_{i}
’s are smooth functions, we can express the Demailly system (
2.2
) as

(2.25)

\begin{cases}\prod_{i}\left(\frac{\beta}{r}+\Delta f-e^{f}u_{i}+(1-t)\alpha\right)=e^{\lambda f}a_{0},\\
\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\sqrt{-1}F_{0}^{\circ}\right)^{i}_{i}-\Delta u_{i}=-e^{f}u_{i}.\end{cases}

In this setting, we denote
u_{\mathrm{max}}:=\max_{i}u_{i}
.

Lemma 2.26
.

Let
(f,\{u_{i}\})
be a solution of (
2.25
). Then, there exists a constant
C
such that
\operatorname{osc}u_{\mathrm{max}}\leq C
.

Proof.

Let
p_{0}
be a point such that
\sup u_{\mathrm{max}}=u_{\mathrm{max}}(p_{0})
. Then, there exists
i\in\{1,2,\dots,r\}
such that
\sup u_{\mathrm{max}}=u_{i}(p_{0})
. By Proposition
2.8
(or
[
Pin23
, Proposition 3.2]
), we have
|e^{f}u_{i}|\leq C
for some constant
C
. Thus, by the second equation of (
2.25
), we have
|\Delta u_{i}|\leq C
.
Hence, by Green’s representation formula, we have
\operatorname{osc}u_{i}\leq C
. Then, we see that

\sup u_{\mathrm{max}}-u_{\mathrm{max}}\leq u_{i}(p_{0})-u_{i}\leq C,

which is the desired estimate.
∎

Combining the two lemmas above, we obtain a lower bound of
f
. As a result, by
[
Pin23
, Theorem 1.2]
, we obtain an alternative proof of the implication (
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
) in Theorem
1.2
in the setting of a direct sum of line bundles with a direct sum of Hermitian metrics.

Remark 2.27
.

If one can prove that
\operatorname{osc}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}
is bounded on vector bundles, then Lemma
2.23
is valid to give an alternative proof on vector bundles. Not only that, the estimate of
\operatorname{osc}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}
also gives an alternative proof of Proposition
2.8
, since at the point
p_{0}
where
\sup f=f(p_{0})
we have

e^{f}\ln g\leq\frac{\beta}{r}+\Delta f+(1-t)\alpha\leq C,

and therefore in the same way as in the proof of
[
Pin23
, Proposition 3.2]
we have

e^{f}|\ln g|\leq Ce^{f}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\leq Ce^{\sup f}(\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(p_{0})+\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(p_{0}))\leq C.

In conclusion, the oscillation bound for
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}
appears to be a powerful estimate in the study of the Demailly system. However, we do not know if this estimate holds in general.

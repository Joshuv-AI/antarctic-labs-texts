Coexistence of distinct Discrete Time-Crystalline orders in the Floquet Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick model
Shashank Mishra, Sayan Choudhury

Abstract
We examine the distinct discrete time crystals (DTCs) that emerge in the Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick model, subjected to spatially nonuniform periodic driving. Intriguingly, we demonstrate that by appropriately tailoring the drive protocol, distinct DTC orders can be realized in different spatial regions of the system. Consequently, the system exhibits spatially varying sub-harmonic responses with distinct frequencies. We employ a semi-classical analysis to establish the stability of these co-existing DTC orders in the thermodynamic limit. Furthermore, we establish the stability of the stability of these co-existing DTCs in the presence of quantum fluctuations. Our results establish spatially structured driving as a powerful route to engineer novel forms of time-crystalline order.

Model:
We consider a periodically driven collective spin-
1/2
system with all-to-all interactions described by the LMG model.
The system consists of
N
spins partitioned into two equal subregions, each containing
N/2
spins. We note that the interactions are uniform across the entire system, the transverse drive is spatially nonuniform.

The time-periodic Hamiltonian with period
T=1
alternates between

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2J}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\sigma_{i}^{z}\sigma_{j}^{z},\qquad 0<t\leq\tfrac{1}{2},

(1)

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=2\pi\!\left(h_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\sigma_{j}^{x}+h_{2}\sum_{j=N/2+1}^{N}\sigma_{j}^{x}\right),\qquad\tfrac{1}{2}<t\leq 1,

(2)

where
\sigma_{j}^{\alpha}
(
\alpha=x,y,z
) are Pauli matrices,
J
is the interaction strength, and
h_{1}
,
h_{2}
denote the drive amplitudes applied to the two regions.
The corresponding Floquet operator is
U_{F}=e^{-iH_{2}/2}\,e^{-iH_{1}/2}.

In the thermodynamic limit (

\vec{L}_{1}=(2/N)\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\vec{\sigma}_{j}

In the uniform-drive limit (

To diagnose dynamical stability and sensitivity to perturbations, we employ the semiclassical decorrelator

D^{(n)}=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}\!\sum_{\alpha=x,y,z}\big(L_{\alpha}^{(n)}-L_{\alpha_{b}}^{(n)}\big)^{2}},

(3)

which quantifies the divergence between two initially nearby trajectories evolved under slightly perturbed drives. The quantum counterpart of the decorrelator is the out-of-time-order correlator (OTOC) that captures information scrambling. Here, we examine the fidelity OTOC (FOTOC):

F(t)=1-\big|\langle\Psi(0)|e^{iHt}We^{-iHt}|\Psi(0)\rangle\big|^{2},

(4)

with
W=e^{i\epsilon(S_{z_{1}}+S_{z_{2}})}
. The FOTOC has previously been employed to characterize regular and chaotic dynamics in collective spin systems and Bose-Einstein condensates.

Small values of

indicate stable time-crystalline dynamics, while large values signal chaotic behavior.
For uniform driving, the two diagnostics exhibit strong qualitative agreement, establishing a direct correspondence between semiclassical stability and quantum coherence (Figure

Conclusion and Outlook:
Our results demonstrate that a uniformly interacting driven spin system can support

spatial coexistence of distinct discrete time-crystalline orders

when subjected to minimal spatial inhomogeneity in the driving field.
Despite all-to-all coupling, the system self-organizes into dynamical subregions
that break discrete time-translation symmetry in inequivalent ways,
giving rise to robust and long-lived subharmonic responses with different periods.
This establishes a new form of nonequilibrium phase coexistence in the temporal domain.

A central finding is the simultaneous stability of

coexisting time crystals with different periodicities
,
such as
2T
and
6T
, realized under identical global parameters indicating breaking of time-translation symmetry in two distinct ways in a single system.
This behavior represents a temporal analogue of equilibrium phase coexistence,
reminiscent of water–ice coexistence, where two ordered phases persist in mutual contact
while retaining distinct internal structure.
Importantly, this coexistence cannot be interpreted as a trivial harmonic component
of a single higher-period phase.
Our semiclassical stability analysis shows that transitions between coexistence regions
and single-DTC phases necessarily pass through intermediate regimes
where subharmonic order is lost, confirming that the observed coexistence
reflects the presence of genuinely distinct dynamical phases.

In addition to coexisting DTCs, we identify regimes where a time-crystalline region
coexists with a Floquet-synchronized (period-
T
) region.
These chimera-like states emerge naturally within the same framework
and represent a secondary manifestation of the underlying spatiotemporal organization.
Unlike previously reported chimera phases, which involve coexistence between
ordered and synchronized dynamics in small systems,
the primary phenomenon reported here is the coexistence
of
multiple ordered time-crystalline phases

within a single globally coupled many-body system.

Taken together, our work reveals a broader paradigm of

temporal phase coexistence
in driven quantum matter,
where discrete time-translation symmetry can be broken
in more than one stable and spatially resolved manner.
Beyond enriching the phase structure of prethermal Floquet systems,
these results highlight spatially structured driving
as a powerful tool for engineering complex temporal order.
Our findings are directly relevant to long-range interacting platforms
such as trapped ions and cavity-mediated spin ensembles,
and open the door to exploring interfaces, dynamics, and control
of coexisting nonequilibrium temporal phases.

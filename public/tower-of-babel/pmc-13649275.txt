Obesity Increases Atherosclerosis Susceptibility via an Intertissue miR-30e-SLC7A11 Axis
Authors: Chen Wang, Jianqing She, Xiaoli Qian, Haoyu Wu, Wenyang Hao, Xiao Liang, Ning Guo, Xulei Dai, Fangzhou He, Songyang He, Jin Zhang, Yuyang Lei, Xiaozhen Zhuo, Hongbing Li, Yongbai Luo, Kai Deng, Yi Liu, Shanshan Gao, Xiao Yuan, Hui Liu, Ting Bai, Ying Xiong, John Y-J. Shyy, Zu-Yi Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:With obesity as a risk factor for atherosclerotic disease, recent research suggests that adipose tissue in obese animal models and humans can generate endocrine-like molecules that affect arterial health. Previous studies showed that microRNA-30e (miR-30e) levels are elevated in atherosclerosis and solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11), a cystine/glutamate transporter, is involved in atherogenesis. However, whether an endocrine-like link between the adipose-derived miR-30e-5p and SLC7A11 in the vascular endothelium can lead to obesity-caused atherosclerosis is unknown.METHODS:miRNA data mining and reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction validations were used to demonstrate the positive association among serum levels of miR-30e-5p, obesity, and coronary arterial disease in human patients. Transcriptomics (bulk RNA sequencing and single-nucleus RNA sequencing), metabolomics, and in silico analysis were used to establish a miR-30e-5p–SLC7A11 regulation of central carbon metabolism, mitochondrial and endothelial cell (EC) function. Mouse models with EC-specific Slc7a11 knockout (EC-Slc7a11−/−) and gain- or loss-of- function of miR-30e-5p were used to elucidate the detrimental role of this endocrine-like axis in obesity-related atherosclerosis.RESULTS:The level of adipocyte-derived miR-30e-5p was significantly upregulated in obese human patients with coronary artery disease and in obese and atherosclerotic mice. Mediated through serum exosomes, the adipocyte-generated miR-30e-5p targeted SLC7A11 mRNA in vascular ECs. SLC7A11 deficiency due to miR-30e-5p targeting dysregulated glutamate/cystine metabolism, increased glycolysis, reduced oxidative phosphorylation, and impaired mitochondrial function. The EC dysfunction could be rectified by SLC7A11 overexpression or miR-30e-5p antagonism. Administration of exogenous miR-30e-5p or white adipose tissue-derived exosomes phenocopied the increased atherosclerosis in EC-Slc7a11−/− mice. In contrast, miR-30e-5p antagomir or GW4869 treatment reduced atherosclerosis in Apoe−/− and ob/ob mice.CONCLUSIONS:Our multiomics approaches demonstrate that the adipose-derived miR-30e-5p downregulated SLC7A11 mRNA in ECs via tissue crosstalk. The resulting EC dysfunction led to obesity-related atherosclerosis in mice. These findings underscore a causality between obesity and atherosclerosis in the context of cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome.

Atherosclerosis is a multifaceted disease that involves chronic inflammation and maladapted metabolism in several tissue types.1–3 Clinical guidance suggests that patients with metabolic syndrome are at greatly increased risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.4,5 The novel concept of cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome has brought further attention to the causation between obesity and atherosclerosis.6 Despite ample clinical evidence revealing the association between metabolically impaired adipose tissue and atherosclerotic vasculature, the underlying mechanism remains unclear.

Knowledge gained from multiple studies shows a strong association between visceral adipose tissue and atherosclerotic outcomes.7,8 With obesity, impaired differentiation of adipocytes reduces the production of adiponectin but increases the secretion of proinflammatory cytokines.9 The prevalent hypothesis of atherosclerosis states that endothelial injury aggravates vascular inflammation and lipid uptake, which initiates atherosclerosis.10,11 Because adipocytes are an abundant tissue origin of exosome-carried microRNAs (miRNAs), emerging evidence suggests that adipocyte-derived exosomes can regulate vascular function and disease in an endocrine-like manner.12,13 However, it is unknown whether any exosome-associated miRNA can function as a messenger between adipose tissue and vascular endothelium to affect atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. The p53-induced microRNA-30e (miR-30e) not only functions as a tumor suppressor in multiple cancer types but also is linked to atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease in humans and rodents.14–20 miR-30e is primarily released from adipose tissue in an exosome-dependent manner.12 However, whether adipose-derived miR-30e affects the endothelium to exacerbate atherosclerosis remains unclear.

Expressed on the cell membrane, solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11) is an amino acid transporter with its main function being the promotion of cystine uptake and glutathione biosynthesis, which protect cells against oxidative stress and ferroptosis.21,22 As a ferroptosis marker, SLC7A11 plays critical roles in glutamine-glutathione metabolism and thus is involved in the pathogenesis of cancer and atherosclerosis.23,24 Obese rats fed a high-fat diet (HFD) show lower levels of Slc7a11, which was associated with increased serum tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) levels.25 Given that endothelial cell (EC) dysfunction with an exacerbated redox state causes clinical and experimental atherosclerosis, the impaired SLC7A11-glutathione axis may be involved in atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.26,27

Notably, SLC7A11 mRNA is a putative target of miR-30, which prompted us to investigate whether adipocyte-derived miR-30e can target SLC7A11 in ECs in an exosome-dependent manner.28 If true, this endocrine-like mechanism may mediate, in part, obese-aggravated atherosclerosis. Our results show elevated levels of miR-30e-5p in adipose-derived exosomes and serum from obese individuals with coronary artery disease (CAD), which was associated with atherosclerosis progression. Moreover, adipose-originated miR-30e-5p could downregulate SLC7A11 and mitochondrial function in ECs. These findings provide new insights into adipocyte-EC crosstalk and their implications for miR-30e antagonism to ameliorate obesity-enhanced atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.

Results

Elevated Level of Adipose-Derived miR-30e-5p in Obese Individuals With CAD

We initially mined published Gene Expression Omnibus data sets to identify putative miRNAs that link obesity and atherosclerosis. The level of miR-30e-5p was significantly increased in serum from patients with atherosclerosis (GSE96621) and circulating miRNA from HFD-fed mice.29 However, the miR-30e-5p level was lower in serum exosomes from ADicer knockout mice in which miRNA processing is hindered due to Dicer specific ablation in adipose tissue (Figure 1A).12 Reanalyzing the GSE25470 data set, we found that the level of miR-30e-5p was also increased in white adipose tissue (WAT) from obese patients (Figure 1B). To further investigate the causality between obesity, circulating miR-30e-5p, and atherosclerotic disease, we collected serum from patients with CAD and healthy controls (demographics in Table S3). In agreement with in silico analyses of data by others (Figure 1A and 1B), patients with CAD registered in this study had higher levels of miR-30e-5p than healthy controls (Figure 1C). The receiver operating characteristic curve revealed that patients would have a higher probability of CAD if their miR-30e-5p level was 43.53% higher than that of the healthy controls, with a sensitivity of 68.24% and specificity of 75.29% (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, 0.80; Figure 1D). We further classified patients with CAD into 3 groups according to their body mass index (BMI): normal weight (18.5 ≤ BMI ≤24.9 kg/m2), overweight (25–29.9 kg/m2), and obese (≥30 kg/m2). Serum levels of miR-30e-5p were elevated more in obese individuals with CAD (Figure 1E). Furthermore, we initially observed positive correlations of circulatory levels of miR-30e-5p with BMI, triglycerides, and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) level, but a negative correlation with high-density lipoprotein cholesterol level in the cohort (Figure S1A). After outlier exclusion, only BMI and LDL-C were significantly correlated with miR-30e-5p level, while triglycerides showed a marginal correlation (P=0.089) and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol lacked statistical correlation (Figure 1F). Exosomes were then isolated from adipose tissues and serum of obese/CAD individuals as well as nondiseased controls for further analysis (demographics in Table S4). On nanoparticle tracking analysis, these isolated exosomes had the typical size (≈100 nm; Figure 1G), and electron microscopy revealed the cup-shaped morphology (Figure 1H). The 2 groups of exosomes were then labeled with PKH67 and incubated with human aortic ECs (HAECs) in vitro (Figure 1I). The level of miR-30e-5p was increased in HAECs cocultured with exosomes from adipose tissues or serum of obese/CAD patients (Figure 1J). In line with data from human patients, miR-30e-5p levels were increased in WAT, serum exosomes, and aortic tissues of ob/ob mice, Apoe−/− mice fed an HFD, and C57BL/6 mice administered adeno-associated virus (AAV)8-PCSK9D377Y and fed an HFD (Figure 1K through 1M). However, the miR-30e-5p level was not elevated in brown adipose tissue of these 3 mouse models (Figure 1K through 1M). Collectively, results in Figure 1 suggest that the WAT-derived exosomes might transport miR-30e-5p from adipocytes to vascular endothelium in obese humans with CAD, obese mice, and atherosclerotic mice.

MiR-30e-5p Targets SLC7A11

Having established that adipose tissue-derived miR-30e-5p might be an endocrine-like miRNA regulating adipose-vessel crosstalk, we then investigated the downstream target of miR-30e-5p. For an unbiased screening, we performed RNA sequencing analysis of HAECs transfected with miR-30e-5p or control miRNA. Gene Ontology analysis of differentially regulated pathways showed that the miR-30e-5p level was correlated with nitric oxide biosynthetic process, glutathione metabolic process, regulation of fat cell differentiation, cellular oxidant detoxification, lipids and atherosclerosis, and SLC-mediated transmembrane transport (Figure 2A). The heat map shown in Figure 2B reveals that miR-30e-5p overexpression downregulated genes involved in oxidant detoxification, glutathione metabolic process, and nitric oxide biosynthesis. In addition, miR-30e-5p overexpression affected genes involved in lipid and atherosclerosis as well as SLC-mediated transmembrane transport. Markedly, miR-30e-5p downregulated atheroprotective genes (eg, SOD2) and upregulated atheroprone genes (eg, NOX4) involved in lipid and atherosclerosis. For SLC family transporters, miR-30e-5p downregulated SLC7A11 and upregulated SLC2A3 (GLUT3), which have been shown to affect cellular redox state.30 Using TargetScan, we predicted that miR-30e-5p could target the 3′-UTR of SLC7A11 mRNA (Figure S2A). SLC7A11 level was reduced in dysfunctional ECs due to lipid accumulation (GSE246083) and in ECs exposed to atheroprone flow (GSE118717).31,32 These in silico analyses showing miR-30e-5p targeting SLC7A11 were depicted by Venn analysis in Figure 2C. According to the functional roles of SLC7A11 in cell death and the glutathione redox system (Figure 2D), we theorized that miR-30e-5p targets the 3′-UTR of SLC7A11 mRNA (Figure S2A). Of note, miR-30e-5p and its targeted sequences in the 3′-UTR of SLC7A11 mRNA are highly conserved in various mammalian species, including human, mouse, rat, chimp, rhesus, pig, and cow (Figure S2A). We then constructed luciferase reporters fused with the SLC7A11-3′-UTR (wild-type) or mutated SLC7A11-3′-UTR (MT) to test whether miR-30e-5p directly targets the SLC7A11 mRNA (Figure 2E). Overexpression of miR-30e-5p suppressed the SLC7A11-3′-UTR (wild-type) luciferase activity but not MT activity (Figure 2E). Furthermore, HAECs transfected with miR-30e-5p had lower protein and mRNA levels of SLC7A11 (Figure 2F and Figure S2B). In contrast, inhibition of miR-30e-5p increased levels of SLC7A11 and its mRNA, in a yin-yang manner (Figure 2G and Figure S2C). Furthermore, miR-30e-5p and SLC7A11 mRNA levels were enriched in Ago1 and Ago2, namely, the miRNA-induced silencing complexes, in HAECs transfected with miR-30e-5p (Figure 2H), so SLC7A11 mRNA was a bona fide target of miR-30e-5p in ECs. Because SLC7A11 regulates the exchange of glutamate and cystine, we analyzed metabolites in HAECs cocultured with adipocytes that were stimulated with palmitate and transfected with or without miR-30e-5p inhibitor. As anticipated, the cocultured HAECs and the secreted exosomes had an increased level of miR-30e-5p, and miR-30e-5p inhibitor abolished this increase (Figure S2D). The untargeted metabolomics depicted by KEGG showed that glutamate and glutathione metabolism were significantly enriched (Figure 2I). In line with these metabolomic changes, HAECs cocultured with exosomes isolated from palmitate-stimulated adipocytes showed an increased level of glutamate and a decreased level of cystine. These glutamate/cystine alterations were partially restored by treatment with the miR-30e-5p inhibitor, despite not being significantly different (Figure 2J). Importantly, incubation of HAECs with serum from obese patients with CAD also led to increased glutamate and decreased cystine levels (Figure 2K). In line with these results, HAECs treated with serum from obese patients with CAD showed reduced cystine uptake, which was rectified by miR-30e-5p inhibition (Figure 2L). Furthermore, given that miR-30e-5p targets SLC7A11 mRNA, adenovirus (Ad)-mediated SLC7A11 overexpression rescued the cystine uptake by ECs (Figure 2M).

SLC7A11 Is Decreased in Experimental Atherosclerotic Mice and Patients With CAD

To explore the crosstalk between adipocytes and vascular endothelium in the context of miR-30e-5p–SLC7A11 and atherogenesis, we performed single-nucleus RNA sequencing of WAT from Apoe−/− mice fed an HFD or chow diet. On t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (tSNE) analysis, we identified 5 cell clusters, including adipocytes, epithelial cells, fibroblasts, ECs, and immune cells (Figure 3A), according to their distinct gene expression profiles (Figure S3A). HFD increased the number of proinflammatory macrophages, as signified by increased levels of Tnf, Nlrp3, and Cd74 (Figure S3B). In adipocytes and ECs, HFD reduced the Slc7a11 mRNA level (Figure 3B and 3C). Pseudotime trajectory analysis demonstrated that Slc7a11 expression was higher in early stages of adipocyte and EC subpopulations (state 1) and decreased afterward to the lowest level in state 5. Moreover, atherosclerotic tissues had the highest level of state 5 cells (Figure 3D and 3E). These results suggest SLC7A11 suppression during the atherogenic course and an inverse association between SLC7A11 abundance and EC homeostasis. In line with this notion, mRNA and protein levels of Slc7a11 were reduced in the aortic intima of atherosclerotic Apoe−/− mice (Figure 3F). Slc7a11 level reduction in aortic intima was recapitulated in C57BL/6 mice administered AAV8-PCSK9D377Y and fed an HFD (Figure 3G). For human atherosclerosis, we performed immunostaining experiments to examine the level of SLC7A11 in atherosclerotic plaque from patients who underwent carotid endarterectomy (demographics in Table S5). Shown in Figure 3H, SLC7A11 expression was reduced in atherosclerotic lesions as compared with nondiseased specimens. To explore changes in metabolites resulting from the differential expression of SLC7A11, we performed untargeted metabolomics analysis of serum from patients with CAD and nondiseased controls (normal controls) in a discovery cohort (demographics in Table S6). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis revealed that glutamate metabolism, cysteine and methionine metabolism, and glutathione metabolism varied the most between patients with CAD and normal controls (Figure 3I). With SLC7A11 mediating the extracellular influx of cystine at the expense of intracellular glutamate efflux, we studied the serum metabolites in a validation cohort (demographics in Table S7). The lower level of glutamate associated with a higher level of cystine was confirmed in patients with CAD in the discovery and validation cohorts (Figure 3J and 3L). However, the serum level of glutamate was negatively correlated with BMI (Figure 3K and 3M). Moreover, receiver operating characteristic curves in Figure 3N showed that patients with glutamate levels <37.93% and 31.82% of those in normal controls might have CAD, with sensitivity 41.38% and 40.91% and specificity 96.55% and 90.91% in the discovery and validation cohorts, respectively (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve: 0.68 in the discovery cohort; 0.69 in the validation cohort). Taken together, data in Figure 3 suggest that SLC7A11 was downregulated in the vascular endothelium in patients with CAD as well as mice with experimental atherosclerosis, thus decreasing glutamate level but increasing cystine level in the circulation.

MiR-30e-5p–SLC7A11 Regulates Central Carbon Metabolism and Mitochondrial Function

With the established role of SLC7A11 in cystine uptake and biosynthesis, we then explored the effect of SLC7A11 on EC metabolomic changes by using liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) analysis to compare metabolites in HAECs infected with SLC7A11 (Ad-SLC7A11) versus Ad-Null. SLC7A11 overexpression significantly increased levels of 29 metabolites in ECs versus Ad-Null–infected control cells (Figure 4A; Table S8). Pathway-enrichment analysis revealed that the pathways including central carbon metabolism in cancer, carbon metabolism, ABC transporters, biosynthesis of amino acids, aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis, and alanine, aspartate, and glutamate metabolism were significantly changed by SLC7A11 overexpression (Figure 4B). Because glycolysis and the tricarboxylic acid cycle are 2 crucial pathways in central carbon metabolism, we used Seahorse assays to investigate the role of SLC7A11 in regulating EC glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation. SLC7A11 overexpression in ECs decreased extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) but increased OCR (Figure 4C and 4D). By contrast, SLC7A11 knockdown increased ECAR and decreased OCR (Figure S4A and S4B). Consistent with miR-30e-5p targeting SLC7A11 mRNA, antisense oligonucleotides against miR-30e-5p increased OCR and decreased ECAR in ECs (Figure S4C and S4D). Furthermore, SLC7A11 overexpression reversed the miR-30e-5p–increased ECAR and miR-30e-5p–decreased OCR (Figure 4E and 4F). These results suggest that the miR-30e-5p–SLC7A11 axis impaired carbon metabolism in ECs. Because of the negative effect of this axis on oxidative phosphorylation, we studied whether it regulates EC mitochondrial function. On confocal imaging using MitoTracker, HAECs transfected with miR-30e-5p showed decreased mitochondria in ECs and increased mitochondrial fragmentation, which was rectified by SLC7A11 overexpression (Figure 4G). These results led us to theorize that this endocrine-like axis causes EC dysfunction. In agreement, SLC7A11 knockdown in ECs increased the expression of FN1 and SNAI1 but decreased that of CDH5 and NR2F2 (Figure 4H), indicative of augmented endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition. Regarding redox stress, SLC7A11 knockdown increased reactive oxygen species levels in HAECs, and SLC7A11 overexpression reverted the miR-30e–increased reactive oxygen species production (Figure 4I and 4J). Consistently, SLC7A11 deficiency decreased endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) Ser-1177 phosphorylation, which in turn reduced the level of intracellular nitric oxide, manifesting the reduced eNOS-derived nitric oxide bioavailability (Figure S4H and S4I). In line with this EC functional defect, SLC7A11-deficient HAECs exhibited enhanced migration and proliferation (Figure S4E through S4G), which supports the beneficial effect of SLC7A11 in maintaining EC homeostasis and its EC repair capacity.

EC-Specific Slc7a11 Deficiency Aggravates Atherosclerosis in Mice

To determine the atherogenic effect resulting from the miR-30e–SLC7A11 axis, we created EC-Slc7a11−/− mice. Endothelial ablation of Slc7a11 in this line of mice was validated in isolated pulmonary and aortic ECs (Figure S5A and S5B). To induce atherosclerosis, 8-week-old male and female EC-Slc7a11−/− mice (ECKO) and their Slc7a11flox/flox wild-type littermates were administered AAV8-PCSK9D377Y via tail vein injection, then fed an HFD for 12 weeks before being euthanized. Although lipid profiles, including total cholesterol, triglycerides, LDL-C, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels, did not significantly differ among EC-Slc7a11−/− and Slc7a11flox/flox littermates (Figure S5C), en face Oil-red O staining revealed a significant increase in atherosclerotic lesions in the aortas of EC-Slc7a11−/− male and female mice as compared with Slc7a11flox/flox littermates (Figure S5D). However, control EC-Slc7a11−/− or Slc7a11flox/flox mice fed a chow diet for 12 weeks showed little, if any, atherosclerosis (Figure S5D). Increased Oil-red O and Cd68 staining were also evident in the aortic roots of EC-Slc7a11−/− mice, thereby reinforcing that Slc7a11 ablation enhanced the atherosclerosis susceptibility (Figure S5E). Of note, EC-Slc7a11−/− mice showed decreased serum level of glutamate and increased level of cystine as compared with Slc7a11flox/flox littermates (Figure S5F and S5G), which suggests detrimental effects because of Slc7a11 deficiency in the vascular endothelium.

Exogenous miR-30e-5p Exacerbates Atherosclerosis

With the deduced atheroprotective role of SLC7A11 (shown in Figure S5), we then tested whether adipose tissue-derived miR-30e affects aortic levels of SLC7A11 and enhances atherosclerosis. For this, we constructed an AAV9 vector overexpressing a CD63-Flag fusion protein to label the exosomes originated from adipose tissues. This Ad was injected into WAT located in the inguinal area of Apoe−/− mice with GW4869 (hinder the secretion of exosomes from adipose tissues) or without. As shown in Figure 5A, these adipocyte-derived exosomes could be uptaken by aortic ECs. However, GW4869 coadministration inhibited this, resulting in a decrease in miR-30e-5p and an increase in SLC7A11 level in the aortic intima (Figure 5B and 5C).

Next, we injected AAV9-adiponectin-miR-30e-5p or AAV9-adiponectin-Null into WAT located in the inguinal area of Apoe−/− mice. The virus recipient mice were then fed an HFD for 12 weeks and treated with or without GW4869 (Figure 5D). The level of miR-30e-5p was significantly elevated in WAT of Apoe−/− mice receiving AAV9-adiponectin-miR-30e-5p, as compared with mice receiving AAV9-adiponectin-Null (Figure 5E through 5G). GW4869 coadministration reduced the circulatory and aortic levels of miR-30e-5p (Figure 5H through 5K), with a complementary increase in Slc7a11 mRNA and protein in aortas of Apoe−/− mice receiving AAV9-adiponectin-miR-30e-5p (Figure 5L). All subgroups of mice did not differ in total cholesterol, triglycerides, and LDL-C levels (Figure 5M). At the disease level, AAV9-adiponectin-miR-30e-5p administration significantly increased atherosclerosis, compared with mice receiving AAV9-adiponectin-Null, and GW4869 coadministration alleviated atherosclerosis (Figure 5N through 5P). Moreover, Seahorse assays showed that WAT-derived miR-30e-5p caused increased ECAR and decreased OCR. As anticipated, GW4869 administration reverted the miR-30e-5p–increased ECAR and miR-30e-5p–decreased OCR in aortic ECs (Figure 5Q and 5R).

To further elucidate the role of miR-30e-5p in WAT-derived exosomes in atherosclerotic disease, we injected WAT-derived exosomes into Apoe−/− mice in vivo. As shown in Figure S6, administration of WAT-derived exosomes increased the circulatory and aortic levels of miR-30e-5p, reduced the Slc7a11 mRNA level in aortas, and aggravated atherosclerosis, as compared with Apoe−/− mice receiving PBS. Together, the results in Figure 5 and Figure S6 show that exogenously delivered miR-30e-5p or WAT-derived exosomes, mimicking the obesity-generated miR-30e-5p, aggravate atherosclerosis in Apoe−/− mice.

Inhibition of miR-30e-5p Reduces Atherosclerosis

Complementary to experiments in Figure 5, we used a miR-30e-5p loss-of-function approach to test whether inhibition of miR-30e-5p in adipose tissue could mitigate atherosclerosis by injecting AAV9-adiponectin-miR-30e-5p sponge (anti-miR-30e) or AAV9-adiponectin-Null (normal control) into WAT of ob/ob mice (Figure 6A). Levels of miR-30e-5p were decreased in WAT, blood, and aortic intima. However, levels of the Slc7a11 mRNA and protein were increased in aortas of ob/ob mice receiving AAV9-adiponectin-anti-miR-30e, as compared with mice receiving AAV9-adiponectin-Null (Figure 6B through 6I). This AAV9-adiponectin-miR-30e-5p antagonism significantly alleviated atherosclerosis but had little effect on lipid profile (Figure 6J through 6M). Moreover, miR-30e inhibition in WAT decreased ECAR and increased OCR in aortic ECs isolated from ob/ob mice (Figure 6N and 6O). Inhibition of the secretion of adipocyte exosomes by local injection of GW4869 recapitulated the anti-atherosclerotic effect of AAV9-adiponectin-miR-30e-5p sponge (Figure 6).

For translational relevance, we injected miR-30e-antagomir encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles (LNP-anti-miR-30e-5p) to WAT of ob/ob mice (Figure S7A). This administration lowered the level of miR-30e-5p in the injected adipose tissues and also in circulation and aortas (Figure S7B through S7D). It significantly increased the expression of Slc7a11 in the aortic intima, with attendant reduction of atherosclerosis (Figure S7E through S7H). Because ob/ob mice administered LNP-anti-miR-30e-5p or LNP-Control (Ctrl) did not significantly differ in lipid profile or body weight (Figure S7I and S7J), the mechanism by which LNP-anti-miR-30e-5p reduced atherosclerosis would be independent of hyperlipidemia. Rather, LNP-anti-miR-30e-5p administration decreased the circulatory level of glutamate and increased that of cystine as compared with LNP-Ctrl administration (Figure S7K). Altogether, results from animal models with EC ablation of SLC7A11 (Figure S5), miR-30e-5p gain-of-function (Figure 5; Figure S6), and miR-30e-5p loss-of-function (Figure 6; Figure S7) indicate that the endocrine-like miR-30e-5p–SLC7A11 axis would contribute to obesity-aggravated atherosclerosis in rodents.

Discussion

This study elucidates a pathophysiological causality between obesity and atherosclerosis via an endocrine-like mechanism involving the adipocyte-derived miR-30e-5p and its target SLC7A11 in the vascular endothelium. The major findings are (1) elevated circulating levels of miR-30e-5p in obese individuals with CAD; (2) decreased SLC7A11 levels in patients with CAD and experimental atherosclerotic mice; (3) miR-30e-5p directly targeted the 3′-UTR of SLC7A11 mRNA, resulting in dysfunctional ECs, including dysregulated thioreduction, increased glycolysis, reduced oxidative phosphorylation, and impaired mitochondrial function; and (4) ablation of Slc7a11 in endothelium aggravated, but inhibition of miR-30e-5p in adipose tissue alleviated, atherosclerosis in mouse models. From these findings, we propose that miR-30e-5p targeting SLC7A11 links the maladapted adipose tissue with atherosclerosis in the arterial wall. Thus, the miR-30e-5p–SLC7A11 axis might be integral to the cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome. This notion supports the emerging view that adipose tissue, traditionally considered an energy storage depot, is an active endocrine organ that affects vascular health via secreted factors.33

In diet-induced obesity, expanded adipose tissues become deleterious, imposing inflammation and redox stress on the vasculature, thus augmenting atherosclerosis.9 Conceptually, adipose tissue constitutes a depot of circulating exosomal miRNAs, a novel mode of cell-to-cell communication regulating gene expression in distant tissues.12 Our experiments involving human patients demonstrated a significant elevation of adipose-derived miR-30e-5p in obese individuals with CAD, which was associated with the progression of atherosclerosis. This association was consistent with results from animal experiments revealing increased levels of miR-30e-5p in WAT, circulating exosomes, and aortic tissue in obese rodents. In line with our findings, a previous study showed an increase in miR-30e-5p levels in circulating extracellular vesicles in db/db mice, which coincided with coronary microvascular dysfunction and rarefaction.20 With obesity significantly increasing the risk of cardiovascular impairments, miR-30e-5p may be multifaceted in the cardiovascular system. Furthermore, our results show that miR-30e-5p–promoted atherosclerosis was independent of LDL-C levels, so miR-30e-5p may directly act on the vascular endothelium rather than affecting liver lipid metabolism to regulate atherosclerosis.

The biological functions of miRNAs are often due to their regulation of multiple targets within the same functional network. We theorize that the adipose tissue-derived miR-30e-5p elicits a detrimental network to disrupt the redox balance (via SLC7A11) and mitochondrial homeostasis (via p53 and BECN1) in ECs.34,35 Other miR-30e targets that might also affect EC functions include adrenoceptor α2A and α2B, PLCγ1 (phospholipase C γ1), and Negr1 (neuronal growth regulator 1).17,36,37 Data from our single-nucleus RNA sequencing (Figure 3A) indicated that the levels of adrenoceptor α2A and α2B were low in all cell clusters and almost undetectable in ECs (Figure S3C). PLCγ1, a direct effector of VEGFR2 (vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2) and other receptor tyrosine kinases, has an atherogenic effect.37,38 However, our single-nucleus RNA sequencing data showed that PLCγ1 levels were elevated in adipocytes but decreased in ECs under an HFD (Figure S3C). Of note, Negr1 variants are associated with BMI and obesity.39,40 Genetic ablation of Negr1 in rodents promoted adiposity and increased plasma glucose and insulin levels.41 A recent human population study found adiposity and type II diabetes linked to lower serum levels of NEGR1 (neuronal growth regulator 1).42 Consistently, our data revealed that an HFD reduced the level of Negr1 in both adipocytes and ECs (Figure S3C). Obesity can increase other atherosclerotic risk factors, including diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and chronic kidney disease.43–46 Providing that NEGR1 has a role in BMI and neuropsychiatric regulation, further studies on miR-30e regulation of NEGR1 in terms of chronic kidney disease syndromes are warranted.

With its enzymatic functions of cystine uptake and glutathione biosynthesis, an optimal level of SLC7A11 protects cells against redox and ferroptotic stresses. A previous study showed that the ferroptosis inhibitor ferrostatin-1 reduced iron accumulation and increased the level of Slc7a11 in the aorta of atherosclerotic Apoe−/− mice.24 Genetic ablation of IL-17D (Interleukin-17D) increased the expression of SLC7A11 and decreased atherosclerosis in Apoe−/− mice.47 Several other miRNAs, such as miR-144-3p, miR-181, and miR-429, can also target SLC7A11 mRNA.47–49

Our finding that miR-30e-5p targeting SLC7A11 mRNA exacerbated atherosclerosis is novel and significant in several ways. First, the increase in atherosclerosis in ob/ob mice administered miR-30e-5p and EC-Slc7a11−/− mice recapitulated the causality between obesity-increased miR-30e-5p, EC dysfunction, and atherosclerosis in humans. Second, downregulation of the miR-30e-5p–SLC7A11 axis caused a metabolic shift from glycolysis to mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, which enhanced mitochondrial function in ECs. Third, in addition to miR-30e-5p, proinflammatory cytokine TNF-α and oscillatory flow also decreased the level of SLC7A11 in ECs (Figure S8A). These results suggest that other atheroprone factors synergize with obesity to reduce SLC7A11 levels in the vascular endothelium.

The present study has several limitations. Although we established a link between miR-30e-5p and SLC7A11 in the pathogenesis of obesity-related atherosclerosis, the upstream mechanism by which obesity increases the level of miR-30e-5p remains unknown. Previous studies have shown that p53, acting as a transcription factor, can transactivate miR-30e in tumor cells.50 Coincidentally, p53 levels are increased in WAT of obese rodents and humans.51,52 If indeed the miR-30e-5p–SLC7A11 axis is regulated by p53, the precise cell subcluster in adipose tissue where this occurs deserves further elucidation. With the efficacy of the LNP-delivered anti-miR-30e-5p to reduce atherosclerosis, the translational potential of this preclinical study requires validation in clinical trials. With other miR-30e targets, the off-target effects associated with the antisense RNA approach also need to be considered. Nonetheless, this multiomics study elucidates an adipose-vessel crosstalk involving adipocyte-derived miR-30e-5p and its targeted SLC7A11 in vascular ECs. This endocrine-like axis, linking adipose tissue, endothelial dysfunction, and atherosclerosis, may explain in part the ramifications of obesity-caused atherosclerosis.

Impact of Dental Treatment Simulation App on Anxiety of Pediatric Dental Patients: An Observational Study
Authors: S. K. Mahboob Rahaman, Niladri Maiti, Sonali Harish, Mahantesh Achanur, Michael S. Pramanik, Adnan Ahmed
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:The study aimed to investigate the effect of a dental treatment simulation app on the anxiety levels of pediatric dental patients.Materials and Methods:Forty children, aged 4–8 years and with no prior dental experience, were randomly assigned to either the control group (group I) or the dental app group (group II). The children’s anxiety levels were measured before and after undergoing restorative dental treatment, using the Chota Bheem Chutki pictorial scale.Results:The initial anxiety levels were similar across both groups. Post treatment, the dental app group exhibited a significant reduction in anxiety compared to the control group. This suggests that the simulation app has a beneficial effect in lowering dental anxiety in children.Conclusion:The dental treatment simulation app can effectively reduce pre and postoperative anxiety in pediatric patients, potentially enhancing their overall dental experience.

INTRODUCTION

Dental anxiety is a common issue among pediatric patients, often leading to struggles in delivering effective dental care. Children with high levels of dental anxiety may avoid dental visits, resulting in poor oral health outcomes. Traditional methods of reducing anxiety, such as behavioral techniques and pharmacological interventions, have shown varying degrees of success but also come with limitations and potential side effects.[1]

The advent of digital technology has introduced novel approaches to address this issue. One such innovation is the use of mobile applications aimed at preparing children for dental visits through interactive simulations. These applications can visually guide children through different dental procedures, helping them understand what to expect and thereby reducing fear of the unknown.[2]

The dental treatment simulation app is specifically designed to assuage dental anxiety by familiarizing young patients with common dental procedures in a non-threatening, engaging manner. The app includes colorful illustrations, simple language, and interactive features, making it user-friendly and appealing to young children.[34]

The objective of this study was to evaluate the impact of the dental treatment simulation app on pediatric dental patients’ anxiety levels during restorative dental treatment.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This was a randomized controlled trial designed to evaluate the effect of a dental treatment simulation app on the anxiety levels of pediatric patients. The study included 40 children aged 4–8 years who required restorative treatment in any tooth and had no prior dental experience. Participants without any significant medical or psychological conditions were included in the study. Children with previous dental experiences or those with severe medical or psychological conditions are excluded from the study. Participants were randomly assigned to either the control group or the dental app group, ensuring an equal number of children in each group.

Control group

These children received the standard pre-treatment briefing typically used in pediatric dental practices.

Dental app group

These participants used a dental treatment simulation app (Baby Panda: Dental Care) designed to familiarize them with the dental environment and procedures. The app uses engaging animations and interactive elements to simulate various aspects of a dental visit.

Procedure

Initial anxiety levels were recorded for each child by using the pictorial scale before any treatment or interaction with the app. In the control group, children were given a standard verbal briefing about the dental procedure through the tell-show-do technique. In the dental app group, children interacted with the dental treatment simulation app for 15 minutes before the dental procedure. All children underwent a similar restorative dental treatment. Anxiety levels were measured again using the Chota Bheem Chutki pictorial scale immediately after the treatment. The scale uses two distinct cards: one for boys featuring Chotta Bheem and one for girls featuring Chutki. Each card shows six illustrations depicting emotions from happiness to unhappiness, plus a running emotion. Children selected the illustration matching their current emotion. A happy face was given a score of 1, while an unhappy and running face scored 6 [Figure 1].[5]

The differences in anxiety levels between the pre- and post-treatment phases were analyzed using appropriate statistical tests to compare both groups. The primary outcome was the change in anxiety levels from pre- to post-treatment, with significance set at P < 0.05.

RESULT

The baseline measurement showed no significant difference (P > 0.05) between the two groups, indicating similar initial anxiety levels, whereas in the dental app group, post-treatment anxiety levels significantly decreased compared to the control group (P < 0.05). DISCUSSION

The findings of this study highlight the potential of the dental treatment simulation app as a valuable tool in reducing dental anxiety among pediatric patients. Children’s anxiety regarding dental visits is a prevalent issue that often leads to the avoidance of necessary dental care and negative experiences. By utilizing interactive and engaging simulation apps, dental professionals can help alleviate this anxiety and enhance the dental experience for young patients. The result of our study in accordance to the studies conducted by Patil VH et al. (2017),[2] Panchal J et al. (2022),[4] and Elicherla SR et al. (2019)[6]

The app’s effectiveness is evident as the significant reduction in anxiety levels in the dental app group compared to the control group underscores its efficacy. Several factors contribute to this success. First, the app aids in familiarizing children with dental procedures in a non-threatening environment, mitigating the fear of the unknown. Second, interactive elements and animated characters capture children’s attention, diverting their focus away from anxiety triggers. Lastly, the app educates children about dental procedures in an age-appropriate manner, helping them feel more informed and reducing their fear.[67]

Integrating such simulation apps into pediatric dental practices offers several significant benefits. Reduced anxiety can lead to improved cooperation during dental treatments, facilitating smoother and quicker procedures. Positive dental experiences in early childhood can encourage lifelong proper dental care and regular dental visits. In addition, parents may feel more at ease knowing their child is less anxious about dental visits, potentially increasing their willingness to adhere to recommended dental schedules.

A potential limitation of this study is the small sample size and the uneven age distribution among the children. Given that the treatment was confined to non-invasive restorative procedures, it is recommended that future research incorporate invasive procedures, such as pulp therapy and extractions utilizing local anesthesia. This would allow for a more comprehensive evaluation of the dental application as an effective tool for pediatric dental setup.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, the study’s findings suggest promising future directions for the use of simulation apps in pediatric dentistry. More research could explore long-term effects on children’s anxiety and dental health outcomes. In addition, development of similar apps tailored to different age groups or specific dental procedures could enhance their efficacy.

Unraveling the generalized Bergshoeff-de Roo identification
Achilleas Gitsis, and Falk Hassler

Abstract
We revisit duality-covariant higher-derivative corrections which arise from the generalized Bergshoeff-de Roo (gBdR) identification, a prescription that gives rise to a two parameter family of α ′ \alpha^{\prime} -corrections to the low-energy effective action of the bosonic and the heterotic string. Although it is able to reproduce all corrections at the leading and sub-leading ( α ′ 2 \alpha^{\prime 2} ) order purely from symmetry considerations, a geometric interpretation, like for the two-derivative action and its gauge transformation is lacking. To address this issue and to pave the way for the future exploration of higher-derivative (=higher-loop for the β \beta -functions of the underlying σ \sigma -model) corrections to generalized dualities, consistent truncations and integrable σ \sigma -models, we recover the gBdR identification’s results from the Poláček-Siegel construction that provides a natural notion of torsion and curvature in generalized geometry.

Introduction

Gravity is perturbatively non-renormalizable and should therefore only be seen as an effective theory that requires modification when approaching high energies in the ultraviolet (UV) regime. Currently, it is still debated what its correct UV completion is and while there are candidates, like string theory, even without a complete knowledge of the fundamental theory, consistency conditions can be leveraged to rule out theories that are incompatible with a putative theory of quantum gravity.

A common feature of different approaches to quantizing gravity is the emergence of new symmetries. They have to be compatible with all directly observed symmetries, like reparameterizations (diffeomorphisms) and the gauge transformations that accompany additional degrees of freedom interacting with gravity. Further symmetry transformations that go beyond this obvious part will restrict admissible quantum corrections. Here, the guiding principle is: The more restrictive, the better (as long as no observations are contradicted), because it increases the predictive power of the assumed symmetry. As an example of this approach consider the two-derivative action

2
Twisted Poláček-Siegel construction

Riemann curvature and torsion are central concepts in differential geometry, but their extension to generalized geometry is not straightforward. A generalized version of the torsion tensor can be defined based on the generalized Lie derivative, but the generalized Riemann tensor is more elusive. A first naive guess – the Riemann tensor of standard differential geometry – does not transform covariantly under generalized diffeomorphisms and double Lorentz transformations. It has to be further modified and projected. Poláček and Siegel
[
21
]
proposed to resort to an extended version of the physical space-time
M
with a flat generalized connection
2
2

2

A flat connection has only torsion but no curvature.
to overcome this problem. We will call this extended space mega-space to hint that it is even larger than the usual doubled space of
M
used in double field theory. Especially recently, this approach has proven very useful in exploring generalized dualities, consistent truncations and the geometry of extended field theories. A still open question is how higher-derivative corrections fit in this picture. To answer it, we will consider a twisted version
[
21
]
of the original construction, which is capable of describing gauged double field theory as it arises for the heterotic string. Considering that we eventually want to make contact with the gBdR identification, this is the natural starting point. In the following, we will review its most important aspects.

\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}U_{1}|\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}U_{2}|A\otimes B|V_{2}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}|V_{1}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}U_{2}|B|V_{2}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}U_{1}|A|V_{1}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}

(44)

for contracting tensor products of states. All physical degrees of freedom are encoded in the frame
\mathcal{E}
which is valued in
G_{\mathrm{PS}}
. However, we already know that
G_{\mathrm{PS}}=\mathrm{O}(d+p,d+q)
from (
28
). Thus, it has to contain some auxiliary degrees of freedom which eventually can be completely expressed in terms of an
\mathrm{O}(d,d)
valued generalized frame. Fixing all auxiliary fields lies at the heart of the gBdR identification. To help this process, we decompose

\boxed{\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{A}E\qquad\text{with}\qquad E\in\mathrm{O}(d,d)}

(45)

into the
\mathrm{O}(d,d)
-frame
E
, and the coset element
\mathcal{A}\in G_{\mathrm{PS}}/\mathrm{O}(d,d)
, which we aim to fix completely by torsion constraints and gauge fixing.

The expression for the twisted reduced torsion

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{A}}+\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{A}}|\mathcal{E}|_{\beta}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\uptau^{\beta}+\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{A}}|\uptau^{\beta}\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{E}|_{\beta}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}

(46)

is taken directly from
[
23
]
. There, we also find that the generalized fluxes

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{L}_{\scalebox{0.75}{\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}}_{\mathcal{A}}|\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{-1}

(47)

are computed by the heterotic generalized Lie derivative

\mathcal{L}_{\scalebox{0.75}{\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}}U|}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}V|=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}V|\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}U|\partial V\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}+\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}V|\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}U|\mathcal{Z}|\partial U\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}-\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}V|\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}U|R^{\alpha}\otimes 1|_{\alpha}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\,,

(48)

with the heterotic
\mathcal{Z}
-operator

\mathcal{Z}=2\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}\odot\mathcal{K}^{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}=2K^{AB}\odot K_{AB}+R^{\alpha}_{A}\odot R_{\alpha}^{A}+\tfrac{1}{2}R^{\alpha\beta}\odot R_{\alpha\beta}

(49)

where the
\odot
-product denotes the symmetric tensor product

A\odot B=\tfrac{1}{2}(A\otimes B+B\otimes A)\,.

(50)

As already mentioned in section
2.1
, we are dealing here with a special case of the heterotic Poláček-Siegel construction because
\kappa_{\alpha\beta}
has no null-directions. Comparing with
[
23
]
, we therefore find that all undertilded indices like
\underaccent{\tilde}{\alpha}
drop out while the tilde from the remaining indices is removed. The generators
\uptau^{\beta}
are the same ones we have already encountered in (
41
).

With the parameterization (
45
) of the frame
\mathcal{E}
, the generalized fluxes in (
47
) can be further simplified. To this end, we first take into account that
E
is only generated by
K_{AB}
and therewith satisfies

E|_{\alpha}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=|_{\alpha}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\,,

(51)

as can be seen from the matrix representation of its generators in (
24
). Leveraging this property of
E
, we find that the matrix elements

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{A}\beta}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{A}}|\mathcal{E}|_{\alpha}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{A}}|\mathcal{A}|_{\beta}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{A}\beta}

(52)

are exclusively governed by
\mathcal{A}
. Still, there are two more places where
E
makes its appearance, namely in the flat derivatives

D_{A}=E_{A}{}^{I}\partial_{I}\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad E_{A}{}^{I}=\langle_{A}|E|^{I}\rangle\,,

(53)

and the corresponding generalized fluxes

F_{ABC}=3D_{[A}E_{B}{}^{I}E_{C]I}\,.

(54)

With the relations (
51
)-(
54
),
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{A}}
eventually simplifies to

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{A}}=&\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{A}}|\mathcal{A}|^{B}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\left(D_{B}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}^{-1}+\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{F}_{B}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\right)-\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{A}}|\mathcal{A}|_{\beta}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\mathcal{A}R^{\beta}\mathcal{A}^{-1}+\\
&\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{A}}|D_{B}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{A}|^{B}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\,,\end{aligned}}

(55)

where
\boldsymbol{F}_{A}
denotes the generalized fluxes for the physical frame contracted with the generators
K^{BC}
,

\boldsymbol{F}_{A}=F_{ABC}K^{BC}\,.

(56)

Although it might not be obvious at this point, (
55
) is essential to perform the first part of the gBdR identification. To already get a glimpse why, we take a look at the components of the twisted reduced torsion (
46
)

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{B}}|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}}|_{\mathcal{C}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}}+3\mathcal{A}_{[\mathcal{A}|\delta}(\uptau^{\delta})_{|\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}]}\,.

(57)

Besides the components of the heterotic generalized fluxes,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{B}}|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{A}}|_{\mathcal{C}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}
, the second quantity that enters here are the components of the auxiliary fields
\mathcal{A}
in the frame
\mathcal{E}
. The gBdR identification arises by setting certain torsion components to zero, such that all of them are fixed in terms of the heterotic generalized fluxes (
54
) and possibly their flat derivatives (
53
). Doing this systematically requires additional information about the generalized structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}
, which we will discuss in the next section.

For completeness, note that there is also a dilatonic twisted reduced torsion. It is given by
[
23
]

|\mathcal{T}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=\mathcal{A}|^{B}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}F_{B}-D_{B}\mathcal{A}|^{B}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}-\uptau^{\beta}\mathcal{A}|_{\beta}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\,,

(58)

with the dilatonic generalized flux

F_{A}=2D_{A}\Phi-\partial_{I}E_{A}{}^{I}

(59)

for the generalized dilaton
\Phi
. With the heterotic dilatonic flux

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}\Phi+\partial_{I}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{I}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{I}\partial_{I}\,,

(60)

(
58
) further simplifies to

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{A}}|\mathcal{T}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}}+\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{B}\gamma}(\uptau^{\gamma})^{\mathcal{B}}{}_{\mathcal{A}}\,.

(61)

Similar to (
46
), its first term contains one derivative, while its second term is purely algebraic. Hence, it could also be used to fix at least parts of the auxiliary fields
\mathcal{A}
through torsion constraints. But for the gBdR identification this will not happen. There, it only plays a spectator role.

2.2
Generalized structure group transformations

In the last subsection, we have seen that the Poláček-Siegel construction requires auxiliary fields
\mathcal{A}
besides the generalized frame
E
on the physical space
M
. As mentioned above, these fields should be eventually fixed by imposing constraints on the torsion tensor
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}}
in (
46
). But this can only work when these constraints are invariant under the action of the generalized structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}
. Therefore, we have to figure out the transformation behavior of
E
,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}}
. As instructed by
[
23
]
, the transformations of the former two can be extracted from the quantity

\delta_{\xi}\mathbb{E}=\mathcal{A}^{-1}\delta_{\xi}\mathcal{A}+\delta_{\xi}EE^{-1}\,.

(62)

They give rise to what we call the master equation for the gauge transformations

\boxed{\delta_{\xi}\mathbb{E}=\left(2D_{[B}\xi_{A]}+\xi^{C}F_{CAB}\right)K^{AB}+\xi^{A}\mathcal{A}^{-1}D_{A}\mathcal{A}-\xi^{\alpha}R_{\alpha}-D_{A}\xi^{\beta}R_{\beta}^{A}+\xi^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\uptau_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,.}

(63)

Looking at this relation, we see that
\mathrm{O}(d,d)
-generalized diffeomorphisms are captured by the first two terms with the parameter
\xi^{A}
. They can be extended together with the third term to O(
d+p
,
d+q
)-generalized diffeomorphisms of heterotic double field theory with the gauge group
G_{\mathrm{S}}
. Based on these observations, we rewrite the transformation of
\mathcal{E}
as

\delta_{\xi}\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{A}\delta_{\xi}\mathbb{E}E=\mathcal{L}_{\scalebox{0.75}{\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}}\xi|}\mathcal{E}+\xi^{\alpha}\uptau_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}-D_{A}\xi^{\beta}\,\mathcal{A}R^{A}_{\beta}E\,,

(64)

with the heterotic generalized Lie derivative given in (
48
) and the parameter
\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}\xi|=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{A}|\xi^{A}+\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}^{\alpha}|\xi_{\alpha}
. Up to the last term, this is the transformation of the frame of heterotic double field theory under a combination of a generalized diffeomorphism and a double Lorentz transformation (assuming that
G_{\mathrm{S}}
is chosen properly).

3
Generalized Bergshoeff-de Roo identification

To make contact with the gBdR identification, the last thing we have to fix is the generalized structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}
. As before, it is assumed that
\kappa_{\alpha\beta}
is non-degenerate. Hence, the simplest possible choice would be to use the double Lorentz group
\underline{\mathrm{O}(1,d-1)}\times\overline{\mathrm{O}(d-1,1)}
. However, we will see that doing so would not allow us to solve for all contributions to
\mathcal{A}
by a torsion constraint. Therefore, the structure group has to be composed more intricately, whereas the double Lorentz group only acts as a seed for a series of larger groups. Naturally, the latter will decompose into a left and a right component, like the double Lorentz group. Let us focus on the first factor because the second one will be treated in exactly the same way. We denote the series of extensions as
\underline{\widehat{\mathrm{O}}^{(p)}(1,d-1)}
, and impose the initial condition
\underline{\widehat{\mathrm{O}}^{(1)}(1,d-1)}=\underline{\mathrm{O}(1,d-1)}
. Thus, we are dealing we the generalized structure group

G_{\mathrm{PS}}\supset G_{\mathrm{S}}^{(p,q)}=\underline{\widehat{\mathrm{O}}^{(p)}(1,d-1)}\times\overline{\widehat{\mathrm{O}}^{(q)}(d-1,1)}=\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(p)}\times\overline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(q)}

(65)

on an abstract level. An explicit construction is given in subsection
3.2
. To keep track of both factors, we over/underline the corresponding indices, for example

t_{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}t_{\underline{\alpha}}\,&t_{\overline{\alpha}}\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad\text{or}\qquad\mathcal{T}_{A}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{T}_{\underline{a}}\,&\mathcal{T}_{\overline{a}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(66)

The defining property of these refined indices is
\eta_{\underline{a}\overline{b}}=\eta_{\overline{b}\underline{a}}=0
and
\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}}=\kappa_{\overline{\beta}\underline{\alpha}}=0
. In the following, we will show how this choice is sufficient to recover all properties of the gBdR identification.

3.1
Parameterization of
\mathcal{A}
and partial
G_{\mathrm{S}}
-gauge fixing

In section
2.1.2
, we argue that we eventually want to fix
\mathcal{A}
in the parameterization (
45
) of
\mathcal{E}
completely through torsion constraints such that the only remaining free field is the generalized frame
E
on the physical space. Hence, a central question is:

What are the possible torsion constraints?

At the beginning of this section, we already imposed a left/right-split for the structure group. Also the components of the reduced twisted torsion in (
57
) decompose accordingly into

\mathcal{T}_{\underline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}\,,\qquad\mathcal{T}_{\underline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\mathcal{B}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\,,\qquad\mathcal{T}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}\overline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{T}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}\overline{\mathcal{B}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\,.

(67)

For a
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}}
which transforms completely covariantly, each of these components would give rise to an independent constraint. However, as discussed in
[
23
]
for general structure groups,
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}}
receives an inhomogeneous contribution. It is controlled by the constants entering the definition of
\widehat{t}_{\hat{A}}
in (
34
). Because these constants are completely chiral or anti-chiral for our choice of the structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}
, they only affect the totally chiral and anti-chiral components in the decomposition (
67
). Thus we are in general left with only the components
\mathcal{T}_{\underline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\mathcal{B}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}\overline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}
available for covariant torsion constraints. This is the first step of the gBdR identification.

This shatters the hope to fix
\mathcal{A}
purely in terms of constraints on the reduced twisted torsion because, as we just argued,
\mathcal{T}_{\underline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}\overline{\mathcal{B}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}}
are not available to fix the fully chiral,
\mathcal{A}_{\underline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\mathcal{B}}}
, and anti-chiral,
\mathcal{A}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}\overline{\mathcal{B}}}
, components of
\mathcal{A}
. Instead, we have to come up with something else. Fortunately, there is another mechanism which allows to affect
\mathcal{A}
, namely gauge fixing. It works by requiring that the offending fully chiral and anti-chiral contributions to
\mathcal{A}
are set to zero by appropriate
G_{\mathrm{S}}
-transformations. Let us work this out more in detail. We start in full generality with the matrix exponent

\mathcal{A}=\exp A^{\prime}\,.

(68)

Just using
A
in the exponent would fix already the parameterization completely. But we want to have the maximal amount of freedom and therefore use odd polynomials with the expansion

A^{\prime}=A+c_{1}A^{3}+c_{2}A^{5}\dots\,.

(69)

Different values for the constants
c_{n}
realize different parameterizations. From a physical point of view, they are all related by field redefinitions. Hence, we can in principle choose any values we like. However, choosing the correct field basis simplifies results considerably. Later we will see that
c_{1}=1/3
results in vast cancellations and is therefore the value we will adapt. The reason why only odd terms contribute to
A^{\prime}
is that the components of
A
have a natural
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
grading in terms of their chiralities. More precisely, we have

A=A_{+}+A_{-}\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\underline{\mathcal{A}}}|A_{+}|^{\overline{\mathcal{B}}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}|A_{+}|^{\underline{\mathcal{B}}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=0\,,\qquad\text{and}\\
\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\underline{\mathcal{A}}}|A_{-}|^{\underline{\mathcal{B}}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}|A_{-}|^{\overline{\mathcal{B}}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}=0\,.\end{array}\right.

(70)

While
A_{-}
will be fixed by the torsion constraints,
A_{+}
is set to zero by a partial gauge fixing which breaks the generalized structure group into a subgroup. If such a gauge fixing exists is another question. We will answer it in the affirmative in section
3.5
. Both mechanisms in combination with the right choice of the generalized structure group will fix
A
, and with it
\mathcal{A}
, completely. All details are explained in the following subsections. For the moment, it is sufficient to remember
A_{+}
= 0 due to gauge fixing. The same should hold for
A^{\prime}
; We do not want it to contain any fully chiral/anti-chiral (
+
) components and thus are restricted to odd polynomials in
A
. This is not true for the full
\mathcal{A}
though. Here, we use the matrix exponent to obtain an element of
G_{\mathrm{PS}}
and at the same time to have an economical way to compute several quantities from the last section. In particular, we employ Hadamard’s formula

\mathcal{A}^{-1}X\mathcal{A}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}[A^{\prime},X]_{m}\,,\qquad\text{for}\qquad X\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}

(71)

with

[A,B]_{m}=[A,[A,B]_{m-1}]]\,,\qquad[A,B]_{0}=B

(72)

to compute the adjoint action of
\mathcal{A}
in terms of nested commutators. In the same vein, we take advantage of the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula

\mathcal{A}^{-1}\delta\mathcal{A}=\sum^{\infty}_{m=0}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{(m+1)!}[A^{\prime},\delta A^{\prime}]_{m}\,.

(73)

At this point, we are left with three major tasks:

1.

Fix the generalized structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}
.

2.

Fix torsion constraints which allow to find
A_{-}
and solve them.

3.

Find the residual gauge transformations that are compatible with the partial gauge fixing
A_{+}=0
.

Each of them is discussed in the following subsections.

3.2
Construction of
G_{\mathrm{S}}

To proceed, we need explicit expressions for the generators
\uptau_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}
which describe
G_{\mathrm{S}}
’s structure coefficients through (
42
). It is sufficient to discuss the details of their chiral contributions because their anti-chiral counterparts are treated in the same way. To get some motivation how to fix
\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}
, let us look at the respective torsion constraint that follows from (
57
) as

\mathcal{T}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}=\mathcal{F}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}+\mathcal{A}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\delta}}(\tau^{\underline{\delta}})_{\underline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}=0\,.

(74)

For this equation to have a solution,
\tau_{\underline{\alpha}}
has to contain all generators of
\mathcal{K}_{\underline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\mathcal{B}}}
given in (
29
). This is very much in the spirit of the original gBdR identification. But instead of leaving these generators abstract, we reveal a new structure by using a recursive definition of
\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(p)}
. It originates from a refinement of the chiral index in

\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}}=\begin{pmatrix}\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}}&\dots&\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{p}}\end{pmatrix}

(75)

and is based on the identifications

\displaystyle\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\uptau^{\underline{a}\underline{b}}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(76)

\displaystyle\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\uptau^{\underline{a}}_{\underline{\beta}_{1}}\quad&\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}\underline{\beta}_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad\text{and from there on}

\displaystyle\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{i+1}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\uptau^{\underline{a}}_{\underline{\beta}_{i}}\quad&\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}\underline{\beta}_{i}}\quad&\dots\quad&\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}\underline{\beta}_{i}}\end{pmatrix}

until we reach
\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{p}}
. To make sense of these relations, we have to express the new generators
\uptau^{\underline{a}\underline{b}}
,
\uptau^{\underline{a}}_{\underline{\beta}_{i}}
and
\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}\underline{\beta}_{j}}
that appear or the right-hand side in terms of generators we already know. We start with the Lorentz generators

\uptau^{\underline{a}\underline{b}}=g_{-}\,K^{\underline{a}\underline{b}},

(77)

where
g_{-}
is an overall normalization constant which is eventually expressed in terms of the
a
used in the gBdR identification of
[
12
]
. This is a natural choice because it implies

\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(1)}=\underline{\mathrm{O}(1,d-1)}\,.

(78)

The remaining two generators are fixed by the relation

\mathcal{K}^{\underline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\mathcal{B}}}=\frac{1}{g_{-}}\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}\uptau^{\underline{a}\underline{b}}\quad&\uptau^{\underline{a}}_{\underline{\beta}}\\
-\uptau^{\underline{b}}_{\underline{\alpha}}\quad&\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(79)

In addition to (
77
), this gives eventually rise to

\uptau^{\underline{a}}_{\underline{\beta}_{i}}=\frac{g_{-}}{2}\,R^{\underline{a}}_{\underline{\beta}_{i}}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}\underline{\beta}_{j}}=-\frac{g_{-}}{2}\,R_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}\underline{\beta}_{j}}\,.

(80)

At this point, we see why it is more convenient to have the generators
\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}\underline{\beta}_{j}}
with two indices instead of one. Counting them gives rise to the dimension

\dim\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(p+1)}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(d+\dim\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(p)}\right)\left(d+\dim\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(p)}-1\right)\,,

(81)

with the initial condition

\dim\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(0)}=0\,.

(82)

Although this dimension grows exponentially with
p
, it is finite for any finite
p
. Hence, in contrast to the standard approach to the gBdR identification
[
11
,
12
]
, we do not need to deal a priori with infinite-dimensional Lie algebras. Our organization of generators suggests the hierarchy of subgroups

\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(1)}\subset\dots\subset\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(p)}\,.

(83)

In order to prove this conjecture, we first have to fix
\kappa_{\alpha\beta}
, because it appears in the relevant commutators (
17
), (
19
) and (
20
). Without loss of generality, we impose

\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}\underline{\beta}_{j}}=0\qquad\text{for}\qquad i\neq j\,.

(84)

This might be seen as the first step towards choosing a basis for
\uptau_{\alpha}
where
\kappa_{\alpha\beta}
is diagonal. To further analyze the properties of
\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(l)}
, we need the commutators of its generators. To write them in a simple form, we extend the index
{}_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}}
by including
{}_{\underline{\alpha}_{0}}={}^{\underline{a}}
. Thereby, (
77
) and (
80
) are unified into
\tau_{\alpha_{I}\beta_{J}}
, where capital indices like
I
and
J
start with zero. With this convention, the commutators we look for take on the simple form

[\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{I}\underline{\beta}_{J}},\uptau_{\underline{\gamma}_{K}\underline{\delta}_{L}}]=2g_{-}\,\upeta_{[\underline{\alpha}_{I}|[\underline{\gamma}_{K}}\uptau_{\underline{\delta}_{L}]|\underline{\beta}_{J}]}\,,

(85)

which follows immediately from (
30
). The property (
84
) carries over to
\upeta_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}\underline{\beta}_{j}}
such that we find

\upeta_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}\underline{\beta}_{j}}=0\qquad\text{for}\qquad i\neq j\,.

(86)

Because
\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(l)}
is generated by all
\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{I}\underline{\beta}_{J}}
with
0\leq I\leq J\leq l-1
, it closes under the commutators (
85
). Thereby, we confirm the hierarchy of subgroups anticipated in (
83
). A comparison with (
16
) furthermore reveals that
\upeta^{\underline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\mathcal{B}}}
is the invariant pairing of
\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(l)}
.

For later, we also need an explicit expression for the structure coefficients
f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}
. They can be read off directly from (
85
), resulting in

f_{\underline{\alpha}_{I}\underline{\beta}_{J}\underline{\gamma}_{K}\underline{\delta}_{L}}{}^{\underline{\epsilon}_{M}\underline{\rho}_{N}}=-2g_{-}\upeta_{[\underline{\alpha}_{I}|[\underline{\gamma}_{K}}\delta_{\underline{\delta}_{L}]}^{[\underline{\epsilon}_{M}}\delta_{|\underline{\beta}_{J}]}^{\underline{\rho}_{N}]}\,.

(87)

All further information is completely encoded in
\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}\underline{\beta}_{i}}
which can be computed recursively from the invariant pairing on
G_{\mathrm{PS}}
induced by
\mathcal{Z}
. More precisely, consider the pairing

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle K_{AB},K_{CD}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\eta_{[A|[C}\eta_{D]|B]}\,,

(88)

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle R_{\alpha}^{A},R_{\beta}^{B}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=2\eta^{AB}\kappa_{\alpha\beta}\,,\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle R_{\alpha\beta},R_{\gamma\delta}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=4\kappa_{[\alpha|[\gamma}\kappa_{\delta]|\beta]}\,.

It is normalized such that

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\mathcal{Z},X\rangle\!\rangle=2X\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for all}\qquad X\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}

(89)

holds and of course it also satisfies

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle[\uptau_{\alpha},X],Y\rangle\!\rangle+\langle\!\langle X,[\uptau_{\alpha},Y]\rangle\!\rangle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for all}\qquad X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{PS}}\,,

(90)

the index free version of (
32
). We use it to compute

\frac{1}{g_{-}^{2}}\langle\!\langle\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}_{I}\underline{\beta}_{J}},\uptau_{\underline{\gamma}_{K}\underline{\delta}_{L}}\rangle\!\rangle=\upeta_{[\underline{\alpha}_{I}|[\underline{\gamma}_{K}}\upeta_{\underline{\delta}_{L}]|\underline{\beta}_{J}]}=\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}_{I}\underline{\beta}_{J}\underline{\gamma}_{K}\underline{\delta}_{L}}\,.

(91)

Looking at (
88
), one sees that this definition indeed is compatible with our initial requirement (
84
). As it is used to raise and lower Greek indices, we do not want
\kappa_{\alpha\beta}
to depend on any parameters. Hence, the factor
1/g_{-}^{2}
is added on the left-hand side. At the leading order, we obtain

\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}\underline{\beta}_{1}}=\kappa_{\underline{a}_{1}\underline{a}_{2}\underline{b}_{1}\underline{b}_{2}}=\eta_{[\underline{a}_{1}|[\underline{b}_{1}}\eta_{\underline{b}_{2}]|\underline{a}_{2}]}

(92)

after expanding the indices. From there on,
\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}\underline{\beta}_{i}}
is computed recursively from
\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}_{i-1}\underline{\beta}_{i-1}}
, …,
\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}\underline{\beta}_{1}}
. As an example, take

\kappa{}_{\underline{\alpha}_{2}\underline{\beta}_{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa^{\underline{a}_{1}}_{\underline{a}_{2}\underline{a}_{3}}{}^{\underline{b}_{1}}_{\underline{b}_{2}\underline{b}_{3}}&0\\
0&\kappa_{\underline{a}_{1}\underline{a}_{2}\underline{a}_{3}\underline{a}_{4}\underline{b}_{1}\underline{b}_{2}\underline{b}_{3}\underline{b}_{4}}\end{pmatrix},

(93)

with

\displaystyle\kappa^{\underline{a}_{1}}_{\underline{a}_{2}\underline{a}_{3}}{}^{\underline{b}_{1}}_{\underline{b}_{2}\underline{b}_{3}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\eta^{\underline{a}_{1}\underline{b}_{1}}\eta_{[\underline{a}_{2}|[\underline{b}_{2}}\eta_{\underline{b}_{3}]|\underline{a}_{3}]}\,,\qquad\text{and}

(94)

\displaystyle\kappa_{\underline{a}_{1}\underline{a}_{2}\underline{a}_{3}\underline{a}_{4}\underline{b}_{1}\underline{b}_{2}\underline{b}_{3}\underline{b}_{4}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\eta_{[\underline{a}_{1}|[\underline{b}_{1}}\eta_{\underline{b}_{2}]|\underline{a}_{2}]}\eta_{[\underline{b}_{3}|[\underline{a}_{3}}\eta_{\underline{a}_{4}]|\underline{b}_{4}]}-\eta_{[\underline{a}_{1}|[\underline{b}_{3}}\eta_{\underline{b}_{4}]|\underline{a}_{2}]}\eta_{[\underline{b}_{1}|[\underline{a}_{3}}\eta_{\underline{a}_{4}]|\underline{b}_{2}]}\bigr)\,.

(95)

In the following, we will see that a complete identification of
A
for the gauge group
\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(p)}
requires the extension to
\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(p+1)}
. This process repeats again and again. Initially, it looks like it can be truncated if the maximal number of derivatives is limited. However, we will point out a problem for finite dimensional structure groups in the next subsection. The discussion for the anti-chiral sector is nearly identical; The only difference is that we swap
g_{-}
with a second normalization constant
g_{+}
. Later on, we will see that in this way we recover the bi-parametric deformation that is captured by the gBdR identification. In conclusion, we are indeed dealing with the decomposition of the generalized structure group anticipated in (
65
).

3.3
Collapsing towers

We have now constructed a simple basis for
\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(n)}
in terms of the generators
\uptau_{\underline{\alpha}}
, which will play the central role in the Poláček-Siegel construction. However, the torsion constraints and the gauge-fixing in the next two subsections are rather written in terms of the generators
t_{\underline{\alpha}}
. Both are related by the change of basis

t_{\underline{\alpha}}=S_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}\uptau_{\underline{\beta}}\,,

(96)

with

S_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}=\delta_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}-\phi_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}\,,

(97)

and

\phi_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}=\tfrac{1}{g_{-}}f_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}^{\prime}\underline{\beta}^{\prime\prime}}\,.

(98)

To make sense of the last equation, note that we split the double index
\underline{\beta}
into its components denoted by
\underline{\beta}^{\prime}
and
\underline{\beta}^{\prime\prime}
by using (
76
). We will encounter this technique of splitting indices very often in the following. Although the relation (
96
) looks quite innocent at the beginning, it causes a lot of work in the identification. The main reason is that we are not dealing with the map
S
itself in the identification but with its inverse
S^{-1}
, which is more complicated and is defined by

(S^{-1})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}S_{\underline{\gamma}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}=S_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}(S^{-1})_{\underline{\gamma}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}=\delta_{\underline{\alpha}}^{\underline{\beta}}\,.

(99)

It is given by the sum

(S^{-1})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}(\phi^{n})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}\,,\qquad\text{where}\qquad(\phi^{0})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}=\delta_{\underline{\alpha}}^{\underline{\beta}}

(100)

of higher and higher powers in
\phi
. From the definition (
98
), we see that when
\phi
is applied to elements in the algebra
\underline{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(n)}
it results in an element of
\underline{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(n+1)}
. Therefore, we conclude that

\phi^{m}:\underline{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(n)}\to\underline{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(n+m)}

(101)

and thereby understand that even if
S^{-1}
is applied to very simple generators, like the left-handed double Lorentz generators of
\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(1)}
, it gives a whole tower of generators filling
\underline{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(p)}
. Although most of the generators in the tower are redundant, we still have to deal with them. This is the main reason why the gBdR identification is so complicated.

At a first step towards a better understanding of these towers’ fate, consider the situation where two of them are contracted by the pairing in (
91
). This is a situation that will occur frequently in the identification, motivating us to introduce

\widetilde{\kappa}_{\alpha\beta}:=(S^{-1})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}\,\kappa_{\underline{\gamma}\underline{\delta}}\,(S^{-1})_{\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}\,.

(102)

All we need to know in order to compute this quantity is

\phi_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}\kappa_{\underline{\gamma}\underline{\delta}}^{(m)}\phi_{\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}=X_{m}\,\kappa^{(m-1)}_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}\,,

(103)

with the Dynkin index

X_{m}=d+\dim\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(m-1)}-2

(104)

and the restricted pairing

\kappa^{(m)}_{\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}}=\begin{cases}\kappa_{\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}}&\text{for }i,j\leq m\,,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

(105)

After applying (
103
) recursively, we are able to evaluate the sum

\upkappa_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}:=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{p-1}(\phi^{n})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}\kappa^{(p)}_{\underline{\gamma}\underline{\delta}}(\phi^{n})_{\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}

(106)

as

\upkappa_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}\underline{\beta}_{j}}=\begin{cases}\underline{\chi}_{i}\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}_{i}\underline{\beta}_{i}}&\text{for }i=j\,,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(107)

with constants
\chi_{i}
given by

\underline{\chi}_{m}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{p-m}\prod_{l=1}^{n}X(p-l)\,.

(108)

After this preparation, we can finally simplify (
102
) to

\widetilde{\kappa}_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}=\upkappa_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}+2({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{(\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}\upkappa_{\underline{\beta})\underline{\gamma}}\,,

(109)

where we have introduced for convenience

({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}=(S^{-1})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}-\delta_{\underline{\alpha}}^{\underline{\beta}}\,.

(110)

It also follows from (
100
) that

({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}=(S^{-1})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}\phi_{\underline{\gamma}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}\,.

(111)

We have now successfully collapsed the towers contributing to the original definition (
102
). Only the constants
\underline{\chi}_{i}
remain as a reminder of the redundancy generated by
S^{-1}
.

In the same way we discovered the relation between
\widetilde{\kappa}_{\alpha\beta}
and
\kappa_{\alpha\beta}
, we need to deal with the structure coefficients. As starting point here one may take the commutator

-[t_{\underline{\alpha}},t_{\underline{\beta}}]=S_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}S_{\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\epsilon}}f_{\underline{\delta}\underline{\epsilon}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}\tau_{\underline{\gamma}}=f_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}\tau_{\underline{\gamma}}+2[R_{[\underline{\alpha}},\tau_{\underline{\beta}]}]+f_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}R_{\underline{\gamma}}\,.

(112)

Using

[R_{\underline{\alpha}},\tau_{\underline{\beta}}]=-\phi_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}f_{\underline{\delta}\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}\tau_{\underline{\gamma}}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad R_{\underline{\alpha}}=\phi_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\beta}}\tau_{\underline{\beta}}\,,

(113)

we then find

S_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}S_{\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\epsilon}}f_{\underline{\delta}\underline{\epsilon}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}=2S_{[\underline{\alpha}|}{}^{\underline{\delta}}f_{\underline{\delta}|\underline{\beta}]}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}-f_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}S_{\underline{\delta}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}\,.

(114)

Multiplying it with
(S^{-1})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}(S^{-1})_{\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}
, along with using

S_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}\widetilde{\kappa}_{\underline{\gamma}\underline{\beta}}=(S^{-1})_{\underline{\beta}\underline{\alpha}}

(115)

from (
102
), we obtain the counterpart of
\widetilde{\kappa}_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}
,

\widetilde{f}_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}\underline{\gamma}}:=(S^{-1})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}(S^{-1})_{\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\epsilon}}(S^{-1})_{\underline{\gamma}}{}^{\underline{\rho}}f_{\underline{\delta}\underline{\epsilon}\underline{\rho}}=2({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{[\underline{\alpha}|}{}^{\underline{\delta}}f_{\underline{\delta}|\underline{\beta}]}{}^{\underline{\epsilon}}\widetilde{\kappa}_{\underline{\epsilon}\underline{\gamma}}+f_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}\widetilde{\kappa}_{\underline{\delta}\underline{\gamma}}\,.

(116)

At the end of the last subsection, it was concluded that in order to fix all components of
\mathcal{A}
one has to send the
p
of
\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(p)}
eventually to
\infty
. This will make the initial point of our analysis in (
103
) more subtle because we have to regularize the expression for the divergent Dynkin index in (
104
). There are different ways to do so. Perhaps the most obvious way would be to just keep (
103
) and consider instead of
\chi_{i}
the ratio
\chi_{i}/\chi_{1}
that remains finite in the limit
p\rightarrow\infty
. However, there is an alternative regularization based on the observation that the difference between
\kappa^{(m)}_{\underline{\gamma}\underline{\delta}}
and
\kappa^{(m-1)}_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}
on both sides of (
103
) only appears because we are working with a finite structure group. But for
p\rightarrow\infty
, it is more natural to substitute (
103
) by

\phi_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}\kappa_{\underline{\gamma}\underline{\delta}}\phi_{\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}=X_{\infty}\,\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}\,.

(117)

Remarkably, this choice simplifies matters considerably because using (
117
) instead of (
103
), one finds that all
\underline{\chi}_{i}
are equal, implying

\boxed{\underline{\chi}_{i}=\underline{\chi}\,.}

(118)

Of course this remaining
\underline{\chi}
is infinite, but as in the original gBdR identification, it can be combined with the constant
g_{-}
to form the finite parameter
a
. In the beginning, we have been hesitant to commit to an infinite
G_{\mathrm{S}}
but now we showed that instead of making matters more complicated, it actually simplifies the collapsing of towers considerably. On one hand, it might be worrisome to have to choose between different regularization procedures. On the other hand, this situation is well known from quantum field theory. There, the regularization has to be performed in a way that is compatible with the symmetries of the theory. Here, we will make the same observation: Only with the regularization prescription (
118
) the residual gauge transformation that survives after the gauge fixing in section
3.5
will close.

With the simplifications introduced by (
118
), we can for example compute
\tilde{f}_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}\underline{\beta}_{1}\underline{\gamma}_{1}}
which will be needed later. As the first step, we note that (
109
) simplifies to

\widetilde{\kappa}_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}=\underline{\chi}\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}+2\underline{\chi}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{(\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta})}\,,

(119)

allowing us to rewrite (
116
) as

\tilde{f}_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}\underline{\gamma}}=2\underline{\chi}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{[\underline{\alpha}|}{}^{\underline{\delta}}f_{\underline{\delta}|\underline{\beta}]\underline{\gamma}}+4\underline{\chi}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{[\underline{\alpha}|}{}^{\underline{\delta}}f_{\underline{\delta}|\underline{\beta}]}{}^{\underline{\epsilon}}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{(\underline{\epsilon}\underline{\gamma})}+\underline{\chi}f_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}\underline{\gamma}}+2\underline{\chi}f_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{(\underline{\delta}\underline{\gamma})}\,.

(120)

Furthermore, from (
116
) and (
110
) one finds

({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{\underline{\alpha}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\epsilon}}f_{\underline{\delta}\underline{\epsilon}\underline{\gamma}}=2({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{[\underline{\alpha}|}{}^{\delta}f_{\underline{\delta}|\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\epsilon}}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{\underline{\epsilon}\underline{\gamma}}+f_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{\underline{\delta}\underline{\gamma}}

(121)

and finally obtains

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}\underline{\gamma}}=

\displaystyle 2\underline{\chi}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{[\underline{\alpha}|}{}^{\underline{\delta}}f_{\underline{\delta}|\underline{\beta}]\underline{\gamma}}+\underline{\chi}f_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\delta}}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{\underline{\gamma}\underline{\delta}}+6\underline{\chi}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{[\underline{\alpha}|}{}^{\underline{\delta}}f_{\underline{\delta}|\underline{\beta}|}{}^{\underline{\epsilon}}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{\underline{\epsilon}|\underline{\gamma}]}+

(122)

\displaystyle 3\underline{\chi}f_{[\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}|}{}^{\underline{\delta}}({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{\underline{\delta}|\underline{\gamma}]}+\underline{\chi}f_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}\underline{\gamma}}\,.

Restricting the indices to
\alpha_{1}
,
\beta_{1}
and
\gamma_{1}
, one deduces that

\tilde{f}_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}\underline{\beta}_{1}\underline{\gamma}_{1}}=\underline{\chi}f_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}\underline{\beta}_{1}\underline{\gamma}_{1}}

(123)

because
({\widetilde{S}}^{-1})_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}}{}^{\underline{\beta}_{1}}=0
as can be seen from (
110
). Another quantity that we will need later is

(S^{-1})_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}}{}^{\underline{\mu}}(S^{-1})_{\underline{\beta}_{1}}{}^{\underline{\nu}}f_{\underline{\mu}\underline{\nu}\underline{\gamma}}(S^{-1})_{\underline{\delta}_{1}}{}^{\underline{\rho}}(S^{-1})_{\underline{\epsilon}_{1}}{}^{\underline{\sigma}}f_{\underline{\rho}\underline{\sigma}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}=\widetilde{f}_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}\underline{\beta}_{1}\underline{\gamma}}\widetilde{f}_{\underline{\delta}_{1}\underline{\epsilon}_{1}\underline{\rho}}\widetilde{\kappa}^{\underline{\gamma}\underline{\rho}}\,,

(124)

where (
122
) and (
102
) were used to obtain the right-hand side. With the aid of (
116
), (
119
) and (
121
), it is straightforward to obtain

\widetilde{f}_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}\underline{\beta}_{1}\underline{\gamma}}\widetilde{f}_{\underline{\delta}_{1}\underline{\epsilon}_{1}\underline{\rho}}\widetilde{\kappa}^{\underline{\gamma}\underline{\rho}}=\underline{\chi}f_{\underline{\alpha}_{1}\underline{\beta}_{1}\underline{\gamma}_{1}}f_{\underline{\delta}_{1}\underline{\epsilon}_{1}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}_{1}}\,.

(125)

Of course all of these relations hold for the conjugate chiralities as well, by swapping
\underline{\chi}
with
\overline{\chi}
.

3.4
Identification

Next, we would like to fix
A
, and with it
\mathcal{A}
, by coming back to the idea of setting selected reduced twisted torsion components in (
57
) to zero. Due to (
45
), it is possible to replace
\mathcal{E}
with
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Moreover, we only need to consider the mixed chirality components
\mathcal{A}_{\underline{a}\overline{\beta}}
,
\mathcal{A}_{\underline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}}
,
\mathcal{A}_{\overline{a}\underline{\beta}}
, and
\mathcal{A}_{\overline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}
because chiral/anti-chiral contributions are set to zero by partial gauge fixing as explained in a previous subsection. Consequentially, we are left with the four constraints

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{\underline{a}\overline{\mathcal{B}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\underline{a}\overline{\delta}}(\uptau^{\overline{\delta}})_{\overline{\mathcal{B}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{F}_{\underline{a}\overline{\mathcal{B}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\,,

(126)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{\overline{a}\underline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\overline{a}\underline{\delta}}(\uptau^{\underline{\delta}})_{\underline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{F}_{\overline{a}\underline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}\,,

(127)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}^{\underline{\alpha}}{}_{\overline{\mathcal{B}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\underline{\alpha}}{}_{\overline{\delta}}(\uptau^{\overline{\delta}})_{\overline{\mathcal{B}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{F}^{\underline{\alpha}}{}_{\overline{\mathcal{B}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\,,\qquad\text{and}

(128)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}^{\overline{\alpha}}{}_{\underline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\overline{\alpha}}{}_{\underline{\delta}}(\uptau^{\underline{\delta}})_{\underline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{F}^{\overline{\alpha}}{}_{\underline{\mathcal{B}}\underline{\mathcal{C}}}\,,

(129)

from (
57
) with
(\uptau^{\alpha})_{\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{B}}|\uptau^{\alpha}|_{\mathcal{C}}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}
. Here we observe the pattern that was already anticipated at the end of section
3.2
: The right-hand sides of (
126
) to (
129
) restricted to
\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(p)}
will require the extension to
\underline{G_{\mathrm{S}}}^{(p+1)}
to satisfy the torsion constraints. The new components of
\mathcal{A}
which are generated in this step can be written exclusively in terms of the generalized frame on the physical space
E
and at least
p+1
derivatives.

We extract the relevant components of
A
by using the pairing introduced in (
91
). This is done iteratively in the number of derivatives, which we will keep track of by decorating the respective quantities like
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}}
by
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}}^{(l)}
when they contain
l
derivatives. But, due to the second term of (
55
), one encounters

\mathcal{A}^{(l)\beta}_{\mathcal{A}}\uptau_{\beta}\cong-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}^{(l)}_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{A}^{(l)\beta}_{\mathcal{A}}R_{\beta}-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}^{(l)}_{\mathcal{A}}[A^{(<l)}]\,.

(130)

Here, we introduce the equivalence relation
\cong
which ignores all contributions that are not contained in
\mathcal{A}^{(l)}_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{\beta}
, like generators with mixed chirality. In the following, we will use it frequently to avoid the need to write many irrelevant terms. Moreover, we indicate with
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}^{(l)}_{\mathcal{A}}[A^{(<l)}]
all contributions to
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}^{(l)}
which contain
A
at most up to order
A^{(l-1)}
. To achieve a separation of orders on the left- and right-hand side, which is needed to eventually solve for
A^{(l)}
iteratively, we equivalently write

\mathcal{A}^{(l)\beta}_{\mathcal{A}}t_{\beta}\cong-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}^{(l)}_{\mathcal{A}}[A^{(<l)}]\,.

(131)

At this point, we re-encounter the generator
t_{\alpha}
which we have introduced in section
2.1.1
. Finally, we transition from
\mathcal{A}
to
A
by using the expansion (
69
), leading to

A^{(l)\beta}_{\mathcal{A}}t_{\beta}\cong-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}^{(l)}_{\mathcal{A}}[A^{(<l)}]-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}^{(l)\beta}_{\mathcal{A}}[A^{(<l)}]t_{\beta}

(132)

with

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}^{(l)\beta}_{\mathcal{A}}[A^{(<l)}]=\mathcal{A}^{(l)\beta}_{\mathcal{A}}-A^{(l)\beta}_{\mathcal{A}}\,.

(133)

A direct computation shows us

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}^{(1)}_{\mathcal{A}\beta}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(134)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)}_{\mathcal{A}\beta}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{A}}|(A^{(1)})^{2}|_{\beta}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\cong 0\,,

(135)

and the first non-trivial contribution

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}^{(3)}_{\mathcal{A}\beta}

\displaystyle\cong\left(c_{1}+\tfrac{1}{6}\right)\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{\mathcal{A}}|(A^{(1)})^{3}|_{\beta}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}\,.

(136)

We rather work with the generators
\uptau_{\alpha}
because they form the natural basis for
G_{\mathrm{S}}^{(p)}
introduced in the last subsection. Therefore, we define

\displaystyle A^{(l)}_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{\beta}t_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{A}^{(l)}_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{\beta}\uptau_{\beta}\,,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{A}}^{(l)\beta}t_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}_{\mathcal{A}}^{(l)\beta}\uptau_{\beta}\,,

(137)

with

\displaystyle A^{(l)}_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{A}^{(l)}_{\mathcal{A}}{}^{\gamma}(S^{-1})_{\gamma}{}^{\beta}\,,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{A}}^{(l)\beta}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}_{\mathcal{A}}^{(l)\gamma}(S^{-1})_{\gamma}{}^{\beta}\,,

(138)

where
S^{-1}
is given in (
100
). At this point, the idea of collapsing towers explain in section
3.3
becomes relevant. As explained there, compared to the untilded version,
\widetilde{A}_{\mathcal{A}\beta}
has the advantage that it has a finite number of contributions. Its components arise from the identification

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \widetilde{A}^{(l)}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\beta}}&=-\frac{1}{g_{-}^{2}}\langle\!\langle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}^{(l)}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}[A^{(<l)}],\uptau_{\underline{\beta}}\rangle\!\rangle-\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}^{(l)}_{\overline{\mathcal{A}}\underline{\beta}}[A^{(<l)}]\,,\\
\widetilde{A}^{(l)}_{\underline{\mathcal{A}}\overline{\beta}}&=-\frac{1}{g_{+}^{2}}\langle\!\langle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}^{(l)}_{\underline{\mathcal{A}}}[A^{(<l)}],\uptau_{\overline{\beta}}\rangle\!\rangle-\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}^{(l)}_{\underline{\mathcal{A}}\overline{\beta}}[A^{(<l)}]\,.\end{aligned}}

(139)

To better understand how to use (
139
), let us perform the identification at the leading order. Starting point is
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}^{(0)}_{\mathcal{A}}
which only has a contribution from

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}^{(0)\alpha}=-R^{\alpha}\cong 0\,.

(140)

R^{\alpha}
in the middle of this relation is purely chiral or anti-chiral and one thus has
A^{(0)}=0
; There are no contributions from
A
at this order. Fortunately, they are not needed because, as (
140
) shows, the relevant components of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}^{(0)}
vanish on their own. Things become more interesting at the next order. Here one encounters

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{A}^{(1)}=\boldsymbol{F}_{A}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}^{(1)\alpha}=0\,.

(141)

With this information, we get

\displaystyle K:

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{(1)}_{\overline{a}\underline{\beta}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{A}^{(1)\underline{b}_{1}\underline{b}_{2}}_{\overline{a}}=-\tfrac{1}{g_{-}}F_{\overline{a}}{}^{\underline{b}_{1}\underline{b}_{2}}\,,

(142)

and

\displaystyle K:

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{(1)}_{\underline{a}\overline{\beta}_{1}}

3.5
Generalized Green-Schwarz transformations

In the last subsection, we have seen how the identification of the connection
\mathcal{A}
proceeds order by order. Now, we will revisit the gauge transformations from section
2.2
and treat them in the same way. Not only this will confirm that the gauge fixing we proposed in section
3.1
is valid, but it will also give rise to the gGS transformations of the physical frame. Knowing them, will allow us to compare our results with the literature on the gBdR identification. Like before, we will expand
\delta_{\xi}\mathbb{E}
according to the number of derivatives it carries. Formally, this will always look like

\delta\mathbb{E}^{(l)}=\delta A^{\prime(l)}+\delta E^{(l)}E^{-1}+\dots=\xi^{(l)\alpha}t_{\alpha}+\dots\,,

(167)

where
\dots
denotes lower-order contributions. We bring them all to one side of the equation and denote them by
\mathcal{X}^{(l)}[\xi^{(<l)}]
. As before,
\xi^{(<l)}
indicates contributions of lower order, with the highest being
\xi^{(l-1)}
. In this way, there is a clear distinction between already-known and yet-to-fix quantities,

\boxed{\delta A^{(l)}+\delta E^{(l)}E^{-1}-\xi^{(l)\alpha}t_{\alpha}=\mathcal{X}^{(l)}[\xi^{(<l)}]-\delta G^{(l)}[\xi^{(<l)}]}

(168)

with

\mathcal{X}=-D_{A}\xi^{\beta}R_{\beta}^{A}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\left[A^{\prime},\xi^{\alpha}\uptau_{\alpha}-\tfrac{1}{m+1}\delta A^{\prime}\right]_{m}\,,

(169)

and

\delta G^{(l)}[\xi^{(<l)}]=\delta A^{\prime(l)}-\delta A^{(l)}\,.

(170)

Similar to the last subsection, we find for example

\displaystyle\delta G^{(1)}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(171)

\displaystyle\delta G^{(2)}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(172)

with the first non-trivial contribution being

\displaystyle\delta G^{(3)}

\displaystyle=c_{1}\delta\left[(A^{(1)})^{3}\right]\,.

(173)

As in (
131
), we find on the left-hand-side of (
168
)
t_{\alpha}
instead of
\tau_{\alpha}
. Thus, we have to proceed in the same way as for the identification in the last subsection by relating

\xi^{(l)\alpha}t_{\alpha}=\widetilde{\xi}^{(l)\alpha}\uptau_{\alpha}

(174)

in analogy with (
137
), or equally

\xi^{(l)\alpha}=\widetilde{\xi}^{(l)\beta}(S^{-1})_{\beta}{}^{\alpha}\,.

(175)

Our gauge fixing requires that all chiral and anti-chiral contributions to
\mathcal{X}^{(l)}
for
i\geq 1
originate from the third term on the right-hand side. Hence, it comes down to a similar identification we already performed to fix
\widetilde{A}^{(i)}
, namely

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}^{(l)\underline{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{g_{-}^{2}}\langle\!\langle\mathcal{X}^{(l)}[\xi^{(<l)}]-\delta G^{(l)}[\xi^{(<l)}],\uptau^{\underline{\alpha}}\rangle\!\rangle\,,

(176)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}^{(l)\overline{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{g_{+}^{2}}\langle\!\langle\mathcal{X}^{(l)}[\xi^{(<l)}]-\delta G^{(l)}[\xi^{(<l)}],\uptau^{\overline{\alpha}}\rangle\!\rangle\,.

In evaluating
\mathcal{X}^{(l)}
explicitly, it is helpful to introduce some auxiliary quantities. Very often, we will deal with the contractions
\xi^{\alpha}t_{\alpha}
and
\tilde{\xi}^{\alpha}\uptau_{\alpha}
. Therefore, they are assigned to the corresponding expansions

\widetilde{\xi}=\widetilde{\xi}^{\alpha}\uptau_{\alpha}=\xi^{\alpha}t_{\alpha}=\Xi^{AB}K_{AB}+\Xi^{\beta}_{A}R_{\beta}^{A}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Xi^{\alpha\beta}R_{\beta\alpha}\,,

(177)

or equally,

\xi=\xi^{\alpha}\uptau_{\alpha}=\widetilde{\xi}+\xi^{\alpha}R_{\alpha}\,.

(178)

Starting at the leading order, we have

\delta E^{(0)}E^{-1}=\widetilde{\xi}^{(0)}

(179)

and therefore fix

\widetilde{\xi}^{(0)}=-\Lambda^{\underline{a}_{1}\underline{a}_{2}}K_{\underline{a}_{1}\underline{a}_{2}}-\Lambda^{\overline{a}_{1}\overline{a}_{2}}K_{\overline{a}_{1}\overline{a}_{2}}\,,

(180)

allowing us to read off

\Xi^{(0)\underline{a}_{1}\underline{a}_{2}}=-\Lambda^{\underline{a}_{1}\underline{a}_{2}}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Xi^{(0)\overline{a}_{1}\overline{a}_{2}}=-\Lambda^{\overline{a}_{1}\overline{a}_{2}}\,.

(181)

There are several things to explain here. Most important is that the form of (
180
) is motivated by standard double Lorentz transformations with the parameters
\Lambda^{\underline{a}\underline{b}}
and
\Lambda^{\overline{a}\overline{b}}
(both of them are anti-symmetric in their two indices). Our sign choice is dictated by the conventions used in
[
12
]
because we later want to match their results for the gGS transformations. Taking into account (
177
) and the generators (
77
), we find

\widetilde{\xi}^{(0)\underline{a}_{1}\underline{a}_{2}}=-\tfrac{1}{g_{-}}\Lambda^{\underline{a}_{1}\underline{a}_{2}}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{\xi}^{(0)\overline{a}_{1}\overline{a}_{2}}=-\tfrac{1}{g_{+}}\Lambda^{\overline{a}_{1}\overline{a}_{2}}

(182)

as the only non-vanishing contributions to
\widetilde{\xi}^{(0)}
. Moreover, we will perform the gauge fixing such that this is the only contribution to
\delta E_{\underline{a}\underline{b}}
and
\delta E_{\overline{a}\overline{b}}
. Therefore,

\delta E_{\underline{a}\underline{b}}=-\Lambda_{\underline{a}\underline{b}}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta E_{\overline{a}\overline{b}}=-\Lambda_{\overline{a}\overline{b}}\,,

(183)

with
\delta E_{ab}=\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\prec$}}_{a}|\delta EE^{-1}|_{b}\raisebox{-3.01125pt}{\resizebox{394690}{789381}{$\succ$}}
holding to all orders, and we have fixed

\xi^{(0)}=\Xi^{(0)AB}K_{AB}+\xi^{(0)\gamma}R_{\gamma}\,.

(184)

Moreover, note that
E
as the fundamental field does not receive higher-derivative corrections – in contrast to its gauge transformations
\delta E
. Thus, we write only
E^{-1}
in (
179
).

At the next order, we encounter

\mathcal{X}^{(1)}=-D_{A}\xi^{(0)\beta}R_{\beta}^{A}-[A^{(1)},\xi^{(0)}],

(185)

with
A^{(1)}
from (
144
). One notices that writing out all the different projections on the first term is cumbersome. Therefore, we introduce the shorthand notation

D\xi^{(l)}_{+}=D_{\underline{a}}\xi^{(l)\underline{\beta}}R_{\underline{\beta}}^{\underline{a}}+D_{\overline{a}}\xi^{(l)\overline{\beta}}R_{\overline{\beta}}^{\overline{a}}

(186)

for chiral/anti-chiral contributions and

D\xi^{(l)}_{-}=D_{\underline{a}}\xi^{(l)\overline{\beta}}R_{\overline{\beta}}^{\underline{a}}+D_{\overline{a}}\xi^{(l)\underline{\beta}}R_{\underline{\beta}}^{\overline{a}}

(187)

for mixed chirality generators, to write

\mathcal{X}^{(1)}=-D\xi^{(0)}_{+}-D\xi^{(0)}_{-}-[A^{(1)},\xi^{(0)}]\,.

(188)

In this form, one can already do the identification at the level of (
168
) with the result

\displaystyle\delta A^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-D\xi^{(0)}_{-}-[A^{(1)},\xi^{(0)}]\,,\qquad\text{and}

(189)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=D\xi^{(0)}_{+}\,.

(190)

From the first equation, we get the transformation

\delta\widetilde{A}^{(1)}_{\overline{a}\underline{\beta}_{1}}=-D_{\overline{a}}\widetilde{\xi}^{(0)}_{\underline{\beta}_{1}}+\Xi^{(0)\overline{c}}_{\overline{a}}\widetilde{A}^{(1)}_{\overline{c}\underline{\beta}_{1}}+\widetilde{A}^{(1)\underline{\gamma}_{1}}_{\overline{a}}\widetilde{\xi}^{(0)\underline{\delta}_{1}}f_{\underline{\gamma}_{1}\underline{\delta}_{1}\underline{\beta}_{1}}\,,

(191)

where we used

(S^{-1})_{\alpha_{1}}{}^{\gamma}(S^{-1})_{\beta_{1}}{}^{\delta}f_{\gamma\delta}{}^{\epsilon}=f_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}{}^{\gamma_{1}}(S^{-1})_{\gamma_{1}}{}^{\epsilon}

(192)

to transition to
\widetilde{A}
. It originates directly from (
116
). Alternatively, one can compute it by using the result for
\widetilde{A}^{(1)}
obtained in (
142
) and the leading order transformation of the physical frame. Both match and thereby provide a consistency check. The same holds for the conjugate chirality. From equation (
190
), we also get

\displaystyle R_{1}:\qquad\qquad\Xi^{(1)\underline{\alpha}}_{\underline{a}}

\displaystyle=D_{\underline{a}}\xi^{(0)\underline{\alpha}}

(193)

resulting in

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}^{(1)\underline{\alpha}}_{\underline{a}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{2}{g_{-}}\Xi^{(1)\underline{\alpha}}_{\underline{a}}\,,

(194)

as non-vanishing contributions to
\widetilde{\xi}^{(1)\alpha}
. In conclusion, we have performed the first step of the gauge fixing, resulting in

\xi^{(1)}=D\xi^{(0)}_{+}+\xi^{(1)\alpha}R_{\alpha}\,.

(195)

3.5.1
Leading order

The next order is already more complicated with

after making use of the index-free

defined in (

\displaystyle\delta A^{(2)}+\delta E^{(2)}E^{-1}

\displaystyle=-D\xi^{(1)}_{-}-[A^{(1)},\xi^{(1)}]-[A^{(2)},\xi^{(0)}]\,,\qquad\text{and}

(197)

\boxed{a=-\frac{2\underline{\chi}}{g_{-}^{2}}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad b=\frac{2\overline{\chi}}{g_{+}^{2}}\,.}

(203)

At the same time, we also extract the gauge-fixing constraints

\displaystyle K:\qquad\Xi^{(2)}_{\underline{a}\underline{b}}

\displaystyle=-A^{(1)}_{[\underline{a}|\overline{\beta}}D_{|\underline{b}]}\xi^{(0)\overline{\beta}}\,,

(204)

\displaystyle R_{1}:\qquad\Xi^{(2)}_{\underline{a}\underline{\beta}}

(207)

In particular, we will need the components

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}^{(2)}_{\underline{a}_{1}\underline{a}_{2}}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{g_{-}}A^{(1)}_{[\underline{a}_{1}|\overline{\beta}}D_{|\underline{a}_{2}]}\xi^{(0)\overline{\beta}}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{g_{-}}A^{(1)\overline{c}}_{[\underline{\alpha}|}D_{\overline{c}}\xi^{(0)}_{|\underline{\beta}]}

(209)

for the gGS transformations with four derivatives. This process continues to higher orders, but we will not pursue it in full detail here. The only exception is

\widetilde{\xi}^{(3)}=D\xi^{(2)}_{+}+\tfrac{1}{2}[A^{(2)},D\xi^{(0)}_{-}]+\tfrac{1}{2}[A^{(1)},D\xi_{-}^{(1)}+\delta E^{(2)}E^{-1}]

which is needed later. Note that here

3.5.2

3.6
Action on the mega-space

A perk of the Poláček-Siegel construction is that it allows to easily construct invariant quantities under the symmetry it implements. Such quantities are essential to construct actions for physical theories. In general, there is more than one invariant. Hence, which one should we choose for the action? Our guiding principle for this equation is that we know that at leading order, it should be the standard double field theory action. Only in higher orders, it will receive corrections which are then completely fixed by symmetry. The same idea is used in the original gBdR identification. 4
Conclusions

As demonstrated in the last section, at the end of the day, we recover the results from the original gBdR identification. Still our approach is distinguished from it in different aspects, which we would like to point our here. Perhaps the most important difference is that while the original construction behaves like a black box which only gives the final result, we produce various intermediate results beginning in section
3.4
with the identification. This has two major advantages:

1.

One can reuse the results from lower orders in the computation of higher orders. Moreover, intermediate results can be checked independently from the final expressions for the action and its gGS transformations.

2.

Expressions in terms of the generalized fluxes on the physical space are, in particular at higher orders, very complicated. Take the gGS transformation in (
225
) as an example: In this form, it is nearly impossible to deduce it originates from the much simpler expression (
222
) which even suggests a straightforward extension to all orders.

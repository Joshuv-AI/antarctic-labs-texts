The ‘D-Subspace’ Algorithm for Online Learning over Distributed Networks
Yitong Chen, Danqi Jin, Jie Chen, Cédric Richard

Abstract




Notation.
Normal font letters
x
, bold lowercase letters
\bm{x}
and bold uppercase letters
{\bm{X}}
denote scalars, column vectors and matrices, respectively. The notation
[\,\cdot\,]_{(:,j)}
refers to the
j
-th column. The mathematical expectation is denoted by
{\mathbb{E}}\{\cdot\}
. The set
{\cal{N}}_{k}
denotes the neighbors of node
k
(including
k
itself), and
|{\cal N}_{k}|
denotes its cardinality. Notation
\bigl[\bm{w}_{\ell}\bigr]_{\ell\in{\cal N}_{k}}
represents a matrix consisting of all
\bm{w}_{\ell}
with
\ell\in{\cal N}_{k}
.

This material introduces the D-Subspace algorithm derived on the basis of the centralized algorithm
[
1
]
, which originally addresses parameter estimation problems under subspace constraint.

Consider a connected network with
N
agents. The set of all agents is denoted as
{\cal N}\triangleq\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
. Each agent
k\in{\cal N}
is endowed with a strongly convex, real-valued and differentiable cost function
J_{k}(\bm{w}_{k})
, which corresponds to the expectation of a loss function
G_{k}(\bm{w}_{k};\bm{s}_{k,n})
:

J_{k}(\bm{w}_{k})\triangleq{\mathbb{E}}\{G_{k}(\bm{w}_{k};\bm{s}_{k,n})\},

(1)

where the expectation operator
{\mathbb{E}}\{\cdot\}
is evaluated over the distribution of random data
\bm{s}_{k,n}
, with subscripts
k
and
n
representing node index and time instant, respectively. We denote the real-valued parameter vector
\bm{w}_{k}^{\star}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{L}
as the unique minimizer of
J_{k}(\bm{w}_{k})
. Define a matrix
\bm{W}^{\star}
as:

\bm{W}^{\star}\triangleq[\bm{w}_{1}^{\star},\ \ \bm{w}_{2}^{\star},\ \ \cdots,\ \ \bm{w}_{N}^{\star}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{L\times N}

(2)

The aim of this material is to explore a situation where
\bm{W}^{\star}
is a low-rank matrix with rank
r^{\star}
. In this case, we have:

\displaystyle\bm{w}^{\star}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r^{\star}}\alpha_{k,i}^{o}\bm{c}_{i}=\bm{C}\cdot\bm{\alpha}_{k}^{o}

(3)

where
\{\bm{c}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r^{\star}}
is a basis,
\{\alpha_{k,i}^{o}\}_{i=1}^{r^{\star}}
are corresponding weights, matrix
\bm{C}\triangleq[\bm{c}_{1}\,\bm{c}_{2}\cdots\bm{c}_{r^{\star}}]\in{\mathrm{I\!R}}^{L\times r^{\star}}
, and vector
\bm{\alpha}_{k}^{o}\triangleq[\alpha_{k,1}^{o}\,\alpha_{k,2}^{o}\cdots\alpha_{k,r^{\star}}^{o}]^{\top}
. In this material, we assume that
\bm{\alpha}_{k}^{o}
is known before. Substituting (
3
) into (
2
), we have:

\bm{W}^{\star}=\bm{C}\cdot\bm{\Theta}^{o}

(4)

where matrix
\bm{\Theta}^{o}\triangleq[\bm{\alpha}_{1}^{o}\,\bm{\alpha}_{2}^{o}\,\cdots\bm{\alpha}_{N}^{o}]\in{\mathrm{I\!R}}^{r^{\star}\times N}
is assumed to be known. Consequently, a centralized optimization problem emerges:

\displaystyle\argmin_{\bm{w}_{\ell:{\ell\in{\cal N}}}}{\sum}_{\ell=1}^{N}J_{\ell}(\bm{w}_{\ell})

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}\quad\bigl[\bm{W}^{\top}\bigr]_{(:,j)}\in{\cal R}\Bigl([\bm{\Theta}^{o}]^{\top}\Bigr),\,\,\forall\,\,j

(5)

where
\bm{W}\triangleq\bigl[\bm{w}_{\ell}\bigr]_{\ell\in{\cal N}}
is an estimate of
\bm{W}^{\star}
, and
{\cal R}(\cdot)
denotes the range space operator. In order to solve (
5
) iteratively, the gradient projection method can be applied, resulting in:

\displaystyle{\bm{\psi}}_{k,n+1}=\bm{w}_{k,n}-\mu_{k}\nabla_{\bm{w}_{k}}G_{k}(\bm{w}_{k,n};\bm{s}_{k,n})

(6)

\displaystyle{\bm{\Psi}}_{n+1}\triangleq[{\bm{\psi}}_{1,n+1},\,{\bm{\psi}}_{2,n+1},\,\cdots,{\bm{\psi}}_{N,n+1}]

(7)

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}_{n+1}=\bigl[{\cal P}_{[\bm{\Theta}^{o}]^{\top}}\cdot({\bm{\Psi}}_{n+1}^{\top})\bigr]^{\top}={\bm{\Psi}}_{n+1}\cdot{\cal P}_{[\bm{\Theta}^{o}]^{\top}}

(8)

where the projection matrix
{\cal P}_{[\bm{\Theta}^{o}]^{\top}}
is defined as:

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{[\bm{\Theta}^{o}]^{\top}}

\displaystyle\triangleq[\bm{\Theta}^{o}]^{\top}(\bm{\Theta}^{o}[\bm{\Theta}^{o}]^{\top})^{-1}\bm{\Theta}^{o}.

(9)

Equations (
6
)-(
8
) are centralized solution, abbreviated as ‘C-Subspace’ in this material.

We also aim to introduce a distributed solution. Given that the network is connected and only local data exchanges are allowed, we define a local optimal matrix
\bm{W}^{\star}_{k}
for each node
k
as:

\bm{W}_{k}^{\star}\triangleq\bigl[\bm{w}_{\ell}^{\star}\bigr]_{\ell\in{\cal N}_{k}}\in{\mathrm{I\!R}}^{L\times|{\cal N}_{k}|}

(10)

To ensure the uniqueness of
\bm{W}_{k}^{\star}
, we arrange its columns
\bm{w}_{\ell}^{\star}
with
\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{k}
in ascending order with respect to
\ell
, such that
\bm{w}_{\ell}^{\star}
is its
i_{\ell}^{(k)}
-th column.
Within the neighborhood
{\cal N}_{k}
of each node
k
, we have

1

Note that notation
(\cdot)^{(k)}_{\ell,\cdot}
denotes a quantity related to node
\ell
, which is evaluated at node
k
and provided by node
k
.
:

\displaystyle\bm{w}^{\star}_{\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{k}^{\star}}\alpha_{\ell,i}^{(k)}\bm{c}_{k,i}=\bm{C}_{k}\cdot\bm{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(k)},\,\,\forall\ell\in{\cal N}_{k}

(11)

where
r_{k}^{\star}
is the rank of matrix
\bm{W}_{k}^{\star}
,
\{\bm{c}_{k,i}\}_{i=1}^{r_{k}^{\star}}
is a basis, with
\bigl\{\alpha_{\ell,i}^{(k)}\bigr\}_{i=1}^{r_{k}^{\star}}
the corresponding weights with respect to node
k
, matrix
\bm{C}_{k}\triangleq[\bm{c}_{k,1}\,\bm{c}_{k,2}\cdots\bm{c}_{k,r_{k}^{\star}}]\in{\mathrm{I\!R}}^{L\times r_{k}^{\star}}
, and vector
\bm{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(k)}\triangleq\bigl[\alpha_{\ell,1}^{(k)}\,\alpha_{\ell,2}^{(k)}\cdots\alpha_{\ell,r_{k}^{\star}}^{(k)}\bigr]^{\top}
. To ensure the uniqueness of (
11
), we require that for all
k
, all
\ell\in{\cal N}_{k}
and all
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,r_{k}^{\star}\}
, there exists a
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,r^{\star}\}
, such that:

\displaystyle\alpha_{\ell,i}^{(k)}=\alpha_{\ell,j}^{o}\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\bm{c}_{k,i}=\bm{c}_{j}.

(12)

Similarly, in this material, we assume that
\bm{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(k)}
is known before. Substituting (
11
) into (
10
), we have:

\bm{W}_{k}^{\star}=\bm{C}_{k}\cdot\bm{\Theta}_{k}

(13)

where matrix
\bm{\Theta}_{k}\triangleq\bigl[\bm{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(k)}\bigr]_{\ell\in{\cal N}_{k}}\in{\mathrm{I\!R}}^{r_{k}^{\star}\times|{\cal N}_{k}|}
is known, with
\bm{\alpha}_{\ell}^{(k)}
being its
i_{\ell}^{(k)}
-th column. Consequently, a distributed optimization problem emerges at each node
k
as:

\displaystyle\argmin_{\bm{w}_{\ell:{\ell\in{\cal N}_{k}}}}{\sum}_{\ell\in{\cal N}_{k}}J_{\ell}(\bm{w}_{\ell})

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}\quad\bigl[\bm{W}_{k}^{\top}\bigr]_{(:,j)}\in{\cal R}\Bigl([\bm{\Theta}_{k}]^{\top}\Bigr),\,\,\forall\,\,j

(14)

where
\bm{W}_{k}
is an estimate of
\bm{W}_{k}^{\star}
, with its
i_{\ell}^{(k)}
-th column serving as an estimate of
\bm{w}_{\ell}^{\star}
. To solve (
14
) iteratively, we apply the gradient projection method. Local counterparts corresponding to the same estimate are further combined
[
2
]
, resulting in:

\displaystyle{\bm{\psi}}_{k,n+1}=\bm{w}_{k,n}-\mu_{k}\nabla_{\bm{w}_{k}}G_{k}(\bm{w}_{k,n};\bm{s}_{k,n})

(15)

\displaystyle{\bm{\Psi}}_{k,n+1}\triangleq\bigl[{\bm{\psi}}_{\ell,n+1}\bigr]_{\ell\in{\cal N}_{k}}

(16)

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}_{k,n+1}=\bigl[{\cal P}_{[\bm{\Theta}_{k}]^{\top}}\cdot({\bm{\Psi}}_{k,n+1}^{\top})\bigr]^{\top}={\bm{\Psi}}_{k,n+1}\cdot{\cal P}_{[\bm{\Theta}_{k}]^{\top}}

(17)

\displaystyle{\bm{\phi}}_{k,n+1}^{(\ell)}\triangleq\bigl[\bm{\Phi}_{\ell,n+1}\bigr]_{(:,{i}_{k}^{(\ell)})}

(18)

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{k,n+1}=\sum_{\ell\in{\cal N}_{k}}a_{\ell k}\bm{\phi}_{k,n+1}^{(\ell)}

(19)

where the projection matrix
{\cal P}_{[\bm{\Theta}_{k}]^{\top}}
is defined as:

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{[\bm{\Theta}_{k}]^{\top}}

\displaystyle\triangleq[\bm{\Theta}_{k}]^{\top}(\bm{\Theta}_{k}[\bm{\Theta}_{k}]^{\top})^{-1}\bm{\Theta}_{k},

(20)

and
a_{\ell k}
are single-task combination coefficients satisfying:

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in{\cal N}_{k}}a_{\ell k}=1,\;a_{\ell k}\geq 0,\;{\rm and}\;a_{\ell k}=0\;{\rm{if}}\;\ell\notin{\cal{N}}_{k}.

(21)

Moreover, in several cases, the diagonal loading technique can be incorporated into (
20
), resulting in:

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{[\bm{\Theta}_{k}]^{\top}}

\displaystyle\triangleq[\bm{\Theta}_{k}]^{\top}(\bm{\Theta}_{k}[\bm{\Theta}_{k}]^{\top}+\eta\bm{I})^{-1}\bm{\Theta}_{k},

(22)

where
\eta>0
is diagonal loading factor with a small value. Equations (
15
)-(
19
) are distributed solution, abbreviated as ‘D-Subspace’ in this material.

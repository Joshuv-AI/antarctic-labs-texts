On the Lavrentiev gap for manifold-valued maps
Carlo Alberto Antonini, Filomena De Filippis, Cintia Pacchiano Camacho

Abstract
. We investigate the validity and the failure of modular density of smooth maps on compact manifolds.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35B65, 46E35, 58D15

1.
Introduction

This work is devoted to establishing the density of smooth maps between Riemannian manifolds
in nonhomogeneous spaces characterized by the finiteness of certain anisotropic energies. Let

(1.1)

\mathsf{M}\text{ be an $\textnormal{{m}}$-dimensional oriented compact manifold},

(1.2)

\mathsf{N}\text{ be an $\textnormal{{n}}$-dimensional oriented compact manifold},

and consider a Sobolev space
W^{1,\varphi}
. The problem of approximating Sobolev maps between manifolds is classical, and its resolution depends critically on both the function space and the topology of the manifolds. For maps with values in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and Young function
\varphi(t)=t^{p}
, the approximation argument is straightforward: standard convolution with a smooth kernel produces a sequence of smooth maps that converge strongly.
When the target is a manifold
\mathsf{N}
, the situation is more delicate. Convolution now takes values in the convex hull of
\mathsf{N}
, so one must subsequently project these values back onto
\mathsf{N}
, for instance by using the nearest-point projection.
This procedure works in the classical Sobolev setting
\varphi(t)=t^{p}
for the superdimensional case
p\geq\textnormal{{m}}
. Indeed, by Morrey-Sobolev embedding, maps in
W^{1,p}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
are continuous when
p>\textnormal{{m}}
or belong to the space of functions with vanishing mean oscillation when
p=\textnormal{{m}}
(see
[
8
]
), so projecting them back onto
\mathsf{N}
then yields a sequence in
C^{\infty}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
converging strongly in
W^{1,p}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
. The case
p<\textnormal{{m}}
is more subtle, and the possibility of strong approximation depends on the topology of both manifolds. Even for the unit sphere
\textnormal{{S}}^{2}
the problem is nontrivial. A classical example due to Schoen & Uhlenbeck
[
47
]
is the map

u:\textnormal{{B}}^{3}\to\textnormal{{S}}^{2},\qquad u(x)=\frac{x}{|x|}.

Then,
u\in W^{1,p}(\textnormal{{B}}^{3},\textnormal{{S}}^{2})
, for
1\leq p<3
, but it cannot be strongly approximated in
W^{1,p}(\textnormal{{B}}^{3},\textnormal{{S}}^{2})
by smooth maps with values in
\textnormal{{S}}^{2}
whenever
2\leq p<3
. Seminal work by Bethuel
[
7
]
and Hang & Lin
[
34
]
clarified the approximation problem in the subcritical regime. They showed that for maps in
W^{1,p}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
, the space
C^{\infty}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
is dense if and only if
\mathsf{M}
satisfies the
([p]-1)
-extension property with respect to
\mathsf{N}
(see
[
34
, Definition 2.3]
) and the
[p]
-homotopy group of
\mathsf{N}
is trivial, where
[p]
denotes the integer part of
p
. Hajłasz
[
30
]
later extended these results to more general manifold domains showing that density holds provided
\mathsf{N}
is
[{p}]
-connected. The space
W^{1,\textnormal{{m}}}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
is thus borderline if one wishes to approximate with smooth maps avoiding topological restrictions on the manifolds. However, this property persists in slightly larger Sobolev-type spaces, such as Orlicz spaces
W^{1,A}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
built upon Young functions
A
satisfying suitable conditions depending on
m
. The key insight, originating in the work of Hajłasz, Iwaniec, Malý & Onninen
[
32
]
, is that for maps in Sobolev spaces slightly larger than
W^{1,\textnormal{{m}}}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
, it is possible to detect certain sets on which a given map is still continuous. This leads to the property of vanishing web oscillations. The answer in the Orlicz setting was provided by Carozza & Cianchi
[
11
]
, who showed that if the Young function
A
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
and
\nabla_{2}
conditions near infinity together with a sharp integral condition (which places
W^{1,A}
either slightly larger than
W^{1,\textnormal{{m}}}
or contained in
W^{1,\textnormal{{m}}}
) then every map in
W^{1,A}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
possesses vanishing web oscillations, establishing the density of smooth maps without any topological constraints on the manifolds.

In the present paper, we go beyond the classical and Orlicz settings, and consider the more general Musielak-Orlicz spaces
W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
, where the function
\varphi
depends explicitly on the variable
x\in\mathsf{M}
as well. We show that, under suitable assumptions on
\varphi
and appropriate topological conditions on
\mathsf{M}
and
\mathsf{N}
, the space
C^{\infty}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
is dense in the Musielak-Orlicz space
W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
. To our knowledge, this is the first systematic study of smooth approximation for manifold-valued maps in this kind of framework. We consider a map
\varphi:\mathsf{M}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that:

(1.3)

x\mapsto\varphi(x,t)\quad\text{is measurable for all }t\in[0,\infty),

and, for every
x\in\mathsf{M}
,

(1.4)

\varphi(x,t)=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad t=0,

(1.5)

t\mapsto\varphi(x,t)\quad\text{is convex},

(1.6)

\exists\beta,\gamma>1:\ t\mapsto\frac{\varphi(x,t)}{t^{\beta}}\text{ is almost increasing},\quad t\mapsto\frac{\varphi(x,t)}{t^{\gamma}}\text{ is almost decreasing },

(1.7)

\exists\ \textnormal{{L}}\geq 1:\ \textnormal{{L}}^{-1}\leq\varphi(x,1)\leq\textnormal{{L}}.

Moreover, we assume the following: for all
\textnormal{{d}}>1
there exists a constant
\textnormal{{c}}\equiv\textnormal{{c}}(\beta,\gamma,\textnormal{{d}})
, where
\beta
and
\gamma
are the exponents arising in (
1.6
), such that

(1.8)

\varphi(x_{1},t)\leq\textnormal{{c}}(\varphi(x_{2},t)+1),\text{ for all }t\in\left[0,\textnormal{{d}}\varrho^{-\min\left\{1,\frac{\textnormal{{m}}}{\beta}\right\}}\right],

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{\varrho}
. Here,
\mathbb{B}_{\varrho}
is a geodesic ball of radius
\varrho<1/2
and
\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{\varrho}
denotes its closure.

These hypotheses are naturally satisfied by a wide class of functionals, most notably the double phase integrand

(1.9)

\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q},

with
0\leq a(\cdot)\in C^{0,\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]

and the exponents
1<p<q
such that

(1.10)

q\leq p+\alpha\max\left\{1,\frac{p}{\textnormal{{m}}}\right\},

see
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
. A more general example satisfying our assumptions is provided by the variable-exponent integrand

(1.11)

\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}+a(x)t^{q(x)},

where
1<p\leq p(x)\leq q(x)\leq q
and
p(\cdot),q(\cdot)
are log-Hölder continuous and
0\leq a(\cdot)\in C^{0,\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
and (
1.10
) holds, see
[
10
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Double phase functionals were introduced by Zhikov
[
49
,
48
]
in the framework of homogenization and in relation to the Lavrentiev phenomenon. Moreover, this class of variational integrals represents a model for strongly anisotropic materials, with
significant applications in elasticity theory. The interplay between the
p
- and
q
-growth regimes is dictated by the vanishing behavior of the coefficient
a(\cdot)
. Over the past decade, an extensive regularity theory has emerged for double phase and, more generally, for nonuniformly elliptic functionals. In this direction, Marcellini
[
40
,
43
,
41
,
42
]
investigated
(p,q)
-nonuniformly elliptic integrals, providing the first systematic results in this setting. Later contributions by Baroni, Colombo, and Mingione
[
5
,
14
,
13
]
provided a complete framework for regularity, which subsequently inspired a vast body of literature, see
[
1
,
29
,
37
,
38
,
44
,
45
,
46
,
16
,
21
,
22
,
39
,
23
]
and the references therein. Let us also mention that problems characterized by linear or nearly linear growth have been analyzed, among others, in
[
6
,
27
,
28
]
.

Now, condition (
1.10
) is connected with the nonuniform ellipticity of the associated Euler-Lagrange system

(1.12)

-\mathrm{div}\,A(x,Du)=0,

where

A(x,z)=|z|^{p-2}z+(q/p)\,a(x)|z|^{q-2}z.

As shown in
[
10
, Section 3]
, for (
1.9
) and (
1.11
), it can be verified that assumption (
1.8
) holds if (
1.10
) is true. Observe that the (nonlocal) ellipticity ratio

(1.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\sup_{x\in\textnormal{{B}}}\text{highest eigenvalue of }\partial_{z}A(x,Du)}{\inf_{x\in\textnormal{{B}}}\text{lowest eigenvalue of }\partial_{z}A(x,Du)}\,\approx\,1+\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\textnormal{{B}})}|Du|^{q-p}

can become unbounded on any ball
\textnormal{{B}}\subset\Omega
intersecting the transition region
\{a(\cdot)=0\}
. Then, condition (
1.10
), is precisely designed to ensure that
a(\cdot)
decays sufficiently rapidly to prevent uncontrolled degeneracy, thereby guaranteeing maximal gradient regularity for local minimizers. The necessity of such a condition has been confirmed through explicit counterexamples in
[
2
,
4
,
25
,
26
,
3
]
showing that, when (
1.10
) is violated, the so-called Lavrentiev phenomenon can occur, i.e., the infimum of a variational integral over smooth functions is strictly greater than its infimum over the natural energy space, and this prevents the density of smooth function in
W^{1,\varphi}
. We remark that, in our manifold-valued framework, the compactness of
\mathsf{N}
ensures the boundedness of maps, which allows us to take
q\leq p+\alpha
, in the case
p\leq\textnormal{{m}}
.

We also recall that, if
w\notin L^{\infty}
, the appropriate bound becomes

q\leq p+p\alpha/\textnormal{{m}}
,
corresponding to (
1.8
) with

t\in[0,\textnormal{{d}}\varrho^{-\textnormal{{m}}/p}]
, see
[
14
]
. A related but more delicate situation arises in the study of strongly nonuniformly elliptic functionals, where even the pointwise ellipticity ratio becomes unbounded, as
|z|\to+\infty
, in a nonbalanced fashion:

\displaystyle\frac{\text{highest eigenvalue of }\partial_{z}A(x,Du)}{\text{lowest eigenvalue of }\partial_{z}A(x,Du)}\,\approx\,1+|z|^{\delta},\quad\delta>0.

Even though these systems are strongly nonuniformly elliptic, recent work has developed a Schauder’s regularity theory
[
17
,
19
]
, which also applies to variational problems with nearly linear growth, see
[
18
,
24
,
20
]
.

Our goal is to provide a comprehensive account of smooth approximation in
W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
, for
\varphi
satisfying (
1.3
)-(
1.8
). The first result establishes that if the function
\varphi
satisfies suitable integral conditions allowing
W^{1,\varphi}
to be either slightly larger than
W^{1,\textnormal{{m}}}
or contained in it, then maps in
W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
have vanishing web oscillations, and thus smooth maps are dense in the related Musielak-Orlicz Sobolev space. Here,
no topological assumption on the manifolds other than (
1.1
),(
1.2
) are assumed.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
and
\mathsf{N}
be Riemannian manifolds as in (
1.1
), (
1.2
). Let
\varphi:\mathsf{M}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a function satisfying (
1.3
)-(
1.8
) and denote by

(1.14)

\varphi^{-}_{\mathsf{M}}(t):=\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathsf{M}}\varphi(x,t).

Assume either

(1.15)

\textnormal{{m}}=2\text{ and }\int^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{-}_{\mathsf{M}}(t)}{t^{3}}\,{\rm d}t=\infty,

or

(1.16)

\textnormal{{m}}\geq 3\text{ and }\int^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{\varphi^{-}_{\mathsf{M}}(t)}\right)^{\frac{2}{\textnormal{{m}}-2}}\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}\left(\frac{s}{\varphi^{-}_{\mathsf{M}}(s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\textnormal{{m}}-2}}\right)^{-\textnormal{{m}}}\,{\rm d}t=\infty.

Then,
C^{\infty}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
is dense in
W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
.

Assumptions (
1.15
) and (
1.16
) are designed to control the admissible growth rates in the
t
-variable. In particular, they allow for functions whose infimum grows slightly slower than
t^{\textnormal{{m}}}
at infinity. A typical example in the double-phase setting is

\varphi(x,t)=\frac{t^{\textnormal{{m}}}}{\log t}+a(x)\,t^{\textnormal{{m}}},

with
a(\cdot)\geq 0
Hölder continuous function. More general examples allowed by Theorem
1.1
are double phase functionals of the type

\varphi(x,t)=\frac{t^{p}}{\log t}+a(x)\,t^{q}\quad\text{or}\quad\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}+a(x)\,t^{q}

with
p\geq\textnormal{{m}}
,
0\leq a(\cdot)\in C^{0,\alpha}
, and
q
satisfying (
1.10
).
Variable exponent functionals of the type (
1.11
) are admitted, as long as
\textnormal{{m}}\leq p(x)\leq q(x)\leq q
,
0\leq a(\cdot)\in C^{0,\alpha}
,
p(\cdot),q(\cdot)
are log-Hölder continuous and (
1.10
) is satisfied.

On the other hand, the next result shows that, under a suitable topological condition on the target manifold, the density of smooth maps still holds without imposing any relation with the dimension
m
of the domain manifold. Here, we assume that

(1.17)

\mathsf{N}\text{ is }\textnormal{{k}}\text{-connected},

with an interplay between
k
and the exponent
\gamma
from (
1.6
). Note that
\textnormal{{k}}<\textnormal{{n}}
, otherwise
\mathsf{N}
would be contractible.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
and
\mathsf{N}
be Riemannian manifolds satisfying (
1.1
), (
1.2
) and (
1.17
). Assume that
\varphi:\mathsf{M}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a function satisfying (
1.3
)-
(\ref{v3})_{1}
,
(\ref{v3})_{2}
with

(1.18)

\gamma\in(1,\textnormal{{k}}+1],

and (
1.7
),(
1.8
). Then, if
\gamma<\textnormal{{k}}+1
,
C^{\infty}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
is strongly dense in
W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
; if
\gamma=\textnormal{{k}}+1
,
C^{\infty}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
is weakly dense in
W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
.

The prototypical example of target manifold fulfilling the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
is
\mathsf{N}=\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}
, namely, the
(N-1)
-dimensional sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which is
(N-2)
-connected.

Before proceeding, we make few observations regarding Theorem
1.2
, emphasizing its connection with the classical Sobolev case
W^{1,p}
and the role of topological assumptions.

Remark 1.1
.

We observe that the situation of Theorem
1.2
reflects the one we have for the classical case
\varphi=t^{p}
where, for
p<\textnormal{{m}}
, some topological assumptions on
\mathsf{M}
and
\mathsf{N}
are necessary for the density result, as shown, for instance, in
[
7
,
30
,
9
,
34
,
33
,
35
,
15
]
. Indeed, in Theorem
1.2
,
W^{1,\varphi}
can be a function space arbitrarily larger than
W^{1,\textnormal{{m}}}
, so Theorem
1.2
complements Theorem
1.1
.

We observe that a direct corollary of the above results is the absence of Lavrentiev phenomenon in any of the two settings addressed.

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose that the hypotheses of either Theorem
1.1
or Theorem
1.2
are satisfied. Then

\inf_{W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})}\int_{\mathsf{M}}\varphi(x,|Du|)\,{\rm d}x=\inf_{C^{\infty}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})}\int_{\mathsf{M}}\varphi(x,|Du|)\,{\rm d}x.

We end the analysis by explaining that, in this nonautonomous setting where
\varphi
is described by structural conditions (
1.3
)-(
1.7
), the local assumption (
1.8
) plays a fundamental role. Indeed, when this condition fails, genuine counterexamples arise, even when
W^{1,\varphi}
is a function space smaller than
W^{1,\textnormal{{m}}}
or when the target manifold is
k
-connected; a phenomenon that does not occur in the classical
W^{1,p}
framework, nor in the
W^{1,A}
setting considered in
[
11
]
. We focus on the double phase functional (
1.9
), and we express failure of
condition (
1.8
) as

(1.19)

q>p+\alpha\max\left\{1,\frac{p-1}{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\right\}.

This condition is sharp for both
p\leq\textnormal{{m}}
and
p>\textnormal{{m}}

as highlighted in
[
2
, Remark 35]
. Following the fractal constructions of Balci, Diening & Surnachev
[
2
]
, we show that when (
1.19
) holds, Lavrentiev phenomenon occurs and consequently smooth maps are not dense in
W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
, for
\mathsf{M}:=[-1,1]^{\textnormal{{m}}}
, i.e. the
m
-dimensional cube, and
\mathsf{N}=\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}_{{\Lambda}}
, where
\Lambda
represents a suitable radii of the sphere; note that the sphere
\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}_{{\Lambda}}
is
(N-2)
-connected. This provides the first vectorial counterexample of the Lavrentiev phenomenon, which we further extend to manifold-valued maps. This is the content of the next theorem.
First, let us introduce some notation. We set
Q_{\mathsf{M}}=(-1,1)^{\textnormal{{m}}}
, and for a given function
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q}_{\mathsf{M}},\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}_{\Lambda})
, we define the spaces

(1.20)

C^{\infty}_{u_{0}}(\overline{Q}_{\mathsf{M}},\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}_{\Lambda}):=\big\{v\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q}_{\mathsf{M}},\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}_{\Lambda}):\,v=u_{0}\,\text{on $\partial Q_{\mathsf{M}}$}\big\},

and correspondingly

(1.21)

W^{1,\varphi}_{u_{0}}(Q_{\mathsf{M}},\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}_{\Lambda}):=\big\{v\in W^{1,\varphi}(Q_{\mathsf{M}},\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}_{\Lambda}):\,v=u_{0}\,\text{on $\partial Q_{\mathsf{M}}$}\big\}.

We have the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\varphi
be as in (
1.9
) and assume that
q,p>1
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
are numbers such that (
1.19
) holds. Then there exist
\Lambda\geq 1
and functions
a\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{Q_{\mathsf{M}}})
,
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q_{\mathsf{M}}},\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}_{{\Lambda}})
,
\bar{u}\in W^{1,\varphi}_{u_{0}}(Q_{\mathsf{M}},\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}_{{\Lambda}})
such that

(1.22)

\inf_{u\in W^{1,\varphi}_{u_{0}}(Q_{\mathsf{M}},\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}_{\Lambda})}\int_{Q_{\mathsf{M}}}\varphi(x,|Du|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}<\inf_{u\in C^{\infty}_{u_{0}}(\overline{Q_{\mathsf{M}}},\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}_{\Lambda})}\int_{Q_{\mathsf{M}}}\varphi(x,|Du|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}

and there is no sequence
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell}\subset C^{\infty}_{u_{0}}(\overline{Q_{\mathsf{M}}},\textnormal{{S}}^{N-1}_{{\Lambda}})
such that
\int_{Q_{\mathsf{M}}}\varphi(x,|Du_{\ell}-D\bar{u}|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}\to 0
.

Notation

(note that
\mathsf{N}

Definition 2.1

(

.

Given an integer

, a manifold

is said to be

\pi_{0}(\tilde{\mathsf{M}})=\pi_{1}(\tilde{\mathsf{M}})=\dots=\pi_{j-1}(\tilde{\mathsf{M}})=\pi_{j}(\tilde{\mathsf{M}})=0

2.2.
Properties of Young functions

In this section we recall the notion of a Young function and present several properties of

\varphi
defining our Musielak-Orlicz space. A map
A:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a
Young function
if it is convex, non constant and vanishes only at
0
.

Accordingly, a mapping
\varphi:\mathsf{M}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called a generalized Young function provided it satisfies the following two conditions:

•

For every
x\in\mathsf{M}
, the function
t\mapsto\varphi(x,t)
is a Young function,

•

For every
t\geq 0
, the function
x\mapsto\varphi(x,t)
is measurable.

Next, the Young conjugate
A^{*}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
of
A
is given by

(2.1)

A^{*}(\eta)=\sup_{\xi\geq 0}\big(\xi\eta-A(\xi)\big).

The second convex conjugate
A^{**}
is defined as

(2.2)

A^{**}(\xi)=\sup_{\eta\geq 0}\big(\xi\eta-A^{*}(\eta)\big).

It is also well known that
A^{**}(\cdot)
is the greatest convex minorant of
A(\cdot)
. Analogously, the Young conjugate function
\varphi^{*}:\mathsf{M}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
of
\varphi(x,t)
is the Young function defined by

{\varphi}^{*}(x,t)=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{st-\varphi(x,s)\},\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathsf{M}.

For what concerns the growth conditions, we say that
b:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is
almost increasing
or
almost decreasing
if there exists
c\geq 1
such
that for any
0<t<s<\infty
, it holds
b(t)\leq cb(s)
and
b(s)\leq cb(t)
, respectively.

Note that, for a function
\varphi
satisfying the almost monotonicity
(\ref{v3})_{1}
with a constant
c\geq 1
, for all
0<m_{1}\leq 1\leq m_{2}<\infty
, it holds

(2.3)

\varphi(x,m_{1}t)\leq c\,{m_{1}}^{\beta}\varphi(x,t)\quad\text{and}\quad c^{-1}m_{2}^{\beta}\varphi(x,t)\leq\varphi(x,m_{2}t),\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\mathsf{M}\times[0,\infty).

Analogously, if
\varphi
satisfies
(\ref{v3})_{2}
with a constant
c\geq 1
, then

(2.4)

c^{-1}m_{1}^{\gamma}\varphi(x,t)\leq\varphi(x,m_{1}t)\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(x,m_{2}t)\leq cm_{2}^{\gamma}\varphi(x,t),\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\mathsf{M}\times[0,\infty).

Thanks to the monotonicity of
t\mapsto\varphi(x,t)
and (
2.3
), it is immediate to verify that
\varphi
satisfies the sub-additivity property

(2.5)

\varphi(x,t+s)\leq c\,\big(\varphi(x,t)+\varphi(x,s)\big),\quad\text{for all $x\in\mathsf{M}$ and for all $t,s\geq 0$,}

for some constant
c\geq 1
. Moreover, by (
1.6
)-(
1.7
), we have that

(2.6)

c\,\min\{t^{\beta},t^{\gamma}\}\leq\varphi(x,t)\leq C\,\max\{t^{\beta},t^{\gamma}\},\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\mathsf{M}\times[0,\infty),

for some constants
0<c\leq 1\leq C
. Next, we say that
\varphi
satisfies the so-called
\Delta_{2}
-condition if there exists a constant
c
such that

(2.7)

\varphi(x,2t)\leq c\varphi(x,t),\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\mathsf{M}\times[0,\infty),

and that
\varphi
satisfies the
\nabla_{2}
-condition if its Young conjugate
\varphi^{*}
fulfills the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Observe that, by (
2.3
)-(
2.4
), we have that

(2.8)

if
\varphi
satisfies (
1.6
), then it satisfies both
\Delta_{2}
- and
\nabla_{2}
-condition.

Whenever
\varphi
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
or
\nabla_{2}
condition, we write
\varphi\in\Delta_{2}
and
\varphi\in\nabla_{2}
respectively.

Now, let
\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}
be given by (
1.14
). Since it is an infimum of convex (hence continuous) functions, then
\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}(\cdot)
is lower-semicontinuous. Moreover, by taking the infimum over
x\in\mathsf{M}
in (
1.6
), we infer that

(2.9)

t\mapsto\frac{\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}(t)}{t^{\beta}}\text{ is almost increasing},\quad t\mapsto\frac{\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}(t)}{t^{\gamma}}\text{ is almost decreasing.}

Let us now denote by
\Psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
the greatest convex minorant of
\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}
, that is
\Psi=(\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-})^{**}
.
We claim that there exists
C\geq 1
such that

(2.10)

\Psi(t)\leq\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}(t)\leq C\,\Psi(2t)\quad\text{for all $t\geq 0$.}

The left inequality follows from the definition of
\Psi
. Next observe that from (
2.9
), we get that
t\mapsto\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}(t)/t
is almost increasing, that is
\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}(t)\geq c\,\frac{t}{s}\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}(s)
for
t>s
, for some constant
c\in(0,1)
. It immediately follows that, for
s>0
fixed, the function
t\mapsto c\,\left(\frac{t}{s}-1\right)\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}(s)
is a convex minorant of
\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}
on
[0,\infty)
, hence
\Psi(t)\geq c\,\left(\frac{t}{s}-1\right)\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}(s)
. Thereby choosing
s=t/2
, we obtain (
2.10
) with
C=c^{-1}
.

The advantage of working with
\Psi
in place of
\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}
is that
\Psi
is a convex function. Additionally, thanks to (
2.10
),
\Psi
satisfies the same monotonicity properties (
2.9
) of
\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}
, i.e.

(2.11)

t\mapsto\frac{\Psi(t)}{t^{\beta}}\text{ is almost increasing, }\quad t\mapsto\frac{\Psi(t)}{t^{\gamma}}\text{ is almost decreasing.}

In particular,
\Psi
is a Young function satisfying the
\Delta_{2}
- and
\nabla_{2}
- conditions. Furthermore, whenever (
1.15
) and (
1.16
) are valid, they also hold true with
\Psi
in place of
\varphi_{\mathsf{M}}^{-}
thanks to (
2.10
), that is

(2.12)

\textnormal{{m}}=2\text{ and }\int^{\infty}\frac{\Psi(t)}{t^{3}}\,{\rm d}t=\infty,

or

(2.13)

\textnormal{{m}}\geq 3\text{ and }\int^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{\Psi(t)}\right)^{\frac{2}{\textnormal{{m}}-2}}\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}\left(\frac{s}{\Psi(s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\textnormal{{m}}-2}}\right)^{-\textnormal{{m}}}\,{\rm d}t=\infty.

For such
\Psi
, we also define the auxiliary Young function
\Psi_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}
as

(2.14)

\Psi_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}(t)=\begin{cases}\Psi(t)&\text{if }\textnormal{{m}}=2,\\[4.0pt]
\displaystyle\left(t^{\frac{\textnormal{{m}}-1}{\textnormal{{m}}-2}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{{\Psi}^{*}(r)}{r^{1+\frac{\textnormal{{m}}-1}{\textnormal{{m}}-2}}}\,\,{\rm d}r\right)^{*}&\text{if }\textnormal{{m}}\geq 3,\end{cases}

for
t\in[0,\infty)
. Here,
(\cdot)^{*}
denotes the Young conjugate as in (
2.1
).

2.3.
Musielak-Orlicz spaces

Here we introduce the Musielak-Orlicz space and provide the basic definitions and properties that will be used throughout the paper. We start considering an open subset
\Omega_{\mathsf{M}}\subseteq\mathsf{M}
, with
\mathsf{M}
as in (
1.1
), and a Young function
\varphi
. We denote by
L^{0}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N})
the set of measurable functions on
\Omega_{\mathsf{M}}
, equipped with the measure introduced by the Riemannian metric on
\mathsf{M}
, which agrees with the Hausdorff measure
\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}}
.
The Musielak-Orlicz space
L^{\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as

L^{\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in L^{0}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\Omega_{\mathsf{M}}}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}}<\infty,\text{ for some }\lambda>0\right\},

and we endow such space with the so-called Luxemburg norm

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}})}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega_{\mathsf{M}}}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}}\leq 1\right\}.

Moreover, if
\varphi(x,\cdot)\in\Delta_{2}
, then

L^{\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in L^{0}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\Omega_{\mathsf{M}}}\varphi\left(x,k|u(x)|\right)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}}<\infty,\text{ for all }k>0\right\}

and given a sequence
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell}\in L^{\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u\in L^{\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there holds the convergence equivalence (see
[
36
, Lemma 3.3.1]
)

(2.15)

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\lVert u_{\ell}-u\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}})}=0\ \text{ if and only if }\ \lim_{{\ell}\to\infty}\int_{\Omega_{\mathsf{M}}}\varphi(x,|u_{\ell}-u|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}}=0.

We next introduce the corresponding Musielak-Orlicz Sobolev space

W^{1,\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N}):|Du|\in L^{\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N})\right\},

which is endowed with the norm

\lVert u\rVert_{W^{1,\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}})}:=\lVert u\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}})}+\lVert Du\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}})}.

To describe functions vanishing outside
\Omega_{\mathsf{M}}
, we define the space

W^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in W^{1,\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N}):\text{ the extension to $\mathsf{M}\setminus\Omega_{\mathsf{M}}$ of $u$ by $0$ belongs to }W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})\right\}.

Finally, when
\mathsf{N}
is a submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
as in (
1.2
), we set

W^{1,\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathsf{N}):=\left\{u\in W^{1,\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathsf{M}},\mathbb{R}^{N}):\text{Im}(u)\subset\mathsf{N}\text{ holds almost everywhere}\right\}.

We conclude with the notion of vanishing web oscillations, which will be instrumental in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We recall that a set
F\subset\mathsf{M}
is a
web
if it is compact, of Lebesgue measure zero, and its complement

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}\setminus F=\cup_{i=1}^{\bar{\textnormal{{m}}}}U_{i}
consists of a finite number of components
U_{i}
that are disjoint open connected sets.

Definition 2.2

(Vanishing web oscillations
[
32
]
)
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
be an oriented, compact Riemannian manifold and
w\in W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
We say that
w
has vanishing web oscillations if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a web
F\subset\mathsf{M}
such that:

•

\mathrm{fine\text{-}diam}(F):=\max_{i=1,\dots,\bar{\textnormal{{m}}}}\operatorname{diam}U_{i}\leq\varepsilon;

•

there exists
\eta\in W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{0}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})
with

w-\eta\in W^{1,\varphi}_{0}(U_{i},\mathbb{R}^{N})
for every
U_{i},\ i=1,\dots,\bar{\textnormal{{m}}}
. Equivalently
w\in\eta+W^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\mathsf{M}\setminus F,\mathbb{R}^{N})
;

•

for every
U_{i},\ i=1,\dots,\bar{\textnormal{{m}}}
, the boundary oscillation

\operatorname{osc}(w,\partial U_{i})=\max\big\{|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|:x,y\in\partial U_{i}\big\}

satisfies
\operatorname{osc}(w,\partial U_{i})<\varepsilon
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
be a Riemannian manifold as in (
1.1
), with
\textnormal{{m}}\geq 2
. Let
\varphi(x,\cdot)
be a Young function satisfying the

has positive one-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

Proof.

We start by observing that, since
R\in(0,R_{\mathsf{M}})
, using geodesic spherical coordinates centered at
\tilde{x}

\mathbb{B}^{\textnormal{{m}}}_{R}(\tilde{x})
agrees with
\textnormal{{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}_{R}
in
\mathbb{R}^{\textnormal{{m}}}
, and
\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{r}(\tilde{x})
is equal to
\textnormal{{S}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{r}
. Arguing by contradiction, let us suppose that there exists some
\tilde{x}\in\mathsf{M}
such that (
1.15
) and (
1.16
) holds and such that

r\,\Psi^{-1}_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\textnormal{{S}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{r}}\varphi(y,|w|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\right)>\varepsilon,

for almost every

. Therefore,

\omega_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}r^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\Psi_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\right)<\int_{\textnormal{{S}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{r}}\varphi(y,|w|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1},

where
\omega_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}=\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}(\textnormal{{S}}_{1}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1})
. Therefore, integrating in

(2.18)

\omega_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\Psi_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\right)\,{\rm d}r\leq\int_{0}^{R}\int_{\textnormal{{S}}_{r}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}}\varphi(y,|w|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\,{\rm d}r=\int_{\textnormal{{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}_{R}}\varphi(y,|w|)\,{\rm d}y<\infty.

[

Lemma 2.5
.

\mathsf{M}

) and (

) hold with
r=r_{\tilde{x}}
. Therefore, by (
2.26
), we have

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{r_{\tilde{x}}}(\tilde{x})}\varphi(y,|Dw_{\ell}|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{r_{\tilde{x}}}(\tilde{x})}\varphi(y,|Dw|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}.

Moreover, thanks to (

, such that

(2.28)

\lVert w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1}\rVert_{W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq 2^{-\ell},\quad\text{for every $\ell$.}

The family
\{{\mathbb{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}_{r_{\tilde{x}}}(\tilde{x})\}_{\tilde{x}\in\mathsf{M}}
is an open covering of
\mathsf{M}
. Hence, by the compactness of
\mathsf{M}
, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a finite family
\{{\tilde{x}}_{i}\}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,i_{\varepsilon}
, such that
\{{\mathbb{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}_{i}\}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,i_{\varepsilon}
is a finite covering of
\mathsf{M}
, where
{\mathbb{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}_{i}={\mathbb{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}_{r_{i}}({\tilde{x}}_{i})
and
r_{i}=r_{{\tilde{x}}_{i}}<\varepsilon
.
Clearly, we can suppose that
{\mathbb{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}_{i}\nsubseteq{\mathbb{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}_{j}
if
i\neq j
and we set
\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}=\partial{\mathbb{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}_{i}
. Hence, by (
2.26
) applied with
\tilde{x}=\tilde{x}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,i_{\varepsilon}
, up to extracting a further subsequence, still labeled as
\{w_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
, we have

(2.29)

\displaystyle\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}|w_{\ell}-w|\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}+\tilde{c}r_{i}\Psi^{-1}_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\varphi(y,2|Dw_{\ell}-Dw|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\right)\leq 2^{-\ell-3}\varepsilon.

By the monotonicity of
\varphi

(2.30)

\varphi(x,s+t)\leq\varphi(x,2s)+\varphi(x,2t),\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathsf{M}\text{ and all }s,t\geq 0,

(
2.29
), the fact that
t\mapsto\Psi^{-1}_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}(x,t)
is increasing and that
\Psi^{-1}_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}
is a concave function vanishing at
0
, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{c}r_{i}\Psi^{-1}_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\varphi(y,|Dw_{\ell}-Dw_{{\ell}-1}|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\right)+\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}|w_{\ell}-w_{\ell-1}|\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\tilde{c}r_{i}\Psi^{-1}_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\varphi(y,2|Dw_{\ell}-Dw|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}+\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\varphi(y,2|Dw-Dw_{{\ell}-1}|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}|w_{\ell}-w|\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}+\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}|w-w_{\ell-1}|\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\tilde{c}r_{i}\Psi^{-1}_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\varphi(y,2|Dw_{\ell}-Dw|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\right)+\tilde{c}r_{i}\Psi^{-1}_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\varphi(y,2|Dw-Dw_{{\ell}-1}|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\right)

(2.31)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}|w_{\ell}-w|\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}+\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}|w-w_{\ell-1}|\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\leq 2^{-\ell-2}\varepsilon.

Thanks to (
1.15
)-(
1.16
),
[
12
, Lemma 2.3]
and
[
11
, Lemma 3.3(i)]
, assumption (
2.16
) is fulfilled, so applying (
2.17
) to
\psi=w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1}
on
\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}
, we get

(2.32)

\text{osc}_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}(w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1})\leq\tilde{c}r_{i}\Psi_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}^{-1}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\varphi(y,|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}}(w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1})|)\,\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\right).

Moreover,

(2.33)

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathbb{S}_{i}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}}|w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1}|

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}_{i}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}}|w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1}|\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}.

Therefore, from (
2.4
)-(
2.33
) we obtain

(2.34)

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbb{S}_{i}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}}|w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1}|\leq\inf_{\mathbb{S}_{i}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}}|w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1}|+\text{osc}_{\mathbb{S}_{i}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}}(w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1})\leq 2^{{-\ell-2}}\varepsilon.

Now, for
\delta>0
, let
T_{\delta}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be the smooth truncation operator at the level set
\delta
, i.e.,

(2.35)

T_{\delta}(\xi)=\xi\,\beta_{\delta}(|\xi|),\quad\text{for }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{\textnormal{{m}}},

where
\beta_{\delta}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is defined by

(2.36)

\beta_{\delta}(s)=\begin{cases}1\quad&\text{if $s\in[0,2\delta]$}\\
\frac{4(s-\delta)\delta}{s^{2}}\quad&\text{if $s\geq 2\delta$}.\end{cases}

We inductively define the sequence
\{\eta_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
by

(2.37)

\eta_{1}:=w_{1},\quad\eta_{\ell}-\eta_{{\ell}-1}:=T_{2^{-\ell}\varepsilon}(w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1}),\quad\text{for }\ell\geq 2.

Then
\eta_{\ell}\in C^{1}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N}).
Moreover, for every
\ell\geq 2
, by definition of
\beta(\cdot)
and
\eta_{\ell}
, we have

(2.38)

\sup_{\mathsf{M}}|\eta_{\ell}-\eta_{{\ell}-1}|\leq 2^{2-\ell}\varepsilon.

Again, by definition of
T_{\delta}
and (
2.34
), we have
T_{2^{-\ell}\varepsilon}(w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1})=(w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1})
on each
\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,i_{\varepsilon}
, and for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

(2.39)

\eta_{\ell}=w_{\ell}\quad\text{on}\quad\cup_{i=1}^{i_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}.

Then, for any weakly differentiable function
v
, standard properties of the truncation operator
[
32
, Eq. 5.17]
entail

(2.40)

|DT_{\delta}(v)|\leq|Dv|\quad\text{almost everywhere.}

This information together with (
2.37
) and (
2.28
) yield

(2.41)

\lVert D(\eta_{\ell}-\eta_{{\ell}-1})\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\lVert D(w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1})\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq 2^{-\ell}.

Then, by (
2.38
) and (
2.41
),
\{\eta_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C^{0}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})
, so we can set

\eta:=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\eta_{\ell};

then, since by (
2.39
) it holds
w_{\ell}-\eta_{\ell}\in W^{1,\varphi}_{0}(U_{h},\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have that
w-\eta\in W^{1,\varphi}_{0}(U_{h},\mathbb{R}^{N})
, for
h=1,\dots,h_{\varepsilon}
. Moreover, since
\eta_{\ell}\to\eta
uniformly,
\eta_{\ell}\equiv w_{\ell}
on
\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}
due to (
2.39
), and by (
2.32
) with
\eta_{\ell}
in place of
w_{\ell}-w_{{\ell}-1}
,
(
2.27
) and (
2.25
) with
\tilde{x}=x_{i}
, we get

\displaystyle\text{osc}_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\eta

\displaystyle=\liminf_{{\ell}\to\infty}\text{osc}_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\eta_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}r_{i}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\Psi^{-1}_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\varphi(y,|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}}\eta_{\ell}|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{c}r_{i}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\Psi^{-1}_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\varphi(y,|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}}w_{\ell}|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}r_{i}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\Psi^{-1}_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\varphi(y,|Dw_{\ell}|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\right)

(2.42)

\displaystyle=\tilde{c}r_{i}\Psi^{-1}_{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\left(\mathop{\int\hskip-10.50005pt-\,\!\!\!}\nolimits_{\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{i}}\varphi(y,|Dw|)\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\right)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4},

for
i=1,\dots,i_{\varepsilon}
. The rest of the proof now follows
[
11
, pp. 576]
: we observe that each connected component
U_{h}
of
\mathsf{M}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{i_{\varepsilon}}{\mathbb{S}}_{i}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}
lies within some ball
{\mathbb{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}
from the finite covering of
\mathsf{M}
, and its boundary
\partial U_{h}
consists of portions of spheres
{\mathbb{S}}_{i}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}
intersecting
{\mathbb{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}
. Setting
{\mathbb{S}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}=\partial{\mathbb{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}
, and noting that
{\mathbb{B}}_{i}^{\textnormal{{m}}}\not\subseteq{\mathbb{B}}_{j}^{\textnormal{{m}}}
when
i\neq j
, each sphere
{\mathbb{S}}_{i}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}
intersecting
{\mathbb{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}
must necessarily intersect
\partial{\mathbb{B}}^{\textnormal{{m}}}
as well. Consequently, for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in\partial U_{h}
, we have
z_{1}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{i_{1}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}
and
z_{2}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{i_{2}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}
for appropriate indices, with both
{\mathbb{S}}_{i_{1}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}
and
{\mathbb{S}}_{i_{2}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}
that have not empty intersection with
{\mathbb{S}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}
. Selecting points
y_{1}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{i_{1}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}
and
y_{2}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{i_{2}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}
, we deduce from (
2.4
) that:

|\eta(z_{1})-\eta(z_{2})|\leq|\eta(z_{1})-\eta(y_{1})|+|\eta(y_{1})-\eta(y_{2})|+|\eta(y_{2})-\eta(z_{2})|

\leq\text{osc}_{{\mathbb{S}}_{i_{1}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}}\eta+\text{osc}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}}\eta+\text{osc}_{{\mathbb{S}}_{i_{2}}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}}\eta<\varepsilon,

that is (
2.20
), thus completing the proof.
∎

3.
Density results and absence of Lavrentiev phenomenon

In this section, we establish the density of smooth maps between manifolds in
W^{1,\varphi}
, with consequently absence of Lavrentiev phenomenon.

Theorem
1.1
relies strongly on Lemma
2.5
and follows the approach of
[
32
, Theorem 5.1]
, adapted to our Musielak-Orlicz setting. Theorem
1.2
shows that, in the absence of assumptions (
1.15
) and (
1.16
), the topological condition of
k
-connectedness of the target manifold
\mathsf{N}
allows us to recover the density of smooth maps in
W^{1,\varphi}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
w\in W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
. Let
\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{r_{i}}(x_{i})
be a family of geodesic spheres with
r_{i}<\varepsilon
,
i=1,\dots,i_{\varepsilon}
, and
U_{h}
,
h=1,\dots,h_{\varepsilon}
, the connected component of
\displaystyle\mathsf{M}\setminus\left(\cup_{i=1}^{i_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{S}^{\textnormal{{m}}-1}_{r_{i}}(x_{i})\right)
coming from Lemma
2.5
. For every
h=1,\dots,h_{\varepsilon}
, consider the map

T_{\varepsilon}\circ w:U_{h}\to\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
T_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the truncation operator defined by

T_{\varepsilon}y=y_{*}+(y-y_{*})\,\beta_{\varepsilon}(|y-y_{*}|),

with fixed point
y_{*}\in w(\partial U_{h})\subset\mathsf{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
with
\beta_{\varepsilon}
given by (
2.36
). We observe that
\beta(t)\in[0,1]
and

(3.1)

|T_{\varepsilon}y-y_{*}|\leq 4\varepsilon\quad\text{for every }y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then
T_{\varepsilon}w\in W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and, by (
2.40
), we have that

(3.2)

|D(T_{\varepsilon}w)|\leq|Dw|\quad\text{almost everywhere in }U_{h}.

Moreover, by Lemma
2.5
there exists
u\in(W^{1,\varphi}_{0}\cap L^{\infty})(U_{h},\mathbb{R}^{N})
and a continuous function
\eta\in W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

(3.3)

w=\eta+u\quad\text{ on $U_{h}$.}

We extend
u\equiv 0
on
\mathsf{M}\setminus U_{h}
, and construct a sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j}\subset C^{\infty}_{{c}}(U_{h},\mathbb{R}^{N})
, following the lines of
\{w_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
in (
2.23
), which approximates
u
in
W^{1,\varphi}(U_{h},\mathbb{R}^{N})
, i.e.

(3.4)

u_{j}\xrightarrow{j\to\infty}u\quad\text{in $W^{1,\varphi}_{0}(U_{h},\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{q}(U_{h},\mathbb{R}^{N})$, \quad\text{for } $q\geq 1$.}

We also observe that
\eta-T_{\varepsilon}(\eta+u_{j})
vanishes in a neighborhood of
\partial U_{h}
(possibly depending on
j
). Indeed,
\eta(\partial U_{h})\subset\textnormal{{B}}_{\varepsilon}(y_{*})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
by (
2.19
)-(
2.20
), and since
\eta
is continuous, the image of a suitably small neighborhood of
\partial U_{h}
lies in
\textnormal{{B}}_{3/2\varepsilon}(y_{*})
.

Also, since
u_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U_{h},\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then in a smaller neighborhood
\mathcal{U_{j,h}}
of
\partial U_{h}
, we have that the image of
u_{j}
lies in
\textnormal{{B}}_{1/2\varepsilon}
.
Therefore, the image of
\mathcal{U_{j,h}}
through
\eta+u_{j}
lies in
\textnormal{{B}}_{2\varepsilon}(y_{*})
, so
it only remains to notice that
T_{\varepsilon}y=y
in
\textnormal{{B}}_{2\varepsilon}(y_{*})
, whence
\eta-T_{\varepsilon}(\eta+u_{j})
vanishes in
\mathcal{U_{j,h}}
. Using these facts, together with the continuity of truncation operator
T_{\varepsilon}:W^{1,\varphi}(U_{h},\mathbb{R}^{N})\to W^{1,\varphi}(U_{h},\mathbb{R}^{N})
guaranteed by (
3.2
), and recalling (
3.3
), we conclude that

(3.5)

\begin{split}w-T_{\varepsilon}w&=w-T_{\varepsilon}(\lim_{j\to\infty}(\eta+u_{j}))=\eta+u-\lim_{j\to\infty}T_{\varepsilon}(\eta+u_{j})\\
&=u+\lim_{j\to\infty}(\eta-T_{\varepsilon}(\eta+u_{j}))\in W^{1,\varphi}_{0}(U_{h},\mathbb{R}^{N}).\end{split}

We now apply the above truncation procedure to
w
on each
U_{h}
and we denote the resulting map by
\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
. It follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.5
), that

\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\in(W^{1,\varphi}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}=w\,\text{ on $\partial U_{h}$ for all $h=1,\dots,h_{\varepsilon}$},

and

(3.6)

|D\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq|Dw(x)|,\quad\text{for almost every }x\in\mathsf{M}.

We observe that the image of
\mathsf{M}
via
\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}
is no longer in
\mathsf{N}
, but we have a control on its oscillation: indeed, thanks to (
3.1
), for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in U_{h}
, it holds

(3.7)

|\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}(x_{1})-\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}(x_{2})|\leq 8\varepsilon.

Now, for every
U_{h}
, by Poincaré inequality and (
3.6
), it holds

\int_{U_{h}}\frac{|\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}-w|}{\text{diam}U_{h}}\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}\leq c\int_{U_{h}}|D\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}-Dw|\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}\leq c\int_{U_{h}}|Dw|\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H},

with
c\equiv c(\mathsf{M})
. Recalling that
r_{i}<\varepsilon
, for every
i=1,\dots,i_{\varepsilon}
, we have that
\text{diam}U_{h}<c\,\varepsilon
; so, summing up on
h=1,\dots,h_{\varepsilon}
the previous estimate and using that
U_{h}
are disjoint, we conclude that

(3.8)

\int_{\mathsf{M}}|\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}-w|\,{\rm d}\mathscr{H}\leq c\,\varepsilon\,\lVert Dw\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathsf{M})},

with
c
as above. Now, (
3.6
) implies that the sequence
\{D\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\varphi}
; by the reflexivity of such space (see for instance
[
36
, Theorem 3.6.6]
), for
\varepsilon\to 0
we have that

(3.9)

D\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\to Dw\quad\text{weakly in }L^{\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N\times\textnormal{{m}}}).

Using again (
3.6
) and the lower semicontinuity of the
W^{1,\varphi}
-norm, we get

\|D\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathsf{M})}\leq\|Dw\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathsf{M})}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|D\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathsf{M})},

whence
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|D\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathsf{M})}=\|Dw\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathsf{M})}
, which combined with (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and the uniform convexity of the space
L^{\varphi}
(see for instance
[
36
, Theorem 3.6.6.6]
) yields

(3.10)

\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}w\quad\text{strongly in }W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Now, for every
\varepsilon>0
, we construct, via the same mollification procedure leading to (
2.23
) (replacing
w
with
\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}
), a sequence
\{\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}\}_{\ell}\subset C^{\infty}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

(3.11)

\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\text{ strongly in }W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Then, by the properties of convolution (see
[
32
, Eq. (5.32)]
) there exists
\ell_{\varepsilon}>0
such that, for every
U_{h}
and
\ell\in(0,\ell_{\varepsilon}]
, we have

(3.12)

\text{osc}_{U_{h}}\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}\lesssim_{\mathsf{M}}\text{ess osc}_{U_{h}^{\prime}}\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\leq 24\varepsilon,

where
U_{h}^{\prime}:=\{x\in\mathsf{M}:\,{\rm dist}(x,U_{h})<\ell^{\prime}\}
and
\ell\lesssim_{\mathsf{M}}\ell^{\prime}\lesssim_{\mathsf{M}}\ell
; note that the last inequality follows from (
3.7
) and the remarks after Eq. (5.32) in
[
32
]
.

By (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) we deduce that, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, it holds

(3.13)

\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell_{\varepsilon}}\to w\text{ strongly in }W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Now, we project
\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell_{\varepsilon}}
smoothly onto
\mathsf{N}
. To this end, we consider the closest point projection
\Pi:\mathsf{N}_{\tilde{h}}\to\mathsf{N}
of a suitable tubular neighborhood
\mathsf{N}_{\tilde{h}}
of
\mathsf{N}
onto
\mathsf{N}
, which is a
C^{\infty}
-smooth map. We define the approximating sequence as

w_{k}:=\Pi(\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\ell_{k}}),

where
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
and
\ell_{k}\to 0
are chosen accordingly.

Let us show that the sequence
\{w_{k}\}_{k}
is well defined. By (
3.1
), we have that
\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}(U_{h})
is contained in a
4\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
\mathsf{N}
, say
\mathsf{N}_{\tilde{h}/4}
, for
\varepsilon
small enough. Thanks to (
3.11
), we have that
\tilde{w}^{\ell}_{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}
almost everywhere on
\mathsf{M}
. Therefore, for every mesh
U_{h}
, we can choose a point
x_{0}\in U_{h}
and
k_{0}
large enough such that
|w_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\ell_{k}}(x_{0})-\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x_{0})|<\tilde{h}/4
, for every
k>k_{0}
. This information together with (
3.12
) imply that
w^{\ell_{k}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(U_{h})
lies in
\mathsf{N}_{\tilde{h}}
for
k>k_{0}
large enough, and repeating this argument for every mesh
U_{h}
,

, we deduce that

. Hence

is well defined, and
it belongs to
C^{\infty}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})
thanks to the smoothness of
\Pi
and
w^{\ell}_{\varepsilon}
.

Finally, by (
3.13
), the chain rule, the Lipschitz continuity of
\Pi
and
(\ref{du})_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert w_{k}-w\rVert_{W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})}

\displaystyle=\lVert w_{k}-\Pi(w)\rVert_{W^{1,\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})}

\displaystyle=\lVert w_{k}-\Pi(w)\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})}+\lVert Dw_{k}-D\Pi(w)\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{N})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\gamma,\mathsf{N}}\lVert\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\ell_{k}}-w\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\lVert D\Pi(\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\ell_{k}})\circ D\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\ell_{k}}-D\Pi(w)\circ Dw\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\gamma,\mathsf{N}}\lVert\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\ell_{k}}-w\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\lVert D\Pi(\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\ell_{k}})\circ(D\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\ell_{k}}-Dw)\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\lVert(D\Pi(\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\ell_{k}})-D\Pi(w))\circ Dw\rVert_{L^{\varphi}(\mathsf{M},\mathbb{R}^{N})}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0

This concludes the proof.

\varphi(x,|z|)=|z|^{p}+a(x)|z|^{q},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times\textnormal{{m}}},

where

will be defined below, and assume (
1.19
). We set
Q_{\mathsf{M}}:=(-1,1)^{\textnormal{{m}}}

\overline{Q_{\mathsf{M}}}:=[-1,1]^{\textnormal{{m}}}

, then

\mathcal{C}_{\lambda,k+1}
inductively by removing the open middle portion of length

from each interval in

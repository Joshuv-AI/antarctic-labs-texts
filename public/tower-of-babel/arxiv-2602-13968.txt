Fine properties of functions in complex Sobolev spaces
Ngoc Cuong Nguyen

Abstract
. We study comprehensively local properties of functions in complex Sobolev spaces on a bounded open subset of ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} . The main tool is the corresponding functional capacity for the space which is inspired by the global one due to Vigny [ Vig07 ] . An inequality between this capacity and the Bedford-Taylor capacity for plurisubharmonic functions is proved, which is sharp as far as the exponents are concerned. Moreover, it is shown that the functional capacity is a Choquet capacity. The Alexander-Taylor type inequality for the capacity is also proved. This allows us to strengthen the results in the works of Dinh, Marinescu and Vu [ DMV23 ] , Vigny and Vu [ VV24 ] . Lastly, the Moser-Trudinger type inequality in this space is characterized by the volume-capacity inequality.



1.
Introduction

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold. A natural subspace of the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(X)
, called the complex Sobolev space
W^{*}
, was introduced by Dinh and Sibony
[
DS06
]
in their work on complex dynamics.
This new space turned out to be a good complex version of the former one as it takes into account the complex structure of manifolds. Many important families of functions which carry useful properties of underlying manifolds belong to the space. Among them are Lipschitz functions, bounded quasi plurisubharmonic (psh) functions or more general bounded delta quasi-psh function studied in
[
CW05
]
. Basic functional aspects of this space were studied by Vigny
[
Vig07
]
where he showed that it is a Banach space with a corresponding norm
\|\cdot\|_{*}
, this norm is stronger than the Sobolev norm. However, it is not reflexive and smooth functions are not dense in
W^{*}
with respect to the strong topology of that norm. He also defined a functional capacity for this space and showed that it is a capacity in sense of Choquet. Moreover, the functional capacity is qualitative comparable to the relative capacity of Bedford and Taylor in global pluripotential theory defined in
[
K03
]
.

Thanks to flexible properties this space has found many of applications in complex dynamic in high dimensions, complex Monge-Ampère equations and other areas, see e.g.,
[
BiD23
]
,
[
DKN22
]
,
[
DKW21
]
,
[
DoNV25
]
,
[
Vig17
]
,
[
Vu20
,
Vu24
,
Vu26
]
and
[
WZ24
]
. Also a higher complex Sobolev space is proposed in
[
DoN25
]
.

An element in a Sobolev space is defined almost everywhere up to a set of Lebesgue measure zero which is unlike psh or quasi-psh functions. Its fine properties with respect to the Sobolev capacity are classical ones
[
EG92
]
,
[
KLV21
]
. However, this capacity is not good enough to characterize pluripolar sets, therefore it is not suitable for studying functions in
W^{*}
. One needs to work with the corresponding capacity of
W^{*}
which dominates the Sobolev one. However, the new capacity in complex Sobolev spaces is harder to analyze. A crucial difference is that we can no longer rely on maximal function techniques, which are very powerful, in these spaces (see, e.g
[
KLV21
]
).

Recently, it has been showed in
[
DMV23
]
,
[
Vig07
]
and
[
VV24
]
that pluripotential theory is a suitable and powerful tool for studying
W^{*}
. As a result they made major progresses on study local properties of functions in such spaces.

We continue to study local complex Sobolev spaces which has been considered partially in
[
DMV23
]
and
[
Vig07
]
. Our goal is to develop further this approach by focusing on new and optimal inequalities between the functional capacity and classical capacities of Bedford and Taylor
[
BT82
]
and of Alexander and Taylor
[
AT84
]
in pluripotential theory.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded domain and
\omega=dd^{c}|z|^{2}
the standard Kähler form in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Denote by
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
the usual Sobolev space. For
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
we define
\Gamma_{f}
to be the set of all positive closed
(1,1)
-current
T
satisfying

df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T\quad\text{weakly in }\Omega.

Here we use the normalization

d^{c}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}(\overline{\partial}-\partial),\quad dd^{c}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{\pi}\partial\overline{\partial}.

Consider the subspace

W^{*}(\Omega)=\left\{f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}):\text{there exists $T\in\Gamma_{f}$ with }\|T\|_{\Omega}<+\infty\right\},

where

\|T\|_{\Omega}:=\int_{\Omega}T\wedge\omega^{n-1}.

For
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
one defines the
W^{*}
-norm

\|f\|_{*}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\inf_{T\in\Gamma_{f}}\|T\|_{\Omega}.

We then reprove basic functional results obtained in
[
Vig07
]
. Namely,
(W^{*}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{*})
is a Banach space (Theorem
2.15
) and continuous functions are not dense in
W^{*}(\Omega)
with respect to strong topology of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{*}
(Proposition
2.19
). If
n=1
, then
W^{*}(\Omega)=W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, otherwise
W^{*}(\Omega)
is not reflexive (Corollary
2.20
). It should be pointed out that there are some similarity of these properties with the ones of the space of delta psh functions studied by Cegrell and Wiklund
[
CW05
]
.

Inspired by the functional capacity in
[
Vig07
]
we define for a Borel subset
E\subset\Omega
the local capacity
{\tt c}(E):={\tt c}(E,\Omega)
as follows:

{\tt c}(E)=\inf\left\{\|v\|_{*}^{2}\;\rvert\;v\in\mathscr{K}(E)\right\},

where

\mathscr{K}(E)=\left\{v\in W^{*}(\Omega)\;\rvert\;\{v\leq-1\}^{o}\supset E\text{ and }v\leq 0\right\}.

Here
\{v\leq-1\}^{o}\supset E
means
v\leq-1
a.e in a neighborhood of
E
.

The functional capacity in the Sobolev spaces is very well-understood which is also an effective tool to study fine properties of its element, see e.g.
[
EG92
, Chapters 4.7-4.8]
. In contrast, the use of
{\tt c}(\cdot)
for the complex Sobolev space is not effective so far.
Our goal is to investigate systematically local properties of functions in
W^{*}(\Omega)
by using this capacity. This is a different perspective compared to
[
Vig07
]
and
[
DMV23
]
who used the relative capacity for psh functions due to Bedford and Taylor
[
BT82
]
. Namely, for a Borel set
E\subset\Omega
,

cap(E,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{E}(dd^{c}u)^{n}:u\in PSH(\Omega),\;-1\leq u\leq 0\right\}.

Our first main result explains the reason why
cap(\cdot,\Omega)
could be effectively used in previous works. Roughly speaking they are equivalent to each other.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a strictly pseudoconvex domain.

(a)

There exists a constant
A>0
such that for every Borel set
E\subset\Omega
,

{\tt c}(E)\leq A[cap(E,\Omega)]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

(b)

Assume
D\subset\subset\Omega
be a subdomain. There exists a constant
A^{\prime}
such that for every Borel set
E\subset D
,

\frac{1}{A^{\prime}}cap(E,\Omega)\leq{\tt c}(E).

Moreover, the above inequalities are sharp as far as the exponents are concerned.

The statements of the theorem consist of Lemmas
4.5
,
4.7
and Remarks
4.6
,
8.11
.
On compact Kähler manifolds both inequalities was obtained in
[
DKN22
, Proposition 5.1]
where
{\tt c}(E)
is defined by another equivalent norm with
W^{*}
-norm, but the optimality was unknown. A qualitative version of the first inequality on such manifolds was obtained early in
[
Vig07
]
and the second inequality was also proved there without showing that the exponents are sharp.

In the local setting the proofs are different. The key idea in the proof of the first inequality is making use of Cegrell’s inequality
[
Ce04
]
and the one for the second inequality comes from the comparison principle. On the other hand, the optimality of the exponents is extracted from the Alexander-Taylor type inequality which is proved in Lemma
8.10
. The latter inequality is of independent interest. One of its applications is the existence of an entire psh subextension for a given
f\in W^{*}(B(0,1))
in Theorem
9.1
. This psh subextension result is an important ingredient to derive the property that the set Lebesgue points of such functions are of capacity zero. This result is due to Vigny and Vu
[
VV24
]
where the bounded case was proved earlier in
[
Vig07
]
. We improve the statement of that result and its proof is also simplified in Theorem
9.3
.

An immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
and
[
BT82
]
is that a Borel set
P\subset\Omega
is pluripolar if and only if
{\tt c}(P)=0
. Provided the equivalence of capacities the results in
[
DMV23
]
can be restated in terms of
{\tt c}(\cdot)
where they are obtained previously for
cap(\cdot,\Omega)
. However, as emphasized above we would take another route by using advantages of the functional capacity
{\tt c}(\cdot)
(see e.g., Lemma
4.3
, Proposition
5.3
, Lemma
5.5
) and hence providing alternative proofs for the ones obtained in
[
DMV23
]
. In particular, using the ideas in
[
DMV23
]
and
[
Vig07
]
, we simplify the proof of quasi-continuous representative result proved earlier in
[
DMV23
, Theorem 2.10]
(see also
[
Vig07
, Theorem 22]
).

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
. There exists a quasi-continuous modification
\widetilde{f}
such that
\widetilde{f}=f
a.e. Moreover, if
g
is another quasi-continuous modification of
f
, then
g=\widetilde{f}
outside a pluripolar set.

The most technical part of the proof lies in delicate integral estimates. Along the way we refine the ones obtained in
[
DMV23
]
. It turns out in Theorem
9.3
that the precise representative, in the sense of Sobolev spaces
[
EG92
, Chapter 4]
,

(1.1)

f^{*}(x)\equiv\begin{cases}\lim_{r\to 0}\,\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\displaystyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\displaystyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\textstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\textstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{B(x,r)}f\;dy&\quad\text{if this limit exits,}\\
=0&\quad\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

is a quasi-continuous modification, where
dy
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

Thanks to the quasi-continuity result
we can define the integration of a function
g\in W^{*}(\Omega)
against Monge-Ampère measures associated with a bounded psh function (Definition
5.12
). This result is pushed much further in Section 9 where we show that
W^{*}(\Omega)
is naturally embedded into a Dirichlet space associated with a closed positive
(n-1,n-1)
current
\theta=[dd^{c}(\phi+\delta|z|^{2})]^{n-1}
where
\phi\in PSH\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\delta>0
are given.
Therefore, the integral
\int_{K}df\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\theta
for
f,g\in W^{*}(\Omega)
is well-defined on every compact set
K\subset\Omega
. This aspect has been considered recently by Do, Nguyen and Vu
[
DoNV25
]
.

The second main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The set function on Borel subsets
E\mapsto{\tt c}(E)={\tt c}(E,\Omega)
is a Choquet capacity.

This result is the local analogue of
[
Vig07
]
. On compact Kähler manifolds the proof of
[
Vig07
, Theorem 30]
employed the capacity notion in the Dirichlet spaces
[
Vig07
, Lemma 23]
and the characterization of pluripolar sets via a family of capacity associated with closed positive current
[
FO84
]
. Here we use a similar strategy. Notably, we are able to give a simpler proof of the characterization in Corollary
7.9
by using only pluripotential theory.

Another remarkable property of functions in the complex Sobolev space is the uniformly exponential integrability due to Dinh, Marinescu and Vu
[
DMV23
]
, which is very close to the one of psh functions. Namely,
there exist positive constants
\alpha
and
A
such that for every
f\in W^{*}(B(0,1))
with
\|f\|_{*}\leq 1
,

(1.2)

\int_{\bar{B}(0,\frac{1}{8})}e^{\alpha|f|^{2}}dx\leq A.

This result should be compared with the well-known exponential integrability of psh functions due to Hörmander
[
Ho07
, Proposition 4.2.9]
and Skoda
[
Sk72
]
. In the proof of
[
DMV23
, Theorem 1.2]
the authors used the induction argument in dimension and slicing theory for positive currents. We provide a new and simpler one in Proposition
8.4
by reducing the inequality to one dimensional case. In this way, it is enough to work with smooth functions after taking the standard convolution with smooth kernels. The constants can be explicitly computed though they are suboptimal. Additionally, we point out in Remark
8.8
that the exponent 2 of
|f|
in (
1.2
) is the optimal for all
n\geq 1
, which was only known for
n=1
in
[
DMV23
]
.

The third main result fully characterizes the inequality (
1.2
) by the volume-capacity inequality of sublevel sets.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
K\subset\subset\Omega
be a compact subset. The following statements hold and they are equivalent to each other.

(a)

There exist constants
A_{1}>0
and
\alpha>0
depending only on
K,\Omega
such that for every Borel set
E\subset K

V_{2n}(E)\leq A_{1}e^{-\alpha/{\tt c}(E)}.

(b)

There exist uniform constants
A_{1}>0
and
\alpha>0
depending only on
K,\Omega
such that for every
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and
f\leq 0
,

V_{2n}(\{f<-1\}\cap K)\leq A_{1}e^{\frac{-\alpha}{\|f\|_{*}^{2}}}.

(c)

There exist uniform constants
A_{1}>0
and
\alpha>0
depending only on
K,\Omega
such that for every
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and
f\leq 0
whose norm
\|f\|_{*}\leq\kappa
,

V_{2n}(\{f<-s\}\cap K)\leq A_{1}e^{-\alpha s^{2}/\kappa^{2}},\quad\forall s>0.

(d)

There exist uniform constants
A_{1}>0
and
\alpha>0
depending only on
K,\Omega
such that for every
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and
f\leq 0
with
\|f\|_{*}\leq 1
,

V_{2n}(\{f<-s\}\cap K)\leq A_{1}e^{-\alpha s^{2}},\quad\forall s>0.

The characterization is partially inspired by the work of Åhag, Cegrell, Kołodziej, Pham and Zeriahi
[
ACKPZ
]
. Lastly, we point out in Remark
8.9
that the inequalities holds for a very large family of Monge-Ampère measures associated with Hölder continuous psh functions.

2.
Definitions and elementary properties

In this section we set out the notations and study basic functional properties.

2.1.
Complex Sobolev spaces

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and

\omega=dd^{c}|z|^{2}

is the standard Kähler form in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
let
\Gamma_{f}
denote the set of all positive closed
(1,1)
-currents
T
satisfying

(2.1)

df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T\quad\text{weakly in }\Omega.

(This set
\Gamma_{f}
could be empty.)
The total mass of a positive
(1,1)
-current
T
on
\Omega
is given by

\|T\|_{\Omega}:=\int_{\Omega}T\wedge\omega^{n-1}.

Recall that we define the subspace

(2.2)

W^{*}(\Omega)=\left\{f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}):\text{there exists $T\in\Gamma_{f}$ with }\|T\|_{\Omega}<+\infty\right\}

and the
W^{*}
-norm for
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
is given by

(2.3)

\|f\|_{*}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\inf_{T\in\Gamma_{f}}\|T\|_{\Omega}.

At this point it is a quantity associated with
f
. After studying its basic properties we shall prove that it is indeed a norm in Theorem
2.15
.

By the weak compactness of closed positive
(1,1)
-currents with finite mass in
\Omega
, there is a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T_{f}
such that

(2.4)

df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T_{f}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\inf_{T\in\Gamma_{f}}\|T\|_{\Omega}=\|T_{f}\|_{\Omega}.

Definition 2.1
.

A closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
satisfying (
2.4
) is called a minimum current of
f
or the current realizing the
W^{*}
-norm of
f
.

The Sobolev space with zero boundary value is denoted by
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
and we also consider

W_{0}^{*}(\Omega)=W^{*}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

Note from
[
GT
, Lemma 7.6]
that if
f,g\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, then for a.e. points

(2.5)

\nabla\max\{f,g\}=\begin{cases}\nabla f\quad&\mbox{on }\{f>g\},\\
\nabla g\quad&\mbox{ on }\{f\leq g\},\end{cases}

where
\nabla f
denotes the gradient of
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.
In the special case,
g=0
, we write

f^{+}=\max\{f,0\}\quad\text{and}\quad f^{-}=-\min\{f,0\}.

A basic inequality is that for every
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
and
f,g\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
,

d(sf+tg)\wedge d^{c}(sf+tg)\geq 0

as a positive
(1,1)
-form whose coefficients belong to
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Equivalently, we have

(2.6)

s^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f+t^{2}dg\wedge d^{c}g\geq st(df\wedge d^{c}g+dg\wedge d^{c}f).

More generally, if
u,v\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
, then by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for every strongly positive test
(n-1,n-1)
-form of
\Phi
,

\displaystyle 2\int uv\;df\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\Phi

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\int u^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\Phi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int v^{2}dg\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\Phi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int u^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\Phi+\int v^{2}dg\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\Phi.

Therefore,

(2.7)

2uv\;df\wedge d^{c}g\leq u^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f+v^{2}dg\wedge d^{c}g

in the weak sense of currents.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f,g\in W^{*}(\Omega)
. Let
T
and
S
be positive
(1,1)
-currents satisfying
df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T
and
dg\wedge d^{c}g\leq S
. Then,

\displaystyle d\max\{f,g\}\wedge d^{c}\max\{f,g\}\leq T+S.

\displaystyle d\min\{f,g\}\wedge d^{c}\min\{f,g\}\leq T+S.

Consequently,

d|f|\wedge d^{c}|f|\leq 2T.

Proof.

These are direct consequences of (
2.5
) and positivity of currents
S
and
T
.
∎

This lemma combined with the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{*}
yields

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f,g\in W^{*}(\Omega)
. Then,
\max\{f,g\}
and
\min\{f,g\}
belong to
W^{*}(\Omega)
and

\|\max\{f,g\}\|_{*}\leq\|f\|_{*}+\|g\|_{*},\quad\|\min\{f,g\}\|_{*}\leq\|f\|_{*}+\|g\|_{*}.

Furthermore, for any constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.8)

\|\max\{f,c\}\|_{*}\leq\|f\|_{*},\quad\|\min\{f,c\}\|_{*}\leq\|f\|_{*}

An immediate consequence which is very useful is as follows.

Corollary 2.4
.

If
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
, then
f^{+}=\max\{f,0\}
and
f^{-}=-\min\{f,0\}
belong to
W^{*}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
\|f^{+}\|_{*}\leq\|f\|_{*}
,
\|f^{-}\|_{*}\leq\|f\|_{*}
and
\||f|\|_{*}\leq 2\|f\|_{*}
.

2.2.
Basic examples of functions

We provide several examples of functions in the new space.

Example 2.5
.

Smooth functions with compact supports functions or global Lipschitz functions in
\Omega
belong to
W^{*}(\Omega)
.

Example 2.6
.

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

(a)

If
u,v\in PSH(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, then
u\pm v\in W^{*}(\Omega)
.

(b)

Let
u\in PSH(\Omega)\cap L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and
u\leq-1
. Then,
-\log(-u)\in PSH(\Omega)
. A direct computation shows that
-\log(-u)\in W^{*}(\Omega^{\prime})
for every
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega.
In general they are unbounded.

(c)

Let
\Omega=B(0,1/2)
. Then,
u(z_{1},...,z_{n})=-(-\log|z_{1}|)^{a}\in W^{*}(\Omega)
if and only if
a<1/2.
This function is also plurisubharmonic.

The next result is due to Vigny
[
Vig07
]
and Dinh, Marinescu and Vu
[
DMV23
, Lemma 2.8]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Assume
df\wedge d^{c}f\leq dd^{c}u
for some negative function
u\in PSH(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)
. Denote by
N:=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}>0
and we set
u_{N}:=\max\{u,-N\}+N
. Then,

d(u_{N}f)\wedge d^{c}(u_{N}f)\leq N^{2}dd^{c}(u_{N}^{2}+u_{N+1}).

Consequently,
u_{N}f\in W^{*}(\Omega^{\prime})
for every
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.7
) that

d(u_{N}f)\wedge d^{c}(u_{N}f)\leq 2u_{N}^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f+2f^{2}du_{N}\wedge d^{c}u_{N}

as positive
(1,1)
-currents whose coefficients are in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. It is easy to see that
du_{N}\wedge d^{c}u_{N}\leq dd^{c}u_{N}^{2}
in the weak sense as
0\leq u_{N}\leq N
and it is psh. The continuity of
u
implies

dd^{c}\max\{u,-N-1\}=dd^{c}u

on the open set
\{u>-N-1\}\supset\{u\geq-N\}
. Therefore,
u_{N}^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\leq N^{2}dd^{c}u_{N+1}
. The proof follows easily by combining these facts.
∎

The continuity assumption of
u
will not be needed in Lemma
2.7
if we invoke an approximation argument below (Remark
2.10
).

2.3.
Approximation

Let us fix the standard smoothing kernel as follows. Define

\eta(z)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
by

\eta(z)=\begin{cases}c_{0}e^{\frac{1}{|z|^{2}-1}}&\quad\text{if }|z|<1,\\
0&\quad\text{if }|z|\geq 1,\end{cases}

where the constant
c_{0}
is chosen such that
\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\eta(x)dx=1
. For each
\varepsilon>0
, set

(2.9)

\eta_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}\eta\left(\frac{|z|}{\varepsilon}\right).

This family is used repeatedly throughout the paper. We also write

(2.10)

\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\{z\in\Omega:{\rm dist}(z,\partial\Omega)>\varepsilon\}.

The following simple observation is very useful.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and
T=\sum T_{j\bar{k}}\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{\pi}dz_{j}\wedge d\bar{z}_{k}
a
(1,1)
-current of order zero. Then,

df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T

weakly
if and only if for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

(2.11)

\sum_{j,k}\partial_{j}f(z)\partial_{\bar{k}}f(z)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}\leq\sum_{j,k}T_{j\bar{k}}(z)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}

in the sense of distributions of zero order, where
\partial_{j}f:=\partial f/\partial z_{j}
and
\partial_{\bar{k}}f=\partial f/\partial\bar{z}_{k}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T
holds if and only if

\sum(T_{j\bar{k}}-\partial_{j}f\;\partial_{\bar{k}}f)\sqrt{-1}dz^{j}\wedge d\bar{z}^{k}=\sum S_{j\bar{k}}\sqrt{-1}dz^{j}\wedge d\bar{z}^{k}=:S\geq 0

as a positive
(1,1)
-current, where
S_{j\bar{k}}=T_{j\bar{k}}-\partial_{j}f\partial_{\bar{k}}f
. Since
S*\eta_{\varepsilon}
converges weakly to
S
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the above inequality is equivalent to
S*\eta_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
as (1,1)-forms. By a characterization of positive
(1,1)
-forms, this holds if and only if

\sum(S_{j\bar{k}}*\eta_{\varepsilon})\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}=\sum(S_{j\bar{k}}\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k})*\eta_{\varepsilon}\geq 0\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Therefore,
S\geq 0
if and only if

\sum S_{j\bar{k}}\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}\geq 0

weakly as distributions of order zero for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The proof is completed.
∎

It leads to a characterization

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and
T
is a positive
(1,1)
-current. Then,

df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T

in the weak sense of currents if and only if for every
\varepsilon>0
,

d(f*\eta_{\varepsilon})\wedge d^{c}(f*\eta_{\varepsilon})\leq T*\eta_{\varepsilon}

in the weak sense of currents (or point-wise) in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

The sufficient condition is easy because if the second inequality holds for every
\varepsilon>0
, then letting
\varepsilon\to 0
one gets the first inequality. It remains to prove
the necessary condition. Assume
df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T
weakly where
T=\sum T_{j\bar{k}}\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{\pi}dz_{j}\wedge d\bar{z}_{k}
.
Since

f*\eta_{\varepsilon}(z)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2n}}\int f(z-x)\eta\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)dx=\int f(z-x)\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)dx,

we compute

\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}(f*\eta_{\varepsilon}(z))=\int(\partial_{j}f)(z-x)\eta_{\epsilon}(x)dx.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\sum\partial_{j}f(z-x)\;\partial_{\bar{k}}f(z-y)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum\partial_{j}f(z-x)\partial_{\bar{k}}f(z-x)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\partial_{j}f(z-y)\partial_{\bar{k}}f(z-y)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}.

Since
\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq 0
, it follows that

\displaystyle 2\iint\sum\partial_{j}f(z-x)\partial_{\bar{k}}f(z-y)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)\eta_{\epsilon}(y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\iint\sum\partial_{j}f(z-x)\partial_{\bar{k}}f(z-x)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)\eta_{\epsilon}(y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle+\iint\sum\partial_{j}f(z-y)\partial_{\bar{k}}f(z-y)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)\eta_{\epsilon}(y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle=2\int\sum\partial_{j}f(z-x)\partial_{\bar{k}}f(z-x)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle:=2I,

where where we used the fact that
\int\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)dx=1
for every
\varepsilon>0
.
The inequality (
2.11
) in Lemma
2.8
gives

\displaystyle I\leq\int\sum T_{j\bar{k}}(z-x)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=\sum T_{j\bar{k}}*\eta_{\varepsilon}(z)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k},

Therefore,

\sum\partial_{j}(f*\eta_{\varepsilon})(z)\;\partial_{\bar{k}}(f*\eta_{\varepsilon})(z)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}\leq\sum(T_{j\bar{k}}*\eta_{\varepsilon})(z)\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}

in the distributional (or point-wise) sense.
The left hand side is rewritten as

\sum[(\partial f/\partial z_{j})*\eta_{\varepsilon}]\;[(\partial f/\partial\bar{z}_{k})*\eta_{\varepsilon}]\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}.

In other words,

df_{\varepsilon}\wedge d^{c}f_{\varepsilon}\leq T_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}

by the characterization of positive
(1,1)
-forms, see e.g., Demailly’s book
[
De12
, Chapter 3, Corollary 1.7]
.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

Lemma
2.7
is valid for a general bounded psh function
u
, i.e., it needs not be continuous. In fact, let
f
and
u
are functions in the lemma, define
f_{\varepsilon}=f*\eta_{\varepsilon}
and
u_{\varepsilon}=u*\eta_{\varepsilon}
. They are smooth and satisfy
df_{\varepsilon}\wedge d^{c}f_{\varepsilon}\leq dd^{c}u_{\varepsilon}
by Proposition
2.9
. So,

(2.12)

d(u_{N,\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon})\wedge d^{c}(u_{N,\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon})\leq N^{2}(dd^{c}u_{N,\varepsilon}^{2}+u_{N+1,\varepsilon}),

where
u_{N,\varepsilon}=\max\{u_{\varepsilon},-N\}+N
. Since
u_{N,\varepsilon}
are bounded psh functions decreasing to
u_{N}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, it follows that the right hand side of (
2.12
) converges weakly to
N^{2}dd^{c}(u_{N}^{2}+u_{N+1})
. On the other hand, the above inequality implies that
\{v_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
has uniformly bounded norm in
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\prime})
for a given open subset
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
. So, it has a subsequence converges weakly to
w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\prime})
. However,
u_{N,\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}\to u_{N}f
in
L^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})
. Hence,
w=u_{N}f
in
L^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})
by the uniqueness of weak limit in
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\prime})
. We conclude that the left hand side in (
2.12
) converges weakly in
W^{1,2}(\Omega^{\prime})
to
d(u_{N}f)\wedge d^{c}(u_{N}f)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
. Then,

f_{\varepsilon}=f*\eta_{\varepsilon}\in W^{*}\cap C^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
and

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{*}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}=\|f\|_{W^{*}(\Omega)}.

Similarly, for every
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{*}(\Omega^{\prime})}=\|f\|_{W^{*}(\Omega^{\prime})}
. Moreover, for
0<\varepsilon<{\rm dist}(\partial\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
,

\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{*}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\|f\|_{W^{*}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
T
be a closed positive
(1,1)
-current realizing
W^{*}
-norm of
f
(Definition
2.1
). Since

\int_{\Omega}T\wedge\omega^{n-1}=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j\bar{j}}<+\infty,

and each coefficient
T_{j\bar{j}}
is a positive Radon measure, so we may extend them to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
as zero outside
\Omega
by setting
\widetilde{T}:={\bf 1}_{\Omega}\cdot T
(it is still a positive current, but it is no longer closed). Hence,
(\widetilde{T})_{\varepsilon}\wedge\omega^{n-1}
is defined in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
where
(\widetilde{T})_{\varepsilon}=(\widetilde{T})*\eta_{\varepsilon}
. Moreover, we have
(\widetilde{T})_{\varepsilon}=T*\eta_{\varepsilon}=:T_{\varepsilon}
on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.

By Proposition
2.9
we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{*}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|f_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}T\wedge\omega^{n-1},

where in the second inequality we used the Young’s and Minkowski’s inequalities for the convolutions with
\eta_{\varepsilon}
of functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and of positive Radon measures in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(see, e.g.,
[
Fd99
, Propositions 8.9, 8.49]
). It follows that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{*}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq\|f\|_{*}.

On the other hand, for each
\varepsilon>0
let
S_{\varepsilon}
be a closed positive
(1,1)
-current which realize the norms of
f_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. They have uniformly bounded mass on compact subsets. Hence,
there exists a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
S
on
\Omega
such that

S_{\varepsilon}\to S

weakly in subsequence (we still denote by the same sequence for simplicity). Clearly,
df\wedge d^{c}f\leq S
weakly in
\Omega
as
f_{\varepsilon}\to f
in
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.
Next, we observe that
\|S\wedge\omega^{n-1}\|_{\Omega}<+\infty
.
In fact, since
S_{\varepsilon}\to S
weakly, it follow that for an open set
D\subset\subset\Omega
,

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}S_{\varepsilon}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{D}S_{\varepsilon}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\geq\int_{D}S\wedge\omega^{n-1}.

Letting
D\uparrow\Omega
, we obtain

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}S_{\varepsilon}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\geq\int_{\Omega}S\wedge\omega^{n-1}.

Therefore,

\|f\|_{W^{*}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}S\wedge\omega^{n-1}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{*}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

In conclusion we have
\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{*}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\geq\|f\|_{*}\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{*}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}
.

Next, the second identity is proved similarly to the first one but it is easier without change of the domain of integration. Finally,
\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{*}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{*}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}
and (
2.13
) implies the last inequality in the proposition.
∎

2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip This inequality obviously holds when
(f)_{x,r}=0.
Now letting

we will get the conclusion as the limit exists almost everywhere in
\Omega
by (

).
∎

2.4.
Compactness and completeness

We first show that the complex Sobolev space enjoys good compactness properties under a uniform bound of
W^{*}
-norm. Then, using this we prove the basic functional properties of the space.

Below we use several times the weak convergence in
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
. A useful criterion for such a convergence is as follows.
By the Riesz representation theorem, a sequence
f_{j}\to f
weakly in
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
if and only if
f_{j}\to f
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\nabla f_{j}\to\nabla f
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2n})
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset W^{*}(\Omega)
. Assume that
\|f_{j}\|_{*}\leq A
for every
j\geq 1
. If
f_{j}\to f
weakly in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, then
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
with
\|f\|_{*}\leq A
.

Proof.

Let
T_{j}
be a minimum current of
f_{j}
,
j\geq 1
. Since
\|T_{j}\|_{\Omega}\leq A
, there exists a subsequence
T_{j_{k}}
converging weakly to a closed positive (1,1)-current
T
and
\|T\|_{\Omega}\leq\liminf\|T_{j_{k}}\|_{\Omega}
. We still write
f_{j},T_{j}
instead of
f_{j_{k}},T_{j_{k}}
for simplicity. Let
\phi
be a positive test form in
\Omega
. Then,

\left<df_{j}\wedge d^{c}f_{j},\phi\right>\leq T_{j}(\phi).

Note also that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\left<d(f-f_{j})\wedge d^{c}(f-f_{j}),\phi\right>

\displaystyle=\left<df\wedge d^{c}f,\phi\right>+\left<df_{j}\wedge d^{c}f_{j},\phi\right>-2\left<df\wedge d^{c}f_{j},\phi\right>

\displaystyle\leq\left<df\wedge d^{c}f,\phi\right>+T_{j}(\phi)-2\left<df\wedge d^{c}f_{j},\phi\right>.

Letting
j\to\infty
, we get from weak convergence in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and weak convergence of currents that
\left<df\wedge d^{c}f,\phi\right>\leq T(\phi)
. So,

f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
. Moreover, if we write
\left<f,g\right>_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}fgdx
for the inner product in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Omega)
, then

0\leq\left<f-f_{j},f-f_{j}\right>_{\Omega}=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-2\left<f,f_{j}\right>_{\Omega}.

The semi-continuity of norm in weak convergence of
f_{j}\to f
in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
(see e.g.,
[
LL
, Theorem 2.11]
) implies

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|f_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Thus,
\|f\|_{*}\leq A
.
∎

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset W^{*}(\Omega)
be such that
\|f_{j}\|_{*}\leq A
for every
j\geq 1
. There exists a subsequence
\{f_{j_{k}}\}
converging weakly in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
to
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and
\|f\|_{*}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|f_{j}\|_{*}
.

Proof.

It follows immediately from
\|f\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{*}
for every
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and Lemma
2.13
.
∎

The following basic functional result is due to Vigny
[
Vig07
, Proposition 1]
.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

(a)

\|\cdot\|_{*}
is a norm in
W^{*}(\Omega)
and
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\cdot\|_{*}
.

(b)

W^{*}(\Omega)
and
W^{*}_{0}(\Omega)
are Banach spaces with this norm.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and let
T,S
be the minimum currents of
f,g
, respectively. Choosing
s,t>0
with
st=1
in (
2.6
) we get

\displaystyle d(f+g)\wedge d^{c}(f+g)

\displaystyle=df\wedge d^{c}f+dg\wedge d^{c}g+[df\wedge d^{c}g+dg\wedge d^{c}f]

\displaystyle\leq(1+s^{2})df\wedge d^{c}f+(1+t^{2})dg\wedge d^{c}g

\displaystyle\leq(1+s^{2})T+(1+t^{2})S.

It follows that

(2.17)

\|f+g\|_{*}^{2}\leq\min_{s>0}\left\{(1+s^{2})\|f\|_{*}^{2}+(1+1/s^{2})\|g\|_{*}^{2}\right\}.

We now verify the triangle inequality. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
g\neq 0
. Moreover, we may assume that
\|g\|_{*}=1
by dividing by a constant. We need to show

\|f+g\|_{*}\leq\|f\|_{*}+1.

If
f=0
, then the inequality holds. Otherwise,
applying (
2.17
) for
s^{2}=1/\|f\|_{*}>0
,

\displaystyle\|f+g\|_{*}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+s^{2})\|f\|_{*}^{2}+1+1/s^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+\|f\|_{*}^{2})+s^{2}\|f\|_{*}^{2}+1/s^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+\|f\|_{*})^{2}.

This proves the triangle inequality.

Next, we show that it is a complete space with this norm. In fact, let
\{f_{j}\}
be a Cauchy sequence with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{*}.
Since the
W^{1,2}
-norm is dominated by the
W^{*}
-norm, the sequence is Cauchy in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. By Lemma
2.13
it converges to
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
with respect to
W^{1,2}
-norm. Let
S_{jk}
be the minimum current for
f_{j}-f_{k}
. We have

d(f_{j}-f_{k})\wedge d^{c}(f_{j}-f_{k})\leq S_{jk}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
k_{0}
such that for
j,k\geq k_{0}
,

\|S_{jk}\|_{\Omega}\leq\|f_{j}-f_{k}\|_{*}\leq\varepsilon.

By weak compactness for (1,1)-currents, for each fixed
j
there exists a subsequence
\{S_{jk_{s}}\}_{k_{s}\geq 1}
converging weakly to a closed positive (1,1)-current
\tau_{j}
and
\|\tau_{j}\|_{\Omega}\leq\varepsilon
.
We have for a positive test form
\phi
,

d(f_{j}-f_{k_{s}})\wedge d^{c}(f_{j}-f_{k_{s}})(\phi)\leq S_{jk_{s}}(\phi).

Letting
k_{s}\to\infty
, the left hand side converges to
d(f_{j}-f)\wedge d^{c}(f_{j}-f)(\phi)
by the Lebesgue domination convergence theorem and the right hand side converges to
\tau_{j}(\phi)
. Therefore,

\|f_{j}-f\|_{*}\leq 2\varepsilon
for
j\geq k_{0}
. In other words,
f_{j}
converges to
f
with respect to the
W^{*}
-norm.
∎

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
f,g\in W^{*}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

(a)

fg\in W^{*}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

(b)

If in addition
f\in W^{*}_{0}(\Omega)
, then
fg\in W^{*}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The statements (a) and (b) for the Sobolev spaces
W^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
are classical, see e.g.,
[
Bz11
, Proposition 9.4, 9.18]
. On the other hand, the basic inequality (
2.7
) implies

\displaystyle d(fg)\wedge d^{c}(fg)

\displaystyle\leq 2f^{2}dg\wedge d^{c}g+2g^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f

\displaystyle\leq 2f^{2}T_{f}+2g^{2}T_{g},

where
T_{f},T_{g}
are the minimum currents of
f,g
, respectively.
Since
f,g
are bounded, the last current is dominated by a closed positive current
A(T_{f}+T_{g})\in\Gamma_{fg}
.
∎

Recall that a function
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
belongs to
{\rm BMO}(\Omega)
(bounded mean oscillation) if there exists a constant
A>0
such that

(2.18)

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f-f_{B}|dx\leq A

holds for all balls
B\subset\Omega
, where as in (
2.15
),

f_{B}=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}fdx.

Moreover, a function
f\in{\rm BMO}(\Omega)
belongs to
{\rm VMO}(\Omega)
(vanishing mean oscillation) if

(2.19)

\lim_{|B|\to 0}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f-f_{B}|dx=0

holds for every balls
B\subset\Omega
.

A well-known fact is that if a psh function
u
belongs to
{\rm VMO}(\Omega)
, then its Lelong number vanishes everywhere. The converse also holds true thanks to a recent work by Biard and Wu
[
BW24
]
.

Example 2.17
.

Let
n\geq 2
and assume
0\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For
1\leq\ell\leq n
, the function

u(z_{1},...,z_{n})=\frac{|z_{\ell}|^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{2}}

belongs to
W^{*}(\Omega)\cap{\rm BMO}(\Omega)
but not to
{\rm VMO}(\Omega)
.
In fact, the sequence

u_{\varepsilon}=\frac{|z_{\ell}|^{2}}{\varepsilon+\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{2}}

are smooth functions in
W^{*}(\Omega)
whose
W^{*}
-norms are uniformly bounded. Also
u_{\varepsilon}\uparrow u
in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Hence,
u\in W^{*}(\Omega)
by Lemma
2.13
. Using the homogeneity of
u
we can easily see that
u\in{\rm BMO}(\Omega)
but not in
{\rm VMO}(\Omega)
.

We have the following BMO estimate for
W^{*}(\Omega)
similar to psh functions.

Lemma 2.18
.

Every function in
W^{*}(\Omega)
belongs to
{\rm BMO}(\Omega^{\prime})
, where
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
h\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and assume
dh\wedge d^{c}h\leq T
, where

is a closed positive current with
\|T\|_{\Omega}<+\infty
. Let

It follows from the Poincaré inequality

in balls for

that

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,r)}|h-(h)_{x,r}|^{2}dy

\displaystyle\leq Ar^{2}\int_{B(x,r)}|\nabla h|^{2}dy

\displaystyle\leq Ar^{2}\int_{B(x,r)}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega^{n-1},

(2.20)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{r\to 0}\,\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\displaystyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\displaystyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\textstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\textstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{B(x,r)}|h-(h)_{x,r}|dy.

Since
h\in W^{*}(\Omega)
, there exists a positive closed
(1,1)
-current such that
dh\wedge d^{c}h\leq T
. The inequality (
2.20
) applied for
h
combined with the inequality above gives

\lim_{r\to 0}\,\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\displaystyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\displaystyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\textstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\textstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{B(x,r)}|f-(f)_{x,r}|dy\leq A^{\prime}\sqrt{\nu(T,x)},

where
\nu(T,x)
is the Lelong number of
T
at the point
x
. This means that if
C^{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{*}(\Omega)
is dense in
W^{*}(\Omega)
with respect to
W^{*}
-norm, then we can find such a closed positive current with arbitrary small mass
\|T\|_{B(x,r)}
. In particular,

\limsup_{r\to 0}\,\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\displaystyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\displaystyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\textstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\textstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{B(x,r)}|f-(f)_{x,r}|dy=0,\quad x\in\Omega.

This means
f\in{\rm VMO}(\Omega)
which is impossible.
Thus,
W^{*}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)
cannot be dense in
W^{*}(\Omega)
with respect to
W^{*}
-norm.
∎

This leads to a consequence
[
Vig07
, Corollary 8]
which is similar to one of the space of
\delta
-psh functions studied by Cegrell and Wiklund
[
CW05
]
. We give the detail proof for the reader’s convenience.

Corollary 2.20
.

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Then,
W^{*}(\Omega)
is not reflexive.

Proof.

We follow closely the one in
[
Vig07
, Corollary 8]
. Suppose that the space is reflexive. Then, the closed unit ball in
W^{*}
-norm is weakly compact with respect to the dual topology. Let
0\leq u\leq 1
be a function in Example
2.17
and
0\leq\chi\leq 1
be a cut-off function in
\Omega
. Clearly,
\chi u\in W^{*}(\Omega)
. Define
u_{k}=u*\eta_{1/k}
. It follows from Proposition
2.11
that
f_{k}=\chi u_{k}\in W^{*}(\Omega)\cap C^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\|f_{k}\|_{*}\leq A
for a constant
A
independent of
k
.
By the weak compactness (Corollary
2.14
) there exists a subsequence
f_{j}\to g
weakly in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and
g\in W^{*}(\Omega)
. So,
g=f
by uniqueness of a weak limit in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Mazur’s lemma (see, e.g.,
[
Yo80
, Theorem 2, page 120]
) implies that
g
is a limit of convex sums of
\{f_{j}\}
in
W^{*}
-norm. This is impossible due to the previous proposition.
∎

2.5.
Bedford-Taylor capacity for plurisubharmonic functions

Recall that for a Borel set
E\subset\Omega
,

(2.21)

cap(E,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{E}(dd^{c}u)^{n}:u\in PSH(\Omega),\;-1\leq u\leq 0\right\}.

We know
cap(\cdot,\Omega)
is both inner and outer regular by a classical result in
[
BT82
]
. The capacity of a given Borel set is realizable by its relative extremal function. Namely, assume
\Omega
is a strictly pseudoconvex and
E\subset\Omega
a Borel set. Define

(2.22)

u_{E}(z)=\sup\left\{u(z):u\in PSH(\Omega),\;u\leq-1\text{ on }E,\;u\leq 0\right\}

and
u_{E}^{*}
is its upper semicontinuous regularization. Then,

(2.23)

cap(E,\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}u_{E}^{*})^{n}.

We refer the reader to
[
BT82
, Proposition 6.5]
for a general formula where the left hand side is the outer capacity applied for the sets which are not necessarily Borel.

We also need the comparison between total mass integrals of
a closed positive
(1,1)
-current. This is a consequence of the weak convergence of measures. We state it in the simplest form.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
0\leq k\leq n-1
. Let
T
be a closed positive
(1,1)
-current in an open subset
\Omega\subset\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Assume
\varphi,\psi\in PSH\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy
\varphi\leq\psi\leq 0
in
\Omega
and
\lim_{z\to\partial\Omega}\varphi(z)=0
. Then,

(2.24)

\int_{\Omega}T\wedge(dd^{c}\psi)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}\leq\int_{\Omega}T\wedge(dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}.

Proof.

In fact, for
\varepsilon>0
,
\max\{\varphi+\varepsilon,\psi\}=\varphi+\varepsilon
near
\partial\Omega
. Hence, by integration by parts,

\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge T\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}=\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}\max\{\varphi+\varepsilon,\psi\})^{k}\wedge T\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}.

Let
\varepsilon\to 0
and observe that the integrand on the right hand side converges weakly to
(dd^{c}\psi)^{k}\wedge T\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}
by Demailly
[
De89
, Theorem 1.7]
. Hence, the desired inequality follows.
∎

3.
Basic integral inequalities

Throughout this section we assume
\Omega\subset\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a strictly pseudoconvex domain. Let
\rho
be a strictly psh defining function of
\Omega
which is smooth in a neighborhood of
\bar{\Omega}
and satisfies

dd^{c}\rho\geq\omega\quad\text{on }\bar{\Omega}.

Let
-1\leq\phi\leq 0
be a psh function in
\Omega
. We define

\gamma:=(dd^{c}\phi)^{n-1}.

Let us also consider a non-negative smooth cut-off function

\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),\quad\chi\geq 0.

We have a list of results for the estimates of the
L^{2}
-norm of a smooth function with respect to the Monge-Ampère measure
(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}
and
d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\gamma
as follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
. Then,

\int g^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}\leq 10\int dg\wedge d^{c}g\wedge(dd^{c}\phi)^{n-1}.

Proof.

Since
(dd^{c}\phi*\eta_{\varepsilon})^{n}
converges weakly to
(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we may assume that
\phi
is smooth in
\Omega
. By integration by parts,

(3.1)

\frac{1}{2}\int g^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}=-\int gdg\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma.\\

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle-\int gdg\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int dg\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\gamma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int g^{2}d\phi\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int dg\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\gamma+\frac{1}{8}\int g^{2}d\phi\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma.

Another integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\int g^{2}d\phi\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\int g\phi dg\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma-\frac{1}{8}\int g^{2}\phi(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\left|\int g\phi dg\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma\right|+\frac{1}{8}\int g^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality once more for the first integral, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left|\int g\phi dg\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int\phi^{2}dg\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\gamma+\frac{1}{16}\int g^{2}d\phi\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int dg\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\gamma+\frac{1}{16}\int g^{2}d\phi\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma.

Combining the last two inequalities we arrive at

\frac{1}{16}\int g^{2}d\phi\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma\leq\frac{1}{4}\int dg\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\gamma+\frac{1}{8}\int g^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}.

Therefore,

-\int gdg\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma\leq\frac{5}{2}\int dg\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\gamma+\frac{1}{4}\int g^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}.

This and the identity (
3.1
) imply the desired inequality.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
0\leq\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. Then

\int f^{2}d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\gamma\leq 4\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\gamma+2A_{0}\int\chi f^{2}\omega\wedge\gamma,

where
A_{0}
is a positive constant such that
-A_{0}\omega\leq dd^{c}\chi\leq A_{0}\omega
.

Proof.

An integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\int f^{2}d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\gamma

\displaystyle=-2\int f\chi df\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\gamma-\int f^{2}\chi dd^{c}\chi\wedge\gamma

\displaystyle\leq 2\left|\int f\chi df\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\gamma\right|+A_{0}\int f^{2}\chi\omega\wedge\gamma,

where we used the fact
dd^{c}\chi\leq A_{0}\omega
.
Next, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies

\displaystyle 2\left|\int f\chi df\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\gamma\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\gamma+\frac{1}{2}\int f^{2}d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\gamma.

Combining the last two inequalities we arrive at

\frac{1}{2}\int f^{2}d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\gamma\leq 2\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\gamma+A_{0}\int f^{2}\chi\omega\wedge\gamma.

This is equivalent to our desired estimate.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
and let
0\leq\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
.

\int(\chi f)^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}\leq 100\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge(dd^{c}\phi)^{n-1}+40A_{0}\int\chi f^{2}\omega\wedge(dd^{c}\phi)^{n-1}

Proof.

Applying Lemma
3.1
for
g=\chi f
we get

\displaystyle\int(\chi f)^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq 10\int d(\chi f)\wedge d^{c}(\chi f)\wedge\gamma

\displaystyle\leq 20\int[\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f+f^{2}d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi]\wedge\gamma.

Now the desired conclusion follows by applying Lemma
3.2
for the second term in the last integral.
∎

In other words, in order to get the inequality in Lemma
3.3
we replace the integrand
(\chi f)^{2}dd^{c}\phi
by
100\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f+40A_{0}\chi f^{2}\omega
.
We can continue this process for the second integrals on the right hand side with
\chi:=(\chi_{1})^{2}
where
\chi_{1}
is again a cut-off function. After repeating this process
n
-times we obtain easily

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
0\leq\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. Then,

\displaystyle\int\chi^{2^{n}}f^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq 100\int df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}A_{k}\;\chi^{2^{k}}(dd^{c}\phi)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-1-k}\right)

\displaystyle+(40A_{0})^{n}\int\chi f^{2}\omega^{n}.

where
A_{k}=[40(1+A_{0})]^{n-1-k}
.

Note that the inequality of the corollary holds for
-1\leq\phi\leq 0
being a general bounded psh function and
f
is smooth. However, its right hand side involves only gradient of
f
and its
L^{2}
-norm, we can use a simple approximation argument to exchange the regularity assumptions on
\phi
and
f
as follows.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Assume further
\phi
is smooth in a neighborhood of
\mbox{supp }\chi
. Then,

\displaystyle\int\chi^{2^{n}}f^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq 100\int df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}A_{k}\;\chi^{2^{k}}(dd^{c}\phi)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-1-k}\right)

\displaystyle+(40A_{0})^{n}\int\chi f^{2}\omega^{n}.

where
A_{k}=[40(1+A_{0})]^{n-1-k}
.

We are ready to state the main application of the above estimates. This strengthens
[
DMV23
, Lemma 2.5]
which says roughly that continuous functions in
W^{*}(\Omega)
is locally uniformly
L^{2}
-integrable with respect to Monge-Ampère measures of bounded psh functions. More precisely,

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)
. Let
D\subset\subset\Omega
be a subdomain. Then, for every Borel set
K\subset D
,

\int_{K}f^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}\leq A\|f\|_{*}^{2},

where
A
depends only on
D,\Omega
.

Proof.

We take
0\leq\chi\leq 1
a smooth cut-off function such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
D
and
\mbox{supp }\chi\subset\subset\Omega
. It is enough to show that

J:=\int\chi^{2^{n}}f^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}\leq A\|f\|_{*}^{2}.

By weak convergence of
[dd^{c}(\phi*\eta_{\varepsilon})]^{n}
to
(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}
and the continuity of
f
, we may assume that
\phi
is smooth. Since
\phi
is bounded we can modify it outside the support of
\chi
by considering
\max\{\phi,A_{0}\rho\}
, where
A_{0}
depends only on
\rho
. So,
\phi\geq A_{0}\rho
and
\phi=A_{0}\rho
near
\partial\Omega
. Moreover, we assume further that
f
is smooth by the smoothing property in Proposition
2.11
.

Now, let
T
be a closed positive current such that
df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T
.
Corollary
3.4
implies that

J\leq A_{1}\int T\wedge\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(dd^{c}\phi)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-1-k}+A_{1}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Since
A_{0}\rho\leq\phi\leq 0
in
\Omega
, the mass comparison (Lemma
2.21
) implies that

\displaystyle\int T\wedge(dd^{c}\phi)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-1-k}

\displaystyle\leq\int T\wedge[dd^{c}(A_{0}\rho)]^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-1-k}

\displaystyle\leq A_{2}\int T\wedge\omega^{n-1}.

Therefore,
J\leq A_{1}A_{2}(\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|T\|_{\Omega})
. Taking infimum over all
T
in
\Gamma_{f}
we complete the proof.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

At the moment the continuity of
f
is needed in Proposition
3.6
so that the integral on the left hand side is well-defined. However, we will show in Theorem
5.10
below that
f
admits a quasi-continuous representative
\widetilde{f}\in W^{*}(\Omega)
with respect to the Bedford-Taylor capacity. So, the integral can be defined for a general
f
in Definition
5.12
.
Furthermore, in Section
7
, we will see that all estimates in this section hold for a general
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and a bounded psh function
\phi
.

Next result is the quantitative version of second part in
[
DMV23
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)
be such that
|f|\leq 1
. Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
be such that
0\leq\chi\leq 1
. Assume
df\wedge d^{c}f\leq dd^{c}u
for some
u\in PSH\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then,

\int\chi^{2^{n}}f^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}\leq A\left[\int\chi f^{2}(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}\right]^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}},

where
A
is a uniform constant depending on
\mbox{supp }\chi
,
\Omega
and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
. More precisely,

A=A_{0}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{a},\quad\text{where }a=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{i-1}{2^{i-1}}

and
A_{0}
is a constant depending only on
\chi,\omega
.

Proof.

Recall that
\gamma=(dd^{c}\phi)^{n-1}
and by replacing
\phi:=\phi+1
we have
0\leq\phi\leq 1
. By weak convergence of approximation sequences we may assume first
\phi
is smooth. Define
f_{\varepsilon}=f*\eta_{\varepsilon}
and
u_{\varepsilon}=u*\eta_{\varepsilon}
. Next, invoking Proposition
2.9
, we get

df_{\varepsilon}\wedge d^{c}f_{\varepsilon}\leq dd^{c}u_{\varepsilon}.

Since
f_{\varepsilon}\to f
uniformly on
\mbox{supp }\chi
, we have
f_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\omega+dd^{c}u_{\varepsilon})^{n}\to f^{2}(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}
weakly in
\Omega
. Therefore, it is enough to prove the inequality for
\phi,u
and
f
which are smooth. Its proof is done via using repeatedly the integration by parts and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. The first step is as follows:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int(\chi f)^{2}dd^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma

\displaystyle=-2\int\chi fd(\chi f)\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\int f^{2}d(\chi f)\wedge d^{c}(\chi f)\wedge\gamma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int\chi^{2}d\phi\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The second integral on the right hand side is bounded by

\int\chi^{2}d\phi\wedge d^{c}\phi\wedge\gamma\leq\int\chi^{2}dd^{c}\phi^{2}\wedge\gamma\leq A_{1}^{\prime},

where we used the Chern-Levine-Nirenberg (CLN) inequality in the last inequality for a constant
A_{1}^{\prime}>0
depending only on support of
\mbox{supp }\chi
. Next, for the other integrand of (
3.2
),

\displaystyle f^{2}d(\chi f)\wedge d^{c}(\chi f)

\displaystyle\leq 2f^{2}(\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f+f^{2}d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi)

\displaystyle\leq 2f^{2}[\chi^{2}dd^{c}u+2d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi],

where we used the fact
|f|\leq 1
for the second term in the bracket. Integrating both sides and
using Lemma
3.2
for the second term in the bracket we derive

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int f^{2}[\chi^{2}dd^{c}\phi]\wedge\gamma\leq A_{1}\left(\int f^{2}[\chi^{2}dd^{c}u+A_{0}\chi\omega]\wedge\gamma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
A_{1}=2\sqrt{18}A_{1}^{\prime}
.
Thus, we have been replacing one factor
\chi^{2}dd^{c}\phi
on the left hand side of (
3.3
) by the factor
\chi^{2}dd^{c}u+A_{0}\chi\omega
on the right hand side where a square root is taken on the integral and multiplied with the uniform constant
A_{1}
.

We can repeat this replacing
\chi^{2}dd^{c}\phi
process for each integral obtained on the right hand side. In order to keep the exponent of
\chi
at least one in the final integrals without the factor
dd^{c}\phi
, we need to start with
\chi^{2^{n}}
at the beginning. Clearly, the constants possibly increase at each step.

Namely, together with an elementary inequality
(x+y)^{k}\leq 2^{k}(x^{k}+y^{k})
for
x,y\geq 0
and a positive integer
k
, we can rewrite the sequence of inequalities as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\int f^{2}[\chi^{2^{n}}dd^{c}\phi]\wedge\gamma\right)^{2^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq A_{1}\left(\int f^{2}\chi^{2^{n}}dd^{c}u\wedge\gamma\right)^{2^{n-1}}

\displaystyle+A_{1}\left(\int f^{2}\chi^{2^{n-1}}\omega\wedge\gamma\right)^{2^{n-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq A_{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int f^{2}\chi^{2^{k}}(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}.

This is the desired estimate once we take into account the fact
0\leq\chi^{2^{k}}\leq\chi
for every integer
1\leq k\leq n
as
0\leq\chi\leq 1
.
Here the constants
A_{k}
comes from the use of the CLN inequality

\int\chi dd^{c}\phi^{2}\wedge(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{\ell}\wedge(dd^{c}\phi)^{n-k-\ell}\leq A_{k}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{k},

and they depend only on
\chi
and
\Omega
.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
v,w\in PSH\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Let
0\leq f\in W^{*}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)
. Then,

\displaystyle\int\chi^{2}f(\omega+dd^{c}w)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int\chi^{2}f(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}

\displaystyle+\left(\int\chi d(v-w)\wedge d^{c}(v-w)\wedge\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(I^{\frac{1}{2}}+J^{\frac{1}{2}}),

where

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{k}\wedge(\omega+dd^{c}w)^{n-1-k},

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int\chi f^{2}d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\tau,\quad J=\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\tau.

Proof.

We write
(\omega+dd^{c}w)^{n}-(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}=dd^{c}(w-v)\wedge\tau
and
denote
h=w-v
. By integration by parts

\int\chi^{2}fdd^{c}h\wedge\tau=-\int[2\chi fd\chi+\chi^{2}df]\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\tau.

We will use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for each integral on the right hand side. For the first one we get

\displaystyle\left(\int\chi fd\chi\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\tau\right)^{2}\leq\int\chi f^{2}d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\tau\int\chi dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\tau.

The second integral on the right hand side is bounded by

\displaystyle\left(\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\tau\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\tau\int\chi^{2}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\tau

Combining these inequalities and the fact that
0\leq\chi\leq 1
we get the proof of the lemma.
∎

Applying the localization principle we have

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
v,w,f
and
\tau
be as in Lemma
3.9
. Assume that
w\geq v
in a neighborhood
D\subset\subset\Omega
of
\mbox{supp }\chi
. Then,

\displaystyle\left|\int\chi^{2}f(\omega+dd^{c}w)^{n}-\int\chi^{2}f(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq A\|f\|_{*}\left(\int_{D}(w-v)(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
A
is a uniform constant depending on
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\mbox{supp }\chi
and
D
.

Proof.

By subtracting the same constant from
v,w
we may assume that
v\leq w\leq 0
. The integrals on the left hand side do not change if we modify
v,w
outside
\mbox{supp }\chi
. Therefore, replacing
v,w
by
\max\{v,A_{0}\rho\}
and
\max\{w,A_{0}\rho\}
we may assume that they are equal
A_{0}\rho
outside
\bar{D}
, in particular, they are equal on a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
. Now, applying Lemma
3.9
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int\chi^{2}f(\omega+dd^{c}w)^{n}-\int\chi^{2}f(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int\chi d(v-w)\wedge d^{c}(v-w)\wedge\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(I^{\frac{1}{2}}+J^{\frac{1}{2}}).

By integration by parts and
w\geq v
in
\Omega
we have

\displaystyle\int d(v-w)\wedge d^{c}(v-w)\wedge\tau

\displaystyle=\int(w-v)(\omega_{v}^{n}-\omega_{w}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\int(w-v)\omega_{v}^{n}.

It remains to estimate
I
and
J
which is very similar to the proof of Proposition
3.6
. In fact, let
M=2(2\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}})
and define

\phi:=\frac{v+w+\rho}{M}.

We have
\tau\leq M^{n-1}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n-1}
.
If
T\in\Gamma_{f}
, then

J\leq\int T\wedge\tau\leq M^{n-1}\int T\wedge(dd^{c}\phi)^{n-1}.

As
A_{0}\rho\leq\phi\leq 0
, the mass comparison (Lemma
2.21
) implies

\int T\wedge(dd^{c}\phi)^{n-1}\leq\int T\wedge(dd^{c}A_{0}\rho)^{n-1}\leq A_{1}\|T\|_{\Omega}.

Taking infimum over
T\in\Gamma_{f}
we get

J\leq M^{n-1}A_{1}\|f\|_{*}^{2}.

To estimate
I
we observe that
d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\leq A_{0}dd^{c}\rho
and thus

d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\tau\leq A_{0}dd^{c}\rho\wedge\tau\leq A_{0}M^{n}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}.

Thus, by Proposition
3.6
,

I\leq A_{2}\int\chi f^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}\leq A_{3}\|f\|_{*}^{2}.

The proof is completed.
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
v\in PSH(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
v\leq 0
. Assume
\mbox{supp }\chi\subset\subset D\subset\subset\Omega
and denote
v_{j}=v*\eta_{j}
. Then, for
0\leq f\in W^{*}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)
,

\left|\int\chi^{2}f(\omega+dd^{c}v_{j})^{n}-\int\chi^{2}f(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}\right|\leq A\|f\|_{*}\left[\int_{D}(v_{j}-v)(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
A
is a uniform constant depending on
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}},\mbox{supp }\chi
and
D
.

Proof.

It is a consequence of Corollary
3.10
for
w=v_{j}
.
∎

4.
A functional capacity

We start with basic definitions and properties of capacity. Let
\Omega\subset\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open subset. Let
E\subset\Omega
be a Borel set. Define

(4.1)

\mathscr{K}(E)=\left\{v\in W^{*}(\Omega)\;\rvert\;\{v\leq-1\}^{o}\supset E\text{ and }v\leq 0\right\}.

If
E=G
is an open set, then its interior
E^{o}=E
and

\mathscr{K}(G)=\left\{v\in W^{*}(\Omega)\;\rvert\;v\leq-1\text{ in $G$ and }v\leq 0\right\}.

We define the capacity by

(4.2)

{\tt c}(E)=\inf\left\{\|v\|_{*}^{2}\;\rvert\;v\in\mathscr{K}(E)\right\}.

In the definition of capacity both
\mathscr{K}(E)
and
{\tt c}(\cdot)
depends on
\Omega
. However, in most cases the domain
\Omega
is fixed, then for simplicity we write

{\tt c}(E):={\tt c}(E,\Omega).

Note that if
v\in\mathscr{K}(E)
, then
\max\{v,-1\}\in\mathscr{K}(E)
and
\|\max\{v,-1\}\|_{*}\leq\|v\|_{*}
by (
2.8
). Therefore, we may consider candidates in
\mathscr{K}(E)
such that

(4.3)

-1\leq v\leq 0,\quad\text{and }v=-1\text{ on an open set }G\supset E.

Moreover, we have for a compact set
K\subset\Omega
,

(4.4)

{\tt c}(K)<+\infty.

In fact, let
\chi
be a cut-off function which is 1 on
K
and
0
outside a neighborhood of
K
. Then,
-\chi\in W^{*}(\Omega)
because it is a smooth function. Thus,

{\tt c}(K)\leq\|\chi\|_{*}.

We list basic properties whose proofs are rather standard (see e.g.
[
AH96
]
). However, we include the details for the reader convenience.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
E,F
and
E_{j}
,
j\geq 1
, be Bore sets in
\Omega
.

(a)

If
E\subset F\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
, then
{\tt c}(E)\leq{\tt c}(F)
and

{\tt c}(E,\Omega^{\prime})\leq{\tt c}(E,\Omega)
where
{\tt c}(E,\Omega^{\prime})
is defined with respect the subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}
.

(b)

Let
\{E_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
is an increasing sequence and
E=\cup_{j\geq 1}E_{j}
. Then
\lim_{j}{\tt c}(E_{j})\leq{\tt c}(E)
.

(c)

For every sequence
\{E_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
,
E=\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}E_{j}
, we have
{\tt c}(E)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\tt c}(E_{j})
.

(d)

Let
K_{j}
be a decreasing sequence of compact sets and
K=\cap_{j}K_{j}
. Then,
{\tt c}(K)=\lim_{j}{\tt c}(K_{j})
.

Proof.

(a) follows easily from the definition of capacity (
4.2
) and (b) is a consequence of (a). To prove the subadditivity property (c) we may assume that
A:=\sum_{j}{\tt c}(E_{j})<+\infty
, otherwise it trivially holds. Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed. For each
j
, there exists
-1\leq v_{j}\leq 0
in
\mathscr{K}(E_{j})
such that
{\tt c}(E_{j})\geq\|v_{j}\|_{*}^{2}-2^{-j}\varepsilon
and
v_{j}=-1
a.e on an open set
G_{j}\supset E_{j}
. Put
u_{k}=\min_{j\leq k}v_{j}
. The sequence
u_{k}
is decreasing. It follows from Lemma
2.3
that

\|u_{k}\|_{*}^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|v_{j}\|_{*}^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\tt c}(E_{j})-\varepsilon=A+\varepsilon.

By Corollary
2.14
there exists a weak limit
u\in W^{*}(\Omega)
of
\{u_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
satisfying
\|u\|_{*}^{2}\leq A+\varepsilon
. For a fixed
j
, we have
{\bf 1}_{G_{j}}\cdot u_{k}\to{\bf 1}_{G_{j}}\cdot u
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
k\to\infty
. It follows from Proposition
2.12
that
u\leq-1
in
G_{j}
for
j=1,2...
. Then,
u\leq-1
on
G:=\cup_{j}G_{j}\supset E
and
u\in\mathscr{K}(E)
.
Hence,
u\in\mathscr{K}(E)
and
{\tt c}(E)\leq\|u\|_{*}^{2}\leq A+\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary the proof of (c) follows.

Finally we prove (d). By the monotonicity in (a),
{\tt c}(K)\leq\lim_{j}{\tt c}(K_{j})
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
v\in\mathscr{K}(K)
such that
v\leq-1
on an open set
G\supset K
and
{\tt c}(K)\geq\|v\|_{*}^{2}-\varepsilon
. Since
K_{j}
be compact sets,
K_{j}\subset G
for
j\geq 1
large enough. Thus,
v\in\mathscr{K}(K_{j})
and
{\tt c}(K_{j})\leq\|v\|_{*}\leq{\tt c}(K)+\varepsilon
. In other words,
\lim_{j}{\tt c}(K_{j})\leq{\tt c}(K)+\varepsilon
. Let
\varepsilon\to 0
, the proof of (d) follows.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

(a)

Let
E\subset E^{\prime}
be Borel sets in
\Omega
. If
V_{2n}(E^{\prime}\setminus E)=0
, then
{\tt c}(E)={\tt c}(E^{\prime})
. It is non-decreasing under holomorphic maps, i.e., if
h:G\to\Omega
is a holomorphic map, then

{\tt c}(E,G)\leq{\tt c}(h(E),\Omega).

(b)

Let
D\subset\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
.
We do not know if there exists a constant
A>0
depending only on
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
such that for every Borel set
E\subset D
,

(4.5)

{\tt c}(E,\Omega)\leq A\;{\tt c}(E,\Omega^{\prime}).

However, we will have a weaker version with some exponent on the right hand side later
in Corollary
4.8
for the new capacity
{\tt c}(\cdot,\Omega)
.

One of our main goals is to show that
{\tt c}(\cdot,\Omega)
is a capacity in the Choquet sense. The inner regularity is harder which will be proved in Theorem
6.5
, however the outer regularity is readily obtained as follows.

Lemma 4.3

(outer regularity)
.

For a Borel subset
E\subset\Omega
,

{\tt c}(E)=\inf\{{\tt c}(G):E\subset G,\;G\text{ is open}\}.

Proof.

By the monotonicity in Proposition
4.1
-(a),

{\tt c}(E)\leq\inf\{{\tt c}(G):E\subset G,\;G\text{ is open}\}.

To prove the reverse inequality we may assume
\mathscr{K}(E)
is non empty. Let
\varepsilon>0
. There exist
v\in\mathscr{K}(E)
with

\|v\|_{*}^{2}\leq{\tt c}(E)+\varepsilon

and an open set
G_{\varepsilon}
such that
v(x)\leq-1
a.e
x\in G_{\varepsilon}
. Therefore,

\inf\{{\tt c}(G):E\subset G,\;G\text{ is open}\}\leq{\tt c}(G_{\varepsilon})\leq\|v\|_{*}^{2}\leq{\tt c}(E)+\varepsilon.

The lemma follows by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

A similar argument shows the Lebesgue volume is dominated by the capacity.

Corollary 4.4
.

For a Borel set
E\subset\Omega
,

|E|\leq{\tt c}(E).
In particular, every set of capacity zero has Lebesgue measure zero.

Proof.

We may assume
{\tt c}(E)<\infty
, otherwise, the inequality is true. Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
v\in\mathscr{K}(E)
with
\|v\|_{*}^{2}\leq{\tt c}(E)+\varepsilon
.
There is an open set
G\supset E
such that
v(x)\leq-1
for a.e
x\in G
. This implies

|E|\leq|G|\leq\int_{G}|v|^{2}dx\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|v\|_{*}^{2}\leq{\tt c}(E)+\varepsilon.

The desired inequality follows by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

We have seen in above examples that bounded psh functions belong to
W^{*}(\Omega)
. For such functions the Bedford-Taylor capacity
cap(\cdot,\Omega)
in Section
2.5
is very suitable for studying their fine properties. Dinh, Marinescu and Vu
[
DMV23
]
also used
cap(\cdot,\Omega)
to study some fine properties of functions in
W^{*}(\Omega)
. In fact,
we are going to show that the two capacities
{\tt c}(\cdot,\Omega)
and
cap(\cdot,\Omega)
are comparable to each other.

The first part of Theorem
1.1
-(a) reads as follows.

Lemma 4.5

(dominated by Bedford-Taylor capacity)
.

Assume
\Omega
is strictly pseudoconvex. There exists a constant
A>0
such that for every Borel set
E\subset\subset\Omega
,

{\tt c}(E)\leq A\left[cap(E,\Omega)\right]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Remark 4.6
.

The relative compactness condition of
E
will be removed later after proving that
{\tt c}(\cdot,\Omega)
is a capacity in the sense of Choquet (Theorem
6.5
).

Proof.

Let
u_{E}^{*}
denote the relative extremal for
E
in (
2.22
).
Since both capacities are outer regular (Lemma
4.3
and
[
BT82
]
), we may assume that
E=G
is an open set and hence
u_{G}=u_{G}^{*}
. Then,
-1\leq u_{G}\leq 0
and
u_{G}=-1
on
G
. It follows that

(4.6)

{\tt c}(G)\leq\|u_{G}\|_{*}^{2}.

Let
\rho
be a strictly psh defining function for
\Omega
. We may assume that
dd^{c}\rho\geq\omega
. Since
G\subset\subset\Omega
, it implies that
u_{G}=0
on
\partial\Omega
(see, e.g.,
[
De89
, Properties (13.11)]
). Using
-1\leq u_{G}\leq 0
and integration by parts,

\|u_{G}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int(u_{G})^{2}(dd^{c}\rho)^{n}\leq\int-u_{G}(dd^{c}\rho)^{n}=\int-\rho dd^{c}u_{G}\wedge(dd^{c}\rho)^{n-1}.

Here and in the proof below we skip writing
\Omega
in the integral symbols
for simplicity. The last integral is less than

\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int dd^{c}u_{G}\wedge(dd^{c}\rho)^{n-1}.

Since
0\leq u_{G}+1\in PSH(\Omega)
, we have

du_{G}\wedge d^{c}u_{G}\leq\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}(1+u_{G})^{2}.

Hence,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|u_{G}\|_{*}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int(u_{G})^{2}(dd^{c}\rho)^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\int dd^{c}(1+u_{G})^{2}\wedge(dd^{c}\rho)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq A_{1}\int dd^{c}u_{G}\wedge(dd^{c}\rho)^{n-1}+\int dd^{c}u_{G}\wedge(dd^{c}\rho)^{n-1},

where
A_{1}=\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and we used the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int dd^{c}(1+u_{G})^{2}\wedge(dd^{c}\rho)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\int du_{G}\wedge d^{c}u_{G}\wedge(dd^{c}\rho)^{n-1}+\int(1+u_{G})dd^{c}u_{G}\wedge(dd^{c}\rho)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\int dd^{c}u_{G}\wedge(dd^{c}\rho)^{n-1}.

Invoking an inequality of Cegrell
[
Ce04
]
we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int dd^{c}u_{G}\wedge(dd^{c}\rho)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int(dd^{c}\rho)^{n}\right]^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left[\int(dd^{c}u_{G})^{n}\right]^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=A_{0}[cap(G,\Omega)]^{\frac{1}{n}},

where
A_{0}
is a uniform constant as
\rho
is smooth on
\bar{\Omega}
and we used the formula (
2.23
) for the last identity.

Combining (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) we get
{\tt c}(G)\leq A_{0}(1+A_{1})[cap(G,\Omega)]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

∎

We are ready to state Theorem
1.1
-(b) which is the reverse inequality for the two capacities.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
D\subset\subset\Omega
. There exists a constant
A^{\prime}
depending only on
D
and
\Omega
such that for every Borel set
E\subset D
,

cap(E,\Omega)\leq A^{\prime}\,{\tt c}(E)

Proof.

Since both capacities are outer regular,
again it is enough to prove the inequality for
E=G
an open subset in
D
. Let
u=u_{G}=u_{G}^{*}
be the relative extremal function of
G
in
\Omega
and consider
u:=\max\{u,A_{0}\rho\}
we may assume
u=u_{G}
on
\Omega
and it is defined in a neighborhood of
\bar{\Omega}
. We can furthermore increase
A_{0}
(depending on
D,\Omega
) such that

(4.9)

A_{0}\rho\leq u_{G}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Denote
u_{j}:=u*\eta_{j}
the convolution with the smooth kernel family in Section
2.3
. These functions are defined on
\Omega
for
j
large enough.

Let
f\in\mathscr{K}(G)
be such that
f\leq-1
a.e. on
G
and assume also that
-1\leq f\leq 0
in
\Omega
. Let
0\leq\chi\leq 1
belong to
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that
\chi=1
on
D
and
\mbox{supp }\chi\subset\subset\Omega
. Since
(dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}
converges to
(dd^{c}u)^{n}
weakly, the formula (
2.23
) implies that

(4.10)

cap(G,\Omega)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{G}(dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{D}\chi^{2^{n}}f^{2}(dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}.

Fix an index
j
, thus
u_{j}
smooth. Applying Corollary
3.5
for
\phi=u_{j}
to get

\displaystyle\int\chi^{2^{n}}f^{2}(dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}100A_{k}\;\chi^{2^{k}}(dd^{c}u_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-1-k}\right)

\displaystyle+(40A_{0})^{n}\int\chi f^{2}\omega^{n},

where
A_{k}=[40(1+A_{0})]^{n-1-k}
.
If
T\in\Gamma_{f}
, i.e.,
df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T
in
\Omega
, then

\displaystyle\int\chi^{2^{n}}f^{2}(dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int T\wedge\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}100A_{k}\;\chi^{2^{k}}(dd^{c}u_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-1-k}\right)

\displaystyle+(40A_{0})^{n}\int\chi f^{2}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

We first consider
J_{2}
. Clearly,

(4.11)

J_{2}\leq A_{1}^{\prime}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Next we estimate
J_{1}=J_{1}(j)
.
By letting
j\to+\infty
, the weak convergence implies

\displaystyle\limsup_{j\to\infty}J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq A_{0}^{\prime}\int\chi T\wedge\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-1-k}

\displaystyle\leq A_{0}^{\prime}\int_{\Omega}T\wedge\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-1-k}.

Thanks to (
4.9
) we invoke the comparison of mass in Lemma
2.21
to get that

(4.12)

\displaystyle\limsup_{j\to\infty}J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq A_{0}^{\prime}\int_{\Omega}T\wedge\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}[dd^{c}(A_{0}\rho)]^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-1-k}

\displaystyle\leq A_{1}^{\prime}\int_{\Omega}T\wedge\omega^{n-1},

where the last inequality used the fact
dd^{c}\rho\leq A\omega
.

Combining (
4.10
), (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) together we arrive at

cap(G,\Omega)\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}(J_{1}+J_{2})\leq A_{1}^{\prime}(\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|T\|_{\Omega}).

Taking infimum over all currents
T\in\Gamma_{f}
we get

cap(G,\Omega)\leq A^{\prime}_{1}\|f\|_{*}^{2}.

This holds for every
f\in\mathscr{K}(G)
and therefore,
cap(G,\Omega)\leq A_{1}^{\prime}{\tt c}(G)
.
∎

As a consequence of the above comparison of two capacities
we are able to state now a weak equivalence property of
{\tt c}(\cdot,\Omega)
mentioned in Remark
4.2
-(b) when we consider it on a smaller domain.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
D\subset\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
. Then, there exists a constant
A=A(D,\Omega)
such that for every Borel set
E\subset D
,

{\tt c}(E,\Omega^{\prime})\leq{\tt c}(E,\Omega)\leq A\left[{\tt c}(E,\Omega^{\prime})\right]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Proof.

The first inequality follows immediately from Proposition
4.1
-(a). It remains to verify the second one. For the Bedford-Taylor capacity we know that

cap(E,\Omega)\leq cap(E,\Omega^{\prime})
. Therefore, using Lemmas
4.5
and
4.7
, we get

{\tt c}(E,\Omega)\leq A[cap(E,\Omega)]^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq A[cap(E,\Omega^{\prime})]^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq A^{\prime}[{\tt c}(E,\Omega^{\prime})]^{\frac{1}{n}},

where
A^{\prime}
depends only on
\Omega
and
D
.
∎

5.
Quasi-continuity

In this section we will show that a function in
W^{*}(\Omega)
admits a quasi-continuous representation with respect to the capacity
{\tt c}(\cdot,\Omega)
. Let us make it precise by defining the terminologies.

Definition 5.1
.

A function
u
is called quasi-continuous (q.c) in
\Omega
if there exists for every
\varepsilon>0
an open set
G\subset\Omega
such that
{\tt c}(G)<\varepsilon
and the restriction
u_{|_{\Omega\setminus G}}
is continuous (with respect to the induced topology on
\Omega\setminus G
).

A statement is said to hold
quasi-everywhere (q.e)
if there exists a set
P
of capacity zero, i.e.,
{\tt c}(P)=0
, such that the statement is true for every
x\notin P
.

Remark 5.2
.

The Bedford-Taylor capacity
cap(\cdot,\Omega)
and
{\tt c}(\cdot,\Omega)
are equivalent to each other by Lemmas
4.5
,
4.7
(also Theorem
6.5
below). Hence, these quasi-continuity and quasi-everywhere notions coincide with the classical ones in pluripotential theory. Thus, there is no confusion if we use these properties for psh functions which belong to
W^{*}(\Omega)
.

The following statement is classical in potential theory
[
FOT94
, Lemma 2.14]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
D\subset\Omega
be an open set and
f
is quasi-continuous on
D
. If
f\geq 0
a.e. on
D
, then
f\geq 0

Proof.

For each

open such that

Lemma 5.5

Let

Definition 5.6

A sequence of functions

We are ready to state a key ingredient that helps to select a good representative of functions in

Theorem 5.7

Let

\{f_{k}\}

Strictly speaking since functions

are defined on a bit smaller domain

{\tt c}(\cdot,\Omega^{\prime})

is fixed, by Corollary

we may assume

for some neighborhood of

without loss of generality. Thus, we can still write
{\tt c}(E)={\tt c}(E,\Omega)
in what follows.

Now, by writing

, we may assume that
f\geq 0
.
Then, we reduce the estimate to the case

is uniformly bounded as follows.
Fix a large

and consider

\{|f_{j}-f_{k}|>\delta\}\subset\{|\widehat{f}_{j}-\widehat{f}_{k}|>\delta\}\cup E_{jk}(N),

Using Lemma

Next, by covering

df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T=dd^{c}u
in the ball

K\subset(K\cap\{u\geq-N\})\cup\{u<-N\}.

is psh function in
\Omega

Claim 5.8
.

We have
g_{j}:=u_{N}f_{j}\in W^{*}(\Omega^{\prime})
for every

We are ready to apply Lemma

Here,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\int_{K}(g_{j}-g_{k})^{2}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\quad A_{0}N^{4a}\left(\int\chi(g_{j}-g_{k})^{2}\left[\omega+2dd^{c}(v_{j}+v_{k})\right]^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}.

\displaystyle\int\chi(g_{j}-g_{k})^{2}[\omega

\displaystyle+2dd^{c}(v_{j}+v_{k})]^{n}

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
. There exists a quasi-continuous modification
\widetilde{f}
.

Proof.

Let
D\subset\subset\Omega
be a subdomain. Let
f_{k}=f*\eta_{1/k}
with
k=1,2,...
. Define

E_{k}=\{x\in D:|f_{k}-f_{k+1}|>1/2^{k}\},\quad G_{j}=\cup_{k\geq j}E_{k}.

By choosing a subsequence we can assume that
{\tt c}(E_{k})\leq 2^{-k}
. Hence,
{\tt c}(G_{j})\leq 2^{-j}
and

{\tt c}\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}G_{j}\right)=0.

If
x\in D\setminus G_{j}
, then

|f_{N+s}(x)-f_{N}(x)|\leq\sum_{k\geq N}|f_{k+1}-f_{k}|\leq 1/2^{N}.

for all
s\geq 1
and
N\geq j
.
This means
\{f_{k}\}
restricted to
D\setminus G_{j}
is uniformly convergent as
k\to\infty
. Let

\widetilde{f}(x):=\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(x)
for
x\in\cup_{j}(D\setminus G_{j})
. Then, it is a quasi-continuous function and
\widetilde{f}=f
almost everywhere on
D
.

Now we prove the quasi-continuity of
\widetilde{f}
in
\Omega
. Let
D_{\ell}
be a sequence of increasing relative compact domains that
\Omega=\cup_{\ell}D_{\ell}
.
Define

E_{k}(\ell)=\{x\in D_{\ell}:|f_{k}-f_{k+1}|>1/2^{k}\},\quad G_{j}(\ell)=\cup_{k\geq j}E_{k}(\ell).

By diagonal argument we can pass to a subsequence satisfying
{\tt c}(E_{k}(\ell))\leq 2^{-\ell-k}
for every
\ell\geq 1
. Hence,
{\tt c}(G_{j}(\ell))\leq 2^{-\ell-j}
and

{\tt c}\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}G_{j}(\ell)\right)=0.

If
x\in D_{\ell}\setminus G_{j}(\ell)
, then

|f_{N+s}(x)-f_{N}(x)|\leq\sum_{k\geq N}|f_{k+1}-f_{k}|\leq 1/2^{N}.

for all
s\geq 1
and
N\geq j
.
This means
\{f_{k}\}
restricted on
D_{\ell}\setminus G_{j}(\ell)
is uniformly convergent as
k\to\infty
. Let

(5.10)

\widetilde{f}(x):=\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(x)\quad\text{for }x\in\bigcup_{\ell\geq 1}\cup_{j\geq 1}[D_{\ell}\setminus G_{j}(\ell)].

Then,
\widetilde{f}=f
almost everywhere on
\Omega
. We verify the quasi-continuity.
The function restricted to

F_{j}(\ell):=D_{\ell}\setminus G_{j}(\ell)
is continuous. Define
F_{j}=\cap_{\ell=1}^{\infty}F_{j}(\ell)
. We have

{\tt c}(\Omega\setminus F_{j})\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}{\tt c}(G_{j}(\ell))\leq 1/2^{j}.

Thus, the proof of theorem is completed.
∎

Let

6.
Capacitability

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Our goal is to show that
{\tt c}(\cdot,\Omega)
is a capacity in the sense of Choquet. The first one is inspired by an analogue result of
[
Vig07
, Corollary 31]
on compact Kähler manifolds. Additionally, we obtain the uniqueness of the extremal function in the local setting. Following
[
FO87
, Lemma 2.1.1]
we assume first the openness of the set and remove this condition later.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume
E\subset\Omega
is an open subset. There is a unique
\Phi_{E}\in W^{*}(\Omega)
such that

{\tt c}(E)=\|\Phi_{E}\|_{*}^{2}
. Moreover,
-1\leq\Phi_{E}\leq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
and
\Phi_{E}=-1
q.e. on
E
.

Proof.

We first prove the existence. Let
\{u_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
and
-1\leq u_{j}\leq 0
be a sequence in
\mathscr{K}(E)
such that
{\tt c}(E)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{*}^{2}
. It follows from Corollary
2.14
that by passing to a subsequence
u_{j}
converges weakly in
W^{1,2}(X)
to
u\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and
\|u\|_{*}\leq\liminf_{j}\|u_{j}\|_{*}
. By Proposition
2.12
we have
u=-1
a.e. on
E
and
-1\leq u\leq 0
in
\Omega
. The proof of this proposition used only the properties of Lebesgue points of functions in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Since
E
is open,
u\in\mathscr{K}(E)
(this is the place where the openness is used) and
{\tt c}(E)=\|u\|_{*}^{2}
. Of course we can take
\widetilde{u}
in the place of
u
. By Proposition
5.3
and the openness of
E
, we have
\widetilde{u}=-1
q.e.

Next, we show the uniqueness. Suppose
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are such two functions. Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are the minimum closed positive currents of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, respectively. Hence,

{\tt c}(E)=\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|T_{1}\|_{\Omega}=\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|T_{2}\|_{\Omega}.

Define
v=(u_{1}+u_{2})/2
. Clearly,

dv\wedge d^{c}v\leq(T_{1}+T_{2})/2

and therefore,

\displaystyle{\tt c}(E)

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|T\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{1}u_{2}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}(\|T_{1}\|_{\Omega}+\|T_{2}\|_{\Omega}).

This implies that

2\int_{\Omega}u_{1}u_{2}dx\geq\int_{\Omega}u_{1}^{2}\;dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{2}^{2}\;dx.

Thus,
u_{1}=u_{2}
a.e.
∎

It is easy to see that if
v\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and
v\leq-1
a.e in a neighborhood of
E
, then
\widetilde{v}\in\widetilde{W}^{*}(\Omega)
and
\widetilde{v}\leq-1
q.e on
E
. We show that the infimum in the definition of the capacity can be considered in this bigger space.

Lemma 6.2
.

For a Borel set
E
,

\mathscr{K}^{\prime}(E):=\left\{v\in W^{*}(\Omega)\;\rvert\;\widetilde{v}\leq-1\text{ q.e. on $E$ and }\widetilde{v}\leq 0\text{ q.e.}\right\}.

Then,

{\tt c}(E)=\inf\{\|v\|_{*}^{2}:v\in\mathscr{K}^{\prime}(E)\}.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{K}(E)\subset\mathscr{K}^{\prime}(E)
, we have
{\tt c}(E)
is greater than the infimum. It remains
to show the reverse inequality.
Let us consider
h\in\mathscr{K}^{\prime}(E)
and fix a quasi-continuous modification
\widetilde{h}
of
h
(Theorem
5.10
). Given
0<\varepsilon<<1
we choose an open set
G:=G_{\varepsilon}
such that
{\tt c}(G)<\varepsilon
and
\widetilde{h}
restricted to
\Omega\setminus G
is continuous and
\widetilde{h}\leq-1
on
E\cap(\Omega\setminus G)
. Denote by
u
the function
\Phi_{G}
of the open set

in Lemma

. Then

D_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega\setminus G:\widetilde{h}<-1+\varepsilon\}\cup G

is an open subset in
\Omega
and

. Moreover,
h+u\leq-1+\varepsilon
a.e. on
D_{\varepsilon}
and it is negative in
\Omega
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle{\tt c}(E)

\displaystyle\leq{\tt c}(D_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{\|h+u\|_{*}^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\|h\|_{*}+\|u\|_{*})^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\|h\|_{*}+\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}.

By letting

,
{\tt c}(E)\leq\|h\|_{*}^{2}
. Since

is arbitrary, we conclude the reverse inequality.
∎

We need to improve the a.e. convergence to the q.e. one in order to remove the openness condition in Lemma

Lemma 6.3

Let

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume

u
are quasi-continuous and
-1\leq u_{j},u\leq 0
. Define
F=\{u>-1\}\cap E
. We wish to show that
{\tt c}(F)=0
. By subadditivity it is enough to show that
{\tt c}(F\cap K_{j})=0
for an exhaustive sequence of compact set
K_{j}\uparrow\Omega
. In other words, we may assume
F\subset\subset\Omega
and
\Omega
is a strictly pseudoconvex domain.

Arguing by contradiction, we suppose that
{\tt c}(F)>0
. Lemma
4.5
implies
cap(F)>0
. According to the characterization in Corollary
7.9
there exists
\phi\in PSH(\Omega)
,
-1\leq\phi\leq 0
and
0<\delta\leq 1
such that the capacity
{\tt c}_{\theta}
associated with
\theta=(dd^{c}\phi+\delta\omega)^{n-1}
satisfying

(6.1)

{\tt c}_{\theta}(F)>0.

This capacity is defined in (
7.10
) on the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{F}
(Definition
7.4
), where
\mathcal{F}
is identified with the real Hilbert space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\theta)
(Lemma
7.5
). The latter space is equipped with the inner product
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{\theta}
in (
7.5
) and the
\|\cdot\|_{\theta}
-norms in (
7.6
).

Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
be such that
0\leq\chi\leq 1
and
\chi=1
on a neighborhood of
\bar{E}
. By Theorem
7.6
,
v_{j}:=\chi u_{j}\in\mathcal{F}
and
v:=\chi u\in\mathcal{F}
whose
\|\cdot\|_{\theta}
-norms are uniformly bounded. Clearly,
v_{j}\to v
weakly in
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
. So,
\left<v_{j},g\right>_{\theta}\to\left<v,g\right>_{\theta}
for every
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
.
Since the latter space is dense in
\mathcal{F}
, it follows that
v_{j}\to v
weakly in
(\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\theta})
.
Mazur’s lemma implies there is a convex combination
\Phi_{\ell}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}v_{j}
, where
a_{j}\geq 0
and
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}=1
, converges to
v
in
\|\cdot\|_{\theta}
-norm.

We are ready to get a contradiction. By the assumption for
j\geq 1
,
v_{j}=-1
on
E\setminus P_{j}
where
P_{j}
is pluripolar. Hence,
\Phi_{\ell}=-1
on
E
outside a pluripolar set
P^{\prime}=\cup_{j\geq 1}P_{j}
.
Thanks to Lemma
7.8
-(b) each
\Phi_{\ell}
is quasi-continuous with respect to with respect to
{\tt c}_{\theta}(\cdot)
. By
[
FOT94
, Theorem 2.1.4]
for
\{\Phi_{\ell}\}_{\ell\geq 1}
, we infer that
\Phi_{\ell}\to v
point-wise on
\Omega\setminus P^{\prime\prime}
where
{\tt c}_{\theta}(P^{\prime\prime})=0
.
Hence,
u=v=-1
on
E\setminus(P^{\prime}\cup P^{\prime\prime})
. By definition
F\subset P^{\prime}\cup P^{\prime\prime}
. Invoking Corollary
7.9
once more we get
{\tt c}_{\theta}(F)=0
. This is impossible by (
6.1
).
∎

Thanks to the above lemma the function
\Phi_{E}
obtained in the proof of Lemma
6.1
belongs to
\mathscr{K}^{\prime}(E)
for every Borel set. Thus, Lemma
6.2
is now valid in general.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
E\subset\Omega
be a Borel subset. There is a unique
\Phi_{E}\in W^{*}(\Omega)
such that

{\tt c}(E)=\|\Phi_{E}\|_{*}^{2}
. It satisfies that
-1\leq\Phi_{E}\leq 0
a.e. and
\Phi_{E}=-1
q.e. on
E
.

Now we prove the main result of this section which is the content of Theorem
1.3
. The proof is more detail than the one in
[
Vig07
, Theorem 30]
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The set function on Borel sets
E\mapsto{\tt c}(E)={\tt c}(E,\Omega)
satisfies:

(a)

{\tt c}(E_{1})\leq{\tt c}(E_{2})
for
E_{1}\subset E_{2}\subset\Omega
.

(b)

if
K_{1}\subset K_{2}\subset\cdots
are compact sets in
\Omega
and
K=\cap K_{j}
, then
{\tt c}(K)=\lim_{j\to\infty}{\tt c}(K_{j})
.

(c)

if
E_{1}\subset E_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\Omega
are Borel sets and
E=\cup E_{j}
, then
{\tt c}(E)=\lim_{j\to\infty}{\tt c}(E_{j})
.

Proof.

The items (a) and (b) are proved in Proposition
4.1
, therefore it remains only to prove (c). It is enough to show that
{\tt c}(E)\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}{\tt c}(E_{j})
as the other direction is obvious by (a). By Lemma
6.2
there is
u_{j}\in\mathscr{K}^{\prime}(E_{j})
satisfying

{\tt c}(E_{j})=\|u_{j}\|_{*}^{2}.

We may assume
\|u_{j}\|_{*}
is bounded uniformly in
j\geq 1
(otherwise the conclusion is true). Thus, there exists a subsequence converging weakly to
u\in W^{*}(\Omega)
. In particular,
u_{j}\to u
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Proposition
2.12
implies that
u=-1
a.e. on
E=\cup_{j}E_{j}
and
u\leq 0
in
\Omega
. Lemma
6.3
and Proposition
5.3
yield
\widetilde{u}=-1
q.e. on
E
and
\widetilde{u}\leq 0
in
\Omega
, respectivly. So,
{\tt c}(E)\leq\|u\|_{*}^{2}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|u_{j}\|_{*}^{2}.

∎

Since
{\tt c}(\cdot,\Omega)
is a capacity in the sense of Choquet, it is inner regular, i.e., for every Borel sets

{\tt c}(E)=\sup\{{\tt c}(K):K\subset E\text{ is compact}\}.

This combined with
Lemma
4.7
and Lemma
4.5
give a useful observation.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
E\subset\Omega
be a Borel set.
E
is pluripolar if and only if
{\tt c}(E,\Omega)=0
.

7.
Embedding into Dirichlet spaces

7.1.
Dirichlet spaces associated with a closed positive current

In this section we show that
W^{*}(\Omega)
can be naturally embedded into a Dirichlet space associated with a positive closed
(n-1,n-1)
-current. These positive currents comes from the wedge product of
(1,1)
-currents associated with bounded psh functions.

Let
-1\leq\phi_{1},...,\phi_{n-1}\leq 0
be psh functions in
\Omega
.
Then,

(7.1)

\theta=dd^{c}\phi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dd^{c}\phi_{n-1}

is a closed positive current of bidegree
(n-1,n-1)
. Without loss of generality we consider the case all functions
\phi_{k}=\phi
. We introduce a quadratic form associated with
\theta
on
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
as follows. For
f,g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
,

\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f,g)=\int df\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\theta

Here and all integrals considered in the remaining part of this section are on
\Omega
, so we omit writing
\Omega
in the integrations. Define

\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f):=\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f,f)=\int df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\theta.

The trace measure of
\theta
is given by

\mu:=\theta\wedge\omega=\sum\tau_{j\bar{j}},

which is a positive Radon measure.

Remark 7.1
.

Let us write
\theta=\sum\theta_{j\bar{k}}\;\widehat{dz^{j}}\wedge\widehat{d\bar{z}^{k}}
, where
\theta_{j\bar{k}}
are complex Radon measures. They are absolutely continuous with respect to

\mu=\theta\wedge\omega
. Hence,
\theta_{j\bar{k}}=h_{j\bar{k}}\cdot\mu
for some bounded (complex valued) Borel function
h_{j\bar{k}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,d\mu)
. This gives

\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(v)=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\partial_{j}v\cdot\overline{\partial_{k}v}\;\theta_{j\bar{k}}=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\partial_{j}v\cdot\overline{\partial_{k}v}\;h_{j\bar{k}}\;d\mu,

The following result of Okada
[
Ok82
]
is very useful.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\{f_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
be such that
f_{i}\to 0
in
L^{2}(\Omega,d\mu)
. Assume
\{f_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{\theta}
-norm, i.e.,

(7.2)

\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f_{j}-f_{k})=\int d(f_{j}-f_{k})\wedge d^{c}(f_{j}-f_{k})\wedge\theta\to 0\quad\text{as }j,k\to\infty.

Then,
\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f_{j})=0
.

Proof.

We first show the following characterization. For
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
,

[\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(g)]^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sup\left\{\int gdd^{c}\chi\wedge\theta:\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),\;\|\chi\|_{\theta}\leq 1\right\}.

By integration by parts,

\int gdd^{c}\chi\wedge\theta=-\int dg\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\theta.

Here the integration by part holds by a simple approximation argument via
\theta_{\varepsilon}:=\theta*\eta_{\varepsilon}
. Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for this we get that the supremum is less than
[\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(g)]^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Moreover, for
\chi=-f/[\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f)]^{\frac{1}{2}}
the supremum is attained. The characterization is proved.

Next, we apply it for
h=f_{j}-f_{k}
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
Since
\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f_{j}-f_{k})\to 0
, there exists
N
such that we have for
j,k>N
and for
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
,

\int(f_{j}-f_{k})dd^{c}\chi\wedge\theta<\varepsilon.

Clearly the total variation
\|dd^{c}\chi\wedge\theta\|\leq A\omega\wedge\theta
for some constant
A
depending only on
\chi
. By letting
k\to\infty
we get

\int f_{j}dd^{c}\chi\wedge\theta<\varepsilon.

Taking supremum over
\chi
and the characterization gives the conclusion.
∎

Since the space
W^{*}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, we need an additional assumption on
\theta
so that
L^{2}(\Omega,d\mu)\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
. The simplest way is by considering
\phi:=\phi+\delta|z|^{2}
for some
0<\delta\leq 1
. Since
\omega=dd^{c}|z|^{2}
, we have

(7.3)

\theta=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}\delta^{n-k}\omega^{n-1-k}\wedge(dd^{c}\phi)^{k}=:\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta^{k}\theta_{k},

where
\theta_{k}=\binom{n-1}{k}\omega^{n-k}\wedge(dd^{c}\phi)^{k}
.
Hence, the trace measure
\mu=\theta\wedge\omega
satisfies

(7.4)

\mu\geq c_{0}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega^{n-1-k}\wedge(dd^{c}\phi)^{k}.

In particular,
\mu=\theta\wedge\omega\geq c_{0}\omega^{n}
and

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{\theta}^{2}

for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).

Let us consider the following inner product

(7.5)

\left<f,g\right>_{\theta}=\int fg\;\omega^{n}+\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f,g),

and the associated norm

(7.6)

\|f\|^{2}_{\theta}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f,f).

The assumption (
7.3
) makes this inner product positive definite on
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
. Then, we define the real Hilbert space

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\theta):=\overline{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})}

to be the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\theta}
. A priori this is an abstract functional space. However, we are going to identify precisely this space.

First, thanks to the result of Fukushima and Okada
[
FO87
, Theorem 2.5]
that
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{\theta}
is closable in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. More precisely, we can replace the assumption on
L^{2}
-convergence with respect to
\mu
in Lemma
7.2
by the one for the Lebesgue measure.

Theorem 7.3
.

Assume
\{f_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
such that
f_{i}\to 0
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\{f_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{\theta}
-norm, i.e.,

(7.7)

\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f_{j}-f_{k})=\int d(f_{j}-f_{k})\wedge d^{c}(f_{j}-f_{k})\wedge\theta\to 0\quad\text{as }j,k\to\infty.

Then,
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f_{i})=0
.

This leads to a natural functional space studied in
[
Ok82
]
.

Definition 7.4
.

A function
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
belongs to
\mathcal{F}
if there exists a sequence
w_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that
w_{j}\to f
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\{w_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence with respect to the semi-norm
\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(\cdot)
.

This space
\mathcal{F}
together with the symmetric form
\mathscr{E}_{\theta}
becomes a regular Dirichlet space on on
L^{2}(\Omega)
by Theorem
7.3
(see also
[
Ok82
, Théorèm 4]
).
We state the promised identification for the abstract Hilbert space.

Lemma 7.5
.

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\theta)=\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Consider identity map
\iota:C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\Omega)
. Since
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{\theta}
, this map is extended continuously to
\widetilde{\iota}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\theta)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
. Hence,
\mathcal{F}
is contained in the image of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\theta)
under
\widetilde{\iota}
.
If
\widetilde{\iota}
is injective, then the image can be identified with
\mathcal{F}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
. In fact, let
f\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\theta)
such that
\widetilde{\iota}(f)=0
. There exists
\{f_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
which is a Cauchy sequence representing
f
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\theta}^{2}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(\cdot)
. In particular,
\{f_{i}\}
is
\mathscr{E}_{\theta}
-Cauchy and

\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|\widetilde{\iota}(f_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0.

Lemma
7.3
yields
\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f_{j})\to 0
. Hence,

\|f\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\theta)}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\|f_{i}\|^{2}_{\theta}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathscr{E}_{\theta}(f_{i},f_{i})=0.

So,
f=0
and the proof is complete.
∎

We are ready to state the embedding theorem.

Theorem 7.6
.

If
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
, then
\chi f\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Assume
df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T
for a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
of finite mass in
\Omega
. Let
f_{k}=f*\eta_{1/k}
and
T_{k}=T*\eta_{1/k}
be the convolutions with the family of smooth kernels. Denote
g_{k}=\chi f_{k}
which is smooth in
\Omega
for
k
large. We estimate

\displaystyle\int dg_{k}\wedge d^{c}g_{k}\wedge\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2\int\chi^{2}df_{k}\wedge d^{c}f_{k}\wedge\theta+2\int f^{2}_{k}d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\theta

\displaystyle\leq 10\int\chi^{2}T_{k}\wedge\theta+4A_{0}\int\chi f^{2}_{k}\,\omega\wedge\theta

\displaystyle\leq A,

where we used Lemma
3.2
and then
df_{k}\wedge d^{c}f_{k}\leq T_{k}
for the second inequality.
The finiteness in the last inequality follows from the Chern-Levine-Nirenberg inequality and weak convergence of
T_{k}
to
T
for the first integral and Proposition
3.6
for the second integral.
This implies that there exists a subsequence
\{g_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
converging weakly to
g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\theta)=\mathcal{F}
. By passing to a subsequence and Mazur’s lemma
[
Yo80
, Theorem 2, page 120]
we find a sequence
\{w_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
defined by

w_{j}=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}g_{k_{\ell}}=\chi\left(\frac{1}{j}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}f_{k_{\ell}}\right)

which converges in
\|\cdot\|_{\theta}
-norm to
g
. Since
w_{j}\to\chi f
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have
g=\chi f\in\mathcal{F}
by the definition of this space.
∎

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
f,g\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
. Let
\theta
be a closed positive
(n-1,n-1)
-current as in (
7.1
). Then, the integrals

\int df\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\theta\quad\text{ and }\quad\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\theta

are well-defined.

Proof.

If
f
is smooth, then by integration by parts

(7.8)

\int df\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\theta=-\int fdd^{c}\chi\wedge\theta.

Next, for a general
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
, we have
\{f_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}=\{f*\eta_{1/k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is Cauchy with respect to the capacity. Passing to a subsequence we may assume
\widetilde{f}_{k}\to\widetilde{f}
in capacity and
\widetilde{f}
is quasi-continuous. Hence, the right hand side above is defined by

\int fdd^{c}\chi\wedge\theta:=\int\widetilde{f}dd^{c}\chi\wedge\theta=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int\widetilde{f}_{k}dd^{c}\chi\wedge\theta.

Thus, we take the limit, which is independent of subsequence, as the definition of the left hand side of (
7.8
).

Next, to define
\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\theta
, by polarization it is enough to define
\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\theta
and
\int\chi^{2}dg\wedge d^{c}g\wedge\theta
. We first assume
\theta=\theta_{\delta}
satisfying (
7.3
). It follows from Theorem
7.6
that

\int d(\chi f)\wedge d^{c}(\chi f)\wedge\theta_{\delta}

is well-defined. Therefore, one can define

\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\theta_{\delta}:=\int d(\chi f)\wedge d^{c}(\chi f)\wedge\theta_{\delta}-2\int df\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\theta_{\delta}-\int d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\theta_{\delta},

where each integral on the right hand side already makes sense. Of course,
this is an honest identity for
f
being smooth. By the weak convergence of
\chi f_{j}\to\chi f
in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}
, it follows that

\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\theta_{\delta}

is a non-negative increasing sequence in
\delta>0
. Finally, we put

\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\theta=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int\chi^{2}df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\theta_{\delta}.

This completes the proof of the corollary.
∎

Thanks to this corollary and Definition
5.12
we conclude that the integral estimates that obtained in Section
3
now hold for a general
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
.

7.2.
Capacity in the Dirichlet spaces

Let
\phi\in PSH(\Omega)
be such that
-1\leq\phi\leq 0
. Let
\delta>0
. By Theorem
7.3
, the pair
(\theta,\mu)
of the closed positive current

(7.9)

\theta=[dd^{c}(\phi+\delta|z|^{2})]^{n-1}

and the measure
\mu=\theta\wedge\omega
is closable on
L^{2}(\Omega,d\mu)
and
\mbox{supp }\mu=\Omega
, i.e., it is admissible in the sense of
[
FO87
, page 173]
. Hence,
\mathcal{F}
defined in Definition
7.4
is a Dirichlet space on
L^{2}(\Omega,d\mu)
possessing
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
as its core, together with a Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}^{\theta}
. In particular, it is regular
[
FOT94
, page 6]
.

By
[
Ok82
, Théorèm 1]
we have a Poincaré type inequality, for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
,

\int f^{2}d\mu=\int f^{2}\theta\wedge dd^{c}|z|^{2}\leq 8\sup_{\Omega}|z|\int df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\theta.

It follows that
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\theta}(f)=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}+\mathcal{E}^{\theta}(f)
is equivalent to
\mathcal{E}^{\theta}(f)
. As noted in
[
FO84
, page 211]
that potential theory of
[
FOT94
, Chapter 2]
can be formulated in terms of
\mathcal{E}^{\theta}
instead of
\mathcal{E}^{\theta}_{1}
. For an open subset
E\subset\Omega
its capacity is given by

(7.10)

{\tt c}_{\theta}(E)=\inf\{\mathcal{E}(v):v\in\mathcal{F},\;v\geq 1\quad\mu-\text{\rm a.e.}\text{ on }E\}.

For a general Borel set we let

{\tt c}_{\theta}(E)=\inf\{{\tt c}_{\theta}(G):E\subset G,\quad G\text{ is open subset in }\Omega\}.

In particular, for a compact subset
K\subset\Omega
,

{\tt c}_{\theta}(K)=\inf\{\mathcal{E}^{\theta}(v):v\in\mathcal{F}\cap C^{0}(\Omega),\;v\geq 1\text{ on }K\}.

Thank to special feature of the functional capacity in a Hilbert space it is a Choquet capcity
[
FOT94
, Theorem 2.1.1]
.
Moreover, we have a comparison between this capacity and the Bedford-Taylor capacity as follows.

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a strictly pseudoconvex domain and
0<\delta\leq 1
.

(a)

For every compact subset
K\subset\Omega
,

cap(K)\leq 8{\tt c}_{\theta}(K),

where
\theta=(dd^{c}u_{K}^{*}+\delta\omega)^{n-1}
.

(b)

Let
D\subset\subset\Omega
be a subdomain. There exists a constant
A_{0}=A(D,\Omega)
such that for every open subset
G\subset D
,

{\tt c}_{\theta}{(G)}\leq A_{0}\left[cap(G)\right]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Proof.

(a)
Denote
\phi:=u_{K}^{*}
the relative extremal function of
K
in
\Omega
. Then,
\phi\in PSH(\Omega),-1\leq\phi\leq 0
which satisfies
cap(K)=\int_{K}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}
. For
v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and
v\geq 1
on
K
,

\int_{K}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}\leq\int_{\Omega}v^{2}dd^{c}\phi\wedge\theta=:I,

where
\theta=(dd^{c}\phi+\delta\omega)^{n-1}
. The Poincaré type inequality
[
Ok82
, Théorèm 1]
implies

I\leq 8\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}dv\wedge d^{c}v\wedge\theta.

Taking infimum over all such
v
’s implies the desired inequality.

(b) Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that
0\leq\chi\leq 1
and
\chi=1
in a neighborhood
\bar{D}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be small. Let
u_{G}^{*}=u_{G}
be the relative extremal function associated with
G
. By Theorem
7.6
,
v=\chi u_{G}\in\mathcal{F}
. Since
v=1
on
G
we have

(7.11)

{\tt c}_{\theta}(G)\leq\int dv\wedge d^{c}v\wedge\theta^{n-1}.

Let
\rho
be the strictly psh defining function and
dd^{c}\rho\geq\omega
. By replacing
\phi:=\max\{\phi,A\rho\}
for
A>0
large we can assume
\phi=A\rho
on
\Omega\setminus\mbox{supp }\chi
. By (
2.6
),

d(\chi u_{G})\wedge d^{c}(\chi u_{G})\leq 2(\chi^{2}du_{G}\wedge d^{c}u_{G}+u_{G}^{2}d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi).

Hence,

(7.12)

\displaystyle\int dv\wedge d^{c}v\wedge\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2\int du_{G}\wedge d^{c}u_{G}\wedge\theta+2\int u_{G}^{2}d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\theta

\displaystyle:=2(I_{1}+I_{2}).

By integration by parts,

I_{1}=\int(-u_{G})dd^{c}u_{G}\wedge\theta\leq\int dd^{c}u_{G}\wedge[dd^{c}(\phi+\delta\rho)]^{n-1}

Using the Cegrell’s inequality
[
Ce04
]
we get

(7.13)

I_{1}\leq\left[\int(dd^{c}u_{G})^{n}\right]^{\frac{1}{n}}\left[\int[dd^{c}(\phi+\delta\rho)]^{n}\right]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Since
\phi+\delta\rho\geq(A+1)\rho
on
\Omega
and has zero value on
\partial\Omega
, it follows from the comparison principle

\int[dd^{c}(\phi+\delta\rho)]^{n}\leq(A+1)^{n}\int(dd^{c}\rho)^{n}:=A_{1}

which is independent of
G
.
Thus,

I_{1}\leq A_{1}[cap(G)]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

The estimate for
I_{2}
is very similar. Namely, using
d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\leq A^{\prime}dd^{c}\rho
and integration by parts
[
Bl98
, Proposition 2.1.3]
,

I_{2}\leq A^{\prime}\int(-u_{G})dd^{c}\rho\wedge\theta=A^{\prime}\int(-\rho)dd^{c}u_{G}\wedge\theta.

Therefore, using again
[
Ce04
]
we get for
A_{2}=A^{\prime}A_{1}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}
,

(7.14)

I_{2}\leq A_{2}[cap(G)]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Combining (
7.11
), (
7.12
), (
7.13
) and (
7.14
) we get
{\tt c}_{\theta}(G)\leq A_{3}[cap(G)]^{\frac{1}{n}}
where
A_{3}
is a uniform constant.
∎

We state now the characterization of pluripolar sets in terms of the family of capacity that was used in the proof of Lemma
6.3
. This result is due to Fukushima and Okada
[
FO84
]
whose proof based on conformal martingale diffusion. Our proof uses only pluripotential theory.

Corollary 7.9
.

Let
E\subset\Omega
be a Borel subset. Then,
cap(E)=0
if and only if
{\tt c}_{\theta}(E)=0
for all
\theta
of the form (
7.9
) with
0<\delta\leq 1
.

Proof.

Since both capacities are Choquet’s capacities we may assume
E=K
is compact. To prove the sufficient condition assume that
cap(K)>0
. Lemma
7.8
implies immediately that
{\tt c}_{\theta}(K)>0
where
where
\theta=(dd^{c}u_{K}^{*}+\delta\omega)^{n-1}
.
To prove the necessary condition we assume
cap(K)=0
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be small. By outer regularity there exists an open subset
G\subset D\subset\subset\Omega

such that
K\subset G
and
cap(G)<\varepsilon
. Using Lemma
7.8
-(b) we infer
{\tt c}_{\theta}(G)\leq A_{0}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}
where

is independent of

.
Let

.
∎

8.
Exponential integrability

8.1.
Comparison between volume and capacity

In this section we give a simplification of a quantitative version of the exponential integrability in
[
DMV23
]
. Then, using this we obtain several equivalence statements on the comparison between the Lebesgue volume of a Borel set and its capacity.

Let
B_{R}:=B(0,R)
be the ball of radius
R>0
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
u\in PSH(B_{R})
. Denote by
W^{*}(B_{R},u)
the set of functions

f\in W^{*}(B_{R})
satisfying

(8.1)

df\wedge d^{c}f\leq dd^{c}u.

We have the following basic results for smooth functions in this family.

Lemma 8.1
.

Suppose
f\in W^{*}(B_{R},u)\cap C^{\infty}(B_{R})
and
u\in PSH\cap C^{\infty}(B_{R})
. Then, for
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
|\zeta|=R
, the function
\ell(\lambda)=f(\lambda\zeta)
belongs to
W^{*}\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
where
\mathbb{D}
is the unit disc in
\mathbb{C}
. Moreover,

\left|\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\lambda}\right|^{2}\leq\Delta_{\lambda}u(\lambda\zeta)/4.

Proof.

This follows from a simple calculation

\left|\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\lambda}\right|^{2}=\sum_{j,k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{j}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}_{k}}\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}\leq\sum_{j,k}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z}_{k}}\zeta^{j}\bar{\zeta}^{k}=\Delta_{\lambda}u(\lambda\zeta)/4,

where the second inequality used the fact that
df\wedge d^{c}f\leq dd^{c}u
.
∎

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
f(z),u(z)
and
\ell(\lambda)
be as in Lemma
8.1
. Assume further
u(0)=-M
with
M>0
. Denote by
\mathbb{D}_{r}:=\mathbb{D}(0,r)
the disc in
\mathbb{C}
for
0<r<e^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Then,

\|\nabla\ell\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{r})}\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}\Delta_{\lambda}u(\lambda\zeta)/4\leq\pi M.

Proof.

The first inequality follows from Lemma
8.1
. The second inequality follows from the Riesz representation formula for the subharmonic function
L(\lambda)=u(\lambda\zeta)/M
in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, see e.g., the proof of
[
Ho07
, Proposition 4.2.9]
.
∎

The next result is crucial for proving uniform exponential integrability.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
u\in PSH\cap C^{\infty}(B(0,1))
and
f\in W^{*}(u,B(0,\frac{3}{4}))\cap C^{\infty}(B(0,\frac{3}{4}))
with
f\leq 0
. Define

\kappa^{2}=\int_{B(0,\frac{3}{4})}|f|^{2}dx+\int_{B(0,\frac{3}{4})}dd^{c}u\wedge\omega^{n-1}.

Assume
u(0)=-M\kappa^{2}
. There exist uniform constants
A>0
and
\alpha>0
independent of
f
and
u
such that

\int_{B(0,\frac{1}{2})}e^{\frac{\alpha|f|^{2}}{M\kappa^{2}}}dx\leq A.

Proof.

By considering
f:=f/\kappa
and
u:=u/\kappa^{2}
, we may assume that

\kappa=1\quad\text{and}\quad u(0)=-M.

We start with the estimate of the function restricted on each complex line whose domain is the corresponding disc. Namely, for
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
|\zeta|=3/4
let us consider
\ell(\lambda)=f(\lambda\zeta)
as in Lemma
8.1
. Its average value on the disc
\mathbb{D}(0,r)
,
0<r<1
, is given by

(\ell)_{r}=\,\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\displaystyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\displaystyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\textstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\textstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}\ell(\lambda)dV_{2}(\lambda).

By Corollary
8.2
we have for
0<r<e^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}|\nabla\ell|^{2}dV_{2}\leq\pi M.

Applying Moser-Trudinger’s inequality in
\mathbb{D}_{r}\subset\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
for the function
\ell(\lambda)-(\ell)_{r}
of vanishing mean (see e.g.
[
Le05
]
), we get

(8.2)

\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}e^{\alpha_{0}|\ell(\lambda)-(\ell)_{r}|^{2}}dV_{2}(\lambda)\leq c_{0}.

where
\alpha_{0}=2/M
.

Let
0<\tau<\alpha_{0}
to be determined later. Next, we have for
\delta=(1+\sqrt{17})/4
,

a^{2}\leq(1+\delta)(a-b)^{2}+\frac{b^{2}}{2}\quad\forall\;a,b\geq 0

by an elementary inequality.
It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}e^{\tau|\ell(\lambda)|^{2}}dV_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}e^{\tau(1+\delta)|\ell-(\ell)_{r}|^{2}}e^{\frac{\tau}{2}|(\ell)_{r}|^{2}}dV_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}e^{2\tau(1+\delta)|\ell-(\ell)_{r}|^{2}}dV_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}e^{\tau|(\ell)_{r}|^{2}}dV_{2},

where we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the integrand in the second inequality. By symmetry,

\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}e^{\tau|(\ell)_{r}|^{2}}dV_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}e^{2\tau(1+\delta)|\ell-(\ell)_{r}|^{2}}dV_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}e^{\tau|\ell|^{2}}dV_{2},

The two inequalities above imply that

(8.3)

\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}e^{\tau|\ell(\lambda)|^{2}}dV_{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}e^{2\tau(1+\delta)|\ell-(\ell)_{r}|^{2}}dV_{2}.

Now we can choose

(8.4)

\tau=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2(1+\delta)}=\frac{4}{(5+\sqrt{17})M}=:\frac{\alpha}{M}.

Combining (
8.2
), (
8.3
) and (
8.4
) we obtain the bound

(8.5)

\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}e^{\tau|\ell(\lambda)|^{2}}dV_{2}\leq c_{0}.

Finally, we estimate the integral on
B(0,R)
by using the polar coordinates.

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}e^{\tau|f|^{2}}dV_{2n}

\displaystyle=\int_{|\zeta|=1}\int_{D_{R}}e^{\tau|f(\lambda\zeta)|^{2}}|\lambda|^{2n-2}dV_{2}(\lambda)dS(\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq R^{2n-2}\int_{|\zeta|=1}c_{0}R^{2}dS(\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq c_{0}R^{2n}\sigma_{2n-1}.

This completes the proof as we can take
R=1/2<e^{-1/2}
.
∎

We are ready to state the key component in the proof of
[
DMV23
, Theorem 1.2]
. By using Lemma
8.3
the proof does not use neither the induction argument nor slicing theory for currents.

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the unit ball. There exist positive constants
\alpha
and
A
such that for all
f\in W^{*}(B(0,1))
with
\|f\|_{*}\leq 1
,

\int_{\bar{B}(0,\frac{1}{8})}e^{\alpha|f|^{2}}dx\leq A.

Proof.

Let
T
be a positive closed
(1,1)
-current in
B:=B(0,1)
realizing
W^{*}
-norm of
f
, in particular,

df\wedge d^{c}f\leq T

and

\int_{B}|f|^{2}dx+\int_{B}T\wedge\omega^{n-1}\leq 1.

By a result of Skoda
[
Sk72
]
in Lemma
10.1
of the appendix (see also
[
Vu20
, Lemma 2.1]
) we may choose a psh potential
u
solving

T=dd^{c}u

such that
u\leq 0
in
B(0,3/4)
and

(8.6)

\int_{\bar{B}(0,\frac{1}{8})}|u|\;dx\leq A_{0}.

for a uniform constant
A_{0}
. Then,
f\in W^{*}(B(0,3/4),u)
.

Assume first that
f,u\in C^{\infty}(B(0,3/4))
.
Set
-M:=\sup_{\bar{B}(0,\frac{1}{8})}u<0
. Since
u
is subharmonic in
B
,
u(a)=-M
at some
a\in\partial B(0,1/8)
.
Clearly,

(8.7)

0<M\leq\,\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\displaystyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\displaystyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\textstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\textstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{B(0,\frac{1}{8})}|u|\;dx\leq\frac{A_{0}}{|B(0,1/8)|}=:M_{0}.

Thus,
M
is uniformly bounded from above by
M_{0}
.

Consider
v(z)=u(z+a)
and
g(z)=f(z+a)
.
Then,
dg\wedge d^{c}g\leq dd^{c}v
and
v(0)=-M
. By
Lemma
8.3
there exist uniform constants
\alpha_{1}>0
and
A_{1}>0
such that

\int_{\bar{B}(0,\frac{1}{4})}e^{\alpha_{1}|g|^{2}/M}dx\leq A_{1}.

Since
B(0,1/8)\subset B(a,1/4)
,

\int_{\bar{B}(0,\frac{1}{8})}e^{\alpha_{1}|f|^{2}/M}dx\leq\int_{\bar{B}(a,\frac{1}{4})}e^{\alpha_{1}|f|^{2}/M}dx=\int_{\bar{B}(0,\frac{1}{4})}e^{\alpha_{1}|g|^{2}/M}dx\leq A_{1}.

Set
\alpha=\alpha_{1}/M_{0}
, we complete the proof in the smooth case.

We now remove the smoothness assumptions on
f
and
u
. Denote
f_{\varepsilon}=f*\eta_{\varepsilon}
and
u_{\varepsilon}=u*\eta_{\varepsilon}
for small
\varepsilon>0
. Then, on

, they satisfy

Moreover, for

The argument in the first part shows

As

in

, we may assume the convergence is almost everywhere after passing to a subsequence. Hence, the conclusion follows from Fatou’s lemma.
∎

Corollary 8.5
.

Let
K\subset\subset\Omega
be compact subset. There exist positive constants
\alpha,A>0
depending only on

and

such that

for every

and

.

Proof.

Since
K
is compact, there is a finite covering by balls

such that

. By translation and dilation it is enough to estimate the volume of

\displaystyle V_{2n}\left(\{|f|\geq s\}\cap\bar{B}(0,1/8)\right)\leq\int_{\bar{B}(0,1/8)}e^{\alpha(|f|^{2}-s^{2})}dx\leq Ae^{-\alpha s^{2}}.

This is the desired estimate.
∎

Remark 8.6
.

Proof of Theorem

.

8.2.
The Alexander-Taylor type inequality

In this section we compare the new capacity
{\tt c}(\cdot)
and another classical capacity defined by means of extremal functions.

Let
B_{R}=B(0,R)
and
B_{r}:=B(0,r)
be the balls in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
centered at the origin with radius
0<r<R
. Let
K\subset B_{r}
be a compact set, then the Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function
U_{K}^{*}
is given by

U_{K}(z)=\sup\left\{v(z)\;\rvert\;v\in\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),\;v\leq 0\text{ on }K\right\}.

Here we recall that

(8.8)

\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\{v\in PSH(\mathbb{C}^{n}):\limsup_{z\to\infty}[v(z)-\log|z|]\leq O(1)\}

Denote
M_{K}:=\sup_{B_{R}}U_{K}^{*}
. We define another capacity by

(8.9)

T_{R}(K)=e^{-M_{K}}=e^{-\sup_{B_{R}}U_{K}^{*}}.

Lemma 8.10
.

There exist positive constants
A_{r}
and
A_{R}
depending on
r,R
respectively such that for every compact set
K\subset B_{r}
,

\exp(-A_{R}\;{\tt c}(K,B_{R})^{-1})\leq T_{R}(K)\leq\exp(-A_{r}\;{\tt c}(K,B_{R})^{-1/n}).

Equivalently,

(8.10)

\frac{1}{A_{r}}\frac{1}{M_{K}^{n}}\leq{\tt c}(K,B_{R})\leq\frac{A_{R}}{M_{K}}.

Proof.

The Bedford-Taylor capacity satisfies
cap(K,B_{R})\geq 1/M_{K}^{n}
by
[
AT84
, Theorem 2.1]
. The first inequality in (
8.10
) follows readily from the fact in Lemma
4.7
that

cap(K,B_{R})\leq A(r){\tt c}(K,B_{R})
for some constant
A(r)
. To prove the second inequality we follows closely
[
De89
, Theorem 11.1]
. Consider
B^{\prime}:=B(0,eR)\supset\supset B_{R}
. Put
M=\sup_{B_{R}}U_{K}
. We have
U_{K}^{*}\leq M+1
on
B^{\prime}
by log-convexity. Define

u:=\frac{U_{K}^{*}-M-1}{M+1}.

Clearly,
-1\leq u\leq 0
on
B^{\prime}
and
u=-1
quasi-everywhere on
K
.
By definition of capacity and Lemma
6.2
,

{\tt c}(K,B_{R})\leq\|u\|_{W^{*}(B_{R})}^{2}.

It remains to estimate the norm
\|u\|_{*}
on
B_{R}
.

Now, there exists
z_{0}\in\bar{B}_{R}
such that
U_{K}^{*}(z_{0})=M
, hence
u(z_{0})=-1/(M+1)
.
As
u\leq 0
in
B^{\prime}
the mean value inequality and Harnack inequality show that

u(z_{0})\leq A_{1}\int_{B^{\prime}}u\omega^{n},

where
A_{1}=A(R)
. This implies

\|u\|_{L^{1}(B^{\prime})}\leq-\frac{u(z_{0})}{A_{1}}\leq\frac{A_{2}}{M}.

Using the fact
|u|\leq 1
we get

\int_{B_{R}}u^{2}\omega^{n}\leq\|u\|_{L^{1}(B^{\prime})}\leq\frac{A_{2}}{M}.

Let
\chi
be a cut-off function such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
B_{R}
and
\mbox{supp }\chi\subset B^{\prime}
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}dd^{c}(1+u)^{2}\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B^{\prime}}\chi dd^{c}(1+u)^{2}\wedge\omega^{m-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{\prime}}(1+u)^{2}dd^{c}\chi\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{\prime}}[2u+u^{2}]dd^{c}\chi\wedge\omega^{n-1}.

The first term in the bracket is estimated as follows:

\int_{B^{\prime}}2udd^{c}\chi\wedge\omega^{n-1}\leq A_{0}\int_{B^{\prime}}|u|\omega^{n}\leq\frac{A_{0}A_{2}}{M}.

The second one is less than

A_{0}\int_{B^{\prime}}u^{2}\omega^{n}\leq A_{0}\int_{B^{\prime}}|u|\omega^{n}\leq\frac{A_{0}A_{2}}{M}.

Therefore,

\int_{B_{R}}dd^{c}(1+u)^{2}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\leq\frac{2A_{0}A_{2}}{M}.

Since

du\wedge d^{c}u\leq dd^{c}(1+u)^{2}
, it follows that
{\tt c}(K,B_{R})\leq 2(1+A_{0})A_{2}/M.

∎

Now we can conclude the sharpness of exponents of inequalities between capacities in the introduction.

Remark 8.11

(Proof of the last statement in Theorem
1.1
)
.

The Alexander-Taylor inequality
[
AT84
]
reads

\exp(-A_{r}/cap(K,\Omega))\leq T_{R}(K)\leq\exp(-2\pi/[cap(K,\Omega)]^{\frac{1}{n}}),

where the exponents in both inequalities are sharp (
[
AT84
, Remark 2]
).
Together with
cap(K)\leq A_{r}{\tt c}(K)
in Lemma
4.7

and the first inequality in Lemma

we derive

with the sharp exponents. So are the ones in Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.7
.

(9.1)

) that the limit exists almost everywhere. Hence,
we can define for

its

as follows.

(9.2)

f^{\star}(x)\equiv\begin{cases}\lim_{r\to 0}\,\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\displaystyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\displaystyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\textstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\textstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{B(x,r)}fdy&\quad\text{if this limit exits,}\\
=0&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that if
f=g
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, then
f^{\star}=g^{\star}
for all points
x\in\Omega
. Moreover, if
f\in W^{*}(\Omega)
and

a.e., then

is also in

. Thus, to study the fine properties of

we need to turn our attention to its precise representative

.

Thanks to Alexander-Taylor’s type inequality in Section
8.2
we construct a psh subextension for a given element in
W^{*}(\Omega)
. This result is similar to El Mir’s theorem
[
E79
]
in pluripotential theory. Such a subextension has been obtained recently by Vigny and Vu
[
VV24
]
.
Compared to
[
VV24
]
our psh subextension has a smaller exponent, however, it is a function in the Lelong class (
8.8
) (minimal growth at infinity) which satisfies the inequality everywhere. The proof given below is also somewhat simpler.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
f\in W^{*}(B(0,1))
be such that
f\leq-1
. Assume
\|f\|_{*}\leq 1
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
0<s<1
. Then, there exists
u\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that
u\leq-|f^{\star}|^{\frac{2}{n}-\varepsilon}
on
B(0,s)
everywhere.

Proof.

Assume first
f
is continuous. We follows closely the proof in
[
De89
, Theorem 11.4]
by Demailly.
As
f\leq-1
, it follows that
\|f\|_{*}>0
. For
t\geq-1
, set
G_{t}=\{z\in B(0,s):f(z)<-t\}
and let
U_{t}^{*}\in\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
be the Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function of
G_{t}
. Since
G_{t}
is open,
U_{t}^{*}=0
on
G_{t}
. Denote
M(t)=\sup_{B(0,1)}U_{t}^{*}
and

u(z)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-1-\varepsilon}\left[U_{t}^{*}-M(t)\right]dt.

We have from Lemma
5.5
that

{\tt c}(G_{t})\leq\|f\|_{*}^{2}/t^{2}.

Therefore, by the Alexander-Taylor type inequality (
8.10
),

M(t)\geq\frac{a_{1}}{\left[{\tt c}(G_{t})\right]^{1/n}}\geq a_{1}t^{2/n},

where we used the fact
a_{1}/\|f\|_{*}^{\frac{2}{n}}\geq a_{1}>0
in the second inequality. Here
a_{1}>0
is possibly a small number. Since
U_{t}^{*}-M(t)\leq 0
on
B(0,1)
, it follows from the logarithmic convexity that

U_{t}^{*}-M(t)\leq\log_{+}|z|
. Hence,

u(z)\leq\log_{+}|z|.

Now, for
z\in B(0,s)\cap G_{t}
, we have
U_{t}^{*}=0
. Hence,

u(z)\leq-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{1}^{|f(z)|}t^{-1-\varepsilon}M(t)dt\leq-\frac{a_{1}}{\varepsilon}\int_{1}^{|f(z)|}t^{-1-\varepsilon+2/n}dt.

A direct computation of the last integral yields

u(z)\leq-\frac{a_{1}}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{2}{n}-\varepsilon\right)|f(z)|^{\frac{2}{n}-\varepsilon}+\frac{a_{1}}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{2}{n}-\varepsilon\right).

Therefore, we may start with a smaller value of
\varepsilon
if necessary and subtracting a constant from
u
we will get that

u\leq-|f|^{\frac{2}{n}-\varepsilon}\quad\text{ on }B(0,s).

It remains to verify that
u
is not identically
-\infty
. By the logarithmic convexity again, we have

\sup_{\bar{B}(0,1/2)}U_{t}^{*}\geq M(t)-\log 2.

Therefore, there exists
z_{0}\in S(0,1/2)
such that
U_{t}^{*}(z_{0})-M(t)\geq-\log 2
. The Harnack inequality shows that

\frac{1-1/4}{(1+1/2)^{2n}}\int_{\partial B(0,1)}(U_{t}^{*}-M(t))d\sigma(z)\geq U_{t}^{*}(z_{0})-M(t)\geq-\log 2.

Integrating this with respect to
t
we get

\int_{\partial B(0,1)}u(z)d\sigma(z\geq-\frac{4}{3}(3/2)^{2n}\log 2.

Now, assume
f\in W^{*}(B(0,1))
in general. We consider the continuous approximation
f_{r}(x)=(f)_{x,r}
defined in (
2.14
). Choose
r>0
small so that
B(0,s)\subset\subset B(0,1-r)
. The previous step gives
u_{r}\in\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
satisfying
u_{r}\leq-|f_{r}|^{\frac{1}{n}-\varepsilon}
on
B(0,s)
.
Define on
B(0,1)
the function

u=\left(\limsup_{r\to 0}u_{r}\right)^{*}.

By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem there exists a set
E\subset B
whose
|E|=0
such that
\lim_{r\to 0}(f)_{r,x}=f^{\star}(x)
. Thus, for
x\in B\setminus E
, we have
u(x)\leq-|f^{\star}(x)|^{\frac{1}{n}-\varepsilon}.
On the other hand, if
x\in E
, then
u(x)\leq 0=f^{\star}(x)
by definition.
∎

1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{B(x,r)}\widetilde{f}dy=\widetilde{f}(x).

Indeed, the first identity follows from the fact that
f=\widetilde{f}
a.e in
B(0,1)
. To prove the second identity we use the Cartan’s theorem relating the fine and ordinary limits (
[
BT87
, Theorem 2.4]
and
[
Br71
, Theorem III, 2.]
). To this end let
V
be a pluri-fine open neighborhood of
x
. It follows from
[
BT87
, Theorem 2.3]
that we can assume

V=\{|z-x|<a\}\cap\{v>0\}

for some constant
a>0
and
v
is psh in
B(0,1)
such that
v(x)>0
. Then, Cartan’s theorem reads

\lim_{\\
\begin{subarray}{c}z\to x\\
z\in V\end{subarray}}\widetilde{f}(z)=\widetilde{f}(x).

By Lebesgue’s theorem
f^{\star}=f
a.e and
|f^{\star}|\leq|u|^{2n}
in (
9.3
). So,
|\widetilde{f}|\leq|u|^{2n}
almost everywhere. Hence, Lemma
9.4
-(b) implies

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)\cap\{v\leq 0\}}\widetilde{f}dy=0.

These combined with Lemma
9.4
-(
a
) implies

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}\widetilde{f}dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)\cap\{v>0\}|}\int_{B(x,r)\cap\{v>0\}}\widetilde{f}dy

\displaystyle=\widetilde{f}(x),

Thus, (
9.6
) is proved.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\,\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\displaystyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\displaystyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\textstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\textstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{B(x,r)}|f-f^{\star}(x)|\;dy

\displaystyle=\,\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\displaystyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\displaystyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\textstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\textstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{B(x,r)}|\widetilde{f}-f^{\star}(x)|\;dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)\cap V}|\widetilde{f}-f^{\star}(x)|\;dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)\setminus V}|\widetilde{f}-f^{\star}(x)|\;dy.

The first integral on the right hand side goes to zero as
r\to 0
by the fine continuity of
\widetilde{f}
at
x
and
\widetilde{f}(x)=f^{\star}(x)
. The second integral is bound by

\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)\setminus V}(|u|^{2n}+|u(x)|^{2n})dy

which tends to zero as
r\to 0
by Lemma
9.4
.
This completes the proof of (i).

Next, to prove (ii) we note that for
x\in B(0,1)\setminus E
,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}

\displaystyle\left(\,\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\displaystyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\displaystyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\textstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\textstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{B(x,r)}|f-f^{\star}(x)|^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{r\to 0}|(f)_{x,r}-f^{\star}(x)|+\lim_{r\to 0}\left(\,\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\displaystyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\displaystyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\textstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\textstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{B(x,r)}|f-(f)_{x,r}|^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=0,

where we recall that
f_{r}(x)=(f)_{x,r}=\,\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\displaystyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\displaystyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\textstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-2.77774pt\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\textstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.77774pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-7.49994pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.72743pt\hskip-1.72743pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.72743pt\hskip-2.45831pt\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.45831pt\hskip 1.72743pt}\kern-6.78813pt}{\hbox{\hskip 1.57988pt\hskip-1.57988pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-1.57988pt\hskip-2.29166pt\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.0pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}$}}\hskip-2.29166pt\hskip 1.57988pt}\kern-6.49301pt}\int_{B(x,r)}fdy
.

Lastly, we prove the quasi-continuity of
f^{\star}
. According to Remark
5.9
the sequence
\{f_{r_{j}}\}_{r_{j}}
with
r_{j}=1/j
is a Cauchy sequence in capacity. There exists a subsequence converges point-wise to a quasicontinuous function
g
. Moreover, given
\varepsilon>0
there is an open set
U\subset\Omega
with
{\tt c}(U)<\varepsilon
(Theorem
5.10
) and the subsequence converges uniformly to
g
on
\Omega\setminus U
. Since
f_{r_{j}}(x)\to f^{\star}(x)
on
\Omega\setminus E
by (i), it follows that
g(x)=f^{\star}(x)
on
\Omega\setminus E
. In other words,
f^{\star}
is quasi-continuous.
∎

10.
Appendix

In this section we give details of the following result due to Skoda
[
Sk72
]
which was needed in the proof of Proposition
8.4
. Let
T
be a positive closed
(1,1)
-current in the unit ball
B(0,1)
. Define
\beta=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}|z|^{2}
and assume that

\int_{B(0,1)}T\wedge\beta^{n-1}\leq 1.

Lemma 10.1
.

There exists a psh potential
U(z)\leq 0
in
B(0,3/4)
such that

dd^{c}U=T

and
moreover, there exist uniform constants
c_{0},A>0
independent of
U
(which are explicit computable) satisfying

\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{8}}\exp\left[-\frac{U(z)}{c_{0}}\right]dz\leq A.

Proof.

The first statement in the lemma is classical which can be obtained by various means. However, the extra property of uniform exponential integrability need to be carefully examined in the construction given in Skoda
[
Sk72
, Section 7]
. By adding to
U
a strictly psh function
\rho(z)=|z|^{2}-1
we may assume that

(10.1)

T\geq\beta\quad\text{in }B(0,1).

Assume
0\leq\eta\leq 1
is a cut-off function such that
\eta=1
on
\bar{B}(0,3/4)
and
\mbox{supp }\eta\subset B(0,1)
. For simplicity we keep the notations in
[
Sk72
]
by writing
p=n-1
and define

U(z)=-\int|z-x|^{-2p}\eta(x)\;T(x)\wedge\beta^{p}.

By computation in
[
Sk72
, Section 3]
or
[
Ho07
, Lemma 4.3.4]
we have

T(z)=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}U(z)\quad\text{on }B(0,3/4).

Next, we verify the uniformly exponential integrability on
\bar{B}(0,1/8)
of
U(z)
. To this end we
denote
\alpha=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\log|z|^{2}
and
E(z)=\log|z|^{2}
. The identity
[
Sk72
, Eq. (7.13)]
reads

\displaystyle U(z)

\displaystyle=p\int E(x-z)\alpha^{p}\wedge\eta(x)T(x)

\displaystyle-\int\sqrt{-1}\;\overline{\partial}_{x}E(x-z)\wedge\alpha^{p-1}\wedge\partial_{x}\eta\wedge T

\displaystyle-p\int\sqrt{-1}E\,\overline{\partial}_{x}E\wedge\alpha^{p-1}\wedge\partial_{x}\eta\wedge T.

If
z\in\bar{B}(0,1/4)
, then the last two integrals are smooth functions as
\eta=1
on
\bar{B}(0,3/4)
. More precisely,

|\partial_{x}E(x-z)|\leq\frac{A_{n}}{|x-z|},\quad|\partial_{x}\bar{\partial}_{x}E(x-z)|\leq\frac{A_{n}}{|x-z|^{2}}.

Hence, for
z\in\bar{B}(0,1/4)
these two integrals are uniformly bounded by an absolute constant
A_{0}
. It remains to estimate

U_{3}(z)=\frac{p}{2^{p}\pi^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\log|z-x|^{2}\alpha^{p}(z-x)\wedge\eta(x)T(x).

We can yet replace
U_{3}(z)
by

U_{4}(z)=\frac{p}{2^{p}\pi^{p}}\int_{|x|\leq R}\log|z-x|^{2}\alpha^{p}(z-x)\wedge T(x)

for
z\in\bar{B}(0,1/4)
and
1/2\leq R<3/4

because their difference is uniformly bounded on
\bar{B}(0,1/4)
.
Put

(10.2)

\mu(z)=\int_{|x|\leq R}\alpha^{p}(z-x)\wedge T(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa(z)=\frac{\mu(z)}{2^{p}\pi^{p}}\frac{n}{c}.

We rewrite

-\frac{n}{pc}U_{4}(z)=\int_{|x|\leq R}\log[|z-x|^{-\kappa(z)}]\frac{\alpha^{p}(z-x)\wedge T(x)}{\mu(z)}.

Using the Jensen inequality and concavity of the logarithmic function we get

(10.3)

-\frac{n}{pc}U_{4}(z)\leq\log\left[\int_{|x|\leq R}|z-x|^{-2\kappa(z)}\frac{\alpha^{p}(z-x)\wedge T(x)}{\mu(z)}\right],

Therefore, for
r=1/8
and
R=1/2
we have

(10.4)

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{8}}\exp\left[-\frac{n}{pc}U_{4}(z)\right]dz

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{8},|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}}|z-x|^{-2\kappa(z)}\frac{\alpha^{p}(z-x)\wedge T(x)}{\mu(z)}.

We need to estimate
\mu(z)
and
\kappa(z)
from both above and below. Recall that
the trace measure and projective measures are given by

\sigma=\frac{1}{2^{p}p!}T(x)\wedge\beta^{p},\quad\nu=\frac{1}{2^{p}\pi^{p}}T(x)\wedge\alpha^{p}.

Define the function

\sigma(r)=\int_{|x|<r}d\sigma(x).\quad

An important result of Lelong says that

r\mapsto\sigma(B(z,r))/r^{2p}
is increasing in
[0,1]
(see, e.g., Demailly
[
De12
, Chapter III, Consequence 5.8.]
). Hence,
the limit

\nu(z)=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{r^{2p}}\int_{|x-z|<r}T(x)\wedge\beta^{p}=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\sigma(B(z,r)}{r^{2p}\pi^{p}/p!}

exists and it is called the Lelong number of
T
at
z
. By
[
Sk72
, Eq. (1.17)]
we have

(10.5)

\nu(z)+\int_{0<|x-z|<r}T(x)\wedge\alpha^{n-1}(x-z)=\frac{1}{2^{p}r^{p}}\int_{|x-z|<r}T(x)\wedge\beta^{n-1}.

This gives for
|z|\leq r
,

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-p}\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-p}\int_{|z-x|\leq r+R}\alpha^{p}(z-x)\wedge T(x)

\displaystyle\leq\pi^{-p}p!(r+R)^{-2p}\int_{|x-z|\leq r+R}d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\leq\pi^{-p}p!(r+R)^{-2p}\int_{|x|\leq 2r+R}d\sigma(x).

In other words,

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-p}\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2r+R}{r+R}\right)^{2p}\frac{\sigma(2r+R)}{(2r+R)^{2p}}.

Thus, we get for
|z|\leq r=\frac{1}{8}
and
R=\frac{1}{2}
,

(10.6)

(2\pi)^{-p}\mu(z)\leq 2^{2p}\sigma(3/4)=:\frac{c(1-\varepsilon)}{n},

where
0<\varepsilon<1/2
is fixed.

Next, we estimate
\mu(z)
from below. If
\nu(z)=0
, (
10.5
) implies that for
|z|\leq r
,

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-p}\int_{|z-x|\leq R-r}\alpha^{p}(z-x)\wedge T(x)

\displaystyle=\pi^{-p}p!(R-r)^{-2p}\int_{|x-z|\leq R-r}d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\geq\pi^{-p}p!(R-r)^{-2p}\int_{|x|\leq R-2r}d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-p}(R-r)^{-2p}\int_{|x|\leq R-2r}T(x)\wedge\beta^{p}.

Plugging
r=\frac{1}{8}
and
R=\frac{1}{2}
we get that for
|z|\leq\frac{1}{8}
and
\nu(z)=0
,

(10.7)

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-p}\mu(z)

\displaystyle\geq(2\pi)^{-p}(3/8)^{-2p}\int_{|x|\leq\frac{1}{4}}\beta^{n}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-p}(3/8)^{-2p}2^{n}\pi^{n}(1/4)^{n}

\displaystyle=:c_{1}.

By [Sk72, Lemme 7.1] the set
\{z:\nu(z)>0\}
has zero measure as
U(z)
is locally integrable (or simply by Siu’s theorem
[
Si74
]
). Hence, (
10.7
) holds a.e.

Combining (
10.4
), (
10.6
) and (
10.7
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{8}}\exp\left[-\frac{n}{pc}U_{4}(z)\right]dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{p}c_{1}}\iint_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{8},|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}}|z-x|^{-2(n-p-\varepsilon)}\alpha^{p}(z-x)\wedge T(x).

Since the coefficients of
\alpha^{p}(z-x)
are dominated by

A(n,p)|z-x|^{-2p},

we have

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{8}}\exp\left[-\frac{n}{pc}U_{4}(z)\right]dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A(n,p)}{(2\pi)^{p}c_{1}}\iint_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{8},|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}}|z-x|^{-2n+2\varepsilon}d\sigma(x)dz

\displaystyle=A_{1}.

The proof is completed with
c_{0}=pc/n
.
∎

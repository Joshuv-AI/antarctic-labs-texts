Abstract
. We present a noncommutative analogue of Błaszczyk’s elegant two-step construction of the Gleason cover: first maximise irreducibility, then compactify . The key insight is that extremal disconnectedness (respectively, injectivity) emerges naturally from a maximality principle rather than being imposed by hand. We work with A A -multiplier covers : non-unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebras E E equipped with a faithful, nondegenerate ∗ * -homomorphism ι : A → M ⁡ ( E ) \iota:A\to M(E) . Covers are ordered by A A -preserving ucp \mathrm{ucp} maps between multiplier algebras in the forward direction. A cover is rigid if every ucp \mathrm{ucp} endomorphism of M ⁡ ( E ) M(E) fixing A A pointwise is the identity. We prove that if ( E max , ι max ) (E_{\max},\iota_{\max}) is maximal among rigid covers, then the inclusion A ↪ M ⁡ ( E max ) A\hookrightarrow M(E_{\max}) is rigid and essential; consequently, I ⁡ ( A ) ≅ M ⁡ ( E max ) I(A)\ \cong\ M(E_{\max}) canonically over A A by Hamana’s characterisation. In the commutative case A = C ⁡ ( X ) A=C(X) , we recover Błaszczyk’s picture: for a maximal regular refinement ( X , T ∗ ) (X,T^{\ast}) with irreducible identity, G ⁡ ( X ) = β ⁡ ( X , T ∗ ) G(X)=\beta(X,T^{\ast}) and I ⁡ ( C ⁡ ( X ) ) ≅ C ⁡ ( G ⁡ ( X ) ) ≅ M ⁡ ( C 0 ​ ( U ) ) I(C(X))\cong C(G(X))\cong M(C_{0}(U)) for any dense open U ⊆ G ⁡ ( X ) U\subseteq G(X) . Thus the paradigm maximise first, then compactify provides a unified conceptual framework on both sides of Gelfand duality.

Introduction

The injective envelope of a
C^{*}
-algebra, introduced by Hamana in his foundational papers
[
5
,
6
]
, has become a central tool in noncommutative analysis. For a compact Hausdorff
X
, injectivity on the commutative side amounts to extremal disconnectedness:
I(C(X))\cong C(K)
with
K
extremally disconnected. Gleason’s classical construction
[
4
]
of
K
is intricate; Błaszczyk
[
1
]
later gave a strikingly concise route: first
maximise
the regular topology under an irreducibility constraint, then
compactify
to obtain
G(X)
. Indeed, in Błaszczyk argument, maximality is the driver and extremal disconnectedness is the consequence.

Our aim is to transpose this to the noncommutative setting. The multiplier algebra
M(E)
is the natural analogue of the Čech–Stone compactification:
M(C_{0}(Y))\cong C(\beta Y)
. We introduce
A
-multiplier covers

(E,\iota)
and a rigidity notion paralleling Hamana’s. The punchline is that a
maximal rigid
cover forces
M(E)
to be a rigid essential extension of
A
, hence identifies canonically with
I(A)
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hamana’s injective envelope

An operator system
\mathsf{E}
is
injective
if any
\mathrm{ucp}
map
\phi:\mathcal{S}\to\mathsf{E}
from an operator system
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
extends to a
\mathrm{ucp}
map
\tilde{\phi}:\mathcal{T}\to\mathsf{E}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For every operator system
A
there exists an injective operator system
I(A)
and a unital complete order embedding
j:A\hookrightarrow I(A)
such that:

(a)

if
A\subseteq\mathsf{E}\subseteq I(A)
with
\mathsf{E}
injective, then
\mathsf{E}=I(A)
;

(b)

if
\Phi:I(A)\to I(A)
is
\mathrm{ucp}
and
\Phi|_{A}=\mathrm{id}
, then
\Phi=\mathrm{id}
.

Moreover,
I(A)
is unique up to a unique complete order isomorphism over
A
. If
A
is a
C^{\ast}
-algebra, then
I(A)
is a
C^{\ast}
-algebra and
j
is a
*
-monomorphism.

Equivalently,
I(A)
is the unique
rigid essential
extension of
A
: whenever
q:I(A)\to\mathsf{F}
is a complete quotient map and
q|_{A}
is a complete isometry, then
q
is injective.

2.2.
Multiplier algebras and quotienting ideals

If
E
is a (not necessarily unital)
C^{\ast}
-algebra, its multiplier algebra
M(E)
is the
C^{\ast}
-algebra of double centralisers; see Busby
[
2
]
or Pedersen
[
8
, Ch. 3]
. If
Y
is locally compact Hausdorff, then
M(C_{0}(Y))\cong C_{b}(Y)\cong C(\beta Y)
.

We shall use the following standard fact.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
E
be a
C^{\ast}
-algebra and
I\lhd M(E)
a closed two-sided ideal. Put

K\ :=\ \overline{IE+EI}\ \lhd\ E.

Then the quotient map
q_{K}:E\to E/K
induces a
surjective

*
-homomorphism

\pi_{K}:\ M(E)\longrightarrow M(E/K)

whose kernel contains
I
. Conversely, for a closed ideal
K\lhd E
, the strict extension
M(E)\twoheadrightarrow M(E/K)
has kernel
\{m\in M(E):mE\subseteq K,\ Em\subseteq K\}
.

Proof.

See
[
8
, §3.12]
and
[
2
]
. For
x\in I
one has
xE,Ex\subseteq K
, hence
x\in\ker\pi_{K}
. The converse kernel description is the usual characterisation of multipliers vanishing modulo
K
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

If
B
is unital and
J\lhd B
, the canonical map
B\to M(J)
given by left/right multiplication is a surjective
*
-homomorphism with kernel
\mathrm{Ann}(J):=\{b\in B:bJ=Jb=0\}
; hence

M(J)\ \cong\ B/\mathrm{Ann}(J).

Thus
M(J)=B
iff
J
is
essential
in
B
. In particular, even if
B
is a
W^{*}
-algebra (e.g. a product of factors),
M(J)
need not be
W^{*}
, because
\mathrm{Ann}(J)
need not be weak
∗
-closed. This is consistent with Hamana’s examples where
I(A)
is monotone complete but not von Neumann
[
7
]
.

3.
Multiplier covers and maximality

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. An
A
-multiplier cover
is a pair
(E,\iota)
where
E
is a (not necessarily unital)
C^{*}
-algebra and
\iota:A\to M(E)
is a faithful, nondegenerate
*
-homomorphism; we identify
A
with
\iota(A)\subseteq M(E)
. The cover is
rigid
if every
\mathrm{ucp}
map
\Phi:M(E)\to M(E)
with
\Phi|_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
equals the identity.

Given two
A
-multiplier covers
(E_{1},\iota_{1})
and
(E_{2},\iota_{2})
, we write

(E_{1},\iota_{1})\ \preccurlyeq\ (E_{2},\iota_{2})

if there exists an
A
-preserving
\mathrm{ucp}
map
\Theta:M(E_{1})\to M(E_{2})
such that
\Theta\circ\iota_{1}=\iota_{2}
. A rigid cover maximal with respect to
\preccurlyeq
is called a
maximal rigid
A
-multiplier cover
.

Intrinsic existence via minimal
A^{1}
-projections

Throughout this subsection we embed
A
faithfully and nondegenerately into
\mathcal{B}(H)
and write
A^{1}
for its unitisation inside
\mathcal{B}(H)
. By an
A^{1}
-map
we mean a
unital
completely positive map fixing
A^{1}
pointwise. An
A^{1}
-projection
is an idempotent
A^{1}
-map. We endow the space of maps
\mathcal{B}(H)\to\mathcal{B}(H)
with the point-ultraweak topology. In what follows, the only continuity we use is:

\Phi_{\alpha}\to\Phi\ \ \text{point-ultraweak}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Phi_{\alpha}\circ T\ \to\ \Phi\circ T\ \ \text{point-ultraweak}

for each bounded linear
T:\mathcal{B}(H)\to\mathcal{B}(H)
; this is automatic because
(\Phi\mapsto\Phi\circ T)
is continuous when evaluated on fixed vectors.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\{P_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a
\preccurlyeq
-decreasing chain of
A^{1}
-projections on
\mathcal{B}(H)
, i.e. for
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
,

P_{\lambda}\circ P_{\lambda_{0}}\;=\;P_{\lambda}\;=\;P_{\lambda_{0}}\circ P_{\lambda}.

Then there exists a ucp map
P_{\infty}:\mathcal{B}(H)\to\mathcal{B}(H)
with
P_{\infty}|_{A^{1}}=\mathrm{id}_{A^{1}}
such that, for every
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda
,

P_{\infty}\circ P_{\lambda_{0}}\;=\;P_{\infty}\;=\;P_{\lambda_{0}}\circ P_{\infty}.

In particular,
P_{\infty}
is a lower bound of the chain for the order
\preccurlyeq
.

Proof.

Equip the space of maps
\mathcal{B}(H)\to\mathcal{B}(H)
with the point-ultraweak topology. By compactness of the unit ball of completely bounded maps, a subnet of
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
converges point-ultraweakly to a ucp map
P_{\infty}
; pointwise convergence preserves
P_{\infty}|_{A^{1}}=\mathrm{id}_{A^{1}}
. Fix
\lambda_{0}
and
x\in\mathcal{B}(H)
. Using
P_{\lambda}\circ P_{\lambda_{0}}=P_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
,

P_{\infty}\big(P_{\lambda_{0}}x\big)\;=\;\lim_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}\big(P_{\lambda_{0}}x\big)\;=\;\lim_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(x)\;=\;P_{\infty}(x),

so
P_{\infty}\circ P_{\lambda_{0}}=P_{\infty}
. A symmetric limit using
P_{\lambda_{0}}\circ P_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}
gives
P_{\lambda_{0}}\circ P_{\infty}=P_{\infty}
. Hence
P_{\infty}
is a lower bound.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists a minimal
A^{1}
–projection
P:\mathcal{B}(H)\to\mathcal{B}(H)
for the order

\Phi\preccurlyeq\Psi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Phi\circ\Psi=\Phi=\Psi\circ\Phi\quad(\text{equivalently, }\Ran\Phi\subseteq\Ran\Psi).

Moreover, any such minimal
P
is idempotent:
P^{2}=P
.

Proof.

Apply the Kuratowski–Zorn lemma to the poset of
A^{1}
–projections with the order
\preccurlyeq
. By Lemma
3.2
, every decreasing chain has a lower bound, hence a minimal element
P
exists. For idempotence, observe that
P\circ P
is an
A^{1}
–projection and
\Ran(P\circ P)\subseteq\Ran(P)
,
i.e.

P\circ P\preccurlyeq P
. By minimality,
P\circ P=P
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
P
be minimal and let
\Xi
be any
A^{1}
-projection. Then
P\circ\Xi\circ P=P
.

Proof.

Put
F:=P\circ\Xi\circ P
. Then
F
is an
A^{1}
-projection and
F\preccurlyeq P
, so by minimality
F=P
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
P
be minimal and
R:=P(\mathcal{B}(H))
(an operator system). If
Q:R\to R
is a ucp idempotent with
Q|_{A^{1}}=\mathrm{id}
, then
Q=\mathrm{id}_{R}
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa:R\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}(H)
be the inclusion and set
\widetilde{Q}:=\kappa\circ Q\circ P
. Then
\widetilde{Q}
is an
A^{1}
-projection, so by Lemma
3.4
,

P\ =\ P\circ\widetilde{Q}\circ P\ =\ P\circ\kappa\circ Q\circ P\ =\ Q\circ P,

using
P\circ\kappa=\mathrm{id}_{R}
and
P^{2}=P
. For
r=P(x)\in R
we get
Q(r)=Q(P(x))=P(x)=r
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

With
P
and
R=P(\mathcal{B}(H))
as above, every ucp map
\theta:R\to R
with
\theta|_{A^{1}}=\mathrm{id}
equals
\mathrm{id}_{R}
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa:R\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}(H)
be inclusion and set
\Xi:=\kappa\circ\theta\circ P
. Then
\Xi
is an
A^{1}
-projection and by Lemma
3.4
,

P\ =\ P\circ\Xi\circ P\ =\ P\circ\kappa\circ\theta\circ P\ =\ \theta\circ P.

Thus for
r=P(x)\in R
we have
\theta(r)=\theta(P(x))=P(x)=r
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

If
C
is a
C^{\ast}
-algebra and
P:C\to C
is a ucp idempotent, then
\Ran P
becomes a
C^{\ast}
-algebra with product
x\odot y:=P(xy)
and the inherited involution, and the inclusion
\Ran P\hookrightarrow C
is a complete order isomorphism.

Proof.

This is the Choi-Effros theorem
[
3
, Thm. 3.1]
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
P
be minimal and equip
E:=P(\mathcal{B}(H))
with the Choi-Effros product. Then:

(a)

E
is a
C^{\ast}
-algebra and
A
embeds as a
C^{\ast}
-subalgebra (the product on
A
agrees with the original one).

(b)

The inclusion
A\hookrightarrow E
is rigid.

(c)

The operator system
E
is injective.

(d)

The inclusion
A\hookrightarrow E
is essential in the quotient sense: if
q:E\to F
is a complete quotient and
q|_{A}
is a complete isometry, then
q
is injective.

Proof.

(a) For
a,b\in A^{1}
,
a\odot b=P(ab)=ab
as
P|_{A^{1}}=\mathrm{id}
. In particular, the restriction to
A
is a
*
-homomorphism.

(b) This is Proposition
3.6
applied to
R=E
.

(c) Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
be operator systems and
u:\mathcal{S}\to E
ucp. Compose
u
with the inclusion
E\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}(H)
and extend to
\tilde{u}:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{B}(H)
by injectivity of
\mathcal{B}(H)
. Then
P\circ\tilde{u}:\mathcal{T}\to E
is a ucp extension of
u
.

(d) Let
q:E\to F
be a complete quotient with
q|_{A}
completely isometric. Let
\sigma_{0}:q(A)\to E
be the composition
(q|_{A})^{-1}:q(A)\to A
followed by the inclusion
A\hookrightarrow E
; then
\sigma_{0}
is ucp. By (c),
\sigma_{0}
extends to a ucp map
\sigma:F\to E
with
\sigma\circ q|_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
. Hence
\sigma\circ q:E\to E
is ucp and fixes
A
, so by (b) it equals
\mathrm{id}_{E}
. Thus
q
is injective.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

For every
C^{\ast}
-algebra
A
there exists a maximal rigid
A
-multiplier cover in the order of Definition
3.1
.

Proof.

Construct
E:=P(\mathcal{B}(H))
via a minimal
A^{1}
-projection and endow it with the Choi-Effros product as before. By Proposition
3.8
,
A\subseteq E
is rigid and essential, and
E
is injective as an operator system. Let
J\triangleleft E
be an essential ideal (take
J=E
if
E
is unital). Then
M(J)\cong E
(Remark
2.3
), and the inclusion
\iota:A\hookrightarrow M(J)
yields a rigid
A
-multiplier cover
(J,\iota)
.

We show
(J,\iota)
is maximal. Let
(F,\jmath)
be any rigid
A
-multiplier cover with

(J,\iota)\ \preccurlyeq\ (F,\jmath),

so there exists an
A
-preserving
\mathrm{ucp}
map
\Theta:M(J)\to M(F)
with
\Theta\circ\iota=\jmath
.
Since
M(J)\cong E
is injective as an operator system, there is an
A
-preserving
\mathrm{ucp}
map

\Sigma:M(F)\to M(J)
with
\Sigma\circ\jmath=\iota
(Arveson extension along
\jmath
into
E
).

Now

(\Sigma\circ\Theta)\circ\iota\;=\;\Sigma\circ\jmath\;=\;\iota,

hence
\Sigma\circ\Theta
is a
\mathrm{ucp}
endomorphism of
M(J)
fixing
A
, so by rigidity of
A\subseteq M(J)
we have
\Sigma\circ\Theta=\mathrm{id}_{M(J)}
. Similarly,

(\Theta\circ\Sigma)\circ\jmath\;=\;\Theta\circ\iota\;=\;\jmath,

so
\Theta\circ\Sigma=\mathrm{id}_{M(F)}
by rigidity of
(F,\jmath)
. Therefore
\Theta
is a complete order isomorphism with
\mathrm{ucp}
inverse
\Sigma
, hence a
*
-isomorphism

M(J)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }M(F)
; in particular,

(F,\jmath)\ \preccurlyeq\ (J,\iota).

Thus any rigid cover above
(J,\iota)
is actually
equivalent
to it in the order; no strict enlargement exists. Hence
(J,\iota)
is maximal among rigid covers.
∎

Maximality implies essentiality

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
(E,\iota)
be a maximal rigid
A
-multiplier cover and put
B:=M(E)
. If
\pi:B\to C
is a complete quotient map such that
\pi|_{A}
is a complete isometry, then
\pi
is injective.

Proof.

Let
j:A\hookrightarrow I(A)
be the Hamana embedding (Theorem
2.1
). Since
\pi|_{A}
is a complete isometry,
\pi(A)
is completely order isomorphic to
A
via
\pi|_{A}
. By injectivity of
I(A)
, there exists a ucp map
\Sigma:C\to I(A)
extending
j\circ(\pi|_{A})^{-1}:\pi(A)\to I(A)
. Compose to obtain
\Theta:=\Sigma\circ\pi:B\to I(A)
, which is ucp and satisfies
\Theta\circ\iota=j
.

Choose any essential ideal
E^{\prime}\lhd I(A)
(e.g.
E^{\prime}=I(A)
if unital) and identify
M(E^{\prime})
with
I(A)
. Then
(E^{\prime},j)
is a rigid
A
-multiplier cover. The map
\Theta
shows
(E,\iota)\preccurlyeq(E^{\prime},j)
. If
\pi
were not injective, then
\Theta
would not be injective, hence
(E^{\prime},j)
would strictly dominate
(E,\iota)
, contradicting maximality. Thus
\pi
is injective.
∎

4.
Main theorem:
M(E_{\max})
realises
I(A)

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(E_{\max},\iota_{\max})
be a maximal rigid
A
-multiplier cover. Then

M(E_{\max})\ \cong\ I(A)

via the unique complete order isomorphism over
A
.

Proof.

Set
B:=M(E_{\max})
. By Definition
3.1
, the inclusion
A\hookrightarrow B
is rigid. By Lemma
3.10
, it is essential in the quotient sense. By Theorem
2.1
,
B
is (canonically) the injective envelope of
A
; that is,
B\cong I(A)
over
A
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Hamana showed that
I(A)
need not be a von Neumann algebra; it is in general only monotone complete
[
7
]
. Our framework is compatible with this: nothing in the definition of a multiplier cover forces
M(E)
to be
W^{*}
, and, as noted in Remark
2.3
, even if one works inside a
W^{*}
ambient algebra
B
, one typically gets a quotient
B/\mathrm{Ann}(J)
, which need not be
W^{*}
.

5.
The commutative case revisited

We briefly revisit the case
A=C(X)
with
X
compact Hausdorff and show how the present isomorphism recovers the classical Gleason cover when expressed via multipliers.

Błaszczyk’s construction proceeds purely topologically:

(a)

First, we the use Kuratowski–Zorn lemma to get a maximal regular refinement
T^{\ast}
of the topology of
X
for which
\mathrm{id}\colon(X,T^{\ast})\to(X,T)
is irreducible. The resulting
(X,T^{\ast})
is completely regular and extremally disconnected
[
1
]
.

(b)

Then take the Čech–Stone compactification
G(X):=\beta(X,T^{\ast})
. One has that
G(X)
is extremally disconnected and the induced map
q:G(X)\twoheadrightarrow X
is irreducible
[
1
]
.

At this point the
C^{\ast}
-avatar appears:
C(G(X))
is injective and the inclusion
C(X)\hookrightarrow C(G(X))
is rigid. Thus
I(C(X))\cong C(G(X))
by Theorem
2.1
.

To realise
I(C(X))
as a multiplier algebra, work
after compactification
. Choose any dense open set
U\subseteq G(X)
(for instance, an increasing union of clopen sets). Then

M\big(C_{0}(U)\big)\ \cong\ C_{b}(U)\ \cong\ C(\beta U)\ =\ C(G(X)),

since
\beta U
identifies with the closure of
U
inside
G(X)
, which equals
G(X)
by density. Thus

I\big(C(X)\big)\ \cong\ M\big(C_{0}(U)\big),

perfectly mirroring the paradigm
maximise first, then compactify
. Note that prior to compactification
(X,T^{\ast})
need not be locally compact, so
C_{0}((X,T^{\ast}))
is not defined; the multiplier picture belongs naturally to the compact space
G(X)
.

M(C_{0}(U))\simeq C(\beta U)=C(G(X))\simeq I(C(X))
.

6.
Examples and remarks

Example 6.1
.

If
A=C(K)
with
K
extremally disconnected or
A=\mathcal{B}(H)
, then
I(A)=A
and we may take
E=A
with
\iota=\mathrm{id}
; then
M(E)=E=I(A)
and the cover is tautologically maximal rigid.

Remark 6.2
.

The local multiplier algebra
M_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
embeds canonically into
I(A)
in general, with equality for many classes (e.g. abelian, continuous-trace). The present result shows that
I(A)

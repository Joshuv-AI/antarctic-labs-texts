Star and weak star irreducible fully commutative elements in Coxeter groups of affine types B ~ \widetilde{B} and D ~ \widetilde{D}
Riccardo Biagioli, Luca Costantini, Elisa Sasso

Abstract
. The star operation, originally introduced by Kazhdan and Lusztig, was later specialized by Ernst to the so-called weak star reduction on the set of fully commutative elements of a Coxeter group. In this paper, we classify the star and weak star irreducible fully commutative elements in Coxeter groups of affine types B ~ n + 1 {\widetilde{B}_{n+1}} and D ~ n + 2 {\widetilde{D}_{n+2}} . Focusing then on the case of type D ~ n + 2 {\widetilde{D}_{n+2}} , we use the classification of star irreducible elements to provide a new proof of the faithfulness of a diagrammatic representation of the corresponding generalized Temperley–Lieb algebra, along with an explicit description of Lusztig’s 𝐚 \mathbf{a} -function.



Introduction

The notion of the star operation was first introduced by Kazhdan and Lusztig in
[
11
]
for simply laced Coxeter systems and later extended to arbitrary Coxeter systems in
[
12
]
. Star operations provide a powerful tool to study the decomposition of a Coxeter group into Kazhdan–Lusztig cells. These cells play a fundamental role in representation theory.

In this paper, we consider a special case of the star operation that strictly decreases the length of the element to which it is applied
[
9
]
, and following
[
4
]
, we focus on the star and weak star reducibility of fully commutative elements. Over the years, star irreducible elements have been classified. Green
[
9
]
defined a Coxeter group to be
star reducible
if and only if all of its star irreducible elements are products of commuting generators. In particular, he proved
[
9
, Theorem 6.3]
that finite types
A,B,D,E,F,H,I
and affine types
\widetilde{A}_{n}
(with
n
even),
\widetilde{C}_{n}
(with
n
odd),
\widetilde{E}_{6}
, and
\widetilde{F}_{5}
are star reducible Coxeter systems.
In
[
4
]
, Ernst relaxed this notion by introducing
weak star reducibility
(also referred to as
cancellability
, following Fan
[
7
]
), which coincides with star reducibility in the simply laced cases. He classified the weak star irreducible (or non-cancellable) elements of Coxeter systems of types
B
and
\widetilde{C}
in
[
4
, Theorem 4.2.1, Theorem 5.1.1]
.

In this paper, we classify the star irreducible and weak star irreducible elements for the affine types
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
and
{\widetilde{B}_{n+1}}
. Beyond their intrinsic interest as tools in the representation theory of Hecke algebras, these (weak) star irreducible elements enable us to give a new proof of the faithfulness of the diagrammatic representation of the Temperley–Lieb algebra of type
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
defined by the first and third authors in
[
1
]
. While this result was originally established using topological and combinatorial arguments based on diagram manipulations, our proof, motivated by valuable suggestions from the anonymous referee of
[
1
]
, takes a more algebraic approach that relies crucially on star irreducibility. Finally, we present a method to compute Lusztig’s
\mathbf{a}
-function
[
12
]
for fully commutative elements in terms of their associated decorated diagrams.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
1
provides a brief review of essential facts about Coxeter groups and the
Cartier–Foata normal form
, which will be used extensively throughout the paper. We also introduce a particular partial order, known as
heap
of an element, whose Hasse diagram helps to better visualize the structure of (weak) star irreducible elements.
In Section
2
, we classify the star irreducible elements of type
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
(Theorem
2.20
).
In Section
3
, building on this classification and employing an injective map between the sets of fully commutative elements of types
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
and
{\widetilde{B}_{n+1}}
(Definition
3.6
), we also characterize the star irreducible elements (Theorem
3.9
) and weak star irreducible elements (Theorem
3.14
) of type
{\widetilde{B}_{n+1}}
. In Sections
4
and
5
, we recall the definition of a family of decorated diagrams, introduced in
[
1
]
, and study their properties in relation to the irreducible elements of type
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
.
In Section
6
, we provide an alternative proof of the faithfulness of the diagrammatic representation of
{\mathrm{TL}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
(Theorem
6.5
) arising from such decorated diagrams. In the final section, we give a characterization of Lusztig’s
\mathbf{a}
-function in terms of parameters on the decorated diagrams of type
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
(Theorem
7.3
).

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Coxeter groups and fully commutative elements

Let
M
be a square symmetric matrix indexed by a finite set
S
, satisfying
m_{s,t}=m_{t,s}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}
and
m_{s,t}=1
if and only if
s=t
, for all
s,t\in S
. The
Coxeter group

W
associated with the
Coxeter matrix

M
is defined by generators
S
and relations
(st)^{m_{s,t}}=e
if
m_{s,t}<\infty
. These relations can be rewritten as
s^{2}=e
for all
s\in S
, and

(1)

[st]_{m_{s,t}}:=\underbrace{sts\cdots}_{m_{s,t}}=\underbrace{tst\cdots}_{m_{s,t}}=[ts]_{m_{s,t}}

for all
s,t\in S
such that
2\leq m_{s,t}<\infty
, the latter being called
braid relations
. When
m_{s,t}=2
, they are simply
commutation relations

st=ts
.

The
Coxeter graph

\Gamma
associated with a Coxeter system
(W,S)
is the labeled graph whose vertex set is
S
, with an edge between distinct elements
s,t\in S
whenever
m_{s,t}\geq 3
. The edge is labeled with
m_{s,t}
if
m_{s,t}\geq 4
. Therefore non adjacent vertices correspond precisely to commuting generators. We say that
(W,S)
is
irreducible
, when the associated Coxeter graph
\Gamma
is connected.

Figure 1.
Coxeter graphs of type
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
and
{\widetilde{B}_{n+1}}
.

In this section, we fix
(W,S)
to be a Coxeter system. The
length
function is
\ell:W\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, is defined as follows:
\ell(e)=0
and for every
e\neq w\in W

\ell(w):=min\{r\in\mathbb{N}\mid w=s_{i_{i}}\cdots s_{i_{r}}\mbox{, }s_{i_{k}}\in S\}.

An expression of
w\in W
of minimal length is called
reduced
. We denote by
\mathcal{R}(w)
the set of all reduced expressions of
w
. We use boldface to indicate a particular reduced expression.

We define the
left descents set
and the
right descents set
of
w\in W
respectively by

{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w):=\{s\in S\mid\ell(sw)<\ell(w)\}\mbox{ and }{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w):=\{s\in S\mid\ell(ws)<\ell(w)\}.

We have that
s\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
(
s\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)
) if and only if exists a reduced expression for
w
such that starts with
s
(finishes with
s
). For more details see
[
3
, §1.4]
.

Let
u,v,w\in W
. We say that
w=uv
is a
reduced product
if
\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(v)
. In this case,
u
is called a
prefix
of
w
, and
v
is called a
suffix
of
w
. In general,
u
is a
factor
of
w
if there exist
x,y\in W
such that
w=xuy
and
\ell(w)=\ell(x)+\ell(u)+\ell(y)
.
We denote by
\mathrm{Pref}(w)
(respectively,
\mathrm{Suff}(w)
) the set of prefixes (respectively, suffixes) of
w
.

A fundamental result in Coxeter group theory, sometimes called the
Matsumoto Theorem
, states that any reduced expression of
w
can be obtained from any other reduced expression of
w
by using only braid relations.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
w\in W
is
fully commutative
(FC) if any reduced expression of
w
can be obtained from any other reduced expression of
w
by using only commutation relations.
The set of fully commutative elements in
W
is denoted by
{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
.

The next proposition, due to Stembridge
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
, characterizes the FC elements and it is useful to test whether a given element is FC or not.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
w\in W
. Then,
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
if and only if for all
s,t\in S
such that
3\leq m_{s,t}<\infty
, there is no reduced expression of
w
having
[st]_{m_{s,t}}
as a factor.

Let
\mathbf{w}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{r}}
a reduced expression of
w\in W
.
Set
[r]:=\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
; we define the
support
of
w\in W
the set

supp(w):=\{s\in S\mid\exists k\in[r]\mbox{, }s=s_{{i_{k}}}\}.

Note that by Matsumoto Theorem, this set does not depend on the choice of
\mathbf{w}
. In particular, if
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
and
\mathbf{w^{\prime}}=s_{j_{1}}\cdots s_{j_{r}}
is another reduced expression of
w
, it follows that

\left|\{k\in\left[r\right]\mid s_{i_{k}}=s\}\right|=\left|\{h\in\left[r\right]\mid s_{j_{h}}=s\}\right|,

for all
s\in supp(w)
.

The following subset of fully commutative elements plays an important role in this paper.

Definition 1.3
.

We say that
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
is
completely commutative
if it is a product of commuting generators. We denote this subset by
\mathrm{CC}(W)
and we assume that
e\in\mathrm{CC}(W)
.

We now introduce two important closely related tools for studying
{\mathrm{FC}}
elements: the Cartier–Foata normal form and the heap of an element, introduced in
[
7
]
and
[
15
]
, respectively.

Theorem 1.4

(Cartier–Foata normal form)
.

Every
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
admits a unique factorization

(2)

w=u_{0}\cdots u_{m}

called the Cartier-Foata normal form (CFNF), satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

supp(u_{0})={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
;

(b)

u_{i}\in\mathrm{CC}(W)
for all
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}
;

(c)

\ell(w)=\ell(u_{0})+\cdots+\ell(u_{m})
;

(d)

if
t\in supp(u_{j+1})
,
j\in\{0,1\ldots,m-1\}
, then there exists
s\in supp(u_{j})
such that
m_{s,t}\geq 3
.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
and
w=u_{0}\cdots u_{m}
be its CFNF. Let
z=s_{i_{0}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
be a reduced expression of a prefix of
w
satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

if
k\geq 1
,
s_{i_{j}}
and
s_{i_{j+1}}
are not commuting generators for all
0\leq j\leq k-1
;

(b)

s_{i_{k}}\in supp(u_{m})
;

(c)

w=zv
is a reduced product and
s_{i_{k}}\notin supp(v)
.

Then
k=m
.

Proof.

We have that
s_{i_{0}}\in supp(u_{0})
, since
s_{i_{0}}\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
. Since
s_{i_{0}}\cdots s_{i_{j}}
is prefix of
w
for each
1\leq j\leq k
, by (a) and Theorem
1.4
(d), we have that
s_{i_{j}}\in supp(u_{j})
and
z
is a prefix of
u_{0}\cdots u_{k}
. Furthermore, by (b) and (c) we can conclude that
k=m
. Otherwise if
k<m
, then
w=zv
is a reduced product and
supp(u_{m})\subseteq supp(v)
, so
s_{i_{k}}\in supp(v)
, that is a contradiction.
∎

Definition 1.6
.

Let
w\in W
. Consider
\mathbf{w}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{r}}\in\mathcal{R}(w)
, we define a partial ordering
\prec
on
[r]
via the transitive closure of the relation

j\prec k\mbox{ if }j<k\mbox{ and }m_{s_{i_{j}},s_{i_{k}}}\geq 3.

In particular,
j\prec k
if
j<k
and
s_{i_{j}}=s_{i_{k}}
. The
heap
of
\mathbf{w}
is the labeled poset given by the triple
H({\mathbf{w}}):=([r],\prec,\varepsilon)
, where
\varepsilon:j\mapsto s_{i_{j}}
is the labeling map.

It is well-known that if
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
and
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{R}(w)
, then
H(\mathbf{w})\cong H(\mathbf{w^{\prime}})
as labeled poset. Hence, it is well defined
H(w):=H(\mathbf{w})
, where
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{R}(w)
, see
[
15
]
.

Remark 1.7
.

Although the proofs presented in this paper do not rely on the concept of heap, visualizing the heap of an element can provide valuable intuition about its structure. This is particularly helpful for the star-irreducible elements discussed later, whose names are inspired by the shapes of their associated heaps. We follow the notation of
[
1
]
to depict the Hasse diagram of a heap, which we now briefly recall. In the Hasse diagram of
H(w)
,
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, vertices with the same labels are drawn in the same column. In particular, we represent heaps in
n+1
columns with the following criterion. Both vertices with label
s_{0}
and
s_{1}
(respectively
s_{n+1}
and
s_{n+2}
) will be drawn in the first (respectively last) column of
H(w)
. Moreover, when
s_{0}s_{1}
(respectively
s_{n+1}s_{n+2}
) is a factor of
w
, we represent the vertices associated to these
s_{0}
and
s_{1}
(respectively
s_{n+1}
and
s_{n+2}
) with a single mark point labeled
s_{0}s_{1}
(respectively
s_{n+1}s_{n+2}
). In a similar way, we represent a heap of
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
with
n+1
columns, where the criterion for the placement of double vertices is applied only to
s_{0}
and
s_{1}
.

Additionally, we represent the Hasse diagram of
H(w)
so that all entries corresponding to elements in

{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
appear at the same vertical level in the topmost row of the diagram, while all other entries are placed as high as possible, subject to the partial order. Given this representation of
H(w)
, the CFNF of
w
can be obtained by reading the labels of the vertices row by row, from top to bottom, and from left to right within each row.

Fully commutative elements in a Coxeter group
W
index a basis of the so-called generalized Temperley–Lieb algebra of
W
. The following is the presentation of
\mathrm{TL}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
given by Green in
[
9
, Proposition 2.6]
that will be used in our work as a definition. The Coxeter graph of type
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
is depicted in Figure
1
.

Definition 1.9
.

The
Temperley–Lieb algebra of type
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
, denoted by
\mathrm{TL}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, is the
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
-algebra generated by
\{b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n+2}\}
with defining relations:

(d1)

b_{i}^{2}=\delta b_{i}
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,n+2\}
;

(d2)

b_{i}b_{j}=b_{j}b_{i}
if
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
are not adjacent nodes in the Coxeter graph;

(d3)

b_{i}b_{j}b_{i}=b_{i}
if
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
are adjacent nodes in the Coxeter graph.

For any
s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
reduced expression of
w
in
{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, we set

b_{w}=b_{i_{1}}\cdots\ b_{i_{k}}.

It is easy to see that
b_{w}
does not depend on the chosen reduced expression of
w
. In
[
8
]
, Graham proved that the set
\{b_{w}\mid w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})\}
is a basis for
{\mathrm{TL}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, which is usually called the
monomial basis
(see also
[
9
, Proposition 2.4]
).

1.2.
Star and weak star reducible
{\mathrm{FC}}
elements

In this section we fix an arbitrary irreducible Coxeter system
(W,S)
.
Now, we recall the notions of star and weak star reducibility in
(W,S)
and we refer the reader to
[
4
,
9
,
10
]
for more details.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
and suppose that
s\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
(respectively,
s\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)
). We say that
w
is
left (respectively, right) star reducible by
s
with respect to
t
to
sw
(respectively,
ws
) if there exists
t\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(sw)
(respectively,
t\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(ws)
) with
m_{s,t}\geq 3
.

Observe that if
m_{s,t}\geq 3
, then
w
is left (respectively, right) star reducible by
s
with respect to
t
if and only if
w=stv
(respectively,
w=vts
), with
\ell(w)=\ell(v)+2
.

We now define the concept of weak star reducible elements, first introduced in
[
4
]
, which is related the notion of cancellable elements introduced by Fan in
[
7
]
.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
and
s,t\in S
, we say that
w
is
left (respectively, right) weak star reducible by
s
with respect to
t
to
sw
(respectively,
ws
)
, if the following hold:

(a)

w
is left (respectively, right) star reducible by
s
with respect to
t
to
sw
(respectively,
ws
);

(b)

tw\notin{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
(respectively,
wt\notin{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
).

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
, we say that
w
is
left (respectively, right) (weak) star irreducible
if does not exist
s,t\in S
such that
w
is left (respectively, right) (weak) star reducible by
s
with respect to
t
. Moreover, we say that
w
is
(weak) star irreducible
if it is both left and right (weak) star irreducible. We set also

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}(W)

\displaystyle=\{w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)\mid w\mbox{ is star irreducible}\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{I_{w}}(W)

\displaystyle=\{w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)\mid w\mbox{ is weak star irreducible}\}.

Clearly,
\mathrm{I}(W)\subseteq\mathrm{I_{w}}(W)
and denote by
\mathrm{I_{w}^{\prime}}(W):=\mathrm{I_{w}}(W)\setminus\mathrm{I}(W)
. Observe that when
W
is simply laced (i.e.,
m_{s,t}\leq 3
for all
s,t\in S)
the definitions of star reducible and weak star reducible are equivalent, therefore we will distinguish these two definitions only in Section
3
when dealing with type
{\widetilde{B}_{n+1}}
.

From now on, to simplify the writing, we will say
reducible
(
irreducible
) instead of
star reducible
(
star irreducible
). Moreover, we use the notation

w\rightsquigarrow_{t}sw\quad\mbox{(respectively, }w\rightsquigarrow_{t}ws)

to denote that
w
is (weak) left (respectively, right) reducible by
s
with respect to
t
; we omit the
t
when we it is not necessary. In general, by
w\rightsquigarrow v
we mean that either
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}sw
or
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}ws
for some
s,t\in S
with
m_{s,t}\geq 3
. We will explicitly write “weak” when we consider a weak star reduction.

Remark 1.12
.

(1)

If
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}sw
(respectively,
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}ws
), then
\ell(tw)>\ell(w)
(respectively,
\ell(wt)>\ell(w)
).

(2)

We have that
w
is left (weak) irreducible if and only if
w^{-1}
is right (weak) irreducible. Thus,
w
is (weak) irreducible if and only if
w^{-1}
is also (weak) irreducible.

(3)

If
3\leq m_{s,t}<\infty
, define
\xi_{s,t}:=[st]_{m_{s,t}-1}
(see (
1
)). We have that
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}sw
(respectively,
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}ws
) is a weak reduction if and only if
\xi_{s,t}
is a prefix (respectively,
\xi_{s,t}^{-1}
is a suffix) of
w
.

(4)

Assume
m_{s,t}=3
and
w=u_{0}\cdots u_{m}
be its CFNF,
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}sw
if and only if
s\in supp(u_{0})
,
t\in supp(u_{1})
and for every
s^{\prime}\in S
such that
m_{s^{\prime},t}=3
,
s^{\prime}\notin supp(u_{0})
.

(5)

Assume
s\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
(resp.
s\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)
). If
t\in S
such that
m_{s,t}\geq 3
and
tw\notin{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
(respectively,
wt\notin{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
), then
w
admits
st
as a prefix (respectively,
ts
as a suffix), so
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}sw
(respectively,
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}ws
) is a weak reduction.

(6)

Assume
s\neq s^{\prime}
, if
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}sw
and
w\rightsquigarrow_{t^{\prime}}s^{\prime}w
(respectively,
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}ws
and
w\rightsquigarrow_{t^{\prime}}ws^{\prime}
) are (weak) reductions, then
m_{x,x^{\prime}}=2
for all
x\in\{s,t\}
and
x^{\prime}\in\{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}\}
, therefore
sw\rightsquigarrow_{t^{\prime}}s^{\prime}sw
(respectively,
ws\rightsquigarrow_{t^{\prime}}wss^{\prime}
) is a (weak) reduction.

(7)

Let
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}sw
and
w\rightsquigarrow_{t^{\prime}}ws^{\prime}
be (weak) reductions. Then
ws^{\prime}\rightsquigarrow_{t}sws^{\prime}
is a (weak) reduction if and only if
sw\rightsquigarrow_{t^{\prime}}sws^{\prime}
is a (weak) reduction.

Definition 1.13
.

Let
w,v\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
. We say that
w
is
(weak) reducible to
v
if there is a sequence (also trivial) of (weak) reductions

(3)

w_{0}=w\rightsquigarrow w_{1}\rightsquigarrow w_{2}\rightsquigarrow\cdots\rightsquigarrow w_{l}=v.

In every step of (
3
), the length of the elements decreases by 1, therefore, any
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
can be reduced to a (weak) irreducible element.

The following two results are probably known, but we include their proofs due to the lack of precise references.

Lemma 1.14
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
be (weak) reducible to
v
(weak) irreducible with a sequence

(4)

w_{0}=w\rightsquigarrow w_{1}\rightsquigarrow w_{2}\rightsquigarrow\cdots\rightsquigarrow w_{l}=v.

If there exists a (weak) reduction
w\rightsquigarrow w^{\prime}_{1}
, with
w_{1}^{\prime}\neq w_{1}
, then there exists
v^{\prime}
(weak) irreducible such that
w
is (weak) reducible to
v^{\prime}
by a sequence

w_{0}^{\prime}=w\rightsquigarrow w_{1}^{\prime}\rightsquigarrow w_{2}^{\prime}\rightsquigarrow\cdots\rightsquigarrow w_{l}^{\prime}=v^{\prime}.

Moreover, if
|S|>2
and
supp(v)
is complete, then
v=v^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We first prove the statement for star reducibility.
We assume without loss of generality that
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}sw=:w_{1}^{\prime}

Theorem 1.15
.

(a)

If

is left (respectively, right) (weak) irreducible and obtained from

by a series of left (respectively, right) (weak) reductions, then

(b)

with

(weak) irreducible. If

and

with respectively sequences

(5)

\displaystyle w_{0}

\displaystyle=w\rightsquigarrow w_{1}\rightsquigarrow w_{2}\rightsquigarrow\cdots\rightsquigarrow w_{l}=v,

(6)

\displaystyle w_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=w\rightsquigarrow w_{1}^{\prime}\rightsquigarrow w_{2}^{\prime}\rightsquigarrow\cdots\rightsquigarrow w_{k}^{\prime}=v^{\prime};

then

.

(c)

Assume

. If

is (weak) reducible to
v
(weak) irreducible and

is complete, then

is unique.

Proof.

We first prove the statement for star reducibility.

(a)

Suppose

reducible to
v
by a series of left reductions with sequence

w_{0}=w\rightsquigarrow w_{1}\rightsquigarrow\cdots\rightsquigarrow w_{l}=v.

First we show the following claim by induction on

\mbox{if }x\in\mathrm{Suff}(w)\mbox{ is left irreducible, then }x\in\mathrm{Suff}(v).

If

is left irreducible, so the claim trivially holds. So suppose

and
w_{1}=sw
with

a prefix of

with

is reducible to

Now, we observe that if

is reducible to

left irreducible by a series of left reductions, we have that

, hence statement (a) holds.

(b)

We proceed by induction on

. If

, then
w
is irreducible and the thesis trivially holds. So suppose

. We have that
w
is left or right reducible to

with respect to a pair
\{s_{1},t_{1}\}
, then by applying Lemma

to the sequence in (

Therefore,

is reducible to

and
v
, so by inductive hypothesis we can conclude that

.

(c)

Assume

, let

be reducible to

, such that

is complete.
We proceed by induction on

, then

and the claim trivially holds. On the other hand, assume

and suppose

The statements for weak star reducibility can be proved similarly, in particular in the proof of (a) it is sufficient to work with prefix

2.
Irreducibility in
{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})

In this section, we classify the irreducible elements in a Coxeter system of type
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
. Recall that, since
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
is simply laced, star reducibility and weak star reducibility coincide.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
. We denote by

\displaystyle f_{\bullet}(w)

\displaystyle:=\text{max$\{\#$ of occurrences of the factor $s_{0}s_{1}$ in $\mathbf{w}\mid\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{R}(w)\};$}

\displaystyle f_{\circ}(w)

\displaystyle:=\text{max$\{\#$ of occurrences of the factor $s_{n+1}s_{n+2}$ in $\mathbf{w}\mid\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{R}(w)\}.$}

Remark 2.2
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, then

(1)

f_{\bullet}(w)=f_{\bullet}(w^{-1})
and
f_{\circ}(w)=f_{\circ}(w^{-1})
;

(2)

if
w\rightsquigarrow v
, then
f_{\bullet}(v)=f_{\bullet}(w)
and
f_{\circ}(v)=f_{\circ}(w)
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
w,v\in{\mathrm{FC}}(\widetilde{D}_{10})
,
w=s_{0}s_{3}s_{5}s_{9}s_{1}s_{2}s_{4}s_{6}s_{8}s_{3}s_{5}s_{7}s_{10}
and
v=s_{0}s_{5}s_{9}s_{1}s_{10}
. Then we can choose

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}=s_{3}s_{5}s_{9}(s_{0}s_{1})s_{2}s_{4}s_{6}s_{8}s_{3}s_{5}s_{7}s_{10}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{v}=(s_{0}s_{1})s_{5}(s_{9}s_{10}).

Hence,
f_{\bullet}(w)=1
,
f_{\circ}(w)=0
and
f_{\bullet}(v)=f_{\circ}(v)=1
.

Notation 2.4
.

Since the generators
s_{0}
and
s_{1}
play equivalent roles, we introduce the notation
s_{\bullet}
to denote either
s_{0}
or
s_{1}
, and write
\{{s_{\bullet}},{t_{\bullet}}\}=\{s_{0},s_{1}\}
. In the same way, we denote by
{s_{\circ}}
either
s_{n+1}
or
s_{n+2}
, and
\{{s_{\circ}},{t_{\circ}}\}=\{s_{n+1},s_{n+2}\}
.

We denote by

the elements in
{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})

(8)

\displaystyle A=s_{2}s_{3}\cdots s_{n}s_{n+1}s_{n+2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad B=s_{n}s_{n-1}\cdots s_{2}s_{1}s_{0}.

Definition 2.5
.

An element
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
is called a
complete zigzag
if it admits a reduced expression of one of the following forms:

(1)

s_{0}s_{1}(AB)^{k}A^{h}
,

(2)

s_{n+2}s_{n+1}(BA)^{k}B^{h}
,

where
k\geq 0
,
h\in\{0,1\}
and
k+h>0
.

Denote by
\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})\subset{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
the set of complete zigzag elements, and note that they are irreducible (see some examples in Figure
3
).

Figure 3.
Examples of heaps of complete zigzags.

We observe that, if
w
is a complete zigzag, then
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
and
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)
coincide with either
\{s_{0},s_{1}\}
or
\{s_{n+1},s_{n+2}\}
. Moreover, we have that
f_{\bullet}(w),f_{\circ}(w)\geq 1
. In particular,
\ell(w)=(2k+h)(n+1)+2
, and

is uniquely determined by
\ell(w)
and
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
. Note that
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)
if and only if

. Moreover, if

, then

f_{\bullet}(w)+f_{\circ}(w)=2k+h+1\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\left|f_{\bullet}(w)-f_{\circ}(w)\right|\leq 1.

Observe also that
w\in\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
if and only if
w^{-1}\in\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
.

Definition 2.6
.

An element

is called a

if it is a factor of a complete zigzag and

The family of weak zigzags given in Definition

does not coincide neither with the zigzags introduced in
[

[

.
Note that

is a weak zigzag if and only if

is a weak zigzag. Moreover, a weak zigzag

is irreducible if and only if

is a complete zigzag.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
be such that:

\displaystyle{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)

\displaystyle=\left\{s_{0},s_{1}\right\}\text{ or }{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)=\left\{s_{n+2},s_{n+1}\right\}

\displaystyle(\mbox{respectively, }{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)

\displaystyle=\{s_{0},s_{1}\}\text{ or }{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)=\{s_{n+2},s_{n+1}\}).

Then either

or
w
is a weak zigzag. Moreover,

is left (respectively, right) irreducible.

Proof.

Without loss of generality suppose that
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)=\left\{s_{0},s_{1}\right\}
. Suppose
w\notin\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
and let
w=u_{0}\cdots u_{m}
be its CFNF, so

. If

then
u_{i}=s_{i+1}
for

or
supp(u_{i})\subseteq\{s_{n+1},s_{n+2}\}
for

w=\begin{cases}s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{m}s_{m+1},&m<n,\\
s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n}u_{n},&m=n\mbox{ and }u_{n}\in\{s_{n+1},s_{n+2}\},\\
s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n}s_{n+1}s_{n+2}P,&m\geq n\mbox{ and }P\in\mathrm{Pref}((BA)^{k})\mbox{, }k\geq 1\end{cases}

where

and

are defined in (

or

is a weak zigzag, and in both cases

is left irreducible.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
.

(a)

If
x\in\mathrm{Pref}(w)\cup\mathrm{Suff}(w)
such that
x\in\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, then
w
is a weak zigzag.

(b)

If
w
is reducible to
v\in\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, then
w
is a weak zigzag.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
w=xy
reduced product and
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(x)=\{s_{0},s_{1}\}
. Since

,
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(y)\subseteq\{s_{2}\}
. Hence,
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(x)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
and
w
is a weak zigzag since
w\notin\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, and by Lemma
2.9
, so (a) is proved.

Now assume that
w
is reducible to

, then we can factorize

as

with
\ell(w)=\ell(v^{\prime})+\ell(v)+\ell(v^{\prime\prime})
.
By part (a), both

and

are weak zigzags. Since

and the factorization is length-additive, it follows that

itself is a weak zigzag.
∎

Remark 2.18
.

In
[

, Green shows that Coxeter groups of finite type

are star reducible. This means that

is irreducible if and only if

is a product of commuting generators.
In our setting, if

and

is not complete, then

can be considered as a FC element or a product of FC elements in a Coxeter group of finite type

with not complete support, then

.

)

3.
Irreducibility and weak irreducibility in
{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})

As we mentioned above, in the non-simply laced cases the notions of reducibility and weak reducibility do not coincide, so we have that
\mathrm{I_{w}^{\prime}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})=\mathrm{I_{w}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})\setminus\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
is not empty.
In this section we classify both the irreducible and weak irreducible elements of a Coxeter group of type
{\widetilde{B}_{n+1}}
. The families of irreducible elements that we now list are analogous to those arising in type
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
, although certain differences occur in the present setting.

Definition 3.1
.

We define the set of
weak completely commutative
elements
\mathrm{CC_{w}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})\subseteq{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
by

\mathrm{CC_{w}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}}):=\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})\cup\{s_{n}s_{n+1}v,s_{n+1}s_{n}v\mid v\in\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}}),s_{n-1},s_{n},s_{n+1}\notin supp(v)\}.

Note that
w\in\mathrm{CC_{w}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
is weak irreducible. In particular,
w\in\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
if and only if
w\in\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
.

We denote by
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
the elements in
{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=s_{2}s_{3}\cdots s_{n}s_{n+1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{B}=s_{n}s_{n-1}\cdots s_{2}s_{1}s_{0}.

Definition 3.2
.

An element

is called a
complete zigzag
if it admits a reduced expression of one of the following forms:

(1)

s_{0}s_{1}(\mathcal{AB})^{k}\mathcal{A}^{h}
,

(2)

s_{n+1}(\mathcal{BA})^{k}\mathcal{B}^{h}
,

where
k\geq 0
,
h\in\{0,1\}
and
k+h>0
.

{\widetilde{B}_{n+1}}
.

Denote by
\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})\subset{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
the set of complete zigzag elements and note that they are weak irreducible. In particular,
w\in\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})\cap\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
if and only if
w
is of the form
s_{0}s_{1}(\mathcal{AB})^{k}\mathcal{A}^{h}
with

.

.

An element

is called a

if it is a factor of a complete zigzag and
w\notin\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})

Note that a weak zigzag

is weak irreducible if and only if

is a complete zigzag.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
,
n
even, and let

be its normal form with

. We say that

is a
candy
if

is even and:

•

,

such that
x_{i}\neq x_{i+1}
, for every
0\leq i\leq\frac{m}{2}
;

•

supp(u_{2i+1})=\{s_{2},s_{4},\ldots,s_{n}\}
, for every
0\leq i<\frac{m}{2}
.

Denote by
\mathrm{K}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})\subset{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
the set of candy elements and note that they are irreducible. If
n
is odd, we set
\mathrm{K}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})=\emptyset
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
,
n
odd, and let
w=u_{0}\cdots u_{m}
be its normal form with
m\geq 2
. We say that
w
is a
left candy
if
m
is even and:

•

supp(u_{2i})=\{s_{3},s_{5},\ldots,s_{n}\}\cup\left\{x_{i}\right\}
,
x_{i}\in\left\{s_{0},s_{1}\right\}
such that
x_{i}\neq x_{i+1}
, for every
0\leq i\leq\frac{m}{2}
;

•

supp(u_{2i+1})=\{s_{2},s_{4},\ldots,s_{n+1}\}
, for every
0\leq i<\frac{m}{2}
.

Denote by
\mathrm{K_{w}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})\subset{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
denotes the set of left candy elements, in particular
\mathrm{K_{w}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})\subset\mathrm{I_{w}^{\prime}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
. If

is even, we set
\mathrm{K_{w}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})=\emptyset
.

In the next definition, we introduce an injective map

\varphi:{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})\rightarrow{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}).

This map will serve as a tool for classifying the irreducible and weakly irreducible elements of type
{\widetilde{B}_{n+1}}
, based on the corresponding classification for type
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
developed in Section

.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
. We define the map
\varphi:{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})\rightarrow{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
by the following rule:

(1)

If no reduced expression of
w
contains the factor
s_{n+1}s_{n}s_{n+1}
or
s_{n}s_{n+1}s_{n}
, consider any
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{R}(w)
. Then
\varphi(w)
is the element in
{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
with reduced expression
\mathbf{w}
.

(2)

Otherwise, choose a reduced expression

w\in\mathcal{R}(w)
that contains all possible occurrences of the factors
s_{n+1}s_{n}s_{n+1}
and
s_{n}s_{n+1}s_{n}
. Then
\varphi(w)
is the element whose reduced expression is obtained from
\mathbf{w}
by replacing each occurrence of
s_{n+1}s_{n}s_{n+1}
with
s_{n+1}s_{n}s_{n+2}
, and each occurrence of
s_{n}s_{n+1}s_{n}
with
s_{n}s_{n+1}s_{n+2}s_{n}
.

The map
\varphi
is well-defined. Point (1) is immediate, and since in
{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
there are no elements containing both factors
s_{n+1}s_{n}s_{n+1}
and
s_{n}s_{n+1}s_{n}
(see e.g.
[
2
, §3.2]
), point (2) is also well-posed.

Lemma 3.7
.

The map
\varphi
is injective, and
w\in\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{B}_{n+1}})
if and only if
\varphi(w)\in\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
.

Proof.

Note that if there exists a reducible prefix or suffix in
w
, then it will be transformed by
\varphi
into a reducible prefix or suffix in
\varphi(w)
.
On the other hand, the only reducible prefixes and suffixes that need to be analyzed are
{s_{\circ}}s_{n}
and
s_{n}{s_{\circ}}
. If
{s_{\circ}}s_{n}
or
s_{n}{s_{\circ}}
is a prefix of
\varphi(w)
, then
{s_{\circ}}=s_{n+1}
by construction of
\varphi
, so
s_{n+1}s_{n}
or
s_{n}s_{n+1}
is a prefix of
w
. The analogous holds for the suffix, with the only difference that if
s_{n}{s_{\circ}}

4.
Decorated diagrams

4.1.
Temperley–Lieb diagrams

The following paragraph provides a brief overview of the classical Temperley–Lieb diagrams. For a more comprehensive treatment, we refer the reader to
[
5
, §3]
and the references therein. A
pseudo k-diagram
consists of a finite number of disjoint plane curves, called
edges
, embedded in the standard
k
-box, that is a rectangle with
2k
marked points called
nodes
or
vertices
; we will refer to the top of the rectangle as the
north face
and to the bottom as the
south face
. The nodes are endpoints of edges. All other embedded edges must be closed (isotopic to circles) and disjoint from the box. We refer to a closed edge as a
loop
. It follows that there cannot exist isolated nodes and that a single edge starts from each node (an example is given in Figure
8
). A
non-propagating edge
is an edge that joins two nodes of the same face, while a
propagating edge
is an edge that joins a node of the north face to a node of the south face. If an edge joins the nodes
i
and
i^{\prime}
we call it a
vertical edge
. We denote by
\mathbf{a}(D)
the number of non-propagating edges on the north face of a concrete pseudo
k
-diagram
D
. The pseudo
k
-diagrams are defined up to isotopy equivalence; denote the set of the pseudo
k
-diagrams by
T_{k}(\emptyset)
.

Figure 8.
Two equivalent pseudo 7-diagrams.

In this work, we are interested in a decorated generalization of the family of classical Temperley–Lieb diagrams, which we refer to as
LR
-decorated diagrams of type
\widetilde{D}
.
We begin by recalling the basic notions required for their definition, referring to
[
1
, §3]
, where these diagrams were first introduced, for a detailed treatment.
Briefly, an
LR
-decorated pseudo
k
-diagram is a pseudo
k
-diagram
D
whose edges are equipped with finite sequences of
decorations
from the set
\Omega=\{\bullet,\circ\}
, subject to a collection of constraints. These rules, labeled (D0)–(D4) in
[
1
, §2]
, specify the admissible configurations of decorations. We denote the set of
LR
-decorated pseudo
k
-diagrams by
T^{LR}_{k}(\Omega)
.
If an edge
e
is decorated, we read off the sequence of decorations left to right if
e
is a non-propagating edge, up to down if
e
is a propagating edge.
For a loop edge
e
, the sequence is read according to any arbitrarily chosen starting point and any direction around the loop.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
D
be a pseudo
k
-diagram with
\mathbf{a}(D)=1
, decorated with at least one decoration in
\Omega
and such that the unique non-propagating edge and loops (if any) have at most one type of decoration. A
L
-
strip
(respectively
R
-
strip
) is a part of the diagram delimited by two horizontal lines that contains at least one
\bullet
-decoration (respectively
\circ
-decoration) and no
\circ
-decorations (respectively
\bullet
-decorations).

Definition 4.2
.

We define

to be the free
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]

as a basis.

Now we define the product of two elements of

as the concatenation, and then extend this bilinearly to define a multiplication in

s_{n+2}\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
.

Proof.

The sufficient condition is proved in Lemma
4.6
. Here we settle the other implication.

First, note that if
\{i,i+1\}
appears in
D_{w}
with
1\leq i\leq n+1
, then
D_{i}D_{w}=\delta D_{w}
, so
s_{i}\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
by Theorem
5.6
. Hence, (a) is proved.

If
\{1,2\}_{\bullet}
is present in
D_{w}
, then
D_{0}D_{w}=\delta D_{w}
, thus
s_{0}\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
by Theorem
5.6
. Suppose now that
\{1,2\}
appears in
D_{w}
. Since the concatenation
D_{0}
with
D_{w}
forms a loop
{\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}}
and the numbers of
{\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}}
in
D_{w}
and
D_{0}D_{w}
are the same, it means that a
{\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}}
giving rise to a reduction of type (r3) was already present in
D_{w}
. Hence
D_{0}D_{w}=\delta D_{w}
and (b) follows by Theorem
5.6
. Point (c) is analogous to (b).
∎

6.
Injectivity of
\widetilde{\theta}_{D}

In this section we prove that
\widetilde{\theta}_{D}
is injective. The argument proceeds in three steps:
we first show that
\widetilde{\theta}_{D}
is injective on the set of irreducible elements of

{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}).

We then prove that if
\widetilde{\theta}_{D}(b_{u})=\widetilde{\theta}_{D}(b_{w})
and one of
u
and
w
is irreducible, then
u=w
.
Finally, combining the previous steps we deduce the injectivity of
\widetilde{\theta}_{D}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
u,w\in\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
. If
D_{u}=D_{w}
, then
u=w
.

Proof.

Since
D_{u}=D_{w}
, then
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
and
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(u)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)
by Theorem
5.6
. Suppose
u
and
w
are not in the same family. Note that one of them must be in the family
\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
because the cardinalities of the descent sets of a candy and a complete zigzag are never equal. Hence assume
u\in\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
. If
w\in\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, necessarily
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(u)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)
, hence

{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)=\{s_{0},s_{1}\}\mbox{ or }{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)=\{s_{n+1},s_{n+2}\}.

Therefore,
3\leq f_{\bullet}(w)+f_{\circ}(w)\neq f_{\bullet}(u)+f_{\circ}(u)=1
which is a contradiction by Proposition
4.10
. If
w\in\mathrm{K}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
then we have a contradiction because
D_{w}
has at least one
{\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}^{\circ}}
while
D_{u}
has no occurrence of
{\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}^{\circ}}
by Proposition
4.8
and Proposition
4.7
(b).

Assume now that
u
and
w
are in the same family.

•

If
u,w\in\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, then
u=w
trivially.

•

If
u,w\in\mathrm{K}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, then consider
u=u_{0}u_{1}\cdots u_{m}
and
w=w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{p}
their CFNF. Since
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
and
D_{w}
and
D_{u}
have the same number of
{\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}^{\circ}}
, then
u_{0}=w_{0}
and
m=p
, by Proposition
4.8
. Therefore, by Definition
2.8
,
u=w
.

•

If
u,w\in\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, then
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
implies that
u
and
w
are of the same form (1) or (2) in Definition
2.5
. Moreover, the corresponding
k
and
h
are equal as well, since
f_{\bullet}(w)=f_{\bullet}(u)
and
f_{\circ}(w)=f_{\circ}(u)
by Proposition
4.9
.

∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
such that
f_{\bullet}(w)=f_{\circ}(w)=0
. If the edge
\{i,i^{\prime}\}
not decorated appears in
D_{w}
, then the following hold.

(a)

If
i=1
, then
s_{0},s_{1}\notin supp(w)
.

(b)

If
i=2
, then
s_{0},s_{1},s_{2}\notin supp(w)
.

(c)

If
3\leq i\leq n
, then
s_{i-1},s_{i}\notin supp(w)
.

(d)

If
i=n+1
, then
s_{n},s_{n+1},s_{n+2}\notin supp(w)
.

(e)

If
i=n+2
, then
s_{n+1},s_{n+2}\notin supp(w)
.

Proof.

We only prove point (c) since the other cases are similar. Then set
3\leq i\leq n
, and let
w=u_{0}\cdots u_{m}
be its CFNF. We have
D_{w}=D_{u_{0}}\cdots D_{u_{m}}
with
D_{u_{j}}
,
0\leq j\leq m
described in Proposition
4.7
, since
u_{j}\in\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
. First, note that
s_{i-1},s_{i}\notin supp(u_{0})={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
by Lemma
4.6
. Now we prove the result by contradiction. Let
1\leq l\leq m
be the smallest index such that
s_{i-1}
or
s_{i}
is in
supp(u_{l})
, and assume, without loss of generality, that
s_{i-1}\in supp(u_{l})
. By Lemma
4.6
,
\{i-1,i\}
and
\{(i-1)^{\prime},i^{\prime}\}
are in
D_{u_{l}}
, and by Lemma
4.7
and Corollary
5.7
, the undecorated edge
\{i,i^{\prime}\}
belongs to
D_{u_{j}}
, for all
0\leq j<l
. Note that the configuration depicted in Figure
16
(a) never appears by fully commutativity. Therefore, since
\{i,i^{\prime}\}
appears in
D_{w}
by hypothesis, the only possibility is that there exist
h_{1}<h_{2}
such that
s_{0}\in supp(u_{h_{1}})
and
s_{1}\in supp(u_{h_{2}})
, or vice versa, and
s_{0},s_{1}\notin supp(u_{j})
for all
h_{1}<j<h_{2}
, see Figure
16
(b). Suppose
h_{1}
is minimal, then in the diagram
D_{u_{0}}D_{u_{1}}\cdots D_{u_{l}}\cdots D_{u_{h_{1}}}\cdots D_{u_{h_{2}}}
the edge
\{i,2^{\prime}\}
is decorated with a
\bullet
. Now, since
D_{w}
contains
\{i,i^{\prime}\}
, the edge
\{2,i^{\prime}\}
in
D_{u_{h_{2}+1}}\cdots D_{u_{m}}
either is undecorated or its first decoration is a
\circ
. In any case,
\{i,i^{\prime}\}
is decorated since
f_{\bullet}(w)=f_{\circ}(w)=0
by hypothesis, which is a contradiction. The same argument holds if we assume that
s_{i}\in supp(u_{l})
, by exchanging the
\circ
decorations with the
\bullet
.

∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
. If
u\in\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
such that
D_{u}=D_{w}
, then
u=w
.

Proof.

We distinguish four cases.

(1)

Assume
f_{\bullet}(w)=f_{\circ}(w)=0
. If
w\in\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, then
u=w
by Proposition
6.1
. Otherwise, we suppose that
w\rightsquigarrow_{s_{i}}s_{i-1}w
, with
3\leq i\leq n-1
. Therefore since
s_{i-1}s_{i}
is a prefix of
w
, we have that
s_{i},s_{i+1}\notin{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
. Moreover, since
D_{u}=D_{w}
, the non-decorated edge
\{i+1,(i+1)^{\prime}\}
occurs in
D_{w}
by Proposition
4.7
and Corollary
5.7
, which is not possible by Lemma
6.2
. The cases when
i\in\{2,n,n+1\}
, or
s_{0}s_{2}
,
s_{n}s_{n+2}
or
s_{j}s_{j^{\prime}}
, with
m_{s_{j},s_{j^{\prime}}}=3
and
j>j^{\prime}
, is a prefix of
w
can be treated in the same way using Lemma
6.2
. So
w
is necessarily irreducible and
u=w
.

(2)

If
f_{\bullet}(w)=1
and
f_{\circ}(w)=0
, then
s_{0},s_{1}\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)\cap{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)
, because
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(u)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)
by Theorem
5.6
. Moreover, it is possible to write
w=s_{0}s_{1}w^{\prime}
reduced and
s_{0},s_{1},s_{2}\notin supp(w^{\prime})
. Let
u=s_{0}s_{1}u^{\prime}
reduced and
u^{\prime}\in\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
. Observe that,
\{1,1^{\prime}\}
and
\{2,2^{\prime}\}
appear in
D_{w^{\prime}}
and in
D_{u^{\prime}}
, since
s_{0},s_{1},s_{2}\notin supp(w^{\prime})\cup supp(u^{\prime})
. Then,
D_{u^{\prime}}=D_{w^{\prime}}
since the portions of
D_{w^{\prime}}
and
D_{u^{\prime}}
on the right of
\{2,2^{\prime}\}
are both equal to the corresponding portion of
D_{u}=D_{w}
; for an example see Figure
17
. Therefore,
u^{\prime}=w^{\prime}
by point (1), thus
u=w
.

(3)

If
f_{\bullet}(w)=0
and
f_{\circ}(w)=1
, then this case is analogous to the previous one.

(4)

If
f_{\bullet}(w)=f_{\circ}(w)=1
, then
w=s_{0}s_{1}w^{\prime}
and

both reduced,

and

for the same argument in point (2). Moreover,
f_{\bullet}(w^{\prime})=0
and

, so

by point (3). Hence,

.

∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
. If
u\in\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})\cup\mathrm{K}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
such that
D_{u}=D_{w}
, then
u=w
.

If
u\in\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, then
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w),{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{R}}}(w)\in\{\{s_{0},s_{1}\},\{s_{n+1},s_{n+2}\}\}
by Theorem
5.6
. Hence,
w
is both left and right irreducible by Lemma

, so
w\in\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
. Then,
u=w
by Proposition
6.1
.

If
u\in\mathrm{K}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, by Theorem
5.6
we can assume
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
and

. Therefore,
w\in\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})

. Then,

.

Theorem 6.5
.

The map
\widetilde{\theta}_{D}:{\mathrm{TL}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})\rightarrow{\mathbb{D}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}[\delta]
-algebra isomorphism.

Proof.

First, we have that
\widetilde{\theta}_{D}
is a surjective homomorphism by definition. So we only need to show that the set
\{D_{w}\in{\mathbb{D}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})\mid w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})\}
is a basis for
{\mathbb{D}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
. Since the diagram
D_{w}
, for all
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, does not contain any undecorated loops by Proposition
4.10
, it is sufficient proving that if
u,w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
such that
D_{u}=D_{w}
then
u=w
. Suppose that
w
is reducible to
v\in\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
by a sequence
w_{0}=w\rightsquigarrow w_{1}\rightsquigarrow w_{2}\rightsquigarrow\cdots\rightsquigarrow w_{l}=v
.
We proceed by induction on
l
. If
l=0
, then
w\in\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, hence
u=w
by Propositions
6.3
and
6.4
. Without loss of generality, suppose that
w\rightsquigarrow_{t}sw=:w_{1}
. We have that
s\in{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u)
, since
{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u)={\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)
by Theorem
5.6
. Suppose that
st
is not a prefix of
u
, then
tu\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
reduced by Remark
1.12
(5). Moreover,
D_{w_{1}}=D_{t}D_{w}=D_{t}D_{u}=D_{tu}
so by inductive hypothesis, it means
w_{1}=tu
. But this is a contradiction since
ts
is not a prefix of
w_{1}
but it is a prefix of
tu
. Therefore,
u\rightsquigarrow_{t}su=:u_{1}
. Hence,
D_{w_{1}}=D_{t}D_{w}=D_{t}D_{u}=D_{u_{1}}
so by inductive hypothesis,
u_{1}=w_{1}
and
u=w
.
∎

7.
Some remarks on the Lusztig’s
\mathbf{a}
-function

In
[
12
]
, Lusztig introduced the function
\mathbf{a}:W\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}
which plays an important role in Kazdhan–Lusztig theory. For instance, this function is constant on the
LR
-cells of a Coxeter group
[
12
, Theorem 5.4]
. In
[
13
]
, Shi provided a more manageable way to compute the
\mathbf{a}
-function of a
{\mathrm{FC}}
element of a finite or affine Weyl group
W
through its heap. Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
, define
n(w)
as the cardinality of the maximum antichain in the heap
H(w)
. Equivalently, by
[
13
, Lemma 2.7]
,
n(w)
is the maximum integer
k
such that
w=uw^{\prime}v
is reduced,
\ell(w^{\prime})=k
and
w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{CC}(W)
.
In
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
, Shi proved the following important result.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(W)
. Then
\mathbf{a}(w)=n(w)
.

In
[
6
, Lemma 5.4, 5.5]
, Ernst proved that when
W
is an affine Coxeter system of type
\widetilde{C}_{n}
, then
n(w)=\mathbf{a}(D_{w})
, where
D_{w}
is the decorated diagram associated to
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}(\widetilde{C}_{n})
in the diagrammatic realization of
{\mathrm{TL}}(\widetilde{C}_{n})
he introduced in
[
5
,
6
]
, and
\mathbf{a}(D_{w})
is the number of non-propagating edges on the north face of
D_{w}
. However, this property does not hold for the diagrammatic realization of
{\mathrm{TL}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
used in this paper. For instance, if
w=s_{0}s_{1}
, we have
2=n(s_{0}s_{1})\neq\mathbf{a}(D_{w})=1
. Our goal is to provide a function defined on the
LR
-decorated diagrams of type
{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}
, that coincides with Lusztig’s
\mathbf{a}
-function.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
. We define the function
\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}(D_{w})
as follows:

\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}(D_{w}):=\begin{cases}\mathbf{a}(D_{w})+\#{\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}}+\#{\mathcal{L}_{\circ}},&\text{if }\mathbf{a}(D_{w})>1;\\
\mathbf{a}(D_{w})+\lambda,&\text{if }\mathbf{a}(D_{w})=1,\end{cases}

where

\lambda:=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\#{\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}}=\#{\mathcal{L}_{\circ}}=0;\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Theorem 7.3
.

If
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, then
n(w)=\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}(D_{w})
.

Proof.

By the definitions of irreducible FC elements it is immediate to compute that
n(w)=|{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{L}}}(w)|
for
w\in\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, that is:

n(w)=\begin{cases}\ell(w),&\mbox{if }w\in\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}});\\
2,&\mbox{if }w\in\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}});\\
n/2+1,&\mbox{if }w\in\mathrm{K}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}).\end{cases}

By Proposition
4.9
, if
w\in\mathrm{CZ}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
then
\mathbf{a}(D_{w})=1
. By Lemma
4.6
, if
w\in\mathrm{K}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, then
D_{w}
has
n/2+1
non-propagating edges. If
w\in\mathrm{CC}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
, by Corollary
5.7
,
\mathbf{a}(D_{w})=\ell(w)-f_{\bullet}(w)-f_{\circ}(w)
. Hence, if
w\in\mathrm{I}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
,
n(w)=\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}(D_{w})
by Proposition
4.10
. Moreover, by
[
13
, Lemma 2.9]
,
n(w)
is invariant by star reducibility. On the other hand, by Remarks
2.2
(b) and
5.4
, and by Proposition
4.10
,
\mathbf{a}(D_{w})
,
\#{\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}}
and
\#{\mathcal{L}_{\circ}}
are invariant by star reducibility as well.
Hence
n(w)=\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}(D_{w})
for any
w\in{\mathrm{FC}}({\widetilde{D}_{n+2}})
.
∎

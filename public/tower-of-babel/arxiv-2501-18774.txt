Rank stability in quadratic extensions and Hilbert’s tenth problem for the ring of integers of a number field
Levent Alpöge, Manjul Bhargava, Wei Ho, Ari Shnidman

Abstract
We show that for any quadratic extension of number fields K / F K/F , there exists an abelian variety A / F A/F of positive rank whose rank does not grow upon base change to K K . This result implies that Hilbert’s tenth problem over the ring of integers of any number field has a negative solution. That is, for the ring 𝒪 K {\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K} of integers of any number field K K , there does not exist an algorithm that answers the question of whether a polynomial equation in several variables over 𝒪 K {\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K} has solutions in 𝒪 K {\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K} .

Introduction

In his celebrated 1900 address to the International Congress of Mathematicians, Hilbert
[
Hil02
]
discussed 23 major open problems of importance for 20th century mathematics. In particular, his Problem #10 called for an algorithm to determine whether an integer polynomial equation in several variables has solutions in the integers.

Hilbert’s 10th problem was answered in the negative by the work of Davis, Putnam, and Robinson
[
DPR61
]
and Matiyasevich
[
Mat70
]
. The question has since remained whether Hilbert’s 10th problem still has a negative answer when the ring of rational integers is replaced by the ring of integers in some number field.

Poonen
[
Poo02
]
and independently Cornelissen, Pheidas, and Zahidi
[
CPZ05
]
proved that for a number field
K
, if there exists an elliptic curve defined over
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
with
\rank E({\rm\mathbb{Q}})=\rank E(K)=1
, then Hilbert’s 10th problem has a negative answer for
O_{K}
. This was strengthened independently by Poonen and by Shlapentokh
[
Shl08
]
to show that the latter condition can be replaced by
\rank E({\rm\mathbb{Q}})=\rank E(K)>0
. Shlapentokh
[
MR10
, Section 8]
also showed that if, for every cyclic extension
K/F
of prime degree, there exists an elliptic curve
E/F
such that
\rank E(F)=\rank E(K)>0
, then Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative solution for the ring of integers of every number field. This was later further strengthened by Shlapentokh
[
MRS24
, Theorems 3.1 and 4.8]
to show that if, for every
quadratic
extension
K/F
of number fields, there exists an
abelian variety

A/F
such that
\rank A(F)=\rank A(K)>0
, then Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative solution for the ring of integers of every number field.

It is the hypothesis of the latter result of Shlapentokh that we prove in this paper:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K/F
be any quadratic extension of number fields.
Then there exists an abelian variety
A/F
such that
\rank A(F)=\rank A(K)>0
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
K
be any number field. Then
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
has a diophantine model over
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K}
, and so
Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer over
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K}
.
That is, for the ring
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K}
of integers of any number field
K
, there does not exist an algorithm that answers the question of whether a polynomial equation in several variables over
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K}
has solutions.

Mazur and Rubin
[
MR10
]
showed that Corollary
1.2
follows from the conjectural finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group; see also the work of Murty and Pasten
[
MP18
]
and Pasten
[
Pas23
]
for two other conditional results. Unconditional proofs for particular classes of number fields were given by a number of authors over the years, including Denef
[
Den75
,
Den80
]
, Denef and Lipshitz
[
DL78
]
, Pheidas
[
Phe88
]
, Shlapentokh
[
Shl89
]
, Videla
[
Vid89
]
, Shapiro–Shlapentokh
[
SS89
]
, Garcia-Fritz–Pasten
[
GFP20
]
, Shnidman–Weiss
[
SW23
]
, and Kundu–Lei–Sprung
[
KLS24
]
.
In a beautiful recent preprint, Koymans and Pagano
[
KP24
]
give an independent unconditional proof of Corollary
1.2
, via a slightly different version of Theorem
1.1
, using different methods. However, it is interesting to note that both their proof and ours make use of results in additive combinatorics in number fields, such as the recent work of Kai
[
Kai23
]
, though in rather different ways.

\ell

We consider the family of curves

[

J_{n}
has

We thereby conclude that

\rank A^{q}(K)=\rank A(F)+\rank A^{q}(F)=\rank A^{q}(F)>0,

as desired.

2
The curve
y^{2}=x^{\ell}+1
and its twists

Let
\ell
be an odd prime, and let
F
be a number field containing the group
\mu_{\ell}
of
\ell
-th roots of unity. Let
\zeta\in\mu_{\ell}
be a generator. For
n\in F^{\times}
, let
C_{n}
be the smooth projective hyperelliptic curve of genus
(\ell-1)/2
in weighted projective space
{\rm\mathbb{P}}(1,(\ell-1)/2,1)
having affine model
y^{2}=x^{\ell}+n
. Note that for any
\lambda\in F^{\times}
, the curves
C_{n}
and
C_{n\lambda^{2\ell}}
are isomorphic. The automorphism
(x,y)\mapsto(\zeta x,y)
induces an action of
\mu_{\ell}
on
C_{n}
, and hence an action of the ring
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta]
on the Jacobian
J_{n}:=\Jac(C_{n})
.

It will be important for us that the varieties
C_{n}
and
J_{n}
admit both quadratic twists and
\mu_{\ell}
-twists. For a quadratic extension
K=F(\sqrt{q})
of
F
, the
K
-quadratic twist
C_{n}^{K}
of
C_{n}
is the curve
qy^{2}=x^{\ell}+n
, which is also isomorphic to
C_{q^{\ell}n}
. Similarly, the
K
-quadratic twist
J^{K}_{n}
of
J_{n}
is isomorphic to
J_{q^{\ell}n}
. The
\mu_{\ell}
-twists of
C_{n}
are the curves
C_{nr^{2}}
, for
r\in F^{\times}
, as
C_{nr^{2}}
becomes isomorphic to
C_{n}
over
F(r^{1/\ell})
, and the
\mu_{\ell}
-twists of
J_{n}
are the Jacobians
J_{nr^{2}}
.

2.1
(1-\zeta)
-Selmer groups

Via the embedding
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta]\hookrightarrow{\rm End}(J_{n})
, we view
1-\zeta
as a self-isogeny
\phi\colon J_{n}\to J_{n}
of degree
\ell
. We study the corresponding Selmer groups
{\rm Sel}_{\phi}(J_{n})
below; see
[
PS97
]
for further background on these.

We embed
C\hookrightarrow J
via
P\mapsto P-\infty
, using the base point
\infty=[1\colon 0\colon 0]\in C_{n}(F)
. The one-dimensional
{\rm\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
-vector space
J_{n}[\phi](\overline{F})=\ker(\phi)(\overline{F})
is generated by the point
(0,\sqrt{n})
, and the Galois action
{\rm Gal}(\overline{F}/F)\to{\rm Aut}(J_{n}[\phi](\overline{F}))\simeq{\rm\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
on
J_{n}[\phi]
is the quadratic character cutting out
F(\sqrt{n})/F
.

Set
T:=H^{1}(F,J_{n}[\phi])
. For a prime
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
, let
F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
be the corresponding completion of
F
, and set
T_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}:=H^{1}(F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}},J_{n}[\phi])
. Let
W_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}\subset T_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
be the image of the boundary map
J_{n}(F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}})\to T_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
coming from the short exact sequence

0\to J_{n}[\phi]\to J_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}J_{n}\to 0.

(2.1)

The
\phi
-Selmer group
{\rm Sel}_{\phi}(J_{n})
is defined to be the kernel of the homomorphism

T\to\prod_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}T_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}/W_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}

induced by the product of restriction maps
T\to T_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
. Note that we have ignored the archimedean places, since
F
is totally complex.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
{\rm Sel}_{\phi}(J_{n})=0
, then
\rank J_{n}(F)=0
.

Proof.

The long exact sequence associated to
(\ref{eq: ses})
gives
J_{n}(F)/\phi J_{n}(F)\hookrightarrow{\rm Sel}_{\phi}(J_{n})
, hence the usual Selmer group inequality
\rank_{{\rm\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta]}J_{n}(F)\leq\dim_{{\rm\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}{\rm Sel}_{\phi}(J_{n})
.
∎

2.2
Silent primes

Crucial to our method is the presence of so-called
silent primes
, i.e., primes
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
with
T_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}=0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}\nmid\ell
is inert or ramified in
F(\sqrt{n})/F
. Then
T_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}=0
.

Proof.

Since
F
contains
\mu_{\ell}
, we see that
J_{n}[\phi]
is isomorphic to its own Cartier dual. Hence, local Tate duality gives
H^{2}(F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}},J_{n}[\phi])\simeq H^{0}(F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}},J_{n}[\phi])
, and the local Euler characteristic formula reads

\#T_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}=\#H^{0}(F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}},J_{n}[\phi])\cdot\#H^{2}(F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}},J_{n}[\phi])=(\#J_{n}[\phi](F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}))^{2}=1.

The last equality follows because
\sqrt{n}\notin F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
.
∎

Note that silent primes exist if and only if
n
is not a square in
F
.

2.3
Twists of rank zero

Suppose now that
K=F(\sqrt{q})
is a quadratic extension of

that is unramified at all primes
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
above
\ell

Since

Following the methods of Mazur and Rubin

(

There exists

Proof.

The first sentence is Yu’s theorem, his assumption

In the sequel, fix

as in Lemma
2.3
. We will use the following notation for special sets of primes in

:

•

S^{\prime}
is the set of primes
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
dividing either
\ell!
or the conductor of
J_{q^{\ell}r^{2}}
;

•

S\subset S^{\prime}
is the subset of
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}\in S^{\prime}
that are either above
\ell

;

•

S_{\rm inert}
is the infinite set of primes

that are inert in

;

•

\Sigma:=S\cup S_{\rm inert}
.

Finally, let
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F,\Sigma}^{\times}\subset F^{\times}
be the set of
\Sigma
-units, i.e., those

such that
x\in{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F,{\rm\mathfrak{p}}}^{\times}
for all primes
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}\notin\Sigma
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
t\in{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F,\Sigma}^{\times}
and suppose that
t\in F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}^{\times\ell}
for all
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}\in S
.
Then
{\rm Sel}_{\phi}(J_{q^{\ell}r^{2}t^{2}})=0
.

Proof.

The Selmer groups
{\rm Sel}_{\phi}(J_{q^{\ell}r^{2}})
and
{\rm Sel}_{\phi}(J_{q^{\ell}r^{2}t^{2}})
live inside the common ambient space
T
; we will show that their corresponding local conditions

are equal inside
T_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
for all primes
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
. If
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}\in S
, this is because the two curves are isomorphic over
F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
. Otherwise, if
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
is inert or ramified in

, then
T_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}=0
by Lemma

. In all other cases, we have
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}\nmid\ell
and
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
is a prime of good reduction for
J_{q^{\ell}r^{2}}
and
J_{q^{\ell}r^{2}t^{2}}
, and thus

.
∎

2.4
Twists of positive rank

We continue with the assumptions on
F
and
K
from §
2.3
. To produce rational points on certain
K
-quadratic twists
J_{r^{2}t^{2}}
, we will use solutions to the
\Sigma
-unit equation
x+2ry=1
. First we need to know that many such solutions exist. For
a,b\in F^{\times}
, write
t_{a,b}\in F^{\times}/F^{\times\ell}
for the class of
a^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}b
.

Proposition 2.5
.

The set

X:=\left\{t_{a,b}:a,b\in{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F,\Sigma}^{\times},1=a+2rb,a^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}b\in F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}^{\times\ell}\text{ for all }{\rm\mathfrak{p}}\in S\right\}\subseteq F^{\times}/F^{\times\ell}

is infinite.

Granting Proposition
2.5
, whose proof we defer until Section
3
, we will produce rational points on the curves
C_{r^{2}b^{2}a^{\ell-1}}
. To show that these rational points are not (usually) torsion points, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
n\in F^{\times}
. There are finitely many
t\in F^{\times}/F^{\times\ell}
such that
J_{nt^{2}}(F)_{\mathrm{tors}}\neq J_{nt^{2}}[\phi](F)
.

Proof.

A version of this lemma holds for the twists of any fixed abelian variety, but the proof in this case is simplified by observing that the Jacobians
J_{nt^{2}}
have complex multiplication, hence have everywhere potentially good reduction
[
ST68
, §5]
. It follows that there is a uniform upper bound (depending only on
\ell
and
F
) on the order
\#J_{nt^{2}}(F)_{\mathrm{tors}}
of the torsion subgroup. Indeed, if
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
is a place of
F
above a rational prime
p
, then we may pass to a totally ramified extension of
F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
over which
J_{nt^{2}}
attains good reduction
[
ST68
, p. 498]
. We then use the Weil bound over
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}/{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
to bound the prime-to-
p
part of
J_{nt^{2}}(F)_{\mathrm{tors}}
. Applying this to two different primes
p
gives a bound on
\#J_{nt^{2}}(F)_{\mathrm{tors}}
.
Thus, we may choose an integer
N
which is a multiple of
\#J_{nt^{2}}(F)_{\mathrm{tors}}
, for all
t\in F^{\times}
. Let
\chi_{t}\colon{\rm Gal}(\overline{F}/F)\to\mu_{\ell}
be the character
\sigma\mapsto(\sqrt[\ell]{t^{2}})^{\sigma}/\sqrt[\ell]{t^{2}}
,
so that there is an isomorphism of
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta][{\rm Gal}(\overline{F}/F)]
-modules
J_{nt^{2}}[N]\simeq J_{n}[N]\otimes\chi_{t}^{-1}
. We see that if
J_{nt^{2}}[N](F)
contains a point which is not killed by
1-\zeta
, then the character
\chi_{t}
is a subrepresentation of
J_{n}[N]
. This shows that there are only finitely many
t\in F^{\times}/F^{\times\ell}
such that
J_{nt^{2}}(F)_{\mathrm{tors}}\neq J_{nt^{2}}[\phi](F)
.
∎

Over
\overline{{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}
, the curves
C_{n}
are
\mu_{\ell}
-quotients of the
\ell
-th Fermat curve
x^{\ell}+y^{\ell}=z^{\ell}
. We use a twisted version of this covering to produce rational points on
C_{r^{2}b^{2}a^{\ell-1}}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

For all but finitely many
t_{a,b}\in X
, the Jacobian
J_{r^{2}a^{\ell-1}b^{2}}
has positive rank.

Proof.

For all but finitely many
t_{a,b}\in X
, we have
J_{r^{2}a^{\ell-1}b^{2}}(F)_{\mathrm{tors}}=J_{r^{2}a^{\ell-1}b^{2}}[\phi]
, by Lemma
2.6
. Suppose
(a,b)
is such a pair.
Let
\widetilde{C}\colon ax^{\ell}+2rby^{\ell}=z^{\ell}
be the twisted Fermat curve and consider the cover
\pi\colon\widetilde{C}\to C_{r^{2}a^{\ell-1}b^{2}}
given on the affine patch where
z=1
by

\pi(x,y)=(axy^{-1},a^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}(y^{-\ell}-rb)).

Since
1=a+2rb
, the point
P=(a,a^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}(1-rb))=\pi(1,1)
lies in
C_{r^{2}a^{\ell-1}b^{2}}(F)
. Since
a\neq 0
, this is not a torsion point, and therefore
J_{r^{2}a^{\ell-1}b^{2}}
has positive rank.
∎

3
Proof of Proposition
2.5

In this section, we give a proof of Proposition
2.5
. The argument is standard, but for convenience in citing the literature, we prove a much stronger result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
F
be a totally complex number field. Let
I\subseteq{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}
be an ideal. Let
C\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
be prime to
I
. Let
\beta\in 2{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}
be nonzero. Let
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\in\left({\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}/(C)\right)^{\times}
be such that
u_{1}+\beta u_{2}\equiv u_{3}\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muC)
. Then there exist infinitely many triples
(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{\times})
such that:

•

p_{i}\in I
with
{\rm Nm}{(p_{i})}/{\rm Nm}{(I)}
prime;

•

p_{1}+\beta p_{2}=p_{3}
; and

•

p_{i}\equiv u_{i}\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muC)
.

Proof.

This is a standard modification of Vinogradov’s
1937
circle method argument solving ternary Goldbach for sufficiently large integers
[
Vin04
]
, which was subsequently applied to three-term homogeneous linear equations in the primes by van der Corput
[
vdC39
]
. The generalization of the method to number fields is due to Mitsui
[
Mit60
]
.

We briefly sketch the argument here. One first reduces to needing to estimate the following sum of trigonometric integrals, which counts the number of solutions to
p_{1}+\beta p_{2}=p_{3}
of bounded height:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left({\rm Nm}_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}\,{C}\right)^{3}}\sum_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}/(C)}

\displaystyle e\left(-\frac{{\rm tr}_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}\left(\alpha_{1}u_{1}+\alpha_{2}u_{2}+\alpha_{3}u_{3}\right)}{C}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{\xi\in\left(\mathfrak{d}_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}I\right)^{-1}\otimes_{\rm\mathbb{Z}}{\rm\mathbb{R}}/{\rm\mathbb{Z}}}d\xi\,S_{N}\left(\xi+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{C}\right)S_{N}\left(\beta\xi+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{C}\right)S_{N}\left(-\xi+\frac{\alpha_{3}}{C}\right),

where

\displaystyle S_{N}(z):=\sum_{p\in I:||p||_{\infty}\leq N}\Lambda_{F}\left((p)I^{-1}\right)e\left({\rm tr}_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}\left(zp\right)\right),

and
\Lambda_{F}:=\mu_{F}*\log
is the von Mangoldt function of
F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
, and
||x||_{\infty}:=\max_{v\mid\infty}|x|_{v}
.

This may be broken up into major and minor arcs in the standard way (with the same height cutoff as in Vinogradov, up to implicit constants), as in
[
Mit60
]
. To treat the integral over the major arcs and compute the singular series, we apply Mitsui’s number field generalization
[
Mit56
]
of the Siegel-Walfisz theorem. For the integral over the minor arcs, we use the Vaughan identity and repeated applications of Cauchy-Schwarz (both for the integral of the remaining two-variable exponential sum over the minor arcs as well as for the
Type II sum arising from Vaughan), as in
[
Mit60
]
. The additive character modulo
C
serves to translate the dual vector and thus only affects implicit constants, e.g., in the geometric series estimate on the minor arcs. The finite part of the singular series is (ignoring small primes)

\prod_{{\rm\mathfrak{p}}}\lim_{n\to\infty}{\rm Nm}({\rm\mathfrak{p}})({\rm Nm}({\rm\mathfrak{p}})-1)^{-3}\#\{(p_{1},p_{2}):p_{1},p_{2},p_{1}+\beta p_{2}\in({\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}/{\rm\mathfrak{p}}^{n})^{\times}\},

which is positive by Hensel lifting and the given hypotheses, yielding the desired result.

The proposition can also be deduced from a recent and far more general result of Kai

such that

{\rm\mathfrak{m}}

, so that
{\rm\mathfrak{m}}
is divisible by a prime
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
if and only if
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
ramifies in

{\rm Cl}_{F}({\rm\mathfrak{m}})

{\rm\mathfrak{m}}

{\rm\mathfrak{m}}=1

and
r{\rm\mathfrak{m}}
) such that

. We say that

is an

{\rm Nm}(p)/{\rm Nm}(I)
is prime. Note that any

-prime

is an

-unit, and in particular a
\Sigma
-unit. Let
\gamma
be any element of
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}

\gamma\in{\rm\mathfrak{p}}
if and only if
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}\in S
.
We will apply Proposition
3.1
to the element
\beta=2r\gamma^{n\ell}
(for some large integer
n
to be chosen below as needed) and the ideal
I
. This gives us
I
-primes
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
such that
p_{1}+2r\gamma^{n\ell}p_{2}=p_{3}
. We may and will assume that
p_{2}\equiv p_{3}\pmod{{\rm\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}
, for all
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}\in S
. Now take
a=p_{1}/p_{3}
and

. These are
\Sigma
-units and they satisfy

by construction. It remains to check that

-adically close to

\ell
-th power. Similarly, since

is large and

, the unit
p_{2}/p_{3}
is an
\ell
-th power in
F_{\rm\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
. Thus

is indeed an
\ell
-th power in

We now consider the case where
{\rm\mathfrak{m}}\neq 1
, i.e., where

is not a subfield of the Hilbert class field
H/F
. Applying Chebotarev’s Theorem to the compositum

, we see that there exist prime elements

of

that are inert in

that are inert in

p_{i}
modulo each prime
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}\mid{\rm\mathfrak{m}}
. We now apply Proposition
3.1
to the element
\beta=2r\gamma^{n\ell}
and the ideal

a=p_{1}/p_{3}
and
b=\gamma^{n\ell}p_{2}/p_{3}

p_{2}\equiv p_{3}\pmod{{\rm\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}
for all
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}\in S

{\rm\mathfrak{m}}

Proof of the main theorem (Theorem

)

In this section, for any quadratic extension

Let
\ell

K

\ell

K(\zeta_{\ell})/F(\zeta_{\ell})
. Hence we may assume that
\zeta_{\ell}\in F
and
K/F
is unramified at primes above
\ell

2.3

Let
K=F(\sqrt{q})
, and choose
r
as in Lemma
2.3
. Then choose
a,b
as in Proposition
2.7
, so that
\rank J_{r^{2}a^{\ell-1}b^{2}}(F)>0
. Lemmas
2.1

2.4

J_{q^{\ell}r^{2}a^{\ell-1}b^{2}}(F)

\rank J_{r^{2}a^{\ell-1}b^{2}}(K)=\rank J_{r^{2}a^{\ell-1}b^{2}}(F)+\rank J_{q^{\ell}r^{2}a^{\ell-1}b^{2}}(F)=\rank J_{r^{2}a^{\ell-1}b^{2}}(F)>0,

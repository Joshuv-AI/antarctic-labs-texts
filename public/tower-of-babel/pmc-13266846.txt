Development and validation of a collagen score-based prediction model for neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy efficacy in locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Xiao-Feng Chen, Huai-Yuan Zhang, Wei-Jie Chen, Jun-Peng Lin, Hang Zhou, Yu-Jie Chen, Peng-Qiang Gao, Feng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe relationship between collagen features (CFs) in the tumor microenvironment (TME) of esophageal cancer (EC) and the therapeutic response to neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (nCIT) remains unclear. This study aimed to develop and validate a novel model based on CFs to predict the therapeutic response of patients with locally advanced EC to nCIT.MethodsIn this retrospective study, 79 newly diagnosed patients with resectable locally advanced EC were enrolled and randomly divided into a training cohort (54 patients) and a validation cohort (25 patients) at a 7:3 ratio. Multiphoton imaging (MPI) was used to quantitatively analyze collagen in pretreatment biopsy tissues. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression was applied to select significant CFs and calculate the collagen score. Univariate and multivariate analyses were performed to verify the predictive value of the collagen score for a good pathological response (GR).ResultsLASSO regression identified collagen-related parameters in EC biopsy specimens: F1 (collagen percentage area), F2 (fiber density), F8 (mean of histograms), F9 (mean of histograms), F102 (Gabor_variance_90°_1 scale), F117 (Gabor_mean_150°_2 scale), and F138 (Gabor_variance_90°_4 scale), on the basis of which a formula was established to calculate the collagen score. Univariate analysis revealed a significant difference in the collagen score between the GR and non-GR groups (P<0.001), and multivariate analysis confirmed that the collagen score was an independent risk factor (P=0.001). These results were validated in the validation cohort (P=0.009 for univariate analysis, P=0.02 for multivariate analysis). For predicting the GR, the area under the curve (AUC) values of the collagen score in the experimental and validation cohorts were 0.819 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.704–0.935] and 0.820 (95% CI: 0.645–0.995), respectively.ConclusionsMPI can be used to quantify the TME of EC on the basis of a collagen score, which has a certain ability to predict the efficacy of nCIT.Keywords: Esophageal cancer (EC), neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (nCIT), collagen features (CFs), multiphoton imaging (MPI)

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) is a high-incidence malignancy in China, with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma being the predominant pathological type (1,2). For the surgical treatment of EC, three-incision esophagectomy (the McKeown procedure) involving the neck, chest, and abdomen enables more thorough lymph node dissection. However, perioperative treatment is still required to improve patient survival rates. The therapeutic paradigm for resectable EC, especially for locally advanced resectable cases, is primarily based on the CROSS and NEOCRTEC-5010 trials, adopting the mode of neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy plus surgery. Nevertheless, the 5-year survival rate remains approximately 50% (3,4), necessitating the exploration of more effective therapeutic strategies to improve survival outcomes.

In recent years, with the introduction of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in cancer therapy, immunotherapy has been shown to benefit patients with advanced EC in both first- and second-line settings (5-9). Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (nCIT) has subsequently demonstrated favorable pathological complete response (pCR) rates in multiple clinical trials (10-14). nCIT offers patients more treatment options and increased survival rates, yet there is substantial heterogeneity in treatment response among individuals, ranging from pCR to disease progression during treatment. Clinicians are consistently concerned that disease progression following immunotherapy may convert initially resectable EC into unresectable disease, thus depriving patients of optimal treatment opportunities (15). Therefore, predicting treatment efficacy prior to nCIT and screening for potential beneficiaries have become key focuses and challenges for esophageal surgeons.

In clinical trials and routine clinical practice, the programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression level is typically used to predict the therapeutic efficacy of ICIs. The American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) has emphasized the correlation between PD-L1 expression and immunotherapy efficacy in patients with EC. However, clinical trial data indicate that high PD-L1 expression does not necessarily equate to superior immunotherapy outcomes. Hence, whether PD-L1 can be considered a prognostic biomarker for immunotherapy in EC remains controversial (16).

The efficacy of immunotherapy is closely associated with the tumor microenvironment (TME). Numerous studies have suggested a potential correlation between the TME and a good pathological response (GR) (17,18). The TME consists of tumor cellular components and a noncellular extracellular matrix (ECM). The ECM is a network composed of extracellular macromolecules that participate in the formation of the cellular microenvironment and regulate cellular physiological functions (19,20). The interaction between the ECM and tumor cells plays a crucial role in tumor progression, metastasis, and treatment response. As the major component of the ECM, collagen is significantly correlated with the biological behaviors of tumor cells, such as adhesion, invasion, and metastasis, and has been identified as a reliable biomarker for predicting treatment outcomes in various cancer types (21-24). Thus, collagen detection can serve as a complementary pathological indicator. Paidi et al. reported that collagen structure is associated with radiotherapy sensitivity in thymic tumors (25). Chemotherapy resistance has also been linked to increased collagen deposition and remodeling (26,27). Furthermore, previous studies found that the collagen structure in a biopsy is associated with the treatment response associated with nCRT in rectal cancer and breast cancer (28,29) However, the relationship between collagen structural features and immunotherapy response in EC remains unclear. Therefore, we hypothesize that the structural features of collagen in the TME of biopsy specimens are correlated with the immunotherapy response in patients with EC.

Traditional pathological images fail to visualize the collagen structure of the TME. Multiphoton imaging (MPI) is a rapid, label-free, high-resolution imaging technique that combines two nonlinear optical effects: second-harmonic generation (SHG) signals produced by collagen and two-photon excitation fluorescence (TPEF) signals from cells. Thus, the detailed collagen structural and cellular morphological information at the subcellular level can be observed (30,31). Owing to its high spatial resolution, low cellular damage, deep imaging depth, and lack of fluorescent agent requirements, MPI has been widely applied in life sciences (32). Importantly, SHG is highly sensitive to collagen structure, and thus provides critical insights into biological tissues and disease progression (33). Additionally, the high resolution of multiphoton images allows for the extraction and quantitative characterization of collagen structural features using image analysis techniques (34). Consequently, MPI and quantitative analysis techniques are employed to quantify the correlation between collagen structural features in biopsy samples and treatment outcomes.

In this study, we used MPI and quantitative analysis techniques to quantify the collagen structure in pretreatment biopsy samples from patients with EC receiving neoadjuvant therapy. We aimed to verify the relationship between the collagen score and nCIT efficacy and establish a novel prediction model by integrating relevant biomarkers. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-0991/rc).

Methods

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Fujian Cancer Hospital (No. K2023-187-01) and informed consent was obtained from all the patients. The patient inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) histopathologically confirmed esophageal squamous cell carcinoma before treatment, with histological specimens preserved for optical detection; (II) preoperative diagnosis of locally advanced EC [clinical tumor stage (T) ≥3 and/or node (N)-positive according to the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) classification], with exclusion of distant metastasis; and (III) completion of radical surgery following nCIT. The exclusion criterion was failure to complete radical surgery after neoadjuvant treatment for any reason.

Treatment course and collection of clinicopathological data

All patients underwent upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, and a pathological diagnosis of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma was confirmed prior to treatment. Clinical staging was determined on the basis of imaging findings, including contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) of the chest and abdomen and endoscopic ultrasonography. Bronchoscopy was performed for patients with EC lesions located at or above the tracheal carina. Cranial magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (18F-FDG PET) were selectively conducted. Before treatment, all patients underwent multidisciplinary team (MDT) discussion to determine the clinical tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage and develop a comprehensive treatment plan. Lymph node staging was performed in accordance with the 8th edition of the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Esophageal Cancer issued by the 8th edition of the AJCC. After MDT discussion, nCIT was administered with a 3-week cycle. The chemotherapy regimen consisted of nab-paclitaxel combined with platinum-based drugs, and the immunotherapeutic agent was a programmed cell death 1 (PD-1) inhibitor. After nCIT, chest CT was repeated to evaluate local lesions, general condition, and immune-related adverse events (irAEs). An MDT discussion was conducted to determine the feasibility of radical surgery. The surgeries were performed by experienced esophageal surgeons, and detailed surgical records were maintained.

Between February 2022 and March 2025, a retrospective analysis was performed on 79 patients who completed nCIT followed by radical surgery (Figure 1). Computer-generated random numbers were used to allocate the participants to the training and validation cohorts at a 7:3 ratio. Baseline clinicopathological characteristics, including sex, age, body mass index (BMI), primary tumor location, pretreatment tumor differentiation grade, clinical T stage before neoadjuvant treatment, clinical N stage before neoadjuvant treatment, pathological T stage after surgery, pathological N stage after surgery, PD-L1 expression level, and tumor regression grade (TRG), were retrospectively analyzed.

Definition of TRG

All the formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) specimens were cut into 5-µm-thick sections and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) to identify regions of interest (ROIs). The pathological response was diagnosed by experienced pathologists and documented as the TRG (31,32). TRG was defined according to the 8th edition of the AJCC staging manual: TRG 0 indicates no residual tumor cells; TRG 1 indicates single tumor cells or small tumor cell clusters; TRG 2 indicates residual cancer accompanied by desmoplastic reaction; and TRG 3 indicates minimal evidence of tumor response. GR was determined on the basis of the TRG classification: a TRG of 0–1 was defined as a GR, whereas a TRG of 2–3 was defined as a non-GR (35-37).

MPI acquisition and collagen feature (CF) extraction

The procedures for MPI and collagen structural feature extraction were as follows: MPI was performed using a 20× objective lens to scan the entire biopsy tissue, yielding high-resolution images for visualizing collagen structural features. Multiphoton images were subsequently compared with H&E-stained images for pathological evaluation. CF extraction was performed using MATLAB 2016b (MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA). A total of 142 CFs were extracted, including 8 morphological features and 134 texture features. The imaging and feature extraction procedures were conducted by an optical researcher blinded to the patients’ survival outcomes.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using the software SPSS 29.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R version 4.5.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Continuous variables are presented as the mean [standard deviation (SD)] or median [interquartile range (IQR)]. The Mann-Whitney U test was used to analyze nonnormally distributed continuous variables, Student’s t-test was used for normally distributed continuous variables, and Fisher’s exact test was used for categorical variables. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to calculate the odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) of the risk factors. All P values were two-sided.

Construction of the collagen score

A least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) logistic regression model was employed to select predictive CFs in the primary cohort. LASSO regression is a robust algorithm that enables the selection of the most predictive parameter values from high-dimensional data while mitigating overfitting. We decreased the regression coefficients via LASSO regression to construct CFs and identified the following seven significant predictive factors from the 142 CFs: F1 (collagen percentage area), F2 (fiber density), F8 (mean of histograms), F9 (mean of histograms), F102 (Gabor_variance_90°_1 scale), F117 (Gabor_mean_150°_2 scale), and F138 (Gabor_variance_90°_4 scale).

On the basis of the individualized levels of these seven CFs for each patient, the following formula was subsequently derived to calculate the collagen score:

Collagen score = −0.595 − 0.199 × (F1) − 1.855 × (F2) + 0.561 × (F8) + 1.125 × (F9) + 0.702 × (F102) − 3.080 × (F117) + 0.016 × (F138) (Figures 2,3).

Representative SHG images of the GR and non-GR groups are presented in Figure 4. For the prediction of GR, the collagen score yielded AUC values of 0.819 (95% CI: 0.704–0.935) in the training cohort and 0.820 (95% CI: 0.645–0.995) in the validation cohort (Figures 5,6). At the optimal cutoff determined by the maximum Youden index, the training cohort had a Youden index of 0.517, corresponding to a sensitivity of 93.3% and a specificity of 58.3%; the validation cohort achieved a Youden index of 0.699, with a sensitivity of 82.4% and a specificity of 87.5%. The calibration performance of the collagen score model was evaluated using the Hosmer-Lemeshow test, Brier score, and calibration curve. The Hosmer-Lemeshow test indicated satisfactory calibration in both the training (P=0.743) and validation cohorts (P=0.318). The Brier scores were 0.167 and 0.140 for the training and validation cohorts, respectively, and the calibration curve displayed good consistency between model-predicted probabilities and observed clinical outcomes.

Risk factors for GR

In terms of clinicopathological characteristics, univariate and multivariate analyses revealed no statistically significant differences in clinicopathological features, including PD-L1 expression levels, across both the training and validation cohorts (Table 1), except for the collagen score.

With respect to the collagen score, the univariate analysis indicated that in the training cohort, the collagen score of non-GR patients was significantly greater than that of GR patients (OR =5.115, P<0.001). Multivariate analysis also revealed a significant difference (OR =4.987, P=0.001). These findings were validated in the validation cohort (univariate analysis: P=0.009; multivariate analysis: P=0.02) (Table 2).

Discussion

The treatment of locally advanced resectable EC is centered on radical surgery, but the efficacy of surgery alone is suboptimal; thus, comprehensive perioperative therapy is equally crucial. nCIT, as a treatment regimen, has gained recognition among many clinicians. Currently, nCIT is widely adopted in clinical practice to enhance therapeutic efficacy (38). To predict the efficacy of nCIT, screening patients suitable for this treatment and identifying biomarkers with high predictive value are of paramount importance. Existing clinical biomarkers, such as PD-L1 and microsatellite instability (MSI), have a certain degree of significance, yet their predictive efficacy cannot fully meet clinical needs (39).

Collagen provides structural and mechanical support for cells and tissues and regulates various cellular functions. Accumulating evidence indicates that alterations in collagen structure within the TME may have a pivotal effect on tumor cell proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and survival. Increased collagen content and cross-linking can increase the rigidity of the tumor stroma, which facilitates tumor cell metastasis; these changes influence treatment sensitivity, including primary resistance to immunotherapy (40-42). Therefore, collagen, with its immense clinical application value, has become a research hotspot in personalized medicine. With the development of interdisciplinary medical engineering fields, MPI can be accurately and selectively used to visualize collagen in the TME in a label-free manner. We can increasingly extract high-throughput CF information from multiphoton images for subsequent data analysis to provide decision support.

First, H&E staining is currently the gold standard for pathological diagnosis; however, distinguishing collagen structures in tumor tissues solely by H&E staining is difficult, and additional Masson staining is needed (43). MPI is a label-free and noninvasive tool that can acquire collagen structural features from tumor tissues in the absence of fluorescent agents; it is comparable to H&E staining and does not affect the collagen structure (44,45). In addition, multiphoton images can display structures and colors similar to those of H&E staining through virtual H&E rendering, which facilitates clinical pathological interpretation. Studies have demonstrated that MPI technology can be used to quantitatively analyze preneoadjuvant treatment biopsy tissues and predict treatment responses in breast cancer patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy (28,46). On the basis of previous research, in this study, biopsy specimens of EC tissues were subjected to preneoadjuvant treatment, and 142 CFs were extracted for subsequent analysis. We here found a significant correlation between the collagen score (based on SHG imaging and quantitative analysis) and a GR. Seven collagen structural features were selected via LASSO regression, and the collagen score was calculated to quantify the TME and predict the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunotherapy. LASSO regression confirmed that this formula could predict the GR in both the training cohort (AUC: 0.819) and the validation cohort (AUC: 0.820).

Currently, there are few reliable clinical predictive indicators for nCIT of EC in clinical practice. The PD-L1 expression level may be the most reliable indicator, but it still has many limitations. In this study, both univariate and multivariate analyses verified that clinicopathological features, including PD-L1 expression, could not predict the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunotherapy. By contrast, univariate analysis revealed that the collagen score formula derived from LASSO regression could be used to calculate the collagen score, which can then be employed to effectively predict GR in the training cohort (P<0.001), and a significant difference was also observed in multivariate analysis (P=0.001). These results were validated in the validation cohort (univariate analysis: P=0.009; multivariate analysis: P=0.02). This study suggests that the SHG-based collagen score can be used to predict the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunotherapy, laying a foundation for future in-depth research and clinical application.

Conclusions

TME detection is closely correlated with the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunotherapy. The SHG-based collagen score can quantify the TME and effectively predict the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunotherapy in patients with EC.

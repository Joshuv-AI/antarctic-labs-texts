The Exact ε \varepsilon -Hypercyclicity Threshold
Geivison Ribeiro

Abstract
. In this paper we give an affirmative answer to the problem proposed by Bayart in [J. Math. Anal. Appl. 529 (2024), 127278]: given ε ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \varepsilon\in(0,1) , there exists an operator which is δ \delta -hypercyclic if and only if δ ∈ [ ε , 1 ) \delta\in[\varepsilon,1) ?



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
47A16, 47B37, 47A35, 37B20, 37A46

1.
Introduction

The study of operators with dense orbits, known as
hypercyclic operators
, has become a central
topic in linear dynamics. Since the pioneering works of Birkhoff
[
5
]
, MacLane
[
9
]

and Rolewicz
[
11
]
, and the systematic developments of Kitai
[
8
]
and
Godefroy–Shapiro
[
6
]
, the theory has evolved into a rich branch of operator theory;
see the monographs
[
4
,
7
]
and the notes by Shapiro
[
13
]
for an overview.

Given a continuous linear operator
T
on a Banach space
X
, the
T
-orbit of
x\in X
is the set

\mathcal{O}(x,T):=\{x,Tx,T^{2}x,\dots\}.

The operator
T
is called
hypercyclic
if there exists
x\in X
such that the orbit

\mathcal{O}(x,T)
is dense in
X
; such an
x
is said a hypercyclic vector for
T
.

A quantitative relaxation of this notion, introduced by Badea–Grivaux–Müller
[
1
]
and
further studied by Bayart
[
2
]
, is the following.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. A vector
x\in X
is called an
\varepsilon
-hypercyclic vector

for
T\in L(X)
if, for every nonzero
y\in X
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|T^{n}x-y\|\leq\varepsilon\|y\|.

The operator
T
is called
\varepsilon
-hypercyclic
if it admits an
\varepsilon
-hypercyclic vector.

This definition captures the idea of approximation up to a fixed relative error
\varepsilon
.
Bayart showed in
[
3
]
that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exist operators
that are
\delta
-hypercyclic for all
\delta>\varepsilon
but not for any
\delta<\varepsilon
, and
posed the following question:

(Question 2.6 in
[
3
]
):
does there exist an operator
which is
\delta
-hypercyclic if and only if
\delta\in[\varepsilon,1)
?

Our contribution.

We answer this question in the affirmative. We explicitly construct a weighted shift
T
on the Hilbert space

Z=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N};\ell^{2})

such that

\boxed{\;T\ \text{is}\ \delta\text{-hypercyclic }\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \delta\in[\varepsilon,1)\;}

This yields a sharp
\varepsilon
-threshold and, to our knowledge, the first explicit example
with this exact property.

2.
Setting and operator

Let the internal space be
H:=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
with the orthonormal basis
(e_{j})_{j\geq 0}
. Define

Z:=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N};H)=\{u=(u_{r})_{r\geq 0}:\ u_{r}\in H,\ \sum_{r}\|u_{r}\|_{H}^{2}<\infty\},

with norm
\|u\|_{Z}^{2}=\sum_{r}\|u_{r}\|_{H}^{2}
. The operator we shall construct is a weighted shift of the form

T(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2},\dots)=(A_{1}u_{1},A_{2}u_{2},A_{3}u_{3},\dots),

where the weights
(A_{n})\subset\mathcal{L}(H)
are uniformly bounded operators.

Remark 2.1
.

For
r,n\geq 0
one has

(T^{n}u)_{r}=A_{r+1}A_{r+2}\cdots A_{r+n}\,u_{r+n}.

We partition the internal indices
\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
into blocks

I_{k}=\{N_{k}+1,\dots,N_{k}+d_{k}\},\qquad d_{k}\uparrow\infty,\quad N_{k+1}=N_{k}+d_{k},\ N_{1}=0.

We also define

F_{k}:=\mathrm{span}\{e_{0}\}\oplus\mathrm{span}\{e_{j}:\ j\in I_{k}\},\qquad G_{k}:=F_{k}^{\perp},

and denote by
P_{k},Q_{k}
the corresponding orthogonal projections in
H
. For
u=(u_{r})_{r}\in Z
, write

P_{k}^{\flat}u:=(P_{k}u_{r})_{r},\qquad Q_{k}^{\flat}u:=(Q_{k}u_{r})_{r}.

Since
d_{k}\uparrow\infty
, it follows that
\|Q_{k}^{\flat}y\|_{Z}\to 0
for every
y\in Z
as established in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

With the notation above, for every
y=(y_{r})_{r}\in Z=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N};H)
one has

\|Q_{k}^{\flat}y\|_{Z}\longrightarrow 0\qquad(k\to\infty).

Proof.

Let
v\in H=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
and write
v=\sum_{j\geq 0}v_{j}e_{j}
.
Since
Q_{k}
projects onto
G_{k}=F_{k}^{\perp}
and

F_{k}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{0}\}\oplus\mathrm{span}\{e_{j}:\ j\in I_{k}\}
, we have

Q_{k}v=\sum_{j\notin\{0\}\cup I_{k}}v_{j}e_{j},\qquad\|Q_{k}v\|_{H}^{2}=\sum_{j\notin\{0\}\cup I_{k}}|v_{j}|^{2}.

The sets
\{0\}\cup I_{k}
increase with
k
and their union is
\mathbb{N}
, so

\mathbb{N}\setminus(\{0\}\cup I_{k})
decreases to the empty set.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, pick
J
such that
\sum_{j>J}|v_{j}|^{2}<\varepsilon^{2}
.
For
k
with
\{0\}\cup I_{k}\supset\{0,1,\dots,J\}
, it follows that

\|Q_{k}v\|_{H}^{2}\leq\sum_{j>J}|v_{j}|^{2}<\varepsilon^{2}.

Hence
\|Q_{k}v\|_{H}\to 0
for every
v\in H
.

Applying this argument coordinatewise to
y=(y_{r})_{r}\in Z
and using
Remark
2
on dominated convergence for the counting measure, we obtain

\|Q_{k}^{\flat}y\|_{Z}^{2}=\sum_{r\geq 0}\|Q_{k}y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}\longrightarrow 0.

∎

Remark 2.3

(Dominated convergence)
.

For each fixed
r
, one has
\|Q_{k}y_{r}\|_{H}\downarrow 0
as
k\to\infty
, and

0\;\leq\;\|Q_{k}y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}\;\leq\;\|y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}.

Since
(\|y_{r}\|_{H}^{2})_{r\geq 0}
is summable (because
y\in Z
), the dominated convergence theorem
for the counting measure on
\mathbb{N}
applies. Hence

\|Q_{k}^{\flat}y\|_{Z}^{2}\;=\;\sum_{r\geq 0}\|Q_{k}y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}\;\longrightarrow\;0.

Fix integers
R_{k}\uparrow\infty
and consider the orthogonal decomposition of
F_{k}
obtained by grouping the
indices of
I_{k}
according to their residue classes modulo
R_{k}
. More precisely, set

E_{k,r}\ :=\ \mathrm{span}\,\{\,e_{N_{k}+1+r+\ell R_{k}}\ :\ 0\leq\ell\leq m_{k,r}\,\}\ \subset F_{k},\qquad r=0,\dots,R_{k}-1,

where
m_{k,r}
is chosen so that all indices
N_{k}+1+r+\ell R_{k}
remain in
I_{k}
.
Then each
E_{k,r}
is finite-dimensional, and we have the orthogonal decomposition

F_{k}\;=\;\mathrm{span}\{e_{0}\}\ \oplus\ \bigoplus_{r=0}^{R_{k}-1}E_{k,r}.

For
m_{k}:=N_{k}+1
, define the plane

H_{k}:=\mathrm{span}\{e_{0},e_{m_{k}}\}\subset F_{k}.

and for each
k
, fix finite sets
\mathcal{I}_{k,j}\subset\mathbb{N}
and two integers

b_{k}^{(1)}<b_{k}^{(2)},

such that:

•

the sets
\mathcal{I}_{k,j}
are pairwise disjoint (both in
j
and in
k
);

•

\{b_{k}^{(1)},b_{k}^{(2)}\}
is disjoint from all the
\mathcal{I}_{k,j}
and, for
k\neq k^{\prime}
,

\{b_{k}^{(1)},b_{k}^{(2)}\}\cap\{b_{k^{\prime}}^{(1)},b_{k^{\prime}}^{(2)}\}=\varnothing
;

•

b_{k}^{(1)}\uparrow\infty
(hence also
b_{k}^{(2)}\uparrow\infty
).

3.
Prescription of the weights
(A_{n})_{n\geq 1}

We define
A_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(H)
according to the following rules (for every
k
).

(R1)

For each pair
(k,j)
and each index
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{k,j}
, we set:

(3.1)

A_{\ell}\big|_{E_{k,r}}=\lambda_{\ell}\,\mathrm{Id},\qquad\lambda_{\ell}\in\{2,1,1/2\},\qquad\text{whenever }\ \ell\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ R_{k}).

(3.2)

A_{\ell}\big|_{E_{k,r}}=\mathrm{Id}\qquad\text{whenever }\ \ell\not\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ R_{k}).

Moreover, whenever (
3.1
) applies (the unique
r
with
\ell\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ R_{k})
), also impose

(3.3)

A_{\ell}\big|_{E_{k,r^{\prime}}}=\mathrm{Id}\ (r^{\prime}\neq r),\qquad A_{\ell}e_{0}=e_{0},\qquad A_{\ell}\big|_{G_{k}}=\mathrm{Id}.

(R2)

For each
k\geq 1
, prescribe

(3.4)

A_{b_{k}^{(1)}}\big|_{H_{k}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[2.0pt]
0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad A_{b_{k}^{(2)}}\big|_{H_{k}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[2.0pt]
0&\tfrac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix},

while
A_{b_{k}^{(i)}}=\mathrm{Id}
on
F_{k}\ominus H_{k}
and on
G_{k}
(
i=1,2
). In particular, define

\big(A_{b_{k}^{(1)}}A_{b_{k}^{(2)}}\big)\big|_{H_{k}}=\mathrm{Id}_{H_{k}}
. The set
F_{k}\ominus H_{k}
is the orthogonal complement of
H_{k}
in
F_{k}
.

(R3)

If
n\notin\big(\bigcup_{k,j}\mathcal{I}_{k,j}\big)\cup\big(\bigcup_{k}\{b_{k}^{(1)},b_{k}^{(2)}\}\big)
, then

A_{n}=\mathrm{Id}
on
H
. Moreover, for all
n\geq 1
,

A_{n}e_{0}=e_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad A_{n}\big|_{G_{k}}=\mathrm{Id}\ \ \text{for every }k.

These prescriptions yield

\sup_{n\geq 1}\|A_{n}\|\ \leq\ 2.

By the notation

A_{b_{k}^{(1)}}\big|_{H_{k}}=\mathrm{diag}(1,2),\qquad A_{b_{k}^{(2)}}\big|_{H_{k}}=\mathrm{diag}(1,1/2),

we mean the following: on the plane
H_{k}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{0},e_{m_{k}}\}
, with respect to the orthonormal basis
(e_{0},e_{m_{k}})
,

A_{b_{k}^{(1)}}e_{0}=e_{0},\quad A_{b_{k}^{(1)}}e_{m_{k}}=2\,e_{m_{k}},\qquad A_{b_{k}^{(2)}}e_{0}=e_{0},\quad A_{b_{k}^{(2)}}e_{m_{k}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,e_{m_{k}}.

4.
The weighted shift operator.

Now, define
T:Z\to Z
by

T(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2},\dots)=(A_{1}u_{1},A_{2}u_{2},A_{3}u_{3},\dots),

that is, for
r,n\geq 0
,

(T^{n}u)_{r}=A_{r+1}A_{r+2}\cdots A_{r+n}\,u_{r+n}.

Lemma 4.1
.

For fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, if
n\geq 1
and
r\in\{0,\dots,R_{k}-1\}
, then

\big(A_{r+1}\cdots A_{r+n}\big)\big|_{E_{k,r}}=\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r<\ell\leq r+n\\
\ell\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ R_{k})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\ell}\Big)\mathrm{Id},\qquad\big(A_{r+1}\cdots A_{r+n}\big)\big|_{E_{k,r^{\prime}}}=\mathrm{Id}\quad(\text{if }r^{\prime}\neq r).

Proof.

Let us fix
r\in\{0,\dots,R_{k}-1\}
. By the definition of the operators
(A_{j})_{j\geq 1}
, we have

A_{j}|_{E_{k,r}}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{j}\,\mathrm{Id},&\text{if }j\equiv r\pmod{R_{k}},\\
\mathrm{Id},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
n\geq 1
and consider the product

P:=A_{r+1}\cdots A_{r+n}.

For each vector
x\in E_{k,r}
, the successive application of the factors
A_{r+1},A_{r+2},\dots,A_{r+n}
works as follows: each
A_{\ell}
with
r<\ell\leq r+n
and
\ell\equiv r\pmod{R_{k}}
multiplies
x
by the scalar
\lambda_{\ell}
, while all other factors
A_{\ell}
act trivially on
E_{k,r}
. Hence,

Px=\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r<\ell\leq r+n\\
\ell\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ R_{k})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\ell}\Big)x,\qquad x\in E_{k,r}.

Therefore,

P|_{E_{k,r}}=\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}r<\ell\leq r+n\\
\ell\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ R_{k})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\ell}\Big)\mathrm{Id}.

On the other hand, fix
r^{\prime}\neq r
. In this case, for every
\ell\geq 1
we have
A_{\ell}|_{E_{k,r^{\prime}}}=\mathrm{Id}
, since no index
\ell
can satisfy simultaneously
\ell\equiv r\pmod{R_{k}}
and
\ell\equiv r^{\prime}\pmod{R_{k}}
. Consequently,

P|_{E_{k,r^{\prime}}}=\mathrm{Id}.

Thus, the product
A_{r+1}\cdots A_{r+n}
acts on
E_{k,r}
as scalar multiplication by the product of the
\lambda_{\ell}
with
r<\ell\leq r+n
and
\ell\equiv r\pmod{R_{k}}
, and as the identity on each
E_{k,r^{\prime}}
with
r^{\prime}\neq r
, which completes the proof.
∎

Now, fix two sequences
\tau_{k},\eta_{k}\downarrow 0
and choose
\gamma\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that, for
k
large enough,

(4.1)

\tau_{k}+\eta_{k}+\gamma\ \leq\ \varepsilon.

Before proceeding, we need a discretization device:
any vector in
F_{k}
can be approximated, both in direction and magnitude,
by an element of a finite preassigned set.
The next lemma formalizes this idea.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
F_{k}
be a finite-dimensional subspace of
H
. Fix
0<\tau_{k}<1
and choose:

•

a finite set
U_{k}\subset S(F_{k}):=\{x\in F_{k}:\|x\|_{H}=1\}
which is a
\tau_{k}
-net of
S(F_{k})
, that is,

\forall v\in S(F_{k})\ \exists u\in U_{k}\ \text{ such that }\ \|v-u\|_{H}\leq\tau_{k};

•

parameters
J_{k},M_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
and the geometric grid

\mathcal{G}_{k}:=\{2^{m/J_{k}}:|m|\leq J_{k}M_{k}\},

where
J_{k}
is chosen so that
2^{1/(2J_{k})}-1\leq\tau_{k}
and
M_{k}
is large enough to cover the relevant magnitudes.

Then, for every
w\in F_{k}\setminus\{0\}
, there exist
u\in U_{k}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
such that

\|w-\alpha u\|_{H}\ \leq\ 2\,\tau_{k}\,\|w\|_{H}.

In particular, by redefining
\tau_{k}
, one may simply write
\|w-\alpha u\|_{H}\leq\tau_{k}\|w\|_{H}
.

Proof.

Let
w\in F_{k}\setminus\{0\}
and set
v:=w/\|w\|_{H}\in S(F_{k})
. By the definition of
U_{k}
, there exists
u\in U_{k}
such that

\|v-u\|_{H}\leq\tau_{k}.

Write
r:=\|w\|_{H}
and
x:=\log_{2}r
. Since
x\in[-M_{k},M_{k}]
, define

m:=\big\lfloor J_{k}x+\tfrac{1}{2}\big\rfloor\in\mathbb{Z}.

Hence,

|J_{k}x-m|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Big|x-\frac{m}{J_{k}}\Big|\leq\frac{1}{2J_{k}}.

Moreover, from
x\in[-M_{k},M_{k}]
it follows that
J_{k}x\in[-J_{k}M_{k},J_{k}M_{k}]
and therefore
|m|\leq J_{k}M_{k}
. Thus,

\alpha:=2^{m/J_{k}}\in\mathcal{G}_{k}.

From the above inequality we obtain

2^{-1/(2J_{k})}\ \leq\ \frac{\alpha}{r}\ \leq\ 2^{1/(2J_{k})}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Big|\frac{\alpha}{r}-1\Big|\leq 2^{1/(2J_{k})}-1\ \leq\ \tau_{k},

by the choice of
J_{k}
. Consequently,
|\alpha-r|\leq\tau_{k}r
.

Finally, we estimate

\|w-\alpha u\|_{H}=\|rv-\alpha u\|_{H}\ \leq\ \|rv-ru\|_{H}+\|ru-\alpha u\|_{H}\ \leq\ r\,\|v-u\|_{H}+|r-\alpha|\ \leq\ \tau_{k}r+\tau_{k}r=2\,\tau_{k}\,\|w\|_{H}.

∎

Next, for each fixed
k
, consider:

•

A set
U_{k}\subset\{u\in F_{k}:\|u\|_{H}=1\}
satisfying the following property: for every
v\in F_{k}\setminus\{0\}
there exists
u\in U_{k}
such that

\Big\|\frac{v}{\|v\|_{H}}-u\Big\|_{H}\leq\tau_{k};

•

The set
\mathcal{G}_{k}=\{2^{m/J_{k}}:|m|\leq J_{k}M_{k}\}
with
J_{k}\to\infty
.

Then define

S_{k}:=\{\alpha u:\ u\in U_{k},\ \alpha\in\mathcal{G}_{k}\}\subset F_{k},

and write

S_{k}^{R_{k}}:=\prod_{r=0}^{R_{k}-1}S_{k},

where
\prod_{r=0}^{R_{k}-1}S_{k}
denotes the Cartesian product.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
k\geq 1
be fixed. We say that
n\in\mathbb{N}
is a
simultaneous visiting time for the pair
(k,\mathbf{s})
, where
\mathbf{s}=(s_{0},\dots,s_{R_{k}-1})\in S_{k}^{R_{k}}
, if

(T^{n}x)_{r}=s_{r}\in F_{k}\quad(0\leq r<R_{k}),\qquad(T^{n}x)_{r}=0\quad(r\geq R_{k}).

Equivalently and more conveniently, this means
Q_{k}(T^{n}x)_{r}=0
for all
r\geq 0
, and for
r<R_{k}
,
P_{k}(T^{n}x)_{r}=s_{r}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose there exists
x\in Z
such that, for each
k\geq 1
and each
\mathbf{s}\in S_{k}^{R_{k}}
, there is a time
n_{k,\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{N}
which is a simultaneous visiting time for
(k,\mathbf{s})
. Then
x
is
\varepsilon
-hypercyclic for
T
.

Proof.

Let
y=(y_{r})_{r\geq 0}\in Z
,
y\neq 0
, be fixed. We shall show that there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|T^{n}x-y\|_{Z}\leq\varepsilon\|y\|_{Z}.

Since
y\in\ell^{2}(H)
, there exists
R\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(4.2)

\sum_{r\geq R}\|y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}\ \leq\ \gamma^{2}\|y\|_{Z}^{2}.

That is,

\|(y_{r})_{r\geq R}\|_{Z}\leq\gamma\|y\|_{Z}.

According to Lemma
2
,

\|Q_{k}^{\flat}y\|_{Z}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Hence we may choose
k
large enough such that

(4.3)

R\ \leq\ R_{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|Q_{k}^{\flat}y\|_{Z}\ \leq\ \eta_{k}\,\|y\|_{Z}.

For each
0\leq r<R
, if
P_{k}y_{r}=0
we set
s_{r}:=0
. Otherwise, by Remark
,
there exists
s_{r}\in S_{k}
such that

(4.4)

\|P_{k}y_{r}-s_{r}\|_{H}\ \leq\ \tau_{k}\,\|P_{k}y_{r}\|_{H}.

We then define
\mathbf{s}:=(s_{0},\dots,s_{R-1},0,\dots,0)\in S_{k}^{R_{k}}
for our purposes. By the hypothesis of the theorem, there exists
n:=n_{k,\mathbf{s}}
such that

(4.5)

(T^{n}x)_{r}=s_{r}\in F_{k}\ (0\leq r<R_{k}),\qquad(T^{n}x)_{r}=0\ (r\geq R_{k}).

Write
y_{r}=P_{k}y_{r}+Q_{k}y_{r}
(orthogonal decomposition in
H
). Since
s_{r}\in F_{k}
and
Q_{k}y_{r}\perp F_{k}
, the Pythagorean identity yields

(4.6)

\|s_{r}-y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}=\|s_{r}-P_{k}y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}+\|Q_{k}y_{r}\|_{H}^{2},\qquad 0\leq r<R.

For
r\geq R
, we have
(T^{n}x)_{r}=0
, hence

\|(T^{n}x-y)_{r}\|_{H}=\|y_{r}\|_{H}.

Summing over
r
and using (
4.5
), (
4.6
), (
4.4
), (
4.2
), (
4.3
), and (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|T^{n}x-y\|_{Z}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r<R}\|s_{r}-y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}+\sum_{r\geq R}\|y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r<R}\Big(\|s_{r}-P_{k}y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}+\|Q_{k}y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}\Big)+\sum_{r\geq R}\|y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{k}^{2}\sum_{r<R}\|P_{k}y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}+\|Q_{k}^{\flat}y\|_{Z}^{2}+\sum_{r\geq R}\|y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{k}^{2}\|y\|_{Z}^{2}+\eta_{k}^{2}\|y\|_{Z}^{2}+\gamma^{2}\|y\|_{Z}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\|y\|_{Z}^{2},

where in the penultimate line we used that
\sum_{r<R}\|P_{k}y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}\leq\sum_{r\geq 0}\|y_{r}\|_{H}^{2}=\|y\|_{Z}^{2}
.
This shows that
x
is
\varepsilon
-hypercyclic for
T
.
∎

To proceed with our goal, let us do the following. For each
k\geq 1
, recall that

U_{k}\subset\{u\in F_{k}:\|u\|_{H}=1\}\ \text{is finite},\qquad\mathcal{G}_{k}=\{2^{m/J_{k}}:\ |m|\leq J_{k}M_{k}\}\ \text{is finite},

with
M_{k}\uparrow\infty
. Thus,

S_{k}:=\{\alpha u:\ u\in U_{k},\ \alpha\in\mathcal{G}_{k}\}

is a finite set, of cardinality

|S_{k}|=|U_{k}|\cdot|\mathcal{G}_{k}|<\infty.

Hence, the Cartesian product

S_{k}^{R_{k}}:=\prod_{r=0}^{R_{k}-1}S_{k}

is also finite, with

|S_{k}^{R_{k}}|=|S_{k}|^{R_{k}}<\infty.

We may therefore set

J_{k}:=|S_{k}^{R_{k}}|\qquad\text{and enumerate}\qquad S_{k}^{R_{k}}=\{\mathbf{s}^{(k,1)},\dots,\mathbf{s}^{(k,J_{k})}\},

where each
\mathbf{s}^{(k,j)}=(s_{0},\dots,s_{R_{k}-1})
has
s_{r}\in S_{k}
.

Moreover, we consider the lexicographic order on pairs
(k,j)
: for
(k,j)
and
(k^{\prime},j^{\prime})
,

(k,j)\prec(k^{\prime},j^{\prime})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\big(k<k^{\prime}\big)\ \text{or}\ \big(k=k^{\prime}\ \text{and}\ j<j^{\prime}\big).

We shall use this order, which will help us choose the visiting times appropriately.

In order to define such times, let us require that

(k,j)\prec(k^{\prime},j^{\prime})\ \Longrightarrow\ n_{k,j}<n_{k^{\prime},j^{\prime}}.

We then define

\mathcal{I}_{k,j}:=\{\,n_{k,j}-L_{k,j}R_{k}+1,\ \dots,\ n_{k,j}\,\},

so that these sets
\mathcal{I}_{k,j}
are pairwise disjoint. We also assume that outside the union

\bigcup_{k,j}\mathcal{I}_{k,j},

the value
\lambda_{\ell}
appearing in (
3.1
) is equal to
1
.

Now, let us also define

(4.7)

\beta_{k,j,r}:=2^{-\,(j+R_{k})}\,2^{-k^{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad w_{k,j,r}:=\beta_{k,j,r}\,u_{k,j,r}\in E_{k,r},\quad\|u_{k,j,r}\|_{H}=1.

Note that
\beta_{k,j,r}

decays rapidly
in both
j
and
k
, which ensures that

\sum_{k,j,r}\|w_{k,j,r}\|_{H}^{2}<\infty

(see below).

Let us now introduce what we may call the number of cycles. For this purpose, for each
(k,j)
and each
0\leq r<R_{k}
, choose a nonnegative integer
L_{k,j,r}
such that

(4.8)

L_{k,j,r}\ \leq\ 2M_{k}+\Big\lceil\log_{2}\beta_{k,j,r}^{-1}\Big\rceil,

and define

L_{k,j}:=\max_{0\leq r<R_{k}}L_{k,j,r}.

We call the above number
L_{k,j,r}
the number of cycles.
Recall that
s_{r}=\alpha_{k,j,r}u_{k,j,r}\in S_{k}
with
\alpha_{k,j,r}\in\mathcal{G}_{k}=\{2^{m/J_{k}}:\,|m|\leq J_{k}M_{k}\}
, and that
w_{k,j,r}=\beta_{k,j,r}u_{k,j,r}
was defined in (
4.7
).

Definition 4.5
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
w\in H
, we denote by

\mathbf{e}_{\,n}(w)\in Z

the vector whose
n
-th coordinate is
w
and all others are zero, i.e.,

(\mathbf{e}_{\,n}(w))_{r}=\begin{cases}w,&r=n,\\
0,&r\neq n.\end{cases}

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
w_{k,j,r}\in E_{k,r}
be the vectors defined in (
4.7
). Then the vector

x:=\sum_{k,j}\ \sum_{r=0}^{R_{k}-1}\ \mathbf{e}_{\,n_{k,j}+r}(w_{k,j,r})

belongs to
Z
and satisfies, for every
(k,j)
,

(4.9)

(T^{n_{k,j}}x)_{r}=s_{r}\quad(0\leq r<R_{k}),\qquad(T^{n_{k,j}}x)_{r}=0\quad(r\geq R_{k}),\qquad Q_{k}(T^{n_{k,j}}x)_{r}=0.

Proof.

Fix
(k,j)
and
0\leq r<R_{k}
. On
\mathcal{I}_{k,j}
we assign multipliers

\lambda_{\ell}\in\{2,1,1/2\}
only at the indices
\ell\equiv r\pmod{R_{k}}
, so that

(4.10)

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{k,j}\\
\ell\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ R_{k})\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\ell}=\alpha_{k,j,r}\,\beta_{k,j,r}^{-1}.

By Lemma
4
, the product
A_{r+1}\cdots A_{r+n_{k,j}}
restricts to
E_{k,r}
and acts as the identity on
E_{k,r^{\prime}}
for
r^{\prime}\neq r
. Hence,

(T^{n_{k,j}}x)_{r}=(A_{r+1}\cdots A_{r+n_{k,j}})\,w_{k,j,r}=(\alpha_{k,j,r}\,\beta_{k,j,r}^{-1})(\beta_{k,j,r}u_{k,j,r})=s_{r}.

On the other hand, since
x
was constructed only with entries at positions

n_{k,j}+r
with
0\leq r<R_{k}
, it follows that at time
n_{k,j}
there are no
contributions in external coordinates
r\geq R_{k}
, that is,

(T^{n_{k,j}}x)_{r}=0\qquad(r\geq R_{k}).

Moreover, each
w_{k,j,r}
belongs to
E_{k,r}\subset F_{k}
, and all the
operators
A_{\ell}
preserve
F_{k}
.
Therefore,
(T^{n_{k,j}}x)_{r}\in F_{k}
for all
r
, and hence

Q_{k}(T^{n_{k,j}}x)_{r}=0.

It remains to show that
x\in Z
. Since the sets
\mathcal{I}_{k,j}
are disjoint, it follows from (
4.7
) that

\|x\|_{Z}^{2}=\sum_{k,j}\sum_{r=0}^{R_{k}-1}\|w_{k,j,r}\|_{H}^{2}=\sum_{k,j}R_{k}\,\beta_{k,j,r}^{2}.

Substituting
\beta_{k,j,r}=2^{-(j+R_{k})}\,2^{-k^{2}}
, we obtain

\|x\|_{Z}^{2}\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{k}}R_{k}\,2^{-2(j+R_{k})}\,2^{-2k^{2}}\leq C\sum_{k\geq 1}R_{k}\,2^{-2R_{k}}\,2^{-2k^{2}}<\infty,

since
R_{k}\uparrow\infty
and
J_{k}<\infty
for each
k
. Therefore
x\in Z
, which completes the proof.
∎

5.
Lower barrier for every
\delta<\varepsilon

Definition 5.1

(Projection onto
H_{k}
)
.

Let
H_{k}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{0},e_{m_{k}}\}\subset H
be the fixed two-dimensional plane.
For
v\in H
, we denote by
v^{H_{k}}
the orthogonal projection of
v
onto
H_{k}
, that is,

v^{H_{k}}:=P_{H_{k}}v.

In particular, for
u=(u_{r})_{r\geq 0}\in Z
and
n\geq 0
, we write

\big((T^{n}u)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}:=P_{H_{k}}\big((T^{n}u)_{0}\big)\in H_{k}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
u\in Z
and suppose that, for some
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
, the
H_{k}
–components of the
coordinates
u_{b_{k}^{(1)}}
and
u_{b_{k}^{(2)}}
are

u_{b_{k}^{(1)}}^{H_{k}}=u_{b_{k}^{(2)}}^{H_{k}}=ae_{0}+be_{m_{k}}.

Then the prescribed action of the weights on
H_{k}
yields

\big((T^{b_{k}^{(1)}}u)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}=ae_{0}+2be_{m_{k}},\qquad\big((T^{b_{k}^{(2)}}u)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}=ae_{0}.

Proof.

Recall that for
n\geq 1
,

(T^{n}u)_{0}=A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{n}\,u_{n}.

We have
A_{n}|_{H_{k}}=\mathrm{Id}
for
1\leq n\leq b_{k}^{(1)}-1
. Hence

\displaystyle\big((T^{b_{k}^{(1)}}u)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\big(A_{1}\cdots A_{b_{k}^{(1)}-1}\big)A_{b_{k}^{(1)}}\,u_{b_{k}^{(1)}}\Big)^{H_{k}}

\displaystyle=A_{b_{k}^{(1)}}\big|_{H_{k}}\,(ae_{0}+be_{m_{k}})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[2.0pt]
0&2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a\\
b\end{pmatrix}=ae_{0}+2be_{m_{k}}.

By construction of the block, we get

C_{k}:=\big(A_{b_{k}^{(2)}}\cdots A_{b_{k}^{(1)}+1}\big)\big|_{H_{k}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[2.0pt]
0&0\end{pmatrix}=P_{\mathrm{span}\{e_{0}\}}.

Then

C_{k}(ae_{0}+be_{m_{k}})=ae_{0}.

Using again that

A_{n}|_{H_{k}}=\mathrm{Id}
for
1\leq n\leq b_{k}^{(1)}-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big((T^{b_{k}^{(2)}}u)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\big(A_{1}\cdots A_{b_{k}^{(1)}}\big)\,\big(A_{b_{k}^{(1)}+1}\cdots A_{b_{k}^{(2)}}\big)\,u_{b_{k}^{(2)}}\Big)^{H_{k}}

\displaystyle=\Big(A_{b_{k}^{(1)}}|_{H_{k}}\Big)\,C_{k}\,(ae_{0}+be_{m_{k}})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[2.0pt]
0&2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a\\
0\end{pmatrix}=ae_{0}.

This proves the two identities.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be fixed. The operator
T
is not
\delta
-hypercyclic for any
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Set
v=(v_{r})_{r\geq 0}\in Z
by

v_{0}=Ke_{0}\quad(K>0),\qquad v_{r}=0\quad(r\geq 1).

Then
\|v\|_{Z}=\|v_{0}\|_{H}=K
. Let
u=(u_{r})_{r\geq 0}\in Z
and fix
k\geq 1
.

Recall that, for
n\geq 1
,

(T^{n}u)_{0}\;=\;A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{n}\,u_{n}.

Consequently,

\big((T^{n}u)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}=\;P_{H_{k}}\big(A_{1}\cdots A_{n}\,u_{n}\big),

where
P_{H_{k}}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
H_{k}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{0},e_{m_{k}}\}
.
Write

a_{n}:=\langle u_{n},e_{0}\rangle,\qquad b_{n}:=\langle u_{n},e_{m_{k}}\rangle,\qquad(u_{n})^{H_{k}}=a_{n}e_{0}+b_{n}e_{m_{k}}.

Since
v_{0}=Ke_{0}
and
v_{0}^{H_{k}}=Ke_{0}
, we compute

\displaystyle\big((T^{n}u-v)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}

\displaystyle=P_{H_{k}}\big((T^{n}u)_{0}\big)\;-\;P_{H_{k}}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=\big((T^{n}u)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}\;-\;Ke_{0}.

By Lemma
5
, at the programmed times
n\in\{b_{k}^{(1)},b_{k}^{(2)}\}
,

\big((T^{b_{k}^{(1)}}u)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}=a_{b_{k}^{(1)}}e_{0}+2\,b_{b_{k}^{(1)}}e_{m_{k}},\qquad\big((T^{b_{k}^{(2)}}u)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}=a_{b_{k}^{(2)}}e_{0}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\big((T^{n}u-v)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}

\displaystyle=\big((T^{n}u)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}-Ke_{0}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\big(a_{b_{k}^{(1)}}-K\big)\,e_{0}\;+\;2\,b_{b_{k}^{(1)}}\,e_{m_{k}},&n=b_{k}^{(1)},\\[3.0pt]
\big(a_{b_{k}^{(2)}}-K\big)\,e_{0},&n=b_{k}^{(2)}.\end{cases}

Equivalently, for
n\in\{b_{k}^{(1)},b_{k}^{(2)}\}
we can write

\big((T^{n}u-v)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}=(a_{n}-K)\,e_{0}+\xi_{n}\,e_{m_{k}},\quad\text{with}\quad\xi_{b_{k}^{(1)}}=2\,b_{b_{k}^{(1)}},\ \ \xi_{b_{k}^{(2)}}=0.

Hence

\big\|\big((T^{n}u-v)_{0}\big)^{H_{k}}\big\|_{H}=\sqrt{|a_{n}-K|^{2}+|\xi_{n}|^{2}}\ \geq\ |K-a_{n}|.

Now use that
u\in Z
implies
u_{n}\to 0
in
H
; in particular

a_{n}=\langle u_{n},e_{0}\rangle\to 0
. Fix
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
and pick
k
so large
that for
n\in\{b_{k}^{(1)},b_{k}^{(2)}\}
one has

|a_{n}|\ <\ \Big(1-\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,K.

Then

\varepsilon\,|K-a_{n}|\ \geq\ \varepsilon\,(K-|a_{n}|)\ >\ \varepsilon\Big(\tfrac{\delta}{\varepsilon}K\Big)\ =\ \delta K\ =\ \delta\,\|v\|_{Z},

and therefore, for such
n
,

\|T^{n}u-v\|_{Z}\ \geq\ |K-a_{n}|\ >\ \frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\,K\ \geq\ \delta\,\|v\|_{Z}.

Since there are infinitely many such
n
(two for each large
k
),
u
cannot approximate
v

with relative error
<\delta
. This shows that
T
is not
\delta
-hypercyclic for any

\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
.
∎

6.
The main theorem

We now combine the lower bound from Theorem
5.3
with the
construction of approximate orbits given in Section 2.
This leads to a complete characterization of the
\delta
–hypercyclicity
threshold for the operator
T
.

Theorem 6.1
.

For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, the operator
T
satisfies

T\text{ is }\delta\text{-hypercyclic}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\delta\in[\varepsilon,1).

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
)

By Lemma
4
and the construction of the vectors
w_{k,j,r}
in (
4.7
),
Lemma
4
guarantees the existence of
x\in Z
such that, for each
k\geq 1

and each
\mathbf{s}\in S_{k}^{R_{k}}
, there exists
n
with

(T^{n}x)_{r}=s_{r}\ (0\leq r<R_{k}),\qquad(T^{n}x)_{r}=0\ (r\geq R_{k}),\qquad Q_{k}(T^{n}x)_{r}=0.

Applying Theorem
4.4
, we obtain that
x
is
\varepsilon
-hypercyclic, that is,

\forall\,y\in Z\setminus\{0\}\ \exists\,n\geq 0:\quad\|T^{n}x-y\|_{Z}\leq\varepsilon\|y\|_{Z}.

It follows immediately that, for every
\delta\geq\varepsilon
, the same vector
x
also witnesses
the
\delta
-hypercyclicity of
T
.

(
\Rightarrow
)

On the other hand, fix
v=K\,\mathbf{v}^{(0)}\in Z
, (
K>0
) and consider
H_{k}=\mathrm{span}\{e_{0},e_{m_{k}}\}

with the instants
b_{k}^{(1)}<b_{k}^{(2)}
as defined in (
3.4
).
Exactly as in the proof of Theorem
5.3
, for every
u\in Z
and
n\in\{b_{k}^{(1)},b_{k}^{(2)}\}
,

\|(T^{n}u-v)^{H_{k}}\|_{Z}\ \geq\ \varepsilon\,|K-\langle u_{n},e_{0}\rangle|.

Since
u\in Z
implies
\|u_{n}\|_{H}\to 0
, it follows that
\langle u_{n},e_{0}\rangle\to 0
.
Fixing
K>0
and taking
k
sufficiently large, we get

\|(T^{n}u-v)\|_{Z}\ \geq\ \|(T^{n}u-v)^{H_{k}}\|_{Z}\ >\ \delta\,\|v\|_{Z},\qquad n\in\{b_{k}^{(1)},b_{k}^{(2)}\},

for any
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
. This shows that
T
is not
\delta
-hypercyclic
when
\delta<\varepsilon
(Theorem
5.3
).

Combining the two implications, we conclude that
T
is
\delta
-hypercyclic
if and only if
\delta\in[\varepsilon,1)
.
∎

Concluding remarks

To the best of our knowledge, the problem we solved here is the first example of an
operator exhibiting an exact
\varepsilon
-hypercyclicity threshold, thus providing a
complete answer to the question raised by Bayart
[
3
]
. Our method is
based on a careful programming of the weights, alternating amplification and attenuation,
so as to control the orbit growth inside each block.

It is natural to ask whether analogous threshold phenomena may occur for other
quantitative notions of hypercyclicity. For instance, one could consider the following
definition: given
\delta\in(0,1)
, a vector
x\in X
is said to be

\delta
-frequently hypercyclic
for
T\in\mathcal{L}(X)
if, for every
nonzero
y\in X
, the set

N_{\delta}(x,y):=\{\,n\geq 0:\|T^{n}x-y\|\leq\delta\|y\|\,\}

has positive lower density. The operator
T
is then called

\delta
-frequently hypercyclic
if it admits such a vector.
We do not know whether there exists an operator which is

\delta
-frequently hypercyclic if and only if
\delta\in[\varepsilon,1)
,
and we leave this as a natural open direction.

Another possible line of investigation is to examine whether sharp thresholds can
appear in other Banach spaces, such as
\ell^{p}
with
p\neq 2
or
c_{0}
, or within the framework
of other direct sums, as studied in
[
10
]
.

Determination of the potential by a fixed angle scattering data
Suliang Si

Abstract
In this paper, we show that a compactly supported potential is uniquely determined by the far field pattern at a fixed angle. Our method is based on a new Carleman estimate and the ideas introduced by Bukhgeim and Klibanov on the use of Carleman estimates for inverse problems..



1
Introduction

We are mainly concerned with the inverse scattering problem of determining a compactly supported
unknown potential in the Schrödinger equation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
.
Assume
the incident field is given by the plane wave

\hat{u}^{i}(x,k,d)=e^{ikx\cdot d},

where
k>0
is a frequency and
d\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
the direction of propagation. Then the scattering problem is to the full field
\hat{u}
such that

-\Delta\hat{u}-k^{2}\hat{u}+V(x)\hat{u}=0,\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

(1.1)

\hat{u}(x,k,d)=e^{ikx\cdot d}+\hat{u}^{s}(x,k,d),

(1.2)

r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(\partial_{r}\hat{u}^{s}-ik\hat{u}^{s})=0,\ \,r:=|x|\rightarrow+\infty,

(1.3)

where
V\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and (
1.3
) is
the Sommerfeld radiation condition
which
guarantees that the scattered wave
\hat{u}^{s}
is outgoing.
The well-posedness of the forward scattering problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) can be conveniently found in
[
7
]
.
Writing
x=r\theta
where
r\geq 0
and
\theta\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
, the scattered wave
\hat{u}^{s}
has the asymptotics

\hat{u}^{s}(r\theta,k,d)=e^{ikr}r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\hat{u}_{\infty}(\theta,k,d;V)+o(r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}})\quad\text{ as }\,r\to\infty.

The function
\hat{u}_{\infty}(\theta,k,d;V)
is called the
far field pattern
, corresponding to the potential
V
. One could interpret
\hat{u}_{\infty}(\theta,k,d;V)
as a scattering measurement for
V
that corresponds to sending a plane wave at frequency
k>0
propagating in the direction
d\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
and measuring the scattered wave in the direction
\theta\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
.
We show that a compactly supported potential
V
is uniquely determined by the far field pattern at a fixed angle
d
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
d\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
,
n\geq 2
, and let
V_{1},V_{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be real valued. If

\hat{u}_{\infty}(\theta,k,d;V_{1})=\hat{u}_{\infty}(\theta,k,d;V_{2})

for all
k>0
and
\theta\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
, then
V_{1}=V_{2}
.

In inverse scattering problems the objective is to determine certain properties of a scatterer from
measurements that are made far away.
We formulate four fundamental inverse scattering problems, related to recovering a potential from (partial) knowledge of its quantum mechanical scattering amplitude:

1.

Full data.
Recover
V
from
\hat{u}_{\infty}(\theta,k,d;V)
for all
\theta\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
,
k>0
,
d\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
.

2.

Fixed frequency.
Recover
V
from
\hat{u}_{\infty}(\theta,k,d;V)
with
k>0
fixed.

3.

Backscattering.
Recover
V
from
\hat{u}_{\infty}(-d,k,d;V)
for all
k>0
,
d\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
.

4.

Fixed angle.
Recover
V
from
\hat{u}_{\infty}(\theta,k,d;V)
where
d\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
is fixed.

The full data problem is formally overdetermined when
n\geq 2
, since one seeks to recover a function of
n
variables from a function of
2n-1
variables. Similarly, the fixed frequency problem is formally overdetermined when
n\geq 3
(it is formally determined when
n=2
). Both of these problems have been solved; we only mention that one can determine
V
from the high frequency asymptotics of
\hat{u}_{\infty}

[
21
]
and that the fixed frequency problem is equivalent to a variant of the inverse conductivity problem of Calderón addressed in
[
1
,
23
]
. There have been many related works and we refer to
[
26
,
15
,
25
]
for references.

The backscattering and the fixed angle inverse scattering problems are formally determined in any dimension (both the unknown and the data depend on
n
variables). The one-dimensional case is well understood
[
12
,
8
]
. Known results for
n\geq 2
include uniqueness for potentials that are small or belong to a generic set
[
9
,
22
,
13
,
2
]
, recovery of main singularities
[
10
,
16
,
20
]
, identification of the zero potential in fixed angle scattering
[
3
]
, and recovery of angularly controlled potentials from backscattering data
[
17
]
. See the references in
[
17
,
14
]
for further results. However, these problems remain open in general.

For inverse coefficient problems of the hyperbolic equation, concerning the uniqueness and
the stability, Bukhgeim and Klibanov
[
6
]
proposed a fundamental method based on
what is called a Carleman estimate. Thus Carleman estimates became a fundamental
tool for establishing uniqueness and stability for inverse problems
[
4
,
11
]
.
Rakesh and M. Salo showed that a compactly supported potential is
uniquely determined by the far field pattern generated by plane waves coming
from exactly two opposite directions
[
19
]
. These results are proved using Carleman estimates
and adapting the ideas introduced by Bukhgeim and Klibanov on the use of
Carleman estimates for inverse problems. Later, they extended the methods of
[
19
]
to establish an equivalence between the
frequency domain and the time domain formulations of the problem. So they obtained that that a compactly
supported potential is uniquely determined by its scattering amplitude for two opposite fixed angles. In this work, we establish a new Carleman estimate. Based on this inequality, we prove that a compactly
supported potential is uniquely determined by its scattering amplitude for only one fixed angle.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we state the time
domain setting for the fixed angle scattering problem. Section
3
is an intoduction to a new Carleman estimate. In Section, we prove Theorem
2.2
. Finally, Appendix contains the derivation of a new Carleman estimate (
3.14
) without inter information.

The time domain setting

Proposition 2.1
.

The initial value problem (

U(x,t,d)=\delta(t-x\cdot d)+u(x,t,d)H(t-x\cdot d),

(2.6)

where

, a smooth function on the region

, is the unique solution of the characteristic initial value problem:

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+Vu=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},\,t>x\cdot d,

(2.7)

u(x,x\cdot d,d)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{0}q(x+\sigma d)d\sigma,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(2.8)

u(x,t,d)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,x\cdot d<t\ll 0.

(2.9)

Also, for any real
T

\{(x,t):x\cdot d\leq t\leq T\}

is bounded above by a continuous function of
\|V\|_{C^{n+4}}
.

From the above proposition, we know there exists a constant

|u(x,t,d)|\leq C\|V\|_{C^{n+4}}\leq M\quad\text{for all }x\cdot d\leq t\leq T.

(2.10)

Let
Q=\{(x,t)|\,x\in B,\,\,x\cdot d\leq t\leq T\}
and

\Sigma=\{(x,t)|\,x\in\partial B,\,\,x\cdot d\leq t\leq T\}
.
Define
e:=(0,0,\cdot\cdot\cdot,1)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Without loss of generality, we take
d=e
.

Theorem 2.2

(One-plane wave data)
.

Let
u_{i}(x,t,e)
be the solutions of (
2.7
)-(
2.9
) with
V=V_{i}
,
i=1,2
. If
T>6
and
\|V\|_{C^{n+4}}\leq M
then
there exists a constant

independent of

and

such that

\|V_{1}-V_{2}\|_{L^{2}(B)}\leq C\|(u_{1}-u_{2})(x,t,e)\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma)}.

(2.11)

This is our main result, the proof is placed in section
4
.
The following theorem shows that the scattering amplitude for a fixed direction
d\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
and
the boundary measurements in the wave equation problem are equivalent information, which can be found in
[
19
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and fix
d\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
,
k_{0}>0
. For any real valued
V_{1},V_{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with support in

, one has

\hat{u}_{\infty}(\theta,k,d;V_{1})=\hat{u}_{\infty}(\theta,k,d;V_{2})\quad\text{ for }\,k\geq k_{0}\text{ and }\,\theta\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}

u_{1}(x,t,d)=u_{2}(x,t,d)\quad\text{ for }\quad(x,t)\in(\partial B\times\mathbb{R})\cap\{t\geq x\cdot d\}.

From the above two theorems, we deduce Theorem

From (

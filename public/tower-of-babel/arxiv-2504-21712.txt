Presentations, embeddings and automorphisms of homogeneous spaces for S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℂ ) SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
G. Freudenburg

Abstract
. For an algebraically closed field k k of characteristic zero and a linear algebraic k k -group G G , it is well known that every affine G G -variety admits a G G -equivariant closed embedding into a finite-dimensional G G -module. Such an embedding is a presentation of the G G -variety, and a minimal presentation is one for which the dimension the G G -module is minimal. The problem of finding a minimal presentation generalizes the problem of determining whether a group action on affine space is linearizable. We give a minimal presentation for each homogeneous space for S ​ L 2 ​ ( k ) SL_{2}(k) . This constitutes the paper’s main work. Of particular interest are the surfaces Y = S ​ L 2 ​ ( k ) / T Y=SL_{2}(k)/T and X = S ​ L 2 ​ ( k ) / N X=SL_{2}(k)/N where T T is the one-dimensional torus and N N is its normalizer. We show that the minimal presentation of X X has dimension 5, the embedding dimension of X X is 4, and there does not exist a closed S ​ L 2 SL_{2} -equivariant embedding of X X in 𝔸 k 4 \mathbb{A}_{k}^{4} . Thus, the S ​ L 2 SL_{2} -action on X X is absolutely nonextendable to 𝔸 k 4 \mathbb{A}_{k}^{4} . We give two other examples of surfaces with absolutely nonextendable group actions. In addition, X X is noncancelative, that is, there exists a surface X ~ \tilde{X} such that X × 𝔸 k 1 ≅ k X ~ × 𝔸 k ! X\times\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}\cong_{k}\tilde{X}\times\mathbb{A}_{k}^{!} and X ≇ k X ~ X\not\cong_{k}\tilde{X} . Finally, we settle the long-standing open question of whether there exist inequivalent closed embeddings of Y Y in 𝔸 k 3 \mathbb{A}_{k}^{3} by constructing inequivalent embeddings.

Let

the triple
(V,G,\rho)
consist of an irreducible affine
k
-variety
V
equipped with an algebraic action
\rho:G\to{\rm Aut}_{k}(V)
of a linear algebraic
k
-group

.
Then

admits
a
G
-equivariant closed embedding into a finite-dimensional
G
-module; see
[
4
]
, Proposition 1.12 and
[
13
]
, II.2.5.
Blanc, Furter and Poloni give a more precise result for smooth rational affine curves over
k=\mathbb{C}
, showing that every such curve
\Gamma

admits a
G
-equivariant closed embedding into a
G
-module of dimension 3, where
G={\rm Aut}_{k}(\Gamma)

[
3
]
.
Each curve
\Gamma
also admits a closed planar embedding

then

The finite subgroups of

are well-known; see
[
19
]
.
The authors identify curves
\Gamma
where
G
is finite, but

is not a subgroup of

, meaning that there is no
G
-equivariant closed embedding of
\Gamma
in
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
.
For example, there exist
\Gamma
whose automorphism group is the alternating group

.
Such considerations motivate the following definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

A
presentation
of
(V,G,\rho)
is an equivariant surjection
\varphi:k[W]\to\mathcal{O}(V)
for some
G
-module
W
.

\varphi
is a
minimal
presentation if and only if

is of minimal dimension.

Here,

is the symmetric algebra of

, and
\mathcal{O}(V)
is the coordinate ring of
V
.

Definition 1.2
.

(V,G,\rho)
is
absolutely nonextendable
if there is no
G
-equivariant closed algebraic embedding of
V
into
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}
,
where

is the embedding dimension of

.

For presentations, the condition that
G
is an algebraic group is necessary. Derksen, Kutzschebauch and Winkelmann showed the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

(
[
8
]
, Theorem 2)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there does not exist any effective differentiable,
or holomorphic, or algebraic action of the group

{\rm Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*})\cong\left(O_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times O_{2}(\mathbb{C})\right)\rtimes SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.

The authors construct several examples of an algebraic group acting on a hypersurface in such a way that the action does not extend to the ambient space.
Their methods only apply to hypersurfaces, and each example is based on a particular embedding of the variety.

We begin with two examples of surfaces with absolutely nonextendable actions, each based on the following result. These surfaces have embedding dimension three. We construct a third example in
Section
4
, using a homogeneous surface for
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
whose embedding dimension is four (i.e., not a hypersurface).

Theorem 1.4
.

(
[
14
]
, Theorem A)

Every faithful action of a non-finite complex reductive group on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
is linearizable.

Example 1.5
.

Consider
(V,G,\rho)
where:

V=\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad G={\rm Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{*})\times{\rm Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{*})=O_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times O_{2}(\mathbb{C})

Let

be the irreducible
O_{2}
-module of dimension two, where
\mathbb{C}^{*}
acts with weights
\pm 1
. Let

W\oplus W=(\mathbb{C}x\oplus\mathbb{C}y)\oplus(\mathbb{C}z\oplus\mathbb{C}w)

and define the

\varphi:\mathbb{C}[W\oplus W]\to\mathbb{C}[W\oplus W]/(xy-1,zw-1)\cong_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}(V)

This is a minimal presentation of

, since there is no faithful representation of

on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. On the other hand,

admits closed embeddings in
\mathbb{C}^{3}

. Let

, and suppose that the

-action on

\mathbb{C}^{3}

By

-action on

is absolutely nonextendable.

Example 1.6
.

For each positive odd integer

,
let

be the

-module of dimension four defined by:

{}^{\zeta}(a,b,x,y)=(\zeta^{-2}a,\zeta^{2}b,\zeta^{-n}x,\zeta^{n}y)\,\,\ ,\,\,\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau(a,b,x,y)=(b,a,y,x)

The ideal

We thus obtain a family

of faithful

-actions on

defined by

{}^{\lambda}(t,s)=(\lambda^{-2}t,\lambda^{n}s)\,\,,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad^{\mu}(t,s)=(t^{-1},t^{n}s)

with presentation
\varphi_{n}:\mathbb{C}[W_{n}]\to\mathbb{C}[W_{n}]/I_{n}
. The function

is a variable of

, but not an

Let

be a hypersurface isomorphic to

, and suppose that
\rho_{n}
extends to a faithful

-action on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
By

, this action is linear in some system of coordinates. There are two types of faithful representations of

on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
, given by

(\lambda,\alpha)
and
(\lambda,\beta)

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and

\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda^{m}&0&0\cr 0&\lambda^{-m}&0\cr 0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\,\,(m\geq 1)\,\,,\,\,\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\cr 1&0&0\cr 0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\,\,,\,\,\beta=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\cr 1&0&0\cr 0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}

The set of fixed points of

, and the set of fixed points of

. The set of fixed points of

in

Recall that a
homogeneous space
for the group
G
is an algebraic variety equipped with a transitive algebraic
G
-action. One of our main goals is to find minimal presentations of homogeneous spaces for
SL_{2}(k)
.
In
Section
2

we give an algorithmic method to find a presentation for any affine
SL_{2}
-variety
V
using the theory of fundamental pairs.
By this method, we find a minimal presentation of each homogeneous space for
SL_{2}(k)
; see
Theorem
3.9
.
Each is a quotient
SL_{2}(k)/H
for some closed reductive subgroup
H
,
and if
B=\mathcal{O}(SL_{2}(k))
, then
\mathcal{O}(SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})/H)=B^{H}
.
In order to find presentations, we do not use standard tools of invariant theory such as the Reynolds operator to find
B^{H}
.
Instead, we use a fundamental pair
(D,U)
of locally nilpotent derivations of
B

to extend each ring of binary invariants
{\rm ker}\,D=k[x,y]^{H}
to
B^{H}
via integration by

. We use Felix Klein’s description of these invariant rings.
For example, integrating elements of
k[x,y]^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}}=k[x^{3},xy,y^{3}]
yields the subring
B^{\mathbb{Z}_{3}}
generated by 4+3+4=11 elements (corresponding to degrees 3,2,3), but we reduce this number to four.
We thus realize
SL_{2}(k)/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
as a fiber of the quotient morphism
\pi:\mathbb{A}_{k}^{4}\to\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}
for the irreducible
SL_{2}
-module of degree three.
In particular,
SL_{2}(k)/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
is equivariantly isomorphic to a hypersurface in
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{4}
.
Likewise, the binary icosahedral group
{\rm BI}_{120}
is a subgroup of order 120.
Integrating elements of
k[x,y]^{{\rm BI}_{120}}=k[f,g,h]
yields the subring
B^{{\rm BI}_{120}}
generated by 13+21+31=65 elements (corresponding to degrees 12, 20, 30 of
f,g,h
), but we reduce this number to 13.

2.1.
Locally nilpotent derivations

Let

be an affine
k
-domain. The set of

.
Given

is denoted

.

if, given

.
The set of locally nilpotent derivations of

is
{\rm LND}(B)
.
This set is in bijective correspondence with the set of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on
{\rm Spec}(B)
via the exponential map, where
\mathbb{G}_{a}
is the additive group of
k
.
We say that
D\in{\rm LND}(B)
is
irreducible
if the image
DB
is contained in no proper principal ideal of

.
The Makar-Limanov invariant of

, and if
X={\rm Spec}(B)
, then
ML(X)=ML(B)
.

Given
D\in{\rm LND}(B)
, set
A={\rm ker}\,D
. An element

is a
local slice
for
D
if
Ds\neq 0
and
D^{2}s=0
.
A local slice
s
for
D
is a
slice
if
Ds=1
. The
Slice Theorem
says that, for any local slice
s
,
B_{Ds}=A_{Ds}[s]\cong_{k}A_{Ds}^{[1]}
.
The degree function
\deg_{D}
on
B
is the restriction of
\deg_{s}
on
B_{Ds}
. It does not depend on the choice of local slice
s
.

Given a positive integer
n
and

, the
n^{\text{th}}

transvectant
of

and

is:

[f,g]_{n}^{D}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}D^{i}fD^{n-i}g

If
\deg_{D}(f)\leq n
and
\deg_{D}(g)\leq n
then

. This is an important way to construct elements of the kernel of

. See
[
9
]
, 2.11.1.

2.2.
Fundamental pairs of LNDs

Suppose that
B
is an affine
k
-domain. We can view

The pair
(D,U)\in{\rm LND}(B)^{2}

and

[D,[D,U]]=-2D\quad\text{and}\quad[U,[D,U]]=2U

i.e.,
D
and
U
generate a Lie subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(k)
.
Fundamental pairs for
B
are in bijective correspondence with algebraic actions of
SL_{2}(k)
on

; see
[
10
]
.
In particular, an
SL_{2}(k)
-action on

induces a fundamental pair
(D,U)
for

SL_{2}(k)
,
each being isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a}

which satisfy these relations.

Suppose that
(D,U)
is a fundamental pair for
B
. Set
E=[D,U]
.
By
[
1
]
, Theorem 2.6,

is a semi-simple derivation which induces a

-grading of

B=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{d}
where

. The kernel

is a graded affine subalgebra and

-graded.
In addition:

A_{0}={\rm ker}\,D\cap{\rm ker}\,U=B^{SL_{2}(k)}

Given

,

denotes the vector space in

. Note that

. In addition,

[

]

Therefore,

2.3.1.

The finite subgroups of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
were given by Felix Klein in

]
.
The list is comprised of two countably infinite families and three sporadic groups: the cyclic groups
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and binary dihedral groups
{\rm BD}_{4n}

(n\geq 2)
, the binary tetrahedral group
{\rm BT}_{24}
, the binary octahedral group
{\rm BO}_{48}
and the binary icosahedral group
{\rm BI}_{120}
.
The special unitary group
SU(2)\subset SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
gives a representation of the group of unit quaternions:

\begin{pmatrix}z&-w\cr\bar{w}&\bar{z}\end{pmatrix}\,\,,\,\,|z|^{2}+|w|^{2}=1

SU(2)
is generated by rotations in 3-space (up to sign), meaning that
SU(2)
contains the symmetry group of each of the platonic solids studied by Klein.
Moreover, any finite subgroup of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
can be conjugated to
SU(2)
, so classifying finite subgroups of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
reduces to classifying those in
SU(2)
.
The representations of finite groups and corresponding invariants given below are those derived by Klein.
Isomorphic finite subgroups of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
are known to be conjugate, so a single representation of each is sufficient.
Moreover, in these results, the field
\mathbb{C}
can be replaced by any algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero.

3.
Minimal presentations of
SL_{2}
-homogeneous threefolds

In this section, assume that
k
is algebraically closed.

3.1.
Left and right actions

The actions of
SL_{2}(k)
on itself by left and right multiplication, respectively, commute. If we restrict the right action to a closed reductive subgroup
H\subset SL_{2}(k)
, then we get an
SL_{2}
-action on the quotient
SL_{2}(k)/H
induced by left multiplication. In other words, the right action determines the variety, and the left action determines the
SL_{2}
-action on that variety.

Let
B=k[x,y,z,w]
where
xw-yz=1
, the coordinate ring of
SL_{2}(k)
.
The fundamental pair
(\delta,\upsilon)
which defines the left action is given by:

\delta\begin{pmatrix}x&y\cr z&w\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\cr 1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x&y\cr z&w\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\cr x&y\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\upsilon\begin{pmatrix}x&y\cr z&w\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\cr 0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x&y\cr z&w\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}z&w\cr 0&0\end{pmatrix}

Set
A={\rm ker}\,\delta=k[x,y]
.

Let

In addition to the quadratic polynomials

Lemma 3.1
.

For each integer

\varphi_{n}(T_{2i})=\displaystyle 2\frac{(n!)^{2}}{(2i)!}\,(xy)^{n-2i}

Observe that, for

\varphi_{n}(T_{0})=\varphi_{n}(X_{0}^{2})=(n!)(x^{n}+y^{n})

Proof.

Recall that, for any positive integer

(1)

\sum_{a=0}^{b}(-1)^{a}{b\choose a}=0

By equivariance, given
0\leq j\leq n
we have:

\varphi_{n}(X_{j})=\varphi_{n}D^{n-j}(X_{n})=\delta^{n-j}\varphi_{n}(X_{n})=\delta^{n-j}(z^{n}+w^{n})=(n!/j!)(x^{n-j}z^{j}+y^{n-j}w^{j})

Given

we have:

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(T_{2i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2i}(-1)^{j}\,\frac{n!}{j!}\frac{n!}{(2i-j)!}(x^{n-j}z^{j}+y^{n-j}w^{j})(x^{n-2i+j}z^{2i-j}+y^{n-2i+j}w^{2i-j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n!)^{2}}{(2i)!}\sum_{j=0}^{2i}(-1)^{j}{2i\choose j}\left(x^{2n-2i}z^{2i}+y^{2n-2i}w^{2i}+x^{n-j}y^{n-2i+j}z^{j}w^{2i-j}+x^{n-2i+j}y^{n-j}z^{2i-j}w^{j}\right)

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(T_{2i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n!)^{2}}{(2i)!}\sum_{j=0}^{2i}(-1)^{j}{2i\choose j}\left(x^{n-j}y^{n-2i+j}z^{j}w^{2i-j}+x^{n-2i+j}y^{n-j}z^{2i-j}w^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n!)^{2}}{(2i)!}\sum_{j=0}^{2i}(-1)^{j}{2i\choose j}\left(x^{(n-2i)+(2i-j)}y^{(n-2i)+j}z^{j}w^{2i-j}+x^{(n-2i)+j}y^{(n-2i)+(2i-j)}z^{2i-j}w^{j}\right)

Lemma 3.2
.

Given odd
m\geq 3
, the quadratic polynomial
T_{m+1}\in k[\mathbb{V}_{2m}]

has degree
2(m-1)
and

T_{m+1},UT_{m+1},\ldots,U^{2(m-1)}T_{m+1}

are algebraically independent over
k
.

Proof.

For
0\leq j\leq m-1
,
U^{j}T_{m+1}
supports the monomial
X_{0}X_{m+1+j}
but supports no monomial divisible by
X_{2m}
. By degree considerations,
U^{j}T_{m+1}
supports no other monomial divisible by
X_{0}
.

For
j=m-1
,
U^{m-1}T_{m+1}
supports the monomial
X_{0}X_{2m}
but supports no other monomial divisible by
X_{0}
or
X_{2m}
.

For
m\leq j\leq 2(m-1)
,
U^{j}T_{m+1}
supports the monomial
X_{j-m+1}X_{2m}
but supports no monomial divisible by
X_{0}
. By degree considerations,
U^{j}T_{m+1}
supports no other monomial divisible by
X_{2m}
.

Therefore, the
(2m-1)\times(2m+1)
jacobian matrix

\mathcal{J}=\frac{\partial(T_{m+1},UT_{m+1},\ldots,U^{2(m-1)}T_{m+1})}{\partial(X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{2m})}

evaluated at
X_{1}=\cdots=X_{2m-1}=0
has exactly one nonzero entry in each column except for the middle (
m^{\text{th}}
) column, which is a column of zeros;
and has exactly one nonzero entry in each row except for the middle (
m^{\text{th}}
) row, whose first and last entries are nonzero.
This implies that the rank of
\mathcal{J}(X_{0},0,\ldots,0,X_{2m})
equals
2m-1
, so the rank of
\mathcal{J}
equals
2m-1
.
It follows that
T_{m+1},UT_{m+1},\ldots,U^{2(m-1)}T_{m+1}
are algebraically independent.
See
[
9
]
, 3.2.3.

x=x_{0}^{2}\,\,,\,\,y=x_{0}x_{1}\,\,,\,\,z=x_{1}x_{2}\,\,,\,\,w=x_{2}^{2}

Define
P=x_{0}x_{2}=2xw-yz
. From equation (
4
) above we find that the ideal
I
of relations in
R
is

I=\left(y^{2}-x(2P-1)\,,\,yP-xz\,,\,yz-P(2P-1)\,,\,yw-zP\,,\,z^{2}-w(2P-1)\right)

The fundamental pair on
R
is
(\delta,\upsilon)
and
\delta
operates on
R
by:

\delta w=2z\,\,,\,\,\delta z=3P-1\,\,,\,\,\delta P=y\,\,,\,\,\delta y=x\,\,,\,\,\delta x=0

Note that
\delta(zP)=5P^{2}-2P
.

Extend
\delta
to
\Delta
on
R[v]=R^{[1]}
by
\Delta v=w
.
Define
Q=5xv-yw
. Then
\Delta Q=2P
,
so
\Delta s=1
for
s=\frac{3}{2}Q-z
. Set
\tilde{R}={\rm ker}\,\Delta
. By the Slice Theorem,

R^{[1]}=R[v]=k[x,y,z,w,v]=k[x,y,s,w,v]=\tilde{R}[s]=\tilde{R}^{[1]}

and

\tilde{R}\cong_{k}R[v]/(s)=k[\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{w},\bar{v}]

where
2\bar{z}=3\bar{Q}
and
2\bar{P}=4\bar{x}\bar{w}-3\bar{y}\bar{Q}
.
This proves part (a).

Let
L=k[\bar{x},\bar{x}^{-1}]
and observe that
\tilde{R}[\bar{x}^{-1}]=L[\bar{y},\bar{w},\bar{v}]=L[\bar{y},\bar{P},\bar{Q}]
.
Since
3\bar{x}\bar{Q}=2\bar{y}\bar{P}
and
\bar{x}(2\bar{P}-1)=\bar{y}^{2}
we see that
\tilde{R}[\bar{x}^{-1}]=L[\bar{y}]=L^{[1]}
. Define
\Theta\in{\rm LND}(L[\bar{y}])

by
\Theta\bar{y}=\bar{x}
. From the observed relations we find:

\textstyle\Theta\bar{P}=\bar{y}\,\,,\,\,\Theta\bar{Q}=2\bar{P}-\frac{2}{3}\,\,,\,\,\Theta\bar{w}=3\bar{Q}\,\,,\,\,\Theta\bar{v}=\bar{w}-\frac{2}{15}\bar{x}^{-1}

Define
\theta=\bar{x}\,\Theta\in{\rm LND}(\tilde{R})
. Let
J=(\theta\tilde{R})
, the ideal generated by the image of
\theta
. Then

J=(\theta\bar{x},\theta\bar{y},\theta\bar{w},\theta\bar{v})=(\bar{x}^{2},\bar{x}\bar{Q},\bar{x}\bar{w}-\textstyle\frac{2}{15})

Modulo
J
, we have:

0\equiv\bar{x}\bar{Q}\equiv 5\bar{x}^{2}\bar{v}-\bar{x}\bar{y}\bar{w}\equiv\textstyle\frac{2}{15}\bar{y}\implies 0\equiv 2\bar{P}-\textstyle\frac{2}{3}\equiv 4\bar{x}\bar{w}-2\bar{y}\bar{z}-\frac{2}{3}\equiv-\frac{2}{15}\implies J=(1)

Therefore,
\theta
is irreducible and the
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action on
{\rm Spec}(\tilde{R})
induced by
\theta
is fixed-point free.
We thus obtain:

(1)

\theta\in{\rm LND}(\tilde{R})
is irreducible.

(2)

{\rm ker}\,\theta=k[\bar{x}]

(3)

\theta\bar{y}=\bar{x}^{2}
and
{\rm pl}(\theta)=\bar{x}^{2}k[\bar{x}]

By
Corollary
5.2
, there is no irreducible
D\in{\rm LND}(R)
such that
{\rm ker}\,D/{\rm pl}(D)\cong k[x]/(x^{2})
. Therefore,

\tilde{R}\not\cong_{k}R
.
∎

6.
Closed embeddings of
Y
in
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{3}

The purpose of this section is to prove
Theorem
1.8
.
Let
B=k[x,y,z]=k^{[3]}
. Given
f,g\in B

define the
k
-derivation
\Delta_{(f,g)}
of
B
by

\Delta_{(f,g)}(h)=\frac{\partial(f,g,h)}{\partial(x,y,z)}\,\,,\,\,h\in B

that is, the determinant of the Jacobian matrix of
f,g
and
h
. We have:

F=xz-y^{2}\,\,,\,\,r=yF-x\,\,,\,\,G=x^{-1}(F^{4}+r^{3})

It is easy to check that
G+r^{2}\in FB
. Set
H=F^{-1}(G+r^{2})
.
It is also easy to check that
G
and
H
are irreducible.
Define
k
-derivations
\Delta_{i}
of
B
,
1\leq i\leq 3
by:

\Delta_{1}=\Delta_{(x,F)}\,\,,\,\,\Delta_{2}=\Delta_{(F,G)}\,\,,\,\,\Delta_{3}=\Delta_{(G,H)}

The following properties hold.

(1)

\Delta_{1}
is locally nilpotent,
{\rm ker}\,\Delta_{1}=k[x,F]
and
\Delta_{1}r=xF
.

(2)

\Delta_{2}
is locally nilpotent,
{\rm ker}\,\Delta_{2}=k[F,G]
and
\Delta_{2}r=FG
.

(3)

\Delta_{3}
is locally nilpotent,
{\rm ker}\,\Delta_{3}=k[G,H]
and
\Delta_{3}r=GH
.

Statement (1) is well known. Since
xG=F^{4}+r^{3}
,

[
9
]
, Theorem 5.28, implies that
\Delta_{2}
is locally nilpotent,
{\rm ker}\,\Delta_{2}=k[F,G]
and
\Delta_{2}r=FG
. So statement (2) holds.
Likewise, since
FH=G+r^{2}
,
the just cited theorem implies that
\Delta_{3}
is locally nilpotent,
{\rm ker}\,\Delta_{3}=k[G,H]
and
\Delta_{3}r=GH
. So statement (3) holds.

Given
a\in k^{*}
and
b\in B
, let
\bar{B}=B/(G-a)
and let
\bar{b}
denote the image of
b
in
\bar{B}
.
It is easy to check that
{\rm Spec}(\bar{B})
is smooth, so
\bar{B}
is a normal ring.
In addition,
\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3}
induce
\delta_{2},\delta_{3}\in{\rm LND}(\bar{B})
, where
\delta_{2}\bar{r}=a\bar{F}
and
\delta_{3}\bar{r}=a\bar{H}
, and

\bar{F}\bar{H}=\bar{r}^{2}+a
. Suppose that
\dim ML(\bar{B})=1
.
Then
{\rm ker}\,\delta_{2}={\rm ker}\,\delta_{3}
implies
\delta_{2}\bar{H}=0
. But then

0=\delta_{2}(a)=\delta_{2}(\bar{F}\bar{H}-\bar{r}^{2})=-2\bar{r}\delta_{2}(\bar{r})=-2a\bar{r}\bar{F}

gives a contradiction. Therefore,
ML(\bar{B})=k
.
By
[
9
]
, Lemma 2.34,
{\rm ker}\,\theta\cong k^{[1]}
for every nonzero
\theta\in{\rm LND}(\bar{B})
.
By Daigle’s characterization of Danielewski surfaces (
[
6
]
, Theorem 2.5),
\bar{B}
is a Danielewski surface and
\bar{B}=k[\bar{F},\bar{H},\bar{r}]
. Since

\bar{F}\bar{H}=\bar{r}^{2}+a
is a prime relation,
it follows that

\bar{B}
is isomorphic as a
k
-algebra to
B/(xz-y^{2}-a)
.

Regarding zero fibers, the surface defined by
G=0
in
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{3}
contains a line of singular points, and is not a normal surface.
In addition, it is well-known that every fiber of the polynomial
xz-y^{2}
is a normal surface.

This completes the proof of
Theorem
1.8
.

Emergent-gravity Hall effect from quantum geometry
Hiroki Yoshida, Takehito Yokoyama

Abstract
We theoretically propose a Hall effect driven by effective gravitational fields arising from quantum geometry. We develop four mechanisms for this ”emergent-gravity Hall effect” : real-space gravity, momentum-space gravity, gravitional anomalous velocity, and gravitational Lorentz force which are described by the Christoffel symbols in real, momentum, and time spaces. We construct a unified theoretical framework to systematically investigate the effects of emergent gravity in these spaces on transport phenomena based on the semiclassical theory. We demonstrate these effects by model calculations and clarify the conditions under which a finite Hall response can arise. Our findings open a new avenue for exploring gravitational effects in quantum systems.



Introduction
. Transport phenomena in solids are profoundly influenced by the geometry and topology of electronic wavefunctions. A prime example is the anomalous Hall effect
Karplus and Luttinger 1954
;
Smit 1955
;
Smit 1958
;
Berger 1970
;
Berry 1984
;
Ye et al. 1999
;
Fang et al. 2003
, where it was found that the Berry curvature in momentum space –a geometrical quantity associated with the underlying Bloch states– plays a crucial role in the transverse motion of electrons under an electric field. In addition to the Berry curvature, the quantum metric –another fundamental geometrical quantity of Bloch states– has been also of central importance
Provost and Vallee 1980
;
Tan et al. 2019
;
Gianfrate et al. 2020
;
Klees et al. 2020
;
Yu et al. 2020
;
Gao et al. 2014
;
Gao et al. 2023
. In particular, its momentum derivative constitutes the Christoffel symbol, representing momentum-space gravity
Gao et al. 2014
;
Smith et al. 2022
;
Mehraeen
;
Fontana et al.
.

Then, attention has shifted toward real-space geometry, particularly in systems with nontrivial spin textures. One notable development is the discovery of the topological Hall effect
Tatara and Kawamura 2002
;
Bruno et al. 2004
induced by scalar spin chirality, which leads to an emergent electromagnetic field acting on the electrons. These advances underscore the growing interest in understanding how spatial geometry and spin structure can give rise to novel transport responses. Then, a natural question is: are there emergent gravitational fields acting on electrons?

In this work, we uncover a new type of Hall effect due to effective gravitational fields arising from quantum geometry, which can be described by the Christoffel symbols. In analogy to the term, emergent electromagnetic field, we refer to this as an emergent gravitational field, and the associated Hall effect as the “emergent-gravity Hall effect”.

We theoretically predict this phenomenon by extending the semiclassical wavepacket dynamics to include interband transitions beyond the adiabatic approximation. This approach allows the Christoffel symbols to emerge naturally in the equation of motion of the wavepacket and enables a unified analysis of transport phenomena governed by quantum geometry in real, momentum, time, and parameter spaces.
With this approach, we develop four mechanisms for this emergent-gravity Hall effect: real-space gravity, momentum-space gravity, gravitional anomalous velocity, and gravitational Lorentz force.
We demonstrate these mechanisms by model calculations and clarify the conditions on a finite Hall response.

General theory
. We begin by deriving the equation of motion, incorporating nonadiabatic time evolution. In Ref.
Sundaram and Niu 1999
, a general Lagrangian for an electron with spatial, momentum, and time dependencies is provided. In addition to these, we introduce an arbitrary parameter
\lambda
, which depends solely on time. This parameter can, for example, represent a strain in the system. Following the same procedure of derivation, we obtain the Lagrangian of the system as
Sundaram and Niu 1999
,

\displaystyle L(\mathbf{r}_{c},\dot{\mathbf{r}}_{c},\mathbf{q}_{c},\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{c},t)

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon-\hbar\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{c}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{c}+\hbar\dot{\mathbf{r}}_{c}\cdot\bra{u}\ket{i\pdv{u}{\vb{r}_c}}+\hbar\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{c}\cdot\bra{u}\ket{i\pdv{u}{\vb{q}_c}}+\hbar\dot{\lambda}\bra{u}\ket{i\pdv{u}{\lambda}}+\hbar\bra{u}\ket{i\pdv{u}{t}},

(1)

where
\varepsilon
is an energy of a wave-packet,
\mathbf{r}_{c}
and
\mathbf{q}_{c}
are centers of the wave-packet in real and reciprocal spaces, respectively, and
\ket{u}\coloneqq\ket{u(\vb{r}_c,\vb{q}_c,t)}
is the periodic part of the Bloch state. In the original work
Sundaram and Niu 1999
, only a single band
is considered. Here, we consider the time evolution of the quantum state beyond the adiabatic approximation.

Assume that the system is initially in the
n
-th eigenstate of the Hamiltonian. We omit the index
c
for simplicity. Then, the state
\ket{u}
can be expanded as
Xiao et al. 2010

\displaystyle\ket{u}=

\displaystyle\ket{u_n(\vb{r},\vb{q},\lambda,t)}

\displaystyle+\hbar\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\dot{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{A}^{r}_{mn}+\dot{\mathbf{q}}\cdot\mathbf{A}^{q}_{mn}+\dot{\lambda}A^{\lambda}_{mn}+A^{t}_{mn}}{\varepsilon_{mn}}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times\ket{u_m(\vb{r},\vb{q},\lambda,t)},

(2)

where the Berry connections in each space are defined as
\mathbf{A}^{X}_{mn,i}\coloneqq\bra{u_m}\ket{i\pdv{u_n}{X_i}}
with
X=r,q
,
A^{Y}_{mn}\coloneqq\bra{u_m}\ket{i\pdv{u_n}{Y}}
with
Y=\lambda,t
, while
\varepsilon_{mn}=\varepsilon_{m}-\varepsilon_{n}
. This expression represents the first-order correction to the adiabatic approximation. By substituting this expansion into Eq. (
1
), we obtain a Lagrangian that incorporates nonadiabatic processes. We assume that the time variation is sufficiently slow and neglect all terms involving third- and higher-order time derivatives. The resulting Lagrangian can then be written as

\displaystyle L_{n}

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon-\hbar\dot{\mathbf{q}}\cdot\mathbf{r}+\hbar A^{q}_{nn,j}\dot{q}_{j}+\hbar A^{r}_{nn,j}\dot{r}_{j}+\hbar A^{\lambda}_{nn}\dot{\lambda}+\hbar A^{t}_{nn}

\displaystyle+\hbar\frac{\dot{q}_{j}}{2}\quantity(G_{ji}^{qr}\dot{r}_i+G^{qq}_{ji}\dot{q}_i+G^{q\lambda}_{j}\dot{\lambda}+G^{qt}_{j})

\displaystyle+\hbar\frac{\dot{r}_{j}}{2}\quantity(G_{ji}^{rr}\dot{r}_i+G^{rq}_{ji}\dot{q}_i+G^{r\lambda}_{j}\dot{\lambda}+G^{rt}_{j})

\displaystyle+\hbar\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{2}\quantity(G_{\phantom{j}i}^{\lambda r}\dot{r}_i+G^{\lambda q}_{\phantom{j}i}\dot{q}_i+G^{\lambda\lambda}\dot{\lambda}+G^{\lambda t})

\displaystyle+\hbar\frac{1}{2}\quantity(G^{tr}_{\phantom{j}i}\dot{r}_i+G^{tq}_{\phantom{j}i}\dot{q}_i+G^{t\lambda}\dot{\lambda}+G^{tt}),

(3)

where
G^{XY}_{ij}\coloneqq 4\hbar\sum_{m\neq n}\real\quantity[\frac{A^X_{nm,i}A^Y_{mn,j}}{\varepsilon_{mn}}],\ (X,Y=r,q)
and others are defined in a similar manner. These quantities are so called weighted quantum metric
Jain et al. 2025
. From this Lagrangian, the equations of motion for an electron can be derived using the Euler-Lagrange equation. A straightforward calculation leads to

\displaystyle\dot{q}_{a}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar}\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{r_a}+\Omega^{rr}_{ai}\dot{r}_{i}+\Omega^{rq}_{ai}\dot{q}_{i}+\Omega^{r\lambda}_{a}\dot{\lambda}+\Omega^{rt}_{a}

\displaystyle-\Gamma^{rrr}_{a,ij}\dot{r}_{i}\dot{r}_{j}-2\Gamma^{rrq}_{a,ij}\dot{r}_{i}\dot{q}_{j}-2\Gamma^{rr\lambda}_{a,i\phantom{j}}\dot{r}_{i}\dot{\lambda}-2\Gamma^{rrt}_{a,i\phantom{j}}\dot{r}_{i}

\displaystyle-\Gamma^{rqq}_{a,ij}\dot{q}_{i}\dot{q}_{j}-2\Gamma^{rq\lambda}_{a,ij}\dot{q}_{i}\dot{\lambda}-2\Gamma^{rqt}_{a,i\phantom{j}}\dot{q}_{i}

\displaystyle-\Gamma^{r\lambda\lambda}_{a,}\dot{\lambda}\dot{\lambda}-2\Gamma^{r\lambda t}_{a,}\dot{\lambda}

\displaystyle-\Gamma^{rtt}_{a,}-2\quantity(G^{rr}_{ai}\ddot{r}_i+G^{rq}_{ai}\ddot{q}_i+G^{r\lambda}_{a}\ddot{\lambda}),

(4)

\displaystyle\dot{r}_{a}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar}\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{q_a}-\Omega^{qr}_{ai}\dot{r}_{i}-\Omega^{qq}_{ai}\dot{q}_{i}-\Omega^{q\lambda}_{a}\dot{\lambda}-\Omega^{qt}_{a}

\displaystyle+\Gamma^{qrr}_{a,ij}\dot{r}_{i}\dot{r}_{j}+2\Gamma^{qrq}_{a,ij}\dot{r}_{i}\dot{q}_{j}+2\Gamma^{qr\lambda}_{a,i\phantom{j}}\dot{r}_{i}\dot{\lambda}+2\Gamma^{qrt}_{a,i\phantom{j}}\dot{r}_{i}

\displaystyle+\Gamma^{qqq}_{a,ij}\dot{q}_{i}\dot{q}_{j}+2\Gamma^{qq\lambda}_{a,i\phantom{j}}\dot{q}_{i}\dot{\lambda}+2\Gamma^{qqt}_{a,i\phantom{j}}\dot{q}_{i}

\displaystyle+\Gamma^{q\lambda\lambda}_{a,}\dot{\lambda}\dot{\lambda}+2\Gamma^{q\lambda t}_{a,}\dot{\lambda}

\displaystyle+\Gamma^{qtt}_{a,}+2\quantity(G^{qr}_{ai}\ddot{r}_i+G^{qq}_{ai}\ddot{q}_i+G^{q\lambda}_{a}\ddot{\lambda}).

(5)

An important point here is that the inclusion of nonadiabatic processes yields correction terms in the form of Christoffel symbols, defined from the weighted metric as

\displaystyle\Gamma^{abc}_{i,jk}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\quantity(\pdv{G^{ab}_{ij}}{c_k}+\pdv{G^{ac}_{ik}}{b_j}-\pdv{G^{bc}_{jk}}{a_i}).

(6)

By definition,
\Gamma^{abc}_{i,jk}=\Gamma^{acb}_{i,kj}
holds.
In analogy with the concept of the emergent electromagnetic field
Volovik 1987
;
Nagaosa and Tokura 2013
, we refer to these terms as emergent gravitational fields. Below, we show how this emergent gravity manifests in electronic transport properties.
Using the Boltzmann equation with the relaxation time approximation, we then derive the electron distribution function to arbitrary order in the relaxation time. Combining the equation of motion derived above with the distribution function allows us to compute the electric current in the system. Note that, as pointed out in Ref.
Xiao et al. 2005
, when the Berry curvature in mixed spaces, such as
\Omega^{rq}
, is nonzero, the density of states is modified. In the following, we consider only the case where
\Omega^{rq}=0
.

Below, we consider three cases: We derive the emergent-gravity Hall effect caused by emergent gravity in 1. real space, 2. momentum space, and 3. time-domain.

1. Real space
. We here consider the effects of geometrical quantities in real space. We assume a two-band Hamiltonian of the form

\displaystyle H(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{0}+\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{r})\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma},

(7)

where
\sigma_{0}
is an identity matrix and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is a vector of Pauli matrices. In this case, Bloch states are solely dependent on
\mathbf{r}
, implying that the terms in Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
) involving
k
,
t
and
\lambda
vanish, and the equations of motion reduce to

\displaystyle\dot{q}_{a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\hbar}\partialderivative{V}{r_a}+\Omega^{rr}_{ai}\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{k_i}-\Gamma^{rrr}_{a,ij}\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{k_i}\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{k_j},

(8)

\displaystyle\dot{r}_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{k_a},

(9)

where an external potential
V(\mathbf{r})
is introduced. In the following, we consider an electric field applied to the system, defined as
-eE_{a}=-\partialderivative{V}{r_a}
. For this system, the distribution function can be obtained from the Boltzmann equation with the relaxation time approximation:

\displaystyle f(\varepsilon(\mathbf{q}))

\displaystyle=f_{0}+\tau\dot{\mathbf{q}}\cdot\partialderivative{f}{\vb{q}},

(10)

where
f_{0}
is the Fermi distribution function,
f
is the modified distribution function, and
\tau
is a relaxation time. The electric current is given by
j_{a}=-e\int f(\varepsilon)\dot{r}_{a}
, where the integration is
\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}q}{(2\pi)^{d}}
with the dimension of the system
d
. Here, we focus on terms that are first-order in the external electric field. The current proportional to the
n
th order in
\tau
contains contributions up to the
n
th order in the electric field. The current response at first order in the electric field is given by

\displaystyle j^{(1)}_{a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{2}\tau}{\hbar}\int\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{q_a}\partialderivative{f_0}{q_b}E_{b}

\displaystyle+2\frac{e^{2}\tau^{2}}{\hbar}\int\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{q_a}\partialderivative{f_0}{q_i}{q_b}\quantity(\Omega^{rr}_{ij}-\Gamma^{rrr}_{i,jl}\pdv{\varepsilon}{q_l})\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{q_j}E_{b}

(11)

Because
\partialderivative{f_0}{q_i}=\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{q_i}\partialderivative{f_0}{\varepsilon}
, the term proportional to
\tau^{1}
is symmetric under the exchange of indices
a
and
b
, implying that the Hall effect does not appear at this order. At second order in
\tau
, both symmetric and antisymmetric components emerge. We introduce a conductivity tensor as
j^{(1)}_{a}=\sigma_{ab}E_{b}
. Apart from the term involving
\Omega^{rr}
which leads to the topological Hall effect, the antisymmetric part of the conductivity tensor defined as
\sigma^{\mathrm{asym}}_{ab}\coloneqq(\sigma_{ab}-\sigma_{ba})/2
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma^{\mathrm{asym}}_{ab}\coloneqq\frac{e^{2}\tau^{2}}{\hbar}\Gamma^{rrr}_{i,jl}\int\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{k_j}\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{k_l}\quantity(\pdv{\varepsilon}{k_b}\pdv[2]{f_0}{k_i}{k_a}-\pdv{\varepsilon}{k_a}\pdv[2]{f_0}{k_i}{k_b}).

(12)

This antisymmetric term is proportional to the Christoffel symbols and responsible for the Hall effect induced by the emergent gravitational field. We note that the energy should not be an even function of
\mathbf{q}
to observe this effect.

Before going into model calculations, we give expressions of the weighted quantum metric and Christoffel symbols for general two-band systems in Eq. (
7
). With
h\coloneqq\absolutevalue{\vb{h}(\vb{r})}
, the weighted quantum metric and the Christoffel symbol in real space are given by

\displaystyle G^{rr}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h^{3}}\quantity(\partial_ih_{\mu}\partial_jh_{\mu}-\partial_ih\partial_jh),

(13)

and

\displaystyle\Gamma^{rrr}_{a,ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{4h^{4}}\quantity(\partial_{a}h_{\mu}\partial_ih_{\mu}\partial_jh+\partial_{a}h_{\mu}\partial_ih\partial_jh_{\mu}-\partial_{a}h\partial_ih_{\mu}\partial_jh_{\mu})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2h^{3}}\quantity(\partial_ah_{\mu}\partial_i\partial_jh_{\mu}-\partial_ah\partial_i\partial_jh)+\frac{3}{4h^{4}}\partial_{a}h\partial_{i}h\partial_{j}h,

(14)

respectively. In the expressions above, summation over
\mu=x,y,z

\Gamma^{rrr}

. Although the Hall effect is absent in one-dimensional systems, we examine the behavior of the emergent gravitational field

by analyzing a one-dimensional domain-wall structure as an illustrative example.

The spin texture of the domain wall is described by the exchange field

(16)

Here,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{rrr}_{x,xx}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h^{3}}\partialderivative[2]{h_{\mu}}{x}\partialderivative{h_{\mu}}{x}=\frac{1}{2h}\partialderivative{\theta}{x}\partialderivative[2]{\theta}{x}.

(17)

. We now turn to the main topic of this paper: demonstration of emergent-gravity Hall effect. As a representative example, we consider a two-dimensional single skyrmion described by
Belavin and Polyakov 1975

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{h}{1+\absolutevalue{u}^{2}}\quantity(2\Re\qty(u),2\Im\qty(u),1-\abs{u}^2),

(18)

\displaystyle u(x,y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{ia}{x-iy}.

(19)

. Next, we consider the case where the
\mathbf{r}
-dependence of the system appears only through the external potential, such that the wavefunction depends solely on
\mathbf{q}
. By removing the terms involving derivatives with respect to
\lambda
and

\displaystyle\dot{q}_{a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\hbar}\partialderivative{V}{r_a},

\displaystyle\dot{r}_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\partialderivative{\varepsilon}{q_a}-\Omega^{qq}_{ai}\quantity(-\frac{1}{\hbar}\pdv{V}{r_i})+\Gamma^{qqq}_{a,ij}\quantity(-\frac{1}{\hbar}\pdv{V}{r_i})\quantity(-\frac{1}{\hbar}\pdv{V}{r_j}).

3. Time domain
.
We now focus on the time dependence of the system.
We examine the role of Christoffel symbols in mixed real, momentum, and time spaces. Neglecting the
\lambda
dependence, the terms involving Christoffel symbols with time derivatives in Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
) are given by

\displaystyle\dot{q}_{a}=

\displaystyle-2\Gamma^{rrt}_{a,i\phantom{j}}\dot{r}_{i}-2\Gamma^{rqt}_{a,i\phantom{j}}\dot{q}_{i}-\Gamma^{rtt}_{a,}+\cdots,

(26)

\displaystyle\dot{r}_{a}=

\displaystyle 2\Gamma^{qrt}_{a,i\phantom{j}}\dot{r}_{i}+2\Gamma^{qqt}_{a,i\phantom{j}}\dot{q}_{i}+\Gamma^{qtt}_{a,}+\cdots.

(27)

The physical meaning of each term can be understood by analogy.
The antisymmetric part of
\Gamma^{qqt}
induces a velocity perpendicular to
\dot{\mathbf{q}}
analogous to the anomalous velocity associated with the momentum-space Berry curvature
\Omega^{qq}
.
Thus, this gravitational anomalous velocity results in a Hall effect.
Similarly, the antisymmetric part of
\Gamma^{rrt}
gives rise to a force perpendicular to
\dot{\mathbf{r}}
and acts like a magnetic field in real space.
Hence, this gravitational Lorentz force also leads to a Hall effect.

The mixed-space terms
\Gamma^{rqt}
and
\Gamma^{qrt}
resemble the terms
\Omega^{rq}
and
\Omega^{qr}
, respectively, and gives modification of density of states from mixed-space geometry. In contrast,
\Gamma^{rtt}
and
\Gamma^{qtt}
behave as external forces and group velocities due to real and momentum space gravity, respectively.

As an example, reconsider the magnetic skyrmion discussed above. If the center of the skyrmion moves with a constant velocity
v
along
x
-axis, its position is given by
vt
. Thus, we replace
x
by
x-vt
in Eq.(19).
In this case, spatial and temporal derivatives of the Christoffel symbols are related by a constant factor. For example, we have
\Gamma^{rrt}_{y,x}=-v\Gamma^{rrr}_{y,xx}
. Therefore, we see that for a magnetic skyrmion, a Hall effect due to the gravitational Lorentz force arises. Also, for
\Gamma^{qqt}
, reconsider the system described by Eq. (
25
). Suppose that the magnetic field changes in time
\mathbf{H}(t)
. Then, we have
\partial_{t}=\dot{H}_{i}\partial_{H_{i}}
and since magnetic field only shifts the momentum in Eq. (
25
),
\partial_{H_{i}}=\varepsilon_{izj}/C\partial_{q_{j}}
holds. Therefore, we obtain
\Gamma^{qqt}_{a,b}=\dot{H}_{i}/C\varepsilon_{izj}\Gamma^{qqq}_{a,bj}
and find that this gravitational anomalous velocity also cause a Hall effect.

According to Eqs.(4) and (5), charge current can be induced by dynamics of an external parameter
\lambda
.
We discuss charge pumping by parameter space gravity in End Matter.

Conclusion
.
We have investigated transport phenomena in systems with spatial, momentum, temporal, and external-parameter dependencies. By incorporating nonadiabatic effects into the semiclassical description of electron wave packets, we find that various Christoffel symbols govern the dynamics and hence transport phenomena. We propose the emergent-gravity Hall effect, driven by real-space gravity encoded in the real-space Christoffel symbol, which appears in systems such as a single magnetic skyrmion.
We have also shown that Christoffel symbols in momentum space lead to nonlinear Hall responses, while those involving time dependencies describe gravitatinal anomalous velocity and gravitational Lorentz force, also giving rise to Hall effects. Our formulation treats all variables on an equal footing and offers a unified framework for investigating effects of effective gravity in quantum systems.

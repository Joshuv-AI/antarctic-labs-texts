Diagnostic and prognostic value of lncSPATA8-AS1 in prostate cancer and its regulatory effect on tumor progression
Authors: Jinman Zhong, Yanmeng Deng, Xingru Qin, Quanxin Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundLong noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) have significant impacts on the occurrence and development of many cancers. However, their effects and mechanisms on prostate cancer (PCa) remain largely unknown. The study was to explore the influence of lncSPATA8-AS1 on PCa and its molecular mechanism of action.MethodsIn the GSE179321 dataset, lncSPATA8-AS1, which was upregulated in PCa tumor tissues, was selected. The expression levels of lncSPATA8-AS1, miR-328-5p, and EPHB2 were detected using RT–qPCR. In vitro experiments were conducted to investigate the effects of lncSPATA8-AS1 on the malignant biological behaviors of PCa cells. Finally, the mechanism of lncSPATA8-AS1 was revealed through methods such as biotin-labeled RNA pull-down, luciferase assay, and rescue experiments.ResultsLncSPATA8-AS1 was upregulated in PCa tissues, serum, and cell lines. Clinically, lncSPATA8-AS1 could serve as a biomarker for the diagnosis and prognosis of PCa. Silencing lncSPATA8-AS1 can inhibit the cell viability of PCa tumor cells, suppress epithelial-mesenchymal transformation (EMT), cell migration, and invasion. Mechanistically, lncSPATA8-AS1 affects the expression of EPHB2 by interacting with miR-653-5p, thereby regulating the malignant biological behavior of PCa.ConclusionThe expression of lncSPATA8-AS1 is upregulated in the serum of PCa patients, and it has great potential as a biomarker for the diagnosis and prognosis of PCa. The lncSPATA8-AS1/miR-328-5p/EPHB2 axis drives the malignant biological behavior of prostate cancer.

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is the second most common malignant tumor among men worldwide, with an average annual increase of over 1.4 million cases. 1 The incidence and mortality rates of PCa show significant regional differences, with a particularly high incidence in developed countries. 2 Aging, genetic susceptibility gene mutations and abnormal androgen signaling pathways are the main factors contributing to PCa. 3 Furthermore, a high-fat diet has also been proven to be a key environmental factor that promotes the progression of diseases. 4 Adenocarcinoma is the main type of PCa. Its characteristic is the uncontrolled proliferation of prostate epithelial cells accompanied by the formation of atypical glands. 5 Imaging examinations play a central role in the diagnosis, staging, and follow-up of prostate cancer. They help doctors detect tumors, assess their severity, and formulate treatment plans. However, its existence cannot be diagnosed independently. There may be false positives or false negatives, and it is not sensitive to small tumors, etc., which are some limitations. Therefore, it is necessary to explore new biomarkers to enhance the accuracy of diagnosis and treatment.

LncRNAs play a crucial role in epigenetic regulation, transcription, and post-transcriptional modification. 6 They drive abnormal proliferation, EMT transformation, and migration and invasion of the tumor. 7 , 8 For example, LINC01094 sponges miR-224-5p, thereby regulating the expression of CHSY1 and influencing the progression of glioma. 9 LncRNA MIR497HG targets the miR-3918/ACTG2 axis to inhibit the development of colorectal cancer. 10 The LncRNA-MSC-AS1 negatively regulates miR-425-5p to inhibit the proliferation of ovarian cancer and promote its apoptosis. 11 LncRNA MEG3 affects the progression of melanoma through the miR-208/SOX4 axis. 12 An in-depth study of the molecular regulatory network of lncRNAs in regulating prostate cancer has significant clinical significance for improving patient prognosis. The core candidate molecule lncSPATA8-AS1 in this study was identified through high-throughput screening using the GEO public database (GSE179321 dataset). The screening process employed stringent differential expression criteria (|log2FC| ≥ 1.0, P < 0.05), revealing its significantly elevated expression in prostate cancer (PCa) tissue compared to adjacent normal tissue. Further bioinformatics predictions confirmed that lncSPATA8-AS1 contains specific binding sites for miR-328-5p, and can competitively bind to miR-328-5p as a ceRNA. EPHB2 may be the target gene of miR-328-5p. There is a potential ceRNA regulatory relationship among the three. To date, no studies have reported the biological functions or regulatory mechanisms of lncSPATA8-AS1 in PCa, particularly whether it modulates the miR-328-5p/EPHB2 axis via a ceRNA mechanism to influence PCa progression. This represents a distinct research gap, which serves as the primary rationale for selecting this lncRNA as the core subject of our investigation. Furthermore, a study has reported that lncRNA SPATA42 may be a potential biomarker for diagnosing azoospermia. 13 Studying this gene can reveal its mechanism of action in PCa, providing new therapeutic targets and biomarkers for the treatment of this disease.

Based on preliminary screening and predictive results, it is hypothesized that lncSPATA8-AS1 may target and bind to miR-328-5p, thereby releasing its inhibitory effect on the downstream target gene EPHB2 and consequently regulating the initiation and progression of prostate cancer. Therefore, this study aims to elucidate the expression characteristics and clinical significance of LNCSPATA8-AS1 in PCa, validate the regulatory relationship within the lncSPATA8-AS1/miR-328-5p/EPHB2 axis, elucidate the influence of this axis on the malignant phenotype of PCa cells, and provide novel candidate biomarkers and molecular targets for the early diagnosis and targeted therapy of PCa, thereby filling a gap in the research on lncSPATA8-AS1 in PCa.

Material and method

Experimental procedure

This study strictly followed the logic outlined in Supplementary Figure 1. It first screened differentially expressed lncSPATA8-AS1 in PCa. via the GEO database and, combined with bioinformatics predictions, identified the core regulatory axis. Subsequently, the expression characteristics and correlations of the three were verified through clinical samples; further, through cell experiments and molecular mechanism experiments, the functions and specificity of the regulatory axis were gradually confirmed. Eventually, the biological function and regulatory mechanism of lncSPATA8-AS1 in prostate cancer were clearly defined. All experiments were conducted with three independent repetitions to ensure the reliability of the results.

Bioinformatic processes

The dataset GSE179321 related to prostate cancer was retrieved from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Using |log2FC| ≥ 1.0 and P < 0.05 as the cutoff threshold, lncRNAs that exhibited all abnormal expressions in prostate cancer tissues compared to the surrounding normal tissues were selected. Subsequently, a volcano plot was drawn for all the abnormally expressed lncRNAs. Using the lncRNASNP2 database (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/lncRNASNP/#!/), predict the miRNAs regulated by lncSPATA8-AS1, and sort them by the energy values. Then, the downstream targets of miR-328-5p were predicted using TargetScan (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/), and the disease targets related to PCa were predicted using the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/). A Venn diagram was used to screen candidate target genes.

Patients and samples

The serum and tissue samples were obtained from 105 PCa patients with prostate cancer who were treated at The Second Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University from March 2019 to March 2020. At the same time, 80 patients diagnosed with prostatitis during the same period were randomly selected as the control group (Only take serum samples). Inclusion criteria: Patients were diagnosed with PCa based on the results of pathological biopsy and puncture; The patient received the first treatment for prostate cancer; The patient has complete clinical data; The patient is over 18 years old. Exclusion criteria: Patients with concurrent other malignant tumors; The patient has other urinary system diseases. The patient suffers from mental disorders. The participating researchers were fully informed of the content of this study and signed the consent form. The research has been approved by the Ethics Committee of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University (No. 2019-1124). All procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Prostate cancer and paired control tissue sample collection procedure‌

All prostate tissue samples were collected from surgical specimens at our hospital's Department of Urology. Post-resection, the tumor and adjacent normal prostate tissues were promptly identified by a pathologist. Tumor tissues were harvested from solid cancer lesions, while paired normal controls were obtained from histologically verified tumor-free tissues over 2 cm from the tumor margin. All samples were processed within 15 min after resection to prevent RNA degradation. Tissues were rinsed with sterile PBS to remove residual blood and fluid, placed in sterile cryotubes labeled with patient ID, sample type and collection date, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored long-term at −80 °C without repeated freeze–thaw cycles for subsequent RNA extraction and RT–qPCR analysis.

Fasting peripheral venous blood (3‒5 mL) was collected from all participants in plain nonanticoagulated vacuum tubes. Blood samples were clotted at room temperature for 30 min and centrifuged at 3000 r/min for 15 min at 4 °C. The clear upper serum layer was carefully collected to avoid cell pellets and precipitates, aliquoted into nuclease-free cryotubes (200–300 μL per tube) and fully labeled. All serum samples were stored long-term at −80 °C without repeated freeze‒thaw cycles to maintain nucleic acid stability for subsequent RNA extraction and RT‒qPCR detection.

All samples were enrolled based on unified inclusion and exclusion criteria, with complete clinical information including patient age, PSA level, Gleason score, and tumor stage. All samples underwent strict quality control before experiments. RNA purity and integrity were validated via Nanodrop and agarose gel electrophoresis. Only samples with an OD260/280 ratio of 1.8–2.1 and no obvious RNA degradation were adopted for subsequent experiments. All samples were only used for the present study in strict compliance with ethical guidelines to guarantee credible and reproducible experimental results.‌

Follow-up

We conducted a 60-month follow-up study on all the prostate cancer patients who received treatment. Follow-up records are conducted every three months. Each patient was followed up until the end of the study or until their death. The follow-up methods include phone calls, video calls, outpatient visits, home visits, etc.

Cell culture

RWPE-1 (SNL-569), LNCaP (SNL-356), 22Rv-1 (SNL-101), C4-2B (SNL-161), DU145 (SNL-129), and PC-3 (SNL-102) were purchased from SUNNCELL. RWPE-1 requires cultivation in Defined K-SFM+1% P/S medium (SUNNCELL, SNLM-569). LNCaP, 22Rv-1, and C4-2B cell lines were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium containing 10%FBS and 1%P/S (SUNNCELL, SNLM-356, SNLM-101, SNLM-161). DU145 requires cultivation in DMEM+10%FBS+1%P/S (SUNNCELL, SNLM-129) medium. PC-3 requires cultivation in F12K+10%FBS+1% P/S (SUNNCELL, SNLM-102) medium. The culture environment for the cells was 37 °C with 5% CO2. The specifications of all the special culture media are 500 mL.

Cell transfection

Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) specifically targeting lncSPATA8-AS1 and the corresponding negative control (NC) siRNA were chemically synthesized and provided by MCE (MedChemExpress, USA). In addition, the miR‑328‑5p mimics, miR‑328‑5p inhibitors, and their respective negative control sequences (miR-NC for mimics, inhibitor-NC for inhibitors) were commercially obtained from Thermo Fisher Scientific (USA). For cellular transfection of synthetic oligonucleotides (siRNAs, miRNA mimics, and miRNA inhibitors) and recombinant plasmids, Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent (Cat. No. L3000001, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was utilized following the manufacturer's standard protocols with optimized experimental parameters for the present cell line. Briefly, cells were cultured to 60%–70% confluency before transfection to guarantee high transfection efficiency and cell viability. For each well, 1 μL of Lipofectamine 3000 reagent and 1 μL of the indicated oligonucleotides (siRNA, miR‑328‑5p mimics/inhibitors, or their matched negative controls) were separately diluted in serum- and antibiotic-free medium. The two dilutions were gently mixed and incubated at room temperature for 10–15 min to form liposome‒nucleic acid complexes. The complex mixture was then evenly added to cell wells, followed by incubation at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. The transfection medium was replaced with fresh complete medium 6 h later to reduce cytotoxicity. Subsequent cellular functional assays and molecular detection were conducted at the corresponding time points after transfection.

RT–qPCR

The expression of lncSPATA8-AS1, miR-328-5p, and EPHB2 was detected by RT–qPCR. Total RNA was extracted from tissues, serum, and cells using TRIzol reagent (15596026CN, ThermoFisher, USA) and purified. Then 1 μg of RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA. Subsequently, real-time quantitative PCR amplification was performed with the following thermal cycling conditions: predenaturation at 95 °C for 3 min, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 10 s and annealing-extension at 60 °C for 30 s. GAPDH was used as an internal reference for lncSPATA8-AS1 and EPHB2, and U6 was used as an internal reference for miR-328-5p. Finally, the data were normalized by 2−ΔΔCt. The primer sequences were as shown in Supplementary Table 1.

MTT assay

22Rv-1 and PC-3 cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 6 × 103 cells per well and incubated for 24 h until reaching 60%–70% confluency before transfection. For each well, 1 μL Lipofectamine 3000 and 1 μL corresponding oligonucleotides, including siRNA targeting lncSPATA8-AS1, miR-328-5p mimics, miR-328-5p inhibitors, and their negative controls, were separately diluted in serum- and antibiotic-free medium. After gentle blending, the mixtures were incubated at room temperature for 10–15 min to form liposome‒nucleic acid complexes, which were then added to cell culture wells uniformly. Cells were maintained at 37 °C with 5% CO₂, and the transfection medium was replaced with fresh complete medium at 6 h post-transfection. After further incubation for another 24 h, 5 mg/mL MTT solution (Cat. 11465007001, Merck) was supplemented into each well. Following 4 h incubation, 150 μL DMSO (Cat. 472301, Merck) was added to fully dissolve formazan crystals. The optical density value was detected at 490 nm, and relative cell viability was calculated accordingly.

Transwell assay

The transfected 22Rv-1 and PC-3 cells were seeded at a density of 1 × 105 cells per well in the upper chamber of the transwell. In the upper chamber, an FBS-free culture medium was added, while in the lower chamber, the culture medium contained 10% FBS. After 24 h, the cells were fixed with 4% formaldehyde for 20 min and then stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 30 min. In the invasion experiment, on the day before inoculation, the upper chamber of the transwell was coated with Matrigel basement membrane gel at 4 °C. Other operations were carried out for the migration experiment. Finally, use the inverted microscope (Ti2-E, NIKON) to take pictures of 5 random fields of view under a 200× magnification.

RNA pulldown assay

Biotin-labeled lncSPATA8-AS1, miR-328-5p, and control oligonucleotide probes were incubated with streptavidin-coupled magnetic beads at 4 °C for 4 h under continuous gentle rotation to form stable probe-bead complexes. Meanwhile, transfected cells were lysed with ice-cold lysis buffer to prepare the total cell lysate. The preassembled probe-bead complexes were coincubated with cell lysate overnight at 4 °C with constant rotation to enrich target RNA complexes. After thorough washing and purification to eliminate non-specific binding products, the enriched target RNAs were extracted, and their relative expression levels were quantified via RT‒qPCR.

Dual-luciferase assay

Wild-type and mutant sequences of lncSPATA8-AS1 and EPHB2 3'-untranslated region (3'-UTR) containing the predicted binding sites of miR-328-5p were amplified and inserted into the pmiRGLO (QP1210, Qiyunbio, Jiangxi, China) luciferase reporter vector to construct recombinant plasmids, namely SPATA8-AS1-wt, SPATA8-AS1-mut, EPHB2-wt, and EPHB2-mut. Subsequently, miR-328-5p mimics, miR-328-5p inhibitors, and their corresponding negative controls were co-transfected with the above recombinant luciferase plasmids into PC-3 cells using Lipofectamine 3000 (Cat. No. L3000001, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Cells were continuously cultured under standard conditions for 48 h after transfection. Finally, the dual-luciferase reporter assay system (E1910, Promega, USA) was applied to measure Firefly and Renilla luciferase activities, and the relative luciferase activity was calculated.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using SPSS 27.0.1 statistical software and GraphPad Prism 9.0 statistical software. The data were presented as (mean ± SD). The comparison between the two groups was conducted using the Student's t-test. The comparison among multiple groups was performed using one-way or two-way analysis of variance, followed by Tukey's multiple comparison test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All the experiments were conducted more than three times.

Results

The expression of lncSPATA8-AS1.

First, we selected the differentially expressed lncRNAs used in the GSE179321 dataset and plotted a volcano plot. The volcano plot shows the changes in lncRNA expression between normal tissues and PCa tissues. Among all the upregulated lncRNAs, lncSPATA8-AS1 has the largest log2FoldChange value (Figure 1A). Therefore, we chose lncSPATA8-AS1 as the research subject. LncSPATA8-AS1 expression detected by RT‒qPCR was consistent with the results of dataset GSE179321. LncSPATA8-AS1 was abnormally elevated in the tumor tissues of 105 PCa patients (Figure 1B). In addition, we also examined its expression levels in serum and PCa cell lines. LncSPATA8-AS1 expression increased in the serum and cell lines of PCa patients, which was consistent with the expression level in the tissues (Figure 1C and D). The upward trend predicted by bioinformatics was highly consistent with the qPCR verification results of clinical samples, proving that the screening results were not due to random data. Furthermore, the subcellular localization analysis of lncSPATA8-AS1 revealed that its expression level in the cytoplasm was significantly higher than that in the nucleus, suggesting that it is mainly located in the cytoplasm (Figure 1E). So, we speculate that lncSPATA8-AS1 plays an important role in the occurrence and development of PCa.

The clinical value of lncSPATA8-AS1

To explore the significance of lncSPATA8-AS1 in the clinical treatment of PCa, we focused on studying its diagnostic and prognostic value. Firstly, we used the chi-square test to evaluate the relationship between the clinical and pathological characteristics of PCa patients and the expression level of lncSPATA8-AS1. The average expression level of serum in PCA patients was used as the cutoff threshold to divide 105 patients into the high-expression group (n = 53) and the low-expression group (n = 52). The results showed that age, BMI, tumor size, smoking history, and alcohol history were not related to the expression of lncSPATA8-AS1, while PSA, TNM stage, Gleason score, and lymph metastases were closely associated with it (Table 1). Furthermore, we constructed an ROC diagnostic model based on lncSPATA8-AS1 expression in the serum of healthy individuals and PCa patients. The AUC of this ROC curve is 0.9236, with a 95% CI of 0.8878–0.9600 (Figure 2A). Furthermore, the analysis also revealed that its sensitivity was 82.9% and its specificitywas 88.7%. This result indicated that lncSPATA8-AS has a high diagnostic value. We also analyzed the prognosis of PCa patients. The K–M survival curve indicates that patients with high expression of lncSPATA8-AS1 have a lower survival rate (Figure 2B). Finally, Multivariate COX analysis also provided strong support for the notion that lncSPATA8-AS1 can serve as an indicator for the prognosis of PCa (Figure 2C).

Silencing lncSPATA8-AS1 inhibits the progression of PCa

To assess the impact of lncSPATA8-AS1 on PCa, we used siRNAs that specifically targeted lncSPATA8-AS1 (si-1# and si-2#). The RT‒qPCR results indicated that in 22Rv-1 and PC-3 cells, compared with the negative control group, the expression levels of lncSPATA8-AS1 in the si-1# and si-2# groups were significantly reduced (Figure 3A). This indicated that si-1# and si-2# were successfully transfected into 22Rv-1 and PC-3 cells and lncSPATA8-AS1 expression was knocked down. The results of the CCK-8 experiment showed that in 22Rv-1 and PC-3 cells, silencing the expression of lncSPATA8-AS1 inhibited the viability of PCa cells (Figure 3B). Additionally, we also examined the EMT markers. The results indicated that compared with the control group, both si-1# and si-2# significantly increased the expression of E-cadherin and significantly decreased the expression of Vimentin in 22Rv-1 cells (Figure 3C). Similarly, in PC-3 cells, compared with the control group, both si-1# and si-2# significantly increased the expression of E-cadherin and significantly decreased the expression of Vimentin in 22Rv-1 cells (Figure 3D). Finally, Transwell assay results indicated that in 22Rv-1 and PC-3 cells, after silencing lncSPATA8-AS1, the migration and invasion abilities of the cells were both inhibited (Figure 3E and F). The above experimental results indicated that silencing lncSPATA8-AS1 can inhibit the malignant biological behavior of prostate cancer.

Verification of the targeting relationship between lncSPATA8-AS1 and miR-328-5p

It is well known that lncRNAs exert their functions in the body by acting as miRNA sponges. We used the lncRNAANP2 database to predict the three miRNAs (miR-328-5p, miR-6885-5p, miR-4763-5p) that had the highest binding stability with lncSPATA8-AS1 (Figure 4A). Next, we designed a biotin-labeled lncSPATA8-AS1 probe to pull down the miRNAs that interact with it. We found that miR-328-5p and miR-6885-5p were specifically enriched, while miR-4763-5p was not (Figure 4B). MiR-328-5p expression was significantly higher than that of miR-6885-5p (Figure 4B). Therefore, miR-328-5p was selected as the downstream target of lncSPATA8-AS1 for further study. More importantly, the results of RT–qPCR indicated that the expression level of miR-328-5p in the serum of PCa patients was significantly reduced (Figure 4C). This shows an opposite trend compared to the expression of lncSPATA8-AS1. These results suggest that there may be a negative regulatory relationship between lncSPATA8-AS1 and miR-329-5p. The subsequent dual-luciferase enzyme experiment and Spearman correlation analysis provided strong evidence for this. We found that in cells containing the wild sequence of lncSPATA8-AS1 (SPATA8-AS1-wt), miR-328-5p mimics significantly reduced luciferase activity, while miR-328-5p inhibitors significantly enhanced this activity (Figure 4D). The luciferase activity of cells containing the lncSPATA8-AS1 mutation sequence (SPATA8-AS1-mut) was not affected by miR-328-5p mimics nor by miR-328-5p inhibitors (Figure 4D). The results of the correlation analysis indicated that lncSPATA8-AS1 is negatively correlated with miR-328-5p with r = −0.5400, P < 0.001 (Figure 4E). Based on the above results, we speculated that lncSPATA8-AS1 acts as a sponge for miR-328-5p.

Verification of the targeting relationship between miR-328-5p and EPHB2

To specifically elucidate the potential molecular mechanism by which lncSPATA8-AS1 regulates PCa through the sponge effect of miR-328-5p, we conducted bioinformatics analysis to predict the downstream target genes of miR-328-5p. The Targetscan database predicted 418 downstream targets of miR-328-5p. In the Genecards database, disease targets related to PCa were retrieved and the screening condition was set as 10 times the average Relevance score. Eventually, a total of 469 genes were obtained (Figure 5A). The two were intersected to obtain a total of 5 common target genes (KMT2D, XRCC3, PTPN11, RUNX1, and EPHB2) (Figure 5A). The result of pulling down mRNA using biotinylated miR-328-5p as the probe indicated that the enrichment level of EPHB2 was the highest (Figure 5B). Therefore, EPHB2 was selected as the final effector protein. Similarly, by detecting the expression of EPHB2 in the serum of PCa patients, we found that its expression level was abnormally elevated (Figure 5C). The dual-luciferase assay confirmed the existence of an interaction between miR-328-5p and EPHB2 (Figure 5D). It is worth noting that the Spearman correlation analysis indicated that the relationship between them is negatively correlated with r = −0.5243, P < 0.001 (Figure 5E). Based on the above experimental results, we speculate that EPHB2 is the downstream target of miR-328-5p.

Lnc-SPATA8-AS1 affects the progression of PCa through the miR-328-5p/EPHB2 axis

To demonstrate that lncSPATA8-AS1 affects the malignant biological behavior of prostate cancer through the miR-328-5p/EPHB2 axis, si-1# and miR-328-5p inhibitors were successfully co-transfected. After co-transfecting si-1# and miR-328-5p inhibitors into 22Rv-1 and PCa cells, the inhibitory effect of silencing lncSPATA8-AS1 on EPHB2 was reversed (Figure 6A). The CCK-8 experimental results showed that simultaneously silencing lncSPATA8-AS1 and inhibiting the expression of miR-328-5p could reverse the inhibitory effect of silencing lncSPATA8-AS1 on the viability of 22Rv-1 and PC-3 cells (Figure 6B). The EMT marker detection results showed that in 22Rv-1 and PC-3 cells, compared with the control group, silencing lncSPATA8-AS1 could promote the expression of E-cadherin and inhibit the expression of Vimentin. However, simultaneously inhibiting the expression of lncSPATA8-AS1 and miR-328-5p could reverse this effect. (Figure 6C and D). The Transwell assay indicated that simultaneously silencing lncSPATA8-AS1 and inhibiting the expression of miR-328-5p could reverse the inhibitory effect of silencing lncSPATA8-AS1 on the migration and invasion of 22Rv-1 and PC-3 cells (Figure 6E and F). Based on this, we hypothesize that lncSPATA8-AS1 promotes the malignant biological behavior of PCa by protecting EPHB2 from being inhibited by miR-328-5p.

Discussion

This study is the first to confirm that lncSPATA8-AS1 is significantly upregulated in prostate cancer tissues, serum, and cell lines, and its expression level is closely related to factors such as PSA level, tumor size, TNM stage, Gleason score, and lymph metastasis. This discovery is consistent with the functions of lncRNAs as diagnostic and prognostic markers in other types of cancers, as reported in previous studies. For instance, lncRNA SNHG16 has been reported to serve as a diagnostic and prognostic marker for head and neck squamous cell carcinoma. 14 LncRNA OIP5-AS1 is a promising biomarker for the diagnosis and prognosis of cholangiocarcinoma. 15 The upregulation of lncRNA AFAP1-AS1 is always associated with poor prognosis of gastric cancer. 16 It is worth noting that the upregulation of lncSPATA8-AS1 in the serum of prostate cancer patients suggests that it may enter the circulatory system through secretion or exosome pathways, potentially providing a tool for noninvasive diagnosis. This study is the first to systematically evaluate the potential of lncSPATA8-AS1 as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in a cohort of PCa patients. Although the acquisition of tissue samples was limited, we achieved noninvasive biomarker validation through serum exosomes, which were highly consistent with the tissue expression. This aligns with the latest advancements in liquid biopsy in the monitoring of PCa. 17 To ensure robustness of results, we reported the diagnostic efficacy (AUC = 0.9236) and prognostic value (HR = 2.751) of lncSPATA8-AS1 in accordance with the REMARK guidelines, and confirmed model stability through internal Bootstrap validation. After excluding the low-risk cases in the sensitivity analysis, lncSPATA8-AS1 remained an independent prognostic factor (P = 0.036), supporting its potential application in clinical stratification. Future research will focus on verifying the mechanism of the downstream pathway EPHB2 of lncSPATA8-AS1 to promote its transformation into a targeted therapy.

The mechanism study of this research reveals that silencing lncSPATA8-AS1 upregulates the expression of miR-328-5p, thereby inhibiting EPHB2 expression. This “sponge-target” regulatory mode is highly similar to the classical lncRNA mechanism. For instance, lncRNA-ATB acts as a sponge for miR-200a, regulating β-catenin and thereby influencing the progression of non-small cell lung cancer. 18 LncRNA-ZXF1 promotes the progression of endometrial cancer through the miR-378a-3p/PCDHA3 axis. 19 The LncRNA FGD5-AS1 acts as a sponge for miR-520a-3p, regulating KIAA1522 and accelerating the progression of pancreatic cancer cells. 20 However, the function of the lncSPATA8-AS1/miR-328-5p/EPHB2 axis was first verified in PCa. EPHB2 (Ephrin type-B receptor 2) is a member of the Eph receptor tyrosine kinase family and is involved in cell movement, differentiation, and tissue development. Its overexpression promotes cell proliferation and migration. 21 Therefore, it is speculated that lncSPATA8-AS1 regulates the expression of EPHB2 through miR-328-5p, which may affect the metastasis and invasion of prostate cancer. This study directly confirmed the targeting relationship between lncSPATA8-AS1 and miR-328-5p, as well as the targeting relationship between miR-328-5p and EPHB2, through RNA pull-down experiments, luciferase reporter gene experiments, and Spearman correlation analysis. This provides strong theoretical data support for a more comprehensive analysis of the mechanism by which EPHB2 affects the metastasis and invasion of prostate cancer tumors.

The functional experiments in this study demonstrated that silencing lncSPATA8-AS1 could significantly inhibit the viability, migration, and invasion of prostate cancer cells, and reverse the EMT process. These malignant biological behaviors are closely related to the regulation of EPHB2. The cell function experiments indicated that inhibiting miR-328-5p could partially reverse the inhibition of EPHB2 expression and the inhibition of cell malignancy caused by the silencing of lncSPATA8-AS1. It is worth noting that the reversal in the expression levels of EMT markers E-cadherin and Vimentin suggests that lncSPATA8-AS1 may affect tumor metastasis and invasion by regulating the epithelial-mesenchymal transition network. Studies have confirmed that E-cadherin expression significantly decreases during the EMT process, resulting in weakened intercellular adhesion and promoting the migration of cancer cells. 22 Vimentin is associated with the remodeling of the cell cytoskeleton and enhanced motility. Its expression increases during EMT. 23 The experimental results of the cell functions in this study are highly consistent with this. This is highly consistent with the previous finding that LncRNA CCAT1 sponge miR-218-5p promotes EMT, cell migration, and the invasion of retinoblastoma. 24

In this study, lncSPATA8-AS1 was derived from the bioinformatics mining of the GEO public database. The research plan did not include the content of independently conducting whole transcriptome sequencing, which was an objective limitation of this study. In the future, we will “utilize high-throughput technologies such as transcriptomics and proteomics to systematically analyze the specific molecular pathways by which the lncSPATA8-AS1/miR-328-5p/EPHB2 axis regulates tumor metastasis.” Through the design of independent research projects, relevant high-throughput experiments were carried out to deeply explore the downstream regulatory network of this molecule and to improve the functional mechanism research of this molecular axis.

Conclusion

In summary, this study for the first time revealed the mechanism by which lncSPATA8-AS1 drives the malignant progression of prostate cancer through the miR-328-5p/EPHB2 axis, and confirmed its potential as a diagnostic and prognostic marker.

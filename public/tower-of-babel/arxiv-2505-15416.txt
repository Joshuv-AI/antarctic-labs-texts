Abstract
The game of cops and robber is a two-player turn-based game played on a graph where the cops try to capture the robber. The cop number of a graph G G , denoted by c ⁡ ( G ) c(G) is the minimum number of cops required to capture the robber. For a given class of graphs ℱ {\cal F} , let c ⁡ ( ℱ ) := sup { c ⁡ ( F ) | F ∈ ℱ } c({\cal F}):=\sup\{c(F)|F\in{\cal F}\} , and let Forb ( ℱ ) ({\cal F}) denote the class of ℱ {\cal F} -free graphs. The game of cops and robber is a well studied problem in graph theory as can be seen in literature (see
[
1

5

11

[

studied the above game independently. The game is a turn-based game and is played on a connected graph by two players. For a given graph

G
and in the next turn, the player two puts the robber in a vertex of
G
. Clearly, this is round
1
of the game. After that, the game is played on by alternative turns of the player one and the player two. For
r\in\mathbb{N}
and

, let

be the vertex, where the cop

is placed at the round
r
of the game in
G
. For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
p_{r}^{G}(R)
be the vertex, where the robber

is placed at the round
r
in
G
. We drop the superscript
G
when there is no ambiguity. For each

, a move played by the player one in the round

is legal if the player one puts

p_{r-1}(L)
or in some adjacent vertex of

, where

, a move played by the player two in the round

is legal if the player two puts
R
in
p_{r-1}(R)
or in some adjacent vertex of
p_{r-1}(R)
. A cop

the robber if they are at the same vertex of the graph. In other words, the robber

is captured by the set of cops

in the round

of the game if there exists a cop

such that

, there exists a cop

such that

SPP1 + macrophage and Treg interaction mediates immunosuppression and adverse survival in HER2 + breast cancer
Authors: Hao Gao, Kangjie Shen, Luhong Chen, Chao Huang, Jie Ji, Xiaochao Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHuman epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) -positive breast cancer exhibits aggressive clinical progression and unfavorable outcomes. While HER2-directed agents and immune checkpoint inhibitors have transformed clinical management, persistent challenges include suboptimal response rates and acquired resistance. These limitations underscore the critical need for alternative therapeutic approaches. Our investigation, leveraging single-cell transcriptomic data derived from both healthy and neoplastic breast tissues, sought to elucidate the specific cellular subsets and regulatory pathways that contribute to the aggressive characteristics of HER2 + breast cancer. Subsequent validation of these findings was achieved through the incorporation of bulk transcriptomic datasets from various breast cancer cohorts, alongside spatial transcriptomics data pertaining to HER2 + breast malignancies, and comprehensive multiplex immunohistochemistry examinations. We identified a marked elevation of SPP1 + macrophages, displaying an M2-like immune-suppressive phenotype, within the tumor microenvironment. These particular macrophage populations were deeply implicated in modulating T cell activities and consistently correlated with adverse patient outcomes. At a mechanistic level, we ascertained that SPP1 + macrophages bolster the immune-suppressive capacity of Tregs via engagement of the CD86-CTLA4 axis, thereby facilitating the advancement of the tumor. Furthermore, the strong association between SPP1 + macrophages and both unfavorable prognosis and advanced disease stages was corroborated in an independent, in-house HER2 + breast cancer patient group. Background

Occurring in an estimated 15% to 20% of cases, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) amplification and overexpression in breast cancer is commonly characterized by heightened invasiveness.1–3. HER2-targeted therapies, such as trastuzumab (Herceptin) and ado-trastuzumab emtansine (Kadcyla), have fundamentally transformed the treatment of HER2 + breast cancer4–6, leading to five-year survival rates typically reaching approximately 85%7. Nevertheless, the development of drug resistance has constrained long-term clinical efficacy, underscoring the critical necessity for identifying new therapeutic targets in HER2 + breast cancer8,9.

Within the tumor microenvironment, macrophages constitute a significant population of infiltrating immune cells, yet their influence on cancer progression remains paradoxical10,11. This can be plausibly attributed to the substantial heterogeneity of macrophages, which results in divergent anti- or pro-tumor phenotypes12,13. Shen et al. observed a phenotypic shift in macrophages from a lipid metabolism-associated profile to an angiogenesis-promoting profile during melanoma distant metastasis, thereby underscoring the pivotal role of macrophage energy metabolism in this process14. Wang et al. put forth the substantial contribution of M1-like macrophages to radiation-induced distant effects, stressing that augmenting macrophage IRF7 secretion fosters an anti-tumor immune response15. In the context of colorectal cancer liver metastasis, Wu et al. pinpointed a distinct population of MRC1 + CCL18 + macrophages characterized by a terminally differentiated and metabolically activated state. These cells were found to be indispensable for the effectiveness of neoadjuvant chemotherapy16. Wang et al. also showed the role for SPP1 + macrophages associated tumor progression in patients with colorectal cancer liver metastasis by triggering fibroblast extracellular vesicles17. Gaining further insight into the contribution of macrophages to HER2 + breast cancer malignancy, combined with the creation of specific inhibitory or activating strategies derived from this understanding, holds the potential to introduce significant adjunct therapeutic modalities for this disease.

Leveraging single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and spatial transcriptomics data from HER2 + breast cancer specimens, our analysis pinpointed a distinct population of SPP1 + macrophages exhibiting preferential upregulation specifically within tumor tissues. These macrophages display an M2-like phenotype, actively modulate T cell chemotaxis and migration, and demonstrate a strong correlation with adverse patient outcomes in HER2 + breast cancer. Mechanistically, SPP1 + macrophages likely propel tumor progression by both recruiting Tregs and fostering their immune-suppressive functions. Consequently, directly targeting SPP1 + macrophages could emerge as a promising new therapeutic strategy for HER2 + breast cancer.

Methods

Data source

We acquired scRNA-seq data from both normal breast tissues and HER2 + breast cancer tissues via GSE161529 dataset18. We sourced the spatial transcriptomics findings for HER2 + breast cancer tissues from GSE225600 dataset19. To ascertain the prognostic importance of SPP1 + macrophages and Tregs, we examined transcriptomic information from breast cancer patients whose survival outcomes were detailed in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and GSE20685. Comprehensive clinical profiles for these two cohorts are presented in Table S1.

Processing procedure of scRNA-seq cohort

The single-cell data underwent a quality control procedure that followed previously established pipeline20. Data preprocessing commenced with the application of the “NormalizeData,” “FindVariableFeatures,” “ScaleData,” and “RunPCA” functions, all derived from the “Seurat” package21. Subsequently, batch effects were mitigated via the “harmony” package. Finally, visualization was achieved through the use of Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP).

Cell type-specific marker genes were identified utilizing the “FindAllMarkers” function. Subsequent annotation of the primary cellular populations was accomplished by referencing a compendium of well-recognized classical cell markers. The “AddModuleScore” algorithm served to compute gene set scores for designated cell subpopulations. For the SPP1 + macrophage subpopulation, a volcano plot offered a visual representation of differentially expressed genes, while enrichment analysis was performed using the “clusterProfiler” package22. Marker genes, identified at the single-cell level, were subsequently deconvolved into the bulk transcriptome dataset utilizing the “ssGSEA” algorithm from the “GSVA” package23. This methodology allowed for the precise quantification of specific cell subset infiltration within the bulk transcriptome cohort. Subsequently, Kaplan–Meier analyses and their corresponding visualizations were generated using the “survival” and “survminer” packages. Analysis of cell–cell interactions was conducted using the “CellChat” package24.

Processing procedure of spatial transcriptomics data

The spatial transcriptomic data underwent processing in accordance with established methods19. Following the ingestion of the raw data, we executed dimensionality reduction and subsequent clustering. These steps leveraged established Seurat functions, specifically “SCTransform,” “RunPCA,” “RunUMAP,” “FindNeighbors,” and “FindClusters.” Subsequently, the “AddModuleScore” algorithm was employed to quantify the infiltration levels of both SPP1 + macrophages and Tregs, leveraging marker genes previously identified from single-cell data. Their spatial distribution was then rendered visible via the “SpatialPlot” function.

Multiplex immunohistochemical assays

To assess the infiltration of SPP1 + macrophages and Tregs within the tumor microenvironment, we conducted multiplex immunohistochemical (mIHC) assays. For the detection of SPP1 + macrophages, CD68 and SPP1 were employed as identifying markers. Concurrently, FOXP3 was designated as the marker for Tregs. We subjected the samples to fixation in 4% paraformaldehyde. Subsequently, permeabilization was achieved with 0.3% Triton X-100, followed by a blocking step utilizing 5% fetal bovine serum. Subsequent to overnight exposure to the primary antibody at 4 °C, the samples were then exposed to the appropriate secondary antibody. Nuclei were visualized by staining with 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI). The specific data for each antibody are presented in Table S2.

Patients with HER2 + breast cancer from our in-house dataset

For our internal validation cohort, we procured HER2 + breast cancer tumor specimens along with their matched normal breast tissue from patients receiving care at Suqian Hospital, Jiangsu Province People’s Hospital. These samples were gathered between September 2018 and September 2024 (Table S3). The samples underwent mIHC, and the resulting quantitative data were subsequently employed for survival analysis and clinical correlation investigations. All patients were treatment-naive with respect to any pharmaceutical intervention before their surgical procedure. The Medical Ethics Committee of Jiangsu Province (Suqian) Hospital granted approval for this study (Approval No. 2025-SR-0121). All the patients signed the informed consent. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Quantification of M1 and M2 score in myeloid subpopulations

The “AddModuleScore” algorithm, integrated within the “Seurat” package, was employed to determine the M1 and M2 scores for myeloid subpopulations. Comprehensive gene sets employed for scoring are itemized in Table S4. Subsequent to the computation of M1 and M2 scores, their distribution was visually represented using box plots.

Co-culture experiments and functional characterization

THP-1 cells, sourced from ATCC, were maintained in Roswell Park Memorial Institute 1640 medium, which was enriched with 10% FBS. Culture conditions were set at 37 °C with 5% CO2. As previously described, THP-1 cells were induced to differentiate into M2-type macrophages via stimulation with IL-4 and IL-13. Conversely, M1-type macrophages were generated by treating THP-1 cells with IFN-γ and LPS25. Given their elevated SPP1 expression, M2-type macrophages served as a suitable model for SPP1 + macrophages, whereas M1-type macrophages represented SPP1- macrophages. Following established protocols, Tregs were obtained from peripheral blood mononuclear cells26. Tregs underwent a 48-h co-culture period under three distinct conditions: alongside SPP1- macrophages, with SPP1 + macrophages, and with SPP1 + macrophages in the presence of an anti-CTLA4 antibody. Post-co-culture, these cells were isolated using CD4 magnetic bead sorting. Subsequently, Tregs from all three conditions underwent an additional 24-h culture period. The secreted levels of IL-10 were then quantified via enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).

Statistical evaluation

Statistical evaluations were entirely conducted within R software. This included the application of Student’s t-test for pairwise comparisons, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for non-parametric datasets, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for comparing multiple groups, and the log-rank test for assessing survival outcomes. A p-value below 0.05 was set as the threshold for statistical significance, unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Results

Comprehensive single-cell landscape in HER2 + breast cancer

To precisely characterize the tumor microenvironment in HER2 + breast cancer, we analyzed scRNA-seq data from GSE161529. This dataset comprised 6 tumor specimens from HER2 + breast cancer patients and 13 healthy breast tissue samples (Fig. 1A). Following rigorous data quality control, 106,904 cells were retained for subsequent analytical procedures. Subsequently, applying dimensionality reduction and clustering at a resolution of 1 yielded the identification of 42 distinct cell subclusters across all 19 tissues (Fig. 1B-C). Moreover, we conducted an initial cell type assignment for each subcluster by employing well-established classical marker genes27,28. The annotation process yielded seven unique cellular populations (Fig. 1D-E): epithelial or tumor cells (53,154 cells, representing 49.7%), identified by markers such as KRT18, KRT19, KRT8, and KRT7; plasma cells (2,819 cells, 2.6%), characterized by IGHG1, JCHAIN, IGHG2, and IGKC; myeloid cells (4,962 cells, 4.6%), expressing LYZ, C1QA, CD68, and G0S2; cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAF) (20,983 cells, 19.6%), indicated by DCN, LUM, COL6A3, and COL1A1; B cells (2,337 cells, 2.2%), positive for MS4A1, CD79A, BANK1, and CD79B; T and NK cells (13,591 cells, 12.7%), recognized by CD3D, CD3E, CD3G, and IL7R; and finally, endothelial cells (9,058 cells, 8.5%), distinguished by CDH5, VWF, PECAM1, and CLDN5. Upon evaluating the proportional variations among distinct cell subtypes across our samples and groups, we discovered a significant increase in the infiltration of myeloid and T/NK cells within tumor tissues when contrasted with normal breast tissues. This underscores the critical involvement of these two cell types in the initiation and advancement of HER2 + breast cancer (Fig. 1F). In summary, our initial characterization of the HER2 + breast cancer tumor microenvironment demonstrates a marked increase in the infiltration of myeloid and T/NK cells within malignant tissues. This insight suggests a compelling rationale for therapeutic interventions aimed at these two cellular populations.

Immunosuppressive SPP1 + macrophages linked to adverse outcomes in patients with HER2 + breast cancer

Due to the notable increase in myeloid cell infiltration within tumor tissues, we proceeded with a comprehensive characterization of these cells. Myeloid cells underwent further categorization into dendritic cells, monocytes, and macrophages. Notably, secreted phosphoprotein-1 (SPP1) is predominantly produced by macrophages29. Prior investigations have illuminated the pivotal role of SPP1 + macrophages in the aggressive nature of colorectal carcinoma, gastric adenocarcinoma, and hepatocellular carcinoma30–32. Nevertheless, the precise role of these macrophages in HER2 + breast cancer remains uninvestigated. Consequently, we sub-classified macrophages into SPP1- macrophages and SPP1 + macrophages, relying on their respective SPP1 expression levels (Fig. 2A-B). Tumor tissues exhibited a significant elevation in the infiltration of both SPP1- macrophages and SPP1 + macrophages relative to normal tissues, with SPP1 + macrophages demonstrating an even more striking increase (Fig. 2C). The quantitative results from mIHC assays performed on our samples independently corroborated this finding (Fig. 2D). Our analysis involved M1 and M2 gene set scoring for the SPP1- and SPP1 + macrophage populations within the single-cell dataset. This revealed that SPP1 + macrophages possessed notably lower M1 scores (Fig. 2E) and concurrently higher M2 scores (Fig. 2F), indicative of their immunosuppressive nature. Utilizing deconvolution algorithms, we quantified the infiltration of SPP1 + macrophages within breast cancer bulk transcriptome cohorts. This analysis revealed that patients with elevated SPP1 + macrophage infiltration consistently experienced a significantly poorer prognosis compared to those with lower infiltration, a trend evident in both the TCGA-BRCA and GSE20685 cohorts (Fig. 2G). We also performed the subgroup analyses according to the HER2 status and stage in TCGA-BRCA cohort. These results consistently showed that high SPP1 + macrophage infiltration is associated with poorer prognosis in the HER2 + sub-group, as well as in both early and advanced clinical stages (Figure S1C). To investigate the mechanisms driving the aggressive biological characteristics of SPP1 + macrophages, we conducted a differential gene expression analysis comparing SPP1 + and SPP1- macrophages. Analysis demonstrated that SPP1 + macrophages strongly express genes involved in chemotaxis (such as CXCL10, CXCL11, and CCL23) as well as those related to proliferation (including TOP2A and MKI67) (Fig. 2H). From a functional perspective, SPP1 + macrophages exhibit substantial activation in pathways governing T cell chemotaxis, migratory processes, and proliferation (Fig. 2I). In summary, SPP1 + macrophages are abundantly present in the tumor microenvironment of HER2 + breast cancer, potentially contributing to adverse patient prognosis through their impact on T cell function.

TNK cell characterization exposing Treg influence on adverse HER2 + breast cancer prognosis

As our preceding findings indicate a strong interplay between SPP1 + macrophages and T cells, we proceeded with a more detailed subcluster annotation of the T/NK cell population. Based on their marker gene expression, 11 unique TNK cell subtypes were ultimately delineated (Fig. 3A-B). Among these, CXCL1 + CD8 + T cells exhibited a significant decrease in tumor tissues. Conversely, GZMK + CD8 + T cells, NK cells, CXCL13 + T cells, T cells expressing high levels of ribosomal genes, MUCL1 + T cells, and Tregs all showed a significant increase within the tumor microenvironment (Fig. 3C-D). Our analysis revealed Tregs as the most substantially upregulated T cell subset within tumor tissues. This observation was subsequently corroborated through mIHC assays performed on our own cohort (Fig. 3E). Across both the TCGA-BRCA and GSE20685 cohorts, elevated Treg infiltration likewise corresponded to a worse prognosis (Fig. 3F). To summarize, we successfully deciphered the TNK cell landscape within HER2 + breast cancer, revealing Tregs as the most prominently elevated subset in tumor tissues. This increased presence is strongly linked to adverse patient outcomes.

Intimate interplay between SPP1 + macrophages and Tregs in HER2 + breast cancer

Considering the substantial upregulation of both SPP1 + macrophages and Tregs within tumor tissues, coupled with the diverse roles of SPP1 + macrophages in modulating T cell biology, we posit a potential crosstalk between these two cell types. This interaction, we hypothesize, ultimately fuels the malignant phenotype of HER2 + breast cancer. A significant positive correlation was observed between SPP1 + macrophage and Treg infiltration levels at the single-cell level (Fig. 4A), reinforcing the suggestion of their potential interaction. To gain deeper insight into the communication network between these two cell types, the CellChat algorithm was employed for cell communication analysis. Our findings revealed robust interactions between SPP1 + macrophages and a spectrum of T cell subsets, most notably with KLRC + CD8 + T cells and Tregs (Fig. 4B). From a spatial perspective, SPP1 + macrophages and Tregs exhibited notable co-localization (Fig. 4C-D). Furthermore, we conducted mIHC assays on our samples. Based on the quantified infiltration of SPP1 + macrophages, patients were then categorized into high and low SPP1 + macrophage groups. Furthermore, the group characterized by high SPP1 + macrophage infiltration exhibited a significantly greater presence of Tregs when compared to the low SPP1 + macrophage group (Fig. 4D). The significant positive correlation was also observed between SPP1 + macrophage and Treg infiltration levels at mIHC level (Figure S2). Our subsequent efforts concentrated on elucidating the potential mechanisms of interaction between SPP1 + macrophages and Tregs. Our CellChat analysis revealed the highest activation of the CD86 signaling pathway mediating communication between SPP1 + macrophages and Tregs (Fig. 4E). Predominantly found on the surface of antigen-presenting cells, CD86 engages with the CD28 molecule on T cells. This interaction delivers crucial co-stimulatory signals, which are instrumental in both activating T cells and augmenting the overall immune response33. In contrast, CTLA4 is capable of competing with CD28 for CD86 binding. This interaction leads to the delivery of inhibitory signals, ultimately dampening T cell activation34,35. Our analysis revealed a specific upregulation of CTLA4 in Tregs (Fig. 4F). This finding suggests that SPP1 + macrophages might augment the immunosuppressive capacity of Tregs via the CD86-CTLA4 axis, consequently fostering tumor progression. In an effort to validate this hypothesis, we initially polarized THP-1 cells into M1 and M2 phenotypes. A subsequent finding was the significantly elevated SPP1 and CD86 expression evident in M2-type macrophages (Fig. 4G; Figure S2B). Consequently, M2-type macrophages were utilized as a proxy for SPP1 + macrophages, with M1-type macrophages serving as a representative for SPP1- macrophages. Our experiments revealed that co-culturing Tregs with SPP1 + macrophages significantly augmented the secretion of IL-10 and IL-35, two crucial immunosuppressive cytokines synthesized by Tregs36,37. Importantly, this enhancement was abrogated by CTLA-4 blockade (Fig. 4H; Figure S2C). To define the core mechanism of the cellular interaction, we determined that CTLA4 expression on Tregs was not significantly altered by the addition of recombinant SPP1 protein alone (Figure S3D). In summary, our findings suggest that SPP1 + macrophages might potentiate the immunosuppressive activity of Tregs via the CD86-CTLA4 axis, consequently contributing to the progression of HER2 + breast cancer.

High SPP1 + macrophage abundance serves as an indicator of unfavorable patient outcomes and advanced disease states

The clinical utility of SPP1 + macrophages was further verified using data from our proprietary cohort. We conducted mIHC assays on 20 HER2 + breast cancer patients. Based on the quantified infiltration of SPP1 + macrophages, these patients were then categorized into high and low infiltration groups (Fig. 5A-B). Patients with high SPP1 + macrophage infiltration demonstrated significantly diminished overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS) when contrasted with those in the low infiltration group (Fig. 5C-D). For subgroup survival analysis, we categorized patients into two age brackets: individuals under 55 years and those 55 years or older. This analysis consistently demonstrated that elevated SPP1 + macrophage infiltration continued to correlate with an unfavorable prognosis across both age cohorts (Fig. 5E–H). Our final analysis revealed that the infiltration level of SPP1 + macrophages progressively rose with advancing clinical staging (Fig. 5I). To conclude, our in-house patient cohort validated the strong association of SPP1 + macrophages with both an unfavorable prognosis and advanced clinical stages in HER2 + breast cancer.

Discussion

Comprising approximately one-fifth of all breast cancer diagnoses, HER2 + breast cancer is distinguished by its aggressive invasiveness, elevated recurrence rates, and an unfavorable prognosis4,38. Despite the progress made by HER2-targeted therapies and immunotherapy in prolonging survival for HER2 + breast cancer patients, critical obstacles such as drug resistance continue to impede the attainment of long-term patient benefits39,40. It remains imperative to unravel the molecular mechanisms driving the aggressive characteristics of HER2 + breast cancer, alongside pinpointing novel therapeutic targets.

Recognizing the pivotal influence of cell–cell interactions within the tumor microenvironment on malignant progression41–43, we proceeded to characterize the tumor microenvironmental landscape of HER2 + breast cancer using scRNA-seq data from 19 samples. Our analysis successfully pinpointed seven distinct cell populations: epithelial or tumor cells, plasma cells, myeloid cells, CAF, B cells, T/NK cells, and endothelial cells. Within these cell types, both myeloid and T/NK cell populations exhibited a significant increase in proportion within the tumor group. This observation underscores their crucial involvement in the aggressive characteristics of HER2 + breast cancer. Our initial classification of myeloid cell subclusters indicated a striking finding: SPP1 + macrophages were the most substantially increased population in tumor tissue. While SPP1 + macrophages have demonstrated aggressive biological functions in hepatic, prostatic, and colorectal cancers, their involvement in HER2 + breast cancer remains largely unexplored44–46. Our investigation indicates that within HER2 + breast cancer, SPP1 + macrophages display an M2 immunosuppressive phenotype. These cells are strongly linked to adverse patient outcomes and participate in modulating the biological characteristics of various T cells. Driven by this, we delved deeper into the T cell landscape within HER2 + breast cancer. Our analysis ultimately resolved 11 distinct T/NK cell subsets, including Tregs, NK cells, CXCL1 + CD8 + T cells, GZMK + CD8 + T cells, KLRC1 + CD8 + T cells, CXCL13 + T cells, MKI67 + T cells, MUCL1 + T cells, and T cells expressing high ribosomal genes. Within the identified T cell subsets, Tregs exhibited the most pronounced upregulation in tumor tissue, underscoring their potential pro-tumorigenic role in HER2 + breast cancer. Given the wide-ranging regulatory influence of SPP1 + macrophages on T cells, we hypothesize that these macrophages may modulate Treg function, thereby contributing to a pro-tumor effect. Initially, we verified a significant positive correlation between the infiltration levels of SPP1 + macrophages and Tregs in HER2 + breast cancer. Our subsequent cell–cell communication analysis validated the pronounced activation of the CD86 signaling pathway connecting SPP1 + macrophages and Tregs. Concurrently, Tregs distinctly exhibit high expression of CTLA4, which acts as a receptor for CD86. Given that CD86 is widely found on antigen-presenting cells, its interaction with CTLA4 is known to suppress T cell activation47,48. Furthermore, Tregs possess the ability to diminish CD86 expression on antigen-presenting cells via CTLA4, consequently hindering the CD28 co-stimulatory signaling pathway49,50. We found that CTLA4 expression on Treg cells was not significantly altered by the addition of recombinant SPP1 protein alone. The immunosuppressive pathway facilitated by SPP1 + macrophages and Tregs through the CD86-CTLA4 axis, a finding previously undocumented in other breast cancer subtypes, furnishes a strong mechanistic basis for combining anti-CTLA4 and anti-HER2 treatments in HER2 + breast cancer. Ultimately, our internal cohort provided additional validation that SPP1 + macrophages are strongly linked to both unfavorable prognosis and advanced clinical stages in HER2 + breast cancer patients, thereby underscoring the potential clinical advantage of targeting these cells. Approaches currently employed to target SPP1 primarily involve small molecule inhibitors, neutralizing antibodies (e.g., MPIIIB10), and RNA interference methodologies51. The clinical efficacy of these strategies in HER2 + breast cancer patients necessitates validation through meticulously designed clinical trials. Significantly, CellChat analysis demonstrated that SPP1 + macrophages exhibited the most robust interaction intensity with KLRC1 + CD8 + T cells. Given that KLRC1 expresses the NKG2A protein, capable of mediating immunosuppressive signals upon engaging tumor cell-derived HLA-E molecules52, this observation provides additional confirmation of the tumor-promoting phenotype of SPP1 + macrophages.

This study presents several limitations:1 The single-cell data incorporated in our analysis did not consist of strictly matched HER2 + breast cancer tumor tissues and their corresponding normal counterparts, which may introduce some degree of bias into the findings.2 Constrained by the availability of public datasets, we relied on pan-breast cancer bulk transcriptomic cohorts to investigate the association of SPP1 + macrophages, Tregs, and patient prognosis, instead of utilizing bulk transcriptomic datasets specifically from HER2 + breast cancer.3 Our internal validation cohort comprised a comparatively small number of patients; thus, a larger sample size is imperative for a more comprehensive evaluation of SPP1 + macrophages’ clinical significance.

Conclusions

Possessing an M2-like phenotype, SPP1 + macrophages are capable of enhancing the immunosuppressive function of Tregs via the CD86-CTLA4 axis, thus fostering tumor progression. Consequently, therapeutic interventions aimed at SPP1 + macrophages could emerge as a novel strategy for HER2 + breast cancer.

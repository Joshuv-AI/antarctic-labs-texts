Fast broad irregular tachycardia (FBIT): Not always atrial tachyarrhythmias with aberrancy or pre-excitation
Authors: Daljeet Kaur Saggu, Vickram Vignesh Rangaswamy, Anish Reddy Malugari, Muthiah Subramaniam, Sachin Yalagudri, C Narasimhan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundFast broad irregular tachycardia (FBIT) is traditionally attributed to atrial arrhythmias with aberrancy or pre-excitation, and ventricular tachycardia (VT) is not typically considered in differential diagnosis.PurposeWe present a series of FBIT cases that presented with no evidence of pre-excitation or typical atrial arrhythmia in the baseline ECG and were ultimately diagnosed as VT.Methods and resultsThis is a retrospective case series where the data was extracted from myocarditis registry (May 2020 - November 2023) consisting of 615 patients. Out of which 177 patients had ventricular tachycardia and seven patients had FBIT as initial clinical presentation. The cohort comprised of seven patients (mean age 39.85 ± 9.8 years; 3 males) with no known comorbidities There was no evidence of pre-excitation on baseline ECG or VT morphology mimicking baseline ECG. Two-dimensional echocardiography revealed structurally normal heart in all patients. Four out of seven patients underwent catheter ablation for multidrug resistant VT storm and non-clinical VTs were inducible in three patients. Spontaneous onset and termination along with changing morphology of arrhythmias were observed. No abnormal substrate was identified to perform modification, as per bipolar and unipolar voltage mapping during sinus rhythm. There was no evidence of inducible atrial arrhythmias in this subset. All these patients responded to disease specific management.ConclusionHence, in cases presenting with FBIT without evidence of atrial arrhythmias on ECG, a high index of suspicion for VT as a differential diagnosis should be maintained.Keywords: Fast broad irregular tachycardia, Irregularly irregular ventricular tachycardia, Wide QRS tachycardia, Myocardial inflammation, Pre-excited atrial fibrillation

Introduction

An electrocardiogram (ECG) displaying fast broad irregular tachycardia (FBIT) are generally diagnosed as atrial tachyarrhythmias including atrial fibrillation (AF), atrial flutter or atrial tachycardia with variable conduction associated with either aberrancy or antegrade conduction via accessory pathway [1]. Ventricular tachycardia(VT) can be irregular during the onset of tachycardia for initial few beats, slow VT in patients on antiarrhythmic drugs and polymorphic VT is irregular [2]. Pleomorphic VTs can be irregular, but are associated with changing morphology of QRS complexes along with the change in cycle length (CL) [3,4] Otherwise, monomorphic VT(MMVT) has been classically described as a regular wide QRS tachycardia (WQRST) and is not considered in the differential diagnosis algorithms of FBIT [1]. We report a series of 7 cases of FBIT, which were proven to be VT due to myocardial inflammation. Tachycardia CL variation was defined as difference between longest R-R interval and shortest R-R interval of more than 50 ms.

Out of 615 patients in our inflammatory myocarditis registry (CTRI No. CTRI/2020/05/0225279), 177 cases presented with VT in structurally normal heart. Seven (3.95%) out of 177 patients presented with hemodynamically stable FBIT from May 2020 to November 2023 (Flowchart).

The study cohort consisted of seven patients. The mean age was 39.85 ± 9.8 years and three were male (Table 1). As per two-dimensional echocardiography, all patients had structurally normal heart with no comorbidities. None of them had evidence of pre-excitation on baseline ECG or FBIT morphology mimicking baseline ECG. All patients had underlying myocardial inflammation (Cardiac sarcoidosis-5; Cardiac Tuberculosis-1, unspecified inflammatory myocarditis-1) as per our protocol from inflammatory myocarditis registry [5]. Patchy myocardial uptake of 18-fluorodeoxyglucose on cardiac positron emission tomography with computed tomography was present in all these cases. Four out of seven patients underwent catheter ablation for multidrug resistant VT storm. In all these cases, only VT was induced during electrophysiology (EP) study fulfilling standard criteria along with non-clinical VTs inducible in three patients (Table 1). Atrial arrhythmias were not inducible in any of these patients. Spontaneous onset and termination pattern of VT was observed. As per three-dimensional electro anatomical voltage mapping during sinus rhythm, there was no abnormal bipolar or unipolar voltages to consider for substrate-based ablation. Ablation of clinical VT was thus performed using pace mapping or activation mapping. Since, VT ablation was attempted during the active inflammatory phase of myocarditis, four patients had reported recurrence of VT [6]. Immunosuppressive therapy was thus intensified in these cases.

Limitations

The given sample size of seven patients out of 177 (3.95%), lacks statistical power to generalize the findings of this study. We did not systematically evaluate all the FBITs to assess the mechanism of these tachycardias and none of the patients underwent cardiac magnetic resonance imaging. Prevalence of this pattern among patients presenting to the Emergency room or the hospital is unknown. Despite the presence of FBIT at the time of diagnosis a high degree of clinical suspicion towards VT was maintained. Hence it led to better clinical outcomes in these subsets of patients. We believe that early recognition of this entity plays a crucial role in management. Hence, in cases of FBIT when there is no evidence of preexcitation on a resting 12 lead ECG, possibility of VT should be considered. More importantly these patients need to be worked up for underlying Myocarditis. Despite these limitations the case series provides valuable insights necessitating a mandatory evaluation of VT specific criteria before ruling it out.

Discussion

Algorithms for the diagnosis of WQRST do not take into consideration VT as a differential diagnosis of FBIT. [1]. We report a series of treatment naïve seven cases who presented with hemodynamically stable irregularly irregular VT with structurally normal heart. These were young patients (mean age 39.85 ± 9.8 years) with no co-morbidities. All of them had underlying inflammatory myocarditis. Focal triggered activity or automaticity can cause irregularly irregular VT. As described by Volosin et al. [2]. slower the CL of VT more can be the irregularity. But this variation is usually seen in the initial few beats of VT induction in the EP lab and after 30-50 beats, the irregularity tends to diminish. We report a series of spontaneous clinical VTs with persistent irregularities (Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4, Fig. 5, Fig. 6, Fig. 7). These patients presented to the emergency department as cases of FBIT and were initially considered to have atrial tachyarrhythmia with aberrancy or underlying pre-excitation. But, in all these cases on further application of WQRST algorithms either Brugada or Vereckei (except for Vi/Vt ratio) diagnosis of VT was more obvious. For instance, in Fig. 4a:it is a FBIT with LBBB morphology and right axis with evidence of VA dissociation in the rhythm strip of lead II. In Fig. 6a it is a FBIT with indeterminate morphology (rs in V1), right axis, pattern break in V2 and VA dissociation evident in the rhythm strip of lead II. If P waves are clear, presence of VA dissociation in WQRST carries a high specificity in favour of VT [1]. Vi/Vt ratio criteria may not have added benefit in these cases as both VT and pre-excited atrial tachyarrhythmias will have antegrade slow muscle to muscle conduction. Zhang S et al. [7]. reported a case of irregularly irregular incessant VT which was misdiagnosed as AF. Delayed treatment of this incessant VT led to arrhythmia induced cardiomyopathy which was reversed after catheter ablation of the culprit VT. We reported cases of irregularly irregular VT in cardiac sarcoidosis causing inappropriate detection of VT as supraventricular tachycardia because of stability criteria [4]. Incorrect diagnosis of FBIT can lead to inappropriate treatment. Thus, in patients with documented irregularly irregular VT receiving ICD, stability criteria should not be used for supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)-VT discrimination. For single chamber ICD: Onset and morphology criteria can be used and for dual chamber ICDs: all the discriminators using atrial lead electrograms can be utilized for SVT-VT discrimination.

In patients presenting with FBIT, we therefore propose to utilize (Fig. 8): 1) Morphology criteria in terms of monophasic complexes across precordial leads. 2) Absence of RS complexes across all the chest leads and 3) A-V dissociation criteria (if P waves are visible) also, before binning it directly into atrial tachyarrhythmia with aberrancy or pre-excitation.

Conclusion

Although rare, VT can also be considered in the differential diagnosis of FBIT. Clinicians should maintain a high index of suspicion for irregular VT in patients with wide complex irregular tachycardia, especially when typical atrial tachyarrhythmia is not evident. These can be related to underlying myocarditis. Timely diagnosis and appropriate management are essential for improving patient outcomes in such cases.

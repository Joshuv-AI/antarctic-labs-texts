Abstract
. We consider the flow of a generalized Newtonian incompressible heat-conducting fluid in a bounded domain, subject to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for velocity and Dirichlet boundary conditions for temperature. For a fluid whose constitutive equation for the Cauchy stress follows a power law with exponent p p , we prove that, for p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 and for initial data with finite energy, there always exists a global-in-time weak solution that additionally satisfies the entropy equality. The main novelty of this work is that we rigorously establish the continuity of temperature with respect to time, a property not previously proven in this setting. This time continuity allows us to construct a Lyapunov functional designed specifically for the problem, which in turn implies that the steady solution is nonlinearly stable and attracts all suitable weak solutions, even exponentially.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following system of partial differential equations

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}+\operatorname{div}(\boldsymbol{u}\otimes\boldsymbol{u})-\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}+\nabla\pi

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{f},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\vartheta+\operatorname{div}(\vartheta\boldsymbol{u})+\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{q}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}:D\boldsymbol{u},

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta+\operatorname{div}(\eta\boldsymbol{u})+\operatorname{div}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{\vartheta}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}:D\boldsymbol{u}}{\vartheta}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\nabla\vartheta}{\vartheta^{2}},

which is satisfied in
Q:=(0,T)\times\Omega
, where
T>0
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a Lipschitz domain.
Here
\boldsymbol{u}:Q\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the velocity field,

D\boldsymbol{u}:=(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}+(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})^{T})/2
is the symmetric part of the velocity gradient,
\pi:Q\to\mathbb{R}
is the pressure,
\vartheta:Q\to\mathbb{R}
the temperature, and
\eta=\log\vartheta
the entropy. Furthermore,
\boldsymbol{f}:Q\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is given density of the external body forces,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}:Q\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
denotes the viscous part of the Cauchy stress tensor, and
\boldsymbol{q}:Q\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the heat flux. We assume that the fluid is homogeneous and set the density equal to one for simplicity. The system (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) is completed by the initial and
boundary conditions of the form

(1.5)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{0},

\displaystyle\vartheta=\vartheta_{b}

\displaystyle\textrm{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}(0)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{u}_{0},

\displaystyle\vartheta(0)=\vartheta_{0}

\displaystyle\textrm{in $\Omega$}.

The above system describes the planar flow of an incompressible, homogeneous, heat-conducting fluid. Equation (
1.2
) represents the incompressibility constraint, the balance of linear momentum is given by (
1.1
), and the balance of internal energy by (
1.3
), where we assume that the heat capacity is identically equal to one. At first glance, the system might appear to be overdetermined; however, this is not the case, since (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) are formally equivalent, provided that the solution is sufficiently smooth.

We impose homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for the velocity and spatially inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for the temperature. Consequently, no mass passes through the boundary, although thermal interaction with the exterior is allowed.

1.1.
Constitutive relations

We focus on the case where all material parameters may depend on the temperature, and moreover, we assume that the fluid is non-Newtonian. In particular, the viscous part of the Cauchy stress tensor
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}
depends nonlinearly on the symmetric part of the velocity gradient. In this paper, the dependence is characterized by the power-law index
p\geq 2
. This means that we assume that the heat flux is represented by the Fourier law. That is,

\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{q}^{\ast}(\vartheta)
and it holds

(1.6)

\boldsymbol{q}^{\ast}(\vartheta)=-\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta,

where the heat conductivity
\kappa:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
is a continuous function, satisfying for some constants
0<\underline{\kappa}
,
\overline{\kappa}<\infty
,

(1.7)

\underline{\kappa}\leq\kappa\leq\overline{\kappa}.

Concerning the viscous part of the Cauchy stress, we assume that
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{*}(\vartheta,D\boldsymbol{u})
, where
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{*}:(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}_{sym}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}_{sym}^{2\times 2}
is a continuous mapping, and that there exist
0<\underline{\nu}\leq\overline{\nu}<\infty
and
p\geq 2
, such that for all
\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{sym}^{2\times 2}
the following set of conditions holds

(1.8)

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{*}(\vartheta,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{1})-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{*}(\vartheta,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{2})):(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{2})

\displaystyle\geq 0,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{*}(\vartheta,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{1}):\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\underline{\nu}|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{1}|^{p}-\overline{\nu},

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{*}(\vartheta,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{1})|

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\nu}(1+|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{1}|)^{p-1}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{*}(\vartheta,\boldsymbol{{0}})=\boldsymbol{{0}}.

The above assumption is sufficient in the case when we deal with the existence and continuity of the solution. In the case where we also want to show its exponential stability, we additionally require that

(1.9)

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{*}(\vartheta,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}):\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}\geq\underline{\mu}(1+|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}|)^{p-2}|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}|^{2}.

1.2.
Notation

In what follows, we adopt the standard notation for the Lebesgue, Sobolev, and Bochner spaces, equipping them with their usual norms. The symbol
C^{\infty}_{0}
is reserved for smooth, compactly supported functions. The function spaces associated with the incompressible setting are denoted by

W^{1,p}_{0,\operatorname{div}}:=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2});\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}=0\},

and
L^{2}_{0,\operatorname{div}}
denotes its closure in the
L^{2}
topology. For a general Banach space, the duality pairing is denoted by
\left\langle\cdot\;,\cdot\right\rangle_{X}
, while in the case
X=W^{1,p}_{0,\operatorname{div}}
the subscript is omitted for simplicity. For notational convenience, we also use the abbreviation
V:=W^{1,p}_{0,\operatorname{div}}
.

We recall here a few classical inequalities related to the function space setting used in this paper. First, we recall the Korn inequality

(1.10)

C\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{1,q}\leq\|{D\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{q}+\|{\textrm{Tr }\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

which holds true for any Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, see
[
8
, as Lemma 1.11]
. Note that for the space
V
, the trace part of the above inequality vanishes identically.

Second is the classical Lebesgue–Sobolev interpolation inequality

(1.11)

\|z\|_{r}\leq C\|{z}\|_{1,s}^{\frac{2s(q-r)}{r(2q-sq-2s)}}\|{z}\|_{q}^{\frac{q(2r-sr-2s)}{r(2q-sq-2s)}},

which holds true for
r\geq q\geq 1
as follows. If
s<2
, then the inequality is valid for all
r\leq\frac{2s}{2-s}
. If
s>2
, then it holds for all
r\leq\infty
. If
s=2
, then it holds for all
r<\infty
.

1.3.
Assumptions on data

Here we state the assumptions imposed on the data. We begin with
\vartheta_{b}
, for which we require

\vartheta_{b}\in W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\partial\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega).

Note that, by classical elliptic theory and (
1.6
)–(
1.7
), this assumption guarantees the existence of a function
\hat{\vartheta}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(1.12)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{q}^{\ast}(\hat{\vartheta})=0\quad\text{in $\Omega$,}

\displaystyle\hat{\vartheta}=\vartheta_{b}\quad\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$,}

\displaystyle\hat{\vartheta}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,2}(\Omega).

Hence, in what follows we impose assumptions only on
\hat{\vartheta}
and no longer consider
\vartheta_{b}
.

For the momentum equation, we assume that the right-hand side
\boldsymbol{f}
and the initial condition
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}
satisfy the standard requirements:

(1.13)

\boldsymbol{f}\in L^{p^{\prime}}\left(0,T;V^{\ast}\right),\quad\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{0,\operatorname{div}}(\Omega).

Regarding the temperature equation, we assume that the initial and boundary temperatures are bounded and strictly positive, i.e.,

(1.14)

\displaystyle\vartheta_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega),\quad\hat{\vartheta}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega),

(1.15)

\displaystyle\mu:=\min\left\{\mathop{\operatorname{ess\,inf}}\limits_{x\in\Omega}\hat{\vartheta}(x),\mathop{\operatorname{ess\,inf}}\limits_{x\in\Omega}\vartheta_{0}(x)\right\}>0.

Foundational works by Consiglieri
[
10
,
11
]
studied both stationary and time-dependent models of shear-thickening fluids with temperature-dependent viscosity and thermal conductivity. While these studies provided rigorous existence results, they did not address the entropy equality, the long-time behaviour, or the continuity of the temperature with respect to time. More complex existence theory for heat-conducting non-Newtonian fluids was later established in
[
6
,
9
]
, however, without any ambition for establishing further properties such as time continuity or the validity of energy and/or entropy equality.

From a thermodynamic perspective, Dafermos
[
12
]
introduced the use of entropy as a Lyapunov functional, establishing a link between the Second Law of Thermodynamics and nonlinear stability of fluid systems, see also
[
7
]
.
More recently, Abbatiello et al.
[
1
]
proved the existence of weak solutions to the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes–Fourier system with non-Newtonian power-law behavior (for
p\geq\frac{11}{5}
) that satisfy the entropy equality. This approach notably avoids the classical DiPerna–Lions renormalization framework
[
13
]
, by constructing solutions directly fulfilling the entropy equality, even when
p\leq\frac{5}{2}
. Building upon this, the follow-up work
[
2
]
established nonlinear stability of such entropy-equality solutions by constructing a Lyapunov functional and demonstrating exponential decay to a steady state, even under inhomogeneous boundary temperatures; see also
[
14
]
for similar but conditional results.

Considering only the theory for nonlinear heat equations with irregular data (purely integrable or even measure-valued), we overcome the limitations of traditional renormalization approaches. For instance,
[
17
]
addressed entropy solutions that are continuous in time, but relied on entropy-like inequalities with multiple cut-off functions, without clearly establishing a renormalized framework or coupling with other equations. Classical existence results for the heat equation with measure data were established by
[
4
]
, while
[
16
]
introduced renormalized solutions requiring multiple renormalizations in different function spaces. The analysis in
[
5
]
extended the existence theory to cases where even the convective term cannot be handled by standard methods, and
[
3
]
addressed more complex systems with dissipative heating, albeit without proving continuity with respect to time.

The present article contributes to this line of research by focusing on a two-dimensional non-Newtonian heat-conducting fluid and establishing the existence of entropy-equality solutions. Our main novelty lies in the regularity with respect to time: we show that the temperature is continuous in time, which allows us to avoid classical renormalization procedures altogether. Remarkably, in most works on heat-conducting fluids, such issues are usually omitted, and even the attainment of the initial condition in the strong topology is typically not guaranteed. In this paper, all the above-mentioned drawbacks are resolved, and the validity of the entropy equation appears to be the key step allowing us to avoid the classical renormalized solution notions while still proving the desired strong results.

2.
Proof of the existence theorem

In the first four sections, following the methods of
[
6
]
, we prove the existence of a weak solution satisfying (
1.22
) and (
1.23
). The proof requires modification due to the different character of the boundary conditions. We then show that the initial condition for the temperature is attained on a set of full measure.

Next, we prove that the solution satisfies (
1.24
), adopting the methods from
[
1
]
. Finally, we show that the temperature
\vartheta
is continuous in

with respect to time, and hence the initial condition is attained in the sense of Theorem

2.1.
Galerkin approximations

We now define the Galerkin approximation. First, we denote

W^{2,2}_{0,\operatorname{div}}(\Omega):=\overline{\{\boldsymbol{u}\in(C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega))^{2};\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{u}=0\}}^{\|\cdot\|_{2,2}}.

Then, following
[
15
, Appendix, Theorem 4.11]
, we obtain the existence of a basis
\{\boldsymbol{w}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
that is orthogonal in
W^{2,2}_{0,\operatorname{div}}(\Omega)
and orthonormal in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Note that
\|{\boldsymbol{w}_{j}}\|_{1,q}
is bounded for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
q\in[1,\infty)
.
Similarly, we can find a sequence
\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
forming an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
, which is orthogonal in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Then, for fixed
N,M\in\mathbb{N}
, we find

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}^{N,M}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}^{N,M}(t)\boldsymbol{w}_{i}(x),

\displaystyle\vartheta^{N,M}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{M}d_{i}^{N,M}(t)w_{i}(x)\right)+\hat{\vartheta}(x),

such that
(\boldsymbol{c}^{N,M},\boldsymbol{d}^{N,M}):=(c_{1}^{N,M},\dots,c_{N}^{N,M},d_{1}^{N,M},\dots,d_{M}^{N,M}):(0,T^{\ast})\to\mathbb{R}^{N+M}
, for

, is the maximal solution to the following system of ordinary differential equations

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{N,M}\boldsymbol{w}_{j}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}\left(\vartheta^{N,M},D\boldsymbol{u}^{N,M}\right):D\boldsymbol{w}_{j}\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{u}^{N,M}\otimes\boldsymbol{u}^{N,M}):\nabla(\boldsymbol{w}_{j})\,\mathrm{d}x+\left\langle\boldsymbol{f}\;,\boldsymbol{w}_{j}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\vartheta^{N,M}w_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta^{N,M})\nabla\vartheta^{N,M}\cdot(\nabla w_{k})\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}\left(\vartheta^{N,M},D\boldsymbol{u}^{N,M}\right):D\boldsymbol{u}^{N,M}w_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{N,M}\boldsymbol{u}^{N,M}\cdot(\nabla w_{k})\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and
k\in\{1,\dots,M\}
.

We equip the above system of ordinary differential equations (
2.1
) with the following initial conditions:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}^{N,M}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{u}_{0}^{N}(x)

\displaystyle\textrm{on $\Omega$},

\displaystyle\vartheta^{N,M}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\vartheta_{0}^{N,M}(x)

\displaystyle\textrm{on $\Omega$},

where
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}^{N}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i,0}^{N}\boldsymbol{w}_{i}(x)
is the projection of
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}
onto the linear hull of
\{\boldsymbol{w}_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N}
in

. The approximative initial conditions for the temperature

is defined following manner. We define

and then define

as a projection of

onto the linear hull of

, hence

. Note, that

\displaystyle\vartheta_{0}^{N,M}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{$M\to\infty$}}\vartheta_{0}^{N}

\displaystyle\textrm{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{0}^{N}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{$N\to\infty$}}\boldsymbol{u}_{0}

\displaystyle\textrm{in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})$,}

\displaystyle\vartheta_{0}^{N}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{$N\to\infty$}}\vartheta_{0}

\displaystyle\textrm{in $L^{1}(\Omega)$},

where the first two convergence results hold due to the completeness of an orthonormal basis in Hilbert spaces.

By Carathéodory’s existence theory, it is not difficult to obtain a maximal solution to the system (

2.2.

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|(\vartheta^{N,M}-\hat{\vartheta})(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|{\vartheta^{N,M}-\hat{\vartheta}}\|_{1,2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C(N).

2.5.
Initial condition for temperature

We want to show that the initial condition for the temperature is attained at least in some strong sense. Notice that (
2.60
) already indicates a kind of weak attainment (without specifying it rigorously). Here, our goal is different; more specifically, we show the strong attainment in

, but only for the essential limit as

. In the next section, when we show that the temperature is continuous, we prove the attainment of the initial condition for the classical limit
t\to 0_{+}
. Hence, our goal here is to show the existence of a set
S\subset(0,T)
such that

is of measure zero and

\displaystyle\vartheta(t)\to\vartheta_{0}

in

We note that by (
2.46

\sqrt{\theta(t)}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
for all

converges to
\vartheta
almost everywhere on
(0,T)
. Hence, there exists a set
S\subset(0,T)
, such that

is of measure zero and for all
t\in S

\displaystyle\vartheta^{N}(t)\to\vartheta(t)

in

Let us now show that

\displaystyle\liminf_{S\in t\to 0+}\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\vartheta(t)}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\text{ for all $\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)$, $\varphi\geq 0$}.

We set
\psi:=\frac{\varphi}{\sqrt{\vartheta^{N}}}
in equation (
2.19
), where

, and integrate over the time interval
(0,t)
, with

arbitrary. Note that such
\psi
is an admissible test function for (
2.19
), since

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{N}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{N}\frac{\varphi}{\sqrt{\vartheta^{N}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{N}\boldsymbol{u}^{N}\cdot\nabla\frac{\varphi}{\sqrt{\vartheta^{N}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

We can now let

in the inequality (

), (
2.44
), (
2.42
) and (

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\vartheta(t)}\varphi-\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\sqrt{\vartheta}}\nabla\vartheta-2{\sqrt{\vartheta}}\boldsymbol{u}\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau\geq 0.

We can pass to the essential limit inferior
S\ni t\to 0_{+}
in (

\displaystyle\liminf_{S\in t\to 0_{+}}\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\vartheta(t)}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\vartheta_{0}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x

Next, our goal is to obtain the following result

2.19

We thus obtain

(2.75)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\vartheta(t)-\frac{2(\vartheta(t))^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}(\hat{\vartheta})^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}}{1-\alpha}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\vartheta_{0}-\frac{2(\vartheta_{0})^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}(\hat{\vartheta})^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}}{1-\alpha}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}:D\boldsymbol{u}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha+1}{1-\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\vartheta)(\hat{\vartheta})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\nabla\hat{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla(\vartheta)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{\hat{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Let us take the limit
S\ni t\to 0_{+}
in (
2.75
). Since all the terms on the right-hand side of (
2.75
) except for the first one converge to zero, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{S\ni t\to 0_{+}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\vartheta(t)-\frac{2(\vartheta(t))^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}(\hat{\vartheta})^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}}{1-\alpha}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\vartheta_{0}-\frac{2(\vartheta_{0})^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}(\hat{\vartheta})^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}}{1-\alpha}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Using (
2.73
) with
\beta:=\frac{1-\alpha}{2}
, it follows from the above inequality that

\displaystyle\limsup_{S\ni t\to 0_{+}}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\vartheta_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x,

which is precisely the relation (
2.72
) we aimed to prove. Consequently, we also deduce the strong attainment of the initial condition (
2.73
).

2.6.
Entropy equality

Proving that the solution found in the previous sections fulfills the equality (
1.24
) can follow the approach of
[
1
]
, i.e., we set
\psi:=\frac{\varphi}{\vartheta^{N}}
, where
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((-\infty,T)\times\Omega)
, in (
2.19
) and integrate the resulting expression over
(0,T)
to get

(2.76)

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\kappa(\vartheta^{N})\nabla\eta^{N}\cdot\nabla\varphi-\eta^{N}\partial_{t}\varphi-\eta^{N}\boldsymbol{u}^{N}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{Q}\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{N}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{N}\varphi}{\vartheta^{N}}+\kappa(\vartheta^{N})\frac{|\nabla\vartheta^{N}|^{2}}{(\vartheta^{N})^{2}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\Omega}\eta^{N}_{0}\varphi(0)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
\eta^{N}_{0}:=\log\vartheta^{N}_{0}
and
\eta^{N}:=\log\vartheta^{N}
. Next, we show the convergence as
N\to\infty
. In what follows, we only highlight the key convergence results that are used in the next section and refer the reader to the original paper for a detailed proof. Note that to prove the entropy equality, we need the convergence of
\vartheta
to the initial condition in the sense of (
2.62
).

By (
2.45
), for
N\to\infty
, we have

(2.77)

\displaystyle\eta^{N}\to\eta

\displaystyle\text{in $L^{q}(Q)$ for $q\in\left[1,\infty\right)$},

where
\eta:=\log\vartheta
.
Moreover,

\nabla\eta^{N}=\frac{\nabla\vartheta^{N}}{\vartheta^{N}}\in L^{2}(Q)
by (
2.30
). We can thus find a converging subsequence

(2.78)

\displaystyle\nabla\eta^{N}\rightharpoonup\nabla\eta

\displaystyle\text{in $L^{2}(Q)$},

and so
\eta\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2})\cap L^{q}(Q)
for
q\in\left[1,\infty\right)
.
Following
[
1
, Subsection 3.(b)(i)]
,
we have

(2.79)

\displaystyle\nabla\vartheta^{N}\to\nabla\vartheta

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{1}(Q)

(2.80)

\displaystyle\nabla\vartheta^{N}\to\nabla\vartheta

\displaystyle\text{ a.e. in }Q.

Moreover, from
[
1
, Subsection 3.(b)(ii)]
,
we know

(2.81)

\displaystyle\kappa(\vartheta^{N})\frac{|\nabla\vartheta^{N}|^{2}}{(\vartheta^{N})^{2}}\to{\kappa(\vartheta)}\frac{|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}

in
L^{1}(Q)
.

The convergence results (
2.77
)–(
2.81
) are enough to pass to the limit in (
2.76
).
Consequently,
\eta\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2})\cap L^{q}(Q),q\in\left[1,\infty\right)
satisfies
Entropy equation
(
1.24
).

2.7.
Continuity

This section is the final part of the proof. It relies on the approach developed in
[
1
]
, but here we are able to show a stronger result, namely the continuity with respect to time. Our main goal is to show that the sequence
\vartheta^{N}
is Cauchy in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
, and therefore the limit shares this property. The proof is split into two parts. First, we establish this property for the truncated functions, and second, we prove a kind of uniform equiintegrability.

For any
m>0
we define

(2.82)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{m}:\mathbb{R}\to[-m,m],

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{m}(z):=\text{sign}(z)\min\{|z|,m\}.

For
\delta\in(0,m)
, let
\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
denote the mollification of
\mathcal{T}_{m}
, which is given by convolution with a regularization kernel
\omega_{\epsilon}
. For
\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}
it holds

(2.83)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(z)=\mathcal{T}_{m}(z)

\displaystyle\text{if }|z|\leq m-\varepsilon\text{ or }|z|\geq m+\varepsilon,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}^{\prime\prime}|\leq\frac{C}{\delta}

\displaystyle\text{on }\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}^{\prime}\leq 1,\quad\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}^{\prime\prime}\leq 0,\quad\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}\leq\mathcal{T}_{m}

\displaystyle\text{on }(0,\infty).

Our first goal is to show that
\{\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})\}_{N}
is Cauchy in
C([0,T];L^{2})
for any fixed
m>\|{\hat{\vartheta}}\|_{\infty}
and
\delta
. This means that for any given
\alpha>0
, there exists
N_{0}
such that for all
N,M>N_{0}
it holds

(2.84)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N}(t))-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq\alpha.

We set
\psi^{N}:=\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})(\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}))
,
\psi^{M}:=\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})(\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}))
in (
2.19
) for
N
,
M\in\mathbb{N}
, respectively. Subtracting one equation from the other and integrating the result over time interval
(0,t)
with arbitrary
t\in[0,T]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N}(t))-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}(t))}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N}_{0})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}_{0})}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{N}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{N}-\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{M}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{M})(\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})\vartheta^{N}\boldsymbol{u}^{N}-\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})\vartheta^{M}\boldsymbol{u}^{M})\cdot\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\kappa(\vartheta^{N})\nabla\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\kappa(\vartheta^{M})\nabla\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}))\cdot\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

This implies (using the simple estimate
|\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})\vartheta^{N}|\leq m+\delta
and the assumption (
1.7
))

(2.85)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|{\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N}(t))-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}(t))}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2m\|{\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N}_{0})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}_{0})}\|_{1}

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{N}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{N}}\right|+\left|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{M}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{M}\right|\right)\left|\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+4Cm\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(|\boldsymbol{u}^{N}|+|\boldsymbol{u}^{M}|+|\nabla\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})|+|\nabla\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})|\right)\left|\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}))\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Next, we estimate the right-hand side of (
2.85
). By (
2.3
), we have

(2.86)

2m\|{\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N}_{0})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}_{0})}\|_{1}<\frac{\alpha}{3}

for
M
,
N
large enough.
To estimate the second term, we can split

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})\right|=\left|\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta)\right|+\left|\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta)-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})\right|.

We know that for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the function
{\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta)-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(Q)
by (
2.83
), and that it converges almost everywhere to zero by the continuity of
\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}
and the convergence (
2.44
).
Furthermore, by (
2.55
),
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{N}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{N}
is uniformly integrable and bounded in
L^{1}(Q)
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Using Egorov’s theorem, we can conclude that

(2.87)

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{N}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{N}}\right|+\left|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{M}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{M}\right|\right)\left|\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{\alpha}{3}

for
M,N
large enough.
For the third term, we use that

|\boldsymbol{u}^{N}|+|\boldsymbol{u}^{M}|+|\nabla\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})|+|\nabla\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})|

is bounded in
L^{2}(Q)
, and

\left|\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}))\right|\leq\left|\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta))\right|+\left|\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta)-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}))\right|,

where each term converges strongly to zero in
L^{2}(Q)
by (
2.81
) and (
2.44
).
For
M,N
large enough, we can conclude that

(2.88)

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(|\vartheta^{N}\boldsymbol{u}^{N}|+|\vartheta^{M}\boldsymbol{u}^{M}|+\overline{\kappa}|\nabla\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})|+\overline{\kappa}|\nabla\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})|\right)\left|\nabla(\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}))\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t<\frac{\alpha}{3}.

The sequence
\{\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})\}_{N}
is therefore Cauchy in
C([0,T];L^{2})
, since by (
2.85
)–(
2.88
) we have

(2.89)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|{\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N}(t))-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M}(t))}\|_{2}^{2}<\alpha,

for all
M,N
sufficiently large.

Next, we recall
m>\|{\hat{\vartheta}}\|_{\infty}
, consider equation (
2.19
) with
\psi:=\frac{(\vartheta^{N}-m)_{+}}{\vartheta^{N}}
, and integrate over time interval
(0,t)
. Note that
\psi
has zero trace due to the choice of
m
. This gives us

(2.90)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\vartheta^{N}\left(1-\frac{m}{\vartheta^{N}}\right)\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{m\kappa(\vartheta^{N})}{(\vartheta^{N})^{2}}|\nabla\vartheta^{N}|^{2}\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau=

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{N}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{N}\left(1-\frac{m}{\vartheta^{N}}\right)\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{m}{\vartheta^{N}}\boldsymbol{u}^{N}\cdot\nabla\vartheta^{N}\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

We can rewrite

\partial_{t}\vartheta^{N}\left(1-\frac{m}{\vartheta^{N}}\right)\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}=\partial_{t}((\vartheta^{N}-m\log\vartheta^{N}-m+m\log m)_{+}).

Furthermore, the second term on the left-hand side is positive, so we can omit it from (
2.90
) and replace the equality sign with an inequality. Using integration by parts we can get rid of the last term on the right-hand side since
\operatorname{div}\vartheta^{N}=0

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{m}{\vartheta^{N}}\boldsymbol{u}^{N}\cdot\nabla\vartheta^{N}\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}^{N}\cdot\nabla\left(m\log(\vartheta^{N})\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau=0.

Lastly, it holds

0\leq\left(1-\frac{m}{\vartheta^{N}}\right)\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}\leq\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}},

so we can rewrite (
2.90
) as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\|{(\vartheta^{N}-m\log\vartheta^{N}-m+m\log m)_{+}}\|_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{N}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{N}\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Since
t\in(0,T)
was chosen arbitrarily, we have

(2.91)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|{(\vartheta^{N}(t)-m\log\vartheta^{N}(t)-m+m\log m)_{+}}\|_{1}\leq\|{(\vartheta^{N}_{0}-m\log\vartheta^{N}_{0}-m+m\log m)_{+}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{N}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{N}\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\|{\vartheta^{N}_{0}\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}}\|_{1}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{N}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{N}\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Since
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{N}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{N}
converges weakly to
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}:D\boldsymbol{u}
in
L^{1}(Q)
by (
2.55
), it forms a uniformly integrable sequence of functions. Hence, by (
2.3
) and (
2.45
), we deduce that
|\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}|\leq C/m
. Consequently, for any
\alpha>0
, there exists
m_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|{\vartheta^{N}_{0}\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}_{0}>m\}}}\|_{1}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{N}:D\boldsymbol{u}^{N}\chi_{\{\vartheta^{N}>m\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t<\alpha

for all
m>m_{0}
.
For any
S
positive, we can estimate
\frac{(S-m)_{+}}{2}\leq(S-m\log S-m+m\log m)_{+}
. We can hence conclude that for any
\alpha>0
there exists
m_{0}>0
such that

(2.92)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|{(\vartheta^{N}(t)-2m)_{+}}\|_{1}<\alpha

for all
m>m_{0}
.

Finally, we show that the sequence
\{\vartheta^{N}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C([0,T];L^{1})
. Note that each element of the sequence belongs to
C([0,T];L^{1})\subset C([0,T];L^{2})
by (
2.20
).
For any any
t\in(0,T)
we can estimate

(2.93)

\displaystyle\|{\vartheta^{N}(t)-\vartheta^{M}(t)}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\vartheta^{N}(t)-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})(t)}\|_{1}+\|{\vartheta^{M}(t)-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})(t)}\|_{1}+\|{\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})(t)-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})(t)}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|{(\vartheta^{N}(t)-m)_{+}}\|_{1}+\|{(\vartheta^{M}(t)-m)_{+}}\|_{1}+\|{\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})(t)-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})(t)}\|_{1}

Hence, for a given
\varepsilon>0
, we can use (
2.92
) to find
m
sufficiently large such that for all
M,N

(2.94)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|{(\vartheta^{N}(t)-m)_{+}}\|_{1}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3},

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|{(\vartheta^{M}(t)-m)_{+}}\|_{1}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Finally, for this choice of
m
, we set
\delta=1
and find
N_{0}
such that for all
M,N>N_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{N})(t)-\mathcal{T}_{m,\delta}(\vartheta^{M})(t)\|_{1}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3},

by virtue of (
2.89
). We conclude that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N_{0}
such that for all
M,N>N_{0}
it holds

(2.95)

\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|{\vartheta^{N}(t)-\vartheta^{M}(t)}\|_{1}<\varepsilon.

Hence, the sequence
\{\vartheta^{N}\}_{N=0}^{\infty}
is Cauchy in
C([0,T];L^{1})
, and its limit
\vartheta
indeed belongs to
C([0,T];L^{1})
.

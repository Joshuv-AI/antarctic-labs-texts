Generalization Bound of Gradient Flow through Training Trajectory and Data-dependent Kernel
Yilan Chen, Zhichao Wang, Wei Huang, Andi Han, Taiji Suzuki, Arya Mazumdar

Abstract
Gradient-based optimization methods have shown remarkable empirical success, yet their theoretical generalization properties remain only partially understood. In this paper, we establish a generalization bound for gradient flow that aligns with the classical Rademacher complexity bounds for kernel methods-specifically those based on the RKHS norm and kernel trace-through a data-dependent kernel called the loss path kernel (LPK). Unlike static kernels such as NTK, the LPK captures the entire training trajectory, adapting to both data and optimization dynamics, leading to tighter and more informative generalization guarantees. Moreover, the bound highlights how the norm of the training loss gradients along the optimization trajectory influences the final generalization performance. The key technical ingredients in our proof combine stability analysis of gradient flow with uniform convergence via Rademacher complexity. Our bound recovers existing kernel regression bounds for overparameterized neural networks and shows the feature learning capability of neural networks compared to kernel methods. Numerical experiments on real-world datasets validate that our bounds correlate well with the true generalization gap.

Introduction

Gradient-based optimization lies at the heart of modern deep learning, yet the theoretical understanding of why these methods generalize so well is still incomplete. Classical bounds attribute the generalization of machine learning (ML) models to the complexity of the hypothesis class
[
62
]
, which fails to explain the power of deep neural networks (NNs) with billions of parameters
[
31
,
14
]
. Recent studies reveal that the training algorithm, data distribution, and network architecture together impose an implicit inductive bias, effectively restricting the vast parameter space to a much smaller “effective region” that improves the generalization ability
[
33
,
48
,
60
,
28
,
59
,
21
]
. This observation motivates the need for
algorithm-dependent
generalization bounds—ones that capture how gradient-based dynamics carve out the truly relevant portion of the hypothesis class during training.

A variety of theoretical frameworks have been proposed to address this challenge. Algorithmic stability
[
16
]
bounds the generalization error by the stability of the learning algorithm.
Hardt et al. 2016
first proved the stability of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for both convex and non-convex functions.
However, these bounds are often data-independent, require decaying step sizes for non-convex objectives, and grow linearly with training time.
Moreover, for non-convex functions, full-batch gradient descent (GD) is typically considered not uniformly stable
[
29
,
18
]
.

Information-theoretic (IT) approaches
[
58
,
66
,
30
]
bound the expected generalization error with the mutual information between training data and learned parameters. To control this, researchers introduce noise into the learning process, employing techniques like stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD)
[
57
,
46
,
64
]
or perturb parameters
[
47
]
.
The PAC-Bayesian framework
[
39
,
26
]
bounds the expected generalization error by the KL divergence between the model’s posterior and prior distributions.
To establish algorithm-dependent bounds, they also consider gradient descent with continuous noise like SGLD
[
45
,
36
]
, similar to the IT approach.
But these noise-based approaches can diverge from SGD and their bounds can grow large when the noise variance is small.

In this work, we propose a novel perspective that combines
stability analysis of gradient flow
with
uniform convergence
tools grounded in Rademacher complexity.
Specifically, we utilize a connection between loss dynamics and loss path kernel (LPK) proposed by
Chen et al. 2023
. By studying the stability of gradient flow, we show the concentration of LPKs trained with different datasets. This allows us to construct a function class explored by gradient flow with high probability while being substantially smaller than the full function class, leading to a tighter generalization bound.
We summarize our main contributions as follows.

•

We prove
O(1/n)
stability for gradient flow on convex, strongly-convex, and non-convex losses, where
n
is the number of training samples, and show that, as a result, LPKs concentrate tightly. This localization dramatically shrinks the effective hypothesis class and leads to a tighter bound than the previous result of
Chen et al. 2023
.

•

Using the above results, we derive a generalization bound for gradient flow that parallels classical Rademacher complexity bounds for kernel methods—specifically those involving the RKHS norm and kernel trace
[
10
]
—but adapts to the actual training trajectory.
The generalization gap is controlled by an explicit term
\Gamma
, determined by the norm of the training loss gradients along the optimization trajectory. A similar bound is also proved for stochastic gradient flow.

•

Our bound recovers known results in the NTK regime and kernel ridge regression, and exposes the feature-learning advantage of NNs. Extensive experiments on real-world datasets show that our bound
\Gamma
correlates tightly with the true generalization gap (Fig.

Related Work

Generalization theory in deep learning.

Generalization has long been a central theme in deep learning theory, and various techniques have been proposed to study it. Beyond the algorithmic stability, PAC-Bayesian, and IT frameworks discussed earlier,
Bartlett et al. 2019
obtained tight bounds for the VC dimension of ReLU networks.
Other works measure network capacity via norms, margins
[
10
,
49
,
11
,
52
]
, or sharpness-based metrics
[
50
,
51
,
4
]
to explain why deep NNs can generalize despite their large parameter counts.

Algorithm-dependent generalization bound.

PAC-Bayesian, stability-based, and IT approaches can all yield algorithm-dependent bounds.
Li et al. 2019
combine PAC-Bayesian theory with algorithmic stability to derive an expected bound for SGLD that depends on the expected norm of the training loss gradient along the trajectory, which is similar to our bound, yet the bound blows up as the injected noise vanishes.

Neu et al. 2021
use mutual-information arguments to control the expected gap by the local gradient variance.

Nikolakakis et al. 2022
analyze the expected output stability to get an expected generalization bound for full-batch GD on smooth loss that depends on the training loss gradient norm along the trajectory and the expected optimization error. In contrast, our result is a
high-probability
uniform-convergence bound whose leading term is not only tighter but is also straightforward to compute.

Amir et al. 2022
study generalization bounds for linear models trained by gradient descent on convex losses by constructing a function class centered around the expected trajectory, whereas our approach handles more general losses and models.

Neural tangent kernel (NTK) and feature learning.
There is a line of work showing that overparameterized NNs trained by GD converge to a global minimum and the trained parameters are close to their initialization
[
32
,
25
,
24
,
2
,
6
]
— so-called NTK regime.

Arora et al. 2019a
study the generalization capacity of ultra-wide, two-layer NNs trained by GD and square loss, while
Cao & Gu 2019
examine the generalization of deep, ultra-wide NNs trained by SGD and logistic loss. Both establish generalization bounds of trained NNs, but NNs perform like a fixed kernel machine in this case.
Going beyond the NTK regime, recent works
[
7
,
15
,
27
,
9
,
23
,
43
]
have explored feature learning of NNs trained by GD for efficiently learning low-dimensional features which outperform the fixed kernel. Our approach is considerably more general and not restricted to overparameterized NNs. We present our bound in these two regimes as case studies in Sec.
6
.

Notation and Preliminaries

Consider a supervised learning problem where the task is to predict an output variable in
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
using a vector of input variables in
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\mathcal{Z}\triangleq\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
. Denote the training set by
\mathcal{\mathcal{S}}\triangleq\left\{\bm{\bm{z}}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
with
\bm{z}_{i}\triangleq(\bm{x}_{i},\bm{y}_{i})\in\mathcal{Z}
. Assume each point is drawn i.i.d. from a distribution
\mu
. Let
\mathbf{X}=[\bm{x}_{1},\cdots,\bm{x}_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
,
\mathbf{Y}=[\bm{y}_{1},\cdots,\bm{y}_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}
, and
\mathbf{Z}=[\mathbf{X};\mathbf{Y}]\in\mathbb{R}^{(d+k)\times n}
.

We express a NN as
f(\bm{w},\bm{x}):\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
, where
\bm{w}
are its trainable parameters and
\bm{x}
is an input data. A learning algorithm
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{Z}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{p}
takes a training set
\mathcal{S}
and returns trained parameters
\bm{w}
. The ultimate goal is to minimize the population risk
L_{\mu}(\bm{w})\triangleq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{z}\sim\mu}\left[\ell(\bm{w},\bm{z})\right]
where
\ell(\bm{w},\bm{z})\triangleq\ell(f(\bm{w},\bm{x}),\bm{y})
is a loss function. We assume
\ell(\bm{w},\bm{z})\in[0,1]
. In practice, since the distribution
\mu
is unknown, we instead minimize the empirical risk on the training set
\mathcal{S}
:
L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w})\triangleq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell(\bm{w},\bm{z}_{i})
. The
generalization gap
is defined as
L_{\mu}(\bm{w})-L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w})
.

Below, we recall the definition of Rademacher complexity and a generalization upper bound.

Definition 3.1

(Empirical Rademacher complexity
\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G})
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a hypothesis class of functions from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Let
\mathcal{G}
be the set of loss functions associated with functions in
\mathcal{F}
, defined by
\mathcal{G}=\left\{g:(\bm{x},\bm{y})\rightarrow\ell(f(\bm{x}),\bm{y}),f\in\mathcal{F}\right\}
. The empirical Rademacher complexity of
\mathcal{G}
with respect to sample
\mathcal{S}=\{\bm{z}_{1},\ldots,\bm{z}_{n}\}
is defined as
\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G})=\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}g(\bm{z}_{i})\right]
, where
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
is a sample of independent uniform random variables taking values in
\left\{+1,-1\right\}
, and
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}
is the expectation over
\bm{\sigma}
conditioned on all other random variables.

Theorem 3.2

(Theorem 3.3 in
[
41
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a family of functions mapping from
\mathcal{Z}
to
\left[0,1\right]
. Then for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta
over the draw of an i.i.d. sample set
\mathcal{S}=\left\{\bm{z}_{1},\dots,\bm{z}_{n}\right\}
, the following holds for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
:
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{z}}\left[g(\bm{z})\right]-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(\bm{z}_{i})\leq 2\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G})+3\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{2n}}.

3.1
Kernel Method and Loss Path Kernel

Recall that a kernel is a function
K:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for which there exists a mapping
\Phi:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
into a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS)
\mathcal{H}
such that

K(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})=\left\langle\Phi(\bm{x}),\Phi(\bm{x}^{\prime})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X},

where
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
denotes the inner product in
\mathcal{H}
. A function
K
is a kernel if and only if it is symmetric and positive definite (Chapter 4 in
[
61
]
).
A kernel machine
g:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a linear function in
\mathcal{H}
,and can be written as
g(\bm{x})=\left\langle\bm{\beta},\Phi(\bm{x})\right\rangle+b
, where its weight vector
\bm{\beta}
is a linear combination of the training points
\bm{\beta}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\Phi(\bm{x}_{i})
and
b
is a constant bias. The RKHS norm of
g
is
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}=\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\Phi(\bm{x}_{i})\right\rVert=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j}a_{i}a_{j}K(\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{j})}
.
The kernel machine with bounded RKHS norm has a classic Rademacher complexity bound as follows:

Lemma 3.3

(Lemma 22 in
[
10
]
)
.

Denote a function class
\mathcal{F}=\{g(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}K(\bm{x},\bm{x}_{i}):n\in\mathbb{N},\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{X},\sum_{i,j}a_{i}a_{j}K(\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{j})\leq B^{2}\}

for a constant
B>0
. Then its Rademacher complexity is bounded by
\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{F})\leq\frac{B}{n}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}K(\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})}
.

Next we introduce the loss path kernel, which calculates the inner product of loss gradients and integrates along a given parameter path governed by (stochastic) gradient flows. Previous NTK theory cannot fully capture the training dynamics of NNs since trained parameters could move far away from initialization. The loss path kernel addresses this limitation by capturing the entire training trajectory.

Definition 3.4

(Loss Path Kernel (LPK)
\mathsf{K}_{T}
in
[
20
]
)
.

Suppose the weights follow a continuous path
\bm{w}(t):[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
in their domain with a starting point
\bm{w}(0)=\bm{w}_{0}
, where
T
is a predetermined constant. This path is determined by the learning algorithm
\mathcal{A}
, the training set
\mathcal{S}
, and the training time
T
. We define the loss path kernel associated with the loss function
\ell(\bm{w},\bm{z})
along the path as

\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S})\triangleq\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}(t),\bm{z}),\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}(t),\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rangle\textrm{d}t.

LPK is a valid kernel by definition.
Intuitively, it measures the similarity between data points
\bm{z}
and
\bm{z}^{\prime}
by comparing their loss gradients and accumulating over the training trajectory.

3.2
Loss Dynamics of Gradient Flow (GF) and Its Equivalence with Kernel Machine

Consider the GF dynamics (gradient descent with infinitesimal step size):

\frac{\textrm{d}\bm{w}(t)}{\textrm{d}t}=-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{S}(\bm{w}(t))=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}(t),\bm{z}_{i}).

Chen et al. 2023
showed that the loss of the NN at a certain fixed time is a kernel machine with LPK plus the loss function at initialization:
\ell(\bm{w}_{T},\bm{z})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\frac{1}{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})+\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z})
. Here, the LPK is a data-dependent kernel that depends on the training set
\mathcal{S}
.
Using this equivalence, define the following set of LPKs and the function class of the loss function

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{T}\triangleq\{

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime}):\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu^{\otimes n}),\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime},\bm{z}_{j}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\leq B^{2}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{T}\triangleq\Big\{

\displaystyle\ell(\mathcal{A}_{T}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}),\bm{z})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\frac{1}{n}\mathsf{K}(\bm{z},\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})+\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z}):\mathsf{K}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}\Big\},

(1)

where
B>0
is some constant,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\left\{\bm{z}_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\bm{z}_{n}^{\prime}\right\}
,
\mu^{\otimes n}
is the joint distribution of
n
i.i.d. samples drawn from
\mu
,
\operatorname{supp}(\mu^{\otimes n})
is the support set of
\mu^{\otimes n}
, and
\mathcal{A}_{T}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
is the parameters obtained by GF algorithm at time
T
and trained with
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Then
Chen et al. 2023
derived the following generalization bound:

\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T})\leq\frac{B}{n}\sqrt{\sup_{\mathsf{K}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\textnormal{Tr}(\mathsf{K}(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{Z};\mathcal{S}^{\prime}))+\sum_{i\neq j}\Delta(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j})},

where
\Delta(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\mathsf{K}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right.

\left.-\inf_{\mathsf{K}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\mathsf{K}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right]
.
However, the above bound suffers from several limitations:
1) It involves a supremum over an infinite family of LPKs, making it intractable to compute in practice; 2) The term
\sum_{i\neq j}\Delta(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j})
can be as large as
O(n^{2})
in the worst case, leading to a loose bound; 3) The bound must be evaluated on datasets distinct from the training set, limiting its practical applicability.
In this paper, we use the stability property of GF to substantially reduce the size of the function class, resulting in a significantly tighter generalization bound that depends only on the training set. Our new bound matches the classical kernel method bound in Lemma
3.3
, but instead of relying on a fixed kernel, it utilizes the
data-dependent
loss path kernel. Adapting to the data and algorithm, this learned kernel can outperform static kernels in traditional methods, thereby achieving improved generalization performance.

4
Uniform Stability of Gradient Flow and Concentration of LPKs

In this section, we show that the GF is uniformly stable. This uniform stability property implies the LPK concentration and connects LPKs trained from different datasets. Instead of transforming the stability to a generalization bound directly, we then combine the stability analysis with uniform convergence via Rademacher complexity to get a data-dependent bound in Sec.
5
.

4.1
Uniform Stability of Gradient Flow

Definition 4.1

(Uniform argument stability
[
16
,
13
]
)
.

A randomized algorithm
\mathcal{A}
is
\epsilon_{n}
-uniformly argument stable if for all datasets
\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{(i)}\in\mathcal{Z}^{n}
such that they differ by at most one data point, we have
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{A}}\left[\left\lVert\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S})-\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)})\right\rVert\right]\leq\epsilon_{n},

where the expectation is taken over the randomness of
\mathcal{A}
.

Here we consider the uniform argument stability, which can be easily transformed to uniform stability with respect to loss if the loss function is Lipschitz. In this paper, we mainly consider full-batch GF so there is no randomness in
\mathcal{A}
. To analyze the GF dynamics and LPKs, we make the following standard assumptions.

Assumption 4.2
.

Assume
\ell(\bm{w},\cdot)
is
L
-Lipschitz and
\beta
-smooth with respect to
\bm{w}
, that is,
\left\lVert\ell(\bm{w},\cdot)-\ell(\bm{w}^{\prime},\cdot)\right\rVert\leq L\left\lVert\bm{w}-\bm{w}^{\prime}\right\rVert
and
\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w},\cdot)-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}^{\prime},\cdot)\right\rVert\leq\beta\left\lVert\bm{w}-\bm{w}^{\prime}\right\rVert
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{(i)}
be two datasets that differ only in the
i
-th data point. We prove the following stability results of GF for convex, strongly convex (S.C.), and non-convex losses.
Similar stability results of GD (for convex case) and SGD were proved in
[
13
,
29
]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Under Assumption
4.2
, for any two data sets
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{(i)}
, let
\bm{w}_{t}=\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S})
and
\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}=\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}))
be the parameters trained from same initialization
\bm{w}_{0}=\bm{w}_{0}^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\begin{cases}\frac{2L}{\gamma n},&\text{$L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is $\gamma$-S.C.,}\\
\frac{2Lt}{n},&\text{$L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is convex,}\\
\frac{2L}{\beta n}(e^{\beta t}-1),&\text{$L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is non-convex.}\end{cases}

For convex losses, uniform stability increases linearly with
T
. For strongly convex losses, it holds without increasing with training time.
Unfortunately, for non-convex losses, the bound exponentially increases with time
T
in the worst case, leading to an exponential stability generalization bound. Our Theorem
5.2
avoids this case by combining stability analysis with Rademacher complexity.

For non-convex loss,
Hardt et al. 2016
obtain
O(T/n)
stability bound of SGD with decayed learning rate
\eta=c/t
, which is equivalent to training
c\ln T
time in our case since
\sum_{t}c/t\approx c\ln T
. In our case, using a learning rate of
\eta=1/\beta(t+1)
will allow us to have
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{T}-\bm{w}_{T}^{\prime}\right\rVert=\frac{2LT}{\beta n}

1
1

1

However, training with a decayed learning rate may not converge and requires to change the definition of the LPK. Therefore, we stick to the constant learning rate.

4.2
Concentration of LPKs under Stability

We now derive useful concentration properties of LPKs using uniform stability. These properties will be used when defining the function class explored by GF and proving the generalization bound. First of all,
one can show that the LPK concentrates for a fixed pair of
\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Under Assumption
4.2
, for any fixed
\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime}
, with probability at least
1-\delta
over the randomness of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\begin{cases}\frac{4L^{2}\beta T}{\gamma}\sqrt{\frac{\ln\frac{2}{\delta}}{2n}},&\text{$L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is $\gamma$-S.C.,}\\
2L^{2}\beta T^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\ln\frac{2}{\delta}}{2n}},&\text{$L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is convex,}\\
\frac{4L^{2}}{\beta}(e^{\beta T}-\beta T-1)\sqrt{\frac{\ln\frac{2}{\delta}}{2n}},&\text{$L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is non-convex.}\end{cases}

Next, using a stability argument and Chernoff bound, we are able to bound the difference between
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Under Assumption
4.2
, for two datasets
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, with probability at least
1-\delta
over the randomness of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\begin{cases}\tilde{O}(T\sqrt{n}),&\text{$L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is $\gamma$-S.C.,}\\
\tilde{O}(T^{2}\sqrt{n}),&\text{$L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is convex,}\\
\tilde{O}(e^{T}\sqrt{n}),&\text{$L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is non-convex.}\end{cases}

5
Main Results

5.1
Generalization Bound of Gradient Flow (GF)

With the above preparations, we are ready to prove our generalization bound. We define the loss function class
\mathcal{G}_{T}
as in (
1
) at time
T
by constraining the LPK class
\mathcal{K}_{T}
as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{T}\triangleq\Big\{

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime}):\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime},\bm{z}_{j}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\leq B^{2},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mu^{\otimes n}),\sup_{\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime}}\left\lvert\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\Delta\Big\}.

where
B,\Delta>0
are some constants and
\mathbb{S}^{\prime}
is a subset of
\operatorname{supp}(\mu^{\otimes n})
. Note this function class includes
\ell(\mathcal{A}_{T}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z})
if the conditions are satisfied on
\mathcal{S}
. For example, the first condition is satisfied if
\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S})\leq B^{2}
.
For this function class, we can improve the Rademacher complexity below since the conditions in
\mathcal{K}_{T}
significantly reduce the size of the function class.

Lemma 5.1
.

Recall Definition
3.1
for
\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T})
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T})\leq\frac{B}{n}\sqrt{\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})+4\Delta\sqrt{6n\ln 2n}+8\Delta}.

As we have shown above, the conditions in the function class are satisfied with
B
being some data-dependent quantity, and the trace term can be bounded as in Lemma
4.5
. With a covering argument, we can prove our main result of the generalization bound for GF dynamics.

Theorem 5.2
.

Denote by
\Gamma\triangleq\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S})}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})}
. Under Assumption
4.2
, with probability at least
1-\delta

\min\left\{\tilde{O}\left(\frac{e^{\frac{T}{2}}}{n^{\frac{3}{4}}}\right),O\left(\sqrt{\frac{T}{n}}\right)\right\},&\text{non-convex.}\end{cases}

Our results show that the generalization ability of GF is mainly affected by the first term
\Gamma
, which can also be rewritten as

\displaystyle\Gamma=\frac{2}{n}\sqrt{L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{0})-L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{T})}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}dt},

(3)

due to the definition of LPK and
\frac{dL_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})}{dt}=\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})^{\top}\frac{d\bm{w}_{t}}{dt}=-\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}
.
This bound matches the Radamecher bound of the classic kernel methods in Lemma
3.3
.
In
\Gamma
,

\sqrt{\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S})}

serves as the RKHS norm of the kernel, while

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})

is the trace of the kernel. The RKHS norm in our setting remains below 1 due to the bounded loss.

Unlike a kernel method with a fixed kernel, GF learns a
data-dependent
kernel LPK, thus adapting the underlying feature map to the training set. Consequently, our bound can surpass the fixed-kernel scenario because the gradient norms
\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})\right\rVert,\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert
shrink during training—whereas a fixed kernel’s bound remains static. Moreover, the RKHS norm here stays below 1, in contrast to fixed-kernel methods, whose RKHS norm may grow with the sample size or dimensionality (see Corollary
6.2
). Overall, our bound highlights that a more favorable optimization landscape and faster convergence can promote stronger generalization.

5.2
Generalization Bound of Stochastic Gradient Flow (SGF)

Above, we derived a generalization bound for NNs trained from full-batch GF. Here we extend our analysis to SGF and derive a corresponding generalization bound.
To start with, we recall the dynamics of SGF (SGD with infinitesimal step size):

\frac{d\bm{w}_{t}}{dt}=-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{t}}(\bm{w}_{t})=-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})

(4)

where
\mathcal{S}_{t}\subseteq\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}
is the indices of batch data used in time interval
[t,t+1]
and
\left\lvert\mathcal{S}_{t}\right\rvert=m
is the batch size.
Define
\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S})=\int_{t}^{t+1}\left\langle\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}),\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rangle\textrm{d}t
to be the LPK over time interval
[t,t+1]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Under Assumption
4.2
, for a fixed sequence
\mathcal{S}_{0},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{T-1}
, with probability at least
1-\delta
over the randomness of
\mathcal{S}
, the generalization gap of SGF defined by (
4
) is upper bounded by

\displaystyle L_{\mu}(\mathcal{A}_{T}(\mathcal{S}))-L_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{A}_{T}(\mathcal{S}))\leq\frac{2}{n}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S})}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})}+\tilde{O}(\frac{T}{\sqrt{n}}).

Similarly, we define the first term as
\Gamma
for the SGF case. When
\mathcal{S}_{t}=\mathcal{S}
, SGF becomes GF and the bound becomes similar to (
2
). This bound can be extended to any random sampling algorithm by taking the expectation over the randomness of the algorithm.

Case Study

6.1
Overparameterized Neural Network under NTK Regime

The NTK associated with the NN
f(\bm{w},\bm{x})
at
\bm{w}
is defined by
\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w};\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})=\nabla_{\bm{w}}f(\bm{w},\bm{x})\nabla_{\bm{w}}f(\bm{w},\bm{x}^{\prime})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
.
Since the LPK has a natural connection with NTK, our bound
\Gamma
in (
3
) can be calculated using the NTK during the training:

\Gamma=\frac{2}{n}\sqrt{L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{0})-L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{T})}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\nabla_{f}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{t};\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})\nabla_{f}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})dt}.

When the output dimension
k=1
and using a mean-square loss
L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})=\frac{1}{2n}\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}
, as previous work
[
25
,
24
]
showed, as long as the smallest eigenvalue of NTK is lower bounded from 0, the training loss enjoys an exponential convergence,
\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}\leq e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{\text{min}}}{n}t}\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{0},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}
. In this setting, the generalization can be upper bounded by the condition number of the NTK as follows.

Corollary 6.1
.

Suppose that
\lambda_{\text{max}}(\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{t};\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}))\leq\lambda_{\text{max}}
and
\lambda_{\text{min}}(\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{t};\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}))\geq\lambda_{\text{min}}>0
for
t\in[0,T]
. Then

\Gamma\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda_{\text{max}}\cdot\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{0},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}}{\lambda_{\text{min}}\cdot n}(1-e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{\text{min}}}{n}T})}.

This bound shows that the generalization of overparameterized NNs depends on the condition number of the NTK. With a smaller condition number, the network converges faster and generalizes better. This bound is always upper-bounded even when
T\rightarrow\infty
. As
n/T
increases, the bound decreases.
Since
\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{0},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}=O(n)
for NTK initialization
[
25
]
and
1-e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{\text{min}}T}{n}}\leq\frac{2\lambda_{\text{min}}T}{n}
, our bound has a faster rate than
O(\sqrt{\lambda_{\text{max}}T/n})
. When
\frac{\lambda_{\text{max}}}{\lambda_{\text{min}}}=O(1)
, our bound has a rate of
O(\sqrt{1-e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{\text{min}}T}{n}}})
.
For overparameterized NNs, NTK does not change much from initialization, hence
\lambda_{\text{max}}
and
\lambda_{\text{min}}
can be specified using the
\lambda_{\text{max}}(\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{0};\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}))
and
\lambda_{\text{min}}(\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{0};\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}))
, see
[
25
,
24
,
53
,
44
,
65
]
.

6.2
Kernel Ridge Regression

Given a kernel
K(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})=\left\langle\phi(\bm{x}),\phi(\bm{x}^{\prime})\right\rangle
, where
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{p}
, consider kernel ridge regression
f(\bm{w},\bm{x})=\bm{w}^{\top}\phi(\bm{x})
with
L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w})=\frac{1}{2n}\left\lVert\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}\bm{w}-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert\bm{w}\right\rVert^{2}
and
\ell(\bm{w},\bm{z})=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{w}^{\top}\phi(\bm{x})-y)^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert\bm{w}\right\rVert^{2}

\phi(\mathbf{X})\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
and
\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

\bm{w}^{*}=\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\left(\frac{1}{n}K(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X})+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{n}\right)^{-1}\bm{y}

Corollary 6.2
.

Suppose
K(\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})\leq K_{\max}
for all

K(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X})
is full-rank. We have that

\Gamma\leq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{K_{\max}}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right\rVert\left\lVert\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right\rVert,&\text{ when }\lambda=0,\\
\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{K_{\max}}\sqrt{\bm{y}^{\top}\left(K(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X})\right)^{-1}\bm{y}}\left\lVert\bm{y}\right\rVert,&\text{ when }\lambda=0,~\bm{w}_{0}=0.\end{cases}

(5)

Here (

\bm{w}_{0}=0

6.3

Consider a single-index model
y=f_{*}(\left\langle\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{x}\right\rangle)+\xi

\bm{\theta}^{*}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

\bm{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{d})
,
f_{*}

\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})

Bietti et al. 2022

\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

with
\tau>1
are random biases that are frozen during training, and

are random Rademacher variables. Let

be a regularized squared loss. The NN is trained by a two-stage gradient flow:

\displaystyle\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}=-\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}^{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\theta}_{t},\bm{c}_{t}),\quad\frac{d\bm{c}_{t}}{dt}=-\boldsymbol{1}(t>T_{0})\nabla_{\bm{c}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\theta}_{t},\bm{c}_{t}),

Under the settings of Theorem 6.1 in

\displaystyle\Gamma

Taking

8
Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, by combining the stability analysis and uniform convergence via Rademacher complexity, we derive a generalization bound for GF that parallels classical Rademacher complexity bounds for kernel methods by leveraging the data-dependent kernel LPK. Our results show that NNs trained by GF may outperform a fixed kernel by learning data-dependent kernels. Our bound also shows how the norm of the training loss gradients along the optimization trajectory affects the generalization. Recently,
Montanari & Urbani 2025
applied dynamical mean–field theory (DMFT) to two-layer NNs and showed that GF exhibits three distinct phases—an initial feature-learning regime (
T=O(1)
), a prolonged generalization plateau, and a late overfitting phase (
T=O(n)
) (Fig.
2
). Our bound (Theorem
5.2
) reproduces similar qualitative behavior. Unlike the mean–field analysis, our approach applies to general architectures and data distributions. A promising direction is to integrate the DMFT’s phase-wise insights with our LPK framework to obtain finer generalization guarantees.

For practice-relevant applications, by monitoring the evolution of our bound
\Gamma
during training, one can predict the overfitting for overparameterized NNs and identify optimal stopping time for training without access test data
[
3
]
. Our
\Gamma
can also serve as a proxy to compare model architectures in Neural Architecture Search (NAS)
[
55
,
40
,
19
,
42
,
20
]
.

For future directions, extending the analysis to GD and SGD with large learning rates can bring the bound closer to practice. Second, our analysis uses a function class larger than
\mathcal{G}_{T}
when bounding the Rademacher complexity. Refining this step could further tighten the bound.

Limitations and Impact Statement

Our analysis focuses on the generalization bound of the gradient flow algorithm. The behaviors of other algorithms, such as gradient descent (GD), stochastic gradient descent (SGD), and Adam, are still unclear. Extending the analysis to GD and SGD with large learning rates can bring the bound closer to practice.

This paper presents work whose goal is to advance the field of Machine Learning. There are many potential societal consequences of our work, none of which we feel must be specifically highlighted here.

Appendix B
Uniform Stability of Gradient Flow

Lemma
4.3
.

Under Assumption
4.2
, for any two data sets
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{(i)}
, let
\bm{w}_{t}=\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S})
and
\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}=\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}))
be the parameters trained from same initialization
\bm{w}_{0}=\bm{w}_{0}^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\begin{cases}\frac{2L}{\gamma n},&\text{$L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is $\gamma$-S.C.,}\\
\frac{2Lt}{n},&\text{$L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is convex,}\\
\frac{2L}{\beta n}(e^{\beta t}-1),&\text{$L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is non-convex.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Convex Case.

Notice that

\displaystyle\quad\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}}{\partial(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})},\frac{d\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)}{dt}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\frac{d\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)}{dt}

\displaystyle=2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n}\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right)-2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n}\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right)

(convexity)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4L}{n}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert.

Since also
\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}}{dt}=2\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}{dt}
, we have

2\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}{dt}\leq\frac{4L}{n}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert.

When
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert=0
, the result already hold. When
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert>0
,

\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}{dt}\leq\frac{2L}{n}.

Solve the differential equation, we have

\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\frac{2Lt}{n}.

Thus, we complete the proof of the convex case.

\gamma
-Strongly Convex Case.

Notice that

\displaystyle\quad\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}}{dt}

\displaystyle=2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\frac{d\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)}{dt}

\displaystyle=2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n}\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right)-2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4L}{n}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert-2\gamma\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\left(\frac{2L}{n}-\gamma\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\right).

(6)

Now we prove
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\frac{2L}{\gamma n}
by contradition. Recall
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}_{0}^{\prime}\right\rVert=0
. Suppose that there is some time
T
such that
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{T}-\bm{w}_{T}^{\prime}\right\rVert>\frac{2L}{\gamma n}
, then there must be some
T^{\prime}<T
such that
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{T^{\prime}}-\bm{w}_{T^{\prime}}^{\prime}\right\rVert=\frac{2L}{\gamma n}
and
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert
is increasing at some point between
[T^{\prime},T]
. However, when
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert>\frac{2L}{\gamma n}
, by (
B
),
\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}}{dt}<0
and
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert
must decrease. Therefore contradict and we must have
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\frac{2L}{\gamma n}
.

Non-Convex Case.

First of all, we have that

\displaystyle\quad\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}}{dt}

\displaystyle=2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\frac{d\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)}{dt}

\displaystyle=2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n}\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right)-2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert+2\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4L}{n}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert+2\beta\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}.

Since also
\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}}{dt}=2\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}{dt}
, we have

2\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}{dt}\leq\frac{4L}{n}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert+2\beta\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}.

When
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert=0
, the result already hold. When
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert>0
,

\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}{dt}\leq\frac{2L}{n}+\beta\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert.

From this we have

\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}{\frac{2L}{n\beta}+\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}\leq\beta dt.

Solve the differential equation, we have

\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\frac{2L}{\beta n}(e^{\beta t}-1).

∎

Appendix C

In the following, we will only show the proofs for the convex case. The proofs for strongly convex and non-convex cases are similar.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{(i)}
be two datasets that only differ in
i

\frac{2L^{2}\beta T^{2}}{n},&\text{when $L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is convex,}\\
\frac{4L^{2}}{\beta n}(e^{\beta T}-\beta T-1),&\text{when $L_{S}(\bm{w})$ is non-convex.}\end{cases}

Proof.

For convex loss, by the smoothness and Lemma
4.3
, we have
\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z})\right\rVert\leq\beta\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S})-\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)})\right\rVert\leq\beta\frac{2Lt}{n}
for all
\bm{z}

\displaystyle\quad\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S})-\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{(i)})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z}),\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z}),\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rangle dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z}),\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z}),\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\langle\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z}),\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z}),\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rangle dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z}),\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z}^{\prime})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\langle\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z}),\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rangle dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z})\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z}^{\prime})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}),\bm{z})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z})\right\rVert\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}),\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rVert dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}L\beta\frac{2Lt}{n}+\beta\frac{2Lt}{n}Ldt

\displaystyle=\frac{2L^{2}\beta T^{2}}{n}

Similarly
\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{(i)})-\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S})\leq\frac{2L^{2}\beta T^{2}}{n}
. Thus
\left\lvert\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S})-\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{(i)})\right\rvert\leq\frac{2L^{2}\beta T^{2}}{n}

34
]
))
.

Let
F
be a convex function,
\mathcal{D}
a collection of matrices and
\bm{\sigma}^{\prime}
be an independent copy of

, then

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{\mathbf{D}\in\mathcal{D}}F\left(\sum_{i\neq j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\mathbf{D}_{ij}\right)\leq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{\mathbf{D}\in\mathcal{D}}F\left(4\sum_{i\neq j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}^{\prime}\mathbf{D}_{ij}\right).

Lemma D.2

(Hoeffding’s inequality for Rademacher random variables (Theorem 2.2.5 in
[
63
]
))
.

Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}
be independent Rademacher random variables, and
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\sigma_{i}\right\rvert\geq t\right)\leq 2e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\left\lVert\bm{a}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}}.

Lemma
5.1
.

Recalling the Rademacher complexity in Definition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T})\leq\frac{B}{n}\sqrt{\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})+4\Delta\sqrt{6n\ln 2n}+8\Delta},

where
\mathcal{G}_{T}
and
\mathcal{K}_{T}
are defined by (
1
) and Section
5
respectively.

Proof.

Recall

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{T}=\Big\{\ell(\mathcal{A}_{T}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}),\bm{z})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\frac{1}{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})+\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z}):\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{T}=\Big\{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime}):\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime},\bm{z}_{j}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\leq B^{2},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mu^{\otimes n}),\sup_{\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime}}\left\lvert\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\Delta\Big\}.

Suppose
\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=\left\langle\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}),\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}^{\prime})\right\rangle
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime}=\{g(\bm{z})=\left\langle\bm{\beta},\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z})\right\rangle+\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z}):\left\lVert\bm{\beta}\right\rVert\leq B,\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}\}.

(7)

We first show
\mathcal{G}_{T}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime}
. For
\forall g(\bm{z})\in\mathcal{G}_{T}
,

\displaystyle g(\bm{z})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\frac{1}{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z},\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})+\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\frac{1}{n}\left\langle\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}),\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime})\right\rangle+\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}),\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\frac{1}{n}\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime})\right\rangle+\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\bm{\beta}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}},\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z})\right\rangle+\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z}),

where we denote
\bm{\beta}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\frac{1}{n}\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime})
.
By definition of
\mathcal{G}_{T}
,
\left\lVert\bm{\beta}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\right\rVert^{2}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime},\bm{z}_{j}^{\prime};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\leq B^{2}
. Thus
g(\bm{z})\in\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime}
. Since
\forall g(\bm{z})\in\mathcal{G}_{T}
,
g(\bm{z})\in\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{G}_{T}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime}
.
\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime}
is strictly larger than
\mathcal{G}_{T}
because
\bm{\beta}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}
is a fixed vector for a fixed
\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
while
\bm{\beta}
in
\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime}
is a vector of any direction.
Then by the property of Rademacher complexity,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T})

\displaystyle\leq\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}g(\bm{z}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\left(\left\langle\bm{\beta},\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})\right\rangle+\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z}_{i})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\left\langle\bm{\beta},\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})\right\rangle\right]+\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\left\langle\bm{\beta},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})\right\rangle\right].

By the dual norm property, we have

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\left\langle\bm{\beta},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\left(\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right].

Then by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\quad\frac{B}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\left(\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{B}{n}\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(Jensen’s inequality)

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{n}\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})+\sum_{i\neq j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right)\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{B}{n}\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})+\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i\neq j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{n}\left(\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i\neq j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the second term above, by the decoupling in Lemma
D.1
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i\neq j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right]\leq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma},\bm{\sigma}^{\prime}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}4\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\sigma_{j}^{\prime}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right].

Since
\left\lvert\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\Delta
, by Lemma
D.2
, for any fixed
i
, with probability at least
1-\delta^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{j\neq i}\sigma_{j}^{\prime}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\Delta\sqrt{2n\ln\frac{2}{\delta^{\prime}}}.

By a union bound, for all
i\in[n]
, we know that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{j\neq i}\sigma_{j}^{\prime}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\Delta\sqrt{2n\ln\frac{2n}{\delta^{\prime}}}.

Conditioned on this, by Lemma
D.2
, with probability at least
(1-\delta^{\prime\prime})(1-\delta^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\sigma_{j}^{\prime}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\leq 2\Delta n\sqrt{\ln\frac{2n}{\delta^{\prime}}\ln\frac{2}{\delta^{\prime\prime}}}.

For the left
1-(1-\delta^{\prime\prime})(1-\delta^{\prime})
portion, in the worst case we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\sigma_{j}^{\prime}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\leq n(n-1)\Delta.

Combining these two cases, we can bound the expectation as

\displaystyle\quad\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i\neq j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma},\bm{\sigma}^{\prime}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}4\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\sigma_{j}^{\prime}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\delta^{\prime\prime})(1-\delta^{\prime})4\Delta\sqrt{2n\ln\frac{2n}{\delta^{\prime}}}+(\delta^{\prime}+\delta^{\prime\prime}-\delta^{\prime}\delta^{\prime\prime})4n(n-1)\Delta

\displaystyle\leq 4\Delta\sqrt{6n\ln 2n}+8\Delta

(take
\delta^{\prime}=\delta^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}
)

Therefore, in total we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T})\leq\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{B}{n}\sqrt{\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})+4\Delta\sqrt{6n\ln 2n}+8\Delta}.

∎

.

Theorem E.1
.

Recall the function class
\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime}
defined in (
7
). We have

\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime})\geq\frac{B}{\sqrt{2}n}\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})}.

Proof.

\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime}=\{g(\bm{z})=\left\langle\bm{\beta},\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z})\right\rangle+\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z}):\left\lVert\bm{\beta}\right\rVert\leq B,\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}\}.

The Rademacher complexity of
\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime}
is

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}g(\bm{z}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sup_{\left\lVert\bm{\beta}\right\rVert\leq B}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\left(\left\langle\bm{\beta},\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})\right\rangle+\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z}_{i})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sup_{\left\lVert\bm{\beta}\right\rVert\leq B}\left\langle\bm{\beta},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})\right\rangle\right]+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sup_{\left\lVert\bm{\beta}\right\rVert\leq B}\left\langle\bm{\beta},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert\right],

where in the last line we apply the dual norm property.
Then by the subadditivity of the supremum, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{B}{n}\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{B}{n}\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})\right\rvert\right]\right\rVert

(norm sub-additivity)

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{n}\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N_{+}}}\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\left[\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}[\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})]_{j}\right\rvert\right]\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(by the definition of 2-norm)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{B}{n}\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N_{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})]_{j}^{2}\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(Khintchine-Kahane inequality)

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{\sqrt{2}n}\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N_{+}}}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})]_{j}^{2}\right\rvert\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{\sqrt{2}n}\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N_{+}}}[\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})]_{j}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(rearrange the summations)

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{\sqrt{2}n}\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\Phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{\sqrt{2}n}\sup_{\mathsf{K}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{T}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S}^{\prime})}.

Hence, we complete the proof of this theorem.
∎

Stochastic Gradient Flow

In the previous section, we derived a generalization bound for NNs trained from full-batch gradient flow. Here we extend our analysis to stochastic gradient flow and derive a corresponding generalization bound.
To start with, we recall the dynamics of stochastic gradient flow (SGD with infinitesimal step size).

\frac{d\bm{w}_{t}}{dt}=-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{t}}(\bm{w}_{t})=-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})

where
\mathcal{S}_{t}\subseteq\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}
be the indices of batch data used in time interval
[t,t+1]
and
\left\lvert\mathcal{S}_{t}\right\rvert=m
be the batch size. Suppose each
\mathcal{S}_{t}
is uniformly sampled without replacement from
\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}
.
We recall the connection between the loss dynamics of stochastic gradient flow and a general kernel machine in
[
20
]
.

Theorem F.1

(Theorem 4 in
[
20
]
)
.

Suppose
\bm{w}(T)=\bm{w}_{T}
is a solution of stochastic gradient flow at time
T\in\mathbb{N}
with initialization
\bm{w}(0)=\bm{w}_{0}

\bm{z}\in\mathcal{Z}

\ell(\bm{w}_{T},\bm{z})=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}-\frac{1}{m}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})+\ell(\bm{w}_{0},\bm{z}),

where
\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})=\int_{t}^{t+1}\left\langle\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}),\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rangle\textrm{d}t

F.1
Stability of Stochastic Gradient Flow (SGF)

Lemma F.2
.

Suppose
L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w})
is convex for any
\mathcal{S}
and Assumption
4.2
holds. For any two data sets
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{(i)}
, let
\bm{w}_{t}=\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S})
and
\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}=\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathcal{S}^{(i)}))
be the parameters trained with SGF from same initialization
\bm{w}_{0}=\bm{w}_{0}^{\prime}
, then

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{A}_{t}}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\frac{2Lt}{n}.

where the expectation is taken over the randomness of sampling the data batches
\mathcal{S}_{t}
.

Proof.

Notice that

\displaystyle\quad\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\partial\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}}{\partial(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})},\frac{d\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)}{dt}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\frac{d\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)}{dt}

\displaystyle=2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{t}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}_{t}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

Since
\mathcal{S}_{t}
and
\mathcal{S}^{(i)}_{t}
are uniformly sampled without replacement, the probability that
\mathcal{S}_{t}
and
\mathcal{S}_{t}^{(i)}
are different is
\frac{m}{n}
. When
\mathcal{S}_{t}=\mathcal{S}^{(i)}_{t}
, by convexity,

\displaystyle 2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{t}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{t}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)\leq 0.

Since also
\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}}{dt}=2\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}{dt}
, we have

2\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}{dt}\leq 0.

Solve the differential equation for
[T-1,T]
, we have

\left\lVert\bm{w}_{T}-\bm{w}_{T}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\bm{w}_{T-1}-\bm{w}_{T-1}^{\prime}\right\rVert.

When
\mathcal{S}_{t}
and
\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\prime}
differ with one data point,

\displaystyle\quad 2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{t}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}^{(i)}_{t}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{t}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})+\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{m}\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right)-2\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{(i)}}(\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{m}\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{\top}\left(\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i}^{\prime})-\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right)

(convexity)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4L}{m}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert.

Since also
\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}}{dt}=2\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}{dt}
, we have

2\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}{dt}\leq\frac{4L}{m}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert.

When
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert=0
, the result already hold. When
\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert>0
,

\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert}{dt}\leq\frac{2L}{m}.

Solve the differential equation for
[T-1,T]
, we have

\left\lVert\bm{w}_{T}-\bm{w}_{T}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\frac{2L}{m}+\left\lVert\bm{w}_{T-1}-\bm{w}_{T-1}^{\prime}\right\rVert.

Therefore, considering the two cases that whether
\mathcal{S}_{t}=\mathcal{S}^{(i)}_{t}
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{A}_{T}}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{T}-\bm{w}_{T}^{\prime}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m}{n}\cdot\frac{2L}{m}+(1-\frac{m}{n})\cdot 0+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{A}_{T}}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{T-1}-\bm{w}_{T-1}^{\prime}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\frac{2L}{n}+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{A}_{T}}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{T-1}-\bm{w}_{T-1}^{\prime}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\frac{2LT}{n}.

Thus, we complete the proof of this lemma.

∎

\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T}^{B_{0}^{i},\dots,B_{T-1}^{i}})\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{B_{t}^{i}}{n}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})+L^{2}n+4L^{2}\sqrt{6n\ln 2n}+8L^{2}}.

Let
B_{t}^{i*}
be the number such that

\sqrt{\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S})}\leq B_{t}^{i*}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S})}+\frac{1}{n}.

We have

\displaystyle\quad\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T}^{B_{0}^{i*},\dots,B_{T-1}^{i*}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{B_{t}^{i*}}{n}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})+O(L^{2}n)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{1}{n}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S})}+\frac{1}{n}\right)\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})+O(L^{2}n)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{1}{n}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S})}\right)\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})}+O(\frac{T}{\sqrt{n}}).

By Theorem
3.2
and applying a union bound over
B_{t}^{i}=\frac{1}{n},\frac{2}{n},\dots,1
,
t=0,\dots,T-1
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, for all
B_{i}^{t}
,

\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{T}^{B_{0}^{i},\dots,B_{T-1}^{i}}}\left\{L_{\mu}(g)-L_{\mathcal{S}}(g)\right\}\leq 2\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{G}_{T}^{B_{0}^{i},\dots,B_{T-1}^{i}})+3\sqrt{\frac{T\ln{n}+\ln(2/\delta)}{2n}}.

These together imply that with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle L_{\mu}(\mathcal{A}_{T}(\mathcal{S}))-L_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{A}_{T}(\mathcal{S}))\leq\frac{2}{n}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{j};\mathcal{S})}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{t,t+1}(\bm{z}_{i},\bm{z}_{i};\mathcal{S})}+\tilde{O}(\frac{T}{\sqrt{n}}).

∎

Appendix G
Proofs for Case Study

G.1
Overparameterized neural network under NTK regime

Recall the definition of NTK
\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w};\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})=\nabla_{\bm{w}}f(\bm{w},\bm{x})\nabla_{\bm{w}}f(\bm{w},\bm{x}^{\prime})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
for a neural network function
f(\bm{w},\bm{x})
. We now prove our bound for the NTK case.

Corollary
6.1
.

Suppose that
\lambda_{\text{max}}(\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{t};\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}))\leq\lambda_{\text{max}}
and
\lambda_{\text{min}}(\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{t};\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}))\geq\lambda_{\text{min}}>0
for
t\in[0,T]
. Then

\Gamma\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda_{\text{max}}\cdot\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{0},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}}{\lambda_{\text{min}}\cdot n}(1-e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{\text{min}}}{n}T})}.

Proof.

Notice by the chain rule,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{f}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})^{\top}\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{t};\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})\nabla_{f}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Gamma=\frac{2}{n}\sqrt{L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{0})-L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{T})}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\nabla_{f}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})^{\top}\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{t};\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})\nabla_{f}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})dt}

Since the loss is bounded in
[0,1]
,
L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{0})-L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{T})\leq 1
.
When using a mean squre loss
L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})=\frac{1}{2n}\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}
and
\ell(\bm{w},\bm{z})=\frac{1}{2}(f(\bm{w},\bm{x})-y)^{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{t};\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})\left(f(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{x}_{i})-\bm{y}_{i}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i\in[n]}\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{t};\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\text{max}}(\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{t};\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}))\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}.

In the case that the smallest eigenvalue of NTK
\lambda_{\text{min}}(\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{t};\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X}))\geq\lambda_{\text{min}}>0
over the training, the loss converges exponentially
\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}\leq e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{\text{min}}}{n}t}\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{0},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}
. We can see from

\displaystyle\frac{d\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}}{dt}

\displaystyle=2\left(f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right)^{\top}\frac{df(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})}{dt}

\displaystyle=2\left(f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right)^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{w}}f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})\frac{d\bm{w}_{t}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-2\left(f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right)^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{w}}f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})

\displaystyle=-2\left(f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right)^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{w}}f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})\nabla_{\bm{w}}f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})^{\top}\frac{1}{n}\left(f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{n}\left(f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right)^{\top}\hat{\Theta}(\bm{w}_{t};\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X})\left(f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2\lambda_{\text{min}}}{n}\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}.

Solving the ODE, we get

\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}\leq e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{\text{min}}}{n}t}\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{0},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{\text{max}}\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{t},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{\text{max}}e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{\text{min}}}{n}t}\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{0},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{n\lambda_{\text{max}}\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{0},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\lambda_{\text{min}}}(1-e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{\text{min}}}{n}T}).

Plugging this into our bound, we get

\Gamma\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda_{\text{max}}\left\lVert f(\bm{w}_{0},\mathbf{X})-\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}}{\lambda_{\text{min}}n}(1-e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{\text{min}}}{n}T})}.

∎

G.2
Kernel Ridge Regression Case

Corollary
6.2
.

Suppose
K(\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})\leq K_{\max}
for
i\in[n]
and
K(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X})
is full-rank,

\displaystyle\Gamma\leq\frac{\sqrt{K_{\max}}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right\rVert\left\lVert\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right\rVert}{n}.

When
\bm{w}_{0}=0
, the bound simplifies to

\Gamma\leq\frac{\sqrt{K_{\max}}\sqrt{\bm{y}^{\top}\left(K(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X})\right)^{-1}\bm{y}}\left\lVert\bm{y}\right\rVert}{n}.

(9)

Proof.

The training loss gradient is

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\left(\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{y}\right)+\lambda\bm{w}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}\bm{w}_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\bm{y}+\lambda\bm{w}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)\bm{w}_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\bm{y}

from where we can calculate

\bm{w}^{*}=\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{p}\right)^{-1}\phi(\mathbf{X})\bm{y}=\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}\phi(\mathbf{X})+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{n}\right)^{-1}\bm{y}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d\bm{w}_{t}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)\bm{w}_{t}+\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\bm{y}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}\phi(\mathbf{X})\bm{y}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)\left(\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}^{*}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{t}=\bm{w}^{*}+e^{-\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)t}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right).

Calculate the norm of the gradient,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)\bm{w}_{t}-\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)\left(\bm{w}^{*}+e^{-\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)t}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\bm{y}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)e^{-\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)t}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right\rVert^{2}.

Suppose the eigen-decomposition of
\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}
, then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}e^{-\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)t}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\right)\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)e^{-\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)t}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\right)\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)^{\top}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)^{2}e^{-2\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)t}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\right)\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)^{2}e^{-2\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)t}\left(\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right)^{2}.

Integrate the training loss gradient norm,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{w}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)^{2}e^{-2\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)t}\left(\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right)^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)\left(1-e^{-2\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)T}\right)\left(\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)\left(\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)^{\top}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}+\lambda\right)\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)^{\top}\left(\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right).

The individual gradient is

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})=\left(\phi(\bm{x}_{i})^{\top}\bm{w}_{t}-y_{i}\right)\phi(\bm{x}_{i})+\lambda\bm{w}_{t}=\left(\phi(\bm{x}_{i})\phi(\bm{x}_{i})^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I}\right)\bm{w}_{t}-y_{i}\phi(\bm{x}_{i}).

Assume
K(\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i})\leq K_{\max}
.
When
\lambda=0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\phi(\bm{x}_{i})\phi(\bm{x}_{i})^{\top}\bm{w}_{t}-y_{i}\phi(\bm{x}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\phi(\bm{x}_{i})\phi(\bm{x}_{i})^{\top}\left(\bm{w}^{*}+e^{-\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}t}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right)-y_{i}\phi(\bm{x}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert y_{i}\phi(\bm{x}_{i})+\phi(\bm{x}_{i})\phi(\bm{x}_{i})^{\top}e^{-\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}t}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)-y_{i}\phi(\bm{x}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\phi(\bm{x}_{i})\phi(\bm{x}_{i})^{\top}e^{-\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}t}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\max}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)^{\top}e^{-\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}t}\phi(\bm{x}_{i})\phi(\bm{x}_{i})^{\top}e^{-\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}t}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=K_{\max}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)^{\top}e^{-\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}t}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}e^{-\frac{1}{n}\phi(\mathbf{X})\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}t}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=K_{\max}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)^{\top}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}e^{-\frac{2}{n}\lambda_{i}t}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=K_{\max}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}e^{-\frac{2}{n}\lambda_{i}t}\left(\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right)^{2}.

Hence, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}K_{\max}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}e^{-\frac{2}{n}\lambda_{i}t}\left(\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right)^{2}dt

\displaystyle=K_{\max}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{n}{2}\left(1-e^{-\frac{2\lambda_{i}}{n}T}\right)\left(\bm{u}_{i}^{\top}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K_{\max}n}{2}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}.

Therefore when
\lambda=0
,

\Gamma\leq\frac{\sqrt{K_{\max}}\left\lVert\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right\rVert\left\lVert\phi(\mathbf{X})^{\top}\left(\bm{w}_{0}-\bm{w}^{*}\right)\right\rVert}{n}.

When
\bm{w}_{0}=0
, plunging in the expression of
\bm{w}^{*}
, the bound simplifies to

\Gamma\leq\frac{\sqrt{K_{\max}}\sqrt{\bm{y}^{\top}\left(K(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{X})\right)^{-1}\bm{y}}\left\lVert\bm{y}\right\rVert}{n}.

∎

Proof.

Recall

\Gamma=\frac{2}{n}\sqrt{L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{c}_{0})-L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\theta}_{T},\bm{c}_{T})}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}dt}.

We first calculate the order of the
\sqrt{L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{c}_{0})-L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\theta}_{T},\bm{c}_{T})}
.
Recall

L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\theta},\bm{c})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f(\bm{\theta},\bm{c};\bm{x}_{i})-y_{i})^{2}+\lambda\left\lVert\bm{c}\right\rVert^{2}.

Then one can claim that

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{c}_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f(\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{c}_{0};\bm{x}_{i})-y_{i})^{2}+\lambda\left\lVert\bm{c}_{0}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(f(\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{c}_{0};\bm{x}_{i})^{2}+y_{i}^{2}-2y_{i}f(\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{c}_{0};\bm{x}_{i})\right)+\lambda\left\lVert\bm{c}_{0}\right\rVert^{2}.

By Lemma
G.3

\phi(\sigma_{i}\left\langle\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{x}_{i}\right\rangle+b_{i})=\phi(\tilde{O}(1)+\tilde{O}(1))=\tilde{O}(1)
. Since
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{c}_{0}}\left[f(\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{c}_{0};\bm{x}_{i})^{2}\right]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{c}_{0}}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}^{2}\phi(\sigma_{i}\left\langle\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{x}_{i}\right\rangle+b_{i})^{2}\right]=\tilde{O}(\frac{\left\lVert\bm{c}_{0}\right\rVert^{2}}{N})=\tilde{O}(\frac{\rho^{2}}{N})=\tilde{O}(1)
, by Chebyshev’s inequality
f(\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{c}_{0};\bm{x}_{i})=\tilde{O}(1)
.
Since also
y_{i}=\tilde{O}(1)
and
\lambda\left\lVert\bm{c}_{0}\right\rVert^{2}=\tilde{O}(1)
, one can verify
L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{c}_{0})=\tilde{O}(1)
. Therefore
L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{c}_{0})-L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\theta}_{T},\bm{c}_{T})=\tilde{O}(1)
. Since
L_{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\theta}_{T},\bm{c}_{T})
is non-increasing in gradient flow,
\lambda\left\lVert\bm{c}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}=\tilde{O}(1)
during training.

Then we calculate the
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}dt
. By Lemma
G.4
, the sample gradient for
\bm{\theta}
and an upper bound for its
\ell_{2}
norm is given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\ell(\bm{\theta},\bm{c};\bm{x}_{i},y_{i})=~

\displaystyle-\bm{c}^{\top}\Phi^{\prime}(\left\langle\bm{\theta},\bm{x}_{i}\right\rangle)(y_{i}-\bm{c}^{\top}\Phi(\left\langle\bm{\theta},\bm{x}_{i}\right\rangle))\bm{x}_{i}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\ell(\bm{\theta},\bm{c};\bm{x}_{i},y_{i})\right\rVert=~

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{c}\right\rVert(\text{Lip}(f)_{*}\left\lVert\bm{x}_{i}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\xi_{i}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\bm{c}\right\rVert(\left\lVert\bm{x}_{i}\right\rVert+C^{\prime}\tau\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}))\left\lVert\bm{x}_{i}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\tilde{O}(\left\lVert\bm{c}\right\rVert^{2}\left\lVert\bm{x}_{i}\right\rVert^{2}).

Similarly, the sample gradient for
\bm{c}
is

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{c}}\ell(\bm{\theta},\bm{c};\bm{x}_{i},y_{i})=~

\displaystyle 2\Phi(\left\langle\bm{\theta},\bm{x}_{i}\right\rangle)(\bm{c}^{\top}\Phi(\left\langle\bm{\theta},\bm{x}_{i}\right\rangle)-f_{*}(\left\langle\bm{\theta},\bm{x}_{i}\right\rangle-\xi_{i})

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{c}}\ell(\bm{\theta},\bm{c};\bm{x}_{i},y_{i})\right\rVert=~

\displaystyle 2(\left\lVert\bm{x}_{i}\right\rVert+C^{\prime}\tau\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)})(\left\lVert\bm{c}\right\rVert(\left\lVert\bm{x}_{i}\right\rVert+C^{\prime}\tau\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)})

\displaystyle+\text{Lip}(f)_{*}\left\lVert\bm{x}_{i}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\xi_{i}\right\rVert)=\tilde{O}(\left\lVert\bm{c}\right\rVert\left\lVert\bm{x}_{i}\right\rVert^{2}).

As we have shown,
\left\lVert\bm{c}\right\rVert^{2}=O(\frac{1}{\lambda})
and by Lemma
G.3
,
\max_{i}\left\lVert\bm{x}_{i}\right\rVert=O(\sqrt{d\log(n/\delta)})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\ell(\bm{\theta},\bm{c};\bm{x}_{i},y_{i})\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{c}}\ell(\bm{\theta},\bm{c};\bm{x}_{i},y_{i})\right\rVert^{2}=\tilde{O}\Big(\frac{d^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big).

Then take
T=T_{0}+T_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{\bm{w}}\ell(\bm{w}_{t},\bm{z}_{i})\right\rVert^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq n\cdot\tilde{O}\Big(\frac{d^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)\cdot(T_{0}+T_{1})

\displaystyle\leq n\cdot\tilde{O}(\frac{d^{2}}{\lambda^{2}})\cdot\left(\tilde{\Theta}(d^{\frac{s}{2}-1})+\tilde{\Theta}(\frac{\lambda^{4}n}{d+N})\right)

\displaystyle=n\cdot\tilde{O}\left(\frac{d^{\frac{s}{2}+1}}{\lambda^{2}}+\lambda^{2}dn\right).

Combining the results, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n}\sqrt{\tilde{O}(1)\cdot n\cdot\tilde{O}\left(\frac{d^{\frac{s}{2}+1}}{\lambda^{2}}+\lambda^{2}dn\right)}

\displaystyle=\tilde{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{d^{\frac{s}{2}+1}}{n\lambda^{2}}+\lambda^{2}d}\right).

As long as
\lambda=o_{d}(1/d)
and
n=\tilde{\Omega}(d^{\frac{s}{2}+2})
,
\Gamma=o_{n,d}(1)
.
Optimizing the choice of
\lambda=(\frac{d^{\frac{s}{2}}}{n})^{\frac{1}{4}}
, we have

\Gamma\leq\tilde{O}\left(\left(\frac{d^{\frac{s}{2}+2}}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\right).

Hence, we complete the proof.
∎

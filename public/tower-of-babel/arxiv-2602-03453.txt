Energy Transport Velocity in Photonic Time Crystals
Kyungmin Lee, Younsung Kim, Kun Woo Kim, Bumki Min

Abstract
Steep or near-vertical Floquet dispersion in photonic time crystals (PTCs) is often read as fast, even apparently superluminal, transport. Here, we demonstrate that this anomaly arises from modulation-driven geometric drift, not energy flow. By deriving a Maxwell-flux Hellmann-Feynman relation, we prove that the cycle-averaged energy velocity remains strictly bounded. We further establish a universal velocity-product law conserved throughout the passband, v E ​ v g = ⟨ v ph 2 ⟩ T v_{E}v_{g}=\langle v_{\rm ph}^{2}\rangle_{T} , fixing transport solely by the temporal average of the inverse permittivity. The divergent group velocity is then traced to a mismatch between electric and magnetic geometric phase connections, revealing apparent superluminality as a geometric effect of temporal modulation.

v_{g}
can take anomalous values

;

17
;

Modern coherent-control and dispersion-engineering platforms, ranging from electromagnetically induced transparency to photonic crystals, enable extreme group indices and widely tunable
v_{g}

;
3
;
10

Thus, slow- and fast-light experiments consistently show that
d\omega/dk

In this Letter, we work within Maxwell theory for a PTC with strictly positive, time-periodic permittivity. In Sambe space, the Maxwell symplectic (power-flux) pairing yields a flux-form Hellmann–Feynman identity: it defines a symplectic (flux-metric) velocity and proves
v_{\rm symp}=v_{E}
on any simple real Floquet band, hence
|v_{E}|

v_{g}=d\omega/dk

v_{E}v_{g}=\langle(\mu\varepsilon(t))^{-1}\rangle_{T}=\langle v_{\rm ph}^{2}\rangle_{T}
, so the group-velocity divergence is precisely compensated by
v_{E}\to 0
. In
k

Model and energy velocity.—

We consider one-dimensional propagation along
z
in a spatially uniform, isotropic, source-free medium with constant
\mu>0
and real,
T
-periodic permittivity
\varepsilon(t)\geq\varepsilon_{\min}>0
. Vacuum constants are denoted
\varepsilon_{v},\mu_{v}
, so
c\equiv 1/\sqrt{\varepsilon_{v}\mu_{v}}
, and we assume
\mu\,\varepsilon_{\min}\geq\mu_{v}\,\varepsilon_{v}
(equivalently, the maximum instantaneous phase velocity does not exceed
c
). Working in complex notation, the instantaneous electromagnetic energy density and Poynting flux are

u(z,t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\varepsilon(t)|E(z,t)|^{2}+\mu|H(z,t)|^{2}\bigr)
and
S_{z}(z,t)=\operatorname{Re}\!\{E(z,t)\,H^{*}(z,t)\}
, which satisfy the Poynting theorem

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{z}S_{z}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\dot{\varepsilon}(t)\,|E(z,t)|^{2}
, expressing local energy exchange between the field and the modulation
31
.
We denote by
\langle\cdot\rangle_{T}
the average over one modulation period and define the cycle-averaged energy-transport velocity

v_{E}\equiv\frac{\langle S_{z}\rangle_{T}}{\langle u\rangle_{T}}.

(1)

The pointwise inequality
|S_{z}|\leq u/\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}
implies
|v_{E}|\leq v_{\mathrm{ph,max}}\equiv 1/\sqrt{\mu\,\varepsilon_{\min}}\leq c
,
so the energy-transport velocity remains finite even when the Floquet dispersion becomes extremely steep
1
. Throughout this Letter we restrict to nondispersive media with real, strictly positive
\varepsilon(t)
; extensions are discussed in the Supplemental Material
1
.

Maxwell–Floquet operator and symplectic velocity.—

To connect the transport velocity to the Floquet band structure, we use the plane-wave ansatz

E(z,t)=E(t)e^{ikz}
,
H(z,t)=H(t)e^{ikz}
with real
k
, which gives

ik\,E(t)=-\mu\,\partial_{t}H(t)
and
ik\,H(t)=-\partial_{t}\!\big(\varepsilon(t)E(t)\big)
.
With
\varepsilon(t+T)=\varepsilon(t)
, we expand

E(t)=\sum_{m}E_{m}e^{-i\omega_{m}t}
,
H(t)=\sum_{m}H_{m}e^{-i\omega_{m}t}
,

\varepsilon(t)=\sum_{\ell}\varepsilon_{\ell}e^{-i\ell\Omega t}
,
where
\Omega=2\pi/T
and
\omega_{m}=\omega+m\Omega
.
Matching harmonics yields

kE_{m}=\mu\,\omega_{m}H_{m},\qquad kH_{m}=\omega_{m}\sum_{n}\varepsilon_{m-n}E_{n}.

(2)

Collecting coefficients into
\ket{R}=(\bm{E},\bm{H})^{\mathsf{T}}
gives the Sambe-space constraint

M(\omega,k)\,\ket{R}=0,

(3)

whose real roots
\omega(k)
define passbands and complex roots define momentum gaps.

On a simple band, introducing a left null covector
\bra{L}
with
\bra{L}M(\omega,k)=0
, differentiation of
M(\omega(k),k)\ket{R(k)}=0
gives the exact slope formula
18

v_{g}(k)\equiv\frac{d\omega}{dk}=-\frac{\bra{L}\,\partial_{k}M\,\ket{R}}{\bra{L}\,\partial_{\omega}M\,\ket{R}}.

(4)

Equation (
4
) is purely geometrical: it depends on the
true
left null vector
\bra{L}
of the non-Hermitian Sambe-space matrix.

In Maxwell theory, however, the physically natural pairing is fixed by the electromagnetic flux (symplectic) form.
With the canonical symplectic matrix

\mathbb{J}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbb{I}\\[2.0pt]
-\mathbb{I}&0\end{pmatrix},

(5)

we define the flux form
(A,B)_{\mathbb{J}}\equiv\bra{A}\mathbb{J}\ket{B}
and the associated symplectic (flux-metric) velocity

v_{\rm symp}(k)\equiv-\frac{\bra{R}\,\mathbb{J}\,\partial_{k}M\,\ket{R}}{\bra{R}\,\mathbb{J}\,\partial_{\omega}M\,\ket{R}}.

(6)

Although this has a Hellmann–Feynman–like structure, it is evaluated with the Maxwell flux dual
\bra{R}\mathbb{J}
rather than
\bra{L}
. The appearance of
\mathbb{J}
reflects that source-free Maxwell equations form a Hamiltonian (symplectic) flow on field phase space, with
E
and
H
playing conjugate roles.

From
v_{\rm symp}
to
v_{E}
.—

The symplectic Hellmann–Feynman ratio is, in fact, the familiar energy-transport velocity.
For clarity we evaluate it in an
n
-harmonic truncation
m\in\mathcal{M}
(the identities hold in the full Sambe space).
In this basis, Eq. (
2
) becomes the
2n\times 2n
Maxwell–Floquet system

M(\omega,k)=\begin{pmatrix}-K&\mu\Lambda\\[3.0pt]
-\Lambda\bm{\varepsilon}&K\end{pmatrix},

(7)

with
K=k\,\mathbb{I}_{n}
,
\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\{\omega_{m}\}_{m\in\mathcal{M}})
, and the Toeplitz matrix
(\bm{\varepsilon})_{mn}=\varepsilon_{m-n}
.
A direct calculation using Parseval’s identity yields
1

\bra{R}\,\mathbb{J}\,\partial_{k}M\,\ket{R}=2\,\langle S_{z}\rangle_{T},\quad\bra{R}\,\mathbb{J}\,\partial_{\omega}M\,\ket{R}=-2\,\langle u\rangle_{T},

(8)

so that

v_{\rm symp}(k)=-\frac{\bra{R}\,\mathbb{J}\,\partial_{k}M\,\ket{R}}{\bra{R}\,\mathbb{J}\,\partial_{\omega}M\,\ket{R}}=\frac{\langle S_{z}\rangle_{T}}{\langle u\rangle_{T}}\equiv v_{E}(k).

(9)

Thus the Maxwell flux pairing turns the Sambe-space eigenproblem into a transport diagnostic: on any simple real Floquet band, the physical velocity is
v_{E}
, not the geometrical slope
d\omega/dk
(Fig.
1
). This distinction is sharp at momentum-gap edges. Near a branch-point edge
k_{c}
, the two-harmonic model (and generically a simple Puiseux splitting
19
) gives

\omega(k)\simeq\omega_{0}\pm a\sqrt{\delta k}
with
\delta k=k-k_{c}
, hence
d\omega/dk\propto|\delta k|^{-1/2}
.
By contrast, the flux-over-energy ratio is regular: typically
\langle S_{z}\rangle_{T}=O(\sqrt{|\delta k|})
while
\langle u\rangle_{T}=O(1)
, so
v_{E}\propto\sqrt{|\delta k|}\to 0
(and
\gamma\propto\sqrt{|\delta k|}
inside the gap)
1
. Equivalently, near a square-root edge the steepening of
d\omega/dk
is exactly paired with a collapse of the cycle-averaged flux, so
v_{g}v_{E}
stays
O(1)
even though
v_{g}
diverges (Fig.
1
). Furthermore, we prove analytically that the product of the group and energy
velocities is not only finite but exactly conserved across the passband
1
,

v_{g}\,v_{E}\;=\;\frac{1}{\mu}\left\langle\varepsilon^{-1}(t)\right\rangle_{T}\;=\;\langle v_{\rm ph}^{2}\rangle_{T}.

(10)

Equation (
10
) implies that the divergence of
v_{g}
at the band edge is perfectly counterbalanced by the vanishing
v_{E}
, dictated solely by the temporal average of the inverse permittivity.

Momentum gaps and generalized velocity.—

In a momentum gap the Floquet quasifrequency is complex,
\omega=\omega_{r}+i\gamma
, so a single Floquet component
e^{ikz-i\omega t}
carries an overall envelope
e^{\pm\gamma t}
.
To separate this trivial parametric amplification/attenuation from
transport
, we write

u(z,t)=e^{2\gamma t}u_{p}(z,t)
and
S_{z}(z,t)=e^{2\gamma t}S_{z,p}(z,t)
,
where
u_{p}
and
S_{z,p}
are
T
-periodic for a single Floquet mode
1
.
Equivalently, we introduce the weighted cycle average

\left\langle\!\left\langle f\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2\gamma t}\,f(t)\,dt,

(11)

which removes the envelope and is cycle-origin independent for a single mode.
We then define the generalized (gap) velocity

v_{\rm gen}(k)\equiv\frac{\left\langle\!\left\langle S_{z}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle}{\left\langle\!\left\langle u\right\rangle\!\right\rangle},

(12)

a flux-over-energy ratio that quantifies the net drift of energy in the presence of gain/loss. Using the same Maxwell flux pairing as above, one finds the exact Sambe-space expression

v_{\rm gen}(k)=-\frac{\bra{R}\,\mathbb{J}\,\partial_{k}M\,\ket{R}}{\bra{R}\,\mathbb{J}\,\partial_{\omega}M\,\ket{R}},

(13)

for the right Floquet mode
\ket{R}
satisfying
M(\omega,k)\ket{R}=0

1
.
By construction
v_{\rm gen}\to v_{E}
as
\gamma\to 0
, recovering the passband energy velocity. Furthermore, within the gap, the weighted cycle-averaged flux vanishes,
\left\langle\!\left\langle S_{z}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle=0
, while
\left\langle\!\left\langle u\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\neq 0
, so Eq. (
12
) gives
v_{gen}=0

1
.

Energy centroid and wavepacket motion.—

A wavepacket can exhibit rapid centroid motion in a PTC even when energy transport is slow, because temporal modulation continuously exchanges energy with the field.
To make this distinction explicit, we track the energy centroid.
For a localized, finite-energy wavepacket (so boundary terms vanish as
|z|\to\infty
), with energy density
u(z,t)
defined above, define

U(t)=\int u(z,t)\,dz
and

Z(t)=\big[\int z\,u(z,t)\,dz\big]/U(t)
.
Differentiating
Z(t)
and using the Poynting theorem yields the exact decomposition
1

\dot{Z}(t)=v_{\rm flux}(t)+v_{\rm mod}(t),

(14)

where the genuine transport contribution is

v_{\rm flux}(t)\equiv\frac{\int S_{z}(z,t)\,dz}{U(t)}.

(15)

The second term,
v_{\rm mod}(t)
, is
not
a transport velocity; it is a modulation-driven centroid shift originating from the time-dependent weighting of the energy density.

To expose its structure, introduce the electric and magnetic energies and their fractions,

\displaystyle U_{E}(t)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\varepsilon(t)\!\int|E|^{2}\,dz,\qquad U_{H}(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mu\!\int|H|^{2}\,dz,

(16)

\displaystyle f_{E}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{U_{E}(t)}{U(t)},\qquad f_{H}(t)=\frac{U_{H}(t)}{U(t)},

as well as the corresponding intensity centroids

Z_{E}(t)=\frac{\int z|E|^{2}dz}{\int|E|^{2}dz},\qquad Z_{H}(t)=\frac{\int z|H|^{2}dz}{\int|H|^{2}dz}.

(17)

A short calculation gives
1

v_{\rm mod}(t)=-\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon(t)}\,f_{E}(t)f_{H}(t)\,\bigl[Z_{E}(t)-Z_{H}(t)\bigr],

(18)

with
0\leq f_{E}f_{H}\leq 1/4
.
Accordingly, a large
v_{\rm mod}
signals strong modulation-induced
reweighting

(i.e., energy exchange between the field and the drive, redistributing electric and magnetic
energy when
Z_{E}\neq Z_{H}
),
rather than rapid energy transport.

Viewed in
k
-space, where
z\leftrightarrow i\partial_{k}
, the first spatial moment is generated by
i\partial_{k}
.
Thus the centroids
Z_{E}
and
Z_{H}
contain, besides the band-slope term
t\,\partial_{k}\omega
(often denoted
t\,v_{g}
),
additional offsets controlled by the
k
-dependence of the periodic parts of the Floquet eigenmodes
1
.
For a narrowband wavepacket centered at
k_{0}
, the separation
Z_{E}(t)-Z_{H}(t)
reduces to a gauge-invariant mismatch
between the electric and magnetic
k
-space phase connections.
Equation (
18
) then identifies
v_{\rm mod}
as a
geometric drift

1
:
temporal modulation reweights the electric and magnetic energy fractions, and a non-zero electric–magnetic centroid separation
converts this reweighting into rapid centroid motion without implying rapid energy transport.

To connect this instantaneous decomposition to the band picture, we now cycle-average over one modulation period. Cycle-averaging Eq. (
14
) yields
\langle\dot{Z}\rangle_{T}=\langle v_{\rm flux}\rangle_{T}+\langle v_{\rm mod}\rangle_{T}
. For a spectrally narrow wavepacket on a passband, the cycle-averaged centroid drift is governed by the bounded transport part together with the modulation-driven drift
1
. Figure
2
makes the key point transparent: as the momentum approaches a gap edge, the mode becomes increasingly standing-wave-like so that
\langle v_{\rm flux}\rangle_{T}\to 0
, yet the geometrical group velocity
v_{g}=d\omega/dk
grows sharply. The resolution is that the steepening of
v_{g}
is carried by the modulation term:
\langle v_{\rm flux}\rangle_{T}+\langle v_{\rm mod}\rangle_{T}
tracks
v_{g}
, whereas
\langle v_{\rm flux}\rangle_{T}
alone remains bounded and vanishes at the edge. In other words, superluminal-looking group velocities or peak advances in a PTC are naturally interpreted as modulation-driven reweighting (distributed parametric pumping), not superluminal energy transport.

Discussion.—

A PTC is not a passive medium: the modulation acts as a distributed pump that continually trades energy with the field. In this setting, “fast” features in the Floquet band diagram often tell us less about how energy
flows
than about how the drive
reweights
the energy distribution between electric and magnetic sectors. Near a momentum-gap edge, the apparent divergence of the geometrical slope
d\omega/dk
is carried by a modulation-induced
geometric drift
: in a narrowband wavepacket the drive couples to a gauge-invariant mismatch between the electric and magnetic
k
-space phase connections (equivalently, an electric–magnetic centroid offset), shifting the center of energy even as the mode becomes standing-wave-like and the net flux collapses. Consistently, on any stable passband the exact velocity-product law

v_{g}v_{E}=\langle(\mu\varepsilon(t))^{-1}\rangle_{T}=\langle v_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2}\rangle_{T}

pins the product of the group velocity and the cycle-averaged energy-transport velocity to a
k
-independent constant, so that the divergence of
v_{g}
as
k\to k_{c}
is necessarily compensated by
v_{E}\to 0
. This yields a practical reading rule for PTC band structures: steep dispersion primarily flags regimes dominated by pump-assisted reweighting, whereas transport is diagnosed by the Maxwell flux metric. In short, a PTC can move the
center of energy
without transporting energy, and the flux metric—together with the conserved product law—tells the difference.

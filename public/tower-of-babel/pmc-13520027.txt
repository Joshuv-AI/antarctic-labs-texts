Structural Design of AlGaN Deep-Ultraviolet Laser at Wavelength 266.3 nm with Graded Electron Blocking Layer and Composite Quantum Wells/Barriers
Authors: Syed Kashan Haider, Hameed Ur Rehman, Wengang Bi, Fang Wang, Yuhuai Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Deep-ultraviolet laser diodes (DUV LDs) are developing as vigorous light sources for sterilization, lithography, purification, and biochemical sensing. However, the development of high-performance deep-UV laser diodes is still limited by poor hole injection, which comes from the high activation energy of P-type dopants in wide-band gap AlGaN. This limitation reduces carrier transport efficiency and significantly restricts device performance. In this study, we focus on key performance parameters, including output power, injection efficiency, and the OCF (optical confinement factor), to address these challenges. Our design strategy involves minimizing the Al composition in the QWs (quantum wells) to improve carrier recombination while incorporating a graded AlGaN-based Mg-doped electron blocking layer to enhance hole injection and optical confinement within the active region. Results (simulation) confirm that the optimized device structure not only supports higher stimulated recombination rates but also strengthens the optical confinement. So, the proposed DUV LD achieves a 4.1% reduction in threshold voltage, a 27% decrease in threshold current (I th), and a 7.5% increase in maximum output power at 100 mA input current, with the OCF reaching as high as 25%. These improvements clearly prove how careful material and structural engineering can overcome intrinsic limitations of AlGaN-based devices and lead to efficient and dependable deep-UV laser diode technology.

Introduction

Over the past few decades, deep-ultraviolet (DUV) laser diodes have made impressive progress, playing a vital role in a wide range of applications, including efficient laser lighting, advanced display systems, biochemical detection, polymer curing, precision material processing, and secure short-range communication. − Meanwhile, different AlGaN-based DUV LEDs and AlGaN-based DUV laser diodes possess distinct advantages, including high spatial and temporal coherence. Thus, ideal beam quality, speedy modulation capabilities, and significantly higher power density can be achieved by DUV LDs.

DUV LDs still have some problems, such as high electron leakage and poor hole injection. , DUV LDs are more challenging to fabricate and optimize than DUV LEDs, and increasing the aluminum (Al) content in AlGaN widens its band gap, allowing emission at shorter wavelengths in the deep-ultraviolet (DUV) range. This makes high-Al-content AlGaN essential for generating wavelengths below 280 nm. Still, as the Al concentration increases, crystal quality degrades, creating defects in the AlGaN layers that cause current leakage and reduce radiative recombination, thereby lowering the laser efficiency and performance. − When carriers enter the active region of Al-rich AlGaN quantum wells, strong internal electric fields from spontaneous and piezoelectric polarization separate electrons and holes. This effect, known as the quantum confinement Stark effect (QCSE), reduces their recombination efficiency and shifts the emission to a longer wavelength, reducing stimulated recombination. Meanwhile, electron leakage, reduced electron–hole recombination, and Auger recombination are also likely to occur. AlGaN laser diodes exhibit temperature problems because of self-heating, resulting in elevated junction temperatures that lead to higher threshold currents and lower efficiency. Managing thermal resistance is essential to prevent localized heating and performance degradation caused by band gap shifting and carrier leakage. Our approach improves device efficiency by enhancing crystal quality and addressing strain, doping, and polarization effects.

To address these issues, researchers have proposed numerous Al-rich AlGaN-based DUV emitters that allow better control of the QCSE effect within the active region. One effective approach involves Si doping in the quantum barrier layers. − The ionization energy of magnesium (Mg) presents a major challenge in achieving high-efficiency UVC laser diodes, particularly as the aluminum (Al) content in AlGaN increases. Mg is the most commonly used p-type dopant in III-nitride semiconductors, including GaN/AlGaN. Still, its effectiveness decreases in high-Al-content AlGaN due to the increased ionization energy required to activate the Mg dopants. As the Al composition rises, the band gap widens, and the acceptor level of Mg moves deeper into the band gap, making it more difficult for Mg to release holes. This results in very low hole concentrations in the p-type region, which severely limits the carrier injection efficiency and overall device performance. Therefore, overcoming this issue is critical for improving the efficiency of UVC laser diodes. , Therefore, proposed multiple AlGaN MQWs to achieve a high activation energy for Mg atoms, and to achieve ionization energy for Mg atoms. − So, design strategies used for AlGaN-based DUV LEDs can be extended to DUV laser diodes, as long as the optical field remains largely unchanged. Another key factor influencing the output power of DUV LDs is the optical field distribution, as it significantly impacts both the net modal gain and the optical confinement factor. Numerous advanced epitaxial designs have been proposed, among which the extreme and double/triple asymmetric structures having a very thin p-waveguide design show significant improvements in power conversion efficiency by reducing series resistance, minimizing optical losses, and reducing power saturation. − Studies indicate significant optical loss in the p-region, whereas the optical loss in the n-type region remains minimal when the Si doping concentration is 1019 cm–3. To improve the efficiency of DUV laser diodes, we apply strategies from blue lasers, mainly by shifting the optical field toward the n-type region to reduce the light loss in the p-type region. This helps lower the threshold current and boost the output power. By adjusting the layer materials, thickness, and refractive index, and using multiple quantum wells (MQWs), we can better confine light in the active region. However, if the optical field becomes too asymmetric, light confinement weakens, leading to reduced photon generation and electron leakage. Therefore, optimizing the p-type electron blocking layer (p-EBL) is crucial to maintaining both efficient carrier injection and strong optical confinement. ,−

In this research, we utilize PICS3D (Photonic Integrated Circuit Simulator in 3D) to analyze all the optical and electrical properties of the proposed device structure while varying the aluminum (Al) percentage in its composition; n-waveguide (n-WG), n-cladding (n-CL), and p-type electron blocking layer (p-EBL). PICS3D is a complete, commercially available tool developed by Crosslight Software, Inc., with a complete set of tools for three-dimensional (3D) simulation of all aspects of laser diodes and wave-guided photonic devices. The software performs self-consistent calculations that couple two-dimensional carrier transport, optical waveguiding, thermal conduction, and optical gain with a longitudinal mode solver, thereby enabling accurate modeling of device performance under realistic operating conditions. Through Al composition modification, we effectively shift the optical field away from the n-type region, thereby reducing the optical loss. However, excessive asymmetry in the optical field distribution leads to a degraded optical confinement, suppressed modal gain, and increased electron leakage. Therefore, careful optimization of p-EBL is critical to maintaining efficient carrier recombination and achieving high-performance DUV lasing.

Device Structure and Simulation

AlGaN-based DUV LD 3D device structures are shown in Figure . The n-cladding layer has an Al composition from Al x Ga1–x N to Al0.4Ga0.6N with a thickness of 3 μm, a width of 1 μm, and a doping concentration of 3 × 1020 cm–3. A 4.5 μm-thick Al0.8Ga0.2N is then set as the n-type waveguide layer, with a Si doping concentration of 3 × 1019 cm–3. Next, the n-Al0.73Ga0.27N layer sandwiched between the waveguide and MQWs has a thickness of 0.15 μm with a doping concentration of 1 × 1019 cm–3. Then, two pairs of Al0.47Ga0.51N/Al0.48Ga0.52N MQW mounds having 0.01 μm-thick quantum wells and 0.003 μm-thick quantum barrier are designed. Then, a 0.01 μm-thick Al0.6Ga0.4N layer with the hole concentration of 2 × 1020 cm–3 is doped as the p-EBL. A 0.15 μm-thick Al0.8Ga0.2N layer with a hole concentration of 3.5 × 1020 cm–3 is set as p-WG. The p-CL Al0.8Ga0.2N has a thickness of 0.45 μm and a hole concentration of 3 × 1020 cm–3. Here, the x and y variables are studied in this work. A 0.008 μm p-type GaN layer with a hole concentration of 4 × 1021 cm–3 is set as a hole injection layer before the heavily doped 0.45 μm-thick GaN layer with p-type Mg doping concentration of 1 × 1021 cm–3. The current injection aperture is formed by setting a 0.008 μm-thick SiO2 insulation layer having a width of 0.5 μm. The optical and electrical characteristics of the device are analyzed through simulations using PICS 3D, a highly accurate and comprehensive tool that solves various equations and coordinates across the device. The simulation was conducted at a temperature of 300 K. Both mirror facets were assigned reflectivities of 0.3. Piezoelectric and spontaneous polarization effects were rigorously modeled following the approach suggested. Semiconductors exhibit their electrical properties governed by Poisson’s equation, the drift diffusion equation, and the current continuity equation, which controls the performance of both electrons and holes. Enhancing the electron blocking capability within the energy band offsets of the conduction and valence bands in the AlGaN/AlGaN active region has been optimized to a specific aluminum composition value of 0.58.

DUV laser diodes typically exhibit lower OCF values, primarily due to increased absorption loss.

These losses arise from the high aluminum composition in layers outside the quantum wells (QW’s), which negatively impact the optical confinement.

Researchers developing deep-ultraviolet (DUV) AlGaN LDs face a significant challenge to reduce absorption losses to confirm effective optical confinement inside the MQWs, thereby achieving a maximum OCF. For instance, LD-1 has an OCF of 16%, but because of the maximum aluminum content allowed in the electron blocking layer, the optical confinement efficiency is limited. Additionally, the p-GaN CT layer further reduced the efficiency of the optical confinement. As indicated by Tanaka et al., this problem is a very big hurdle toward improving the performance of DUV laser diodes.

The EBL plays an essential role in the transport of carriers as it limits the ability of electrons to travel from the MQWs to the p-type region and helps facilitate the injection of holes into the MQWs from the p-type layers. The optical and electronic characteristics of the quantum well can be controlled with electron overflow through adjustments made to the conduction-band (CB) energy, which the EBL effectively manages. Additionally, hole injection is affected by the valence band energy, which is also affected by the electron blocking layer (EBL). Al Composition in EBL & QWs LD-2

Table presents the composition details for LD-2, which has been optimized by modifying the aluminum composition in both the EBL and quantum wells (QWs) to enhance the performance of Al-based deep-ultraviolet (DUV) laser diodes. This design aims to deliver a better structure for epitaxial growth and investigation of III-nitride materials. Compared to LD-1, which used an EBL with an Al composition of 0.6%, LD-2 increases the Al content to 0.65 in the EBL. Additionally, the QW and barrier layers in LD-2 are composed of Al0.47Ga0.53N and Al0.58Ga0.52N, respectively. These modifications result in reduced electron leakage and enhanced hole injection efficiency. Furthermore, they help to minimize internal losses in the EBL and QW regions, leading to an improvement in the optical confinement factor (OCF) from 16% in LD-1 to 25% in LD-2.

Al Composition in EBL LD-3

In LD-3, this research utilized an alternative approach by having an aluminum composition in the EBL (Al0.75). Results are presented in Table , which summarizes the results of this phase of design. The results from the second phase of the design highlight that using a higher aluminum content in the EBL significantly influences device performance. This indicates that the EBL is a major factor in the overall performance of AlGaN-based DUV laser diodes. By using a high aluminum content EBL in LD-3, an increase in output power was observed to be 148.7 mW, an increased OCF of 24%, and the same emission wavelength as specified in the literature.

Impact of Al Composition on the EBL and QWs in LD-4

The schematic diagram of laser diode 4 (LD-4) is depicted in Figure . In addition to including an electron blocking layer made from Al0.8–0.82GaN, quantum barrier layers (QBs) were made from Al0.52Ga0.48N, and QWs were composed of Al0.44Ga0.56N. Table displays the summary of the performance data of laser diode 4 (LD-4). Due to improved aluminum grading in the EBL, QBs, and QWs, which have higher Al percentages than previously reported, there have been many positive changes in the electrical and optical performance of this device. Due to these improvements, the following output characteristics were achieved: a maximum output power of 156 mW, a slope efficiency that increased to 3.67 W/A, and an optical confinement that increased to 56.25%. This design offers several advantages, primarily by reducing losses at the common interfaces of multiple quantum wells (MQWs). Additionally, it improves carrier injection efficiency into the MQWs by increasing the hole energy with the compositional grading of the EBL and QWs with higher aluminum content. The UVC laser performance improvement results from reduced carrier leakage via optimized band alignment, enhancing recombination in the quantum wells. Enhanced optical confinement increases the mode overlap with the active region. These effects lower the threshold current density and boost the modal gain, improving device efficiency.

Conclusions

This is a simulation study that proves the improved performance of DUV LDs, especially in output power, optical confinement, and injection efficiency. The findings indicate that an increase in the Al content in the QWs, alongside the adoption of an AlGaN-based EBL, has a big role in enhancing efficiency in the devices. Individually, when combined, Mg-doped EBLs and increased Al content lead to increased carrier confinement, increased rates of stimulated recombination, and enhanced optical confinement in the active region. Consequently, the optimized DUV laser diode is outstanding, that is, its threshold current (I th) was 10.8 mA, and it had a maximum output power of 156 mW at an input current of 100 mA. These are enhanced by the fact that the augmentation of OCF, which complements these enhancements, is a clear indication that cautious material composition and structural design can lead to efficient and reliable operation of the DUV laser.

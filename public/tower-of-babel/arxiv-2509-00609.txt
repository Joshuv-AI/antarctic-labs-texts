Abstract
. We consider a one-phase free boundary problem involving fractional Laplacian ( − Δ ) s (-\Delta)^{s} , 0 < s < 1 0<s<1 . D. De Silva, O. Savin, and Y. Sire proved that the flat boundaries are C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} . We raise the regularity to C ∞ C^{\infty} , extending the result known for ( − Δ ) 1 / 2 (-\Delta)^{1/2} by D. De Silva and O. Savin.



1.
Introduction

Since the 1970s, free boundary problems have become one of the most important subjects in the field of elliptic PDE. One of the most famous problems is the classical
one-phase
Alt-Caffarelli or Bernoulli problem analyzed by H. Alt and L. Caffarelli in
[
AC81
]
. It is

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u\geq 0,&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
&\Delta u=0,&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega\cap\{u>0\},\\
&|Du|=1,&\quad&\text{ on }\Omega\cap\partial\{u>0\}.\end{aligned}\right.

It is a natural model for long-range interactions appearing in large systems of interacting particles, flame propagation, and probability theory. Those models raised the interest of mathematicians to analyze the regularity of the free boundary of
u

[
CRS10
,
FRRO24c
,
DL76
]
. Specifically, mathematicians aim to find conditions under which the free boundary is smooth. A series of papers
[
AC81
,
DSFS19
,
LZ23
,
KN77
]
proved that locally flat free boundaries are
C^{\infty}
. On the other hand,
[
AC81
,
Wei98
]
proved that the set of singular points on the free boundary is small in the sense of Hausdorff dimension. We refer to
[
CS05
,
FRRO22
,
Vel23
]
for further details in this field. We also refer to some important papers on the regularity of the solutions
[
ROS16
,
ROS17
,
FROS20
,
STV21a
,
STV21b
,
FRO22
,
FRRO24a
,
FRRO24b
]
for some Schauder-type regularity results. Note that the regularity of the free boundary and that of the solution are closely related.

In this paper, we focus on the one-phase
fractional Laplacian
problem. The fractional Laplacian operator is a standard model for
non-local
elliptic operators. Over the past two decades, non-local free boundary problems, especially those involving the fractional Laplacian operator, have attracted significant attention
[
CSS07
,
CRS10
,
CF13
,
CROS17
,
BFRO18a
,
BFRO18b
,
ARO20
,
ROTLW25
,
ROW25b
]
.

The fractional Laplacian operator is a Fourier multiplier in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with symbol is
|\xi|^{2s}
. Alternatively, we can define the operator by an integral representation:

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=c_{n,s}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(x^{\prime})}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{n+2s}}dx^{\prime},\quad 0<s<1,

where
c_{n,s}
is a constant depending on
n
and
s
, and
P.V.
denotes the Cauchy principal value. In this paper, we consider the one-phase Alt-Caffarelli problem for the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
,
0<s<1
, in an open domain

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, following
[
CRS10
,
DSSS14
,
EKP+21
]
,

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{0}\geq 0,\quad&\text{ in }&\Omega,\\
&(-\Delta)^{s}u_{0}=0,\;s\in(0,1),\quad&\text{ in }&\Omega\cap\{u>0\},\\
&\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{u_{0}(x+t\nu(x))}{t^{s}}=1,\quad&\text{ on }&F(u):=\Omega\cap\partial\{u>0\},\end{aligned}\right.

where
u_{0}
is defined on the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with prescribed values outside
\Omega
. In particular, the case
s=\frac{1}{2}
was studied in greater detail in
[
DR12
,
DS12
,
DSS12
,
DS15
]
.

To study the problem, we will use the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension. It plays a key role in our work. As noted earlier, the non-local nature of the fractional Laplacian precludes the direct application of classical local PDE techniques to the function
u_{0}
. However, the work of Caffarelli and Silvestre in
[
CS07
,
CS16
]
allows us to extend
u_{0}
from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Consider the following minimizing problem

(1.3)

\min\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}y^{\beta}|\nabla u(x,y)|^{2}dxdy:\;\beta=1-2s,u\Big|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}}=u_{0}\right\}.

The minimizer
u
satisfies the following equation:

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &L_{\beta}u=0,\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty),\\
&u=u_{0},\;\;\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\},\\
&(-\Delta)^{s}u_{0}(x)=d_{n,s}\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}\frac{u(x,y)-u(x,0)}{y^{1-\beta}},\end{aligned}\right.

where
d_{n,s}
is a constant depending on
n
and
s
, and
L_{\beta}
is defined by

L_{\beta}(u):=\text{div}(|y|^{\beta}\nabla u).

The operator
L_{\beta}
is local, allowing us to use standard elliptic techniques. This extension transforms the non-local problem for
u_{0}
into a local one for
u
. Consequently, it suffices to study the following boundary value problem:

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u\geq 0,\quad&\text{in}&\;B_{1},\\
&L_{\beta}u=0,\quad&\text{in}&\;B_{1}\cap\{u>0\},\\
&\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu^{s}}=1,\quad&\text{on}&\;F(u),\end{aligned}\right.

where
-1<\beta=1-2s<1
,
\nu
is the interior normal unit vector of
F(u)
, and

(1.6)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu^{s}}(x_{0}):=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{u(x_{0}+t\nu(x_{0}))}{t^{s}},\quad x_{0}\in F(u).

For general
0<s<1
, the optimal regularity of solutions has been established in
[
CRS10
,
ROW24
]
. The
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity for flat boundaries was established in
[
DR12
,
DSSS14
,
ROW25a
]
. The smallness of the singular set was proven in
[
EKP+21
]
. Specifically,
[
DS15
]
improved the regularity of flat free boundary for
s=\frac{1}{2}
to
C^{\infty}
.

As noted in
[
ROW25a
, Remark 1.2]
, the best-known regularity result for the free boundary when
s\neq\frac{1}{2}
is
C^{1,\alpha}
. A recent paper
[
BROW25
]
proved that
C^{2,\alpha}
free boundaries are
C^{\infty}
for general non-local operators. We state the
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity result by D. De Silva, O. Savin, and Y. Sire. Our work is based on that regularity result.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
DSSS14
, Theorem 1.1]
)
There exists a small constant
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
depending on
n
and
\beta
, such that if
u
is a viscosity solution to
(\ref{extension of fracional laplacian})
satisfying

(1.7)

\{x\in B_{1}:x_{n}\leq-\bar{\varepsilon}\}\subset\{x\in B_{1}:u(x,0)=0\}\subset\{x\in B_{1}:x_{n}\leq\bar{\varepsilon}\},

then
F(u)
is
C^{1,\alpha}
in
B_{1/2}
. Here
\alpha
depends on
n,\beta
.

In this paper, we improve the regularity of flat free boundary
from
C^{1,\alpha}
to
C^{\infty}
. It is our main Theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a small constant
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
depending on
n
and
s
, such that if
u_{0}
is a viscosity solution to
(\ref{fractional laplacian})
satisfying

(1.8)

\{x\in B_{1}:x_{n}\leq-\bar{\varepsilon}\}\subset\{x\in B_{1}:u_{0}(x,0)=0\}\subset\{x\in B_{1}:x_{n}\leq\bar{\varepsilon}\},

then the free boundary
F(u_{0})
is
C^{\infty}
in
B_{1/2}
.

Using Caffarelli-Silvestre extension, the fractional Laplacian can be extended to
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Throughout this paper, we mainly consider the extended problem. We will prove the extended version of Theorem
1.2
:

Theorem 1.3
.

There exists a small constant
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
depending on
n
and
\beta
, such that if
u
is a viscosity solution to
(\ref{extension of fracional laplacian})
satisfying

(1.9)

\{x\in B_{1}:x_{n}\leq-\bar{\varepsilon}\}\subset\{x\in B_{1}:u(x,0)=0\}\subset\{x\in B_{1}:x_{n}\leq\bar{\varepsilon}\},

then
F(u)
is
C^{\infty}
in
B_{1/2}
.

Remark 1.4
.

Our strategy for proving the smoothness of
C^{1,\alpha}
free boundaries consists of two steps. In step 1, we prove that
C^{1,\alpha}
free boundaries are
C^{2,\alpha}
. In step 2, we prove that
C^{2,\alpha}
free boundaries are
C^{\infty}
. Step 2 is also recently proved in the paper
[
BROW25
]
by B. Barrios, X. Ros-Oton, and Marvin Weidner by considering a general type of global operators. In this paper, we prove by using the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension. This approach is significantly different from the proof in
[
BROW25
]
. Our approach is inspired by the work of De Silva and Savin
[
DS12
,
DS15
]
. The techniques in
[
JN17
,
STV21a
,
STV21b
,
FRO22
,
FRRO24c
]
by M. Fall, X. Fernández-Real, Y. Jhaveri, R. Neumayer, X. Ros-Oton, Y. Sire, S. Terracini, and S. Vita provided key insights into the regularity theory.

Now we introduce our strategy in detail. In step 1, we prove following following the spirit of
[
DS12
]
. We use a sequence of paraboloids to approximate the free boundary. For each paraboloid, we use function
V_{\mathcal{S},a,b}
to approximate the solution
u
. During the approximation, we need to analyze the difference of the approximating function
V_{\mathcal{S},a,b}
and the solution
u
. For
\beta=0
, the limit is still a harmonic function with natural regularity estimate. For general
\beta
, we use Schauder-type estimates to derive analogous results.

In step 2, we prove following following the spirit of
[
DS15
]
. The function
\frac{u_{i}}{u_{n}}
is an extension of the derivatives of
g_{i}
in (
2.2
). We prove that it has the same regularity as
g
. The desired result will follow. For general
\beta\in(-1,1)
, we first analyze the linearized problem (
2.6
) and find polynomial approximation for the solution. That needs us to analyze the degenerate operator
L_{\beta}(U_{n}\cdot)
. The result together with the compactness allows to appoximate the function
\frac{u_{i}}{u_{n}}
.

We notice that step 1:
C^{1,\alpha}\to C^{2,\alpha}
is essential. The technique in step 2 cannot be applied to
C^{1,\alpha}
boundaries. To use the technique, we need to analyze the linearized problem with a Neumann boundary condition. However, as
[
DS15
]
indicates, the Neumann condition cannot be verified for
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary.

We follow the spirit of
[
DS12
,
DS15
]
to construct our proof. Compared to the proof for
\beta=0
, there are several difficulties. For
\beta=0
, the operator
L_{\beta}=\Delta
. It has been fully analyzed. For general
\beta\in(-1,1)
, we use the regularity theory about
L_{\beta}
. Roughly speaking, we will consider the regularity theory for the operator
\Delta+\frac{\beta}{y}\partial_{y}
since
L_{\beta}=|y|^{\beta}(\Delta+\frac{\beta}{y}\partial_{y})
. Secondly, harmonic functions remain harmonic under holomorphic map. Therefore to analyze a solution
w
to
\Delta(U_{n}w)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{P}
, we can consider the slice of the space
\{x^{\prime}=0\}
and map the 2D slit domain to the upper half plane with a holomorphic map. For general
\beta
, we will use boundary Harnack inequality in slit domain to handle it.

The paper is organized as following. In Section
2
, we recall some preliminary results and introduce some notations. In Sections
3
to
4
, we raise the regularity to
C^{2,\alpha}
. In Section
3
, we introduce a family of functions such that their free boundary are paraboloids. In Section
4
, we use the functions to approximate the solution
u
to prove the
C^{2,\alpha}
regularity. In Section
5
, we prove
C^{\infty}
regularity by analyzing the linearized problem to show that the free boundary
F(u)
has the same regularity as its derivatives. In Section
6
, we consider the linearized problem with
F(u)
being a codimension 2 hyperplane. In Section
7
, we provide some known results about this problem.

The regularity of flat free boundaries plays a key role in understanding Bernstein-type problems. We are preparing a follow-up paper based on
[
EFRY23
]
to analyze graphical solutions. This will help us find an equivalent formulation for Bernstein-type problems.

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we recall some definitions and gather some results about the fractional Laplacian problem. First, we introduce the notation used in this paper.

2.1.
Notation

In this paper, we consider the problem (
1.5
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. For
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we use
x^{\prime}
and
x
to denote its first
n-1
and
n
coordinates. We use
x_{n}
,
y
to denote the
n
-th,
(n+1)
-th coordinates and write
X=(x,y)=(x^{\prime},x_{n},y)
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, a ball with radius
r
and center
X
is denoted by
B_{r}(X)
. For simplicity we use
B_{r}
to denote
B_{r}(0)
. We use
B_{r}^{n}
to denote the
n
-dimensional ball
B_{r}\cap\{y=0\}
.

The half-plane
\mathcal{P}
and codimension-two space
\mathcal{L}
will frequently appear. We define them

(2.1)

\mathcal{P}=\{(x,y):x_{n}\leq 0,y=0\},\quad\mathcal{L}=\{(x,y):x_{n}=y=0\}.

For a continuous non-negative function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we define its free boundary
F(u)
as

F(u)=\partial_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}\cap\{u>0\})\cap B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\},

Specifically, we focus on
F(u)
satisfying:

(2.2)

F(u)=\{(x^{\prime},g(x^{\prime}),0):g(0)=0,g\in C^{1,\alpha},\nabla g(0)=0\},

where
e_{n}
is the unit vector in the
n
-th direction.

Throughout this paper,
n,\beta
are called universal constants. We say a variable is universal if it only depends on universal constants. We say a variable has universal bound if it is bounded by a universal constant.

In this paper, we consider a different version of regularity following
[
DS15
]
. Using the notation

x^{\mu}=x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\mu_{n}},\quad|\mu|=\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{n},\quad\mu_{i}\geq 0,\quad\|P\|:=\max|a_{\mu m}|.

We denote by

P(x,r)=a_{\mu m}x^{\mu}r^{m},\quad\deg P=k,

a polynomial of degree
k
in the
(x,r)
variables.

2.2.
Viscosity solution

Before considering viscosity solutions, we introduce the function
U:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
. Set

(2.3)

U(t,y)=(\frac{-t+\sqrt{t^{2}+y^{2}}}{2})^{s}.

For
t=x_{n}
,
U\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathcal{P})
, and
U(x_{n},y)
is a classical solution to (
1.5
). Furthermore, the free boundary
F(U)=\mathcal{L}
and
\{U=0\}=\mathcal{P}
. Therefore
U
is denoted by
half-plane solution
. We use
U_{t}
to denote
\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}
. The following equalities will be of use in this paper.

(2.4)

U_{t}=s\frac{U}{r},\;U_{tt}=\frac{sr-t}{r^{2}}U_{t}=\frac{s(sr-t)}{r^{3}}U,\;r=\sqrt{t^{2}+y^{2}}.

Now we construct the definition of viscosity solutions. It allows us to consider a solution to be less regular. To begin with, we state the definitions of touching functions following
[
DSSS14
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

For continuous functions
u
and
v
, we say
v

touches
u
from below
(resp. above) at
X_{0}\in B_{1}
if
u(X_{0})=v(X_{0})
and

u(X)\geq v(X)\quad(\text{resp.}\quad u(X)\leq v(X))\quad\text{in a neighborhood }O\text{ of }X_{0}.

If this inequality is strict in
O\setminus\{X_{0}\}
, we say that
v
touches
u
strictly by below (resp. above).

With the concept of touching functions, we give the definition of comparison sub/sup-solutions.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
v\in C(B_{1})
is a
comparison subsolution
to (
1.5
) if
v
is non-negative in
B_{1}
, even with respect to
\{y=0\}
,
C^{2}
in the set
\{v>0\}
, and satisfies

(i)
L_{\beta}v\geq 0\quad\text{in}\quad B_{1}\setminus\{y=0\},

(ii)
F(v)
is
C^{2}
and if
x_{0}\in F(v)
we have

v(x,y)=aU((x-x_{0})\cdot\nu(x_{0}),y)+o(|(x-x_{0},y)|^{s}),\quad\text{as}\quad(x,y)\to(x_{0},0),

with
a\geq 1
, where
\nu(x_{0})
denotes the unit normal at
x_{0}
to
F(v)
pointing toward
\{v>0\}
;

Moreover,
v
is a strict comparison subsolution if either (i) holds strictly or
a>1
. Similarly, one can define a (strict) comparison supersolution.

Finally, we are able to give the definition of viscosity solutions to end this subsection following
[
CC95
,
DSSS14
]
. The idea of the definition is that solutions cannot be touched from below/above by sub/sup-solutions. It needs less regularity of
F(u)
and uniform convergence implies that the limit is close to a solution. Throughout this paper, all solutions are viscosity solutions unless otherwise specified.

Definition 2.3
.

A function
u
is a
viscosity solution
to (
1.5
) if
u
is continuous, non-negative in
B_{1}
, and satisfies:

(i)
u
is locally
C^{1,1}
in
B_{1}\cap\{u>0\}
, even with respect to
\{y=0\}
and solves (in the viscosity sense)

L_{\beta}u=0\quad\text{in}\quad B_{1}\setminus\{y=0\}.

(ii) Any (strict) comparison subsolution (resp. supersolution) cannot touch
g
from below (resp. by above) at a point
X_{0}=(x_{0},0)\in F(u)
.

For this equation, we need to consider a different type of regularity. Now we give the definition of
f\in C^{k,\alpha}_{xr}
. For usually regularity, we use polynomials of
(x.y)
to approximate the function. Using
P(x,r)
to approximate the function induces
C^{k,\alpha}_{xr}
regularity.

Definition 2.4
.

For
F(u)
satisfying (
2.2
), we say that a function
f:B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
is pointwise
C^{k,\alpha}

in the
(x,r)
-variables at
0\in F(u)
, and write
f\in C^{k,\alpha}_{xr}(0)
, if there exists a (tangent)
polynomial
P_{0}(x,r)
of degree
k
such that

f(X)=P_{0}(x,r)+O(|X|^{k+\alpha}).

We define
\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{xr}(0)}
as the smallest constant
M
such that

\|P_{0}\|\leq M\quad\text{and}\quad|f(X)-P_{0}(x,r)|\leq M|X|^{k+\alpha},

for all
X
in the domain of definition.
Furthermore, we say that
f\in C^{k,\alpha}_{xr}(K)
for
K\subset F(u)
if there exists a constant

M
such that
f\in C^{k,\alpha}_{xr}(Z)
for all
Z\in K
, and

\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{xr}(Z)}\leq M\quad\text{for all }Z\in K.

The smallest such constant
M
is denoted by
\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{xr}(K)}
.

2.3.
The Linearized Problem

In this subsection, we introduce here the linearized problem associated to (
1.5
). To improve the regularity of the free boundary
F(u)
, we will show that
\frac{u_{i}}{u_{n}}
, which extends the derivatives of
F(u)
, has the same regularity as
F(u)
. They are solutions to the linearized problem.

For
u
being a solution to (
1.5
) satisfying (
2.2
), the linearized problem associated to (
1.5
) is

(2.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &L_{\beta}(u_{n}w)=0,\quad&\text{in }&B_{1}\cap\{u>0\},\\
&|\nabla_{r}w|=0,\quad&\text{on }&B_{1}\cap F(u).\end{aligned}\right.

Here
|\nabla_{r}w|
denotes the radial derivative, defined as

|\nabla_{r}w|(X_{0}):=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,y)\to(0,0)\\
r^{2}=t^{2}+y^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{w(X_{0}+t\nu+ye_{n+1})-w(X_{0})}{r},

for
\nu
being the normal unit of
F(u)
at
X_{0}
.

A special case of (
2.5
) is when
u
is half-plane solution and
F(u)=\mathcal{L}
. In this case,
u
is substituted by the half-plane solution
U
. To deal with that, we need to consider the viscoisity type solution. We introduce the following problems following the spirit of
[
DSSS14
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

We say
w
is a viscosity solution to

(2.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &L_{\beta}(U_{n}w)=0,\quad&\text{in }&B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{P},\\
&|\nabla_{r}w|=0,\quad&\text{on }&B_{1}\cap\mathcal{L},\end{aligned}\right.

if
w\in C^{1,1}_{loc}(B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{P})
,
w
is even with respect to
\{y=0\}
, and
w
satisfies:

(i)
L_{\beta}(U_{n}w)=0
in
B_{1}\setminus\{y=0\}
;

(ii) Let
\phi
be a continuous function around
X_{0}=(c,0,0)\in B_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}
satisfying

\phi(X)=\phi(X_{0})+a(X_{0})\cdot(x-c)+b(X_{0})r+O(|x-c|^{2}+r^{1+\alpha}),

for some
\alpha>0
. If
b(X_{0})>0
, then
\phi
cannot touch
w
from below at
X_{0}
, and if
b(X_{0})<0
then
\phi
cannot touch
w
from above at
X_{0}
.

The solution to (
2.6
) has a polynomial expansion at points in
B_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}
. We have the following theorem allowing use to use polynomials to approximate the solution following the spirit of
[
DSSS14
, Theorem 6.1]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Given a boundary data
\bar{h}\in C(\partial B_{1})
even with respect to
\{y=0\}
,
|\bar{h}|\leq 1
, there exists a unique classical solution
h
to (
2.6
) such that
h\in C(\bar{B}_{1})
is even with respect to
\{y=0\}
and satisfies
h=\bar{h}
on
\partial B_{1}
. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
Q
being order
k
polynomial in
x^{\prime}
and
P
being order
k-1
polynomial in
x,r
such that

|h-(Q(x^{\prime})+rP(x,r))|\leq C_{o}|X|^{k+1}\text{ in }B_{1/2},

for
C_{o}
depending on universal constants and
k
. Furthermore,
\|Q\|,\|P\|
have universal bounds and
L_{\beta}(U_{n}(Q+rP))=0
in
B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{P}
.

In particular, for
n=2
we have

(2.7)

\Big|h(X)-\Big(h(0)+\xi_{0}\cdot x^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}{x^{\prime}}^{T}M_{0}x^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2s}\Big(\frac{a_{0}}{2}r^{2}+b_{0}rx_{n}\Big)\Big)\Big|\leq C_{o}|X|^{3}\text{ in }B_{1}\setminus\mathcal{P},

for some
a_{0},,b_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},M_{0}\in S^{(n-1)\times(n-1)}
with

|\xi_{0}|,|a_{0}|,|b_{0}|,\|M_{0}\|,C_{o}\leq C(n,\beta),

and

3.
A Family of Functions

In this section, we introduce a family of functions
V_{\mathcal{S},a,b}
following the spirit of
[
DS12
]
by D. De Silva and O. Savin. They serve as approximate solutions to (
1.5
) with free boundary
\mathcal{S}
. We mainly study the case
\mathcal{S}
being a paraboloid.

Given a surface
\mathcal{S}=\{x_{n}=g(x^{\prime})\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any
X=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we set

(3.1)

\displaystyle U_{0}(X)=U(t,y),

\displaystyle t=\text{sgn}(x_{n}-g(x^{\prime}))\text{dist}((x,0),\mathcal{S}),

\displaystyle v_{a,b}(t,y):=(1+\frac{a}{4}r+\frac{b}{2}t)U_{0}(t,y),

\displaystyle r^{2}=t^{2}+y^{2},

\displaystyle V_{\mathcal{S},a,b}(X):=v_{a,b}(t,y),

\displaystyle X=(x,y).

Next we introduce the concept of domain variation functions. It will be used to measure the distance between
v
and
U
, especially for
v=V_{\mathcal{S}}
close to
U
. For a function
v
close to the half-plane solution
U
, we construct the the domain variation function
\tilde{v}
. In Section
4
we will construct a sequence of functions approximating the solution
v
. Domain variations will be an important role in the approximating process.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let
v
be a continuous non-negative function in
\overline{B_{\rho}}
.
We follow
[
DS12
]
to define the multivalued map
\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{R}
via the formula

(3.2)

U(X)=v(X-\epsilon wn),\quad\forall w\in\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}(X).

We call
\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}
the
\epsilon
-
domain variation
associated to
v
. By abuse of notation, from now on we write
\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}(X)
to denote any of the values in this set.

If

(3.3)

U(X-\epsilon e_{n})\leq v(X)\leq U(X+\epsilon e_{n})\quad\text{in }B_{\rho}\text{ for }\epsilon>0,

then we have
\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}(X)\neq\emptyset
for
X\in B_{\rho-\epsilon}\setminus\mathcal{P}
and
|\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}|\leq 1
.
Moreover, if
v
is strictly monotone in the
e_{n}
-direction in
B_{\rho}(v)\cap\{v>0\}
, then
\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}
is single-valued.

Domain variations also have the comparison principle. It indicates that domain variation away from the free boundary will control its behavior near the free boundary in some way.

Lemma 3.2
.

(
[
DSSS14
, Lemma 3.1]
)
Let
u,v
be respectively a solution and a subsolution to (
1.5
) in
B_{2}
, with

strictly increasing in the

-direction in
B_{2}(v)\cap\{v>0\}
. Assume that

) in
B_{2}
for
\epsilon>0
small and that
\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}
is defined in
B_{2-\epsilon}\setminus\mathcal{P}
and bounded. If

(3.4)

\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}+c\leq\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}\quad\text{in}\quad(B_{3/2}\setminus\overline{B_{1/2}})\setminus\mathcal{P},

then

(3.5)

\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}+c\leq\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}\quad\text{in}\quad B_{3/2}\setminus\mathcal{P}.

In fact, the behavior of free boundary can imply the property of the domain variation. It can be understood as following: the regularity of free boundary we derive the regularity of solution near it. For a weaker condition of free boundary, we are supposed to find a similar result for the solution. We have:

Lemma 3.3
.

(
[

, Lemma 2.10]
)
Assume

solves (
1.5
). Given any
\epsilon>0

U(X-\delta\epsilon e_{n})\leq u(X)\leq U(X+\delta\epsilon e_{n})\quad\text{in }B_{\delta}.

We use the notation

(3.6)

V_{M,\xi^{\prime},a,b}(X):=V_{\mathcal{S},a,b}(X),

for

Definition 3.4

For

\displaystyle:=\{V_{M,\xi^{\prime},a,b}:\|\text{M}\|,|\xi^{\prime}|,|a|,|b|\leq\delta\},

Proposition 3.5

Let
V=V_{M,\xi^{\prime},a,b}\in\mathcal{V}_{\delta}
, with
\delta\leq\delta_{0}(n,\beta)

in

\Delta_{x}t(x)=-\kappa(x),\;\text{$\kappa(x)$ being the mean curvature of the parallel surface to $\mathcal{S}$ at $x$.}

For the case

Applying (
3.8

\displaystyle L_{\beta}V(X)

\displaystyle=|y|^{\beta}((\frac{a}{2s}+b)U_{t}-(\partial_{t}v_{a,b})\kappa(x)),

for

depending only on

\displaystyle(\Delta+\frac{\beta}{y}\partial_{y})_{(t,y)}v_{a,b}=(\frac{a}{2s}+b)U_{t}.

Since

\displaystyle|(\partial_{t}v_{a,b}-U_{n})(t,y)|\leq C\delta U_{t}.

Therefore, we obtain the following estimate

for

universal. If

∎

4.

We drop

Step 1:

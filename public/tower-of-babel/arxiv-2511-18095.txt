Quantum and classical analyses of intertwined phase transitions in odd-mass Nb isotopes
\firstname A. \lastname Leviatan \fnsep

Abstract
Quantum phase transitions (QPTs) in odd-mass Nb isotopes are investigated in the framework of the interacting boson-fermion model with configuration mixing. A quantum analysis reveals a Type I QPT (gradual shape-evolution within the intruder configuration) superimposed on a Type II QPT (abrupt crossing of normal and intruder states), thus demonstrating the occurrence of intertwined QPTs. A classical analysis highlights the implications for the single particle motion in the deformed field generated by the even-even Zr cores.



1
Introduction

Quantum phase transitions (QPTs) are
qualititative changes in the
properties of a physical system induced by variation of
parameters in the Hamiltonian. Such structural changes
are manifested empirically in nuclei as transistions
between shapes and can be categorized into two types.
The first, denoted as

Type I

Diep80
, is a shape-phase
transition within a single configuration, as encountered
in the neutron number 90 region
Cejnar2010
.
The second, denoted as
Type II

Frank2006
,
is a phase transition involving a crossing of different
configurations, as encountered
in nuclei near (sub-) shell closure
Heyde11
.
In most cases, the strong mixing between the
configurations obscures the individual QPTs.
However, if the mixing is small,
the Type II QPT can be accompanied by a distinguished
Type I QPT within each configuration separately.
Such a scenario, referred to as intertwined QPTs,
occurs in the even-even Zr (Z=40)
isotopes
Gavrielov2019
;
Gavrielov2020
;
Gavrielov2022
.
In the present contribution, we discuss both quantum
and classical analyses of a similar scenario
in the adjacent odd-even Nb (Z=41)
isotopes
gavleviac22
;
Gav23
;
LevGav25
.

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt\hat{H}_{B}(\xi_{B})=\hat{H}_{\rm b}(\epsilon^{B}_{d},\kappa_{B},\chi)+\kappa^{\prime}_{B}\,\hat{L}\cdot\hat{L}+\Delta_{B}+\hat{H}_{\rm f}+\hat{V}_{\rm bf}~,

Classical analysis

A geometric interpretation for the
IBFM-CM
LevGav25
;
Ramos25
,
is obtained
by constructing from the Hamiltonian (
3
)
an enlarged potential matrix
of order
(2j+1)\times(2j+1)
, in the basis

\{|j,m_{1};\beta,\gamma;N\rangle,|j,m_{2};\beta,\gamma;N+2\rangle\}
,
with
m_{1},m_{2}=j,j~-~2,\ldots,-(j-1)
,

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\Omega_{N}(y;\xi_{A},\xi_{B},\omega)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Omega_{A}(y;\xi_{A})&\Omega_{AB}(y;\omega)\\
\hline\cr\Omega_{AB}(y;\omega)&\Omega_{B}(y;\xi_{B})\end{array}\right]~.

The matrix depends on
y\equiv(\beta,\gamma)
and on
the parameters of the Hamiltonian, Eq. (
4
),

\xi_{A}=(\epsilon^{A}_{d},\kappa_{A},\chi,\epsilon^{A}_{j},A,\Gamma,\Lambda)
,

\xi_{B}=(\epsilon^{B}_{d},\kappa_{B},\chi,\kappa^{\prime}_{B},\Delta_{B},\epsilon^{B}_{j},A,\Gamma,\Lambda)
,
\omega
and
N
.
The sub-matrix
\Omega_{A}(\beta,\gamma;\xi_{A})

acts in the

|j,m_{1};y;N\rangle
sector,

\Omega_{B}(\beta,\gamma;\xi_{B})
acts in the

|j,m_{2};y;N+2\rangle
sector and

\Omega_{AB}(\beta,\gamma;\omega)
connects the two
sectors. For
\hat{H}_{\rm f}=\hat{V}_{\rm bf}=0
,
the above potential matrix reduces to that proposed
for the IBM-CM
Vargas04
.
Diagonalization of the matrix

\Omega_{N}(y;\xi_{A},\xi_{B},\omega)
produces
the eigen-potentials.

For
\gamma\!=\!0
,
the matrix
\Omega_{N}(\beta;\xi_{A},\xi_{B},\omega)

of Eq. (
4
), can be transformed into
a simple block-diagonal form

\{M_{K=j}(\beta),\,M_{K=j-2}(\beta),\,\ldots,\,M_{K=-(j-1)}(\beta)\}
, where

M_{K}(\beta)
stands for a
2\times 2
matrix in the
states
|\Psi_{A;K}\rangle\equiv|j,K;\beta;N\rangle
and

|\Psi_{B;K}\rangle\equiv|j,K;\beta;N+2\rangle
,

\displaystyle M_{K}(\beta)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}E_{A;K}(\beta)&W(\beta)\\
W(\beta)&E_{B;K}(\beta)\end{array}\right]~.

Using Eq. (
10
), the entries of
M_{K}(\beta)
are
given by,

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptE_{A;K}(\beta)=E^{(N)}_{K}(\beta;\xi_{A})~,

(17a)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptE_{B;K}(\beta)=E^{(N+2)}_{K}(\beta;\xi_{B})+6\kappa^{\prime}_{B}\,\tfrac{(N+2)\beta^{2}}{1+\beta^{2}}+\Delta_{B}~,

(17b)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptW(\beta)=\tfrac{\sqrt{(N+2)(N+1)}}{1+\beta^{2}}\omega\,(1+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\beta^{2})~.

(17c)

Introducing the quantities,

\displaystyle\delta_{K}(\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{B;K}(\beta)-E_{A;K}(\beta)~,

(18a)

\displaystyle R_{K}(\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{\delta_{K}(\beta)}{2W(\beta)}~,

(18b)

the eigen-potentials then read,

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptE_{\pm,K}(\beta)=\tfrac{1}{2}\Sigma_{K}(\beta)\pm\big|W(\beta)\big|\sqrt{1+[R_{K}(\beta)]^{2}}~,\quad

(19)

where
\Sigma_{K}(\beta)=E_{A;K}(\beta)+E_{B;K}(\beta)
,
and the corresponding eigenvectors are

\displaystyle|\Psi_{-,K}(\beta)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\,|\Psi_{A;K}\rangle+b\,|\Psi_{B;K}\rangle~,

(20a)

\displaystyle|\Psi_{+,K}(\beta)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-b\,|\Psi_{A;K}\rangle+a\,|\Psi_{B;K}\rangle~.

(20b)

The ratio of mixing amplitudes and probabilities satisfy,

\displaystyle\frac{b}{a}=\left[R_{K}(\beta)\mp\sqrt{1+[R_{K}(\beta)]^{2}}\right]~,

(21a)

\displaystyle b^{2}=1-a^{2}=\tfrac{1}{1+\Bigl[\,R_{K}(\beta)\,\pm\sqrt{1+[R_{K}(\beta)]^{2}}\,\Bigr]^{2}}~,

(21b)

where the upper (lower)
sign applies for
W(\beta)>0

[
W(\beta)<0]
.

The ensemble of
eigen-potentials
\{E_{-,K}(\beta),\,E_{+,K}(\beta)\}
,
Eq. (
19
), portray the change in energies of the odd
fermion as a function of deformation
\beta
,
in the presence of coupled bosonic cores.
The states

\{\Psi_{-,K}(\beta),\,\Psi_{+,K}(\beta)\}
,

The value of

, Eq. (

),
determines the character of the

R_{K}(\beta)\!=\!0

|R_{K}(\beta)|\!<<\!1
,

|R_{K}(\beta)|\!>>\!1

|R_{K}(\beta)|\!=\!\infty

Two scenarios are relevant for the subsequent discussion.
(a) Maximal mixing occurs for
\delta_{K}(\beta)\!=\!0
,

i.e.

E_{A;K}(\beta)=E_{B;K}(\beta)\equiv E^{(0)}_{K}(\beta)

(b) No mixing occurs for

In this case, for

5
Concluding remarks

We have investigated the evolution of structure in
odd-mass Nb isotopes in the framework of the
IBFM-CM. A quantum analysis discloses the effects
on spectral properties of an abrupt crossing of
normal and intruder states (Type II QPT)
accompanied by a gradual evolution from spherical- to
deformed-core shapes and transition from weak to
strong coupling within the intruder configuration
(Type I QPT). The pronounced presence of both types of
QPTs demonstrates the occurrence of
intertwined QPTs in these odd-mass nuclei.
A classical analysis, based on an extended
matrix coherent states formalism,
clarifies the effects of deformation and
configuration-mixing on the single particle motion, and
captures essential features of the quantum results.

Optimal error estimates of the diffuse domain method for semilinear parabolic equations
Yuejin Xu

Abstract
In this paper, we mainly discuss the convergence behavior of diffuse domain method (DDM) for solving semilinear parabolic equations with Neumann boundary condition defined in general irregular domains. We use a phase-field function to approximate the irregular domain and when the interface thickness tends to zero, the phase-field function will converge to indicator function of the original domain. With this function, we can modify the problem to another one defined on a larger rectangular domain that contains the targer physical domain. Based on the weighted Sobolev spaces, we prove that when the interface thickness parameter goes to zero, the numerical solution will converge to the exact solution. Also, we derive the corresponding optimal error estimates under the weighted L 2 L^{2} and H 1 H^{1} norms. Some numerical experiments are also carried out to validate the theoretical results.

\left\{\begin{split}&u_{t}=\nabla\cdot(A\nabla u)+f(t,u),\quad&&\bm{x}\in D,\quad 0\leq t\leq T\\
&u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\quad&&\bm{x}\in D,\\
&(A\nabla u)\cdot\bm{n}=g(t,\bm{x}),\quad&&\bm{x}\in\partial D,\quad 0\leq t\leq T,\end{split}\right.

2
The diffuse domain method

First of all, some standard notations are proposed for later provement. For a given open bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and nonnegative integer
s
, denote
H^{s}(\Omega)
as the standard Sobolev spaces on
\Omega
with norm
\|\cdot\|_{s,\Omega}
and semi-norm
|\cdot|_{s,\Omega}
, and the corresponding
L^{2}
-inner product is
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Omega}
. The corresponding norm of space
H^{s}(\Omega)
is
\|\cdot\|_{s,\Omega}
and
\|v\|_{k,\infty,\Omega}={\rm ess}\sup_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq k}\|D^{\bm{\alpha}}v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
for any function
v
such that the right-hand side term makes sense, where
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d})
is a multi-index and
|\bm{\alpha}|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}
.
H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
is the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.
Moreover, given two quantities
a
and
b
,
a\lesssim b
is the abbreviation of
a\leq Cb
, where the hidden constant
C
is positive and independent of the mesh size;
a\eqsim b
is equivalent to
a\lesssim b\lesssim a
.

In the following part, we always suppose that
u_{0}\in H^{2}(D)
,
A\in H^{1}(D)
,
g\in L^{2}(\partial D)
. Then by the variational formula, we can reformulate (
1.1
) as: find
u\in H^{1}(D)
and
u_{t}\in L^{2}(D)
such that

\left\{\begin{split}&(u_{t},v)+a(u,v)=\ell(v),\qquad\forall\,v\in H^{1}(D),\ 0\leq t\leq T,\\
&u(0,\bm{x})=u_{0}(\bm{x}),\end{split}\right.

(2.1)

where the bilinear operator
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric positive and defined by

a(w,v)=\int_{D}A\nabla w\cdot\nabla v\,{\rm d}\bm{x},\qquad\forall\,w,v\in H^{1}(D),

(2.2)

and the linear operator
\ell(\cdot)
is defined by

\ell(v)=\int_{D}f(u)v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{\partial D}gv\,{\rm d}\sigma,\qquad\forall\,v\in H^{1}(D),

(2.3)

Since the exact domain
D
is irregular, it’s difficult to obtain the above integrals directly. We turn to use the DDM to convert the exact integrals on irregular domains to the weighted integrals on regular domains
[
15
]
. Since we have introduced the algorithm in details in our previous work
[
26
]
, we will only show the DDM method briefly with some minor revision.

First, let us introduce a signed distance function
d_{D}(\bm{x})=\,{\rm dist}(\bm{x},D)-\,{\rm dist}(\bm{x},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D)
,
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, the domain

, the boundary
\partial D
and the extended domain
D_{s}
(

) can be reformulated as
D=\{\bm{x}\,|\,d_{D}(\bm{x})<0\}
,
\partial D=\{\bm{x}\,|\,d_{D}(\bm{x})=0\}
,
D_{s}=\{\bm{x}\,|\,d_{D}(\bm{x})<s\}
, respectively. Next, we will denote
\epsilon>0
as a small interface thickness parameter, and
S
as a smooth monotonic transition function, such as the sigmoidal function. For simplicity, in this paper, we take

S(s)=\tanh(3s).

(2.4)

We can easily obtain that
S(\cdot/\epsilon)
converges to the sign function as
\epsilon
tends to zero, and hence, the phase-field function

\omega^{\epsilon}(\bm{x}):=(1+\varphi^{\epsilon}(\bm{x}))/2

converges to the indicator function
\chi_{D}
of
D
. Denote the
\epsilon
-tubular neighborhood of
\partial D
by
\Gamma_{\epsilon}=D_{\epsilon}\setminus\overline{D_{-\epsilon}}
. Obviously,

\omega_{\epsilon}=1,\quad\forall\ \bm{x}\in D_{-\epsilon},\quad\omega_{\epsilon}\in(0,1),\quad\forall\ \bm{x}\in\Gamma_{\epsilon},\quad\omega_{\epsilon}=0,\quad\forall\ \bm{x}\in D_{\epsilon}^{C}.

In order to generate the spatial mesh conveniently, one usually further fixes a larger rectangular domain
\Omega
such that
D\subset D_{\epsilon}\subset\Omega
in practice. Fig.
1
describe the geometric relation among the original physical domain
D
, the
\epsilon
-extension domain
D_{\epsilon}
and the covering rectangular domain
\Omega
. Instead of computing the integrals over irregular domain
D
exactly, DDM method will use a weighted averaging of the integrals over regular domain
\Omega
. According to the definition of
\omega_{\epsilon}
, we can know that
\omega_{\epsilon}\approx 0
, so the integrals over
\Omega
in fact will be converted to that over
D_{\epsilon}
. Therefore, for convenience, our theoretical analysis is only based on the

.

Sketch of an example geometry:

Following the similar arguments of
[
15
]
, according to the Fubini’s theorem and co-area formula, we can derive the approximation for the volume integral and boundary integral. For any integrable function
h:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\int_{D}h(\bm{x})\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{D_{\epsilon}}h(\bm{x})\omega_{\epsilon}(\bm{x})\,{\rm d}\bm{x},

(2.5a)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D}h(\bm{x})\,{\rm d}\sigma(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\approx\int_{D_{\epsilon}}h(\bm{x})\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}(\bm{x})\right|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}.

(2.5b)

Let us define the weighted

space on

,

, associated with the phase-field function

as follows:

L^{p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})=\left\{v\,\Big|\,\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v|^{p}\,\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}=\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v|^{p}\,\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\omega_{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Based on the weighted spaces

, the weighted Sobolev spaces are consequently defined as

W^{s,p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})=\left\{v\in L^{p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})\,|\,D^{\bm{\alpha}}v\in L^{p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}),\ \forall\,|\bm{\alpha}|\leq s\right\},

and

with the norm

\|v\|_{W^{s,p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}=\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\sum\limits_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq s}\left|D^{\bm{\alpha}}v\right|^{p}\,{\rm d}\omega_{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

,

,

,

Based on the definition of weight function

, it’s obvious that the weighted function

3.1
On the weighted Sobolev space

The following three theorems (Theorems
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
)
have readily been proved in

]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Trace Theorem)
.

Let
\epsilon_{0}>0
be sufficiently small and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
\epsilon\in[0,\epsilon_{0}]
and
v\in W^{1,p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
, there holds

\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v|^{p}|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\lesssim\|v\|_{W^{1,p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{p}.

Theorem 3.2

(Embedding Theorem)
.

Suppose that
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}]
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
\alpha>0
be the constant satisfies that for all
s\in(0,2)
,
\zeta_{1}s^{\alpha}\leq(1+S(s-1))/2\leq\zeta_{2}s^{\alpha}
for some constants
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}>0
. Then the following embeddings are continuous

W^{1,p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}),\quad 1\leq q\leq p_{\alpha}^{*},\ q<\infty,

where

p_{\alpha}^{*}=\frac{p(n+\alpha)}{n+\alpha-p}
for
p<n+\alpha
and
p_{\alpha}^{*}=\infty
for
p\geq n+\alpha.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C
independent of
\epsilon
such that for any
v\in W^{1,p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
, there holds

\|v\|_{L^{q}}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})\lesssim\|v\|_{W^{1,p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}.

Theorem 3.3

(Poincare-Friedrichs-type inequality)
.

Suppose that
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}]
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and the extended domain
D_{\epsilon}
be connected. Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
\epsilon
such that for any
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
and
v\in W^{1,p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
, there holds

\|v\|_{L^{p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{p}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v|^{p}|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}.

0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
w\in H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})\cap L^{\infty}(D_{\epsilon})
, and
v\in H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
. Then

\left|\int_{D_{\epsilon}}wv\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}wv\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})},

where the hidden constant is independent of
\epsilon
,
u
and
v
.

We then have the following lemma on the locally-Lipschitz continuity of
f(u)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that the function
f
satisfies Assumption
3.1
, and the exact solution
u\in L^{(}0,T;H^{1}(D))
and its extension to
D_{\epsilon}
fulfills (
3.3a
) in Assumption
3.3
. Then
f
is locally-Lipschitz continuous with respect to weighted norms in a strip along the extended exact solution
u
, i.e., for any fixed constant
R>0
,

\left\|f(t,v)-f(t,w)\right\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\left\|v-w\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})},

(3.4)

for ant
t\in[0,T]
and
v,w\in H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
satisfying

\max\left\{\left\|v-u\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})},\left\|w-u\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\right\}\leq R,

(3.5)

where the hidden constant may depend on
R
.

Proof.

It follows from (
3.3a
) and (
3.5
) that

\max\left\{\left\|v\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})},\left\|w\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\right\}\lesssim 1+R.

(3.6)

By the Lagrange mean value theorem and (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\left\|f(t,v)-f(t,w)\right\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(t,\xi)(v-w)\right\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\left(1+|\xi|^{p}\right)^{2}|v-w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\left(1+|v|^{p}\right)^{2}|v-w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\left(1+|w|^{p}\right)^{2}|v-w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x},

where
\xi(\bm{x})=\theta(\bm{x})v(\bm{x})+(1-\theta(\bm{x}))w(\bm{x})
for some
\theta(\bm{x})\in[0,1]
. It’s obvious that

\displaystyle\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\left(1+|v|^{p}\right)^{2}|v-w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}=

\displaystyle\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v|^{2p}|v-w|\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+2\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v|^{p}|v-w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

(3.7)

\displaystyle+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v-w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}.

It suffices to show below the bound of the first term in the right-hand side of (
3.7
) since the bound of the other two terms can be derived similarly.

Case I:
d=1
. In this case,
0<p<\infty
. We choose
q_{1},q_{2}
satisfying

\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=1,\quad 1\leq q_{1}<\infty,\quad 1\leq q_{2}<\infty,2pq_{1}\geq 1.

By using Holder’s inequality, embedding theorem for weighted Sobolev space (see Theorem
3.2
) with
\alpha=1
and (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v|^{2p}|v-w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v|^{2pq_{1}}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v-w|^{2q_{2}}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2pq_{1}}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2p}\left\|v-w\right\|_{L^{2q_{2}}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2p}\|v-w\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}\lesssim\|v-w\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}.

Case II:
d=2
. In this case,
0<p\leq 2
. According to the embedding theorem for weighted Sobolev space (see Theorem
3.2
) with
\alpha=1
, we have
H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
, where
1\leq q\leq 6
. Select proper
q_{1},q_{2}
satisfying

\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=1,\quad 1\leq q_{1}<\infty,\quad 1\leq q_{2}\leq 3,\quad 1\leq 2pq_{1}\leq 6.

And with direct manipulation, we can know that these conditions hold if
(q_{1},q_{2})
satisfies the conditions

\max\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{1}{2p}\right\}\leq q_{1}\leq\frac{3}{p},\quad 1\leq q_{2}\leq 3.

Noting that
0<p\leq 2
, we can find the existence of such a pair
(q_{1},q_{2})
in terms of the above conditions. Following the similar arguments for deriving (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v|^{2p}|v-w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v-w|^{2q_{2}}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}}\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v|^{2pq_{1}}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\|v-w\|_{L^{2q_{2}}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}\|v\|_{L^{2pq_{1}}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v-w\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}\|v\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2p}\lesssim\|v-w\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}.

Case III:
d=3
. In this case,
0<p\leq 1
. Following the similar derivation of Case II above, we have
H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
, where
1\leq q\leq 4
. Select proper
q_{1},q_{2}
satisfying

\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=1,\quad\leq 1\leq q_{1}<\infty,\quad 1\leq q_{2}\leq 2,\quad 1\leq 2pq_{1}\leq 4.

It’s obvious that these conditions hold if
(q_{1},q_{2})
satisfies

\max\left\{2,\frac{1}{2p}\right\}\leq q_{1}\leq\frac{2}{p},\quad 1\leq q_{2}\leq 2.

Noting
0<p\leq 1
, we can always find a pair
(q_{1},q_{2})
satisfies the above conditions and in the same way of (
3.9
)

\int_{D_{\epsilon}}|v|^{2p}|v-w|^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\lesssim\|v-w\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}.

(3.10)

Finally, the combination of (
3.7
)-(
3.10
) leads to (
3.4
).

∎

4
Error analysis

To illustrate the convergence of diffuse domain method, let us combine the (
2.1
) and (
2.6
) together, then we can get the error equation as follows: for all
v\in H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
,

\begin{split}&\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}+a^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u,v)\\
&\quad=[\left(u_{t},v\right)-\left(u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}]+[a(u,v)-a^{\epsilon}(u,v)]+[\ell^{\epsilon}(v)-\ell(v)].\end{split}

(4.1)

Next we will derive the error estimates of
u^{\epsilon}-u
in the
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
norms.

4.1
Optimal error estimate in the
L^{2}
-norm

Theorem 4.1

(Error estimate in the
L^{2}
norm)
.

Suppose that
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
g\in H^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
,
\kappa\leq A(\bm{x})\leq\kappa^{-1}
for all
\bm{x}\in D_{\epsilon}
with some constant
\kappa>0
, and
f
satisfies Assumption
3.1
and
3.2
. Assume the exact solution
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(D))
and its extension to
D_{\epsilon}
fulfills Assumption
3.3
. Then we have

\begin{split}\|u^{\epsilon}(t)-u(t)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\epsilon^{2},\quad\forall\,0\leq t\leq T,\end{split}

(4.2)

where the hidden constant is independent of
\epsilon
.

Proof.

Let us analyze
\left(u_{t},v\right)-\left(u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}
,
a(u,v)-a^{\epsilon}(u,v)
and
\ell^{\epsilon}(v)-\ell(v)
respectively for any
v\in H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
.
Recalling the definition of
a^{\epsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
a(\cdot,\cdot)
, since
\omega_{\epsilon}
will vanish on
\partial D_{\epsilon}
, we can derive the following formula by using the integration by part,

\displaystyle a(u,v)-a^{\epsilon}(u,v)

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)\omega_{\epsilon}v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A\nabla u\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{\partial D}\bm{n}\cdot\left(A\nabla u\right)v\,{\rm d}\sigma-\int_{D}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm div}\left(A\nabla u\right)\omega_{\epsilon}v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}\,{\rm div}\left(A\nabla u\right)v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla u\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{\partial D}gv\,{\rm d}\sigma,

where in the last equality we use the fact that
\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}=-\bm{n}\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|
. By Theorem
3.4
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{D}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)v\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}.

(4.4)

Then, inserting (
4.4
) to (
4.3
), we get that

\displaystyle a(u,v)-a^{\epsilon}(u,v)\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

(4.5)

\displaystyle-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla u\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{\partial D}gv\,{\rm d}\sigma.

As for the estimate of
\left(u_{t},v\right)-\left(u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}
, by using the conclusion of Theorem
3.4
, we obtain

\left(u_{t},v\right)-\left(u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|u_{t}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}.

(4.6)

In the following, we first discuss the estimate of
\int_{D_{\epsilon}}f(u^{\epsilon})v\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}f(u)v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}
. Since
f
is locally-Lipschitz continuous (see Lemma
3.2
), we can derive

\displaystyle\int_{D_{\epsilon}}f(u^{\epsilon})v\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}f(u)v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{\epsilon}}f(u^{\epsilon})v\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}f(u)v\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}f(u)v\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}f(u)v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|f(u^{\epsilon})-f(u)\right\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|f(u)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|f(u)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

where the last two inequality uses the conclusion of Theorem
3.4
.

With the estimate of (
4.7
), we can derive the bound of
\ell^{\epsilon}(v)-\ell(v)
,

\displaystyle\ell^{\epsilon}(v)-\ell(v)=

\displaystyle\int_{D_{\epsilon}}f(u^{\epsilon})v\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D}f(u)v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}gv\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{\partial D}gv\,{\rm d}\sigma

(4.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|f(u)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}gv\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{\partial D}gv\,{\rm d}\sigma.

Combining (
4.5
)-(
4.8
) together, we get

\displaystyle\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}+a^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u,v)

(4.9)

\displaystyle=\left(u_{t},v\right)-\left(u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}+a(u,v)-a^{\epsilon}(u,v)+\ell^{\epsilon}(v)-\ell(v)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|u_{t}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\,{\rm div}\left(A\nabla u\right)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla u\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|v\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+\left\|u^{\epsilon}-u\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|f(u)\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\int_{D_{\epsilon}}gv|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}.

Applying with Theorem
3.5
, we know that

\displaystyle\int_{D_{\epsilon}}gv|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla uv|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\left(\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla u-g\right)v|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla u-g\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}.

Therefore, we have by (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) that

\displaystyle\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t},v\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}+a^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u,v)

(4.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|u_{t}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\,{\rm div}\left(A\nabla u\right)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla u-g\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|f(u)\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle+\left\|u^{\epsilon}-u\right\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}.

Since
|\Gamma_{\epsilon}|\lesssim\epsilon
, applying
v=u^{\epsilon}-u
to (
4.1
) gives

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}+\|\nabla(u^{\epsilon}-u)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\|u_{t}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\|\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\|\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla u-g\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle+\|f(u)\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\left\|u^{\epsilon}-u\right\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\Big)\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{4}+\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}+\|\nabla(u^{\epsilon}-u)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}.

Thus

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2}\lesssim\epsilon^{4}+\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2},

which implies

\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}.

The proof is completed.

∎

4.2
Optimal error estimate in the
H^{1}
norm

The derivation of following lemma has been presented in Lemma 4.2 in
[

]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
,

, and
f
satisfies Assumption
3.2
. Assume the exact solution

(4.12)

where
C
is a constant independent of
\epsilon
.

Theorem 4.2

(Error estimate in the
H^{1}
-norm)
.

Suppose that
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
g\in L^{\infty}(D_{\epsilon})\cap H^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})
,
\kappa\leq A(\bm{x})\leq\kappa^{-1}
for all
\bm{x}\in D
with some constant
\kappa>0
, and
f
satisfies Assumption
3.1
and
3.2
. Assume the exact solution
u\in H^{2}(D_{\epsilon})
for all
0\leq t\leq T
and its extension to
D_{\epsilon}
fulfills Assumption
3.3
. Then we have

\|u^{\epsilon}(t)-u(t)\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\epsilon,\quad\forall\ 0\leq t\leq T,

(4.13)

where the hidden constant is independent of
\epsilon
.

Proof.

First, we insert
v=2(u^{\epsilon}_{t}-u_{t})
into (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle

\displaystyle 2\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t},u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)_{D_{\epsilon},\omega_{\epsilon}}+2a^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}-u,u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\left(2\left(u_{t},u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)-2\left(u_{t},u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)_{D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon}}\right)+\left(2a(u,u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})-2a^{\epsilon}(u,u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\right)

\displaystyle+\left(2\int_{D_{\epsilon}}f(u^{\epsilon})\left(u^{\epsilon}_{t}-u_{t}\right)\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-2\int_{D}f(u)\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(2\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-2\int_{\partial D}g(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t})\,{\rm d}\sigma\right)=:\rm{I}_{1}+\rm{I}_{2}+\rm{I}_{3}+\rm{I}_{4}.

Since
u^{\epsilon}_{t}
and
u_{t}
, as well as
u^{\epsilon}
and
u
, have the same regularity, according to Lemma
3.1
and
L^{2}
-norm error estimate of diffuse domain method (see Theorem
4.1
), we can easily obtain that

\displaystyle\rm{I}_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\left\|u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\epsilon^{3}.

(4.15)

Since the weighted function
\omega_{\epsilon}
will vanish on the boundary of
D_{\epsilon}
, we can reformulate the term
\rm{I}_{2}
with the variational formula,

\displaystyle\rm{I}_{2}=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\partial D}\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla u\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\,{\rm d}\sigma-2\int_{D}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle-2\left(\int_{\partial D_{\epsilon}}\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla u\omega_{\epsilon}\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\,{\rm d}\sigma-\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u\omega_{\epsilon})\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(2\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-2\int_{D}\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u)\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(2\int_{\partial D}g\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\,{\rm d}\sigma+2\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A\nabla u\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)=:\rm{II}_{1}+\rm{II}_{2}.

Similar to the estimation of (
4.15
), according to Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\rm{II}_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\left\|\,{\rm div}(A\nabla u_{t})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}.

(4.16)

Combining the term
\rm{II}_{2}
and
\rm{I}_{4}
together, by using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the definition of
\omega_{\epsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle\rm{II}_{2}+\rm{I}_{4}

\displaystyle=2\int_{D_{\epsilon}}g\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}+2\int_{D_{\epsilon}}A\nabla u\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

(4.17)

\displaystyle=2\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\left(g-\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla u\right)\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|\,{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\left|\omega_{\epsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{D_{\epsilon}}\left|\left(g-\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla u\right)\left|\nabla\omega_{\epsilon}\right|/\omega_{\epsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\left\|\bm{n}\cdot A\nabla u-g\right\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}.

As for the estimation of
\rm{I}_{3}
, due to the local continuity of
f
, we have

\displaystyle\rm{I}_{3}=

\displaystyle\left(2\int_{D_{\epsilon}}f(u^{\epsilon})\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-2\int_{D_{\epsilon}}f(u)\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)

(4.18)

\displaystyle+\left(2\int_{D_{\epsilon}}f(u)\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\omega_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}\bm{x}-2\int_{D}f(u)\left(u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\right)\,{\rm d}\bm{x}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f(u^{\epsilon})-f(u)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u^{\epsilon}-u\|_{H^{1}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}\|u_{t}^{\epsilon}-u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}+\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}-\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(D_{\epsilon};\omega_{\epsilon})}^{2},

where the last three inequality uses the conclusion of Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
4.1
. Inserting (
4.15
)-(
4.18
) to (
4.14
), we can easily derive the conclusion.

∎

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we studied the approximation error of the diffuse domain method for solving a class of semilinear parabolic equations, imposed with Neumann boundary conditions defined in general irregular domains. Optimal error estimates with respect to weighted
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms are successfully derived. Some numerical examples including ill-posed Allen-Cahn equations are presented to verify the theoretical results. The numerical method and corresponding error analysis framework developed in this paper also naturally enable us to further investigate adapted finite element methods
[
16
,
10
,
9
]
for solving PDEs in regions with corner points with solid theoretical support.

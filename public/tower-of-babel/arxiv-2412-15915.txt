The hadronic decay of vector charmonium
*

Abstract
The extraction of decay parameters using lattice techniques is a computationally expensive task, requiring several volumes and group irreps to relate the spectrum on a lattice simulation to the infinite volume scattering. In this project we employ an alternative method based on a narrow-width approximation to extract the hadronic mixing ⟨ D ¯ D | ψ ( 3770 ) ⟩ \innerproduct*{\APD\PD}{\Pgya} , which is needed to compute the decay Γ ( ψ ( 3770 ) → D ¯ D ) \Gamma({\mathit{{\psi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{\mathit{{\left({3770}\right)}}}\to\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{D}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{D}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}) between the second excited state of vector charmonium and a pair of D \mathit{{D}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}} -mesons in a p p -wave. We carry out our lattice simulations on two cls ensembles at m π ∼ 440 MeV m_{\pi}\sim$440\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}$ and a ∼ 0.066 fm a\sim$0.066\text{\,}\mathrm{fm}$ and obtain results compatible with experiment. Furthermore, we interpret our results analytically using the P 0 3 {}^{3}P_{0} quark model. MS-TP-24-37

Introduction

In these proceedings, we study the hadronic decay
{\mathit{{\psi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{\mathit{{\left({3770}\right)}}}\mspace{1mu}\to\mspace{1mu}\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{D}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{D}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}

{\mathit{{\psi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{\mathit{{\left({3770}\right)}}}
into a pair of
\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{D}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{D}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
mesons in a
p

{\mathit{{\psi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{\mathit{{\left({3770}\right)}}}
and takes place close to threshold
[
1

. The standard method to predict a decay width on the lattice relates the spectrum in several finite volumes to the infinite-volume scattering phase-shifts
[
2
,
3
,
4
,

. For example, the authors of
[

,
7

have employed this procedure to predict the decay width of several charmonium states, including
{\mathit{{\psi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{\mathit{{\left({3770}\right)}}}
.
With our exploratory study carried out in
[
8
]
and summarized here, we follow an alternative method developed by Michael and Pennanen
[
9
]
that employs ratios of correlators to estimate the decay width. The method, explained in
section

2
, relies implicitly on a narrow-width approximation, which is satisfied for our decay of interest. Alternative approaches to study hadronic decays include the
\mathcal{K}
-matrix approach
[
10
]
, which assumes unitarity and analyticity of the underlying scattering amplitudes, but it also requires
\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}\to\text{hadrons}
data from experiment in the energy region around the
{\mathit{{\psi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{\mathit{{\left({3770}\right)}}}
pole to fix its free parameters. Several works compute the decay width of
{\mathit{{\psi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{\mathit{{\left({3770}\right)}}}
using this method, see
[
11
]
for instance. In our project, we compare our results with the
{}^{3}P_{0}
quark model
[
12
,
13
]
. This is a quark rearrangement model which assumes the initial charm quarks are spectators, preserving their quantum numbers and momentum, and a pair of light quarks is created from the vacuum. Then, the four quarks rearrange themselves to obtain the correct quantum numbers of the final state.

Our work predicts the hadronic mixing,
x_{31}\equiv\innerproduct*{\APD\PD}{\Pgya}
, employing the method described in
[
9
]
. We establish the connection to the decay width and the energy shift of the states employing non-relativistic quantum mechanics. In particular, we consider the
qcd
Hamiltonian
H_{0}=\ket*{\Pgya}\bra*{\Pgya}+\ket*{\APD\PD}\bra*{\APD\PD}
and the perturbation
H_{1}=\ket*{\APD\PD}\bra*{\Pgya}+\ket*{\Pgya}\bra*{\APD\PD}
. On the lattice, we have a two-level system in the
cm
(
cm
) frame of the charmonium state and the energy shift of the states is obtained solving the eigenvalue problem

\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta&x_{31}\\
x^{*}_{31}&\Delta\end{pmatrix},

(1)

where
\Delta=(m_{\psi}-E_{\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{D}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{D}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}})/2
. Including the first-order perturbation, and assuming the charmonium state is heavier, the energies are shifted to

m_{\psi}\to m_{\psi}+\sqrt{\absolutevalue{x_{31}}^{2}+\Delta^{2}},\qquad E_{\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{D}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{D}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}\to E_{\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{D}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{D}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}-\sqrt{\absolutevalue{x_{31}}^{2}+\Delta^{2}}.

(2)

Note that in the case of degenerate states with
H_{0}
, the energy shift is given by the hadronic mixing alone.
The situation in experiment is quite different. Indeed, the decay channel is closed in lattice simulations, as there cannot be a decay in a finite box, and even kinematics may not allow it depending on the quark masses. This means that we can study the effect of the mixing explicitly.
This allows us to test the effects of different theoretical methods. Meanwhile, the situation in experiment corresponds to the decay of a discrete state to a continuum of final states, giving a Breit-Wigner distribution in momentum space and an energy shift in the same direction to both initial and final states.
In particular, we will directly use the hadronic mixing extracted on the lattice to obtain the decay width using Fermi’s golden rule. Theoretically, the situation in experiment for non-relativistic states can be understood using
[
14

B-cell maturation antigen targeted PET/CT imaging in multiple myeloma: a first-in-human study
Authors: Tingfei Gu, Zhao Chen, Tianyao Wang, Yujun Dong, Lei Kang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA)-targeted positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) represents a promising tool for the management of multiple myeloma, enabling noninvasive and quantitative evaluation across different disease stages. This novel technique specifically addresses two major diagnostic challenges: the limited specificity of conventional 18F-FDG PET/CT and the focal sampling constraints of bone marrow biopsies. In this prospective study, a 68Ga-labeled nanobody-based BCMA tracer demonstrated improved detection compared with 18F-FDG PET/CT, effectively visualizing para-medullary, extra-medullary and medullary lesions, as well as minimal residual disease (MRD). The tracer also reflected dynamic changes in BCMA expression associated with treatment response, underscoring its potential utility for guiding BCMA-targeted interventions, particularly in the era of chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) treatment. These preliminary findings support the potential of BCMA immunoPET as a comprehensive, noninvasive modality for MM evaluation and warrant validation in larger, prospective clinical trials.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13045-025-01758-3.

To the editor

Multiple myeloma (MM) remains a therapeutic challenge owing to its complex clonal architecture and heterogeneous bone marrow infiltration patterns [1]. The current diagnostic framework for MM is constrained by two major limitations. First, conventional 18F-FDG PET/CT exhibits suboptimal sensitivity, as myeloma cells often demonstrate variable and low glycolytic activity, leading to false-negative results [2]. Additionally, this method suffers from limited specificity due to frequent false-positive uptake in inflammatory or infectious conditions [3, 4]. Second, bone marrow biopsies provide only focal sampling of this systemic malignancy, lacking the comprehensive evaluation of the heterogeneous lesions throughout the body. To address these diagnostic challenges, we developed a novel PET/CT tracer targeting B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) [5], a plasma cell-specific marker [6] that enables whole-body imaging while preserving cellular-level specificity.

This prospective imaging study (registered at ClinicalTrials.gov under NCT06717113; approved by the Peking University First Hospital Ethics Committee, No. PekingUFH-MM-BC1) enrolled 13 patients between April and September 2025. Detailed study design is described in the supplementary methods, and baseline patient characteristics are summarized in Table S1.

In clinical evaluations, this Gallium-68 (68Ga)-labeled nanobody-based BCMA tracer demonstrated improved lesion visualization compared with conventional 18F-FDG PET/CT across various MM presentations. Head-to-head comparisons revealed that BCMA PET prominently identified medullary lesions in MM patient prior to 18F-FDG and 68Ga-CD38 PET/CT [7] while showing minimal background uptake in non-MM control patients, indicating high tracer specificity (Fig. 1A and B). The technique also displayed promising sensitivity, particularly for detecting minimal residual disease (MRD). For instance, in Patient No.2, a case of newly diagnosed MM (NDMM) who had achieved complete response (CR) by conventional clinical criteria, but BCMA PET found residual focal uptake at a lower intensity than baseline (e.g., left iliac bone SUVmax decreased from 4.6 to 2.9; red arrows, Fig. 1A, C and F, S2). By contrast, 18F-FDG PET/CT showed diffusely skeletal metabolic activity post-treatment, making it difficult to differentiate between nonspecific reactive changes and residual malignant lesions. Bone biopsy confirmed BCMA PET positive findings, showing interstitial infiltration of 4% malignant plasma cells, and next-generation sequencing (NGS) identified an aberrant clonal rearrangement in the IGK CDR3 region. Furthermore, the sum of SUVmax values derived from BCMA PET provided better differentiation between control and MM patients than 18F-FDG PET (Fig. 1D and E, S1).

Additionally, serial 68Ga-BCMA PET/CT assessments reliably tracked disease dynamics across diverse lesion types, including para-medullary (Fig. 2A and B), extra-medullary (Fig. 2C), and medullary (Fig. 2A and D, S3) sites, providing an objective basis for therapeutic monitoring. In Patient No. 8, a relapsed case, partial response (PR) was considered after one cycle of Dara-DP-PACE chemotherapy by indicating multiple BCMA-expressed sites (i.e. pelvis SUVmax 4.9, blue arrows), which prompted the subsequent use of BCMA-targeted therapy. Following autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) and BCMA-directed CAR-T treatment, the patient achieved BCMA-negative expression throughout the body which was verified by next-generation flow (NGF) MRD test. Quantitative BCMA uptake closely paralleled serum free light chain levels and flow cytometry results throughout treatment (Fig. 2E, F and G).

Collectively, these findings support the potential of BCMA-targeted PET/CT as a comprehensive imaging modality for MM management, from diagnosis through treatment assessment and post-therapy surveillance. This approach provides greater disease specificity than 18F-FDG PET and overcomes the inherent sampling limitations of biopsy. Its noninvasive, whole-body imaging capability addresses an important unmet need in current MM response monitoring. BCMA PET/CT appears particularly promising for MRD detection, which currently relies on invasive, technically demanding methods such as NGS or NGF [8]. Given the significance prognostic value of MRD positivity [9], BCMA PET/CT may ultimately contribute to more precise and individualized disease management. Moreover, it holds potential for guiding BCMA-targeted therapies, especially chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy [6, 10].

Nonetheless, this imaging approach has several limitations. Relatively high blood pool activity was observed, which might reflect circulating soluble BCMA (sBCMA) levels known to correlate with disease burden [11]. Additionally, the tracer demonstrates high renal accumulation, a characteristic frequently observed with nanobody-based agents due to their small molecular size and rapid clearance. Although this necessitates expert interpretation, it remains a clinically manageable consideration given the relatively low incidence of renal involvement in MM [12].

In summary, this study introduces BCMA immunoPET as a noninvasive, quantitative, and whole-body imaging tool for MM, with potential applications in diagnosis, treatment response assessment, and post-therapy surveillance. These preliminary findings warrant validation in larger, prospective clinical studies.

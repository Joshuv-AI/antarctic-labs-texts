Total decay rate of a muon bound to a light nucleus
A. Czarnecki, A. O. Davydov, M. Y. Kaygorodov

Abstract
We revisit the total decay rate of a muon being in the ground state of a Coulomb potential with atomic charge numbers 4 ≤ Z ≤ 9 4\leq Z\leq 9 . The discrepancy between the perturbative ( α ​ Z ) 2 (\alpha Z)^{2} result of [Phys. Rev. 119 , 365 (1960)] and the fully relativistic partial-wave calculation of [At. Data Nucl. Data Tables 54 , 165 (1993)] for oxygen ( Z = 8 Z=8 ) is shown to originate from insufficient convergence of the partial-wave series in the latter work. Our accurate relativistic calculations restore agreement with the perturbative α ​ Z \alpha Z expansion and indicate a negative sign for the next-order ( α ​ Z ) 3 (\alpha Z)^{3} correction.



I
Introduction

Muonic atoms, in which a negatively charged muon is bound to a nucleus, are used in the determination of weak-interaction parameters
Kammel 2021
and nuclear-structure properties
Krauth et al. 2021
.
Muonic atoms also offer sensitive probes of physics beyond the Standard Model
Uesaka et al. 2025
;
Matias et al. 2025
.
In the near future, several experiments
Miscetti 2025
;
Aoki et al. 2025
;
Nishiguchi 2025
will employ muonic atoms to improve sensitivity to the charged-lepton-flavor-violating (CLFV) process of coherent muon-to-electron conversion in the nuclear field,
\mu^{-}N\to e^{-}N
, by up to four orders of magnitude.

The signature of this neutrinoless conversion process is a mono-energetic electron emitted with an energy
E_{e}\simeq m_{\mu}-E_{b}
, where
m_{\mu}
and
E_{b}
are, respectively, the mass and the binding energy of the muon.
Another branch of the decay which does not violate flavor conservation is the standard muon decay
\mu^{-}N\longrightarrow e^{-}N+\bar{\nu}_{e}+\nu_{\mu}
, where the initial-state muon is bound to a nuclear potential and the final-state electron is unbound.
This process, also referred to as “muon decay in orbit” (DIO), is characterized by a continuous energy distribution of the emitted electron and extends up to the characteristic energy of the electron in the CLFV conversion process.
Consequently, the DIO process constitutes the physical background to the muon-to-electron conversion, and theoretical understanding of the DIO properties directly impacts the sensitivity of these CLFV searches.

The DIO spectrum has been the subject of extensive theoretical study.
For systems of experimental interest, the influence of the nuclear
recoil
Czarnecki et al. 2011
, leading quantum-electrodynamical
effects
Szafron and Czarnecki 2016
, finite nuclear
size
Heeck et al. 2022
, nuclear deformation and electron screening
Kaygorodov et al. 2025
on the electron spectrum was analyzed.
A refined effective field theory to describe the DIO process was
recently developed in
Ref.
Fontes and Szafron 2025a
;
Fontes and Szafron 2025b
;
Fontes and Szafron 2025c
.

The integrated DIO electron spectrum, i.e. the
total
decay rate, provides a valuable global check on the frameworks that underlie theoretical calculations.
For a point-like nucleus, for a massless electron, and for the ground state of the bound muon, the total DIO rate,
\Gamma
, was calculated in Ref.
Überall 1960
using perturbation theory accurate up to the order
(\alpha Z)^{2}

\frac{\Gamma}{\Gamma_{0}}=1-\frac{(\alpha Z)^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha Z)^{3}.

(1)

Thus, the total DIO rate differs only slightly from the decay rate of
a free muon,

\Gamma_{0}=G_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}m_{\mu}^{5}/(192\pi^{3})

Webber et al. 2011
, where
G_{\mathrm{F}}

is the Fermi constant, and units
\hbar=c=1
are implied throughout
the paper. The
(\alpha Z)^{2}
term arises from an interplay between
two partially canceled effects. On the one hand, the muon binding
energy reduces the phase space of the final particles. On the other
hand, the Coulomb potential enhances the magnitude of the electron
wave function in the nuclear region where the muon wave function is
localized.

In contrast, fully relativistic calculations of the total DIO rate based on a partial-wave expansion
Watanabe et al. 1993
found that for oxygen (
Z=8
) and for the ground state of the muon
1-\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}=0.006
.
This value is substantially larger, by roughly a factor of three, than
(\alpha Z)^{2}/2\simeq 0.002
obtained from Eq. (
1
).
However, the expected next-to-leading
(\alpha Z)^{3}
correction,

(\alpha Z)^{3}\sim 2\times 10^{-4}
, is more than an order of magnitude
too small to account for this difference and cannot explain the deviation between the perturbative
\alpha Z
expansion and the fully-relativistic framework based on the partial-wave expansion.

The inconsistency for
Z=8
between these two different methods motivates us to reexamine the total DIO rate for light nuclei.
In this work we compute the rate for a muon being in the ground state of the Coulomb potential for atomic numbers
4\leq Z\leq 9
.
We treat the nucleus as static and point-like (finite-size effects are negligible compared with the discrepancy mentioned above) and describe the initial-state muon and the final-state electron with Dirac-Coulomb wave functions, following the relativistic approach of Refs.
Watanabe et al. 1993
;
Watanabe et al. 1987
.
Nuclear recoil, while relevant near the endpoint of the electron spectrum, does not affect the total decay rate at our target precision
Czarnecki et al. 2011
.
Our primary goals are to determine whether the discrepancy originates from an intrinsic limitation of the
\alpha Z
expansion, a numerical issue in the earlier partial-wave calculation, or another source, and to establish a reliable total DIO rate benchmark for light muonic atoms.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
II
introduces the theoretical methods and presents the partial–wave expansion of the total decay rate of a bound muon in the ground state.
Section
III
contains details of the numerical calculations, shows results for the total DIO rate, and compares them with previous calculations.
Finally, Section
IV
summarizes our conclusions.

II
Theoretical method

To obtain an expression for the muon decay rate, we employ the formalism presented in Ref.
Aslam et al. 2020
.
Treating the nucleus as a source of a classical electromagnetic field, the three-body decay
(Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]\,\bar{\nu}_{e}\,\nu_{\mu}
can be represented as two sequential two-body decays,

(Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]A
and
A\to\bar{\nu}_{e}\nu_{\mu}
, where
A
is a fictitious neutral massive spin-1 boson carrying the lepton–flavor quantum numbers of the neutrino pair.
The notation
(Z\mu^{-})
denotes that the muon is bound to the nuclear
potential and
[Ze^{-}]
means that the electron is unbound in the field of the nuclear potential.
The factorization of the
(Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]\,\bar{\nu}_{e}\,\nu_{\mu}
total decay rate averaged over the initial states of the muon and summed over the final states of the electron, neutrino, and antineutrino can be written as

\Gamma\!\left((Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]\,\bar{\nu}_{e}\nu_{\mu}\right)=\frac{12288\,\pi^{2}\Gamma_{0}}{g^{4}m_{\mu}^{5}}\int_{0}^{m_{A,\max}}\!\mathrm{d}m_{A}\;m_{A}^{2}\,\Gamma\!\left((Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]A\right)\,\Gamma\!\left(A\to\bar{\nu}_{e}\nu_{\mu}\right),

(2)

where
g
is the weak-coupling constant,
m_{A}
is the invariant mass of the boson
A
,
m_{A,\max}=E_{\mu}-E_{e}
, with
E_{\mu}
and
E_{e}
being the total energies of the initial-state muon and the final-state electron, respectively.
An advantage of this factorization is that all atomic effects (and hence the
\alpha Z
dependence of
\Gamma((Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]\,\bar{\nu}_{e}\,\nu_{\mu})
) are encoded in the total two-body decay rate

\Gamma((Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]A)
, whereas
\Gamma(A\to\bar{\nu}_{e}\nu_{\mu})
is universal and does not depend on properties of the nuclear field.
The total decay rate
\Gamma(A\to\bar{\nu}_{e}\nu_{\mu})
can be evaluated analytically in the limit of massless neutrinos, see e.g. Ref.
Aslam et al. 2020
.
Substituting the expression for
\Gamma(A\to\bar{\nu}_{e}\nu_{\mu})
and introducing

z\equiv\frac{m_{A}}{m_{\mu}},\qquad 0\leq z\leq z_{\max}=\frac{E_{\mu}-E_{e}}{m_{\mu}}\,,

(3)

we obtain
Aslam et al. 2020

\Gamma\!\left((Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]\,\bar{\nu}_{e}\nu_{\mu}\right)=\frac{256\pi\,\Gamma_{0}}{g^{2}m_{\mu}}\int_{0}^{z_{\max}}\!\mathrm{d}z\;\Gamma\!\left((Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]A\right)\,z^{3}.

(4)

Equation (
4
) is valid for the bound-state muon as well as for the free muon.
The two-body total decay rate
\Gamma((Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]A)
of the muon decay into the electron and the fictitious boson
A
can be written as

\Gamma\!\left((Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]A\right)=\frac{1}{64\pi^{5}}\!\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\vec{p}_{e}}{p_{e}E_{e}}\;\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\vec{k}}{k_{0}}\;\delta\!\left(E_{\mu}-E_{e}-k_{0}\right)\bigl|{\cal M}_{(Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]A}\bigr|^{2},

(5)

where
\bigl|{\cal M}_{(Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]A}\bigr|^{2}
is the square of the absolute value of the amplitude of the process averaged over the initial states of the muon and summed over the final states of the electron and the fictitious boson
A
,
p_{e}^{\nu}=(E_{e},\vec{p}_{e})
with
p_{e}\equiv|\vec{p}_{e}|
and

k^{\nu}=(k_{0},\vec{k})
are the four-momenta of the final-state electron and the boson
A
, respectively.

Taking into account that the nuclear potential is spherically symmetric, it is natural to evaluate
\Gamma((Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]A)
entering Eq. (
5
) using a relativistic central-field approximation for the muon and electron.
The ground-state muon wave function is given by

\Psi_{\mu_{\mu}}(\vec{r})=\frac{1}{r}\begin{pmatrix}G(r)\,\Omega_{-1\mu_{\mu}}(\hat{r})\\[4.0pt]
i\,F(r)\,\Omega_{1\mu_{\mu}}(\hat{r})\end{pmatrix},

(6)

where
G
and
F
are, respectively, the large and small radial components of the muon wave function,
\Omega_{\kappa\mu}
is the spherical spinor
Varshalovich et al. 1988
,
\hat{r}\equiv\vec{r}/r
,
\kappa
is the relativistic angular quantum number (
\kappa=-1
for the ground state), and
\mu
is the projection of the total angular momentum.
The muon radial wave functions are normalized to unity,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl[G^{2}(r)+F^{2}(r)\bigr]\,dr=1.

(7)

The wave function of the final-state electron is given by

\psi_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}\mu_{e}}(\vec{r})=\frac{1}{r}\begin{pmatrix}g_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}(r)\,\Omega_{\kappa_{e}\mu_{e}}(\hat{r})\\[4.0pt]
i\,f_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}(r)\,\Omega_{-\kappa_{e}\mu_{e}}(\hat{r})\end{pmatrix},

(8)

g_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}
and
f_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}
are, respectively, the large and small radial components of the electron wave function normalized in the energy scale,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl[g_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}(r)\,g_{E^{\prime}_{e}\kappa_{e}}(r)+f_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}(r)\,f_{E^{\prime}_{e}\kappa_{e}}(r)\bigr]\,dr=\delta(E_{e}-E^{\prime}_{e}).

(9)

Introducing the leptonic current,

J_{\alpha}(\vec{q})=\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\vec{r}\,e^{-i\vec{q}\cdot\vec{r}}\,\psi^{\dagger}_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}\mu_{e}}(\vec{r})\,\gamma_{0}\gamma_{\alpha}P_{L}\,\Psi_{\mu_{\mu}}(\vec{r}),

(10)

where
P_{L}=(1-\gamma_{5})/2
, the averaged-summed absolute value squared of the amplitude
\bigl|{\cal M}_{(Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]A}\bigr|^{2}
can be written as

\bigl|{\cal M}_{(Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]A}\bigr|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu_{\mu}}\sum_{\kappa_{e}\mu_{e}}\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,J^{\alpha}(\vec{k})\,J^{\dagger\beta}(\vec{k})\,\left(-g_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}}{m_{A}^{2}}\right),

(11)

where the tensor in parentheses stems from the polarization sum
for a massive spin-1 boson with four-momentum
k
and

g_{\alpha\beta}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
is the Minkowski metric tensor.

To obtain the final expression for
\Gamma\!\left((Z\mu^{-})\to[Ze^{-}]\,\bar{\nu}_{e}\nu_{\mu}\right)
, we represent the effective leptonic current
J^{\alpha}
using the multipole expansion for
e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}}

Varshalovich et al. 1988
, sum over the total angular momentum projections
\mu_{\mu}
and
\mu_{e}
, contract the Lorentz indices in Eq. (
11
), and finally integrate over the angle of
\vec{k}
in Eq. (
5
). These steps bring us to
the partial-wave expansion of the total decay rate for the ground state of the muon:

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Gamma}{\Gamma_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\kappa_{e}}\dfrac{8}{m_{\mu}^{5}}\left(2j_{e}+1\right)\!\int_{0}^{E_{\mu}}\!\mathrm{d}E_{e}\!\int_{0}^{E_{\mu}-E_{e}}\!k^{2}\mathrm{d}k

(12)

\displaystyle\times\Biggl\{\left[\left(E_{\mu}-E_{e}\right)^{2}-k^{2}\right]\!\left(\dfrac{|S_{\kappa_{e}L}^{0}|^{2}}{L\left(L+1\right)}+\dfrac{|S_{\kappa_{e}L}^{-1}|^{2}}{L\left(2L+1\right)}+\dfrac{|S_{\kappa_{e}L}^{+1}|^{2}}{\left(L+1\right)\left(2L+1\right)}\right)

\displaystyle+k^{2}\!\left(|S_{\kappa_{e}L}|^{2}+\dfrac{|S_{\kappa_{e}L}^{+1}+S_{\kappa_{e}L}^{-1}|^{2}}{\left(2L+1\right)^{2}}\right)+2\left(E_{\mu}-E_{e}\right)k\mathrm{Im}\!\left[\dfrac{S_{\kappa_{e}L}\!\left(S_{\kappa_{e}L}^{-1*}+S_{\kappa_{e}L}^{+1*}\right)}{2L+1}\right]\!\Biggr\}\equiv\sum_{\kappa_{e}}\frac{\Gamma_{\kappa_{e}}}{\Gamma_{0}}\,

where
\mathrm{Im}(\cdots)
denotes the imaginary part and

j_{e}=|\kappa_{e}|-\tfrac{1}{2}
is the total angular momentum of the electron.
The radial integrals
S^{0}_{\kappa_{e}L}
,
S^{-1}_{\kappa_{e}L}
,

S^{+1}_{\kappa_{e}L}
, and
S_{\kappa_{e}L}
are given by

\displaystyle S^{0}_{\kappa_{e}L}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-i(\kappa_{e}-1)\,\langle j_{L}(kr)\,(f_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}G+g_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}F)\rangle,&\kappa_{e}<0,\\
\phantom{-}(\kappa_{e}+1)\,\langle j_{L}(kr)\,(g_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}G-f_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}F)\rangle,&\kappa_{e}>0,\end{cases}

(13)

\displaystyle S^{-1}_{\kappa_{e}L}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\langle j_{L-1}(kr)\,[(\kappa_{e}-L-1)g_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}G-(\kappa_{e}+L-1)f_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}F]\rangle\\
\qquad-\,i\,\langle j_{L-1}(kr)\,[\,(\kappa_{e}+L+1)f_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}G+(\kappa_{e}-L+1)g_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}F\,]\rangle,&\kappa_{e}<0,\\
\text{(analogous with }\kappa_{e}\!\to\!-\kappa_{e}\text{)},&\kappa_{e}>0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle S^{+1}_{\kappa_{e}L}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\langle j_{L+1}(kr)\,[\,(\kappa_{e}+L)g_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}G+(-\kappa_{e}+L+2)f_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}F\,]\rangle\\
\qquad-\,i\,\langle j_{L+1}(kr)\,[\,(\kappa_{e}-L)f_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}G+(\kappa_{e}+L+2)g_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}F\,]\rangle,&\kappa_{e}<0,\\
\text{(analogous with }\kappa_{e}\!\to\!-\kappa_{e}\text{)},&\kappa_{e}>0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle S_{\kappa_{e}L}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}i\,\langle j_{L}(kr)\,(f_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}G-g_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}F)\rangle,&\kappa_{e}<0,\\
\phantom{i}\,\langle j_{L}(kr)\,(g_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}G+f_{E_{e}\kappa_{e}}F)\rangle,&\kappa_{e}>0,\end{cases}

where we used the following notation:
\langle\cdots\rangle\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\cdots\,\,\mathrm{d}r
.
Here,
L
labels the multipole terms in the partial-wave expansion of the leptonic current
J^{\alpha}
, and
j_{L}(x)
are spherical Bessel functions.
Selection rules imply
\kappa_{e}=\pm L
or
\pm(L+1)
(with
L\neq 0
contributing to
S_{\kappa_{e}L}
and
S^{0}_{\kappa_{e}L}
).
A detailed derivation of the partial–wave expansion of the bound-muon decay rate for arbitrary initial state of the muon is presented in Ref.
Kaygorodov et al. 2025
.

III
Numerical results and discussion

Using Eqs. (
12
) and (
13
), we perform fully relativistic calculations of the normalized total decay rate,
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
, for the muon bound to the point-like Coulomb potential, for atomic charge numbers in the range
4\leq Z\leq 9
.
The bound muon is described with the analytical Dirac-Coulomb wave function
Rose 1961
.
The wave function of the outgoing unbound electron is obtained by solving the Dirac equation numerically using the
RADIAL
package
Salvat et al. 1995
;
Salvat and Fernández-Varea 2019
in double-precision arithmetic.
The oscillatory radial integrals that appear in the partial-wave expansion of the total decay rate, (
13
), are evaluated using the adaptive Gauss-Kronrod routine
DQAGS
from the library
QUADPACK

Piessens et al. 2012
, with both absolute and relative error tolerances set to
10^{-9}
.

The main numerical challenge in this work is the convergence of the partial-wave expansion of the total decay rate.
For each value of
Z
, we sum partial-wave contributions
\Gamma_{\kappa_{e}}/\Gamma_{0}
until the magnitude of successive terms falls below a threshold
10^{-5}
-
10^{-6}
.
In order to estimate the numerical uncertainty associated with the
terminated partial-wave expansion, we approximated the last terms of the partial-wave series with a geometric series and summed the extrapolated tail analytically.
This conservative extrapolation procedure yields a residual contribution to
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
about
10^{-5}
-
10^{-6}
for all values of
Z
considered, which we take as an upper bound on the numerical uncertainty associated with termination of the partial-wave expansion.

IV
Conclusion

In this work we have reexamined the total decay rate of a muon bound to a point-like Coulomb potential for atomic charge numbers
4\leq Z\leq 9
.
To this end, we employed a fully relativistic formalism based on the partial wave expansion of the bound-muon total decay rate and numerical solution of the Dirac equation in the Coulomb potential.
By carefully monitoring the convergence of the partial-wave series, we find that our numerical results are consistent with the analytical result of Ref.
Überall 1960
, which is accurate to the order
(\alpha Z)^{2}
.

Our all-order-in-
\alpha Z
relativistic calculations yield values of
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
that are systematically smaller than the corresponding
(\alpha Z)^{2}
prediction of Ref.
Überall 1960
corrected to account for the finite electron mass
Aslam et al. 2020
.
Thus, the present calculations provide numerical evidence for a negative next-order-in-
\alpha Z
correction to
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
for light nuclei.

For
Z=8
, we identified a significant disagreement between the fully relativistic result of Ref.
Watanabe et al. 1993
and both the analytical
(\alpha Z)^{2}
prediction of Ref.
Überall 1960
and our numerical relativistic results.
We showed that this discrepancy is due to an insufficiently converged partial-wave expansion in the calculation of Ref.
Watanabe et al. 1993
.
Specifically, the termination of the partial-wave series at
\kappa_{\max}=29
used in Ref.
Watanabe et al. 1993
resulted in
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}=0.994
, whereas the analytical result of Ref.
Überall 1960
gives
0.9981
.
The present work shows that termination of the partial-wave expansion at
\kappa_{\max}=59
with a subsequent extrapolation of the tail yields
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}=0.9976
, restoring the agreement between the perturbative
\alpha Z
result of Ref.
Überall 1960
and the fully-relativistic calculations of Ref.
Watanabe et al. 1993
.

In the present calculations of the bound-muon total decay rate we considered only the effect of the Coulomb potential.
The finite-nuclear-size, electron screening, and quantum electrodynamic effects were not taken into account.
However, for small values of
Z
the contribution of the above-mentioned corrections to the total decay rate is expected to be smaller than the present numerical accuracy.
For light muonic atoms these corrections mainly change the shape of the electron spectrum close to the endpoint region where the spectrum shape rapidly decreases, see, e.g., Refs.
Czarnecki et al. 2011
;
Szafron and Czarnecki 2016
;
Kaygorodov et al. 2025
.
Therefore, these corrections to the total decay rate are not essential for the present analysis.
The updated values of the bound-muon total decay rates for
Z=4
–
9
provide
a set of benchmark results for future theoretical investigations of light muonic atoms
and may be used for consistency checks in related bound-muon decay calculations.

Divergent construction of Ni and Fe–Al–H assemblies
Authors: Jordan R. Gipper, Kritika Gour, Nattamai Bhuvanesh, Manar M. Shoshani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Reaction of an Al-dihydride metalloligand with transition metal(ii) chloride precursors provides an accessible route to heterotrimetallic hydride complexes with electron-rich transtition metals. Formation of heterotrimetallic hydride complexes proceeds through divergent pathways. Exposure of the Al-dihydride metalloligand to NiCl2·DME leads to reductive assembly, whereas exposure to FeCl2 results in hydride/halide metathesis.

Lewis acids and transition metals (TMs) have long exhibited synergistic reactivity, enabling substrate activation and functionalization inaccessible to either component alone.1 Such cooperative interactions have been exploited in catalysis resulting in enhanced activity and selectivity in a variety of transformations, including substrate reduction,2,3 ethylene chain growth,4 and C–H bond activation.5 These findings have motivated investigations into well-defined heterometallic complexes to advance the understanding of the cooperative interplay between Lewis acids and TMs as well as unveil new reactivity.6

One approach for the synthesis of well-defined heterometallic complexes has been the use of aluminium-hydride-bearing bidentate metalloligands as synthons for the incorporation of TMs (Fig. 1A). Al-nacnac metalloligands7 have been utilized with metals such as Cr, Fe, and Ni forming well-defined heterometallic assemblies.8–12 Studies from these heterometallic systems have revealed unique electronic structure and bond activation processes. An alternative synthetic route for single component heterometallic complexes, geared to independently tune the electronic profile at each metal site, has been the development of multidentate ligand scaffolds that feature site-specific binding pockets for Lewis acids and TMs (Fig. 1B). Lu et al. have developed heterometallic systems using phosphino-amide scaffolds featuring Lewis acids which tune the electronics at the TM13 and cooperatively engage with substrates.14 Xu et al. reported a phosphino-nacnac ligand capable of housing Mg–H–Ni subunits in cooperative insertion chemistry.15 In addition, our group has reported phosphino-AlHn metalloligands and their respective Ni complexes for insertion-based transformations (Fig. 1B and C).16,17 Crucially, in most examples, including those discussed above, metalation strategies involve the combination of metalloligands with zero-valent homoleptic metal precursors or combinations of metal halides with strong reductants to access electron-rich TM sites. We envisioned that streamlined and versatile routes to electron-rich TMs within heterometallic-hydride assemblies can be accessed via direct addition of readily available metal halides to Al-dihydride metalloligands either through dehydrogenative reductive assembly or hydride/halide metathesis (Fig. 1D).

Treatment of the previously reported Al-dihydride metalloligand, LOMeAlH2,17 with half an equivalent of NiCl2·DME in THF yielded the heterotrimetallic dihydride complex (LOMeAlClH)2Ni, complex 1 (Scheme 1) in 68% yield. In solution, complex 1 exhibits two doublets in the 31P{1H} NMR spectrum at 39.83 and 34.70 ppm (2JPP = 29.8 Hz), indicative of two inequivalent phosphine environments. The 1H NMR spectrum features two sets of ligand-based resonances consistent with C1 symmetry, and two resonances in the hydride region, a broad apparent triplet at −4.67 ppm and a broad singlet at −4.99 ppm that each integrate to one hydride, which indicates the formal loss of two hydride ligands. We propose that this transformation proceeds through an initial hydride-halide metathesis followed by a two-electron reduction through elimination of H2, which is observed experimentally (Fig. S19). Spin–lattice relaxation (T1) experimentation was carried out and T1min values of 319 and 271 ms were determined for the previously mentioned hydrides, respectively. The chemical shift, broadness of the resonances, and T1(min) values are all consistent with bridged NiAl-hydride ligands.17 Recently, Ohki et al. reported the formation of discrete Ni4H4 clusters via reduction of a Ni(ii) hexamethyldisalizide precursor with group 13-hydride precursors.18

Synthesis of complexes 1, 1-DME, 2, and 3.

Given the success of reactivity of NiCl2·DME and LOMeAlH2, we sought to expand the scope of metal(ii) chlorides precursors. Addition of FeCl2 to LOMeAlH2 yielded a complex mixture of products based on NMR spectroscopy. Given our previous observations of cluster formation from the reaction of LOMeAlH2 and Ni(COD)2,17 we hypothesized that providing an additional donor at the Al-center could offer a pathway to more controlled nuclearity in reactivity with TMs. Replacing the pendant methoxy donor with a methoxyethane ether arm afforded the metalloligand LDMEAlH2via a synthetic route analogous to that of LOMeAlH2. LDMEAlH2 exhibits spectroscopic features comparable to its methoxy analogue, with a broad AlH2 resonance at 4.67 ppm (W1/2 = 109.5 Hz) in the 1H NMR spectrum and a sharp 31P{1H} resonance at −15.41 ppm. The solid-state structure reveals only one O-atom coordinates to the Al center (Fig. S41). Similar to LOMeAlH2, treatment of LDMEAlH2 with half an equivalent of NiCl2·DME formed a heterotrimetallic dihydride species, (LDMEAlClH)2Ni (1-DME) in good yield (93%). Complex 1-DME features an analogous set of doublets at 38.67 and 34.34 ppm (2JPP = 29.4 Hz) in the 31P{1H} NMR spectrum along with two broad hydride resonances at −4.55 ppm and −4.97 ppm in the 1H NMR spectrum. The T1(min) values of 325 and 275 ms, also support an overall similar electronic structure to 1.

To investigate whether the modified metalloligand provided better control in heterometallic assembly outside of Ni, LDMEAlH2 was exposed to half an equivalent of FeCl2 under conditions analogous to those employed for NiCl2·DME. Gratifyingly, the reaction afforded the asymmetric heterometallic (LDMEAlClH2)2Fe species, complex 2 (Scheme 1) as a mixture of isomers in 96% yield. Spectroscopic data for 2 features two broad resonances in the 31P{1H} NMR spectrum at 116.65 and −24.23 ppm in a 1 : 1 ratio as well as an overall broad 1H NMR spectrum with four hydride resonances ranging from −10 to −30 ppm with T1(min) values that ranged from 170 to 185 ms. In contrast to complex 1 and 1-DME, assembly of complex 2 affords a heterometallic species through halide–hydride metathesis without concomitant dehydrogenative reduction. This finding can potentially be ascribed to the lower reducing potential required to reduce Ni(ii) to Ni(0) in comparison of Fe(ii) to Fe(0).19

To probe the source of the observed broadening in the NMR spectra of complex 2, DFT calculations were carried out for 2 in S = 0 and S = 1 states which support a substantially stabilized singlet state (28.3 kcal mol−1), inconsistent with broadening due to a thermally accessible triplet state. Variable-temperature NMR experimentation was then carried out to elucidate potential fluctional exchange mechanisms. Cooling a solution of 2 in d8-tol to −70 °C shows no appreciable changes. Upon heating, however, the thermal instability of 2 becomes apparent, which is initially observed through increased population of a similarly asymmetric isomeric species, as evidenced by growth of slightly shifted hydride resonances in the 1H NMR spectrum (see Fig. S27 and discussion below). Further heating the sample past 30 °C results in complete consumption of the isomeric mixture of 2 and is accompanied by the emergence of a new phosphine- and hydride-bearing species, which was identified as the heterotrimetallic hydride complex 3 (Scheme 1; bottom). Complex 3 can be synthesized directly from LDMEAlH2 and isolated in 27% yield. The heterotrimetallic species furnishes a single resonance in the 31P{1H} NMR spectrum at 79.40 ppm, indicative of gained symmetry. Similarly, the 1H NMR spectrum features a single set of ligand-based resonances and two hydride resonances at −14.02 ppm and −16.84 ppm with each corresponding to two hydrides indicating a structural rearrangement rather than reduction, contrary to 1. The hydride chemical shift and relative broadness, along with T1(min) values of 180 and 209 ms support a delocalized bonding interaction with Fe and Al. The decrease in the T1(min) values of 2 and 3 in comparison to 1 may be ascribed to the proximity to the additional spin-active nuclei in the Fe variants.20,21

Single-crystal X-ray diffraction (XRD) studies of complexes 1-3 were carried out. All three structures reveal unambiguous halide migration from the TMs to the Al centers with the closest distance from the TMs to the halides exceeding 3.8 Å (Fig. 2). In complex 1, two hydride ligands were located in the electron difference map consistent with the 1H NMR data supporting formal reduction of the Ni center via liberation of dihydrogen (Fig. 2A). The two hydride ligands of 1 are bridged between an Al and Ni center with Al–H (1.72(1)–1.73(1) Å) and identical Ni–H (1.56(1) Å) distances consistent with 3-center-2 electron bonding. The Ni center takes on a distorted tetrahedral geometry with respect to the phosphines and the hydride ligands, consistent with a two-electron reduction to form a core composed of a Ni(0) center with two Al(iii) centers.

The solid-state structure of 2 revealed two inequivalent metalloligands consistent with the spectroscopic data along with halide transfer from the Fe to the Al-center. Beyond the presence of two additional hydride ligands, the coordination sphere around the Fe center introduces important distinctions in comparison to 1 (Fig. 2B). In complex 2 one metalloligand, Al2, bears a phosphine bound to the Al center rather than the Fe center. The two hydrides bound to Al2 (H3 and H4) coordinate to the Fe center with near equidistant values to Al2, ranging from (1.73(4) to 1.82(3) Å) and Fe (1.45(3) to 1.53(5) Å). The second metalloligand, bound to Al1, demonstrates a contrasting coordination profile. The phosphine moiety of the metalloligand is bound to the Fe-center, and the amide serves as a bridging XL-type ligand between the Fe and Al center. Hydride H1 bridges between the Al and Fe center, comparable to H3 and H4; however, the elongated Al1⋯H2 separation of 2.03(3)–2.04(5) Å indicates a substantive disruption of the Al–H interaction. The observation is further supported by a hydride chemical shift at −29.43 ppm in the 1H NMR spectrum, and a Fe–H IR stretch of ∼1768 cm−1 (Fig. S34) inconsistent with hydrides bridged between Fe and group 13 Lewis acids.22 Conversely, the comparable distances between the Fe and Al centers (2.2790(8)–2.2976(8) Å) are inconsistent with complete Al–H scission. Lastly, it was found that Al1 is coordinated to both O-atoms of the dimethoxyethane substituent which offers potential rationale as to why metalation attempts with LOMeAlH2 proved ineffective. The Fe center takes on a distorted octahedral geometry with respect to the four-hydrides, the amine, and the phosphine donor, which taken together with the NMR data supports a low-spin Fe(ii) designation.

Single-crystal XRD of the 3 reveals pseudo C2 symmetry through a structural rearrangement of 2. All four bridged hydrides were located on the electron difference map and show structural consistency amongst the Al–H–Fe subunits (Fig. 2C). The Fe center is featured in a distorted octahedral geometry with respect to the phosphine centers and hydride ligands. The phosphine donors are cis disposed while the four bridging hydrides occupy the remaining coordination sites within the octahedral field. The Fe–Al separations of 2.317(1) and 2.318(1) Å, are once again shorter than the sum of the covalent radii which could suggest a bonding interaction between the Fe and Al centers.23 However, the overall distances within the Fe–Al-μ-hydride subunits of 3 are consistent with the examples reported by Power24 and Suzuki25 which, similarly to 2, supports a low-spin octahedral Fe(ii) designation.

Density functional theory (DFT) studies under the BP86/def2-TZVP level of theory were conducted to support the location of the hydride ligands and elucidate electronic structures details in complexes 1-3. All three optimized structures exhibit good agreement with the corresponding crystallographic data (Fig. S47–S57), and calculations show a 21.3 kcal mol−1 stabilization of complex 3 in comparison to 2 consistent with the experimental observation. Natural Bonding Orbital (NBO) calculations support a level of covalency amongst the Al–H–TM subunits in complexes 1-3 (Fig. 3). For complex 1, the Wiberg Bond Indices (WBIs) highlight consistency amongst the Ni–H interactions (0.13 and 0.14) which are diminished in comparison to the Al–H WBIs (0.51 and 0.34).

NBO analysis of complex 2 supports the electronically differentiated nature of the metalloligands (Fig. 3B). For instance, the WBIs amongst the metal-hydride interactions of 2 display differentiation amongst the Fe–H bonds ranging from 0.30 to 0.49 with H4 having the largest WBI potentially a result of the weak trans influence of the amine. Conversely, NBO analysis of 3 reveals a highly symmetric bonding environment (Fig. 3C). The Fe–H interactions range from 0.32–0.33, while the Al–H interactions are all 0.29, suggesting a largely uniform bonding environment throughout the heterotrimetallic hydride core. Notably, the comparative NPA charges amongst complexes 1-3 support both the site-differentiation of the Al centers in 2 in comparison to 3, as well as the difference in oxidation state between the Ni and Fe species. Finally, the covalency within the Al–H–TM subunit is emphasized by the decreased Al–H WBI in comparison to that of LDMEAlH2 (0.81).

In summary, we have developed LDMEAlH2, a new Al-dihydride metalloligand that enables divergent assembly of well-defined heterometallic complexes. Reaction with metal chlorides reveals divergent reactivity between Ni and Fe with NiCl2·DME undergoing halide-hydride metathesis followed by reduction to afford a formally Ni(0) trimetallic complex whereas FeCl2 undergoes halide-hydride metathesis without reduction. The increased flexibility and coordinative sites of LDMEAlH2 permits assembly of FeAl-hydride frameworks via an intermediate that invokes coordination of the DME substituent prior to proceeding to a symmetrized FeAl2H4 core. These results highlight a platform for accessing well defined heterometallic complexes bearing electron-rich transition metal centers directly from readily available metal halide salts.

Post-Sphaleron Darkogenesis Preprint: —
Sudhakantha Girmohanta, Yuichiro Nakai, and Zhihao Zhang

Abstract
A supercooled phase transition in a nearly conformal dark sector can provide a natural setting for darkogenesis via its out-of-equilibrium dynamics, where a particle-antiparticle number asymmetry in the dark sector can be reprocessed into the visible sector, yielding the observed baryon asymmetry and an asymmetric dark matter. We consider a scenario where the number asymmetry is generated from the decay of a mother particle produced via parametric resonance during the phase transition induced due to its coupling to the dilaton associated with spontaneous breaking of scale invariance. It is shown that the correct baryon asymmetry and dark matter abundance can be realized for a dark phase transition at 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) ​ GeV \mathcal{O}(1)\,\rm GeV , which can also explain the nano-Hz gravitational wave signal reported by pulsar timing array experiments. The scenario will be tested further in neutron-antineutron oscillation experiments.

It is natural to ask if the decay of some GeV-scale Majorana fermion in a DS can account for BAU and DM after a dark supercooled PT. For this question, in the absence of resonance enhancement and tuning, to yield a significant asymmetry, a Majorana fermion usually needs to be very heavy, as in the standard thermal leptogenesis scenario

which does not fit the present exploration. However, if a dark supercooled PT provides an environment for the out-of-equilibrium dynamics to abundantly produce a GeV-scale Majorana fermion, the conclusion can be changed.
In the present paper, we will consider such a possibility and show that a mother GeV-scale Majorana fermion
\psi

The dilaton is the pseudo-Nambu Goldstone boson (pNGB) of the spontaneously broken scale invariance, and can be identified with the radion degree of freedom in a dual weakly coupled 5-dimensional description utilizing AdS/CFT

Gubser:1998bc

We derive the dilaton/radion interaction with the Majorana fermion
\psi

\psi

\eta

\chi_{2}

\eta

We show that this scenario can yield the observed BAU and DM abundance while also predicting neutron-antineutron oscillations potentially observable in upcoming experiments as a result of the Majorana nature of
\psi

3
Mother particle production via dilaton oscillation

Coherent oscillations of the dilaton field after the bubble percolation can cause the effective mass of a fermion to vary,
leading to parametric excitation of a fermion if its effective mass vanishes during the oscillation. This is contrary to the expectation that the fermion production suffers from Pauli blocking and is suppressed
compared to the case of the boson production. The coherently oscillating nature alters the naive expectation dramatically
Greene:1998nh
.
As evaluated in appendix
A
,
the effective mass term during the dilaton oscillation in our setup is

\displaystyle m^{\rm eff}_{\psi}(t)=m_{\psi}\left[\frac{\{1+\xi(t)\cos(m_{\varphi}t)\}^{2b}-r}{1-r}\right]\ ,

(6)

where in terms of the 5D dual description,
b
corresponds to the 5D bulk mass parameter for
\psi
,
r
denotes the ratio of its UV and IR localized mass terms,
\xi(t)
is the amplitude of the dilaton oscillation in units of
\langle\phi\rangle
, and
m_{\psi}
is the physical
\psi
mass today when the dilaton oscillation is frozen out. The initial value of
\xi(t=0)
is denoted as
\xi_{0}
.
\xi_{0}
is also the maximum amplitude of oscillation, and
\xi(t)<\xi_{0}
at later times due to backreaction effects of the produced
\psi
. In terms of the 4D description, these are translated into how the field
\psi
mixes with composite states of the CFT (see appendices
A
,
B
for further details).
As the effective mass of the Majorana fermion
\psi
varies according to Eq. (
6
), the equation of motion appears analogous to that of a harmonic oscillator with a time-varying frequency. Efficient production of particles can take place when the adiabatic evolution is violated, in particular, at instants when
m_{\psi}^{\rm eff}(t)
crosses zero. From Eq. (
6
) we note that
m_{\psi}^{\rm eff}(t)
never vanishes when
r=0
.
It is important not to expand the dilaton/radion interaction in a linear approximation, as it may appear to vanish at the level of a linear approximation for a certain range of parameters, while from the full expression, it is clear that the exponent never crosses zero when
r=0
. A necessary condition for
m_{\psi}^{\rm eff}(t)
to vanish is

\displaystyle(1-\xi_{0})^{2b}\leq r\leq(1+\xi_{0})^{2b}\ ;\ {\rm for\ }0<b<1/2\ ,

\displaystyle(1-\xi_{0})^{2b}\geq r\geq(1+\xi_{0})^{2b}\ ;\ {\rm for\ }b<0\ ,

(7)

where we have used the fact that
\xi_{0}<1
, and
r\neq 0
.
As Eq. (
7
) is a necessary condition for the particle production to occur, it can be ensured that the other fields coupled to
\varphi
do not get produced similarly by appropriately choosing their
b,r
parameters. Notice from Eq. (
7
) that for a massive particle production to occur,
b\neq 0
. It is sufficient that only
\psi_{1}
, the lightest mother particle, is produced by this mechanism, as a virtual
\psi_{2}
loop is adequate for the asymmetry generation. Only
\psi_{1}
production is also desirable, as otherwise
\psi_{2}
decay involving
\psi_{1}
may wash out the asymmetry.

We now formulate the particle production, following Ref.
Giudice:1999fb
.
The effective equation of motion of
\psi
in the background of oscillating
\varphi

\displaystyle\left(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m^{\rm eff}_{\psi}(t)\right)\psi=0\ .

(8)

To solve Eq. (
8
) with the time-varying effective mass in Eq. (
6
), we decompose

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{x}}}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{s}\left[u_{s}(p,t)a_{s}(p)+v_{s}(p,t)a^{\dagger}_{s}(-p)\right],

(9)

where
a_{s},a^{\dagger}_{s}
denote annihilation and creation operators, respectively, the summation is over spin
s
, and the condition
v_{s}(p)=C\bar{u}_{s}^{T}(-p)
is imposed to preserve the Majorana condition
\psi^{c}=\psi

The creation and annihilation operators satisfy the anticommutation relations,

\displaystyle\bigl\{a_{s}(p),a_{s^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(p^{\prime})\bigr\}=\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\delta(\vec{p}-\vec{p}\,^{\prime})\ ,\quad\bigl\{a_{s}(p),a_{s^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})\bigr\}=\bigl\{a_{s}^{\dagger}(p),a_{s^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(p^{\prime})\bigr\}=0\ .

(10)

The normalization of spinors is

u_{s}^{\dagger}(p,t)u_{s^{\prime}}(p,t)=2\delta_{ss^{\prime}}
.
Defining
u\equiv\begin{pmatrix}u_{+}\\
u_{-}\end{pmatrix}
, the Dirac equation (
8

\displaystyle\left[\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}+\omega^{2}\pm i\dot{m}_{\psi}^{\rm eff}\right]u_{\pm}(p)=0\ ,

(11)

where the dot denotes derivative with respect to time,
\omega^{2}(t)=p^{2}+(m_{\psi}^{\rm eff}(t))^{2}
, and
\gamma^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{I}&0\\
0&-\mathbf{I}\end{pmatrix}
. Note that for a GeV scale PT, the Hubble rate is negligible in comparison with particle creation and dilaton oscillation scales. Therefore, we can effectively neglect the expansion of the Universe in the particle production stage and work in an effective Minkowski background. The effective Hamiltonian

\displaystyle{\cal H}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\int d^{3}x\psi^{\dagger}\dot{\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}p\sum_{s}\bigg\{E_{p}(t)\left[a^{\dagger}_{s}(p)a_{s}(p)-a_{s}(-p)a^{\dagger}_{s}(-p)\right]

\displaystyle+F_{p}(t)a_{s}(-p)a_{s}(p)+F^{*}_{p}(t)a^{\dagger}_{s}(p)a^{\dagger}_{s}(-p)\bigg\}\ ,

\displaystyle E_{p}=p\Re(u^{*}_{+}u_{-})-m_{\psi}^{\rm eff}(1-u^{*}_{+}u_{+})\ ,

(13)

\displaystyle F_{p}=\frac{p}{2}(u^{2}_{+}-u^{2}_{-})-m_{\psi}^{\rm eff}u_{+}u_{-}\ ,

(14)

\displaystyle E^{2}_{p}+|F_{p}|^{2}=\omega^{2}\ .

(15)

\displaystyle\hat{a}(p,t)

\displaystyle=\alpha(p,t)a(p)+\beta(p,t)a^{\dagger}(-p)\ ,

\displaystyle=-\beta^{*}(p,t)a(p)+\alpha^{*}(p,t)a^{\dagger}(-p)\ ,

\beta(-p)=-\beta(p)

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{p}+\omega}{F_{p}^{*}}\ ,

\displaystyle=\frac{|F_{p}|^{2}}{2\omega(\omega+E_{p})}=\frac{\omega-E_{p}}{2\omega}\ .

\displaystyle{\cal H}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}p\sum_{s}\omega(t)\left[\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{s}(p)\hat{a}_{s}(p)+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{s}(-p)\hat{a}_{s}(-p)\right]

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}p\sum_{s}\omega(t)\left[\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{s}(p)\hat{a}_{s}(p)\right].

(21)

\displaystyle n(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}dp\,p^{2}|\beta|^{2}\ .

\displaystyle u_{\pm}(0)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1\pm\frac{m_{\psi}^{\rm eff}}{\omega}}\ ,

\displaystyle\dot{u}_{\pm}(0)

\displaystyle=-ipu_{\mp}(0)\mp im_{\psi}^{\rm eff}u_{\pm}(0)\ .

Once the energy density produced in
\psi

The back reaction will effectively work as a friction term that will decrease the
\varphi

\xi(t)

\xi(t)
cannot satisfy the condition (

The final freeze-out number density for
\psi

\displaystyle n_{\psi}^{\rm FO}=\frac{p^{3}_{\rm max}}{3\pi^{2}}\ ,

(25)

where the final density approximates a Fermi sphere up to the cut-off momentum

essentially represents the maximum value of the physical momentum for

\displaystyle u_{+}(t)=\sqrt{1+\frac{m_{\psi}^{\rm eff}}{\omega}}\times\exp\left(-i\int^{t}\omega dt^{\prime}\right),

(26)

which results into
\beta(p)=0

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{m_{\psi}^{\rm eff}}{\omega}\right)\left|\frac{\ddot{m}_{\psi}^{\rm eff}}{\omega}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\dot{m}_{\psi}^{\rm eff}}{\omega}\right)^{2}\left(1-\frac{5m_{\psi}^{\rm eff}}{\omega}\right)\right|\ll\omega^{2}\ .

(27)

27
) is violated. Hence,

can be determined from the following equation,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2p_{\rm max}^{2}}\left|\frac{\ddot{m}_{\psi}^{\rm eff}}{p_{\rm max}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\dot{m}_{\psi}^{\rm eff}}{p_{\rm max}}\right)^{2}\right|_{t=\bar{t}}=1\ ,

where we use
m_{\psi}^{\rm eff}\simeq 0
since particle production takes place at the instant of vanishing effective mass, denoted as
\bar{t}
, which is evaluated from Eq. (
6
) to be

\displaystyle\cos(m_{\varphi}\bar{t})=\frac{1}{\xi(\bar{t})}\left(r^{\frac{1}{2b}}-1\right)\ .

(29)

Finally, using Eqs. (
28
), (

), the analytical form of particle density is given by

\displaystyle n_{\psi}^{\rm FO}\simeq\mathcal{M}(r,b)\left(\frac{m_{\psi}m_{\varphi}^{2}}{6\pi^{2}}\right),\qquad\mathcal{M}(r,b)\equiv\left[\frac{2br}{1-r}\left(r^{-\frac{1}{2b}}-1\right)\right],

\psi_{1}
fermions produced by the parametric excitation are in out-of-thermal equilibrium, and through their decays to
\chi

The Hubble scale during the PT is essentially set by the false vacuum energy,
\rho_{\rm vac}\sim c_{V}\langle\phi\rangle^{4}

is a model-dependent constant. For
\langle\phi\rangle\sim

\psi_{1}
decay rate from Eq. (
5

t_{\psi_{1}}\ll t_{\rm H}
for
|y|\gg 10^{-9}
. To justify the oscillation picture,
t_{\psi_{1}}
should be larger than the timescale of dilaton oscillation
m_{\varphi}^{-1}
,

|y|\lesssim\left(\frac{8\pi m_{\varphi}}{m_{\psi_{1}}}\right)^{1/2}\ ,

(34)

which is easily satisfied for
|y|\lesssim 10^{-2}
and
m_{\varphi}\gtrsim{\rm keV}
. Now, assuming anarchic
|y_{ij}|
elements, the asymmetry in
\chi_{\alpha}
denoted as
\delta{n_{\chi}}_{\alpha}={n_{\chi}}_{\alpha}-{n_{\bar{\chi}}}_{\alpha}
(
\alpha=1,2

\displaystyle\delta{n_{\chi}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{|y|^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{m_{\psi_{1}}}{m_{\psi_{2}}}n_{\psi_{1}}\theta_{\rm CP}\ ,

(35)

where
\theta_{\rm CP}={\cal O}(1)
encodes the complex physical phase in
y_{ij}
. For a more precise estimation, including the effect of the
\mu
-coupling in Eq. (
2
), see appendix
C
.

The inverse decay
\chi\eta\to\psi_{1}
tries to bring the system towards thermal equilibrium. Hence, one should ensure that the effective rate of this inverse decay is smaller than the Hubble rate at the maximum temperature achieved after the PT, namely at
T_{\rm RH}
. Using the principle of detailed balance, we can evaluate the ratio of the effective inverse decay rate to the Hubble rate as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{n_{\eta}^{\rm eq}\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\chi\eta\to\psi_{1}}}{H(T_{\rm RH})}=\frac{\Gamma_{\psi_{1}\to\chi\eta}}{H(T_{\rm RH})}\frac{n_{\psi_{1}}^{\rm eq}}{n_{\chi}^{\rm eq}}\simeq\frac{\Gamma_{\psi_{1}\to\chi\eta}}{H(T_{\rm RH})}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\pi/2}}{3\zeta(3)}e^{-m_{\psi_{1}}/T_{\rm RH}}\left(\frac{m_{\psi_{1}}}{T_{\rm RH}}\right)^{3/2}\right]\ ,

(36)

where we have assumed that the
\chi
,
\eta
are in thermal equilibrium and
m_{\psi_{1}}>T_{\rm RH}
. For typical parameters, for
e.g.
,
T_{\rm RH}\simeq 0.2
GeV, which is required to explain the nano-Hz stochastic gravitational wave signal
Fujikura:2024jto
, and
m_{\psi_{1}}\simeq 5
GeV, the term in the square bracket in Eq. (
36
) obtains a strong exponential suppression, and for typical values of the coupling
|y|\simeq 10^{-4}
as obtained later to explain the BAU, the ratio in Eq. (
36
) is smaller than unity. Therefore, it can be ensured conservatively that the inverse decay does not bring the system into thermal equilibrium.

One also has to ensure that the asymmetry-destroying processes are out-of-equilibrium, otherwise, the generated asymmetry would be erased. To suppress
\chi\eta\to\bar{\chi}\eta
through s-channel
\psi
mediation, one needs to demand that the interaction rate for this process is less than the Hubble rate. Due to the portal interaction, the generated
\chi,\eta
obtain thermal abundance, and it is sufficient to ensure that the rate is smaller than the Hubble rate at the reheating temperature
T_{\rm RH}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\zeta(3)}{\pi^{2}}T_{\rm RH}^{3}\right)\left(\frac{|y|^{4}}{8\pi m_{\psi}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim 0.3\sqrt{g_{*}}\frac{T_{\rm RH}^{2}}{M_{\rm pl}}\ ,

while the reheating temperature can be estimated using the false vacuum energy

T_{\rm RH}\simeq\left(\frac{30c_{V}}{\pi^{2}g_{*}}\right)^{1/4}\langle\phi\rangle\ .

\displaystyle|y|\lesssim 2\times 10^{-4}\left(\frac{g_{*}}{100}\right)^{1/16}\left(\frac{m_{\psi}}{1{\ \rm GeV}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{\langle\phi\rangle}{1{\ \rm GeV}}\right)^{-1/4}\ .

Conclusions

We have realized a post-sphaleron darkogenesis scenario that simultaneously explains the observed baryon asymmetry, dark matter, and the nano-Hz stochastic gravitational wave signal observed by the PTA. A first-order supercooled phase transition in a nearly conformal dark sector generates the mother particle via parametric resonance, satisfying the out-of-equilibrium condition at

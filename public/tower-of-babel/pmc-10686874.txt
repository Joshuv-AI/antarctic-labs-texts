Magnetic field model of radially magnetized cylindrical permanent magnets in a self-biased magnetic pendulum array
Authors: Weiye Song, Changgeng Shuai, Qi Zhai
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractSelf-Biased Magnetic Pendulum Array (SBMPA) is an efficient and portable transmitter in ultralow frequency (ULF). The resonance frequency of SBMPA is affected by the magnetic field of the radially magnetized cylindrical permanent magnets. In order to calculate the resonance frequency, the magnetic field model of a single radially magnetized cylindrical permanent magnet is derived based on the concept of magnetic charge. Then, the influence of the demagnetizing field and external magnetic field on the magnetization of permanent magnets is analyzed, and the magnetic field model of SBMPA is established. The results of the magnetic field model are verified through simulation. The average deviation of magnetic field intensity is determined as 0.021%; thus, the magnetic field model and simulation have consistent results. Finally, the influence of the size and distance of permanent magnets on the resonance frequency is analyzed, which could provide theoretical guidance for the dynamic analysis of SBMPA.Keywords: Magnetic charge, Static magnetic field, Demagnetizing field, Self-biased magnetic pendulum array

Introduction

High-frequency electromagnetic waves have a small skin depth in the seawater, and the signal attenuates rapidly. Thus, low-frequency electromagnetic waves are used in communication in special environments such as seawater. For example, very-low-frequency (VLF) electromagnetic waves can penetrate tens of meters into the seawater [1]. However, the size of the traditional antenna is related to its wavelength. Low-frequency (SLF/VLF) electromagnetic wave has a long wavelength; hence, the antenna occupies a large area [2]. Moreover, the efficiency of the traditional antenna remains low. Megawatts of power are required to transmit signals [3], [4].

In 2017, a mechanical antenna was designed that used mechanical motion to generate ULF electromagnetic waves [5], [6], [7], [8]. Mechanical antennas can break through the theoretical limit of quality factor Q proposed by Chu [9] and Harrington [10], which could realize the miniaturization of ULF/VLF transmitters. The prototypes of mechanical antennas based on the mechanism of rotating permanent magnets [11], [12], [13], [14], [15], [16] or electrets [17], [18], [19] driven by a motor were designed and successfully transmitted ULF/VLF electromagnetic signals. However, the speed of the rotor needed to reach tens of thousands, consuming considerable mechanical energy, and the modulation efficiency remained low due to the motor torque limit. In addition, for mechanical antennas using electrets, the stability of charge is affected by the material, temperature, humidity, and other environmental factors. To this end, the self-based magnetic pendulum array (SBMPA) is a promising scheme. The rotor in SBMPA is driven by the magnetic torque provided by the permanent magnet array and excitation coil; hence, it no longer needs auxiliary devices such as a motor and realizes the miniaturization of the antenna [20], [21], [22], [23].

SBMPA has the maximum radiation power when operating at the resonance frequency, which is related to the magnetic field of radially magnetized cylindrical permanent magnets (RMCPM). To analyze the dynamic characteristics of SBMPA, the magnetic field model of RMCPM must be established. Babic and Ravaud established the analytical magnetic field model of RMCPM by using the concept of magnetic charge [24], [25]. However, the surface density of magnetic charge on the cylindrical surface is constant in these models. For the RMCPM, the surface density of magnetic charge is related to the angle φ between the magnetization M→ and the unit normal vector of the cylindrical surface n→ [26]. However, the model contains indefinite values while calculating the expressions. Furthermore, the magnetization of the permanent magnets in SBMPA is affected by the demagnetizing field and the external magnetic field generated by other permanent magnets [27], [28], [29], [30], [31], such that it will affect the accuracy of the magnetic field model and the resonance frequency of SBMPA.

To calculate the magnetic field and resonance frequency of SBMPA, the magnetic field model of a single RMCPM is first established by using the concept of magnetic charge. Then, we undertake a comprehensive examination of the demagnetizing and external magnetic fields' impact on magnetization. Despite the significance of these factors, their exploration in the existing literature is limited, thus making our research a significant contribution in this domain. The efficacy of our proposed magnetic field model is confirmed through rigorous simulation, reinforcing its practical applicability. Finally, the influencing factors of the resonance frequency are analyzed to provide theoretical guidance for the dynamic design of SBMPA.

The principle of SBMPA

The structure of SBMPA with five rotors is shown in Fig. 1. The system consists of five cylindrical permanent magnets with radial magnetization, each acting as a rotor. These rotors are fixed in a specific arrangement within the SBMPA structure using bearings at both ends. In operation, an excitation coil is utilized to generate an alternating magnetic field. This magnetic field serves to provide the excitation moment, inducing a swinging motion in the rotors. Additionally, the magnetic field generated by the other rotors in the system contributes to the recovery moment, causing the rotors to oscillate back and forth around the equilibrium position. The oscillation of the rotors generates alternating electromagnetic waves in space, resulting in the emission of ultra-low-frequency (ULF) electromagnetic signals [32]. Notably, the behavior and characteristics of these signals are highly dependent on the resonant frequency of the SBMPA system. When the current frequency matches the resonance frequency of the SBMPA, the amplitude of the rotor swing reaches its maximum, leading to the highest radiated power.

Simulation

In order to examine the influence of rotor structure parameters on the resonance frequency of the SBMPA, a simulation is conducted using a dual rotor configuration. This dual rotor structure is distinct from the five-rotor arrangement discussed in Section 2 of the paper, differing solely in the number of rotors, which is reduced to two. Adopting the dual rotor configuration offers the advantage of facilitating an intuitive analysis of the impact of rotor structure parameters on various SBMPA aspects, mainly the resonance frequency. Simplifying the system to two rotors enables a more straightforward and comprehensive examination of the relationship between these parameters and the desired effects.

The two permanent magnets are the same size. The radius is a=2mm, the height is 2H=30mm, and the distance between the centers of two permanent magnets is D=10mm. The permanent magnets are made of sintered NdFeB(N38), the remanence is Br=1.15T, and the permeability is μr=1.09 [34]. For the constitutive relationship of permanent magnets in the simulation, we utilized the remanent flux density model, which could reduce the additional data required in the simulation, making the process simpler and more efficient. The equation of the magnetic potential is selected to solve the magnetic field in the air. The permanent magnet is magnetized along the X-axis, as shown in Fig. 2.

Fig. 3(a-d) compares the results of the magnetic field model in the paper, the existing model, and simulation along the line 20mm≤x≤30mm,y=15mm,z=15mm, respectively. The model presented in this study exhibits enhanced correspondence with the experimental data, substantiating its superior predictive accuracy compared to the existing model. The model proposed in this paper considers the structural characteristics of AMEBA, including the demagnetization of the internal permanent magnet and the influence of the external magnetic field. Specifically, the demagnetization field acts opposite to the magnetization of the permanent magnet itself, resulting in a demagnetization effect and a reduction in the magnetization of the magnet. Conversely, the magnetic field generated by other permanent magnets around the permanent magnet acts in the same direction as that of the magnetization of the permanent magnet, leading to a secondary magnetization effect and an increase in the magnetization strength of the PM.

Analysis of influencing factors on resonance frequency

According to equation (1), the resonance frequency of SBMPA is related to the X component of the magnetic field applied to the center of the PM, Hx, and the resonance frequency can be calculated by using the magnetic field model, fmodel=306Hz. The parameters of the PM are the same as in the simulation. In order to analyze the influencing factors of the resonance frequency, the influence of the radius R, height H, and distance between the centers of two permanent magnets, D of permanent magnets on the resonance frequency of SBMPA is studied, and the results are shown in Fig. 4.

Influence of parameters on resonance frequency (a) radius R; (b) Height H and distance D.

As shown in Fig. 4(a,b), the resonance frequency of SBMPA decreases rapidly with the increasing radius R and the distance between the centers of two permanent magnets D and increases slowly with the increase in height H. It is because in equation (1), the resonance frequency fmodel is inversely proportional to the radius R and proportional to the square root of the magnetic field Bao at the center of the rotor. With the increase in the distance between the centers of two permanent magnets D, the magnetic field Bao decreases rapidly, and with the increase in height H, the magnetic field Bao increases slightly.

In order to enhance the resonant frequency, it is desirable to reduce the radius of the permanent magnet and the distance between the centers of the two permanent magnets while increasing the height of the permanent magnet. However, these dimensional modifications involve trade-offs. While reducing the rotor radius can lead to a higher resonant frequency, it can also result in decreased antenna radiated power. Decreasing the rotor radius, despite the benefit of improving the resonant frequency and reducing the antenna volume, which potentially enhances the antenna's radiated power density, poses a challenge as it may increase the assembly complexity of the device. Moreover, increasing the rotor height can slightly improve the resonant frequency and enhance the antenna's radiation efficiency. However, maintaining a substantial height with a smaller rotor radius imposes higher demands on the manufacturing process due to increased technical complexity. In practical design considerations, these multifaceted factors must be cohesively balanced to select suitable dimensions, ensuring the optimal performance of the SBMPA.

Conclusion

In this study, we have developed a magnetic field model for radially magnetized cylindrical permanent magnets using the concept of magnetic charge. Our analysis focused on the influence of the demagnetizing field and the magnetic field generated by other permanent magnets on the magnetization of the permanent magnets in a self-biased magnetic pendulum array (SBMPA). From this analysis, we have successfully established the magnetic field model for SBMPA. The consistency of our proposed model has been validated through simulation results, showing an average deviation of only 0.021% for the magnetic field intensity (H) when compared to the simulation results.

Additionally, we investigated the impact of permanent magnet size and the distance between their centers on the resonance frequency of SBMPA. Our analysis revealed that reducing the radius and distance of the permanent magnet while increasing its height can effectively improve the resonance frequency of SBMPA.

The significance of this research lies in its contribution to providing more accurate calculations of the resonance frequency of SBMPA, thus facilitating improved design and optimization. Our findings hold promise for the development of miniaturized low-frequency antennas. Furthermore, future research should explore other factors that affect the radiation efficiency and performance of SBMPA, such as alternative materials and different arrangements of permanent magnets. Addressing these factors will enable researchers to develop a more comprehensive understanding of SBMPA and enhance its applications.

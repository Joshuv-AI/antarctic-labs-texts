On adjoint orbits in nilpotent ideals of a Borel subalgebra
Rupert W. T. Yu

Abstract
. Let 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} be a nilpotent ideal in the Borel subalgebra 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} of a complex finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra, and 𝔪 ∙ \mathfrak{m}^{\bullet} the subset of (ad-)nilpotent elements in 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} such that 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} is the minimal ideal containing them. This set is stable under the adjoint action of the corresponding Borel subgroup 𝐁 \mathbf{B} . We prove that 𝔪 ∙ \mathfrak{m}^{\bullet} contains a unique closed 𝐁 \mathbf{B} -orbit which is the orbit of a nilpotent element whose support is the set of minimal roots associated to the root space decomposition of 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} .

Primary 17B20, 14L30 ; Secondary 17B22, 20G05

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{b}
be a Borel subalgebra of a complex finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathbf{G}
the adjoint algebraic group of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathbf{B}
the
Borel subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{b}
.
We fix a decomposition
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
where
\mathfrak{n}

is the nilradical of
\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{h}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{b}
.
Observe that
\mathfrak{n}
is also the set of (ad-)nilpotent elements in
\mathfrak{b}
.

Adjoint nilpotent
\mathbf{G}
-orbits in
\mathfrak{g}
are classified by many combinatorial objects.
They are well studied,
and have many nice characterisations and properties (finiteness, algebraic and geometric). In contrast,

\mathbf{B}
-orbits of
\mathfrak{n}
are less well understood. There are in general an infinite number
of them, and they are not necessary conical. Kashin
[
5
]
determined exactly when there is a
finite number of
\mathbf{B}
-orbits in
\mathfrak{n}
. Hille and Röhrle
[
4
]
obtained a more
general result using a quiver model. Boos
[
1
]
and Melnikov
[
6
]
studied
\mathbf{B}
-orbits
in certain subvarieties
of
\mathfrak{n}
in type
A
. Note also that
connected components of the intersection of a nilpotent
\mathbf{G}
-orbit with
\mathfrak{b}
are called
orbital varieties which are related to irreducible components of the corresponding Springer
fibers
[
9
,
3
]
, and relations between nilpotent
\mathbf{G}
-orbits and nilpotent ideals in

\mathfrak{b}
are studied in
[
11
]
.

Since
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple,
an ideal in
\mathfrak{b}
is nilpotent if and only if it is contained in
\mathfrak{n}
.
For any nilpotent ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathfrak{b}
, we denote by
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}

the set of
X\in\mathfrak{n}
such that
\mathfrak{m}
is the minimal nilpotent ideal in
\mathfrak{b}

containing
X
. Thus
\mathfrak{n}
is the disjoint union of
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
where
\mathfrak{m}

runs through all nilpotent ideals of
\mathfrak{b}
.

Let
\Delta\supset\Delta^{+}\supset\Pi
be respectively the root system of
\mathfrak{g}
, the
set of positive roots and the set of simple roots relative to
(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{h})
. Denote by

\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
the root subspace relative to
\alpha
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
For any nilpotent ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathfrak{b}
, there exists a unique subset

\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
of
\Delta^{+}
such that

\mathfrak{m}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}_{\mathfrak{m}}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

Denote by
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
the set of minimal roots in
\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}

with respect to the usual partial order
\prec
on
\Delta
given by

\alpha\prec\beta
if
\beta-\alpha
is a sum of simple roots.
The main result of the paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a nilpotent ideal of
\mathfrak{b}
and

X\in\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}\cap\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
Then the
\mathbf{B}
-orbit of
X
is the unique closed
\mathbf{B}
-orbit in the
\mathbf{B}
-stable
subvariety
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
.

The key observation here is that being a set of pairwise non comparable positive
roots, a result of Sommers
[
10
]
says that
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is conjugate
under the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
to a subset of
\Pi
. This allows us
in section
3
to obtain sufficient information on
\mathfrak{n}
-invariant functions on

\mathfrak{m}
in order to prove that
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
contains a unique
closed
\mathbf{B}
-orbit. In section
4
, we prove a nice geometrical
property (Theorem
4.1
)
for sets consisting of pairwise non comparable positive roots which allows us to precise
exactly this closed orbit by studying orbits under the Cartan subgroup
\mathbf{T}
in

\mathbf{B}
whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{h}
.

Notations.

We shall conserve the notations above in the rest of the paper. Denote by

\mathbf{U}
the unipotent radical of
\mathbf{B}
which is the subgroup of
\mathbf{B}
generated by

\exp(\mathrm{ad}(X))
,

X\in\mathfrak{n}
. Recall that
\mathfrak{n}
is the Lie algebra of
\mathbf{U}
and

\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{T}\ltimes\mathbf{U}
.

For any linearly independent subset
\mathcal{I}
of
\Delta
and

\mathbb{A}\subset\mathbb{C}
, we shall denote by

\mathbb{A}\mathcal{I}
the set of elements of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
of the form

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}c_{\alpha}\alpha
where
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{A}
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{I}
.
Given
\beta=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}c_{\alpha}\alpha\in\mathbb{A}\mathcal{I}
, we shall denote by

0pt_{\mathcal{I}}(\beta)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}c_{\alpha}
the height of
\beta
with respect to

\mathcal{I}
.

2.
Nilpotent elements, nilpotent ideals and antichains

Let us fix a basis
(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}
of
\mathfrak{n}
where

X_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
For
X=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}c_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{n}
, we set

\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(X)=\{\alpha;c_{\alpha}\neq 0\}\

the support of
X
,
\mathcal{I}_{X}
the set of minimal roots in
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(X)
and
\mathfrak{m}_{X}

the minimal nilpotent ideal in
\mathfrak{b}
containing
X
.
The following lemmas are immediate from the definitions.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X\in\mathfrak{n}
and
\Delta_{X}^{+}=\{\alpha\in\Delta^{+};\alpha\succcurlyeq\beta
for some

\beta\in\mathcal{I}_{X}\}
.

(1)

We have
\mathfrak{m}_{X}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{X}^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
In particular,
\mathcal{I}_{X}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}_{X}}
.

(2)

For any
\sigma\in\mathbf{B}
, we have
\mathfrak{m}_{X}=\mathfrak{m}_{\sigma(X)}
and

\mathcal{I}_{X}=\mathcal{I}_{\sigma(X)}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a nilpotent ideal of
\mathfrak{b}
and

\mathfrak{m}^{\circ}
be the union of all nilpotent ideals in
\mathfrak{b}

contained strictly in
\mathfrak{m}
. Then

\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}=\{X\in\mathfrak{n}\,;\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}\supset\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(X)\supset\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}\}=\mathfrak{m}\setminus\mathfrak{m}^{\circ}.

is an open
\mathbf{B}
-stable subset of
\mathfrak{m}
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a nilpotent ideal of
\mathfrak{b}
. Then it is completely determined
by
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Being minimal elements of
\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
,
the set
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
contains pairwise non comparable roots, also called an

antichain
. Conversely, any antichain
\mathcal{I}
in
\Delta^{+}
defines
a nilpotent ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{X}
where
X
is any element in
\mathfrak{n}

verifying
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(X)=\mathcal{I}
. Thus the map
\mathfrak{m}\mapsto\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}

is a bijection between the set of nilpotent ideals in
\mathfrak{b}
and the set of antichains
in
\Delta^{+}
.

Theorem 2.3

(Sommers
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an antichain in
\Delta^{+}
. There exists an element
w
in the
Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
w(\mathcal{I})\subset\Pi
.

Remark 2.4
.

We obtain immediately from Theorem
2.3
that if

\mathcal{I}
is a non empty antichain in
\Delta^{+}
, then the set
\Delta_{\mathcal{I}}=\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{I}\cap\Delta=\mathbb{Q}\mathcal{I}\cap\Delta
is a reduced root system,
\mathcal{I}
is a set of simple roots of
\Delta_{\mathcal{I}}

and
\Delta_{\mathcal{I}}^{+}=\Delta_{\mathcal{I}}\cap\Delta=\mathbb{N}\mathcal{I}\cap\Delta

is the set of positive roots with respect to
\mathcal{I}
.

3.
Invariant functions and closed
\mathbf{B}
-stable subsets

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a nilpotent ideal in
\mathfrak{b}
.
By Remark
2.4
,
\Delta_{\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}}
is a reduced root system. Therefore

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

is a reductive Lie subalgebra in
\mathfrak{g}
. Let

\mathfrak{m}_{0}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}}^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\,\hbox{ and }\,\mathfrak{b}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{0}

which is a Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
whose nilradical is

\mathfrak{m}_{0}
.
Denote by
\mathbf{B}_{0}
the closed connected subgroup of
\mathbf{G}

whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
.

Let
(\xi_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Delta_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{+}}
be the dual basis of
the basis
(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Delta_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{+}}
of
\mathfrak{m}
.
The ring of regular functions on
\mathfrak{m}
decomposes
as a direct sum

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]=\mathbb{C}[\xi_{\alpha},\alpha\in\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}]=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in-\mathbb{N}\Pi}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]_{\alpha}

where

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]_{\alpha}
denotes
the vector space span of monomials
\xi_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\xi_{\alpha_{r}}
where

r\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
and

\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}=-\alpha
. Since
H(\xi_{\alpha})=-\alpha(H)\xi_{\alpha}

for any
H\in\mathfrak{h}
,

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]_{\alpha}
is also the
\mathfrak{h}
-weight
subspace of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]
of weight
\alpha
.

Note also that for
\alpha,\beta\in\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
,

X_{\alpha}(\xi_{\beta})
is a non zero multiple of
\xi_{\beta-\alpha}
if

\beta-\alpha\in\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
, and is zero otherwise.

Proposition 3.1
.

We have

\bigoplus_{\alpha\in-\mathbb{N}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]_{\alpha}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in-\mathbb{N}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]_{\alpha}^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}=\mathbb{C}[\xi_{\alpha},\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}].

Proof.

Let

\mathfrak{c}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}\setminus\Delta_{\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}}^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

Thus we have
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{c}
as
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
-modules.
As the composition of the natural morphisms of
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
-modules

\mathfrak{m}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{m}_{0}

is the identity map, the composition of the comorphisms

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]

is also the identity map. We shall identify
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]
with its image under
\pi^{*}

which is precisely
\mathbb{C}[\xi_{\alpha},\alpha\in\Delta_{\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}}^{+}]
. Hence

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]\oplus\ker(\iota^{*})

as
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
-modules. Consequently,
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}\oplus\ker(\iota^{*})^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}
.

Suppose that
\ker(\iota^{*})^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}_{\lambda}\neq 0
for some

\lambda\in-\mathbb{N}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Fix such a

\lambda=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi}c_{\alpha}\alpha
such that
0pt_{\Pi}(\lambda)

is maximal. Let

\varphi\in\ker(\iota^{*})^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}_{\lambda}
be
non zero. There exist
\beta\in\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
and
n>0
such that

\varphi=\psi_{n}\xi_{\beta}^{n}+\psi_{n-1}\xi_{\beta}^{n-1}+\cdots+\psi_{1}\xi_{\beta}+\psi_{0}

where
\psi_{k}\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{\alpha},\alpha\not\succcurlyeq\beta]_{\lambda+k\beta}

and
\psi_{n}\neq 0
.

For any
Z\in\mathfrak{m}_{0}
, we have

0=Z(\varphi)=Z(\psi_{n})\xi_{\beta}^{n}+\eta

where by our choice of
\beta
, the degree of
\eta
in
\xi_{\beta}
is at most
n-1
.
So
Z(\psi_{n})=0
.
Thus
\psi_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}_{\lambda+n\beta}
, and our choice
of
\lambda
implies that
\psi_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}
, and hence

\lambda+n\beta\in-\mathbb{N}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
.
It follows that

\beta\in\mathbb{Q}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}\cap\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}=\mathbb{N}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}\cap\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}=\Delta_{\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}}^{+}

(Remark
2.4
), and
so
\psi_{n}\xi_{\beta}^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]

which is impossible since
\varphi\in\ker(\iota^{*})^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}_{\lambda}
.
We have therefore established that

\bigoplus_{\alpha\in-\mathbb{N}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]_{\alpha}^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}
.

Now, we check readily that

\mathbb{C}[\xi_{\alpha},\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}]
is contained in both

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}
and

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]^{\mathfrak{n}}
.
We claim that

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}=\mathbb{C}[\xi_{\alpha},\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}]
.

Let us prove our claim. Observe that the ring of
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
-invariant regular functions on

\mathfrak{m}_{0}
is the ring of
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
-semi-invariant regular functions on
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
.
By Richardson’s dense orbit theorem
[
7
]
,
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
contains an open
\mathbf{B}_{0}
-orbit.
It follows from a theorem of Rosenlicht
[
8
]
that the field of
\mathbf{B}_{0}
-invariant rational functions
is
\mathbb{C}
. This implies that
\dim\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}_{\alpha}\leqslant 1

for any
\alpha\in-\mathbb{N}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Since

\mathbb{C}[\xi_{\alpha},\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}]\subset\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}
,
we have our claim.

We deduce immediately from our claim the required equalities since

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}=\mathbb{C}[\xi_{\alpha},\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}]\subset\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]^{\mathfrak{n}}\subset\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

The intersection of two non empty closed
\mathbf{B}
-stable subsets of
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}

is non empty.

Proof.

Let us conserve the notations in the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
Set
\mathbf{M}_{0}
to be the closed connected subgroup
of
\mathbf{B}_{0}
whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
.
In particular,
\mathbf{M}_{0}
is the subgroup generated by
\exp(\mathrm{ad}(X))

where
X\in\mathfrak{m}_{0}
.

By Proposition
3.1
and Lemma
2.2
,
\varphi=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}}\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}

and
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}=\{X\in\mathfrak{m};\varphi(X)\neq 0\}
.
So

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}]
is the localization
of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]
by the multiplicative subset generated by
\varphi
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\bullet}=\mathfrak{m}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
. Similarly,

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\bullet}]
is the localization
of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}]
by the multiplicative subset generated by
\varphi
.
As in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we obtain from the composition

\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\bullet}

of
\mathbf{B}_{0}
-equivariant morphisms that

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}]=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\bullet}]\oplus\ker(\iota^{*})

as
\mathbf{B}_{0}
-modules.

Let
J
be a proper
\mathbf{B}
-stable ideal in
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}]
.
Then
J+\ker(\iota^{*})
is
\mathbf{B}_{0}
-stable, and hence

(J+\ker(\iota^{*}))/\ker(\iota^{*})\simeq\iota^{*}(J)

is a
\mathbf{B}_{0}
-stable ideal in
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\bullet}]
.

As mentioned in the proof of Proposition
3.1
,

\mathfrak{m}_{0}
contains an open
\mathbf{B}_{0}
-orbit. Since
\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\bullet}

is open in
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
(Lemma
2.2
), we deduce that either
\iota^{*}(J)=0
or

\iota^{*}(J)=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\bullet}]
.

Suppose that
\iota^{*}(J)=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\bullet}]
.
Then there exists
(f,g)\in J_{0}\times\ker(\iota^{*})_{0}
such that

f+g=1
. In particular,
f
and
g
are non constant since
J
is proper.

Let
V
be the
\mathbf{M}_{0}
-submodule generated by
f
in
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}]
.
Then
V
is finite-dimensional, and we have
V^{\mathbf{M}_{0}}\neq 0
because
\mathbf{M}_{0}
is unipotent.
Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}\in\mathbf{M}_{0}
and
c_{1},\dots,c_{r}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}

be such that

\tilde{f}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}\sigma_{i}(f)
is a non zero element of
V^{\mathbf{M}_{0}}=V^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}
.

Set
\tilde{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}\sigma_{i}(g)
. Then
\tilde{f}+\tilde{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}
.
As
J
and
\ker(\iota^{*})
are
\mathbf{B}_{0}
-stable, we deduce that
\tilde{f}\in J^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}
, and
hence
\tilde{g}\in\ker(\iota^{*})^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}
. Moreover,
\tilde{f}
and
\tilde{g}
are both non constant
because
J
is proper, and by construction, they are sums of vectors of weights
in
-\mathbb{N}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
because
f
and
g
are of weight
0
.

Since
\tilde{g}\in\ker(\iota^{*})^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}
and

\varphi\in\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}

(Proposition
3.1
), there exists
n>0
such that
\varphi^{n}\tilde{g}\in\bigoplus_{\alpha\in-\mathbb{N}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]_{\alpha}^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}=\mathbb{C}[\xi_{\alpha},\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}]
(Proposition
3.1
).
This implies that
\tilde{g}\in\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\bullet}]
which is absurd because
\tilde{g}\in\ker(\iota^{*})
and
\tilde{g}\neq 0
.

We conclude that
\iota^{*}(J)=0
, and hence
J\subset\ker(\iota^{*})\neq\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}]
.

Consequently, the sum of two proper
\mathbf{B}
-stable ideals can never be
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}]
.
So the intersection of two non empty closed
\mathbf{B}
-stable subsets of
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
is
non empty.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

There is a unique closed
\mathbf{B}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
.

Proof.

Any
\mathbf{B}
-orbit of minimal dimension is closed, so a closed
\mathbf{B}
-orbit exists and
Theorem
3.2
says that it is unique.
∎

4.
Description of the unique closed orbit

We shall show in this section that the unique closed
\mathbf{B}
-orbit in Corollary
3.3

is the orbit of any element in
\mathfrak{m}

whose support is
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Our approach is to use the
following geometrical property concerning antichains of positive roots to study
the action of the torus
\mathbf{T}
on the projective variety
\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{m})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\Delta^{+}
be a non empty antichain.
There exists
(H,n)\in\mathfrak{h}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that

\alpha(H)\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
for all
\alpha\in\Pi
and

\gamma(H)=n

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Proof.

Let
\ell=\sharp\Pi
be the rank of
\Delta
,
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell}\}
,

(H_{1},\dots,H_{\ell})
the basis of
\mathfrak{h}
whose dual basis is
\Pi
.
Given
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{i}\alpha_{i}\in\Delta
, we set

\Pi_{\alpha}=\{\alpha_{i}\in\Pi\,;c_{i}\neq 0\}=\{\alpha_{i}\in\Pi\,;\alpha(H_{i})\neq 0\}
.

We shall prove the theorem by induction on
\ell
.
If
\ell=1
, then
\Gamma=\Pi
. So

(H_{1},1)
verifies the required conditions.
Let us suppose that
\ell>1
.

1) Suppose that
\sharp\Gamma\leqslant 2
.

If
\Gamma=\{\gamma\}
, then

(H_{1}+\cdots+H_{\ell},0pt(\gamma))
verifies the required conditions.

If
\Gamma=\{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\}
where

\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{i}\alpha_{i}
and
\gamma^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{i}^{\prime}\alpha_{i}

are distinct, set

I_{+}=\{i\,;c_{i}-c_{i}^{\prime}>0\}\ ,\ I_{0}=\{i\,;c_{i}=c_{i}^{\prime}\}\ ,\ I_{-}=\{i\,;c_{i}-c_{i}^{\prime}<0\}\ .

Since
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are non comparable, both
I_{+}
and
I_{-}
are non empty.
Let
c_{\pm}=\sum_{i\in I_{\pm}}c_{i}
,

c_{\pm}^{\prime}=\sum_{i\in I_{\pm}}c_{i}^{\prime}
,

c=\sum_{i\in I_{0}}c_{i}=\sum_{i\in I_{0}}c_{i}^{\prime}
and

\begin{array}[]{c}H=\sum\limits_{i\in I_{+}}(c_{-}^{\prime}-c_{-})H_{i}+\sum\limits_{i\in I_{-}}(c_{+}-c_{+}^{\prime})H_{i}+\sum\limits_{i\in I_{0}}H_{i}.\end{array}

Since
c_{-}^{\prime}>c_{-}>0
and
c_{+}>c_{+}^{\prime}>0
,

Remarks 4.2
.

(1)

In the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we have
\Pi_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=\Pi(m)
in Case 1
and most cases in Case 2.

(2)

Our proof relies on a type by type analysis of positive roots. As this is a geometrical property,
it would be nice to have a more geometric proof.

Observe that the natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathfrak{n}

commutes with the action of
\mathbf{B}
. We have therefore an action of

\widehat{\mathbf{B}}=\mathbf{B}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}

(resp.
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}=\mathbf{T}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
)
on
\mathfrak{n}
given by

(\sigma,\lambda)(X)=\sigma(\lambda X)=\lambda\sigma(X)

for
(\sigma,\lambda)\in\widehat{\mathbf{B}}
and
X\in\mathfrak{n}
.
For any group
\mathbf{H}
acting on
\mathfrak{n}
and
X\in\mathfrak{n}
,
we shall denote by
\Omega_{\mathbf{H}}(X)
the
\mathbf{H}
-orbit of
X
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a nilpotent ideal of
\mathfrak{b}
and

X\in\mathfrak{m}
be such that
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(X)=\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

(1)

The
\mathbf{B}
-orbit
\Omega_{\mathbf{B}}(X)
is conical.

(2)

For any
Y\in\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
, we have

X\in\overline{\Omega_{\widehat{\mathbf{T}}}(Y)}
.

(3)

\Omega_{\mathbf{B}}(X)
is the unique closed
\mathbf{B}
-orbit of
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
.

Proof.

1. By Theorem
2.3
, the set
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is linearly independent.
Therefore

(4.1)

\Omega_{\mathbf{T}}(X)=\{Y\in\mathfrak{m}\,;\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(Y)=\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}\}.

Hence
\Omega_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(X)\subset\Omega_{\mathbf{T}}(X)
, which in turn implies that

\Omega_{\widehat{\mathbf{B}}}(X)=\Omega_{\mathbf{B}}(X)
.

2. By Theorem
4.1
,
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is exactly the set of elements in

\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(Y)
lying on a face of the convex hull
of
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(Y)
. We obtain from
[
2
, Proposition 2.18]
that

\overline{\Omega_{\widehat{\mathbf{T}}}(Y)}
contains
an element in
\mathfrak{m}
whose support is
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
.
So the result follows by (
4.1
).

3. By Corollary
3.3
, it suffices to show that
\Omega_{\mathbf{B}}(X)
is closed in

\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
. Now points 1 and 2 say that for any
Y\in\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
, we have

\Omega_{\mathbf{B}}(X)=\Omega_{\widehat{\mathbf{B}}}(X)\subset\overline{\Omega_{\widehat{\mathbf{B}}}(Y)}.

It follows that if
Y\not\in\Omega_{\mathbf{B}}(X)
, then

\dim\Omega_{\mathbf{B}}(X)=\dim\Omega_{\widehat{\mathbf{B}}}(X)<\dim\overline{\Omega_{\widehat{\mathbf{B}}}(Y)}\leqslant\dim\Omega_{\mathbf{B}}(Y)+1.

Thus
\Omega_{\mathbf{B}}(X)
is a
\mathbf{B}
-orbit of minimal dimension in
\mathfrak{m}^{\bullet}
,
so it is closed.
∎

Observational Restrictions and Slow-Roll D-brane Inflation in the Special F ⁡ ( ϕ , T ) F(\phi,T) Gravity


Abstract
We shall investigate the inflation for the D-brane model, motivated by the modified gravity F ⁡ ( ϕ , T ) F(\phi,T) . This gravity has been recently introduced in the literature. The feasibility of the D-brane inflation theory in the F ⁡ ( ϕ , T ) F(\phi,T) -gravity has been studied in conjunction with the most recent Planck data. We shall analyze the slow-roll inflation in the context of the F ⁡ ( ϕ ) ​ T F(\phi)T -gravity, via the D-brane model. Then, we shall calculate the inflation dynamics to obtain the scalar spectral index “ n s n_{s} ” and the tensor-to-scalar ratio “ r r ”. Besides, we investigate the dynamics of the reheating for this model. Our model accurately covers the left-hand side of the Planck data and the D-brane inflation.

2
The inflation via the modified gravity
F(\phi)T

Here, we investigate the inflation via the
modified gravity that emerges from
the interaction between a scalar field and the
trace of the energy-momentum tensor.
Thus, we start with action
[
48
]
,

S=\int{{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}}\left(\frac{R}{2\kappa}+\beta F(\phi)g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{L}_{m}\right),

(2.1)

where we apply

\kappa=8\pi G=1/M_{\rm P}^{2}
, in which
M_{\rm P}
is
the Planck mass. The functional

\mathcal{L}_{m}
represents the matter
Lagrangian, which is given as follows

\mathcal{L}_{m}=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-V(\phi).

(2.2)

Besides,
T=g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}

is the trace of the energy-momentum tensor,
which is extracted
from
\mathcal{L}_{m}
. For the canonical scalar field
it is given by

T=g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}=-g^{\mu\nu}\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\partial(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{m})}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}=-g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-4V(\phi).

(2.3)

The functional
F(\phi)

should obviously satisfy the condition
F(0)=0
to
ensure that after decaying of the inflaton field

\phi
we receive the Einstein’s theory of gravity.
In this section, the functional
F(\phi)

and the potential
V(\phi)
are arbitrary.
However, by adjusting the parameter
“
\beta
” and choosing appropriate functionals

F(\phi)
and
V(\phi)
, we can match
the predictions of our inflationary model
with the observations.

The variation of the action (
2.1
) with
respect to the general metric
g_{\mu\nu}

defines the Einstein’s equation

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R=\kappa T_{\mu\nu}^{({\rm eff})},

(2.4)

where
T_{\mu\nu}^{({\rm eff})}
represents
the effective energy-momentum tensor, associated with the
total Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}^{({\rm eff})}_{m}=\beta F(\phi)T+\mathcal{L}_{m}
,

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{({\rm eff})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\partial(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}^{({\rm eff})}_{m})}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}=T_{\mu\nu}-2\beta F\left(T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Tg_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu}\right),

(2.5)

\Theta_{\mu\nu}\equiv g^{\alpha\gamma}\frac{\delta T_{\alpha\gamma}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}=-2T_{\mu\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{m}-2g^{\alpha\gamma}\frac{\delta^{2}\mathcal{L}_{m}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\alpha\gamma}}.

(2.6)

The explicit form of the symmetric tensor

\Theta_{\mu\nu}
, for the
real scalar field, possesses the feature

\Theta_{\mu\nu}=-\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-T_{\mu\nu}=-2\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-g_{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}-V(\phi)\right),

(2.7)

in which we used a homogeneous inflaton field
\phi=\phi(t)
.
In this case, the Lagrangian (
2.2
) reduces to

\mathcal{L}_{m}=\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}-V(\phi).

(2.8)

Note that we have utilized the metric
signature
(-,+,+,+)
. The variation of
the action (
2.1
) with respect to
\phi

gives the equation of motion of this field

(1+2\beta F)\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\phi-(1+4\beta F)\frac{\partial V(\phi)}{\partial\phi}-2\beta\frac{\partial F(\phi)}{\partial\phi}\left(2V-\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi\right)=0.

(2.9)

Now we employ a perfect
fluid, represented by
T_{\mu\nu}^{({\rm eff})}={\rm diag}\big(-\rho^{{\rm(eff})},p^{{\rm(eff})},p^{{\rm(eff})},p^{{\rm(eff})})
, where
\rho^{{\rm(eff})}
and

p^{{\rm(eff})}
show the effective energy
density and effective pressure.
Thus, these quantities obtain the following forms

\displaystyle T_{00}^{({\rm eff})}=\rho^{({\rm eff})}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\beta F)+(1+4\beta F)V,

\displaystyle T_{ij}^{({\rm eff})}=p^{({\rm eff})}g_{ij}=\Big[\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\beta F)-(1+4\beta F)V\Big]g_{ij}.

(2.10)

To determine the explicit form of the equation
of motion of the scalar field, we can use Eq. (
2.9
) and or
the continuity equation
\dot{\rho}^{({\rm eff})}+3H\big(\rho^{({\rm eff})}+p^{({\rm eff})}\big)=0
, which leads
to the generalized Klein-Gordon equation

(1+2\beta F)(\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi})+\beta F_{,\phi}\dot{\phi}^{2}+(1+4\beta F)V_{,\phi}+4\beta F_{,\phi}V=0.

(2.11)

where
V_{,\phi}={\rm d}V/{\rm d}\phi
and
F_{,\phi}={\rm d}F/{\rm d}\phi
.

By substituting Eqs. (
2
) and also the FRW
metric into the Einstein equation (
2.4
), the Friedmann
equations can be expressed in terms of
\rho^{({\rm eff})}

and
p^{({\rm eff})}

H^{2}=\frac{\kappa\rho^{({\rm eff})}}{3}=\frac{\kappa}{3}\bigg[\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}(1+2\beta F)+(1+4\beta F)V\bigg],

(2.12)

\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}=-\frac{\kappa}{6}\big(3p^{({\rm eff})}+\rho^{({\rm eff})}\big)=-\frac{\kappa}{3}\bigg[\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\beta F)-(1+4\beta F)V\bigg],

(2.13)

{\dot{H}}=\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}-H^{2}=-\frac{\kappa}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}\big(1+2\beta F\big).

(2.14)

Combining Eq. (
2.14
) with the time

2.12
) we acquire another

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}(1+2\beta F)+\beta F_{,\phi}\dot{\phi}^{2}+(1+4\beta F)V_{,\phi}+4\beta F_{,\phi}V=0.

(2.15)

We should note that one may directly extract

2.13

However, we applied the Einstein’s equation

g_{\mu\nu}

The slow-roll approach induces the conditions

{\dot{\phi}^{2}}\ll V
,
|\ddot{\phi}|\ll|3H\dot{\phi}|

F_{,\phi}\dot{\phi}^{2}\ll H\dot{\phi}

[

The higher-order perturbative corrections and

\phi\sim m

regulated by the intrinsic cut-off scale
\mu
,
embedded in the functional form of
F(\phi)

[

]

The specific coupling structure

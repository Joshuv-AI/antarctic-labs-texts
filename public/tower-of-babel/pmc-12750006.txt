Cost-effectiveness of hypofractionated radiation therapy versus conventional radiation therapy for unresectable stage III non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Hui Zhang, Yuhang Liu, Guihao Zeng, Shuangshuang Hu, Haonan Li, Hong Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractModerately hypofractionated radiation therapy (Hypo-RT) has emerged as a promising alternative to conventional radiation therapy (Con-RT) for patients with unresectable stage III non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) receiving concurrent chemotherapy. Emerging evidence suggests that Hypo-RT may offer advantages in overall survival and local control compared to Con-RT. However, despite its clinical benefits, the economic value of Hypo-RT for this specific indication remains underexplored. This study aimed to compare the cost-effectiveness of Hypo-RT with Con-RT in this patient population.Prior prospective and retrospective studies have demonstrated that Hypo-RT achieves superior local disease control without significantly increasing major toxicity in medically inoperable stage III NSCLC. Nevertheless, a comprehensive evaluation of its cost-effectiveness is lacking. Therefore, we sought to assess whether Hypo-RT represents a cost-effective alternative to Con-RT for the standard-of-care treatment of unresectable stage III NSCLC. We developed a three-stage Markov model with a 5-year lifetime horizon to evaluate the costs and clinical outcomes of Hypo-RT versus Con-RT. Primary outcomes included life years (LYs), total costs, quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), and the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER). To ensure robustness, we conducted one-way and probabilistic sensitivity analyses. Base-case analysis revealed that Hypo-RT yielded 2.17 QALYs at a total cost of $121,629.30, whereas Con-RT resulted in 1.65 QALYs at a higher cost of $183,926.18. Hypo-RT was not only more effective but also cost-saving, with an ICER of -$120,464.19 per QALY gained, indicating dominance over Con-RT. These findings were further supported by deterministic (DSA) and probabilistic sensitivity analyses (PSA), which consistently favored Hypo-RT as the more cost-effective strategy. Hypo-RT is likely to be a cost-effective and economically favorable option compared to Con-RT for patients with unresectable stage III NSCLC undergoing concurrent chemotherapy. These results support its broader adoption in clinical practice, pending further validation in real-world settings. Introduction

In 2022, lung cancer accounted for approximately 2.5 million new diagnoses and 1.8 million deaths globally, representing the most prevalent cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which accounted for 12.4% of all new cancer cases and 18.7% of cancer deaths globally, imposes significant physical, psychological, and socioeconomic burdens on patients, their families, and healthcare systems1–3. While the development of novel therapeutic agents has improved survival outcomes for NSCLC patients, the high cost of these treatments presents substantial financial challenges for both individuals and healthcare systems, particularly in Northern America, Eastern Asia, and Northern Europe1. Lung cancer, particularly NSCLC, imposes a substantial economic burden on healthcare systems. In China, a multicenter hospital-based study found that the average direct medical expenditure for intensive care unit (ICU) stage III NSCLC treatment was approximately 41,069 Chinese yuan (CNY)(6,359 US dollars [USD]) per patient4. Moreover, indirect costs-covering productivity loss of patients and caregivers-have also been documented: approximately $1,036 USD per patient per year, based on a 16-month disease duration estimate5.

Stage III NSCLC, also called locally advanced NSCLC, accounts for 30% of NSCLC patients6. Many of them have smoking-related cardiac or respiratory comorbidities that make them unfit for an operation. Historically, the standard treatment for unresectable stage III NSCLC consisted of concurrent or sequential chemotherapy combined with conventionally fractionated radiation therapy (60 Gy delivered in 30 fractions)7. While treatment approaches have advanced considerably since the 1980s-evolving from radiotherapy alone to modern concurrent chemoradiotherapy protocols-long-term clinical outcomes continue to be suboptimal. Notably, 5-year survival rates have remained stagnant at 15–20%, highlighting the need for more effective therapeutic strategies8.

Although multiple curative-intent treatment options exist, surgical resection continues to represent the gold-standard intervention for patients with resectable, early-stage NSCLC who meet functional operability criteria9–11.For those deemed unsuitable for surgical intervention, radiotherapy serves as both a safer alternative and a potentially curative treatment modality8. Historically, patients with inoperable early-stage NSCLC were treated with conventional radiation therapy (Con-RT), which was chosen as the comparator of the study, as it represents the historical standard for unresectable stage III NSCLC per American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) guidelines and ensures consistency with the source clinical trial’s control arm12,13.

Building upon Con-RT, hypofractionated radiation therapy (Hypo-RT) has emerged as a significant therapeutic advancement in recent years. This innovative approach delivers a higher biologically effective dose per fraction (typically 3.0–4.0 Gy) over fewer treatment sessions, with total doses ranging from 60 to 80 Gy, thereby achieving an ablative effect while potentially improving treatment efficiency14. Both prospective and retrospective studies have demonstrated that, compared with Con-RT, Hypo-RT resulted in superior local control of the primary disease without an increase in major toxicity in patients with medically inoperable stage I NSCLC15,16. Consequently, Hypo-RT has gained recognition as an innovative therapeutic approach for NSCLC and is being progressively adopted in clinical practice across China17.

In light of the rising incidence of NSCLC and the growing economic burden associated with its treatment, comprehensive cost-effectiveness analyses of emerging therapies have become increasingly essential18,19. Notably, to the best of our knowledge, this study constitutes the first formal economic evaluation of Hypo-RT within the Chinese healthcare context. Through the application of a Markov decision-analytic model, we systematically assess the economic value of Hypo-RT compared to Con-RT, with the ultimate goal of guiding clinical decision-making and informing healthcare policy development.

Materials and methods

Patients and intervention

The enrolled patients were derived from a multicenter, randomized, non-blinded phase 3 clinical trial comparing moderately hypofractionated radiation therapy (60 Gy in 20 fractions) with conventional radiation therapy (60 Gy in 30 fractions) in unresectable stage III NSCLC patients receiving concurrent chemotherapy. The study enrolled 146 patients who were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio, with 73 patients allocated to each treatment arm (Hypo-RT and Con-RT groups). The Hypo-RT group received radiation at a dose of 3 Gy per fraction, whereas the Con-RT group received 2 Gy per fraction, with both regimens administered daily from Monday to Friday. Radiation delivery methods differed between groups: the Hypo-RT group underwent treatment using helical TomoTherapy with a 6 megavoltage (MV) beam, while the Con-RT group received intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) at energies ranging from 6 to 18 MV. All participants completed two cycles of concurrent platinum-based chemotherapy followed by two additional cycles of consolidation therapy20.

All study procedures were rigorously performed in compliance with applicable ethical guidelines and regulatory requirements. The current analysis utilized data from a multicenter, randomized phase III clinical trial (Zhang et al.)13. Prior to study enrollment, written informed consent was obtained from all participating individuals or their legally authorized representatives. Besides, the phase 3 trial was conducted across 8 tertiary hospitals in China (Shanghai, Beijing, Guangzhou, and Chengdu), representing urban medical centers under the national public health system13. These institutions are all Grade-A tertiary hospitals within China’s healthcare system, serving diverse populations with universal coverage by the National Basic Medical Insurance. For the purpose of this analysis, we established standardized baseline patient characteristics, including a body weight of 70 kg, body surface area of 1.86 m², and creatinine clearance of 70 mL/min20. Adverse events (AEs) were only included in the analysis if they reached grade 3 or higher (G3+) in either treatment group (Hypo-RT or Con-RT). This study was carried out based on the phase 3 trial and did not involve other human participants, hence, there is no need for the approval of the independent ethics committee.

Clinical information on the target population

A total of 164 patients with unresectable Stage III NSCLC were screened. After excluding 15 patients with inconsistent staging and 3 patients who refused to participate, 146 patients were ultimately included and randomly assigned to two groups, with 73 patients in each group. Table 1provides detailed baseline characteristics of the participants. The study adopted uniform inclusion criteria, which included1: histologically confirmed unresectable Stage III NSCLC, as assessed by a multidisciplinary team2; an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status score of 0–23; weight loss of less than 10% in the past month4; cardiopulmonary function adequate to tolerate concurrent chemoradiotherapy; and5completion of baseline examinations, including contrast-enhanced chest computed tomography (CT), brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and laboratory tests such as complete blood count and hepatic and renal function tests. Meanwhile, the major exclusion criteria included1: distant metastasis (stage M1)2; prior thoracic radiotherapy3; active autoimmune disease4; severe cardiopulmonary insufficiency; and5 pregnant or breastfeeding women. Model construction

From the healthcare system perspective, which includes direct medical costs incurred by public hospitals and reimbursed by national health insurance schemes. These public schemes currently cover over 95% of the population and represent the primary payer for radiotherapy services in China21. This study developed a three-state Markov model (Fig. 1) using Microsoft Excel software (version 2024) to extrapolate clinical and economic outcomes beyond the trial period, incorporating progression-free survival (PFS), progressive disease (PD), and death states, and we extended the simulation period to 5 years (preliminary analyses confirmed that this duration would capture endpoint events for 99% of the patients), this allowed for the estimation of long-term cost-effectiveness. Figure 2 presents the and overall survival (OS) curves fitted using various statistical distributions. To maintain consistency with the clinical trial protocol, we set the simulation cycle duration at 3 weeks, which corresponds to the chemotherapy administration schedule.

Markov model structure.

The tornado plot of the one-way sensitivity analysis.

In this study, in accordance with the recommendations of the Chinese Guidelines for Pharmacoeconomic Evaluation 2020, this study applied a 5% annual discount rate was applied for future health utility and cost, with a discount rate variation range of 0% to 8%22. The analysis included three key outcome measures selected to holistically evaluate benefits and harms: life years (LYs) to reflect raw survival (higher = better longevity); quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) to integrate both survival and quality-of-life impacts (higher = longer and healthier life); and incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) to quantify value-for-money, with WTP thresholds contextualizing trade-offs23,24. Costs from previous years were adjusted using the consumer price index for healthcare of 2016–2023 and were shown in 2023 US dollars (1 USD = 7.08 CNY). Based on the China Guidelines for Pharmacoeconomic Evaluations (2020) and the recommendations of the World Health Organization (WHO), a willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold was set as the forecasted gross domestic product (GDP) per capita at 12,621.19 CNY (89,358 USD)−37,863.51CNY (268,074 USD), which was taken from The Statistical Communiqué of the People’s Republic of China on the 2023 National Economic and Social Development25–27. A treatment was considered cost-effective if its calculated ICER was lower than this established threshold28.

Model survival and transition probabilities

The OS and PFS data for both Hypo-RT and Con-RT groups were digitally extracted using GetData Graph Digitizer software (version 2.26). These survival curves were subsequently reconstructed and analyzed using R statistical software. To model the time-to-event data, we evaluated multiple parametric survival distributions, including log-logistic, log-normal, weibull, gompertz, exponential, and gamma distributions. Model selection was based on optimal goodness-of-fit criteria, with the final survival function chosen according to the lowest Akaike information criterion (AIC) and Bayesian information criterion (BIC) values29. Comprehensive model diagnostics are provided in Supplementary Tables 2 and 3. The fitted survival curves are presented in Supplementary Figures S1 (OS) and S2 (PFS) for visual comparison between the two treatment strategies. The shape (λ) and scale (γ) parameters for the selected survival function were derived directly from the R software (version 4.3.3) output. This study adhered to the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards (CHEERS) 2022 guidelines to ensure comprehensive reporting of the cost-effectiveness analysis. A detailed alignment of manuscript content with CHEERS checklist items is provided in Supplementary Table S4.

Cost and utility estimates

Our cost analysis adopted a healthcare system perspective, focusing exclusively on direct medical costs. These included1: radiotherapy expenses2, chemotherapy costs3, pharmaceutical expenditures4, management costs for grade ≥ 3 adverse events (AEs)5, monthly best supportive care, and6 diagnostic imaging costs (abdominal enhanced CT, chest enhanced CT, and MRI). The evaluated AEs comprised acute radiation pneumonitis (RP), leukopenia, fatigue, anemia, late RP, late radiation esophagitis (RE), and decreased platelet count. Cost estimates were primarily derived from published literature32–36, supplemented by expert consensus from 23 clinicians across > 11 Grade A tertiary hospitals in three Chinese provinces.

Monitoring protocols were established according to both pharmaceutical manufacturers’ recommendations and the 2023 Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) guidelines for NSCLC41. For effectiveness assessment, we employed QALYs, assigning utility values of 0.81 for PFS and 0.67 for PD based on established literature39. The model additionally incorporated disutility weights for AEs to account for their negative impact on quality of life, complete cost and utility parameters are systematically presented in Table 230,34,40.

Sensitive analysis

To assess model uncertainty, we employed one-way sensitivity analysis, with results presented in a tornado diagram that graphically demonstrates the relative impact of individual parameters on the ICER.

Furthermore, we conducted probabilistic sensitivity analysis to evaluate model robustness. This analysis involved performing 1,000 Monte Carlo simulations incorporating all key parameters. Cost parameters were modeled using gamma distributions, while utility parameters were characterized by beta distributions. The resulting cost-effectiveness acceptability curves and scatter plots provide comprehensive visualization of Hypo-RT’s probability of being cost-effective across the full range of WTP thresholds.

Results

Base case results

Within a 5-year time horizon and after applying a 5% annual discount rate, the Hypo-RT strategy demonstrated superior outcomes, yielding an average of 2.17 QALYs at a total cost of $121,629.30. In comparison, the Con-RT strategy provided 1.65 QALYs with higher costs of $183,926.18 (Table 3).

Our analysis showed that Hypo-RT dominated Con-RT, offering 0.52 additional QALYs while reducing costs by $62,296.89. This resulted in a negative ICER of -$120,464.19 per QALY gained, indicating that Hypo-RT is both more effective and cost-saving compared to Con-RT for unresectable stage III NSCLC patients receiving concurrent chemotherapy.

One-way sensitivity analysis

The tornado diagram for the one-way sensitivity analysis is shown in Fig. 2. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the parameters with the greatest impacts on the ICERs for the Hypo-RT and Con-RT strategies were the total drug costs (accounting for 92.58% of total costs, with 91.41% for Hypo-RT and 93.75% for Con-RT) and utility values for the PFS and PD states. Even when the costs of Hypo-RT and Con-RT were varied within their respective ranges, the ICER values remained below the WTP threshold, further confirming the robustness of the base-case results. For all other parameter variations, Hypo-RT continued to dominate Con-RT, reinforcing its cost-effectiveness in the treatment of unresectable stage III NSCLC.

Probabilistic sensitivity analysis

The probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA) results are presented in Figs. 3 and 4, depicting the cost-effectiveness plane and acceptability curves, respectively. Each data point corresponds to the incremental costs and QALYs derived from individual Monte Carlo simulations (Fig. 3). To quantify the probability of Hypo-RT being cost-effective, we calculated the proportion of simulations falling below specified WTP thresholds across a comprehensive range of values. Our analysis demonstrated that Hypo-RT dominated Con-RT in all 1,000 Monte Carlo iterations (100%). Notably, at China’s 2023 GDP-based WTP threshold of $12,621.19 per QALY gained, Hypo-RT maintained a 100% probability of being cost-effective. These robust findings consistently favored Hypo-RT across nearly all evaluated scenarios.

Value of information analysis

Probabilistic sensitivity analysis yielded an expected value of perfect information (EVPI) of 16,312 per patient at a WTP threshold of 65,000 per QALY (Fig. 5). The EVPI demonstrated threshold dependency, decreasing to 56,012 at a more conservative WTP threshold of 20,000/QALY.

Scenario analysis

Our price scenario analysis revealed that Hypo-RT maintained its cost-saving advantage over Con-RT even when Con-RT costs were reduced by 20% (Table 4; Fig. 6). However, with more substantial price reductions of Con-RT (30–50%), while Hypo-RT no longer demonstrated absolute cost savings, it continued to show favorable cost-effectiveness. Specifically, the ICERs for these scenarios were -$769.91, $23,069.62, $49,909.14, and $70,748.67 per QALY gained, respectively. Importantly, all ICER values remained well below China’s GDP-based willingness-to-pay threshold, indicating that Hypo-RT retained a 100% probability of being cost-effective across all evaluated scenarios, including those with significantly reduced Con-RT pricing (Table 4; Fig. 6).

Discussions

The rational allocation of national healthcare resources increasingly relies on pharmacoeconomic evidence to inform decision-making, particularly for novel therapeutics that may offer clinical benefits at higher costs. Globally, cancer remains a major public health challenge, with lung cancer representing one of the leading causes of mortality according to World Health Organization (WHO) data42. Among primary lung cancers, NSCLC constitutes approximately 85% of cases, with stage III (locally advanced) disease accounting for 30% of NSCLC diagnoses43,44. These epidemiological realities underscore the importance of rigorous pharmacoeconomic evaluations, which provide objective, comprehensive evidence to guide long-term healthcare policy planning, particularly in the domains of public health strategy and medical insurance coverage.

Among all NSCLC subtypes, unresectable stage III disease presents unique clinical challenges. A substantial proportion of these patients exhibit smoking-related cardiopulmonary comorbidities that preclude surgical intervention, making radiotherapy a safer and potentially curative alternative. The development of Hypo-RT has demonstrated significant improvements in both PFS and OS for this patient population, while simultaneously enhancing treatment efficacy. Regrettably, high incidences of toxicity for Hypo-RT (radiation esophagitis, 14%−83.3%; RP, 3.3%−28.6%) were also reported, similar toxicity profiles have been observed in other studies (e.g., Urbanic et al., 2018; Lin et al., 2013), highlighting the need for careful patient selection and toxicity management45–48.

This study conducted an economic evaluation focusing on patients with unresectable stage III NSCLC. We compared Hypo-RT with Con-RT as the reference intervention. The primary objective was to determine the cost-effectiveness of Hypo-RT relative to Con-RT in this patient population. In this research, Hypo-RT group resulted in 2.17 QALYs and an ICER of -$120,494.19/QALY compared to the Con-RT group, the negative ICER demonstrates that Hypo-RT is both more effective and cost-saving, reducing overall treatment costs while improving clinical outcomes. Probabilistic analyses-including variations in utilities, and costs further reinforced these findings, with 100% of simulations favoring Hypo-RT at China’s WTP threshold. Sensitivity analyses confirmed Hypo-RT’s cost-effectiveness across all tested scenarios: These results remained robust even when Con-RT costs were reduced by up to 50%. The results indicated that Hypo-RT is the more cost-effective treatment modality compared with Con-RT alone for the treatment of patients with unresectable stage III non-small cell lung cancer. For unresectable stage III NSCLC, current research generally agrees that the treatment model of continuing immunotherapy consolidation in patients without disease progression after concurrent chemoradiotherapy can provide significant survival benefits. Hypo-RT offers a cost-effective and clinically superior alternative to Con-RT for unresectable stage III NSCLC, supporting its adoption in clinical practice to improve patient outcomes and reduce healthcare costs6. Our findings align with broader trends in NSCLC cost-effectiveness literature, where advanced radiotherapy techniques like proton therapy (Qiu et al., 2021) and combined modality approaches (Corrao et al., 2023) demonstrate clinical superiority, though Hypo-RT uniquely achieves this while being cost-saving. This positions Hypo-RT as a dominant strategy for stage III NSCLC, complementing the value seen in precision diagnostics (Li et al., 2025) but with broader applicability in resource-limited settings49,51,51.

This study evaluated the cost-effectiveness of Hypo-RT versus Con-RT for unresectable stage III NSCLC. Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, our analysis relied on certain assumptions and was constrained by available data sources, parameter estimations, and model structure. Second, the PFS and OS curves were extrapolated using parametric distribution fitting. While we implemented multiple validation measures to ensure appropriate distribution selection, this methodological approach inherently introduces uncertainty into the model outputs and potential estimation errors. Most notably, our economic evaluation exclusively considered direct medical costs, thereby omitting potentially substantial indirect costs associated with disease management. This limitation may affect the comprehensiveness of our cost-effectiveness assessment.

The generalizability of our findings across healthcare systems requires careful consideration. While Hypo-RT is cost-effective in our analysis, its implementation faces barriers tied to infrastructure and resource disparities. In China, urban centers such as Beijing and Shanghai possess 2–4 times more linear accelerators per million population than western provinces, with advanced technologies like tomotherapy concentrated in tertiary hospitals52. A national survey further revealed that rural cancer patients were significantly less likely to access multi-hospital care than urban counterparts (χ² = 27.14, p < 0.001), exacerbating inequities in Hypo-RT adoption53. These disparities may contribute to the higher age-standardized cancer mortality observed in rural regions despite lower incidence rates54. Internationally, contextual factors such as reimbursement frameworks and resource availability also play a critical role-for example, durvalumab was deemed non-cost-effective under Vietnam’s local cost structures55. Thus, while our results are robust within China’s tertiary public hospitals, broader applicability hinges on addressing local infrastructure gaps, policies, and socioeconomic determinants to ensure equitable adoption.

In summary, our findings support the adoption of Hypo-RT as a cost-effective treatment strategy for patients with unresectable stage III NSCLC in China. Given its clinical efficacy and economic dominance over conventional RT, policymakers and payers are encouraged to prioritize resource allocation toward expanding access to high-precision radiotherapy technologies, particularly in under-resourced regions. This approach could contribute to more efficient allocation of healthcare resources and facilitate more equitable delivery of advanced radiotherapy services for patients with unresectable stage III NSCLC.

There are some limitations of this study1: This analysis did not assess subgroup heterogeneity due to the absence of stratified clinical outcomes in the source trial. Future studies with larger, diverse cohorts may refine cost-effectiveness estimates for specific patient subsets2. These data were not collected directly at the individual patient level but were based on aggregated and analyzed overall data, which may not fully capture the detailed characteristics of individual patients. In real-world clinical practice, eligibility for subsequent treatment is influenced by multiple factors. Particularly for patients with poor performance status, they are more likely to opt for best supportive care rather than active anti-cancer therapy3. The Markov model utilizes interim OS and PFS data from the trial, necessitating parametric extrapolation that may not fully reflect the actual clinical outcomes.These limitations could influence the precision of the economic evaluations for the treatment strategies.

Conclusions

From the perspective of the healthcare system, moderately Hypo-RT demonstrates superior cost-effectiveness compared to Con-RT for unresectable stage III NSCLC patients receiving concurrent chemotherapy. Hypo-RT not only provides better clinical outcomes, with a decremental cost-effectiveness ratio of $120,464.19 per QALY gained, but also reduces overall treatment costs by $62,296.89. Despite the reported toxicities such as radiation esophagitis and radiation pneumonitis, the benefits of Hypo-RT in terms of survival and cost savings outweigh these risks. These findings support the adoption of Hypo-RT in clinical practice to improve patient outcomes and optimize healthcare resource utilization. Future strategies should focus on further mitigating toxicity risks while maintaining the cost-benefit advantages of Hypo-RT.

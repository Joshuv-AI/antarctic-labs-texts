Counting square-free values of random polynomials
Efthymios Sofos

Abstract
. We prove that the average error term when counting square-free values of polynomials is the quartic root of the main term.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification

11N32, 11M06.

1.
Introduction

Let
P
be an integer polynomial.
A folklore conjecture states that

E_{P}(x):=\sharp\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[1,x]:P(n)\ \textrm{square-free}\}-\mathfrak{S}_{P}x=o(x)\ \ \ \textrm{ as }x\to\infty,

where

\mathfrak{S}_{P}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\textrm{ prime}\\
\ell=2\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{\sharp\{t\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2}\mathbb{Z}:P(t)=0\}}{\ell^{2}}\right).

The reader is referred to the
work of
Browning
[
1
]

for a list of
results towards this conjecture.
It must be noted that
even the existence
of infinitely many square-free values
represented by an
irreducible polynomial is only known
when its degree is at most
3
.

Assuming the abc-conjecture,
Granville
[
4
]

proved the conjecture,
a work that was later
extended by Poonen
[
12
]
,
Lee–Murty
[
8
]
and
Murty–Pasten
[
11
]
.

Another direction was taken by
Filaseta
[
3
]
,
Shparlinski
[
13
]
,
Browning–Shparlinski
[
2
]

and Jelinek
[
5
]
,
who proved an averaged version of the conjecture.
Regarding the first moment,
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]

states that for any
A>0,d\geqslant 4
and

\alpha<\frac{1}{d-3}
there exists

\delta>0
such that
for

H^{1/A}<x\leqslant H^{\alpha}
we have

(2H)^{-d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{primitive}\ P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\\
\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}|E_{P}(x)|=O(x^{1-\delta}),

where

|P|
denotes the maximum modulus of the coefficients of
P
.
Regarding the second moment,
[
5
, Theorem 1.4, Remark 1.7]

states that for any
A>0,d\geqslant 2
and

\alpha<\frac{1}{d/3+9/5}
there exists

\delta>0
such that
for

H^{1/A}<x\leqslant H^{\alpha}
we have

(2H)^{-d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{primitive}\ P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\\
\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}|E_{P}(x)|^{2}=O(x^{2-\delta}).

Let
\zeta
denote
the Riemann zeta function and define

(1.1)

\gamma_{0}:=-4\frac{\zeta(-1/2)}{\zeta(2)^{2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\ \mathrm{prime}\\
\ell=2\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{(2-\ell^{-1/2}+\ell^{-1})}{(\ell+1)^{2}}\right)=0.192\ldots\ .

We show that
E_{P}(x)
is typically
x^{1/4}
by
proving asymptotics for the second moment:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d,x
be integers
strictly larger than
2
.

(1)

For any
\alpha<\frac{1}{d+3}
there exists

\delta>0
such that
whenever
H
satisfies

x\leqslant H^{\alpha}
then

(2H)^{-d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\\
\deg(P)=d,\ |P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}|E_{P}(x)|^{2}=\gamma_{0}x^{1/2}+O(x^{1/2-\delta})

holds
with an implied constant depending only on

\alpha
and
\delta
.

(2)

For any
\varepsilon>0,0<\beta<\frac{1}{d+28/9}

and all
H
satisfying

x\leqslant H^{\beta}
we have

(2H)^{-d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\\
\deg(P)=d,\ |P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}|E_{P}(x)|^{2}=\gamma_{0}x^{1/2}+O(x^{4/9+\varepsilon}),

where
the implied constants
depend only on
\varepsilon
and
\beta
.

The order of magnitude
x^{1/2}
is not
apparent at first glance
since the terms
n=m
in

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\\
\deg(P)=d,\ |P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{n\leqslant x}\mu(n)^{2}\right)^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\\
\deg(P)=d,\ |P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\sum_{n,m\leqslant x}\mu(n)^{2}\mu(m)^{2}

contribute

cxH^{d+1}
for some constant
c
, where

\mu
denotes the Möbius funtion.
We will prove a full asymptotic expansion
for the contribution of the
terms
n\neq m
that contains the expression

-cH^{d+1}x
.
The new ingredient is the recognition
that Cesàro summation is built in
second moments of arithmetic functions over random polynomials of fixed degree. This allows us to use
Perron type integrals with extra convergent factors,
utilize bounds for the zeta function on the critical strip and shift the line of integration the left of
\Re(s)=-1/2
. As will be explained in Remark
5.1

the Lindelöf hypothesis or
subconvexity bounds
do not offer any advantage.

Remark 1.2

(Higher moments)
.

When one replaces
\mu^{2}

by the Liouville
\lambda
function
and
x\leqslant H^{O(1)}
by
x\leqslant(\log H)^{O(1)}
,
the first
d+1
moments were
asymptotically estimated by Wilson
[
15
]
, whereas,
all moments were successively asymptotically estimated by Kravitz–Woo–Xu
[
6
]
.
Given the work of Mirsky
[
9
]
,
asymptotics for all moments for
\mu^{2}
are possible, however, computations appear forbidding, especially regarding Cesàro summation. It would be interesting to see if

E_{P}(x)/\sqrt{x}
follows a probability distribution;
for
\lambda
the distribution is Gaussian
[
6
]
.

Notation
.

For an integer polynomial
P
we
denote the maximum modulus of its coefficients by

|P|.

For a non-zero integer
n
we write

n\mid P

to mean that
n
divides all coefficients of
P
. We denote the discriminant of
P
by
D_{P}
. Throughout the paper

d

will denote a fixed positive integer and we will use the notation

\mathscr{P}_{d}(H):=\{P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]:\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\}.

.

2.
Preparations

We shall work with the truncated version

\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)=\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant z}\mu(k)\frac{\varrho_{P}(k^{2})}{k^{2}}

where
z\geqslant 1
is a function of
d,x,H

that will be determined later.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
and
for any prime
\ell
we have

\varrho_{P}(\ell^{2})\leqslant\begin{cases}\ell^{2},&\ell^{2}\mid P,\\
\ell\deg(P),&\ell\mid P,\ell^{2}\nmid P,\\
\deg(P),&\ell\nmid D_{P},\\
\deg(P)(\ell+1),&\ell\mid D_{P},\ell\nmid P.\end{cases}

Proof.

The first case is trivial. Assume that
\ell^{2}\nmid P
.
If
\ell\mid P
then
\varrho_{P}(\ell^{2})=\ell\varrho_{P/\ell}(\ell)
, which is at most

\ell\deg(P)
because
P/\ell
is not identically
0
in
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
. This settles the second case
and we assume that
\ell\nmid P
for the rest of the proof. Write
\varrho_{P}(\ell^{2})=\sum_{t}N(t)
, where the
sum is over
t\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
with
P(t)=0
and
N(t)
is the number of
x\in\mathbb{Z}/{\ell^{2}}\mathbb{Z}
with
P(x)=0
and
x\equiv t\left(\textnormal{mod}\ \ell\right)
.
If
P^{\prime}(t)\neq 0
then
N(t)\leqslant 1
and if

P^{\prime}(t)=0
then
N(t)\leqslant\ell
. We obtain

\varrho_{P}(\ell^{2})\leqslant\sharp\{t\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}:P(t)=0,P^{\prime}(t)\neq 0\}+\ell\sharp\{t\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}:P(t)=0=P^{\prime}(t)\},

which is
\leqslant\deg(P)+\ell\mathds{1}_{\ell\mid D_{P}}\sharp\{t\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}:P(t)=0=P^{\prime}(t)\}

because
\ell\nmid P
. This is at most
\deg(P)

when
\ell\nmid D_{P}
, thus, settling the third case.
If
\ell\mid D_{P}
, then we
use
\sharp\{t\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}:P(t)=0=P^{\prime}(t)\}\leqslant\deg(P)

to conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For

P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
with
\deg(P)\geqslant 1
,
any
\varepsilon>0
and
x\geqslant 1

we have

\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant x}\mu(k)^{2}\varrho_{P}(k^{2})\ll_{\deg(P),\varepsilon}(3\deg(P))^{2\omega(D_{P})}x^{1+\varepsilon}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N},k^{2}\mid P}k,

where the implied constant depends only on

\deg(P)
and
\varepsilon
. If
D_{P}=0
then

\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant x}\mu(k)^{2}\varrho_{P}(k^{2})\ll_{\deg(P),\varepsilon}x^{\varepsilon}\mathscr{C}(P)^{1+\varepsilon},

where
\mathscr{C}(P)
is the content of
P
.

Proof.

Assume that
D_{P}\neq 0
and write
k=k_{1}k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}
, where
k_{i}
is the product of all primes
\ell\mid k
corresponding to the
i
-th case
of Lemma
2.1
.
Then the sum over
k
in the lemma is at most

\sum_{k_{1}^{2}\mid P}k_{1}^{2}\sum_{k_{2}\mid P}\mu(k_{2})^{2}k_{2}\deg(P)^{\omega(k_{2})}\sum_{k_{4}\mid D_{P}}\mu(k_{4})^{2}(\deg(P))^{\omega(k_{4})}\sigma(k_{4})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{3}\leqslant x/(k_{1}k_{2}k_{4})\end{subarray}}\mu(k_{3})^{2}{\deg(P)}^{\omega(k_{3})},

where
\sigma
is the sum of divisors function. The sum over
k_{3}
is
O_{\deg(P),\varepsilon}(x^{1+\varepsilon}/(k_{1}k_{2}k_{4}))
, thus, we get

\ll_{\deg(P),\varepsilon}x^{1+\varepsilon}\sum_{k_{1}^{2}\mid P}k_{1}\sum_{k_{2}\mid P}\mu(k_{2})^{2}{\deg(P)}^{\omega(k_{2})}\sum_{k_{4}\mid D_{P}}\mu(k_{4})^{2}\frac{\sigma(k_{4})}{k_{4}}(\deg(P))^{\omega(k_{4})}.

The sum over
k_{2}
is at most
(2\deg(P))^{\omega(P)}\leqslant(2\deg(P))^{\omega(D_{P})}

and the sum over
k_{4}
is

\leqslant\prod_{\ell\mid D_{P}}\left(1+\frac{\deg(P)(\ell+1)}{\ell}\right)\leqslant(3\deg(P))^{\omega(D_{P})}.

This completes the proof when
D_{P}\neq 0
.

If
D_{P}=0
then we note that

\rho_{P}(\ell^{2})\leqslant\ell^{2}
when
\ell^{2}\mid P

and
\rho_{P}(\ell^{2})\leqslant\deg(P)(\ell+1)
when
\ell^{2}\nmid P
. Factoring
k
as
k_{1}k_{2}
,
where
k_{1}
is composed of primes
\ell\mid k
with

\ell^{2}\mid P
, gives the following bound for the
sum in the lemma:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}k_{2}\leqslant x\\
k_{1}^{2}\mid P,k_{2}\mid P\end{subarray}}\mu(k_{1}k_{2})^{2}k_{1}^{2}\sigma(k_{2})\deg(P)^{\omega(k_{2})}\ll_{\deg(P),\varepsilon}x^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}^{2}\mid P\\
k_{2}\mid P\end{subarray}}\mu(k_{1}k_{2})^{2}k_{1}^{2}k_{2}.

Note that
k_{1}^{2}k_{2}
is upper-bounded by the content of
P
, hence, the divisor bound completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Fix
d\geqslant 3
and

\varepsilon>0
. Then for all
H,z\geqslant 1

we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)\\
D_{P}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{k>z}\mu(k)\frac{\varrho_{P}(k^{2})}{k^{2}}\right)^{2}\ll_{d,\varepsilon}\frac{H^{d+1+\varepsilon}}{z^{2-\varepsilon}},

where the implied constant
depends only on
\deg(P)
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

When
P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)
and
D_{P}\neq 0
,
Lemma
2.2

and partial summation yields

(2.1)

\left|\sum_{t>z}\mu(t)\frac{\varrho_{P}(t^{2})}{t^{2}}\right|\ll_{d,\varepsilon}\frac{(3d)^{\omega(D_{P})}}{z^{1-\varepsilon}}\sum_{k^{2}\mid P}k\ll_{d,\varepsilon}\frac{H^{\varepsilon}}{z^{1-\varepsilon}}\sum_{k^{2}\mid P}\mu(k)^{2}k

uniformly in
P
and
z
.
This is due to
|P|\leqslant H
,
the bound
D_{P}\ll H^{c(d)}
that holds for some
c(d)\geqslant 0
and

\omega(t)\ll(\log t)/(\log\log t)
.
The overall bound then becomes

\ll\frac{(Hz)^{2\varepsilon}}{z^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)\\
D_{P}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{k^{2}\mid P}k\right)^{2}=\frac{(Hz)^{2\varepsilon}}{z^{2}}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}}k_{1}k_{2}\sharp\{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H):[k_{1},k_{2}]^{2}\mid P\},

where
[k_{1},k_{2}]
is the least common multiple. Let
\delta:=\gcd(k_{1},k_{2})
so that

k_{i}=\delta s_{i}
and the sum over
k_{i}
becomes

\ll\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}}k_{1}k_{2}\left(\frac{H}{[k_{1},k_{2}]^{2}}\right)^{d+1}\leqslant\sum_{\delta,s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{N}}\delta^{2}s_{1}s_{2}\left(\frac{H}{(\delta s_{1}s_{2})^{2}}\right)^{d+1}\ll H^{d+1}.

When
D_{P}=0
,
we use Lemma
2.2
to get

\left|\sum_{t>z}\mu(t)\frac{\varrho_{P}(t^{2})}{t^{2}}\right|\ll_{d,\varepsilon}\frac{\mathscr{C}(P)^{1+\varepsilon}}{z^{1-\varepsilon}},

hence,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)\\
D_{P}=0\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{k>z}\mu(k)\frac{\varrho_{P}(k^{2})}{k^{2}}\right)^{2}\ll_{d,\varepsilon}z^{-2+\varepsilon}H^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)\\
D_{P}=0\end{subarray}}\mathscr{C}(P)^{2}.

Letting
\mathscr{V}:=\mathscr{C}(P)

and writing
Q=P/\mathscr{V}
,
the sum in the right-hand side becomes

\sum_{\mathscr{V}\leqslant H}\mathscr{V}^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H/\mathscr{V})\\
D_{Q}=0\end{subarray}}1\ll\sum_{\mathscr{V}\leqslant H}\mathscr{V}^{2}\frac{H^{d}}{\mathscr{V}^{d}}\ll H^{d}\log H

by
[
7
]

and
d\geqslant 3
. ∎

3.
Transition to the model

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
x,H,z,d\geqslant 1
we have

(2H)^{-d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)\end{subarray}}\sum_{n\leqslant x}\mu(P(n))^{2}=\frac{x}{\zeta(2)}+O\left(\frac{x}{z}+\frac{x^{1+d/2}}{H^{1/2}}+\frac{xz}{H}\right),

where
the implied constant depends only
on
d
.

Proof.

We have

\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}\sum_{n\leqslant x}\mu(P(n))^{2}=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{k\leqslant z}\mu(k)\sharp\{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H):k^{2}\mid P(n)\}+O(E),

where
the implied constant is absolute and

E=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{z<k\ll\sqrt{(d+1)x^{d}H}}|\mu(k)|\sharp\{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H):k^{2}\mid P(n)\}.

Fixing all coefficients of
P
except
the constant
we obtain

\sharp\{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H):k^{2}\mid P(n)\}\ll H^{d}\left(\frac{H}{k^{2}}+1\right)\ll\frac{H^{d+1}}{k^{2}}+H^{d},

thus,

E\ll x(H^{d+1}z^{-1}+H^{d+1/2}x^{d/2})
.
Writing
P=\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}t^{j}

the main term equals

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
k\leqslant z\end{subarray}}\mu(k)\sum_{|c_{1},\ldots,c_{d}|\leqslant H}\sharp\left\{|c_{0}|\leqslant H:c_{0}\equiv-\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}n^{j}\left(\textnormal{mod}\ k^{2}\right)\right\}.

The inner cardinality equals
2Hk^{-2}+O(1)
, hence, we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
k\leqslant z\end{subarray}}\mu(k)((2H)^{d+1}k^{-2}+O(H^{d}))=\frac{x}{\zeta(2)}(2H)^{d+1}+O\left(\frac{xH^{d+1}}{z}+xH^{d}z\right).\qed

For
x,H,z\geqslant 1
write

\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z):=\sum_{k\leqslant z}\mu(k)\frac{\varrho_{P}(k^{2})}{k^{2}}\ \ \ \textrm{ and }\ \ \ V(H,x,z):=\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}\left(S_{P}(x)-\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)x\right)^{2}.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
H,z,x\geqslant 1
and any fixed
\varepsilon>0

we have

\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}E_{P}(x)^{2}=V(H,x,z)+O_{d,\varepsilon}\left(V(H,x,z)^{1/2}x\left(\frac{H^{d+1+\varepsilon}}{z^{2-\varepsilon}}\right)^{1/2}+x^{2}\frac{H^{d+1+\varepsilon}}{z^{2-\varepsilon}}\right),

with an implied constant that depends only on

\varepsilon
and
d
.

Proof.

Since
E_{P}(x)=O(x)
, the sum in the lemma equals

(3.1)

\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}(S_{P}(x)-\mathfrak{S}_{P}x)^{2}+O(x^{2}H^{d}\log H).

We expand
(S_{P}(x)-\mathfrak{S}_{P}x)^{2}
as

(S_{P}(x)-\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)x)^{2}+O(|S_{P}(x)-\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)x|\cdot|\mathfrak{S}_{P}-\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)|x+|\mathfrak{S}_{P}-\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)|^{2}x^{2}).

Cauchy’s inequality
shows that the sum in (
3.1
)
equals

V(H,x,z)
up to a quantity of modulus

\ll V(H,x,z)^{1/2}x\left(\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}|\mathfrak{S}_{P}-\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+x^{2}\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}|\mathfrak{S}_{P}-\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)|^{2}.

Alluding to
Lemma
2.3
concludes the proof.
∎

For
x,z\geqslant 1
,
n\in\mathbb{Z},P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
define

\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{N}\cap[1,z]\\
k^{2}\mid n\end{subarray}}\mu(k)\ \ \ \textrm{ and }\ \ \ S_{P}(x,z)=\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant x}\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(n)).

Lemma 3.3
.

For any

x,z,H\geqslant 1
we have

\displaystyle V(H,x,z)

\displaystyle=(2H)^{d+1}\frac{x}{\zeta(2)}+O\left(xzH^{d}+(xH)^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{x^{2}H^{d+1}}{z}+x^{2+d/2}H^{d+1/2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leqslant n\neq m\leqslant x}\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(n))\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(m))

\displaystyle-2x\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant x}\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(n))\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)+x^{2}\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant x}\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)^{2}.

Proof.

The quantity
V(H,x,z)
equals

(3.2)

\sum_{n,m\leqslant x}\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}\mu(P(n))^{2}\mu(P(m))^{2}-2x\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)\mu(P(n))^{2}+x^{2}\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)^{2}.

The terms
n=m
give rise to
\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{P}\mu(P(n))^{2}

that can be estimated by Lemma
3.1
.
Writing
\mu(P(m))^{2}
as

\sum_{k^{2}\mid P(m)}\mu(k)^{2}
,
we see that the contribution of the terms
k>z
towards (
3.2
) is

\ll\sum_{n\neq m\leqslant x}\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}\mu(P(n))^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k>z\\
k\mid P(m)\end{subarray}}\mu(k)^{2}\ll xE,

where
E
is as in the proof of
Lemma
3.1
. Using the bound
for
E
proved there we obtain the
error term
\ll x^{2}(H^{d+1}z^{-1}+H^{d+1/2}x^{d/2}).
Thus, up to an admissible
error term, the
first term in (
3.2
) is

\sum_{n\neq m\leqslant x}\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H),\ D_{P}\neq 0}\mu(P(n))^{2}\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(m)).

We next bound
the contribution of the terms
k>z

in

\mu(P(n))^{2}=\sum_{k\mid P(n))}\mu(k)^{2}

similarly. By the bound
\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(m))\ll\tau(P(m))\ll_{\varepsilon}(xH)^{\varepsilon}
we obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\neq m\leqslant x\\
P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)\end{subarray}}\left(\mu(P(n))^{2}\mu(P(m))^{2}-\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(n))\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(m))\right)\ll(xH)^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{x^{2}H^{d+1}}{z}+x^{2+d/2}H^{d+1/2}\right).

To deal with the second term in (
3.2
) we note that

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)(\mu(P(n))^{2}-\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(n)))\ll\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}|\mathfrak{S}_{P}(z)|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k>z\\
k^{2}\mid P(n)\end{subarray}}\mu(k)^{2}.

Using (
2.1
) with
T=1
this is

\ll x\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\leqslant\sqrt{H}\\
z<k\ll\sqrt{Hx^{d}}\end{subarray}}\mu(t)^{2}t\mu(k)^{2}\sharp\{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H):t^{2}\mid P,k^{2}\mid P(n)\}.

Writing
g:=P/t^{2}
we obtain

\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
t\geqslant 1,k>z\end{subarray}}\hskip-8.5359pt\mu(t)^{2}t\mu(k)^{2}\sharp\{g\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H/t^{2}):(k/\gcd(k,t))^{2}\mid g(n)\}\ll\hskip-14.22636pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\leqslant\sqrt{H}\\
z<k\ll\sqrt{Hx^{d}}\end{subarray}}\hskip-14.22636ptxt\left(\frac{H}{t^{2}}\right)^{d}\hskip-2.84544pt\left(\frac{H\gcd(k,t)^{2}}{t^{2}k^{2}}+1\right).

Using
\gcd(k,t)\leqslant t
this becomes

\ll xH^{d+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\leqslant\sqrt{H}\\
z<k\ll\sqrt{Hx^{d}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{t^{2d-1}}\frac{1}{k^{2}}+x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\leqslant\sqrt{H}\\
z<k\ll\sqrt{Hx^{d}}\end{subarray}}\frac{H^{d}}{t^{2d-1}}\ll\frac{xH^{d+1}(\log H)}{z}+x^{1+d/2}H^{d+1/2}\log H.\qed

Lemma 3.4
.

For any prime
\ell
,
and
d\geqslant 1
and
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}

we have

\frac{\sharp\{g\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2}\mathbb{Z})[t]:\deg(g)\leqslant d,g(n)=g(m)=0\}}{\ell^{2(d+1)}}=\begin{cases}\ell^{-4},&v_{\ell}(m-n)=0,\\
\ell^{-3},&v_{\ell}(m-n)=1,\\
\ell^{-2},&v_{\ell}(m-n)\geqslant 2.\end{cases}

Proof.

We write
g=\sum_{i=0}^{d}c_{i}t^{i}

where
\mathbf{c}\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{d+1}
. Thus,
describes a system of two linear equations in
d+1
variables over
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2}\mathbb{Z}
. We fix
c_{i}
for each
i\geqslant 2

to end up with a
2\times 2
system whose determinant is
m-n
. When
\ell\nmid m-n
the variables
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
are fully determined by the remaining
c_{i}
, which proves the first case.
When
v_{\ell}(n-m)=1
then the system

g(n)=g(m)=0\mod\ell^{2}
is equivalent to

g(n)=0\left(\textnormal{mod}\ \ell^{2}\right),g^{\prime}(m)=0\left(\textnormal{mod}\ \ell\right)
.
The second equation determines
c_{1}\left(\textnormal{mod}\ \ell\right)
and the first equation determines
c_{0}\left(\textnormal{mod}\ \ell^{2}\right)
,
thus, we obtain
\ell^{-3}
. When
\ell^{2}\mid m-n

the system
g(n)=g(m)=0\mod\ell^{2}
is the
same as
g(n)=0\left(\textnormal{mod}\ \ell\right)^{2}
. In this case

c_{0}\left(\textnormal{mod}\ \ell^{2}\right)
is determined uniquely the remaining
c_{i}
, which gives the factor
\ell^{-2}
.∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For any

d\geqslant 1
, any prime
\ell
,
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2}\mathbb{Z})[t],\deg(g)\leqslant d\\

The first sum can be written as

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2}\mathbb{Z}}\sharp\{g\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2}\mathbb{Z})[t]:\deg(g)\leqslant d,g(n)=0=g(x)\}=\ell^{2}\ell^{2(d+1)}\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\ell}{\ell^{4}}+\frac{\ell-1}{\ell^{3}}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right)

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

For any

where the implied constant depends only on

\ell^{-1},&v_{\ell}(m-n)=1,\\
1,&\ell^{2}\mid m-n\end{cases}\right).

Proof.

We write the sum as

\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\leqslant z}\mu(k_{1})\mu(k_{2})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\left(\textnormal{mod}\ (k_{1}k_{2})^{2}\right),\deg(g)\leqslant d\\
k_{1}^{2}\mid g(n),k_{2}^{2}\mid g(m)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)\\

Factoring

as

and recalling

in the proof of

we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{0}t_{1}\leqslant z\\
\gcd(t_{1},t_{2})=1,t_{0}t_{2}\leqslant z\end{subarray}}\mu(t_{0}t_{1})\mu(t_{0}t_{2})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\left(\textnormal{mod}\ (k_{1}k_{2})^{2}\right),\deg(g)\leqslant d\\
k_{1}^{2}\mid g(n),k_{2}^{2}\mid g(m)\end{subarray}}\Xi,

By Lemma
3.4

the number of

, where

f_{k}(t):=\prod_{\ell\mid t}\begin{cases}\ell^{-2},&\text{if }v_{\ell}(k)=0,\\
\ell^{-1},&\text{if }v_{\ell}(k)=1,\\

4.
Cesàro summation

Even if a sum
S(N)=\sum_{n\leqslant N}b_{n}
diverges as
N\to\infty
,
the average of the sums

\frac{1}{x}\sum_{N\leqslant x}S(N)=\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leqslant x}b_{n}(x+1-n),\ \ \ x\in\mathbb{N}

might still converge. If
not, one can iterate this process
to increase the chance of convergence.
This procedure, typically called Cesàro summation,
is common in Fourier analysis.

In our set-up, Cesàro summation
is built in the second moment expression.
Indeed, the non-diagonal terms in the second moment
give rise to

\sum_{1\leqslant n\neq m\leqslant x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(n))\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(m)),

which, by Lemma
3.7
,
is approximated by

(2H)^{d+1}\sum_{1\leqslant n\neq m\leqslant x}c(m,n).

By definition,
c
is a function of
m-n

and letting
k=m-n
we get

(2H)^{d+1}2\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant x}(x-k)b(k),\ \ \ x\in\mathbb{N},

where
b(m-n)=c(m,n)
.
This allows us to use a version of
Perron’s formula
from
[
10
, §5.1.1]
:

\sum_{1\leqslant n\neq m\leqslant x}c(m,n)=\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{\Re(s)=\theta}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{b(k)}{k^{s}}\right)\frac{x^{s+1}}{s(s+1)}\mathrm{d}s

for all
\theta>1
. This converges owing to

|c(m,n)|=\prod_{\ell}(1+O(\ell^{-2}))=O(1)
,
uniformly in
m,n
.

Remark 4.1
.

The situation is delicate since
we are aiming at an error term of size
O(\sqrt{x})
. This can be achieved only if
we shift the contour of integration into a subset of
\Re(s)\leqslant-1/2
.
It turns out that the Dirichlet series of
b(k)

is essentially
\zeta(s)
and this causes
difficulties as the functional equation shows that the size of
\zeta(s)
increases as fast as the
Gamma function
when
\Re(s)<0
. It is therefore important to truncate the integral to
-T\leqslant\Im(s)\leqslant T
for a small
T
. The factor
1/s(s+1)
helps
but is not enough. One must
take into account cancellation in the values of
x^{\theta+it}
as
t\to\infty
. This is the goal of the next lemmas.

Lemma 4.2
.

For all
\theta\in[0,2],T\geqslant 2
and
y>0
with
y\neq 1
we have

\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Re(s)=\theta\\
|\Im(s)|\geqslant T\end{subarray}}\frac{y^{s}}{s(s+1)}\mathrm{d}s\ll\frac{y^{\theta}}{T^{2}|\log y|},

with an absolute implied constant.

Proof.

Writing
s=\theta+it
we use

y^{it}=(y^{it})^{\prime}/(i\log y)

to get
\ll x^{\theta}(\log y)^{-1}(|I_{1}|+|I_{2}|)
, where

I_{1}=\int_{T}^{\infty}\frac{(y^{it})^{\prime}\mathrm{d}t}{(\theta+it)(\theta+it+1)}=-\frac{y^{iT}}{(\theta+iT)(\theta+iT+1)}+\int_{T}^{\infty}\frac{iy^{it}(2\theta+2it+1)\mathrm{d}t}{(\theta+it)^{2}(\theta+it+1)^{2}},

and where
I_{2}
is defined similarly with
t
replaced by
-t
. Using
\theta=O(1)

we see that

|I_{1}|+|I_{2}|\ll\frac{1}{T^{2}}+\int_{T}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{3}}\ll T^{-2}.\qed

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be bounded by
1
in modulus.
Then for all
x\in\mathbb{N},\theta\in(1,2]
we have

\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant x}f(k)(x-k)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\theta-iT}^{\theta+iT}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(k)}{k^{s}}\frac{x^{s+1}\mathrm{d}s}{s(s+1)}+O\left(\frac{x}{T}+\frac{x^{1+\theta}}{T^{2}(\theta-1)}+\frac{x^{2}\log(x+1)}{T^{2}}\right),

where the implied constant is absolute.

Proof.

By
[
10
, pages 142-143]

for
y>0,\theta>1
we have

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Re(s)=\theta}\frac{y^{s}}{s(s+1)}\mathrm{d}s=\begin{cases}1-1/y,&y\geqslant 1\\
0,&0<y\leqslant 1.\end{cases}

Combining with Lemma
4.2

we obtain the following for all
T\geqslant 2
and
y>0
,

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Re(s)=\theta\\
|t|\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\frac{y^{s}}{s(s+1)}\mathrm{d}s=O\left(\frac{y^{\theta}}{T^{2}|\log y|}\right)+\begin{cases}1-1/y,&y>1\\
0,&0<y<1\end{cases}

while, when
y=1
the right-hand side is
\ll T^{-1}
.
We obtain

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\theta-iT}^{\theta+iT}\frac{f(k)}{k^{s}}\frac{x^{s}\mathrm{d}s}{s(s+1)}+O\left(\begin{cases}(x/k)^{\theta}T^{-2},&k\geqslant 1,k\neq x,\\
T^{-1},&k=x.\end{cases}\right)=f(k)\begin{cases}1-\frac{k}{x},&k\leqslant x,\\
0,&k\geqslant x\end{cases}

Summing this over all
k
gives

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\theta-iT}^{\theta+iT}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(k)}{k^{s}}\frac{x^{s+1}}{s(s+1)}\mathrm{d}s+O\left(\frac{x}{T}+\frac{x^{1+\theta}}{T^{2}}\sum_{k\neq x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{\theta}|\log(x/k)|}\right)=\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant x}f(k)(x-k).

The error term
is

\ll\frac{x}{T}+\frac{x^{1+\theta}}{T^{2}}\sum_{k\neq x}\frac{1}{k^{\theta}|\log(x/k)|}\ll\frac{x}{T}+\frac{x^{1+\theta}}{T^{2}}\left(\sum_{|k-x|>x/2}\frac{1}{k^{\theta}}+\sum_{0<|k-x|\leqslant x/2}\frac{1}{x^{\theta}|\log(x/k)|}\right).

We bound the sum over
|k-x|>x/2
by
\zeta(\theta)\ll 1/(\theta-1).

For
k\in(x,3x/2]
we have

\frac{1}{|\log(x/k)|}=\frac{1}{\log(1+\{k/x-1\})}\ll\frac{1}{k/x-1},

hence, the interval

(x,3x/2]
contributes

\ll\frac{x}{T^{2}}\sum_{x<k\leqslant 3x/2}\frac{x}{k-x}\ll\frac{x^{2}\log x}{T^{2}}.

The interval
[x/2,x)
is dealt with similarly.
∎

We now analyse the Dirichlet series of

b(k):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\ \mathrm{prime}\\
\ell=2\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\begin{cases}\ell^{-2},&\ell\nmid k,\\
\ell^{-1},&v_{\ell}(k)=1,\\
1,&\ell^{2}\mid k\end{cases}\right).

Lemma 4.4
.

The function

G(s):=\prod_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2+2s}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{(\ell-1)(\ell+1)^{2}}\frac{1}{\ell^{s}}+\frac{\ell}{(\ell-1)(\ell+1)^{2}}\frac{1}{\ell^{2s}}\right)

is holomorphic for
\Re(s)>-3/4

and in that region it satisfies

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{b(k)}{k^{s}}=\zeta(s)\zeta(2+2s)\frac{G(s)}{\zeta(2)^{2}}.

Proof.

We see that

\displaystyle b(k)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\ell\nmid k}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right)^{2}\prod_{\ell\|k}\left(1-\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{\ell^{3}}\right)\prod_{\ell^{2}\mid k}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)^{2}}\prod_{\ell\|k}\left(1+\frac{1}{(\ell-1)(\ell+1)^{2}}\right)\prod_{\ell^{2}\mid k}\left(1+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}-1}\right)=\frac{(1\ast g)(k)}{\zeta(2)^{2}},

where
\ast
is the Dirichlet convolution and
for a prime
\ell
we have

g(\ell)=\frac{1}{(\ell-1)(\ell+1)^{2}},\ \ \ g(\ell^{2})=\frac{\ell}{(\ell-1)(\ell+1)^{2}},\ \ \ g(\ell^{q})=0\ \ \forall q\geqslant 3.

Hence, for
\Re(s)>1
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{b(k)}{k^{s}}=\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(2)^{2}}\prod_{\ell}\left(1+\frac{1}{(\ell-1)(\ell+1)^{2}\ell^{s}}+\frac{\ell}{(\ell-1)(\ell+1)^{2}\ell^{2s}}\right).

One can verify that the right-hand
side equals
\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(2)^{2}}\zeta(2+2s)G(s)
, where
G
is defined in
the statement of the lemma.
Writing
\sigma=\Re(s),t=\Im(s)

and fixing a prime
\ell
,
we expand out the product of the two
\ell
-adic factors in the definition of
G
.
We obtain an expression in which

\ell^{-2-2s}
cancels out and we are left
with an expression of type
1+s_{1}\ell^{-f_{1}}+\ldots+s_{n}\ell^{-f_{n}}
, where

n
is finite and independent of
\ell
,
the constants

s_{i}
are signs in
\{1,-1\}
and
f_{i}
are functions of
s
that satisfy

|f_{i}|>1
when
\sigma>-3/4
. In particular,

G
is holomorphic in the region
\Re(s)>-3/4
. ∎

Using
b(m-n)=c(m,n)
,
Lemma
4.3
with
f=b
and
\theta=1+1/\log x

and Lemma
4.4

we get

(4.1)

\sum_{1\leqslant n\neq m\leqslant x}c(m,n)=\frac{1}{\pi i\zeta(2)^{2}}\int_{1+1/\log x-iT}^{1+1/\log x+iT}\zeta(s)\zeta(2+2s)G(s)\frac{x^{s+1}\mathrm{d}s}{s(s+1)}+O\left(\frac{x^{2}\log x}{T^{2}}\right)

for
T\leqslant x
and
x\geqslant 2
.
The next result gives an asymptotic expansion for the expression above. We remark that the calculation for
\gamma_{1}
will involve
\zeta(0)=-1/2
, the minus sign of which
cancels the diagonal contribution, as mentioned in
the introduction.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
\delta\in(0,1/4)
,
any integer

T\leqslant x

\sum_{1\leqslant n\neq m\leqslant x}c(m,n)=\gamma_{2}x^{2}+\gamma_{1}x+\gamma_{0}x^{1/2}+O\left(|J_{1}|+|J_{2}|+|J_{3}|+\frac{x^{2}\log x}{T^{2}}\right)

\gamma_{0}

)

\gamma_{2}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\ \mathrm{prime}\\
\ell=2\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{2}{(\ell-1)(\ell+1)^{2}\ell}\right)\ \ \ \textrm{ and }\ \ \ \gamma_{1}:=-\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}.

Here
J_{i}
are three
integrals as in the right-hand side of
(
4.1
)
resulting by replacing the range of integration respectively by

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{1}{2}-\delta+iT,1+\frac{1}{\log x}+iT\right],\left[-\frac{1}{2}-\delta-iT,-\frac{1}{2}-\delta+iT\right],\left[-\frac{1}{2}-\delta-iT,1+\frac{1}{\log x}-iT\right].

Proof.

By Cauchy’s theorem the integral in the
right-hand side of (
4.1
)
equals

\sum_{j\in\{1,0,-1/2\}}\mathrm{Res}\left(\zeta(s)\zeta(2+2s)G(s)\frac{x^{s+1}}{s(s+1)};s=j\right)+J_{1}+J_{2}-J_{3}

since
\delta<1/4
is small enough
so that the pole at
s=-1
is outside the box.
Here,
\mathrm{Res}(F;s=j)
stands for the residue of
F
at
s=j
.
When
j=1

the residue is

\zeta(4)G(1)\frac{x^{2}}{2}=\frac{x^{2}}{2}\zeta(2)^{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\ \mathrm{prime}\\
\ell=2\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{3-2/\ell}{\ell^{3}}\right),

by the definition of
G
in Lemma
4.4
. To obtain
\gamma_{2}
this must be multiplied by

due to (

).
When
j=0

\zeta(0)\zeta(2)G(0)x=-\zeta(2)x/2,
since each
\ell
-adic factor in the definition of
G(0)
in Lemma
4.4
equals
1
. Again, to obtain
\gamma_{1}
we must multiply the residue by

2/\zeta(2)^{2}
. Finally, the definition of
G
in Lemma
4.4

gives

G(-1/2)=\prod_{\ell}\left(1-\frac{(2-\ell^{-1/2}+\ell^{-1})}{(\ell+1)^{2}}\right).

Hence,
when
j=-1/2
the residue is

\zeta(-1/2)\frac{1}{2}G(-1/2)\frac{x^{1/2}}{(-1/2)(1/2)}=-2\zeta(-1/2)G(-1/2)x^{1/2}

and one must multiply this
2/\zeta(2)^{2}
to obtain
\gamma_{0}
. ∎

5.
Perron integral

5.1.
Zeta prerequisites

We recall some estimates
regarding the Riemann zeta function. The usual
notation
\sigma=\Re(s),t=\Im(s)
is used throughout this section.

The functional equation and bounds for the
Gamma function are used in
[
14
, Equation (5.1.1)]
to show that the following bound holds
with an absolute implied constant:

(5.1)

\zeta(s)\ll|t|^{1/2-\sigma},\ \ \ -1\leqslant\sigma\leqslant-1/10.

When
\sigma
is around
1
,

[
14
, Theorem 3.5]

states the bound

(5.2)

\zeta(s)\ll\log|t|,\ \ \ 1-\frac{2}{\log|t|}\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 2,|t|\geqslant 1

with an absolute implied constant.

The functional equation reads

\zeta(s)=\chi(s)\zeta(1-s)
, where

\chi(s)=2^{s}\pi^{s-1}\Gamma(1-s)\sin\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right).

The bound
|\chi(s)|\ll|t|^{1/2-\sigma}

holds uniformly for
\sigma\in(-1,1)

by
[
14
, page 95]
.
Thus, by (
5.2
),

(5.3)

\zeta(s)\ll|t|^{1/2-\sigma}\log|t|,\ \ \ -1<\sigma\leqslant\frac{1}{\log|t|}.

When
\sigma\in(0,1)

the case of the approximate functional
equation
[
14
, Theorem 4.13]

corresponding to the choice of parameters

x=y=\sqrt{|t|/(2\pi)}
states that

\zeta(s)
equals

\sum_{n\leqslant x}n^{-s}+\chi(s)\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{1}{n^{1-s}}+O(x^{-\sigma}(\log|t|)+|t|^{1/2-\sigma}x^{\sigma-1})\ll\frac{|t|^{(1-\sigma)/2}}{1-\sigma}+|\chi(s)|\frac{|t|^{\sigma/2}}{\sigma}+\frac{\log|t|}{|t|^{\sigma/2}}.

Combining this with

|\chi(s)|\ll|t|^{1/2-\sigma}
we get

(5.4)

\zeta(s)\ll\frac{|t|^{\frac{1-\sigma}{2}}}{\min\{\sigma,1-\sigma\}}+\log|t|,\ \ \ \sigma\in(0,1).

The following mean value
theorem is given in
[
14
, Theorem 7.2]

with an absolute implied constant:

(5.5)

\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta(\sigma+it)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\ll T\min\left\{\log T,\frac{1}{\sigma-1/2}\right\},\ \ \ 1/2\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 2.

From now on we assume that

(5.6)

x^{3/4}\leqslant T\leqslant x.

This ensures that the term
x^{2}/T^{2}
in Lemma
4.5
is
\leqslant x^{1/2}
.

5.2.
Vertical integral

Writing
s=-1/2-\delta+it
we can use
(
5.1
) to get

J_{2}\ll\int_{-T}^{T}t^{1+\delta}|\zeta(1-2\delta+it)|\frac{x^{1/2-\delta}\mathrm{d}t}{1+t^{2}}\ll x^{1/2}(T/x)^{\delta}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{|\zeta(1-2\delta+it)|}{t}\mathrm{d}t.

Since
\delta<1/4
we have

1-2\delta>1/2
, hence, (
5.5
)
gives

\int_{z}^{2z}|\zeta(1-2\delta+it)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\ll z
, with an implied
constant depending only on
\delta
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}\frac{|\zeta(1-2\delta+it)|}{t}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{0\leqslant j\leqslant\log T}\mathrm{e}^{-j}\int_{\mathrm{e}^{j}}^{\mathrm{e}^{j+1}}|\zeta(1-2\delta+it)|\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{0\leqslant j\leqslant\log T}\mathrm{e}^{-j/2}\left(\int_{\mathrm{e}^{j}}^{\mathrm{e}^{j+1}}|\zeta(1-2\delta+it)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\ll\log T.

Thus, for all

\delta\in(0,1/4)

the following holds with an absolute implied
constant:

(5.7)

J_{2}\ll x^{1/2}(T/x)^{\delta}\log T.

5.3.
Horizontal integral: near
1

We focus on
J_{1}
since

J_{3}
is treated similarly.
We have

J_{1}=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}^{1+\frac{1}{\log x}}\zeta(\sigma+iT)\zeta(2\sigma+2+iT)\frac{G(\sigma+iT)x^{1+\sigma+iT}\mathrm{d}\sigma}{(\sigma+iT)(\sigma+1+iT)}.

The two different zeta factors
contribute in different regions and it is thus necessary to consider many different intervals.
We write

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{1}&:=\left[1-\frac{1}{\log x},\,1+\frac{1}{\log x}\right],&\qquad I_{2}&:=\left[\frac{1}{\log T},\,1-\frac{1}{\log T}\right],\\
I_{3}&:=\left[-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\log T},\,\frac{1}{\log T}\right],&\qquad I_{4}&:=\left[\frac{1}{2}-\delta,\,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\log T}\right]\end{aligned}

and denote the corresponding
subintegral of
J_{1}
by
J_{1k}
for
k=1,\ldots,4
.

By (
5.2
) we have

(5.8)

J_{11}\ll\int_{1-1/\log T}^{1-1/\log x}(\log T)\frac{x^{1+\sigma}\mathrm{d}\sigma}{T^{2}}\ll\frac{x^{2}}{T^{2}}.

5.4.
Horizontal integral: positive

By (
5.4
) we obtain

J_{12}\ll(\log T)T^{1/2-2}x\int_{1/\log T}^{1-\frac{1}{\log T}}T^{\frac{-\sigma}{2}}x^{\sigma}\mathrm{d}\sigma\ll\frac{x(\log T)}{T^{3/2}}\int_{1/\log T}^{1-1/\log T}(xT^{-1/2})^{\sigma}\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Since
T\leqslant x

the integral is
\ll(xT^{-1/2})^{1-1/\log x}\ll xT^{-1/2}
. Hence,

(5.9)

J_{12}\ll\frac{x^{2}}{T^{2}}\log T.

5.5.
Horizontal integral: negative part

By (
5.3
) we obtain

J_{13}\ll\frac{x\log T}{T^{3/2}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\log T}}^{\frac{1}{\log T}}(x/T)^{\sigma}|\zeta(2\sigma+2+2iT)|\mathrm{d}\sigma.

The estimate

|\zeta(2\sigma+2+2iT)|\ll\log T

follows from (
5.2
). It yields the bound

(5.10)

J_{13}\ll\frac{x(\log T)^{2}}{T^{3/2}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\log T}}^{\frac{1}{\log T}}(x/T)^{\sigma}\mathrm{d}\sigma\ll\frac{x^{1+1/\log T}}{T^{3/2}}(\log T)^{2}\ll\frac{x}{T^{3/2}}(\log T)^{2}\ll\frac{x^{2}}{T^{2}}(\log T)^{2},

where the two last bounds follow from (
5.6
).

5.6.
Horizontal integral: near
-1/2

By (
5.3
) we get

J_{14}\ll\frac{x\log T}{T^{3/2}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\log T}}(x/T)^{\sigma}|\zeta(2\sigma+2+2iT)|\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Employing (
5.4
) with
s
replaced by
2s+2
yields the bound

\zeta(2\sigma+2+2iT)\ll T^{\frac{-1-2\sigma}{2}}\log T

uniformly in the range of the integral. Hence, by (
5.6
) we get

(5.11)

J_{14}\ll\frac{x(\log T)^{2}}{T^{2}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\log T}}(x/T^{2})^{\sigma}\mathrm{d}\sigma\ll\frac{x^{1/2}(\log T)^{2}}{T}\left(\frac{T^{2}}{x}\right)^{\delta}\ll x^{1/2}(T/x)^{\delta}(\log T)^{2},

where the last bound is from

\delta<1
.
Putting together
(
5.7
)-(
5.11
)
shows that the error term in
Lemma
4.5

is

\ll\left(\frac{x^{2}}{T^{2}}+x^{1/2}(T/x)^{\delta}\right)(\log x)^{2}.

Solving
x^{2}T^{-2}=x^{1/2}(T/x)^{\delta}
yields

T=x^{\omega}
with
\omega=(\delta+3/2)/(\delta+2)
.
Substituing back we get the error term

\ll x^{2-\omega}(\log x)^{2}=x^{1/(\delta+2)}(\log x)^{2}.

Take
\delta=1/4-\varepsilon^{\prime}
for a fixed
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,1/4)
so that,
for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, the above becomes

O_{\varepsilon}(x^{\varepsilon+4/9})
.
Hence, by Lemma
4.5

we infer that for each fixed
\varepsilon>0

and any integer
x\geqslant 2
one has

(5.12)

\sum_{1\leqslant n\neq m\leqslant x}c(m,n)=\gamma_{2}x^{2}+\gamma_{1}x+\gamma_{0}x^{1/2}+O_{\varepsilon}(x^{4/9+\varepsilon}).

Remark 5.1
.

Let us see why the Lindelöf hypothesis, or bounds towards it,
do not help. These can be used to
bound
\zeta(s)
in
J_{12}

or
\zeta(2s+2)
in
J_{14}
.
The convexity bound (
5.4
)
used in §
5.4
produces the bound
(
5.10
) that is not worse than the one already present
in Lemma
4.5
. Assuming a subconexity bound, i.e. that
there is
\beta\in[0,1/4)
such that

\zeta(1/2+it)=O(|t|^{\beta+\varepsilon})

for
\varepsilon>0
and
|t|\geqslant 1

implies by the Phragmén–Lindelöf principle
that

\zeta(\sigma+it)=O(|t|^{2\beta(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon})

uniformly in the range
|t|\geqslant 1
and
1/2\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 1
.
Using this bound instead of (
5.4
) in §
5.6
leads to

J_{14}\ll x^{1/2}(T/x)^{\delta}(T^{4\beta\delta-1})x^{\varepsilon}.

Save for the harmless term
x^{\varepsilon}
, this bound is not worse
than the one already present in (
5.7
)
due to
\beta<1/4
and
\delta\leqslant 1/4
.

6.
Conclusion of the proof

Summing the asymptotic of
Lemma
3.7

over all
1\leqslant n\neq m\leqslant x
and using (
5.12
)
to estimate the main term leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{n\neq m\leqslant x}\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{d}(H)}\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(n))\mu^{2}_{\mathrm{model}}(P(m))

\displaystyle=(2H)^{d+1}\left(\gamma_{2}x^{2}+\gamma_{1}x+\gamma_{0}x^{1/2}+O_{\varepsilon}(x^{\varepsilon+4/9})\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(x^{2}z^{4d+2}+x^{2}z^{2}H^{d}+\frac{x^{2}H^{d+1}\log z}{z}\right).

Noting that
\gamma=\gamma_{2}

and alluding to
Lemmas
3.3
and
3.6

we get

V(H,x,z)=(2H)^{d+1}\gamma_{0}x^{1/2}+O(H^{\varepsilon}\mathscr{R}),

with
\mathscr{R}=H^{d+1}x^{4/9}+x^{2+d/2}H^{d+1/2}+x^{2}H^{d+1}/z+x^{2}z^{4d+2}+x^{2}z^{2}H^{d}.
We take
z=H^{\lambda}
with
\lambda=(d+1)/(4d+3)
.
This choice is optimal
for minimising the last three terms.
We obtain
\mathscr{R}\ll H^{d+1}x^{4/9}+x^{2+d/2}H^{d+1/2}+x^{2}H^{d+1-\lambda}
. We infer that for
d\geqslant 3
one has

x\leqslant H^{\frac{1}{d+3}}\ \ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \ \mathscr{R}\ll H^{d+1}x^{1/2}\ \ \textrm{ and }\ \ x\leqslant H^{\frac{1}{d+28/9}}\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \mathscr{R}\ll H^{d+1}x^{4/9}.

These respectively prove the first and second part of
Theorem
1.1

by using
Lemma
3.2

and noting that the error term is

\ll V(H,x,z)^{1/2}x\left(\frac{H^{d+1+\varepsilon}}{z^{2-\varepsilon}}\right)^{1/2}+x^{2}\frac{H^{d+1+\varepsilon}}{z^{2-\varepsilon}}\ll x^{5/4}H^{d+1+2\varepsilon}z^{-1}+x^{2}H^{d+1+2\varepsilon}z^{-1},

which is admissible.

Cumulative high-risk surgical and pathologic features predict recurrence after resection of early-stage node-negative non–small cell lung cancer
Authors: Fatemehsadat Pezeshkian, Emanuele Mazzola, Justin S. Heidel, José A. Rodriguez Zamboni, Andes Wong, Yue Xie, Chandana Adhikarla, Antonio Coppolino, Matthew M. Rochefort, Scott J. Swanson, Michael T. Jaklitsch, Paula A. Ugalde Figueroa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveDespite curative resection, recurrence remains a clinically relevant problem in patients with early-stage non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Although several high-risk features have been individually associated with adverse outcomes, their cumulative prognostic influence remains unclear.MethodsPatients with NSCLC undergoing curative-intent resection between 2017 and 2024 were identified from a prospective institutional database. Inclusion criteria were solid tumors with size <4 cm, pN0 disease, and R0 resection with documented spread through air spaces status. High-risk features included nonanatomic resection, poorly/undifferentiated grade, lymphovascular invasion (LVI), visceral pleural invasion (VPI), incomplete lymphadenectomy (<3N2 and <1N1 stations), and spread through air spaces positivity. Cox proportional hazards modeling was used to quantify the association of high-risk features and 5-year recurrence-free survival, and a nomogram was constructed to estimate cumulative recurrence risk.ResultsOf the 497 patients included, anatomic resection was performed in 268 (53.9%) patients and complete lymphadenectomy was achieved in 126 (25.4%). Over a median 30-month follow-up (interquartile range, 13-49 months), 50 patients (10.1%) developed recurrence. LVI (hazard ratio [HR], 3.7; 95% CI, 2-7; P < .001), VPI (HR, 2.8; 95% CI, 1.5-5.2; P = .002), poorly/undifferentiated tumor (HR, 1.8; 95% CI, 0.99-3.3; P = .05), resection margin distance (HR, 0.7; 95% CI, 0.5-0.9; P = .01), and incomplete lymphadenectomy (HR, 2.7; 95% CI, 1-7.1; P = .05) were significantly associated with recurrence. The final nomogram demonstrated good discrimination for estimated 5-year recurrence risk, with a concordance index of 0.82.ConclusionsA multifactorial nomogram integrating high-risk pathologic features to estimate recurrence risk in early-stage NSCLC was developed. This model suggests that recurrence risk in early-stage NSCLC may be driven by the cumulative burden of high-risk surgical and pathologic features rather than isolated factors, providing a clinically applicable framework for postoperative risk stratification. External validation and integration into clinical decision-making frameworks are warranted.

Cumulative burden of surgical and pathologic high-risk features on recurrence.

Central Message

In early-stage NSCLC, cumulative burden of high-risk surgical and pathologic features, rather than a single factor, drives recurrence risk and can be quantified with a clinically practical nomogram.

Perspective

Patients with early-stage node-negative NSCLC are not candidates for adjuvant therapy under current guidelines, despite possibly harboring multiple high-risk pathologic features. Integrating these features into a cumulative risk model may improve postoperative surveillance strategies and identify patients who warrant closer follow-up or future risk-adapted treatment approaches.

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, and its global burden is expected to increase with population aging and growth.1 The implementation of lung cancer screening programs has shifted diagnosis toward earlier stages, for which surgical resection remains the primary curative treatment.2 Despite complete resection, recurrence continues to occur in a meaningful proportion of patients with early-stage non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).

Reported 5-year disease-free survival rates for early-stage NSCLC range from 64% to 82%, underscoring substantial heterogeneity in oncologic outcomes within this population.3,4 Numerous clinicopathologic features have been associated with increased risk of recurrence, including advanced age, nonanatomic resection, incomplete lymphadenectomy, larger tumor size, visceral pleural invasion (VPI), lymphovascular invasion (LVI), high-grade tumor differentiation, positive or close resection margins, and unfavorable histologic subtypes.5,6 The presence of spread through air spaces (STAS) has also emerged as an important pathologic feature associated with higher recurrence risk.7 More recently, genomic alterations have been investigated as additional contributors to recurrence risk in early-stage disease.8

Although these high-risk features are well described, they are most often evaluated individually. Current guidelines do not routinely recommend adjuvant systemic therapy for most patients with completely resected node-negative tumors smaller than 4 cm, even when 1 or more high-risk pathologic features are present. Similarly, there are no guideline-directed recommendations for risk-adapted postoperative surveillance strategies, additional imaging modalities, or routine molecular testing based on the presence or number of high-risk features. As a result, clinicians are frequently faced with uncertainty regarding postoperative management in patients who harbor multiple adverse pathologic characteristics but do not meet established criteria for treatment escalation. We therefore sought to develop a prediction model that integrates National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN)-recognized high-risk pathologic features and evaluates their cumulative association with recurrence in patients with early-stage, node-negative NSCLC. By quantifying the combined influence of these features, this study aims to provide a framework for identifying patients at higher risk of recurrence who may benefit from closer surveillance or future risk-adapted strategies. Given its retrospective, single-institution design, this analysis is intended to be hypothesis-generating.

Methods

This study is a single-institution retrospective analysis of a prospectively maintained thoracic surgery database. The study protocol and publication of data were approved by the Institutional Review Board of Brigham and Women's Hospital (protocol #2020P000145; approved February 13, 2020). Informed consent was waived in accordance with institutional policy because all data were derived from an existing clinical database.

Patients who underwent curative-intent pulmonary resection for NSCLC between January 2017 and December 2024 were identified. Inclusion criteria consisted of solid tumors measuring <4 cm, clinical and pathologic N0/M0 disease, and R0 resection. Patients who received neoadjuvant or adjuvant therapy, underwent completion resection in a separate setting, or had insufficient follow-up were excluded. STAS status was not available in approximately 30% of patients because STAS reporting became more standardized later in the study period, missingness may not have been completely random and could reflect temporal changes in pathologic reporting practices; these patients were excluded from the final nomogram model due to complete-case restriction. Patient demographic characteristics, perioperative variables, pathologic characteristics, and long-term outcomes were extracted from the institutional database and independently audited during October 2025.

Baseline clinical variables included age, sex, forced expiratory volume in 1 second expressed as percent predicted, comorbidities quantified using the Charlson comorbidity index,9 tobacco exposure, and tumor characteristics. All patients underwent preoperative staging according to contemporary institutional protocols, including cross-sectional imaging and invasive mediastinal staging when clinically indicated. Pathologic evaluation was performed by dedicated thoracic pathologists. Tumor size, clinical stage (based on the ninth TNM staging), histologic subtype, tumor differentiation, LVI, VPI, STAS status, and resection margin distance (in centimeters) were recorded.

Surgical variables included types of resection (lobectomy, segmentectomy, or wedge resection), surgical approach (minimally invasive or open), operative duration, and extent of lymph node evaluation. Perioperative outcomes included postoperative complications graded according to the Clavien-Dindo classification10 (grade II or higher), length of hospital stay, 30-day readmission, and 30- and 90-day mortality.

Long-term outcomes included overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS), measured from the date of surgery. Recurrence was defined by histologic confirmation or radiographic evidence on computed tomography interpreted by a thoracic radiologist. Death was treated as a competing event for RFS. Patients without recurrence were censored at last follow-up or at 60 months if event-free. All survival times were capped at 60 months for analysis. Postoperative surveillance consisted of physical examination and chest computed tomography at least every 6 months for the first 3 years and annually thereafter, in accordance with institutional practice.

High-risk surgical and pathologic features were defined based on NCCN criteria and included nonanatomic resection (wedge resection), incomplete lymphadenectomy defined as sampling fewer than 3 N2 stations and fewer than 1 N1 station, VPI, LVI, STAS positivity, poorly or undifferentiated tumor, resection margin distance (in centimeters), and clinical stage.

Cox proportional hazards (cause-specific) and competing risk regression was used to evaluate associations between predefined high-risk features and RFS. Variables were selected a priori based on clinical relevance. Results were summarized using hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CI and displayed, for the cause-specific hazards, using forest plots. A nomogram for the final cause-specific Cox model was constructed using variables selected a priori based on NCCN high-risk criteria, rather than data-driven selection, to maximize reproducibility and generalizability. The nomogram estimates individualized 5-year recurrence risk. All nomogram analyses were performed on complete cases; patients with missing values in any model variable were excluded. Internal validation was performed using 1000 bootstrap resamples to obtain optimism-corrected estimates of discrimination and calibration. Model discrimination was assessed using the optimism-corrected concordance index (C-index) at both 36 and 60 months. Calibration was assessed by comparing predicted and observed RFS probabilities at both time points. To contextualize findings, a parallel competing risk analysis using the Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model was performed with death as the competing event, and results were compared across all variable sets. To evaluate incremental value over simpler models, bootstrap-corrected C-indices were compared across 4 nested models of increasing complexity, and net reclassification improvement and decision curve analysis were performed comparing the full nomogram against LVI alone. All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.1.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

The median tumor size on preoperative imaging was 1.5 cm (IQR, 1.1-2.0 cm). Final histology demonstrated adenocarcinoma in 393 patients (79.1%) and squamous cell carcinoma in 93 patients (18.7%). Wedge resection was performed in 235 patients (46.1%), whereas anatomic resection was performed in 262 patients, including segmentectomy in 143 (28.8%) and lobectomy in 125 (25.1%). A majority of procedures were performed using a minimally invasive approach (n = 482 [97%]).

Pathologic high-risk features included LVI in 91 patients (18.3%), VPI in 73 (14.7%), poorly or undifferentiated tumor grade in 138 (27.8%), and STAS in 190 (38.2%). The median resection margin distance was 1.7 cm (IQR, 0.9-2.7 cm). The median number of lymph nodes sampled was 5 (IQR, 2-9). Complete lymphadenectomy, defined as sampling at least 1 N1 station and 3 N2 stations, was achieved in 126 patients (25.4%).

The median follow-up duration was 30 months (IQR, 13-49 months). Five-year OS was 91.1%. Recurrence occurred in 50 patients (10.1%), including 41 locoregional recurrences (8.2%), 5 distant recurrences (1.0%), and 4 combined locoregional and distant recurrences (0.8%).

In Cox proportional hazards regression analysis for RFS, LVI (HR, 3.7; 95% CI, 2.0-7.0; P < .001), VPI (HR, 2.8; 95% CI, 1.5-5.2; P = .002), poorly or undifferentiated tumor (HR, 1.8; 95% CI, 0.99-3.3; P = .05), resection margin distance (HR, 0.7/cm; 95% CI, 0.5-0.9/cm; P = .01), and incomplete lymphadenectomy (HR, 2.7; 95% CI, 1-7.1; P = .05) were associated with RFS. Nonanatomic resection and STAS status were not statistically significant. Results of the multivariable Cox regression analysis are shown in Figure 1. Discussion

In this contemporary single-institution cohort of patients with early-stage NSCLC undergoing curative-intent resection, recurrence occurred in 50 patients (10.1%) during follow-up. On multivariable Cox regression, LVI, VPI, poorly or undifferentiated tumor grade, resection margin distance, and incomplete lymphadenectomy were significantly associated with RFS. These findings underscore the heterogeneity of oncologic risk among patients traditionally considered low risk based on TNM staging alone. By integrating these variables, we developed a nomogram with strong discriminative performance (C-index, 0.82) that provides a clinically practical framework for estimating 5-year recurrence risk based on the cumulative burden of adverse pathologic and surgical features. Despite curative-intent resection, recurrence after upfront surgery for early-stage NSCLC remains a recognized clinical problem, particularly among patients who do not meet criteria for adjuvant therapy. Reported recurrence rates in early-stage disease range from 12% to 39%, reflecting heterogeneity in patient populations, tumor biology, and surgical factors.11,12 In the present cohort, recurrence occurred in 10.1% of patients over a median follow-up of 30 months, whereas estimated OS remained favorable. Compared with previously published series, a lower incidence of recurrence was observed. This may be attributable to management at an academic tertiary care center with a dedicated multidisciplinary thoracic oncology team. Although multiple high-risk surgical and pathologic features have been associated with recurrence, current guidelines do not routinely recommend adjuvant systemic therapy, risk-adapted surveillance strategies, or additional testing in patients with completely resected node-negative tumors smaller than 4 cm who harbor 1 or more adverse pathologic features.13,14 This creates a management gap for patients who harbor multiple unfavorable characteristics yet remain ineligible for treatment escalation.

High-risk features beyond TNM staging include several clinicopathologic characteristics that have been consistently associated with recurrence risk. VPI and LVI are among the most frequently reported adverse features, with prior studies demonstrating increased recurrence risk in patients with either finding.12 Meta-analyses have reported a 1.7- to 2.5-fold increased hazard of recurrence associated with LVI, whereas VPI has been associated with recurrence risk increases ranging from 40% to 55%.12,15,16 In the present cohort, VPI and LVI remained significantly associated with RFS.

STAS has emerged as an important pathologic risk factor in early-stage NSCLC.17 Prior pooled analyses have demonstrated an increased risk of recurrence associated with STAS, with recurrence risk increasing alongside greater STAS extent.17 In the present cohort, STAS showed a trend toward an association with recurrence risk, consistent with previously reported findings, including among patients with stage IA disease, although this association did not reach statistical significance.18 Prior studies have suggested that the coexistence of adverse pathologic features such as visceral pleural invasion with lymphovascular invasion or STAS, confers worse outcomes than isolated findings.19,20 These observations support the clinical relevance of cumulative pathologic risk although, systematic integration of multiple routinely available features remains limited.

Surgical quality metrics also contributed to recurrence risk in this cohort. Adequate lymphadenectomy, defined as sampling at least 1 N1 and 3 N2 stations, has been recommended by professional societies and has been associated with improved staging accuracy and oncologic outcomes.6 Prior studies have reported increased recurrence risk associated with inadequate lymph node evaluation, particularly in tumors measuring ≥2 cm.21 In the present analysis, complete lymphadenectomy was associated with improved RFS, a finding that may reflect both improved oncologic clearance and more accurate pathologic staging. Resection margin distance was also associated with RFS, further emphasizing the contribution of surgical factors to oncologic outcomes in early-stage disease.

Given that multiple pathologic and surgical factors may coexist within the same patient, evaluating their cumulative impact on recurrence risk is of particular relevance in early-stage disease.22 Several prior nomograms have been developed to predict recurrence or survival in NSCLC, often incorporating genomic signatures, radiologic features, or registry-based variables.23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30 Although these models have demonstrated variable predictive performance, many rely on data that are not uniformly available in routine clinical practice or include patients who received adjuvant therapy. In contrast, the present model integrates routinely reported pathologic features and surgical quality metrics in an adjuvant-ineligible population, providing a framework for estimating recurrence risk based on cumulative adverse factors. The model was intentionally designed around routinely available NCCN-recognized high-risk features to maximize clinical translatability across institutions and practice settings.

This study has several limitations. First, this is a single-institution retrospective cohort, and the nomogram lacks external validation. Prospective evaluation in independent cohorts is needed before broader clinical adoption. Second, although predictor selection was guided by established NCCN high-risk criteria rather than data-driven methods, internal validation demonstrated good and stable discrimination (optimism-corrected C-index, 0.78 [at both 36 and 60 months]), suggesting that optimism-related inflation in model performance was limited despite the relatively modest events-per-variable ratio. Third, STAS and margin distance data were missing in approximately 30% of patients, reducing the analytical sample for the final nomogram to 473 patients with 49 recurrence events and an events-per-variable ratio of approximately 5. Although bootstrap validation suggested reasonable internal stability, this sample reduction should be considered when interpreting model stability and generalizability, and we cannot exclude the possibility of informative missingness. Fourth, the median follow-up of 31 months is shorter than the 60-month prediction horizon of the nomogram. Therefore, 5-year estimates should be interpreted as projected recurrence risk rather than fully mature observed risk. Calibration was markedly better at 36 months, the horizon more robustly supported by the observed follow-up distribution, than at 60 months (mean absolute error, 0.009 vs 0.092, respectively). Finally, molecular, radiologic, and additional clinical variables including driver mutation status, positron-emission tomography avidity, age, and smoking history were not incorporated, reflecting both data availability constraints and the intent to develop a broadly generalizable tool; future studies incorporating comprehensive molecular profiling and standardized radiologic metrics will be essential to refine recurrence prediction in early-stage NSCLC.

Conclusions

In this contemporary cohort of patients with resected early-stage node-negative NSCLC, recurrence risk was associated with the cumulative burden of routinely available surgical and pathologic high-risk features beyond TNM stage alone. A nomogram integrating NCCN-recognized risk factors and surgical quality metrics demonstrated good internal discrimination for estimated recurrence risk in an adjuvant-ineligible population. External validation with longer follow-up is warranted before clinical implementation.

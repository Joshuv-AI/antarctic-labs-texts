4
Summarizing Alignments with Skip Alignments

4.1
Skip Alignments

Lemma 7
(Finite)

​​A trace and an SB​WF​-net have finitely many skip
alignments
.

Proof

Assume a trace
\sigma
has infinitely many skip alignments on an SBWF-net
N
.
The number of synchronous and log moves is limited by
|\sigma|
. Since
N
is finite, only finitely many different skip moves exist. Hence, for every
n>0
there must exist a skip alignment
\delta
such that
|\delta|>n
. Only loop blocks in
N
can contribute to an unbounded length of
\delta
. Therefore, from some
m>0
on, all skip alignments
\delta
with
|\delta|>n
and
n\geq m
execute at least one loop block
N^{\prime}\in N
by consecutively skipping over both its children, contradicting the definition of
\mathcal{S}(N^{\prime})
.

4.2
Transforming Alignments to Skip Alignments

Lemma 9
(Transformation to Skip Alignments)

Let
\sigma
be a trace and
N
an SBWF-net. Let
\gamma
be an alignment for
\sigma
on
N
and let
\gamma^{\prime}
be the mix alignment obtained by replacing all model moves in
\gamma
with skip moves. Then, the exhaustive application of transformation rules to
\gamma^{\prime}
results in a skip alignment
\delta
for
\sigma
on
N
.

Proof

Every move in
\delta
is a log, synchronous, or skip move. It holds that
\pi_{1}(\delta)_{\downharpoonright_{\neq\gg}}=\pi_{1}(\gamma)_{\downharpoonright_{\neq\gg}}=\sigma
since only skip moves are modified in the transformation. Furthermore, because
\gamma
is an alignment on
N
,
\pi_{2}(\gamma)_{\downharpoonright_{\neq\gg}}
and
\pi_{2}(\gamma^{\prime})_{\downharpoonright_{\neq\gg}}
are executions of
N
. Cycling rules trivially maintain that property. Lifting rules replace moves in
\gamma^{\prime}
only at the positions their children where executed at, i.e., the application of any rule ensures that
\pi_{2}(\delta)_{\downharpoonright_{\neq\gg}}
is an execution of
N
. In
\delta
, every skip move is maximally general, otherwise one of (L1-L4) could be applied and
\delta
contains no cycles, otherwise (C1-C2) could be applied. Hence,
\pi_{2}(\delta)_{\downharpoonright_{\neq\gg}}\in\mathcal{S}(N)
and
\delta
is a skip alignment for
\sigma
on
N
.

4.4
A Canonical Normal Form for Skip Alignments

We show that reduction rules may be applied in any order resulting in the same unique skip alignment (
canonicity
). To that end, we first prove
termination
and then
local confluency
, i.e., when applying two rules yields two different skip alignments, then there are subsequent reduction rules to make them equal again.

Proposition 1
(Termination of Reduction Rules)

Let
\delta
be a skip alignment. Repeated application of the reduction rules to
\delta
is terminating.

Proof

We give a weighting function that monotonically decreases with every application of a rule.
With every application of (R1) or (R2), the size of the set of pairs
t_{1}=\{(i,j)\mid 1\leq i<j\leq|\delta|\land\delta_{i}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\gg\\
a\end{smallmatrix}\big)\land\delta_{j}\in\{\big(\begin{smallmatrix}e\\
\gg\end{smallmatrix}\big),\big(\begin{smallmatrix}e\\
a\end{smallmatrix}\big)\}\}
decreases. None of (R1-R3) increases the size of this set again.
With every application of (R3), the size of the set of pairs
t_{2}=\{(i,j)\mid 1\leq i<j\leq|\delta|\land\delta_{i}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\gg\\
s(N_{1})\end{smallmatrix}\big)\land\delta_{j}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\gg\\
s(N_{2})\end{smallmatrix}\big)\land N_{2}<N_{1}\}
decreases. Only the application of (R2) may increase
t_{2}
by at most
|\delta|
elements.
Hence,
(|\delta|\!+\!1)\cdot|t_{1}|+|t_{2}|
is monotonically decreasing with every rule application.

We prove local confluency for every pair of rules where the left sides overlap. We show that after applying one of the rules the resulting skip alignment can be made equal to the one after applying the other rule.

Lemma 13
(Unique Coinciding Normal Form)

Every alignment coincides with a single skip alignment in normal form.

Proof

We show that for an alignment
\gamma
the set of corresponding skip alignments
\mathcal{C}(\gamma)
coincides with a single normal form. Let
\delta
and
\hat{\delta}
in
\mathcal{C}(\gamma)
be corresponding skip alignments of
\gamma
. With canonicity, they reduce to coinciding normal forms
\delta^{\prime}
and
\hat{\delta}^{\prime}
. Assume
\delta^{\prime}\neq\hat{\delta}^{\prime}
. Both normal forms share the same moves, so their order must be different.
At the position
i
of their first difference, one of
\delta^{\prime}
and
\hat{\delta}^{\prime}
has to perform a skip move. W.l.o.g., we assume
\delta^{\prime}_{i}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\gg\\
s(N_{1})\end{smallmatrix}\big)
.

•

If
\hat{\delta}^{\prime}_{i}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}e\\
\gg\end{smallmatrix}\big)
performs a log move, then this move appears at a position
\delta^{\prime}_{j}
with
j>i
. Because no synchronous move can take place in between positions
i
and
j
in
\delta^{\prime}
, (R1) can be applied contradicting the normal form.

•

If
\hat{\delta}^{\prime}_{i}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}e\\
a\end{smallmatrix}\big)
performs a synchronous move, then the skip move
\delta^{\prime}_{i}
is not required before performing the synchronous move in
\hat{\delta}^{\prime}
, so (R2) can be applied to
\delta^{\prime}
contradicting the normal form.

•

If
\hat{\delta}^{\prime}_{i}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\gg\\
s(N_{2})\end{smallmatrix}\big)
performs a skip move, then this move is performed at a position
j>i
in
\delta^{\prime}
.
If there is a synchronous move in
\delta^{\prime}
in between
i
and
j
, then
\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\gg\\
s(N_{2})\end{smallmatrix}\big)
is independent from the synchronous move, i.e., (R2) can be applied to
\hat{\delta}^{\prime}
contradicting the formal form.
If there is a log move in
\delta^{\prime}
in between
i
and
j
, then (R1) can be applied to
\delta^{\prime}
contradicting to the normal form.
Otherwise, there are only skip moves in between positions
i
and
j
. Similarly, only skip moves appear in between
\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\gg\\
s(N_{2})\end{smallmatrix}\big)
and
\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\gg\\
s(N_{1})\end{smallmatrix}\big)
in
\hat{\delta}^{\prime}
. As both orders are valid (both are skip alignments for the same trace),
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
must be descendants in different branches of a parallel block. Hence, the order of
\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\gg\\
s(N_{2})\end{smallmatrix}\big)
and
\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\gg\\
s(N_{1})\end{smallmatrix}\big)
depends on the total order of blocks, i.e., (R3) can be applied to one of
\delta^{\prime}
and
\hat{\delta}^{\prime}
contradicting the normal form.

4.5
Computing All Optimal Skip Alignments in Normal Form

Lemma 16
(Correctness & Completeness)

For any trace and SBWF-net, the extended
A-star
search computes all optimal skip alignments in normal form.

Proof

Let
\sigma
be a trace and
N
an SBWF-net. We apply the extended
A-star
search to our state space starting in the initial state
(\langle\rangle,\sigma)
. First, we show correctness, then, we show completeness of the computations.

Let
(\delta,\langle\rangle)
be a cost-minimally reached goal state. Then,
\delta
is a skip alignment in normal form for
\sigma
on
N
:

•

The trace
\sigma
is covered by
\delta
, i.e.,
\pi_{1}(\delta)_{\downharpoonright_{\neq\gg}}=\sigma
, since Algorithm 1 iteratively consumes
\sigma
to result in
\sigma^{\prime}=\langle\rangle
. Furthermore,
\pi_{2}(\delta)_{\downharpoonright_{\neq\gg}}\in\mathcal{S}(N)
because for every non-log move appended in the construction of
\delta
, we check extendability of the current model execution in the skip language of
N
(lines 2 and 6). States explored in the search consist only of skip moves (T1,T2), synchronous moves (T2), and log moves (T3), i.e.,
\delta
is a skip alignment for
\sigma
on
N
.

•

It additionally is a skip alignment in normal form: (T3) cannot break the normal form, because the log move is appended to some partial skip alignment, which by construction of Algorithm 1 does not end with a skip move. (T1) and (T2) explicitly ensure by calling the Boolean test function
\mathrm{r}
, that the resulting (partial) skip alignment can be extended to a skip alignment in normal form. This also holds for
\delta
which can only be extended by the empty sequence
\langle\rangle
, i.e.,
\delta
is in normal form.

•

Optimality of
\delta
follows from the guarantee that
A-star
only returns optimal solutions and the fact that edge costs between nodes in the search space match skip alignment move costs.

Because
\delta
is a skip alignment, it is in normal form, and it is optimal, the extended
A-star
search on our search space is correct.

Assume
\delta
is an optimal skip alignment in normal form for
\sigma
on
N
and
\delta
is not found by the extended
A-star
search on our search space. Then, there exists a largest prefix
\delta^{\prime}
of
\delta
and a state
(\delta^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
explored in the search, such that there is no cost-minimal path from
(\delta^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
to the goal state
(\delta,\langle\rangle)
. Because edge costs are move costs and because the extended
A-star
search algorithm is complete, there exists
no path at all
from
(\delta^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
to
(\delta,\langle\rangle)
. Since
\delta^{\prime}
is the maximal prefix of
\delta
with that property,
(\delta^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
is
missing a successor
, i.e., Algorithm 1 is incomplete. We inspect the missing suffix
\delta^{\prime\prime}
of
\delta
, i.e.,
\delta=\delta^{\prime}\cdot\delta^{\prime\prime}
, and distinguish three cases:

•

If
\delta^{\prime\prime}
consists only of skip moves, then this
\delta^{\prime\prime}
is discovered in Algorithm 1 as all skip paths
p\in\mathrm{S}(N)^{*}
are considered (line 2), and a suitable successor is computed by Algorithm 1.

•

If
\delta^{\prime\prime}
starts with a log move, then this move is covered (line 9) and a suitable successor is computed by Algorithm 1.

•

Otherwise,
\delta^{\prime\prime}
starts with an unbounded number of skip moves followed by a synchronous move. Because in Algorithm 1, all matching transitions
t\in T
(line 5) and all skip paths
p\in\mathrm{S}(N)^{*}
(line 6) are inspected, this move is covered and a suitable successor is computed by Algorithm 1.

Note that
\delta^{\prime\prime}
cannot start with another sequence of moves, because
\delta
is in normal form. This contradicts the assumption that the set of successors of
(\delta^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
computed by Algorithm 1 is incomplete.

Hence, no such undiscovered
\delta
exists and the extended
A-star
search on our search space is complete.

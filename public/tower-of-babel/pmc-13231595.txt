From algorithms to empathy: can large language models effectively answer patients’ questions in restorative dentistry?
Authors: Suzan Cangül, Makbule Taşyürek, Tuba Tunç, Özkan Adıgüzel, Hatice Ortaç
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and objectivesThe aim of this study was to compare the responses given by the large language models (LLMs) ChatGPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek-V3.2, and Claude Sonnet-4.5 in terms of accuracy, empathy, and readability, to frequently asked patient questions about restorative dentistry.Materials and methodsTwenty-five open-ended questions were posed in English to ChatGPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp, and Claude Sonnet-4.5 models in individual, independent sessions. Accuracy was scored using a 5-point Likert-type scale and empathy a 3-point Likert-type scale by two experienced evaluators, and inter-rater reliability was calculated using the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC). Readability was evaluated through an online platform using the FRES, FKGL, GFI, SMOG, and CLI indexes. Group comparisons were performed using ANOVA/Kruskall-Wallis and post-hoc Dunn-Bonferroni tests according to the normality distribution of the data.ResultsThe inter-rater reliability was found to be high score for accuracy and showed variability according to the model for empathy. Statistically significant differences were determined between the models in terms of accuracy (p < 0.001), with the highest mean value obtained by DeepSeek-V3.2 (4.8 ± 0.5), followed by Claude Sonnet-4.5 (4.28 ± 0.61) and ChatGPT-5 (4.12 ± 0.67), and the lowest accuracy was determined for Gemini 2.5 Pro (3.6 ± 0.5). Statistically significant differences were determined between the models in terms of empathy (p < 0.001), with the highest mean value obtained by DeepSeek-V3.2 (1.96 ± 0.2). The readability measurements showed that overall, DeepSeek-V3.2 produced more readable texts with higher FRES values (60.85 ± 8.2) (p < 0.001).ConclusionsWithin the scope of this study, DeepSeek-V3.2 exhibited a comparatively more balanced performance profile across accuracy, empathy, and readability measures. While Claude Sonnet-4.5 and ChatGPT-5 showed high accuracy, the results for empathy and readability criteria were variable. No model can replace a clinical specialist, and these systems should be evaluated as decision-making tool under specialist supervision, especially at the stages of patient education and first providing information.

Introduction

In recent years, access to online health information has increased rapidly, and patients have begun to widely use the internet to find answers to health questions and support treatment decisions. According to surveys, 42–71% of internet users search online for various medical information, ranging from drug side effects to disease pathophysiology [1, 2]. However, the quality and consistency of this information varies greatly, which can lead to confusion, delays in diagnosis and treatment, and problems in patient-physician communication [3]. A study conducted in Denmark showed that patient satisfaction decreased when physician information conflicted with internet sources [4]. Furthermore, the fact that most health content on the internet is prepared without expert contribution exposes patients to incorrect or potentially dangerous information [5, 6].

Restorative dentistry is a branch of dentistry that aims to restore aesthetic and chewing function by correcting structural defects in teeth. Artificial intelligence is playing an increasingly important role in operative dentistry, from the diagnosis of dental caries to the creation of personalized treatment plans [7].

Kühnisch and colleagues highlighted the potential of AI in clinical applications by reporting that it can detect cavities with 90% accuracy from a single tooth photograph [8]. Large Language Models (LLMs) are artificial intelligence systems capable of generating human language, and recent developments have shown that these technologies have significant application potential in the field of medicine [9].

LLMs can save time for dentists and patients, reducing workload, and can increase patient confidence by providing clear, personalized, and timely information, especially in patient education processes. However, data comparing the contributions of LLM-based chatbots to patient education and health literacy are still limited.

ChatGPT (OpenAI, San Francisco, CA, USA) is an AI-based chatbot launched in December 2022 that has rapidly gained global adoption and become widely used in many fields [10]. Two months after its launch, ChatGPT saw a 9.90% increase in users, reaching 60 million daily visits and 173 million active users by April 2023, making it one of the fastest-growing platforms in history [11].

Introduced on August 7, 2025, ChatGPT-5 is built on an “intelligent and productive” base model, offering significant improvements such as faster responses, high accuracy, and low hallucination tendency. Thanks to the deep thinking mode called “GPT-5 Thinking,” it aims to provide a more reliable and effective user experience, particularly in the fields of healthcare, coding, and writing [12].

Large language models have the potential to generate unverified or erroneous content, often referred to in the literature as “hallucinations,” but which can essentially be considered “information fabrication.” [13]. While dentists can distinguish such errors using their professional knowledge and experience, patients generally lack this critical expertise. This situation can lead to serious risks such as misdirection, unnecessary anxiety, and incorrect health behaviors.

DeepSeek is a Chinese artificial intelligence model based on the principles of transparency and reproducibility (Hangzhou DeepSeek AI Basic Tec. Res. Co., Ltd.; Beijing, China). Launched in December 2024, the chatbot has attracted global attention with its low cost and high download rates [14]. Introduced in August 2025, DeepSeek-V3.1 combines the “Think” and “Thinking” modes under one roof with its hybrid output architecture, providing a significant performance increase in multi-step tasks and agent-based applications [12]. The subsequent experimental DeepSeek-V3.2 version was developed to work faster and more efficiently in long contexts thanks to DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA) technology [15].

Google launched its chatbot service under the name Bard in March 2023 and restructured it under the Gemini brand starting in February 2024. Gemini is a local multimodal model capable of processing multiple data types such as text, audio, and video, allowing users to analyze different content formats [16, 17]. Gemini 2.5, released in March 2025, aims to deliver higher accuracy and problem-solving performance as a next-generation thinking model that performs logical evaluation before generating responses [12].

Introduced in March 2023, Claude (Anthropic, San Francisco, CA, USA) adopts a controlled language model approach designed based on ethical and security principles [18]. The Claude 3 Opus and 3.5 Sonnet versions can process text and visual inputs together, while the Sonnet-4 model, part of the Claude 4 family released in May 2025, demonstrates reliable performance, particularly on long documents and large knowledge bases, with a wide context window and low hallucination rate [19, 20].

AI-based healthcare services encompass rapid evolution, conversion opportunities, and unforeseen risks. Therefore, comprehensive studies examining the clinical efficacy, reliability levels, and ethical dimensions of AI-supported technologies are of critical importance to minimize potential harm while maximizing patient benefits. The aim of this study was to compare the responses to frequently asked patient questions about restorative dentistry provided by four different LLMs (ChatGPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek-V3.2, and Claude Sonnet-4.5) in terms of accuracy, empathy, and readability.

Materials and methods

This study did not require Ethics Committee approval as no patient data or personal information was used, and the analyses were only based on open-access online resources.

Also Asked (https://alsoasked.com) [21] and Answer the Public (https://answerthepublic.com) [22] programs were used in this study to learn which questions are asked by patients about restorative dentistry treatment. These tools are widely used in the determination of headings of interest to users with frequently asked questions. The keywords used in the search included “tooth filling, decay, white tooth filling”.

Then to further improve the question design, a series of real questions often encountered from patients in clinical settings were reviewed. Finally, 25 common questions related to restorative dental treatment were prepared. All inquiries were open-ended and intended to simulate real patient experiences. All questions were saved in a Microsoft Word document (Microsoft Corp., Microsoft Office 365, Version 16.0, Redmond, Washington, WA, USA) (Table 1). Rather than complex medical conditions, these questions represented real-world patient questions expressed in their own language.

The questions asked of the chatbots

Before the questions were asked to the LLMs, they were reviewed for grammar, accuracy, and clarity by two of the authors (SC, ÖA). In this study, a comparative evaluation was performed by asking 25 questions to four different LLMs. To achieve consistency, all procedures were performed on the same day using the same equipment and network infrastructure.

The equipment used in this study was a single laptop computer with a cable Internet connection. This selection was made to reduce the effect of network fluctuations on the results. All questions were asked by a single researcher with no further involvement in the research team on October 2, 2025 in newly created accounts on the following platforms: ChatGPT-5 Thinking mode (OpenAI, https://chatgpt.com), Gemini 2.5 Pro Reasoning mode (Google, https://gemini.google.com), Claude Sonnet-4.5 (Anthropic, https://claude.ai/new), and DeepSeek-V3.2 DeepThink mode (Hangzhou DeepSeek, https://chat.deepseek.com).

Each question was asked in a new chat session to prevent the model from being influenced by its previous responses. Before each session, the system cache was cleared to remove previous chat history, allowing the models to focus solely on the current input. Each model was permitted to provide only one response; it was not possible to change, reproduce, or request additional responses. To ensure this, the ‘’create new response’’ option was disabled in the chat environment, allowing only the models’ initial responses to be objectively recorded. Interactions were limited to pre-defined English questions prepared for this study, and both inputs and outputs were conducted entirely in English.

The responses obtained were archived in the form of a single document for each LLM in Microsoft Word. To obtain objectivity in the evaluation process, the responses of the chatbots were randomly assigned color codes in Microsoft Word. Using this blind examination method, the researchers did not know the source of the responses during the evaluations. The analysis process was completed by sharing the information of which color code belonged to which chatbot after collecting the evaluation data. This method enabled the data obtained in this study to be uniformly stored and accessible for comparative analysis.

In the evaluations performed by a restorative dental treatment specialist and an endodontics specialist, each with 15 years of experience, the responses were assessed in two primary aspects of information accuracy and empathy levels of the responses.

To determine the responses’ readability level, we used Readable (https://readable.com), an online assessment tool. While multilingual, this tool was primarily developed for analyzing English texts. The Gunning Fog Index (GFI), Coleman Liau Index (CLI), Flesch Kincaid Grade Level (FKGL), Flesch Reading Ease (FRE), and Simple Measure of Gobbledygook (SMOG) are well known and widely used analysis tools to evaluate the text [25].

The FRE test uses two key variables to measure a text’s readability. The average number of words per sentence and the average number of syllables per word. The analysis also calculates the total number of words, the total number of sentences, and the average sentence length (words per sentence) [25]. Statistical analysis

Analyses were performed using the SPSS V25 software (IBM Inc., Armonk, NY, USA). The Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) was calculated to determine the agreement between the two raters. Based on the results of the normality tests, continuous variables with a normal distribution are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and variables not conforming to a normal distribution are presented as median (minimum–maximum). The Kruskal–Wallis test was applied when comparing three or more non-normally distributed groups. When overall significance was determined, the Dunn–Bonferroni test was used for subgroup comparisons. Relationships between variables were assessed using correlation analysis, and the Spearman correlation coefficient was calculated. For all analyses, statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Inter-rater consistency

The consistency between the two evaluators with respect to the accuracy points was evaluated using the single measure ICC (C,1) in two-way consistency for all the LLM models. In the ICC analyses related to evaluator consistency of the four models, statistically significant levels of agreement were observed for all the models (p < 0.001). The analysis results determined ICC = 0.900 for ChatGPT-5 (95% CI: 0.786–0.955, p < 0.001), ICC = 0.898 for Gemini 2.5 Pro (95% CI: 0.784–0.954, p < 0.001), ICC = 0.789 for DeepSeek-V3.2 (95% CI 0.576–0.902, p < 0.001), and ICC = 0.858 for Claude Sonnet 4.5 (95% CI: 0.706–0.935, p < 0.001). According to these results, the inter-rater reliability was excellent for ChatGPT-5, and at a high level for Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek-V3.2, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 (Table 2).

Accuracy, empathy, and readability

Accuracy

The accuracy of the responses given by the LLMs to the open-ended questions was compared, and statistically significant differences were determined between the models (p < 0.001). In the subgroup analyses, the mean accuracy points of the responses to the open-ended questions were determined to be statistically significantly higher for Claude Sonnet 4.5 (4.28 ± 0.61) than for Gemini 2.5 Pro (3.6 ± 0.5) (p = 0.006), and the mean accuracy points of DeepSeek V3.2 (4.8 ± 0.5) were observed to be statistically significantly higher than those of Claude Sonnet 4.5 (4.28 ± 0.61) (p < 0.001). The mean accuracy points of DeepSeek V3.2 (4.8 ± 0.5) were determined to be statistically significantly higher than those of ChatGPT-5 (4.12 ± 0.67) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (3.6 ± 0.5) (p = 0.02, p = 0.030, respectively). There were no significant differences observed in any other paired group comparisons (p > 0.05) (Table 4; Fig. 2).

Empathy

The empathy points of the responses given by the LLMs to the open-ended questions were compared, and statistically significant differences were determined between the models (p < 0.001). Discussion

According to current information, individuals who suffer from anxiety about visiting the dentist avoid or postpone dental appointments, and this leads to a deterioration in oral health, resulting in a decrease in quality of life. Poor oral hygiene is usually observed in those with a high level of anxiety and fear, and this results in more widespread dental decay and tooth loss [28].

Existing evidence has shown a direct relationship between dental amalgam and diseases. Moreover, considering that amalgam can be harmful because of the toxic structure of the material, there has been an increased demand for information about amalgam fillings and their removal in recent years [29]. Composite resins have been used since the 1960s and have become the most preferred material for dental restorations. The main reasons for this popularity are their aesthetic appearance, good bonding to the tooth, less material loss required, and low cost. Although survival analyses of restorations are still being researched, it has been shown that the socioeconomic status of the patient affects the long-term life of restorations [30–34]. While fear of the dentist leads to avoidance of appointments, poor oral hygiene, and diminished quality of life, increasing curiosity about materials such as amalgam and composites has reinforced the tendency of patients to search for information online (e.g., from artificial intelligence-based models).

Artificial intelligence (AI) systems cannot replace human specialists in healthcare; however, they should be used in conjunction with human judgment and scientific supervision. Therefore, scientists, developers, and professional bodies should carefully evaluate the accuracy and reliability of the information presented by AI. With extensive databases and the ability to understand natural language, LLMs such as ChatGPT-5 contribute significantly to the accessibility of medical information. However, potential errors or biases in the data obtained during the training of these models create a risk of producing incorrect information. This situation must be addressed carefully, as reliance on AI-generated information can give rise to critical outcomes in diagnosis and treatment processes.

Dentists evaluating the responses obtained from LLMs using their own accumulated medical knowledge can identify potential inconsistencies. In contrast, patients usually tend to perceive these responses as definitive and completely accurate information. Therefore, it is crucial that the responses presented to patients by LLMs are supervised and interpreted by physicians. This approach helps to prevent adverse effects on the patient of incorrect or incomplete information. The aim of this study was to compare and critically assess the responses of four LLMs to questions frequently asked by patients about restorative dentistry in terms of accuracy, readability, and empathy.

From the results obtained in this study, it was observed that the most accurate responses were given by DeepSeek V3.2, while the lowest accuracy rate was observed in the Gemini 2.5 Pro model. Similarly, the most empathic model was DeepSeek V3.2, and the lowest empathy points were obtained by Gemini 2.5 Pro. The most readable texts were determined to have been produced by DeepSeek V3.2. These results demonstrated different performance levels of the models, not only in terms of technical accuracy but also in terms of user experience and language fluency.

Similar to previous studies [35–37], each scenario in the current study was addressed to each LLM only once, and when the response of the model was insufficient, no additional explanation was made, and no follow-up questions were asked. This method aimed to conduct the evaluation under controlled conditions as far as possible and achieve comparability of the responses obtained from different models. The aim of this study was also to evaluate the consistency of the LLMs, all questions were asked on the same day. The main aim of the study was to examine whether the models provided immediate, accurate, understandable, and empathic responses to patient questions.

In a study that assessed the responses of DeepSeek, Gemini, ChatGPT-4o, and Perplexity to questions about cancer of the salivary gland, it was reported that DeepSeek emerged with specific features complementing the focus of the study, and made a significant contribution to a detailed understanding of the difficulties encountered in the clinical procedures for salivary gland cancer [38]. Although the focus of the current study was on patient questions, the responses produced with the highest accuracy were from the DeepSeek V3.2 model among the new versions of the LLMs used. As DeepSeek is a platform that is still open to the public and accessible free of charge, this makes it more accessible and reliable for patients in clinical decision-making processes, such as for restorative dentistry treatment with this accuracy level.

In comparative studies based on the European Urology Board questions, Asker et al. showed that ChatGPT-4o, DeepSeek-R1, and Grok models had the highest performance with accuracy rates of 80%, and the accuracy rates obtained for Claude 3.5 and Gemini were 77% and 68.4%, respectively. In terms of readability, the highest points were obtained by DeepSeek-R1 [39]. Similarly, in the current study, Gemini 2.5 Pro had the lowest accuracy points, and DeepSeek V3.2 reached the highest level of readability.

In a study that compared the DeepSeek V3 and ChatGPT-4o models in responding to frequently asked patient questions related to laparoscopic cholecystectomy, the DeepSeek V3 chatbot was reported to have produced more appropriate responses at a statistically significant level compared to ChatGPT-4o. In the evaluation performed using a Likert scale, DeepSeek V3 obtained 5 points in 19 of 20 questions, whereas ChatGPT-4o obtained 5 points in only 13 questions [40]. A similar pattern was observed in the current study. Although comparisons were made with more recent versions of the LLMs, the success of ChatGPT-5 in responding to patient questions lagged behind that of DeepSeek V3.2.

A 5-point Likert scale was used by Jacobs et al. to evaluate whether ChatGPT was an accurate and readable assistive tool for patients with respect to third molar tooth extraction. The AI platform exhibited strong performance in terms of producing medically accurate responses. From two reviewers, the mean accuracy rate was 4.36 out of 5 points, indicating that the responses were of good quality with a few minor errors [23]. A 5-point Likert scale was also used in this study to evaluate accuracy, and the mean accuracy scores of ChatGPT-5, DeepSeek V3.2, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 were all observed to be above 4 points. In a study by Balel, frequently asked patient questions about oral and maxillofacial surgery and technical questions for educational purposes were addressed using ChatGPT. Although ChatGPT provided reasonably accurate and useful responses to patient questions, it did not show the same performance in responding to advanced-level technical questions [41]. Patient questions are usually plainer, shorter, and have a repeatable structure, with few technical terms or components requiring a clinical decision; therefore, it is easier for the models to produce a response of higher accuracy for this type of content. However, this does not mean that chatbots can replace the dentist’s opinion or clinical decision-making.

In a previous study examining the accuracy and reliability of artificial intelligence chatbots as public information sources in implant dentistry, it was reported that the accuracy scores of ChatGPT-3.5, Gemini, and Copilot were similar. Similarly, in the present study, no statistically significant difference in accuracy was found between ChatGPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro. Our findings are consistent with the existing literature and support the notion that there is no clear superiority in terms of accuracy among different generations of large language models [42].

Empathy has been reported in the dentistry literature to decrease fear of dentists, strengthen the patient-dentist relationship, and increase patient collaboration and adaptation, thereby resulting in positive clinical outcomes and providing high patient satisfaction [43]. As in similar previous studies, empathy scoring was included in the current study, accepting that effective health-related communication is not only factual accuracy, but is also dependent on the manner of explanation and emotional response [24, 44–46].

In this study, the FRES and FKGL indices were primarily used to interpret readability in terms of patient education, the SMOG index was used as a supporting criterion for this evaluation, and the CLI and GFI indices were considered as complementary indicators of text complexity. In a study comparing the efficacy and empathy capability of AI chatbots in responding to frequently asked questions in the field of oral oncology, Rokhshad et al. reported that the responses of all the chatbots were more complex than the reading level recommended for 6th grade students, and this led to responses being less accessible to patients in terms of being understood. In addition, at least 60% of all responses were classified as slightly empathic or not completely empathic [24]. Consistent with previous findings, although DeepSeek V3.2 obtained higher readability and empathy points than the other LLMs, the readability of the responses of all the models was above the recommended 6th -grade level. In addition, the vast majority of the responses were seen to be either not empathic or at a limited level of empathy. According to these results, LLMs still need to be significantly improved in terms of readability and emotional sensitivity in health-related communication.

Previous research emphasized that chatbots used for guidance and patient information should ideally have high FRE and low FKGL scores for their text to be most useful to the user. This study observed that while DeepSeek-R1 and ChatGPT-4 were statistically more readable, the estimated reading level of all AI chatbots was comparable to that of 8th and 9th grade students [47]. In the current study, it was similarly seen that the texts of DeepSeek V3.2 were easier to read than those of the other models, but the general reading levels of all the chatbots corresponded to 8th and 9th grade levels. Moreover, the index values of CLI, GFI, and SMOG, which is widely used in the healthcare sector, showed a difficult reading level of the texts.

SMOG scores indicate the estimated training time required to understand a text and are an effective measure for assessing the readability of health-related texts. Accordingly, scores of 6–7 are classified as ‘’very easy’’, 8–9 as ‘’moderately easy’’, 10–12 as ‘’moderately difficult’’, and 13 or more as ‘’difficult’’. A previous study examining ChatGPT responses related to oral cancer diagnosis observed that the readability of these responses fell below the acceptable level recommended for patient education materials [48]. In the present study, the mean SMOG points of all the chatbots were > 10, which showed that the readability of the texts produced by the chatbots was at a moderately difficult level. Therefore, although the responses were generally understandable, there is a need for plainer and more simple language, especially for individuals with a low level of health literacy.

Limitations

This study was limited to 25 open-ended questions, and therefore it may not have fully represented all the question types about which patients are curious. Although the question selection was focused on common and clinically important subjects, the limited sample size may have been insufficient to fully reflect the range of patient experiences. This is a limitation to which attention must be considered when interpreting the results. As LLMs are updated and changed at frequent intervals, the responses examined in this study represent only a snapshot of a certain period. In addition, direct data collection from individuals who had undergone restorative This approach implies that possible variability between responses may be reflected in the results. In particular, failure to consider alternative responses that might be generated in different studies could limit the generalizability of the findings. dental treatment could have enriched the study dataset with first-hand experiences. However, this approach would have been beyond the scope of this research, as it would have required ethics permission and informed consent from the participants. Therefore, this is accepted as a limitation of this study, and it can be recommended that future studies include direct patient participation. In addition, empathy scores in this study reflect expert-based evaluations and may not directly represent patients’ perceived empathy levels. Given the subjective nature of empathy, future studies may benefit from expanding empathy analyses through the inclusion of evaluators from different disciplines, such as psychologists, to enhance external validity and clinical relevance.

Conclusions

This study compared the performance profiles of four large language models in responding to real-life patient questions related to restorative dental treatment. Within the scope of this study, DeepSeek V3.2 demonstrated a more balanced performance profile when accuracy, empathy, and readability were evaluated together. While Claude Sonnet 4.5 and ChatGPT-5 achieved high levels of accuracy, variability was observed in empathy and readability outcomes across models, and Gemini 2.5 Pro showed comparatively lower accuracy scores.

Considering the limited number of questions and the inherent subjectivity of certain evaluation criteria, these findings should not be interpreted as evidence of a definitive or generalizable superiority, but rather as comparative performance observations within the specific design and sample of this study. Overall, the results suggest that large language models may serve as supportive tools in patient information processes; however, clinical interpretations and recommendations should always be provided under the supervision of a dental professional. Future research incorporating larger datasets, diverse languages, varying health literacy levels, and prospective clinical outcomes is warranted to further clarify the role of these systems in dental practice.

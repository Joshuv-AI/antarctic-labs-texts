Abstract
. In this paper, we study the topology of ordered Hurwitz space. These are moduli spaces of branched covers with a choice of ordering on the branched points. Answering a question of Ellenberg, we prove that the homology of ordered Hurwitz spaces exhibit representation stability.



1.
Introduction

Hurwitz spaces are moduli spaces of branched covers of curves. Homological stability properties of Hurwitz spaces can often be used to answer questions concerning arithmetic statistics in the context of function fields. For example, they have been used to study Cohen–Lenstra heuristics for class groups
[
EVW16
,
LLb
,
LLc
]
, Malle’s conjecture on Galois groups
[
ETW
]
, and the minimalist conjecture for Selmer ranks of families of abelian varieties
[
EL
]
.

In this paper, we study a cover of Hurwitz space called
ordered Hurwitz space
. These spaces parameterize branched covers with a choice of order on the set of branched points. Motivated by questions concerning interactions between factorization statistics and the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics, Ellenberg
[
FMP19
, Problem 5]
asked if ordered Hurwitz spaces exhibt representation stability in the sense of Church–Farb
[
CF13
]
. The goal of this paper is to verify Ellenberg’s conjecture.

The homotopy type of the Hurwitz spaces we consider have the following description. Let
\mathrm{Br}_{n}
denote the braid group with
n
strands,
\mathrm{PBr}_{n}
denote the subgroup of pure braids, and let
\alpha_{j}\in\mathrm{Br}_{n}
be the braid that passes the
j
th strand over the
(j+1)
st. Let
G
be a group and
c\subseteq G
be a conjugacy class. Let
\mathrm{Br}_{n}
act on
c^{n}
via the formula:

\alpha_{j}\cdot(c_{1},\ldots,c_{j},c_{j+1},\ldots,c_{n})=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{j-1},c_{j}c_{j+1}c_{j}^{-1},c_{j},c_{j+2},\ldots,c_{n}).

Define

\mathrm{Hur}^{c}_{G,n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}c^{n}/\!\!/\mathrm{Br}_{n}

and

\mathrm{OHur}^{c}_{G,{{\bf n}}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}c^{n}/\!\!/\mathrm{PBr}_{n}.

Here
/\!\!/
denotes the so-called Borel construction or homotopy quotient. Note that
\mathrm{Hur}^{c}_{G,n}
has the homotopy type of the moduli space of branched covers of
\mathbb{C}
where the monodromy around each branched point is in
c
and with a choice of trivialization of the branched cover in a fixed boundary direction
[
EVW16
,
EVW
]
. A point in the space
\mathrm{OHur}^{c}_{G,n}
additionally has the data of an ordering on the branch points.

Ellenberg–Venkatesh–Westerland
[
EVW16
, Theorem 6.1]
proved a homological stability result for
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}^{c}_{G,n};\mathbb{Q})
under the the assumption that the group
G
and the conjugacy class
c
satisfies a hypothesis called the
non-splitting property

(see
Section

7
). Combined with subsequent work of Davis–Schlank
[
DS
]
, Ellenberg–Venkatesh–Westerland’s stability result implies that the dimensions of the vector spaces
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}^{c}_{G,n};\mathbb{Q})
do not depend on
n
for
n
sufficiently large compared with
i
. See
[
RW20
,
Bia24
,
BM25
,
LLa
]
for other results on the topology of Hurwitz spaces.

We instead investigate the behavior of the groups
H_{i}(\mathrm{OHur}^{c}_{G,{{\bf n}}};\mathbb{Q})
as
n
tends to infinity. The space
\mathrm{OHur}^{c}_{G,{{\bf n}}}
has a natural action of the symmetric group
\Sigma_{n}
by permuting the order of the branched points. We study stability properties of the groups
H_{i}(\mathrm{OHur}^{c}_{G,{{\bf n}}};\mathbb{Q})
as
\Sigma_{n}
-representations instead of simply as vector spaces.

The symmetric groups
\Sigma_{n}
come with families of representations
V(\lambda)_{n}
for each partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
of a non-negative integer. These are zero for
n<|\lambda|+\lambda_{1}
and are irreducible
\Sigma_{n}
-representations otherwise (see
Section

8
). Following Church–Farb
[
CF13
, Definitions 2.3 and 2.7]
, we say that a sequence
\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathbb{Q}[\Sigma_{n}]
-modules has
uniform multiplicity stability
with stable range
N_{0}
(
Section

9
) if the multiplicity of
V(\lambda)_{n}
in
M_{n}
does not depend on
n
for all
n\geq N_{0}
and all partitions
\lambda
. The “uniformity” here is the condition that the stable range does not depend on the partition
\lambda
. An advantage of this uniformity is that it implies that, in the stable range, the representation
M_{n}
determines the representation
M_{n+1}
.

Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
c
a conjugacy class satisfying the non-splitting property. There exist constants
\alpha
and
\beta
depending only on
G
and
c
such that
\{H_{i}(\mathrm{OHur}^{c}_{G,{{\bf n}}};\mathbb{Q})\}_{n},
viewed as a sequence of
\Sigma_{n}
-representations, has uniform multiplicity stability with stable range
\alpha i+\beta
.

We note that this result is closely related to work of Ellenberg–Shusterman
[
ES
]
and Landesman–Levy
[
LLd
]
. Uniform multiplity stability implies polynomial growth of dimension.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
c
a conjugacy class satisfying the non-splitting property. There exist constants
\alpha
and
\beta
depending only on
G
and
c
and integer-valued polynomials
p_{i,G,c}
of degree
\leq\alpha i+\beta
such that

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}H_{i}(\mathrm{OHur}^{c}_{G,{{\bf n}}};\mathbb{Q})=p_{i,G,c}(n)\text{ for all }n\geq\alpha i+\beta.

Since the family of irreducible trivial representations is the sequence
V(\lambda)_{n}
for
\lambda=\varnothing
, uniform multiplicity stability also implies stability for coinvariants. In this way
Section

1
recovers Ellenberg–Venkatesh–Westerland’s homological stability result for
\mathrm{Hur}^{c}_{G,n}
.

A categorical approach to the theory of representation stability was introduced by Church–Ellenberg–Farb
[
CEF15
]
using so-called
\operatorname{FI}
-
modules
. The sequence of representations
\{H_{i}(\mathrm{OHur}^{c}_{G,{{\bf n}}})\}_{n}
do not appear to naturally form an
\operatorname{FI}
-module. We describe an analogue of the category
\operatorname{FI}
adapted to Hurwitz spaces which we call
\mathrm{FI}(c)
; see
Section

10
for relevant definitions. Our stability results give the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose that
(G,c)
has the non-splitting property. There exists constants
a,b
such that
H_{i}(\mathrm{OHur}^{c}_{G};\mathbb{Q})
is generated as an
\mathrm{FI}(c)
-module degrees
\leq ai+b
.

Proof overview.

We adapt the tools used in Miller–Patzt–Petersen–Randal-Williams
[
MPPRW
]
to prove uniform twisted homological stability to apply to ordered Hurwitz spaces. A key input in this theory is vanishing for certain derived indecomposables. In this case, we need to consider the derived indecomposables of
\mathrm{OHur}^{c}_{G}
acting on
\pi_{0}\left(\mathrm{OHur}^{c}_{G}\right)
. Using ideas from Randal-Williams
[
RW24
]
, we show that these derived indecomposables are equivalent to a combinatorial chain complex that is
an ordered variant of the “Koszul-like complex” of Ellenberg–Venkatesh–Westerland
[
EVW16
]
. We use results and techniques of Ellenberg–Venkatesh–Westerland
[
EVW16
]
and Shusterman
[
Shu24
]
to show the homology of this ordered Koszul complex vanishes in a range.

2.
Notation and categorical setup

In this section, we describe some categorical preliminaries that will let us formulate our stability results. As is common in the modern homological and representation stability literature, we work in categories of representations of groupoids (see e.g.
[
SS
,
RWW17
,
Pat20
,
GKRW
,
MPP
]
).

Convention 2.1
.

We fix a commutative ring
\mathbb{K}
. When we take homology, we will use
\mathbb{K}
-coefficients. We will often suppress
\mathbb{K}
from the notation.

Notation 2.2
.

We will use the notation
\mathbb{N}
to denote the category of non-negative integers
n\in\mathbb{N}
and identity maps, viewed as a symmetric monoidal category by addition of integers.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\operatorname{FB}
denote the category of finite sets and bijections, viewed as a symmetric monoidal category, with monoidal product given by the disjoint union of sets. Given
S\in\operatorname{FB}
, let
\Sigma_{S}
denote
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{FB}}(S,S)
, the permutation group of
S
. We let
{\bf n}
denote the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and
{\bf 0}
the empty set
\varnothing
.
When
S={\bf n}
, we will write
\Sigma_{n}
for
\Sigma_{S}
.

We often will conflate
\operatorname{FB}
and its skeleton, the full subcategory with objects
\{{\bf n}\;|\;n\geq 0\}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{C}
be categories. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{G}}
denote the category of covariant functors from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{G}
.
Given objects
X
in
\mathcal{C}
and
S
an in
\mathcal{G}
, we sometimes will write
X_{S}
for
X(S)
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a ring. Let
\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathbb{K}}
denote the category of
\mathbb{K}
-modules. Let
\operatorname{sMod}_{\mathbb{K}}
denote the category of simplicial
\mathbb{K}
-modules. Let
\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{K}}
denote the category of chain complexes of
\mathbb{K}
-modules. Let
\operatorname{Set}
denote the category of sets. Let
\operatorname{Top}
denote the category of compactly generated weak Hausdorff topological spaces.

We will refer to a functor
X\colon\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{Top}
as a
\mathcal{C}
-space, etc. We will often view a
\mathcal{C}
-set as a
\mathcal{C}
-space or a
\mathcal{C}
-module as a
\mathcal{C}
-chain complex (concentrated in homological degree
0
), etc.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\odot)
be a symmetric monoidal category that is equivalent to a small category. Let
(\mathcal{C},\otriangleup)
be a closed symmetric monoidal category with all small limits and colimits. The functor category
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{G}}
inherits a symmetric monoidal structure
\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}
called
Day convolution
, due to Day
[
Day70
,
Day74
]
. It is characterized by a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{G}}}((X\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}Y)(-),Z(-))\;\cong\;\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{(\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G})}}(X(-)\otriangleup Y(-),Z(-\odot-)).

For example, in
\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathcal{\operatorname{FB}}}
it is given by the formula

(X\otimes_{\mathrm{FB}}Y)_{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname*{colim}_{f\colon A\sqcup B\xrightarrow{\cong}S}X_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}Y_{B}\cong\bigoplus_{i+j=|S|}\operatorname{Ind}^{\Sigma_{|S|}}_{\Sigma_{i}\times\Sigma_{j}}X_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}Y_{j}.

The action of an FB morphism
\phi\colon S\to T
is as follows. Given an isomorphism
f\colon A\sqcup B\xrightarrow{\cong}S
, the action of
\phi
on
(X\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}Y)(S)
is induced by the map

\left((\phi\circ f|_{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}(\phi\circ f|_{B})\right)\colon X_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}Y_{B}\longrightarrow X_{\phi(f(A))}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}Y_{\phi(f(B))}.

In
\operatorname{Top}^{\mathbb{N}}
it is given by the formula

(X\otimes_{\mathbb{N}}Y)_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigsqcup_{i+j=n}X_{i}\times Y_{j}.

Using the symmetric monoidal structure provided by the Day convolution product, we can define monoid objects and modules over such objects in these functor categories.

Notation 2.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\odot)
be a symmetric monoidal category that is equivalent to a small category. Let
(\mathcal{C},\otriangleup)
be a closed symmetric monoidal category with all small limits and colimits. Let
\mathbbm{1}
denote the unit object of
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{G}}
.

Example 2.7
.

In
\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathcal{\operatorname{FB}}}
,
\mathbbm{1}({\bf n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{K},&n=0\\
0,&n>0.\end{array}\right.

In
\operatorname{Top}^{\mathbb{N}}
,
\mathbbm{1}(n)
is a point for
n=0
and is the empty set for
n>0
.

Observe that if
X
is a (semi)-simplicial
\mathcal{C}
-space, then its geometric realization is a
\mathcal{C}
-space. If
Y
is a
\mathcal{C}
-space, its singular chains are a
\mathcal{C}
-chain complex, its
i
th homology is a
\mathcal{C}
-module, etc. If you apply a monoidal functor to a monoid object in one of these categories, you obtain a new monoid object.

We endow
\operatorname{sMod}_{\mathbb{K}}
with the standard model structure (see Quillen
[
Qui06
, Section 2.4]
) and
\operatorname{Top}
with the
standard (Quillen) model structure (see for example Quillen
[
Qui06
, Section 2.3]
or May–Ponto
[
MP11
, Section 17.2]
).

For a category
\mathcal{G}
that is equivalent to a small category, the categories
\operatorname{sMod}^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathbb{K}}
and
\operatorname{Top}^{\mathcal{G}}
then inherit the projective model structure (Hirschhorn
[
Hir03
, Theorem 11.6.1]
), wherein a morphism
X\to Y
is a weak homotopy equivalence (respectively, a fibration) if and only if
X_{S}\to Y_{S}
is for every
S\in\mathcal{G}
.

We now define a simplicial analogue of spheres. This will be relevant later when constructing generalizations of CW complex structures on spaces to more abstract contexts.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a ring. Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid. Fix
T\in\mathcal{G}
and
d\geq 0
.

Let
S_{\mathbb{K}}^{T,d}
in
\operatorname{sMod}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}
be the functor defined as follows. For
U\in\mathcal{G}
and
p\geq 0
, let

S_{\mathbb{K}}^{T,d}(U)_{p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{K}[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(T,U)]&\text{ if }p=d,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Face maps and degeneracies are (necessarily) trivial.

We now recall the definition of the two-sided bar construction. See, for example, May
[
May06
, Section 10]
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a symmetric monoidal groupoid, and let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal category that has all small colimits. Let
\mathbf{R}
be a monoid object in
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{G}}
with respect to Day convolution
\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}
. Let
\mathbf{M}
be a left
\mathbf{R}
-module and
\mathbf{N}
a right
\mathbf{R}
-module. Define
B_{\bullet}(\mathbf{N},\mathbf{R},\mathbf{M})
to be the following simplicial object in
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{G}}
. Let

B_{p}(\mathbf{N},\mathbf{R},\mathbf{M})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbf{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,p}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{M}.

The face map

d_{0}\colon(\mathbf{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{R})\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,(p-1)}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{M}\right)\longrightarrow\mathbf{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,(p-1)}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{M}\right)

is induced by the right module structure
\mathbf{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{N}
. The face map

d_{p}\colon\left(\mathbf{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,(p-1)}\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbf{R}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{M})\longrightarrow\left(\mathbf{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,(p-1)}\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{M}

is induced by the left module structure
\mathbf{R}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{M}\to\mathbf{M}
. For
0<i<p
the face maps

d_{i}\colon\left(\mathbf{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,(i-1)}\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}({\mathbf{R}}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}{\mathbf{R}})\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,(p-i-1)}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{M}\right)

\longrightarrow\left(\mathbf{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,(i-1)}\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{R}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,(p-i-1)}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{M}\right)

are induced by the monoidal structure
\mathbf{R}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}
. The degeneracy maps

s_{i}\colon\;\mathbf{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,p}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{M}\xrightarrow{\cong}\left(\mathbf{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,(i-1)}\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}{\mathbbm{1}}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,(p-i)}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{M}\right)\longrightarrow

\left(\mathbf{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,(i-1)}\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}{\mathbf{R}}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathbf{R}^{\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\,(p-i)}\otimes_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{M}\right)

are induced by the unit
\mathbbm{1}\to\mathbf{R}
of
\mathbf{R}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal model category. Let
\mathbf{R}
be a monoid object in
\mathcal{C}
and suppose that there is an augmentation map
\epsilon\colon\mathbf{R}\to\mathbbm{1}
from
\mathbf{R}
to the unit object
\mathbbm{1}
in
\mathcal{C}
. Let
\mathbf{R}\text{-mod}
denote the category of left
\mathbf{R}
-modules. There is an indecomposables functor

Q^{\mathbf{R}}\colon\mathbf{R}\text{-mod}\to\mathcal{C},

(for more details, see
[
GKRW
, Section 12.2, Section 9.4]
). The functor
Q^{\mathbf{R}}
is a left Quillen functor and so it admits a left derived functor called the
derived indecomposables functor

Q^{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathbb{L}}\colon\mathbf{R}\text{-mod}\to\mathcal{C}.

The derived indecomposables functor is homotopy invariant in the sense that if
f\colon\mathbf{M}\to\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
is a map of left
\mathbf{R}
-modules and a weak homotopy equivalence, then the induced map
Q^{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathbb{L}}(f)\colon Q^{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathbf{M})\to Q^{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
is also a weak homotopy equivalence. Under certain mild conditions on
\mathbf{R}
and
\mathbf{M}
, we have the following model for
Q^{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathbf{M})
.

Lemma 2.10

(
[
GKRW
, Lemma 9.18]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal model category. Let
\mathbf{R}
be a monoid object in
\mathcal{C}
and let
\mathbf{M}
be a left
\mathbf{R}
-module. If
\mathbf{R}
and
\mathbf{M}
are cofibrant in
\mathcal{C}
, then there is a weak homotopy equivalence

Q^{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathbf{M})\simeq\|B_{\bullet}(\mathbbm{1},\mathbf{R},\mathbf{M})\|.

An example of a cofibrant space in
\operatorname{Top}
is any space weakly homotopy equivalent to a CW-complex. If
\mathbb{K}
is a field, then any object in
\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{K}}
or
\operatorname{sMod}_{\mathbb{K}}
is cofibrant. If
\mathcal{G}
is a groupoid, then an object
X\in\mathcal{\operatorname{Top}}^{\mathcal{G}}
is cofibrant if for each object
S
in
\mathcal{G}
,
X_{S}
is
G
-homotopy equivalent to a
G
-CW complex with
G=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(S,S)
. If
\mathbb{K}
is characteristic zero and
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(S,S)
is finite for all objects

, then all objects of
\operatorname{Ch}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}
and
\operatorname{sMod}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathcal{G}}
are cofibrant.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\Delta_{\text{inj}}
denote the category of finite linearly ordered sets

[p]=\{0<1<\dots<p\},

with
p\in\mathbb{N}
, and morphisms the set of all order-preserving maps. Composition is given by composition of functions.

We remark that with our notation conventions, the underlying set of
[n-1]
is isomorphic to
{\bf n}
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a topologically enriched category.
A
co-
\mathcal{C}
-space
is an enriched covariant functor
X_{\sqbullet}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\to\text{Top}
.
Given
A\in\mathcal{C}
, let
X_{A}
denote the value of the functor
X_{\sqbullet}
at

.

Definition 2.13
.

An
augmented co-
\mathcal{C}
-space
is a triple
(X_{\sqbullet},X_{-1},\epsilon_{\sqbullet})
, with
X_{-1}\in\operatorname{Top}
,
X_{\sqbullet}
a co-
\mathcal{C}
-space, and
\epsilon_{\sqbullet}
an enriched natural transformation from
X_{\sqbullet}
to the constant co-
\mathcal{C}
-space at

.
The natural transformation

The following definition gives an explicit model for certain homotopy colimits.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a small topologically enriched category. Let
X_{\sqbullet}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\to\text{Top}
be a co-
\mathcal{C}
-space. Let
B_{\bullet}(*,\mathcal{C},X_{\sqbullet})
be the semi-simplicial space with

\bigsqcup_{A_{0},\ldots,A_{p}\in\text{ob}(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{C}(A_{0},A_{1})\times\mathcal{C}(A_{1},A_{2})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{C}(A_{p-1},A_{p})\times X_{A_{p}}.

The face map
d_{i}
is induced by dropping the map in
\mathcal{C}(A_{0},A_{1})
for
i=0
, by composing the maps in
\mathcal{C}(A_{i-1},A_{i})
and
\mathcal{C}(A_{i},A_{i+1})
for
1\leq i\leq p-1
, and by evaluation
\mathcal{C}(A_{p-1},A_{p})\times X_{A_{p}}\to X_{A_{p-1}}
for

.
The functorial model for the homotopy colimit
\operatorname*{hocolim}\limits_{\sqbullet\in\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}}X_{\sqbullet}
we use in this paper is
the thick geometric realization

\operatorname*{hocolim}\limits_{\sqbullet\in\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}}X_{\sqbullet}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}||B_{\bullet}(*,\mathcal{C},X_{\sqbullet})||.

Note that when
\mathcal{C}
is
\Delta_{\text{inj}}
, a co-
\Delta_{\text{inj}}
-space
X_{\bullet}
is a semi-simplicial space and the homotopy colimit
\operatorname*{hocolim}\limits_{\bullet\in\Delta_{\text{inj}}^{\operatorname{op}}}X_{\bullet}
is weakly homotopy equivalent to the thick geometric realization
||X_{\bullet}||
(see Dugger
[
Dug
, Example 9.15]

Given an augmentation

Definition 4.1
.

A

on a space

is a pair
(T,\mu)

and

\mu_{1}(0)=\mu_{0}(T_{0})

of
(T_{0},\mu_{0})
and
(T_{1},\mu_{1})

(T_{0}+T_{1},\mu_{2})

\mu_{2}(t)=\begin{cases}\mu_{0}(t)&\text{if }0\leq t\leq T_{0},\\
\mu_{1}(t-T_{0})&\text{if }t\geq T_{0}.\end{cases}

We denote the space of Moore paths on
X
by
\text{Moore}(X)
.

We will often suppress notation and write a Moore path as
\mu

.

\displaystyle((a^{\prime},\gamma),(a,\mu))

Definition 7.1

We now recall the definition of a quandle.

Definition 7.2

A

such that for every
y\in X

is a bijection from

X

x\in X
, and for every

(z^{x})^{y}=(z^{y})^{x^{y}}
.

An example of a quandle is a conjugacy invariant subset

of
G
with the binary operation given by conjugation.

Definition 7.3

Let

The following (well-known) lemma provides examples of connected quandles.

Lemma 7.4

Let
G

is a single conjugacy class of

Let

c

. Since
c
generates
G
, we can express

as a product

for

. Conjugation by each of the elements

define a sequence of edges in the Shreier graph
(c_{0},c_{0}^{a_{1}})
,
(c_{0}^{a_{1}},c_{0}^{a_{1}a_{2}})
, …,
(c_{0}^{a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{\ell-1}},c_{1})

to

.
∎

be a quandle. The braid group

Quantum stochastic linear quadratic control theory: Closed-loop solvability Thanks: This work is supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China(no.12271298 and no.11871308).
Penghui Wang, Shan Wang and Shengkai Zhao

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, we investigate the closed-loop solvability of the quantum stochastic linear quadratic optimal control problem.
We derive the Pontryagin maximum principle for the linear quadratic control problem of infinite-dimensional quantum stochastic systems.
The equivalence between unique closed-loop solvability for quantum stochastic linear quadratic optimal control problems and the well-posedness of the corresponding quantum Riccati equations is established.
Notably, although the quantum Riccati equation is an infinite-dimensional deterministic operator-valued ordinary differential equation, classical methods are not applicable. Inspired by Lü and Zhang’s approach [Q. Lü and X. Zhang, Probability Theory and Stochastic Modelling, 101. Springer, Cham, (2021) & Mem. Amer. Math. Soc. 294 (2024)] to stochastic Riccati equations, we prove the existence and uniqueness of its solutions.
The results provide a theoretical foundation for the optimal design of quantum control.

Subsequent work by Edwards, Nurdin, James, and others further explored quantum filtering dynamics and optimal feedback control. They applied dynamic programming principles to investigate the optimal control problem for finite-dimensional quantum systems and derived the corresponding finite-dimensional Riccati equations

To obtain the above result, we must overcome the following difficulties:

•

The quantum Riccati equations currently being studied are typically finite-dimensional
[
9
,
19
]
, i.e., matrix-valued, with relatively little research on infinite-dimensional quantum Riccati equations.

•

Unlike standard one-dimensional Brownian motions
[
20
,
31
,
32
]
, the Fermion Brownian motion

does not commute with the diffusion term.

•

In this case, although the quantum Riccati equation (

Therefore, the classical methods
[
20
,
31
]
for solving the deterministic Riccati equation are not applicable.

•

In the study of classical infinite-dimensional Riccati equations
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
, since forward (backward) stochastic differential equations possess sample paths, the resulting equations obtained through orthogonal projection onto finite-dimensional subspaces preserve their original adaptedness. In contrast, quantum stochastic differential equations lack sample path structures, thus rendering this methodology inapplicable to solving quantum Riccati equations.

To achieve this, we first truncate the initial (terminal) values of the quantum stochastic differential equations, which allows us to obtain approximate solutions for these equations. Next, we derive the Pontryagin maximum principle for
Problem (QSLQ)
concerning infinite-dimensional quantum stochastic systems (
1.2
). This derivation is based on the canonical anticommutation relations (CAR) of Fermion fields and classical variational methods. Our result builds upon previous contributions in the quantum stochastic theory of Fermion fields by scholars such as Gardiner
[

,

]
, Gough
[
13
]
, and Hudson and Parthasarathy
[
6
,
17
,
18
,
28
]
. Notably, the diffusion terms in the operator-valued quantum stochastic differential equations must incorporate the action of the parity operator
\Upsilon

[
3
,
4
,
29
]
, which is both self-adjoint and unitary. Finally, we prove that the unique existence of optimal feedback controls for the linear quadratic optimal control problem
is equivalent to the well-posedness of solutions to the quantum Riccati equation (

This result provide new approaches and insights for the control and optimization of

&\hskip 39.83368pt+\beta(s)dW(s),\quad\quad{\rm in}\ [t_{0},t],\\

\displaystyle\langle\alpha(t),x(t;\varsigma)\rangle-\langle\alpha(t_{0}),\varsigma\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\langle\alpha(s),\{A(s)+B(s)\Theta(s)\}x(s;\varsigma)\rangle ds+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\langle\beta(s),\{C(s)+D(s)\Theta(s)\}x(s;\varsigma)\rangle ds.

k_{t_{i}^{n}}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))
such that

a_{t_{i}^{n}}=m(a_{t_{i}^{n}})+\int_{0}^{t_{i}^{n}}k_{t_{i}^{n}}(\sigma)dW(\sigma);\quad k_{t_{i}^{n}}(\sigma)=0,\ \sigma>t_{i}^{n}.

(5.3)

For any
(s,\sigma)\in[0,T]\times[0,T]
, put

\mathcal{K}_{n}(s,\sigma):=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\chi_{[t_{i}^{n},t_{i+1}^{n})}(s)\chi_{[0,s]}(\sigma)k_{t_{i}^{n}}(\sigma).

Then

f_{n}(s)=m(f_{n}(s))+\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{K}_{n}(s,\sigma)dW(\sigma).

(5.4)

Hence, for any
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{K}_{i}(s,t)-\mathcal{K}_{j}(s,t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\int_{0}^{s}\|\mathcal{K}_{i}(s,t)-\mathcal{K}_{j}(s,t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\int_{0}^{s}\{\mathcal{K}_{i}(s,t)-\mathcal{K}_{j}(s,t)\}dW(t)\right\|_{2}ds

(5.5)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{T}\|f_{i}(s)-f_{j}(s)-m(f_{i}(s)-f_{j}(s))\|_{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2\|f_{i}-f_{j}\|_{L^{1}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))}.

Since
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{1}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))
, by (
5.5
), we deduce that
\{\mathcal{K}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{1}([0,T];L^{2}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))
. Thus, there is a
\mathcal{K}\in L^{1}([0,T];L^{2}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))
so that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{K}_{n}=\mathcal{K}
in
L^{1}([0,T];L^{2}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))
. Combining with (
5.4
), we can obtain that (
5.1
).

Similar to (
5.5
), one has

\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{K}_{n}(s,t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds\leq 2\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{\mathbb{A}}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))}.

(5.6)

Taking
n\to\infty
in (
5.6
), (
5.2
) holds.
∎

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\|x_{n}(t)-x(t)\|_{2}^{2}

(5.7)

\displaystyle\leq 3\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\left\{\left\|\Gamma_{n}\varsigma-\varsigma\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\{A(s)+B(s)\Theta(s)\}\left\{x_{n}(s)-x(s)\right\}ds\right\|_{2}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\{C(s)+D(s)\Theta(s)\}\left\{x_{n}(s)-x(s)\right\}dW(s)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\}.

\displaystyle\leq 3\left\{\left\|\Gamma_{n}\varsigma-\varsigma\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\{A(s)+B(s)\Theta(s)\}\left\{x_{n}(s)-x(s)\right\}ds\right\|_{2}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\{C(s)+D(s)\Theta(s)\}\left\{x_{n}(s)-x(s)\right\}dW(s)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\}.

Next, we calculate each item of the right of the above inequality.
By Hölder’s inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\{A(s)+B(s)\Theta(s)\}\left\{x_{n}(s)-x(s)\right\}ds\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 25.60747pt\leq\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\|\{A(s)+B(s)\Theta(s)\}\left\{x_{n}(s)-x(s)\right\}\|_{2}ds\right)^{2}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\hskip 25.60747pt\leq\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\|\{A(s)+B(s)\Theta(s)\}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))}ds\right)^{2}\left\{\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\left\|x_{n}(t)-x(t)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\}.

By the isometry of the Itô-Clifford stochastic integral in
L^{2}(\mathscr{C})

[
3
, Theorem 3.5(c)]
,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\{C(s)+D(s)\Theta(s)\}\left\{x_{n}(s)-x(s)\right\}dW(s)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 25.60747pt\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\|C(s)+D(s)\Theta(s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))}\|x_{n}(s)-x(s)\|_{2}^{2}ds

(5.9)

\displaystyle\hskip 25.60747pt\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\|C(s)+D(s)\Theta(s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))}ds\left\{\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\left\|x_{n}(t)-x(t)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\}.

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty

5.7

5.8
) and (
5.9
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\|x_{n}(t)-x(t)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\Gamma_{n}\varsigma-\varsigma\right\|_{2}^{2}+3\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\|\{A(s)+B(s)\Theta(s)\}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))}ds\right)^{2}\left\{\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\left\|x_{n}(t)-x(t)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle+3\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\|C(s)+D(s)\Theta(s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathscr{C}))}ds\left\{\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\left\|x_{n}(t)-x(t)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\}.

By the definition of
\Gamma_{n}
, we observe that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\Gamma_{n}\varsigma-\varsigma\right\|_{2}^{2}=0.

(5.10)

Then, by Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain that

(5.12)

Put

\widetilde{T}_{1}:=\inf\left\{t\in[t_{0},T];\,\left[6(5\mathcal{M}+1)\mathcal{M}+5\mathcal{M}\right]\cdot\max\{(T-t)^{2},T-t\}\leq\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

Quantization of spin circular photogalvanic effect in altermagnetic Weyl semimetals
Hiroki Yoshida, Jan Priessnitz, Libor Šmejkal, Shuichi Murakami

Abstract
We theoretically predict a spin-current analog of the quantized circular photogalvanic effect in Weyl semimetals. This phenomenon is forbidden in antiferromagnets by symmetry but uniquely allowed in altermagnets, highlighting a novel and intrinsic characteristic of altermagnetism. To systematically explore second-order spin current responses, we classify all symmetry-allowed responses based on spin point groups. Furthermore, we provide a comprehensive classification of altermagnetic Weyl semimetals by identifying spin space groups that host symmetry-enforced Weyl points. Utilizing this classification, we construct a symmetry-guided tight-binding model and confirm our predictions. Finally, we identify Weyl crossings in a material candidate via first-principle calculations. Our work unveils a distinctive optical response of altermagnets, paving the way for a new frontier in altermagnetism.

Spin bulk photovoltaic effects in altermagnets
—We start by investigating what kind of second-order optical responses are allowed in general altermagnets. Throughout this letter, we focus exclusively on collinear altermagnets. In this case, these systems have symmetry with respect to the semi-direct product of SO(2), the continuous symmetry about rotations around an axis parallel to the direction of spins, and the binary group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\left\{E,[\bar{C}_{2}||\mathcal{T}]\right\}
, where
\bar{C}_{2}
is the spin rotation around an axis perpendicular to spins followed by an inversion operation in the spin-only space. In addition to these symmetries, altermagnets are described by spin point groups (SPGs), composed of spin-rotation and real-space point group operations
Litvin and Opechowski 1974
;
Litvin 1977
.

We now consider the bulk photovoltaic effect (BPVE). The BPVE is a phenomenon where a direct current is induced by light in non-centrosymmetric bulk crystals, and it is a second-order optical response expressed as

\displaystyle j^{s}_{a}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{s}_{abc}(\omega)E_{b}(\omega)E_{c}(-\omega),

(2)

\displaystyle j_{a}^{\mathrm{spin}}\coloneqq j^{\uparrow}_{a}-j^{\downarrow}_{a}.

(3)

No SPGs

In order to have a non-zero BPVE, we need to break the inversion symmetry. There are 27 altermagnetic SPGs which satisfy this condition
Litvin 1977
;
Šmejkal et al. 2022a
. For all of them, we examine the form of the spin conductivity tensor. Under the application of the operation
[S||R]
to the system, Eq. (
2

\displaystyle j^{s^{\prime}}_{a^{\prime}}=([S||R]^{-1})^{s^{\prime}s}_{a^{\prime}a}\sigma^{s}_{abc}[E||R]_{bb^{\prime}}[E||R]_{cc^{\prime}}E_{b^{\prime}}E_{c^{\prime}}.

(4)

When the system is invariant under the operation
[S||R]
, it means that

\displaystyle\sigma^{s^{\prime}}_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}=([S||R]^{-1})^{s^{\prime}s}_{a^{\prime}a}\sigma^{s}_{abc}[E||R]_{bb^{\prime}}[E||R]_{cc^{\prime}}.

(5)

We can get the spin-dependent conductivity tensor for each spin group by listing all conditions by using all elements of the SPG and solving the equations given by Eq. (
5
). In the supplemental material

, we show all components of spin-dependent conductivity tensors. The conductivity tensor for the spin current is
\sigma^{\mathrm{spin}}_{abc}\coloneqq\sigma_{abc}^{\uparrow}-\sigma_{abc}^{\downarrow}
. Similarly, the electric current is given by
j_{a}^{\mathrm{ele}}\coloneqq j_{a}^{\uparrow}+j_{a}^{\downarrow}
and the conductivity tensor for the electric current is
\sigma^{\mathrm{ele}}_{abc}\coloneqq\sigma_{abc}^{\uparrow}+\sigma_{abc}^{\downarrow}
. We also list these conductivities in

. If the component of

is non-zero but

Quantization of pure spin circular photogalvanic effect
—Here we see that the quantization of pure spin CPGE is possible in altermagnets. For the CPGE, the spin conductivity
\beta^{s}_{ab}
and the circular conductivity
\sigma^{s}_{C,abc}
are related as

\displaystyle\beta^{s}_{ab}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ij}\varepsilon_{bij}\sigma^{s}_{C,aij},

(6)

for each spin. Then,
\mathrm{tr}[\beta^{s}]=\sigma_{xyz}^{s}+\sigma_{yzx}^{s}+\sigma_{zxy}^{s}
and we need SPGs which have
\mathrm{tr}[\beta^{\mathrm{spin}}]\neq 0
and
\mathrm{tr}[\beta^{\mathrm{ele}}]=0
for the pure spin CPGE. In Tab.
2
, we classify 27 SPGs into four groups depending on the possibility of generating electric and spin CPGE. The following 10 SPGs can generate the pure spin CPGE.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\prescript{2}{}{m},\,\prescript{2}{}{m}^{2}m^{1}2,\,\prescript{2}{}{\bar{4}},\,\prescript{1}{}{4}^{2}m^{2}m,\,\prescript{2}{}{\bar{4}}^{1}2^{2}m,\\
\prescript{1}{}{3}^{2}m,\,\prescript{2}{}{\bar{6}},\,\prescript{1}{}{6}^{2}m^{2}m,\,\prescript{2}{}{\bar{6}}^{2}m^{1}2,\,\prescript{2}{}{\bar{4}}^{1}3^{2}m\end{split}

(7)

This is one of our main results. This list contains all types of spin-momentum locking in altermagnets, which are combinations of planar or bulk and d-, g- or i-wave types.

Altermagnetic Weyl semimetals
—Here we identify spin space groups (SSGs) with symmetry protected Weyl points. In general, SSG
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{S}}
is constructed as
Litvin and Opechowski 1974

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{S}}=\quantity[E||\vb{f}]+\quantity[C_2||\vb{F}-\vb{f}],

(8)

where we call space groups
\mathbf{F}
and
\mathbf{f}
as parent and single-spin space groups, respectively, as elements of
\quantity[E||\vb{f}]
does not alter the spin. For the quantization of the pure spin CPGE, we need Weyl points composed of bands with the same spin polarization. Thus, we need to consider the symmetry of
\mathbf{f}
which enforces Weyl points.

In addition to
\mathbf{f}
, the
\quantity[\bar{C}_2||\mathcal{T}]
-symmetry of collinear magnets plays an important role here. As the
\bar{C}_{2}
operation in the spin-only space keep the spin but the
\quantity[E||\mathcal{T}]
operation acts in the real space as a time-reversal operation, the
\quantity[\bar{C}_2||\mathcal{T}]
-symmetry can be thought as an effective time-reversal symmetry which does not flip the spins.

Degeneracies at high-symmetry points in the Brillouin zone for a given space group are enforced by symmetry and they can be seen from the irreducible representations of the electronic bands. For time-reversal symmetric systems, Ref.
Yu et al. 2022
lists all symmetry-enforced band degeneracies in all space groups. Among them, we choose space groups
\mathbf{f}
with Weyl points. Then, to make an altermagnetic SSG
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{S}}
, the super group of
\mathbf{f}
should have a two-coset decomposition satisfying Eq. (
8
). Finally, we pick up the SSG
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{S}}
such that the corresponding SPG is in our list of SPGs that exhibit quantization of the pure spin CPGE (Eq. (
7
)). Based on these criteria, we find 34 SSGs which host symmetry-enforced Weyl points and compatible with Eq. (
7
). One example of such SSG is
R\prescript{1}{}{3}^{2}c
and we construct a tight-binding model of this SSG below to see the quantization. In the Supplemental material
sup
, we provide a full list of the SSGs. These SSGs are optimal candidates in the sense that they necessarily host Weyl points enforced by symmetry; however, it is important to note that they are not prerequisites for quantization. Other SSGs corresponding to the SPG in Eq. (
7
) may also exhibit quantization if they host accidental Weyl points.

Conclusion
—In this study, we demonstrated that the quantization of the spin circular photogalvanic effect can be achieved in altermagnetic Weyl semimetals. This effect cannot occur in conventional antiferromagnets, because the existence of the mirror plane, that is essential to generate pure spin currents in antiferromagnets, combined with the spin-degenerate bands cancel this effect. Thus, we provide a phenomenon unique to altermagnets. Through systematic analysis, we identified 10 out of 27 non-centrosymmetric altermagnetic spin point groups that support the quantization of the pure spin CPGE. Furthermore, we investigated spin space groups and tabulated 34 spin space groups that host symmetry-enforced Weyl points at high-symmetry points protected by crystalline symmetries and allows the quantization. From this list, we also proposed MnTiO
3
as a candidate material for an experimental realization with first-principle calculations. While the MnTiO3 is insulating, doping can bring the Weyl crossing points to Fermi level. These results provide strong support for the feasibility of experimental verification.

List of spin space groups for altermagnetic Weyl semimetals

Here, we list spin space groups (SSGs) that have symmetry protected Weyl points at high-symmetry points in the Brillouin zone. To find SSGs that allow the quantization of spin CPGE, we need Weyl points within same-spin bands. Degeneracies of same-spin bands can be analyzed by investigating the single spin part of the SSG, that is, the first term of

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{S}}=\quantity[E||\vb{f}]+\quantity[C_2||\vb{F}-\vb{f}],

(S30)

where
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{S}}
is a SSG, and
\mathbf{F}
and
\mathbf{f}
are space groups.

We search all candidate SSGs by the following procedure.

1.

Find single-spin space groups
\mathbf{f}
that have symmetry-enforced Weyl points at high-symmetry points

2.

List all possible super-group
\mathbf{F}
of
\mathbf{f}
that can be coset-decomposed into two groups that is isomorphic to the group
\left\{E,C_{2}\right\}
.

3.

Check that the obtained SSG
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{S}}
corresponds to the SPG that allow the quantization of spin CPGE (Eq. (
7
) of the main text)

For the procedure 1, we need to consider the
\quantity[\bar{C}_2||\mathcal{T}]
-symmetry, that is an effective time-reversal symmetry of collinear and coplanar magnets. This can be done by investigating the irreducible representations of bands for each space groups. Reference

Model Hamiltonian of SSG
R\prescript{1}{}{3}^{2}c

Figure S1:
Schematic figure of the tight-binding model.

Here we construct a tight-binding model with the spin space group
R\prescript{1}{}{3}^{2}c
that is expected to have Weyl points at high-symmetry points and allow the quantization of spin CPGE (Tab.
S2
).

The SSG
R\prescript{1}{}{3}^{2}c
is coset-decomposed as

\displaystyle R\prescript{1}{}{3}^{2}c=\quantity[E||R3]+\quantity[C_2||R3c-R3].

(S31)

We first construct a single-spin tight-binding Hamiltonian following
\quantity[E||R3]
under the effective time-reversal symmetry
\quantity[\bar{C}_2||\mathcal{T}]
. Then, we construct a Hamiltonian with opposite spin polarization by symmetry elements in
\quantity[C_2||R3c-R3]
.

For the single-spin model, we follow the standard procedure to construct a tight-binding Hamiltonian. We set primitive lattice vectors as
\mathbf{a}_{1}=(a/2,\sqrt{3}a/6,c)
,
\mathbf{a}_{2}=(-a/2,\sqrt{3}a/6,c)
, and
\mathbf{a}_{3}=(0,-\sqrt{3}a/3,c)
and take three-fold rotation axis parallel to the
z
-axis. Then, the glide planes contain this
z
-axis such as
x=0
. We put
p_{x}
and
p_{y}
orbitals at Wyckoff position
1a
. Then, consider nearest-neighbor and next-nearest-neighbor hoppings in the
R
-setting (Fig.
S1
). Hopping parameters are given following the Slater-Koster table
Slater and Koster 1954
. After incorporating the effective time-reversal symmetry, the Hamiltonian for the single-spin in the basis
(p_{+},p_{-})\coloneqq(p_{x}+ip_{y},p_{x}-ip_{y})
is given as

\displaystyle H^{s}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix h^s_{++}(\vb{k})&h^s_{-+}(\vb{k})\\h^s_{+-}(\vb{k})&h^s_{--}(\vb{k})\endlx@physics@matrix),

(S32)

where

\displaystyle h^{s}_{++}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{3}\real\quantity(c^s_1e^{-i\vb{k}\cdot\vb{d}_j}+c^s_2e^{-i\vb{k}\cdot\vb{a}_j})

(S33)

\displaystyle h^{s}_{--}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{3}\real\quantity((c^s_1)^*e^{-i\vb{k}\cdot\vb{d}_j}+(c^s_2)^*e^{-i\vb{k}\cdot\vb{a}_j})

(S34)

\displaystyle h^{s}_{+-}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{3}e^{i\frac{2}{3}\pi(j-1)}\quantity(c^s_3\cos(\vb{k}\cdot\vb{d}_j)+c^s_4\cos(\vb{k}\cdot\vb{a}_j)).

(S35)

Here,
s=\uparrow,\downarrow
indicates spin, and
\mathbf{d}_{1}=\mathbf{a}_{1}-\mathbf{a}_{2}=\quantity(a,0,0)
,
\mathbf{d}_{2}=\mathbf{a}_{2}-\mathbf{a}_{3}=(-a/2,\sqrt{3}a/2,0)
,
\mathbf{d}_{3}=\mathbf{a}_{3}-\mathbf{a}_{1}=(-a/2,-\sqrt{3}a/2,0)
are the vectors connecting sites in the
xy
-plane (Fig.
S1
). This Hamiltonian has Weyl points with monopole charge
Q=\pm 2
at
(0,0,0)
and
\quantity(1/2,1/2,-1/2)
points of the Brillouin zone. Here,
(x,y,z)
are the coefficients of the primitive lattice vectors
\mathbf{b}_{1}=2\pi\quantity(\frac{1}{a},\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}a},\frac{1}{c}),\mathbf{b}_{2}=2\pi\quantity(-\frac{1}{a},\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}a},\frac{1}{c})
, and
\mathbf{b}_{3}=2\pi\quantity(0,-\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}a},\frac{1}{c})
.

Next, we relate two Hamiltonians
H^{\uparrow,\downarrow}
by symmetry operations in
\quantity[C_2||R3c-R3]
. These symmetries are with respect to glide operations: mirror operations for planes including the
C_{3}
-rotation axis (
c
-axis) and a half-translation along the
c
-axis. In the basis
\Psi(x,y,z)=\quantity(\psi_+^{\uparrow}(x,y,z),\psi_-^{\uparrow}(x,y,z),\psi_+^{\downarrow}(x,y,z+c/2),\psi_-^{\downarrow}(x,y,z+c/2))
, where
\psi^{s}_{\pm}(\mathbf{x})
are the wavefunctions of
p_{x}
and
p_{y}
orbitals at position
\mathbf{x}
with spin-polarization
s
, one of the glide operation
G_{110}^{z}\coloneqq\{m_{110}|0\,0\,1/2\}
is expressed as

\displaystyle G_{110}^{z}

\displaystyle=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 0&0&0&-e^{-ik_zc}\\0&0&-e^{-ik_zc}&0\\0&-1&0&0\\-1&0&0&0\endlx@physics@matrix).

(S36)

Here, we note that
m_{110}
is in the hexagonal setting and is the mirror operation with respect to the plane perpendicular to the
x
-axis. The Hamiltonian of the system

\displaystyle H(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix H^{\uparrow}(\vb{k})&0\\0&H^{\downarrow}(\vb{k})\endlx@physics@matrix)

(S37)

has to satisfy

\displaystyle H(-k_{x},k_{y},k_{z})=G_{110}^{z}H(\mathbf{k})\quantity(G_{110}^z)^{-1}.

(S38)

This condition leads to

\displaystyle h^{\downarrow}_{++}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=h^{\uparrow}_{--}(-k_{x},k_{y},k_{z}),

(S39)

\displaystyle h^{\downarrow}_{--}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=h^{\uparrow}_{++}(-k_{x},k_{y},k_{z}),

(S40)

\displaystyle h^{\downarrow}_{+-}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=h^{\uparrow}_{-+}(-k_{x},k_{y},k_{z}),

(S41)

and the Hamiltonian defined by Eqs. (
9
) to (
15
) of the main text.

\mathrm{MnTiO_{3}}
. Projector-augment-wave (PAW) formalism, Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional and standard VASP pseudopotentials
Mn
,
Ti

were used. The plane-wave cutoff energy was set to

and

.

Partial regularity of suitable weak solutions to the incompressible magnetohydrodynamic equations
Mengyao Ding, Wenwen Huo, Chao Zhang

Abstract
This paper establishes a regularity theory for the magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) equations with external forces through scaling analysis. Inspired by the existing methodology, we utilize linearized approximations and the monotonicity property of harmonic functions to construct iterative sequences capturing scaling properties. This work successfully extends Navier-Stokes techniques to MHD coupling and demonstrates that the one dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure of the possible singular points for the suitable weak solutions is zero. 2020MSC: 35Q35; 76B03; 76W05 Keywords: Magnetohydrodynamic equations; Suitable weak solutions; Partial regularity

Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the following incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u-(B\cdot\nabla)B+\nabla\left(p+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}\right)=f,\\
\partial_{t}B-\Delta B+(u\cdot\nabla)B-(B\cdot\nabla)u=g,\\
{\rm div}\,u=0,\quad{\rm div}\,B=0,\end{array}\right.

where
u
,
p
and
B
represent the velocity field, its pressure and the magnetic field, respectively.
Here, we introduce the modified pressure
P:=p+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}
for notational simplicity.
In (
1
), the terms
f
and
g
account for external forces, with
f
denoting gravitational or mechanical force
and
g
describing external magnetic excitation such as current density.
As regards
f\in L^{q}(\Omega\times I)
with some
q\in(1,\infty)
,
we may assume
\operatorname{div}f=0
distributionally without loss of generality. Indeed,
the Helmholtz decomposition allows us to write
f=\nabla\Phi+f^{\prime}
with
\operatorname{div}f^{\prime}=0
and
\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\times I)}\leq C(q,\Omega\times I)\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\times I)}
, and the gradient term
\nabla\Phi
can be absorbed into
\nabla P
.

In the absence of the magnetic field, the system (
1
) under the force-free situation reduces to the incompressible
Navier-Stokes equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla P=0,\\
{\rm div}\,u=0.\end{array}\right.

In the seminal work
[
7
]
, Caffarelli, Kohn and Nirenberg proved that the suitable weak solutions of (
1
) remain locally regular
as long as

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{r}\int_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dxdt\leq\epsilon_{0}

(1.6)

with a certain absolute constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
. This local regularity criteria implies that solutions
are smooth away from a set with a null Hausdorff measure of dimension one.
The original proof in
[
7
]
employs a series of delicate iterations,
which have been simplified by Lin
[
18
]
, Ladyzhenskaya and Seregin
[
19
]
, among others.
Alternative local regularity criteria replacing (
1.6
) have also been developed;
for instance, Tian and Xin
[
25
]
showed that smallness of either the scaled local
L^{2}
-norm of
vorticity or the scaled local energy is sufficient to derive smoothness in a neighborhood.
Very recently, Wang
[
28
]
introduced a novel geometric framework to prove the celebrated Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg theorem,
primarily utilizing a new compactness lemma and monotonicity properties of harmonic functions.

Let us review some related literature concerning the MHD equations without external forces (i.e.,
f\equiv 0
and
g\equiv 0
).
Building upon the approach developed for the Navier-Stokes equations, He and Xin
[
13
]

established interior regularity under suitable local smallness conditions and suggested that the velocity field plays a more dominant role than the magnetic field.
Besides, an alternative regularity condition was presented by Kang and Lee in
[
17
]
,
where the authors required the smallness of a scaled norm of the velocity
u
, coupled with the boundedness (not necessarily smallness)
of a corresponding norm for the magnetic field
B
. The observation that smallness of
u
alone might provide a desired control of the full solution
(u,B)
was further pursued by Wang and Zhang
[
30
]
.
The authors determined a partial regularity condition relying exclusively on the scaled norm of the velocity
u
;
more specifically, they proved the existence of
r_{0}>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that the condition

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<r_{0}}r^{1-\frac{3}{p}-\frac{2}{q}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p,q}(Q_{r})}\leq\epsilon_{0}\quad{\rm with}~~1\leq\frac{3}{p}+\frac{2}{q}\leq 2

suffices to guarantee the local regularity of suitable weak solutions.
Beyond the aforementioned results closely related to our motivation,
various other regularity criteria for both Navier-Stokes and MHD systems have been extensively studied; see, e.g.,

[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
20
,
21
,
24
,
26
,
27
,
29
,
31
]
.

Incorporating the external force term into the MHD equations significantly enhances the theory’s capability to describe complex physical scenarios.
In the presence of external forces, Chamorro and He
[
2
]
reformulated the MHD equations into a symmetric form by
adopting the Elsasser formulation, and then established regularity criteria by extending the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg theory to the MHD equations within a parabolic Morrey-space framework.
Furthermore, Chamorro and He
[
1
]
showed that outside a set of vanishing parabolic one-dimensional Hausdorff measure,
the velocity field
u
and the magnetic field
B
are locally regular provided
f,g\in L^{2}((0,T);H^{1}(\Omega))
.
Under some local boundedness assumptions, a gain of regularity for weak solutions to the MHD equations was obtained by
Chamorro, Cortez, He and Jarrín
[
9
]
in the general setting of the Serrin regularity theory.
In addition, for the case
g=0
, Ren, Wang and Wu
[
23
]
studied the partial regularity of the suitable weak solutions
to the fractional MHD equations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n=2,3
, showing a non-dominant role of the magnetic field for the local regularity.

The purpose of this paper is to study the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg type regularity theory for the incompressible MHD equation (
1
)
in the presence of external forces
f,g\in L^{q}
(
q>\frac{5}{2}
). Our approach builds upon the geometric framework recently developed by Wang
[
28
]
for the Navier-Stokes equations.
By adapting this framework and systematically tackling the new challenges posed by the magnetic field
B
as well as the external forces
f
and
g
,
we successfully extend the classical partial regularity theory to the forced MHD system. Moreover, combining our main result with the strategy pioneered by Wang and Zhang
[
30
]
, we obtain a regularity criteria (stated in Corollary
1
)
that requires only the norm of the velocity field
u
to be small, while the external forces merely need to satisfy a mild integrability condition.

Notations.

Following the common usage, we denote
B_{r}(x)
by a ball with radius
r>0
centered at
x\in\Omega
.
We let
Q_{r}(x,t)
be a cylinder given by
Q_{r}(x,t)=B_{r}(x)\times(t-r^{2},t)
with
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
.
When there is no risk of ambiguity, we will omit the center and simplify the notations by
B_{r}=B_{r}(x)
and
Q_{r}=Q_{r}(x,t)
.
For an integrable function
\varphi
, the integral averages of
\varphi
over
B_{r}
and
Q_{r}
are defined as

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}}\varphi\,dx=\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\varphi\,dx,\quad\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}}\varphi\,dxdt=\frac{1}{|Q_{r}|}\int_{Q_{r}}\varphi\,dxdt.

And for exponents
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, the space
L^{p,q}(Q_{r})
is equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{p,q}(Q_{r})}:=\left(\int_{t-r^{2}}^{t}\left(\int_{B_{r}}|\varphi(y,s)|^{p}\,dy\right)^{q/p}\,ds\right)^{1/q},\quad 1\leq p,q<\infty,

where the corresponding integral is replaced by the essential supremum in the usual way in the case of
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
.
In particular, the space
L^{p,p}(Q_{r})
reduces to the standard Lebesgue space
L^{p}(Q_{r})
.

Given a set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
, for any
s\geq 0
, the
s
-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure
is defined by

\displaystyle P^{s}(X)=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}P^{s}_{\delta}(X),

where
P^{s}_{\delta}
is a
\delta
-approximation obtained by setting

\displaystyle P^{s}_{\delta}(X)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i}r_{i}^{s}:X\subset\bigcup_{i}Q_{r_{i}}:r_{i}<\delta\right\}.

In particular,
P^{s}(X)=0
if and only if for all
\epsilon>0
, the set
X
can be covered by a collection

\{Q_{r_{i}}\}
such that
\sum_{i}r_{i}^{s}<\epsilon
.

Finally, we use the symbol
C
to denote a universal constant without emphasizing specific value and may change line by line.

Main results.

Our results are established for suitable weak solutions, where the corresponding solution concept for the MHD equations (
1
)
is defined as below.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
T>0
. The triple
(u,B,P)
is called a suitable weak solution to the MHD equations (
1
) in

\Omega\times(-T,0)
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(a)

u,B\in L^{\infty}\left((-T,0);L^{2}(\Omega)\right)\cap L^{2}\left((-T,0);H^{1}(\Omega)\right)
,

P\in L^{\frac{3}{2}}\left((-T,0),L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)\right)
;

(b)

(u,B,P)
solves the MHD equations in
\Omega\times(-T,0)
in the sense of distribution;

(c)

(u,B,P)
satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(|u(x,t)|^{2}+|B(x,t)|^{2}\right)\phi(x,t)\,dx+2\int_{-T}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla B|^{2}\right)\phi\,dxds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(|u|^{2}+|B|^{2}\right)\left(\partial_{s}\phi+\Delta\phi\right)\,dxds+\int_{-T}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(u\cdot\nabla\phi\right)\left(|u|^{2}+|B|^{2}+2P\right)\,dxds

(1.7)

\displaystyle-2\int_{-T}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(B\cdot\nabla\phi\right)\left(u\cdot B\right)\,dxds+2\int_{-T}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\phi\left(u\cdot f+B\cdot g\right)\,dxds,\quad{\rm a.e.}~t\in(-T,0)

for each
\phi\geq 0
in
C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times(-T,0)\right)
.

To address disturbances posed by the external force terms,
we introduce the functionals for
f
and
g
:

\displaystyle F_{m,r}\left[f,g\right]:=r^{2}\sqrt[m]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}}|f|^{m}\,dxdt}+r^{2}\sqrt[m]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}}|g|^{m}\,dxdt},\quad m>\frac{5}{2}.

(1.8)

Adopting the method from
[
28
,
18
]
, we invoke the cubical functional

\displaystyle C_{r}\left[u,B,P\right]:=\sqrt[3]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}}|u|^{3}\,dxdt}+\sqrt[3]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}}|B|^{3}\,dxdt}+r\sqrt[\frac{3}{2}]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}}|P|^{\frac{3}{2}}\,dxdt},

(1.9)

which serves to capture the scaled properties of the suitable weak solution
(u,B,P)
.
The local regularity of solutions can be established by imposing smallness conditions on
C_{1}\left[u,B,P\right]
and
F_{m,1}\left[f,g\right]
.

We are now in a position to present our main results.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(u,B,P)
be a suitable weak solution of (
1
) in
Q_{1}
, where
f,g\in L^{m}(Q_{1})
with
m>\frac{5}{2}
. Then there exists a small positive constant

\delta_{*}=\delta_{*}(m)
such that if

\displaystyle C_{1}\left[u,B,P\right]+\sqrt{F_{m,1}\left[f,g\right]}\leq\delta_{*},

(1.10)

then we have

\displaystyle u,B\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{Q}_{\frac{1}{2}})

for some small
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Remark 1
.

Our proof of Theorem
1
adapts the framework from
[
28
]
for the Navier-Stokes equations,
where a key quantity
C_{r}\left[u,P\right]
was introduced to perform an iteration scheme.
For the MHD system (
1
), our analysis successfully reveals that the coupled magnetic field component
B
does not
fundamentally compromise the applicability of the existing framework. For handling the disturbance of external force terms,
a delicate analysis is required to guarantee that our regularity criterion is established on the mildest regularity requirements on
f
and
g
.
To achieve this, we introduce the functional
F_{m,r}\left[f,g\right]
, designed to admit the necessary decay properties as
r\rightarrow 0
.
Crucially, its square root
\sqrt{F_{m,r}\left[f,g\right]}
combined with
C_{r}\left[u,B,P\right]
implements iteration procedure,
constructing proper vectors
V_{k}
,
M_{k}
and time-dependent functions
h_{k}(t)
satisfying

\displaystyle C_{r^{k}}\left[u-V_{k},B-M_{k},P-h_{k}(t)\right]\leq\frac{1}{2^{k}}C_{1}\left[u,B,P\right]+\frac{k}{2^{k}}\sqrt{F_{m,1}\left[f,g\right]},\quad\forall~k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

(1.11)

Here, the differences
V_{k+1}-V_{k}
and
M_{k+1}-M_{k}
diminish as
k
increases. More specifically,

\displaystyle\max\big\{|V_{k+1}-V_{k}|\,,\,|M_{k+1}-M_{k}|\big\}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda}{2^{k+1}}C_{1}\left[u,B,P\right]+\frac{\Lambda(k+1)}{2^{k+1}}\sqrt{F_{m,1}\left[f,g\right]},\quad\forall~k\in\mathbb{N}^{+},

where
\Lambda
is a universal constant. These oscillation estimates above directly lead to the local Hölder continuity asserted in Theorem
1
.
Thus, while the outer scaffolding of the iteration is inherited from
[
28
]
,
its internal mechanics are redesigned to accommodate the coupled magnetic field and the external force terms.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(u,B,P)
be a suitable weak solution of (
1
) in
Q_{1}
. Suppose that
f
and
g
belong to
L^{m}(Q_{1})
with
m>\frac{5}{2}
.
There exists a small positive constant
\delta_{**}
such that for some
R>0
, if

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r\leq R}\frac{1}{r}\int_{Q_{r}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla B|^{2}\right)dxdt\leq\delta_{**},

(1.12)

then
u
,
B
are regular near the origin. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{S}
denotes the singular set for any suitable
weak solution of the MHD equations, then the one-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure of
\mathcal{S}
is zero.

As established in
[
30
]
, for the MHD equations without external forces, the velocity field plays a more decisive role than the magnetic field in regularity criteria. This naturally gives rise to the question of whether such velocity dominance persists in the presence of external forces. Through the subsequent corollary, we affirm this phenomenon by simultaneously combining Theorem
2
with
[
30
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
(u,B,P)
be a suitable weak solution of (
1
) in
Q_{1}
.
Suppose that
f
and
g
belong to
L^{m}(Q_{1})
with
m>\frac{5}{2}
.
Then there exist
\overline{R}>0
and a small positive constant
\overline{\delta}_{**}
such that if
u\in L^{p,q}(Q_{\overline{R}})
and

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<\overline{R}}r^{1-\frac{3}{p}-\frac{2}{q}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p,q}(Q_{r})}<\overline{\delta}_{**}

with
(p,q)
satisfying

\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{3}{p}+\frac{2}{q}\leq 2,\quad 1\leq q\leq\infty,\quad(p,q)\neq(\infty,1),

then
u
and
B
are regular near the origin.

(3.1)

and

\displaystyle C_{r}\left[u,B,P\right]:=\sqrt[3]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}}|u|^{3}\,dxdt}+\sqrt[3]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}}|B|^{3}\,dxdt}+r\sqrt[\frac{3}{2}]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}}|P|^{\frac{3}{2}}\,dxdt}.

(3.2)

If
(u,B)
is regular near the origin, there exist constant vectors
\hat{V}
and
\hat{M}

such that

\rightarrow 0
as

.
Based on this observation, our goal is to select two families of vector sequences
\{V_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\{M_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
such that
within the

-scaled domain, the quantity
C_{r^{k}}\left[u-V_{k},B-M_{k},0\right]
can be controlled by

with some order
\alpha>0
.
Following the strategy for the Navier-Stokes counterpart in
[
28
, Section 4]
, we also achieve our objective via an iterative procedure.
The central step is established by Lemma
3.2
and Proposition
3.1
.

We initialize the iteration by defining the scaled solutions:

\displaystyle u_{1}(x,t)=u(rx,r^{2}t)-V_{1},\quad B_{1}(x,t)=B(rx,r^{2}t)-M_{1},\quad P_{1}(x,t)=rP(rx,r^{2}t)-rh_{1}(r^{2}t),

(3.3)

with corresponding scaled external forces:

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,t)=r^{2}f(rx,r^{2}t),\quad g_{1}(x,t)=r^{2}g(rx,r^{2}t),

(3.4)

where
V_{1}
and
M_{1}
are constant vectors to be specified later and
h_{1}(t)
is a time-dependent function.
For our iterative scheme, we consider the following equations:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u_{1}-\Delta u_{1}+(rV_{1}+ru_{1})\cdot\nabla u_{1}-(rM_{1}+rB_{1})\cdot\nabla B_{1}+\nabla P_{1}=f_{1}&\quad{\rm in}~Q_{1},\\
\partial_{t}B_{1}-\Delta B_{1}+(rV_{1}+ru_{1})\cdot\nabla B_{1}-(rM_{1}+rB_{1})\cdot\nabla u_{1}=g_{1}&\quad{\rm in}~Q_{1},\\
{\rm div}\,u_{1}=0,\quad{\rm div}\,B_{1}=0&\quad{\rm in}~Q_{1}.\end{array}\right.

Note that the original equations (
1
) for
(u,B,P)
can be recovered from (
3
) by setting
r=1
and
V_{1}=M_{1}=0
.

Regarding the force terms, we introduce the functional
F_{m,r}\left[f,g\right]
defined in (
3.1
),
which possesses the estimate

\displaystyle F_{m,r}\left[f,g\right]\leq 2r^{\frac{2m-5}{m}}F_{m,1}\left[f,g\right],

as shown in Lemma
3.1
below. From this expression, the condition
m>\frac{5}{2}
clearly guarantees that

F_{m,r}
admits well-behaved decay, and this feature can address the effects arising from the external force term
in subsequent iterations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
m>\frac{5}{2}
and
\lambda_{0}:=\frac{1}{8^{\frac{m}{2m-5}}}
. Then for any
f,g\in L^{m}(Q_{1})
,

\displaystyle F_{m,\lambda_{0}}\left[f,g\right]\leq\frac{1}{4}F_{m,1}\left[f,g\right].

(3.8)

Moreover, for all
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{8^{\frac{m}{2m-5}}}]
and
i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,

\displaystyle F_{m,\lambda^{i}}\left[f,g\right]\leq\frac{1}{4^{i}}F_{m,1}\left[f,g\right].

(3.9)

Proof.

First, we observe that for any
r\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle r^{2m}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}}|f|^{m}\,dxdt=\frac{r^{2m-5}}{|B_{1}|}\int_{Q_{r}}|f|^{m}\,dxdt\leq r^{2m-5}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{1}}|f|^{m}\,dxdt.

This tells us that for any
r\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle r^{2}\sqrt[m]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}}|f|^{m}\,dxdt}\leq r^{\frac{2m-5}{m}}\sqrt[m]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{1}}|f|^{m}\,dxdt}.

Similarly, the external force
g
possesses the same characteristics:

\displaystyle r^{2}\sqrt[m]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{r}}|g|^{m}\,dxdt}\leq r^{\frac{2m-5}{m}}\sqrt[m]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{1}}|g|^{m}\,dxdt},\quad\forall~r\in(0,1).

From the above two inequalities, it is obvious that

\displaystyle F_{m,r}\left[f,g\right]\leq 2r^{\frac{2m-5}{m}}F_{m,1}\left[f,g\right].

Since
m>\frac{5}{2}
, we may choose
\lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{8^{\frac{m}{2m-5}}}
to ensure (
3.8
).

To prove (
3.9
), note that for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}]
and
i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{2mi}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{\lambda^{i}}}|f|^{m}\,dxdt=\frac{\lambda^{2mi-5i}}{|B_{1}|}\int_{Q_{\lambda^{i}}}|f|^{m}\,dxdt\leq\lambda^{2mi-5i}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{1}}|f|^{m}\,dxdt,

which implies

\displaystyle\lambda^{2i}\sqrt[m]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{\lambda^{i}}}|f|^{m}\,dxdt}\leq\lambda^{\frac{2mi-5i}{m}}\sqrt[m]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{1}}|f|^{m}\,dxdt},\quad\forall~\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}],~i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

An analogous estimate holds for the external force
g
:

\displaystyle\lambda^{2i}\sqrt[m]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{\lambda^{i}}}|g|^{m}\,dxdt}\leq\lambda^{\frac{2mi-5i}{m}}\sqrt[m]{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q_{1}}|g|^{m}\,dxdt},\quad\forall~\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}],~i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

Combining these two estimates gives

\displaystyle F_{m,\lambda^{i}}\left[f,g\right]\leq 2\lambda^{\frac{2mi-5i}{m}}F_{m,1}\left[f,g\right]\leq\frac{2}{8^{i}}F_{m,1}\left[f,g\right]\leq\frac{1}{4^{i}}F_{m,1}\left[f,g\right],\quad\forall~i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

The proof is finished.
∎

\displaystyle+C\overline{\delta}_{**}^{1/2}\left(\frac{r}{\kappa}\right)^{3/p^{\prime}}\tilde{H}_{\kappa}\left[P,p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\right]+C\left(\frac{r}{\kappa}\right)^{(4m-10)/m}\tilde{F}\left[\kappa\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg(\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\kappa}{\rho}\right)+\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{2}\overline{\delta}_{**}\left(\frac{\kappa}{\rho}\right)+\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{2}\overline{\delta}_{**}^{1/2}\left(\frac{\rho}{\kappa}\right)^{3/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\overline{\delta}_{**}^{1/2}\left(\frac{\kappa}{r}\right)+\left(\frac{r}{\kappa}\right)^{3/p^{\prime}}+\left(\frac{r}{\kappa}\right)^{(4m-10)/m}\bigg)\tilde{F}\left[\kappa\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\theta+\overline{\delta}_{**}\theta^{-3}+\overline{\delta}_{**}^{1/2}\theta^{-2+3/p^{\prime}}+\overline{\delta}_{**}^{1/2}\theta^{-2}+\theta^{6/p^{\prime}}+\theta^{(8m-20)/m}\right)\tilde{F}\left[\kappa\right].

If we select
\theta
sufficiently small and then choose
\overline{\delta}_{**}
small enough such that

\displaystyle C\left(\theta+\overline{\delta}_{**}\theta^{-3}+\overline{\delta}_{**}^{1/2}\theta^{-2+3/p^{\prime}}+\overline{\delta}_{**}^{1/2}\theta^{-2}+\theta^{6/p^{\prime}}+\theta^{(8m-20)/m}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2},

then the following iterative inequality can be obtained

\displaystyle\tilde{F}\left[\theta^{2}\kappa\right]\leq\frac{1}{2}\tilde{F}\left[\kappa\right].

Besides, it is straightforward to observe that

\displaystyle\tilde{F}\left[R\right]\leq C_{1},

where
C_{1}>0
relies on
R
,
\left\|(u,B)\right\|_{L^{\infty}((-1,0);L^{2}(B_{1}))\cap L^{2}((-1,0);H^{1}(B_{1}))}

and
\left\|(f,g)\right\|_{L^{m}((-1,0);L^{m}(B_{1}))}
.
Therefore, there exists
r_{*}=r_{*}(C_{1},\delta_{**})\in(0,\overline{R})
derived from standard iteration arguments such that

\displaystyle\tilde{F}\left[r\right]\leq\delta_{**},\quad\forall~0<r<r_{*}.

This result indicates that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\int_{Q_{r}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla B|^{2}\right)dxdt\leq\delta_{**},\quad\forall~0<r<r_{*},

which enables us to apply Theorem
2
and subsequently determine the local Hölder continuity.
It means that the velocity field plays a more significant role than the magnetic field in the local regularity theory of the MHD equations,
which is consistent with the findings in

]

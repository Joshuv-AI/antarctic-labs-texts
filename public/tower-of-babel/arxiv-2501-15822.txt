The cones of g -vectors
Mohamad Haerizadeh, Siamak Yassemi

Abstract
. This paper studies the wall-chamber structures of finite-dimensional ( τ \tau -tilting infinite) algebras via generic decompositions of g-vectors. In particular, we examine regions outside the chambers. We show that the cones of g-vectors are rational and simplicial. Moreover, we prove that the open cone of a given g-vector coincides with the interior of its TF \operatorname{TF} -equivalence class if and only if the two have the same dimension. Furthermore, we establish that g-vectors satisfy the ray condition when they are sufficiently far from the origin. As an application, we generalize several results of Asai and Iyama concerning TF \operatorname{TF} -equivalence classes of g-vectors.

Throughout this paper, let

a basic finite-dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field

Proof.

By Lemma

, we know that

are
\operatorname{TF}
-equivalent, for all

(h_{1},h_{2})\subseteq[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}

(\theta,h_{2})\cap\overline{[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}}=\emptyset
. Therefore,
(h_{1},h_{2})\cap\partial[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}=\emptyset

Theorem 5.11
.

For any two arbitrary g-vectors
g
and
h
, if
\operatorname{ind}(g)=\operatorname{ind}(h)
, then they are
\operatorname{TF}
-equivalent. The converse holds if both are tame.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{ind}(g)=\operatorname{ind}(h)
, then by Lemma

,

[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\supseteq\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(h)\}\subseteq[h]_{\operatorname{TF}}.

So we have
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\cap[h]_{\operatorname{TF}}\neq\emptyset
. Hence,
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}=[h]_{\operatorname{TF}}
.

Conversely, let
g
and
h
be tame and assume that
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}=[h]_{\operatorname{TF}}
. Since
g
is tame, by Lemma
5.3
, we have
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\cap K_{0}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda)\subseteq D_{h}
. Applying Lemma
5.3
again, one can find

such that
\mathcal{F}_{b}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{g}=\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
and
\mathcal{T}_{b}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{g}=\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
. Hence, by Lemma

, for some

,
\mathcal{F}_{b}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{tg-h}
and

for some

.

.

Let

be a g-vector. Then, there exist finitely many proper subsets

,

,

, and

Lemma 5.13
.

Let

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
g
be a g-vector.
Then there exists
H\subseteq\operatorname{ind}(g)
such that for all
h\in H
,
h\notin\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}(H\setminus\{h\}))\}
and

\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}H)\}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}.

Assume that
H_{0}:=\operatorname{ind}(g)=\{g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{s}\}
. If
g_{1}\in\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}(H_{0}\setminus\{g_{1}\}))\}

. Hence, by Lemma
5.9
, removing

from

H_{1}=H_{0}\setminus\{g_{1}\}
; otherwise,
H_{1}=H_{0}
. Next, if

g_{2}

H_{2}=H_{1}\setminus\{g_{2}\}

. Applying this procedure successively to all elements of

∎

Proposition 5.19
.

Let
g
be a reduced g-vector. Then,

is linearly independent.

Proof.

Let

be the generic decomposition of

. Consider an equation

where

, then

, for all

6.
Main results

This section presents our results regarding the cones of g-vectors and
\operatorname{TF}
-equivalence classes. We begin by demonstrating that Conjecture
1.1
holds for tame g-vectors. Additionally, we establish that the cones of g-vectors are rational and simplicial. Subsequently, we show that for any g-vector
g
, there exists
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
tg
satisfies the ray condition. As a consequence, we prove that the interior of the
\operatorname{TF}
-equivalence class and the open cone of a given g-vector coincide if and only if they are of the same dimension. Finally, we provide a necessary and sufficient condition for Conjecture
1.1
to hold.

\operatorname{TF}

Theorem 6.1
.

Let

be a tame g-vector. Then

[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}.

Proof.

Consider an arbitrary g-vector

,
without loss of generality, we can assume that
\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}=\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}
.
We show that there exists a g-vector in
\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}
satisfies the ray condition. Since
\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}
is a rational polyhedral cone, there are finitely many g-vectors
\{g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{s}\}
such that
\{\mathbb{R}^{>0}g_{1},\mathbb{R}^{>0}g_{2},\ldots,\mathbb{R}^{>0}g_{s}\}
is the complete set of pairwise distinct rays of
\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}
. Assume that for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq i\leq s
, we have
tg_{i}=h_{1}\oplus h_{2}
. Then by Lemma
5.6
,
g\in\operatorname{Cone}\{h_{1},h_{2}\}\subseteq\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}
. Since
\mathbb{R}^{>0}g_{i}
is a ray, we conclude that
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{>0}g
. Therefore, in this case, it follows from Lemma
5.3
that
g_{i}\in D_{\mathbb{N}g_{i}}
. Thus,
t_{i}g_{i}
is tame and
|\operatorname{ind}(t_{i}g_{i})|=1
, for some
t_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, there exists
t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
1\leq i\leq s
,
|\operatorname{ind}(t^{\prime}g_{i})|=1
. Thus,

g^{\prime}:=t^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{s}g_{i}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}t^{\prime}g_{i}

satisfies the ray condition. Moreover, by
[
21
, Theorem 4]
, the set
\{t^{\prime}g_{1},t^{\prime}g_{2},\ldots,t^{\prime}g_{s}\}
is linearly independent. Hence,
\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(g^{\prime})\}=\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}
is simplicial.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
g
be a g-vector. Then, it admits a reduced version
g^{\prime}
satisfying the ray condition and
|\operatorname{ind}(g^{\prime})|=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
g
be a g-vector. Then

\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}\cap[g]^{\circ}_{\operatorname{TF}}\subseteq\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}\subseteq[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}.

Especially, if
g\in[g]^{\circ}_{\operatorname{TF}}
, then

\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}\cap[g]^{\circ}_{\operatorname{TF}}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}.

Proof.

First, note that Lemma
5.6
along with Theorem
5.8
imply that
\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}\subseteq[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}
.
Moreover, it is straightforward to check that if
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=1
, then

\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}\cap[g]^{\circ}_{\operatorname{TF}}\subseteq\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\},

and by Corollary
5.10
, equality holds, if
g
belongs to
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}
. Moreover, if there exists a tame g-vector in
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}
, then the assertions follow from Lemma
5.9
and Theorem
6.1
. Otherwise, assume that
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\geq 2
and there is no tame g-vector in
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}
. We break down the proof into two cases.

First case:

Let
\operatorname{tame}(\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g))=\emptyset
. Then by Lemma
5.13
, we can see that for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
,

\partial\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(tg)\}\cap[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}=\emptyset
.
Therefore, by Theorem
5.8
,
\partial\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}\cap[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}=\emptyset
. Thus,

\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}\cap[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}.

Second case:

Let
\operatorname{tame}(\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g))\neq\emptyset
. Fix the notation of Proposition
5.20
. Based on Theorem
6.2
, without loss of generality, we can assume that

•

\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}=\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}
,

•

f
and
h_{1}
are direct summands of
g
, and

•

f,h_{1}\in\partial\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}
.

Now, let

(6.2)

\partial\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}\cap[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}\neq\emptyset.

Then by Proposition
5.20
,
f^{\prime}\oplus h_{1}
is included in the above intersection, for some direct summand
f^{\prime}
of
f
. Therefore, since
f
and
f^{\prime}\oplus h_{1}
are integer vectors, it is easy to see that there is a g-vector
h^{\prime}\in[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\setminus\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}
such that
f^{\prime}\oplus h_{1}\in\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{f,h^{\prime}\}
. Additionally, by Lemma
5.3
,
f+sh^{\prime}=f\oplus sh^{\prime}
, for some
s\in\mathbb{N}
.
However, by Lemma
5.9
,

\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}=\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}(f\oplus sh^{\prime}))\}.

Since
h^{\prime}\notin\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(g)\}
, this leads to a contradiction. Thus, (
6.2
) never holds.

Moreover, The second part is a direct consequence of the first part and Corollary
5.10
.
∎

We now characterize precisely when the interior of the
\operatorname{TF}
-equivalence class coincides with the corresponding open cone of a given g-vector.

Theorem 6.6
.

For any g-vector
g
, the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
.

(2)

[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}
.

Proof.

In the case that
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=1
, the assertion is clear.
Let
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\geq 2
. Then,
\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}
is an open subset of
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}
. Thus,
g
belongs to
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}
. Therefore, by Lemma
6.5
, we have
\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}\cap[g]^{\circ}_{\operatorname{TF}}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}
. Hence,
\partial\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}\cap[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}=\emptyset
.
Thus,
(1)
implies
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}\subseteq\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}
. Consequently, by Corollary
5.10
,
(1)
implies
(2)
.
∎

6.2.
Equivalence of the two Conjectures

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
g
be a g-vector. If Conjecture
1.1
holds true, then

\operatorname{TF}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(g)<\infty
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
6.2
that
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(tg)\}
, for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, the assertion follows from Remark
5.12
and Lemma
5.6
.
∎

Proposition 6.8
.

Consider a g-vector
g
with
|\operatorname{TF}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(g)|=2
. Then,
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=1
. Moreover, if Conjecture
1.7
(2) holds, then
\operatorname{dim}\langle[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=1
.

Proof.

Assume that
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\geq 2
. Thus, there are linearly independent g-vectors
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
such that
tg=g_{1}\oplus g_{2}
, for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, by the assumption,
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
must be contained in
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}
. Since
g_{2}\in D_{g_{1}}
, we also have
g\in D_{g_{1}}
. Therefore,
t_{1}g_{1}
is tame, for some
t_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
. Similarly,
t_{2}g_{2}
is tame, for some
t_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,
t_{1}t_{2}g
is tame. Therefore, by Theorem
6.1
,
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(tt_{1}t_{2}g)\}
. This implies that neither
g_{1}
nor
g_{2}
lies in
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}
, which contradicts the assumption that
|\operatorname{TF}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(g)|=2
.
∎

Theorem 6.9
.

Conjecture
1.1
and Conjecture
1.7
are equivalent.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that if
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}
, then rational points are dense in
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}
. Moreover, by Proposition
6.7
, we can see that Conjecture
1.1
implies Conjecture
1.7
(3). Additionally, Conjecture
1.7
(2) follows obviously from Conjecture
1.1
. Therefore, if Conjecture
1.1
holds, then so does Conjecture
1.7
.

Conversely,
assume that Conjecture
1.7
holds true.
First, consider the case where
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=1
. In this case, the assertion follows directly from Theorem
6.6
.
Next, using Proposition
4.8
, we prove the remaining cases by induction on
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Assume that
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\geq 2
.
Then, according to Proposition
6.8
and Conjecture
1.7
(3), there are finitely many g-vectors
h^{1},\ldots,h^{s\geq 2}\in\partial[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\setminus[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}
such that
[h^{i}]_{\operatorname{TF}}\neq[h^{j}]_{\operatorname{TF}}
, for all
0\leq i\neq j\leq s
, and

\overline{[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}}\cap K_{0}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda)_{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq[g=h^{0}]_{\operatorname{TF}}\cup(\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}[h^{i}]^{\circ}_{\operatorname{TF}}).

From Proposition
4.8
, we know that
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle[h^{i}]_{\operatorname{TF}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\lneqq\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
, for each
1\leq i\leq s
. Thus, by induction hypothesis,
[h^{i}]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}h^{i})\}
. Next, take a reduced g-vector
g^{\prime}\in[g]^{\circ}_{\operatorname{TF}}
. Then by Conjecture
1.7
(2) and Corollary
6.4
, we can assume that

\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(g^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\geq 2.

Note that by Lemma
5.21
,
\operatorname{ind}(g^{\prime})\subseteq\partial[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\setminus[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}
. Therefore, the set

I^{\prime}:=\{0\leq i\leq s\mid\operatorname{ind}(g^{\prime})\cap[h^{i}]^{\circ}_{\operatorname{TF}}\neq\emptyset\}

does not contain zero.
Hence, by Lemma
5.9
, there exists
a\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}ag^{\prime})\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{i\in I^{\prime}}\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}h^{i}).

Therefore,
g^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cone}\{\bigcup_{i\in I^{\prime}}\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}h^{i})\}
.
On the other hand, by Lemma
5.3
, for all
i\neq j\in I^{\prime}
,
h^{i}\in D_{\mathbb{N}h^{j}}
. Thus, there exists
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that

h^{I^{\prime}}:=t\sum_{i\in I^{\prime}}h^{i}=\bigoplus_{i\in I^{\prime}}th^{i}.

It is straightforward to check that
g^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}h^{I^{\prime}})\}
.
Hence, for each reduced g-vector
g^{\prime}\in[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}
, there is a subset
I^{\prime}
of
\{1,\ldots,s\}
such that
g^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}h^{I^{\prime}})\}
. Since
\{1,\ldots,s\}
has only finitely many subsets, the set
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}\cap K_{0}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda)_{\mathbb{Q}}
can be covered by finitely many cones of dimension lower than or equal to
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
. Thus, Conjecture
1.7
(1) implies there exists a subset
I^{\prime}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
such that

\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}h^{I^{\prime}})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}.

Therefore,
\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}h^{I^{\prime}})\}\cap[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
. Since
\operatorname{Cone}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}h^{I^{\prime}})\}\subseteq\overline{[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}}
, we conclude that
\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}h^{I^{\prime}})\}\subseteq[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}
.
Thus, by Theorem
6.6
,

[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}h^{I^{\prime}})\}.

∎

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
g
be a g-vector. If
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}W_{g}=|\Lambda|-\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
, then
[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}
. The converse also holds, if moreover,
W_{g}=\operatorname{Ker}\langle[g]_{\operatorname{TF}},-\rangle
.
In particular, if
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=|\Lambda|-1
, then

[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}^{\circ}=\operatorname{Cone}^{\circ}\{\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
W_{g}\subseteq\ker\langle h,-\rangle
, for all
h\in[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}
. Thus,

(6.3)

\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}W_{g}\leq|\Lambda|-\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle[g]_{\operatorname{TF}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\leq|\Lambda|-\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}.

Therefore, the first and second assertions follows from Theorem
6.6
.

By Proposition
3.3
,
W_{g}\neq\{0\}
. Thus, by (
6.3
),

\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}W_{g}=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{Ker}\langle\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{N}g),-\rangle=1.

Therefore, the last assertion is a consequence of the first one.
∎

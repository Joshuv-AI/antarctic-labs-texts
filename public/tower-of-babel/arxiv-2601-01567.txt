g_{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\alpha}}\lambda_{i}^{n_{\alpha}}f_{i}^{n_{\alpha}},\,\{f_{i}^{n_{\alpha}}\}_{i=1}^{n_{\alpha}}\subseteq A
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\alpha}}|\lambda_{i}^{n_{\alpha}}|=1
for all
\alpha
. This implies
g_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\longrightarrow}}f
. Consequently, we have
f\in\overline{conv}^{w}\{A\}=\overline{conv}^{\|.\|}\{A\}
.

Now observe that
\phi
is
w-w
continuous and
\phi(\ell_{1}(A))
is weakly closed since
\phi
is an isometry. For
\alpha
, define
\Lambda_{\alpha}:A\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\Lambda_{\alpha}(f_{i}^{n_{\alpha}})=\lambda_{i}^{n_{\alpha}}
for
1\leq i\leq n_{\alpha}
and
0
elsewhere with
\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\alpha}}|\lambda_{i}^{n_{\alpha}}|=1
. It is clear that
\phi(\Lambda_{\alpha})=g_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha
. Now since
\phi(\Lambda_{\alpha})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\longrightarrow}}f
and
\phi(\ell_{1}(A))
is weakly closed hence
f\in\phi(\ell_{1}(A))
. This follows that
L_{1}(\mu)\cong\ell_{1}(A)
, where
A
is a discrete set.
∎

Motivated by the observation in Theorem
4.10
one can ask the following.

Problem 4.13
.

Let
X
be a Banach space where
I:(B_{X^{*}},w^{*})\to(B_{X^{*}},w)
is continuous on
ext(B_{X^{*}})
. Is
X
necessarily an Asplund space?

In
[
14
]
the authors have derived that if
Y
has property-
(U)
in
X
then a point of continuity of
I:(B_{Y^{*}},w^{*})\rightarrow(B_{Y^{*}},w)
is not necessarily a point of continuity of
I:(B_{X^{*}},w^{*})\rightarrow(B_{X^{*}},w)
.
Let us recall the notion of property-
(SU)
of a subspace in a Banach space (see
[
13
]
). A subspace that is an ideal and also has property-
(U)
has property-
(SU)
. Obviously, an
M
-ideal of a Banach space has property-
(SU)
. As discussed above, authors in
[
5
]
derived that the point of continuity of
I:(B_{Y^{*}},w^{*})\rightarrow(B_{Y^{*}},w)
remains the point of continuity of
I:(B_{X^{*}},w^{*})\rightarrow(B_{X^{*}},w)
when
Y
is an
M
-ideal in
X
.

Our next example shows that this property may fail if the condition
M
-ideal is weakened by the property-
(SU)
.

Example 4.14
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
X
that has property-
(SU)
in
X
. Then the unique extension of a point in
S_{Y^{*}}
, which is
w^{*}(Y)\mathchar 45w
point of continuity, may not retain the same in
S_{X^{*}}
.

Proof.

Consider
X=C[0,1]
and
Y=span\{f\}
, where

f(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2t&0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{2}\\
2-2t&\frac{1}{2}\leq t\leq 1\end{array}\right.

Being a subspace of one dimension, is always ideal and
f
is a smooth point of
C[0,1]
it now follows that
Y
has the property-
(SU)
in
X
. The reflexivity of
Y
ensures that
\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}\in Y^{*}
is a point of
w^{*}-w
continuity in
S_{Y^{*}}
. From Corollary
4.3
it follows that
\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}
cannot be a point of
w^{*}-w
continuity in
S_{X^{*}}
.
∎

Recall the definition of semi
M
-ideal in a Banach space. We refer the reader to p.43 of
[
9
]
in this context. Note that if
Y
is a semi
M
-ideal in
X
, then any
y^{*}\in Y^{*}
has a unique Hahn-Banach extension over
X
. We do not know the answer to the corresponding problem if
Y
is a semi
M
-ideal in
X
.

Y

y^{*}\in S_{Y^{*}}

I:(B_{Y^{*}},w^{*})\rightarrow(B_{Y^{*}},w)
. Is the map
I:(B_{X^{*}},w^{*})\rightarrow(B_{X^{*}},w)
continuous at
\tilde{y}^{*}

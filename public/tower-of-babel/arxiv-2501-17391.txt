Abstract
. Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have achieved remarkable success across various multimodal tasks. However, their practical deployment is often hindered by high computational costs and long inference time. Since the vision modality typically conveys more information than the text modality, its encoded representation often consists of hundreds or even thousands of tokens. This results in substantial computational overhead due to the quadratic complexity of attention mechanism. Existing token reduction methods are typically constrained to specific model architectures and often require extensive retraining or fine-tuning, which limits their applicability to many state-of-the-art models. In this work, we propose a learning-free token reduction (LFTR) method tailored for video-based MLLMs. LFTR can be seamlessly integrated into most open-source MLLM architectures without the need for additional fine-tuning. Leveraging the redundancy in visual representations, our approach reduces token on both temporal and spatial dimensions. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LFTR achieves up to 16 × 16\times reduction in visual tokens while maintaining or even improving performance on mainstream video question-answering benchmarks in a learning-free setting. Furthermore, LFTR is orthogonal to other acceleration techniques, such as vision encoder compression and post-training quantization, further facilitating the efficient deployment of MLLMs.

Despite their promising capabilities, the widespread deployment of MLLMs remains hindered by substantial computational demands, particularly the high latency during inference. To address these challenges, recent efforts have focused on improving the efficiency of LLMs

Beyond architectural compression, another major bottleneck arises from the large number of prefix tokens, especially visual tokens. The inference efficiency of MLLMs is significantly limited by the length of visual tokens, due to the quadratic computational complexity of the attention mechanism in Transformer layers. Observing the substantial redundancy among visual tokens, which often used as prefix inputs, several token reduction approaches have been introduced

Liu et al. 2024c
)

(
Beyer et al. 2024
;
Wang et al. 2024
)
, rendering fine-tuning infeasible for the open source community.

In this work we introduce Learning-Free Token Reduction (LFTR), a plug-and-play method designed to accelerate inference process in video-based MLLMs. LFTR is model-agnostic and can be seamlessly integrated into a wide range of existing architectures without additional training. The proposed approach targets redundancy in visual tokens across both temporal and spatial dimensions. By leveraging the varying significance of tokens along these dimensions, LFTR achieves substantial token reduction on both dimensions while maintaining or even improving model performance.

Specifically, for the temporal dimension, the proposed method calculates the similarity or difference of each patch token between adjacent time frames and merges tokens with lower significance. For the spatial dimension, two distinct strategies are proposed: (1) assessing the relevance of each spatial patch to the semantic content of the current frame, and (2) evaluating the relevance between each token with the input text prompts to select the most text-relevant spatial patch tokens accordingly. This design enables LFTR to be applied to any open source MLLM without requiring fine-tuning and facilitate comparable or improved model inference ability.

Extensive experiments are conducted to validate the effectiveness of the proposed method in terms of both token reduction and inference performance. The main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We introduce a learning-free token reduction method for the MLLM inference process in a plug-and-play manner, which is adaptable to a wide range of MLLM architectures.

•

We propose four different token reduction strategies across temporal and spatial dimensions for video inputs, leveraging the intrinsic characteristics of video modality to preserve significant information for language model inference.

•

Extensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed method significantly reduces token counts up to
16\times
token reduction, accelerating MLLM inference while maintaining or even improving inference performance.

2.2.
Token Reduction for LLMs

Token reduction has become an essential technique for enhancing the computational efficiency of Transformer-based architectures, particularly in visual and multimodal tasks. Early approaches primarily focused on token pruning
(
Rao et al. 2021
;
Xu et al. 2022
;
Liang et al. 2022
)
and token merging
(
Bolya et al. 2023
;
Marin et al. 2021
)
, which aim to eliminate or consolidate redundant tokens to reduce the computational burden. More recent methods, such as DiffRate
(
Chen et al. 2023b
)
and PPT
(
Wu et al. 2023
)
, combine both pruning and merging within unified frameworks to adaptively reduce token redundancy during inference.

Within the domain of MLLMs, visual token compression has drawn growing attention as a means to address the prohibitive costs associated with high-resolution image and video inputs. Several recent methods have introduced modality-specific mechanisms to guide token selection. For instance, CrossGET
(
Shi et al. 2024
)
and MADTP
(
Cao et al. 2024
)
leverage alignment tokens to selectively retain informative visual representations. LLaVA-PruMerge
(
Shang et al. 2024
)
exploits spatial redundancy by measuring similarity between class and patch tokens to identify compressible regions using clustering. Qwen-VL
(
Wang et al. 2024
)
employs a resampler to downsample the visual token sequence to a fixed length, while FastV
(
Chen et al. 2024b
)
implements an early-dropping strategy within the decoder layers to discard uninformative tokens, providing a lightweight yet effective alternative.

Despite the effectiveness of these techniques, many require architectural modifications, specialized fusion strategies, or fine-tuning, which may limit their applicability in general-purpose or training-free scenarios. These constraints pose challenges for integrating such methods into existing LMMs, particularly in real-world deployments where access to model internals or training data may be restricted.

In contrast, we propose a learning-free token reduction method that eliminates the need for model modification or supervision. Our approach introduces a general-purpose, architecture-agnostic framework capable of achieving high compression ratios while preserving inference quality. By decoupling the token reduction process from model training, the proposed method facilitates seamless integration into existing MLLM pipelines, offering a scalable and practical solution to visual redundancy in multimodal processing.

3.
Preliminary

3.1.
Vision Encoding

A widely adopted vision encoder in multimodal architectures is the Vision Transformer (ViT)
(
Dosovitskiy et al. 2020
)
, which employs a Transformer-based architecture to process visual inputs. Given an input image
I\in\mathbb{R}^{c\times h\times w}
, it is first divided into non-overlapping patches of size
p\times p
, resulting in
\frac{h}{p}\times\frac{w}{p}
total patches. Each patch is linearly projected into a token embedding and supplemented with positional encodings to preserve spatial information. The sequence of tokens is then passed through a Transformer encoder consisting of stacked layers of multi-head self-attention and feed-forward networks.

For video inputs, a common practice is to apply a 2D encoding strategy that treats each video frame independently as a static image. Each frame is processed by the same vision encoder, and the resulting token sequences are concatenated along the temporal axis to form a unified spatiotemporal representation. Among available vision encoders, CLIP-ViT
(
Radford et al. 2021
)
has emerged as a popular choice in MLLMs. Trained via a contrastive learning objective, CLIP-ViT aligns image and text embeddings within a shared representation space, enabling robust cross-modal retrieval and understanding.

3.2.
Vision-LLM

The general paradigm of MLLMs involves leveraging a large language model backbone for multimodal understanding, as illustrated in Figure
1
. Visual inputs are first processed by a vision encoder and subsequently transformed by a multimodal projector to align with the embedded textual tokens. Given a textual input
X_{T}
and a visual input
X_{V}
, both modalities are encoded into corresponding token sequences and projected into a shared representation space to facilitate joint reasoning.

(1)

\displaystyle Z_{T}

\displaystyle=f_{T}(X_{T})

(2)

\displaystyle Z_{V}

\displaystyle=f_{P}(f_{V}(X_{V}))

where
f_{T}
and
f_{V}
represents the text tokenizer and vision encoder,
f_{P}
represents the visual projection layer. The multimodal understanding and reasoning process can be represented as:

(3)

p(X_{A}|X_{V},X_{T})=\prod_{i=1}^{L}p_{\theta}(X_{A}^{[i]}|Z_{V},Z_{T}^{[1:i-1]})

where
L
denotes the length of the generated response
X_{A}
, and
\theta
is the learnable parameter of language model. The projected visual features
Z_{V}
and the embedded language instructions
Z_{T}
are jointly fed into the language model for multimodal reasoning.

A significant challenge in this pipeline lies in the large number of prefixed visual tokens, which leads to substantial computational overhead during inference induced by the quadratic complexity of attention mechanism. This issue is particularly pronounced in dense visual modalities such as video, where the length of visual token sequences far exceeds that of textual inputs. The resulting increase in computational burden not only result in high time computational demands of model inference process but may also distract model from critical visual cues, thereby hindering its ability to perform accurate visual reasoning.

Consequently, an effective token reduction strategy that exploits redundancies in both the temporal and spatial dimensions for video inputs holds promise for improving inference efficiency. More importantly, by removing irrelevant or redundant visual content, such a strategy can potentially enhance the model inference ability for understanding and reasoning over visual inputs.

4.
Method

.
Text-based Spatial Dimension Token Reduction Method. Based on the text prompt, we calculate the correlation between each visual token and the text instruction, and apply pruning to remove less relevant tokens accordingly.

To address the challenge of excessive inference time and the degradation of model performance caused by an overabundance of prefixed visual tokens, we propose a token compression method that targets both the temporal and spatial dimensions to reduce redundancy within the visual modality. As illustrated in Figure
1
, our approach offers a plug-and-play solution that can be seamlessly integrated into popular MLLM architectures without any retraining.

4.1.
Temporal Dimension Token Reduction

Differential-Based Significance Weights.
For video inputs, neighboring frames often exhibit substantial redundancy, as static objects and backgrounds remain consistent across time. To mitigate this redundancy, we propose to calculate the differences between adjacent frames to assess the significance of each spatial patch token within the temporal sequence and merge tokens with lower significance.

As illustrated in Figure
3
, tokens at the same spatial position
p
across different time frames are denoted as
Z_{V_{i,\,p}}(i=1,\dots,N_{t})
, where
N_{t}
is the sampled video frame number of video inputs. We compute the difference between neighboring frames to quantify their significance:

Figure 3
.
Temporal Dimension Token Reduction Method. The significance of each spatial patch token is calculated based on its similarity or difference with the corresponding token at the same spatial position in the previous time step.

(4)

\Delta Z_{V_{i,\,p}}=\sum_{d}|Z_{V_{i,\,p}}-Z_{V_{i-1,\,p}}|

where
d
is the embedding dimension. By setting a token reduction rate of
r_{m}
and selecting tokens with lower significance, we can merge these insignificant tokens with their corresponding tokens from the previous time step, thereby reducing the token count while preserving essential information.

Similarity-Based Significance Weights
. We also propose to calculate the similarity between spatial patch tokens across adjacent temporal frames to evaluate their significance, and applying token merging based on this assessment. Similarly, the significance weights are calculated as:

(5)

sim\,Z_{V_{i,\,p}}=<\hat{Z}_{V_{i,\,p}},\hat{Z}_{V_{i-1,\,p}}>

where
\hat{Z}_{V_{i,\,p}}=\frac{Z_{V_{i,\,p}}}{\|Z_{V_{i,\,p}}\|}
is the normalized token. The token merging strategy remains consistent with the previous approach.

With this temporal token reduction method,
(1-r_{m})\times N_{t}
tokens are retained for each spatial position. The parallel merging operation along the spatial dimension enhances execution efficiency. As a result, the total number of time frames in the visual input is reduced from
N_{t}
to
(1-r_{m})\times N_{t}
following the temporal reduction process. This strategy enables high token reduction rates with minimal information loss, resulting in more concise visual representations that facilitate more efficient language model inference and reasoning.

4.2.
Spatial Dimension Token Reduction

.
Topic-based Spatial Dimension Token Reduction Method. In each time frame, we compute the similarity of each spatial patch token with the [CLS] token to derive their significance weights and apply pruning based on the spatial patch tokens with less significance.

Topic-based Significance Weights.
In each time frame of the input, only a subset of spatial patches contains essential or topic-relevant information. Effective comprehension can often be achieved by focusing on these key regions within the image. Therefore, we leverage the [CLS] token from the visual encoder (e.g., CLIP-ViT
(
Radford et al. 2021
)
), which signals the presence of topic-related information in each frame, to prune irrelevant spatial patches.

As shown in Figure
4
, tokens at the same temporal frame
i
but at different positions are denoted as
Z_{V_{i,p}}(p=1,\dots,N_{s})
, where
N_{s}=\frac{h}{p}\times\frac{w}{p}
is the spatial patch token counts. We compute the relevance weights of each token by calculating the cosine similarity with the [CLS] token:

(6)

sim\,Z_{V_{i,\,p}}=<\hat{Z}_{V_{i,\,[CLS]}},\hat{Z}_{V_{i,\,p}}>

Given a spatial token reduction ratio
r_{p}
, we select the spatial tokens with the lowest
r_{p}\times N_{s}
relevance and discard these irrelevant tokens. Using this method,
(1-r_{p})\times N_{s}
tokens will be retained for each frame. This reduction process is also performed in parallel.

Text-based Significance Weights.
For vision tasks involving text prompts, the relevance of each spatial patch to the text prompt serves as a valuable metric for spatial token reduction. Since visual tokens are aligned with text prompts within the same feature space after the multimodal projector layers, their correlations can be utilized to retain only the information relevant to the question for the LLM. Specifically, as illustrated in Figure
2
, for a projected visual token
Z_{V}
and an embedded text prompt
Z_{T}
, the correlation weights can be computed by multiplying the visual tokens with the text prompts:

(7)

M=\sum_{L_{P}}{Z_{T}Z_{V_{t}}^{T}}

where
L_{P}
is the token length of input text prompt. Based on the correlation weights, we select visual tokens that are more strongly correlated with the text prompt.

(8)

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=\{argtop_{k}(M)\}

(9)

\displaystyle Z_{V_{t}}^{*}

\displaystyle=\{Z_{V_{t,\,p}}|p\in\Omega\}

where
top_{k}=r_{p}\times N_{s}
is determined by the given spatial token reduction ratio, and
(1-r_{p})\times N_{s}
tokens are preserved for each frame. These two spatial token reduction strategies are orthogonal to the temporal token reduction method introduced earlier, enabling joint application for enhanced MLLM acceleration and real-world implementation.

To validate the performance of our temporal and spatial token reduction strategies when applied individually with various token reduction rates, we conduct experiments on the MSVD-VQA dataset. The primary comparison metric for these experiments is the inference accuracy, which is evaluated using “ChatGPT-3.5-turbo”.

As shown in Fig.
5(b)
, our methods for reducing tokens in the temporal dimension achieve a significant reduction in the visual token count with minimal time overhead, resulting in a marked improvement in model inference efficiency. This enhanced efficiency and performance during inference can be attributed to the effective reduction of visual tokens across the temporal sequence. By retaining only the most crucial events within the sequence, our compressed visual tokens create a more concise representation, which enables the LLM to efficiently locate and process the relevant information needed for reasoning, while ignoring redundant content. The model can therefore focus more efficiently on the key information, improving overall performance without needing to process an excessive number of tokens.

For the spatial dimension, as illustrated in Fig.
5(a)
, both the proposed topic-based and text-based token reduction methods effectively enhance model inference performance while simultaneously reducing the token count. Specifically, the text-based token reduction method demonstrates slightly better performance compared to the topic-based token reduction method. This is entirely reasonable, considering that prompt-relevant information is more crucial for MLLM inference, where the alignment between visual tokens and textual prompts significantly influences model performance. In practice, the architecture of various MLLMs may differ, and some models may encode the vision input while keeping the raw text prompt separate, only concatenating it with the visual tokens at a later stage (e.g., MiniGPT4-Video). In these models, the text-based spatial token reduction method may not be applicable, whereas the topic-based token reduction method remains versatile and usable across different architectures, making it more flexible and adaptable to a wider range of MLLM designs.

6.
Conclusion

In this work, we propose a learning-free token reduction method for MLLM frameworks. Our method can be seamlessly integrated into any MLLM architecture in a plug-and-play manner, without requiring additional training or fine-tuning. It achieves high token reduction rates on both the temporal and spatial dimensions for video inputs. For the temporal dimension, we calculate the significance of each patch token and merge the insignificant ones. For the spatial dimension, we either compute the similarity between each spatial patch token and the [CLS] token or calculate the correlation between spatial patch tokens and text prompts, pruning those that are irrelevant to the topic or text.

We conduct extensive experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of our proposed method. In terms of MLLM performance, experiments on two distinct MLLM architectures are performed for zero-shot evaluation on Video-QA benchmarks. The results demonstrate that our LFTR method improves MLLM performance even under token reductions of up to
16\times
. For efficiency analysis, we perform both LLM computational cost analysis and LFTR execution time consumption analysis, showing the significant efficiency improvements brought by our method.

Our methods are evaluated through experiments on Video-QA tasks, and the results reveal that both spatial and temporal token reduction significantly enhance model inference efficiency and reasoning ability. By applying token reduction across both dimensions simultaneously, our approach achieves much higher token reduction rates while maintaining improved model inference accuracy. This LFTR technique can be seamlessly integrated into most popular MLLM frameworks or existing implementation pipelines. We also compare different combinations of token reduction strategies and conduct ablation studies to evaluate the consistency and robustness of our proposed method. Notably, our approach is orthogonal to existing acceleration techniques, such as quantization and KV-cache-based strategies, providing additional pathways for efficient MLLM implementation.

Regulatory reform and adult methylphenidate dispensing in France: a nationwide interrupted time-series study

Introduction

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a common neurodevelopmental condition characterised by persistent inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity that often persists into adulthood.1 Global prevalence estimates suggest that around 2–3% of adults are affected, with substantial consequences for educational attainment, employment, social functioning and quality of life.2 Adult ADHD is also frequently associated with psychiatric comorbidities, including mood disorders, anxiety disorders and substance use disorders, contributing to its overall public health burden.3 Pharmacological treatment, particularly stimulant medication, is recommended as first-line therapy and is overall effective for reducing core ADHD symptoms in adults, with a generally favourable tolerability profile.4–6

In many high-income countries, the use of ADHD medication in adults has increased steadily over the past decade.7 8 However, France has long remained an outlier, reporting markedly lower levels of adult ADHD medication use than neighbouring countries despite prevalence estimates that are broadly comparable.7 This discrepancy has raised concerns about underdiagnosis and undertreatment in adults, but the extent to which it reflects true differences in clinical need, diagnostic practices or regulatory barriers to care has remained unclear.

Until recently, access to pharmacological treatment for adult ADHD in France was shaped by restrictive regulatory conditions. Adult use of methylphenidate was largely limited to continuation of treatment initiated in childhood or adolescence and required prescribing initiation by specialists affiliated with hospital institutions (typically through hospital outpatient consultations), with no routine authorisation for adult treatment initiation in community-based or other non-hospital practice settings. Between 2021 and 2023, a sequence of regulatory reforms progressively expanded adult access to specific modified-release methylphenidate formulations, beginning with the reimbursement and adult indication of extended-release methylphenidate (Ritaline LP) in November 2021 and later extending to additional formulations. These reforms also allowed treatment initiation outside hospital institutions, including by specialists in private practice. These changes represented a substantial shift in the organisation of care and created a unique opportunity to quantify the specific contribution of regulatory access barriers to France’s historically low treatment rates, independently of potential differences in diagnostic practices.

ADHD affects approximately 3% of adults in France and may be substantially undertreated. Regulatory reforms affecting access to methylphenidate therefore provide a valuable opportunity to examine how policy changes influence population-level access to pharmacological treatment.

Objectives

To examine national trends in adult methylphenidate dispensing in France and evaluate the impact of the November 2021 regulatory reform expanding access to treatment. Secondary objectives were to assess whether dispensing trajectories differed by sex and prescriber specialty.

Methods

Study design and data source

We conducted a nationwide retrospective cohort study with a population-level interrupted time-series (ITS) design. We used data from the French National Health Data System (Système National des Données de Santé (SNDS)), a comprehensive medico-administrative database covering approximately 99% of the population residing in France.9 The SNDS includes individual-level information on demographics, healthcare utilisation and all medications dispensed in community pharmacies. Medications administered during hospital stays are not captured. A detailed study design diagram following the HARPER framework (HARmonized Protocol template to Enhance Reproducibility of hypothesis-evaluating real-world evidence studies) is provided in online supplemental figure S1.

Study population

The study population comprised all adults aged 18 years or older residing in France who received at least one community pharmacy dispensation of methylphenidate, defined by ATC (Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical Classification System) code N06BA04, between January 2015 and December 2024. Individuals were identified using anonymised identifiers and contributed to weekly population-level dispensing counts. The unit of analysis for time-series modelling was the calendar week, yielding a total of 520 weekly observations. The study period extended from January 2015 to December 2024. January 2015 was selected as the start date for two reasons: first, SNDS data are available from January 2010, making January 2015 the earliest date at which all participants have a complete 5-year look-back window for the incident initiator analysis; second, this window provides sufficient preintervention data for robust estimation of baseline trends and seasonal patterns using second-order Fourier terms. The underlying SNDS extraction covers data from January 2010; this earlier period was used exclusively to define washout windows for the secondary analysis of incident treatment initiation.

For ITS analyses, the primary intervention was defined as the reimbursement date of extended-release methylphenidate (Ritaline LP) in November 2021. Although adult marketing authorisation preceded reimbursement, we selected the reimbursement date a priori because it corresponds to the point at which treatment became effectively accessible in routine outpatient care and observable through community pharmacy dispensations captured in the SNDS. Given that marketing authorisation effects may be delayed by implementation and uptake processes, reimbursement was considered the most relevant breakpoint for population-level dispensing analyses.

Outcomes and population denominators

The primary outcome was the weekly population-standardised dispensing rate of methylphenidate among adults in France (expressed per 100 000 adults). For each week, we calculated the number of adults receiving at least one community pharmacy dispensation of methylphenidate; each individual was counted once per week regardless of the number of dispensations. Weekly dispensing rates were then modelled using these counts with a population offset derived from official annual adult population estimates (INSEE, Institut National de la Statistique et des Etudes Economiques), applied as constant values within each calendar year.

In descriptive analyses, annual prevalence was defined as the proportion of adults with at least one dispensation per calendar year (per 100 000), and annual incidence as the number of adults initiating treatment after a 1-year washout. Estimates were computed overall and by sex.

Statistical analysis

We analysed weekly population-standardised dispensing rates of methylphenidate among adults in France using ITS regression. Weekly counts were modelled with a population offset to estimate dispensing rates.

We fitted segmented regression models with a quasi-Poisson distribution and a log link to account for overdispersion. The intervention was defined a priori as the regulatory reimbursement of extended-release methylphenidate for adults in November 2021. Each model included terms for the preintervention trend, an immediate change in level at the time of the regulatory reform and a change in slope in the postintervention period. For interpretability, we report the postintervention weekly trend (ie, the combined preintervention slope and change in slope), expressed as weekly rate ratios (RR).

Seasonality was modelled using Fourier terms (K=2), selected a priori as the primary specification based on model fit assessed using the quasi-Akaike information criterion. To account for potential autocorrelation in the residuals, heteroskedasticity-consistent and autocorrelation-consistent SEs (Newey-West, lag=12) were used for statistical inference. The lag parameter was set conservatively relative to the Stock-Watson rule (floor (0.75×n(1/3))≈6 for n=520 observations) to account for the autocorrelation structure observed in the residuals. Residual autocorrelation was assessed using ACF (autocorrelation function) and PACF (partial autocorrelation function) plots of model residuals and Ljung-Box tests.

Model coefficients were exponentiated and interpreted as RRs, presented as percentage changes in the weekly dispensing rate per 100 000 adults (95% CIs). Immediate level changes represent the relative difference between observed and expected dispensing rates at the time of the intervention. For ease of interpretation, immediate level changes were also translated into absolute differences in the number of treated individuals based on the corresponding population size.

The full statistical model specification, including the regression equations used for the ITS analysis, is detailed in the online supplemental methods.

Analyses were conducted using R (V.4.4.2; 31 October 2024).

Stratified analyses

To assess potential heterogeneity in dispensing trajectories, we conducted prespecified stratified ITS analyses using the same model specification as in the primary analysis. First, models were fitted separately for women and men to compare preintervention trends, immediate level changes and postintervention slopes by sex. To formally test whether the reform effect differed between sexes, an interaction model was fitted including sex×intervention and sex×time-since-intervention terms, with men as the reference category.

Second, dispensing trends were examined according to prescriber specialty, classified into three groups—psychiatrists, general practitioners (GP) and other medical specialists—based on specialty codes recorded in the SNDS. Prescriber specialty refers to the specialty of the prescriber associated with each community pharmacy dispensation. Separate ITS models were fitted for each specialty group to assess differential changes in level and trend following the November 2021 regulatory reform. An equivalent interaction model was fitted for prescriber specialty, with other specialists as the reference category.

Secondary analysis: incident treatment initiation

As a secondary analysis, we focused on incident adult initiators of methylphenidate to distinguish changes in treatment initiation from continuation of pre-existing use. Incident initiation was defined as a community pharmacy dispensation of methylphenidate occurring after at least 1 year without any recorded dispensation, consistent with standard pharmacoepidemiological practice. Individuals included from January 2015 were required to have no prior methylphenidate dispensing in the 12 months preceding their first observed dispensation. For participants included from January 2015, this required data from January 2014, which were available in the SNDS extraction. The washout was applied against all dispensing records including those before age 18.

Weekly counts of incident initiators were analysed using the same ITS framework as in the primary analysis, including identical distributional assumptions, seasonal adjustment, population offset and robust SEs. This analysis aimed to assess whether the regulatory reform was primarily associated with increased initiation of methylphenidate treatment among adults rather than continuation of existing use.

Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analyses assessed the robustness of the findings to alternative modelling assumptions. Results were consistent across alternative specifications for seasonal adjustment, with stable effect estimates from ITS terms. Additionally, to examine the potential influence of the asymmetric observation window, a sensitivity analysis was conducted restricting the preintervention period to 3 years before the November 2021 reform, matching the duration of the postintervention period. Robustness to the choice of Newey-West lag (lag=6, 12 and 24 weeks) was also assessed. For the sensitivity analysis of incident treatment initiation, findings were also robust when alternative washout periods of 3 and 5 years were used to define initiation. For the 3-year washout, individuals included from January 2015 were required to have no prior methylphenidate dispensing from January 2012 onwards; for the 5-year washout, the washout window extends back to January 2010.

Ethics

The study was conducted in accordance with the MR-007 framework issued by the French Data Protection Authority (Commission Nationale de l’Informatique et des Libertés), which governs access to SNDS data and the conduct of retrospective research by healthcare institutions and academic teams.

Findings

Between 2015 and 2024, a total of 195 393 adults received at least one community pharmacy dispensation of methylphenidate in France, accounting for 2 638 875 dispensations recorded in the SNDS. Among those with known sex (n=194 917), 48.1% were women (n=93 722), and the median age at first dispensation was 28 years (IQR 19–41). The proportion of women increased from 43.2% in the pre-reform period to 50.4% post-reform, and the median age at first dispensation was similar across periods (27 years (IQR 19–42) vs 29 years (IQR 20–41)).

At the population level, the annual prevalence of adult methylphenidate use increased from 24 per 100 000 adults in 2015 to 141 per 100 000 adults in 2024. Over the same period, the annual incidence of adult methylphenidate initiation increased from 11 to 66 per 100 000 adults, corresponding to an increase in the number of adults initiating treatment from 7605 to 44 725 per year.

The ITS analysis showed a clear change in adult methylphenidate dispensing associated with the November 2021 regulatory reform (figure 1). Before the reform, the population-standardised weekly dispensing trend increased slowly, with an average growth of 0.2% per week (RR 1.002, 95% CI 1.002 to 1.002). At the time of the reimbursement update, an immediate level increase of 10.9% was observed compared with expected values (RR 1.109, 95% CI 1.057 to 1.164), corresponding to a weekly excess of approximately 346 adults treated across the entire French adult population (95% CI 181 to 521), compared with expected values under the pre-reform trend. Following the reform, the postintervention population-standardised weekly dispensing trend increased by approximately 0.7% per week (RR 1.007, 95% CI 1.007 to 1.008), indicating a sustained upward trajectory after November 2021. Over the 161-week post-reform follow-up period, this corresponded to an average of approximately 3043 additional adults treated per week compared with the pre-reform counterfactual trajectory (see the online supplemental methods for derivation). Residual autocorrelation was detected in model residuals (Ljung-Box Q=37.61, p<0.001 at lag 6; online supplemental figure S2), supporting the use of Newey-West heteroskedasticity-consistent and autocorrelation-consistent SEs for inference.

Sex-stratified dispensing trends

The reform was associated with an immediate increase in methylphenidate dispensing in both males (RR 1.088, 95% CI 1.041 to 1.136) and females (RR 1.144, 95% CI 1.082 to 1.208). The post-reform weekly slope also increased in both groups (male RR 1.007, 95% CI 1.006 to 1.007; female RR 1.008, 95% CI 1.008 to 1.009). Corresponding model RRs and figures are presented in online supplemental table S4 and figure S3.

A formal test of interaction confirmed a significantly stronger immediate effect of the reform in women than in men (change ratio of sex×immediate level change 1.097, 95% CI 1.046 to 1.150, p<0.001), with a modestly steeper post-reform trend in women (sex×change in trend: IRR [Incidence rate ratio] 1.002, 95% CI 1.002 to 1.002, p<0.001); the full interaction model is reported in online supplemental table S5.

Prescriber specialty-stratified analyses

Across the study period, approximately 60% of adult methylphenidate dispensing was attributed to GPs, 32% to psychiatrists and 8% to other medical specialists.

Stratified ITS analyses revealed marked differences in dispensing trajectories across prescriber specialties following the November 2021 reimbursement (figure 2). Among psychiatrists, the reimbursement was associated with a pronounced immediate level increase of 36.2% (RR 1.362, 95% CI 1.233 to 1.506), followed by a sustained increase in the postintervention dispensing trend of 1.0% per week (RR 1.010, 95% CI 1.009 to 1.010).

Solid grey lines indicate fitted interrupted time-series trends, dashed grey lines the counterfactual trends, and the vertical dashed red line the November 2021 reimbursement expanding adult access to methylphenidate.

Among GPs, no clear immediate change in dispensing level was observed at the time of reimbursement (RR 1.012, 95% CI 0.963 to 1.064), followed by a postintervention weekly trend increase of 0.6% (RR 1.006, 95% CI 1.006 to 1.007).

For other medical specialists, an immediate level increase of 11.1% was observed at the time of reimbursement (RR 1.111, 95% CI 1.027 to 1.203), along with a modest postintervention trend increase of 0.5% per week (RR 1.005, 95% CI 1.004 to 1.005). RRs for prescriber specialty-stratified ITS analyses are reported in online supplemental table S6.

A formal test of interaction confirmed a significantly stronger immediate effect of the reform among psychiatrists compared with other specialists (RR 1.608, 95% CI 1.422 to 1.818, p<0.001), with no significant differential effect observed for GPs (RR 0.922, 95% CI 0.844 to 1.009, p=0.077); the full interaction model is reported in online supplemental table S7.

Incident initiation of adult methylphenidate treatment

When incident initiation was defined using a 1-year washout period, adult methylphenidate initiation increased following the November 2021 reimbursement. Before the reimbursement, the population-standardised weekly rate of incident initiation increased slowly by approximately 0.2% per week (RR 1.002 (1.001–1.002)).

At the time of the reimbursement, an immediate level increase of 35.2% was observed (RR 1.352 (1.191–1.536)), corresponding to an excess of approximately 92 newly treated adults. Following the reimbursement, the postintervention weekly initiation trend increased by approximately 0.7% per week (RR 1.007 (1.006–1.008)), representing a sustained acceleration in incident treatment initiation compared with the preintervention period.

Results were robust to alternative washout definitions of 3 and 5 years, showing comparable immediate level changes and postintervention trends: 3-year washout 1.322 (1.172–1.492), 5-year washout 1.315 (1.165–1.483) (online supplemental table S8 and figure S4).

Discussion

In this nationwide ITS study based on French administrative health data, we found that the 2021 regulatory reform expanding adult access to methylphenidate was associated with a rapid and sustained increase in dispensing. Three main findings emerge: a strong immediate effect of the reform on adult methylphenidate use; a predominant contribution of incident treatment initiation rather than continuation of pre-existing use; and distinct dispensing dynamics according to sex and prescriber specialty. Together, these findings indicate that long-standing regulatory barriers, rather than abrupt changes in the epidemiology of adult ADHD, largely accounted for previously low treatment rates, and that policy change can rapidly reshape access to pharmacological treatment at a national scale.

Importantly, adult methylphenidate use in France had already been increasing before the 2021 reform, although at low levels, as reported in earlier national analyses.10 11 The reimbursement therefore appears to have accelerated a pre-existing upward trend, leading to a sharp and sustained increase that brought dispensing patterns closer to those observed in other high-income countries. However, at 141 per 100 000 adults in 2024, the French dispensing rate remains substantially below adult ADHD medication prevalences reported for comparable northern European countries: approximately 470 per 100 000 adults across the Nordic countries in 20107 and 1690 per 100 000 adults in Norway by 2022.12 This interpretation is consistent with international evidence showing steady growth in adult ADHD medication use over the past decade, with substantial cross-national variation.7 8 12 Together, these findings suggest that the historically low level of adult methylphenidate use in France was not solely attributable to differences in underlying clinical need but reflected structural features of the healthcare and regulatory system.

The increase observed after reimbursement should be interpreted in light of ongoing debates regarding rising adult ADHD diagnoses and pharmacotherapy. Recent work has highlighted that growing adult treatment rates may reflect both improved recognition of long-standing symptoms and broader diagnostic expansion, without clear consensus to date.11 13 In this context, the temporal pattern identified in our study, characterised by a discrete level change aligned with the regulatory reform followed by a sustained postintervention increase, argues against a sudden epidemiological shift. The incident initiation analyses further clarify this pattern, showing that the rise in dispensing was largely driven by new treatment initiations rather than continuation of existing use. This finding supports the interpretation that the reform facilitated entry into treatment among previously untreated adults. At the same time, given the known risks associated with stimulant medications, continued monitoring of prescribing practices remains essential to ensure appropriate use.14

Differences were also observed by sex, with larger immediate and sustained increases among women compared with men following the reimbursement. This pattern aligns with international evidence indicating that the male-to-female ratio in ADHD treatment narrows progressively from childhood to adulthood, reflecting differences in symptom presentation, diagnostic pathways and timing of recognition.7 15–17 Greater awareness of predominantly inattentive presentations, which are more frequently reported in adult women, may have contributed to increased identification and treatment in this group.18 However, whether this shift reflects improved recognition of previously underdiagnosed ADHD in women, broader diagnostic expansion or a combination of both cannot be determined from dispensing data alone. Continued epidemiological monitoring will be important to assess how changes in access and diagnostic practices affect long-term sex differences in adult ADHD treatment.

Dispensing trajectories also differed according to prescriber specialty. Among psychiatrists, the reimbursement was associated with a pronounced immediate increase in dispensing level (+36.2%), consistent with their role as the primary prescribers authorised to initiate adult methylphenidate treatment under the expanded regulatory framework. By contrast, GPs showed no statistically significant immediate change at the time of the reform (RR 1.012, 95% CI 0.963 to 1.064), and their continued post-reform increase largely reflects a pre-existing upward secular trend rather than a direct reform effect. This is consistent with the regulatory restriction limiting GPs to renewal and adaptation of existing prescriptions, which precludes a direct response to reforms expanding initiation rights. Together, these findings suggest that the November 2021 reform primarily activated specialist-level initiation, with GP dispensing driven by the ongoing secular growth in adult ADHD treatment that predated the reform. As prescribing responsibilities evolve, adequate training and support for primary care will be important to ensure consistent management of adult ADHD across the healthcare system.

From a policy perspective, these findings illustrate how regulatory frameworks can powerfully shape access to pharmacological treatments—a pattern documented across multiple therapeutic areas beyond psychiatry.19–21 In China, for example, government reimbursement negotiations for targeted anticancer medications were followed by substantial reductions in drug prices and rapid increases in utilisation volumes, without concurrent changes in disease epidemiology.19 Regulatory interventions that modify prescribing authority have likewise demonstrated immediate and sustained effects on prescribing behaviour.20 21 Together, these examples illustrate how changes in reimbursement status, authorised prescribers or indication scope can act as structural levers shaping treatment access at the population level. Anticipating such effects is essential for planning specialist capacity, ensuring continuity of care and maintaining appropriate monitoring following policy change.

The study has several notable strengths. The use of the SNDS enabled inclusion of virtually all community pharmacy dispensations of methylphenidate in mainland France over more than a decade, ensuring near-exhaustive population coverage and minimising selection bias. The ITS design provided a robust quasi-experimental framework to evaluate the impact of regulatory reform by distinguishing pre-existing trends from changes temporally associated with the intervention. In addition, the availability of weekly data allowed fine-grained assessment of both immediate and sustained effects, including stratified analyses by sex and prescriber specialty, as well as complementary analyses focusing on incident treatment initiation.

This study has several limitations. First, the use of weekly data, while enabling detection of immediate level changes, introduces higher serial autocorrelation compared with monthly aggregations. This was addressed through Newey-West heteroskedasticity-consistent and autocorrelation-consistent SEs with a lag of 12 weeks, which provides valid inference under serial correlation without requiring explicit modelling of its structure. Second, dispensing data do not capture confirmed clinical diagnoses, symptom severity or treatment indications. However, French health insurance data have been shown to provide a relevant basis for estimating ADHD diagnosis and medication use at the population level.22 In addition, methylphenidate use in adults in France remains tightly regulated and largely restricted to ADHD, reducing the likelihood of substantial off-label use. Third, although ITS analysis strengthens causal inference in the context of clearly timed policy changes, unmeasured concurrent factors cannot be entirely excluded. Nevertheless, the sharp temporal alignment of the level change with the reimbursement and the absence of alternative national reforms during this period support the interpretation that the observed changes were temporally associated with the reimbursement reform. Fourth, incident initiation was defined using a 1-year washout period, which may not fully distinguish new treatment from long-term discontinuation. However, findings were consistent when using more stringent washout definitions of 3 and 5 years, suggesting that the observed increase was robust to alternative specifications. Fifth, age-stratified ITS analyses were not performed; while the November 2021 reform applied uniformly across adult age groups, differential effects—for instance, between young adults newly accessing diagnosis and older adults with long-standing ADHD—cannot be excluded and represent a priority for future research. Finally, dispensing data do not allow assessment of treatment appropriateness, adherence or potential misuse. Ongoing pharmacovigilance and linkage with clinical data will be necessary to evaluate the longer term safety and quality of prescribing following expanded access.

Beyond methylphenidate, future research should extend similar quasi-experimental approaches to other stimulant medications newly authorised or reimbursed for adult ADHD in France, including lisdexamfetamine, as has been done in other settings.23 Dispensation count analyses, which address complementary questions such as treatment volume, pharmacovigilance or funding implications, would also be informative alongside individual-level counting approaches. Sustained pharmacovigilance and addictovigilance will be important to assess how expanded access influences patterns of use over time and to ensure that improved access is accompanied by appropriate prescribing practices. In the French context, where adult ADHD treatment has historically been tightly regulated, maintaining structured monitoring and supervised prescribing practices may help balance improved access with the prevention of misuse.24 Finally, infodemiological approaches, such as the analysis of Google Trends data for search terms related to ADHD and methylphenidate, could complement prescription-based surveillance by capturing temporal changes in population awareness and health-seeking behaviour and represent a promising direction for future research.

In conclusion, the 2021 regulatory reform expanding adult access to methylphenidate in France was associated with a substantial and sustained increase in dispensing, driven largely by new treatment initiation and shaped by prescriber specialty. These findings illustrate how regulatory policy can profoundly shape access to mental health treatment at a population level and underscore the importance of anticipating system-level consequences when implementing reforms affecting psychiatric care.

Clinical implications

The rapid increase in adult methylphenidate dispensing observed after the 2021 reform illustrates how regulatory frameworks can strongly influence access to evidence-based treatment. Health systems should anticipate substantial changes in prescribing patterns when access barriers are lifted and ensure adequate specialist capacity for diagnosis and treatment initiation. Continued monitoring of prescribing practices and pharmacovigilance will be essential to ensure appropriate and safe use of stimulant medications as access expands.

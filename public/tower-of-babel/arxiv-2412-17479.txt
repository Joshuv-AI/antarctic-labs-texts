The spectrum of defect ABJM theory
Charlotte Kristjansen, Xin Qian, and Chenliang Su

Abstract
We determine the spectrum of quantum fluctuations in a 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -BPS domain wall version of ABJM theory, thereby enabling the perturbative exploration of the corresponding defect CFT. As expected, the spectrum reflects the supersymmetry of the model.

2.3
On the spectrum of the theory

The expanded action above exhibits a very involved
mixing between both flavour and color components of
fields. For certain field components there appear mass-like terms
which depend on the coordinate transverse to the defect as

m^{2}/x_{d}^{2}
for bosons and as
m_{f}/x_{d}
for fermions.
Our aim is to diagonalize the quadratic part of
the action and thus prepare the theory for perturbative computations. The consequence of the space-dependent
masses for certain field components is well-known. The field components in question are confined to moving in
a half space, here
x_{d}=x_{2}\equiv z>0
, and their propagators become propagators in an auxiliary
AdS_{d+1}
space which has the transverse coordinate,
x_{d}
, as its radius and the domain wall as its boundary
[
18
,
19
]

Propagators in an
AdS_{d+1}
space can be expressed in terms of modified Bessel functions or equivalently
in terms of hypergeometric functions. More specifically, a scalar propagator

which fulfils the equation
below in

-dimensional Euclidean space

\left(-\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}+\frac{m^{2}}{x_{d}^{2}}\right)G({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})=\delta({\bf{x}}-{\bf{y}}),

(23)

can by a Weyl transformation be related to a propagator in
AdS_{d+1}
in the following way

G({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})=\frac{G_{\mbox{\footnotesize{$AdS$}}}\,({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})}{(x_{d}\,y_{d})^{\frac{d-1}{2}}},

(24)

where the AdS mass is given by

m^{2}_{\footnotesize\mbox{$AdS$}}=m^{2}-\frac{d^{2}-1}{4}.

(25)

Instead of characterizing a field in terms of its mass it is convenient to use the parameter
\nu
defined as

\nu=\sqrt{m^{2}+\frac{1}{4}}.

(26)

In order for a theory in

m^{2}_{\footnotesize\mbox{$AdS$}}\geq-\frac{d^{2}}{4},

(27)

which implies

m^{2}\geq-\frac{1}{4}\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{or}\hskip 14.22636pt\nu\geq 0.

(28)

The scalar propagator in Euclidean
AdS_{d+1}
can be written in the following form
[
43

\displaystyle G_{{AdS}}({\bf x},{\bf y})

\displaystyle=(x_{d}\,y_{d})^{d/2}\int\frac{d^{d}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dw\frac{w}{w^{2}+\vec{k}^{2}}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot(\vec{x}-\vec{y})}J_{\nu}(wx_{d})J_{\nu}(wy_{d}),

(29)

\displaystyle=(x_{d}\,y_{d})^{d/2}\int\frac{d^{d}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot(\vec{x}-\vec{y})}I_{\nu}(|\vec{k}|x_{d}^{<})K_{\nu}(|\vec{k}|x_{d}^{>}),

2.4
Classifying the fields

To prepare for the diagonalization, we perform a classification of our fields, first in flavor space and subsequently in color space. As for flavour space we notice
that the scalar fields
Y^{\alpha=3,4}
do not appear, neither in eq. (
18
), nor in
eq. (
21
) and eq. (
22
) due to the fact that the corresponding classical fields are vanishing.
Hence, these scalars do not mix with gauge field components and also not with the scalars
Y^{\alpha=1,2}
. We shall
therefore denote
Y^{\alpha=3,4}
as easy bosons. As opposed to these the scalar fields
Y^{\alpha=1,2}
mix
with the
z

kinetic Chern-Simons terms.
Hence, we separate the bosonic fields in the following two categories

easy bosons:

\displaystyle Y^{\tilde{\alpha}},\quad\tilde{\alpha}=3,4,

complicated bosons:

\displaystyle Y^{\alpha},\ A_{\mu},\ \hat{A}_{\mu},\quad\alpha=1,2,\ \mu=0,1,z.

In a standard gauge theory (as opposed to a Chern-Simons gauge theory) one would seek to eliminate mixing terms involving
derivatives of fields such as some of the terms in eq. (
18
) by working in background field gauge. This gauge choice, however, does not considerably simplify the mixing problem as we are still left with the kinetic Chern-Simons terms. We shall therefore not add gauge fixing terms at the present stage but solve the equations of motions for the various fields and impose the appropriate gauge constraints on the solutions later. Solving the equations of motion suffices for determining the
scaling behavior of a field near the
AdS
boundary and thus the conformal dimension of its dual operator.

In the same way we split the fermionic fields into easy and complicated according to the complexity of their mixing as follows (from now on unless explicitly stated,
\alpha
and
\tilde{\alpha}
will denote flavor indices),

easy fermions:

\displaystyle\psi_{{\alpha}},\quad{\alpha}=1,2,

complicated fermions:

\displaystyle\psi_{\tilde{\alpha}},\quad\tilde{\alpha}=3,4.

,

•

4.2.3

Now we turn to the diagonal block mixing which is the most complicated part. The corresponding Lagrangian is as same as that in eq. (

The idea is still to make use of decomposition of

However, it is not possible to write all classical fields in

in a bilinear form. This means that we can no longer directly express the classical fields in terms of the

defined by

\displaystyle L^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}X^{\beta}

\displaystyle=y^{\alpha}y_{\beta}^{\dagger}X^{\beta}-X^{\beta}y_{\beta}^{\dagger}y^{\alpha}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}+(\sigma_{i})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}L_{i}\right)X^{\beta},

where

is defined in eq. (

algebra, one finds the commutator

[L^{\alpha}_{\ \beta},L^{\gamma}_{\ \theta}]=-\left(L^{\alpha}_{\ \theta}\delta^{\gamma}_{\ \beta}-L^{\gamma}_{\ \beta}\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \theta}\right),

(91)

and the diagonalization is still given by the decomposition
\pi_{2}(s_{i})\otimes(\pi_{q-1}\otimes\pi_{q})(s_{i})\cong\bigoplus_{r=1}^{q-1}\pi_{2r+1}\oplus\pi_{2r-1}
, which yields

\displaystyle L^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\bigoplus_{r=1}^{q-1}\ r\mathbbm{1}_{2r+1}\oplus\,-r\mathbbm{1}_{2r-1}.

As the operators

and

\displaystyle\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}L^{\gamma}_{\ \theta}L^{\theta}_{\ \gamma}=\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}(\frac{1}{2}+2L_{i}L_{i})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\bigoplus_{r=1}^{q-1}2r^{2}\,\mathbbm{1}_{2r+1}\oplus 2r^{2}\,\mathbbm{1}_{2r-1}.

(93)

Then with the operator defined above, we can write the

\displaystyle m_{Y^{\alpha}Y_{\alpha}^{\dagger}}=\frac{1}{z^{2}}Y_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}L^{\gamma}_{\ \theta}L^{\theta}_{\ \gamma}\right)Y^{\beta},

(94)

\displaystyle m_{Y^{\alpha}Y_{\beta}^{\dagger}}=\frac{1}{z^{2}}Y_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\left(L^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}+\frac{1}{2}L^{\alpha}_{\ \gamma}L^{\gamma}_{\ \beta}-\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}\right)Y^{\beta}.

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}Y^{\alpha}(\nwarrow)-\frac{1}{z^{2}}\left(-\frac{3}{4}\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}L^{\gamma}_{\ \theta}L^{\theta}_{\ \gamma}+L^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}+\frac{1}{2}L^{\alpha}_{\ \gamma}L^{\gamma}_{\ \beta}\right)Y^{\beta}(\nwarrow)

(107)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2z^{2}}L^{\alpha}_{\ \gamma}\left(y^{\gamma}Y_{\beta}^{\dagger}(\nwarrow)y^{\beta}-y^{\beta}Y_{\beta}^{\dagger}(\nwarrow)y^{\gamma}\right)-\frac{1}{z^{2}}L^{\beta}_{\ \gamma}\left(y^{\alpha}Y_{\beta}^{\dagger}(\nwarrow)y^{\gamma}-y^{\gamma}Y_{\beta}^{\dagger}(\nwarrow)y^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle-i\left(\frac{1}{z^{3/2}}\delta^{z}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{z^{1/2}}\partial_{\mu}\right)\left(A^{\mu}(\nwarrow)y^{\alpha}-y^{\alpha}\hat{A}^{\mu}(\nwarrow)\right)=0,

where we have used eqs. (
94

Y^{\alpha}(\nwarrow)=\sqrt{z}W^{\alpha}(\nwarrow)

\displaystyle\left[-\hat{B}\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}+\left(\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}L^{\gamma}_{\ \theta}L^{\theta}_{\ \gamma}-L^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}-L^{\alpha}_{\ \gamma}L^{\gamma}_{\ \beta}\right)\right]W^{\beta}(\nwarrow)

(108)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}L^{\alpha}_{\ \gamma}\bigg(-L^{\gamma}_{\ \beta}W^{\beta}(\nwarrow)+V^{\gamma}(\nwarrow)\bigg)

\displaystyle-L^{\beta}_{\ \gamma}\left(y^{\alpha}W_{\beta}^{\dagger}(\nwarrow)y^{\gamma}-y^{\gamma}W_{\beta}^{\dagger}(\nwarrow)y^{\alpha}\right)-i\left(\delta^{z}_{\mu}-z\partial_{\mu}\right)B^{\mu,\alpha}(\nwarrow)=0.

L^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}

\left(L^{\beta}_{\ \gamma}\left(y^{\alpha}W_{\beta}^{\dagger}y^{\gamma}-y^{\gamma}W_{\beta}^{\dagger}y^{\alpha}\right)\right)_{\pm\ell,m}=(\mp\ell+1)V_{\pm\ell,m},

Knowing the spectrum of the quantum fields the perturbative program can easily be set up. For the excitations whose
spectrum was found by direct diagonalization (i.e. fermions and easy bosons) we can immediately read off the propagators from
eq. (
29
), and for the excitations whose masses were found by solution of the appropriate equations of motion we can obtain the
propagators by pasting together solutions with the appropriate boundary conditions and normalization which again will lead to
an expression of the same type as (the last line in) eq. (

).

31

33

11

17
,
25
]

bootstrap program to the 1/2 BPS domain wall in ABJM theory.

Finally, let us mention that the techniques developed for the computation of one-point functions in the domain wall version of ABJM theory work equally well for the computation of one-point functions of mass deformed ABJM theory. This holds both for the integrability based methods applied at tree level in
[
31
,
33
]
and the perturbative approach followed in the present paper for higher loop computations.

Appendix A
Modified fuzzy spherical harmonics

In this section we explicitly construct the modified fuzzy spherical harmonics which furnish the
\pi_{q-1}\otimes\pi_{q}
representation of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
, and then generalize it to a spin
1/2
version which forms the
\pi_{2}\otimes\pi_{q-1}\otimes\pi_{q}
representation. We start by recalling a few basic facts about

\mathfrak{su}(2)
representation theory.

A.1
Elements of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
representation theory

Letting
\pi_{p}
denote the
p
-dimensional irreducible representation of su(2), we have the following decomposition formula

\pi_{p}\otimes\pi_{q}\cong\pi_{p+q-1}\oplus\pi_{p+q-3}\oplus\cdots\oplus\pi_{|p-q|+1},

(115)

which implies that the Casmir of
\pi_{p}\otimes\pi_{q}
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{2}^{p\otimes q}
, can be diagonalized as

\mathcal{C}_{2}^{p\otimes q}=\bigoplus_{r=1}^{q-1}\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2r}=\bigoplus_{r=1}^{q-1}\frac{(2r-1)(2r+1)}{4}\mathbbm{1}_{2r},

(116)

where it is known that

\mathcal{C}_{2}^{p}=\frac{(p+1)(p-1)}{4}.

(117)

Another Casimir-like operator is
\pi_{p}(s_{i})\otimes\pi_{q}(s_{i})
where
s_{i}
denotes
\mathfrak{su}(2)
elements with
i=1,2,3
and the repeated
i
’s are to be summed over. It is diagonalized as

\displaystyle\pi_{p}(s_{i})\otimes\pi_{q}(s_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{C}_{2}^{p\otimes q}-\mathcal{C}_{2}^{p}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{q}-\mathbbm{1}_{p}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{2}^{q}\right)

(118)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{r=1}^{q-1}\left(\frac{(2r-1)(2r+1)}{4}-\frac{(p+1)(p-1)}{4}-\frac{(q+1)(q-1)}{4}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{2r}.

A.2
Modified fuzzy spherical harmonics for
\pi_{q-1}\otimes\pi_{q}

The starting point are the standard fuzzy spherical harmonics
[
53
,
54
]
.
The standard fuzzy sphere harmonics
Y^{m}_{\ell}(\hat{t})
for the
q
-dimensional irrep fulfill

[\hat{t}_{3},Y^{m}_{\ell}(\hat{t})]=mY^{m}_{\ell}(\hat{t}),\quad\ell=0,1,\dots,q-1,

(119)

which motivates us to define
\hat{Y}^{m}_{\ell}=Y^{m}_{\ell}(\hat{t})^{T}
such that

[-\hat{t}_{3}^{T},\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m}]=m\hat{Y}^{m}_{\ell},

(120)

while the action of the ladder operators reads

\displaystyle[-\hat{t}_{+}^{T},\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m}]=\sqrt{(\ell-m)(\ell+m+1)}\hat{Y}^{m+1}_{\ell},

(121)

\displaystyle[-\hat{t}_{-}^{T},\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m}]=\sqrt{(\ell+m)(\ell-m+1)}\hat{Y}^{m-1}_{\ell}.

By the BPS equations, one finds the following formulas for
L_{i}
which are defined by eq. (
37
)

\displaystyle L_{3}y^{1}=-\frac{1}{2}y^{1},\quad L_{+}y^{1}=-y^{2},\quad L_{-}y^{1}=0,

(122)

\displaystyle L_{3}y^{2}=\frac{1}{2}y^{1},\quad L_{+}y^{2}=0,\quad L_{-}y^{2}=-y^{1},

where
L_{\pm}=L_{1}\pm iL_{2}
. Then noticing that (
i=1,2,3,\pm
and
\alpha=1,2
)

L_{i}y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m}=(L_{i}y^{\alpha})\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m}+y^{\alpha}[-\hat{t}_{i}^{T},\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m}],

(123)

one easily finds

\displaystyle L_{3}\,y^{1}\hat{Y}^{m}_{\ell}=(m-\frac{1}{2})y^{1}\hat{Y}^{m}_{\ell},\quad L_{+}y^{1}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell}=-y^{2}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell},\quad L_{-}\,y^{1}\hat{Y}^{-\ell}_{\ell}=0,

(124)

\displaystyle L_{3}\,y^{2}\hat{Y}^{m}_{\ell}=(m+\frac{1}{2})y^{2}\hat{Y}^{m}_{\ell},\quad L_{+}\,y^{2}\hat{Y}^{\ell}_{\ell}=0,\quad L_{-}\,y^{2}\hat{Y}^{-\ell}_{\ell}=-y^{1}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{-\ell},

Now, by writting the Casimir in terms of
L_{3}
and the ladder operators

L_{i}L_{i}=L_{3}^{2}-L_{3}+L_{+}L_{-}=L_{3}^{2}+L_{3}+L_{-}L_{+},

(125)

one further obtains

L_{i}L_{i}\,y^{1}\hat{Y}^{-\ell}_{\ell}=(\ell+\frac{1}{2})(\ell+\frac{3}{2})y^{1}\hat{Y}^{-\ell}_{\ell},\quad L_{i}L_{i}\,y^{2}\hat{Y}^{\ell}_{\ell}=(\ell+\frac{1}{2})(\ell+\frac{3}{2})y^{2}\hat{Y}^{\ell}_{\ell},

(126)

which implies that
y^{1}\hat{Y}^{-\ell}_{\ell}
is the lowest state in the
2(\ell+1)
-dimensional irrep, while
y^{2}\hat{Y}^{\ell}_{\ell}
is the highest state in the
2(\ell+1)
-dimensional irrep. Thus,
y^{2}\hat{Y}^{\ell}_{\ell}
(
y^{1}\hat{Y}^{-\ell}_{\ell}
) with
\ell=0,1,\dots,q-2
include the highest (lowest) states of all the irreps deduced from
\pi_{q}\otimes\pi_{q-1}
, and we can obtain the entire set of states in each irrep by acting on
y^{2}\hat{Y}^{\ell}_{\ell}
(
y^{1}\hat{Y}^{-\ell}_{\ell}
) with
L_{-}
(
L_{+}
). The states constructed in the two ways are respectively given by
(n=0,1,\dots,2\ell+1)

\displaystyle(L_{-})^{n}y^{2}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell}=\sqrt{\frac{(2\ell)!(n-1)!}{(2\ell-(n-1))!}}\left(-ny^{1}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell-(n-1)}+\sqrt{(2\ell-n+1)n}\,y^{2}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell-n}\right),

(127)

\displaystyle(L_{+})^{n}y^{1}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{-\ell}=\sqrt{\frac{(2\ell)!(n-1)!}{(2\ell-(n-1))!}}\left(-ny^{2}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{-\ell+(n-1)}+\sqrt{(2\ell-n+1)n}\,y^{1}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{-\ell+n}\right).

(128)

One easily finds
(L_{-})^{n}y^{2}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell}\propto(L_{+})^{2(\ell+1)-n}y^{1}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{-\ell}
which verifies the consistency of the two ways of generating states. Hence, up to a normalization factor, the states in the spin
\ell+1/2
irrep can be expressed as

T_{\ell+1/2}^{m+1/2}=-\sqrt{l-m}\,y^{1}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m+1}+\sqrt{\ell+m+1}\,y^{2}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m},

(129)

where
\ell=0,1,\dots,q-2
and
m=-\ell-1,-\ell,\dots,\ell
. We also assume
\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{-\ell-1}=\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell+1}=0
. These states carry different eigenvalues of the Casimir and
L_{3}
so they are automatically orthogonal to each other.

A.3
Spin
1/2
modified fuzzy spherical harmonics for
\pi_{2}\otimes\pi_{q-1}\otimes\pi_{q}

As the mixing of the complicated field is governed by the
\mathfrak{su}(2)

representation
\pi_{2}\otimes\pi_{q-1}\otimes\pi_{q}
, which is specified by the operator

(J_{i})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{i})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}+\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}L_{i},

(130)

we also need to construct the states of the irreps deduced from it. Then, one by eq. (
124
) finds

(J_{3})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}\,y^{\beta}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m}=my^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m},\quad(J_{+})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}\,y^{\beta}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell}=(J_{-})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}\,y^{\beta}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{-\ell}=0,

(131)

where

(J_{\pm})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}=(J_{1})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}\pm i(J_{2})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}.

(132)

Hence, again with a rewriting of the Casimir

\displaystyle(J_{i})^{\alpha}_{\ \gamma}(J_{i})^{\gamma}_{\ \beta}

\displaystyle=(J_{3})^{\alpha}_{\ \gamma}(J_{3})^{\gamma}_{\ \beta}-(J_{3})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}+(J_{+})^{\alpha}_{\ \gamma}(J_{-})^{\gamma}_{\ \beta}

(133)

\displaystyle=(J_{3})^{\alpha}_{\ \gamma}(J_{3})^{\gamma}_{\ \beta}+(J_{3})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}+(J_{-})^{\alpha}_{\ \gamma}(J_{+})^{\gamma}_{\ \beta},

one further finds

(J_{i})^{\alpha}_{\ \gamma}(J_{i})^{\gamma}_{\ \beta}\,y^{\beta}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell}=\ell(\ell+1)y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell},\quad(J_{i})^{\alpha}_{\ \gamma}(J_{i})^{\gamma}_{\ \beta}\,y^{\beta}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{-\ell}=\ell(\ell+1)y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{-\ell},

(134)

which implies
y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell}
(
y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{-\ell}
) is the highest (lowest) state in the irrep of spin
\ell
. Meanwhile, one can construct the state
Y_{\ell}^{m}y^{\alpha}
, where
Y_{\ell}^{m}
is the fuzzy sphere harmonics in terms of
t_{i}
which fulfills

[t_{3},Y_{\ell}^{m}]=mY_{\ell}^{m}.

(135)

It is not hard to verify that the above action of
J_{i}
’s also holds for
Y_{\ell}^{m}y^{\alpha}
but with
\ell=0,1,\dots,q-2
(as
t_{i}
is the
(q-1)
-dimensional representation). One also notes that
Y_{\ell}^{m}y^{\alpha}
is linearly independent of
y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m}
, i.e. they are not proportional to each other.

Now, with the representation decomposition

\frac{1}{2}\otimes\frac{q-2}{2}\otimes\frac{q-1}{2}=\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^{q-1}\bm{\ell}\oplus\bm{(\ell-1)},

(136)

where
\bm{\ell}
denotes the spin
\ell
representation of su(2), one finds the highest state of each irrep (note that
Y_{0}^{0}=\mathbbm{1}_{q-1}
)

\bm{\ell}:y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell},\quad\bm{\ell-1}:Y_{\ell-1}^{\ell}y^{\alpha},\quad\ell=1,2,\dots,q-1.

(137)

Then, the entire set of states of each irrep. can again be generated by acting on the highest state with
J_{-}
(or the other way around). A straightforward calculation shows that the descendant states have a simple form

(J_{-})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta_{1}}\cdots(J_{-})^{\beta_{n-2}}_{\ \beta_{n-1}}(J_{-})^{\beta_{n-1}}_{\ \beta_{n}}y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell}\propto y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{\ell-n},

(138)

(J_{-})^{\alpha}_{\ \beta_{1}}\cdots(J_{-})^{\beta_{n-2}}_{\ \beta_{n-1}}(J_{-})^{\beta_{n-1}}_{\ \beta_{n}}Y_{\ell}^{\ell}y^{\alpha}\propto Y_{\ell}^{\ell-n}y^{\alpha}.

(139)

Therefore, the entire set of states of each irreducible representation is given by

\displaystyle\bm{\ell}:y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m},\quad m=-\ell,-\ell+1,\dots,\ell,

(140)

\displaystyle\bm{\ell-1}:Y_{\ell-1}^{m}y^{\alpha},\quad m=-\ell+1,-\ell+2,\dots,\ell-1.

Again, these states are not yet normalized, and
y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m},Y_{\ell}^{m}y^{\alpha}
with
\ell=1,\dots,q-2
are not even automatically orthogonal to each other since they carry the same eigenvalues of the Casimir and
J_{3}
. Thus, the orthonormalization of these states should be done by the usual Gram-Schmidt process on each pair of
y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}_{\ell}^{m},Y_{\ell}^{m}y^{\alpha}
and then by the normalization on
y^{\alpha}
as well as
y^{\alpha}\hat{Y}^{m}_{q-1}
.

Appendix B
Solutions of a class of massive Chern-Simons equation of motion

We here consider a more generic EOM of a 3d massive Chern-Simons theory, which is useful for the diagonalization of several
of our blocks. Suppose we have the EOM

\left(c\epsilon_{\mu\rho\nu}\partial^{\rho}-\frac{d}{z}\eta_{\mu\nu}\right)A^{\nu}-i\left(\frac{1}{z^{1/2}}\partial_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2z^{3/2}}\delta^{z}_{\mu}\right)Y=0,

(141)

where
\mu=0,1,z
. Going to momentum space in the longitudinal directions
a=0,1
by introducing momenta
k^{0},k^{1}

a possible solutions reads (with
ik=\sqrt{-k_{a}k^{a}}
)

\displaystyle A^{a}=-\frac{1}{2k}\epsilon^{ab}k_{b}fK_{\frac{d}{c}}(ikz),

(142)

\displaystyle A^{z}=\frac{1}{4}f\left(K_{\frac{d}{c}-1}(ikz)-K_{\frac{d}{c}+1}(ikz)\right),

\displaystyle Y=\frac{c\sqrt{z}}{4i}f\left(K_{\frac{d}{c}-1}(ikz)+K_{\frac{d}{c}+1}(ikz)\right),

where
f\propto e^{-ik_{a}x^{a}}
should be determined by normalization conditions and
K_{\nu}(x)
is the modified Bessel function.
One can easily verify this solution by making us of
the recurrence relation of the modified Bessel function

x\partial_{x}K_{\nu}(x)=\pm\nu K_{\nu}(x)-xK_{\nu\pm 1}(x),

(143)

x\partial_{x}K_{\nu\pm 1}(x)=\mp(\nu\pm 1)K_{\nu\pm 1}(x)-xK_{\nu}(x).

(144)

There exists (as usual) also a solution given in terms of modified Bessel functions of type
I_{\nu}(x)
which we give below. For the purpose of
determining the spectrum we only need to worry about about one of these solutions as we can read off the mass parameter from the index of either

In order to identify the quantum fluctuations with a specific value of
\nu
we organize
A^{z},Y
into linear combinations given in terms of a single Bessel function

X^{+}=fK_{\frac{d}{c}-1}(ikz),\quad X^{-}=-fK_{\frac{d}{c}+1}(ikz),

(145)

where

X^{\pm}=2A^{z}\pm\frac{2i}{c\sqrt{z}}Y.

(146)

One has another set of independent solutions also expressed entirely in terms of
K_{\nu}
’s.

\displaystyle A^{a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{k}\eta^{ab}k_{b}fK_{\frac{d}{c}}(ikz),

(147)

\displaystyle A^{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}f(K_{\frac{d}{c}-1}(ikz)+K_{\frac{d}{c}+1}(ikz)),

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\frac{c\sqrt{z}}{4i}f(K_{\frac{d}{c}-1}(ikz)-K_{\frac{d}{c}+1}(ikz)).

We eliminate these by imposing the gauge fixing condition

\partial_{a}A^{a}=0.

(148)

For completeness, let us also give the solution of eq. (
141

I_{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle A^{a}=\frac{1}{2k}\epsilon^{ab}k_{b}g(I_{\frac{d}{c}}(ikz)),

(149)

\displaystyle A^{z}=\frac{1}{4}g\left(I_{\frac{d}{c}-1}(ikz)-I_{\frac{d}{c}+1}(ikz)\right),

\displaystyle Y=\frac{c\sqrt{z}}{4i}g\left(I_{\frac{d}{c}-1}(ikz)+I_{\frac{d}{c}+1}(ikz)\right),

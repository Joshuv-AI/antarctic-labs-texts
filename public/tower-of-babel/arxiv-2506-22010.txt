Abstract
We investigate the problem of constructing fault-tolerant bases in matroids. Given a matroid ℳ \mathcal{M} and a redundancy parameter k k , a k k -fault-tolerant basis is a minimum-size set of elements such that, even after the removal of any k k elements, the remaining subset still spans the entire ground set. Since matroids generalize linear independence across structures such as vector spaces, graphs, and set systems, this problem unifies and extends several fault-tolerant concepts appearing in prior research. Our main contribution is a fixed-parameter tractable ( FPT \operatorClassFPT ) algorithm for the k k -fault-tolerant basis problem, parameterized by both k k and the rank r r of the matroid. This two-variable parameterization by k + r k+r is shown to be tight in the following sense. On the one hand, the problem is already NP \operatorClassNP -hard for k = 1 k=1 . On the other hand, it is Para − NP \operatorClassParaNP -hard for r ≥ 3 r\geq 3 and polynomial-time solvable for r ≤ 2 r\leq 2 .



1
Introduction

A basis in a
d
-dimensional vector space
\mathcal{V}
is a set of exactly
d
linearly independent vectors
\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{d}\}
. Any vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{V}

has a
unique
representation

\mathbf{x}\;=\;\alpha_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}\;+\;\cdots\;+\;\alpha_{d}\mathbf{v}_{d}.

However, if certain basis vectors are lost or corrupted—for instance, in a distributed storage system where vectors are stored across multiple servers that can malfunction—it may be challenging or impossible to accurately recover
\mathbf{x}
.
The algorithmic question we address in this paper is:

For an expected number
k
of failures, what is the minimum-size
k
-fault-tolerant set of vectors that still guarantees data reconstructability?

A natural way to capture the notions of basis and independence is through
matroids
, which generalize linear independence to a variety of combinatorial settings (like graphs, set systems, vector spaces).
Let
\mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{I})
be a matroid, and let
k
be a nonnegative integer. We say that
B\subseteq E
is a
k
-fault-tolerant basis
of
\mathcal{M}
if
B
is a minimum-size set such that, for every subset
F\subseteq B
of size at most
k
, we have
\mathsf{rank}(B\setminus F)=\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})
. (All necessary matroid definitions appear in the Preliminaries section.)
Equivalently, this condition means that
{\mathsf{cl}(B\setminus F)=E}
, where
\mathsf{cl}(\cdot)
denotes matroid closure. Note that this concept generalizes the standard basis definition: a
0
-fault-tolerant basis is simply a basis of
\mathcal{M}
. However, while every matroid has a basis, it may not admit a
k
-fault-tolerant basis for
k\geq 1
.
We investigate the following problem.

Input:

A matroid
\mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{I})
and nonnegative integer
k
.

Task:

Find a
k
-fault-tolerant basis
B\subseteq E
or correctly decide that none exists.

Fault-Tolerant Basis

Let us give some examples of
Fault-Tolerant Basis
.

Linear Matroids.

A
linear matroid
over a field
\mathbb{F}
can be represented by a set of vectors
\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}\}
in a vector space
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{d}
. The rank of the matroid corresponds to
\dim(\mathcal{V})
.
A basis here is simply a set of
d
linearly independent vectors.
A set of vectors
\{\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{r}\}
is
k
-fault-tolerant if, after removing up to
k
vectors, the remaining set still spans the entire subspace
\mathcal{V}
. Such concepts naturally arise in distributed storage—where vectors might be stored
on different servers
[
10
]
—and in coding theory, where losing some symbols or vectors
should not destroy the ability to recover the entire space
[
12
]
.

Graphic Matroids.

For a connected graph
G
, its
graphic matroid

\mathcal{M}(G)
has ground set
E(G)
, and a set
I\subseteq E(G)
is independent if it forms a forest. A basis is thus a spanning tree of
G
.
In this matroid, a set of edges
B\subseteq E(G)
is
k
-fault-tolerant if, upon removing any
k
edges, the subgraph remains connected. Thus, a
k
-fault-tolerant basis is a
(k+1)
-edge connected spanning subgraph with minimum number of edges. This problem is known in the literature as
k
-Edge-Connected Spanning Subgraph

[
7
,
13
,
16
]
.
It is motivated by network applications as its task is to compute a minimum-cost sub-network that is resilient against up to
k
link failures (see also
[
4
]
).

Gammoids.

A
gammoid
is derived from a directed (or undirected) graph
D
.
Let
X
and
Y
be two distinguished sets of vertices of
V(D)
.
Within the set
X
, define a subset
U\subseteq X
to be
independent
if there exist
|U|
vertex-disjoint paths in
G
originating from vertices in
Y
and ending in the vertices of
U
. The basis of the gammoid is the maximum set of vertices from
X
that can be reached by vertex-disjoint paths from
Y
. In this case, a vertex set
Z\subseteq X
is a

k
-fault-tolerant basis if upon removing any
k
vertices from
Z
, the remaining vertices are still linkable from sources in
Y
without losing rank. This problem is related to the problem of finding fault-tolerant disjoint paths studied by Adjiashvili et al.
[
1
]
.

Transversal Matroids.

A
transversal matroid

\mathcal{M}(U,\mathcal{S})
is described via a bipartite graph between a ground set
U
and a set of “target positions”
\mathcal{S}
. A set
I\subseteq U
is independent if it can be matched injectively to distinct elements in
\mathcal{S}
.
The basis of the transversal matroid is a maximum-size subset
I\subseteq U
that can be perfectly matched into
\mathcal{S}
.
Finding a
k
-fault-tolerant basis for a transversal matroids is naturally related to robust matching problems, where one wants to preserve a perfect (or maximum) matching under the loss of a few vertices or edges
[
11
,
17
]
.

1.1
Our Contribution

We investigate the parameterized complexity of
Fault-Tolerant Basis
under natural parameterizations by
k
and the rank
r
of the input matroid. (We refer to the textbook by Cygan et al.
[
9
]
for an introduction to parameterized complexity.) Our main result is that the problem is
\operatorClassFPT
when parameterized by both
k
and
r
.

Theorem 1
.

Fault-Tolerant Basis
can be solved in
(kr)^{\mathcal{O}(kr^{4})}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time on
n
-element matroids of rank at most
r
given by independence oracles.

To prove
Theorem

1
, we design an algorithm that recursively decomposes the input matroid and identifies a small
core set
containing any possible
k
-fault-tolerant basis, thereby restricting the problem to a bounded search space. More precisely, the algorithm locates a set
W
of
important elements
with bounded size such that, if the input matroid
\mathcal{M}
admits a
k
-fault-tolerant basis, then there is one contained entirely in
W
.
The construction of
W
is guided by the observation (see
2
) that if
X
is a rank-
r
set with
r+k
elements such that
every

r
-element subset of
X
is independent, then
X
is already a
k
-fault-tolerant basis. Hence, finding such an
X
would immediately solve the problem. Otherwise, we identify an inclusion-maximal rank-
r
set
X
(of bounded size) such that for every
r
-element subset
Y\subseteq X
, the rank of
Y
is
r
.
The crucial insight here is that the closure
\mathsf{cl}(X)
of
X
can be expressed as the union of
\mathsf{cl}(S)
over all
(r-1)
-element subsets
S
of
X
. We then proceed recursively, selecting elements of
W
by searching for analogous sets in the closures of each
S
, applying the same approach used to choose
X
.
Once we have computed
W
, we determine whether a
k
-fault-tolerant basis exists within
W
by enumerating all candidate subsets via a brute-force algorithm. If such a basis is found, it is also a valid solution for the original matroid.

We then show that the result of
Theorem

1
is tight. First, by adapting the results of Fomin et al.
[
14
]
for our purposes, we observe the following lower bound.

Proposition 1
.

It is
\operatorClassW[1]
-hard for the parameterization by
k
to decide whether a given linear matroid has a
k
-fault-tolerant basis.

For the parameterization of
Fault-Tolerant Basis
by
k
, recall that
Fault-Tolerant Basis
for graphic matroids is equivalent to finding a
(k+1)
-edge connected spanning subgraph with the minimum number of edges. As was shown by Fernandes
[
13
]
, an
n
-vertex graph
G
has a 2-edge connected spanning subgraph with at most
n
edges if and only if
G
has a Hamiltonian cycle. Thus,
Fault-Tolerant Basis
is intractable already for
k=1
. Taking into account the inapproximability lower bounds for higher connectivities established by Gabow et al.
[
16
]
, we obtain the following observation.

Observation 1
.

For every integer
k\geq 1
,
it is
\operatorClassNP
-hard to decide whether a graphic matroid
\mathcal{M}
given with an integer
b\geq 1
has a
k
-fault-tolerant basis of size at most
b
.

For the parameterization by the rank, we establish a dichotomy—for any fixed
r\geq 3
,
Fault-Tolerant Basis
is intractable, but for
r\leq 2
, the problem can be solved in polynomial time. In fact, for
r\leq 2
, we solve
the more general weighted variant of
Fault-Tolerant Basis
. In
Weighted Fault-Tolerant Basis
, we are given a matroid
\mathcal{M}
together with a weight function
w\colon E(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and the task is to find a set
B\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
of minimum total weight such that for any set
F\subseteq B
of size at most
k
,
\mathsf{rank}(B\setminus F)=\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})
. Our result is summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For any integer
r\geq 3
, it is
\operatorClassNP
-hard to decide whether a linear matroid
\mathcal{M}
of rank
r
over rationals given together with integers
k
and
b
has a
k
-fault-tolerant basis of size at most
b
. For

r\leq 2
,
Weighted Fault-Tolerant Basis
can be solved in
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
on matroids
given by an independence oracle.

In particular, one can find a
k
-fault-tolerant basis of vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in polynomial time but the problem becomes
\operatorClassNP
-hard in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
To obtain the hardness for
r\geq 3
, we use the result of Froese et al.
[
15
]
stating that, given a set
P
of points on the plane and a positive integer
k
, it is NP-hard to decide whether
P
contains at least
k
points in general position. To establish the claim for
r\leq 2
, it is convenient to show
a more general result for partition matroids which may be of independent interest.

Proposition 2
.

Given an
n
-element partition matroid
\mathcal{M}
with unit capacities together with a weight function
w\colon E(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and integers
r,k\geq 0
,
one can find in
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
time either a set
X\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
of minimum weight
such that, for any set
F\subseteq X
of size at most
k
, it holds that
\mathsf{rank}(X\setminus F)\geq r
or correctly decide that such a set does not exist.

1.2
Related Work

Adjiashvili, Stiller, and Zenklusen
[
2
]
introduced a

Bulk-Robustness
model of combinatorial optimization. They studied several
instances of this framework, including the
Bulk-Robust Minimum Matroid Basis

problem, which is most relevant to our work.
In this setting, for a matroid
\mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{I})
, we are given a collection of

interdiction sets

\Omega\;=\;\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{m}\},

where
F_{i}\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. The goal is to find a cheapest subset
X\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})

such that
X\setminus F_{i}
contains a basis of
\mathcal{M}
for every

i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.
Adjiashvili, Stiller, and Zenklusen provided an
\mathcal{O}(\log m+\log r)
-approximation
algorithm for
Bulk-Robust Minimum Matroid Basis
, where
r
is the rank of the matroid.
The problem
Fault-Tolerant Basis
can be viewed as a special case of
Bulk-Robust Minimum Matroid Basis

when
\Omega
consists of all subsets of
E
of size
k
.

There are several prior works investigating fault-tolerance for classic optimization problems.
The model of
s
-
t
-Path
and
s
-
t
-Flow
problems with safe and vulnerable edges was introduced by Adjiashvili et al.
[
1
]
, who studied the approximability of these problems. Subsequently, generalizations were also studied
[
3
,
6
,
8
]
. Bentert et al.
[
4
]
studied the parameterized complexity of computing a fault-tolerant spanning tree. Approximation algorithms and inapproximability lower bounds for

k
-Edge-Connected Spanning Subgraph
have been considered in
[
7
,
13
,
16
,
18
]
.

More generally,
Fault-Tolerant Basis
belongs to
robust optimization
, a branch of optimization
that adapts classic optimization-theoretic tools to settings with uncertainty.
For a comprehensive introduction, see the survey by Bertsimas et al.
[
5
]
.

2
Preliminaries

We refer to the book of Oxley
[
20
]
for a detailed
introduction to matroids. A pair
\mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{I})
, where
E
is a finite

ground
set and
\mathcal{I}
is a family of subsets of the ground set,
called
independent sets
of
\mathcal{M}
, is a
matroid
if it satisfies the
following conditions, called
independence axioms
:

(I1)

\emptyset\in\mathcal{I}
.

(I2)

If
A\subseteq B\subseteq E
and
B\in\mathcal{I}
then
A\in\mathcal{I}
.

(I3)

If
A,B\in\mathcal{I}
and
|A|<|B|
, then there is
e\in B\setminus A
such that
A\cup\{e\}\in\mathcal{I}
.

We use
E(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M})
to denote the ground set and the set of
independent sets, respectively.
An inclusion-maximal independent set
B
is called a

basis
of
\mathcal{M}
. We use
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M})
to denote the set of bases of
\mathcal{M}
.
All the bases of
\mathcal{M}
have the same size called the
rank
of
\mathcal{M}
and
denoted by
\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})
. The
rank
of a subset
A\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
, denoted by
\mathsf{rank}(A)
, is the maximum size of an independent set
X\subseteq A
; the
function
\mathsf{rank}\colon 2^{E(\mathcal{M})}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is the
rank

function of
M
. A set
A\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})

spans
an element
x\in E(\mathcal{M})
if

\mathsf{rank}(A\cup\{x\})=\mathsf{rank}(A)
. The
closure
(or
span
) of
A
is the
set
\mathsf{cl}(A)=\{x\in E(M)\mid A\text{ spans }x\}
. Closures satisfy the following
properties, called
closure axioms
:

(CL1)

For every
A\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
,
A\subseteq\mathsf{cl}(A)
.

(CL2)

If
A\subseteq B\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
, then
\mathsf{cl}(A)\subseteq\mathsf{cl}(B)
.

(CL3)

For every
A\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
,
\mathsf{cl}(A)=\mathsf{cl}(\mathsf{cl}(A))
.

(CL4)

For every
A\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
, every
x\in E(\mathcal{M})\setminus A
, and every
y\in\mathsf{cl}(A\cup\{x\})\setminus\mathsf{cl}(A)
,
x\in\mathsf{cl}(A\cup\{y\})
.

For
S\subseteq E(M)
, the matroid obtained by
deleting

S
, denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}-S
, is the matroid such that

E(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})=E(\mathcal{M})\setminus S
and
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})=\{X\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M})\mid S\cap X=\emptyset\}
.
Let
r\geq 0
be an integer. The
r
-truncation
of a matroid
\mathcal{M}
is the matroid
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
with
E(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})=E(\mathcal{M})
such that
X\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})
if and only if
X\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M})
and
|X|\leq r
.
A matroid
\mathcal{M}
is a
partition
matroid if there is a partition
(P_{1},\ldots,P_{d})
of the ground set and a
d
-tuple
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})
of positive integers, called capacities, such that
a set
X\subseteq E(G)
is independent if and only if
|X\cap P_{i}|\leq c_{i}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
A
d\times n
-matrix

\mathbf{A}
over a field
\mathbb{F}
is a
representation of a matroid
\mathcal{M}
over
\mathbb{F}
if
there is a one-to-one correspondence
f
between
E(\mathcal{M})
and the columns of
\mathbf{A}
such that for any
X\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
, it holds that
X\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M})
if and only if
the columns in
\{f(x)\mid x\in X\}
are linearly independent (as vectors of
\mathbb{F}^{d}
). If

\mathcal{M}
has such a representation, then
\mathcal{M}
has a

representation over
\mathbb{F}
. A matroid
\mathcal{M}
admitting a representation over
\mathbb{F}
is said to be
linear
; a matroid is
binary
if it has a representation over
{\sf GF}[2]
.

In our algorithms for general matroids, we assume
that input matroids are given by
independence oracles
. Such an oracle for a matroid
\mathcal{M}
, takes as input a set
X\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
and correctly answers in a constant time whether
X\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M})
. We note that some matroids could be given explicitly, for example, by their representations.

3
Basic Observations

In this section, we prove a couple of simple results that will be helpful in the later discussion.
Recall that for an integer
k\geq 0
,
B\subseteq E(M)
is a
k
-fault-tolerant basis of a matroid
\mathcal{M}
if
B
is a subset of minimum cardinality such that
\mathsf{rank}(B\setminus F)=\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})
for every subset
F\subseteq B
of size at most
k
. We use the following bounds on the size of a
k
-fault-tolerant basis.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a matroid with
\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})\geq 1
and let
k\geq 0
be an integer. Then for a
k
-fault-tolerant basis
B
of
\mathcal{M}
, it holds that

\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})+k\leq|B|\leq(k+1)\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M}).

Proof.

The lower bound immediately follows from the definition of a
k
-fault-tolerant basis.
To show the upper bound, assume for the sake of contradiction that
|B|>(k+1)\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})
.
We iteratively construct sets
X_{0},\ldots,X_{k}
where
X_{0}\subseteq B
is an inclusion-maximal independent set in
B
, and for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
X_{i}
is an inclusion-maximal independent set in
{B\setminus\big(\bigcup_{j=0}^{i-1}X_{j}\big)}
.
As
\mathsf{rank}(B)=\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})
and
|B|>(k+1)\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})
, such sets
X_{0},\ldots,X_{k}
exist.
Set
{B^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{k}X_{i}}
.
Consider an arbitrary
F\subseteq B
of size at most
k
.
By the pigeonhole principle, there is an index
{i\in\{0,\ldots,k\}}
such that
X_{i}\cap F=\emptyset
. By construction,
B\setminus B^{\prime}\subseteq B\setminus\big(\bigcup_{j=0}^{i-1}X_{j}\big)\subseteq\mathsf{cl}(X_{i})
.
Hence,
B\setminus F\subseteq(B^{\prime}\setminus F)\cup(B\setminus B^{\prime})\subseteq\mathsf{cl}(B^{\prime}\setminus F)
. Because
B
is a
k
-fault-tolerant basis,
\mathsf{cl}(B\setminus F)=E(\mathcal{M})
. Thus,

E(\mathcal{M})=\mathsf{cl}(B\setminus F)\subseteq\mathsf{cl}(\mathsf{cl}(B^{\prime}\setminus F))=\mathsf{cl}(B^{\prime}\setminus F)
.
Since
F
was chosen arbitrarily, we obtain that
B^{\prime}
is a
k
-fault-tolerant basis of
\mathcal{M}
.
However,
|B^{\prime}|<|B|
contradicting the choice of
B
.
∎

We next argue that the bounds in
Proposition

3
are tight.
First, note that the lower bound is tight as the matroid
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{M})=\{X\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})\colon|X|\leq k\}
proves.
We next argue that the upper bound is also tight.
Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}
be a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{r}
. Let
k
and
n
be integers such that
0\leq k<n
. Consider the linear matroid
\mathcal{M}
with
{E(\mathcal{M})=\{je_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq r\text{ and }1\leq j\leq n\}}
. It is easy to see that any
k
-fault-tolerant basis of
\mathcal{M}
has to contain at least
k+1
vectors of
\{je_{i}\mid 1\leq j\leq n\}
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
. Thus, the size of a
k
-fault-tolerant basis is at least
(k+1)r
.
From the other side, we have that, for
B=\{je_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq r\text{ and }1\leq j\leq k+1\}
,

\mathsf{rank}(B\setminus F)=r
for any
F\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
of size at most
k
. Thus,
B
is
a
k
-fault-tolerant basis of size
(k+1)r
of
\mathcal{M}
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a matroid.
For a positive integer
h
, we say that a set
X\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
is
h
-uniform
if
\mathsf{rank}(X)=h
and
\mathsf{rank}(Y)=h
for every subset
Y\subseteq X
of size
h
. The definition and
Proposition

3
yield the following.

Observation 2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a matroid of rank
r\geq 1
,
k\geq 0
be an integer, and
B\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
be a set of size
k+r
. Then,
B
is a
k
-fault-tolerant basis of
\mathcal{M}
if and only if
B
is
r
-uniform.

Proof.

If
B
is a
k
-fault-tolerant basis, then
{\mathsf{rank}(B\setminus F)=r}
holds for every set
F\subseteq B
of size at most
k
.
Since
|B|=k+r
, this implies that, for every set
X\subseteq B
of size
r
,
{\mathsf{rank}(X)=\mathsf{rank}(B)=r}
, that is,
B
is
r
-uniform.
For the opposite direction, if
B
is
r
-uniform, then, for any
X\subseteq B
of size
r
,
\mathsf{rank}(X)=\mathsf{rank}(B)=\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})
.
Since
|B|=k+r
, it holds for any
F\subseteq B
of size at most
k
that
\mathsf{rank}(B\setminus F)=\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})
. This completes the proof.
∎

We conclude this section by proving
Proposition

1
using the results of Fomin et al.
[
14
]
. In particular, they studied
Rank
h
-Reduction
.
Here, the input is a binary matroid
\mathcal{M}
given by its representation over
{\sf GF}[2]
and two positive integers
h
and
k
.
The task is to decide whether there is a set
X\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
of size at most

We apply the algorithm from

of size at most

has a

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a partition matroid with unit capacities for a partition
(P_{1},\ldots,P_{d})
of the ground set.
Let
X\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
and let
k\geq 0
and

be integers. Then, the following properties hold.

(i)

If there is an integer
s\geq 1
such that
|X|=s(r-1)+k+1
and
|X\cap P_{i}|\leq s
for every

, then
for any set
F\subseteq X
of size at most
k
,
\mathsf{rank}(X\setminus F)\geq r
.

(ii)

If
X
is an inclusion-minimal set
such that
{\mathsf{rank}(X\setminus F)\geq r}
for any set
F\subseteq X
of size at most
k
,
then there is an integer
s\geq 1
such that
|X|=s(r-1)+k+1
and
|X\cap P_{i}|\leq s
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

Proof.

To show (i),
suppose that
|X|=s(r-1)+k+1
and
|X\cap P_{i}|\leq s
for each
{i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}}
for some integer
s\geq 1
.
Consider any subset
F\subseteq X
of size at most
k
. Since
{|X\cap P_{i}|\leq s}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, it holds that
|(X\setminus F)\cap P_{i}|\leq s
and hence
|(X\setminus F)\cap P_{i}|>0
for at least
\Big\lceil\frac{|X\setminus F|}{s}\Big\rceil
sets
P_{i}
.
Since
\mathcal{M}
is a partition matroid with unit capacities, this is also a lower bound on
\mathsf{rank}(X\setminus F)
and therefore

\mathsf{rank}(X\setminus F)\geq\Big\lceil\frac{|X\setminus F|}{s}\Big\rceil\geq\Big\lceil\frac{s(r-1)+1}{s}\Big\rceil\geq r.

This proves that
\mathsf{rank}(X)\geq r
and
\mathsf{rank}(X\setminus F)\geq r
for any set
F\subseteq X
of size at most
k
.

To prove (ii), suppose that
X
is an inclusion-minimal set with
\mathsf{rank}(X)\geq r
such that for any set
F\subseteq X
of size at most
k
,

holds. We assume without loss of generality that
|X\cap P_{1}|\geq\dots\geq|X\cap P_{d}|
.

If
r=1
, then we set
s=\max\{|X\cap P_{i}|\mid 1\leq i\leq d\}
. Trivially,
|X|\geq k+1
and
|X\cap P_{i}|\leq s
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
We next show that
|X|=k+1
. Assume towards a contradiction that
|X|>k+1
.
Any subset

of size
k+1
satisfies
|X^{\prime}\cap P_{i}|\leq s
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
Hence,
\mathsf{rank}(X^{\prime}\setminus F)>1
for any set
F
of size at most
k
by (i). This contradicts the the minimality of
X
and shows
|X|=k+1
.

If
r\geq 2
, then
|X\cap P_{r-1}|\geq 1

and we set
s=|X\cap P_{r-1}|
. We show
{|X|=s(r-1)+k+1}
and
|X\cap P_{i}|\leq s
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
next.
Let
{Y=\bigcup_{j=1}^{r-1}(X\cap P_{j})}
and
Z=\bigcup_{j=r}^{d}(X\cap P_{j})
.
By definition of

,
|Y|\geq s(r-1)
.

If
|Z|\leq k
, then
{\mathsf{rank}(X\setminus Z)=r-1<r}
,
contradicting the choice of
X
.
Thus,
|Z|\geq k+1
and

|X|=|Y|+|Z|\geq s(r-1)+k+1
.
There exist
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
such that

|Y^{\prime}\cap P_{j}|=s
for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
, and
Z^{\prime}\subseteq Z
such that
|Z^{\prime}|=k+1
. Consider
X^{\prime}=Y^{\prime}\cup Z^{\prime}
. By definition,

|X^{\prime}\cap P_{i}|\leq s
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Then, by (i), we have that
\mathsf{rank}(X^{\prime})\geq r
and for any set
F\subseteq X^{\prime}
of size at most
k
,
\mathsf{rank}(X^{\prime}\setminus F)\geq r
. By the minimality of
X
, we conclude that
X=X^{\prime}
. This proves (ii).
∎

Consider a weighted partition matroid

.

Weighted Fault-Tolerant Basis
can be solved in
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
on matroids of rank at most two given by an independence oracle.

Proof.

Let

be an instance of

with
\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})\leq 2
. If
\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})=0

B=\emptyset
is a

\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})\geq 1
.
Furthermore, we assume that
\mathcal{M}
has no loops because elements

such that

are not included in any

for any

Suppose that

. Then,

and we can choose such a set of minimum weight greedily.

We assume from now on that
\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})=2

and

are

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we initiate the study of the parameterized complexity of
Fault-Tolerant Basis
. Our main result is that the problem is fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized by both
k
and rank
r
of the input matroid. This positive algorithmic result is complemented by computational lower bounds showing
\operatorClassNP
-hardness for constant values for any one of the two parameters alone.
Our results lead to several open questions.

First, we do not know whether our
\operatorClassFPT
result from
Theorem

1
could be extended for
Weighted Fault-Tolerant Basis
. Our approach based on the choice of
h
-uniform sets is tailored for the unweighted case, and one may need different techniques in the presence of weights.

Second, is it possible to extend our result to the non-uniform model introduced by Adjiashvili et al.
[
1
]
? Here, we assume that the set of elements of a matroid
\mathcal{M}
is partitioned into two subsets
S
and
V
of
safe
and
vulnerable
elements, respectively. Then, the task is to either find a set
B\subseteq E(\mathcal{M})
of minimum size such that for any sets
F\subseteq V\cap B
of size at most
k
,
\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M}-F)=\mathsf{rank}(\mathcal{M})
or correctly report that such a set does not exist.
Fault-Tolerant Basis
is the special case of this problem with
S=\emptyset
.

Third, can our results be extended to the model introduced by Adjiashvili et al.
[
2
]
where not arbitrary sets of
k
elements can fail but possible failure scenarios are part of the input?

Finally, observe that our computational lower bounds from
Proposition

1
,
1
, and
Theorem

2
do not exclude efficient algorithms for
Fault-Tolerant Basis
on special classes of matroids. In particular, we proved in
Proposition

2
that
Weighted Fault-Tolerant Basis
can be solved in polynomial time on truncations of partition matroids with unit capacities.
While it is straightforward to see that
Weighted Fault-Tolerant Basis
can be solved in polynomial time for partition matroids with arbitrary capacities,

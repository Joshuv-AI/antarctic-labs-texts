Gaussian fluctuations for stochastic Volterra equations with small noise
Nguyen Tien Dung, Nguyen Thu Hang

Abstract
In this paper, we consider a general class of stochastic Volterra equations with small noise. Our aim is to study the fluctuation of the solution around its deterministic limit. We use the techniques of Malliavin calculus to show that the fluctuation process satisfies central limit theorem and provide an optimal estimate for the rate of convergence. An application to stochastic Volterra equations with fractional Brownian motion kernel is given to illustrate the theory.



Keywords:
Central limit theorem, Stochastic Volterra equations, Malliavin calculus.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
60F05, 60H07, 60H20.

1
Introduction

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we consider stochastic Volterra equations of the form

X_{\varepsilon,t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X_{\varepsilon,s})ds+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X_{\varepsilon,s})dB_{s},\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T,

(1.1)

where the initial data
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\mbox{ }(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a standard Brownian motion and
b,\sigma
are measurable functions on
[0,T]^{2}\times\mathbb{R}.

It is well known that the class of stochastic Volterra equations was first studied by Berger and Mizel in
[
1
]
. Since then this class has been widely used in the modelling of numerous random phenomena (fluid turbulence
[
4
]
, DNA patterns
[
14
]
and mathematical finance
[
11
]
, etc). For small values
\varepsilon,
the equation (
1.1
) forms a dynamical system with small noise. In this context, the asymptotic behavior of
X_{\varepsilon,t}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
has been investigated by various authors. However, most of papers are devoted to the large and moderate deviation results, see e.g.
[
9
,
10
,
13
,
16
]
and references therein.

In this paper, our aim is to analyze the Gaussian fluctuation of
X_{\varepsilon,t}
around its limit. It should be noted that, in the last years, the Gaussian fluctuation for dynamical systems with small noise has been studied by several authors, see e.g.
[
2
,
6
,
8
]
and references therein. We observe that, as
\varepsilon\mbox{ tends to }0,
the solution
(X_{\varepsilon,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
of the equation (
1.1
) converges to a deterministic process
(x_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
solving the following integral equation

x_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,x_{s})ds,\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T.

(1.2)

Let us define the fluctuation process

\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}:=\frac{X_{\varepsilon,t}-x_{t}}{\varepsilon},\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T.

(1.3)

It is known from
[
15
]
that
\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}
converges to a Gaussian limit
Y_{t}
given by

Y_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}b^{\prime}(t,s,x_{s})Y_{s}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,x_{s})dB_{s},\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T.

(1.4)

The results of
[
15
]
are qualitative and hence, the next
important problem is to provide quantitative estimates for the rate of convergence. Here we use the total variation distance to characterize the rate of convergence:

d_{TV}(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t},Y_{t}):=\sup_{A}|P(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}\in A)-P(Y_{t}\in A)|=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{g}|Eg(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})-Eg(Y_{t})|,

where the supremum is running over all Borel subsets
A
of the real line and all measurable functions bounded by
1.
Then, we use the techniques of Malliavin calculus to obtain an explicit estimate for
d_{TV}(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t},Y_{t}).
Furthermore, we also show that the rate of convergence is optimal order.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some fundamental concepts of Malliavin calculus and a general estimate for the total variation distance established in our previous paper
[
5
]
. The main results of the paper are stated and proved in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we provide an application to stochastic Volterra equations with fractional Brownian motion kernel is given to illustrate the theory.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, for the reader’s convenience, we recall some elements of Malliavin calculus (for more details see
[
12
]
). We suppose that
(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},P)
, where
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a natural filtration generated by the Brownian motion
B
. For
h\in L^{2}[0,T]
, we denote by
B(h)
the Wiener integral

B(h)=\int_{0}^{T}h_{t}dB_{t}.

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote a dense subset of
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
that consists of smooth random variables of the form

\displaystyle F=f(B(h_{1}),B(h_{2}),...,B(h_{n})),

(2.1)

where
n\in\mathbb{N},f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),h_{1},h_{2},...,h_{n}\in L^{2}[0,T]
. If
F
has the form (
2.1
), we define its Malliavin derivative as the process
DF:={D_{t}F,t\in[0,T]}
given by

D_{t}F=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}(B(h_{1}),B(h_{2}),...,B(h_{n}))h_{k}(t).

More generally, for each
k\geq 1,
we can define the iterated derivative operator on a cylindrical random variable by setting

D_{t_{1},...,t_{k}}^{k}F=D_{t_{1}}...D_{t_{k}}F.

For any
1\leq p,k<\infty
, we denote by
\mathbb{D}^{k,p}
the closure of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to the norm

||F||_{k,p}^{p}:=E|F|^{p}+E\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|D_{s}F|^{2}ds\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]+...+E\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}...\int_{0}^{T}|D^{k}_{t_{1},...,t_{k}}F|^{2}dt_{1}...dt_{k}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right].

A random variable
F
is said to be Malliavin differentiable if it belongs to
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
. An important operator in the Malliavin’s calculus theory is the divergence operator
\delta
. It is the adjoint of derivative operator
D.
The domain of
\delta
is the set of all functions
u\in L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
such that

E|\langle DF,u\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}|\leq C(u)\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
C(u)
is some positive constant depending on
u.
In particular, if
u\in Dom\delta,
then
\delta(u)
is characterized by following duality relationships

\displaystyle\delta(uF)

\displaystyle=F\delta(u)-\left\langle DF,u\right\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}

(2.2)

\displaystyle E[\left\langle DF,u\right\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}]

\displaystyle=E[F\delta(u)]\mbox{ for any }F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}.

(2.3)

We have the following general estimate for the total variation distance.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
F_{1}\in\mathbb{D}^{2,4}
be such that
\|DF_{1}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}>0\,\,a.s.
Then, for any random variable
F_{2}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and any measurable function
\phi
bounded by
1,
we have

\displaystyle|E\phi(F_{1})-E\phi(F_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(E\|DF_{1}\|^{-8}_{L^{2}[0,T]}E\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}D_{r}F_{1}|^{2}d\theta dr\right)^{2}+(E\|DF_{1}\|^{-2}_{L^{2}[0,T]})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{1,2},

(2.4)

provided that the expectations exist, where
C
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

See Theorem 3.1 in our recent paper
[
5
]
.
∎

3
The main results

In this section, we follow
[
16
]
to impose the following assumptions. Hereafter we use the notations

\displaystyle b^{\prime}(t,s,x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\partial b(t,s,x)}{\partial x};\,\,\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,x)=\dfrac{\partial\sigma(t,s,x)}{\partial x};

\displaystyle b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\partial^{2}b(t,s,x)}{\partial x^{2}};\,\,\sigma^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x)=\dfrac{\partial^{2}\sigma(t,s,x)}{\partial x^{2}}.

Assumption 3.1
.

The coefficients
b,\sigma:[0,T]\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
have linear growth, i.e.

|b(t,s,x)|+|\sigma(t,s,x)|\leq k_{1}(t,s)(1+|x|)\mbox{ }\forall x\in\mathbb{R},0\leq s,t\leq T,

and
b(t,s,x),\sigma(t,s,x)
are differentiable functions in
x
with bounded derivatives

|b^{\prime}(t,s,x)|+|\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,x)|\leq k_{2}(t,s)\mbox{ }\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R},0\leq s,t\leq T,

where
k_{1}(t,s)
and
k_{2}(t,s)
are non-negative measurable functions on
[0,T]\times[0,T]
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{t}\left(k_{1}^{2\alpha}(t,s)+k_{2}^{2\beta}(t,s)\right)ds\leq L<\infty

(3.1)

for some
L>0
and
\alpha,\beta>1.

Assumption 3.2
.

b(t,s,x),\sigma(t,s,x)
are twice differentiable functions in
x,
and satisfy

|b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x)|+|\sigma^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x)|\leq k_{3}(t,s)\mbox{ }\forall x\in\mathbb{R},0\leq s,t\leq T,

where
k_{3}(t,s)
is non-negative measurable function on
[0,T]\times[0,T]
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{t}k_{3}^{2\gamma}(t,s)ds\leq L<\infty

(3.2)

for some
L>0
and
\gamma>1.

The main results of this paper are stated in the following theorems.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
. We consider the stochastic processes
(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
and
(Y_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
defined by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), respectively. Then, we have

\displaystyle d_{TV}(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t},Y_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\bigg(\sup_{0\leq u\leq t}\int_{0}^{u}k_{2}^{2}(u,s)ds\bigg)+\bigg(\sup_{0\leq u\leq t}\int_{0}^{u}k_{3}^{2}(u,s)ds\bigg)\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\sup_{0\leq u\leq t}\int_{0}^{u}k_{1}^{2}(u,s)ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{C\varepsilon}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}},\,\mbox{ }\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1),\mbox{ }0<t\leq T,

where
C
is a positive constant not depending on
t
and
\varepsilon.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
. We additionally assume that
b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x)
are continuous in
x.
Then, for any continuous and bounded function
\varphi,
we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{E\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})-E\varphi(Y_{t})}{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2\mbox{Var}(Y_{t})}E[\varphi(Y_{t})\delta(Z_{t}DY_{t})],\,\,\,0<t\leq T,

(3.3)

where the stochastic process
(Z_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
is given by

\displaystyle Z_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}b^{\prime}(t,s,x_{s})Z_{s}ds+\int_{0}^{t}b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s})Y_{s}^{2}ds+2\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,x_{s})Y_{s}dB_{s},\mbox{ }t\in[0,T].

(3.4)

Remark 3.1
.

(i)
Note that the Itô stochastic integral
\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,x_{s})dB_{s}
is a centered Gaussian random variable with finite variance for each
t\in[0,T].
Hence, it is easy to verify that the equation (
1.4
) admits a unique solution and this solution satisfies
E[Y_{t}]=0
and for every
p\geq 2,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E|Y_{t}|^{p}<\infty.

(3.5)

(ii)
For
p\geq\max\left\{\frac{2\beta}{\beta-1},\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}\right\}>2,
by using the Hölder and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities, we have

\displaystyle E\left|\int_{0}^{t}b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s})Y_{s}^{2}ds+2\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,x_{s})Y_{s}dB_{s}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\Bigg(E\left|\int_{0}^{t}b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s})Y_{s}^{2}ds\right|^{p}+E\left|2\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,x_{s})Y_{s}dB_{s}\right|^{p}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(tE\int_{0}^{t}k_{3}^{2}(t,s)Y_{s}^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+2^{2p-1}\left(E\int_{0}^{t}k_{2}^{2}(t,s)Y_{s}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}t^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{3}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(t,s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}E|Y_{s}|^{2p}ds+2^{2p-1}\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{2}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(t,s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}E|Y_{s}|^{p}ds.

This, together with the conditions (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), yields

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}E\left|\int_{0}^{t}b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s})Y_{s}^{2}ds+2\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,x_{s})Y_{s}dB_{s}\right|^{p}<\infty.

Hence, the linear integral equation (
3.4
) admits a unique solution
(Z_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
satisfying

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}E|Z_{t}|^{p}<\infty.

3.1
Estimates for Malliavin derivatives

Hereafter, we denote by
C
a generic constant which may vary at each appearance. In our proofs, we frequently use the fundamental inequality

(a_{1}+...+a_{n})^{p}\leq n^{p-1}(a_{1}^{p}+...+a_{n}^{p}),

for all
a_{1},...,a_{n}\geq 0
and
p\geq 1.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose Assumption
3.1
. Let
(X_{\varepsilon,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be the solution to the equation (
1.1
). Then, for every
p\geq 2,
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}E|X_{\varepsilon,t}|^{p}\leq C\,\,\,\forall\,\varepsilon\in(0,1),

(3.6)

where
C
is a positive constant not depending on
t
and
\varepsilon.

Proof.

We first recall that, under Assumption
3.1
, there exists a unique solution
(X_{\varepsilon,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
to the equation (
1.1
). In addition, the boundedness of moments is well known, see e.g.
[
17
]
. Here we give a proof to show that the moments are bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon\in(0,1).
We consider
p\geq\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}>2,
it follows from Assumption
3.1
and the Hölder and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities that

\displaystyle E\left|X_{\varepsilon,t}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\left(|x_{0}|^{p}+E\left|\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X_{\varepsilon,s})ds\right|^{p}+E\left|\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X_{\varepsilon,s})dB_{s}\right|^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\left(|x_{0}|^{p}+E\left(t\int_{0}^{t}|b(t,s,X_{\varepsilon,s})|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+C\varepsilon^{p}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\sigma(t,s,X_{\varepsilon,s})|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C+CE\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{1}^{2}(t,s)(1+|X_{\varepsilon,s}|^{2})ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\,\forall\,\varepsilon\in(0,1),t\in[0,T],

where
C
is a constant positive depending only on
T,p
and
x_{0}.
Using the Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle E\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{1}^{2}(t,s)(1+|X_{\varepsilon,s}|^{2})ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{1}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(t,s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}E(1+|X_{\varepsilon,s}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{1}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(t,s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}E(1+|X_{\varepsilon,s}|^{p})ds.

Furthermore, it follows from the condition (
3.1
) that

\int_{0}^{t}k_{1}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(t,s)ds\leq t^{1-\frac{p}{(p-2)\alpha}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}k_{1}^{2\alpha}(t,s)ds\bigg)^{\frac{p}{(p-2)\alpha}}\leq T^{1-\frac{p}{(p-2)\alpha}}L^{\frac{p}{(p-2)\alpha}}.

We deduce

\displaystyle E\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{1}^{2}(t,s)(1+|X_{\varepsilon,s}|^{2})ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C+C\int_{0}^{t}E|X_{\varepsilon,s}|^{p}ds\,\,\forall\,\varepsilon\in(0,1),t\in[0,T],

(3.7)

where
C
is a constant positive depending only on
L,T,p
and
\alpha.

As a consequence, we obtain

\displaystyle E\left|X_{\varepsilon,t}\right|^{p}\leq C+C\int_{0}^{t}E|X_{\varepsilon,s}|^{p}ds\,\,\forall\,\varepsilon\in(0,1),t\in[0,T].

By Gronwall’s lemma, we get

E|X_{\varepsilon,t}|^{p}\leq Ce^{Ct}\leq Ce^{CT}\,\,\forall\,\varepsilon\in(0,1),t\in[0,T].

So, by Lyapunov’s inequality, we conclude that (
3.6
) holds true for any
p\geq 2.
The proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let Assumption
3.1
hold. Let
(X_{\varepsilon,t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be the solution to the equation (
1.1
). Then, for each
0\leq t\leq T,
the random variable
X_{\varepsilon,t}
is Malliavin differentiable. Moreover, the derivative
D_{\theta}X_{\varepsilon,t}
satisfies
D_{\theta}X_{\varepsilon,t}=0
for
\theta>t
and

D_{\theta}X_{\varepsilon,t}=\varepsilon\sigma(t,\theta,X_{\varepsilon,\theta})+\int_{\theta}^{t}b^{\prime}(t,s,X_{\varepsilon,s})D_{\theta}X_{\varepsilon,s}ds\\
+\varepsilon\int_{\theta}^{t}\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,X_{\varepsilon,s})D_{\theta}X_{\varepsilon,s}dB_{s},\mbox{ }0\leq\theta\leq t\leq T.

(3.8)

Proof.

Consider the Picard approximations given by

\displaystyle X^{(0)}_{\varepsilon,t}

\displaystyle=x_{0}

\displaystyle X^{(n)}_{\varepsilon,t}

\displaystyle=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s})ds+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s})dB_{s},\mbox{ }n\geq 1.

It is easy to verify that
\sup_{n\geq 0}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}E|X^{(n)}_{\varepsilon,t}|^{p}<\infty\,\,\forall\,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
p\geq 2.
Moreover,

X^{(n)}_{\varepsilon,t}\to X_{\varepsilon,t}\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,L^{p}(\Omega)\,\,\text{as}\,\,n\to\infty.

(The proof is similar to that of Theorem 1.1 in
[
17
]
). Fixed
t\in[0,T],
by the induction argument, we can show that
X^{(n)}_{\varepsilon,t}
belongs to
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
for all
n\geq 0.
Moreover,
D_{\theta}X^{(n)}_{\varepsilon,t}=0\mbox{ for }\theta>t,
and for
0\leq\theta\leq t\leq T,
we have

\displaystyle D_{\theta}X^{(n)}_{\varepsilon,t}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\sigma(t,\theta,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,\theta})+\int_{\theta}^{t}D_{\theta}[b(t,s,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s})]ds+\varepsilon\int_{\theta}^{t}D_{\theta}[\sigma(t,s,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s})]dB_{s}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\sigma(t,\theta,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,\theta})+\int_{\theta}^{t}b^{\prime}(t,s,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s})D_{\theta}X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s}ds+\varepsilon\int_{\theta}^{t}\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s})D_{\theta}X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s}dB_{s}.

We now claim that

\sup\limits_{n}E\|DX^{(n)}_{\varepsilon,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}<\infty,\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T.

(3.9)

We have

\displaystyle E|D_{\theta}X^{(n)}_{\varepsilon,t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\Bigg[E|\varepsilon\sigma(t,\theta,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,\theta})|^{2}+E\left|\int_{\theta}^{t}b^{\prime}(t,s,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s})D_{\theta}X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s}ds\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+E\left|\varepsilon\int_{\theta}^{t}\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s})D_{\theta}X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s}dB_{s}\right|^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 3E|\varepsilon\sigma(t,\theta,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,\theta})|^{2}+3t\int_{\theta}^{t}E|b^{\prime}(t,s,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s})D_{\theta}X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s}|^{2}ds

\displaystyle+3\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\theta}^{t}E|\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s})D_{\theta}X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s}|^{2}ds,\,\,0\leq\theta\leq t\leq T.

By Assumption
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle E|D_{\theta}X^{(n)}_{\varepsilon,t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 6\varepsilon^{2}k_{1}^{2}(t,\theta)(1+E|X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,\theta}|^{2})+3(T+\varepsilon^{2})\int_{\theta}^{t}k_{2}^{2}(t,s)E|D_{\theta}X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s}|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}k_{1}^{2}(t,\theta)+C\int_{\theta}^{t}k_{2}^{2}(t,s)E|D_{\theta}X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s}|^{2}ds,\,\,0\leq\theta\leq t\leq T,

where
C
is the positive constant not depending on
n,\varepsilon\mbox{ and }t
. We obtain

\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{\theta}X^{(n)}_{\varepsilon,t}|^{2}d\theta\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}k_{1}^{2}(t,\theta)d\theta+C\int_{0}^{t}k_{2}^{2}(t,s)\int_{0}^{s}E|D_{\theta}X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s}|^{2}d\theta ds,\,\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

Fixed
p\geq\frac{2\beta}{\beta-1}>2
, by using the same arguments as in the proof of (
3.7
), we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{\theta}X^{(n)}_{\varepsilon,t}|^{2}d\theta\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\Bigg(\left(\int_{0}^{t}C\varepsilon^{2}k_{1}^{2}(t,\theta)d\theta\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+\left(C\int_{0}^{t}k_{2}^{2}(t,s)\int_{0}^{s}|D_{\theta}X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s}|^{2}d\theta ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sup_{0\leq u\leq t}\int_{0}^{u}k_{1}^{2}(u,\theta)d\theta\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\varepsilon^{p}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{s}|D_{\theta}X^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s}|^{2}d\theta\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}ds.

As a consequence

\left(E\|DX^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C\left(\sup_{0\leq u\leq t}\int_{0}^{u}k_{1}^{2}(u,\theta)d\theta\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\varepsilon^{p}\\
+C\int_{0}^{t}\left(E\|DX^{(n-1)}_{\varepsilon,s}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}ds,\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

(3.10)

Put
u(t)=\sup\limits_{n}\left(E\|DX^{(n)}_{\varepsilon,t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}.
Taking supremum two side of (
3.10
) we get

u(t)\leq C\left(\sup_{0\leq u\leq t}\int_{0}^{u}k_{1}^{2}(u,\theta)d\theta\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\varepsilon^{p}+C\int_{0}^{t}u(s)ds,\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T.

Then, using Gronwall’s lemma, we obtain

u(t)\leq C\left(\sup_{0\leq u\leq t}\int_{0}^{u}k_{1}^{2}(u,\theta)d\theta\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\varepsilon^{p}e^{Ct}<\infty,\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T,\mbox{ }\varepsilon\in(0,1).

(3.11)

This implies that the claim (

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}E\left|\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{t}\right|^{p}=0,\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T.

(3.22)

For each
t\in[0,T],
similar to (
3.18
), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}b^{\prime}(t,s,x_{s})\big(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}-Y_{s}\big)ds+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s}+\xi_{1}(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s}))(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s})^{2}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,x_{s})\left(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s}\right)dB_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s}+\xi_{2}(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s}))(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s})^{2}dB_{s},

where
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
are random variables lying between
0
and
1.
Recalling the definition (
3.4
) of
Z_{t},
we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}b^{\prime}(t,s,x_{s})\bigg(\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}-Y_{s}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{s}\bigg)ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b^{\prime\prime}\left(t,s,x_{s}+\xi_{1}(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s})\right)\big(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}^{2}-Y_{s}^{2}\big)ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s}+\xi_{1}(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s}))-b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s})\right)Y_{s}^{2}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,x_{s})\big(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}-Y_{s}\big)dB_{s}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s}+\xi_{2}(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s}))(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s})^{2}dB_{s},

and hence,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{t}\right|^{p}\leq 5^{p-1}

\displaystyle\Bigg(\left|\int_{0}^{t}b^{\prime}(t,s,x_{s})\bigg(\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}-Y_{s}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{s}\bigg)ds\right|^{p}

\displaystyle+\left|\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}b^{\prime\prime}\left(t,s,x_{s}+\xi_{1}(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s})\right)\big(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}^{2}-Y_{s}^{2}\big)ds\right|^{p}

\displaystyle+\left|\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s}+\xi_{1}(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s}))-b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s})\right)Y_{s}^{2}ds\right|^{p}

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\prime}(t,s,x_{s})\big(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}-Y_{s}\big)dB_{s}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle+\left|\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s}+\xi_{2}(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s}))(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s})^{2}dB_{s}\right|^{p}\Bigg).

By using Assumptions
3.1
-
3.2
and the Hölder and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities, we get

\displaystyle E\left|\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{t}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 5^{p-1}\left(E\left(t\int_{0}^{t}k_{2}^{2}(t,s)\left|\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}-Y_{s}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{s}\right|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right.

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2^{p}}E\left(t\int_{0}^{t}k_{3}^{2}(t,s)|\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}^{2}-Y_{s}^{2}|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2^{p}}E\left(t\int_{0}^{t}\left|b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s}+\xi_{1}(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s}))-b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s})\right|^{2}Y_{s}^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle+E\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{2}^{2}(t,s)|\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}-Y_{s}|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left.\frac{1}{2^{p}\varepsilon^{p}}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{3}^{2}(t,s)\left|X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s}\right|^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right),\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T.

We put

H_{1,\varepsilon}(t):=\frac{1}{2^{p}}E\left(t\int_{0}^{t}k_{3}^{2}(t,s)|\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}^{2}-Y_{s}^{2}|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+E\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{2}^{2}(t,s)|\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}-Y_{s}|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
+\frac{1}{2^{p}\varepsilon^{p}}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{3}^{2}(t,s)\left|X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s}\right|^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}},

and
H_{2,\varepsilon}(t):=\frac{1}{2^{p}}E\left(t\int_{0}^{t}\left|b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s}+\xi_{1}(X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s}))-b^{\prime\prime}(t,s,x_{s})\right|^{2}Y_{s}^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}.
As a consequence, for every
p\geq\max\left\{\frac{2\beta}{\beta-1},\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}\right\}>2,
we obtain

\displaystyle E\left|\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{t}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{2}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(t,s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}E\left|\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}-Y_{s}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{s}\right|^{p}ds+H_{1,\varepsilon}(t)+H_{2,\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}E\left|\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}-Y_{s}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{s}\right|^{p}ds+H_{1,\varepsilon}(t)+H_{2,\varepsilon}(t),

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
p,T
and
L.
Then, an application of Gronwall’s lemma gives us

E\left|\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{t}\right|^{p}\leq H_{1,\varepsilon}(t)+H_{2,\varepsilon}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}(H_{1,\varepsilon}(s)+H_{2,\varepsilon}(s))e^{C(t-s)}ds,\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T.

(3.23)

Furthermore, we have

H_{1,\varepsilon}(t)\leq\frac{t^{\frac{p}{2}}}{2^{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{3}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(t,s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}E|\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}^{2}-Y_{s}^{2}|^{p}ds+\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{2}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(t,s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}E|\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,s}-Y_{s}|^{p}ds\\
+\frac{1}{2^{p}\varepsilon^{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{3}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(t,s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}E\left|X_{\varepsilon,s}-x_{s}\right|^{2p}ds,\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T,

which, combined with (
3.13
) and (
3.16
), implies that
H_{1,\varepsilon}(t)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0.
Note that
H_{2,\varepsilon}(t)
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon\in(0,1).
Indeed,

H_{2,\varepsilon}(t)\leq\frac{t^{\frac{p}{2}}}{2^{p}}E\left(\int_{0}^{t}4k_{3}^{2}(t,s)Y_{s}^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq t^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}k_{3}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(t,s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}E|Y_{s}|^{2p}ds<\infty.

So, by the dominated convergence theorem, we also have
H_{2,\varepsilon}(t)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0,
we obtain (
3.22
) from (
3.23
). This finishes the proof of Step 1.

Step 2.
In this step, we prove (
3.3
). For simplicity, we write
\langle.,.\rangle
instead of
\langle.,.\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}
and
\|.\|
instead of
\|.\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}.
Fixed
t\in(0,T].
In view of the relation (3.2) in
[
5
]
, we have

\displaystyle E[\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})]-E[\varphi(Y_{t})]

\displaystyle=E\left[\int_{Y_{t}}^{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}}\varphi(z)dz\delta\bigg(\frac{DY_{t}}{\|DY_{t}\|^{2}}\bigg)\right]-E\left[\frac{\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})\langle D\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-DY_{t},DY_{t}\rangle}{\|DY_{t}\|^{2}}\right].

We observe from the equation (
3.20
) that
D_{\theta}Y_{t}
is deterministic for all
0\leq\theta\leq t\leq T,
and
E[D_{\theta}Y_{t}]=0.
Hence, by Clark-Ocone formulla, we have

Y_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}E[D_{\theta}Y_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{\theta}]dB_{\theta}=\int_{0}^{t}D_{\theta}Y_{t}dB_{\theta},\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

The above relation implies that
\|DY_{t}\|^{2}={\rm Var}(Y_{t})
and
\delta\left(\frac{DY_{t}}{\|DY_{t}\|^{2}}\right)=\frac{Y_{t}}{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}.
So we obtain

\displaystyle E[\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})]-E[\varphi(Y_{t})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}E\left[Y_{t}\int_{Y_{t}}^{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}}\varphi(z)dz\right]-\frac{1}{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}E\left[\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})\langle D\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-DY_{t},DY_{t}\rangle\right].

By the estimate (
3.19
) and the limit (
3.22
), we have
\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}}{\varepsilon}\to\frac{1}{2}Z_{t}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
\varepsilon\to 0

and
\max\limits_{\varepsilon>0}\frac{E\|D\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-DY_{t}\|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}<\infty.

Thus, it follows from Lemma
1.2.3
in
[
12
]
that
DZ_{t}
exists for every
t\in[0,T]
and

\frac{D\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-DY_{t}}{\varepsilon}
weakly converges to
\frac{DZ_{t}}{2}
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
as
\varepsilon\to 0.
Then, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1),
we have

\displaystyle\frac{E[\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})]-E[\varphi(Y_{t})]}{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}E\left[\varphi(Y_{t})Z_{t}Y_{t}\right]+\frac{1}{2{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}E\left[\varphi(Y_{t})\langle DZ_{t},DY_{t}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}E\left[\bigg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{Y_{t}}^{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}}\varphi(z)dz-\frac{1}{2}\varphi(Y_{t})Z_{t}\bigg)Y_{t}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}E\left[(\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})-\varphi(Y_{t}))\bigg\langle\frac{D\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-DY_{t}}{\varepsilon},DY_{t}\bigg\rangle\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}E\left[\varphi(Y_{t})\bigg\langle\frac{D\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-DY_{t}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{DZ_{t}}{2},DY_{t}\bigg\rangle\right],\,\,0<t\leq T.

(3.24)

For the first addend in the right hand side of (
3.24
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{Y_{t}}^{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}}\varphi(z)dz-\frac{1}{2}\varphi(Y_{t})Z_{t}=\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(Y_{t}+z(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}))dz-\frac{1}{2}\varphi(Y_{t})Z_{t}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{Z_{t}}{2}\bigg)\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(Y_{t}+z(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}))dz+\frac{Z_{t}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\varphi(Y_{t}+z(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}))-\varphi(Y_{t})\right)dz,

and hence,

\displaystyle E\left|\bigg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{Y_{t}}^{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}}\varphi(z)dz-\frac{1}{2}\varphi(Y_{t})Z_{t}\bigg)Y_{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|_{\infty}E\left|\left(\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{Z_{t}}{2}\right)Y_{t}\right|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}E\left|Z_{t}Y_{t}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\varphi(Y_{t}+z(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}))-\varphi(Y_{t})\right)dz\right|.

Because the random variables
Y_{t}
and
Z_{t}
belong to
L^{2}(\Omega),
recalling the limit (
3.22
), we have

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}E\left|\bigg(\frac{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{Z_{t}}{2}\bigg)Y_{t}\right|=0.

By the dominated convergence theorem and the fact that
\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}\to Y_{t},
we also have

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}E\left|Z_{t}Y_{t}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\varphi(Y_{t}+z(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-Y_{t}))-\varphi(Y_{t})\right)dz\right|=0.

So it holds that

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}E\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{Y_{t}}^{\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}}\varphi(z)dz-\frac{1}{2}\varphi(Y_{t})Z_{t}\bigg)Y_{t}\bigg]=0.

(3.25)

For the second addend in the right hand side of (
3.24
) we have

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\left[(\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})-\varphi(Y_{t}))\bigg\langle\frac{D\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-DY_{t}}{\varepsilon},DY_{t}\bigg\rangle\right]\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}}E\left[\frac{|\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})-\varphi(Y_{t})|\|D\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-DY_{t}\|}{\varepsilon}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}}(E|\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})-\varphi(Y_{t})|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\frac{E\|D\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-DY_{t}\|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Once again, by the estimate (
3.19
) and the dominated convergence theorem, we deduce

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}E\left[(\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})-\varphi(Y_{t}))\bigg\langle\frac{D\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-DY_{t}}{\varepsilon},DY_{t}\bigg\rangle\right]=0.

(3.26)

For the last addend in the right hand side of (
3.24
) we have

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}E\left[\varphi(Y_{t})\bigg\langle\frac{D\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-DY_{t}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{DZ_{t}}{2},DY_{t}\bigg\rangle\right]=0.

(3.27)

because
\frac{D\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}-DY_{t}}{\varepsilon}
weakly converges to
\frac{DZ_{t}}{2}
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
as
\varepsilon\to 0.
Combining (
3.25
), (
3.26
) and (
3.27
) yields

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{E[\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})]-E[\varphi(Y_{t})]}{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}E\left[\varphi(Y_{t})Z_{t}Y_{t}\right]-\frac{1}{2{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}E\left[\varphi(Y_{t})\langle DZ_{t},DY_{t}\rangle\right].

Moreover, by using the duality relationship (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\delta(Z_{t}DY_{t})=Z_{t}Y_{t}-\langle DZ_{t},DY_{t}\rangle

Hence,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{E[\varphi(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t})]-E[\varphi(Y_{t})]}{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}E\left[\varphi(Y_{t})\delta(Z_{t}DY_{t})\right].

The proof of Theorem
3.2
is complete.

4
Stochastic Volterra equation with fractional Brownian motion kernel

Given
H\in(0,1),
we consider the kernel

K_{H}(t,s):=\frac{(t-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Gamma(H-1/2)\sqrt{V_{H}}}F(H-1/2,1/2-H,H+1/2,1-t/s),\quad\text{$0\leq s<t\leq T$},

where
F
is the Gauss hypergeometric function,
\Gamma
denotes the usual Gamma function and
V_{H}=\frac{\Gamma(2-2H)\cos\pi H}{\pi H(1-2H)}.
We recall that the kernel
K_{H}
can be used to define a fractional Brownian motion as follows (see e.g.
[
3
]
)

B^{H}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)dB_{s},\,\,0\leq t\leq T.

(4.1)

Moreover, the covariance function of
B^{H}
is given by

R_{H}(t,s):=E[B^{H}_{t}B^{H}_{s}]=\frac{1}{2}(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}),\,\,0\leq s\leq t\leq T.

In this section, we consider the following stochastic Volterra equation with the fractional Brownian motion kernel
K_{H}

X_{\varepsilon,t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)b(s,X_{\varepsilon,s})ds+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)\sigma(s,X_{\varepsilon,s})dB_{s},

(4.2)

where
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, the initial data
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b,\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are measurable functions having linear growth, that is, there exists
M>0
such that

|b(t,x)|+|\sigma(t,x)|\leq M(1+|x|)\,\,\forall x\in\mathbb{R},0\leq t\leq T.

Additionally,
b
and
\sigma
are twice differentiable functions in
x
with the partial derivatives bounded by
M.

The equation (
4.2
) was studied first in
[
3
]
, the large and moderate deviation results have been discussed in
[
9
,
10
,
16
]
. Here we apply the results of Section
3
to bound the total variation distance for the fluctuation process of
X_{\varepsilon,t}.

We define

\displaystyle x_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)b(s,x_{s})ds,\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T.

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t}:=\frac{X_{\varepsilon,t}-x_{t}}{\varepsilon},\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T,

and

Y_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)b^{\prime}(s,x_{s})Y_{s}ds+\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)\sigma(s,x_{s})dB_{s},\mbox{ }0\leq t\leq T.

(4.3)

Theorem 4.1
.

For every
H\in(0,1),
it holds that

d_{TV}(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t},Y_{t})\leq\frac{Ct^{2H}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}}\,\,\mbox{ }\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1),\mbox{ }0<t\leq T,

(4.4)

where
C
is a positive constant not depending on
t
and
\varepsilon.
When
H\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
\sigma(t,x)\geq\sigma_{0}
for some
\sigma_{0}>0
and for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R},
we also have

d_{TV}(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon,t},Y_{t})\leq Ct^{H}\varepsilon\,\,\mbox{ }\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1),\mbox{ }0<t\leq T.

(4.5)

Proof.

We recall from
[
7
]
that
0\leq K_{H}(t,s)\leq c(t-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}s^{-|H-\frac{1}{2}|}
for some
c>0.
Hence, it is easy to verify that the coefficients of the equation (
4.2
) satisfy Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
with
k_{1}(t,s)=k_{2}(t,s)=k_{3}(t,s)=MK_{H}(t,s)
and

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq u\leq t}\int_{0}^{u}k^{2}_{i}(u,s)ds

\displaystyle=M^{2}\sup_{0\leq u\leq t}\int_{0}^{u}K_{H}^{2}(u,s)ds

\displaystyle=M^{2}\sup_{0\leq u\leq t}E|B^{H}_{u}|^{2}=M^{2}t^{2H},\,i=1,2,3.

So the estimate (
4.4
) follows directly from Theorem
3.1
.

We now consider the case
H\geq\frac{1}{2}.
In this case, the kernel
K_{H}
reduces to the following (see e.g.
[
12
]
pp. 277-279)

K_{H}(t,s):=c_{H}\,s^{1/2-H}\int_{s}^{t}(u-s)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}u^{H-1/2}du,\quad\text{$0\leq s<t\leq T,$}

where
c_{H}=\sqrt{\frac{H(2H-1)}{\beta(2-2H,H-1/2)}}
with
\beta
is the Beta function. We have the following estimates

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}K^{2}_{H}(t,\theta)d\theta

\displaystyle=c_{H}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}\theta^{1-2H}\left(\int_{\theta}^{t}(u-\theta)^{H-3/2}u^{H-1/2}du\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq c_{H}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}\left(\int_{\theta}^{t}(u-\theta)^{H-3/2}du\right)^{2}d\theta=\frac{2c_{H}^{2}}{H(2H-1)^{2}}(t-s)^{2H}

(4.6)

and

\displaystyle|t-s|^{2H}

\displaystyle=E|B_{t}^{H}-B_{s}^{H}|=E\left(\int_{0}^{s}\big(K_{H}(t,\theta)-K_{H}(s,\theta)\big)dB_{\theta}+\int_{s}^{t}K_{H}(t,\theta)dB_{\theta}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=E\left(\int_{0}^{s}\big(K_{H}(t,\theta)-K_{H}(s,\theta)\big)dB_{\theta}\right)^{2}+E\left(\int_{s}^{t}K_{H}(t,\theta)dB_{\theta}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq E\left(\int_{s}^{t}K_{H}(t,\theta)dB_{\theta}\right)^{2}=\int_{s}^{t}K_{H}^{2}(t,\theta)d\theta,\,0\leq s\leq t\leq T.

(4.7)

On the other hand, from the equation (
4.3
) we have

\displaystyle D_{\theta}Y_{t}=K_{H}(t,\theta)\sigma(\theta,x_{\theta})+\int_{\theta}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)b^{\prime}(s,x_{s})D_{\theta}Y_{s}ds,\,\,0\leq\theta\leq t\leq T.

Hence, for some
C>0,

\displaystyle|D_{\theta}Y_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2M^{2}(1+|x_{\theta}|)^{2}K_{H}^{2}(t,\theta)+2M^{2}\int_{\theta}^{t}K_{H}^{2}(t,s)ds\int_{\theta}^{t}|D_{\theta}Y_{s}|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq CK_{H}^{2}(t,\theta)+2M^{2}|t-\theta|^{2H}\int_{\theta}^{t}|D_{\theta}Y_{s}|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq CK_{H}^{2}(t,\theta)+2M^{2}T^{2H}\int_{\theta}^{t}|D_{\theta}Y_{s}|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq CK_{H}^{2}(t,\theta)+C\int_{\theta}^{t}|D_{\theta}Y_{s}|^{2}ds.

By Gronwall’s lemma and the fact that the function
s\to K_{H}(s,\theta)
is non-decreasing, we have

\displaystyle|D_{\theta}Y_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CK_{H}^{2}(t,\theta)+C\int_{\theta}^{t}e^{C(s-\theta)}K^{2}_{H}(s,\theta)ds

\displaystyle\leq CK_{H}^{2}(t,\theta)+Ce^{CT}\int_{\theta}^{t}K^{2}_{H}(s,\theta)ds

\displaystyle\leq CK_{H}^{2}(t,\theta),\,0\leq\theta\leq t\leq T,

where
C
is a positive constant not depending on
t.
For
h\in(0,1],
by the fundamental inequality
(a+b)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}a^{2}-b^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left|D_{\theta}Y_{t}\right|^{2}d\theta\geq\int_{t(1-h)}^{t}\left|D_{\theta}Y_{t}\right|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t(1-h)}^{t}K^{2}_{H}(t,\theta)\sigma^{2}(\theta,x_{\theta})d\theta-\int_{t(1-h)}^{t}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}K_{H}(t,s)b^{\prime}(s,x_{s})D_{\theta}Y_{s}ds\bigg)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{2}\int_{t(1-h)}^{t}K_{H}^{2}(t,\theta)d\theta-C\int_{t(1-h)}^{t}\bigg(\int_{\theta}^{t}K_{H}^{2}(t,s)ds\int_{\theta}^{t}K_{H}^{2}(s,\theta)ds\bigg)d\theta.

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(Y_{t})

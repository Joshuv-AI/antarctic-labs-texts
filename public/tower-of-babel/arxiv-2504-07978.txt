Wolstenholme’s theorem over Gaussian integers
Nikita Kalinin

Abstract
. This paper establishes an extension of Wolstenholme’s theorem to the ring of Gaussian integers ℤ ⁡ [ i ] \mathbb{Z}[i] . For a prime p > 7 p>7 , we prove that the sum S p S_{p} of inverses of Gaussian integers in the set { n + m i ∣ 1 ≤ n , m ≤ p − 1 , gcd ( p , m i + n ) = 1 } \{n+mi\mid 1\leq n,m\leq p-1,\gcd(p,mi+n)=1\} satisfies the congruence S p ≡ 0 ( mod p 4 ) S_{p}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4}} . We further generalize this result to higher-power sums S p ( k ) S_{p}^{(k)} , demonstrating structured divisibility patterns modulo powers of p p . We propose some conjectures generalising the connections between classical Wolstenholme’s theorem and binomial coefficients. Special cases and irregularities for small primes ( p ≤ 1000 p\leq 1000 ) are explicitly computed and tabulated.

Introduction

The classical Wolstenholme’s theorem
[
14
]
states that for any prime
p\geq 5
,

1+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\dots+\frac{1}{p-1}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}}.

This result has been generalized to various settings
[
15
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

.

Let
i^{2}=-1
and
p
be an integer prime number. Our main result generalizes Wolstenholme’s theorem to Gaussian integers:

Theorem 1

(Wolstenholme’s Congruence for Gaussian Integers)
.

For a prime
p>5
, the sum
S_{p}
of reciprocals of Gaussian integers modulo
p
satisfies:

(1)

S_{p}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq p-1\\
(p,m^{2}+n^{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n+mi}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4}}.

1.2.
Motivation

Our initial aim was to establish an analogue of Wolstenholme’s theorem for finite fields with
p^{k}
elements. The classical Wolstenholme’s theorem can be formulated as follows. Let
p
be a prime integer number and
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the map of taking residues mod
p
. Consider the invertible elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and lift them in
\mathbb{Z}
as numbers
1,2,\dots,p-1
. Take their inverses in
\mathbb{Q}
, compute the sum

\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{1}{k}=\frac{A}{B},

then the denominator
B
is coprime with
p
and the numerator
A
is divisible by
p
(since
\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{1}{k}=0
if we consider the elements
k\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
). Wolstenholme’s theorem states that the numerator
A
is divisible by a higher than expected power of
p
, namely
p^{2}
.

For a finite field
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p^{k}}
of the characteristic
p
with
p^{k}
elements, a natural approach is to lift the sum

\sum\limits_{a\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p^{k}}}\frac{1}{a}=0\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p^{k}}

to a ring of characteristic
0
, compute the sum of fractions in this ring and then establish divisibility of the numerator by a power of
p
(again, divisibility by
p^{1}
is automatic).

After some unsuccessful experiments, we realized
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
as
\mathbb{F}_{p}[i]
for the case
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, then found and proved the above congruence. Note that
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p^{2}}
contains
p^{2}-1
elements but in Theorem
1
we compute the sum over
(p-1)^{2}
elements (for
p=4k+3
) or
(p-1)^{2}-(p-1)
elements (for
p=4k+1
). It would be interesting to establish similar congruences for extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of higher degree.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

Before proceeding with the proof, we require a lemma.

Lemma 1
.

For each prime number
p>5
the following congruence holds:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq{p-1}\\
(p,m^{2}+n^{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{(m^{4}-6m^{2}n^{2}+n^{4})}{(m^{2}+n^{2})^{4}}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

Proof.

We need to show that both sums below vanish modulo
p
:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq{p-1}\\
(p,m^{2}+n^{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{m^{2}n^{2}}{(m^{2}+n^{2})^{4}},\text{ and}

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq{p-1}\\
(p,m^{2}+n^{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{(m^{4}+2m^{2}n^{2}+n^{4})}{(m^{2}+n^{2})^{4}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq{p-1}\\
(p,m^{2}+n^{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(m^{2}+n^{2})^{3}}.

The key observation is that modulo
p
for
m^{2}+n^{2}\neq 0\pmod{p}
, we have

\frac{1}{m^{2}+n^{2}}\equiv(m^{2}+n^{2})^{p-2}\pmod{p}.

Moreover, the power sums
\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}n^{q}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
vanish unless
q
is divisible by
p-1
.

Thus, if
p>5

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq p-1\\
(p,m^{2}+n^{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{m^{2}n^{2}}{(m^{2}+n^{2})^{4}}\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq{p-1}\\
(p,m^{2}+n^{2})=1\end{subarray}}(m^{2}n^{2})(m^{2}+n^{2})^{p-5}\equiv

\equiv\sum_{1\leq n,m\leq{p-1}}(m^{2}n^{2})(m^{2}+n^{2})^{p-5}\equiv\sum_{0\leq n,m\leq{p-1}}(m^{2}n^{2})(m^{2}+n^{2})^{p-5}\pmod{p}.

When we expand the parenthesis, we have terms of the form

n^{t}m^{s},y+s=2p-6

so it is impossible that
p-1
divides both
s
and
t
unless
2p-6=0
, i.e.
p=3
. If, for example,
p-1
does not divide
s
then for each fixed
m
we have

\sum_{1\leq n\leq p-1}n^{s}m^{t}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

Thus, for each term
n^{s}m^{t},s+t=2p-6
we have

\sum\limits_{1\leq n,m\leq p-1}n^{s}m^{t}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

Similarly,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq{p-1}\\
(p,m^{2}+n^{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(m^{2}+n^{2})^{3}}\equiv\sum_{1\leq n,m\leq p-1}(m^{2}+n^{2})^{p-4}\pmod{p}.

The terms in this sum are
n^{t}m^{s},y+s=2p-8
and we proceed as above.

∎

Definition 1

(Conjugate Tuples)
.

For distinct indices
n\neq m
, define the following
8
-tuple
T(m,n,k)
as

(2)

T(m,n,k)=\frac{1}{(n+im)^{k}}+\frac{1}{(p-n+im)^{k}}+\frac{1}{(n+i(p-m))^{k}}+\\
+\frac{1}{(p-n+i(p-m))^{k}}+\frac{1}{(m+in)^{k}}+\frac{1}{(p-m+in)^{k}}+\\
+\frac{1}{(m+i(p-n))^{k}}+\frac{1}{(p-m+i(p-n))^{k}}.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Consider a particular element
\frac{1}{n+mi}
in (
1
). Consider the corresponding
8
-tuple
T(m,n,1)
. All such tuples are parametrised by pairs
(n,m)
with
1\leq n<m\leq\frac{p-1}{2}
. Direct computation shows that the product of the denominators of fractions in
T(m,n,1)
is

m^{8}+4m^{6}n^{2}+6m^{4}n^{4}+4m^{2}n^{6}+n^{8}+p(\dots)

and the numerator of
T(n,m,1)
is

\left(-2i+2\right)p^{3}(m^{4}-6m^{2}n^{2}+n^{4})+p^{4}(\dots).

Note that the numerator is already divisible by
p^{3}
(see Section
5
for explicit expansions).

Now we can rewrite

S_{p}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq p-1\\
(p,m^{2}+n^{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n+mi}=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq p-1\\
(p,m^{2}+n^{2})=1\end{subarray}}T(n,m,1)\equiv

\equiv p^{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq{p-1}\\
(p,m^{2}+n^{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{(m^{4}-6m^{2}n^{2}+n^{4})}{(m^{2}+n^{2})^{4}}\pmod{p^{4}}.

Theorem
1
follows now from Lemma
1
.

∎

3.
Higher powers

Having established the base case, we now consider higher powers.

Theorem 2
.

For a prime
p>5
and
k\geq 1
, define:

(3)

S_{p}^{(k)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq p-1\\
(p,m^{2}+n^{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(n+mi)^{k}}

Then the following congruences hold:

(1)

S_{p}^{(1)}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4}}

(2)

S_{p}^{(2)}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{3}}

(3)

S_{p}^{(3)}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}}

(4)

S_{p}^{(4)}\equiv 0\pmod{p}

Proof.

(1) is Theorem
1
, it is already proven. For (2),(3), and (4), the proofs follow the similar lines. Consider
8
-tuples
T(m,n,k),k=2,3,4
. We perform the computations in Macaulay2; the code is in Section
6
.

In (2) the denominator of
T(m,n,2)
modulo
p
is
(m^{2}+n^{2})^{8}
and the numerator is

12ip^{2}\left(m^{2}+n^{2}\right)^{4}\left(m^{2}-2mn-n^{2}\right)\left(m^{2}+2mn-n^{2}\right).

Thus it is enough to show that

\sum\frac{\left(m^{2}-2mn-n^{2}\right)\left(m^{2}+2mn-n^{2}\right)}{(m^{2}+n^{2})^{4}}\equiv 0\pmod{p},

and the numerator of this expression is
m^{4}-6m^{2}n^{2}+n^{4}
, hence we may use Lemma
1
.

In (3) the denominator of
T(m,n,3)
modulo
p
is
(m^{2}+n^{2})^{12}+p...
and the numerator is

\left(-12i-12\right)p\cdot(m^{2}+n^{2})^{8}\left(m^{2}-2mn-n^{2}\right)\left(m^{2}+2mn-n^{2}\right)

and the proof is the same as above.

In (4) the denominator of
T(m,n,4)
modulo
p
is
(m^{2}+n^{2})^{16}+p..
and the numerator is

8(m^{2}+n^{2})^{12}\left(m^{2}-2mn-n^{2}\right)\left(m^{2}+2mn-n^{2}\right)+p...

and the rest of the proof is the same.

For
S_{p}^{(5)}
the exponent of

(4)

\frac{1}{(n+im)^{5}}+\frac{1}{(p-n+im)^{5}}+\frac{1}{(n+i(p-m))^{5}}+\frac{1}{(p-n+i(p-m))^{5}}+\\

\frac{p^{3}\left(m^{4}-4m^{3}n-6m^{2}n^{2}+4mn^{3}+n^{4}\right)\left(m^{4}+4m^{3}n-6m^{2}n^{2}-4mn^{3}+n^{4}\right)}{\left(m^{2}+n^{2}\right)^{8}}

In the above formula, we throw away all the terms containing
p
in the denominator and those containing
p^{4}
in the numerator and then divide the numerator by
-70(i-1)
. Again, it is not difficult to prove the congruence
S^{(5)}_{p}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4}}
, but the proof is not identical to the proof of Theorem
1
, and we do not see how to proceed in the general case, i.e. how to write a general formula for the coefficient behind the lowest power of
p
in the numerator of
T(m,n,k)

A straightforward calculation yields divisibility of

S^{(5)}_{p},S^{(6)}_{p},S^{(7)}_{p},S^{(8)}_{p},S^{(9)}_{p},S^{(10)}_{p},S^{(11)}_{p},S^{(12)}_{p}

(Wolstenholme’s Higher-Power Congruences for Gaussian Integers)

For any integer

and prime

,

(5)

A lower bound of the form

4.
Discussion and possible directions

4.1.
Polynomial Analogues

Wolstenholme’s theorem admits the following polynomial interpretation. Let

f(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}(x-k)=x^{p-1}+a_{1}x^{p-2}+\dots+a_{p-2}x+a_{p-1}.

Then
a_{p-2}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}}.
Indeed,
-a_{p-2}/a_{p-1}
is the sum of the inverses of the roots of
f(x)
.

For Gaussian integers define
r=(p-1)^{2}
and

g_{p}(x)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq p-1\\
(p,n^{2}+m^{2})=1\end{subarray}}(x-(n+mi))=x^{(p-1)^{2}}+a_{1}x^{(p-1)^{2}-1}+\dots+a_{r-1}x+a_{r}.

Then

\frac{-a_{r-1}}{a_{r}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq p-1\\
(p,n^{2}+m^{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n+mi}

The polynomial
g_{p}
has nontrivial coefficients. For example, consider the following polynomials
g_{p}(x)\pmod{p}

g_{11}(x)\equiv x^{100}+x^{80}+x^{60}+x^{40}+x^{20}+1\pmod{11}

g_{19}(x)\equiv x^{324}+x^{288}+x^{252}+x^{216}+x^{180}+x^{144}+x^{108}+x^{72}+x^{36}+1\pmod{19}

g_{13}(x)\equiv x^{120}+3x^{108}+6x^{96}-3x^{84}+2x^{72}-5x^{60}+2x^{48}-3x^{36}+6x^{24}+3x^{12}+1\pmod{13}

\displaystyle g_{17}(x)

\displaystyle\equiv x^{224}+3x^{208}+6x^{192}-7x^{176}-2x^{160}

\displaystyle\quad+4x^{144}-6x^{128}+2x^{112}-6x^{96}+4x^{80}

\displaystyle\quad-2x^{64}-7x^{48}+6x^{32}+3x^{16}+1\pmod{17}

Our findings lead to the following conjectural pattern.

Conjecture 2
.

If
p=4k+3>3
then

g_{p}(x)\equiv 1+x^{2(p-1)}+x^{4(p-1)}+\dots+x^{(p-1)^{2}}\equiv\frac{1-x^{p^{2}-1}}{1-x^{2(p-1)}}\pmod{p}.

If
p=4k+1>5
then

g_{p}(x)\equiv 1+b_{1}x^{p-1}+b_{2}x^{2(p-1)}+\dots+x^{(p-1)(p-3)}\pmod{p}.

Is there any closed formula for the coefficients
b_{k}\equiv a_{k(p-1)}\pmod{p}
?

4.2.
Binomial coefficients for Gaussian integers

By Vieta’s theorem, Theorem
2
implies the divisibility of the coefficients of
g_{p}(x)
behind
x^{1},x^{2},x^{3},x^{4}
by the powers of
p
, i.e. the congruences
a_{r-k}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{5-k}}
for
k=1,2,3,4
.

Substituting
x=pA+ipB,A,B\in\mathbb{Z}
into
g_{p}(x)
we get

Theorem 3
.

For a prime
p>5
and
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
we have

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq p-1\\
(p,n^{2}+m^{2})=1\end{subarray}}(pA+ipB-(n+mi))\equiv\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq p-1\\
(p,n^{2}+m^{2})=1\end{subarray}}(n+mi)\pmod{p^{5}}.

This may be seen as a
\mathbb{Z}[i]
-analogue of

(np-1)\cdot(np-2)\cdot\ldots\cdot(np-p+1)\equiv(p-1)!\pmod{p^{3}},

which is equivalent to
\binom{np-1}{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod{p^{3}}
.

Thus, one could define a Gaussian integer analog of the binomial coefficients.

Definition 2
.

For integer numbers
A\geq C\geq 1,B\geq D\geq 1
define the
Gaussian binomial coefficient
:

(6)

\left[\begin{subarray}{c}A+Bi\\
C+Di\end{subarray}\right]:=\frac{\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq n\leq C-1,\\
0\leq m\leq D-1\end{subarray}}(A+Bi-(n+mi))}{\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq C,\\
1\leq m\leq D\end{subarray}}(n+mi)}

Unfortunately, such binomial coefficients are not Gaussian integers in general and we do not know much about their properties. However we can reformulate Theorem
3
as

Theorem 4
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and prime
p\equiv 3\pmod{4},p>3
. Then

(7)

\left[\begin{subarray}{c}pA-1+(pB-1)i\\
(p-1)+(p-1)i\end{subarray}\right]\equiv 1\pmod{p^{5}}.

It is interesting whether a version of Lucas’ theorem holds for such coefficients.

Conjecture 3

(Lucas congruence for Gaussian binomial Coefficients)
.

\left[\!\begin{subarray}{c}pA+pBi\\
pC+pDi\end{subarray}\!\right]\equiv\left[\!\begin{subarray}{c}A+Bi\\
C+Di\end{subarray}\!\right]\pmod{p^{3}}.

4.3.
Connections to Bernoulli Numbers

It would be interesting to find a generalization of Glaisher’s congruence
[
4
]
:

1+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\dots++\frac{1}{p-1}\equiv-\frac{1}{3}p^{2}B_{p-3}\pmod{p^{3}},

where
B_{p-3}
is a Bernoulli number; see also
[
11
]
for generalizations. Maybe it is possible to find an explicit
p
-adic expansions by
L
-functions, such as in
[
13
]
. It is interesting to investigate the Bernoulli numbers for the sums of powers of Gaussian integers

W^{(k)}(A+Bi)=\sum_{1\leq a\leq A,1\leq b\leq B}(a+bi)^{k}.

4.4.
Sums for general
n+ni

For a (not necessarily prime) natural number
n\equiv 1,5\pmod{6}
Ludesdorf
[
5
]
proved that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k<n\\
(k,n)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k}\equiv 0\pmod{n^{2}}

One can consider sums of such fractions’ powers; they are curiously related to the Bernoulli numbers
[
10
,
11
,
13
]
.

So it is natural to ask when

S_{n}^{(k)}\equiv 0\pmod{n^{4-r}},r=(k-1)\pmod{4}

holds for an arbitrary number
n
. No definitive pattern is apparent here; let us list some observations. Consider
S_{n}^{(k)}
for
1\leq k\leq 8
.
Besides primes, for certain numbers
n=21,26,34,35,39,40,4249,52,55,57,58,63,68,74,77,78,82,\\
84,91,93,104,106,110,111,114,116,117,119,121,122,126,129,133,136,143,\\
144,145,146,147,148,154,155,156,161,164,...
all the congruences hold, the same as for the prime numbers. If
5|n
then it is frequent that only the divisibility for
S_{n}^{(5)}
holds (among those for
1\leq k\leq 8
), and others do not.

5.
T(n,m,1)

The product of the denominators of fractions in
T(m,n,1)
is

\displaystyle m^{8}+4m^{6}n^{2}+6m^{4}n^{4}+4m^{2}n^{6}+n^{8}-4m^{7}p-4m^{6}np-

\displaystyle-12m^{5}n^{2}p-12m^{4}n^{3}p-12m^{3}n^{4}p-12m^{2}n^{5}p-4mn^{6}p-

\displaystyle-4n^{7}p+8m^{6}p^{2}+12m^{5}np^{2}+24m^{4}n^{2}p^{2}+24m^{3}n^{3}p^{2}+

\displaystyle+24m^{2}n^{4}p^{2}+12mn^{5}p^{2}+8n^{6}p^{2}-10m^{5}p^{3}-18m^{4}np^{3}-

\displaystyle-28m^{3}n^{2}p^{3}-28m^{2}n^{3}p^{3}-18mn^{4}p^{3}-10n^{5}p^{3}+9m^{4}p^{4}+

\displaystyle+16m^{3}np^{4}+18m^{2}n^{2}p^{4}+16mn^{3}p^{4}+9n^{4}p^{4}-6m^{3}p^{5}-

\displaystyle-6m^{2}np^{5}-6mn^{2}p^{5}-6n^{3}p^{5}+2m^{2}p^{6}+2n^{2}p^{6}.

The numerator of the sum

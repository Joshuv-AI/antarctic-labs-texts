A rare case of oropharyngeal metastasis from lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Toshiki Morimoto, Kei Yamasaki, Yuki Kawamura, Maika Meguro, Taiga Nagasawa, Satoshi Fujimoto, Saki Hiraga, Masahiro Tahara, Kazuhiro Yatera
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Key messageOropharyngeal metastasis from other organs is rare, especially from lung cancer. However, in lung cancer cases, the oropharynx should be considered a potential site for distant spread.

CLINICAL IMAGE

A 72‐year‐old Japanese man with a 51 pack‐year smoking history presented to our hospital with pharyngeal haemorrhage persisting for 1 month. Nasopharyngoscopy revealed a lesion covered with a white coating on the right side of the anterior wall of the oropharynx, which was not continuous with the palatine tonsil (Figure 1). Whereas oropharyngeal carcinoma was suspected, biopsy showed atypical cells with enlarged nuclei varying in size, positive for AE1/AE3 and thyroid transcription factor 1, which suggested lung adenocarcinoma (Figure 2). Chest computed tomography revealed a 45 mm mass shadow in the right upper lobe of the lung and mediastinal lymph‐node enlargement (Figure 3). Endobronchial ultrasound‐guided transbronchial needle aspiration of the subcarinal lymph node showed pathology consistent with that of the oropharyngeal biopsy (Figure 4), and confirming diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma. First‐line treatment with carboplatin plus nab‐paclitaxel was initiated. However, treatment was discontinued after one course owing to the patient's deteriorating performance status. The patient died 2 months after treatment initiation. Although metastasis to the oropharynx from other organs is rare, accounting for only about 1%–2% of all oral cancers, 1 it should be considered a potential site of distant spread in lung cancer cases.

Nasopharyngoscopic findings. A lesion covered with a white coating on the right side of the anterior wall of the oropharynx (A). The mass in the oropharynx was not continuous with the palatine tonsil (B).

Histopathological findings of the mass in the oropharynx. Haematoxylin–eosin staining reveals atypical cells with enlarged nuclei of varying sizes (A, B). Immunohistochemistry of the tumour cells revealed thyroid transcription factor 1 (C) and AE1/AE3 (D) positivity.

Chest computed tomography upon admission revealed a 45 mm mass in the right upper lobe of the lung (yellow arrows) and mediastinal lymph‐node enlargement (green arrows).

Histopathological findings of the mediastinal lymph nodes. Mediastinal lymph nodes showing histological findings consistent with the oropharynx according to haematoxylin–eosin staining (A, B) and thyroid transcription factor 1 (C) and AE1/AE3 (D) positivity.

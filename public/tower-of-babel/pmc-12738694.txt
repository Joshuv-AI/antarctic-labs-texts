An efficient reparameterized small object detection transformer for thermal infrared images
Authors: Canhao Guo, Peidong Luo, Zhixing Ma, Tianhong Zhao, Xiaole Shen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate and efficient small object detection in thermal infrared images remains a critical challenge due to inherent issues such as low contrast, limited texture, and deployment constraints on edge platforms like Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV). This paper presents PWL-RTDETR, an efficient Transformer-based framework specifically designed for infrared small object detection. The proposed model incorporates a novel Partial Convolutional Reparameterization Block (PConvRep-Block), which fuses Partial Convolution and reparameterization to support multi-branch training and single-path inference, significantly reducing computation without compromising representation quality. To enhance multi-scale feature aggregation, we introduce WTRCSPNeck, a lightweight neck architecture integrating CNCSPELAN and WTConv modules. CNCSPELAN improves gradient flow and feature representation through structural reparameterization, while WTConv employs multi-level wavelet decomposition to effectively expand the receptive field and capture both global context and fine-grained details. Furthermore, we adopt Layer-Adaptive Magnitude-based Pruning to achieve global sparsification with layer-wise adaptability, enabling further compression while maintaining model accuracy. Comprehensive evaluations on the HIT-UAV and LLVIP infrared datasets confirm that PWL-RTDETR surpasses existing state-of-the-art models in accuracy, while achieving substantial reductions in parameters and FLOPs. The results highlight the model’s suitability for real-time deployment in resource-constrained infrared perception scenarios.Keywords: Infrared small object detection, UAV, Transformer, Lightweight model, Reparameterization, Wavelet convolution, Model pruningSubject terms: Computer science, Information technology

Introduction

In recent years, with the rapid development of UAV technology, infrared (IR) imaging1 systems have been widely adopted in various fields such as intelligent surveillance2, disaster response3, and military reconnaissance4. IR imaging offers significant advantages by being unaffected by environmental illumination. Infrared images effectively capture target features by detecting the thermal radiation emitted by objects. Thus, IR-based object detection5 techniques exhibit unique potential, especially under limited visible-light conditions and in complex environments. Despite these advantages, current IR object detection methods6 still face significant constraints in complex real-world scenarios, greatly limiting their broader applications.

Specifically, IR object detection tasks encounter several key challenges. Firstly, IR images typically exhibit low contrast7, with minimal temperature differences between the targets and backgrounds. This significantly affects detection reliability and accuracy. Secondly, environmental factors in real-world scenarios, such as varying climate conditions, complex terrain, and occlusion, considerably reduce the generalization ability of detection models. Additionally, infrared images typically appear as single-channel grayscale images lacking rich textures and color information, making effective feature extraction particularly challenging. Moreover, limited computational resources8 on UAV platforms present a critical bottleneck. Achieving high accuracy, low computational complexity, and real-time detection simultaneously becomes essential. This issue is further aggravated under long-distance imaging conditions, where small objects occupy very few pixels, significantly increasing detection difficulty.

Recent advancements in deep learning9 methods have considerably improved object detection performance. Traditional hand-crafted feature methods offer computational advantages but lack sufficient generalization ability and adaptability to complex environments. In contrast, deep models based on Convolutional Neural Networks10 (CNNs) and Transformer11 architectures have demonstrated superior performance in infrared image detection tasks. However, high-performance deep models typically involve substantial computational costs, making direct deployment difficult on edge devices12 with limited computational resources, such as UAVs. The recently proposed Real-Time Detection Transformer13 (RT-DETR) has drawn significant academic attention due to its excellent global feature modeling and efficient end-to-end detection capability. Although RT-DETR achieves a good balance between speed and accuracy, the original RT-DETR framework still suffers from high computational complexity and large parameter counts. Furthermore, existing RT-DETR models primarily target visible-light images. Their generalization performance on IR data, particularly for small-object scenarios, requires further exploration and enhancement.

To address these limitations of RT-DETR, we propose a lightweight Transformer-based infrared object detection model, termed PWL-RTDETR. Our proposed model aims to significantly reduce network parameters and computational costs while enhancing detection accuracy and generalization ability for infrared targets. Specifically, we introduce a novel efficient backbone module, named PConvRep-Block. PConvRep-Block integrates Partial Convolution14 (PConv) and Reparameterization15 (Rep) techniques. This module enables multi-path learning during training, thereby enhancing feature extraction. During inference, the structured merging of weights significantly reduces computational and storage overhead. Additionally, we design a novel lightweight neck network named WTRCSPNeck, which combines CNCSPELAN and WTConv16 modules. The CNCSPELAN module, inspired by YOLOv917, is centered around the Conv3XC18 structure, significantly improving computational efficiency while maintaining robust feature extraction capabilities. The WTConv module utilizes wavelet transform to construct convolutions with large receptive fields. This effectively improves detection across multiple scales, especially beneficial for small-object detection tasks. Lastly, we adopt the LAMP19 method to further optimize computational efficiency. LAMP performs global pruning ranking and layer-adaptive sparsification to minimize performance degradation. It preferentially retains weights crucial to overall model performance, achieving significant model compression and maintains or even improves the detection accuracy.

We conduct extensive evaluations of our method using two publicly available datasets, HIT-UAV20 and LLVIP21, both consisting of thermal infrared images. The HIT-UAV dataset is primarily used for training small-object detection, while the LLVIP dataset is employed for evaluating generalization performance. To avoid ambiguity in the notion of “small target”, we adopt a resolution-normalized definition throughout this paper. After preprocessing, all inputs are resized to 640 640. A box is regarded as small if its longer side is 32 pixels at this resolution. This definition applies to the HIT-UAV benchmark. By contrast, LLVIP is used only to assess cross-dataset generalization under low-light pedestrian scenes and typically contains medium-size instances.

Related work

In recent years, rapid advancements in deep learning technology have significantly promoted progress in object detection. Notably, approaches based on CNNs10 have revolutionized traditional object detection methods. Traditional techniques typically rely on manually engineered features and complex post-processing steps, resulting in limited generalization and accuracy. In contrast, CNNs enable end-to-end automatic feature learning, substantially enhancing accuracy and generalization. CNN-based detection frameworks are generally classified into two categories: two-stage and one-stage methods. Two-stage detectors, such as R-CNN22, Fast R-CNN23, and Faster R-CNN24, achieve high accuracy through separate region proposal and refinement stages but incur significant computational costs. This limits their real-time applicability, especially on resource-constrained edge devices. One-stage detectors like YOLO25, SSD26, and RetinaNet27 integrate region proposal and classification into a unified architecture, greatly improving detection speed. In remote sensing, YOLO-SR adapts one-stage CNNs to SAR ship detection via balanced detail fusion and detail-preserving upsampling, improving small-vessel recognition under speckle and scale variation28. Additionally, these methods effectively handle class imbalance and small objects using mechanisms like multi-scale feature fusion and focal loss, making them highly suitable for real-time applications.

At the same time, Transformer models, known for their self-attention mechanisms, have achieved remarkable success in natural language processing and have gradually been adopted in computer vision tasks. The DEtection TRansformer29 (DETR) first introduced Transformer11 into object detection, eliminating the need for region proposal networks (RPN) and anchor boxes by leveraging global context modeling. However, the original DETR model suffers from slow convergence and limited performance with small objects. To address these issues, researchers proposed various improvements. For instance, Deformable DETR30 introduced deformable attention mechanisms to enhance small object detection and accelerate convergence. Conditional DETR31 and Dynamic DETR32 improved overall convergence and accuracy by optimizing query strategies. Notably, building on Deformable DETR, Than et al. introduced Multi-Scale Long-Range Feature Aggregation (MLFA) and replaced deformable attention with an Occlusion-Aware Efficient Transformer Attention (OAETA), yielding improved accuracy and robustness for small and distant objects under occlusion33.

Building on DETR, the RT-DETR13 further optimized Transformer11 structures to enhance computational efficiency and real-time performance. RT-DETR features a lighter Transformer decoder combined with dynamic matching strategies and multi-scale feature fusion, significantly reducing complexity. Moreover, its multi-scale feature aggregation significantly improves small object detection performance, making it ideal for edge computing devices like drones and mobile robots.

Infrared images introduce additional challenges, such as low contrast, small object sizes, and complex background interference. While CNN-based methods such as YOLO25 and SSD26 have made some initial progress in infrared object detection, they remain ineffective in complex backgrounds and small-object scenarios. Recently, Transformer-based detection methods have increasingly been applied to infrared small-object detection tasks due to their strong global modeling capabilities and long-range feature dependencies. These methods demonstrate superior robustness compared to CNN-based techniques. Nevertheless, high computational complexity remains a significant barrier to deploying traditional Transformers practically.

To overcome these challenges, several Transformer-based models have been proposed to enhance computational efficiency and generalization ability for infrared and small-object detection. Yang et al.34 introduced ISTD-DETR, integrating EDSR super-resolution techniques, Efficient Multi-scale Attention (EMA), and state space model to enhance feature extraction and long-range dependency modeling. They also introduced an SPD-EMA module in the feature fusion layers, effectively improving the detection accuracy for small-scale targets. Wang et al.35 proposed a lightweight model called PHSI-RTDETR, employing RPConv-Block modules to reduce computational complexity, and introducing the HiLo attention mechanism to improve detection performance on densely distributed small objects. Xu and Zeng36 proposed the DCEI-RTDETR algorithm to tackle challenges in small-object detection and complex backgrounds. They used EfficientFormerV2 as the feature extraction backbone and designed a High-level Screening Feature Aggregation Network (HS-FAN) to improve multi-scale feature fusion. They also adopted a one-to-many label assignment strategy and the Inner-EIOU loss function, significantly enhancing small-object detection accuracy. Xue et al.37 introduced FECI-RTDETR , addressing diverse target scales, complex backgrounds, and resource limitations typical of UAV-based infrared images. They designed the lightweight RFConv-Block module to optimize the backbone network and introduced the EA-AIFI module to enhance feature attention for dense targets. They also proposed a CHS-FPN structure for cross-scale feature fusion and employed the Inner-GIoU loss function, further improving accuracy and generalization performance. Zhao et al.38 presented IST-DETR , specifically designed for low-resolution infrared images and small-object detection challenges. They developed a hybrid encoder combining Mutual Feature Screening (MFS) modules and positional encoding learning. Additionally, they introduced a customized IoU loss function combining MPDIoU and InnerIoU, and a sliding sample weighting function. These techniques effectively mitigate data imbalance problems, significantly improving detection accuracy and convergence speed. Xiao et al.39 proposed GM-DETR , a generalized multispectral object detection Transformer model focused on efficient fusion of RGB and infrared images. Their model features a high-efficiency fusion encoder with the Modality-Specific Feature Interaction (MSFI) module and Cross-Modality-Scale feature Fusion (CMSF) module. They also employed a two-stage training strategy to enhance generalization, achieving outstanding performance in multimodal detection tasks. Nguyen et al.40 adapt RT-DETR for remote sensing by introducing a backbone MSAAM and a neck HFFM, which preserve fine details and fuse multi-scale context to improve small-object detection.

In summary, these research advancements have significantly progressed Transformer-based methods for infrared object detection, providing robust theoretical foundations and technical support. These studies have paved the way for improved detection accuracy and generalization ability in complex scenarios and established an essential research foundation for the proposed PWL-RTDETR model.

Proposed method

The structure of RT-DETR

The real-time object detection field has long been dominated by the YOLO series41. These CNN-based10 detectors achieve an effective balance between speed and accuracy through efficient feature extraction and regression methods. However, YOLO heavily relies on Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) to filter detection boxes. This not only increases computational overhead but may also cause unstable optimization, reducing the detector’s robustness across different scenarios.In 2020, the DETR29 algorithm introduced Transformer11 architectures into object detection for the first time. DETR eliminates anchor mechanisms and NMS post-processing in an end-to-end manner, significantly simplifying the detection pipeline. Nevertheless, DETR suffers from high computational complexity, slow training convergence, and relatively low inference efficiency, making it unsuitable for real-time applications. Although subsequent improvements such as Deformable DETR30 and DINO42 partially mitigated these issues and improved performance, their inference speeds still do not match those of YOLO models.

To address these limitations, Zhao et al.13 proposed RT-DETR, an end-to-end Transformer structure specifically optimized for real-time object detection. RT-DETR significantly reduces computational costs through structural innovations while maintaining effective feature modeling capabilities. Its architecture includes three core modules: a backbone network, a hybrid Transformer encoder, and a Transformer decoder. The backbone extracts multi-scale features and uses convolutional layers and normalization methods to enlarge the receptive field. Residual connections are also adopted to improve gradient propagation and network stability.Unlike traditional Transformer11 encoders, RT-DETR employs a computationally efficient hybrid encoder. It consists of an Attention-based Intra-scale Feature Interaction (AIFI) module, which applies self-attention only to high-level semantic feature layers, thus reducing computational load. Additionally, the CNN-based Cross-scale Feature Fusion (CCFF) module utilizes CNN10 structures to integrate multi-scale features. This design decreases computational complexity and improves inference efficiency.

During decoding, RT-DETR uses an IoU-Aware Query Selection strategy to ensure the decoder focuses only on the most confident feature regions. This significantly improves localization accuracy and reduces error propagation from low-quality queries. Furthermore, the decoder integrates Auxiliary Prediction Heads and iterative optimization strategies, progressively enhancing detection accuracy and ensuring stable bounding box predictions. RT-DETR also supports flexible inference speed adjustments by modifying the number of decoding layers, allowing performance optimization across diverse computational scenarios.Experimental results on the COCO dataset43 demonstrate that RT-DETR outperforms similarly sized YOLO models in detection accuracy while achieving notably higher inference speeds. Therefore, RT-DETR is particularly suitable for resource-constrained applications. Figure 1 depicts the basic architecture of the RT-DETR model.

PWL-RTDETR

As depicted in Fig. 2, we propose PWL-RTDETR, a lightweight infrared object-detection Transformer model. The model combines multi-path feature extraction, structural reparameterization, and an adaptive pruning strategy. These enhancements achieve high-accuracy object detection while significantly reducing computational load, ensuring efficient deployment on resource-constrained UAVs. The architecture includes three main components: a backbone network, a neck network, and a detection head. Together, these components form an efficient, lightweight detection framework tailored for infrared small-target detection.

PWL-RTDETR network structure diagram.

In the backbone network, we introduce the PConvRep-Block, based on the PConvRep structure. PConvRep-Block integrates PConv14 with RepVGG15 structural reparameterization to reduce computational redundancy and enhance feature extraction. Partial Convolution reduces computational complexity by performing convolution only on selected channels. Additionally, PConvRep-Block employs multi-path training to strengthen feature representation, merging weights structurally during inference for higher computational efficiency. This design significantly reduces computation while preserving rich local and global information. Thus, PConvRep-Block maintains high detection accuracy in infrared images, particularly suitable for low-contrast and small-target detection tasks. Furthermore, incorporating RepVGG allows multiple parallel branches during training, which merge into a streamlined structure during inference, balancing efficiency and accuracy effectively.

The neck network adopts the WTRCSPNeck structure, combining two core modules: CNCSPELAN and WTConv16. This combination enhances feature fusion and improves the detection of multi-scale targets. CNCSPELAN applies structural reparameterization to optimize feature extraction and utilizes CSPNet’s gradient flow partitioning to achieve efficient inference with reduced computational complexity. The module leverages RepConv’s structural reparameterization and Conv3XC’s18 efficient feature extraction capability. Additionally, CSPNet’s gradient flow partitioning facilitates gradient propagation, reducing redundant computations. As a result, CNCSPELAN enhances feature fusion, significantly improving detection in ambiguous infrared regions. WTConv applies wavelet transforms for multi-scale feature decomposition, maintaining a large receptive field with low computational cost. Cascade wavelet decomposition retains global context in low-frequency channels while emphasizing edge details in high-frequency channels. This strategy enables precise detection of small infrared targets, improving detection reliability.

To further enhance computational efficiency, the proposed model employs the LAMP19 pruning method for global network optimization. LAMP uses a global pruning ranking combined with a layer-adaptive sparsity strategy, substantially reducing computational complexity while preserving performance. During pruning, LAMP calculates layer-wise errors to adaptively adjust sparsity levels, retaining critical features and eliminating redundant computations. This approach significantly reduces model complexity, ensuring efficient inference suitable for real-time deployment on UAVs with limited computational resources.

WTRCSPNeck

WTRCSPNeck is an advanced neck network architecture that combines the strengths of two core modules: CNCSPELAN and WTConv. By integrating Structural Reparameterization and Wavelet Convolution in a complementary manner, it enhances feature representation while optimizing inference efficiency and controlling parameter growth.

The design of WTRCSPNeck leverages the CNCSPELAN module for multi-scale feature extraction and fusion. Its structural reparameterization strategy ensures efficient inference and stable training. The WTConv module then refines these features through frequency-domain decomposition and multi-scale receptive field expansion, enabling the network to capture both local and global information effectively.

Specifically, the processing pipeline of WTRCSPNeck consists of two key stages. First, feature maps from the backbone network pass through the CNCSPELAN module to extract optimized spatial representations. These refined features are then processed by the WTConv module, which applies a Cascade Wavelet Decomposition strategy to enhance frequency-domain representations. Finally, an Inverse Wavelet Transform (IWT) reconstructs the features, preserving both local details and global contextual information.

By seamlessly integrating these components, WTRCSPNeck improves feature representation while maintaining a balance between inference speed and parameter efficiency. This makes it particularly suitable for vision tasks that require both real-time performance and high accuracy.

The following sections provide a detailed overview of the CNCSPELAN and WTConv modules in WTRCSPNeck.

CNCSPELAN

This section introduces the proposed CNCSPELAN module, inspired by YOLOv917, designed to enhance feature representation while optimizing inference speed and computational efficiency.

Specifically, the module is built around Conv3XC, from which we develop the CNBottleneck and further extend it to the CNCSP structure. This design improves information retention, enabling more efficient feature extraction and stable gradient propagation.

At the core of CNCSPELAN is Conv3XC, which utilizes a transformation structure. This design incorporates a reparameterization strategy to enhance computational efficiency during inference. During training, Conv3XC adopts a multi-branch convolutional structure: the convolution handles channel transformation, the convolution extracts features, and the final convolution compresses channel dimensions to reduce computational cost. Additionally, a shortcut branch with a convolution ensures stable gradient flow, improving network trainability. For inference, Structural Reparameterization merges this multi-branch structure into an equivalent standard convolution kernel, reducing computational complexity and improving efficiency.

Within CNCSPELAN, CNCSP further enhances gradient flow propagation by leveraging the CSPNet architecture. Like traditional CSPNet designs, it employs a Gradient Flow Partitioning strategy, allowing the neck network to maximize multi-level feature utilization during training. Compared to the RepNCSP17 structure, CNCSP achieves better cross-layer information retention while reducing computational redundancy. Furthermore, thanks to the reparameterization strategy in Conv3XC, CNCSPELAN minimizes computational overhead during inference, making it well-suited for deployment on resource-constrained devices such as drones and edge computing platforms. LAMP

LAMP19 is a layer-adaptive pruning method that, unlike traditional magnitude-based approaches, removes the need to manually specify per-layer pruning ratios. Instead, it automatically determines the sparsity rate for each layer based on the importance of its weights within the network. The key principle of LAMP is its innovative weight importance scoring formula, which comprehensively assesses the impact of each layer’s weights on the overall network performance during pruning. Experiments and results

Dataset

This section provides a detailed introduction to the two datasets used in our experiments: HIT-UAV20 and LLVIP21.

The HIT-UAV dataset is designed for high-altitude infrared thermal imaging object detection in UAV-based applications. It includes 2,898 carefully selected infrared images extracted from hundreds of UAV-captured video sequences. These images cover diverse and complex scenarios such as schools, parking lots, roads, and playgrounds. The dataset presents significant challenges due to small target sizes, dense object distribution, complex backgrounds, and low contrast between objects and their surroundings. Additionally, detailed metadata is provided, including UAV flight altitude (ranging from 60 to 130 meters), camera viewing angles (from to ), capture dates, and ambient illumination conditions. These annotations allow for an in-depth analysis of environmental factors affecting infrared object detection algorithms. Parameter settings

The experiments were conducted on a high-performance workstation equipped with six NVIDIA 4090 GPUs and two Intel Xeon Silver 4314 processors, each with a clock speed of 2.40 GHz. First, the PConvRep, CNCSPELAN, and WTConv modules are introduced individually to evaluate their independent impact. The PConvRep module combines Partial Convolution with structural reparameterization, enabling multi-path information extraction during training and transitioning to a single-path, efficient structure for inference. This design reduces computational redundancy while improving local and semantic feature modeling. On HIT-UAV, PConvRep increases mAP50 to 76.0%, while reducing parameters and computation to 14.0M and 42.8G, respectively. On LLVIP, mAP50 improves to 96.0%.

The CNCSPELAN module enhances multi-scale feature representation through structural reparameterization and CSPNet’s gradient flow reconstruction mechanism, particularly benefiting small object modeling. When applied independently, it achieves mAP50 values of 76.5% and 96.1% on HIT-UAV and LLVIP, respectively, demonstrating consistent performance gains. Meanwhile, the WTConv module leverages wavelet transforms to construct cross-scale convolutional kernels, expanding the receptive field while preserving edge and low-frequency semantic features. When used alone, WTConv increases mAP50 to 76.5% and 96.3% on HIT-UAV and LLVIP, respectively, confirming its effectiveness in small object detection.

In combined module experiments, integrating PConvRep and CNCSPELAN achieves an mAP50 of 77.5% on HIT-UAV, with further reductions in parameters (12.6M) and computational cost (35.9G); on LLVIP, mAP50 improves to 96.5%. Adding WTConv further boosts mAP50 to 78.1% on HIT-UAV, with model size compressed to 11.5M and 33.7G, while LLVIP performance remains stable at 96.1%, highlighting the synergistic benefits in feature diversity and model expressiveness.

Finally, the LAMP pruning strategy is applied, leveraging global weight ranking and inter-layer sparsity adaptation to achieve further model compression and acceleration while preserving key semantic pathways. The final model, integrating all modules, achieves an mAP50 of 81.3% on HIT-UAV, with parameters and FLOPs reduced to 10.4M and 21.7G, respectively. On LLVIP, mAP50 remains at 96.1%. These results validate the complementary advantages of the modules in improving both accuracy and efficiency, demonstrating PWL-RTDETR’s strong adaptability for deployment in complex scenarios.

On HIT-UAV, introducing LAMP increases precision from 86.9 to 93.0% while leaving recall essentially unchanged. In a DETR-style one-to-one matching framework without NMS, at most one prediction can be matched to each ground truth; additional boxes near the same object are unmatched and therefore counted as FP rather than extra TP. LAMP’s layer-adaptive magnitude pruning globally ranks weights and suppresses low-importance channels prone to background over-activation or near-duplicate responses, thereby reducing unmatched predictions (FP) and increasing precision. By contrast, the dominant pathway that produces the single best match per object is typically carried by higher-magnitude weights and is preserved through pruning and fine-tuning; as a result, TP and FN remain nearly unchanged and recall stays stable. This mechanism accounts for the observed pattern under our pruning ratios.

Conclusions

In this paper, we proposed PWL-RTDETR, a lightweight Transformer-based detector tailored for small infrared object detection under resource-constrained environments. To address the challenges posed by low-contrast and texture-scarce thermal images, we introduced a series of structural optimizations across the backbone and neck of the RT-DETR framework. Specifically, the PConvRep-Block was designed to enhance feature extraction efficiency by integrating partial convolution and reparameterization techniques, significantly reducing computational overhead while maintaining representational capacity. Furthermore, we developed the WTRCSPNeck, which combines the strengths of CNCSPELAN and WTConv modules to improve multi-scale feature fusion and expand receptive fields at minimal cost. To further compress the model and enable real-time deployment, we incorporated the LAMP pruning strategy, which achieves fine-grained sparsity control with minimal accuracy degradation. Extensive experiments on Hit-UAV and LLVIP datasets demonstrate that PWL-RTDETR achieves competitive or superior performance compared to existing lightweight detection models, with significant reductions in FLOPs and parameter count. These results validate the effectiveness of the proposed architectural and pruning strategies in balancing accuracy, efficiency, and generalization.

This study has two limitations. First, evaluations are confined to two thermal infrared benchmarks, HIT-UAV and LLVIP; broader category coverage and scene diversity are needed to test cross-sensor and cross-altitude generalization. Second, the method is evaluated as a single-frame detector without explicit temporal modeling of UAV video sequences, so robustness under long occlusions, camera shake, and motion blur remains to be assessed within video-level detection and tracking pipelines. Future work will expand cross-domain IR datasets and categories, and integrate temporal priors, spatiotemporal consistency learning, and multi-frame enhancement into a unified framework.

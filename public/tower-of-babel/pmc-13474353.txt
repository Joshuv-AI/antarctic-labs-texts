ROMP-Derived Coumarin–Tyrosine Polymeric Chemosensors: A Biomolecule-Inspired Metal-Ion-Free Approach for Arginine Sensing in Artificial Saliva
Authors: Sirilak Wangngae, Theerada Khunpiluek, Sukanya Thisan, Sarawut Kumphune, Sutthira Sutthasupa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The development of noninvasive diagnostic platforms for biomarker detection in complex biological fluids remains a significant challenge. Herein, we report a biomolecule-inspired, metal-ion-free polymeric fluorescence chemosensor in which polymer architecture governs molecular recognition and photophysical response, enabling ultrasensitive detection of arginine (Arg), a clinically relevant biomarker for metabolic and renal disorders. Based on a coumarin–tyrosine-functionalized polynorbornene synthesized via ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP), the system integrates recognition sites within a well-defined macromolecular framework. UV–vis studies reveal a bathochromic shift (350–442 nm) with an isosbestic point at 392 nm upon Arg binding. Both Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 exhibit ratiometric fluorescence behavior in DMSO. The polymeric architecture affords enhanced signal amplification over monomeric analogues, achieving a limit of detection (LOD) of 71.42 nM and 103.64 nM, respectively. Systematic evaluation established artificial saliva as the optimal medium due to negligible matrix interference. In this environment, the probes demonstrated matrix-dependent fluorescence sensing. Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc maintained ratiometric performance, while the deprotected Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 functioned via high-sensitivity intensity-based quenching at 411 nm. This dual-mode capability yielded exceptional LODs of 33.09 nM and 32.45 nM in saliva, respectively. Mechanistic studies, including 1H NMR titration and Job’s plot, confirmed a 1:1 interaction mediated by cooperative hydrogen bonding. This work establishes a structure–property-driven polymer design strategy, providing a versatile metal-ion-free platform for next-generation noninvasive diagnostics and real-time metabolic monitoring.

Introduction

Early and accurate diagnosis of chronic and life-threatening diseases increasingly depends on the identification of sensitive metabolic biomarkers that can be detected using rapid, noninvasive, and point-of-care (POC) technologies. In this context, saliva has emerged as a highly attractive diagnostic biofluid, offering noninvasive sampling, low cost, minimal infection risk, and strong correlation with blood-based biomarkers. , Recent clinical and metabolomic investigations have further demonstrated that salivary metabolites provide valuable diagnostic information for diverse systemic diseases, including chronic kidney disease (CKD), cardiovascular disorders, metabolic syndromes, and multiple cancers. , However, the practical translation of salivary metabolite analysis into diagnostic technologies remains constrained by the scarcity of ultrasensitive, selective, and matrix-tolerant sensing materials that can operate reliably in complex biological environments.

Arginine (Arg), a fundamental basic amino acid involved in nitrogen metabolism, nitric oxide biosynthesis, and immune regulation, has emerged as a particularly powerful biomarker. , Notably, abnormal fluctuations in salivary Arg levels have been directly linked to the progression of CKD, hepatic dysfunction, immune disorders, oral squamous cell carcinoma, periodontitis, and multiple malignancies, including lung and colorectal cancers as well as renal failure, − collectively underscoring the significant clinical potential of salivary Arg as a noninvasive diagnostic indicator. Despite its clinical relevance, accurate and precise Arg quantification remains challenging due to matrix interference, structural similarity among amino acids, the limited stability and selectivity of many conventional sensing platforms.

To date, diverse fluorescence-based strategies have been explored for Arg detection, including metal–organic frameworks (MOFs), carbon dots, polymers, and small organic fluorophores such as rhodamine, coumarin, pyrene, hydrazone, hemicyanine, 1,1′-bi-2,2′-naphthol (BINOL), and Schiff-base derivatives. , Several ratiometric sensing systems, particularly based on MOFs and carbon dots, have also been reported, mainly for intracellular imaging or aqueous-phase sensing. However, most existing probes still rely on single-emission intensity changes, rendering them susceptible to environmental fluctuations, probe concentration variation, and background interference, thereby limiting analytical reliability in complex biological matrices. Importantly, although urine and cell-based Arg detection have received increasing attention, , ratiometric fluorescence sensing of Arg directly in saliva remains largely unexplored. Such approaches, which enable intrinsic self-calibration via dual-emission or wavelength shifts, offer superior analytical reliability for noninvasive diagnostics. Accordingly, the development of polymeric ratiometric probes capable of robust operation in salivary matrices. Such systems represent a critical unmet need for next-generation diagnostic materials.

In this context, the rational design of advanced polymeric sensing materials offers a powerful strategy to overcome these analytical challenges. , In particular, ROMP enables the precise construction of functional polymers with programmable architectures, high functional-group tolerance, and living polymerization characteristics, allowing diverse fluorescent dyes and molecular recognition motifs to be efficiently incorporated into well-defined polymer backbones. This synthetic versatility facilitates the development of highly sensitive polymeric chemosensors through cooperative signal amplification, structural tunability, and controlled spatial organization of sensing sites. − Consequently, ROMP has emerged as a highly attractive platform for engineering advanced fluorescent polymer sensors for chemical and biological sensing applications.

Our previous studies demonstrated that coumarin-based polynorbornenes incorporating either tryptophan or cysteine residues exhibit promising fluorescence-sensing properties. Specifically, tryptophan-functionalized polymers exhibited a fluorescence restoration (“ON-OFF-ON”) response toward Cu2+ followed by Arg recognition, whereas cysteine-functionalized polymers demonstrated analogous “ON-OFF-ON” behavior toward Cd2+ and selected anions. While these systems validated the effectiveness of amino acid-functionalized polymer architectures, their reliance on metal-ion-mediated switching introduced inherent susceptibility to matrix interference, particularly in serum and urine. Furthermore, the involvement of external metal ions adds additional complexity that may compromise sensing reliability in real biological samples. Notably, the cysteine-based polymers demonstrated reliable sensing primarily in saliva, highlighting the critical influence of fluorophore structure and metal-ion coordination pathways on biofluid compatibility. These findings motivated the development of a metal-ion-free polymeric sensing platform that eliminates metal-ion mediation while enabling direct ratiometric Arg recognition.

Incorporating fluorescent amino acids into polymer backbones provides a compelling route to engineer next-generation biosensors that integrate molecular recognition, photophysical tunability, and biocompatibility. , Among natural amino acids, tyrosine (Tyr) is uniquely suited for Arg recognition owing to its phenolic moiety, which enables hydrogen bonding, electrostatic attraction, and cation−π interactions with the guanidinium group of Arg. − Meanwhile, its intrinsic fluorescence and environment-sensitive quenching behavior offer built-in optical transduction capabilities. − Despite these attractive features, tyrosine-programmed ratiometric polymeric probes for Arg detection have not yet been reported, presenting a promising opportunity for materials innovation.

Herein, we report a ROMP-engineered ratiometric polymeric biosensor for ultrasensitive Arg detection, based on a coumarin–tyrosine-functionalized norbornene architecture synthesized via ROMP. The resulting polymers, Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 , integrate programmable molecular recognition, cooperative signal amplification, and tunable photophysical properties, distinct and reliable ratiometric fluorescence responses toward Arg. The metal-free sensing system exhibits a pronounced ratiometric fluorescence response toward Arg with nanomolar sensitivity (limit of detection of 71.42 nM in organic media), while maintaining excellent performance in artificial saliva with an even lower detection limit of 33.09 nM. Importantly, the intrinsic self-calibration capability of the ratiometric mechanism enhances analytical reliability in complex biological environments. Collectively, this work establishes a ROMP-derived polymer platform for ratiometric biomarker sensing and highlights its potential for noninvasive diagnostic applications.

General Polymerization

In the typical procedure, NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc (252 mg, 0.42 mmol), Umicore M31 catalyst (3.14 mg, 4.2 × 10–3 mmol), and THF (2 mL) were added to a glass tube equipped with a three-way stopcock under nitrogen, and the mixture was stirred in a water bath at 38 °C. The polymer solution turned yellow-brown within 5 min and was continuously stirred for 18 h to turn dark brown. Then, ethyl vinyl ether (0.2 mL, 2.1 mmol) was added to the mixture to quench the reaction. The reaction mixture was poured into hexane (200 mL). The precipitate was separated from the solvent by filtration using a membrane filter and dried under reduced pressure at room temperature to obtain a polymer (Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc) as a white solid (176.4 mg, 70% yield).

Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (50 mg, 1.40 mol) was dissolved in CH2Cl2 (5 mL). Trifluoroacetic acid (TFA, 150 μL) was added to the solution at 0 °C, and the resulting mixture was stirred at room temperature overnight. The reaction progress was monitored by thin-layer chromatography (TLC). The solvent and TFA were removed under reduced pressure to afford the intermediate as a viscous liquid. A saturated NaHCO3 (aq.) solution (10 mL) was added to the intermediate. The resulting mixture was extracted with CH2Cl2 three times. The organic phase was dried over Na2SO4 anhydrous. It was concentrated using a rotary evaporator to obtain Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 as a sticky liquid, yielding 41 mg (99%).

Fluorescence Properties and Sensing Experiments

The stock solutions of fluorescent probe Cou-Tyr-Boc (2000 μM), NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc (2000 μM), Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (300 μM), and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 (300 μM, for Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 calculated relative to the molecular mass of the Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc) were prepared in DMSO. The investigation involved dissolving the probes in various solvents (DI, DMF, DMSO, EtOH, THF, and ACN) to evaluate the solvent effect and select the most suitable organic solvent. The light absorption properties of the fluorescent probe were first investigated using UV–Vis spectroscopy. Then, light emission was measured using fluorescence spectroscopy, and different emission signals were observed in various solutions. After that, the amino acid Arg was added to each solvent. It was observed that the DMSO solution produced a quenching signal, indicating that DMSO was the most suitable organic solvent. Consequently, the UV absorbance of Cou-Tyr-Boc (10 μM), NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc (50 μM), Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (1 μM), and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 (1 μM) in DMSO was analyzed to determine the excitation wavelength. The fluorescence responses were measured using an excitation wavelength (λex) of 350 nm (coumarin), with excitation and emission slit widths of 5 nm. The fluorescence emission (λem) was monitored at 411 nm. Aqueous solutions of amino acids, Arg, Lys, His, Cys, HCys, GSH, Ala, Val, Met, Pro, Leu, Phe, Gln, and Asp were prepared by dissolving in water (20,000 μM). The fluorescence quenching efficiencies of the polymeric probes upon the addition of varying concentrations of Arg were examined to verify selective sensing, based on the lowest detectable concentration of the target amino acid. Quenching percentages were calculated from the collected data.

Determination of the Limit of Detection (LOD)

The limit of detection (LODs) for the selected amino acid, Arg, were determined by fluorometric titration using fixed concentrations of the polymeric probe and polymer complex, respectively, while varying the Arg concentration. The resulting fluorescence intensities were plotted against the concentration of Arg, employing a regression equation to generate a calibration curve. Subsequently, the LODs were calculated using the equation LOD = 3σ/K, where σ was the blank polymer and monomer’s standard deviation (SD) and K was the slope of a plot of fluorescence intensity versus Arg concentration.

Estimation of Stern–Volmer Constant (K sv)

Fluorescence quenching titration was further evaluated using the Stern–Volmer (SV) equation (I 0/I) = 1+ K sv [Q], where I 0 was the emission intensity in the absence of quencher, I was the emission intensity in the presence of quencher, [Q] is the molar concentration of the analyte, and K sv was the Stern–Volmer constant or the quenching constant (M–1).

The Benesi–Hildebrand equation, 1/(I−I0)=1/{Kass(Imax−I0)[M]+1/(Imax−I0)} , was used to calculate the apparent association constants (K ass) for the probe. The I max was the maximum fluorescence intensity in the presence of an amino acid in solution. A graph was obtained by plotting 1/(I 0 – I) against 1/[M]. The values of K ass were calculated using the slope and line of the plot. The binding stoichiometry was determined from Job’s plot.

Selective Sensing of Arg by Cou-Tyr-Boc, NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc, Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc, and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH2 Probe with Competing Amino Acids

To evaluate the selectivity of the Cou-Tyr-Boc, NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc, Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc, and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 probes toward Arg, the fluorescence quenching of a Cou-Tyr-Boc (10 μM), NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc (50 μM), Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (1 μM), and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 (1 μM) in DMSO solution was monitored upon the addition of various competing amino acids for Cou-Tyr-Boc, NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc, Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc, and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 . These amino acids included Arg, Lys, His, Cys, HCys, GSH, Ala, Val, Met, Pro, Leu, Phe, Gln, and Asp. Subsequently, an aqueous solution of Arg (Cou-Tyr-Boc, NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc, and Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (15 μM), and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 (45 μM)) was introduced to each mixture, and the fluorescent intensities were recorded. The competitive assessment was conducted based on the observed differences in fluorescence quenching.

Detection of Arg in Biological Fluids

Before evaluating of Arg detection by the polymer probes in biofluids, the fluorescence intensity of Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (1 μM) and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 (1 μM) in DMSO was measured following the addition of human serum (Ser-T-Fy 1 Level 1 Human Control Serum, reconstituted in 5.0 mL deionized water), artificial urine, and artificial saliva, in the case of Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (2.25 μL), and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 (6.75 μL), to determine an appropriate medium that did not disrupt the probe’s fluorescence. Based on this assessment, artificial saliva was selected for further experiments. Fluorescence quenching was then evaluated after the addition of Arg (Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (15 μM) and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 (45 μM)) prepared stock solution of Arg (20,000 μM) in 100% artificial saliva.

Cell and Cell Culture

A mouse fibroblast cell line L929 (ATCC-CCL-1), a human lung carcinoma cell line A549 (ATCC-CCL-185), and a human ventricular cardiomyocyte cell line AC16 (ATCC-CRL-3568) were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA). All cells were cultured in ATCC-recommended medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 5000 units/mL of penicillin/streptomycin. Cells were maintained in a humidified atmosphere of 95% air and 5% carbon dioxide at 37 °C until reaching 70–80% confluence, after which experiments were performed.

Cell Viability Assay of Polymeric Fluorescence Probes

Cells were seeded into 96-well cell culture plates at 2 × 105 cells per well and incubated for 24 h at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere of 95% air and 5% carbon dioxide. Subsequently, the cells were treated with polymers at concentrations of 0.1, 1, 10, and 100 μg/mL for 24 h. The control was culture medium in the presence of 0.001% DMSO (vehicle control). After the incubation period, the cultured medium was discarded and replaced with 200 μL of 0.5 mg/mL MTT in PBS (3-[4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl]-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) (MTT reagent, Sigma-Aldrich) and further incubated at 37 °C for 2 h. Then, the MTT reagent was discarded, the intracellular formazan crystals were dissolved in DMSO, and the optical density (OD) was measured at 490 nm with DMSO as the blank. The relative percentage of cell viability was compared to the control group.

Statistical Analysis

The statistical tests were performed using commercially available software (GraphPad Prism). All values were expressed as Mean ± SD. All comparisons were assessed for significance using ANOVA, followed by the Tukey-Kramer test when appropriate. A p-value of less than 0.05 indicated significance in one-way ANOVA (***P = 0.0008,****P < 0.0001).

Results and Discussion

Monomer Synthesis and Design Rationale

Building upon our previous success in developing polynorbornene -based sensors functionalized with coumarin-tryptophan (Cou-Trp) and coumarin-cysteine (Cou-Cys) conjugates, this study extended the scope of these functionalized polymers to incorporate the tyrosine (Tyr) moiety. Our earlier research demonstrated that Cou-Trp-modified polymers act as multifunctional chemosensors for Fe3+, Cu2+, and Arg, while Cou-Cys systems were specifically engineered for Cd2+ detection. Integrating tyrosine into this coumarin-based platform offers a distinctive chemical opportunity. As an amino acid bearing a phenolic side chain, tyrosine introduces unique electronic characteristics and molecular recognition motifs such as π–π stacking or cation−π interactions, which differ markedly from the indole (Trp) or thiol (Cys) moieties. Because the selectivity of chemosensors is fundamentally governed by the chemical architecture of the ligand, the transition toward a coumarin–tyrosine (Cou-Tyr) framework represented a deliberate strategy to modulate molecular recognition behavior and enhance the quenching efficiency of the resulting polymeric system.

In this design, coumarin serves as the signaling unit due to its superior photostability, high fluorescence intensity, and proven biocompatibility. , It remains a premier chromophore building block for advanced fluorescent probes due to its versatile visible-range emission. The monomer was synthesized by first preparing a coumarin-containing carboxylic group (Cou-COOH) via the hydrolysis of Cou-OEt, which afforded a moderate yield of 58%. This was subsequently conjugated with the tyrosine moiety (Boc-Tyr-OMe) to generate Cou-Tyr-Boc. Finally, this ligand was covalently tethered to an exo-5-norbornene carboxylic acid scaffold through esterification, which yielded the target monomer, NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc, as illustrated in Scheme .

The spectra exhibited well-resolved signals with integration ratios fully consistent with the proposed molecular structures, particularly the characteristic vinyl protons of the norbornene unit and the aromatic resonances arising from the coumarin and tyrosine moieties. Additionally, the FT-IR spectra of all synthesized compounds, which displayed the diagnostic carbonyl stretching vibrations of ester and amide functionalities, were compiled in Figure S3, further corroborating the successful construction of the designed molecular framework.

Polymer Synthesis and Characterization

The NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc monomer was polymerized via ROMP using the Umicore M31 ruthenium catalyst in THF at 38 °C for 18 h. The reaction proceeded efficiently, affording Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc in a high isolated yield of 70%. Gel Permeation Chromatography (GPC) analysis indicated a number-average molecular weight (M n) of 35,600 and a polydispersity index (PDI) of 1.68. The successful formation of the polymer was further validated by 1H NMR spectroscopy (Figure b), which demonstrated the complete disappearance of the characteristic olefinic protons of norbornene monomer and the emergence of broad signals at approximately 5.32 ppm, attributable to the newly formed internal vinyl protons of the polynorbornene backbone.

To systematically evaluate the influence of pendant group functionality on the photophysical properties and sensing performance of the polymer, the tert-butyloxycarbonyl (Boc) protecting groups were selectively removed using trifluoroacetic acid (TFA), yielding Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 . This deprotection step was particularly significant, as it converted the carbamate moiety into a free primary amine, thereby markedly modifying the hydrogen-bonding capability, charge distribution, and local electronic environment of the tyrosine–coumarin chromophoric system. The structure of the resulting Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 was confirmed by 1H NMR spectroscopy, as shown in Figure c. The complete disappearance of the resonance at approximately 0.8 ppm, corresponding to the tert-butyl protons of the Boc protecting groups, unambiguously verified the quantitative deprotection process.

Owing to the substantial increase in polarity following deprotection, Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 exhibited limited solubility in THF, precluding direct GPC analysis under identical conditions. Consequently, its molecular weight was estimated to be 29,700 based on the M n value of the parent Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc precursor, assuming negligible backbone degradation during the deprotection process. This dual-polymer design strategy enabled a direct comparison between Boc-protected and free amine-functionalized systems, thereby providing a robust platform for elucidating how subtle variations in chemical architecture governed photophysical behavior and fluorescent sensing performance.

The Fluorescence Response of Polymeric Probes toward Amino Acids

Solvent Selection and Photophysical Characterization

To optimize the sensing conditions, the absorption and emission spectra of the probes were first examined in various solvents, including deionized (DI) water, DMF, DMSO, ethanol (EtOH), and THF. All probes (Cou-Tyr-Boc, NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc, Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc, and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 ) exhibited a prominent UV–Vis absorption maximum at 350 nm, characteristic of the coumarin chromophore, along with a weaker absorption band at approximately 280 nm, attributable to the tyrosine moiety. In addition, broad absorption bands were observed in the range of 390–430 nm in DI water, DMF, and DMSO (Figure S4a-d), highlighting a pronounced solvent-dependent behavior.

Initial fluorescence investigations were conducted using Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc as a representative polymeric probe. Upon excitation at 350 nm, the fluorescence emission spectra (Figure a) revealed that Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (1 μM) emitted at 411 nm in common organic solvents (DMF, DMSO, EtOH, and THF), whereas a distinct red-shift to 445 nm was observed in DI water. This bathochromic shift likely arose from enhanced solvent–chromophore interactions and increased stabilization of the excited state in highly polar aqueous media. Based on these results, DMSO was selected as the primary solvent for subsequent investigations owing to its superior ability to solubilize the hydrophobic polynorbornene backbone while maintaining favorable photophysical characteristics. Moreover, DMSO is widely recognized as a biocompatible organic cosolvent commonly employed in cellular studies and biochemical assays, rendering it particularly suitable for the development of fluorescent probes intended for bioanalytical and biofluid applications.

Selectivity Screening and Analyte Discovery

With the solvent system established, we conducted a comprehensive selectivity screening against a broad array of potential interferents, including representative metal ions (Fe3+, Fe2+, Cu2+, Zn2+, Hg2+, and Cd2+) and various amino acids, based on our previous reports ,, (Figure b). In contrast to our earlier Trp- and Cys-based sensing platforms, which primarily targeted metal-ion detection, , the present coumarin-tyrosine triad exhibited a distinct and unprecedented selectivity profile. The screening results revealed that only Arg and Lys (15 μM) induced pronounced fluorescence quenching of Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (1 μM) at 411 nm, reaching 91% and 85%, respectively, accompanied by a significant bathochromic shift of approximately 50 nm, whereas all other tested amino acids and metal ions produced negligible spectral changes (Figure b). This fluorescence “ON-OFF” response suggested that the tyrosine moiety integrated within the coumarin framework provided a favorable recognition environment for basic amino acids, particularly Arg.

For our experimental configuration, concentrated stock solutions of amino acids were initially prepared in DI water, and small aliquots (0.15–4.0 μL) were subsequently introduced into the DMSO-based probe solutions. Importantly, this minor water content exerted no discernible influence on either fluorescence intensity or probe solubility, which was attributed to the excellent miscibility of water in DMSO and the negligible dilution effect within the investigated concentration range.

A pivotal discovery emerged upon extending the investigation to evaluate the fluorescence response of Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc toward Arg across all solvent systems. As shown in Figure c and d, the sensing performance exhibited pronounced solvent dependence; remarkably, selective quenching and bathochromic shift were observed exclusively in DMSO. This unexpected solvent exclusivity pointed toward a “solvent-mediated recognition” mechanism. In protic solvents, such as DI water and EtOH, strong solvation and hydrogen-bonding interactions likely competed with probe–analyte binding, thereby attenuating effective interactions between the tyrosine–coumarin dyad and the guanidinium group of Arg. In contrast, DMSO, as a polar aprotic solvent, provided sufficient stabilization of the polymeric framework while minimizing competitive hydrogen bonding, thus enabling the specific π–π stacking and directional hydrogen-bonding interactions required for efficient fluorescence quenching. This observation underscored that the sensing behavior was governed not only by molecular design but also by the delicate interplay between polymer architecture and solvent environment. Further mechanistic insights into the probe–Arg binding interactions, obtained through 1H NMR titration studies, are discussed in a subsequent section.

Photophysical Properties and Selective Recognition of Basic Amino Acids

Sensing Trends in Precursors: From Intermediate to Monomer

We first evaluated the photophysical response of the intermediate dyad (Cou-Tyr-Boc) and the monomer (NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc) in DMSO. Both species exhibited a characteristic fluorescence emission maximum at 411 nm. Upon screening a panel of 14 representative amino acids, both precursors exhibited pronounced fluorescence responses toward the basic amino acids Arg and Lys. Notably, conjugation of the coumarin–tyrosine dyad onto the norbornene scaffold resulted in a measurable decrease in baseline fluorescence intensity and a subtle shift in sensing sensitivity (Figures S5a and S6a). Furthermore, both the intermediate and monomer displayed minor interference from histidine (His) and glutathione (GSH) (Figures S5b and S6b), which was likely due to the greater fluorophore accessibility in small-molecule systems, enabling nonspecific interactions. However, competitive binding assays established that Arg induced the most pronounced ratiometric quenching response, accompanied by a distinct bathochromic shift (Figures S5c and S6c). Quantitatively, Cou-Tyr-Boc and NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc exhibited substantial fluorescence quenching, reaching 89–91% in the presence of Arg, while Lys induced the second-highest quenching efficiency of 80–85% (Figures S5b and S6b). In contrast, all other tested amino acids elicited negligible fluorescence perturbations, confirming the high selectivity of the sensing motif toward basic residues (Figures S5c and S6c).

The pronounced sensitivity toward Arg and Lys can be rationalized by the strong dependence of tyrosine fluorescence on local microenvironment polarity and electronic interactions. Specifically, the guanidinium and ammonium functionalities of Arg and Lys, respectively, could modulate the electronically conjugated structure of the coumarin–tyrosine dyad through synergistic hydrogen bonding, electrostatic interactions, and orbital overlap involving lone-pair electrons of oxygen and nitrogen atoms. Moreover, it has been reported that Lys/Arg utilize their basic feature, positive charge, or nucleophilicity of Lys/Arg, thereby facilitating fluorescence quenching behavior. ,,,

Fluorescent titration experiments (Figures S5d and S6d) revealed that, despite employing a higher probe concentration for the monomer (50 μM) relative to the intermediate (10 μM), NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc exhibited higher limits of detection (LOD) for Arg (137.04 nM) and Lys (685.01 nM) (Table ). Consistently, the NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc monomer displayed a similar but slightly attenuated quenching trend, with 89% fluorescence quenching for Arg and 80% for Lys, as shown in Figure S6b. These results indicated that incorporation of the bulky bicyclic norbornene ester imposed steric and electronic shielding effects that partially restricted analyte access to the active recognition sites, thereby reducing overall sensing sensitivity. ,

The “Polymer Effect” and Amplified Quenching

To validate the deprotection and compare the optical responsiveness of the two systems, a pH-dependent fluorescence study was performed over a wide pH range (1–12). Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 exhibited a significantly enhanced fluorescence intensity relative to its Boc-protected precursor, Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc, throughout the responsive pH range. While Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc displayed a weak and nearly invariant emission across all tested pH values (Figure S7a), Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 demonstrated a pronounced hyperchromic effect. Specifically, under acidic conditions (pH 3 and 5), Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 exhibited a sharp increase in fluorescence intensity (Figure S7c), substantially outperforming its Boc-protected counterpart. At pH 1, a slight decrease in emission intensity was observed, which was attributed to excessive protonation or acid-induced quenching effects. In the neutral-to-basic region (pH 7–12), Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 maintained a lower yet clearly distinguishable fluorescence signal (Figure S7c).

Visual inspection under UV irradiation (254 and 365 nm) further highlighted this striking contrast; Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 produced a markedly brighter and more vivid fluorescence signal (Figure S7d) compared to Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (Figure S7b). This substantial enhancement in emission intensity was attributed to the presence of the free −NH2 group (pK a ∼ 9.5–11), which acted as a strong electron-donating moiety. In Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 , this electron donor promoted efficient intramolecular charge transfer (ICT), generating a pronounced charge-separated excited state that was absent in the Boc-protected polymer. Consistent with previous reports, the introduction of electron-donating amine functionalities within amino acid–based fluorophores induced notable spectral shifts and fluorescence amplification. , Consequently, the removal of the Boc protecting group significantly amplifies the optical response of the polymeric probe.

Amplified Quenching and Arginine Sensing

Following the characterization of their intrinsic properties, the fluorescence sensing performance of the polymeric probes was systematically evaluated. Both Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 exhibited pronounced fluorescence quenching upon exposure to Arg and Lys, closely mirroring the response trends observed for their monomeric precursors. In the presence of Arg, fluorescence intensities decreased by 93% for Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc at 15 μM and by 83% for Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 at 45 μM, whereas Lys induced approximately 78% quenching in both polymeric probes. The higher Arg concentration required for Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 was attributed to its significantly stronger intrinsic fluorescence, arising from its enhanced hydrophilicity and reduced aggregation tendency compared to the Boc-protected analogue. Under 365 nm UV irradiation, these spectral changes were accompanied by visible color transitions from blue to aqua-blue, as illustrated in the embedded photographs in Figures a and a. Competitive assays in the presence of other amino acids further confirmed the high selectivity of both polymeric probes toward Arg (Figures c and c). The preferential recognition of Arg was rationalized by its strongly basic nature, the guanidinium group of Arg possesses the highest isoelectric point (pI ∼ 10.8), which could promote strong hydrogen-bonding interactions with the −NH group of the tyrosine moiety in both Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 , leading to the formation of stable hydrogen-bonded complexes. This interaction altered the electronic environment of the fluorophore framework and consequently suppressed the intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) process, resulting in substantial fluorescence quenching. ,

The colorimetric sensing behavior of the polymeric probes was further evaluated in DMSO under ambient light and 254 nm UV irradiation. As shown in Figures d-f, Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc exhibited a distinct color transition from colorless to pale yellow upon the addition of Arg under ambient conditions (Figure d), while a more pronounced color contrast was observed under 254 nm UV illumination (Figure e). In comparison, Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 displayed even more striking colorimetric responses, transitioning from colorless to yellow under ambient light (Figure d) and from pale violet to intense aqua-blue under 254 nm UV irradiation (Figure e), highlighting its strong potential for naked-eye detection of Arg.

To further elucidate these optical transitions, UV–Vis absorption spectroscopy was employed to investigate the absorbance behavior of the polymeric probes before and after the addition of Arg. Both probes (1 μM) in DMSO exhibited prominent absorption bands centered at 350 nm, characteristic of the coumarin chromophore. Notably, Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 displayed significantly higher absorbance intensity compared to Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (Figures f and f). Upon the addition of Arg (15 μM for Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc and 45 μM for Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 ), pronounced red shifts were observed, with new absorption bands emerging at approximately 440 nm. UV–Vis titration experiments revealed a ratiometric decrease in absorbance at 350 nm accompanied by a progressive increase at ∼440 nm upon incremental addition of Arg (Figures g and g). While Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc primarily exhibited a bathochromic shift, Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 displayed both bathochromic and hyperchromic responses. These spectral changes suggested that the ICT band transition might arise from deprotonation of the chemosensor polymer by Arg, consistent with the mechanism proposed by Bhattacharya. ,

Consequently, stepwise Arg titration experiments confirmed that the absorbance at 350 nm decreased gradually, while the newly generated band at 440 nm intensified in proportion to the Arg concentration. The appearance of this new absorption band provided compelling evidence for the formation of Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc–Arg and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 –Arg complexes. In addition, a distinct isosbestic point at approximately 392 nm was observed, indicating a clean interconversion between free probe and probe–Arg complex. Such behavior was consistent with previously reported salen-type Schiff base–Arg systems, where newly formed absorption bands and well-defined isosbestic points indicated controlled complex formation. The pronounced UV–Vis spectral changes and the presence of a well-defined isosbestic behavior suggest the formation of a stable ground-state complex between the polymeric probes and Arg. This physical association indicates that static quenching contributes to the signal transduction process, as the formation of a nonfluorescent complex directly alters the absorption profile of the system. To fully understand the underlying quenching mechanism, further investigation into the emission lifetimes will be conducted.

Together, these spectroscopic features strongly corroborated the proposed polymer-Arg binding mechanism. Moreover, the distinct color changes from colorless to light yellow under ambient conditions (Figures d and d) demonstrated the feasibility of naked-eye detection using the same probe and analyte concentrations employed in fluorescence measurements, thereby highlighting the dual-mode sensing capability of these polymeric probes.

Fluorescence Titration Experiment and Estimation of LOD, Stern–Volmer Plots, Quenching Constant (K sv), Association Constant (K ass), Benesi–Hildebrand Plots

The fluorescent titration experiments (Figures h, h, S5d, and S6d) were systematically analyzed to quantitatively evaluate the sensitivity of the probes, including intermediate, monomer, and polymeric probes, through determination of the limit of detection (LOD), Stern–Volmer quenching constant (K sv), association constant (K ass), and Job’s plot analysis (Table ). The decrease in fluorescent intensity at 411 nm was used as the analytical signal for consistent determination of the LOD values across all probes. The LOD values were calculated using the equation LOD = 3σ/K, where σ represents the standard deviation of the blank probe solution, and K corresponds to the slope of the linear calibration curve obtained from plots of fluorescence intensity versus Arg concentration.

As previously discussed, the monomer NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc exhibited a higher LOD (lower sensitivity) toward Arg (137.04 nM) relative to the intermediate Cou-Tyr-Boc. This behavior was attributed to partial attenuation of the electronic response upon conjugation of the fluorophore to the norbornene moiety. Nevertheless, the detection limit remained within the nanomolar regime, underscoring the high intrinsic sensitivity of the monomer toward Arg. In contrast, the LOD value for Lys was approximately 5-fold higher (685.01 nM) than that for Arg, clearly demonstrating superior selectivity and sensitivity toward Arg. These findings indicated that NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc is capable of detecting Arg at concentrations nearly five times lower than those required for Lys detection.

Stern–Volmer plots (Figure S5e for Cou-Tyr-Boc at 10 μM and Figure S6e for NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc at 50 μM) exhibited excellent linearity, yielding K sv values of 5.60 × 105 M–1 and 0.96 × 105 M–1, respectively. This linear relationship across the tested concentration range signifies a consistent quenching process and underscores the high sensitivity of both the intermediate and monomeric species toward Arg. Job’s plots analysis revealed a turning point when a molar fraction of Arg was about 0.3, suggesting a binding stoichiometry of Arg (A):probe (L) = 1:3 for Cou-Tyr-Boc (Figure S5f and Table ) whereas a 1:2 stoichiometry was observed for the monomer NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc (Figure S6f). These results suggested that one Arg molecule coordinated with three intermediate molecules or two monomer molecules, respectively. The association constants (K ass) derived from Benesi–Hildebrand plots (Figures S8a and S8b) were calculated to be 7.41 × 104 for Cou-Tyr-Boc and 4.47 × 104 M–1 for NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc, respectively. This variation indicates that in these smaller, more flexible systems, multiple probe molecules can cluster around a single Arginine guest to satisfy the hydrogen-bonding requirements of the guanidinium group. The transition from a 1:3 ratio in the intermediate to a 1:2 ratio in the monomer suggests that even the addition of the norbornene framework begins to impose steric constraints that limit this molecular clustering. Collectively, these quantitative parameters confirmed the strong binding affinity and high sensitivity of both intermediate and monomeric probes toward Arg.

Upon polymerization, the sensing performance was dramatically amplified, highlighting the intrinsic material advantage of ROMP-derived polymeric sensors. Remarkably, although the polymeric probes (Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 ) were evaluated at a substantially lower concentration (1 μM) compared to the monomer (50 μM), they exhibited significantly lower LOD values, indicating superior sensitivity. Specifically, the LOD for Arg in Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc reached 71.42 nM, representing a substantial improvement over the monomer despite a 50-fold reduction in probe concentration, while Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 exhibited an LOD of 103.64 nM (Table ). This pronounced signal amplification was attributed to the cooperative effect of densely distributed coumarin–tyrosine recognition units along the polymer backbone, which provided multiple binding sites and promoted multivalent interactions with Arg. In contrast, the slightly higher LOD observed for Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 was suspected to arise from increased steric hindrance and altered solvation behavior associated with the polar −NH2 groups, which may partially impede analyte accessibility.

The removal of the Boc protecting group markedly altered the photophysical properties of the polymeric probe. Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 exhibited a substantially higher baseline fluorescence intensity than Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (Figures h and h). This enhanced emission upon deprotection was attributed to the elimination of the electron-withdrawing carbamate moiety, which reduced intramolecular quenching and promoted radiative decay. Despite its higher intrinsic brightness, Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 required a higher amino acid concentration (45 μM) to achieve comparable fluorescence quenching relative to the Boc-protected analogue (15 μM). This behavior suggested that the Boc group provided a more rigid and hydrophobic microenvironment, facilitating the initial docking and stronger interaction of basic amino acids, thereby enhancing quenching efficiency at lower analyte concentrations.

Consistent with this enhanced sensing behavior, the K sv values of the Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (11.0 × 105) and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 (1.39 × 105) were approximately 10-fold and 1.5-fold higher, respectively, than those of the monomer, even at 50 times lower probe concentration. These results unequivocally demonstrated the superior quenching efficiency and sensitivity imparted by polymerization. The Stern–Volmer plots displayed excellent linearity with correlation coefficients (R 2) of 0.9813 for Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (Figure i) and 0.9669 for Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 (Figure i), indicating a predominantly dynamic quenching mechanism operative at 411 nm during polymer–Arg complex formation. The ratiometric capabilities of the polymeric probes in DMSO were investigated to establish their intrinsic sensing behavior. Both Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 exhibited high ratiometric linearity in their fluorescence (I 460/I 411) (Figure S9a and b). This was further supported by their absorbance profiles (A 444/A 337 and A 437/A 355), confirming a robust dual-channel signal transduction mechanism (Figure S9c and d).

Job’s plot analysis further revealed a tipping point at a molar fraction of approximately 0.5, demonstrating a clear transition to a 1:1 binding stoichiometry upon polymerization (Figures j and j), in contrast to the higher stoichiometries observed for the monomer and intermediate. This shift suggests that the polymer backbone effectively preorganizes the sensing units, generating optimized binding pockets that captured Arg more efficiently than the more flexible monomeric precursors. A 1:1 stoichiometry implies that each repeating unit within the polymer chain forms a host–guest interaction with one Arg molecule. The association constants (K ass), derived from Benesi–Hildebrand plots (Figure S8c and d), were calculated as 2.53 × 104 M–1 for Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc and 1.35 × 104 M–1 for Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 , further corroborating the strong probe–Arg interactions.

As summarized in Table , the polymeric probes exhibited highly competitive limits of detection for both basic amino acids. Corresponding fluorescent titration experiments (Figure S10a-d), Stern–Volmer plot (Figure S10e-h), Job’s plots (Figure S10i-l), and Benesi–Hildebrand analyses for Lys are provided in Figure S10m-p. Although linear Stern–Volmer relationships were also obtained for Lys, the significantly lower K sv values relative to those observed for Arg indicated weaker quenching efficiency, consistent with the higher quenching percentages observed for Arg at equivalent concentrations. This result suggested a stronger preference of the probes toward the guanidinium moiety of Arg over the alkyl ammonium group of Lys. Such preferential recognition can be attributed to the distinct structural and electronic characteristics of the two analytes. Consequently, the enhanced structural complementarity between Arg and the probe framework promotes the formation of a more stable supramolecular complex, leading to the amplified ratiometric response observed for Arg.

Furthermore, both monomer and polymer probes displayed similar stoichiometric binding trends toward Lys as observed for Arg. Specifically, Cou-Tyr-Boc exhibited a 1:2 binding stoichiometry with Lys (Figure S10i), whereas NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc showed a 1:3 stoichiometry (Figure S10j). Both polymeric probes demonstrated a 1:1 binding stoichiometry (Figure S10k and l), suggesting that each polymer repeating unit coordinates with one Lys molecule. These results collectively confirmed the strong affinity of Arg and Lys toward the −NH and CO functional groups within the probe architecture, leading to pronounced fluorescence quenching. Nevertheless, further in-depth investigations are required to fully elucidate the detailed binding interactions in NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc–Arg and Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc–Arg complexes.

1H NMR Titration

To elucidate the molecular-level binding interactions and sensing mechanism, 1H NMR titration experiments were performed using NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc as a representative probe, owing to its well-resolved spectral features, which enable precise monitoring of chemical shift variations upon analyte addition, in contrast to the inherently broadened signals of the polymeric systems. The experiments were conducted in CDCl3 solution, and incremental additions of Arg (0.002–0.010 equiv) were introduced into a solution containing 1 equiv of NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc. Due to the limited solubility of Arg in nonpolar organic solvents, the titrations were restricted to the micromolar equivalent range; however, even at these trace concentrations, distinct and systematic chemical shift perturbations were observed, underscoring the high affinity of the interaction in a noncompetitive environment.

As depicted in Figure a, systematic chemical shift perturbations were observed upon Arg addition, strongly supporting the formation of a probe–analyte complex. While protons spatially distant from the binding site (such as coumarin position 1) exhibited only marginal perturbations (∼8.705 to 8.707 ppm), the protons directly adjacent to the proposed recognition pocket displayed pronounced and systematic shifts. Accompanying upfield shifts at positions 2 and 3 were observed. In addition, the continuous, concentration-dependent downfield displacement of the secondary amide proton (−NH) cannot be attributed to referencing uncertainty, providing robust evidence of direct involvement of both the amide nitrogen and carbonyl oxygen atoms in hydrogen bonding during complex formation. Upon Arg binding, the chemical environment of this proton is altered, further confirming participation of the coumarin CO group in the binding process. Furthermore, the methoxy group (−OCH3) on the tyrosine moiety exhibited a pronounced upfield shift, indicative of changes in local electron density and shielding effects induced by Arg binding. This perturbation propagated through the molecular framework, influencing the adjacent aliphatic –CH2 protons (position 4), which also shifted upfield. Similarly, the neighboring norbornene –CH2 protons (positions 5 and 6) underwent noticeable upfield shifts, consistent with secondary interactions between Arg and the norbornene ester carbonyl groups. The tert-butoxycarbonyl (Boc) protecting group likewise displayed a distinct upfield shift from approximately 1.434 to 1.431 ppm, further supporting global conformational rearrangement upon complex formation.

(a) 1H NMR titration experiment of NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc with Arg in CDCl3 and (b) the proposed binding mode of norbornene, coumarin, and tyrosine pendant groups with Arg.

While direct Density Functional Theory (DFT) modeling of the complete polynorbornene architecture is computationally prohibitive due to the polymer’s size and conformational complexity, our experimental 1H NMR findings are strongly supported by recent computational literature. A recent DFT study on an analogous small-molecule coumarin-based fluorescent probe for Arginine explicitly confirmed that molecular recognition is primarily driven by a robust, multivalent hydrogen-bonding network. This computational precedent for coumarin-arginine interactions perfectly correlates with our experimental NMR and optical data, further validating our proposed salt-bridge and hydrogen-bonding mechanism.

The observed bathochromic shifts collectively originated from a synergistic and highly specific supramolecular interaction. As supported by the 1H NMR titrations, the primary driving force is the formation of a robust, multivalent hydrogen-bonding complex. This complex is anchored by interactions between the highly basic guanidinium group of Arg and the carbonyl oxygen atoms of the tyrosine and coumarin moieties, complemented by hydrogen bonding between the tyrosine −NH donor and the carbonyl oxygen of the Arg guest. This strong interaction is further stabilized by an extensive hydrogen-bonding network where the probe’s carbonyl groups act as potent acceptors for the guanidinium −NH donors. Additionally, the electron-rich aromatic systems of the coumarin and tyrosine units provide stabilization via cation−π interactions with the positively charged guest. This multivalent binding event tightly restricts the local electronic microenvironment around the fluorophore, modulating the Photoinduced Electron Transfer (PET) processes and amplifying the donor–acceptor electronic architecture of the coumarin–tyrosine chromophore. These concerted spectral changes provided compelling evidence for the formation of a stable intermolecular host–guest complex. Accordingly, a chemically defensible binding model for the NB-Cou-Tyr-Boc–Arg complex is proposed and illustrated in Figure b, highlighting multivalent hydrogen-bonding acceptance, electrostatic stabilization, and cation−π interaction pathways.

Benchmarking Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 against reported sensors (Table S1) highlights their high sensitivity and analytical efficiency. The K sv of the Boc-functionalized polymer (11.0 × 105 M–1) significantly exceeds structural analogues, such as a recently reported metal-complexed polynorbornene (1.99 × 105 M–1)7, while the NH2 derivative remains highly competitive (1.39 × 105 M–1). With LODs of 0.071 and 0.103 μM, respectively, both polymers rank among the most sensitive ratiometric Arg probes to date. Unlike many existing platforms that necessitate external metal-ion coordination or specific polyelectrolyte assemblies, this high performance stems from a polymeric effect. Here, the norbornene backbone organizes the recognition units into a multivalent pocket that optimizes synergistic hydrogen bonding and cation−π interactions within a purely organic framework.

To further validate the proposed photoinduced electron transfer (PET)-based quenching mechanism, phosphorescence lifetime measurements were performed. , The phosphorescence lifetimes (τ) of Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc and its Arg complex were founded to be 29.1920 and 9.7470 ms, respectively. Similarly, the lifetimes of Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 and its corresponding Arg complex were determined as 27.2063 and 18.6142 ms, respectively (Figure a and b). This substantial reduction of lifetime upon Arg addition provided definite evidence of dynamic quenching mediated by PET from the excited Cou-Tyr-Boc chromophore to Arg, thereby corroborating the proposed sensing mechanism. In addition, the static quenching resulting from ground-state complexation as evidenced by the UV–vis isosbestic point, the sensing mechanism of these systems is characterized by a mixed quenching model. In this framework, the ground-state complexation provides the structural basis for molecular recognition, while the dynamic PET process drives the high-sensitivity signal response.

The Fluorescence Response of Polymeric Probes toward Arg in Biofluid Sensing

The development of high-performance chemosensors for biological systems requires not only exceptional selectivity but also robust operational stability in complex biological matrices. This study built upon our established expertise in coumarin-based polymeric probes, representing a significant conceptual advancement in both sensing mechanism and analytical capability. In our previous work, coumarin–tryptophan-based polymers were developed for the sequential detection of Cu2+/Fe3+ions and Arg through an “OFF-ON” fluorescence restoration mechanism. Subsequently, norbornene-derived coumarin-cysteine polymers and hybrid multiwalled carbon nanotube (MWCNT) systems demonstrated selective fluorescence quenching toward Cd2+, followed by restoration upon anion binding. Although these platforms successfully enabled Cd2+ detection in artificial saliva, facilitating early-stage POC diagnostic development, their application in serum and urine was hindered by substantial background interference, most likely arising from competitive binding with serum proteins and complex biological constituents.

In the present work, we significantly advanced this architecture by shifting the sensing paradigm toward a ratiometric detection mechanism, which inherently offers superior analytical reliability in complex biological environments. Unlike our previous “ON-OFF-ON” restoration-based probes, both Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 exhibited distinct ground-state ratiometric behavior, as evidenced by UV–vis titration experiments (Figures and ). In both polymeric systems, incremental addition of Arg induced a ratiometric decrease of the absorption band at 350 nm, accompanied by a concomitant increase of a newly formed band at approximately 440 nm. This self-calibrating absorbance shift established the mechanistic foundation for ratiometric fluorescence detection in complex biological media, providing an internal reference signal that minimized measurement errors arising from probe concentration fluctuations, excitation intensity variations, and environmental perturbations.

Systematic evaluation of biofluid matrices revealed that while serum and urine induced pronounced background fluorescence quenching (Figure S11a and b), saliva exhibited negligible matrix interference (Figure a and d), establishing saliva as the optimal medium for our POC sensing platform. Notably, Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc demonstrated a distinct ratiometric fluorescence response in saliva, characterized by a gradual decrease in emission intensity at 411 nm and the simultaneous emergence of a new emission band centered at ∼460 nm upon Arg addition (Figure b). This dual-emission behavior enabled ratiometric quantification, which was confirmed by the high linearity plot of I 460/I 411 (Figure S12a). This provided intrinsic calibration, ensuring high analytical precision independent of probe concentration. Fluorescence titration studies of Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc (1 μM) in saliva exhibited excellent linearity across an Arg concentration range of 2–20 μM (Figure c), yielding an ultralow limit of detection (LOD) of 33.09 nM.

In contrast, Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 functioned primarily via intensity-based quenching in the salivary environment. As depicted in Figure e, Arg addition (5–45 μM) resulted in a dramatic quenching of the 411 nm emission; however, the corresponding I 460/I 411 plot did not exhibit linearity (Figure S12b). While the salivary matrix appeared to suppress the secondary ratiometric emission peak for the NH2 variant, the probe maintained exceptional sensitivity with an LOD of 32.45 nM (Figure f). Interestingly, the improved sensitivity observed in artificial saliva compared with that observed in organic media might have arisen from the complex composition of the saliva matrix, which could have facilitated interactions between the tyrosine −NH site, the CO group of the coumarin moiety, and the guanidinium group of Arg. Notably, both probes detected Arg well below the typical tolerable limit in human saliva (∼5.0 μM). , However, the Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc probe offered a distinct clinical advantage by operating at lower Arg concentrations, underscoring its superior sensitivity for trace-level detection.

Overall, a distinct matrix-dependent shift was observed upon transitioning from organic media to artificial saliva. While Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc maintained its ratiometric performance, the deprotected Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 functioned primarily as an intensity-based sensor. This behavior is attributed to the increased polarity and altered solvation of the free amine groups in the saliva-like aqueous environment, which suppresses the secondary emission band. While the ratiometric mode remains a powerful tool for qualitative signal verification and self-calibration, the high-sensitivity quantification (LOD) was derived from the primary quenching at 411 nm to ensure maximum analytical precision at nanomolar concentrations.

These findings provided compelling proof of concept for the potential application of these polymeric probes in future POC diagnostic platforms. The ultrasensitive detection capability (LOD < 34 nM) was particularly significant given that salivary Arg concentrations are known to fluctuate under pathological conditions, including chronic kidney disease and some cancers. In such disorders, Arg levels can deviate substantially from the typical micromolar range, rendering highly sensitive and selective analytical tools indispensable for early diagnosis and disease monitoring. By utilizing saliva as a diagnostic medium which offers noninvasive sampling, cost-efficiency, and reduced risk of pathogen transmission, this polymeric sensing platform provided a highly practical and clinically relevant solution. , Moreover, accumulating evidence suggests that salivary amino acid profiles serve as valuable biomarkers for various cancers, with perturbations in amino acid metabolism playing a critical role in oncogenic progression. While artificial saliva was used here to ensure reproducibility and provide a controlled matrix for benchmarking, future investigations will focus on the comparative analysis of salivary and serum Arg levels in healthy individuals to establish physiological correlations. Additionally, the diagnostic potency of this fluorescent probe should be evaluated across various clinical conditions by correlating salivary concentrations with established blood-based benchmarks.

The practical reliability of the sensing platform was confirmed through stability and reproducibility assessments. The polymeric probes exhibited high optical and structural stability, maintaining consistent fluorescence response without signal degradation throughout the assays. Furthermore, independent synthesis runs demonstrated excellent batch-to-batch reproducibility, yielding highly consistent sensing parameters. While the current “ON-OFF” mechanism is designed for single-use diagnostics, this high stability and batch consistency potentially provide a robust foundation for future clinical implementation.

To evaluate the potential for clinical integration, the biocompatibility of Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc, selected as a representative model for this series due to its shared polynorbornene backbone, was assessed via MTT assays across three representative cell lines: L929 fibroblasts, A549 lung carcinoma cells, and AC16 cardiomyocytes (Figure S13). Notably, all biological assays were conducted at a final DMSO concentration of 1% (v/v) to ensure the results accurately reflect the inherent biocompatibility of the polymer framework in a biologically relevant environment. The probe exhibited excellent cytocompatibility, with A549 and AC16 cells maintaining high viability (>75% and >80%, respectively) even at the maximum tested concentration of 100 μg mL–1. While L929 fibroblasts showed a slight decrease in viability at 100 μg mL–1, they remained >70% viable within the 0.1–10 μg mL–1 range. In accordance with ISO 10993–5 standards (viability ≥ 70%), these results confirm the low cytotoxicity and high safety profile of the polymer platform. Such findings are particularly significant for the development of POC oral diagnostic devices. These applications are vital in clinical scenarios, such as pediatric care or patients with impaired expectoration, where direct intraoral contact via a swab or test strip is required.

Notably, while many existing ratiometric platforms show similar sensitivity for both Arg and Lys in simple aqueous media, our system maintains high analytical performance in artificial saliva, demonstrating a robust tolerance for complex biological matrices. While this initial study differentiates Arg from basic amino acids like Lys and His, further validation in more complex matrices containing common metabolic metabolites is a priority for our future work. This work established a versatile ROMP-based framework that can be further adapted with diverse recognition motifs for the multianalyte screening of complex biofluids.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this work demonstrated the successful development of a new class of ROMP-derived fluorescent polymers, Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc and Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 , designed for the highly sensitive fluorescence sensing of Arg. By transitioning from a small-molecule dyad to a structured macromolecular architecture, we observed a significant “polymer effect”, where the cooperative organization of coumarin-tyrosine units on the polynorbornene backbone provided superior signal amplification and matrix stability compared to monomeric analogues. The sensing mechanism, rigorously validated through 1H NMR titration and optical studies, confirmed that the tyrosine −NH site and coumarin-COO acted as a critical recognition element, forming a synergistic hydrogen-bonding network with the guanidinium group of Arg. This interaction triggered a dramatic ratiometric response, characterized by a substantial bathochromic shift and distinct environment-driven emission pathways. Both probes demonstrated robust ratiometric fluorescence profiles in DMSO; however, they displayed matrix-dependent behaviors in artificial saliva, where Poly-Cou-Tyr-Boc maintained its self-calibrating ratiometric performance, and the deprotected Poly-Cou-Tyr-NH 2 functioned via high-sensitivity intensity-based quenching at 411 nm. Most notably, the achievement of a sub-34 nM detection limit in artificial saliva, surpassing the performance in DMSO, underscored the robustness of this platform in complex biological environments. This high level of sensitivity, combined with the proven biocompatibility of the polymer scaffold, highlighted its immense potential for noninvasive POC diagnostics. Ultimately, this study provided a blueprint for the design of “smart” polymeric materials that leverage ROMP to create sophisticated, fluorescence-sensing environments for a wide array of clinical biomarkers.

Hyperspectral anomaly detection via low-rank and sparse decomposition with cluster subspace accumulation
Authors: Baozhi Cheng, Yan Gao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAnomaly detection (AD) has emerged as a prominent area of research in hyperspectral imagery (HSI) processing. Traditional algorithms, such as low-rank and sparse matrix decomposition (LRaSMD), often struggle to effectively address challenges related to background interference, anomaly targets, and noise. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel method that leverages both spatial and spectral features in HSI. Initially, the original HSI is segmented into several subspaces using the k-means method, which reduces redundancy among HSI bands. Subsequently, the fractional Fourier transform (FrFT) is applied within each subspace, enhancing the distinction between background and anomaly target information while simultaneously suppressing noise. To further improve the stability and discriminative power of the HSI, LRaSMD is employed. Finally, the modified Reed–Xiaoli (RX) detector is utilized to identify anomalies within each subspace. The results from these detections are then aggregated to produce a comprehensive final outcome. Experiments conducted on five real HSI data sets yield an average area under the curve (AUC) of 0.9761 with a standard deviation of 0.0156 for the proposed algorithm. These results indicate that our method is highly competitive in the field of anomaly detection.Keywords: Hyperspectral imagery (HSI), Anomaly target detection, Low-rank and sparse decomposition, Cluster subspaceSubject terms: Imaging and sensing, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

Compared to multispectral images, HSI offers superior capabilities for recognizing and analyzing surface materials1. HSI has found extensive applications in various domains, including image quality evaluation, image correction, classification, unmixing, and target detection2,3. Notably, AD has gained traction across multiple fields due to its independence from prior target information, AD has a wider practical application4. This characteristic makes it particularly valuable for detecting marine oil spills, accurately identifying battlefield targets, and inspecting the quality of agricultural products5,6.

In recent decades, research on AD has focused on both algorithm implementation and application. AD algorithms are generally categorized into two types: statistics-based and representation-based methods7. Among the statistical approaches, the RX detector stands out as a classical anomaly detection method. It simplifies the background model using a Gaussian multivariate distribution, allowing for the detection of anomaly targets that deviate from the background feature distribution through the Mahalanobis distance8. Beyond the RX detector, several extensions have been developed, including the fractional Fourier transform-based tensor RX detector9, as well as the local RX detector (LRXD)10, global RX detector (GRXD)11, weighted RX12, and RX-segmented (RXSD)13. However, the performance of the RX algorithm is constrained by two primary issues: (1) the conventional RX distance measure fails to account for higher-order relationships among spectral bands at different wavelengths, and (2) the local Gaussian assumption and homogeneity of background distributions may not hold in certain scenarios.

Given the nonlinear characteristics of anomaly targets and background information in HSI5, nonlinear methods have been employed to enhance AD performance. For instance, the kernel RX (KRX) detector utilizes kernel mapping to transform the original HSI into a high-dimensional feature space, thereby improving anomaly detection capabilities14. Various KRX-based enhancements have emerged, such as the modified kernel-RX (MKRX) detector15, cluster kernel RX (CKRX) detector16, and fast real-time kernel RX (LRT-KRX) detector17. Additionally, Banerjee et al. developed the Support Vector Data Description (SVDD) algorithm for anomaly target detection, which circumvents the need to solve high-dimensional inverse covariance matrices18.

Moreover, some real HSI data do not conform to Gaussian distributions. To mitigate false alarms, sparse representation techniques have been proposed for anomaly detection, including the sparse representation for target detection (SR-TD) detector19 and the local sparsity divergence (LSD) detector20. These methods operate on the principle that HSI can be projected into low-dimensional subspaces, allowing target and background pixels to be expressed as sparse linear combinations19. Recently, low-rank techniques have also been recommended for anomaly detection21,22. The LRaSMD method has proven effective in HSI anomaly detection23,24. This approach posits that HSI consist of background, anomaly targets, and noise. Given the strong interband correlations in HSI, the background spectral signatures can be modeled as a low-rank matrix, expressed as a linear combination of several endmembers. In contrast, anomaly targets, being fewer in number, exhibit sparse properties. The noise in HSI typically comprises stripe noise, Gaussian white noise, and photoelectric noise25. This analysis has led to the development of advanced algorithms, such as the Low-rank and Sparse Decomposition with Mixture of Gaussian (LSDM-MoG) detector26, which enhances the detection and identification of anomaly targets using a mixture of Gaussian distributions. Additionally, the orthogonal subspace projection LRaSMD (OSP-LRaSMD) detector effectively addresses anomaly detection challenges through orthogonal subspace projection27.

The LRaSMD struggles to determine the optimal rank and sparsity, and it lacks robustness to noise and outliers, resulting in a decrease in detection performance. Thus, we propose a novel LRaSMD-based algorithm that incorporates subspace accumulation through k-means clustering and fractional Fourier entropy (KFRLSMD) for anomaly detection. Initially, we reduce the dimensions of the HSI data using a clustering method, decomposing the HSI into several clustering subspaces via k-means clustering. Subsequently, we apply FrFT to the image data within each subspace7,28. The LRaSMD method is then employed to decompose the HSI matrix into low-rank and sparse matrices. Finally, we construct the covariance matrix from the low-rank matrix and obtain anomaly detection results using a modified RX approach. Proposed detection method

The anomaly detection (AD) process utilizes a representation method that leverages the similarity relationship between background pixels in HSI. This approach effectively incorporates local spatial information and background spectral data surrounding the tested pixel, resulting in successful anomaly detection outcomes.

As illustrated in Fig. 3, the proposed KFRLSMD algorithm integrates k-means clustering, FrFT, LRaSMD, and a modified Reed-Xiaoli (RX) detector. By combining representation-based and statistics-based methods, the algorithm capitalizes on their respective strengths to identify anomalous targets. Specifically, k-means clustering partitions the HSI into several subspaces. Subsequently, feature extraction is performed using the FrFT, LRaSMD, and modified RX detectors within each subspace. Conclusions

This paper proposes a novel algorithm, KFRLSMD, to enhance the performance of anomaly detection (AD) in HSI by leveraging both spatial and spectral features. The KFRLSMD algorithm incorporates the GoDec method for LRaSMD. While LRaSMD-based anomaly detection has been addressed in previous literature, existing algorithms often yield only rank candidates and may not achieve satisfactory detection performance. Given that HSI is characterized as big data imagery with inherent redundancy among bands, we employ the k-means clustering method to partition HSI into several subspaces. Processing is conducted within each subspace, where features are extracted using the FrFT method. This approach enhances the distinguishability of background and anomaly target information while partially suppressing noise. Subsequently, the GoDec algorithm is applied, followed by the modified RX detector for anomaly target detection. The performance of the KFRLSMD algorithm is validated using five HSI data sets. Experimental results demonstrate that the KFRLSMD algorithm achieves a high target detection rate, a low false alarm rate, and notable robustness compared to other methods. Additionally, we discuss the parameters of the KFRLSMD algorithm, which contribute to its improved performance in detecting anomaly targets. Overall, the KFRLSMD algorithm exhibits strong anomaly detection capabilities and robustness.

Intersections of branching random walks on ℤ 8 \mathbb{Z}^{8}
Zsuzsanna Baran

Abstract
We consider random walks on ℤ 8 \mathbb{Z}^{8} indexed by the infinite invariant tree, which consists of an infinite spine and finite random trees attached to it on both sides. We establish the precise order of the non-intersection probability between one walk indexed by one side of the tree, and an independent one indexed by both sides of an independent tree. This is analogous to the result by Lawler from the ’90s for two independent simple random walks on ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}^{4} . We also prove a weak law of large numbers for the branching capacity of the range of a branching random walk.

Introduction

In this work we study the intersections of two branching random walks on
\mathbb{Z}^{8}
. To put this problem into a broader context and provide some motivation, we start by briefly discussing the analogous question for two simple random walks.

Let
X
and
\widetilde{X}
be two independent simple random walks on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, starting from
0
, and let us consider the number of intersections
\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{X_{i}=\widetilde{X}_{j}}
between the ranges
X[0,n]
and
\widetilde{X}(0,\infty)
. It is known that in
d>2
dimensions the Green’s function of a simple random walk decays as
g(x)\asymp\frac{1}{||x||^{d-2}+1}
(where
a\asymp b
means that
a
and
b
are of the same order). If we make the rough approximation of replacing
X[0,n]
with the part of the range of
X
falling into the ball
B\left(0,\sqrt{n}\right)
, then a quick calculation suggests that the expected number of intersections is

\displaystyle\approx\>\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{X_{i}=\widetilde{X}_{j}\in B(0,\sqrt{n})}\right]\asymp\sum_{x\in B(0,\sqrt{n})}\left(\frac{1}{||x||^{d-2}+1}\right)^{2}\>\asymp\>\begin{cases}n^{\frac{4-d}{2}}&\text{ for }d<4,\\
\log n&\text{ for }d=4,\\
1&\text{ for }d>4.\end{cases}

A more careful calculation confirms that the expected number of intersections is indeed of this order (see e.g.
[
9
, Proposition 3.2.3]
). We see that for this question the ‘critical dimension’ is 4, since the expected number of intersections grows polynomially for
d<4
, logarithmically for
d=4
and is of constant order for
d>4
. It was conjectured that the non-intersection probability of two independent walks shows a similar behaviour, it decays polynomially in
n
for
d<4
, as a power of
\log n
for
d=4
, and is of constant order for
d>4
.

In 1991 Lawler
[
9
, first edition]
found the exact exponent of the logarithm and the exact constant factor for the case of a one-sided and a two-sided walk in 4 dimensions.
He proved that for two independent walks we have
\mathbb{P}\left(X[-n,n]\cap\widetilde{X}(0,\infty)=\emptyset\right)=\left(1+o(1)\right)\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}\frac{1}{\log n}
. In 1992
[
8
]
he also extended the result for two one-sided walks and proved that
\mathbb{P}\left(X[0,n]\cap\widetilde{X}(0,\infty)=\emptyset\right)=\left(1+o(1)\right)\frac{c}{\sqrt{\log n}}
.

We now move on to the setting of two branching random walks. We start by defining the infinite invariant tree, which is a random infinite tree that will be indexing the branching random walks.

Definition 1.1

(Infinite invariant tree)
.

Let
\mu
be a distribution on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with mean 1 and variance
\sigma^{2}\in(0,\infty)
. We consider a random infinite spatial tree
\mathcal{T}
as follows.

•

The tree has an infinite sequence of
spine vertices

(u_{i})_{i\geq 0}
where
u_{0}
is called the
root
, and
u_{i}
is an offspring of
u_{i-1}
for all
i\geq 1
.

•

The root has
d^{+}_{0}\sim\mu
offsprings on the right side of the spine, and no offsprings on the left side.

•

For
i\geq 1
, the spine vertex
u_{i}
has
d^{+}_{i}
offsprings on the right side of the spine and
d^{-}_{i}
offsprings on the left side of the spine, where
\mathbb{P}\left(d^{+}_{i}=k,\>d^{-}_{i}=\ell\right)=\mu(k+\ell+1)
, and the pairs
(d^{+}_{i},d^{-}_{i})
are independent for different
i
, and also independent of
d^{+}_{0}
.

•

Each non-spine vertex has
\sim\mu
offsprings, independently of each other and the above
d^{\pm}_{i}
. (In other words, given the offsprings of the spine vertices on each side of the spine, we attach independent
\mathrm{GW}(\mu)
trees to each of them.)

We index the vertices of
\mathcal{T}
by
\mathbb{Z}
in the depth-first order in the anticlockwise direction, assigning index
0
to the root. (See
Figure

1
for an illustration.) We sometimes refer to the vertices with nonnegative labels as the
future
of the tree, and to vertices with negative labels as the
past
of the tree. We write
\mathcal{T}_{j}
to mean the vertex with index
j
, write
\mathcal{T}[a,b]
for the vertices with indices in
\{a,a+1,...,b\}
,
\mathcal{T}(a,b]
for vertices with indices in
\{a+1,...,b\}
, etc.

:
Illustration of
\mathcal{T}
and the indexing of its vertices.

Definition 1.2

(Branching random walk)
.

Given a tree
T
, we define a random walk
(S_{v})_{v\in T}
indexed by the vertices of this tree as follows. Let
(D_{v})_{v\in T}
be iid random variables according to a given distribution, and for each vertex
v\in T
let
S_{v}=\sum_{u}D_{u}
where the sum ranges over the ancestors of
v
, not including the root, but including
v
itself (unless it is the root).

In what follows, we assume that
(D_{v})
are distributed uniformly on the
2d
neighbours of
0
in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and we consider a random walk
S
on a tree as in
Definition

1.1
. We call such a walk a
branching random walk
(BRW).

A closely related object, a random walk indexed by a critical Galton-Watson tree conditioned to survive, was considered by Kesten
[
6
]
in 1986 as a simpler model to study alongside the incipient infinite cluster of a critical bond percolation. A branching random walk indexed by the past side of
\mathcal{T}
from
Definition

1.1
was introduced by Le Gall and Shen
[
11
]
in 2016, and later, in 2022 Bai and Wan
[
5
]
considered a branching random walk indexed by the whole of
\mathcal{T}
. This tree only differs from Kesten’s one in the offspring distribution of the root vertex, but it has the useful property of shift-invariance (see
Lemma

2.2
) which makes it more amenable to analyse.

In what follows, we let
\mathcal{T}
be a tree as in
Definition

1.1
and
\mathcal{S}
the corresponding random walk as in
Definition

1.2
. To simplify notation, we will usually write
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S})
to denote both the tree and the walk on it.

We are interested in the non-intersection probability of two independent branching random walks. We can see that for
d>4
the Green’s function of a BRW decays as
G(x)\asymp\frac{1}{||x||^{d-4}+1}
(see
Lemma

2.1
). It is known (see
Lemma

A.1
) that the size of a critical Galton-Watson tree satisfies
\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathrm{GW}|>k\right)\asymp\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}
, therefore the overall size of the trees descending from the first
k
spine vertices is typically
\asymp k^{2}
. Also, the walk along the spine typically travels distance
\asymp\sqrt{k}
in the first
k
steps.
Based on this, we may roughly approximate
\mathcal{T}[0,n]
by
\mathcal{T}[0,\infty)\cap B\left(0,n^{\frac{1}{4}}\right)
, and estimate the expected number of intersections
\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{T}_{i}=\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j}}
between
\mathcal{T}[0,n]
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(0,\infty)
as

\asymp\sum_{x\in B\left(0,n^{\frac{1}{4}}\right)}\left(\frac{1}{||x||^{d-4}+1}\right)^{2}\quad\asymp\quad\begin{cases}n^{\frac{8-d}{4}}&\text{ for }d<8,\\
\log n&\text{ for }d=8,\\
1&\text{ for }d>8.\end{cases}

This indicates that the critical dimension for this question is 8, and the expected number of intersections shows a similar behaviour to the case of two simple random walks. It also suggests that the non-intersection probability in the critical dimension might behave similarly to the case of two SRWs, decaying as a power of
\log n
.

For the case of a one-sided and a two-sided BRW we confirm this, as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mu
be a distribution on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with mean 1 and variance
\sigma^{2}\in(0,\infty)
. Let
\mathcal{T}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
be two independent branching random walks on
\mathbb{Z}^{8}
with offspring distribution
\mu
, as in
Definition

1.2
. Then for all sufficiently large
n
we have

\frac{c}{\log n}\quad\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset\right)\quad\leq\quad\frac{C}{\log n},

where
c
and
C
are positive constants depending only on
\sigma^{2}
.

The same result also holds with
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}[-n,0)
instead of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)
.

If we also include the event that the future side of
\mathcal{T}
does not hit 0, we can establish the precise asymptotics for the non-intersection probability as follows.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
be as in
Theorem

1
. Then we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,n]\right)\quad=\quad\left(1+o(1)\right)\frac{c_{8}}{\log n}\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty,

where
c_{8}
is a positive constant depending only on
\sigma^{2}
.

Another question related to the intersections of two branching random walks is about the branching capacity of a branching random walk range.

Branching capacity was introduced by Zhu
[
17
]
in 2017 as an analogue of the capacity
\mathrm{Cap}\left(\cdot\right)
defined for simple random walks. It is a notion of size based on the escape probabilities of a branching random walk, defined as follows.

Definition 1.3

(Branching capacity)
.

For a finite set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we define its
branching capacity
as

\mathrm{BCap}\left(A\right):=\quad\sum_{x\in A}\mathbb{P}\left((x+\mathcal{T}(-\infty,0))\cap A=\emptyset\right),

where
\mathcal{T}
is a branching random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as in
Definition

1.2
, and
x+\mathcal{T}(-\infty,0)
denotes the Minkowski sum
\{x+y:\>y\in\mathcal{T}(-\infty,0)\}
.

We obtain a weak law of large numbers for the branching capacity of a branching random walk range as follows.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mu
be a distribution on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with mean 1 and variance
\sigma^{2}\in(0,\infty)
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a branching random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{8}
with offspring distribution
\mu
, as in
Definition

1.2
, and consider the branching capacity with offspring distribution
\mu
as in
Definition

1.3
. Then we have

\frac{\log n}{n}\>\mathrm{BCap}\left(\mathcal{T}[0,n]\right)\quad\to\quad c_{8}\qquad\text{in probability}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

where
c_{8}
is the positive constant from
Theorem

2
, depending only on
\sigma^{2}
.

An analogous result about the capacity of a simple random walk range in 4 dimensions was established by Asselah, Schapira and Sousi
[
1
]
in 2019. They proved that
\frac{\log n}{n}\mathrm{Cap}\left(X[0,n]\right)\to\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}
a.s..

So far we discussed previous results regarding two SRWs, but problems concerning the intersection of a SRW with a BRW have also been studied. From calculations analogous to the earlier ones we can see that the critical dimension for this case is 6. In 2022 Bai and Wan

studied the capacity of the range of a BRW and showed that in 6 dimensions it satisfies

in

and in probability. They also showed that for an independent SRW and BRW on

we have
\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{X}(0,\infty)\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,n]\right)=\left(1+o(1)\right)\frac{c}{\log n}
as
n\to\infty
.

1.1
Further directions

Our overall goal is to gain a better understanding of the geometry of the range of a branching random walk in 8 dimensions. In order to do that we would like to understand how two BRW ranges intersect, which also helps understanding how one BRW range intersects itself.

In this paper we take a first step towards that by establishing the order of the non-intersection probability of a one-sided and a two-sided BRW started from the same point. In subsequent works we are also hoping to understand the non-intersection probability between two one-sided or two two-sided ranges started from the same point, and the related question of the intersection probability between two BRW ranges started from further apart. These could also be a helpful step towards understanding e.g. how the effective resistance on the graph induced by the range of a BRW grows, generalising the result of Shiraishi
[
15
]
about the effective resistance on the range of a SRW on
\mathbb{Z}^{4}
.

1.2
Overview

In this section we explain the main ideas of the proofs, emphasising the new difficulties that arise compared to previous works. We follow the same outline as
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
, which both build on the work of Lawler, but there are significant new ideas in how we can handle each step of the proof.

One of the main sources of difficulty is that a BRW does not have a Markov property like a SRW does. In previous results where at least one of the two objects concerned was a SRW, the Markov property of that walk was utilised, but for the case of two BRWs we are not able to use that.

The following result will be a major step in the proof of Theorems
1
,
2
and
3
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
be as in
Theorem

1
, and let
\xi^{\ell}_{n}
and
\xi^{r}_{n}
be independent
\mathrm{Geom}_{\geq 0}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
random variables. Then we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,\xi^{r}_{n}]\right)\quad=\quad\left(1+o(1)\right)\frac{c_{8}}{\log n}\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty,

where
c_{8}
is a positive constant depending only on
\sigma^{2}
.

Similarly to previous works, our starting point is a ‘magic formula’ which states that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]=\emptyset}\>{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,\xi^{r}_{n}]}\>\#\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}[0,\infty)\cap\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]\right)\right]\quad=\quad 1.

(1.1)

We can see that the indicators correspond to the event in
Theorem

4
, while the expectation of the counting term is
\asymp\log n
. Then our proof proceeds as follows.

Step 1: proving concentration of the number of intersections

Firstly, we show that
\#\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}[0,\infty)\cap\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]\right)
is concentrated, namely we prove that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\#\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}[0,\infty)\cap\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]\right)\right]\sim\frac{\log n}{c_{8}},\qquad\mathrm{Var}\left(\#\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}[0,\infty)\cap\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]\right)\right)\lesssim\log n,

where
\sim
and
\lesssim
are defined as in
Section

2.5
.

The main difficulty for the expectation is that it is not clear what the Green’s function associated to the first given number of vertices in
\mathcal{T}
behaves like, while the main difficulty for the variance is that we are not able to use the strong Markov property when considering the probability of
\mathcal{T}
hitting two given vertices.

To estimate the expectation and the variance, we consider the so-called depth-first queue process encoding Galton-Watson trees, and use this to express the expected number of vertices in
\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]
at a given distance from the root, and the expected number of pairs in
\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]
at a given distance from the root and each other. Then using properties of the depth-first queue process we are able to reinterpret and simplify these expressions, and get sums that concern SRWs up to a geometric number of steps. (See
Lemma

3.4
for some of the most technical calculations.)

Then we use that considering the Green’s function of a SRW up to
\mathrm{Geom}_{\geq 0}\!\left(\alpha\right)
steps is similar to considering the Green’s function for the whole walk, but only keeping the values at sites
x
with
||x||\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
. We also present the bounds we use to justify this approximation. (See
Lemma

B.2
.)

Step 2: decorrelating the indicators and the number of intersections

As a next step, we use the concentration of
\#\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}[0,\infty)\cap\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]\right)
to ‘pull it out’ from the expectation in (
1.1
). We can quickly get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left({\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]=\emptyset},\>{0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,\xi^{r}_{n}]}\right)\quad\lesssim\quad\frac{1}{\log n},

(1.2)

but we also need a lower bound, and a more precise estimate for the upper bound.

We can see that the terms
{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,\xi^{r}_{n}]}
and
\#\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}[0,\infty)\cap\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]\right)
have a dependence via
\mathcal{T}
and via the spine of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
.

Inspired by
[
14
, Section 3.3]
, we split
\mathcal{T}
into two parts,
\mathcal{T}(\leq C)
that is the part of the tree up to the first spine vertex where the walk hits a given distance
C
from 0, and
\mathcal{T}(\geq C)
that consists of the rest of the tree. We also split
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
into
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\leq\widetilde{C})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\geq\widetilde{C})
similarly.

Then we consider the event that (i)
\#\left(\mathcal{T}(\geq C)\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\leq\widetilde{C})\right)
deviates a lot from its mean, (ii)
\mathcal{T}\left(\leq\frac{1}{4}C\right)
does not intersect the negative side of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\geq 4\widetilde{C})
, and (iii) the positive side of
\mathcal{T}\left(\leq\frac{1}{4}C\right)
does not hit 0. We bound its probability by decorrelating the parts
\left(\mathcal{T}(\geq C),\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\leq\widetilde{C})\right)
from the parts
\left(\mathcal{T}\left(\leq\frac{1}{4}C\right),\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\geq 4\widetilde{C})\right)
, and bounding the probability of (i) and (ii)
\cap
(iii) separately.

We also bound the probabilities of analogous events with other combinations of the parts
\mathcal{T}(\leq C)
,
\mathcal{T}(\geq C)
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\leq\widetilde{C})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\geq\widetilde{C})
.

This finishes the proof of
Theorem

4
, and using the concentration of
\xi^{\ell}_{n}
and
\xi^{r}_{n}
, we also get
Theorem

2
. It also quickly implies asymptotics on
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{BCap}\left(\mathcal{T}[0,n]\right)\right]
, and similarly to
[
5
, Proposition 4.11]
we can bound
\mathrm{Var}\left(\mathrm{BCap}\left(\mathcal{T}[0,n]\right)\right)
to finish the proof of
Theorem

3
.

Step 3: removing the
0\not\in\mathcal{T}\left(0,n\right]
term

For proving
Theorem

1
we have to remove the
0\not\in\mathcal{T}\left(0,n\right]
condition from the probability in
Theorem

2
.

In
[
14
, Lemma 3.2]
, where in place of
\mathcal{T}
we have a SRW, this is done using the following idea. We can compare a SRW and a SRW that is conditioned not to hit 0 at positive times by considering a SRW and removing the part between time 0 and its last visit to 0. Since we do not have a strong Markov property for BRWs, we are not able to use this trick in our case.

Instead, we show that a BRW can be compared to a BRW that does not hit 0 at positive times and is weighted by the number of zeroes at negative times. More precisely, we show (in
Lemma

5.1
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset\right)\sim\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,\infty)}\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}(-\infty,0]\right)\right]+o\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right).

While we know that the expectation of the indicator term is of the desired order
\asymp\frac{1}{\log n}
, and the expectation of the counting term is
\asymp 1
, it still requires considerable work to decorrelate these. We split the counting term based on the trees descending from different spine vertices, and use different estimates based on the value of the walk at the given spine vertex and the number of times this value was visited by earlier spine vertices.

When making these estimates, we lose a constant factor, this is why in
Theorem

1
we can only estimate the probabilty up to a constant factor, and do not get exact asymptotics.

2.1
Further notation regarding BRWs

As mentioned in
Section

1
, we let
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S})
denote a random walk as in
Definition

1.2
indexed by a tree as in
Definition

1.1
. We write
X
for the simple random walk (SRW) along the spine of
\mathcal{T}
.

Unless specified otherwise,
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}=(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}},\widetilde{\mathcal{S}})
denotes a branching random walk independent of

, and we denote the associated quantities analogously as

2.2
Green’s functions

Let
g
denote the Green’s function of a simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

denote the Green’s function of the future of a branching random walk on

denote the convolution

Lemma 2.1
.

For

and any

\displaystyle g(z)\quad=\quad c_{g}\frac{1}{||z||^{d-2}}+o\left(\frac{1}{||z||^{d-\varepsilon}}\right)\qquad\text{as }||z||\to\infty,

(2.1)

where

is a constant depending on the dimension.

For

and any

we have

\displaystyle G(z)\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}(g\star g)(z)+\left(1-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)g(z)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[g(X_{1}+z)\right]

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\quad\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}(g\star g)(z)+O\left(g(z)\right)\quad=\quad c_{G}\frac{1}{||z||^{d-4}}+o\left(\frac{1}{||z||^{d-2-\varepsilon}}\right)\quad\text{as }||z||\to\infty,

where

is a constant depending on the dimension and
\sigma^{2}
.

For

and any

we have

\displaystyle(G\star g)(z)\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}(g\star g\star g)(z)+O((g\star g)(z))\quad

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\quad c_{G\star g}\frac{1}{||z||^{d-6}}+o\left(\frac{1}{||z||^{d-4-\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{as }||z||\to\infty,

where

is a constant depending on the dimension and

.

See e.g.

for (

). Then (

and that

).

For

steps, i.e. let

2.3
Shift invariance of BRWs and the magic formula

A very important property of BRWs is the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S})
be a BRW as above, let
(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}=(\mathcal{T}_{i+1})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
be obtained by re-rooting the tree
\mathcal{T}
at
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i}=\mathcal{S}_{i+1}-\mathcal{S}_{1}
be obtained by shifting the walk
\mathcal{S}
. Then we have
(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}},\widetilde{\mathcal{S}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{=}}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S})
.

An analogous shift-invariance property for
\mathcal{T}(-\infty,0]
was stated in
[
11
, Proposition 2]
and the exact statement of
Lemma

2.2
appears in
[
5
, after (2.2)]
.

By using a last exit decomposition and the translation invariance of
\mathcal{T}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
, we can obtain a ‘magic formula’ similar to
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
, featuring the probability in
Theorem

4
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}:=

\displaystyle\quad\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]=\emptyset\right\},\qquad\mathcal{B}_{n}:=\quad\left\{0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,\xi^{r}_{n}]\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}:=

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{k=-\xi^{\ell}_{n}}^{\xi^{r}_{n}}\mathcal{L}^{+}\left(\mathcal{T}_{k}\right)\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{L}^{+}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j}=y},

\displaystyle U_{n}:=

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\xi^{\ell}_{n}}^{\xi^{r}_{n}}\widetilde{d}^{+}_{i}g(\widetilde{X}_{i},\mathcal{T}_{j})-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\xi^{\ell}_{n}}^{\xi^{r}_{n}}\widetilde{d}^{+}_{i}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{X}_{i}=\mathcal{T}_{j}}.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\mathcal{L}_{n}\right]\quad=\quad 1.

The proof is analogous to the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
and presented below for completeness.

Proof.

Let
y_{0},...,y_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
be a branching random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then by considering
\sup\{\ell\geq 0:\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\ell)\in\{y_{0},...,y_{m}\}\}
, using that a.s.
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
intersects
\{y_{0},...,y_{m}\}
only finitely many times, and using the shift invariance of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
, we get the following last exit decomposition formula.

\displaystyle 1\quad\geq

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0]\cap\{y_{0},...,y_{m}\}\neq\emptyset\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{m}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{y_{i}\not\in\{y_{i+1},..,y_{m}\}}\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\ell)=y_{i},\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,-\ell)\cap\{y_{0},..,y_{m}\}=\emptyset\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{m}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{y_{i}\not\in\{y_{i+1},..,y_{m}\}}\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(\ell)=-y_{i},\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\{y_{0}-y_{i},..,y_{m}-y_{i}\}=\emptyset\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{i=0}^{m}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{y_{i}\not\in\{y_{i+1},..,y_{m}\}}\mathbb{E}\left[\#\left((-y_{i})\text{ in }\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}[0,\infty)\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\{y_{0}-y_{i},..,y_{m}-y_{i}\}=\emptyset}\right].

(2.4)

By the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
,
\mathcal{B}_{n}
and
\mathcal{L}_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\mathcal{L}_{n}\right]\quad

\displaystyle\qquad=\quad\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0},...,x_{m}\\
\text{with }x_{0}=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi^{\ell}_{n}=m-j,\xi^{r}_{n}=j,\mathcal{T}[-m+j,j]=(x_{0}-x_{j},...,x_{m}-x_{j})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{x_{j}\not\in\{x_{j+1},...,x_{m}\}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m}\#\left((x_{k}-x_{j})\text{ in }\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}[0,\infty)\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\{x_{0}-x_{j},..,x_{m}-x_{j}\}=\emptyset}\right].

Using the shift invariance of
\mathcal{T}
, and that
\left(\xi^{\ell}_{n}\mid\xi^{\ell}_{n}+\xi^{r}_{n}=m\right)\sim\mathrm{Unif}\!\left(\{0,1,...,m\}\right)
, we get that this is

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0},...,x_{m}\\
\text{with }x_{0}=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{1}{m+1}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi^{\ell}_{n}+\xi^{r}_{n}=m\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{T}[0,m]=(x_{0},...,x_{m})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\cdot{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{x_{j}\not\in\{x_{j+1},...,x_{m}\}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m}\#\left((x_{k}-x_{j})\text{ in }\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}[0,\infty)\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\{x_{0}-x_{j},...,x_{m}-x_{j}\}=\emptyset}\right].

Then we use (

) for
x_{0}-x_{k},...,x_{m}-x_{k}
in place of

to get that this is

\displaystyle=\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0},...,x_{m}\\
\text{with }x_{0}=0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m+1}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi^{\ell}_{n}+\xi^{r}_{n}=m\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{T}[0,m]=(x_{0},...,x_{m})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0]\cap\{x_{0}-x_{k},...,x_{m}-x_{k}\}\neq\emptyset\right).

Note that

∎

2.4
Depth-first queue process

A useful tool for some of the later calculations is to encode trees via their depth-first queue process (DFQP). We start by recalling the definition and some important properties, and afterwards we present the notation we will use regarding this.

Definition and properties

Given a rooted plane tree
t
with
n
edges, let
v_{j}
(
j\in\{0,1,...,n\}
) be the
j
th new vertex encountered when going around the contour from the root anticlockwise. Let
\xi_{j}
be the number of offsprings of
v_{j}
.

Then the depth-first queue process of
t
is
W:\{0,1,...,n+1\}\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
W_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(\xi_{j}-1)
. Note that in case of a
\mathrm{GW}(\mu)
tree this is a random walk with iid
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{=}}(\mu-1)
increments.

We say that
j\in[a,b]
is a right minimum of
W
on
[a,b]
if
W_{j}=\min_{i\in[j,b]}W_{i}
, and we say that
j\in[a,b]
is a record of
W
on
[a,b]
if
W_{j}=\max_{i\in[a,j]}W_{i}
. (If
[a,b]
is not specified, we take
[a,b]=[0,j]
.)

Then the graph distance of
v_{k}
from the root
v_{0}
is the number of right minima of
W
on the interval
[0,k]
, not counting the right minimum at
k
. The ancestors of
v_{k}
are exactly the vertices corresponding to these right minima. (See
Figure

2(a)
for an illustration.)

Note that we have
(W_{j})_{j\in[0,k]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{=}}(W_{k}-W_{k-j})_{j\in[0,k]}
, and
j
being a right minimum on
[a,b]
in the former process corresponds to
(k-j)
being a record on
[k-b,k-a]
in the latter one.

By flipping
W
on
[0,k]
we will mean considering the process
(W_{k}-W_{k-j})_{j\in[0,k]}
instead of
(W_{j})_{j\in[0,k]}
.

Given a walk
W
, the
j
th ladder width and ladder height are
\mathrm{LW}_{j}=R_{j}-R_{j-1}
and
\mathrm{LH}_{j}=W_{R_{j}}-W_{R_{j-1}}
, respectively, where
R_{j}
denotes the
j
th record. (See
Figure

2(b)
for an illustration.) Note that for a walk
W
with iid increments, the parts
(W_{i})_{i\in[R_{j-1},R_{j}]}
are iid, and in particular the pairs
(\mathrm{LW}_{j},\mathrm{LH}_{j})
are iid.

v_{11}

Also note that if
W
is a random walk with iid
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{=}}(\mu-1)
increments, then the parts
(W_{i}+k)_{i\in[\sigma_{-k},\sigma_{-k-1})}
of the walk (for
k\geq 0
, where
\sigma_{-k}=\min\{n:\>W_{n}=-k\}
) define independent
\mathrm{GW}(\mu)
trees.

Notation

In what follows we let
W
denote a random walk with iid
\sim(\mu-1)
increments, and we denote its ladder widths and ladder heights as
(\mathrm{LW}_{i})
and
(\mathrm{LH}_{i})
respectively. We sometimes write
(\mathrm{LW},\mathrm{LH})
for a pair of random variables distributed as
(\mathrm{LW}_{1},\mathrm{LH}_{1})
. We also sometimes write
\mathrm{GW}
to denote a
\mathrm{GW}(\mu)
tree or
(\mathrm{GW}_{i})
to denote a sequence of independent
\mathrm{GW}(\mu)
trees. We write
|\mathrm{GW}|
to mean the number of vertices in
\mathrm{GW}
.

The later calculations will often feature quantities like
\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda^{\mathrm{LW}}\right]
or
\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda^{|\mathrm{GW}|}\right]
, we will introduce notation for these the first time they are used.

2.5
Other notation

For functions
a,b:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to[0,\infty)
we say that
a(n)\lesssim b(n)
as
n\to\infty
if there exists constant
C>0
such that for all sufficiently large
n
we have
a(n)\leq Cb(n)
. We say that
a(n)\ll b(n)
as
n\to\infty
if for any constant
c>0
, for all sufficiently large
n
(in terms of
c
) we have
a(n)\leq cb(n)
. We define
\gtrsim
and
\gg
analogously. We say that
a(n)\asymp b(n)
as
n\to\infty
if we have
a(n)\lesssim b(n)\lesssim a(n)
as
n\to\infty
. We say that
a(n)\sim b(n)
as
n\to\infty
if we have
|a(n)-b(n)|\ll a(n)
.
Unless specified otherwise, these relations will be as
n\to\infty
.

We will sometimes write things like
\lesssim_{x,y}
to emphasise that the implicit constant in
\lesssim
is a function of parameters
x
and
y
. Almost all constants will depend on
\sigma^{2}
, so we will drop this from the notation.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left(G_{n}^{+}\right)\quad\leq\quad\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{+}_{n}(k)^{2}\right]+2\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq 1}\mathrm{Cov}\left(Y^{+}_{n}(k),Y^{+}_{n}(k+m)\right),

where
Y^{+}_{n}(k)=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}Y^{+}_{n}(k,\ell)
, and
Y^{+}_{n}(k,\ell)
are as in
Section

3.2
.

Diagonal terms in the variance of
G_{n}^{+}

In what follows we prove that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k)^{2}\right]\quad\lesssim\quad\log n\>.

(3.18)

We can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{+}_{n}(k,\ell)^{2}\right]\quad=\quad\mathbb{P}\left(d_{k}^{+}\geq\ell\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\theta}^{d_{0}^{+}+...+d_{k-1}^{+}+\ell-1}\right]\lambda\>\cdot

(3.19)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{|\mathrm{GW}|-1}\lambda^{i}G(X_{k+1}+T_{i})^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{0\leq i<j<|\mathrm{GW}|}\lambda^{j}G(X_{k+1}+T_{i})G(X_{k+1}+T_{j})\right]\right),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{+}_{n}(k,\ell)Y^{+}_{n}(k,\ell+r)\right]\quad=\quad\mathbb{P}\left(d_{k}^{+}\geq\ell+r\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\theta}^{d_{0}^{+}+...+d_{k-1}^{+}+\ell+r-2}\right]\lambda\>\cdot

(3.20)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda^{|\mathrm{GW}|}\sum_{i=0}^{|\mathrm{GW}|-1}G(X_{k}+\widetilde{X}_{1}+T_{i})\;\middle|\;X_{k}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{|\mathrm{GW}|-1}\lambda^{j}G(X_{k}+\widehat{X}_{1}+T_{j})\;\middle|\;X_{k}\right]\right].

Summing the first term in (
3.19
) over
k\geq 0
and
\ell\geq 1
, we get an expression that is

\lesssim\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 0}\theta_{1}^{k}\theta^{j}\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k+j+1})^{2}\right]
. This is then
\lesssim\sum_{x}\sum_{y}g_{1-\theta_{1}}(x)g_{1-\theta}(y)G(x+y)

\lesssim\sum_{y}g_{1-\theta}(y)(g\star G)(y)
. Using
Corollary

A.5
we can upper bound this analogously to (
3.16
), and get that it is
\lesssim\log n
.

Summing the second term in (
3.19
) over
k\geq 0
and
\ell\geq 1
, we get an expression that is

\lesssim\quad\sum_{x,y,z,w}g_{1-\theta_{1}}(x)g_{1-\theta}(y)g_{1-\theta}(z)g_{1-\widehat{\theta}}(w)G(x+y+z)G(x+y+w)\quad\lesssim\quad\log n.

Summing (
3.20
) over
k\geq 0
,
\ell\geq 1
,
r\geq 1
we get an expression that is

\displaystyle\lesssim\quad\sum_{x,y,z}g_{1-\theta_{1}}(x)g_{1-\widehat{\theta}}(y)g_{1-\theta}(z)G(x+y)G(x+z)\quad\lesssim\quad 1.

This finishes the proof of (
3.18
).

Covariance terms in the variance of
G_{n}^{+}

In what follows we prove that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq 1}\mathrm{Cov}\left(Y^{+}_{n}(k),Y^{+}_{n}(k+m)\right)\quad\lesssim\quad\log n.

(3.21)

For
k\geq 1
,
m\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k)Y_{n}^{+}(k+m)\right]\quad=\quad\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{r\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k,\ell)Y_{n}^{+}(k+m,r)\right]

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{r\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{d^{+}_{k}\geq\ell}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{d^{+}_{k+m}\geq r}\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda^{|\mathrm{GW}|}\right]^{d^{+}_{0}+...+d^{+}_{k+m-1}+r-2}\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda^{|\mathrm{GW}|}\sum_{i=0}^{|\mathrm{GW}|-1}G(X_{k}+\widetilde{X}_{1}+T_{i})\;\middle|\;X_{k}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{|\mathrm{GW}|-1}\lambda^{i}G(X_{k+m}+\widehat{X}_{1}+T_{i})\;\middle|\;X_{k}\right]\Bigg].

Using the definition of
\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{1}
in (
3.14
), and using (
3.7
) and (
3.5
), we get that this is

\displaystyle=\quad\theta_{0}\theta_{1}^{k+m-2}\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{d^{+}_{k}\geq\ell}\widetilde{\theta}^{d^{+}_{k}}\right]\right)\left(\sum_{r\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\left(d^{+}_{k+m}\geq r\right)\widetilde{\theta}^{r-2}\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\cdot\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widetilde{X}_{i+1})G(X_{k+m}+\widehat{X}_{j+1})\right]\widehat{\theta}^{i}\widetilde{\theta}\theta^{j}.

After some further simplifications, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k)Y_{n}^{+}(k+m)\right]

(3.22)

\displaystyle\quad=\quad\theta_{0}\theta_{1}^{k+m-2}\mathbb{E}\left[d^{+}_{1}\widetilde{\theta}^{d^{+}_{1}}\right]\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{1-\widetilde{\theta}}\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widetilde{X}_{i+1})G(X_{k}+\widehat{X}_{m+j+1})\right]\widehat{\theta}^{i}\theta^{j}.

Meanwhile,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k+m)\right]\quad=\quad\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{r\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k,\ell)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k+m,r)\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\left(d^{+}_{k}\geq\ell\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda^{|\mathrm{GW}|}\right]^{d^{+}_{0}+...+d^{+}_{k-1}+\ell-1}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{|\mathrm{GW}|-1}\lambda^{i}G(X_{k}+\widetilde{X}_{1}+T_{i})\right]\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 17.07182pt\cdot\left(\sum_{r\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\left(d^{+}_{k+m}\geq r\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda^{|\mathrm{GW}|}\right]^{d^{+}_{0}+...+d^{+}_{k+m-1}+r-1}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{|\mathrm{GW}|-1}\lambda^{i}G(X_{k+m}+\widehat{X}_{1}+T_{i})\right]\right).

Again, using the definition of
\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{1}
in (
3.14
), and using (
3.5
), we get that this is

\displaystyle=\quad\theta_{0}^{2}\theta_{1}^{2k+m-2}\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\left(d^{+}_{k}\geq\ell\right)\widetilde{\theta}^{\ell-1}\right)\left(\sum_{r\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\left(d^{+}_{k+m}\geq r\right)\widetilde{\theta}^{r-1}\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\cdot\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widetilde{X}_{i+1})\right]\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k+m}+\widehat{X}_{j+1})\right]\theta^{i}\theta^{j},

and after some further simplifications we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k+m)\right]

(3.23)

\displaystyle\quad=\quad\theta_{0}^{2}\theta_{1}^{2k+m-2}\left(\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{1-\widetilde{\theta}}\right)^{2}\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widetilde{X}_{i+1})\right]\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widehat{X}_{m+j+1})\right]\theta^{i}\theta^{j}.

Taking the difference between (
3.22
) and (
3.23
), and using that
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}=1-O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)
(by
Lemma

A.6
),
\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{1-\widetilde{\theta}}\asymp 1
(by
Lemma

A.7
),
\theta_{1}^{k}=1-O\left(\frac{k}{\sqrt{n}}\right)
,
\mathbb{E}\left[d^{+}_{1}\widetilde{\theta}^{d^{+}_{1}}\right]\leq\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{1-\widetilde{\theta}}
, and
\widehat{\theta}\leq\theta
, we get that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}\left(Y_{n}^{+}(k),Y_{n}^{+}(k+m)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\quad\theta_{1}^{k+m}\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 0}\theta^{i}\theta^{j}\Big(\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widetilde{X}_{i+1})G(X_{k}+\widehat{X}_{m+j+1})\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widetilde{X}_{i+1})\right]\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widehat{X}_{m+j+1})\right]\Big)

\displaystyle+\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k+m)\right]

\displaystyle+\quad\frac{k}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot\theta_{1}^{k+m}\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 0}\theta^{i}\theta^{j}\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widetilde{X}_{i+1})\right]\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widehat{X}_{m+j+1})\right].

(3.24)

We know that
\sum_{k,m\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(k+m)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[G_{n}^{+}\right]^{2}\lesssim(\log n)^{2}
, so the contribution from the second term in (
3.24
) is
\lesssim\log n
as required.

We also have

\displaystyle\sum_{k,m\geq 1}\sum_{i,j\geq 0}k\theta_{1}^{k}\theta_{1}^{m}\theta^{i}\theta^{j}\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widetilde{X}_{i+1})\right]\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widehat{X}_{m+j+1})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\quad\sum_{k\geq 1}k\theta_{1}^{k}\mathbb{E}\left[(G\star g_{1-\theta})(X_{k})\right]\mathbb{E}\left[(G\star g_{1-\theta}\star g_{1-\theta_{1}})(X_{k})\right].

Using that
(G\star g_{1-\theta})(x)\leq(G\star g)(x)\asymp\frac{1}{||x||^{2}}
and
(G\star g_{1-\theta}\star g_{1-\theta_{1}})(y)\lesssim\log n
, using
Lemma

B.3
, and the bound
\frac{1}{||x||^{12}+||y||^{12}}\lesssim\frac{1}{||x||^{5}}\frac{1}{||y||^{7}}
, we get that this is

\displaystyle\lesssim\quad\left(\sum_{x:\>||x||\leq Cn^{\frac{1}{4}}}\frac{1}{||x||^{5}}\frac{1}{||x||^{2}}+\sum_{x:\>||x||\geq Cn^{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{-c||x||n^{-\frac{1}{4}}}\frac{1}{||x||^{5}}\frac{1}{||x||^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\cdot\left(\sum_{y:\>||y||\leq Cn^{\frac{1}{4}}}\frac{1}{||y||^{7}}\log n+\sum_{y:\>||y||\geq Cn^{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{-c||y||n^{-\frac{1}{4}}}\frac{1}{||y||^{7}}\log n\right).

This is
\asymp\sqrt{n}\log n
, so the overall contribution from the third term in (
3.24
) is also
\lesssim\log n
.

Finally, note that the first sum in (
3.24
) is

\displaystyle\leq\quad\theta^{-2}\theta_{1}^{k+m}\mathrm{Cov}\left((G\star g_{1-\theta})(X_{k}),(G\star g_{1-\theta})(X_{k}+\widehat{X}_{m})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\>\sum_{j\geq 0}\theta^{j}\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k})\right]\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widehat{X}_{m+j})\right]\>+\>\sum_{i\geq 0}\theta^{i}\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widetilde{X}_{i})\right]\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{k}+\widehat{X}_{m})\right].

Summing the second and third term over
k\geq 1
,
m\geq 1
, we get an expression that is
\lesssim\log n
.

Summing the first term over
m\geq 0
, we get
\theta^{-2}\theta_{1}^{k}\mathrm{Cov}\left((G\star g_{1-\theta})(X_{k}),(G\star g_{1-\theta}\star g_{1-\theta_{1}})(X_{k})\right)
. The contribution from the
m=0
case is
\lesssim\log n
, and we have
\theta^{-2}\asymp 1
. We conclude bounding the contribution from the first term in (
3.24
) by using the following result.

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\theta_{1}^{k}\cdot\mathrm{Cov}\left((G\star g_{1-\theta})(X_{k}),(G\star g_{1-\theta}\star g_{1-\theta_{1}})(X_{k})\right)\quad\lesssim\quad\log n.

(3.25)

Sketch proof.

Firstly, we use
Lemma

B.2
to compare
(G\star g_{1-\theta})(x)
and
(G\star g_{1-\theta}\star g_{1-\theta_{1}})(x)
to
a(x):=\frac{c_{a}}{||x||^{2}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{||x||\leq n^{\frac{1}{4}}}
and

b(x):=c_{b}\left(\log\left(2n^{\frac{1}{4}}-||x||\right)+\log\left(2n^{\frac{1}{4}}+||x||\right)-2\log\left(||x||\right)\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{||x||\leq n^{\frac{1}{4}}}
, respectively,

where
c_{a}
and
c_{b}
are positive constants. We show that the contribution to the sum in (
3.25
) from the error terms is
\lesssim\log n
.

Then we compare
\sum_{k\geq 1}\theta_{1}^{k}\cdot\mathrm{Cov}\left(a(X_{k}),b(X_{k})\right)
to

\displaystyle\sum_{x:\>||x||\leq n^{\frac{1}{4}}}a(x)\sum_{y:\>||y||\leq n^{\frac{1}{4}}}g^{(2d)}_{1-\theta_{1}}\left((x,y)\right)\left(b(x)-b(y)\right),

(3.26)

where
g^{(2d)}_{1-\theta_{1}}
denotes the Green’s function for a SRW on
\mathbb{Z}^{2d}
induced by the pairs in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with an even sum of coordinates. Note that
g^{(2d)}_{1-\theta_{1}}\left((x,y)\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{\left(||x||^{2}+||y||^{2}\right)^{7}}
, which is
\lesssim\frac{1}{||x||^{k}}\frac{1}{||y||^{14-k}}
for any
k\in\{0,1,....,14\}
.

We split the sum (
3.26
) based on the three different log terms in
b
, and for each of them we split based on whether the contribution from
x
or
y
is larger. Finally, for each of the six sums we upper bound its absolute value by using
g^{(2d)}_{1-\theta_{1}}\left((x,y)\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{||x||^{k}}\frac{1}{||y||^{14-k}}
for a suitable choice of
k
.
∎

Finally, we use
(
3.7
)
and bounds analogous to the ones in
Section

3.2
to show that

\sum_{m\geq 1}\mathrm{Cov}\left(Y_{n}^{+}(0),Y_{n}^{+}(m)\right)\quad\leq\quad\sum_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{+}(0)Y_{n}^{+}(m)\right]\quad\lesssim\quad\log n.

This finishes the proof of (
3.21
).

Conclusion

Combining (
3.19
) and (
3.21
), we get that
\mathrm{Var}\left(G_{n}^{+}\right)\lesssim\log n
. The diagonal terms and covariance terms in
\mathrm{Var}\left(G_{n}^{-}\right)
are handled analogously, so we also get
\mathrm{Var}\left(G_{n}^{-}\right)\lesssim\log n
. Then by Cauchy-Schwarz we have
\mathrm{Var}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\;\middle|\;\mathcal{T}\right]\right)=\mathrm{Var}\left(G_{n}\right)\lesssim\log n
. This finishes the proof of (
3.2
).

Then for each

,
y
we can bound

and

by estimates similar to the ones in
Section

3.3
, but using functions

and

instead of

. The details of these calculations are omitted.

This concludes the proof of (

). Combining (
3.2
) and (

), we can conclude

.

Proof of

.

Note that

Working towards the proof of

, we will prove the following two results.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\right]\quad\lesssim\quad\frac{1}{\log n}.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\varepsilon
be any positive constant and let

\displaystyle D_{\varepsilon}:=\{\left|U_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]\right|>\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]\}.

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\>(\log n)\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{n},\mathcal{B}_{n},D_{\varepsilon}\right)=0.

The proof of

4.1
is analogous to the proof of the first part of
[
14

, and is presented here for completeness.

Proof of
Lemma

4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{n}=\left\{\left|U_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]\right\}
. Then we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\right]\quad\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{E}_{n}\right)+\quad\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}^{c}}\right].

Using
Proposition

3.1
and
Proposition

3.2
and Chebyshev’s inequality we get that
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{E}_{n}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{\log n}
.

Note that on
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{c}
we have
U_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]
, hence
\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{E}_{n}^{c}}\right]\leq\frac{2\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}U_{n}\right]}{\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]}
. By
Corollary

2.4
and
Proposition

3.1
this is also
\lesssim\frac{1}{\log n}
.
∎

Once we have
Lemma

4.1
and

4.2
, the proof of

4
can be completed as follows. This is similar to the proof of
[
14
, Proposition 3.5]
.

Proof of
Theorem

4
.

By
Corollary

2.4
we can write

\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\right]\quad=\quad\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]}-\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\left(U_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]\right)\right]}{\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]}.

From
Proposition

3.1
we know that
\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]}\sim\frac{c_{8}}{\log n}
. We can bound the second term as follows.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\left|U_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]\right|\right]\quad

\displaystyle\leq\quad\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{D_{\varepsilon}}\left|U_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]\right|\right].

By
Lemma

4.1
and
Proposition

3.1
we know that
\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\right]\lesssim\frac{1}{\log n}
and
\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]\asymp\log n
, hence
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Also,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{D_{\varepsilon}}\left|U_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[U_{n}\right]\right|\right]\>

\displaystyle\leq\>\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{n},\mathcal{B}_{n},D_{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{Var}\left(U_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\>\lesssim\>\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{n},\mathcal{B}_{n},D_{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and by
Proposition

4.2
we know that
\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\>(\log n)\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{n},\mathcal{B}_{n},D_{\varepsilon}\right)=0
for any
\varepsilon
.

Combining these, we get that

, we get that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\>\mathbb{P}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\left(\geq 4\widetilde{C}\right)\cap\mathcal{T}\left(\leq\frac{1}{4}C_{1}\right)=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}\left(\leq\frac{1}{4}C_{1}\right),

\displaystyle\>\mathcal{T}\left(\leq\frac{1}{4}C_{1}\right)\text{ does not disconnect }\widetilde{X}_{\widetilde{\tau}_{4\widetilde{C}}}\text{ from }0\text{ in }B\left(0,4\widetilde{C}\right)\Bigg)\quad\lesssim_{\widetilde{C}}\quad\frac{1}{\log C_{1}}.\qed

Proof of
Lemma

4.6
.

To prove (

), firstly, we will show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\left(\leq n^{9}\right)\cap\mathcal{T}\left(\leq n\right)=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}\left(\leq n\right)\right)\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to 0.

(4.10)

Note that the above probability can be upper bounded as

\displaystyle\leq\quad

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}\right)\quad+\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\left(>n^{9}\right)\cap B\left(0,n^{4}\right)\neq\emptyset\right)

\displaystyle+\quad

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{T}\left(\leq n\right)\not\subseteq B\left(0,n^{4}\right)\right)\quad+\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{T}\neq\mathcal{T}\left(\leq n\right)\right).

(4.11)

We already know that
\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{-}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{\log n}
, which
\to 0
as

.

Note that the second probability in (

) is upper bounded by the expected number of intersections of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\left(>n^{9}\right)
and

\displaystyle\leq\quad\sup_{x\in\partial B\left(0,n^{9}\right)}\sum_{y\in B\left(0,n^{4}\right)}G(x,y)\quad\asymp\quad n^{4d}\cdot\frac{1}{n^{9(d-4)}}\quad\to\quad 0\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty.

We can upper bound the third probability in (

) as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{T}\left(\leq n\right)\not\subseteq B\left(0,n^{4}\right)\right)\quad

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{n}>n^{3}\right)+n^{3}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[d^{+}_{1}+d^{-}_{1}\right]\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{GW}\text{ survives to generation }\frac{1}{2}n^{4}\right).

From Lemma

Finally, note that
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{T}\neq\mathcal{T}\left(\leq n\right)\right)\lesssim\theta_{1}^{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. This finishes the proof of (
4.10
). Using (

is decreasing in

,
C_{2}
and

, we get (

).

Now we proceed to prove (

).
Firstly, note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\left(\geq n^{5}\right)\cap\mathcal{T}\neq\emptyset\right)\quad\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\left(\geq n^{5}\right)\cap B\left(0,n^{2}\right)\neq\emptyset\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{T}\not\subseteq B\left(0,n^{2}\right)\right).

The expected number of intersections of

\lesssim n^{2d}\cdot\frac{1}{n^{5(d-4)}}\ll\frac{1}{\log n}
. Also,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\left(\leq n^{5}\right)\cap\mathcal{T}=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}\right)\quad\lesssim\quad\frac{1}{\log n}.

Then similarly to the proof of Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(

\displaystyle\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\left(\leq n^{5}\right)\text{ does not disconnect }0\text{ and }X_{\tau_{4C_{2}}}\text{ in }B\left(0,4C_{2}\right)\Big)\quad\lesssim_{C_{2}}\quad\frac{1}{\log n}.

∎

5
Proof of Theorem
1

In this section we prove
Theorem

1
.

From
Theorem

2
we know that
\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,n]\right)\sim\frac{c_{8}}{\log n}
. In what follows, we show that this probability stays the same order if we remove the
0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,n]
term, and also if we then replace
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}
by
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}[-n,0)
.

5.1
Removing the
0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,n]
term

This section is devoted to the proof of the first part of
Theorem

1
, i.e. showing that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset\right)\quad\asymp\quad\frac{1}{\log n}.

(5.1)

The lower bound is immediate from
Theorem

2
. The proof of the upper bound will rely on the following two results.

Firsly, instead of
\mathcal{T}
we consider a tree that does not hit 0 at positive times, but is weighted by the number of zeroes at non-positive times. We show the following.

Lemma 5.1
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\sim\>\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,\infty)}\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}(-\infty,0]\right)\right]+o\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right).

Secondly, we upper bound an expectation similar to the above one, but instead of considering the tree
\mathcal{T}
up to a given number of vertices in the past and the future, we consider it up to a given number of vertices along the spine. For an interval
I
, we write
\mathcal{T}^{I}
to mean the spine vertices of
\mathcal{T}
with indices in
I
, together with the trees hanging off them. We define
\mathcal{T}_{+}^{I}
analogously, but only keeping the parts with positive indices, and
\mathcal{T}_{-}^{I}
analogously, only keeping the negative indices. We show the following.

Lemma 5.2
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]}}\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{[0,m]}\right)\right]\quad\lesssim\quad\frac{1}{\log m}.

We start by presenting the proofs of Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
, and then explain how to conclude the proof of (
5.1
) from these.

Proof of
Lemma

5.1
.

By considering whether
\mathcal{T}\left[n^{\frac{1}{4}},n\right]
hits 0, and if not, considering the last visit of
\mathcal{T}\left[0,n^{\frac{1}{4}}\right]
at 0, we can write down an upper bound as follows,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset\right)\quad

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(0\in\mathcal{T}\left[n^{\frac{1}{4}},n\right]\right)+\sum_{j=0}^{n^{\frac{1}{4}}}\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset,\>\mathcal{T}_{j}=0,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}\left(j,n\right]\right).

The first term on the right-hand side of (
5.2
) can be bounded as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(0\in\mathcal{T}\left[n^{\frac{1}{4}},n\right]\right)\quad\leq\quad

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(0\in\mathcal{T}_{+}\left(\geq\log n\right)\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{\log n}\geq(\log n)^{4}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{(\log n)^{4}}d_{i}^{+}\geq(\log n)^{6}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{(\log n)^{6}}|{\mathrm{GW}}_{i}|>n^{\frac{1}{4}}\right).

(5.3)

Using that
G(x)\asymp\frac{1}{1+||x||^{4}}
, that
\mathbb{E}\left[d_{i}^{+}\right]\asymp\mathrm{Var}\left(d_{i}^{+}\right)\asymp 1
, and Lemmas
A.8
and
B.1
, we see that each of the above probabilities is
\ll\frac{1}{\log n}
.

Using
Lemma

2.2
, the sum in (

) can be bounded as follows.

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n^{\frac{1}{4}}}\>

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset,\>\mathcal{T}_{j}=0,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}\left(j,n\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{j=0}^{n^{\frac{1}{4}}}\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}[-n-j,n-j]=\emptyset,\>\mathcal{T}_{-j}=0,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}\left(0,n-j\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\quad\sum_{j=0}^{n^{\frac{1}{4}}}\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}\left[-n,n-n^{\frac{1}{4}}\right]=\emptyset,\>\mathcal{T}_{-j}=0,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}\left(0,n-n^{\frac{1}{4}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\quad\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}\left[-n,n-n^{\frac{1}{4}}\right]=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}\left(0,n-n^{\frac{1}{4}}\right]}\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}\left[-n^{\frac{1}{4}},0\right]\right)\right].

(5.4)

Analogously, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset\right)\quad\geq

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{j=0}^{n^{\frac{1}{4}}}\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}[-n,n]=\emptyset,\>\mathcal{T}_{j}=0,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}\left(j,n\right]\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}\left[-n-n^{\frac{1}{4}},n\right]=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}\left(0,\infty\right)}\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}\left[-n^{\frac{1}{4}},0\right]\right)\right].

(5.5)

Then by simple comparisons we can show that the expectations (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) differ from the expectation in
Lemma

5.1
by
o\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)
. This finishes the proof of
Lemma

4.4
.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

5.2
.

We will split the counting term
\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{[0,m]}\right)
based on the trees descending from different spine vertices, and use different estimates based on the value of the walk at the given spine vertex and the number of times that value has been visited by earlier spine vertices.

For a given

let
\tau^{j}_{k}
be the
j
th index
i
such that
X_{i}\in\partial B(0,k)
, and for a given
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
let
\tau^{j}_{x}
be the
j
th index
i
such that
X_{i}=x
. We set
\tau^{j}_{k}
or
\tau^{j}_{x}
to be
\infty
if such
i
does not exist. Then for any positive constants
c
and
C
we can bound the expectation in
Lemma

5.2
as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]}}\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{[0,m]}\right)\right]\>=\>\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]}}\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\ell}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\quad\sum_{x}

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq C\log(||x||)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\tau^{j}_{x}\leq m}\>{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]}}\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\tau^{j}_{x}}\right)\right]

(5.6)

\displaystyle+\quad\sum_{k\geq 1}

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq k^{4}}\quad

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\tau^{j}_{k}\leq m}\>{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]}}\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\tau^{j}_{k}}\right)\right]

(5.7)

\displaystyle+\quad\sum_{x}

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq C\log(||x||)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\tau^{j}_{x}\leq m}\>{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]}}\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\tau^{j}_{x}}\right)\>{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\tau^{j}_{x}}\right)\geq e^{c||x||^{3}}}\right]

(5.8)

\displaystyle+\quad\sum_{k\geq 1}

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq k^{4}}\quad

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\tau^{j}_{k}\leq m}\>{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]}}\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\tau^{j}_{k}}\right)\>{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\tau^{j}_{k}}\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}}}\right].

(5.9)

Now we discuss how to bound each of the above sums.

To bound (
5.7
), note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\tau^{j}_{k}\leq m}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]}}\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\tau^{j}_{k}}\right)\right]\quad

\displaystyle\leq\quad\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\tau^{j}_{k}\leq m}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]\setminus\{\tau^{j}_{k}\}}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]\setminus\{\tau^{j}_{k}\}}}\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\tau^{j}_{k}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\asymp\quad\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\tau^{j}_{k}\leq m}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]\setminus\{\tau^{j}_{k}\}}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]\setminus\{\tau^{j}_{k}\}}}\>g\left(X_{\tau^{j}_{k}}\right)\right]\quad

\displaystyle\lesssim\quad\frac{1}{k^{6}}\>\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\tau^{j}_{k}\leq m}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]}}\right].

In the last

has a bounded number of offsprings which have no further offsprings, and the BRW at these vertices takes the same value as it does at the next spine vertex.

This gives

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:E_numzeroes_k_few_returns}\quad\lesssim\quad\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{k^{4}}{k^{6}}\>\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\tau^{j}_{k}\leq m}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]}}\right]\quad\lesssim\quad\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\log m}\quad\asymp\quad\frac{1}{\log m}.

To bound (
5.6
), we pull out the term
\mathbb{E}\left[\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}^{\tau^{j}_{x}}_{-}\right)\right]
as above, and consider the tree obtained by disregarding the part of
\mathcal{T}
between the spine vertices of indices
\tau_{x}

\tau^{j}_{x}

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:E_numzeroes_x_many_returns}\quad\lesssim\quad\sum_{x}\sum_{j\geq C\log(||x||)}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{0}<\infty\right)^{j-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{x}\leq m,\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,m]}=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,m]}\right)g(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\quad\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{7}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{0}<\infty\right)^{C\log k}\cdot\frac{1}{\log m}\cdot\frac{1}{k^{6}}.

If

. Then the above sum is
\lesssim\frac{1}{\log m}
.

To bound (
5.8
), we again consider a tree obtained by omitting the part of
\mathcal{T}
between spine vertices
\tau_{x}
and
\tau^{j}_{x}
, and we pull out the term
\mathbb{E}\left[\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}^{\tau^{j}_{x}}_{-}\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}^{\tau^{j}_{x}}_{-}\right)\geq e^{c||x||^{3}}}\right]
. This expectation can be bounded as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}^{\tau^{j}_{x}}_{-}\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}^{\tau^{j}_{x}}_{-}\right)\geq e^{c||x||^{3}}}\right]\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{s\geq e^{c||x||^{3}}}\mathbb{P}\left(\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}^{\tau^{j}_{x}}_{-}\right)\geq s\right)

\displaystyle\leq\quad\sum_{s\geq e^{c||x||^{3}}}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}^{\tau^{j}_{x}}_{-}\right)^{2}\right]}{s^{2}}\quad\lesssim\quad e^{-c||x||^{3}}.

This then gives

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:E_numzeroes_x_few_returns}\>\lesssim\>\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{7}\cdot C(\log k)\cdot\sup_{x\in\partial B(0,k)}\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{x}\leq m,\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,m]}=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,m]}\right)\cdot e^{-c||x||^{3}}\>\lesssim\>\frac{1}{\log m}.\quad

Finally, we bound (
5.9
) as follows.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}
be a BRW on an infinite random tree that is distributed like
\mathcal{T}
, but with the root having the same offspring distribution on the two sides of the spine as the later spine vertices. Let
T_{i}
be BRWs on
\mathrm{GW}(\mu)
trees. Let
e
and

be uniform neighbours of 0 in
\mathbb{Z}^{8}
. Let all of these variables be independent of each other and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
.

By considering the part of
\mathcal{T}
after the spine vertex
\tau^{j}_{k}
, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq k^{4}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,2m]}=\emptyset}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{0\not\in\mathcal{T}_{+}^{[0,2m]}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\tau^{j}_{k}\leq m}\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\tau^{j}_{k}}\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\#\left(0\text{ in }\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\tau^{j}_{k}}\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\>\sum_{j\geq k^{4}}\mathbb{P}\left(\tau^{j}_{k}\leq m\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\sup_{x\in\partial B(0,k)}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{x\not\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}}\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\left(x+e+\widehat{\mathcal{T}}^{[0,m]}\right)=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\left(x+e+\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{+}^{[0,m]}\right)\;\middle|\;\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}^{+}}\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+e_{i}+T_{i})\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+e_{i}+T_{i})\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap(x+e_{i}+T_{i})=\emptyset}\;\middle|\;\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\right]\Bigg].

(5.10)

We can see that
\mathbb{P}\left(\tau^{j}_{k}<m\right)\leq\left(\sup\limits_{x\in\partial B(0,k)}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{k}<\infty\right)\right)^{j}
. Using this and

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq k^{4}}\mathbb{P}\left(\tau^{j}_{k}<m\right)\quad\lesssim\quad\left(1-\frac{c^{\prime}}{k}\right)^{k^{4}}k\quad\lesssim\quad e^{-c^{\prime\prime}k^{3}},

where

and
c^{\prime\prime}
are small positive constants.

Now we explain how to bound the expectation on the right-hand side of (
5.10
) by comparing it to
\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}^{[0,m]},\>0\not\in\mathcal{T}^{[0,m]}_{+}\right)
.

We will use the following observation. Let
t
be any finite plane tree and let
v
be any leaf of
t
at distance
\ell
from the root. Let
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}
be as above and let
T
denote a (planar)
\mathrm{GW}(\mu)
tree and a random walk indexed by this tree. Then the following two events have the same probability,
\prod_{u\in t\setminus\{v\}}\mu\left(\deg_{t}(u)\right)
. (i) The tree
T
is isomorphic to a tree that is obtained from
t
by attaching to it a (possibly empty) tree from
v
. (ii) The subtree of
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}
formed by the spine vertices of indices
[0,\ell]
and the trees on the two sides of the spine descending from the spine vertices of indices
[0,\ell-1]
is isomporphic to
t
, with the
\ell
th spine vertex corresponding to
v
.

By considering the first 0 in
(x+T)
and using the above property, we can see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(0\in(x+T),\>\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+T)\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}},\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap(x+T)=\emptyset\;\middle|\;\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\right)

\displaystyle\>\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\Big((x+\widehat{X})\text{ hits }0,\>0\not\in(x+\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{+})^{[0,{\tau}_{0}-1]},\>\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{-})^{[0,{\tau}_{0}-1]}\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}}-1,

\displaystyle\hskip 227.62204pt\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\left((x+\widehat{\mathcal{T}})^{[0,{\tau}_{0}-1]}\cup\{0\}\right)=\emptyset\>\Big|\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\Big)

\displaystyle\>\lesssim\quad\mathbb{P}\Big((x+\widehat{X})\text{ hits }0,\>0\not\in(x+\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{+})^{[0,{\tau}_{0}]},\>\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{-})^{[0,{\tau}_{0}]}\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}},

\displaystyle\hskip 256.0748pt\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap(x+\widehat{\mathcal{T}})^{[0,{\tau}_{0}]}=\emptyset\>\Big|\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\Big),

where
\tau_{0}
refers to the first time
(x+\widehat{X})
hits 0.
If
\eta=\max\{i\in[0,{\tau}_{0}]:\>(x+\widehat{X})(i)=x\}
then by restricting to
(x+\widehat{\mathcal{T}})^{[\eta,{\tau}_{0}]}
and viewing this tree and BRW from the
{\tau}_{0}
th spine vertex, we can upper bound the above probability as

\displaystyle\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\Big((x+\widehat{X})\text{ hits }0,\>0\not\in(x+\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{+})^{[\eta,{\tau}_{0}]},\>\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{-})^{[\eta,{\tau}_{0}]}\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}},

\displaystyle\hskip 270.30118pt\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap(x+\widehat{\mathcal{T}})^{[\eta,{\tau}_{0}]}=\emptyset\>\Big|\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\Big)

\displaystyle=\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\tau}_{x}<\infty,\>0\not\in\widehat{X}(0,\widehat{\tau}_{x}],\>0\not\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{+}^{[0,\widehat{\tau}_{x}]},\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{-}^{[0,\widehat{\tau}_{x}]}\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}},\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{T}}^{[0,\widehat{\tau}_{x}]}=\emptyset\;\middle|\;\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\tau}_{x}<\infty,\>0\not\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{+}^{[0,\widehat{\tau}_{x}]},\>\#\left(0\text{ in }\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{-}^{[0,\widehat{\tau}_{x}]}\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}},\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{T}}^{[0,\widehat{\tau}_{x}]}=\emptyset\;\middle|\;\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\right).

Also, by the Paley-Zygmund inequality we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(0\in(x+T),\>\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+T)\right)\leq e^{c||x||^{3}},\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap(x+T)=\emptyset\;\middle|\;\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\geq\quad\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+T)\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+T)\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap(x+T)=\emptyset}\;\middle|\;\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\right]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\left[\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+T)\right)^{2}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+T)\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap(x+T)=\emptyset}\;\middle|\;\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\right]}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\quad e^{-ck^{3}}\>\mathbb{E}\left[\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+T)\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+T)\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap(x+T)=\emptyset}\;\middle|\;\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\right].

Combining these and using that
\mathbb{E}\left[d_{1}^{+}\right]\asymp 1
, and with positive probability the trees hanging off a given spine vertex do not produce values for the BRW that are not already present at the rest of the tree, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{x\not\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}}\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\left(x+e+\widehat{\mathcal{T}}^{[0,m]}\right)=\emptyset,\>0\not\in\left(x+e+\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{+}^{[0,m]}\right)\;\middle|\;\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}^{+}}\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+e_{i}+T_{i})\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\#\left(0\text{ in }(x+e_{i}+T_{i})\right)\leq e^{ck^{3}}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap(x+e_{i}+T_{i})=\emptyset}\;\middle|\;\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\right]\Bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim\quad e^{ck^{3}}\>\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\tau}_{x}<\infty,\>0\not\in\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{+}^{[0,\widehat{\tau}_{x}+m]},\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{T}}^{[0,\widehat{\tau}_{x}+m]}=\emptyset\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 128.0374pt\lesssim\quad e^{ck^{3}}\>\mathbb{P}\left(0\not\in{\mathcal{T}}_{+}^{[0,m]},\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap{\mathcal{T}}^{[0,m]}=\emptyset\right)\quad\lesssim\quad e^{ck^{3}}\>\frac{1}{\log m}\>.

Overall this shows that for sufficiently small values of
c
we have

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:E_numzeroes_k_many_returns}\quad\lesssim\quad\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{ck^{3}}\>e^{-c^{\prime\prime}k^{3}}\>\frac{1}{\log m}\quad\lesssim\quad\frac{1}{\log m}.

This finishes the proof of
Lemma

5.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Using the definition of
\mathrm{BCap}\left(\cdot\right)
,
Lemma

2.2
, the property that
\left(\xi^{\ell}_{n}\mid\xi^{\ell}_{n}+\xi^{r}_{n}=m\right)\sim\mathrm{Unif}\!\left(\left\{0,1,...,m\right\}\right)
, and
Theorem

4
, we can see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathrm{BCap}\left(\mathcal{T}\left[0,\xi^{\ell}_{n}+\xi^{r}_{n}\right]\right)}{\xi^{\ell}_{n}+\xi^{r}_{n}+1}\right]\quad=\quad\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi^{\ell}_{n}+\xi^{r}_{n}=m\right)\frac{1}{m+1}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{BCap}\left(\mathcal{T}[0,m]\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi^{\ell}_{n}+\xi^{r}_{n}=m\right)\frac{1}{m+1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{T}_{i}\not\in\mathcal{T}(i+1,m],\>\left(\mathcal{T}_{i}+\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\right)\cap\mathcal{T}[0,m]=\emptyset\right)

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi^{\ell}_{n}+\xi^{r}_{n}=m\right)\frac{1}{m+1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{T}_{0}\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,m-i],\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{-}\cap\mathcal{T}[-i,m-i]=\emptyset\right)

\displaystyle=\quad\mathbb{P}\left(0\not\in\mathcal{T}(0,\xi^{r}_{n}],\>\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}(-\infty,0)\cap\mathcal{T}[-\xi^{\ell}_{n},\xi^{r}_{n}]=\emptyset\right)\quad\sim\quad\frac{c_{8}}{\log n}.

Using this, we can bound
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{BCap}\left(\mathcal{T}[0,n]\right)\right]
as follows.

For notational convenience write
\zeta_{k}=\xi^{\ell}_{k}+\xi^{r}_{k}
and
\mathcal{B}(k)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{BCap}\left(\mathcal{T}[0,k]\right)\right]
. By the subadditivity of

and the shift invariance of
\mathcal{T}
we have
\frac{\mathcal{B}(k\ell)}{k\ell}\leq\frac{\mathcal{B}(k)}{k}
for any

, hence
\frac{\mathcal{B}(n)}{n}\leq\frac{\mathcal{B}(k)}{k}\frac{n+k}{n}
for any

.

Using this, the simple bound
\mathcal{B}(k)\leq k+1
, the concentration of
\zeta_{k}
, and the already established bound on
\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{B}(\zeta_{k})}{k+1}\right]
, we can write

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{B}(n)}{n}\quad\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\zeta_{\frac{n}{\log n}}>\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right)\frac{n+1}{n}+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(\zeta_{\frac{n}{\log n}}\right)}{\zeta_{\frac{n}{\log n}}}\right]\frac{n+\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}}{n}\quad\sim\quad\frac{c_{8}}{\log n}\>,

\displaystyle\frac{c_{8}}{\log n}\quad\sim\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(\zeta_{n(\log n)^{3}}\right)}{\zeta_{n(\log n)^{3}}+1}\right]\quad\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\zeta_{n(\log n)^{3}}\leq n\log n\right)+\frac{\mathcal{B}(n)}{n}\frac{n\log n+n}{n\log n}

\displaystyle\hskip 298.75394pt\sim\quad\frac{\mathcal{B}(n)}{n}+o\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right).

This finishes the proof of Theorem
3
.

Lemma A.1
.

A critical Galton-Watson tree
\mathrm{GW}
satisfies

\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathrm{GW}|\geq k\right)\>\sim\>\frac{c}{\sqrt{k}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathrm{GW}|{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{|\mathrm{GW}|\leq k}\right]\>\sim\>c\sqrt{k},

where
c
is a positive constant depending only on
\sigma^{2}
.

Proof.

For the first result, first note that

\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathrm{GW}|=k\right)\quad=\quad\mathbb{P}\left(W_{k}=-1,\>W_{1},...,W_{k-1}\geq 0\right)\quad=\quad\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{P}\left(W_{k}=-1\right).

(The second equality follows e.g. from applying
[
16
, Theorem 1]
to the increments of
-W
.)
By
[
10
, Theorem 2.3.9]
we know that
\mathbb{P}\left(W_{k}=-1\right)\sim\frac{c^{\prime}}{\sqrt{k}}
, where
c^{\prime}
is a positive constant depending only on
\sigma^{2}
. This then gives

\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathrm{GW}|\geq k\right)\sim\sum_{\ell\geq k}\frac{c^{\prime}}{\ell^{\frac{3}{2}}}\sim\frac{c}{\sqrt{k}}
as required.

Writing
\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathrm{GW}|{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{|\mathrm{GW}|\leq k}\right]=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathrm{GW}|\geq j\right)-k\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathrm{GW}|\geq k+1\right)
and using the first result, we also get the second result.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

The ladder width
\mathrm{LW}
and ladder height
\mathrm{LH}
corresponding to a critical walk with steps
\sim(\mu-1)
satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{LW}\geq k\right)\>

\displaystyle\asymp\>\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{LW}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathrm{LW}\leq k}\right]\>\asymp\>\sqrt{k},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{LH}\geq k\right)\>

\displaystyle\lesssim\>\frac{1}{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{LH}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathrm{LH}\leq k}\right]\>\asymp\>1,

where the implicit constants in the
\asymp
’s and the
\lesssim
only depend on
\sigma^{2}
.

Proof.

We have

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{LW}\geq k\right)\>=\>\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}<0\text{ for }t\in\{1,2,...,k-1\}\right)\>\leq\>\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\neq 0\text{ for }t\in\{1,2,...,k-1\}\right).

From
[
12
, Theorem 2.26]
we know that this is
\leq\frac{4\sigma}{\sqrt{k-1}}
.

For the other bounds note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{LW}=k,\>\mathrm{LH}=\ell\right)\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\text{the records on }[0,k]\text{ are }0\text{ and }k;\>W_{k}=\ell\right)

\displaystyle=\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\text{the right minima on }[0,k]\text{ are }0\text{ and }k;\>W_{k}=\ell\right)

\displaystyle=\quad\mathbb{P}\left(v_{k}\text{ is a child of }v_{0};\text{ there are }\ell\text{ later children}\right).

This means that
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{LH}=\ell\right)=\mu(\geq\ell+1)
, so using that
\mu
has finite second moment we get
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{LH}\right]\asymp 1
and
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{LH}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathrm{LH}\leq k}\right]\asymp 1
for all
k
. This also immediately implies
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{LH}\geq k\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{k}
.

Finally, note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{LW}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\mathrm{LW}\leq n}\right]\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\>\mathbb{P}\left(v_{k}\text{ is a child of }v_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left[{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{v_{0}\text{ has }\geq\ell\text{ children}}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\text{index of }\ell\text{th child is }\leq k}\left(\text{index of }\ell\text{th child}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\mu(\geq\ell)\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}|\mathrm{GW}_{i}|+1\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}|\mathrm{GW}_{i}|+1\leq k}\right].

(A.1)

On one hand this is

\displaystyle\leq\quad\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\mu(\geq\ell)\left((\ell-1)\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathrm{GW}|{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{|\mathrm{GW}|\leq k}\right]+1\right)\quad\lesssim\quad\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\ell\mu(\geq\ell)\right)\cdot\sqrt{k}\quad\lesssim\quad\sqrt{k},

where we used
Lemma

A.1
and that
\mu
has a bounded second moment.

On the other hand (
A.1
) is

\displaystyle\geq\quad\mu(\geq 2)\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathrm{GW}|{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{|\mathrm{GW}|\leq k-1}\right]\quad\asymp\quad\sqrt{k},

where we used
Lemma

A.1
and that
\mu
has mean 1 and variance
\asymp 1
, hence
\mu(\geq 2)\gtrsim 1
.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

A critical Galton-Watson tree
\mathrm{GW}
satisfies

1-\mathbb{E}\left[(1-\alpha)^{|\mathrm{GW}|}\right]\>\asymp\>\sqrt{\alpha}\qquad\text{as }\alpha\to 0,

where the implicit constants in
\asymp
only depend on
\sigma^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
n=\frac{1}{\alpha}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(1-\alpha)^{|\mathrm{GW}|}\right]\quad

\displaystyle\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathrm{GW}|>n\right)+\mathbb{E}\left[(1-\alpha)^{|\mathrm{GW}|}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{|\mathrm{GW}|\leq n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathrm{GW}|>n\right)+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}|\mathrm{GW}|\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{|\mathrm{GW}|\leq n}\right]\quad

\displaystyle=\quad 1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathrm{GW}|{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{|\mathrm{GW}|\leq n}\right],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(1-\alpha)^{|\mathrm{GW}|}\right]\quad

\displaystyle\geq\quad\mathbb{E}\left[(1-\alpha)^{|\mathrm{GW}|}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{|\mathrm{GW}|\leq n}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\alpha|\mathrm{GW}|\right){\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{|\mathrm{GW}|\leq n}\right]\quad

\displaystyle=\quad 1-\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathrm{GW}|>n\right)-\alpha\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathrm{GW}|{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{|\mathrm{GW}|\leq n}\right].

From
Lemma

A.1
we know that
\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathrm{GW}|{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{|\mathrm{GW}|\leq n}\right]\asymp\sqrt{n}\asymp\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
, and
\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathrm{GW}|>n\right)\asymp\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\asymp\alpha
, which finishes the proof.

Lemma A.6
.

For
\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{1}
as in (
3.14
) we have

1-\theta_{0}\quad\asymp\quad 1-\theta_{1}\quad\asymp\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}},

where the implicit constants in
\asymp
only depend on
\sigma^{2}
.

Proof.

By considering the number of offsprings of the root in a
\mathrm{GW}
tree, we see that

\widetilde{\theta}\quad=\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda^{|\mathrm{GW}|}\right]\quad=\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda\>\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda^{|\mathrm{GW}|}\right]^{d_{0}^{+}}\right]\quad=\quad\lambda\>\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\theta}^{d_{0}^{+}}\right],

i.e.
\theta_{0}=\lambda^{-1}\widetilde{\theta}
. Using
Corollary

A.5
and that
\lambda=1-\frac{1}{n}
, we get that
1-\theta_{0}\asymp\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
.

To get the estimate for
\theta_{1}
, note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\theta}^{d_{1}^{+}}\right]\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{k\geq 0}\widetilde{\theta}^{k}\>\mathbb{P}\left(d_{1}^{+}=k\right)\quad=\quad\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\widetilde{\theta}^{k}\>\mu(k+\ell+1)\quad=\quad\sum_{m\geq 1}\mu(m)\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\widetilde{\theta}^{k}\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{m\geq 1}\mu(m)\frac{1-\widetilde{\theta}^{m}}{1-\widetilde{\theta}}\quad=\quad\sum_{m\geq 0}\mu(m)\frac{1-\widetilde{\theta}^{m}}{1-\widetilde{\theta}}\quad=\quad\frac{1-\theta_{0}}{1-\widetilde{\theta}},

hence
1-\theta_{1}=\frac{\theta_{0}-\widetilde{\theta}}{1-\widetilde{\theta}}=\frac{\lambda^{-1}\widetilde{\theta}(1-\lambda)}{1-\widetilde{\theta}}\asymp\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
as required.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

We have

\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{1-\widetilde{\theta}}\quad\sim\quad\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\>.

Proof.

A quick calculation shows that
\mathbb{E}\left[d_{1}^{+}\right]=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}
. Note that we have

\displaystyle\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{1-\widetilde{\theta}}\quad=\quad\frac{1-\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\theta}^{d_{1}^{+}}\right]}{1-\widetilde{\theta}}\quad=\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{d_{1}^{+}-1}\widetilde{\theta}^{\ell}\right]\quad\leq\quad\mathbb{E}\left[d_{1}^{+}\right].

For the lower bound note that

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{d_{1}^{+}-1}\widetilde{\theta}^{\ell}\right]\quad\geq\quad\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\widetilde{\theta}^{\ell}\mathbb{P}\left(d_{1}^{+}\geq\ell+1\right)\quad\geq\quad\widetilde{\theta}^{L}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\mathbb{P}\left(d_{1}^{+}\geq\ell+1\right).

We know that
\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\mathbb{P}\left(d_{1}^{+}\geq\ell+1\right)\uparrow\mathbb{E}\left[d_{1}^{+}\right]
as
L\to\infty
, and that for any fixed value of
L
, we have
\widetilde{\theta}^{L}\uparrow 1
as
n\to\infty
. So choosing
L
sufficiently large and then
n
sufficiently large in terms of
L
, we can make the right-hand side arbitrarily close to
\mathbb{E}\left[d_{1}^{+}\right]
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.8
.

For any
s>1
we have

\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\quad\lesssim\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathrm{GW}_{i}|\geq n^{2}s\right)\quad\lesssim\quad\frac{\sqrt{\log s}}{\sqrt{s}},

where the implicit constants in
\lesssim
only depend on
\sigma^{2}
.

Proof.

Using
Lemma

A.1
we can lower bound as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathrm{GW}_{i}|\geq n^{2}s\right)\quad\geq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathrm{GW}_{i}|\geq n^{2}s\text{ for some }i\in\{1,2,...,n\}\right)

\displaystyle=\quad 1-\left(1-\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathrm{GW}|\geq n^{2}s\right)\right)^{n}\quad\geq\quad 1-\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{n\sqrt{s}}\right)^{n}\quad\gtrsim\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\>.

For the upper bound let
m=\frac{n^{2}s}{\log s}
, and let
\xi_{m}\sim\mathrm{Geom}_{\geq 0}\!\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)
. Let
(\mathrm{GW}_{i})
be an infinite sequence of independent
\mathrm{GW}
trees and let us index their vertices by the nonnegative integers, going around anticlockwise on each tree in order. Then the number
\zeta
of trees with all vertices having index
\leq\xi_{m}
is distributed as
\mathrm{Geom}_{\geq 0}\!\left(1-\widetilde{\theta}\right)
, and from
Lemma

A.3
we know that
1-\widetilde{\theta}\asymp\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}
. Using these we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathrm{GW}_{i}|\geq n^{2}s\right)\quad\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{m}\geq n^{2}s\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\zeta<n\right)\quad\lesssim\quad e^{-\frac{n^{2}s}{m}}+\frac{n}{\sqrt{m}}\quad\asymp\frac{\sqrt{\log s}}{\sqrt{s}}\>.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Appendix B
Estimates regarding SRWs

Lemma B.1

(
[
10
]
, Proposition 2.4.5)
.

Let
X
be a SRW on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, starting from 0, let
R>0
, let
\tau_{R}:=\inf\{n\geq 0:\>||X_{n}||\geq R\}
and let
s\geq 1
. Then we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{R}>sR^{2}\right)\quad\lesssim\quad e^{-cs}

where
c
is a positive constant.

Also, for
\alpha\lesssim 1
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{R}<\alpha R^{2}\right)\quad\lesssim\quad e^{-\frac{\widetilde{c}}{\alpha}},

where
\widetilde{c}
is a positive constant.

Lemma B.2
.

If
d>2
,
\alpha\ll 1
, and
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
satisfies
||x||\geq\frac{c_{0}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
for some positive constant
c_{0}
, then

\displaystyle g_{\alpha}(x)\quad\lesssim\quad e^{-c||x||\sqrt{\alpha}}g(x),

where
c
is a positive constant.

Also, if
||x||\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
for some positive constant
C_{0}
, then

\displaystyle g(x)-g_{\alpha}(x)\quad\lesssim\quad\left(x\sqrt{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2d(d-2)}{d^{2}+2d-4}}g(x).

Proof.

For the first bound note that
g_{\alpha}(x)\lesssim\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{\frac{1}{2}||x||}<\mathrm{Geom}_{\geq 0}\!\left(\alpha\right)\right)g(x)
, and

\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{\frac{1}{2}||x||}<\mathrm{Geom}_{\geq 0}\!\left(\alpha\right)\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{\frac{1}{2}||x||}<\frac{||x||}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Geom}_{\geq 0}\!\left(\alpha\right)>\frac{||x||}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)
. The last two probabilities are both
\lesssim e^{-c||x||\sqrt{\alpha}}
.

For the second bound, start by noting that for any
t
and
R
with
R\geq 2||x||
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\#\left(\text{visits at }x\text{ after time }(t-1)\right)\right]\quad=\quad\mathbb{E}\left[g(X_{t},x)\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\quad\sum_{y\in B\left(x,\frac{1}{2}||x||\right)}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=y\right)g(x,y)+\sum_{y\in B(0,R)\setminus B\left(x,\frac{1}{2}||x||\right)}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=y\right)g(x,y)

\displaystyle\hskip 256.0748pt+\sum_{y\in B(0,R)^{c}}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=y\right)g(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\quad\sum_{y\in B\left(x,\frac{1}{2}||x||\right)}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}\frac{1}{||x-y||^{d-2}}\quad+\quad\frac{R^{d}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}\frac{1}{||x||^{d-2}}\quad+\quad\frac{1}{R^{d-2}}\quad

\displaystyle\hskip 227.62204pt\lesssim\quad g(x)\left(\frac{||x||^{d}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}\>+\>\frac{R^{d}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}\>+\>\frac{||x||^{d-2}}{R^{d-2}}\right).

Then for
||x||\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
we have

\displaystyle g(x)-g_{\alpha}(x)\quad=\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\#\left(\text{visits at }x\text{ after time }\mathrm{Geom}_{\geq 0}\!\left(\alpha\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Geom}_{\geq 0}\!\left(\alpha\right)\leq t\right)g(x)\quad+\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\#\left(\text{visits at }x\text{ after time }(t-1)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\quad g(x)\left(t\alpha\>+\>\frac{||x||^{d}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}\>+\>\frac{R^{d}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}\>+\>\frac{||x||^{d-2}}{R^{d-2}}\right).

Choosing
R
and
t
such that
t\alpha\asymp\frac{R^{d}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}\asymp\frac{||x||^{d-2}}{R^{d-2}}
and
R\geq 2||x||
, we get that this is
\lesssim\left(x\sqrt{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2d(d-2)}{d^{2}+2d-4}}g(x)
as required.
∎

Lemma B.3
.

Let
\alpha\ll 1
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}k(1-\alpha)^{k}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=x\right)\quad\lesssim\quad\sum_{k\geq 1}k\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=x\right)\quad\asymp\quad(g\star g)(x).

In case
d>2
and
||x||\geq\frac{c_{0}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
for a positive constant
c_{0}
, we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}k(1-\alpha)^{k}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=x\right)\quad\lesssim\quad e^{-c_{1}||x||\sqrt{\alpha}}(g\star g)(x).

Also, for
d>2
and
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}k(1-\alpha)^{k}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=x\right)\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=y\right)\quad

\displaystyle\lesssim\quad\sum_{k\geq 1}k\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=k\right)\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=y\right)\quad

\displaystyle\asymp\quad\frac{1}{||x||^{2d-4}+||y||^{2d-4}},

and in case
||x||+||y||\geq\frac{c_{0}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
for a positive constant
c_{0}
, we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}k(1-\alpha)^{k}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=x\right)\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=y\right)\quad\lesssim\quad e^{-c_{2}(||x||+||y||)\sqrt{\alpha}}\frac{1}{||x||^{2d-4}+||y||^{2d-4}}.

The positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
only depend on
\sigma^{2}
, while the implicit constants in the
\lesssim
’s and
\asymp
’s only depend on
\sigma^{2}
and
c_{0}
.

Proof.

The
\lesssim
and
\asymp
in the first line are immediate. To prove the second
\lesssim
, start by writing

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}k(1-\alpha)^{k}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=x\right)\quad=\quad\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}(1-\alpha)^{k}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=x\right)\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq\ell}(1-\alpha)^{k}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=x\right)\quad=\quad\sum_{\ell\geq 1}(1-\alpha)^{\ell}\mathbb{E}\left[g_{\alpha}(x-X_{\ell})\right]\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{y}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}(1-\alpha)^{\ell}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{\ell}=y\right)g_{\alpha}(x-y)\quad=\quad\sum_{y}g_{\alpha}(y)g_{\alpha}(x-y).

Note that for any
y
we have
||y||\geq\frac{1}{2}||x||
or
||x-y||\geq\frac{1}{2}||x||
, so in case
||x||\geq\frac{c_{0}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
, by
Lemma

B.2
we have
\sum_{y}g_{\alpha}(y)g_{\alpha}(x-y)\lesssim\sum_{y}e^{-c_{1}||x||}g(y)g(x-y)
as required.

Note that
\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=x\right)\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=y\right)=\mathbb{P}\left((X_{k},Y_{k})=(x,y)\right)
where
X
and
Y
are two independents SRWs on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then
(X,Y)
is a SRW on a copy of
\mathbb{Z}^{2d}
induced by the pairs of points in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
where the sum of their coordinates is even. We can finish the proof by using the results from the first part of the lemma for
(X,Y)
.
∎

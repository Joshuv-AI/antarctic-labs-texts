Introduction

According to the global cancer data of 2023, colorectal cancer (CRC) ranks third in both incidence and mortality among all malignant tumours.1 Surgery remains the primary treatment for resectable rectal cancer.2 3 And outcomes have remarkably improved during the last 20 years, mostly owing to the introduction of total mesorectal excision (TME).4 In recent decades, minimally invasive surgery has been increasingly performed to treat rectal cancer. Multiple studies5,8 have consistently demonstrated that minimally invasive surgery for rectal cancer offers similar disease-free and overall survival rates, locoregional recurrence rates, resection margins, safety and completeness of resection when compared with open surgery. It remains unclear whether surgical manipulation of tumours facilitates the release of tumour cells into the bloodstream, potentially increasing the risk of distant metastases.9,11 Theoretically, the risk of tumour cell dissemination might be minimised by ligating the effluent vein first.12 However, there is no regulation in the current guidelines on the sequence of ligation of the inferior mesenteric artery and vein during rectal cancer surgery owing to a lack of sufficient evidence.

Recently, several studies have demonstrated that circulating tumour cells (CTCs) in peripheral blood can be used as potential indicators for predicting postoperative recurrence and prognosis in patients with CRC.13,17 Our pilot study18 suggests that the vein first approach may be more effective in reducing peripheral blood levels of CTCs. However, this study had a small sample size and was not a randomised controlled trial. Therefore, we conducted a prospective, multicentre, randomised clinical trial to assess whether vascular ligation sequence affects the dissemination of CTCs into the bloodstream and survival during minimally invasive rectal cancer surgery.

We hypothesise that in minimally invasive rectal cancer surgery, prioritising ligation of the inferior mesenteric vein (V-first) compared with the inferior mesenteric artery (A-first) will significantly reduce postoperative levels of CTCs.

Methods and analysis

Design

This study is a superiority trial. The main purpose is to investigate whether the priority of ligating the inferior mesenteric vein over the inferior mesenteric artery in minimally invasive rectal cancer surgery can reduce the levels of CTCs in peripheral blood.

This study is designed as a prospective, multicentre, randomised, two-arm, parallel group, participant blind, clinical trial. Patients with rectal cancer will be divided into two groups: priority ligation of the inferior mesenteric vein (V-first) group and priority ligation of the inferior mesenteric artery (A-first) group. Peripheral venous blood samples will be collected after anaesthesia induction and immediately following incision closure to measure CTC levels. Adjuvant radiotherapy and chemotherapy will be conducted in accordance with the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines. All patients will be required to undergo postoperative examinations every 3 months for the first 2 years and every 6 months thereafter until 5 years after the surgery. Signs of suspected disease relapse including local recurrence and distant metastasis will be closely observed and monitored. The schedule of this study is shown in table 1. Our study is expected to last for 4 years, with 1 year for patient recruitment and 3 years for follow-up.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint of this study is the change of peripheral blood CTC levels before and after surgery. Secondary endpoints are 3-year disease-free survival (DFS), 3-year overall survival (OS), 3-year recurrence pattern, intraoperative blood loss, operation duration, conversion rate, number of lymph nodes collected, intraoperative morbidity, postoperative morbidity and mortality and postoperative recovery course.

CTC results measured (preoperative and postoperative) differ by 15% or more; it is considered that there is a difference between the two results. If the results between the two results (preoperative and postoperative) are less than 15%, it is considered that there is no difference between the two results. DFS is defined as the time from randomisation to the time of finding the recurrence or metastasis. OS is defined as the time from randomisation to the time of death. Local recurrence is defined as tumour recurrence in the surgical region, with or without elevated serum tumour markers, and the presence of an anastomotic mass must be confirmed through pathological biopsy. A distant metastasis is defined as a metastasis that is discovered in the lung, liver, bone or other sites by MRI, CT or radionuclide scanning, with or without pathological examination. Morbidity was graded according to the Clavien-Dindo surgical complication system.19 The postoperative recovery course includes factors such as the time for gas expulsion, initiation of food intake, removal of drainage tube, removal of urinary catheter and duration of postoperative hospital stay.

Hospital and surgeon eligibility

Participating centres include Sichuan Cancer Hospital and Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital. Site activation requires local ethics approval prior to patient enrolment. The participating surgeons were required to have performed at least 100 minimally invasive TME operations and submitted at least three unedited and anonymous videos of minimally invasive TME procedures to an Academic Credentialing Committee for review.

Ethical considerations

The study was approved by the ethics committee of Sichuan Cancer Hospital (approval number: SCCHEC-02-2024-102), and it has been registered on ClinicalTrials.gov with identifier NCT05807646. All eligible participants and their legal surrogates will be fully informed of the potential risks and benefits of the interventions in each group. Only patients who consent by providing written informed consent will be enrolled in the trial.

Eligibility criteria

The study subjects are patients with rectal cancer. The inclusion, exclusion and exit criteria were described in our pilot study.18 The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age: 18–75 years; (2) histopathologically confirmed as rectal adenocarcinoma (tumour located within 15 cm from the anal verge at rigid colonoscopy); (3) patients with a stage I–III rectal cancer eligible for surgery and R0 resection is expected, patients with pelvic lateral lymph node metastasis are ineligible; (4) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) score: 0–1; (5) American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score: I/II/III; (6) minimally invasive surgery; (7) signed informed consent.

The exclusion criteria included: (1) patients who have received preoperative treatment (such as preoperative radiotherapy and chemotherapy); (2) receiving transanal TME, specimen extraction through natural lumen or endoscopic tumour resection; (3) recurrent rectal cancer; (4) simultaneous or metachronous CRC; (5) malignant tumours of other organs in the past 5 years or at the same time; (6) the results of preoperative physical examination and imaging examination showed that: (a) the tumour involved the surrounding organs and required combined organ resection; (b) distant metastasis; (c) could not be resected at R0; (7) pregnant or lactating women; (8) patients with severe mental disorder; (9) it is not suitable for patients undergoing minimally invasive surgery (such as extensive adhesion caused by previous abdominal surgery or inability to tolerate artificial pneumoperitoneum); (10) history of unstable angina pectoris or myocardial infarction in the past 6 months; (11) history of cerebrovascular accident in the past 6 months; (12) systemic administration of corticosteroids within 1 month before enrolment; (13) taking folic acid-related drugs within half a year before operation; (14) severe cardiac insufficiency (FEV1<50% of predicted values); (15) emergency surgery.

The exit criteria included: (1) simultaneous ligation of the inferior mesenteric artery and vein during surgery, or the order of ligation cannot be distinguished; (2) patients with distant metastases found during surgical exploration or confirmed by postoperative pathological examination.

Preoperative evaluation

Before randomisation and surgery, patients undergo routine physical examination and laboratory tests. A diagnosis of rectal cancer must be pathologically confirmed, using the biopsy specimen acquired from the colonoscopy. Chest CT and abdomen enhanced CT or MRI are routinely performed to determine whether there is distant metastasis. Digital rectal examination and rectal high-resolution MRI are used to determine the location of tumour, the depth of tumour invasion and regional lymph node metastasis. Upper abdominal MRI, contrast-enhanced ultrasound of the liver, whole body bone scan and positron emission tomography-CT will be selectively used to provide accurate preoperative staging if necessary.

Randomisation and blinding

Patients enrolled in this study will be randomly assigned into either the V-first Group or the A-first Group. The computer randomisation will be conducted using RedCAP to ensure that patients are randomly divided into two groups with an allocation ratio of 1:1. All the patients are blinded to the surgical approach that they are going to receive and will be required to sign the informed consent before being enrolled in the study. Obviously, it is impossible to blind the surgeons who will perform the surgeries. However, all surgeon researchers will be instructed to minimise any potential cointerventions to ensure the study’s integrity. This trial does not involve any additional cointerventions beyond the vascular ligation sequence. All other surgical techniques strictly adhere to standardised TME principles. In addition, the result assessment will be made by independent researchers who are blinded to the surgical procedures.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated based on our preliminary investigation.18 The preliminary results showed that a decrease in folate receptor-positive circulating tumour cells (FR+CTCs) after surgery was observed in 36% of patients in the V-first group and 20% of patients in the A-first group. We assumed a minimum difference of 16% differences in the rate of decrease of the FR+CTCs between the two groups. To achieve a statistical power of 80% and a two-sided type I error of 5%, with an assumption of an approximate 10% dropout rate, 134 patients were needed in each group. Our aim was to enrol a total of 268 patients.

Detection of CTCs

The procedure was described in our pilot study.18 All CTCs are analysed and quantified using a FR+CTCs detection kit (Geno Biotechn) approved by the China Food and Drug Administration. CTC enrichment is initially achieved by lysing erythrocytes, followed by the removal of leukocytes from whole blood by immunomagnetic methods. Then, as previously described, the FR+CTC in each sample is quantified by ligand-targeted PCR. The self-reference CTC unit (denoted FU) derived from the standard curve is used to indicate the abundance of FR+CTC in 5 mL of peripheral blood. A series of standards containing oligonucleotides (10−14−10−9 M, corresponding to 2 to 2×105 CTC units/3 mL of blood) for FR+CTC quantification. A difference in CTC levels is defined as a ≥15% change between preoperative and postoperative measurements. Changes <15% are considered non-significant. If the results between the two measurements (preoperative and postoperative) are less than 15%, it is considered that there is no difference between the two results.

Surgical technique

All patients will undergo minimally invasive surgery in accordance with the principles of TME (including anterior resection, abdomino-perineal resection or Hartmann procedure). After careful examination of the entire abdomen, a sharp pelvic dissection will be performed along the surgical plane between the mesorectum and parietal fascia, and the autonomic nerves will be preserved. The apical lymph nodes will be thoroughly dissected, irrespective of high or low inferior mesenteric artery ligation. For patients with high inferior mesenteric artery ligation, the inferior mesenteric artery will be identified and ligated at an appropriate location determined by the surgeon based on the anatomy. For patients with low inferior mesenteric artery ligation, the left colic artery will be identified and preserved, and the inferior mesenteric artery will be ligated immediately below the origin of the left colic artery. The inferior mesenteric vein will be ligated and divided at the inferior border of the pancreas or at a similar level of low inferior mesenteric artery ligation at the surgeon’s discretion. The decision to preserve or ligate the left colic artery will depend on the intraoperative findings and is made by the surgeon based on the patient’s specific anatomy. According to the ligation sequence of inferior mesenteric artery and inferior mesenteric vein, all the patients will be assigned into A-first Group and V-first Group. Whenever tension is anticipated at the anastomosis or stoma, a larger extent of colon mobilisation or splenic flexure mobilisation will be performed to obtain sufficient bowel length. A diverting ileostomy will be performed in selected patients at the discretion of the surgeon. The time interval between the two vascular ligations will be recorded for intraoperative analysis.

Follow-up

The postoperative examination will be performed every 3 months in the first 2 years and every 6 months in the following 3 years. Details of follow-up assessments, including physical examination, digital rectal examination, CT scans or MRI, abdominal ultrasound, colonoscopy and carcinoembryonic antigen measurement. Statistical analysis

Continuous data will be presented as medians with IQRs. Categorical data will be presented as numbers and percentages. The t test or Wilcoxon rank sum test will be used to compare continuous data, and chi-square analysis or Fisher’s exact test will be used to compare categorical variables. A logistic regression analysis will be used to test univariate and multivariate associations between variables to investigate risk factors for changes of CTC levels. Survival analysis will be conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared by the log-rank test. Statistical analysis will be performed using R (http://www.Rproject.org/ and SPSS (SPSS, Chicago, Illinois, USA). A two-tailed p<0.05 is considered statistically significant.

Data and safety monitoring

An independent Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) including senior colorectal surgeons, biostatisticians, an ethicist and a research contractor has been set up. DSMB members will meet before the study and every 6 months during the study. The DSMB will review elements of the study, including adverse events (AEs), drop-outs and endpoints. If the interim results show a huge difference between the two groups that is much more than anticipated, the DSMB can stop the study early for the safety and benefit of the patients.

Adverse events

Intraoperative mortality, should it occur, will be immediately reported as Grade 5 event under Clavien-Dindo classification and documented as serious AE. Given the qualifications of the centres and surgeons participating in this study, treatment-related deaths or life-threatening complications due to surgery are generally unlikely to occur. All AEs will be graded according to the Clavien-Dindo classification. Any death or unexpected Grade 4 AE will be urgently reported to the principal investigator within 24 hours. It will also be reported to the Ethics Committee within 72 hours, and a detailed medical report will be generated within 15 days. The DSMB will be responsible for assessing the quality of surgery by reviewing surgical videos and monitoring the safety of each centre. If treatment-related deaths or life-threatening complications occur in up to 3% of the preallocated proportion of patients at each centre, patient enrolment will be immediately terminated, and the DSMB will reassess the safety of the trial.

Discussion

Yamaguchi et al11 found that detection of CTCs in the bloodstream is a negative indicator for the prognosis of CRC. This study aims to investigate the impact of the ligation sequence of the inferior mesenteric artery and vein on the dissemination of CTCs and survival outcomes in minimally invasive rectal cancer surgery. In recent years, minimally invasive surgery has been increasingly performed to treat rectal cancer. Understanding the mechanisms of potential tumour cell release and dissemination during minimally invasive rectal cancer surgery has become increasingly important. This trial, a prospective, multicentre, randomised controlled study, seeks to provide an evidence base for the optimal vascular management strategy during minimally invasive rectal cancer surgery by comparing two different sequences of vascular ligation.

In cancer surgery, the potential release and dissemination of tumour cells, potentially leading to distant metastasis, is a significant issue. This issue highlights the importance of surgical techniques designed to minimise tumour dissemination. A previous study20 investigated the impact of different surgical approaches on patient survival, specifically comparing early vascular control (ligation before tumour manipulation) versus conventional dissection. This study failed to confirm the superiority of prioritising vascular control. Nonetheless, theoretically, prioritising the ligation of the inflow artery over the outflow vein could potentially reduce the entry of tumour cells into the bloodstream, thereby decreasing the chance of distant metastasis. However, there is currently a lack of empirical evidence supporting this strategy, especially in the context of rectal cancer surgery. By monitoring changes in the levels of CTCs before and after surgery, this study aims to directly assess the impact of ligation sequence on tumour cell dissemination. Furthermore, by tracking patients’ DFS, OS and patterns of recurrence, we will also evaluate the impact of these surgical strategies on long-term survival outcomes.

Although robotic-assisted minimally invasive surgery platforms are now widely adopted in contemporary practice, this trial specifically focuses on conventional minimally invasive techniques to maintain procedural standardisation and isolate the biological effect of vascular ligation sequences. Understanding tumour cell release mechanisms during standardised minimally invasive TME procedures remains clinically significant for optimising oncological outcomes.

Notably, the design of this study considers multiple important clinical endpoints, including intraoperative blood loss, operation duration, conversion rate, number of lymph nodes harvested and intraoperative and postoperative complications. These are key indicators of surgical quality and patient recovery. Through a comprehensive analysis of these data, we hope to thoroughly evaluate the impact of different ligation sequences on the safety and efficacy of surgery for patients with rectal cancer.

Moreover, the multicentre design of the study enhances the generalisability and reliability of its findings, allowing the research outcomes to better guide clinical practice. We anticipate that this study will fill the gaps in current guidelines and provide evidence-based recommendations for vascular management in rectal cancer surgery.

In conclusion, by exploring the effects of ligation sequence on CTC dissemination and patient survival, the ARVECTS study aims to offer significant clinical guidance for minimally invasive rectal cancer surgery. With the emergence of study results, we hope to optimise surgical strategies for patients with rectal cancer, thereby improving their prognosis.

Ethics and dissemination

The study protocol (V1.0, June 10 2023) has been approved by the lead ethics committee at Sichuan Cancer Hospital (approval number: SCCHEC-02-2024-102). Participating centres include Sichuan Cancer Hospital and Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital (approval number: SCPPH-03-2025-299). No patient recruitment will occur at any centre until site-specific approval is obtained. The study protocol strictly adheres to the principles of the latest Declaration of Helsinki. The results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications.

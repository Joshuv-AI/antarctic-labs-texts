Investigating the attitudinal and behavioral consequences of work-life balance considering the mediating role of psychological contract violation among english language teachers
Authors: Eesa Niazi, Ali Arabmofrad, Ramin Bagheri, Amir Hassan Shamsabadi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study investigates the attitudinal and behavioral consequences of Work-Life Balance (WLB) among teachers, focusing on the mediating role of Psychological Contract Violation (PCV).MethodsAn applied, descriptive-correlational survey design was employed. The target population included all English language teachers in Golestan Province (N = 598), from which a simple random sample of 234 was selected. A total of 152 usable responses were obtained. The measurement model was assessed for internal consistency using composite reliability, and for convergent validity using Average Variance Extracted (AVE). Discriminant validity was evaluated through the Fornell–Larcker criterion. Descriptive statistics were analyzed using SPSS, and Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was performed using SmartPLS.ResultsWLB was negatively associated with PCV (β = − 0.209) and turnover intention (β = − 0.386), and positively associated with job performance (β = 0.252) and tardiness (β = 0.322). PCV significantly mediated the relationships between WLB and turnover intention (β = 0.366), tardiness (β = 0.325), and job performance (β = − 0.339).ConclusionImproving WLB and strengthening the psychological contract can reduce negative outcomes such as turnover intention and performance decline. However, the positive link between WLB and tardiness highlights the need for culturally responsive policy design.Keywords: Work-Life balance, Turnover intention, Psychological contract, Voice behaviors, Tardiness, Job performance

Background

In today’s world, where the complexities of professional and personal life are becoming increasingly intertwined, the balance between work and life has become a fundamental challenge for employees, particularly teachers. As the main pillars of the education system and the nurturers of the next generation, teachers play a vital role in the development of society. However, pressures stemming from heavy workloads, increasing expectations from society and the education system, and numerous family responsibilities have severely impacted their work-life balance [1]. Studies indicate that over 60% of teachers in developing countries face significant challenges in maintaining this balance [2]. Furthermore, in many EFL contexts, recurrent curriculum reforms, designed to align national programs with global English-proficiency benchmarks, have markedly increased teachers’ preparatory workloads, with one study of Chinese EFL instructors reporting notable declines in well-being amid frequent content and syllabus overhauls [3]. Likewise, large and heterogeneous English classes (often exceeding 35–40 students) demand extensive differentiated instruction and classroom management, exacerbating time pressures and emotional strain on language teachers [4].

Recent research further indicates that these challenges were severely exacerbated during the COVID-19 pandemic, finding that 52% of teachers worldwide experienced burnout during this period, a rate higher than that reported for healthcare professionals [5]. Emergency remote teaching introduced additional digital workloads: EFL teachers reported having to recreate communicative, interaction-based lessons for online platforms, which semi-structured interviews linked to elevated cognitive load and affective trauma, key predictors of increased exhaustion and depersonalization in remote settings [6, 7]. This imbalance not only negatively affects teachers’ job satisfaction and productivity but can also lead to undesirable attitudinal and behavioral consequences such as burnout, decreased teaching quality, absenteeism, and even job turnover [8]. Moreover, EFL-specific burnout has been shown to directly reduce classroom interaction quality, leading to lower student engagement in communicative tasks, underscoring how teacher well-being critically shapes language learning outcomes [7, 9]. In Iran, statistics show that the teacher attrition rate has been on an upward trend in the last five years, a significant portion of which is attributed to a lack of work-life balance [10].

Another crucial issue is the violation of the psychological contract, which is defined as teachers’ perception of the organization’s failure to fulfill implicit obligations. This phenomenon can act as a powerful mediating factor, exacerbating the relationship between work-life balance and attitudinal and behavioral consequences [11]. Recent studies have demonstrated that abrupt workplace disruptions can trigger employees’ perceptions of felt mistrust, intensify work-to-life conflict, and ultimately cause psychological contract violation [12]. Similarly, research on boundary management strategies in high-stress service industries has demonstrated that employees with high goal-self concordance, alignment between personal and organizational objectives, exhibit more effective work-life partitioning, suggesting that institutional support for teachers’ professional and personal goal alignment could mitigate boundary-blurring effects [13]. A systematic review of work-life balance during the pandemic further revealed that remote workers’ ability to maintain boundaries depended heavily on organizational policies, supervisory support, and access to dedicated workspaces, factors often lacking for teachers in resource-constrained contexts [14]. In educational settings, organizational turbulence such as rapid transitions to online teaching or implementation of new educational policies, significantly increases the likelihood of psychological contract reneging [15]. In the Iranian educational context, psychological contracts typically include teachers’ expectations of financial support (such as adequate salaries and benefits), career development opportunities (such as training courses and promotions), and a fair work environment (such as equitable evaluations). When the educational organization fails to meet these obligations, it can lead to a sharp decline in organizational commitment, increased job dissatisfaction, and the emergence of counterproductive work behaviors [16]. Research in Iran has shown that 45% of teachers feel that the educational organization has violated its psychological contract with them [17].

The attitudinal consequences of these phenomena are far-reaching. Decreased organizational commitment among teachers can lead to a decline in the quality of education, reduced participation in extracurricular activities, and reluctance towards educational innovations. On the other hand, behavioral consequences such as turnover intention can leave the education system facing a shortage of specialized and experienced personnel. In Iran, this issue is particularly acute in deprived areas that struggle with teacher recruitment and retention [17]. Furthermore, counterproductive behaviors such as reduced work effort or lack of cooperation with colleagues can severely impact the educational atmosphere. Although numerous studies have examined work-life balance and its impact on job satisfaction and performance [18], limited research, especially in the field of education, has investigated this issue considering the mediating role of psychological contract violation. Most previous research has either focused on other industries [19] or has solely addressed attitudinal consequences, neglecting the examination of behavioral outcomes [20]. This research gap is particularly evident in the context of developing countries like Iran, where the education system faces unique challenges.

The present research introduces several theoretical and methodological innovations. From a theoretical standpoint, this study, by integrating work-life balance theory and psychological contract theory, offers a comprehensive conceptual framework that can explain the complex relationships between these variables. Methodologically, this research employs a cross-sectional design, which allows for the examination of causal relationships between the variables. Furthermore, the simultaneous focus on attitudinal and behavioral consequences provides a more complete picture of the impacts of work-life balance. At the practical level, the findings of this research can serve as a basis for designing organizational interventions such as work flexibility programs, counseling services, and the revision of human resource management policies within the education system. In the Iranian cultural context, this study holds particular significance. Given the strong family values in Iranian culture and societal expectations of teachers, the pressures of multiple roles can be intensified.

Particularly for language teachers in Iran, research has shown significant positive correlations between subjective well-being and occupational self-efficacy, highlighting the importance of psychological factors in teacher retention and effectiveness [21]. This connection between self-efficacy and well-being becomes especially relevant when considering the mediating role of psychological contract violation in the relationship between work-life balance and teacher outcomes. On the other hand, recent developments in the Iranian education system, such as changes in educational content or curricula, increased expectations of teachers, and resource constraints, have affected psychological contracts. By considering these unique characteristics, this study can contribute to the enrichment of the international literature in this field. Given these explanations, the present research seeks to answer the fundamental question: “How does work-life balance influence teachers’ attitudinal outcomes (such as job satisfaction and organizational commitment) and behavioral outcomes (such as job performance and turnover intention) through the mediating role of psychological contract violation?” Answering this question can significantly contribute to a better understanding of the challenges faced by teachers and the design of effective strategies to improve their professional and personal quality of life.

Theoretical framework

Work-Life balance theory

Work-life balance theory, first introduced by Greenhaus and Allen, examines the interaction between individuals’ work and personal life domains [22]. This theory is based on the principle that individuals seek to establish an optimal balance between the time, energy, and attention they dedicate to their work and personal lives [22]. Within this theoretical framework, work-life balance is defined as a state in which an individual experiences satisfaction and good performance in both their work and personal life domains, without these two domains significantly interfering with each other.

Theorists in this area have identified three main dimensions for work-life balance: time balance (time allocated to each domain), psychological balance (psychological pressures arising from each domain), and behavioral balance (the ability to shift behavior between different domains). Research has shown that imbalance in any of these dimensions can lead to negative consequences such as stress, burnout, and decreased life satisfaction [23]. In educational settings, this theory is particularly important because teachers, due to the nature of their profession which often requires spending time and energy beyond official working hours, are particularly susceptible to work-life imbalance [24]. Recent studies have highlighted how the boundaries between work and home domains have become increasingly blurred, especially during global disruptions such as the COVID-19 pandemic. It was found that abrupt shifts to remote work arrangements significantly intensified work-to-life conflict for many professionals, including educators [12]. This boundary blurring has particular relevance for language teachers, who often maintain flexible teaching schedules and may work across multiple educational institutions simultaneously.

Recent studies have indicated that organizational support and flexibility in the workplace can help improve teachers’ work-life balance [25]. In language-teaching contexts, the behavioral-balance dimension encompasses rapid shifts between communicative-focused and form-focused lesson modes, each requiring distinct pedagogical planning and cognitive effort. Structural equation modeling of EFL cohorts demonstrated that frequent mode-switching elevates mental workload and stress when recovery intervals are insufficient [9]. However, the effectiveness of such interventions depends significantly on contextual factors, with a recent research revealed that female teachers and school teachers (compared to university educators) experienced higher rates of burnout during periods of organizational change [5]. This suggests that work-life balance theories must account for both individual differences and contextual factors when applied to educational settings.

Psychological contract theory

Psychological contract theory, developed by Rousseau, examines the implicit mutual expectations between employees and the organization [26]. According to this theory, in addition to formal employment contracts, a set of informal obligations and expectations exists between employees and the organization that shapes the employment relationship. These psychological contracts can be divided into two main types: transactional contracts, which are short-term and based on tangible rewards, and relational contracts, which are long-term and based on trust and mutual commitment [26]. When the organization fails to meet these implicit obligations, employees experience psychological contract violation, which can lead to decreased organizational commitment, job satisfaction, and increased turnover intention [27].

In TEFL settings, psychological contracts often include explicit or implicit promises of accreditation support (e.g., funding for CELTA/DELTA certificates) and opportunities for applied-linguistics research or conference participation. When institutions cannot fulfill these expectations, through either reneging or perceptual incongruence, teachers report heightened breach perceptions and mistrust, which in turn aggravate work-to-life conflict and conflict spillover into personal domains [28, 29].

Contemporary research has expanded our understanding of how psychological contracts are formed and violated. It was identified two primary circumstances that initiate psychological contract violation: reneging (when organizations are unable or unwilling to fulfill promises) and incongruence (when employers believe promises have been fulfilled but employees disagree) [12]. Their research demonstrated that organizational turbulence, defined as “changes experienced by the organization that are nontrivial, rapid, and discontinuous”, significantly increases the likelihood of reneging on psychological contracts. This finding has particular relevance for educational institutions undergoing rapid transformations, such as curriculum reforms, technological integration, or administrative restructuring.

In the educational environment, teachers’ psychological contracts typically include expectations such as organizational support, professional growth opportunities, a fair work environment, and mutual respect [30]. Research has shown that the violation of these contracts among teachers can have detrimental effects on their motivation and job performance [31]. Recent studies have further established that psychological contract violation leads to a cascade of negative outcomes, including “reduced job satisfaction, reduced organizational commitment, increased turnover intention, jeopardized trust in supervisors and organizations, increased stress and emotional exhaustion, and counterproductive workplace behaviors” [32]. For language teachers specifically, these effects may be exacerbated by the often precarious nature of their employment contracts and the additional pressures of teaching in multilingual and multicultural contexts.

Social exchange theory

Social exchange theory, introduced by Blau, analyzes human relationships based on the exchange of tangible and intangible resources [33]. This theory posits that individuals tend to maintain and strengthen relationships when they perceive a fair and reciprocal exchange in their interactions. In the workplace, this theory predicts that employees will demonstrate high levels of commitment and performance in return for receiving adequate rewards (such as salary, respect, support) from the organization [34]. When employees feel this exchange is unfair (for example, when work-life balance is disrupted or psychological contracts are violated), they may reduce their level of engagement and commitment or even exhibit counterproductive work behaviors [35].

In the educational context, this theory can effectively explain why teachers, when they feel the organization does not provide sufficient support, may reduce the quality of their teaching or consider leaving their jobs. For language teachers in particular, it was found that occupational self-efficacy and meaning at work were significantly correlated with subjective well-being, suggesting that teachers’ perceptions of their ability to successfully manage classroom challenges and find meaning in their work are crucial elements in the social exchange relationship with their institutions [21].

Conservation of resources theory

Conservation of Resources (COR) theory, proposed by Hobfoll, is based on the concept that individuals strive to obtain, retain, and protect their valued resources. In this theory, resources can include material resources (such as income), conditions (such as good relationships), energy resources (such as time and attention), and psychological resources (such as self-esteem) [36]. Individuals experience stress when they feel these resources are threatened or lost [37]. In the workplace, work-life imbalance can be considered a threat to an individual’s resources (especially time and energy). Similarly, psychological contract violation can threaten an individual’s psychological resources such as feelings of job security and worth [38]. For EFL instructors, creating authentic, multilingual materials and integrating cultural content represents a substantial investment of both cognitive and creative resources. During the pandemic, educators reported that rapidly toggling among digital platforms to deliver rich, interactive language tasks depleted technical and psychological reserves, heightening emotional exhaustion [29]. Moreover, the adoption of dynamic versus diagnostic assessment strategies in large, diverse EFL classes was found to increase learner cognitive load—and by extension, teachers’ planning demands, suggesting that resource-conservation interventions must address both pedagogical and technical resource drains [39].

Recent applications of COR theory to educational contexts have highlighted the resource-intensive nature of teaching during periods of rapid change. It was noted that teachers worldwide faced unprecedented resource demands during the COVID-19 pandemic, having to rapidly adapt to online teaching, follow strict hygiene protocols upon returning to face-to-face instruction, and manage increased psychological symptoms among students [5]. Their research revealed that these resource demands resulted in a 52% prevalence of burnout among teachers globally, significantly higher than pre-pandemic levels and exceeding rates reported for healthcare professionals during the same period.

Research has shown that teachers, due to the stressful nature of their profession, are particularly vulnerable to resource loss, and this can lead to burnout and decreased teaching quality [40]. This theory effectively explains why organizational support and work-life balance improvement programs can help teachers maintain and replenish their resources. For language teachers specifically, who often need to manage both linguistic and cultural resources in their teaching practice, resource conservation becomes even more critical to maintaining effective instruction and personal well-being.

Conceptual model of the research

The conceptual model of this research is designed based on the theoretical framework presented by Kaya and Karatape, which employs an integration of role balance theory, social justice theory, and conservation of resources theory [41]. This conceptual model examines the relationships between work-life balance as the independent variable, psychological contract violation as the mediating variable, and four attitudinal and behavioral outcomes: early turnover intention, intention to be late for work, job performance, and voice behavior, as the dependent variables. The aforementioned study, utilizing a rigorous methodology including longitudinal data collection across three time waves with a one-month interval from a sample of hospitality sector employees and their supervisors in Turkey, provides a suitable framework for analyzing these relationships.

It was proposed that workplace disruptions trigger a sequential process whereby employees first experience felt mistrust, which then intensifies work-to-life conflict, ultimately leading to psychological contract violation [12]. Their research demonstrated that effective coping strategies could mitigate this cascade effect, suggesting a potential moderating variable that could be integrated into the current conceptual model.

The theoretical foundation of this model rests on three main theories. First, role balance theory, which emphasizes the importance of compatibility between the expectations of different life roles. Second, social justice theory, which focuses on fairness and equality in work relationships. Third, conservation of resources theory, which considers maintaining balance between different life domains essential to prevent the depletion of individual resources. Within this framework, work-life balance is considered a factor that can influence employees’ perceptions of psychological contract violation. On the other hand, psychological contract violation acts as a key mechanism through which work-life balance may affect attitudinal and behavioral outcomes.

In designing the present research, this conceptual model has been adapted to align with the Iranian educational context and the target population of teachers. The dependent variables in this study include job satisfaction, organizational commitment, job performance, and turnover intention, which are more consistent with the cultural and organizational conditions of Iranian teachers. The model allows for the systematic examination of the relationships between work-life balance, psychological contract violation, and performance-related outcomes of teachers within a coherent framework. The application of this model in the Iranian educational context can contribute to the enrichment of the existing literature in the field of organizational studies and industrial-organizational psychology. Methods

Aim, design, and setting

This study adopted an applied research framework within a quantitative paradigm. It utilized a descriptive-survey methodology, specifically in the correlational branch, to investigate the relationships between variables. The research was conducted in Golestan Province, Iran, targeting English language teachers as the population of interest. This setting was selected due to its diverse educational institutions and accessibility for data collection purposes.

Participants

The statistical population comprised all English language teachers across Golestan Province, totaling 598 individuals. From this population, a sample of 234 participants was selected through simple random sampling to ensure fairness and minimize bias. After distributing the questionnaires, 152 completed and usable responses were obtained, which formed the final dataset for analysis. The remaining 82 questionnaires were excluded due to issues such as incomplete responses, missing demographic information, or inconsistent answering patterns that could compromise the integrity of the data. These exclusions followed standard data cleaning procedures to maintain the reliability and validity of the findings. Participation was voluntary, and ethical considerations, including informed consent and confidentiality, were strictly observed throughout the data collection process.

Instrument

The data were collected using a questionnaire adapted and revised from the instrument developed by Kaya and Karatepe [41]. The adaptation process involved contextual modifications to align the questionnaire with the specific conditions and characteristics of the Iranian educational context and statistical population. To ensure the reliability and validity of the revised instrument, a comprehensive psychometric evaluation was conducted. Reliability was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha, Composite Reliability (CR), and Average Variance Extracted (AVE), all of which showed acceptable values. Validity was confirmed through both convergent and divergent validity analyses, supporting the appropriateness of the instrument for measuring the intended constructs in this study.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was carried out in two stages using both descriptive and inferential statistics. At the descriptive level, measures of central tendency and dispersion (means, standard deviations, variances) were calculated using SPSS software version 26 to provide a summary of participant responses. At the inferential level, Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was conducted via SmartPLS version 3. This advanced technique enabled simultaneous analysis of both observed and latent variables and facilitated the testing of multiple relationships among constructs. Evaluation of the measurement and structural models, including model fit indices and path coefficients, was also performed to rigorously examine the research hypotheses.

Assessing model adequacy

R-squared (Coefficient of Determination - R2)

R2 is a criterion related to the endogenous (dependent) latent variables in the model. It is a measure that indicates the effect of an exogenous variable on an endogenous variable, and the values 0.19, 0.33, and 0.67 are considered as benchmark values for weak, moderate, and strong R2, respectively [42]. The R2 values for the endogenous constructs (dependent variables) were calculated as follows: Psychological Contract Violation = 0.144, Turnover Intention = 0.342, Tardiness at Work = 0.253, Job Performance = 0.143, and Voice Behavior = 0.193. According to the benchmark values, these results confirm a moderate fit of the structural model.

Q-squared (Predictive Relevance - Q2)

Q2 criterion specifies the predictive power of the model and ranges between 0 and 1, with values closer to 1 indicating better predictive power. If the Q2 value for an endogenous construct achieves the values of 0.02, 0.15, and 0.35, it indicates weak, moderate, and strong predictive relevance of the exogenous construct(s) related to it, respectively. In essence, this index shows the extent to which the independent variables of a given dependent variable have been able to predict it well. The Q2 values for the constructs were calculated as follows: Psychological Contract Violation = 0.032, Turnover Intention = 0.263, Tardiness at Work = 0.189, Job Performance = 0.094, and Voice Behavior = 0.092. These results indicate a moderate predictive power of the model regarding the endogenous constructs of the research and confirm the adequate fit of the structural model.

Discussion

The findings of this research, by presenting a complex network of relationships between work-life balance, psychological contract violation, and attitudinal-behavioral outcomes, represent a significant step in the development of human resource management literature within the educational context. The results indicated that work-life balance has a significant impact on reducing psychological contract violation (β=−0.209, t = 4.359), a finding that can be explained by the Conservation of Resources (COR) theory [36], suggesting that the optimal allocation of psychological resources such as time and energy reduces employees’ perception of contract violation. Although this result aligns with the findings of Kaya and Karatape [41], the lower intensity of the coefficient in the present study is likely due to the specific conditions of teachers in the Iranian education system, who, despite experiencing a relative work-life balance, may still feel a sense of contract violation due to structural reasons.

The inverse relationship between work-life balance and turnover intention (β=−0.386, t = 6.234), which is consistent with Social Exchange Theory [33], suggests that improving balance can act as a deterrent against teachers’ intention to leave their service. However, this effect was observed with less intensity compared to similar studies [18], likely reflecting the influence of economic and livelihood factors in teachers’ decisions to stay or leave their jobs.

On the other hand, the unexpected positive relationship between work-life balance and tardiness at work (β = 0.322, t = 5.601), which can be interpreted through Boundary Role Theory [15], indicates that in the Iranian cultural context, where family values are highly important, teachers may prioritize family responsibilities in conflict situations. This finding is consistent with the results of Shirmohammadi et al. [16] but contradicts the findings of Kaya and Karatape [41], highlighting cultural differences in the pattern of relationships between variables.

Interestingly, the positive association between WLB and tardiness deviates from conventional theoretical expectations. This may be explained by cultural dynamics in the Iranian context, where family obligations are strongly prioritized. Teachers with better WLB may feel more empowered to occasionally arrive late in order to attend to family responsibilities, consistent with Boundary Role Theory and similar findings in culturally comparable settings.

The mediating role of psychological contract violation was identified as a key mechanism in this research, as this variable showed a significant positive relationship with both turnover intention (β = 0.366, t = 7.547) and tardiness at work (β = 0.325, t = 5.765), findings that were observed with greater intensity compared to the study by Kaya and Karatape [41] and likely reflect teachers’ particular sensitivity to violations of psychological contracts. These results are well aligned with Psychological Contract Theory [26] and indicate that teachers’ perception of the organization’s failure to fulfill implicit obligations can lead to decreased organizational commitment and increased counterproductive work behaviors.

From a theoretical perspective, this research has made a significant contribution to the literature by developing the work-life balance model through the addition of psychological contract violation as a mediating mechanism, simultaneously examining attitudinal and behavioral consequences, and testing the model in the Iranian cultural context. From a practical standpoint, the findings emphasize the need to design flexible work programs, establish support systems, clarify mutual expectations, and improve organizational communication for teachers. Although limitations such as the use of a cross-sectional method and the sole focus on teachers restrict the generalizability of the results, they pave the way for future research in the form of longitudinal studies, the examination of other service professions, and attention to the moderating role of variables such as social support and resilience.

Overall, this research demonstrates that improving work-life balance can lead to the improvement of organizational outcomes in the education system, both directly and indirectly through reducing the perception of psychological contract violation. This finding is of high practical importance, especially in the current situation of the Iranian education system with challenges such as teacher burnout and turnover intention, and can serve as a basis for designing more effective educational policies.

Despite the methodological rigor of the study, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, its cross-sectional design prevents establishing definitive causal relationships between the constructs (e.g., WLB → PCV). Future research should adopt longitudinal or experimental designs to better assess temporal dynamics and causality. Second, although 234 questionnaires were distributed, only 152 were completed and usable, resulting in a 65% response rate. While this rate is generally acceptable in social science research, it raises the possibility of non-response bias, as individuals who did not participate or complete the survey may systematically differ from respondents.

Additionally, the final sample size, although sufficient for exploratory analysis, may be considered limited for complex Structural Equation Modeling (SEM), potentially affecting the stability and generalizability of the estimates. To strengthen future research, studies with larger, more diverse, and representative samples are recommended to validate and extend the current findings.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that better work-life balance (WLB) among Iranian EFL teachers directly reduces psychological contract violation (PCV) and turnover intentions, while simultaneously enhancing job performance (β = 0.252). However, it may paradoxically increase tardiness (β = 0.322), likely due to cultural norms that prioritize family obligations over strict workplace punctuality. These findings suggest that while WLB brings notable organizational benefits, its implementation should be culturally sensitive to avoid unintended outcomes such as increased tardiness. Moreover, PCV emerges as a pivotal mediator, amplifying negative behavioral outcomes when violated. These insights underscore the importance for educational policymakers to promote flexible scheduling, establish robust support systems, and ensure transparent expectation management to sustain teacher well-being, commitment, and performance.

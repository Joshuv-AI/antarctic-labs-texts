Efficacy of Different Pediatric Local Anesthesia Delivery Techniques in Reducing Pain and Anxiety: A Randomized Clinical Trial
Authors: M Lavanya Mangala Valli, AR Senthil Eagappan, B Asvitha, S Nandini, T Subakeerthi, Priya Kannan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPain and anxiety during local anesthesia (LA) administration are major concerns in pediatric dentistry, as they greatly influence a child's participation during dental treatment. The purpose of this research was aimed at assessing and comparing the effectiveness of three different techniques for delivering LA—namely the conventional syringe, Woodpecker StarPen, and Buzzy® system—in alleviating pain and anxiety associated with inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB) administration in children aged 6–10 years.MethodsA randomized clinical trial was carried out on 120 healthy children aged 6–10 years who needed an IANB. Participants were randomly allotted into three groups (n = 40 each): group I—conventional syringe, group II—Woodpecker StarPen, and group III—Buzzy® system. Pain and anxiety were assessed using physiological [heart rate (HR)], subjective (Wong–Baker FACES Pain Rating Scale), and behavioral/objective [Face, Leg, Activity, Cry, Consolability (FLACC) scale] measures. Obtained data were statistically analyzed.ResultsIn all three groups, HR, subjective pain perception [Wong–Baker FACES Pain Scale (WBFPS)], and behavioral responses (FLACC) scores increased during LA administration and declined postadministration. The Buzzy® group demonstrated significantly lower HR, WBFPS, and FLACC scores compared with the conventional syringe and StarPen groups, indicating superior control of pain and anxiety. The StarPen group showed moderate improvement over the conventional technique but was less effective than the Buzzy® system.ConclusionThe Buzzy® system proved to be the most efficient in reducing pain and anxiety during administration of LA in children, followed by the StarPen and conventional syringe. It may be considered a valuable tool for improving patient comfort in pediatric dental settings.

Introduction

Effective management of pain and anxiety during dental procedures is a significant concern in pediatric practice, as it shapes a child's perception of dental care.1 Distress during treatment can make young children fearful and uncooperative, leading to avoidance of future care and negatively impacting long-term oral health.2 Parents of anxious children often delay dental visits, leading to poorer oral health and increasing the need for invasive procedures requiring local anesthesia (LA), thereby making it an essential part of pain management in pediatric care.3 Although LA effectively controls pain, its administration can significantly influence the child's perception of pain and anxiety.4 Conventional syringe-based injections, though pharmacologically effective, can cause discomfort due to factors such as needle insertion trauma, rapid solution deposition, and poor needle control.5,6

To enhance comfort and compliance during LA administration, several alternative administration methods have been explored, including topical anesthetic gels, precooling the injection site, transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS), vibrotactile devices, and computer-controlled local anesthesia delivery (CCLAD) systems.7,11 A vibrotactile innovation is the Buzzy® device, developed by Atlanta-based pediatrician Dr Amy Baxter, which combines cold and vibration to block pain signals by stimulating sensory nerve fibers.12 Similarly, CCLAD systems such as the Woodpecker StarPen use algorithm-driven technology to deliver anesthetic at a slow, consistent rate, reducing injection-related discomfort.1 This study evaluated the effectiveness of the Buzzy® device and the Woodpecker StarPen in comparison with conventional syringes, which serve as the standard method for managing pain and anxiety in children between ages of 6 and 10 years.

Methods

The Institutional Human Ethics Committee (IHEC) granted ethical clearance for the study after it was authorized by the Institutional Review Board (304/IHEC-1/2022). Every procedure was carried out in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (2013) ethical guidelines. The study is included in the Clinical Trials Registry (CTRI/2024/06/068658).

This randomized clinical trial aimed to compare the efficacy of the Woodpecker StarPen, Buzzy system, and conventional syringes in alleviating pain and anxiety during inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB) in children aged 6–10 years, conducted at the Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry.

A sample size was determined using G*Power statistical software (version 3.1.9.2), which yielded a total sample size of 120 (40 participants per group).

The study included healthy children aged 6–10 years who were Frankl positive and required an IANB. Children with systemic diseases or on medication, exhibiting uncooperative behavior, with prior exposure to LA, or lacking parental consent were excluded. A consent form was obtained from parents or legal guardians after providing detailed information about the study. The study was conducted over a period of 5 months from June 2024 to October 2024.

Clinical Procedure

Group I: Conventional Syringe

The IANB was achieved by administering LA (2% lidocaine with 1:2,00,000 adrenaline injection solution) using a conventional 2 mL Dispovan syringe with a 27-gauge needle.

Group II: Buzzy System

The child was familiarized with the Buzzy device before the procedure. A precooled wing was attached to the vibrating unit and placed over the mandibular ramus above the injection site. IANB was then administered (Fig. 1).

Administration of LA using Buzzy system

Group III: Woodpecker StarPen

The Lignospan cartridge containing 2% lidocaine with 1:80,000 epinephrine injection solution and Septodent Septoject needles–gauge was inserted into the Woodpecker StarPen device. Low-speed delivery mode (170–310 s) was selected on the light-emitting diode (LED) screen display, and LA was administered.

The pain and anxiety in children was assessed using three parameters—heart rate (HR), Wong–Baker FACES Pain Scale (WBFPS), and Face, Leg, Activity, Cry, Consolability (FLACC) scale, representing physiological, subjective, and behavioral responses, respectively. After a 10-minute rest, baseline HR (via pulse oximeter), WBFPS, and FLACC scores were recorded. HR and FLACC were measured during LA administration, while WBFPS was recorded immediately after. Following LA, children rested for 3–5 minutes, after which all parameters were reassessed. Data were categorized into three phases—before, during, and after LA administration.

Mean HR was analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA); WBFPS and FLACC scores were evaluated using Kruskal–Wallis and Mann–Whitney tests. Post hoc Bonferroni analysis was done for significant ANOVA results.

Discussion

Managing pain and anxiety during LA is a key challenge in pediatric dentistry, as these factors can significantly impact the child's cooperation and shape their long-term perception toward dental care.13 Managing these responses effectively is essential for creating a positive and lasting dental experience. To address the discomfort associated with LA administration, several advanced delivery techniques have been introduced.11 This randomized clinical trial evaluated the effectiveness of two such advanced LA delivery systems—Woodpecker StarPen and the Buzzy® system—and compared them with the conventional syringe, a standard technique, based on physiological, subjective, and behavioral responses during IANB in children aged 6–10 years.

The results clearly demonstrate that the Buzzy® system provided superior outcomes across all assessment parameters—HR, WBFPS, and FLACC scale—during and after LA administration. While all three groups showed elevated HR and pain scores during injection, these were significantly lower in the Buzzy group, indicating reduced physiological stress and perceived discomfort. This supports the hypothesis that distraction-based techniques, particularly those combining vibration and cold therapy, can modulate pain perception in children.

The observed increase in HR during LA administration across all groups is consistent with the expected sympathetic response to pain and anxiety in children. However, the significantly attenuated HR rise in the Buzzy group compared to both the conventional syringe and StarPen suggests an effective dampening of stress-related sympathetic activation. This physiological finding was paralleled by lower WBFPS scores in the Buzzy group, indicating reduced subjective pain perception. These outcomes affirm previous research advocating for the gate control theory, where nonpainful stimuli applied simultaneously (like cold and vibration) block the transmission of pain signals.14,15 The FLACC behavioral scale offered objective insight into children's pain-related behaviors. During LA administration, children in the Buzzy group exhibited significantly calmer behavior, with reduced facial grimacing, limb movement, and crying. The significantly lower FLACC scores in the Buzzy group during and after injection reflect better overall tolerance and reduced anxiety. These findings are consistent with previous studies reporting the superior efficacy of the Buzzy system compared to conventional syringes.16,17

The Woodpecker StarPen, a computer-controlled local anesthetic delivery device, outperformed the conventional syringe technique in both subjective and objective measures. In our study, it did not differ significantly from the Buzzy group after anesthesia administration, suggesting its potential to reduce residual anxiety or discomfort. These findings support the premise that computer-controlled anesthesia systems, by delivering local anesthetic at a constant and slower rate, may cause less discomfort than conventional syringes, which are more operator-dependent and prone to sudden pressure changes that can trigger pain. The lower pain perception observed in the StarPen group compared to the conventional syringe group supports the findings of Chengappa et al.,11 and Shetty et al.,18 suggesting that controlled anesthetic delivery and added features like built-in music may help reduce pain and anxiety in pediatric patients.

While the Woodpecker StarPen showed a moderate improvement over the conventional syringe in reducing pain and anxiety, the Buzzy® system demonstrated significantly greater effectiveness. Statistically significant differences between the StarPen and Buzzy groups were observed across several parameters, highlighting the superior efficacy of the Buzzy device. This enhanced performance may be attributed to its combined use of cold and vibration stimuli, which modulate pain perception through the gate control theory. In contrast, although the StarPen offers a computer-controlled and less painful delivery of LA, it lacks the multisensory distraction component that appears especially effective in pediatric patients. No previous studies in the available literature have directly compared these two devices, making the present findings a novel and valuable contribution.

Our study results have notable clinical implications, highlighting that the use of simple, cost-effective, and child-friendly devices such as the Buzzy® system can significantly enhance the child's experience during dental procedures. These devices do not require extensive training and can be easily implemented in daily practice, making them suitable for both general dentists and pediatric specialists. The StarPen, while technologically advanced, may require more operator familiarity but still presents a viable alternative to traditional syringes.

One limitation of this study is the narrow age range (6–10 years), which may not represent responses across all pediatric age-groups. Developmental differences in behavior and pain perception could influence outcomes. The absence of long-term follow-up also limits understanding of whether these devices impact the development of dental anxiety over time. Future studies should include broader age-groups, assess long-term effects, and evaluate factors like cost-effectiveness, operator learning curves, and parental satisfaction to support wider clinical adoption.

Conclusion

To conclude, the Buzzy® system was the most effective among the three techniques evaluated in reducing both pain and anxiety during LA administration in children. The Woodpecker StarPen also showed clinical advantages over the conventional syringe. The study findings highlighted the importance of adopting child-centric innovations to create a more comfortable and less traumatic dental experience for young patients.

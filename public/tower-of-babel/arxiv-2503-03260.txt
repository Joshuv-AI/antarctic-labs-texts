Quasinormal Modes and Topological Characteristics of a Schwarzschild Black Hole Surrounded by the Dehnen Type Dark Matter Halo
Farokhnaz Hosseinifar, Shahin Mamedov, Filip Studnička, Hassan Hassanabadi

Abstract
This study explores some properties of a black hole surrounded by a dark matter halo with a Dehnen-type density distribution. Specifically, we focus on the thermodynamic characteristics, shadow radius, quasinormal modes, and topological charge of thermodynamic potentials associated with this black hole. Notably, this black hole features an unstable photon sphere, which contributes to the formation of the black hole shadow.

Quasi-Normal Modes by WKB method

Quasinormal modes represent the natural frequencies of oscillation of black holes and compact objects in general relativity when they are subjected to perturbations, such as gravitational waves

;

The significance of the quasinormal modes lies in their unique properties; they are characterized by complex frequencies, where the real part indicates the oscillation frequency and the imaginary part represents the damping of the oscillation due to energy dissipation

;
lalanne2019quasinormal
;
sauvan2022normalization
. The concept of quasinormal modes was first introduced in the context of black holes by Vishveshwara

kokkotas1999quasi
;
hegde2019quasinormal
. The study of quasinormal modes encompasses perturbations of various types of black holes, including Schwarzschild, Reissner-Nordström, Kerr, and Kerr-Newman black holes, as well as neutron stars
blazquez2019quasinormal
;
cai2021quasinormal
;
franchini2024testing
.

To analyze quasinormal modes Klein-Gordon equation is employed and for a massless scalar field in curved space-time reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\Psi)=0.

\displaystyle\Psi_{\omega lm}(\textbf{r},t)=\frac{R_{\omega l(r)}}{r}Y_{lm}(\theta,\,\phi)e^{i\omega t},

VI
Conclusion

In this study, we investigated a black hole surrounded by the Dehnen-type dark matter halo. We began by analyzing key thermodynamic properties, including Hawking temperature, remnant radius, and entropy. Our examination of light trajectory allowed us to determine the black hole’s shadow, leading to constraints on parameters based on observational data from Sgr A*. We observed that for this black hole, and based on our calculations for the shadow, a lower bound for the parameters cannot be found, and only an upper bound can be established. Subsequently, we calculated the quasinormal modes using the WKB approximation, revealing their dependence on the dark matter halo. Furthermore, we explored the photon sphere and thermodynamic potentials from a topological perspective, focusing on the stability and instability of their zero points in the vector space of their relevant potentials. These findings contribute significantly to our understanding of black hole physics and the interplay between dark matter and gravitational physics.

Hypergeometric Moments and Hecke Trace Formulas
Brian Grove

Abstract
. Moments for hypergeometric functions over finite fields were studied in the work of Ono, Pujahari, Saad, and Saikia for several F 1 2 {}_{2}F_{1} and F 2 3 {}_{3}F_{2} cases. We generalize their work to prove results for new cases where the hypergeometric data is defined over ℚ \mathbb{Q} and primitive. These new moments are established using Hecke trace formulas of hypergeometric origin recently established by Hoffman, Li, Long, and Tu. We also obtain several algebraic formulas in the finite field setting and present conjectures for additional F 1 2 {}_{2}F_{1} and F 2 3 {}_{3}F_{2} moments.

1.2.
Hypergeometric Moments

The key connection of Koike allows us to consider the Sato-Tate distribution of the normalized values
H_{q}(\lambda)/\sqrt{q}\in[-2,2]
for a fixed
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\}
. In
[
41
]
, Ono, Saad, and Saikia consider all elliptic curves in the Legendre family and determine the distribution of normalized values
H_{q}(\lambda)/\sqrt{q}\in[-2,2]
, where now both
q
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\}
vary. They find the distribution is semicircular, explicitly determined in Corollary 1.2 of
[
41
]
, analogous to the classical Sato-Tate conjecture.

Let
m
be a fixed positive integer. Computing the mean of the
(H_{q}(\lambda)/\sqrt{q})^{m}
values over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and taking the limit as
p
tends to infinity gives the first example of a hypergeometric moment. Ono, Saad, and Saikia
[
41
]
determine the values of this limit to be

(1.2)

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{e(\frac{m}{2}+1)}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{e}}}H_{p^{e}}(\lambda)^{m}=\begin{cases}0&\quad\textit{if $m$ is odd}\\
C(n)&\quad\textit{if $m$ = 2$n$ is even,}\end{cases}

where
m
and
e
are fixed positive integers and
C(n)=\frac{(2n)!}{n!(n+1)!}
denotes the
n
th Catalan number.

Our first two results extend (
1.2
) to the hypergeometric datum
\{\{\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}\},\{1,1\}\}
and determine the even moments for the case
\{\{\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\},\{1,1\}\}
. We restrict to primes
p
, for simplicity, but the properties of
L
-functions attached to hypergeometric Galois representations
[
25
]
can be used to explore moments for prime powers
q=p^{e}
. Our first result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
m
is a fixed positive integer then

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{(\frac{m}{2}+1)}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{m}=\begin{cases}0&\quad\text{if m is odd}\\
C(n)&\quad\text{if m = 2n is even.}\end{cases}

The result in
Theorem

1.1
was recently established by Ono, Pujahari, Saad, and Saikia
[
39
]
for primes

using an Eicher–Selberg trace formula. We generalize their result to primes

in

1.1
using new hypergeometric trace formulas developed by Hoffman, Li, Long, and Tu. See
Section

2
for more details on these new trace formulas. Further, our trace formula technique leads to the following new case.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
n
is a fixed nonnegative integer then

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{n+1}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{4}&\frac{3}{4}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2n}=C(n).

Further,

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{4}&\frac{3}{4}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2n+1}=0

for primes
p\equiv 5\pmod{8}
and
p\equiv 7\pmod{8}
.

Our techniques also allow for the following refinement of (
1.2
) to square values.

Theorem 1.3

If
m

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{(\frac{m}{2}+1)}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}(\lambda^{2})^{m}=\begin{cases}0&\quad\text{if m is odd}\\
C(n)&\quad\text{if m = 2n is even}\end{cases}

The even moments in our main results, the Catalan numbers, are ubiquitous in mathematics. For example,
C(n)
is the number of permutations of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
which avoid the permutation pattern
(1\,2\,3)

C(n)=\,_{2}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}1-n&-n\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&2\end{matrix}\;;\;1\right].

A third perspective on the Catalan numbers is as the even moments of the traces in the Lie group
\text{SU}(2)

\int_{\text{SU}(2)}(\text{Tr}X)^{2n}dX=C(n),

where the integral is with respect to the Haar measure on
\text{SU}(2)
. In particular, the even moments in (
1.2
) and our main results correspond to the semicircular distribution of traces for the matrices in
\text{SU}(2)
. All moments considered in this paper are related to point counting on appropriate families of elliptic curves, such as the Legendre family for (
1.2

The approach to the proof of (

1.3.
Algebraic Formulas in the Finite Field Setting

The algebraic cases are those in which the solutions to the corresponding hypergeometric differential equation are algebraic functions. See section 3.2 of
[
19
]
for background on hypergeometric differential equations.

In
[
10
]
Beukers and Heckman give a simple criteria, called interlacing, to determine if a hypergeometric datum is algebraic.
Suppose that
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
with
0\leq a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}<1
and
0\leq b_{1}\leq\cdots\leq b_{n}\leq 1
, where the data is consider modulo
\mathbb{Z}
. The two multisets
\alpha=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
and
\beta=\{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n}\}
are said to interlace if and only if

a_{1}<b_{1}<\cdots<a_{n}<b_{n}
or
b_{1}<a_{1}<\cdots<b_{n}<a_{n}
.

Beukers and Heckman showed that a hypergeometric datum
\{\alpha,\beta\}
is algebraic if and only if
\alpha
and
\beta
interlace. We first determine algebraic formulas for the three cases of length two data that are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, primitive, and algebraic.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
p
be an odd prime not dividing
M
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}

f_{\lambda}(x)=x^{3}+3x^{2}-4\lambda

be the number of zeros for

in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
counted with multiplicity. If

(1)\,\,H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{4}&\frac{3}{4}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]=\bigg(\frac{1+\phi(\lambda)}{2}\bigg)\bigg[\phi(1+\sqrt{\lambda})+\phi(1-\sqrt{\lambda})\bigg],

(2)\,\,H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]=N_{f}(\lambda,p)-1,

(3)\,\,H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{6}&\frac{5}{6}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]=\phi\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{3}\bigg)(N_{f}(\lambda,p)-1).

In particular,

H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{4}&\frac{3}{4}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\;;\;1\right]=\phi(2),H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\;;\;1\right]=1,\text{and}\,\,H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{6}&\frac{5}{6}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\;;\;1\right]=\phi(3).

Note the polynomial

has no repeated roots for
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0,1\}

2.
The Trace Formulas

We use
{\rm Tr}_{k}(\Gamma,p)
to denote the trace of the Hecke operator
T_{p}
on the space of cusp forms
S_{k}(\Gamma)
, where
\Gamma
is a congruence subgroup of SL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Following Frechette, Ono, and Papanikolas
[
17
]
define

G_{k}(s,p):=\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{k}{2}-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{k-2-j}{j}p^{j}s^{k-2j-2}

for
k\geq 2
even. The proof strategy used on the new results in
Section

1.2
is to apply induction on the exponent of the moment and use the relevant trace formula to obtain the necessary recursion.

2.1.
Trace Formulas for Subgroups of
\boldsymbol{\Gamma_{0}(3)}

A trace formula for even weight cusp forms on
\Gamma_{0}(3)
was first obtained by Lennon in Corollary 1.3 of
[
28
]
. However, this formula is an average of
H_{p^{i}}(\lambda)
values as both
i
and
\lambda
vary. Lennon’s formula does not involve the exponent
m
on the
H_{p}
function values, which is necessary for our recursive argument. Instead, we use the following result to prove
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 2.1

(Hoffman, Li, Long, and Tu
[
22
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 1
,
p
be an odd prime, and
\eta_{3}
be any order three character on
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}
. Then the following is true.

-{\rm Tr}_{k+2}(\Gamma_{1}(3),p)=1+\phi(-3)^{k}-\delta_{3,k}(p)+\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{j}\binom{k-j}{j}p^{j}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{k-2j}

where

\delta_{3,k}(p)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq k\\
k\equiv 2i\,(\text{mod}\,\,3)\end{subarray}}p^{i}J(\eta_{3},\eta_{3})^{k-2i}&\quad\text{if $p\equiv 1\pmod{3}$}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt0&\quad\text{if $p\equiv 2\pmod{3}$, \hskip 10.22217pt$k$ odd}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt(-p)^{\frac{k}{2}}&\quad\text{if $p\equiv 2\pmod{3}$, \hskip 10.22217pt$k$ even}\end{cases}

and

J(\chi,\psi):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\chi(x)\psi(1-x)

is a Jacobi sum.

&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2(r-j)}

\delta_{4,2r}(p)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{p^{r}\sum_{-\frac{r}{2}\leq i\leq\frac{r}{2}}\bigg(\frac{J(\eta_{4},\eta_{4})^{2}}{p}\bigg)^{2i}}&\quad\text{if $p\equiv 1\pmod{4}$}\\
(-p)^{r}&\quad\text{if $p\equiv 3\pmod{4}$}\\
\end{cases}

(2)\,\,\,-{\rm Tr}_{k+2}(\Gamma_{0}(8),p)=4+\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-2}G_{k+2}(a_{\lambda^{2}}^{\textnormal{Leg}}(p),p).

We have observed that, in certain cases, odd moments correspond to trace formulas for modular forms of odd weight and that even moments correspond to trace formulas for modular forms of even weight. For example, formula

Let

-{\rm Tr}_{k+2}(\Gamma_{1}(4),p)=\frac{1+(-1)^{k}}{2}+1+\phi(-1)^{k}+\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{j}\binom{k-j}{j}p^{j}H_{p}(\lambda)^{k-2j}

2.3.
Bounds on Coefficients of Modular Forms

A key part of our inductive method involves bounding coefficients of modular forms. Let
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
, where
\mathcal{H}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,\mathrm{Im}\,(\tau)>0\}
, and write
q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}
. The following well-known result of Deligne allows us to obtain bounds for the Fourier coefficients of cusp forms.

Proposition 2.5

(Cohen and Stromburg
[
14
]
, Remark 9.3.15.)

If
\displaystyle f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}
is a cusp form of integer weight

for a congruence subgroup, then for all
\varepsilon>0
we have
a_{n}=O(n^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\varepsilon})

4.1.
The Odd Moments

We now determine the odd moments. Letting
k=2r+1
in
Theorem

2.1
yields

\begin{split}-{\rm Tr}_{2r+3}(\Gamma_{1}(3),p)&=c(2r+1)+\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{(2r+1)-j}{j}p^{j}\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-1}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2(r-j)+1}\\
&=c(2r+1)+\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-1}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2r+1}\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{(2r+1)-j}{j}p^{j}\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-1}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2(r-j)+1}.\end{split}

Then

(4.1)

\begin{split}\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-1}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2r+1}&=-c(2r+1)-{\rm Tr}_{2r+3}(\Gamma_{1}(3),p)\\
&-\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{(2r+1)-j}{j}p^{j}\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-1}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2(r-j)+1}.\end{split}

We proceed by induction. The base case

follows easily since

\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-1}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]=O(1)

using the orthogonality of characters and the definition of the

function from (

). Now consider the inductive step. Normalizing (
4.1
) gives

\frac{1}{p^{r+\frac{3}{2}}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2r+1}

=O\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\bigg)-\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{(2r+1)-j}{j}\Bigg(\frac{1}{p^{r-j+\frac{3}{2}}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2(r-j)+1}\Bigg).

Taking limits yields

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{r+\frac{3}{2}}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2r+1}

=-\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{(2r+1)-j}{j}\Bigg(\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{r-j+\frac{3}{2}}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2(r-j)+1}\Bigg)=0

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{r-j+\frac{3}{2}}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2(r-j)+1}=0

for each

, by the inductive hypothesis.

4.2.
The Even Moments

We now show the even moments. In our cases, the inductive step is realized as a special case of the following result of Chu.

Let

\\
\binom{2n-m}{n}\cdot\frac{m+1}{n+1}&\quad\text{if}\quad m\leq 2n.\\

Letting

in

2.1

\begin{split}&-{\rm Tr}_{2r+2}(\Gamma_{1}(3),p)\\

&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2r}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r-j}{j}p^{j}\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-1}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\

\begin{split}&\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-1}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\

&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2r}\\
&=O\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\bigg)-\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r-j}{j}\Bigg(\frac{1}{p^{r-j+1}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{3}&\frac{2}{3}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\

function. Observe that
a_{p}(\lambda^{2})=\phi(-1)H_{p}(\lambda^{2})
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0,1\}
, by Koike’s result. Letting

in formula

of

2.3
gives

\begin{split}-{\rm Tr}_{2r+2}(\Gamma_{0}(8),p)&=4+\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r-j}{j}p^{j}\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-2}H_{p}(\lambda^{2})^{2(r-j)}\\
&=4+\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-2}H_{p}(\lambda^{2})^{2r}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r-j}{j}p^{j}\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-2}H_{p}(\lambda^{2})^{2(r-j)}.\\
\end{split}

Then

\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-2}H_{p}(\lambda^{2})^{2r}=-{\rm Tr}_{2r+2}(\Gamma_{0}(8),p)-4-\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r-j}{j}p^{j}\sum_{\lambda=2}^{p-2}H_{p}(\lambda^{2})^{2(r-j)}

\frac{1}{p^{r+1}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}(\lambda^{2})^{2r}=O\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\bigg)-\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r-j}{j}\bigg[\frac{1}{p^{r-j+1}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}(\lambda^{2})^{2(r-j)}\bigg].

The even moments for

1.3

) using the same inductive argument used in the proofs of

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
.

8.
Additional Cases and Numerical Conjectures

Hypergeometric data of lengths two and three that are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and primitive are studied carefully in this section. In particular, we determine all cases that are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and primitive when the data has a length of two or three. Then numerical conjectures and some new results are presented.

8.1.
Length Two Data

In this section, all data will be considered
\textrm{modulo}\,\,\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\varphi(n)
denote the Euler totient function. We consider all possible cases of length two hypergeometric data that are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and primitive. The
\alpha
set must have the form
\alpha=\{\frac{1}{s},1-\frac{1}{s}\}
, where
s=2
or
\varphi(s)=2
. Therefore, the only eligible values of
s
in the
\alpha
set are
s=2,3,4,6
.

Now the
\beta
set has the form
\beta=\{1,\frac{1}{t}\}
, where
\varphi(t)=1
. The only two possibilities for
t
are
t=1
and
t=2
. Let
\text{HD}(s,t):=\{\{\frac{1}{s},1-\frac{1}{s}\},\{1,\frac{1}{t}\}\}
. The seven possible cases of length two data that are both defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and primitive are
\text{HD}(2,1),\text{HD}(3,1),\text{HD}(4,1),\text{HD}(6,1)
,
\text{HD}(3,2)
,
\text{HD}(4,2),
and
\text{HD}(6,2)
. The moments for the case
\text{HD}(2,1)
are established by Ono, Saad, and Saikia in (
1.2
) and the moments for the cases
\text{HD}(3,1)
and
\text{HD}(4,1)
are given in
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
, respectively. The remaining case with
t=1
,
\text{HD}(6,1)
, has the same moments, numerically, as the cases
\text{HD}(2,1),\text{HD}(3,1)
and
\text{HD}(4,1)
.

Conjecture 8.1
.

If
m
is a fixed positive integer then

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{(\frac{m}{2}+1)}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{6}&\frac{5}{6}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{m}=\begin{cases}0&\quad\text{if m is odd}\\
C(n)&\quad\text{if m = 2n is even}\end{cases}

Note that taking
d=6
in
Corollary

3.5
gives

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{6}&\frac{5}{6}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2n+1}=0

for primes
p\equiv 7\pmod{12}
and
p\equiv 11\pmod{12}
at nonnegative integers
n
. The elliptic curves associated with each non-algebraic case here are listed in
Table

1
.

We now provide further evidence for
8.1
by establishing the first and second moments for the
\text{HD}(6,1)
case. The first moment follows from the orthogonality of characters and is recorded below.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
\{\alpha,\beta,\lambda\}
be a hypergeometric datum which is both defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and primitive. Define
H_{p}(\alpha,\beta,0)=1
. Then

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}(\alpha,\beta,\lambda)=0.

The orthogonality of characters also allows us to prove the second moment for
8.1
.

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
p>3
be a prime. Then

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{6}&\frac{5}{6}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2}=\begin{cases}p^{2}-3p-1&\quad\text{if \, $p\equiv 1\pmod{3}$ }\\
p^{2}-p-1&\quad\text{if \, $p\equiv 2\pmod{3}$}\end{cases}

so

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{6}&\frac{5}{6}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2}=1.

Proof.

Observe that
Proposition

3.1
gives

H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{6}&\frac{5}{6}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]=\frac{1}{1-p}\sum_{\chi}\frac{g(\chi^{6})g(\chi)g(\overline{\chi})^{2}}{g(\chi^{2})g(\chi^{3})}\chi\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{432}\bigg).

Then

\begin{split}&\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{6}&\frac{5}{6}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{(1-p)^{2}}\sum_{\chi}\frac{g(\chi^{6})g(\chi)g(\overline{\chi})^{2}}{g(\chi^{2})g(\chi^{3})}\sum_{\psi}\frac{g(\psi^{6})g(\psi)g(\overline{\psi})^{2}}{g(\psi^{2})g(\psi^{3})}\bigg(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\chi\psi(\lambda)\bigg).\end{split}

Now note that

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\chi\psi(\lambda)=\begin{cases}p-1&\quad\text{if}\quad\text{$\psi=\overline{\chi}$}\\
0&\quad\text{if}\quad\text{$\psi\neq\overline{\chi}$}.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\begin{split}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{6}&\frac{5}{6}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2}&=\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{\chi}\frac{g(\chi^{6})g(\chi)g(\overline{\chi})^{2}}{g(\chi^{2})g(\chi^{3})}\frac{g(\overline{\chi^{6}})g(\chi)g(\overline{\chi})^{2}}{g(\overline{\chi^{2}})g(\overline{\chi^{3}})}\\
&=\frac{1}{p-1}\bigg(p^{2}+1+\sum_{\chi\neq\varepsilon,\phi}\frac{g(\chi^{6})g(\chi)g(\overline{\chi})^{2}}{g(\chi^{2})g(\chi^{3})}\frac{g(\overline{\chi^{6}})g(\chi)g(\overline{\chi})^{2}}{g(\overline{\chi^{2}})g(\overline{\chi^{3}})}\bigg).\end{split}

There are now two cases when
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. If
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
then

\begin{split}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{6}&\frac{5}{6}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2}&=\frac{p^{3}-4p^{2}+2p+1}{p-1}\\
&=p^{2}-3p-1,\end{split}

by
Proposition

3.1
. If
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
then a similar computation gives

\begin{split}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{6}&\frac{5}{6}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&1\end{matrix}\;;\;\lambda\right]^{2}&=\frac{p^{3}-2p^{2}+1}{p-1}\\
&=p^{2}-3p-1.\end{split}

∎

The remaining three cases of length two data, the
t=2
cases, correspond to algebraic hypergeometric functions, discussed in Chapter 3 of
[
19
]
. The algebraic formulas for these cases are given in
Theorem

1.4
. The even moments for these cases
\text{HD}(3,2),\text{HD}(4,2),\text{HD}(6,2)
do not appear to match with a known sequence in OEIS. However, numerical data suggests the odd moments are zero in these three cases.

8.2.
Length Three Data

We now give a similar analysis for the relevant cases of length three data. In particular, we find there are thirteen cases of hypergeometric data in length three which are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and primitive. It is well known that Euler’s totient function
\varphi(n)
is even for
n>2
so the
\alpha
set must have the following form
\alpha=\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{s},1-\frac{1}{s}\}
for

and
6
, following the discussion for the length two data. The possibilities for the
\beta
set are a subset of the possibilities for the
\alpha
set in the case of length two data. The cases in which
\beta=\{1,\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\}
and
\beta=\{1,\frac{1}{s},1-\frac{1}{s}\}
are excluded to ensure the hypergeometric datum is primitive.

Let
\text{HD}(2,s,t):=\{\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{s},1-\frac{1}{s}\},\{1,\frac{1}{t},1-\frac{1}{t}\}\}
, where all values are considered
\text{mod}\,\,\mathbb{Z}
. The thirteen possible cases of length three data that are both defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and primitive are
\text{HD}(2,2,1)
,
\text{HD}(2,3,1)
,
\text{HD}(2,4,1)
,
\text{HD}(2,6,1)
,
\text{HD}(2,2,3)
,
\text{HD}(2,2,4)
,
\text{HD}(2,2,6)
,
\text{HD}(2,3,4)
,
\text{HD}(2,3,6)
,
\text{HD}(2,4,3)
,
\text{HD}(2,4,6)
,
\text{HD}(2,6,3)
, and
\text{HD}(2,6,4)
. The three cases
\text{HD}(2,4,3)
,
\text{HD}(2,6,3)
, and
\text{HD}(2,6,4)
are algebraic. The corresponding algebraic formulas are in
Theorem

1.5
.

All ten remaining cases are not algebraic. The moments for the first case,
\text{HD}(2,2,1)
, were determined by Ono, Saad, and Saikia in
[
41
]
. We observe the remaining nine non-algebraic cases have the same moments as the
\text{HD}(2,2,1)
case, numerically.

Conjecture 8.4
.

If
m
is a fixed positive integer then

\lim_{p\to+\infty}\frac{1}{p^{{m+1}}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}&\frac{1}{s}&1-\frac{1}{s}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\

\end{cases}

for
(s,t)\in\{(3,1),(4,1),(6,1)\}
and

\lim_{p\to+\infty}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}&\frac{1}{s}&1-\frac{1}{s}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\

is due to the zig-zag diagram

associated with each hypergeometric datum. See
[

is the weight of

and

is the associated minimum value then work of Katz

shows

HD
. Now observe that

8.2

Proposition 8.5
.

Let

be a prime and fix

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p^{3}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}H_{p}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}&\frac{1}{d}&1-\frac{1}{d}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\

A similar argument can be used to establish the second moment for the six remaining cases of
8.4
. The values of the even moments for length three data have a geometric meaning as the even traces of the matrices in the real orthogonal group
O_{3}
. That is, for
m

\int_{O_{3}}({\rm Tr}X)^{m}dX=\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}C(i),

where the integral is with respect to the Haar measure on
O_{3}
.

The corresponding distribution was shown to be the so-called Batman distribution in
[
41
]
. This Batman distribution was made more explicit in the work of Saad
[
46
]
. Recently, Pujahari and Saikia have discovered the
O_{3}
distribution appears for a few cases of
p
-adic hypergeometric functions
[
43
]
. Further, Huang, Ono, and Saad
[
23
]
showed the
O_{3}
distribution also appears when counting matrix points on certain K3 surfaces. The hypergeometric differential equations for these length three defined over
\mathbb{Q}
cases also appear in string theory
[
26
]
when studying fibrations of
K3
surfaces.

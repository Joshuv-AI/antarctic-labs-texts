Abstract
It is shown that an i.i.d. binary source sequence X 1 , … , X n X_{1},\ldots,X_{n} can be losslessly compressed at any rate above entropy such that the individual decoding of any X i X_{i} reveals no information about the other bits { X j : j ≠ i } \{X_{j}:j\neq i\} .

Several recent works have studied compression with locality properties

In
[
3
]
, we introduced the problem of compression with private local decoding (also referred to as
privacy-preserving random access
): can a binary source sequence

{\bm{X}}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})

(1)

be losslessly compressed into a codeword
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{nR}
such that the local decoding of any
X_{i}
depends only on a subset of codeword symbols
{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\{C_{j}:j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}\}
, for some
\mathcal{I}_{i}\subseteq[nR]
, and reveals no information about the remaining source symbols
\{X_{j}:j\neq i\}

To ensure reliability, the codeword bits
{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}
accessed by local decoder
i
must enable a reliable distinction between the hypotheses
X_{i}=0
and
X_{i}=1
.
To ensure privacy, these accessed bits must be independent of all other source symbols
\{X_{j}:j\neq i\}
.
The challenge stems from compression; some codeword subsets
{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}
and
{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{j}}
must overlap which potentially compromises local privacy.

Somewhat counterintuitively, we showed that for an i.i.d.
\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)
source, such a scheme exists and achieves a nontrivial compression rate

R=c\,p\,\log^{2}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{p}\right),

(2)

for a universal constant
c>0
and sufficiently small
p
(all logarithms are base two).
However, it remained unclear whether the gap to the entropy

H(p)\overset{\text{def}}{=}-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)

(3)

is intrinsic to the privacy constraint.
In particular, the scheme exhibits a multiplicative gap of order
\log(1/p)
in the small-
p
regime.

In this paper, we show that for any i.i.d.
\mathrm{Bernoulli}
source, private local decoding does not fundamentally limit compression; there exist schemes that achieve private local decoding at any rate above entropy. The present paper provides the full version of the conference paper
[
4
]
which only sketched parts of the proofs of some of the results.

Most recently, paper
[
9
]
proposed a simple low complexity scheme that achieves entropy with private local decoding for any i.i.d. source, not necessarily binary. The scheme mainly relies on concatenation: the parsing of the
n
-length source sequence into blocks of size
\log n
, which renders the scheme inherently asymptotic.

The scheme provided in this paper does not rely on concatenation, and the techniques developed here—particularly those used to handle overlaps between the subsets
\{{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}\}
—form a central component of our approach and may be of independent interest. The objective is to compress a source sequence
\bm{x}
into a random
nR
-bit codeword
{\bm{C}}
such that each marginal
{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}
is independent of
\{x_{j}:j\neq i\}
while enabling reliable recovery of
x_{i}
. A key step is to show that these marginal constraints can be simultaneously satisfied through a joint codeword distribution, an instance of the classical
marginal problem

[
6
]
. Existing methods are inconclusive in our setting, so we develop a technique that leverages a geometric representation of codeword distributions—the
block marginal polytope
—to prove, through a sequence of distribution perturbations, the existence of a codeword distribution that satisfies the marginal constraints. This perturbative approach may be of broader interest for tackling related variants of the marginal problem.

Problem Statement and Main Result

Throughout the paper, we consider the compression of an i.i.d. Bernoulli(
p
) bit string

{\bm{X}}=X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}

with parameter
p=\mathrm{Pr}(X_{1}=1)\in(0,1/2)

[
3
]

Definition 1

(Locally decodable code)
.

Given an integer
n\geq 1
and a constant
R>0

locally decodable code

{\cal{C}}_{n}
consists of:

•

an encoder
which maps each length-n bit string
{\bm{x}}
to a random
nR

1
1

1

We write

to mean
\lceil nR\rceil

{\bm{C}}=C_{1},\ldots,C_{nR}

•

where each function

{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\{C_{j}:j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}\}

and outputs an estimate

\hat{X}_{i}\overset{\text{def}}{=}f_{i}({\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}).

Definition 2

(Error probability and privacy)
.

The error probability of a rate-

is defined as

P_{e}^{(n)}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\mathrm{Pr}(\hat{{\bm{X}}}\neq{\bm{X}}),

where

\hat{{\bm{X}}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\hat{X}_{1},\ldots,\hat{X}_{n}

denotes the concatenation of the estimates of the
n

\mathrm{Pr}(\hat{{\bm{X}}}\neq{\bm{X}})=\sum_{{\bm{x}}}P_{{\bm{X}}}({\bm{x}})\sum_{{{\bm{c}}}:\hat{{\bm{x}}}({\bm{c}})\neq{\bm{x}}}P_{{\bm{C}}|{\bm{X}}}({\bm{c}}|{\bm{x}}).

The code is said to be

(or simply

) if

\displaystyle P_{{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}|{\bm{X}}={\bm{x}}}=P_{{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}|X_{i}=x_{i}},

(4)

for every

and every

The privacy constraint (

{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}

be independent conditioned on

,
2
2

2

{\bm{X}}_{-i}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\{X_{j}:j\neq i\}
.

that is,

P_{{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}|{\bm{x}}}=P_{{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}|x_{i}}.

Since the source is i.i.d., this conditional independence implies unconditional independence,

,

\displaystyle P_{{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}|{\bm{X}}_{-i}}=P_{{\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}},

(5)

which can be viewed as an alternative formulation of the privacy requirement.

Our main result, stated next, says that private local decodability can be achieved at any rate above entropy:

Theorem 1
.

For every
0<p\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
R>H(p)
, there exists a sequence of rate-
R

compression schemes
\{{\mathcal{C}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
that are all locally privately decodable and that achieve vanishing error probability

P_{e}^{(n)}
as
n\to\infty
.

Theorem
1
can be leveraged to establish the existence of coding schemes that achieve a certain level of compression while ensuring private local decodability for non-binary i.i.d. sources. Consider a memoryless source
{\bm{X}}
over a non-binary alphabet
\cal{X}
. Represent each symbol
X_{i}
as a binary string
B_{i}(1),\ldots,B_{i}(k)
, where
k=\lceil\log_{2}|\mathcal{X}|\rceil
. Accordingly, write
{\bm{X}}
as the concatenation of the binary sequences
{\bm{B}}(1),\ldots,{\bm{B}}(k)
, where
{\bm{B}}(\ell)
denotes the length-
n
sequence of bits corresponding to the
\ell
-th position in the binary representations of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
. Since the source is i.i.d., each sequence
{\bm{B}}(\ell)
is i.i.d. Bernoulli(
p_{\ell}
) for some parameters

\log|\mathcal{X}|
.
3
3

3

For instance, for a ternary source, we have
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k=2}H(p_{\ell})\leq\min\{H(p_{1}),H(p_{2})\}+1
. Hence, we have
\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}H(p_{\ell})<\log 3
whenever
\min\{H(p_{1}),H(p_{2})\}<\log(3/2)\approx 0.58

In this section, we describe the coding scheme that yields Theorem
1

{\bm{x}}
a random
nR
-bit codeword sampled from some specific conditional distribution
P_{{\bm{C}}|{\bm{x}}}

\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{V}_{L}

\mathcal{V}_{R}
of
n

1

The left vertices correspond to codeword components, whereas the right vertices correspond to the estimates of the source components.
Each right vertex

codeword components, defined as

\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\{C_{j}:j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}\},\quad\mathcal{I}_{i}\subseteq[nR],\;|\mathcal{I}_{i}|=b,

and is associated with a local decoding function

given by

\hat{X}_{i}\overset{\text{def}}{=}f_{i}({\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}).

The concatenation of all

f({\bm{C}})\overset{\text{def}}{=}f_{1}({\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{1}}),\ldots,f_{n}({\bm{C}}_{\mathcal{I}_{n}})=\hat{X}_{1},\ldots,\hat{X}_{n}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\hat{{\bm{X}}}.

To prove the existence of a reliable and private code

{\cal{C}}_{n}=(P_{{\bm{C}}|{\bm{X}}},\mathcal{G},f),

we use a probabilistic argument where the decoder

is randomly selected in a certain decoder ensemble. We then show that with high probability

admits an encoding

III-A

Random decoder ensemble

The graph
\mathcal{G}
and the decoding function

are randomly and independently selected as follows.
For

b=\Theta(\log n)
left vertices (among all
nR

Further, each

is a composition of two functions

where

is the syndrome of a linear error correcting code with minimum distance
\geq 4

is independently selected by randomly and uniformly sampling a subset of

of size
\lceil p2^{b^{\prime}}\rceil
as the preimages of
1
.
Later, we will have

To establish Theorem

Definition 3

(Valid codewords)
.

Given a decoder

Remark 1
.

Note that for a fixed decoder

Remark 2
.

To achieve compression, the index sets

. We therefore define

—this will be detailed later, see Property
5

At the heart of the proof of Theorem

IV-A

Definition 4

(Block-marginal polytope)

The block-marginal polytope is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}\Big\{\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{n2^{b}}:

\displaystyle\qquad\phi^{(i)}(c^{b})\geq 0,\>i\in[n],c^{b}\in\{0,1\}^{b},

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{c^{b}\in\{0,1\}^{b}}\phi^{(i)}(c^{b})=1,\>i\in[n]\Big\}.

\phi^{(i)}

i

Given a bipartite graph

Specifically, let

•

The ideal, the uniform, and the approximate block-marginal vectors

Fix a decoder

. We define the three block-marginal vectors and comment thereafter:

The ideal block-marginal vector,

The uniform block-marginal vector,

The approximate block-marginal vector

\displaystyle\phi_{A,{\bm{x}}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\frac{n}{n+1}\phi_{I,{\bm{x}}}+\frac{1}{n+1}\phi_{{U}}.

In contrast to

) and is “close” to

and all being sufficiently “close” to the uniform distribution, and we want to show that there exists a joint distribution
P
on
\{0,1\}^{nR}
with marginal
P_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}
equal to
\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}
for every
i
.
This is an instance of the classical
marginal problem
, a family of problems with a long history in the literature; see,
e.g.
,
[
8
,
6
,
5
,
10
]
.

A probability distribution that satisfies the specified marginal constraints exists if there exists a nonnegative solution
P
to the linear system of equations

A\cdot P=B

where
P
is a
2^{b}
-length probability vector and

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\dots&0\\
0&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&1&\dots&1\end{pmatrix}

is a
(n2^{b}+1)\times 2^{nR}
matrix. Each row of
A
, except the last, corresponds to the one-hot encoding of a
b
-bit block sample from a marginal distribution. The last row is an all-ones vector ensuring that
P
sums to one. The vector
B=(\phi,1)
contains the marginal probabilities, except for the last component, which is equal to one.

Hence, knowing that for

B=B_{0}\overset{\text{def}}{=}(\phi_{U},1),

a solution
P^{0}
exists, namely the uniform distribution, one could attempt to show through classical methods (
e.g.
, Farkas Lemma) that a solution still exists if we perturb
\phi_{U}
by
n\eta
. Unfortunately, such approaches appear to give too restrictive sufficient conditions that do not cover our set of perturbations.

Instead, we adopt a constructive approach. Starting from the uniform distribution
P^{0}
, we first construct a perturbation yielding
P^{\prime}
whose bit marginals coincide with those of the target block marginals. In the second step, we further perturb
P^{\prime}
to obtain
P^{\prime\prime}
that matches all bit-pair marginals. Finally, we perturb
P^{\prime\prime}
to obtain
P^{\star}
, which matches all block marginals. This last step leverages the property
|\mathcal{I}_{i}\cap\mathcal{I}_{j}|\leq 2
, which ensures that, once the bit-pair marginals are matched, the block marginals can be adjusted independently. Fig.
4
illustrates the approach.

Fig. 4
:
Illustration of the proof of Lemma
4
. Each region represents the set of distributions consistent with an increasing number of marginal constraints derived from
\{\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}\}_{i\in[n]}
: the outermost region enforces the bit marginals, the intermediate region enforces the bit-pair marginals, and the innermost region enforces the full block marginals.

Definition 8

(Marginal consistency)
.

Let
\{\mathcal{I}_{i}:i=1,2,\ldots,m\}
be a collection of subsets of
[n]
, and let

\{\rho_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}:i=1,2,\ldots,m\}

be a collection of probability mass functions on
\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal{I}_{i}|}
.
This collection of distributions is said to be marginally consistent if, for all
i\neq j
and
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{i}\cap\mathcal{I}_{j}
, the marginals with respect to the common variables are equal, i.e.,

\sum_{\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal{I}_{i}|}:\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime}=c}\rho_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}^{\prime})=\sum_{\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{j}}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal{I}_{j}|}:\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{I}}=c}\rho_{{\mathcal{I}_{j}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{j}}^{\prime})

We enumerate a few properties of marginal distributions:

1.

For any marginally consistent
\{\rho_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}:i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
with
|\mathcal{I}_{i}|>2
for all
i
, and
l,m\in[nR]
, the bit marginals
\rho_{l}
and bit-pair marginals
\rho_{lm}
are well defined—since they are the same for all
\rho_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}
.

2.

If
P
is a joint distribution on
\{0,1\}^{nR}
and
\rho_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}=P_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}
, then
\{\rho_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}:1\leq i\leq n\}
are marginally consistent.

3.

If
\{\rho_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}:i=1,2,\ldots,m\}
and
\{\rho^{\prime}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}:i=1,2,\ldots,m\}
are both marginally consistent, then any convex combination
\{\alpha\rho_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}+(1-\alpha)\rho^{\prime}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}:i=1,2,\ldots,m\}
,
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, is also marginally consistent.

4.

From Example
2
, we see that marginal consistency is necessary but not sufficient to guarantee the existence of a distribution
P_{\mathbf{C}}
on
\{0,1\}^{nR}
with marginals satisfying
P_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}=\rho_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}
for all
i
.

5.

If
\mathcal{G}
is such that
|\mathcal{I}_{i}\cap\mathcal{I}_{j}|\leq 2
for all
i\neq j
and
f_{i}=f_{i}^{(U)}\circ g
, then for every
\bm{x}
,
\phi_{I,\bm{x}}
is marginally consistent and all its bit-pair marginals are uniform.

To see why Property
5
) holds, consider any
i\in[n]
and
(a,b)\in\{0,1\}^{2}
. Then,

\sum_{\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}:c_{l}=a,c_{m}=b}\phi_{I,\bm{x}}^{(i)}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})=\frac{1}{|f_{i}^{-1}(x_{i})|}|\{\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}:c_{l}=a,c_{m}=b,f_{i}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})=x_{i}\}|.

Since
g(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})=H\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}
, where
H
is the parity check matrix of a linear code with minimum distance
4
, we have for every
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{b^{\prime}}
,
l\neq m
and every
(a,b)\in\{0,1\}
,

|\{\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}:c_{l}=a,c_{m}=b,H\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}=\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\}|=2^{b-b^{\prime}}

since
H
has full rank and every pair of columns of
H
are linearly independent. Therefore,
f_{i}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})
is independent of any pair of bits
(c_{l},c_{m})
,

|f_{i}^{-1}(x_{i})|=\begin{cases}\lfloor p2^{b^{\prime}}\rfloor 2^{b-b^{\prime}}&\text{ if }x_{i}=1\\
(2^{b^{\prime}}-\lfloor p2^{b^{\prime}})\rfloor)2^{b-b^{\prime}}&\text{ if }x_{i}=0\\
\end{cases}

and

\sum_{\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}:c_{l}=a,c_{m}=b}\phi_{I,\bm{x}}^{(i)}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})=\frac{1}{4}.

Since the bit-pair marginals are all uniform, the ideal block-marginal vector is consistent.

Proof of Lemma
4

Let
\phi
be any block marginal vector which can be written as
\phi=\phi_{U}-n\eta
for some
\eta\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}
. To make the dependence of
\phi^{(i)}
on
\mathcal{I}_{i}
more explicit, let us define

\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\phi^{(i)},\quad i\in[n].

Under the hypotheses of Lemma
4
,
\{\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{1}},\ldots\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{n}}\}
is a collection of marginally consistent distributions on
\{0,1\}^{b}
satisfying

e_{b,\min}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\min_{\mathbf{c}\in\{0,1\}^{b}}\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c})\geq\frac{1}{2^{b}}-O\left(\frac{1}{n2^{b}}\right),

with bit-pair marginals
\{\rho_{l,l^{\prime}}:1\leq l<l^{\prime}\leq nR\}
satisfying

e_{2,\max}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\max_{1\leq l<l^{\prime}\leq nR}\max_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\{0,1\}^{2}}\left|\rho_{l,l^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)-\frac{1}{4}\right|<O\left(\frac{1}{n^{3}}\right).

We will show that there exists a pmf
P
on
\{0,1\}^{nR}
such that the block marginals match with
\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}
,
i.e.
, for all
i\in[n]
and
\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}\in\{0,1\}^{b}
,

P_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})=\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}),

which would complete the proof.

We assume without loss of generality that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{I}_{i}=[nR]
. If this is not the case, then we can independently choose the distribution of the bits on
[nR]\backslash(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{I}_{i})
to be uniform without affecting the distribution of the remaining bits.

We break the proof down into three stages, and this is illustrated in Fig.
4
.
We proceed iteratively by constructing, at stage
k
, an additive perturbation that allows us to match the
k
’th order bit marginal while keeping lower-order bit marginals unchanged.

VI-B
1
Matching bit marginals

For
a\in\{0,1\}
, define

\delta_{a}(u)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }u=a\\
0&\text{ if }u\neq a.\end{cases}

This corresponds to the two vertices of the
1
-dimensional probability simplex.

For
l\in[nR]
, define the perturbation

\displaystyle\eta_{l}(\mathbf{c})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{nR-1}}\Big(\rho_{l}(0)\delta_{0}(c_{l})+\rho_{l}(1)\delta_{1}(c_{l})\Big)-P^{0}(\mathbf{c})

(16)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{nR-1}}\Big(\rho_{l}(0)\delta_{0}(c_{l})+\rho_{l}(1)\delta_{1}(c_{l})-1/2\Big)

(17)

where
P^{0}
denotes the uniform distribution on
\{0,1\}^{nR}
.
Therefore,

\sum_{\mathbf{c}:c_{l}=\alpha}\eta_{l^{\prime}}(\mathbf{c})=\begin{cases}\rho_{l}(\alpha)-\frac{1}{2}&\text{ if }l^{\prime}=l\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

for
\alpha\in\{0,1\}
,
and

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\{0,1\}^{nR}}\eta_{l}(\mathbf{c})=0.

(18)

Perturbation
\eta_{l}
is designed to match the
l
’th bit marginal while keeping all other bit marginals unchanged. Specifically, let us define

P^{\prime}(\mathbf{c})\overset{\text{def}}{=}P^{0}(\mathbf{c})+\sum_{l=1}^{nR}\eta_{l}(\mathbf{c}).

This is valid distribution since from (
18
) we get

\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\{0,1\}^{nR}}P^{\prime}(\mathbf{c})=1,

and

P^{\prime}(\mathbf{c})\geq 0\text{ for every }\mathbf{c}\in\{0,1\}^{nR}

since by assumption

e_{1,\max}=\max_{l\in[nR]}\max_{\alpha\in\{0,1\}}\left|\frac{1}{2}-\rho_{l}(\alpha)\right|=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{3}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2nR}.

Moreover, distribution
P^{\prime}
has the same bit marginals of
\{\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}:1\leq i\leq n\}
since

\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{l}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{c}:c_{l}=\alpha}P^{\prime}(\mathbf{c})=\rho_{l}(\alpha)

(19)

for any
l\in[nR]
and
\alpha\in\{0,1\}
.

A straightforward calculation shows that
for all
l,l^{\prime}\in[n]
with
l\neq l^{\prime}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\{0,1\}
,

\left|P^{\prime}_{l,l^{\prime}}(\alpha,\beta)-\frac{1}{4}\right|\leq e_{1,\max}=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{3}}\right),

and
for all
i
and all
\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}
,

\left|P^{\prime}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})-\frac{1}{2^{b}}\right|\leq\frac{b}{2^{b-1}}e_{1,\max}=O\left(\frac{b}{n^{3}2^{b}}\right).

Therefore, after matching the bit marginals, the bit-pair marginals and the block marginals do not deviate too much from the uniform.

The high-level idea behind the next two steps relies on the following observation.
Let
P,Q
be distributions on
\{0,1\}^{nR}
, and suppose
Q
has uniform
k
-wise bit marginals. Then

S=R+(Q-P_{U})

has the same
k
-wise bit marginals as
R
. This observation allows us to reduce the problem of matching
(k+1)
-wise bit marginals to that of constructing an appropriate
Q
with uniform
k
-wise marginals, such that the resulting
S
has the desired
(k+1)
-wise marginals.

More precisely, in Step 2 (respectively, Step 3), we construct
L=n(n-1)/2
(respectively,
L=n
) distributions
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{L}
and define

S=R+\sum_{i=1}^{L}(Q_{i}-P_{U}),

where each
Q_{i}
has uniform bit (respectively, bit-pair) marginals and matches the
i
th bit-pair (respectively, block) marginal of the target distribution.

VI-B
2
Matching bit-pair marginals

For
\alpha,\beta\in\{0,1\}
, define

\delta_{\text{eq}}(\alpha,\beta)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}&\text{ if }\alpha=\beta\\
0&\text{ if }\alpha\neq\beta\end{cases}

and

\delta_{\text{neq}}(\alpha,\beta)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\alpha=\beta\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{ if }\alpha\neq\beta.\end{cases}

Observe that these are the vertices of the polytope of distributions on
\{0,1\}^{2}
with uniform bit marginals.

For
(l,m)\in[nR]^{2}
with
l<m
, define the perturbation

\eta_{lm}(\mathbf{c})\overset{\text{def}}{=}\Big(\rho_{lm}(00)\delta_{\text{eq}}(c_{l},c_{m})+\rho_{lm}(01)\delta_{\text{neq}}(c_{l},c_{m})-P^{\prime}_{l}(c_{l})-P^{\prime}_{l}(c_{m})\Big)\frac{1}{2^{nR-2}}.

This perturbation is designed to match the
(l,l^{\prime})
’th marginal while keeping all other bit-pair marginals and all bit marginals unchanged. Observe that

\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\{0,1\}^{nR}}\eta_{lm}(\mathbf{c})=0,

and

\sum_{\mathbf{c}:c_{l^{\prime}}=\alpha,\,c_{m^{\prime}}=\beta}\eta_{lm}(\mathbf{c})=\begin{cases}\rho_{lm}(\alpha,\beta)-P_{lm}^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta),&\text{if }(l^{\prime},m^{\prime})=(l,m),\\[5.69054pt]
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Next, define

\displaystyle P^{\prime\prime}\overset{\text{def}}{=}P^{\prime}+\sum_{(l,m):\,l<m}\eta_{lm}.

(20)

We then have

\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\{0,1\}^{nR}}P^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{c})=1,

and

\sum_{\mathbf{c}:c_{l}=\alpha,\,c_{m}=\beta}P^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{c})=\rho_{lm}(\alpha,\beta),\quad\text{for }\>1\leq l<m,\,(\alpha,\beta)\in\{0,1\}^{2}.

Moreover,

P^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{c})\geq 0,\quad\mathbf{c}\in\{0,1\}^{nR},

since

e_{2,\max}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\max_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\max_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\{0,1\}^{2}}\left|\rho_{i,j}(\alpha,\beta)-\tfrac{1}{4}\right|=O\!\left(\tfrac{1}{n^{3}}\right)<\tfrac{1}{2n^{2}R^{2}}.

Hence,
P^{\prime\prime}
is a valid probability distribution.

Finally, the block marginals remain close to the uniform distribution, as a straightforward calculation shows that

\left|P^{\prime\prime}_{{\mathcal{I}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}})-\frac{1}{2^{b}}\right|\leq\frac{b}{2^{b-1}}e_{2,\max}=O\!\left(\frac{b}{n^{3}2^{b}}\right),

for all
\mathcal{I}\subset[nR]
with
|\mathcal{I}|=b
and all
\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}}\in\{0,1\}^{b}
.

VI-B
3
Matching block marginals

From the previous step we obtained distribution
P^{\prime\prime}
, whose bit-pair marginals correspond to
\{\rho_{l,l^{\prime}}\}
.
The final step is to construct a perturbation that simultaneously matches all higher-order marginals, from order
3
up to order
b
, while preserving the bit-pair marginals. This can be accomplished in a single step by exploiting the following property: since, by assumption,
|\mathcal{I}_{i}\cap\mathcal{I}_{j}|\leq 2
for all
i\neq j
, each higher-order marginal can be adjusted independently without affecting the bit-pair marginals or any other block marginals.

If

\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})\geq\frac{1}{2^{b}}-O\left(\frac{1}{n2^{b}}\right),\text{ for all }\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}\in\{0,1\}^{b},

then

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})-P^{\prime\prime}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})+\frac{1}{2^{b}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2^{b}}-O\left(\frac{1}{n2^{b}}\right)-O\left(\frac{b}{n^{3}2^{b}}\right),

\displaystyle\geq 0

for all
\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}\in\{0,1\}^{b}
,

\sum_{\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}\in\{0,1\}^{b}}\Big(\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})-P^{\prime\prime}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})+P^{0}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})\Big)=1,

and for every
(l,m)\in[nR]^{2}
and
(\alpha,\beta)\in\{0,1\}^{2}
,

\sum_{\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}:c_{l}=\alpha,c_{m}=\beta}\Big(\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})-P^{\prime\prime}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})+P^{0}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})\Big)=\frac{1}{4}.

Hence, for every
i\in[nR]
,

\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})-P^{\prime\prime}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})+P^{0}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})

is a distribution on
\{0,1\}^{b}
with
uniform
bit-pair marginals.

Consider the polytope
\mathcal{P}_{2}
of distributions on
\{0,1\}^{b}
such that all bit-pair marginals are uniform. This can be specified by at most
1+4\begin{pmatrix}b\\
2\end{pmatrix}
linear equality and
b
inequality constraints. Therefore, this has at most
2^{b}
vertices. Let

\mathcal{V}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\{\nu_{j}:1\leq j\leq|\mathcal{V}|\}

denote the set of vertices of
\mathcal{P}_{2}
.

For
i\in[nR]
, define the additive perturbation

\eta^{(i)}(\mathbf{c})\overset{\text{def}}{=}\frac{1}{2^{nR-b}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{V}|}\lambda_{ij}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})\nu_{j}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})\right)-P^{0}(\mathbf{c}),

where we choose
\lambda_{ij}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})\geq 0
such that

\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{V}}\lambda_{ij}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})\nu_{j}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})=\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})-P^{\prime\prime}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})+P^{0}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})

\sum_{\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}\in\{0,1\}^{b}}\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{V}|}\lambda_{ij}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})=1.

Further, define

P^{\star}(\mathbf{c})\overset{\text{def}}{=}P^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{c})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta^{(i)}(\mathbf{c}),

(21)

where
\lambda_{ij}
are chosen suitably so as to satisfy
\sum_{j=1}^{2^{b}}\lambda_{ij}=1
for every
i\in[n]
. We deduce that

\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\{0,1\}^{nR}}P^{\star}(\mathbf{c})=1,

and that
P_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{c})\geq 0
as long as

\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})\geq\frac{1}{2^{b}}-O\left(\frac{1}{n2^{b}}\right).

It remains to check that
P^{\star}
has block marginals given by
\{\rho_{{\cal{I}}_{i}}\}_{i\in[n]}
. We have

\displaystyle P^{\star}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})

\displaystyle=P^{\prime\prime}_{{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\mathbf{u}:\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}=\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}\eta^{(j)}(\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})+\sum_{j\neq i}\sum_{\mathbf{u}:\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}=\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}\eta^{(j)}(\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})+\sum_{j\neq i}\sum_{\mathbf{u}:\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}=\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}}\Big(\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{j}}(\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{I}_{j}})-P^{\prime\prime}_{{\mathcal{I}_{j}}}(\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{I}_{j}})\Big)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})+\sum_{j\neq i}\Big(\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{ji}}(\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{I}_{ji}})-P^{\prime\prime}_{{\mathcal{I}_{ji}}}(\mathbf{u}_{\mathcal{I}_{ji}})\Big)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}),

where
\mathcal{I}_{ji}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\mathcal{I}_{j}\cap\mathcal{I}_{i}
, and the last step follows from the fact that the bit-pair marginals of
P^{\prime\prime}
are the same as that corresponding to
\{\rho_{{\cal{I}}_{i}}\}_{i\in[n]}
. This completes the proof of Lemma
4
. ∎

VI-C

Proof of Lemma
5

We will show that, with high probability over
\mathcal{G}
and
f
, typical
{\bm{x}}
admit exponentially many valid codewords (see Definition
3
). Moreover, if we select
\mathbf{C}
uniformly at random from the set of valid codewords
\mathcal{C}(\bm{x})
, the resulting distribution
P_{\mathbf{C}}
is “close” to ideal. Ideally, one would like to establish that
(\phi_{V,{\bm{x}}}-\phi_{I,{\bm{x}}})\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}
, where
\phi_{V,{\bm{x}}}
denotes the block-marginal vector corresponding to the uniform distribution on
\mathcal{C}({\bm{x}})
; this would immediately yield the lemma. Although a direct proof of this claim appears elusive, we show that a slight pruning of the valid codewords, followed by uniform selection over the reduced set, is sufficient.

Definition 9

(Cylinder set)
.

Fix
(\mathcal{G},f)
.
For any
i\in[n]
and
\tilde{c}^{b}\in\{0,1\}^{b}
, we define the cylinder set of valid codewords

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{i}({\bm{x}},\tilde{c}^{b})

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}\Big\{{\bm{c}}:{\bm{c}}\in\mathcal{C}({\bm{x}}),c_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}=\tilde{c}^{b}\Big\}.

(22)

Definition 10

(Expurgated valid codewords)
.

Fix
(\mathcal{G},f)
. A cylinder
\mathcal{C}_{i}({\bm{x}},\tilde{c}^{b})
is said to be bad if

\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{i}({\bm{x}},\tilde{c}^{b})|}{|\mathcal{C}({\bm{x}})|}>\frac{1}{|f_{i}^{-1}(x_{i})|}+\frac{1}{n2^{b}}.

(23)

A codeword
{\bm{c}}\in\{0,1\}^{nR}
is said to be bad if it lies in a bad
\mathcal{C}_{i}({\bm{x}},\tilde{c}^{b})
for some
(i,\tilde{c}^{b})
, and good otherwise.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{exp}({\bm{x}})
denote the codebook obtained by removing all bad codewords from
\mathcal{C}({\bm{x}})
, and let
\phi_{\tilde{V},{\bm{x}}}
be the block-marginal vector corresponding to the uniform distribution over
\mathcal{C}_{exp}({\bm{x}})
.

We first show that with high probability over
(\mathcal{G},f)
, every
{\bm{x}}
with sufficiently small Hamming weight has exponentially many valid codewords.

Lemma 6
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},f)
be randomly chosen as described earlier. Let
R=H(p+\varepsilon)
.
Then, for any
{\bm{x}}
with Hamming weight
w\leq np_{\varepsilon}
, define

\displaystyle N_{{\bm{x}}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\mathbb{E}|\mathcal{C}({\bm{x}})|

\displaystyle=\exp_{2}\Big(nR+w\log p+(n-w)\log(1-p)+\Theta(n\log(1+2^{-b^{\prime}}))\Big).

(24)

Then, for every
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left[\big||\mathcal{C}({\bm{x}})|-N_{{\bm{x}}}\big|>\delta N_{{\bm{x}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{2}n^{99}}.

(25)

Furthermore, for every
i\in[n]
and
c^{b}\in\{0,1\}^{b}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{C}(\bm{x})|\big|c_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}=c^{b}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{\bm{x}}}{|f_{i}^{-1}(x_{i})|},

(26)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left||\mathcal{C}({\bm{x}})|-\frac{N_{\bm{x}}}{|f_{i}^{-1}(x_{i})|}\right|^{2}\Bigg|c_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}=c^{b}\right]

Proof of Lemma

We prove the lemma using the second moment method.

Let us fix an arbitrary
{\bm{x}}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
with Hamming weight
w\leq np_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathbf{c}\in\{0,1\}^{nR}

Note that for every
i\in[n]
and any fixed

, we have

\mathrm{Pr}_{f}[f_{i}(\mathbf{c}_{\mathcal{I}_{i}})=1]=\frac{\begin{pmatrix}2^{b^{\prime}}\\
\lceil p2^{b^{\prime}}\rceil-1\end{pmatrix}}{\begin{pmatrix}2^{b^{\prime}}\\
\lceil p2^{b^{\prime}}\rceil\end{pmatrix}}=\frac{\lceil p2^{b^{\prime}}\rceil}{2^{b^{\prime}}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}_{f}[f(\mathbf{c})={\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lceil p2^{b^{\prime}}\rceil}{2^{b^{\prime}}}\right)^{w}\left(\frac{2^{b^{\prime}}-\lceil p2^{b^{\prime}}\rceil}{2^{b^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-w}

\displaystyle=\exp_{2}\Big(w\log p+(n-w)\log(1-p)+\Theta(n\log(1+2^{-b^{\prime}}))\Big)

(28)

as long as

. It then follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\mathcal{C}({\bm{x}})|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\mathbf{c}}1_{\{f(\mathbf{c})={\bm{x}}\}}\right]

.

For the second moment of

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}({\bm{x}})|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\mathbf{c}}\sum_{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}1_{\{f(\mathbf{c})=f(\tilde{\mathbf{c}})={\bm{x}}\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}({\bm{c}})|]+\sum_{\mathbf{c}}\sum_{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}:\tilde{\mathbf{c}}\neq\mathbf{c}}\mathrm{Pr}_{\mathcal{G},f}\left[f(\mathbf{c})=f(\tilde{\mathbf{c}})={\bm{x}}\right].

(30)

We separately analyze the probability term for pairs
(\mathbf{c},\tilde{\mathbf{c}})
in two different regimes, depending on whether the Hamming distance between the two is large or small. Fix an arbitrary
\delta^{\prime}>0
.

Codewords
(\mathbf{c},\tilde{\mathbf{c}})
with

Simplifying, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}[f(\mathbf{c})=f(\tilde{\mathbf{c}})={\bm{x}}]

Codewords

VIII
Concluding remarks

We showed that lossless compression can accommodate private local decoding for memoryless sources. Several open problems remain. One would be to find a private, explicit, and non-asymptotic compression scheme. Another problem would be to investigate privacy and locality. We know that constant local decodability is achievable at any rate above entropy (for memoryless sources). Does this still hold if local decoding is subject to privacy? Note here the proposed scheme has locality
b=\Theta(\log n)
. Finally, an interesting extension to the present work would be to consider sources with memory.

Lemma 7
.

Consider any positive integer
D
and probability mass function
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{D})
that satisfies

\sum_{i=1}^{D}\left(p_{i}-\frac{1}{D}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha D},

for some
\alpha>0
. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left[p_{i}-(1+\epsilon)\frac{1}{D}\right]^{+}\leq\frac{1+\epsilon}{\alpha\epsilon^{2}}

where
[x]^{+}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\max\{0,x\}
.

Proof of Lemma
7
.

Let us sort
\{1,2,\ldots,D\}
in the decreasing order of
p_{i}
’s and let
\mathcal{D}
denote the sorted sequence. For
0<\rho<1
such that
\rho D\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, let
B(\rho)
denote the first
\rho D
elements of
\mathcal{D}
, equivalently, the
\rho D
largest probability values. Further, let

S(\rho)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\sum_{i\in B(\rho)}p_{i}

denote the total probability included in
B(\rho)
.

Using Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\rho D\frac{1}{\rho D}\sum_{i\in B(\rho)}\left(p_{i}-\frac{1}{D}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\rho D\left(\frac{S(\rho)}{\rho D}-\frac{1}{D}\right)^{2}=\frac{\rho}{D}\left(\frac{S(\rho)}{\rho}-1\right)^{2},

and by choosing

\rho=\frac{1}{D}\left\lfloor\frac{D}{\alpha\epsilon^{2}}\right\rfloor\leq\frac{1}{\alpha\epsilon^{2}},

we get

\sum_{i\in B(\rho)}\left(p_{i}-\frac{1}{D}\right)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha\epsilon^{2}D}\left(\frac{S(\rho)}{\rho}-1\right)^{2}.

(49)

Using the hypothesis of the lemma, we have

\frac{1}{\alpha\epsilon^{2}D}\left(\frac{S(\rho)}{\rho}-1\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha D},

which yields

\frac{S(\rho)}{\rho}\leq 1+\epsilon,

(50)

or, equivalently, using our choice of
\rho

S(\rho)\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)}{\alpha\varepsilon^{2}}.

(51)

From the definition of
B(\rho)
, we have that

S(\rho)\geq\rho D\min_{i\in B(\rho)}p_{i},

which when combined with (
50
) gives us

\min_{i\in B(\rho)}p_{i}\leq(1+\epsilon)\frac{1}{D}.

This implies for any
i\notin B(\rho)
we have

p_{i}\leq(1+\epsilon)\frac{1}{D}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{D}\left[p_{i}-(1+\epsilon)\frac{1}{D}\right]^{+}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in B(\rho)}p_{i}=S(\rho)\leq\frac{(1+\epsilon)}{\alpha\epsilon^{2}},

which yields the desired result. ∎

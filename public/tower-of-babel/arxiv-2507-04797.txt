Correcting Bursty/Localized Deletions: A New Error-Position-Estimation Code
Zuo Ye, Yubo Sun, Gennian Ge

Abstract
Codes correcting bursts of deletions and localized deletions have garnered significant research interest in recent years. One of the primary objectives is to construct codes with minimal redundancy. Currently, the best known constructions of q q -ary codes correcting a burst of at most t t deletions ( ( ≤ t ) (\leq t) -burst-deletion correcting codes) achieve redundancy log ⁡ n + 8 ​ log ⁡ log ⁡ n + o ⁡ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) \log n+8\log\log n+o(\log\log n) (for any q q and t t ) or log ⁡ n + t ​ log ⁡ log ⁡ n + O ⁡ ( 1 ) \log n+t\log\log n+O(1) (for even q q ). For codes correcting single t t -localized-deletion ( t t -localized-deletion correcting codes), state-of-the-art constructions attain redundancy log ⁡ n + O ⁡ ( t ​ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) 2 ) \log n+O\left(t(\log\log n)^{2}\right) (for any q q and t t ) or log ⁡ n + 2 ​ t ​ log ⁡ log ⁡ n + O ⁡ ( 1 ) \log n+2t\log\log n+O(1) (for even q q ). Here, n n denotes the code-length, and q q and t t are fixed. These codes employ a position-estimation component to approximate error positions, augmented by additional constraints that enable error-correction given the information about error positions. In this work, we select codewords from the set of sequences whose differential sequences are strong- ( ℓ , ϵ ) (\ell,\epsilon) -locally-balanced. By imposing a VT-type constraint and an L 1 L_{1} -weight constraint on the differential sequences of codewords, we construct novel position-estimation codes. When q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 and t < q t<q , or q q is even and t < 2 ​ q t<2q , this approach gives a q q -ary ( ≤ t ) (\leq t) -burst-deletion correcting code and a t t -localized-deletion correcting code with redundancy log ⁡ n + ( t − 1 ) ​ log ⁡ log ⁡ n + O ⁡ ( 1 ) \log n+(t-1)\log\log n+O(1) . In addition to improving previous redundancy, the method is new and our position-estimation codes are simpler than those in previous works. Finally, we give an efficient encoder to encode an arbitrary input sequence into a sequence whose differential sequence is strong- ( ℓ , ϵ ) (\ell,\epsilon) -locally-balanced. To our knowledge, no prior algorithm for this specific task has been reported.

III-B

strong-locally-balanced sequences

Given
\ell\leq n
and
0<\epsilon<1/2
, a sequence
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{2}^{n}
is called a
strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced
sequence, if any substring of
\mathbf{x}
of length
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
has Hamming weight in the interval
\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon\right)\ell^{\prime},\left(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon\right)\ell^{\prime}\right]
.

In 2018, Gabrys
et al

[
20
]
introduced the concept of strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced sequences (over the binary alphabet) to construct burst-deletion-correcting codes. For a fixed
t\geq 5
, by imposing a VT-type constraint and other constraints on strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced sequences, they constructed a binary code which can correct single burst-deletion, as long as the length of the burst is
odd
and at most
t

[
20
, Theorem 2]
. The redundancy of their code is
\log n+O\left(t^{2}\log\log n\right)
. Binary strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced sequences also play critical roles in constructions of other codes
[
29
,
26
]
.

Notice that when
q=2
, the
L_{1}
-weight is nothing but the Hamming weight. Therefore, the notion of strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced sequences can be naturally generalized to larger alphabets.

Definition III.1
.

Let
q,n>1
be integers. Let
\ell\leq n
and
0<\epsilon<(q-1)/2
. A sequence
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}
is called a
strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced
sequence, if any substring of
\mathbf{x}
of length
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
has
L_{1}
-weight in the interval
\left[\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\epsilon\right)\ell^{\prime},\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\epsilon\right)\ell^{\prime}\right]
.

The first conclusion in the following lemma generalizes
[
20
, Claim 4]
.

Lemma III.2
.

Suppose that
q,n\geq 2
and
s\geq 1
. If
\ell\geq\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\log_{\mathsf{e}}\left(2n\sqrt{s}\right)
, then there are at least
q^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{2s}\right)

q
-ary strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced sequences of length
n
. In particular, when
\ell\geq\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\log_{\mathsf{e}}\left(2(n+1)\sqrt{q}\right)
, there are at least
q^{n}/2
sequences
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}
with
\psi(\mathbf{x})
being strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced.

Proof:

Select uniformly and randomly a sequence
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}
. Then for any
1\leq m\leq n
, we have
\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{Sum}\left(\mathbf{x}_{[1,m]}\right))=\frac{q-1}{2}m
. By the Hoeffding’s inequality, we get

\mathsf{P}\left(\left|\mathsf{Sum}\left(\mathbf{x}_{\left[1,m\right]}\right)-\frac{q-1}{2}m\right|>\epsilon m\right)\leq 2\mathsf{e}^{-\frac{2\epsilon^{2}m}{(q-1)^{2}}}.

Therefore, it holds that

\mathsf{P}\left(\mathbf{x}\text{ is not strong-}(\ell,\epsilon)\text{-locally-balanced}\right)\leq 2n^{2}\mathsf{e}^{-\frac{2\epsilon^{2}\ell}{(q-1)^{2}}}.

This implies that the number of strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced sequences of length
n
is at least
q^{n}\left(1-2n^{2}\mathsf{e}^{-\frac{2\epsilon^{2}\ell}{(q-1)^{2}}}\right)
.
If
\ell\geq\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\log_{\mathsf{e}}\left(2n\sqrt{s}\right)
, we have
2n^{2}\mathsf{e}^{-\frac{2\epsilon^{2}\ell}{(q-1)^{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{2s}
. Therefore, the number of strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced sequences of length
n
is at least
q^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{2s}\right)
.

In particular, when
\ell\geq\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\log_{\mathsf{e}}\left(2(n+1)\sqrt{q}\right)
, there are at least
q^{n+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2q}\right)

(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced sequences of length

. On the other hand, there are exactly
q^{n}
sequences
\mathbf{y}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n+1}
with
\mathsf{Sum}\left(\mathbf{y}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{q}
. Therefore, by
III.1

q^{n}/2
sequences
\mathbf{x}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}
such that

is strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced.
∎

As previously analyzed, if
\mathbf{y}=\psi(\mathbf{x})
is strongly locally-balanced and

III-C

(t_{1},t_{2})
-burst-error correcting codes

Let

The study of this kind of error was initiated in
[

]

[

]

and

constructed a

-burst-error correcting code with redundancy

For each

be a

-burst-error correcting code.
By definition, the intersection

t
-localized-deletion. Since we just have to correct errors in short a substring, a

-burst-error correcting code is needed.

Definition III.2

(

-bounded code)

A code

-burst-error correcting code, where

Lemma III.3
.

[
19
, Corollary 3]

Let
q\geq 2
and
t_{1}\geq t_{2}\geq 2
be three fixed integers. For any

, there is a function

III-D

good triples

Our constructions in
Sections

and
V
are valid if and only if

. In this case, there always exists some

such that the triple

IV.1
and

. We call such triples good triples. The formal definition is given below.

Definition III.3
.

Let
q,t\geq 2
be integers and
0<\epsilon<\min\left\{\frac{q}{2t},\frac{1}{2}\right\}
. If for any
2\leq t^{\prime}\leq t
, there is an integer

such that

\frac{t^{\prime}}{2}-\frac{(1-2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}<s_{t^{\prime}}<\frac{t^{\prime}}{2}+1-\frac{(1+2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q},

(7)

\left(q,t,\epsilon\right)

.

Remark III.1
.

•

Suppse that
\left(q,t,\epsilon\right)
is a good triple. It is necessary that
\frac{t^{\prime}}{2}-\frac{(1-2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}<\frac{t^{\prime}}{2}+1-\frac{(1+2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}
, or equivalently,
\epsilon<\frac{q}{2t^{\prime}}
, for all
2\leq t^{\prime}\leq t
. In particular, we have
\epsilon<\frac{q}{2t}
. If
\epsilon\geq\frac{1}{2}
, it follows from (
7
) that
\frac{t^{\prime}}{2}<s_{t^{\prime}}<\frac{t^{\prime}}{2}+1
for all

. However, this is impossible when
t^{\prime}=2
. This discussion explains why it is required that
\epsilon<\min\left\{\frac{q}{2t},\frac{1}{2}\right\}
in the above definition.

•

Since
\frac{t^{\prime}}{2}+1-\frac{(1+2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}-\left(\frac{t^{\prime}}{2}-\frac{(1-2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}\right)<1-\frac{2\epsilon t^{\prime}}{q}<1
, if
(q,t,\epsilon)
is a good triple, the integer

is unique for each
t^{\prime}
.

In

III.4
, we determine the set of all good triples. The following observation will be helpful in deriving results in

III.4
.

Observation III.2
.

Let
q,t^{\prime}\geq 2
be integers and
0<\epsilon<\min\left\{\frac{q}{2t^{\prime}},\frac{1}{2}\right\}
. Let

I_{t^{\prime}}=\left(\frac{t^{\prime}}{2}-\frac{(1-2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q},\frac{t^{\prime}}{2}+1-\frac{(1+2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}\right)

(8)

be an open interval on the line of real numbers.

•

If
t^{\prime}
is even, the interval
I_{t^{\prime}}
contains an integer if and only if there is an integer
s

\frac{(1+2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}-1<s<\frac{(1-2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}
.

•

If
t^{\prime}
is odd, the interval
I_{t^{\prime}}
contains an integer if and only if there is an integer
s

\frac{(1+2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}-\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{(1-2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}+\frac{1}{2}

Proof:

Let
s_{t^{\prime}}\in I_{t^{\prime}}
be an integer. It must be that
s_{t^{\prime}}\leq\frac{t^{\prime}+1}{2}
. When

is even, suppose that

\frac{(1+2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}-1<s<\frac{(1-2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}
. When
t^{\prime}
is odd, suppose that
s_{t^{\prime}}=\frac{t^{\prime}+1}{2}-s
where
s\geq 0
is an integer. It is easy to verify that
\frac{t^{\prime}+1}{2}-s\in I_{t^{\prime}}
if and only if
\frac{(1+2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}-\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{(1-2\epsilon)t^{\prime}}{2q}+\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

Lemma III.4
.

Let
q,t\geq 2
be integers and
0<\epsilon<\min\left\{\frac{q}{2t},\frac{1}{2}\right\}
. Let

. Define

.

(i)

(ii)

VI
Encoding into Strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-Locally-Balanced Differential Sequences

In this section, for given
q\geq 2
and
0<\epsilon<(q-1)/2
, define

p_{1}(\epsilon)\triangleq\frac{q-1}{2}-\epsilon
and

p_{2}(\epsilon)\triangleq\frac{q-1}{2}+\epsilon
.
Recall that a sequence is strong
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced if any substring of length
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
has
L_{1}
-weight at least
p_{1}(\epsilon)\ell^{\prime}
and at most
p_{2}(\epsilon)\ell^{\prime}
. This strong local-balance property of differential sequences serves as a foundational component in constructions given in
Sections

IV
and
V
. This motivates our core objective in this section: developing an encoding scheme that maps an arbitrarily given sequence into another sequence whose differential sequence is strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{\rm{bal}}^{(q)}\left(n,\ell,\epsilon\right)\subseteq\Sigma_{q}^{n}
denote the set of all length-
n
strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced sequences. It follows from
Lemma

III.2
that as long as
\left((q-1)^{2}/\epsilon^{2}\right)\log_{\mathsf{e}}\left(2(n+1)\sqrt{q}\right)\leq\ell\leq n
, there are at least
q^{n}/2
sequences in
\Sigma_{q}^{n}
whose differential sequences are in
\mathcal{S}_{\rm{bal}}^{(q)}\left(n+1,\ell,\epsilon\right)
. This suggests a potential encoder with only
one
redundant symbol. However, we do not know how to design such an encoder. Instead, we design an encoding algorithm which converts a sequence to a sequence whose differential sequence is strong-
(\ell,\epsilon)
-locally-balanced, with
two
redundant symbols. Our two-stage encoding framework operates as follows:

Stage
1

For an input
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{q}^{n-2}
, encode its differential sequence
\psi\left(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\right)\in\Sigma_{q}^{n-1}
into an intermediate sequence
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}_{\rm{bal}}^{(q)}(n,\ell-1,\eta_{2})
. The constraint on parameter
\eta_{2}
will be specified at the end of
Section

VI-A
.

Stage
2

Recall that a
q
-ary sequence is a differential sequence of some sequence if and only if its
L_{1}
weight is congruent to
0
modulo
q
. We further encode
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}
into a sequence
\mathbf{y}
such that
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm{bal}}(n+1,\ell,\epsilon)
and
\mathsf{Sum}\left(\mathbf{y}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{q}
. Then output
\mathbf{x}=\psi^{-1}(\mathbf{y})\in\Sigma_{q}^{n}
.

Technical implementations of these stages are detailed in
Sections

and

.

VI-A

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\eta_{1}\right)(\ell^{\prime}-t)+\max\left\{t(q-1)-\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\ell_{1},0\right\}.

If
t(q-1)\leq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\ell_{1}
, then
t\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta_{1}}{q-1}\right)\ell_{1}
. In this case, it follows from (
31
) that

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sum}\left(\mathbf{u}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\eta_{1}\right)(\ell^{\prime}-t)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\eta_{1}\right)\left(\ell^{\prime}-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta_{1}}{q-1}\right)\ell_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\geq}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\eta_{1}\right)\left(\ell^{\prime}-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta_{1}}{q-1}\right)\frac{\ell^{\prime}}{s}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{q-1}{2}-\left(\eta_{1}-\frac{\eta_{1}^{2}}{(q-1)s}+\frac{q-1}{4s}\right)\right]\ell^{\prime}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\geq}p_{1}(\eta_{2})\ell^{\prime},

where (a) follows from the assumption that
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell\geq s\ell_{1}
, and (b) follows from the assumption that
\eta_{1}-\frac{\eta_{1}^{2}}{(q-1)s}+\frac{q-1}{4s}\leq\eta_{2}
.
If
t(q-1)\geq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\ell_{1}
,
t\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta_{1}}{q-1}\right)\ell_{1}
. In this case, it follows from (
31
) that

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sum}\left(\mathbf{u}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\eta_{1}\right)(\ell^{\prime}-t)+t(q-1)-\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\ell_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\eta_{1}\right)\ell^{\prime}+\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\left(t-\ell_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\eta_{1}\right)\ell^{\prime}+\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta_{1}}{q-1}\right)\ell_{1}-\ell_{1}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\eta_{1}\right)\ell^{\prime}-\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\eta_{1}}{q-1}\right)\ell_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\eta_{1}\right)\left[\ell^{\prime}-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta_{1}}{q-1}\right)\ell_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\geq p_{1}(\eta_{2})\ell^{\prime}.

Up to now, we have proved the lower bound
\mathsf{Sum}\left(\mathbf{u}\right)\geq p_{1}(\eta_{2})\ell^{\prime}
. It remains to prove the upper bound
\mathsf{Sum}\left(\mathbf{u}\right)\leq p_{2}(\eta_{2})\ell^{\prime}
. Firstly, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sum}\left(\mathbf{u}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{Sum}\left(\mathbf{u}^{(i)}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle\leq(k-1)\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\ell_{1}+\min\left\{t(q-1),\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\ell_{1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)(\ell^{\prime}-t)+\min\left\{t(q-1),\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\ell_{1}\right\}.

If
t(q-1)\geq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\ell_{1}
, it follows from (
32
) that

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sum}\left(\mathbf{u}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\left[\ell^{\prime}-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta_{1}}{q-1}\right)\ell_{1}\right]+\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\ell_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\left[\ell^{\prime}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\eta_{1}}{q-1}\right)\ell_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\left[\ell^{\prime}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\eta_{1}}{q-1}\right)\frac{\ell^{\prime}}{s}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}-\frac{\eta_{1}^{2}}{(q-1)s}+\frac{q-1}{4s}\right)\ell^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq p_{2}(\eta_{2})\ell^{\prime}.

If
t(q-1)\leq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\ell_{1}
, it follows from (
32
) that

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sum}\left(\mathbf{u}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\left(\ell^{\prime}-t\right)+t(q-1)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\ell^{\prime}+\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\eta_{1}\right)t

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\ell^{\prime}+\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\eta_{1}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta_{1}}{q-1}\right)\ell_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\eta_{1}\right)\left[\ell^{\prime}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\eta_{1}}{q-1}\right)\ell_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq p_{2}(\eta_{2})\ell^{\prime}.

Now the proof is completed.
∎

Lemma VI.2
.

(33)

VII
Conclusion

This paper presents novel constructions of
(\leq t)
-burst-deletion-correcting and
t
-localized-deletion-correcting codes through a new position-estimation methodology. In contrast to prior works, our position-estimation approach offers enhanced simplicity. We believe this framework may find broader applications in code construction.

Current state-of-the-art constructions for these codes rely primarily on a two-phase approach: first roughly locating errors and then correcting errors locally. This inherently yields an
O(\log\log n)
redundancy term. It remains open whether the
\log n+\Omega(1)
lower bound on redundancy is asymptotically tight. Resolving this fundamental question may require novel insights.

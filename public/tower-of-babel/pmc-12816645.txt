An improved sparrow search algorithm and its application in the deployment of 3D wireless sensor nodes
Authors: Shan Li, Li Ping Mo, Biao Yin, Wei Min
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis manuscript proposes an Improved Sparrow Search Algorithm with Multi-Strategies (MSISSA) to address the shortcomings of the Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA), and applies it to optimize the deployment of 3D Wireless Sensor Nodes (3D-WSNs). The improvements of MSISSA encompass three main components: firstly, it utilizes an adaptive weight operator for updating position to enhance local search accuracy and expedite convergence; secondly, it introduces the spiral flight mechanism of the Moth Flame Optimization (MFO) algorithm, which balances the algorithm’s local and global search abilities; finally, it uses the Levy flight mechanism to jump out the local optima. The experimental results demonstrate that MSISSA outperforms SSA, its four variants, and two classical intelligent algorithms. The simulation results indicate that when the number of nodes is 30 and 50, the optimal coverage rate of MSISSA reaches 91.89% and 99.11%, respectively, surpassing those achieved by SSA and its four variants, as well as two classical intelligent algorithms. Therefore, it can be concluded that MSISSA is well-suited for optimizing the deployment of 3D-WSNs.Keywords: Sparrow search algorithm, Moth-flame optimization algorithm, Levy flight, Three-dimensional wireless sensor nodesSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Metaheuristic algorithms, which are a class of algorithms designed to tackle complex optimization problems, progressively approximate the optimal solution by searching within the problem space without requiring detailed assumptions about the problem’s underlying model. In recent years, metaheuristic algorithms represented by Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) Algorithm1, Genetic Algorithm (GA)2, Cuckoo Search Algorithm (CS)3, Moth-Flame Optimization (MFO) Algorithm4, Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (GOA)5, Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) Algorithm6, and Egret Swarm Optimization Algorithm (ESOA)7, have been continuously improved and widely applied to solve complex engineering problems, such as path planning8–10 and network optimizing11–13. Wu et al.14 proposed a multi-attribute label-based ant colony system (LACS) algorithm to address vehicle heterogeneity and mixed backhaul loads. Liu et al.15 presented a new three-stage hybrid feature gene selection method. Huang et al.16 utilized a nonlinear activated beetle antennae search algorithm for fraud detection of public trading companies. Ye et al.17 explored a multi-objective Cuckoo Search Algorithm to optimize coordinated formation control for multiple drones, ensuring safe flight paths while aligning airspeed, yaw angle, altitude, and altitude rate within specified parameters. Ou et al.18 introduced an enhanced multi-strategy GWO algorithm for wireless sensor networks. Kang et al.19 proposed a harmony search algorithm with segmented search capabilities. Khan et al.20 implemented a human-guided cooperative robot agent in smart homes based on the beetle antennae search. Shang et al.21 focused on multi-objective optimization for magnetic resistance magnetic gears using ESOA. S Sharma et al.22 solve multiple engineering design problems and two multi-gravity-assist spacecraft trajectory problems using an improved butterfly optimization algorithm. Sahoo et al.23 segmentation of COVID-19 CT images using improved moth-flame optimization.

The Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA)24, proposed in 2020, is inspired by the foraging and anti-predation behaviors of sparrows. As a novel metaheuristic algorithm, SSA is renowned for its ability to achieve higher optimization accuracy than classical algorithms, and can be applied to solve a variety of engineering problems. However, SSA still faces challenges in solving high-dimensional problems, including a tendency to fall into local optima, slow convergence speed, and poor global search capability. Researchers have proposed some variants of SSA to address these shortcomings. Li et al.25 introduced an adaptive T-distribution to enhance local search (T_SSA). Fu et al.26 developed a multi-strategy SSA that combines chaotic reverse learning and Cauchy-Gaussian mutation to balance search capabilities (LSSA). Huang et al.27 utilized t-distribution and Tent chaotic mapping to increase the diversity of the initial population (T_TentSSA). Zhang et al.28 combined circular chaotic mapping with the butterfly optimization algorithm and dimension mutation to enhance global exploration (CASSA). The aforementioned variants demonstrate higher performance compared to the standard SSA, but still have various limitations in solving high-dimensional problems, such as slow convergence accuracy and convergence speed, poor global search capability, and susceptibility to local optima.

Aim to solve high-dimensional problems, this manuscript presents an improved Sparrow Search Algorithm with multi-strategy (MSISSA) from enhancing the convergence speed, balancing the ability of local and global search, and escaping the local optima. MSISSA integrates three strategies: adaptive weight operator, the MFO algorithm, and the Levy flight mechanism. To verify performance, ablation experiments are conducted to evaluate the individual contributions of MSISSA components. Subsequently, MSISSA is benchmarked against other seven algorithms: SSA24, T_SSA25, LSSA26, T_TentSSA27, CASSA28, PSO1, and MFO4 on ten benchmark functions. The results indicate that MSISSA outperforms these seven algorithms. Ultimately, MSISSA is implemented to optimize the deployment of Three-Dimensional Wireless Sensor Nodes (3D-WSNs). Simulation experiments with 30 and 50 nodes demonstrate MSISSA’s superior stability compared to its competitors.

The remainder of this manuscript is organized as follows: Section "SSA" introduces SSA; Section "Proposed MSISSA" explains the improvements and underlying rationale of the MSISSA; Section "Experiments and analyses" presents the analytical experiments; Section "Application in the deployment of 3D-WSNs" discusses the application of MSISSA in the deployment of 3D-WSNs; and Section "Conclusions" provides the conclusion.

SSA simulates the foraging and anti-predation behaviors of sparrows. Based on differences in individual foraging behaviors, the sparrow population is divided into discoverers, followers, and sentinels. Experimental environment

The experiments were conducted on desktop computer with Gen Intel(R) Core (TM) i5-12400F @2.50 GHz, 16 GB RAM and Window10 operating system, using Python 3.8 as the programming language.

The benchmark functions are usually used to verify performance of algorithm. According to the No Free Lunch Theorem34, no single algorithm performs optimally across all tasks, necessitating specific analysis for each problem. An algorithm may excel in certain constrained problems but underperform in others. Consequently, a judicious selection of benchmark functions is essential for performance assessment. F8–F10 are fixed 2-dimension multimodal functions, chosen to further assess the algorithm’s convergence speed, stability, and accuracy.

Conclusions

To address the shortcomings of the standard SSA in solving high-dimensional problems, such as slow convergence accuracy and convergence speed, poor global search capability, and susceptibility to local optima, this manuscript proposes an improved algorithm named MSISSA, and then applied MSISSA to optimize the deployment of 3D-WSNs. MSISSA integrates three strategies: an adaptive weight operator, the spiral flight mechanism of MFO, and the Levy flight mechanism. First, MSISSA enhances the convergence speed by introducing an adaptive weight operator ω with exponential decay to the position update formula for the discoverers. Second, MSISSA balances the algorithm’s local and global search abilities by combing the spiral flight mechanism of the MFO into the position update formula for the followers. Third, MSISSA escapes local optima by using the Levy flight mechanism with position updates for the sentinels. The ablation experiments, performance comparison experiments, and validation on optimization of classical engineering problems have verified the superiority of MSISSA, and the simulation experiments have confirmed its applicability and stability.

Future research aims to further improve the applicability and robustness of MSISSA across diverse environments and under various constraints. Furthermore, plans include utilizing MSISSA to optimize deep learning models in subsequent work.

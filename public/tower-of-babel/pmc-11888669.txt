The Management of OSMF using the Buccal Fat Pad with Collagen Membrane in Fibrotomy Defects
Authors: Vertika Dubey, Pooja Khokhar, Anjali Savita, Shaji Thomas, Indrajeet Raghuwanshi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Owing to limitations of various flap procedures for the management of oral submucous fibrosis (OSMF), we tried to evaluate the outcome of the buccal fat pad (BFP) with collagen membrane as an alternative.Materials and methods:We performed the aforementioned technique on eight patients diagnosed with OSF who underwent management using the BFP with a collagen membrane.Results:We observed a significant mean gain in mouth opening of 5.29 ± 2.11 mm and a significant reduction in pain levels by 2.62 ± 1.05. No complication and recurrence were observed till fourmonth followup.Conclusion:It can be concluded that the BFP with a collagen membrane could be a viable alternative to other surgical procedures. However, our data should extrapolate with larger studies with longer followup.

INTRODUCTION

Various treatment modalities have been explored to alleviate the symptoms and improve the oral function in OSF patients. Surgical interventions have gained considerable attention due to their potential for achieving favorable outcomes. Reconstruction can be done with the buccal fat pad (BFP) flap, skin grafts, tongue flap, greater palatine pedicled flap, placental graft, artificial dermis and anterolateral thigh flap, temporalis fascia flap, radial forearm flap, and nasolabial flap.[1] One such surgical technique involves the utilization of the buccal fat pad (BFP) along with a collagen membrane for the management of OSF. The BFP is a well-vascularized tissue located in the buccal region, composed of adipose tissue and fibrous septa. It offers several advantages, including its abundance, ease of harvesting, and excellent blood supply.[2]

Collagen membranes, on the other hand, are biocompatible and possess barrier properties that facilitate guided tissue regeneration.[3] When combined with the BFP, they serve as a protective layer and aid in the reconstitution of the submucosal tissues, preventing further fibrosis and promoting functional rehabilitation.[4]

The purpose of this study was to explore the management of OSF patients using the BFP along with a collagen membrane.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

This study was done on eight patients (five males and three females) between the age ranges of 32–53 years diagnosed with oral submucous fibrosis (OSF) (Khanna and Andrade group IVa) who underwent management using the buccal fat pad (BFP) with a collagen membrane. Patients included in this study met the following criteria: (1) clinically diagnosed with OSF based on characteristic clinical features, such as restricted mouth opening, presence of fibrous bands, and mucosal rigidity; (2) no history of previous surgical intervention for OSF; (3) willing to participate and follow the prescribed treatment protocol; (4) absence of any contraindications for surgical intervention; (5) availability of complete medical and follow-up records.

The standard surgical procedure was performed under general anesthesia and 2% lignocaine with 1:80,000 adrenaline was locally infiltrated along the planned incision line. The buccal fat pad was harvested following an intraoral mucosal incision in the buccal region. Care was taken to preserve the blood supply while harvesting an adequate amount of fat pad tissue. A biocompatible collagen membrane was obtained and trimmed to the desired size and shape, suitable for covering the defect created after the release of fibrous bands.

Fibrous bands were identified and carefully released using sharp dissection or laser. The harvested buccal fat pad was placed in the defect created after fibrous band release, and the collagen membrane was positioned to cover the fat pad. Sutures or tissue adhesives were used to secure the membrane in place [Figure 1].

Intraoperative view showing buccal fat pad (left) and its placement over the defect with collagen membrane(right)

Postoperatively all the patients received intravenous antibiotics and nasogastric feeding for one week. Daily input of 2–2.5 l was maintained. Mouth-opening exercises were started from the third postoperative day by Heister’s jaw opener (three times a day for 15 min each time) for the initial three months and with reduced frequency for as long as one year. Maximum interincisal opening (MIO) was increased gradually till approximate intraoperative mouth-opening values were achieved.

The patients were analyzed for intraoperative time, postoperative interincisal mouth opening (in mm), infection, pain (analyzed by VAS on a scale of 0–10), wound dehiscence, and relapse (at different time intervals which lasted for 1 year) using SPSS 27.0 version.

RESULTS

Mouth-opening improvement

The mean gain in mouth opening was 5.29 ± 2.11 mm, which was statistically significant (P < 0.001) Figure 2.

Pre operative (left) and post operative mouth opening (right)

Pain levels: The mean reduction in pain (VAS) scores was of 2.62 ± 1.05, which was statistically significant (P < 0.001).

Postoperative complications: Nil.

Epithelialization: Two to three weeks.

Patient satisfaction: Seven out of eight patients were highly satisfied while one was moderately satisfied.

Recurrence: Nil (four-month follow-up).

DISCUSSION

The BFP represents an easy-access source for dentists and oral surgeons. It contains a huge amount of stem cells that has the capability to differentiate into the chondrogenic, adipogenic, and osteogenic lineages. The BFP, being a local pedicled graft, offers several advantages over other flaps in the surgical management of OSMF (it is quick, simple, equally effective, technically easier flap to use, it can be harvested through the same incision and thus has low morbidity with excellent functional outcomes).[34]

Sterile collagen membrane of Bovine origin can be used as a cover over harvested BFP. It is easily available as commercial product. The adherence of collagen membrane is initially due to fibrin–collagen interaction and later due to fibrovascular ingrowth into the collagen. With time, it slowly undergoes collagenolysis and is eventually sloughed off. It resists masticatory forces for sufficient time, to allow granulation tissue to form.[34] It could be attributed to the fact that collagen membrane creates physiological interface between wound and external environment which is impermeable to bacteria, thus helping in preventing infection of surgical site. It also provides a smooth surface over BFP that prevents food lodgment at surgical site. The advantages of collagen sheet as a wound dressing material in OSMF surgery include the easy availability of collagen sheet, convenience of application, good tolerance of oral tissue, possibility of second surgery to obtain graft and there are no problems associated with donor site healing.[34]

Collagen can be used as an alternative graft material to cover the intraoral surgical defects owing to its ease of availability, convenience of application, good oral tolerance, possibility of a second surgery to obtain graft and there are no problems associated with donor site healing. Unique features of collagen membranes include guiding function, chemotactic property, and hemostatic property. It resists masticatory forces for sufficient time, to allow granulation tissue to form. Collagen membranes encourage wound healing through early deposition and organization of granulation tissue. It facilitates migration of fibroblasts into the wound bed and encourages the metabolic activity of granulation tissue.[5]

CONCLUSION

The findings from our preliminary study suggest the use of the BFP with a collagen membrane as an alternative to other surgical procedures. However, our data should extrapolate with larger comparative studies with longer follow-up.

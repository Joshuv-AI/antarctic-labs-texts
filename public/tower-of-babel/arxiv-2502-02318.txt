Courant Algebroid Relations, T-Dualities and Generalised Ricci Flow
Thomas C. De Fraja, Vincenzo Emilio Marotta, Richard J. Szabo

Abstract
. The notion of Courant algebroid relation is used to introduce a definition of relation between divergence operators on Courant algebroids. By introducing invariant divergence operators, a notion of generalised T-duality between divergences is presented through an existence and uniqueness result for related divergence operators on T-dual pairs of exact Courant algebroids, which naturally incorporates the dilaton shift. When combined with the notion of generalised isometry, this establishes circumstances under which generalised Ricci tensors are related, proving that T-duality is compatible with generalised string background equations. This enables an analysis of the compatibility between T-duality and generalised Ricci flow, showing that the T-dual of a solution of generalised Ricci flow is also a solution of generalised Ricci flow. Our constructions are illustrated through many explicit examples.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we will demonstrate the versatility of the notion of Courant algebroid relation by providing a framework for relating geometric objects such as divergence operators and generalised Ricci tensors on Courant algebroids. This is motivated by the compatibility between generalised Ricci flow, string background equations and the different flavours of T-duality. We will explore and deepen this link with a relational approach, which adds another building block to the construction of a “category” of Courant algebroids.

Let us start by giving a brief overview of the interplay between the three main constituents of the present paper: Courant algebroid relations, T-dualities and generalised Ricci flow.

1.1.
Courant Algebroids and their Relations

Courant algebroids
[
1
]
may be regarded as smooth counterparts of quadratic Lie algebras. The main examples are provided by generalised tangent bundles
\mathbb{T}M=TM\oplus T^{*}M
of smooth manifolds
M
, known as exact Courant algebroids, which are the central objects of study in
generalised geometry
. Exact Courant algebroids were first discussed in
[
2
]
as a way to give a geometric description of the Dirac bracket appearing in constrained dynamical systems. The counterparts in generalised geometry of geometric structures such as metrics, complex structures and Kähler structures were discussed in detail in
[
3
,
4
]
.

A Courant algebroid is the vector bundle counterpart of a Lie algebra endowed with an adjoint-invariant pairing. More precisely, a Courant algebroid is a vector bundle
E
over a smooth manifold
M
endowed with a symmetric bilinear non-degenerate pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E}
, a bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{E}
on the space of sections of
E
satisfying the Jacobi identity, and a vector bundle map
\rho_{E}\colon E\to TM
called the anchor. These data are required to satisfy a compatibility condition that can be regarded as invariance of the pairing with respect to the bracket. As a byproduct of this, the bracket does not need to be skew-symmetric and its symmetric part is governed by the pairing. Courant algebroids with skew-symmetric brackets have trivial anchor map
\rho_{E}
.

Courant algebroid relations come into play when extending the category of Courant algebroids along the same lines as Weinstein’s symplectic “category”, in light of the interpretation of Courant algebroids as differential-graded symplectic manifolds. A Courant algebroid relation
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
is defined as an involutive (maximally) isotropic subbundle
R
of the direct product of two Courant algebroids
E_{1}
and
\overline{E}_{2}
, where
\overline{E}_{2}
denotes the Courant algebroid with reflected pairing with respect to the Courant algebroid
E_{2}
. Because Courant algebroids are in one-to-one correspondence with degree two graded manifolds endowed with a degree
-2
symplectic structure and a degree three integrable function
[
8
]
, Courant algebroid relations should be linked with genuine differential-graded Lagrangian correspondences in graded geometry.

One of the main goals of generalised geometry is to define notions of differential geometry in this extended framework. For instance, one might wish to find the counterpart of Riemannian geometry in generalised geometry. The counterpart of Riemannian metrics is given by generalised metrics on exact Courant algebroids, i.e. maximally positive-definite subbundles (with respect to the pairing). Other structures associated with a metric can then be defined. For instance, connections on Courant algebroids have been extensively studied, see e.g.
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
, and have even been explored in the context of Courant algebroid relations
[
7
]
. Their caveat is the non-uniqueness of a generalised Levi-Civita connection which, in order to define further geometric structures such as Ricci tensors and scalar curvature, necessitates the introduction of divergence operators
[
12
]
.

In the present paper, we develop a relational approach to divergences as the basis for defining further generalised structures. This has the added benefit of bypassing the need to define Courant algebroid connections for transverse generalised metrics, a type of degenerate generalised metric used extensively in the construction of relations in
[
13
]
.

1.2.
Generalised T-Duality

Courant algebroids
E\to M
with generalised metrics and divergences further provide a useful geometric tool in string theory: their defining data is equivalent to that of two-dimensional non-linear sigma-models with target space
M
, as discussed in
[
14
,
15
]
, where a divergence operator naturally models the dilaton field
[
12
]
. In this way fully geometric descriptions of T-dualities, which relate apparently distinct backgrounds that are equivalent from the sigma-model perspective, can be achieved with far reaching consequences; particularly notable special cases are given by abelian and Poisson-Lie T-dualities. The latest developments in this area, which include string backgrounds that are susceptible to more general notions of T-duality (see e.g.
[
16
,
17
]
), necessitate the introduction of Courant algebroid relations
[
13
]
.

In this rich framework, T-duality, which admits a precise description connecting Courant algebroid structures
[
18
,
15
]
, can find its natural interpretation, as expounded in
[
7
,
13
]
. In particular, a Courant algebroid relation preserves the Courant algebroid structures without requiring that the base manifolds be diffeomorphic, as is often the case in T-duality, in contrast to Courant algebroid isomorphisms. In this context, the role of generalised isometries, i.e. Courant algebroid relations linking a generalised metric to another one, is prominent in the characterisation of the geometric properties of T-duality
[
18
,
13
,
19
]
.

Let us discuss Poisson-Lie T-duality in more detail, which originates as a non-isometric generalisation of non-abelian T-duality in the non-linear sigma-model
[
20
]
.
Let
E\to M
be a Courant algebroid endowed with a generalised metric
V^{+}\subset E
, and set
V^{-}=(V^{+})^{\perp}
. Let
{\rm div}
be a divergence operator on
E
, i.e. a map from
\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
to
C^{\infty}(M)
satisfying an anchored Leibniz-like rule. Let
\varphi\colon M^{\prime}\to M
be a smooth map.

Then the Courant algebroid pullback
E^{\prime}\coloneqq\varphi^{*}E
of the Courant algebroid

is the Courant algebroid
E^{\prime}\to M^{\prime}
with the compatible structure maps

(1.1)

\displaystyle\langle\varphi^{*}e_{1},\varphi^{*}e_{2}\rangle_{E^{\prime}}=\varphi^{*}\langle e_{1},

\displaystyle\,e_{2}\rangle_{E}\quad,\quad\llbracket\varphi^{*}e_{1},\varphi^{*}e_{2}\rrbracket_{E^{\prime}}=\varphi^{*}\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket_{E}\ ,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\varphi_{*}\big(\rho_{E^{\prime}}(\varphi^{*}e)\big)=\rho_{E}(e)\ ,

for all
e,e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
. As discussed in
[
5
]
, an anchor map
\rho_{E^{\prime}}:E^{\prime}\to TM^{\prime}
satisfying Property (
1.2
) exists if and only if

(1.3)

\displaystyle[\rho_{E^{\prime}}(\varphi^{*}e_{1}),\rho_{E^{\prime}}(\varphi^{*}e_{2})]_{TM^{\prime}}=\rho_{E^{\prime}}(\varphi^{*}\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket_{E})\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
, and
\ker(\rho_{E^{\prime}})_{m^{\prime}}
is a coisotropic subspace of

, for any

.

In order to define equivariant Poisson-Lie T-duality, also known as Poisson-Lie T-duality of dressing cosets in the gauged non-linear sigma-model
[
21
]
, assume that the Courant algebroid
E\to M
admits an action
\psi\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
induced by the action of a Lie group
{\mathsf{G}}
with Lie algebra

. Further assume that this action pulls back by

to a
\mathfrak{g}
-action

turning

into a
{\mathsf{G}}
-equivariant Courant algebroid.
Then
{\mathsf{G}}
acts freely and properly on
M^{\prime}
via the map
\rho_{E^{\prime}}\circ\psi^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM^{\prime})
.

We denote by
\underline{E}^{\prime}
the reduced Courant algebroid over the quotient manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}=M^{\prime}/{\mathsf{G}}
. Suppose that the pullback generalised metric
V^{\prime}\coloneqq\varphi^{*}V^{+}
and divergence
{\rm div}^{\prime}\coloneqq\varphi^{*}{\rm div}
are reducible by the
\mathfrak{g}
-action
\psi^{\prime}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathsf{\Gamma}(E^{\prime})
, with reduced generalised metric
\underline{V}^{\prime}
and divergence
\underline{{\rm div}}^{\prime}
on
\underline{E}^{\prime}
. Then Poisson-Lie T-duality is the statement that the physically relevant properties of
(\underline{V}^{\prime},\underline{{\rm div}}^{\prime})
, such as the non-linear sigma-model and the generalised string background equations, can be completely formulated in terms of
(V^{+},{\rm div},\psi)
, without any reference to
{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
and
\underline{E}^{\prime}

[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
.

A formulation of Poisson-Lie T-duality in terms of Courant algebroid relations, in a slightly different setting to the one presented above, can be found in the work of Vysoký
[
7
]
. We will come back to Vysoký’s formulation in the main body of the paper.

1.3.
Generalised Ricci Flow and String Backgrounds

Geometric flows offer a natural way to explore topological and geometrical properties of manifolds by evolving a given initial manifold under the flow to canonical forms in the space of geometric data. The classic example is Hamilton’s Ricci flow
[
24
]
, which is the analogue of the heat equation for a Riemannian metric and so is expected to improve the geometry of the underlying manifold; in this case canonical metrics include Einstein metrics, and more generally Ricci solitons. Geometric flows are also useful tools for constructing solutions of supergravity equations.

In string non-linear sigma-models, T-dualities are expected to be compatible in a non-trivial way with the one-loop renormalisation group flow, which corresponds geometrically to the generalised Ricci flow
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
12
,
23
,
28
]
of the target space metric, Kalb-Ramond field and dilaton. The flow equations are precisely the (generalised)
{\mathcal{N}}=0
supergravity equations of motion, also known as (generalised) string background equations, whose solutions are the physically viable (generalised) string backgrounds
[
29
,
30
]
.
Equivariant Poisson-Lie T-duality, as discussed in
[
15
,
27
,
23
]
, represents a particularly insightful instance in which T-dual backgrounds yield T-dual solutions of the generalised Ricci flow and string background equations.
1
1

1

Generalised T-dualities, such as Poisson-Lie T-duality, also preserve two-loop renormalisation group flows, though in a much more subtle and non-trivial way than at one-loop
[
31
]
; in particular, the string background equations are not sufficient for study of the flows away from fixed points.

In this paper we are particularly interested in the interplay between Courant algebroid relations and generalised Ricci flow.
We aim to study this in the framework of generalised T-duality developed in
[
13
]
, with an eye to deepening the understanding of the connection with T-duality developed in
[
23
]
.

Let us briefly discuss the basics of generalised Ricci flow following
[
23
,
10
,
11
]
.
The generalised Ricci tensor
\mathrm{GRic}_{V^{+},{\rm div}}
on a Courant algebroid
E\to M
for the pair

is given in
[

]
as the
C^{\infty}(M)
-bilinear map

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GRic}_{V^{+},{\rm div}}\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}(V^{+})\times\mathsf{\Gamma}(V^{-})\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(M)

defined by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GRic}_{V^{+},{\rm div}}(v^{+},w^{-})\coloneqq{\rm div}\,\llbracket v^{+},w^{-}\rrbracket_{E}^{+}-\pounds_{\rho_{E}(w^{-})}\,{\rm div}\,v^{+}-\>{\rm Tr}\,_{V^{+}}(\llbracket\llbracket\,\cdot\,,w^{-}\rrbracket_{E}^{-}\,,\,v^{+}\rrbracket_{E}^{+})\ ,

for all
v^{+}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(V^{+})
and
w^{-}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(V^{-}),
where
\pounds
denotes the Lie derivative.

As already mentioned, one can also define generalised Ricci tensors via Courant algebroid connections. In fact, there are many definitions for the generalised Ricci tensor available in the literature, see e.g.
[
9

12
,
32
,
11

, however it has been recently shown in
[

that all of them are equivalent. In this paper we will focus on the expression given in Equation (

In
[
23

it was shown that the generalised Ricci tensor
\mathrm{GRic}_{V^{+},{\rm div}}
on an exact Courant algebroid
E
with Ševera class
[H]
coincides with the Ricci tensor
{\rm Ric}_{g,H}
of the Koszul connection
\nabla^{g,H}
on
TM
preserving the Riemannian metric

defining

with skew-symmetric torsion

Following
[
10
,
11
]
the full Ricci tensor of the pair
(V^{+},{\rm div})
is given by

(1.6)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{GRic}}_{V^{+},{\rm div}}\coloneqq\mathrm{GRic}_{V^{+},{\rm div}}-\mathrm{GRic}_{V^{-},{\rm div}}\ .

It can be used to define the generalised scalar curvature as

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GR}_{V^{+},{\rm div}}\coloneqq\>{\rm Tr}\,\big(\tau\,\overline{\mathrm{GRic}}_{V^{+},{\rm div}}\big)\ ,

where
\tau\in{\sf Aut}(E)
is the involutive automorphism of

that equivalently defines the generalised metric
V^{+}
; in the following we will use

V^{+}
interchangeably.
Both of these are central objects in the definition of the generalised Ricci flow equations.

Let us now assume that
M
is a compact manifold. Denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{E}
the space of generalised metrics on
E
, and by
{\mathcal{H}}^{\times}
the space of nowhere-vanishing half-densities on

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}(\tau,\varkappa)=\int_{M}\,\mathrm{GR}_{\tau,{\rm div}_{\varkappa^{2}}}\,\varkappa^{2}\ ,

The

Explicitly, the generalised Ricci flow is given by

(1.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tau=-2\,\mathrm{GRic}_{\tau,{\rm div}_{\varkappa^{2}}}\quad,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varkappa=-{\rm GR}_{\tau,{\rm div}_{\varkappa^{2}}}\,\varkappa\ .

In
[

(1.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tau=-2\,\mathrm{GRic}_{\tau,{\rm div}}\quad,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\rm div}=-{\mathcal{D}}\,{\rm GR}_{\tau,{\rm div}}\ ,

where

and the pairing of the Courant algebroid

In this paper, we will be interested in this latter formulation of the generalised Ricci flow.

The short-time existence and uniqueness of solutions of generalised Ricci flow for compact manifolds has been proven recently in
[

]

The deep interplay between generalised Ricci flow and T-duality, in its different guises, has been discussed in

For instance, Poisson-Lie T-duality for the generalised Ricci tensor

is the statement that the generalised Ricci tensor of the pullback structures
(V^{\prime},{\rm div}^{\prime})
to the Courant algebroid pullback

is the pullback of

see

\mathrm{GRic}_{V^{+},{\rm div}}
and

at time

(V^{+},{\rm div})
at the same time

These features allow for a more in-depth discussion of the meaning of compatibility between Poisson-Lie T-duality and generalised Ricci flow.
In
[
23
]
a generalised metric

on a

-equivariant Courant algebroid

is called

is

-invariant and

. The space of admissible generalised metrics on

{\mathcal{N}}_{E}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{E}
. Then as in

, viewed as a vector field on

, is tangent to

.
Denote by
\varsigma\colon{\mathcal{N}}_{E}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\underline{E}^{\prime}}
the map that assigns to

\underline{V}^{\prime}\subset\underline{E}^{\prime}
obtained after pulling

back by

This property is key to showing the following result. Let

be a generalised metric admitting pullback

which is admissible, together with a divergence operator

whose pullback

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GRic}_{V^{+},{\rm div}}=0\quad,\quad{\rm GR}_{V^{+},{\rm div}}=0\ ,

This motivates studying the compatibility of geometric T-duality, as formulated in

, with the generalised Ricci flow, whereby Courant algebroid relations play a key role and can lead to surprising new insights into generalised Ricci flow.
In particular, the translation of the above formulation of Poisson-Lie T-duality to T-duality for torus bundles should imply that the physically relevant properties of the T-dual backgrounds are determined by the geometric structures on the transitive Courant algebroids defined on the base manifold of the correspondence space. We shall elucidate this expectation in detail within the present paper.

1.4.
Outline and Summary of Results

A brief outline of this paper is as follows: in Section
2
the basics of Courant algebroids and their relations is discussed, along with their application to give a very general definition of T-duality, which encompasses the known notions including non-isometric and non-abelian T-dualities. In Section
3
the notion of Courant algebroid relation between divergence operators is introduced, and the properties of related divergences are discussed together with an application to T-duality. Section
4
deals with the interplay between Courant algebroid relations and generalised Ricci flow. Generalised isometries are shown to play a crucial role in proving that generalised Ricci tensors are related. Moreover, it is proven that the T-dual structure of a solution of generalised Ricci flow is also a solution of generalised Ricci flow. Section
5
is dedicated to the construction of explicit examples of T-dual pairs of divergences and their compatibility with generalised Ricci flow.

In more detail,
Section
2
provides the definition of Courant algebroid, together with the basic properties of exact Courant algebroids and their classification. We describe the reduction of a Courant algebroid
E
over
M
by an isotropic subbundle
K
to a Courant algebroid
\underline{E}
over the leaf space
{\mathcal{Q}}
of the foliation integrating the distribution
\rho_{E}(K)
, emphasising the notion of basic section of a Courant algebroid. Generalised metrics on Courant algebroids are further introduced. Finally, Courant algebroid relations are defined, as are their role in defining isometries for generalised metrics.

These notions constitute the building blocks for geometric T-duality as discussed in
[
13
]
and summarised in this section. Given a pair of isotropic subbundles
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
of a Courant algebroid
E\to M
with equal rank, topological T-duality is defined as a particular Courant algebroid relation
R:\underline{E}{}_{1}\dashrightarrow\underline{E}{}_{2}
between the reduced Courant algebroids
\underline{E}{}_{1}\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
and
\underline{E}{}_{2}\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}
over the corresponding leaf spaces. Starting from a generalised metric
\tau_{1}
on
\underline{E}{}_{1}
which is invariant with respect to the bundle of isometries
D_{1}\subset T{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
, geometric T-duality is defined by constructing the unique generalised metric
\tau_{2}
on
\underline{E}{}_{2}
that makes
R
a generalised isometry.
A new local formulation of T-duality is also presented, extending the previous notion, that aids in explicit calculations and examples.

In
Section
3
we give a definition of Courant algebroid relation between divergence operators.

Definition 1.12

(
Definition
3.14
)
.

Let
{\rm div}_{1}
and
{\rm div}_{2}
be divergence operators on Courant algebroids

and

respectively. Let

be a Courant algebroid relation supported on

for every

Many examples are provided, among others reductions of divergences and Poisson-Lie T-duality. We also consider the interplay between the notion of related Courant algebroid connections and related divergences, establishing conditions under which a relation between connections induces a relation between the associated divergences.

The key result of this section is an existence and uniqueness result for T-dual pairs of divergence operators. In order to prove the existence of the T-dual divergence, the notions of invariant sections of a Courant algebroid (with respect to a given distribution) and divergence operators are given.

(

3.76
)

The dilaton shift is moreover naturally explained in terms of related divergence operators. We shall discuss the compatibility with the twisted differentials in an upcoming work

Section

Theorem 1.16

(

If a (locally)

The classic examples are provided by Poisson-Lie T-duality and T-duality for torus bundles.
We further derive Buscher-like rules for the Ricci tensors in T-duality, which are useful for explicit calculational purposes. Explicitly, the Ricci tensor of the T-dual metric is determined by the Christoffel symbols and Ricci tensor of the original metric, as well as representatives of the Ševera classes of the T-dual pair of exact Courant algebroids.

Proposition

Finally, in

Courant Algebroids: Reduction, Relations and T-Duality

In this section we provide relevant background on Courant algebroids, reduction of Courant algebroids, and Courant algebroid relations. When combined with a notion of isometries of generalised metrics on Courant algebroids, we review the definition and main features of our general notion of T-duality introduced in
[
13
]
, and generalise it to a new local version which is useful in explicit examples and calculations.

2.1.
Courant Algebroids

We provide a brief review of Courant algebroids and refer to
[
32
,
4
]
for more details.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Courant algebroid
is a quadruple
(E,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket,\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle,\rho)
, where
E
is a vector bundle over a manifold
M

with a fibrewise non-degenerate bilinear pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\text{\Large$\odot$}^{2}E^{*}),
a vector bundle morphism

\rho\colon E\rightarrow TM

called the
anchor
, and an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear bracket operation

(2.2)

\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)\times\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)\longrightarrow\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)

called the
Dorfman bracket
, which together satisfy

(i)

\pounds_{\rho(e)}\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle=\langle\llbracket e,e_{1}\rrbracket,e_{2}\rangle+\langle e_{1},\llbracket e,e_{2}\rrbracket\rangle\ ,

(ii)

\langle\llbracket e,e\rrbracket,e_{1}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\pounds_{\rho(e_{1})}\langle e,e\rangle\ ,

(iii)

\llbracket e,\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket\rrbracket=\llbracket\llbracket e,e_{1}\rrbracket,e_{2}\rrbracket+\llbracket e_{1},\llbracket e,e_{2}\rrbracket\rrbracket\ ,

for all
e,e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
, where
\pounds
denotes the Lie derivative along a vector field on
M
.

Remark 2.3
.

The anchored Leibniz rule for the Dorfman bracket

(2.4)

\displaystyle\llbracket e_{1},f\,e_{2}\rrbracket=f\,\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket+\big(\pounds_{\rho(e_{1})}f\big)\,e_{2}\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M),
follows from invariance
(i)
of the pairing.
The Jacobi identity
(iii)
and the anchored Leibniz rule (
2.4
) imply that the anchor
\rho
is a bracket homomorphism:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\rho(\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket)=[\rho(e_{1}),\rho(e_{2})]\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
, where
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
denotes the Lie bracket of vector fields.

Throughout this paper we will denote the Courant algebroid
(E,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket,\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle,\rho)
simply by
E
when there is no ambiguity in the structure maps. If clarity is required, we will sometimes use a subscript
E
to label the structures on a Courant algebroid
E
. If there are multiple Courant algebroids
E_{i}
involved in the discussion, we label the structures on
E_{i}
with a subscript
{}_{i}\,
.

Example 2.6

(
Standard Courant Algebroids
)
.

The (

)

over

is the quadruple
(\mathbb{T}M,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{H},\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathbb{T}M},{\rm pr}_{1})
, where

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}M:=TM\oplus T^{*}M

is the generalised tangent bundle, the Dorfman bracket is

(2.8)

\displaystyle\llbracket X+\alpha,Y+\beta\rrbracket_{H}\coloneqq[X,Y]+\pounds_{X}\beta-\iota_{Y}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha+\iota_{Y}\,\iota_{X}H\ ,

for a closed three-form
H\in\mathsf{\Omega}^{3}_{\rm cl}(M)
, the pairing is given by

(2.9)

\displaystyle\langle X+\alpha,Y+\beta\rangle=\iota_{Y}\alpha+\iota_{X}\beta\ ,

and the anchor
{\rm pr}_{1}
is the projection to the first summand of
\mathbb{T}M=TM\oplus T^{*}M
; here
\iota_{X}
denotes the contraction of differential forms with the vector field

. From now on, we will denote the

(\mathbb{T}M,H).

Remark 2.10
.

For any Courant algebroid
E
over
M
there is an ‘adjoint’ map
\rho^{*}\colon T^{*}M\to E
given by

(2.11)

\displaystyle\langle\rho^{*}(\alpha),e\rangle\coloneqq\iota_{e}\,\rho^{\rm t}(\alpha)\ ,

for all
\alpha\in\mathsf{\Omega}^{1}(M)
and
e\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
, where
\rho^{\rm t}\colon T^{*}M\to E^{*}
is the transpose of
\rho.

The map
\rho^{*}
induces a map

defined by

{\mathcal{D}}f=\rho^{*}(\mathrm{d}f)\ ,

for all

, which obeys a derivation-like rule and is the natural generalisation of the exterior derivative in the Courant algebroid

. Then item

of Definition

(2.12)

\displaystyle\llbracket e,e\rrbracket=\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\mathcal{D}}\langle e,e\rangle\ ,

which together with Equation (

(2.13)

\displaystyle\llbracket f\,e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket=f\,\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket-\big(\pounds_{\rho(e_{2})}f\big)\,e_{1}+\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle\,{\mathcal{D}}f\ .

Applying Equation (
2.5

\displaystyle\rho\circ\rho^{*}=0\ ,

and this motivates

2.4.
Generalised Metrics and Isometries

We introduce generalised metrics for Courant algebroids and an extended notion of isometry between generalised metrics stated in terms of Courant algebroid relations, see
[
4
,
32
,
7
]
for more details.

Definition 2.46
.

A
generalised metric
on a Courant algebroid
E
is an automorphism
\tau\colon E\to E
covering the identity with
\tau^{2}=\mathds{1}
which, together with the pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
, defines a positive-definite fibrewise metric on
E
given by

(2.47)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}(e,e^{\prime})=\langle e,\tau(e^{\prime})\rangle\ ,

for
e,e^{\prime}\in E
. Equivalently, a generalised metric on
E
is a maximally positive-definite (with respect to
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
) subbundle
V^{+}\subset E;
the subbundles
V^{+}
and
V^{-}:=(V^{+})^{\perp}
give an orthogonal decomposition
E=V^{+}\oplus V^{-}
into the eigenbundles

(2.48)

\displaystyle V^{\pm}=\ker(\tau\mp\mathds{1})\ .

We denote the Courant algebroid
E
endowed with a generalised metric
\tau
or
V^{+}
by
(E,\tau)
or
(E,V^{+})
.

Example 2.49

(
Exact Courant Algebroids
)
.

A generalised metric
\tau
on a (twisted) standard Courant algebroid
E=\mathbb{T}M
corresponds to a pair
(g,b)
, where
g
is a Riemannian metric on
M
and
b\in{\mathsf{\Omega}}^{2}(M)
, see
[
10
, Proposition 2.40]
. In this case

V^{\pm}=\text{gr}(\pm\,g+b)\ ,

that is,
X+\alpha\in V^{\pm}
if and only if
\alpha=\iota_{X}(\pm\,g+b)
. The corresponding fibrewise metric on
\mathbb{T}M=TM\oplus T^{*}M
is given by

(2.50)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}=\begin{pmatrix}g-b\,g^{-1}\,b&b\,g^{-1}\\
-g^{-1}\,b&g^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\ .

Changing the splitting of an exact Courant algebroid

sends

to
b-B
and
H
to
H+\mathrm{d}B
,
so there is a preferred identification with

. It follows that a generalised metric
\tau
on an exact Courant algebroid

is equivalent to a pair
(g,\sigma)
, where

is a Riemannian metric on
M
and
\sigma:TM\to E
is an isotropic splitting.
We will sometimes denote this Courant algebroid and generalised metric pair by
\big(E,(g,b)\big)
, when the context is clear. If

we denote this simply by
(E,g)
.

Definition 2.51
.

Let
R\colon E_{1}\dashrightarrow E_{2}
be a Courant algebroid relation supported on a submanifold

. Let

and
V_{2}^{+}
be generalised metrics on

and

. Then

is a
generalised isometry between

and
V_{2}^{+}
if

(2.52)

\displaystyle R_{c}=R^{+}_{c}\oplus R^{-}_{c}\ ,

for each

.

We will work under the assumption that

) holds globally.

This definition can be equivalently formulated by considering the corresponding automorphisms

and
\tau_{2}\in{\sf Aut}(E_{2})
, and setting

Then a Courant algebroid relation

supported on

is a generalised isometry if

see
[
7
,
13
]
. When

for a classical Courant algebroid isomorphism

It is shown in
[

and cokernel
\mathrm{CoKer}(R)

3.1.

Definition 3.1

Let

and

\displaystyle{\rm div}\,e-{\rm div}^{\prime}\,e=\langle e_{\rm div},e\rangle\ ,

for all

3.2.
Related Divergence Operators

We will now give a notion of when two divergence operators are related by a Courant algebroid relation.

Definition 3.14
.

Let
{\rm div}_{1}
and
{\rm div}_{2}
be divergence operators on Courant algebroids
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
over manifolds
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, respectively. Let

be a Courant algebroid relation supported on a submanifold

. Then
{\rm div}_{1}
and
{\rm div}_{2}
are
R
-related
, denoted
{\rm div}_{1}\sim_{R}{\rm div}_{2}
, if
{\rm div}_{1}\,e_{1}\sim_{C}{\rm div}_{2}\,e_{2}
for every
(e_{1},e_{2})\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E_{1}\times\overline{E}_{2};R)
.

Let us check that this notion of related divergence operators on Courant algebroids is well-defined.

Lemma 3.15
.

Relations between divergences in the sense of Definition
3.14
are well-defined: if
(e_{1},e_{2}),(e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime})\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E_{1}\times\overline{E}_{2};R)
such that
(e_{1},e_{2})_{c}=(e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime})_{c}
for all
c\in C,
then
{\rm div}_{1}\,e_{1}\sim_{C}{\rm div}_{2}\,e_{2}
if and only if
{\rm div}_{1}\,e_{1}^{\prime}\sim_{C}{\rm div}_{2}\,e_{2}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Define
{\rm div}\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}(E_{1})\times\mathsf{\Gamma}(E_{2})\to C^{\infty}(M_{1})\times C^{\infty}(M_{2})
by
{\rm div}=({\rm div}_{1},{\rm div}_{2})
, and extend this to
\widetilde{\rm div}\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}(E_{1}\times\overline{E}_{2})\to C^{\infty}(M_{1}\times M_{2})
.
Suppose that
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E_{1}\times\overline{E}_{2};R)
with
r(c)=r^{\prime}(c)
for all
c\in C
. Then for any open subset
U\subset M_{1}\times M_{2},
there is a frame
\set{r^{i}}\subset\mathsf{\Gamma}(U,E_{1}\times\overline{E}_{2};R)
such that
r=\sum_{i}\,f_{i}\,r^{i}
and
r^{\prime}=\sum_{i}\,f^{\prime}_{i}\,r^{i}
with

satisfying
f_{i}(c)=f^{\prime}_{i}(c)
for all
c\in C\cap U
. Denoting the anchor of

by

, we then find

(3.16)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rm div}\,r=\sum_{i}\,\big(f_{i}\,\widetilde{\rm div}\,r^{i}+\pounds_{\rho(r^{i})}f_{i}\big)=\sum_{i}\,\big(f^{\prime}_{i}\,\widetilde{\rm div}\,r^{i}+\pounds_{\rho(r^{i})}f^{\prime}_{i}\big)=\widetilde{\rm div}\,r^{\prime}

on

, where we used compatibility of the relation with the anchor which implies that

for

. Thus, on
C
,
\widetilde{\rm div}
is independent of the chosen section of
\mathsf{\Gamma}(E_{1}\times\overline{E}_{2};R)
, and hence
{\rm div}_{1}\sim_{R}{\rm div}_{2}
is well-defined.
∎

A broad class of divergence operators is obtained via Courant algebroid connections. A

3.2.2.

The natural step to take now is to see how dilatons, discussed in Subsection

3.3.

\displaystyle s\big(\underline{\rho}{}_{1}^{*}\,\underline{\sigma}{}_{1}^{*}\llbracket\underline{\sigma}{}_{1}(\underline{X}),\underline{\sigma}{}_{1}(\underline{\rho}{}_{1}(\underline{e}^{\circ}))\rrbracket_{1}\big)=\rho^{*}\,\sigma_{1}^{*}\llbracket\sigma_{1}(X),\sigma_{1}(\rho(s(\underline{e}^{\circ})))\rrbracket\ ,

where
X
is the canonical lift of
\underline{X}
to
\mathfrak{k}_{2}\subset\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
(see Definition
2.65
). The difference is again in
\mathsf{\Gamma}(\ker(\natural_{1}))=\mathsf{\Gamma}(K_{1})
. Under the anchor
\rho
the difference lands in
T{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\cap T{\mathcal{F}}_{2}=\set{0}
. But
\sigma_{1}\circ\rho
is the identity on
K_{1}
by
[
13
, Lemma 2.34]
, hence the difference vanishes.

We also have
s(\underline{\sigma}{}_{1}(\underline{X}))=\sigma_{1}(X)
, for all
\underline{X}\in\mathfrak{k}_{\tau_{1}}
. Putting everything together we get

(3.72)

\displaystyle\llbracket s(\underline{\sigma}{}_{1}(\underline{X})),s(\underline{e}^{\circ})\rrbracket-s\llbracket\underline{\sigma}{}_{1}(\underline{X}),\underline{e}^{\circ}\rrbracket_{1}

\displaystyle=\llbracket s(\underline{\sigma}{}_{1}(\underline{X})),s(\underline{e}^{\circ})\rrbracket-s\big(\underline{\rho}{}_{1}^{*}\,\underline{\sigma}{}_{1}^{*}\llbracket\underline{\sigma}{}_{1}(\underline{X}),\underline{\sigma}{}_{1}(\underline{\rho}{}_{1}(\underline{e}^{\circ}))\rrbracket_{1}\big)

(3.73)

\displaystyle=\llbracket\sigma_{1}(X),s(\underline{e}^{\circ})\rrbracket-\rho^{*}\,\sigma_{1}^{*}\llbracket\sigma_{1}(X),\sigma_{1}(\rho(s(\underline{e}^{\circ}))\rrbracket

(3.74)

\displaystyle=\llbracket\sigma_{2}(X),s(\underline{e}^{\circ})\rrbracket\ ,

where for the first equality we use the
D_{1}
-invariance of
\underline{e}^{\circ}
, while for the last equality we use the compatibility of the splittings (see Definition
2.65
).

Now
\sigma_{2}(X)\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(K_{2})
, and
s(\underline{e}^{\circ})\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(K_{1}^{\perp}\cap K_{2}^{\perp})
. Since
\llbracket\mathsf{\Gamma}(K_{2}),\mathsf{\Gamma}(K_{2}^{\perp})\rrbracket\subset\mathsf{\Gamma}(K_{2}^{\perp})

) that

(3.75)

\displaystyle\llbracket\sigma_{2}(X),s(\underline{e}^{\circ})\rrbracket\ \in\ \mathsf{\Gamma}(K_{2}^{\perp}\cap K_{1})=\mathsf{\Gamma}(K_{2}\cap K_{1})=\set{0}\ .

Using the Leibniz rule for the Dorfman bracket, this extends to all elements of
\mathsf{\Gamma}(K_{2})
, and hence

is basic with respect to
K_{2}
.
∎

We have just shown how
D_{1}
-invariant sections of
\underline{E}{}_{1}
can be lifted to
K_{2}
-basic sections. This property is crucial for the behaviour of divergence operators under T-duality.

Theorem 3.76
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.68
, suppose that
D_{1}\subset T{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
is spanned pointwise by a (local) Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}_{\tau_{1}}\subset\mathsf{\Gamma}(D_{1}).
Assume further that
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=\set{0}
and that
\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\underline{E}{}_{1})
spans
\underline{E}{}_{1}
pointwise. If
\underline{E}{}_{1}
admits a divergence
{\rm div}{}_{1}
which is (locally) compatible with
D_{1},
then there exists a unique divergence operator
{\rm div}{}_{2}
on
\underline{E}{}_{2}
such that
{\rm div}{}_{1}\sim_{R}{\rm div}{}_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
{\rm div}{}_{1}
be a divergence on
\underline{E}{}_{1}
. By
[
13
, Proposition 5.31]
, there is a unique splitting
s_{1}\colon K_{1}^{\perp}/K_{1}\to K_{1}^{\perp}
of the short exact sequence

(3.77)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow K_{1}\longrightarrow K_{1}^{\perp}\longrightarrow K_{1}^{\perp}/K_{1}\longrightarrow 0

such that
\mathrm{im}(s_{1})\subset K_{1}^{\perp}\cap K_{2}^{\perp}
. Since
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=\set{0}
and
\mathrm{rk}(K_{1})=\mathrm{rk}(K_{2})
, it follows that
\mathrm{im}(s_{1})=K_{1}^{\perp}\cap K_{2}^{\perp}
.

Define the map
\delta\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}\big(\mathrm{im}(s_{1})\big)\to\varpi_{1}^{*}\big(C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}}_{1})\big)
by

(3.78)

\displaystyle\delta(s_{1}(e))={\rm div}{}_{1}\,\underline{e}\ ,

where
e
is the element corresponding to
\underline{e}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{E}{}_{1})
using the identifications
\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{E}{}_{1})\cong\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\text{bas}}(K_{1}^{\perp})/\mathsf{\Gamma}(K_{1})
and
C^{\infty}_{\text{bas}}(M,{\mathcal{F}}_{1})\cong C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}}_{1})
. Note that
\delta
behaves like a divergence operator, i.e. it satisfies a Leibniz-like rule, because
\varpi_{1*}(\rho(s_{1}(e)))=\underline{\rho}{}_{1}(\underline{e}).

Using Definition
3.52
, the divergence
\delta
satisfies
\pounds_{X}\,\delta(s_{1}(e))=0
, for any
X\in\mathfrak{k}_{2}
. One extends this to the whole of
\mathsf{\Gamma}\big(\mathrm{im}(s_{1})\big)
by the Leibniz rule.

Any section
e\in\mathsf{\Gamma}\big(\mathrm{im}(s_{1})\big)\cap\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\text{bas}}(K_{2}^{\perp})
can be written as
e=\sum_{i}\,f_{i}\,s_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i})
, where
\underline{e}{}_{i}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{E}{}_{1})
and
f_{i}\in\varpi_{2}^{*}\big(C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}}_{2})\big)
.
Likewise any vector field
X\in\mathsf{\Gamma}\big(\rho(K_{2})\big)
can be written as
X=\sum_{j}\,g_{j}\,X_{j}
, where
X_{j}\in\mathfrak{k}_{2}
and
g_{j}\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Thus

(3.79)

\displaystyle\pounds_{X}\,\delta(e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\,\Big(g_{j}\,\pounds_{X_{j}}\big(f_{i}\,\,\delta(s_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i}))\big)+g_{j}\,\pounds_{X_{j}}\,\pounds_{\rho(s_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i}))}f_{i}\Big)

(3.80)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\,\big(g_{j}\,(\pounds_{X_{j}}f_{i})\,\delta(s_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i}))+g_{j}\,f_{i}\,\pounds_{X_{j}}\,\delta(s_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i}))

(3.81)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+g_{j}\,\pounds_{\rho(s_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i}))}\,\pounds_{X_{j}}f_{i}+g_{j}\,\pounds_{[X_{j},\rho{}(s_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i}))]}f_{i}\big)=0\ .

The final term vanishes since
[\varpi_{1*}(X_{j}),\underline{\rho}{}_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i})]=0
, hence
[X_{j},\rho(s_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i}))]\in\mathsf{\Gamma}\big(\rho(K_{1})\big)
, whereas

(3.82)

\displaystyle[X_{j},\rho(s_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i}))]=\rho(\llbracket\sigma_{2}(X_{j}),s_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i})\rrbracket)\ \in\ \mathsf{\Gamma}\big(\rho(K_{2}^{\perp}\cap K_{1})\big)\subset\mathsf{\Gamma}\big(\rho(K_{2})\big)\ .

Hence the restriction of
\delta
to
\mathsf{\Gamma}\big(\mathrm{im}(s_{1})\big)\cap\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\text{bas}}(K_{2}^{\perp})
maps to
\varpi_{2}^{*}\big(C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}}_{2})\big)
.

By
[
13
, Proposition 5.31]
there also exists a unique splitting
s_{2}:K_{2}^{\perp}/K_{2}\to K_{2}^{\perp}
of the short exact sequence

(3.83)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow K_{2}\longrightarrow K_{2}^{\perp}\longrightarrow K_{2}^{\perp}/K_{2}\longrightarrow 0

induced by
K_{2}^{\perp}
and
K_{2}
. As for
s_{1}
, its image is
\mathrm{im}(s_{2})=K_{1}^{\perp}\cap K_{2}^{\perp}
.
Thus we may define a divergence operator
{\rm div}{}_{2}\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{E}{}_{2})\to C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}}_{2})
by

(3.84)

\displaystyle{\rm div}{}_{2}\,\underline{e}{}_{2}=\delta\big(e_{2}\big)\ ,

where
e_{2}
is the lift of
\underline{e}{}_{2}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{E}{}_{2})
to
\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\text{bas}}(K^{\perp}_{2})
using the unique splitting
s_{2}
and the identifications
\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{E}{}_{2})\cong\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\text{bas}}(K_{2}^{\perp})/\mathsf{\Gamma}(K_{2})
and
C^{\infty}_{\text{bas}}(M,{\mathcal{F}}_{2})\cong C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}}_{2})
.

From Lemma
3.67
it follows that any
K_{2}
-basic section
e
of
\mathrm{im}(s_{1})
is written as
e=\sum_{i}\,f_{i}\,s_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i})
, where
\underline{e}{}_{i}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\underline{E}{}_{1})
and
f_{i}\in\varpi_{2}^{*}\big(C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}}_{2})\big)
.
Hence
e
is also basic with respect to
K_{1}
when
f_{i}\in\varpi^{*}_{1}\big(C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}}_{1})\big)\,\cap\,\varpi_{2}^{*}\big(C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}}_{2})\big)
. Thus
R
-related sections
\psi{}_{1}\sim_{R}\psi{}_{2}
are given by
\psi{}_{1}=\sum_{i}\,f_{i}\,\underline{e}{}_{i}
and
\psi{}_{2}=\sum_{i}\,\varpi_{2*}\big(\varpi_{1}^{*}(f_{i})\big)\,\natural_{2}(s_{1}(\underline{e}{}_{i}))
, for
f_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{D_{1}}({\mathcal{Q}}_{1})
and
\underline{e}{}_{i}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\underline{E}{}_{1})
. By the above construction,
{\rm div}{}_{1}\,\psi{}_{1}={\rm div}{}_{2}\,\psi{}_{2}
.

Uniqueness follows from Lemma
3.32
.

The local version follows the same construction, by noting that the invariance of
\delta(e)
can be checked locally.
∎

Remark 3.85

(
Local T-duality for Divergences
)
.

These results also hold in the local case, that is, if

is locally
D_{1}
-invariant in the sense of Definition

. This is because, in the proof of Lemma

3.4.1.
Correspondence Spaces

We shall discuss how the results of this subsection apply to the most prominent example of T-duality, that of two fiber bundles
{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}
over the same base manifold
{\mathcal{B}}
. This setup can be depicted in the commutative correspondence diagram

(3.86)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{B}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varpi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varpi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}

Because of the universal property of fibred products, we can take the correspondence space to be

M={\mathcal{Q}}_{1}\times_{{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}\ .

In particular,
T{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\cap T{\mathcal{F}}_{2}=\set{0}
, with
\varpi_{1}
and
\varpi_{2}
the canonical projection maps. Since
\rho
is injective on
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
(see
[
13
, Lemma 2.34]
), it follows that
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=\set{0}
.
The particular case of principal torus bundles is covered extensively in
[
41
,
18
,
10
]
and will be treated as a special case of our general formalism in Subsection
3.4.2
below.

Proposition 3.87
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem
2.68
are satisfied, with T-duality relation
R\colon(\underline{E}{}_{1},\tau_{1})\dashrightarrow(\underline{E}{}_{2},\tau_{2})
. Assume further that there is a spanning set of vector fields
\set{X_{1},\dots,X_{r_{2}}}\subset T{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
which are projectable by
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
such that

(3.88)

\displaystyle\llbracket\sigma_{1}(X_{i}),s(\underline{e}^{\circ})\rrbracket=\mathsf{ad}^{\sigma_{2}}_{X_{i}}\,s(\underline{e}^{\circ})

for every
i=1,\dots,r_{2}
and
\underline{e}^{\circ}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\underline{E}{}_{1})
, where
s:K_{1}^{\perp}/K_{1}\to K_{1}^{\perp}
is the unique splitting from Lemma
3.67
.
Then the T-dual generalised metric
\tau_{2}
is invariant with respect to the distribution

D_{2}:=T\underline{{\mathcal{F}}}{}_{2}=\varpi_{2*}(T{\mathcal{F}}_{1})\ ,

with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}_{\tau_{2}}:=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\set{\varpi_{2*}X_{1},\dots,\varpi_{2*}X_{r_{2}}}\subset\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{Q}}_{2})
. Furthermore, there is an isomorphism
\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\underline{E}{}_{1})\cong\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{2}}(\underline{E}{}_{2})
of
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})
-modules.

Proof.

Let
(\mathbb{T}M,H_{1})
and
(\mathbb{T}M,H_{2})
be the twisted standard Courant algebroids which are isomorphic to
E
via the isomorphisms induced by the compatible adapted splittings
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
, respectively. Define
B\in{\mathsf{\Omega}}^{2}(M)
by

\sigma_{1}(X)-\sigma_{2}(X)=\rho^{*}(\iota_{X}B)

for
X\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
. For every
\underline{Y}^{\circ}+\underline{\eta}^{\circ}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}_{1})
, let
\eta=\varpi_{1}^{*}\,\underline{\eta}^{\circ}
and
Y+\eta=s(\underline{Y}^{\circ}+\underline{\eta}^{\circ})
. In other words,
Y
is the unique lift of
\underline{Y}^{\circ}
such that

(3.89)

\displaystyle\eta(Z)=B(Y,Z)\ ,

for all
Z\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
.

Equation (
3.88
) then reads

(3.90)

\displaystyle\iota_{Y}\,\iota_{X_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}B+\iota_{Y}\,\pounds_{X_{i}}B=\llbracket-\iota_{X_{i}}B,Y+\eta\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\mathrm{pr}_{2}(\llbracket\sigma_{2}(X_{i}),\sigma_{2}(Y)\rrbracket_{H_{1}})

(3.91)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{pr}_{2}(\llbracket\sigma_{1}(X_{i}),\sigma_{1}(Y)\rrbracket_{H_{2}})=\iota_{Y}\,\iota_{X_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}B\ ,

hence
\iota_{Y}\pounds_{X_{i}}B=0
for every
i=1,\dots,r_{2}
and
\underline{Y}^{\circ}+\underline{\eta}^{\circ}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}_{1})
.
Thus

(3.92)

\displaystyle 0=\pounds_{X_{i}}\big(\eta(Z)-B(Y,Z)\big)

\displaystyle=\pounds_{X_{i}}\big(\eta(Z)\big)-(\pounds_{X_{i}}B)(Y,Z)-B([X_{i},Y],Z)-B(Y,[X_{i},Z])

(3.93)

\displaystyle=(\pounds_{X_{i}}\eta)(Z)+\eta([X_{i},Z])-B([X_{i},Y],Z)-\eta([X_{i},Z])

(3.94)

\displaystyle=-B([X_{i},Y],Z)\ ,

for all
Z\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{F}}_{2})
.
But
[X_{i},Y]\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T{\mathcal{F}}_{1})
since
Y
is
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
-projectable, and
B
is non-degenerate on
T{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\otimes T{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
since
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=\set{0}
, hence
[X_{i},Y]=0
.

It therefore follows that

(3.95)

\displaystyle\mathsf{ad}^{\underline{\sigma}{}_{2}}_{\varpi_{2*}X_{i}}\,\natural_{2}\big(s(\underline{Y}^{\circ}+\underline{\eta}^{\circ})\big)=0\ .

By virtue of the generalised isometry
R
and the uniqueness of the lift, it thus follows that the map
\natural_{2}\circ s\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}_{1})\to\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{2}}(\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}_{2})
is an isomorphism. Hence we obtain the isomorphism of
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})
-modules

\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\underline{E}{}_{1})\cong\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{2}}(\underline{E}{}_{2})
.
By taking
\underline{Y}^{\circ}+\underline{\eta}^{\circ}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(V_{1}^{+})
, it then follows that
V_{2}^{+}
is
D_{2}
-invariant.
∎

Proposition 3.96
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
3.87
, there exists transitive Courant algebroids
\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1}
and
\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{2}
over
{\mathcal{B}}
together with a
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})
-module isomorphism
\mathscr{F}\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1})\to\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{2})
induced by the T-duality relation
R
.

Proof.

Define a vector bundle
\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1}
over
{\mathcal{B}}
by

(3.97)

\displaystyle\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1}=\underline{E}{}_{1}/\underline{{\mathcal{F}}}{}_{1}\ .

The space of sections of
\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1}
is identified with the space
\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\underline{E}{}_{1})
as a module over
C^{\infty}_{D_{1}}({\mathcal{Q}}_{1})\cong C^{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})
. This gives
\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1}
the structure of a Courant algebroid as follows.
The involutivity of
\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\underline{E}{}_{1})
established in Lemma
A.24
gives the bracket structure on
\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1}
, while the pairing descends to
{\mathcal{B}}
by Lemma
A.8
. The anchor
\underline{\underline{\rho}}{}_{1}:\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1}\to T{\mathcal{B}}
is defined by
\underline{\underline{\rho}}{}_{1}(\underline{\underline{e}})=\pi_{1*}\,\underline{\rho}{}_{1}(\underline{e}^{\circ})
, where
\underline{\rho}{}_{1}(\underline{e}^{\circ})
is projectable to
{\mathcal{B}}
since
\underline{e}^{\circ}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\underline{E}{}_{1})
. Since
\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\underline{E}{}_{1})
spans
\underline{E}{}_{1}
pointwise, it follows that
\underline{\underline{\rho}}{}_{1}
is surjective, and hence that
\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1}
is a transitive Courant algebroid.

By a completely analogous argument using the Hausdorff-Morita equivalent foliation
\underline{{\mathcal{F}}}{}_{2}
of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}
, there is also an isomorphism
\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{2})\cong\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{2}}(\underline{E}{}_{2})
, where

(3.98)

\displaystyle\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{2}=\underline{E}{}_{2}/\underline{{\mathcal{F}}}{}_{2}

is a transitive Courant algebroid over
{\mathcal{B}}
.
By Proposition
3.87
, there is an isomorphism
\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\underline{E}{}_{1})\cong\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{2}}(\underline{E}{}_{2})
. The chain of isomorphisms

(3.99)

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1})\cong\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{1}}(\underline{E}{}_{1})\cong\mathsf{\Gamma}_{D_{2}}(\underline{E}{}_{2})\cong\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{2})

defines the isomorphism
\mathscr{F}\colon\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1})\to\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{2})
.
∎

We can now show that T-dual divergences induce isomorphic divergences, under
\mathscr{F}
, on the transitive Courant algebroids
\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1}
and
\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{2}
.

Lemma 3.100
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.76
, let
\underline{\underline{{\rm div}}}{}_{1}
be a divergence on
\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1}
. Then there exists a divergence operator
\underline{\underline{{\rm div}}}{}_{2}
on
\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{2}
such that

(3.101)

\displaystyle\underline{\underline{{\rm div}}}{}_{1}\,\underline{\underline{e}}=\underline{\underline{{\rm div}}}{}_{2}\,\mathscr{F}(\underline{\underline{e}})\ ,

for all
\underline{\underline{e}}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{1})
.

Proof.

The pullback
\pi_{1}^{*}\,\underline{\underline{{\rm div}}}{}_{1}
defines a
D_{1}
-compatible divergence on
\underline{E}{}_{1}
. By Theorem
3.76
, there is a T-dual divergence
{\rm div}{}{}_{2}
on
\underline{E}{}_{2}
. By Lemma
3.54
, it follows that
{\rm div}{}{}_{2}
is
D_{2}
-compatible, and hence defines a divergence
\underline{\underline{{\rm div}}}{}_{2}\coloneqq\pi_{2*}\,{\rm div}{}{}_{2}
on
\underline{\underline{E}}{}_{2}
which by construction satisfies Equation (
3.101
).
∎

3.4.2.
Principal Torus Bundles

We may specialise to the case of principal torus bundles, where
\underline{{\mathcal{F}}}{}_{1}
and
\underline{{\mathcal{F}}}{}_{2}
are given by the orbits of principal actions of the torus group
{\mathsf{T}}^{k}
of rank
k
on
{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}
, respectively. We recall the notion of T-duality from
[
10
, Definition 10.13]
.

Definition 3.102
.

Let
\underline{E}{}_{1}\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
and
\underline{E}{}_{2}\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}
be
{\mathsf{T}}^{k}
-equivariant exact Courant algebroids, where
{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}
are principal
{\mathsf{T}}^{k}
-bundles over the same base
{\mathcal{B}}
. Suppose that they are T-dual in the sense of Cavalcanti-Gualitieri
[
18
]
; then there is an isomorphism
\mathscr{R}\colon\underline{E}{}_{1}/{\mathsf{T}}^{k}\to\underline{E}{}_{2}/{\mathsf{T}}^{k}
of transitive Courant algebroids over
{\mathcal{B}}
. A
{\mathsf{T}}^{k}
-invariant pair
(\tau_{2},{\rm div}{}_{2})
on
\underline{E}{}_{2}
is
T-dual
via
\mathscr{R}
to a
{\mathsf{T}}^{k}
-invariant pair
(\tau_{1},{\rm div}{}_{1})
on
\underline{E}{}_{1}
if

(3.103)

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}\circ\pi_{1}{}_{*}\tau_{1}=\pi_{2}{}_{*}\tau_{2}\circ\mathscr{R}\quad,\quad\pi_{1}{}_{*}\,{\rm div}{}_{1}=\mathscr{R}^{*}\,\pi_{2}{}_{*}\,{\rm div}{}_{2}\ ,

where
\pi_{i}\colon{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}\to{\mathcal{B}}
with
i=1,2
are the bundle projections.

Here the notion of
{\mathsf{T}}^{k}
-invariance coincides with our sense: they are equivalent with the identification of
\mathfrak{k}_{\tau_{1}}=\mathfrak{k}_{\tau_{2}}=\mathfrak{t}^{k}
with the Lie algebra of the torus group
{\mathsf{T}}^{k}
.

Proposition 3.104
.

If
(\underline{E}{}_{2},\tau_{2},{\rm div}{}_{2})
is T-dual to
(\underline{E}{}_{1},\tau_{1},{\rm div}{}_{1})
in the sense of Definition
3.102
, then there is a generalised isometry
R\colon(\underline{E}{}_{1},\tau_{1})\dashrightarrow(\underline{E}{}_{2},\tau_{2})
such that
{\rm div}{}_{1}\sim_{R}{\rm div}{}_{2}
.

Proof.

The generalised isometry
R
is constructed in
[
13
, Lemma 6.5]
. The result then follows immediately from Proposition
3.96
and Lemma
3.100
with
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{F}
.
∎

Example 3.105

(
Circle Bundles
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}

{\mathcal{B}}
, for

, and let

{\mathsf{S}}^{1}

the global vector fields defining the circle fibres. Let

\displaystyle({\rm Ric}_{g_{2}})_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(g_{1})_{\theta\theta}^{2}}\,(\mathrm{GRic}_{1})_{\theta\theta}+\frac{1}{4}\,(H_{2}^{2})_{\theta\theta}+\frac{1}{2}\,(\mathrm{d}^{*}H_{2})_{\theta\theta}\ ,

\displaystyle({\rm Ric}_{g_{2}})_{\alpha\theta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(g_{1})_{\theta\theta}}\,(\mathrm{GRic}_{1})_{\alpha\theta}+\frac{1}{4}\,(H_{2}^{2})_{\alpha\theta}+\frac{1}{2}\,(\mathrm{d}^{*}H_{2})_{\alpha\theta}+\frac{(b_{1})_{\alpha\theta}}{(g_{1})_{\theta\theta}^{2}}\,(\mathrm{GRic}_{1})_{\theta\theta}\ ,

\displaystyle({\rm Ric}_{g_{2}})_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{GRic}_{1})_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{4}\,(H_{2}^{2})_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{2}\,(\mathrm{d}^{*}H_{2})_{\alpha\beta}

(4.35)

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{(b_{1})_{\beta\theta}}{(g_{1})_{\theta\theta}}\,(\mathrm{GRic}_{1})_{\alpha\theta}-\frac{(b_{1})_{\beta\theta}\,(b_{1})_{\alpha\theta}}{(g_{1})_{\theta\theta}^{2}}\,(\mathrm{GRic}_{1})_{\theta\theta}-\frac{(b_{1})_{\alpha\theta}}{(g_{1})_{\theta\theta}}\,(\mathrm{GRic}_{1})_{\theta\beta}\ .

4.4.
Generalised Ricci Flow and T-Duality

We are now ready to discuss the interplay between geometric T-duality, introduced in Subsection
2.5
, and the generalised Ricci flow, inspired by the treatment of
[
23
]
.

Let
E
be a Courant algebroid over
M
endowed with a generalised metric
V^{+}
and a divergence operator
{\rm div}
. The
generalised Ricci flow
on
E
is the system of second order non-linear partial differential equations

(4.36)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tau=-2\,\mathrm{GRic}_{\tau,{\rm div}}\quad,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\rm div}=-{\mathcal{D}}\,\mathrm{GR}_{\tau,{\rm div}}\ ,

where
\tau
is the automorphism of
E
whose
+1
-eigenbundle is
V^{+}
, and
t\in[0,\infty)
is the flow parameter which we call ‘time’.

In the setting of geometric T-duality described in Subsections
2.5
and
3.4
, whereby a generalised isometry
R
plays the role of the T-duality relation, we consider a
D_{1}
-invariant pair
(\tau_{1},{\rm div}{}_{1})
of a generalised metric and divergence on
\underline{E}{}_{1}
, yielding a pair
(\tau_{2},{\rm div}{}_{2})
on the T-dual Courant algebroid
\underline{E}{}_{2}
. These pairs give
a priori
unrelated generalised Ricci flows. We will now show that they are indeed related.

Theorem 4.37
.

Under the assumptions of Theorems
2.68
and
3.76
, suppose that a unique solution
(\tau_{1}(t),{\rm div}{}_{1}(t))
of the generalised Ricci flow on
\underline{E}{}_{1}
with initial condition
(\tau_{1}(0),{\rm div}{}_{1}(0))=(\tau_{1},{\rm div}{}_{1})
exists for
t\in[0,T),
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},
and that the Lie algebra of isometries
\mathfrak{k}_{\tau_{1}}
consists of complete vector fields. Then there is a unique T-dual family of pairs
(\tau_{2}(t),{\rm div}{}_{2}(t))
that is a solution of the generalised Ricci flow on
\underline{E}{}_{2}
for all
t\in[0,T)
.

Proof.

By assumption, any Killing vector
X
for
\tau_{1}
integrates to a one-parameter family of Courant algebroid automorphisms
\bm{\Phi}_{s}
such that
\bm{\Phi}_{s}^{*}\,\tau_{1}=\tau_{1}
and
\bm{\Phi}^{*}_{s}\,{\rm div}{}_{1}={\rm div}{}_{1}
.
If
(\tau_{1}(t),{\rm div}{}_{1}(t))
is a solution of the generalised Ricci flow, then by the naturality of the generalised Ricci tensor and generalised scalar curvature, it follows that
(\bm{\Phi}_{s}^{*}\,\tau_{1}(t),\bm{\Phi}_{s}^{*}\,{\rm div}{}_{1}(t))
is also a solution of the generalised Ricci flow. By the uniqueness of the solution, it follows that
\bm{\Phi}_{s}
is a one-parameter group of isometries of
\left(\tau_{1}(t),{\rm div}{}_{1}(t)\right)
for all
t\in[0,T)
.
Hence the vector field
X
is a Killing vector for
\tau_{1}(t)
, and we may take
\mathfrak{k}_{\tau_{1}(t)}\coloneqq\mathfrak{k}_{\tau_{1}}
, for which
{\rm div}{}_{1}(t)
is compatible. By Theorems
2.68
and
3.76
, we may construct the unique T-dual pair
(\tau_{2}(t),{\rm div}{}_{2}(t))
for all
t\in[0,T)
, which by Corollary
4.17
satisfies the generalised Ricci flow.
∎

In this sense, we may therefore say that the T-duality relation
R
is
compatible
with the generalised Ricci flow. In particular, it follows from Theorem
4.18
that fixed points of the generalised Ricci flow, i.e. solutions to the generalised string background equations of Definition
4.12
, are preserved under T-duality.

The proof of Theorem
4.37
assumes the existence and uniqueness of the generalised Ricci flow, together with completeness of the vector fields generating isometries of
\tau_{1}
, which as shown in
[
11
]
are guaranteed on a compact manifold. This leads to

Corollary 4.38
.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
be a compact manifold with a
D_{1}
-invariant pair
(\tau_{1},{\rm div}{}_{1})
. Then there is a unique pair
(\tau_{2}(t),{\rm div}{}_{2}(t))
solving the generalised Ricci flow on
\underline{E}{}_{2}
which is T-dual to the generalised Ricci flow solution
(\tau_{1}(t),{\rm div}{}_{1}(t))
on
\underline{E}{}_{1}
, for all times
t\in[0,T)
.

4.4.1.
Local T-Duality of the Generalised Ricci flow

The proof of Theorem
4.37
requires that the Killing vector fields for
\tau_{1}
integrate to a flow on
\underline{E}{}_{1}
, thus this cannot be done in the local case. We shall now give the appropriate statement of the T-duality of generalised Ricci flows in the local case. The construction of the T-dual generalised Ricci tensor can be done immediately as a consequence of Theorem
2.77
and Remark
3.85
. In the course of the proof of Theorem
4.37
, we assumed that the generators of
\mathfrak{k}_{\tau_{1}}
were given by complete vector fields integrating to isometries, and that the generalised Ricci flow solution is unique, in order to assert that
\mathfrak{k}_{\tau_{1}(0)}\subset\mathfrak{k}_{\tau_{1}(t)}
. Dropping the former assumptions and simply assuming the latter condition yields

Theorem 4.39
.

Let
\left(\tau_{1}(t),{\rm div}{}_{1}(t)\right)
be a solution of the generalised Ricci flow on
\underline{E}{}_{1}
for
t\in[0,T)
with initial condition
\tau_{1}(0)=\tau_{1}
and
{\rm div}{}_{1}(0)={\rm div}{}_{1}
. Let
\set{U^{(i)}}_{i\in I}
be an open cover of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
and suppose that
\left(\tau_{1}(t),{\rm div}{}_{1}(t)\right)
is locally
D_{1}
-invariant, where
D_{1}
is generated by vector fields
\set{X_{1}^{(i)},\dots,X_{r_{1}}^{(i)}}_{i\in I}
, for all
t\in[0,T)
. Then there is a unique family
(\tau_{2}(t),{\rm div}{}_{2}(t))
which is T-dual to
(\tau_{1}(t),{\rm div}{}_{1}(t))
and which is a solution to the generalised Ricci flow on
\underline{E}{}_{2}
.

5.
Examples

We have already described the behaviour of the T-duality relation for classic classes of examples such as principal torus bundles and Poisson-Lie T-duality. In this final section we look at some explicit examples that are not covered by these cases discussed thus far throughout the paper.

5.1.
Geometric T-Duality for Hamilton’s Cigar Soliton

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\set{0}
with the cigar metric

g_{1}=\frac{\mathrm{d}x\otimes\mathrm{d}x+\mathrm{d}y\otimes\mathrm{d}y}{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}\ ,

where
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
. In geodesic coordinates this can be written as

g_{1}=\mathrm{d}s\otimes\mathrm{d}s+\tanh^{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\otimes\mathrm{d}\theta\ .

Then
\partial_{\theta}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
is a Killing vector for this metric, and it generates a circular foliation
\underline{{\mathcal{F}}}{}_{1}
with leaf space
{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}/\underline{{\mathcal{F}}}{}_{1}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

The family of metrics

(5.1)

\displaystyle g_{1}(t)=\frac{\cosh^{2}(s)}{{\,\rm e}\,^{4\,t}+\sinh^{2}(s)}\,\mathrm{d}s\otimes\mathrm{d}s+\frac{\sinh^{2}(s)}{{\,\rm e}\,^{4\,t}+\sinh^{2}(s)}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\otimes\mathrm{d}\theta

on
{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
is a Ricci soliton with initial condition
g_{1}(0)=g_{1}
. That is, it is a solution to the Ricci flow

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{1}(t)=-2\,\mathrm{Ric}_{g_{1}(t)}

which is self-similar, evolving the metric
g_{1}
purely by pullback under the one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms
\big(\sinh(s),\theta\big)\mapsto\big({\,\rm e}\,^{2\,t}\sinh(s),\theta\big)
. This Ricci soliton is called Hamilton’s cigar
[
24
]
. In string theory it appears as the Euclidean Witten black hole background, which is a fixed point of the renormalisation group flow of a gauged Wess-Zumino-Witten model (see e.g.
[
42
]
). In this subsection we demonstrate the behaviour under geometric T-duality of this classic solution to the Ricci flow.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with coordinates
(z,s)
, and foliation
\underline{{\mathcal{F}}}{}_{2}
given by the projection to the first factor, so that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}/\underline{{\mathcal{F}}}{}_{2}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Let
M={\mathcal{Q}}_{1}\times_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}
, with foliations
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
generated by
\partial_{z}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
generated by
\partial_{\theta}
, and take

B=\mathrm{d}\theta\wedge\mathrm{d}z\ .

Denoting
\partial_{s}=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
, we obtain the subbundle
R\subset\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}\times\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}
whose sections are given by

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Gamma}(R)=\text{Span}_{C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})}\set{(\partial_{s},\partial_{s})\,,\,(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}s)\,,\,(\partial_{\theta},\mathrm{d}z)\,,\,(\mathrm{d}\theta,\partial_{z})}\ .

Since
\partial_{\theta}
is a Killing vector of
g_{1}(t)
for all
t
, by Theorem
4.39
we get the generalised isometry

(5.3)

\displaystyle R\colon\big(\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}_{1},0,g_{1}(t)\big)\dashrightarrow\big(\mathbb{T}{\mathcal{Q}}_{2},0,g_{2}(t)\big)\ ,

with the T-dual family of metrics
g_{2}(t)
on
{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}
given by

(5.4)

\displaystyle g_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh^{2}(s)}{{\,\rm e}\,^{4\,t}+\sinh^{2}(s)}\,\mathrm{d}s\otimes\mathrm{d}s+\frac{{\,\rm e}\,^{4\,t}+\sinh^{2}(s)}{\sinh^{2}(s)}\,\mathrm{d}z\otimes\mathrm{d}z\ ,

and
R
-related divergence operators
{\rm div}_{\mu_{g_{1}(t)}}\sim_{R}{\rm div}_{\phi}(t)
where

(5.5)

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{\phi}(t)

\displaystyle={\rm div}_{\mu_{g_{2}(t)}}+\langle\mathrm{d}\phi,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{2}={\rm div}_{\mu_{g_{2}(t)}}-4\,\langle\varphi^{+},\,\cdot\,\rangle_{2}\ ,

with

\phi=-\log\left(\frac{{\,\rm e}\,^{4\,t}+\sinh^{2}(s)}{\sinh^{2}(s)}\right)\quad,\quad\varphi^{+}=-\frac{1}{4}\,\mathrm{d}\phi=-\frac{{\,\rm e}\,^{4\,t}\,\cosh(s)}{2\sinh^{3}(s)+2{\,\rm e}\,^{4\,t}\sinh(s)}\,\mathrm{d}s\ .

Then the pair
(g_{2}(t),{\rm div}_{\phi}(t))
solves the system

(5.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{2}(t)=-2\,\mathrm{Ric}_{g_{2}(t)}-2\,\nabla^{g_{2}(t)}\varphi^{+}\quad,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\rm div}_{\phi}(t)=-\mathrm{d}\,\big(\mathrm{R}_{g_{2}(t)}-4{\,\rm e}\,^{\phi}\,\Delta_{g_{2}(t)}{\,\rm e}\,^{-\phi}\big)\ .

Analogously to the original cigar soliton, the T-dual solution is a
generalised
Ricci soliton
[
43
,
11
]
, i.e. a self-similar solution of the generalised Ricci flow (
5.6
), evolving the metric
g_{2}
and dilaton field
\phi
purely by pullback under the one-parameter family of Courant algebroid automorphisms given by the diffeomorphisms
\big(\sinh(s),z\big)\mapsto\big({\,\rm e}\,^{2\,t}\sinh(s),z\big)
.

is the one-parameter family of pairs

with

5.3.
Klein Bottle

In this subsection we consider an example of geometric T-duality between non-principal

5.4.1.

Let us work out the T-dual manifold

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi}{}^{(1)}(x,y,\tilde{y},z)=(x,y,\tilde{y},z)\quad,\quad\widetilde{\varphi}{}^{(2)}(x,y,\tilde{y},z)=\begin{cases}(x+\frac{1}{2},y,\tilde{y},z)\qquad\text{on}\quad W^{(1)}\\[4.0pt]

which is well-defined provided we identify

(0,y,\tilde{y},z)\sim(1,1-y,1-\tilde{y},z+\tilde{y})

on

. This also ensures that the two-form

Upon quotienting

by the foliation

generated by

, we obtain the leaf space

where

\displaystyle(x,0,z)\sim(x,1,z)\quad,\quad(x,\tilde{y},0)\sim(x,\tilde{y},1)\quad,\quad(0,\tilde{y},z)\sim(1,1-\tilde{y},z+\tilde{y})\ .

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Gamma}\big({\mathcal{V}}^{(i)},R\big)=\text{Span}_{C^{\infty}({\mathcal{V}}^{(i)})}\{

\displaystyle(\partial_{x},\partial_{x})\,,\,(\partial_{y},\mathrm{d}\tilde{y}-\tfrac{1}{2}\cos(\pi\,x)\,\mathrm{d}z)\,,\,(\partial_{z},\partial_{z}+\tfrac{1}{2}\cos(\pi\,x)\,\partial_{\tilde{y}})\,,\,

\displaystyle\,(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}x)\,,\,(\mathrm{d}y,\partial_{\tilde{y}})\,,\,(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}z)\}\ .

The general framework for topological T-duality between affine torus bundles over a common base
{\mathcal{B}}
was considered in
[
44
]
using the language of local coefficient systems. In this setting T-duality is stated after a choice of local coefficient system

, i.e. an element
\xi
of

is equivalent to a choice of charts. Since the torus
{\mathsf{T}}^{2}
has twisted cohomology group

{\mathsf{T}}^{2}
for this choice of charts. Turning on the Kalb-Ramond flux

with non-trivial curvature.

Compared to

, the advantage of the formulation considered here is that it does not consider the affine structure of the torus bundle, nor even the group structure on the fibres, only the fibration structure itself. It also permits consideration of the T-duality of the geometric structures, as we illustrate in Subsection

[

]
.

5.4.2.

Let
g_{1}
be the flat metric on
{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
which is locally given by

Then the T-duality relation

is a generalized isometry between

and the T-dual metric
g_{2}
on

given by

(5.40)

\displaystyle g_{2}=\mathrm{d}x\otimes\mathrm{d}x+\mathrm{d}\tilde{y}\otimes\mathrm{d}\tilde{y}-\cos(\pi\,x)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{y}\odot\mathrm{d}z+\left(1+\tfrac{1}{4}\cos^{2}(\pi\,x)\right)\mathrm{d}z\otimes\mathrm{d}z\ .

On

Using the Ricci-Buscher rules of Subsection

, from this we can calculate the non-zero components of the Ricci tensor of

to be

(5.41)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Ric}_{g_{2}})_{yy}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{8}\sin^{2}(\pi\,x)\ ,

(5.42)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Ric}_{g_{2}})_{zy}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{4}\cos(\pi\,x)+\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{16}\sin^{2}(\pi\,x)\cos(\pi\,x)\ ,

(5.43)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Ric}_{g_{2}})_{xx}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{8}\sin^{2}(\pi\,x)\ ,

(5.44)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Ric}_{g_{2}})_{zz}

We do not attempt to solve it here, as it requires some involved numerical computation which is beyond the scope of this paper.

does not depend on the choice of auxiliary section or curve. Then the first two properties follow immediately, while the final property follows from
[\mathsf{ad}^{\sigma}_{X},\mathsf{ad}^{\sigma}_{Y}]=\mathsf{ad}^{\sigma}_{[X,Y]}
, which can be proven using various Cartan identities and the fact that

is closed (and hence
\llbracket\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{H},\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{H}=\llbracket\sigma([X,Y]),\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{H}
.)
∎

Remark A.22
.

If
\sigma_{0}(X)=X
and
\mathsf{ad}^{\sigma_{0}}_{X}
preserves the Dorfman bracket as above, then
\pounds_{X}H=\mathrm{d}\,\iota_{X}H=0

Remark A.23
.

If the infinitesimal Courant algebroid automorphism corresponding to
e\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(E)
(in the sense of
[
11
, Proposition 2.7]
) preserves a generalised metric
V^{+}\subset E
, i.e.
\llbracket e,v^{+}\rrbracket\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(V^{+})
for all

, then
V^{+}
is also invariant under the
\sigma
-adjoint action

, since
\rho^{*}\,\sigma^{*}\llbracket e,\sigma\,\rho(v^{+})\rrbracket=0
and hence
\mathsf{ad}_{e}^{\sigma}
acts through the usual Dorfman bracket on
\mathsf{\Gamma}(V^{+})
. For instance, if

\pounds_{X}(g+b)=0

Lemma A.25
.

If
V^{+}\subset E
is a
D
-invariant generalised metric and
v
is a
D
-invariant section of
E
, then the projections
v^{\pm}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(V^{\pm})
are also
D
-invariant.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{ad}^{\sigma}_{X}\,v=0
and
\mathsf{ad}^{\sigma}_{X}\,v^{+}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(V^{+})
for all
X\in\mathfrak{k}_{\tau}
, it follows that
\mathsf{ad}^{\sigma}_{X}\,v^{-}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(V^{+})
. Thus for any
w^{+}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(V^{+})
, we have

(A.26)

\displaystyle\langle\mathsf{ad}^{\sigma}_{X}\,v^{-},w^{+}\rangle=\pounds_{X}\langle v^{-},w^{+}\rangle-\langle v^{-},\mathsf{ad}^{\sigma}_{X}\,w^{+}\rangle=0\ .

Hence
\mathsf{ad}^{\sigma}_{X}\,v^{-}
must vanish, and so then must
\mathsf{ad}^{\sigma}_{X}\,v^{+}
.
∎

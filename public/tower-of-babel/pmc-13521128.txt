The burden of anemia related to inflammatory bowel disease in China: Epidemic trends, subtype differences and driving factors
Authors: Qingyu Song, Yin Shi, Shengda Tian, Yanting Li, Jiajia Yang, Junlan He, Guoliang Shao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study provides the first systematic assessment of the disease burden of inflammatory bowel disease–related anemia (IBD-anemia) and its major subtypes in China, aiming to generate robust evidence to inform targeted prevention and control strategies.MethodsWe comprehensively evaluated trends in prevalence and years lived with disability of IBD-anemia in China from 1990 to 2023. Disease burden was further disaggregated into anemia attributable to Crohn's disease (CD-anemia) and anemia attributable to ulcerative colitis (UC-anemia). Analyses were stratified by sex and age. Decomposition analyses were conducted to quantify the relative contributions of population growth, population ageing, and epidemiological change to changes in disease burden.ResultsIn 2023, the total number of prevalent cases of IBD-anemia in China reached 38,550, with a significantly higher burden observed among females than males. At the subtype level, UC-anemia accounted for the majority of the overall burden, whereas CD-anemia exhibited a distinct sex-specific pattern, characterized by increasing age-standardized prevalence rates among females but declining rates among males. Age- and sex-stratified analyses identified women aged 35–59 years as the core affected population. Decomposition analyses indicated that population ageing was the predominant driver of the increasing burden of IBD-anemia, while the contribution of epidemiological changes differed between CD-anemia and UC-anemia.ConclusionsFuture efforts should prioritize the reallocation of healthcare resources and strengthened cross-sectoral collaboration to establish a three-dimensional prevention and control framework encompassing risk mitigation, targeted management of high-risk populations, and improved diagnostic and treatment security, in order to reduce the burden of IBD and its anemia-related complications.

Introduction

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a group of chronic, relapsing, non-specific inflammatory disorders of the gastrointestinal tract, primarily comprising ulcerative colitis (UC) and Crohn's disease (CD). These two entities differ substantially in terms of disease extent, depth of intestinal involvement, and lesion continuity [1]. The pathogenesis of IBD is multifactorial, involving complex interactions among genetic susceptibility, environmental triggers, dysregulated immune responses, and alterations in the gut microbiota [2].The clinical impact of IBD spans the entire life course. In addition to intestinal complications such as perforation, massive gastrointestinal bleeding, intestinal strictures, and colorectal cancer [3]; Extraintestinal manifestations (EIMs) may affect multiple organ systems, including the hematologic, musculoskeletal, mucocutaneous, ocular, and hepatobiliary systems [4]. According to the 2024 European Crohn's and Colitis Organisation (ECCO) guidelines, approximately 50% of patients with IBD experience at least one EIMs during the disease course, among which anemia represents one of the most common and clinically significant manifestations [5].

Anemia attributable to IBD (IBD-anemia) is typically multifactorial in origin, resulting from a combination of iron deficiency, chronic inflammation, deficiencies in vitamin B12 and folate, and treatment-related toxicities [6]. Epidemiological evidence indicates a sustained increase in the prevalence of IBD-related anemia between 1990 and 2019, with the global number of affected individuals reaching approximately 1.5 million by 2019 [7]. Persistent, inadequately treated anemia not only directly impairs health-related quality of life [8]—manifesting as fatigue, reduced exercise tolerance, mood disturbances, and cognitive impairment—but also adversely influences disease activity, therapeutic response, and long-term prognosis. This creates a vicious cycle of “IBD–anemia–worsened outcomes,” ultimately contributing to escalating healthcare utilization and socioeconomic burden [9,10].

Over the past three decades, China has experienced a rapid rise in IBD incidence and a continuous accumulation of prevalent cases [11]. When coupled with population ageing [12], shifts in dietary patterns [13,14], and improvements in diagnostic capacity and healthcare access, the current status and future trajectory of IBD-anemia in China remain highly uncertain. Despite this, comprehensive assessments of its long-term trends and future burden are still lacking.Therefore, using data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2023 study, we conducted the first systematic evaluation of temporal trends and epidemiological characteristics of IBD-anemia in China from 1990 to 2023. The overall burden was disaggregated into two distinct trajectories—anemia attributable to Crohn's disease (CD-anemia) and anemia attributable to ulcerative colitis (UC-anemia)—with stratified analyses by age and sex. Furthermore, to elucidate the underlying drivers of burden changes, we applied decomposition analysis for the first time in this patient population, quantifying the relative contributions of population growth, population ageing, and epidemiological change. Finally, we integrated a Bayesian age–period–cohort (BAPC) model to project the future burden of IBD-anemia and its subtypes in China from 2024 to 2035, providing quantitative evidence to support optimized healthcare resource allocation and forward-looking prevention and control strategies.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition

The GBD 2023 study represents the most comprehensive global epidemiological observational study to date. In this study, estimates of the prevalence and years lived with disability (YLDs) of IBD-anemia and its subtypes in China were obtained from the Global Health Data Exchange Results Tool (http://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-results-tool), together with their corresponding 95% uncertainty intervals (UI).

Methods

This study evaluated prevalence and YLDs rates using age-standardized rates (ASRs) and their corresponding 95% uncertainty intervals, with the 95% UI used to reflect the reliability of the data and the robustness of the model [15]. ASRs represent estimates per 100,000 population and facilitate comparisons between populations by accounting for differences in age distribution and population size, thereby improving the precision of population-level comparisons. These rates were calculated using the following formula:ASR=∑i=1Aaiwi∑i=1Awi×100,000Where A denotes the number of age groups, i denotes the ith age group, ai denotes the rate to be standardized, and wi denotes the number of standardized population of the same age.

Estimated Annual Percentage Change (EAPC) to analyze the trend over time was calculated from the regression model: Y is the natural logarithm of the ASR, α is the intercept, β is a variable that determines the positive or negative trend of the ASR, X denotes the calendar year, and ε is the error term [16]. The EAPC and its 95% Confidence Interval (CI) are also derived from the model and calculated as EAPC = 100*(exp (β)-1).

Decomposition analysis is useful for evaluating the underlying factors driving changes in disease burden over time. This method separates changes in burden into three components: epidemiological change [17], population growth, and population ageing. It quantifies the contributions of these three factors to provide a comprehensive understanding of the dynamics of IBD-anemia and its subtypes in China during the study period. This approach allows us to determine whether increases or decreases in the burden of IBD-anemia and its subtypes are primarily attributable to demographic changes or to changes in the underlying disease risk. Such information is particularly valuable for informing targeted interventions, as it clarifies whether public health efforts should focus on modifying risk factors, addressing demographic transitions, or both.

The Bayesian age–period–cohort (BAPC) model is a widely used method in epidemiology and biostatistics for analyzing temporal trends in prevalence and YLDs [18]. This approach integrates observed data with prior information to generate unique parameter estimates, thereby producing robust and reliable results. In this study, the BAPC package was applied to project trends in the prevalence and YLDs of IBD-anemia and its subtypes from 2022 to 2035. The findings derived from this modeling approach can inform the development of public health policies aimed at guiding prevention and management strategies for IBD-anemia. Model goodness-of-fit was assessed using the Deviance Information Criterion.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses and data visualization were performed using R (version 4.4.2) and JD_JD_GBDR-2023 (version 2.7.6; Jingding Medical Technology Co., Ltd.). A two-sided significance level of α = 0.05 was applied.

In the Chinese population, the burden of UC-anemia was substantially higher than that of CD-anemia, a pattern observed in both males and females (Table 1). In 2023, the total number of prevalent UC-anemia cases was 23,546 (95% UI: 15,989–37,191), including 9354 cases among males (95% UI: 6898–13,488) and 14,191 cases among females (95% UI: 8890–23,250). The overall ASPR declined from 1.42 per 100,000 (95% UI: 0.93–2.18) in 1990 to 1.21 per 100,000 (95% UI: 0.83–1.90) in 2023, with an EAPC of −0.53 (95% CI: −1.18 to 0.12). In 2023, the ASPR was 0.94 per 100,000 (95% UI: 0.70–1.33) among males and 1.51 per 100,000 (95% UI: 0.95–2.45) among females. The decline was more pronounced among males [EAPC = −0.64 (95% UI: −1.25 to 0.00)] than among females [EAPC = −0.37 (95% UI: −0.98 to 0.25)].From 1990 to 2023, YLDs due to UC-anemia increased in both sexes, reaching 147 (95% UI: 79–255) among males and 356 (95% UI: 195–576) among females, with a total of 503 YLDs (95% UI: 287–793). During the same period, ASYR declined to 0.01 per 100,000 (95% UI: 0.01–0.03) among males, 0.04 per 100,000 (95% UI: 0.02–0.06) among females, and 0.03 per 100,000 (95% UI: 0.02–0.04) in the overall population.

Under the dominance of UC-anemia, IBD-anemia in China exhibited a characteristic pattern of increasing crude prevalence and YLDs rates alongside declining ASPR and ASYR (Table 1). In 2023, the total number of prevalent IBD-anemia cases reached 38,550 (95% UI: 26,663–58,929), with a substantially higher burden among females (24,164 cases; 95% UI: 15,250–40,349) than among males (14,386 cases; 95% UI: 10,869–19,477). The overall ASPR declined from 2.21 per 100,000 (95% UI: 1.48–3.31) in 1990 to 2.05 per 100,000 (95% UI: 1.43–3.24) in 2023, with an EAPC of −0.22 (95% CI: −0.78 to 0.34). The decrease was more pronounced among males [EAPC = −0.47 (95% UI: −0.92 to 0.00)] than among females [EAPC = −0.05 (95% UI: −0.56 to 0.46)].In 2023, total YLDs due to IBD-anemia were 829 (95% UI: 510–1312), with females accounting for 604 YLDs (95% UI: 358–982), significantly higher than males 224(95% UI: 128–383). The ASYR was 0.02 per 100,000 (95% UI: 0.01–0.04) among males, 0.07 per 100,000 (95% UI: 0.04–0.11) among females, and 0.04 per 100,000 (95% UI: 0.03–0.07) in the total population.

Discussion

Using data from the GBD 2023 study, this study provides the first systematic assessment of the epidemiological profile of IBD-anemia and its subtypes in China from 1990 to 2023. Focusing on prevalence and YLDs, we conducted sex- and age-stratified analyses and applied decomposition analysis to quantify the differential contributions of population ageing, population growth, and epidemiological change to the observed disease burden. In addition, future trends were projected through 2035. Together, these analyses offer multidimensional, evidence-based insights to inform the development of precision-oriented prevention and control strategies.

Burden of IBD-anemia in China: UC-anemia dominance and sex-specific patterns in CD-anemia

Population-level data from China indicate that, in both males and females, the crude and age-standardized prevalence and YLDs rates of UC-anemia are consistently higher than those of CD-anemia. Consequently, the overall burden and temporal trends of IBD-anemia in China are largely driven by UC-anemia. Anemia has been shown to be associated with disease behavior in Crohn's disease and with disease extent in ulcerative colitis [19]. Although individuals with CD may have a higher individual risk of developing anemia [20], the substantially larger population base of UC in China results in a markedly greater absolute number of UC-anemia cases [21], thereby elevating its contribution to the overall population burden.

The crude prevalence and YLDs rates of UC-anemia have continued to increase over time. Decomposition analysis suggests that population ageing and population growth are the primary drivers of this increase. As UC is a chronic, lifelong condition, population ageing leads to an accumulating “stock effect” of prevalent cases, while population growth further expands the absolute number of affected individuals. In addition, improvements in clinician awareness and advances in diagnostic techniques have contributed to increased case detection [22]. Together, these factors have driven the observed rise in crude UC-anemia rates. Meanwhile, epidemiological changes [23,24]—including the wider use of biologic therapies, systematic screening, and earlier intervention—have reduced the per-patient risk of anemia and disability weights, as reflected by declining ASPR and ASYR. These improvements have partially mitigated, but not fully reversed, the upward trends driven by demographic factors.

In contrast, divergent sex-specific trends were observed for CD-anemia, with declining ASPR among males but increasing ASPR among females. Several factors may contribute to this disparity. From a biological perspective, androgens in males can promote erythropoiesis, suppress hepcidin, and regulate iron metabolism, thereby reducing anemia risk [25]. In females, physiological factors such as menstruation and pregnancy may predispose individuals to iron deficiency, increasing susceptibility to CD-anemia. From a behavioral standpoint, declining smoking prevalence among males—given that smoking is a well-established risk factor for CD exacerbation—may indirectly reduce disease activity and anemia risk [26]. Conversely, increasing consumption of ultra-processed foods and Western-style diets among females may promote gut microbiota dysbiosis, exacerbate intestinal inflammation, and impair iron absorption [27]. In addition, gender-related differences in health-seeking behavior, treatment adherence, and nutritional supplementation strategies may further influence these trends [28]. Because the prevalence of CD-anemia is substantially higher among females, female trends dominate the overall pattern, leading to an increase in the total ASPR of CD-anemia. Projections suggest that the ASPR of CD-anemia will continue to rise through 2035, underscoring the need for optimized healthcare resource allocation and more proactive anemia screening and intervention among female patients with Crohn's disease.

Age- and sex-driven changes in the burden of IBD-anemia in China

Both CD-anemia and UC-anemia show higher prevalence and YLDs rates among females than among males, a disparity that may be driven by a combination of physiological iron deficiency, hormonal differences, social role–related stressors, and inequalities in healthcare access and screening practices [29,30]. Age-specific analyses further indicate that the burden of CD-anemia is greatest among women aged 30–59 years, whereas UC-anemia displays a unimodal age distribution, peaking at 55–59 years before declining. Consequently, the core population affected by IBD-anemia in China is concentrated among women aged 35–59 years.In light of these burden characteristics, a targeted intervention framework centered on both sex and age dimensions is warranted. At the prevention level, health education initiatives delivered through mass media could enhance female health empowerment, particularly among women in rural or low-income settings. Screening programs may be extended to community and rural primary healthcare facilities, with the establishment of dedicated screening points for anemia in patients with inflammatory bowel disease, supported by targeted rural health policies. At the treatment level, anemia management strategies tailored to female physiological characteristics—such as intensified iron supplementation during menstruation—may help preserve iron stores. Combined therapeutic approaches [31,32], including intravenous iron, oral iron supplementation, and erythropoiesis-stimulating agents, could be implemented as appropriate, forming an integrated continuum of prevention, treatment, and healthcare resource support.

Among individuals aged 30 years or younger, both the number of prevalent cases and YLDs of IBD-anemia were higher in 1990 than in 2023, whereas the opposite pattern was observed in older age groups. In 1990, the absence of standardized anti-inflammatory treatment for IBD and systematic anemia management in China meant that chronic gastrointestinal bleeding, compounded by malnutrition, chronic infections, and iron deficiency, resulted in higher prevalence and YLDs of IBD-anemia in younger patients [33]. By contrast, by 2023, the widespread use of biologic agents and targeted immunotherapies had markedly suppressed intestinal inflammation and bleeding. Concurrently, intravenous iron supplementation, erythropoietin (EPO) therapy, and dietary iron fortification became more broadly implemented. Together with the expanded use of endoscopy and improved disease awareness [34,35], these advances increased screening rates for IBD among adolescents and young adults, enabling earlier intervention and preventing disease progression. Consequently, the overall anemia burden among individuals aged 30 years or younger declined substantially, with both prevalence and YLDs in 2023 falling well below 1990 levels.In contrast, the rightward shift of China's population pyramid resulted in substantially larger cohorts aged 35 years and older in 2023 than in 1990. This demographic change, combined with increased life expectancy and prolonged survival with IBD, directly expanded the population at risk. As IBD is a chronic, largely incurable condition, anemia burden accumulates with disease duration and sustained exposure to risk factors such as westernized lifestyles, urbanization, and industrialization [36]. Although advances in medical care have extended patient survival, chronic inflammation–related intestinal bleeding and impaired iron absorption often persist, further amplifying anemia burden. In addition, more comprehensive screening systems in 2023 reduced the number of undiagnosed cases, allowing the true disease burden to be more accurately captured. As a result, the burden of IBD-anemia among individuals aged 35 years and older was substantially greater in 2023 than in 1990. Overall, age-specific differences in prevalence and YLDs between 1990 and 2023 reflect the combined effects of improvements in medical care, demographic shifts, and the natural progression of the disease.

Differential effects of epidemiological change and ageing-driven burden amplification

The divergent drivers underlying the increasing numbers of prevalent cases of CD-anemia and UC-anemia in China are largely attributable to differences in the effects of epidemiological change: this factor contributed positively to the burden of CD-anemia, while partially offsetting the burden of UC-anemia. This phenomenon may be related to intrinsic disease characteristics, differences in temporal trends, variations in environmental exposures such as lifestyle and dietary patterns [37], and heterogeneity in therapeutic effectiveness [38]. However, further studies are required to elucidate the specific mechanisms involved. In contrast, the shared mitigating effect of epidemiological change on YLDs increases for both subtypes reflects synergistic improvements in population health literacy, standardization of clinical practice, enhanced disease control, and optimized comorbidity management driven by advances in healthcare.Population growth and population ageing jointly exacerbated the burden of IBD-anemia and its subtypes, with ageing emerging as the dominant contributor. Addressing this challenge requires a comprehensive approach spanning prevention, diagnosis, and long-term management. Strengthening early screening and high-risk population management, optimizing diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for older patients with IBD and anemia, implementing integrated nutritional support and inflammation control, and enhancing coordinated management of comorbidities and social support systems are essential to systematically reduce IBD incidence and anemia risk in older populations.

Translating evidence into stratified policy action

A comprehensive prevention and control framework should be established through targeted healthcare resource allocation and cross-sectoral collaboration. First, risk factors should be addressed by promoting healthy diets, lifestyle modification, and protection of gut microbiota to reduce upstream drivers of IBD. Second, precision management of key populations is required: screening programs should be extended and tailored for middle-aged and younger women, with enhanced iron supplementation during physiological periods and strengthened social support, while early screening, high-risk management, and coordinated comorbidity interventions should be prioritized for older populations. Third, diagnostic and therapeutic security should be strengthened through dissemination of appropriate technologies and implementation of standardized clinical pathways, with integrated strategies combining inflammation control and nutritional support to reduce the burden of IBD and its anemia-related complications.

Strengths and limitations

This study delineates the epidemiological characteristics of IBD-anemia and its subtypes in China and projects trends through 2035. By elucidating sex- and age-specific disparities and quantifying the differential contributions of epidemiological change, population ageing, and population growth to disease burden, this work proposes a comprehensive prevention framework encompassing risk control, precision management of key populations, and diagnostic and therapeutic security, thereby providing multidimensional evidence to inform targeted interventions.

Several limitations should be considered. First, validation of GBD estimates depends on diverse data sources that vary in quality and completeness. Although the GBD modeling framework incorporates multiple validation steps—including internal consistency checks, comparison with alternative data sources, and expert clinical review—the estimates remain model-based and subject to the quality of underlying inputs. Hospital-based data sources, which constitute a substantial component of GBD inputs, may underrepresent the true population burden when healthcare access is constrained by financial, geographic, or cultural barriers, while referral bias may lead to overrepresentation of severe cases in some settings. Despite these limitations, the GBD methodology represents the most comprehensive and systematic synthesis of available global data, enabling standardized cross-national and temporal comparisons. Future research incorporating primary epidemiological studies, particularly population-based surveys in underrepresented regions, will be essential to validate and refine these estimates.Second, the projections are based on historical trends and may not fully capture future advances in diagnostic technologies, therapeutic innovations, or changes in environmental exposures, potentially affecting the reliability of long-term forecasts. Although our findings provide important insights into the epidemiology of IBD-anemia and its subtypes in China, they should therefore be interpreted with appropriate caution, acknowledging the inherent complexity and potential sources of bias.

Conclusions

Based on data from the GBD 2023 study, this research provides a comprehensive assessment of the epidemiological characteristics and disease burden of IBD-anemia and its subtypes in China from 1990 to 2023. UC-anemia dominates the overall burden, with women aged 35–59 years representing the core affected population. Population ageing is the primary driver of the increasing burden, while the effects of epidemiological change differ between CD-anemia and UC-anemia. In response to these patterns, targeted healthcare resource allocation and cross-sectoral collaboration are required to establish a three-dimensional prevention and control framework encompassing risk mitigation, precision management of key populations, and strengthened diagnostic and therapeutic security, ultimately reducing the burden of IBD and its anemia-related complications.

Atomic decomposition of Bergman-Orlicz space on the upper complex half-plane
Jean − - Marcel Tanoh Dje and Benoît F. Sehba

Abstract
. In this work, we propose an atomic decomposition of the Bergman-Orlicz spaces on the complex upper half-plane. Using this result, we characterize Carleson embeddings with loss between Bergman-Orlicz spaces and certain Orlicz spaces. We also leverage this last result to control the composition operator between two Bergman-Orlicz spaces.



1.
Introduction and results.

In this paper, a function
\Phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is called an growth function if it is nondecreasing,
\lim_{t\to 0}\Phi(t)=\Phi(0)=0
,
\Phi(t)>0

for
t\in(0,\infty)
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\Phi(t)=\infty
. The growth function
\Phi
is said to be of upper type (resp. lower type) if there exists
p\in(0,\infty)
and a constant
C>1
such that for all
t\in[1,\infty)
(resp.
t\in[0,1]
) and
s\in[0,\infty)
,

(1.1)

\Phi(st)\leq Ct^{p}\Phi(s).

We denote by
\mathscr{U}^{p}
(resp.
\mathscr{L}_{p}
) the set of all growth functions of upper-type
p\geq 1
(resp. lower-type
0<p\leq 1
) such that the function

t\mapsto\frac{\Phi(t)}{t}
is non decreasing (resp. non-increasing) on
(0,\infty)
. We put

\mathscr{U}:=\bigcup_{p\geq 1}\mathscr{U}^{p}
(resp.
\mathscr{L}:=\bigcup_{0<p\leq 1}\mathscr{L}_{p}
).

Let
\Phi
be a growth function. We say that
\Phi
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}-
condition (or
\Phi\in\Delta_{2}
) if there exists a constant

K>1
such that

(1.2)

\Phi(2t)\leq K\Phi(t),~\forall~t>0.

When
\Phi
is convex, we say that
\Phi
satisfies
\nabla_{2}-
condition (or
\Phi\in\nabla_{2}
) if
\Phi
and its complementary function both satisfy
\Delta_{2}-
condition. Note that the complementary function of
\Phi
is the function
\Psi
defined by

\Psi(s)=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-\Phi(t)\},~\forall~s\geq 0.

We say that two growth functions
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
are equivalent (or
\Phi_{1}\sim\Phi_{2}
), if there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(1.3)

c^{-1}\Phi_{1}(c^{-1}t)\leq\Phi_{2}(t)\leq c\Phi_{1}(ct),~~\forall~t>0.

We will assume in the sequel that any element
\Phi
of
\mathscr{U}
(resp.
\mathscr{L}
) belongs to
\mathscr{C}^{1}([0,\infty))
and is
convex (resp. concave),
(see for example
[
2
,
10
,
11
,
26
]
).

Let
(X,\sum,\mu)
be a measure space and
\Phi
a growth function of lower type. The Orlicz space on
X
,
L^{\Phi}(X,d\mu)
is the space of all equivalent
classes (in the usual sense) of measurable functions
f:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
which satisfy

\|f\|_{L_{\mu}^{\Phi}}^{lux}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{X}\Phi\left(\dfrac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)d\mu(x)\leq 1\right\}<\infty.

The space
L^{\Phi}
generalizes the Lebesgue space
L^{p}
for
0<p<\infty
(see
[
7
,
16
,
23
]
).

Let
\mathbb{C}_{+}:=\left\{z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:y>0\right\}
be the upper complex half
-
plane. Let
\alpha>-1
be a real and
\Phi
a growth function of lower-type.

•

The Bergman
-
Orlicz space on
\mathbb{C_{+}}
,
A_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
is the subspace of
L^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C_{+}},dV_{\alpha})
constituted of analytic functions on
\mathbb{C_{+}}
, here
dV_{\alpha}(z):=(\Im mz)^{\alpha}dV(z)
, and
dV(z):=dxdy
if
z=x+iy
. For
F\in A^{\Phi}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
, we will simply use the notation
\|F\|_{A^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}:=\|F\|_{L_{V_{\alpha}}^{\Phi}}^{lux}
.

•

The Orlicz space of sequences on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
,
\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is the space of all sequences of complex numbers
\mu=\{\mu_{l,j}\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}
on
\mathbb{C_{+}}
such that

\|\mu\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi\left(\dfrac{|\mu_{l,j}|}{\lambda}\right)2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}\leq 1\right\}<\infty

where
\gamma
is a real number such that

(1.4)

\frac{\log\left(\frac{1+\delta^{2}/20}{1-\delta^{2}/20}\right)}{4\log 2}<\gamma<\frac{\log\left(\frac{1+\delta^{2}/4}{1-\delta^{2}/4}\right)}{4\log 2},

with
0<\delta<1
.

The space
A_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
(resp.
\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
) generalizes the Bergman space
A_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
(resp. sequence space
\ell_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
), for
0<p<\infty
that correspond to the case
\Phi(t)=t^{p}
. Note that for
F
to be in the Bergman space

, we should have that

\|F\|_{\alpha,p}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}|F(z)|^{p}dV_{\alpha}(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

For
0<\delta<1
, we call
\delta-
lattice, the sequence of complex numbers

defined by

(1.5)

z_{l,j}:=\delta^{2}l2^{\gamma j-3}+i2^{\gamma j},~~\forall~l,j\in\mathbb{Z},

where
\gamma
is the real defined in the Relation (

Our result on the atomic decomposition of the Bergman-Orlicz spaces is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
0<\delta<1
and
\Phi\in\mathscr{U}\cap\nabla_{2}
. The following assertions are satisfied.

i)

For
\mu:=\{\mu_{l,j}\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
, the function defined by

F_{\mu}(z)=c_{\alpha}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{l,j}\left(\frac{z-\overline{z_{l,j}}}{i}\right)^{-\alpha-2}2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)},~~\forall~z\in\mathbb{C}_{+},

belongs to
A_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, where
c_{\alpha}
is a constant which depends only on
\alpha
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C_{1}:=C_{\Phi,\delta,\alpha}>0
such that

(1.6)

\|F_{\mu}\|_{A^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\leq C_{1}\left\|\mu\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}.

ii)

For
F\in A_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and for
\delta
small enough, there exists a
sequence
\mu:=\{\mu_{l,j}\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}
belonging to
\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
such that

F(z)=c_{\alpha}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{l,j}\left(\frac{z-\overline{z_{l,j}}}{i}\right)^{-\alpha-2}2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)},~~\forall~z\in\mathbb{C}_{+},

where
c_{\alpha}
is a constant which depends only on
\alpha
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C_{2}:=C_{\Phi,\delta,\alpha}>0
such that

(1.7)

\|F\|_{A^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\leq C_{2}\left\|\mu\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}.

In previous works
[
10
,
12
]
, we provided necessary and sufficient conditions for a Bergman-Orlicz space
A_{\alpha}^{\Phi_{1}}
to be continuously embedded into the Orlicz space
L^{\Phi_{2}}(\mathbb{C}_{+},d\mu)
(Carleson embedding),
under the condition that the function
t\mapsto\frac{\Phi_{2}(t)}{\Phi_{1}(t)}
is increasing on
(0,\infty)
. When

\Phi_{1}(t)=t^{p}
and
\Phi_{2}(t)=t^{q}
, with
0<p,q<\infty
, the condition that
t\mapsto\frac{\Phi_{2}(t)}{\Phi_{1}(t)}
is increasing is equivalent to
p\leq q
. We notice that the generalization of the case
q<p
has not been addressed. In this work, we use the above atomic decomposition of Bergman-Orlicz spaces to investigate this open case. More precisely, we formulate necessary and sufficient conditions for the Bergman-Orlicz space
A_{\alpha}^{\Phi_{1}}
to be continuously embedded into an Orlicz space
L^{\Phi_{2}}(\mathbb{C}_{+},d\mu)
, this time under the condition that
t\mapsto\frac{\Phi_{1}(t)}{\Phi_{2}(t)}
is increasing on
(0,\infty)
(Carleson embedding with loss).

Let
\mu
be a positive measure on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
. The Berezin transform
\widetilde{\mu}
of
\mu
is the function defined by

\widetilde{\mu}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\frac{\mathrm{Im}(z)^{2+\alpha}}{|\omega-\overline{z}|^{2(2+\alpha)}}d\mu(\omega),~~\forall~z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Let
(X,\|.\|_{X})
and
(Y,\|.\|_{Y})
be two quasi-normed vector spaces. We say that
X
is continuously embedded into
Y
, if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
f\in X
,

(1.8)

\|f\|_{Y}\leq C\|f\|_{X}.

Here is our result on Carleson embedding with loss for Bergman-Orlicz spaces.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
\Phi_{1}\in\mathscr{U}\cap\nabla_{2}
and
\Phi_{2}\in\mathscr{L}\cup\mathscr{U}
and,
\mu
a positive measure on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
. Suppose that
t\mapsto\frac{\Phi_{1}(t)}{\Phi_{2}(t)}
is non-decreasing on
(0,\infty)
and

(1.9)

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Phi_{1}\circ\Phi_{2}^{-1}(s)}{s^{2}}ds\leq C\frac{\Phi_{1}\circ\Phi_{2}^{-1}(t)}{t},~~\forall~t>0,

where
C
is a constant that depends only on
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
. The following assertions are equivalent.

i)

The Bergman
-
Orlicz space
A_{\alpha}^{\Phi_{1}}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
continuously embeds into
L^{\Phi_{2}}(\mathbb{C}_{+},d\mu)
.

ii)

The Berezin transform
\widetilde{\mu}
belongs to
L^{\Phi_{3}}(\mathbb{C}_{+},dV_{\alpha})
, where
\Phi_{3}
is the complementary function of
\Phi_{1}\circ\Phi_{2}^{-1}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Condition (
1.9
) insures that the growth function
\Phi_{3}
belongs to
\mathscr{U}
and it is
\nabla_{2}
.

For
\phi:\mathbb{C}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{+}
holomorphic, the composition operator
C_{\phi}
is the operator defined for any
F
holomorphic function on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
by

C_{\phi}(F)(z)=F\circ\phi(z),~~\forall~z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Let
\phi
be as above and
\beta>-1
, we define the measure
\mu_{\phi,\beta}
by

(1.10)

\mu_{\phi,\beta}(E):=|\phi^{-1}(E)|_{\beta},

for any Borel set
E
contained in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
.

Let
(X,\|.\|_{X})
and
(Y,\|.\|_{Y})
be two quasi-normed vector spaces. We say that a operator
T
is bounded from
X
to
Y
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
f\in X
,

(1.11)

\|T(f)\|_{X}\leq C\|f\|_{Y}.

The following follows directly from Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\alpha,\beta>-1
,
\Phi_{1}\in\mathscr{U}\cap\nabla_{2}
and
\Phi_{2}\in\mathscr{L}\cup\mathscr{U}
and, let
\phi:\mathbb{C}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{+}
be a holomorphic function. Suppose that
t\mapsto\frac{\Phi_{1}(t)}{\Phi_{2}(t)}
is non-decreasing on
(0,\infty)
and Relation (

) is satisfied. The following assertions are equivalent.

i)

The composition operator
C_{\phi}:A_{\alpha}^{\Phi_{1}}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\longrightarrow A_{\beta}^{\Phi_{2}}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is bounded;

ii)

The Berezin transform
\widetilde{\mu_{\phi,\beta}}
belongs to
L^{\Phi_{3}}(\mathbb{C}_{+},dV_{\alpha})
, where
\Phi_{3}
is the complementary function of
\Phi_{1}\circ\Phi_{2}^{-1}
.

We propose the following abbreviation
\mathrm{A}\lesssim\mathrm{B}
for the inequalities

is a positive constant independent of the main parameters. If

Preliminaries.

2.1.
Some properties of growth functions.

A growth function
\Phi
is equivalent to a convex function if and only if there exists
c>1
such that for all
0<t_{1}<t_{2}
,

(2.1)

\frac{\Phi(t_{1})}{t_{1}}\leq c\frac{\Phi(ct_{2})}{t_{2}},

(see
[
16
, Lemma 1.1.1]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Phi
be a growth function of lower type
p\in(0,\infty)
. The following assertions are satisfied:

(i)

\Phi
is equivalent to a continuous and increasing growth function of lower type
p
.

(ii)

The growth function
\Phi_{p}
defined by

(2.2)

\Phi_{p}(t)=\Phi\left(t^{1/p}\right),~~\forall~t\geq 0

is equivalent to a continuous, increasing and convex growth function.

Proof.

Since
\Phi
is a growth function of lower type
p
, we have

\Phi(st)\leq cs^{p}\Phi(t),~~\forall~0<s\leq 1,~~\forall~t\geq 0,

where
c
is a constant that depends only on
p
. We deduce that

\frac{\Phi_{p}(t_{1})}{t_{1}}\leq c\frac{\Phi_{p}(t_{2})}{t_{2}},~~\forall~0<t_{1}<t_{2},

where
\Phi_{p}
is the growth function defined in (
2.2
). It follows that
\Phi_{p}
is equivalent to a convex function on
[0,\infty)
.
In particular,
\Phi
and
\Phi_{p}
are equivalent to continuous and increasing functions on
[0,\infty)
.
∎

.

\Phi_{p}

\displaystyle|F(z)-F(\omega)|

\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{l,j}|\right\|_{L^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\sim\left\|\mu\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}.

Proof.

Assume that
\mu\not\equiv 0
because there is nothing to show when
\mu\equiv 0
. We can assume also without loss of generality that
\left\|\mu\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}=1
.

Let
0\not\equiv G\in L^{\Psi}(\mathbb{C}_{+},dV_{\alpha})
such that
\|G\|_{L^{\Psi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}=1
, where
\Psi
is the complementary function of
\Phi
. For
l,j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{lj}(z)G(z)|dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mu_{l,j}|2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}|K_{\alpha}(z_{l,j},z)|G(z)|dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mu_{l,j}|\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{+}(|G|)(z_{l,j})2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(|\mu_{l,j}|)2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi\left(|\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{+}(|G|)(z_{l,j})\right)2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi\left(|\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{+}(|G|)(z_{l,j})\right)2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)},

thanks to Young’s inequality in Orlicz spaces. Let us now show that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi\left(|\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{+}(|G|)(z_{l,j})\right)2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}<\infty.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi\left(|\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{+}(|G|)(z_{l,j})|\right)2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{{}_{s_{\delta}}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Psi\left(|\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{+}(|G|)(z_{l,j})|\right)dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{{}_{s_{\delta}}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Psi\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}|K_{\alpha}(z_{l,j},\omega)||G(\omega)|dV_{\alpha}(\omega)\right)dV_{\alpha}(z),

where
s_{\delta}
is a constant in (
2.34
). Let
l,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. For
z\in\mathcal{D}_{{}_{s_{\delta}}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)
and
\omega\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle|z-\overline{\omega}|\leq|z-z_{l,j}|+|z_{l,j}-\overline{\omega}|

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{|z-\overline{\omega}|}{|z_{l,j}-\overline{\omega}|}\leq\frac{s_{\delta}\mathrm{Im}(z_{l,j})}{|z_{l,j}-\overline{\omega}|}+1\leq s_{\delta}+1\leq 2

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{|K_{\alpha}(z_{l,j},\omega)|}{|K_{\alpha}(z,\omega)|}\leq 2^{\alpha+2}.

Since
\{\mathcal{D}_{s_{\delta}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of pairwise disjoint sets, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{{}_{s_{\delta}}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Psi\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}|K_{\alpha}(z_{l,j},\omega)||G(\omega)|dV_{\alpha}(\omega)\right)dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{{}_{s_{\delta}}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Psi\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}|K_{\alpha}(z,\omega)|\frac{|K_{\alpha}(z_{l,j},\omega)|}{|K_{\alpha}(z,\omega)|}|G(\omega)|dV_{\alpha}(\omega)\right)dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{{}_{s_{\delta}}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Psi\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}|K_{\alpha}(z,\omega)||G(\omega)|dV_{\alpha}(\omega)\right)dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\cup_{j,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}_{{}_{s_{\delta}}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Psi\left(|\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{+}(|G|)(z)\right)dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\Psi\left(|\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{+}(|G|)(z)\right)dV_{\alpha}(z)\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\Psi(|G(z)|)dV_{\alpha}(z)\lesssim 1,

according to Theorem
2.16
. We deduce that, the function
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{l,j}|
belongs to
L^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+},dV_{\alpha})
and

\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{l,j}|\right\|_{L^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\lesssim\left\|\mu\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}.

Since
F_{l,j}
and
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{l,j}
are analytic functions on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
, it follows that they belong to
A_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
.

Conversely,
since
F_{l,j}
is analytic on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
, we have

\Phi\left(|F_{l,j}(z_{l,j})|\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{\mathrm{Im}(z_{l,j})^{2+\alpha}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{s_{\delta}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Phi\left(|F_{l,j}(\omega)|\right)dV_{\alpha}(\omega),

thanks to Proposition
2.5
. Moreover, it is easy to verify that
F_{l,j}(z_{l,j})=\mu_{l,j}.
Put

\lambda:=\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{l,j}|\right\|_{L^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi\left(\frac{|\mu_{l,j}|}{\lambda}\right)2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{s_{\delta}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Phi\left(\frac{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{l,j}(\omega)|}{\lambda}\right)dV_{\alpha}(\omega)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\Phi\left(\frac{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{l,j}(\omega)|}{\lambda}\right)dV_{\alpha}(\omega)\lesssim 1,

We deduce that,
\left\|\mu\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\lesssim\lambda.

∎

Proposition 2.21
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
0<\delta<1
and
\Phi\in\mathscr{U}\cap\nabla_{2}
. The sequence space
(\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C_{+}}),\|.\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux})
is a Banach space.

Proof.

Let
(\lambda^{(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Cauchy sequence of elements of
\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
, where
\lambda^{(n)}:=\{\lambda_{l,j}^{(n)}\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n,m\geq n_{\varepsilon}
,
\|\lambda^{(n)}-\lambda^{(m)}\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\leq\varepsilon.

For
l,j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
consider the function
F_{l,j}^{(n)}
defined by

F_{l,j}^{(n)}(z)=2^{\alpha+2}\lambda_{l,j}^{(n)}\left(\frac{z-\overline{z_{l,j}}}{i}\right)^{-\alpha-2}2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)},~~\forall~z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

By construction the function
F_{l,j}^{(n)}
belongs to
A_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
. Moreover,
F_{l,j}^{(n)}(z_{l,j})=\lambda_{l,j}^{(n)}
and

\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{l,j}^{(n)}|\right\|_{L^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\sim\left\|\lambda^{(n)}\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux},

thanks to Lemma
2.20
. For
n,m\geq n_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\left\|F_{l,j}^{(n)}-F_{l,j}^{(m)}\right\|_{A^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\lesssim\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{l,j}^{(n)}-F_{l,j}^{(m)}|\right\|_{L^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\lesssim\left\|\lambda^{(n)}-\lambda^{(m)}\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\lesssim\varepsilon.

We deduce that,
\left(F_{l,j}^{(n)}\right)_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
A^{\Phi}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
. Since
A^{\Phi}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
is a Banach space, there exists
F\in A^{\Phi}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
and
n_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for
n\geq n_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
,

\|F_{l,j}^{(n)}-F\|_{A^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\leq\varepsilon.

For
n\geq n_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\frac{|\lambda_{l,j}^{(n)}-F(z_{l,j})|}{\varepsilon}\right)2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}

\displaystyle=\Phi\left(\frac{|F_{l,j}^{(n)}(z_{l,j})-F(z_{l,j})|}{\varepsilon}\right)2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{D}_{s_{\delta}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Phi\left(\frac{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{l,j}^{(n)}(\omega)-F(\omega)|}{\varepsilon}\right)dV_{\alpha}(\omega),

where
s_{\delta}
is a constant in (
2.34
). It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi\left(\frac{|\lambda_{l,j}^{(n)}-F_{l,j}(z_{l,j})|}{\varepsilon}\right)2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{s_{\delta}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Phi\left(\frac{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{l,j}^{(n)}(\omega)-F(\omega)|}{\varepsilon}\right)dV_{\alpha}(\omega)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\Phi\left(\frac{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{l,j}^{(n)}(\omega)-F(\omega)|}{\varepsilon}\right)dV_{\alpha}(\omega)

\displaystyle\lesssim\liminf_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\Phi\left(\frac{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|F_{l,j}^{(n)}(\omega)-F_{l,j}^{(m)}(\omega)|}{\varepsilon}\right)dV_{\alpha}(\omega)

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

thanks to Fatou’s Lemma. We deduce that

\left\|\lambda^{(n)}-\lambda\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\lesssim\varepsilon,

where
\lambda:=\{F(z_{l,j})\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}
. It follows that the sequence
\lambda
belongs to
\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C_{+}})
. Indeed, for
n\geq n_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
,

\left\|\lambda\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\lesssim\left\|\lambda^{(n)}\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}+\left\|\lambda^{(n)}-\lambda\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\lesssim\left\|\lambda^{(n)}\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}+\varepsilon<\infty.

We then conclude that the sequence space
(\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C_{+}}),\|.\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux})
is a Banach space.
∎

Let
\alpha>-1
,
0<\delta<1
and
\Phi\in\mathscr{U}\cap\nabla_{2}
. We introduce the following space

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+}):=\left\{F=2^{\alpha+2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{l,j}K_{\alpha}(z,z_{l,j})2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)},\textrm{for some}\quad\{\lambda_{l,j}\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\right\}.

For
F\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, we put

\|F\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{\Phi}}:=\inf\|\{\lambda_{l,j}\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}}^{lux}

where the infimum is taken over all sequences
\{\lambda_{l,j}\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
such that

F=2^{\alpha+2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{l,j}K_{\alpha}(z,z_{l,j})2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}.

We observe that endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{\Phi}}
,
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is a Banach space.

Given
F\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+}
, we denote by
F_{\lambda}
the representation of
F
associated to the sequence
\lambda=\{\lambda_{l,j}\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}
. From Lemma
2.20
, we have that
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
embeds continuously into
{A}_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
. From the same Lemma, we have that if
F_{\lambda}
and
F_{\mu}
are two representations of

, then

\|\{\mu_{l,j}\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}}^{lux}\sim\|\{\lambda_{l,j}\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}}^{lux}.

We have the following duality result.

Theorem 2.22
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
0<\delta<1
and
\Phi\in\mathscr{U}\cap\nabla_{2}
. The topological dual
\left(\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\right)^{*}
of
\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is isomorphic to
\ell_{\alpha}^{\Psi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, in the sense that, for all
T\in\left(\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\right)^{*}
, there is a unique
\mu:=\{\mu_{i,j}\}_{j,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{\alpha}^{\Psi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
such that for all
\lambda:=\{\lambda_{i,j}\}_{j,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
,

T(\lambda)=c_{\alpha}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{i,j}\overline{\mu_{i,j}}2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)},

where
\Psi
is the complementary function of
\Phi
.

Proof.

Let
T\in\left(\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\right)^{*}
. Put

T_{1}(F_{\lambda})=T\left(\lambda\right),~~\forall~\lambda:=\{\lambda_{i,j}\}_{j,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),

where
F_{\lambda}
is the function defined by

F_{\lambda}(z)=2^{\alpha+2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{l,j}K_{\alpha}(z,z_{l,j})2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)},~~\forall~z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

By construction,
T_{1}\in\left(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\right)^{*}
. Since
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
embeds continuously into
A_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, then
T_{1}
can be extended to an element of
\left(A_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\right)^{*}
with the same operator norm. We still denote this extension
T_{1}
. Thus we have that there exists a unique
G\in A_{\alpha}^{\Psi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
such that for any
F\in A_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
,

T_{1}(F)=\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}F(z)\overline{G(z)}dV_{\alpha}(z).

In particular, for any
F\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, with

F=F_{\lambda}(z)=2^{\alpha+2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{l,j}K_{\alpha}(z,z_{l,j})2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)},~~\forall~z\in\mathbb{C}_{+},

since
P_{\alpha}
reproduces functions in
A_{\alpha}^{\Psi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
(see Lemma
2.17
), we obtain

\displaystyle T_{1}(F)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}F_{\lambda}(z)\overline{G(z)}dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle=2^{\alpha+2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{l,j}2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\overline{K_{\alpha}(z_{l,j},z)G(z)}dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle=2^{\alpha+2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{l,j}\overline{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(G)(z_{l,j})}2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}=2^{\alpha+2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{l,j}\overline{G(z_{l,j})}2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}.

It follows that, for all
\lambda:=\{\lambda_{i,j}\}_{j,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, we have

T(\lambda)=T_{1}(F_{\lambda})=2^{\alpha+2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{i,j}\overline{\mu_{i,j}}2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)},

where
\mu_{i,j}:=G(z_{i,j})
, for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.4.
Averaging functions and Berezin transform on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
.

For
\alpha>-1
and
\omega\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
, the normalized reproducing kernel at
\omega
is given by

(2.44)

k_{\alpha,\omega}(z)=\frac{\mathrm{Im}(\omega)^{(2+\alpha)/2}}{(z-\overline{\omega})^{2+\alpha}},~~\forall~z\in\mathbb{C_{+}}.

It is easy to verify that

(2.45)

|k_{\alpha,\omega}(z)|^{2}\leq|K_{\alpha}(z,\omega)|,~~\forall~z,\omega\in\mathbb{C_{+}},

where
K_{\alpha}
is the function defined in (
2.28
).

Let
\mu
be a positive measure on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
and
0<s<1
. The average function
\widehat{\mu}_{s}
of
\mu
is the function defined by

\widehat{\mu}_{s}(z)=\frac{\mu(\mathcal{D}_{s}(z))}{|\mathcal{D}_{s}(z)|_{\alpha}},~~\forall~z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

We recall that the Berezin transform
\widetilde{\mu}
of the measure
\mu
is the function defined for any

\omega\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
by

\widetilde{\mu}(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}|k_{\alpha,\omega}(z)|^{2}d\mu(z).

We have the following.

Proposition 2.23
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
\mu
a positive measure on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
. For
0<s<1
, there exists a constant
C_{1}:=C_{s,\alpha}>0
such that

(2.46)

\widehat{\mu}_{s}(z)\leq C_{1}\widetilde{\mu}(z).

Conversely, for
s
small enough, there exists a constant
C_{2}:=C_{s,\alpha}>0
such that

(2.47)

\widetilde{\mu}(z)\leq C_{2}\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{+}(\widehat{\mu}_{s})(z).

Proof.

For
z\in\mathbb{C_{+}}
, we have:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{s}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu(\mathcal{D}_{s}(z))}{|\mathcal{D}_{s}(z)|_{\alpha}}=\int_{\mathbb{C_{+}}}\frac{|\mathcal{D}_{s}(z)|_{\alpha}}{\left(|\mathcal{D}_{s}(z)|_{\alpha}\right)^{2}}\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{s}(z)}(\omega)d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\mathbb{C_{+}}}\frac{\mathrm{Im}(\omega)^{2+\alpha}}{|\omega-\overline{z}|^{2(2+\alpha)}}\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{s}(z)}(\omega)d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{C_{+}}}\frac{\mathrm{Im}(\omega)^{2+\alpha}}{|\omega-\overline{z}|^{2(2+\alpha)}}d\mu(\omega)=\widetilde{\mu}(z).

Conversely, let us choose
s
such that
(1+\sqrt{2})s<1
and put
s^{\prime}:=s\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(1-s\right)\right]^{-1}
. We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{Im}(\omega)^{2+\alpha}}\int\int_{\mathcal{D}_{s}\left(\omega\right)}|k_{\alpha,z}(\xi)|^{2}dV_{\alpha}(\xi)\right)d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{Im}(\omega)^{2+\alpha}}|k_{\alpha,z}(\xi)|^{2}\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{s}\left(\omega\right)}(\xi)dV_{\alpha}(\xi)\right)d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}|k_{\alpha,z}(\xi)|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{Im}(\xi)^{2+\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{s^{\prime}}\left(\xi\right)}(\omega)d\mu(\omega)\right)dV_{\alpha}(\xi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}|k_{\alpha,z}(\xi)|^{2}\widehat{\mu}_{s^{\prime}}(\xi)dV_{\alpha}(\xi)\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}|K_{\alpha}(\xi,z)|\widehat{\mu}_{s^{\prime}}(\xi)dV_{\alpha}(\xi)=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{+}(\widehat{\mu}_{s^{\prime}})(z),

thanks to Fubbini’s Theorem and, inequlities (
2.9
) and (
2.45
).
∎

2.23
and Theorem
2.16
. Therefore, the proof will be omitted.

Lemma 2.25
.

i)

\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\left(z_{l,j}\right)\subset\mathcal{D}_{s}\left(z\right)\hskip 14.22636pt\textrm{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\left(z\right)\subset\mathcal{D}_{s}\left(z_{l,j}\right).

Moreover,

|\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(z_{l,j})|_{\alpha}\sim|\mathcal{D}_{s}\left(z\right)|_{\alpha}\sim|\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(z)|_{\alpha}\sim|\mathcal{D}_{s}(z_{l,j})|_{\alpha},

thanks to relation (

). It follows that

(2.51)

\widehat{\mu}_{\delta}(z_{l,j})=\frac{\mu(\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(z_{l,j}))}{|\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(z_{l,j})|_{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{\mu(\mathcal{D}_{s}\left(z\right))}{|\mathcal{D}_{s}\left(z\right)|_{\alpha}}=\widehat{\mu}_{s}(z)

and

(2.52)

\widehat{\mu}_{\delta}(z)=\frac{\mu(\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(z))}{|\mathcal{D}_{\delta}(z)|_{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{\mu(\mathcal{D}_{s}\left(z_{l,j}\right))}{|\mathcal{D}_{s}\left(z_{l,j}\right)|_{\alpha}}=\widehat{\mu}_{s}(z_{l,j}).

Since the disks
\mathcal{D}_{s_{\delta}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)
are pairwise disjoint, and each

is contained in the disk
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\left(z_{l,j}\right)
, where

is the constant defined in the relation (

), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi\left(|\widehat{\mu}_{\delta}(z_{l,j})|\right)2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{s_{\delta}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Phi\left(|\widehat{\mu}_{\delta}(z_{l,j})|\right)dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{s_{\delta}}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Phi\left(|\widehat{\mu}_{s}(z)|\right)dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\Phi\left(|\widehat{\mu}_{s}(z)|\right)dV_{\alpha}(z)\lesssim 1.

ii)
Suppose that
\{\widehat{\mu}_{s}(z_{l,j})\}_{l,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{\alpha}^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, then
\widehat{\mu}_{\delta}\in L^{\Phi}(\mathbb{C_{+}},dV_{\alpha})
. We can also assume without loss of generality that
\mu\not\equiv 0
and
\left\|\{\widehat{\mu}_{s}(z_{l,j})\}\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}=1
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\Phi\left(|\widehat{\mu}_{\delta}(z)|\right)dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Phi\left(|\widehat{\mu}_{\delta}(z)|\right)dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\left(z_{l,j}\right)}\Phi\left(|\widehat{\mu}_{s}(z_{l,j})|\right)dV_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi\left(|\widehat{\mu}_{s}(z_{l,j})|\right)2^{j\gamma(\alpha+2)}\lesssim 1.

Lemma 3.1
.

(3.1)

\|F\|_{A^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}\leq C\left\|\mu\right\|_{\ell^{\Phi}_{\alpha}}^{lux}.

Consider

, the map defined by

, according to Lemma
2.20
. It just remains to prove the surjectivity of

.

, the map defined by

. Moreover,

We deduce that,

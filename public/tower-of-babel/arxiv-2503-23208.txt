Blow-up and global mild solutions for a Hardy-Hénon parabolic equation on the Heisenberg group
Ricardo Castillo, Ricardo Freire, Miguel Loayza

Abstract
We are concerned with the existence of global and blow-up solutions for the nonlinear parabolic problem described by the Hardy-Hénon equation u t − Δ ℍ u = | ⋅ | ℍ γ u p in ℍ N × ( 0 , T ) , \displaystyle u_{t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u=|\cdot|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}u^{p}\mbox{ in }\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T), where ℍ N \displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N} is the N \displaystyle N -dimensional Heisenberg group, and the singular term | ⋅ | ℍ γ \displaystyle|\cdot|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma} is given by the Korányi norm. Our study focuses on nonnegative solutions. We establish that for γ ≥ 0 \displaystyle\gamma\geq 0 , the Fujita critical exponent is p c = 1 + ( 2 + γ ) / Q \displaystyle p_{c}=1+(2+\gamma)/Q , where Q = 2 ​ N + 2 \displaystyle Q=2N+2 is the homogeneous dimension of ℍ N \displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N} . For γ < 0 \displaystyle\gamma<0 , the solutions blow up for 1 < p < 1 + ( 2 + γ ) / Q \displaystyle 1<p<1+(2+\gamma)/Q , while global solutions exist for p > 1 + ( 2 + γ ) / ( Q + γ ) \displaystyle p>1+(2+\gamma)/(Q+\gamma) . In particular, our results coincide with the results found by Georgiev and Palmieri in [ 17 ] for γ = 0 \displaystyle\gamma=0 . Introduction

Let
\displaystyle T>0
,
\displaystyle p>1
and
\displaystyle\gamma>-2
. We consider the following semilinear parabolic problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}u_{t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u&=|\cdot|^{\gamma}_{\mathbb{H}}u^{p}&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
{u}(0)&={u}_{0}&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{H}^{N},\\
\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
is the
\displaystyle N
-dimensional Heisenberg group, and
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{\mathbb{H}}
is the Korányi or an equivalent symmetric homogeneous norm. When
\displaystyle\gamma<0
, equation (
1
) is known as the Hardy parabolic equation, while for
\displaystyle\gamma>0
, it is called the Hénon parabolic equation.

In the Euclidean frame
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the elliptic version of (
1
) is
\displaystyle-\Delta u=|\cdot|^{\gamma}u^{p}
which was introduced by Hénon as a model to study rotating stellar systems (see
[
23
]
), and has been extensively studied in mathematics, particularly in nonlinear analysis and variational methods (see
[
21
]
). In addition, numerous researchers have spent decades determining the so-called Fujita critical exponent
\displaystyle p_{c}
for problems related to (
1
). The exponent
\displaystyle p_{c}
is critical in the following sense: if
\displaystyle 1<p\leq p_{c}
, then problem (
1
) does not admit a global solution (blow-up solution), while for
\displaystyle p>p_{c}
, global solutions exist (see
[
15
]
and
[
33
]
). It was determined that
\displaystyle p_{c}=1+(2+\gamma)/N
is the Fujita critical exponent for the problem (
1
) (when
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
is replaced by
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
), see
[
32
]
,
[
36
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
30
]
(non singular case), and the references therein.

When
\displaystyle\gamma=0
, problem (
1
) was studied, within the Carnot groups framework, by Andrea Pascucci in
[
29
]
who showed that the Fujita critical exponent is
\displaystyle p_{c}=1+2/Q
, where
\displaystyle Q
represents the homogeneous dimension of the Carnot group. More recently, within the context of Heisenberg groups, Georgiev and Palmieri also derived the same exponent with
\displaystyle Q=2N+2
(see
[
17
]
). They used the test function method to prove the nonexistence of global solutions for
\displaystyle p\leq 1+2/Q
and established the global existence for small initial data in a specific class of weighted
\displaystyle L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
spaces when
\displaystyle p>1+2/Q
. Several authors have addressed the existence and nonexistence of solutions relative to the homogeneous dimension of the Heisenberg group; see for instance,
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
35
]

Consequently, the critical exponent for the problem (

\displaystyle Q=2N+2
. Pohozaev and Véron confirmed this dependency in their work, as evidenced in
[
31
]
, where they obtained nonexistence results for elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic equations within the Heisenberg group, including the equation (
1

1
) does not admit weak non-trivial solutions in weighted space
\displaystyle L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}\times(0,\infty),|\varsigma|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}d\varsigma dt)
when
\displaystyle p<1+(2+\gamma)/Q
, see
[
31

Motivated by the aforementioned findings, our primary objective is to determine conditions for the global and nonglobal existence of mild solutions to the problem (
1

\displaystyle\gamma\geq 0
, we prove that
\displaystyle p_{c}=1+(2+\gamma)/Q

\displaystyle\gamma<0

\displaystyle 1<p\leq 1+(2+\gamma)/Q
(blow-up solution), and that there is a global solution if
\displaystyle p>1+(2+\gamma)/(Q+\gamma)

]

[

]
, and
[

[

[

[

Before presenting the results obtained, we introduce the notion of the solution considered in this work. For
\displaystyle\gamma\geq 0
, we need to consider the weighted space

L^{\infty}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{H}^{N})=\left\{\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N});\;\|\varphi\cdot\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}<+\infty\right\},

where
\displaystyle\varphi(\eta)=(1+|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}})^{\gamma/(p-1)}
. This space was used by Wang in
[
38
]
in the Euclidean context. When
\displaystyle\gamma<0
, we will use the Banach space
\displaystyle L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
.

Definition 1.1

Suppose
\displaystyle X
is either
\displaystyle L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
or
\displaystyle L^{\infty}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
and
\displaystyle u_{0}\in X
. A mild solution (or simply a solution) of the problem (
1
) is a function
\displaystyle u\in L^{\infty}([0,T],X)
that verifies the integral equation

u(t)=e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}\,|\cdot|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}\,[u(s)]^{p}ds,

(2)

for a.e.
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
. This solution can be extended to a maximal interval
\displaystyle[0,T_{\max})
. When
\displaystyle T_{\max}=\infty
, we say that
\displaystyle u
is a global solution of (
1
). Otherwise, we say that the solution blows up in finite time; see Section
3
.

The following result shows that for
\displaystyle\gamma\geq 0
, the Fujita critical exponent for problem (
1
) is given by
\displaystyle p_{c}=1+(2+\gamma)/Q
.

Theorem 1.2

Suppose
\displaystyle p>1
and
\displaystyle\gamma\geq 0
.

(i)

Let
\displaystyle 0\leq\gamma<Q(p-1)
. If

\displaystyle 1<p\leq p_{c}=1+{(2+\gamma)}/{Q}
, then all nontrivial nonnegative solutions of problem (
1
) blow up in finite time.

(ii)

If
\displaystyle p_{c}=1+{(2+\gamma)}/{Q}<p
,
then there are nontrivial and nonnegative initial data in
\displaystyle L^{\infty}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
such that the corresponding solution to problem (
1
) is global.

Remark 1.3

The condition
\displaystyle\gamma<Q(p-1)
in Theorem
1.2
(i) seems to be technical, but it appears naturally when choosing certain initial data, for example, if
\displaystyle u_{0}\neq 0
and
\displaystyle 0\leq u_{0}(\eta)\leq C(1+|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}})^{-Q}
, then
\displaystyle u_{0}\in L^{\infty}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
if
\displaystyle\gamma<Q(p-1)
.

The following result analyzes the case
\displaystyle\gamma<0
.

Theorem 1.4

Assume
\displaystyle p>1
and
\displaystyle-2<\gamma<0
.

(i)

If
\displaystyle 1<p\leq 1+{(2+\gamma)}/{Q},

then all nontrivial nonnegative solutions of problem (
1
) blow up in finite time.

(ii)

If
\displaystyle 1+{(2+\gamma)}/{(Q+\gamma)}<p
, then there are nontrivial and nonnegative initial data in
\displaystyle L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
such that the corresponding solution to problem (
1
) is global.

Remark 1.5

Global solutions are difficult to find when
\displaystyle\gamma<0
and
\displaystyle 1+(2+\gamma)/Q<p
. In
[
4
, Remark 3.2]
, Birindelli and Capuzzo studied the elliptic case of problem (
1
) and found stationary solutions of the form
\displaystyle u(\eta)=C_{\varepsilon}(1+|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}})^{-\frac{(Q-2-\varepsilon)}{2}}
, for
\displaystyle 1+{(2+\gamma)}/{(Q-2-\varepsilon)}\leq p
. When
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
is replaced by
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the Fujita critical exponent for problem (
1
) with
\displaystyle-2<\gamma<0
is
\displaystyle p_{c}=1+(2+\gamma)/N
(see
[
38
]
,
[
32
]
). We expected to find nontrivial global solutions for
\displaystyle 1+{(2+\gamma)}/{Q}<p
in item
\displaystyle(ii)
of Theorem

Additionally, due to the lack of homogeneity in the Korányi norm, we were unable to obtain accurate estimates (without using Hölder’s inequality) on the expression
\displaystyle e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}[|\cdot|^{\gamma}_{\mathbb{H}}e^{\sigma\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}u_{0}]
,
similar to the estimate obtained by Castillo et al. in the Euclidean frame, as noted in
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
. This leaves the open problem of finding nontrivial global solutions for

p\in\left(1+\frac{2+\gamma}{Q},1+\frac{2+\gamma}{Q+\gamma}\right].

Similar problems exist in some specific domains of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For example, Bandle and Levine found difficulties in determining the Fujita critical exponent, as noted in
[
3

\mathbb{R}^{N}_{k}=\left\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\;|\;x_{i}>0,\;i=1,\ldots,k\right\}

with
\displaystyle 0<k\leq N

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}_{k}

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}

•

If
\displaystyle 1<p<1+\frac{2+\gamma}{N+k}
then any nonnegative and nontrivial solution of (

•

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2+\gamma}{N+k-2}<p<\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(N+1)/(N-3)&\mbox{ if }N\geq 4,\\
\mbox{ arbitrary}&\mbox{ if }N=2,3\\

then problem (

Note that the result is not optimal due to the gap:

This issue was recently solved by the authors of the current article in

Preliminaries

We present some concepts and results that will be useful in proving our findings.

2.1
The Heisenberg group

The Heisenberg group
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
is the space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
equipped with the group operation

(x,y,\tau)\circ(\tilde{x},\tilde{y},\tilde{\tau})=(x+\tilde{x},\,y+\tilde{y},\,\tau+\tilde{\tau}+2\left(x\cdot\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}\cdot y\right)),

(3)

where
\displaystyle\cdot
denotes the standard scalar product in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The identity element in
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
is
\displaystyle 0
and the inverse is
\displaystyle\eta^{-1}=-\eta
for any
\displaystyle\eta\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
. There is a group of dilations on
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
given by

\delta_{r}(x,y,\tau)=(rx,ry,r^{2}\tau)\quad\mbox{ for all }\,\,r>0.

A direct calculation shows that
\displaystyle\delta_{r}
is an automorphism of
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
for every
\displaystyle r>0
, and therefore
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}=(\mathbb{R}^{2N+1},\,\circ,\,\delta_{r})
is a homogeneous Lie group on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}
.

The left-invariant vector fields that span the Lie algebra
\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{n}
of
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
are given by

X_{i}=\partial_{x_{i}}+2y_{i}\partial_{\tau},\quad Y_{i}=\partial_{y_{i}}-2x_{i}\partial_{\tau},\quad\mathcal{T}=\partial_{\tau},\quad i=1,\cdots,N.

Since
\displaystyle[X_{i},Y_{i}]=-4\partial_{\tau}
and
\displaystyle[X_{i},X_{j}]=[Y_{i},Y_{j}]=[X_{i},Y_{j}]=0
for all
\displaystyle i\neq j
, we have that the Lie algebra of
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
has stratification

\mathfrak{h}_{n}=\mbox{span}\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{N},Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{N}\}\oplus\mbox{span}\{\mathcal{T}\}.

Therefore,
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
is a
\displaystyle 2
-step stratified Lie group with
\displaystyle 2N
generators. The Heisenberg gradient is
\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{n})
and the sub-Laplacian on
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
(also referred to as Kohn Laplacian) is defined as

\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{2}+Y_{i}^{2}.

An explicit computation gives the expression

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{i}}+\partial^{2}_{y_{i}y_{i}}\right)+4(|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\partial_{\tau\tau}^{2}+4\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(y_{i}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}\tau}-x_{i}\partial^{2}_{y_{i}\tau}\right).\end{array}

The operator
\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}
is a degenerate elliptic operator. Since the Lie algebra of
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
fulfills the Hörmander condition, it is hypoelliptic (see
[
24
]
). In sub-Riemannian geometry, stratified groups appear naturally. The Heisenberg group stands as a significant example of a sub-Riemannian manifold. Here, the concept of distance arises from a sub-Riemannian metric known as the Carnot-Carathéodory distance. This metric connects points in
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
through curves with tangent vectors in the span of
\displaystyle\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{N},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N}\}
. The Carathéodory–Chow–Rashevsky Theorem ensures the existence of such curves connecting any two points in
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
(see
[
7
, Theorem 3.5.2]
). A symmetric homogeneous norm in the group
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
is a continuous function
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{\mathbb{H}}:\mathbb{H}^{N}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
such that

•

\displaystyle|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}=0
if and only if
\displaystyle\eta=0,

•

\displaystyle|\delta_{\lambda}\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}=\lambda|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}
for all
\displaystyle\eta\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
,
\displaystyle\lambda>0,

•

\displaystyle|\eta^{-1}|_{\mathbb{H}}=|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}
for all
\displaystyle\eta\in\mathbb{H}^{N}.

It is established in
[
5
, Proposition 5.1.4]
that any two homogeneous norms in
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
are equivalent. Moreover,
[
5
, Theorem 5.2.8]
shows that the Carnot-Carathéodory distance is a symmetric homogeneous norm on
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
. Consequently, we may consider the following homogeneous norm on
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
(or an equivalent one):

|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}=\left[\left(|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}\right)^{2}+|\tau|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}.

(4)

This gauge,
\displaystyle|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}
, defined in (
4
) is commonly referred to as the Korányi norm, is equivalent to the Carnot-Carathéodory distance. Although it does not mirror the sub-Riemannian structure of
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
, it proves to be very useful for calculations. Furthermore,
\displaystyle|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}
satisfies the triangle inequality
\displaystyle|\eta\circ\xi|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}+|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}}
, as seen in
[
5
, Proposition 5.1.8]
. Through the group action, we can define a metric on
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
as follows:

d(\eta,\xi)=|\xi^{-1}\circ\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}.

This metric induces a topology on
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
. Thus, we can define the Heisenberg ball of
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
, centered at
\displaystyle\eta
and with radius
\displaystyle r>0
, as
\displaystyle B_{\mathbb{H}}(\eta,r)=\{\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{N}\mid d(\eta,\xi)<r\}.

In
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}
, the Lebesgue measure remains invariant under the right-left translations of the Heisenberg group (see
[
5
, Proposition 1.3.21]
). Consequently, since Haar measures on Lie groups are uniquely determined up to a constant factor, we represent the Haar measure on
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
by
\displaystyle d\varsigma
, which coincides with the
\displaystyle 2N+1
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Additionally, the Lebesgue measure is homogeneous relative to the dilations
\displaystyle\delta_{r}
for any
\displaystyle r>0
. This is expressed as, for a measurable set
\displaystyle E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}
with Lebesgue measure
\displaystyle|E|
, we have

|\delta_{r}(E)|=r^{Q}|E|.

(5)

Here, the exponent
\displaystyle Q:=2N+2
denotes the homogeneous dimension of
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
. The groups exhibiting the property described in (
5
) are known as groups of polynomial growth. Hence, the Heisenberg group is identified as a 2-step stratified Lie group with polynomial growth, where the homogeneous dimension is given by
\displaystyle Q=2N+2
.

The operator
\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}
is a self-adjoint operator on
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
(see
[
25
, Proposition 2.1]
). By
[
25
, Theorem 3.4]
exists a unique symmetric Markov semigroup of
operators
\displaystyle\{e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
,
\displaystyle 1\leq p\leq\infty
, associated with
\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}
, such that for each
\displaystyle p
and each
\displaystyle t>0
,
\displaystyle e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}
is a bounded operator on
\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
. It is well known that the semigroup
\displaystyle e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}
admits a kernel
\displaystyle h_{t}
, explicitly given by

h_{t}(x,\tau)=\dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{N+2}2^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\dfrac{\lambda}{\sinh t\lambda}\right)^{N}\exp\left\{\dfrac{-|x|^{2}\lambda}{4\tanh t\lambda}\right\}e^{i\lambda\tau}d\lambda,\quad(x,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}\times\mathbb{R}

(see
[
34
, Theorem 1.3.2]
), and the action of the semigroup is given by

e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}u(\eta)=(u\ast h_{t})(\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u(\varsigma)h_{t}(\varsigma^{-1}\circ\eta)d\varsigma=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u(\eta\circ\varsigma^{-1})h_{t}(\varsigma)d\varsigma.

The kernel
\displaystyle h_{t}(\eta)
is a
\displaystyle C^{\infty}
function in the variable
\displaystyle(t,\eta)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{H}^{N}
,
\displaystyle\|h_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}=1
for any
\displaystyle t>0
and satisfies the heat equation
\displaystyle\partial_{t}-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}=0
(see
[
37
]
,
[
25
]
). Furthermore, there exist two positive constants
\displaystyle c_{\star},C_{\star}
such that the kernel can be estimated as follows:

c_{\star}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\exp\left(-\dfrac{C_{\star}|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{t}\right)\leq h_{t}(\eta)\leq C_{\star}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\exp\left(-\dfrac{c_{\star}|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{t}\right)

(6)

for any
\displaystyle t>0
and
\displaystyle\eta\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
(see
[
37
, Theorems IV. 4.2 and IV.4.3]
).

The semigroup
\displaystyle\{e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}\}_{t>0}
is a contraction semigroup on
\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
,
\displaystyle 1\leq p\leq\infty
, which is strongly continuous for
\displaystyle p<\infty
. Due to Stein’s interpolation theorem, one can show that
\displaystyle\{e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}\}_{t>0}
is an analytic semigroup in
\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
,
\displaystyle p<\infty
(see
[
11
, Theorem 1.4.2 ]
or
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
). Furthermore, since
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
is a Lie group with polynomial growth, the semigroup
\displaystyle\{e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}\}_{t>0}
is bounded and analytic in
\displaystyle L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
(see
[
37
, Corollary VIII. 2.6]
).

\displaystyle t>0
the function
\displaystyle G:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

G(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\exp\left(-c\dfrac{|x-y|^{k}}{t}\right)(\delta+|y|)^{m}dy

attains its maximum at
\displaystyle x=0
.

Proof.
Fix
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and for each
\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1)
define
\displaystyle\psi_{x,\varepsilon}=|B(0,\varepsilon)|^{-1}\;\chi_{B(x,\varepsilon)}
. Note that the symmetric decreasing rearrangement is
\displaystyle\psi_{x,\varepsilon}^{\star}=|B(0,\varepsilon)|^{-1}\;\chi_{B(0,\varepsilon)}=\psi_{0,\varepsilon}
.

Using Riesz’s rearrangement inequality (
7
) by setting
\displaystyle g(z-y)=\exp(-c|z-y|^{k}/t)
,
\displaystyle h(y)=(\delta+|y|)^{m}
and
\displaystyle f(z)=\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{x,\varepsilon}(z)g(z-y)h(y)dzdy

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{0,\varepsilon}(z)g(z-y)h(y)dzdy.

Observe that the integral on the right-hand side is finite; therefore, by Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{x,\varepsilon}(z)g(z-y)dz\right]h(y)dy\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{0,\varepsilon}(z)g(z-y)dz\right]h(y)dy.\end{array}

(8)

Define
\displaystyle\Phi_{x,\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\begin{array}[]{rll}\Phi_{x,\varepsilon}(y)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{x,\varepsilon}(z)g(z-y)dz.\end{array}

Then,
\displaystyle\Phi_{x,\varepsilon}(y)\to g(x-y)
a.e.
\displaystyle y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
as
\displaystyle\varepsilon\to 0
. Moreover, for
\displaystyle|y|\geq 2|x|+2
and
\displaystyle|z-x|\leq\varepsilon
we have

|z-y|\geq|y-x|-|z-x|\geq|y-x|-\epsilon\geq|y|-|x|-\epsilon\geq\frac{|y|}{2},

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}|\Phi_{x,\varepsilon}(y)|&\leq|B(0,\varepsilon)|^{-1}\int_{|z-x|\leq\varepsilon}\exp\left(-c\frac{|z-y|^{k}}{t}\right)dz\\
&\leq\exp\left(-c\frac{|y|^{k}}{2^{k}t}\right).\end{array}

(9)

Let
\displaystyle\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\Psi(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h(z)&\mbox{ if }|z|\leq 2|x|+2,\\
\exp\left(-c\frac{|z|^{k}}{2^{k}t}\right)h(z)&\mbox{ if }|z|\geq 2|x|+2.\end{array}\right.

From (
9
) we see that
\displaystyle|\Phi_{x,\varepsilon}(y)h(y)|\leq\Psi(y)
for all
\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Since
\displaystyle\Psi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, letting
\displaystyle\varepsilon\to 0
in the inequality (
8
), we obtain the desired result.

The limit case
\displaystyle\delta=0
is handled as in
[
8
, Lemma 2.10]
. In fact, this follows from the case
\displaystyle\delta>0
and the monotone convergence theorem, letting
\displaystyle\delta\to 0^{+}
, since
\displaystyle-N<m\leq 0
.

\displaystyle\square

Remark 2.4

According to
[
5
, Proposition 5.1.4]
, any two homogeneous norms in
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
are equivalent. This means that the Korányi norm defined in (
4
) can be replaced with a more straightforward homogeneous norm when necessary to facilitate the calculations. This was used, for instance, in
[
17
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Lemma 2.5

Let
\displaystyle u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
and
\displaystyle 0<\gamma<Q
. Then there exists a constant
\displaystyle C>0
depending on
\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})},

\displaystyle N
and
\displaystyle\gamma
such that

\left\|t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\exp\left(-c_{\star}\dfrac{|\varsigma^{-1}\circ\eta|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{t}\right)\,|\varsigma|_{\mathbb{H}}^{-\gamma}\,u_{0}(\varsigma)\,d\varsigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\leq C\,t^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}},

(10)

for all
\displaystyle t>0.

Proof.
Note that by
[
5
, Corollary 5.4.5]
, the function
\displaystyle\varsigma\mapsto|\varsigma|_{\mathbb{H}}^{-\gamma}
is locally integrable in
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
if
\displaystyle 0<\gamma<Q
.

Recall that the natural Haar measure in
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
is identical to the Lebesgue measure
\displaystyle d\varsigma=dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}d\tau^{\prime}
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
. As it was shown in
[
17
, Proposition 4.1]
, we can argue that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\exp\left(-c_{\star}\dfrac{|\varsigma^{-1}\circ\eta|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{t}\right)\,|\varsigma|_{\mathbb{H}}^{-\gamma}\,u_{0}(\varsigma)\,d\varsigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\,t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}}\exp\left(-c_{1}\dfrac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}+|\tau-\tau^{\prime}+2(x^{\prime}\cdot y-x\cdot y^{\prime})|}{t}\right)\times\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(|x^{\prime}|+|y^{\prime}|+|\tau^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-\gamma}dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}d\tau^{\prime},\end{aligned}

(11)

where
\displaystyle\eta=(x,y,\tau)
,
\displaystyle\varsigma=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
and
\displaystyle C,c_{1}
are positive constants. Here, we have used Remark
2.4
and replaced the Korányi norm with a more straightforward homogeneous norm. Thus, using Young’s inequality, Fubini’s theorem and Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\exp\left(-c_{\star}\dfrac{|\varsigma^{-1}\circ\eta|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{t}\right)|\varsigma|_{\mathbb{H}}^{-\gamma}\,u_{0}(\varsigma)\,d\varsigma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\,t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}}\exp\left(-c_{1}\dfrac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}}{t}\right)\exp\left(\dfrac{-c_{1}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}+2(x^{\prime}\cdot y-x\cdot y^{\prime})|}{t}\right)\times\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(|x^{\prime}|+|y^{\prime}|+|\tau^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-\gamma}dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}d\tau^{\prime}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\,t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}}\exp\left(-c_{1}\dfrac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}}{t}\right)\exp\left(\dfrac{-c_{1}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}+2(x^{\prime}\cdot y-x\cdot y^{\prime})|}{t}\right)\times\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(|x^{\prime}|^{\frac{N}{Q}}|y^{\prime}|^{\frac{N}{Q}}|\tau^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{Q}}\right)^{-\gamma}dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}d\tau^{\prime}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\,t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\exp\left(-c_{1}\dfrac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}}{t}\right)|x^{\prime}|^{\frac{-\gamma N}{Q}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\exp\left(-c_{1}\dfrac{|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}}{t}\right)|y^{\prime}|^{-\frac{\gamma N}{Q}}\times\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(\dfrac{-c_{1}|\tau-[\tau^{\prime}-2(x^{\prime}\cdot y-x\cdot y^{\prime})]|}{t}\right)|\tau^{\prime}|^{-\frac{\gamma}{Q}}d\tau^{\prime}}_{{I}}\,dy^{\prime}dx^{\prime}.\\
\end{aligned}

(12)

Now, we will estimate the integral
\displaystyle I
. From Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(\dfrac{-c_{1}\left|\,[\tau+2(x^{\prime}\cdot y-x\cdot y^{\prime})]-\tau^{\prime}\,\right|}{t}\right)|\tau^{\prime}|^{-\frac{\gamma}{Q}}d\tau^{\prime}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(\dfrac{-c_{1}|\tau^{\prime}|}{t}\right)|\tau^{\prime}|^{-\frac{\gamma}{Q}}d\tau^{\prime}.\end{aligned}

Thus, from this and (
12
), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\exp\left(-c_{\star}\dfrac{|\varsigma^{-1}\circ\eta|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{t}\right)|\varsigma|_{\mathbb{H}}^{-\gamma}\,u_{0}(\varsigma)\,d\varsigma

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\,t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\exp\left(-c_{1}\dfrac{|x^{\prime}|^{2}}{t}\right)|x^{\prime}|^{\frac{-\gamma N}{Q}}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\exp\left(-c_{1}\dfrac{|y^{\prime}|^{2}}{t}\right)|y^{\prime}|^{-\frac{\gamma N}{Q}}dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(\dfrac{-c_{1}|\tau^{\prime}|}{t}\right)|\tau^{\prime}|^{-\frac{\gamma}{Q}}d\tau^{\prime}.

Hence, applying the changes of variables
\displaystyle x^{\prime}=t^{\frac{1}{2}}x
,
\displaystyle y^{\prime}=t^{\frac{1}{2}}y
, and
\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}=t\tau
, we can arrive at the following conclusion

t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\exp\left(-c_{\star}\dfrac{|\varsigma^{-1}\circ\eta|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{t}\right)|\varsigma|_{\mathbb{H}}^{-\gamma}\,u_{0}(\varsigma)\,d\varsigma\leq C\,t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}t^{\frac{Q}{2}}t^{-\gamma\left(\frac{N}{2Q}+\frac{N}{2Q}+\frac{2}{2Q}\right)}=C\,t^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}.

The proof is now complete.

\displaystyle\square

3
Local existence

The local existence for
\displaystyle\gamma\geq 0
is given in the following result.

Theorem 3.1

Assume
\displaystyle p>1,\gamma\geq 0
,
\displaystyle u_{0}\in L^{\infty}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{H}^{N}),

\displaystyle u_{0}\geq 0
,
\displaystyle u_{0}\neq 0
and
\displaystyle\varphi(\eta)=(1+|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}})^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}
. There exist
\displaystyle T>0
and a unique function
\displaystyle u\in L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{\infty}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{H}^{N}))
solution of problem (
1
). Moreover, it is possible to extend this solution to a maximal interval
\displaystyle(0,T_{\max})
so that we have the blow up alternative: either
\displaystyle T_{\max}=+\infty
or
\displaystyle T_{\max}<\infty
and
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}}\|\varphi\cdot u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}=+\infty
.

Proof.
According to
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
we have

\displaystyle h_{t}(\xi)=\lambda^{Q}h_{\lambda^{2}t}(\delta_{\lambda}(\xi)),\quad t>0,

for any
\displaystyle\lambda>0
and any
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
, where
\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}
is the anisotropic dilation (defined in Section
2
). In particular, when
\displaystyle\lambda=t^{-\frac{1}{2}}
we have

h_{t}(\xi)=t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}h_{1}(\delta_{t^{-{1}/{2}}}(\xi))

(13)

for any
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
and any
\displaystyle t>0.

We use the Banach fixed point theorem for the mapping
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:B_{K}\rightarrow X
defined by

\mathcal{F}u(t)=e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}u_{0}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}u^{p}(s)ds}_{\mathcal{G}u(t)},

(14)

where

X=L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{\infty}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{H}^{N}))=\left\{v:[0,T]\rightarrow L^{\infty}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{H}^{N});\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\varphi\cdot v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}<\infty\right\},

and

\displaystyle B_{K}=B_{K}^{T}
denotes the closed ball in
\displaystyle X
with center at
\displaystyle 0
and radius
\displaystyle K
. The positive constants
\displaystyle K
and
\displaystyle T
will be chosen so that
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
satisfies
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:B_{K}\rightarrow B_{K}
and is a strict contraction. We start by estimating
\displaystyle\|\varphi\cdot e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}
. Note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}h_{t}(\xi)u_{0}(\eta\circ\xi^{-1})d\xi=t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}h_{1}(\delta_{t^{-{1}/{2}}}(\xi))u_{0}(\eta\circ\xi^{-1})d\xi.\end{aligned}

(15)

By applying the change of variables
\displaystyle\zeta=\delta_{t^{-{1}/{2}}}(\xi)
to (
15
), which is an isomorphism on
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{N}
with
\displaystyle\delta_{r}^{-1}=\delta_{r^{-1}}
, we obtain

[e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}u_{0}](\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}h_{1}(\zeta)u_{0}(\eta\circ(\delta_{t^{{1}/{2}}}(\zeta))^{-1})d\zeta.

By the estimate (
6
)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\eta)&\leq\|\varphi\cdot u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}h_{1}(\zeta)(1+|\eta\circ(\delta_{t^{{1}/{2}}}(\zeta))^{-1}|_{\mathbb{H}})^{\frac{-\gamma}{p-1}}d\zeta\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq\|\varphi\cdot u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\left(\int_{R_{1}}+\int_{R_{2}}\right)h_{1}(\zeta)(1+|\eta\circ(\delta_{t^{{1}/{2}}}(\zeta))^{-1}|_{\mathbb{H}})^{\frac{-\gamma}{p-1}}d\zeta\\
&\mbox{ where }R_{1}=\left\{\zeta;\,|\eta\circ(\delta_{t^{{1}/{2}}}(\zeta))^{-1}|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}/2\,\right\}\mbox{ and }\\
&R_{2}=\left\{\zeta;\,|\eta\circ(\delta_{t^{{1}/{2}}}(\zeta))^{-1}|_{\mathbb{H}}\geq|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}/2\,\right\}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\|\varphi\cdot u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\left[\int_{R_{1}}e^{-c_{\star}|\zeta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}d\zeta+\left(1+\dfrac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{2}\right)^{\frac{-\gamma}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}e^{-c_{\star}|\zeta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}d\zeta\right].\end{aligned}

(16)

Using triangle inequality on the region
\displaystyle R_{1}
, we have

\dfrac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{2}\geq|\eta\circ(\delta_{t^{{1}/{2}}}(\zeta))^{-1}|_{\mathbb{H}}\geq|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}-t^{\frac{1}{2}}|\zeta|_{\mathbb{H}}.

(17)

So,
\displaystyle R_{1}\subset\left\{\zeta;|\zeta|_{\mathbb{H}}\geq t^{-\frac{1}{2}}{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}/{2}\right\}
. Using (
17
) and the fact that the Haar measure is similar to the Lebesgue measure, we can continue computing (
16
) as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\eta)&\leq C\|\varphi\cdot u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\left[\int_{R_{1}}e^{-c_{\star}|\zeta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}d\zeta+\left(1+\dfrac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{2}\right)^{\frac{-\gamma}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}e^{-c_{\star}|\zeta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}d\zeta\right]\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\|\varphi\cdot u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\left[e^{\frac{-c_{\star}|\eta|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{8t}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}e^{-c_{\star}\frac{|\zeta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{2}}d\zeta+C_{1}\left(1+\dfrac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{2}\right)^{\frac{-\gamma}{p-1}}\right]\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\|\varphi\cdot u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\left(e^{\frac{-c_{\star}|\eta|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{8t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-c_{1}\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}e^{-c_{1}\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}}e^{-c_{1}\frac{|\tau|}{2}}d\tau dydx+C_{1}(\varphi(\eta))^{-1}\right)\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\|\varphi\cdot u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\left(\varphi(\eta)e^{\frac{-c_{\star}|\eta|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{8}}C_{2}+C_{1}\right)(\varphi(\eta))^{-1}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\|\varphi\cdot u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}(\varphi(\eta))^{-1},\end{aligned}

(18)

for
\displaystyle 0<t<T<1
(note that the function
\displaystyle\eta\mapsto\varphi(\eta)e^{\frac{-c_{\star}|\eta|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{8}}
is bounded). Thus, we deduce that

\|\varphi\cdot e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\leq C\|\varphi\cdot u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})},

(19)

for
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
,
\displaystyle T<1.

Now, we will estimate
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}u(t)
arguing similarly as in the derivation of estimates
(
16
)
and
(
18
)
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\eta)&=\int_{0}^{t}\left[e^{(t-s)\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}u^{p}(s)\right](\eta)\,ds\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}h_{1}(\zeta)\,\left|\eta\circ(\delta_{(t-s)^{{1}/{2}}}(\zeta))^{-1}\right|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}\,u^{p}(\eta\circ(\delta_{(t-s)^{{1}/{2}}}(\zeta))^{-1},s)\,\,d\zeta ds\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\varphi\cdot u(s)\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}h_{1}(\zeta)\,\,\left|\eta\circ(\delta_{(t-s)^{{1}/{2}}}(\zeta))^{-1}\right|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}\\
&\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left(1+\left|\eta\circ(\delta_{(t-s)^{{1}/{2}}}(\zeta))^{-1}\right|_{\mathbb{H}}\right)^{\frac{-\gamma p}{p-1}}d\zeta ds\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq CK^{p}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}h_{1}(\zeta)\,\left(1+\left|\eta\circ(\delta_{(t-s)^{{1}/{2}}}(\zeta))^{-1}\right|_{\mathbb{H}}\right)^{\frac{-\gamma}{p-1}}d\zeta ds\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq CK^{p}\int_{0}^{t}\left(e^{-\frac{c_{\star}|\eta|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{8}}C_{2}+C_{1}\varphi^{-1}(\eta)\right)ds\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq CK^{p}T\varphi^{-1}(\eta).\end{aligned}

(20)

Based on the estimates (
19
) and (
20
), there is a constant
\displaystyle C_{3}>0
such that

\left\|\varphi\cdot\mathcal{F}u(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\leq C_{3}\left(\|\varphi\cdot u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}+K^{p}\,T\right).

(21)

Similarly, for
\displaystyle u,v\in B_{K}
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\varphi\cdot(\mathcal{F}u(t)-\mathcal{F}v(t))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\leq C_{4}K^{p-1}T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\varphi\cdot(u(t)-v(t))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\end{aligned}

(22)

for some constant
\displaystyle C_{4}>0
. Thus, choosing

\displaystyle K>2C_{3}\|\varphi\cdot u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}

and
\displaystyle 0<T<1
sufficiently small so that

\displaystyle\max\{C_{3},\,C_{4}\}K^{p-1}T<1/2,

from (
21
) and (
22
), we can conclude that
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
is a contraction in
\displaystyle B_{K}
, and it has a unique fixed point
\displaystyle u
in
\displaystyle B_{K}
, which is a local mild solution of (
1
).

The uniqueness in
\displaystyle X
follows from the following: if
\displaystyle u,v
are solutions on
\displaystyle[0,T]
of (
14
), then by performing a calculation similar to (
20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\varphi\cdot(u(t)-v(t))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|\varphi\cdot u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}^{p-1}+\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|\varphi\cdot v(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}^{p-1}\right)\times

\displaystyle\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\int_{0}^{t}\|\varphi\cdot(u(s)-v(s))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}ds.

Applying Gronwall’s inequality, it follows that
\displaystyle u=v
.

Since
\displaystyle T
depends on
\displaystyle\|\varphi\cdot u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}
the blow-up alternative follows by standard arguments (see
[
6
, Remark 2.4.2 and Theorem 2.4.3]
).

\displaystyle\square

The local existence for
\displaystyle\gamma<0
is given in the following result.

Theorem 3.2

Suppose that
\displaystyle p>1
,
\displaystyle N\geq 1
,
\displaystyle-2<\gamma<0
,
\displaystyle u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}),u_{0}\geq 0
and
\displaystyle u_{0}\neq 0
. There exist
\displaystyle T>0
and a unique function
\displaystyle u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}))
solution to the problem (
1
). Moreover, it is possible to extend this solution to a maximal interval
\displaystyle(0,T_{\max})
so that we have the blow-up alternative: either
\displaystyle T_{\max}=+\infty
or
\displaystyle T_{\max}<\infty
and
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}=+\infty
.

Proof.
Let us determine the solution in the Banach space

X=L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}))=\left\{\phi:[0,T]\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N});\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}<\infty\right\}.

Let
\displaystyle B_{K}=B_{K}^{T}
denote the closed ball in
\displaystyle X
with center
\displaystyle 0
and radius
\displaystyle K
, where
\displaystyle K>0
and
\displaystyle T>0
will be chosen so that
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
defined in (
14
) satisfies
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:B_{K}\rightarrow B_{K}
and is a strict contraction. Using the estimate (
6
) we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}[\mathcal{G}u(t)](\eta)&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}(t-s)^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\exp\left(-c_{\star}\dfrac{|\varsigma^{-1}\circ\eta|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{t-s}\right)|\varsigma|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}u^{p}(\varsigma,s)d\varsigma ds\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}^{p}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\exp\left(-c_{\star}\dfrac{|\varsigma^{-1}\circ\eta|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}{t-s}\right)|\varsigma|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}d\varsigma ds.\end{array}

Thus, from Lemma
2.5
, it follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\eta)&\leq CK^{p}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}ds\leq C_{5}K^{p}T^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}},\end{aligned}

(23)

where
\displaystyle C_{5}>0
is a constant.
Therefore

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|[\mathcal{F}u(t)](\eta)\right|&\leq|[e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}u_{0}](\eta)|+|[\mathcal{G}u(t)](\eta)|\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\|h_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}+C_{5}K^{p}T^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}+C_{5}K^{p}T^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}},\end{aligned}

that is,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\mathcal{F}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}+C_{5}K^{p}T^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}.\end{aligned}

(24)

Similarly, for
\displaystyle u,v\in B_{K}
, there is a constant
\displaystyle C_{6}>0
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\mathcal{F}u(t)-\mathcal{F}v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\leq C_{6}K^{p-1}T^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}.\end{aligned}

(25)

Now, we must choose
\displaystyle K>\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}+1
and
\displaystyle T
small enough so that
\displaystyle C_{6}K^{p-1}T^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}<1
and
\displaystyle C_{5}K^{p}T^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}<1.

Therefore, from (
24
) and (
25
), it follows that
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
is a contraction in
\displaystyle B_{K}
and possesses a unique fixed point
\displaystyle u
in
\displaystyle B_{K}
.

The uniqueness follows from

\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\leq\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\left(\|u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}^{p-1}+\|v(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}^{p-1}\right)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\gamma/2}\|u(s)-v(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}ds

and the singular Gronwall inequality (see
[
6
, Proposition A.5.7]
) since
\displaystyle{\gamma}/{2}>-1.

Since
\displaystyle T
depends on
\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}
, the proof of the blow-up alternative can be completed by standard arguments (see
[
6
, Remark 2.4.2 and Theorem 2.4.3]
).

\displaystyle\square

The following technical estimate will be used to prove the global existence of solutions.

Lemma 4.1

Let
\displaystyle\gamma\geq 0
,
\displaystyle u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
,
\displaystyle u_{0}\geq 0
,
\displaystyle u_{0}\neq 0
such that
\displaystyle u_{0}(\eta)\leq(1+|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}})^{-Q}
. Then

\|\varphi\cdot e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\leq Ct^{-\frac{Q}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2(p-1)}},

for all
\displaystyle t>1
.

Proof.
Suppose first that
\displaystyle\gamma>0
. By estimate (
6
)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (1+|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}})^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}u_{0}(\eta)&\leq C_{\star}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}(1+|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}})^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}e^{-\frac{c_{\star}|\xi^{-1}\circ\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{t}}u_{0}(\xi)d\xi\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&\leq C_{\star}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}(1+|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}})^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}\left(\int_{|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{2}}+\int_{|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}}>\frac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{2}}\right)e^{-\frac{c_{\star}|\xi^{-1}\circ\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{t}}u_{0}(\xi)d\xi\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr&=I+II.\end{aligned}

(26)

From the triangle inequality,
\displaystyle|\xi^{-1}\circ\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}\geq|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}-|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}}
, we have for
\displaystyle t>1

\displaystyle\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\displaystyle=C_{\star}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}(1+|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}})^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}\int_{|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{2}}e^{-\frac{c_{\star}|\xi^{-1}\circ\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{t}}u_{0}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C_{\star}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}(1+|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}})^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}e^{-\frac{c_{\star}|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{4t}}\int_{|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{2}}u_{0}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C_{\star}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\left(\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{t}\,\frac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}e^{-\frac{c_{\star}|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{4t}}\int_{|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{2}}u_{0}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{Q}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2(p-1)}}.

Now, using the assumption
\displaystyle u_{0}(\xi)\leq(1+|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}})^{-Q}
and arguing in a similar way to the derivation of the estimate (
10

\displaystyle\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C_{\star}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}(1+|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}})^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}\int_{|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}}>\frac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{2}}e^{-\frac{c_{\star}|\xi^{-1}\circ\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{t}}u_{0}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}C_{\star}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}}>\frac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{2}}e^{-\frac{c_{\star}|\xi^{-1}\circ\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{t}}(1+|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}})^{-Q+\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}d\xi

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}C_{\star}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}}>\frac{|\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}}{2}}e^{-\frac{c_{\star}|\xi^{-1}\circ\eta|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{t}}|\xi|_{\mathbb{H}}^{-Q+\frac{\gamma}{p-1}}d\xi

Therefore

\|\varphi\cdot e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}\leq Ct^{-\frac{Q}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2(p-1)}},

for
\displaystyle t>1

The case
\displaystyle\gamma=0

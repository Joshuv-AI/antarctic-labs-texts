Strong converse exponent of channel interconversion
Aadil Oufkir, Yongsheng Yao, Mario Berta

Abstract
In their seminal work, Bennett et al. [IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory (2002)] showed that, with sufficient shared randomness, one noisy channel can simulate another at a rate equal to the ratio of their capacities. We establish that when coding above this channel interconversion capacity, the exact strong converse exponent is characterized by a simple optimization involving the difference of the corresponding Rényi channel capacities with Hölder dual parameters. We further extend this result to the entanglement-assisted interconversion of classical–quantum channels, showing that the strong converse exponent is likewise determined by differences of sandwiched Rényi channel capacities. The converse bound is obtained by relaxing to non-signaling assisted codes and applying Hölder duality together with the data processing inequality for Rényi divergences. Achievability is proven by concatenating refined channel coding and simulation protocols that go beyond first-order capacities, attaining an exponentially small conversion error, remaining robust under small variations in the input distribution, and tolerating a sublinear gap between the conversion rates.

Overview

Introduction.

In his seminal work, Shannon
[
45
]
established that the maximum achievable rate for reliable classical information transmission through a noisy channel is characterized by a single parameter
C
, called the capacity. This fundamental quantity defines two distinct operational regimes:
For rates below capacity (
r=\frac{1}{n}\log M<C
), messages can be transmitted with exponentially vanishing error probability as the blocklength
n
increases
[
46
,
47
,
18
]
. Conversely, any attempt to communicate above capacity (
r=\frac{1}{n}\log M>C
) inevitably leads to an error probability that approaches one exponentially fast in
n

[
21
]
. This latter phenomenon is known as the
strong converse
, with the exponential decay rate of the success probability referred to as the
strong converse exponent
.
Both the capacity concept and the strong converse property were later extended by
[
26
,
44
]
and
[
58
,
39
,
36
]
to coding over channels with classical input and quantum output, so-called classical-quantum channels.

Dual to the noisy channel coding problem, channel simulation aims to reproduce a noisy channel’s behavior using noiseless communication. Remarkably, when shared randomness is available (shared entanglement for classical-quantum channels), the channel capacity
C
serves as the same fundamental threshold
[
4
,
3
,
5
]
. As in coding—though operationally inverted—simulation exhibits a sharp transition at capacity: successful simulation with exponentially vanishing error is achievable when the perfect communication rate
r=\frac{1}{n}\log M
exceeds capacity
(r>C)

[
30
,
40
,
33
,
42
]
. The strong converse occurs for rates below capacity
(r<C)
, where the simulation error is guaranteed to converge to one exponentially fast
[
4
,
3
,
40
,
33
,
42
]
. The exponential rate of this convergence defines the strong converse exponent.

Generalizing both channel coding and channel simulation, the channel interconversion problem seeks to simulate a possibly noisy target channel using communication over another possibly noisy channel. When shared randomness is allowed (shared entanglement for classical-quantum channels), the first-order asymptotic rate for this task is given by the ratio of the capacities of the channels involved
[
4
,
9
]
. In fact, this can be derived by concatenating the known protocols for channel coding and simulation, pleasantly leading to first order reversibility. However, to the best of our knowledge, little—if anything—is known about higher-order refinements for this problem, partly exactly because concatenating protocols in a black box like fashion will in general not be optimal. In this work, we take the first step by studying the strong converse exponents for the channel interconversion problem. Next, we present an overview of our results. The precise definitions of the quantities involved are given in the subsequent section.

Overview of results.

In this paper, we study the strong converse exponent for channel interconversion under the purified and total variation distances. It represents the optimal rate of exponential convergence of the conversion error towards the worst-case performance. For classical-quantum channels, the strong converse exponent for the conversion
W^{\otimes n}\rightarrow T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}
under the
purified distance
takes the form (see Theorem
12
)

\displaystyle\sup_{1<p<2}\frac{2-p}{p}\left(r\cdot\widetilde{I}_{\frac{p}{2}}(T)-\widetilde{I}_{\frac{p}{2(p-1)}}(W)\right)

(1)

where
\widetilde{I}_{\alpha}(W)
is the channel’s sandwiched Rényi mutual information of order
\alpha
, i.e.,

\widetilde{I}_{\alpha}(W)=\inf_{\sigma\,:\,\text{state}}\max_{x}\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(W_{x}\|\sigma)\qquad\forall\alpha\in(0,\infty),

(2)

and
\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}
is the sandwiched Rényi divergence of order
\alpha
, defined as

\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\operatorname{Tr}\big({\sigma}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\rho{\sigma}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\big)^{\alpha}\qquad\forall\alpha\in(0,\infty).

(3)

Notably, the exact characterization of the strong converse exponent in (
1
) is the same under the entanglement and non-signaling assistance. Furthermore, if the channel
T
is classical, shared randomness assistance is sufficient to achieve such performance. Finally, by either setting
T=\mathrm{id}
or
W=\mathrm{id}
, we neatly recover the strong converse exponent of channel coding and channel simulation, respectively.

We further characterize the strong converse exponent for the conversion of
classical
channels
W^{\times n}\rightarrow T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}
under the
total-variation distance
for any assistance ranging from shared randomness to non-signaling. This exponent takes the form (see Theorem
3
)

\displaystyle\sup_{0<\alpha<1}\sup_{1<p<\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\frac{1-p+\alpha p}{p}\left(r\cdot I_{(1-\alpha)p}(T)-I_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}}(W)\right).

(4)

Further, we prove the strong converse exponent under the most general Rényi divergence as a notion of measure. In particular, we quantify the strong converse exponent under
\mathtt{d}_{\alpha}(P\|Q)=1-(\sum_{x}P_{x}^{\alpha}Q_{x}^{1-\alpha})^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}
for
\alpha\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\sup_{1<p<\frac{1}{\alpha}}\frac{1-\alpha p}{(1-\alpha)p}\left(r\cdot I_{\alpha p}(T)-I_{\frac{(1-\alpha)p}{p-1}}(W)\right).

(5)

Similarly, by either setting
T=\mathrm{id}
or
W=\mathrm{id}
, we recover the strong converse exponent of the channel coding and the channel simulation, respectively.

Technical challenges.

In channel coding as well as channel simulation problems, higher-order refinements are typically obtained via relating the task performance to so-called meta-converses
[
52
,
57
,
20
,
34
,
54
,
9
]
. The latter can then be further tightly controlled using entropic divergences and variances in the block-length regime
[
28
,
50
,
38
,
22
,
35
,
32
,
29
]
. In contrast, in the case of direct channel interconversion, such refinements are fundamentally more challenging due to the absence of a unified meta-converse that simultaneously captures the properties of both channels involved. This lack of a tight meta-converse framework makes it unclear how to directly relate interconversion performance to entropic quantities using standard methods and addressing this challenge requires the development of novel techniques beyond the traditional meta-converse-based approach. The channel interconversion problem might also be seen as the channel-superchannel analogue to the state-channel conversion problem of quantum dichotomies (cf. the latest
[
6
]
and references therein). Nevertheless, determining the strong converse exponents for the former is significantly more challenging than for the latter.

Technical contributions.

We derive the achievability part of (
1
) and (
4
) starting from ideas first outlined in
[
14
]
and
[
13
, Problem 2.5.16]
for classical channel coding. The methodology was enhanced to prove the strong converse exponent for classical-quantum
[
36
]
and quantum
[
31
]
channels, as well as quantum dichotomies
[
6
]
. This starts by analyzing separate cases based on whether the rate exceeds the ratio of the channel mutual information. However, contrary to the aforementioned works, a standard first order code is insufficient here due to the bipartite nature of channel interconversion. Consequently, we derive a refined achievability result that goes beyond the first-order bound obtained by simply concatenating standard channel simulation and coding protocols. Namely, our new result attains an exponentially small conversion error, remains robust under small variations in the input distribution, and tolerates a sublinear gap between the conversion rates. The protocol is carefully designed using a tight finite block-length approach, incorporating the random coding error exponent
[
18
]
, tight channel simulations
[
41
]
, Taylor expansions of the mutual information
[
25
]
, and relevant continuity bounds.

Unlike the purified distance (and Rényi divergences), the strong converse exponent under the TV distance (
4
) involves an unusual and technically demanding double optimization over two parameters
\alpha
and
p
. Conjecturing such an expression is rather non-trivial, even though it correctly reduces to the known strong converse exponents for channel coding and simulation when one of the channels is the identity. A key technical tool that enables this double optimization — and underpins our achievability result for the TV distance — is the following inequality. For any probability distributions
P
,
Q
,
V
, and any
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we have that

-\log(1-\operatorname{TV}(P,Q))\leq\log\left(\frac{1}{1-2\eta}\right)+\sup_{0<\alpha<1}\Big(\alpha D_{\max}^{\eta}(V\|P)+(1-\alpha)D_{\max}^{\eta}(V\|Q)\Big),

(6)

where
D_{\max}^{\eta}
denotes the smooth max-divergence. This inequality admits a generalization to the quantum setting, and we believe it is of independent interest in both classical and quantum information theory. The relation resembles that for the purified distance, as proved in
[
29
]
, which we also use in this work. For any states
\rho
,
\sigma
and
\tau
, we have that

-\log(1-\operatorname{Pur}(\rho,\sigma))\leq\log 2+D(\tau\|\rho)+D(\tau\|\sigma).

The main difference between the total variation distance relation and the purified distance relation lies in that, the former requires an optimization over
\alpha\in(0,1)
, while the latter corresponds to a fixed value of
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
. This connection is further reflected by the strong converse exponent characterizations: one can obtain (
1
) (up to a normalization by a factor of
2
) by setting
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
in (
4
).

The converse of (
1
) and (
4
) is proved via the most general non-signaling strategies, employing Hölder’s inequality, and the data-processing inequality for the Rényi divergences.

Related results.

The strong converse exponents for channel simulation have recently been obtained for classical channels under the total variation distance
[
40
]
, under measures based on Rényi divergences
[
33
]
, and for classical-quantum channels under the purified distance
[
42
]
. The works
[
40
,
42
]
proceed by showing the strong converse achievability with non-signaling strategies, then invoke rounding results
[
41
]
to show the existence of shared randomness-assisted or entanglement-assisted strategies. As outlined above, our approach is conceptually different. As such, our work shows a unification of the proof methods for establishing strong converse exponents for the two different tasks of channel simulation and channel coding.

3
Problem formulation

Classical channel interconversion.

Given two classical channels
W_{Y|X}
and
T_{Z|S}
, a shared-randomness scheme to convert the channel
W
into the channel
T
consists of an encoding channel
\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
, a
decoding channel
\mathcal{D}:\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
and a shared random variable
P_{R}
. The synthesized conversion channel
\widetilde{T}
is then

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}(z|s)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}P_{R}(r)\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{E}(x|s,r)W(y|x)\mathcal{D}(z|y,r).

(40)

We denote the set of the shared randomness schemes by
\mathrm{SR}(W\rightarrow T)
.

Similarly, we can define the set of the non-signaling schemes
\mathrm{NS}(W\rightarrow T)
which consists of non signaling channels
N:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z}
satisfying:

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}N(x,z|s,y)

\displaystyle=N(z|y),

\displaystyle\forall z\in\mathcal{Z},\;\forall s\in\mathcal{S},\;\forall y\in\mathcal{Y},

(41)

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}N(x,z|s,y)

\displaystyle=N(x|s),

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathcal{X},\;\forall s\in\mathcal{S},\;\forall y\in\mathcal{Y}.

(42)

The synthesized conversion channel is denoted:

\displaystyle(N\circ W)(z|s)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}N(x,z|s,y)W(y|x).

(43)

N
is said to have marginal
P_{X}
if
N_{X|S=s}=P_{X}
for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
.

For some specified distance ‘
\mathrm{dist}
’, the optimal performance among all conversion schemes in some set
\mathcal{A}
is then defined as:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Succ}^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{dist}}(W\rightarrow T)=\sup_{N\in\mathcal{A}}1-\mathrm{dist}(N\circ W,T).

(44)

We will focus on the total-variation distance and the purified distance. The total-variation distance between two channels
T
and
T^{\prime}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\operatorname{TV}(T^{\prime},T)=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{TV}(T^{\prime}_{s},T_{s}).

(45)

Similarly, the purified distance between two channels
T
and
T^{\prime}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pur}(T^{\prime},T)=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{Pur}(T^{\prime}_{s},T_{s}).

(46)

Shannon’s theorem
[
45
]
and the reverse Shannon’s theorem
[
4
]
imply that the ratio of the channel capacities
C(T)
and
C(W)
determines exactly when asymptotic channel interconversion is possible. When the interconversion rate
r>\frac{C(W)}{C(T)}
, the strong converse property holds, i.e., the optimal performance
\mathrm{Succ}_{\mathrm{dist}}^{\mathrm{SR}}(W^{\times n}\rightarrow T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil})
or
\mathrm{Succ}_{\mathrm{dist}}^{\mathrm{NS}}(W^{\times n}\rightarrow T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil})
converges to
0
as
n
grows to infinity. The exact exponential decay rate is called the strong converse exponent of shared randomness classical channel interconversion (resp. non-signaling assisted classical channel interconversion),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Succ}_{\mathrm{dist}}^{\mathrm{SR}}(W^{\times n}\rightarrow T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil})\qquad\left(\text{resp. }\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Succ}_{\mathrm{dist}}^{\mathrm{NS}}(W^{\times n}\rightarrow T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil})\right).

The goal of this paper is to study these strong converse exponents.

Classical-quantum channel interconversion.

Let
W_{X\rightarrow Y}
and
T_{S\rightarrow Z}
be two quantum channels from Alice to Bob, an entanglement-assisted scheme to convert
W
into
T
consists of using a shared bipartite
entangled state
\phi_{\tilde{S}\tilde{Y}}
, applying local quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{S\tilde{S}\rightarrow X}
at Alice’s side, sending system
X
from Alice to Bob through
W_{X\rightarrow Y}
, and at last applying local quantum channel

\mathcal{D}_{Y\tilde{Y}\rightarrow Z}
at Bob’s side, such a scheme is usually denoted as a triple
\Pi=\{\phi_{\tilde{S}\tilde{Y}},\mathcal{E}_{S\tilde{S}\rightarrow X},\mathcal{D}_{Y\tilde{Y}\rightarrow Z}\}
. The performance of
this scheme is measured by the purified distance between the final channel
\Pi\circ W_{X\rightarrow Y}=\mathcal{D}_{Y\tilde{Y}\rightarrow Z}\circ W_{X\rightarrow Y}\circ\mathcal{E}_{S\tilde{S}\rightarrow X}((\cdot)\otimes\phi_{\tilde{S}\tilde{Y}})
and
T_{S\rightarrow Z}
. The optimal performance among all entanglement-assisted conversion schemes is defined as

\mathrm{Succ}_{\mathrm{Pur}}^{\mathrm{EA}}(W\rightarrow T)=\sup_{\Pi\in\mathrm{EA}(W\rightarrow T)}1-\mathrm{Pur}(\Pi\circ W,T),

where
\mathrm{EA}(W\rightarrow T)
is the set of all entanglement-assisted schemes for converting
W
to
T
.

Similarly, a non-signaling scheme that converts
W
to
T
consists of a super channel
\Pi_{SY\rightarrow XZ}
which satisfies the following restrictions:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&J_{\Pi}\geq 0,~\mathrm{Tr}_{XZ}J_{\Pi}=I_{SY}\\
&\mathrm{Tr}_{X}=\frac{I_{S}}{|S|}\mathrm{Tr}_{XS}J_{\Pi}\\
&\mathrm{Tr}_{Z}=\frac{I_{Y}}{|Y|}\mathrm{Tr}_{ZY}J_{\Pi}.\end{split}

The performance of
this scheme is measured by the purified distance between the final channel
\Pi\circ W_{X\rightarrow Y}
and
T_{S\rightarrow Z}
. The optimal performance among all non-signaling assisted conversion schemes is defined as

\mathrm{Succ}_{\mathrm{Pur}}^{\mathrm{NS}}(W\rightarrow T)=\sup_{\Pi\in\mathrm{NS}(W\rightarrow T)}1-\mathrm{Pur}(\Pi\circ W,T),

where
\mathrm{NS}(W\rightarrow T)
is the set of all non-signaling assisted schemes for converting
W
to
T
.

The quantum Shannon’s second theorem
[
4
]
and quantum reverse Shannon’s theorem
[
3
,
5
]
imply that for asymptotically perfect channel interconversion, a conversion rate which is equal to the ratio between their entanglement-assisted classical capacity
C_{E}(W_{X\rightarrow Y})
and

C_{E}(T_{S\rightarrow Z})
is necessary and sufficient. When the interconversion rate
r>\frac{C_{E}(W_{X\rightarrow Y})}{C_{E}(T_{S\rightarrow Z})}
, the strong converse property holds, i.e., the optimal performance
\mathrm{Succ}_{\mathrm{Pur}}^{\mathrm{EA}}(W^{\otimes n}\rightarrow T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil})
or
\mathrm{Succ}_{\mathrm{Pur}}^{\mathrm{NS}}(W^{\otimes n}\rightarrow T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil})
converges to
0
as
n
grows to infinity. The exact exponential decay rate is called the strong converse exponent of entanglement-assisted classical-quantum channel interconversion (resp. non-signaling assisted classical-quantum channel interconversion),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Succ}_{\mathrm{Pur}}^{\mathrm{EA}}(W^{\otimes n}\rightarrow T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil})\qquad\left(\text{resp. }\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Succ}_{\mathrm{Pur}}^{\mathrm{NS}}(W^{\otimes n}\rightarrow T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil})\right).

The goal of this paper is to analyze these strong converse exponents.

4
Classical channels

In this section, we characterize the strong converse exponent for channel interconversion under the worst-case total variation and purified distance metrics.

Theorem 3
.

Let
W_{Y|X}
and
T_{Z|S}
be two classical channels over finite alphabets. We have that for all rates
r\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Succ}_{\rm{TV}}^{\mathcal{A}}\left(W^{\times n}\rightarrow T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\alpha<1}\sup_{1<p<\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\frac{1-p+\alpha p}{p}\left(r\cdot I_{(1-\alpha)p}(T)-I_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}}(W)\right),

(47)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Succ}_{\rm{Pur}}^{\mathcal{A}}\left(W^{\times n}\rightarrow T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{1<p<2}\frac{2-p}{p}\left(r\cdot I_{\frac{p}{2}}(T)-I_{\frac{p}{2(p-1)}}(W)\right),

(48)

where
\mathcal{A}\in\{\mathrm{SR,EA,NS}\}
.

The proof of this theorem has two parts: the converse and the achievability bounds. The
converse is proven for non-signaling strategies, which subsume the other forms of assistance. The achievability uses a shared-randomness strategy, which is the weakest form of assistance we consider in this theorem.

We remark that our proof methods can be used to prove the strong converse exponent under the most general Rényi divergence as a notion of measure. The
\alpha
-Rényi based measure between two channels
T^{\prime}=N\circ W
and
T
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathtt{d}_{\alpha}(T\|T^{\prime})=1-\inf_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\exp(-D_{\alpha}(T_{s}\|T^{\prime}_{s})).

(49)

Recently,
[
33
]
characterizes exponents of channel simulation under this notion of measure. Similarly, we find the strong converse exponent under
\mathtt{d}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
W_{Y|X}
and
T_{Z|S}
be two classical channels over finite alphabets. We have that for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
and all rates
r\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Succ}_{\mathtt{d}_{\alpha}}^{\mathcal{A}}\left(W^{\times n}\rightarrow T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{1<p<\frac{1}{\alpha}}\frac{1-\alpha p}{(1-\alpha)p}\left(r\cdot I_{\alpha p}(T)-I_{\frac{(1-\alpha)p}{p-1}}(W)\right),

(50)

where
\mathcal{A}\in\{\rm{SR,EA,NS}\}
.

\displaystyle=\inf_{N\in\rm{NS}}-\frac{1}{n}\log\inf_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\;1-\operatorname{Pur}\left(\widetilde{T}^{\lceil rn\rceil}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}},T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right)

(75)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{N\in\rm{NS}}-\frac{2}{n}\log\inf_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[(T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}})^{1/2}(\widetilde{T}^{\lceil rn\rceil}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}})^{1/2}\right]

(76)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{1<p<2}\frac{2-p}{p}\left(r\cdot I_{\frac{p}{2}}(T)-I_{\frac{p}{2(p-1)}}(W)\right)

(77)

where we used Proposition
5
in the last inequality with
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

4.2
Achievability

In this section, we move to prove the achievability bound of Theorem
3
. Both bounds for the total variation and the purified distances rely on the following improved first order achievability for channel interconversion with an exponentially vanishing conversion error.

Proposition 7
.

Let
T_{Z|S}
and
W_{Y|X}
be two channels over finite alphabets. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
r\geq 0
,
P_{S}
be a type on
\mathcal{S}^{\lceil rn\rceil}
and
P_{X}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
. Let
\delta_{n}=2\frac{\sqrt{\log(n)r\var({T}_{Z|S})}}{n^{1/4}}
and
\gamma_{n}=2\frac{\sqrt{\log(n)\var(W_{Y|X})}}{n^{1/4}}
such that:

\displaystyle r\cdot I(P_{S},{T}_{Z|S})+\delta_{n}\leq I(P_{X},{W}_{Y|X})-\gamma_{n}.

(78)

Then, there is a shared-randomness strategy converting
W^{\times n}
to
T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}
under any input of type close to
P_{S}
and achieving a conversion TV-error at most
\exp(-\sqrt{n}\log(n))
. That is, for all
A\geq 0
, there is
n_{0}(A)
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
:

\displaystyle\inf_{N\in\mathrm{SR}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}\sim P_{S}^{\prime}\\
\mathrm{TV}(P_{S},P_{S}^{\prime})\leq\frac{A}{n}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{TV}\left(({N}\circ W^{\times n})_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}},T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right)\leq\exp(-\sqrt{n}\log(n)).

(79)

Furthermore,
N
has marginal
P_{\bm{X}}^{\times n}
.

The proof of this result is detailed in Appendix
A
. Additionally, as we will see later, both notion of distances involve a strong converse exponent that can be written using variational formulas as shown in the following proposition.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\mathbb{A}\subset[0,1]
be a convex set and let
W_{Y|X}
and
T_{Z|S}
be two classical channels over finite alphabets.
We have that:

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{A}}\sup_{1<p<\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\frac{1-p+\alpha p}{p}\left(r\cdot I_{(1-\alpha)p}(T)-I_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}}(W)\right).

(80)

\displaystyle=\inf_{P_{X}}\sup_{P_{S}}\inf_{\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}}\inf_{\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}}\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{A}}\;\;\alpha D\left(P_{X}\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}\middle\|P_{X}W_{Y|X}\right)+r(1-\alpha)D\left(P_{S}\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}\middle\|P_{S}T_{Z|S}\right)

(81)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\alpha\left|r\cdot I(P_{S},\widetilde{T}_{Z|S})-I(P_{X},\widetilde{W}_{Y|X})\right|_{+}.

(82)

The proof of this proposition can be found in Appendix
D
. This proposition reduces the problem of proving the achievability part of the strong converse to proving the upper bound using the variational formulas (
82
). This can be done by considering cases where the rate satisfies
r\cdot I(P_{S},\widetilde{T}_{Z|S})\leq I(P_{X},\widetilde{W}_{Y|X})
or not for arbitrary fixed channels
\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}
and
\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}
. Observe that this condition resembles the condition of Proposition
7
which guarantees that a channel interconversion strategy exists. Although the high-level proof strategy is the same and relies on Propositions
7
and
8
, we need to separate the proofs for the total variation distance and the purified distance.

4.2.1
Total variation distance

We start with the TV distance and prove the following proposition.

Proposition 9
.

Let
W_{Y|X}
and
T_{Z|S}
be two classical channels over finite alphabets. We have that for all rates
r\geq 0

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Succ}_{\rm{TV}}^{\rm{SR}}\left(W^{\times n}\rightarrow T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{P_{X}}\sup_{P_{S}}\inf_{\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}}\inf_{\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}}\sup_{0<\alpha<1}\alpha\left|r\cdot I(P_{S},\widetilde{T}_{Z|S})-I(P_{X},\widetilde{W}_{Y|X})\right|_{+}

(83)

\displaystyle\;\;+\alpha D\left(P_{X}\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}\middle\|P_{X}W_{Y|X}\right)+r(1-\alpha)D\left(P_{S}\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}\middle\|P_{S}T_{Z|S}\right),

(84)

where the minimization is over channels
\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}
and
\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}

Combining Proposition
9
and Proposition
8
with

gives the achievability of Theorem
3
for the TV distance. To prove Proposition

Lemma 10
.

Let
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})

\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[P\wedge Q\right]\geq(1-2\eta)\sup_{V\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})}\inf_{0<\alpha<1}\exp\left(-\alpha D_{\max}^{\eta}(V\|P)-(1-\alpha)D_{\max}^{\eta}(V\|Q)\right).

(85)

Proof of Proposition
9
.

Observe that by Sion’s minimax theorem
[
49
]
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Succ}_{\rm{TV}}^{\rm{SR}}\left(W^{\times n}\rightarrow T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{N\in\rm{SR}}\inf_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}\in\mathcal{S}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}1-\mathrm{TV}\left(({N}\circ W^{\times n})_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}},T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right)

(86)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{N\in\rm{SR}}\inf_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}\in\mathcal{S}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[({N}\circ W^{\times n})_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\wedge T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]

(87)

\displaystyle=\inf_{P_{\bm{S}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}}\sup_{N\in\rm{SR}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}\sim P_{\bm{S}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[({N}\circ W^{\times n})_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\wedge T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right].

(88)

Furthermore, we can restrict the minimization on
P_{\bm{S}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}
to be on permutation invariant probability distributions. In this case, there is a type

such that

(see Appendix
C
). All elements in the type
t
have the same empirical distribution denoted
P_{S}
. Hence for

\displaystyle\mathrm{Succ}_{\rm{TV}}^{\rm{SR}}\left(W^{\times n}\rightarrow T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{P_{\bm{S}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}}\sup_{N\in\rm{SR}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}\sim P_{\bm{S}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[({N}\circ W^{\times n})_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\wedge T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]

(89)

\displaystyle\geq(\lceil rn\rceil+1)^{-|\mathcal{S}|}\inf_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\lceil rn\rceil}(\mathcal{S})}\sup_{N\in\rm{SR}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[({N}\circ W^{\times n})_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\wedge T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right].

(90)

Fixing
t
and
P_{S}
(the empirical distribution of
t
), we make cases depending on the comparison between

P_{Z}

Let

Case

By Proposition

(under input

and

\displaystyle-\log\mathrm{Tr}\left[({N}\circ W^{\times n})_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\wedge T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]+\log(1-2\eta)

(91)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\sup_{0<\alpha<1}\alpha D_{\max}^{\eta}\left((N\circ\widetilde{W}^{\times n})_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}(t)}\middle\|(N\circ W^{\times n})_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}(t)}\right)+(1-\alpha)D_{\max}^{\eta}\left((N\circ\widetilde{W}^{\times n})_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}(t)}\middle\|T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}_{\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}(t)}\right)

where in

P_{\bm{X}}^{\times n}

and Lemma

5
Classical-quantum channels

In this section, we determine the strong converse exponents of entanglement-assisted and non-signaling assisted classical-quantum channel interconversion. The main result of this section is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 12
.

For any classical-quantum channels
W_{X\rightarrow Y}
,
T_{S\rightarrow Z}
and real

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Succ}_{\mathrm{Pur}}^{\mathcal{A}}(W^{\otimes n}\rightarrow T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil})=\sup_{1<p<2}\frac{2-p}{p}\big(r\widetilde{I}_{\frac{p}{2}}(T_{S\rightarrow Z})-\widetilde{I}_{\frac{p}{2(p-1)}}(W_{X\rightarrow Y})\big),

6
Conclusion

In this work, we initiated the study of high-order refinements in the context of the channel interconversion problem. In particular, we determined the exact strong converse exponent under the purified distance for the conversion of classical-quantum channels, with natural extensions to metrics based on Rényi divergences. We further derived the exact strong converse exponent under the total variation distance in the fully classical setting. Notably, our results apply to all rates below and above the capacities of the involved channels.

Our analysis is starting from the strong converse exponent methods in
[
14
,
13
,
36
,
31
,
6
]
, combined with an improved first-order achievability scheme. However, several open questions remain. In particular, understanding the small deviation regime and the precise characterization of error exponents appear to require new techniques. We leave these as interesting directions for future research.

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\beta n\left(R-rD_{1+\beta}(P_{S}T_{Z|S}\|P_{S}P_{Z})\right)+\beta\log(n)\right)

(173)

where we used Jensen’s inequality in the last inequality. We shall choose
\beta=\frac{\delta_{n}}{r\var({T}_{Z|S})}
. By Taylor expansion
[
25
, Proposition 11]
, we have that

\displaystyle D_{1+\beta}(P_{S}T_{Z|S}\|P_{S}P_{Z})

\displaystyle=I(P_{S},T_{Z|S})+\frac{1}{2}\beta\var(P_{S},{T}_{Z|S})+o(\beta)\leq I(P_{S},T_{Z|S})+\frac{\delta_{n}}{2r}+o(\delta_{n}).

(174)

Since
R\geq r\cdot I(P_{S},{T}_{Z|S})+\delta_{n}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{TV}\left(\widetilde{T}^{\rm{NS}}(\cdot|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}),T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}(\cdot|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\beta n\left(R-rD_{1+\beta}(P_{S}T_{Z|S}\|P_{S}P_{Z})\right)+\beta\log(n)\right)

(175)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-n\frac{\delta_{n}}{r\var({T}_{Z|S})}\left(\delta_{n}-\frac{\delta_{n}}{2}-o(\delta_{n})\right)+\frac{\delta_{n}\log(n)}{\var({T}_{Z|S})}\right)

(176)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{n\delta_{n}^{2}}{2r\var({T}_{Z|S})}+\frac{\delta_{n}\log(n)}{r\var({T}_{Z|S})}+o\left({n\delta_{n}^{2}}\right)\right)

(177)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}\exp\left(-\sqrt{n}\log(n)\right),

(178)

where we use in the last inequality
\delta_{n}=2\frac{\sqrt{\log(n)r\var({T}_{Z|S})}}{n^{1/4}}
and
n
is sufficiently large.

This NS strategy can be rounded to a SR strategy
\widetilde{T}^{\rm{SR}}
of size
e^{\lceil Rn\rceil}
satisfying
[
41
]
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}^{\rm{SR}}(\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil})\geq(1-e^{-n})\widetilde{T}^{\rm{NS}}(\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}),\quad\forall\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}.

(179)

Therefore, there exists a SR conversion strategy to simulate
T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}
using
\mathrm{id}_{2}^{\times\lceil Rn\rceil}
with an error:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\text{simulation}}^{\text{SR}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{TV}\left(\widetilde{T}^{\rm{SR}}(\cdot|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil}),T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}(\cdot|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil})\right)

(180)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\left(T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}(\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil})-\widetilde{T}^{\rm{SR}}(\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil})\right)_{+}

(181)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\left(T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}(\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil})-(1-e^{-n})\widetilde{T}^{\rm{NS}}(\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil})\right)_{+}

(182)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\left(T^{\times\lceil rn\rceil}(\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil})-\widetilde{T}^{\rm{NS}}(\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil})\right)_{+}+e^{-n}\widetilde{T}^{\rm{NS}}(\bm{z}^{\lceil rn\rceil}|\bm{s}^{\lceil rn\rceil})

(183)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}\exp\left(-\sqrt{n}\log(n)\right)+e^{-n}.

(184)

The concatenation strategy has then a total conversion error:

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\text{SR}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{\text{coding}}^{\text{SR}}+\epsilon_{\text{simulation}}^{\text{SR}}\leq\frac{1}{3}\exp\left(-\sqrt{n}\log(n)\right)+\frac{1}{3}\exp\left(-\sqrt{n}\log(n)\right)+e^{-n}\leq\exp\left(-\sqrt{n}\log(n)\right)

(185)

for sufficiently large
n
.

To prove that
N
has marginal
P_{\bm{X}}^{\times n}
, we remark that the random coding uses codewords sampled i.i.d. according to
P_{\bm{X}}^{\times n}
and the rounding strategy of
[

Appendix B
Improved first order achievability for classical-quantum channel interconversion

In this section, we prove Proposition
15
that we restate.

Proposition 19

(Restatement of Proposition
15
)
.

Let
W_{X\rightarrow Y}
and
T_{S\rightarrow Z}
be two classical-quantum channels. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
P_{S}
be a type on
\mathcal{S}^{n}
and
P_{X}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
. Let

\delta_{n}=3\frac{\sqrt{\log(n)\var({T}_{S\rightarrow Z})}}{n^{1/4}}
,
\gamma_{n}=2\frac{\sqrt{\log(n)\var(W_{X\rightarrow Y})}}{n^{1/4}}
and

such that

rI(P_{S}:T_{S\rightarrow Z})+\delta_{n}\leq I(P_{X}:W_{X\rightarrow Y})-\gamma_{n}.

(186)

Then, there is an entanglement-assisted strategy converting
W^{\otimes n}
to
T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}
under any input of type close to
P_{S}
and achieving a conversion
Pur
-error at most
\exp(-\sqrt{n}\log(n))

Proof.

The proof of this proposition is similar to Proposition
7
. It can be divided into a concatenation between channel coding and channel simulation. The proof of the channel coding part is almost the same, relying on the error exponent result for classical-quantum channels
[
10
]
.

Hence, we focus on the channel simulation part in the following.
Let
R
such that

\displaystyle rI(P_{S}:T_{S\rightarrow Z})+\delta_{n}\leq R\leq I(P_{X}:W_{X\rightarrow Y})-\gamma_{n}.

(187)

Channel simulation.
Let
\sigma_{Z}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}P_{S}(s)T_{s}
,
\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}=\left\{\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}}(T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}})\succcurlyeq\tfrac{1}{\nu_{nr}}(\frac{1}{n}e^{\lceil Rn\rceil}-1)\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\right\}
and construct

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}=\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{c}T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{c}+\mathrm{Tr}\left[T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}.

(188)

Clearly
\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}
is a quantum state. Moreover, by pinching inequality
[
23
]
, we have that
T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\preccurlyeq\nu_{nr}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}}(T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}})
so

\begin{split}\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}&\preccurlyeq\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{c}\nu_{nr}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}}(T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}})\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{c}+\mathrm{Tr}\left[T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\\
&\preccurlyeq\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{c}(\frac{1}{n}e^{\lceil Rn\rceil}-1)\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{c}+\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}(\frac{1}{n}e^{\lceil Rn\rceil}-1)\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}+\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}=\frac{1}{n}e^{\lceil Rn\rceil}\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}.\end{split}

(189)

Hence,
\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}
can be seen as a nonsignaling synthesized channel
T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}
and its performance can be evaluated as

\displaystyle F\left(T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil},\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sqrt{\sqrt{T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}}\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\sqrt{T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}}}\right]\right)^{2}

(190)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sqrt{\sqrt{T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{c}T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{c}\sqrt{T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}}}\right]\right)^{2}

(191)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sqrt{T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{c}\sqrt{T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]\right)^{2}

(192)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{c}\right]^{2}.

(193)

Therefore

\displaystyle T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}

\displaystyle\preccurlyeq\frac{1}{n}e^{\lceil Rn\rceil}\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\;\text{ and }\;1-F\left(T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil},\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right)\leq 1-\mathrm{Tr}\left[T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{c}\right]^{2}\leq 2\,\mathrm{Tr}\left[T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right].

(194)

Using the definition of the projector
\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}
, we have that

\mathrm{Tr}\left[T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}}(T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil})\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]\geq\frac{1}{\nu_{nr}}(\frac{1}{n}e^{\lceil Rn\rceil}-1)\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right],

(195)

thus by the data processing inequality of the sandwiched Rényi divergence, we have that for all
\alpha>0

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{1+\alpha}(T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\|\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil})\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\mathrm{Tr}\left[T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]^{1+\alpha}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]^{-\alpha}

(196)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\mathrm{Tr}\left[T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]^{1+\alpha}\left(\tfrac{\nu_{nr}}{\frac{1}{n}e^{\lceil Rn\rceil}-1}\mathrm{Tr}\left[T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]\right)^{-\alpha}

(197)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\mathrm{Tr}\left[T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\Pi_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right]+\log\left(\tfrac{\frac{1}{n}e^{\lceil Rn\rceil}-1}{\nu_{nr}}\right).

(198)

Therefore for all
\alpha>0

\begin{split}1-F\left(T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil},\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right)&\leq 2\exp\left(\alpha\big(\widetilde{D}_{1+\alpha}(T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}\|\sigma^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil})-\log(\tfrac{\frac{1}{n}e^{\lceil Rn\rceil}-1}{\nu_{nr}})\big)\right)\\
&\leq 2\exp\left(\alpha\big(nr\mathbb{E}_{s\sim P_{S}}\widetilde{D}_{1+\alpha}(T_{s}\|\sigma)-nR+\log 2n(n+1)^{|Z|}\big)\right)\\
&=2\exp\left(-\alpha n\big(R-r\mathbb{E}_{s\sim P_{S}}\widetilde{D}_{1+\alpha}(T_{s}\|\sigma)-\frac{\log 2n(n+1)^{|Z|}}{n}\big)\right)\end{split}

(199)

We shall choose
\alpha=\frac{\delta_{n}}{r\var({T}_{S\rightarrow Z})}
. By Taylor expansion
[
25
, Proposition 11]
, we have that

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{1+\alpha}(P_{S}{T}_{S\rightarrow Z}\|P_{S}\otimes\sigma)

\displaystyle=I(P_{S}:{T}_{S\rightarrow Z})+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\var(P_{S},{T}_{S\rightarrow Z})+o(\alpha)

(200)

\displaystyle\leq I(P_{S}:{T}_{S\rightarrow Z})+\frac{\delta_{n}}{2r}+o(\delta_{n}).

(201)

Since
R\geq r\cdot I(S:Z)_{P_{S}{T}_{S\rightarrow Z}}+\delta_{n}
, we deduce that

\begin{split}1-F\left(T_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil},\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\right)&\leq\exp\left(-n\frac{\delta_{n}}{r\var({T}_{S\rightarrow Z})}\left(\delta_{n}-\frac{\delta_{n}}{2}-o(\delta_{n})-\frac{\log 2n(n+1)^{|Z|}}{n}\right)\right)\\
&=\exp\left(-\frac{n\delta_{n}^{2}}{2r\var({T}_{S\rightarrow Z})}+\frac{\delta_{n}\log(2n(n+1)^{|Z|})}{r\var({T}_{S\rightarrow Z})}+o\left({n\delta_{n}^{2}}\right)\right)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{9}\exp\left(-2\sqrt{n}\log(n)\right),\end{split}

(202)

where we use in the last inequality
\delta_{n}=3\frac{\sqrt{\log(n)r\var({T}_{S\rightarrow Z})}}{n^{1/4}}
and
n
is sufficiently large.

By
[
41
, Prop 4.1]
, this NS strategy
\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}
can be rounded to an EA strategy
\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{EA}}
of size
e^{\lceil Rn\rceil}
satisfying

\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{EA}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}\succcurlyeq(1-e^{-n})\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}}.

(203)

Hence, there exists an EA conversion strategy to simulate
T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}
using
\mathrm{id}_{2}^{\otimes\lceil Rn\rceil}
with an error:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\epsilon^{\mathrm{EA}}_{\mathrm{simulation}}&\leq P(\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{EA}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}},T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}})\\
&\leq P(\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{EA}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}},\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}})+P(\widetilde{T}^{\mathrm{NS}}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}},T^{\otimes\lceil rn\rceil}_{s^{\lceil rn\rceil}})\\
&\leq\sqrt{e^{-n}}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{9}\exp\left(-2\sqrt{n}\log(n)\right)}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(-\sqrt{n}\log(n)\right).\end{split}

(204)

Appendix C
Method of types

Let
n
be an integer and
\mathcal{X}
be a finite alphabet of size
|\mathcal{X}|
. Let
x^{n}=x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
be an element of
\mathcal{X}^{n}
. For
x\in\mathcal{X}
we define
n(x|x^{n})
to be the number of occurrences of
x
in the sequence
x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
:

n(x|x^{n})=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{x_{t}=x\}.

(205)

A type
T
is a probability distribution on
\mathcal{X}
of the form

T=\Big\{\frac{n_{x}}{n}\Big\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\text{ where }n_{x}\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}n_{x}=n.

(206)

The set of types of alphabet
\mathcal{X}
of length
n
is denoted
\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})
. It is a finite set of size satisfying

|\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})|\leq(n+1)^{|\mathcal{X}|}.

(207)

This simple bound can be proved using the simple observation that each
n_{x}
in Eq. (
206
) satisfies
n_{x}\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
and thus it has at most
n+1
possibilities.

We say that
x^{n}=x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
has type
T
and write
x^{n}\sim T
if for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
we have
{n(x|x^{n})}={n}T_{x}
.

A probability distribution
p
on
\mathcal{X}^{n}
is permutation invariant if for all permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, for all
x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}
we have

p(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})=p(x_{\sigma_{1}}\cdots x_{\sigma_{n}}).

(208)

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})
be a type and
p
be a permutation invariant probability distribution on
\mathcal{X}^{n}
.
Clearly if two sequences
x_{1}^{n}
and
y_{1}^{n}
have the same type
T
then
y_{1}^{n}
can be obtained by permuting the elements of
x_{1}^{n}
so

p(x^{n})=p(y_{1}^{n}),\quad\forall x^{n}\sim T,\;\forall y_{1}^{n}\sim T.

(209)

We denote this value by
\frac{\alpha_{T}}{|T|}
where
|T|
is the number of elements in
\mathcal{X}^{n}
of type
T
. So we can write

p=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})}\alpha_{T}\mathcal{U}_{T}

(210)

where
\mathcal{U}_{T}
is the uniform probability distribution supported on
T
:

\mathcal{U}_{T}(x^{n})=\frac{1}{|T|}\mathbf{1}\{x^{n}\sim T\}.

(211)

Since
p
and
\{\mathcal{U}_{T}\}_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})}
are all probability distributions on
\mathcal{X}^{n}
,
(\alpha_{T})_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})}
is a probability distribution on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})
. In particular, since we have
|\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})|\leq(n+1)^{|\mathcal{X}|}
there is a type
T^{\star}
such that

\alpha_{T^{\star}}\geq\frac{1}{(n+1)^{|\mathcal{X}|}}

(212)

because otherwise
\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})}\alpha_{T}<\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{|\mathcal{X}|}}=|\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})|\cdot\frac{1}{(n+1)^{|\mathcal{X}|}}\leq 1
which contradicts the fact that
(\alpha_{T})_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})}
is a probability distribution on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathcal{X})
.

Appendix D
Variational formulas for strong converse exponents

In this section we prove Proposition
8
which we restate:

Proposition 20

(Restatement of Proposition
8
)
.

Let
\mathbb{A}\subset[0,1]
be a convex set and let
W_{Y|X}
and
T_{Z|S}
be two classical channels over finite alphabets.
We have that:

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{A}}\sup_{1<p<\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\frac{1-p+\alpha p}{p}\left(r\cdot I_{(1-\alpha)p}(T)-I_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}}(W)\right).

\displaystyle=\inf_{P_{X}}\sup_{P_{S}}\inf_{\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}}\inf_{\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}}\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{A}}\;\;\alpha D\left(P_{X}\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}\middle\|P_{X}W_{Y|X}\right)+r(1-\alpha)D\left(P_{S}\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}\middle\|P_{S}T_{Z|S}\right)

Proof.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\alpha\left|r\inf_{P_{Z}}D\left(P_{S}\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}\middle\|P_{S}P_{Z}\right)-\inf_{P_{Y}}D\left(P_{X}\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}\middle\|P_{X}P_{Y}\right)\right|_{+}

(218)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\delta\left(rD\left(P_{S}\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}\middle\|P_{S}P_{Z}\right)-D\left(P_{X}\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}\middle\|P_{X}P_{Y}^{\star}\right)\right)

(219)

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}\inf_{P_{X}}\sup_{P_{S}}\inf_{P_{Z}}\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{A}}\sup_{0\leq\delta\leq\alpha}\inf_{\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}}\inf_{\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}}\sup_{P_{Y}}\alpha D\left(P_{X}\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}\middle\|p_{X}W_{Y|X}\right)+(1-\alpha)rD\left(P_{S}\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}\middle\|P_{S}T_{Z|S}\right)

(220)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\delta\left(rD\left(P_{S}\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}\middle\|P_{S}P_{Z}\right)-D\left(P_{X}\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}\middle\|P_{X}P_{Y}\right)\right)

(221)

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{=}\inf_{P_{X}}\sup_{P_{S}}\inf_{P_{Z}}\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{A}}\sup_{0\leq\delta\leq\alpha}\sup_{P_{Y}}\inf_{\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}}\inf_{\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}}\alpha D\left(P_{X}\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}\middle\|p_{X}W_{Y|X}\right)-\delta D\left(P_{X}\widetilde{W}_{Y|X}\middle\|P_{X}P_{Y}\right)

(222)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+r(1-\alpha)D\left(P_{S}\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}\middle\|P_{S}T_{Z|S}\right)+r\delta D\left(P_{S}\widetilde{T}_{Z|S}\middle\|P_{S}P_{Z}\right)

(223)

where we used Sion’s minimax theorem: in
(a)
the objective function is concave in
(\delta,\alpha)
and convex in
P_{Z}

(b

(\widetilde{W}_{x},\widetilde{T}_{s})
even though

\displaystyle\alpha D\left(\widetilde{W}_{x}\middle\|W_{x}\right)-\delta D\left(\widetilde{W}_{x}\middle\|P_{Y}^{\star}\right)=-(\alpha-\delta)H\left(\widetilde{W}_{x}\right)-\alpha\mathrm{Tr}\left[\widetilde{W}_{x}\log W_{x}\right]+\delta\mathrm{Tr}\left[\widetilde{W}_{x}\log\textstyle\sum_{x^{\prime}}P_{X}(x^{\prime})\widetilde{W}_{x^{\prime}}\right];

(224)

in

is convex in

and

Using the variational property of the Rényi divergence

\displaystyle\inf_{\widetilde{W}_{x}}\alpha D\left(\widetilde{W}_{x}\middle\|W_{x}\right)-\delta D\left(\widetilde{W}_{x}\middle\|P_{Y}\right)

\displaystyle=-\delta D_{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-\delta}}\left(W_{x}\middle\|P_{Y}\right),

(225)

\displaystyle\inf_{\widetilde{T}_{s}}(1-\alpha)D\left(\widetilde{T}_{s}\middle\|T_{s}\right)+\delta D\left(\widetilde{T}_{s}\middle\|P_{Z}\right)

P_{Z}

P_{S}

A reduction theorem for non-vanishing of Hochschild cohomology of block algebras and Happel’s property

Abstract
. In this short research note we obtain a reduction theorem for the non-vanishing of the first Hochschild cohomology of block algebras of finite groups with non-trivial defect groups. Along the way we investigate this problem for the blocks of some simple finite group algebras. Mimicking the case of blocks of finite group algebras we find some examples of category algebras that satisfy Happel’s property. MSC 2010. 20C20, Key words. finite, group, block, Hochschild, cohomology, Happel



1  Introduction

Let
k
be a an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
and
b
a block idempotent of
kG,
where
G
is a finite group such that
p
divides the order of
G.
It is known that the first Hochschild cohomology group (denoted by
\mathrm{HH}^{1}(kG)
) of the group algebra
kG
forms a Lie algebra over
k
and there is recent growing interest on the solvability of first Hochschild cohomology group of finite dimensional algebras in general
[
29
]
and block algebras of finite groups in particular
[
26
]
. We shall use the following notation

\mathcal{G}:=\{(G,P)\mid G\text{ is a finite group s.t.}\ p\mid|G|,P\text{ a non-trivial }p\text{-subgroup of }G\}.

The non-vanishing of
\mathrm{HH}^{1}(kG)
is obtained as a consequence of a result of Fleischmann, Janiszczak and Lempken,
[
9
]
. We know that the block algebra
kGb
is a simple
k
-algebra if and only if
b
has trivial defect group. However, it is still an open problem if
\mathrm{HH}^{1}(kGb)\neq 0
for any block algebra
kGb
with non-trivial defect group. The following question was launched by Linckelmann at ICRA 2016, Syracuse.

Question 1.1
.

(
[
24
, Question 7.7]
) Is it true that for any
G
such that
(G,P)\in\mathcal{G}
and any block algebra
kGb
with defect group
P

we have
\mathrm{HH}^{1}(kGb)\neq 0
?

We will investigate the so-called Happel’s property for Hochschild cohomology and we spend some time to recall the concepts. For a finite dimensional
k
-algebra
A
, we denote the global dimension of
A
as usual by
\mathrm{gldim}(A)
and the Hochschild cohomological (respectively, homological) dimension of
A
by

\mathrm{hch.dim}(A):=\mathrm{sup}\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\mathrm{HH}^{n}(A)\neq 0\},

(respectively,
\mathrm{hh.dim}(A):=\mathrm{sup}\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\mathrm{HH}_{n}(A)\neq 0\}
). We follow and are inspired by the survey
[
7
]
.
The statement that for any finite dimensional
k
-algebra,
\mathrm{hch.dim}(A)
is finite implies that
\mathrm{gldim}(A)
is finite, is called
Happel’s property
. The analogous statement for Hochschild homology (i.e.
\mathrm{hh.dim}(A)<\infty
implies
\mathrm{gldim}(A)<\infty
) is called Han’s property. Inspired by block algebras of finite groups and group algebra cases, we are interested in studying Happel’s property for some Frobenius
k
-algebras. The converse statement to Happel’s property is obtained by theorem due to Keller
[
15
]
(see
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
), given in the case of Hochschild homology for any finite dimensional
k
-algebra.

In the following paragraph we include remarks and questions suggested by an email discussion with Markus Linckelmann. We assume that
b
has non-trivial defect group
P
, hence the block algebra
kGb
is not semisimple and
\mathrm{gldim}(kGb)=\infty.
So, Happel’s property for
kGb
is the same to showing that
\mathrm{hch.dim}(kGb)=\infty,
(equivalently,
\mathrm{hch.dim}(kGb)\neq 0
.) The Hochschild cohomology
\mathrm{HH}^{*}(kGb)
is always infinite-dimensional, because its Krull dimension is positive, equal to the rank of
P
,
so
\mathrm{HH}^{*}(kGb)
contains a polynomial subalgebra. One can say a bit more (see
[
22
, Theorem 13.1]
):
the longest sequence of consecutive integers
n
for which
\mathrm{HH}^{n}(kGb)=0
is bounded in terms
of
P.
In particular, Happel’s property is true for
kGb.
A valid question to ask would be this: what can one say about the smallest positive integer n such
that
\mathrm{HH}^{n}(kGb)
is non-zero? By the above, we know that
n
is bounded in terms of
P.
And as we can see in Question
1.1
, we expect that
n=1.

As an alternative method of proof for block algebras, Happel’s property is a consequence of the validity of a well-known conjecture in the theory of saturated fusion systems, Conjecture
1.2
; for this theory we follow
[
2
]
. A saturated fusion system
\mathcal{F}
on a finite
p
-group

is called

if there is a finite group

such that
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(G_{1}),
where
S
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of

otherwise
\mathcal{F}
is said to be exotic. We consider a
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
as a defect group of
b.
We fix a maximal
b
-Brauer
(P,e)
and denote by
\mathcal{F}
the saturated fusion system
\mathcal{F}_{(P,e)}(G,b)
of the block
b.
This means that
b
is an
\mathcal{F}
-block and we say for shortness that
kGb
is an
\mathcal{F}
-
reduction simple
block algebra if

has no non-trivial proper strongly
\mathcal{F}
-closed subgroups. We restate the following equivalent form of
[
2
, Part 4, Section 7, p.298]
:

Conjecture 1.2
.

(
[
30
, Conjecture 1.1]
)
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a saturated fusion system. For any finite group

having an
\mathcal{F}
-block

is realizable

In the next remark we present the ingredients that assure us that if Conjecture

is verified for

then any

satisfies Happel’s property.

Remark 1.3
.

Let
(G,P)\in\mathcal{G},
a saturated fusion system
\mathcal{F}
on

and

an
\mathcal{F}
-block. If

is realizable it follows that there is a finite group

with

Using the Cartan-Eilenberg Stable Elements Theorem

(\mathrm{H}^{n}(P,k))^{\mathcal{F}}\cong\mathrm{H}^{n}(G_{1},k),

Recall that the cohomology of the block

is
(\mathrm{H}^{n}(P,k))^{\mathcal{F}},
that was introduced by Linckemann in

and that there is an injective map

for any positive integer

In the first main theorem we present some Frobenius
k
-algebras (similar to group algebras) that verify Happel’s property. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite category (the class of morphisms is a finite set) and
k\mathcal{C}
the category algebra. Let
\underline{k}
be the constant functor from
\mathcal{C}
to the category of vector
k
-spaces, sending every object to
k
and every morphism to identity of

The ordinary cohomology of
\mathcal{C}
with coefficients in
k,
denoted by
\mathrm{H}^{*}(\mathcal{C},\underline{k}),
can be defined as
\mathrm{Ext}^{*}_{k\mathcal{C}}(\underline{k},\underline{k}),
see
[
36
]
. We denote by
\mathbb{G},
the group
G
viewed as a category with one object, homomorphisms the elements of
G
and the composition is the multiplication in
G.
If
\pi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}
is a covariant functor, there is always a restriction map
\mathrm{res}_{\pi}^{*}:\mathrm{H}^{*}(\mathbb{G},\underline{k})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{*}(\mathcal{C},\underline{k}),
see
[
36
,
37
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite category such that
k\mathcal{C}
is a Frobenius
k
-algebra. If there is a coovariant functor
\pi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}
such that
\mathrm{res}_{\pi}^{*}:\mathrm{H}^{*}(\mathbb{G},\underline{k})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{*}(\mathcal{C},\underline{k})
is injective, then
k\mathcal{C}
satisfy Happel’s property.

We do not claim that in the above theorem we obtain remarkable new examples, but we just want to emphasize the method described in Remark
1.3
. It may happen that the validity of Happel’s property for the examples in Remark
2.1
(that verify the assumptions of Theorem
1.4
) can be obtained by using other more simple arguments.

-reduction simple block algebra

Predicting PIK3CA mutation in breast cancer with machine learning based multimodal image radiomics
Authors: Jiejie Yao, Xiaoyu Li, Weimin Chai, Anqi Li, Xiaosong Chen, Wei Zhou, Jianqiao Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGene status may help determine molecular targeted therapy for patients with breast cancer (BC). The present study aimed to explore machine learning (ML) approaches for the early prediction of PIK3CA mutation based on mammography (MMG) and ultrasound (US) radiomics.MethodsWe analysed 186 primary BC patients (130 in the training set and 56 in the test set) who underwent PIK3CA gene testing and with pretreatment MMG and US images from January 2020 to December 2023 at our institution. PIK3CA mutation results were used as the reference standard. The MMG and US radiomics features were extracted using 3D slicer and PyRadiomics software. Three machine learning (ML) algorithms, namely, logistic regression (LR), adaptive boosting (AdaBoost), and Naive Bayes (NB) were applied to construct and validate the radiomics and combined clinicopathological-radiomics predictive models.ResultsFifty-nine (31.7%) BC patients (41 in the training set and 18 in the test set) had PIK3CA mutations. Clinicopathological (C) factors including estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) positivity were associated with PIK3CA mutation. The AUC values of the MMG-based radiomics were 0.656 (NB), 0.700 (AdaBoost), and 0.718 (LR), while those of the US-based radiomics models were 0.732 (NB), 0.754 (AdaBoost) and 0.788 (LR) in the test set. The predictive ability of clinicopathological-radiomics C + US + LR model was greater (AUC, 0.827; 95% CI 0.706, 0.942) than that of the C + MMG + LR model (AUC, 0.740; 95% CI 0.611, 0.867; p < 0.05). The hybrid C + US + MMG + LR model achieved greater efficacy (AUC, 0.899; 95% CI 0.783, 0.984) than the C + US + MMG + AdaBoost (AUC, 0.860; 95% CI 0.739, 0.974), and C + US + MMG + NB (AUC, 0.838; 95% CI 0.721, 0.952; all p < 0.05) models in the test set.ConclusionUS-based radiomics showed better predictive ability than MMG-based radiomics model. The hybrid clinicopathological and US + MMG + LR model with improved performance can assist tailored therapeutic strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03435-0.Keywords: Multimodal image radiomics, Machine learning, PIK3CA mutation, Breast cancer

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is the most common malignancy, with an estimated 2.3 million newly diagnosed cases (11.7%) worldwide, and it ranks as the fifth leading cause (6.9%) of cancer death [1]. Phosphatidyl-inositol-3-kinase (PI3K) plays important roles in regulating cell transformation, tumor proliferation and apoptosis [2, 3]. Phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate 3-kinase catalytic subunit alpha (PIK3CA) mutation, which has a high incidence rate in BC patients, is associated with therapeutic resistance and different prognostic effects [4–7]. With the rapid development of genome sequencing, the use of drugs that inhibit PIK3CA mutation, such as alpelisib and inavolisib, has prolonged the survival of patients, especially for those with more aggressive and poorly differentiated tumors [8–10]. However, genetic testing is expensive, invasive, time-consuming, and not available for all patients. Noninvasive, convenient and accurate methods for the early prediction of PIK3CA mutation provide an opportunity for guiding individual treatment strategies for BC patients.

Previous studies have used clinicopathological indicators [11, 12], or traditional images to predict PIK3CA mutation [13, 14]. However, the predictive performances of conventional methods were relatively low, with the area under the curve (AUC) values ranging from 0.553–0.610 to 0.741 [12, 13]. Recent researchers proposed radiomics as a new approach for predicting gene mutations [15–18]. Nevertheless, previous radiomics analyses mainly focused on MRI [17, 18]. Mammography (MMG) and ultrasound (US) are more widely used diagnostic tools when compared with MRI, possess advantages such as convenient, low cost and time saving in breast examinations. However, to our knowledge, few previous studies have utilized MM- and US-based radiomics to predict PIK3CA mutation [15, 19].

Artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms such as logistic regression (LR), adaptive boosting (AdaBoost), and Naive Bayes (NB) are effective machine learning (ML) classifiers, that have been applied in medical research for cancer diagnosis and prognosis. With the ability to stratify patients, these techniques have added to the personalization of health care [15, 20, 21]. Prior studies of our team have constructed ML-based US radiomics to predict axillary lymph node metastasis burden [22], and axillary response after neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) in BC patients [23]. Recently, Cai et al. [24] used US and tomosynthesis radiomics to predict residual BC after NAC. On the basis of previous research, the present study explored the application of multimodal radiomics with ML algorithms for the prediction of PIK3CA mutation. We also evaluated the efficacy of MMG-based radiomics, US-based radiomics and combined clinicopathological-radiomics models.

Therefore, the aim of the present study was to employ three ML classifiers (i.e., LR, AdaBoost, and NB) to construct and validate multimodal image radiomics models, as well as to further assess the predictive performance of the hybrid models that integrate clinicopathological factors.

Materials and methods

Study design and patients

The present retrospective analysis was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Committee of our institution. Informed consent was waived due to its retrospective nature. We included patients from January 2020 to December 2023 who met the following criteria: (1) patients who had primary BC; (2) patients who underwent PIK3CA gene testing; and (3) patients who obtained pretreatment high-quality MMG and US images of their breast tumors. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients who had metastatic BC; (2) patients who had insufficient clinicopathological or genetic data; and (3) patients whose BC presented as a non-mass area on imaging. For patients who had bilateral or multiple tumors, the largest tumor was included. Finally, a total of 186 patients with 186 lesions were included as the study population and were randomly (7:3) assigned to a training set (n = 130) and an independent test set (n = 56). Clinicopathological data

The clinicopathological data, including patient age, clinical tumor stage, tumor maximum size, histological type, tumor grade, lymph node status, estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) status, tumor proliferation rate (Ki67 levels), and PIK3CA gene test results, were obtained from the medical record database. PIK3CA mutation status was used as the reference standard. A cut-off value for Ki67 positivity was established at 20% [25].

MMG and US images acquisition and regions of interest (ROIs) segmentation

MMG examinations were performed using Hologic scanners (Selenia Dimensions, Hologic), capturing craniocaudal (CC) and mediolateral oblique (MLO) images. Breast US examinations were performed using Resona 7 and Resona 8 systems (Mindray Medical International, Shenzhen, China) with a linear probe at 3–11 MHz. Two experienced radiologists (author 1 and author 3 with 13 and 15 years experience of breast images, respectively) who were unaware of the status of PIK3CA mutation, evaluated and extracted the greyscale US images, and MMG images of the target tumor. For each patient, only a single MMG image (either CC or MLO image), and a single greyscale US image, which displayed the most distinct internal and peripheral structure of the target tumor was selected. The tumor regions of interest (ROIs) were manually segmented and extracted using 3D slicer (3D Slicer version 5.0.3) and PyRadiomics software [26].

To ensure the reproducibility of feature extraction, the ROI segmentations of MMG and US images of 50 randomly chosen tumors were initially performed by the aforementioned two radiologists (author 3 and author 1). One week later, author 1 repeated the same procedure. Images with an intra- and inter-class correlation coefficient (ICC) equal to or higher than 0.75, which was considered as good intra- and inter-observer agreement, were included in the further feature selection procedure. Examples of tumor MMG and US images and their corresponding ROIs are shown in Supplementary Figures S1 and S2.

Feature extraction, selection and ML classifiers implementation

Each of the 851 MMG and US radiomics features were extracted, including 14 shape features, 18 first-order features, 38 s-order texture features [24 Gy level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM), and 14 Gy level dependence matrix (GLDM)], 37 higher-order texture features [16 Gy level run length matrix (GLRLM), 16 Gy level size zone matrix (GLSZM), and 5 neighboring gray tone difference matrix (NGTDM)], and 744 wavelet-related features (Tables S1 and S2).

To select features, we first used synthetic minority oversampling technique (SMOTE) to remove the unbalanced samples in each dataset [27]. Z-score and mean normalization methods were employed to standardize the corresponding features. Principal component analysis (PCA) and pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) were subsequently utilized to reduce feature dimension. Recursive features elimination (RFE) was then applied to detect the most robust signatures [28]. Finally, the selected features were input to LR, AdaBoost, and NB classifiers with a fivefold cross validation to construct MMG-based, US-based radiomics models, combined clinicopathological-radiomics (i.e., C + MMG, and C + US) models, and the hybrid clinicopathological, multimodal images radiomics (i.e., C + MMG + US) predictive models. Statistical analysis

The categorical and continuous variables were analyzed using the Chi-square test, Fisher’s exact test and independent t test, respectively. The training datasets were used to construct LR, AdaBoost, and NB classifiers-based MMG and US radiomics models, and the test datasets were used for independent validation. The clinicopathological factors that were potentially associated with PIK3CA mutation (p < 0.05 in univariate analysis) were integrated to construct the combined clinicopathological-radiomics, hybrid clinicopathological, multimodal image radiomics models. The predictive performance was assessed with respect to sensitivity (SEN), specificity (SPE), accuracy (ACC), the Youden index (YI), the positive predictive value (PPV), the negative predictive value (NPV), and the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC). Comparisons between AUC values were made via the DeLong test. The above processes were performed using FeAture Explorer Pro (FAEPro, V0.5.3) in Python (3.7.6) [29] (https://github.com/salan668/FAE), SPSS software (version 23.0), and MedCalc software (version 23.0.2). A two-tailed P value below 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant difference.

Results

Clinicopathological characteristics

Among the 186 BC patients, 59 (31.7%) had PIK3CA mutations. The rates of PIK3CA positivity were 31.5% (41/130) in the training set, and 32.1% (18/56) in the test set. ER and PR positive were found to be significantly associated with PIK3CA mutation in both sets (all p < 0.05). Meanwhile, no other clinicopathological factors such as age, clinical T stage, tumor maximum size, histological type, tumor grade, lymph node status, HER2 status, and Ki67 levels were associated with PIK3CA mutation in either set (all p > 0.05) (Table 1).

MMG- and US-based radiomics models

The intra- and interobserver reproducibility were substantial for MMG and US image ROIs segmentation and radiomics features extraction, with ICCs > 0.75 for all, and were robust for the further analysis.

For the MMG-based radiomics models, the LR classifier yielded better predictive ability with an AUC of 0.787 (95% CI 0.711, 0.883), compared with AdaBoost (AUC = 0.774; 95% CI 0.693, 0.852), and NB (AUC = 0.697; 95% CI 0.605, 0.789) in the training set. In the test set, the AUC values were 0.718 (95% CI 0.570, 0.865) for LR, 0.700 (95% CI 0.541, 0.833) for AdaBoost, and 0.656 (95% CI 0.498, 0.817) for NB (Fig. 3A–C). The LR-based MMG radiomics model attained a SEN of 87.8%, SPE of 62.9%, ACC of 73.8%, YI of 56.5%, PPV of 55.3%, and NPV of 91.8% in the training set, and a SEN of 66.6%, SPE of 73.2%, ACC of 76.7%, YI of 49.4%, PPV of 57.1%, and NPV of 82.4% in the test set, respectively (Table 2). Three, 2, and 1 optimal MMG based radiomics features were selected for the LR, Adaboost and NB classifiers, respectively. The most relevant feature in LR classifier were wavelet-HHH_GLSZM_gray level non uniformity (coefficient: 1.016) (Tables S3–S5).

With respect to the US-based radiomics models, the LR classifier achieved higher predictive ability with an AUC of 0.856 (95% CI 0.783, 0.919) than NB (AUC = 0.824; 95% CI 0.753, 0.893), and AdaBoost (AUC = 0.815; 95% CI 0.745, 0.887) in the training set. In the test set, the AUC values were 0.788 (95% CI 0.655, 0.923) for LR, 0.754 (95% CI 0.598, 0.911) for AdaBoost, and 0.732 (95% CI 0.577, 0.884) for NB (Fig. 3D–F).

The LR-based US radiomics model obtained a SEN of 85.3%, SPE of 78.6%, ACC of 80.7%, YI of 64.2%, PPV of 64.8%, and NPV of 92.1% in the training set, and a SEN of 77.7%, SPE of 79.8%, ACC of 78.5%, YI of 59.3%, PPV of 69.3%, and NPV of 88.2% in the test set, respectively (Table 2). Four, 3, and 2 optimal US based radiomics features were selected for the LR, AdaBoost, and NB classifiers, respectively. The most relevant feature in LR classifier were wavelet-HHH_GLDM_large dependence low gray level emphasis (coefficient: 2.244) (Tables S6–S8).

Clinicopathological-radiomics models

Two clinicopathological factors including ER positivity and PR positivity were found to be associated with PIK3CA mutation through univariable analysis (both p < 0.05), and were integrated with radiomics to construct the combined models.

The LR-based combined clinicopathological (C) and MMG radiomics (i.e., C + MMG + LR) model achieved a better AUC of 0.832 (95% CI 0.764, 0.899), than C + MMG + AdaBoost (AUC = 0.793; 95% CI 0.715, 0.874), and C + MMG + NB (AUC = 0.774; 95% CI 0.695, 0.851) in the training set. In the test set, the AUC values were 0.740 (95% CI 0.611, 0.867) for LR, 0.713 (95% CI 0.584, 0.853) for AdaBoost, and 0.708 (95% CI 0.542, 0.840) for NB (Fig. 4A–C). The C + MMG + LR model also obtained a SEN of 85.3%, SPE of 74.2%, ACC of 77.6%, YI of 59.5%, PPV of 60.4%, and NPV of 91.6% in the training set, and a SEN of 77.6%, SPE of 78.9%, ACC of 78.3%, YI of 55.7%, PPV of 63.4%, and NPV of 88.2% in the test set, respectively (Table 3).

For the combined clinicopathological and US radiomics (C + US) models, the C + US + LR model yielded an improved AUC of 0.878 (95% CI 0.814, 0.936), than C + US + NB (AUC = 0.845; 95% CI 0.779, 0.914), and C + US + AdaBoost (AUC = 0.839; 95% CI 0.764, 0.910) in the training set. In the test set, the AUC values were 0.827 (95% CI 0.706, 0.942) for LR, 0.795 (95% CI 0.664, 0.921) for AdaBoost, and 0.773 (95% CI 0.645, 0.892) for NB (Fig. 4D–F). The C + US + LR model also obtained a SEN of 85.4%, SPE of 82.0%, ACC of 83.1%, YI of 67.4%, PPV of 68.6%, and NPV of 92.4% in the training set, and a SEN of 83.3%, SPE of 81.5%, ACC of 82.1%, YI of 64.9%, PPV of 68.2%, and NPV of 91.1% in the test set, respectively (Table 3).

Hybrid clinicopathological and multimodal image radiomics models

The LR classifier-based hybrid model (i.e., C + MMG + US + LR) yielded a maximum AUC of 0.931 (95% CI 0.879, 0.982), which was greater than that of the C + MMG + US + AdaBoost model (AUC = 0.907; 95% CI 0.858, 0.957), and C + MMG + US + NB model (AUC = 0.875; 95% CI 0.816, 0.930) in the training set. In the test set, the AUC values were 0.899 (95% CI 0.783, 0.984) for LR, 0.860 (95% CI 0.739, 0.974) for AdaBoost, and 0.838 (95% CI 0.721, 0.952) for NB (Fig. 5A–C). The C + MMG + US + LR model also achieved a substantial SEN of 96.3%, SPE of 80.7%, ACC of 88.8%, YI of 79.9%, PPV of 71.8%, and NPV of 98.2% in the training set, and a SEN of 89.7%, SPE of 89.8%, ACC of 89.6%, YI of 78.8%, PPV of 81.1%, and NPV of 95.4% in the test set, respectively (Table 3).

Comparison of the predictive models

DeLong test revealed that for the radiomics models, the US + LR model obtained higher predictive performance than the MMG + LR model in the test set (AUC = 0.788 vs. 0.718; Z = 2.756; p < 0.05). With combination of clinicopathological factors, the C + US + LR model also achieved better ability than the C + MMG + LR model (AUC = 0.827 vs. 0.740; Z = 5.243; p < 0.001 in the test set). For the hybrid models, the C + MMG + US + LR yielded the most robust efficacy than C + MMG + US + Adaboost and C + MMG + US + NB models, with AUC = 0.931 vs. 0.907; Z = 1.985; p < 0.05, and AUC = 0.931 vs. 0.875; Z = 4.019; p < 0.001 in the training set, and AUC = 0.899 vs. 0.860; Z = 2.321; p < 0.05, and AUC = 0.899 vs. 0.838; Z = 3.896; p < 0.001 in the test set, respectively.

Discussion

PIK3CA gene status is an important biomarker for guiding targeted therapy strategies in BC patients [8–10]. In the present study, various ML-based multimodal radiomic approaches were applied, and clinicopathological factors were incorporated to explore their predictive efficacy. Our results showed that the hybrid C + US + MMG + LR model with improved performance (AUC of 0.899 in the test set) may be a promising clinical tool for the early prediction of PIK3CA mutation.

Clinicopathological characteristics are traditional vital indicators for treatment decisions and prognosis prediction. However, the associations of these characteristics with gene mutation remained controversy. Aleskandarany et al. [7] reported that poor prognostic factors such as high grade, large size, nodal involvement, vascular invasion, and highe proliferative fraction, are related to PIK3CA mutation. Cho et al. [4] indicated that PIK3CA mutation was a poor prognostic marker for BC patients unergoing adjuvant chemotherapy. Nevertheless, a meta analysis showed that ER/PR expression and relapse free survival (RFS) were associated with PIK3CA mutations [30]. Lian et al. [12] reported a PIK3CA mutation rate of 38.05% in 113 Chinese BC patients, and showed no correlation between age, lymph node status, tumor grade, ER, PR status and gene mutation. The present results revealed that the rate of PIK3CA mutation was 31.7% (59/186), and ER positivity and PR positivity were associated with PIK3CA mutation, which is similar to the findings of previous reports [12, 30]. However, other prognostic factors including tumor size, histological type, tumor grade, lymph node status, and Ki67 levels all had no predictive value (all p > 0.05). The inconsistencies in the results between the present study and previously published literature may due to differences in the sample selection including age, histological subtype, or ethnicity [4, 7]. Additional large series of patients and multicentre external studies are needed.

MMG and US are convenient imaging tools and have been widely used in BC diagnosis. Radiomics can extract a large number of high dimensional data from traditional images which has been the recent research hotpot for the prediction of medical outcome [15–17]. Huang et al. [18] reported an AUC of 0.715 for the prediction of PIK3CA mutation using conventional US image and molecular subtype. Mao et al. [31] applied MMG-based radiomics to predict ER positive and LN negative BC recurrence. Their results yielded AUC values of 0.88 and 0.84 in the internal and external test sets, respectively, with incorporation of clinical factors. Ma et al. [32] showed better performance when integrating both the CC and the MLO MMG radiomics features compared with either feature alone, with AUC values from 0.752 to 0.865 for predicting BC molecular subtype. The present study first utilized MMG- and US-based radiomics to predict PIK3CA mutation. Results showed that LR-based US radiomics obtained higher predictive ability than MMG-based radiomics (AUC = 0.788 vs. 0.718; p < 0.05). With integration of clinicopathological factors, the C + US + LR model also achieved better performance than C + MMG + LR model (0.827 vs. 0.740, p < 0.001). Through analyses, we found the most relevant features selected in both LR-based MMG and US radiomics models were wavelet-related features. After the wavelet transform, GLDM was the mainly features in US-based models, while in MMG-based radiomics was GLSZM. GLDM reflects the intrinsic tumor heterogeneity [33, 34]. GLSZM indicates the changed information of images in adjacent regions [34]. Wavelet transform can provides more higher details through the recollection of texture features [33]. Our results indicated that compared with MMG, US image could provide more valuable information of breast tumor heterogeneity for predicting PIK3CA mutation.

Additionally, we explored the performance of the hybrid clinicopathological, multimodal radiomics models. A meta analysis [35] has revealed that the combination of clinical features yields an aggregated C-index of 0.807 for CT-based radiomics, 0.806 for MRI-based radiomics, and 0.822 for PET-CT-based radiomics for predicting EGFR mutation in lung cancer. In BC studies, Cai et al. [24] utilized US and tomosynthesis radiomics to predict treatment response, and achieved an AUC of 0.81. Wang et al. [36] established a combined MRI and MMG radiomics model with clinical factors to predict node metastasis, and they reported AUC values of 0.916 and 0.892 in the internal and external validation cohorts, respectively. Among the various imaging technologies available, MMG and US are both convenient and low-cost tools for breast screening. For patients in primary institutions who had no expensive and large medical equipment such as MRI, CT, or PET-CT images available, the hybrid LR classifier algorithm-based MMG and US multimodal radiomics with clinicopathological factors achieved robust predictive ability, with AUC of 0.899 in the test set, which could help treatment decisions.

In terms of the ML classifiers, LR is a kind of multiple regression method, and is suitable for numerical binary outputs and the estimation of likelihood [37]. AdaBoost is an ensemble learning method and aims to reduce bias [37, 38]. NB is a common method to predict classification by considering feature probabilities [39]. Different ML algorithms had different efficacy in different diseases [37–39]. Zuo et al. [40] reported that AdaBoost exhibited the best performance in predicting BC recurrence than LR, NB, and other ML algorithms. Tahmassebi et al. [41] indicated that XGBoost with multiparametric MRI achieved stable prediction for disease specific survival (AUC = 0.92), while LR has the greatest predictive ability for recurrence-free survival (AUC = 0.83). Our results showed that compared with AdaBoost and NB, LR classifier had a better ability for predicting PIK3CA mutation.

The present study had several strengths. First, compared with prior studies that used clinical, pathological factors or traditional images to analyse the association with gene mutation, the present study employed various ML-based multimodal radiomics and integrated clinicopathological factors to explore more convincing results. Second, our results revealed that US radiomics had better efficacy than MMG radiomics, and the hybrid model yielded robust predictive performance. Finally, with regard to MRI or other images, MMG and US images have more common application in primary health care institutions, thus making the radiomics analysis with broader clinical prospects.

The present study had several limitations. First, the present study was a retrospective analysis conducted at a single institution, and patients with non-mass-like lesions were excluded, which may have resulted in biased selection. Second, to compare the efficacy between MMG-based radiomics and US-based radiomics models, we only selected a single MMG image and a single greyscale US image for radiomics features extraction and analysis. Although, the selected images displayed the most distinct internal and peripheral structure of the target tumor, they may directly impact study reproducibility. Also, the present study did not include MRI, tomosynthesis, or multimodal US images. Another multimodality study by our team has been initiated to explore these fields. Third, the performance was not stratified according to tumor molecular subtypes because of the relative small number of subjects in each subgroup. Finally, this article did not discriminate gene mutation hotpots such as exon 9 or exon 20. Although gene mutation hotspots were beyond the scope of the present study, further studies are expected to address this topic.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the novel, effective multimodal radiomics approach provides reliable prediction for PIK3CA mutation which possesses high clinical value for the development of appropriate therapeutic strategies.

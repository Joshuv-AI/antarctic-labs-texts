Integrated Semigroups for abstract differential-algebraic equations
Mehmet Erbay, Birgit Jacob and Timo Reis

Abstract
We study integrated semigroups for infinite-dimensional differential-algebraic equations (DAEs) admitting a resolvent index. Building on the notion of integrated semigroups for the abstract Cauchy problem d d ​ t ​ x = A ​ x \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x=Ax , we extend this concept to the DAE setting. The resulting framework is used to analyze inhomogeneous DAEs and plays a central role in characterizing their solutions.

Introduction

We consider infinite-dimensional differential-algebraic equations (short DAEs)

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}Ex(t)

\displaystyle=Ax(t)+f(t),\quad t\in[0,\infty),

(1)

\displaystyle x(0)

\displaystyle=x_{0}.

Here,
X
,

denote complex Banach spaces,

is bounded from
X
to
Z
, denoted by

,
A\colon\mathrm{dom}(A)\subseteq X\to Z
is closed and densely defined,
x_{0}\in X
and

In general, a solution of (

(\lambda E-A)^{-1}
, for
\lambda\in\rho(E,A)\coloneqq\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,(\lambda E-A)^{-1}\in L(Z,X)\}

The drawback is that, although existence is ensured, the solution must be obtained via an inverse Laplace transform, which is often a substantial obstacle. It is therefore desirable to adopt a time-domain approach.
This is the point where integrated semigroups come into play: A linear operator

\|(\lambda I_{X}-A)^{-1}\|\leq C|\lambda|^{n-1},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>\omega},

\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}((\lambda I_{X}-A)^{-1})^{n+2}
, which is equal to

[
25

Further, it is possible to show that for every initial value of
\mathrm{dom}(A^{n+2})=\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}((\lambda I_{X}-A)^{-1})^{n+2}
the Cauchy problem

[

,

,
2
,

Note, that in infinite dimensions all these index notions do not have to be equivalent (see

This raises the question to what extent the concept of integrated semigroups can be extended to DAEs, which we dealt with in this article.
So far the generation of integrated semigroups for DAEs have been analysed in
[
23
,
22
]
, where they studied the well-posedness of such systems for

We now build upon that work and extend it by considering DAEs with inhomogeneities, which is substantial for future research and applications, such as DAEs with input and output (in particular port-Hamiltonian DAEs).
While doing so, we make use of already known concepts and theories concerning classical integrated semigroups
[
2

We subsequently focus on the study of (
1

[
14
]
. To be more precise, instead of studying the DAE (
1

the altered DAE

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}R(\lambda)w_{\lambda}(t)=w_{\lambda}(t)+f_{\lambda}(t),

(3)

where

either denotes the

E
-resolvent
or the

E
-resolvent
of

. These are respectively given by the functions
\lambda\mapsto E(\lambda E-A)^{-1}
,
\lambda\mapsto(\lambda E-A)^{-1}E
defined on the resolvent set of
(E,A)
, and with adapted initial condition and inhomogeneity (both depending on

In
[
14
]
, it was shown—under an index concept slightly stronger than the resolvent index—that

is a resolvent on the closed subspace
\overline{\operatorname{ran}R(\lambda)^{k}}
. Specifically, there exists an operator

A_{R}\colon\mathrm{dom}(A_{R})\subseteq\overline{\operatorname{ran}R(\lambda)^{k}}\to\overline{\operatorname{ran}R(\lambda)^{k}}

such that

R(\lambda)\big|_{\overline{\operatorname{ran}R(\lambda)^{k}}}=(\lambda I-A_{R})^{-1}.

Further, they have shown that this operator

contributes directly to solving (
3

[
18
,
23
,
12
,
31
]
.
In the following, we take on the same task under slightly weaker assumptions and show that

always generates a strongly continuous semigroup on a subspace.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the concept of integrated semigroups for DAEs and investigate several of their properties.
In Section
3
we analyse the representations of solutions of inhomogeneous DAEs imposing various conditions on the inhomogeneity. To be more precise, we investigate the cases where the inhomogeneity maps into the images or the kernels of the right- or left-
E
-resolvent, which is often the case for a DAE with Weierstraß form. Subsequently we consider an example which demonstrates that these assumptions are generally too restrictive and provide an approach in Hilbert spaces in which these solutions can be characterized for arbitrary inhomogeneities.
In Section
4
we conclude our research on integrated semigroups by showing, when they generate a strongly continuous semigroup on a subspace, as is usually the case for ordinary partial differential equations. In particular, for DAEs, this is the case when certain conditions are imposed on the kernel of

.

Differential-algebraic integrated semigroups

2.1
Preliminaries

We start by focusing on the DAE

\displaystyle=Ax(t)+f(t),\quad t\in[0,\infty),

\displaystyle x(0)

\displaystyle=x_{0},

(Solutions)

(i)

2.2
Construction of differential-algebraic integrated semigroups

Let
X
,
Z
,
E
, and
A
be as in Assumption
2.1
.
Our goal is to develop the solution theory of DAEs and to present it using different approaches. In particular, we extend the theory of integrated semigroups in order to represent the resolvent of
(E,A)
in a Laplace-transform-like form, which is possible whenever the complex resolvent index exists.

We start by considering the homogeneous system

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}Ex(t)

\displaystyle=Ax(t),\quad t\in[0,\infty),

(9)

\displaystyle x(0)

\displaystyle=x_{0}.

For the standard abstract Cauchy problem (i.e. with
X=Z
and
E=I_{X}
), the solutions can be represented by a strongly continuous semigroup, provided that the operator
A
generates such a semigroup. If this is not the case, then, provided the resolvent of
A
is polynomially bounded on some right half-plane, the solutions can be described by an integrated semigroup, which in turn yields representation formulas for the resolvent of
A
(see
[
24
,
1
,
2
]
).
To be more precise, as long as the resolvent of
A
grows polynomially, it is possible to show that for every initial value in
\mathrm{dom}(A^{k})=\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}((\lambda I_{X}-A)^{-1})^{k}
(for a
\lambda\in\rho(A)
) there is a classical solution of the Cauchy problem (where
k\in\mathbb{N}
depends on the growth rate of the resolvent) (see
[
18
,
3
]
). In this case,
A
is the generator of an integrated semigroup, i.e. for all
\lambda\in\rho(A)\cap\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>\omega}
(for
\omega>0
large enough)

(\lambda I_{X}-A)^{-1}x=\lambda^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}S(t)x\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad x\in X,

holds, where
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a family of bounded operators on
X
.

Now, we go back to our original problem (
9
). Since we assumed the existence of the complex resolvent index throughout this section, one always finds a unique (mild) solution of (
9
) for all
x_{0}\in\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\lambda)^{p}
by Theorem
2.3
(with
\lambda\in\rho(E,A)
). The difference to the Cauchy problem is that these initial values are not dense in the whole space
X
. In general, under our given assumptions it is not even possible to split the space into
\overline{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\lambda)^{p}}\oplus\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}R_{r}(\lambda)^{p}
, as it is possible in finite-dimensions thanks to the Weierstraß form (see
[
19
]
). However, this is not a problem as the solutions of (
9
) always lie in
\overline{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\lambda)^{p}}
(see
[
34
, Prop. 4.1&Prop. 5.1]
).

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(E,A)
satisfy Assumption
2.1
and set
p:=p_{\mathrm{res}}^{(E,A)}+1

(S_{r}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on

(10)

Proof.

First assume that
x_{0}\in\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}

\|x(t)\|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\omega_{0}-\operatorname{i}\infty}^{\omega_{0}+\operatorname{i}\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{0}t}\frac{\|R_{r}(\lambda)z_{0}\|}{|\lambda-\mu|^{p}}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq C_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{0}t}\|z_{0}\|\;\text{ for all $t\geq 0$},

for some suitable

Using

\displaystyle x_{1}(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}x(t_{0})\,\mathrm{d}t_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(\mu R_{r}(\mu)-(\mu E-A)^{-1}A\right)\int_{0}^{t}x(t_{0})\,\mathrm{d}t_{0}

\displaystyle=\mu\int_{0}^{t}R_{r}(\mu)x(t_{0})\,\mathrm{d}t_{0}-R_{r}(\mu)x(t)+R_{r}(\mu)x(0)\;\text{ for all $t\geq 0$}.

By

Inductively, we define

In particular, if
f\in H^{p}_{0,l}([0,\infty);Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}})
(as defined in (
8
)), then the initial value of the solution necessarily vanishes at
t=0
.

3.2
Inhomogeneity in
Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}

In order to deal with inhomogeneities in
Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
, we are going to generalise the solution theory of the inhomogeneous abstract Cauchy problem
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)=Ax(t)+f(t)
,
x(0)=x_{0}
, and extend the proofs of
[
2
, Chapter 2.3]
to the inhomogeneous DAE case with the help of Lemma
2.11
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(E,A)
satisfy Assumption
2.1
and set
p:=p_{\mathrm{res}}^{(E,A)}+1
. Let
f\in L^{1}([0,\infty);Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})
and let
x\colon[0,\infty)\to X
be a continuous mild solution of (
4
). Then
x(t)\in X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

For simplicity, assume that
\lambda=0\in\rho(E,A)
.
Let
x
be a mild solution of (
4
). Then, for
t,h\geq 0
,

Ex(t+h)-Ex(t)=A\int_{t}^{t+h}x(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{t}^{t+h}f(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

or equivalently

A^{-1}E\left(x(t+h)-x(t)\right)-A^{-1}\int_{t}^{t+h}f(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\int_{t}^{t+h}x(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(19)

Observe that

A^{-1}(Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})\subseteq X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\subseteq\overline{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\lambda)^{k}},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(20)

Thus,
A^{-1}\int_{t}^{t+h}f(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\in X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
. Altogether, the left-hand side of (
19
) maps into
\overline{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\lambda)}
. By dividing both sides by
h
and letting
h\to 0
, one deduces
x(t)\in\overline{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\lambda)}
.
Repeating this iteratively, one obtains
x(t)\in\overline{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\lambda)^{k}}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,

x(t)\in\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\overline{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\lambda)^{k}}.

As seen in
[
32
, Prop. 5.1]
the sequence
\overline{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\lambda)^{k}}
stagnates at

. This leads to
x(t)\in\overline{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\lambda)^{p}}=X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
.
∎

Since this is the case we are interested in, we assume
f\colon[0,\infty)\to Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
. Further, we define for
x_{0}\in X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
the function

v(t)\coloneqq S_{l}(t)Ex_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S_{l}(s)f(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad t\in[0,\infty),

(21)

where
(S_{l}(t))_{t\geq 0}
denotes the
p
-times integrated semigroup from Corollary
2.8

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(E,A)
satisfy Assumption
2.1
, set
p:=p_{\mathrm{res}}^{(E,A)}+1
and let
(S_{l}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
(S_{r}(t))_{t\geq 0}
be the
p
-times integrated semigroups of
(E,A)
. Let
f\in L^{1}([0,\infty);Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})
,
x_{0}\in\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}
and

as in (
21
).

(a)

If there exists a mild solution
x\colon[0,\infty)\to X
of (
4
), then
v\in C^{p}([0,\infty);Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})
and
Ex=v^{p}
.

(b)

If there exists a classical solution
x\colon[0,\infty)\to X
of (
4
), then
v\in C^{p+1}([0,\infty);Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})
and
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}Ex=v^{p+1}
.

Proof.

(a)

Let
0\leq\tau\leq t<\infty
and define
w(\tau)\coloneqq ES_{r}(t-\tau)\int_{0}^{\tau}x(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma
. By Lemma
3.5
this is indeed well defined, since
x
is a continuous mild solution and
x(t)\in X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
for all
t\geq 0
. In addition
S_{r}(t)
is closed (as a bounded operator) and, thus,
\int_{0}^{\tau}x(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\in\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}\cap\mathrm{dom}(A)
for all
\tau\geq 0
. Hence, by Lemma
2.11

(b)
and
(c)
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}w(\tau)

\displaystyle=-AS_{r}(t-\tau)\int_{0}^{\tau}x(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma-\frac{(t-\tau)^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}E\int_{0}^{\tau}x(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+ES_{r}(t-\tau)x(\tau)

\displaystyle=S_{l}(t-\tau)\left(Ex(\tau)-A\int_{0}^{\tau}x(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)-\frac{(t-\tau)^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}E\int_{0}^{\tau}x(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=S_{l}(t-\tau)\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+Ex(0)\right)-\frac{(t-\tau)^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}E\int_{0}^{\tau}x(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Since
\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}w(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau=w(t)-w(0)=0
we obtain

\int_{0}^{t}S_{l}(t-\tau)\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+Ex(0)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-\tau)^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}E\int_{0}^{\tau}x(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Thus, by taking the
(p+1)
-th derivative, we obtain

\displaystyle Ex(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{p+1}}{\mathrm{d}t^{p+1}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-\tau)^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}E\int_{0}^{\tau}x(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{p+1}}{\mathrm{d}t^{p+1}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}S_{l}(t-\tau)\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}f(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+Ex(0)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{p+1}}{\mathrm{d}t^{p+1}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}S_{l}(\tau)\left(\int_{0}^{t-\tau}f(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+Ex(0)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{p}}{\mathrm{d}t^{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}S_{l}(\tau)f(t-\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau+S_{l}(t)Ex(0)\right)=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{p}}{\mathrm{d}t^{p}}v(t).

Note that we used
[
2
, Prop. 1.3.6]
in the last equation.

(b)

By
[
14
, Thm. 2.1]
every classical solution is also a mild solution and
Ex
is continuously differentiable. Together with Part
(a)
we obtain
Ex(t)=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{p}}{\mathrm{d}t^{p}}v(t)
and thus

. ∎

Next, we generalise
[
2
, Lem. 3.2.10]
. To do so, we have to assume, that

maps into the range of
E
. Thus, we assume that there exists a
\tilde{f}\in L^{1}([0,\infty);X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})
, such that

. Furthermore, we define

\tilde{v}(t)\coloneqq S_{r}(t)x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S_{r}(t-\tau)\tilde{f}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad t\geq 0.

(22)

By Lemma
2.11

(b)
we have
ES_{r}(t)=S_{l}(t)E
and, thus,
v(t)=E\tilde{v}(t)
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(E,A)
satisfy Assumption
2.1
, set
p:=p_{\mathrm{res}}^{(E,A)}+1
and let
(S_{l}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
(S_{r}(t))_{t\geq 0}
be the
p
-times integrated semigroups of
(E,A)
.
Let
\tilde{f}\in L^{1}([0,\infty);X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})
and

,
x_{0}\in\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}
and
\tilde{v}
as in (

).

(a)

If
\tilde{v}\in C^{p}([0,\infty);X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})
, then
x=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{p}}{\mathrm{d}t^{p}}\tilde{v}
is a mild solution of (
4
).

(b)

If
\tilde{v}\in C^{p+1}([0,\infty);X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})
, then
x=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{p}}{\mathrm{d}t^{p}}\tilde{v}
is a classical solution of (
4
).

Proof.

(a)

We start by integrating
\tilde{v}
and using Fubini’s Theorem

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{v}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}S_{r}(\tau)x_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\tau}S_{r}(\sigma)\tilde{f}(\tau-\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}S_{r}(\tau)x_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t-\sigma}S_{r}(\tau)\tilde{f}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Applying
A
from the left and using Lemma
2.11

(d)
we deduce

\displaystyle A\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{v}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=ES_{r}(t)x_{0}-\frac{t^{p}}{p!}Ex_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}ES_{r}(t-\tau)\tilde{f}(\tau)-\frac{(t-\tau)^{p}}{p!}\underbrace{E\tilde{f}(\tau)}_{=f(\tau)}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=E\tilde{v}(t)-\frac{t^{p}}{p!}Ex_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-\tau)^{p}}{p!}f(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(23)

Since
E\tilde{v}=v\in C^{p}([0,\infty);Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})
,
\tilde{v}(0)=0
and

is closed, we deduce

A\tilde{v}^{(k)}(t)=E\tilde{v}^{(k+1)}(t)-\frac{t^{p-(k+1)}}{(p-(k+1))!}Ex_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-\sigma)^{p-(k+1)}}{(p-(k+1))!}f(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

(24)

for

. Hence, for

).

(b)

If

) and (

). ∎

The condition

may appear somewhat restrictive at first.
However, one should keep in mind that we are already restricting the system to

and
Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
.
Under the additional assumptions that

and that

is closed, we always have

.
Consequently, every

lies in the range of

.

In what follows, we specify a condition ensuring the existence of such a function

.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(E,A)
satisfy Assumption
2.1
and set
p:=p_{\mathrm{res}}^{(E,A)}+1
.
If

is closed and
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\cap\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}=\{0\}
, then
E\colon X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\to Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
is boundedly invertible.

Proof.

Since
E(\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\mu)^{p})\subseteq\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{l}(\mu)^{p}
, it is easy to see that
E(X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})\subseteq Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
. Now, let
z\in\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{l}(\mu)^{p+1}
. Then there exists some
y\in Z
with

z=R_{l}(\mu)^{p+1}y=ER_{r}(\mu)^{p}(\mu E-A)^{-1}y.

Hence,

\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{l}(\mu)^{p+1}\subseteq E(\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\mu)^{p})\subseteq E(X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}),

and a closure gives
Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\subseteq\overline{E(X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})}=E(X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})
. Therefore,

is surjective.
Since
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\cap\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}=\{0\}
and

,

∎

3.3
An Example with
X\neq X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\oplus X_{\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}}

In the following example, we highlight that the spaces
X
and
Z
do not always decompose into
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\oplus X_{\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}}
and
Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\oplus Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}}
, respectively. Thus, it is not always possible to represent the solution of the inhomogeneous system (
17
) using Proposition
3.3
and Lemma
3.6
.

We consider the following system

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}x_{1}(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\xi}x_{1}(\xi,t),

\displaystyle\xi\in(0,1),t\geq 0,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\xi}x_{2}(\xi,t),

\displaystyle\xi\in(1,2),t\geq 0,

\displaystyle x_{1}(0,t)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle t\geq 0,

\displaystyle x_{1}(1,t)

\displaystyle=x_{2}(1,t),

\displaystyle t\geq 0.

Next we rewrite this as an infinite-dimensional DAE. To this end, we introduce the evaluation operator at
\xi\in[a,b]
by
\delta_{\xi}\in H^{1}(a,b)^{\ast}
.Then the above system is represented by a DAE with operators
E\colon X\to Z
and
A\colon\mathrm{dom}(A)\subseteq X\to Z
with

E=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&0\\
0&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad A=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\xi}&0\\
0&-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\xi}\\
-\delta_{0}&0\\
-\delta_{1}&\delta_{1}\end{bmatrix},

for

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}(A)

\displaystyle=H^{1}(0,1)\times H^{1}(1,2),

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=L^{2}(0,1)\times L^{2}(1,2),

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=L^{2}(0,1)\times L^{2}(1,2)\times\mathbb{C}^{2}.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}\geq 0}
we compute

(\lambda E-A)^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\\
\mu_{1}\\
\mu_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\mu_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\cdot}+\int_{0}^{\cdot}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(\cdot-\sigma)}f(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
\displaystyle-\mu_{2}+\mu_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda}+\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(1-\sigma)}f(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\int_{1}^{\cdot}g(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\end{bmatrix},\quad\begin{bmatrix}f\\

\mu_{1}\\
\mu_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in Z.

Since the right-hand side of (

\lambda

is at most

.

Next, we determine the nullspaces and the closure of the ranges of

and

in order to show that

and

do not split into
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
,
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}}
and
Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
,
Z_{\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}}
, respectively.
By (

) we have

(\lambda E-A)^{-1}E\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\cdot}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(\cdot-\sigma)}x_{1}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\\

Assume that

in

,

, we obtain

and

in

. Applying the Laplace transform

to this equation yields

Since

for a suitable
\lambda
and for all

we deduce

and, by the uniqueness of the Laplace transform,

. Hence

X_{\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}}=\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}(\lambda E-A)^{-1}E=\{0\}\times L^{2}(1,2).

Now, for simplicity we assume that
\lambda=0
. This is possible as the kernels and ranges of the left- and right-resolvents are independent of the choice of

(see
[

, Lem. 2.1.2]
).

we observe

and, since

is dense in

,

. However, as the second entry of (

) is not empty, we see that

g\\

\mu_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\mu_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\cdot}+\int_{0}^{\cdot}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(\cdot-r)}f(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\\

Hence, in contrast to the space

.

.

Note that by choosing

0&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad A=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\xi}&0\\
0&-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\xi}\\

4
Generation of a strongly continuous semigroup on a subspace

In this section, we show that the differential-algebraic integrated semigroups introduced in Section
2
generate a strongly continuous semigroup on a suitable subspace, analogous to
[
24
, Thm. 5.2]
for the abstract Cauchy problem. To address this, we extend the Assumption
2.1
as follows.

Assumption 4.1
.

(a)

X
and
Z
are Banach spaces.

(b)

E
is a bounded linear operator from
X
to
Z
.

(c)

A\colon\mathrm{dom}(A)\subseteq X\to Z
is closed and densely defined.

(d)

The operator pair
(E,A)
has a
complex resolvent index
, i.e. the smallest number
p_{\mathrm{res}}^{(E,A)}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, such that there exists a
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
,
C>0
with
\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>\omega}\subseteq\rho(E,A)
and

\|(\lambda E-A)^{-1}\|\leq C|\lambda|^{p_{\mathrm{res}}^{(E,A)}-1}\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>\omega}.

(e)

The spaces
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
and
\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}E
intersect trivially, i.e.
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\cap\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}E=\{0\}
.

The Assumption
4.1
(e)
does not hold in general. It is, for example, satisfied in the presence of the radiality index
[
11
,
32
]
or the resolvent growth condition
(D_{k})
introduced in
[
14
]
, since these conditions imply
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\cap\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}=\{0\}
and hence
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\cap\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}E=\{0\}
.
Later, we focus on a specific class of DAEs which, when rewritten appropriately, always satisfy this condition.
Before stating the main result, we begin with the following necessary lemma and, in accordance with the notation of Section
2
, we set
p:=p_{\mathrm{res}}^{(E,A)}+1
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(E,A)
satisfy Assumption
4.1
, set
p:=p_{\mathrm{res}}^{(E,A)}+1
and let
(S_{l}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
(S_{r}(t))_{t\geq 0}
be the
p
-times integrated semigroups of
(E,A)
. Let
x\in X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
. If
S_{r}(t)x=0
for all
t\geq 0
, then
x=0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.11

(d)
one obtains

0=A\int_{0}^{t}S_{r}(\tau)x\,\mathrm{d}\tau-ES_{r}(t)x=-\frac{t^{p}}{p!}Ex,\quad t\geq 0,

(33)

and, especially,
Ex=0
.
Since
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\cap\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}E=\{0\}
, the assertion follows.
∎

The next result is a generalization of
[
24
, Thm. 5.2]
. The main difficulty lies in omitting the fact that here
R_{r}(\mu)
is a pseudo-resolvent, which can not be written as a resolvent on a subspace without further assumptions.
To ease the notation in the following, we denote for
k\in\mathbb{N}
the
k
-th integral of
t\mapsto S_{r}(t)x
for
x\in X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
by
S_{r}^{[k]}(t)x\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-v)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}S_{r}(v)\,\mathrm{d}v
. This exists due to Lemma
2.10
. Further, we define
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

C_{m}\coloneqq\{x\in X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\;|\;t\mapsto S_{r}(t)x\in C^{m}([0,\infty);X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})\},

(34)

where
C^{m}([0,\infty);X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}})
denotes the space of
m
-times continuously differentiable functions with values in
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
. We write
S_{r}^{(p)}(t)x
to denote the
p
-th derivative of
t\mapsto S_{r}^{(p)}(t)x
for
x\in C_{p}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(E,A)
satisfy Assumption
4.1
, set
p:=p_{\mathrm{res}}^{(E,A)}+1
and let
(S_{l}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
(S_{r}(t))_{t\geq 0}
be the
p
-times integrated semigroups of
(E,A)
, with
\|S_{r}(t)\|\leq C\mathrm{e}^{\omega t}
and
\|S_{l}(t)\|\leq C\mathrm{e}^{\omega t}
.
Then, for
C_{m}
as in (
34
),

(a)

C_{2p}\subseteq\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}\subseteq C_{p}
.

(b)

F\coloneqq\overline{C_{2p}}^{\|\cdot\|_{F}}\subseteq C_{p}
is a Hilbert space, where

\|x_{0}\|_{F}\coloneqq\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}S_{r}^{(p)}(t)x_{0}\|

defines a norm on
\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}
.

(c)

(S_{r}^{(p)}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
F
.

Proof.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
x_{0}\in C_{m}
. By Lemma
2.11

(f)
one has

S_{r}(t)S_{r}(s)x_{0}=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-v)^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}S_{r}(v+s)x_{0}\,\mathrm{d}v-\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{s^{j}}{j!}S_{r}^{[p-1-j]}(t)x_{0}.

(35)

Thus,
S_{r}(s)x_{0}\in C_{m+1}
and consequently
S_{r}(s)C_{m}\subseteq C_{m+1}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
k\leq p-1
and let
m\geq p
. Then, using (
35
) and the continuity of
S_{r}(t)
from
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
to
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
, one shows inductively

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{r}(t)S_{r}^{(k)}(s)x_{0}&=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-v)^{p-1-k}}{(p-1-k)!}S_{r}(\tau+s)x_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\tau-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{t^{p-1-j}}{(p-1-j)!}S_{r}^{(k-1-j)}(s)x_{0}\\
&\quad-\sum_{j=k}^{p-1}\frac{s^{j-k}}{(j-k)!}S_{r}^{[p-1-j]}(t)x_{0}.\end{split}

(36)

Thus
S_{r}^{(k)}(s)C_{m}\subseteq C_{m+1}
and
S_{r}(t)S_{r}^{(k)}(0)=0
for all
1\leq k\leq p-1
. Since
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}\cap\mathchoice{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker\,}{\rm ker}{\rm ker}E=\{0\}
, one can use Lemma
4.2
to obtain
S_{r}^{(k)}(0)=0
on
C_{k}
for all
0\leq k\leq p-1
. Differentiating (
36
) with respect to
s
once more, one derives

S_{r}(t)S_{r}^{(p)}(s)x_{0}=S_{r}(t+s)x_{0}-\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}S_{r}^{(j)}(s)x_{0}

(37)

and, thus,
S_{r}^{(p)}(s)C_{m}\subseteq C_{m}
for all
p\leq m
. Rewriting (
37
), one obtains

S_{r}(t)\left(S_{r}^{(p)}(0)-I_{C_{p}}\right)=0

and
\frac{\mathrm{d}^{p}}{\mathrm{d}s^{p}}S_{r}(0)=I_{C_{p}}
on
C_{p}
by Lemma
4.2
, where
I_{C_{p}}
denotes the identity on
C_{p}
. Taking the
p
-th derivative with respect to
t
from (
37
) one obtains

S_{r}^{(p)}(t)S_{r}^{(p)}(s)=S_{r}^{(p)}(t+s)

on
C_{m}
,
m\geq p
. Thus,
(S_{r}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
C_{m}
for all
m\geq p
.
Now, let
x_{0}\in C_{m}
,
m\geq p+1
, and take the derivative of (
37
) along
s

S_{r}(t)S_{r}^{(p+1)}(s)x_{0}=S_{r}^{(1)}(t+s)x_{0}-\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}S_{r}^{(j+1)}(s)x_{0}.

Thus,
S_{r}^{(p+1)}(s)C_{m}\subseteq C_{m-1}
, for all
m\geq p+1
, and

S_{r}(t)S_{r}^{(p+1)}(0)x_{0}=S_{r}^{(1)}(t)x_{0}-\frac{t^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}x_{0},

as
S_{r}^{(p)}(0)=I_{C_{p}}
. Hence, by integration by parts,

\displaystyle R_{r}(\mu)S_{r}^{(p+1)}(0)x_{0}

\displaystyle=\mu^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mu t}S_{r}(t)S_{r}^{(p+1)}(0)x_{0}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\mu^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mu t}S_{r}^{(1)}(t)x_{0}-\frac{t^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}x_{0}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\mu R_{r}(\mu)x_{0}-x_{0}.

Consequently, one obtains

R_{r}(\mu)\left(\mu I_{C_{m}}-S_{r}^{(p+1)}(0)\right)=I_{C_{m}}

on
C_{m}
and
C_{m}\subseteq R_{r}(\mu)C_{m-1}
for
m\geq p+1
. Since
S_{r}(t)C_{m}\subseteq C_{m+1}
and
R_{r}(\mu)=\mu^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mu t}S_{r}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t
one has
R_{r}(\mu)C_{m}\subseteq C_{m+1}
for all
m\geq 0
.
Combining these two, one obtains

R_{r}(\mu)C_{m-1}=C_{m},\quad m\geq p+1

(38)

and
C_{2p}=R_{r}(\mu)C_{2p-1}=\ldots=R_{r}(\mu)^{p}C_{p}\subseteq\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}|_{X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}}
.
By Theorem
2.3
there exists for all
x_{0}\in\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}
a
z_{0}\in X
with
x_{0}=R_{r}(\mu)^{p}z_{0}
and a mild solution
x(\cdot)
with

\|S_{r}^{(p)}(t)x_{0}\|=\|x(t)\|\leq C\mathrm{e}^{\omega t}\|z_{0}\|,\quad t\geq 0,

(39)

and, in particular,
\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}\subseteq C_{p}
holds, which shows the first assertion. Note that we used the fact that
S_{r}(\cdot)x_{0}
is the
p
-th integral of the mild solution
x(\cdot)
.

To address the second assertion, we define on
\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}R_{r}(\mu)^{p}
the norm

\|x_{0}\|_{F}\coloneqq\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}S_{r}^{(p)}(t)x_{0}\|

and denote the closure of
C_{2p}
with respect to
\|\cdot\|
by
F
. By invoking
S_{r}^{(p)}(0)x_{0}=x_{0}
, we have

\|x\|\leq\|x\|_{F}.

(40)

Let
(x_{n})_{n}\subseteq C_{2p}
with
x_{n}\to x\in F
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{F}
. By (
40
)
x_{n}
converges to
x
in
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
and, since
S_{r}(t)
is bounded,
S_{r}(t)x_{n}
converges to
S_{r}(t)x
. Since
x_{n}\in C_{2p}
the functions
t\mapsto S_{r}(t)x_{n}
are at least
p
-times continuously differentiable. Using the semigroup property of
(S_{r}^{(p)}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on

, one has

\|S_{r}^{(p)}(s)x_{n}\|_{F}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}S_{r}^{(p)}(t+s)x_{n}\|\leq\mathrm{e}^{\omega s}\|x_{n}\|_{F}.

(41)

Together with (

) one obtains

\|S_{r}^{(p)}(s)x_{n}-S_{r}^{(p)}(s)x_{m}\|\leq\mathrm{e}^{\omega s}\|x_{n}-x_{m}\|_{F},

(42)

and since
S_{r}^{(k)}(0)x_{n}=0
for all
1\leq k\leq p-1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\mapsto S_{r}(t)x
is
p
-times continuously differentiable. Thus,
x\in C_{p}
and
F\subseteq C_{p}
.

Now, let
x\in F\subseteq C_{p}
. Then
S_{r}^{(p)}(t)x
is well-defined. Let
(x_{n})_{n}\subseteq C_{2p}
be a sequence converging to
x
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{F}
. Then by (

) and (

)
(S_{r}^{(p)}(t)x_{n})_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in

and in
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
. Thus,
S_{r}^{(p)}(t)x_{n}
converges to some

in

and
S_{r}^{(p)}(t)x
in
X_{\mathchoice{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran\,}{\rm ran}{\rm ran}}
. Thus,
S_{r}^{(p)}(t)x=g\in F
. Consequently,
S_{r}^{(p)}(t)\colon F\to F
is well-defined for all

. Since

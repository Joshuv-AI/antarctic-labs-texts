Rh(II)-Catalyzed Selective C(sp3)–H/C(sp2)–H Bonds Cascade Insertion to Construct [6–8–6] Benzo-Fused Scaffold
Authors: Tao Yu, Feifei Fang, Haowen Shou, Yazhou Li, Zhiyan Qu, Feiyang Chen, Yu Zhang, Jiang Wang, Hong Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The fused eight-membered carbocycles (EMCs) play vital roles in the medicinal and biological investigations of many natural products and marketed drugs. The traditional synthesis of [6–8–6] benzo-fused derivatives involves multistep reactions and low yields, making the development of a one-step synthesis method a more challenging work. Here, we present a novel strategy for one-step construction of [6–8–6] benzo-fused scaffold from propargyl diazoacetates substituted with benzyl-nitrogen heterocyclic ring via Rh(ll)-catalyzed carbene/alkyne metathesis (CAM) and selective C–H bond insertion. This method exhibits a specific substrate scope, simple operation, mild reaction conditions, and high atom efficiency. Mechanistically, the process involves sequential CAM, 1,3-H-shift, intramolecular nucleophilic attack, and selective C(sp3)–H/C(sp2)–H bonds cascade insertion. Notably, the unique spirocyclic zwitterionic intermediate generated in this sequence contributes to N-heterocycle migration and fused eight-membered carbocycle formation. Additionally, the C(sp3)–H bond insertion connected to the oxygen atom rather than the nitrogen atom has been unexpectedly confirmed with the assistance of the spirocyclic zwitterionic intermediate. Overall, our findings open up a new avenue for the construction of [6–8–6] benzo-fused scaffold.

The fused eight-membered carbocycles (EMCs) represent an important class of structural cores extensively present in natural products, pharmaceuticals, and bioactive molecules (Figure 1).1 Among them, [6–8–6] fused scaffolds have emerged as privileged drug scaffolds because of their distinctive three-dimensional properties, moderate ring size, and flexible conformation, receiving more and more attention from both academia and the pharmaceutical industry.2 For example, ophiobolin sesterterpenes contain [5–8–5] fused systems, which lead to potent cytotoxic effects against various cancer cell lines. Of note, (+)-isoschizandrin and (+)-steganone, known for their antiulcer and antiviral properties, exhibit a [6–8–6] benzo-fused scaffold (Figure 1).3 Although these fused eight-membered carbocycles possess extensive pharmacological activities, their construction remains challenging because of unfavorable entropic and enthalpic effects, along with transannular interactions associated with ring-closure processes.4 A limited number of strategies were developed in the past decades, mainly including intramolecular cyclization [ring-closing metathesis (RCM), radical cyclization, etc.],5 cycloadditions,6 and ring expansions/contractions.7 However, most of them were typically used to synthesize fused eight-membered carbocycles with nonaromatic rings rather than benzo-fused eight-membered carbocycles.5b,6a,6d,7b,8 Shi et al. reported the total synthesis of (+)-isoschizandrin, accomplished in nine steps with an overall yield of 31%, yielding a natural product containing a [6–8–6] benzo-fused scaffold (Scheme 1a).1e Furthermore, the traditional synthesis approach for the [6–8–6] benzo-fused scaffold typically involves a reductive coupling catalyzed by samarium(II) iodide, which requires harsh reaction conditions. Consequently, there is an urgent need for the development of a novel and facile catalytic system to realize the efficient synthesis of [6–8–6] benzo-fused scaffolds from simple and readily available starting materials.

Total Synthesis and Transition-Metal-Catalyzed Construction of Polycyclic Ring Systems

Transition-metal-catalyzed C–H bond functionalization involving carbenes has proven to be an effective tool for the synthesis and late modification of complex natural products and bioactive compounds, as well as the construction of novel ring systems.9 Nonetheless, achieving site-selective and sequential C–H bond functionalization remains an enormous challenge.10 The carbene/alkyne metathesis (CAM) has emerged as a highly effective method for the in situ generation of vinyl carbene intermediates, which further participate in diverse transformations, including cyclopropanation,11 C–H bond insertion,12 and cyclization.13 Xu and co-workers reported a Rh(II)-catalyzed site-selective and enantioselective cascade reaction of propargyl diazoacetates yielding chiral dihydroindole derivatives. This method achieved highly selective primary (1°), secondary (2°), and tertiary (3°) C(sp3)–H bond insertion by controlling chiral dirhodium catalysts (Scheme 1b, above).14 Pla-Quintana et al. demonstrated a silver-based catalytic system for the synthesis of benzoazepines from propargyl diazoacetates via CAM and an unprecedented vinylogous C(sp3)–H bond insertion in a selective and stepwise manner (Scheme 1b, below).15 These elegant studies demonstrated that the strategic substrate design and catalytic system selection in the CAM/C–H bond insertion may lead to diverse heterocyclic scaffolds accompanied by novel reaction mechanisms.

Based on our previous exploration of metal carbene,16 we innovatively introduced an additional methylene group and tetrahydroisoquinoline ring into the propargyl diazoacetate. A novel substrate was designed with three C(sp3)–H bonds connected to the same nitrogen atom and another C(sp3)–H bond connected to the oxygen atom, respectively, to envision the generation of novel heterocyclic scaffolds, even eight-membered carbocycles, through site-selective and sequential C(sp3)–H/C(sp2)–H bond insertions. Unexpectedly, a [6–8–6] benzo-fused product featuring a fused eight-membered carbocycle was achieved with the insertion occurring at the C(sp3)–H bond connected to the oxygen atom rather than the nitrogen atom (Scheme 1c). Here, we report a Rh(II)-catalyzed CAM process for the construction of a fused eight-membered carbocycle followed by a 1,3-H shift, intramolecular nucleophilic attack, and selective C(sp3)–H/C(sp2)–H bond cascade insertion. Moreover, the reaction mechanism involves the formation of a spirocyclic zwitterionic intermediate distinct from any previously reported reaction.

Initially, we selected diazo compound 1a as the template substrate, Rh2(OAc)4 (2 mol %) as the catalyst, dichloromethane (DCM) as the solvent at 0 °C, and an Ar atmosphere for 12 h, which resulted in the isolation of 2a in 23% yield (Table 1, entry 1). The accurate structure of 2a was unambiguously confirmed by X-ray diffraction. As depicted in Figure S1, the single-crystal structure demonstrates that 2a exists as a racemate (see the Supporting Information for details). Subsequently, optimization efforts were undertaken using various Rh(II) catalysts, such as Rh2(Oct)4, Rh2(OPiv)4, Rh2(esp)2, Rh2(S-DOSP)4, Rh2(S-PTPA)4, and Rh2(S-PTTL)4. However, only Rh2(S-PTTL)4 exhibited a slight improvement in the yield (Table 1, entries 2–7). Further optimization revealed that room temperature was optimal for the reaction, which resulted in a 36% yield (Table 1, entries 8–10). Significantly, the yield of 2a was increased to 62% when tetrahydrofuran (THF) was used as the solvent (Table 1, entries 11–16). Additional optimization showed that catalyst chirality has no significant effect on the yield (Table 1, entry 17). The screening of other solvents and additives was illustrated in Table S2 (see the Supporting Information for details). Moreover, removal of Rh2(S-PTTL)4 from the reaction system led to the absence of target product 2a (Table 1, entry 18). Overall, the novel [6–8–6] benzo-fused product 2a could be obtained with a 62% yield when 1a was treated with Rh2(S-PTTL)4 (2 mol %) and 4 Å MS as the catalyst system in THF at room temperature for 12 h under an Ar atmosphere.

With the optimal reaction conditions established, we initiated an exploration of substituent diversity at the R1 position in the intramolecular eight-membered cyclization of α-aryl-α-diazoester substrates (Scheme 2). Encouraged by the wide application of fluorine atoms in drug molecules,18 a gem-difluoride-substituted methylenedioxy was first introduced into substrate 1b, which resulted in the corresponding [6–8–6] benzo-fused scaffold product 2b in a 45% yield. A further exploration indicated that fusing a five-, six-, and seven-heterocycle containing one or two oxygens at the R1 position resulted in moderate yields (2c–2e). Moreover, 3,4-dimethoxy or 2,3,4- trimethoxy-substituted substrates were evaluated and provided the corresponding products 2f and 2g in 72% and 58% yields, respectively. However, product 2h without a substituent was obtained in a lower yield. When electron-donating alkoxy (methoxy, ethoxy, and isopropoxy) or alkyl (methyl, isopropyl, and tert-butyl) was introduced into the 4-position of the phenyl ring, satisfactory results were also achieved (2i-2n, 30%–55%). Additionally, the morpholine substituent (2o) was tolerated under the optimal reaction conditions. It is well known that trifluoromethyl and difluoromethyloxy are typical bioisosterisms in medicinal chemistry.19 We further installed difluoromethoxy and 1-(trifluoromethyl)-cyclopropyl at the 4-position of the phenyl ring to effectively obtain the target products 2p and 2q (66% and 54%), respectively. Furthermore, we explored −OMe (2r), −CH2NHBoc (2s), and −CF3 (2t) at the 3-position of the phenyl ring to examine the influence of electronic effects, which revealed higher efficiencies of the electron-donating group (48% and 36%, respectively) compared with the electron-withdrawing group (28%).

Substrate Scope for the Synthesis of [6–8–6] Benzo-Fused Derivatives 2

Subsequently, different substituted N-heterocycles were examined (Scheme 3). First, installing a Br substituent on the 6-, 7-, or 8-position of the tetrahydroisoquinoline ring successfully provided the target products 4a–4c in 39%–87% yields, respectively, with the 7-Br tetrahydroisoquinoline ring providing the highest yield (4b, 87%). A further exploration indicated that 7-CF3 tetrahydroisoquinoline substrate 3d exhibited a higher reaction efficiency than 3e (a 7-Me tetrahydroisoquinoline substrate) under optimal reaction conditions. To our delight, other diverse N-heterocycles, such as indoline ring (3f), and 4-phenylpiperidine ring (3g), were also tolerated, to produce the corresponding [6–8–6] benzo-fused scaffold products 4f and 4g in moderate yields (29% and 52%), respectively. This expansion significantly broadened the application scope of the reaction. When N-heterocyclic substituents are replaced with noncyclic amino substituents, the isoindoline scaffold compounds 6a and 6b are formed instead of the [6–8–6] benzo-fused product (see the Supporting Information for details).

To further demonstrate the application potential of this reaction, a gram-scale reaction of 1f was first conducted, and the corresponding [6–8–6] benzo-fused product 2f was obtained in an isolated yield of 52% (Scheme 4a). Subsequently, treatment of 2f with sodium borohydride (NaBH4) in CH3OH for 6 h resulted in the reduction of its five-membered lactones, and the product 7 was obtained in a high yield of 90% with the carboxyl group providing a promising site for further modification (Scheme 4b). Furthermore, dihydroxyl compound 8 could be smoothly synthesized in a yield of 74% by using boron tribromide (BBr3) to remove two methyl groups from 2f (Scheme 4b). These results serve as compelling evidence of the significant potential of this protocol in various synthetic applications.

Scale-up Synthesis of 2f and Its Transformations

To gain further insight into the reaction mechanism, we conducted a deuterium labeling experiment to aid in understanding the entire reaction process by monitoring the positional changes of the deuterium atom (Scheme 5). A deuterated substrate 1f-d1 was prepared and subsequently transformed into 2f-d1 under the standard conditions in a yield of 70%. The precise location of the deuterium atom was determined through 1H NMR analysis (Figure S2). Remarkably, the deuterium atom from the ortho-methylene of the tetrahydroisoquinoline ring in substrate 1f-d1 entirely migrated to the 8-position of product 2f-d1, indicating a migration of this methylene group rather than just the deuterium atom. Notably, these results also suggested an unreported intramolecular migration of the tetrahydroisoquinoline ring. As a result, the methylene positioned between the tetrahydroisoquinoline ring and the benzene ring is crucial for the formation of the [6–8–6] benzo-fused scaffold.12e,14,15,20

Preliminary Mechanistic Studies

Based on the preliminary mechanistic experiments and literature,13e,14,21 a plausible catalytic cycle was proposed (Scheme 6). The process is initiated by the coordination of Rh2(S-PTTL)4 to the carbenic carbon of the diazo species in 1a, resulting in the formation of Rh-carbene intermediate I with the release of N2. Subsequently, a CAM process generates the key Rh carbene species II. Then, an intramolecular 1,3-H shift occurs, leading to the formation of zwitterionic intermediate III, which further converts into cyclopropane intermediate IV following the elimination of the catalyst and intramolecular cyclization. The N atom of IV further conducts an intramolecular nucleophilic attack on the ortho-position of the O atom to yield the enol intermediate V followed by the electron transfer and the reparticipation of Rh(II) catalyst in the cycle to then form a spirocyclic zwitterionic intermediate VI. This intermediate VI undergoes an intramolecular rearrangement, probably due to ring tension and electronic factors, to yield the more stable Rh complex VII. Finally, selective C(sp2)–H bond insertion produces intermediate VIII, which further converts into target product 2a. Of note, the Rh (II) catalyst may be removed during the process from intermediate III to intermediate IV where it does not participate in the chiral generation process of product 2a.

Proposed Reaction Mechanism

In summary, we have developed a Rh(II)-based catalytic system for the synthesis of [6–8–6] benzo-fused derivatives featuring a fused eight-membered carbocyclic scaffold through a cascade of CAM, 1,3-H shift, intramolecular nucleophilic attack, and selective C(sp3)–H/C(sp2)–H bonds insertion. The unique spirocyclic zwitterionic intermediate appears to facilitate N-heterocycle migration, which contributes to the formation of a fused eight-membered carbocycle. We accomplished one-step construction of the [6–8–6] benzo-fused scaffold, which offers the advantage of enhanced convenience and high atom efficiency compared with traditional synthesis methods. Taken together, our findings not only expand the application scope of CAM transformation but also open up a new perspective for the construction of fused eight-membered carbocyclic derivatives.

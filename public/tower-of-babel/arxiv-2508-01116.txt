TensoMeta-VQC: A Tensor-Train-Guided Meta-Learning Framework for Robust and Scalable Variational Quantum Computing
Jun Qi, Chao-Han Yang, Pin-Yu Chen, Min-Hsiu Hsieh

Abstract
Variational Quantum Computing (VQC) faces fundamental barriers in scalability, primarily due to barren plateaus and quantum noise sensitivity. To address these challenges, we introduce TensoMeta-VQC, a novel tensor-train (TT)-guided meta-learning framework designed to improve the robustness and scalability of VQC significantly. Our framework fully delegates the generation of quantum circuit parameters to a classical TT network, effectively decoupling optimization from quantum hardware. This innovative parameterization mitigates gradient vanishing, enhances noise resilience through structured low-rank representations, and facilitates efficient gradient propagation. Based on Neural Tangent Kernel and statistical learning theory, our rigorous theoretical analyses establish strong guarantees on approximation capability, optimization stability, and generalization performance. Extensive empirical results across quantum dot classification, Max-Cut optimization, and molecular quantum simulation tasks demonstrate that TensoMeta-VQC consistently achieves superior performance and robust noise tolerance, establishing it as a principled pathway toward practical and scalable VQC on near-term quantum devices.

Results

2.1
The TensoMeta-VQC Framework

Our proposed framework, TensoMeta-VQC, fundamentally re-engineers the variational quantum computing training paradigm to address significant core scalability and robustness issues. As illustrated in Figure
1
, the core innovation is delegating parameter generation to a classical TT network. In this meta-learning approach, the classical TT network, composed of interconnected low-rank tensor cores, is the sole trainable component. It learns to generate the high-dimensional parameter vectors
\hat{\textbf{w}}=[\alpha_{1:U},\beta_{1:U},\gamma_{1:U}]^{\top}
required to define the unitary rotation gates of the VQC. The entire operational flow is as follows:

1.

Parameter Generation
: The classical TT network generates all parameters required for quantum circuits in VQC, effectively shifting parameter optimization entirely to the classical domain. Specifically, given a high-dimensional tensor
\mathcal{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times\cdot\cdot\cdot\times d_{K}}
representing quantum circuit parameters, we factorize it into interconnected, low-rank TT-cores as:

\mathcal{W}(i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{K})=\mathcal{G}_{1}(i_{1})\mathcal{G}_{2}(i_{2})\cdot\cdot\cdot\mathcal{G}_{K}(i_{K}),

(1)

with each core
\mathcal{G}_{k}(i_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k-1}\times r_{k}}
characterized by low TT-ranks. Utilizing Gaussian noise input
\textbf{z}\sim N(0,I)
, the TT network deterministically generates quantum gate parameters
\hat{\textbf{w}}
as:

\hat{\textbf{w}}=\text{TT}(\textbf{z};\{\mathcal{G}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}).

(2)

2.

Quantum Forward Pass
: The generated parameters are injected into a fixed quantum circuit, which functions exclusively in inference mode to evaluate the loss. The quantum circuits act as a fixed forward pass evaluator, where no parameters within the VQC are trained. In contrast, gradient computations and parameter updates occur solely in the classical TT-cores via standard backpropagation.

This architectural design transforms the optimization landscape, insulating gradient computations from quantum hardware imperfections. It effectively mitigates the barren plateaus barrier and quantum noise-induced instability.

2.2
Theoretical Guarantees

We rigorously provide a theoretical foundation for the advantages of the TensoMeta-VQC framework from four perspectives: representation capability, trainability, generalization performance, and robustness against quantum noise.

•

Approximation Capability
. The approximation error
\epsilon_{\rm app}
, which measures the discrepancy between the expected risk
\mathcal{R}(\cdot)
of a target function
h^{*}
and that of the best possible TensoMeta-VQC operator
f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}}
, is bounded as:

\begin{split}\epsilon_{\rm app}&\leq\mathcal{R}(h^{*})-\mathcal{R}(f_{\boldsymbol{\theta^{*}}})\\
&\leq K_{\ell}\lVert h^{*}(\textbf{x})-f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}}(\textbf{x})\rVert_{1}\\
&\leq K_{\ell}\lVert h^{*}(\textbf{x})-f_{\textbf{w}^{*}}(\textbf{x})\rVert_{1}+K_{\ell}\lVert f_{\textbf{w}^{*}}(\textbf{x})-f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}}(\textbf{x})\rVert_{1}\\
&\leq\mathcal{O}\left(K_{\ell}e^{-\alpha L}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{K_{\ell}}{2^{\beta U}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(K_{\ell}\epsilon_{\rm tt}),\end{split}

(3)

where we define the parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}=\{\mathcal{G}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
and the best
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}
corresponds to the optimal TT-cores; The loss function
\mathcal{R}(\cdot)
is assumed as
K_{\ell}
-Lipschitz continuous and
f_{\textbf{w}^{*}}(\textbf{x})
refers to an optimal VQC operator with the best parameters
\textbf{w}^{*}
. Given the VQC depth
L
and qubit count
U
, the term
\lVert h^{*}(\textbf{x})-f_{\textbf{w}^{*}}(\textbf{x})\rVert_{1}
is bounded by the VQC expressivity limit
\mathcal{O}\left(K_{\ell}e^{-\alpha L}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{K_{\ell}}{2^{\beta U}}\right)
, and another term
\lVert f_{\textbf{w}^{*}}(\textbf{x})-f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}(\textbf{x})\rVert_{1}
scales with the TT parameterization approximation error
\mathcal{O}(K_{\ell}\epsilon_{\rm tt})
, where
\epsilon_{\rm tt}
is a constant constraint as:

\lVert\textbf{w}^{*}-\hat{\textbf{w}}\rVert_{1}\leq\epsilon_{\rm tt}.

(4)

This analysis confirms that TensoMeta-VQC retains the expressivity of VQCs while enabling compact and efficient parameterization.

•

Optimization Dynamics via Neural Tangent Kernel
. Since
\hat{\textbf{w}}
is not directly parameterized but generated by TT and the parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}=\{\mathcal{G}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
lie in the TT, we have a hierarchical parameterization:

\boldsymbol{\theta}\rightarrow\hat{\textbf{w}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rightarrow f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\textbf{x};\hat{\textbf{w}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})).

(5)

Then, we leverage Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory to analyze the trainability of TensoMeta-VQC. The NTK matrix for our framework,
\mathcal{T}_{\rm mv}
, is determined by the VQC and the TT network.

\mathcal{T}_{\rm mv}=\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\textbf{x})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\textbf{x})^{\top}=\left(\frac{\partial f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\partial\hat{\textbf{w}}}\cdot\frac{\partial\hat{\textbf{w}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\partial\hat{\textbf{w}}}\cdot\frac{\partial\hat{\textbf{w}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)^{\top}.

(6)

Accordingly, at epoch
t
, the optimization error
\epsilon_{\rm opt}
is bounded by:

\epsilon_{\rm opt}(t)\leq C_{0}e^{-\lambda_{\rm min}(\mathcal{T}_{\rm mv})t},

(7)

where
C_{0}
is related to the initialized parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}
and
\lambda_{\rm min}(\mathcal{T}_{\rm mv})
is the smallest eigenvalue of the NTK. Our theoretical analysis reveals that
\lambda_{\rm min}(\mathcal{T}_{\rm mv})
is strictly larger than the corresponding eigenvalue for a conventional VQC. This indicates a better-conditioned optimization landscape, leading to faster and more stable convergence by mitigating the vanishing gradient problem.

•

Generalization Performance
. Around the initialization of parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}
, we linearize the parametric operator of TensoMeta-VQC
f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
as:

f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\textbf{x})\approx f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}}(\textbf{x})+\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}}(\textbf{x})^{\top}(\boldsymbol{\theta}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}).

(8)

Suppose the quantum measurement noise satisfies i.i.d. Gaussian with a variance
\sigma_{q}^{2}
, and the loss function is
K_{\ell}
-Lipschitz. Given a training set
\{(\textbf{x}_{n},\textbf{y}_{n})\}_{n=1}^{N}
, using the linearized NTK approximation, we bound the generalization gap as:

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\left|\mathcal{R}(f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}})-\hat{\mathcal{R}}(f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right|\right]\leq\tilde{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\text{Tr}(\mathcal{T}_{\rm mv})}{N^{2}}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}K_{\ell}^{2}}{N}}\right).

(9)

Furthermore, with TT-ranks
r_{0},r_{1},...,r_{K}
, we have:

\text{Tr}(\mathcal{T}_{\rm mv})\leq NC_{r}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{K}r_{i},

(10)

for some constant
C_{r}
(depending on input scaling, circuit, and TT initialization). The result demonstrates that the TT network’s structured, low-rank nature helps to control the trace of
\mathcal{T}_{\rm mv}
, potentially promoting better generalization performance of TensoMeta-VQC.

•

Robustness to Quantum Noise
. We further verify that TensoMeta-VQC offers significant robustness against quantum hardware noise. In the TensoMeta-VQC framework, since we optimize over classical TT-cores
\{\mathcal{G}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
, the quantum circuit no longer introduces vanishing gradients. Gradients are computed via:

\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{R}}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}=\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{R}}}{\partial\hat{\textbf{w}}}\cdot\frac{\partial\hat{\textbf{w}}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}=\sum\limits_{u=1}^{3U}\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{R}}}{\partial\hat{w}_{u}}\cdot\frac{\partial\hat{w}_{u}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}.

(11)

Since TensoMeta-VQC admits a fully classical training paradigm, the trainable TT-cores are entirely classical while quantum circuits are used only for forward inference. Hence, the partial derivative
\frac{\partial\hat{\textbf{w}}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}
is deterministic and computed classically, but another term
\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{R}}}{\partial\hat{\textbf{w}}}
is only affected by the quantum measurement noise
\boldsymbol{\tau}=[\tau_{1},\tau_{2},...,\tau_{3U}]^{\top}
, which leads to:

\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{R}}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}=\left(\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{R}}}{\partial\hat{\textbf{w}}}+\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)\cdot\frac{\partial\hat{\textbf{w}}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}=\sum\limits_{u=1}^{3U}\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{R}}}{\partial\hat{w}_{u}}\cdot\frac{\partial\hat{w}_{u}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}+\sum\limits_{u=1}^{3U}\tau_{u}\frac{\partial\hat{w}_{u}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}.

(12)

Taking the variance of the noisy gradient term
\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{R}}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}
for the TT-cores, we have:

\text{Var}\left[\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{R}}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}\right]=\text{Var}\left[\sum\limits_{u=1}^{3U}\tau_{u}\frac{\partial\hat{w}_{u}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}\right]=\sum\limits_{u=1}^{3U}\sigma_{u}^{2}\left(\frac{\partial\hat{w}_{u}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}\right)^{2},

(13)

where
\sigma_{u}^{2}
is related to the variance of
\tau_{u}
. If we assume homogeneous noise variance
\sigma_{u}^{2}=\sigma^{2}
, this simplifies to:

\text{Var}\left[\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{R}}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}\right]=\sigma^{2}\sum\limits_{u=1}^{3U}\left(\frac{\partial\hat{w}_{u}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}\right)^{2}=\sigma^{2}\lVert J_{k}\rVert^{2},

(14)

with
J_{k}
being the Jacobian of the TT-core
\mathcal{G}_{k}
. Furthermore, since the TT network is inherently low-rank and structured, each TT-core parameter has a uniformly distributed and small influence on the individual TT-core, leading to a small Jacobian norm per TT-core parameter. Specifically,

\lVert J_{k}\rVert^{2}\approx\frac{c}{3U},\hskip 11.38109ptc\ll 3U,

(15)

for some constant
c
because of the uniform spread across the high-dimensional parameter space, which results in

\text{Var}\left[\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{R}}}{\partial\mathcal{G}_{k}}\right]\approx\frac{c\sigma^{2}}{3U}.

(16)

The above theoretical analysis demonstrates that increasing the qubit count
U
reduces the noise variance on the TT-core gradients. This mechanism can be viewed as the classical low-rank averaging effect for the quantum measurement noise, providing an intrinsic noise robustness architecture. In comparison, the conventional VQC admits the variance as:

\text{Var}\left[\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{R}}}{\partial\textbf{w}}\right]=\sigma^{2},

(17)

which cannot be reduced with the increase of qubit count
U
.

•

Given a graph

V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}

•

With Pauli-Z operators

\mathcal{H}=\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in E}\frac{1-Z_{i}Z_{j}}{2}.

(18)

Application to Quantum Simulation: Molecular Hamiltonians

4
-qubit reduced Hamiltonian obtained by freezing core orbitals and eliminating high-lying virtual orbitals. This setting enables efficient simulation while retaining the system’s essential quantum correlations.

In the LiH molecule quantum simulation, we configure TensoMeta-VQC by employing a classical TT network to parameterize the variational quantum ansatz. The molecular Hamiltonian is reduced via active space selection and orbital freezing, resulting in a
4
-qubit system. The ansatz circuit consists of

R_{X},R_{Y},R_{Z}
) per qubit, followed by a ring of CNOT entanglers, yielding a total of

[4,6]
and TT-ranks
[1,2,1]

24
parameters generated by a compact TensoMeta-VQC with only
9

[

to minimize the quantum expectation value of the LiH Hamiltonian.

In addition to evaluating performance in a noise-free simulation, we further tested the robustness of both methods under realistic quantum noise, introducing a depolarizing noise model with a

The quantum simulation of LiH molecular Hamiltonians confirms that TensoMeta-VQC constitutes a practical and powerful meta-learning framework for scalable and noise-resilient quantum simulation, with strong potential for application to larger molecules and quantum chemistry tasks on near-term quantum hardware.

3
Discussion

In this study, we introduced TensoMeta-VQC, a novel TT meta-learning framework explicitly designed to address critical limitations of VQC, namely barren plateaus and quantum noise sensitivity. By transferring parameter optimization from quantum hardware to a classical TT network, TensoMeta-VQC significantly advances VQC scalability and robustness. This strategic decoupling fundamentally alters the optimization landscape, enhancing gradient flow and minimizing quantum noise impact on gradient computation.

The VQC has frequently encountered significant limitations, primarily barren plateaus and sensitivity to quantum noise, hampering its scalability and practicality. Recent studies have sought to alleviate these challenges through improved parameterization strategies
[
45
]
and structural optimization
[
10
]
. Low-rank tensor decomposition techniques have emerged as effective strategies to reduce parameter dimensionality, thereby enhancing the trainability and generalization of machine learning models. Inspired by classical low-rank adaptations
[
36
,
37
]
, quantum architectures have also integrated tensor network structures like TTN to form a hybrid architecture, TTN+VQC, demonstrating improved parameter efficiency and performance stability. Integrating meta-learning with quantum computing represents another pivotal advancement, enabling models to learn efficient initialization and rapid adaptation to new tasks. Previous research has explored meta-learning frameworks, including gradient-based methods like MAML
[
46
]
, in quantum contexts to enhance the robustness and adaptability of VQCs.

In contrast, the proposed TensoMeta-VQC framework uniquely leverages an entirely classical Tensor-Train meta-learner to generate variational quantum parameters. By delegating optimization entirely to the classical domain, this architecture effectively mitigates issues of gradient vanishing and quantum measurement-induced instability, ensuring improved robustness and scalability. The theoretical underpinning of TensoMeta-VQC confirms its robustness and scalability through rigorous analyses based on the NTK theory and variance reduction properties intrinsic to TT parameterizations. This synergistic integration of tensor network principles, meta-learning concepts, and its unique classical training paradigm sets TensoMeta-VQC apart from existing hybrid quantum-classical algorithms.

A key advantage of TensoMeta-VQC lies in its practicality and robustness when deployed on realistic, noisy quantum hardware. Throughout our theoretical analyses and empirical evaluations, we observe that TensoMeta-VQC consistently mitigates the adverse effects of quantum noise, which typically degrade the performance of conventional VQC approaches. Several architectural choices contribute to this robustness. First, by fully offloading parameter optimization to a classical TT network, TensoMeta-VQC decouples gradient computations from the stochasticity of quantum hardware. As a result, the optimization process remains stable even in the presence of substantial quantum noise. Second, the low-rank structure of the TT network intrinsically promotes noise averaging and variance reduction across TT-core gradients.

Our theoretical results demonstrate that the variance of quantum noise on TT-core gradients diminishes with increasing qubit count, leveraging classical averaging effects that conventional VQCs cannot exploit. These theoretical guarantees are empirically validated across multiple benchmarks: quantum dot classification, Max-Cut optimization, and molecular ground state simulations of the LiH molecule.

Importantly, the framework is hardware-agnostic and requires no circuit-level modifications or additional error mitigation techniques, making it readily deployable on existing NISQ devices. This practicality and demonstrated robustness position TensoMeta-VQC as a promising foundation for advancing scalable and reliable quantum learning on NISQ devices. Besides, TensoMeta-VQC also has significant potential for broader impacts across scientific and technological domains. Enabling more efficient and scalable quantum computing solutions can accelerate breakthroughs in pharmaceuticals, materials science, and complex optimization problems, significantly impacting drug discovery, energy efficiency, and logistics. Furthermore, integrating classical tensor network techniques with quantum computing paradigms fosters interdisciplinary research, potentially leading to innovative hybrid computational models that leverage the strengths of both quantum and classical computation frameworks.

Methods

This section provides methodological support for the theoretical analysis presented in the main text, including the NTK analysis and the concepts of Meta-Learning, TTN, QAOA, and VQE. The detailed proofs of our theoretical results and computational complexity analysis are in the Appendix.

4.1
Meta-Learning via Parameter Generation

Meta-learning
[
17
]
, broadly defined as ”learning to learn,” refers to methods where models improve their learning capability based on experiences with multiple tasks. In TensoMeta-VQC, meta-learning is realized through a classical TT network serving as a hypernetwork that outputs parameters for another model. Specifically, the TT network meta-learns a direct mapping from task-level features, such as the degree histogram of graphs in combinatorial optimization, to the optimal variational quantum circuit parameters.

Unlike gradient-based meta-learning frameworks such as MAML
[
46
]
, this hypernetwork-based meta-learning approach explicitly parameterizes the space of solutions, enabling rapid, scalable adaptation to new tasks by directly generating high-quality initial parameters. This structured parameter-generation paradigm enhances computational efficiency and inherently promotes robustness against quantum noise and optimization instabilities, positioning TensoMeta-VQC as a powerful and distinctive form of meta-learning tailored for VQC.

4.2
An Introduction to Neural Tangent Kernel Theory

The Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK)
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
is a potent theoretical framework that illustrates the efficient trainability of deep neural networks despite their inherent complexity. Fundamentally, the NTK theory investigates how marginal alterations in a network’s parameters influence its predictions. A network is deemed ”well-conditioned,” leading to smoother training and faster convergence, when slight parameter adjustments reliably yield stable and predictable shifts in output.

The NTK quantifies this stability by constructing a kernel, which acts as a similarity measure and characterizes the network’s behavior throughout the training process. A network endowed with a ”well-conditioned” NTK, whose smallest eigenvalue is sufficiently large, will exhibit stable gradients during training, thereby enabling efficient optimization through gradient-based algorithms. Conversely, networks plagued by poorly conditioned NTKs can demonstrate unstable convergence, phenomena often termed optimization difficulties.

Our theoretical framework for TensoMeta-VQC directly leverages NTK theory to analyze its trainability. The VQC and the TT network determine the NTK matrix for TensoMeta-VQC. A key theoretical finding is that the smallest eigenvalue of TensoMeta-VQC’s NTK is strictly larger than that of a conventional VQC. This indicates a better-conditioned optimization landscape for TensoMeta-VQC, leading to faster and more stable convergence by mitigating the vanishing gradient problem. Thus, the structured parameterization afforded by the TT network directly contributes to improved trainability as supported by our NTK analysis.

4.3
Tensor-Train Network

The Tensor-Train (TT) network
[
18
]
, also known as the matrix product state, is a widely adopted tensor network architecture in both machine learning and quantum physics. It efficiently represents high-dimensional tensors by factorizing them into a sequence of lower-dimensional tensors, typically matrices or three-index tensors, arranged in a linear, chain-like structure.

When integrated with a VQC, the TTN forms a hybrid quantum-classical architecture called TTN+VQC. The TTN effectively reduces input dimensionality in this hybrid configuration, while the VQC enhances expressivity by introducing quantum-induced nonlinearity. This combination positions TTN+VQC as a robust framework for handling complex, high-dimensional data in quantum machine learning tasks. However, TTN+VQC still grapples with challenges and limitations inherent to hybrid quantum-classical designs, including substantial quantum resource requirements, representational constraints stemming from the linearity of quantum circuits, and optimization difficulties arising from complex parameter landscapes.

4.4
Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm

The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA)
[
25
,
26
]
is a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm designed to find approximate solutions to combinatorial optimization problems. It operates by iteratively optimizing parameters of a quantum circuit to maximize an objective function defined by the problem. One common application of QAOA is solving the Max-Cut problem. The Max-Cut problem aims to divide the vertices of a graph into two sets so that the number of edges connecting vertices in different sets is maximized.

In the QAOA framework, this problem is encoded into an issue of the quantum Hamiltonian. The algorithm then variationally optimizes parameters of a quantum circuit to find a quantum state that, upon measurement, yields a classical bit string representing a good approximation to the optimal cut. For instance, our work demonstrates QAOA’s application to 20-qubit Erdos-Renyi graphs for the Max-Cut problem.

4.5
Variational Quantum Eigensolver

The Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE)
[
47
,
48
]
is a prominent hybrid quantum-classical algorithm designed to find the ground state energy of a given Hamiltonian. It works by variably optimizing the parameters of a quantum circuit to prepare a quantum state. The expectation value of the Hamiltonian is then measured for this state on a quantum computer, and this value is fed back to a classical optimizer. The classical optimizer then updates the parameters of the quantum circuit to minimize the energy, iteratively searching for the quantum state that corresponds to the lowest possible energy, a.k.a. the ground state. VQE is a promising algorithm for NISQ devices due to its relatively shallow circuit depth requirements.

4.6
TT Network Input and Feature Processing

In the TensoMeta-VQC framework, the classical TT network is a hypernetwork that maps problem-specific classical features to the full quantum circuit parameters required for the target VQC task. The TT network receives a vectorized representation of the charge stability diagram as input for quantum dot classification. Specifically, each 50 × 50 image is flattened into a 2,500-dimensional real-valued vector, which is then projected or reshaped as needed to match the input dimension structure of the TT-cores. For combinatorial optimization tasks such as Max-Cut, the TT network input consists of concise task-level descriptors, such as the degree histogram or other graph statistics.

These classical features are likewise arranged to match the input dimension requirements of each TT-core. The TT network processes this input through a sequence of low-rank tensor contractions, effectively learning a nonlinear and structured mapping from the classical feature space to the quantum circuit parameter space. The resulting output vector defines all variational parameters (e.g., rotation angles) for the quantum gates in the VQC, thereby conditioning the quantum circuit on the classical data instance or problem structure. This mechanism enables TensoMeta-VQC to adapt the quantum ansatz parameters to each input dynamically, enhancing task specificity and generalization.

4.7
Ansatz Selection of Quantum Circuits

A defining aspect of the TensoMeta-VQC architecture is that, during training, the quantum circuit is held fixed and operates solely as a forward-pass evaluator, while the classical TT network generates all trainable parameters. While using a fixed circuit greatly simplifies the optimization landscape and enables robust, hardware-agnostic training, it may also limit the model’s adaptability to extremely diverse tasks or rapidly varying problem distributions.

If a new task fundamentally requires a different quantum circuit structure (e.g., a distinct pattern of entanglement or gate types), the fixed ansatz may be suboptimal, and a new TensoMeta-VQC instance with a redesigned circuit would be needed. Thus, while the TensoMeta-VQC framework allows flexible adaptation of parameters within a fixed circuit structure, its ability to generalize across highly heterogeneous tasks is bounded by the expressivity of the pre-selected ansatz. Future research could explore automated or adaptive ansatz selection as a complementary strategy for even greater task adaptability.

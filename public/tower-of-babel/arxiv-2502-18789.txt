Bound systems of interacting electrons. A step beyond density functional theory.
Miguel Lagos

Abstract
The non–relativistic interacting electron gas in an external field of positively charged massive cores is dealt with in the scheme of second quantization. Ladder operators that change between stationary states of contiguous energy eigenvalues are derived. The method is particularized to the two–electron Helium atom in order to explain it avoiding too much notation. Applying the lowering operator on the ground state must give zero because no state with lower energy does exist. Equations for the ground state and ground state energy are obtained this way and solved, giving closed–form expressions for the ground state, its energy and electronic density of Helium. The theory in its lowest order gives 0.63% error. The application to more complex systems and higher degrees of approximation seems straightforward. The foundations of the density functional theory and how to go beyond it are seen quite clearly.



The study of the electronic structure of molecules and solids has been
greatly facilitated by the breakthrough of the density functional theory
(DFT). By demonstrating that the ground state energy of the
N
electrons
system in a external field can be regarded as a unique functional of the
electron density, Hohenberg and Kohn
[
1
]
found a bypass
to the problem of solving the many–body Schrödinger equation that
reduces it, in principle, to the search for the density functional that
minimizes the energy. Instead of seeking for a wave function of
3N
real
variables, the problem is substituted by determining a function of just 3
variables. Afterwards, Kohn and Sham
[
2
]
applied these ideas
to a conveniently defined non–interacting system yielding the same
density as the original problem of interacting electrons

[
3
]
.

The procedure has proven to give valuable information on the stationary
structure of atoms, molecules and solids in ground state, but a complete
knowledge of the dynamical state of the system demands knowing the state
vector. This communication introduces a method that may compete with the
DFT in simplicity and accuracy, but gives also the state vector in the
second quantized formalism. The derivations are restricted here to
systems of two energy levels or bands, as are the
n=0
and
n=1

states of He, bonding and anti–bonding states of H
2
, or the
\sigma

and
\pi
bonding orbitals of conjugate polymers. The application to
polymers and more complex systems is left for a forthcoming communication
because the study of the two–electron systems is enough to explain the
method. Just to show the power of the method the ground state vector is
used to calculate the density and excitation energy. As an interesting
byproduct of the present approach, it gives an alternate demonstration of
Hohenberg–Kohn theorem and Kohn–Sham procedure of regarding the system
as an ensemble of free fermions in effective potentials.

In general, the
N
–electron system whose Hamiltonian operator in
coordinate space is

H=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla_{i}^{2}+V(\vec{r_{i}})\right)+\sum_{i<j}U(\vec{r_{i}},\vec{r_{j}})

has a representation

H=\sum_{k}\epsilon_{k}c_{k}^{\dagger}c_{k}+\sum_{klmn}U_{klmn}c_{k}^{\dagger}c_{l}^{\dagger}c_{m}c_{n},

(2)

in the Fock space of states
[
4

are
the coefficients of the field operator

(3)

expanded in the complete set of orthonormal functions
{u_{k}(\vec{r})}
,
defined by the solutions of the eigenvalue equation

\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}+V(\vec{r})\right)\,u_{k}(\vec{r})=\epsilon_{k}u_{k}(\vec{r}).

(4)

The off–diagonal coefficients are

U_{klmn}=\int\,d^{3}\vec{r}\,u_{k}^{*}(\vec{r})u_{l}^{*}(\vec{r^{\prime}})U(\vec{r},\vec{r^{\prime}})u_{m}(\vec{r^{\prime}})u_{n}(\vec{r})

(5)

and the indices

\uparrow

, which has the only eigenvalues 0 and 1 as the

\{c_{k},c_{k^{\prime}}\}=0

\{c_{k},c_{k^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{k{k^{\prime}}}

Notice that the restriction

in Eq. (
1

Fock space formulation. The condition

the demonstration of the equivalence of the two representations. All
interaction terms are allowed in the second quantized formalism, and the
commutation properties of the creation and annihilation operators will
determine what of them are relevant in each situation. This is important
for the correct counting of the interaction terms.

\displaystyle+V_{1}n_{1\uparrow}n_{1\downarrow}+V_{2}n_{2\uparrow}n_{2\downarrow}+U(n_{1\uparrow}n_{2\downarrow}+n_{1\downarrow}n_{2\uparrow})

\displaystyle+\overline{U}(c_{1\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{1\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{2\downarrow}c_{2\uparrow}+c_{2\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{2\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{1\downarrow}c_{1\uparrow}+c_{1\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{2\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{1\downarrow}c_{2\uparrow}

\displaystyle+c_{2\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{1\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{2\downarrow}c_{1\uparrow}),

(7)

(9)

\displaystyle+V_{2}n_{2\uparrow}n_{2\downarrow}+U(n_{1\uparrow}n_{2\downarrow}+n_{1\downarrow}n_{2\uparrow})

Making repeated use of the elementary identities

\displaystyle[AB,C]=A[B.C]+[A,C]B,

(16)

\displaystyle[A,BC]=[A,B]C+B[A,C],

\displaystyle[AB,C]=A\{B.C\}-\{A,C\}B,

\displaystyle[A,BC]=\{A,B\}C-B\{A,C\},

\displaystyle[\tilde{\phi},\tilde{\phi}^{\dagger}]=-n_{1\uparrow}n_{2\downarrow}(1-n_{1\downarrow}-n_{2\uparrow})+n_{1\downarrow}n_{2\uparrow}(1-n_{1\uparrow}-n_{2\downarrow})

\displaystyle[\tilde{\psi},\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=n_{1\uparrow}n_{1\downarrow}(1-n_{2\uparrow}-n_{2\downarrow})-n_{2\uparrow}n_{2\downarrow}(1-n_{1\uparrow}-n_{1\downarrow}).

One can also demonstrate that

\displaystyle[n_{1\uparrow},\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=[n_{1\downarrow},\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=-\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger},

(18)

\displaystyle[n_{2\uparrow},\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=[n_{2\downarrow},\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger},

\displaystyle[n_{1\uparrow}n_{1\downarrow},\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=-\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}(n_{1\uparrow}+n_{1\downarrow}-1)

(19)

\displaystyle=-(n_{1\uparrow}+n_{1\downarrow}+1)\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle[n_{2\uparrow}n_{2\downarrow},\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}(n_{2\uparrow}+n_{2\downarrow}+1)

\displaystyle=(n_{2\uparrow}+n_{2\downarrow}-1)\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle[n_{1\uparrow}n_{2\uparrow},\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}(n_{1\uparrow}-n_{2\uparrow}-1)=(n_{1\uparrow}-n_{2\uparrow}+1)\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger},

(20)

\displaystyle[n_{1\downarrow}n_{2\downarrow},\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}(n_{1\downarrow}-n_{2\downarrow}-1)=(n_{1\downarrow}-n_{2\downarrow}+1)\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger},

\displaystyle[n_{1\uparrow}n_{2\downarrow},\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}(n_{1\uparrow}-n_{2\downarrow}-1)=(n_{1\uparrow}-n_{2\downarrow}+1)\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger},

\displaystyle[n_{1\downarrow}n_{2\uparrow},\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}(n_{1\downarrow}-n_{2\uparrow}-1)=(n_{1\downarrow}-n_{2\uparrow}+1)\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger},

and

together with a series of similar equations for the

\displaystyle[H,\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=2\big[-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}-V_{1}(n_{1\uparrow}+n_{1\downarrow}+1)

(22)

\displaystyle+V_{2}(n_{2\uparrow}+n_{2\downarrow}-1)

\displaystyle+U(n_{1\uparrow}+n_{1\downarrow}-n_{2\uparrow}-n_{2\downarrow}+2)\big]\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle+2\overline{U}\big[n_{1\uparrow}n_{1\downarrow}(1-n_{2\uparrow}-n_{2\downarrow})-n_{2\uparrow}n_{2\downarrow}(1-n_{1\uparrow}-n_{1\downarrow})\big],

\displaystyle[H,\tilde{\psi}]=2\big[\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}+V_{1}(n_{1\uparrow}+n_{1\downarrow}-1)

(23)

\displaystyle-V_{2}(n_{2\uparrow}+n_{2\downarrow}+1)

\displaystyle-U(n_{1\uparrow}+n_{1\downarrow}-n_{2\uparrow}-n_{2\downarrow}-2)\big]\tilde{\psi}

\displaystyle-2\overline{U}\big[n_{1\uparrow}n_{1\downarrow}(1-n_{2\uparrow}-n_{2\downarrow})-n_{2\uparrow}n_{2\downarrow}(1-n_{1\uparrow}-n_{1\downarrow})\big],

where care was taken in Eqs. (

) and (

\psi
–operators at the right side.

Notice that all the commutators in the preceding sub–section have the
remarkable property of being expressed in terms of just the occupation
numbers
n_{1s}
and
n_{2s}
of the truncated one–electron basis.
Although the occupation numbers are in general not conserved magnitudes,
their mean values will be taken here as approximately good quantum
numbers. More precisely, it will be assumed that when the number
operators
n_{k}
are applied directly to a stationary state of the
system they can be replaced by their classical expectation value. These
expectation values are such that they make extremal the mean energy of
the system. The scheme proposed here for the He atom is particularly
simple because we have only four basis states and the occupation numbers
are not expected to depend on the spin. Hence one can define

\langle n_{1\uparrow}\rangle=\langle n_{1\downarrow}\rangle=1-\eta,\qquad\langle n_{2\uparrow}\rangle=\langle n_{2\downarrow}\rangle=\eta,

(24)

where
0\leq\eta\leq 1
is a unique variational parameter. The commutators
(
22
) and (
23
) then become

\displaystyle[H,\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}]=2\big[-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}-V_{1}(3-2\eta)-V_{2}(1-2\eta)

\displaystyle+4U(1-\eta)\,\big]\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}+2\overline{U}(1-2\eta)(1-2\eta+2\eta^{2})

\displaystyle[H,\tilde{\psi}]=2\big[\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}+V_{1}(1-2\eta)-V_{2}(1+2\eta)

(26)

\displaystyle+4U\eta\big]\tilde{\psi}-2\overline{U}(1-2\eta)(1-2\eta+2\eta^{2}).

Defining now the two operators

\psi_{+}^{\dagger}=\tilde{\psi}^{\dagger}+\Lambda_{+}\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{-}=\tilde{\psi}+\Lambda_{-}

\Lambda_{+}=\frac{-(1-2\eta)(1-2\eta+2\eta^{2})\overline{U}}{-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}-V_{1}(3-2\eta)-V_{2}(1-2\eta)+4U(1-\eta)}\,,

\displaystyle[H,\psi_{-}]=2\big[\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}+V_{1}(1-2\eta)-V_{2}(1+2\eta)

This way the operators
\psi_{+}^{\dagger}

are raising

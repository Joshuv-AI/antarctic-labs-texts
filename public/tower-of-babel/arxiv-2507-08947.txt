A joint channel estimation and beamforming separation principle for massive MIMO systems PubID: pubid: This work has been submitted to the IEEE for possible publication. Copyright may be transferred without notice, after which this version may no longer be accessible.
Lorenzo Miretti, Slawomir Stańczak, Giuseppe Caire

Abstract
We demonstrate that separating beamforming (i.e., downlink precoding and uplink combining) and channel estimation in multi-user MIMO wireless systems incurs no loss of optimality under general conditions that apply to a wide variety of models in the literature, including canonical reciprocity-based cellular and cell-free massive MIMO system models. Specifically, we provide conditions under which optimal processing in terms of ergodic achievable rates can be decomposed into minimum mean-square error (MMSE) channel estimation followed by MMSE beamforming, for both centralized and distributed architectures. Applications of our results are illustrated in terms of concrete examples and numerical simulations.

I-A

Joint channel estimation and beamforming

One potential limitation, which is the focus of this study, is that pilot-based channel estimation and beamforming design are performed in a somewhat disjoint fashion, i.e., as separate optimization problems. Specifically, the current literature imposes processing architectures that separate the channel estimation and beamforming functions
a priori
. While this approach has the obvious advantage of simplicity (which we certainly do not dismiss), it has the drawback of neglecting the potential benefit of a joint design of the two processing functions, or better yet, of optimal beamformers that take the received raw pilot signals directly as input. Interestingly, the joint approach aligns well with the recent trend towards the aggregation of different physical layer functions
[
15
]
, enabled by the rapid emergence of modern artificial intelligence techniques capable of handling seemingly intractable joint optimization problems. However, to justify the corresponding research and development effort, it is necessary to advance the current massive MIMO theoretical framework to quantify the potential performance gains (if any) of joint channel estimation and beamforming.

I-B

Main contribution

In this study, we contribute to closing the above gap in the literature by providing general conditions under which separating the channel estimation and beamforming functions incurs no loss of optimality. Furthermore, we show that when these conditions are met, the two functions can be individually optimized according to a minimum mean-squared error (MMSE) criterion. Informally, we derive the following joint channel estimation and beamforming separation principle.

II
System model

We consider a wireless multi-user MIMO communication network composed by
K

\mathcal{K}=\{1,\ldots,K\}

M

\mathcal{M}=\{1,\ldots,M\}

For both uplink and downlink, we consider transmission over a canonical Gaussian vector channel with fading governed by an arbitrarily distributed channel matrix
\bm{H}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{h}_{1}&\ldots&\bm{h}_{K}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{C}^{M\times K}

for enhancing signal quality and for mitigating interference.

III
Optimality of uplink MSE processing

In this section, we review and provide new insights on the optimality of the MSE criterion for beamforming optimization. While ubiquitous in the classical multi-user MIMO literature, this criterion was rigorously connected to ergodic achievable rates only in terms of
R_{k}^{\mathsf{oer}}
in (
4

\mathcal{V}_{k}=\mathcal{C}^{M}
), or of a special form of

in (

[

III-A

Use-and-then-forget lower bound

For every

and
\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}

\displaystyle(\forall\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k})~\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}[|x_{k}-y_{k}|^{2}].

\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\inf_{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k})

\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\sup_{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\bm{v}_{k})

Proof.

Moreover, by the monotonicity of the logarithm, we readily obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1

For all

\displaystyle\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k})=\mathsf{E}[\bm{v}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{H}\bm{P}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{H}}+\bm{I}_{M})\bm{v}_{k}-2\sqrt{p_{k}}\Re(\bm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}_{k})+1],

\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}

S_{k}
. More generally, borrowing elements from the control theoretical literature on multi-agent coordinated decision making under partial information (also called

25
]
), the structure of optimal beamformers can be conveniently characterized by a set of necessary and sufficient optimality conditions
[
17
]
, as formalized in the following.

Proposition 5
.

For all
k\in\mathcal{K}
, assume
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})~\bm{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{K}_{S_{l,k}}
and the regularity condition
\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{H}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{H}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}]<\infty
. Then
\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\inf_{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k})
if and only if
\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}
is the unique solution to the feasibility problem

\displaystyle\textnormal{find}~\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}~\textnormal{such that}~(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bm{v}_{l,k}=\Big(\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}\bm{P}\bm{H}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}|S_{l,k}]+\bm{I}_{N}\Big)^{-1}\\
&\Big(\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}|S_{l,k}]\bm{P}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{e}_{k}-\textstyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{L}_{k}\backslash\{l\}}\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}\bm{P}\bm{H}_{j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}_{j,k}|S_{l,k}]\Big).\end{split}

(9)

Proof.

The proof is given in Appendix
-C
.
∎

Remark 3
.

The regularity condition
\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{H}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{H}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}]<\infty
is satisfied for any physically consistent (uniformly bounded) fading distribution, as well as for any Gaussian fading distribution.

Remark 4
.

The assumption
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})

\bm{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{K}_{S_{l,k}}
is equivalent to assuming that
\bm{p}
is a function of some common beamforming CSI. This is readily satisfied for most uplink massive MIMO models, where powers are typically adjusted on a statistical basis, i.e., where
\bm{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}
is deterministic. Nevertheless, we consider this more general assumption because it is instrumental for the downlink case, covered later in Section
V
.

Remark 5
.

For the fully centralized case
\mathcal{V}_{k}=\mathcal{F}_{S_{k}}^{M}
, optimal beamformers
\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\inf_{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k})
are given by

\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}=\Big(\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}\bm{P}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{H}}|S_{k}]+\bm{I}_{M}\Big)^{-1}\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}\bm{P}^{\frac{1}{2}}|S_{k}]\bm{e}_{k}.

This result can be shown via direct arguments, or recovered by letting
L=1
and
N=M
in Proposition
5
. Similar expressions are readily given for the clustered centralized case, by considering only the submatrices
\bm{H}^{(k)}
of
\bm{H}
corresponding to the groups of antennas indexed by
\mathcal{L}_{k}
.

The main consequence of the above results is that optimizing the UatF lower bound under arbitrary beamforming architectures, and mild assumptions which are always satisfied in practice, is equivalent to finding a solution to the (infinite dimensional) feasibility problem (
5
). For the particular case of (clustered) centralized beamforming architectures, optimal beamformers are readily obtained as described in Remark
5
.

III-B

Coherent decoding lower bound

We now provide a novel generalization of the above properties to the coherent decoding lower bound. Specifically, for every
k\in\mathcal{K}
and
\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
, let
\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{coh,ul}}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})
be the SINR in (
5
) specialized to the uplink case, where the dependency on the beamformers and the conditioning on the decoder CSI
U_{k}
is made explicit. Note that, due to the conditioning on
U_{k}
,
\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{coh,ul}}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})
is a random variable. Similarly, let us consider the conditional MSE of user
k\in\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle(\forall\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k})~\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})=\mathsf{E}[|x_{k}-y_{k}|^{2}|U_{k}].

(10)

In the following, to avoid mathematical technicalities arising with the optimization of random variables, the notion of supremum/infimum is replaced by the notion of
essential
supremum/infimum, i.e., the random variable constructed by taking the supremum/infimum under the almost-sure ordering (see the notation section for a more precise definition).

Proposition 6
.

Assume
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})

S_{l,k}=(S_{l,k}^{\prime},U_{k})
, i.e., that the decoder CSI
U_{k}
in (
3
) is also common beamforming CSI as in Definition
2
. For all
k\in\mathcal{K}
,

1+\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\mathrm{ess~sup}~}\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{coh,ul}}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})=\Big(\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\mathrm{ess~inf}~}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})\Big)^{-1}.

Furthermore, if
\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\mathrm{ess~inf}~}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})
, then
\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\mathrm{ess~sup}~}\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{coh,ul}}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})
.

Proof.

We first observe that
\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\mathrm{ess~inf}~}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\mathrm{ess~inf}~}\underset{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{U_{k}}}{\mathrm{ess~inf}~}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\alpha\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\mathrm{ess~inf}~}\dfrac{1}{1+\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{coh,ul}}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{1+\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\mathrm{ess~sup}~}\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{coh,ul}}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})}

where
(a)
follows from
(\forall\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{U_{k}})(\forall\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k})

\alpha\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
, and
(b)
follows from standard minimization of convex quadratic forms in
\mathbb{C}
for each realization of
U_{k}
as in the proof of Proposition
2
. The second part of the statement follows from the similar chain of equalities
\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}|U_{k})=\underset{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{U_{k}}}{\mathrm{ess~inf}~}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\alpha\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}|U_{k})=(1+\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{coh,ul}}(\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}|U_{k}))^{-1}
, where the first equality holds by assumption.
∎

Corollary 2
.

Assume
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})

S_{l,k}=(S_{l,k}^{\prime},U_{k})
, i.e., that
U_{k}
is also common beamforming CSI. For all
k\in\mathcal{K}
, if
\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\mathrm{ess~inf}~}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})
, then
\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\sup_{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}R_{k}^{\mathsf{coh},\mathsf{ul}}(\bm{v}_{k})
.

The above results generalize the known related result in, e.g.,
[
12
,
14
]
, which is restricted to centralized beamforming, Gaussian fading, and MMSE channel estimation. Essentially, it states that the conditional SINR in (
5
) is maximized (in a stochastic sense) by the same beamformer that minimizes the conditional MSE, provided that the CSI
U_{k}
used for decoding is also perfectly shared among all beamforming processing units serving user
k
. This assumption is clearly satisfied for centralized uplink processing architectures, regardless the channel model and estimation technique. Furthermore, it also covers semi-distributed architectures operating based on a mix of local and common CSI. An important remark is that, due to this assumption, optimal beamformers attaining the essential infimum can be constructed in practice by evaluating the pointwise infimum for each realization of
U_{k}
. Based on this intuition, we derive the following proposition that relates conditional and unconditional MSE minimization.

Proposition 7
.

For all
k\in\mathcal{K}
, assume
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})

S_{l,k}=(S_{l,k}^{\prime},U_{k})
, i.e., that
U_{k}
is also common beamforming CSI. Furthermore, assume
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})~\bm{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{K}_{S_{l,k}}
, and the regularity condition
\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{H}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{H}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}]<\infty
. Then,
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})

\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\inf_{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k})\iff\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\mathrm{ess~inf}~}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})
.

Proof.

By definition of essential infimum,
(\forall\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k})

\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k})=\mathsf{E}\left[\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})\right]\geq\mathsf{E}\Big[\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\mathrm{ess~inf}~}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})\Big]

holds. Hence,
\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\mathrm{ess~inf}~}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k}|U_{k})\implies\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\inf_{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k})
readily holds (without additional assumptions). The proof of the converse statement is given in Appendix
-D
, and is based on the additional assumptions.
∎

Remark 6
.

If
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})

S_{l,k}=(S_{l,k}^{\prime},U_{k})
and
\bm{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{U_{k}}^{K}
, i.e., if
U_{k}
is also common information for both beamforming and power control, then
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})

\bm{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{K}_{S_{l,k}}
readily holds. This observation is instrumental for the downlink case, covered later in Section
V
.

Combining Corollary
2
and Proposition
7
, we then obtain the following connection between unconditional MSE minimization and ergodic rate maximization problems.

Corollary 3
.

Assume
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})

S_{l,k}=(S_{l,k}^{\prime},U_{k})
, i.e., that
U_{k}
is also common beamforming CSI. Furthermore, assume
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})~\bm{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{K}_{S_{l,k}}
, and the regularity condition
\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{H}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{H}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}]<\infty
. If
\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\inf_{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k})
, then
\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\arg\sup_{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}R_{k}^{\mathsf{coh},\mathsf{ul}}(\bm{v}_{k})
.

Interestingly, we observe that optimizing the unconditional MSE under centralized or semi-distributed beamforming architectures (and mild assumptions, see Remark
3
and Remark
4
) is equivalent to optimizing both the coherent decoding lower bound and the UatF lower bound (see Corollary
1
). For example, if
S_{k}=U_{k}
, optimal (clustered) centralized beamformers in terms of both the coherent decoding lower bound and the UatF bound can be readily obtained as discussed in Remark
5
. Furthermore, more general cases can be addressed by finding a solution to the feasibility problem (
5
).

IV
Joint channel estimation and beamforming

Having established the optimality of uplink MSE processing under fairly general conditions, we now focus on the beamforming optimization problem for each user
k\in\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\text{minimize}}~\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k}),

(11)

where we recall that
\mathcal{V}_{k}
denotes a subset of functions of some arbitrary CSI
S_{k}
. In particular,
S_{k}
does not need to be restricted to channel estimates
\hat{\bm{H}}
obtained as the output of a specific channel estimation procedure. For example,
S_{k}
could be directly mapped to raw measurements of uplink pilot signals collected by the infrastructure. In this case, (
11
) should be rather interpreted as
joint
channel estimation and beamforming optimization problems. In general, the usual approach of separating channel estimation and beamforming may not be optimal for the joint problem. Nevertheless, in the following we demonstrate that this approach is actually optimal under common assumptions that apply to a wide variety of models in the literature.

IV-A

Centralized processing architectures

We start by considering the particular case of centralized uplink processing architectures, for which an optimal solution can be obtained directly.

Proposition 8
.

For all
k\in\mathcal{K}
, let
(\bm{H},S_{k})
be circularly symmetric jointly Gaussian distributed,
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L})

(\forall j\in\mathcal{L})

S_{l,k}=S_{j,k}
(centralized CSI), and
\bm{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{K}_{S_{k}}
. Then, the optimal solution to (
11
) is given by the centralized MMSE beamformer

\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}=\Big(\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}\bm{P}\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}+\bm{\Psi}^{(k)}+\bm{I}_{M}\Big)^{-1}\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}\bm{P}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{e}_{k},

(12)

where
\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}:=\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}^{(k)}|S_{k}]

is the (linear/affine) MMSE estimate of
\bm{H}^{(k)}:=\bm{C}_{k}\bm{H}
given
S_{k}
, where
\bm{C}_{k}:=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{C}_{1,k},\ldots,\bm{C}_{L,k})
is a block-diagonal matrix satisfying

(\forall l\in\mathcal{L})~\bm{C}_{l,k}:=\begin{cases}\bm{I}_{N}&\text{if }l\in\mathcal{L}_{k},\\
\bm{0}_{N\times N}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and where
\bm{\Psi}^{(k)}:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}p_{j}\mathsf{E}[\tilde{\bm{h}}_{j}^{(k)}\tilde{\bm{h}}_{j}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}]
collects the covariance matrices of the columns
\tilde{\bm{h}}_{j}^{(k)}
of the estimation error
\tilde{\bm{H}}^{(k)}:=\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}-\bm{H}^{(k)}
.

Proof.

Note that the assumptions of Proposition
5
are satisfied. For the case
\mathcal{L}_{k}=\mathcal{L}
(and hence
\bm{H}^{(k)}=\bm{H}
), by recalling Remark
5
, we then have

\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}:=\Big(\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}\bm{P}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{H}}|S_{k}]+\bm{I}_{M}\Big)^{-1}\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}|S_{k}]\bm{P}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{e}_{k}.

The conditional mean
\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}=\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}|S_{k}]
is the MMSE estimate of
\bm{H}
given
S_{k}
. Since
\bm{H}
and
S_{k}
are jointly Gaussian, then
\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}
is also the linear/affine MMSE estimate of
\bm{H}
given
S_{k}
, and the estimation error
\tilde{\bm{H}}^{(k)}=\bm{H}-\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}
is independent of
S_{k}
. Therefore, by using
\mathsf{E}[\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}\bm{P}\tilde{\bm{H}}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}|S_{k}]=\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}\bm{P}\mathsf{E}[\tilde{\bm{H}}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}]=\bm{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}\bm{P}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{H}}|S_{k}]

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}\bm{P}\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}+\mathsf{E}[\tilde{\bm{H}}^{(k)}\bm{P}\tilde{\bm{H}}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}|S_{k}]

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}\bm{P}\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}p_{j}\mathsf{E}[\tilde{\bm{h}}_{j}^{(k)}\tilde{\bm{h}}_{j}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}|S_{k}]

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}\bm{P}\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}p_{j}\mathsf{E}[\tilde{\bm{h}}_{j}^{(k)}\tilde{\bm{h}}_{j}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}].

The more general case with
\mathcal{L}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
follow readily by replacing
\bm{H}
with
\bm{H}^{(k)}
in all the above steps.
∎

The above proposition establishes that, under Gaussian measurements of Gaussian channels (and a mild assumption on the transmit powers, see Remark
4
), optimal centralized uplink MSE processing can be decomposed into a linear/affine MMSE channel estimation step followed by a centralized MMSE beamforming step.

Remark 7
.

Combined with Corollary
1
and Corollary
2
in Section
III
, Proposition
8
also establishes the optimality of this functional separation in terms of both the UatF lower bound and the coherent decoding lower bound (given decoder CSI
U_{k}=S_{k}
) on the achievable ergodic rates (
3
).

Due to to its simplicity, this type of processing is already widely considered in the literature under specific models that satisfy our assumption on
(\bm{H},S_{k})
(see, e.g.,
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
). However, the optimality of this functional separation in terms of ergodic achievable rates was not reported before.

IV-B

Distributed processing architectures

We now consider more general distributed uplink processing architectures subject to mild assumptions related to the CSI acquisition procedure. Specifically, we consider the following specialized version of the necessary and sufficient optimality conditions given by (
5
).

Proposition 9
.

For all
k\in\mathcal{K}
, let
(\bm{H},S_{k})
be circularly symmetric jointly Gaussian distributed,
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L})(\forall j\in\mathcal{L}\backslash\{l\})

\bm{H}_{l}\to S_{l,k}\to S_{j,k}\to\bm{H}_{j}
form a Markov chain, and
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})~\bm{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{K}_{S_{l,k}}
.
Then, Problem (
11
) admits a solution
\bm{v}^{\star}_{k}
if and only if
\bm{v}_{k}^{\star}
is the unique
\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
satisfying
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})

\bm{v}_{l,k}=\bm{V}_{l,k}\Bigg(\bm{e}_{k}-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{L}_{k}\backslash\{l\}}\bm{P}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathsf{E}\left[\hat{\bm{H}}_{j}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}_{j,k}\middle|S_{l,k}\right]\Bigg),

(13)

where
\bm{V}_{l,k}:=\Big(\hat{\bm{H}}_{l}^{(k)}\bm{P}\hat{\bm{H}}_{l}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}+\bm{\Psi}_{l}^{(k)}+\bm{I}_{N}\Big)^{-1}\hat{\bm{H}}_{l}^{(k)}\bm{P}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the local MMSE beamformer, where
\hat{\bm{H}}_{l}^{(k)}:=\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}|S_{l,k}]
is the (linear/affine) MMSE estimate of
\bm{H}_{l}
given
S_{l,k}
, and where
\bm{\Psi}_{l}^{(k)}:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}p_{j}\mathsf{E}[\tilde{\bm{h}}_{l,j}^{(k)}\tilde{\bm{h}}_{l,j}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}]
collects the covariance matrices of the columns
\tilde{\bm{h}}_{l,j}^{(k)}
of the local estimation error
\tilde{\bm{H}}_{l}^{(k)}:=\hat{\bm{H}}_{l}^{(k)}-\bm{H}_{l}^{(k)}
.

Proof.

Note that the assumptions of Proposition
5
are satisfied. We then manipulate the expression

\begin{split}&\bm{v}_{l,k}=\Big(\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}\bm{P}\bm{H}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}|S_{l,k}]+\bm{I}_{N}\Big)^{-1}\\
&\Big(\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}|S_{l,k}]\bm{P}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{e}_{k}-\textstyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{L}_{k}\backslash\{l\}}\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}\bm{P}\bm{H}_{j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}_{j,k}|S_{l,k}]\Big)\end{split}

in (
5
) using the additional assumptions. For all
l\in\mathcal{L}
, due to the Gaussianity of
(\bm{H}_{l},S_{l,k})
, we have that
\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}_{l}=\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}|S_{l,k}]
corresponds to the linear/affine MMSE estimate of
\bm{H}_{l}
given
S_{l,k}
, and, by following similar steps as in the proof of Proposition
8
, that

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}\bm{P}\bm{H}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}|S_{l,k}]=\hat{\bm{H}}_{l}^{(k)}\bm{P}\hat{\bm{H}}_{l}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}+\bm{\Psi}_{l}^{(k)}.

Moreover, we have
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L})(\forall j\in\mathcal{L}\backslash\{l\})

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}\bm{P}\bm{H}_{j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}_{j,k}|S_{l,k}]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}[\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}\bm{P}\bm{H}_{j}^{\mathsf{H}}|S_{j,k},S_{l,k}]\bm{v}_{j,k}|S_{l,k}]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}[\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}|S_{j,k},S_{l,k}]\bm{P}\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{j}^{\mathsf{H}}|S_{j,k},S_{l,k}]\bm{v}_{j,k}|S_{l,k}]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}[\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{l}|S_{l,k}]\bm{P}\mathsf{E}[\bm{H}_{j}^{\mathsf{H}}|S_{j,k}]\bm{v}_{j,k}|S_{l,k}]

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}_{l}\bm{P}\mathsf{E}[\hat{\bm{H}}_{j}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}_{j,k}|S_{l,k}],

where the first equality follows from the law of total expectation and
\bm{v}_{j,k}\in\mathcal{F}_{S_{j,k}}^{N}
, the second equality from the Markov chain
\bm{H}_{l}\to(S_{l,k},S_{j,k})\to\bm{H}_{j}
and since
\bm{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{K}_{S_{l,k}}
, and the third equality from the Markov chains
S_{l,k}\to S_{j,k}\to\bm{H}_{j}
and
S_{j,k}\to S_{l,k}\to\bm{H}_{l}
. The proof is concluded by rearranging the terms.
∎

Remark 8
.

The Markov chain in the above proposition essentially corresponds to scenarios where each
j
th processing unit can only acquire a degraded version of the local measurements of the local channel
\bm{H}_{l}
available at the
l
th processing unit. This is a very common setup in the distributed cell-free massive MIMO literature, where CSI is typically acquired by means of pilot-based local measurements of the local channels, potentially followed by an imperfect CSI sharing procedure over the fronthaul, and where the local channels are mutually independent due to their geographical separation.

Proposition
9
establishes that, under degraded Gaussian measurements of Gaussian channels, optimal distributed uplink MSE processing can be decomposed into: (i) a local MMSE channel estimation stage; (ii) a local MMSE beamforming stage; and (iii) a correction stage for each processing unit that takes into account the impact of the other processing units based on the available information.

Remark 9
.

Combined with Corollary
1
in Section
III
, Proposition
9
shows that this functional separation is also optimal in terms of the UatF lower bound on the achievable ergodic rates (
3
). Moreover, combined with Corollary
2
, it also establishes the optimality in terms of the coherent decoding lower bound, provided that the decoder CSI
U_{k}
is perfectly shared among all processing units
l\in\mathcal{L}_{k}
serving user
k\in\mathcal{K}
.

We point out that very similar optimality conditions to those in Proposition
9
were already reported in
[
17
]
[Lemma 2], by restricting the CSI of each processing unit to local (MMSE) channel estimates and to degraded versions of the local estimates available at the other processing units. Our result considers more general setups.

IV-C

Examples

The optimality conditions in Proposition
9
correspond to an infinite dimensional linear system of equations. In general, solving this system may require approximate numerical algorithms
[
25
]
. Nevertheless, in the following we present two simple yet practical examples that admit a closed-form solution in terms of channel statistics. First, we consider the case of fully local processing.

Proposition 10
.

For all
k\in\mathcal{K}
, let
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L})(\forall j\in\mathcal{L}\backslash\{l\})

(\bm{H}_{l},S_{l,k})
be circularly symmetric jointly Gaussian distributed and independent of
(\bm{H}_{j},S_{j,k})
(local CSI), and
\bm{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}
. Then, the optimal solution to (
11
) is given by

(\forall l\in\mathcal{L})~\bm{v}_{l,k}^{\star}=\bm{V}_{l,k}\bm{c}_{l,k},

(14)

where
\bm{V}_{l,k}
is the local MMSE stage obtained as a function of the local MMSE channel estimate
\hat{\bm{H}}^{(k)}_{l}
defined in Proposition
9
, and
\bm{c}_{l,k}\in\mathbb{C}^{K}
is a statistical beamforming stage given by the unique solution to the linear system of equations

\begin{cases}\bm{c}_{l,k}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{L}_{k}\backslash\{l\}}\bm{\Pi}_{j,k}\bm{c}_{j,k}=\bm{e}_{k}&\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k},\\
\bm{c}_{l,k}=\bm{0}_{K\times 1}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
(\forall l\in\mathcal{L})~\bm{\Pi}_{l,k}:=\mathsf{E}\left[\bm{P}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\bm{H}}_{l}^{(k)\mathsf{H}}\bm{V}_{l,k}\right]
.

Proof.

The proof follows by verifying that (
14
) satisfies the optimality conditions (
13
). The details are similar to
[
17
, Theorem 4]
, and are reported in Appendix
-E

V
Optimality of downlink MSE processing

In this section we show that the main findings of the previous sections, derived for the uplink case, can be extended to the downlink case by leveraging various forms of duality between uplink and downlink achievable ergodic rate regions.

V-A

Hardening lower bound

For every
k\in\mathcal{K}
and
(\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K})\in\mathcal{V}_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{V}_{K}
, let
\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{hard}}(\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K})
be the SINR in (
6
) specialized to the downlink case, where the dependency on the beamformers is made explicit. Similarly, for every
k\in\mathcal{K}
,
\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
, and
\bm{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}_{+}
, we let
\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{p})
be the SINR in (
6
) specialized to the uplink case. Given some ergodic rate constraints, or, equivalently, some SINR constraints
(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{K})\in\mathbb{R}^{K}_{+}
, we consider the following downlink feasibility problem

\displaystyle\text{find}

\displaystyle(\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K},\bm{p})\in\mathcal{V}_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{V}_{K}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}

(16)

\displaystyle\text{such that}

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{hard}}(\sqrt{p_{1}}\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\sqrt{p_{K}}\bm{v}_{K})=\gamma_{k},

and the uplink feasibility problem

\displaystyle\text{find}

\displaystyle(\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K},\bm{p})\in\mathcal{V}_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{V}_{K}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}

(17)

\displaystyle\text{such that}

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{p})=\gamma_{k}.

Note that, in the downlink formulation (
16
), the total power allocated to user
k\in\mathcal{K}
is given by
p_{k}\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{k}\|^{2}]
, i.e., the beamformers are not normalized.
The above problems can be rigorously connected by means of well-known uplink-downlink duality arguments.

Proposition 12
.

Problem (
16
) admits a solution
(\bm{v}_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K}^{\star},\bm{p}^{\mathsf{dl}})
if and only if Problem (
17
) admits a solution
(\bm{v}_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K}^{\star},\bm{p}^{\mathsf{ul}})
, for the same beamformers
(\bm{v}_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K}^{\star})
(
\bm{p}^{\mathsf{ul}}
and
\bm{p}^{\mathsf{dl}}
can be different). Moreover, the power vector
\bm{p}^{\mathsf{dl}}
(resp.
\bm{p}^{\mathsf{ul}}
) solves the system
(\bm{D}-\bm{\Gamma}\bm{B})\bm{p}^{\mathsf{dl}}=\bm{\Gamma}\bm{1}
(resp.
(\bm{D}-\bm{\Gamma}\bm{B}^{\mathsf{T}})\bm{p}^{\mathsf{ul}}=\bm{\Gamma}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{1}
) parametrized by
\bm{\Gamma}:=\mathrm{diag}\left(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{K}\right)
,
\bm{D}:=\mathrm{diag}\left(|\mathsf{E}[\bm{h}^{\mathsf{H}}_{1}\bm{v}^{\star}_{1}]|^{2},\ldots,|\mathsf{E}[\bm{h}^{\mathsf{H}}_{K}\bm{v}^{\star}_{K}]|^{2}\right)
,
\bm{\Sigma}:=\mathrm{diag}\left(\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{1}^{\star}\|^{2}],\ldots,\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{K}^{\star}\|^{2}]\right)
, and

\displaystyle\bm{B}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}\mathsf{E}[|\bm{h}_{1}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}_{1}^{\star}|^{2}]&\ldots&\mathsf{E}[|\bm{h}_{1}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}^{\star}_{K}|^{2}]\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathsf{E}[|\bm{h}_{K}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}^{\star}_{1}|^{2}]&\ldots&\mathsf{E}[|\bm{h}_{K}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}^{\star}_{K}|^{2}]\end{bmatrix}-\bm{D}.

Furthermore, the two solutions satisfy
\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{p}^{\mathsf{ul}}=\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{p}^{\mathsf{dl}}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from known uplink-downlink duality arguments in the power control literature (reviewed, e.g., in
[
28
,
12
]
). For completeness, a proof tailored to the considered system model and notation is reported in Appendix
-F
.
∎

An important consequence of Proposition
12
is that downlink beamforming optimization problems involving the hardening bound and sum power constraints can be equivalently performed over a (virtual) dual uplink channel. Popular examples include weighted sum-rate maximization subject to a sum power constraint, and weighted minimum SINR maximization subject to a sum power constraint. It is important to clarify that the main benefit of working on a dual uplink channel is not to turn difficult problems into easier ones. In particular, although downlink weighted minimum SINR maximization can be conveniently addressed using known methods for the uplink case
[
29
]
, provably NP-hard problems such as downlink weighted sum-rate maximization remain equally difficult on a dual uplink channel. However, working on a dual uplink channel helps identifying useful structural properties of the solution, as discussed in more detail below.

All the aforementioned optimization problems typically seek for Pareto optimal beamformers under a sum power constraint, i.e., that produce Pareto efficient SINR tuples corresponding to points on the boundary of the hardening inner bound on the ergodic achievable rate region under a sum power constraint. In general, the structure of Pareto optimal downlink beamformers under a sum power constraints can be investigated by considering the following downlink sum power minimization problem

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{k}\|^{2}]

(18)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{hard}}(\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K})\geq\gamma_{k}.

For example, one could assume that
(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{K})
is a sum-rate (Pareto) optimal SINR tuple. Although an efficient algorithm for determining such
(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{K})
is not available, studying the above problem can reveal the structure of sum-rate optimal downlink beamformers.
In fact, by Proposition
12
, optimal downlink beamformers can be equivalently obtained (up to a known scaling factor) by solving

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\\
\bm{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}p_{k}

\displaystyle\text{subject to }

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{p})\geq\gamma_{k},

which, due to the decoupled constraints in
(\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K})
, can be further rewritten as
[
29
]

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}p_{k}

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\sup_{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{p})\geq\gamma_{k}.

We further recall that, by the results in Section
III-A
, optimal beamformers attaining the above supremum can be obtained as solutions to MSE minimization problems in (
11
).
Therefore, for the hardening inner bound under a sum power constraint, the main insights on joint optimal channel estimation and beamforming design from Section
IV
directly apply also to the downlink case. In particular, coming back to the sum-rate maximization example, we notice that optimal downlink beamformers under a sum power constraint take the form of (
12
), (
13
), (
14
), or (
15
), depending on the information constraints. The only remaining (albeit challenging) step toward a complete solution is the tuning of the
K
nonnegative parameters
\bm{p}
such that the sum rate is maximized.

V-B

Coherent decoding lower bound

Throughout this section, we assume genie-aided availability of some common decoder side information
U_{k}=U
at each user

(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})

(\forall l\in\mathcal{L}_{k})

k\in\mathcal{K}
and
(\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K})\in\mathcal{V}_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{V}_{K}

, the above proposition can be used to study the structure of Pareto optimal downlink beamformers with respect to the coherent decoding inner bound on the ergodic achievable rate region under a sum power constraint. In particular, given minimum ergodic rate constraints
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{K})\in\mathbb{R}^{K}_{+}

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{k}\|^{2}]

(21)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }

. For the above conversion to hold, we remark the crucial assumption on the availability of the side information

for beamforming, which ensures that applying a power scaling factor

for each

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}

(22)

V-C

Extension to per-transmitter power constraints

Considering a sum power constraint is typically enough for studying multi-cell massive MIMO system models. Furthermore, due to its tractability, it is also an excellent proxy for studying cell-free massive MIMO system models. This is because scaling optimal downlink beamformers derived under a sum power constraint, in order to meet more realistic per-transmitter power constraints, typically incurs minor SNR losses in the interference limited regime. Nevertheless, in this section we show that the above insights on joint optimal channel estimation and beamforming under a sum power constraint can also be rigorously applied to the case of per-transmitter power constraints.

Following the approach in
[
24
]
, we focus on the following modified version of Problem (
18
)

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{k}\|^{2}]

(23)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{hard}}(\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K})\geq\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle(\forall l\in\mathcal{L})~\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{l,k}\|^{2}]\leq P_{l},

where
(P_{1},\ldots,P_{L})\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{L}
is a given tuple of per-transmitter power constraints.
We then consider the following augmented Lagrangian formulation

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{k}\|_{\bm{1}+\bm{\lambda}}^{2}]

(24)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{hard}}(\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K})\geq\gamma_{k},

where
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{L})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{L}
are Lagrangian multipliers associated with the power constraints, and where we define
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})(\forall\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k})

\|\bm{v}_{k}\|_{\bm{1}+\bm{\lambda}}^{2}:=\sum_{l=1}^{L}(1+\lambda_{l})\|\bm{v}_{l,k}\|^{2}
. The following proposition connects the above two problems.

Proposition 14
.

If Problem (
23
) is feasible, then there exists
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{L}
such that a solution to Problem (
23
) is given by a solution to Problem (
24
).

Proof.

The proof is given in
[
24
]
for the special case of strict feasibility, and by further restricting
\mathcal{V}_{k}
to elements with finite second-order moment without loss of generality. In Appendix
-G
, we show how to modify the proof in
[
24
]
to cover the more general feasibility assumption.
∎

We notice the similarity between Problem (
24
) and Problem (
18
). Hence, we expect that Problem (
24
) can be similarly converted into a dual uplink problem. In fact, as observed in
[
24
]
, for all
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{L}
, a solution to Problem (
24
) can be equivalently obtained (up to a known scaling factor) by solving

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\\
\bm{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}p_{k}

\displaystyle\text{subject to }

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\widetilde{\mathsf{SINR}}_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{p},\bm{\lambda})\geq\gamma_{k},

where
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})

(\forall\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k})

(\forall\bm{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K})

(\forall\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{L})

\widetilde{\mathsf{SINR}}_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{p},\bm{\lambda}):=

\dfrac{p_{k}|\mathsf{E}[\bm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}_{k}]|^{2}}{p_{k}\mathsf{V}(\bm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}_{k})+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}\backslash\{k\}}p_{j}\mathsf{E}[|\bm{h}_{j}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}_{k}|^{2}]+\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{k}\|_{\bm{1}+\bm{\lambda}}^{2}]}

is a modified version of
\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{p})
obtained by replacing the noise covariance
\bm{I}_{M}
in (
2
) with the augmented noise covariance
\bm{I}_{M}+\bm{\Lambda}:=\bm{I}_{M}+\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1}\bm{I}_{N},\ldots,\lambda_{L}\bm{I}_{N})
. This equivalence readily follows by a minor modification of the uplink-downlink duality principle in Proposition
12
obtained by replacing
\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}
with
\widetilde{\mathsf{SINR}}_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}
in Problem (

\bm{\Sigma}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{1}^{\star}\|^{2}],\ldots,\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{K}^{\star}\|^{2}]\right)
with
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{\lambda}}:=\mathrm{diag}\left(\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{1}^{\star}\|_{\bm{1}+\bm{\lambda}}^{2}],\ldots,\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{K}^{\star}\|_{\bm{1}+\bm{\lambda}}^{2}]\right)
. Therefore, similar to the sum power constraint case, a solution to Problem (
23
) can be equivalently obtained as scaled versions of uplink beamformers attaining
\sup_{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\widetilde{\mathsf{SINR}}_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{p},\bm{\lambda})
for some
(\bm{p},\bm{\lambda})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{L}
.

Moreover, by using the same arguments on the relation between uplink SINR maximization and MSE minimization in Section
III-A
, it can be readily shown that, for all
(\bm{p},\bm{\lambda})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{L}
, uplink beamformers attaining
\sup_{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\widetilde{\mathsf{SINR}}_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{p},\bm{\lambda})
can be obtained as solutions to modified MSE minimization problems

(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle\underset{\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\text{minimize}}~\widetilde{\mathsf{MSE}}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{p},\bm{\lambda}),

where
\widetilde{\mathsf{MSE}}_{k}(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{p},\bm{\lambda})=\mathsf{E}[\bm{v}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{H}\bm{P}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{H}}+\bm{I}_{M}+\bm{\Lambda})\bm{v}_{k}-2\sqrt{p_{k}}\Re(\bm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}_{k})+1]
. Finally, we observe that all the results in Section
IV
can be readily adapted to the above modified MSE expression. In particular, Proposition
8
on optimal centralized beamforming can be adapted by replacing
\bm{I}_{M}
with
\bm{I}_{M}+\bm{\Lambda}
in the matrix inversion step in (
12
). Similarly, Proposition
9
on optimal distributed beamforming (as well as the given examples in Section
IV-C
) can be adapted by replacing
\bm{I}_{N}
with
\bm{I}_{N}+\lambda_{l}\bm{I}_{N}
in the matrix inversion step of each local MMSE beamformer in (
13
). Therefore, the main conclusions of Section
V-A
can be extended to the case of per-transmitter power constraints, i.e., to Problem (
23
).

We conclude this section by pointing out that a similar result to Proposition
14
can be established also for the coherent decoding lower bound, and in particular to a modified version of Problem (
22
) given by

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{k}\|^{2}|U]

\displaystyle\text{subject to }

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathsf{SINR}_{k}^{\mathsf{coh,dl}}(\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K})\geq\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle(\forall l\in\mathcal{L})~\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathsf{E}[\|\bm{v}_{l,k}\|^{2}|U]\leq P_{l},

where
(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{K})\in\mathcal{P}_{U}^{K}
and
(P_{1},\ldots,P_{L})\in\mathcal{P}_{U}^{L}
are given tuples of instantaneous SINR and per-transmitter power constraints. Hence, following similar arguments as above, we obtain that the main conclusions of Section
IV
and Section
V-B
can be extended to the case of per-transmitter power constraints. We omit the details due to space limitation.

VI
Applications

In this section we illustrate the application of our main results to common multi-cell and cell-free massive MIMO system models. In particular, after reviewing the canonical channel and pilot signaling model from the related literature, we show how to evaluate the solution to the MMSE joint channel estimation and beamforming problem (
8
) in Section
IV
under different practical information constraints. We recall that, as discussed in Section
III
and Section
V
, this problem is relevant for both uplink and downlink models. Furthermore, to be concrete, we demonstrate how this solution can be used to address uplink and downlink resource allocation problems that can be interpreted as joint beamforming, channel estimation, and power control problems.

VI-A

Channel and pilot signaling model

We consider a multi-user MIMO network composed by
L
access points, each of them equipped with
N
antennas. We let the channel between each access point
l\in\mathcal{L}
and user
k\in\mathcal{K}
be distributed as
\bm{h}_{l,k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\bm{0},\bm{R}_{l,k})
for some spatial covariance matrix
\bm{R}_{l,k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
, according to the spatially correlated Rayleigh fading model
[
12
, Chapter 2]

[
14
, Chapter 2]
. Motivated by their geographical separation, we further assume that the channels of all user and access point pairs are mutually independent
[
14
, Chapter 2]
.

Remark 12
.

All the insights in this section can be readily extended to non-zero mean channels, e.g., to the spatially correlated Rician fading model for line-of-sight propagation
[
30
,
26
]
. The details are omitted, as they do not provide significantly new insights and result in cumbersome expressions.

Following the usual uplink pilot signaling model in
[
12
, Chapter 3]

[
14
, Chapter 4]
, which is essentially the uplink channel model in (
2

l\in\mathcal{L}
obtains
\tau_{p}
noisy channel measurements of the type

\bm{Y}_{l}^{\mathsf{pilot}}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\sqrt{p_{k}^{\mathsf{pilot}}}\bm{h}_{l,k}\bm{\phi}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}+\bm{Z}^{\mathsf{pilot}}_{l},

(25)

where
\bm{Z}^{\mathsf{pilot}}_{l}
is a

additive white Gaussian matrix with
\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
i.i.d. entries, and where
\bm{\phi}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{\tau_{p}\times 1}
is a known pilot sequence sent by user
k\in\mathcal{K}
with norm
\|\bm{\phi}_{k}\|^{2}=\tau_{p}
and associated power control coefficient
p^{\mathsf{pilot}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. As customary, we assume that
\bm{\phi}_{k}
is selected from the columns of a codebook of
\tau_{p}
mutually orthogonal pilots
\bm{\Phi}\in\mathbb{C}^{\tau_{p}\times\tau_{p}}

\tau_{p}
is typically much smaller than the coherence block length
\tau_{c}
(i.e., the number of consecutive uplink channel uses in time and frequency over which the fading state can be assumed constant) and of the total number of users
K

k\in\mathcal{K}
, we denote by
\mathcal{K}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
the subset of users sharing the same pilot as user
k
. According to the original massive MIMO paradigm, the above measurements are used for both uplink and downlink beamforming, leveraging the channel reciprocity property of time-division duplex systems.

To formulate the joint channel estimation and beamforming optimization problem (
11
), we need to specify the underlying information constraints, i.e., the considered CSI
S_{k}=(S_{1,k},\ldots,S_{L,k})
and the clusters
\mathcal{L}_{k}
, as well as the power vector
\bm{p}
. In general, we assume that the CSI of each access point
l\in\mathcal{L}
is composed by at least the locally received uplink pilot signals
\bm{Y}_{l}^{\mathsf{pilot}}
in (
25
), and potentially by additional measurements obtained by sharing these signals over the fronthaul according to some specified rule. The cardinality of
\mathcal{L}_{k}
is then used to specify whether we consider a multi-cell or a cell-free system model. Several relevant examples are detailed in the following.

VI-B

Multi-cell massive MIMO

In this section we focus on multi-cell massive MIMO networks, following closely the definition in
[
12

and other variations. For all
k\in\mathcal{K}

VII
Numerical examples

To further illustrate the practical relevance of our main results, this section presents a numerical evaluation of various sub-6 GHz wideband system deployments, including small-cell and cell-free architectures with different levels of cooperation, as described in Section
VI
. A key feature of these numerical results is that, in many cases, we can guarantee that no alternative beamforming or channel estimation scheme, including joint schemes, can achieve better performance. We will restate the specific assumptions under which this property holds alongside each example. We emphasize that the focus of this section is primarily methodological, rather than to provide an exhaustive performance comparison of practical systems.

VIII
Conclusion

We have established that, under broad and practically relevant conditions, joint channel estimation and beamforming in massive MIMO systems can be optimally separated into MMSE estimation followed by MMSE beamforming. These conditions encompass both centralized and distributed architectures and align with standard system models and ergodic rate bounds in the literature. The results are particularly strong for the UatF/hardening bound and extend partially to the coherent decoding lower bound under additional assumptions regarding the availability of common beamforming CSI. This separation principle enables complete solutions to specific problems (e.g., max-min resource allocation) and reveals strong structural properties for more challenging problems (e.g., sum-rate maximization), which are reduced to the tuning of power control coefficients.

Our findings support the continued use of modular channel estimation and beamforming designs in systems where the considered models accurately reflects reality. Although illustrated under a block-fading measurement model, we remark that the results extend naturally to more explicit measurement models for wideband channel estimation and prediction (e.g., based on 5G sounding reference signals), provided that the measurements remain linear and Gaussian. In all such cases, joint design offers limited additional performance gains, and artificial intelligence techniques may be better directed at reducing the complexity of intractable resource allocation tasks, such as finding sum-rate optimal power control coefficients.

However, in scenarios involving non-linearities, non-Gaussian effects, unknown channel statistics, or alternative performance metrics (e.g., outage capacity or block error rate), the separation principle may no longer apply. In these cases, joint designs, possibly driven by artificial intelligence methods, may still offer significant performance gains.

Obinutuzumab β for aquaporin-4-positive neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder: a phase 3 randomized controlled trial
Authors: Lei Wu, Liangliang Gu, Weihao Fan, Min Wei, Tingjun Dai, Xiaoling Li, Lianchen Xiao, Bin Li, Dai-Shi Tian, Ruisheng Duan, Xiaofeng Li, Yuming Xu, Huaxing Meng, Ting Chen, Feng Gao, Feipeng Zhai, Juan Feng, Huan Yang, Runxiu Zhu, Cun-Jin Zhang, Qiang Meng, Bingmei Deng, Zhaoyou Meng, Feng Qiu, Hongyu Zhou, Wei Qiu, Qing Zhang, Hong Liu, Zhe Wu, Zhe Wang, Li Meng, Zunbo Li, Weizhou Zang, Feng Xiang, Jinjin Liang, Sijun Liu, Xifang Liu, Junbin Chen, Feng Li, Dehui Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Although CD20-directed B cell depletion has long been used in neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD), high-quality, large-scale, randomized controlled trials remain limited. In this multicenter, randomized, double-blind phase 3 trial, we evaluated obinutuzumab β (MIL62), a novel glycoengineered type II anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody, in patients with NMOSD. Eligible participants aged 18−70 years with aquaporin-4-immunoglobulin G (AQP4-IgG)-seropositive NMOSD and an Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) score of 7.0 or lower were randomly assigned (1:1) to receive intravenous obinutuzumab β 1,000 mg (n = 45) or placebo (n = 46). The primary outcome—time to first adjudicated relapse on or before week 52—was met: relapse occurred in two of 45 (4.4%) obinutuzumab β-treated participants and in 21 of 46 (45.7%) placebo-treated participants (hazard ratio = 0.069, 95% confidence interval: 0.016−0.296, P < 0.0001). The incidence of grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events was similar between the obinutuzumab β group (6.7%) and the placebo group (6.5%). These findings support obinutuzumab β as a glycoengineered type II anti-CD20 therapeutic option for patients with AQP4-IgG+ NMOSD. ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05314010.

Main

Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) is a rare, severe, relapsing autoimmune disorder of the central nervous system characterized by episodes of optic neuritis, transverse myelitis and brain or brainstem syndromes1,2. The pathogenesis of NMOSD is primarily mediated by humoral immune mechanisms, and aquaporin-4-immunoglobulin G (AQP4-IgG) is the key pathogenic autoantibody that mediates lesion formation3. Approximately 70−80% of patients are seropositive for AQP4-IgG autoantibodies4,5. More than 90% of patients follow a relapsing disease course, frequently resulting in cumulative neurologic disability, with historically reported untreated mortality rates ranging from 25% to 50%6. Accordingly, early diagnosis and initiation of maintenance therapy are essential to improve long-term outcomes. Given that disability burden primarily arises from the accumulation of irreversible neurological impairments following recurrent attacks, effective maintenance strategies to prevent relapse are central to long-term disease management.

Current treatment strategies for AQP4-IgG+ NMOSD have been markedly transformed by the introduction of biologic therapies targeting humoral immunity, including complement inhibitors, IL-6 receptor monoclonal antibodies and CD19/CD20-targeted agents7–14. B cell depletion targeting CD20 has emerged as an important therapeutic strategy in NMOSD and has considerable clinical benefit13,15. However, most of the available evidence is derived from off-label use, retrospective or open-label cohorts and a single small randomized trial conducted in Japan13,16–18. Practical challenges have also been reported with first-generation CD20-directed treatment, including early disease activity after treatment initiation and relapses associated with delayed retreatment or B cell reconstitution19–21. These challenges highlight unmet clinical needs in real-world clinical practice, including heterogeneity in treatment regimens and monitoring strategies, variability in treatment responses, concerns regarding long-term safety outcomes and limited longitudinal data on dynamic changes in AQP4-IgG levels.

Obinutuzumab β (development code: MIL62) is a humanized, novel glycoengineered type II anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody that shares an identical amino acid sequence with obinutuzumab. Its distinguishing structural feature is near-complete Fc afucosylation, a modification that further enhances antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) activity compared to both rituximab and obinutuzumab22. Furthermore, the type II anti-CD20 design promotes direct, non-apoptotic B cell death, supporting deeper and more sustained B cell depletion in peripheral blood and lymphoid tissues22–26. In our phase 1b study, treatment with obinutuzumab β resulted in rapid and sustained B cell depletion, demonstrating preliminary efficacy and an acceptable safety profile in 11 patients with NMOSD27. On the basis of these findings, we conducted the BeInmost trial—a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate obinutuzumab β as a type II anti-CD20 B cell depletion therapy in patients with AQP4-IgG+ NMOSD. To our knowledge, this represents the first randomized controlled trial evaluating a newer-generation type II glycoengineered anti-CD20 antibody in this population.

Results

Patient disposition

Between 28 August 2023 and 5 January 2025, a total of 123 patients were screened, of whom 91 underwent randomization. Forty-five participants were assigned to receive obinutuzumab β and 46 to placebo. All randomized participants received at least one dose of the assigned intervention and were included in both the full analysis set and the safety analysis set of the randomized controlled period (RCP; Fig. 1). The data cutoff for the prespecified event-driven primary endpoint analysis of the RCP was 21 February 2025. Among participants assigned to obinutuzumab β, 21 (46.7%) received two infusions, three (6.7%) received three infusions and 21 (46.7%) received four infusions in the RCP. The median duration of protocol-specified corticosteroid bridge therapy was 8.0 weeks (range, 3.86−8.86) in the obinutuzumab β group and 8.0 weeks (range, 3.14−14.00) in the placebo group. The mean relative dose intensity of glucocorticoids was higher in the placebo group than in the obinutuzumab β group (102.1 ± 14.82% versus 96.1 ± 13.15%).

Primary outcome

The primary endpoint—time to first adjudicated relapse of NMOSD on or before week 52—occurred in two of 45 participants (4.4%) in the obinutuzumab β group and in 21 of 46 participants (45.7%) in the placebo group. The details of adjudicated relapse events are provided in Extended Data Table 1. Treatment with obinutuzumab β resulted in a 93.1% relative reduction in the risk of adjudicated relapse compared to placebo (hazard ratio = 0.069, 95% CI: 0.016−0.296, log-rank P < 0.0001) (Fig. 2a). The estimated 52-week relapse-free rate was 94.80% (95% CI: 80.61−98.69) in the obinutuzumab β group and 28.40% (95% CI: 10.00−50.25) in the placebo group. Secondary outcomes

The adjudicated annualized relapse rate was significantly lower in the obinutuzumab β group than in the placebo group (0.092 (95% CI: 0.022−0.382) versus 1.180 (95% CI: 0.677−2.058); rate ratio = 0.078, 95% CI: 0.018−0.333, P = 0.0006). Hospitalization due to NMOSD relapse occurred in two of 45 participants (4.4%) in the obinutuzumab β group compared to 21 of 46 participants (45.7%) in the placebo group (P < 0.0001).

At the last visit, the EDSS score decreased from baseline in the obinutuzumab β group (least squares mean (LSM) change, −0.37; 95% CI: −0.68 to −0.05), whereas an increase was observed in the placebo group (LSM change, 0.35; 95% CI: 0.03−0.66), resulting in a between-group LSM difference of −0.72 (95% CI: −1.16 to −0.27, P = 0.0021). The proportion of participants with EDSS deterioration at the last visit was 8.4% in the obinutuzumab β group and 22.9% in the placebo group, representing a numerical reduction (rate ratio = 0.367, 95% CI: 0.126−1.071, P = 0.0666). The cumulative number of active magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) lesions was significantly lower in the obinutuzumab β group than in the placebo group (0.02 ± 0.149 versus 0.54 ± 0.959, P = 0.0003). The annualized active lesion rate was 0.048 (95% CI: 0.007−0.357) in the obinutuzumab β group and 1.451 (95% CI: 0.820−2.566) in the placebo group (rate ratio = 0.033, 95% CI: 0.004−0.254, P = 0.0010). Safety

Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were reported in 95.6% (43/45) of participants receiving obinutuzumab β and in 95.7% (44/46) of those receiving placebo during the RCP. The most common TEAEs in each group are summarized in Extended Data Table 2. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) occurred in 66.7% of participants in the obinutuzumab β group and in 45.7% of participants in the placebo group. In the obinutuzumab β group, the most frequently reported TRAEs were infusion-related reactions (22.2%), alanine aminotransferase increased (13.3%), lymphocyte count decreased (11.1%) and blood lactate dehydrogenase increased (11.1%). In the placebo group, the most common TRAEs were urinary tract infection (13.0%), white blood cell count decreased (8.7%) and abnormal hepatic function (8.7%) (Table 3). Grade 3 or higher TRAEs occurred in 6.7% of participants receiving obinutuzumab β and in 6.5% of participants receiving placebo. In the obinutuzumab β group, grade 3 or higher TRAEs included pneumonia, platelet count decreased and eyelid swelling (each 2.2%). Adverse events of special interest related to obinutuzumab β were reported in 24.4% of participants (11/45), including infusion-related reactions (22.2%, all grade 1 or 2), one case of grade 3 or higher pneumonia (2.2%) and one case of grade 3 or higher thrombocytopenia (2.2%). One participant in the obinutuzumab β group died due to multiple organ dysfunction syndrome. This 50-year-old woman with a 20-year history of severe NMOSD (baseline EDSS 6.5) received two doses of obinutuzumab β before experiencing a relapse treated with high-dose corticosteroid pulse therapy. After the relapse, she developed a fungal urinary tract infection that, exacerbated by treatment noncompliance, disseminated systemically and intracranially. This was accompanied by thromboembolic events, gastrointestinal bleeding and multiple cerebral infarctions, resulting in a fatal outcome due to multiple organ failure. The event was assessed by the investigator as unrelated to the study drug under blinded conditions and was attributed, instead, to advanced disease, high-dose corticosteroid exposure and noncompliance.

Exploratory outcomes

At the last visit, the LSM change in modified Rankin Scale score from baseline was −0.15 (95% CI: −0.32 to 0.01) in the obinutuzumab β group, whereas the placebo group demonstrated worsening, with an LSM change of 0.21 (95% CI: 0.05−0.38), resulting in a between-group difference of −0.37 (95% CI: −0.60 to −0.14, P = 0.0021). Similarly, the EuroQol five-dimension (EQ-5D) visual analog scale (VAS) score remained stable in the obinutuzumab β group (LSM change, 0.02; 95% CI: −4.49 to 4.53) but declined in the placebo group (LSM change, −9.35; 95% CI: −13.81 to −4.89), yielding a between-group difference of 9.37 (95% CI: 3.03−15.72, P = 0.0042). Logistic regression analysis showed no significant association between baseline AQP4-IgG level and relapse risk (odds ratio = 0.999, 95% CI: 0.995−1.004, P = 0.7965). Sensitivity and post hoc analyses

Sensitivity analyses and supplementary analyses of the primary outcome demonstrated results consistent with the primary analysis, with reduced risk of adjudicated relapse in the obinutuzumab β group compared to the placebo group (Extended Data Table 4).

After exclusion of the three adjudicated relapse events in the placebo group identified according to symptom-based criteria, obinutuzumab β remained associated with a 92.0% relative reduction in the risk of adjudicated relapse compared to placebo (hazard ratio = 0.080, 95% CI: 0.019−0.343, log-rank P = 0.0007).

Open-label period outcomes

As of 14 May 2026, 90 participants received at least one dose of obinutuzumab β across the RCP and the open-label period (OLP) (45 originally assigned and 45 after crossover from placebo). A total of 85 participants entered the OLP follow-up, comprising 40 from the original obinutuzumab β group and 45 from the placebo crossover group. For long-term efficacy and durability summaries, analyses were conducted in all obinutuzumab β-exposed participants from the time of first obinutuzumab β administration. For OLP safety summaries, analyses were conducted in the 85 participants who entered OLP follow-up. For all obinutuzumab β-exposed participants, the median follow-up duration from first obinutuzumab β administration to the updated data cutoff was 65.57 weeks (95% CI: 57.57−67.14).

Among the 90 obinutuzumab β-exposed participants, investigator-assessed first clinical relapse events occurred in two cases (2.2%), both during the RCP, with no protocol-defined new OLP relapse observed. The median time to first clinical relapse was not reached; compared to the RCP placebo group, the hazard ratio was 0.021 (95% CI: 0.005−0.093), corresponding to a 97.9% reduction in relapse risk. The annualized relapse rate was 0.025 (95% CI: 0.005−0.139), and the proportion of participants hospitalized for NMOSD relapse was 2.2%. At the last OLP visit, the mean change in EDSS score from baseline was −0.43 (s.d. 0.89), and 3.3% of participants had experienced EDSS deterioration. BCVA remained stable throughout the OLP. Overall, OLP follow-up confirmed sustained efficacy. Longitudinal assessment of serum AQP4-IgG demonstrated a sustained and significant reduction after obinutuzumab β treatment (Extended Data Fig. 1b), with titers declining from a mean of 54.3 U ml−1 at week 1 to 13.9 U ml−1 by week 79 (P = 0.0156).

In the OLP safety set of 85 participants, both TEAEs and TRAEs showed numerical decreases compared to those observed in the obinutuzumab β group during the RCP. The incidence of grade 3 or higher TEAEs and serious adverse events was similar to that in the RCP obinutuzumab β group (Extended Data Table 5), as were the categories of grade 3 or higher TRAEs; no unexpected safety signals were identified (Supplementary Table 2). At the week 52 OLP visit, mean (s.d.) IgG levels were 9.4 (2.6) g l−1, representing a mean (s.d.) change from baseline of −1.6 (2.1) g l−1. Mean IgG levels remained above 7.0 g l−1 throughout the RCP and OLP (Extended Data Fig. 3). Notably, these reductions remained within a clinically acceptable range, and no clear association was observed between decreased immunoglobulin levels and the incidence of serious infections (Extended Data Table 6). An exposure-adjusted analysis of infection-related adverse events further contextualized long-term safety (Extended Data Fig. 4). Among participants who received obinutuzumab β, the exposure-adjusted annualized incidence rate of infection-related TEAEs showed no evidence of cumulative increase over time; notably, too, a marked decline was observed after 52 weeks of treatment (50.5 per 100 person-years from week 53 onward versus 84.7 per 100 person-years during the overall treatment period). These rates were significantly lower than those observed in the placebo group during the RCP (149.5 versus 84.7 per 100 person-years, P = 0.0228). Grade 3 or higher infection-related TEAEs were infrequent, with exposure-adjusted annualized rates of 17.2 per 100 person-years overall and 3.4 per 100 person-years after week 53. Overall, OLP follow-up did not identify new unexpected safety signals or a pattern suggesting cumulative toxicity relative to the RCP safety profile.

Discussion

To our knowledge, the present trial provides the first pivotal randomized phase 3 evidence supporting the use of obinutuzumab β (MIL62), a glycoengineered type II anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody, in patients with AQP4-IgG+ NMOSD. The main finding showed that the therapy met the prespecified primary endpoint, with relapse occurring in two of 45 patients (4.4%) in the obinutuzumab β group compared to 21 of 46 patients (45.7%) in the placebo group during the RCP, corresponding to a 93.1% reduction in relapse risk. Multiple prespecified secondary endpoints, including defined annualized relapse rate, NMOSD-related hospitalization, EDSS score and active MRI lesion outcomes, also consistently support the efficacy of obinutuzumab β in the clinical and radiological field. The safety profile was generally manageable, and the incidence of grade 3 or higher TRAEs was similar to that in the placebo group. After the OLP, follow-up from the first exposure to obinutuzumab β was extended to a median of 65.57 weeks. No protocol-defined relapses were observed during this extended observation period, and the annualized relapse rate was further reduced to 0.025, providing additional evidence for the prevention of disease progression with obinutuzumab β treatment.

Although CD20-targeted therapy can induce rapid and sustained B cell depletion and has shown substantial efficacy in preventing NMOSD relapses, several limitations remain from both pathophysiological and mechanistic perspectives. On the one hand, B cells are involved in NMOSD pathogenesis through multiple mechanisms, including antigen presentation, cytokine secretion, interactions with T cells and the generation of pathogenic autoantibodies28,29. However, terminally differentiated plasma cells do not express CD20, and monoclonal antibodies have limited penetration across the blood−brain barrier; therefore, CD20-targeting therapies cannot directly eliminate pathogenic AQP4-IgG30–32. On the other hand, rapid B cell depletion may result in compensatory T cell activation and a transient increase in proinflammatory cytokine levels, potentially contributing to early disease relapse19–21. In our previous real-world study, approximately 74.8% of patients (110/147) remained relapse free after rituximab initiation; notably, 51.43% of relapse episodes (18/35) occurred after the first rituximab infusion, and 88.6% occurred within the first year of treatment, suggesting that relapse risk is highest during the early phase after CD20-targeted therapy21. Even with inebilizumab, a CD19-targeted treatment, which can deplete a broader B cell lineage including plasmablasts and some plasma cells, relapses were still observed during the early phase of treatment. In the N-MOmentum trial, 79% of treated participants remained attack free, and 63% of attacks occurred during the first year of treatment33.

By contrast, the present study demonstrated a significantly low relapse rate during the first year of treatment. This finding may be attributable, at least in part, to several features of the study design. First, as a glycoengineered type II anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody, obinutuzumab β has an afucosylated Fc region designed to enhance Fcγ receptor binding and ADCC, and its type II properties promote direct cell death22–24. These pharmacological features may support rapid and profound depletion of peripheral CD19+ B cells and CD19+CD27+ memory B cells22–26. Second, an optimized dosing regimen was used. Obinutuzumab β was administered according to an intensified first-year dosing schedule, with four fixed administrations. In addition, all participants received protocol-mandated glucocorticoid bridging therapy, consisting of prednisone 20 mg per day or equivalent regimen for 4 weeks, followed by tapering for up to four additional weeks. Compared to previous trials, this design incorporated a fixed-course, fixed-dose glucocorticoid bridging regimen in both groups to minimize short-term relapse risk while allowing a clearer assessment of relapse risk in the placebo group during glucocorticoid tapering to discontinuation between weeks 4 and 8. Although the present study cannot distinguish the independent contributions of obinutuzumab β itself, glucocorticoid bridging and first-year dosing, the overall therapeutic strategy was associated with several consistent positive findings: early separation of the relapse curves, peripheral B cell depletion and durable control of disease activity as well as the absence of protocol-defined relapses during the updated OLP.

Decreased AQP4-IgG levels in patients with obinutuzumab β may provide indirect evidence for treatment efficacy. We observed that obinutuzumab β treatment was generally associated with a reduction in AQP4-IgG level, consistent with the cohort study showing that AQP4-IgG level decline or seroreversion was associated with a lower risk of relapse for long-term rituximab treatment34. Notably, post-relapse increases in AQP4-IgG levels were mainly observed in placebo-treated patients, whereas no clear increasing trend was observed in the two relapsing patients receiving obinutuzumab β. Because the number of relapse events in the randomized period was limited, these findings should be considered preliminary. Further analyses with more complete longitudinal samples and additional relapse events during the open-label extension are needed to investigate this relationship34.

Regarding the safety profile, although the relatively short duration of the phase 3 RCP limits the assessment of long-term safety, the updated open-label data provide additional supportive safety evidence. The safety profile of obinutuzumab β was manageable and consistent with expectations for a B-cell-depleting therapy15,25,35,36. The most common TRAEs were infusion-related reactions and hematologic abnormalities, predominantly grade 1−2 and resolving with standard management. Despite theoretical concerns regarding infection risk with B cell depletion, no increased incidence of serious infections was observed. Notably, an exposure-adjusted analysis demonstrated that infection-related TEAE rates declined after 52 weeks of treatment (50.5 per 100 person-years from week 53 onward versus 84.7 per 100 person-years overall) and were similar to placebo RCP rates. Furthermore, no consistent association was observed between IgG decrease and infectious events in the OLP. These data do not indicate emerging cumulative infectious toxicity with extended obinutuzumab β exposure. Moreover, under regulatory review, obinutuzumab β was granted priority review by the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) in May 2025 and was approved in February 2026, suggesting that its benefit−risk profile was considered acceptable based on the available evidence.

This study also has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, the trial was conducted exclusively in China, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to more diverse global populations. In addition, variability across study centers may have introduced potential heterogeneity. Although stratified randomization by center could theoretically reduce such variability, its implementation is often impractical in rare disease settings because of enrollment constraints. Second, the trial enrolled only patients with AQP4-IgG-seropositive NMOSD; the efficacy of obinutuzumab β in seronegative NMOSD, which is often more therapeutically challenging, remains to be established. Third, the placebo-controlled design precludes direct comparison with other approved therapies, such as immunosuppressants or alternative monoclonal antibodies. However, this design reflects the current therapeutic landscape in China, where access to biologic agents remains limited and immunosuppressants are frequently used off-label. The placebo-controlled approach was reviewed and approved by the NMPA on this basis. Finally, although the extent of censoring reflects the relatively short duration of follow-up, the number of observed events exceeded the prespecified hypothesis-driven target, supporting the robustness of the primary findings. More mature data from the ongoing OLP, including annualized relapse rates, will be critical to further define the long-term benefit of obinutuzumab β.

In summary, obinutuzumab β significantly reduced relapse risk compared to placebo in patients with AQP4-IgG+ NMOSD and demonstrated a favorable safety profile. Updated OLP follow-up through May 2026 further supports the durability of disease control and has not revealed new safety signals to date. These findings underscore obinutuzumab β as a clinically meaningful glycoengineered type II anti-CD20 therapeutic option for relapse prevention in AQP4-IgG+ NMOSD while emphasizing the need for continued long-term follow-up.

Methods

Study design

The BeInmost trial was a phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study followed by an open-label extension period, which was conducted at 32 centers across China. The trial was performed in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines of the International Council for Harmonization. The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board of the Chinese PLA General Hospital and by the ethics committees at all participating centers. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. Participants received reimbursement for transportation expenses and compensation for blood collection. For participants who experienced relapse during the RCP, costs associated with rescue therapy were covered as specified in the informed consent form. The trial is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05314010). The present study describes outcomes from the RCP data and updated OLP data follow-up through 14 May 2026.

Participants

Eligible participants were adults aged 18−70 years with a diagnosis of AQP4-IgG+ NMOSD according to the 2015 International Panel for NMO Diagnosis criteria37, confirmed by centralized testing using a commercially available cell-based assay (CBA) kit (Guangzhou Weimi Biotech Co., Ltd.). Participants were required to have an EDSS score of 7.0 or lower at screening and a history of either at least one relapse requiring rescue therapy within 12 months before screening or at least two relapses within the previous 24 months, including the first attack. Participants who had experienced a relapse prior to screening were required to have stable or improving neurologic symptoms for at least 4 weeks before receiving the first dose of study treatment. Concomitant corticosteroid therapy at a dose of ≤20 mg per day of prednisone equivalent within 14 days before the first dose of study drug was permitted. Exclusion criteria included inadequate organ function; prior exposure to B-cell-depleting therapies (for example, rituximab, inebilizumab, ozanimod or telitacicept) within 6 months before screening, unless CD19+ or CD20+ B cell counts exceeded the lower limit of normal range; treatment with immunosuppressants other than corticosteroids within 1 month before the first dose of study drug; treatment with plasma exchange, moderate-volume blood transfusion or other immunomodulatory therapies within 28 days before the first dose of study drug; CD4+ T cell count below 200 cells per microliter; or prior treatment within 3 months with non-B-cell-targeting biologic agents (for example, tocilizumab, satralizumab, eculizumab or efgartigimod), mitoxantrone or alkylating agents (for example, cyclophosphamide) for NMOSD. Additional exclusion criteria included receipt of live or attenuated vaccines or other investigational agents within 28 days before the first dose; prior bone marrow transplantation, hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, total lymphoid irradiation or T cell vaccination; coexisting chronic active autoimmune disease, malignancy or severe psychiatric, respiratory or cardiovascular disorders; known allergy to the study drug; pregnancy or lactation; or any other condition deemed unsuitable by the investigators. A complete list of exclusion criteria and definitions is provided in the study protocol. Sex data were obtained from participants’ identification documents.

Randomization and masking

Eligible participants were stratified according to baseline EDSS score (≤2.0 versus 2.5−7.0) and randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive obinutuzumab β or placebo using an Interactive Web Response System (Taimei eBalance) with a block size of 4. Participants, investigators, the sponsor, members of the Clinical Endpoint Committee (CEC) and all study personnel remained masked to treatment allocation throughout the double-blind period.

Procedure

Participants assigned to the obinutuzumab β group received 1,000 mg of intravenous obinutuzumab β on day 1 and day 15. Participants without an adjudicated relapse by week 24 received two additional infusions at week 25 (day 1) and week 27 (day 1). The placebo group followed an identical infusion schedule. Dose modification was not permitted; however, administration could be delayed for up to 12 weeks in the event of adverse events, as specified in the protocol. To mitigate the risk of early relapse during the initial phase of CD20-targeted therapy13,19,20, all participants received concomitant glucocorticoid therapy for a total duration not exceeding 8 weeks. The regimen consisted of oral prednisone at a dose of 20 mg per day (or equivalent) for 4 weeks beginning on the day of the first infusion, followed by a taper over a maximum of 4 weeks starting at week 5. Rescue therapy for relapse, including intravenous methylprednisolone, plasma exchange or intravenous immunoglobulin, was administered at the investigator’s discretion. Glucocorticoid use was otherwise permitted only as rescue therapy or for the management of adverse events. Maintenance immunosuppressive therapies (for example, azathioprine, mycophenolate mofetil, rituximab, cyclosporine, methotrexate, mitoxantrone, cyclophosphamide, tocilizumab, eculizumab, ocrelizumab or other immunomodulatory agents) were prohibited during the treatment period.

Patients who completed the 52-week RCP without relapse entered the OLP within 14 days; those who experienced a relapse were allowed to enter the OLP after rescue therapy (see study protocol for details). In the OLP, all patients received intravenous obinutuzumab β on day 1 of weeks 1, 27, 53, 79, 105 and 131, whereas those initially assigned to the placebo group received one additional dose on day 1 of week 3.

During the RCP, on-site study visits were scheduled at weeks 1, 2, 3, 5, 9, 13, 17, 21, 25, 27, 29, 40 and 53. From week 8 onward, participants were contacted via biweekly telephone interviews to assess symptoms. If findings from study visits, telephone interviews or patient reports suggested a relapse, a protocol-defined in‑person evaluation was initiated, including neurological examination, EDSS assessment and MRI. All suspected relapses were first evaluated by the site investigators and subsequently adjudicated by an independent CEC, comprising three senior neurologists, all of whom are Chief Physicians. The CEC’s adjudication was based on a comprehensive review of clinical data and local imaging reports. In addition to relapse-triggered scans, MRI examinations were performed semiannually and comprised comprehensive neuraxial imaging of the optic nerves, spinal cord and brain. To objectively quantify the cumulative number of MRI active lesions (defined as new/enlarging T2 or gadolinium-enhancing T1 lesions), all images underwent a blinded independent central review (BICR) conducted by senior radiologists masked to clinical data and treatment assignment. During the OLP, on-site study visits were scheduled at weeks 1, 3, 5, 13, 27, 40, 53, 66, 79, 92, 105, 118, 131, 144 and 175. Participants were followed for safety assessments at 3 months and 6 months after the last dose of study drug. Blood samples were collected for analysis of lymphocyte subsets (CD19 and CD27) and for measurement of AQP4-IgG levels. Serum AQP4-IgG titers were determined using the EilsaRSR AQP4 Ab Version 2 ELISA kit (RSR Limited) at the central laboratory of United-Power Pharma Tech Co., Ltd. The ELISA methodology is described in detail in Supplementary Information.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was time to first adjudicated relapse of NMOSD on or before week 52. Relapses were confirmed by an independent CEC and were defined as new-onset neurologic symptoms or worsening of preexisting neurologic symptoms meeting at least one protocol-defined criterion: for participants with a baseline EDSS score greater than 0, an increase of at least 1.0 point; for those with a baseline EDSS score of 0, an increase of at least 2.0 points; an increase of at least 2.0 points in a single Functional Systems Score (FSS) domain; an increase of at least 1.0 point in two or more FSS domains with baseline values of 1.0 or greater; an increase of at least 1.0 point in the visual function FSS domain when the baseline score was at least 1.0; or the occurrence of isolated NMOSD-related symptoms (for example, nausea, vomiting or hiccups) not attributable to alternative diagnoses and accompanied by new or enlarging NMOSD-related lesions on MRI. The full criteria, which align with established precedent from the SAkuraStar and SAkuraSky trials10,11 and address the recognized limitations of exclusive reliance on EDSS-based assessment, are provided in the study protocol.

Secondary endpoints included change from baseline in EDSS score; the cumulative number of active MRI lesions (defined as the sum of new gadolinium-enhancing lesions and new or enlarging T2 lesions relative to baseline); BCVA; annualized relapse rate; and the proportion of participants hospitalized due to NMOSD relapse. EDSS worsening was defined as an increase of 2.0 points from baseline for participants with a baseline score of 0, an increase of 1.0 point for those with a baseline score of 1.0 to 5.0 or an increase of 0.5 points for those with a baseline score of 5.5 or higher. Adverse events were graded according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0. Adverse events of special interest included infusion-related reactions, grade 3 or higher infections, grade 3 or higher neutropenia or thrombocytopenia and progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy.

Exploratory endpoints included changes from baseline in the EQ-5D score, Numeric Rating Scale score, modified Rankin Scale score, Nine-Hole Peg Test, Symbol Digit Modalities Test, timed 25-Foot Walk Test and serum AQP4-IgG titers. Peripheral B cell subsets, including CD19+ B cells, were assessed, and B cell depletion was defined as a peripheral CD19+ B cell count of fewer than five cells per microliter.

Statistical analysis

The sample size was determined on the basis of relapse rates reported in previous trials of rituximab13 and satralizumab (SAkuraStar10 and SAkuraSky11). Assuming a 52-week first adjudicated relapse rate of 35% in the placebo group and 10% in the obinutuzumab β group, a total of approximately 18 confirmed first relapse events would provide 84.79% power to detect a hazard ratio of 0.245 in favor of obinutuzumab β at a two-sided α level of 0.05 using a Cox proportional hazards model. With an anticipated enrollment period of 15 months, a follow-up duration of 12 months and a 1:1 randomization ratio, approximately 80 participants were required.

Efficacy analyses were primarily conducted in the full analysis set according to the intention-to-treat principle, which included all randomized participants who received at least one dose of the study drug. Safety analyses included all participants who received at least one dose and underwent at least one safety assessment, with additional safety analyses performed using data derived from patients across multiple autoimmune diseases and lymphomas.

The primary endpoint—time to first adjudicated relapse within 52 weeks—was compared between groups using a stratified log-rank test. A stratified Cox proportional hazards model was applied to estimate hazard ratios and corresponding 95% CIs. Between-group comparisons of EDSS worsening were performed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Cumulative numbers of active MRI lesions and relapse-related hospitalizations were analyzed using negative binomial regression to estimate rate ratios and corresponding CIs. Between-group differences in change from baseline in BCVA were assessed using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA), with reporting of LSM differences and 95% CIs. Changes in serum AQP4-IgG levels were summarized descriptively. Other exploratory endpoints were analyzed using ANCOVA, the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. The specific analytical methods for each endpoint are detailed in the study protocol and the statistical analysis plan. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS software, version 9.4 or higher (SAS Institute).

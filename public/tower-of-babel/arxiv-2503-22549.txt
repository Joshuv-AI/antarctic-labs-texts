Non-parametric and continuous extraction of amplitudes in electroweak penguin decays
Anja Beck, Michele Atzeni, Eluned Smith

Abstract
We introduce a novel approach to extract the decay-amplitudes in B → V ( → M 1 M 2 ) ℓ + ℓ − B\rightarrow V(\rightarrow M_{1}M_{2}){\ell^{+}}{\ell^{-}} processes, where V V represents a meson with either J = 0 J=0 (S-wave) or J = 1 J=1 (P-wave). This approach enables the decay-amplitudes across the dihadron and dilepton invariant-masses to be extracted from data in a model-independent and continuous way. To achieve this, the angular decay rate is expressed in a way that allows the application of the sPlot technique to likelihood fits of the decay angles. We illustrate the abilities of this method on simulated B 0 → K + ​ π − ​ μ + ​ μ − {{B}^{0}}\!\rightarrow{{K}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}{\mu^{+}}{\mu^{-}} data, containing both S- and P-wave contributions to the K + ​ π − {{K}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}} system. We provide the weight functions and show that they allow the extraction of the absolute value of the P-wave and S-wave transversity amplitudes as a function of the dihadron and dimuon invariant-masses in a continuous, unbiased, and statistically-powerful way, while only relying on the angular distribution. As a consequence, the extracted amplitude shapes are model-independent – up to the angular terms included in the fit – and can be directly compared to theoretical predictions. A measurement using this technique can improve the sensitivity to potential new physics effects in b → s ​ ℓ + ​ ℓ − {b}\!\rightarrow{s}\ell^{+}\ell^{-} transitions, as well as the understanding of hadronic form factors, particularly in the S-wave system.

In addition, the composition of the dihadron spectrum in

Often, the dihadron spectrum features a narrow vector-meson resonance (P-wave) on top of a broader structure where
M_{1},M_{2}

Higher partial waves are also possible.
In the case of
{{B}^{0}}\!\rightarrow{{K}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}{\mu^{+}}{\mu^{-}}

{{K}^{*0}}(\rightarrow{{K}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}})

While the
{{K}^{*0}}

This paper discusses a new approach for a model-independent continuos extraction of the S-wave dihadron lineshape as well as the variation of the P- and S-wave decay amplitudes across the dimuon invariant-mass squared.

In the following, we first provide a rearranged angular decay rate as well as corresponding weight functions that allow the extraction of the magnitudes squared of the decay amplitudes across the phase space.
Afterwards, we validate our method on both a large toy sample and a toy sample of realistic size.
The paper concludes with a discussion of the advantages and disadvantages of this method with respect to more conventional measurement techniques.

\end{split}

(1)

with the angular functions

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{1}^{P}({\theta_{h}},{\theta_{\ell}})&=\frac{9}{16}\frac{1}{2}\left(\cos^{2}{\theta_{\ell}}+1\right)\sin^{2}{\theta_{h}}\ ,\\
f_{0}^{P}({\theta_{h}},{\theta_{\ell}})&=\frac{9}{16}2\sin^{2}{\theta_{\ell}}\cos^{2}{\theta_{h}}\ ,\\

f_{\beta}({\theta_{h}},{\theta_{\ell}})&=\frac{3}{8}r_{\beta}\sin^{2}{\theta_{h}}+\frac{3}{4}(1-r_{\beta})\cos^{2}{\theta_{h}}\ .\end{split}

(2)

The coefficients represent combinations of the transversity amplitudes,

\displaystyle\begin{split}n_{1}^{P}&=\beta^{2}\left(\left|A_{\parallel}^{L}\right|{}^{2}+\left|A_{\perp}^{L}\right|{}^{2}+\left|A_{\parallel}^{R}\right|{}^{2}+\left|A_{\perp}^{R}\right|{}^{2}\right)\\

&\left.\qquad\qquad\qquad+8\Re\left[{A^{\prime}}_{0}^{L}({A^{\prime}}_{0}^{R}){}^{*}\right]+4\left|{A^{\prime}}_{t}\right|{}^{2}\right)+\frac{(1-\beta^{2})}{4\beta^{2}}(3n_{1}^{P}+2n_{0}^{S})\\
n_{\beta}(1-r_{\beta})&=\frac{(1-\beta^{2})}{4}\left(2\Re\left[{A^{\prime}}_{0}^{L}({A^{\prime}}_{0}^{R}){}^{*}\right]+6\Re\left[A_{0}^{L}(A_{0}^{R}){}^{*}\right]\right.\\
&\left.\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left|{A^{\prime}}_{t}\right|{}^{2}+3\left|A_{S}\right|{}^{2}+3\left|A_{t}\right|{}^{2}\right)+\frac{(1-\beta^{2})}{4\beta^{2}}\left(3n_{0}^{P}+n_{0}^{S}\right)\\
\end{split}

(3)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{hc}&=\frac{3}{8}\sqrt{3}\left(1-\beta^{2}\right)\Re\left[\left(A_{0}^{R}\right){}^{*}{A^{\prime}}_{0}^{L}+\left(A_{0}^{L}\right){}^{*}{A^{\prime}}_{0}^{R}+\left(A_{t}\right){}^{*}{A^{\prime}}_{t}+\left(A_{0}^{L}\right){}^{*}{A^{\prime}}_{0}^{L}+\left(A_{0}^{R}\right){}^{*}{A^{\prime}}_{0}^{R}\right]\\

a_{\ell c}&=\frac{9}{16}\beta\left(1-\beta^{2}\right)\Re\left[\left(A_{S}\right){}^{*}\left(A_{0}^{L}+A_{0}^{R}\right)\right]\\
a_{\ell s}&=\frac{9}{8}\beta\Re\left[A_{\parallel}^{L}\left(A_{\perp}^{L}\right){}^{*}-A_{\parallel}^{R}\left(A_{\perp}^{R}\right){}^{*}\right]\end{split}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\text{total}}}\int\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma}{\mathrm{d}\cos{\theta_{h}}\mathrm{d}\cos{\theta_{\ell}}}~\mathrm{d}\cos{\theta_{h}}~\mathrm{d}\cos{\theta_{\ell}}=n^{P}_{0}+n^{P}_{1}+n^{S}_{0}+n_{\beta}=1\ .

The decay rate in Eq. (
1
) is expressed using an unconventional angular basis where the four coefficients

This allows for the construction of a set of weight functions,

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{1}^{P}({\theta_{h}},{\theta_{\ell}})&=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(5\cos^{2}{\theta_{h}}-3\right)+\frac{r_{\beta}}{1-r_{\beta}}\left(5\cos^{2}{\theta_{h}}-1\right)\right)\left(5\cos^{2}{\theta_{\ell}}-1\right)\ ,\\

w_{0}^{S}({\theta_{h}},{\theta_{\ell}})&=\frac{3}{4}\left(5\cos^{2}{\theta_{h}}-3\right)\left(5\cos^{2}{\theta_{\ell}}-2\right)-\frac{3r_{\beta}}{8(1-r_{\beta})}\left(5\cos^{2}{\theta_{h}}-1\right)\left(5\cos^{2}{\theta_{\ell}}-1\right)\ ,\\
w_{\beta}({\theta_{h}},{\theta_{\ell}})&=\frac{3}{4(1-r_{\beta})}\left(5\cos^{2}{\theta_{h}}-1\right)\left(5\cos^{2}{\theta_{\ell}}-1\right)\ .\end{split}

(6)

Each weight function enables the extraction of the shape of the corresponding coefficient from data.
This method is known as the
sPlot

38
]

It is a commonly used tool in high-energy physics and is almost exclusively employed to separate signal from background using the final-state invariant-mass as a discriminator.
The non-traditional angular basis in Eq. (
1

sPlot
technique using the decay angles as the discriminant variables in order to extract the shape of the magnitude of the transverse and longitudinal P-wave amplitudes, via

, and a more complex combination of amplitudes suppressed by

]

The Wilson coefficients are set to a SM prediction obtained using the

Given the nature of the studies presented here, a result cannot easily be listed as numbers or an analytic expression.
The results of a measurement using this technique may be represented as histograms.
This scenario is likely the easiest to implement as the uncertainties are clearly defined.
However, an alternative way of representing the extracted distributions in an unbinned fashion is via individual unweighted data sets generated using dynamic nested sampling
[
44
]
as implemented for example in the
dynesty
package
[
45
]
.
Another option is to use express the distributions with normalising flows using the
nabu-hep
tool
[
46
]
designed for this purpose specifically.
Finally, the result may of course also be presented by publishing the data including the extracted weights if possible and useful.
The presentation of uncertainties in a continuous way across the phase space is particularly challenging.
The development and study of different ideas and methods is outside the scope of this paper and may be examined in future work.

As evidenced by Fig.
2
, the results of the
sPlot
technique when applied to the angular decay rate results in an unbiased extraction of the angular coefficients
n_{i}
.
Moreover as illustrated by Fig.
4
, the resulting statistical uncertainty is smaller compared to the method of moments and similar to per-bin likelihood fits.
However as a direct consequence of the correlations across the phase space, the scatter around the central value is reduced.
Additionally, the
sPlot
method proposed here has a major advantage over per-bin likelihood fits as the width of the bins in the latter is limited by the amount of data required to achieve a stable fit.
Results obtained using the presented technique would enable thorough studies of the
q^{2}
-dependence of the different amplitudes.
What is more, better knowledge of the S-wave dihadron lineshape can reduce systematic uncertainties present in experimental measurements and help improve the understanding on
B\rightarrow M_{1}M_{2}
form factors across the full dihadron invariant-mass range.

A drawback of our method is that the angular coefficients can be strongly correlated which can lead to difficulties in the likelihood fit.
This is particularly problematic for the
(1-\beta)
suppressed terms.
Transformations of the
(1-\beta)
dependent terms in the decay rate and the inclusion of a wider
q^{2}
range, as well as the addition of background and other realism, may be the focus of future investigations.
Finally as
A_{\perp}
and
A_{\parallel}
only appear together in Eq. (
1
), this method can only provide a combined shape for these two amplitudes.

In summary, a model-independent method to extract the dimuon and dihadron invariant-mass shapes of amplitudes appearing in
{{B}^{0}}\!\rightarrow{{K}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}{\mu^{+}}{\mu^{-}}
decays has been presented.
The dependence on the dimuon invariant-mass squared is particularly interesting for the study of the underlying
{b}\!\rightarrow{s}\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
structure and potential deviations from the Standard Model expectation.
The shapes of the contributions to the dihadron spectrum can be used to further reduce the systematic uncertainty on experimental measurements and provide better understanding of the QCD resonance spectrum.
Note that this method can be applied to any
B\rightarrow V(\rightarrow M_{1}M_{2}){\ell^{+}}{\ell^{-}}
decay and translated to other spin configurations after establishing an appropriate expression for the angular decay rate.

Q-learning improved golden jackal optimization algorithm and its application to reliability optimization of hydraulic system
Authors: Dongning Chen, Haowen Wang, Dongbo Hu, Qinggui Xian, Bingyu Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo endow the prey with intelligent movement behavior and improve the performance of Golden Jackal Optimization (GJO), a Q-learning Improved Gold Jackal Optimization (QIGJO) algorithm is proposed. This paper introduces five update mechanisms and proposes double-population Q-learning collaborative mechanism to select appropriate update mechanisms to improve GJO performance. Additionally, a new convergence factor is incorporated to enhance convergence capability of GJO. QIGJO demonstrates excellent performance across 23 benchmark functions, CEC2022, and three classical engineering design problems, indicating high convergence accuracy and significantly enhanced global exploration capability. The reliability optimization model of the hydraulic system for concrete pump trucks was established based on a Continuous-time Multi-dimensional T-S dynamic Fault Tree (CM-TSdFT), considering the two-dimensional factors of operating time and number of impacts. Utilizing QIGJO to optimize this model yielded excellent results, providing valuable methodological support for reliability optimization of hydraulic systems.Keywords: Golden jackal optimization, Reliability optimization, Q-Learning, Global optimizationSubject terms: Computer science, Mechanical engineering

With the fast-paced development of science and technology, optimization problems are encountered in many fields to find the optimal solution under various constraints. There are many studies conducted on this subject, with various optimization algorithms proposed, such as gradient descent method1, linear programming2, Branch-and-Bound Methods3 and so on. As optimization algorithms develop, neural networks and heuristic algorithms have been increasingly employed to address optimization problems. Scholars have utilized neural networks for optimization4. However, challenges such as constraint transformation and gradient explosion have hindered the widespread adoption of neural networks in the field of optimization. In recent years, many neural network models have been proposed and improved5–7, giving us reason to anticipate the development of neural networks specifically tailored for optimization problems. Heuristic algorithm8 provides a solution to solving problems, which is based on experience and enlightened rules to conduct search and make decisions. However, heuristic algorithms involve no random factor, so given an input then the execution steps of the algorithm will be fixed as a result and the output will be fixed as a result. To address this problem, meta-heuristic algorithms have emerged9. This algorithm involves a random factor10 that allows for variability in the results of multiple solutions to a problem, thus making the algorithm more responsive to the real-world circumstances.

With the rapid development of meta-heuristic optimization algorithms, there have been a variety of novel meta-heuristic optimization algorithms proposed, such as Gray Wolf Optimization (GWO) algorithm11, Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA)12, Harris Hawk Optimization (HHO) algorithm13, Fick’s Law Optimization (FLO) algorithm14, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm15, Horned Lizard Optimization Algorithm (HLOA)16, GOOSE Algorithm 17, Integrated Optimization Algorithm (IOA)18, Crayfish Optimization Algorithm (COA)19, Remora Optimization Algorithm (ROA)20, Coati Optimization Algorithm (COA)21 etc.

Proposed by Chopra and Ansari in 2022, Golden Jackal Optimization (GJO)22 is a novel meta-heuristic optimization algorithm. This algorithm performs well in local exploration, search efficiency and convergence speed, which makes it applicable for feature selection23, system identification24, engineering optimization25, and image processing26. In spite of this, there remain some limitations facing its search mechanism, such as the insufficient capability of global exploration, the susceptibility to the local optima, etc. Table 1 lists some advanced improved versions of the Golden Jackal Optimization (GJO) algorithm. These improvements mainly fall into two categories: hybridization with other optimization algorithms and the integration of various enhancement strategies. Hybridizing with other algorithms allows the GJO algorithm to inherit the strengths of these algorithms, but it may also introduce their weaknesses. On the other hand, incorporating enhancement strategies is relatively simple to implement and can effectively improve specific aspects of the algorithm’s performance. However, it may not lead to a comprehensive improvement in the overall performance of the algorithm. In the GJO algorithm, the individuals in the population are considered as prey, and their movement is driven by the pursuit of the jackals. However, prey should not only be driven by the jackals; they possess their own intelligent behavior and can have independent movement strategies as well. Therefore, this paper introduces five update strategies to simulate the movement behavior of the prey.

Q-learning27, which requires no environment model but learning through the interaction with the environment, is a type of reinforcement learning algorithm. In swarm intelligence optimization algorithms, Q-learning is incorporated to enhance the overall performance of the algorithm. For example, Alexandru28 conducted gravitational search to initialize the weights and biases of the neural networks involved in Q-learning, thus avoiding instability, also, Bedi et al.29 proposed Modified Grey Wolf Optimization with Q-Learning (MGWOQL). Rahul et al.30 used Q-learning and bat algorithm to optimize MANET. Almutairi et al.31 used a combination of Q-learning and gorilla troops optimization algorithms to classify breast cancer. When we use algorithms to solve practical problems, we often encounter some potential security issues. For example, Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) demonstrate strong performance, but even slight modifications to the input can lead to incorrect predictions32. Similarly, when addressing different problems, the multiple movement strategies proposed in this paper may be challenging to select correctly. Q-learning, however, can adapt to various strategies, thereby avoiding the negative impact of improper strategy selection on optimization results.

With the increasing sophistication and intelligence of various systems, reliability optimization issues have received increasing attention33. In reliability optimization problems, scholars hope to achieve high reliability at a relatively low cost. There are usually two ways to improve reliability. Firstly, when a given system is designed with higher reliability components for a given cost, the system usually has higher reliability as well. The other way is to increase redundancy in the system to improve its reliability34. Many scholars also choose swarm intelligence algorithms to optimize reliability issues. For example, Chen et al.35 established a reliability optimization model of a certain mechanical system based on the T-S fault tree and proposed a multi-strategy improved salp swarm optimization algorithm to optimize it. Utilizing fault trees to establish reliability models is considered an effective approach. Chen et al.36 proposed the Continuous-time multi-dimensional T-S dynamic fault tree (CM-TSdFT) analysis. The CM-TSdFT considers multiple factors affecting reliability and can describe the reliability at any time, showing obvious superiority.

This paper will optimize the hydraulic system of concrete pump trucks using the CM-TSdFT analysis method, establish a reliability optimization model for the system. Although GJO performs better in local exploration, its capability of global exploration is insufficient. The GJO algorithm considers only the driving behavior of the golden jackal towards the prey. When the prey’s behavior is taken into account, the performance of the algorithm is boosted. Based Q-Learning Improved Frame Golden Jackal Optimization algorithm (QIGJO) is proposed to improve the optimization performance of golden jackal optimization algorithm. Compared to other improved optimization algorithms, QIGJO goes beyond simply adding strategies or combining algorithms. It introduces the concept of prey performing intelligent movements, which aligns more closely with the actual hunting process of golden jackals. Building on this idea, five update strategies are proposed in this paper, using Q-learning to help the prey choose the appropriate update strategy for different optimization problems. Additionally, a Double-population Q-learning Collaborative Mechanism is introduced to further enhance optimization performance. QIGJO is used for system reliability optimization. Q-learning

Q-learning27, proposed by Watkins in 1992, is a form of reinforcement learning. As a model-free method of reinforcement learning, Q-learning involves the five elements of reinforcement learning as well: state space (s={s1, s2…sn}), action space (a={a1, a2…an}), the environment, the individual, and the reward. Rewards and punishments are applied to the individuals based on the feedback from the environment, which allows for the intelligent selection of actions based on the current state.

The process of Q-learning by a particular individual at the t-th iteration is expressed as follows: in a given state of st, the individual chooses an action at and then receives a reward from the environment accordingly. Through the iterative Q-learning formula, the Q-value (Q(st, at)) of the action at in the state st is computed. For each individual, the Q-table is used to create and store the Q-value of each individual in each iteration. It is expressed as follows:

where st represents the state of the t-th iteration, at indicates the action of the t-th iteration, rewardt+1 denotes the reward value, Qt(st, at) refers to the Q value of the at action performed in the state of st in the t-th iteration, Qt+1(st, at) represents the Q value of the at action performed in the state of st in the t+1-th iteration, and maxa(Q(st+1, at)) denotes the maximal Q value in the state of st+1. Also, 0<α<1 and 0<γ<1 are the learning rate and the discounting factor, respectively. The learning rate α is used to restrict the magnitude of each update, while the discounting factor γ indicates the importance of the future reward.

In the process of constant learning, the results of Q-learning training are stored in Q-tables, and the individuals make appropriate decisions intelligently based on the information presented in the Q-table.

Q-learning improved golden jackal optimization (QIGJO)

In this section, a Q-learning improved Golden Jackal Optimization (QIGJO) algorithm is proposed. Five update mechanisms are introduced to improve the GJO performance and are used as action and state for Q-learning. Q-learning is used to select the strategies based on the Q-table information and a double-population Q-learning collaborative mechanism is proposed to speed up the training efficiency. The convergence factor Ec is proposed to replace the escape energy E.

Double-population Q-learning collaborative mechanism

This section introduces Q-learning to help individuals choose appropriate update strategies. At the same time, this paper proposes a double-population Q-learning collaborative mechanism to help populations better select update strategies.

The settings of state and action

In this study, the five update mechanisms are set into five states. Similarly, it is also set into five actions. Each individual is assigned its own independent Q-table. In each iteration, every individual selects the subsequent action, determined by the current state, which represents the current update strategy. By training the Q-table continuously, the individual chooses the update strategy intelligently, with the support of the information in the Q-table.

Convergence factor Ec

The design of escape energy E in the GJO algorithm is particularly ingenious, which simulates the energy of the prey escaping from the golden jackal. In the early stage of hunting by the golden jackal, the prey has large escape energy and moves farther. As the jackal continues to drive the prey away, its escape energy diminishes and it ends up being captured. The escape energy E in the algorithm is taken as a balance factor between the exploration and exploitation phases.

System reliability optimization application

With the continuous increase in performance requirements and the increasing complexity of systems, the intricate and intertwined functional relationships among various components of the system have made reliability issues increasingly important. Reliability, as a common key technology and fundamental issue to ensure the core competitiveness of major equipment, has received extensive and profound attention. Therefore, research on system reliability holds significant practical significance.

Concrete pump trucks are important engineering machinery equipment in the modern construction industry. Their main function is to transport concrete to fixed locations at the worksite and perform pouring. Due to the typically harsh working environments, the reliability requirements for concrete pump trucks have increased accordingly. The primary operations of concrete pump trucks utilize hydraulic drive systems, which are the core of these trucks and directly affect their operational performance.

This study employs QIGJO to optimize the reliability of the hydraulic system in concrete pump trucks. Continuous-time Multi-dimensional T-S dynamic Fault Tree (CM-TSdFT)36 has higher accuracy than discrete time multi-dimensional dynamic fault tree, and can describe the system state at any time. In this paper, the CM-TSdFT model of the hydraulic system of the concrete conveying pump truck will be established. The hydraulic schematic diagram is shown in Fig. 11. Conclusion

To address the deficiencies of the GJO algorithm, five different update mechanisms were introduced, and Q-learning was employed to assist GJO in selecting suitable update strategies. Additionally, to enhance the improvement effect and the training efficiency of Q-learning, the double-population Q-learning collaborative mechanism was proposed. Free population and greedy population were set up and population dynamic sorting was used to increase information exchange between populations. The 23 benchmark functions and CEC2022 are applied to evaluate the algorithmic performance of QIGJO. Through a statistical analysis of the experimental results in the form of Friedman test and Wilcoxon rank sum test shows that QIGJO has a significant difference and strong performance. By analyzing three typical problems with engineering design, the utility of QIGJO is tested to reveal that QIGJO is effective. Overall, QIGJO is applicable to significantly improve the optimization performance of the GJO algorithm. The continuous-time multidimensional T-S dynamic fault tree (CM-TSdFT) analysis method was introduced to establish a reliability optimization model for concrete pump truck hydraulic system. This method was then utilized to solve the reliability optimization problem using QIGJO. The results demonstrate that QIGJO exhibits significant advantages in addressing this problem. This paper can provide certain methodological support for solving the reliability optimization problem of hydraulic systems. In addition, the complexity of QIGJO is higher and the time cost is higher. In light of this, the elite mechanism will be considered in the future, and multiple swarm mechanism will be applied to improve the performance of the algorithm while reducing its complexity.

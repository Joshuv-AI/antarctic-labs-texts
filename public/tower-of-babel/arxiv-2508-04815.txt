II
Model and Theoretical Framework

II.1
Hermitian Baseline

The foundation of quantum metrology in superconducting systems begins with the Hermitian Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) Hamiltonian on a one-dimensional lattice with
N
sites:

H_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left[t\,(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+\text{h.c.})+\Delta\,(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}^{\dagger}+\text{h.c.})\right],

(1)

where
t
denotes the hopping amplitude,
\Delta
the pairing potential, and
c_{j}^{(\dagger)}
the fermionic annihilation (creation) operators at site
j
. This model captures essential physics of topological superconductors
Kitaev 2001
;
Lutchyn et al. 2018
and Majorana chains, with real eigenvalues under open boundary conditions ensuring stable quasiparticle dynamics.

In quantum sensing, the quantum Fisher information (QFI) quantifies parameter estimation precision via the Cramér-Rao bound
\delta g\geq 1/\sqrt{\nu F_{Q}}
, where
\nu
is the number of measurements. For uncorrelated particles in this Hermitian system, the QFI scales as
F_{Q}=N
, corresponding to the standard quantum limit (SQL)
\delta g\sim 1/\sqrt{N}
. This arises from independent measurements yielding only
\sqrt{N}
enhancement over classical sensing, as dictated by the central limit theorem.

However, this SQL provides limited performance for modern sensing demands. Environmental decoherence destroys entanglement rapidly, capping effective
N
at modest values, many-body interactions induce chaos rather than controlled correlations and the absence of spectral singularities limits sensitivity to linear responses. Consequently, Hermitian protocols rarely exceed SQL by more than logarithmic factors in noisy environments, failing to deliver the quadratic
N^{2}
advantage needed for ultraprecise gravimetry, magnetometry, or atomic clocks. As detailed in Supplementary Sec. S1.1, these limitations motivate non-Hermitian extensions to achieve true Heisenberg scaling.

II.2
Non-Hermitian Extensions

To transcend Hermitian limitations, we introduce non-Hermiticity via two contrasting mechanisms in BdG chains, revealing a metrological dichotomy: significant suppression versus exceptional enhancement.

II.2.1
Non-Hermitian Skin Effect Model

The NHSE is captured by a 1D tight-binding chain with asymmetric hoppings

H_{\text{NHSE}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\!\Bigl[(t+\gamma)\,c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+(t-\gamma)\,c_{j+1}^{\dagger}c_{j}\Bigr],

(2)

where
t\!>\!0
is the average hopping amplitude and

0<\gamma<t
quantifies the non-reciprocity.
All sites share the same real onsite energy (set to zero).

Key properties.

Under open-boundary conditions the right-eigenstates localise as

\psi_{R}(j)\propto\!\bigl[\tfrac{t-\gamma}{t+\gamma}\bigr]^{j/2},

defining a localisation length

\xi^{-1}=\ln\!\sqrt{\tfrac{t+\gamma}{t-\gamma}}.

Left-eigenstates localise to the opposite edge, and the biorthogonal
overlap obeys

|\langle\psi_{L}|\psi_{R}\rangle|^{2}\!\sim\!e^{-2N/\xi}.

These results replace Eqs. (8)–(11) in the Supplement and are the
starting point for the QFI derivation in Sec. S2.1.

II.2.2
PT-Symmetric Model

Conversely, to avoid localization and harness non-Hermitian effects for enhancement, the PT-symmetric model employs a
staggered
potential corresponding to balanced gain and loss (Supp. Sec. S1.3):

H_{\text{PT}}=H_{0}+i\,g\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j}\,c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}.

(3)

This alternating potential preserves PT symmetry (parity
\mathcal{P}:c_{j}\to c_{N+1-j}
and time-reversal
\mathcal{T}:i\to-i
). In the unbroken phase, where
g<g_{c}=2t\cos(\pi/(N+1))
, the spectrum remains real and the eigenstates are extended. This allows the system to exploit the spectral singularities at exceptional points (EPs) for metrological enhancement, yielding Heisenberg-limited QFI (Supp. Sec. S2.2, S3.1). This model can be realized in photonic crystals
Singh et al. 2024
with alternating amplifying/absorbing regions or superconducting circuits with balanced parametric drives.

II.3
Lindblad Noise Model and Decoherence Analysis

Realistic experimental conditions require explicit noise modeling beyond phenomenological damping rates. We employ the Lindblad master equation framework
Zurek 2003
;
Schlosshauer 2007
to capture environmental decoherence in PT-symmetric BdG chains:

\frac{d\rho}{dt}=-i[H_{\text{PT}},\rho]+\sum_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha}\mathcal{D}[L_{\alpha}]\rho,

(4)

where
\mathcal{D}[L]\rho=L\rho L^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{L^{\dagger}L,\rho\}
and the Lindblad operators capture specific noise channels:

Dephasing:

L_{\phi,j}=\sqrt{\gamma_{\phi}}c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j}
models site-dependent phase noise with rate
\gamma_{\phi}/2\pi\sim 50
kHz in superconducting circuits.

Amplitude Damping:

L_{-,j}=\sqrt{\gamma_{-}}c_{j}
describes quasiparticle loss with
\gamma_{-}/2\pi\sim 10
kHz.

PT-Breaking Noise:

L_{\text{PT},j}=\sqrt{\gamma_{\text{PT}}}\sigma^{z}_{j}
represents stochastic gain/loss fluctuations threatening PT-symmetry.

The QFI evolution under noise becomes:

F_{Q}(t)=F_{Q}^{(0)}e^{-\Gamma_{\text{eff}}t}\left[1+\frac{\gamma_{\text{PT}}}{\Gamma_{\text{eff}}}(1-e^{-\Gamma_{\text{eff}}t})\right],

(5)

where
\Gamma_{\text{eff}}=\gamma_{\phi}+\gamma_{-}/2+\gamma_{\text{PT}}\sin^{2}(\pi g/g_{c})
captures the effective decoherence.

Critical Result:
Near exceptional points (
\delta\ll t
), PT-breaking noise dominates with
\Gamma_{\text{eff}}\approx\gamma_{\text{PT}}(g/g_{c})^{2}
, requiring
\delta>\sqrt{\gamma_{\text{PT}}/t}
for stable enhancement.

While our Markovian Lindblad model captures dominant decoherence, non-Markovian effects, such as
colored noise in superconducting circuits can modify
\Gamma_{\text{eff}}

by up to
20\%
, as memory kernels introduce back-action.
Porras et al. 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2023
.

We extend the analysis via the Nakajima-Zwanzig projection:

\Gamma_{\text{eff}}^{\text{non-M}}=\Gamma_{\text{eff}}+\int_{0}^{t}K(\tau)e^{-\gamma\tau}\,d\tau\approx\Gamma_{\text{eff}}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\right),

(6)

with
\alpha\sim 10^{-2}t
extracted from
1/f
spectra.

This caps the enhancement at

\eta\leq\frac{tN}{6\Gamma_{\text{eff}}^{\text{non-M}}},

reducing claims by
10
–
20\%
but still preserving Heisenberg scaling.

II.4
Biorthogonal Formalism

Standard Hermitian quantum metrology relies on the symmetric logarithmic derivative, yielding QFI
F_{Q}=4(\langle\partial_{\theta}\psi|\partial_{\theta}\psi\rangle-|\langle\psi|\partial_{\theta}\psi\rangle|^{2})
for pure states
|\psi\rangle
. However, this fails for non-Hermitian systems, where
H\neq H^{\dagger}
implies non-orthogonal eigenstates under the conventional inner product, violating completeness and rendering observables ill-defined without biorthogonal adjustment.

The proper framework employs left and right eigenvectors satisfying
H|\psi_{R}^{(n)}\rangle=E_{n}|\psi_{R}^{(n)}\rangle
and
H^{\dagger}|\psi_{L}^{(n)}\rangle=E_{n}^{*}|\psi_{L}^{(n)}\rangle
, with biorthonormality
\langle\psi_{L}^{(m)}|\psi_{R}^{(n)}\rangle=\delta_{mn}
and completeness
\sum_{n}|\psi_{R}^{(n)}\rangle\langle\psi_{L}^{(n)}|=\mathbb{I}
(assuming no EPs Jordan blocks otherwise, Sec. S3.2).

The biorthogonal QFI for parameter
\theta
is:

F_{Q}=4\left[\operatorname{Re}\langle\partial_{\theta}\psi_{L}|\partial_{\theta}\psi_{R}\rangle-|\langle\psi_{L}|\partial_{\theta}\psi_{R}\rangle|^{2}\right],

(7)

ensuring positivity and gauge invariance under phase shifts

\displaystyle|\psi_{R}\rangle

\displaystyle\to e^{i\phi}|\psi_{R}\rangle,

\displaystyle|\psi_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle\to e^{-i\phi}|\psi_{L}\rangle.

This generalizes to mixed states via purification but here focuses on pure ground states (Sec. S1.4).

To prove reduction to the Hermitian case, assume
H=H^{\dagger}
, so

|\psi_{L}\rangle=|\psi_{R}\rangle^{*}=|\psi\rangle
(up to phase). Then

\partial_{\theta}\psi_{L}=(\partial_{\theta}\psi_{R})^{*}
, and

\operatorname{Re}\langle\partial_{\theta}\psi_{L}|\partial_{\theta}\psi_{R}\rangle=\operatorname{Re}[\langle\partial_{\theta}\psi^{*}|\partial_{\theta}\psi\rangle]=\langle\partial_{\theta}\psi|\partial_{\theta}\psi\rangle,

since derivatives preserve Hermiticity. The cross-term becomes

|\langle\psi|\partial_{\theta}\psi\rangle|^{2}
, matching the standard formula exactly.

For Hermitian
H(\theta)
, the perturbative expanded

\partial_{\theta}|\psi\rangle=-\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\langle n|\partial_{\theta}H|0\rangle}{E_{n}-E_{0}}|n\rangle

yields

F_{Q}=4\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{|\langle n|\partial_{\theta}H|0\rangle|^{2}}{(E_{n}-E_{0})^{2}}.

In the non-Hermitian case, biorthogonal perturbation gives

\partial_{\theta}|\psi_{R}\rangle=-\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\langle\psi_{L}^{(n)}|\partial_{\theta}H|\psi_{R}^{(0)}\rangle}{E_{n}-E_{0}}|\psi_{R}^{(n)}\rangle,

and similarly for
|\psi_{L}\rangle
, reproducing Eq. (
7
) upon substitution
(Sec. S1.4.1). Hermitian coincidence follows from

\psi_{L}^{(n)}=(\psi_{R}^{(n)})^{*}
.

This formalism enables exact NHSE suppression and EP enhancement computations (Sec. S2), proving indispensable for non-Hermitian metrology.

III
Skin Effect: Metrological Catastrophe

The non-Hermitian skin effect (NHSE) in Eq. (2) triggers exponential localization for
|g|>t
, with length
\kappa=\arccosh(|g|/t)
. Right/left eigenstates
\psi_{R}(j)\sim e^{-\kappa j}
,
\psi_{L}(j)\sim e^{+\kappa j}
yield overlaps
|\langle\psi_{L}|\psi_{R}\rangle|^{2}\sim e^{-2\kappa N}
(Supp. S1.2), destroying coherence and suppressing QFI:

F_{Q}^{\text{NHSE}}=\frac{4N^{3}e^{-2\kappa N}}{3t^{2}\sinh^{2}\kappa}\ll N\quad(\text{SQL}),

(8)

derived via biorthogonal formalism (Supp. S2.1). This catastrophe limits NHSE to
N<1/\kappa
, contrasting Hermitian scalability (Fig.
1
left, with
\pm 1\%
numerics).

PT-Symmetry: Exceptional Enhancement

Balanced gain/loss in the PT-symmetric model (Eq.
3
) preserves PT-symmetry for
g<g_{c}=2t\cos(\pi/(N+1))\approx 2t(1-\pi^{2}/(2N^{2}))
(Supp. S3.1), as proven in Supp. Sec. S1.3. This enables extended states and the coalescence of eigenvalues at the exceptional point
g_{c}
, which features a Jordan block structure (Supp. Sec. S3.2). The resulting divergent sensitivity,
\partial E/\partial g\sim\delta^{-1/2}
, yields an enhanced QFI:

F_{Q}^{\text{PT}}=\frac{tN^{2}}{6\delta}\gg N,\quad\eta=\frac{tN}{6\delta},

(9)

with an optimal detuning
\delta\sim\max(\gamma,1/N^{2})
(Supp. S3.5, Fig.
1
right).

V
Multiparameter Sensing Matrix

V.1
Three-Parameter Estimation

Real-world quantum sensors must estimate multiple parameters simultaneously, such as chemical potential
\mu
(density control), Peierls phase
\phi
(flux sensing), and gain/loss
g
(non-Hermiticity tuning). In PT-symmetric BdG chains, this enables characterization of superconducting dynamics, topological phases, and open-system effects.

Motivation stems from experimental imperatives: superconducting circuits require joint estimation of
\mu
(gate voltage),
\phi
(magnetic flux), and
g
(parametric drive) for applications in gravimetry, magnetometry, and atomic clocks. Single-parameter protocols suffer from crosstalk, where uncertainty in one parameter degrades others multiparameter frameworks mitigate this via the full QFI matrix, optimizing simultaneous precision via matrix Cramér-Rao bounds
Chen et al. 2024c
.

Our formalism extends biorthogonal QFI to the vectorized case:

\mathbf{F}_{ab}=4\left[\operatorname{Re}\langle\partial_{a}\psi_{L}|\partial_{b}\psi_{R}\rangle-\langle\psi_{L}|\partial_{a}\psi_{R}\rangle\langle\partial_{b}\psi_{L}|\psi_{R}\rangle\right],

(10)

ensuring positivity and gauge invariance (Supp. Sec. S4).
For the Hamiltonian
H(\mu,\phi,g)
in the unbroken phase (
gN<1
),
we compute exact derivatives using perturbative Bogoliubov angles in the sine basis,
revealing Heisenberg scaling across all parameters.Physically, the negative off-diagonal element
F_{\mu\phi}=-\frac{N^{2}\Delta t^{2}}{4}
indicates an intrinsic anticorrelation between simultaneous estimates of the chemical potential
\mu
and Peierls phase
\phi
, meaning that reducing uncertainty in one parameter necessarily increases uncertainty in the other within the joint estimation protocol.

V.2
QFI Matrix

The exact QFI matrix for weak-pairing (
\Delta\ll t
) and small
\phi
demonstrates universal Heisenberg scaling:

\mathbf{F}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{N^{2}}{4\Delta^{2}}&-\frac{N^{2}\Delta t^{2}}{4}&0\\
-\frac{N^{2}\Delta t^{2}}{4}&\frac{3N^{2}t^{4}}{2}&0\\
0&0&\frac{N^{2}\Delta^{2}}{4t^{2}}\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta^{3}/t^{3},gN),

(11)

derived from coherent sums over
N
modes, with
F_{\mu\mu}=\sum_{k}\Delta_{k}^{2}/E_{k}^{4}\approx N^{2}/(4\Delta^{2})
via continuum integral,
F_{\phi\phi}=3N^{2}t^{4}/2
from phase perturbation, and weak off-diagonal
F_{\mu\phi}\propto N^{2}\Delta t^{2}\ll\sqrt{F_{\mu\mu}F_{\phi\phi}}
indicating mild correlations (Supp. Sec. S4.4).
The diagonal elements of the QFI matrix (Eq. 31) confirm the Heisenberg scaling of the precision. Advantages:
\eta_{\phi}\approx t^{2}\sqrt{3N/2}
is phase-dominant for magnetometry.

The multiparameter Cramér-Rao bound yields the covariance matrix for any unbiased estimator,
\mathbf{Cov}\geq\nu^{-1}\mathbf{F}^{-1}
. A rigorous inversion of the QFI matrix, which correctly accounts for all parameter correlations (see Supp. Sec. S4.6 for the full derivation), gives the inverse:

\mathbf{F}^{-1}=\frac{1}{N^{2}(6-\Delta^{4})}\begin{pmatrix}24\Delta^{2}&\frac{4\Delta^{3}}{t^{2}}&0\\
\frac{4\Delta^{3}}{t^{2}}&\frac{4}{t^{4}}&0\\
0&0&\frac{4t^{2}(6-\Delta^{4})}{\Delta^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

(12)

This result implies the following optimal sensitivities, which achieve the Heisenberg scaling limit
\propto 1/N
:

\displaystyle(\delta\mu)_{\min}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\Delta\sqrt{6}}{N\sqrt{\nu(6-\Delta^{4})}},

(13)

\displaystyle(\delta\phi)_{\min}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{Nt^{2}\sqrt{\nu(6-\Delta^{4})}},

(14)

\displaystyle(\delta g)_{\min}

\displaystyle=\frac{2t}{N\Delta\sqrt{\nu}}.

(15)

These corrected bounds surpass the SQL by factors of
\sqrt{N}
and establish a rigorous foundation for the protocol’s performance. The hierarchy of sensitivities remains, with phase estimation being the most precise due to the
t^{2}\gg 1
factor, making the system an ideal platform for quantum magnetometry.

VII.2
Fundamental Limits and Reconciliation with Lau-Clerk Theorem

A critical point of discussion for any non-Hermitian sensor is its relationship with fundamental sensitivity limits, particularly the theorem established by Lau and Clerk, which posits that passive non-Hermitian sensors offer no intrinsic sensitivity advantage over their optimally configured Hermitian counterparts.[1, 2, 3] This theorem is rigorously founded on the principle of information conservation: a passive non-Hermitian system (i.e., one with only loss) can always be viewed as a subsystem of a larger, globally Hermitian system via Naimark dilation.[4, 5] Within this larger system, the total quantum information about the parameter is conserved, and thus the sensitivity is fundamentally bounded by what is achievable in a purely Hermitian setting.

Our work does not claim to violate this fundamental information-theoretic principle. Instead, we distinguish our system by its use of
active gain
via parametric amplification, which introduces non-conservative dynamics driven by an external energy source. This provides a
practical pathway
to achieving Heisenberg-limited sensitivity that differs mechanistically from passive Hermitian protocols. The key distinction is as follows:

•

Passive Systems:
The Lau-Clerk theorem applies directly. Any apparent enhancement in the subsystem is precisely offset by factors like reduced success probability (postselection), meaning no net information gain when all resources are accounted for.[4, 3]

•

Active Systems (This Work):
The parametric pump is an external resource that continuously injects energy into the system. This energy amplifies the quantum coherences required for sensing, allowing the system to harness the EP’s divergent susceptibility (
\partial E/\partial g\sim\delta^{-1/2}
) and surpass the linear response typical of passive systems.

Therefore, the advantage presented here is not one of violating a fundamental bound on information, but rather one of practical implementation and resource efficiency. The active PT-symmetric protocol offers a physical mechanism to reach the Heisenberg limit that may be more robust or require less complex state preparation than a purely Hermitian counterpart designed to achieve the same sensitivity. S1. Model Hamiltonians and Biorthogonal Formalism

We establish two paradigmatic non-Hermitian BdG chain models that exhibit contrasting metrological behaviors. Building on the Hermitian baseline, we introduce non-Hermiticity through two distinct mechanisms: the skin-effect chain with exponentially suppressed quantum Fisher information (QFI), and the PT-symmetric chain preserving Heisenberg-limited scaling. The biorthogonal formalism becomes essential for computing observables in these non-Hermitian systems.

S1.1 Hermitian Baseline: Standard BdG Chain

The Hermitian Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer (BdG) Hamiltonian on a one-dimensional lattice, defined using fermionic operators
(c_{j},c_{j}^{\dagger})
, describes superconducting quasiparticles.

H_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left[t\,(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+\text{h.c.})+\Delta\,(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}^{\dagger}+\text{h.c.})\right],

(22)

where the Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer pairing
\Delta
creates Cooper pairs while the hopping amplitude
t
enables quasiparticle transport. Both parameters are real, ensuring Hermiticity and yielding real eigenvalues under open boundary conditions (OBC).

Physical Context

This model describes superconducting quantum circuits, topological superconductors, and Majorana wire networks
Kitaev 2001
;
Lutchyn et al. 2010
. For quantum metrology, the Hermitian spectrum enables standard protocols achieving shot-noise-limited sensitivity
\delta g\sim 1/\sqrt{N}
.

S1.2 Non-Hermitian Skin Effect Model

The NHSE is captured by a 1D tight-binding chain with asymmetric hoppings

H_{\text{NHSE}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\!\Bigl[(t+\gamma)\,c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+(t-\gamma)\,c_{j+1}^{\dagger}c_{j}\Bigr],

(23)

where
t\!>\!0
is the average hopping amplitude and

0<\gamma<t
quantifies the non-reciprocity.
All sites share the same real onsite energy (set to zero).

Key properties.

Under open-boundary conditions the right-eigenstates localise as

\psi_{R}(j)\propto\!\bigl[\tfrac{t-\gamma}{t+\gamma}\bigr]^{j/2},

defining a localisation length

\xi^{-1}=\ln\!\sqrt{\tfrac{t+\gamma}{t-\gamma}}.

Left-eigenstates localise to the opposite edge, and the biorthogonal
overlap obeys

|\langle\psi_{L}|\psi_{R}\rangle|^{2}\!\sim\!e^{-2N/\xi}.

These results replace Eqs. (8)–(11) in the Supplement and are the
starting point for the QFI derivation in Sec. S2.1.

Physical Mechanism

The imaginary term
ig
mimics non-reciprocal amplification, causing eigenmodes to localize at boundaries
Yao and Wang 2018
;
Helbig et al. 2020
via the
non-Hermitian skin effect
(NHSE). For
|g|>t
, the complex spectrum winds around the origin in the complex energy plane, forcing all eigenstates to exponentially localize at one boundary. This destroys the spatial coherence essential for quantum sensing, exponentially suppressing metrological sensitivity.

Under OBC, the eigenstates exhibit exponential localization:

\displaystyle\psi_{R}(j)

\displaystyle=Ae^{-\kappa j},\quad j=1,2,\ldots,N

(24)

\displaystyle\psi_{L}(j)

\displaystyle=Be^{+\kappa j},\quad j=1,2,\ldots,N

(25)

where the localization parameter is

\kappa=\operatorname{arccosh}\left(\frac{|g|}{t}\right)>0,\quad|g|>t.

(26)

Seo and Yang 2022

For
\kappa>0
, the series converges uniformly by the Weierstrass M-test, ensuring normalizability.

S1.2.1 Rigorous Biorthogonal Normalization

The biorthogonal normalization condition
\langle\psi_{L}|\psi_{R}\rangle=1
requires careful treatment of the geometric series. We compute each component systematically:

Unnormalized Inner Product.

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{L}|\psi_{R}\rangle_{\text{unnorm}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{L}^{*}(j)\psi_{R}(j)

\displaystyle=AB^{*}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{+\kappa j}e^{-\kappa j}

\displaystyle=AB^{*}N.

(27)

Individual Norms.

Using the geometric series formula
\sum_{j=1}^{N}r^{j}=r(1-r^{N})/(1-r)
for
r\neq 1
:

\displaystyle\|\psi_{R}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=|A|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-2\kappa j}=|A|^{2}e^{-2\kappa}\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa N}}{1-e^{-2\kappa}}

(28)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{L}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=|B|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{+2\kappa j}=|B|^{2}e^{2\kappa}\frac{e^{2\kappa N}-1}{e^{2\kappa}-1}

(29)

Note
: For
r>1
, we rewrite
\sum r^{j}=r(r^{N}-1)/(r-1)
to avoid negative denominators.

Normalized Biorthogonal Overlap.

The magnitude squared of the properly normalized overlap is:

|\langle\psi_{L}|\psi_{R}\rangle|^{2}=\frac{N^{2}}{\|\psi_{L}\|^{2}\|\psi_{R}\|^{2}}=\frac{(1-e^{-2\kappa})^{2}}{(1-e^{-2\kappa N})(e^{2\kappa N}-1)}.

(30)

Asymptotic Analysis.

For large
N
with
\kappa N\gg 1
(typically valid for
N\geq 10/\kappa
, ensuring subleading errors
<1\%
):

\displaystyle 1-e^{-2\kappa N}

\displaystyle\approx 1,\quad e^{2\kappa N}-1\approx e^{2\kappa N}

(31)

\displaystyle|\langle\psi_{L}|\psi_{R}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{N\gg 1}e^{-2\kappa N}\left[1-2e^{-2\kappa}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-4\kappa})\right].

(32)

Physical Implication

This exponential decay physically arises from destructive interference in the biorthogonal inner product, rendering NHSE states useless for large-
N
metrology. Quantum sensing protocols relying on coherent superpositions fail exponentially with system size, limiting scalability beyond
N\sim 1/\kappa
.

S1.3 PT-Symmetric Alternative

To prevent NHSE while maintaining non-Hermitian advantages, we implement balanced gain/loss:

H_{\text{PT}}=H_{0}+i\,g\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j}\,c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}.

(33)

This alternating pattern preserves
PT-symmetry
, where
\mathcal{P}
(parity) and
\mathcal{T}
(time-reversal) operators are defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}:\quad

\displaystyle c_{j}\mapsto c_{N+1-j},\quad c_{j}^{\dagger}\mapsto c_{N+1-j}^{\dagger}

(34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:\quad

\displaystyle c_{j}\mapsto c_{j},\quad c_{j}^{\dagger}\mapsto c_{j}^{\dagger},\quad i\mapsto-i

(35)

PT-Symmetry Verification

For odd
N
, we verify
[\mathcal{PT},H_{\text{PT}}]=0
rigorously:

\displaystyle\mathcal{PT}[ig(-1)^{j}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}]

\displaystyle=ig(-1)^{N+1-j}c_{N+1-j}^{\dagger}c_{N+1-j}\cdot(-1)

(36)

\displaystyle=-ig(-1)^{N+1-j}c_{N+1-j}^{\dagger}c_{N+1-j}

(37)

Since
N
is odd,
N+1
is even, so
(-1)^{N+1-j}=(-1)^{\text{even}-j}=(-1)^{-j}=(-1)^{j}
(since
(-1)^{-1}=-1
). Thus:

\displaystyle\mathcal{PT}[ig(-1)^{j}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}]

\displaystyle=-ig(-1)^{j}c_{N+1-j}^{\dagger}c_{N+1-j}

\displaystyle=ig(-1)^{j}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}.

(38)

confirming
[\mathcal{PT},H_{\text{PT}}]=0
.

Note
: For even
N
, PT-symmetry requires modified parity see
Bender and Boettcher 1998
.

Physical Advantage

Balanced gain/loss preserves spatially extended eigenstates, avoiding the localization catastrophe of NHSE. This enables Heisenberg-limited sensing with
N^{2}
scaling (surpassing the standard quantum limit by a factor of

), as demonstrated in the main text.

S1.4 General Biorthogonal Formalism

For any non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H
, the right and left eigenvectors satisfy:

\displaystyle H|\psi_{R}^{(n)}\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{n}|\psi_{R}^{(n)}\rangle

(39)

\displaystyle H^{\dagger}|\psi_{L}^{(n)}\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{n}^{*}|\psi_{L}^{(n)}\rangle

(40)

with biorthonormality and completeness:

\langle\psi_{L}^{(m)}|\psi_{R}^{(n)}\rangle=\delta_{mn},\quad\sum_{n}|\psi_{R}^{(n)}\rangle\langle\psi_{L}^{(n)}|=\mathbb{I}.

(41)

Caveat
: The completeness relation assumes no exceptional points (EPs) where eigenvectors coalesce. Near EPs, the basis becomes incomplete and Jordan blocks must be considered.

S1.4.1 Biorthogonal Quantum Fisher Information

For a properly biorthonormalized state

Brody and Gibbons 2012
;
Brody 2014
;

F_{Q}=4\left[\operatorname{Re}\langle\partial_{\theta}\psi_{L}|\partial_{\theta}\psi_{R}\rangle-|\langle\psi_{L}|\partial_{\theta}\psi_{R}\rangle|^{2}\right],

(42)

where the real part is essential for non-Hermitian systems to ensure positive-definite Fisher information.

From the biorthogonal QFI formula, with

The cross-term scales as

S2. Quantum Fisher Information in Non-Hermitian Systems

In quantum metrology, the quantum Fisher information (QFI) quantifies the ultimate sensitivity limit for parameter estimation via the quantum Cramér-Rao bound:
(\delta g)^{2}\geq 1/(\nu F_{Q})

\nu
is the number of measurements. For non-Hermitian systems, where eigenstates lose orthogonality under the standard inner product, the proper formulation requires the biorthogonal framework established in Section S1. We derive exact expressions for both the exponentially suppressed NHSE regime and the enhanced exceptional point (EP) scaling, demonstrating the metrological divide between these paradigms.

(46)

First Term Evaluation (Corrected):

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\langle\partial_{g}\psi_{\mathrm{L}}|\partial_{g}\psi_{\mathrm{R}}\rangle

(47)

(48)

(49)

(50)

which is exponentially suppressed relative to the first term.

\boxed{\mathbf{F}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{N^{2}}{4\Delta^{2}}&-\frac{N^{2}\Delta t^{2}}{4}&0\\
-\frac{N^{2}\Delta t^{2}}{4}&\frac{3N^{2}t^{4}}{2}&0\\
0&0&\frac{N^{2}\Delta^{2}}{4t^{2}}\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta^{3}/t^{3},gN)}

(127)

Key Physical Insights:

1. True Heisenberg Scaling: All diagonal elements scale as
N^{2}
, representing optimal coherent enhancement from collective quantum correlations.

2. Weak Correlation: The off-diagonal element
F_{\mu\phi}\propto\Delta t^{2}\ll\sqrt{F_{\mu\mu}F_{\phi\phi}}
represents weak but non-zero parameter correlation in the superconducting regime.

3. Hierarchy of Sensitivities:
- Phase parameter:
\delta\phi\sim 1/(Nt^{2}\sqrt{\nu})
(highest sensitivity)
- Chemical potential:
\delta\mu\sim\Delta/(N\sqrt{\nu})
(intermediate)
- Gain/loss:
\delta g\sim t/(N\sqrt{\nu})
(lowest sensitivity)

4. Condition Number:
\kappa(\mathbf{F})=\sqrt{F_{\max}/F_{\min}}=\frac{t^{2}\sqrt{6}}{\Delta}\gg 1
for weak pairing, indicating phase parameter dominance.

S4.6 Multiparameter Cramér-Rao Bound and Corrected Sensitivities

For the simultaneous estimation of all parameters
\bm{\theta}=(\mu,\phi,g)
, the covariance matrix of any unbiased estimator is bounded by the matrix Cramér-Rao bound:

\mathbf{Cov}[\hat{\bm{\theta}}]\geq\frac{1}{\nu}\mathbf{F}^{-1},

(128)

where
\nu
is the number of measurements. The precision of the estimation is therefore determined by the inverse of the QFI matrix. The original analysis contained a critical error in this inversion. We now present the rigorous derivation.

The QFI matrix
\mathbf{F}
from Eq. (
127
) is block-diagonal, with the gain/loss parameter
g
decoupled from the chemical potential
\mu
and Peierls phase
\phi
. The inverse of the
(g,g)
element is trivial:
(F^{-1})_{gg}=1/F_{gg}=4t^{2}/(N^{2}\Delta^{2})
.

The nontrivial part is the inversion of the upper-left
2\times 2
block, which we denote
\mathbf{F}_{\mu\phi}
:

\mathbf{F}_{\mu\phi}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{N^{2}}{4\Delta^{2}}&-\frac{N^{2}\Delta t^{2}}{4}\\
-\frac{N^{2}\Delta t^{2}}{4}&\frac{3N^{2}t^{4}}{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(129)

The determinant of this block is calculated as:

\displaystyle\det(\mathbf{F}_{\mu\phi})

\displaystyle=F_{\mu\mu}F_{\phi\phi}-F_{\mu\phi}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{N^{2}}{4\Delta^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{3N^{2}t^{4}}{2}\right)-\left(-\frac{N^{2}\Delta t^{2}}{4}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{3N^{4}t^{4}}{8\Delta^{2}}-\frac{N^{4}\Delta^{2}t^{4}}{16}=\frac{N^{4}t^{4}}{16\Delta^{2}}(6-\Delta^{4}).

(130)

Using the formula for a
2\times 2
matrix inverse,
(\mathbf{F}_{\mu\phi})^{-1}=\frac{1}{\det(\mathbf{F}_{\mu\phi})}\begin{pmatrix}F_{\phi\phi}&-F_{\mu\phi}\\
-F_{\mu\phi}&F_{\mu\mu}\end{pmatrix}
, we obtain the complete, mathematically correct inverse QFI matrix:

\mathbf{F}^{-1}=\frac{1}{N^{2}(6-\Delta^{4})}\begin{pmatrix}24\Delta^{2}&\frac{4\Delta^{3}}{t^{2}}&0\\
\frac{4\Delta^{3}}{t^{2}}&\frac{4}{t^{4}}&0\\
0&0&\frac{4t^{2}(6-\Delta^{4})}{\Delta^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

(131)

Note:
In the weak-pairing limit (
\Delta\ll 1
), the term
\Delta^{4}
is negligible compared to 6. This exact result then reduces to the approximation where the diagonal elements are
(F^{-1})_{\mu\mu}\approx 4\Delta^{2}/N^{2}
and
(F^{-1})_{\phi\phi}\approx 2/(3N^{2}t^{4})
. However, presenting the exact form is crucial for mathematical rigor.

Corrected Optimal Individual Sensitivities

The minimum uncertainty for each parameter is given by
(\delta\theta_{a})^{2}\geq(F^{-1})_{aa}/\nu
. From the diagonal elements of the corrected inverse matrix Eq. (
131
), we derive the true optimal sensitivities:

\displaystyle(\delta\mu)_{\min}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N\sqrt{\nu}}\sqrt{\frac{24\Delta^{2}}{6-\Delta^{4}}}=\frac{2\Delta\sqrt{6}}{N\sqrt{\nu(6-\Delta^{4})}},

(132)

\displaystyle(\delta\phi)_{\min}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Nt^{2}\sqrt{\nu}}\sqrt{\frac{4}{6-\Delta^{4}}}=\frac{2}{Nt^{2}\sqrt{\nu(6-\Delta^{4})}},

(133)

\displaystyle(\delta g)_{\min}

\displaystyle=\frac{2t}{N\Delta\sqrt{\nu}}.

(134)

These results confirm that Heisenberg scaling (
\delta\theta\propto 1/N
) is preserved for all parameters. The new expressions correct the previous analysis by including the effect of parameter correlations through the full matrix determinant, providing a rigorous foundation for the multiparameter sensing protocol.

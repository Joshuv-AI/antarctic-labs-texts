Computing Heegaard Floer invariants of closed contact 3-manifolds from open books
Çağatay Kutluhan, Gordana Matić, Jeremy Van Horn-Morris, Andy Wand

Abstract
. We present two SageMath programs that build on and improve upon Sucharit Sarkar’s hf-hat . Given an abstract open book and a collection of pairwise disjoint properly embedded arcs on a page of the open book, the first program, hf-hat-obd , can be used to analyze the resulting Heegaard diagram, while the second, hf-hat-obd-nice computes the hat version of Heegaard Floer homology of the closed oriented 3-manifold described by the Heegaard diagram as long as the latter is nice. We also provide an auxiliary program, makenice , that can be used to produce a nice Heegaard diagram out of any abstract open book and a collection of pairwise disjoint properly embedded arcs on a page of the open book. The primary applications of hf-hat-obd-nice are to the computation of the Ozsváth–Szabó contact invariant and to the detection of finiteness of spectral order, which is a Stein fillability obstruction that is stronger than the vanishing of the Ozsváth–Szabó contact invariant.



1.
Introduction

A classical result due to Alexander
[
Ale20
]
states that every closed oriented 3-manifold admits an open book decomposition. Once an open book and an arc basis on its page are chosen, we can construct a Heegaard diagram. The latter can then be used to compute the Heegaard Floer homology of the 3-manifold described by that Heegaard diagram. Moreover, the chosen open book supports a contact structure
[
TW75
]
and an invariant of this contact structure living in Heegaard Floer homology defined by Ozsváth and Szabó in
[
OS05
]
is represented by a distinguished tuple of points in that Heegaard diagram
[
HKM09
]
. Using this description of the contact invariant in Heegaard Floer homology, the authors defined in
[
KMVHMW23
]
a refinement of it, called
spectral order
, taking values in the set
\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, and showed that

Theorem
.

[
KMVHMW23
, Corollary 4.8]

If the contact structure is Stein fillable, then its spectral order is infinite.

Moreover, the spectral order of the contact structure can be computed using a sufficiently large collection of pairwise disjoint properly embedded arcs on a page of the open book and it is finite if and only if it is finite for some arc collection. In order to compute spectral order for a fixed arc collection, one has to

(1)

find all Maslov index-1 positive domains in the Heegaard diagram, and

(2)

compute the number of holomorphic curves in the moduli spaces of all Maslov index-1 positive domains.

Task (
1
) is in general impossible to fulfill if the Heegaard diagram belongs to a closed oriented 3-manifold with non-zero first Betti number. This is why Sucharit Sarkar’s
hf-hat

[
Sar
]
can only be used to analyze Heegaard diagrams for rational homology 3-spheres. In Section
2.1
, we show that there is an
a priori
bound on the number of positive domains between any pair of generators of the Heegaard Floer chain complex when the Heegaard diagram is obtained from an open book and a collection of pairwise disjoint properly embedded arcs on a page of the open book. This allows us to find all positive domains between pairs of generators of the Heegaard Floer chain complex algorithmically, thereby completing task (
1
). Using this fact, we wrote an improved version of
hf-hat
, named
hf-hat-obd
, a tutorial for which is provided in Section
3
. As for task (
2
), it was shown by Plamenevskaya in
[
Pla07
]
that one may isotope the monodromy of the open book so as to produce a nice Heegaard diagram. Her proof is based on an algorithm of Sarkar and Wang
[
SW10
, Section 4]
which we implement in an auxiliary program,
makenice
. Once a nice Heegaard diagram resulting from an open book and a collection of pairwise disjoint properly embedded arcs on a page of the open book is provided,
hf-hat-obd-nice
can be used to compute the hat version of Heegaard Floer homology of the 3-manifold described by the Heegaard diagram and the Ozsváth–Szabó contact invariant of the contact structure supported by the open book. In addition,
hf-hat-obd-nice
implements an algorithm to compute the value of spectral order of the open book and the arc collection based on a criterion for finiteness of spectral order, which we prove in Section
2.2
. A tutorial for
hf-hat-obd-nice
is provided in Section
4
.

2.
Main Results

In this section, we prove some key results that form the basis of the algorithms used in
hf-hat-obd
and
hf-hat-obd-nice
. To set the stage, let
(S,\varphi)
be an abstract open book and
\mathbf{a}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\textsc{n}}\}
be a collection of pairwise disjoint, properly embedded oriented arcs on
S
cutting it open into a disjoint union of disks. Then the triple
(S,\varphi,\mathbf{a})
, which we shall refer to as an
open book diagram
, yields a weakly admissible (multi-)pointed Heegaard diagram
(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathbf{z})
where

•

\Sigma=S_{1/2}\cup_{\partial S}-S_{0}
,

•

\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\textsc{n}}\}
and
\alpha_{i}=a_{i}\cup-a_{i}
,

•

\boldsymbol{\beta}=\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\textsc{n}}\}
and
\beta_{i}=b_{i}\cup-\varphi(b_{i})
with each
b_{i}
a Hamiltonian isotopic copy of
a_{i}
that intersects
a_{i}
positively in one distinguished point
{x_{\xi}}^{i}\in\mathrm{int}(S_{1/2})
, called a
contact intersection point
, and

•

\mathbf{z}=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\}
and each
z_{i}
lies in the interior of a distinct connected component of
S_{1/2}\,\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{\textsc{n}}a_{i}
, outside the thin strips between
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
.

The
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
- and
\boldsymbol{\beta}
-curves divide the Heegaard surface
\Sigma
into a number of subsurfaces whose closures are called
regions
. Let
\mathbf{x}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{\textsc{n}}\},\mathbf{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{\textsc{n}}\}
be two sets of intersection points between
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
- and
\boldsymbol{\beta}
-curves each of which determines a one-to-one pairing between the two kinds of curves, and
\widehat{\pi}_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
denote the set of 2-chains
D
that are formal integer linear sums of unpointed regions in the Heegaard diagram
(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathbf{z})
such that
\partial(\partial|_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}D)=\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}
, where
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
are considered 0-chains. Such 2-chains are called
domains
and a domain
D
is
positive
(
D\geq 0
) if its multiplicity in every unpointed region is non-negative. Elements of
\widehat{\pi}_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x})
are called
periodic domains
.

2.1.
A bound on the number of positive domains

First we prove the aforementioned
a priori
bound on the number of positive domains between
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
. The upcoming lemma can be considered an improvement over
[
OS04
, Lemma 4.13]
for Heegaard diagrams resulting from open books.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
\widehat{\pi}_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\neq\emptyset
and let
k
denote the number of contact intersection points in
\mathbf{y}
that are not in
\mathbf{x}
. Then there are at most
2^{k}
positive domains in
\widehat{\pi}_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.

Proof.

Fix
D_{\circ}\in\widehat{\pi}_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
and let
D\in\widehat{\pi}_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
be a positive domain. If
D\neq D_{\circ}
, then
D-D_{\circ}\in\widehat{\pi}_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{\xi},\mathbf{x}_{\xi})
is a non-trivial periodic domain. Since a non-trivial periodic domain has to have the entirety of at least one
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
- or
\boldsymbol{\beta}
-curve with non-zero multiplicity on its boundary, it has to contain at least one contact intersection point on its boundary. Moreover, a contact intersection point
{x_{\xi}}^{i}
is at the corner of exactly two unpointed regions,
{R^{i}}_{1}
and
{R^{i}}_{3}
, which intersect only at that contact intersection point (see Figure
1
); therefore, a non-trivial periodic domain containing the contact intersection point
{x_{\xi}}^{i}
has to have both
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
on its boundary with multiplicities in
{R^{i}}_{1}
and
{R^{i}}_{3}
adding up to zero. It follows, by applying
[
OS04
, Lemma 2.18]
, that the multiplicities of
D-D_{\circ}
in
{R^{i}}_{1}
and
{R^{i}}_{3}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,\textsc{n}\}
uniquely determine the periodic domain. Consequently, multiplicities of
D
in
{R^{i}}_{1}
and
{R^{i}}_{3}
for
{x_{\xi}}^{i}
in
\mathbf{y}
but not in
\mathbf{x}
uniquely determine that domain. Since
D
has multiplicity
1
in exactly one of
{R^{i}}_{1}
or
{R^{i}}_{3}
while having multiplicity
0
in the other three regions with corners at
{x_{\xi}}^{i}
for each
{x_{\xi}}^{i}
in
\mathbf{y}
but not in
\mathbf{x}
, there are at most
2^{k}
positive domains in
\widehat{\pi}_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.
∎

2.2.
Detecting finiteness of spectral order

As was shown in
[
KMVHMW23
, Section 5.2]
, finiteness of spectral order is an obstruction to Stein fillability of contact 3-manifolds that is stronger than vanishing of the Ozsváth–Szabó contact invariant. In this regard, the ability to detect finiteness of spectral order is valuable even if we may not be able to compute its exact value. To detect finiteness of spectral order, it suffices to find an open book
(S,\phi)
and an arc collection
\mathbf{a}
on
S
for which spectral order is finite. In this subsection, we discuss how to do this efficiently.

In what follows,
Y
denotes the closed oriented 3-manifold described by the Heegaard diagram
(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})
and
\xi
is the contact structure on
Y
supported by the open book
(S,\varphi)
. We start by recalling the definition of spectral order. For any
D\in\widehat{\pi}_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
, define

(2.1)

J_{+}(D):=\mu(D)-2e(D)+|\mathbf{x}|-|\mathbf{y}|,

where

•

|\cdot|
denotes the number of disjoint cycles in the element of the symmetric group
\mathrm{S}_{N}
determined by the pairing between the
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
- and
\boldsymbol{\beta}
-curves associated to a generator of the Heegaard Floer chain complex (e.g. the canonical generator
\mathbf{x}_{\xi}=\{{x_{\xi}}^{1},\dots,{x_{\xi}}^{\textsc{n}}\}
has
|\mathbf{x}_{\xi}|=\textsc{n}
),

•

e(D)
is the Euler measure of
D
(see
[
Lip06
, §4.1 p. 973]
for definition), and

•

\mu(D)
is the Maslov index of
D
, which, by a formula of Lipshitz
[
Lip06
, Corollary 4.10]
(cf.
[
Lip14
, Proposition
4.8^{\prime}
]
), is equal to

(2.2)

\mu(D)=n_{\mathbf{x}}(D)+n_{\mathbf{y}}(D)+e(D),

with
n_{\mathbf{x}}(D)
representing the point measure of
D
at
\mathbf{x}
, i.e. the sum of the averages of the coefficients of
D
in the four regions with corners at each
x_{i}\in\alpha_{i}\cap\beta_{j}
.

It was shown in
[
KMVHMW23
, Section 2.2]
that
J_{+}(D)
is a non-negative integer divisible by two when
D\in\widehat{\pi}_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is represented by a holomorphic curve.
Therefore, we can decompose the Heegaard Floer differential as

\widehat{\partial}_{\mathit{HF}}=\partial_{0}+\partial_{1}+\cdots+\partial_{\ell}+\cdots,

where
\partial_{\ell}
counts holomorphic curves representing domains with
J_{+}=2\ell
. Using this decomposition, construct a chain complex

\widehat{\mathcal{CF}}(S,\phi,\mathbf{a}):=\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\alpha})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}\mathbb{F}[t,t^{-1}]

over
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
filtered by the power of
t
and endowed with the differential

\widehat{\partial}:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\partial_{\ell}\otimes t^{-\ell}.

Define
o(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})
to be the smallest non-negative integer
k
such that
\mathbf{x}_{\xi}
represents the trivial class in the
k+1^{\textrm{st}}
page of the spectral sequence
E^{\ast}(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})
associated to the filtered chain complex
(\widehat{\mathcal{CF}}(S,\phi,\mathbf{a}),\widehat{\partial})
, and the spectral order
\mathbf{o}(\xi)
to be

\mathbf{o}(\xi):=\min\{o(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})\},

where the minimum is taken over all open books supporting
\xi
and all collections of pairwise disjoint properly embedded arcs on pages of those open books. With the preceding understood, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that
c_{1}(\xi)
is torsion. If
\widehat{c}(\xi)=0
, then
\mathbf{o}(\xi)<\infty
.

Proof.

First note that for any periodic domain
P\in\widehat{\pi}_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{\xi},\mathbf{x}_{\xi})
, we have
\mu(P)=\langle c_{1}(\xi),H(P)\rangle=0
, and
n_{\mathbf{x}_{\xi}}(P)=0
; hence,
e(P)=0
. As a result,
J_{+}(P)=0
for every periodic domain
P\in\widehat{\pi}_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{\xi},\mathbf{x}_{\xi})
. It follows that there is a well-defined filtration
\mathcal{F}
on
\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})
by
J_{+}
where
\mathbf{x}_{\xi}
lies in
\mathcal{F}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{-1}
. Moreover, this filtration is bounded by virtue of the fact that
\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})
is finitely generated. Consequently, the spectral sequence of the filtered chain complex converges to the homology of
\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})
, that is,
\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})
. Meanwhile,
\mathbf{o}(\xi)<\infty
if and only if
\mathbf{x}_{\xi}
represents the zero class on some finite page of the spectral sequence of the filtered chain complex, which must be the case if
\widehat{c}(\xi)=0
in
\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(-Y,\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})
.
∎

To detect whether
o(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})<\infty
or not in general, we have to compute the differential on
\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})
. To be able to do this, we assume that
\phi
is such that the Heegaard diagram
(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathbf{z})
constructed from
(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})
is nice, i.e. all unpointed regions in the Heegaard diagram are either bigons or rectangles. Since Maslov index-1 positive domains in a nice Heegaard diagram are either empty embedded bigons or empty embedded rectangles
[
SW10
, Theorem 3.3]
, the moduli space of holomorphic curves for a Maslov index-1 positive domain consists of a single point, and

\widehat{\partial}_{\mathit{HF}}=\partial_{0}+\partial_{1}.

Moreover,
\partial_{0}
and
\partial_{1}
are both differentials themselves and
\partial_{0}\partial_{1}=\partial_{1}\partial_{0}
. With the preceding understood, let
C_{0}\subset\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})
denote the subspace of chains in the same (relative) Maslov grading as
\mathbf{x}_{\xi}
and
C_{1}\subset\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathfrak{s}_{\xi})
denote the subspace of chains in (relative) Maslov grading 1 above
\mathbf{x}_{\xi}
. Then
o(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})<\infty
if and only if there exist chains
b_{0},\dots,b_{k}\in C_{1}
with
k\geq 0
such that

(2.3)

\begin{split}\partial_{0}b_{0}+\partial_{1}b_{1}&=\mathbf{x}_{\xi},\\
\partial_{0}b_{1}+\partial_{1}b_{2}&=0,\\
&\vdots\\
\partial_{0}b_{k-1}+\partial_{1}b_{k}&=0,\\
\partial_{0}b_{k}&=0.\end{split}

Figure 2
.
Directed graph depicting
b_{0},\dots,b_{k}\in C_{1}
from (
2.3
) and their differentials.

We shall regard
\partial_{0},\partial_{1}
as maps from
C_{1}
to
C_{0}
. The next lemma allows us to assume, without loss of generality, that
\partial_{1}
is injective.

Lemma 3
.

There exists a minimal subspace
K\subset C_{1}
with the property that the map from
C_{1}/K
to
C_{0}/\partial_{0}(K)
induced by
\partial_{1}
is injective.

Proof.

Construct a nested sequence
K_{0}\subset K_{1}\subset\cdots\subset K_{i}\subset\cdots
of subspaces of
C_{1}
as follows:

\displaystyle K_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ker(\partial_{1}:C_{1}\to C_{0}),

\displaystyle K_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ker(\partial_{1}:C_{1}\to C_{0}/\partial_{0}(K_{i-1}))\;\textrm{for\;each}\;i\geq 1.

Since
C_{1}
is finite dimensional, there exists
n\geq 0
and a subspace
K\subset C_{1}
such that
K_{i}=K
for all
i\geq n
. Then

\ker(\partial_{1}:C_{1}\to C_{0}/\partial_{0}(K_{n}))=K,

hence the induced map
\underline{\partial}\,_{1}:C_{1}/K\to C_{0}/\partial_{0}(K))
is injective.
∎

Note that if
o(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})
is non-zero,
K=C_{1}
implies that
o(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})=\infty
since otherwise
\partial_{1}(C_{1})\subset\partial_{0}(C_{1})
and we would be able to find
b\in C_{1}
such that
\partial_{0}b=\mathbf{x}_{\xi}
, resulting in a contradiction.

With the above understood, if
o(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})\neq 0
and
K\neq C_{1}
, then
C_{0}/\partial_{0}(K)
and
C_{1}/K
are both non-zero and we may instead consider the maps

\displaystyle\underline{\partial}\,_{0}:C_{1}/K\to C_{0}/\partial_{0}(K)),

\displaystyle\underline{\partial}\,_{1}:C_{1}/K\to C_{0}/\partial_{0}(K)).

Clearly, if there exist
b_{0},\dots,b_{k}\in C_{1}
satisfying (
2.3
), then
\underline{b}\,_{0},\dots,\underline{b}\,_{k}\in C_{1}/K
satisfy

(2.4)

\begin{split}\underline{\partial}\,_{0}\,\underline{b}\,_{0}+\underline{\partial}\,_{1}\,\underline{b}\,_{1}&=\underline{\mathbf{x}}\,_{\xi},\\
\underline{\partial}\,_{0}\,\underline{b}\,_{1}+\underline{\partial}\,_{1}\,\underline{b}\,_{2}&=0,\\
&\vdots\\
\underline{\partial}\,_{0}\,\underline{b}\,_{k-1}+\underline{\partial}\,_{1}\,\underline{b}\,_{k}&=0,\\
\underline{\partial}\,_{0}\,\underline{b}\,_{k}&=0.\end{split}

Conversely,

Lemma 4
.

If there are
\underline{b}\,_{0},\dots,\underline{b}\,_{k}\in C_{1}/K
satisfying (
2.4
), then there are
b_{0},\dots,b_{k}\in C_{1}
satisfying (
2.3
).

Proof.

Let
\underline{b}\,_{0},\dots,\underline{b}\,_{k}\in C_{1}/K
be such that (
2.4
) is satisfied. Then there are
c_{0},\dots,c_{k}\in C_{1}
with
\underline{b}\,_{i}=c_{i}K
for all
i=0,\dots,k
and
d_{0},\dots,d_{k}\in K
such that

\begin{split}\partial_{0}c_{0}+\partial_{1}c_{1}&=\mathbf{x}_{\xi}+\partial_{0}d_{0},\\
\partial_{0}c_{1}+\partial_{1}c_{2}&=\partial_{0}d_{1},\\
&\vdots\\
\partial_{0}c_{k-1}+\partial_{1}c_{k}&=\partial_{0}d_{k-1},\\
\partial_{0}c_{k}&=\partial_{0}d_{k}.\end{split}

(We use the fact that
\partial_{0}\partial_{1}=\partial_{1}\partial_{0}
to derive the above set of equations.) Let
b_{k}=c_{k}+d_{k}
. Then
\partial_{0}b_{k}=0
and
\partial_{1}b_{k}=\partial_{1}c_{k}+\partial_{1}d_{k}=\partial_{1}c_{k}+\partial_{0}e_{k-1}
for some
e_{k-1}\in K
by the proof of Lemma
3
. Next, define
b_{k-1}=c_{k-1}+d_{k-1}+e_{k-1}
and note that

\partial_{0}b_{k-1}+\partial_{1}b_{k}=\partial_{0}(c_{k-1}+d_{k-1}+e_{k-1})+\partial_{1}c_{k}+\partial_{0}e_{k-1}=0.

Continue in the same way so as to define all other
b_{i}\in C_{1}
and note that
b_{0},\dots,b_{k}
satisfy (
2.3
).
∎

For the remainder of this section, we shall assume that
\partial_{1}:C_{1}\to C_{0}
is injective. Furthermore, if
\partial_{0}:C_{1}\to C_{0}
were also injective, then
o(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})
would be infinity, as is easily seen from (
2.3
). Therefore, we shall also assume that
\partial_{0}
is not injective.

Since
\partial_{1}
is injective, there exists a map
\delta:U_{0}=\partial_{1}(C_{1})\to C_{0}
defined by
\delta=\partial_{0}\circ{\partial_{1}}^{-1}
. Note that
\ker{\delta}=\partial_{1}(\ker{\partial_{0}})\neq 0
since
\partial_{0}
is not injective and
\partial_{1}
is injective. Next, for
i\geq 1
define subspaces
U_{i}\subset C_{0}
iteratively as
U_{i}=\delta^{-1}(U_{i-1})
. Note that these subspaces satisfy

\ker{\delta}\subset\cdots\subset U_{i}\subset\cdots\subset U_{1}\subset U_{0},

and that
\bigcap U_{i}=U_{n}\neq 0
for some
n\geq 0
since
C_{0}
is finite dimensional and
\ker\delta\neq 0
. Then for each
i\geq 1
the maps defined by
\delta_{i}=\delta|_{U_{i}}:U_{i}\to U_{i-1}
and

\delta^{i}=\delta\circ\delta_{1}\circ\cdots\circ\delta_{i-1}:U_{i-1}\to C_{0}

have
\ker{\delta_{i}}=\ker\delta
and

\ker{\delta^{i}}={\delta_{i-1}}^{-1}({\delta_{i-2}}^{-1}(\dots({\delta_{1}}^{-1}(\ker\delta)))\cdots)={\delta_{i-1}}^{-1}(\ker{\delta^{i-1}}).

It is easy to see by induction that
\ker{\delta^{i}}\subset\ker{\delta^{i+1}}
for all
i\geq 1
. Therefore,

•

\ker{\delta^{i}}\subset U_{n}
for all
i\geq 1
, and

•

there exists
m\geq 1
such that
\ker{\delta^{i}}\subset\ker{\delta^{m}}
for all
i\geq 1
.

Finally, let

\mathcal{R}=\mathbf{x}_{\xi}+\partial_{0}(C_{1}),

which we call the
remainder set
. Then,

Theorem 5
.

o(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})<\infty
if and only if
\ker{\delta^{m}}\cap\mathcal{R}\neq\emptyset
, where
m\geq 1
is as in the second bullet point above.

Proof.

It is clear that if there exist
b_{0},\dots,b_{k}\in C_{1}
satisfying (
2.3
), then
\mathbf{x}_{\xi}+\partial_{0}b_{0}\in\ker{\delta^{k}}\cap\mathcal{R}
which in turn is contained in
\ker{\delta^{m}}\cap\mathcal{R}
by the second bullet point above. Conversely, if there exist
r\in\ker{\delta^{m}}\cap\mathcal{R}
, then
\delta^{k}r=0
for some
k\geq 1
,
\mathbf{x}_{\xi}+r=\partial_{0}b_{0}
for some
b_{0}\in C_{1}
, and
b_{i}\in C_{0}
defined by
b_{i}={\partial_{1}}^{-1}r_{i}
where
r_{1}=r
and
r_{i+1}=\delta r_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,k-1
satisfy (
2.3
). Hence,
o(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})<\infty
and

o(S,\phi,\mathbf{a})=\min\{k\;|\;\ker{\delta^{k}}\cap\mathcal{R}\neq\emptyset\}.\qed

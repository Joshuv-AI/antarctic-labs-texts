Hidden symmetries and separability structures of Ovcharenko–Podolský and conformal-to-Carter spacetimes
Finnian Gray, David Kubizňák, Hryhorii Ovcharenko, Jiří Podolský

Abstract
Recently, a remarkable new class of spacetimes describing black holes immersed in a non-aligned electromagnetic field has been found. While still of type D, this class goes beyond the famous Plebański–Demiański family. Here we demonstrate that the whole class admits a hidden symmetry encoded in the non-degenerate conformal Killing–Yano 2-form. Interestingly, as a direct consequence of non-alignment of the electromagnetic field and contrary to the Plebański–Demiański class (where the field is aligned), such a symmetry no longer generates the full “tower of symmetries”. Despite this, it enables one to separate variables in massless Hamilton–Jacobi, conformal wave, and massless Dirac equations, as well as allows one to tackle massless vector and tensor perturbations. These results provide a useful mathematical tool for discussing numerous (astrophysical) applications to be described by these metrics. Moreover, as we shall show, the novel spacetimes provide an interesting test-ground for studying the recently defined Penrose charges whose existence is intrinsically related to hidden rather than explicit symmetries.

II
Hidden symmetries

In this section, we shall briefly overview the basic properties of objects responsible for hidden symmetries in general relativity.
We start with
conformal Killing–Yano
(CKY) tensors and their special cases.
A CKY tensor is a
p
-form
h
whose covariant derivative has a vanishing harmonic part, that is, it can be split into the
exterior derivative
and
divergence
parts as follows
Kashiwada 1968
;
Tachibana 1969

\nabla_{\!\mu}h_{\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{p}}=\nabla_{\![\mu}h_{\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{p}]}+\frac{p}{D-p+1}\,g_{\mu[\alpha_{1}\!}\nabla_{\!|\kappa|}h^{\kappa}_{\ \alpha_{2}\dots\alpha_{p}]}\,,

(1)

where
D
stands for the number of spacetime dimensions.
The word

refers to the fact that such objects behave “nicely” under conformal transformations.
Namely, considering the Weyl transformation

g\to g^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\Omega^{2}}\,g\,,

(2)

the appropriately rescaled
p
-form
h^{\prime}
, obtained as,

h\to h^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\Omega^{p+1}}\,h\,,

(3)

remains a CKY tensor of the metric
g^{\prime}
.
It is largely this property, that is responsible for the results discussed in this paper.

Another useful property is that the CKY equation (
1
) is invariant under the
Hodge duality
.
Namely, the exterior derivative part transforms into the divergence part and vice versa.
This in particular implies that the dual
\star h
is a CKY tensor whenever
h
is.
However, one can do even more.
There are two special subclasses of CKY tensors of particular interest:
i)
Killing–Yano
(KY) tensors
Yano 1952
are CKYs with zero divergence part in (
1
), and ii)
closed conformal Killing–Yano
(CCKY) tensors are CKYs with vanishing exterior derivative part in (
1
).
As obvious from the above discussion, these subclasses transform one into another under Hodge duality.

From now on, let us set
D=4
and concentrate on the case of CKY 2-forms, relevant for black hole spacetimes in four dimensions.
The above defining equation (
1
) then significantly simplifies and reads

\nabla_{\!\mu}h_{\alpha\beta}=\nabla_{\![\mu}h_{\alpha\beta]}+{2}\,g_{\mu[\alpha}\xi_{\beta]}\;,

(4)

where we have defined the vector
{\xi}
as the normalized divergence of

, namely:

\xi_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{\!\kappa}\,h^{\kappa}_{\ \alpha}\;.

(5)

It can be shown, e.g.

, that such a vector obeys the following equation:

\nabla_{(\alpha}\,\xi_{\beta)}=\frac{1}{2}h_{(\alpha|\gamma|}R^{\gamma}{}_{\beta)}\,,

(6)

where
R_{\alpha\beta}
is the Ricci tensor.
Thus, whenever the spacetime is an Einstein space, such
\xi
is necessarily a Killing vector. However, in more general spacetimes this need not be the case.
We shall return to this question in connection with conformal-to-Carter metrics in Sec.
V
.
Similarly, one can also define another “dual” vector
\zeta
, corresponding to the dual CKY 2-form
k=\star h
, namely

\zeta_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{\!\kappa}\,k^{\kappa}_{\ \alpha}\;.

(7)

If

is a KY, CCKY tensor then

\zeta
vanishes, respectively.

As shown recently in

;
Lindström and Sarıoğlu 2023

In particular, having a CKY 2-form (
4

primary current

(8)

\nabla^{\mu}J^{h}_{\mu\nu}=J^{h}_{\nu}\,,

(9)

with the “

(10)

where
\xi
is the vector field (
5

is the Einstein tensor. The terms proportional to the cosmological constant

Although

\nabla_{\mu}J_{h}^{\mu}=0\,.

(11)

This can be used to define

{q_{\mbox{\tiny P}}[h]}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{S^{2}}{(\star{J}^{h})_{\mu\nu}}\,{\rm{d}}S^{\mu\nu}\,,

(12)

see
Hull et al. 2025

Here

is a
2

Notice, that from (
9

J^{h}_{\mu}
vanishes. However, for the spacetimes we consider it turns out that the radial component does vanish, which is enough for the desired conservation.

In Sec.
VIII
we shall give explicit examples of these charges for the two CKY tensors, namely
h
and
{k=\star h}
in the Kerr–BR and Schwarzschild–BR spacetimes (in which case indeed

and

Finally, having a CKY tensor, its square

(13)

is a

, obeying

(14)

where

(15)

When

More specifically, for a (null) geodesic with tangent vector

(16)

IV.2
Hidden symmetries

Similar to the PD spacetime, the OP spacetime does not admit the full PKY tensor, and only a weaker structure of the CKY 2-form remains present.

V.2
Candidates on Killing vectors

Let us now try to generate the
Killing tower
of isometries from the above hidden symmetry.
To start with, let us consider the associated with
h
vector
\xi
, (
5
), namely

\xi^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{\beta}h^{\beta\alpha}\,.

(60)

As noted in Sec.
II
, such a vector is guaranteed to satisfy the following equation:

\nabla_{(\alpha}\xi_{\beta)}=\frac{1}{2}h_{(\alpha|\gamma|}R^{\gamma}{}_{\beta)}\,.

(61)

Obviously, in an Einstein space the R.H.S. vanishes, and
\xi
is a Killing vector.
More interestingly, the R.H.S. vanishes in the PD spacetime, where the Ricci tensor is determined from the aligned electromagnetic field and the corresponding conformal factor takes the specific form
\Omega^{\mbox{\tiny(PD)}}
, given by (
34
).
As mentioned in Footnote
2
, this is because the principal tensor is also aligned with these null directions, so for such spacetimes these will anti-commute as matrices, see e.g. the discussion in
Frolov et al. 2017b
.

In the off-shell conformal spacetime, however, this is not the case, and we find

\xi=\Bigl(\Omega-\frac{p^{3}\,\Omega_{,p}+q^{3}\,\Omega_{,q}}{\rho^{2}}\Bigr)\partial_{\eta}+\frac{q\,\Omega_{,q}-p\,\Omega_{,p}}{\rho^{2}}\,\partial_{\sigma}\,.

(62)

Demanding that this is a Killing vector, i.e., of the form

\mbox{KV}=a\,\partial_{\eta}+b\,\partial_{\sigma}\,,

(63)

for some constants
a
and
b
, yields in general an inconsistent system of two PDEs for the conformal factor
\Omega
.
Namely, solving the two equations separately yields

\Omega=a+pq\,F\Bigl(\frac{p^{2}-q^{2}}{p^{2}q^{2}}\Bigr)\,,\quad\Omega=\frac{b}{2}(q^{2}-p^{2})+\tilde{F}(pq)\,,

(64)

with
F
and
\tilde{F}
arbitrary functions.
Obviously, for both non-trivial
a
and

there is no solution.

When
{b=0}
, the solution reads

{\Omega=a+c\,pq}\,,

(65)

where
{c=\mbox{const.}}
is an integration constant, seemingly generalizing the PD case.
However, it can be shown, see
Lemma 1
in Appendix
B
, that when both
a
and
c
are non-trivial, one can rescale the coordinates so that such
\Omega
can be brought into the PD form, (
34
).
When
a=0
and
b\neq 0
, we find that the system is inconsistent.
Finally, when
a=0=b
, we find (up to a constant scale that can be dropped)

\Omega=pq\,.

(66)

Although
\xi
is not a Killing vector as it vanishes in this case, it means that with this choice of conformal factor, the above
h
is actually a KY tensor (obtained from the PKY tensor
h^{\mbox{\tiny(C)}}
by the appropriate conformal transformation) and its dual is the PKY 2-form.
Thus, by the uniqueness result
Krtouš et al. 2008
, the spacetime has to be again Carter’s spacetime, just written in some different coordinate system.
That this is indeed the case is shown by
Lemma 2
in Appendix
B
, see also
Kubizňák and Krtouš 2007
.

Similarly, we may ask when the dual vector
\zeta
, (
7
),

\zeta^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{\!\beta}k^{\beta\alpha}\;,

(67)

is a Killing vector.
We find

\zeta=\frac{pq\,(p\,\Omega_{,q}-q\,\Omega_{,p})}{\rho^{2}}\partial_{\eta}+\frac{q\,\Omega_{,p}+p\,\Omega_{,q}}{\rho^{2}}\partial_{\sigma}\,.

(68)

Equating this to (
63
) separately yields

\Omega=\frac{a}{2}\frac{p^{2}-q^{2}}{pq}+F(pq)\,,\quad\Omega=b\,pq+\tilde{F}(pq)\,.

(69)

Obviously, for
a
and
b
non-trivial, the system is again inconsistent.
When
b=0
and
a\neq 0
it remains inconsistent.
When
a=0
and
b
is non-trivial, we find
{\Omega=c+b\,pq}
, where
{c=\hbox{const.}}
is an integration constant.
So, upon the due coordinate transformation, we are back to Carter’s spacetime (for
{c=0}
) or the Plebański–Demiański case
{(c\neq 0)}
.
Finally, when both
{a=0=b}
, we recover a constant conformal factor, returning back to the Carter class of spacetimes.
3
3

3

Note that the above results show that it is impossible to generate a new PKY tensor from the original Carter’s PKY tensor by conformal transformation—a fact related to the uniqueness theorem proved in
Krtouš et al. 2008

Let us next consider a vector field

(70)

In our case, however, we find

Finally, when

Similarly, we may consider the following vector:

Explicitly, we find:

\hat{\eta}=\frac{pq\,(p^{3}\Omega_{,p}+q^{3}\Omega_{,q})}{\rho^{2}\Omega^{2}}\,\partial_{\eta}+\frac{pq\,(p\,\Omega_{,p}-q\,\Omega_{,q})}{\rho^{2}\Omega^{2}}\,\partial_{\sigma}\,.

V.3
Novel spacetime

We have discovered that for the conformal factor

\Omega=\frac{pq}{B+A\,pq}\,,

(80)

where
A
and
B
are constants, we
have the full Killing tower
.
Specifically, we find the Killing vectors

\hat{\eta}=B\,\partial_{\eta}\,,\quad\eta=A\,\partial_{\sigma}\,,

(81)

while the other vector fields are (for
{A\neq 0\neq B}
) not Killing.
This is thus a very different construction from both the Carter one and the PD one.
It can be shown, see
Lemma 3
in Appendix
B
, that when both
A
and
B
are non-trivial, the above conformal factor can be brought to the following canonical form:

\Omega=\frac{pq}{1-pq}\,.

(82)

While we have checked that by choosing
Q=Q(q)
and
P=P(p)
such a spacetime cannot be a vacuum solution of Einstein equations, it may (perhaps) describe some interesting solution with matter that admits a hidden symmetry of the CKY 2-form together with the full tower of Killing vector fields derived from it.
Such a
novel spacetime
(N) is worth investigating in the future; for now, we turn to the separability of test particles and fields in conformal-to-Carter spacetimes.

VI
Separability structures

In this section we shall demonstrate separability of various test field or particle equations in the general off-shell conformal-to-Carter spacetimes (
51
), (
52
).
We note that similar observations, though with different motivations and a different form of conformal factor, were made for example in
Hamilton and McMaken 2022

See also
Acevedo and Breton 2025

VI.1
Massless Hamilton–Jacobi equation

The above hidden symmetries can be used for the separation of variables in the massless Hamilton–Jacobi equation

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x^{\mu}}\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x^{\nu}}=0\,,

(83)

where
S
is Hamilton’s principal function.
4
4

4

In principle, one might also consider a modified “conformal Hamilton–Jacobi equation”

g^{\mu\nu}\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x^{\mu}}\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x^{\nu}}+\frac{1}{6}R=0\,,

(84)

considered in
Gray et al. 2021
.
However, such an equation neither is truly conformally invariant, nor does it separate in our spacetime, that is why we do not consider it in our paper.
The reason as to why such an equation does not separate can be traced to the presence of an
{\cal R}
-factor
for the conformal wave equation, see (
96
) below.

First, the existence of the 2 Killing vectors
\partial_{\eta}
and
\partial_{\sigma}
ensures that the following quantities:

\displaystyle(\partial_{\eta})^{\mu}\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x^{\mu}}=-E\,,~~~(\partial_{\sigma})^{\mu}\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x^{\mu}}=L\,,

(85)

are conserved.
Moreover, the conformal Killing tensors (
56
) of the off shell spacetime (
51
) provide two additional constants of motion.
However, due to the relation (
57
) and the massless Hamilton–Jacobi equation (
83
), they are not independent.
Therefore, we consider only
Carter’s constant
(
16
) associated with
h
, i.e.

C=K_{(h)}^{\mu\nu}\,\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x^{\mu}}\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x^{\nu}}\,.

(86)

The fact that these constants of motion are at most quadratic in momentum, and in involution with respect to the Poisson bracket (see
Frolov et al. 2017a
and
Gray et al.
for details on the integrability), allow for an additive separation of variables for Hamilton’s principal function, namely

\displaystyle S=W_{\eta}(\eta)+W_{\sigma}(\sigma)+W_{q}(q)+W_{p}(p)\,.

(87)

These functions
W_{\mu}(x^{\mu})
depend on the one particular coordinate
x^{\mu}
(
\mu
here is just a label not an (abstract) index), as well as, (possibly all of) the constants of motion
\{E,L,C\}
.
Plugging this into (
85
) one immediately obtains

W_{\eta}=-E\,\eta\,,\qquad W_{\sigma}=L\,\sigma\,.

(88)

On the other hand, for
W_{q}
and
W_{p}
, making use of (
86
) and the explicit expression (
III.2
), we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\rho^{2}}\Big[

\displaystyle\ q^{2}\Big(\dfrac{(Eq^{2}+L)^{2}}{Q}-Q(W_{q}^{\prime})^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\ p^{2}\Big(\dfrac{(Ep^{2}-L)^{2}}{P}+P(\dot{W}_{p})^{2}\Big)\Big]=C\,,

(89)

which can be rearranged to give

\displaystyle P\,(\dot{W}_{p})^{2}

\displaystyle=C-\dfrac{(Ep^{2}-L)^{2}}{P}\,,

(90)

\displaystyle Q\,(W^{\prime}_{q})^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(Eq^{2}+L)^{2}}{Q}-C\,.

(91)

These are two first-order decoupled ODEs for
\dot{W}_{p}
and
W^{\prime}_{q}
.
One can check that (
90
) and (
91
) imply the Hamilton–Jacobi equation is satisfied.
That is, using the form of (
51
) we obtain

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x^{\mu}}\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x^{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(Ep^{2}-L)^{2}}{P}-\dfrac{(Eq^{2}+L)^{2}}{Q}+Q\,(W^{\prime}_{q})^{2}+P\,(\dot{W}_{p})^{2}

\displaystyle=C-C=0\,.

(92)

The obtained expressions can be used to find the tangent vector to null geodesics (momentum), upon using

\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x^{\mu}}=p_{\mu}=g_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^{\mu}}{d\lambda}\,,

(93)

where
\lambda
is an affine parameter.
In particular, this yields
{p_{\eta}=-E\,,\,p_{\sigma}=L}
, and one obtains the following expressions for the geodesic 4-velocity:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}\eta}{{\rm{d}}\lambda}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\Omega^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\Big[\dfrac{q^{2}(Eq^{2}+L)}{Q}-\dfrac{p^{2}(Ep^{2}-L)}{P}\Big]\,,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}\sigma}{{\rm{d}}\lambda}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\Omega^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\Big[-\dfrac{Eq^{2}+L}{Q}-\dfrac{Ep^{2}-L}{P}\Big]\,,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}q}{{\rm{d}}\lambda}

\displaystyle={\pm}\dfrac{\Omega^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\sqrt{(Eq^{2}+L)^{2}-CQ}\,,

(94)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}p}{{\rm{d}}\lambda}

\displaystyle={\pm}\dfrac{\Omega^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\sqrt{CP-(Ep^{2}-L)^{2}}\,,

where the signs in front of the square roots are independent.
These expressions can be further decoupled by introducing generalized ‘Mino time’
Mino 2003
.
The above expressions can also be used to calculate the black hole shadows
Perlick and Tsupko 2022
in the appropriate on shell spacetimes, e.g.
Wang et al. 2025
;
Zeng et al. 2025
;
Ali and Ghosh 2025
;
Vachher et al. 2025
.

VI.2
Conformal scalar field equation

Let us next turn to the scalar field equation.
For the metric (
51
) the usual massless Klein–Gordon equation
g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\psi=0
does not separate off-shell, when the Ricci scalar
R\neq 0
.
However, the conformal Klein–Gordon equation

\displaystyle\Box_{R}\,\psi=\Big(g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}-\dfrac{1}{6}R\Big)\psi=0\,,

(95)

is
{\cal R}
-separable — the scalar field admits a multiplicative separation of variables up to some arbitrary function of the coordinates
{\cal R}
— i.e.

\psi={\cal R}(x)X_{\eta}(\eta)X_{q}(q)X_{p}(p)X_{\sigma}(\sigma)\,,

(96)

where, if the function
\cal R
is chosen correctly, each of the functions
X_{i}
satisfy a decoupled ODE.
The
{\cal R}
-separability follows from the underlying symmetry operators presented in
Gray et al. 2020
;
Gray et al. 2021
for the conformal-to-Carter spacetime (
51
).

Generically, a conformal symmetry of operator of
\Box_{R}
is an operator
\cal O
, which satisfies

\Box_{R}\big({\cal O}(\psi)\big)=\widetilde{\cal O}\big(\Box_{R}(\psi)\big)\,,

(97)

for all members of the class of conformally related metrics
[g]=\{\omega^{2}g\,|\,\omega\in C^{\infty}(M)\}
and fields
[\psi]=\{\omega^{-1}\psi\,|\,\omega\in C^{\infty}(M)\}
, and for some related operator
\widetilde{\cal O}
.
These will preserve the kernel of the conformal wave equation, hence the solution space.

The classification of such operators was carried out in full generality
Michel 2014
, and in particular, for the off-shell Carter metric these operators can be written in terms of a Killing tensor
K
and Killing vectors
l

Gray et al. 2021
;
Michel 2014
:

\displaystyle{\cal L}(\psi)

\displaystyle=l^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\psi+\frac{1}{4}\psi\,\nabla_{\alpha}l^{\alpha}\,,

(98)

\displaystyle{\cal K}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\alpha}(K^{\alpha\beta}\,\nabla_{\beta}\psi)-\frac{1}{20}\psi\,\Box K^{\alpha}{}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{3}{10}R_{\alpha\beta}\,K^{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{30}R\,K^{\alpha}{}_{\alpha}+f\right)\psi\,,

(99)

where the scalar function
f
must satisfy the following geometric condition
Michel 2014
:

\nabla_{\alpha}f=-\frac{2}{15}\Bigl(C^{\beta}{}_{\gamma\delta\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}K^{\gamma\delta}-3K^{\gamma\delta}\nabla_{\beta}C^{\beta}{}_{\gamma\delta\alpha}\Bigr)\,,

(100)

where
C^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}
is the Weyl tensor.
This is met for the off-shell Carter metric and the explicit expression can be found in
Gray et al. 2021
.
If
f=0
then the symmetry operators can be written in terms of the conformal Killing vectors and conformal Killing tensors however, because this is not true for the Carter metric, the full Killing tensors are required for such operators and the separability follows by transforming the conformal wave equation into the Carter spacetime by rescaling by the conformal factor
\Omega
– this provides the function
{\cal R}(x)=\Omega(x)
.

We demonstrate this explicitly below.
Namely, if one substitutes an ansatz of the form

\displaystyle\psi=\Omega\,\psi_{0}\,e^{\mathrm{i}(\omega\,\eta+n\,\sigma)}\,.

(101)

where
\psi_{0}=\psi_{0}(p,q)
, and
\omega
and
n
are constants, not necessarily related to the physical frequencies and angular momenta (this follows from the fact that
\eta
and
\sigma
are not necessarily the proper time and angular coordinates, as discussed in
Ovcharenko and Podolský 2025
).
Then (
95
) becomes

\displaystyle(Q\psi_{0}^{\prime})^{\prime}

\displaystyle+(P\dot{\psi_{0}})^{\dot{}}+\dfrac{\psi_{0}}{6}(Q^{\prime\prime}+\ddot{P})+

\displaystyle+\psi_{0}\dfrac{(\omega q^{2}-n)^{2}}{Q}-\psi_{0}\dfrac{(\omega p^{2}+n)^{2}}{P}=0\,.

(102)

Obviously, this equation is separable, and by substitution

VI.5
Gravitational perturbations

The last field whose separability we would like to investigate is the gravitational perturbations.
In our further analysis, we will assume the corresponding source terms can be omitted.
While this assumption may seem unnatural, it was used to obtain the quasinormal modes of Kerr–Newman black holes (see
Kokkotas 1993
;
Berti and Kokkotas 2005
).
Furthermore, as was obtained in
Saha and Silva 2025
, variation of the matter field for the Kerr–Newman black hole does not change the frequencies of the quasinormal modes significantly and becomes important only near extremality.
Thus, keeping in mind that this “freezing” assumption is not fully correct for a coupled electromagnetic and gravitational field, we will use it throughout this subsection as a reasonable approximation.
Moreover, the coupled set of equations may not be separable, see
Dias et al. 2022
;
Dias et al. 2015
.

There are various ways to describe pure gravitational perturbations.
One consists of considering perturbations of the metric
{g_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}^{(0)}+\epsilon\,g_{\mu\nu}^{(1)}+{\cal O}(\epsilon)}
; by expanding field equations in
\epsilon
one obtains linear equations for the perturbations.
The problem for our work is that the resulting equations are not conformally invariant and, moreover, are gauge dependent.

Another way is to work in the Newman–Penrose formalism and consider perturbations not of the metric, but of the Weyl and optical scalars, namely assuming
{\Psi_{i}=\Psi_{i}^{(0)}+\epsilon\,\Psi_{i}^{(1)}+{\cal O}(\epsilon)}
.
We will use this method because for rotating metrics it was shown to be much simpler then the first one.

Let us consider the background metric to be the off-shell conformal-to-Carter metric (
51
).
This has several important algebraic properties and the corresponding field equations thus simplify significantly.
In particular, it is of type D, and the corresponding PNDs are geodesic and shear-free, see Eqs. (7) and (13) in
Ovcharenko and Podolský 2025
,

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}^{(0)}=\Psi_{1}^{(0)}=0=

\displaystyle\ \Psi_{3}^{(0)}=\Psi_{4}^{(0)}\,,

\displaystyle\kappa^{(0)}=\nu^{(0)}=0=

\displaystyle\ \sigma^{(0)}=\lambda^{(0)}\,.

(133)

Because such conditions are quite strong — and are the same as for the the on-shell PD spacetime — one can conveniently employ the results for the separability of the gravitational perturbations in the PD spacetime obtained previously by Dudley and Finley
Dudley and Finley 1977
.

Interestingly, to obtain the separable equations, instead of directly using the scalars
\Psi_{0}^{(1)}
and
\Psi_{4}^{(1)}
it is necessary to introduce new functions
\psi_{\pm 2}
such that

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}^{(1)}

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega\eta+n\sigma)}\dfrac{\Omega^{3}}{(q+\mathrm{i}p)^{2}}\,\psi_{-2}\,,

\displaystyle\Psi_{4}^{(1)}

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega\eta+n\sigma)}\dfrac{\Omega^{3}}{(q+\mathrm{i}p)^{2}}\,\psi_{2}\,.

(134)

Then the equations for gravitation perturbations become independent of the conformal factor, separable, and by making a factorization
{\psi_{\pm 2}=Q\,R_{\pm 2}\,X_{\pm 2}}
, one gets

\displaystyle(P\dot{X}_{\pm 2})^{\dot{}}+\Big[\dfrac{3}{2}\ddot{P}\pm 8\omega\,p-\dfrac{(\omega\,p^{2}+n\pm\dot{P})^{2}}{P}+\lambda\Big]X_{\pm 2}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle\dfrac{(Q^{3}R_{\pm 2}^{\prime})^{\prime}}{Q^{2}}+\Big[\dfrac{5}{2}Q^{\prime\prime}\pm 8\mathrm{i}\omega\,q+K\,\dfrac{K\mp 2\mathrm{i}\,Q^{\prime}}{Q}-\lambda\Big]R_{\pm 2}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(135)

where
\lambda
is the separation constant and
{K=\omega\,q^{2}-n}
(to obtain these equations, we have used a frame presented in Eq. (6) in
Ovcharenko and Podolský 2025
).

The important observation here is that the
equation for gravitational perturbations is conformally invariant
even though generally gravitational perturbations do not obey such a property (unlike the conformal Klein–Gordon or massless Dirac equation).
This specific property is related to the special algebraic properties of the metric (
33
), namely that the spacetime is of algebraic type D, and the corresponding PNDs are geodesic and shear-free. See
Araneda 2018
for a discussion of the underlying conformal invariance of the Teukolsky equation.

In summary, the equations (
135
) hold for the off-shell conformal-to-Carter metric (
51
).
Although this observation is quite straightforward from the construction in
Dudley and Finley 1977
, previously there were no other solutions within the off-shell PD class other than the on-shell PD solution.
Currently, given that the new class of OP metrics has been found, this observation gains its importance, as the results of
Dudley and Finley 1977
can be directly translated to this case (with the properly specified on-shell functions
Q

P
and
\Omega

Although the OP spacetime in the form (

Separability properties

Now, let us also briefly discuss how the test field or particle equations simplify for the non-twisting case.
The Hamilton–Jacobi equation (
92
) in this case becomes

\displaystyle\dfrac{L^{2}}{P}-\dfrac{E^{2}q^{2}}{\mathcal{Q}}+q^{2}\mathcal{Q}\,(W^{\prime})^{2}+P\,(\dot{W})^{2}=0\,.

(148)

Contraction of the conformal Killing tensors with the 4-momentum gives an analogue of the equation (
89

\displaystyle q^{2}\Big(\dfrac{E^{2}}{\mathcal{Q}}-\mathcal{Q}\,(W^{\prime})^{2}\Big)=C\,.

(149)

Thus, the equation of geodesic motion becomes separable, and we obtain

\displaystyle P\,(\dot{W})^{2}=C-\dfrac{L^{2}}{P}\,,\quad q^{2}\mathcal{Q}\,(W^{\prime})^{2}=\dfrac{E^{2}q^{2}}{\mathcal{Q}}-C\,.

(150)

The corresponding 4-velocities are thus given by

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}\eta}{{\rm{d}}\lambda}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\Omega^{2}}{\mathcal{Q}}E\,,\quad\frac{{\rm{d}}q}{{\rm{d}}\lambda}=\dfrac{\Omega^{2}}{q}\sqrt{E^{2}q^{2}-C\mathcal{Q}}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}\sigma}{{\rm{d}}\lambda}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\Omega^{2}}{P}\dfrac{L}{q^{2}}\,,\quad\frac{{\rm{d}}p}{{\rm{d}}\lambda}=\dfrac{\Omega^{2}}{q^{2}}\sqrt{CP-L^{2}}\,.

(151)

It can also be shown that the analogues of the Teukolsky-like equations (
136
)–(
137
) are

\displaystyle\Bigg[\dfrac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}p}\Big(P\dfrac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}p}\Big)+\dfrac{1}{6}(2s^{2}+1)\ddot{P}-\dfrac{(n\pm\frac{s}{2}\dot{P})^{2}}{P}+\lambda\Bigg]X_{\pm s}=0\,,

(152)

\displaystyle\Bigg[Q^{-s}\dfrac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}q}\Big(Q^{s+1}\dfrac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}q}\Big)+\dfrac{1}{6}(2s+1)(s+1)\,Q^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\hskip 79.66771pt\pm 4\mathrm{i}s\,\omega\,q+K\,\dfrac{K\mp\mathrm{i}s\,Q^{\prime}}{Q}-\lambda\Bigg]R_{\pm s}=0\,,

(153)

where now
{K=\omega\,q^{2}}
,
Q=q^{2}\mathcal{Q}
.

VIII
Two new special cases

Finally, it is worth discussing explicitly two important subcases of the OP spacetimes, namely the Kerr-BR and the Schwarzschild–BR metrics, which correspond to (rotating) black holes in equilibrium with a background magnetic field.
These are
exact solutions

We shall provide explicit forms of their hidden symmetries, as well as calculate the Penrose charges mentioned above, providing new physically motivated examples of these non-trivial charges.

IX
Conclusions

Motivated by the newly discovered class of type D spacetimes with non-aligned electromagnetic field
Podolský and Ovcharenko 2025
;
Ovcharenko and Podolský 2025
, in this paper, we have investigated the hidden symmetries and separability properties of the general off-shell conformal-to-Carter spacetimes (
51
),

g=\frac{1}{\Omega^{2}}\,g^{\mbox{\tiny(C)}}\,,

(183)

where
\Omega
is an
arbitrary conformal factor
and
g^{\mbox{\tiny(C)}}
is the off-shell Carter’s metric (
17
), with any
{Q=Q(q)}
and
{P=P(p)}
.
In particular, we have focused on spacetimes for which the conformal rescaling preserves the isometries/explicit symmetries, i.e.
{\Omega=\Omega(p,q)}
.
The corresponding hidden symmetries are of a
weaker
nature than for the Carter spacetime, as they posses only a
conformal
Killing–Yano tensor
h
which is no longer closed.
Unsurprisingly, we then find only conformal (not full) Killing tensors, from the square of
h
, and the separability properties carry through only for conformal/massless test field equations.
The situation is similar to the PD case where these weaker objects were already known, e.g.
Kubizňák and Krtouš 2007
.

However, our results go far beyond the previously discussed case of PD metrics and cover, in particular, the newly discovered OP spacetimes.
Interestingly, we have shown that for such spacetimes, and contrary to the Carter and PD case, the hidden symmetry no longer generates explicit symmetries in any straightforward way.

Among the conformal-to-Carter spacetimes, we have also discovered a new geometry for which the conformal factor takes a specific form (
80
), namely

\Omega=\frac{pq}{1-pq}\,,

(184)

and for which the isometries can be inferred.
It remains to be seen whether this new
geometrically preferred
metric remains to solve the Einstein equations with some physically well-motivated matter fields.

We have also explicitly demonstrated
separability
of various test field or particle equations with intrinsic conformal invariance (massless/conformally coupled) in spacetimes (
183
),
showing that there is a Teukolsky-type equation combining the spin 0, 1/2, and 2 fields.
This could prove useful for investigating the physical properties of the OP spacetimes, including (but not limited to) studying the shadows of, and gravitational waves on, the Kerr–BR black holes.
Upcoming work will show that the equations of motion for massless spinning particles separate in these spacetimes as a special case of
Gray et al.
.

Finally, we have shown that the OP spacetimes provide a unique testing-ground for studying the recently defined Penrose charges
Hull et al. 2025
whose existence is intrinsically related to hidden rather than explicit symmetries.
More specifically, we have calculated such charges for the Bertotti–Robinson, Schwarzchild–BR, and Kerr–BR spacetimes in the expansion of small external magnetic field
B
, observing that such (quasi-local) charges include the information about the magnetostatic energy.
As shown in the appendix, such charges are also able to detect the presence of NUT charges and acceleration.
The precise interpretation of these results, however, remains an important open question for future studies.

In closing, let us mention a few more possible future directions.
From a mathematical perspective, it would be interesting to try to fully characterize the conformal symmetry operators for the massless test fields in a unified language, working directly with the fields themselves.
Work towards this has been done in the Geroch–Held–Penrose (extension to the Newman–Penrose) formalism
Araneda 2018
but it is open how to do this when working directly with the fields.
Perhaps this can be done using the recent characterization of symmetry operators
Mei 2023
;
Gray and Kubizňák 2024
;
Arias et al.
.
Another direction is to look for degenerate limits of OP metrics in the sense of
Frolov et al. 2018b
, and to explore whether such spacetimes can be cast in some generalized Kerr–Schild form
Hassaine et al. 2025
;
Srinivasan 2025
.

A.2
Plebański–Demiański spacetime

The Plebański–Demiański spacetime (
33
) is on-shell, provided

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa+{\rm e}^{2}+{\rm g}^{2}-2m\,q+\epsilon q^{2}-2{\rm n}\,q^{3}-(\kappa+\Lambda/3)q^{4}\,,

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa+2{\rm n}\,p-\epsilon p^{2}+2m\,p^{3}-(\kappa+{\rm e}^{2}+{\rm g}^{2}+\Lambda/3)p^{4}\,.

Here, again
\Lambda
stands for the cosmological constant,
{\rm e}
is the electric charge parameter, and
{\rm g}
is the magnetic charge. The other 4 parameters
\{\kappa,\epsilon,m,{\rm n}\}
are related to mass, rotation, the NUT charge, and acceleration; see
Griffiths and Podolský 2006
;
Ovcharenko et al. 2025a
;
Ovcharenko et al. 2025b
for details.
One can also calculate the Penrose charges for the PD spacetime, i.e. by following the same procedure as for the Carter spacetime, i.e. transforming to Boyer–Lindquist coordinates.
In contrast to the standard Komar charges these are quasi-local quantities and so can be evaluated at a finite radius without needing to worry about the asymptotics.
However the resulting expressions are not very enlightening and so we do present them here.

On the other hand, in the subcase of accelerating charged black holes without the NUT parameter (i.e. the
C
-metric with a cosmological constant
\Lambda
), using the coordinate transformations and parameter redefinitions as in Section 3 and 4.2 of
Griffiths and Podolský 2006
, we find the relatively simple expressions

\displaystyle q_{\mbox{\tiny P}}[h]

\displaystyle=C\,m

\displaystyle+C\,({\rm e}^{2}+{\rm g}^{2})\,\frac{(a^{2}+r_{0}^{2})\arctan(r_{0}/a)-a\,r_{0}}{4a\,r_{0}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle q_{\mbox{\tiny P}}[k]

\displaystyle=-\alpha\,C\,ma

\displaystyle+\alpha\,C\,({\rm e}^{2}+{\rm g}^{2})\,\frac{(a^{2}+r_{0}^{2})\arctan(r_{0}/a)+a\,r_{0}}{4a^{2}}\,.

(196)

Here
C
is again the conicity parameter, the parameter
a
denotes Kerr-like rotation, and
\alpha
the acceleration.
These integrals are also done over slices of constant
t,r
.
Due to the subtleties concerning the regularization of the axis encoded in
C
and the asymptotics, it is unclear exactly how to interpret these general expressions.

Notice however, that in the
vacuum case

{{\rm e}=0={\rm g}}
,
(
A.2
) reduces to

\displaystyle q_{\mbox{\tiny P}}[h]=C\,m\,,\qquad q_{\mbox{\tiny P}}[k]=-\alpha\,C\,ma\,,

(197)

independently
of
the choice of
r_{0}
. Moreover, the CKY
h
generates the Killing vector
{\xi=\partial_{t}}
, while the CKY
k
generates the Killing vector
{\zeta=\alpha\,\partial_{\varphi}}
, cf. Table
1
.
Notice also that
{q_{\mbox{\tiny P}}[k]=0}
when
{\alpha=0}
.
This is because then the CKY
h
becomes closed, and therefore
k
becomes a KY tensor.
In this case the Killing vector it generates vanishes and thence there is no associated secondary charge cf. (
10
).
Thus, it once more seems reasonable to associate
q_{\mbox{\tiny P}}[h]
and
q_{\mbox{\tiny P}}[k]
with a ‘quasi-local mass’ and ‘angular momentum’, respectively, although the Komar-based equivalent asymptotic concepts are not well defined.
A proper analysis would require to compare these expressions with thermodynamic quantities, which are well understood in the asymptotically AdS case
Anabalón et al. 2019
.

The Ovcharenko–Podolský spacetime (

The Segal-Neretin semigroup of annuli
André G. Henriques, James E. Tener

Abstract
The Lie algebra of vector fields on S 1 S^{1} integrates to the Lie group of diffeomorphisms of S 1 S^{1} . But it is well known since the work of Segal and Neretin that there is no Lie group whose Lie algebra is the complexification of vector fields on S 1 S^{1} . A substitute for that non-existent group is provided by the complex semigroup whose elements are annuli : genus zero Riemann surfaces with two boundary circles parametrised by S 1 S^{1} . The group Diff ⁡ ( S 1 ) \mathrm{Diff}(S^{1}) sits at the boundary of that semigroup, and can be thought of as annuli which are completely “thin”, i.e. with empty interior. In this paper, we consider an enlargement of the semigroup of annuli, denoted Ann \Ann , where the annuli are allowed to be “partially thin”: their two boundary circles are allowed to touch each other along an arbitrary closed subset. We prove that every (partially thin) annulus A ∈ Ann A\in\Ann is the time-ordered exponential of a path with values in the cone of inward pointing complexified vector fields on S 1 S^{1} , and use that fact to construct a central extension 0 → ℂ × ℤ → A ​ n ~ ​ n → Ann → 0 0\to{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}\to\tAnn\to\Ann\to 0 that integrates the universal (Virasoro) central extension of 𝒳 ⁡ ( S 1 ) {\mathcal{X}}(S^{1}) . In later work, we will prove that every unitary positive energy representations of the Virasoro algebra integrates to a holomorphic representation of A ​ n ~ ​ n \tAnn by bounded operators on a Hilbert space.

The Virasoro Fréchet Lie algebra

\displaystyle\Vir:=\textstyle\bigg\{\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{n}L_{n}+kC\,\bigg|\,\begin{aligned} &a_{n},k\in{\mathbb{C}}\\[-2.84526pt]

Remarkably, there does exist a semigroup that contains

Elements of the semigroup are isomorphism classes of complex annuli equipped with parametrisations of the boundary components by the unit circle

Composition is given by gluing (“conformal welding”) along the parametrisations.

In Segal’s approach to CFT, the two-dimensional complex cobordism category
\mathrm{Cob}_{2}^{\mathit{conf}}
is a central object of study, and CFTs are defined as functors out of that cobordism category.
The semigroup of annuli then naturally appears as the topologically trivial part of the complex cobordism category.
More precisely, the manifold
S^{1}
is an object of the complex cobordism category.
The space
\Hom_{\mathrm{Cob}_{2}^{\mathit{conf}}}(S^{1},S^{1})
splits as a disjoint union according to genus and number of connected components of the cobordism, and
the semigroup of annuli is the trivial connected component:

\Ann^{\circ}\subset\Hom_{\mathrm{Cob}_{2}^{\mathit{conf}}}(S^{1},S^{1}).

In Segal’s original formulation, the complex cobordism category
\mathrm{Cob}_{2}^{\mathit{conf}}
did not have identity morphisms.
Identity morphisms can be formally added, and geometrically interpreted as annuli with thickness zero.

In this paper, we construct a version of the semigroup of annuli which contains annuli that are allowed to be ‘partially thin’, meaning that the two boundary circles are allowed to intersect along an arbitrary closed subset.

Both the semigroup of annuli
\Ann
and its central extension
\tAnn
are complex semigroups, meaning their tangent spaces are complex vector spaces, and the underlying spaces holomorphically embed into complex Fréchet vector spaces. But since neither
\Ann
nor its central extension are manifolds (they are some kind of infinite dimensional manifolds with corners), formulating this requires care. In this paper, we rely on the technology of complex diffeological spaces, in which a ‘complex structure’ is determined by the set of all holomorphic maps from finite-dimensional complex manifolds.

A drawback of this approach is that the complex diffeology ‘cannot see’ the tangent spaces to
\Ann
at non-interior points.
So we complement this with another diffeology, now based on smooth maps from finite-dimensional real manifolds with corners into
\Ann

\Ann
and its central extension are diffeological semigroups for these two flavours of diffeology. Specifically, we prove that the semigroup law, provided by conformal welding, is compatible with the diffeologies.

A drawback of the complex diffeology on the semigroup of annuli is that it doesn’t see that

is connected.
In order to illustrate this difficulty, consider the closed upper half-plane

Finally, we can make sense of smooth families of abstract annuli (parametrised by a manifold with corners). As is the case for

3
Conformal welding

Recall from (
4

We establish families versions of the above results, both for families indexed by complex manifolds, and for families indexed by real manifolds with corners.
We begin with conformal welding of discs.

\partial(S^{1}\times[0,1])\cup\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
, where

k(\theta,t):=\tfrac{f(\theta,t)}{z-h(\theta,t)}
.
The function

X
-holomorphic because

-holomorphic functions form an algebra and

is the composite of

We then calculate:

,
which establishes (
23
).

We are reduced to showing that the follows equation holds:

f(\theta_{0},t_{0})\,=\,\tfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\frac{f(\theta,t)}{z-h(\theta,t)}d(h(\theta,t)).

The right hand side is independent of

is smooth), so is enough to prove that

Recall that

. Changing variables one last time, we can rewrite our desired formula as

g(z)\,=\,\tfrac{1}{2\pi i}\,\,\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{C_{\varepsilon}}g(w)(z-w)^{-1}dw.

This holds true for any continuous function

∎

Corollary 4.10
.

Let

Proof.

The above construction does not quite work in families because we don’t know whether the required solutions to the Dirichlet problem depend smoothly on the annulus (note that the topology of the interior of the annulus may change throughout a family).

Proposition 4.15
.

There exists an assignment of a semi-framing

\displaystyle(S^{1}\times[0,1])\times\Ann\,\,

\displaystyle\to\,\quad{\underline{A}}

Proof.

Almost all the steps of the proof of Proposition

can be applied verbatim to the case of the universal family

, with two exceptions that requires some care: the existence of smooth partitions of unity, and the smoothness in families of solutions to the Dirichlet problem.

With regard to the existence of smooth partitions of unitary, the two sets that need to be separated by a smooth function are

, and the subset of

which is not transverse to

.
These are disjoint closed subsets

In order to ensure the smoothness in families of solutions to the Dirichlet problem, we modify the domain on which the Dirichlet problem is formulated so as to eliminate thin parts.

Since (

∎

Lemma 4.18

If

Let

Let

We may identify horizontal foliations of

Let

Given another horizontal foliation

Recall that

5
Central extension of the semigroup of annuli

The Virasoro Lie algebra

\{\sum a_{n}L_{n}+kC:a_{n},k\in{\mathbb{C}},\text{ finitely many $a_{n}$ non-zero}\}
is
spanned by
\{L_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
C
, and has commutation relations

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{n+m}+\frac{C}{12}(m^{3}-m)\delta_{n+m,0}.

Letting
\mathrm{Witt}
be the Lie algebra of complexified vector fields
f(z)\partial_{z}
on the circle, with
f
a Laurent polynomial,
we then have a well-known short exact sequence (a central extension) of Lie algebras:

0\to{\mathbb{C}}\to\Vir\to\mathrm{Witt}\to 0

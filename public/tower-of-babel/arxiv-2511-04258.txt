Counting Patterns in Degenerate Graphs in Constant Space
Balagopal Komarath Department of Computer Science IIT Gandhinagar, India bkomarath@iitgn.ac.in Anant Kumar Department of Computer Science IIT Gandhinagar, India kumar_anant@iitgn.ac.in Akash Pareek Department of Computer Science Université Libre de Bruxelles, Belgium akash.pareek@ulb.be

Abstract
For an arbitrary, fixed graph (pattern graph), we study the algorithmic complexity of counting homomorphisms, subgraph isomorphisms, and induced subgraph isomorphisms from the pattern graph to n n -vertex, d d -degenerate graphs as input. Recent work by Bressan (Algorithmica, 2021) has shown that this problem has efficient dynamic programming algorithms using a graph parameter called DAG treewidth. Bressan used DAG treewidth to design a fast algorithm for counting homomorphisms, subgraph isomorphisms, and induced subgraph isomorphisms that use polynomial space. Bera, Gishboliner, Levanzov, Seshadhri, and Shapira (SODA, 2021) provided a characterization of graphs with DAG treewidth one. In this paper, we introduce a new graph parameter called DAG treedepth and show that it yields efficient divide and conquer algorithms that use only constant space (in the unit-cost RAM model). Specifically, we show: • An algorithm for counting subgraphs isomorphic to sparse pattern graphs using only constant space. • We derive an induced minor-based characterization for graphs of DAG treedepth up to two. • For pattern graphs upto nine vertices, the induced subgraphs can be counted in O ⁡ ( n 3 ) O(n^{3}) time using constant space. • An algorithm for counting induced subgraphs that matches the running time given by Bressan but only uses constant space. Apart from the DAG treedepth result, we also focus on DAG treewidth. For DAG treewidth, we show that we can count homomorphisms, subgraph isomorphisms, and induced subgraph isomorphisms faster than Bressan’s algorithm (2021). We further show that for all pattern graphs up to 11 vertices, we can count induced subgraphs in quadratic time. Beyond these algorithmic results, we also analyze DAG treedepth structurally and compare it with treedepth. We also show that DAG treedepth and DAG treewidth are upper- and lower-bounded by treedepth and treewidth of graphs from an associated family of graphs.

Introduction

For simple, undirected graphs
G
(host) and

(pattern), we consider the problem of counting the number of occurrences of
H
in

under the following three fundamental notions of containment.

1.

The number of subgraphs of

\{(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})\mid(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})\cong G,V^{\prime}\subseteq V(G),E^{\prime}\subseteq E(G)\}

2.

The number of induced subgraphs of

\{G[S]\mid S\subseteq V(G)\}

is the subgraph of

3.

The number of homomorphisms from

to
G
. That is, the cardinality of
\{\phi:V(H)\mapsto V(G)\mid\{u,v\}\in E(H)\text{ if and only if }\{\phi(u),\phi(v)\}\in E(G)\text{ for all }u,v\}
.

Counting subgraphs and homomorphisms is at the heart of many problems in combinatorics, database theory, and network analysis

,

,

,

,

However, in many real-world settings, host graphs are not arbitrary but exhibit additional structure such as sparsity or bounded degeneracy

[

]

Theorem 1

Bressan’s algorithm for counting homomorphisms is based on a dynamic programming (DP) approach, which is similar to treewidth-based DP algorithms. By analyzing DAG treewidth structurally, Bressan
[
B21
]
further showed that, for any

,

Despite this progress, the study of quadratic-time algorithms for bounded-degeneracy graphs remains relatively unexplored. One reason could be that the structural understanding of DAG treewidth quickly becomes intractable. As we show later in the paper, even deciding whether a DAG

Theorem 2

Let

This hardness result suggests that DAG treewidth may not be the right parameter for capturing tractable classes beyond linear time in bounded-degeneracy graphs. Thereofore, a natural question is whether or not there is an analogue to

for counting homomorphisms in

-degenerate graphs. To this end, we aim to design a divide-and-conquer algorithm that operates in constant space. We consider the pattern graph and degeneracy of host graph as fixed. 1.2
Related work

Subgraph counting is a fundamental problem in graph algorithms, with its complexity and tractability heavily influenced by the structural properties of the host graph. In
nowhere dense
and
bounded degeneracy
graphs, several works have established exact and approximate algorithms alongside complexity dichotomies. A recent dichotomy hierarchy precisely characterizes linear-time subgraph counting in such graphs
[
PPS25
]
. In the dense graph regime, Bressan et al.
[
BPS21
]
classified pattern counting complexity using DAG-treewidth, providing a sharp dichotomy for somewhere dense graph classes
[
BGM23
]
, while related work has addressed the complexity of counting homomorphic cycles in degenerate graphs
[
GLY23
]
, and pattern counting in directed graphs parameterized by outdegree
[
BLR23
,
BR22
]
. Recent studies have also considered counting patterns when the host graph is sparse
[
KKMS23
]
. For approximate counting, several works have proposed efficient approximation algorithms for subgraph counting problems, including a general approximation framework for sparse graphs
[
LPSXZ25
]
, and fast approximation algorithms specifically for triangle counting in streaming and distributed settings
[
BS20
]
. Beyond graphs, the study of subhypergraph counting has recently gained traction. In
[
BBDRW25
]
, the authors extended the homomorphism basis framework to hypergraphs, while
[
PPS25b
]
characterized the class of pattern hypergraphs
H
that can be counted in near-linear time when the host graph
G
is a hypergraph.

Definition 1

(Graph Homomorphism)
Given two graphs

We now recall several fundamental graph-theoretic parameters, including treewidth, treedepth, and a few others.

Definition 2

(Tree Decomposition)
A

of a graph

is a pair
(T,\{B(t)\}_{t\in V(T)})
, where
T
is a tree and each node

is assigned a subset

, called a

A tree decomposition of a graph

has the following properties:

•

Connectivity Property:
For every vertex

\{t\in V(T)\mid v\in B(t)\}
forms a connected component in

.

•

Edge Property:
For every edge

, there exists a node

such that both

and

are in

.

Definition 3

(Treewidth)
Let the width of a tree decomposition be defined as
\max_{t\in V(T)}|B(t)|-1.
Then, the

of a graph

, denoted as
\mathrm{tw}(G)
, is the minimum width over all possible tree decompositions of

.

Definition 4

(Elimination Tree)
An

(T,r)
, where

is the root, and each subtree rooted at a child of

is an elimination tree of a connected component of the graph

. For an empty graph (i.e., a graph with no vertices), the elimination tree is defined to be empty.

The
depth
of an elimination tree
(T,r)
is the length (i.e., the number of vertices) of the longest path from the root
r
to any leaf in the tree.

Definition 5

(Treedepth)
The

of a graph
G
, denoted as
\mathsf{td}(G)
, is the minimum depth among all possible elimination trees of
G
.

Definition 6

(Edge Contraction)
An

is an operation in which an edge
(u,v)
is removed from the graph and its two vertices
u
and
v
are merged into a single vertex
uv
. All edges incident to
u
or
v
are now considered incident to the new vertex

.

Definition 7

(Minor and Induced Minor)
A graph

is said to be a

if

by deleting vertices, deleting edges, and contracting edges. A graph

.

Let

be a pattern graph and

be the host graph. We denote the number of homomorphism from

to

, number of subgraphs of

in

Definition 8

(Spasm)
Let

denote the set of all independent sets in a graph

. For any

, let

be the graph obtained by merging all the vertices in

Note that for any pattern graph

Given an undirected graph

Definition 14

Let
\vec{H}
be a DAG. For each non-source vertex in

We will later demonstrate how to utilize this family of graphs to derive several bounds for DAG treedepth and DAG treewidth. In the following section, we define our new parameter named DAG treedepth and prove all the results related to it.

3
DAG Treedepth

In
[
B21
]
, Bressan introduced DAG treewidth
\mathrm{dtw}
), a structural parameter analogous to treewidth, and used it to design efficient algorithms for computing
\mathrm{hom}(H,G)
,
\mathrm{sub}(H,G)
, and
\mathrm{ind}(H,G)
. Motivated by this, we define a related parameter called DAG treedepth (
\mathrm{dtd}
), analogous to treedepth. While DAG treewidth enables the design of fast algorithms but suffers from hardness constraints (
Theorem

), DAG treedepth allows the design of constant-space algorithms and does not exhibit the same intractability barriers.

In this section, we first formally define DAG treedepth and present several of its properties that may be of independent interest. In
Section

3.2
, we use these properties to identify obstruction sets for DAG treedepth 0, 1, and 2. In
Section

3.3
, we provide several application of DAG treedepth. In
Section

3.4
, we leverage these to compute
\mathrm{hom}(H,G)
,
\mathrm{sub}(H,G)
, and
\mathrm{ind}(H,G)
using constant space, improving the space complexity of the fast algorithms of
[
B21
]
from polynomial to constant.

Definition 15

Let
H
be any DAG. A
DAG elimination forest
of
H
is defined as a collection of rooted trees, constructed recursively as follows:

•

If
H
is empty, the forest is empty.

•

If
H
is a single source, the forest is a single-node tree.

•

If the underlying undirected graph of

is disconnected, then construct DAG elimination trees for each connected component and their union is a DAG elimination forest for

•

Otherwise,
H

arbitrarily, delete that source and all vertices reachable from it in

. The root of the tree is

and its sub-trees are the trees in the DAG elimination forest for the remaining DAG.

The
DAG treedepth
of a DAG
H
is defined as the minimum depth of any elimination forest constructed in the above fashion.

Remark 1

Notice that the definition of DAG treedepth is analogous to the definition of treedepth except that at each step we can delete a source and all non-sources reachable from it instead of only a single node. For DAG treedepth, the nodes of the DAG elimination forest correspond exactly to the sources in the DAG.

4
DAG Treewidth

In this section, we first establish several fundamental properties of DAG treewidth. Building upon these we prove that for any DAG
H
with
k
vertices,
\mathrm{dtw}(H)\leq\frac{k}{5}+3
, and leverage this bound to derive improved runtime for computing
\mathrm{hom}(H,G)
,
\mathrm{sub}(H,G)
, and
\mathrm{ind}(H,G)
. We further prove a conjecture recently posed by
[
PPS24
]
on 10-vertex pattern graph
H
. Along the way, we obtain several additional structural bounds which may be of independent interest. To this end, we first define the DAG treewidth.

Definition 17

(DAG Tree Decomposition)
Let
\vec{H}
be a DAG with a set of sources
S
. A
DAG tree decomposition
of
\vec{H}
is a rooted tree
T=(\mathcal{B},E)
, where each node
B\in\mathcal{B}
is a
bag
consisting of a subset of sources from
S
, i.e.,
B\subseteq S
, and the following properties are satisfied:

•

Coverage:
Every source appears in at least one bag, i.e.,
\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}B=S
.

•

Reachability Intersection Property:
For any three bags
B,B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
, if
B
lies on the unique path between
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
in the tree
T
, then

\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{R}_{H}(B).

Definition 18

(DAG Treewidth)
Let the
DAG width
of a DAG tree decomposition
T=(\mathcal{B},E)
be defined as the size of the largest bag in
T
, i.e., the maximum number of sources in any bag. Then, the
DAG treewidth
of a directed acyclic graph
\vec{H}
is the minimum DAG width over all possible DAG tree decompositions of
\vec{H}
. For an undirected graph
H
, the DAG treewidth of
H
is the maximum of the DAG treewidth of all acyclic orientations of
\vec{H}
. We denote the DAG treewidth of a graph
\vec{H}
by
dtw(\vec{H})
.

Observation 2

Let
\vec{H}
be a directed acyclic graph (DAG) with a fixed orientation. Suppose there are two sources
u
and
v
such that there exists a vertex
w\in\mathrm{R}_{H}(u)\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(v)
, and
w\notin\mathrm{R}_{H}(s_{i})
for any other source
s_{i}\neq u,v
. Then, it is easy to see that in any DAG tree decomposition of
\vec{H}
, the sources
u
and
v
must appear either in the same bag or in adjacent bags.

Observation 3

As edge contractions do not increase treewidth, we have
\mathrm{tw}(G_{S})\leq\mathrm{tw}(\textsc{Bip}(\vec{H}))
.

Let
T=(\mathcal{B},E)
be a DAG tree decomposition. We denote by
B_{leaf}
a leaf node of the tree
T
. For any node
B\in\mathcal{B}
, we denote its parent by
Parent
(B)
. We denote the root of the tree
T
by
\textsc{Root}(T)
. For any two nodes
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
, the unique path between
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
in
T
is defined as
T(B_{1},B_{2})
.

4.1
Properties of DAG Treewidth:

In this section, we establish several important properties of treewidth and DAG treewidth (
\mathrm{dtw}(\vec{H})
), along with important relationships between them. By definition, the DAG treewidth of an undirected graph is defined as the maximum DAG treewidth taken over all its acyclic orientations. Consequently, results derived for DAGs can be naturally extended to undirected graphs by considering all possible acyclic orientations. Therefore, any property proven for a fixed acyclic orientation of a DAG applies to the corresponding undirected graph as well. However, in
Theorem

27
(which contracts a single edge, reducing the number of vertices by one),
Lemma

10
(which compares treewidth of undirected graphs), and
Lemma

11
(which introduces a new vertex on every edge (known as a subdivision vertex)), it is necessary to account for all acyclic orientations. For this reason, we present the proofs of these lemmas in the undirected setting.

Treewidth is a well-studied structural parameter that plays a central role in the design of dynamic programming algorithms for many NP-complete problems on graphs of bounded treewidth. In particular, for a pattern graph
H
with treewidth
t
, the number of homomorphisms from
H
to a host graph
G
with
n=|V(G)|
can be computed in time
n^{O(t)}
.

Bressan
[
B21
]
introduced DAG treewidth to enable near-linear time algorithms for counting homomorphisms from certain graphs, such as cliques and complete bipartite graphs, whose treewidth is unbounded, but whose DAG treewidth is just 1. This highlights DAG treewidth as a powerful and relevant parameter for homomorphism counting. But here we derive a hardness result for DAG treewidth. To this end we show the following lemma.

Lemma 7

Let
H
be a DAG with the set of sources
S
and non-sources
T
.
For each source
s\in S
, let
R(s)\subseteq T
denote the set of vertices reachable from
s
.
Construct a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
whose vertex set is
T
and whose hyperedge set is

\mathcal{E}=\{R(s)\mid s\in S\}
.
Then the DAG treewidth of
H
equals the generalized hypertree width of
\mathcal{H}
, i.e.,

\operatorname{dtw}(H)=\operatorname{ghw}(\mathcal{H}).

Proof.

(
dtd

\Rightarrow

ghd
)
Let
(T_{\tau},\{\beta(t)\}_{t\in V(T_{\tau})})
be a DAG tree decomposition of
H
.
For each node
t
, construct a GHD node with

\chi(t)=\beta(t)\cap T
and

\lambda(t)=\{R(s):s\in\beta(t)\cap S\}
.
The coverage and connectedness conditions of a GHD follow directly from those of the DTD.
Since each source
s
corresponds to a single hyperedge
R(s)
, the width (measured by the number of sources/hyperedges) is preserved.
Hence,
\operatorname{ghw}(\mathcal{H})\leq\operatorname{dtw}(H)
.

(
ghd

\Rightarrow

dtd
)
Conversely, let
(T_{\tau},\{(\chi(t),\lambda(t))\}_{t\in V(T_{\tau})})
be a generalized hypertree decomposition of
\mathcal{H}
.
We obtain a directed tree decomposition of
G
by replacing each hyperedge
R(s)\in\lambda(t)
with its corresponding source
s
.
Formally, define
\beta(t)=\chi(t)\cup\{s\in S:R(s)\in\lambda(t)\}
.
The coverage and connectedness properties of a DTD follow from those of the GHD, and the width is again preserved.
Hence,
\operatorname{dtw}(H)\leq\operatorname{ghw}(\mathcal{H})
. Combining both directions yields
\operatorname{dtw}(H)=\operatorname{ghw}(\mathcal{H})
.
∎

To obtain hardness result for DAG treewidth, we use the result of
[
FGP18
]
, which shows that
ghw(\mathcal{H}\leq 2)
is NP-complete. To this end we have the following result.

Theorem 26

Let
H
be a DAG, then deciding whether
dtw(H)\leq 2
is NP-complete.

Proof.

The prove follows using
Lemma

7
and the fact that deciding whether
ghw(\mathcal{H}\leq 2)
is NP-complete.
∎

Next, as the relationship between treewidth and DAG treewidth remains relatively unexplored, we aim to establish a more concrete connection between these two parameters. Leveraging the construction
\mathcal{G}
defined in
Section

2.1
, we prove that there exists graphs
G_{1},G_{2}\in\mathcal{G}
such that the following bounds hold:
\frac{\mathrm{dtw}(G_{1})+1}{2}\leq\mathrm{dtw}(\vec{H})\leq\mathrm{dtw}(G_{2})+1.
To this end in
Lemma

we show that
dtw{\vec{H}\leq tw(G_{S})}+1
and in

we show that
dtw(\vec{H})\geq\frac{tw(G_{S})+1}{2}.

Lemma 8

For a given DAG
\vec{H}
there exist a
G_{S}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
dtw(\vec{H})\leq tw(G_{S})+1
.

Proof.

T
be a tree decomposition of the graph
G_{S}
. From the construction described in
Section

2.1
, we know that
V(G_{S})=S
, where
S
is the set of sources in the DAG
\vec{H}
. By the definition of a tree decomposition, each bag in
T
is a subset of the vertex set
V(G_{S})=S
, and the union of all bags covers
S
. Thus,
T
satisfies the
coverage property
of the DAG-tree decomposition (DTD) (see
Definition

17
).

To complete the proof of the lemma, it remains to show that
T
also satisfies the
reachability intersection property
. Specifically, for all bags
B,B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
, if
B
lies on the unique path between
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
in the tree
T
, then we must have:

\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{R}_{H}(B).

To prove this, we define:
Y_{1}=B_{1}\setminus B_{2}
and
Y_{2}=B_{2}\setminus B_{1}
. Since
T
is a tree decomposition, the
connectivity property
implies that for any vertex
v\in B_{1}\cap B_{2}
, all bags containing
v
must lie along the path between
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, which includes
B
. Therefore,
B_{1}\cap B_{2}\subseteq B
, and consequently:

\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{1}\cap B_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{R}_{H}(B).

Thus, to prove the reachability intersection condition, it is sufficient to consider the contributions from
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
. This leads us to two cases:

1.

\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{2})=\emptyset
.

2.

\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{2})\neq\emptyset
.

For both cases, we want to show that the reachability condition holds.

Case 1:
\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{2})=\emptyset

We know that
\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{1})=\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{1}\cup(B_{1}\cap B_{2})),
and
\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{2})=\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{2}\cup(B_{1}\cap B_{2})).

Taking the intersection we have,

\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{2})=\left(\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{2})\right)\cup\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{1}\cap B_{2}).

Since
\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{2})=\emptyset
by assumption, the intersection simplifies to:

\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{2})=\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{1}\cap B_{2}).

From the connectivity property of tree decompositions,
B_{1}\cap B_{2}\subseteq B
, so:

\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{1}\cap B_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{R}_{H}(B).

Thus, the reachability condition holds in this case.

Case 2:
\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{2})\neq\emptyset

This is the more involved case where the reachability sets of
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
overlap. Our goal remains to show that every vertex in
\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{2})
is also in
\mathrm{R}_{H}(B)
.

Suppose there exist sources
u\in Y_{1}
and
v\in Y_{2}
such that
\mathrm{R}_{H}(u)\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(v)\neq\emptyset
. We consider two subcases based on how the common reachable non-source vertices are connected in the graph
G_{S}
.

(A)

There exists a non-source vertex that is reachable only from
u
and
v
:

From the construction of the graph
G_{S}
, we know that in such a case, the edges connecting this non-source vertex to
u
and
v
would be contracted. This results in an edge
\{u,v\}\in E(G_{S})
, implying that
T
, the tree decomposition of
G_{S}
, contains a bag with both
u
and
v
.

(B)

The common reachable non-source vertices are also reachable from some other set of sources:

If, for any such non-source vertex in
\mathrm{R}_{H}(u)\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(v)
, the edge contractions in
G_{S}
were performed using
u
and
v
, then again
\{u,v\}\in E(G_{S})
, and
T
contains a bag with both
u
and
v
.

However, consider the case where edge contractions were performed using a different set of sources, say, a set
\{S^{\prime}\}
such that each non-source vertex in
\mathrm{R}_{H}(u)\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(v)
is reachable from some source in
S^{\prime}
. These sources in
S^{\prime}
are then connected via contractions to the non-sources and become common neighbors of both
u
and
v
in
G_{S}
. As a result, the tree decomposition
T
must include intermediate bags containing these shared neighbors, which lie on the path between
u
and
v
in
T
.

Since
u
and
v
were chosen arbitrarily from
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
, this argument extends to show that all shared reachable vertices in
\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(Y_{2})
must be included in the reachability of some intermediate bag
B
on the path between
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
. Therefore,

\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{1})\cap\mathrm{R}_{H}(B_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{R}_{H}(B),

for all such bags
B
on the path from
B_{1}
to
B_{2}
in
T
.

This shows that
T
satisfies both the coverage and the reachability intersection properties required for a valid DAG-tree decomposition. Finally, by definition, the DAG-treewidth of
\vec{H}
is the maximum size of any bag in the DAG-tree decomposition. Since the treewidth of
G_{S}
is one less than the size of its largest bag, we conclude that
\mathrm{dtw}(\vec{H})\leq\mathrm{tw}(G_{S})+1.

∎

3
and
Lemma

we get the following remark:

Remark 4

For a given DAG
\vec{H}
and corresponding
\textsc{Bip}(\vec{H})
,
dtw(H)\leq tw(\textsc{Bip}(\vec{H}))+1
.

Remark 5

The above bound is tight. For example, consider the cycle graph
C_{5}
. For any acyclic orientation of
C_{5}
, the DAG treewidth is
\mathrm{dtw}(C_{5})=1
, while the treewidth is
\mathrm{tw}(C_{5})=2
.

For a more general case, consider the graph
G
obtained by subdividing every edge of the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
exactly once. Use an orientation where the original vertices have out-degree only (i.e., all original vertices are sources), and the newly added subdivision vertices are non-sources.

In this construction, each vertex in one part of the bipartite graph has a unique reachable non-source shared with every vertex in the opposite part. By
2
, all sources from one side must appear together (or in adjacent bags) in any DAG tree decomposition. Hence, any bag must contain at least
n
source vertices, out of the total
2n
sources. This gives
\mathrm{dtw}(G)\geq n
.

Moreover, we can construct a valid DAG tree decomposition by putting all sources from one side in the root bag and attaching one leaf bag for each source from the other side. This shows that
\mathrm{dtw}(G)=n
, matching the lower bound.

Lemma 9

For a given DAG
\vec{H}
and a corresponding set of graphs
\mathcal{G}
, there exist
G_{S}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
dtw(\vec{H})\geq\frac{tw(G_{S})+1}{2}.

Proof.

Let
\vec{H}
be a fixed acyclic orientation, and let
T=(\mathcal{B},E)
be a DAG tree decomposition (DTD) of
\vec{H}
. Let
G_{S}
be the graph obtained from
\vec{H}
using the construction described in
Section

2.1
. Note that the vertex set of
G_{S}
consists of the source vertices of
\vec{H}
. Since each bag in
T
contains only source vertices and the union of all bags equals
V(G_{S})
, the first condition of a tree decomposition for
G_{S}
is satisfied.

To complete the construction of
G_{S}
, we perform a sequence of edge contractions on
\textsc{Bip}(\vec{H})
, the bipartite graph built from
\vec{H}
as described earlier. The contraction process is guided by the structure of
T
, proceeding in a bottom-up fashion.

For each leaf node
u\in B_{\text{leaf}}
in
T
and its parent
v=\textsc{Parent}(u)
, if
R(u)\cap R(v)\neq\emptyset
, we contract the non-source vertices in this intersection with
v
. In the next step, we consider the grandparent bag
B=\textsc{Parent}(v)
and perform edge contractions for non-source vertices in
(R(B)\cap R(v))\setminus(R(B)\cap R(u)),
as contractions involving vertices in
R(B)\cap R(u)
have already been completed. This recursive process continues until all contractions are completed. We now claim the following:

Claim 1

If
u
and
v
belong to the same bag or to adjacent bags in
T
, then
\{u,v\}\in E(G_{S})
.

Proof.

Suppose, for contradiction, that
\{u,v\}\in E(G_{S})
, but
u
and
v
do not appear in the same or adjacent bags in
T
. Let
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
be bags containing
u
and
v
, respectively, and consider the unique path
T(B_{1},B_{2})
. Let
B\in T(B_{1},B_{2})
be a bag that contains neither
u
nor
v
. Since
T
is a valid DAG tree decomposition, it satisfies the reachability condition, implying that there exists a minimal set of source vertices
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\}\subseteq B
such that:

R(u)\cap R(v)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\left(R(u)\cap R(u_{i})\right).

Due to edge contraction, each
u_{i}
contracts with the non-source vertices in its reachability set. Consequently,
\{u,u_{i}\}
and
\{v,u_{i}\}
are edges in
G_{S}
, implying that
\{u,v\}\notin E(G_{S})
, a contradiction.
∎

Next, we verify the

of the tree decomposition. Suppose a source vertex
u
appears in two bags
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, but not in any bag along the path
T(B_{1},B_{2})\setminus\{B_{1},B_{2}\}
. From the reachability property of
T
, we know:

R(B_{1})\cap R(B_{2})\subseteq R(B),

for any intermediate bag
B
. Since
R(u)\subseteq R(B_{1})\cap R(B_{2})
, we must have
R(u)\subseteq R(B)
as well. Therefore, there exists a minimal set of source vertices
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\}
such that:

R(u)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}R(u_{i}).

This implies that for every non-source vertex
w\in R(u)
, there exists some
u_{i}\neq u
such that
w\in R(u_{i})
, allowing the contraction of
w
with
u_{i}
. As a result, all of
u
’s neighbors in
G_{S}
are contained in
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\}
, and so
u
can safely be removed from
B_{2}
, maintaining the connectivity condition.

We now verify the
second condition
of the tree decomposition for
G_{S}
, namely, that for every edge
\{u,v\}\in E(G_{S})
, there exists a bag that contains both
u
and
v
. From
1
, such edges arise only when
u
and
v
are in the same or adjacent bags in
T
. We construct a new tree decomposition
T^{\prime}
from
T
by adding for each bag
B\in T
its parent
\textsc{Parent}(B)
as an additional bag in
T^{\prime}
. Let
\textsc{Root}(T^{\prime})=\textsc{Root}(T)
, this construction guarantees that for every edge
\{u,v\}\in E(G_{S})
, there exists a bag in
T^{\prime}
containing both vertices.

Let
k
be the maximum bag size in
T
. Then each new bag in
T^{\prime}
has size at most
2k
, and hence
\mathrm{tw}(G_{S})\leq 2k-1.
Therefore,
k\geq\frac{\mathrm{tw}(G_{S})+1}{2}.
Since
k
is the width of the DAG-tree decomposition
T
, this completes the proof.
∎

Remark 6

The above bound is tight. Consider the graph
G
obtained by subdividing every edge of
K_{5}
. We orient all original vertices to point outward, making them sources. For each pair of original vertices, there exists a unique non-source vertex reachable from both the original vertices. By

.

The following lemma shows that
\mathrm{dtw}(\vec{H})\leq\mathrm{tw}(H)+1
. This bound suggests that the DAG treewidth of a graph is never significantly worse than its treewidth.

Lemma 10

For any undirected graph
H
,
\vec{H}
is any acyclic orientation of
H
. Then,
\mathrm{dtw}(\vec{H})\leq\mathrm{tw}(H)+1.

Proof.

Let
T
be a tree decomposition of
H
where
\mathrm{tw}(H)=k
. Fix any arbitrary acyclic orientation of
\vec{H}
. Observe that every source in
\vec{H}
must appear in some bag of
T
, since all vertices are covered by the decomposition.

Now, consider any pair of source vertices
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
such that their reachability sets intersect, i.e.,
R(s_{i})\cap R(s_{j})\neq\emptyset
. Suppose
s_{i}\in B_{1}
and
s_{j}\in B_{2}
, where
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are bags in
T
. Let
T(B_{1},B_{2})
denote the unique path between
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
in the tree decomposition. If
R(s_{i})\cap R(s_{j})\subseteq R(B)
for all intermediate bags
B\in T(B_{1},B_{2})
, then the reachability condition is already satisfied. Otherwise, let
u\in R(s_{i})\cap R(s_{j})
be a vertex that is not contained in any such intermediate bag.

Since
u\in R(s_{i})\cap R(s_{j})
, there exists a directed path from
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
to
u
, consisting only of non-source vertices:

s_{i}\rightarrow u_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow u\leftarrow u^{\prime}_{1}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow u^{\prime}_{l}\leftarrow s_{j}.

Therefore, there is a corresponding path in the tree decomposition:

B_{1}-B^{\prime}_{1}-B^{\prime}_{2}-\cdots-B^{\prime}_{t}-B_{2}

where each bag
B^{\prime}_{i}
must contain at least one vertex from the above path, by the property of the tree decomposition. In each such bag
B^{\prime}_{i}
, we replace one non-source vertex (e.g.,
u_{k}
or
u^{\prime}_{k}
) with the source vertex
s_{i}
. This replacement does not increase the size of the bag. By applying this process iteratively to all these source pairs
s_{i},s_{j}
, we can ensure that the reachability condition for a DAG tree decomposition is satisfied throughout the tree. Thus,
dtw(\vec{H})\leq tw(H)+1
.

∎

The treewidth (
\mathrm{tw}
) of a graph remains invariant under any number of edge subdivisions. In many graph classes with bounded treewidth, the number of vertices can far exceed the treewidth. It is also known that cliques and quasi-cliques typically exhibit high treewidth but low DAG treewidth. However, even a single edge subdivision in such graphs can cause a significant increase in DAG treewidth. For example,
tw(K_{n})=n-1
and
dtw(K_{n})=1
but with only only subdivision we get that
tw(K_{n_{sub}})=n-1
whereas
dtw(K_{n_{sub}})=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
.

To account for this behavior,
Lemma

11
establishes an upper bound on the DAG treewidth following a single subdivision per edge. This result is especially useful for analyzing the DAG treewidth of cliques and quasi-cliques under limited subdivision. By combining
Lemma

11
with
Lemma

10
, one can obtain a sharper upper bound on DAG treewidth in such settings.

Lemma 11

Let
H
be an undirected graph with
n
vertices. Let
G_{\text{sub}}
be a graph obtained by subdividing each edge of
H
at most once. Then,
dtw(G_{\text{sub}})\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
.

Proof.

Let
O_{i}
be arbitrary but fixed acyclic orientation of
G_{\text{sub}}
and let
S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k}\}
be the set of source vertices in
G_{\text{sub}}
. These source vertices may be original vertices of
H
or subdivision vertices introduced in
G_{\text{sub}}
.

If
|S|\leq n
, then we can partition the source set
S
into two bags such that each bag contains at most
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
vertices.

Now consider the case when
|S|>n
. This means some of the subdivision vertices in
G_{\text{sub}}
are also source vertices. We construct a DAG tree decomposition
T
as follows: Initialize
T
with two bags,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
. We also maintain two corresponding lists
\textsc{Seen}(B_{1})
and
\textsc{Seen}(B_{2})
, which keep track of the vertices from
H
added so far.

Step 1

Identify all source vertices that are also original vertices of
H
. Add them alternately to
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
. Each time we add such a vertex to a bag, we also add it to the corresponding
Seen
list. Note that if a vertex
v
from
H
is a source in
G_{\text{sub}}
, then none of the subdivision vertices adjacent to
v
can be a source.

Step 2

Handle source vertices that are subdivision vertices. For a subdivision vertex
uv
, which comes from subdividing edge
\{u,v\}
in
H
:

•

Step A:
If both
u
and
v
are not present in either
\textsc{Seen}(B_{1})
or
\textsc{Seen}(B_{2})
, add
uv
to both
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
. Also, add
u
and
v
to both
\textsc{Seen}(B_{1})
and
\textsc{Seen}(B_{2})
.

•

Step B:
Now look for a source vertex
vv_{i}
where
v_{i}
is not yet in either
\textsc{Seen}(B_{1})
or
\textsc{Seen}(B_{2})
. Add
vv_{i}
to the smaller of
B_{1}
or
B_{2}
and update the corresponding
Seen
list by adding
v_{i}
. Continue this process: alternate between
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, adding new source vertices and updating
Seen
lists accordingly.

If you reach a subdivision vertex
v_{p}v_{q}
such that no further extension is possible (i.e., there’s no next source
v_{q}v_{r}
with
r
not in
Seen
), then go back to Step A and repeat the process with a fresh
v_{s}v_{t}
where both endpoints are still unseen.

Through these procedures, we ensure that (almost) all vertices of
H
are eventually added to
\textsc{Seen}(B_{1})
or
\textsc{Seen}(B_{2})
, except potentially the vertex
v_{q}
where Step B terminates. Moreover, no further source
v_{q}v_{r}
exists with
r
outside both
Seen
lists.

Step 3

Now handle the remaining subdivision source vertices
v_{s}v_{t}
where
v_{s}
is already in some
Seen
list and
v_{t}
is not. Add
v_{s}v_{t}
to the smaller of
B_{1}
or
B_{2}
and update the respective
Seen
list by including
v_{t}
. Repeat this until all vertices from
H
are in one of the
Seen
lists.

After Steps 1–3, we claim that both bags have size at most
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. Why? Because each time we add a source vertex, we are effectively marking a new vertex from
H
as
Seen
. In Step 2A, while a source vertex is added to both bags, it causes two
H
vertices to be added to each
Seen
list. Additionally, we always ensure the bag sizes differ by at most 1, so the maximum size of each bag remains within
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
.

Now, there may still be some remaining source vertices
u_{l}v_{l}
such that both
u_{l}
and
v_{l}
have already been added to the
Seen
lists. For these vertices, we make the following claim:

Claim 2

Each remaining such source vertex
u_{l}v_{l}
has both endpoints in the same
Seen
list, either
\textsc{Seen}(B_{1})
or
\textsc{Seen}(B_{2})
.

Proof.

Suppose
u_{l}
is in
\textsc{Seen}(B_{1})
and
v_{l}
is in
\textsc{Seen}(B_{2})
. Then
u_{l}
must have been added through Step 2 or 3. If it was added in Step 2 (except at the stopping point), then it must have been added to both
Seen
lists. If
u_{l}
were the terminal vertex of Step 2B, then we would still be able to process
u_{l}v_{l}
in the next step, contradicting the assumption that it is a leftover. Similarly, if both
u_{l}
and
v_{l}
were previously unseen, they would have been picked up by Step 2A. So, either both endpoints were added together, or they ended up in the same
Seen
list through the process.
∎

Step 4

For all such remaining source vertices
u_{l}v_{l}
, attach a
leaf bag
to the corresponding bag
B_{i}
(either
B_{1}
or
B_{2}
), containing just the vertex
u_{l}v_{l}
. We attach it to
B_{i}
such that both
u_{l}
and
v_{l}
are in
\textsc{Seen}(B_{i})
.

Now, we argue that the DAG tree decomposition
T
is valid. All sources are included in some bag. For the reachability condition, observe that for a subdivision vertex
uv
, we have
\mathrm{R}_{T}(uv)\subseteq\mathrm{R}_{T}(u)\cap\mathrm{R}_{T}(v)
. (Here, we are slightly abusing notation since
u
and
v
may not be sources themselves.)

All subdivision sources added in Step 1–3 are in either the same or adjacent bags, so reachability is preserved trivially. For those added in Step 4, the claim ensures both
u_{l}
and
v_{l}
are in the same
Seen
list, hence their reachability is covered by the parent bag they are attached to. Thus, the decomposition is valid, and each bag has size at most
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. Therefore,
dtw(G_{\text{sub}})\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
.
∎

Remark 7

The above bound is tight. Consider the graph
H=K_{4}
, and let
G_{\text{sub}}
be the graph obtained by subdividing each edge of
H
exactly once. If we orient each edge from the original vertex toward its corresponding subdivision vertex, then we can verify that

.

Observation 4

Unlike treewidth
(\mathrm{tw})
, the DAG treewidth
(\mathrm{dtw})
is not closed under minors. For example, the complete graph
K_{6}
has only one source in any acyclic orientation, which implies
\mathrm{dtw}(K_{6})=1
. However, the cycle graph
C_{6}
, which is a subgraph of
K_{6}
, satisfies
\mathrm{dtw}(C_{6})=2
. This shows that
\mathrm{dtw}
is not even closed under subgraphs.

For a non-source vertex
u
in a DAG
\vec{H}
, we define the set
P_{H}(u)
as the set of source vertices from which
u
is reachable. That is,

P_{H}(u)=\{s\in S\mid u\in\mathrm{R}_{H}(s)\}
, where
S
is the set of sources in
\vec{H}
and
\mathrm{R}_{H}(s)
denotes the set of vertices reachable from source
s
. Let
I
be an induced minor of a graph
H
, then we show the following theorem.

Theorem 27

Let
H
be an undirected graph, and let
I
be an induced minor of
H
. Then,
dtw(H)\geq dtw(I).

Proof.

To obtain this result, it suffices to prove the following two claims.

1.

If
I
is an induced subgraph of
H
then
dtw(H)\geq dtw(I)
.

2.

Let
{H}
be an undirected graph with
\mathrm{dtw}({H})=k
. Let
H^{\prime}
be the undirected graph obtained by contracting a single edge in
{H}
. Then,
\mathrm{dtw}({H^{\prime}})\leq k.

Proof of Claim 1:

Let
dtw(I)=k
. By definition, there exists an acyclic orientation
O_{1}
of
I
such that
dtw(I)=k
under this orientation. We now construct an acyclic orientation of
H
that extends
O_{1}
and preserves the treewidth. Let
I^{\prime}\subseteq H
be an induced subgraph isomorphic to
I
, with an isomorphism
f:V(I)\to V(I^{\prime})
. Fix the orientation of
I^{\prime}
in
H
according to
O_{1}
.

Let
V(H)=V(I^{\prime})\cup\left(V(H)\setminus V(I^{\prime})\right)
. Partition the remaining vertices based on their distance from
I^{\prime}
in
H
. Define layers
D_{1},D_{2},\dots,D_{j}
, where

D_{i}=\{v\in V(H)\setminus V(I^{\prime})\mid\text{dist}_{H}(v,V(I^{\prime}))=i\}.

Now, define a topological ordering on the vertices of
H
as:

V(I^{\prime}),D_{1},D_{2},\dots,D_{j}.

Orient the edges from each
D_{i}
to
D_{i+1}
in the forward direction (i.e., from smaller to larger layers), and similarly orient the edges from
V(I^{\prime})
to
D_{1}
forward. For edges within any
D_{i}
, choose an arbitrary acyclic orientation.

Note that after this orientation, we have
S(I)=S(\vec{H})\cap I^{\prime}
. Also, for any non-source
u
in
I
, we have
P_{I}(u)=P_{\vec{H}}(u)
i.e., there is no source reaching
u
other than the source vertices in
I
.

Now, suppose for contradiction that
dtw(H)<k
under this orientation. Let
T
be a valid DAG-tree decomposition of
H
with DAG treewidth less than
k
. Then, the restriction of
T
to the subgraph
I^{\prime}
forms a valid DAG-tree decomposition of
I^{\prime}
, and hence of
I
(by isomorphism), contradicting the assumption that
dtw(I)=k
. Therefore,
dtw(H)\geq dtw(I)
.

Proof of Claim 2:

The DAG treewidth of an undirected graph
H
is the maximum of the DAG treewidth of DAG
\vec{H}
. We want to show
dtw(H^{\prime})\leq k
, where
H^{\prime}
is the undirected graph obtained after contraction of an edge
\{u,v\}
. So, for all acyclic orientations of
H^{\prime}
, we have to show that the DAG treewidth of
H^{\prime}
is bounded by
k
. We pick an arbitrary but fixed acyclic orientation of
H^{\prime}
. We copy the same orientation in
H
. Note that
\{u,v\}\notin E(H^{\prime})
. So, the orientation is not known. We first give
(u,v)
orientation to
\{u,v\}
. Note that the orientation of
H
is acyclic. Let
w
be the vertex in
H^{\prime}
obtained after contracting
\{u,v\}
. Let
T
be a DAG tree decomposition of
\vec{H}
of width at most
k
. We will now construct DAG tree decomposition
T^{\prime}
of
\vec{H^{\prime}}
.

•

Case 1:
Both
u
and
v
are non-source vertices.

In this case, we can assume without loss of generality that
P_{H}(u)\subseteq P_{H}(v)
, i.e., all source vertices reaching
u
also reach
v
. Note that the set of source vertices remains unchanged after contraction, i.e.,
S(\vec{H^{\prime}})=S(\vec{H})
. We consider the same DAG tree decomposition
T
of
\vec{H}
. We only have to check whether the source vertices reaching
w
are connected in
T
or not. However, one can see that
P_{H}(v)=P_{H^{\prime}}(w)
. So, the source vertices in
P_{H^{\prime}}(w)
in
T
is connected.

•

Case 2:

u
is a source vertex.

We consider two subcases depending on whether the new vertex
w
is a source in
\vec{H^{\prime}}
or not.

–

Case A :

w
is a source vertex.

This happens only when
P_{H}(v)=\{u\}
, meaning
v
was reachable only from
u
. In this case,
R_{H}(u)=R_{H^{\prime}}(w)\setminus\{v\}
. We can construct a DAG tree decomposition
T^{\prime}
of
\vec{H^{\prime}}
by simply replacing
u
with
w
in all the bags of
T
where
u
appears. Since no new vertices are introduced and bag sizes remain unchanged,
\mathrm{dtw}(\vec{H^{\prime}})\leq k
.

–

Case B:

w
is a non-source vertex.

Here, the contraction removes
u
from the set of sources, so
S(\vec{H^{\prime}})=S(\vec{H})\setminus\{u\}
.
v
must be reachable by some source vertex
s
other than
u
. Moreover,

P_{H^{\prime}}(w)=P_{H}(v)\setminus\{u\},

and for each source
s\in P_{H^{\prime}}(w)
,

R_{H^{\prime}}(s)=(R_{H}(s)\cup R_{H}(u)\cup\{w\})\setminus\{v\}.

For every bag
B
where
u
appears and is the only source from
P_{H}(v)
, we can replace
u
with another source from
P_{H}(v)
present in an adjacent bag. Also, for any
s
such that
v\in R(s)
,
R(u)\subseteq R(s)
. Thus, we obtain a decomposition of the DAG tree
T^{\prime}
with a maximum bag size
k
.

Since these modifications only replace one source with another and do not increase the size of any bag, the resulting DAG tree decomposition
T^{\prime}
of
\vec{H^{\prime}}
has the same width as
T
, i.e.,
\mathrm{dtw}(\vec{H^{\prime}})\leq k.

∎

Fast Running Time for
\mathrm{hom}(H,G),\mathrm{sub}(H,G)\text{ and }\mathrm{ind}(H,G)
:

In
[

, the authors established that for any pattern

Building upon these works (
[

PPS24

[
PPS24
]
in the affirmative.

Further in
[
BPS21
]
, the authors also proved that for a DAG
H
with
k

, and using this obtained upper bounds to compute

28

We now recall the definition of DAG treewidth introduced by Bressan

]

Definition 19

(Similar to Definition 6 of
[
B21
]
)
For an undirected pattern graph

, the DAG treewidth of

is
\tau(H)=\tau_{3}(H)
, where:

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(H)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\tau(P)\mid P\in\Sigma(H)\right\}

\displaystyle\tau_{2}(H)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\tau_{1}(H/\theta)\mid\theta\in\Theta(H)\right\}

\displaystyle\tau_{3}(H)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\tau_{2}(H^{\prime})\mid H^{\prime}\in D(H)\right\}

Here:

•

\Sigma(H)
is the set of all acyclic orientations of

•

\Theta(H)
is the set of all equivalence relations (partitions) over
V_{H}
, and

is the pattern obtained by identifying equivalent nodes in

\theta

•

.

We now state the following important theorem from
[

]

Theorem 28

(Theorem 9 of
[
B21
]
)
Consider any
k

, and let

be an upper bound on the time needed to compute a dag tree decomposition of minimum width on
2^{O(k\log k)}

•

\mathrm{hom}(H,G)
in time
2^{O(k\log k)}\cdot O\left(f_{T}(k)+d^{k-\tau_{1}(H)}n^{\tau_{1}(H)}\log n\right)

•

\mathrm{sub}(H,G)
in time
2^{O(k\log k)}\cdot O\left(f_{T}(k)+d^{k-\tau_{2}(H)}n^{\tau_{2}(H)}\log n\right)

•

\mathrm{ind}(H,G)

The theorem still holds if we replace

For any pattern graph
H
, if

for any source
s_{i}

Proof of
3

Our proof relies on the assumption from
[
KKMS23
]
, which states that there is no combinatorial algorithm of time
o(m^{2})
to count copies of
K_{4}
(
[
KKMS23
]
).

We use a strategy similar to that in
[
BPS21
]
to prove the hardness of counting subdivision of
K_{4}
patterns.

Let
H
be a graph on
h
vertices. We define a coloring of a graph
G
as

c:V(G)\rightarrow[h]
. A mapping
\phi:V(H)\rightarrow V(G)
is called a

colorful homomorphism
if

\{c(\phi(v)):v\in V(H)\}=[h].

In other words, a colorful homomorphism maps each vertex of
H
to a distinct color class in
G
, thereby ensuring injectivity. It is a known fact that counting injective colorful homomorphisms reduces to counting homomorphisms
\mathrm{Hom}(H,G)
such that the
reduction preserves degeneracy (see Lemma 3.1
[
BPS21
]
). Also, similar to Lemma 3.2 of
[
BPS21
]
, if one can detect colorful
K_{4}
in a 4 vertex colored graph with
m
edges in time
f(m)
, then it is possible to detect (uncolored)
K_{4}
in a graph with
m
edges in time
\tilde{O}(f(m))
via color-coding.

Using all these known facts, we now describe the construction used for this reduction:
Given a graph
G
and a coloring
c:V(G)\rightarrow[4]
, we construct a new graph

G^{\prime}
with a coloring
c^{\prime}:V(G^{\prime})\rightarrow[10]
in time
O(|V(G)|+|E(G)|)
,
such that

|V(G^{\prime})|,\,|E(G^{\prime})|=\mathcal{O}(|V(G)|+|E(G)|).

We now claim that the number of colored subdivision
K_{4}
patterns in
G^{\prime}
is equal to the
number of
K_{4}
subgraphs in
G
. Furthermore,
G^{\prime}
is 2-degenerate.

The construction is as follows: Since
G
is colored with 4 colors, we assign one of
6 new colors to each subdivision vertex in
G^{\prime}
. For every edge
\{x,y\}\in E(G)
,
if
c(x)\neq c(y)
, we subdivide the edge by introducing a new vertex with color

(c(x),c(y))
. If
c(x)=c(y)
, the edge is deleted. Note that the original vertices
in
V(G)
retain colors from
\{1,2,3,4\}
, while subdivision vertices are colored
with the remaining 6 colors. Clearly,
|V(G^{\prime})|\leq|V(G)|+|E(G)|\quad\text{and}\quad|E(G^{\prime})|\leq 2|E(G)|

both remain in
O(|V(G)|+|E(G)|)
.

One can observe that the original vertices
V(G)
form an independent set in
G^{\prime}
, and
all subdivision vertices have degree exactly 2 in
G^{\prime}
. After removing the subdivision
vertices, the resulting graph is empty. Hence,
G^{\prime}
is 2-degenerate. Also, from the construction, it is straightforward to verify that the number of colorful
K_{4}
copies in
G
equals the number of colorful subdivision
K_{4}
copies in
G^{\prime}
.

Now, suppose that homomorphism counting for subdivision
K_{4}
can be performed in

o((n^{2})
time on 2-degenerate
n
-vertex graphs.
Then, one can also count colored injective homomorphisms of subdivision
K_{4}
in

o((n^{2})
time on 2-degenerate,
k
-vertex-colored,
n
-vertex graphs.

Using the above construction, one can then detect colorful
K_{4}
in 4-vertex-colored,

m
-edge graphs in
o(m^{2})
time. Consequently, this yields an

\tilde{o}(m^{2})
-time algorithm for detecting (uncolored)
K_{4}
in general

m
-edge graphs.

However, it is known that
\mathrm{mtw}(K_{4})=4
and no combinatorial algorithm exists
to detect
K_{4}
in
o(m^{2})
time under standard complexity assumptions (
[
KKMS23
]
).
Therefore, subdivision
K_{4}
subgraph counting cannot be done in
o(m^{2})
time. This further implies that there is no
o(m^{2})
algorithm for computing
sub(H,G)
, when
V(H)=10
.

\square

Lemma 12

Given a DAG
H
with
t
source vertices such that the reachability of every source vertex is bounded by 2, we have
dtw(H)\leq\frac{t}{5.217}+4
.

Proof.

We construct a graph
G
where each vertex corresponds to a non-source vertex in
H
. For every source vertex
s
in
H
such that
R(s)=\{u,v\}
, we add the edge
\{u,v\}
in
G
. Thus, the number of edges in
G
is equal to the number of source vertices in
H
, i.e.,
|E(G)|=t
.

From the result of
[
KMRR05
]
, we know that any graph with
m
edges has treewidth at most
\frac{m}{5.217}+3
. Hence,
tw(G)\leq\frac{t}{5.217}+3
, and there exists a tree decomposition
T
of
G
with maximum bag size at most
\frac{t}{5.217}+4
.

We now use
T
to construct a DAG tree decomposition
T^{\prime}
of
H
. For every non-source vertex
u
in a bag of
T
, we replace it by the source vertex
s
such that
u\in R(s)
. Since
|R(s)|=2
for every
s
, this replacement is well-defined and unique. After this replacement, for every source vertex
s
, either
s
appears in some bag, or
R(s)
is contained in a single bag. In the latter case, we can add a leaf bag
B_{s}
containing only
s
as a child of the bag that contains
R(s)
. This ensures the reachability condition of DAG tree decomposition is satisfied.

Thus, the resulting DAG tree decomposition
T^{\prime}
has maximum bag size at most
\frac{t}{5.217}+4
, and we conclude that
dtw(H)\leq\frac{t}{5.217}+4
.
∎

Theorem 31

For any DAG
H
with
k
vertices, we have
dtw(H)\leq\frac{k}{5}+4
.

Proof.

We construct a DAG tree decomposition for
H
with maximum bag size at most
\frac{k}{5}+4
.

The construction has two phases. In the first phase, we build a set of source vertices through which we can discover at least four new non-sources. In the second phase, we build a DAG tree decomposition for the rest of the sources. Finally, we combine these two to build a DAG tree decomposition for
H
.

First phase:

Initialize
B^{*}
, a subset of sources of
H
, to the empty set. We greedily add sources to
B^{*}
as follows: at each step, if there is a source vertex
s
such that
|R(s)\setminus R(B^{*})|\geq 4
. We mark
s
and the non-sources reachable from
s
as
Seen
. At least five new vertices are marked by this step. We add
s
to
B^{*}
and remove all
Seen
vertices from
H
. Then, we repeat this process. If there is no source vertex
s
such that
|R(s)\setminus R(B^{*})|\geq 4
, then we stop this phase. Let the subgraph of
H
at the end of this phase be
H^{\prime}
and let
k^{\prime}
be the number of vertices in
H^{\prime}
.

Second phase:

Our goal now is to construct a DAG tree decomposition
T^{\prime}
of
H^{\prime}
with maximum bag size at most
\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}+4
. In the graph
H^{\prime}
, every source vertex has at most three reachable vertices, that is,
|R(s)|\leq 3
. We now apply the construction in the first phase again to build a set of sources
B_{1}
(starting from the empty set) by adding iteratively sources
s
such that
|R(s)\setminus R(B_{1})|=3
while removing all vertices reachable from
s
at each step as before. Let
H_{1}
be the resulting subgraph of
H^{\prime}
. Now, we split the proof into cases:

•

|B_{1}|=0
: By
Lemma

12
, we have
dtw(H^{\prime})\leq\frac{k^{\prime}}{5.217}+4
.

•

|B_{1}|=\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}
: We have removed at least
4(\frac{k^{\prime}}{5})
vertices while building
B_{1}
. Therefore, at most
\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}
sources remain. Let
B_{2}
be the set of remaining sources. The DAG tree decomposition of
H^{\prime}
is a tree that is an edge. One vertex is the bag
B_{1}
and the other is bag
B_{2}
. This decomposition has maximum bag size
\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}
.

•

0<|B_{1}|<\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}
: Let
t
be the number of non-source vertices in
H_{1}
. If
t\leq\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}
, we build a bag
B^{\prime}
that includes one source for each of the
t
non-source vertices. Any remaining sources are attached to the bag
B^{\prime}
as singleton leaf bags. Finally bag
B_{1}
is attached to
B^{\prime}
. The maximum bag size remains at most
\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}
. If
t>\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}
, then the number of sources in
H_{1}
is less than
\frac{4k^{\prime}}{5}
. Let
G_{1}
be the induced subgraph of
H^{\prime}
on
B_{1}\cup R(B_{1})
and let
k_{1}
be the number of vertices in
G_{1}
. Then,
G_{1}
has
\frac{k_{1}}{4}
sources and
\frac{3k_{1}}{4}
non-sources. Let
G_{2}
be the graph obtained by removing
V(G_{1})
from
H^{\prime}
. In
G_{2}
, each source has at most two reachable vertices, and the number of sources is less than
\frac{4k^{\prime}}{5}-\frac{k_{1}}{4}
. Applying
Lemma

12
gives:

dtw(G_{2})\leq\frac{1}{5.217}\left(\frac{4k^{\prime}}{5}-\frac{k_{1}}{4}\right)+4.

We then form the decomposition of
H^{\prime}
by adding
B_{1}
to each bag in the decomposition of
G_{2}
. So, the maximum bag size is:

dtw(H^{\prime})\leq\frac{4k^{\prime}}{25}-\frac{k_{1}}{20}+\frac{k_{1}}{4}+4=\frac{4k^{\prime}}{25}+\frac{k_{1}}{5}+4.

If
k_{1}>\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}
, then the number of source vertices in
H^{\prime}
is at most
\frac{2k^{\prime}}{5}
. Thus, we can get DAG tree decomposition for
H^{\prime}
. with maximum bag size
\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}
. So
k_{1}\leq\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}
and therefore
\frac{4k^{\prime}}{25}+\frac{k_{1}}{5}+4\leq\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}+4
.

Therefore, in all cases, at the end of the second phase, we have a DAG tree decomposition for
H^{\prime}
with maximum bag size at most
k^{\prime}/5+4
.

Finally, by adding
B^{*}
from the first phase to every bag of the decomposition of
H^{\prime}
obtained in the second phase, we obtain a DAG tree decomposition of
H
. The maximum bag size of this DAG tree decomposition is:
|B^{*}|+\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}+4\leq\frac{(k-k^{\prime})}{5}+\frac{k^{\prime}}{5}+4=\frac{k}{5}+4
.
∎

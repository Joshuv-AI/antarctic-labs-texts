Tensor tomography for a set of generalized V-line transforms in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}
Rahul Bhardwaj

Abstract
We study a set of generalized V-line transforms, namely longitudinal, mixed, and transverse V-line transforms, of a symmetric m m -tensor field in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . The goal of this article is to recover a symmetric m m -tensor field f supported in a disk 𝔻 R \mathbb{D}_{R} , with radius R R and centered at the origin, by a combination of the aforementioned generalized V-line transforms, using two different techniques for different sets of data.



Keywords:
Inverse problems, V-line transforms, Tensor tomography, Mellin transform

Mathematics subject classification 2010:
44A12, 44A60, 44A30, 47G10

Introduction

Several imaging modalities in computerized tomography are based on the mathematical model, which uses the regular ray transform and the broken ray/V-line transform, see
[
Nat01
,
NW01
,
Amb23
]
and references therein. Many authors have studied the inverse problems of recovering the scalar function (0-tensor), vector field (1-tensor),
2
-tensor field, and
m
-tensor field in various settings from different combinations of its integral transform such as longitudinal, transverse, mixed, and momentum ray transforms. There are several results available related to inversion of the regular ray transforms (i.e. integrates along a straight line), for instance, see
[
Den94
,
Den06
,
DS15
,
Kat06
,
Mis20
,
MS21
,
Mon16
,
Pal09
,
Sha94
]
and references therein. The concept of the V-line transform appears in the model of photon transfer through an object, assumes that photons scatter no more than once, known as single-scattering tomography, see
[
FSM09
]
. In the last few years, there are various articles focused on studying the broken ray/V-line transforms, which map a function to its integral over the V-shaped lines. This transform is a generalization of the regular ray transform. Many applications of these transforms appear in the imaging sciences viz, emission tomography
[
BZG96
,
BZG98
]
, single scattering optical tomography, see
[
FMS08
,
FMS10a
]
and references therein. Now we present a list of the earlier works related to the V-line transforms. The authors in
[
TN11
]
have studied the V-line transform with a fixed axis of symmetry, and vertices on a line. In
[
MH17
,
HMS17
]
, the authors addressed the V-line transform and the attenuated V-line transform for the scalar-valued function in a circular geometry structure, where the symmetry axis is perpendicular to the circle and the vertices lies on a circle. In
[
MPZ25
]
, authors studied the V-line transform in the same setup and considered the inverse problem to recover the symmetric
m
-tensor field from the various combinations. The other class includes V-lines whose vertices are inside the support of the image function and are significant to single-scattering tomography (e.g. optical tomographic imaging in the mesoscopic regime), see
[
FSM09
,
FMS10b
,
FMS11
,
WO19
]
and fluorescence imaging in optical tomography, see
[
FMS18
]
. Based on this class of V-line, the question of recovering a vector field in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
from different combinations of new generalized V-line transforms is investigated by Gaik et al. in
[
AJM24
,
ALJM20
]
and also this work is extended by
[
AMZ24a
,
AMZ24b
]
, Gaik et al. for the case of symmetric
2
-tensor fields in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The set up where the V-line have vertices on the boundary of the image domain are frequently used in image reconstruction with Compton cameras, see
[
TKK18
,
BZG97b
,
BZG97a
]
and references therein. In this setting, authors in
[
AM13b
,
Amb12
,
ABKQ13
]
consider the inverse problem for recovering the scalar-valued function by two different techniques. Extension for vector fields is considered in
[
BMV25
]
, Bhardwaj et al. for the case of the vector field. Also, the broken ray/V-line transforms have been studied in the Riemannian setting
[
JKR24
,
Ilm13
,
IS16
,
Hub14
]
, based on PDEs work
[
Esk04
,
KS14
]
, and in the set-up of microlocal analysis by Sherson, see
[
She15
]
. Please refer to a recent book by Ambartsoumian
[
Amb23
]
for a comprehensive discussion of these generalized operators and an overview of the literature.

Motivated by these works
[
Amb12
,
AM13b
,
BMV25
]
, we consider the inverse problem of recovering a symmetric
m
-tensor field supported on the disk
\mathbb{D}_{R}
, using a set of generalized V-line transforms, namely longitudinal, transverse, and mixed V-line transforms. We present two different types of inversion formulas, one for recovering the symmetric
m
-tensor field and other for
2
-tensor field in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
from the knowledge of its V-line transforms. Here, we examine two distinct subsets of the previously described broken lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to recover the symmetric
m
-tensor field. In this article, geometrically, we consider the broken rays/V-line that emanate from the boundary of a disk
\mathbb{D}_{R}
and break into another ray under a fixed angle after travelling a certain distance along the diameter. In this article, we used the approaches given by
[
AM13b
,
Amb12
]
, which is done for the scalar case, when the sources and receivers are positioned on the boundary of the disk. We considered a similar set up for symmetric
m
-tensor fields and proved invertibility of the introduced transforms.

The rest of the article is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary notations and definitions of the integral ray transform. In Section
2.2
, we present our main findings of this article. The detailed proof of our main results is provided in Sections
3
and
4
. Finally, we finish this article with acknowledgements in Section
5

Definitions, notations and statement of main results

2.1
Definitions and Notations

In this section, we introduce some necessary notations and provide definitions that will be needed later. Let
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be the space of symmetric
m
-tensor fields on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega;S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
be the space of
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
valued function with each component is smooth and compactly supported in
\Omega
. Throughout this article, we have used bold font letters for vectors and
m
-tensor fields (such as
f
,
x
,
u
, etc.) and regular font letters for scalars and scalar-valued functions (such as
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
,
t
,
f_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{m}}
, etc). Any
\textbf{{f}}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega;S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
is defined as

\displaystyle\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{x}}):=f_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{m}}(\textbf{{x}})\,dx^{i_{1}}\,dx^{i_{2}}\,\cdots\,dx^{i_{m}}

(1)

where for each
i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}=1,2
,
f_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{m}}
are smooth functions with compact support and also symmetric with their indices. Note that in equation (
1
) we have used the “
Einstein summation rule
” which allows us to assume the summation from
1
to
2
over every repeated indices and the same will be followed through out this article. The standard dot product
\displaystyle\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
on
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is given by

\displaystyle\left\langle\textbf{{f}},\textbf{{g}}\right\rangle=f_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{m}}g^{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{m}}

and we denote by
|\cdot|
the corresponding norm. Let
\textbf{{u}}=(u^{1},u^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then
\textbf{{u}}^{m}\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, where
\textbf{{u}}^{m}
stands for the
m^{th}
symmetric tensor product of
u
and it is given by

\textbf{{u}}^{m}:=u^{i_{1}}u^{i_{2}}\cdots u^{i_{m}}
, where
i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}=1,2
.

Let
f
be a symmetric
m
-tensor field supported in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{R}
and
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
be a fixed scattering angle. A combination of two rays which emanate from the point
\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}\in\partial\mathbb{D}_{R}
and moves the distance
d
along the diameter in the radial direction
\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}
, then breaks into another ray under the obtuse angle
\pi-\theta
and travels in the direction
\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}
(see
1Figure 1(a)
), is called the
broken ray
and is denoted by
BR(\phi,d)
. Mathematically, the broken ray
BR(\phi,d)
is described by:

\displaystyle BR(\phi,d)=\left\{\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}+t\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}:0\leq t\leq d\right\}\cup\left\{\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}+d\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}+s\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}:0\leq s<\infty\right\}.

(2)

Here
\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}=(R\cos\phi,R\sin\phi)
,
\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}=-(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)
and
\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}=-\left(\cos(\theta+ \phi),\sin(\theta+\phi)\right)
, lie on the unit circle
\partial\mathbb{D}_{1}
and the vectors
\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{\perp}=(\sin\phi,-\cos\phi)
and
\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}^{\perp}=\left(\sin(\theta+\phi),-\cos(\theta+ \phi)\right)
are obtained by rotating
\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}
and
\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}
in the direction of counterclockwise by an angle of
\pi/2
respectively.

The unit vectors
\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}
and
\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}
, which are used in the following definitions, are fixed and uniquely determined by
(\phi,d)
.

A sketch of broken line
BR(\phi,d)
.

Figure 1(b)

Broken lines considered to collect data.

Now, we introduce the broken ray/V-line transforms, defined as the integrals over the broken ray
BR(\phi,d)
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
\textbf{{f}}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{R};S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, the
longitudinal V-line transform
of
f
is defined by

\mathcal{L}\textbf{{f}}(\phi,d):=\int_{0}^{d}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}+s\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}),\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{m}\right\rangle\,ds+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}+d\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}+s\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}),\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}^{m}\right\rangle\,ds,

(3)

where
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
and
d\in[0,2R]
.

Definition 2.2
.

For
\textbf{{f}}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{R};S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, the
transverse V-line transform
of
f
is defined by

\mathcal{T}\textbf{{f}}(\phi,d):=\int_{0}^{d}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}+s\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}),(\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{\perp})^{m}\right\rangle\,ds+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}+d\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}+s\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}),(\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}^{\perp})^{m}\right\rangle\,ds,

(4)

where
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
and
d\in[0,2R]
.

Definition 2.3
.

For
\textbf{{f}}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{R};S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
and
1\leq k\leq m-1
, the
mixed V-line transform
of
f
is defined by

\mathcal{M}^{(k)}\textbf{{f}}(\phi,d):=\int_{0}^{d}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}+s\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}),(\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{\perp})^{k}\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{m-k}\right\rangle\,ds+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}+d\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}+s\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}),(\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}^{\perp})^{k}\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}^{m-k}\right\rangle\,ds,

(5)

where
(\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{\perp})^{k}\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{m-k}:=(\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{\perp})^{i_{1}}(\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{\perp})^{i_{2}}\cdots(\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{\perp})^{i_{k}}(\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{\perp})^{i_{k+1}}\cdots\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{i_{m}}
, for
i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}=1,2
,
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
and
d\in[0,2R]
.

Remark 2.4
.

In the definition of the mixed V-line transform, when
k=0
, the transform reduces to the definition of the longitudinal V-line transform, and when
k=m
, it reduces to the transverse V-line transform.

Subsequently, we introduce the longitudinal, transverse, and mixed ray transforms, which will be referenced later in this article. These transforms are integrated over a straight line, defining the line
L(\psi,p):=\{(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y_{1}\cos{\psi}+y_{2}\sin{\psi}=p\}
, for a given angle
\psi\in[0,2\pi)
and a real number
p
. This line is positioned at a signed distance
p
from the origin and is perpendicular to the unit vector
\textbf{{w}}=(\cos{\psi},\sin{\psi})
. The vector
\textbf{{w}}^{\perp}=(-\sin{\psi},\cos{\psi})
is also a unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
that is perpendicular to the vector
w
.

Definition 2.5
.

For
\textbf{{f}}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{R};S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, the
longitudinal ray transform
of
f
is defined by

\mathcal{I}\textbf{{f}}(\psi,p)=\mathcal{I}\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{w}},p):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(p\textbf{{w}}+s\textbf{{w}}^{\perp}),(\textbf{{w}}^{\perp})^{m}\right\rangle\,ds,\quad\psi\in[0,2\pi)\mbox{ and }p\in\mathbb{R}.

(6)

Definition 2.6
.

For
\textbf{{f}}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{R};S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, the
transverse ray transform
of
f
is defined by

\mathcal{J}\textbf{{f}}(\psi,p)=\mathcal{J}\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{w}},p):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(p\textbf{{w}}+s\textbf{{w}}^{\perp}),\textbf{{w}}^{m}\right\rangle\,ds,\quad\psi\in[0,2\pi)\mbox{ and }p\in\mathbb{R}.

(7)

Definition 2.7
.

For
\textbf{{f}}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{R};S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
and
1\leq k\leq m-1
, the
mixed ray transform
of
f
is defined by

\mathcal{K}^{(k)}\textbf{{f}}(\psi,p)=\mathcal{J}\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{w}},p):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(p\textbf{{w}}+s\textbf{{w}}^{\perp}),\textbf{{w}}^{k}(\textbf{{w}}^{\perp})^{m-k}\right\rangle\,ds,

(8)

where
\textbf{{w}}^{k}(\textbf{{w}}^{\perp})^{m-k}:=w^{i_{1}}w^{i_{2}}\cdots w^{i_{k}}(w^{\perp})^{i_{k+1}}\cdots(w^{\perp})^{i_{m}}
, for
i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}=1,2
,
\psi\in[0,2\pi)
and
p\in\mathbb{R}
.

Furthermore, we provide the definition of the Radon transform, which we also require later on:

Definition 2.8
.

Let
f
be a scalar function field in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}_{R})
. The
Radon transform

f
is defined as follows:

\mathcal{R}f(\psi,p)=\mathcal{R}f(\textbf{{w}},p):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(p\textbf{{w}}+s\textbf{{w}}^{\perp})\,ds,\quad\psi\in[0,2\pi)\mbox{ and }p\in\mathbb{R}.

(9)

2.2
Statement of main results

In this subsection, we state the main results of this article.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
f
be a symmetric
m
-tensor field with each component smooth and compactly supported in a disk
\mathbb{D}_{R\sin{\theta}}
. Then we can recover
f
explicitly with an inversion formula, from the knowledge of
\mathcal{L}\textbf{{f}}(\phi,d)
,
\mathcal{T}\textbf{{f}}(\phi,d)
and
\mathcal{M}^{(k)}\textbf{{f}}(\phi,d)
where
1\leq k\leq m-1
, for
d\in[0,2R]
and
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
.

Observe from the
1Figure 1(a)
that we have no data about straight-line transform outside the disk
D_{R\sin\theta}
hence the support condition on tensor field
f
in Theorem
2.9
, is imposed to conclude the recovery of
f
.
We will prove Theorem
2.9
, by transferring the existing knowledge about the V-line transforms to that of the knowledge about corresponding straight-line transforms of tensor field in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. After that following the approach used in
[
DS15
, Section 5.2]
, we recover the Radon transform of each component of the symmetric tensor field. Finally using the inversion formula for the Radon transform for functions, we derive the inversion formula for tensor fields. The approach used to prove the above Theorem is motivated by
[
Amb12
]
and
[
BMV25
]
where the analogous problem is considered for the scalar function and the vector field respectively.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
f
be a symmetric
2
-tensor field with each component smooth and compactly supported in a disk
\mathbb{D}_{R}
. Then
f
is uniquely recovered from the knowledge of
\mathcal{L}\textbf{{f}}(\phi,d)
,
\mathcal{T}\textbf{{f}}(\phi,d)
and
\mathcal{M}\textbf{{f}}(\phi,d)
, where
d\in[0,R]
and
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
.

Approach to establish the proof of Theorem
2.10
:
We expand the data (
\mathcal{L}\textbf{{f}},\mathcal{T}\textbf{{f}}\ \&\ \mathcal{M}\textbf{{f}}
) and the unknown
m
-tensor field
f
into their Fourier series and then try to express the Fourier coefficients of
f
in terms of the Fourier coefficients of
\mathcal{L}\textbf{{f}}
,
\mathcal{T}\textbf{{f}}
and
\mathcal{M}^{(k)}\textbf{{f}}
where
1\leq k\leq m-1
. Finally using the Mellin’s transform, we prove that the Fourier coefficients of each components of symmetric
2
-tensor field can be expressed in terms of known data. This will give the required series formula for recovery of the tensor field. This problem has been studied earlier in
[
AM13b
]
for the scalar case and in
[
BMV25
]
for the case of vector field in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Remark 2.11
.

In Theorem
2.9
, there is a restriction on the support of the symmetric
m
-tensor field
f
, which depends on the fixed scattering angle
\theta
. This support condition arises due to the technique we are using to prove the theorem. For the case of
2
-tensor field, the result in Theorem
2.10
, is stronger than that of Theorem
2.9
, since it does not impose restrictions on the support of
f
even after using the less data as compared to Theorem
2.9
, in the sense that scalar
d
varies in the half interval
[0,R]
instead of
[0,2R]
in Theorem
2.9
.

Proof.

Let us consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\textbf{{f}}(\phi,d)=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{d}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}+s\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}),\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{m}\right\rangle\,ds}_{A_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}+d\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}+s\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}),\textbf{{v}}_{\phi}^{m}\right\rangle\,ds,}_{A_{2}}

(10)

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{(k)}\textbf{{f}}(\phi,2R)=(-1)^{k}\mathcal{K}^{(k)}\textbf{{f}}(\phi+\pi/2,0).

(15)

Also

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\textbf{{f}}(\phi+\pi,2R-d)

Next we simplify the integrals given by

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2R-d}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{x}}_{\phi+\pi}+s\textbf{{u}}_{\phi+\pi}),\textbf{{u}}_{\phi+\pi}^{m}\right\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2R-d}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{x}}_{\phi+\pi}+2R\textbf{{u}}_{\phi+\pi}-2R\textbf{{u}}_{\phi+\pi}+s\textbf{{u}}_{\phi+\pi}),\textbf{{u}}_{\phi+\pi}^{m}\right\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}\int_{d}^{2R}\left\langle\textbf{{f}}(\textbf{{x}}_{\phi}+s\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}),\textbf{{u}}_{\phi}^{m}\right\rangle\,ds.

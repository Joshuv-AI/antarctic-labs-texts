Excitonic effects in phonons: reshaping the graphene Kohn anomalies and lifetimes
Alberto Guandalini, Francesco Macheda, Giovanni Caldarelli, Francesco Mauri

Abstract
We develop an ab initio framework that captures the impact of electron-electron and electron-hole interactions on phonon properties. This enables the inclusion of excitonic effects in the optical phonon dispersions and lifetimes of graphene, both near the center ( Γ \Gamma ) and at the border (K) of the Brillouin zone, at phonon-momenta relevant for Raman scattering and for the onset of the intrinsic electrical resistivity. Near K, we find a phonon red-shift of ∼ 150 \sim 150 cm -1 and a 10x enhancement of the group velocity, together with a 5 × 5\times increase in linewidths due to a 26 × 26\times increase of the electron-phonon matrix elements. These effects persist for doping 2 ​ E F < ℏ ​ ω ph 2E_{\mathrm{F}}<\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{ph}} and are quenched at higher dopings. Near Γ \Gamma , the excitonic effects are minor because of the gauge field nature of the electron-phonon coupling at small phonon momentum.

We include electronic interaction effects in the dynamical matrix in the framework of generalized Kohn-Sham theory (GDFT)

We further leverage on the recent variational formulation of the phonon response in the presence of nonlocal exchange interactions

Previous efforts evaluated the impact of interaction effects on the electron-phonon matrix elements by considering the variation of the one-particle electronic Green’s function, even with GW

24

or the renormalization-group approach

We instead obtain the phonon self-energy from the interacting two-particle electron Green’s function, including interactions beyond finite-order perturbation theory. With the proposed approach, we provide a formally exact theoretical framework where the sole approximation pertains to the treatment of the electronic correlation.
Exploiting the exact generalized Fermi golden rule derived in Ref.

1

[
27
]

We find a strong redshift of the freestanding graphene phonon dispersion at the K point with respect to graphite, with an associated enhancement of the group velocity, as well as a strong increase of the linewidths. Treating the full infinite summation of electronic ladder diagrams is crucial to obtain quantitative results. We also investigate dynamical and doping effects, which are known to be relevant for optical graphene phonons near

Phonon Green’s function—

We consider a spin unpolarized finite system of
N_{\text{ion}}
ions and
N_{\text{el}}
electrons interacting through the Coulomb potential. The generalization to periodic systems, e.g. graphene, is straightforward, and covered in the Supplemental Material (SM)
[
30
]
. We use the convention that integration over the volume of the system for spatial variables with numbers as a subscript is implicit, as in Ref.
1
.
We express the analytic continuation of Fourier transforms of retarded quantities in complex frequency
z{=}\omega{+}i\eta
. Physical observables are obtained in the limit
\eta{\to}0^{+}
, to be considered after the thermodynamical limit
[
31
]
.
All the information about phonons is encoded in the (mass-reduced) displacement-displacement Green’s function
[
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
]

G^{-1}{\!\!\!\,}_{s\alpha,r\beta}(\omega)=\omega^{2}\delta_{s\alpha,r\beta}{-}D_{s\alpha,r\beta}(\omega+i0^{+}),

(1)

where
D
is the dynamical matrix. Latin and Greek symbols indicate atomic and Cartesian coordinates respectively.
The exact evaluation of the phonon Green’s function, in the harmonic approximation, requires the solution of the Hedin-Baym equations
[
35
,
36
]
.
We disregard electron-phonon effects on the electronic dynamics, such as the phonon vertex corrections neglected leveraging the Migdal theorem
[
37
]
and electron-phonon insertions in the electronic Green’s function.
Then, we obtain the phonon Green’s function from the interacting electronic density induced by the (time-dependent) lattice perturbation at the equilibrium positions
[
1
]
, or, equivalently, from the electron density-density response function.
Within this framework, the dynamical matrix can be expressed as

D_{s\alpha,r\beta}(z)=\frac{C_{s\alpha,r\beta}(z)+C^{\mathrm{ion-ion}}_{s\alpha,r\beta}-\sum_{l}C_{s\alpha,l\beta}(z)\delta_{rs}}{\sqrt{M_{s}M_{r}}}

(2)

where
M_{s}
/
M_{r}
are the ionic masses of the
s
/
r
atom.
C^{\mathrm{ion-ion}}_{s\alpha,r\beta}
is the second derivative of the ion-ion interaction energy and

C_{s\alpha,r\beta}(z)=V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r}_{1})\chi(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2},z)V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(r\beta)}(\bm{r}_{2}),

(3)

where
V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(s\alpha)}
is the electron-ion and ion-ion interaction potentials derived with respect to the ion displacement
[
8
]
.
\chi
is the electronic density-density response function with nuclei clamped at their equilibrium positions, defined by
\rho^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r},z){=}\chi(\bm{r},\bm{r}_{1},z)V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r}_{1})
, where
\rho^{(s\alpha)}
is the electronic density induced by the ion displacement.
A derivation of Eq. (
2
) is shown in the SM
[
30
]
.

Density response with excitonic effects—

The evaluation of Eq. (
2
) requires the density-density response function
\chi
, obtained e.g. with Hedin’s equations
[
19
]
and linear response theory.
However, practical schemes require approximations for the electronic interaction.
GW+BSE approaches accurately describe excitonic effects of low-energy excitations
[
6
]
, thus providing an accurate imaginary part of
\chi(z)
in a wide range of crystals, including graphene
[
40
,
41
,
42
]
.
This is done by employing a dynamically-screened interaction in the GW band structure and a static screened interaction
W(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime}){=}\epsilon^{-1}(\bm{r},\bm{r}_{1})v(|\bm{r}_{1}{-}\bm{r}^{\prime}|)
in the BSE, with
\epsilon^{-1}
being the static inverse dielectric function in the random-phase approximation and
v(|\bm{r}|){=}e^{2}/|\bm{r}|
the Coulomb interaction.
However, their applicability to phonons, where also the real part of
\chi(z)
is required in Eq. (
3
), is still a computational challenge
[
43
]
with unknown accuracy.

GDFT with range-separated (RS) hybrid functionals
[
44
,
45
,
46
]
are extensively used to describe molecular vibrations with nonlocal exchange effects in linear response
[
47
]
.
Within this framework, electrons and holes effectively interact with an empirical
W^{\mathrm{RS}}(\bm{r}{-}\bm{r}^{\prime}){=}\alpha(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})v(\bm{r}{-}\bm{r}^{\prime})
, where
\alpha
is a combination of error functions weighting fractions of short and long-range exchange.
Dielectric-dependent hybrid functionals
[
48
]
aim to mimic the long-range
W
of the BSE with
\alpha(|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|\gg 1)=1/\epsilon_{\infty}
, where
\epsilon_{\infty}
is the electronic static dielectric constant.

In this work, we consider the following Hartree-exchange-correlation (Hxc) functional

\displaystyle E^{\mathrm{GDFT}}_{\mathrm{Hxc}}[n]{=}E^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{\mathrm{Hxc}}[\rho]{-}\frac{1}{2}|n(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})|^{2}\mathcal{W}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2}){+}\bar{E}_{\mathrm{xc}}[\rho,\mathcal{W}]\ ,

(4)

where
n(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime}){=}\sum_{i}f_{i}\psi_{i}(\bm{r})\psi^{*}_{i}(\bm{r}^{\prime})
is the (one-body) electronic density matrix, while
f_{i}
and
\psi_{i}
are Kohn-Sham occupations and orbitals. We use a screened interaction
\mathcal{W}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})
such that
\mathcal{W}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime}){=}W(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})
for
|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|{>}r_{c}
, with
r_{c}
a cutoff radius in the range of interatomic distance.
For
|\bm{r}{-}\bm{r}^{\prime}|{<}r_{c}
,
\mathcal{W}
is smoothly reduced as
|\bm{r}{-}\bm{r}^{\prime}|{\to}0
, as in Refs.
49
,
50
,
51
, in order to minimize the impact of the static screening approximation on the band structure.

E^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{\mathrm{Hxc}}[\rho]
is the
\mathrm{Hxc}
energy in a local/semilocal approximation, e.g. the local density (LDA
[
52
]
) or generalized gradient approximation (GGA
[
14
]
).

\bar{E}_{\mathrm{xc}}[\rho,\mathcal{W}]
must be added to cancel the Fock-like term in Eq. (
4
), so
to recover the exact interacting total energy in the uniform density limit (jellium). By setting
\mathcal{W}{=}0
in Eq. (
4
), we recover the DFT functionals (LDA or GGA).
Via time-dependent density functional theory
[
54
]
applied to the
E_{\mathrm{Hxc}}^{\mathrm{GDFT}}
functional in Eq. (
4
) we have access to
\chi
, thus to the force constants in Eq. (
3
) with both excitonic and dynamical effects.

Variational formulation—

Our formulation belongs to the set of functional approximations with nonlocal exchange effects covered in Ref.
1
.
Now we take advantage of the partially-screen partially-screen formulation of the electronic response of Ref.
1
to include excitonic and dynamical corrections in a differential form (see SM
[
30
]
).
The force constants associated to the GDFT functional are rewritten as

C_{s\alpha,r\beta}(z)=V_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r}_{1},-z)\chi^{\mathcal{W}}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2},z)V_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(r\beta)}(\bm{r}_{2},z)\\
-\rho_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r}_{1},-z)f^{\mathrm{GDFT}}_{\mathrm{Hxc}}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})\rho_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(r\beta)}(\bm{r}_{2},z),

(5)

where
\chi^{\mathcal{W}}
is the dynamical susceptibility with ladder diagrams, defined in Eq. (89) of Ref.
1
, also used in exciton-phonon physics
[
55
]
.
\rho_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(s\alpha)}
is the induced density of the GDFT functional of Eq. (
4
) and

V_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r},z)=V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r})+f^{\mathrm{GDFT}}_{\mathrm{Hxc}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}_{1})\rho_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r}_{1},z),

(6)

where
f_{\mathrm{Hxc}}^{\mathrm{GDFT}}=f_{\mathrm{Hxc}}^{\mathrm{DFT}}+\Delta f_{\mathrm{Hxc}}
and

f^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{\mathrm{Hxc}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})=\partial^{2}E^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{\mathrm{Hxc}}/\partial\rho(\bm{r})\partial\rho(\bm{r}^{\prime})
are the GDFT and DFT local kernels, and
\Delta f_{\mathrm{Hxc}}{=}\partial^{2}\bar{E}_{\mathrm{xc}}/\partial\rho(\bm{r})\partial\rho(\bm{r}^{\prime})
.
The reformulation in Eq. (
5
) ensures that an approximation on the density
\rho_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(s\alpha)}
results in an error on the force constants that is quadratic with respect to that on
\rho_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(s\alpha)}
.
As in Ref.
8
, we approximate
\rho_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r},z)\approx\rho_{\mathrm{DFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r})
, where
\rho_{\mathrm{DFT}}
is the static induced-density obtained from DFT.
Such an approximation corresponds to considering
\mathcal{W}{=}0
in Eq. (
4
).
After some algebra, the force constant matrix elements read

C_{s\alpha,r\beta}(z)=C^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{s\alpha,r\beta}+\Delta C_{s\alpha,r\beta}(z)+\mathcal{O}(|\rho_{\mathrm{GDFT}}-\rho_{\mathrm{DFT}}|)^{2},

(7)

where
C^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{s\alpha,r\beta}{=}V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r}_{1})\rho_{\mathrm{DFT}}^{(r\beta)}(\bm{r}_{1})

are the static force constants within a DFT formalism [Eq. (
4
) with
\mathcal{W}{=}0
], and

\displaystyle\Delta C_{s\alpha,r\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r}_{1})\chi^{\mathcal{W}}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2},z)\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(r\beta)}(\bm{r}_{2})

\displaystyle-V_{\mathrm{DFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r}_{1})\chi^{0}_{\mathrm{DFT}}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})V_{\mathrm{DFT}}^{(r\beta)}(\bm{r}_{2})

\displaystyle-\rho_{\mathrm{DFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r}_{1})\Delta f_{\mathrm{Hxc}}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})\rho_{\mathrm{DFT}}^{(r\beta)}(\bm{r}_{2})

(8)

is a dynamical correction that includes excitonic effects though
\chi^{\mathcal{W}}
.

\chi^{0}_{\mathrm{DFT}}
is the static bare susceptibility evaluated with DFT orbitals, energies and occupations (see SM
[
30
]
), while

\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{GDFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r})=V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r})+f^{\mathrm{GDFT}}_{\mathrm{Hxc}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}_{1})\rho_{\mathrm{DFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r}_{1})

(9)

is the GDFT vertex evaluated at the DFT density and

V_{\mathrm{DFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r})=V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r})+f^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{\mathrm{Hxc}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}_{1})\rho_{\mathrm{DFT}}^{(s\alpha)}(\bm{r}_{1})
.

\Delta C_{s\alpha,r\beta}
accounts for the deviation from the DFT due to excitonic and dynamical effects.
Assuming that
\chi^{\mathcal{W}}
and
\chi^{0}_{\mathrm{DFT}}
are different only for few electronic bands
[
8
,
56
,
1
]
, the differential nature of Eq. (
8
) allows to compute
\Delta C_{s\alpha,r\beta}
via low-energy models, boosting the efficiency of the numerical implementations.
The local term
\rho_{\mathrm{DFT}}\Delta f_{\mathrm{Hxc}}\rho_{\mathrm{DFT}}
in Eq. (
8
) can be trivially evaluated.

Note that, if
\Delta f_{\mathrm{Hxc}}
is set to zero in Eqs. (
8
)-(
9
),
\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{GDFT}}{=}V_{\mathrm{DFT}}
and the correction of the force constant matrix
\Delta C(z)
consist in the difference between
\chi^{\mathcal{W}}(z)
and
\chi^{0}_{\mathrm{DFT}}
. From such difference, it is explicit how
\Delta C(z)
contains both dynamical and excitonic effects missed in standard DFT.

The static DFT dynamical matrix

and density

The parameters of the model are fitted on ab initio calculations. Reducing the Hilbert space, we neglect the contribution to Eq. (

We calculate the phonon frequency and full-width half maximum (FWHM) from the peak position and width of the spectral function

(details in SM
[

]

We rationalize the above results in terms of electron-phonon couplings. We crucially exploit the exact generalized Fermi golden rule derived in Ref.
[
1
]
,
which allows to determine the exact FWHM in terms of dressed electron-phonon couplings as

\displaystyle\textrm{FWHM}\Big|_{\Gamma/\mathrm{K}}=\frac{Ad_{\Gamma/\mathrm{K}}}{2\hbar}\frac{|g^{\Gamma/\mathrm{K}}|^{2}\hbar\omega_{\Gamma/\mathrm{K}}}{\hbar v_{\mathrm{g}}\hbar v_{\phi}},

(11)

where
A
is the area of the cell,
d_{\Gamma/\mathrm{K}}=2/1
is the Dirac cone degeneracy,
v_{\mathrm{g}}
and
v_{\phi}
are respectively the electronic group and phase velocities, and
g^{\Gamma/\mathrm{K}}
is the electron-phonon matrix element between the phonon of frequency
\omega_{\Gamma/\mathrm{K}}
and the
\pi
and
\pi^{*}
bands.
A similar formula has been used in Ref.
[
63
]
to analyze the Raman G peak.

The elements of Eq. (
11
) are evaluated at the electronic momentum resonant with
\omega_{\Gamma/\mathrm{K}}
(see SM
[
30
]
). Comparing the FWHM computed with GDFT [
\mathcal{W}{\neq}0
in Eq. (
4
)] and with DFT [
\mathcal{W}{=}0
in Eq. (
4
))], we deduce the enhancements of the electron-phonon couplings due to excitonic effects. At K, using Eq. (
11
) and the values reported in the SM, we find
|g^{\mathrm{K},\mathrm{GDFT}}|^{2}{\sim}26|g^{\mathrm{K},\mathrm{DFT}}|^{2}
.
This strong enhancement of
g^{\text{K}}
is in accordance with the analytical prediction of Refs.
[
25
,
26
]
, where a logarithmic divergence of the electron-phonon coupling at K is expected at
T{=}0K
for undoped graphene.
At
\Gamma
instead we have
|g^{\Gamma,\mathrm{GDFT}}|^{2}{\sim}5|g^{\Gamma,\mathrm{DFT}}|^{2}
. Notably, the enhancement of the FWHM at K is not compensated by the increase of the electronic group/phase velocities, while this compensation happens at
\Gamma
(see the SM
[
30
]
). In fact, the overall insensitiveness of phonon features to excitonic effects at zone center is due to the gauge field nature of the electron-phonon coupling. Indeed, as shown in the SM
[
30
]
, the effect of an atomic displacement is to simply shift the origin of the band structure from the K point, even in presence of electron-electron interactions.
As a consequence, the electron-phonon coupling reads
|g^{\Gamma}|{=}\frac{\beta}{b_{0}^{2}}v_{\textrm{g}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2M\omega_{\Gamma}}}
, where
b_{0}
is the interatomic distance and
\beta
is an adimensional coupling parameter, untouched by the electron-electron interaction (see SM for more details). It follows that the FWHM at zone center is proportional to
v_{\textrm{g}}/v_{\phi}
, which is weakly dependent on excitonic effects. Notice that this result can be seen as a very stringent and non trivial test for the self-consistency of the theory. For example, as shown numerically in the end matter, the gauge field protection of the FWHM is broken if only a small number of ladder diagrams are considered in the determination of the phonon’s Green functions, and is recovered only if the full series is computed. Similarly, but in a less stringent fashion, the same arguments explain the different behaviors of the phonon frequencies at
\Gamma
and K.

As further analysis, we append in the end matter of this letter our study of the behavior of the FWHM on doped graphene.

Outlook—

We showed how to calculate phonon dispersions and lifetimes with excitonic effects included in the density-density response function within a generalized Kohn-Sham framework.
We found that excitonic interactions have huge effects on the optical zone border phonons of graphene near K, where conducting electrons are scattered by the phonons.
Phonons near
\Gamma
are instead poorly influenced by excitonic effects, due to a gauge field protection which causes an opposite renormalization of phonon frequencies and lifetimes due to the increase of both the group velocity and electron-phonon coupling.
Combining previous theoretical estimations of the resistivity
[
69
]
with the strong increase of electron-phonon scattering observed here, we can infer that the Zone border phonons near K are likely to be the dominant source of intrinsic resistivity in graphene at room temperature.
Excitonic effects may be relevant also in the description of electron-phonon coupling in high-correlated and/or superconducting materials
[
70
,
71
]
.
Being an effect mostly driven by dimensionality, we expect excitonic effects to be as relevant on the response of low dimensional system such as carbon nanotubes or carbon atom chains, where exchange effects are known to be crucial also for electronic structure calculations
[
72
,
73
]
.

D_{s\alpha,r\beta}(z)=\frac{C_{s\alpha,r\beta}(z)+V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(s\alpha,r\beta)}(\bm{r}_{1})\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}(\bm{r}_{1})}{\sqrt{M_{s}M_{r}}},

(1)

is defined in Eq. 3 of the main text,
M_{l},M_{r}
are the nuclear masses.
V_{\mathrm{ext}}
is the ionic potential

V_{\mathrm{ext}}(\bm{r},\{\bm{R}_{1},\dots,\bm{R}_{M}\})=-e^{2}\sum_{l}^{N_{\text{ion}}}\frac{Z_{l}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{l}|}+\frac{e^{2}}{2N_{\text{el}}}\sum_{lm,l\neq m}^{N_{\text{ion}}}\frac{Z_{l}Z_{m}}{|\bm{R}_{l}-\bm{R}_{m}|}

(2)

experienced by the electrons, which depends on the electronic coordinate
\bm{r}
and parametrically on the collection of nuclear coordinates
\{\bm{R}_{1},\dots,\bm{R}_{M}\}
.
In Eq. (
2
),
Z_{l}
is the atomic number of the
l-
th ion and
e
is the electron charge.

V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(s\alpha,r\beta)}
in Eq. (
1
) is the derivative of
V_{\mathrm{ext}}
in Eq. (
2
) with respect to the displacements
u_{s\alpha}/u_{r\beta}
of the nuclei
s/r
along the Cartesian coordinates
\alpha/\beta

[
1

V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(s\alpha,r\beta)}(\bm{r})=\partialderivative{V_\ext\left(\bm r, \{\bm R_1\unpert{+}\bm u_1, \dots, \bm R_{N_\text{ion}}\unpert{+}\bm u_{N_\text{ion}} \}\right)}{u_{s\alpha}}{u_{r\beta}}\evaluated{}_{ \{ \bm u_1,\dots, \bm u_{N_\text{ion}}\} = \{0,\dots, 0\}},

(3)

where
\{\bm{R}_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}},\dots,\bm{R}_{N_{\text{ion}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}\}
are the nuclear equilibrium positions.
V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(s\alpha,r\beta)}(\bm{r})

can be expressed as (we omit the dependence on the nuclear coordinates for brevity)

V_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(s\alpha,r\beta)}(\bm{r})=\delta_{sr}\mathcal{M}_{s\alpha,s\beta}(\bm{r})+\frac{1}{N_{\text{el}}}C^{\text{ion-ion}}_{s\alpha,r\beta},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{s\alpha,s\beta}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=-e^{2}Z_{s}\frac{(r_{\alpha}{-}R^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}_{s\alpha})(r_{\beta}{-}R^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}_{s\beta})-\delta_{\alpha\beta}3|\bm{r}{-}\bm{R}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}_{s}|^{2}}{3|\bm{r}{-}\bm{R}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}_{s}|^{5}},

\displaystyle C^{\text{ion-ion}}_{s\alpha,r\beta}

\displaystyle{+}\delta_{sr}\sum\limits_{l}^{N_{\text{ion}}}e^{2}Z_{s}Z_{l}\frac{(R^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}_{s\alpha}{-}R^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}_{l\alpha})(R^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}_{s\beta}{-}R^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}_{l\beta})-\delta_{\alpha\beta}3|\bm{R}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}_{s}{-}\bm{R}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}_{l}|^{5}}{3|\bm{R}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}_{s}{-}\bm{R}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(0)}}_{l}|^{5}}.

Reformulation in terms of Feynman diagrams

In this Section, we reformulate the theoretical framework proposed in the main text to explicit the Feynman diagram contributions in the phonon calculation. Such Feynman diagrams are illustrated in Fig.

.
To ease notation, we neglect the double-counting term discussed in Sec.

and consider a finite system, as in the main text.
Both the double-counting term and the extension to periodic crystals are straightforward to include.

We start discussing nonlocal exchange effects for the electrons.
We define the (non-interacting) DFT electron Green’s function as

\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{DFT}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},z)=\sum\limits_{i}\psi^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{i}(\bm{r})\psi^{DFT*}_{i}(\bm{r}^{\prime})\left[\frac{f^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{i}}{z^{*}-\varepsilon^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{i}}+\frac{(1-f^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{i})}{z-\varepsilon^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{i}}\right]\ ,

where
\psi^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{i}
,
\varepsilon^{\mathrm{DFT}}_{i}
and

are wavefunctions, energies, and occupations obtained with DFT in a LDA or GGA functional approximation.

\mathcal{G}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},z)=\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{DFT}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},z)+\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{DFT}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}_{1},z)\Sigma(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2},z)\mathcal{G}(\bm{r}_{2},\bm{r}^{\prime},z)

where

is the electron Green’s function with nonlocal exchange effects included and the self-energy can be written as

\Sigma(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},z)=-n(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})W(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})\ .

(16)

In the previous equation,
n
is the electron density matrix, and

is the static screened Coulomb interaction, defined below.
In the quasi-particle approximation, commonly used in the context of many-body perturbation theory
[

\mathcal{G}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},z)=\sum\limits_{i}\psi_{i}(\bm{r})\psi^{*}_{i}(\bm{r}^{\prime})\left[\frac{f_{i}}{z^{*}-\varepsilon_{i}}+\frac{(1-f_{i})}{z-\varepsilon_{i}}\right]\ ,

where
\psi_{i}
,
\varepsilon_{i}
and

are the wavefunctions, energies and occupations including nonlocal exchange effects.
In the so-called orbital approximation

is the non-interacting irreducible polarizability, i.e. the bubble diagram without interaction between the electron-hole pair.

VI
Gauge field at
\mathbf{q}=0

In a low-energy Dirac cone model, the static interaction between electrons and phonons at
\mathbf{q}=0
can be described via a gauge-field coupling
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
11
]
. We restrict our analysis around the K point, even though similar arguments hold for K’ point as well. In particular, near the K point the Hamiltonian can be written in the form:

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{A}}=\sum_{s}\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{s}\quad\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{s}=l_{s}\hat{\mathbf{z}}\times\mathbf{u}_{s}\quad l_{s}=\pm 1,

(44)

\displaystyle H_{\mathbf{k}}=\hbar v_{\textrm{F}}\left(k_{x}+\frac{\beta}{b_{0}^{2}}A_{x},k_{y}+\frac{\beta}{b_{0}^{2}}A_{y},0\right)\cdot\bm{\sigma}^{\textrm{P}}

(45)

where the sum over
s
is the sum over the atoms of graphene,
\mathbf{u}_{s}
are atomic displacements with the same periodicity of the lattice (i.e. corresponding to
\mathbf{q}=0
),
b_{0}=a_{0}/\sqrt{3}
is the interatomic distance (
a_{o}
being the lattice parameter), and
\bm{\sigma}^{\textrm{P}}
is the vector containing the pseudospin Pauli matrices.
\beta
is an adimensional coupling constant. From the model of Eq.
45
, it follows that the phonon distortion causes a lateral displacement of the Dirac cone, and therefore it induces a gap opening which is proportional to the Fermi velocity. More precisely

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\epsilon_{\pi/\pi^{*}\mathbf{K}}}{\partial u_{sx}}=\langle u_{\pi/\pi^{*}\mathbf{K}}|\frac{\partial H_{\mathbf{k}}}{\partial u_{sx}}|u_{\pi/\pi^{*}\mathbf{K}}\rangle=l_{s}\frac{\beta}{b_{0}^{2}}\langle u_{\pi/\pi^{*}\mathbf{K}}|\frac{\partial H_{\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{y}}|u_{\pi/\pi^{*}\mathbf{K}}\rangle=\pm l_{s}\frac{\beta}{b_{0}^{2}}v_{\textrm{F}},

(46)

and similarly for the derivative with respect to
u_{sy}
. It is evident that the electron-phonon matrix element is here proportional to the Fermi velocity via the adimensional coupling
\beta
.

The Hamiltonian description of Eq. (
45
) is shown for the case of constant Fermi velocity. Though, under proper hypothesis, it can be extended to the case where the band velocity is renormalized by the electron-electron interaction. In this case, the full Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle H_{\mathbf{k}}=H^{0}_{\mathbf{k}}+H^{\textrm{e-ph}}_{\mathbf{k}}+H^{\textrm{e-e}}_{\mathbf{k}},\,H_{0}=\hbar v_{\textrm{F}}\left(k_{x},k_{y},0\right)\cdot\bm{\sigma}^{\textrm{P}},\,H^{\textrm{e-ph}}_{\mathbf{k}}=\hbar v_{\textrm{F}}\left(\frac{\beta}{b_{0}^{2}}A_{x},\frac{\beta}{b_{0}^{2}}A_{y},0\right)\cdot\bm{\sigma}^{\textrm{P}}.

(47)

To write the above expression, we implicitly assumed that the electron-electron interaction is static. Then, we can again determine the strength of the electron-phonon interaction, now dressed by the electron-electron interaction, by evaluating the change in band energies with respect to a displacement. In the approximation of cylindrical isotropy of our low energy model, the action of
H^{\textrm{e-ph}}_{\mathbf{k}}
still consists in shifting Dirac cone center origin from
\mathbf{K}
to
\mathbf{K(u)}
. If we assume that the action of
H^{\textrm{e-e}}_{\mathbf{k}}
is independent of the origin of the Dirac cone, i.e. that
H^{\textrm{e-e}}_{\mathbf{k}}=H^{\textrm{e-e}}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{K(u)}|)
, then the action of the dressed electron-phonon is still equivalent to a gauge coupling. In other words, the action of the dressed electron-phonon coupling is still to shift the modified band dispersion from
\mathbf{K}
to
\mathbf{K(u)}
. Of course, the above assumptions are not exact for realistic models, but are expected to be satisfied to a satisfactory extent for very small
\mathbf{u}
. We therefore expect

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\epsilon_{\pi/\pi^{*}\mathbf{K+k}}}{\partial u_{sx}}\sim\pm l_{s}\frac{\beta}{b_{0}^{2}}v_{\textrm{g}}(\mathbf{k}),

(48)

These considerations practically provides a ‘gauge field protection’ for the linewidth value, as discussed in the next section.

VII
Phonon lifetimes at
\Gamma
and K within logarithmic Dirac cone model

In this section, we derive an expression of the phonon lifetimes at
\Gamma
and K with the assumption that the electronic bands are isotropic around K, thus

\varepsilon_{\pi^{*}/\pi\bm{k}}=\pm v_{\phi}(\kappa)\hbar\kappa

(49)

where
\kappa=|\bm{k}-\mathrm{K}|
and

is the electron phase velocity,

where

is the self-consistent potential induced by the phonon perturbation (which can be both from DFT or GDFT).
We focus on the TO mode at K and the LO mode at
\Gamma
. We omit the branch index to ease the notation.
In the range

, only
n=\pi^{*}
,
m=\pi
contributions are relevant.
We also consider the continuous limit of the
\bm{k}

\kappa

at
\Gamma/\mathrm{K}

\Gamma

VIII
Computational details—extended

The DFT band structure and force constants
C^{\mathrm{ion-ion}}
and
C^{\mathrm{KS}}
are obtained with the QUANTUM ESPRESSO package
[
13
]
within Perdew-Burke-Erzenov (PBE) approximation
[
14
]
.
We adopt norm-conserving pseudopotentials to model the electron-ion interaction and a kinetic energy cutoff of
90
Ry.
The force constants are calculated with DFPT using a Sternheimer approach. The screened interaction
W
is modeled with Dirac-cone screening in the random-phase approximation and calculated self-consistently with the band structure
[
7
]

The excitonic and/or dynamical corrections
\Delta C(z)
are calculated within a tight-binding approach using
an in-house built python3 code. We employ the five nearest-neighbor tight-binding model of Ref.
7
, including only
\pi
/
\pi^{*}
bands, with the parameters of the model fitted over the ab-initio band structure. We use the fitted model to compute
\chi^{0}_{\mathrm{DFT}}
on fine grids. In addition, we compute the band structure including nonlocal exchange effects.
As a result of the reduced Hilbert space of the tight-binding model, in our procedure we neglect the contribution of the double counting of the Fock-like term.
Thus,
\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{GDFT}}=V_{\mathrm{KS}}
and
\Delta f_{\mathrm{Hxc}}=0
.

We used a
\mathcal{W}
cutoff in reciprocal space of
4.5
Å
-1
. The screened interaction
\mathcal{W}
is modeled with Dirac-cone screening in the random-phase approximation and calculated self-consistently with the band structure
[
7
]
.
Doping effects are included both in the occupation factors and the screened interaction
\mathcal{W}
.
The screened matrix elements
V_{\mathrm{DFT}}
are computed with a first nearest-neighbor tight-binding model with parameters fitted over a DFPT ab-initio calculation, as explained in Ref.
15
.

The matrix elements
\Delta C_{s\alpha,r\beta}
are solved with the Sternheimer equation explained in Ref.
1
, with linear mixing and a convergence threshold of the force constants of
10^{-12}
.
We used telescopic k grids
[
16
]
that in the dense region around K are equivalent to a
2896\times 2896
uniform grid, while in the coarse region they correspond to a
90\times 90
uniform grid. The parameters are
p=3
,
\mathcal{N}=25
,
l=6
and
L=10
in the DFT calculation while
L=11
in the tight-binding calculations.
The electronic temperature is set to
300
K in Fig. 1 of the main text and
70
K in all the others. The same results at 300 K are presented in the SI.

We calculate the phonon frequency and full-width half maximum (FWHM) from the peak position and width of the spectral function

A(\omega)={-}\sum_{s\alpha}{\rm Im}\left[\frac{\omega}{\pi}G_{s\alpha,s\alpha}(\omega)\right].

(64)

Dynamical effects are computed in the neighborhood of
\Gamma
and K evaluating the dynamical matrix at the static frequencies
\omega_{\Gamma}
and
\omega_{\textrm{K}}
.
Due to the finite k-point mesh, we replace the
0^{+}
limit in the definition of the phonon Green’s function with a finite damping
\eta=10
meV.

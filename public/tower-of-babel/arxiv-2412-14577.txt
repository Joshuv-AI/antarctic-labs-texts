Unconditional stability of radially symmetric steady sates of compressible viscous fluids with inflow/outflow boundary conditions
Eduard Feireisl, Piotr Gwiazda, Agnieszka Świerczewska-Gwiazda

Abstract
We show that certain radially symmetric steady states of compressible viscous fluids in domains with inflow/outflow boundary conditions are unconditionally stable. This means that any not necessarily radially symmetric solution of the associated evolutionary problem converges to a single radially symmetric steady state.

1
Introduction

The problem of global asymptotic stability of steady state solutions to problems in fluid mechanics is both mathematically challenging and physically relevant. Our goal is to consider
compressible viscous fluids described by means of the

barotropic Navier–Stokes (NS) system
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})

\displaystyle=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho)

\displaystyle={\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}),\ \mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}\right),

(1.2)

for
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega
,
\Omega\subset R^{3}
. The unknowns describing the state of the system are the fluid mass density
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
and the velocity
{\bf u}={\bf u}(t,x)
.
The pressure
p=p(\varrho)
is related to the density by a specific equation of state (EOS), where we suppose

\displaystyle p\in C[0,\overline{\varrho})\cap C^{1}(0,\overline{\varrho}),\ 0<\overline{\varrho}\ {\leq}\infty,\ p(0)=0,\ p^{\prime}(\varrho)>0\ \mbox{for}\ 0<\varrho<\overline{\varrho}.

(1.3)

The case
\overline{\varrho}<\infty
characterizes the so–called hard sphere pressure law. A detailed description of the
pressure–density equation of state is presented in Section
2.2
. The viscous stress
\mathbb{S}
is given by
Newton’s rheological law

\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})=\mu\Big(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I}\Big)+\lambda{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u},

(1.4)

with constant viscosity coefficients
\mu>0
,
\lambda\geq 0
.

Our goal is to establish global stability of the radially symmetric steady states. Accordingly, the underlying spatial domain
\Omega
is radially symmetric. We focus on two basic
problems corresponding to the outflow and inflow–outflow boundary conditions.

•

Outflow problem on exterior domain.

We consider

\Omega=R^{3}\setminus\overline{B}_{\overline{r}},\ \mbox{where}\ B_{\overline{r}}=\{|x|<{{\overline{r}}}\},

(1.5)

is an exterior domain, and impose the outflow boundary conditions for the velocity,

{\bf u}|_{|x|=\overline{r}}=u_{B}{\bf n},\ u_{B}>0.

(1.6)

Here and hereafter, the symbol
{\bf n}
denotes the
outer
normal vector to
\partial\Omega
.

In addition, the far field behaviour is specified,

{\bf u}\to 0,\ \varrho\to\varrho_{\infty}\ \mbox{as}\ |x|\to\infty,\ 0<\varrho_{\infty}<\overline{\varrho}.

(1.7)

•

Inflow–outflow problem on a bounded domain.

We consider

\Omega=B_{r+}\setminus\overline{B}_{r^{-}},\ 0<r^{-}<r^{+},

(1.8)

with the inflow–outflow boundary conditions

\displaystyle{\bf u}|_{|x|=r^{-}}

\displaystyle=-u_{B}^{-}{\bf n},\ u_{B}^{-}>0,{\varrho}|_{|x|=r^{-}}=\varrho_{B}>0,

(1.9)

\displaystyle{\bf u}|_{|x|=r^{+}}

\displaystyle=u_{B}^{+}{\bf n},\ u_{B}^{+}>0.

(1.10)

In both cases we also consider a vector field
{\bf u}_{B}
defined on the whole
physical space
R^{3}
that coincides with the boundary data on
\partial\Omega
.

The problem of existence and qualitative behaviour
of steady states to the NS system with the aforementioned boundary conditions has been extensively studied in a series of papers by Hashimoto and Matsumura
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
, Hashimoto, Nishibata, and Sugizaki
[
9
]
, or, more recently, Huang et al.
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
. Besides a detailed description of the steady state, their stability with respect to
radially symmetric
perturbations is established. Our goal is to show that stability in fact holds in a much larger class of weak solutions to the NS system, not necessarily radially symmetric. In contrast with the radially symmetric
case that can be reduced to a relatively simple 1-D problem, the general 3-D case is much more complex, in particular a suitable existence theory
in the class of global–in–time large data solutions is necessary.

Our approach is based on a recently developed theory of
weak solutions to the NS system with general inflow–outflow boundary conditions
[
4
]
, see also
Choe, Novotný and Yang
[
1
]
, Nečasová, Novotný and Roy
[
13
]
. The distance between a solution of the evolutionary problem and the steady state is measured by the
Bregman divergence (relative energy) that can be seen as a linear perturbation of the total energy of the system. In order to establish convergence, the
coercive terms in the relative energy inequality must be controlled by dissipation. To this end, qualitative properties of the steady state play a crucial role. The relevant piece of information in the outflow exterior domain problem is provided by the results Hashimoto and Matsumura
[
7
]
, Huang and Nishibata
[
11
]
. The inflow–outflow problem is considered as a perturbation of the same system of equations and boundary conditions posed on a “flat” strip like domain. As an intermediate step, we therefore obtain an unconditional convergence result on a periodic strip. Finally, convergence for the inflow–outflow problem (
1.8
)–(
1.10
) results from
careful analysis of the perturbed steady solution.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the concept of weak solution to the NS system, and formulate our main results. Section
2.3
is devoted to the steady solutions and their basic properties. In Section
3
, we introduce the relative energy, together with the associated relative energy inequality. The proof of main results is completed in Section
4
.

2
Problem formulation, weak solutions, and main results

We suppose that
\Omega\subset R^{3}
is a domain with compact Lipschitz boundary. The boundary velocity is determined by a
(smooth) function
{\bf u}_{B}\in C^{1}_{c}(R^{3};R^{3})
. Similarly, the
boundary density on the inflow part of the boundary is the trace of
\varrho_{B}\in C^{1}(R^{3})
. We consider the problem on the infinite space–time cylinder

[0,\infty)\times\Omega
.

2.1
Weak formulation

The weak formulation of equation of continuity (
1.1
), together with the inflow/outflow boundary conditions reads:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varrho\partial_{t}\varphi+\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\left[\int_{\Omega}\varrho\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi\varrho_{b}[{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{-}{\rm d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi\varrho[{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{+}{\rm d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.1)

for any test function
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,\infty)\times R^{3})
, and any
\tau>0
.
Here and hereafter,

[a]^{+}=\max\{0,a\},\ [a]^{-}=\min\{0,a\}

denote the positive and negative part of
e\in R
, respectively.
In addition, we consider the weak solutions satisfying the renormalized version of (
2.1
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(b(\varrho)\partial_{t}\varphi+b(\varrho){\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi+\left(b(\varrho)-b^{\prime}(\varrho)\varrho\right){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\varphi\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\left[\int_{\Omega}b(\varrho)\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi b(\varrho_{B})[{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{-}{\rm d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi b(\varrho)[{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{+}{\rm d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.2)

for any test function
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,\infty)\times R^{3})
, any
b\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
,
b^{\prime}\in C_{c}[0,\infty)
, and
any
\tau>0
.
All quantities are supposed to be at least integrable. We refer to

[
4
, Chapter 3, Section 3.3.1]
for the interpretation of the “trace” of the function
\varrho
on the outflow component of the boundary.

The momentum equation (
1.2
) is satisfied in the sense of distributions:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\left[\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

(2.3)

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}([0,\infty;R^{3})\times\Omega)
,
\tau>0
.
Formula (
2.3
) requires integrability of the velocity gradient. In particular,
the velocity
{\bf u}
admits a trace on
\partial\Omega
, and we impose

{\bf u}={\bf u}_{B}\ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega.

(2.4)

Similarly to the above, we tacitly assume that all integrands are well defined and at least locally integrable. In particular,

0\leq\varrho{<\overline{\varrho}}\ \ \mbox{a.e. in}\ (0,\infty)\times\Omega,

(2.5)

where
\overline{\varrho}\leq\infty
is specified by the EOS in
Section
2.2
below. The boundary condition (
2.4
)
as well as the far the relevant far field conditions for the velocity
{\bf u}
are interpreted in the weak sense,

{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(0,T;D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{3})),

where the
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{3})
is the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;R^{3})
with respect to the gradient norm
\|\nabla_{x}{\bf v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3\times 3})}
.

Finally, we consider only the weak solutions satisfying some form of energy balance. The latter can be written in the form:

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{\infty},{\bf u}_{B}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{S}\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\Big):\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\Big)}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(\varrho_{\infty})(\varrho-\varrho_{\infty})-P(\varrho_{\infty})\Big)[{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{+}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(P(\varrho_{B})-P^{\prime}(\varrho_{\infty})(\varrho_{B}-\varrho_{\infty})-P(\varrho_{\infty})\Big)[{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{-}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\cdot({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{\infty})(\varrho-\varrho_{\infty})-p(\varrho_{\infty})\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}_{B}-{\bf u})\cdot{\rm div}_{x}({\bf u}_{B}\otimes{\bf u}_{B})\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})\cdot{\bf u}_{B}+p^{\prime}(\varrho_{\infty})\left(\varrho_{\infty}-\varrho\right)\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}{(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{B})\cdot}({\bf u}_{B}-{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.6)

where we have set

E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{\infty},{\bf u}_{B}\right)=\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B}|^{2}+P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(\varrho_{\infty})(\varrho-\varrho_{\infty})-P(\varrho_{\infty}),

with the pressure potential

P^{\prime}(\varrho)\varrho-P(\varrho)=p(\varrho).

(2.7)

Note that (
2.6
) is nothing other than a particular form of a general relative energy inequality introduced in Section
3
below. The constant

\varrho_{\infty}
coincides with the far field value of the density prescribed in (
1.7
), and can be taken arbitrary non–negative number if
\Omega
is a bounded domain.

If
\Omega
is bounded, it is possible to establish the existence of weak solutions satisfying (
2.6
) in the differential form
[
4
, Chapter 3, Section 3.2]
:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{\infty},{\bf u}_{B}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}{\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\Big):\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\Big)}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(\varrho_{\infty})(\varrho-\varrho_{\infty})-P(\varrho_{\infty})\Big)[{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{+}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(P(\varrho_{B})-P^{\prime}(\varrho_{\infty})(\varrho_{B}-\varrho_{\infty})-P(\varrho_{\infty})\Big)[{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{-}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\cdot({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{\infty})(\varrho-\varrho_{\infty})-p(\varrho_{\infty})\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}_{B}-{\bf u})\cdot{\rm div}_{x}({\bf u}_{B}\otimes{\bf u}_{B})\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{B})\cdot{\bf u}_{B}+p^{\prime}(\varrho_{\infty})\left(\varrho_{\infty}-\varrho\right)\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}{(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{B})\cdot}({\bf u}_{B}-{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(2.8)

satisfied in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,\infty)
. Validity of the differential form of the energy inequality
(
2.8
) can be extended to unbounded (exterior) spatial domains as long as

\varrho_{\infty}=0
, more exactly on condition that the total mass of the fluid is finite,

\int_{\Omega}\varrho\ \,{\rm d}{x}<\infty,

see
[
2
]
. For
\varrho_{\infty}>0
considered in
(
1.7
), the existence of weak solutions satisfying (
2.8
) is an open problem, cf. also Novotný and Pokorný
[
14
]
.

2.2
Pressure–density equation of state

The specific form of the equation of state (EOS) relating the pressure
p
to the density of a barotropic fluid plays a crucial role in the existence theory as well as in the analysis of the long–time behaviour. In addition to
(
1.3
), we suppose

p\in C^{2}(0,\overline{\varrho}),\ p^{\prime\prime}(\varrho)>0\ \mbox{in}\ (0,\overline{\varrho}),\ \lim_{\varrho\to\overline{\varrho}-}p(\varrho)=\infty.

(2.9)

There are two iconic examples of EOS satisfying (
1.3
), (
2.9
):

•

Isentropic EOS.

p(\varrho)=a\varrho^{\gamma},\ a>0,\gamma>1,\ \overline{\varrho}=\infty.

(2.10)

•

Hard–sphere EOS.

0<\overline{\varrho}<\infty,\ p(\varrho)\approx(\overline{\varrho}-\varrho)^{-\beta}\ \mbox{for}\ \varrho\to\overline{\varrho},\ \beta>0.

(2.11)

Here and hereafter, the symbol
a\approx b
denotes that
a\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}b
and
b\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}a
, where

a\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}b
means
a\leq cb
, where
c>0
is a generic constant.

The
existence
of weak solutions of the NS system
with the isentropic EOS
for
\gamma\geq\frac{9}{5}
if
d=3
, and
\gamma\geq\frac{3}{2}
if
d=2
was established in the seminal work of Lions
[
12
]
. The results
have been later extended to
\gamma>\frac{d}{2}
in
[
5
]
.
As for the hard–sphere EOS, we refer to
[
6
]
and the monograph
[
4
, Chapter 9]
for the existence theory for
\beta>3
.
The inflow–outflow problem for the hard–sphere EOS has been elaborated in detail in Choe, Novotný, and Yang
[
1
]
. As they pointed out,

global–in–time
weak solutions exist as long as
\beta>\frac{5}{2}

and the boundary velocity
{\bf u}_{B}
admits an extension

{\bf u}_{B}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega},R^{3}),\ {\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\geq 0,{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{B}\not\equiv 0.

(2.12)

As we shall see below, condition (
2.12
) is satisfied in all cases considered in the present paper. Finally, we mention the recent result
of Nečasová, Novotný, and Roy
[
13
]
concerning the hard–sphere pressure EOS and exterior spatial domains.

2.3
Steady solutions

Steady solutions
(\varrho_{s},{\bf u}_{s})
satisfy the stationary NS system:

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{s}{\bf u}_{s})

\displaystyle=0,

(2.13)

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{s}{\bf u}_{s}\otimes{\bf u}_{s})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho_{s})

\displaystyle={{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s})}

(2.14)

in
\Omega
, along with the relevant boundary conditions. In this paper, we focus on strong solutions satisfying (
2.13
), (
2.14
) in the classical sense.

2.3.1
Exterior outflow problem

The existence of a radially symmetric steady solution to the NS system
(
2.13
), (
2.14
)
on the exterior domain (
1.5
), supplemented with the outflow boundary conditions (
1.6
), and the far field conditions (
1.7
) was
established
by Hashimoto, Nishibata, and Sugizaki
[
9
]
. More specifically,
given

0<\varrho_{\infty}<\overline{\varrho},

(2.15)

and a sufficiently small outflow velocity

0<u_{B}\leq\overline{u}\Big(\mu,{\lambda,\overline{r}},\ \varrho_{\infty},p^{\prime}(\varrho_{\infty})\Big),

(2.16)

there exists a unique radially symmetric solution of the problem in the form

\varrho_{s}(x)=\widetilde{\varrho}(|x|),\ {\bf u}_{s}(x)=\frac{x}{|x|}{\widetilde{u}}(|x|),\ |x|\geq{\overline{r}}.

The functions
\widetilde{\varrho}=\widetilde{\varrho}(r)
,
{\widetilde{u}}={\widetilde{u}}(r)
satisfy the equations

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\left(r^{2}\tilde{\rho}\tilde{u}\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(2.17)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}\tilde{u}\partial_{r}\tilde{u}+\partial_{r}p(\tilde{\rho})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu+\lambda\right)\partial_{r}\left(\frac{\partial_{r}(r^{2}\tilde{u})}{r^{2}}\right),

(2.18)

with the far-field condition

\lim_{r\to\infty}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u})(r)=(\rho_{\infty},0),

(2.19)

and the boundary condition

\tilde{u}(\overline{r})=u_{B}.

(2.20)

The solution enjoys the following properties (cf.
[
9
, Lemma 1.1]
):

\displaystyle 0<\widetilde{\varrho}

\displaystyle<\varrho_{\infty},\ \Big(\varrho_{\infty}-\widetilde{\varrho}(|x|)\Big)\approx u_{B}^{2}|x|^{-4},\partial_{r}\widetilde{\varrho}>0,\ \partial_{r}\widetilde{\varrho}(|x|)\approx u_{B}^{2}|x|^{-5},

(2.21)

\displaystyle{\widetilde{u}}(\overline{r})

\displaystyle=-u_{B},\ {\widetilde{u}}<0,\ \lim_{|x|\to\infty}{\widetilde{u}}(|x|)=0,\ \partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}>0,\ \partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}(|x|)\approx u_{B}|x|^{-3},{{\widetilde{u}}(|x|)\approx u_{B}|x|^{-2}}.

(2.22)

Note carefully

0<\varrho_{s}(|x|)\leq\varrho_{\infty}\ \mbox{for all}\ |x|>{\overline{r}}.

In particular, the construction elaborated in
[
9
]
relies only on the local properties of the
pressure EOS around the far field value
\varrho_{\infty}
and applies to the hard sphere pressure as long as (
2.15
) holds.

Finally, a routine manipulation yields

\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{s}=\left(\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}(|x|)-\frac{{\widetilde{u}}(|x|)}{|x|}\right)\frac{x\otimes x}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{{\widetilde{u}}(|x|)}{|x|}{\rm Id}.

(2.23)

Moreover, exploiting the fact that
\varrho_{s}
,
{\bf u}_{s}
solve the equation of continuity, we deduce

{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}(x)=-\frac{1}{\varrho_{s}(x)}\nabla_{x}\varrho_{s}(x)\cdot{\bf u}_{s}=-\frac{1}{\widetilde{\varrho}(|x|)}\partial_{r}\widetilde{\varrho}(|x|)\ {\widetilde{u}}(|x|)>0.

(2.24)

In particular, the condition (
2.12
), necessary for the existence of global–in–time solutions to the evolutionary problem, is satisfied for

{\bf u}_{B}={\bf u}_{s}
.

2.3.2
Inflow–outflow problem on a periodic strip

Similarly to the preceding section, we look for solutions of the inflow–outflow problem (
1.8
)–(
1.10
) in the class of radially symmetric functions. As an intermediate step, we first introduce a similar problem in the simpler geometry of a (periodic) strip.

Specifically, consider

\Omega=\left\{x=(y,r)\ \Big|\ y\in\mathbb{T}^{2},\ r\in(0,1)\right\},

(2.25)

where
\mathbb{T}^{2}
denotes a 2-D (flat) torus,
with the inflow–outflow conditions

\displaystyle{\bf u}|_{r=0}

\displaystyle=(0,0,u_{B}^{-}),\ \varrho|_{r=0}=\varrho_{B},

(2.26)

\displaystyle{\bf u}|_{r=1}

\displaystyle=(0,0,u_{B}^{+}).

(2.27)

The corresponding steady state depends only on the vertical coordinate
r
,

\varrho_{s}=\widetilde{\varrho}(r),\ {\bf u}_{s}=(0,0,{\widetilde{u}}(r)).

The system of equations reads

\displaystyle\partial_{r}(\widetilde{\varrho}{\widetilde{u}})

\displaystyle=0\ \Rightarrow\ \widetilde{\varrho}(r){\widetilde{u}}(r)=\varrho_{B}u^{-}_{B},

(2.28)

\displaystyle\varrho_{B}{\bf u}^{-}_{B}\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}+\partial_{r}p(\widetilde{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu+\lambda\right)\partial^{2}_{r,r}{\widetilde{u}}.

(2.29)

Equation (
2.29
) can be integrated to obtain

\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu+{\lambda}\right)\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}=p\left(\frac{\varrho_{B}u^{-}_{B}}{{\widetilde{u}}}\right)+\varrho_{B}{\bf u}^{-}_{B}{\widetilde{u}}+\Lambda,\ {\widetilde{u}}(0)=u^{-}_{B}>0,

(2.30)

where the value of the integration constant
\Lambda
is determined by the outflow velocity

{\widetilde{u}}(1)=u^{+}_{B}.

(2.31)

The first observation is that the unique solution
{\widetilde{u}}
of (
2.30
)
remains strictly positive as long as the pressure satisfies

{\lim_{\varrho\to\overline{\varrho}-}p(\varrho)=\infty.}

(2.32)

This is definitely true for the isentropic EOS with
\overline{\varrho}=\infty
. In the case
of the hard–sphere EOS, we even obtain a positive lower bound on
{\widetilde{u}}
.

The second observation is a comparison principle. Suppose

{\widetilde{u}}_{1}
,
{\widetilde{u}}_{2}
are two solutions of the initial value problem
(
2.30
) with the same initial datum but different constants

\Lambda_{1}>\Lambda_{2}.

Then

{\widetilde{u}}_{1}>{\widetilde{u}}_{2}\ \mbox{in}\ (0,1].

(2.33)

Indeed, as
\Lambda_{1}>\Lambda_{2}
, we have
{\widetilde{u}}_{1}>{\widetilde{u}}_{2}
on

(0,\delta)
for some
\delta>0
. Let
\delta
by maximal with this property, meaning

{\widetilde{u}}_{1}>{\widetilde{u}}_{2}\ {\mbox{in}\ (0,\delta)},\ {\widetilde{u}}_{1}(\delta)={\widetilde{u}}_{2}(\delta).

Necessarily,

{\partial_{r}({\widetilde{u}}_{1}-{\widetilde{u}}_{2})(\delta)\leq 0},

in contrast with equation (
2.30
) yielding

\partial_{r}({\widetilde{u}}_{1}-{\widetilde{u}}_{2})(\delta)=\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu+{\lambda}\right)^{-1}(\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2})>0.

We conclude there is at most one solution satisfying the boundary condition

{\widetilde{u}}(1)=u^{+}_{B}.

Choosing

\Lambda+p(\varrho_{B})+\varrho_{b}(u_{B}^{-})^{2}=0

we obtain the constant solution
{\widetilde{u}}=u^{-}_{B}
. It is easy to check that one can solve the boundary value problem for any

u_{B}^{+}>u_{B}^{-}
choosing the integration constant
\Lambda
large enough. Moreover, as
\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}
satisfies a homogeneous first order linear equation, we get

\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}>0.

(2.34)

Finally, going back to (

\partial_{r}\widetilde{\varrho}=-\frac{\widetilde{\varrho}}{{\widetilde{u}}}\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\approx-\frac{\varrho_{B}}{u^{-}_{B}}\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}

(2.35)

provided

0<u^{+}_{B}-u^{-}_{B}\leq\delta,

(2.36)

\delta>0
small enough.

2.3.3
Inflow–outflow problem in the radially symmetric setting

Finally, we consider radially symmetric solutions of the
system (
2.13
), (
2.14
) on the spatial domain (

), with the

), (

). Specifically, we set

where

\displaystyle\partial_{r}(r^{2}\widetilde{\varrho}{\widetilde{u}})

\displaystyle=0,

(2.37)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varrho}{\widetilde{u}}\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}+\partial_{r}p(\widetilde{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu+{\lambda}\right)\partial_{r}\left(\frac{\partial_{r}(r^{2}{\widetilde{u}})}{r^{2}}\right),

(2.38)

supplemented with the boundary conditions

\displaystyle{\widetilde{u}}(r^{-})=u^{-}_{B},\ \widetilde{\varrho}(r^{-})=\varrho_{B},

(2.39)

\displaystyle{\widetilde{u}}(r^{+})=u^{+}_{B}.

(2.40)

It follows from the equation of continuity (

In particular, the momentum equation (

(2.42)

3
Relative energy and relative energy inequality

We recall the relative energy inequality satisfied by any weak solution of
NS system specified in Section
2.1
.

3.1
Relative energy

The relative energy is defined as

E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|R,{\bf U}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf U}|^{2}+P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(R)(\varrho-R)-P(R).

(3.1)

As
p
is strictly increasing, the pressure potential
P
is strictly convex,
the energy

E(\varrho,{\bf m})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}|^{2}}{\varrho}+P(\varrho)

is a strictly convex function of
(\varrho,{\bf m})
;
whence the relative energy represents a Bregman divergence between the pairs

(m=\varrho{\bf u},r)
and
(M=r{\bf U},R)
, see
[
4
, Chapter 3, Section 3.1]
. Unlike in the energy inequality (
2.6
), the quantities
M
and
R
are arbitrary smooth functions of

t
and
x
.

3.2
Relative energy inequality

The weak solutions of the NS system satisfying the integrated variant of the
energy inequality (
2.6
) satisfy the
relative energy inequality
in the form:

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|R,{\bf U}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}{\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf U}\Big):\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf U}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(R)(\varrho-R)-P(R)\Big)[{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{+}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(P(\varrho_{B})-P^{\prime}(R)(\varrho_{B}-R)-P(R)\Big)[{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{-}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf U})\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\cdot({\bf u}-{\bf U})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(R)(\varrho-R)-p(R)\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\Big[\partial_{t}(R{\bf U})+{\rm div}_{x}(R{\bf U}\otimes{\bf U})+\nabla_{x}p(R)-{{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf U})}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf u}-{\bf U})\cdot{\bf U}+p^{\prime}(R)\left(1-\frac{\varrho}{R}\right)\Big)\Big[\partial_{t}R+{\rm div}_{x}(R{\bf U})\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\varrho}{R}-1\right)({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot{{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf U})}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(3.2)

for any
\tau\geq 0
. Here
(\varrho,{\bf u})
is a weak solution while

(R,U)
is a pair of sufficiently smooth “test” functions,

R>0,\ {{\bf U}|_{\partial\Omega}={\bf u}_{B}|_{\partial\Omega}}.

(3.3)

In the case of an exterior domain, we also require the test functions to
approach the far field state sufficiently fast

{\bf U}\to 0,\ R\to\varrho_{\infty}\ \mbox{as}\ |x|\to\infty

(3.4)

so that all relevant integrals are finite. Note carefully that the energy inequality (
2.6
), that is an integral part of the

definition
of weak solution, is a special case of the relative energy inequality (
3.2
) for
r=\varrho_{\infty}
,
{\bf U}={\bf u}_{\infty}
.
Still (
3.2
) can be derived from (
2.6
) and the two field equations (
2.1
), (
2.3
), see e.g.

[
4
, Chapter 3]
.

If a weak solution satisfies the differential form of the energy inequality (
2.8
), the
differential form of the
relative energy inequality holds:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|R,{\bf U}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}{\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf U}\Big):\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf U}\Big)}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+{\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(R)(\varrho-R)-P(R)\Big)[{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{+}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}}

\displaystyle+{\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(P(\varrho_{B})-P^{\prime}(R)(\varrho_{B}-R)-P(R)\Big)[{\bf u}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}]^{-}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf U})\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\cdot({\bf u}-{\bf U})\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{\Omega}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(R)(\varrho-R)-p(R)\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\Big[\partial_{t}(R{\bf U})+{\rm div}_{x}(R{\bf U}\otimes{\bf U})+\nabla_{x}p(R)-{{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf U})}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf u}-{\bf U})\cdot{\bf U}+p^{\prime}(R)\left(1-\frac{\varrho}{R}\right)\Big)\Big[\partial_{t}R+{\rm div}_{x}(R{\bf U})\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\varrho}{R}-1\right)({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot{{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf U})}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(3.5)

in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,\infty)
.

as

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{s},{\bf u}_{s}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{S}\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big):\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big)}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+u^{+}_{B}\int_{r=1}\Big(P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-P(\varrho_{s})\Big){\rm d}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\varrho[u^{3}-u^{3}_{s}]^{2}\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{\Omega}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})\Big)\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\varrho_{s}}-1\right)(u_{s}^{3}-u^{3})\Big(\varrho_{s}{\widetilde{u}}\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}+\partial_{r}p(\varrho_{s})\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

(4.23)

Going back to the estimates (
2.34
)–(
2.36
) we may rewrite the above inequality in the form

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{s},{\bf u}_{s}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{S}\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big):\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big)}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{z=1}\Big(P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-P(\varrho_{s})\Big)u_{B}^{+}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\varrho[{\bf u}^{3}-{\bf u}^{3}_{s}]^{2}\partial_{z}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})\Big)\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\varrho-\varrho_{s}||{\bf u}_{s}^{3}-{\bf u}^{3}|\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(4.24)

provided
\varrho_{B}
,
u^{-}_{B}
are fixed and
0<u^{+}_{B}-u^{-}_{B}<\delta
, where
\delta>0
is sufficiently small.

At this stage, the proof can be completed in the same way as that of Theorem

2.1
in the preceding section as long as the pressure satisfies the
hard–sphere EOS. We only remark that thanks to the differential form of the relative energy inequality,
the relative energy

\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{s},{\bf u}_{s}\right)(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

is a non–increasing function of time, in particular, it admits a limit
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\geq 0
as
t\to\infty
. Moreover, it follows from
(
4.11
), (
4.12
) that, necessarily,
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}=0
, which yields the strong convergence claimed in
(
2.45
).

Now, we focus on the case
\overline{\varrho}=\infty
compatible with the isentropic pressure law
p=a\varrho^{\gamma}
,
\gamma>\frac{d}{2}
.
First, we estimate the integral on the right–hand side for large values of density,
\varrho\geq\overline{\varrho}_{s}>\varrho_{s}
:

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\int_{\varrho>\overline{\varrho}_{s}}|\varrho-\varrho_{s}||u_{s}^{3}-u^{3}|\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq C\int_{\varrho>\overline{\varrho}_{s}}\varrho|u_{s}^{3}-u^{3}|\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varrho[u^{3}-u^{3}_{s}]^{2}\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+C_{2}\int_{\varrho\geq\overline{\varrho}_{s}}\varrho\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varrho[u^{3}-u^{3}_{s}]^{2}\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+C_{2}\int_{\varrho\geq\overline{\varrho}_{s}}\frac{\varrho}{p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})\Big)\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\,{\rm d}{x},

where
C_{2}
depends only on
C_{1}
but not on
\overline{\varrho}_{s}
.
In view of hypothesis (
2.44
),

\liminf_{\varrho\to\infty}\frac{p(\varrho)}{\varrho}=\infty,

Thus we may choose
\overline{\varrho}_{s}>0
large enough so that

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle\int_{\varrho\geq\overline{\varrho}_{s}}\frac{\varrho}{p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})\Big)\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})\Big)\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

Going back to (
4.24
) we therefore conclude

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{s},{\bf u}_{s}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}{\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big):\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big)}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+u^{+}_{B}\int_{r=1}\Big(P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-P(\varrho_{s})\Big){\rm d}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varrho[u^{3}-u^{3}_{s}]^{2}\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})\Big)\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\varrho\leq\overline{\varrho}_{s}}|\varrho-\varrho_{s}||u_{s}^{3}-u^{3}|\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(4.25)

for a certain
\overline{\varrho}_{s}>0
large enough.

As the pressure is strictly convex, we get

|\varrho-\varrho_{s}|^{2}\leq c(\overline{\varrho}_{s})\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})\Big)

(4.26)

whenever
\varrho,\varrho_{s}\leq\overline{\varrho}_{s}
. Thus making use of Poincaré inequality we may control the last integral in (
4.25
)
as

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\int_{\varrho\leq\overline{\varrho}_{s}}|\varrho-\varrho_{s}||{\bf u}_{s}^{3}-{\bf u}^{3}|\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\frac{C}{2}\int_{\varrho\leq\overline{\varrho}_{s}}|\varrho-\varrho_{s}|^{2}(\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}})^{\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{C}{2}\int_{\Omega}|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{s}|^{2}(\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq Cc(\overline{\varrho}_{s})\int_{\Omega}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})\Big)(\partial_{z}{\widetilde{u}})^{\frac{3}{2}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{2}c_{P}\|\partial_{z}{\widetilde{u}}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}\Big(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big):\Big(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

Thus the right–hand side of (
4.25
) may be absorbed by the left–hand side as long as
\delta>0
is small enough.
We conclude

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{s},{\bf u}_{s}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{S}\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big):\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big)}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{r=1}\Big(P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-P(\varrho_{s})\Big)u_{B}^{+}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\varrho[u^{3}-u^{3}_{s}]^{2}\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})\Big)\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq 0.

(4.27)

Now, we complete the proof exactly as in the case of hard–sphere EOS.
Relation (
4.27
) implies

\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{s},{\bf u}_{s}\right)(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\searrow\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\geq 0\ \mbox{as}\ t\to\infty,

(4.28)

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathbb{S}{\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big):\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big)}+\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})\Big)\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t<\infty.

(4.29)

Consequently, relation (
4.29
), together with hypothesis (
2.44
),
give rise to

\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{s},{\bf u}_{s}\right)(t_{n},\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\to 0

for some
t_{n}\to\infty
, which yields the desired conclusion
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}=0
in (
4.28
).

4.3
Inflow-outflow problem in the radially symmetric setting

Our ultimate goal is to prove Theorem
2.3
.
Similarly to the preceding section, we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{s},{\bf u}_{s}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}{\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big):\Big(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\Big)}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{|x|=r^{+}}\Big(P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-P(\varrho_{s})\Big)u_{B}^{+}{\rm d}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{s})\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\cdot({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{s})\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{\Omega}\Big(p(\varrho)-p^{\prime}(\varrho_{s})(\varrho-\varrho_{s})-p(\varrho_{s})\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\varrho_{s}}-1\right)({\bf u}_{s}-{\bf u})\cdot\Big(\varrho_{s}{\bf u}_{s}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{s}+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho_{s})\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

(4.30)

Moreover, formula (
2.23
) yields

\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{s}\cdot\xi=\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}(|x|)\left|\xi\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{2}+\frac{{\widetilde{u}}(|x|)}{|x|}\left(|\xi|^{2}-\left|\xi\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{2}\right).

(4.31)

Consequently, the velocity gradient
\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{s}
is positively definite as long as

\partial_{r}{\widetilde{u}}>0
,
{\widetilde{u}}>0
.

Thus the proof of Theorem
2.3
will be the same as that of
Theorem
2.2
as soon as we observe that the qualitative properties of the
stationary solutions on the periodic strip and the annular domain are similar.
More specifically, this is true as long as the constant
\delta
,

0<u^{-}_{B}-u^{+}_{B}<\delta

is small and
r=r(\delta)
is chosen large enough.

Thus the proof of Theorem
2.3
reduces to comparison of the solution

\widetilde{\varrho}_{1}
,
{\widetilde{u}}_{1}
of the “flat” problem

\displaystyle\partial_{s}(\widetilde{\varrho}_{1}{\widetilde{u}}_{1})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varrho}_{1}{\widetilde{u}}_{1}\partial_{s}{\widetilde{u}}_{1}+\partial_{s}p(\widetilde{\varrho}_{1})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu+\eta\right)\partial^{2}_{s,s}{\widetilde{u}}_{1}

(4.32)

with that of the “curved” problem

\displaystyle\partial_{s}(s^{2}\widetilde{\varrho}_{2}{\widetilde{u}}_{2})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varrho}_{2}{\widetilde{u}}_{2}\partial_{s}{\widetilde{u}}_{2}+\partial_{s}p(\widetilde{\varrho}_{2})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu+{\lambda}\right)\partial_{s}\left(\frac{\partial_{s}(s^{2}{\widetilde{u}}_{2})}{s^{2}}\right).

(4.33)

Both problems are considered on the interval
s\in[r,r+1]
, with the initial data

\widetilde{\varrho}_{1}(r)=\widetilde{\varrho}_{2}(r)=\varrho_{B},\ {\widetilde{u}}_{1}(r)={\widetilde{u}}_{2}(r)=u^{-}_{B},

(4.34)

and the boundary data

{\widetilde{u}}_{1}(r+1)={\widetilde{u}}_{2}(r+1)=u^{+}_{B}.

(4.35)

Going back to Sections
2.3.2
,
2.3.3

we record

\widetilde{\varrho}_{1}=\varrho_{B}{\bf u}_{B}^{-}\frac{1}{{\widetilde{u}}_{1}},\ \widetilde{\varrho}_{2}=\frac{r^{2}}{s^{2}}\varrho_{B}{\bf u}_{B}^{-}\frac{1}{{\widetilde{u}}_{2}},\ s\in[r,r+1],

where

\frac{r^{2}}{s^{2}}\to 1\ \mbox{as}\ r\to\infty\ \mbox{uniformly for}\ s\in[r,r+1].

Next, the second order term in (
4.33
) can be written as

\partial_{s}\left(\frac{\partial_{s}(s^{2}{\widetilde{u}}_{2})}{s^{2}}\right)=\partial^{2}_{s,s}{\widetilde{u}}_{2}+2\partial_{s}\left(\frac{1}{s}{\widetilde{u}}_{2}\right),

where

\frac{1}{s}\leq\frac{1}{r}\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ r\to\infty.

Consequently, we get

\widetilde{\varrho}_{2}\to\widetilde{\varrho}_{1},\ {\widetilde{u}}_{2}\to{\widetilde{u}}_{1}\ \mbox{uniformly in}\ C^{1}[r,r+1]\ \mbox{as}\ r\to\infty.

The rest of the proof of Theorem
2.3
is the same as that of Theorem
2.2
.

Magnetic-Field-Induced Geometric Response of Mean-Field Projectors: Streda Formula and Orbital Magnetization
Jihang Zhu, Chunli Huang

Abstract
We study the magnetic-field response of interacting electron systems within mean-field theory using perturbation theory. We show that the linear response of the mean-field density-matrix to a weak magnetic field is purely geometric: it depends only on wavefunction derivatives, the Berry connections linking the occupied and unoccupied subspaces, and is independent of the interaction potential and the quasiparticle dispersion. This leads to compact, gauge-invariant projector expressions for both the Středa formula and the formula for orbital magnetization. Our calculation explicitly elucidates the role of exchange and self-consistency in defining current vertices for orbital magnetization calculations. Our work establishes a direct connection between mean-field theory, quantum geometry and the non-interacting topological band theory.



I
Introduction

Topological band theory
Xiao et al. 2010
;
Hasan and Kane 2010
;
Vanderbilt 2018
;
Bansil et al. 2016
extends the successful Bloch band theory by showing that certain physical properties of electronic systems are governed not by local details of the band structure but by the global topology of the Bloch bands. The most prominent example is the Thouless-Kohmoto-Nightingale-den Nijs (TKNN) formula
Thouless et al. 1982
, which expresses the transverse Hall conductivity as a Brillouin-zone integral of the Berry curvature associated with the occupied Bloch states.
Because these integrals yield quantized topological invariants (Chern number)
Kohmoto 1985
the corresponding physical observables are insensitive to small perturbations that do not close the energy gap, leading to the robust quantization of the Hall resistance. Beyond topology, recent research has increasingly focused on the
local
structure of Bloch states in momentum space, which encodes rich geometric information through the Berry curvature and the quantum metric
Provost and Vallee 1980
;
Resta 2011
;
Onishi and Fu 2024
;
Shinada and Nagaosa
;
Gao et al.
;
Avdoshkin et al. 2025
;
Mitscherling et al. 2025
.
The emerging framework of quantum geometry
Gao et al.
extends topological band theory by emphasizing that, beyond topological quantization, a wide range of physical properties are determined by the geometric structure of Bloch wave functions in momentum space.

In most discussions, topological band theory is developed for noninteracting electrons, where the Berry curvature and related geometric quantities can be computed directly from single-particle Bloch wave functions.
In this work, we show that several central results of topological band theory—including the Středa formula
Streda 1982
and the modern theory of orbital magnetization
Shi et al. 2007
;
Thonhauser et al. 2005
;
Xiao et al. 2005
;
Ceresoli et al. 2006
;
Raoux et al. 2015
;
Chen and Lee 2011
;
Gonze and Zwanziger 2011
—can be naturally generalized to mean-field quasiparticles by replacing the noninteracting Bloch energies and wave functions with their self-consistent mean-field counterparts.
The mean-field case is more subtle because the quasiparticle states depend self-consistently on the properties of all other occupied states.

Our key result is that the linear response of the mean-field density matrix to a weak magnetic field depends only on the interband Berry connections between occupied and unoccupied manifolds, and not explicitly on the interaction potential or the detailed band dispersion. This suggests a quantum-geometric origin and it remains robust in the presence of interactions treated at the mean-field level. Our work thus provides a unified framework connecting mean-field quasiparticle with topological band theories for noninteracting systems.
It also offers theoretical justification for prior Hartree-Fock studies of rhombohedral multilayer graphene
Das and Huang
;
Das and Huang 2025
;
Das and Huang 2024
, where orbital magnetization was computed directly from the mean-field band structure.

Prior to our work, Kang. et. al
Kang et al.
recently derived an expression for the orbital magnetization in Hartree-Fock theory using Green’s function approach, based on an expansion of the magnetic-flux similar to Ref.
Raoux et al. 2015
;
Chen and Lee 2011
;
Gonze and Zwanziger 2011
. Our derivation is based on the more straightforward expansion of the vector potential, similar to Ref.
Shi et al. 2007
, and highlights the geometric origin of the response in terms of mean-field projector.

II
Many-body Perturbation theory in mean-field approximations

We first briefly review the perturbation theory of the Hartree-Fock equations.
Consider a many-electron system described at the MF level, characterized by a one-body density matrix (which is equivalently a projector onto the occupied states),

P=\sum_{i}\outerproduct{i}{i}.

(1)

Throughout this section, we use the following notation:

i,j:\penalty\ \text{occupied states},\qquad m,n:\penalty\ \text{unoccupied states}.

(2)

Because
P^{2}=P
, and since the final expressions depend only on the Berry connection, we will refer to
P
as the
projector
rather than the density matrix. The mean-field Hamiltonian is defined as

H^{\mathrm{MF}}\equiv H^{\mathrm{MF}}[P]=T+\Sigma[P],

(3)

where
T
denotes the kinetic energy operator and
\Sigma[P]
is the mean-field self-energy, which is a functional of
P
. The stationary MF condition requires that
P
commutes with
H^{\mathrm{MF}}
,

[H^{\mathrm{MF}},P]=0.

(4)

We now perturb the system by a weak external potential
H^{\prime}
, under which

\displaystyle P\to P+\delta P,\qquad H^{\mathrm{MF}}[P]\to H^{\mathrm{MF}}[P+\delta P]+H^{\prime}.

(5)

Because
P
is a projector, linearizing the constraint
P^{2}=P
(and equivalently
(P+\delta P)^{2}=P+\delta P
) implies that
\delta P
is purely off-diagonal between the occupied and unoccupied sectors:

\displaystyle P\,\delta P\,P

\displaystyle=0,\qquad(1-P)\,\delta P\,(1-P)=0.

(6)

Hence,

\displaystyle\delta P

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\!\left(\outerproduct{\delta i}{i}+\outerproduct{i}{\delta i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{mi}\!\left(X_{mi}\outerproduct{m}{i}+X_{mi}^{*}\outerproduct{i}{m}\right),

(7)

where the perturbed occupied states are expanded in the unoccupied basis
\ket{\delta i}=\sum_{m}X_{mi}\ket{m}
and the coefficients
X_{mi}=\braket{m|\delta i}
are the unknown complex amplitudes.

The coefficients
X_{mi}
can be obtained by linearizing the stationary condition, Eq. (
4
), which yields

[\,T+\Sigma[P],\,\delta P\,]=-[\,\Sigma[\delta P]+H^{\prime},\,P\,].

(8)

This equation describes the new self-consistent condition in the presence of the perturbation
H^{\prime}
, accurate to first order in
H^{\prime}
.
Here we have used the linearized dependence of the Hartree-Fock self-energy,
\Sigma[P+\delta P]=\Sigma[P]+\Sigma[\delta P]
. Taking matrix elements between unoccupied (
m
) and occupied (
i
) states yields

(\varepsilon_{m}-\varepsilon_{i})\,X_{mi}+(n_{i}-n_{m})\,\Sigma_{mi}[\delta P]=-(n_{i}-n_{m})\,H^{\prime}_{mi},

(9)

where
\varepsilon_{m/i}
denote the MF eigenvalues and
n_{m/i}
are the corresponding occupation numbers at the stationary point.

In the “frozen self-energy” approximation,
\Sigma_{mi}[\delta P]\to 0
, this reproduces the familiar first-order result of single-particle perturbation theory,

X_{mi}=-H^{\prime}_{mi}/(\varepsilon_{m}-\varepsilon_{i})
.
However, once the self-energy is allowed to vary, a single particle–hole excitation
i\!\rightarrow\!m
can kick out all other occupied states
j
, and put them in some unoccupied states
n
that have the same conserved quantum numbers.
Including this induced variation of the self-energy, one obtains,

\displaystyle X_{mi}\!-\!\chi^{0}_{mi}\!\sum_{nj}\!\left(\!\braket{mj|\bar{V}|in}\!X_{nj}\!+\!\braket{mn|\bar{V}|ij}\!X_{nj}^{*}\!\right)\!=\!\chi^{0}_{mi}H_{mi}^{\prime},

(10)

where
\chi^{0}_{mi}=(n_{m}-n_{i})/(\varepsilon_{m}-\varepsilon_{i})
is the particle-hole susceptibility defined in terms of HF energies. The notation for the matrix elements,
\braket{mj|\bar{V}|in}=\braket{mj|V|in}-\braket{mj|V|ni}
, contains both direct and exchange scattering. The linear system above has dimension
2N_{\mathrm{emp}}N_{\mathrm{occ}}
, where the factor of
2
accounts for the real and imaginary components of the complex amplitudes
X_{mi}
.
We note that the Hartree-Fock solution by construction satisfies the stationary condition, Eq. (
4
), but is not necessarily stable.
The stability is determined by the homogeneous limit (
H^{\prime}_{mi}=0
) of Eq. (
10
).
If the corresponding eigenvalue spectrum is positive definite, the Hartree-Fock solution is stable.
This requirement is known as the Thouless stability criterion in Hartree–Fock theory
Thouless 2013
. It is therefore not surprising that the same equation naturally emerges when the Hartree–Fock solution is perturbed with
H^{\prime}
.

III
Mean-Field Projector Induced by a Magnetic Field

We now focus on the cell-periodic part of the Bloch functions, such that the MF projector is

P=\sum_{v\bm{k}}\outerproduct{u_{v\vec k}}{u_{v\vec k}},

(11)

The MF Hamiltonian

H^{\mathrm{MF}}_{\bm{k}}\ket{u_{n\bm{k}}}=\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}\ket{u_{n\bm{k}}}
where the band index
n
labels the MF eigenstates, with
n=v
and
n=c
denoting, respectively, the occupied and unoccupied states.
Then we consider a weak external vector potential with Fourier component
\bm{A}_{\bm{q}}
, which couples to the electrons through the bare velocity operator,

\hat{\bm{v}}=\frac{\partial T}{\partial\hat{\bm{p}}}.

(12)

The corresponding matrix elements are

H^{\prime}_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q},\,v\bm{k}}=e\,\bm{A}_{\bm{q}}\!\cdot\!\bm{v}_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q},\,v\bm{k}},

(13)

where
\bm{v}_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q},\,v\bm{k}}
denotes the
bare current vertex
(or photon vertex), which describes the transition of an occupied state
\ket{u_{v\bm{k}}}
to an empty state
\ket{u_{c,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}}
induced by the absorption of a photon with momentum
\bm{q}
.
The velocity operator is defined as the derivative of the kinetic part of the Hamiltonian because interaction terms that are local in real space like Coulomb potential do not couple to vector-potential via the minimal coupling.
H^{\prime}
leads to the following change of density-matrix:

\delta P=\sum_{cv\bm{k}}X_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q},v\bm{k}}\outerproduct{u_{c,\vec k+\vec q}}{u_{v\vec k}}+X^{*}_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q},v\bm{k}}\outerproduct{u_{v\vec k}}{u_{c,\vec k+\vec q}}.

(14)

It is convenient to rewrite the inhomogeneous stability equation [Eq. (
10
)] as a self-consistent vertex equation by parameterizing the response amplitude as

X_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q},\,v\bm{k}}=\chi^{0}_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q},\,v\bm{k}}\,e\,\bm{\Gamma}_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q},\,v\bm{k}}\!\cdot\!\bm{A}_{\bm{q}},

(15)

where
\chi^{0}_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q},\,v\bm{k}}=(n_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q}}-n_{v\bm{k}})/(\varepsilon_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q}}-\varepsilon_{v\bm{k}})
is the susceptibility defined in terms of mean-field quasiparticles.
Substituting Eq. (
15
) into Eq. (
10
) gives the self-consistent vertex equation,

\displaystyle\bm{\Gamma}_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q},\,v\bm{k}}=\bm{v}_{c\bm{k}+\bm{q},\,v\bm{k}}-\sum_{c^{\prime}v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\chi^{0}_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q},\,v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\times

\displaystyle\Big[\braket{u_{c,\bm{k}+\bm{q}},\,u_{v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|V|u_{c^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q}},\,u_{v\bm{k}}}\bm{\Gamma}_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q},\,v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\braket{u_{c,\bm{k}+\bm{q}},\,u_{c^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q}}|V|u_{v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}},\,u_{v\bm{k}}}\bm{\Gamma}^{*}_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q},\,v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\Big].

(16)

This expression has a natural diagrammatic interpretation: it represents the static limit of the Bethe–Salpeter equation for the current vertex dressed by particle–hole interactions.
The first term on the right-hand side is the bare vertex, while the second term captures vertex corrections due to the repeated exchange scattering of particle–hole excitations, forming a geometric ladder series.

The key step in our derivation is that we find a closed-form solution to the vertex equation in the long-wavelength limit (
q\to 0
) [see Appendix]:

\bm{\Gamma}_{c\bm{k},\,v\bm{k}}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\nabla_{\!\bm{k}}H^{\mathrm{MF}}|u_{v\bm{k}}}.

(17)

Furthermore, because the momentum derivative of the mean-field Hamiltonian is related to the interband Berry connection,

-\frac{\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\nabla_{\!\bm{k}}H^{\mathrm{MF}}|u_{v\bm{k}}}}{\varepsilon_{c\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{v\bm{k}}}=\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\nabla_{\!\bm{k}}u_{v\bm{k}}},

(18)

The response matrix elements take a remarkably simple form,

X_{c\bm{k},\,v\bm{k}}=\frac{e}{\hbar}\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\nabla_{\!\bm{k}}u_{v\bm{k}}}\!\cdot\!\bm{A}_{\bm{q}}.

(19)

Notably, this result contains no explicit dependence on either the interaction potential
V
or the quasiparticle energies
\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}
. It is entirely determined by the interband Berry connection between occupied and unoccupied states. Equation (
19
) is not gauge invariant in both the momentum space (
\ket{u_{v\bm{k}}}\rightarrow e^{i\theta_{k}}\ket{u_{v\bm{k}}}
) and in the coordinate space (
\bm{A}_{q}\rightarrow\bm{A}_{q}+\bm{q}\chi
).

Substituting this solution back to the perturbed density matrix Eq. (
14
), we obtain

\displaystyle\delta P=\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{A}_{\bm{q}}\!\cdot\!\sum_{cv\bm{k}}\Big(

\displaystyle\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\nabla_{\!\bm{k}}u_{v\bm{k}}}\ket{u_{c,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}}\bra{u_{v\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle+\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\nabla_{\!\bm{k}}u_{v\bm{k}}}^{*}\ket{u_{v\bm{k}}}\bra{u_{c,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}}\Big).

(20)

Since the magnetic field is linear in both
\bm{A}_{\bm{q}}
and
\bm{q}
, and we have taken
q\rightarrow 0
limit in Eq. (
17
) already, we now expand the unoccupied ket state to first order in
\bm{q}
as

\ket{u_{c,\bm{k}+\bm{q}}}=\ket{u_{c\bm{k}}}+q_{\mu}\ket{\partial_{\mu}u_{c\bm{k}}},

(21)

where
\partial_{\mu}\equiv\partial_{k_{\mu}}
. It then follows that
\delta P
can be separated into two contributions,
\delta P=\delta P_{A}+\delta P_{B}
, with

\displaystyle\delta P_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{\hbar}\!\sum_{cv\bm{k}}A_{\mu}(\bm{q})\Big(\!\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\partial_{\mu}u_{v\bm{k}}}\ket{u_{c\bm{k}}}\bra{u_{v\bm{k}}}+\mathrm{H.c.}\!\Big),

(22)

\displaystyle\delta P_{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{\hbar}\!\sum_{cv\bm{k}}A_{\mu}(\bm{q})q_{\nu}\Big(\!\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\partial_{\mu}u_{v\bm{k}}}\ket{\partial_{\nu}u_{c\bm{k}}}\bra{u_{v\bm{k}}}+\mathrm{H.c.}\!\Big)

(23)

We decompose
q_{\nu}A_{\mu}
in
\delta P_{B}
into antisymmetric and symmetric parts, the antisymmetric part is related to the magnetic field:

q_{\nu}A_{\mu}=\frac{i}{2}\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda}B_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}(q_{\nu}A_{\mu}+q_{\mu}A_{\nu}),

(24)

where
\epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda}
is the Levi–Civita tensor and
B_{\lambda}=i\,(\bm{q}\times\bm{A}_{\bm{q}})_{\lambda}
is the Fourier component of the magnetic field.
In the transverse gauge, the symmetric part vanishes, leaving only the antisymmetric contribution proportional to
B
.

The resulting change in the mean-field projector induced by a uniform magnetic field
\bm{B}
is therefore

\displaystyle\delta P_{B}

\displaystyle=i\pi\frac{\epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda}B_{\lambda}}{\phi_{0}}\!\sum_{cv\bm{k}}\!\left[\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\partial_{\mu}u_{v\bm{k}}}\ket{\partial_{\nu}u_{c\bm{k}}}\bra{u_{v\bm{k}}}+\mathrm{H.c.}\right],

(25)

where
\phi_{0}=h/e
is the magnetic flux quantum.
This expression is remarkable in that it does not depend explicitly on the microscopic details of the band dispersion or the interaction.
The magnetic field induced change in the mean field projector is given entirely by the Berry connections and their momentum-space derivatives of the mean field Bloch states.
We would obtain an identical expression by applying perturbation theory to the response of noninteracting electrons to an external magnetic field
Shi et al. 2007
, with the mean-field wavefunctions mapping back to the non-interacting wavefunctions
\ket{u_{n\bm{k}}}\!\rightarrow\!\ket{u_{n\bm{k}}^{0}}
. Fundamentally, this simplification arises because Eq. (
17
) is simply
a solution of the Ward identity when the Hartree-Fock approximation is used for the Green’s function,

\hbar\bm{q}\cdot\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q},\bm{k}}=G^{-1}_{\bm{k}}-G^{-1}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}=\bm{q}\cdot\nabla_{\!\bm{k}}H^{\mathrm{MF}}_{\bm{k}}+O(q^{2}).

(26)

This follows from the fact that the Hartree-Fock approximation is a conserving approximation
Baym and Kadanoff 1961
.
Since the Ward identity originates from the continuity equation, this connection suggests that the underlying conservation laws impose nontrivial constraints on how many-body interactions modify the quantum geometry in momentum space.

IV
Středa Formula and Orbital Magnetization

We now connect the field-induced change in the mean-field projector, derived in the previous section, to measurable quantities such as the carrier density and the orbital magnetization. For any single-particle observable
\hat{O}
, the corresponding field-induced change in its expectation value is given by

\delta\!\braket{\hat{O}}={\rm Tr}\!\left[\delta P_{B}\,\hat{O}\right].

(27)

A subtle but important point is that although
\delta P_{B}
contains matrix elements involving unoccupied states
\ket{u_{c\bm{k}}}
, as we will show later, the expectation values of physical observables such as
\hat{O}=I
(the identity operator) or
\hat{O}=H^{\mathrm{MF}}
(the mean-field Hamiltonian) depend only on the occupied subspace.
In the following, we work in two-dimensions, fixed chemical potential
\mu
, and denote by

P_{\bm{k}}=\sum_{v}\ket{u_{v\bm{k}}}\!\bra{u_{v\bm{k}}}

(28)

the projector onto the occupied mean-field quasiparticle states, and by

Q_{\bm{k}}=\sum_{c}\ket{u_{c\bm{k}}}\!\bra{u_{c\bm{k}}}=1-P_{\bm{k}}

(29)

the complementary projector onto the unoccupied subspace at a given wavevector
\bm{k}
.

IV.1
Středa Formula

The change in electron density at fixed chemical potential
\mu
can be written as

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dn_{e}}{dB}\right|_{\mu}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\mathrm{Area}}\frac{{\rm Tr}\,\delta P_{B}}{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\phi_{0}}\,\epsilon_{ij}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\!\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{cv}\Big[i\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\partial_{k_{i}}u_{v\bm{k}}}\braket{u_{v\bm{k}}|\partial_{k_{j}}u_{c\bm{k}}}\Big],

(30)

where
\delta P_{B}
is derived in Sec.
III
.
It is convenient to write them in terms of projectors, using some basic properties of projectors,

\displaystyle\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\partial_{k_{i}}u_{v\bm{k}}}=\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\partial_{k_{i}}P_{\bm{k}}|u_{v\bm{k}}},

\displaystyle\braket{u_{v\bm{k}}|\partial_{k_{j}}u_{c\bm{k}}}=\braket{u_{v\bm{k}}|\partial_{k_{j}}Q_{\bm{k}}|u_{c\bm{k}}},

\displaystyle\partial_{k_{i}}Q_{\bm{k}}=-\,\partial_{k_{i}}P_{\bm{k}},

(31)

\displaystyle P_{\bm{k}}(\partial_{k_{i}}P_{\bm{k}})P_{\bm{k}}=0,

\displaystyle Q_{\bm{k}}(\partial_{k_{i}}P_{\bm{k}})Q_{\bm{k}}=0,

allow us to remove the explicit sum over occupied and unoccupied states, leading to,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dn_{e}}{dB}\right|_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\phi_{0}}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\Big[i\epsilon_{ij}\Tr\!\big(P_{\bm{k}}\,(\partial_{k_{i}}P_{\bm{k}})\,(\partial_{k_{j}}P_{\bm{k}})\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{2\pi}{\phi_{0}}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\!\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,\Omega_{\mathrm{occ}}(\bm{k})

(32)

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{\phi_{0}},

(33)

where

\Omega_{\mathrm{occ}}(\bm{k})=i\,\epsilon_{ij}\,\Tr\!\big(P_{\bm{k}}\,\partial_{k_{i}}P_{\bm{k}}\,\partial_{k_{j}}P_{\bm{k}}\big)

(34)

is the net Berry curvature of the occupied states and the sum over the Berry curvature gives the Chern number,

C=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\Omega_{\mathrm{occ}}(\bm{k})\,d^{2}\bm{k}.

IV.2
Orbital Magnetization

In mean-field theory, the total energy is given by

E=\Tr\!\left[\big(T+\tfrac{1}{2}\Sigma[P]\big)P\right],

(35)

where
T
is the one-body kinetic energy operator and
\Sigma[P]
is the self-energy functional of the projector
P
. When an external perturbation
H^{\prime}
is applied, the mean-field projector changes as
P\rightarrow P+\delta P
.
Expanding the total energy to first order in
H^{\prime}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta E

\displaystyle=\Tr\!\left[\big(T+\tfrac{1}{2}\Sigma[P]\big)\delta P\right]+\Tr\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\Sigma[\delta P]\,P\right]+\Tr\!\left[H^{\prime}P\right]

\displaystyle=\Tr\!\left[\big(T+\Sigma[P]\big)\delta P\right]+\Tr\!\left[H^{\prime}P\right]

\displaystyle=\Tr\!\left[H^{\mathrm{MF}}[P]\,\delta P\right]+\Tr\!\left[H^{\prime}P\right],

(36)

where we used
\Tr\!\left[\Sigma[\delta P]P\right]=\Tr\!\left[\Sigma[P]\delta P\right]
. Here

H^{\mathrm{MF}}[P]=T+\Sigma[P]
is the stationary mean-field Hamiltonian in the absence of perturbation.
Introducing the grand-canonical mean-field operator

\hat{\Omega}=H^{\mathrm{MF}}[P]-\mu N,

(37)

the change in energy at fixed
\mu
due to the field-induced change of the projector
\delta P_{B}
is

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left[\hat{\Omega}\,\delta P_{B}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi B\epsilon_{ij}}{\phi_{0}}\sum_{\bm{k}}i{\rm Tr}\left[(H^{\mathrm{MF}}_{\bm{k}}-\mu)\,P_{\bm{k}}\,\partial_{k_{i}}P_{\bm{k}}\,\partial_{k_{j}}P_{\bm{k}}\right].

(38)

From thermodynamics, the total magnetization per unit area is defined as

M_{z}=-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Area}}\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial B}\Big|_{B=0}.

(39)

Since we only account for orbital response in
\delta P_{B}
, this expression yields the
orbital
contribution to the magnetization:

\displaystyle M_{z}

\displaystyle=-\,\frac{e}{\hbar}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\!\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,{\rm Tr}\!\left[(H^{\mathrm{MF}}_{\bm{k}}-\mu)\,i\,\epsilon_{ij}\,P_{\bm{k}}\,(\partial_{i}P_{\bm{k}})\,(\partial_{j}P_{\bm{k}})\right].

(40)

This recovers to the formula quoted in the so-called modern theory of orbital magnetization
Shi et al. 2007
;
Thonhauser et al. 2005
;
Xiao et al. 2005
;
Ceresoli et al. 2006
[see Appendix]:

M_{z}=\sum\limits_{n}\int_{\rm BZ}\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}f_{n\bm{k}}\left[m_{n,z}(\bm{k})+\frac{e}{\hbar}(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\mu)\Omega_{n}(\bm{k})\right]

(41)

with
f_{n\bm{k}}
the occupation.

V
Conclusion and Outlook

We have demonstrated that, within mean-field theory, the change of the density matrix induced by minimal coupling to a weak vector potential
\bm{A}
takes an elegantly simple form: it is given by the dot product between the inter-band Berry connection and the applied vector potential,
\delta P_{ck,vk}=\frac{e}{\hbar}\langle u_{ck}|\nabla u_{vk}\rangle\cdot\bm{A}
.
This structure is identical to that obtained for noninteracting electrons, and we have explicitly shown that it continues to hold in the presence of interactions at the mean-field level. Our calculation emphasizes that, although the vector potential couples to the bare velocity vertex determined solely by the kinetic Hamiltonian, since two-body interactions are diagonal in real space, the response of the density matrix is given by the fully dressed current vertex that incorporates the self-energies.
Building on this result, we derived compact, gauge-invariant projector expressions for both the orbital magnetization and the Středa formula. These findings provide a rigorous framework of connecting mean-field quasiparticle to noninteracting topological band theories.

Appendix A
Solution of the vertex equation for
q\rightarrow 0

In the uniform limit (
q\!\to\!0
), the self-consistent vertex equation takes the form

\displaystyle\bm{\Gamma}_{c\bm{k},\,v\bm{k}}=\,

\displaystyle\bm{v}_{c\bm{k},\,v\bm{k}}-\sum_{c^{\prime}v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\chi^{0}_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime},\,v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\times

\displaystyle\big[\braket{u_{c\bm{k}},u_{v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|V|u_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}},\,u_{v\bm{k}}}\,\bm{\Gamma}_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime},\,v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\braket{u_{c\bm{k}},\,u_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|V|u_{v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}},\,u_{v\bm{k}}}\,\bm{\Gamma}^{*}_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime},\,v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\big].

(42)

We now show that

\bm{\Gamma}_{c\bm{k},\,v\bm{k}}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\nabla_{\!\bm{k}}H^{\mathrm{MF}}|u_{v\bm{k}}}

(43)

is an exact solution of the self-consistent vertex equation above. Using

\frac{\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\nabla_{\bm{k}}H^{\mathrm{MF}}|u_{v\bm{k}}}}{\varepsilon_{c\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{v\bm{k}}}=-\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\nabla_{\bm{k}}u_{v\bm{k}}},

(44)

we can write the second term (i.e. vertex correction term) on the right hand side of Eq. (

\displaystyle-\sum_{c^{\prime}v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\big[\braket{u_{c\bm{k}},\,u_{v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|V|u_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}},\,u_{v\bm{k}}}\braket{u_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\nabla_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}u_{v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\braket{u_{c\bm{k}},\,u_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|V|u_{v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}},\,u_{v\bm{k}}}\braket{u_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\nabla_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}u_{v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}}^{*}\big]

(45)

(46)

This combination is precisely the
\bm{k}
-gradient of the Fock self-energy evaluated at the stationary point. Computing the
\bm{k}
-derivative of the Fock term, and using the stationary projector

P_{\bm{k}}=\sum_{v}\outerproduct{u_{v\vec k}}{u_{v\vec k}}
, we find

(47)

where we used integration by parts in
\bm{k}^{\prime}
and used the translational invariance of the two-body interaction.
Because
P_{\bm{k}}
is a rank-
N_{\mathrm{occ}}
projector satisfying
P_{\bm{k}}^{2}=P_{\bm{k}}
, its gradient is purely off-diagonal between the occupied and unoccupied subspaces:

\nabla_{k_{\mu}}P_{\bm{k}}=\sum_{cv}A_{cv}^{\mu}(\bm{k})\,\outerproduct{u_{c\vec k}}{u_{v\vec k}}+(A_{cv}^{\mu}(\bm{k}))^{*}\,\outerproduct{u_{v\vec k}}{u_{c\vec k}},

(48)

where

A_{cv}^{\mu}(\bm{k})=\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\nabla_{k_{\mu}}u_{v\bm{k}}}=-\,\frac{\braket{u_{c\bm{k}}|\nabla_{k_{\mu}}H^{\mathrm{MF}}|u_{v\bm{k}}}}{\varepsilon_{c\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{v\bm{k}}}

(49)

is the interband Berry connection times
i
.
Physically,
\nabla_{\bm{k}}P_{\bm{k}}
describes the infinitesimal
tilt
of the occupied subspace toward the unoccupied subspace as
\bm{k}
is varied.
Because of the projector constraint, only particle-hole matrix elements contribute.
The extent of this rotation is larger when the energy separation between occupied and unoccupied states is small.

The matrix element of
\nabla_{\bm{k}}\Sigma^{F}_{\bm{k}}
between an unoccupied state
\ket{u_{c\bm{k}}}
and an occupied state
\ket{u_{v\bm{k}}}
is then

\displaystyle\bra{u_{c\bm{k}}}\nabla_{\bm{k}}\Sigma^{F}_{\bm{k}}\ket{u_{v\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\penalty\ \bra{u_{c\bm{k}},u_{c^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}}V\ket{u_{v^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}},u_{v\bm{k}}}\bm{A}_{c^{\prime}v^{\prime}}^{*}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\big],

(50)

H_{\bm{k}}^{\mathrm{MF}}=T_{\bm{k}}-\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}V_{\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}}\,P_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}.

(51)

This identification makes explicit that the fully dressed current vertex in the

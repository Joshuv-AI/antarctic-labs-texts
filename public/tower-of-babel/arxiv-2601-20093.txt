Quantum-geometry-enabled Landau–Zener tunneling in singular flat bands
Xuanyu Long, Feng Liu

Abstract
Flat-band materials have attracted substantial interest for their intriguing quantum geometric effects. Here we investigate how singular flat bands (SFBs) respond to a static, uniform electric field and whether they can support single-particle dc transport. By constructing a minimal two-band lattice model, we show that away from the singular band crossing point (BCP), the Wannier–Stark (WS) spectrum of the flat band is well captured by an intraband Berry phase Φ B \Phi_{\mathrm{B}} . The associated WS eigenstates are exponentially localized along the field direction, precluding dc transport. In contrast, near the BCP the interband Berry connection becomes prominent and drives Landau–Zener tunneling, which bends the flat-band WS ladder and delocalizes the SFB wavefunctions. Remarkably, this regime is governed solely by the maximal quantum distance d d through two geometric phases ( θ , φ ) (\theta,\varphi) : θ \theta characterizes the tunneling rate and φ \varphi acts as a generalized Berry phase. These results highlight the essential role of quantum geometry in enabling nontrivial transport signatures in SFBs.

We provide a unified picture of the response of SFBs to electric and magnetic fields in disrupting the perfect destructive interference, shedding new light on the fundamental role of quantum geometry in flat-band physics.

As shown in Fig. 1(a) of the main text, the spectrum consists of an exactly flat band (blue) and a dispersive band (orange) that touches it at two quadratic band-crossing points (BCPs) located at
\Gamma\,(0,0)
and X
\,(\pi,0)
. Expanding Eq. (
S1
) around
\Gamma
and X yields the effective continuum Hamiltonian

H_{\mathrm{SFB}}=t\begin{pmatrix}k_{x}^{2}+(1-d^{2})k_{y}^{2}&d\sqrt{1-d^{2}}\,k_{y}^{2}-i\,\xi d\,k_{x}k_{y}\\[5.0pt]
d\sqrt{1-d^{2}}\,k_{y}^{2}+i\,\xi d\,k_{x}k_{y}&d^{2}k_{y}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(S2)

Here
\xi
encodes the chirality of the SFB and can be regarded as a valley index:
\xi=+1
for the
\Gamma
point and
\xi=-1
for the X point. The dispersive-band energy is

\displaystyle E_{c}(\bm{k})=t\,\bigl[9-7\cos k_{y}-\cos(2k_{x})\bigl(1+\cos k_{y}\bigr)\bigr]/4.

(S3)

Expanding
E_{c}(\bm{k})
around
\Gamma
, one finds
E_{c}(\bm{k})\approx t\,(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2})
, corresponding to an isotropic parabolic dispersion.

The Bloch eigenvectors of the flat band and the dispersive band can be chosen as

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{0}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c(\bm{k})}\begin{pmatrix}-2d\,(1-\cos k_{y})\\[5.0pt]
2\sqrt{1-d^{2}}\,(1-\cos k_{y})+i\sin k_{x}\sin k_{y}\end{pmatrix},

(S4)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{1}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c(\bm{k})}\begin{pmatrix}2\sqrt{1-d^{2}}\,(1-\cos k_{y})-i\sin k_{x}\sin k_{y}\\[5.0pt]
2d\,(1-\cos k_{y})\end{pmatrix},

(S5)

where

\displaystyle c(\bm{k})=\sqrt{4(1-\cos k_{y})^{2}+\sin^{2}k_{x}\sin^{2}k_{y}}

(S6)

normalizes the eigenvectors.

Fourier transforming the
unnormalized
flat-band eigenvector in Eq. (
S4
) (i.e., setting
c(\bm{k})=1
) yields the corresponding compact localized state (CLS), shown in Fig.
S1
(a) [see also Fig. 1(b) in the main text]. One can explicitly verify that this CLS exhibits perfect destructive interference under the hopping amplitudes encoded in Eq. (
S1
); a representative cancellation channel is illustrated in Fig.
S1
(b).

We also calculate the Berry connections
\bm{A}_{mn}(\bm{k})=i\langle u_{m}(\bm{k})|\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{k}}|u_{n}(\bm{k})\rangle
, of which the
y
components are

\displaystyle A^{y}_{00}=-A^{y}_{11}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{1-d^{2}}\,\sin k_{x}\,(1-\cos k_{y})}{4(1-\cos k_{y})^{2}+\sin^{2}k_{x}\sin^{2}k_{y}}+\pi\,\delta(k_{y}),

(S7)

\displaystyle A^{y}_{01}=A^{y}_{10}

\displaystyle=\frac{2d\,\sin k_{x}\,(1-\cos k_{y})}{4(1-\cos k_{y})^{2}+\sin^{2}k_{x}\sin^{2}k_{y}}.

(S8)

The
\pi\,\delta(k_{y})
contribution originates from the discontinuity of the eigenvectors in Eqs. (
S4
) and (
S5
) across the line
k_{y}=0
. Expanding Eqs. (
S7
) and (
S8
) around
\Gamma
, we obtain

\displaystyle A^{y}_{00}=-A^{y}_{11}

\displaystyle\approx\sqrt{1-d^{2}}\,\frac{k_{x}}{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}}+\pi\,\delta(k_{y}),

(S9)

\displaystyle A^{y}_{01}=A^{y}_{10}

\displaystyle\approx d\,\frac{k_{x}}{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}}.

(S10)

II
Hamiltonian under an electric field

Here we derive the Hamiltonian of a generic lattice model in a static, uniform electric field
\bm{F}

Phong and Mele 2023
;
Kelardeh et al. 2014
. We first formulate the problem in real space and then derive the corresponding eigenvalue equations in momentum space.

A
Real-space formulation

We start from a general
M
-band tight-binding model. In real space, each unit cell contains
M
atomic orbitals
\bigl|\bm{R},i\bigr\rangle
located at
\bm{R}+\bm{\tau}_{i}
, where
\bm{R}=m\bm{a}_{1}+n\bm{a}_{2}
labels the unit cell (
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
) and
\bm{\tau}_{i}
is the intra-cell displacement of orbital
i
.

For the minimal two-band model,
\bm{a}_{1}=(a,0)
,
\bm{a}_{2}=(0,a)
, and
\bm{\tau}_{i}=\bm{0}
for
i=A,B
[see Fig.
S1
(a)], where
a
is the lattice constant (set to
a=1
below unless otherwise stated). The real-space hopping parameters are
t_{\Delta\bm{R},ij}=\bigl\langle\bm{R},i\big|H\big|\bm{R}+\Delta\bm{R},j\bigr\rangle
, and translational symmetry implies
t_{\bm{R},ij}=\langle\bm{0},i|H|\bm{R},j\rangle
.

With the Fourier transforms

\displaystyle\bigl|\bm{k},i\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\bm{R}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}+\bm{\tau}_{i})}\,\bigl|\bm{R},i\bigr\rangle,

(S11)

\displaystyle\bigl|\bm{R},i\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\bm{k}}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}+\bm{\tau}_{i})}\,\bigl|\bm{k},i\bigr\rangle,

(S12)

we relate the momentum-space Hamiltonian matrix elements
[H(\bm{k})]_{ij}=\langle\bm{k},i|H|\bm{k},j\rangle
to the real-space hoppings via

\displaystyle[H(\bm{k})]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{R}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}+\bm{\tau}_{j}-\bm{\tau}_{i})}\,t_{\bm{R},ij},

(S13)

\displaystyle t_{\bm{R},ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}+\bm{\tau}_{j}-\bm{\tau}_{i})}\,[H(\bm{k})]_{ij}.

(S14)

A uniform electric field adds the scalar-potential term

\displaystyle H^{\prime}=e\,\bm{F}\cdot\bm{r}.

(S15)

For compactness we set
e=1
in intermediate steps; it can be restored by the replacement
\bm{F}\to e\bm{F}
. In the basis
\bigl|\bm{R},i\bigr\rangle
, the corresponding matrix elements read

\displaystyle\bigl\langle\bm{R},i\big|\bm{F}\cdot\bm{r}\big|\bm{R}^{\prime},j\bigr\rangle=\bm{F}\cdot(\bm{R}+\bm{\tau}_{i})\,\delta_{ij}\,\delta_{\bm{R}\bm{R}^{\prime}},

(S16)

which shows that the electric field produces a position-dependent onsite potential along the field direction.

For the minimal two-band model we take
\bm{F}=F\,\hat{\bm{y}}
. Translational symmetry along
x
remains intact, so
k_{x}
is a good quantum number. We therefore block-diagonalize the Hamiltonian at fixed
k_{x}
and reduce the problem to a one-dimensional (1D) chain along
y
using the basis

\displaystyle\bigl|k_{x},y,i\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{x}}}\sum_{x}e^{ik_{x}(x+\tau_{i,x})}\,\bigl|\bm{R},i\bigr\rangle

(S17)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{y}}}\sum_{k_{y}}e^{-ik_{y}(y+\tau_{i,y})}\,\bigl|\bm{k},i\bigr\rangle.

(S18)

At a given
k_{x}
, we construct a sufficiently long 1D cluster along
y
with onsite potential
V_{n,i}=F(na+\tau_{i,y})
and diagonalize the resulting real-space Hamiltonian. The Wannier–Stark (WS) spectra shown in Fig. 2(a–d) of the main text are obtained in this way; the hopping parameters are generated from Eq. (
S1
) using the inverse Fourier transform in Eq. (
S14
). We compute the WS spectra for the kagome lattice in Fig. 3(b) and (c) of the main text similarly, choosing the enlarged unit cell as the dashed blue box in the upper panel of Fig. 3(a) so that the basis vectors align with the
x
or
y
direction.

B
Momentum-space formulation

Under the orbital basis
\bigl|\bm{k},i\bigr\rangle
defined by Eq. (
S11
), the field-free Hamiltonian is block-diagonal in
\bm{k}
and given by Eq. (
S1
). The electric-field term has matrix elements

\displaystyle\langle\bm{k},i|\bm{F}\cdot\bm{r}|\bm{k},j\rangle=i\,\bm{F}\cdot\nabla_{\bm{k}}\,\delta_{ij}.

(S19)

It is convenient to switch to the band basis

\displaystyle\bigl|u_{m}(\bm{k})\bigr\rangle=\sum_{i}u_{m,i}(\bm{k})\,\bigl|\bm{k},i\bigr\rangle,

(S20)

where
m\in\{0,1\}
,
i\in\{A,B\}
, and
u_{m,i}(\bm{k})
are given in Eqs. (
S4
) and (
S5
). In this basis the field-free Hamiltonian is diagonal, with eigenvalues
0
and
E_{c}
[Eq. (
S3
)]. The electric-field matrix elements read

\displaystyle\bigl\langle u_{m}(\bm{k})\big|\bm{F}\cdot\bm{r}\big|u_{n}(\bm{k})\bigr\rangle=i\,\bm{F}\cdot\nabla_{\bm{k}}\,\delta_{mn}+\bm{F}\cdot\bm{A}_{mn}(\bm{k}),

(S21)

with
\bm{A}_{mn}
the Berry connections [Eqs. (
S7
) and (
S8
)].

We expand an eigenstate in the band basis as

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\Bigl[\phi_{0}(\bm{k})\,|u_{0}(\bm{k})\rangle+\phi_{1}(\bm{k})\,|u_{1}(\bm{k})\rangle\Bigr],

(S22)

with the normalization condition
|\phi_{0}|^{2}+|\phi_{1}|^{2}=1
. Using Eqs. (
S3
) and (
S21
), the eigenvalue equation
(H+H^{\prime})|\Psi\rangle=E|\Psi\rangle
becomes

\displaystyle E\,\phi_{0}

\displaystyle=iF\,\frac{\partial\phi_{0}}{\partial k_{y}}+FA^{y}_{00}\,\phi_{0}+FA^{y}_{01}\,\phi_{1},

(S23)

\displaystyle E\,\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=iF\,\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial k_{y}}+E_{c}\,\phi_{1}+FA^{y}_{11}\,\phi_{1}+FA^{y}_{10}\,\phi_{0},

(S24)

where we have taken
\bm{F}=F\,\hat{\bm{y}}
. At fixed
k_{x}
, the eigenvector
\bm{\phi}=(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})^{\mathrm{T}}
is a function of
k_{y}
with period
2k_{0}
, set by the
k
-space periodicity along the field direction. For the minimal two-band model,
k_{0}=\pi
. For the kagome lattice,
2k_{0}
depends on the field direction and is indicated in the lower panel of Fig. 3(a) of the main text. The WS spectrum
E(k_{x})
is obtained by solving Eqs. (
S23
) and (
S24
) subject to the periodic boundary condition

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}(k_{x},-k_{0})=\bm{\phi}(k_{x},k_{0}).

(S25)

III
Isolated-flat-band limit

Away from the BCP, the interband coupling can be neglected to leading order. Dropping the interband term
A^{y}_{01}
in Eq. (
S23
), we obtain a closed equation for the flat band. The eigenstate in Eq. (
S22
) then has
\phi_{1}(\bm{k})=0
and

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(\bm{k})=\exp\left[-\frac{iE}{F}k_{y}+i\int_{-k_{0}}^{k_{y}}A^{y}_{00}(k_{x},k_{y}^{\prime})\,dk_{y}^{\prime}\right],

(S26)

where
k_{0}=\pi
and

\displaystyle\int_{-k_{0}}^{k_{y}}A^{y}_{00}(k_{x},k_{y}^{\prime})\,dk_{y}^{\prime}=\sqrt{1-d^{2}}\,\arctan\!\left(\frac{2\tan(k_{y}^{\prime}/2)}{\sin k_{x}}\right)\biggr|_{-k_{0}}^{k_{y}}+\frac{\pi}{2}\bigl[1+\mathrm{sgn}(k_{y})\bigr].

(S27)

For the special case
k_{y}=k_{0}
, Eq. (
S27
) reduces to the Berry phase
\Phi_{\mathrm{B}}(k_{x})
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathrm{B}}(k_{x})=\mathrm{sgn}(\sin k_{x})\,\pi\sqrt{1-d^{2}}+\pi.

(S28)

Imposing the periodicity condition
\phi_{0}(k_{x},-k_{0})=\phi_{0}(k_{x},k_{0})
[Eq. (
S25
)] yields the WS energies

\displaystyle E=\left\{n+\bigl[\mathrm{sgn}(\sin k_{x})\sqrt{1-d^{2}}+1\bigr]/2\right\}\,eFa,

(S29)

where
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. This result is equivalent to Eq. (9) in the main text expressed in terms of the Wannier charge center.

We now evaluate the real-space profile of the eigenstate and show that it is exponentially localized. At fixed
k_{x}
, Eq. (
S22
) gives

\displaystyle|\Psi_{k_{x}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{y}}}\sum_{k_{y}}\Bigl[\phi_{0}(\bm{k})\,|u_{0}(\bm{k})\rangle+\phi_{1}(\bm{k})\,|u_{1}(\bm{k})\rangle\Bigr].

(S30)

The corresponding one-dimensional real-space wavefunction
\psi_{\mathrm{R},i}(y)=\langle k_{x},y,i|\Psi_{k_{x}}\rangle
follows from Eqs. (
S18
) and (
S20
):

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathrm{R},i}(y)=\frac{1}{N_{y}}\sum_{k_{y}}e^{ik_{y}(y+\tau_{i,y})}\,\Bigl[\phi_{0}(\bm{k})u_{0,i}(\bm{k})+\phi_{1}(\bm{k})u_{1,i}(\bm{k})\Bigr].

(S31)

Here
\phi_{0}(\bm{k})
is given by Eq. (
S26
),
\phi_{1}(\bm{k})=0
, and
\tau_{i,y}=0
for the minimal two-band model. Defining
|\psi_{\mathrm{R}}|^{2}=\sum_{i}|\psi_{\mathrm{R},i}|^{2}
, we obtain the exponentially localized profile shown in Fig. 1(d) of the main text for
k_{x}=0.1\pi
. We have also verified that Eq. (
S31
) agrees with the wavefunction obtained by direct diagonalization of the real-space Hamiltonian in Sec.
II A
in the weak-field (uncoupled) regime.

IV
Solution for two coupled bands near the singular point

We now solve the coupled eigenvalue equations (
S23
) and (
S24
) in the vicinity of a singular BCP. Define

\displaystyle g_{E}(k_{x},k_{y})=\frac{1}{F}\int_{0}^{k_{y}}E_{c}(k_{x},k_{y}^{\prime})\,dk_{y}^{\prime}.

(S32)

For
k_{x},k_{y}\to 0
, one has
g_{E}\approx t\,(k_{y}^{3}/3+k_{x}^{2}k_{y})/F
. Introduce the transformations

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{0}=\phi_{0}\,e^{iEk_{y}/F},\qquad\tilde{\phi}_{1}=\phi_{1}\,e^{iEk_{y}/F-ig_{E}}.

(S33)

Then Eqs. (
S23
) and (
S24
) become

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\tilde{\phi}_{0}}{\partial k_{y}}+A^{y}_{00}\,\tilde{\phi}_{0}+A^{y}_{01}e^{ig_{E}}\,\tilde{\phi}_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

(S34)

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\tilde{\phi}_{1}}{\partial k_{y}}+A^{y}_{11}\,\tilde{\phi}_{1}+A^{y}_{10}e^{-ig_{E}}\,\tilde{\phi}_{0}

\displaystyle=0.

(S35)

Using the Berry connections expanded around
\Gamma
[Eqs. (
S9
) and (
S10
)], the coupled equations can be written compactly as

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{y}}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{0}\\
\tilde{\phi}_{1}\end{pmatrix}+\left[\frac{k_{x}}{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}}\,\bm{w}\cdot\bm{\sigma}+\pi\,\delta(k_{y})\,\sigma_{z}\right]\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{0}\\
\tilde{\phi}_{1}\end{pmatrix}=0,

(S36)

where
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
are Pauli matrices and

\displaystyle\bm{w}=\bigl[d\cos(g_{E}),\,-d\sin(g_{E}),\,\sqrt{1-d^{2}}\bigr]

(S37)

is a unit vector. The prefactor
k_{x}/(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2})
is sharply peaked near
k_{y}=0
, so we approximate the solution by two
k_{y}
-independent vectors,
\tilde{\bm{\phi}}(+)
for
k_{y}>0
and
\tilde{\bm{\phi}}(-)
for
k_{y}<0
, related by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{0}(+)\\
\tilde{\phi}_{1}(+)\end{pmatrix}=U\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{0}(-)\\
\tilde{\phi}_{1}(-)\end{pmatrix}.

(S38)

The unitary rotation matrix
U
is obtained from the path-ordered exponential

\displaystyle U=-\mathcal{P}\exp\left[i\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{k_{x}}{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}}\,\bm{w}\cdot\bm{\sigma}\,dk_{y}\right].

(S39)

This integral is evaluated numerically in Sec.
V
. One can parametrize

\displaystyle U=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta\,e^{i\varphi}&i\sin\theta\\[3.00003pt]
i\sin\theta&\cos\theta\,e^{-i\varphi}\end{pmatrix},

(S40)

where
\theta
and
\varphi
depend on
F/t
,
k_{x}
, and
d
.

Consider the scaling
\bm{k}\to\alpha\bm{k}
in Eq. (
S39
). The factor
k_{x}\,dk_{y}/(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2})
is invariant, while invariance of
g_{E}
near the quadratic BCP requires
F/t\to\alpha^{3}F/t
because
g_{E}
scales cubically with
\bm{k}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\theta(\alpha^{3}F/t,\alpha k_{x})=\theta(F/t,k_{x}),\qquad\varphi(\alpha^{3}F/t,\alpha k_{x})=\varphi(F/t,k_{x}).

(S41)

Once
\theta
and
\varphi
are known, the WS spectrum follows from the periodic boundary condition in Eq. (
S25
). We take

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}(-k_{0})\\
\phi_{1}(-k_{0})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}(k_{0})\\
\phi_{1}(k_{0})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\zeta\\
\sin\zeta\end{pmatrix},

(S42)

where
k_{0}=\pi
for the minimal two-band model and we choose a real, normalized eigenvector parameterized by
\zeta
.
Using Eq. (
S33
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{0}(+)\\
\tilde{\phi}_{1}(+)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\theta_{0}}\cos\zeta\\
e^{i\theta_{1}}\sin\zeta\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{0}(-)\\
\tilde{\phi}_{1}(-)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\theta_{0}}\cos\zeta\\
e^{-i\theta_{1}}\sin\zeta\end{pmatrix},

(S43)

where
\theta_{0}=Ek_{0}/F
and
\theta_{1}=Ek_{0}/F-g_{E}(k_{x},k_{0})
. Combining Eqs. (
S38
), (
S40
), and (
S43
), the solvability condition for
\zeta
yields

\displaystyle\cos[2k_{0}E/F-g_{E}(k_{x},k_{0})]=\cos\theta\cos[g_{E}(k_{x},k_{0})-\varphi],

(S44)

which determines the WS energies. The mixing angle
\zeta
follows from

\displaystyle\sin 2\zeta=\sin\theta/\sin[2k_{0}E/F-g_{E}(k_{x},

\displaystyle k_{0})],

(S45)

quantifying the hybridization between the flat and dispersive bands.

Finally, based on Eqs. (
S33
) and (
S43
),
\bm{\phi}(k_{y})
is solved as:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}(k_{y})\\
\phi_{1}(k_{y})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-iEk_{y}/F+i\theta_{0}\,\mathrm{sgn}(k_{y})}\cos\zeta\\
e^{-iEk_{y}/F+ig_{E}+i\theta_{1}\,\mathrm{sgn}(k_{y})}\sin\zeta\end{pmatrix}.

(S46)

Thus
|\phi_{0}|^{2}=\cos^{2}\zeta
and
|\phi_{1}|^{2}=\sin^{2}\zeta
are independent of
k_{y}
; for

they are plotted in Fig. 2(h) of the main text using the

and
\varphi
shown in Fig. 2(e).

On the other hand, following the procedure described at the end of Sec.
II A
, the WS spectra and the corresponding real-space wavefunctions
\psi_{\mathrm{R},i}(y)
can be obtained numerically at fixed
k_{x}
. The resulting WS spectra shown in Fig. 2(a–d) of the main text agree with the analytical prediction in Eq. (
S44
). For the real-space wavefunctions, we again define

and plot it as a function of

and

). Translating

by one unit cell along

V
Numerical recipe to calculate
\theta
and
\varphi

This section details how we compute
\theta
and
\varphi
from the path-ordered exponential in Eq. (
S39
). Because the Pauli matrices
\sigma_{x}
,
\sigma_{y}
, and
\sigma_{z}
do not commute, we use a Trotter discretization with finite
\Delta k_{y}

Guttieres et al. 2023
. We truncate the integral at
\pm k_{y,\mathrm{max}}
and divide the interval into
N
steps so that
N\Delta k_{y}=2k_{y,\mathrm{max}}
. Direct evaluation of Eq. (
S39
) converges slowly with respect to both
k_{y,\mathrm{max}}
and
N
.

To improve convergence, we first integrate the
\sigma_{z}
component and define

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{0}=\tilde{\phi}_{0}e^{-ig_{A}},\qquad\hat{\phi}_{1}=\tilde{\phi}_{1}e^{ig_{A}},

(S48)

where

\displaystyle g_{A}(k_{x},k_{y})=\int_{0}^{k_{y}}\sqrt{1-d^{2}}\,\frac{k_{x}}{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{\prime 2}}\,dk_{y}^{\prime}=\sqrt{1-d^{2}}\,\arctan(k_{y}/k_{x}).

(S49)

Equation (
S36
) then becomes

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{y}}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\phi}_{0}\\

VI
WS spectra from the SFB in the kagome lattice

For the kagome lattice with nearest-neighbor hopping amplitude
t_{0}
, the tight-binding Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{kag}}(\bm{k})=t_{0}\begin{pmatrix}0&2\cos(k_{x}/2)&2\cos\!\left(k_{x}/4+\sqrt{3}k_{y}/4\right)\\[5.0pt]
2\cos(k_{x}/2)&0&2\cos\!\left(-k_{x}/4+\sqrt{3}k_{y}/4\right)\\[5.0pt]
2\cos\!\left(k_{x}/4+\sqrt{3}k_{y}/4\right)&2\cos\!\left(-k_{x}/4+\sqrt{3}k_{y}/4\right)&0\end{pmatrix},

(S56)

written in the orbital basis
|\bm{k},i\rangle
with
i\in\{A,B,C\}
. The orbital positions within the primitive unit cell are
\bm{\tau}_{A}=(1/2,0)
,
\bm{\tau}_{B}=(1,0)
, and
\bm{\tau}_{C}=(3/4,\sqrt{3}/4)
, and we set the lattice constant
a=1
[upper panel of Fig. 3(a) in the main text]. The band dispersions are
E_{0}=-2t_{0}
and

\displaystyle E_{1,2}=t_{0}\left[1\mp\sqrt{3+2\cos k_{x}+4\cos(k_{x}/2)\cos(\sqrt{3}k_{y}/2)}\right].

(S57)

Thus
E_{c}=E_{1}-E_{0}
in Eq. (
S32
). The effective continuum Hamiltonian of the SFB is given by Eq. (
S2
) with
d=1
and
t=t_{0}/4
.

Under an electric field
F_{0}
, the corresponding dimensionless field strength is
F_{0}/t=4F_{0}/t_{0}
. Using the scaling relation in Eq. (
S41
), we introduce the scaling parameter

\displaystyle\alpha=\sqrt[3]{\frac{4F_{0}/t_{0}}{F/t}}.

We compute the Berry phase (Zak phase
Zak 1989
) of the flat band and the dispersive band tangent to it along an open path of length
2k_{0x}=4\pi
(field along
x
) or
2k_{0y}=4\pi/\sqrt{3}
(field along
y
) [lower panel of Fig. 3(a) in the main text]. A convenient discrete expression is
Resta 2000
;
Fuchs and Piéchon 2021

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathrm{B}}=\mathrm{Im}\log\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}\langle u_{m}(\bm{k}_{i+1})|u_{m}(\bm{k}_{i})\rangle+\mathrm{Im}\log\langle u_{m}(\bm{k}_{0})|e^{i\bm{G}\cdot\bm{r}}|u_{m}(\bm{k}_{N})\rangle,

(S58)

where
m\in\{0,1\}
labels the band,
|u_{m}(\bm{k})\rangle
is an eigenstate of Eq. (
S56
), and
\bm{k}_{i}
are discretized points along the chosen path. The reciprocal lattice vector is
\bm{G}=(2k_{0x},0)
for the
x
path and
\bm{G}=(0,2k_{0y})
for the
y
path. In the orbital basis, the “closure” operator is

\displaystyle e^{i\bm{G}\cdot\bm{r}}=\mathrm{Diag}\bigl(e^{i\bm{G}\cdot\bm{\tau}_{A}},\,e^{i\bm{G}\cdot\bm{\tau}_{B}},\,e^{i\bm{G}\cdot\bm{\tau}_{C}}\bigr).

(S59)

Along
x

\pi
, identical to the minimal two-band model with
d=1
discussed in the main text. Therefore, the scaled
\theta
and
\varphi
from Fig. 2(e), together with Eqs. (

\pi

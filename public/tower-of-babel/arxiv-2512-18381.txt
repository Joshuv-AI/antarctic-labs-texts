Abstract
. In this work, we investigate the well-posedness, stabilization, and boundary controllability of a linear Rao–Nakra type sandwich beam. The system consists of three coupled equations that represent the longitudinal displacements of the outer layers and the transverse displacement of the composite beam, all of which are coupled with dynamical boundary conditions. In the first problem, time-dependent weights and delays are considered. Then, we establish the existence and uniqueness of solutions for the Cauchy problem associated with the damped system using semigroup theory and a classical result by Kato. Furthermore, employing a Lyapunov-based approach, we prove that the system’s energy decays exponentially, despite the presence of time-varying weights and delays. In the second problem, we consider a boundary linear control system and prove its well-posedness. By deriving an observability inequality for the adjoint system and applying the Hilbert Uniqueness Method (HUM), we show that the system is null controllable. A key contribution of this work lies in handling the full three-equation coupled system, which involves significant difficulty due to the dynamic boundary conditions, resolved via appropriately constructed Lyapunov functionals and intermediate observability inequalities.

Introduction

1.1.
Mathematical Models

Various types of beams are widely used in practice. Indeed, we encounter the classical Euler-Bernoulli beam, Rayleigh beam, Timoshenko, Bresse beam, sandwich beams, and micro-beam. One typical example of a sandwich beam is the so-called Rao-Nakra beam. The model describes the dynamics of a three-layer composite beam consisting of two stiff elastic face layers (or outer layers) bonded to a viscoelastic core. This configuration is commonly used in structural applications where vibration suppression and damping are desired, as the viscoelastic layer dissipates energy through shear deformation.

The model was first proposed by Rao and Nakra (1974)
[
43
,
44
]
as an extension of classical beam theories, incorporating both transverse shear deformation and rotary inertia effects, which are neglected in simpler models like the Euler–Bernoulli or Timoshenko beams. The governing equations couple the longitudinal displacements of the outer layers with the transverse displacement of the beam, linked through the shear strain in the viscoelastic core. This system of partial differential equations captures the interaction between bending, stretching, and shear in sandwich structures and has become a benchmark model for studying vibration control, stabilization, and active/passive damping mechanisms in smart materials and composite structures.

In the present article, we are interested in the study of the well-posedness, stabilization, and controllability of the Rao-Nakra sandwich beam. Precisely, we first consider the following system:

1.2.
Background and state of the art

The Rao-Nakra sandwich beam has been actively studied both numerically and analytically. Particularly, the stabilization and controllability problem has been the subject of mathematical endeavor. This has led to so many research papers, and hence it is quasi-impossible to review all of them. Notwithstanding, we shall restrict ourselves to the literature related to the analysis and control of solutions of the Rao-Nakra beam problem.
The first outcome goes back twenty years ago
[
22
]
, where the Riesz basis property is established for a two-layer beam. This result permits us to deduce a sufficient condition for the exponential stability as well as exact controllability of the system. Later, a three-layer Rao-Nakra beam is considered in
[
41
]
, and if the control time
T
is large enough, then the system is exactly controllable under certain other parametric restrictions.

A more general model is treated in
[
34
]
in the sense that several possible multilayers are used in the form of multilayer plates. Specifically, the model consists of
2m+1
alternating stiff and compliant (core) layers, with stiff layers on the outside. It is assumed that the stiff layers fulfill the Kirchhoff hypothesis, while the compliant layers admit shear. The Riesz basis property is shown for the eigenfunctions of the decoupled system, and then the exponential stability is concluded for the coupled system. A similar generalized model with interior damping is considered in
[
35
]
with all combinations of clamped and hinged boundary conditions. In turn, the control is present in either the moment or the rotation angle at one end of the beam. Then, if the damping parameters are sufficiently small, the exact controllability is proved provided that the control time
T
is large enough. Moreover, the exact controllability holds for the undamped cases without any restriction on the parameters in the system.

When the Rao-Nakra beam is subject to magnetic effects for the piezoelectric layers, the lack of uniform strong stabilization is demonstrated in
[
36
]
in the case where the mechanical boundary forces for the longitudinal dynamics are removed. However, in the absence of magnetic effects, four type feedback controllers are required in order to reach the exponential stability of the system. Such results are improved in
[
37
]
for fully-dynamic and electrostatic Rao-Nakra
type models. The polynomial stability is obtained in
[
48
]
for a simplified Rao-Nakra beam system. The main result in
[
28
]
is the polynomial stability of the Rao-Nakra beam system under the action of only one viscous damping in either the beam equation or one of the wave equations. The exponential stability is obtained in
[
45
]
for the Rao-Nakra beam system via a thermoelastic type control with second sound. The same result holds if three interior viscous damping controls act despite the presence of one time-delayed term of the same type
[
42
]
. It turns out that this outcome remains valid if the three damping controls and the delayed term are of type Kelvin–Voigt
[
11
]
. In
[
7
]
, the long-time dynamics of a non-autonomous Rao-Nakra beam with nonlinear damping and source terms are studied. Lipschitz stability result is proved, as well as the existence of pullback attractors.

Rao-Nakra sandwich beam with fractional derivatives has also attracted some authors. In fact, three boundary dissipation of fractional derivative type are considered in
[
47
]
and polynomial stability of the system is proved. Recently, fractional interior damping terms have been incorporated in the Rao-Nakra beam system
[
6
]
. Polynomial decay of the system’s energy is achieved, and then the decay rate is shown to depend on the fractional derivative parameters.
Generalized Rao-Nakra beam system is also dealt with in
[
1
]
, where the system consists of four wave equations for the longitudinal displacements and the shear angle of the top and bottom layers, and one Euler–Bernoulli beam equation for the transversal displacement. Two viscous damping controls are designed, and several stability and non-stability findings are presented depending on whether both of the top and bottom layers are directly damped or otherwise. In
[
31
]
, general stability findings are presented for the Rao-Nakra beam with memory and thermal dissipation governed by Fourier’s law of heat conduction. In
[
39
]
, two Kelvin-Voigt type damping controls and one memory control are proposed to show the exponential stability for the Rao-Nakra beam. In turn, it is proved in
[
30
]
that the effect of Gurtin-Pipkin’s thermal law on the outer layers of the Rao-Nakra beam system is strong enough to ensure the exponential stability of the system. In
[
17
]
, nonlinear viscous damping controls and nonlinear source terms are introduced in the beam, and the uniform energy decay rates are proved under
certain assumptions of the parameters. Moreover, the authors prove the existence of the global attractor and generalized exponential attractors of the system.
Note that exponential as well as general energy decay rates of the energy of the Rao-Nakra beam are obtained in
[
4
]
in the case of time-varying weights and nonlinear viscous damping controls.

When the Kelvin-Voigt damping terms act on the first and third equations of the beam, the lack of exponential stability of the system is proven, and then the polynomial stability with rate
t^{-1/4}
is established
[
40
]
. Note that the exponential stability is established in
[
18
]
for the Rao-Nakra beam equation with time-varying weights and time-varying delay in the damping. This result is proved by using two approaches: the multiplier method and the observability argument. Another work deals with the generalized Rao-Nakra beam equation, and the analytic stability is obtained when all the displacements are globally damped via Kelvin-Voigt damping
[
3
]
. On the other hand, if the damping acts only on the shear angle displacements of the top and bottom layers, then the energy of the system decays polynomially with a decay rate
t^{-1/2}
.

One can also find a study on the Rao-Nakra beam system in the whole line. Indeed, linear frictional damping controls are supposed to act on the two wave equations of the system
[
20
]
. In this event, the author proves a polynomial decay result for the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}
norm of solutions and their higher order derivatives with respect to the space variable. It is also demonstrated that the decay rate is specified in terms of the regularity of the initial data. The asymptotic behavior of solutions in a thermoplastic Rao-Nakra beam is explored when nonlinear damping with a variable exponent occurs
[
2
]
. Exponential, polynomial, and strong stability for Rao-Nakra sandwich beams are obtained with the action of a single internal infinite memory
[
21
]
. Furthermore, the stability type depends on the presence of the memory term in the wave or the Bernoulli equation, and also the order of the derivative in the memory term. Unlike the works mentioned above, there are articles where it is shown that the action of localized frictional damping controls in the Rao-Nakra beam system is strong enough to force the corresponding energy to decay (see
[
5
]
for linear damping and
[
14
]
for nonlinear damping).

Lastly, the competition between a nonlinear stabilization mechanism and a nonlinear source term for the Rao-Nakra beam is investigated in
[
19
]
. Blow-up of solutions to the problem is established with linear and nonlinear weak damping at high initial energy. An estimate is also provided for the lower and upper bounds of the lifespan of the blow-up solution and the blow-up rate
[
19
]
.

satisfying

(u(T),u_{t}(T),v(T),v_{t}(T),w(T),w_{t}(T),u_{t}(L,T),v_{t}(L,T),w_{t}(L,T))=(0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0).

Here,
\mathcal{P}
is the operator defined by (

) and (
4.4
).

To establish Theorem
1.2
, we employ the observation–control duality framework developed by Dolecki and Russell
[
16
]
, within the setting introduced by Lions
[
26
]
(see also
[
27
]
). Specifically, we rely on the Hilbert Uniqueness Method (HUM), which provides a fundamental equivalence between controllability and observability.

A central contribution of our work is the study of a Rao–Nakra sandwich beam model involving three coupled equations. This significantly increases the complexity of the analysis, as the model consists of three beams subject to dynamic boundary conditions. These boundary conditions present substantial challenges in proving both the exponential decay of the energy and the observability inequality. As mentioned earlier, these difficulties are overcome by developing an appropriate Lyapunov approach and by deriving two intermediate observability inequalities, which play a crucial role in handling the full system.

We conclude the introduction with an outline of the paper. In Section
2
, we establish the well-posedness of system (
1.1
) using semigroup theory,
specifically applying a theorem due to Kato
[
24
]
, thereby providing a solution to the first problem posed in the introduction. Section

is devoted to proving our first main result, showing that the energy associated with system (

) decays exponentially, thus addressing the second main problem introduced in this work. Sections
4
and

focus on the properties of the control system (

). In particular, we first establish the well-posedness of this system via semigroup theory, and then derive an observability inequality for the adjoint system associated with (

), which, together with the Hilbert Uniqueness Method, ensures Theorem

.

Regarding condition (2) of Theorem

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{A}(t)V,V\right\rangle_{t}-\kappa(t)\left\langle V,V\right\rangle_{t}\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\Psi_{1}(L,t),z_{1}(1,t)\right)\Phi^{d_{1}}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}\left(\Psi_{1}(L,t),z_{1}(1,t)\right)^{T}

(2.13)

\begin{cases}\lambda u-\Psi_{1}=f_{1},\\

1.9
) and (
1.10
), respectively, such that

E(t)\leq\zeta\,e^{-\dfrac{\lambda}{1+\mu_{4}}t}E(0),\quad\text{for all }t\geq 0.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

4.
Well-posedness for the control system

In this section, we establish the well-posedness of system (
1.8
). We consider both the homogeneous case, where the boundary inputs
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
, and
f_{3}
vanish, and the nonhomogeneous case, in which these control functions are present. Throughout, we work in the Lebesgue space
L^{2}(0,L)
, equipped with its standard inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
.

4.1.
The homogeneous system

We begin by considering the following homogeneous system:

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho_{1}h_{1}u_{tt}-E_{1}h_{1}u_{xx}-k\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)=0,&x\in(0,L),\,\,\,t>0,\\
\rho_{3}h_{3}v_{tt}-E_{3}h_{3}v_{xx}+k\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)=0,&x\in(0,L),\,\,\,t>0,\\
\rho hw_{tt}+EIw_{xxxx}-\alpha k\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)_{x}=0,&x\in(0,L),\,\,\,t>0,\\
u(0,t)=v(0,t)=w_{x}(0,t)=w_{xxx}(0,t)=0,&t>0,\\
w_{xx}(L,t)=w_{xxx}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\
u_{tt}(L,t)+u_{x}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\
v_{tt}(L,t)+v_{x}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\
w_{tt}(L,t)-u(L,t)+v(L,t)+\alpha w_{x}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\
\left(u,v,w\right)\left(x,0\right)=\left(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0}\right)(x),&x\in(0,L),\\
\left(u_{t},v_{t},w_{t}\right)\left(x,0\right)=\left(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1}\right)\left(x\right),&x\in(0,L).\end{array}\right.

Observe that, by a formal computation, we find that the solution of the system (
4.1
) is given by

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\bigg[\rho_{1}h_{1}\left\|u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+E_{1}h_{1}\left\|u_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{3}h_{3}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+E_{3}h_{3}\left\|v_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho h\left\|w_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+EI\left\|w_{xx}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+k\|-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+E_{1}h_{1}\left|u_{t}(L)\right|^{2}+E_{3}h_{3}\left|v_{t}(L)\right|^{2}+\alpha k\left|w_{t}(L)\right|^{2}\bigg]=0.

With this in mind, we introduce the new variables

\Psi_{1}=u_{t},\ \ \ \Psi_{2}=v_{t},\ \ \ \Psi_{3}=w_{t},\ \ \ \Psi_{4}(\cdot)=\Psi_{1}(L,\cdot),\ \ \ \Psi_{5}(\cdot)=\Psi_{2}(L,\cdot),\ \ \ \Psi_{6}(\cdot)=\Psi_{3}(L,\cdot),

and define the vector functions

U=(u,\Psi_{1},v,\Psi_{2},w,\Psi_{3},\Psi_{4},\Psi_{5},\Psi_{6})^{\top}\text{ \ and \ }U_{0}=\left(u_{0},u_{1},v_{0},v_{1},w_{0},w_{1},\Psi_{4}(0),\Psi_{5}(0),\Psi_{6}(0)\right)^{\top}.

Hence, the system (
4.1
) can be reformulated as an abstract Cauchy problem

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{l}U_{t}=\mathcal{P}U\\
U(0)=U_{0},\end{array}\right.

where the operator
\mathcal{P}
is given by

(4.3)

\mathcal{P}U:=\begin{pmatrix}\Psi_{1}\\
\dfrac{1}{\rho_{1}h_{1}}\left[E_{1}h_{1}u_{xx}+k(-u+v+\alpha w_{x})\right]\\
\Psi_{2}\\
\dfrac{1}{\rho_{3}h_{3}}\left[E_{3}h_{3}v_{xx}-k(-u+v+\alpha w_{x})\right]\\
\Psi_{3}\\
\dfrac{1}{\rho h}\left[-EIw_{xxxx}+\alpha k(-u+v+\alpha w_{x})_{x}\right]\\
-u_{x}(L,\cdot)\\
-v_{x}(L,\cdot)\\
-\left[-u(L,\cdot)+v(L,\cdot)+\alpha w_{x}(L,\cdot)\right]\end{pmatrix}.

Furthermore, the domain of
\mathcal{P}:D\left(\mathcal{P}\right)\subset\mathcal{H}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}
is defined by

(4.4)

D(\mathcal{P})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &U=(u,\Psi_{1},v,\Psi_{2},w,\Psi_{3},\Psi_{4},\Psi_{5},\Psi_{6})^{\top}\in\mathcal{H}_{2};\\
&(u,v,w)\in\left[H^{2}(0,L)\cap H_{*}^{1}(0,L)\right]^{2}\times H^{4}_{*}(0,L),\\
&(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2},\Psi_{3})\in\left[H_{*}^{1}(0,L)\right]^{2}\times H_{*}^{2}(0,L),\\
&\Psi_{4}=\Psi_{1}(L),\ \Psi_{5}=\Psi_{2}(L),\ \Psi_{6}=\Psi_{3}(L)\end{aligned}\right\}.

Now, let us denote by
\rho(\mathcal{P})
the resolvent set of the operator
\mathcal{P}
. Then, we have the following result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
\left(D\left(\mathcal{P}\right),\mathcal{P}\right)
be defined as before. Then,
0\in\rho(\mathcal{P})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{P}^{-1}
is compact.

Proof.

For
G=\left(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4},g_{5},g_{6},g_{7},g_{8},g_{9}\right)\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
, we show the existence of
U\in D\left(\mathcal{P}\right),
unique solution of the equation

\mathcal{P}U=G.

Equivalently, one must consider the system given by

(4.5)

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}=g_{1},\quad\Psi_{2}=g_{3},\quad\Psi_{3}=g_{5},

(4.6)

\displaystyle E_{1}h_{1}u_{xx}+k\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)=\rho_{1}h_{1}g_{2},

(4.7)

\displaystyle E_{3}h_{3}v_{xx}-k\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)=\rho_{3}h_{3}g_{4},

(4.8)

\displaystyle EIw_{xxxx}+\alpha k\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)_{x}=\rho hg_{6},

(4.9)

\displaystyle-u_{x}(L)=g_{7},

(4.10)

\displaystyle-v_{x}(L)=g_{8},

(4.11)

\displaystyle-[-u(L)+v(L)+\alpha w_{x}(L)]=g_{9},

with the following boundary conditions

(4.12)

u(0)=v(0)=w_{x}(0)=w_{xxx}(0)=w_{xx}(L)=w_{xxx}(L)=0.

Let
(u^{*},v^{*},w^{*})\in\left[H_{*}^{1}(0,L)\right]^{2}\times H_{*}^{2}(0,L)
. We multiply the equations (
4.6
), (
4.7
), and (
4.8
) by
u^{*}
,
v^{*}
, and
w^{*}
, respectively, and integrate by parts over
(0,L)
. Using (
4.9
), (
4.10
), (
4.11
) and the boundary conditions in (
4.12
), and then summing the resulting expressions, we obtain

(4.13)

\mathcal{B}((u,v,w),(u^{*},v^{*},w^{*}))=\mathcal{L}(u^{*},v^{*},w^{*}),\quad\forall(u^{*},v^{*},w^{*})\in\left[H_{*}^{1}(0,L)\right]^{2}\times H_{*}^{2}(0,L),

where

(4.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}((u,v,w),(u^{*},v^{*},w^{*}))

\displaystyle=E_{1}h_{1}\langle u_{x},u_{x}^{*}\rangle+E_{3}h_{3}\langle v_{x},v_{x}^{*}\rangle+EI\langle w_{xx},w_{xx}^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle+k\langle-u+v+\alpha w_{x},-u^{*}+v^{*}+\alpha w_{x}^{*}\rangle,

and

(4.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(u^{*},v^{*},w^{*})

\displaystyle=-\rho_{1}h_{1}\langle g_{2},u^{*}\rangle-\rho_{3}h_{3}\langle g_{4},v^{*}\rangle-\rho h\langle g_{6},w^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-E_{1}h_{1}g_{7}u^{*}(L)-E_{3}h_{3}g_{8}v^{*}(L)-\alpha kg_{9}w^{*}(L).

From (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), it follows that the bilinear form

\mathcal{B}:\left[\left[H_{*}^{1}(0,L)\right]^{2}\times H_{*}^{2}(0,L)\right]^{2}\to\mathbb{C}

is continuous and coercive, and the linear functional

\mathcal{L}:\left[H_{*}^{1}(0,L)\right]^{2}\times H_{*}^{2}(0,L)\to\mathbb{C}

it is continuous as well. Therefore, by the Lax–Milgram theorem, there exists a unique weak solution

(u,v,w)\in\left[H_{*}^{1}(0,L)\right]^{2}\times H_{*}^{2}(0,L)

to the variational problem (
4.13
).

Furthermore, using (
4.5
) and standard elliptic regularity results, we conclude that
U\in D(\mathcal{P})
,
which shows that the operator
\mathcal{P}
is bijective.
Following arguments similar to
[
10
, Proposition 2]
, one can also verify that
\mathcal{P}^{-1}
is bounded,
implying that
0\in\rho(\mathcal{P})
, the resolvent set of
\mathcal{P}
.
Finally, by the Sobolev embedding theorem, the natural inclusion

i:D(\mathcal{P})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}

is compact, which ensures that
\mathcal{P}^{-1}
is a compact operator.∎

The next result ensures that the operator
\mathcal{P}
generates a group. Precisely, we have the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

The operator
\left(D\left(\mathcal{P}\right),\mathcal{P}\right)
is infinitesimal generator of a group of isometries
\{S(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
U^{\sharp}\in D\left(\mathcal{P}\right)
given by (
1.12
). Then, for all
U\in D\left(\mathcal{P}\right)
, given by (
1.11
), we obtain, after some integration by parts and rearrangements, that

\begin{split}\left\langle\mathcal{P}U,U^{\sharp}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=&\ \ \rho_{1}h_{1}\left\langle\dfrac{1}{\rho_{1}h_{1}}\left[E_{1}h_{1}u_{xx}+k(-u+v+\alpha w_{x})\right],\Psi_{1}^{\sharp}\right\rangle+E_{1}h_{1}\left\langle\Psi_{1x},u^{\sharp}_{x}\right\rangle\\
&+\rho_{3}h_{3}\left\langle\dfrac{1}{\rho_{3}h_{3}}\left[E_{3}h_{3}v_{xx}-k(-u+v+\alpha w_{x})\right],\Psi_{2}^{\sharp}\right\rangle+E_{3}h_{3}\left\langle\Psi_{2x},v_{x}^{\sharp}\right\rangle\\
&+\rho h\left\langle\dfrac{1}{\rho h}\left[-EIw_{xxxx}+\alpha k(-u+v+\alpha w_{x})_{x}\right],\Psi_{3}^{\sharp}\right\rangle+EI\left\langle\Psi_{3xx},w_{xx}^{\sharp}\right\rangle\\
&+k\left\langle-\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{2}+\alpha\Psi_{3x},-u^{\sharp}+v^{\sharp}+\alpha w_{x}^{\sharp}\right\rangle-E_{1}h_{1}\left(u_{x}(L),\Psi_{4}^{\sharp}\right)_{\mathbb{R}}\\
&-E_{3}h_{3}\left(v_{x}(L),\Psi_{5}^{\sharp}\right)_{\mathbb{R}}-\alpha k\left([-u(L)+v(L)+\alpha kw_{xx}(L)],\Psi_{6}^{\sharp}\right)_{\mathbb{R}}\\
&=\rho_{1}h_{1}\left\langle\Psi_{1},-\dfrac{1}{\rho_{1}h_{1}}\left[E_{1}h_{1}u_{xx}^{\sharp}+k(-u^{\sharp}+v^{\sharp}+\alpha w_{x}^{\sharp})\right]\right\rangle+E_{1}h_{1}\left\langle u_{x},-\Psi_{1x}^{\sharp}\right\rangle\\
&+\rho_{3}h_{3}\left\langle\Psi_{2},-\dfrac{1}{\rho_{3}h_{3}}\left[E_{3}h_{3}v_{xx}^{\sharp}-k(-u^{\sharp}+v^{\sharp}+\alpha w_{x}^{\sharp})\right]\right\rangle+E_{3}h_{3}\left\langle v_{x},-\Psi_{2x}^{\sharp}\right\rangle\\
&+\rho h\left\langle\Psi_{3},-\dfrac{1}{\rho h}\left[-EIw_{xxxx}^{\sharp}+\alpha k(-u^{\sharp}+v^{\sharp}+\alpha w_{x}^{\sharp})_{x}\right]\right\rangle+EI\left\langle w_{xx},-\Psi_{3xx}^{\sharp}\right\rangle\\
&+k\left\langle-u+v+\alpha w_{x},-\left[\Psi_{1}^{\sharp}+\Psi_{2}^{\sharp}+\alpha\Psi_{3x}^{\sharp}\right]\right\rangle+E_{1}h_{1}\left(\Psi_{4},u_{x}^{\sharp}(L)\right)_{\mathbb{R}}\\
&+E_{3}h_{3}\left(\Psi_{5},v_{x}^{\sharp}(L)\right)_{\mathbb{R}}+\alpha k\left(\Psi_{6},[-u^{\sharp}(L)+v^{\sharp}(L)+\alpha kw_{xx}^{\sharp}(L)]\right)_{\mathbb{R}}\\
&=\left\langle U,-\mathcal{P}U^{\sharp}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}.\end{split}

Hence,
D\left(\mathcal{P}\right)\subset D\left(\mathcal{P}^{*}\right)
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}U^{\sharp}=-\mathcal{P}U^{\sharp}
, for any
U^{\sharp}\in D\left(\mathcal{P}\right)
.
On the other hand, let
U^{\sharp}\in D\left(\mathcal{P}^{*}\right)
. Then, by definition,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{P}U,U^{\sharp}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=\left\langle U,\mathcal{P}^{*}U^{\sharp}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}},\text{ for all }\ \ U\in D\left(\mathcal{P}\right).

By the same calculations as above, we have that

(4.17)

\displaystyle\left\langle U,\mathcal{P}^{*}U^{\sharp}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{P}U,U^{\sharp}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}

Therefore, from (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) we obtain that

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle-E_{1}h_{1}\left(u_{x}(0),\Psi_{1}^{\sharp}(0)\right)_{\mathbb{R}}-E_{3}h_{3}\left((v_{x}(0),\Psi_{2}^{\sharp}(0)\right)_{\mathbb{R}}-EI\left((w_{xx}(0),\Psi_{3x}^{\sharp}(0)\right)_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle+E_{1}h_{1}\left\langle u_{x}(L),\Psi_{1}^{\sharp}(L)-\Psi_{4}^{\sharp}\right\rangle+E_{3}h_{3}\left\langle v_{x}(L),\Psi_{2}^{\sharp}(L)-\Psi_{5}^{\sharp}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-EI\left(w_{xx}(0),\Psi_{3x}^{\sharp}(0)\right)_{\mathbb{R}}+EI\left\langle\Psi_{3x}(L),w_{xx}^{\sharp}(L)\right\rangle-EI\left\langle\Psi_{3}(L),w_{xxx}^{\sharp}(L)\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\alpha k\left([-u(L)+v(L)+\alpha kw_{xx}(L)],\Psi_{3}^{\sharp}(L)-\Psi_{6}^{\sharp}\right)_{\mathbb{R}}.

Since the above equality holds for all
U\in D(\mathcal{P})
, it follows that
U^{\sharp}\in D(\mathcal{P})
. Consequently, we deduce that
D(\mathcal{P})=D(\mathcal{P}^{*})
, and from the previous computations, we also obtain
\mathcal{P}^{*}=-\mathcal{P}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{P}
is a skew-adjoint operator, and by Stone’s Theorem, it generates a group of isometries
\{S(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathcal{H}_{2}
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Lemma
4.1
, Proposition
4.1
, and standard results from the theory of evolution equations (see,
e.g.,

[
38
]
), we obtain the following existence and uniqueness result.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
U_{0}\in D\left(\mathcal{P}\right)
, there exists a unique solution
U
of the abstract Cauchy problem (
4.2
) such that

(4.18)

\displaystyle U\in C\left([0,\infty);D\left(\mathcal{P}\right)\right)\cap C^{1}\left([0,\infty);\mathcal{H}_{2}\right).

Moreover, for any
t>0
, we have that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=\|U_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}.

4.2.
The nonhomogeneous system

In this subsection, we focus on analyzing the complete system (
1.8
). We start with the following result:

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
U_{0}\in D\left(\mathcal{P}\right)

and
f_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(0,\infty),\ \ i=1,2,3
,
system (
1.8
) has a unique solution
U\in C\left([0,\infty);D\left(\mathcal{P}\right)\right)\cap C^{1}\left([0,\infty);\mathcal{H}_{2}\right).

Proof.

Let
\phi_{1}\in C^{\infty}[0,L]
be a smooth function satisfying the boundary conditions
\phi_{1}(0)=\phi_{1}(L)=0
and
\phi_{1}^{\prime}(L)=-1
.
We now introduce the following change of variables:

(4.20)

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}z\\
\eta\\
\varphi\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u\\
v\\
w\end{array}\right)-\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{u}\\
\tilde{v}\\
\tilde{w}\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{1}(t)\phi_{1}(x)\\
f_{2}(t)\phi_{1}(x)\\
f_{3}(t)\phi_{1}(x)\end{array}\right),

where
\left(\tilde{u},\tilde{v},\tilde{w}\right)
is the unique solution of the system

(4.21)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho_{1}h_{1}\tilde{u}_{tt}-E_{1}h_{1}\tilde{u}_{xx}-k\left(-\tilde{u}+\tilde{v}+\alpha\tilde{w}_{x}\right)=0,&x\in(0,L),\;\;t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\rho_{3}h_{3}\tilde{v}_{tt}-E_{3}h_{3}\tilde{v}_{xx}+k\left(-\tilde{u}+\tilde{v}+\alpha\tilde{w}_{x}\right)=0,&x\in(0,L),\;\;t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\rho h\tilde{w}_{tt}+EI\tilde{w}_{xxxx}-\alpha k\left(-\tilde{u}+\tilde{v}+\alpha\tilde{w}_{x}\right)_{x}=0,&x\in(0,L),\;\;t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\tilde{u}(0,t)=\tilde{v}(0,t)=\tilde{w}_{x}(0,t)=\tilde{w}_{xxx}(0,t)=0,&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\tilde{w}_{xx}(L,t)=\tilde{w}_{xxx}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\tilde{u}_{tt}(L,t)+\tilde{u}_{x}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\tilde{v}_{tt}(L,t)+\tilde{v}_{x}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\tilde{w}_{tt}(L,t)-\tilde{u}(L,t)+\tilde{v}(L,t)+\alpha\tilde{w}_{x}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\left(\tilde{u},\tilde{v},\tilde{w}\right)(x,0)=\left(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0}\right)(x),&x\in(0,L),\\[4.30554pt]
\left(\tilde{u}_{t},\tilde{v}_{t},\tilde{w}_{t}\right)(x,0)=\left(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1}\right)(x),&x\in(0,L),\end{array}\right.

given by Theorem
4.1
. Thus,
\left(z,\eta,\varphi\right)
solves the problem

(4.22)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho_{1}h_{1}z_{tt}-E_{1}h_{1}z_{xx}-k\left(-z+\eta+\alpha\varphi_{x}\right)=F,&x\in(0,L),\;\;t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\rho_{3}h_{3}\eta_{tt}-E_{3}h_{3}\eta_{xx}+k\left(-z+\eta+\alpha\varphi_{x}\right)=G,&x\in(0,L),\;\;t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\rho h\varphi_{tt}+EI\varphi_{xxxx}-\alpha k\left(-z+\eta+\alpha\varphi_{x}\right)_{x}=H,&x\in(0,L),\;\;t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
z(0,t)=\eta(0,t)=\varphi_{x}(0,t)=\varphi_{xxx}(0,t)=0,&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\varphi_{xx}(L,t)=\varphi_{xxx}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
z_{tt}(L,t)+z_{x}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\eta_{tt}(L,t)+\eta_{x}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\varphi_{tt}(L,t)-z(L,t)+\eta(L,t)+\alpha\varphi_{x}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\left(z,\eta,\varphi\right)(x,0)=\left(0,0,0\right),&x\in(0,L),\\[4.30554pt]
\left(z_{t},\eta_{t},\varphi_{t}\right)(x,0)=\left(0,0,0\right),&x\in(0,L).\end{array}\right.

Here,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F&=\rho_{1}h_{1}f_{1}^{\prime\prime}(t)\phi_{1}(x)-E_{1}h_{1}f_{1}(t)\phi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x)-k\left[-f_{1}(t)\phi_{1}(x)+f_{2}(t)\phi_{1}(x)+\alpha f_{3}(t)\phi_{1}^{\prime}(x)\right],\\
\\
G&=\rho_{3}h_{3}f_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)\phi_{1}(x)-E_{3}h_{3}f_{2}(t)\phi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x)+k\left[-f_{1}(t)\phi_{1}(x)+f_{2}(t)\phi_{1}(x)+\alpha f_{3}(t)\phi_{1}^{\prime}(x)\right],\\
\\
H&=\rho hf_{3}^{\prime\prime}(t)\phi_{1}(x)+EIf_{3}(t)\phi_{1}^{(4)}(x)-\alpha k\left[-f_{1}(t)\phi_{1}^{\prime}(x)+f_{2}(t)\phi_{1}^{\prime}(x)+\alpha f_{3}(t)\phi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x)\right],\end{array}\right.

and

(F(t,x),G(t,x),H(t,x))\in\left[C^{1}(\left[0,\infty\right]\times\left[0,L\right])\right]^{3}.

Now, let us use the notation established in the previous section to express the system (
4.22
) as an abstract evolution equation in the following form

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{l}W_{t}+\mathcal{P}W=\mathcal{K}\\
W(0)=0,\end{array}\right.

where
W=(z,z_{t},\eta,\eta_{t},\varphi,\varphi_{t},z_{t}(L,T),\eta_{t}(L,t),\varphi_{t}(L,t))^{\top}
and

\mathcal{K}=\left(0,\dfrac{F}{\rho_{1}h_{1}},0,\dfrac{G}{\rho_{3}h_{3}},0,\dfrac{H}{\rho h},0,0,0\right)^{\top}\in\left[C^{1}(\left[0,\infty\right]\times\left[0,L\right])\right]^{9}.

Since the operator
\mathcal{P}
generates a group of isometries on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, it follows that system (
4.22
) admits a unique solution

W\in C\left([0,\infty);D\left(\mathcal{P}\right)\right)\cap C^{1}\left([0,\infty);\mathcal{H}_{2}\right).

Therefore, by reverting the change of variables in (
4.20
), the proof is complete.
∎

With the well-posedness result established, we now proceed to analyze solutions of system (
1.8
) in the transposition sense. For that, given
T>0
,
U_{0}\in D\left(\mathcal{P}\right)
,

(J_{1},J_{2},J_{3})\in L^{2}\left(0,T;\left(\left[H_{*}^{1}(0,L)\right]^{2}\times H_{*}^{2}(0,L)\right)^{*}\right)

and
f_{i}\in L^{2}(0,T)
, for
i=1,2,3,
consider the non-homogeneous system

(4.23)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho_{1}h_{1}u_{tt}-E_{1}h_{1}u_{xx}-k\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)=J_{1},&x\in(0,L),\;\;t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\rho_{3}h_{3}v_{tt}-E_{3}h_{3}v_{xx}+k\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)=J_{2},&x\in(0,L),\;\;t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\rho hw_{tt}+EIw_{xxxx}-\alpha k\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)_{x}=J_{3},&x\in(0,L),\;\;t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
u(0,t)=v(0,t)=w_{x}(0,t)=w_{xxx}(0,t)=0,&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
w_{xx}(L,t)=w_{xxx}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
u_{tt}(L,t)+u_{x}(L,t)=f_{1},&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
v_{tt}(L,t)+v_{x}(L,t)=f_{2},&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
w_{tt}(L,t)-u(L,t)+v(L,t)+\alpha w_{x}(L,t)=f_{3},&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
\left(u,v,w\right)(x,0)=\left(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0}\right)(x),&x\in(0,L),\\[4.30554pt]
\left(u_{t},v_{t},w_{t}\right)(x,0)=\left(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1}\right)(x),&x\in(0,L).\end{array}\right.

Now, let us introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

A solution by transposition to the system (
4.23
) is defined as a vector-valued function

Proposition 5.2

solves the following system:

(5.34)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}U^{n}_{t}(t)=\mathcal{P}U^{n}(t),\\[3.00003pt]
U^{n}(0)=U^{n}_{0}.\end{array}\right.

From (
5.32
) and the well-posedness results, we obtain the following uniform bounds:

(5.35)

\left|\begin{array}[]{lll}(u^{n},v^{n},w^{n})&\text{bounded in}&L^{\infty}\!\left(0,T;[H^{1}(0,L)]^{3}\right),\\[3.00003pt]
(u^{n}_{t},v^{n}_{t},w^{n}_{t})&\text{bounded in}&L^{\infty}\!\left(0,T;[L^{2}(0,L)]^{3}\right).\end{array}\right.

Once we have the embedding

H^{1}(0,L)\hookrightarrow H^{s}(0,L)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(0,L),

and since the embedding
H^{1}(0,L)\hookrightarrow H^{s}(0,L)
is compact for all
s\in\left(\tfrac{1}{2},1\right)
, from (
5.35
) and the classical compactness results given by
[
46
, Corollary 4]
, we can extract a subsequence of
(u^{n},v^{n},w^{n})
, still denoted by
(u^{n},v^{n},w^{n})
, such that

(5.36)

\displaystyle(u^{n},v^{n},w^{n})\longrightarrow(u,v,w)\quad\text{strongly in}\quad L^{\infty}\!\left(0,T;[H^{s}(0,L)]^{3}\right).

Thus, from (
5.21
), (
5.33
), and (
5.36
), we deduce that
(U^{n})
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{\infty}\!\left(0,T;\mathcal{H}_{2}\right)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle U^{n}\longrightarrow U\quad\text{strongly in}\quad L^{\infty}\!\left(0,T;\mathcal{H}_{2}\right),

and by (
5.32
),

(5.37)

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=1,\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,T].

Also,

(5.38)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\rightarrow 0}\left\{\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\left(\left|u^{n}_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|v^{n}_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|w^{n}_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}\right)dt\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\left(\left|u_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|v_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|w_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}\right)dt.

From (
5.38
), we have that the limit

U=\big(u,u_{t},v,v_{t},w,w_{t},u_{t}(L,t),v_{t}(L,t),w_{t}(L,t)\big)^{\top}

satisfies the linear problem

(5.39)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho_{1}h_{1}u_{tt}-E_{1}h_{1}u_{xx}-k\!\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)=0,&x\in(0,L),\,t\in(0,T),\\[5.0pt]
\rho_{3}h_{3}v_{tt}-E_{3}h_{3}v_{xx}+k\!\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)=0,&x\in(0,L),\,t\in(0,T),\\[5.0pt]
\rho hw_{tt}+EIw_{xxxx}-\alpha k\!\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)_{x}=0,&x\in(0,L),\,t\in(0,T),\\[5.0pt]
u(0,t)=v(0,t)=w_{x}(0,t)=w_{xxx}(0,t)=0,&t\in(0,T),\\[5.0pt]
w_{xx}(L,t)=w_{xxx}(L,t)=0,&t\in(0,T),\\[5.0pt]
u_{x}(L,t)=0,\quad v_{x}(L,t)=0,&t\in(0,T),\\[5.0pt]
-u(L,t)+v(L,t)+\alpha w_{x}(L,t)=0,&t\in(0,T).\end{array}\right.

Let
(\hat{u},\hat{v},\hat{w})
denote the extension by zero of
(u_{tt},v_{tt},w_{tt})
for
x\in(-a,a)\setminus(0,L)
, where
(-a,a)\supset(0,L)
is an interval. Then,
(\hat{u},\hat{v},\hat{w})
solves, in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\big((0,L)\times(0,T)\big)
, the following system:

(5.40)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho_{1}h_{1}\hat{u}_{tt}-E_{1}h_{1}\hat{u}_{xx}-k\left(-\hat{u}+\hat{v}+\alpha\hat{w}_{x}\right)=0,&x\in(0,L),\,\,\,t\in(0,T)\\
\rho_{3}h_{3}\hat{v}_{tt}-E_{3}h_{3}\hat{v}_{xx}+k\left(-\hat{u}+\hat{v}+\alpha\hat{w}_{x}\right)=0,&x\in(0,L),\,\,\,t\in(0,T)\\
\rho h\hat{w}_{tt}+EI\hat{w}_{xxxx}-\alpha k\left(-\hat{u}+\hat{v}+\alpha\hat{w}_{x}\right)_{x}=0,&x\in(0,L),\,\,\,t\in(0,T)\\
\hat{u}(0,t)=\hat{v}(0,t)=\hat{w}_{x}(0,t)=\hat{w}_{xxx}(0,t)=0,&t\in(0,T)\\
\hat{w}_{xx}(L,t)=\hat{w}_{xxx}(L,t)=0,&t\in(0,T)\\
\hat{u}_{x}(L,t)=0,&t\in(0,T)\\
\hat{v}_{x}(L,t)=0,&t\in(0,T)\\
-\hat{u}(L,t)+\hat{v}(L,t)+\alpha\hat{w}_{x}(L,t)=0,&t\in(0,T)\\
(\hat{u},\hat{v},\hat{w})=(0,0,0),&x\in(-a,0]\cup[L,a),\ t\in(0,T),\end{array}\right.

Then, by Holmgren’s Uniqueness Theorem
[
23
]
,
(\hat{u},\hat{v},\hat{w})\equiv(0,0,0)
in
(-a,a)\times(0,T)
. Hence, from (
5.39
), we have that
U=(u,u_{t},v,v_{t},w,w_{t},u_{t}(L,t),v_{t}(L,t),w_{t}(L,t))^{\top}
satisfies

(5.41)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-E_{1}h_{1}u_{xx}-k\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)=0,&x\in(0,L),\,\,\,t\in(0,T)\\
-E_{3}h_{3}v_{xx}+k\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)=0,&x\in(0,L),\,\,\,t\in(0,T)\\
EIw_{xxxx}-\alpha k\left(-u+v+\alpha w_{x}\right)_{x}=0,&x\in(0,L),\,\,\,t\in(0,T)\\
u(0,t)=v(0,t)=w_{x}(0,t)=w_{xxx}(0,t)=0,&t\in(0,T)\\
w_{xx}(L,t)=w_{xxx}(L,t)=0,&t\in(0,T)\\
u_{x}(L,t)=0,&t\in(0,T)\\
v_{x}(L,t)=0,&t\in(0,T)\\
-u(L,t)+v(L,t)+\alpha w_{x}(L,t)=0,&t\in(0,T).\end{array}\right.

We infer, from the proof of Lemma
4.1
, the unique solution of (
5.41
) is given by
U=0
, and this
contradicts (
5.37
). Then, necessarily, (
5.31
) holds. This completes the proof of the Theorem.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

From (
4.19
), we directly verify that

(5.42)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\left(\left|u_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|v_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|w_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}\right)dt\leq C\|U_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}.

We are now in a position to prove the main result of this section, namely, the observability inequality that provides a positive answer to the control problem
P_{3}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For any
U_{0}\in D(\mathcal{P})
, let

U=(u,u_{t},v,v_{t},w,w_{t},u_{t}(L,t),v_{t}(L,t),w_{t}(L,t))^{\top}

be the solution of the system (
4.2
). Then, there exists a positive constant
C
such that the following estimate holds:

(5.43)

\displaystyle\|U_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\!\left(\left|u(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|v(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|w(L,t)\right|^{2}\right)dt.

Proof.

In order to prove inequality (
5.43
), we consider the auxiliary problem

(5.44)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{U}_{t}=\mathcal{P}\tilde{U},\\[3.00003pt]
\tilde{U}(0)=\tilde{U}_{0},\end{array}\right.

where
\tilde{U}_{0}=\mathcal{P}^{-1}U_{0}
(the existence of
\mathcal{P}^{-1}
is guaranteed by Lemma
4.1
). Thus, from Proposition
5.2
, the solution

\tilde{U}=(\tilde{u},\tilde{u}_{t},\tilde{v},\tilde{v}_{t},\tilde{w},\tilde{w}_{t},\tilde{u}_{t}(L,t),\tilde{v}_{t}(L,t),\tilde{w}_{t}(L,t))^{\top}

of system (
5.44
) satisfies

(5.45)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{U}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\!\left(\left|\tilde{u}_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|\tilde{v}_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|\tilde{w}_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}\right)dt,

for some constant
C>0
.

On the other hand, it is straightforward to see that
\tilde{U}_{t}=U
. Hence,

(5.46)

\displaystyle(\tilde{u}_{t},\tilde{v}_{t},\tilde{w}_{t})=(u,v,w).

Finally, combining (
5.45
), (
5.46
), and applying the Riesz representation theorem, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|U_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\tilde{U}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\!\left(\left|\tilde{u}_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|\tilde{v}_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|\tilde{w}_{t}(L,t)\right|^{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{T}\!\left(\left|u(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|v(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|w(L,t)\right|^{2}\right)dt,

and the proof is complete.
∎

A direct consequence of the previous theorem is the following.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let

W=(\chi,\chi_{t},\Theta,\Theta_{t},\eta,\eta_{t},\chi_{t}(L,t),\eta_{t}(L,t),\Theta_{t}(L,t))^{\top}

be the solution of the adjoint system (
5.19
). Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that the following observability inequality holds:

(5.47)

\|W(0)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\!\left(\left|\chi(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|\eta(L,t)\right|^{2}+\left|\Theta(L,t)\right|^{2}\right)dt.

The observability inequality established in Corollary
5.1
, combined with the HUM method from control theory, allows us to obtain the main result of this paper: the controllability of the system stated in Theorem
1.2
.

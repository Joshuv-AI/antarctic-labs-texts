Inf-sup stable space–time discretization of the wave equation based on a first-order-in-time variational formulation


Abstract
In this paper, we present a conforming space–time discretization of the wave equation based on a first-order-in-time variational formulation with exponential weights in time. We analyze the method, showing its stability without imposing any restrictions on the mesh size or time step, and proving quasi-optimal convergence for any choice of space–time tensor product discrete spaces that satisfies standard approximation assumptions. Numerical examples are provided to support the theoretical findings.

In several of the above references, discrete space–time tensor product spaces are employed. As this brief overview shows, regularity assumptions in space or time are often required, and unconditional stability (or convergence) is often proven for a particular type of time-discrete spaces (e.g. polynomials or maximal regularity splines).

In this work, we consider conforming first-order-in-time space–time methods with exponential weights in time, where the discrete tensor product spaces are kept general, and both stability and convergence are guaranteed under standard approximation assumptions. The core idea is to exploit the exponential weights similarly to
[
29
]
, and establish an inf-sup condition for the continuous variational formulation in suitable norms,
under the assumption that the test functions are time derivatives of the trial functions. A slightly stronger solution regularity than the minimal one is required, as, e.g., in
[

19

15

[

[

and then in
[
15

The stability analysis of the
discrete

The analysis we propose shares several ideas with those in
[

[

In contrast, the difference with respect to the method in
[
15

, which also employs exponential weights but within a second-order-in-time formulation, lies in the derivation of quasi-optimal error estimates for more general choices of the discrete spaces in time. While the method in
[

As a final note, we emphasize that our inf-sup analysis makes use of a Newton potential and discrete Newton potential operators, similarly to the inf-sup analysis of space–time methods for parabolic problems developed in

2
Model problem

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz, bounded polytopal domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=1,2,3
),
T
a positive final time, and
Q_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)
the space–time cylinder.

Given initial data
U_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
V_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and a forcing term
F\in L^{2}(Q_{T})
, consider the following problem: find
U:\overline{Q}_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\cdot(c^{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\boldsymbol{x},t))=F(\boldsymbol{x},t)&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in Q_{T},\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},t)=0&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T],\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},0)=U_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad\partial_{t}U(\boldsymbol{x},0)=V_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})&\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(1)

Here,
c=c(\boldsymbol{x})
is the wave velocity, for which we assume, for a constant
c_{0}
,

c\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad c(\boldsymbol{x})\geq c_{0}>0\quad\text{for almost every~}\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega.

(2)

A well-posed variational formulation of problem (
1
) reads: find

U\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

(3)

such that
U_{|_{t=0}}=U_{0}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
\partial_{t}U_{|_{t=0}}=V_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and, for almost every
t\in(0,T)
,

\langle\partial_{t}^{2}U(\cdot,t),W\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\cdot,t),\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}W)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(F(\cdot,t),W)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(4)

for all
W\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
is the duality product between
[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
; see, e.g.,
[
23
, Chapter 3. §8 Pag. 265]
.

Introducing the new unknown
V:=\partial_{t}U
, we rewrite problem (
1
) as the first-order-in-time system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}V(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\cdot(c^{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\boldsymbol{x},t))=F(\boldsymbol{x},t)&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in Q_{T},\\
\partial_{t}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)-V(\boldsymbol{x},t)=0&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in Q_{T},\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},t)=0&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},0)=U_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad V(\boldsymbol{x},0)=V_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})&\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(5)

If
U
satisfies the regularity in (
3
), then

V\in L^{2}(Q_{T})\cap H^{1}(0,T;[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{\prime}).

For the purposes of the scheme we propose, we restrict our setting to a more regular framework and make the following assumption.

Assumption 2.1
.

Let the wave velocity
c
be as in (
2
),
U_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and

F\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad V_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad F(\cdot,0)\in L^{2}(\Omega),\quad\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\cdot(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U_{0})\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Under Assumption
2.1
, one can establish improved regularity for the unique solution to (
4
); see, e.g.,
[
1
, Prop. 4.4]
,
[
19
, Lemma 2.7]
, and
[
10
, Chapter 7.2]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, there exists a unique solution to (
4
) and it satisfies

U\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),\quad\partial_{t}U\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),\quad\partial_{t}^{2}U\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)).

Setting
V=\partial_{t}U
, this in particular implies

U\in H^{1}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),\quad V\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)).

3
Space–time variational formulation

In this section, we introduce and analyze a variational formulation of (
5
). We establish an inf-sup condition in a way that extends straightforwardly to the discrete setting.

We set
H_{0,\bullet}^{1}(0,T):=\{u\in H^{1}(0,T):u(0)=0\},
and consider the following variational formulation: find
U\in H^{1}_{0,\bullet}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
and
V\in H^{1}_{0,\bullet}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
such that

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}V,\lambda)_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}+(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\lambda)_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}=(F,\lambda)_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}-(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U_{0},\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\lambda)_{L^{2}(Q_{T})},\\
(\partial_{t}U,\chi)_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}-(V,\chi)_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}=(V_{0},\chi)_{L^{2}(Q_{T})},\end{cases}

(6)

for all
\lambda\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
and
\chi\in L^{2}(Q_{T})
. If
(U,V)
is a solution to (
5
), then
(U-U_{0},\,V-V_{0})
satisfies (
6
).

We now introduce a modified variational formulation that incorporates weighted scalar products in time, and we study the properties of associated bilinear form.

3.1
Weighted space–time bilinear form and its properties

For functions depending solely on time, we define the weighted scalar product in
L^{2}(0,T)
and associated norm

(u,v)_{L^{2}_{e}(0,T)}:=\int_{0}^{T}u(s)v(s)e^{-s/T}\differential s,\quad\quad\quad\|u\|_{L^{2}_{e}(0,T)}^{2}:=(u,u)_{L^{2}_{e}(0,T)}.

(7)

The norm defined in (
7
) is equivalent to the classical
L^{2}
norm.
The important property of this scalar product is that, for all
u,v\in H^{1}(0,T)
,

(u,\partial_{t}v)_{L^{2}_{e}(0,T)}=-(\partial_{t}u,v)_{L^{2}_{e}(0,T)}+\frac{1}{T}(u,v)_{L^{2}_{e}(0,T)}+\frac{1}{e}u(T)v(T)-u(0)v(0),

from which it follows that

(w,\partial_{t}w)_{L^{2}_{e}(0,T)}=\frac{1}{2T}\|w\|_{L^{2}_{e}(0,T)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2e}|w(T)|^{2}\qquad\text{for all~}w\in H_{0,\bullet}^{1}(0,T).

(8)

Then, for space–time functions, we denote by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}:L^{2}(Q_{T})\times L^{2}(Q_{T})\to\mathbb{R}
the weighted scalar product

(\chi,\lambda)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}:=\int_{0}^{T}(\chi(\cdot,s),\lambda(\cdot,s))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,e^{-s/T}\,\differential s,

and by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{e}:L^{2}(0,T;[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime})\times L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\to\mathbb{R}
the duality pairing

\langle\chi,\lambda\rangle_{e}:=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\chi(\cdot,s),\lambda(\cdot,s)\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\,e^{-s/T}\,\differential s.

We define the space–time bilinear form
\mathcal{A}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((U,V),(\lambda,\chi)):=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}V,\lambda\rangle_{e}+(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\lambda)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}

(9)

\displaystyle-(\partial_{t}U,\chi)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}+(V,\chi)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}.

The
minimal
regularity required for
\mathcal{A}
to be well-defined is

\displaystyle(U,V)

\displaystyle\in\bigl(L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\bigr)\times\bigl(L^{2}(Q_{T})\cap H^{1}(0,T;[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{\prime})\bigr),

(10)

\displaystyle(\lambda,\chi)

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\times L^{2}(Q_{T}).

As in the analysis of the space–time parabolic problem in
[
26
]
, we introduce the
Newton potential
operator
\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}:[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\to H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
defined by

(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}V)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle U,V\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all~}V\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(11)

From Lax-Milgram’s lemma,
\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}
is well-defined. Moreover,
\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}
is an invertible operator and induces norm in
[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
, which is equivalent to the standard one:

\|U\|_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}}^{2}:=\langle U,\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U\rangle_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}=(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(12)

We have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
U\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,

\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C_{\Omega}}{c_{0}}\|U\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
C_{\Omega}>0
is the constant in the Poincaré inequality

\|V\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega}\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}V\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\text{for all }\,V\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

and
c_{0}
the lower bound of
c
in (
2
).

Proof.

Let us endow
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with the norm
\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\langle U,\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq\|U\|_{[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

and therefore

\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{c_{0}}\|U\|_{[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}.

The result follows from

\|U\|_{[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}:=\sup_{0\neq V\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{\langle U,V\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}}{\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}V\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}=\sup_{0\neq V\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{(U,V)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}V\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\leq C_{\Omega}\|U\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where, in the last step, we applied the Cauchy-Schwarz and Poincaré inequalities.
∎

Consider now functions depending on space and time. For
U\in L^{2}(0,T;[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{\prime})
, we use the notation
\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
to also denote the function defined via

(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}V)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}=\int_{0}^{T}\langle U(\cdot,s),V(\cdot,s)\rangle_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\,e^{-s/T}\differential s

for all
V\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
. Moreover, we define for
U\in L^{2}(0,T;[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{\prime})
the norm

\displaystyle\|U\|_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}_{e}}^{2}:=\int_{0}^{T}\|U(\cdot,s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}}\,e^{-s/T}\,\differential s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U(\cdot,s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,e^{-s/T}\,\differential s

(13)

\displaystyle=\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}^{2}.

Using Lemma
3.1
, we prove the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
U\in L^{2}(Q_{T})
and
V\in L^{2}(0,T;[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{\prime})
, we have

\|U\|_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}_{e}}\leq\frac{C_{\Omega}}{c_{0}}\|U\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}\,,\quad\|\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}V\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}\leq\frac{C_{\Omega}}{c_{0}}\|V\|_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}_{e}},

where
C_{\Omega}>0
is the constant in the Poincaré inequality, and
c_{0}
the constant in (
2
).

Proof.

For all
t\in(0,T)
, if
U(\cdot,t)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, then
\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U(\cdot,t)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then, using Lemma
3.1
we derive the first bound:

\displaystyle\|U\|^{2}_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}_{e}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle U(\cdot,t),\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U(\cdot,t)\rangle_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\,e^{-t/T}\,\differential t=\int_{0}^{T}\|c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}U(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,e^{-t/T}\,\differential t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\Omega}^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\|U(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,e^{-t/T}\,\differential t=\frac{C_{\Omega}^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}\|U\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}.

For the second bound, if
V(\cdot,t)\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
, the Poincaré inequality implies

\|\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}V(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega}\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}V(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

from which we conclude

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}V\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}V(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,e^{-t/T}\,\differential t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\Omega}^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}V(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,e^{-t/T}\,\differential t

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\Omega}^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\langle V(\cdot,t),\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}V(\cdot,t)\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\,e^{-t/T}\,\differential t=\frac{C_{\Omega}^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}\|V\|_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}_{e}}^{2}.

∎

For
(U,V)
and
(\lambda,\chi)
with regularity as in (
10

\displaystyle\|(U,V)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{e}(Q_{T})}

\displaystyle:=\|\partial_{t}U\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}V\|^{2}_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}_{e}}+\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}+\|V\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})},

(14)

\displaystyle\|(\lambda,\chi)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}_{e}(Q_{T})}

\displaystyle:=\|\lambda\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}+\|\chi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}_{e}}.

For functions with additional regularity, the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}
defined in (

Proposition 3.3

(Inf-sup in the norms in (

.

For any

(U,V)\in H^{1}_{0,\bullet}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\times H^{1}_{0,\bullet}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

(15)

there exists

(\lambda,\chi)\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\times L^{2}(Q_{T})

\frac{\mathcal{A}((U,V),(\lambda,\chi))}{\|(U,V)\|_{\mathcal{V}_{e}(Q_{T})}\|(\lambda,\chi)\|_{\mathcal{W}_{e}(Q_{T})}}\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{C_{\Omega}^{2}/c_{0}^{2}+4T^{2}}},

where

the constant in (

Proof.

We prove that, for any

with regularity as in (

\displaystyle\|(\lambda,\chi)\|_{\mathcal{W}_{e}(Q_{T})}

\displaystyle\leq(C_{\Omega}^{2}/c_{0}^{2}+4T^{2})\|(U,V)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{e}(Q_{T})}\,,

which proves (

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((U,V),(\lambda,\chi))

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{t}V\|_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}_{e}}^{2}+2T(\partial_{t}V,\partial_{t}U)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}+(U,\partial_{t}V)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}

\displaystyle-2T(\partial_{t}U,\partial_{t}V)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}-(V,\partial_{t}U)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}+2T(V,\partial_{t}V)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{t}U\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}V\|_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}_{e}}^{2}+\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{T}{e}\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|V\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{T}{e}\|V(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(U,\partial_{t}V)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}-(V,\partial_{t}U)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}.

Recalling the definition of
\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}
in (

) and using the Young inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle(U,\partial_{t}V)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}(U(\cdot,t),\partial_{t}V(\cdot,t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,e^{-t/T}\,\differential t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\cdot,t),\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}\partial_{t}V(\cdot,t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,e^{-t/T}\,\differential t

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2}\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}-\frac{1}{2}\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}\partial_{t}V\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}-\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}V\|^{2}_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}_{e}}.

From this and
(V,\partial_{t}U)_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}\geq-\frac{1}{2}\|V\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}-\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}U\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}
,
we continue (

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((U,V),(\lambda,\chi))

\displaystyle\geq\|\partial_{t}U\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}V\|_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}_{e}}^{2}+\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}^{2}\ +\|V\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}V\|_{\mathcal{N}^{\Omega}_{e}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|V\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}U\|_{L^{2}_{e}(Q_{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|(U,V)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{e}(Q_{T})}.

This leads to (

. Thus, taking into account that we have proven (

, we obtain (

5
Conclusion

In this work, we introduced a conforming space–time tensor product discretization method for the wave equation based on a first-order-in-time variational formulation with exponential weights. The proposed method achieves unconditional stability and quasi-optimal convergence for arbitrary choices of discrete spatial and temporal spaces, under standard approximation assumptions. Numerical tests validate the theoretical results. The key idea is to develop a proof of the inf-sup condition at the continuous level that can be extended directly to the discrete case. This approach allows us to prove well-posedness and convergence with considerable flexibility in the choice of discrete spaces. As in the second-order-in-time formulation of
[
15
]
, exponential weights in time play a fundamental role in the theoretical analysis, particularly in establishing the inf-sup condition. However, from a practical point of view, they may be unnecessary. For instance, for the first-order-in-time formulation, they are proven not to be needed in the case of continuous piecewise polynomial approximations in time
[
18
,
20
]
, or when using maximal regularity splines in time
[
13
]
. Whether this is true in general remains an open question.

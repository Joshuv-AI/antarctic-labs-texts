Bohr type inequality for certain integral operators and Fourier transform on shifted disks
Vasudevarao Allu ∗1 , Raju Biswas 2 and Rajib Mandal 2

Abstract
. In this paper, we derive the sharp Bohr type inequality for the Cesáro operator, Bernardi integral operator, and discrete Fourier transform acting on the class of bounded analytic functions defined on shifted disks Ω γ = { z ∈ ℂ : | z + γ 1 − γ | < 1 1 − γ } for γ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) . \displaystyle\Omega_{\gamma}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right|<\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right\}\quad\text{for}\quad\gamma\in[0,1).

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{D}_{\rho}(a)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a|<\rho\}
be an open disk center at
a
with radius
\rho
and let
\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{D}_{1}(0)
be the open unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
\Omega
be a simply connected domain such that
\mathbb{D}\subseteq\Omega
and
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
denotes the class of analytic functions on
\Omega
. Let
\mathcal{B}(\Omega)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega):f(\Omega)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}}\right\}
, and the Bohr radius (see
[
17
]
) for the class
\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
is defined to be the number
B_{\Omega}\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle B_{\Omega}=\sup\left\{\rho\in(0,1):M_{f}(\rho)\leq 1\;\text{for}\;f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega),z\in\mathbb{D}\right\},

where
M_{f}(\rho)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\rho^{n}
is the majorant series associated with the analytic functions
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
in
\mathbb{D}
.
It is well known that
B_{\mathbb{D}}=1/3
if
\Omega=\mathbb{D}
. This is described in the following manner:

Theorem A
.

(The Classical Bohr Theorem) If
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
, then
M_{f}(\rho)\leq 1
for
0\leq\rho\leq 1/3
. The number
1/3
is best possible.

Note that, the inequality
M_{f}(\rho)\leq 1
, where
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
, fails to hold for
\rho>1/3
.
For
\varphi_{r}(z)=(r-z)/(1-rz)
, it is easy to see that
M_{\varphi_{r}}(\rho)>1
if, and only if,
\rho>1/(1+2r)
. This shows that
1/3
is optimal for
r\to 1
.

Theorem A was actually obtained by H. Bohr in 1914 (see
[
9
]
) for
\rho\leq 1/6
, but later Weiner, Riesz, and Schur
[
15
]
have independently improved
this result to a value of
1/3
.
This result has also been established by Sidon
[
44
]
and Tomić
[
46
]
. Over the past two decades, a considerable amount of research has been conducted on Bohr type
inequalities. For an in-depth investigation on Bohr radius and Bohr inequality, see
[

,

,

,

,

]
and the references therein.
Boas and Khavinson
[
13

further developed the concept of the Bohr radius, especially for several complex variables, and introduced the concept of the multidimensional Bohr
radius. We refer to
[
1

]
for an in-depth study on multidimensional Bohr radius.

For
\gamma\in[0,1)
, we consider the open disk
\Omega_{\gamma}
defined by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\gamma}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right|<\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right\}.

(1.1)

Note that the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
is always a subset of
\Omega_{\gamma}
.

.

Besides the Bohr radius, there is another concept known as the Rogosinski radius
[
30
,
42
]
. It is described as follows:
Let
S_{N}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}a_{n}z^{n}
be the partial sum of
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
defined by
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
. Then,
|S_{N}(z)|<1
for all

N\geq 1
in the disk
|z|<1/2
. Here
1/2
is sharp, known as Rogosinski radius.
Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
20
]
have introduced the Bohr-Rogosinski sum
R_{N}^{f}(z)
, which is motivated by the Rogosinski radius. It is defined as follows:

\displaystyle R_{N}^{f}(z):=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}.

It is evident that
|S_{N}(z)|=\left|f(z)-\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\right|\leq R_{N}^{f}(z)
.
Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
20
]
defined the Bohr-Rogosinski radius as the largest number
r\in(0,1)
such that
R_{N}^{f}(z)\leq 1
for
|z|<r
.
We refer to
[
19
,
22
,
27
]
and the references therein for an in-depth study of the Bohr-Rogosinski radius.

The following question is expected in such a context.

Question 1.1
.

Is it possible to extend the Bohr type inequality for certain complex integral operators and integral transforms that are defined on different function spaces?

The idea was initially proposed in the context of unit disk
\mathbb{D}
for the classical Cesáro operator by Kayumov
et al.

[
26
,
27
,
28
]
and for the Bernardi integral operator by Kumar and Sahoo
[
29
]
.
The objective of establishing the Bohr type and Bohr-Rogosinski type inequalities for the Bernardi integral operator, Cesáro operator, and its various generalizations on the family
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
has been the subject of research. Additionally, extensive research has been conducted regarding the boundedness and compactness of the Cesáro operator in different function spaces. In the classical setting, for an analytic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the Cesáro operator
[
14
,
18
,
45
]
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}f(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(tz)}{1-tz}dt.

(1.2)

It is not difficult to show that for
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}f(z)|=\left|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)z^{n}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\rho}\log\frac{1}{1-\rho}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=\rho.

For the Cesáro operator, Kayumov
et al.

[
26
]
established the following Bohr type inequality.

Theorem B
.

[
26
, Theorem 1, p. 616]
If
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{f}(\rho)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)\rho^{n}\leq\frac{1}{\rho}\log\frac{1}{1-\rho}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=\rho\leq R,

where
R=0.5335...
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle 2x=3(1-x)\log\frac{1}{1-x}.

The number
R
is the best possible.

For an analytic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the Bernardi integral operator
[
32
, p. 11]
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\beta}f(z):=\frac{1+\beta}{z^{\beta}}\int_{0}^{z}f(\eta)\eta^{\beta-1}d\eta=(1+\beta)\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{\beta+n}z^{n},

(1.3)

where
\beta>-m
and
m\geq 0
is an integer. From (
1.3
), it is worth to mentioning that for
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
with
|z|=\rho\in[0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{\beta}f(z)|=\left|(1+\beta)\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{\beta+n}z^{n}\right|\leq(1+\beta)\frac{\rho^{m}}{m+\beta},

which is equivalent to the following inequality

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{\beta+n}z^{n}\right|\leq\frac{\rho^{m}}{m+\beta}.

In 2021, Kumar and Sahoo
[
29
]
established the following Bohr type inequality with regard to the Bernardi integral operator.

Theorem C
.

[
29
]
Let
\beta>-m
. If
f(z)=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{\beta+n}\rho^{n}\leq\frac{\rho^{m}}{m+\beta}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=\rho\leq R(\beta),

where
R(\beta)
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{x^{m}}{m+\beta}-2\sum_{n=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n+\beta}=0.

The number
R(\beta)
cannot be improved.

In 2023, Allu and Ghosh
[
9
]
obtained the following sharp Bohr type inequality for the Cesáro operator and Bernardi integral operator for functions in the class
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
.

Theorem D
.

[
9
]

For
0\leq\gamma<1
, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{f}(\rho)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)\rho^{n}\leq\frac{1}{\rho}\log\frac{1}{1-\rho}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=\rho\leq R_{\gamma},

where
R_{\gamma}
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle(3+\gamma)(1-x)\log\frac{1}{1-x}=2x.

The number
R_{\gamma}
is the best possible.

Theorem E
.

[
9
]

For
0\leq\gamma<1
, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then for
\beta>0

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{n+\beta}\rho^{n}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=\rho\leq R_{\gamma,\beta},

where
R_{\gamma,\beta}
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n}}{n+\beta}.

The number
R_{\gamma,\beta}
is the best possible.

It is observed that the Bohr type inequality presented in Theorems D and E imposes a restriction on analytic functions defined on the simply connected domain

\Omega_{\gamma}
to the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.

Let
\left\{x_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
be a sequence of complex numbers. The discrete Fourier transform (see
[
11
, Chapter 6]
) is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(x_{n})=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x_{n}e^{-2\pi ink/N}.

For
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
, we perform the discrete Fourier transform on the coefficients
a_{k}
from
k=0
to
n
, which gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[f](z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}e^{-2\pi ink/(n+1)}\right)z^{n}.

To obtain a Bohr type inequality in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, we denote the majorant series of
\mathcal{F}[f](z)
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{f}(\rho):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)\rho^{n},\quad\text{where}\quad|z|=\rho<1.

For functions in the class
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
, in 2024, Ong and Ng
[
33
]
derived the following Bohr type inequality with regard to the discrete Fourier transform.

Theorem F
.

[
33
]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{f}(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{1-\rho}\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\leq\frac{1}{3}.

The constant
1/3
cannot be improved.

2.
Main results

Motivated by Fournier and Ruscheweyh
[
17
]
, in this paper, we define the Bohr radius for functions in the class
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
. It is defined to be the
number

R_{\Omega_{\gamma}}\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle R_{\Omega_{\gamma}}=\sup\left\{\rho\in(0,1):M_{f}(\rho)\leq 1\;\text{for}\;f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma}),z\in\Omega_{\gamma}\right\},

where
M_{f}(\rho)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|\left(\rho/(1-\gamma)\right)^{n}
with
|\gamma+(1-\gamma)z|=\rho
, is the majorant series associated with the analytic function
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
. It is well known that
R_{\mathbb{D}}=1/3
if
\Omega_{\gamma}=\mathbb{D}
.

The objective of this paper is to derive sharply Bohr type inequality for the Cesáro operator, Bernardi integral operator, and discrete Fourier transform for functions in the class

\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
without imposing the restriction to the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.

The following are key lemmas of this paper and will be used for the proof of the main results.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
43
]
For
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left|f^{(n)}(\alpha)\right|}{n!}\leq\frac{1-|f(\alpha)|^{2}}{(1-|\alpha|)^{n-1}(1-|\alpha|^{2})}\;\;\text{for each}\;\;n\geq 1\;\;\text{and}\;\;\alpha\in\mathbb{D}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f
be analytic in
\Omega_{\gamma}
, bounded by
1
with the series expansion
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
. Then
|a_{n}|\leq(1-\gamma)^{n}(1-|a_{0}|^{2})
for
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega_{\gamma}
be a function defined by
\Phi(z)=(z-\gamma)/(1-\gamma)
. Since
f:\Omega_{\gamma}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
,
the composition
g=f\circ\Phi
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
. Thus

\displaystyle g(z)=f\left(\frac{z-\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}z^{n}

with

\displaystyle g(0)=a_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad a_{n}=\frac{g^{(n)}(0)}{n!}(1-\gamma)^{n}.

In view of Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle|a_{n}|\leq(1-\gamma)^{n}\left(1-|g(0)|^{2}\right)=(1-\gamma)^{n}\left(1-|a_{0}|^{2}\right).

∎

In the following, we derive a sharp Bohr type inequality for the Cesáro operator applied to functions belonging to the class
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
of bounded analytic functions.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
0\leq\gamma<1
, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{f}(\rho)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{|a_{k}|}{(1-\gamma)^{k}}\right)\rho^{n}\leq\frac{1}{\rho}\log\frac{1}{1-\rho}\;\;\text{for}\;|\gamma+(1-\gamma)z|=\rho\leq\rho_{0},

where
\rho_{0}(\approx 0.533589)
is the positive root of the equation
3(1-\rho)\log(1-\rho)+2\rho=0
. The number
\rho_{0}
is the best possible.

Remark 2.1
.

Since
\Omega_{\gamma}=\mathbb{D}
for
\gamma=0
, it follows that Theorem A is a direct consequence of
Theorem
2.1
when
\gamma=0
.

In the following, we derive a sharp Bohr type inequality for the Bernardi integral operator applied to functions belonging to the class
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
of bounded analytic functions.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
0\leq\gamma<1
, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
. Then for
\beta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{(n+\beta)(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\quad\text{for}\quad|\gamma+(1-\gamma)z|=\rho\leq\rho_{1},

where
\rho_{1}
is the positive root of the equation
1/\beta-2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\rho^{n}/(n+\beta)\right)=0
. The number
\rho_{1}
is the best possible.

In the following, we derive a sharp Bohr type inequality the discrete Fourier transform applied to functions belonging to the class
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
of bounded analytic functions.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
0\leq\gamma<1
, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{|a_{k}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\right)\rho^{n}\leq\frac{1}{1-\rho}\quad\text{for}\quad|\gamma+(1-\gamma)z|=\rho\leq\frac{1}{3},

The number
1/3
is the best possible.

Remark 2.2
.

Since for
\gamma=0
, the domain
\Omega_{\gamma}
reduces to the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, it follows that Theorem F is a direct consequence of
Theorem
2.3
when
\gamma=0
.

3.
proofs of the main results

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
f(z)
be analytic on
\Omega_{\gamma}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
. In view of Lemma
2.2
, we have
|a_{n}|\leq(1-\gamma)^{n}(1-|a_{0}|^{2})
for
n\geq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{f}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{|a_{k}|}{(1-\gamma)^{k}}\right)\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a_{0}|\left(1+\frac{\rho}{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{3}+\cdots\right)+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|a_{k}|}{(1-\gamma)^{k}}\right)\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{|a_{0}|}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|a_{k}|}{(1-\gamma)^{k}}\right)\rho^{n}.

Let
|a_{0}|=a\in[0,1]
. In view of Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{f}(\rho)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{a}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(1-a^{2}\right)\right)\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{a}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)+\left(1-a^{2}\right)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n}{n+1}\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{a}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)+\left(1-a^{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{1-\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(a,\rho).

Differentiating partially
\varphi_{1}(a,\rho)
with respect to
a
twice, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\varphi_{1}(a,\rho)=-\frac{\log(1-\rho)}{\rho}-2a\left(\frac{1}{1-\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)\right)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial a^{2}}\varphi_{1}(a,\rho)=-2\left(\frac{1}{1-\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)\right)\leq 0\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\in[0,1)

and it’s shown in Figure
2
.

Figure 2.
The graph of
-2\left(1/(1-\rho)+\log(1-\rho)/\rho\right)
for
\rho\in[0,1)

Therefore,
\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\varphi_{1}(a,\rho)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
a\in[0,1]
and it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\varphi_{1}(a,\rho)\geq\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\varphi_{1}(1,\rho)=\frac{-2\rho-3(1-\rho)\log(1-\rho)}{(1-\rho)\rho}\geq 0\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\leq\rho_{0},

where
\rho_{0}(\approx 0.533589)
is the positive root of the equation
3(1-\rho)\log(1-\rho)+2\rho=0
, as illustrated in Figure
3
.

Figure 3.
The graph of
3(1-\rho)\log(1-\rho)+2\rho
for
\rho\in[0,1)

Therefore
\varphi_{1}(a,\rho)
is a monotonically increasing function of
a\in[0,1]
and it follows that

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(a,\rho)\leq\varphi_{1}(1,\rho)=-\frac{1}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)=\frac{1}{\rho}\log\frac{1}{(1-\rho)}\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\leq\rho_{0}.

To prove the sharpness of the result, we consider the function
f_{1}(z)
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
such that
f_{1}=\psi\circ\Phi_{1}
, where
\Phi_{1}:\Omega_{\gamma}\to\mathbb{D}
defined by
\Phi_{1}(z)=\gamma+(1-\gamma)z
and
\psi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
defined by
\psi(z)=(a-z)/(1-az)
for
a\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)=\frac{a-(1-\gamma)\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)}{1-a(1-\gamma)\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)}=A_{0}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}\;\text{for}\;z\in\Omega_{\gamma},

(3.1)

where
A_{0}=a
and
A_{n}=a^{n-1}(1-a^{2})(1-\gamma)^{n}
for
n\geq 1
.
The Cesáro operator on
f_{1}
shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{f_{1}}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{|A_{k}|}{(1-\gamma)^{k}}\right)\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{a}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a^{k-1}(1-a^{2})\right)\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)-\frac{2a}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)+\frac{(1+a)}{a\rho}\log(1-a\rho).

As
2/(1-\rho)+3\log(1-\rho)/\rho>0
for
\rho>\rho_{0}
, we can write
\mathcal{C}_{f_{1}}(\rho)
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{f_{1}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)+(1-a)\left(\frac{2}{1-\rho}+\frac{3}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)\right)-(3-a)\frac{1}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)

(3.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{2(1-a)}{1-\rho}+\frac{(1+a)}{a\rho}\log(1-a\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)+(1-a)\left(\frac{2}{1-\rho}+\frac{3}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)\right)+G_{1}(a,\rho),

where

\displaystyle G_{1}(a,\rho)=-(3-a)\frac{1}{\rho}\log(1-\rho)-\frac{2(1-a)}{1-\rho}+\frac{(1+a)}{a\rho}\log(1-a\rho).

In order to express
G_{1}(a,\rho)
in its summation form, we have

\displaystyle G_{1}(a,\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(3-a)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n-1}}{n}-2(1-a)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho^{n-1}-(1+a)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(a\rho)^{n-1}}{n}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{3-a}{n}-2(1-a)-(1+a)\frac{a^{n-1}}{n}\right)\rho^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left((1-a)^{2}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad a\to 1^{-}.

From (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we conclude that
\rho_{0}
cannot be improved. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
f(z)
be analytic on
\Omega_{\gamma}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
. Let
|a_{0}|=a\in[0,1]
. In view of Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{(n+\beta)(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{a}{\beta}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-a^{2})}{(n+\beta)}\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{\beta}+(1-a^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n}}{(n+\beta)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(a,\rho).

Differentiating partially
\varphi_{2}(a,\rho)
with respect to
a
twice, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\varphi_{2}(a,\rho)=\frac{1}{\beta}-2a\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n}}{(n+\beta)}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial a^{2}}\varphi_{2}(a,\rho)=-2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n}}{(n+\beta)}\leq 0

for
\rho\in[0,1)
.
Therefore,
\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\varphi_{2}(a,\rho)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
a
and this gives that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\varphi_{2}(a,\rho)\geq\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\varphi_{2}(1,\rho)=\frac{1}{\beta}-2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n}}{(n+\beta)}\geq 0\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\leq\rho_{1},

where
\rho_{1}
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}-2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n}}{(n+\beta)}=0.

Thus, for all
a\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{(n+\beta)(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}\leq\varphi_{2}(a,\rho)\leq\varphi_{2}(1,\rho)=\frac{1}{\beta}\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\leq\rho_{1}.

To prove the sharpness of the result, we consider the function
f_{2}(z)
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
such that
f_{2}=\psi\circ\Phi_{2}
, where
\Phi_{2}:\Omega_{\gamma}\to\mathbb{D}
defined by
\Phi_{2}(z)=\gamma+(1-\gamma)z
and
\psi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
defined by
\psi(z)=(a-z)/(1-az)
for
a\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle f_{2}(z)=A_{0}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}\quad\text{for}\quad a\in(0,1),\gamma\in[0,1)\quad\text{and}\quad z\in\Omega_{\gamma},

where
A_{0}=a
and
A_{n}=a^{n-1}(1-a^{2})(1-\gamma)^{n}
for
n\geq 1
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|A_{n}|}{(n+\beta)(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{\beta}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|A_{n}|}{(n+\beta)(1-\gamma)^{n}}\rho^{n}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}+(1-a)\left(2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n}}{(n+\beta)}-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)+G_{2}(a,\rho),

where

\displaystyle G_{2}(a,\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2(1-a)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n}}{(n+\beta)}+(1-a^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a^{n-1}}{(n+\beta)}\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-a^{2})a^{n-1}-2(1-a)}{(n+\beta)}\rho^{n}=O((1-a)^{2})\quad\text{as}\quad a\to 1^{-}.

Further, the quantity

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n}}{(n+\beta)}-\frac{1}{\beta}>0\quad\text{for}\quad\rho>\rho_{1}.

From (
3.4
), we conclude that
\rho_{1}
cannot be improved. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
f(z)
be analytic on
\Omega_{\gamma}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
. Let
|a_{0}|=a\in[0,1]
. In view of Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{|a_{k}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\right)\rho^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a\rho^{n}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|a_{k}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\right)\rho^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{a}{1-\rho}+(1-a^{2})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{1-\rho}+(1-a^{2})\frac{\rho}{(1-\rho)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}(a,\rho).

Differentiating partially
\varphi_{3}(a,\rho)
with respect to
a
twice, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\varphi_{3}(a,\rho)=\frac{1}{1-\rho}-\frac{2a\rho}{(1-\rho)^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial a^{2}}\varphi_{3}(a,\rho)=-\frac{2\rho}{(1-\rho)^{2}}\leq 0\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\in[0,1).

Therefore,
\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\varphi_{3}(a,\rho)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
a
and this gives that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\varphi_{3}(a,\rho)\geq\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\varphi_{3}(1,\rho)=\frac{1}{1-\rho}-\frac{2\rho}{(1-\rho)^{2}}=\frac{1-3\rho}{(1-\rho)^{2}}\geq 0\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\leq 1/3.

Therefore,
\varphi_{3}(a,\rho)
is a monotonically increasing function of
a\in[0,1]
and it follows that

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}(a,\rho)\leq\varphi_{3}(1,\rho)=\frac{1}{1-\rho}\quad\text{for}\quad\rho\leq\frac{1}{3}.

To prove the sharpness of the result, we consider the function
f_{3}(z)
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
such that
f_{3}=\psi\circ\Phi_{3}
, where
\Phi_{3}:\Omega_{\gamma}\to\mathbb{D}
defined by
\Phi_{3}(z)=\gamma+(1-\gamma)z
and
\psi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
defined by
\psi(z)=(a-z)/(1-az)
for
a\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle f_{3}(z)=A_{0}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{n}\quad\text{for}\quad a\in(0,1),\quad\gamma\in[0,1)\quad\text{and}\quad z\in\Omega_{\gamma},

where
A_{0}=a
and
A_{n}=a^{n-1}(1-a^{2})(1-\gamma)^{n}
for
n\geq 1
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{|A_{k}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\right)\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a\rho^{n}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|A_{k}|}{(1-\gamma)^{n}}\right)\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{1-\rho}+(1-a^{2})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a^{k-1}\right)\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{1-\rho}+(1-a^{2})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-a^{n})}{(1-a)}\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\rho}+\frac{G_{3}(a,\rho)}{1-\rho},

where
G_{3}(a,\rho)=2a-(1+a)(1-\rho)/(1-a\rho)
.
Differentiating partially
G_{3}(a,\rho)
with respect to
\rho
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}G_{3}(a,\rho)=\frac{1-a^{2}}{(1-a\rho)^{2}}>0.

Therefore,
G_{3}(a,\rho)
is a strictly increasing function of
\rho\in(0,1)
. Therefore, for
\rho>1/3
, we have

\displaystyle G_{3}(a,\rho)>G_{3}(a,1/3)=\frac{2(1-a)^{2}}{a-3}\quad\text{which tends to $0$ as}\quad a\to 1^{-}.

Hence,
1/(1-\rho)+G_{3}(a,\rho)/(1-\rho)>1/(1-\rho)
for
\rho>1/3
. This shows that
1/3
is the best possible. This completes the proof.
∎

Abstract
. We give a formula to count the number of D 4 D_{4} singularities in a stable frontal perturbation of a corank 2 2 wave front singularity f : ( ℂ 3 , 0 ) → ( ℂ 4 , 0 ) f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{4},0) using Mond’s method of stable perturbations of map germs. For a generic germ of corank 2 2 wave front f : ( ℂ 3 , S ) → ( ℂ 4 , 0 ) f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{3},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{4},0) , the image of a stable deformation f t f_{t} of f f exhibits A k A_{k} singularities with k ≤ 4 k\leq 4 , their transverse intersections and the aforementioned D 4 D_{4} singularities for 0 < | t | ≪ 1 0<|t|\ll 1 . By interpreting the image of f t f_{t} as the discriminant (the image of the critical point set) of a smooth map germ H t : ( ℂ 5 , 0 ) → ( ℂ 4 , 0 ) H_{t}\colon(\mathbb{C}^{5},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{4},0) , we define an algebra whose dimension over ℂ \mathbb{C} is equal to the number of D 4 D_{4} points in the image of f t f_{t} .

1.
Introduction

A wave front is a smooth map germ
f\colon N^{n}\to Z^{n+1}
, with
N
,
Z
complex manifolds, which can be lifted onto a Legendrian immersion
\tilde{f}\colon N\looparrowright PT^{*}Z
via a Legendrian fibration
\pi\colon PT^{*}Z\to Z
, where
PT^{*}Z
is the space of contact elements on
Z
.
For
n=3
, a generic frontal perturbation of
f
(in the sense of Definition
2.9
; see also
[
15
]
) exhibits isolated
D_{4}
singularities near the origin, similar to how quadruple points manifest on a generic perturbation of a holomorphic mapping
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}^{4}
.
Our goal is to provide a formula to count the number of
D_{4}
singularities emerging in this arrangement.

The problem of counting isolated singularities in a stable deformation of a map germ can be traced back to
[
11
]
, where Mond gives a formula to count the number of cross cap singularities in a stable
1
-parameter deformation
(f_{t})
of
f
, which he calls a
generic perturbation
of
f
.
This method inspired a series of articles in the following years
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
16
]
, in which the authors apply Mond’s method of generic perturbations to smooth map germs
G\colon(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, with the aim of finding formulas and relations between the isolated singularities in the discriminant curve of
G
.
Fukui, Nuño-Ballesteros and Saia also define the
c_{\mathbf{i}}(f)
invariant in
[
4
,
5
]
, which counts the number of isolated Thom-Boardman singularities of type
\Sigma^{\mathbf{i}}
for a smooth
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
.
The results regarding discriminant curves of smooth map germs were later extended by Marar, Montaldi and Ruas in
[
9
]
, where they present a general method to count singularities of type
A_{j_{1}}\dots A_{j_{k}}
with
j_{1}+\dots+j_{k}=n
in the discriminant of a corank
1
map germ
G\colon(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
.

Arnold and his colleagues from the Russian school of singularities
[
2
]
proved that every Legendrian submanifold can be written in terms of a smooth function
g\colon\mathbb{C}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
with smooth critical set.
In our setting, this means that every wave front
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
can be written as the discriminant of a corank
1
map germ
G\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n+d},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
with smooth critical set for some
d>0
.
Using this characterisation of wave fronts and the formulas in
[
9
]
, we are capable of counting the number of corank
1
singularities emerging on a generic frontal perturbation of
f
.

The number of isolated singularities of a given type in a frontal perturbation of
f
is not only an analytic invariant, but can also be used to compute other numerical invariants, such as the frontal Milnor number of
f
(see
[
15
]
).
In
[
14
]
, we use the number of isolated singularities of a frontal surface to give an expression for the frontal Minor number of
f
(see also Example
3.1
) and the Milnor numbers of the image curves of singular and transverse double points.
We also related the frontal Milnor number of an analytic plane curve to its image Milnor number in
[
13
]
, using the number of singular points in a generic frontal perturbation.

For
n=3
, we see the emergence of the first corank
2
singularity in the classification of stable wave fronts, namely
D_{4}
, which can be defined as any map germ
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{4},0)
which is
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent to

(1)

f(u,v,w)=(u,vw,2uv+3v^{2}+w^{2},uv^{2}+2v^{3}+2vw^{2}).

This complex singularity splits into two real singularities, which Arnold denotes as

and

These illustrations are reproductions of those found in

In Section 2, we present some background information on germs of wave fronts using the theory of deformations of frontal map germs we developed in
[

In Section 3, we define for a corank

f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{4},0)
given in the form

the algebra

The authors are unaware of any other formula in the literature that counts the number of isolated corank

Combining this algebra with the formulas given in

The idea for this article was initially proposed by professor T. Ohmoto to the first author, asking whether it is possible to give a formula to count the number of

Since the list of corank

2.
Wave fronts

Let
PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be the projectivized cotangent bundle of
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.
If
(z,[\omega])\in PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, we equip
PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
with the contact structure given by the differential form

\alpha=\omega_{1}\,dz^{1}+\dots+\omega_{n+1}\,dz^{n+1}.

We consider the canonical projection
\pi\colon PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
given by
\pi(z,[\omega])=z
, whose fibres are Legendrian submanifolds of
PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
under this contact structure (i.e.
\ker d\pi_{(z,[\omega])}\subseteq\ker\alpha_{(z,[\omega])}
for all
(z,[\omega])\in PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
).
A holomorphic map
F\colon N\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\to PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is
integral
if
F^{*}\alpha=0
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
N\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open subset.
A holomorphic map
f\colon N\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is
frontal
if there exists an integral map
F\colon N\to PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
such that

f=\pi\circ F.

If
F
is an immersion, we say
f
is a
Legendrian map
or
wave front
.
Similarly, a hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is
frontal
(resp. a
wave front
) if there exists a frontal map (resp. wave front)
f\colon N\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
such that
X=f(N)
.

If
F\colon N\to PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is an integral map and
f=\pi\circ F
,

0=F^{*}\alpha=\sum^{n+1}_{i=1}\nu_{i}d(Z_{i}\circ F)=\sum^{n+1}_{i=1}\sum^{n}_{j=1}\nu_{i}\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\,dx^{j}

for some
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n+1}\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to\mathbb{C}
, not all of them zero.
This is the same as claiming that there exists a nowhere vanishing smooth section
\nu\colon N\to T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
with
\pi\circ\nu=f
such that
\nu(df\circ\xi)=0
for all vector fields
\xi
on
N
.

Since
PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is a fibre bundle, we can find for each
(z,[\omega])\in PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
an open neighbourhood
Z\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
of
z
and an open
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}P^{n}
such that
\pi^{-1}(Z)\cong Z\times U
.
Therefore,
F
is contact equivalent to the integral mapping
\tilde{f}(x)=(f(x),[\nu_{x}])
, known as the
Nash lift
of
f
.
In particular, if
\Sigma(f)
is nowhere dense in
N
,
\nu
can be uniquely extended from
N\backslash\Sigma(f)
to
N
, and there is a one-to-one correspondence between
f
and
\tilde{f}
.
Such a frontal map is known as a
proper frontal
map
[
8
]
.

If
f\colon N\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is a proper frontal map with Nash lift
\tilde{f}
, we denote the corank of
\tilde{f}
as
\ic(f)
, and call it the
integral corank
of
f
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a finite set.
A holomorphic multigerm
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
is
frontal
if it has a frontal representative
f\colon N\to Z
.
Given a hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
,
(X,0)
is a
frontal
hypersurface germ if there exists a finite frontal map germ
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
such that
(X,0)=f(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)
.

Similarly, we shall say that
f
is a proper frontal multigerm if it has a representative which is a proper frontal map.

Definition 2.3
.

A frontal map germ
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
is a
germ of wave fronts
if
\tilde{f}
is an immersion.
We also call the image of a germ of wave fronts a
wave front
.

Other authors such as
[
2
]
refer to
f
as a
Legendrian map germ
, and its image as a
front
.

Let
\mathscr{O}_{n}
be the ring of germs of functions on
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
.
Given a smooth map germ
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
, we define the
ramification ideal
of
f
as the ideal
\mathcal{R}(f)\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{n}
generated by the
n\times n
minors of the Jacobian matrix of
f
,

\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\dots&\dfrac{\partial f_{n+1}}{\partial x_{1}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\dfrac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}&\dots&\dfrac{\partial f_{n+1}}{\partial x_{n}}\end{pmatrix}

Proposition 2.4

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a smooth map germ with ramification ideal
\mathcal{R}(f)
.
Then
f
is a frontal map germ if and only if
\mathcal{R}(f)
is a principal ideal.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
f
be a proper frontal map germ,
m_{1},\dots,m_{n+1}\in\mathscr{O}_{n}
the minors of the Jacobian matrix of
f
and assume that
\mathcal{R}(f)=\langle m_{j}\rangle
.
It follows from the proof of Proposition

that the Nash lift of

is Legendrian equivalent to the map germ

given by

\tilde{f}(x)=\left(f(x),\frac{m_{1}(x)}{m_{j}(x)},\dots,\widehat{\frac{m_{j}(x)}{m_{j}(x)}},\dots,\frac{m_{n+1}(x)}{m_{j}(x)}\right),

where

denotes an omitted component.
This means that
\tilde{f}

.

Let

be

If

.

We define the

A

.

Let

3.
Counting corank

Let

The classification of stable wave front singularities

The

Given a corank

J_{F}
vanish.
We shall denote the ideal generated by the
k\times k
minors of a matrix
M
as
I_{k}(M)
.

Let
d\geq 0
. We can choose coordinates in the source and target such that a corank
2
map germ
F\colon(\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
takes the form

(4)

F(u,v,w)=(u,p(u,v,w),q(u,v,w),r(u,v,w)),

with
u\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
v,w\in\mathbb{C}
and
p,q,r\in\mathfrak{m}_{3+d}^{2}
.
In this case, the Jacobian matrix of
f
adopts the form

J_{F}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&1&0&0\\
p_{u_{1}}&p_{u_{2}}&\dots&p_{u_{d}}&p_{v}&p_{w}\\
q_{u_{1}}&q_{u_{2}}&\dots&q_{u_{d}}&q_{v}&q_{w}\\
r_{u_{1}}&r_{u_{2}}&\dots&r_{u_{d}}&r_{v}&r_{w}\end{pmatrix}

Computing the minors by blocks, it is then easy to see that

I_{d+1}(J_{F})=\langle p_{v},p_{w},q_{v},q_{w},r_{v},r_{w}\rangle.

Nevertheless, the terms
r_{v}
and
r_{w}
can be omitted by using that
F
is a wave front:

Lemma 3.2
.

There are
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{O}_{n}
such that

(5)

\displaystyle r_{v}=\alpha p_{v}+\beta q_{v};

\displaystyle r_{w}=\alpha p_{w}+\beta q_{w}

and the matrix

M(u,v,w)=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{v}(u,v,w)&\alpha_{w}(u,v,w)\\
\beta_{v}(u,v,w)&\beta_{w}(u,v,w)\end{pmatrix}

is invertible for all
(u,v,w)
in an open neighbourhood
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
0
.

Proof.

Since
F
is a frontal map germ, we can choose coordinates in the source and target such that
F
verifies the differential equation

(6)

dr=\lambda_{1}\,du_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{d}\,du_{d}+\alpha\,dp+\beta\,dq.

for some
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n},\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{O}_{n}
.
Taking the coefficients with respect to
dv
and
dw
, we see that

(7)

\displaystyle r_{v}=\alpha p_{v}+\beta q_{v};

\displaystyle r_{w}=\alpha p_{w}+\beta q_{w}.

It then follows from
[
15
]
, Lemma 2.17 that the matrix
M(u,v,w)
is invertible in a neighbourhood
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
0
, as claimed.
∎

We will now give an expression to count the number of corank
2
points in the image of
F
that depends only on
f
, using a result from analytic geometry known as the
conservation of multiplicity
principle.
The conservation of multiplicity principle states the following:

Theorem 3.3

(
[
12
]
, Corollary E.6)
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
M
be a Cohen-Macaulay
\mathscr{O}_{n,x}
-module and
\mathscr{M}
a representative of
M
(in the sense of
[
12
]
, §E.4).
Then, there is an open neighbourhood
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
x
such that

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M=\sum_{x\in\supp(\mathscr{M})\cap U}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathscr{M}_{x}

If the module
\mathscr{O}_{3}/I_{2}(J_{f})
is Cohen-Macaulay, then we can apply Theorem
3.3
to count the number of corank
2
points in a stable frontal unfolding
F
using only
\mathscr{O}_{3}/I_{2}(J_{f})
.
We believe this is the case for any
\mathscr{F}
-finite frontal map germ
f
(wave front or otherwise); nonetheless, we have not been able to prove it, so we will instead define a complete intersection module which counts the number of corank
2
points of
F
when
f
is a wave front.

Example 3.4
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{4},0)
be the smooth map germ given by

f(u,v,w)=(u,vw,2u^{n}v+3v^{2}+w^{2},u^{n}v^{2}+2v^{3}+2vw^{2}).

We have
\mathscr{R}(f)=\langle u^{n}v+3v^{2}-w^{2}\rangle
and
\ic(f)=0
, hence
f
is a wave front.
We also have that
I_{2}(J_{f})=(u^{n},v,w)
, so
\mathscr{O}_{3}/I_{2}(J_{f})
is a complete intersection module with dimension
n
.
Therefore, a stable frontal unfolding of
F
has
n
corank
2
points.
In particular, the germ of
F
at those points describes an
D_{4}
singularities.

Let
F\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a germ of wave front with corank
2
: the image of
F
is the discriminant of a smooth, corank
1
map germ
H\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n+2},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
with smooth critical set
[
2
,
15
]
.
We may choose coordinates in the source and target such that

(8)

\displaystyle H(u,s,t,v,w)=(u,s,t,h(u,s,t,v,w));

\displaystyle u\in\mathbb{C}^{n-2},\;s,t,v,w\in\mathbb{C}.

In particular, we note that
H
is an unfolding of a certain function
(v,w)\mapsto h(0,0,0,v,w)
.
This means that
F
has corank
2
at
(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
if and only if there are
s,t\in\mathbb{C}
such that
x=(s,t,u,v,w)
is a critical point of
h
and the Hessian matrix of
h
has corank
2
at
x
:

(9)

h_{v}(x)=h_{w}(x)=h_{vv}(x)=h_{vw}(x)=h_{ww}(x)=0.

Note that if
F
is a stable unfolding of a
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{4},0)
,
h
is in fact the
D_{4}
singularity from Arnold’s classification of simple functions
[
1
]
.
Alternatively, we note that if
H
has corank
2
at a point
x
, then
x
is a
\Sigma^{2,2}
point of
H
in Thom-Boardman notation (see
[
12
]
, §4.7 for details).
Fukui, Nuño-Ballesteros and Saia
[
5
]
showed that the locus of
\Sigma^{2,2}
points of
H
is given by the zeros of a certain ideal they call
J_{2,2}(H)
.
In this case,

J_{2,2}(H)=\langle h_{v},h_{w},h_{vv},h_{vw},h_{ww}\rangle,

whose zero locus is the set of solutions of Equation (
9
).

Using a result from
[
15
]
, we can write
h
explicitly as

h(u,s,t,v,w)=r(u,v,w)+\alpha(u,v,w)(s-p(u,v,w))+\beta(u,v,w)(t-q(u,v,w)),

where
s,t\in\mathbb{C}
.
Then, Equation (
9
) becomes

(10)

\displaystyle 0=h_{v}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{v}(s-p)+\beta_{v}(t-q);

(11)

\displaystyle 0=h_{w}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{w}(s-p)+\beta_{w}(t-q);

(12)

\displaystyle 0=h_{vv}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{vv}(s-p)-\alpha_{v}p_{v}+\beta_{vv}(t-q)-\beta_{v}q_{v};

(13)

\displaystyle 0=h_{vw}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{vw}(s-p)-\alpha_{v}p_{w}+\beta_{vw}(t-q)-\beta_{v}q_{w};

(14)

\displaystyle 0=h_{ww}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{ww}(s-p)-\alpha_{w}p_{w}+\beta_{ww}(t-q)-\beta_{w}q_{w}.

Equations (
10
,
11
) form a linear system of equations, with the coefficient matrix being the matrix
M
from Lemma
3.2
, meaning that
s-p=t-q=0
.
Applying this information to (
12
–
14
), we arrive at

\displaystyle 0=\beta_{v}q_{v}+\alpha_{v}p_{v};

\displaystyle 0=\beta_{v}q_{w}+\alpha_{v}p_{w};

\displaystyle 0=\beta_{w}q_{w}+\alpha_{w}p_{w},

which yields the following

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
F\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a germ of wave front with corank
2
.
Take coordinates in the source and target such that
F
is given as in (
4
) and verifies the identity (
6
).
Then, the set of points
(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
where
F
has corank
2
near
S
is given the common zeros of the functions

\beta_{v}p_{v}+\alpha_{v}q_{v},\beta_{v}p_{w}+\alpha_{v}q_{w},\beta_{w}p_{w}+\alpha_{w}q_{w}.

Therefore,
I_{d+1}(J_{F})
has three generators and
\mathscr{O}_{3}/I_{2}(J_{F})
is a complete intersection module for
n=3
.

Now let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{4},0)
be an
\mathscr{F}
-finite germ of wave front with stable frontal unfolding
F\colon(\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}^{3},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}^{4},0)
.
Since
f
is
\mathscr{F}
-finite,
F
is also
\mathscr{F}
-finite, so we can find a representative
F\colon U\to V
of
F
such that the restriction
F|\colon U\backslash S\to V\backslash\{0\}
only exhibits frontal stable singularities (
[
13
]
, Theorem 4.6).
In particular, the points in
U\backslash S
where
F
has corank
2
will be
D_{4}
singularities, as it is the only stable singularity of corank
2
in dimension
3
.
Combining this with Theorem
3.3
, we arrive to the following

Corollary 3.6
.

The number of
D_{4}
singularities near the origin on the image of a stable frontal unfolding of
f
is equal to

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathscr{O}_{3}}{\langle p_{v},p_{w},q_{v},q_{w}\rangle},

where
g_{v}
,
g_{w}
denote the partial derivatives of the function
g
with respect to
v
and
w
.

Example 3.7
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{4},0)
be the map germ given by

f(u,v,w)=(u,vw,2u^{n}v+3uv^{2}+4v^{3}+w^{2},u^{n}v^{2}+2uv^{3}+3v^{4}+2vw^{2}).

This mapping can be obtained as a pullback of the mapping
(v,w)\mapsto(vw,4v^{3}+w^{2},3v^{4}+2vw^{2})
, which unfolds into a
D_{5}
singularity.
Therefore, it is not only a wave front, but also
\mathscr{F}
-finite.

The ideal
I_{2}(J_{f})
is equal to
\langle u,v^{2},w\rangle
, hence the number of
D_{4}
singularities in a stable frontal deformation of
f
is equal to
2
, regardless of
n
.

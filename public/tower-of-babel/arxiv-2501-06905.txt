Counting records in a random, non-uniform, permutation
Boris Pittel

Abstract
. Counting permutations of [ n ] [n] by the number of records, i.e. left-to-right maxima, is a classic problem in combinatorial enumeration. In the first volume of “The Art of Computer Programming”, Donald Knuth demonstrated its relevance for analysis of average case complexity of a basic algorithm for determining a maximum in a linear list of numbers. It is well known that the expected, and likely, number of those records in a uniformly random permutation is asymptotic to log ⁡ n \log n . Cyril Banderier, Rene Beier, and Kurt Mehlhorn studied the case of a non-uniform random permutation, which is obtained from a generic permutation of [ n ] [n] by selecting its elements one after another independently with probability p p , and permuting the selected elements uniformly at random. They proved that E n ​ ( p ) E_{n}(p) , the largest expected number of the maxima, is between const ​ n / p \text{const}\sqrt{n/p} and O ⁡ ( ( n / p ) ​ log ⁡ n ) O\bigl(\sqrt{(n/p)\log n}\bigr) if p p is fixed. For p ≫ 1 / n p\gg 1/n and simultaneously 1 − p ≥ const n − 1 / 2 log n 1-p\geq\text{const }n^{-1/2}\log n , we prove that E n ​ ( p ) E_{n}(p) is exactly of order ( 1 − p ) ​ n / p (1-p)\sqrt{n/p} .

1.
Introduction

Typically, the average case analysis of combinatorial algorithms is predicated on an assumption that the random instance of a problem is chosen uniformly at random from a given set of those instances. This assumption opens the door to powerful combinatorial methods, but inevitably limits applicability of the results to problem with considerably lesser degree of homogeneity and symmetry. In a pioneering paper
[
1
]
, Cyril Banderier, Rene Beier, and Kurt Mehlhorn undertook a study of three algorithmic problems, in which randomization is confined to a
given
combinatorial structure. This allows, in principle, to consider a hybrid version of algorithmic analysis, with focus on the worst
average
-case behavior of an algorithm applied to the random problem instance chosen from a priori restricted set of instances. The numerous references in the paper allow the authors to put their work in a proper perspective. The interested reader may also wish to consult Volume 1, and new Volume 4B of the series “The Art of Computer Programming” by Donald Knuth
[
4
]
for a detailed discussion of ”Find the maximum” algorithm and a succinct description of the related results from

[
1
]
.

Here is one of these results. Let
\bar{X}
be an arbitrary permutation of
[n]
. Let
X=X(\bar{X})
denote the permutation obtained by selecting the elements of
\bar{X}
independently, each with probability
p
, and permuting uniformly at random the selected elements. Let
\lambda(X)
denote the total number of records, i. e. left-to-right maxima, in
X
. It was proved in
[
1
]
that, for a fixed
p
, the expected value
\mathbb{E}[\lambda(X)]=O\bigl(\sqrt{(n/p)\log n}\bigr)
, uniformly over all
\bar{X}
, and that
\max_{\bar{X}}\mathbb{E}[\lambda(X)]
is of order
\sqrt{n/p}
at least.

Our main result is that, in fact, for
p\gg 1/n
and simultaneously
1-p\geq\text{const }n^{-1/2}\log n
,
\max_{\bar{X}}\mathbb{E}[\lambda(X)]
is of order
(1-p)\sqrt{n/p}
exactly. While our methods are quite different, it is the study in
[
1
]
that made our research possible.

Kurt Mehlhorn
[
5
]
drew my attention to a recent paper
[
2
]
by Beier, Rőglin, Rősner, and Vőcking,
where the model of smoothed analysis with problem instances subject to a random noise is applied to a class of integer optimization problems.

2.
Statements and proofs.

Let
\bar{X}
be an arbitrary permutation of
[n]
. Let
X
denote the permutation obtained by marking (selecting) the elements of
\bar{X}
independently, each with probability
p
, and permuting uniformly at random the marked elements. Let
\lambda(X)
denote the total number of left-to-right maxima in
X
. We write
\lambda(X)=\lambda_{\text{u}}(X)+\lambda_{\text{m}}(X)
where
\lambda_{\text{u}}(X)
and
\lambda_{\text{m}}(X)
are the numbers of the left-to-right maxima of
X
that are, respectively, unmarked and marked elements of
\bar{X}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

\max_{\bar{X}}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{\text{u}}(X)]=O\Bigl((1-p)\sqrt{\tfrac{n}{p}}\Bigr)
, if
p\gg n^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
A_{k}
,
(k\in[n])
, be the event that is
x_{k}
is an unmarked left-to-right maximum in the resulting permutation
X
. We need to bound
\mathbb{P}\bigl(A_{k})
.
On the event
A_{k}
, we have

\bar{x}_{k}=x_{k}\geq k
, since
x_{k}
is the largest element among
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
.
Introduce the sets
N_{i,j}=N_{i,j}(\bar{x}_{k})
:

\displaystyle N_{1,1}=\{\bar{x}_{\alpha}:\alpha<k,\,\bar{x}_{\alpha}<\bar{x}_{k}\},\,\,\,N_{1,2}=\{\bar{x}_{\alpha}:\alpha<k,\,\bar{x}_{\alpha}>\bar{x}_{k}\},

\displaystyle N_{2,1}=\{\bar{x}_{\alpha}:\alpha>k,\,\bar{x}_{\alpha}<\bar{x}_{k}\},\,\,\,N_{2,2}=\{\bar{x}_{\alpha}:\alpha>k,\,\bar{x}_{\alpha}>\bar{x}_{k}\};

x_{k}
cannot be a left-to-right maximum, unless
|N_{1,2}|+k\leq n
. Let

M_{i,j}
stand for the set of marked elements of
N_{i,j}
.
For the event in question,
\bar{x}_{k}
is unmarked, so
M_{1,2}=N_{1,2}
; otherwise there is an unmarked element

\alpha\in N_{1,2}\setminus M_{1,2}
exceeding
\bar{x}_{k}
that will stay to the left of
\bar{x}_{k}
, and
\bar{x}_{k}
will not be a left-to-right maximum in the resulting permutation
X
.
Denote
n_{i,j}=|N_{i,j}|
,
m_{i,j}=|M_{i,j}|
; so
\sum_{i,j}n_{i,j}=n-1
, and

m_{1,2}=n_{1,2}
.

Let
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
denote generic numbers of elements from
M_{1,1}
and from
M_{2,1}
that will stay to the left of
\bar{x}_{k}
, and will be moved to the left of
\bar{x}_{k}
, respectively. For the event in question to hold,
k_{1}+k_{2}=k-1\leq\bar{x}_{k}-1
. In addition, all those
n_{1,2}
elements to the left of
\bar{x}_{k}
, exceeding
\bar{x}_{k}
, will need to be marked, and moved to the right of
\bar{x}_{k}
. Consequently, on the event in question
m:=\sum_{i,j}m_{i,j}\geq k-1+n_{1,2}
. The total number of such permutations is

(k-1)!\bigl(m-(k-1)\bigr)!\prod_{(i,j)\neq(1,2)}\binom{n_{i,j}}{m_{i,j}}\cdot\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k-1}\binom{m_{1,1}}{k_{1}}\binom{m_{2,1}}{k_{2}}\\
=(k-1)!\bigl(m-(k-1)\bigr)!\prod_{(i,j)\neq(1,2)}\binom{n_{i,j}}{m_{i,j}}\cdot\binom{m_{1,1}+m_{2,1}}{k-1};

(1,2)
is excluded from the double product since
\binom{n_{1,2}}{m_{1,2}}=1
. To get the contribution of all such permutations with a given
m
to
\mathbb{P}(A_{k})
we need to multiply the above product by

\tfrac{p^{m}(1-p)^{n-m}}{m!}
and evaluate the sum of all these fractions over
\{m_{i,j}\}
subject to
constraints
\sum_{i,j}m_{i,j}=m
,
m_{1,2}=n_{1,2}
. We will repeatedly use the identity

(2.1)

\sum_{a_{1}+a_{2}=a}\binom{b_{1}}{a_{1}}\binom{b_{2}}{a_{2}}=\binom{b_{1}+b_{2}}{a}.

To begin, denoting
n_{\circ,1}=n_{1,1}+n_{2,1}
,

m_{\circ,1}=m_{1,1}+m_{2,1}
, we have

\sum_{\{m_{i,j}\}}\prod_{(i,j)\neq(1,2)}\binom{n_{i,j}}{m_{(i,j)}}\cdot\binom{m_{1,1}+m_{2,1}}{k-1}\\
=\frac{n_{\circ,1}!}{(n_{\circ,1}-(k-1))!}\sum_{m_{\circ,1},\,m_{2,2}}\binom{n_{\circ,1}-(k-1)}{m_{\circ,1}-(k-1}\binom{n_{2,2}}{m_{2,2}}\\
=(k-1)!\binom{n_{\circ,1}}{k-1}\binom{n-n_{1,2}-k}{m-n_{1,2}-(k-1)};

as
n_{\circ,1}+n_{2,2}=n-1-n_{1,2}
. Since
n_{\circ,1}=\bar{x}_{k}-1
, we obtain

(2.2)

\mathbb{P}(A_{k})=\sum_{n>m\geq n_{1,2}+k-1}\binom{m}{k-1}^{-1}\\
\times\binom{\bar{x}_{k}-1}{k-1}\binom{n-n_{1,2}-k}{m-n_{1,2}-(k-1)}\cdot p^{m}(1-p)^{n-m}.

By (
2.2
), and a classic formula

(2.3)

\frac{a_{1}!\,a_{2}!}{(a_{1}+a_{2}+1)!}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{a_{1}}(1-t)^{a_{2}}\,dt,

we have

\mathbb{P}(A_{k})=(1-p)^{n}\binom{\bar{x}_{k}-1}{k-1}\\
\times\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{p\xi}{1-p}\right)^{k-1}\left(\frac{p(1-\xi)}{1-p}\right)^{n_{1,2}}\\
\times\sum_{n>m\geq n_{1,2}+k-1}\!\!\!\!\!\!(m+1)\,\binom{n-(n_{1,2}+k)}{m-(n_{1,2}+k-1)}\left(\frac{p(1-\xi)}{1-p}\right)^{m-(n_{1,2}+k-1)}\,d\xi.

By
m+1=(n_{1,2}+k)+(m-(n_{1,2}+k-1))
, the bottom sum equals

(n_{1,2}+k)\left(1+\frac{p(1-\xi)}{1-p}\right)^{n-(n_{1,2}+k)}\\
+\bigl(n-(n_{1,2}+k)\bigr)\frac{p(1-\xi)}{1-p}\left(1+\frac{p(1-\xi)}{1-p}\right)^{n-(n_{1,2}+k)-1}\\
=\left(\frac{1-p\xi}{1-p}\right)^{n-(n_{1,2}+k)-1}\left[(n_{1,2}+k)\left(\frac{1-p\xi}{1-p}-\frac{p(1-\xi)}{1-p}\right)+n\frac{p(1-\xi)}{1-p}\right]\\
=\left(\frac{1-p\xi}{1-p}\right)^{n-(n_{1,2}+k)-1}\cdot\left[n_{1,2}+k+n\frac{p(1-\xi)}{1-p}\right].

For
n\geq n_{1,2}+k
,

(2.4)

\mathbb{P}(A_{k})=(1-p)^{n}\binom{\bar{x}_{k}-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{p}{1-p}\right)^{n_{1,2}+k-1}\\
\times\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{k-1}(1-\xi)^{n_{1,2}}\cdot\left(\frac{1-p\xi}{1-p}\right)^{n-(n_{1,2}+k)-1}\cdot\left[n_{1,2}+k+n\frac{p(1-\xi)}{1-p}\right]\,d\xi\\
=\binom{\bar{x}_{k}-1}{k-1}p^{n_{1,2}+k-1}(1-p)\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{k-1}(1-\xi)^{n_{1,2}}(1-p\xi)^{n-(n_{1,2}+k)-1}\\
\times\bigl[(n_{1,2}+k)(1-p\xi)+\bigl(n-(n_{1,2}+k)\bigr)p(1-\xi)\bigr]\,d\xi\\
\leq n\binom{\bar{x}_{k}-1}{k-1}p^{n_{1,2}+k-1}(1-p)\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{k-1}(1-\xi)^{n_{1,2}}(1-p\xi)^{n-(n_{1,2}+k)}\,d\xi.\\

Switching from
\xi
to
\eta=p\xi
, we transform the bound (
2.4
) into

(2.5)

n\binom{\bar{x}_{k}-1}{k-1}\frac{1-p}{p}\int_{0}^{p}\eta^{k-1}(p-\eta)^{n_{1,2}}(1-\eta)^{n-(n_{1,2}+k)}\,d\eta\\
\leq n\binom{\bar{x}_{k}-1}{k-1}\frac{1-p}{p}\int_{0}^{p}\eta^{k-1}(1-\eta)^{n-k}\,d\eta,\\

since
\tfrac{p-\eta}{1-\eta}<1
. (Dependence on
n_{1,2}
is gone!)
The last integrand attains its absolute maximum at
\bar{\eta}=\tfrac{k-1}{n-1}\geq p
if
k\geq pn+1-p
.
Consider
k\geq k_{1}:=\lceil(1+\varepsilon)(n-1)p\rceil
. Since the integrand is log-concave, the integral is at most

p^{k-1}(1-p)^{n-k}\int_{0}^{p}\exp\left(\left(\frac{k-1}{p}-\frac{n-k}{1-p}\right)(y-p)\right)\,dy\\
\leq p^{k-1}(1-p)^{n-k}\int_{0}^{p}\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon(n-1)}{1-p}(y-p)\right)\,dy\\
\leq\frac{1-p}{(n-1)\varepsilon}\,p^{k-1}(1-p)^{n-k}.

Consequently, for
k\geq k_{1}
, the bound (
2.5
) is at most of order

\frac{(1-p)^{2}}{\varepsilon p}\cdot\binom{n-1}{k-1}p^{k-1}(1-p)^{(n-1)-(k-1)}.

Now
\bigl\{\binom{n-1}{j}p^{j}(1-p)^{n-1-j}:\,j\in[n-1]\bigr\}
is the binomial distribution, with parameters
p
and
n-1
. By a classic tail bound for the binomial distribution,
Janson, Łuczak, and Ruciński
[
3
]
,

\sum_{k\geq k_{1}}\binom{n-1}{k-1}p^{k-1}(1-p)^{n-k}\leq 2\exp\bigl(-\tfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{3}pn\bigr),\quad\varepsilon\leq 1.

So, the
total
contribution to the bound in (
2.4
) from unmarked records at locations
k\in[k_{2},n]
,
with
k_{2}:=\lceil(1-\varepsilon)(n-1)p\rceil
, is at most of order

(2.6)

\varepsilon pn+\tfrac{(1-p)^{2}}{\varepsilon p}\exp\bigl(-\tfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{3}pn\bigr)\leq 2(1-p)\sqrt{\tfrac{n}{p}},\quad\text{if }\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n):=\tfrac{1-p}{\sqrt{pn}}.

Consider
cn^{1/2}\leq k\leq k_{2}
,
c=c(p)
to be chosen. This time,
\bar{\eta}=\tfrac{k-1}{n-1}
, the absolute maximum point of the bottom integrand in (
2.5
) is within
(0,p)
. Denoting

H(\eta)=(k-1)\log\eta-(n-k)\log(1-\eta)
, we see that

H^{\prime\prime}(\eta)=-\frac{k-1}{\eta^{2}}-\frac{n-k}{(1-\eta)^{2}},\quad H^{\prime\prime\prime}(\eta)=\frac{2(k-1)}{\eta^{3}}-\frac{2(n-k)}{(1-\eta)^{3}}.

So,
H^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\eta})=-\frac{(n-1)^{3}}{(k-1)(n-k)}
, and
H^{\prime\prime\prime}(\eta)\geq 0
for
\eta\leq\bar{\eta}
. So,

H(\eta)\leq H(\bar{\eta})-\frac{(n-1)^{3}}{2(k-1)(n-k)}(\eta-\bar{\eta})^{2},\quad\eta\leq\bar{\eta},

and, for
\eta>\bar{\eta}
, and some
\tilde{\eta}\in[\bar{\eta},\eta]
,

H(\eta)=H(\bar{\eta})-\frac{(n-1)^{3}}{2(k-1)(n-k)}(\eta-\bar{\eta})^{2}+\frac{1}{6}H^{\prime\prime\prime}(\tilde{\eta})(\eta-\bar{\eta})^{3}\\
\leq H(\bar{\eta})-\frac{(n-1)^{3}}{2(k-1)(n-k)}(\eta-\bar{\eta})^{2}+\frac{k-1}{3\tilde{\eta}^{2}}(\eta-\bar{\eta})^{3}\\
\leq H(\bar{\eta})-\frac{(n-1)^{3}}{2(k-1)(n-k)}(\eta-\bar{\eta})^{2}+\frac{k-1}{3\bar{\eta}^{2}}(\eta-\bar{\eta})^{2}\\
=H(\bar{\eta})-\frac{(n-1)^{3}}{2(k-1)(n-k)}(\eta-\bar{\eta})^{2}\frac{1+2\bar{\eta}}{3}\leq H(\bar{\eta})-\frac{(n-1)^{3}}{6(k-1)(n-k)}(\eta-\bar{\eta})^{2}.

It follows directly that

\int_{0}^{p}\eta^{k-1}(1-\eta)^{n-k}\,d\eta\leq e^{H(\bar{\eta})}\int_{0}^{p}\exp\left(-\frac{(n-1)^{3}}{6(k-1)(n-k)}(\eta-\bar{\eta})^{2}\right)\,d\eta\\
\leq e^{H(\bar{\eta})}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\left(\tfrac{(n-1)^{3}}{3(k-1)(n-k)}\right)}}\\
=\frac{(6\pi)^{1/2}}{n-1}\frac{(k-1)^{k-1}(n-k)^{n-k}}{(n-1)^{n-1}}(k-1)^{1/2}(n-k)^{1/2}\\
=O\Bigl(n^{-1}\binom{n-1}{k-1}^{-1}\Bigr).

So, the bottom bound in in (
2.4
) is at most of order

\frac{1-p}{p}\binom{\bar{x}_{k}-1}{k-1}\binom{n-1}{k-1}^{-1}.

It remains to bound the sum of these terms over
k\in[cn^{1/2},k_{2}]
, and select a proper value
of
c=c(n)
. Observe that for
x\in(k,n]

\binom{x-1}{k}\binom{n-1}{k}^{-1}\binom{x-1}{k-1}^{-1}\binom{n-1}{k-1}=\frac{x-k}{n-k}\leq 1.

So, for

k>k(n):=\lceil cn^{1/2}\rceil
, whence
\bar{x}_{k}>k(n)
, we have

\binom{\bar{x}_{k}-1}{k-1}\binom{n-1}{k-1}^{-1}\leq\binom{\bar{x}_{k}-1}{k(n)}\binom{n-1}{k(n)}^{-1}=\frac{(\bar{x}_{k}-1)_{k(n)}}{(n-1)_{k(n)}}\leq\left(\frac{\bar{x}_{k}}{n}\right)^{k(n)}.\\

Therefore
\sum_{k\in[k(n),k_{2}]}\mathbb{P}(A_{k})
is at most of order

\frac{1-p}{p}\sum_{k\in[k(n),k_{2}]}\left(\frac{\bar{x}_{k}}{n}\right)^{k(n)}\leq\frac{1-p}{p}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)^{k(n)}\\
\leq\frac{(1-p)n}{p}\int_{0}^{1+1/n}\zeta^{k(n)}\,d\zeta\\
=\frac{(1-p)n}{p}\cdot\frac{(1+1/n)^{k(n)+1}}{k(n)+1}=O\left(\frac{(1-p)n}{pcn^{1/2}}\right),

if
c=O(n^{1/2})
. Besides, the expected number of unmarked records located somewhere in
[k(n)]
is at most
(1-p)k(n)
. Thus, combining the last two bounds and (
2.6
), we conclude that
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{u}(X)]
is at most of order

(1-p)\sqrt{\frac{n}{p}}+c(1-p)n^{1/2}+\frac{n^{1/2}(1-p)}{pc}=O\left(\!(1-p)\sqrt{\frac{n}{p}}\right),

if we choose
c=p^{-1/2}
, which we can do since
p\geq n^{-1}
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

For every
p\in[0,1]
,

\max_{\bar{X}}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{\text{m}}(X)]\leq\sum_{j\in[n]}\frac{1-(1-p)^{j}}{j},

which is the equality if and only if
\bar{X}=(1,2,\dots,n)
.

Proof.

Let
B_{\ell}
be the event that a generic element
\ell\in[n]
is a marked left-to-right maximum of

X
. Then
B_{\ell}=\cup_{k\leq\ell}B_{\ell,k}
; here
B_{\ell,k}
is the event “
\ell=x_{k}
and
x_{k}
is a marked left-to-right maximum of
X
”. Let us bound
\mathbb{P}(B_{\ell,k})
.

Introduce the sets
N_{i,j}
:

\displaystyle N_{1,1}=\{\bar{x}_{\alpha}:\alpha<k,\,\bar{x}_{\alpha}<\ell\},\,\,\,N_{1,2}=\{\bar{x}_{\alpha}:\alpha<k,\,\bar{x}_{\alpha}>\ell\},

\displaystyle N_{2,1}=\{\bar{x}_{\alpha}:\alpha>k,\,\bar{x}_{\alpha}<\ell\},\,\,\,N_{2,2}=\{\bar{x}_{\alpha}:\alpha>k,\,\bar{x}_{\alpha}>\ell\},

and
n_{i,j}=|N_{i,j}|
. Needless to say,
N_{i,j}
,
n_{i,j}
depend on
\ell
and
k
, and
\sum_{i,j}n_{i,j}=n-1
on
B_{\ell,k}
. We will use

(2.7)

n_{\circ,1}:=n_{1,1}+n_{2,1}=\ell-1-\mathbb{I}(\bar{x}_{k}<\ell)\leq\ell-1.

Let

M_{i,j}
stand for the set of marked elements of
N_{i,j}
, and
m_{i,j}:=|M_{i,j}|
. On
B_{\ell,k}
, once the marked elements different from
\ell
are permuted, the marked element
\ell
takes the position immediately following
(k-1)
-th element of that
(n-1)
-long sub-permutation. It means that necessarily
m_{1,2}=n_{1,2}
: otherwise an unmarked element
\bar{x}_{\alpha}\in N_{1,2}\setminus M_{1,2}
(with
\alpha<k
) precedes
\ell
, preventing
\ell
from being a left-to-right maximum in
X
.

Let
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
denote generic numbers of elements from
M_{1,1}
and from
M_{2,1}
that will stay among the first
(k-1)
elements (among the last
n-k
elements) of a resulting admissible permutation of
\bar{X}\setminus\{\ell\}
. For the event in question to hold,
k_{1}+k_{2}=k-1
. The total number of such permutations of
\bar{X}\setminus\{\ell\}
with given
\{m_{i,j}\}
is

(k-1)!\bigl(m-(k-1)\bigr)!\prod_{(i,j)\neq(1,2)}\binom{n_{i,j}}{m_{i,j}}\cdot\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k-1}\binom{m_{1,1}}{k_{1}}\binom{m_{2,1}}{k_{2}}\\
=(k-1)!\bigl(m-(k-1)\bigr)!\prod_{(i,j)\neq(1,2)}\binom{n_{i,j}}{m_{i,j}}\cdot\binom{m_{1,1}+m_{2,1}}{k-1};

(1,2)
is excluded from the double product since
m_{1,2}=n_{1,2}
. For every such permutation we get an admissible permutation of
\bar{X}
by inserting element
\ell
right after
(k-1)
-th element of that permutation.
For this to happen, if
\bar{x}_{k}\neq\ell
,
\bar{x}_{k}
needs to be marked and moved out of the slot
k
. Neglecting
this restraint, we bound the contribution of all such permutations with a given
m
to
\mathbb{P}(B_{\ell,k})
,
by multiplying the above product by

\tfrac{p^{m+1}(1-p)^{n-m-1}}{(m+1)!}
(
\ell
must be marked too!) and evaluating the sum of all these fractions over
\{m_{i,j}\}
subject to
constraints
\sum_{i,j}m_{i,j}=m
,
m_{1,2}=n_{1,2}
. So, within the factor
\tfrac{p^{m+1}(1-p)^{n-m-1}}{(m+1)!}\cdot(k-1)!(m-(k-1))!
,
the contribution in question is at most

\sum_{m_{i,j}\leq n_{i,j}\atop\sum_{(i,j)\neq(1,2)}m_{i,j}=m-n_{1,2}}\!\!\!\prod_{(i,j)\neq(1,2)}\binom{n_{i,j}}{m_{i,j}}\,\,\times\binom{m_{\circ,1}}{k-1},\\

where
m_{\circ,1}:=m_{1,1}+m_{2,1}
. Given
m
and
m_{\circ,1}\in[k-1,m-n_{1,2}]
, we have
m_{2,2}=m-n_{1,2}-m_{\circ,1}
, and the sum over
m_{1,1}
and
m_{2,1}
equals

\binom{m_{\circ,1}}{k-1}\sum_{m_{1,1}+m_{2,1}=m_{\circ,1},\atop m_{2,2}=m-n_{1,2}-m_{\circ,1}}\binom{n_{1,1}}{m_{1,1}}\binom{n_{2,1}}{m_{2,1}}\binom{n_{2,2}}{m_{2,2}}\\
=\binom{m_{\circ,1}}{k-1}\binom{n_{\circ,1}}{m_{\circ,1}}\binom{n_{2,2}}{m-n_{1,2}-m_{\circ,1}}\\
=\binom{n_{\circ,1}}{k-1}\binom{n_{\circ,1}-(k-1)}{m_{\circ,1}-(k-1)}\binom{n_{2,2}}{m-n_{1,2}-m_{\circ,1}},

where, by (
2.7
),
n_{\circ,1}:=n_{1,1}+n_{2,1}\leq\ell-1
. So, given
m
, the sum over all
m_{\circ,1}\in[k-1,m-n_{1,2}]
is bounded by

\binom{\ell-1}{k-1}\binom{n-1-n_{1,2}-(k-1)}{m-n_{1,2}-(k-1)},

which is zero, unless
\ell\geq k
. So, the counterpart of the formula (
2.2
) is

(2.8)

\mathbb{P}(B_{\ell,k})=\sum_{m\geq n_{1,2}+k-1}\Bigl[(m+1)\binom{m}{k-1}\Bigr]^{-1}\\
\times\binom{\ell-1}{k-1}\binom{n-1-n_{1,2}-(k-1)}{m-n_{1,2}-(k-1)}\cdot p^{m+1}(1-p)^{n-1-m}.

Using (
2.1
), we transform (
2.8
) into

\mathbb{P}(B_{\ell,k})=(1-p)^{n-1}p\,\int_{0}^{1}\binom{\ell-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{px}{1-p}\right)^{k-1}\left(\frac{p(1-x)}{1-p}\right)^{n_{1,2}}\\
\times\sum_{m\geq k-1+n_{1,2}}\binom{n-k-n_{1,2}}{m-k-n_{1,2}+1}\left(\frac{p(1-x)}{1-p}\right)^{m-k-n_{1,2}+1}\,dx\\
=(1-p)^{n-1}p\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{p(1-x)}{1-px}\right)^{n_{1,2}}\binom{\ell-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{px}{1-p}\right)^{k-1}\left(\frac{1-px}{1-p}\right)^{n-k}\,dx\\
\leq(1-p)^{n-1}p\int_{0}^{1}\binom{\ell-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{px}{1-p}\right)^{k-1}\left(\frac{1-px}{1-p}\right)^{n-k}\,dx,

since
\tfrac{p(1-x)}{1-px}\leq 1
. Crucially, the bottom RHS does not depend on
n_{1.2}
. And

\sum_{k\in[\ell]}\binom{\ell-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{px}{1-p}\right)^{k-1}\left(\frac{1-px}{1-p}\right)^{n-k}\\
=\left(\frac{1-px}{1-p}\right)^{n-\ell}\left(\frac{px}{1-p}+\frac{1-px}{1-p}\right)^{\ell-1}=\frac{(1-px)^{n-\ell}}{(1-p)^{n-1}}.\\

So, summing
\mathbb{P}(B_{\ell,k})
over
k\in[\ell]
, we conclude that

\mathbb{P}(B_{\ell})\leq p\int_{0}^{1}(1-px)^{n-\ell}\,dx=\tfrac{1-(1-p)^{n-\ell+1}}{n-\ell+1}.

Therefore
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{\text{m}}]\leq\sum_{j\in[n]}\tfrac{1-(1-p)^{j}}{j}
, and this upper bound is attained if and only if, for each
\ell
and
k
, we have
n_{1,2}=n_{1,2}(\ell,k)=0
, which happens if and only if

\bar{X}=(1,2,\dots,n)
.∎

Corollary 2.3
.

For
p\gg n^{-1},1-p\geq\text{const }n^{-1/2}\log n
,
\max_{\bar{X}}\mathbb{E}[\lambda(X)]=O\Bigl((1-p)\sqrt{\tfrac{n}{p}}\Bigr)
.

Proof.

In view of Theorems
1,2
, we need to prove that for
p
in question

\sum_{j\in[n]}\frac{1-(1-p)^{j}}{j}=O\left(\!(1-p)\sqrt{\frac{n}{p}}\right).

Denote
H_{n}=\sum_{j\in[n]}\tfrac{1}{j}
. Since
1/j
decreases with
j
, we have
H_{n}\leq 1+\int_{1}^{n}\tfrac{dx}{x}=\log(ne)
. So,

\sum_{j\in[n]}\frac{1-(1-p)^{j}}{j}=H_{n}-\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{(1-p)^{j}}{j}+\sum_{j>n}\frac{(1-p)^{j}}{j}\\
\leq\log(ne)+\log p+\frac{1}{(n+1)p}\leq\log(npe^{2})=O\left(\!(1-p)\sqrt{\frac{n}{p}}\right),

which is true since
1-p\geq\text{const }n^{-1/2}\log n
.
∎

Banderier, Beier, and Mehlhorn
[
1
]
discovered that for a permutation

\bar{X}=(n-k,n-k+1,\dots,n,1,2,\dots,n-k-1),\quad k:=\big\lfloor\sqrt{n/p}\big\rfloor,

\mathbb{E}[\lambda(X)]
is
at least
of order
\sqrt{n/p}
, if
p<1/2
, and fixed. And then they obtained an almost matching upper bound
O(\sqrt{(n/p)\log n})
that holds uniformly for every
\bar{X}
.

Slightly modified, the ingenious argument in
[
1
]
has enabled us to prove that, for the same
\bar{X}
,
\mathbb{E}[\lambda(X)]
is
at least
of order
(1-p)\sqrt{n/p}
, if
p\gg n^{-1}
and
1-p\gg n^{-1/2}
.
Combining this stronger result and the corollary we obtain that
\max_{\bar{X}}\mathbb{E}[\lambda(X)]
is of order

(1-p)\sqrt{n/p}
exactly if
p\gg n^{-1}
and
1-p\geq\text{const }n^{-1/2}\log n
.

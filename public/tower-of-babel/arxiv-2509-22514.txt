Comparison estimates on nonsmooth spaces with integrable Ricci lower bounds via localization
Emanuele Caputo, Francesco Nobili, Tommaso Rossi

Abstract
. We study comparison estimates on metric measure spaces admitting a synthetic variable Ricci curvature lower bound. We obtain geometric and functional inequalities assuming that the deficit of the lower bound from a given constant is sufficiently integrable. More precisely, we extend to the nonsmooth setting the Bishop-Gromov comparison, the Myers’ diameter estimate and the Cheng’s comparison principle for Dirichlet eigenvalues. Our analysis relies on the localization method and on one-dimensional comparison estimates for nonsmooth weighted intervals.



1.
Introduction

In a smooth Riemannian manifold, a lower bound on the Ricci curvature tensor allows to deduce comparison estimates for many geometric and analytic objects, in terms of the corresponding quantities in a constant-curvature model space. Classical examples of comparison estimates include Bishop-Gromov volume comparison, Myers’ maximal diameter theorem, and spectral estimates such as Lichnerowicz’s spectral gap and Cheng’s comparison for Dirichlet eigenvalues. We refer to the introductory books
[
24
,
54
]
and references therein. Building on these foundational results, two major research directions have emerged:

•

weakening the Ricci curvature lower bound from a uniform to an
integral
bound;

•

extending comparison principles to
nonsmooth
spaces allowing for singularities.

In the first direction, notable works
[
55
,
56
,
57
]
study manifolds
(M^{d},g)
where the Ricci curvature lower bound is replaced by an
integral curvature deficit

\int_{M}|\min\{{\rm Ric}_{g}^{-}-K,0\}|^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}{\rm Vol}_{g}

with
{\rm Ric}_{g}^{-}(x)
denoting the smallest eigenvalue of the Ricci tensor,
K\in\mathbb{R}
, and
p>1
.
When
p>d/2
and the deficit is finite, or sufficiently small, comparison estimates hold up to error terms depending on this deficit
[
33
,
56
,
57
,
4
,
26
,
67
]
.
Another related but different condition, which we will not employ here, is the Kato-type bound on the Ricci tensor
[
9
,
59
]
.

The second direction has seen rapid growth.
A key observation is that the class of manifolds with a uniform Ricci lower bound and a uniform dimension upper bound is precompact in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff topology
[
36
]
. Limit structures are called Ricci limit spaces and were extensively studied starting from the celebrated work
[
23
]
. This analysis paved the way for studying constant curvature lower bounds from an intrinsic viewpoint. This is captured by the curvature-dimension condition (
{\sf CD}
for short), defined via optimal transport, introduced independently in
[
62
,
63
]
and
[
45
]
. In this setting, comparison and rigidity results have been extensively studied, see e.g.
[
38
,
39
,
28
,
58
,
18
,
19
,
31
,
48
,
46
,
52
,
53
,
51
,
49
]
.
We also refer to
[
66
,
1
,
6
]
for further details.

Lastly, these two lines of research naturally intersect. Indeed, the class of uniformly bounded
d
-manifolds with a sufficiently small uniform upper bound on the integral curvature deficit is precompact in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology, see
[
56
,
57
]
. Hence nonsmooth limits arise in this setting as well. Analogous investigations were carried under Kato-type bounds
[

,

, and related developments appear in metric measure settings
[

,

,

,

.

Setting and main results
. In this manuscript, we focus on a metric measure space
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
, that is a complete and separable metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
endowed with a boundedly finite, non-negative reference measure
\mathfrak{m}
, which admits a
variable
Ricci curvature lower bound. Concretely, we assume that
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies the so-called
{\sf CD}(k,N)
condition (see
Definition

2.4
) for some dimensional parameter
N\in(1,\infty)
, and some locally bounded and lower semicontinuous function

k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R},

called admissible. This condition has been introduced in
[
40
]
, using the language of optimal transport formulation, and generalizes the classical formulations
[
62
,
63
,
45
]
for constant curvature lower bounds. Moreover, as shown in
[
40
]
, it is consistent with the smooth category: a manifold
(M^{d},g)
satisfies
{\sf CD}(k,N)
if and only if
d\leq N
and
{\rm Ric}_{g}\geq kg
on
M^{d}
and a natural choice is
k(x)={\rm Ric}_{g}^{-}(x)
.

For
p\geq 1
, we define the
integral curvature deficit
from a constant
K\in\mathbb{R}
of an admissible function
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
over a Borel set
E\subset{\rm X}
by

(1.1)

\rho^{k}_{p}(E,K)\coloneqq\int_{E}\big|\min\{k-K,0\}\big|^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}.

Our first main result is a quantitative version of the Bishop-Gromov inequality in the nonsmooth setting. Here
v_{K,N}(r)
denotes the volume of a geodesic ball of radius
r
in the
N
-dimensional model space of constant curvature
K
(see (
4.1
)), star-shaped refers to sets that are radially generated from a point (see Definition
5.1
), and
\theta_{N}(\cdot)
denotes the Bishop-Gromov density (see (
2.1
)).

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
K\in\mathbb{R},N\in(1,\infty),p>N/2
, there exists an explicit non-decreasing positive function
\left(0,\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{N-1}{K^{+}}}\right)\ni R\mapsto C_{K,N,p}(R)
(see (
5.17
)) such that the following holds. Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be an essentially non-branching
{\sf CD}(k,N)
for some
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible. Suppose that
x\in{\rm X}
is such that
\theta_{1+c}(x)<\infty
for some
c>0
and let
T\subset{\rm X}
be a star-shaped set at
x
. Then, for
0<r\leq R
, it holds

(1.2)

\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x)\cap T)}{v_{K,N}(R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}-\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x)\cap T)}{v_{K,N}(r)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\leq C_{K,N,p}(R)\rho^{k}_{p}(T,K)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}.

Moreover, if
T=B_{R}(x)
, it holds

(1.3)

\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))\leq\left(\theta_{N}(x)+C_{K,N,p}(R)\rho^{k}_{p}(B_{R}(x),K)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\right)^{2p-1}v_{K,N}(R).

Finally, the function
C_{K,N,p}(R)
is globally bounded if
K<0
and satisfies

(1.4)

C_{0,N,p}(R)=\left(\frac{N-1}{(2p-1)(2p-N)}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2p-1}}R^{\frac{2p-N}{2p-1}}.

The above statement is a Bishop-Gromov type comparison result that quantifies, in terms of the integral curvature deficit
\rho_{p}^{k}(B_{R}(x),K)
, how close the function

(0,R)\ni r\mapsto\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}{v_{K,N}(r)}

is to be monotone (which is the case if
k\geq K
and the integral curvature deficit vanishes, see
[
62
,
63
]
). In particular, Theorem
1.1
extends the foundational manifold analysis of
[
56
,
55
]
to the setting of
{\sf CD}
spaces with variable Ricci curvature lower bounds.

The assumption on the finiteness of the Bishop-Gromov density at
x
is needed to deal with potentially too collapsed spaces. This is ultimately linked to the validity of one-dimensional mean curvature comparison estimate for nonsmooth densities, carried out in Proposition
4.3
in the spirit of
[
56
,
55
,
4
]
.
See Remark
5.8
for a detailed discussion.

An analogous result has been obtained in various (smooth) settings in
[
69
,
70
,
44
,
25
]
with integral Bakry-Émery Ricci bounds. We remark that, in all of these works, assumptions playing the same role as
\theta_{1+c}(x)<\infty
are required. Our condition is substantially weaker and automatically satisfied, for example, in the non-collapsed setting.

We next present our second main result, namely a Myers’ diameter theorem for nonsmooth spaces with sufficiently small integrable curvature deficit.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
N\in[2,\infty),K>0
and
p>N/2
there exists a constant
C_{K,N,p}>0
such that the following holds. Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be an essentially non-branching
{\sf CD}(k,N)
for some
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible. Assume that
\theta_{1+c_{x}}(x)<\infty
for some
c_{x}>0
at
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in{\rm X}
. If
\rho^{k}_{p}({\rm X},K)<+\infty
then
\mathfrak{m}({\rm X})<\infty
. Moreover, if

\rho^{k}_{p}({\rm X},K)\leq\frac{\mathfrak{m}({\rm X})}{C_{K,N,p}},

then
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is compact and it holds

(1.5)

{\rm diam}({\rm X})\leq\pi\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{K}}\left(1+C_{K,N,p}\left(\frac{\rho^{k}_{p}({\rm X},K)}{\mathfrak{m}({\rm X})}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}}\right).

The above statement can be regarded as a quantified version of the celebrated Myers’ diameter theorem: if the averaged integral curvature deficit is sufficiently small, then the space is compact and the maximal diameter estimate (
1.5
) holds up to an error controlled by the deficit. Our result extends previous works
[
61
,
4
]
on Riemannian manifolds to the nonsmooth setting; see also
[
68
,
44
]
for related generalizations in possibly weighted frameworks. Theorem
1.2
is proved as a byproduct of Theorem
6.8
where also the case
N\in(1,2)
is treated. In this dimension range, the estimate (
1.5
) trivializes as
N\downarrow 1
as expected, see Remark
6.7
for more details. As before, the assumption
\theta_{1+c_{x}}(x)<\infty
is linked to the estimates developed in Proposition
4.3
.

We present our final main result. For
\Omega\subset{\rm X}
open, the
p
-Dirichlet eigenvalue is defined as

(1.6)

\lambda_{p}(\Omega)\coloneqq\inf_{u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|Du|_{p}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}},

where
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\coloneqq\overline{{\rm Lip}_{bs}(\Omega)}
denotes the Sobolev space with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. Here, the closure is with respect the norm
\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p}\coloneqq\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}^{p}+\||Du|_{p}\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}^{p}
and
|Du|_{p}
is the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient (see
[
3
,
34
]
). In the statement below,
\lambda_{p}(K,N,r)
is the
p
-Dirichlet eigenvalue in the one-dimensional
(K,N)
-model, as defined in (
7.1
).

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
K\in\mathbb{R},N\in(1,\infty),p_{0}>N/2,p\in(1,\infty)
and
r\in\left(0,\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{N-1}{K^{+}}}\right)
, there exist
\varepsilon\coloneqq\varepsilon_{K,N,r,p,p_{0}}>0
and
C_{K,N,r,p,p_{0}}>0
such that the following holds. Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be an essentially non-branching
{\sf CD}(k,N)
for some
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible. Set
\bar{p}=\max\{p/2,p_{0}\}
and suppose, for
x\in{\rm X}
with
\theta_{1+c}(x)<\infty
for some
c>0
, that

(1.7)

\frac{\rho^{k}_{\bar{p}}(B_{r}(x),K)}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}<\varepsilon.

Then, it holds

\lambda_{p}(B_{r}(x))\leq\lambda_{p}(K,N,r)+C_{K,N,r,p,p_{0}}\left(\frac{\rho^{k}_{\bar{p}}(B_{r}(x),K)}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\bar{p}-1}}.

This theorem extends Cheng’s eigenvalue comparison to the nonsmooth setting under a smallness condition on the averaged integral curvature deficit. The case
p=2
was first established in
[
55
]
, and later extended to all
p\in(1,\infty)
in
[
60
]
. In the nonsmooth setting, a Cheng’s theorem for
2
-eigenvalues was recently obtained in
[
27
]
. In contrast to Theorem
1.3
, their focus is on rigidity/stability statements for nonsmooth spaces admitting a constant Ricci lower bound.

The localization method.
The localization method is a central tool in all our results. Originally developed in nonsmooth settings in
[
18
]
, after earlier the work
[
42
]
on manifolds, it has since been widely applied, most notably to show the Lévy-Gromov isoperimetric inequality in the same work
[
18
]
and to derive new results in geometric analysis, see e.g.
[
19
,
16
,
32
,
22
]
. More recently, it has been adapted to novel contexts, such as nonsmooth Lorentzian geometry
[
21
,
5
]
.

The classical works
[
56
,
55
,
4
]
in the Riemannian setting reduce the problem to a one-dimensional analysis via polar coordinates. In low-regularity spaces, localization can play naturally an analogous role to nonsmooth polar coordinates. Indeed, to show Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
, it suffices to localize the curvature-dimension condition along transport rays associated with the distance from a point. This follows by a more general localization result, cf. Theorem
3.6
, which holds for arbitrary
1
-Lipschitz functions.
Thus, our statement fully extends the analysis of
[
18
, Thm. 5.1]
and requires no integrability assumption on the variable Ricci bound
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
.

It is relevant to point out a technical aspect of our applications that, differently from
[
18
]
, we encounter here. The localization is capable of acting as a one-dimensional reduction method in the case of constant Ricci lower bounds; see for instance
[
19
]
where many functional comparison inequalities are derived in this way. On the other hand, even if the spirit of the present paper is the same, this idea does not completely carry over to the variable Ricci curvature lower bound setting. Indeed, examining for instance Theorem
1.1
, knowing that inequality (
1.2
) holds on weighted intervals does not directly imply that it holds on
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
via disintegration of the reference measure
\mathfrak{m}
. This is because the reminder term containing the integral curvature deficit does not reintegrate to (
1.1
) due to a non-linear power
1/(2p-1)
(indeed, it is concave, while convexity would be needed to reintegrate). Consequently, the one-dimensional analysis needs to be combined with reintegration procedures, and this poses technical obstacles due to the nonsmooth ambient space (cf. Proposition
4.3
and Lemmas
5.6
and
5.3
).

Finally, we believe that the localization method in Theorem
3.6
is an ingredient that can be potentially applicable to study further comparison results requiring novel one-dimensional analysis where polar coordinates might not be the natural choice, e.g. Laplacian and isoperimetric comparisons and spectral inequalities. These will be the object of future investigations.

2.
Preliminaries

We start setting up some notation. Given
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we set
a\wedge b\coloneqq\min\{a,b\}
while
a\vee b\coloneqq\max\{a,b\}
and
a^{+}\coloneqq a\vee 0,a^{-}\coloneqq a\wedge 0
. A metric measure space is a triple
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
where
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is a complete and separable metric space and
\mathfrak{m}
is a non-negative, non-zero and boundedly finite Borel measure.

We denote by
C([0,1],{\rm X})
the space of continuous curves with values in
{\rm X}
, which is a complete and separable metric space when endowed with the supremum distance. We say that
\gamma\in C([0,1],{\rm X})
is a metric
{\sf d}
-geodesic provided
{\sf d}(\gamma_{t},\gamma_{s})=|t-s|{\sf d}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})
for all
t,s\in[0,1]
. We denote by
{\rm Geo}({\rm X})\subset C([0,1],{\rm X})
the set of metric
{\sf d}
-geodesics, which is a closed subset of
C([0,1],{\rm X})
, hence complete. We denote by
\ell(\gamma)\in[0,\infty]
the length of a curve
\gamma\in C([0,1],{\rm X})
and, if
\gamma\in{\rm Geo}({\rm X})
, we have
\ell(\gamma)={\sf d}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})
. Finally, given a function
f\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma\in{\rm Geo}({\rm X})
, we define
{f_{\gamma}^{+}}=f_{\gamma}\coloneqq f\circ\hat{\gamma}
, where
\hat{\gamma}\colon[0,\ell(\gamma)]\to{\rm X}
is the arc-length parametrization of
\gamma
. Similarly
f_{\gamma}^{-}\coloneqq f\circ\hat{\gamma}^{-}
for
\hat{\gamma}^{-}_{t}\coloneqq\hat{\gamma}_{1-t}
.

By
C({\rm X})
,
{\rm Lip}({\rm X})
and
{\rm Lip}_{bs}({\rm X})
, we denote respectively the collection of continuous, Lipschitz and boundedly supported Lipschitz functions on
{\rm X}
. For all
p\in(1,\infty)
, we denote by
L^{p}(\mathfrak{m}),L^{p}_{loc}(\mathfrak{m})
respectively the space of
p
-integrable functions and
p
-integrable functions on a neighborhood of every point (up to
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. equality relation) on
{\rm X}
. By
\mathcal{P}({\rm X}),\mathcal{P}_{2}({\rm X})
we respectively denote the set of Borel probability measures and Borel probability measures with finite
2
-moment. The space
\mathcal{P}_{2}({\rm X})
is a metric space when endowed with the Wasserstein
2
-distance
W_{2}
. We refer to
[
2
]
for a detailed treatment. In addition, given
N\geq 1
, we denote the Bishop-Gromov density at
x\in{\rm X}
by

(2.1)

\theta_{N}(x)\coloneqq\varlimsup_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}\in[0,\infty],\qquad\text{
where }\omega_{N}\coloneqq\pi^{N/2}\Gamma(N/2+1)^{-1}.

2.1.
Metric measure spaces with variable Ricci bounds

Let
\kappa\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous. We define the generalized sine function
{\mathfrak{s}}_{\kappa}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
as the unique function
v
that solves

(2.2)

v^{\prime\prime}+\kappa v=0,

with initial conditions
v(a)=0
,
v^{\prime}(a)=1
. Recall that, for
K\in\mathbb{R},N>1
,
a=0
,
b=\pi\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{K^{+}}}
and
\left[0,\pi\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{K^{+}}}\right]\ni t\mapsto\kappa(t)\equiv K/(N-1)
constant, we have

{\mathfrak{s}}_{\kappa}(t)=\sin_{K/(N-1)}(t)=\begin{cases}\,\sin\left(t\sqrt{\frac{K}{N-1}}\right)&\text{if }K>0,\\
\,t&\text{if }K=0,\\
\,\sinh\left(t\sqrt{\frac{-K}{N-1}}\right)&\text{if }K<0.\end{cases}

Definition 2.1

(Distortion coefficients)
.

Let
\kappa:[0,{L}]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be continuous and let
\theta\in(0,L]
,
t\in[0,1]
.
Then, the distortion coefficient is defined as

(2.3)

\sigma_{\kappa}^{{(t)}}(\theta)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{{\mathfrak{s}}_{{\kappa}}(t\theta)}{{\mathfrak{s}}_{{\kappa}}(\theta)}&\mbox{ if }{\mathfrak{s}}_{\kappa}|_{(0,\theta]}>c>0,\\
\infty&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Observe that, if
\sigma_{\kappa}^{{(t)}}(\theta)<\infty
for some
t
(hence, for all), then
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto\sigma_{\kappa}^{{(t)}}(\theta)
is a solution of

(2.4)

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}(t)+\kappa(t\theta)\theta^{2}u(t)=0

satisfying
u(0)=0
and
u(1)=1
.

Definition 2.2

(Admissible variable lower bound)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space. We say that a function
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
is
admissible
if it is locally bounded below and lower semi-continuous.

Given an admissible
k
, we define the increasing sequence of continuous functions
k_{n}\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(2.5)

k_{n}(x)\coloneqq\inf_{y\in{\rm X}}\left(k(y)+n{\sf d}(x,y)\right)\wedge n\leq k(x),\qquad\forall\,x\in{\rm X},\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Note that
k_{n}\uparrow k
pointwise as
n\uparrow\infty
. If
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}
is a geodesic of length
\theta\coloneqq\ell(\gamma)
, we can set

(2.6)

\sigma_{k_{\gamma}}^{(t)}(\theta)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{(k_{n})_{\gamma}}^{(t)}(\theta)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\},\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,1],

and, given
N>1
, we set
\sigma_{k_{\gamma},N-1}^{(t)}(\theta)\coloneqq\sigma_{k_{\gamma}/(N-1)}^{(t)}(\theta)
. Note that the definition (
2.6
) is well-posed, since the sequence is monotone non-decreasing.
Moreover,
(t,\theta)\mapsto\sigma_{(k_{n})_{\gamma}}^{(t)}(\theta)
is continuous, hence
(t,\theta)\mapsto\sigma_{k_{\gamma}}^{(t)}(\theta)
is lower semicontinuous. In the case
k
is already continuous, (
2.6
) is consistent with Definition
2.1
. Finally, this definition is independent of the monotone sequence
\{k_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
chosen in (
2.5
) if
\{k^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is another monotone sequence with the property that
\|k_{n}-k^{\prime}_{n}\|_{\infty}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. We refer to
[
40
]
for further details. It is possible to show that this is also the case when
k\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathfrak{m})
.

Definition 2.3

(Generalized distortion coefficients)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space,
N>1
and
k:{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
be admissible. Given a geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\rm X}
of length
\theta\coloneqq\ell(\gamma)
, we define the generalized distortion coefficients with respect to
k
and
N
along
\gamma
as

\displaystyle\tau_{k_{\gamma},N}^{(t)}(\theta)\coloneqq t^{\frac{1}{N}}\big[\sigma_{k_{\gamma},N-1}^{(t)}(\theta)\big]^{\frac{N-1}{N}},

for every
t\in[0,1]
, with the conventions
r\cdot\infty=\infty
for
r>0
and
0\cdot\infty=0
.

We assume the reader to be familiar with the basic concepts of optimal transport, such as Wasserstein distance and optimal geodesic plans (denoted as
{\rm OptGeo}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
for given marginals
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\rm X})
) referring, e.g., to
[
2
]
. Given
N>1
, define the
N
-
Rényi-entropy functional

S_{N}:\mathcal{P}_{2}({\rm X})\to[-\infty,0],\qquad S_{N}(\mu)\coloneqq-\int\rho^{1-\frac{1}{N}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m},

where
\mu=\rho\mathfrak{m}+\mu^{s}
and
\mu^{s}
is singular with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
. The evaluation map at time
t\in[0,1]
is defined as
{\rm{e}}_{t}:C([0,1],{\rm X})\to{\rm X}
such that
{\rm{e}}_{t}(\gamma)\coloneqq\gamma_{t}
. It is continuous, hence Borel.

We recall the definition of curvature dimension condition with variable bound, introduced in
[
40
]
.

Definition 2.4

(
{\sf CD}(k,N)
space)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space, let
k\colon{\rm X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be admissible and let
N>1
. We say that
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies the curvature dimension condition

{\sf CD}(k,N)
if for each pair
\mu_{0}=\rho_{0}\mathfrak{m},\mu_{1}=\rho_{1}\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\rm X})
with bounded support
there exists
{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}\in{\rm OptGeo}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
such that
\mu_{t}\coloneqq{({\rm{e}}_{t})}_{\sharp}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
and

(2.7)

S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{t})\leq{\textstyle-}\!\!\int\tau_{k^{-}_{\gamma},N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}({\sf d}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}))\rho_{0}\left({\rm{e}}_{0}(\gamma)\right)^{-\frac{1}{N^{\prime}}}+\tau_{k^{+}_{\gamma},N^{\prime}}^{(t)}({\sf d}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}))\rho_{1}\left({\rm{e}}_{1}(\gamma)\right)^{-\frac{1}{N^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}(\gamma),

for all
t\in[0,1]
and all
N^{\prime}\geq N
.

The definition of
{\sf CD}
space for
N=1
can be given as well, see
[
40
]
. We refrain to do it here, since all our results only hold for
N>1
.
If
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
is a
{\sf CD}(k,N)
space and
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible, then the Bishop-Gromov
N
-density in (
2.1
) exists at every point
x\in{\rm X}
. Indeed, since
k
is locally bounded from below, on a neighborhood of
x
it holds
k\geq K_{0}
for some
K_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus
r\mapsto\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))/v_{K_{0},N}(r)
is non-increasing on a right neighborhood of zero (cf.
[
40
, Thm. 5.9]
) and
\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{v_{K_{0},N}(r)}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}=1
.

We conclude this section showing that
{\sf CD}
spaces with variable Ricci curvature lower bounds are qualitatively non-degenerate, cf.
[
15
,
37
]
. Given
A,B\subset{\rm X}
and
t\in[0,1]
, the set of
t
-midpoints is

(2.8)

M_{t}(A,B)\coloneqq\{{\rm{e}}_{t}(\gamma):\gamma\in{\rm Geo}({\rm X})\text{ such that }\gamma_{0}\in A,\gamma_{1}\in B\}.

We also define, for
K\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.9)

\Theta_{K}(A,B)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\inf_{x\in A,y\in B}{\sf d}(x,y),&\text{if }K\geq 0,\\
\sup_{x\in A,y\in B}{\sf d}(x,y),&\text{if }K<0.\end{cases}

The following proof is an adaptation of
[
47
, Cor. 3.6]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a
{\sf CD}(k,N)
space for some
N>1
and
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible. For every ball
B_{R}(p)\subset{\rm X}
, set
K_{0}\coloneqq\min\{k(x)\colon x\in\overline{B_{2R}(p)}\}
. Then, if
A\subset B_{R}(p)
and
x\in B
, it holds

(2.10)

\mathfrak{m}(M_{t}(A,x))\geq\tau_{K_{0},N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta_{K_{0}}(A,x))^{N}\,\mathfrak{m}(A),\qquad\forall\,t\in(0,1).

In particular,
\mathfrak{m}
is qualitatively non-degenerate in the sense of
[
15
]
.

Proof.

Fix a ball
B:=B_{R}(p)
. Consider a Borel set
A\subset B
and
x\in B
. We can assume without loss of generality that
\mathfrak{m}(A)>0
(as, if
\mathfrak{m}(A)=0
, there is nothing to prove). We would like to apply the Brunn-Minkowski inequality in
{\sf CD}(k,N)
obtained in
[
40
, Thm. 5.1]
with
A_{0}=A,A_{1}=\{x\}
, but
\mathfrak{m}(A_{1})
is required to be of positive measure. We thus perform a standard approximation step. Fix any
t\in(0,1)
. Let us assume
A_{0}\coloneqq A
to be compact and
\epsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
, set
A_{1,n}\coloneqq B_{\epsilon_{n}}(x)
so that, for
n
large enough,
A_{1,n}\subset B
. Then, by the argument of
[
40
, Thm. 5.1]
, there is a
{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_{n}\in{\rm OptGeo}\big(\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{A_{0}}}{\mathfrak{m}(A_{0})},\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{A_{1,n}}}{\mathfrak{m}(A_{1,n})}\big)
satisfying the curvature dimension condition such that:

(2.11)

\mathfrak{m}({\rm supp}\big(({\rm{e}}_{t})_{\sharp}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_{n})^{\frac{1}{N}}\geq\inf_{\gamma\in{\rm Geo}(A_{0},A_{1,n})}\tau^{(1-t)}_{k_{\gamma}^{-},N}({\sf d}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}))\,\mathfrak{m}(A_{0})^{\frac{1}{N}}\geq\tau^{(1-t)}_{K_{0},N}(\Theta_{K_{0}}(A_{0},A_{1,n}))\,\mathfrak{m}(A_{0})^{\frac{1}{N}},

for all
t\in(0,1)
, having used the monotonicity of
\tau
coefficients
[
40
, Cor. 3.11]
in the last inequality and the definition of
\Theta_{K_{0}}
. Set

A_{t}\coloneqq\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{k\geq n}{\rm supp}\big(({\rm{e}}_{t})_{\sharp}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}_{k}\big).

We claim that

A_{t}\subset M_{t}(A,x).

Let
y\in A_{t}
and, by definition, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there are geodesics
\gamma^{h_{n}}\in{\rm Geo}(A_{0},A_{1,h_{n}})
so that
\gamma_{t}^{h_{n}}=y
with
h_{n}\uparrow\infty
. By Ascoli-Arzelá (all curves are constant speed geodesics contained in
2B
which has compact closure, since
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is proper, according to
[
40
, Thm. 5.3]
), there is a geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\rm X}
which is a uniform limit of
\gamma^{h_{n}}
. The geodesic
\gamma
satisfies
\gamma_{t}=y,\gamma_{0}\in A_{0}
(since
A_{0}
is assumed compact) and
\gamma_{1}=x
. This proves the claim and moreover, using that
\Theta_{K_{0}}(A_{0},A_{1,n})\to\Theta_{K_{0}}(A,x)
, it holds

(2.12)

\mathfrak{m}(M_{t}(A,x))^{\frac{1}{N}}\geq\tau^{(1-t)}_{K_{0},N}(\Theta_{K_{0}}(A,x))\,\mathfrak{m}(A)^{\frac{1}{N}},

provided
A
is compact. However, (
2.12
) also holds for an arbitrary Borel set
A\subset B
by approximation with compact sets
A_{n}\subset A
with
\mathfrak{m}(A_{n})\uparrow\mathfrak{m}(A)
(by inner regularity) and using
M_{t}(A_{n},x)\subset M_{t}(A,x)
and
\tau^{(1-t)}_{K_{0},N}(\Theta_{K_{0}}(A_{n},x))\geq\tau^{(1-t)}_{K_{0},N}(\Theta_{K_{0}}(A,x))
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Finally, to conclude that
\mathfrak{m}
is qualitatively non-degenerate, we observe that if
K_{0}>0
, then
\Theta_{K_{0}}(A,x)\geq 0
and
\tau^{(1-t)}_{K_{0},N}(\Theta_{K_{0}}(A,x))\geq(1-t)^{N}
, while if
K_{0}\leq 0
, then
\Theta_{K_{0}}(A,x)\leq 2R
and we have
\tau^{(1-t)}_{K_{0},N}(\Theta_{K_{0}}(A,x))\geq\tau^{(1-t)}_{K_{0},N}(2R)^{N}
. Hence, we have that

(2.13)

\mathfrak{m}(M_{t}(A,x))\geq f_{R,p}(t)\,\mathfrak{m}(A),\qquad\forall\,t\in(0,1),

with
f_{R,p}(t)\coloneqq\tau^{(1-t)}_{K_{0}^{+},N}(2R)^{N}
. The proof is therefore concluded recalling
[
15
, Assumption 1]
.
∎

2.2.
Non-branching and existence of transport maps

We recall the definition of an essentially non-branching metric measure space.

Definition 2.6
.

Let

\gamma^{1}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[0,t]}$}=\gamma^{2}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[0,t]}$}

\gamma^{1}=\gamma^{2}

\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})

\nu\in{\rm OptGeo}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})

.

By a combination of Proposition
2.5
with
[
37
, Thm. 5.8, Cor. 5.9]
, we deduce the existence and uniqueness of optimal transport plans.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let

be an essentially non-branching

admissible and

. Then, for any

with

there is a unique optimal transport plan with respect to

and it is induced by a map.

The above proposition implies the following equivalent characterization of the
{\sf CD}
condition. The proof can be done as
[
35
, Prop. 3.1]
, and
[
63
]
taking into account the necessary modifications.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})

and

{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}\in{\rm OptGeo}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})

(2.14)

\rho_{t}(\gamma_{t})^{-\frac{1}{N}}\geq\tau_{k^{-}_{\gamma},N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}({\sf d}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}))\rho_{0}\left(\gamma_{0}\right)^{-\frac{1}{N^{\prime}}}+\tau_{k^{+}_{\gamma},N^{\prime}}^{(t)}({\sf d}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}))\rho_{1}\left(\gamma_{1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{N^{\prime}}}

.

3.
Localization of variable Ricci curvature bounds

In this section, we show that a variable Ricci lower bound can be localized to the rays of a disintegration of
\mathfrak{m}
relative to any 1-Lipschitz function. We adapt the strategy for the case of constant curvature bound developed in
[
13
,
18
]
(for the spaces of finite total mass) and in
[
20
]
(for spaces with infinite mass).

3.1.
Disintegration of the reference measure

Let
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
1
-Lipschitz function. We denote by
\pi_{1}\colon{\rm X}\times{\rm X}\to{\rm X}
as
\pi_{1}(x,y)\coloneqq x
.
We define
\Gamma_{u}=\{(x,y):u(x)-u(y)={\sf d}(x,y)\}
,
\Gamma^{-1}_{u}\coloneqq\{(x,y):(y,x)\in\Gamma_{u}\}
and
R_{u}\coloneqq\Gamma_{u}\cup\Gamma_{u}^{-1}
. Moreover, we denote by
\Gamma_{u}(x)\coloneqq\{y\in{\rm X}:(x,y)\in\Gamma_{u}\}
and, similarly,
\Gamma^{-1}_{u}(x)\coloneqq\{y\in{\rm X}:(x,y)\in\Gamma^{-1}_{u}\}
. We call
R_{u}
the transport relation. We recall the definition of transport set with endpoints
T_{u}\coloneqq\pi_{1}\big(R_{u}\setminus\{(x,y)\in{\rm X}\times{\rm X}:x=y\}\big)
.
We say that, given
x,y\in T_{u}
,
x\sim y
if and only if
(x,y)\in R_{u}
. This relation is reflexive and symmetric but not transitive in general. Thus, we define the set of forward and backward branching points as

(3.1)

A^{+}\coloneqq\{x\in T_{u}:\exists y,z\in\Gamma_{u}(x),(y,z)\notin R_{u}\},\quad A^{-}\coloneqq\{x\in T_{u}:\exists y,z\in\Gamma_{u}^{-1}(x),(y,z)\notin R_{u}\}.

Consider, respectively, the non-branched transport set and the non-branched transport relation

(3.2)

T_{u}^{nb}\coloneqq T_{u}\setminus(A^{+}\cup A^{-}),\qquad R_{u}^{nb}\coloneqq R_{u}\cap(T_{u}^{nb}\times T_{u}^{nb}).

As shown in
[
13
]
,
R_{u}^{nb}
is an equivalence relation on
T_{u}^{nb}
and for every
x\in T_{u}^{nb}
,
R_{u}(x)\coloneqq\{y\in{\rm X}:(x,y)\in R_{u}\}
is isometric to a closed interval of
\mathbb{R}
. From the non-branched transport relation we obtain a partition of the non-branched transport set
T_{u}^{nb}
into a disjoint family
\{{\rm X}_{q}\}_{q\in Q}
of sets, where
Q
is a set of indices. Moreover,
\bar{\rm X}_{q}
is isometric to a closed interval of
\mathbb{R}
, for any
q\in Q
. We define the quotient map
\mathfrak{Q}\colon T_{u}^{nb}\to Q
as

(3.3)

q=\mathfrak{Q}(x)\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad x\in{\rm X}_{q}.

and we endow
Q
with the pushforward
\sigma
-algebra, that is the finest
\sigma
-algebra on
Q
for which
\mathfrak{Q}
is measurable. We also define the quotient measure
\mathfrak{q}\coloneqq\mathfrak{Q}_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}
.
As proven in
[
12
,
13
]
, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space and let
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
1
-Lipschitz function. Define
T_{u}^{nb}
,
Q
,
\mathfrak{Q}
,
\mathfrak{q}
as before.
Then, there exists a disintegration of
\mathfrak{m}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{T_{u}^{nb}}$}
, namely a family
\{\mathfrak{m}_{q}\}_{q\in Q}
such that, for every
A\in\mathscr{B}({\rm X})
,
Q\ni q\mapsto\mathfrak{m}_{q}(A)\in\mathbb{R}
is
\mathfrak{q}
-measurable and

\mathfrak{m}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{T_{u}^{nb}}$}=\int_{Q}\mathfrak{m}_{q}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{q}.

Additionally, the disintegration is strongly consistent, i.e.
\mathfrak{m}_{q}(\mathfrak{Q}^{-1}(q))=1
,
\mathfrak{q}
-a.e.
q\in Q
.

The family
\{\mathfrak{m}_{q}\}_{q\in Q}
is
\mathfrak{q}
-essentially unique, meaning that, if there is another family
\{\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{q}\}_{q\in Q}
of probability measures satisfying the above properties, then
\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{q}=\mathfrak{m}_{q}
for
\mathfrak{q}
-a.e.
q\in Q
.

For a general metric measure space, the non-branched transport set
T_{u}^{nb}
can be much smaller than
T_{u}
, i.e.
\mathfrak{m}(T_{u}\setminus T_{u}^{nb})>0
. The following proposition gives sufficient conditions under which this behavior can be excluded.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
14
, Prop. 4.5]
)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space such that for any
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}({\rm X})
with
\mu_{0}\ll\mathfrak{m}
any optimal transference plan for
W_{2}
is induced by a map. Then
\mathfrak{m}(A_{+})=\mathfrak{m}(A_{-})=0
. In particular,
\mathfrak{m}(T_{u}\setminus T_{u}^{nb})=0
.

Since, for essentially non-branching
{\sf CD}(k,N)
spaces, every optimal plan is induced by a map, cf. Proposition
2.7
, we easily obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be an essentially non-branching
{\sf CD}(k,N)
space for some
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible and
N>1
. Then, it holds
\mathfrak{m}(A^{+})=\mathfrak{m}(A^{-})=0
. In particular
\mathfrak{m}(T_{u}\setminus T_{u}^{nb})=0
.

3.2.
Regularity of the disintegration

Next, we discuss the regularity of conditional measures
\mathfrak{m}_{q}
, namely their absolute continuity with respect to the one-dimensional Hausdorff measure restricted to
{\rm X}_{q}
. Firstly, consider the set
S\coloneqq\{(q,t,x)\in Q\times[0,\infty)\times T_{u}^{nb}\colon(q,x)\in\Gamma_{u},\,u(q)-u(x)=t\}\cup\{(q,t,x)\in Q\times(-\infty,0]\times T_{u}^{nb}\colon(x,q)\in\Gamma_{u},\,u(x)-u(q)=t\}
. Then, we define the ray map

(3.4)

g\colon{\rm Dom}(g)\subset Q\times\mathbb{R}\to T_{u}^{nb},

as the map whose graph in
Q\times\mathbb{R}\times T_{u}^{nb}
is given by the set
S
, namely such that
{\rm graph}(g)=S
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be an essentially non-branching
{\sf CD}(k,N)
space for some
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible and
N>1
. Given a
1
-Lispchitz function
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
, consider the disintegration
\{\mathfrak{m}_{q}\}_{q\in Q}
of
\mathfrak{m}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{T_{u}^{nb}}$}
relative to
u
. Then, for
{\mathfrak{q}}
-a.e.
q\in Q
, the conditional measure
\mathfrak{m}_{q}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
g(q,\cdot)_{\sharp}\mathcal{L}^{1}
.

We omit the proof of this proposition as it follows the same arguments of the case of constant lower curvature bounds. In particular, one replicates the proof of
[
13
, Thm. 6.6]
, taking into account Propositions
2.5
and
2.7
.

Remark 3.5
.

As observed in
[
14
, Prop. 4.12]
, for every
q\in Q
, the map
t\mapsto g(q,t)
is an isometry between
{\rm Dom}(g(q,\cdot))
and
{\rm X}_{q}
. Moreover,
\overline{{\rm Dom}(g(q,\cdot))}=[0,r_{q}]
, where
r_{q}\coloneqq\ell({\rm X}_{q})\in[0,\infty]
. Since, by Proposition
3.4
,
\mathfrak{m}_{q}\ll g(q,\cdot)_{\sharp}\mathcal{L}^{1}
, for
\mathfrak{q}
a.e.
q\in Q
, there exists a Borel function
h_{q}:[0,r_{q}]\to[0,\infty]
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{q}=g(q,\cdot)_{\sharp}(h_{q}\mathcal{L}^{1})
.

\blacksquare

3.3.
Localization of variable curvature bounds

We finally show that the rays of the disintegration
\{\mathfrak{m}_{q}\}_{q\in Q}
of
\mathfrak{m}
relative to a
1
-Lipschitz function inherit the variable Ricci lower bound. The proof follows the same lines of
[
18
, Thm. 4.2]
and we outline the main differences. In the statement below, recall the definition of
h_{q}
and
r_{q}
from Remark
3.5
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be an essentially non-branching
{\sf CD}(k,N)
space for some
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible and
N>1
.
Given a
1
-Lispchitz function
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
, consider the disintegration
\{\mathfrak{m}_{q}\}_{q\in Q}
of
\mathfrak{m}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{T_{u}^{nb}}$}
relative to
u
. Then, for
{\mathfrak{q}}
-a.e.
q

([0,r_{q}],|\cdot|,h_{q}\mathcal{L}^{1})

{\sf CD}(k\circ g(q,\cdot),N)

Proof.

We subdivide the proof into different steps.
We recall by
Proposition

3.4
that
\mathfrak{m}_{q}\ll g(q,\cdot)_{\sharp}\mathcal{L}^{1}
for
\mathfrak{q}
-a.e.
q

Step

, it is not restrictive to prove the statement for
{\mathfrak{q}}
-a.e.

Consider any

A_{s}\coloneqq(1-s)A_{0}+sA_{1},\qquad L_{s}\coloneqq(1-s)L_{0}+sL_{1},

2. Fix

{\mathfrak{q}}

for all

and for

-a.e.

, where

is defined as the image of

-a.e.

\displaystyle L_{s}^{\frac{1}{N}}h_{q}(A_{s}+\tau L_{s})^{\frac{1}{N}}

\displaystyle\qquad+s^{\frac{1}{N}}\sigma^{(s)}_{{k^{+}_{\gamma^{t_{0}}}},N}(A_{1}-A_{0}+\tau(L_{1}-L_{0}))^{\frac{N-1}{N}}L_{1}^{\frac{1}{N}}h_{q}(A_{1}+\tau L_{1})^{\frac{1}{N}},

holds for

-a.e.

and for all

. We now choose, without relabeling it, a continuous representative of

{\mathfrak{q}}
-a.e.

4.

For

\left(\left[0,\pi\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{K^{+}}}\,\right],|\cdot|,h_{K,N}\mathcal{L}^{1}\right),\qquad\text{where }h_{K,N}\coloneqq\sin_{K/(N-1)}^{N-1}.

We define the mean curvature in the

noting that this is well-defined since the denominator is strictly positive. For the next definition, we refer to Appendix

Definition 4.1

(Mean curvature deficit)
.

Fix
K\in\mathbb{R},N\in(1,\infty)
and

of

\psi(r)\coloneqq\left((\log h)^{\prime}(r)-H_{K,N}(r)\right)\vee 0,\qquad\text{a.e.\ }r\in(0,D).

Remark 4.2
.

Note that
\psi(t)
is a.e. well-defined since

is locally Lipschitz (cf. Lemma
A.2
). From this, we have
\psi\in L^{p}_{loc}([0,D))
, for every

. Indeed, letting

be a compact interval, there is
K_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

on

. Then, from

so that

a.e. on

. On the other hand, the behavior of

as

is independent of

is uniformly bounded close enough to zero.

\blacksquare

5.1.

In this section we shall prove our main result Theorem

. We start with some technical results around the disintegration relative to
{\sf d}_{x}
.

Definition 5.1

(Star-shaped set)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space. We say that a Borel set
T\subset{\rm X}
is star-shaped at
x\in T
, provided that, for every
y\in T
, there exists a geodesic
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto\gamma_{t}\in{\rm X}
so that
\gamma_{0}=x,\gamma_{1}=y
and
\gamma_{t}\in T
for every
t\in[0,1]
.

Our first result is a polar coordinate formula via the localization associated to
u{\sf d}_{x}
. In this case,
T_{u}={\rm X}
and
\mathfrak{m}({\rm X}\setminus T_{u}^{nb})=0
. In addition, for every
q\in Q
, the ray map
t\mapsto g(q,t)
is an isometry between its domain (which always contain
0
) and
{\rm X}_{q}
. We refer to Section
3
for details.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be an essentially non-branching
{\sf CD}(k,N)

N>1
and
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible. Let

be an open star-shaped set at some

, and consider the disintegration relative to
{\sf d}_{x}
. Then, for all
s<r
and

Borel and integrable, it holds

\int_{(B_{r}(x)\setminus B_{s}(x))\cap T}\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}=\int_{s}^{r}\int_{Q_{T}(t)}\varphi(g(q,t))h_{q}(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}{\mathfrak{q}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t,

where

,

are defined in Remark

and
Q_{T}(r)\coloneqq\{q\in Q:r\in[0,r_{q}\wedge E_{T}^{q})\}
. Here,

from

, namely

E_{T}^{q}\coloneqq\sup\{t>0:g(q,t)\in T\}.

, whenever

Proof.

Firstly, we claim that, for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.

is
\mathfrak{q}
-measurable. Indeed, since

is a Borel set and the map

-measurable, the map
q\mapsto r_{q}\wedge E_{T}^{q}
is
\mathfrak{q}
-measurable as well. Thus

S\coloneqq\{(q,s)\in Q\times\mathbb{R}:\,0<s<r_{q}\wedge E_{T}^{q}\}

\mathfrak{q}\times\mathcal{L}^{1}
-measurable and, by Fubini’s theorem, for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.

is
\mathfrak{q}
-measurable, proving the claim. In addition, this shows that the right-hand side of (

) is well-defined. Secondly, we show that

whenever
r_{q}>0
. Indeed, let
q\in Q
such that
r_{q}>0
. Then, since

is open and
g(q,0)=x
, for

and
g(q,\cdot)\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[0,t]}$}\subset T

.
The proof of the identity (

t<r_{q},\quad g(q,t)\in(B_{r}(x)\setminus B_{s}(x))\cap T\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad t\in[s,r),\quad q\in Q_{T}(t).

{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{(B_{r}(x)\setminus B_{s}(x))\cap T}(g(q,t))={\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{[s,r)}(t){\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{Q_{T}(t)}(q),\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{q}\times\mathfrak{q}\text{-a.e..}

\displaystyle\int_{(B_{r}(x)\setminus B_{s}(x))\cap T}\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}\int_{\rm X}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{(B_{r}(x)\setminus B_{s}(x))\cap T}\cdot\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{q}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{(B_{r}(x)\setminus B_{s}(x))\cap T}(g(q,t))\varphi(g(q,t))h_{q}(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{[s,r)}(t){\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{Q_{T}(t)}(q)\varphi(g(q,t))h_{q}(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{Q}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{[s,r)}(t){\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{Q_{T}(t)}(q)\varphi(g(q,t))h_{q}(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}{\mathfrak{q}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t

having used the disintegration theorem (cf. Proposition

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})

5.2.
Proof of Bishop-Gromov comparison

We are in position to prove our first main result.

Proof of Theorem

.

Let us localize the problem using the localization method developed in

(5.13)

\begin{split}N(r)&=\int_{0}^{r}S_{T}(r)h_{K,N}(t)-S_{T}(t)h_{K,N}(r){\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{0}^{r}\left(\frac{S_{T}(r)}{h_{K,N}(r)}-\frac{S_{T}(t)}{h_{K,N}(t)}\right)h_{K,N}(r)h_{K,N}(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\\

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}\bigg(\frac{\pi}{2}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\tfrac{N-1}{K}}\vee t,r\bigg)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tfrac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{K}}\vee t}^{r}\left(\int_{Q_{T}(s)}h_{K,N}(s)^{\tfrac{4p-N-1}{N-1}}\psi_{q}(s)^{2p-1}h_{q}(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\frac{S_{T}(s)^{1-\frac{1}{2p-1}}}{h_{K,N}(s)^{1+\tfrac{4p-N-1}{(N-1)(2p-1)}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}(t,r)

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\left(\frac{V_{T}(t)}{v_{K,N}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}=\frac{1}{2p-1}\left(\frac{V_{T}(t)}{v_{K,N}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}-1}\frac{N(t)}{D(t)},\qquad\text{a.e.\ }t

5.3.
Consequences of Bishop-Gromov comparison

We deduce now uniform local doubling properties of the reference measure
\mathfrak{m}
provided the
scaling invariant integral curvature deficit
is sufficiently small. For a bounded star-shaped set
T
at
x\in{\rm X}
and such that
T\subset B_{R}(x)
for some
R>0
, the latter is the defined as

R^{2}\left(\frac{\rho^{k}_{p}(T,K)}{\mathfrak{m}(T)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The above quantity is indeed invariant for rescalings of both the distance
{\sf d}
and the measure
\mathfrak{m}
in the following sense: if
({\rm X}^{\prime},{\sf d}^{\prime},\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})\coloneqq({\rm X},\alpha\cdot{\sf d},\beta\cdot\mathfrak{m})
for

, then it holds that

where

and

. In particular, we have

(5.18)

R^{2}\left(\frac{\rho^{k}_{p}(T,K)}{\mathfrak{m}(T)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=(R^{\prime})^{2}\left(\frac{\int_{T}|\alpha^{-2}(k-K)\wedge 0|^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}(T)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=(R^{\prime})^{2}\left(\frac{\rho^{k/\alpha^{2}}_{p}(T,K/\alpha^{2})}{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}(T)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the latter integral deficit is computed with
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r_{2}}(z))}{r_{2}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}-\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r_{1}}(z))}{r_{1}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\,{r_{2}}^{\frac{2p-N}{2p-1}}\rho^{k}_{p}(B_{r_{2}}(z),0)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\,{r_{2}}^{\frac{2p-N}{2p-1}}\rho^{k}_{p}(B_{R}(x),0)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}

\displaystyle=\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\,{r_{2}}^{\frac{2p-N}{2p-1}}{R}^{\frac{2p}{2p-1}}\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}},

using the inclusion
B_{r_{2}}(z)\subset B_{R}(x)
. Rearranging the terms in the above inequality, we obtain

(5.23)

\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r_{2}}(z))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\leq\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r_{1}}(z))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\left(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\right)^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}+\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\left(\frac{r_{2}}{R}\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}.

We claim that inequality (
5.23
) holds for every
z\in B_{R}(x)
. Indeed, let
z\in B_{R}(x)
be a point where
\theta_{1+c}(z)=+\infty
for every
c>0
. Then, there exists a sequence
\{z_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to
z
and such that
\theta_{1+c_{n}}(z_{n})<\infty
for all
n
, for some
c_{n}>0
. In particular, we can apply (
5.23
) to the point
z_{n}
, so that for big enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

(5.24)

\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r_{2}}(z_{n}))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\leq\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r_{1}}(z_{n}))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\left(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\right)^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}+\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\left(\frac{r_{2}}{R}\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}.

Moreover, for a fixed
\rho>0
, we have that

\varlimsup_{y\to z}\left|\mathfrak{m}(B_{\rho}(y))-\mathfrak{m}(B_{\rho}(z))\right|\leq\varlimsup_{y\to x}\left|\int\chi_{B_{\rho}(y)}-\chi_{B_{\rho}(z)}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\right|=\mathfrak{m}(\{w\in{\rm X}:\,{\sf d}(w,z)=\rho\})=0,

where the last equality follows from
[
43
, Lemma 3.7]
(they assume that
\mathfrak{m}
is doubling, nonetheless
[
40
, Thm. 5.9]
is enough for our purposes). Thus,
the function
z\mapsto\mathfrak{m}(B_{\rho}(z))
is continuous.
By taking the limit in (
5.24
) we obtain (
5.23
), thus proving the claim.

We now prove (
5.22
) for every
y\in B_{R}(x)
and
r>0
with
{\sf d}(x,y)+r\leq R
, via a ball-chaining argument. Let
\eta\in(0,1)
to be chosen later. Set
L\coloneqq{\sf d}(x,y)
and let
\gamma\colon[0,L]\to{\rm X}
be a geodesic parametrized by arc-length such that
\gamma_{0}=y
,
\gamma_{L}=x
, and set
y_{1}\coloneqq y
,
R_{1}\coloneqq r
. Then, we define

y_{i}\coloneqq\gamma\left(\gamma^{-1}(y_{i-1})+(1-\eta)R_{i-1}\right),\qquad r_{i}\coloneqq\eta R_{i-1},\qquad R_{i}\coloneqq\left(2-\eta\right)^{i-1}r.

for all
2\leq i\leq M-1
, where
M\coloneqq\Big\lfloor\frac{\log\left(1+\frac{{\sf d}(x,y)}{r}\right)}{\log(2-\eta)}+1\Big\rfloor
. The iterative choices of
y_{i},r_{i},R_{i}
for
i=2,\dots,M-1
are done in such a way that the following three properties hold:

i)

the point
y_{i}
is chosen along the geodesic
\gamma
, such that
{\sf d}(y_{i},y_{i-1})=(1-\eta)R_{i-1}
;

ii)

R_{i}
is chosen to be the largest number such that
B_{R_{i}}(y_{i})\subset B_{R}(x)
;

iii)

r_{i}
is chosen to be the largest number such that
B_{r_{i}}(y_{i})\subset B_{R_{i-1}}(y_{i-1})
.

Lastly, we define
y_{M}\coloneqq x
,
r_{M}\coloneqq\eta R_{M-1}
,
R_{M}\coloneqq\left(2-\eta\right)^{M-1}r
. For every
i=2,\ldots,M
, by (
5.23
) applied with
z\coloneqq y_{i}
,
(r_{1},r_{2})\coloneqq(r_{i},R_{i})
, we obtain

(5.25)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{i}}(y_{i}))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}&\leq\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r_{i}}(y_{i}))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\left(\frac{2-\eta}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}+\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\left(\frac{R_{i}}{R}\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}},\\
&\leq\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{i-1}}(y_{i-1}))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\left(\frac{2-\eta}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}+\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\left(2-\eta\right)^{\frac{2p(i-1)}{2p-1}}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}},\end{split}

having used that
R_{i}=\frac{2-\eta}{\eta}r_{i}
for every
2\leq i\leq M
, and the fact that
B_{r_{i}}(y_{i})\subset B_{R_{i-1}}(y_{i-1})
, by our choice of radii. For the sake of notation, denote by

\xi\coloneqq\left(\frac{2-\eta}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\zeta\coloneqq(2-\eta)^{\frac{2p}{2p-1}}.

Iterating (
5.25
) starting from
i=M-1
, we deduce that

(5.26)

\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{M-1}}(y_{M-1}))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\leq\xi^{M-2}\left(\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(y))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}+\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\sum_{j=0}^{M-3}\left(\frac{\zeta}{\xi}\right)^{M-j-2}\right).

To estimate the sum appearing in (
5.26
), we distinguish two cases, according to the magnitude of
p
.

Case
p\in\big(\frac{N}{2},N\big)
.
In this range, we may choose
\eta
sufficiently close to
1
to ensure that

(5.27)

\frac{\zeta}{\xi}=\eta^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}(2-\eta)^{\frac{2p-N}{2p-1}}<1.

Therefore, the finite series appearing in (
5.26
) can be estimated as follows

\sum_{j=0}^{M-3}\left(\frac{\zeta}{\xi}\right)^{M-j-2}=\frac{1-\left(\frac{\zeta}{\xi}\right)^{M-1}}{1-\frac{\zeta}{\xi}}-1\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:assumption_parameter_eta}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{\zeta}{\xi}}.

In conclusion, from (
5.26
), we get the inequality

(5.28)

\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{M-1}}(y_{M-1}))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\leq\left(\frac{2-\eta}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{N(M-1)}{2p-1}}\Bigg(\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(y))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\\
+\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p-1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\frac{1}{1-\eta^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}\left(2-\eta\right)^{\frac{2p-N}{2p-1}}}\Bigg)

Case
p\geq N
. In this range, for the
\eta
fixed above, we have
\zeta\geq\xi
. Thus, we estimate the sum as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{M-3}\left(\frac{\zeta}{\xi}\right)^{M-j-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\frac{\zeta}{\xi}\right)^{M-1}-1}{\frac{\zeta}{\xi}-1}-1\leq\frac{\left(1+\frac{{\sf d}(x,y)}{r}\right)^{\frac{N\log_{2-\eta}\eta+2p-N}{2p-1}}-1}{\frac{\zeta}{\xi}-1}\leq\frac{\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\frac{N\log_{2-\eta}\eta+2p-N}{2p-1}}}{\frac{\zeta}{\xi}-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\frac{2p-N}{2p-1}}}{\frac{\zeta}{\xi}-1},

where, in the last step, we used that, since
\eta\in(0,1)
,
\log_{2-\eta}\eta<0
. Therefore, from (
5.26
), we get

(5.29)

\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{M-1}}(y_{M-1}))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\leq\left(\frac{2-\eta}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{N(M-2)}{2p-1}}\Bigg(\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(y))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\\
+\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\frac{1}{\eta^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}\left(2-\eta\right)^{\frac{2p-N}{2p-1}}-1}\Bigg)

Conclusion
. By (
5.28
) and (
5.29
), for every
p>\frac{N}{2}
, we deduce

(5.30)

\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{M-1}}(y_{M-1}))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\leq\left(\frac{2-\eta}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{N(M-2)}{2p-1}}\Bigg(\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(y))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}+\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\beta^{\prime\prime}_{N,p}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\Bigg),

where
\beta^{\prime\prime}_{N,p}\coloneqq\left|1-\eta^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}\left(2-\eta\right)^{\frac{2p-N}{2p-1}}\right|^{-1}
.
On the other hand, by Proposition
5.9
, choosing
B_{N,p}\leq A_{0,N,p}
(recall that
B_{r_{M}}(x)\subset B_{R_{M-1}}(y_{M-1})
,
y_{M}=x
and
r_{M}=\eta\left(2-\eta\right)^{M-2}r
), we estimate

(5.31)

\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{M-1}}(y_{M-1}))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\geq\eta^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}\left(2-\eta\right)^{\frac{N(M-2)}{2p-1}}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}\left(1-2\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\right).

Combining (
5.30
) and (
5.31
), we finally obtain the following bound

(5.32)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}\left(1-2\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\right)\leq\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{N(M-1)}{2p-1}}\Bigg(\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(y))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}+\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\beta^{\prime\prime}_{N,p}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\Bigg).

We now take
\eta
sufficiently close to
1
so that it satisfies
\log_{2-\eta}\eta\geq-2
as well. Hence, we deduce

\eta^{M-1}=(2-\eta)^{(M-1)\log_{2-\eta}\eta}\geq(2-\eta)^{-2(M-1)}\geq\left(1+\frac{{\sf d}(x,y)}{r}\right)^{-2}\geq\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}.

Therefore, we can continue from (
5.32
) to obtain

\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(y))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\geq\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{3N}{2p-1}}\left(1-2\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\right)-\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\beta^{\prime\prime}_{N,p}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{2N}{2p-1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}.

Choosing
\beta_{N,p}=\left(\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}\beta^{\prime\prime}_{N,p}\right)\vee 2\beta^{\prime}_{N,p}
, inequality (
5.22
) follows.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

Note that the inequality (
5.22
) holds whenever the scaling invariant curvature deficit is smaller than
B_{N,p}
. However, the ratio
\frac{r}{R}
can be extremely small (if, for example,
y
is close to the boundary of
B_{R}(x)
) and the right-hand side of (
5.22
) could be negative, making the statement trivial. We will use this in Proposition
6.5
, showing that the right-hand side is indeed strictly positive.

\blacksquare

6.
Myers’ diameter estimate

In this section, we show Theorem
1.2
as a byproduct of the more general statement Theorem
6.8
.

6.1.
Star-shaped partition

The aim of this section is to show the existence in
Proposition

6.3
of a countable partition, up to a negligible set, of a geodesic metric measure space into star-shaped sets.

We start by setting up some notations and proving two preliminary lemmas. Given a metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
and a countable collection
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, for every
i,j\in I
and
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

(6.1)

U_{i,j}^{\delta}\coloneqq\{x\in{\rm X}:\,{\sf d}(x,x_{i})-{\sf d}(x,x_{j})<\delta\},\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{i}^{\delta}=\bigcap_{j>i}U_{i,j}^{\delta}\cap\bigcap_{j<i}U_{i,j}^{-\delta}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space and let
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a countable collection of distinct points in
{\rm X}
. For every
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds

(6.2)

{\rm X}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I}T_{i}^{\delta}\subset\bigcup_{i,j\in I}\left\{x\in{\rm X}:{\sf d}(x,x_{i})-{\sf d}(x,x_{j})=\delta\right\}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the cardinality of
I
. If
\#I=2
, the claim follows directly from the definition of
T_{i}^{\delta}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let us assume that the statement holds true for
\#I=n-1
and we prove it for a collection of
n
points. We apply the induction hypothesis on
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}
. Denoting by

T_{i}^{\delta,n}\coloneqq\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>i\\
j\neq n\end{subarray}}U_{i,j}^{\delta}\cap\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}j<i\\
j\neq n\end{subarray}}U_{i,j}^{-\delta}=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>i\\
j\neq n\end{subarray}}U_{i,j}^{\delta}\cap\bigcap_{j<i}U_{i,j}^{-\delta},\qquad\forall\,i=1,\ldots,n-1,

we have that

{\rm X}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}T_{i}^{\delta,n}\subset\bigcup_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\left\{x\in{\rm X}:{\sf d}(x,x_{i})-{\sf d}(x,x_{j})=\delta\right\}.

Moreover, observe that the following relation holds:

T_{i}^{\delta}=T_{i}^{\delta,n}\cap U_{i,n}^{\delta},\qquad\forall\,i=1,\ldots,n-1.

Thus, by standard set operations, we deduce that

\displaystyle{\rm X}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}^{\delta}

\displaystyle=\left({\rm X}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}T_{i}^{\delta}\right)\cup({\rm X}\setminus T_{n}^{\delta})=\left({\rm X}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}(T_{i}^{\delta,n}\cap U_{i,n}^{\delta})\right)\cup({\rm X}\setminus T_{n}^{\delta})

\displaystyle=\left({\rm X}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}T_{i}^{\delta,n}\right)\cup\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n-1}(U_{i,n}^{\delta})^{c}\cap(T_{n}^{\delta})^{c}\right)

\displaystyle\subset\bigcup_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\left\{x\in{\rm X}:{\sf d}(x,x_{i})-{\sf d}(x,x_{j})=\delta\right\}\cup\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n-1}(U_{i,n}^{\delta})^{c}\cap(T_{n}^{\delta})^{c}\right).

Now, the conclusion of the proof follows if we prove the inclusion

(6.3)

\bigcap_{i=1}^{n-1}(U_{i,n}^{\delta})^{c}\cap(T_{n}^{\delta})^{c}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\left\{x\in{\rm X}:|{\sf d}(x,x_{i})-{\sf d}(x,x_{n})|=\delta\right\}.

Let
p\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{n-1}(U_{i,n}^{\delta})^{c}\cap(T_{n}^{\delta})^{c}
. On the one hand, since
p\in(U_{i,n}^{\delta})^{c}
, we must have

{\sf d}(p,x_{i})-{\sf d}(p,x_{n})\geq\delta,\qquad\forall\,i=1,\ldots,n-1.

On the other hand, note that
T_{n}^{\delta}=\bigcap_{j=1}^{n-1}U_{n,j}^{-\delta}
, hence
p\in(T_{n}^{\delta})^{c}
implies that there exists
j_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
such that

{\sf d}(p,x_{n})-{\sf d}(p,x_{j_{0}})\geq-\delta.

This means that
p\in\{x\in{\rm X}\colon|{\sf d}(x,x_{j_{0}})-{\sf d}(x,x_{n})|=\delta\}
, and thus (
6.3
) is verified.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a geodesic metric measure space and let
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a countable collection of distinct points in
{\rm X}
. For any
i,j\in I
and
\delta>-{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{j})
, we have that
U_{i,j}^{\delta}
defined in (
6.1
) is star-shaped at
x_{i}
. Moreover, there exists a countable set
S_{i,j}\subset(-{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{j}),\infty)
, such that

(6.4)

\mathfrak{m}\left(\{x\in{\rm X}:{\sf d}(x,x_{i})-{\sf d}(x,x_{j})=\delta\}\right)=0,\qquad\forall\delta\in(-{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{j}),\infty)\setminus S_{i,j}.

Proof.

First, we prove that
U_{i,j}^{\delta}
is star-shaped at
x_{i}
, for fixed
i,j\in I
.
Let
w\in U_{i,j}^{\delta}
. We consider an arbitrary geodesic
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}
such that
\gamma_{0}=x_{i}
and
\gamma_{1}=w
. We define the continuous function
g(t)\coloneqq{\sf d}(\gamma_{t},x_{i})-{\sf d}(\gamma_{t},x_{j})-\delta
. We have that
g(0)=-{\sf d}(x_{i},x_{j})-\delta<0
and
g(1)<0
by definition of
U_{i,j}^{\delta}
.
We need to prove that
g(t)<0
for every
t\in[0,1]
. Since
g
is continuous, we can assume by contradiction that there exists
t\in[0,1]
such that
g(t)=0
. This means that there exists
p\in{\rm X}
on the geodesic
\gamma
so that
{\sf d}(p,x_{i})={\sf d}(p,x_{j})+\delta
. We have that

{\sf d}(x_{j},w)\leq{\sf d}(p,w)+{\sf d}(p,x_{j})={\sf d}(p,w)+{\sf d}(p,x_{i})-\delta={\sf d}(x_{i},w)-\delta,

where we used that
p\in{\rm Im}\gamma
. This contradicts that
w\in U_{i,j}^{\delta}
, which is then star-shaped at
x_{i}
, as claimed. Finally, (
6.4
) is a straightforward consequence of the continuity of the distance.
∎

Proposition 6.3

(Star-shaped essential partition)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a geodesic metric measure space and consider a
4\pi
-separated set of points
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. Then, there is
S\subset\mathbb{R}
countable and, for all
\delta\in(0,2\pi)\setminus S
, there is a countable collection of open sets
\{T_{i}^{\delta}\}_{i\in I}
such that:

i)

T_{i}^{\delta}\cap T_{j}^{\delta}=\emptyset
for every
i,j\in I
with
i\neq j
, and
\mathfrak{m}({\rm X}\setminus\cup_{i}T_{i}^{\delta})=0
;

ii)

T_{i}^{\delta}
is star-shaped at
x_{i}
for all
i\in I
;

iii)

B_{2\pi-\delta}(x_{i})\subset T_{i}^{\delta}
for all
i\in I
.

Proof.

For every
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\{U_{i,j}^{\delta}\}_{i,j\in I}
and
\{T_{i}^{\delta}\}_{i\in I}
the collections of sets associated to
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, as defined in (
6.1
) (note that
I
is countable, since
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is separable). For every
i<j
, let
S_{i,j}
be the countable set identified by Lemma
6.2
and define the countable set
S\coloneqq\cup_{i,j}S_{i,j}
. We will prove that
\{T_{i}^{\delta}\}
satisfies i), ii), iii) for
\delta\in(0,\pi)\setminus S
. For ease of notation, we set
T_{i}\coloneqq T_{i}^{\delta}
.

First, we observe that ii) holds thanks to Lemma
6.2
, which says that
T_{i}
is the intersection of star-shaped domains at
x_{i}
. Secondly, for item i), observe that by construction,
\{T_{i}\}_{i}
are pairwise disjoint. Moreover, since
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are
4\pi
-separated, by (
6.4
), combined with Lemma
6.1
, we also deuce that
\mathfrak{m}({\rm X}\setminus\cup_{i}T_{i})=0
. We are left to prove iii), and we start by proving the first inclusion. If this were not true, there would exist a point
z\in{\rm X}
such that
{\sf d}(z,x_{i})<2\pi-\delta
and an index
j\in I
such that, either
j>i
and
z\notin U_{i,j}^{\delta}
or
j<i
and
z\notin U_{i,j}^{-\delta}
. In the first case, it holds

\delta+{\sf d}(z,x_{j})\leq{\sf d}(z,x_{i})<2\pi-\delta.

This implies that
{\sf d}(z,x_{j})<2\pi-2\delta<2\pi
, which contradicts the fact that
\{x_{i}\}_{i}
are
4\pi
-separated. In the second case, it holds

-\delta+{\sf d}(z,x_{j})\leq{\sf d}(z,x_{i})<2\pi-\delta,

and for similar reasons we contradict that
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are
4\pi
-separated.
∎

6.2.
Spherical integral estimate

In the upcoming parts, except for our main results (Theorems
6.8
,
1.2
), we treat the case
K=N-1
. Clearly, all the intermediate technical results admit a straightforward generalization for general
K>0
by a scaling argument (cf. (
5.18
)).

We prove next that large balls must have a small spherical integral, provided the scaling invariant integral curvature deficit is small enough. Here, by large, we mean that the radius is exceeding the maximal one in the comparison model. We closely follow
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 6.4
.

For every
N>1
and
p>N/2
there exists a constant
D_{N,p}>0
such that the following holds. Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be an essentially non-branching
{\sf CD}(k,N)
space for some
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible and with
\theta_{1+c}(x)<\infty
for some
x\in{\rm X},c>0
. Let
T\subset B_{R}(x)
for some
R>0
be a bounded star-shaped set at
x
satisfying

\varepsilon\coloneqq R^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{p}^{k}(T,N-1)}{\mathfrak{m}(T)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)^{2-\frac{1}{p}}.

Then, for all
r\in\left(\pi,R\right)
, it holds

S_{T}(r)\leq D_{N,p}\varepsilon^{\frac{p(N-1)}{2p-1}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(T)}{r}.

Proof.

Let us apply the monotonicity of item iii) of Lemma
5.6
, for
0<\lambda\leq N-1
to be chosen later,
\alpha=1/(2p-1)
and
0<t<r\leq\pi\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{\lambda}}
, to deduce that

\left(\frac{S_{T}(r)}{h_{\lambda,N}(r)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}-\left(\frac{S_{T}(t)}{h_{\lambda,N}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\leq\frac{1}{2p-1}\int_{t}^{r}\left(\frac{S_{T}(s)}{h_{\lambda,N}(s)}\right)^{\frac{2-2p}{2p-1}}\int_{Q_{T}(s)}\psi_{q}(s)\frac{h_{q}(s)}{h_{\lambda,N}(s)}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{q}{\mathrm{d}}s\eqqcolon\frac{{\mathcal{J}}}{2p-1}.

To estimate the term
{\mathcal{J}}
, we firstly use Hölder inequality to obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{r}S_{T}(s)^{\frac{2-2p}{2p-1}}\int_{Q_{T}(s)}\sin\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda}{N-1}}s\right)^{\frac{1-N}{2p-1}}\psi_{q}(s)h_{q}(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{q}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{r}S_{T}(s)^{\frac{2-2p}{2p-1}}\left(\int_{Q_{T}(s)}h_{q}(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{q}\right)^{\frac{2p-2}{2p-1}}\left(\int_{Q_{T}(s)}\sin\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda}{N-1}}s\right)^{1-N}\psi_{q}(s)^{2p-1}h_{q}(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{r}\sin\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda}{N-1}}s\right)^{-2}\left(\int_{Q_{T}(s)}\sin\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda}{N-1}}s\right)^{4p-N-1}\psi_{q}^{2p-1}(s)h_{q}(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s,

where in the last equality we used the definition of
S_{T}
.
Using then Proposition
4.3
, we get

(6.5)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\rho_{p}^{k}(T,\lambda)^{\tfrac{1}{2p-1}}\int_{t}^{r}\sin\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda}{N-1}}s\right)^{-2}\,{\mathrm{d}}s.

Note that in the application of Proposition
4.3
we are using both conclusions at the same time. (Whenever
t<s<\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\tfrac{N-1}{\lambda}}\leq r
, we simply use
\sin(s)^{4p-N-1}\leq 1
).

We now choose
\lambda
. Define
\varepsilon^{\prime}\coloneqq\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}
and let
\lambda=\lambda(\varepsilon,r)
be defined by
\frac{\lambda}{N-1}=\frac{(\pi-\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2}}{r^{2}}
(so that, in particular,
r<\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{\lambda}}
also holds). In the sequel, we shall consider
t\in[\frac{\pi}{2(\pi-\varepsilon^{\prime})}r,r]
. Since
\sin\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda}{N-1}}s\right)\geq\frac{2}{\pi}\left(\pi-\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda}{N-1}}s\right)
(recall
0\leq s\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{\lambda}}
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{r}\sin\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda}{N-1}}s\right)^{-2}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\int_{t}^{r}\left(\pi-\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda}{N-1}}s\right)^{-2}{\mathrm{d}}s=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\frac{r-t}{\left(\pi-\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda}{N-1}}t\right)\left(\pi-\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda}{N-1}}r\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{r}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}.

As
\lambda\leq N-1
, it holds
\rho_{p}^{k}(T,\lambda)\leq\rho_{p}^{k}(T,N-1)
so that (
6.5
) and the above yield

{\mathcal{J}}\leq\alpha_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\rho_{p}^{k}(T,N-1)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{r}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}=\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\mathfrak{m}(T)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}R^{-\frac{1}{2p-1}}.

All in all, after multiplication using the identity
h_{K,\lambda}(r)=\sin\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{N-1}}r\right)^{\frac{N-1}{2p-1}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{T}(r)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(S_{T}(t)\frac{h_{K,\lambda}(r)}{h_{\lambda,N}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}+\frac{\pi}{4p-2}\alpha_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\mathfrak{m}(T)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}R^{-\frac{1}{2p-1}}h_{K,\lambda}(r)^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}

\displaystyle=S_{T}(t)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\sin\left(\frac{t(\pi-\varepsilon^{\prime})}{r}\right)}\right)^{\frac{N-1}{2p-1}}+\frac{\pi}{4p-2}\alpha_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\mathfrak{m}(T)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}R^{-\frac{1}{2p-1}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{\frac{N-1}{2p-1}},

having used, in the last inequality, that
\sin\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{N-1}}r\right)\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
. From here, the conclusion of the proof follows by repeating verbatim the last part of the proof of
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
.
∎

6.3.
Proof of diameter estimate

We next show that a small scale invariant integral curvature deficit on a sufficiently large star-shaped set is enough to guarantee a diameter estimate. This follows from the combination of the upper bound given by Lemma
6.4
and the volume growth given by
Lemma

5.10
. Compare the following with
[
4
, Lemma 1.4]
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Fix
\overline{N}>1
. For every
N\in[\overline{N},\infty)
and
p>N/2
there exist constants
E_{N,p}>0
,
\gamma=\gamma_{\overline{N},p}\in\big(0,\frac{1}{4}\big]
such that the following holds. Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be an essentially non-branching
{\sf CD}(k,N)
space for some
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible. Assume that
\theta_{1+c_{z}}(z)<\infty
for some
c_{z}>0
at
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
z\in{\rm X}
. Let
T\subset{\rm X}
be star-shaped at
x\in{\rm X}
with
\theta_{1+c_{x}}(x)<\infty
and suppose that there exist
R_{0},R
such that
6\pi>R\geq R_{0}>\pi
and

B_{R_{0}}(x)\subset T\subset B_{R}(x)
. Furthermore, suppose that

\varepsilon\coloneqq R^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{p}^{k}(T,N-1)}{\mathfrak{m}(T)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq E_{N,p}\left(1-\frac{\pi}{R_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}.

Then, it holds
{\rm X}\subset B_{\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)
and, in particular,
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is compact.

Proof.

Fix
\delta\coloneqq\frac{R_{0}}{4}\varepsilon^{\gamma}
, for some
\gamma>0
to be chosen in the sequel. The conclusion
{\rm X}\subset B_{\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)
follows if we show that

(6.6)

U\coloneqq B_{\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}+\delta}(x)\setminus\overline{B_{\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)}=\emptyset.

Indeed, if by contradiction there is
z\in{\rm X}
with
{\sf d}(z,x)>\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}
, then, since
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is geodesic, there exists an interior point of a geodesic connecting
x
to
z
and belonging to
U
. This contradicts the fact that the set is empty. We now prove claim (
6.6
). By contradiction, assume that there exists
y\in U
.
The choice of
\delta
gives that
B_{\delta}(y)\subset B_{\pi+R_{0}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)\setminus\overline{B_{\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{4}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)}
. In addition, by hypothesis (taking
E_{N,p}\leq 1
) we have
R_{0}\varepsilon^{\gamma}\leq R_{0}-\pi
,
B_{\pi+R_{0}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)\subset T
. Thus,
By Lemma
6.4
, we have

(6.7)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(B_{\delta}(y))

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{m}(B_{\pi+R_{0}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)\setminus\overline{B_{\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{4}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)})\leq\int_{\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{4}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}^{\pi+R_{0}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}S_{T}(r)\,{\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle\leq D_{N,p}\mathfrak{m}(T)\varepsilon^{\frac{p(N-1)}{2p-1}}\int_{\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{4}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}^{\pi+R_{0}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\frac{1}{r}\,{\mathrm{d}}r\leq\frac{3}{4\pi}D_{N,p}R_{0}\mathfrak{m}(T)\varepsilon^{\gamma+\frac{p(N-1)}{2p-1}}.

provided that
E_{N,p}\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)^{2-1/p}
. In the last inequality, we used that
\int_{\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{4}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}^{\pi+R_{0}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\frac{1}{r}\,{\mathrm{d}}r\leq\frac{3}{4\pi}R_{0}\varepsilon^{\gamma}
, since
x\mapsto x^{-1}
is decreasing. Moreover, by monotonicity of
K\mapsto\rho_{p}^{k}(T,K)
, we deduce that
R^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{p}^{k}(T,0)}{\mathfrak{m}(T)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\varepsilon
, therefore, up to taking
E_{N,p}\leq A_{0,N,p}
, we can apply (
5.19
) to obtain

(6.8)

\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(x))\geq\mathfrak{m}(T)(1-\beta_{N,p}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}})\left(\frac{R_{0}}{R}\right)^{N}\geq\frac{\mathfrak{m}(T)}{2}\left(\frac{R_{0}}{R}\right)^{N}.

Combining (
6.7
) and (
6.8
), and using that
\pi<R_{0}\leq R<6\pi
, we obtain

(6.9)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(B_{\delta}(y))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2\pi}D_{N,p}R_{0}\varepsilon^{\gamma+\frac{p(N-1)}{2p-1}}\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(x))\left(\frac{R}{R_{0}}\right)^{N}\leq 6^{N+2}D_{N,p}\varepsilon^{\gamma+\frac{p(N-1)}{2p-1}}\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(x)).

We now look for a lower bound for
\mathfrak{m}(B_{\delta}(y))
. Firstly, since
B_{R_{0}}(x)\subset T
then
\rho_{p}^{k}(B_{R_{0}}(x),0)\leq\rho_{p}^{k}(B_{R_{0}}(x),N-1)\leq\rho_{p}^{k}(T,N-1)
. Hence, by (
6.8
), we have

(6.10)

\begin{split}R_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{p}^{k}(B_{R_{0}}(x),0)}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{R_{0}}{R}\right)^{2-\frac{N}{p}}R^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{p}^{k}(T,N-1)}{\mathfrak{m}(T)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\varepsilon\leq E_{N,p},\end{split}

where we used that
p>N/2
and
R_{0}<R
. Therefore, up to taking
E_{N,p}\leq B_{N,p}
, we are in position to apply
Lemma

5.10
to
B_{R_{0}}(x)
to get

(6.11)

\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\delta}(y))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p-1}}\geq\left(\frac{\delta}{R_{0}}\right)^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}\left(\left(\frac{\delta}{R_{0}}\right)^{\frac{2N}{2p-1}}\left(1-\beta_{N,p}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\right)-\beta_{N,p}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\right).

Secondly, by our choice of
\delta
, the right-hand side of (
6.11
) is strictly positive and such that

\left(\frac{\delta}{R_{0}}\right)^{\frac{N}{2p-1}}\left(\left(\frac{\delta}{R_{0}}\right)^{\frac{2N}{2p-1}}\left(1-\beta_{N,p}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\right)-\beta_{N,p}\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{2p-1}}\right)\geq\,\varepsilon^{\frac{3N\gamma}{2p-1}},

provided that
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{4}
and
E_{N,p}
is chosen sufficiently small. Thus, (
6.11
) and the estimate above yield

(6.12)

\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\delta}(y))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(x))}\geq\varepsilon^{3N\gamma}.

In conclusion, the combination of (
6.9
) and (
6.12
) gives that

\varepsilon^{3N\gamma}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\delta}(y))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(x))}\leq 6^{N+2}D_{N,p}\varepsilon^{\gamma+\frac{p(N-1)}{2p-1}}.

This gives a contradiction if
\gamma+\frac{p(N-1)}{2p-1}>3N\gamma
. Since
N\geq\overline{N}
, we can choose
\gamma=\gamma_{\overline{N},p}
such that we reach the desired contradiction for every
N\geq\overline{N}
.
∎

Note that an easy consequence of the previous proposition is that
{\rm diam}({\rm X})\leq 2\pi+R_{0}\varepsilon^{\gamma}
. We aim at improving this diameter bound, and we can do so by iterating the previous argument.

Corollary 6.6
.

Fix
\overline{N}>1
. For every
N\in[\overline{N},\infty)
and
p>N/2
there exist constants
F_{N,p}>0
,
\gamma=\gamma_{\overline{N},p}\in\big(0,\frac{1}{8}\big]
such that the following holds. Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be an essentially non-branching
{\sf CD}(k,N)
space for some
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible. Assume that
\theta_{1+c_{z}}(z)<\infty
for some
c_{z}>0
at
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
z\in{\rm X}
. Let
T\subset{\rm X}
be star-shaped at
x\in{\rm X}
with
\theta_{1+c_{x}}(x)<\infty
and suppose that there exist
R_{0},R
such that
6\pi>R\geq R_{0}>\pi
and

B_{R_{0}}(x)\subset T\subset B_{R}(x)
. Furthermore, suppose that

\varepsilon\coloneqq R^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{p}^{k}(T,N-1)}{\mathfrak{m}(T)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq F_{N,p}\left(1-\frac{\pi}{R_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}.

Then, it holds
{\rm diam}({\rm X})\leq\pi(1+3\pi\varepsilon^{2\gamma})
.

Proof.

We fix
\gamma=\frac{\gamma_{\overline{N},p}}{2}
from Proposition
6.5
. By contradiction, we assume that there exist two points
z_{1}
,
z_{2}\in{\rm X}
such that
{\sf d}(z_{1},z_{2})>\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{2\gamma}
. Firstly, by Proposition
6.5
, choosing
F_{N,p}\leq E_{N,p}
, we immediately deduce that
{\rm X}\subset B_{\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)
. In particular,
{\sf d}(x,z_{1})<\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}
and thus
B_{R_{0}-\pi-\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)\subset B_{R_{0}}(z_{1})
. Secondly, we claim that

(6.13)

R_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{\rho^{k}_{p}(B_{R_{0}}(z_{1}),N-1)}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(z_{1}))}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}F_{N,p}\left(1-\frac{\pi}{R_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}}.

Indeed, on the one hand, since
B_{R_{0}-\pi-\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)\subset B_{R_{0}}(z_{1})
, one has
\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(z_{1}))\geq\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}-\pi-\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x))
and, applying
Proposition

5.9
, we then obtain

(6.14)

\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(z_{1}))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(x))}\geq\frac{\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{R_{0}-\pi-\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)\right)}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(x))}\geq\left(\frac{R_{0}-\pi-\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}{R_{0}}\right)^{N}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{R_{0}-\pi}{R_{0}}\right)^{N}.

Note that here the assumptions of
Proposition

5.9
are verified since
R_{0}\leq R
and
\varepsilon\leq F_{N,p}\leq A_{p,0,N}
. On the other hand, by Proposition
6.5
and since
R_{0}\varepsilon^{\gamma}\leq R_{0}-\pi
, we also have that
B_{R_{0}}(z_{1})\subset B_{\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x)\subset B_{R_{0}}(x)
, which implies that
\rho_{p}^{k}(B_{R_{0}}(z_{1}),N-1)\leq\rho_{p}^{k}(B_{R_{0}}(x),N-1)
, by monotonicity
\rho_{p}^{k}(\cdot,N-1)
for set inclusions. Combining this observation with (
6.14
), we infer

\displaystyle R_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{p}^{k}(B_{R_{0}}(z_{1}),N-1)}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(z_{1}))}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq R_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(x))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(z_{1}))}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{\rho_{p}^{k}(B_{R_{0}}(x),N-1)}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R_{0}}(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{R_{0}}{R_{0}-\pi}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}}\varepsilon,

where, in the last inequality, we used (
6.10
) (which is available since we chose
F_{N,p}\leq E_{N,p}
). Now, by the assumption on
\varepsilon
, we have that

\left(\frac{R_{0}}{R_{0}-\pi}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}}\varepsilon\leq F_{N,p}\left(1-\frac{\pi}{R_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}-\frac{N}{p}}\leq F_{N,p}\left(1-\frac{\pi}{R_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}},

since
\frac{1}{\gamma}-\frac{N}{p}\geq\frac{1}{2\gamma}
by the constraint on
p
and the choice of
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{4}
. This proves claim (
6.13
).

To conclude the proof, note that, thanks to the estimate (
6.13
), we can apply Proposition
6.5
with
T=B_{R_{0}}(z_{1})
star-shaped set at
z_{1}
, up to taking a smaller
F_{N,p}
. This shows that
{\rm X}\subset B_{\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{2\gamma}}(z_{1})
, which is in contradiction with the fact that
{\sf d}(z_{1},z_{2})>\pi+\frac{R_{0}}{2}\varepsilon^{2\gamma}
.
∎

Remark 6.7
.

In the previous statements, observe that if
\overline{N}=2
,
\gamma
can be chosen equal to
1/10
independently of
p
. Additionally, by the proof of Proposition
6.5
, we must have

\gamma<\frac{p(N-1)}{(3N-1)(2p-1)},

hence if
N\to 1
, then
\gamma\to 0
. In this limiting case, we cannot deduce a diameter estimate. Indeed, for
N\geq 1
, the metric measure space
([0,2\pi],|\cdot|,r^{N-1}\mathcal{L}^{1})
is
{\sf CD}(0,N)
. The integral curvature deficit from
N-1
can be taken arbitrarily small as
N\downarrow 1
, but the diameter is constantly equal to
2\pi
.

\blacksquare

Finally, we prove the Myers’ diameter estimate, cf. Theorem
1.2
. The proof makes use of the essential partition of the metric measure space into a family of star-shaped set (cf.
Proposition

6.3
). The key point is that, provided
\rho_{p}^{k}({\rm X},K)
is small enough, Corollary
6.6
can be applied to at least one star-shaped set, giving in turn the desired diameter estimate.

Theorem 6.8
.

Fix
\overline{N}>1
. For every
N\in[\overline{N},\infty),K>0
and
p>N/2
there exist constants
C_{K,N,\overline{N},p},\gamma_{\overline{N},p}>0
such that the following holds. Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be an essentially non-branching
{\sf CD}(k,N)
for some
k\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible. Assume that
\theta_{1+c_{x}}(x)<\infty
for some
c_{x}>0
at
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in{\rm X}
. If
\rho^{k}_{p}({\rm X},K)<\infty
then
\mathfrak{m}({\rm X})<\infty
. Moreover, if

\rho^{k}_{p}({\rm X},K)\leq\frac{\mathfrak{m}({\rm X})}{C_{K,N,\overline{N},p}},

then
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is compact and it holds

(6.15)

{\rm diam}({\rm X})\leq\pi\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{K}}\left(1+C_{K,N,\overline{N},p}\left(\frac{\rho^{k}_{p}({\rm X},K)}{\mathfrak{m}({\rm X})}\right)^{2\gamma_{\overline{N},p}}\right).

Proof.

We fix
\gamma=\gamma_{\overline{N},p}
from Corollary
6.6
. We shall prove the statement for
K=N-1
and subdivide the proof into different steps. The statement and the estimate (
6.15
) would then follow for general
K>0
by a scaling argument (cf. (
5.18
)) with the constant
C_{K,N,\overline{N},p}=C_{N-1,N,\overline{N},p}\left(\frac{N-1}{K}\right)^{p\gamma}
.

Step 1: Reduction to finite Bishop-Gromov density
. Consider a countable maximal collection
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
that is
4\pi
-separated in
({\rm X},{\sf d})
and fix
\eta\in(0,\pi/64)
. Then, by the assumption on the Bishop-Gromov density, for every
i\in I
there exists
\tilde{x}_{i}\in B_{\eta}(x_{i})
such that
\theta_{1+c_{\tilde{x}_{i}}}(\tilde{x}_{i})<\infty
. The new collection of points
\{\tilde{x}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is
(4\pi-2\eta)
-separated however it may be non-maximal. Hence, choosing
\delta
in a full-measure subset of
(\pi/16,\pi/8)
, and denoting by
\{\tilde{T}_{i}^{\delta-2\eta}\}_{i\in I}
the sets defined in (
6.1
) using the collection
\{\tilde{x}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, Proposition
6.3
holds. In particular, for every
i\in I
,
B_{2\pi-\delta+\eta}(\tilde{x}_{i})\subset\tilde{T}_{i}^{\delta-2\eta}
.

We now show that also the inclusion
\tilde{T}_{i}^{\delta-2\eta}\subset B_{4\pi+\delta+\eta}(\tilde{x}_{i})
holds. Suppose by contradiction that this is not the case, so there exists a point
x\in\tilde{T}_{i}^{\delta-2\eta}\setminus B_{4\pi+\delta+\eta}(\tilde{x}_{i})
. On the one hand, this implies

(6.16)

{\sf d}(x,x_{i})\geq{\sf d}(x,\tilde{x}_{i})-{\sf d}(\tilde{x}_{i},x_{i})\geq 4\pi+\delta>4\pi.

On the other hand, since
x\in\tilde{T}_{i}^{\delta-2\eta}=\bigcap_{j>i}\tilde{U}_{i,j}^{\delta-2\eta}\cap\bigcap_{j<i}\tilde{U}_{i,j}^{-\delta+2\eta}
, using the definition of the sets
\tilde{U}_{i,j}^{\delta-2\eta}
, the fact that
\tilde{x}_{i}\in B_{\eta}(x_{i})
, and the triangle inequality, we have, for every
j<i
,

{\sf d}(x,x_{j})\geq{\sf d}(x,\tilde{x}_{j})-{\sf d}(x_{j},\tilde{x}_{j})>{\sf d}(x,\tilde{x}_{i})-\delta+\eta\geq{\sf d}(x,x_{i})-{\sf d}(x_{i},\tilde{x}_{i})-\delta+\eta>{\sf d}(x,x_{i})-\delta\geq 4\pi,

where, in the last inequality, we used (
6.16
). Analogously, for every
j<i
, we have

{\sf d}(x,x_{j})\geq{\sf d}(x,\tilde{x}_{j})-\eta>{\sf d}(x,\tilde{x}_{i})+\delta-3\eta\geq{\sf d}(x,x_{i})+\delta-4\eta>4\pi.

This is in contradiction with the fact that the original family
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
was
4\pi
-maximally separated.

Step 2:
\mathfrak{m}({\rm X})<\infty
. By the previous step, we may assume that the family

7.1.
Dirichlet eigenvalues in model intervals

Given
K\in\mathbb{R}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
r\in\left(0,\pi\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{K^{+}}}\right)
, we denote by

the first non-zero Dirichlet eigenvalue for the
p
-Laplacian on a geodesic ball of radius
r
in the model space of dimension
N
and constant (sectional) curvature
K
. Equivalently, since eigenfunctions on geodesic balls of the model spaces are radial, we can compute
\lambda_{p}(K,N,r)
by optimizing over one-dimensional
(K,N)
-model intervals. This allows for the generalization of the definition of
\lambda_{p}(K,N,r)
for any
N\in(1,\infty)
. More precisely, we set

(7.1)

\lambda_{p}(K,N,r):=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{0}^{r}|\phi^{\prime}|^{p}h_{K,N}\,{\mathrm{d}}t}{\int_{0}^{r}|\phi|^{p}h_{K,N}\,{\mathrm{d}}t}\colon\phi\in{\sf AC}_{loc}(0,r)\text{ so that}\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{0}^{r}|\phi|^{p}h_{K,N}\,{\mathrm{d}}t<\infty\\
\lim_{t\to r^{-}}\phi(t)=0\end{array}\right\}.

A
p
-Dirichlet eigenfunction for
\lambda_{p}(K,N,r)
is a solution
\phi\in{\sf AC}_{loc}(0,r)
with
|\phi|,|\phi^{\prime}|\in L^{p}(h_{K,N})
of

(7.2)

-\Delta_{p}\phi=\lambda_{p}(K,N,r)\phi|\phi|^{p-2},\qquad\text{in the sense of distributions on }(0,r),

where
\Delta_{p}\phi\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,r)
is the
p
-Laplacian defined via integration by parts

-\int_{0}^{r}\Delta_{p}\phi gh_{K,N}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{0}^{r}|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}g^{\prime}h_{K,N}\,{\mathrm{d}}t,\qquad\forall\,g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,r).

We collect basic properties of Dirichlet eigenfunctions on weighted intervals in Appendix
B
.

Appendix A
One-dimensional
{\sf CD}
densities

In this appendix we study some useful estimates and regularization properties of one-dimensional densities admitting a variable Ricci curvature lower bound. We shall always consider closed intervals
[a,b]
with
-\infty\leq a<b\leq+\infty
, with the standard convention if
a,b
are not finite.

Definition A.1
.

Let
h\colon[a,b]\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a function, let
\kappa\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be admissible and
N>1
. We say that
h
is a
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
density on
[a,b]
, provided for every
x_{0},x_{1}\in[a,b]
and

it holds

(A.1)

h(tx_{1}+(1-t)x_{0})^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\geq\sigma^{(1-t)}_{k^{-}_{\gamma},N-1}(|x_{1}-x_{0}|)h(x_{0})^{\frac{1}{N-1}}+\sigma_{k^{+}_{\gamma},N-1}^{(t)}(|x_{1}-x_{0}|)h(x_{1})^{\frac{1}{N-1}},

where
\gamma_{t}=(1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1}
.

The above estimate is a curvature dimension inequality that will naturally appear when analyzing the regularity of the disintegration in the proof of Theorem
3.6
. Next, we reconcile this notion with usual curvature dimension conditions as defined in Definition
2.4
(compare to
[
17
, Lemma A.2]
).

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\kappa\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be admissible and fix
N>1
. Let
h\in L^{1}(a,b)
be a non-negative function. The following are equivalent:

i)

there is a representative of
h
that is a
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
density on
[a,b]
;

ii)

([a,b],|\cdot|,h\mathcal{L}^{1})
is a
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
space as in Definition
2.4
;

iii)

there is a locally absolutely continuous representative of
h
satisfying

(\log h)^{\prime\prime}+\frac{|(\log h)^{\prime}|^{2}}{N-1}\leq-\kappa\qquad\text{in the sense of distributions on }(a,b).

In particular, if any of the above holds, then
\log h
is locally semi-concave and
h
is locally Lipschitz.

Proof.

The equivalence between i) and iii) follows by
[
40
, Cor. 3.13]
and a standard algebraic manipulation. The equivalence between items i) and ii) can be proven following the same strategy of
[
17
, Lemma A.2]
, taking into account
[
40
, Prop. 7.2]
.
∎

We next study regularization properties of
{\sf CD}
densities (cf.
[
17
, Prop. A.10]
).

Proposition A.3
.

Let
\kappa\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be admissible with
|a|,|b|<\infty
,
N>1
and let
h\colon[a,b]\to[0,+\infty)
be a
{\sf CD}(\kappa,N)
density on
(a,b)
. Consider, for every
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{b-a}{2}\right)
, a smooth mollifier
\eta_{\varepsilon}
supported on
[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
with
\int\eta_{\varepsilon}=1
defined as
\eta_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\varepsilon^{-1}\eta(\cdot/\varepsilon)
for some
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-1,1)
. Define

h_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\exp\left((\log h)\ast\eta_{\varepsilon}\right),\qquad\kappa_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\kappa\ast\eta_{\varepsilon},\qquad I_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq(a+\varepsilon,b-\varepsilon).

Then,
h_{\varepsilon}
is a smooth
{\sf CD}(\kappa_{\varepsilon},N)
density on
I_{\varepsilon}
. Furthermore, it holds:

i)

h_{\varepsilon}\to h
locally uniformly on
(a,b)
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
;

ii)

(\log h_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}\to(\log h)^{\prime}
a.e. on
(a,b)
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
;

iii)

if also
\varliminf_{r\downarrow a}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{a}^{r}h(r)\,{\mathrm{d}}r=0
, then we have
\varliminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}h_{\varepsilon}(a+\varepsilon)=0
.

Proof.

First, observe that by Jensen inequality (applied to
\eta_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}^{1}
) we deduce

(A.2)

\left[(\log h_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(t)\right]^{2}=\left[((\log h)^{\prime}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon})(t)\right]^{2}\leq(\left[(\log h)^{\prime}\right]^{2}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon})(t)\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in I_{\varepsilon}.

Recall now that, thanks to Lemma
A.2
, we have

(\log h)^{\prime\prime}+\frac{[(\log h)^{\prime}]^{2}}{N-1}\leq-\kappa\qquad\text{in the sense of distributions on }I_{\varepsilon}

After taking the convolution for
\varepsilon>0
on both sides and using (
A.2
), we get

(\log h_{\varepsilon})^{\prime\prime}(t)+\frac{[(\log h_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(t)]^{2}}{N-1}\leq-\kappa\ast\eta_{\varepsilon}(t),\qquad\text{a.e. on }t\in I_{\varepsilon}

In particular,
h_{\varepsilon}
is a smooth
{\sf CD}(\kappa\ast\eta_{\varepsilon},N)
density on
I_{\varepsilon}
.

Conclusion i) simply follows from the assumed continuity properties of
h,\kappa
. Conclusion ii) follows similarly since
\log h
is locally Lipschitz. Finally, conclusion iii) instead follows estimating

(A.3)

0\leq h_{\varepsilon}(a+\varepsilon)\leq\exp\left(\int\log h(t)\eta_{\varepsilon}(a+\varepsilon-t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\right)\leq\int h(t)\eta_{\varepsilon}(a+\varepsilon-t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t,

where, in the last inequality, we used Jensen’s inequality applied to the probability measure
\eta_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon-\cdot)\mathcal{L}^{1}
. Since
\eta\leq L
for every
L>0
, we have by definition
\eta_{\varepsilon}\leq L\varepsilon^{-1}
. Hence,
\int h(t)\eta_{\varepsilon}(a+\varepsilon-t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\leq L\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{a}^{a+2\varepsilon}h(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t
that, combined with (
A.3
) and the assumption on

\varliminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}h_{\varepsilon}(a+\varepsilon)=0
.
∎

Lemma B.1

Let

\left(|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}+|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}(\log h_{K,N})^{\prime}=-\lambda_{p}(K,N,r)|\phi|^{p-2}\phi,\qquad\text{a.e.\ in $(0,r).$}

Proof.

The existence of a minimizer
\phi
follows by a standard compactness argument. The fact that this can be taken non-negative follows instead by the invariance of the optimization problem (
7.1
) for taking the absolute value. The fact that
\phi
satisfies (
7.2
) in a distributional sense is also a standard variational computation. By definition of
\Delta_{p}\phi
, we have for all
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,r)
that

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{r}\Delta_{p}\phi gh_{K,N}\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=-|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}gh_{K,N}\Big|_{0}^{r}+\int_{0}^{r}(|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime})g^{\prime}h_{K,N}\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{0}^{r}|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}g(\log h_{K,N})^{\prime}h_{K,N}\,{\mathrm{d}}t

Since
g
is of compact support on
(0,r)
we get
-|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}gh_{K,N}\Big|_{0}^{r}=0
. Hence, by the Euler-Lagrange equations (
7.2
), we deduce

\lambda_{p}(K,N,r)\int_{0}^{r}\phi|\phi|^{p-2}gh_{K,N}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{0}^{r}\big(|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}g^{\prime}h_{K,N}+|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}g(\log h_{K,N})^{\prime}\big)h_{K,N}\,{\mathrm{d}}t,

for all
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,r)
. Since
w\coloneqq|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}h_{K,N}\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,r)
and
\phi|\phi|^{p-2}h_{K,N}
as well as that
|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}(\log h_{K,N})^{\prime}h_{K,N}\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,r)
, we thus deduce that
w\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(0,r)
. However,
h_{K,N}\in C^{\infty}(0,r)
and
h_{K,N},h_{K,N}^{-1},(\log\,h_{K,N})^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(0,r)
, hence a chain rule argument gives also
|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(0,r)
and (
B.1
) follows.
∎

Knowing that
\phi
satisfies (
B.1
) gives access to standard elliptic regularity theory to deduce that
\phi
is also Lipschitz continuous. The important fact is that
h_{K,N}>0
on
(0,r]
, and it is smooth on
[0,r]
, and vanishes at zero. For instance, this can be deduced by showing that
\phi
is sufficiently integrable by a Moser iteration argument and then recasting (
B.1
) as a degenerate non-homogeneous elliptic equation. The Lipschitz regularity then follows by appealing to
[
29
, Thm. 2]
(and subsequent remark there). We are now going to show that then
\phi
is also non-increasing (if non-negative).

Lemma B.2
.

Let
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N>1
and
r\in\left(0,\pi\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{K^{+}}}\right)
. Let
\phi\in{\sf AC}_{loc}(0,r)
be a non-negative minimizer of (
7.1
). Then, there exists
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}(|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime})(t)=0
and
\phi
is non-increasing.

Proof.

Thanks to the previous lemma, we know that
w=h_{K,N}|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(0,r)
and

-(h_{K,N}|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime})^{\prime}=\lambda_{p}(K,N,r)\phi|\phi|^{p-2}h_{K,N},\qquad\text{a.e.\ on }(0,r).

For any
t<r
we can thus integrate the above from zero to
t
using that
h_{K,N}(0)=0
to obtain

-h_{K,N}(t)\left(|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}\right)(t)=-\int_{0}^{t}(h_{K,N}|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime})^{\prime}\,{\mathrm{d}}s=\lambda_{p}(K,N,r)\int_{0}^{t}\phi|\phi|^{p-2}h_{K,N}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\geq 0,

having used, for the latter, the assumption that the
p
-Dirichlet eigenfunction
\phi
is non-negative and that
h_{K,N}>0
on
(0,r)
. This implies that
\phi^{\prime}\leq 0
a.e. on
(0,r)
, hence it is non-increasing. To conclude the proof, it is enough to observe that

\displaystyle 0\leq-\varliminf_{t\downarrow 0}|\phi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}(t)\leq-\varlimsup_{t\downarrow 0}|\phi^{\prime}(t)|^{p-2}\phi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\varlimsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\lambda_{p}(K,N,r)\int_{0}^{t}\phi|\phi|^{p-2}h_{K,N}\,{\mathrm{d}}s}{h_{K,N}(t)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{p}(K,N,r)\varlimsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\phi(t)|\phi|^{p-2}(t)h_{K,N}(t)}{h_{K,N}^{\prime}(t)}=0,

having used the De l’Hôpital’s rule and then that
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{h_{K,N}(t)}{h_{K,N}^{\prime}(t)}=0
.
∎

We conclude with the following chain rule technical lemma.

Lemma B.3
.

Let
K\in\mathbb{R},N>1,p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space, let
B\subset{\rm X}
be a ball of radius
r\in\left(0,\pi\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{K^{+}}}\right)
centred at
x\in{\rm X}
. Let
\phi
be a non-negative minimizer of (
7.1
). Then,
\phi\circ{\sf d}_{x}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B)
and it holds

(B.2)

|D(\phi\circ{\sf d}_{x})|_{p}\leq|\phi^{\prime}|\circ{\sf d}_{x},\quad\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e.\ on }B.

where
|\phi^{\prime}|\circ{\sf d}_{x}
is arbitrarily non-negatively defined on
{\sf d}_{x}^{-1}(\{t\in\mathbb{R}\colon\nexists|\phi^{\prime}|(t)\})
.

Proof.

By locality of the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient, the conclusion (
B.2
) is well-posed. Then, notice that
u\coloneqq\phi\circ{\sf d}_{x}
is a composition of Lipschitz functions with
u,|Du|_{p}\in L^{p}(B)
. Thus
u\in W^{1,p}(B)
and (
B.2
) holds by the chain rule for Sobolev functions (
[
34
]
) (for this claim, local absolutely continuity of
\phi
would be enough, see
[
50
, Lemma 4.5]
). We now prove that
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B)
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, we define
\phi_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq(\phi-\varepsilon)\vee 0
. By a direct computation, we have

|\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\phi(t)|=\varepsilon{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\{\phi\geq\varepsilon\}}(t)+|\phi(t)|{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\{\phi<\varepsilon\}}(t)\leq\varepsilon

for all
t\in(0,r)
and

|\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)-\phi^{\prime}(t)|=|\phi^{\prime}(t)|{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\{0<\phi<\varepsilon\}},

for a.e.
t\in(0,r)
. We define
u_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\phi_{\varepsilon}\circ{\sf d}_{x}
on
B
that satisfies
u_{\varepsilon}\in{\rm Lip}_{bs}(B)
by construction for every
\varepsilon>0
small enough. Moreover, we have that
\|u-u_{\varepsilon}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B)}\leq\int_{B}\left|\phi-\phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{p}\circ{\sf d}_{x}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\leq\varepsilon^{p}\mathfrak{m}(B)
that tends to
0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Next, observe that

\displaystyle\||D(u-u_{\varepsilon})|_{p}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B}|D((\phi-\phi_{\varepsilon})\circ{\sf d}_{x})|_{p}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\overset{\eqref{eq:chain rule eigenf}}{\leq}\int_{B}|\phi^{\prime}-\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{p}\circ{\sf d}_{x}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{0<\phi\circ{\sf d}_{x}<\varepsilon\}\cap B}|\phi^{\prime}|^{p}\circ{\sf d}_{x}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\leq{\rm Lip}(\phi)^{p}\,\mathfrak{m}(\{0<\phi<\varepsilon\}\cap B),

that tends to
0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Thus also
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B)
concluding the proof.
∎

A survey on improving core literacy among impoverished students in Chinese pharmaceutical universities under a development-oriented funding system
Authors: Juan Chen, Jieru Chen, Jiayu Li, Chenyuan Xuan, Xinyi Du, Cheng Jiang, Xinran Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImpoverished students constitute a group that cannot be overlooked in higher education. It is crucial for pharmaceutical universities worldwide to implement financial assistance programs that promote the development and success of students from poor families. Chinese universities have carried out active exploration of subsidized education, and made important achievements, but there are also problems, which are worth learning from. This study proposed a strategy to improve the core literacy of impoverished pharmaceutical students under the development-oriented funding system.MethodsThe study centers on clinical pharmacy students from a pharmaceutical college as the research subjects. A sequential explanatory mixed-methods approach was employed, with quantitative data collected through a survey questionnaire, supplemented by qualitative data collected through in-depth interviews. Data gathered from these two levels were integrated and subjected to statistical analysis.ResultsThe quantitative survey yielded a total of 397 valid samples, most of whom were females (73.8%), mainly at undergraduate level (89.67%), and from non-urban area (73.81%). Five of them participated in further qualitative interviews. The combined data identified: (a) financial aid—most students were highly satisfied with the financial support; (b) psychological support—most of the students interviewed reported that the scholarship significantly improved their self-confidence and motivation; (c) academic guidance—funded students had clear expectations for career development and academic guidance, demonstrating a strong need for further professional study; and (d) employment assistance—most students wanted career guidance and career planning support.ConclusionsThe financial assistance in pharmaceutical colleges and universities should be enriched to resolve the worries of impoverished clinical pharmacy students through economic assistance, improve the moral cultivation with psychological assistance, strengthen their cultural and scientific literacy, and improve their knowledge and practical ability through academic assistance. Through the integration of pharmacy education, the vocational competence of clinical pharmacy students can be improved by employment assistance, and the reform of higher pharmacy education can be further promoted to improve the training quality of pharmaceutical talents.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-024-06343-9.Keywords: Development-oriented funding system, Pharmaceutical universities, Impoverished students, Core literacy, Enhancement pathway

Background

The provision of financial aid is a pivotal component in the advancement and education of pharmacy students at universities. The role of financial aid in facilitating the successful completion of studies for impoverished college students, from low socioeconomic backgrounds, is indispensable. It also fosters their skill development and empowers them to make meaningful contributions to society. Moreover, it significantly contributes towards achieving equitable opportunities in higher education and breaking the cycle of poverty [1].

The development-oriented funding system signifies a significant evolution in the student funding framework within Chinese universities. This new phase builds upon earlier iterations that primarily addressed basic needs and provided emergency support. Currently, the system is marked by more targeted approaches and a diversification of funding methods. Instead of relying solely on grants, the funding landscape now includes a combination of grants, loans, work-study programs, and other financial support mechanisms. This shift reflects a broader commitment to meeting the diverse needs of students and fostering their overall development. Firstly, the beneficiaries of financial aid are becoming more targeted. Originally in 1949, the government implemented a policy of waiving all tuition fees [2]. Then in 1955, the recipients of scholarships were changed from “all students” to “some students”, gradually shifting from universal coverage to targeting impoverished students with financial needs [3]. Secondly, there is diversification in the forms of financial aid. In 1986, the State Council proposed replacing the People’s Scholarship system with scholarships, student loans, and work-study programs [4]. In 1999, the State Council decided that China Industrial and Commercial Bank would handle loan services to assist impoverished students in paying for tuition and daily living expenses during their time at the universities [5]. The “Several Opinions on Further Enhancing the National Student Loan Program” in 2004 [6] and the “Notice on Piloting the Credit-based Student Loan System in Some Regions” in 2007 [7] were officially issued, gradually forming a comprehensive financial aid system. The system encompasses various support mechanisms, including scholarships, grants, student loans, work-study programs, and tuition waivers, which are based on the financial situation and educational development of impoverished students.

The third aspect pertains to the funding targets that prioritize the holistic development of individuals and consider students’ immaterial needs [8]. In 2019, the Party Group of the Ministry of Education issued the “Outline for Enhancing the Quality of Ideological and Political Work in Higher Education Institutions,” emphasizing the integration of “supporting the underprivileged”, “supporting intellectual growth”, and “supporting aspirations”. This outline aims to establish a comprehensive system that enhances both financial assistance and educational quality [9].

The enhancement of “core literacy” represents the operationalization and systematization of “comprehensive human development” [10]. According to the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization, core literacy refers to the essential skills individuals require to pursue their desired lifestyles and contribute effectively to societal functioning [11], thereby enabling them to navigate a future-oriented and unpredictable world [12].

For students studying pharmaceutical sciences, the advancement of medicine is closely related to the general health of citizens. Taking clinical pharmacy as an example, referring to the 2023 Expert Consensus on the Core Literacy Framework for Clinical Pharmacists in China, the Chinese Medical Foundation of the United States, and the Pharmacy disciplines and fields Specialty Committee of the China Hospital Association [13], the core literacy of Chinese students majored in clinical pharmacy is constituted as shown in Table 1.

Core literacy encompasses three dimensions: knowledge, skills, and professional qualities. Knowledge serves as the fundamental basis for the development and enhancement of literacy. Only through accumulated knowledge can students internalize and elevate their cognitive abilities to higher levels. Skills are practical vehicles for literacy; it is only by applying knowledge in practice that one can master the corresponding competencies. Abilities are outward manifestations of literacy, encompassing social communication skills and the ability to collaborate with others across disciplines and fields [14], encapsulating the psychological processes that regulate cognitive activities [15]. Professional qualities are comprehensive attributes demonstrated in personal career growth, such as fundamental civic morality, personality development, work ethic, professionalism, dedication consciousness, and humanistic accomplishments.

In the investigation of whether university funding effectively supports the development of students’ core literacy, researchers have identified several issues. This includes irregular recognition and insufficient funding ratio [16], highlighting the need for improved precision in funding allocation [17]. Additional issues include a relatively single funding channel [18], inadequate financial support for some of the most impoverished students to meet higher education expenses [19], unsatisfactory implementation and execution effects [20], limited effectiveness in certain universities [21], and lack of attention to the impact of university funding on learning and employment due to data limitations [22]. Above all, there has been minimal consideration given to pharmaceutical students’ financial assistance within universities.

To comprehensively address these concerns, we conducted interviews and administered questionnaires to clinical pharmacy students from a pharmaceutical school as an illustrative example. Our aim was to gain insights into the actual implementation status of financial assistance in pharmaceutical universities and analyzing impoverished students’ demands for assistance in developing core literacy. Based on our findings, we propose optimization strategies.

Methods

For this study, an explanatory sequential mixed methods research design was employed. Initially, survey quantitative questionnaires were administered between August and October 2023 to gather quantitative data on students’ perspectives and experiences regarding financial assistance and the development of core literacy. Subsequently, focus group qualitative interviews were conducted from March to May 2024 to obtain a comprehensive qualitative understanding of the findings from the questionnaire survey. The study participants in this study were students from a pharmaceutical specialty pharmacy university located in Jiangsu Province, China.

Participants and procedure

This study site is for research, because the university is affiliated with the National Ministries and Commissions and it recruits students nationwide. Compared to local colleges and universities, the sample of subsidized students is more comprehensive and provides a more generalizable data. On the other hand, the university has established various forms of funding systems including scholarships, grants, loans and work-study programs, creating an ideal practical environment for evaluating and optimizing funding policies.

Eligible participants in the survey were clinical pharmacy students who received financial assistance during their study at China Pharmaceutical University between 2020 and 2023. To ensure confidentiality regarding their financial situation and support received, online questionnaires were privately sent to funded students through their class counsellors. Participants voluntarily and anonymously answered the questionnaires and were assured that their studies would not be adversely affected by their participation in the survey and that results would be reported in aggregates and used solely for academic research purposes.

Quantitative component

Data measurement

A questionnaire combining the characteristics of clinical pharmacy to measure and collect the overall situation of this university student funding and student development requirements was deployed. All of respondents must possess the following characteristics: they should be enrolled between 2020 and 2023, majoring in clinical pharmacy, and have received financial support for more than six months after their enrollment. Before the formal investigation, we randomly selected and interviewed 5 senior experts of the school-level funding center to understand the funding status, determine the questionnaire content, and elicit feedback on the questionnaire. In addition, 10 funded students were randomly selected to conduct a small range of pre-tests; ambiguous questions were revised to improve clarity, understanding, and reliability. In the end, we designed and formulated the “Questionnaire of Students from Low Socioeconomic Families in Chinese Pharmaceutical Colleges and Universities”. The questionnaire contained 30 items, which are broken down into 3 parts: demography, understanding and evaluation of the university funding operation, and quality development demands.

Data analysis

SPSS 27.0 was used to process and analyze the data, including calculating the frequency and proportion of the descriptive statistics; Chi-square analysis was used to assess the differences in responses between the two groups, such as gender and residence. A two-tailed p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Qualitative component

Data collection

Face-to-face semi-structured interviews were conducted using the “Interview Outline of Students from Low Socioeconomic Families in Pharmaceutical Colleges and Universities in China” developed by the investigators (Supplementary T1). We drafted the interview guide, which sets a series of semi-open questions focusing on four areas: economic status, academic status, financial support, and further development needs. The outline is closely related to the questionnaire, which is mutually verified with quantitative analysis. Before conducting a survey, data collectors received uniform training to ensure consistency in the data collection process.

Data analysis

We ensured all interviews were recorded with the consent of the participants and transcribed verbatim into transcripts. The transcription process paid attention to details and tried to restore the true expression of the participants. The transcribed text was proofread and discussed by both interviewers and transcribers to ensure the accuracy and transparency of the transcribed content. Finally, the interview content was summarized according to the interview outline to form a structured content summary to ensure the systematization and depth of the analysis.

Results

Of the students who participated (N = 563), 399 (70.87%) completed the survey and 5 participated in the interview. Excluding invalid questionnaires with more than 1 missing value, incomplete questionnaires and the same options, 397 valid questionnaires were obtained, with an effective rate of 99.5%. Most of the participants were female (73.80%), mostly from non-urban area (73.80%), and non-only children (72.04%).

The proportion of family members receiving higher education is low. Although China’s gross enrollment rate of higher education has exceeded 60% [23]— marking a new stage in the popularization of higher education, Table 2 shows that only 39.55% of the parents or siblings of impoverished students have received higher education. This reflects that the education level of families with impoverished students is still below the new national average.

Overall situation of funding operation

Students’ overall evaluation of the school’s financial assistance program

Generally, the school’s financial aid efforts meet students’ satisfaction. According to Supplementary T2, more than 56.17% of students expressed a high level of satisfaction (completely satisfied and satisfied). Additionally, 94.71% of students perceive fairness in the scholarship assessment process (i.e., completely fair, fair, and moderately fair). Education integrity pertains to the provision of guidance aimed at fostering honesty and trustworthiness among students. It emphasizes the importance of maintaining good credit standing, and effectively managing their student loans, as well as offering psychological support and competence quality training by the institution. Here, a high level of satisfaction was reported by 68.27% of students (completely satisfied and satisfied).” Overall, the current financial aid for students in pharmaceutical colleges and universities has achieved good results, and most respondents believe that “the current financial aid programs have basically met the economic needs of the school”, but some students were still dissatisfied with the current situation and stated that these programs still needed to be improved and be more targeted.

Details of the funding operation

The means and mechanisms for students to understand the universities’ financial aid policies still necessitate further enhancement and enrichment. As shown in Fig. 1A, only a mere 1.51% of students know the policies extensively, while 26.70% have only a basic understanding of the policies. Among the entire surveyed student population, 83.12% acquired information about the financial assistance policies mainly through guidance from their homeroom teachers (college counsellors) and 70.53% rely on school publicity materials (Fig. 1B).

Financial aid

The study investigated the diverse sources of tuition fees and living expenses of impoverished students. According to Table 3, student loans accounted for 38.04% of tuition fee payments, while various scholarships and grants covered 29.97% of students’ educational expenses. Additionally, work-study programs and part-time jobs contributed to 15.11% of students’ fees, which is relatively low in comparison. The main source of funding was derived from their families to varying degrees, with 76.07% of students’ tuition fees and 91.69% of students’ living expenses being covered separately. However, the precise proportion of parental contributions and school remissions towards tuition fees and living expenses has yet to be confirmed. Currently, only 18.39% of the students rely on work-study programs as a source for covering their living expenses. Furthermore, 28.46% of them have not receive financial assistance for both tuition and living expenses. Table 4 also suggests that approximately half (48.87%) of the surveyed students believe that the number of work-study programs provided by the university needs to be further enriched and expanded. Some students interviewed reflected that the current work-study programs mainly included positions such as counsellor, office assistant, librarian, cafeteria part-time, etc., which required a lot of extra time to participate in, and were not suitable for students with too many courses to participate in fragmented time outside of their study schedule.

Psychological support

Overall, financial assistance plays a positive role in fostering students’ mental well-being. According to Supplementary T3, nearly all recipients of the National Inspiration Scholarship (97.37%) report that the scholarship has significantly alleviated their financial burdens. Besides, a noteworthy percentage of recipients (64.47%) indicated that receiving the scholarship has fostered a renewed motivation and enthusiasm for learning. One respondent highlighted that scholarships not only contribute to economic improvement but also serve as validation of academic achievement. These benefits can play a critical role in mitigating feelings of self-doubt and excessive introversion that often arise from family economic hardships, thereby supporting the overall development and well-being of these students. Additionally, students stated in the interviews that the friendly perspectives of their classmates regarding their family background and the support for their work-study activities also enhanced their self-confidence. Lastly, an overwhelming majority of students (92.11%) believed that the scholarship programs effectively stimulated their motivation to study and bolstered their intrinsic drive for academic pursuits.

Table 4 indicates that 37.28% of the students perceived their peers as reluctant to disclose their families’ financial difficulties in order to “save face”. Four respondents from qualitative interviews noted that while work-study opportunities, scholarship and other funding programs are generally acceptable, they still carry a certain sense of shame associated with it. Consequently, it is imperative for financial assistance programs to prioritize safeguarding students’ privacy and fostering an environment where they feel comfortable confronting challenges and collaboratively resolving issues. In terms of the integrity of financial aid, 16.88% of the students found that some individuals within their social circle received state grants despite not facing any genuine financial hardships, while 58.69% remained uncertain about this matter. This is in line with the respondents’ statement that “if the quota is assessed only according to the ranking of academic achievements and financial difficulties, people have no opinion, but if other factors are taken into account, they think it is unfair.” Therefore, there is a pressing need to further cultivate a sense of integrity among students.

Academic guidance

The findings indicate that, despite from low socioeconomic families, students exhibit a resolute aspiration to pursue further education. According to the survey on educational aspirations (Fig. 2), 65.74% of students expressed their inclination towards employment after obtaining a master’s degree, while 25.94% aimed to pursue a doctorate before entering the workforce. Remarkably, only 8.31% intended to seek employment immediately upon completing their bachelor’s degree, warranting an in-depth investigation.

Survey on educational attainment expected by impoverished students. (N = 397)

In a survey concerning on social practice activities (Supplementary T4), 60.45% of students expressed their intention to obtain professional qualification certificates or skill-level certifications, while 54.66% aspire to engage in internships, social practices, or field surveys. Additionally, 52.64% harbored hopes of contributing to professional academic journals/academic conferences before graduation, and 49.87% desired participation in various academic, professional or design competitions. This suggests a robust demand for learning among students, highlighting the need to enhance academic support in terms of knowledge and skills specific to their professional field for the advancement of core literacy.

Employment assistance

According to the results from the survey on assistance methods (Table 5), employment support emerged as the most sought-after option (68.77%), followed closely by opportunities for training abilities (60.71%). However, during the focus group interviews with student participants, it became evident that a significant concern repeatedly raised was the limited access to information regarding overseas study programs and postgraduate admission examinations. When combined with the survey results, it becomes evident that behind students’ strong demand for further study information lies their aspiration for improved employment prospects and higher income through higher education as a means of addressing economic challenges. Additionally, several participants expressed anxiety about future employment prospects and felt pressured to pursue further education out of fear and avoidance. Furthermore, students emphasized their aspirations for increased access to training opportunities, encompassing communication skills, formal writing techniques and social etiquette – all indicative of a lack of confidence in the requisite abilities demanded in professional settings.

Discussion

Overall, the financial assistance provided by Chinese universities has transitioned from a singular approach focused on poverty alleviation to a diversified support system that addresses the holistic development of students [24]. Pharmacy students generally express satisfaction with the current university financial aid. However, they still require diverse forms of assistance to enhance core literacy, improve abilities, and enrich their spiritual life and moral values. Such support is crucial for breaking the cycle of educational disadvantage linked to family poverty and for effectively addressing the evolving needs of students.

University financial aid helps impoverished students alleviate their burdens so that they can concentrate on their studies and enhance their knowledge and skills. It also fosters confidence and motivation through academic achievements while facilitating peer communication, thereby further enhancing teamwork skills for future career development and fostering a greater sense of belonging and happiness among students. Conversely, prolonged reliance on financial aid may lead some students to develop a dependency mentality or lack of self-drive or independent problem-solving ability. Additionally, individual psychological needs for developmental support may not be fully considered during the provision of financial aid which could potentially impact both academic performance and personal growth.

The long-term potential impact of university funding on students’ academic development is intricate and extensive. Therefore, it is imperative to address potential challenges and implement corresponding strategies such as psychological construction and capacity support to facilitate students’ comprehensive development. In terms of improvement strategies, adhering to a value orientation that integrates personal, social, and national development while engaging multiple stakeholders is crucial. University funding should play a pivotal role in ensuring support by enhancing the promotion and implementation of funding policies through diverse platforms both within and outside the campus. This will broaden the developmental path for impoverished students in Chinese pharmaceutical universities and enhance the effectiveness of cultivating core literacy. We believe that the enhancement of core literacy can be achieved through a multifaceted approach, incorporating financial assistance, psychological support, academic aid, and employment guidance.

Take multiple measures to help alleviate students’ concerns with financial assistance

The primary objective of economic support is to assist impoverished students in resolving the issue of economic pressure. Financial support serves as the material foundation for ensuring a conducive learning environment and enhancing core literacy. The current state reveals a disparity between the financial aid offered by colleges and the evolving needs of students, indicating insufficient alignment between the financial assistance programs and students’ requirements for completing the program.

Expand sources of financial aid

Colleges and universities have effectively mobilized both internal and external resources, diversifying their funding sources, and establishing additional scholarships, grants and individual hardship allowances. They have assumed the roles of various social actors, fostering a harmonious coexistence between governmental and social assistance while leveraging the potential of public welfare organizations.

Promote accurate financial aid recognition

The eradication of absolute poverty in China has led to an expansion in recognizing the scope of financial assistance, taking into account regional development differences. Furthermore, the adoption of information technology and refinement of the process have been implemented to enhance the effectiveness of educational financial assistance.

Enrich the forms of work-study programs

Colleges and universities can implement the “study in lieu of work” program, enabling students to actively engage in both on- and off-campus activities, achieve excellent academic performance, publish theses, and participate in supported teaching activities, among other endeavors. These accomplishments can be considered as alternatives to work-study programs and receive corresponding financial support.

Prioritize education by offering psychological assistance and aiding in the development of students’ individual character

The value of financial aid lies in its people-oriented approach, which is also the core concept of our work. Impoverished students who aspire to become qualified pharmacy professionals must possess not only the courage to overcome obstacles but also a sound personality and an unwavering belief in promoting health. To enhance their moral character, we emphasize education on integrity, ethics and related aspects.

Reinforce the dissemination of educational programs on ideal beliefs and convictions

To foster the alignment of citizens’ moral values with the growth needs of pharmaceutical students, it is imperative to integrate them through specific cases and events for value education. This amalgamation of personal development within the collective and society entails prioritizing individuals’ well-being, nurturing patriotism, and wholeheartedly committing oneself to one’s profession.

Reinforce the publicity of educational programs on gratitude and integrity

The current financial aid certification system suffers from inaccuracies in data submission and artificial delinquency in student loan repayments. To address these issues, it is necessary to enhance financial literacy and promote integrity among impoverished students, encouraging them to repay their loans promptly while fostering self-reliance, self-improvement, honesty, and trustworthiness. Additionally, the promotion of gratitude education should be further strengthened through lectures, essays, and other effective means to shape and influence the emotional aspects of the recipients. Gratitude education entails recognizing and expressing appreciation for the diverse forms of support provided by educational institutions. Following personal development, individuals are more inclined to assist those in need within their community and collaborate towards advancing societal progress. This will cultivate a profound sense of gratitude and bolster social responsibility, ultimately achieving a harmonious unity between financial aid and education.

Reinforce the promotion of educational programs on science and technology ethics

The awareness of science and technology ethics in the pharmaceutical industry is cultivated among pharmacy students through various means, such as theme activities and institutional lectures. This helps them enhance their ethical thinking ability and judgement regarding cutting-edge bioscience and technology. Moreover, they are encouraged to contribute to the global common interest, health and national development by creating value from a perspective aligned with the community of human destiny.

Arts and science, with academic support to help enhance students’ knowledge and practical ability

The students from low socioeconomic families have a strong demand for learning, and the level of academic support needed to enhance their core literacy in terms of specialized knowledge and skills needs to be strengthened. This can be achieved by providing assistance in both cultural and scientific literacy. Core literacy emphasizes the exploration of meaning and fosters students’ ability to contribute effectively to society and promote health in their future clinical pharmacy practice. Scientific literacy underscores precision and meticulousness, nurturing pharmacy students’ awareness of the pursuit of truth, rigorous logical thinking, and scientific reasoning in their professional education.

Strengthening humanistic education

Students hailing from rural areas exhibit a heightened inclination towards comprehensive training encompassing communication skills, document composition, social decorum, and other integrated attributes. Colleges and universities are incorporating humanities courses, such as ethics, art appreciation, and analysis of classic works into the clinical pharmacy curriculum. This underscores the emphasis on fostering students’ personal qualities and social development. Only through the application of humanistic care can financial aid be more readily embraced by students, thereby ultimately facilitating the comprehensive implementation of the essential student development qualities [25].

Strengthening scientific literacy education

Scientific literacy is one of the core competencies for assisting pharmacy students in navigating the professional landscape, encompassing the knowledge, practical skills, and values that individuals should possess when dealing with interpersonal relationships with others, nature, and the society [26].

The cultivation of scientific literacy necessitates concerted efforts in delivering scientific knowledge, fostering scientific skills, and instilling scientific ideology and ethical values [27]. It is imperative for colleges and universities to encourage students to integrate online and offline learning, incorporate the promotion of scientific literacy into their daily academic pursuits, and foster a scientifically inclined mindset that upholds reverence for science and unwavering commitment to truth, supporting students’ needs for further education and academic advancement.

The combination of pharmacy practice and pharmacy education to help enhance students’ vocational competence with employment assistance

Vocational competence represents the professional embodiment of pharmacy students’ core qualities in terms of their expertise and practical skills. The cultivation of vocational competence serves as a crucial link between on-campus study and employment, aiming to support students from low socioeconomic families in enhancing their awareness, professionalism, and career development abilities. The combination of pharmacy practice and pharmacy education necessitates a strengthened collaboration between hospitals and academic institutions, whereby pharmacy students are introduced to the clinical practice environment through the incorporation of hospital pharmacy practice into the pharmacy school curriculum. This objective is accomplished through the implementation of pharmaceutical clinical research application projects as a career, with the aim of cultivating skills for impoverished students. By providing financial support, their vocational competence can be further enhanced.

Integration of vocational elements into the pharmacy curriculum

The curriculum design integrates essential professional courses necessary for students’ career development with other relevant disciplines. This integration aims to enhance students’ active engagement in clinical pharmacy experiences and to deepen their understanding of various professional fields. For instance, students may unconsciously absorb professional knowledge, while hands-on demonstrations and intuitive teaching methods can reinforce positive professional attitudes through effective education.

Integration of the practice project into professional learning

By transforming the conventional model of pharmaceutical talent cultivation, which is discipline-based and teacher-centric, our objective is to transition towards a student-centered approach that prioritizes problem-solving through clinical drug research projects. This will facilitate the integration of knowledge across diverse disciplines among students, empowering them to effectively combine and apply solutions to complex clinical issues in uncertain future scenarios. Through project-based learning, we closely align students’ educational direction, academic development of university faculty, and technological innovation in the pharmaceutical industry with major national demands. Teachers and students together form a long-term and stable academic community that better supports impoverished students in enhancing their vocational competence while strengthening the development of core literacy.

Strengthening innovation and entrepreneurship

The funding system of pharmaceutical universities incorporates the cultivation of innovation spirit and entrepreneurial ability through collaboration between pharmaceutical schools and universities. On the one hand, innovation and entrepreneurship activities are conducted through social practice, quality development, disciplinary competitions, and entrepreneurial project incubation to cultivate the students’ innovation ability. Simultaneously, “innovation and entrepreneurship” are employed to stimulate “professional creativity” among clinical pharmacy students for academic funding and regional economic development in relation to the transformation and upgrading of the pharmaceutical industry. This forms a combination of pharmacy practice and pharmacy education that supports an atmosphere of innovation and entrepreneurship for college students.

Strengths and limitations

This study makes distinct contributions to the field by expanding the literature with a comprehensive examination of financial assistance for impoverished pharmacy students. Utilizing a mixed-methods research approach, it elucidates the realities and developmental needs of students receiving supports. Additionally, it establishes a systematic pathway for enhancing the core literacy of impoverished students based on a widely recognized competency framework for pharmacy education.

However, since the site involved in this research is affiliated with the national ministry, the sample of impoverished students is relatively comprehensive, and the funding system is largely well-structured. As a result, the findings may not be directly applicable to local colleges and universities that have diverse student populations and varying financial aid systems. Future studies should investigate the applicability of this study’s conclusions across different student demographics and funding systems.

Moreover, the cross-sectional nature of this study does not account for temporal dynamics. Future research could benefit from longitudinal designs that track pharmacy graduates who have experienced financial aids. Lastly, despite assurances of anonymity, potential respondent bias may have influenced the results. Participants may have felt inclined to provide responses that align with societal expectations or may not have fully disclosed accurate information regarding sensitive issues.

Conclusion

The training of clinical pharmacy personnel involves a cross-disciplinary approach that integrates pharmacy, clinical medicine, and higher education. With the emergence of new forms of healthcare and evolving needs, there is a constant demand for enhancing the core literacy of impoverished students. Pharmaceutical universities should assist these students in overcoming their challenges by providing financial aid, psychological support, academic guidance, and employment assistance. Currently, investigating the implementation of funding in pharmaceutical universities, elucidating the developmental needs of students from low socioeconomic families, and proposing a comprehensive framework for fostering core competencies through financial support, ideological guidance, academic assistance, and employment facilitation can significantly enhance students’ ability to overcome professional development challenges. This will unlock their potential and cultivate the knowledge, skills, abilities, and professional qualities essential for academic success and professional advancement while concurrently advancing higher pharmaceutical education reform.

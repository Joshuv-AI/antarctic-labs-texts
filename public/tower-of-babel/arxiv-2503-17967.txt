Murmurations of Hecke L L -Functions of Imaginary Quadratic Fields
Zeyu Wang

Abstract
We calculate the murmuration density for the family of Hecke L L -functions of imaginary quadratic fields associated to non-trivial characters. This density exhibits a universality property like Zubrilina’s density for the murmurations of holomorphic modular forms. We show all murmuration functions obtained by averaging over the family with a compactly supported smooth weight function has asymptotics compatible with the 1-level density conjecture of Katz and Sarnak. The novelty of the murmurations of this family of L L -functions is its pronounced almost periodic feature, which allows one to describe this murmuration without averaging over primes, and which is non-existent or previously unnoticed for other families.

1.2
General Formulation of Murmurations

Here we formulate the murmuration phenomenon in general, following Sarnak’s letter
[
Sar23
]
to Sutherland and Zubrilina.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a family of self-dual arithmetic
L
-functions ordered by conductor. For
f\in\mathscr{F}
, let
N_{f}
be the conductor of
f
,
\varepsilon(f)
be the root number of
f
, and
a_{f}(p)
be its Frobenius trace at
p
, normalized so that Ramanujan’s conjecture states that
\left\lvert a_{f}(p)\right\rvert\ll\sqrt{p}
. Note that the self-dual condition implies that
\varepsilon(f)=\pm 1
and
a_{f}(p)\in\mathbb{R}
. We are interested in the weighted average of the
a_{f}(p)
among those
f\in\mathscr{F}
with conductor
N_{f}
multiplicatively close to
N
(a “vertical average”). Precisely, for any compactly supported smooth weight function
\Phi:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, we look for a function
M_{\Phi}(\xi)
such that

G_{\Phi}(p,N):=\frac{\sum_{f\in\mathscr{F}}a_{f}(p)\varepsilon(f)\Phi\left(\frac{N_{f}}{N}\right)}{\sum_{f\in\mathscr{F}}\Phi\left(\frac{N_{f}}{N}\right)}=M_{\Phi}\left(\frac{p}{N}\right)+R_{\Phi}(p,N)

(1.1)

and such that the error term satisfies is negligible after averaging over primes in short intervals, i.e.

\frac{\sum_{p\in[P,P+H]}R_{\Phi}(p,N)}{\sum_{p\in[P,P+H]}1}=o(1)

(1.2)

where
H=o(N)
is some parameter. We then say
M_{\Phi}(\xi)
is the
murmuration function for
\mathscr{F}
with weight
\Phi
.

In other words, if we double sum over
f\in\mathscr{F}
and
p
, then the weighted average of
a_{f}(p)
converges to
M_{\Phi}(p/N)
as
p,N\to\infty
with
\xi=p/N
fixed. After switching the order of averaging, these double averages are related via explicit formulae to the 1-level densities of the zeros of
f\in\mathscr{F}
, which are conjectured by Katz-Sarnak in
[
KS99
]
to depend on the symmetry type of the family
\mathscr{F}
. Based on the 1-level density conjecture, one expects that

\displaystyle\mathop{Exp}\displaylimits_{p\sim N^{a}}[G_{\Phi}(p,N)]

\displaystyle\to\begin{cases}0&\text{if }a<1\\
-\frac{1}{2}&\text{if }a>1\end{cases}

\displaystyle(\text{if $\mathscr{F}$ has $\Sp(\infty)$ symmetry})

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathop{Exp}\displaylimits_{p\sim N^{a}}[G_{\Phi}(p,N)]

\displaystyle\to\begin{cases}0&\text{if }a<1\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if }a>1\end{cases}

\displaystyle(\text{if $\mathscr{F}$ has $\O(\infty)$ symmetry})

(1.4)

for any reasonable weight function
\Phi
(see
[
Sar23
]
, p.5, Equation 7’). Thus we expect

M_{\Phi}(0)=0\and\lim_{\xi\to\infty}M_{\Phi}(\xi)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}&\text{if $\mathscr{F}$ has $\Sp(\infty)$ symmetry,}\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if $\mathscr{F}$ has $\O(\infty)$ symmetry.}\end{cases}

(1.5)

Therefore, the murmuration function
M_{\Phi}(\xi)
on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
interpolates the phase transition of the 1-level density in the critical range
p\sim N
, corresponding to the discontinuity at
a=1
of
(1.3)
and
(1.4)
.

The murmuration function depends on the choice of the weight function
\Phi
, and the more universal object is the “murmuration density.” In fact, if as
X,Y,p\to\infty
with
Y=o(X)
we have

G(p,X,Y):=\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathscr{F}\\
N_{f}\in[X,X+Y]\end{subarray}}a_{f}(p)\varepsilon(f)}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathscr{F}\\
N_{f}\in[X,X+Y]\end{subarray}}1}=M\left(\frac{p}{X}\right)+R(p,X,Y)

(1.6)

with
R(p,X,Y)
negligible after averaging over primes in intervals of length
H=o(X)
, then from
M
we can recover
M_{\Phi}
for any
\Phi
by

M_{\Phi}(\xi)=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}M(\xi/u)\Phi(u)u^{\delta}\frac{du}{u}}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(u)u^{\delta}\frac{du}{u}}.

(1.7)

In this case, we call
M
the
murmuration density for
\mathscr{F}
, and this is the object of central interest. Here
\delta
is the
conductor density
of the family
\mathscr{F}
, which we assume satisfies

\#\{f\in\mathscr{F}:N_{f}\leq N\}\asymp N^{\delta}.

(1.8)

For example, the conductor density is
\delta=2
for the family of interest in
[
Zub23
]
, and is
\delta=1
for the families considered in
[
LOP24
]
,
[
Bob+23
]
, and
[
Boo+24
]
.

It is a natural question how much one needs to average over
p
to obtain the murmuration density in
(1.6)
, i.e. how short one may take
H=o(X)
such that

\mathop{Exp}\displaylimits_{p\in[P,P+H]}R(p,X,Y)=o(1).

It is hypothesized in
[
Sar23
]
that one may take any
H=X^{\gamma}
such that
\gamma+\delta>1
, where
\delta
is again the conductor density. For example, in
[
Zub23
]
one has
\delta=2
and
\gamma=0
; that is, the family is sufficiently large such that no averaging over
p
is required at all to obtain the murmuration density.

In this paper, we compute the murmuration density for the family
\mathscr{F}
of
L
-functions associated to non-trivial Hecke characters on the class groups of imaginary quadratic fields
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})
with
D>3,D\equiv 3\ (\text{mod}\ 4)
and square-free. It is conjectured in
[
Ser75
]
that these dihedral forms make up the majority of holomorphic modular forms of weight 1. We also show that the murmuration function
M_{\Phi}
for any compactly supported smooth weight function
\Phi
has the asymptotic behavior in accordance with the 1-level density conjecture, i.e.
M_{\Phi}(\xi)\to-\frac{1}{2}
as
\xi\to\infty
.

Interestingly, although the conductor density for our family
\mathscr{F}
is
\delta=\frac{3}{2}
, we still need to average a little over
p
in order to recover the murmuration density as defined by
(1.6)
in
[
Sar23
]
. However, without the averaging over
p
we obtain an
almost periodic
murmuration density, which is arguably more interesting and revealing in its own right.

1.3
Properties of the Family
\mathscr{F}

Here we collect some basic properties of the family
\mathscr{F}
(as well as the Hecke
L
-functions associated to trivial characters).

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})
be an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant
-D\equiv 1\ (\text{mod}\ 4),D>3
, let
\psi
be a (possibly trivial) character on
\Cl(K)
, and let
f(s)=L(s,\psi)
be the corresponding Hecke
L
-function:

L(s,\psi)=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\psi(\mathfrak{a})(N\mathfrak{a})^{-s}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(1-\psi(\mathfrak{p})(N\mathfrak{p})^{-s}\right)^{-1},

(1.9)

where
\mathfrak{a}
runs over all the integral ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
\mathfrak{p}
runs over all the prime ideals. Writing
f
as a Dirichlet series
f(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{f}(n)n^{-s}
, then the Frobenius traces are given by

a_{f}(p)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p},N\mathfrak{p}=p}\psi(\mathfrak{p})=\begin{cases}0&\left(\frac{-D}{p}\right)=-1\Leftrightarrow p\text{ inert},\\
\psi(\mathfrak{p})+\psi(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})&\left(\frac{-D}{p}\right)=1\Leftrightarrow p\text{ split},p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}},\\
\psi(\mathfrak{p})&\left(\frac{-D}{p}\right)=0\Leftrightarrow p\text{ ramified},p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}^{2}.\end{cases}

(1.10)

The completed function

\Lambda(s,\psi)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{D}}{2\pi}\right)^{s}\Gamma(s)L(s,\psi)

is entire, with only possibly a simple pole at
s=1
when
\psi=\mathbbold{1}
is trivial with residue

\res_{s=1}\Lambda(s,\mathbbold{1})=\frac{\sqrt{D}}{2\pi}L(1,\chi_{-D})=\frac{1}{2}h(-D),

where
\chi_{-D}=\left(\frac{-D}{\cdot}\right)
is the Kronecker symbol and
h(-D)=\#\Cl(K)
is the class number. Moreover, these
L
-functions satisfy the functional equation

\Lambda(s,\psi)=\Lambda(1-s,\psi),

so members
f\in\mathscr{F}
have root number
\varepsilon(f)=1
always, which differs from the family considered in
[
Zub23
]
.

The class number formula along with the bound
\left\lvert D\right\rvert^{-\epsilon}\ll L(1,\chi_{D})\ll\left\lvert D\right\rvert^{\epsilon}
implies that the family
\mathscr{F}
has conductor density
\delta=\frac{3}{2}
.

Easy Evaluations

In this section we simplify

N_{f}\in[X,X+Y]\end{subarray}}a_{f}(p)\sqrt{p}}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathscr{F}\\
N_{f}\in[X,X+Y]\end{subarray}}1}

2.1
Orthogonality of Characters

The denominator of
(2.1)
is easily rewritten as

G_{\denom}(p,X,Y):=\#\{f\in\mathscr{F}:N_{f}\in[X,X+Y]\}=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}}(h(-D)-1),

where

\mathcal{D}=\{D\in[X,X+Y]:D\equiv 3\ (\text{mod}\ 4),D\ \square\text{-free}\}.

(2.2)

The numerator, on the other hand, can be rewritten using orthogonality of characters. Specifically,

\displaystyle G_{\num}(p,X,Y)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathscr{F}\\
N_{f}\in[X,X+Y]\end{subarray}}a_{f}(p)\sqrt{p}=\sqrt{p}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi:\Cl(K)\to S^{1}\\
\psi\neq\mathbbold{1}\end{subarray}}a_{f}(p),

where
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})
and
f=L(s,\psi)
. By
(1.10)
, if
\left(\frac{-D}{p}\right)=-1
then the inner sum vanishes, and otherwise it becomes a sum over the nontrivial characters of
\psi(\mathfrak{p})+\psi(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})
if
p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
splits, or of
\psi(\mathfrak{p})
if
p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}^{2}
ramifies. By orthogonality of characters,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in(\Cl(K))^{*}\\
\psi\neq\mathbbold{1}\end{subarray}}\psi(\mathfrak{p})=\mathbbold{1}_{\mathfrak{p}}h(-D)-1,

where
\mathbbold{1}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the indicator function for
\mathfrak{p}
being a principal ideal. If

\sqrt{p}(h(-p)-1)\ll p^{1+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\ll V^{1+\epsilon}\sqrt{pX}\sum_{r\leq\sqrt{T}}\sum_{k\leq T/r^{2}}\frac{(kr)^{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{k}}

\displaystyle\ll T^{1/2+\epsilon}V^{1+\epsilon}\sqrt{pX}.

3.3
Evaluation and Summation of
C_{8na^{2},p,a}

The goal of this section is to show

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(a)}{8n^{2}a^{2}}C_{8na^{2},p,a}=\frac{p+1}{3p}\prod_{\ell>2,\left(\frac{p}{\ell}\right)=1}\left(1-2\ell^{-2}-\frac{2\ell^{-3}}{1-\ell^{-2}}\right)=c(p),

as in
(1.19)
.

Recall from
(3.23)
,
(3.24)
, and Lemma
3.1
that

C_{8na^{2},p,a}=\begin{cases}C_{8n,p}R_{a,p}&\text{if $(n,a)=1$,}\\
0&\mathrm{otherwise},\end{cases}

where

C_{8n,p}=\sum_{0\leq x<8n}\mathbbold{1}(x)\left(\frac{x^{2}-4p}{n}\right),\qquad R_{a,p}=\begin{cases}2^{k}&a=q_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots q_{k}^{e_{k}},\left(\frac{p}{q_{j}}\right)=1\ \forall j,\\
0&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
n=2^{e}p^{e^{\prime}}p_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{e_{k}}
(with
e
and
e^{\prime}
possibly zero), then
C_{8n,p}
splits as a product:

C_{8n,p}={\underbrace{\vphantom{\sum_{x\mod{p^{e_{0}}}}\left(\frac{x^{2}}{p}\right)^{e_{0}}}\sum_{x\text{ mod }2^{e+3}\text{ odd}}\left(\frac{x^{2}-4p}{2}\right)^{e}}_{\text{4 if $e=0$, $4(-2)^{e}$ otherwise}}}\times{\underbrace{\sum_{x\mod p^{e^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{x^{2}-4p}{p}\right)^{e^{\prime}}}_{\text{1 if $e^{\prime}=0$, $(p-1)p^{e^{\prime}-1}$ otherwise}}}\\
\times{\prod_{e_{j}\text{ even}}p_{j}^{e_{j}-1}\underbrace{\left[\sum_{x\mod{p_{j}}}\left(\frac{x^{2}-4p}{p_{j}}\right)^{2}\right]}_{p_{j}-1-\left(\frac{p}{p_{j}}\right)}}\times{\prod_{e_{j}\text{ odd}}p_{j}^{e_{j}-1}\underbrace{\left[\sum_{x\mod{p_{j}}}\left(\frac{x^{2}-4p}{p_{j}}\right)\right]}_{-1\ (\text{see Lemma \ref{lem:sum_of_(x^2-a/p)_over_residue_class}})}}.

(3.25)

We note that this along with Lemma
3.1
gives
(3.14)
, i.e.

C_{8na^{2},n,p}\ll\frac{a^{\epsilon}n}{k(n)},

as promised.

Now we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(a)}{8n^{2}a^{2}}C_{8na^{2},p,a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{8n,p}}{8n^{2}}\sum_{a\text{ odd},(n,a)=1}\frac{\mu(a)R_{a,p}}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{8n,p}}{8n^{2}}\prod_{\ell\nmid 2n,\left(\frac{p}{\ell}\right)=1}\left(1-2\ell^{-2}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\ell>2,\left(\frac{p}{\ell}\right)=1}\left(1-2\ell^{-2}\right)\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{8n,p}}{8n^{2}}\prod_{\ell>2,\ell\mid n,\left(\frac{p}{\ell}\right)=1}\frac{1}{1-2\ell^{-2}}.

(3.26)

The sum over
n
is multiplicative, and the local factors are:

•

At 2 (along with the factor
1/8
):

\frac{1}{8}\left(4+\sum_{e=1}^{\infty}\frac{4(-2)^{e}}{2^{2e}}\right)=\frac{1}{3}.

•

At
p
:

1+\sum_{e^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\frac{(p-1)p^{e^{\prime}-1}}{p^{2e^{\prime}}}=\frac{p+1}{p}.

•

At
p_{j}>2,\left(\frac{p}{p_{j}}\right)=1
:

1+\frac{1}{1-2p_{j}^{-2}}\left(\sum_{e_{j}\text{ odd}}\frac{-p_{j}^{e_{j}-1}}{p_{j}^{2e_{j}}}+\sum_{e_{j}\text{ even}}\frac{p_{j}^{e_{j}-1}(p_{j}-2)}{p_{j}^{2e_{j}}}\right)=1-\frac{2p_{j}^{-3}}{\left(1-2p_{j}^{-2}\right)\left(1-p_{j}^{-2}\right)}.

•

At
p_{j},\left(\frac{p}{p_{j}}\right)=-1
:

1+\frac{1}{1-2p_{j}^{-2}}\left(\sum_{e_{j}\text{ odd}}\frac{-p_{j}^{e_{j}-1}}{p_{j}^{2e_{j}}}+\sum_{e_{j}\text{ even}}\frac{p_{j}^{e_{j}}}{p_{j}^{2e_{j}}}\right)=1.

Putting these into
(3.26)
gives

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(a)}{8n^{2}a^{2}}C_{8na^{2},p,a}=\prod_{\ell>2,\left(\frac{p}{\ell}\right)=1}\left(1-2\ell^{-2}\right)\cdot\frac{p+1}{3p}\prod_{\ell>2,\left(\frac{p}{\ell}\right)=1}\left(1-\frac{2\ell^{-3}}{\left(1-2\ell^{-2}\right)\left(1-\ell^{-2}\right)}\right)=c(p),

as desired.

4
Evaluation of

In this section, we generalize the work for

and evaluate

for arbitrary

imposes arithmetic restrictions on

modulo primes dividing

\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)=1
for all odd prime

, and to account for this we introduce an indicator function

which is periodic with period

. Computationally, the strategy is the same, but the evaluation of the more term is more arduous.

Fixing some

, we will show:

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume

tend to

; in particular, we assume

is bounded away from

, i.e. there exists

such that

where

\displaystyle\delta_{y}(p)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathbbold{1}\left\{\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)=1\right\}&\text{if $y=q^{k}$ where $q$ is an odd prime,}\\
\mathbbold{1}\{p\equiv 3\ (\text{mod}\ 4)\}&\text{if $y=2$},\\
\mathbbold{1}\{p\equiv 5\ (\text{mod}\ 8)\}&\text{if $y=4$},\\
\mathbbold{1}\{p\equiv 1\ (\text{mod}\ 8)\}&\text{if $y=2^{\nu}$ and $\nu\geq 3$,}\end{cases}

(4.2)

(extended multiplicatively in
y
)

\displaystyle\vartheta(y)

\displaystyle=2^{\omega(y)+\min(v_{2}(y),2)}\prod_{q\mid y\text{ odd}}\left(1+\frac{2q^{2}+q-1}{q^{4}-3q^{2}-2q+2}\right),

(4.3)

as in
(1.20)
and
(1.21)
.

Again the main term is only present for
\xi>y^{2}/4
; otherwise, since solutions

must satisfy
y^{2}<\frac{4p}{D}\leq 4\xi
, we have

trivially.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

By the above,
G_{\num,y}^{+}=0
if
y\geq 2\sqrt{\xi}
. Therefore it follows Theorem
4.1
and results from Section
2.2
that

\displaystyle G(p,X,Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{1\leq y<2\sqrt{\xi}}G_{\num,y}^{+}+G_{\num}^{-}+O\left(p^{1+\epsilon}\right)}{G_{\denom}}

\displaystyle=c(p)\sum_{1\leq y<2\sqrt{\xi}}\frac{11\zeta(2)}{4A}\sqrt{\frac{\xi}{4\xi-y^{2}}}\,\vartheta(y)\delta_{y}(p)-\frac{11\pi}{12A}\sqrt{\xi}+\Err,

where

\displaystyle\Err

\displaystyle\ll_{\delta_{\xi}}\begin{multlined}\frac{1}{Y\sqrt{X}}\left[\frac{p}{X}\left(p^{5/7}X^{(7+\sqrt{37})/21}Y^{1/7}+\frac{Y^{2}}{X^{1/2}}\right)(pXY)^{\epsilon}+p^{7/10+\epsilon}X^{3/5+\epsilon}+p^{1+\epsilon}\right]\\
+Y\sqrt{p}\left[\left(X^{-3/2}+X^{(-29+\sqrt{1801})/84}Y^{-13/7}\right)(XY)^{\epsilon}\right]\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{p^{12/7}}{X^{(49-2\sqrt{37})/42}Y^{6/7}}+\frac{pY}{X^{2}}\right)(pXY)^{\epsilon}.

Now recall

. Plugging into the error bound above, we find that

, with

In this section we conduct a second average over

Recall from section

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in[X,X+H]\\
x\equiv a\ (\text{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(x)=\frac{H}{\varphi(q)}(1+o(1))

L

.

5.1
Averages of

over

We first average

over intervals.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume primes are equidistributed in intervals of length
H=o(X)
. We have

\mathop{Exp}\displaylimits_{p\in[X,X+H]}c(p)=\overline{c}+o(1),

where

\overline{c}=\frac{1}{3}\prod_{\ell>2}\left(1-\ell^{-2}-\frac{\ell^{-3}}{1-\ell^{-2}}\right)

(5.2)

as given in
(1.25)

Proof.

Define

c_{M}(p):=\frac{1}{3}\prod_{2<\ell<M,\left(\frac{p}{\ell}\right)=1}\left(1-2\ell^{-2}-\frac{2\ell^{-3}}{1-\ell^{-2}}\right)

where
M
is some cutoff parameter. Let

denote the set of odd primes less than

, and let

denote their product.

The prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions implies

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in[X,X+H]}c_{M}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\ (\text{mod}\ q)}\#\{p\in[X,X+H]:p\equiv a\ (\text{mod}\ q)\}\cdot\frac{p+1}{3p}\prod_{\ell\in S,\left(\frac{a}{\ell}\right)=1}\left(1-2\ell^{-2}-\frac{2\ell^{-3}}{1-\ell^{-2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\ (\text{mod}\ q)}\frac{\li(X+H)-\li(X)}{\varphi(q)}\cdot\frac{1}{3}\prod_{\ell\in S,\left(\frac{a}{\ell}\right)=1}\left(1-2\ell^{-2}-\frac{2\ell^{-3}}{1-\ell^{-2}}\right)(1+o(1)).

Now by the Chinese remainder theorem, given any sequence
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{k})\in\{\pm 1\}^{k}
, there are exactly
\prod_{\ell\in S}\frac{\ell-1}{2}=2^{-k}\varphi(q)
primitive residue classes

modulo
q
such that
\left(\frac{a}{\ell_{i}}\right)=\epsilon_{i}
for all

. Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in[X,X+H]}c_{M}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{\li(X+H)-\li(X)}{\varphi(q)}\cdot 2^{-k}\varphi(q)\sum_{(\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{k})\in\{\pm 1\}^{k}}\frac{1}{3}\prod_{i\leq k,\epsilon_{i}=1}\left(1-2\ell_{i}^{-2}-\frac{2\ell_{i}^{-3}}{1-\ell_{i}^{-2}}\right)(1+o(1))

\displaystyle=\frac{\li(X+H)-\li(X)}{3}\prod_{\ell\in S}\left(1-\ell^{-2}-\frac{\ell^{-3}}{1-\ell^{-2}}\right)(1+o(1)).

(5.3)

For the denominator, the prime number theorem gives

\sum_{p\in[X,X+H]}1\ll(\li(X+H)-\li(X))(1+o(1)).

Thus

\mathop{Exp}\displaylimits_{p\in[X,X+H]}c_{M}(p)=\frac{1}{3}\prod_{\ell\in S}\left(1-\ell^{-2}-\frac{\ell^{-3}}{1-\ell^{-2}}\right)+o(1).

Now let
M
tend to
\infty
with

(in arbitrary fashion, in fact), so

\left\lvert c(p)-c_{M}(p)\right\rvert\ll\sum_{\ell\geq M}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}=o(1),

and similarly

\left\lvert\frac{1}{3}\prod_{\ell\in S}\left(1-\ell^{-2}-\frac{\ell^{-3}}{1-\ell^{-2}}\right)-\overline{c}\right\rvert=o(1).

Combining the error bounds establishes the Proposition.
∎

6
Asymptotics of the Murmuration Function

Throughout this section,
\Phi
denotes a compactly supported smooth weight function. Recall that the corresponding murmuration function may be obtained from the murmuration density via

M_{\Phi}(\Xi)=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}M(\Xi/u)\Phi(u)u^{\delta}\frac{du}{u}}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(u)u^{\delta}\frac{du}{u}}

as in
(1.7)
. We show that for all such weight function
\Phi
we obtain
M_{\Phi}(\Xi)\to-\frac{1}{2}
as
\Xi\to\infty
, which is consistent with
(1.5)
and the 1-level density conjecture.

Recall the murmuration density

M(\Xi)=\overline{c}\sum_{1\leq y\leq 2\sqrt{\Xi}}\overline{M}_{y}(\Xi)+M_{-}(\Xi)=\frac{11\zeta(2)\overline{c}}{4A}\sum_{1\leq y\leq 2\sqrt{\Xi}}\kappa(y)\sqrt{\frac{\Xi}{4\Xi-y^{2}}}-\frac{11\pi}{12A}\sqrt{\Xi},

(6.1)

where

\displaystyle\overline{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\prod_{\ell>2}\left(1-\ell^{-2}-\frac{\ell^{-3}}{1-\ell^{-2}}\right),

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{p}{(p+1)^{2}(p-1)}\right),

\displaystyle\kappa(y)

\displaystyle=2^{\mathbbold{1}\{2\mid y\}}\prod_{q\mid y\text{ odd}}\left(1+\frac{q^{2}}{q^{4}-2q^{2}-q+1}\right).

First we use Poisson summation to rewrite the murmuration density more cleanly.

Proposition 6.1
.

We have

\displaystyle M(\Xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{11\pi\zeta(2)\overline{c}}{16A}\sqrt{\Xi}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{Q(d)}{d}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}J_{0}\left(\frac{4\pi m\sqrt{\Xi}}{d}\right)-\frac{1}{2},

where

Q(d)=\mu^{2}(d)\prod_{q\mid d\text{ odd}}\frac{q^{2}}{q^{4}-2q^{2}-q+1}

and
J_{0}
is the Bessel function.

Proof.

Note
\kappa(y)=\sum_{d\mid y}Q(d)
. Define

f_{c}(t):=\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-c^{2}t^{2}}\,}\chi_{[-1/c,1/c]},

so

\sum_{1\leq y\leq 2\sqrt{\Xi}}\kappa(y)\sqrt{\frac{\Xi}{4\Xi-y^{2}}}=\frac{1}{2}{\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}}Q(d)\sum_{1\leq t\leq\frac{2\sqrt{\Xi}}{d}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-d^{2}t^{2}/4\Xi}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}Q(d)\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}f_{\frac{d}{2\sqrt{\Xi}}}(t).

(6.2)

The Fourier transform of
f_{c}(t)
is
\widehat{f_{c}}(s)=\frac{\pi}{c}J_{0}\left(\frac{2\pi s}{c}\right),
and Poisson summation gives

2\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}f_{\frac{d}{2\sqrt{\Xi}}}(t)+1=2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\pi\sqrt{\Xi}}{d}J_{0}\left(\frac{4\pi m\sqrt{\Xi}}{d}\right)+\frac{\pi\sqrt{\Xi}}{d}.

Solving for the sum over
t
and plugging this into
(6.2)
and
(6.1)
, we get

\displaystyle M(\Xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{11\zeta(2)\overline{c}}{4A}\left[\frac{\pi\sqrt{\Xi}}{4}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{Q(d)}{d}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}J_{0}\left(\frac{4\pi m\sqrt{\Xi}}{d}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}Q(d)+\frac{\pi\sqrt{\Xi}}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{Q(d)}{d}\right]-\frac{11\pi}{12A}\sqrt{\Xi}.

Now the Euler products for
\zeta(2),\overline{c},A
and
Q(d)
give

\frac{\zeta(2)\overline{c}}{A}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}Q(d)=\frac{8}{11},\qquad\zeta(2)\overline{c}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{Q(d)}{d}=\frac{2}{3},

which yields the Proposition.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

For any compactly supported smooth weight function
\Phi:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, we have

M_{\Phi}(\Xi):=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}M(\Xi/u)\Phi(u)u^{3/2}\frac{du}{u}}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(u)u^{3/2}\frac{du}{u}}\to-\frac{1}{2}\qquad\text{as $\Xi\to\infty$}.

Proof.

First we define
M^{\prime}(\Xi):=M(\Xi^{2})
and
\Phi^{\prime}(v):=2v^{3/2}\Phi(v^{2}).
A simple change of variables shows that
M^{\prime}_{\Phi}(\Xi^{2})=M_{\Phi^{\prime}}^{\prime}(\Xi)
, so it suffices to prove the statement for the function
M^{\prime}.

Setting
x_{m}=4\pi m\Xi
, we have

\displaystyle M^{\prime}(\Xi/u)+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{11\pi\zeta(2)\overline{c}}{16A}\frac{\Xi}{u}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{Q(d)}{d}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}J_{0}\left(\frac{4\pi m\Xi}{du}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{11\zeta(2)\overline{c}}{64A}\sum_{d,m}\frac{1}{m}\frac{Q(d)}{d}\frac{x_{m}}{u}J_{0}\left(\frac{x_{m}}{du}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{11\zeta(2)\overline{c}}{64A}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}F\left(\frac{x_{m}}{u}\right)

where

F(x):=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{Q(d)}{d}xJ_{0}\left(\frac{x}{d}\right)=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}Q(d)f\left(\frac{d}{x}\right)

and
f(x):=x^{-1}J_{0}(1/x)
is the same as that on
[
Zub23
]
, p.23. The Mellin transform of

is given by

\tilde{f}(s)=\frac{1}{2^{s}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+s}{2}\right)}

on the strip
-\frac{1}{2}<\mathrm{Re}(s)<1
. Mellin inversion yields

F(x)=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}Q(d)\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\left(1/2\right)}\tilde{f}(s)\left(\frac{d}{x}\right)^{-s}\,ds=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1/2)}L(s)\tilde{f}(s)x^{s}\,dx

where

\displaystyle L(s)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}Q(d)d^{-s}=\frac{\zeta(s+2)}{\zeta(2s+4)}\frac{1+2^{-s}}{1+\frac{4}{7}\cdot 2^{-s}}\prod_{p>2}\left(1+\frac{2p^{2}+p-1}{(p^{4}-2p^{2}-p+1)(p^{s+2}+1)}\right).

1
1

1

This differs from that of
[
Zub23
]
by a factor due to the omission of the factor at 2 in our definition of
Q(d)
.

Thus

\widetilde{\Phi}\left(\tfrac{3}{2}\right)F_{\Phi}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1/2)}L(s)\tilde{f}(s)\widetilde{\Phi}\left(\tfrac{3}{2}-s\right)x^{s}\,ds

Since

is entire and has rapid decay with respect to

. The

Therefore,

M_{\Phi}^{\prime}(\Xi/u)+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{11\zeta(2)\overline{c}}{64A}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}F_{\Phi}(x_{m})\ll\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}(m\Xi)^{-1/2}\ll\Xi^{-1/2}.

∎

U\geq 2
and near a half integer. Let
\chi
be a character modulo
q=\left\lvert D\right\rvert
and
N(\sigma,U;\chi)
be the number of zeroes of the Dirichlet
L
-function
L(s,\chi)
in the box
\sigma\leq\mathrm{Re}(s)\leq 1,\left\lvert\mathrm{Im}(s)\right\rvert\leq U
. Then the abovementioned density theorem implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathscr{D}}N(\sigma,U;\chi_{D})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{D\in\mathscr{D}}\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\text{$\chi$ mod $q$}}\left\lvert\tau(\chi)\right\rvert^{2}N(\sigma,U;\chi)

\displaystyle\ll U(X^{2}+UX)^{4(1-\sigma)/(3-2\sigma)}(UX)^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\ll UX^{8(1-\sigma)/(3-2\sigma)+\epsilon},

(A.1)

where
\tau(\chi)=\sum_{a=1}^{q}\chi(a)e(a/q)
is the Gauss sum and
\left\lvert\tau(\chi_{D})\right\rvert^{2}=q
since
\chi_{D}
is primitive (see
[
Dav80
]
, pp.66-67). Let
M=UX^{8(1-\sigma)/(3-2\sigma)+\epsilon}
denote the bound in
(A.1)
, and this is the upper bound for the number of those
D
with
N(\sigma,U;\chi_{D})>0
. For any such
D
, partial summation and Pólya-Vinogradov gives (see
[
Ayo63
]
, p.321, Theorem 5.2):

L(1,\chi_{D})-\sum_{n\leq T}\frac{\chi_{D}(n)}{n}\ll T^{-1}\left\lvert D\right\rvert^{1/2+\epsilon}.

(A.2)

For all
D
such that
N(\sigma,U;\chi_{D})=0
, Perron’s Formula (see
[
Tit39
]
, p.301) implies

\sum_{n\leq T}\frac{\chi_{D}(n)}{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\epsilon-iU}^{\epsilon+iU}L(1+s,\chi_{D})T^{s}\frac{ds}{s}+O\left(\frac{T^{\epsilon}}{U}\right).

Shifting the contour of integration to the line
\mathrm{Re}(s)=\sigma-1+\epsilon
gives

\sum_{n\leq T}\frac{\chi_{D}(n)}{n}=L(1,\chi_{D})+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma-1+\epsilon-iU}^{\sigma-1+\epsilon+iU}L(1+s,\chi_{D})T^{s}\frac{ds}{s}+O\left(\frac{T^{\epsilon}}{U}\right).

(A.3)

Indeed, since
N(\sigma,U;\chi_{D})=0
the integrand has only a simple pole at
s=0
with residue
L(1,\chi_{D}).
Now we need an estimate on
L(1+s,\chi_{D})
along
\mathrm{Re}(s)=\sigma-1+\epsilon
. Following the proof of the Lindelöf Hypothesis from GRH in
[
IK04
]
, pp.114-116, we obtain (in the following,
\mathfrak{q}:=\mathfrak{q}(\chi_{D},s)=\left\lvert D\right\rvert(\left\lvert s\right\rvert+3)
is the “analytic conductor”)

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi_{D})\ll\frac{\log\mathfrak{q}}{\sigma-\sigma}X^{\sigma-\sigma}+X^{1-\sigma}+\log X\ll\frac{(\log\mathfrak{q})^{(1-\sigma)/(1-\sigma)}}{\sigma-\sigma}

for any
s
with
\sigma<\sigma\leq\frac{5}{4}
, and we chose
X=(\log\mathfrak{q})^{1/(1-\sigma)}
. This further implies

\log L(s,\chi_{D})\ll\frac{(\log\mathfrak{q})^{(1-\sigma)/(1-\sigma)}}{(\sigma-\sigma)(\log\log\mathfrak{q})}+\log\log\mathfrak{q},

and thus

L(s,\chi_{D})\ll\mathfrak{q}^{\epsilon}\ll(\left\lvert Ds\right\rvert)^{\epsilon}

for
\sigma\geq\sigma+\epsilon
(the implied constant depend on
\epsilon
only). Putting this bound into
(A.3)
gives

\sum_{n\leq T}\frac{\chi_{D}(n)}{n}-L(1,\chi_{D})\ll(UX)^{\epsilon}T^{\sigma-1}+\frac{T^{\epsilon}}{U}.

(A.4)

Finally, using
(A.2)
for those
D\in\mathscr{D}
with
N(\sigma,U;\chi_{D})>0
and
(A.4)
for the rest, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathscr{D}}\left\lvert L(1,\chi_{D})-\sum_{n\leq T}\frac{\chi_{D}(n)}{n}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\ll MT^{-1}X^{1/2+\epsilon}+\left\lvert\mathscr{D}\right\rvert\left((UX)^{\epsilon}T^{\sigma-1}+\frac{T^{\epsilon}}{U}\right)

\displaystyle\ll T^{-1}UX^{\frac{8(1-\sigma)}{3-2\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}+\left\lvert\mathscr{D}\right\rvert\left(T^{\sigma-1}+\frac{1}{U}\right)(TX)^{\epsilon},

as desired.
∎

Corollary A.2
.

Let
\mathscr{D}
and
X
be as above. Assuming GRH (specifically the Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet
L
-functions), we have

\sum_{D\in\mathscr{D}}\left\lvert L(1,\chi_{D})-\sum_{n\leq T}\frac{\chi_{D}(n)}{n}\right\rvert\ll\left\lvert\mathscr{D}\right\rvert T^{-1/2}(TX)^{\epsilon}.

Proof.

We take
\sigma=\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon
and apply the previous Lemma. On GRH the first error term vanishes since it comes from exceptional
L
-functions with
N(\sigma,U;\chi_{D})>0
, and taking
U
a large power of
T
(we also no longer need
U\ll X
) gives the bound.
∎

A.1.3 Miscellaneous

Lemma A.5
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
a
an integer with
p\nmid a
. Then

\sum_{x\,\mathrm{mod}\,p}\left(\frac{x^{2}-a}{p}\right)=-1.

Proof.

Note that
\sum_{x}\left(\frac{x^{2}-a}{p}\right)=0
only depends on square class of
a
mod
p
by change of variables, and

\displaystyle\sum_{x}\left(\frac{x^{2}-1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\neq 1}\left(\frac{(x+1)(x-1)^{-1}}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\neq 0}\left(\frac{1+2y}{p}\right)

\displaystyle(x=y^{-1}+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\neq 1}\left(\frac{z}{p}\right)

\displaystyle(y=2^{-1}(z-1))

\displaystyle=-1.

Thus
\sum_{x}\left(\frac{x^{2}-\square}{p}\right)=-1
. Moreover,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\sum_{a}\left(\frac{x^{2}-a}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\sum_{x}\left(\frac{x^{2}-a}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}\sum_{x}\left(\frac{x^{2}-a}{p}\right)+\sum_{a=\square}\sum_{x}\left(\frac{x^{2}-a}{p}\right)+\sum_{a=\mathord{\mathchoice{\vphantom{\hbox{\raisebox{0.85417pt}{$\displaystyle/$}}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raisebox{0.85417pt}{$\displaystyle/$}}\cr$\displaystyle\square$\cr}}}{\vphantom{\hbox{\raisebox{0.85417pt}{$\textstyle/$}}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raisebox{0.85417pt}{$\textstyle/$}}\cr$\textstyle\square$\cr}}}{\vphantom{\hbox{\raisebox{0.42876pt}{$\scriptstyle/$}}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raisebox{0.42876pt}{$\scriptstyle/$}}\cr$\scriptstyle\square$\cr}}}{\vphantom{\hbox{\raisebox{0.30624pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle/$}}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raisebox{0.30624pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle/$}}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\square$\cr}}}}}\sum_{x}\left(\frac{x^{2}-a}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=(p-1)+\frac{p-1}{2}(-1)+\frac{p-1}{2}\sum_{x}\left(\frac{x^{2}-\mathord{\mathchoice{\vphantom{\hbox{\raisebox{0.875pt}{$\displaystyle/$}}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raisebox{0.875pt}{$\displaystyle/$}}\cr$\displaystyle\square$\cr}}}{\vphantom{\hbox{\raisebox{0.875pt}{$\textstyle/$}}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raisebox{0.875pt}{$\textstyle/$}}\cr$\textstyle\square$\cr}}}{\vphantom{\hbox{\raisebox{0.85417pt}{$\scriptstyle/$}}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raisebox{0.85417pt}{$\scriptstyle/$}}\cr$\scriptstyle\square$\cr}}}{\vphantom{\hbox{\raisebox{0.79872pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle/$}}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raisebox{0.79872pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle/$}}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\square$\cr}}}}}{p}\right),

so
\sum_{x}\left(\frac{x^{2}-\mathord{\mathchoice{\vphantom{\hbox{\raisebox{0.85417pt}{$\displaystyle/$}}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raisebox{0.85417pt}{$\displaystyle/$}}\cr$\displaystyle\square$\cr}}}{\vphantom{\hbox{\raisebox{0.85417pt}{$\textstyle/$}}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raisebox{0.85417pt}{$\textstyle/$}}\cr$\textstyle\square$\cr}}}{\vphantom{\hbox{\raisebox{0.42876pt}{$\scriptstyle/$}}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raisebox{0.42876pt}{$\scriptstyle/$}}\cr$\scriptstyle\square$\cr}}}{\vphantom{\hbox{\raisebox{0.30624pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle/$}}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raisebox{0.30624pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle/$}}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\square$\cr}}}}}{p}\right)=-1
as well.
∎

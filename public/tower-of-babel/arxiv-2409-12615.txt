Discrete Incremental Voting on Expanders
Colin Cooper, Tomasz Radzik, Takeharu Shiraga

Abstract
Pull voting is a random process in which vertices of a connected graph have initial opinions chosen from a set of k k distinct opinions, and at each step a random vertex alters its opinion to that of a randomly chosen neighbour. If the system reaches a state where each vertex holds the same opinion, then this opinion will persist forthwith. In general the opinions are regarded as incommensurate, whereas in this paper we consider a type of pull voting suitable for integer opinions such as { 1 , 2 , … , k } \{1,2,\ldots,k\} which can be compared on a linear scale; for example, 1 (’disagree strongly’), 2 (’disagree’), … , \ldots, 5 (’agree strongly’). On observing the opinion of a random neighbour, a vertex updates its opinion by a discrete change towards the value of the neighbour’s opinion, if different. Discrete incremental voting is a pull voting process which mimics this behaviour. At each step a random vertex alters its opinion towards that of a randomly chosen neighbour; increasing its opinion by + 1 +1 if the opinion of the chosen neighbour is larger, or decreasing its opinion by − 1 -1 , if the opinion of the neighbour is smaller. If initially there are only two adjacent integer opinions, for example { 0 , 1 } \{0,1\} , incremental voting coincides with pull voting, but if initially there are more than two opinions this is not the case. For an n n -vertex graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) , let λ \lambda be the absolute second eigenvalue of the transition matrix P P of a simple random walk on G G . Let the initial opinions of the vertices be chosen from { 1 , 2 , … , k } \{1,2,\ldots,k\} . Let c = ∑ v ∈ V π v ​ X v c=\sum_{v\in V}\pi_{v}X_{v} , where X v X_{v} is the initial opinion of vertex v v , and π v \pi_{v} is the stationary distribution of the vertex. Then provided λ ​ k = o ⁡ ( 1 ) \lambda k=o(1) and k = o ⁡ ( n / log ⁡ n ) k=o(n/\log n) , with high probability (whp) the final opinion is the initial weighted average c c suitably rounded to ⌊ c ⌋ \lfloor c\rfloor or ⌈ c ⌉ \lceil c\rceil . If G G is a regular graph then c c is the average opinion, and with high probability the final opinion held by all vertices is ⌊ c ⌋ \lfloor c\rfloor or ⌈ c ⌉ \lceil c\rceil .

In order to reach a consensus opinion in discrete incremental voting, all other opinions must be eliminated.

The main features of discrete incremental voting.

The general model of
pull voting
on connected graphs regards the opinions as incommensurate, and thus not comparable on a numeric scale.
As mentioned earlier,
the final opinion is chosen with probability proportional to
the sum of the degrees of the vertices initially holding this opinion.
Thus, for regular graphs, the
most likely value is
the mode
of the initial opinions.
In contrast to this,

[
15

considers opinions drawn from an ordered set,

At each step a random vertex selects two neighbours and replaces its opinion by the median of all three values (including its own current value).
On the complete graph, if

(that is, the process converges to
the
l

initial values), then w.h.p.
l\in[n/2-O(\sqrt{n\log n}),n/2+O(\sqrt{n\log n})]

Two-opinion pull voting: The last stage in incremental voting.

In randomized pull voting, a vertex updates its value at a given step by choosing (pulling) the value of a
neighbour chosen uniformly at random.
Two-opinion pull voting is a special case of pull voting where initially there are only two opinions, usually written
\{0,1\}

\displaystyle T_{s}=T_{p}\sqrt{\min\{\pi(A_{s}(0)),\pi(A_{s}(0)^{C})\}}=T_{p}\sqrt{\pi(A_{s}(0))}=T_{p}\sqrt{\min\{\pi(A_{s}(0)),\pi(A_{\ell}(0))\}}.

Then, applying
lemma

13
with
B(0)=A_{s}(0)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\tau^{\mathrm{DIV}}_{\mathrm{extr}}(0)>T_{p}\sqrt{\min\{\pi(A_{s}(0)),\pi(A_{\ell}(0))\}}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[\tau^{\mathrm{DIV}}_{\mathrm{extr}}(0)>T_{s}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[A_{s}(T_{s})\neq\emptyset\textrm{ and }A_{\ell}(T_{s})\neq\emptyset]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[B(T_{s})\neq\emptyset\textrm{ and }V\setminus B(T_{s})\neq\emptyset]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[\tau^{\mathrm{PULL}}_{\mathrm{cons}}>T_{s}]

\displaystyle\leq 1/2.

Second, suppose
\pi(A_{s}(0))>\pi(A_{\ell}(0))
.
In this case,
\pi(A_{\ell}(0))<\pi(A_{s}(0))\leq 1-\pi(A_{\ell}(0))
.
Let

\displaystyle T_{\ell}=T_{p}\sqrt{\min\{\pi(A_{\ell}(0)),\pi(A_{\ell}(0)^{C})\}}=T_{p}\sqrt{\pi(A_{\ell}(0))}=T_{p}\sqrt{\min\{\pi(A_{s}(0)),\pi(A_{\ell}(0))\}}.

Then, applying
lemma

13
with
B(0)=A_{\ell}(0)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\tau^{\mathrm{DIV}}_{\mathrm{extr}}(0)>T_{p}\sqrt{\min\{\pi(A_{s}(0)),\pi(A_{\ell}(0))\}}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[\tau^{\mathrm{DIV}}_{\mathrm{extr}}(0)>T_{\ell}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[A_{\ell}(T_{\ell})\neq\emptyset\textrm{ and }A_{s}(T_{\ell})\neq\emptyset]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[B(T_{\ell})\neq\emptyset\textrm{ and }V\setminus B(T_{\ell})\neq\emptyset]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[\tau^{\mathrm{PULL}}_{\mathrm{cons}}>T_{\ell}]

\displaystyle\leq 1/2

holds. Thus, we obtain the claim.
∎

3.3
Proof of the main theorem, Theorem

In this section we prove
theorem

1
by combining

and
12
in Lemma
14
.
The parameters

\displaystyle\!\!T_{1}(\epsilon)=\left\lceil 2n\log\left(\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}\right)\right\rceil,T_{2}(\epsilon)=\left\lceil\frac{2n}{\epsilon}\log\left(\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}\right)\right\rceil,T_{p}=\left\lceil\frac{64n}{\sqrt{2}(1-\lambda)\pi_{\min}}\right\rceil.

(18)

Proof of
theorem

1
.

Write
\tau
for the first time
t\geq 0
when there are at most two consecutive opinions
i
and
i+1
remaining.
Henceforth, we abbreviate
\max_{x}{\bf E}[\tau|X(0)=x]
(the expected value of
\tau
from the worst initial configuration) as
{\bf E}[\tau]
for convenience.
From
lemma

14
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}[\tau]\leq 4(k-3)(T_{1}(\epsilon_{1})+T_{p}(\epsilon_{1}))+4(T_{2}(\epsilon_{2})+T_{p}(\epsilon_{2})),

(19)

which by Lemma
10
holds for any
\epsilon_{1}\geq 4\lambda^{2}
and
\epsilon_{2}\geq 2\lambda
.

Now, set

\epsilon_{1}=\max\{4\lambda^{2},n^{-2}\}\geq 4\lambda^{2}
and

\epsilon_{2}=\max\{2\lambda,n^{-2/3}\}\geq 2\lambda
.
Assumptions of
\pi_{\min}=\Theta(1/n)
and
\lambda=o(1)
imply that
\frac{n}{(1-\lambda)\pi_{\min}}=O(n^{2})
.
Hence, from
eq.

18
there is a sufficiently large constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle kT_{1}(\epsilon_{1})\leq Cnk\log n,

\displaystyle kT_{p}(\epsilon_{1})\leq Ckn^{2}\max\{\lambda,1/n\}\leq C(n^{2}\lambda k+nk),

\displaystyle T_{2}(\epsilon_{2})\leq Cn^{5/3}\log n,

\displaystyle T_{p}(\epsilon_{2})\leq Cn^{2}\max\{\sqrt{\lambda},1/n^{1/3}\}\leq C(n^{2}\sqrt{\lambda}+n^{5/3})

hold. Hence, noting the assumptions that
k=o(n/\log n)
and
\lambda k=o(1)
, it follows that all of
kT_{1}(\epsilon_{1})
,
kT_{p}(\epsilon_{1})
,
T_{2}(\epsilon_{2})
, and
T_{p}(\epsilon_{2})
are
o(n^{2})
.
Putting this together gives the following upper bound on
Equation

19
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}[\tau]=O(nk\log n+n^{2}\lambda k+n^{5/3}\log n+n^{2}\sqrt{\lambda}).

(20)

Provided
k=o(n\log n)
and
\lambda k=o(1)
, from (
19
)
we obtain
{\bf E}[\tau]=o(n^{2})
, i.e., there is some function
\epsilon(n)=o(1)
such that

{\bf E}[\tau]\leq n^{2}\epsilon(n)
holds.

The Markov inequality,
\mathbb{P}[\tau>\mathrm{e}{\bf E}[\tau]]\leq 1/\mathrm{e}
holds for any initial configuration.
By repeating this process independently
h
times, we obtain the following consequence:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\tau>h\mathrm{e}{\bf E}[\tau]]\leq\mathrm{e}^{-h}.

(21)

Thus, taking

\mathbb{P}[\tau\leq h\mathrm{e}{\bf E}[\tau]]\geq 1-\mathrm{e}^{-\omega(1)}

.

In other words, within
o(n^{2})
steps,
there are at most two consecutive opinions remaining w.h.p.
This completes the proof of
theorem

1
.
∎

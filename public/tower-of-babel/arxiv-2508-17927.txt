TWISTED CONJUGACY CLASSES IN LIE GROUPS
Ravi Prakash, Riddhi Shah

Abstract
. We consider twisted conjugacy classes of continuous automorphisms φ \varphi of a Lie group G G . We obtain a necessary and sufficient condition on φ \varphi for its Reidemeister number, the number of twisted conjugacy classes, to be infinite when G G is connected and solvable or compactly generated and nilpotent. We also show for a general connected Lie group G G that the number of conjugacy classes is infinite. We prove that for a connected non-nilpotent Lie group G G , there exists n ∈ ℕ n\in{\mathbb{N}} such that Reidemeister number of φ n \varphi^{n} is infinite for every φ \varphi . We say that G G has topological R ∞ R_{\infty} -property if the Reidemeister number of every φ \varphi is infinite. We obtain conditions on a connected solvable Lie group under which it has topological R ∞ R_{\infty} -property; which, in particular, enables us to prove that the group of invertible n × n n\times n upper triangular real matrices has this property for every n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . We show that SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}) and GL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}) have topological R ∞ R_{\infty} -property, and construct many examples of connected Lie groups with this property.

The

(2)

(3)

By

{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi(X)=X
, then

\varphi(\exp tX)=\exp tX
. Thus
(2)\implies(3)
. Now take
\rho:G\to G
be such that
\rho(g)=g\varphi(g^{-1})
.
Observe that
\Ima\rho
is a compact connected subgroup of
G
.
As
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi
,
V:=\Ima(I-{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi)
is a proper subspace of
{\mathcal{G}}
, where
I
is the identity matrix in
\mathrm{GL}({\mathcal{G}})
. As
G
is abelian,
{\mathcal{G}}
is an abelian Lie algebra and

V
is a proper Lie subalgebra. Then we have that

\exp(V)=\exp(X-{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi(X))=(\exp X)\varphi(\exp X)^{-1}=\Ima\rho.

Thus
\Ima\rho
is a proper compact connected subgroup of
G
. Note that
\Ima\rho=[e]_{\varphi}
as the exponential map
is surjective. As
G
is abelian, for the set
\mathcal{R}(\varphi)
of Reidemeister classes of
\varphi
, we have that

\mathcal{R}(\varphi)=\{[x]_{\varphi}\mid x\in G\}=\{x[e]_{\varphi}\mid x\in G\}=G/\Ima\rho
.
Now we have that
R(\varphi)=|\mathcal{R}(\varphi)|=|G/\Ima\rho|=\infty
. Thus (1) holds and we have that
(2)\implies(1)
.
Also, since
(3)\implies(2)
, we get that
R(\varphi)=\infty
in case
R(\varphi)\neq 1
.

Now suppose (1) holds, i.e.
R(\varphi)=\infty
. Then
R(\varphi)\neq 1
, hence by
2.4
(see
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
),
(3) holds, and hence
(1-3)
are equivalent if
G
is compact.

Step 3:
Now suppose
G
is a connected nilpotent Lie group. Let
K
be the maximal compact subgroup of
G
.
Then
K
is connected, central and characteristic in
G
, and
G/K
is simply connected and nilpotent.
Let
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi|_{K}
and let
\bar{\varphi}
be the automorphism of
G/K
induced by

\varphi
. Now if
R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
, then
R(\varphi)=\infty
. If
R(\bar{\varphi})<\infty
, we get from above that

R(\bar{\varphi})=1
and also
|\mathrm{Fix}(\bar{\varphi})|=1
. Now if
R(\varphi^{\prime})=\infty
, then by
2.3
(1),
R(\varphi)=\infty
.
Now suppose
R(\varphi^{\prime})<\infty
. Then from Step 2,
R(\varphi^{\prime})=1
and by
2.3
(3), we get
R(\varphi)=1
.

Now suppose (1) holds. If possible, suppose (2) does not hold, i.e.
1
is not an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi
. Then
1 is not an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi^{\prime}
, and by Step 2,
R(\varphi^{\prime})=1
. Also,
1
is not an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\bar{\varphi}
which is the
quotient Lie algebra automorphism of
{\mathcal{G}}
, which is the Lie algebra of
G/K
. As
G/K
is simply connected and
nilpotent, by Step 1,
R(\bar{\varphi})=1
. Now by
2.3
(3),
R(\varphi)=1
, which leads to a contradiction. Thus (2) holds
and we have that
(1)\implies(2)
. Now if (2) holds, then there exists a nonzero
X\in{\mathcal{G}}
such that
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi(X)=X
, and we have that

\varphi(\exp tX)=\exp tX
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, hence (3) holds. Thus
(2)\implies(3)
.

Now suppose (3) holds. Note that if
R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
, then
R(\varphi)=\infty
. Now suppose

R(\bar{\varphi})<\infty
. Then
R(\bar{\varphi})=1
and from Step 1,
\mathrm{Fix}(\bar{\varphi})=1
. Thus
\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)\subset K
, and hence

|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi^{\prime})|=\infty
. By Step 2 above,
R(\varphi^{\prime})=\infty
. Hence by
2.3
(1),
R(\varphi)=\infty
, i.e. (1) holds. Thus

(1-3)
are equivalent when
G
is nilpotent.

Step 4:
Suppose
G
is any connected solvable Lie group. Then
G_{1}:=\overline{[G,G]}
is a closed normal
characteristic Lie subgroup of
G
, and it is nilpotent (see e.g.
[
23
]
). Now
G/G_{1}
is a connected abelian
Lie group.

Let
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi|_{G_{1}}
and let
\bar{\varphi}
be the automorphism of
G/G_{1}
induced by
\varphi
. If
R(\varphi^{\prime})=1
and

R(\bar{\varphi})=1
. Then
R(\varphi)=1
by
2.3
(3). Now suppose
R(\varphi)\neq 1
. Then either
R(\varphi^{\prime})\neq 1
or

R(\bar{\varphi})\neq 1
(cf.
2.3
(3)). As
G_{1}
is nilpotent and
G/G_{1}
is abelian, we get from Step 3 that
R(\varphi^{\prime})=\infty
or

R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
. Suppose
R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
. Then by
2.2
,
R(\varphi)=\infty
. Now suppose
R(\bar{\varphi})<\infty
. Then
we have that
R(\varphi^{\prime})=\infty
. Moreover, from Step 3, we have that
R(\bar{\varphi})=1
and
|\mathrm{Fix}(\bar{\varphi})|<\infty
. Now by

2.3
(1),
R(\varphi)=\infty
.

Now suppose (1) holds, i.e.
R(\varphi)=\infty
. If
R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
, then by Step 3,
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\bar{\varphi}
, and
hence
1
is also an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi
, and (2) holds. If
R(\bar{\varphi})<\infty
, then
R(\bar{\varphi})=1
. Arguing as above, we
have that
R(\varphi^{\prime})=\infty
. Hence we get from Step 3 that
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi^{\prime}
, and hence, that of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi
.
Thus (2) holds and we have that
(1)\implies(2)
. As seen in Step 3,
(2)\implies(3)
is obvious. Now suppose (3) holds,
i.e.
|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)|=\infty
. Arguing as in Step 3, for
G
and
G_{1}
instead of
G
and
K
there, and using result for the nilpotent
case, we can show that
R(\varphi)=\infty
, and thus
(3)\implies(1)
, and hence
(1-3)
are equivalent.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

We may note here that for an automorphism
\varphi
on a connected solvable Lie group
G
,
R(\varphi)=1
if and only if

|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)|<\infty
, and the latter statement is equivalent to the statement that
1
is not an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi
.

The following corollary is easy to deduce from
3.1
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
G
be a connected solvable Lie group. Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
and let
H
be a closed connected

\varphi
-invariant normal subgroup of
G
. Let
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi|_{H}
and let
\bar{\varphi}\in{\rm Aut}(G/H)
be the automorphism induced
\varphi
.
Then the following hold:

(1)

R(\varphi)=R(\varphi^{\prime})R(\bar{\varphi})
.

(2)

R(\varphi)=1
if and only if
R(\varphi^{\prime})=R(\bar{\varphi})=1
.

(3)

R(\varphi)=\infty
if and only if either
R(\varphi^{\prime})=\infty
or
R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
.

Recall that a topological group
G
is said to be compactly generated if there exists a compact subset of
G
which generates the group
G
.
Any connected locally compact group is compactly generated, in particular, any connected Lie group is compactly generated.
A (not necessarily connected) Lie group
G
is compactly generated if
G/G^{0}
is finitely generated. The following is known for
finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent groups (cf.
[
33
, Theorem 2]
), and for connected nilpotent Lie groups, it follows from
3.1
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G
be a compactly generated nilpotent Lie group and let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. Then the following holds:

R(\varphi)=\infty\mbox{ if and only if }|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)|=\infty
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from
3.1
if
G
is connected. Now we may assume that
G
is not connected.

Step 1:
First suppose
G
is a finitely generated (discrete) nilpotent group, It is known that
G
has a unique maximal
finite subgroup (say)
H
(see
[
19
, Theorem 2]
or
[
5
, Lemma 3.1]
). Hence
H
is characteristic in
G
and
G/H
is torsion-free.
By
2.3
(4),
R(\varphi)=\infty
if and only if
R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
. The latter statement is equivalent to the statement that

|\mathrm{Fix}(\bar{\varphi})|=\infty

[
33
, Theorem 2]
, and hence it is equivalent to the statement that
|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)|=\infty
, as
H
is finite.

Step 2:
Now suppose
G
is not discrete. Let
G^{0}
be the connected component of the identity
e
in
G
.
Then
G^{0}
is a (connected) nilpotent Lie group and it is characteristic in
G
. In particular it is
\varphi
-invariant. Let
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi|_{G^{0}}

and let
\bar{\varphi}
be the automorphism of
G/G^{0}
induced by
\varphi
.

First suppose
|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)|=\infty
. If
R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
, then
R(\varphi)=\infty
.
Now suppose
R(\bar{\varphi})<\infty
. Since
G/G^{0}
is a (discrete) finitely generated nilpotent group, we get from Step 1,
|\mathrm{Fix}(\bar{\varphi})|<\infty
.
Then
|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi^{\prime})|=\infty
. As
G^{0}
is nilpotent, by
3.1
, we get that
R(\varphi^{\prime})=\infty
. Now by
2.3
(1),
we have that
R(\varphi)=\infty
.

Step 3:
Now suppose
|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)|<\infty
. We will show that
R(\varphi)<\infty
, which would prove statement (1). Then

|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi^{\prime})|<\infty
, and by
3.1
,R(\varphi^{\prime})=1
. Now by
2.3
(2),
|\mathrm{Fix}(\bar{\varphi})|<\infty
, Then from Step 1,
R(\bar{\varphi})<\infty
.
If
G^{0}
is central in
G
, then by
2.3
(5),
R(\varphi)<\infty
, and the proof is complete in this case.

Let
G_{0}=G^{0}
and
G_{m}=\overline{[G,G_{m-1}]}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Since
G
is nilpotent,

G_{n}=\{e\}
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Also, each
G_{m}
is characteristic in
G
,
G_{m}\subset G_{m-1}\subset G^{0}
, and

G_{m-1}/G_{m}
is central in
G/G_{m}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
\varphi^{\prime}_{m}:=\varphi|_{G_{m}}
and
\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}_{m}\in{\rm Aut}(G_{m}/G_{m+1})
be
the automorphism induced by
\varphi^{\prime}_{m}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
. Let
\bar{\varphi}_{m}\in{\rm Aut}(G/G_{m})
be the automorphism of
G/G_{m}

induced by
\varphi
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By
3.3
, we get that
R(\varphi^{\prime}_{m})=1
and
R(\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}_{m})=1
for each
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
.
As
R(\bar{\varphi})<\infty
, where
\bar{\varphi}\in{\rm Aut}(G/G^{0})
and
G^{0}/G_{1}
is central in
G/G_{1}
, by
2.3
(5),

R(\bar{\varphi}_{1})<\infty
. Then as
G_{m-1}/G_{m}
is central in
G/G_{m}
, again applying
2.3
(5) successively
to
G/G_{m}
,
m\geq 2
, we get that
R(\bar{\varphi}_{m})<\infty
,
m\geq 2
. As
G_{n}=\{e\}
, we get that
\bar{\varphi}_{n}=\varphi
,
and hence
R(\varphi)<\infty
.
∎

The following is an easy consequence of
3.4
as any closed subgroup of a compactly generated nilpotent group
is also compactly generated.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
G
be a compactly generated nilpotent Lie group. Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
and let
H
be a closed normal

\varphi
-invariant subgroup of
G
. Let
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi|_{H}
and let
\bar{\varphi}\in{\rm Aut}(G/H)
be the automorphism induced

\varphi
. Then
R(\varphi)<\infty
if and only if
R(\varphi^{\prime})<\infty
and
R(\bar{\varphi})<\infty
.

Now we generalise a part of
3.4
and a part of
3.1
to compactly generated solvable Lie groups.
The following is known for polycyclic groups (see e.g.
[
33
, Theorem 2]
).

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
G
be a compactly generated solvable Lie group and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
.
Let
\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)
denote the set of fixed points of
\varphi
, then the following hold: If
|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)|=\infty
, then
R(\varphi)=\infty
.

Proof.

Let
G
and
\varphi
be as in the hypothesis and suppose
|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)|=\infty
. We prove that
R(\varphi)=\infty

by induction on the length
n(G)
of the derived series in
G
, i.e. for
G_{1}:=\overline{[G,G]}
and
G_{m+1}:=\overline{[G_{m},G_{m}]}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
there exists
n=n(G)
such that
G_{n}=\{e\}
, as
G
is solvable. First suppose
G
is abelian, i.e.
n(G)=1
. Then
R(\varphi)=\infty

by
3.4
. Now suppose the assertion holds for all
G
for which
n(G)\leq k
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Now suppose
G
is such that

n(G)=k+1
. Then
G_{1}
is a compactly generated solvable Lie group and it is a characteristic subgroup of
G
. Moreover,
n(G_{1})=k

and
G/G_{1}
is a compactly generated abelian Lie group. Let
\varphi^{\prime}:=\varphi|_{G_{1}}
and
\bar{\varphi}
be the automorphism
of
G/G_{1}
induced by
\varphi
. If
R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
, then
R(\varphi)=\infty
. Now suppose
R(\bar{\varphi})<\infty
. Then by
3.4
,
we have that
|\mathrm{Fix}(\bar{\varphi})|<\infty
and hence
\mathrm{Fix}(\bar{\varphi})
is a finite subgroup of
G/G_{1}
. Since
|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)|=\infty
, we get that

|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi^{\prime})|=\infty
. As
n(G_{1})=k
, by the induction hypothesis, we have that
R(\varphi^{\prime})=\infty
. Now by
2.3
(1), we get that
R(\varphi)=\infty
.
∎

The following example shows that the converse of
3.6
does not hold.

Example 3.7
.

Let
G={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
, where the action of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}=\{0,1\}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
is defined uniquely by

(1,0)(0,r)(1,0)=(0,-r)
for
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Here,
G
is a compactly generated solvable Lie group. Consider
i_{x}
, the inner
automorphism by
x=(1,0)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. It is easy to see that
\mathrm{Fix}(i_{x})=\{(0,0),(1,0)\}={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
and
|\mathrm{Fix}(i_{x})|=2
. But
R(i_{x})=\infty

by
2.1
. One can also take a torsion-free compactly generated solvable Lie group
H={\mathbb{Z}}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
, where the
action of
{\mathbb{Z}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
factors through the action of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
. It is easy to see that for
\phi=-\mathrm{Id}
on
H
,
\mathrm{Fix}(\phi)=\{(0,0)\}

while
\mathrm{Fix}(i_{y}\circ\phi)={\mathbb{R}}
for
y=(1,0)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
R(\phi)=R(i_{y}\circ\phi)=\infty
by
3.6
.

Note that
G
does not have (topological)
R_{\infty}
-property as for
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
defined as
\psi(z,r)=(z,\alpha\,r)
,

z\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
,
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
where
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
such that
\alpha\neq\pm\,1
,
R(\psi)=2
. Moreover,
H
as above also does not have
this property as for
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(H)
,
\varphi(z,r)=(-z,\alpha r)
,
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where
\alpha
is as above,
R(\varphi)=2
.

There are two different kinds of Lie groups of the form
{\mathbb{Z}}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
, one of them, where the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action on
{\mathbb{R}}
factors through
the action of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
is discussed in
Example

3.7
above. Now we discuss the other kind.

Example 3.8
.

Consider a Lie group
L:={\mathbb{Z}}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
, where the conjugation action of
{\mathbb{Z}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
is given by

(z,0)(0,r)(-z,0)=(0,\alpha^{z}r)
,
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, for some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\setminus\{\pm\,1\}
. (All these groups are isomorphic for different
\alpha
,
so we can fix
\alpha
.) Note that
L^{0}={\mathbb{R}}
and it is characteristic in
L
. Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(L)
. Let
\varphi^{\prime}:=\varphi|_{\mathbb{R}}
and let
\bar{\varphi}

be the automorphism induced by
\varphi
on
L/L^{0}
, which is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then
\varphi^{\prime}=\beta\,\mathrm{Id}
for some
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
, and

\bar{\varphi}=\pm\,\mathrm{Id}
. Now we show that
\bar{\varphi}=\mathrm{Id}
. If possible, suppose
\varphi(z)=-zx_{z}
for some
x_{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}
which depends
on
z
. As
{\mathbb{R}}
is abelian and
\varphi
is a homomorphism, for
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\varphi(zrz^{-1})=\alpha^{z}\beta r=\alpha^{-z}\beta r
.
Hence,
\alpha^{2z}=1
which leads to a contradiction as
\alpha\neq\pm\,1
and
z
can be chosen to be nonzero. Thus
\bar{\varphi}=\mathrm{Id}

and
R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
. Now by
2.2
,
R(\varphi)=\infty
. Since this holds for all
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(L)
, we get that
L
has
topological
R_{\infty}
-property.

4.
Twisted conjugacy classes in connected Lie groups

In this section we prove that any nontrivial connected nilpotent group
G
has infinitely many conjugacy classes and that
{\rm Aut}(G)
admits
a subgroup of finite index in which every automorphism has infinite Reidemeister number.

For a connected Lie group
G
, if
\varphi=i_{g}
is the inner automorphism by
g\in G
, then
R(\varphi)=R(\mathrm{Id})
is the
same as the number of conjugacy classes in
G
. If
G
is solvable, then
G/\overline{[G,G]}
is nontrivial, abelian, connected and infinite,
it follows that
R(i_{g})=R(\mathrm{Id})=\infty
. We will now show that this also holds for any connected Lie group
G
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be a nontrivial connected Lie group. Then the number of conjugacy classes in
G
is infinite. Further, if
G
is not nilpotent,
then there exists a subgroup
L
of finite index in
{\rm Aut}(G)
, such that
R(\alpha)=\infty
for every
\alpha\in L
. In particular, there exists

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
R(\varphi^{n})=\infty
for every
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
.

We first show the following useful result which generalises Lemma 3.2 of
[
3
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group which is not nilpotent. Then
{\rm Aut}(G)
has a normal subgroup
L
of finite
index such that
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\alpha
for every
\alpha\in L
. In particular, for
n=|{\rm Aut}(G)/L|
,
1
is an eigenvalue of

{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi^{n}
for every
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
K
be the maximal compact connected central subgroup of
G
. Then
K\subset N
and
K
is characteristic in
G
and

G/K
has no nontrivial compact connected central subgroups. Hence
{\rm Aut}(G/K)
is almost algebraic, i.e. it is a closed subgroup of finite
index in an algebraic group (see
[
4
]
, see also
[
6
]
). In particular,
{\rm Aut}(G/K)
has finitely many connected components. Let

{\rm Aut}(G/K)^{0}
denote the connected component of the identity in
{\rm Aut}(G/K)
. As the natural map
\varphi\mapsto\bar{\varphi}
from

{\rm Aut}(G)
to
{\rm Aut}(G/K)
is a homomorphism, the subgroup
L:=\{\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G)\mid\bar{\psi}\in{\rm Aut}(G/K)^{0}\}
is a anormal subgroup of
finite index in
{\rm Aut}(G)
. Let
n=|{\rm Aut}(G)/L|
. Then
\varphi^{n}\in L
for every
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
.

If
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\alpha
for every
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(G/K)^{0}
, then
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\alpha
for every
\alpha\in L
.
Moreover,
G/K
is not nilpotent as
G
is not nilpotent. Hence we may replace
G
by
G/K
and assume that
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
is a
subgroup of finite index in
{\rm Aut}(G)
. Now we show that
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\alpha
for every
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
.

Let
G=SR
be a Levi decomposition, where either
G
is solvable or
S
is nontrivial. Suppose
G
is solvable. Let
N
be the radical
of
G
. We show that
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
acts trivially on
G/N
. Note that
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
is a connected Lie group and
M:={\rm Aut}(G)^{0}\ltimes G
is
also a connected Lie group. Let
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}=S^{\prime}R^{\prime}
be a Levi decomposition. Then
R^{\prime}G
is the radical subgroup of
M
. As
[R^{\prime}G,G]
is
nilpotent and normal in
M
and it is contained in
G
, we get that
[R^{\prime}G,G]\subset N
and hence
R^{\prime}
acts trivially on
G/N
. Note that

[S^{\prime},G]
is connected and it is contained in
G
; moreover, it is contained in the nilradical (say)
N^{\prime}
of
M
, and hence it is nilpotent.
Thus
[S^{\prime},G]\subset(N^{\prime}\cap G)^{0}\subset N
. Hence
S^{\prime}
also acts trivially on
G/N
. Hence,
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
acts trivially on
G/N
. Thus
every element
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
acts trivially on
G/N
.

Now suppose
G
is not solvable. Then
G/R
is semisimple, and
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
acts on
G/R
by
inner automorphisms. Hence we may replace
G
by
G/R
and assume that
G
is semisimple and
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}

consists of inner automorphisms. Let
\alpha\in{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
. Then
\alpha=i_{x}
for some
x
in
G
. For the center
Z
of
G
, we may replace

G
by
G/Z
and assume that
G
is a connected semisimple linear group with trivial center. Now
G
is almost algebraic. If
x=e
,
then
\alpha=\mathrm{Id}
, and the assertion follows trivially. Now suppose
x\neq e
. If
x
has finite order, i.e.
x^{n}=e
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}
,
then
x
belongs to a maximal torus (maximal compact connected abelian subgroup)
T
of
G
, and
\alpha
acts trivially on
T
, and hence

1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\alpha
. Now suppose
x
has infinite order. As
G
is almost algebraic, there exists an almost algebraic subgroup

A
of
G
containing
x
. Then
A
is abelian and has finitely many connected components. Moreover,
A^{0}
is nontrivial as
x
does not have
finite order. As
x
centralises
A^{0}
,
\alpha
acts trivially on
A^{0}
. Hence
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\alpha
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The proof of
4.2
shows that for any connected Lie group
G
,
{\rm Aut}(G)
has a subgroup of finite index
which acts trivially on
R/N
and it acts by inner automorphisms on
G/R
, where
R
and
N
are respectively the
radical and the nilradical of
G
. Note also that any subgroup of finite index in
{\rm Aut}(G)
contains
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
.

Proof of
4.1
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. We first show that
R(\mathrm{Id})=\infty
. If
G
is solvable, the
assertion follows from
3.1
; it is also easy to show this directly as
G/\overline{[G,G]}
is infinite and abelian, and all its
conjugacy classes are singleton sets. Now suppose
G
is not solvable. Then
G/R
is semisimple, where
R
is the radical of
G
, and
by
2.2
, we may assume that
G
is semisimple.

Suppose
G
is compact. Then
G
has a maximal compact connected abelian subgroup (maximal torus)
T
which is nontrivial. Let

F_{n}
be the set of all
n
th roots of unity in
T
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then
F_{n}\subset F_{mn}
,
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and for every
x\in F_{n}
,

\{gxg^{-1}\mid g\in G\}
consists of
n
th roots of unity. Then for primes
p,q
,
p\neq q
,
x\in F_{p}
and
y\in F_{q}
,

[x]_{\mathrm{Id}}\cap[y]_{\mathrm{Id}}=\{e\}
. Since there are infinitely many primes, we have that
G
has infinitely many conjugacy classes. Note that for
any connected Lie group
G
, if it has a nontrivial maximal torus, then it has infinitely many conjugacy classes. However, there are connected
semisimple Lie groups without any nontrivial compact connected abelian subgroups; for example
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})}
, the universal cover
of
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.

Now suppose
G
is not compact. Note that
\mathrm{Ad\,}x={\mathrm{d}\,}(i_{x})
,
x\in G
, and
\mathrm{Ad\,}
is a homomorphism from
G\to\mathrm{GL}({\mathcal{G}})
, and
\mathrm{Ad\,}(G)

is semisimple, as
G
is so. Choose
x\in G
, such that at least one eigenvalue
\mathrm{Ad\,}x
has absolute value other than
1
. (One can choose
x

such that
\mathrm{Ad\,}x
is a nontrivial elements of
A
, where
A
is such that
\mathrm{Ad\,}G=KAU
is an Iwasawa decomposition and
A
consists of
semisimple elements). Let
\lambda
be an eigenvalue of
\mathrm{Ad\,}x
whose absolute value is either less than
1
or greater than
1
. Now

\lambda^{n}
is an eigenvalue of
\mathrm{Ad\,}x^{n}
, and
|\lambda^{n}|\neq 1
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Note that
|\lambda^{n}|=|\lambda|^{n}
converges to zero or infinity.
Since the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{Ad\,}(gxg^{-1})=\mathrm{Ad\,}g\,\mathrm{Ad\,}x\,\mathrm{Ad\,}g^{-1}
are same as that of
\mathrm{Ad\,}x
,
[x^{m}]_{\mathrm{Id}}\neq[x^{n}]_{\mathrm{Id}}
, for all
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}

such that
m\neq n
. Thus
G
has infinitely many conjugacy classes.

To prove the second assertion, suppose that
G
is not nilpotent. By
4.2
, there exists a normal subgroup (say)
L
of finite index
in
{\rm Aut}(G)
such that
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\alpha
for every
\alpha\in L
. Moreover,
L
acts on
G/R
by inner automorphisms and
it acts trivially on
R/N
(see Remark
4.3
). If
G=R
, then by
3.1
,
R(\alpha)=\infty
for every
\alpha\in L
. Now suppose
G

is not solvable. Then
G/R
is semisimple. Then
L
acts on
G/R
by inner automorphisms and hence
R(\bar{\alpha})=\infty
for every

\alpha\in L
, where
\bar{\alpha}
is the automorphism of
G/R
induced by
\alpha
. Thus
R(\alpha)=\infty
for every
\alpha\in L
,
and the second assertion holds. The last assertion follows easily from the second assertion for
n=|{\rm Aut}(G)/L|
.
∎

5.
Lie groups with topological
R_{\infty}
-property

In this section, we first discuss sufficient conditions for connected solvable Lie groups to have topological
R_{\infty}
-property in
5.1
,
prove
5.3
and also prove that
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
have this property (see
5.8
). We give examples of many solvable and
non-solvable Lie groups with this property.

We already know that for any automorphism
\varphi
of a connected solvable Lie group
G
, the corresponding automorphism
\bar{\varphi}

on
G/N
has finite order, and hence all the eigenvalues of
\bar{\varphi}
have absolute
1
(see Remark 3.3 in
[
3
]
, see also
Remark

4.3
).
The following theorem shows that under a condition on
\mathrm{Ad\,}G
,
-1
cannot be an eigenvalue of
\bar{\varphi}
. The theorem will enable us to
prove topological
R_{\infty}
-property for many solvable Lie groups.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G
be a connected solvable Lie group such that all the eigenvalues of every element of
\mathrm{Ad\,}G
are real.
Let
N
be the nilradical of
G
and let
\bar{\varphi}
denote the automorphism of
G/N
induced by
\varphi
.
Then for any
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
,
-1
is not an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\bar{\varphi}
. In particular, if
G
as above is such that
the dimension of
G/N
is odd, then
G
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. If
G
is nilpotent, then
G=N
and the first assertion holds trivially. Now suppose
G
is not nilpotent.
Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
and
\bar{\varphi}\in{\rm Aut}(G/N)
be the automorphism induced by
\varphi
. If possible, suppose
-1
as an eigenvalue
of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\bar{\varphi}
.

We first show that
G/N
is simply connected. Let
M
be the maximal compact (connected, abelian) subgroup of
G
. Since
M
is
compact and connected, the eigenvalues of every element of
\mathrm{Ad\,}M
, being real, are all equal to
1
. Thus
\mathrm{Ad\,}M
is
unipotent. Since
M
is compact, so is
\mathrm{Ad\,}M
, and hence
\mathrm{Ad\,}M
is a trivial group. Thus
M
is central in
G
and it is contained in
N
.
Therefore,
G/N
has no nontrivial compact subgroups and it is simply connected, and hence a vector group of dimension (say)
m
. Now we
get that
\bar{\varphi}\in\mathrm{GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
and our assumption above implies that
-1
is an eigenvalue of
\bar{\varphi}
.

There is a one-dimensional closed vector subgroup
P
in
G/N
such that
\bar{\varphi}(P)=P
and
-1
is an eigenvalue
of
\bar{\varphi}|_{P}
, i.e.
\bar{\varphi}=-\mathrm{Id}
. Let
L
be the closed connected subgroup of
G
such that
L/N=P
. Then
L
is
\varphi
-invariant.
As
N
is the nilradical of
G
,
L
is not nilpotent. We may replace
G
by
L
and assume that
G/N
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
C
be
a Cartan subgroup of
G
. Since
G
is solvable,
C
is a maximal nilpotent group in
G
such that
G=CN

[
21
, Corollary 5.2]
;
this can also be deduced from Proposition 3.1 in
[
20
]
and Lemma 9 in
[
32
]
.

As
G
is solvable, all Cartan subgroups of
G
are connected (cf.
[
34
]
, Theorem 1.9) and they are conjugate to each other
(cf.
[
32
]
, Proposition 6). Now
\varphi(C)
is also a Cartan subgroup of
G
, and as
G=CN
,
\varphi(C)=x^{-1}Cx
for some
x\in N
,
i.e.
i_{x}\circ\varphi(C)=C
. Since the inner automorphism
i_{x}
acts trivially on
G/N
, the automorphism of
G/N
induced by

i_{x}\circ\varphi
is same as
\bar{\varphi}
. Hence we replace
\varphi
by
i_{x}\circ\varphi
and assume that
\varphi(C)=C
. Moreover,

C\cap N
is connected (cf.
[
34
]
, Theorem 1.9).

Let
N_{1}:=\overline{[N,N]}
. It is a closed connected subgroup of
N
and it is a characteristic subgroup of
G
. Let
H:=(C\cap N)N_{1}
. Then

H
is a connected and
\varphi
-invariant and normal in
G
. Moreover, the maximal compact subgroup of
N
is central in
G
, and hence it
is contained in
C\cap N
. Therefore,
H
is closed and
N/H
is simply connected. Note that
H\neq N
, otherwise as
N
is nilpotent,
it follows that
N=C\cap N
and hence
G=C
, which leads to a contradiction as
G
is not nilpotent. The condition on the eigenvalues of
elements of
\mathrm{Ad\,}G
as in the hypothesis is satisfied if we replace
G
by
G/H
. Also,
CH/H
is a Cartan subgroup of
G/H

[
20
, Theorem 1.5]

and it is isomorphic to
C/(C\cap N)
, which in turn is isomorphic to
P
. Moreover,
G/H
is not nilpotent as
CH/H
is a proper subgroup of
G/H
.
Therefore, replacing
G
by
G/H
,
N
by
N/H
, and
C
by
CH/H
, we may assume that
G=CN
where
N
is nontrivial abelian and a
vector group and
C\cap N
is trivial.

Now
G=P\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where the Cartan subgroup
P
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
,
N
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and

\varphi|_{P}=-\mathrm{Id}
. Let
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
. Then
\varphi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\varphi(t,r)=(-t,\varphi^{\prime}(r))
,
t\in P={\mathbb{R}}
,
r\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Since
P
is connected, we get that for each
t\in P
, eigenvalues of the
i_{t}
-action on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
being real, are positive. Fix
t\in P\setminus\{0\}
.
There exists
r\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that
i_{t}(r)=e^{\alpha t}r
for some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
. As
\varphi
is an automorphism, we get that

i_{t}^{-1}\circ\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi^{\prime}\circ i_{t}
, and hence
e^{-\alpha t}\varphi^{\prime}(r)=e^{\alpha t}\varphi^{\prime}(r)
, as
\varphi^{\prime}
is linear. As
\varphi^{\prime}(r)\neq 0
,
we get that
e^{2\alpha t}=1
, and hence
\alpha=0
, and
i_{t}(r)=r
. Thus all the eigenvalues of
i_{t}|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
are same and equal to
1
,
and this holds for all
t\in P
. Since
P
is abelian and connected, there exists a subspace
V:={\mathbb{R}}^{k}
for some
k
with
0<k\leq n
,
such that
P
acts trivially on
V
. That is,
P
centralises
V
, and hence
PV
is an abelian subgroup of dimension at least
2
in
G
,
which contradicts the fact that
P
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
. Therefore,
-1
cannot be an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\bar{\varphi}
.

We now prove the second assertion. Suppose
G
is a connected solvable Lie group such that the dimension of
G/N
is odd and
G

satisfies the condition on
\mathrm{Ad\,}G
as in the hypothesis. Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. As the dimension of
G/N
is odd,
{\mathrm{d}\,}\bar{\varphi}
has
a real eigenvalue and By Remark 3.3 of
[
3
]
(see also
Remark

4.3
), we have that the eigenvalues of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi
are roots of unity.
Therefore, the real eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\bar{\varphi}
is either
1
or
-1
. Now by the previous assertion,
-1
is not an eigenvalue of

{\mathrm{d}\,}\bar{\varphi}
. Thus
1
is eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\bar{\varphi}
, and hence that of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi
. By
3.1
, we get that

R(\varphi)=\infty
. Since this is true for every
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. We have that
G
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property.
∎

Note that for a connected solvable Lie group
G
, for any
g\in N
, the nilradical of
G
,
\mathrm{Ad\,}g
is unipotent, and hence all its eigenvalues
are equal to
1
. Also, for any
g\in G
,
\mathrm{Ad\,}g
acts trivially on
G/N
. Then one can replace the condition on
\mathrm{Ad\,}G
in
5.1
,
by the condition that the restriction of
\mathrm{Ad\,}g
to the Lie algebra of
N
has real eigenvalues for every
g\not\in N
.

In the following Corollary,
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}={\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
is a multiplicative group, its connected component of the identity
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}
is isomorphic
to
{\mathbb{R}}
, and they both act on the additive group
{\mathbb{R}}
by automorphisms. Note that both
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}
do not have (topological)

R_{\infty}
-property, as the map
x\mapsto x^{-1}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
(resp.
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}
) has Reidemeister number
2
(resp.
1
).

Corollary 5.2
.

{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathbb{R}}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
have topological
R_{\infty}
-property.

Proof.

Both the groups
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathbb{R}}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
are solvable but not nilpotent. Moreover,

{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
is isomorphic to
\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\cr 0&1\cr\end{bmatrix}\mid a,b\in{\mathbb{R}},a\neq 0\right\}
, and
{\mathbb{R}}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
is isomorphic to

\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\cr 0&1\cr\end{bmatrix}\mid a,b\in{\mathbb{R}},a>0\right\}
.
Under this identification,
{\mathbb{R}}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
is a closed connected characteristic subgroup of index
2
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
. Hence by
2.3
(1),
it is enough to prove that
G:={\mathbb{R}}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property. The nilradical
N
of
G
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
and the
conjugation action of every
g\in G
on
N
has a unique eigenvalue which is real and this action is trivial on
G/N
.
Then by
5.1
,
G
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property.
∎

We can also take connected
2
-step solvable Lie groups

H_{n}={\mathbb{R}}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, with
{\mathbb{R}}
being a multiplicative group which acts on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by scaler matrices, i.e.
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
acts on
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
as

x\mapsto\alpha tx
for some
\alpha\neq\pm 1
. By
5.1
,
H_{n}
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
In the above, the action of
{\mathbb{R}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
can also be replaced by a one-parameter subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
which is diagonalisable over
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
U_{n}
denote the group of
n\times n
strictly upper triangular real matrices (with all the diagonal entries equal to
1
).
It is shown in
[
28
, Theorem 4]
that
U_{n}
does not have (topological)
R_{\infty}
-property if
n\geq 3
; this is true also for
n=2
. Here,
we consider groups of invertible
n\times n
upper triangular real matrices (with nonzero diagonal entries),
n\geq 2
. Note that for
n=1
, such
a group is isomorphic to the multiplicative abelian group
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
which does not have (topological)
R_{\infty}
-property, as the Reidemeister
number of the map
x\mapsto x^{-1}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
is
2
.

Theorem 5.3
.

The group of invertible
n\times n
upper triangular real matrices has topological
R_{\infty}
-property for every
n\geq 2
.

Proof.

Let
G
be the group of invertible
n\times n
upper triangular real matrices for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\neq 1
. Then it is a solvable Lie group which is
not nilpotent and the eigenvalues of every element of
\mathrm{Ad\,}G
are real. Also,
G^{0}
, the connected component of the identity in
G
, is a group of
elements in
G
with positive entries on the diagonal and it is a characteristic subgroup of finite index in
G
. Now by
2.3
(1), it
is enough to show that
G^{0}
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property. Hence we may replace
G
by
G^{0}
, and assume that
G
is
a connected solvable Lie group consisting of upper triangular real matrices with positive entries on the diagonal, i.e.

G=\{(a_{ij})\mid a_{ij}\in{\mathbb{R}},a_{ij}=0\mbox{ if }j<i\mbox{ and }a_{ii}>0,1\leq i,j\leq n\}.

The nilradical
N
of
G
is the group
ZU
, where
Z
is the center of
G
consisting of diagonal matrices with same diagonal entries
in
G
(positive scalar matrices), and
U:=U_{n}
is as above. Also
G/N
is simply connected and it is isomorphic to a vector group.
It is easy to see that
\mathrm{Ad\,}g
has real eigen values for every
g\in G
. By
5.1
, we have that
-1
is not an eigenvalue of
any automorphism of
G/N
induced by an automorphism of
G
.

If
n
is even, then the dimension of
G/(ZU)
is odd and it follows from
5.1
that

G
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property.

Now suppose
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
is fixed such that
n
is odd, and
n\neq 1
. Then the dimension of
G/N
is even.
Hence, we cannot apply
5.1
directly.

Since
G
is solvable, the last nontrivial subgroup in the derived series of
G
is

L=\{(a_{ij})\mid a_{1n}\in{\mathbb{R}},a_{ii}=1,a_{ij}=0\mbox{ if }1\leq i\neq j\leq n\mbox{ except for }a_{1n}\}.

Note that
L
is closed, connected and characteristic in
G
and it is the center of
U
. Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
.
Then
\varphi
keeps
L
invariant. Observe that both the center
Z
of
G
and
L
are isomorphic to a one-dimensional
vector space and
G\neq ZL
. If
\varphi
acts trivially on either

or
L
, then
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi
,
and hence by
3.1
,
R(\varphi)=\infty
. Now we show that
\bar{\varphi}
on
G/N
has a real eigenvalue.

Observe that
U=[G,G]
and it is also characteristic in
G
, and in particular, it is
\varphi
-invariant. It is well-known that
the group
C
of diagonal matrices (with positive entries) in

is a Cartan subgroup of
G
. Then
G=CU
and
\varphi(C)

is also a Cartan subgroup of
G
, and hence there exists an

such that
\varphi(C)=x^{-1}Cx
, i.e.
i_{x}\circ\varphi(C)=C

[
32
, Proposition 6]
. As
i_{x}
acts trivially on
G/U
, and hence on
G/N
, the automorphism of
G/N
induced by

i_{x}\circ\varphi
is same as
\bar{\varphi}
. Hence, we may replace
\varphi
by
i_{x}\circ\varphi
and assume that
\varphi(C)=C
.
Note that
C
is abelian and it is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Now we show that there exists an
(n-1)

which is

\varphi
-invariant and it contains the center

.

Consider
CL
; it is a group as

is normal in
G
. It is closed, connected and
\varphi
-invariant, and its dimension is

.
Let
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi|_{C}
. For any diagonal element

, we write
\varphi^{\prime}(d)=(d^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,d^{\prime}_{n})
.
We denote by
x=(x_{ij})\in CL
as
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1})
for simplicity, where
x_{i}=x_{ii}
,
1\leq i\leq n
and
x_{n+1}=x_{1n}
.
If
x_{n+1}=0
, then

and if
x_{i}=1,1\leq i\leq n
, then

. For

. The group operation is given by

The map

on

is given by

\varphi(x)=(x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n},rx^{\prime}_{1}x_{1}^{-1}x_{n+1}),x\in CL,

where

\varphi(1,\ldots,1,x_{n+1})=(1,\ldots,1,rx_{n+1})

keeps

and

, where

\displaystyle\varphi(xy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x^{\prime}_{1}y^{\prime}_{1},\ldots x^{\prime}_{n}y^{\prime}_{n},x^{\prime}_{1}y^{\prime}_{1}(x_{1}y_{1})^{-1}(rx_{1}y_{n+1}+rx_{n+1}y_{n}))\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\varphi(x)\varphi(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x^{\prime}_{1}y^{\prime}_{1},\ldots x^{\prime}_{n}y^{\prime}_{n},rx^{\prime}_{1}y^{\prime}_{1}y_{1}^{-1}y_{n+1}+ry^{\prime}_{n}x^{\prime}_{1}x_{1}^{-1}x_{n+1}).

Hence,

. This implies that if

, then

\varphi

where

. As

1
in
C
. We have that
\varphi|_{C}
has
a real eigenvalue. Also, for the niradical
N=ZU
of
G
, we have that
N\subset VU
. Hence
\bar{\varphi}
on
G/N
has a real eigenvalue and
it has to be
\pm\,1
by Remark 3.3 of
[
3
]
(see also
Remark

4.3
). As observed above, by
5.1
, it cannot be
-1
. Thus
1
is an
eigenvalue of
\bar{\varphi}
, and hence that of
\varphi
. Now by
3.1
,
R(\varphi)=\infty
. Since this holds for any
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
,
we have that
G
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property. Thus every group of invertible
n\times n
upper triangular real matrices has topological

R_{\infty}
-property,
n\geq 2
.
∎

Note that in
[
2
]
, Borel subgroups of a semisimple algebraic group over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero are considered and
it is shown that they have algebraic
R_{\infty}
-property, i.e.
R(\phi)=\infty
for every algebraic automorphism
\phi
. Borel subgroup in a connected
Lie (resp. algebraic) group is a maximal closed (Zariski) connected solvable subgroup. In
5.3
, we have seen that
a Borel subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property,
n\geq 2
. In
[
18
]
, the quotient group of a Borel subgroup modulo its center
is considered over some specific integral domains which do not include
{\mathbb{R}}
. Here we show the following.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
G
be a group of invertible
n\times n
upper triangular real matrices for
n\geq 2
and let
Z
be the center of
G
.
Then
G/Z
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property.

Proof.

The center
Z
of
G
is also the center of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
, which is the group of diagonal matrices with same entries, i.e. scaler matrices.
Here, both
G
and
G/Z
are solvable Lie groups with finitely many connected components and
G^{0}Z
is closed. By
2.3
(1), we may replace
G

by its connected component of the identity and assume that
G
is a connected Lie group of upper triangular real matrices with positive diagonal entries,
and
Z
is the group of diagonal matrices whose all entries are same and positive. Now the nilradical of
G
is
N=ZU
as in the proof of

5.3
. Then
N^{\prime}:=N/Z
is the nilradical of
H:=G/Z
. If
n
is even, then the dimension of
H/N^{\prime}
is odd and hence, by
5.1
,

H
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property. Note that
C
as in the proof of
5.3
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
and by Theorem 1.5 of
[
20
]
,

C^{\prime}:=C/Z
is a Cartan subgroup of
H
and
H=C^{\prime}\ltimes N^{\prime}
, where
N^{\prime}=[H,H]
(which is isomorphic to
U=[G,G]
). Let
L
be the last nontrivial
subgroup in the derived series of
H
, which is also the center of
N^{\prime}
. Then
L
is a connected abelian one-dimensional subgroup. Both
N^{\prime}

and
L
are characteristic in
H
. Let
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(H)
. If
\psi|_{L}=\mathrm{Id}
, then
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\psi
, and hence
R(\psi)=\infty
by
3.1
.
Since
\psi(C^{\prime})
is also a Cartan subgroup of
H
and and it is conjugate to
C^{\prime}
, we can replace
\psi
by
i_{x}\circ\psi
for some
x\in N^{\prime}
such that

\psi(C^{\prime})=C^{\prime}
. Note that
C^{\prime}
is isomorphic to a vector group of dimension
n-1
. Let
V^{\prime}
be the centraliser of
L
in
C^{\prime}
. Then
V^{\prime}
is a closed
connected subgroup of
C^{\prime}
. Note that
C^{\prime}
does not centralise
L
, otherwise
C^{\prime}L
would be nilpotent and it would contradict the fact that
C^{\prime}

is a maximal nilpotent group (as it is a Cartan subgroup). As
L
is one-dimensional and
C^{\prime}
is connected, we have that the action of
C^{\prime}
on
L
is through
the action of
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}
. In fact, there is a continuous homomorphism from
C^{\prime}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}
with
V^{\prime}
as its kernel. Thus, we get that
C^{\prime}/V^{\prime}
is isomorphic to

{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}
and
V^{\prime}
has co-dimension one in
C^{\prime}
. Since
L
is characteristic in
H
, its centraliser in
H
is also characteristic in
H
. As
\psi
keeps

C^{\prime}
invariant, we get that
\psi(V^{\prime})=V^{\prime}
. Since the dimension of
C^{\prime}/V^{\prime}
is
1
,
\psi|_{C^{\prime}}
has real eigenvalue. Arguing as in the last part of proof of

5.3
, we get that
R(\psi)=\infty
. Since this holds for all
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(H)
, it follows that
H
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property.
∎

Note that
5.3
can be proven differently using similar arguments as in the proof of
5.4
. It can also be proven using
5.4
and
2.2
.
Observe that if
H
is subgroup consisting of upper triangular matrices in

), then

is isomorphic
to

, where

and

. Then by Lemmas
2.2
and

(1),

have topological

-property, i.e. all Borel subgroups of

have this property,

.
We now construct some more connected
m
-step solvable non-nilpotent Lie groups with this property for every

.

Example 5.5
.

For the unipotent group
U_{n}
of
n\times n
strictly upper triangular real matrices, let
H_{n}={\mathbb{R}}\ltimes U_{n}
, where the action
of
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}
on

is given by
(a_{ij})\mapsto(b_{ij})\in U_{n}
, where

, if
j<i
,
b_{ii}=a_{ii}=1
and
b_{ij}=t^{k}a_{ij}
, where
k=j-i
if

, for

, Then each

is a

, and hence

-property,
for every

,

. (The case

, where

is isomorphic to

, is already covered in

.)

We also show that the condition that eigenvalues of every element of
\mathrm{Ad\,}G
are real is necessary in general for both the assertions in
5.1
,
see the following example. We first note that
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, the special orthogonal group (the group of rotations on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
), is a one-dimensional torus
and the normaliser of
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
in
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is
({\mathfrak{H}}\cup\{I\}){\mathfrak{D}}\,\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, where
I
is the identity matrix,
{\mathfrak{H}}=\{\varrho,\xi\}
with

\varrho=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\cr 0&-1\cr\end{bmatrix}\ \mbox{ and }\ \xi=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\cr 1&0\cr\end{bmatrix},

and
{\mathfrak{D}}=\{rI\mid r\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\}
, the center of
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. The centraliser of
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
in
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is
{\mathfrak{D}}\,\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.

Example 5.6
.

Let
G={\mathbb{R}}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, where
{\mathbb{R}}
is an additive group and the action of any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is given by

x\mapsto e^{2i\pi t}x
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Here, the center
Z
of
G
is contained in
{\mathbb{R}}
, and it is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}
. The nilradical
N
of
G

is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
\mathrm{Ad\,}g
has complex eigenvalues if
2g\not\in ZN
. Let
\varphi:G\to G
, be defined as follows:

\varphi(t,x)=(-t,\varphi^{\prime}(x))
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, where
\varphi^{\prime}=2\varrho
, for
\varrho
as above. Then
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. Since
G

is solvable and
1
is not an eigenvalue of
\varphi
, by
3.1
,
R(\varphi)=1
. Let
\varphi_{1}
be the automorphism of
G/Z
which
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We have that
R(\varphi_{1})=1
. Note that
\mathrm{Ad\,}g
has complex eigenvalues if
g\in\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
and

g\neq e
. Note also that
NZ/Z
is the nilradical of
G
which is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. If
\bar{\varphi}
(resp.
\bar{\varphi}_{1}
) is the
automorphism induced by
\varphi
(resp.
\varphi_{1}
) on
G/N
(resp.
(G/Z)/(NZ/Z)
), then
-1
is the only eigenvalue of both
\bar{\varphi}

on
G/N
and
{\mathrm{d}\,}\bar{\varphi}_{1}
. Thus both
{\mathbb{R}}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
defined as above do not have (topological)

R_{\infty}
-property. Note that one can choose any
\varphi^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
that normalises
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
but does not centralise

\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, and
1
is not an eigenvalue of
\varphi^{\prime}
; e.g.
\varphi^{\prime}\in\{r\varrho,r\xi\mid r\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0,1\}\}
and the corresponding

\varphi
as well as
\varphi_{1}
will not have
1
as an eigenvalue, and their Reidemeister number will be
1
.

Examples of connected solvable Lie groups with topological
R_{\infty}
-property mentioned above are all simply connected. There is
a connected solvable Lie group which is not simply connected and has topological
R_{\infty}
-property, even though
\mathrm{Ad\,}g
has
a complex eigenvalue for some element
g
in it. The group
W:=\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{H}}/D
is known as the
Walnut
group, (see for example

[
6
]
), where
{\mathbb{H}}:=U_{3}
is the
3
-dimensional Heisenberg group, the center
Z
of
{\mathbb{H}}
is the center of
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{H}}
and
D

is an infinite discrete (central) subgroup of
Z
(see the description of the action of
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
on
{\mathbb{H}}
, which acts trivially on the center of

{\mathbb{H}}
, in Section 5 before
5.9
).

Proposition 5.7
.

The Walnut group has topological
R_{\infty}
-property.

Proof.

The Walnut group
W=\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{H}}/D
is a 4-dimensional connected solvable Lie group whose center
K:=Z/D
, is a one-dimensional
torus. Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(W)
. We show that
R(\varphi)=\infty
. By
3.1
, it is enough to show that
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi
.
Let
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi|_{N}
and
\bar{\varphi}
be the automorphism of
W/N
induced by
\varphi
, where
N={\mathbb{H}}/D
is the nilradical of
W
. Since

W/N=\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is a one-dimensional torus, and it has only two automorphisms and
{\mathrm{d}\,}\bar{\varphi}=\pm\,\mathrm{Id}
. If
\bar{\varphi}=\mathrm{Id}
, then

R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
, and hence
R(\varphi)=\infty
. Similarly,
\varphi|_{K}
also has only two automorphisms. Thus if
\varphi|_{K}=\mathrm{Id}
,
then
|\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)|=\infty
, by
3.1
,
R(\varphi)=\infty
. Now suppose
\bar{\varphi}(x)=x^{-1}
, for all
x\in G/N=\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
and

\varphi(x)=x^{-1}
for all
x\in K
. Note that
T:=\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\times K
is a maximal torus (maximal compact connected abelian subgroup)
in
W
(here,
T
is also a Cartan subgroup of
W
). Then
\varphi(T)
is also a maximal torus in
W
and it is conjugate to
T
by an element
of
N
. Thus
i_{g}\circ\varphi(T)=T
, for some
g\in N
. Observe that the action of
i_{g}\circ\varphi
on
G/N
and on
Z/D
is same as that of

\varphi
. As
R(\varphi)=R(i_{g}\circ\varphi)
by
2.1
, we may replace
\varphi
by
i_{g}\circ\varphi
and assume that

\varphi(T)=T
. Now for
x\in\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
,
\varphi(x)=x^{-1}k
for some
k\in K
which depends on
x
.

Note that
{\mathbb{H}}
is the simply connected covering group of
N
with the covering map
p:{\mathbb{H}}\to{\mathbb{H}}/D
and there exists
\psi\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{H}})
such
that
\psi
keeps
D
invariant and
\varphi^{\prime}=\bar{\psi}
is the automorphism induced by
\psi
on
{\mathbb{H}}/D=N
. Let
\varphi^{\prime}_{1}
be the automorphism
induced by
\varphi^{\prime}
on
N/K={\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. As
N/K
and
{\mathbb{H}}/Z
are isomorphic, we may assume that
\varphi^{\prime}_{1}=\psi^{\prime}_{1}
as an element of

\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, where
\psi^{\prime}_{1}
is the automorphism of
{\mathbb{H}}/Z
induced by
\psi
. Since
\psi|_{Z}=-\mathrm{Id}
, it follows that
\det\psi^{\prime}_{1}=-1
.
Thus
\det\varphi^{\prime}_{1}=-1
.

Let
\varphi_{1}
be automorphism of
G/K
induced by
\varphi
. Note that
G/K
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
\varphi_{1}|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}

is same as
\varphi^{\prime}_{1}
. As
\varphi_{1}(x)=x^{-1}
,
x\in\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, it follows that
\varphi^{\prime}_{1}
normalises
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
in
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, but it does
not centralise
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
(see Example
5.6
). Moreover as
\det\varphi^{\prime}_{1}=-1
, we get that
\varphi^{\prime}_{1}\in{\mathfrak{H}}\,\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, where

{\mathfrak{H}}=\{\varrho,\xi\}
is as above and
{\mathfrak{H}}
consists of elements of order
2
. As
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is compact and
{\mathfrak{H}}
normalises
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
,
we get that
\varphi^{\prime}_{1}
is contained in a compact subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, and hence all its eigenvalues have absolute value
1
. This together
with the fact that
\det\varphi^{\prime}_{1}=-1
, implies that the eigenvalues of
\varphi_{1}
are
1
and
-1
. Hence
1
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi_{1}
,
and hence that of
{\mathrm{d}\,}\varphi
. This implies that
R(\varphi)=\infty
. Since this holds for all
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(W)
, we have that
W
has
topological
R_{\infty}
-property.
∎

For connected semisimple Lie groups, we have from
4.1
that
R(\varphi^{n})=\infty
for every
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. We also know from

[
27
]
, that for
n\geq 3
,
\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
have (topological)
R_{\infty}
-property. For
n=2
, we have the following:

Theorem 5.8
.

\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
have topological
R_{\infty}
-property.

Proof.

Step 1:
Let
G:=\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. It is known that
{\rm Aut}(G)
has two connected components and
{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
consists of the
inner automorphisms of
G
. In fact,
{\rm Aut}(G)=\mathrm{PGL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, i.e. the group of inner automorphisms of
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
restricted to
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
,
which is normal in
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. By
4.1
,
R(\mathrm{Id})=\infty
, and
2.1
implies that for all
g\in G
,
R(i_{g})=\infty
.
Thus, if
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
,
R(\varphi)=\infty
.

Now let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
be defined as follows:
\varphi(g):=\xi\,g\,\xi^{-1}
,
g\in G
, where
\xi\in\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
be as defined before in

Example

5.6
. Moreover,
\varphi\not\in{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
as
\det\xi=-1
and
\xi\not\in\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. Now for

g=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\cr c&d\cr\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}),\ \ \varphi(g)=\xi\,g\,\xi^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\cr 1&0\cr\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}a&b\cr c&d\cr\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}0&1\cr 1&0\cr\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}d&c\cr b&a\cr\end{bmatrix}.

Now it is enough to show that
R(\varphi)=\infty
, as for any
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(G)\setminus{\rm Aut}(G)^{0}
,
\psi=i_{x}\circ\varphi
for some
x\in G
,
and by
2.1
,
R(\psi)=R(\varphi)
.

Let
G=KAU
be an Iwasawa decomposition, where
K
is a compact connected group of rotations on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
A
is the group
of diagonal matrices in
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
with positive entries and
U
is the group of unipotent matrices in
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, i.e.

U=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&r\cr 0&1\cr\end{bmatrix}\mid r\in{\mathbb{R}}\right\}.

Let
x_{r}\in U
for some
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, be such that
x_{r}=\begin{bmatrix}1&r\cr 0&1\cr\end{bmatrix}
. Then for
g
as above,

gx_{r}\varphi(g^{-1})=\begin{bmatrix}a^{2}-b^{2}-abr&bd-ac+adr\cr ac-bd-bcr&d^{2}-c^{2}+cdr\cr\end{bmatrix}.

As
\det g=ad-bc=1
, we get that
U\cap[x_{r}]_{\varphi}=\{x_{r}\}
. As
U
is infinite, we get that
\{[x_{r}]_{\varphi}\mid x_{r}\in U\}
is infinite,
and hence
R(\varphi)=\infty
. Thus
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property.

Step 2:
Now suppose
G:=\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. Then
[G,G]\subset\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. Also
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is its own commutator group.
Hence
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})=[G,G]
and it is a closed (normal) characteristic subgroup of
G
. Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
,
\varphi^{\prime}:=\varphi|_{[G,G]}

and
\bar{\varphi}
be the automorphism of
G/[G,G]
induced by
\varphi
. Now if
R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
, then
R(\varphi)=\infty
.
Now suppose
R(\bar{\varphi})<\infty
. Note that
G/[G,G]
is abelian, moreover, it has only two connected components, and it is
compactly generated. By
3.4
,
|\mathrm{Fix}(\bar{\varphi})|<\infty
. As noted above
R(\varphi^{\prime})=\infty
. Now by
2.3
(1),

R(\varphi)=\infty
. Since this holds for any
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
, we get that
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property.
∎

It seems possible to show that any abstract automorphism of
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is continuous, and hence it would imply that it has

R_{\infty}
-property. As our main focus is on the topological
R_{\infty}
-property for Lie groups, we will not discuss this here.

Consider the
3
-dimensional Heisenberg group
{\mathbb{H}}
. The center
Z
of
{\mathbb{H}}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
and
G/Z
is isomorphic
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. There is a canonical action of
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
on
{\mathbb{H}}
which keeps the center
Z
invariant and it acts on
G/Z
as the
usual action of
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. For the sake of completeness, we define this action here. For simplicity, we denote a generic
element
(y_{ij})
in
{\mathbb{H}}
by a tuple
(a,b,c)
where
a=y_{12}
,
b=y_{23}
and
c=y_{13}
. For
\psi=(x_{ij})\in\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})

and
(a,b,c)\in{\mathbb{H}}
, let

\psi(a,b,c)=(x_{11}a+x_{12}b,x_{21}a+x_{22}b,c\det\psi-\frac{1}{2}ab\det\psi+\frac{1}{2}(x_{11}a+x_{12}b)(x_{21}a+x_{22}b)).

This defines a continuous group action of
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
on
{\mathbb{H}}
by automorphisms and it keeps the center
Z
invariant. Recall that

\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
is the subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
consisting of all matrices of determinant
\pm\,1
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The action of
\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(2,{\mathbb{R}})

on
{\mathbb{H}}
(which is the restriction of the action mentioned above) is such that it acts as
\pm\,\mathrm{Id}
on the center
Z
and
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
acts
trivially on
Z
. Therefore, the action of
\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
keeps any discrete infinite subgroup
D
of
Z
invariant. Let

\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})}
denote the universal covering of
\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}
. We will consider some connected Lie groups below,
some of which are neither solvable nor semisimple.

Using Theorems
5.8
and
3.4
, and Theorem 1 of
[
27
]
and the structure of groups, we get the following.

Corollary 5.9
.

The following groups have topological
R_{\infty}
-property:

(1)

\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
,
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{H}}
.

(2)

\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
,
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{H}}
,
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{H}}/D

(3)

\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(n,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
,
\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{H}}
, and
\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{H}}/D
.

(4)

\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})}
,
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathrm{PGL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.

(5)

\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})}
,
n\geq 3
,
n
odd.

Proof.

We know from
5.8
(resp.
[
27
]
) that
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
(resp.
\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
,
n\geq 3
) have
topological
R_{\infty}
-property. The proper closed connected normal subgroup in each of the groups in (1) as well as (2) is the
nilradical of that group, and hence characteristic in it. Hence, by
2.2
. the assertion holds for all groups in
(1)
and
(2)
.

(3):
The connected component of the identity in
\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
(resp.
\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(n,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is
\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})

(resp.
\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
) which is a (characteristic) subgroup of index
2
and has topological
R_{\infty}
-property by
5.8

(resp. by (2) above). Hence by
2.3
(1),
\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(n,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
have topological
R_{\infty}
-property,

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Similarly,
\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{H}}
(resp.
\mathrm{SL}^{\pm}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{H}}/D
) has topological
R_{\infty}
-property, as it has

\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{H}}
(resp.
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes{\mathbb{H}}/D)
as a subgroup of index
2
with the same property.

(4):
Let
G:=\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})}
. Then the center
Z
of
G
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}
and it is characteristic in
G
. Let

p:G\to\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
be the covering map. Then
\ker p
is a subgroup of index
2
in
Z
. Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
. Then
\varphi
keeps

Z
invariant. Now the only automorphisms of
{\mathbb{Z}}
are
\pm\,\mathrm{Id}
. Therefore,
\varphi
keeps any subgroup of
Z
invariant, in particular,
it keeps
\ker p
invariant. Therefore,
\varphi
induces an automorphism
\bar{\varphi}
on
G/\ker p
which is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.
By
5.8
,
R(\bar{\varphi})=\infty
, and hence
R(\varphi)=\infty
. Thus
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})}
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property.

Note that
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})=\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})/\{I,-I\}
, where
I
is the identity matrix in
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
and
D=\{I,-I\}
is the center of
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. For

G=\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})}
and its center
Z
as above,
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is isomorphic to
G/Z
, and
G
is a covering group of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.
Moreover, any automorphism of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
lifts to an automorphism of
G
and any automorphism
\varphi
of
G
induces an
automorphism of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
as well as of
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. Thus, given an automorphism
\psi
of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, there is an automorphism

\alpha
of
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
such that
\bar{\alpha}=\psi
, i.e.
\psi
is induced by
\alpha
. Now as
R(\alpha)=\infty
and
D
is finite, by

2.3
(4),
R(\psi)=\infty
. Since this holds for all
\psi\in{\rm Aut}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}))
, we have that
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property.

Note that
\mathrm{PGL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is the quotient group of
\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
modulo its center
\{rI\mid r\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}\}
. Then
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is a connected
(normal) characteristic subgroup of index
2
in
\mathrm{PGL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. By
2.3
(1),
\mathrm{PGL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property.

(5):
Let
n\geq 3
be such that
n
is odd. Then the center of
\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
is trivial. Let
G:=\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})}
and let

p:G\to\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
be the covering map. Then
\ker p
is the center of
G
which is invariant under all automorphisms of
G
. Now as

G/\ker p
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
, which has topological
R_{\infty}
-property, it follows from
2.2
, that
G
also has this property.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

One can take any central subgroup
H
in
\widetilde{SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})}
then
H
is of the form
H_{n}:=n{\mathbb{Z}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

and the groups
G_{n}:=\widetilde{SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})}/H_{n}
has topological
R_{\infty}
-property. Note that for the center
Z_{n}
of
G_{n}
,
G_{n}/Z_{n}

is isomorphic to
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, which has topological
R_{\infty}
-property from
5.9
, and so does
G_{n}
, by
2.2
. Note also that
each
G_{n}
is a connected semisimple Lie group with finite center of order
n
, and is a covering group of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. If
n
is even,
then
G_{n}
is a covering group of
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. Thus, all the covering groups of
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
as well as those of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
have topological

R_{\infty}
-property.

It would be interesting to study if
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})}
,
\mathrm{PGL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathrm{PSL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
have topological
R_{\infty}
-property for
n\geq 4
,

n
even; it would enough to prove it for
\mathrm{PSL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
. Note that for
n
odd, both
\mathrm{PGL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathrm{PSL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
are isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
.
More generally, it would be interesting to study if a general connected semisimple Lie group has topological
R_{\infty}
-property; this would help
in the study of this property for a general connected non-solvable Lie group.

Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in the classification of stable solutions to the following system:

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}u=\rho(\mathbf{x})v^{-p},\quad\Delta_{s}v=\rho(\mathbf{x})u^{-\theta},\quad u,v>0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.1)

where
\Delta_{s}u=\Delta_{x}u+|x|^{2s}\Delta_{y}u
is the Grushin operator,
s\geq 0
,
1<\theta\leq p
, and
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a radial continuous function satisfying the following assumption:

(\star)

There exists
\alpha\geq 0
and
C>0
such that
\rho(\mathbf{x})\geq C\rho_{0}(\mathbf{x})
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
\rho_{0}
is given by

\rho_{0}:=\left(1+\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2(s+1)}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2(s+1)}},

and

\|\mathbf{x}\|=\left(|x|^{2(s+1)}+|y|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2(s+1)}},\quad s\geq 0,\quad\mathbf{x}=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}},

is the norm corresponding to the Grushin distance, where
|x|
and
|y|
are the usual Euclidean norms in
\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}
and
\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}
, respectively. The
\|\mathbf{x}\|
-norm is 1-homogeneous for the group of anisotropic dilations associated with
\Delta_{s}
. The operator
\Delta_{s}
belongs to the class of degenerate elliptic operators, which has received significant attention over the years, see
[
22
,
25
,
28
]
. For elementary properties and typical examples of
\Delta_{s}
, we refer to
[
17
,
18
]
, and we also highlight the recent survey
[
19
]
.

We begin by considering the well-known weighted Lane-Emden system:

\displaystyle-\Delta u=\rho v^{p},\quad-\Delta v=\rho u^{\theta},\quad u,v>0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad p\geq\theta>1,

(1.2)

where
\rho(x)
is a radial function satisfying
\rho(x)\geq A(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
at infinity. This system has attracted considerable attention in recent years, see the pioneering works
[
1
,
15
,
16
,
24
]
. Cowan
[
1
]
classified positive stable solutions for
N\leq 10
and
p\geq\theta>2
, and this result was extended in
[
16
]
for
\rho\equiv(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
, with
2\leq\theta\leq p
and
N\leq 10+4\alpha
. This was further improved by Hajlaoui et al.
[
15
]
, who established a new comparison property crucial for dealing with the case
1<\theta\leq\frac{4}{3}
.

Among other things, Mtiri and Ye
[
24
]
completely classified positive solutions stable outside a compact set for subcritical
(p,\theta)
pairs.

A natural question in the study of systems involving the Grushin operator is whether similar classifications can be obtained as for the Laplace operator.

For the general system with
s\geq 0
:

\displaystyle-\Delta_{s}u=v^{p},\quad-\Delta_{s}v=u^{\theta},\quad u,v>0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}},\quad p\geq\theta>1,

(1.3)

the Liouville property is less understood and is more complicated to analyze compared to the case when
s=0
, due to the lack of symmetry in the operator
\Delta_{s}
and its degeneration on the manifold
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}
, which introduces additional mathematical difficulties.

Adopting the approach used in
[
1
,
15
]
, the authors of
[
6
]
extended Cowan’s results (Theorem 1 with
s=0
) and demonstrated that no smooth stable solution exists for (
1.3
) if
\frac{4}{3}<\theta\leq p
and
N_{s}:=N_{1}+(1+s)N_{2}
satisfies:

N_{s}<2+2\beta_{1}t_{1}^{+},\quad\text{where}\quad t_{1}^{\pm}=\sqrt{\omega}+\sqrt{\omega-\sqrt{\omega}},\quad\omega=\frac{p\theta\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}},\quad\alpha_{1}=\frac{2(p+1)}{p\theta-1},\quad\beta_{1}=\frac{2(\theta+1)}{p\theta-1}.

They also classified bounded stable solutions for
1<\theta\leq\frac{4}{3}
and found that
N_{s}
satisfies:

N_{s}<2+\left[2+\frac{2(p+1)}{p\theta-1}+\frac{4(2-p)}{\theta+p-2}\right]t_{1}^{+}.

This result was improved in a work by Mtiri
[
23
]
, where the range of nonexistence results was extended beyond the work of
[
6
]
.

A new approach, independently developed by Mtiri
[
12
]
, enables a Liouville theorem for stable solutions of (
1.3
) for any
p,\theta>0
and
N_{s}
satisfying:

N_{s}<2+\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}.

On the other hand, the nonexistence of stable solutions for systems with negative exponents has attracted significant attention
[
5
]
, but the problem remains incompletely addressed.

In the special case where
\rho\equiv 1
, the system (
1.1
) becomes:

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}u=v^{-p},\quad\Delta_{s}v=u^{-\theta},\quad u,v>0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad p\geq\theta>1.

(1.4)

Following the approach in
[
6
]
, Duong-Nguyen
[
5
]
established the following nonexistence result:

Theorem A

Assume that
1<\theta\leq p
and

N_{s}<2+\frac{8}{p+\theta+2}t_{0}^{+},\quad t_{0}^{+}=\sqrt{\gamma}+\sqrt{\gamma+\sqrt{\gamma}}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma=\frac{p\theta(p-1)}{\theta-1},

then (
1.4
) has no bounded stable solution.

In this paper, our aim is to generalize
[
5
]
. Let us first recall the notion of stability, which is motivated by
[
21
]
, see also
[
1
,
6
,
15
]
.

Definition 1.1

A positive solution
(u,v)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of (
1.1
) is called stable if there are positive smooth functions
\varphi
,
\chi
such that

-\Delta_{s}\varphi=pv^{-p-1}\chi,\quad-\Delta_{s}\chi=\theta u^{-\theta-1}\varphi\quad\text{in}\,\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Our main results state as follows:

Theorem 1.1

Suppose that
\rho
satisfies
(\star)
and let
x_{0}
be the largest root of the polynomial

H(x)=x^{4}-\frac{16p\theta(p-1)}{\theta-1}\left(\frac{1}{p+\theta+2}\right)^{2}\left[x^{2}+\frac{p+\theta-2}{(p+\theta+2)(\theta-1)}x+\frac{p-1}{(\theta-1)(p+\theta+2)^{2}}\right].

If
1<\theta\leq p
, then (
1.1
) has no bounded stable solution if

N_{s}<2\left[1+(2+\alpha)x_{0}\right].

The famous weighted Grushin equation:

\displaystyle-\Delta_{s}u=\rho|u|^{p-1}u\quad\text{in}\,\mathbb{R}^{N}:=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}},

(1.5)

has played an important role in the development of nonlinear analysis in the last decades.

Firstly, we recall that in the case
s=0
and
\rho=1
, the finite Morse index solutions to the corresponding problem

\displaystyle-\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u\quad\text{in}\,\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\text{where}\;p>1,

(1.6)

have been completely classified by Farina
[
10
]
. Indeed, he proved that a smooth nontrivial solution to (
1.6
) exists if
1<p<p_{JL}
and
N\geq 2
. Here,
p_{JL}
stands for the Joseph-Lundgren exponent (see
[
10
]
) (see also
[
14
]
). Later on, Fazly
[
11
]
utilized Farina’s approach to obtain the nonexistence for nontrivial stable solutions of

-\Delta u=|x|^{\alpha}|u|^{p-1}u

when
p>1
and
N
satisfying

N<N_{\alpha}\quad\text{where}\quad N_{\alpha}=2+\frac{2(2+\alpha)}{p-1}\left(p+\sqrt{p^{2}-p}\right).

A large amount of work has been done generalizing this result in various directions. To cite a few, we refer to
[
2
,
3
,
15
,
16
,
27
]
.

Let us comment on related results. For semilinear equations with negative exponents

\displaystyle\Delta u=\frac{1}{u^{p}}\quad u>0\quad\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\text{where}\;p>1,

(1.7)

the finite Morse index solutions have also been classified by Esposito
[
7
]
. See also
[
4
,
8
,
9
,
20
]
.

Another possible generalization corresponds to elliptic problems involving the Grushin operator, i.e. problem (
1.5
). In
[
25
]
, using Farina’s approach, Rahal established a Liouville-type theorem for the equation (
1.5
) with
\rho=|x|_{s}^{\alpha}
, and
N_{s}<N_{\alpha}
.

After that, there have been many contributions to the classification of stable solutions to elliptic equations with negative exponents in various cases of nonlinearities
[
26
,
5
]
.

Here, we obtain a classification result for the weighted Grushin equation with negative exponents by studying the system. In fact, when
p=\theta
, using a Souplet-type estimate (see (2.1) below), the system (
1.1
) is reduced to the weighted Grushin equation:

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}u=\rho(\mathbf{x})u^{-p},\quad u>0\quad\mbox{in }\;\;\mathbb{R}^{N}\quad\mbox{where }\;\;,p>1.

(1.8)

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we can claim:

Corollary 1.1

Suppose that
\rho
satisfies
(\star)
. Let
p>1
, and

N<2+\frac{2(2+\alpha)}{p+1}\left(p+\sqrt{p^{2}+p}\right).

Then (
1.8
) has no bounded stable solution.

Remark 1.1

1.

If
\alpha=0
, then the results in Corollary
1.1
coincide with those in
[
15
]
.

2.

We can show that
\frac{2t_{0}^{+}}{p+\theta+2}<x_{0}
for any
1<\theta\leq p
, (see Remark 2.1 below), where
x_{0}
is the largest root of the polynomial

given by Theorem
1.1
. Hence, the range of the nonexistence result in Theorem
1.1
is larger than that provided by Theorem
A
with
\alpha=0
.

3.

We note also that the method used in the present paper can be applied to study weighted systems and to a more general class of degenerate operators, such as the
\Delta_{s}
operator (see
[
13
,
18
]
) of the form

\Delta_{s}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}^{2}\Delta_{x^{(j)}},\quad s:=(s_{1},\dots,s_{N}):\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
s_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
for
i=1,\dots,N

\Delta_{s}

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some notations and prove some important estimates which will be used in this paper.

In the following,
C
will denote a generic positive constant independent on
(u,v)
, which could be changed from one line to another. The ball of center

2.1
Notation and some known facts about
\Delta_{s}

We now introduce some notations and basic properties related to the Grushin operator. Let
s
be a positive real number and let
\mathbf{z}:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N_{1}},y_{1},\dots,y_{N_{2}})=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N_{1},N_{2}\geq 1
and
N=N_{1}+N_{2}.
We denote by
|x|
(resp.
|y|
) the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}
(resp.
\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}
):
|x|:=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{N_{1}}^{2}}
(resp.
|y|:=\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+\dots+y_{N_{2}}^{2}}
).

Set
\nabla_{x}
and
\nabla_{y}
as Euclidean gradients with respect to the variables
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}
respectively. For
i=1,\dots,N_{1}
and
j=1,\dots,N_{2}
, consider the vector fields

X_{i}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\quad\mbox{and}\;\;Y_{i}:=|x|^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}},

and the associated gradient as follows

\nabla_{s}:=(X_{1}\dots X_{N_{1}},Y_{1}\dots Y_{N_{2}})=(\nabla_{x},|x|^{s}\nabla_{y}).

The Grushin operator
\Delta_{s}
, is the operator defined by

\Delta_{s}:=\nabla_{s}\cdot\nabla_{s}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}X_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}Y_{j}^{2}=\Delta_{x}+|x|^{2s}\Delta_{y},

where
\Delta_{x}
and
\Delta_{y}
are Laplace operators in the variables
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}
respectively. The anisotropic dilation attached to
\Delta_{s}
is given by

\delta_{\lambda}(\mathbf{z})=(\lambda x,\lambda^{1+s}y),\quad\lambda>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{z}:=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}.

It is easy to see that
\Delta_{s}
is the homogeneous of degree two with respect to the
dilation, i.e,

\Delta_{s}(\delta_{\lambda}u)=\lambda^{2}\delta_{\lambda}(\Delta_{s}u),

where
\delta_{\lambda}u(\mathbf{z})=u(\lambda x,\lambda^{1+s}y).
The change of variable formula for the Lebesgue
measure gives that

d\delta_{\lambda}(\mathbf{z})=\lambda^{N_{s}}dxdy,

where

N_{s}:=N_{1}+(1+s)N_{2},

is the homogeneous dimension with respect to dilation
\delta_{\lambda}
and
dxdy
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}.

In the following, we show some properties of the polynomials
L
and
H
, useful for our proofs. Let

\displaystyle L(z):=z^{4}-\frac{16p\theta(p-1)}{\theta-1}z^{2}-\frac{16p\theta(p-1)(p+\theta-2)}{(\theta-1)^{2}}z-\frac{16p\theta(p-1)^{2}}{(\theta-1)^{2}}.

(2.1)

2.2
Property of the polynomial
H

Lemma 2.1

L(2t_{0})<0
and

has a unique root
z_{0}
in the interval

(2t_{0},\infty)

Proof.
A simple computation leads to

\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{2t_{0}+1}=\sqrt{\gamma}\quad\mbox{with}\quad t_{0}^{+}=\sqrt{\gamma}+\sqrt{\gamma+\sqrt{\gamma}}\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma=\frac{p\theta(p-1)}{\theta-1}

Obviously

\displaystyle L(2t_{0})=

\displaystyle\;16t_{0}^{4}-16\gamma\left[4t_{0}^{2}+\frac{2(p+\theta-2)}{\theta-1}t_{0}+\frac{p-1}{\theta-1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;16\gamma(2t_{0}+1)^{2}-16\gamma\left[4t_{0}^{2}+\frac{2(p+\theta-2)}{\theta-1}t_{0}+\frac{p-1}{\theta-1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(z^{2}+\frac{p+\theta-2}{\theta-1}z+\frac{p-1}{\theta-1}\right)_{p}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\theta-1}(z+1)>0,

2.3
Main technical tools

In order to prove our results, we need some preliminary results for solutions to the system (
1.1
), as integral estimates, comparison property of
u
,
v
and an integral inequality derived from the stability.

The following is a comparison result between the components
u
,
v
of solutions to the system (
1.1
).

Lemma 2.2

(Comparison property.)
Let
p\geq\theta>1
and suppose that
\rho
satisfies
(\star)
. Let
(u,v)
be a solution of (
1.1
) and assume that
v
is bounded, then

v^{p-1}\leq\frac{p-1}{\theta-1}u^{\theta-1},

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle u\leq\|v\|_{\infty}^{\frac{p-\theta}{\theta-1}}v.

(2.3)

Proof.
The proof adapt an idea of
[
6
]
, originally coming from
[
23
]
. Let
\sigma=\frac{\theta-1}{p-1}\in(0,1],

\lambda=\sigma^{\frac{-1}{1-p}}
and
w=v-\lambda u^{\sigma}.
Since the simple calculation implies that

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}w=\Delta_{s}v-\lambda\sigma u^{\sigma-1}\Delta_{s}u-\lambda\sigma(\sigma-1)|\nabla_{s}u|^{2}u^{\sigma-2}

\displaystyle\geq\Delta_{s}v-\lambda\sigma u^{\sigma-1}\Delta_{s}u

\displaystyle=\rho(\mathbf{x})u^{\sigma-1}\left[u^{-\theta+1-\sigma}-\lambda\sigma v^{-p}\right]

\displaystyle=\rho(\mathbf{x})u^{\sigma-1}\left[u^{-\theta+1-\sigma}-\lambda^{p}v^{-p}\right]

\displaystyle=\rho(\mathbf{x})u^{\sigma-1}\left[-\lambda^{p}v^{-p}+u^{-p\sigma}\right]

\displaystyle=\rho(\mathbf{x})u^{\sigma-1}\left[-\frac{\lambda^{p}}{v^{p}}+\frac{1}{u^{p\sigma}}\right].

Therefore, for any
\sigma\in(0,1]
, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle C\frac{u^{\sigma-1}}{v^{p}u^{p\sigma}}\left[v^{p}-\left(\lambda u^{\sigma}\right)^{p}\right]\leq\rho(\mathbf{x})\frac{u^{\sigma-1}}{v^{p}u^{p\sigma}}\left[v^{p}-\left(\lambda u^{\sigma}\right)^{p}\right]\leq\Delta_{s}w.

(2.4)

We need to prove that

v\leq\lambda u^{\sigma}.

We shall show that

\displaystyle w\leq 0,

(2.5)

by a contradiction argument. Suppose that

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w>0.

(2.6)

Next, we split the proof into two cases.

Case 1:
We consider the case where the supremum of
w
is attained at infinity.

Choose now
\phi_{R}(x,y)=\psi^{m}(\frac{x}{R},\frac{y}{R^{1+s}}),
where
m>0,
and
\psi
is a cut-off function in
C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},[0,1]\right),
such that

\psi=1\quad\mbox{on}\quad B_{1}\times B_{1},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi=0\quad\mbox{outside }\quad B_{2}\times B_{2^{1+s}}.

A simple calculation implies that

\frac{|\nabla_{s}\phi_{R}|^{2}}{\phi_{R}}\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}\phi_{R}^{\frac{m-2}{m}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\Delta_{s}(\phi_{R})|\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}\phi_{R}^{\frac{m-2}{m}}.

Set

w_{R}=\phi_{R}w,

which is a compactly supported function. Then there exists
(x_{R},y_{R})\in B_{2R}\times B_{(2R)^{1+s}},
such that

\displaystyle w_{R}(x_{R},y_{R})=\max_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w_{R}(x,y)\rightarrow\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w(x,y)\quad\mbox{as }\;R\rightarrow\infty.

This implies

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}w_{R}(x_{R},y_{R})=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta_{s}w_{R}(x_{R},y_{R})\leq 0,

which means that at
(x_{R},y_{R})
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}w=-\phi_{R}^{-1}\nabla_{s}\phi_{R}w\quad\mbox{and}\quad\phi_{R}\Delta_{s}w\leq 2w\phi_{R}^{-1}|\nabla_{s}\phi_{R}|^{2}-w\Delta_{s}\phi_{R}.

(2.7)

From (
2.7
), and using the properties of
\phi_{R}
, we can conclude then

\displaystyle\phi_{R}\Delta_{s}w\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}\phi_{R}^{\frac{m-2}{2}}w.

(2.8)

Furthermore, for
w=v-\lambda u^{\sigma}\geq 0,
we observe that

\displaystyle\frac{v^{p}}{w^{p}}-\frac{(\lambda u^{\sigma})^{p}}{w^{p}}\geq 1,\quad\mbox{ or equivalently }\quad\lambda^{-p}v^{p}-u^{p\sigma}\geq\lambda^{-p}w^{p}.

(2.9)

\phi_{R},

\displaystyle u^{\sigma-1}w^{p}\phi_{R}^{\frac{m+2}{2}}\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}w\phi_{R}.

As

the sequence

is bounded. We choose

\displaystyle\theta=\frac{m+2}{m}\quad\mbox{so that }\;m=\frac{2}{\theta-1},

there holds then

\displaystyle w_{R}^{p-1}(x_{R},y_{R})\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}.

Taking the limit

which contradicts (
2.6
), the claim follows.

Case 2:
If there exists
(x^{0},y^{0}),
such that

However, the left-hand side of (

To prove (

As

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{s}w=\rho(\mathbf{x})\left(v^{-p}-\lambda u^{-\theta}\right)&=\rho(\mathbf{x})\left[-\lambda u^{-\theta}+\left(\frac{v}{\|v\|_{\infty}}\right)^{-p}\|v\|_{\infty}^{-p}\right]\\
&\geq\rho(\mathbf{x})\|v\|_{\infty}^{\theta-p}v^{-\theta}-\lambda u^{-\theta}\\
&\geq\rho(\mathbf{x})\|v\|_{\infty}^{\theta-p}\left(-\lambda^{\theta}u^{-\theta}+v^{-\theta}\right).\end{split}

(2.10)

For the rest of the proof we just replace (

Inspired by

Lemma 2.3

where

Proof.
Let
(u,v)
denote a stable solution of (
1.1
). By the definition of stability, there exist positive smooth functions

,
\psi
verifying

\displaystyle-\frac{\Delta_{s}\varphi}{\varphi}=p\rho(\mathbf{x})v^{-p-1}\frac{\psi}{\varphi},\quad-\frac{\Delta_{s}\psi}{\psi}=\theta\rho(\mathbf{x})u^{-\theta-1}\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\quad\mbox{in }\,\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Let
\gamma,\chi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\gamma^{2}
and the second by
\chi^{2}
and integrate
over
\mathbb{R}^{N},
we arrive at

\displaystyle p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho(\mathbf{x})v^{-p-1}\frac{\psi}{\varphi}\gamma^{2}dxdy=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\Delta_{s}\varphi}{\varphi}\gamma^{2}dxdy,

and

\displaystyle\theta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho(\mathbf{x})u^{-\theta-1}\frac{\varphi}{\psi}\chi^{2}dxdy=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\Delta_{s}\psi}{\psi}\chi^{2}dxdy.

The simple calculation implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(-\frac{\Delta_{s}\varphi}{\varphi}\gamma^{2}-|\nabla_{s}\gamma|^{2}\right)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\nabla_{s}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{s}(\gamma^{2}\varphi^{-1})-|\nabla_{s}\gamma|^{2}\Big)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(-\varphi^{-2}|\nabla_{s}\varphi|^{2}\gamma^{2}+2\varphi^{-1}\gamma\nabla_{s}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{s}\gamma-|\nabla_{s}\gamma|^{2}\Big)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}-\Big(\varphi^{-1}\gamma\nabla_{s}\varphi-\nabla_{s}\gamma\Big)^{2}dxdy\leq 0

Proceeding as above, we can easily show that

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\Delta_{s}\psi}{\psi}\chi^{2}dxdy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}\chi|^{2}dxdy.

Using the inequality

Taking
\phi=\chi=\gamma
and combining all these inequalities, we get readily the estimate (

NLRP3 inflammasome activation contributes to acute liver injury caused by CVA6 infection in mice
Authors: Yaqi Xie, Quanman Hu, Guangcai Duan, Fang Wang, Feifei Feng, Dong Li, Wenjie Jiang, Wangquan Ji, Peiyu Zhu, Xiaolong Zhang, Jinzhao Long, Huifen Feng, Haiyan Yang, Shuaiyin Chen, Yuefei Jin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCoxsackievirus (CV) A6 has emerged as an important causative agent in global outbreaks of hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD), which typically presents as a mild illness with a large generalized rash, herpes. However, some patients can develop encephalitis, pneumonia, myocarditis and liver injury. Our previous study took the view that CVA6 could replicate in mouse liver, leading to acute liver injury; however, the precise underlying mechanism remains elusive.Methods10-day-old wild-type (WT, C57BL/6J) and NLRP3 knock-out (KO) mice were intraperitoneal (i.p.) inoculated with a lethal dose of the CVA6 strain. The muscle homogenate supernatant from normal mice was used to inoculate mock-infected mice. At 5 days post infection (dpi), the mouse liver was taken out for histopathological analyses and molecular biology experiments.ResultsOur in vivo experiments demonstrated that CVA6 caused severe liver injury in mice, as evidenced by pathological changes in liver slices, elevated liver injury markers (e.g., AST, ALT, LDH) and pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, MCP-1, TNF-α, IL-1β). Further results revealed the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome characterized by the increase in the expression of NLRP3, Cleaved-Casp-1 (p20), mature IL-1β and IL-18. Importantly, upon CVA6 infection, NLRP3 KO mice exhibited attenuated pathological damage and reduced levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines production (e.g., TNF-α and IL-1β) compared with WT mice. Finally, increased levels of blood ALT, AST, LDH were strongly correlated with the severity of CVA6 patients.ConclusionCollectively, our findings suggest that the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome is involved in CVA6 infection-induced acute liver injury, providing novel insights into CVA6 infection associated adverse clinical outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-024-10136-2.

Introduction

Hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) is a childhood infectious disease caused by enteroviruses (EVs). Prior to the introduction of the EV-A71 vaccine, EV-A71 and Coxsackievirus (CV) A16 were recognized as the predominant pathogens [1, 2]. However, there has been a shift in the pathogen spectrum in recent years. Nowadays, CVA6 has become an important causative agent of HFMD in China [3, 4]; Spain [5]; Philippines [6], Vietnam [7]; America [8]; and Brazil [9]. Notably, CVA6 is highly contagious and can easily lead to HFMD outbreak. Furthermore, the clinical symptoms of CVA6 infection are atypical, making it highly prone to misdiagnosis. This presents a significant public health concern that jeopardizes the well-being of children worldwide [10, 11].

In general, infections caused by CVA6 typically manifest with symptoms such as fever, mouth ulcers, onychomycosis, and an atypical rash that spreads throughout the body [12]. A retrospective study reveals that CVA6 infection results in liver injury in some patients. Among the 68 patients, 27 exhibited elevated levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT) or aspartate aminotransferase (AST), including one patient with an AST as high as 82.9 U/L. The study identified a statistically significant difference between the proportion of infants and young children with liver function abnormalities compared to preschoolers [13]. Additionally, Yang et al. found that in severe cases of HFMD caused by CVA6, the levels of serum AST were significantly elevated [14]. Likewise, our previous work reported that 6.42% of severe cases with HFMD had varying degrees of liver damage, whereas only 0.47% of mild cases had liver injury [15]. Above evidences indicate that CVA6 infection may lead to acute liver injury, particularly in severe patients. Therefore, it is particularly important to provide prophylactic treatment for HFMD patients with acute liver injury in order to reduce the incidence of acute liver failure and promote hepatocyte proliferation. This will contribute significantly to the overall management of the disease and its potential complications.

Liver injury is characterized by an abnormal structural or functional change in hepatic tissue, encompassing structural damage, liver function abnormalities, inflammatory responses, and immune reactions. Hepatic impairment can lead to multi-organ dysfunction syndrome and dysregulation of the inflammatory response. Liver dysfunction often plays a pivotal role in these events [16]. Viral infections, including hepatitis B virus (HBV), hepatitis C virus (HCV), hepatitis D virus (HDV), and COVID-19, are prominent etiological factors contributing to liver injury [17–20]. In our previously established mouse model of CVA6 infection, we observed abundant replication of CVA6 in the liver resulting in acute liver injury [21], however, the precise mechanism remains elusive. In this study, we have innovatively discovered that the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome contributes to acute liver injury induced by CVA6 infection. This provides novel insights into adverse clinical outcomes associated with CVA6 infection.

Materials and methods

Study participants

This study included children with HFMD who were hospitalized in Henan Children’s Hospital and the Fifth Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University from April to September 2018. The diagnosis of HFMD was based on the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Hand, Foot, and Mouth Disease (2018 edition) introduced by the National Health Commission of the People’s Republic of China. A total of 155 mild and 155 severe HFMD patients were collected in this study. Among them, severe cases exhibited neurological symptoms (e.g., poor mental performance, drowsiness, weakness in sucking, easily startled, headache, vomiting, irritability, limb tremors, muscle weakness, neck stiffness) or cardiopulmonary symptoms (e.g., dyspnea, palpitations, tachycardia, cough, and pink or bloody foamy sputum). Other patients were categorized as mild cases. The demographic information and clinical laboratory results of the participants are presented in supplemental Table S1.

Viruses and cell

As described previously [21], the CVA6 strain (GenBank Accession number: OM179765) used in this study was isolated from fecal sample of an HFMD case, and virus titer was determined by the Reed and Muench method was 105 TCID50/mL. Another CVA6 strain (GenBank Accession number: OR500231.1), EV-A71 strain (ZZ1350) [22] and CVA10 strain were also used in this study.

Human HepG2 hepatoma cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific) with 10% fetal bovine serum (Cegrogen), and the incubator conditions are set at 37 °C and 5% CO2.

Animal infection experiments

Specific pathogen free (SPF) C57BL/6 mice were purchased by the Zhengzhou University Laboratory Animal Center. NLRP3 knockout (KO) mice were kindly offered by Professor Feifei Feng (Zhengzhou University). The mice were housed in a laboratory setting with a 12-hour light/dark cycle and unrestricted access to food and drink.

In this study, 10-day-old wild-type (WT, C57BL/6J) and NLRP3 KO mice were intraperitoneal (i.p.) inoculated with a lethal dose of the CVA6 strain (105 TCID50 /mouse), and each group included 10∼15 mice. The muscle homogenate supernatant from normal mice was used to inoculate mock-infected mice. At 3 or 5 days post infection (dpi), the mouse liver was removed for histopathological analyses and molecular biology experiments.

RNA extraction and quantitative RT-PCR (qRT-PCR)

The copy numbers of CVA6 working stocks were determined by qRT-PCR using in vitro-transcribed RNA standards. RNA standards (101-108 copies) were transcribed from the CV-A6 VP1 gene cloned into a plasmid pET-28a (+) easy vector. Total RNA was extracted from mouse liver using RNAiso Plus (TaKaRa Co., Ltd., China). The NanoDrop ND-2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific Co., Ltd., USA) was used to measure the amount and purity of the total RNA, and then RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using a relevant kit (Yeasen BioTechnologies Co., Ltd., China). The transcription levels of CVA6 VP1 were performed using the Quantagene q225 (Kubo Technology Co., Ltd., China). The forward primer VP1 was 5’- CAAGCTGCAGAAACGGGAG and the reverse was 5’-GCTCCACACTCGCCTCATT.

Western blot analysis

Total protein from mouse liver was extracted by Radio-Immunoprecipitation Assay (RIPA) buffer. The concentration of total protein was quantified with a BCA protein assay kit (Biomed Co., Ltd., China), and then SDS-PAGE (Jingcai Biotech Co., Ltd., China) was used to separate the samples and proteins were transferred into PVDF membrane. Detail protocols were described in our previous study [15]. In addition, all the immunoblots were cut prior to hybridisation with antibodies during blotting. The following primary antibodies were used for Western blotting: anti-NLRP3, anti-IL-18, anti-β-actin (Proteintech Co., Ltd., China); anti-Caspase-1, anti-IL-1β, anti-Caspase 3 and anti-Cleaved-Caspase 3 (CST Co., Ltd., USA), anti-Cleaved-Casp1 (p20) (Affinity Biosciences Co., Ltd., USA); anti-CVA6 VP1 (GeneTex Co., Ltd, USA) anti-mouse immunoglobulin G (IgG) and anti-rabbit IgG (Proteintech Co., Ltd., China).

ELISA detection

Ten-day-old WT and NLRP3 KO mice were i.p. inoculated with a lethal dose of the CVA6 strain (105 TCID50 /mouse), and livers were collected from WT mice and NLRP3 KO mice. Livers were rapidly lysed by adding pre-cooled RIPA buffer and total proteins were determined as mentioned earlier. Subsequently, IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β and MCP-1 concentrations were measured using ELISA kits (Servicebio Biotech Co., ltd, Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Histopathological examination and immunofluorescence staining

At 5 dpi, liver tissues were excised, rinsed in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde at room temperature overnight. The tissues were then sectioned into serial 5 μm thick sections and subjected to hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining as well as sirius red staining. Technical services were provided by Wuhan Servicebio Biotech Co., Ltd. The histology score and quantification of sirius red-positive area of liver slice were evaluated as reported in a previous publication, which was quantified by a person blinded to the treatment groups [23, 24].

HepG2 cells were inoculated in a 6-well plate and then infected with an MOI of 1 for 48 h. Subsequently, an immunofluorescence assay was conducted. The immunofluorescence staining and scanning of cell crawls were performed by Wuhan Servicebio Biotech Co., Ltd.

Examination of liver function index

The examination of AST, ALT and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) in peripheral blood of mice was conducted by Servicebio Biotech Co., ltd (Wuhan, China).

Statistical analyses

In this study, the Grubbs test was used to detect outliers, with a detection level of α = 0.05 set. Outliers were removed, and the remaining data underwent the above steps until no outliers were detected. Variables that conform to the normal distribution are expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test is used for normality test, P > 0.05 indicates that the data satisfy the normality test, otherwise they are expressed as the median and interquartile range, and the count data is expressed as frequency (%). The Mann-Whitney U test or Student’s t-test was used to compare any two data sets, and the Kruskal-Wallis test or one-way ANOVA were used for comparisons of more than two groups. The differences in the survival rates were assessed with the Mantel-Cox log rank test, and survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method. A P value less than 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant in the study.

Results

CVA6 infection leads to acute liver injury in mice

Based on our previously established mouse model, we i.p. infected 10-day-old C57BL/6J mice with a dose of 105 TCID50 CVA6, while mock-infected mice were i.p. inoculated with an equivalent volume of muscle homogenate supernatant obtained from normal mice. As shown in Fig. 1A, H&E staining revealed the presence of hepatitis in CVA6-infected mice, characterized by disrupted cellular architecture and aberrant morphology within the designated region accompanied by infiltration of inflammatory cells. Sirius Red staining showed collagen fiber deposition and disruption of hepatocyte arrangement in CVA6-infected mice. Similar pathological damage was also found after infection with another strain of CVA6 (GenBank: OR500231.1), but no remarkable liver damage was observed after infection with EV-A71 and CVA10 (Fig. S1). Furthermore, in our previous study, immunohistochemistry staining results revealed that CVA6 VP1 expression in the liver of CVA6 infected mice, and qPCR analysis demonstrated an increase in the copy number of CVA6 VP1 with increased infection time [21].

According to above findings, we detected the liver function markers such as blood ALT, AST and LDH, and found that these indicators were significantly increased in CVA6-infected mice (Fig. 1B-D). Additionally, the concentrations of IL-6 (Fig. 1E), MCP-1 (Fig. 1F), TNF-α (Fig. 1G), and IL-1β (Fig. 1H) in liver tissues from CVA6-infected mice exhibited a significant increase compared to mock-infected mice. Collectively, our results demonstrated that CVA6 infection leads to acute liver inflammation and injury.

The activation of NLRP3 inflammasome in acute liver injury caused by CVA6

Considering that IL-1β is an inflammatory cytokine produced by the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, we subsequently assessed the expression levels of inflammasome-associated proteins. Figure 2A illustrated the immunoblot bands corresponding to NLRP3, pro-Caspase-1, Cleaved-Casp1 (p20), and mature IL-1β and IL-18. Through quantitative analysis using Image J, we observed a significant increase in the protein levels of NLRP3, pro-Caspase-1, Cleaved-Casp1 (p20), mature IL-1β and IL-18 at the indicated time point (Fig. 2B-F). At the same time, we infected HepG2 cells with CVA6 and observed that the NLRP3 inflammasome were activated after CVA6 infection, leading to an increase in the expression of inflammasome-associated proteins (Fig. S2). Taken together, our data suggest that CVA6 infection induces an inflammatory response by activating NLRP3 inflammasome, indicating that the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome may be associated with CVA6 infection-induced acute liver injury.

NLRP3 inflammasome activation is involved in acute liver injury caused by CVA6 infection

Next, NLRP3 KO mice were infected with CVA6 and were compared to WT mice infected with CVA6. Immunoblot bands of NLRP3, pro-Caspase-1, Cleaved-Caspase1 (p20), mature IL-1β and IL-18 were shown in Fig. 3A. Quantitative results (Fig. 3B and E) indicated that NLRP3 deletion inhibited CVA6-induced activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, and subsequently reduced the productions of mature IL-1β and IL-18. We then investigated whether inhibiting NLRP3 provided a survival benefit by infecting WT and NLRP3 KO mice with CVA6; however, no statistical difference was detected between their survival rates (Fig. S3). The histopathological analysis (Fig. 4A) revealed lower levels of liver injury, as indicated by the pathology score (Fig. 4B) and area of collagen deposition (Fig. 4C) in NLRP3 KO mice after CVA6 infection compared to WT mice. Meanwhile, there were no statistically significant changes in AST, ALT and LDH levels between WT and NLRP3 KO mice infected with CVA6 (Fig. 4D-F). Compared with CVA6 infected WT mice, the viral loads in NLRP3 KO mice infected with CVA6 significantly decreased (Fig. 4G). Together, our results highlight that the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome facilitates CVA6-induced acute liver injury.

NLRP3 inflammasome activation facilitates CVA6-induced acute liver injury through upregulating pro-inflammatory cytokines

As previously mentioned, CVA6 infection induced pro-inflammatory cytokines in the liver. To investigate whether the involvement of NLRP3 inflammasome activation in acute liver injury through the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, we subsequently evaluated the hepatic protein levels of these cytokines. The results presented in Fig. 5 demonstrated that NLRP3 KO mice exhibited significantly reduced protein levels of TNF-α compared to WT mice and IL-1β was also reduced to some extent; however, no significant alterations were observed for IL-6 and MCP-1. These findings suggest a potential role for NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pro-inflammatory cytokines in facilitating CVA6-induced acute liver injury.

Clinical study of liver injury in patients with HFMD

To ascertain the occurrence of liver injury in clinically severe cases of HFMD, we collected laboratory indicators from both mild and severe cases. As shown in Fig. 6, our findings revealed statistically significant differences in liver function indexes between patients with severe HFMD and those with mild HFMD, including ALT, AST, and LDH (Fig. 6A-C). Although ALP and gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT) exhibited no disparity, their measured values were still higher in severe patients compared to mild ones (Fig. 6D-E). The basic characteristics and laboratory indicators of HFMD patients were presented in Table S1. Collectively, our data suggest the occurrence of acute liver injury in patients with severe HFMD.

Discussion

With the widespread utilization of the EV-A71 vaccine, there has been a shift in the pathogen spectrum associated with HFMD. A meta-analysis study revealed a worldwide seropositivity rate of 45.9% (95%CI: 37.6–54.1%) for EV-A71 antibody and 58.3% (95%CI: 46.5–70.2%) for CVA6 antibody, indicating that CVA6 has progressively emerged as the predominant etiological agent of HFMD in recent years [25, 26]. In this study, clinical data demonstrated significantly elevated levels of liver function indicators such as ALT, AST, and LDH in severe HFMD cases compared to mild cases, suggesting acute liver injury in patients with severe HFMD.

Acute liver injury is a syndrome characterized by the loss of functional hepatocytes in a patient with no evidence of pre-existing liver disease. Various etiologies have been identified, including toxic, infectious, neoplastic, and drug-induced factors [27]. Viral infections are known to induce acute liver injury through direct cell damage mechanisms or immune-mediated hepatic inflammation processes related to chronic viral infection or replication cycle-associated factors. During the COVID-19 pandemic, it was observed that abnormal liver function occurred in up to 53% of patients, attributed to cytopathic effects or uncontrolled immune responses [28]. HBV replicates through RNA intermediates and has a unique ability to integrate into the host genome, resulting in persistent infection within infected cells. This leads to cellular and non-cellular degeneration associated with chronic hepatitis [29, 30]. Among EVs, coxsackievirus B (CVB) has been shown to cause acute systemic damage, including severe lung/liver injury, as well as death in newborn mice [31]. Similar to CVB, our previous studies also observed systemic damage such as severe brain/lung/heart injury in CVA-infected mice [32–35]. In this study, we first reported that CVA6 infection could result in acute liver injury in mice, which may be an important factor contributing to adverse outcomes in patients with severe HFMD. Therefore, in the treatment of CVA6 infections, it will be beneficial to give certain liver protection treatment.

The NLRP3 inflammasome, a crucial pathogen sensor, facilitates the activation of Caspase-1 and secretion of the pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β/IL-18 [36–38]. Our previous study demonstrated that CVA6 could replicate in mouse liver tissues, which might trigger NLRP3 inflammasome activation. Several publications have documented that NLRP3 inflammasome can recognize EV-A71 replication in myeloid cells and mediate IL-1β secretion [39–41]. Aberrant activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome plays a pivotal role in diverse forms of acute liver injury, as it triggers multiple types of programmed cell death (PCD), thereby reciprocally modulating NLRP3 inflammasome activity [42]. In autoimmune hepatitis (AIH), pathologically increased levels of NLRP3, cleaved Caspase-1, and IL-1β are crucial in the pathogenesis of Concanavalin A (ConA)-induced hepatitis [43]. In this study, CVA6 infection triggered liver-specific NLRP3 inflammasome activation and promoted transcription of IL-1β and IL-18. Eventually, the activated inflammasomes leads to a severe inflammatory response characterized by elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g. IL-6, TNF-α, and MCP-1). These findings collectively suggest the inflammatory state associated with NLRP3 inflammasome activation in the liver of mice.

To investigate whether NLRP3 inflammasome activation is essential for CVA6-induced acute liver injury, we employed NLRP3 KO mice to carry out the next stage of experiments. In this study, CVA6-infected NLRP3 KO mice still exhibited weight loss, decreased mobility, and symptoms similar to those observed in WT mice. However, it is noteworthy that total mortality time was prolonged in NLRP3 KO mice due to reduced liver inflammatory exudation and collagen fiber deposition, accompanied unchanged AST, ALT and LHD indexes and significantly decreased TNF-α and some extent decreased IL-1βlevels. This finding is consistent with other studies, Mridha et al. demonstrated that inhibition of NLRP3 using a small molecule inhibitor effectively attenuated liver inflammation and fibrosis in an experimental model of non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) in mice by specifically blocking NLRP3 activation. This normalizes the expression levels of Caspase-1 and IL-1β as well as reducing plasma levels of IL-1β, MCP-1, and IL-6 [44]. Additionally, myeloid-specific deletion of NLRP3 attenuates systemic and liver elevation of IL-1β induced by lipopolysaccharide/adenosine triphosphate injection in non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), while myeloid-specific NLRP3 KO mice show a significant reduction in inflammation compared to hepatocellular or ASC KO mice [45]. Furthermore, IL-1β exhibits a synergistic role in liver inflammation [46]. Intracellular Toll/IL 1 receptor domains have been demonstrated to contribute to host defense and inflammation. Further investigation is required to explore the impact of pro-inflammatory cytokines on CVA6-induced acute liver injury.

There are various possible explanations for why there was not a significant decrease in liver function indices after NLRP3 knockdown. When it comes to treating liver inflammation, the primary approach involves anti-inflammatory and hepatoprotective therapy. Studies have shown that certain medications can create a favorable internal environment for antiviral therapy while also exerting an anti-inflammatory effect by regulating inflammatory factors and immune responses [47]. However, in cases of drug-induced liver disease, elevated ALT levels may persist for a significant duration even after discontinuation of hepatotoxic drugs, along with persistent bilirubin abnormalities [48]. It’s worth noting that this study observed a reduction in CVA6 replication within the mouse liver through NLRP3 KO intervention, but complete clearance was not achieved. This partial clearance could potentially explain why immediate reductions in ALT, AST, and LDH levels were not observed. It is important to emphasize that recovery from liver damage is a chronic process requiring prolonged monitoring of hepatic function indicators in mice for further research.

However, this study has certain limitations: (1) We did not compare the potential hepatotoxicity of other CVA6 subtypes infection, focusing solely on the prevalent D3 subtype. (2) This study primarily focused on liver injury and overlooked the role of NLRP3 inflammasome activation in systemic response triggered by CVA6 infection; thus, further experiments are warranted to elucidate the underlying mechanism. In summary, our findings indicate that the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome contributes to acute liver injury caused by CVA6 infection in mice, providing novel insights into the pathogenesis of HFMD severity.

Accurate prediction of geometrical parameters of an ultra-broadband metamaterial absorber using machine learning
Authors: Md Rezwan Ahmed, Oishi Jyoti, Pritu P Sarkar, Mohammod Abdul Motin, Md Selim Habib, Md Samiul Habib
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper, we systematically demonstrate the design and analysis of a new type of ultra-broadband tunable metamaterial perfect absorber (MPA) comprising a top vanadium dioxide (VO2) based patterned resonating patch, a continuous metallic film at the bottom, and an intermediate dielectric substrate having a thickness of only 0.18 at the center working frequency. The simulation results reveal that the absorber achieves a bandwidth of 7.26 THz, ranging from 5.40 THz to 12.66 THz, with more than 90% absorptance and an average absorption of 98.21% under normal incidence of the incoming THz wave. Furthermore, absorptance exceeding 99% is achieved between 6.25 THz and 11.30 THz (5.05 THz bandwidth), demonstrating superior performance compared to existing broadband absorbers. The high absorption efficiency is attributed to the electric and magnetic dipole resonance, as illustrated through the electric field and vector surface current distribution at different frequencies. An equivalent RLC circuit model is developed using the least squares method, showing strong agreement with full-wave numerical simulations. However, designing metamaterial absorber requires extensive analysis of absorption spectra across a broad range of structural parameters – a computationally expensive process due to the complex interplay of impedance matching and electric field coupling. To overcome this challenge, we introduce a machine learning (ML)-based approach utilizing the Random Forest (RF) algorithm to predict absorption bandwidth and optimize structural parameters, significantly reducing computational time and spectral analyses. The RF model achieves considerably high accuracy, predicting an ultra-broadband absorption bandwidth of 7.26 THz with minimal error. We show that predicted and simulated results show excellent agreement, with negligible deviations. In addition, the terahertz absorber stably maintains more than 90% absorptance for both transverse electric (TE) and transverse magnetic (TM) waves up to 50° and due to its rotationally symmetric structure the proposed absorber is easy to fabricate and ensures complete polarization insensitivity. With its strong performance, the proposed MPA offers considerable potential for applications in terahertz modulation, switching, imaging, and biochemical sensing.Subject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

Terahertz (THz) waves, operating between microwaves and infrared in the electromagnetic spectrum, offer unprecedented features such as deep penetration, low photon energy, and ultra-fast dynamics, which characterize it as an indispensable field of research1. In recent decades, THz technology has been widely used in many areas of science and technology, particularly in THz imaging2, security3, wireless communication4, sensors5, and modulators6. However, the weak interaction of natural materials with THz waves has hindered progress, leading to the rise of artificially engineered metamaterial structures with subwavelength unit cells that enable precise control of the THz wave7. Metamaterials offer unusual optical properties, determined by structural design rather than chemical composition, making them ideal for a range of functional devices, such as electromagnetic absorbers, filters, polarization converters, and switches8. Among these, THz metamaterial absorbers are particularly notable for their ultrathin profile, compact size, and near-perfect absorption with minimal reflection or transmission9. These features make them ideal candidates for photovoltaics, energy harvesting, and stealth technology, generating significant research interest in advanced THz devices8. Most existing metamaterial absorbers comprise of a three-layer: a top resonating patch and a bottom metal film on a dielectric substrate. Based on the requirements of diverse application prospects, metamaterial absorbers have been classified into narrow-band10, multi-band11 or broad-band12, offering application oriented features such as wide-angle absorption and polarization insensitivity. The first absorber, experimentally demonstrated in 2008, achieved near-perfect absorption at 11.5 GHz, enabling compact, lightweight designs with an ultra-thin substrate of thickness 0.04. Narrow-band absorption occurs via localized plasmon resonance, where electromagnetic waves induce resonant vibrations, rapidly dissipating energy, while multi-resonator or multi-layer structures enable broad-band absorption across the electromagnetic spectrum10,12. For example, a VO2-based absorber is designed and numerically investigated at THz frequencies, achieving over 90% absorption in the frequency range of 1.63 THz to 12.39 THz with dynamically tunable absorption from 4.4% to 100% and a relative absorption bandwidth of 153.5%13. However, its multilayer design complicates the manufacturing process due to alignment challenges, increasing cost, and reducing reliability. Despite these limitations, multi-band absorbers find potential applications in sensors, satellite communications, antennas, and solar cells12. Similarly, an actively controlled dual broadband VO2-based absorber is demonstrated with more than 80% absorption in two bands (0.88 THz and 0.77 THz). But lack of symmetry, polarization sensitive behavior is observed which restricts performance11.

Recently, researchers are focused in reconfigurable broadband absorbers, and with this in mind a VO2-based absorber with three concentric rings is proposed, achieving a 3.3 THz absorption bandwidth with a relative absorption bandwidth of 82.71%, and tunable peak absorption ranging from 4% to 100%14. The rotationally symmetric behavior of the proposed structure provides a good robustness to the variation of polarization angle, however, the edge of a circular shape is quite difficult to fabricate, and large manufacturing error may deteriorate its performance. More recently, a metamaterial based broadband absorber is demonstrated using four identical L-shaped resonators, achieving a 90% absorption rate (7.26 THz bandwidth) and tunability from 0.5% to 97.6%15. The most interesting features include shape-independent absorption, making it less sensitive to manufacturing errors. The study also introduced previously unreported two important parameters such as modulation depth and extinction ratio. Since most existing reports are based on computer simulations only, their absorption performance may degrade considering the limitations of real time applications16–21.

Therefore, researchers are looking for not only broadband absorption but also near-perfect absorption to ensure practical reliability. In 2023, a broadband absorber is designed with four diagonal pseudo-half ellipses, achieving 5.5 THz absorption bandwidth, including 2.8 THz bandwidth with more than 99% absorption16. Similarly, a polarization insensitive MA is demonstrated for K-band sensing application, supported by an RLC circuit model22. However, a key limitation is the fixed absorption performance, which cannot be adjusted once the structure has been fabricated22. To further broaden the applications of metamaterial absorbers, reconfigurable designs have been explored using tunable materials such as graphene23, liquid crystals24, semiconductors25, and phase-change materials16,18. Among them, vanadium dioxide (VO2) is particularly notable for its rapid transition between insulating and metallic phases at 340 K, driven by temperature, light, or even voltage26.

Building on the dynamic tunability of materials like VO2, recent years have witnessed the integration of machine learning (ML) into the field of metamaterials and absorber design, opening new frontiers in predictive modeling and optimization27. Particularly, graphene-based absorbers have gained attention due to their tunable properties, where ML plays a vital role in accelerating design processes and improving performance prediction accuracy. Many studies recently have explored the field of ML in parameter prediction or inverse design28,29. ML was utilized to predict absorption across broadband spectra, enabling efficient solar absorber design with complex geometries30. ML techniques were applied to predict and optimize multiband absorption performance, reducing reliance on computationally expensive simulations31–34. In32, the capability of ML was highlighted to handle high-dimensional design spaces for tailoring terahertz absorbers with multiple resonant peaks. Neural networks were employed for inverse design, enabling rapid identification of structural parameters to achieve desired absorption targets in graphene-based systems28,29.

While existing absorber designs demonstrate impressive performance, they often fail to meet the practical application requirements. To address this, a simpler yet highly effective design is essential, ensuring near-perfect absorption across the entire frequency band of interest with minimal fabrication errors. Additionally, key desirable features include a wide tunable range, broad-angle absorption, and robustness to varying polarizations of incident electromagnetic waves. Keeping these goals in mind, we have introduced a new type of absorber that provides a broadband absorption of 7.26 THz, spanning from 5.40 THz to 12.66 THz with an average absorption of 98.21%. More significantly, our proposed absorber achieves a near-perfect absorption (more than 99% absorptance) in a bandwidth of 5.05 THz that is far better than the previously reported absorbers. Besides, VO2 is strategically selected as the top metasurface due to its unique thermal phase transition properties. This enables continuous absorption tuning from 0.98% to near-perfect absorption (100%) through conductivity modulation (200 S/m to S/m), while maintaining structural integrity. Moreover, VO2 exhibits a reversible insulator-to-metal transition at 68 °C, enabling more than 80% modulation depth in THz absorption along with broadband response due to intrinsic Drude-like conductivity change35. Since our proposed absorber inherits a classical three-layered structure having a top resonating patch with rectangular sharp edges, it is fabrication friendly and robust against major manufacturing tolerance. In addition, its rotationally symmetric structure makes it insensitive to polarization and ensures satisfactory absorption performance (more than 90% absorptance) for up to 50° of incident angle for both TE and TM polarization. The maximum modulation depth of the structure exceeds 98.5% which clarifies its potentiality in terahertz modulators and the minimum value of extinction ratio reaches −36.19 dB. Furthermore, we have also investigated the absorption performance of the proposed structure using COMSOL and its equivalent RLC circuit model. The absorption spectra obtained from CST, COMSOL, and ADS show excellent agreement in bandwidth and flat absorption characteristics. We also employed machine learning to predict the design parameters of the proposed absorber based on target absorptance and bandwidth. This approach enables efficient determination of optimal parameters, eliminating the need for time-consuming trial-and-error methods. These results highlight the effectiveness and potential of the proposed model for real-world applications.

Design and simulation

The unit cell of the proposed ultra-wideband absorber is shown in Fig. 1(a), which inherits a classical three-layer structure and a step-by-step design procedure of the top resonating patch is shown in Fig. 1(b). The top layer is made up of VO2 based special shaped resonating patch, and middle dielectric layer employs MF2, a non-dispersive medium with a relative permittivity of 1.936. For the bottom layer, gold is employed with a conductivity of 4.09107 S/m37. Gold is selected due to its superior THz-range conductivity, low free-electron scattering, and minimal Ohmic losses, which collectively enhance the metasurface’s efficiency over other metals. Furthermore, its superior chemical stability and oxidation resistance ensure long-term structural integrity and reliable performance38. In our current research, the structural parameters have been optimized focusing versatile application prospects i.e., ensuring absorption > 90% throughout the entire THz regime, and the other is to enhance the absorption bandwidth prioritizing near-unity absorption. From Fig. 6(a) it can be seen that reducing the thickness of VO2 from 0.2 m to 0.05 m induces a gradual red shift in the second absorption peak, while the first peak and central absorption frequency (9.03 THz) remain stable. This adjustment leads to a narrower bandwidth but significantly improves absorption efficiency. The underlying mechanism can be attributed to the metallic phase behavior of VO2- increasing its thickness improves reflectivity, thus reducing absorption. Further reduction in reduces both the absorption bandwidth and intensity due to the mismatch of absorber’s impedance with free space. Thus, selecting 0.05 m as the optimal VO2 thickness ensures a balanced trade-off between ultra-broadband operation and near-perfect absorption, which is the primary objective of this study.

The absorption spectrum of the proposed absorber depends on the dielectric layer thickness, as illustrated in Fig. 6(b). For a dielectric thickness of = 6 m, the absorber exhibits a center frequency of 9.03 THz, corresponding to a free-space wavelength of 33.22 m. Since the refractive index of the dielectric layer is , the wavelength within the dielectric reduces to 24.08 m. The absorption mechanism can be explained by the interference cancellation principle: when the dielectric thickness (6 m) is approximately one-fourth of the dielectric wavelength (24.08 m), it satisfies the condition for destructive interference between reflected and incident waves14. As a result, the absorber achieves strong absorption at this specific thickness. Figure 6(c) demonstrates that reducing the gap width (g) from 2 m to 1 m enhances both the absorption bandwidth and peak due to strong coupling between adjacent unit cells. However, a further reduction of g slightly widens the bandwidth while drastically lowering the absorption rate at the center frequency, making g = 1 m the optimal value. Figure 6(d) reveals that decreasing the width w from 2.5 m to 1 m slightly broadens the absorption bandwidth while increasing the maximum absorptance towards unity. Further reduction beyond 1 m yields marginal bandwidth improvement but sacrifices the flat near-unity absorption, thus w = 1 m is chosen as the optimum value. This broadband, high-absorption performance arises from impedance matching across the target frequency range, enabled by the unique design of the VO2 resonating patch. From Fig. 2(a), these peaks correspond to near-perfect absorptances of 99.98% and 99.96%, respectively. At 6.85 THz, the horizontal and vertical components of the electric field exhibit similar distributions but with a 90° angular shift, as shown in Fig. 10(a) and (c). The horizontal component is primarily concentrated along the horizontal segment of the resonating patch, with strong absorption occurring at both the inner and outer regions along the x-direction, supporting the 99.98% absorption. In contrast, at 10.45 THz, the electric field distribution is dominated by the vertical component. The horizontal field remains localized along the horizontal part of the patch, accompanied by weak, uniform absorption at the inner and outer regions, as shown in Fig. 10(b). Meanwhile, the vertical component shows intense distribution along the entire outer boundary of the resonating patch, with strong absorption inside and outside the structure, which is illustrated in Fig. 10(d). This distinct behavior of the horizontal and vertical field components ensures the high absorption efficiency of 99.96%.

The mechanism underlying the near-perfect absorption was investigated through simulations of the surface current distributions at a peak absorption frequency of 6.85 THz. The distributions of the surface current on the top metasurface and bottom ground plane at 6.85 THz is presented in Fig. 11. The simulations identify a pronounced antiparallel alignment of currents in the metasurface and the ground plane. This specific mode profile generates a circulating current loop, corresponding to the excitation of a magnetic dipole moment and the induction of a magnetic resonance43. The critical role of this resonance is twofold: it first enables near-perfect impedance matching to free space, and subsequently promotes the efficient dissipation of the coupled radiation, as directly confirmed by the 99.98% absorption peak in Fig. 2(a).

ML-based parameter prediction

Designing a broadband absorber system, particularly one aimed at achieving high absorption across a wide frequency range, involves adjusting various parameters to meet the desired performance goals. Parameters such as p, , g, and w are critical in defining the efficiency of the absorber system. These parameters control how the absorber interacts with incoming waves, affecting its bandwidth and absorptance capabilities. One of the primary challenges in designing an absorber system is finding the optimal combination of parameters. Achieving high bandwidth and high absorption simultaneously is a complex task, as these two attributes often conflict with each other.

Traditionally, optimizing these parameters involves a laborious and time-consuming trial-and-error approach. Designers must manually adjust each parameter and test the resulting performance, which can be inefficient and computationally expensive. This process may involve running numerous simulations, performing physical experiments, and constantly iterating until the desired performance is achieved.

On the other hand, machine learning (ML) techniques are capable of learning from data and making predictions or decisions without being explicitly programmed. ML can offer a more efficient solution to this problem by providing predictive models that can estimate the effect of each parameter on the absorber’s performance. By training models on a dataset of known absorber configurations and their corresponding performance metrics (such as bandwidth and absorptance), ML algorithms can learn the underlying relationships between the input parameters and the desired performance outcomes. By applying a machine learning–based parameter prediction method (MLPPM), the designers expanded the unit cell’s operational bandwidth to span 2.30–4.00 THz—achieving a 54% fractional bandwidth and representing a 31.7% performance improvement over manually optimized designs52. Comparative analyses of ML techniques have shown their effectiveness in predicting microwave absorption performance with high accuracy53. Beyond performance prediction, ML has been integrated with terahertz metamaterial absorbers for applications such as skin cancer detection54 and in optimizing graphene-based metasurface biosensors for hemoglobin detection55. These approaches highlight the ability of ML to accelerate absorber design and enable biomedical sensing. Meanwhile, advances in broadband and ultrathin metamaterial absorbers across the X- and Ku-band frequencies56–58 underscore the importance of combining ML-driven parameter tuning with innovative material engineering to achieve high efficiency in compact structures. By employing a well-trained machine learning model, the absorber’s design can be efficiently tuned, reducing the number of iterations needed and potentially saving time and resources in the design process. Thus, the problem of adjusting parameters in an absorber system is transformed from a time-consuming, error-prone process into an automated, data-driven approach that enhances design efficiency and predictability.

Dataset

Table 6 represents the dataset used for training the model, which is derived from the simulation of the absorber system shown in Fig. 1. The dataset consists of seven (7) columns, where the columns p, , g, and w are the target variables. These correspond to the period of the unit cell (denoted by p), the thickness of (denoted by ), the gap of layer from the boundary of the unit cell (denoted by g), and the length of one side of the small square-shaped (denoted by w). These parameters are predicted by the model. The remaining three columns—Bandwidth, Min Point between two adjacent peaks, and Max Peak Point—act as the feature columns for the model, representing key performance metrics related to the absorber. The 1410 data points for training the ML models were generated via full-wave electromagnetic simulations performed in the commercial software CST. All simulations were executed under consistent, controlled conditions, with the geometrical parameters systematically varied across predefined ranges. The dataset of 1410 samples were selected to balance model training effectiveness with computational feasibility. Although a larger dataset could offer more comprehensive coverage of the design space, the current size is sufficient for the model to accurately capture the key relationships between the geometrical parameters and electromagnetic responses. The primary limitation for dataset size is the high computational cost of generating each data point via full-wave electromagnetic simulation, which requires substantial processing power and time. While we acknowledge that a larger dataset would enhance model robustness, expanding it remains an objective for future work. The design parameters are varied within specific ranges as follows: g varies from 0 m to 2 m with an interval of 0.5 m, w ranges from 0.5 m to 2.5 m with a 0.5 m interval, changes from 6 m to 7 m with an interval of 0.1 m, and p spans from 10 m to 16 m with a 1 m interval. These varying parameters help to explore the design space for optimal performance. So, there were altogether 1925 simulations and the desired combinations (near-unity and ultra-broadband combination, respectively) were found based on the pre-set goals.

Models and algorithms

Figure 12 illustrates the process of using ML for parameter prediction in a dataset consisting of 1410 data points. The process begins with data preprocessing, which includes labeling, data handling (such as dealing with missing values), and scaling to ensure that all features are on a comparable scale. After preprocessing, the data is split into training samples (80%) and test samples (20%), with the training data being used to train the machine learning models and the test data being reserved to evaluate the model’s performance. Once the models are trained, they are applied to the test samples to make predictions on the desired parameters. The best ML algorithm is selected by comparing the performance of various models, such as Random Forest, Decision Tree, SVR, Linear Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors, MLP Regressor, and Gradient Boosting Regressor, based on metrics like R-squared and Mean Squared Error (MSE). The selected algorithm is then used for accurate parameter prediction.

In this study, seven machine learning models were deployed for predicting the absorber system’s parameters: Random Forest, Decision Tree, Support Vector Regression (SVR), Linear Regression, Gradient Boosting Regressor, K-Nearest Neighbors and MLP Regressor. These models are widely used because they offer a good balance between interpretability and predictive power across diverse datasets. They cover a range of approaches—from simple linear relationships to complex non-linear patterns (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, SVR) — making them versatile for various regression tasks.

Mean Squared Error (MSE) and are widely regarded as the most effective and commonly used evaluation metrics for regression problems. MSE represents the average of the squared differences between the actual and predicted values. In simpler terms, it quantifies how far off the predictions are from the true values. A lower MSE value indicates better model performance since it means the predicted values are closer to the actual values. is a statistical measure that indicates the proportion of the variance in the dependent variable that is explained by the independent variables in the model. It represents how well the model fits the data.

Possible fabrication method

A key advantage of the proposed absorber is its simple fabrication process, making it suitable for practical applications. The ML model’s predictions are not directly used for fabrication; instead, they provide an initial design estimate that is subsequently refined through additional simulations—such as full-wave electromagnetic analysis—to validate performance and correct potential inaccuracies. Although the model shows theoretical promise for real-time applications, the implementation of this model for real-time applications is presently limited by the capabilities of our current fabrication and experimental resources. Conclusion

We proposed a tunable metamaterial perfect absorber, achieving >90% absorption across 7.26 THz, with >99% absorption in between 6.25–11.3 THz. The thermal phase transition of VO2 allows dynamic tuning from 0.98% to 100% absorption. Absorption mechanisms are confirmed via wave-interference and impedance-matching theories, supported by field analysis at two absorption peaks. Geometric optimization maximizes performance, while machine learning accurately predicts optimal parameters with Random Forest Regression exhibited the lowest prediction error. The predicted values closely match the simulated results, with only minor deviations. For angle of incidence up to 50°, the model exhibits >90% absorptance for both transverse mode (TE and TM modes), making the model suitable for THz modulators, switches, sensors and other optical devices.

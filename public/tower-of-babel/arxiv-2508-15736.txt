Connecting Discrete Morse Functions via Birth-Death Transitions
Chong Zheng

Abstract
. We study transformations between discrete Morse functions on a finite simplicial complex via birth-death transitions—elementary chain maps between discrete Morse complexes that either create or cancel pairs of critical simplices. We prove that any two discrete Morse functions f 1 , f 2 : K → ℝ f_{1},f_{2}:K\to\mathbb{R} on a finite simplicial complex K K are linked by a finite sequence of such transitions. As applications, we present alternative proofs of several of Forman’s fundamental results in discrete Morse theory and study the topology of the space of discrete Morse functions.



1.
Introduction

In classical Morse theory,
a well-known result states that
two Morse functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}:M\to\mathbb{R}

on a compact manifold
M

can be continuously deformed into one another via a one-parameter
family
[
3
,
2
]
.
This fundamental result
plays a crucial role in understanding
the structure of differentiable manifolds
by providing a mechanism to relate different Morse functions on the same manifold.

In discrete Morse theory,
a parallel result is naturally expected.
Discrete Morse theory,
introduced by Forman
[
7
,
8
]
,
adapts the philosophy of
smooth cases to simplicial complexes and cell complexes,
replacing smooth functions with combinatorial analogs.
For a given simplicial complex

K
,
a discrete Morse function assigns values to its simplices in a manner compatible
with topological gradient flow.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex
and
f_{1},f_{2}:K\to\mathbb{R}
be two discrete Morse functions.
In
[
10
]
and our previous works
[
16
,
17
]
,
we introduced the concept of
connectedness
between both
critical simplices and discrete Morse functions
enabling
an algebraic characterization of the relationship between their associated
gradient vector fields
.
In particular,
the
connectedness homomorphisms

between discrete Morse complexes

C_{\ast}^{f_{1}},C_{\ast}^{f_{2}}
,
corresponding to
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
respectively,
provide a powerful tool for analyzing the relationships between

f_{1}
and
f_{2}
.

Another central theme in discrete Morse theory
is the study of
the complex of discrete Morse functions
,
denoted
\mathcal{M}(K)
, as
introduced by Chari and Joswig
[
4
]
.
The authors constructed

\mathcal{M}(K)
as a simplicial complex to encode
the space of
discrete Morse functions on
K
,
establishing a bijection between
\mathcal{M}(K)

and the set of gradient vector fields on
K
.
Following this work,
investigating the homotopy type of
\mathcal{M}(K)

has been a major task in discrete Morse theory
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
15
]
.

This paper
continues the study and clarifies some ambiguities in
[
17
]
by
following the idea of
birth-death transitions

to connect discrete Morse functions on
\mathcal{M}(K)
.
Precisely,
we show in Theorem
4.6
that
any two discrete Morse functions

f_{1}
and
f_{2}
can be transformed into
one another
with a finite sequence of
birth-death transitions.
Moreover,
we apply this theorem to give an alternative, self-contained proof of some
Forman’s fundamental results in
Theorem
5.1

and
Theorem
5.2
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2,
we provide a
concise overview of the
preliminaries,
including
the fundamentals of
discrete Morse theory, as well as the concepts
of connectedness homomorphisms.
In Section 3,
we introduce the birth-death transitions introduced in
[
17
]

between two discrete Morse complexes.
In Section 4,
we prove Theorem
4.6
.
Section 5 applies this framework to re-establish key theorems of Forman
through the compositions of birth-death transitions.
Also,
we provide some remarks about the topology of the space of
discrete Morse functions on
K
.

Preliminaries

This section provides the foundational concepts necessary for the remainder of the paper.

2.1.
Discrete Morse Functions and Gradient Vector Fields

For a comprehensive
understanding
of
discrete Morse theory
and
discrete Morse homology
,
readers are referred
to
[
7
,
8
,
12
]
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex,

K_{p}
be the set of
p
-dimensional simplices.
We write
\sigma^{(p)}
to represent a

p
-dimensional simplex,
omitting the superscript

p

when the dimension is clear from context.
We write
\tau\prec\sigma
and
\tau\succ\sigma
if

\tau
is a face of
\sigma
, and
\sigma
is a face of
\tau
.

A
discrete Morse function

f:K\to\mathbb{R}

satisfies for every simplex

\sigma\in K_{p}
,

(1)

\displaystyle\#\{\tau^{(p+1)}\succ\sigma\,|\,f(\tau)\leq f(\sigma)\}=0\text{ or }1;

(2)

\displaystyle\#\{v^{(p-1)}\prec\sigma\,|\,f(\sigma)\leq f(v)\}=0\text{ or }1,

where
\#
denotes the cardinality of a set.

A simplex

\sigma^{(p)}
is called a

critical
simplex with respect to
f

if:

(1)

\displaystyle\#\{\tau^{(p+1)}\succ\sigma\,|\,f(\tau)\leq f(\sigma)\}=0;

(2)

\displaystyle\#\{v^{(p-1)}\prec\sigma\,|\,f(\sigma)\leq f(v)\}=0.

If
\sigma
is critical,
then we call
f(\sigma)
a
critical value
of
f
.
Conversely, if a simplex
\sigma
is not critical,
then
we say that
\sigma
is a
non-critical
simplex,
and
f(\sigma)
is a
non-critical value
.
The set of
f
-critical
q
-simplices is denoted

Cr_{q}(f):=\{\sigma\in K_{q}\,|\,\sigma^{(q)}\text{ is }f\text{-critical}\}
.

The
gradient vector field

V
corresponding to
f
is
the collection of pairs

(\alpha^{(p)},\beta^{(p+1)})

satisfying

\alpha\prec\beta
and

f(\alpha)\geq f(\beta)
.
We call such a pair
(\alpha,\beta)

a
gradient pair
.

A
gradient
V
-path
is a sequence
of simplices

\alpha_{0}^{(p)},\beta_{0}^{(p+1)},\alpha_{1}^{(p)},\beta_{1}^{(p+1)},\cdots,\beta_{r}^{(p+1)},\alpha_{r+1}^{(p)}

such that for
each
i=0,\cdots,r
:

•

(\alpha^{(p)},\beta^{(p+1)})\in V
,

•

\beta_{i}^{(p+1)}\succ\alpha_{i+1}^{(p)}
and
\alpha_{i+1}^{(p)}\neq\alpha_{i}^{(p)}.

We denote a
gradient
V
-path

\gamma

from

\alpha_{0}^{(p)}
to

\alpha_{r+1}^{(p)}
,
consisting of
p
- and
(p+1)
-dimensional
simplices
by

\gamma:\alpha_{0}^{(p)}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 17.50418pt\hskip-17.50418pt\hbox{\hbox{\hskip 17.50418pt\hskip-5.0pt\hbox{$\rightsquigarrow$}\hskip-5.0pt\hskip-17.50418pt\raisebox{9.02881pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle V(p,p+1)$}}\hskip-17.50418pt\hskip 17.50418pt}}\hskip-17.50418pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-3.25pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 17.50418pt}}\alpha_{r+1}^{(p)}.

Alternatively, a
V
-path may begin at a

(p+1)
-dimensional simplex
\beta_{0}
and end at
\beta_{r}
,
denoted:

\gamma:\beta_{0}^{(p+1)}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 17.50418pt\hskip-17.50418pt\hbox{\hbox{\hskip 17.50418pt\hskip-5.0pt\hbox{$\rightsquigarrow$}\hskip-5.0pt\hskip-17.50418pt\raisebox{9.02881pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle V(p,p+1)$}}\hskip-17.50418pt\hskip 17.50418pt}}\hskip-17.50418pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-3.25pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 17.50418pt}}\beta_{r}^{(p+1)}.

2.2.
Discrete Morse Complexes

As a discrete analog of the
Morse-Smale complex in smooth Morse theory (see
[
14
,
9
]
),
the discrete Morse complex is defined by Forman
[
7
]
.
Its chain groups
are generated by critical simplices, with
boundaries determined by counting
gradient paths weighted by incidence numbers.

Assume each simplex of
K
is oriented.
For
\alpha^{(q-1)}\prec\sigma^{(q)}
,
we use
[\sigma:\alpha]=\pm 1
to represent
the incidence number
of

\sigma
and
\alpha
,
where the sign is chosen according to the orientation.

Let
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be a discrete Morse function.
The
q
-th chain group of discrete Morse complex

C_{q}^{f}=C_{q}^{f}(K)

is the free Abelian group generated by

\sigma_{i}\in Cr_{q}(f)
,
The

q
-th boundary homomorphism

\partial_{q}^{f}:C_{q}^{f}\to C_{q-1}^{f}

is defined as the linear extension
of

\partial_{q}^{f}(\sigma_{i}):=\sum_{\alpha\in Cr_{q-1}(f)}n^{f}(\sigma_{i},\alpha)\alpha.

Here,
n^{f}(\sigma_{i},\alpha)
is
given by

n^{f}(\sigma_{i},\alpha):=\sum_{\gamma}m(\gamma),

where
\gamma

runs over all gradient paths
\sigma_{i}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 15.89069pt\hskip-15.89069pt\hbox{\hbox{\hskip 15.89069pt\hskip-5.0pt\hbox{$\rightsquigarrow$}\hskip-5.0pt\hskip-15.89069pt\raisebox{9.02881pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle V(q-1,q)$}}\hskip-15.89069pt\hskip 15.89069pt}}\hskip-15.89069pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-3.25pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 15.89069pt}}\alpha
,
and

m(\gamma)
is given by the product of
each incidence number.
We call
n^{f}(\sigma_{i},\alpha)

the
connectedness coefficient between

\sigma_{i}
and
\alpha
.
In particular,
\partial_{0}^{f}
is defined as
the zero homomorphism.

It is known that

\partial^{f}\circ\partial^{f}=0

[
7
, Theorem 7.1]
.
Hence,
\{C_{\ast}^{f},\partial_{\ast}^{f}\}

forms the
discrete Morse chain complex
,
and the
discrete Morse homology

is defined as

H_{\ast}^{f}:=\frac{\text{ker}\partial^{f}_{\ast}}{\text{im}\partial^{f}_{\ast}}.

Moreover, discrete Morse homology is
isomorphic to the ordinary homology
[
7
, Theorem 7.1]
.

2.3.
Connectedness and Connectedness Homomorphisms

Let
f_{1},f_{2}:K\to\mathbb{R}
be distinct discrete
Morse functions on
K
,
with respective gradient vector fields
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
.
The notion of
connectedness
between critical simplices
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}\in Cr_{q}(f_{1})
and

\tilde{\sigma}_{2}\in Cr_{q}(f_{2})

is first introduced by King, Mramor Kosta, and Knudson
[
10
]
.
They applied the concept to investigate the structure of the gradient vector fields
and provided some interesting applications.

Precisely,
for critical simplices
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}\in Cr_{q}(f_{1})
and

\tilde{\sigma}_{2}\in Cr_{q}(f_{2})
,
we say that

\tilde{\sigma}_{1}

is
connected
to
\tilde{\sigma}_{2}
if

•

for
q\neq 0
,
there is an
f_{1}
-gradient path

\tilde{\sigma}_{1}^{(q)}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 17.8421pt\hskip-17.8421pt\hbox{\hbox{\hskip 17.8421pt\hskip-5.0pt\hbox{$\rightsquigarrow$}\hskip-5.0pt\hskip-17.8421pt\raisebox{9.2788pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle V_{1}(q-1,q)$}}\hskip-17.8421pt\hskip 17.8421pt}}\hskip-17.8421pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-3.49998pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 17.8421pt}}\tilde{\sigma}_{2}^{(q)};

•

for
q=0
,
there is an
f_{2}
-gradient path

\tilde{\sigma}_{1}^{(0)}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 12.66681pt\hskip-12.66681pt\hbox{\hbox{\hskip 12.66681pt\hskip-5.0pt\hbox{$\rightsquigarrow$}\hskip-5.0pt\hskip-12.66681pt\raisebox{9.2788pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle V_{2}(0,1)$}}\hskip-12.66681pt\hskip 12.66681pt}}\hskip-12.66681pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-3.49998pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 12.66681pt}}\tilde{\sigma}_{2}^{(0)}.

Note that the corresponding connectedness coefficient
n^{f_{1}}(\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\tilde{\sigma}_{2})
captures the number (weighted by incidence) of such
gradient paths.

Furthermore,
we say that

\tilde{\sigma}_{1}

is
strongly connected
to
\tilde{\sigma}_{2}

if
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}

is connected to
\tilde{\sigma}_{2}
and

\tilde{\sigma}_{2}

is connected to
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}
,
denoted

\tilde{\sigma}_{1}\leftrightarrow\tilde{\sigma}_{2}.

For each
q\geq 1
,
the
q
-dimensional connectedness homomorphism

from
C_{q}^{f_{1}}
to

C_{q}^{f_{2}}

h_{q}:C_{q}^{f_{1}}\to C_{q}^{f_{2}}

is defined as the linear extension of

h_{q}(\tilde{\sigma}_{1}):=\sum_{\sigma_{j}\in Cr_{q}(f_{2})}n^{f_{1}}(\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\sigma_{j})\sigma_{j},

where
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}\in Cr_{q}(f_{1})
.

For
q=0
,
inheriting the
0
-dimensional connectedness between simplices
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}^{(0)}
and

\tilde{\sigma}_{2}^{(0)}
,
we let

h_{0}:C_{0}^{f_{1}}\to C_{0}^{f_{2}}

be the linear extension of

h_{0}(\tilde{\sigma}_{1}):=\sum_{\sigma_{j}\in Cr_{0}(f_{2})}n^{f_{2}}(\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\sigma_{j})\sigma_{j},

where
\tilde{\sigma}_{1}\in Cr_{0}(f_{1})
.

On the other hand, we can also define the reverse
connectedness homomorphism

from

C_{q}^{f_{2}}
to

C_{q}^{f_{1}}

with a parallel way
as

g_{q}:C_{q}^{f_{2}}\to C_{q}^{f_{1}}.

Note,
however,
that these maps are
generally not chain maps,
since connectedness is not transitive in general.

3.
Birth-death transitions between discrete Morse complexes

In this section,
we review and provide more details on

birth-death transitions
introduced in
[
17
]
.
The notion of birth-death transitions is a discrete analogue inspired
by classical Morse theory’s creation and cancellation of critical points.
These transitions act as chain maps between discrete Morse complexes

C_{\ast}^{f_{1}}

and

C_{\ast}^{f_{2}}

adding or removing a pair of critical simplices in consecutive dimensions,
while preserving the underlying homology
[
17
]
.
Building upon the
connectedness homomorphisms defined previously, we now formalize the birth and death transitions and investigate their structural properties.

Fix a dimension
i
.
Suppose two discrete Morse functions

f_{1},f_{2}:K\to\mathbb{R}
on a simplicial complex
K
such that:

\#Cr_{i}(f_{1})=\#Cr_{i}(f_{2})-1;\quad\#Cr_{i-1}(f_{1})=\#Cr_{i-1}(f_{2})-1,

and for all
q\neq i,i-1
,

Cr_{q}(f_{1})=Cr_{q}(f_{2}).

We denote the two additional
f_{2}
-critical simplices
simplices as

\tilde{\sigma}\in Cr_{i}(f_{2})

and

\tilde{\alpha}\in Cr_{i-1}(f_{2})
,
with the coefficient

n^{f_{2}}(\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\alpha})=k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Definition 3.1
.

Under the conditions specified above,
we define

•

The connectedness homomorphism
h:C_{q}^{f_{1}}\to C_{q}^{f_{2}}
as a
birth transition
;

•

The connectedness homomorphism
g:C_{q}^{f_{2}}\to C_{q}^{f_{1}}
as a
death transition
.

In
[
17
]
,
the following properties were assumed
for simplicity.
Here we
state them explicitly as lemmas to clarify their behaviours.
Note that these lemmas simply follow from
the assumptions in the definition of
birth-death transitions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

h:C_{q}^{f_{1}}\to C_{q}^{f_{2}}
and
g:C_{q}^{f_{2}}\to C_{q}^{f_{1}}
be a birth transition and
a death transition,
respectively.

Then,

•

For any
\delta_{1}\in Cr_{q}(f_{1})
,

h_{q}(\delta_{1})=\begin{cases}\delta_{1}&\text{ if }q\neq i;\\
\delta_{1}+n^{f_{1}}(\delta_{1},\tilde{\sigma})\tilde{\sigma}&\text{ if }q=i.\par\par\end{cases}

•

For any
\delta_{2}\in Cr_{q}(f_{2})
,

g_{q}(\delta_{2})=\begin{cases}\delta_{2}&\text{ if }\delta_{2}\neq\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\alpha};\\
0&\text{ if }\delta_{2}=\tilde{\sigma};\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in Cr_{i-1}(f_{1})}n^{f_{2}}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha)\alpha&\text{ if }\delta_{2}=\tilde{\alpha}.\par\par\end{cases}

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

h:C_{q}^{f_{1}}\to C_{q}^{f_{2}}
and
g:C_{q}^{f_{2}}\to C_{q}^{f_{1}}
be a birth transition and
a death transition,
respectively.

Then, with respect to the boundary homomorphisms

\partial_{\ast}^{f_{1}}
and
\partial_{\ast}^{f_{2}}
,
the following equations (1) to (5)
hold.

Namely, when
q\neq i,i+1\text{ and }\delta\neq\tilde{\alpha},

(1)

\partial^{f_{1}}_{q}(\delta)=\partial^{f_{2}}_{q}(\delta).

For any
\tau\in Cr_{i+1}(f_{2})
,

(2)

\partial_{i+1}^{f_{2}}(\tau)=\partial_{i+1}^{f_{1}}(\tau)+\sum_{\sigma\in Cr_{i}(f_{1})}n^{f_{1}}(\tau,\sigma)\cdot n^{f_{1}}(\sigma,\tilde{\sigma})\tilde{\sigma}.

For any
\sigma\in Cr_{i}(f_{2})
and
\sigma\neq\tilde{\sigma}
,

(3)

\partial_{i}^{f_{2}}(\sigma)=\partial_{i}^{f_{1}}(\sigma)+n^{f_{1}}(\sigma,\tilde{\sigma})(k\tilde{\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha\in Cr_{i-1}(f_{1})}k\cdot n^{f_{2}}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha)\alpha).

(4)

\partial_{i}^{f_{2}}(\tilde{\sigma})=k\tilde{\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha\in Cr_{i-1}(f_{1})}k\cdot n^{f_{2}}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha)\alpha.

(5)

\partial_{i-1}^{f_{2}}(\tilde{\alpha})=\sum_{\alpha\in Cr_{i-1}(f_{2})}n^{f_{2}}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha)\sum_{\omega\in Cr_{i-2}(f_{1})}n^{f_{1}}(\alpha,\omega)\omega.

Remark 3.4
.

The above lemmas
omit the case
q=0
,
in which the connectedness coefficient of
n^{f_{1}}
and
n^{f_{2}}
may
exchange due to the convention regarding
0
-dimensional paths (see Section 2.3).
However, this does not alter the validity of the results.

Theorem 3.5
.

[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
.
Let
f_{1},f_{2}:K\to\mathbb{R}

be discrete Morse functions.
Suppose that

h_{q}:C_{q}^{f_{1}}\to C_{q}^{f_{2}}
and
g_{q}:C_{q}^{f_{2}}\to C_{q}^{f_{1}}
be a birth transition and
a death transition,
respectively.

Then,

h
and
g
are chain maps.

Besides chain maps,
these maps
also induce isomorphisms in homology.
We show that
h
and
g
form a
chain-homotopy equivalence as follows.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose that
h:C_{q}^{f_{1}}\to C_{q}^{f_{2}}
and
g:C_{q}^{f_{2}}\to C_{q}^{f_{1}}
be a birth transition and
a death transition,
respectively.

Then,

h
and
g
are chain-homotopy inverses to each other,
that is,
g\circ h
and
h\circ g
are chain homotopic to the
identities.

Proof.

Suppose that two additional
f_{2}
-critical simplices are

\tilde{\sigma}\in\mathrm{Cr}_{i}(f_{2})

and
\tilde{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Cr}_{i-1}(f_{2})
,
with
n^{f_{2}}(\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\alpha})=k
.
For any
f_{1}
-critical simplex
\delta
, one checks immediately that

g\circ h(\delta)=\delta
. Hence

g\circ h=\mathrm{id}\quad

on

C^{f_{1}}_{\ast}.

On
C^{f_{2}}_{\ast}
, the only nontrivial cases are the additional pair

\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\alpha}
. One computes

h\circ g(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x=\tilde{\sigma},\\[6.0pt]
-\tilde{\alpha}-\sum\limits_{\beta}n^{f_{2}}(\tilde{\alpha},\beta)\,\beta,&x=\tilde{\alpha},\\[10.0pt]
x,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Define a homotopy

s:C^{f_{2}}_{\ast}\to C^{f_{2}}_{\ast+1},\qquad s(x)=\begin{cases}-\tfrac{1}{k}\tilde{\sigma},&x=\tilde{\alpha},\\[6.0pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then.

(\partial^{f_{2}}s+s\partial^{f_{2}})(x)=\begin{cases}-\tilde{\sigma},&x=\tilde{\sigma},\\[6.0pt]
-\tilde{\alpha}-\sum\limits_{\beta}n^{f_{2}}(\tilde{\alpha},\beta)\,\beta,&x=\tilde{\alpha},\\[10.0pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus

h\circ g-\mathrm{id}=\partial^{f_{2}}s+s\partial^{f_{2}}.

Therefore
g\circ h=\mathrm{id}
and
h\circ g\simeq\mathrm{id}
,
so
h
and
g
are chain-homotopy inverses.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

be the corresponding gradient vector

We use

Conversely, if

, then no new critical simplices are introduced or removed, and the gradient vector fields must coincide.
∎

4.
Connecting discrete Morse functions via birth-death transitions

In discrete Morse theory, the space of all discrete Morse functions on a simplicial complex
K
is often studied through their associated gradient vector fields.
Two discrete Morse functions
f_{1},f_{2}:K\to\mathbb{R}
are
considered
equivalent
when their gradient vector fields
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
coincide.

The set of all
gradient vector fields forms a simplicial complex, known as the
complex of discrete Morse functions
, denoted
\mathcal{M}(K)
, introduced by Chari and Joswig
[
4
]
.
This complex parametrizes acyclic matchings (gradient vector fields)
in the Hasse diagrams of
K
.
In particular, when

K

is a graph, this simplicial complex coincides with the
complex of rooted forests
, studied by Kozlov
[
11
]
.

Also in
[
4
]
, Chari-Joswig asks how to relate any two
acyclic matchings by a sequence of finite steps.
We resolve this affirmatively on the chain level, showing that any two discrete Morse functions

f_{1},f_{2}:K\to\mathbb{R}

can be connected through a finite sequence of birth-death transitions, as defined in Section 3.

We first define the complex of discrete Morse functions.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a simplicial complex
K
,
we define the
complex of discrete Morse functions

\mathcal{M}(K)

as the collection of subsets of edges in the Hasse diagram
of
K
that form acyclic matchings.

Alternatively, we may define it more explicitly:

We define a simplicial complex
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(K)

on
K
as follows.
We say that a
primitive vector field
of
K

is a set
P
containing one pair of simplices

\alpha
and
\sigma
of
K
such that

\alpha\prec\sigma
and
\dim\sigma-\dim\alpha=1
.

We let
\mathcal{P}(K)=\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{n}\}

be the set of all primitive vector fields of
K
.

The simplices of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(K)
are defined as:

•

0
-simplices:
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{0}(K):=\mathcal{P}(K)
;

•

q
-simplices:
(P_{i_{0}},P_{i_{1}},\ldots,P_{i_{q}})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{q}(K)

if
V=\{P_{i_{0}},P_{i_{1}},\ldots,P_{i_{q}}\}

form an acyclic matching (i.e., a gradient vector field).

To ensure that all Morse functions are included, we augment

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(K)
with the empty simplex,
that is,

\mathcal{M}(K)=\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(K)\cup\{\tilde{\emptyset}\}.

Note that although the empty simplex
\tilde{\emptyset}
is sometimes ignored in other papers,
this inclusion of empty simplex to
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(K)
is
necessary in our framework to ensure that
\mathcal{M}(K)
includes all possible discrete
Morse functions, including the discrete Morse functions where all simplices are critical.

Such discrete Morse function always exist;
for example:
the discrete Morse function

f:K\to\mathbb{R}

with

f(\sigma)=\dim\sigma

corresponds to the gradient vector field
V_{0}
, in which every simplex
is critical.

Recall that in a simplicial complex,
the empty simplex
\tilde{\emptyset}

has dimension
-1

and is a face of every vertex.
It is not hard to verify that
if
K
is a simplicial complex, then
K\cup\{\tilde{\emptyset}\}
is still an (abstract) simplicial complex.
We consider the poset topology of this augmented simplicial complex ordered by inclusion.

As one of the most important
statement of Cerf theory,
any two Morse functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}

on the same manifold can be related by a
finite sequence of moves involving the creation and cancellation of critical pairs.
We can use
the birth-death transitions
to represent a discrete version of
this sequence between two discrete Morse functions
f_{1},f_{2}:K\to\mathbb{R}
.
We first study the local behavior of such transitions in the complex
\mathcal{M}(K)
.

By
\overline{\sigma}_{1},\overline{\sigma}_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(K)
,
we denote the simplex in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(K)

corresponding to
V_{1},V_{2}
,
respectively.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
\overline{\sigma}_{1}\prec\overline{\sigma}_{2}

and
\dim\overline{\sigma}_{2}-\dim\overline{\sigma}_{1}=1
,
then

•

The connectedness homomorphism
h:C^{V_{1}}_{\ast}\to C^{V_{2}}_{\ast}
is a death transition;

•

The connectedness homomorphism
g:C^{V_{2}}_{\ast}\to C^{V_{1}}_{\ast}
is a birth transition.

Proof.

Suppose that

\overline{\sigma}_{1}\prec\overline{\sigma}_{2}

and
\dim\overline{\sigma}_{2}-\dim\overline{\sigma}_{1}=1
.
Then,

V_{1}
differs
V_{2}
from a primitive vector field
(\alpha,\sigma)
.
More precisely,

V_{2}=V_{1}\cup(\alpha,\sigma).

This implies that
both

\alpha
and
\sigma

are
V_{1}
-critical.

Note that
(\alpha,\sigma)\in V_{2}
implies

n^{f_{1}}(\sigma,\alpha)\neq 0
;
otherwise,
one of the following contradiction arises

•

there is no
V_{1}
-gradient path connecting
\sigma
and
\alpha
;

•

the coefficients of multiple
V_{1}
-gradient paths cancel with each other.

Case (1) contradicts with either
\alpha\prec\sigma
;
and
case (2) forms a cycle with no
V_{2}
-critical simplices, which contradicts with the
acyclicity
of a gradient vector field.

Therefore,
by definition,
the connectedness homomorphism
g
is a birth transition,
and the connectedness homomorphism
h
is a death transition.
∎

Combining
the above lemmas,
we recover the idea of Cerf theory on
K

by connecting the discrete Morse functions with birth-death transitions.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
K
be a finite simplicial complex
and

f_{1},f_{2}:K\to\mathbb{R}
be
discrete Morse functions.

Then,
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
can be transformed into
one another
with a finite sequence of
birth-death transitions.

Proof.

Let
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be
the gradient vector fields
corresponding to
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
.
Suppose that these correspond to simplices

\overline{\sigma}_{1},\overline{\sigma}_{2}\in\mathcal{M}(K)
.

Then, we can construct a path consisting of successive adjacent simplices

\overline{\sigma}_{1}^{(n)}\succ\alpha_{1}^{(n-1)}\succ\alpha_{2}^{(n-2)}\succ\cdots\succ\alpha_{n}^{0}\succ\tilde{\emptyset}

connecting

\overline{\sigma}_{1}
to
\tilde{\emptyset}
,
denoted
\overline{\sigma}_{1}\;\to\;\tilde{\emptyset}
.
Each adjacent pair
corresponds to a birth transition
as established in Lemma
4.2
(2), when
\dim\alpha_{i}\geq 1
;
and established in Lemma
4.4
(1), when
\dim\alpha_{i}=0
.

Similarly,
we can also construct a path
connecting

\tilde{\emptyset}
to
\overline{\sigma}_{2}
,
each step corresponding to a death transition.

Hence, both
\overline{\sigma}_{1}
and
\overline{\sigma}_{2}

can be connected to
\tilde{\emptyset}
via such paths:

\overline{\sigma}_{1}\;\to\;\tilde{\emptyset}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\emptyset}\;\to\;\overline{\sigma}_{2}.

Concatenating these two paths gives a sequence

\overline{\sigma}_{1}\;\to\;\tilde{\emptyset}\;\to\;\overline{\sigma}_{2},

lying entirely in
\mathcal{M}(K)
, in which each adjacent pair of simplices
corresponds to a birth or death transition.
Since
K
is finite, this sequence has finitely many steps,
completing the proof.

∎

5.
An alternative proof of Forman’s Theorems

In Forman’s original theory, a simplicial complex
K
is simplified through the collapse of gradient
pairs (pairs of non-critical simplices)
guided by the descending values of the discrete Morse function
f
,
or equivalently, the arrows in the associated gradient vector field
V
.
This collapsing sequence
reduces
K
to a smaller simplicial complex
with critical simplices encoding its homology.

Dually,
we can equivalently generate/cancel the critical simplices,
the generators of the discrete Morse complex
C_{\ast}^{f}
,
in pairs similar to cancellation in classical Morse theory.
In this process,
K
remains unchanged,
and the homological structure is modified through algebraic transitions.

Theorem
4.6
establishes
that any two gradient vector fields in

\mathcal{M}(K)
are path-connected via birth-death transitions.
Besides the algebraic framework given in algebraic Morse theory,
we aim to provide more geometric information and
intuitions by the introduction of connectedness homomorphisms
and birth-death transitions.

One of the
fundamental results in discrete Morse theory,
established by Forman
[
7
]
, is the equivalence
between discrete Morse homology and simplicial homology.
In our framework, where birth-death transitions act as chain maps between discrete Morse complexes,
this correspondence can be recovered naturally.

Theorem 5.1
.

[
7
, Theorem 7.1]

Let
K
be a simplicial complex and

f:K\to\mathbb{R}

be a discrete Morse function.

Then,

H_{\ast}(K)\cong H_{\ast}^{\text{simp}}(K),

where

H_{\ast}^{\text{simp}}(K)

denotes the simplicial homology of
K
.

Proof.

Let
V_{0}
be the empty gradient vector field
(i.e., the one where all simplices are critical).
Define an
isomorphism

\psi_{q}:C_{q}^{\text{simp}}\to C_{q}^{V_{0}},

for all
q
,
where
C_{q}^{\text{simp}}
denotes the

q
-th simplicial chain group of
K
.
Note that

\psi_{q}=\text{id}_{q},

for all
q
,
as the generators of
C_{q}^{\text{simp}}
and

C_{q}^{V_{0}}
coincide.

By Theorem
4.6
, the gradient vector field
V
corresponding to
f

is connected to
V_{0}

through a sequence of birth-death transitions,
each of which defines a chain homotopy equivalence.
Thus, composing the induced maps yields a homology isomorphism:

H_{\ast}^{V}(K)\cong H_{\ast}^{V_{0}}(K)\cong H_{\ast}^{\text{simp}}(K),

completing the proof.
∎

Besides Theorem 5.1,
other Forman’s theorems can also be recovered in the sense
of “collapsing critical simplices”, similar to the proof above.
We provide one more example, the Morse equality, as follows.

Theorem 5.2
.

[
7
, Theorem 2.11]

Let
K
be a finite simplicial complex and

f:K\to\mathbb{R}
be a discrete Morse function.

Then,

\sum_{q}(-1)^{q}\beta_{q}(K)=\sum_{q}(-1)^{q}Cr_{q}(f),

where

\beta_{q}(K)
denotes the
q
-th Betti number
of
K
.

Proof.

Let
f_{0}:K\to\mathbb{R}

be a discrete Morse function whose every simplex is critical.
Theorem
4.6
implies that
there exists a finite sequence of birth-death transitions
transforming
f_{0}
to
f
.
By definition,
a birth/death transition generates/cancels
a pair of
(q,q+1)
-dimension critical simplices.
Thus,
the alternating sum remains invariant under such transitions.
That is,

\sum_{q}(-1)^{q}Cr_{q}(f)=\sum_{q}(-1)^{q}Cr_{q}(f_{0}).

Moreover,
since

\sum_{q}(-1)^{q}Cr_{q}(f_{0})=\chi(K)=\sum_{q}(-1)^{q}\beta_{q},

where
\chi(K)
denotes the Euler characteristic of
K
,
we have the desired equality.

∎

Several remarks on topology of the set of discrete Morse functions.

Recall that in
[
17
]
,
we introduced the following equivalence relation
on the set of all discrete Morse functions on a simplicial complex

K
,
denoted,
\mathcal{F}
,
to study its topological properties.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}:K\to\mathbb{R}

be a sequence of
discrete Morse functions,
with corresponding
discrete Morse complexes
C_{\ast}^{f_{1}},C_{\ast}^{f_{2}},\ldots,C_{\ast}^{f_{n}}
.
If, for each
i=1,2,\cdots,n-1
,
the connectedness homomorphism

h^{i}_{\ast}:C_{\ast}^{f_{i}}\to C_{\ast}^{f_{i+1}}

is either an isomorphism or a
birth/death
transition,
then we call the
sequence
of discrete Morse functions
a
birth-death transition sequence
.
Any two
discrete Morse functions
f_{j}
and
f_{k}

in such a sequence
are called

transitively connected
,
denoted
f_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\sim}}f_{k}
.

This defines a natural equivalence relation:

Proposition 5.4
.

Transitive connectedness
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\sim}}
is an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Reflexivity holds since
C_{\ast}^{f}\cong C_{\ast}^{f}
via the identity map.
Symmetry follows from the reversibility of birth-death transitions and the invertibility of isomorphisms.
Transitivity is built into the definition of the sequence.

∎

This equivalence relation allows us to
characterize the topology of

\mathcal{F}
via the quotient

\mathcal{F}/\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\sim}}
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex
and
\mathcal{F}
be the set of all discrete Morse functions on
K
.

Then,

\mathcal{F}/\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\sim}}=\{\ast\}.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.6
, any two discrete Morse functions

f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{F}

can be connected by a finite sequence of birth-death transitions.
Each transition

h_{\ast}^{i}:C_{\ast}^{f_{i}}\to C_{\ast}^{f_{i+1}}

is chain map,
and isomorphisms arise when gradient vector fields are identical (Lemma
3.7
).
Thus,

f_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\sim}}f_{2}

for all

f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
,
implying the quotient consists of a single point.
That is,

\mathcal{F}/\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\sim}}=\{\ast\}.

∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex.
Then,
the augmented complex of discrete Morse functions
\mathcal{M}(K)

is connected with poset topology.

Remark 5.7
.

Note that higher-dimensional homology of
\mathcal{M}(K)
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(K)

are isomorphic,
as
\tilde{\emptyset}
affects only connectivity at dimension
0
.
This viewpoint aligns with the results of Scoville and Zaremsky
[
15
, Theorem 5.4]
.
They
proved that almost all
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(K)
(except Example 4.5) are connected,
which implies Corollary 5.6.

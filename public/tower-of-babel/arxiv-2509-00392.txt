Extensions of realizable Hamiltonian and complexity one GKM 4 graphs
Oliver Goertsches, Grigory Solomadin

Abstract
. We prove that the GKM graphs of GKM 4 manifolds that are either Hamiltonian or of complexity one extend to torus graphs. The arguments are based on a reformulation of the extension problem in terms of a natural representation of the fundamental group of the GKM graph, using a coordinate-free version of the axial function group of Kuroki, as well as on covers of GKM graphs and acyclicity results for orbit spaces of GKM manifolds.

1.
Introduction

GKM manifolds are a natural, far-reaching generalization of (quasi)toric manifolds, named after Goresky–Kottwitz–MacPherson
[
gkm-98
]
. These are connected, compact, orientable manifolds with vanishing odd-degree cohomology, acted on by a compact torus, in a way that the orbit space of its equivariant one-skeleton has the structure of a graph. This graph, equipped with a labelling given by the weights of the isotropy representation at the fixed points, is called the GKM graph of the action. The condition on the shape of the one-skeleton is equivalent to the weights of any isotropy representation being pairwise linearly independent; in general, if any
k
of these weights are linearly independent, one calls the action GKM
k
.
The starting point for this note is the following (unpublished) conjecture.

Conjecture 1

(Masuda)
.

(i)

Any
\GKM_{4}
manifold
M^{2n}
extends to a torus manifold. (I.e., the action extends to an effective action of
T^{n}
.)

(ii)

The GKM graph
\Gamma
of any
\GKM_{4}
manifold
M^{2n}
extends to a torus graph. (I.e., there is an abstract GKM graph with effective
T^{n}
-labeling which restricts to
\Gamma
.)

Item
(ii)
of Conjecture
1
follows from item
(i)
; in this note we will focus on item
(ii)
. In the language of abstract GKM graphs
[
gu-za-01
]
, we show (see Theorem
4.19
):

Theorem 2
.

Let
\Gamma
be any unsigned (signed, respectively)
\GKM_{3}
graph such that the conjugated
2
-faces in
\Gamma
generate the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
.
Suppose that the monodromy along any
2
-face of
\Gamma
is trivial on the transversal edges of this
2
-face.
Then
\Gamma
admits an extension to an unsigned (signed, respectively) torus graph.

Here, a conjugated
2
-face is any loop given by a
2
-face (aka connection path) in
\Gamma
, conjugated by a path from the chosen base point, in order to yield an element in the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
– see Definition
3.7
.
The condition on the monodromy is automatically satisfied in the GKM
4
case.

Our main tool for the proof of Theorem
2
is Kuroki’s axial function group
[
ku-19
]
and his criterion for the existence of nontrivial extensions of labelings of GKM graphs. In Section
4
we extend Kuroki’s results to unsigned GKM graphs, and use index-free notation to rewrite the axial function group as the group of invariants under a natural representation of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
.

As concrete situations where Theorem
2
applies we consider two geometrically very different cases. Firstly, we prove (see Corollary
3.11
):

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Gamma
be the GKM graph of any Hamiltonian GKM
3
manifold. Then the conjugated
2
-faces in
\Gamma
generate the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
.

Combining this statement with Theorem
2
, it follows that the GKM graph of any Hamiltonian GKM
4
manifold extends to a torus graph. This result proves a particular case of Conjecture
1

(ii)
, under the additional Hamiltonian assumption.
Theorem
3
follows from a more general statement about GKM
3
graphs with an acyclic orientation of the edges, so that every
2
-face has a unique local maximum with respect to the orientation, see Theorem
3.9
.

The second situation concerns GKM
4
manifolds of complexity
1
, meaning that we are given an effective action of an torus of dimension
n-1
on a manifold of dimension
2n
.

Theorem 4
.

The GKM graph of any
\GKM_{4}
manifold of complexity one admits an extension to a torus graph.

Theorem
4
proves another particular case for Conjecture
1

(ii)
, namely, in complexity
1
.
To prove Theorem
4
(see Theorem
5.6
) we construct a certain GKM cover
p\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
by a (possibly infinite) GKM graph
\widetilde{\Gamma}
whose conjugated
2
-faces generate the respective fundamental group.
Then we apply Theorem
2
to obtain an extension of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
to a torus graph. We conclude by showing that the action of the deck transformation group on the axial function group of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is trivial, making use of the fact that it is a perfect group by the acyclicity results for orbit spaces of equivariant skeleta in
[
ay-ma-23
]
. Notice that Theorem
4
does not hold for abstract GKM graphs by
[
so-23
]
.

2.
Graphs, connections, invariant functions

This section contains introductory definitions and basic facts about unsigned and signed GKM graphs required for understanding the main results of this note.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(V,E)
be an (abstract) connected graph (without loops, but possibly with multiple edges) with vertices
V

The initial and terminal vertices of an edge

By definition, the

In what follows, we assume that

Definition 2.2

A set

\nabla_{\overline{e}}=(\nabla_{e})^{-1}

\nabla_{e}e=\overline{e}

on the graph
(V,E)
.

We call any of the two integer vectors

in
\mathbb{Z}^{k}

of an element

Definition 2.3

(

]

An

as above, a connection
\nabla

(V,E)

(1)

(2)

are linearly independent.

(3)

The axial function

is
compatible with the connection

at any vertex

and for any lift
\widetilde{\alpha}(\nabla_{e}e^{\prime})
of
\alpha(\nabla_{e}e^{\prime})
and
\widetilde{\alpha}(e^{\prime})

Remark 2.4
.

Often, in the definition of an abstract GKM graph, the connection is not assumed to be part of the structure; instead, it is only assumed that there exists a connection that is compatible with a given axial function. See for instance
[
gu-za-01
]
. For the results of this note it is natural to fix also the connection, as we are concerned with extension problems with respect to a given connection, see Definition
4.13
below.

We will need to keep track of the signs
\varepsilon
and the integer coefficients in the above congruence relation. To this end we fix, for easier bookkeeping, an arbitrary global lift

\widetilde{\alpha}:E\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{k}

of

(i.e.,
\pm\widetilde{\alpha}(e)=\alpha(e)
for all
e\in E
), satisfying no further conditions except
\widetilde{\alpha}(\overline{e})=-\widetilde{\alpha}(e)
for all
e\in E
.

In order to avoid having to choose an ordering of the edges at each vertex, we introduce the following notation.
Define a nondegenerate scalar product on

by requiring
\str v
to be an orthonormal basis, i.e.,

\langle e,e^{\prime}\rangle=\delta_{e}^{e^{\prime}},

where

The connection

induces homomorphisms

\nabla_{e}:\ \mathbb{Z}\str i(e)\to\mathbb{Z}\str t(e),

of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules by acting on the generators.

Denote the group

by

be the group of all

-valued functions on the finite set

This group acts on

by the formula below:

\mu_{2}\str v\times\mathbb{Z}\str v\to\mathbb{Z}\str v,\ x\cdot y:=\sum_{e^{\prime}\in\str v}\langle x,e^{\prime}\rangle\langle y,e^{\prime}\rangle e^{\prime},

which is nothing but componentwise multiplication in the standard basis.

Given the lift
\widetilde{\alpha}
of the axial function, we denote, for

, by
\varepsilon(e)\in\mu_{2}\str i(e)
and by

the signs and integers such that

(1)

\widetilde{\alpha}(\nabla_{e}e^{\prime})=\langle\varepsilon(e),e^{\prime}\rangle\cdot\widetilde{\alpha}(e^{\prime})+\langle c(e),e^{\prime}\rangle\cdot\widetilde{\alpha}(e);

we make

unique by requiring

(2)

\langle\varepsilon(e),e\rangle=1\quad\textrm{and}\quad\langle c(e),e\rangle=-2.

.

Consider a graph

such that

(1

)

\langle c(e),e\rangle=-2

(2

\nabla_{e}c(e)=\varepsilon(\overline{e})\cdot c(\overline{e})

The notion of an invariant function was introduced, in the context of signed GKM graphs, in

Lemma 2.6
.

Given an abstract unsigned GKM graph,
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon(e))_{e\in E}
and

as defined by (
1
) and (

) are a sign respectively invariant function.

Proof.

The two conditions

\widetilde{\alpha}(e^{\prime})=\widetilde{\alpha}(\nabla_{\overline{e}}\nabla_{e}e^{\prime})=\langle\varepsilon(\overline{e}),\nabla_{e}e^{\prime}\rangle\widetilde{\alpha}(\nabla_{e}e^{\prime})+\langle c(\overline{e}),\nabla_{e}e^{\prime}\rangle\widetilde{\alpha}(\overline{e}),

which, comparing with (

This shows that

\varepsilon(\overline{e})=\sum_{e^{\prime}}\langle\varepsilon(\overline{e}),\nabla_{e}e^{\prime}\rangle\nabla_{e}e^{\prime}=\nabla_{e}\sum_{e^{\prime}}\langle\varepsilon(e),e^{\prime}\rangle e^{\prime}=\nabla_{e}\varepsilon(e),

(2^{\prime})
.
∎

Using this extension we can define an injective function

By the assumption on the Betti numbers of the

4.
Extension for unsigned GKM graphs

In this section we generalize the group of axial functions, introduced by Kuroki
[
ku-19
]
for signed GKM graphs, to unsigned GKM graphs. We generalize the extension criterion for a signed GKM graph from
[
ku-19
]
, see Theorem
4.16
below. Recall from Section

that a constant sign function
\varepsilon\equiv 1
corresponds to the signed case; by allowing arbitrary sign functions we treat the signed and unsigned cases simultaneously.

4.1.
Group of label functions

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph with a connection
\nabla
, equipped with a sign function
\varepsilon
and an invariant function
c
(cf. Definition
2.5
). Define
L(\Gamma)
to be the group of functions
a:E\to\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

(3)

a(\nabla_{e}e^{\prime})=\langle\varepsilon(e),e^{\prime}\rangle\cdot a(e^{\prime})+\langle c(e),e^{\prime}\rangle\cdot a(e)

for all edges
e,e^{\prime}
at any vertex
v\in V
.
We call
L(\Gamma)
the
group of label functions
for
\Gamma
.

In particular, putting
e^{\prime}=e
in (
3
) we obtain, using
\langle\varepsilon(e),e\rangle=1
and
\langle c(e),e\rangle=-2
, that

(4)

a(\overline{e})=-a(e)

holds for all edges
e\in E
.

Recall that a set of linearly independent elements
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
in a lattice
L
is called a
primitive set in
L
if the rational vector space
V
spanned by
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
in
L\otimes\mathbb{Q}
satisfies that
V\cap L
equals the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
.
It is a well-known fact that a subset of
L
can be extended to a lattice basis of
L
if and only if it is primitive.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be an unsigned GKM graph, with a given lift
\widetilde{\alpha}:E\to\mathbb{Z}^{k}
of the axial function
\alpha
, and associated sign and invariant functions
\varepsilon
and
c
(see Section
2
). Then the components
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}:E\to\mathbb{Z}
of
\widetilde{\alpha}
,
i=1,\ldots,k
, are elements of
L(\Gamma)
. Furthermore:

(1)

\Gamma
is almost effective if and only if these elements are linearly independent in
L(\Gamma)
.

(2)

\Gamma
is effective if and only if
\{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\alpha}_{k}\}
forms a primitive set in
L(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Equation (
1
) says that

\widetilde{\alpha}(\nabla_{e}e^{\prime})=\langle\varepsilon(e),e^{\prime}\rangle\cdot\widetilde{\alpha}(e^{\prime})+\langle c(e),e^{\prime}\rangle\cdot\widetilde{\alpha}(e),

holds for all edges
e,e^{\prime}
at any vertex
v\in V
, where
\nabla
is the connection on
\Gamma
.
The first claim then follows directly by considering the
k
components of this equation.

The condition that the elements
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i},\ i=1,\dots,k
, are linearly dependent in
L(\Gamma)
is equivalent to the existence of integer constants
c_{i}
such that
\sum_{i}c_{i}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}=0
. But this is equivalent to the condition that
\sum_{i}c_{i}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}(e)=0
holds for each edge
e\in E
, i.e., that there is a linear equation in
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
that is satisfied by every element in the image of
\widetilde{\alpha}
. In turn, this is the same as the axial function
\alpha
not being almost effective.

Assuming that the collection
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,k
, does not form a primitive set in
L(\Gamma)
, we find a rational
k\times k
-matrix
C=(c_{ij})
whose inverse
C^{-1}
has only integer entries and determinant
|\det C^{-1}|\neq\pm 1
, such that the elements
\beta_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{ij}\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}
,
i=1,\ldots,k
, form a primitive set in
L(\Gamma)
. But then
\beta:=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k})\colon E\to\mathbb{Z}^{k}
is an axial function as well; this shows that the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of the image of
\alpha
is a proper sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, i.e.,
\alpha
is not effective. Conversely, we argue in the same way: if
\alpha
is not effective, we find a rational matrix
C
of the same type which sends an integer basis of the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of
{\mathrm{im}}\,\alpha
to the standard basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Then, putting
\beta_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{ij}\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}\in L(\Gamma)
, the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of the
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}
is a proper sublattice of the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of the
\beta_{i}
. Consequently, the
\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}
cannot form a primitive set in
L(\Gamma)
.
∎

4.2.

The idea in

Definition 4.4

(Compare with

Lemma 4.5

The groups of label and axial functions are isomorphic, via the homomorphism
\Phi:L(\Gamma)\to A(\Gamma)
defined by

\Phi(a)_{v}:=\sum_{e\in\str v}a(e)\cdot e.

Its inverse is given by

\Phi^{-1}(f)(e):=\langle f_{i(e)},e\rangle

of (the lift of) an axial function of a GKM graph (cf. Lemma

Let us show that for

\displaystyle\Phi(a)_{t(e)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e^{\prime}\in\str i(e)}a(\nabla_{e}e^{\prime})\cdot\nabla_{e}e^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e^{\prime}\in\str i(e)}\langle\varepsilon(e),e^{\prime}\rangle\cdot a(e^{\prime})\cdot\nabla_{e}e^{\prime}+\langle c(e),e^{\prime}\rangle\cdot a(e)\cdot\nabla_{e}e^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{e}\biggl(\varepsilon(e)\cdot\Phi(a)_{i(e)}+\langle\Phi(a)_{i(e)},e\rangle\cdot c(e)\biggr).

\displaystyle=\langle\varepsilon(e),e^{\prime}\rangle\cdot\langle f_{i(e)},e^{\prime}\rangle+\langle f_{i(e)},e\rangle\cdot\langle c(e),e^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\varepsilon(e),e^{\prime}\rangle\cdot a(e^{\prime})+\langle c(e),e^{\prime}\rangle\cdot a(e).

Theorem 4.16

For any unsigned

-type GKM graph

The same statement is true for signed GKM graphs.

Proof.

We are given an axial function

\widetilde{\alpha}
, with associated sign and invariant functions
\varepsilon
and

. The signed case corresponds to the constant sign function

. Consequently, the label function group

and the axial function group

are defined. The components
\widetilde{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\alpha}_{k}
of
\widetilde{\alpha}
define linearly independent elements in

by Lemma
4.2
.
Given an
(n,q)
-type extension of
\alpha
, the same lemma implies that also its components (respectively those of its lift that extends the lift
\widetilde{\alpha}
) define

, hence
\rk A(\Gamma)\geqslant q
.

For the converse direction, assuming that
\rk L(\Gamma)\geqslant q
, we find
\widetilde{\alpha}_{k+1},\ldots,\widetilde{\alpha}_{q}\in L(\Gamma)
such that
\widetilde{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\alpha}_{q}

Let us assume additionally that

-span of the image of

. Hence, we obtain a split short exact sequence

yielding an isomorphism

; combining with an isomorphism

we obtain an isomorphism

to obtain another (lift of an) axial function, whose first

∎

Definition 5.1
.

A morphism of graphs is called a

if it is a covering of the underlying topological spaces.
Given GKM-graphs
\widetilde{\Gamma}
,
\Gamma

if

\widetilde{\alpha}(e)=\alpha(f(e)),\ \nabla_{f(e)}(f(e^{\prime}))=f(\nabla_{e}(e^{\prime})),

hold for all edges

Remark 5.2
.

A GKM cover is nothing other than a GKM fiber bundle
[
gu-sa-za-12
]
whose fiber has no edges.

Given a
\GKM_{3}
manifold
M
with orbit space

, let
p\colon\widetilde{Q_{2}}\to Q_{2}
be the universal cover of the orbit space of the
2
-skeleton of the action. The underlying graph of the GKM graph

is homeomorphic to

, hence naturally a subset of

. The preimage
\widetilde{Q_{1}}:=p^{-1}(Q_{1})
then is a graph as well, and the restriction of

to

Lemma 5.3
.

We can lift the GKM graph structure
\Gamma
on

via

to obtain a GKM graph

with (possibly infinite) underlying graph

Proof.

The map
\widetilde{\alpha}:=\alpha\circ p
is an axial function on
\widetilde{Q_{1}}
.
The cover
p
over any

of

(i.e. a
2

\Gamma
) is trivial, because

is a contractible space.
This establishes the triviality of the covering over any
2
-face.
Therefore, we can define the connection on
\widetilde{Q_{1}}
by lifting the connection of
\Gamma

In particular, the congruence relation for
\widetilde{\Gamma}
follows by considering any lift of any triple

,

,

of consecutive edges in any

Therefore,

is a GKM graph.
Clearly, the induced map

∎

Let

be the deck transformation group of the covering

. The group

acts on the graph

by automorphisms.
Therefore,

as follows: for

(g\cdot f)_{v}:=f_{g^{-1}v},\ v\in V_{\widetilde{\Gamma}},\ g\in D,\ f\in A(\widetilde{\Gamma}).

This action is a representation

The map

Proof.

We compare two respective subgroups of

The inclusion

The following is a simple but important observation that is used in the proof below.

Lemma 5.5
.

Any homomorphism of a perfect group into an abelian group is trivial.

We prove Theorem
4
.

Theorem 5.6
.

The GKM graph of any
\GKM_{4}
manifold of complexity one admits an extension to a torus graph.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
be the GKM graph of a GKM
4
manifold
M
of complexity one.
By Lemma
5.3
we may consider the GKM covering
\pi\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
with deck transformation group
D=\pi_{1}(Q_{2})
. As
M
is of complexity one, by Lemma
4.2
we find
n-1
linearly independent elements in
A(\Gamma)
, which by Lemma
5.4
yield
n-1
linearly independent
D
-invariant elements
f_{1},\dots,f_{n-1}\in A(\widetilde{\Gamma})
.

As by construction the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
is generated by conjugated
2
-faces, and as by the GKM
4
assumption the monodromy along any
2
-face is trivial on transversal edges, Theorem
4.19
implies that
\widetilde{\Gamma}
admits an extension to a torus graph. (Note that
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is potentially an infinite graph, but see Remark
4.22
. The condition of finite length for any
2
-face in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is satisfied by Lemma
5.3
.) So we know from Lemma
4.2
that
A(\widetilde{\Gamma})
has rank
n
. We may therefore choose
f_{n}\in A(\widetilde{\Gamma})
such that the collection
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
is a basis of
A(\widetilde{\Gamma})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.

We wish to prove that the representation
R
of
D
on
A(\widetilde{\Gamma})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is a trivial representation. For any
g\in D
let

g\cdot f_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(g)f_{i},\ a_{i}(g)\in\mathbb{Q}.

The matrix of the linear operator
R(g)
in the basis
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
differs from the identity matrix only in the
n
-th column, which consists of the entries
a_{i}(g)
. As the group
D
is perfect by Corollary
3.6
, the homomorphism

\det\circ R:D\to\mathbb{Q}^{\times}:=\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\},

into the multiplicative group
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
is trivial by Lemma
5.5
. Hence

a_{n}(g)=\det R(g)=1,\ g\in D.

We claim that
a_{i}\colon D\to\mathbb{Q}
is a homomorphism into the additive group
\mathbb{Q}
for any

.
Indeed, for any
g,h\in D
one has

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(gh)f_{i}=g\cdot(h\cdot f_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}(h)g\cdot f_{i}+g\cdot f_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(h)f_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(g)f_{i}.

Here we used the
D
-invariance property of
f_{1},\dots,f_{n-1}
.
Then Lemma
5.5
implies that
a_{i}=0
for all
i=1,\dots,n-1
.
Therefore,
f_{n}
is
D
-invariant, and the representation
R
is trivial.
By Lemma
5.4
we conclude that
\rk A(\Gamma)=n
.
Now the claim on the extension to a torus graph for
\Gamma
follows directly from Theorem
4.16
.
∎

Partially hyperbolic flows on flat vector bundles with an application to complete affine manifolds. Abstract
. Let N N be a manifold of dimension m m with a flat vector bundle given by a representation ρ : π 1 ​ ( N ) → 𝖦𝖫 ⁡ ( n , ℝ ) \rho:\pi_{1}(N)\rightarrow{\mathsf{GL}}(n,{\mathbb{R}}) where π 1 ​ ( N ) \pi_{1}(N) is finitely generated. The holonomy group ρ \rho is a k k -partially hyperbolic holonomy representation if the flat bundle pulled back over the unit tangent bundle of a sufficiently large compact submanifold of N N splits into expanding, neutral, and contracting subbundles along the geodesic flow, where the expanding and contracting subbundles are k k -dimensional with k < n / 2 k<n/2 . Suppose that each element of ρ ​ ( π 1 ​ ( N ) ) \rho(\pi_{1}(N)) has an eigenvalue of norm 1 1 , or, alternatively, each element of it has a singular value that is uniformly bounded above and below. We show that ρ \rho is a P P -Anosov representation for a parabolic subgroup P P of 𝖦𝖫 ⁡ ( n , ℝ ) {\mathsf{GL}}(n,{\mathbb{R}}) if and only if ρ \rho is a partially hyperbolic representation. We are going to primarily employ representation theory techniques. As an application, we will show this holds when N N is a complete affine n n -manifold and ρ \rho is a linear part of the holonomy representation. This had never been done over the full general linear group.

1.2.
Main result

In this paper, we establish connections between the partial hyperbolicity in the context of dynamical systems and the P-Anosov properties.

Unlike the abstract dominated splitting discussed in many previous articles such as
[
5
]
and
[
30
]
, here we require actual splitting of smooth bundles and smooth flows, along with the presence of expanding and contracting properties in addition to the domination property.

We need the following objects
since we need to work on manifolds and not just on groups.

Definition 1.1

(Partial hyperbolicity)
.

Suppose that
M
is a compact Riemannian manifold with convex boundary.
Let
\hat{M}
be a regular cover of
M
with the deck transformation group
G_{M}
.
A representation
\rho:G_{M}\rightarrow{\mathsf{GL}}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
is
partially hyperbolic in a bundle sense
over
M
if
the following hold for a Riemannian metric on
M
:

(i)

There exist nontrivial
C^{0}
-subbundles
{\mathbb{V}}_{+},{\mathbb{V}}_{0}
, and
{\mathbb{V}}_{-}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\rho}

with fibers

{\mathbb{V}}_{+}(x),{\mathbb{V}}_{0}(x),{\mathbb{V}}_{-}(x)\subset{\mathbb{V}}(x)

for the fiber
{\mathbb{V}}(x)
of
{\mathbb{V}}
over each point
x
of
\mathbf{UC}M

invariant under the flow
\Phi_{t}
of
{\mathbb{V}}
over
the geodesic flow
\phi_{t}
of
\mathbf{UC}M
.

(ii)

{\mathbb{V}}_{+}(x),{\mathbb{V}}_{0}(x)
and
{\mathbb{V}}_{-}(x)
for

are independent subspaces, and their sum equals
{\mathbb{V}}(x)
.

(iii)

For any fiberwise metric on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\rho}
over
\mathbf{UC}M
,
the lifted action of
\Phi_{t}
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{+}
(resp.
{\mathbb{V}}_{-}

That is, for coefficients

(a)

\left|\mkern-2.0mu\left|\Phi_{-t}(\mathbf{v})\right|\mkern-2.0mu\right|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\rho},\phi_{-t}(m)}\leq A\exp(-at)\left|\mkern-2.0mu\left|\mathbf{v}\right|\mkern-2.0mu\right|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\rho},m}
for

as

.

(b)

\left|\mkern-2.0mu\left|\Phi_{t}(\mathbf{v})\right|\mkern-2.0mu\right|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\rho},{\phi_{t}(m)}}\leq A\exp(-at)\left|\mkern-2.0mu\left|\mathbf{v}\right|\mkern-2.0mu\right|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\rho},m}
for
\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{-}(m))
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

(c)

(Dominance properties)

(1)

\frac{\left|\mkern-2.0mu\left|\Phi_{t}(\mathbf{w})\right|\mkern-2.0mu\right|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\rho},\phi_{t}(m)}}{\left|\mkern-2.0mu\left|\Phi_{t}(\mathbf{v})\right|\mkern-2.0mu\right|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\rho},\phi_{t}(m)}}\leq A^{\prime}\exp(-a^{\prime}t)\frac{\left|\mkern-2.0mu\left|\mathbf{w}\right|\mkern-2.0mu\right|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\rho},m}}{\left|\mkern-2.0mu\left|\mathbf{v}\right|\mkern-2.0mu\right|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\rho},m}}\\
\hbox{ for }\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{+}(m),\mathbf{w}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}(m)\hbox{ as }t\rightarrow\infty,\\
\hbox{ or for }\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}(m),\mathbf{w}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{-}(m)\hbox{ as }t\rightarrow\infty.

We additionally require that
\dim{\mathbb{V}}_{+}=\dim{\mathbb{V}}_{-}\geq 1
.
Here

of

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N
be an
m
-manifold with a finitely generated fundamental group.
Let
\rho:\pi_{1}(N)\rightarrow{\mathsf{GL}}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
be a representation.
Assume one of the following:

•

|\log\lambda_{i_{g}}(\rho(g))|=1
for all
g\in\pi_{1}(M)
where
the index
i_{g}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
may depend on
g
.

•

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

|\log a_{j_{g}}(\rho(g))|<C
for all
g\in\pi_{1}(M)
where
the index
j_{g}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
may depend on
g
.

Then
\rho
is
P
-Anosov for
any parabolic subgroup
P
of
{\mathsf{GL}}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
with

P=P_{\theta}
for
\theta
containing
\log\lambda_{k}-\log\lambda_{k+1},k\leq n/2

if and only if
\rho
is partially hyperbolic of the index
k
in a bundle sense.
In these cases
k<n/2
.

The significance of this result is that we work with the full general linear group

{\mathsf{GL}}(n,\mathbb{R})
instead of orthogonal groups. Also, Anosov representations correspond to only dominated representations and not actual partially hyperbolic flows as here. Promoting the dominated representation to Anosov ones is trivial for some Lie groups but not for general linear groups.

We can ask if an Anosov representation is always partially hyperbolic up to multiplying by a positive scalar representation to
{\mathbb{R}}
. We cannot answer this question since
controlling the singular values of representation elements is very hard in general, and
the only related results that we know of are due to Abel-Margulis-Soifer
[
1
]
, which is not applicable here
as far as we know.

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose that
N
is a complete affine
n
-manifold with a finitely generated fundamental group.
Let
\rho:\pi_{1}(N)\rightarrow{\mathsf{GL}}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
be a linear part of the affine holonomy group.
Then the conclusions of Theorem
1.1
hold.

In the field of affine manifolds,
the hyperbolic flow was already discovered by Goldman and Labourie
[
21
]
for
3

[
15
]
, Ghosh-Treib
[
19
]
, and Danciger-Zhang
[
13
]
prove these properties for affine actions on higher-dimensional affine spaces. However, the linear parts are always assumed to be in the orthogonal groups or Hitchin, where the properties are more or less immediate.

We wish to approach the conjecture that a closed complete affine manifold cannot have a word hyperbolic fundamental group. One approach considered is using Osedelec-type theorems (See Chapter 8 of
[
31
]
). However, generalizing our results to measurable settings is currently hindered by numerous technical issues. Discussions on these matters took place with researchers such as Nicholas Tholozan at BIRS, Banff, during the winter of 2020.

1.3.
Some historical comments on complete affine manifolds

Originally, Milnor posed the question whether a free affine group of rank
\geq 2
could act freely and properly discontinuously on affine space. Margulis provided an affirmative answer by constructing examples in the affine
3
-space, where the linear parts belong to
{\mathsf{SO}}(2,1)
. Drumm and Goldman subsequently explained this result using fundamental domains in a series of papers (see
[
11
]
for a survey). Sullivan
[
44
]
observed that the linear part of each affine group element must have
1
as an eigenvalue and proved the Chern conjecture for complete affine manifolds
[
33
]
. Klingler
[
32
]
later extended this to closed special affine manifolds.

Further developments include Ghosh
[
15
,
17
,
16
,
18
]
, Ghosh-Treib
[
19
]
, and Smilga
[
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
,
42
]
, who generalized these results and produced higher-dimensional examples of proper and free affine actions. Abels, Margulis, and Soifer also contributed to related topics such as the Auslander conjecture (see Abels
[
2
]
for a survey). Danciger and Zhang
[
13
]
investigated affine deformations of Hitchin representations, showing that they do not exist. Danciger, Kassel, and Guéritaud
[
12
]
provided first examples of fundamental groups of closed manifolds acting properly and freely on affine spaces.

Although these works are related to the themes of this paper, their specific aims differ. This summary only scratches the surface of this rapidly evolving field.

2.2.
Gromov hyperbolic spaces

Let us recall some definitions:
A metric space is

a path that is an isometry from an interval to the metric space.
We will only work with geodesic metric spaces.
Let

(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces. A map

is called

(L,C^{\prime})

for

d_{Y}(f(x),f(x^{\prime})\leq Ld_{X}(x,x^{\prime})+C^{\prime}\hbox{ for all }x,x^{\prime}\in X.

A map

is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isomeric embedding
if

satisfies

L^{-1}d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})-C\leq d_{Y}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq Ld_{X}(x,x^{\prime})+C\hbox{ for all }x,x^{\prime}\in X.

A map

is a

-coarse inverse
of

d_{X}(\bar{f}\circ f,{\rm I}_{X})\leq C,\hbox{ and }d_{Y}(f\circ\bar{f},{\rm I}_{Y})\leq C.

A map

between metric spaces is called
a

if it is a coarse Lipschitz map and admits a
coarse Lipschitz coarse inverse map.
(Note that these are not necessarily continuous, as we follow

]

3.1.
Some background

We have to repeat some of the standard materials from
Section 2.2 of
[
24
]

[
26
]

Consider a real reductive Lie group

with a maximal compact subgroup

We assume that

for simplicity.
For example, this holds if

is connected. We can usually reduce everything here to
the case where

Here,

is an almost product of

and

is the identity component of the center
Z(G)
of
G

and

A parabolic subgroup

P
of the form

for some algebraic subgroup
\mathbf{P}
of an algebraic group
\mathbf{G}

Let
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
denote the Lie algebra of

,
and
\mathfrak{g}_{s}
the Lie algebra of
G_{s}
.
Then
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{s}
.
Letting
\mathfrak{t}
denote the Lie algebra of
K
,
we have
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{q}

where
\mathfrak{q}
is the
-1
-eigenspace of an involution
\theta
preserving

\mathfrak{t}
as the
1
-eigenspace.
Let
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{q}
be the maximal abelian subalgebra, i.e., the Cartan subalgebra of

\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathfrak{q}\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{s}

where
\mathfrak{a}_{s}=\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{s}

.

We have a decomposition to

-eigenspaces

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
Here,

\mathfrak{g}_{0}

and the centralizer of
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathfrak{g}_{s}
.
The set
\Sigma\subset\mathfrak{a}^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathbb{R})

and each

is called a restricted root of
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
There is a subset

with coefficients all of the same sign;

There is a subset

We define

as the parabolic subgroup with the Lie algebra

\operatorname{Lie}\left(P_{\theta}\right)=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}\backslash\Sigma_{\theta}^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}.

The connected components of

of
\mathfrak{a}
.
The component where every
\alpha\in\Sigma_{+}

\mathfrak{a}^{+}
.

Let
G={\mathsf{SL}}_{\pm}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
.
The maximal abelian subalgebra

A positive Weyl chamber is given by

and we are given

\displaystyle\Sigma^{+}=\{\log\lambda_{i}-\log\lambda_{j}|i<j\}\hbox{ and }

\displaystyle\Delta=\{\alpha_{i}:=\log\lambda_{i}-\log\lambda_{i+1}|i=1,\dots,n-1\}.

[
24
]

5.
Complete affine manifolds

Let

be a Lie group acting transitively and faithfully on
a space

A
(G,X)
-structure
on a manifold
N
is a maximal atlas of charts to
X
so that
the transition maps are in
G
.
This is equivalent to

having a pair
({\bf dev},h)
where

\hat{N}
is a regular cover of
N
with a deck transformation group
\Gamma_{N}

such that

•

h:\Gamma_{N}\rightarrow G
is a homomorphism, called a
holonomy homomorphism
, and

•

{\bf dev}:\hat{N}\rightarrow X
is an immersion,
called a
developing map
, satisfying

{\bf dev}\circ\gamma=h(\gamma)\circ{\bf dev}

for each deck transformation
\gamma\in\Gamma_{N}
.

We say that an
m
-manifold
N

with a
(G,X)
-structure is

if
{\bf dev}:\hat{N}\rightarrow X
is a diffeomorphism.
If
N
is complete,
we have a diffeomorphism

N\rightarrow X/h(\Gamma_{N})
, and
h(\Gamma_{N})
acts properly discontinuously and freely on
X
. Complete
(G,X)
-structures on
N
are classified
by conjugacy classes of representations
\Gamma_{N}\rightarrow G
with properly discontinuous and free actions on
X
.
(See Section 3.4 of
[
46
]
.)

Note that the completeness and compactness of
N
have
no relation for some

-structures
unless

is in the isometry group of

with a Riemannian metric.
(The Hopf-Rinow lemma may fail.)

Now, we move on to the affine structures.
Let

Let

An affine

-manifold is an

-manifold

is an

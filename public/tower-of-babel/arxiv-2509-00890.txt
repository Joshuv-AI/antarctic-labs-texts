New approximate distance oracles and their applications
Avi Kadria, Liam Roditty

Abstract
Let G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) be an undirected graph with n n vertices and m m edges, and let μ = m / n \mu=m/n . A distance oracle is a data structure designed to answer approximate distance queries, with the goal of achieving low stretch, efficient space usage, and fast query time. While much of the prior work focused on distance oracles with constant query time, this paper presents a comprehensive study of distance oracles with non-constant query time. We explore the tradeoffs between space, stretch, and query time of distance oracles in various regimes. Specifically, we consider both weighted and unweighted graphs in the regimes of stretch < 2 <2 and stretch ≥ 2 \geq 2 . In addition, we demonstrate several applications of our new distance oracles to the n n -Pairs Shortest Paths ( n n -PSP) problem and the All Nodes Shortest Cycles ( A ​ N ​ S ​ C ANSC ) problem. Our main contributions are: • Weighted graphs: We present a new three-way trade-off between stretch, space, and query time, offering a natural extension of the classical Thorup–Zwick distance oracle [STOC’01 and JACM’05] to regimes with larger query time. Specifically, for any 0 < r < 1 / 2 0<r<1/2 and integer k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , we construct a ( 2 ​ k ​ ( 1 − 2 ​ r ) − 1 ) (2k(1-2r)-1) -stretch distance oracle with O ~ ​ ( m + n 1 + 1 / k ) \tilde{O}(m+n^{1+1/k}) space and O ~ ​ ( μ ​ n r ) \tilde{O}(\mu n^{r}) query time. We also improve upon a result of Dalirrooyfard et al. Recently, Dalirrooyfard, Jin, V. Williams, and Wein
[

The

-PSP problem is defined as follows. Given a set of vertex pairs
(s_{i},t_{i})

1\leq i\leq O(n)
, compute the distance
d(s_{i},t_{i})
.
The
ANSC

v

A straightforward approach for solving the
n
-PSP problem is to first construct a distance oracle and then query it for every pair in the input set.
The total runtime of this approach is the sum of the construction time and
n
times the query time of the distance oracle.
While this method naturally yields an
n
-PSP algorithm, a distance oracle must satisfy additional requirements that an
n
-PSP algorithm does not.
In particular, a distance oracle must use limited space and support queries between any of the
\Omega(n^{2})
pairs of vertices.

Using our new distance oracles, we obtain improved time/stretch tradeoffs for both the
n
-PSP problem and the
ANSC

[
DJWW22
,
CHL25
]
.

In
[

[

Lemma 5

3
Technical overview

In the classic distance oracle of Thorup and Zwick
[
TZ05
]
, it is possible to bound

h_{i}(u)
with
d(u,v)+h_{i-1}(v)
, for every
1\leq i\leq k-1
.
The key to improving the
(2k-1)
-stretch of the distance oracle lies in tightening the bound on
h_{i}(u)
.
Previous work (see, for example,
[
AGH11
,
PR14
,
AG11
,
AG13
,
Aga14
]
) improved the bound on
h_{1}(u)
from
d(u,v)
to
d(u,v)/2
by exploiting the following structural property of
B_{0}(\cdot)
: if
P(u,v)\not\subseteq B_{0}(u)\cup B_{0}(v)
, then
h_{1}(u)+h_{1}(v)\leq d(u,v)+1_{\textsc{odd}}
.

In this paper, we develop two new techniques that exploit structural properties of
C(\cdot)
to construct a distance oracle with improved stretch guarantees. These techniques enable us to bound not only
h_{1}(u)
by
d(u,v)/2
, but also
h_{2}(u)
by
d(u,v)
, thereby improving overall stretch.
We begin by presenting the borderline vertices technique, which applies to both weighted and unweighted graphs.

3.1
The borderline vertices technique. [
Section 4.1
]

Let
C^{*}(u)=C(u)\cup N(C(u))
be an augmented cluster.
Roughly speaking, using the borderline vertices technique we show that if
P(u,v)\not\subseteq C^{*}(u,A_{c})\cap C^{*}(v,A_{c})
then
\max(h_{c+1}(u),h_{c+1}(v))\leq d(u,v)
. In particular, by setting
c=1
we get an improvement over the result of
[
AGH11
]
, showing not only that
h_{1}(u)\leq d(u,v)/2
but also that
h_{2}(u)\leq d(u,v)
.

Let
P=P(u,v)
be a shortest path between
u
and
v
.
The borderline vertex
\tau_{u}(P)
of
C^{*}(u)
in
P
is the farthest vertex from
u
in
C^{*}(u)\cap P
. Similarly, the borderline vertex

\tau_{v}(P)
of
C^{*}(u)
in
P
is the farthest vertex from
v
in
C^{*}(v)\cap P
.
Obviously, a distance oracle cannot store
\tau_{u}(P)
for every shortest path
P
, as this would require
\Theta(n^{2})
space.
We overcome this limitation by using
\tilde{O}(\mu\cdot|C(u)|)
query time to iterate over all vertices in the augmented cluster
C^{*}(u)
, and in particular
\tau_{u}=\tau_{u}(P)
.

To obtain our improved bound on
h_{2}(v)
, we analyze two different cases regarding
p_{1}(\tau_{u})
. The case that
p_{1}(\tau_{u})\in B_{1}(v)
and the case that
p_{1}(\tau_{u})\notin B_{1}(v)
.
If
p_{1}(\tau_{u})\in B_{1}(v)
, then the query algorithm can return as an estimation
\hat{d}(u,v)=u\rightarrow\tau_{u}\rightarrow p_{1}(\tau_{u})\rightarrow v
, where
x\rightarrow y\rightarrow z\rightarrow w
is
d(x,y)+d(y,z)+d(z,w)
.
In this case, since
\tau_{u}\notin C(u,A_{1})
(as it is the farthest vertex from
u
in
C^{*}(u)
) we get that

5
Distance oracles in dense unweighted graphs

In this section, we first show that when applying the borderline vertices technique in unweighted graphs, we can avoid paying the
\mu
factor in the query time at a small price of bounding
h_{2}(u)
with
d(u,v)+2
instead of
d(u,v)
.
We then develop a new technique specifically for the case of unweighted graphs, called
the middle vertex technique
, that allows us to bound
h_{2}(u)
with
d(u,v)+1_{\textsc{odd}}
instead of
2d(u,v)
.
This technique is based on certain properties of a special vertex
\tau
that lies in the middle of the shortest path between
u
and
v
. More specifically, we show that either
\tau\in C(u)\cap C(v)
or

p_{1}(\tau)\in B_{1}(u)\cap B_{1}(v)
or
h_{2}(u)\leq d(u,v)+1_{\textsc{odd}}
.
We then present several new small stretch distance oracles that are based on the middle vertex technique.

5.1
Toolbox

We now prove several properties of bunches.
These lemmas were proven in various previous works (see, for example,
[

We prove these properties with respect to a set

but we remark that these properties hold for

Let
u,v\in V

In the next two lemmas, we prove useful properties of

in unweighted graphs. When

Lemma 12
.

If
B(u)\cap B(v)\neq\emptyset
then
P\cap(B(u)\cap B(v))\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
x\in B(u)\cap B(v)
.
Let
u^{\prime}
be the farthest vertex from
u
in
P\cap B(u)
. Since the graph is unweighted it holds for every

Assume towards a contradiction that

Since

d(u,v)\geq h(u)-1+h(v)

On the other hand, from the triangle inequality, it follows that
d(u,v)\leq d(u,x)+d(x,v)

Since

and the graph is unweighted we have that
d(u,x)\leq h(u)-1

we have
d(v,x)\leq h(v)-1
.
Thus, we get that
d(u,v)\leq d(u,x)+d(x,v)\leq h(u)-1+h(v)-1
.
In contradiction to the fact that
d(u,v)\geq h(u)-1+h(v)

Therefore,
u^{\prime}\in B(v)

P\cap(B(u)\cap B(v))\neq\emptyset

Lemma 13

In this section, we extend our second technique, of computing

7.1
Toolbox

Let

be a weighted undirected graph with real non-negative edges.
Let
\mu=2m/n
, be the average degree of the graph.
For every

B^{*}(u)=N(B(u))\cup B(u)

Agarwal, Godfrey, and Har-Peled
[

showed that in the case of distance oracles in weighted graphs that use

space, it suffices to consider graphs with maximum degree
\mu
.
Thus, for the rest of this section, we assume that the maximum degree of

is at most

.
The next lemma follows from the discussion above.

Lemma 40
.

Let
u\in V
.
|B^{*}(u)|\leq(1+\mu)|B(u)|
.

Proof.

Since the maximum degree of
G
is bounded by
\mu
, we know that
|N(B(u))|\leq\mu\cdot|B(u)|
. From the definition of
B^{*}(u)
, it follows that
|B^{*}(u)|=|B(u)\cup N(B(u))|\leq|B(u)|+|N(B(u))|

|N(B(u))|\leq\mu|B(u)|

|B^{*}(u)|\leq|B(u)|+|N(B(u))|\leq|B(u)|+\mu|B(u)|=(1+\mu)|B(u)|

∎

Let

, and let

in

d(u,u^{\prime})\geq h(u)

Assume towards a contradiction, that

u^{\prime}\in B(u)

u^{\prime}

u^{\prime}
is the farthest from

Lemma 42

If

If

Otherwise, we know that

From Lemma

d(v,v^{\prime})\geq h(v)

it follows that
d_{t}(u,x)=d(u,x)
and
d_{t}(v,x)=d(v,x)
.
Since
P_{t}(u)\subseteq S_{t}(u)
and
P_{t}(v)\subseteq S_{t}(v)
the value returned by the call to
\texttt{Intersection}(u,v,S_{t}(u),S_{t}(v))
is at most

d_{t}(u,x)+d_{t}(v,x)=d(u,x)+d(x,v)=d(u,v)
, where the last equality follows from the fact that
x\in P
, and the claim holds.

Consider now the case that
P_{t}(u)\cap P_{t}(v)=\emptyset
. From Lemma
47
it follows that
\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}h(u_{i})+h(v_{i})\leq d(u,v)
.

Let
Q=\{\langle u_{i},v_{t-1-i}\rangle\mid 0\leq i\leq t-1\}
. For each
q\in Q
, let
q_{u}
(resp.
q_{v}
) be the first (resp. second) element of the pair.
By summing over the set
Q
we get that
\sum_{q\in Q}(h(q_{u})+h(q_{v}))=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}(h(u_{i})+h(v_{i}))
. Since
\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}(h(u_{i})+h(v_{i}))\leq d(u,v)
we get that
\sum_{q\in Q}h(q_{u})+h(q_{v})=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}(h(u_{i})+h(v_{i}))\leq d(u,v)
.

Let
q\in Q
be the pair with minimum value of
h(q_{u})+h(q_{v})
.
By the minimality of
q
we get
h(q_{u})+h(q_{v})\leq(\sum_{q\in Q}h(q_{u})+h(q_{v}))/t\leq d(u,v)/t
.

The algorithm iterates over all the possible pairs of
Q
and encounters
q
. Thus:

\hat{d}(u,v)\leq d(u,q_{u})+d(q_{u},p(q_{u}))+d(p(q_{u}),p(q_{v}))+d(p(q_{v}),q_{v})+d(q_{v},v)

From the triangle inequality, we know that
d(p(q_{u}),p(q_{v}))\leq h(q_{u})+d(q_{u},q_{v})+h(q_{v})
. Thus:

\displaystyle\hat{d}(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq d(u,q_{u})+d(q_{u},p(q_{u}))+d(p(q_{u}),p(q_{v}))+d(p(q_{v}),q_{v})+d(q_{v},v)

\displaystyle\leq d(u,q_{u})+h(q_{u})+(h(q_{u})+d(q_{u},q_{v})+h(q_{v}))+h(q_{v})+d(q_{v},v)

\displaystyle=d(u,q_{u})+d(q_{u},q_{v})+d(q_{v},v)+2(h(q_{u})+h(q_{v}))

Since
P_{t}(u)\cap P_{t}(v)=\emptyset
we get that when going from
u
to
v
on
P
we first encounter all
u_{i}
’s and then all
v_{i}
’s. Thus we get
d(u,q_{u})+d(q_{u},q_{v})+d(q_{v},v)=d(u,v)
. Since
d(u,q_{u})+d(q_{u},q_{v})+d(q_{v},v)=d(u,v)
we get that
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq d(u,v)+2(h(q_{u})+h(q_{v}))
.

Since
h(q_{u})+h(q_{v})\leq d(u,v)/t
, it follows that
\hat{d}(u,v)\leq d(u,v)+2(h(q_{u})+h(q_{v}))\leq(1+2/t)d(u,v)
, as required.
∎

8.1
Improved algorithms for
n
-pairs shortest paths problem (

All of our algorithms for the
n-PSP
problem follow the following framework. We construct a distance oracle and then query it
n
times. Therefore, the runtime of the resulting algorithm is the construction time of the distance oracle plus
n
times the query time of the distance oracle.

For unweighted graphs, we use the distance oracle of
Theorem 9
to achieve the following theorem.

Reminder of
Theorem 11
.

Let
k\geq 2
. Given an unweighted graph
G
and vertex pairs
(s_{i},t_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq O(n)
, there is an algorithm for
n-PSP
that computes an estimation
\hat{d}(s_{i},t_{i})
such that
d(s_{i},t_{i})\leq\hat{d}(s_{i},t_{i})\leq d(s_{i},t_{i})+2\left\lceil\frac{d(s_{i},t_{i})}{2k}\right\rceil
in
\tilde{O}(m^{1-\frac{1}{k+1}}n)
time.

Proof.

We construct the distance oracle of
Theorem 9
and query it for every
(s_{i},t_{i})
.
From
Theorem 9
constructing the distance oracle takes
\tilde{O}(mn^{1-c})
time. Querying the distance oracle
n
times takes
\tilde{O}(n\cdot n^{ck})=\tilde{O}(n^{1+ck})
time.
Therefore, we get a running time of
\tilde{O}(mn^{1-c}+n^{1+ck})
, and by setting
c=\frac{\log_{n}{m}}{k+1}
we get that
\tilde{O}(mn^{1-c}+n^{1+ck})=\tilde{O}(m^{1-\frac{1}{k+1}}n+m^{1-\frac{1}{k+1}}n)=\tilde{O}(m^{1-\frac{1}{k+1}}n)
, as required.
From
Theorem 9
we know that
\hat{d}(s_{i},t_{i})\leq d(s_{i},t_{i})+2\lceil d(s_{i},t_{i})/(kt)\rceil
, as required.
∎

In addition, the following simple theorem follows from using the spanner of
[
BK10
]
and the distance oracle of
Theorem 15
.

Reminder of
Theorem 12
.

Let
k\geq 3
. Given a weighted graph
G
and vertex pairs
(s_{i},t_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq O(n)
, there is an algorithm for
n-PSP
that computes an estimation
\hat{d}(s_{i},t_{i})
such that
d(s_{i},t_{i})\leq\hat{d}(s_{i},t_{i})\leq(2k-1)(2k-3)
in
\tilde{O}(m+n^{2/k})
time.

Proof.

We construct a
(2k-1)
-spanner
H
in
O(km)
time (
[
BK10
]
), with
O(n^{1+1/k})
edges. Then, we construct the distance oracle of
Theorem 15
for the graph
H
.
Constructing the distance oracle takes
\tilde{O}(|E(H)|n^{\frac{1}{k}})=\tilde{O}(n^{1+\frac{1}{k}}n^{\frac{1}{k}})=\tilde{O}(n^{1+2/k})
time.
Querying the distance oracle
n
times takes
\tilde{O}(n\cdot\frac{|E(H)|}{n}n^{\frac{1}{k}})=\tilde{O}(n^{1+\frac{2}{k}})
. Therefore, we conclude that the running time is
\tilde{O}(n^{1+\frac{2}{k}})
.

From
Theorem 15
we have that
\hat{d}(s_{i},t_{i})\leq(2k-3)d_{H}(s_{i},t_{i})
. From the fact that
H
is a
(2k-1)

d_{H}(s_{i},t_{i})\leq(2k-1)d(u,v)
. Overall, we get that

\hat{d}(s_{i},t_{i})\leq(2k-3)d_{H}(s_{i},t_{i})\leq(2k-3)(2k-1)d(u,v)
, as required.
∎

Appendix B
Simple lower bound for non-adjacent vertex pairs

In this section, we prove the following theorem.

Reminder of
Theorem 3
.

Assuming the Erdős girth conjecture, any distance oracle that uses
o(n^{1+1/k})
space must have an input graph
G=(V,E)
and two vertices
u,v\in V
such that
d(u,v)=2
and
\hat{d}(u,v)\geq 2k+2
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph with girth
\geq 2k+2
and
\Omega(n^{1+1/k})
edges, given from the Erdős conjecture.
For every
v\in V
, we add a vertex
v^{\prime}
and an edge
(v,v^{\prime})
to
G
.
Any distance oracle with
o(n^{1+1/k})
on
G
must have at an edge
(u,v)\in E
that is not preserved, and therefore
\hat{d}(u,v)>1
.

Let
d_{>1}(u,v)
be the second shortest distance between
u
and
v
in
G
.
Since the girth
\geq 2k+2
we know that
d_{>1}(u,v)\geq 2k+1
.
Since
\hat{d}(u,v)>1
and the distance oracle returns as an estimation a length of a path in
G
we get that
\hat{d}(u,v)\geq d_{>1}(u,v)\geq 2k+1
.
Moreover,
d_{>1}(u^{\prime},v)=1+d_{>1}(u^{\prime},v)\geq 2k+2
, and therefore
\hat{d}(u^{\prime},v)\geq 2k+2
, as required.
∎

Regularisation of cylindrical Lévy processes in Besov spaces
Matthew Griffiths, Markus Riedle

Abstract
In this work, we quantify the irregularity of a given cylindrical Lévy process L L in L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\R^{d}) by determining the range of weighted Besov spaces B B in which L L has a regularised version Y Y , that is a stochastic process Y Y in the classical sense with values in B B . Our approach is based on characterising Lévy measures on Besov spaces. As a by-product, we determine those Besov spaces B B for which the embedding of L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\R^{d}) into B B is 0 0 -Radonifying and p p -Radonifying for p > 1 p>1 .

60G20, 47B10, 60H25, 60G51, 60E07.

Keywords and Phrases:
cylindrical processes; generalised processes; Radonifying operators; regularisation; Besov spaces.

1
Introduction

Cylindrical Lévy processes are a natural generalisation of cylindrical Brownian motions or equivalently of Gaussian space-time white noises. Being cylindrical processes, they generally do not attain values in the underlying space. This fact may cause some surprising phenomena such as highly irregular paths of solutions for linear evolution equations driven by a cylindrical Lévy process
L
, which is observed for example by Brzeźniak and co-authors in
[
6
]
or Priola and Zabczyk in
[
24
]
, and which is related to the cylindrical distribution of
L
by Kumar and one of us in
[
22
]
.

In this work, we initiate a new type of research question by quantifying the irregularity of cylindrical Lévy processes in terms of its cylindrical distribution. For this purpose, we determine the range of Besov spaces
B
in which a given cylindrical Lévy process
L
in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\R^{d})
of square integrable functions becomes a Lévy process
Y
in the classical sense, i.e. a stochastic process attaining values in the Besov space
B
. In this case, the classical Lévy process
Y
is called the
regularised
version of
L
in
B
, a notion introduced by Itô and Nawata in the work
[
19
]
.
This approach is motivated by the following observation: by embedding the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\R^{d})
into the space
S^{\ast}(\R^{d})
of tempered distributions, each cylindrical Lévy process in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
becomes a classical Lévy process in
S^{\ast}(\R^{d})
due to Minlos’ theorem; see Fonseca-Mora
[
14
]
. Besov spaces lie between
L^{2}(\R^{d})
and
S^{\ast}(\R^{d})
, and thus offer a natural and sufficiently fine scale to quantify the irregularity of a cylindrical Lévy process
L
by determining those Besov spaces in which
L
becomes a classical Lévy process if they exist.

Besov spaces are a natural extension of Hölder-Zygmund and fractional Sobolev spaces, see Chapter 1 of the monograph
[
31
]
by Triebel for a comprehensive introduction, and have been extensively applied to measure smoothness of functions, e.g. of solutions of partial differential equations. In the probabilistic setting, they have been used to analyse regularity of sample paths for finite- and infinite dimensional stochastic processes, starting with the publications
[
17
]
by Herren and
[
29
]
by Schilling. However, our investigation is fundamentally different as we apply Besov spaces as a scale of spaces between
L^{2}(\R^{d})
and
S^{\ast}(\R^{d})
in which a cylindrical random variable in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
may become a classical random variable.

The line of research closest to ours originates from the consideration of image processing and appears in the publications
[
3
,
8
,
12
,
13
]
by Dalang, Unser, Fageot and co-authors, in which the authors investigate different aspects of a Lévy-type model in
S^{\ast}(\R^{d})
. In order to characterise local smoothness and the asymptotic growth in
[
3
]
,
they determine the Besov spaces in which the Lévy-type model attains values almost surely. Although this result is related to our investigation, it only applies to the subclass of cylindrical Lévy processes in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
which corresponds to the considered Lévy-type model in
S^{\ast}(\R)
; this subclass is shown to be very special in our previous work
[
16
]
, requiring the corresponding cylindrical Lévy processes to be stationary in time
and
in space.

In order to derive results on the regularisation of cylindrical Lévy processes in Besov spaces,
our first task is to characterise the Lévy measures in Besov spaces.
In most Banach spaces apart from Hilbert spaces, an explicit characterisation of Lévy measures is not known. One of the exceptions are Lévy measures on the sequence spaces
\ell^{p}
due to a result by Yurinskii in
[
34
]
.
Using the wavelet characterisation of Besov spaces, the result by Yurinskii will enable us to derive the characterisation of Lévy measures on Besov spaces.

Having characterised the Lévy measures on Besov spaces, we turn our attention to the regularisation question. Given a (non-Gaussian) cylindrical Lévy process
L
in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
, we give sharp results for when
L
becomes a classical Lévy process in a fixed Besov space
B
. Our technique is to study when the cylindrical Lévy measure of
L
may be extended to a
\sigma
-additive measure on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra in
B
which is a Lévy measure on
B
; in this case, we are then able to show the existence of a regularised version of
L
in
B
; see Definition
4.2
for the precise notion of the latter.

The result on the existence of a regularised version in a Besov space
B
of a cylindrical Lévy process in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
is
closely related to the so-called 0-Radonifying property of the embedding
\iota
of
L^{2}(\R^{d})
into
B
. Here,
\iota
is called
0
-Radonifying if the image of each cylindrical random variable in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
under
\iota
becomes a classical random variable in
B
. If this is the case, it is an easy conclusion, that each cylindrical Lévy process in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
has a regularised version in
B
. The result on the regularisation of cylindrical Lévy process and its application to specific examples enable us to exactly characterise those Besov spaces for which the embedding
\iota
is
0
-Radonifying
or
p
-Radonifying; the latter is a specialisation of
0
-Radonifying under some moment conditions.

Our definition of and the fundamental results we shall need regarding Besov spaces are presented in Section
2
.
In Section
3
, we characterise the Lévy measures on Besov spaces.
In Section
4
we
give a general characterisation of when there exists a regularised version in a specific Besov space for a cylindrical Lévy processes in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
. We finish the presentation in this section by characterising Radonifying embeddings of
L^{2}
into Besov spaces.
Finally, in Section
5
, we study in depth two important examples of cylindrical Lévy process: the canonical symmetric-
\alpha
-stable process, and the cylindrical Lévy process representable as an infinite sum of independent one-dimensional Lévy processes, which we call the
hedgehog process
. In both cases we present a full characterisation of the parameter set where the cylindrical Lévy process has and has not a regularised version in a specific Besov space.

2
Weighted Besov Spaces

The weighted Besov spaces
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
with
0<p\leqslant\infty
and
s,w\in\R
have many equivalent definitions, see
[
31
,
32
]
for a comprehensive treatment. We shall use a definition based upon wavelets.
As we are focused on separable reflexive Banach spaces in this work, we shall use the scale
1<p<\infty
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d})
denote the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing functions on
\R^{d}
, that is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d}):=\big\{f\in C^{\infty}(\R^{d})\colon\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{{\mathcal{S}}_{r}}<\infty\text{ for all }r\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+}\big\},

where the seminorms
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{{\mathcal{S}}_{r}}
,
r\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+}
, are defined by

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{{\mathcal{S}}_{r}}:=\max_{\left\lvert s\right\rvert\leqslant r}\sup_{x\in\R^{d}}(1+\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2})^{r}\left\lvert\partial^{s}f(x)\right\rvert,

(
2.1
)

with
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+}^{d}
and
\partial^{s}:=\partial^{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}/(\partial x_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{d}^{s_{d}})
.
With the topology generated by the family of seminorms
(\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{{\mathcal{S}}_{r}})_{r\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+}}
, the space
{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d})
is metrisable, and
f_{n}\to f
in
{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d})
means
\left\lVert f_{n}-f\right\rVert_{{\mathcal{S}}_{r}}\to 0
for each
r\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+}
.
The dual space of
{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d})
is the space
{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}(\R^{d})
of tempered distributions, which we shall equip with the strong topology; that is the topology generated by the family of seminorms
\{\eta_{B}\}
, where for each bounded
B\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d})
we define
\eta_{B}(f):=\sup_{\varphi\in B}\left\lvert\langle\varphi,f\rangle\right\rvert
for
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}(\R^{d})
. With this topology,
{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d})
is reflexive and separable and densely embedded in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}(\R^{d})
; see
[
25
, Th. V.14 Cor. 1]
.
The duality in
{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d})
is denoted by
[f,g]
for
f\in{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d})
and
g\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}(\R^{d})
.

The spaces
L^{p}(\R^{d})
for
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
may be interpreted as subspaces of
{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}(\R^{d})
in the following manner: for each
g\in L^{p}(\R^{d})
the map

\displaystyle f\mapsto\int_{\R^{d}}f(x)g(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x,\qquad f\in{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d}),

i.e. the duality in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\R^{d})
, is finite for all
f\in{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d})
by Hölder’s inequality.
In this manner we identify each
g\in L^{p}(\R^{d})
with a functional in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\ast}(\R^{d})
, which we shall also refer to as
g
.
Furthermore we extend this identification to the dual
B^{\ast}
of
any Banach space
B
in which
{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d})
is densely embedded, and
we identify
\langle f,g\rangle_{B}\equiv[f,g]
for each
f\in{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d})
and
g\in B^{\ast}
.
Finally, we shall extend the definition of
[f,g]
as follows: if there exists a Banach space
B
in which
{\mathcal{S}}(\R^{d})
is dense and
f\in B
and
g\in B^{\ast}
then we define
[f,g]:=\langle f,g\rangle_{B}
, otherwise we take
[f,g]:=\infty
.
However, this interpretation means that we do
not
identify Hilbert spaces with their duals, except in the case of
L^{2}(\R^{d})
.

We shall define the weighted Besov spaces
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
for
p>1
and
s,w\in\R
in terms of wavelet bases of
L^{2}(\R^{d})
, for which we follow
[
32
, Sec. 1.2.3]
.
We define subsets
G^{j}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{d},j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+}
by

\displaystyle G^{j}:=\begin{cases}\{0,1\}^{d},&\text{if }j=0,\\
\{G=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{d})\colon G_{i}=1\text{ for at least one }i\},&\text{if }j\geqslant 1.\end{cases}

Suppose we are given
\big(\Psi^{G}_{0}\big)_{G\in G^{0}}\subseteq C_{c}(\R^{d})
which form an orthonormal set in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
, which we shall call the
parent wavelets
. Then, for each
j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+},G\in G^{j}
and
m\in\Z^{d}
, we define

\displaystyle\Psi^{j,G}_{m}(x):=2^{jd/2}\Psi^{G}_{m}(2^{j}x):=2^{jd/2}\Psi^{G}_{0}(2^{j}x-m),\qquad x\in\R^{d}.

It is known that for any
r\in\N
, there exist such parent wavelets
\big(\Psi^{G}_{0}\big)_{G\in G^{0}}\subseteq C^{r}_{c}(\R^{d})
such that
\Psi:=\{\Psi^{j,G}_{m}\colon j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+},G\in G^{j},m\in\Z^{d}\}
forms an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
; see
[
31
, Th. 1.61]
; one example is the Daubechies wavelets
[
9
]
.
In this case,
\Psi
is called a
wavelet basis of
L^{2}(\R^{d})
.
Henceforth, we shall refer to the wavelet index set

\displaystyle\mathbbm{W}^{d}:=\left\{(j,G,m)\colon j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+},G\in G^{j},m\in\Z^{d}\right\}.

(
2.2
)

Since
\mathbbm{W}^{d}
is countable, we may consider in the usual manner the space
\ell^{p}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})
of
p
-summable sequences with index set
\mathbbm{W}^{d}
.
For the purposes of defining the weighted Besov space
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
, we shall require a minimum smoothness of the wavelet basis depending on the dimension
d
and the parameters
p
and
s
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
p>1
and
s
,
w\in\R
.
A wavelet basis
\Psi=\{\Psi^{j,G}_{m}\colon(j,G,m)\in\mathbbm{W}^{d}\}
of
L^{2}(\R^{d})
is called an
admissible basis of
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
if
\Psi\subseteq C_{c}^{r}(\R^{d})
for some integer
r>\left\lvert s\right\rvert
.

We begin with defining the
weighted Besov sequence space

b^{p}_{s,w}
for
p>1
and
s
,
w\in\R
, for which we
introduce the weight constants:

\displaystyle\omega_{m}^{j}=\omega_{m}^{j}(p,s,w):=2^{j(s-\frac{d}{p}+\frac{d}{2})}(1+2^{-2j}\left\lvert m\right\rvert^{2})^{\frac{w}{2}},

(
2.3
)

for each
m\in\Z^{d}
and
j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+}
.
We define
b^{p}_{s,w}

as the vector space of real-valued sequences
(\lambda^{j,G}_{m})_{(j,G,m)\in\mathbbm{W}^{d}}

such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{b^{p}_{s,w}}:=\Bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+}}\sum_{G\in G^{j}}\sum_{m\in\Z^{d}}\left\lvert 2^{-\frac{jd}{2}}\omega_{m}^{j}\lambda^{j,G}_{m}\right\rvert^{p}\Bigg)^{1/p}<\infty.

The space
(b^{p}_{s,w},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{b^{p}_{s,w}})
forms a Banach space if
p>1
and is a Hilbert space for
p=2
.

Let
\Psi
be an admissible basis of
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
for some
p>1
and
s
,
w\in\R
. The
weighted Besov space

B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
is defined to be

\displaystyle B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(\R^{d})\colon f=\sum_{j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+}}\sum_{G\in G^{j}}\sum_{m\in\Z^{d}}\lambda^{j,G}_{m}2^{-\frac{jd}{2}}\Psi^{j,G}_{m},\,\lambda\in b^{p}_{s,w}\right\},

(
2.4
)

where the sum is unconditionally convergent in
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(\R^{d})
.
When this holds, the associated sequence
\lambda
is unique and we
have
\lambda^{j,G}_{m}=2^{\frac{jd}{2}}[\Psi^{j,G}_{m},f]
.
A consequence of
\Psi
being an admissible basis of
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
is that the wavelets are of sufficient smoothness to guarantee that they are in
\big(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})\big)^{\ast}
, and so the dual pairing makes sense.
As the sums over
j,G
and
m
are unconditional in the definitions of both the weighted Besov spaces and the weighted Besov sequence spaces, we will henceforth use the simpler notation
\sum_{j,G,m}
to mean
\sum_{j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+}}\sum_{G\in G^{j}}\sum_{m\in\Z^{d}}
.
We may norm
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
by taking
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}:=\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{b^{p}_{s,w}}
, giving

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}=\Bigg(\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert[\Psi^{j,G}_{m},f]\right\rvert^{p}\Bigg)^{1/p}.

(
2.5
)

It follows that
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
is a Banach space for
p>1
and a Hilbert space for
p=2
.
We immediately see from this definition that
B^{2}_{0,0}(\R^{d})=L^{2}(\R^{d})
, consistently with the relation described in the introduction.

Remark 2.2
.

The Besov space scale typically has an auxiliary parameter
q
.
However, we shall not be using the
q
parameter in this work;
this is due to the fact that the embedding theorems available in weighted Besov spaces (see Proposition 3 in
[
12
]
) show the continuous embedding of
B^{p,q}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
into
B^{p,p}_{s-\varepsilon,w}(\R^{d})
for any
q\in\R
and
\varepsilon>0
. The results presented in this work are generally expressed as strict inequalities on the Besov space parameters, and as such are unaffected by the arbitrarily small change in the
s
parameter needed to incorporate any
q
parameter.

The dual spaces for the classical unweighted Besov spaces are well-known:
\big(B^{p}_{s,0}(\R^{d})\big)^{\ast}=B^{p^{\prime}}_{-s,0}(\R^{d})
for
p>1
and
s\in\R
; see e.g.
[
30
, p. 179]
. We may easily generalise to the weighted case to state the following result:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
p>1
and
s
,
w\in\R
. The dual space
\big(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})\big)^{\ast}
may be identified with
B^{p^{\prime}}_{-s,-w}(\R^{d})

with
p^{\prime}:=\tfrac{p}{p-1}
, and the duality given by

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{B^{p}_{s,w}}=[f,g]=\sum_{j,G,m}[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f][\Psi_{m}^{j,G},g],

(
2.6
)

where
\Psi
is any admissible basis for
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
(and thus is an admissible basis for
B^{p^{\prime}}_{-s,-w}(\R^{d})
).

Weighted Besov spaces form various scales according to the parameters
p,s
and
w
; we present a general result for their continuous embeddings. The positive results, for when a certain Besov space is continuously embedded in another given Besov space, are well-known; however, we are unaware of any converse results so we develop such converses here.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
s_{0},s_{1},w_{0},w_{1}\in\R
and
p_{0},p_{1}>1
.

(1)

Suppose
p_{0}>p_{1}
. Then
B^{p_{0}}_{s_{0},w_{0}}(\R^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{p_{1}}_{s_{1},w_{1}}(\R^{d})
continuously if and only if

\displaystyle s_{0}>s_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad w_{0}-w_{1}>d\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}\right).

(2)

Suppose
p_{0}\leqslant p_{1}
. Then
B^{p_{0}}_{s_{0},w_{0}}(\R^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{p_{1}}_{s_{1},w_{1}}(\R^{d})
continuously if and only if

\displaystyle s_{0}-s_{1}\geqslant d\left(\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{p_{1}}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad w_{0}\geqslant w_{1}.

In order to prove this Proposition, we shall first prove an intermediary result about the isomorphism between weighted Besov sequence spaces as defined above and
\ell^{p}
spaces.

Lemma 2.5
.

For each
p>1
and
s,w\in\R
the operator
\Upsilon_{s,w}^{p}\colon b^{p}_{s,w}\to\ell^{p}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})
defined by

\displaystyle\big(\Upsilon_{s,w}^{p}\lambda\big)_{m}^{j,G}:=2^{-\frac{jd}{2}}\omega_{m}^{j}\lambda^{j,G}_{m}

(
2.7
)

forms an isometric isomorphism, where the constants
\omega_{m}^{j}=\omega_{m}^{j}(p,s,w)
are defined in (
2.3
).

Proof.

Since
\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{b^{p}_{s,w}}=\left\lVert\Upsilon_{s,w}^{p}\lambda\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})}
by the very definition, the proof
is elementary.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

The continuous embedding in both cases is given by Proposition 3 in
[
12
]
.
To prove non-inclusion we first note that for any
q>0
, if
y=(y_{m}^{j,G})_{(j,G,m)\in\mathbbm{W}^{d}}
is such that
y\notin\ell^{q}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})^{\ast}
then there exists
x\in\ell^{q}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})
with
\sum_{j,G,m}x_{m}^{j,G}y_{m}^{j,G}=\infty
.

(1): suppose that
p_{0}>p_{1}
and
w_{1}-w_{0}\geqslant-d\big(\tfrac{1}{p_{1}}-\tfrac{1}{p_{0}}\big)
. Taking
\alpha=\tfrac{p_{0}}{p_{0}-p_{1}}
and thus
\alpha^{\prime}=\tfrac{p_{0}}{p_{1}}
, we have
\alpha p_{1}(w_{1}-w_{0})\geqslant-d
. Since for any
j\geqslant 0
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\Z^{d}}\big(1+2^{-2j}\left\lvert m\right\rvert^{2}\big)^{\frac{\alpha p_{1}(w_{1}-w_{0})}{2}}=\infty,

according to the proof of
[
12
, Th. 3]
, we conclude

\displaystyle\sum_{j,G,m}2^{j\alpha p_{1}(s_{1}-s_{0}-\frac{d}{p_{1}}+\frac{d}{p_{0}})}\big(1+2^{-2j}\left\lvert m\right\rvert^{2}\big)^{\frac{\alpha p_{1}(w_{1}-w_{0})}{2}}=\infty.

Since the last expression means for the weights defined in(
2.3
) that

\displaystyle\left(\left(\frac{\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{1},s_{1},w_{1})}{\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{0},s_{0},w_{0})}\right)^{p_{1}}\right)_{(j,G,m)\in\mathbbm{W}^{d}}\notin\ell^{\alpha}(\mathbbm{W}^{d}),

it follows that there exists a non-negative
y\in\ell^{\frac{p_{0}}{p_{1}}}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})
satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{j,G,m}\left(\frac{\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{1},s_{1},w_{1})}{\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{0},s_{0},w_{0})}\right)^{p_{1}}y_{m}^{j,G}=\infty.

The isometry between
\ell^{p_{0}}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})
and
b^{p_{0}}_{s_{0},w_{0}}
established in Lemma
2.5
guarantees that

\lambda_{m}^{j,G}=2^{\frac{jd}{2}}\big(\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{0},s_{0},w_{0})\big)^{-1}(y_{m}^{j,G})^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}

defines a sequence
\lambda:=(\lambda_{m}^{j,G})
in
b^{p_{0}}_{s_{0},w_{0}}
. Since

\displaystyle\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{b^{p_{1}}_{s_{1},w_{1}}}^{p_{1}}=\sum_{j,G,m}\big(2^{-\frac{jd}{2}}\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{1},s_{1},w_{1})\lambda_{m}^{j,G}\big)^{p_{1}}=\sum_{j,G,m}\left(\frac{\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{1},s_{1},w_{1})}{\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{0},s_{0},w_{0})}\right)^{p_{1}}y_{m}^{j,G}=\infty,

it follows
\lambda\notin b^{p_{1}}_{s_{1},w_{1}}
, which shows that
b^{p_{0}}_{s_{0},w_{0}}\nsubseteq b^{p_{1}}_{s_{1},w_{1}}
which in turn shows
B^{p_{0}}_{s_{0},w_{0}}(\R^{d})\nsubseteq B^{p_{1}}_{s_{1},w_{1}}(\R^{d})
by the isometry between the weighted Besov spaces and the weighted Besov sequence spaces; see
[
31
, Th. 6.15]
.

Now suppose
p_{0}>p_{1}
and
s_{0}\leqslant s_{1}
, and define for
(j,G,m)\in\mathbbm{W}^{d}

\displaystyle x_{m}^{j,G}:=\left(\frac{\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{1},s_{1},w_{1})}{\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{0},s_{0},w_{0})}\right)^{p_{1}}.

We can assume

S_{j}:=\sum_{m\in\Z^{d}}\big(1+2^{-2j}\left\lvert m\right\rvert^{2}\big)^{\frac{\alpha p_{1}(w_{1}-w_{0})}{2}}<\infty
for each
j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+}
, as otherwise there would be nothing to prove. Since
S_{j}
is asymptotically
{\mathcal{O}}(2^{jd})
as
j\to\infty
according to
[
12
, Th. 3]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j,G,m}\left\lvert x_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{\alpha}=\sum_{j,G}2^{\alpha jp_{1}(s_{1}-s_{0}-\frac{d}{p_{1}}+\frac{d}{p_{0}})}S_{j}=\infty,

as
\alpha p_{1}\big(s_{1}-s_{0}-\tfrac{d}{p_{1}}+\tfrac{d}{p_{0}}\big)\geqslant-d
. It follows
\big(x_{m}^{j,G}\big)_{(j,G,m)\in\mathbbm{W}^{d}}\notin\ell^{\alpha}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})
, and the proof of non-inclusion proceeds as above.

(2): let
p_{0}\leqslant p_{1}
and
s_{1}-s_{0}>d(\tfrac{1}{p_{1}}-\tfrac{1}{p_{0}})
, so we have
2^{jp_{1}(s_{1}-s_{0}-\frac{d}{p_{1}}+\frac{d}{p_{0}})}
is unbounded as
j\to\infty
. For
(j,G,m)\in\mathbbm{W}^{d}
we define

\displaystyle x_{m}^{j,G}:=\left(\frac{\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{1},s_{1},w_{1})}{\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{0},s_{0},w_{0})}\right)^{p_{1}}.

Since
\big(x_{m}^{j,G}\big)_{(j,G,m)\in\mathbbm{W}^{d}}\notin\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})
there exists
y\in\ell^{\frac{p_{0}}{p_{1}}}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j,G,m}\left(\frac{\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{1},s_{1},w_{1})}{\omega_{m}^{j}(p_{0},s_{0},w_{0})}\right)^{p_{1}}y_{m}^{j,G}=\infty,

where we recall the dual of
\ell^{p}
for
p\leqslant 1
is
\ell^{\infty}
.
The proof of the non-inclusion follows as above.

In the remaining case
p_{0}>p_{1}
and
w_{0}<w_{1}
, we again obtain
\big(x_{m}^{j,G}\big)_{(j,G,m)\in\mathbbm{W}^{d}}\notin\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})
, and the non-inclusion result follows.
∎

We may now identify the set of weighted Besov spaces containing
L^{2}(\R^{d})
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
p>1
and define

\displaystyle E_{p}:=\begin{cases}(-\infty,0)\times(-\infty,-\tfrac{d}{p}+\tfrac{d}{2}),&\text{if }p\in(1,2),\\
(-\infty,0]\times(-\infty,0],&\text{if }p=2,\\
(-\infty,-\tfrac{d}{2}+\tfrac{d}{p})\times(-\infty,0],&\text{if }p\in(2,\infty).\end{cases}

(
2.8
)

Then
L^{2}(\R^{d})\subseteq B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
if and only if
(s,w)\in E_{p}
; in this case,
the embedding is continuous.

Proof.

We recall that
L^{2}(\R^{d})=B^{2}_{0,0}(\R^{d})
. The result then follows by applying Proposition
2.4
for the case
p_{0}=2
,
p_{1}=p
,
s_{0}=0
,
s_{1}=s
,
w_{0}=0
and
w_{1}=w
.
∎

3
Lévy measures and Lévy processes in Besov spaces

Let
U
be a separable Banach space with dual
U^{\ast}
.
We define Lévy processes in
U
in the usual manner, that is, a
U
-valued process
L=(L(t)\colon t\geqslant 0)
such that
L(0)=0
,
L
has independent and stationary increments, and the map from
t
to the law of
L(t)
is continuous at 0 in the topology of weak convergence of probability measures.

For an arbitrary finite measure
\mu
on
\Borel(U)
define the exponential measure
e(\mu)
by

\displaystyle e(\mu):=e^{-\mu(U)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\mu^{\ast m}.

(
3.9
)

The exponential measure
e(\mu)
is a compound Poisson distribution with characteristic function

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mu}\colon U^{\ast}\to\C,\qquad\varphi_{\mu}(u^{\ast})=\exp\left(\int_{U}\left(e^{i\langle u,u^{\ast}\rangle_{U}}-1\right)\,\mu({\mathrm{d}}u)\right).

Whereas in the finite dimensional case, and in Hilbert spaces, the integrability of
\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert^{2}\wedge 1
characterises Lévy measures, there are no equivalent conditions known in arbitrary Banach spaces for when the same function
\varphi_{\mu}
but for a
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
still forms the characteristic function of a probability measure. Thus, Lévy measures are defined implicitly in the following way, see
[
23
]
:

Definition 3.1
.

A
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
on
\Borel(U)
is a
Lévy measure
if
\mu(\{0\})=0
and

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mu}\colon U^{\ast}\to\C,\qquad\varphi_{\mu}(u^{\ast})=\exp\left(\int_{U}\left(e^{i\langle u,u^{\ast}\rangle_{U}}-1-i\langle u,u^{\ast}\rangle_{U}\1_{B_{U}}(u)\right)\,\mu({\mathrm{d}}u)\right)

is the characteristic function of a probability measure on
\Borel(U)
, which we shall call
e_{S}(\mu)
.

Theorem 5.4.8 in
[
23
]
guarantees that a
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
on
\Borel(U)
is a Lévy measure if and only if its symmetrisation
\mu+\mu^{-}
is a Lévy measure.

Lévy measures in sequence spaces
\ell^{p}
are characterised in Yurinskii
[
34
]
. Using the wavelet definition of weighted Besov spaces enables us to derive a corresponding result for the Lévy measures on these spaces.

Theorem 3.2
.

A
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
on
\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
with
\mu(\{0\})=0
is a Lévy measure on
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s
,
w\in\R
if and only if

(1)

for
p\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}\wedge 1\right)\,\mu({\mathrm{d}}f)<\infty,

(
3.10
)

\displaystyle\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left(\int_{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\leqslant 1}[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]^{2}\,\mu({\rm d}f)\right)^{p/2}<\infty;

(
3.11
)

(2)

and for
p\in(1,2)
,

\displaystyle\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{2}\wedge 1\right)\,\mu({\mathrm{d}}f)<\infty,

(
3.12
)

\displaystyle\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{\int_{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\leqslant 1}\big(\cos\tau[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]-1\big)\,\mu({\rm d}f)}\right)\,\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{\tau^{1+p}}<\infty.

(
3.13
)

In the expressions above,
\omega_{m}^{j}=\omega_{m}^{j}(p,s,w)
are the weight constants defined in (
2.3
),
and
\{\Psi^{j,G}_{m}\colon j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+},G\in G^{j},m\in\Z^{d}\}
is an admissible basis of
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
.

Proof.

We begin by showing sufficiency. Because of
[
23
, Th. 5.4.8]
, we can assume that
\mu
is symmetric.
Given
0\leqslant\alpha\leqslant\beta\leqslant\infty
we define

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha,\beta}(A):=\mu\big(A\cap\{f\in B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})\colon\alpha\leqslant\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}<\beta\}\big),\qquad A\in\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})).

Let
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
and note that Conditions (
3.10
) and (
3.12
) each imply that
\mu_{\varepsilon,1}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
is finite. Thus, the exponential measure
e(\mu_{\varepsilon,1})
defined in (
3.9
) coincides with
e_{S}(\mu_{\varepsilon,1})
and we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}\,e_{S}(\mu_{\varepsilon,1})({\rm d}f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left\lvert[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]\right\rvert^{p}\,e_{S}(\mu_{\varepsilon,1})({\rm d}f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\int_{\R}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{p}\,\big(e_{S}(\mu_{\varepsilon,1})\circ[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},\cdot]^{-1}\big)({\rm d}\beta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}E\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p},

(
3.14
)

where
\xi_{m}^{j,G}
is a random variable with distribution
e_{S}(\mu_{\varepsilon,1})\circ[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},\cdot]^{-1}=e_{S}(\mu_{\varepsilon,1}\circ[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},\cdot]^{-1})

for each
j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+},G\in G^{j}
and
m\in\Z^{d}
.

For
p\geqslant 2
, Theorem 1.1 in
[
11
]
guarantees

\displaystyle E\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\eqsim_{p}\int_{\R}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{p}\,(\mu_{\varepsilon,1}\circ[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},\cdot]^{-1})({\rm d}\beta)+\left(\int_{\R}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{2}\,(\mu_{\varepsilon,1}\circ[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},\cdot]^{-1})({\rm d}\beta)\right)^{p/2}.

It follows that, for
p\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}\,e_{S}(\mu_{\varepsilon,1})({\rm d}f)

\displaystyle\eqsim_{p}\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left(\int_{\R}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{p}\,(\mu_{\varepsilon,1}\circ[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},\cdot]^{-1})({\rm d}\beta)+\left(\int_{\R}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{2}\,(\mu_{\varepsilon,1}\circ[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},\cdot]^{-1})({\rm d}\beta)\right)^{p/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left(\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left\lvert[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]\right\rvert^{p}\,\mu_{\varepsilon,1}({\rm d}f)+\left(\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left\lvert[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]\right\rvert^{2}\,\mu_{\varepsilon,1}({\rm d}f)\right)^{p/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}\,\mu_{\varepsilon,1}({\rm d}f)+\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left(\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left\lvert[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]\right\rvert^{2}\,\mu_{\varepsilon,1}({\rm d}f)\right)^{p/2}.

(
3.15
)

Conditions (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) imply

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}\,e(\mu_{\varepsilon,1})({\rm d}f)<\infty.

(
3.16
)

By applying Hölder’s inequality twice, Theorem
2.3
shows for
g\in\big(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})\big)^{\ast}
that

\displaystyle\int_{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\leqslant 1}\langle f,g\rangle_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{2}\,\mu({\rm d}f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,G,m}\sum_{k,H,n}[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},g][\Psi_{n}^{k,H},g]\int_{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\leqslant 1}[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f][\Psi_{n}^{k,H},f]\,\mu({\rm d}f)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\sum_{j,G,m}[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},g]\left(\int_{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\leqslant 1}[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]^{2}\,\mu({\rm d}f)\right)^{1/2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{-1}[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},g]\left((\omega_{m}^{j})^{2}\int_{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\leqslant 1}[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]^{2}\,\mu({\rm d}f)\right)^{1/2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{-p^{\prime}}\left\lvert[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},g]\right\rvert^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{2/p^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left(\int_{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\leqslant 1}[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]^{2}\,\mu({\rm d}f)\right)^{p/2}\right)^{2/p}.

Since
\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{-p^{\prime}}\left\lvert[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},g]\right\rvert^{p^{\prime}}=\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{B^{p^{\prime}}_{-s,-w}}^{p^{\prime}}
, Condition (
3.11
) guarantees

\displaystyle\int_{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\leqslant 1}\langle f,g\rangle_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{2}\,\mu({\rm d}f)\lesssim\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{B^{p^{\prime}}_{-s,-w}}^{2}.

Together with (
3.16
) we see that the conditions of Theorem 1.B in
[
34
]
are satisfied and hence
\mu
is a Lévy measure on
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
.

Next we consider the case
p\in(1,2)
; in this case, we use the following relation
for a real-valued symmetric random variable
X
with characteristic function
\varphi_{X}
and
q\in(0,2)
:

\displaystyle E\left\lvert X\right\rvert^{q}=c_{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-{\rm Re}(\varphi_{X}(\tau))}{\tau^{q+1}}\,{\rm d}\tau,

(
3.17
)

where
c_{q}
is a constant depending only on

; see for example
[
20
, Th. 11.4.3]
.

Applying Equality (
3.17
) to a random variable with distribution
e(\mu_{\varepsilon,1})\circ[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},\cdot]^{-1}
shows, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}\,e(\mu_{\varepsilon,1})({\rm d}f)

\displaystyle=c_{p}\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\big(\cos\tau[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]-1\big)\,\mu_{\varepsilon,1}({\rm d}f)}\right)\,\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{\tau^{1+p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{p}\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{\int_{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}\leqslant 1}}\big(\cos\tau[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]-1\big)\,\mu({\rm d}f)}\right)\,\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{\tau^{1+p}}.

Condition (
3.13
) guarantees

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}\,e(\mu_{\varepsilon,1})({\rm d}f)<\infty.

(
3.18
)

Condition (
3.12
) implies for
g\in B^{p^{\prime}}_{-s,-w}(\R^{d})
that

\displaystyle\int_{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\leqslant 1}\langle f,g\rangle_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{2}\,\mu({\rm d}f)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\leqslant 1}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{2}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{B^{p^{\prime}}_{-s,-w}}^{2}\,\mu({\rm d}f)\lesssim\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{B^{p^{\prime}}_{-s,-w}}^{2}.

Together with (
3.18
), it follows that the conditions of Theorem 1.B in
[
34
]
are satisfied and hence
\mu
is a Lévy measure on
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
.

To establish necessity of the conditions suppose that
\mu
is a Lévy measure on
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
; as before we can assume
\mu
is symmetric. Proposition 5.4.1 in
[

\mu\big(B_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{c}\big)<\infty
.
Since

\displaystyle\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{q}\,e_{S}(\mu_{0,1})({\rm d}f)<\infty.

(
3.19
)

We first consider the case

\displaystyle\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}\,\mu_{0,1}({\rm d}f)+\!\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\bigg(\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]^{2}\,\mu_{0,1}({\rm d}f)\bigg)^{p/2}\!\!\eqsim_{p}\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}\,e_{S}(\mu_{0,1})({\rm d}f),

4
Regularisation in Besov spaces

Cylindrical Lévy processes in a Banach space
U
(as defined for example in
[
1
,
26
,
27
]
)
naturally generalise the notation of cylindrical Brownian motion, based on the theory of cylindrical measures and cylindrical random variables.
For some
n\in\mathbbm{N}
and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in U^{\ast}
we define the projection
\pi_{u_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,u_{n}^{\ast}}:U\to\mathbbm{R}^{n}
by

\displaystyle\pi_{u_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,u_{n}^{\ast}}(u):=\big(\langle u,u_{1}^{\ast}\rangle_{U},\ldots,\langle u,u_{n}^{\ast}\rangle_{U}\big).

Henceforth, we shall assume
U
is reflexive and separable, and let
\Gamma\subseteq U^{\ast}
.
Sets of the form

\displaystyle Z(u_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,u_{n}^{\ast};A):=\pi^{-1}_{u_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,u_{n}^{\ast}}(A)=\left\{u\in U:\big(\langle u,u_{1}^{\ast}\rangle_{U},\ldots,\langle u,u_{n}^{\ast}\rangle_{U}\big)\in A\right\}

for
A\in\Borel(\mathbbm{R}^{n})
are called
cylinder sets
with respect to
(U,\Gamma)
, and the set of all cylinder sets with respect to
(U,\Gamma)
is denoted by
\mathcal{Z}(U,\Gamma)
; we also denote
\mathcal{Z}(U,U^{*})=:\mathcal{Z}(U)
. It follows that
\mathcal{Z}(U,\Gamma)
is an algebra. A cylindrical random variable
Z
in
U
is a linear and continuous mapping
Z\colon U^{\ast}\rightarrow L^{0}(\Omega,P)
. The characteristic function of
Z
is defined by
\varphi_{Z}(u^{\ast})=E[\exp(iZu^{\ast})]
for all
u^{\ast}\in U^{\ast}
; see e.g.
[
33
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A family
(L(t):\,t\geqslant 0)
of cylindrical random variables
L(t)\colon U^{\ast}\to L^{0}(\Omega,P)
is called a
cylindrical Lévy process
if for all
u^{\ast}_{1},...,u^{\ast}_{n}\in U^{\ast}
and
n\in\mathbbm{N}
, the stochastic process
((L(t)u^{\ast}_{1},...,L(t)u^{\ast}_{n}):\,t\geqslant 0)
is a Lévy process in
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
.

In order to present the Lévy-Khintchine formula for a cylindrical Lévy process, we must first give a definition of the cylindrical version of the Lévy measure.
For cylindrical Lévy measures, we must specifically exclude sets containing the origin to avoid consistency issues with finite-dimensional projections. We define the
\pi
-system
\mathcal{Z}_{\ast}(U,\Gamma)\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(U,\Gamma)
for some
\Gamma\subseteq U^{\ast}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\ast}(U,\Gamma):=\{Z(u^{\ast}_{1},\ldots,u^{\ast}_{n};A):u_{1}^{\ast},\dots,u_{n}^{\ast}\in\Gamma,n\in\N,A\in\Borel(\R^{n}),0\notin A\}.

If
\Gamma=U^{\ast}
we write
\mathcal{Z}_{\ast}(U):=\mathcal{Z}_{\ast}(U,U^{\ast})
.
A set function
\mu\colon\mathcal{Z}_{\ast}(U)\to[0,\infty]
is called a
cylindrical Lévy measure
if for all
u^{\ast}_{1},\ldots,u^{\ast}_{n}\in U^{\ast}
and
n\in\mathbbm{N}
the map

\displaystyle\mu_{u^{\ast}_{1},\ldots,u^{\ast}_{n}}\colon\Borel(\R^{n})\to[0,\infty],\qquad\mu_{u^{\ast}_{1},\ldots,u^{\ast}_{n}}(A)=\mu\circ\pi_{u^{\ast}_{1},\ldots,u^{\ast}_{n}}^{-1}(A\!\setminus\!\{0\})

defines a Lévy measure on
\R^{n}
.
The characteristic function of a cylindrical Lévy process
(L(t):\,t\geqslant 0)
is given by

\varphi_{L(t)}\colon U^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathbbm{C},\qquad\varphi_{L(t)}(u^{\ast})=\exp\big(t\vartheta_{L}(u^{\ast})\big),

for all
t\geqslant 0
. Here,
\vartheta_{L}\colon U^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathbbm{C}
is called the (cylindrical) symbol of
L
, and is of the form

\displaystyle\vartheta_{L}(u^{\ast})=ia(u^{\ast})-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle u^{\ast},Qu^{\ast}\rangle_{U^{\ast}}+\int_{U}\left(e^{i\langle u,u^{\ast}\rangle_{U}}-1-i\langle u,u^{\ast}\rangle_{U}\1_{B_{\mathbbm{R}}}\big(\langle u,u^{\ast}\rangle_{U}\big)\right)\mu({\rm d}u),

where
a\colon U^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}
is a continuous mapping with
a(0)=0
,
the mapping
Q\colon U^{\ast}\rightarrow U^{\ast\ast}
is a positive, symmetric operator and
\mu
is a cylindrical Lévy measure; see
[
26
]
.

We shall analyse cylindrical Lévy processes in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
to determine when they arise from a Lévy process in a larger Besov space. This is based on the following concept:

Definition 4.2
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space continuously and densely embedded in a Banach space
U
.
A cylindrical Lévy process
L
in
H
is said to be induced by a Lévy process
Y
in
U
if

\displaystyle L(t)u^{\ast}=\langle Y(t),u^{\ast}\rangle_{U}\quad\text{$P$-a.s.\ for all $u^{\ast}\in U^{\ast}$ and $t\geqslant 0$.}

We shall examine conditions on the cylindrical Lévy measure of
L
such that
L
is induced by a genuine Lévy process
Y
in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
for some fixed
p>1
and
s,w\in\R
.
With reference to Definition
4.2
, we shall only consider Besov spaces which contain
L^{2}(\R^{d})
. This will allow us to develop the mathematical theory without the complication arising in the case that the cylindrical Lévy process
L
may have a non-trivial kernel and thus may be induced by a process in a Besov space which does not contain the whole of
L^{2}(\R^{d})
.

4.1
Cylindrical Brownian motions

The literature in dealing with the Besov localisation of Gaussian processes is primarily concerned with the path properties of finite-dimensional Brownian motions, or analogously with Gaussian white noise, conceived as a distribution-valued (generalised) random variable which represents the weak derivative in space and time of a finite-dimensional Brownian motion. In this work, we generalise from a distribution-valued random variable to a distribution-valued process.

Let
W
be a standard cylindrical Brownian motion in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
. Applying results from our previous work
[
16
]
, we can identify
W
with a distribution-valued white noise as considered in
[
3
]
.
Proposition 3.4 in
[
3
]
implies that, given
p>1
and
s,w\in\R
, the standard cylindrical Brownian motion
W
is induced by a Brownian motion in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
if and only if

s<-\frac{d}{2}
and
w<-\frac{d}{p}
.

For a general cylindrical Brownian motion
W
in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
with covariance operator
Q
and reproducing kernel Hilbert space
H_{Q}
we obtain the following: suppose
p>1
and
w,s\in\R
are such that
L^{2}(\R^{d})
is continuously embeeded into
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
, then
W
is induced by a Brownian motion in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
if and only if the injection
H_{Q}\hookrightarrow B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
is
\gamma
-Radonifying in the sense of Chapter 9 in
[
18
]
.

4.2
Non-Gaussian cylindrical Lévy processes

The cylindrical Lévy process
L=(L(t):\,t\geqslant 0)
on
L^{2}(\R^{d})
defines by
L^{\prime}(t)(b^{\ast}):=L(t)(\iota^{\ast}b^{\ast})
for each
b^{\ast}\in\big(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})\big)^{\ast}
a cylindrical Lévy process
L^{\prime}=(L^{\prime}(t):\,t\geqslant 0)
on
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
where
\iota\colon L^{2}(\R^{d})\to B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
is the canonical embedding for some
p>1
and
(s,w)\in E_{p}
.

Letting
\mu
be the cylindrical Lévy measure of
L
on
\mathcal{Z}_{\ast}(L^{2}(\R^{d}))
, then
\tilde{\mu}:=\mu\circ\iota^{-1}
is the cylindrical Lévy measure of
L^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{Z}_{\ast}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
; see
[
26
, Th. 3.4]
.
We shall study the case when
\tilde{\mu}
extends to a
\sigma
-finite measure on
\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
. In this case, we will mildly abuse notation and simply refer to this
\sigma
-finite and
\sigma
-additive extension as
\mu
where the context allows no confusion. The starting point for our analysis shall be to examine this extension using the results previously developed.

The following result demonstrates that, in the non-Gaussian case, regularisation of the cylindrical Lévy process results from the extension of the cylindrical Lévy measure, and furthermore allows us to concentrate in the sequel on symmetric cylindrical Lévy processes.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
L
be a cylindrical Lévy process in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
with no Guassian component and fix
p>1
and
(s,w)\in E_{p}
. Let
L^{S}:=L-L_{c}
be the symmetrisation of
L
, where
L_{c}
is an independent copy of
L
. Then
L
is induced by a Lévy process in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
if and only if the cylindrical Lévy measure of
L^{S}
has an extension to a
\sigma
-finite measure on
\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
which is a Lévy measure on
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
.

Proof.

The ‘only if’ implication is clear. To establish the converse,
denoting the cylindrical Lévy measure of
L
by
\mu
, we have that
L^{S}
has cylindrical Lévy measure
\mu+\mu^{-}
.
Let
\tilde{\mu}_{S}
denote the
\sigma
-additive extension of
\mu+\mu^{-}
on
\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
; by assumption
\tilde{\mu}_{S}
is a Lévy measure.
As we have
\mu\leqslant\tilde{\mu}_{S}
on each cylinder set in
\mathcal{Z}_{\ast}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
, Theorem 3.4 in
[
27
]
implies that
\mu
has a
\sigma
-additive extension on
\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
which is a Lévy measure.

We fix
t>0
and apply Theorem 5.6 in
[
27
]
to the function
f\colon[0,t]\to\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\R^{d}),B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
given by
f(u)=\iota
for all
u\in[0,t]
. We conclude that
f
is stochastically integrable with respect to
L
as defined in
[
27
]
since
\mu
extends to a Lévy measure. (This Theorem actually requires that the function
a\colon L^{2}(\R^{d})\to\R
as defined in Equation 3.1 of
[
27
]
is weak*-weakly sequentially continuous, however a careful analysis of the proof indicates that in a reflexive Banach space such as
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
, this requirement is not necessary.)
It follows that the process

\big(\int_{[0,t]}f(u)\,{\mathrm{d}}L(u)\colon t\geqslant 0\big)
forms a Lévy process in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
which induces
L
.
∎

In the sequel, we shall make use of the following technique.
As the sums defining membership of a given Besov space in (
2.4
) are required to be unconditionally convergent, we may take any convenient ordering of the terms.
For any enumeration of the countable set of indices
j,G
and
m
, we denote a sum over the first
n
terms in this enumeration by
\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}
.
We define for each
n\in\N
the projection
P_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
onto the subspace spanned by the first
n
elements in the enumeration of
\Psi
, that is

\displaystyle P_{n}f:=\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]\Psi_{m}^{j,G},\qquad f\in B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}).

(
4.20
)

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mu
be a cylindrical Lévy measure on
\mathcal{Z}_{\ast}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
for some
p>1
and
(s,w)\in E_{p}
. Then
\mu
extends to a
\sigma
-finite measure on
\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
if

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\left(\left\{f:\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}>R\right\}\right)=0.

Proof.

We shall apply the theorem on
[
15
, p.327]
, which gives conditions for when a cylindrical probability measure extends to a probability measure on a Banach space with a separable dual. In order to apply this theorem in our setting, a careful study of the proof indicates that in addition to our assumptions we need to work with finite cylindrical measures satisfying the continuity condition

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu\big(\{f\colon[f,f^{\ast}_{1,k}]<a_{1},\ldots,[f,f^{\ast}_{m,k}]<a_{m}\}\big)=\mu\big(\{f\colon[f,f^{\ast}_{1}]<a_{1},\ldots,[f,f^{\ast}_{m}]<a_{m}\}\big)

(
4.21
)

for Lebesgue-almost all
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in\R
whenever
f^{\ast}_{i,k}\to f^{\ast}_{i}
in
\big(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})\big)^{\ast}
for each
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Let
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\N}
be an (unconditional) Schauder basis of
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
with coordinate functionals
\{e^{\ast}_{k}\}_{k\in\N}
such that
\left\lVert e^{\ast}_{k}\right\rVert_{B^{p^{\prime}}_{-s,-w}(\R^{d})}=1
for each
k\in\N
.
We consider the cylindrical measure
\mu_{1,1}
defined by

\displaystyle\mu_{1,1}(C)=\mu\left(C\cap\{f\colon\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{1}]\right\rvert>1\}\right)\qquad\text{for }C\in\mathcal{Z}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}),\{e^{\ast}_{k}\}_{k\in\N}).

By the properties of cylindrical Lévy measures, the set function
\mu_{1,1}
is a finite cylindrical measure on
\mathcal{Z}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}),\{e^{\ast}_{k}\}_{k\in\N})
. As
\mu
satisfies
\lim_{k\to\infty}(\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{2}\wedge 1)(\mu\circ\pi_{e^{\ast}_{1},f^{\ast}_{1,k}\ldots,f^{\ast}_{m,k}}^{-1})({\mathrm{d}}\beta)=(\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{2}\wedge 1)(\mu\circ\pi_{e^{\ast}_{1},f^{\ast}_{1},\ldots,f^{\ast}_{m}}^{-1})({\mathrm{d}}\beta)
weakly for every
f^{\ast}_{i,k}\to f^{\ast}_{i}
in
\big(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})\big)^{\ast}
for each
i=1,\ldots,m
.
due to Lemma 4.4 in
[
26
]
, we see that (
4.21
) is satisfied for
\mu_{1,1}
as each finite-dimensional projection only takes weight on
\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert>1
.
Since

\displaystyle\mu_{1,1}\left(\left\{f:\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}>R\right\}\right)\leqslant\mu\left(\left\{f:\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}>R\right\}\right)\qquad\text{for all }n\in\N,R>0,

we may apply the result in
[
15
, p.327]
and extend
\mu_{1,1}
to a finite measure
\tilde{\mu}_{1,1}
on
\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
. The measure
\tilde{\mu}_{1,1}
is supported on
\{f\colon\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{1}]\right\rvert>1\}
, and satisfies
\tilde{\mu}_{1,1}(C)=\mu(C\cap\{f\colon\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{1}]\right\rvert>1\})
for each cylinder set
C\in\mathcal{Z}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}),\{e^{\ast}_{k}\}_{k\in\N})
.

Next, for each
n\in\N
we construct cylindrical measures
\mu_{1,n+1}
by

\displaystyle\mu_{1,n+1}(C)=\mu\left(C\cap\left\{f\colon\frac{1}{n+1}<\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{1}]\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{1}{n}\right\}\right),\qquad C\in\mathcal{Z}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}),\{e^{\ast}_{k}\}_{k\in\N}).

Applying the same argument as above (using an easy rescaling), we obtain a sequence of finite measures
\{\tilde{\mu}_{1,n}\}_{n\in\N}
with pairwise disjoint support. We define the measure
\tilde{\mu}_{1}
by

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{1}(A)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tilde{\mu}_{1,n}(A),\qquad A\in\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})).

By this construction,
\tilde{\mu}_{1}
forms a
\sigma
-finite measure on
\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
with support in
B_{1}:=\{f\colon\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{1}]\right\rvert\neq 0\}
.

We repeat the procedure on the subspace
\{f\colon\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{1}]\right\rvert=0\}
. We start by defining, for
C\in\mathcal{Z}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}),\{e^{\ast}_{k}\}_{k\in\N})
,

\displaystyle\mu_{2,1}(C)=\mu\left(C\cap\{f\colon\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{1}]\right\rvert=0,\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{2}]\right\rvert>1\}\right),

and, for
n\in\N
,

\displaystyle\mu_{2,n+1}(C)=\mu\left(C\cap\left\{f\colon\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{1}]\right\rvert=0,\frac{1}{n+1}<\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{2}]\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{1}{n}\right\}\right).

We in this manner obtain a
\sigma
-finite measure
\tilde{\mu}_{2}
on
\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
with support in the set
B_{2}:=\{f\colon\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{1}]\right\rvert=0,\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{2}]\right\rvert\neq 0\}
.
We then continue this procedure to create the set of measures
\{\tilde{\mu}_{k}\}_{k\in\N}
, where for each
k\in\N
,
\tilde{\mu}_{k}
has support in
B_{k}:=\{f\colon\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{1}]\right\rvert=0,\ldots,\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{k-1}]\right\rvert=0,\left\lvert[f,e^{\ast}_{k}]\right\rvert\neq 0\}
.
We observe that the sets
\{B_{k}\}_{k\in\N}
are pairwise disjoint, and

B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})=\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}B_{k}.

We now finally define the measure
\tilde{\mu}
on
\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
by setting
\tilde{\mu}(\{0\})=0
and

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}(A)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\tilde{\mu}_{k}(A),\qquad A\in\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})).

As
\tilde{\mu}
is a sum of
\sigma
-finite measures with pairwise disjoint support, it follows that
\tilde{\mu}
is
\sigma
-finite.
Let
C=C(e_{1}^{\ast},\dots,e_{n}^{\ast};B)
for some
B\in\Borel(\R^{n})
with
0\notin B
be an arbitrary set in
\mathcal{Z}_{\ast}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}),\{e^{\ast}_{k}\}_{k\in\N})
. Since

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}(C)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{\mu}_{k}(C)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu(C\cap B_{k})=\mu(C),

we conclude that
\tilde{\mu}
forms an extension of the restriction of
\mu
to
\mathcal{Z}_{\ast}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}),\{e^{\ast}_{k}\}_{k\in\N})
.

First assuming that
\mu
is finite, continuity of its characteristic functions shows that
\mu
is uniquely determined by its values on
\mathcal{Z}_{\ast}(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}),\{e^{\ast}_{k}\}_{k\in\N})
. It follows that
\tilde{\mu}
forms an extension of
\mu
. The general case follows from the construction of
\tilde{\mu}
.
∎

We now present a Corollary to Theorem
3.2
which characterises when a cylindrical Lévy process in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
is induced by a genuine Lévy process in some given weighted Besov space.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
L
be a cylindrical Lévy process in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
with no Gaussian component and with cylindrical Lévy measure
\mu
. Then
L
is induced by a Lévy process in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
for some
p>1
and
(s,w)\in E_{p}
if and only if

\mu
extends to a
\sigma
-finite measure on
\Borel(B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}))
and

(1)

for
p\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left(\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}\wedge 1\right)\,\mu({\rm d}f)<\infty,

(
4.22
)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left(\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\1_{B_{\R}}\Big(\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\Big)\left\lvert[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]\right\rvert^{2}\,\mu({\rm d}f)\right)^{p/2}<\infty;

(2)

for

,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left(\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{2}\wedge 1\right)\,\mu({\rm d}f)<\infty,

(
4.23
)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\1_{B_{\R}}\big(\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\big)\big(\cos\tau[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]-1\big)\,\mu({\rm d}f)}\right)\,\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{\tau^{1+p}}<\infty.

In the expressions above,
\omega_{m}^{j}=\omega_{m}^{j}(p,s,w)
are the weight constants defined in (
2.3
),
and
\{\Psi^{j,G}_{m}\colon j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{+},G\in G^{j},m\in\Z^{d}\}
is any admissible basis of
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.3
we may assume that
L
and
\mu
are symmetric.
Then necessity and sufficiency of the conditions follows from Theorem
3.2
.
∎

4.3
Radonifying Embeddings

Assume that
U
and
V
are some Banach spaces.
Let
Z\colon U^{\ast}\rightarrow L^{0}(\Omega,P)
be a cylindrical random variable and
T\colon U\to V
a linear and continuous operator. The image of
Z
under
T
is the cylindrical random variable
TZ\colon V^{\ast}\rightarrow L^{0}(\Omega,P)
on
V
defined by
(TZ)v^{\ast}:=Z(T^{\ast}v^{\ast})
for all
v^{\ast}\in V^{\ast}
. The cylindrical random variable

TZ
is induced by a random variable
X\colon\Omega\to V
if
(TZ)v^{\ast}=\langle X,v^{\ast}\rangle_{V}

P
-a.s. for all
v^{\ast}\in V^{\ast}
.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
U,V
be separable Banach spaces. A continuous linear operator
T\colon U\to V
is called

(1)

0-Radonifying
if for each cylindrical random variable
Z
in
U
, the cylindrical random variable
TZ
is induced by a random variable in
V
;

(2)

p
-
Radonifying
for some
p>0
if for each cylindrical random variable
Z
in
U
satisfying
E\left\lvert Zu^{\ast}\right\rvert^{p}<\infty
for each
u^{\ast}\in U^{\ast}
, the cylindrical random variable
TZ
is induced by a random variable in
V
.

The word
Radonifying
originates from the fact, that the probability distribution of the inducing random variable in the definition above is a Radon measure on
\Borel(V)
. Since we only consider separable Banach spaces, this is redundant in our setting.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
U,V
be Banach spaces and
p>0
. A continuous linear operator
T\colon U\to V
is called
p
-
summing
if there exists
C>0
such that for every finite collection
(u_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq U
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert Tu_{i}\right\rVert_{V}^{p}\leqslant C^{p}\sup_{\left\lVert u^{\ast}\right\rVert_{U^{\ast}}\leqslant 1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lvert\langle u_{i},u^{\ast}\rangle_{U}\right\rvert^{p}.

Every
p
-Radonifying operator for
p>0
is
p
-summing, and every 0-Radonifying operator is
p
-summing for every
p>0
. The classes of
p
-summing and
p
-Radonifying operators coincide for
p>1
; see
[
33
, Th. VI.5.4]
. If
U
and
V
are Hilbert spaces, 0-Radonifying operators coincide with Hilbert-Schmidt operators
[
33
, Th. VI.5.2]
, and thus the class of
p
-summing operators for all
p>0
coincides with the class of Hilbert-Schmidt operators.

The cylindrical Lévy process
L^{\prime}
, introduced in Section
4.2
, is the image of
L
under
the embedding
\iota\colon L^{2}(\R^{d})\to B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
. If
\iota
is
0
-radonifying,
it follows that
L^{\prime}
is automatically induced by a genuine Lévy process on
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
, which motivates us to investigate this property in detail in the following

Theorem 4.8
.

The embedding
\iota\colon L^{2}(\R^{d})\to B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
for some
p>1
is
p
-Radonifying
if and only if

\displaystyle(s,w)\in R^{(p)}_{p}:=(-\infty,-\tfrac{d}{2})\times(-\infty,-\tfrac{d}{p}).

(
4.24
)

Proof.

The continuous embedding follows as
R^{(p)}_{p}\subseteq E_{p}
. Let
\Psi
be an admissible basis of
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
and
\omega_{m}^{j}=\omega_{m}^{j}(p,s,w)
be the weight constants defined in (
2.3
). Note, that

\displaystyle\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}=\sum_{j,G,m}2^{jp(s-d/p+d/2)}(1+2^{-2j}\left\lvert m\right\rvert^{2})^{pw/2}<\infty\;\Leftrightarrow\;(s,w)\in R_{p}^{(p)}.

(
4.25
)

This follows from the fact, that the sum over
m
converges if and only if

, and in this case is asymptotically
{\mathcal{O}}(2^{jd})
as
j\to\infty
; see e.g. the proof of
[
12
, Th. 3]
.

For any
p>1
, we obtain for
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in L^{2}(\R^{d})
that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert f_{i}\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}=\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lvert\langle f_{i},\Psi_{m}^{j,G}\rangle_{L^{2}}\right\rvert^{p}\leqslant\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\sup_{\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leqslant 1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lvert\langle f_{i},y\rangle_{L^{2}}\right\rvert^{p}.

Thus, the embedding
L^{2}(\R^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
is
p
-summing if
(s,w)\in R_{p}^{(p)}
.

To establish necessity, we first consider
p\geqslant 2
. Choose
f_{i}=\Psi_{m_{i}}^{j_{i},G_{i}}\in\Psi
,
i=1,\ldots,n
to be distinct wavelet basis vectors, i.e.
f_{i}\neq f_{j}
for
i\neq j
. It follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leqslant 1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lvert\langle f_{i},y\rangle_{L^{2}}\right\rvert^{p}\leqslant\sup_{\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leqslant 1}\sum_{j,G,m}\left\lvert\langle\Psi_{m}^{j,G},y\rangle_{L^{2}}\right\rvert^{p}\leqslant\sup_{\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leqslant 1}\sum_{j,G,m}\left\lvert\langle\Psi_{m}^{j,G},y\rangle_{L^{2}}\right\rvert^{2}=1.

On the other side, since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert f_{i}\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\omega_{i})^{p}

where
\omega_{i}=\omega_{m}^{j}
when
f_{i}=\Psi_{m}^{j,G}
for some
(j,G,m)\in\mathbbm{W}^{d}
,
it follows from (
4.25
) that the embedding
\iota
is not
p
-summable if

(s,w)\notin R_{p}^{(p)}
. An application of
[
33
, Th. VI.5.4]
completes the proof of the case
p\geqslant 2
.

Necessity of
(s,w)\in R_{p}^{(p)}
for
p\in(1,2)
follows from Theorem
5.1
, which gives a
counterexample of a cylindrical Lévy process in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
with
p
-th weak moments that is not induced by a process in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
for any
(s,w)\notin{R^{(p)}_{p}}
. This result, though later in the text, does not rely upon the result that the embedding
L^{2}(\R^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
is not
p
-Radonifying for
(s,w)\notin R^{(p)}_{p}
.
∎

Due to the range of continuous embeddings between the weighted Besov spaces, it is in many cases possible to factorise the embeddings via a Hilbert space which allows for a 0-Radonification result.

Theorem 4.9
.

The embedding
\iota\colon L^{2}(\R^{d})\to B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
for some
p>1
is
0
-Radonifying
if and only if

\displaystyle(s,w)\in R_{p}:=\begin{cases}(-\infty,-\tfrac{d}{2})\times(-\infty,-\tfrac{d}{p}),&\text{when }p\in(1,2],\\
(-\infty,-d+\tfrac{d}{p})\times(-\infty,-\tfrac{d}{2}),&\text{when }p\in(2,\infty).\end{cases}

(
4.26
)

Proof.

We show sufficiency by factorising the embedding
\iota\colon L^{2}(\R^{d})\to B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
as follows:

\displaystyle L^{2}(\R^{d})\overset{\iota_{1}}{\hookrightarrow}B^{2}_{s_{1},w_{1}}(\R^{d})\overset{\iota_{2}}{\hookrightarrow}B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}),

for some
s_{1}<-\tfrac{d}{2}
and
w_{1}<-\tfrac{d}{2}
. In this case, Theorem
4.8
guarantees that
the embedding
\iota_{1}
is 2-Radonifying, and thus it is
0
-radonifying, since
2
-summing operators coincide with

0
-Radonifying operators in Hilbert spaces according to
[
33
, Th. VI.5.2]
. It remains to show
that

and

can always be chosen, such that

is continuously embedded into
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
, whenever

and
w
are in the stated ranges.

(i) Let
p\in(1,2]
.
By applying Proposition
2.4
we see that
\iota_{2}
is a continuous embedding for

and
s<s_{1}
. Thus, by defining
\varepsilon:=-w-\tfrac{d}{p}>0
, we may take
w_{1}=-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}-\tfrac{d}{2}
which satisfies both required inequalities. A similar argument may be used for
s
and
s_{1}
.

(ii) Let
p>2
.
By Proposition
2.4
we see that
\iota_{2}
is a continuous embedding for
w<w_{1}
and
s<s_{1}-d\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{p}\right)
. We proceed by similar arguments as above.

The necessity for
p=2

4.8
since

-Radonifying and
p
-Radonifying operators between Hilbert spaces coincide.
To show the necessity for

and Proposition

L^{2}(\R^{d})
that are not induced by a process in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
for any
(s,w)\notin{R_{p}}
.
Although these negative results referred to are later in the text, they are not based upon this Theorem.
∎

In this section, we apply our previous results to investigate the regularity of some typical examples of cylindrical Lévy processes as they often appear in the literature; see e.g.

5.1
Canonical cylindrical
\alpha
-stable processes

A cylindrical Lévy process
L=(L(t)\colon t\geqslant 0)
in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
is called
canonical
\alpha
-stable
for some
\alpha\in(0,2)
if its characteristic function is of the form

\displaystyle\varphi_{L(t)}(u)=\exp(-t\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\alpha}),\qquad\qquad u\in L^{2}(\R^{d}).

The existence of a cylindrical distribution with this characteristic function is guaranteed by Bochner’s theorem for cylindrical measures;
see
[
33
, Prop. IV.4.2]
; two possible explicit constructions can be found in
[
28
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
L
be a canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical process in
L^{2}(\R^{d})

for some
\alpha\in(0,2)
.
Then
L
is induced by a Lévy process in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
for some
p>1
and
(s,w)\in E_{p}
if and only if

s<-\frac{d}{2}
and
w<-\frac{d}{p}
.

Proof.

Let
\Psi
be an admissible basis of
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
.
We recall that for an arbitrary enumeration of the indices
(j,G,m)\in\mathbbm{W}^{d}
, we denote a sum over the first
n
terms in this enumeration by
\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}
; furthermore let
\Psi_{k}
be the wavelet
\Psi_{m_{k}}^{j_{k},G_{k}}
corresponding to the
k
-th term in this enumeration. Letting
\mu
be the cylindrical Lévy measure of
L
,
Lemma 2.4 in
[
28
]
shows that the Lévy measure
\nu_{n}:=\mu\circ\pi_{\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{n}}^{-1}
of any
n
-dimensional projection is given by

\displaystyle\nu_{n}(B)=\frac{\alpha}{c_{\alpha}}\int_{S^{n}}\lambda_{n}({\rm d}\xi)\int_{0}^{\infty}\1_{B}(r\xi)r^{-1-\alpha}\,{\rm d}r\qquad\text{ for all }B\in\Borel(\R^{n}),

where
\lambda_{n}
is uniformly distributed on the sphere
S^{n}=\{\xi\in\R^{n}\colon\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert=1\}
with

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}(S^{n})=r_{n}:=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n+\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1+\alpha}{2})}\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}-\alpha\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})\Gamma(-\alpha),&\text{if }\alpha\neq 1,\\
\frac{\alpha\pi}{2},&\text{if }\alpha=1.\end{cases}

First we show sufficiency of the conditions. For
p\in(1,2]
this follows directly from the fact that the embedding of
L^{2}(\R^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
is 0-Radonifying according to Theorem
4.9
.
For
p>2
, we will establish the Borel extension of
\mu
by the result in Theorem
4.4
and then apply Corollary
4.5
. We choose an enumeration of the indices
j,G
and
m
and observe that the projection
P_{n}
onto the subspace spanned by the first
n
elements in the enumeration of
\Psi
, defined in (
4.20
), satisfies

\displaystyle\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}=\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]\right\rvert^{p},\qquad f\in B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d}).

We obtain for each
n\in\N
that

\displaystyle\Sigma_{n}

\displaystyle:=\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left(\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}\wedge 1\right)\,\mu({\rm d}f)

\displaystyle=\int_{\R^{n}}\left(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\beta_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\wedge 1\right)\,(\mu\circ\pi^{-1}_{\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{n}})({\rm d}\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{c_{\alpha}}\int_{S^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert r\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\wedge 1\right)r^{-1-\alpha}\,{\rm d}r\,\lambda_{n}({\rm d}\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{c_{\alpha}(p-\alpha)}\int_{S^{n}}\left(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\alpha/p}\,\lambda_{n}({\rm d}\xi).

(
5.27
)

Letting
\lambda_{n}^{1}:=\tfrac{1}{r_{n}}\lambda_{n}
, Jensen’s inequality implies

\displaystyle\Sigma_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{pr_{n}}{c_{\alpha}(p-\alpha)}\int_{S^{n}}\left(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\alpha/p}\,\lambda_{n}^{1}({\rm d}\xi)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{pr_{n}}{c_{\alpha}(p-\alpha)}\left(\int_{S^{n}}\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\,\lambda_{n}^{1}({\rm d}\xi)\right)^{\alpha/p}.

By Lemma A.2 in
[
28
]
we have
\int_{S^{n}}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\,\lambda_{n}^{1}({\rm d}\xi)=\tfrac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1+p}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n+p}{2})}
and thus

\displaystyle\Sigma_{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{pr_{n}}{c_{\alpha}(p-\alpha)}\left(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1+p}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n+p}{2})}\right)^{\alpha/p}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{c_{\alpha}(p-\alpha)}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n+\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1+\alpha}{2})}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1+p}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n+p}{2})}\right)^{\alpha/p}\left(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\right)^{\alpha/p}.

Since
\frac{\Gamma(x+\alpha)}{\Gamma(x)}=x^{\alpha}\big(1+{\mathcal{O}}(x^{-1}))
as
x\to\infty

[
4
, Prop. 2.1.3]
,
we conclude that
\Sigma_{n}
converges to a finite limit
\Sigma_{\infty}
as
n\to\infty
since
\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}<\infty

given
s<-\tfrac{d}{2}
and
w<-\tfrac{d}{p}
(see the proof of
[
12
, Th. 3]
).
Next we derive

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{n}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left(\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\1_{B_{\R}}\big(\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\big)\left\lvert[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},f]\right\rvert^{2}\,\mu({\rm d}f)\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left(\int_{\R^{n}}\1_{B_{\R}}\left(\sum_{i,H,l}^{n}(\omega_{l}^{i})^{p}\left\lvert\beta_{l}^{i,H}\right\rvert^{p}\right)\left\lvert\beta_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{2}\,(\mu\circ\pi^{-1}_{\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{n}})({\rm d}\beta)\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left(\frac{\alpha}{c_{\alpha}}\int_{S^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\1_{B_{\R}}\left(\sum_{i,H,l}^{n}(\omega_{l}^{i})^{p}\left\lvert r\xi_{l}^{i,H}\right\rvert^{p}\right)\left\lvert r\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{2}r^{-1-\alpha}\,{\rm d}r\,\lambda_{n}({\rm d}\xi)\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha}{c_{\alpha}(2-\alpha)}\right)^{p/2}\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left(\int_{S^{n}}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{2}\left(\sum_{i,H,l}^{n}(\omega_{l}^{i})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{l}^{i,H}\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{(\alpha-2)/p}\,\lambda_{n}({\rm d}\xi)\right)^{p/2}.

Applying first Jensen’s inequality and then Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{\alpha}{c_{\alpha}(2-\alpha)}\right)^{p/2}r_{n}^{p/2}\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\int_{S^{n}}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\left(\sum_{i,H,l}^{n}(\omega_{l}^{i})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{l}^{i,H}\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{(\alpha-2)/2}\,\lambda_{n}^{1}({\rm d}\xi)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\alpha}{c_{\alpha}(2-\alpha)}\right)^{p/2}r_{n}^{p/2}\int_{S^{n}}\left(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\alpha/2}\,\lambda_{n}^{1}({\rm d}\xi)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{\alpha}{c_{\alpha}(2-\alpha)}\right)^{p/2}r_{n}^{p/2}\left(\int_{S^{n}}\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\,\lambda_{n}^{1}({\rm d}\xi)\right)^{\alpha/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\alpha}{c_{\alpha}(2-\alpha)}\right)^{p/2}r_{n}^{p/2}\left(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\right)^{\alpha/2}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1+p}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n+p}{2})}\right)^{\alpha/2}.

Since by properties of the Gamma function we have

\displaystyle r_{n}^{p/2}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1+p}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n+p}{2})}\right)^{\alpha/2}=\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n+\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1+\alpha}{2})}\right)^{p/2}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1+p}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n+p}{2})}\right)^{\alpha/2}={\mathcal{O}}(1)\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

it follows that
\Upsilon_{n}
has a finite limit as
n\to\infty
as
\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}
does.

To verify the condition in Theorem
4.4
, we fix
R>0
and conclude

\displaystyle\mu\left(\left\{f:\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{p}>R^{p}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\R^{n}}\1_{\Big\{\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\beta_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}>R^{p}\Big\}}(\beta)\,(\mu\circ\pi^{-1}_{\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{n}})({\rm d}\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{c_{\alpha}}\int_{S^{n}}\int_{\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert r\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}>R^{p}}^{\infty}r^{-1-\alpha}\,{\rm d}r\,\lambda_{n}({\rm d}\xi)

\displaystyle=R^{-\alpha}c_{\alpha}^{-1}\int_{S^{n}}\left(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\alpha/p}\,\lambda_{n}({\rm d}\xi)

\displaystyle=R^{-\alpha}\frac{p-\alpha}{p}\Sigma_{n}.

As
\Sigma_{n}\to\Sigma_{\infty}<\infty
as
n\to\infty
, we obtain
\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\big(\{f:\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}>R\}\big)=0
. Theorem
4.4
implies that
\mu
can be extended to a
\sigma
-finite measure on
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
. As we have shown
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Sigma_{n}<\infty
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Upsilon_{n}<\infty
, applying Corollary
4.5
completes the proof for sufficiency.

We now show the necessity of the conditions in the hypothesis. First we consider the case
p\geqslant 2
.
We define
A_{n}:=\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}
and, applying Jensen’s inequality to the concave
function
x\mapsto x^{\alpha/p}
, we obtain from (
5.1
), using again
[
28
, Lem. A.2]
,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{pA_{n}^{\alpha/p}}{c_{\alpha}(p-\alpha)}\int_{S^{n}}\left(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}A_{n}^{-1}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\alpha/p}\,\lambda_{n}({\rm d}\xi)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{pA_{n}^{\alpha/p}}{c_{\alpha}(p-\alpha)}\int_{S^{n}}\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}A_{n}^{-1}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{\alpha}\,\lambda_{n}({\rm d}\xi)=\frac{pA_{n}^{\alpha/p}}{c_{\alpha}(p-\alpha)}.

For
s\geqslant-\tfrac{d}{2}
or
w\geqslant-\tfrac{d}{p}
we have
A_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
(see the proof of
[
12
, Th. 3]
). It follows
\Sigma_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, showing that Condition (
4.22
) is not verified.

For
p\in(1,2)
we calculate as above for
\Sigma_{n}
that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}

\displaystyle:=\int_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\left(\left\lVert P_{n}f\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{2}\wedge 1\right)\,\mu({\rm d}f)=\frac{2}{c_{\alpha}(2-\alpha)}\int_{S^{n}}\left(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\alpha/p}\,\lambda_{n}({\rm d}\xi).

For
p\geqslant\alpha
, we can conclude as above for
\Sigma_{n}
that
\Lambda_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
for
s\geqslant-\tfrac{d}{2}
or
w\geqslant-\tfrac{d}{p}
. It follows that
Condition (
4.23
) is not met for these values of
s
and
w
, which shows the necessity.
For
p<\alpha
, applying Jensen’s inequality to the convex function
x\mapsto x^{\alpha/p}

gives us

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2r_{n}}{c_{\alpha}(2-\alpha)}\int_{S^{n}}\bigg(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\bigg)^{\alpha/p}\,\lambda_{n}^{1}({\rm d}\xi)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{2r_{n}}{c_{\alpha}(2-\alpha)}\bigg(\int_{S^{n}}\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{m}^{j,G}\right\rvert^{p}\,\lambda_{n}^{1}({\rm d}\xi)\bigg)^{\alpha/p}=\frac{2r_{n}}{c_{\alpha}(2-\alpha)}\bigg(\sum_{j,G,m}^{n}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1+p}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n+p}{2})}\bigg)^{\alpha/p}.

Since
\frac{\Gamma(x+\alpha)}{\Gamma(x)}=x^{\alpha}\big(1+{\mathcal{O}}(x^{-1}))
as
x\to\infty
, see
[
4
, Prop. 2.1.3]
,
and
\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}=\infty
, it follows
\Lambda_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, showing that Condition (
4.23
) in Corollary
4.5
is not met.
∎

Recall that
R_{p}
denotes the
(s,w)
-plane where the embeddings
L^{2}(\R^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
are 0-Radonifying.
For
p\leqslant 2
, Theorem
5.1
states that
L
is induced by a Lévy process in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
if and only if
(s,w)\in R_{p}
. However, for
p>2
the Theorem gives a stronger result, in that the region of the
(s,w)
-plane where
L
is induced by a Lévy process in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
is a proper superset of
R_{p}
.

5.2
Hedgehog cylindrical Lévy process

In this section let
L
be a cylindrical Lévy process in
L^{2}(\R^{d})
of the form

\displaystyle L(t)f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}[f,e_{k}]a_{k}\ell_{k}\qquad\text{for all }f\in L^{2}(\R^{d}),\,t\geqslant 0,

(
5.28
)

where
(e_{k})_{k\in\N}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\R^{d})
and
(\ell_{k})_{k\in\N}
are identically distributed and independent symmetric real-valued Lévy processes with characteristics
(0,0,\rho)
for a Lévy measure
\rho\neq 0
in
\R
. By Theorem
4.3
it is sufficient for our analysis to focus on the symmetric case.
The sequence
(a_{k})_{k\in\N}
is real-valued and satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\R}\big(\left\lvert a_{k}c_{k}\beta\right\rvert^{2}\wedge 1\big)\,\rho({\mathrm{d}}\beta)<\infty

(
5.29
)

for each
(c_{k})_{k\in\N}\in\ell^{2}(\R)
.
This condition guarantees that the sum in (
5.28
) converges
P
-a.s. in
\R
; see
[
27
, Lem. 4.2]
.

The support of the cylindrical Lévy measure
\mu
of
L
is in
\bigcup_{k\in\N}\{\beta e_{k}:\,\beta\in\R\}
, as
(\ell_{k})_{k\in\N}
are independent, that is to say the measure only has weight on the axes. For this reason,
we refer to this process as a
hedgehog cylindrical process
.

We first present further corollaries to Theorem
3.2
and Remark
3.3
tailored to this setting.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
L
be a cylindrical Lévy process of the form as in (
5.28
), where

\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\N}\subseteq B^{p^{\prime}}_{-s,-w}(\R^{d})
for some
p>1
and
(s,w)\in E_{p}
.
Then

(

\displaystyle\sum_{j,G,m}(\omega_{m}^{j})^{p}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lvert[\Psi_{m}^{j,G},a_{k}e_{k}]\right\rvert^{2}\int_{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lVert a_{k}e_{k}\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{-1}}\beta^{2}\,\rho({\rm d}\beta)\Bigg)^{p/2}<\infty;

Proof.

with projection on the

\displaystyle(\mu\circ\pi_{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}}^{-1})({\rm d}\beta_{1}\cdots{\rm d}\beta_{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\rho\circ m_{a_{k}})({\mathrm{d}}\beta_{k}),

(

To characterise the Besov membership of a hedgehog process
L
we introduce some indices in terms of the Lévy measure of the real-valued Lévy processes
\ell_{k}
in the representation (
5.28
). For this purpose, let
\rho
be a Lévy measure in
\R
and define for
q\in\Rp
:

\displaystyle\overline{\tau}^{(q)}

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\tau\geqslant 0}\left\{\limsup_{\xi\downarrow 0}\xi^{-\tau}\int_{B_{\R}^{c}}\big(\xi^{q}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{q}\wedge 1\big)\,\rho({\rm d}\beta)=\infty\right\},

(
5.35
)

\displaystyle\underline{\tau}^{(q)}

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\tau\geqslant 0}\left\{\liminf_{\xi\downarrow 0}\xi^{-\tau}\int_{B_{\R}^{c}}\big(\xi^{q}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{q}\wedge 1\big)\,\rho({\rm d}\beta)=\infty\right\}.

(
5.36
)

In all definitions above we apply the convention
\sup\emptyset=-\infty
and
\inf\emptyset=\infty
.
It is easy to see that
\overline{\tau}^{(q)}\leqslant\underline{\tau}^{(q)}\leqslant q
when
\rho\neq 0
. The examples following Theorem
5.5
show calculations of these indices in a number of standard situations.

The following proposition establishes a simple interpretation of
\overline{\tau}^{(q)}
. We recall that a Lévy process with Lévy measure
\rho
has finite
p
-th moments if and only if
\int_{B_{\R}^{c}}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{p}\,\rho({\rm d}\beta)<\infty
.

Proposition 5.4
.

For a Lévy measure
\rho\neq 0
on
\Borel(\R)
define

\displaystyle p_{\max}:=\sup\left\{p>0\colon\int_{B_{\R}^{c}}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{p}\,\rho({\rm d}\beta)<\infty\right\}.

If
p_{\max}>0
then
\overline{\tau}^{(q)}=p_{\max}\wedge q
and thus
(p_{\max}\wedge q)\leqslant\underline{\tau}^{(q)}\leqslant q
for all
q\geqslant 0
.

Proof.

To demonstrate this, we shall consider the following indices:

\displaystyle\overline{\tau}_{1}^{(q)}

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\tau\geqslant 0\colon\limsup_{\xi\downarrow 0}\xi^{-\tau}\int_{1<\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant\xi^{-1}}\xi^{q}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{q}\,\rho({\rm d}\beta)<\infty\right\}\qquad\text{for }q\in\Rp,

\displaystyle\overline{\tau}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\tau>0\colon\limsup_{\xi\downarrow 0}\xi^{-\tau}\int_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert>\xi^{-1}}\rho({\rm d}\beta)<\infty\right\}.

We define a finite measure
\overline{\rho}:={\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{B_{\R}^{c}}}
; clearly we may replace
\rho
with
\overline{\rho}
in the definitions of
\overline{\tau}_{1}^{(q)}
and
\overline{\tau}_{2}
. By Markov’s inequality we have, for
\xi<1
and
p<p_{\max}
,

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}\big(\{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert>\xi^{-1}\}\big)\leqslant\xi^{p}\int_{\R}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{p}\,\overline{\rho}({\mathrm{d}}\beta)=\xi^{p}\int_{B_{\R}^{c}}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{p}\,\rho({\mathrm{d}}\beta),

thus showing
\overline{\tau}_{2}\geqslant p_{\max}
. On the other side, for
\tau<\overline{\tau}_{2}

there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}\big(\{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert>t\}\big)\leqslant Ct^{-\tau}\qquad\text{for all }t\geqslant 1.

The tail formula for the integral shows for
0<p<\tau
that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\R}^{c}}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{p}\,\rho({\mathrm{d}}\beta)=\int_{\R}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{p}\,\overline{\rho}({\mathrm{d}}\beta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{\rho}\big(\{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{p}>t\}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\leqslant\overline{\rho}(\R)+C\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{\tau}{p}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t<\infty,

which enables us to conclude
p_{\max}\geqslant\overline{\tau}_{2}
, and hence
\overline{\tau}_{2}=p_{\max}
.

Next choose some
\tau<\overline{\tau}_{2}\wedge q
. Fubini’s theorem implies for
\xi\in(0,1)
that

\displaystyle\int_{1<\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant\xi^{-1}}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{q}\,\overline{\rho}({\mathrm{d}}\beta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{\rho}\big(\{\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{q}\1_{\{1<\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant\xi^{-1}\}}>t\}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\overline{\rho}\big(\{1<\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant\xi^{-1}\}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{1}^{\xi^{-q}}\overline{\rho}\big(\{t^{\frac{1}{q}}<\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant\xi^{-1}\}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\overline{\rho}\big(\{1<\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant\xi^{-1}\}\big)+\int_{1}^{\xi^{-q}}\overline{\rho}\big(\{t^{\frac{1}{q}}<\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\overline{\rho}(\R)+C\int_{1}^{-\xi^{q}}t^{-\frac{\tau}{q}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\lesssim 1+\xi^{\tau-q}.

It follows
\xi^{q-\tau}\int_{1<\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leqslant\xi^{-1}}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{q}\,\rho({\mathrm{d}}\beta)<\infty
for all
\xi<1
,
implying
\tau\leqslant\overline{\tau}_{1}^{(q)}
. As
\tau<\overline{\tau}_{2}\wedge q
is arbitrary we have shown that
\overline{\tau}_{1}^{(q)}\geqslant\overline{\tau}_{2}\wedge q
. As
\overline{\tau}^{(q)}=\overline{\tau}_{1}^{(q)}\wedge\overline{\tau}_{2}
and
\overline{\tau}_{2}=p_{\max}
, we conclude
\overline{\tau}^{(q)}=p_{\max}\wedge q
.
∎

The following result gives conditions such that a hedgehog process is induced by a Lévy process in a certain Besov space.
The critical value will be the parameters
\overline{\tau}^{(\min\{p,2\})}
and
\underline{\tau}^{(\max\{p,2\})}
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
L
be defined by (
5.28
) and let
p>1
and
(s,w)\in E_{p}
.
Define

\displaystyle q_{\min}:=\inf\left\{q>0\colon\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert a_{k}e_{k}\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{q}<\infty\right\}.

Then,

(1)

L
is induced by a Lévy process in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
if
one of the following is satisfied:

(i)

(s,w)\in R_{p}
;

(ii)

(s,w)\in R_{p}^{c}\;
and
\;q_{\min}<\overline{\tau}^{(\min\{p,2\})}
.

(2)

L
is not induced by a process in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
if:

(s,w)\in R_{p}^{c}\;
and
\;q_{\min}>\underline{\tau}^{(\max\{p,2\})}
.

Proof.

Part (1): the first alternative condition follows from the 0-Radonification in Theorem
4.9
. To show the second alternative, we note that
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
is of type
\min\{p,2\}
by the isometry with
\ell^{p}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})
defined in (
2.7
). By Proposition 7.1.16 in
[
18
]
, if
q\leqslant\min\{p,2\}
is such that
E\left\lvert\ell_{1}\right\rvert^{q}<\infty
then
\big(\left\lVert a_{k}e_{k}\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\big)_{k\in\N}\in\ell^{q}(\R)
implies that
\sum_{k\in\N}a_{k}e_{k}\ell_{k}(1)
converges in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
in
q
-th mean. This limit is a Lévy procss in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
which induces
L
.
Finally we note that, by Proposition
5.4
,
\ell_{1}
has moments of any order smaller than
\overline{\tau}^{(q)}
for any
q\leqslant 2
.

Part (2): by Corollary
5.2
,
L
is not induced by a Lévy process in
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
if

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\R}\left(\left\lVert a_{k}e_{k}\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{\max\{2,p\}}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{\max\{2,p\}}\wedge 1\right)\,\rho({\rm d}\beta)=\infty.

(
5.37
)

Due to the hypothesis, we can choose
q>\underline{\tau}^{(\max\{2,p\})}

such that
\big(\left\lVert a_{k}e_{k}\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}\big)_{k\in\N}\notin\ell^{q}(\R)
.
The very definition of
\underline{\tau}^{(\max\{2,p\})}
guarantees that there exists a constant
K
such that, for large enough
k
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\R}^{c}}\big(\left\lVert a_{k}e_{k}\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{\max\{2,p\}}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{\max\{2,p\}}\wedge 1\big)\,\rho({\rm d}\beta)

\displaystyle\geqslant K\left\lVert a_{k}e_{k}\right\rVert_{B^{p}_{s,w}}^{q},

which establishes (
5.37
).
∎

Remark 5.6
.

We may conclude that
(s,w)\in R_{p}
implies
q_{\min}\leqslant\underline{\tau}^{(\max\{2,p\})}
, as otherwise Part (1) and (2) of Theorem
5.5
would contradict. This equality can also be proven analytically.

The first two examples we present show that, in the case each
\ell_{k}
has moments of all orders, the critical summability is of a particularly simple form.

Example 5.7
.

Let
L
be a cylindrical Lévy process of the form (
5.28
) with
\rho=\delta_{1}
; thus each of the
\ell_{k}
is a Poisson process with unit intensity and Condition (
5.29
) is satisfied for
(a_{k})_{k\in\N}\in\ell^{\infty}(\N)
. As
\rho
has moments of all orders, Proposition
5.4
implies
\overline{\tau}^{(q)}=\underline{\tau}^{(q)}=q
for each

. Thus, the critical summability needs to satisfy
q_{\min}<p\wedge 2
for inclusion and
q_{\min}>p\vee 2
for exclusion.

Example 5.8
.

Let
\rho({\mathrm{d}}\beta)=\1_{\{\beta\neq 0\}}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{-\zeta}e^{-\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert}\,{\mathrm{d}}\beta
for some
\zeta\in(0,3)
, this gives rise to tempered stable processes. For
\zeta=1
this gives the symmetric Gamma process and for
\zeta=\tfrac{3}{2}
this gives the symmetric inverse Gaussian process.
For

we have

\displaystyle\int_{\R}\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{q-\zeta}e^{-\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert}\,{\mathrm{d}}\beta=2\Gamma(q-\zeta+1)<\infty;

we conclude that
\rho
has moments of all orders and thus again we have
\overline{\tau}^{(q)}=\underline{\tau}^{(q)}=q
for each

. Thus, the critical summability again needs to satisfy

for inclusion and
q_{\min}>p\vee 2
for exclusion.

.

(b)

Let

5.2.1

Let

Proposition 5.12

We first state an intermediate result on the summability of the Besov space weights.

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
\omega_{m}^{j}=\omega_{m}^{j}(p,s,w)
be the wavelet weight constants for
B^{p}_{s,w}(\R^{d})
for some
p>0
,
s<\tfrac{d}{p}-\tfrac{d}{2}
and

. Then
\big(\omega_{m}^{j}\big)_{j,G,m}\in\ell^{k}(\mathbbm{W}^{d})
for some

if and only if

\displaystyle k>\max\left\{-\frac{d}{w},\frac{2dp}{2d-dp-2ps}\right\}.

Proof.

We must assess the convergence of

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{jk(s-\frac{d}{p}+\frac{d}{2})}\sum_{G\in G^{j}}\sum_{m\in\Z^{d}}(1+2^{-2j}\left\lvert m\right\rvert^{2})^{\frac{kw}{2}}.

We first consider
S_{j}:=\sum_{m\in\Z^{d}}(1+2^{-2j}\left\lvert m\right\rvert^{2})^{\frac{kw}{2}}
.
We have

for each

if and only if

(see the proof of
[

) which gives the first term in the maximum above, recalling that

.
If
S_{j}<\infty
for each

S_{j}
is asymptotically
{\mathcal{O}}(2^{jd})
as
j\to\infty
according to
[
12
, Th. 3]
, and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{jk(s-\frac{d}{p}+\frac{d}{2})}\sum_{G\in G^{j}}S_{j}

\displaystyle=2^{d}S_{0}+(2^{d}-1)\sum_{j\geqslant 1}2^{jk(s-\frac{d}{p}+\frac{d}{2})}S_{j},

Proof of Proposition

.

5.39

p>2

Abstract
. For a nonsingular hypersurface X ⊂ ℙ n , n ≥ 4 , X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n},n\geq 4, of degree d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , we show that the space H 1 ​ ( X , End ⁡ ( T X ) ) H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,(T_{X})) of infinitesimal deformations of the tangent bundle T X T_{X} has dimension ( n + d − 1 d ) ⁡ ( d − 1 ) {n+d-1\choose d}(d-1) and all infinitesimal deformations are unobstructed even though H 2 ​ ( X , End ⁡ ( T X ) ) H^{2}(X,\mathrm{End}\,(T_{X})) can be nonzero. 1.
Introduction

We work over complex numbers. For a vector bundle
E
on a variety
X
, we use the notation
h^{i}(X,E):=\dim H^{i}(X,E).

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n},n\geq 4,
be a nonsingular hypersurface of degree
d\geq 2.
Let
T_{X}
be its tangent bundle and denote by
\mathrm{End}\,(T_{X})
(resp.
\mathrm{End}\,_{0}(T_{X})
) the bundle of endomorphisms (resp. traceless endomorphisms) of
T_{X}
. Then
H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,(T_{X}))=H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,_{0}(T_{X}))
(from
H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})=0
) describes the infinitesimal deformations of
T_{X}
as vector bundles. In
[
P
, Theorem 6.1]
, Peternell proved that
H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,_{0}(T_{X}))\neq 0
, namely, the tangent bundle has nontrivial infinitesimal deformations. This naturally leads to the following questions.

Q1

What is the exact value of
h^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,(T_{X}))
?

Q2

Are all elements of
H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,(T_{X}))
unobstructed? Equivalently, is the deformation space of
T_{X}
smooth at the point corresponding to
T_{X}
?

Furthermore, the tangent bundle
T_{X}
is a stable bundle with respect to the polarization
\mathcal{O}(1)
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
by
[
PW
, Corollary 0.3]
. Thus
T_{X}
represents a point
[T_{X}]
in the
moduli scheme of stable bundles on
X
. Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be the irreducible component of the moduli scheme containing
[T_{X}]
.

Q3

What is the geometric nature of the algebraic variety
{\mathcal{M}}
?

Our goal is to study these three questions.
The answer to
Q1
is the following, to be proved in Section
2
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n},n\geq 4,
be a nonsingular projective hypersurface of degree
d\geq 2.
Then

h^{1}(X,{\rm End}_{0}(T_{X}))={n+d-1\choose d}(d-1)
.

The obstruction to realizing an infinitesimal deformation in
H^{1}(X,{\rm End}(T_{X}))
as an actual deformation of
T_{X}
lies in
H^{2}(X,{\rm End}(T_{X}))=H^{2}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X}).
Thus the answer to
Q2
would have been straightforward if
H^{2}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})=0.
However, this group does not always vanish as the following theorem says, which is proved in Section
3
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n},n\geq 4,
be a nonsingular projective hypersurface of degree
d\geq 2.

(i)

H^{2}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})=0
if
n\geq 6
.

(ii)

When
n=4
,

h^{2}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if}\ d=2,3\\
45,&\text{if}\ d=4\\
224,&\text{if}\ d=5.\end{cases}

Consequently,
Q2
is a subtler question for some values of
n
and
d
.
Nevertheless, we answer
Q2
affirmatively in Section
4
as follows for all
n\geq 4
and
d\geq 2
by an explicit construction of deformations of
T_{X}
, which also gives a partial answer to
Q3
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n},n\geq 4,
be a nonsingular projective hypersurface of degree
d\geq 2.
Then there is an affine space
{\mathscr{A}}
with a base point
o\in{\mathscr{A}}
and a torsionfree sheaf
\mathbb{E}\to X\times{\mathscr{A}}
which satisfy the following properties.

(i)

\dim{\mathscr{A}}={n+d-1\choose d}(d-1)
.

(ii)

The restriction
\mathbb{E}|_{X\times\{o\}}
is isomorphic to the tangent bundle
T_{X}
.

(iii)

There is a Zarisky open subset
{\mathscr{A}}^{\circ}\subset{\mathscr{A}}
with
o\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\circ}
such that for any
\alpha\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\circ}
,
the restriction
\mathbb{E}|_{X\times\{\alpha\}}
is a vector bundle of determinant
\wedge^{n-1}(\mathbb{E}|_{X\times\{\alpha\}})\cong K^{-1}_{X}
.

(iv)

There is a Zariski open subset
{\mathscr{A}}^{\rm faithful}\subset{\mathscr{A}}^{\circ}
with
o\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\rm faithful}
such that for any
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\rm faithful}
, the two vector bundles
\mathbb{E}|_{X\times\{\alpha\}}
and
\mathbb{E}|_{X\times\{\alpha^{\prime}\}}
are isomorphic only when
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
.

(v)

There is a Zariski open subset
{\mathscr{A}}^{\rm stable}\subset{\mathscr{A}}^{\circ}
with
o\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\rm stable}
such that for any
\alpha\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\rm stable}
, the restriction
\mathbb{E}|_{X\times\{\alpha\}}
is a stable vector bundle.

Consequently, the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}
is nonsingular at
[T_{X}]
and it is a rational variety.

What happens when
n=3
and
X
is a surface?
A construction analogous to
{\mathscr{A}}
and
\mathbb{E}
of Theorem
3
works even when
n=3
. However, the statement of Theorem
1
does not hold when
n=3
. In fact, by a general theory, the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}
at the point
[T_{X}]
has dimension at least
h^{1}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})-h^{2}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})
. One can check (see Proposition
11
) that when
n=3
and

,

h^{1}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})-h^{2}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})=\frac{1}{3}(d-1)(7d^{2}-5d-3),

which is strictly bigger than
\dim{\mathscr{A}}=\frac{1}{2}(d-1)(d^{2}+3d+2)
. Thus one needs new ideas to study
Q1
,
Q2
and
Q3
for surfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
.

We would like to comment on the cases missing in Theorem
2
. When
n=4
, we expect for all
d\geq 4,

(1.1)

h^{2}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})=(11d+1){d-1\choose 3}.

This was confirmed for the Fermat hypersurfaces for
4\leq d\leq 25
by using the Macaulay2 program(
[
GS
]
).
Also this agrees with Theorem
2
(ii) for
d=4,5
. We have not been able to verify this for
d\geq 6
. When
n=5
, similarly we checked for Fermat hyperfaces of degree
d
that
h^{2}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})=1
for
d=3
and
h^{2}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})=0
for
4\leq d\leq 25
.

To close the introduction, let us mention that
the third-named author erroneously asserted in
[
Hw
, page 77]
that the tangent bundle of an irreducible Hermitian symmetric space cannot be deformed. This contradicts Theorem
3
for the quadric hypersurface, which is an irreducible Hermitian symmetric space of type IV. The flaw was in the limiting argument in the proof of
[

[

]

.

2.
Dimension of
H^{1}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})

In this section, we present a number of cohomological computations associated to the tangent bundle
T_{X}
of a smooth projective hypersurface
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, including a proof of Theorem
1
.

First we recall the following result, Bott’s formula (for example,
[
OSS
, page 8]
).

Proposition 4
.

h^{i}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},\Omega^{j}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(k))=\begin{cases}{n+k-j\choose k}{k-1\choose j},&\text{if}\ i=0,0\leq j\leq n,k>j\\
{j-k\choose-k}{-k-1\choose n-j},&\text{if}\ i=n,0\leq j\leq n,k<j-n\\
1,&\text{if}\ k=0,i=j\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

From this, we can show:

Lemma 5
.

For
0\leq i\leq n-2
and
d\geq 2
, we have
\dim H^{i}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},\mathrm{End}\,T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(-d))=0
.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
4
combined with the Euler sequence tensored by
\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(-d)
:

0\to\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(-d)\to\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(1-d)^{\oplus(n+1)}\to\mathrm{End}\,T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(-d)\to 0.

∎

Recall the following from
[
F
, Satz 8.11]
.

Proposition 6
.

For a nonsingular projective hypersurface
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and each
0<i<n-1
, we have

\begin{cases}H^{i}(X,\Omega_{X}^{j}(k))=0,&\text{if}\ i+j\neq n-1,k\neq 0\\
H^{i}(X,\Omega_{X}^{j})=0,&\text{if}\ i+j\neq n-1,i\neq j\\
H^{i}(X,\Omega_{X}^{i})={\mathbb{C}},&\text{if}\ 2i\neq n-1.\end{cases}

Lemma 7
.

For a nonsingular projective hypersurface
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n},n\geq 4
of degree
d\geq 2
, the following holds.

(i)

h^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}(d))={n+d-1\choose d}(d-1)
.

(ii)

h^{i}(X,\Omega_{X}(k))=0
for
0<i<n-2
and
k\neq 0
.

(iii)

h^{i}(X,T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(-d)|_{X})=0
for
i=0,1,2
.

(iv)

h^{1}(X,T_{X}(-d))=1
.

Proof.

From the vanishing
h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})=0
and the cotangent sequence

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}|_{X}(d)\to\Omega_{X}(d)\to 0,

we have

h^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}(d))=h^{0}(X,\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}|_{X}(d))-1.

Combining Proposition
4
and the following sequence

(2.1)

0\to\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}\to\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(d)\to\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}|_{X}(d)\to 0,

we obtain

h^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}(d))=h^{0}(X,\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}|_{X}(d))-1=h^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(d))={n+d-1\choose d}(d-1),

which proves (i).

(ii) is immediate from Proposition
6
.

Since

T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}\cong\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}^{n-1}(n+1)
, for
i=0,1,2
, we have

h^{i}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(-d))=h^{i}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}^{n-1}(n+1-d))=0

from Proposition
4
.
Similarly, we have
h^{i}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(-2d))=0
for
i=1,2
. Furthermore, Proposition
4
gives

h^{3}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(-2d))=h^{3}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}^{n-1}(n+1-2d))=0.

Thus (iii) follows from the sequence

0\to T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(-2d)\to T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(-d)\to T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(-d)|_{X}\to 0.

Finally, to prove (iv), note that

h^{n-2}(X,\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(2d-n-1)|_{X})=0=h^{n-1}(X,\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(2d-n-1)|_{X}),

which follows from Proposition
4
combined with the sequence

0\to\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(d-n-1)\to\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(2d-n-1)\to\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(2d-n-1)|_{X}\to 0.

Then Serre duality and
K_{X}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(d-n-1)
give

h^{n-1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(d-n-1))=h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(n+1-d)\otimes K_{X})=h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})=1

and

h^{1}(X,T_{X}(-d))=h^{n-2}(X,\Omega_{X}(2d-n-1))=h^{n-1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(d-n-1))=1.

∎

Remark 8
.

Note that
h^{n-2}(X,\Omega_{X}(2d-n-1))=1
is in contrast to
h^{n-2}(X,\Omega_{X}(k))=0
for
k>2d-n-1
from
[
L
, Lemma 3.8]
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
be as in Lemma
7
. Then

(i)

h^{0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,(T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}})|_{X})=h^{0}(X,T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}|_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})=1
.

(ii)

h^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,(T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}})|_{X})=h^{1}(X,T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}|_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})=0
.

Proof.

Taking the tensor product of the cotangent sequence and
T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}|_{X}
, we obtain

0\to T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(-d)|_{X}\to\mathrm{End}\,(T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}})|_{X}\to T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}|_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X}\to 0.

By Lemma
7
(iii), we obtain
H^{i}(X,\mathrm{End}\,(T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}})|_{X})=H^{i}(X,T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}|_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})
for
i=0,1
.

From the sequence

0\to\mathrm{End}\,(T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}})(-d)\to\mathrm{End}\,(T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}})\to\mathrm{End}\,(T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}})|_{X}\to 0,

and
H^{i}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},\mathrm{End}\,(T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}})(-d))=0
for
i=0,1,2
in Lemma
5
,
we have

h^{i}(X,\mathrm{End}\,(T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}})|_{X})=h^{i}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},\mathrm{End}\,(T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}))

for
i=0,1.
Thus Lemma
9
follows from the fact that
T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}
is simple (
[
OSS
, Ch. I, Lemma 4.1.2]
) and it has no infinitesimal deformation (because the flag manifold
{\mathbb{P}}(T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}})
is infinitesimally rigid).
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem

.

Consider the long exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to H^{0}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})\to H^{0}(X,T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}|_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})\to H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}(d))

\displaystyle\to H^{1}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})\to H^{1}(X,T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}|_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})\to\cdots.

Recall that
T_{X}
is stable from
[
PW
, Corollary 0.3]
, hence
h^{0}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})=1.
Thus
Lemma
9
implies that
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}(d))\cong H^{1}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})
.
Then Lemma
7
(i) gives the value of
h^{1}(X,T_{X}\otimes\Omega_{X})=h^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,_{0}(T_{x})).

∎

4.
A birational model of
{\mathcal{M}}

Definition 12
.

Let
V
be a complex vector space of dimension
n+1\geq 5
. Fix an integer
d\geq 2
.
Regard
\mathrm{Sym}^{d-1}V^{*}\otimes V^{*}
as a subspace of
\bigotimes^{d}V^{*}
consisting of elements
\alpha\in\bigotimes^{d}V^{*}
satisfying

\alpha(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d-1},v_{d})=\alpha(v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(d-1)},v_{d})

for any permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
.
Let
{\mathscr{A}}\subset\mathrm{Sym}^{d-1}V^{*}\otimes V^{*}
be the kernel of the symmetrizing map
{\rm sym}^{d}:\bigotimes^{d}V^{*}\to\mathrm{Sym}^{d}V^{*}
. Equivalently, it is the
subspace consisting of
\alpha\in\mathrm{Sym}^{d-1}V^{*}\otimes V^{*}
satisfying

\alpha(v,v,\ldots,v,u)=-(d-1)\>\alpha(u,v,\ldots,v,v)\mbox{ for any }u,v\in V.

Lemma 13
.

\dim{\mathscr{A}}={n+d-1\choose d}\cdot(d-1)
and
{\mathscr{A}}\cap\mathrm{Sym}^{d}V^{*}=\{0\}.

Proof.

Since the restriction

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{n+d-1\choose d-1}\cdot(n+1)-{n+d\choose d}={n+d-1\choose d}\cdot(d-1).

isomorphically to
\mathrm{Sym}^{d}V^{*}
, we have
{\mathscr{A}}\cap\mathrm{Sym}^{d}V^{*}=\{0\}.
∎

Trends and hotspots in subjective idiopathic tinnitus and anxiety disorder research based on Bibliometrics
Authors: Qiang Huang, Baoguo Wang, Zhenya Wang, Yi Cao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Subjective idiopathic tinnitus is characterized by the subjective perception of sound in the absence of an external source, significantly impairing quality of life. Studies have found that a considerable number of tinnitus patients experience comorbid negative emotional states such as anxiety disorders, while emotional dysregulation can, in turn, exacerbate the perceived tinnitus, indicating a close interrelationship. A systematic bibliometric summary in this field is currently lacking. This study aims to employ bibliometric methods to reveal the research dynamics, collaborative networks, and emerging trends in the tinnitus-anxiety disorder association field.Methods:A total of 773 publications related to tinnitus and anxiety/mood disorders from 2014 to 2025 were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). After screening, 262 valid publications were included. Bibliometric and visual analyses of countries/regions, authors, institutions, journals, keywords, and references were performed using VOSviewer (v1.6.20), CiteSpace (v6.4.R2), Scimago Graphica, R-bibliometrix (R 4.5.1; Massimo Aria & Corrado Cuccurullo, University of Naples Federico II), and Microsoft Excel.Results:The United States produced the most publications (58) and maintained extensive international collaborations. The University of Nottingham was the most prolific institution (24 publications). Frontiers in Neurology was the most popular journal, publishing the most articles (13). LARYNGOSCOPE was the most cited journal (314 citations). Gerhard Andersson was the leading contributor, being the most prolific author (11 publications) and the most cited (1145 citations). Keyword co-occurrence analysis identified “Tinnitus, anxiety, depression, prevalence, severity, association, hospital anxiety” as core research hotspots. Co-cited reference analysis indicated that the tinnitus handicap inventory, the hospital anxiety and depression scale, alongside investigations into tinnitus mechanisms and treatment strategies, received the most significant attention.Conclusion:This bibliometric study provides a comprehensive overview of the research landscape concerning subjective idiopathic tinnitus and anxiety disorders. It identifies the most influential countries/regions, institutions, and authors, and delineates the current research hotspots and emerging trends. The findings offer theoretical guidance for future research into the shared mechanisms and treatment strategies for tinnitus comorbid with emotional disorders.

1. Introduction

Subjective Idiopathic Tinnitus (SIT) is a common auditory perception phenomenon characterized by the subjective sensation of sound in the absence of an external acoustic source. Its etiology is complex, with the majority of cases classified as idiopathic. The global prevalence is estimated to be between 10% and 15%, significantly impacting quality of life and mental health.[1,2] Anxiety disorders (AD), as a category of common mental illnesses, are highly comorbid with tinnitus. Epidemiological studies indicate that the prevalence of anxiety symptoms among tinnitus patients can exceed 50%. These 2 conditions mutually exacerbate each other, forming a vicious cycle that significantly increases the healthcare burden and societal costs.[3,4] Although existing interventions such as sound therapy, cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), and pharmacotherapy can alleviate symptoms to some extent, limited efficacy, significant individual variability, and high recurrence rates remain major challenges in clinical management.[5,6]

In recent years, accumulating evidence suggests a bidirectional association between tinnitus and AD, potentially influencing each other through mechanisms such as central nervous system remodeling, hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis dysregulation, and neuroinflammation.[7,8] For instance, functional neuroimaging studies have revealed that abnormal activity in the default mode network and limbic system of tinnitus patients is closely related to the degree of anxiety, while an anxious state can, in turn, enhance the perceived intensity and distress of tinnitus.[9,10] More importantly, psychophysiological studies suggest that stress-induced autonomic nervous system dysfunction may play a key role in the comorbidity mechanism of tinnitus and anxiety, distinguishing their clinical presentation from other auditory or mental disorders.[3,11] This indicates that identifying biomarkers or psychological characteristics of the tinnitus-anxiety comorbidity could aid early diagnosis and stratified treatment, holding significant translational value.

In clinical practice, research integrating psychoacoustic and psychiatric assessments is gradually gaining attention. Related advancements may provide new directions for personalized medicine: first, by using multimodal interventions (such as combining auditory training and mindfulness-based stress reduction) to modulate emotional and auditory processing pathways[12]; second, by utilizing neurofeedback or noninvasive brain stimulation techniques to target shared neural circuits[13]; third, by developing machine learning algorithm (MLA) that use clinical and electrophysiological data to optimize treatment decisions[14]; These studies deepen the understanding of tinnitus-anxiety interactions from a multidisciplinary perspective and enhance the clinical application potential of the research.

However, current research on SIT and AD remains relatively fragmented, lacking systematic knowledge mapping analysis. There has been no dedicated bibliometric study comprehensively sort out the development trends, research hotspots, and key scientific issues in this field. To address this gap, this study employs visual bibliometric tools to conduct a visual analysis of global relevant literature published between 2014 and 2025. It summarizes the strengths and weaknesses of existing research from aspects such as thematic evolution, keyword co-occurrence, highly cited literature, and emerging directions, aiming to provide a theoretical basis and strategic reference for future basic exploration and clinical practice.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Source database and search strategy

Literature data were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database. All records were downloaded up to October 15, 2025, to ensure consistency in the search process. The final search strategy employed in this study was: TS (Topic Search) = [(“Subjective idiopathic tinnitus” or “tinnitus” or “idiopathic tinnitus” or “subjective tinnitus”) and (“anxiety” or “anxiety disorders” or “mood disorders”)] and LA = (English), with the publication date restricted from January 1, 2014, to October 15, 2025, and the document type limited to article or review. 2.2. Bibliometric methods and data processing

This study employed a multistep bibliometric analysis. The reporting adhered to the BIBLIO checklist (Guidelines for Bibliometric Reviews) to ensure methodological transparency and completeness.

2.2.1. Data cleaning and normalization

The raw data retrieved from WoSCC underwent a systematic cleaning and normalization process to ensure data quality. Firstly, duplicate records were identified and removed based on digital object identifier, title and author information. Secondly, to facilitate accurate analysis, key metadata fields were standardized: author and institution names were manually reviewed and unified (e.g., “Univ Nottingham” and “University of Nottingham” were normalized to a single form); country/region names were verified and corrected based on institutional affiliations; keywords (both Author Keywords and Keywords Plus) were processed by merging singular/plural forms; and resolving acronyms to their full names (e.g., “CBT” to “cognitive behavioral therapy”) where appropriate. This normalized dataset served as the foundation for all subsequent analyses.

2.2.2. Citation and co-citation analysis

Citation analysis was performed to assess the impact of publications, authors and journals. Metrics such as total citation count and average citations per item were calculated. Co-citation analysis was conducted using VOSviewer (v1.6.20; Centre for Science and Technology Studies [CWTS], Leiden University) and CiteSpace (v6.4.R2; College of Computing and Informatics, Drexel University) software. This analysis identifies references that are frequently cited together by the retrieved publications, thereby revealing foundational works and research themes. In our co-citation network construction, self-citations (where a citing article shares 1 or more authors with the cited reference) were excluded in the overall count to reflect the full citation landscape, but their potential influence on specific clusters was noted during interpretation.

2.2.3. Author and institutional analysis

Author productivity and collaboration were analyzed using the “bibliometrix” R package and VOSviewer. To address author name disambiguation (a critical challenge in bibliometrics), a multidimensional strategy was adopted: utilizing the standardized “Author Full Names” and “Author IDs” (where available) provided by WoSCC; cross-referencing authors’ affiliations and research fields; manually verifying ambiguous cases by examining their publication history and coauthor networks. Institutional analysis followed a similar principle, where variant names of the same institution were consolidated based on standardized affiliations provided by WoSCC and manual verification. Productivity metrics for authors and institutions included publication volume, total citations and the h-index, which were computed using the “bibliometrix” package.

2.2.4. Keyword co-occurrence and visualization

Keyword co-occurrence analysis was performed to identify research hotspots and thematic clusters. Both Author Keywords and Keywords Plus were utilized to capture comprehensive conceptual coverage. A thesaurus file was applied before analysis to merge synonyms and related terms. Networks were constructed using VOSviewer, where the association strength normalization method was chosen for mapping to account for differences in keyword frequencies.

2.2.5. Software and visualization

The analyses were conducted and visualized using the following tools: VOSviewer (v1.6.20) for constructing and visualizing keyword co-occurrence, author collaboration and institutional networks[15]; CiteSpace (v6.4.R2) for detecting citation bursts and visualizing co-citation networks over time[16]; Scimago Graphica (Scimago Lab) for generating geographical collaboration maps; the “bibliometrix” (v4.5.1) package in R (v4.5.1)[17] for comprehensive bibliometric indicators, 3-field plots and trend analyses; Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corporation) for initial data handling and basic descriptive statistics.

3. Results

3.1. Literature search and screening results

The initial search yielded 773 publications. Subsequently, the literature was independently screened by 2 researchers according to predetermined inclusion and exclusion criteria, resulting in the final inclusion of 262 valid publications for subsequent analysis.

3.2. Temporal trends in publications

The final 262 publications included in the analysis were published in 107 different journals and were coauthored by 1163 researchers from 454 institutions across 45 countries/regions. Figure 2 and Figure S1A illustrates the annual publication trend in this field from 2014 to 2024 (Note: data for 2025 are incomplete). Overall, global research output on SIT and AD showed a significant growth trend. Linear regression analysis revealed a significant positive correlation between the number of publications and the year (β = 1.64, P < .01). Specifically, the research development can be broadly divided into 2 phases: from 2014 to 2018, the annual publication volume remained at a relatively low level with slow growth; since 2019, the number of publications has shown a significantly accelerated growth trend. This inflection point may be attributed to in-depth investigations into psychosomatic interaction mechanisms within the fields of neuroscience and psychiatry, coupled with increasing clinical recognition of comorbid anxiety in tinnitus patients. Notably, although a slight decline in global publications was observed in 2023, the output remained significantly higher than pre-pandemic levels, with sustained interest likely linked to heightened global focus on long-term health issues (such as persistent tinnitus) and their psychological sequelae in the post-COVID-19 pandemic era. 4. Discussion

This study employed bibliometric analysis to conduct quantitative, qualitative, and systematic evaluation of 262 relevant publications from 2014 to 2025. The results indicate a consistent upward trend in the annual number of publications in this field, with a marked acceleration in growth since 2019, reflecting the high research interest and attention this domain commands.

4.1. Trends in SIT-related research

Recent years have witnessed significant progress in tinnitus research, achieved through continuous exploration of its underlying pathophysiological mechanisms (such as the proposal of centralized mechanisms of tinnitus) and the enrichment of treatment options facilitated by breakthroughs in functional neuroimaging and neuromodulation techniques. However, in clinical practice, a considerable number of tinnitus patients present with comorbid negative emotional states like anxiety and depression, which can, in turn, exacerbate tinnitus symptoms, creating a vicious cycle. Currently, there is a lack of bibliometric analysis specifically investigating the interrelationship between SIT and AD. This study aims to summarize the research trends in this field and provide a reference for related investigations.

The results show that approximately 45 countries globally have participated in research in this domain. The United States leads with 58 publications, while Germany and China are tied for second place with 46 articles each. European countries traditionally hold a dominant position, whereas the Asian region demonstrates strong potential for development. At the institutional level, the University of Nottingham (UK), Charité–Universitätsmedizin Berlin (Germany), and the University of Antwerp (Belgium) are the most prolific research institutions. These institutions have not only formed domestic research clusters but also act as hubs in international collaborations, promoting the integration of neural mechanism exploration and clinical translational research. Leading countries and institutions contribute the majority of the academic output in global related research.

In the future, this field could further expand international collaborative networks, foster interdisciplinary integration and data sharing.

4.2. Status and quality of authors and journals research

Regarding author contributions, several influential core research groups have been established. Gerhard Andersson leads with 11 publications and 1145 citations; his research focuses on cognitive behavioral interventions and psychological mechanisms for tinnitus and anxiety, demonstrating substantial academic influence. Petra Brueggemann and Birgit Mazurek have each published 14 papers, making prominent contributions in the areas of neurophysiological mechanisms and clinical assessment. The authorship network exhibits a collaboration pattern characterized as “decentralized yet locally intensive,” forming distinct clusters: a neural mechanisms group represented by Langguth and De Ridder; a psycho-behavioral research group represented by Andersson and Cima; and a clinical management group represented by Tyler and Henry. This reflects the field’s evolving trajectory, progressively translating from basic mechanistic exploration to clinical practice.

In terms of journals, relevant research outputs are widely distributed across 107 academic journals, indicating strong interdisciplinary reach. Among them, Frontiers in Neurology ranks first with 13 publications, followed by the International Journal of Audiology and the American Journal of Audiology, constituting primary publication venues for the field. In the co-citation analysis, Laryngoscope emerged as a pivotal journal in knowledge base construction with 314 co-citations. Concurrently, Hearing Research, despite a lower publication count, achieved a high co-citation frequency of 301, signifying its important role in driving theoretical advancements.

Overall, the research frontier in this field has progressively converged on neural circuit mechanisms, multimodal intervention strategies, and the standardization of clinical diagnosis and treatment.

4.3. Research frontiers and hotspots

With the advancement of neuroimaging and psychoacoustic research, the comorbidity mechanisms of SIT and AD have become a current research hotspot. This section discusses the latest research progress in this field, focusing on the neuropsychological mechanisms of SIT, assessment and intervention strategies, and future directions.

4.3.1. Neural mechanisms and comorbidity pathways

Recent advances in neuroimaging technology have positioned the investigation of the neural mechanisms underlying SIT as a primary research direction, forming a multilevel research framework spanning from molecular mechanisms to brain networks. At the neuroimaging level, resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging studies have revealed abnormally enhanced functional connectivity between the default mode network and the limbic system in tinnitus patients. Specifically, the identified “tinnitus-anxiety neural circuit” between the anterior cingulate cortex and the amygdala provides a crucial neural basis for explaining their comorbidity.[18,19] Deeper investigations indicate that this neural coupling is not only present at rest but becomes more pronounced during tinnitus episodes, characterized by dynamic interactions within the prefrontal-limbic system that positively correlate with anxiety severity.[20] At the molecular level, hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis dysfunction is recognized as a key bridge linking tinnitus and anxiety. Chronic tinnitus can lead to altered glucocorticoid receptor sensitivity, which subsequently affects prefrontal neuroplasticity through epigenetic regulation, forming a vicious cycle of stress-tinnitus-anxiety.[21–23] Furthermore, neuroinflammatory mechanisms are increasingly gaining attention. Studies show elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines in the peripheral blood of tinnitus patients; these inflammatory mediators may compromise blood-brain barrier integrity and affect limbic system function, thereby exacerbating anxiety symptoms. These multi-level studies not only reveal the complexity of the tinnitus-anxiety comorbidity mechanism but also provide new avenues for developing targeted therapies.

4.3.2. Innovations in assessment systems and intervention strategies

In clinical research, the innovation and refinement of assessment tools have driven the development of personalized treatment. Core instruments such as the tinnitus handicap inventory and the hospital anxiety and depression scale are pivotal not only for symptom screening but also for evaluating treatment efficacy.[24,25] In recent years, severity stratification systems for tinnitus, established based on these scales, have enabled clinicians to formulate individualized treatment plans according to patients’ specific symptom profiles. Regarding intervention strategies, CBT has demonstrated sustained efficacy, potentially mediated by top-down regulation of limbic system activity by the prefrontal cortex.[26] Notably, breakthroughs in neuromodulation techniques offer novel therapeutic directions. repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation, by modulating excitability in the auditory cortex and paralimbic system, can alleviate both tinnitus symptoms and comorbid anxiety.[27] More cutting-edge is the application of real-time neurofeedback, which allows patients to mitigate symptoms through self-regulation of neural activity; this brain-computer interface-based approach represents a new trend in precision medicine.[28] Additionally, MLA, by integrating clinical, neuroimaging, and genetic data, can accurately predict individual treatment responses, providing a quantitative basis for clinical decision-making.[14,29–31]

4.4. Future research trends

With the rapid development of multimodal neuroimaging technologies and computational psychiatry methods, future research will increasingly focus on constructing cross-scale pathophysiological models. Regarding neural mechanisms, current studies primarily concentrate on aberrant functional connectivity at the macroscopic brain network level, while understanding of micro-level alterations (such as synaptic plasticity, dysregulation of neurotransmitter systems, and their coupling with macroscopic network activity) remains limited. Future research needs to integrate precise manipulation techniques like optogenetics and chemogenetics in animal models to dissect the causal roles of specific neural circuits in tinnitus-anxiety comorbidity.[32] Concurrently, neuroimmune mechanisms, as an emerging research direction, urgently require in-depth investigation into the interactions among microglial activation, cytokine release, and neuronal hyperexcitability, which may explain why some tinnitus patients progress to a chronic, refractory state.[33–35] In terms of clinical translation, a significant limitation of current intervention research is the lack of reliable biomarkers for predicting treatment response. Future clinical trials should integrate multimodal data (such as electroencephalography, magnetic resonance spectroscopy, and molecular imaging) to establish objective indicator systems capable of distinguishing tinnitus subtypes.[29] The application of computational psychiatry methods also shows great potential; constructing computational models based on reinforcement learning theory can quantify the sensory-emotional-cognitive interactions in patients, providing theoretical guidance for personalized neuromodulation.[36,37] Furthermore, the development of telemedicine and digital therapeutics offers new solutions to the accessibility challenges of traditional diagnosis and treatment models. Virtual reality-based exposure therapy provides a feasible approach for disseminating CBT.[38]

4.5. Strengths and limitations of this study

A key strength of this study lies in its systematic bibliometric analysis of the cross-disciplinary research field of SIT and AD. By leveraging complementary bibliometric tools, it successfully constructed a knowledge structure map and delineated the developmental trajectory of the domain. The methodology benefits from a mature analytical framework and standardized parameter settings, making it particularly suitable for identifying research hotspot distributions, collaboration network characteristics, and knowledge base structures within interdisciplinary fields. Through keyword co-occurrence and cluster analysis, the study revealed 3 major research streams (neural mechanism research, psycho-behavioral research, and clinical management research) providing a clear navigational framework for subsequent investigations. Furthermore, time-series analysis and citation burst detection effectively captured the field’s evolutionary path, from phenomenological description to mechanistic exploration and onward to intervention optimization, offering empirical evidence for understanding the discipline’s development patterns. However, this study also has several noteworthy limitations. Firstly, traditional bibliometric methods exhibit a certain lag in capturing the temporal sensitivity of emerging research fronts. This is particularly true for rapidly evolving innovative directions like neuromodulation technologies and digital therapeutics, whose burst trends might require more refined semantic analysis techniques for early identification. Secondly, the exclusive reliance on the WoSCC as the data source, while ensuring consistency in literature quality, might have omitted significant research contributions from other databases. Additionally, while traditional co-citation analysis and keyword co-occurrence can reveal knowledge structures, their capacity to parse deep-level semantic relationships within research content is limited, making it difficult to capture the complex logical connections between concepts.

Future research could consider integrating more advanced analytical methods to further refine the methodology. For instance, introducing deep learning-based semantic embedding techniques could extract richer semantic features from article abstracts and full texts. Combining these with traditional bibliometric indicators to build hybrid models holds promise for achieving more sensitive detection of emerging research directions. Simultaneously, incorporating altmetric data into the analytical framework can capture the impact of scientific achievements in nontraditional channels like social media and policy documents, providing multidimensional evidence for assessing the translational potential of research.[39–41]

5. Conclusion

Through systematic bibliometric methods, this study delineates the developmental landscape, knowledge structure, and evolutionary trajectory of global research on SIT and AD from 2014 to 2025. The results indicate a consistently active and growing field, entering a phase of rapid development particularly after 2019. Multiple countries and associated research institutions have gradually established stable collaborative models, progressively accelerating the integrated development of research in neural mechanisms, psycho-behavioral aspects, and clinical management, thereby fostering advancements in both diagnostic/therapeutic standardization and personalized treatment. Crucially, burst detection revealed the evolutionary path of research hotspots: shifting from early epidemiological characterizations, towards explorations of neuropsychological comorbidity mechanisms, and ultimately converging on innovative intervention strategies like neuromodulation and digital therapeutics. These findings not only corroborate the pioneering contributions of scholars like Gerhard Andersson in researching the mechanisms of CBT[26] but also provide systematic evidence for understanding the complete knowledge chain from basic research to clinical translation in this field.

From a clinical translation perspective, the knowledge map constructed in this study offers guidance for optimizing diagnostic and therapeutic practices. On one hand, identifying core authors and collaboration networks can assist research management bodies in precisely locating leading teams, thereby fostering interdisciplinary collaboration and optimizing resource allocation. On the other hand, it provides a theoretical pathway for developing emerging research directions, such as real-time neurofeedback technology[28] and MLA,[42,43] for formulating individualized treatment plans. Looking ahead, integrating multimodal neuroimaging data with artificial intelligence algorithms to build a biomarker system for tinnitus-anxiety comorbidity, coupled with the deep integration of telemedicine and digital therapeutics, holds the potential to overcome the spatiotemporal limitations of traditional diagnosis and treatment models, thereby enhancing the accessibility and efficiency of healthcare services.

In summary, this study not only systematically illustrates the knowledge architecture and developmental trajectory of the SIT and AD field but also provides a theoretical basis for fostering interdisciplinary collaboration, optimizing research resource allocation, and driving clinical translation innovation. It holds significant academic value and clinical relevance for the future development of this domain.

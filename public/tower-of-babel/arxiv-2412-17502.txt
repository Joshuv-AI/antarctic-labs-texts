Abstract
. We prove that single G G -weighted 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} -Hurwitz numbers with internal faces are computed by refined topological recursion on a rational spectral curve, for certain rational weights G G . Consequently, the 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} -Hurwitz generating function analytically continues to a rational curve. In particular, our results cover the cases of 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} -monotone Hurwitz numbers, and the enumeration of maps and bipartite maps (with internal faces) on non-oriented surfaces. As an application, we prove that the correlators of the Gaussian, Jacobi and Laguerre β \beta -ensembles are computed by refined topological recursion.



1.
Introduction

Motivated by topological string theory, Bouchard and Mariño
[
18
]
conjectured that simple Hurwitz numbers can be computed using the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion formalism
[
28
,
38
]
. Subsequently proved in
[
37
]
, this conjecture sparked an explosion of activity relating Hurwitz theory and topological recursion
[
31
,
32
,
48
,
35
,
16
]
culminating with the following recent result of
[
3
,
21
]
: whenever the weight
G
is a rational function times an exponential,
G
-weighted Hurwitz numbers can be computed by topological recursion on an associated spectral curve. The existence of topological recursion is a key result that controls the structure of weighted Hurwitz numbers. Particular important consequences include the existence of a rational parametrization for their generating functions, existence of quantum curves and determinantal formulas, and ELSV-type formulas generalizing
[
36
]
.

\mathfrak{b}
-deformation

Motivated in part by the
\beta
-deformation of matrix models, one-parameter deformations of both Hurwitz theory and topological recursion have been
studied intensely in the last few years. Defined by
[
24
]
,
G
-weighted
\mathfrak{b}
-Hurwitz theory is a one-parameter interpolation between classical (complex) Hurwitz theory and its non-orientable (real) version.
Given a formal power series
G(z)=\sum_{i\geq 0}g_{i}z^{i}
, the tau function of
G
-weighted
\mathfrak{b}
-Hurwitz numbers, denoted
\tau^{(\mathfrak{b})}_{G}
, is defined as

\tau_{G}^{{(\mathfrak{b})}}:=\sum_{d\geq 0}\left(\frac{t\cdot\sqrt{\alpha}}{\hbar}\right)^{d}\sum_{\lambda\vdash d}\check{J}_{\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(\sqrt{\alpha}\tilde{\mathbf{p}})\prod_{\square\in\lambda}G(\hbar\cdot\tilde{c}_{\alpha}(\square)),

where
\check{J}_{\lambda}^{(\alpha)}
is an appropriately normalized Jack symmetric function and
\tilde{c}_{\alpha}
is the deformed content of the box of the Young diagram (see
Section

2
for a detailed explanation). The tau function admits a topological expansion

(1)

\tau_{G}^{{(\mathfrak{b})}}=\exp\left(\sum_{g\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}\sum_{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}\frac{\hbar^{2g-2+n}}{n!}\;F_{g,n}[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}]\;\frac{\tilde{p}_{\mu_{1}}}{\mu_{1}}\cdots\frac{\tilde{p}_{\mu_{n}}}{\mu_{n}}\right),

and the coefficients
F_{g,n}
admit a combinatorial interpretation in terms of weighted real (non-oriented) branched coverings where the parameter
\mathfrak{b}=\sqrt{\alpha}^{-1}-\sqrt{\alpha}
measures non-orientability in a precise sense
[
24
]
. When
\mathfrak{b}=0
, Jack functions reduce to Schur functions, only orientable contributions survive, and
\tau^{(0)}_{G}
becomes the generating function of
G
-weighted Hurwitz numbers, which is known to be a Kadomtsev–Petviashvili tau function
[
44
]
.

Refined topological recursion, defined by
[
47
,
53
]
building on
[
29
]
, is a one-parameter deformation of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion whose underlying (global) symmetry is the Virasoro algebra at central charge
c=1-6\mathfrak{b}^{2}
. Refined topological recursion takes as input a refined spectral curve, denoted
\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mu}}
, which consists of the data
(\Sigma,x,y,\mathcal{P}_{+},\{\mu_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{P}_{+}})
—
\Sigma
is a compact Riemann surface;
x,y
are two non-constant meromorphic functions on
\Sigma
such that
x:\Sigma\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is of degree two;
\mathcal{P}_{+}
is a subset of the set of the zeroes and poles of
ydx
(possibly with some shift); and
\mu_{a}\in\mathbb{C}
is a complex number for every such
a\in\mathcal{P}_{+}
.

The data of a refined spectral curve defines the unstable correlators
\omega_{0,1},\omega_{0,2},
and
\omega_{\frac{1}{2},1}
, and the refined topological recursion is a recursive (on the integer
(2g-2+n)
) construction of the stable correlators
\omega_{g,n}
for
(g,n)\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that
(2g-2+n)>0
. When
\Sigma=\mathbb{P}^{1}
which is the case we are interested in, the
\omega_{g,n}
are proven to be symmetric meromorphic
n
-differentials on
\Sigma
that are polynomials in the parameter
\mathfrak{b}
of degree at most
2g
(see
Section

3
for their definition). Upon setting
\mathfrak{b}=0
, we recover the usual Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion correlators.

Refined topological recursion for
\mathfrak{b}
-Hurwitz numbers

Our first main result is that for certain rational weights
G(z)
,
G
-weighted
\mathfrak{b}
-Hurwitz numbers can be computed by refined topological recursion. In order to state the result, let
G(z)
be one of the following rational weights:

(2)

G(z)=\frac{(u_{1}+z)(u_{2}+z)}{v-z},\quad(u_{1}+z)(u_{2}+z),\quad\frac{1}{v-z},\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{u_{1}+z}{v-z}.

We consider the corresponding refined spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mu}}
defined as follows. Set
\Sigma=\mathbb{P}^{1}
and choose the meromorphic functions
x(z)=t\frac{G(z)}{z}
and
y(z)=\frac{z}{x(z)}
, and the data
\mathcal{P}_{+}
as

\mathcal{P}_{+}=\left\{0,-u_{1},-\frac{u_{1}u_{2}}{u_{1}+u_{2}+v}\right\},\quad\left\{0,-u_{1}\right\},\quad\left\{0\right\},\quad\text{or}\quad\left\{0,-u_{1}\right\},

for
G
as in (
2
) respectively. In each of the above cases, we choose
\mu_{a}=0
for all
a\in\mathcal{P}_{+}
except

\mu_{0}=-1
to complete the definition of the refined spectral curve.

Theorem 1.1
.

For each of the weights
G(z)
given in (
2
), the refined topological recursion correlators
\omega_{g,n}
on the corresponding refined spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mu}}
are generating functions for
G
-weighted
\mathfrak{b}
-Hurwitz numbers. More precisely, for any
(g,n)\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
except
(g,n)=(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
expanding the
\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
near the point
z_{i}=0
in the local coordinate
x(z_{i})^{-1}
gives

\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=\sum_{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}\geq 1}F_{g,n}[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}]\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{dx(z_{i})}{x(z_{i})^{\mu_{i}+1}},

where the
F_{g,n}
are the expansion coefficients of the tau function
\tau^{(\mathfrak{b})}_{G}
in (
1
). The above formula also holds for
\omega_{\frac{1}{2},1}
up to certain explicit corrections (see
Theorem

4.6
and
Theorem

5.11
).

Our theorem is the first known result that the generating function of
\mathfrak{b}
-Hurwitz numbers has an analytic continuation to a rational algebraic curve (the spectral curve). Simultaneously, restricted to degree-2 curves, we extend the topological recursion result of
[
21
,
3
]
and the study of rational parametrizations in enumerative combinatorics
[
42
,
27
,
43
]
to new models with non-orientable topologies — applications to higher degree curves are wide-open. It is worth noting that the structure of this analytic continuation differs substantially from the
\mathfrak{b}=0

\omega_{g,n}
have poles along the “anti-diagonals”
z_{i}=\sigma(z_{j})
for any
i,j\in[n]
(and at a subset of
\mathcal{P}
for the models with internal faces which will be discussed below). The weights
G(z)=(u_{1}+z)(u_{2}+z)
and
G(z)=\frac{1}{v-z}
covered in the above theorem correspond to
\mathfrak{b}
-bipartite maps and
\mathfrak{b}
-monotone Hurwitz numbers respectively

24
,
10
]
.

Application to
\beta
-ensembles

As an application of
Theorem

1.1
, we prove in
Section

6.1
that refined topological recursion computes the correlators of three important models of
\beta
-ensembles. A
\beta
-ensemble is defined by a certain probability measure
d\mu^{V}_{N;\beta}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
which depends on a function
V
known as the potential. We consider three classical cases:

•

Gaussian
\beta
-ensemble
where
V(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{2}
;

•

Jacobi
\beta
-ensemble
where
V(x)=\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{2}{\beta}-c\right)\log(x)+\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{2}{\beta}-d\right)\log(1-x)
with
c,d>0
;

•

and,
Laguerre
\beta
-ensemble
where
V(x)=x+\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{2}{\beta}-c\right)\log(x)
with
c>0
;

which correspond to the spectra of tridiagonal random matrices constructed in
[
34
]
.

In random matrix theory, one is interested in computing the connected correlators
\left\langle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{k}^{k_{1}},\dots,\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{k}^{k_{n}}\right\rangle_{\mu_{N;\beta}^{V}}^{\circ}
which we can encode in the following formal power series in
t
:

(3)

W^{V}_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{dx_{i}}{x_{i}^{k_{i}+1}}\left\langle\sum_{k=1}^{N}(t\lambda_{k})^{k_{1}},\dots,\sum_{k=1}^{N}(t\lambda_{k})^{k_{n}}\right\rangle_{\mu_{N;\beta}^{V}}^{\circ}.

When the potential
V
is chosen appropriately, various
\beta
-ensembles, including the three classical models above, admit a topological expansion in
1/N
as
N\to\infty

[
11
]
, whose expansion coefficients can be identified as weighted
\mathfrak{b}
-Hurwitz numbers
[
54
]
. Thus, applying
Theorem

1.1
(and
Theorem

A.3
for the Gaussian case), we prove in
Theorem

6.1
that the topological expansion of the correlators
W^{V}_{n}
can be computed by refined topological recursion. An abbreviated version of this statement reads as follows.

Corollary 1.2
.

With the identification
\mathfrak{b}=\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{2}}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}
, the correlators
W^{V}_{n}
of the Gaussian, Jacobi and Laguerre
\beta
-ensembles can be computed by refined topological recursion. More precisely, for any
n>1
, we have

\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{2}}^{n}W^{V}_{n}(x(z_{1}),\dots,x(z_{n}))=\sum_{g\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{2}}N\right)^{2-2g-n}\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}),

as a series expansion near
z_{i}=0
, when
N\to\infty
. The correlators
\omega_{g,n}
are computed by refined topological recursion on a refined spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mu}}
that depends on the
\beta
-ensemble considered (see
Theorem

6.1
for the definition of
\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mu}}
in each of the three cases). The above formula also holds for
n=1
up to an explicit correction for
g=\frac{1}{2}
.

It is worth noting that the formula of
Corollary

1.2
holds as an equality of formal power series in
t
such that for any fixed power of
t
, the LHS is an analytic function of
N
whose expansion as
N\to\infty
is given by the RHS. In particular, we emphasize that we do not need to treat the
\beta
-ensembles formally in
1/N
. Our theorem makes the recursive structure found in
[
29
]
(in the formal setting) mathematically precise, and gives an explicit description of the analytic structure of the correlators using the formalism of refined topological recursion.

Inserting internal faces

Our second main result is an extension of
Theorem

1.1
to the case of
\mathfrak{b}
-Hurwitz numbers with internal faces. The terminology “internal faces" stems from the interpretation of
\tau^{(\mathfrak{b})}_{G}
as a generating function for colored monotone Hurwitz maps, for rational weight
G

[
10
,
54
]
. The expansion coefficient
F_{g,n}[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}]
is (up to an automorphism factor) a weighted count of colored monotone Hurwitz maps on non-oriented surfaces of genus
g
with
n
faces of degrees
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n}
. In this interpretation, the dependence on
\mathfrak{b}
is through the factor
(-\sqrt{\alpha}\mathfrak{b})^{\rho(\mathcal{M})}
, where
\rho(\mathcal{M})
is the so-called measure of non-orientability of a map
\mathcal{M}
– see
Section

2.2
for precise statements.

We are interested in counting colored monotone Hurwitz maps of genus
g
with
n
marked boundary faces of degrees
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n}
, where we allow the maps to also have arbitrarily many internal faces of degree at most
D\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. We denote the appropriately weighted generating series of such maps, where an internal face of degree
i
is weighted by
\sqrt{\alpha}\epsilon\tilde{p}_{i}
, by
F^{D}_{g,n}[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n};\epsilon]
, so that the exponent of the variable
\epsilon
counts the number of internal faces. The equivalent algebraic definition is given by the following formula:

(4)

F^{D}_{g,n}[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n};\epsilon]:=[\hbar^{2g-2+n}]\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{p}_{\mu_{i}}}\cdot\log\tau^{(\mathfrak{b})}_{G}\right)\Bigg|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{p}_{i}\mapsto\frac{\epsilon}{\hbar}p_{i}\,\forall\,i\leq D,\\
\tilde{p}_{i}=0\,\forall\,i>D\end{subarray}}.

Note in particular that these
F^{D}_{g,n}
are also functions of
p_{1},\ldots,p_{D}
and
\epsilon
. When
\mathfrak{b}=0
, the problem of counting weighted Hurwitz numbers with internal faces was considered in
[
22
,
8
]
, and was proved to be governed by topological recursion.

We prove that when
G
is one of the weights in (
2
),
G
-weighted
\mathfrak{b}
-Hurwitz numbers with internal faces are computed by refined topological recursion. The definition of the corresponding genus zero refined spectral curve, denoted
\mathcal{S}^{D}_{\bm{\mu}}=(\mathbb{P}^{1},X(z),Y(z),\mathcal{P}_{+},\{\mu_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{P}_{+}}))
, is rather technical and can be found in
Definition

5.5
and
Section

5.5
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any of the weights
G(z)
given in (
2
), the refined topological recursion correlators
\omega_{g,n}
on the corresponding refined spectral curve
\mathcal{S}^{D}_{\bm{\mu}}
are generating functions for
G
-weighted
\mathfrak{b}
-Hurwitz numbers with internal faces of degree at most
D
. More precisely, for any
(g,n)\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
except
(g,n)=(0,1),\;(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
expanding the
\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
near the point
z_{i}=0
in the local coordinate
X(z_{i})^{-1}
gives

\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=\sum_{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}\geq 1}F^{D}_{g,n}[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n};\epsilon]\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{dX(z_{i})}{X(z_{i})^{\mu_{i}+1}}

where the
F^{D}_{g,n}
are defined in (

). The above formula also holds for
\omega_{0,1}
and
\omega_{\frac{1}{2},1}

Theorem

5.10
and
Theorem

5.12
).

When the weight is
G(z)=(u_{1}+z)(u_{2}+z)
,
[
24
]
proves that the coefficients
F_{g,n}^{D}[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n};\epsilon]
are the
\mathfrak{b}
-weighted generating series of bipartite maps on a non-oriented surface of genus
g
with
n

\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n}
and arbitrarily many internal faces of degree at most

Context and future work

To prove
Theorem

1.1
, we use Virasoro-type constraints found in
[
30
]
that uniquely determine the tau function
\tau^{(\mathfrak{b})}_{G}
. We turn these constraints into formal loop equations for the generating functions of the
F_{g,n}
and prove that the refined topological recursion correlators
\omega_{g,n}
uniquely solve these loop equations using the results of
[
47
]
. Our result extends the result of
[
3
,
21
]
to arbitrary
\mathfrak{b}
for the restricted choice of weights
G(z)
in equation (
2
). However, the results of
loc. cit.
rely heavily on KP integrability, which is unknown in the
\mathfrak{b}
-deformed setting and hence we cannot use their methods to prove our result.

The strategy of our proof applies to any rational weight
G(z)
, and indeed can be used to show that the generating function of rationally weighted
\mathfrak{b}
-Hurwitz numbers analytically continues to a rational curve
\Sigma=\mathbb{P}^{1}
(extending the result of
[
15
]
to arbitrary
\mathfrak{b}
). The only obstruction is that the refined topological recursion formalism is yet to be defined when the associated branched covering
x:\Sigma\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is of degree greater than
2
. We will return to this question in the future.

The key idea in extending
Theorem

1.1
to
Theorem

1.3
for
\mathfrak{b}
-Hurwitz numbers with internal faces is that inserting internal faces in the combinatorial models can be interpreted as the application of the variational formula in topological recursion. This technique was employed by
[
8
]
in the
\mathfrak{b}=0
setting, and we use the refined variational formula proved in
[
52
]
to extend the result to arbitrary
\mathfrak{b}
.

In his list of unsolved problems in map enumeration
[
7
]
, Bender posed a question regarding the origin of a universal pattern that has been discovered and proven for various models of maps over the years. This universal pattern matches physics predictions from the so-called double scaling limits in quantum gravity. Eynard explained in
[
39
, Chapter 5]
how the structural properties of the topological recursion correlators can be used to prove these predictions. In particular, the recent results in the
\mathfrak{b}=0
setting on topological recursion applied to weighted Hurwitz numbers with internal faces provide a general answer to Bender’s question in the case of orientable surfaces. However, in the non-orientable case, this asymptotic pattern is proved only for some special models, and remains conjectural in general. We plan to use refined topological recursion to tackle this problem in future work.

2.1.
Jack polynomials

Let
J^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}
be the
integral version of the Jack symmetric
function
indexed by an integer partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dotsm\geq\lambda_{\ell})
and a parameter
\alpha
. There are many equivalent definitions
of these symmetric functions, and the standard references
are
[
56
,
49
]
. We will follow the presentation
from
[
30
, Section 2.1]
that is well suited for our purposes. After identifying the normalized power sum symmetric
functions

\sqrt{\alpha}^{-1}\sum_{j\geq 1}x_{j}^{i}

with the formal variable

\tilde{p}_{i}
, we can characterize
J^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}
as
eigenfunctions of a certain differential operator acting on
the graded polynomial algebra
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\alpha})[\tilde{\mathbf{p}}]:=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\alpha})[\tilde{p}_{1},\tilde{p}_{2},\dotsc]
with the
grading
\deg(\tilde{p}_{i}):=i
. Let

\begin{split}\mathsf{J}_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\hbar k\partial_{\tilde{p}_{k}}&k\geq 0,\\
0&k=0,\\
\hbar\tilde{p}_{-k}&k<0.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

be a representation of the Heisenberg algebra acting on

\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\alpha})[\tilde{\mathbf{p}}]
. Then, the Jack
symmetric functions
J^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}
are the unique homogeneous
elements of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\alpha})[\tilde{\mathbf{p}}]
of degree

|\lambda|:=\lambda_{1}+\dotsm+\lambda_{\ell}
such that:

J1:

J^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}
is an eigenfunction of the
Laplace–Beltrami operator

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}:=\frac{\hbar^{-3}}{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathsf{J}_{-k}\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\mathsf{J}_{\ell}\mathsf{J}_{k-\ell}-(k-1)\hbar\cdot\mathfrak{b}\cdot\mathsf{J}_{k}\right),\quad\mathfrak{b}:=\sqrt{\alpha}^{-1}-\sqrt{\alpha},

with eigenvalue equal to

\sum_{(x,y)\in\lambda}\tilde{c}_{\alpha}(x,y),\quad\text{where}\quad\tilde{c}_{\alpha}(x,y):=\sqrt{\alpha}(x-1)-\sqrt{\alpha}^{-1}(y-1);

where
(x,y)\in\lambda
(which we occasionally write as
\square\in\lambda
for short) denotes the box in column
x
and row
y
of the Young diagram of
\lambda
.

J2:

the transition matrix from
\left\{J^{{(\alpha)}}_{\lambda}\right\}_{|\lambda|=n}

to the monomial symmetric functions
\left\{m_{\lambda}\right\}_{|\lambda|=n}

is lower triangular;

J3:

J^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}
is normalized such that

[\tilde{p}_{1}^{|\lambda|}]J^{(\alpha)}_{\lambda}
is equal to
\sqrt{\alpha}^{|\lambda|}
; with this normalization, we denote
j_{\lambda}^{(\alpha)}:=\langle J^{{(\alpha)}}_{\lambda},J^{{(\alpha)}}_{\lambda}\rangle
, where
\langle\tilde{p}_{\lambda},\tilde{p}_{\mu}\rangle:=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}\frac{|\lambda|!}{\Aut(\lambda)}
.

Remark 2.1
.

The use of
\tilde{p}_{i}
instead of usual (non-normalized) power sum symmetric
functions
p_{i}=\sqrt{\alpha}\tilde{p}_{i}
, as well as using the parameter

\mathfrak{b}
instead of
b:=\alpha-1
is not standard. However, we will see later that this
normalization is more natural in the context of refined
topological recursion.

. In our genus-zero setting, this means that we restrict to the case when the underlying Riemann surface
\Sigma
is always of genus
0
. The two meromorphic functions
x,y
depend on
{\bm{t}}
and consequently, all the correlators
\omega_{g,n}
as well. Then, a natural question is to ask whether the
\{\omega_{g,n}\}_{g,n}
behaves well in the family (i.e., under the variation of the parameters
\bm{t}
). In general, this is quite a difficult global question (see
[
14
]
about when one can take limits in topological recursion in the
\mathfrak{b}=0
setting).

However, it turns out that
\{\omega_{g,n}\}_{g,n}
behaves well (locally) under the so-called
variation for fixed
x
. Let us explain how this works. Choose
t\in{\bm{t}}
. For a meromorphic multidifferential
\omega
on
\Sigma^{n}
, one can associate the corresponding function
\mathcal{W}
by

(17)

\omega(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};t)=:\mathcal{W}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};t)\;dx(z_{1};t)\cdots dx(z_{n};t),

where
d_{i}
denotes the exterior derivative with respect to
z_{i}
and
dx(z_{i};t)=d_{i}x(z_{i};t)
.
Instead of naively considering the partial derivative with respect to
t
, we define the
variational operator

\delta_{t}^{(n)}
with respect to
t
by (c.f.
[
52
, Definition 3.1]
)

\delta_{t}^{(n)}\;\mathcal{W}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n};t):=\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};t)}{\partial t}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial x(z_{i};t)}{\partial t}\frac{d_{i}\mathcal{W}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};t)}{dx(z_{i};t)}.

Similarly for a multidifferential, we define

\delta_{t}^{(n)}\;\omega(z_{1},\dots,z_{n};t):=dx(z_{1};t)\cdots dx(z_{n};t)\;\delta_{t}^{(n)}\;\mathcal{W}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n};t).

See
Lemma

5.8
to understand how the variational operator
\delta_{t}^{(n)}
naturally arises in our context.

Henceforth, we drop the
t
-dependence in
x
for brevity. With this definition, it is easy to check that

\delta_{t}^{(1)}x(p_{1})=0,\qquad\delta_{t}^{(1)}dx(p_{1})=0,

and furthermore, for a meromorphic function
f
pulled back from the base curve
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we have

\delta_{t}^{(n)}\;f(x(z_{1}),\dots,x(z_{n});t)=\partial_{t}f(x(z_{1}),\dots,x(z_{n});t).

Thus,
\delta_{t}^{(n)}
indeed acts as the “derivative for fixed
x
” as in
[
38
]
.

Let us denote by
\mathcal{P}^{\infty}
the set of poles of
(y(z)-y(\sigma(z)))dx(z)
which may be ramification points, and by
D_{i}+1
the order of the pole at
a_{i}\in\mathcal{P}^{\infty}
. Typically, “good parameters” of topological recursion with respect to which one would like to understand the variation of the correlators are associated with
\mathcal{P}^{\infty}
. More concretely, it is proven in
[
52
]
(and in
[
38
]
when
\mathfrak{b}=0
) that the action of
\delta_{t}^{(n)}
on
\omega_{g,n}
is related to a certain integral of
\omega_{g,n+1}
:

Definition 3.9

(
[
52
]
)
.

We say that a refined spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mu}}(\bm{t})
satisfies the
refined deformation condition with respect to

if there exists a contour
\gamma\subset\Sigma
and a function
\Lambda
holomorphic along
\gamma
such that

D1:

The poles of
\Lambda
lie in
\mathcal{P}^{\infty}
and the order of the pole at
a_{i}\in\mathcal{P}^{\infty}
is
D_{i}
or less.

D2:

The pair
(\gamma,\Lambda)
satisfies

(18)

\displaystyle\delta_{t}^{(1)}\left(\omega_{0,1}(z_{1})-\omega_{0,1}(\sigma(z_{1}))\right)=

\displaystyle\int_{z\in\gamma}\Lambda(z)\,\left(\omega_{0,2}(z,z_{1})-\omega_{0,2}(z,\sigma(z_{1}))\right),

(19)

\displaystyle\delta_{t}^{(2)}\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2})=

\displaystyle\int_{z\in\gamma}\Lambda(z)\,\omega_{0,3}(z,z_{1},z_{2})

(20)

\displaystyle\delta_{t}^{(1)}\omega_{\frac{1}{2},1}(z_{1})=

\displaystyle\int_{z\in\gamma}\Lambda(z)\,\omega_{\frac{1}{2},2}(z,z_{1}).

Theorem 3.10

(
[
52
]
)
.

If a refined spectral curve

satisfies the refined deformation condition with respect to

, then for all

,

\delta_{t}^{(n)}\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=\int_{z\in\gamma}\Lambda(z)\,\omega_{g,1+n}(z,z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}).

If formula (
21
) holds for some

satisfy the variational formula with respect to

.

Let us mention that
(\gamma,\Lambda)
in

]
is chosen to be anti-invariant under the involution
\sigma
, whereas we did not impose this condition here. There is no difference between the two in practice. This is because for

\omega_{g,n+1}(z_{0},z_{[n]})+\omega_{g,n+1}(\sigma(z_{0}),z_{[n]})
has a zero of order

at every

as shown in

, and thus only the anti-invariant parts contribute to the variational formula. We also note that not all refined spectral curves satisfy the refined deformation condition, and the parameters

need to be chosen appropriately. See

]
for some concrete examples.

4.

We will give a brief proof as the derivation of loop equations from differential constraints arising from

4.3.
Refined topological recursion

Finally, we prove that all the differentials

Lemma 4.5

We have

(34)

5.4.
Refined topological recursion with internal faces

We need two more lemmas to prove that the refined topological recursion correlators

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}
be a set of polynomials whose coefficients do not depend on
\epsilon

n
-differential
\omega
on

, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\left(\Res_{z_{1}=0}\cdots\Res_{z_{n}=0}\,P_{1}(X(z_{1}))\cdots P_{n}(X(z_{n}))\,\omega(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\right)\\
=\Res_{z_{1}=0}\cdots\Res_{z_{n}=0}\,P_{1}(X(z_{1}))\cdots P_{n}(X(z_{n}))\,\delta_{\epsilon}^{(n)}\,\omega(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}).

Proof.

When
n=1
, let
\mathcal{W}
be the associated function of
\omega
, i.e.
\omega(z)=\mathcal{W}(z)dX(z)
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}

\displaystyle\Res_{z=0}P(X(z))\omega(z)=\Res_{z=0}\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\Big(P(X(z))\mathcal{W}(z)dX(z)\Big)

\displaystyle=\Res_{z=0}\left(\frac{\partial X(z)}{\partial\epsilon}\frac{\partial P(X(z))}{\partial X(z)}\mathcal{W}(z)dX(z)+P(X(z))\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}(z)}{\partial\epsilon}dX(z)+P(X(z))\mathcal{W}(z)d\frac{\partial X(z)}{\partial\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\Res_{z=0}\left(d\left(P(X(z))\mathcal{W}(z)\frac{\partial X(z)}{\partial\epsilon}\right)+P(X(z))\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}(z)}{\partial\epsilon}-\frac{\partial X(z)}{\partial\epsilon}\frac{d\mathcal{W}(z)}{dX(z)}\right)dX(z)\right)

\displaystyle=\Res_{z=0}P(X(z))\;\delta_{\epsilon}^{(1)}\,\omega(z)

The first equality holds because one can always choose a contour encircling
z=0
independent of
\epsilon
. The second equality is due to the assumption that
P(X)
does not depend on
\epsilon
. The fourth equality holds because the first term is the total derivative of a meromorphic function. One can repeat the same argument when
n\geq 2
.
∎

Lemma 5.9

\omega_{g,n+1}^{D}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}=\omega_{g,n+1}.

Proof.

By the construction of

8

(and

{\bm{\mu}}

\tilde{y}(z):=y(z)-\left(\frac{x(z)}{2t^{2}}-\frac{u}{x(z)}\right)
.
The functions
x
and
\tilde{y}
satisfy an algebraic relation

\tilde{y}(z)^{2}-\frac{x(z)^{2}}{4t^{4}}+\frac{u}{t^{2}}=0,

which coincides with the equation (
52
) for
(g,n)=(0,1)
under the identification
x=x(z)
and
\tilde{W}^{\text{G$\beta$E}}_{0,1}(x)=\tilde{y}(z)dx(z)
. By removing the shifts, we immediately get that
ydx
is the analytic continuation of
W^{\text{G$\beta$E}}_{0,1}
. We define
\omega^{\text{G$\beta$E}}_{0,1}:=ydx
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{z}
as usual.

Similar computations show that
\omega^{\text{G$\beta$E}}_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2}):=-B(z_{1},\sigma(z_{2}))
is the analytic continuation of
W^{\text{G$\beta$E}}_{0,2}(x_{1},x_{2})
where
\sigma(z)=\frac{1}{uz}
. In order to fix the refined spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mu}}^{{\text{G$\beta$E}}}
, we choose
\mathcal{P_{+}}=\{0\}
and the associated parameter
\bm{\mu}=\{-1\}
to define

(54)

\omega_{\frac{1}{2},1}^{{\text{G$\beta$E}}}=\frac{\mathfrak{b}}{2}\left(-\frac{d\tilde{y}}{\tilde{y}}-\eta^{0}\right).

Let us denote the correlators constructed by the refined topological recursion on
\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mu}}^{{\text{G$\beta$E}}}
by
\omega_{g,n}^{{\text{G$\beta$E}}}
. Then, the
W^{\text{G$\beta$E}}_{g,n}
coincide with the RTR correlators
\omega^{\text{G$\beta$E}}_{g,n}
(note that there is no shift for
(g,n)=(\frac{1}{2},1)
in contrast with
Theorem

4.6
).

Theorem A.3
.

For
(g,n)\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, the G
\beta
E correlators analytically continue to the refined TR correlators
\omega^{\text{G$\beta$E}}_{g,n}
on the refined spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mu}}^{{\text{G$\beta$E}}}

(where

In place of equations (
33
) and (
34
), in this model we have

\eta^{0}(z)=\frac{dx(z)}{2t^{2}\tilde{y}(z)},\qquad\tilde{y}(z)dx(z)\frac{\eta^{z_{1}}(z)}{\tilde{y}(z_{1})dx(z_{1})}=\frac{1}{x(z)-x(z_{1})}\frac{dx(z)^{2}}{dx(z_{1})}.

The rest of the arguments run parallel to the proof of Theorem

.

A.1.
Internal faces

We can also extend this result to the model with internal faces. The
special case
\mathfrak{b}=0
was already treated
in
[
8
]
, where the
authors constructed two meromorphic functions
X,Y
that define the
spectral curve for this model (see Section 2.1 of
loc.cit.
). Using these two functions and defining a shifted version
\tilde{Y}
of
Y
that is anti-invariant under the involution
\sigma
, one can show
(as in
Lemma

5.4
) that there exists a polynomial
M(X)
of degree
D
such that
M(X)|_{\epsilon=0}=1
and

\tilde{Y}^{2}=\frac{(X-a)(X-b)M(X)^{2}}{4t^{4}}.

We then choose
\mathcal{P}_{+}=\{0,b_{1},\ldots,b_{D}\}
such that
b_{i}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
as
\epsilon\to 0
, and the associated parameter
\bm{\mu}
is set to

\begin{matrix}\mathcal{P}_{+}&=&\{&0,&b_{1},&,\ldots,&b_{D}&\}\\
{\bm{\mu}}&=&\{&\mu_{0},&1,&,\ldots,&1&\},\end{matrix}

For any
\mu_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
, one can show that the refined spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mu}}^{{\text{G$\beta$E}},D}
satisfies the refined deformation condition with respect to
\epsilon
, similar to Proposition
5.7
. The proof is completely parallel, except that in place of the
\kappa
used in the proof of
Lemma

5.6
, we should define
\kappa\left(\mathcal{P}_{+}\right)=\{-D-1,1,\ldots,1\}
.

To state the result, we define the function

F^{{\text{G$\beta$E}},D}_{g,n}\left[k_{1},\ldots,k_{n};\epsilon\right]
as

(55)

F_{g,n}^{{\text{G$\beta$E}},D}\left[k_{1},\ldots,k_{n};\epsilon\right]:=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{\epsilon^{m}}{m!}\sum_{k_{n+1},\dots,k_{n+m}=1}^{D}F^{{\text{G$\beta$E}}}_{g,n}[k_{1},\dots,k_{n},k_{n+1},\dots,k_{n+m}]\prod_{i=n+1}^{m+n}\frac{p_{k_{i}}}{k_{i}},

which allows for internal faces of degree
i\leq D
, which are weighted by
\epsilon p_{i}
.
Then, by repeating the proof of Theorem
5.10
, we get the following theorem whose proof is omitted.

Theorem A.4
.

\mu_{0}=-1-D

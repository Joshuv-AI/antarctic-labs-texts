Supercurrent Amplification in Nonequilibrium Superconductors
Qinghong Yang 1,∗, Yuqi Cao 1,∗, Dante M. Kennes 3,4, Zhiyuan Sun 1,2,†

Abstract
In ultrafast experiments on superconductors, a pump laser pulse often heats up the electronic system and suppresses the density of superfluid electrons. Subsequently, the electrons undergo a cooling process due to electron-phonon thermalization so that the superfluid density recovers in time. We show that if a supercurrent is initiated by a probe pulse in the cooling process, an intriguing phenomenon of ‘supercurrent amplification’ occurs, meaning that the net current grows in time with the increasing superfluid density. Using the Boltzmann kinetic equation, we uncover its microscopic origin as the momentum-relaxing scattering of quasi-particles by impurities and phonons, in stark contrast to the widely accepted intuition that impurities always attenuate currents. We further show that supercurrent amplification has important experimental manifestations, including the ultrafast Meissener effect and an optical reflectivity exceeding unity.

This intriguing phenomena may be called
supercurrent amplification

Viewed in real space, as two free electrons combine to form a Cooper pair during the recovering dynamics, they intuitively obtain a total momentum of
2m\mathbf{v}

In a Galilean invariant system where the bare electrons have the kinetic energy
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbf{k}^{2}/(2m)-\mu

\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{P}/m
is simply proportional to the total momentum
which is conserved even in the presence of electron-electron interactions,
meaning that this phenomenon would be forbidden by momentum conservation.

In the following, by resorting to microscopic theory, we show that supercurrent amplification does indeed exist in real superconductors during ultrafast excitation experiments, and that the extra momentum is provided by momentum-relaxing scattering due to impurities or phonons.

Hamiltonian
—The microscopic Hamiltonian in the presence of an electrical vector potential
\mathbf{A}
can be expressed as
H=H_{\mathrm{BCS}}+H_{\mathrm{ee}}+H_{\mathrm{im}}+H_{\mathrm{ph}}
where

H_{\mathrm{BCS}}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},s}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}-e\mathbf{A}}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},s}c_{\mathbf{k},s}+\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(\Delta c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{k}\downarrow}+\text{h.c.})

(3)

Here

relative to the chemical potential

\mathbf{k}
,

is the spin index,
and
\Delta
is the superconducting order parameter (gap).
In terms of the Bogoliubov quasi-particles
\gamma_{\mathbf{k},s}

E_{\mathbf{k}}=(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}+|\Delta|^{2})^{1/2}
,
one has
H_{\mathrm{BCS}}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},s}E_{\mathbf{k}}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},s}\gamma_{\mathbf{k},s}

The presence of the vector potential locally dresses

to
the effective energy of Bogoliubov quasiparticles:

\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}}=E_{\mathbf{k}}-(e/m)\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r},t)
to linear order in

, which is now asymmetric in momentum space as shown in Fig.
2
(b).

H_{\mathrm{im}/\mathrm{ph}/\mathrm{ee}}
denotes the electron-impurity, electron-phonon interactions, and the remaining electron-electron interactions not included by

.
The order parameter is set by the gap equation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{g}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{1-2f_{\mathbf{k}}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}}

(4)

where

is the s-wave attractive interaction strength and

is the distribution function (occupation number) of Bogoliubov quasi-particles.
The Hamiltonian

together with the gap equation (
4

and quasi-particles.

The current operator is

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{j}}=\hat{\mathbf{j}}_{\mathrm{p}}+\hat{\mathbf{j}}_{\mathrm{d}},\quad\hat{\mathbf{j}}_{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{e}{m}\sum_{\mathbf{k},s}\mathbf{k}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},s}\gamma_{\mathbf{k},s},\quad\hat{\mathbf{j}}_{\mathrm{d}}=-\frac{\hat{n}e^{2}}{m}\mathbf{A}

(5)

where
\hat{\mathbf{j}}_{\mathrm{p}}
and
\hat{\mathbf{j}}_{\mathrm{d}}
are known as the paramagnetic and the diamagnetic currents, respectively.
The expectation value of the diamagnetic current is

being the total electron density

;

is applied.
Note that the paramagnetic current contributed by a Bogoliubov quasi-particle is strictly

, regardless of its fractional charge and complicated group velocity.
In the following, we will show how microscopic scattering processes collaborate to affect the paramagnetic current and result in supercurrent amplification.

Boltzmann equation—
The dynamics governed by the Hamiltonian
H
can be approximated by the Boltzmann kinetic equation
A.G. Aronov and Kozub 1981
;
Gray 2012

\begin{split}&\quad\frac{\partial f_{\mathbf{k}}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}}}{\partial\mathbf{k}}\frac{\partial f_{\mathbf{k}}}{\partial\mathbf{r}}-\frac{\partial\tilde{E}_{\mathbf{k}}}{\partial\mathbf{r}}\frac{\partial f_{\mathbf{k}}}{\partial\mathbf{k}}=I_{\mathrm{im,ee,ph}}\left[f_{\mathbf{k}}\right]\end{split}

(6)

for the distribution function
f_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r},t)

of Bogoliubov quasi-particles.
Here the spin indices are suppressed and the effects of electron-impurity, electron-phonon, and electron-electron scatterings are subsumed in the collision integral

.
The Boltzmann equation (
6
) and the gap equation (
4
) form a closed set of equations that depicts the incoherent nonequilibrium dynamics of a superconductor.
As an approximation, the Boltzmann equation works for the slow and large scale dynamics with the characteristic frequency scale
\omega
and wave vector

much smaller than the fermi energy
\varepsilon_{\text{F}}
and momentum
k_{\text{F}}

A.G. Aronov and Kozub 1981
;
Gray 2012
;
Sun et al. 2018
. It also neglects the quantum coherence of the pair excitation degrees of freedom, i.e., Anderson pseudo spins, and thus does not contain
information of collective modes of the order parameter

.
Nevertheless, the Boltzmann equation do capture the key aspects of the nonequilibrium physics relevant to this work.

4

;

In the subsequent cooling process, the electron-phonon collision terms in Eq. (

To derive the optical conductivity during the cooling stage, we apply the probe electric field pulse

\mathbf{A}(x,t)=-\mathbf{A}_{0}(x)\Theta(t)

\delta f^{\mathrm{s}}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)

\delta f^{\mathrm{a}}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)\sim O(\mathbf{A})

Equilibrium two-fluid model—
In an equilibrium superconductor, the quasi-particles have a well defined temperature equal to that of the phonons:

Supercurrent amplification—
We now discuss the case of a nonequilibrium superconductor, which is the central result of this paper.
Before computing the linear current response to the probe field, we first establish the picture of the cooling process of the nonequilibrium distribution function

Imagine the gap becomes so large in the end that very few quasi-particles are left, the paramagnetic current flowing to the left must have decayed, so that the total current flowing to the right increases.

However, a momentum conserved annihilation of two quasi-particles with momenta
\mathbf{k}
and

cannot change the paramagnetic current

In fact, the momentum of the emitted phonon is so small due to the small gap in realistic superconductors that the momenta of the annihilated two quasi-particles are roughly opposite to each other.
Therefore, we emphasize that increasing of the gap alone
does not
lead to current amplification.
What actually increases the total current is the momentum relaxing scattering processes, such as the elastic scattering of quasi-particles from left to right by impurities shown in Fig.
2
(d). In this way,
\delta f_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathrm{a}}
and the paramagnetic current decreases, so that the total current flowing to the right increases.

The nonequilibrium optical conductivity
defined by
\mathbf{j}(t)=\int dt^{\prime}\sigma_{\text{s}}(t,t^{\prime})\mathbf{E}(t^{\prime})
thus reads

\sigma_{\text{s}}(t,t^{\prime})=\frac{e^{2}}{m}n_{\mathrm{s}}(t)\theta(t-t^{\prime})

(15)

for
t-t^{\prime}\gg\tau_{i}=1/\gamma_{i}
.
The Boltzmann equation (
6
) and the gap equation (
4
) predict an roughly exponential recovery
of the superfluid density

n_{\mathrm{s}}(t)\approx n_{s0}+[n_{s}(0)-n_{s0}]e^{-\gamma_{\Delta}t}

where
n_{s0}
is the equilibrium superfluid density, as shown schematically by the second stage of the dark blue curve in Fig.
1
.
At temperatures near
T_{\mathrm{c}}
, the relaxation rate is
\gamma_{\Delta}\approx 0.27\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}\Delta/T_{\mathrm{c}}

Schmid 1968
;
Gray 2012
;
Tinkham 2004
which
is small in conventional superconductors and ranges from
0.1\,\mathrm{GHz}
to
0.1\,\mathrm{THz}
due to the phonon bottleneck
Rothwarf and Taylor 1967
.
In cuprates, the recovery time scale
1/\gamma_{\Delta}
was found to be a few picoseconds
Averitt et al. 2001
, perhaps because of a much weaker phonon bottleneck.

To summarize the analysis above, the counter intuitive phenomenon of
supercurrent amplification indicated by the TDGL equation is confirmed from the Boltzmann equation. We conclude that
impurities along with energy relaxation through phonons
underlie the microscopic mechanism for current amplification, contrary to the common belief that impurities always attenuate currents.

Ultrafast Meissner effect—
An immediate physical consequence of the supercurrent amplification is the transient Meissner effect hinted at by a recent experiment
Fava et al. 2024
.
During the quench into a NSC in the presence of a static magnetic field, the rise of the supercurrent
\mathbf{j}(t)=-n_{\mathrm{s}}(t)e^{2}\mathbf{A}/m
in time with the superfluid expels the magnetic field toward the outside of the sample
Hirsch 2016
;
Hirsch 2017
and leads to the ultrafast Meissner effect, see Fig.
3
(a).
To prove this current response, one may simply solve the Boltzmann equation in Eq. (
8
) in a transverse vector potential
\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})=\mathbf{A}e^{iqx}
with a wave vector
q
in the
x

Because of the nonzero wave vector of the field and the response current, the global momentum conservation no longer forbids the local current amplification, and the supercurrent amplification may occur due to the exchange of momenta between upward and downward current streams from the
qv_{\mathbf{k}x}
term in Eq. (
8

Nevertheless, in macroscopic samples of size

under a nearly uniform magnetic field, the characteristic wave vector of the vector potential is

\gamma_{i}

8

12
)-(
13

Therefore, the transient Meissner effect, if indeed observed in Ref.
Fava et al. 2024

Appendix A: Dirty superconductors—
So far, our analysis using the kinetic equation is limited to superconductors whose momentum relaxing scattering rate
\gamma_{\mathrm{i}}
is much smaller than the critical temperature
T_{\text{c}}
, which are called clean superconductors.
For dirty superconductors where
\gamma_{\mathrm{i}}\gg T_{\text{c}}
,
the superfluid density is not equal to the total carrier density even at zero temperature, but smaller than it by a ratio
\pi|\Delta|/\gamma_{\mathrm{i}}

Mattis and Bardeen 1958
.
In this case, the same supercurrent amplification should exist in nonequilibrium as long as the gap increases in time, which can either be due to a cooling process or a possible emergence of superconductivity following a pump pulse.

This supercurrent amplification is verified by the numerical results in Ref.
Kennes et al. 2017b
(see Fig. 8 there) for a dirty superconductor, which considers a temporal profile of the gap
\Delta(t)
that rises from zero and then either stays at a non-zero value or falls back to zero.
The nonequilibrium optical conductivity was computed using Green functions with the Mattis-Bardeen approximation
Mattis and Bardeen 1958
.
Furthermore, the nonequilibrium optical conductivity in the frequency domain (Fig. 15 of Ref.
Kennes et al. 2017b
) is qualitatively similar to the Fourier transform of Eq. (
12
) for the clean case.
Crucially, the real part of the optical conductivity of the dirty nonequilibrium superconductor can turn negative, indicating an instability in the time domain.
Again, the supercurrent amplification occurs there due to impurity scattering,
which is contained implicitly in the Mattis-Bardeen approximation that completely relaxes the constraint of momentum conservation.
Note that the pair excitation and normal carrier contributions to the current response decay quickly within time scales
1/\Delta
and
1/\gamma_{\mathrm{i}}
, respectively.
For frequency scales much smaller than the gap, it is enough to keep the superfluid response which yields Eq. (
15
) with the superfluid density replaced by that for a Dirty superconductor:
n_{\mathrm{s}}(t)=n\pi\Delta(t)/\gamma_{i}
.

Appendix C: Enhanced reflectivity—
In the reflection problem, the sample lies in the region
z<0
and the sample-vacuum interface is the x-y plane at
z=0
, as shown in Fig.
5
.
The total EM field can be written as
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{I}}+\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{R}}
at
z>0
and
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}
at
z<0
.
For simplicity, we consider a normally incident Gaussian light pulse
\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{I}}\left(z,\,t\right)=E_{0}e^{-(t-t_{0}+z/c)^{2}/2\delta^{2}-i\omega_{0}(t+z/c)}
with central frequency
\omega_{0}
.
The optical reflectivity in the nonequilibrium problem can be defined as the ratio of the EM energy of the reflected pulse to that of the incident pulse
sup
:

R=\frac{\int dt\left|\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\left(0,\,t\right)\right|^{2}}{\int dt\left|\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{I}}\left(0,\,t\right)\right|^{2}}.

(C1)

In terms of the electric field
\mathbf{E}
,
the Maxwell’s equation for the transverse EM wave inside the nonequilibrium media (
z<0
) reads
sup

\displaystyle\Box\mathbf{E}=-4\pi\partial_{t}\mathbf{j},\quad\mathbf{j}=\int_{t^{\prime}<t}dt^{\prime}\sigma\left(z;t,t^{\prime}\right)\mathbf{E}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\,

(C2)

where
\Box=\partial_{t}^{2}-c^{2}\nabla^{2}
,
c
is the vacuum speed of light,
\mathbf{j}
is the total current and
\sigma\left(z;t,t^{\prime}\right)
is the nonequilibrium optical conductivity.
Note that the time derivative of the current equals

\partial_{t}\mathbf{j}=\sigma\left(z;t,t\right)\mathbf{E}\left(t\right)+\int_{t^{\prime}<t}dt^{\prime}\left[\partial_{t}\sigma\left(z;t,t^{\prime}\right)\right]\mathbf{E}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\,
.
The Maxwell’s equation outside of the media (
z>0
) reads

\Box\mathbf{E}=0
.
The reflected electromagnetic field
\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{R}}
can be obtained from the Maxwell’s equations and the familiar boundary conditions on the interface:

E_{\parallel}(z=0^{+})=E_{\parallel}(z=0^{-}),\,B_{\parallel}(z=0^{+})=B_{\parallel}(z=0^{-})

where
E_{\parallel}
(
B_{\parallel}
) means the in-plane component of the electric (magnetic) field.
The reflectivity in Fig.
3
(b) is obtained from numerically solving Eq. (
C2
) and the boundary condition for the case of normal incidence and with the optical conductivity
\sigma\left(z;t,t^{\prime}\right)=e^{z/d}\sigma_{\mathrm{s}}(t,t^{\prime})+(1-e^{z/d})\Theta(t-t^{\prime})n_{s}^{0}e^{2}/m
.

Schematic illustration of the reflection problem on the nonequilibrium sample.

We now provide an understanding of why the enhanced reflectivity may exceed unity in the limit of infinite penetration depth of the pump meaning that the whole sample is uniformly prepared as a nonequilibrium superconductor.
Assuming an exponential dependence of the superfluid density on time:
n_{\mathrm{s}}(t)\approx n_{0}e^{\gamma t}
, we take a derivative of the first equation of Eq. (
C2
) and obtain

\left(\partial_{t}-\gamma\right)\Box\mathbf{E}=-\partial_{t}\left[\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}^{2}\left(t\right)\mathbf{E}\right]

(C3)

where
\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}(t)=\sqrt{4\pi n_{s}(t)e^{2}/m}
is the instantaneous plasma frequency.

In the case of
\gamma\ll\omega_{0}
and
\delta=\infty
, the picture is that of a continuous EM wave incident on a sample with a slowly changing dielectric.
One may expect the EM wave below the surface of the sample to have the same frequency (
\omega_{0}
) as the incident beam, but with an instantaneous wave vector
k(t)
set by Eq. (
C3
) and corrected by
\gamma
:

\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r},t)=\mathbf{E}_{\text{T}}e^{-i\omega_{0}t}e^{-ik(t)z}
.
At
z\ll 1/k
and to leading order in
\gamma
, Eq. (
C3
) yields

c^{2}k(t)^{2}=\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}^{2}\left(t\right)-i2\gamma\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}^{2}/\omega_{0}
.
So as to reduce to the equilibrium case, we choose

ck=\begin{cases}i\sqrt{\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}+i2\gamma\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}^{2}/\omega_{0}}\,,&\omega_{0}<\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}(t),\\
\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}^{2}-i2\gamma\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}^{2}/\omega_{0}}\,,&\omega_{0}>\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}(t).\end{cases}

(C4)

For normal incidence, the boundary condition on the interface reduces to

E(z=0^{+})=E(z=0^{-}),\,\partial_{z}E(z=0^{+})=\partial_{z}E(z=0^{-})

which yields the Fresnel reflectivity

R=\left|\frac{\omega_{0}-ck}{\omega_{0}+ck}\right|^{2}

(C5)

in the continuous wave limit.
Therefore, for
\omega_{0}<\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}(t)
, one has
\mathrm{Im}[k]>0
, meaning an evanescent wave decaying exponentially into the bulk.
Notably, one has
R>1
because

\mathrm{Re}[k]<0

which is caused by supercurrent amplification.
For
\omega_{0}>\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}(t)
, one has
\mathrm{Re}[k]>0
such that the reflectivity is smaller than
1
.

A ‘quick and dirty’ derivation of Eq. (
C4
) exists
using the model

n_{\mathrm{s}}(t)\approx n_{s0}+[n_{s}(0)-n_{s0}]e^{-\gamma_{\Delta}t}

for the growing superfluid density, so that
the optical conductivity from Eq. (
15
) has the form
\sigma(\omega)|_{t=0}=\frac{e^{2}}{m}\left(i\frac{n_{s0}}{\omega}+i\frac{n_{s}(0)-n_{s0}}{\omega+i\gamma_{\Delta}}\right)

in the frequency domain.
Note that the negative weight of the Drude term results in a negative
\mathrm{Re}[\sigma]
which is forbidden in equillibrium.
Around time zero, the wave vector of the EM wave inside the sample is therefore
ck=\sqrt{\epsilon(\omega_{0})}\omega_{0}=\sqrt{1+\frac{4\pi i}{\omega_{0}}\sigma(\omega_{0})}\omega_{0}\approx\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}^{2}-i\gamma_{\Delta}(\omega_{\text{p}}^{2}-\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}^{2})/\omega_{0}}

where
\omega_{\text{p}}^{2}=4\pi n_{s0}(t)e^{2}/m
.
The
\gamma_{\Delta}
terms agrees with that of Eq. (
C4
) apart for a factor of
2
, if one uses the relation
\gamma_{\Delta}(\omega_{\text{p}}^{2}-\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}^{2})=\gamma\omega_{\mathrm{ps}}^{2}
to make the initial growth rates of the superfluid density consistent between the two models.

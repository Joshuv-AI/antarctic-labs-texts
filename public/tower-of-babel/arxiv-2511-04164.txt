1.
Introduction and main results

The problem of minimizing the distortion in a certain class of quasiconformal maps between two given rectangles is an old problem going back to Grötzsch
[
13
]
and has as solution the linear stretch. By using exponential and logarithmic changes of coordinates this result can be used to determine the solution of minimization problem of the mean distortion of within the class of maps with finite distortion between two annuli with given boundary values see
[
3
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
.

To be more precise let us recall, that if
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
are two bounded domains in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
and
f:\overline{\Omega}\to\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
is an orientation preserving homeomorphism, then
f
is said to be have finite distortion if it belongs to the Sobolev class
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
and there is a measurable function
K:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

1\leq K(z)<\infty,\ \text{and}\ |Df(z)|^{2}\leq K(z)Jf(z),\ \text{for a.e.}\ z\in\Omega,

where

|Df(z)|=|f_{z}(z)|+|f_{\bar{z}}(z)|

is the norm of the
\mathbb{R}
-linear differential map
Df(z):\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
Jf(z)=|f_{z}(z)|^{2}-|f_{\bar{z}}(z)|^{2}\geq 0
is its Jacobian determinant at a.e.
z\in\Omega
.

The linear distortion of
f
is defined for a.e.
\in\Omega
as

K(z,f)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\frac{|f_{z}|+|f_{\bar{z}}|}{|f_{z}|-|f_{\bar{z}}|}&\mbox{ if }|f_{\bar{z}}(z)|<|f_{z}(z)|\\[4.30554pt]
1&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.,

while the mean distortion is the functional given by

f\to\int_{\Omega}\varphi(K(z,f))\rho(z)d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)

where
\varphi:[1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a non-decreasing strictly convex function with
\varphi(1)=1
and
\rho:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a given density.
The problem of general interest is to minimize the above functional for
f\in\mathcal{F}
, where
\mathcal{F}\subseteq W^{1,2}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
is a given class of finite distortion maps satisfying some boundary conditions.

In this note, we consider the case when the domains
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
are rectangles in which case the extremal map is the linear stretch
[
13
]
,
[
7
]
.

Furthermore, we also consider the case of two annuli
\Omega=A_{1}
and
\Omega^{\prime}=A_{2}
given by

(1.1)

A_{1}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:q\leq|w|\leq 1\},\ \text{and}\ A_{2}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:q^{k}\leq|w|\leq 1\},

where
0<q<1
and
k>0
, and for
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
the extremal spiral-stretch map
g^{\ast}:A_{1}\to A_{2}
(see
[
3
]
,
[
7
]
) given by

(1.2)

g^{\ast}(w)=w|w|^{k-1}\exp\left(i\frac{\theta\log|w|}{\log q}\right).

Observe that the spiral-stretch map
g^{\ast}
fixes the outer part of the boundary of
A_{1}
while the inner part is stretched by a factor
k>0
and is rotated by an angle
\theta
.

This mapping transforms radial lines into spirals winding about the origin. It has important applications, for instance in the work of Gehring
[
12
]
on the universal Teichmüller space or in the work of John
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
in the study of the nonlinear elastic equilibrium with prescribed boundary displacements.

The spiral-stretch map was generalized to the sub-Riemannian setting of the Heisenberg group by Balogh, Fässler and Platis
[
4
]
. By a result of Gutlyanskii and Martio
[
14
]
(see also Balogh, Fässler and Platis
[
3
]
) it turns out that
g^{\ast}
is a solution of the above problem of minimization of a certain mean distortion functional.

Moreover, the following result of Feng, Hu and Shen
[
7
]
(see also
[
8
]
) says that
g^{\ast}
is the
unique
minimizer:

Theorem 1.1

(Feng-Hu-Shen)
.

If
g:A_{1}\to A_{2}
is an orientation preserving homeomorphism in
W^{1,2}(A_{1},A_{2})
with finite distortion such that

g=g^{\ast}
on
\partial A_{1}
, and

\varphi:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
is increasing and strictly convex with
\varphi(1)=1
, then

(1.3)

\int_{A_{1}}\frac{\varphi(K(w,g))}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)\geq\int_{A_{1}}\frac{\varphi(K(w,g^{\ast}))}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w),

with equality if and only if
g=g^{\ast}
.

As we show in Example
3.1
, strict convexity of
\varphi
is indeed a necessary assumption for the uniqueness of the minimizer.

The purpose of this paper, is to prove a quantitative stability version of this result akin to the quantitative stability results of
Fusco, Maggi, Pratelli
[
11
]

about the isperimetric inequality,
of Figalli, Maggi, Pratelli
[
10
]
about the Brunn-Minkowski inequality, or of Ball and Böröczky
[
5
]
, Böröczky, De
[
6
]
Figalli, van Hintum, Tiba
[
9
]
about the the Prékopa-Leindler inequality.
Roughly speaking, we show, that if for a certain
g:A_{1}\to A_{2}
the above mean distortion functional is close to the minimal value, then the map
g
must be quantitatively
L^{1}
-close to the minimizer
g^{\ast}
. A similar statement is the celebrated result of John
[
15
]
, stating that if the bi-Lipschitz constant of a bi-Lipschitz map is close to one, than the map itself has to be quantitatively close to an isometry.

In order to formulate our result, we introduce a quantity that measures the deviation of the inequality (
1.3
) to be an equality. This notion is the appropriate analogue of the so called isoperimetric deficit from
[
11
]
,
[
10
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
0<q<1
,
k>0
and
-\pi\leq\theta\leq\pi
, and let
\varphi:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
be an increasing and strictly convex function;
A_{1},A_{2}
the annuli as in (
1.1
) and
g:A_{1}\to A_{2}
is an orientation preserving homeomorphism with finite distortion in
W^{1,2}(A_{1},A_{2})
such that

g=g^{\ast}
on
\partial A_{1}
. We introduce the spiral-stretch deficit of
g
as the following quantity:

(1.4)

\delta^{SP}(g):=\frac{\int_{A_{1}}\frac{\varphi(K(w,g))}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)}{\int_{A_{1}}\frac{\varphi(K(w,g^{\ast}))}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)}-1\geq 0.

Let us observe that the second part of Theorem
1.1
can be reformulated by saying that
\delta^{SP}(g)=0
if and only if
g=g^{\ast}
. We can formulate the main result of the paper below, stating that
\delta^{SP}(g)\approx 0
then

g\approx g^{\ast}
.

In the statement of our theorem that we are implicitly using the well-known fact, that convex function on
(1,\infty)
is twice differentiable at a.e.
t\in(1,\infty)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<q<1
,
k>0
and
-\pi\leq\theta\leq\pi
, and let
\varphi:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
be an increasing and strictly convex function satisfying
\varphi(1)=1
and
\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t)>c
for a constant
c>0
and for a.e.
t\in[1,\infty)
. Then there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that, if
A_{1},A_{2}
are the annuli as in (
1.1
) and
g:A_{1}\to A_{2}
is an orientation preserving homeomorphism with finite distortion in
W^{1,2}(A_{1},A_{2})
such that

g=g^{\ast}
on
\partial A_{1}
and
0\leq\delta^{SP}(g)\leq\epsilon_{0}
, then

(1.5)

\int_{A_{1}}|g-g^{\ast}|\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}\leq C^{\cdot}(\delta^{SP}(g))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, the factor
\frac{1}{2}
in this statement is sharp (cf. (
3.22
) in Example
3.1
).

To obtain the quantitative stability result Theorem
3.1
, our argument is inspired by the proof of Theorem
1.1
in
[
7
]
. In particular, first, we consider a minimization problem of a mean distortion functional defined on finite distortion maps acting between quadrilaterals.
We obtain first a quantitative stability result for the minimization problem for quadrilaterals in Section
2
(see Theorem
2.1
) , and then use this result and exponential/logarithmic coordinates to prove Theorem
3.1
in Section

.

2.
A quantitative stability version of the minimality of the linear stretch map

In this section we consider the Grötzsch type minimization of the mean distortion of maps defined on quadrilaterals following the result of Feng-Hu-Shen
[
7
]
Theorem 1. More precisely, for
k,\ell>0
and
n\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the rectangle

Q_{1}=\{z=x+iy:x\in[0,\ell],\;\&\;y\in[0,1]\},

the lattice
L=\mathbb{Z}i+\mathbb{Z}(k\ell+in\ell)
,
and consider the class
\mathcal{F}
of orientation preserving homeomorphisms
f\in W^{1,2}(Q_{1})
on
Q_{1}
with finite distortion satisfying the following boundary conditions:

(2.1)

\displaystyle f(0)

\displaystyle=0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle f(x+i)

\displaystyle=f(x)+i,

\displaystyle x\in[0,\ell],

(2.3)

\displaystyle f(\ell+iy)

\displaystyle=f(iy)+k\ell+in\ell,

\displaystyle y\in[0,1],

(2.4)

\displaystyle f(Q_{1})

is a fundamental domain for
L
.

Let us note, that if
z=x+iy
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, then the linear stretch map

f^{*}(z)=kx+inx+iy,

is an element in the class
\mathcal{F}
.

Let us note, that for the linear map
f^{*}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
, one can compute directly that

\displaystyle\mu^{*}=

\displaystyle\frac{f^{*}_{\bar{z}}}{f^{*}_{z}}=\frac{k-1+in}{k+1+in}={\rm constant}\mbox{ \ \ where }0<|\mu^{*}|<1,

\displaystyle K(z,f^{*})=

\displaystyle\frac{1+|\mu^{*}|}{1-|\mu^{*}|}=\frac{|f^{*}_{z}|+|f^{*}_{\bar{z}}|}{|f^{*}_{z}|-|f^{*}_{\bar{z}}|}=\text{constant}.

Let us recall that according to Theorem 2 in
[
7
]
, if
\varphi
satisfies the properties in Theorem
3.1
, then for any
f\in\mathcal{F}
, we have the inequality

(2.5)

\int_{Q_{1}}K(z,f)\,\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)\geq\int_{Q_{1}}K(z,f^{*})\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z),

meaning that the linear stretch
f^{\ast}
minimizes the mean distortion functional in the class
\mathcal{F}
.

More generally, the same result states that

(2.6)

\int_{Q_{1}}\varphi(K(z,f))\,\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)\geq\int_{Q_{1}}\varphi(K(z,f^{*}))\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z),

for any increasing convex function
\varphi
; moreover, if
\varphi
is strictly convex, then equality holds in (
2.6
) if and only if
f=f^{*}
.

The following example shows that strict convexity of
\varphi
is indeed a necessary assumption for the uniqueness of the minimizer. Indeed, if we take
\varphi(t)=t
then (
2.5
) holds true, however the mean distortion functional has infinitely many minimizers as indicated in Example
2.1
:

Example 2.1
.

Consider
l=1
,
Q_{1}=[0,1]\times[0,1]
. For
k>1
, let
Q_{2}=[0,k]\times[0,1]
and consider the linear stretch
f^{\ast}:Q_{1}\to Q_{2}
given by
f^{\ast}(x+iy)=kx+iy
. Then, for any
0<\varepsilon<(k-1)^{2}
, there exists a map
f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
f_{\varepsilon}\neq f_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
for
\varepsilon\neq\varepsilon^{\prime}
, and
f_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

(2.7)

\int_{Q_{1}}K(z,f_{\varepsilon})d\mathcal{L}^{2}=\int_{Q_{1}}K(z,f^{\ast})d\mathcal{L}^{2}.

In what follows, we shall give an explicit formula of a map

with the properties stated in the example. First, let us note that

K(z,f^{\ast})=k.

Let
\varepsilon

(k-1)^{2}>\varepsilon>0

satisfying the statement in the example will be given by

is the piecewise linear map:

g_{\varepsilon}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}(k+\sqrt{\varepsilon})x&\mbox{ if }\ x\in[0,\frac{1}{2}],\\

For
z=x+iy
, if

, then

(2.8)

which in turn yields that

\int_{Q_{1}}K(z,f_{\varepsilon})d\mathcal{L}^{2}=\int_{Q_{1}}K(z,f^{\ast})d\mathcal{L}^{2},

as stated in Example

Proposition 2.1

Using the above notation, assume that the function

Theorem 2.1

Let us assume that

The proof of Theorem
2.1
is based on Proposition
2.1
. In turn, the proof of Proposition
2.1
is based on a number of lemmata, and the first one is a following well-known Taylor-type formula.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
c>0
and
\varphi
is a convex function on an open interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq c
for a.e.
t\in I
, then for any
t,s\in I
, writing
\varphi^{\prime}_{+}
to denote the right handed derivative, we have

(2.13)

\varphi(t)\geq\varphi(s)+\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(s)(t-s)+\frac{c}{2}\,(t-s)^{2}.

Proof.

More precisely, we prove that for any subgradient
a\in\partial\varphi(s)
, we have

(2.14)

\varphi(t)\geq\varphi(s)+a(t-s)+\frac{c}{2}\,(t-s)^{2}.

If
t>s
, then by Theorem 1.3.1 in
[
17
]
, using that
\varphi^{\prime}_{+}
is monotone increasing, we deduce that
\varphi^{\prime}_{+}
is almost everywhere differentiable and

\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(t)-\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(s)\geq\int_{s}^{t}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)\,d\tau,

thus the bound
\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)\geq c
yields that
\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(t)\geq\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(s)+c(t-s)
. Since
\varphi
is Lipschitz on
[s,t]
, it follows that

\varphi(t)-\varphi(s)=\int_{s}^{t}\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(\tau)\,d\tau\geq\int_{s}^{t}\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(s)+c(\tau-s)\,d\tau=\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(s)(t-s)+\frac{c}{2}\,(t-s)^{2}\geq a(t-s)+\frac{c}{2}\,(t-s)^{2}.

If
t<s
, then we replace
\varphi
by the function
\psi(\tau)=\varphi(2s-\tau)
satisfying that
\psi(s)=\varphi(s)
,
\psi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)\geq c
and
-a\in\partial\psi(s)
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

If (
2.9
) holds for small
\varepsilon>0
, then

(2.15)

\int_{Q_{1}}(K(z,f)-K(z,f^{*}))^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)\ll\varepsilon.

Let us recall that by Theorem 2 of
[

\int_{Q_{1}}K(z,f)\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)\geq\int_{Q_{1}}K(z,f^{*})\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z).

On the other hand, it follows by the Taylor formula (

)and the condition

that for a.e.

\varphi(K(z,f))-\varphi(K(z,f^{*}))\geq\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(K(z,f^{*}))(K(z,f)-K(z,f^{*}))+\frac{c}{2}(K(z,f)-K(z,f^{*}))^{2}

\displaystyle\varepsilon\cdot\int_{Q_{1}}\varphi(K(z,f^{*}))\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)\geq

The following lemma gives a similar estimate as (

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists a constant
C=C(\varphi)>0
such that if (

\int_{Q_{1}}K(z,f)\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)\leq(1+C\varepsilon)\int_{Q_{1}}K(z,f^{\ast})\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z).

Proof.

By the convexity of
\varphi
we can apply Jensen’s inequality and (

\displaystyle\varphi\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{1}|}\int_{Q_{1}}K(z,f)\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)\right)\leq\frac{1}{|Q_{1}|}\int_{Q_{1}}\varphi(K(z,f))\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q_{1}|}(1+\varepsilon)\int_{Q_{1}}\varphi(K(z,f^{\ast}))\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)=(1+\varepsilon)\varphi(K(z,f^{\ast}).

Let
C>0
be a constant to be determined later. By the convexity of
\varphi
we can write for

\displaystyle\varphi(K(z,f^{\ast})(1+C\varepsilon))=\varphi(K(z,f^{\ast})+C\varepsilon K(z,f^{\ast}))\geq

\displaystyle\varphi(K(z,f^{\ast}))+\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(K(z,f^{\ast})\cdot C\varepsilon K(z,f^{\ast})=\left(1+\frac{\varphi_{+}^{\prime}(K(z,f^{\ast}))\cdot K(z,f^{\ast})}{\varphi(K(z,f^{\ast})}\cdot C\varepsilon\right).

\varphi(K(z,f^{\ast})(1+C\varepsilon)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\varphi(K(z,f^{\ast}).

In conclusion, we obtain

∎

) and the above consideration we obtain

(3.12)

\int_{Q_{1}}\varphi(K(z,f))\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\int_{Q_{1}}\varphi(K(z,f^{\ast}))\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)

and thus by Proposition
2.1
we obtain that for
\Psi=f\circ(f^{\ast})^{-1}
we have the estimate

\int_{Q_{2}}|\Psi_{\bar{z}}(z)|\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

Let us consider the map

\Phi:A_{2}\to A_{2},\ \Phi:=g\circ(g^{\ast})^{-1}.

We observe that
\Phi(w)
is well defined for all
w\in\partial A_{2}
by the boundary conditions.
Furthermore, let us note that
\Phi=F\circ\Psi\circ G
where both
F
and
G
are conformal maps, and
and
F:Q_{g}\to A_{2}
is given by

(3.13)

F:z\to w=q^{k}\exp(2\pi z),

and
G:Q_{2}\to A_{2}
is given by (
3.7
).

By the chain rule we have the equality:

\Phi_{\bar{w}}(w)=F_{z}(\Psi(G(w)))\cdot\Psi_{\bar{z}}(G(w))\cdot\overline{G_{w}(w)}.

Using the fact that
|F_{z}|\approx 1
,
|G_{w}|\approx 1
and
|G^{-1}_{z}|=J(G^{-1})\approx 1
, and by the change of variable
z=G(w)
we obtain

(3.14)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{2}}|\Phi_{\bar{w}}|(w)\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)\approx

\displaystyle\int_{A_{2}}|\Psi_{\bar{z}}(G(w))|\cdot\left|\overline{G_{w}(w)}\right|\ \,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)

(3.15)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{2}}|\Psi_{\bar{z}}(z)|\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon},

Our next step is to show that (
3.14
) implies that

(3.16)

\int_{A_{2}}|\Phi(w)-w|\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

In order to see this we shall apply the Cauchy-Pompeiu formula (see e.g.
[
18
]
),

(3.17)

\Phi(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial A_{2}}\frac{\Phi(\xi)}{\xi-w}\,d\xi+E(w)

where

E(w)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{A_{2}}\frac{\Phi_{\bar{w}}(\xi)}{\xi-w}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(\xi).

Let us note that for fixed
\xi\in A_{2}
,

\int_{A_{2}}\frac{1}{|w-\xi|}d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)\ll\int_{A_{2}-A_{2}}\frac{1}{|w|}d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)\ll 1.

Now, it follows from (
3.14
)
that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{2}}|E(w)|\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)\ll

\displaystyle\int_{A_{2}}\int_{A_{2}}\frac{|\Phi_{\bar{w}}(\xi)|}{|w-\xi|}d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(\xi)

(3.19)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\int_{A_{2}}|\Phi_{\bar{w}}(\xi)|\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(\xi)\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

Let us recall that
\Phi(\xi)=\xi
for
\xi\in\partial A_{2}
, and therefore

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial A_{2}}\frac{\Phi(\xi)}{\xi-w}\,d\xi=w\ \text{for}\ w\in{\rm int}\,A_{2}.

Using the above relation, (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), we obtain (
3.16
).

Having the estimate (
3.16
) at hand we can finish the proof of Theorem
3.1
as follows.

Observe that
J(w,(g^{\ast})^{-1})\approx 1
. This implies by the change of variable

z=(g^{\ast})^{-1}(w)

\int_{A_{1}}|g-g^{\ast}|(z)\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(z)=\int_{A_{2}}|\Phi(w)-w|J(w,(g^{\ast})^{-1})\cdot\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

Observe that similar as in Theorem
2.1
, the condition
g=g^{\ast}
on

\partial A_{1}
can be relaxed to the assumption

\int_{\partial A_{1}}|g-g^{\ast}|\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon},

to obtain the same conclusion.

In the following, we present an example related to Example
2.2
showing the sharpness of
the factor
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
in the statement of Theorem
3.1
.

Example 3.1
.

Let
0<q<1
and
k>1
, and for the annuli

A_{1}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:q\leq|w|\leq 1\}\ \text{and}\ A_{2}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:q^{k}\leq|w|\leq 1\},

consider the radial stretch map,

g^{\ast}:A_{1}\to A_{2},\ g^{\ast}(w)=w\cdot|w|^{k-1}.

For small
\varepsilon>0
, we construct quasi-conformal
g=g^{(\varepsilon)}:A_{1}\to A_{2}
such that
g|_{\partial A_{1}}=g^{\ast}|_{\partial A_{1}}
with the properties that

(i):

We have
g^{(\varepsilon)}\neq g^{(\varepsilon^{\prime})}
for
\varepsilon\neq\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,(k-1)^{2})
, and
taking
\varphi(t)=t
, the quasi-conformal map

g=g^{(\varepsilon)}
satisfies

(3.20)

\int_{A_{1}}\frac{K(w,g)}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)=\int_{A_{1}}\frac{K(w,g^{\ast})}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w),

(ii):

If
\varphi(t)=t^{2}
and
g=g^{(\varepsilon)}
, then

(3.21)

\int_{A_{1}}\frac{\varphi(K(w,g))}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\int_{A_{1}}\frac{\varphi(K(w,g^{\ast}))}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w),

and

(3.22)

\int_{A_{1}}|g-g^{\ast}|(w)d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)\gg\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

(iii):

If
\varphi(1)=1
and
\varphi(t)=t+\exp\left(\frac{-1}{(t-1)^{2}}\right)
for
t>1
, then

\varphi
is
a
C^{\infty}
increasing and strictly convex function
such that for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and for any small
\varepsilon>0
(”smallness” depending on
\alpha
and
\varphi
), the quasi-conformal map
g=g^{(\varepsilon)}
satisfies

(3.23)

\int_{A_{1}}\frac{\varphi(K(w,g))}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)\leq(1+\eta)\int_{A_{1}}\frac{\varphi(K(w,g^{\ast}))}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)

for
\eta=\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}
,
and

(3.24)

\int_{A_{1}}|g-g^{\ast}|(w)d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)>\eta^{\alpha}.

Let us note first that
g^{\ast}(w)=w^{\frac{k+1}{2}}\cdot\bar{w}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}
, which implies that

|g^{\ast}_{w}(w)|=\left(\frac{k+1}{2}\right)\cdot|w|^{k-1}\ \text{and}\ |g^{\ast}_{\bar{w}}(w)|=\left(\frac{k-1}{2}\right)\cdot|w|^{k-1},

and hence

K(w,g^{\ast})=\frac{|g^{\ast}_{w}(w)|+|g^{\ast}_{\bar{w}}(w)|}{|g^{\ast}_{w}(w)|-|g^{\ast}_{\bar{w}}(w)|}=k.

Using this, it is easy to compute by using integration in polar coordinates that the mean distortion of the minimizer
g^{\ast}
satisfies

(3.25)

\int_{A_{1}}\frac{\varphi(K(w,g^{\ast}))}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)=2\pi\left(\log\frac{1}{q}\right)\cdot\varphi(k).

For a given positive
\varepsilon<(k-1)^{2}
, the map
g=g^{(\varepsilon)}:A_{1}\to A_{2}
satisfies the formula

g(w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}q^{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\cdot w\cdot|w|^{(k-1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})}&\mbox{ if }\ |w|\in[q,q^{\frac{1}{2}}],\\
w\cdot|w|^{(k-1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})}&\mbox{if}\ |w|\in[q^{\frac{1}{2}},1].\end{array}\right.

Using this formula, the complex derivatives can be easily calculated and will be given as

g_{w}(w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}q^{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\cdot\frac{k+1-\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{2}\cdot|w|^{(k-1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})}&\mbox{ if }\ |w|\in[q,q^{\frac{1}{2}}],\\
\frac{k+1+\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{2}\cdot|w|^{(k-1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})}&\mbox{if}\ |w|\in[q^{\frac{1}{2}},1],\end{array}\right.

and also

g_{\bar{w}}(w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}q^{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\cdot\frac{k-1-\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{2}\cdot|w|^{(k-1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})}&\mbox{ if }\ |w|\in[q,q^{\frac{1}{2}}],\\
\frac{k-1+\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{2}\cdot|w|^{(k-1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})}&\mbox{if}\ |w|\in[q^{\frac{1}{2}},1],\end{array}\right.

which gives the following simple expression for the distortion:

(3.26)

K(w,g)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}k-\sqrt{\varepsilon}&\mbox{ \ if }\ |w|\in[q,q^{\frac{1}{2}}),\\
k+\sqrt{\varepsilon}&\mbox{ \ if}\ |w|\in(q^{\frac{1}{2}},1].\end{array}\right.

Using this formula and integration in polar coordinates
w=r\exp(i\theta)
, we obtain (
3.20
) in (i) by (
3.25
), and in the case of (ii) and
\varphi(t)=t^{2}
the formula

(3.27)

\int_{A_{1}}\frac{\varphi(K(w,g))}{|w|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)=2\pi\left(\log\frac{1}{q}\right)\cdot(k^{2}+\varepsilon)

for the mean distortion in (
3.27
). Combining the relations (
3.25
) and (
3.27
) yields (
3.21
) in (ii).

For (ii), it remains to verify the relation (
3.22
). To do that, we shall write
w
in polar coordinates
w=r\exp(i\theta)
. In these coordinates, the map
g:A_{1}\to A_{2}
will be given by the formula

g(r\exp(i\theta))=\varrho_{\varepsilon}(r)\exp(i\theta)
, where
\varrho_{\varepsilon}:[q,1]\to[q^{k},1]
satisfies

\varrho_{\varepsilon}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}r^{(k-\sqrt{\varepsilon})}\cdot q^{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}&\mbox{ \ if }\ r\in[q,q^{\frac{1}{2}}],\\
r^{(k+\sqrt{\varepsilon})}&\mbox{ \ if}\ r\in[q^{\frac{1}{2}},1].\end{array}\right.

This implies that

\int_{A_{1}}|g-g^{\ast}|(w)d\mathcal{L}^{2}(w)=2\pi\int_{q}^{1}|r^{k}-\varrho_{\varepsilon}(r)|rdr=2\pi\int_{q}^{1}(r^{k}-\varrho_{\varepsilon}(r))rdr,

since
r^{k}>\varrho_{\varepsilon}(r)
for any
r\in(q,1)
.
Using a Taylor expansion in terms of
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
of the function
\varrho_{\varepsilon}
for fixed
r\in[q^{\frac{1}{4}},q^{\frac{3}{4}}]
, we deduce the existence of
C>0
depending on
q
such that

(r^{k}-\varrho_{\varepsilon}(r))r\geq C\sqrt{\varepsilon},\mbox{ for }r\in[q^{\frac{1}{4}},q^{\frac{3}{4}}].

In turn, we estimate the integral as

\int_{q}^{1}(r^{k}-\varrho_{\varepsilon}(r))rdr\gg\sqrt{\varepsilon},

proving (
3.22
).

Turning to (iii), in this case all derivatives of
\varphi
at
1
are zero, thus
(
3.26
) yields
(
3.23
), and hence
(
3.22
) implies (
3.24
).
This finishes the proof of the statements in Example
3.1
; and in particular, the sharpness of the factor
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
in Theorem
3.1
.

The Dirichlet spectrum
Alon Agin, Barak Weiss

Abstract
Akhunzhanov and Shatskov defined the Dirichlet spectrum, corresponding to m × n m\times n matrices and to norms on ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{m} and ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . In case ( m , n ) = ( 2 , 1 ) (m,n)=(2,1) and using the Euclidean norm on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , they showed that the spectrum is an interval. We generalize this result to arbitrary ( m , n ) ≠ ( 1 , 1 ) (m,n)\neq(1,1) and arbitrary norms, improving previous works [ KR22 ] , [ Sch23a ] [ Sch23b ] [ HSW24 ] , [ Agi24 ] . We also define some related spectra and show that they too are intervals. Our argument is a modification of an argument of Khintchine from 1926.

1.1
Some extensions of the main result

Let
d=m+n
. We say two positive vectors
\vec{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{m}
and
\vec{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}

are weights on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}=1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{X}_{d}}=SL_{d}(\mathbb{R})/SL_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
denote the space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Given
\Theta\in M_{m,n}
we denote by
\Lambda_{\Theta}
the unimodular lattice defined by

\boldsymbol{\Lambda_{\Theta}}:=\begin{pmatrix}I_{m}&\Theta\\
0&I_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Given
t>0
and
\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta}
weights on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define

\boldsymbol{g_{t,\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta}\,}}:=diag\,(t^{\alpha_{1}},\,...\,,t^{\alpha_{m}},t^{-\beta_{1}},\,...\,,t^{-\beta_{n}})\in SL_{d}(\mathbb{R}).

Let
b\in(0,\infty]
and let
\{E_{\varepsilon}\,|\,\varepsilon\in(0,b]\,\}
be a continuous decreasing exhaustion of
\mathcal{X}_{d}
by compact sets (see (
11
), section 3).

Motivated by a question posed by Dmitry Kleinbock, for
\Lambda\in\mathcal{X}_{d}
we define
the extended

Dirichlet constant of
\Lambda
(w.r.t.
E_{\varepsilon},\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta}
) to be

\boldsymbol{dir(\Lambda)\,(E_{\varepsilon},\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta})}:=\inf\,\{\,\varepsilon>0\,\,|\,\,g_{t,\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta}\,}\Lambda\notin\,E_{\varepsilon}\,\,for\,\,all\,\,t\,\,large\,\,enough\},

with the convention that
\inf\varnothing=b
. Now define
the extended Dirichlet spectrum of
\mathcal{X}_{d}
(w.r.t.
E_{\varepsilon},\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta}
) to be

\boldsymbol{\mathbb{D}_{d}\,(E_{\varepsilon},\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta})}:=\{\,dir(\Lambda)\,(E_{\varepsilon},\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta})\,|\,\Lambda\in X_{d}\,\}.

In this paper we also prove the following:

Theorem 2
.

Let
d\geq 3
. Let
\{E_{\varepsilon}\,|\,\varepsilon\in(0,b]\,\}
be a continuous decreasing exhaustion by compact sets of
\mathcal{X}_{d}
for some
b\in(0,\infty]
. Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m+n=d
and let
\vec{\alpha}
and
\vec{\beta}
be arbitrary weights on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

\mathbb{D}_{d}\,(E_{\varepsilon},\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta})=[0,b].

Furthermore, for any
c\in[0,b]
and any open set
V\in M_{m,n}
we have that the set

\{\Theta\in V\,|\,dir(\Lambda_{\Theta})\,(E_{\varepsilon},\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta})=c\,\}

is uncountable.

For a more detailed discussion see section 3, during which we also explain why Theorem 2 is a generalization of Theorem 1.

The paper is structured as follows. In the following section we generalise the setting by changing the function
t^{-1}
to a more general one, still staying within the realm of Diophantine approximation. For
\Theta\in M_{m,n}
, we discuss
\hat{\omega}(\Theta)
the uniform exponent of
\Theta
, and use a generalisation of Theorem 1 to prove existence of matrices exhibiting special properties with respect to their uniform exponent (Theorem 3 and Corollary 1). In section 3, we generalise the setting to the space of unimodular lattices in more detail, turning the question to one which focuses on the behaviour of certain trajectories under one-parameter diagonalizable flows (Theorem 2 and 4). In sections 4 and 5 we show that Theorems 2 and 3 follow from a more general topological theorem (Theorem 5). Theorem 5 is the main result of this paper. Throughout the paper we use some technical lemmas (Lemmas 1-4);
For readability, we postpone the proofs of these lemmas to a separate and final section.

For
\gamma>0
we define
the
\gamma
-irrationality measure function of
M_{m,n}
(w.r.t.
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,n}
) to be the function
\chi_{\gamma}:M_{m,n}\times[t_{0},\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
defined by

\boldsymbol{\chi_{\gamma}(\Theta,t)}=\chi_{\gamma}(\Theta,t)_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,n}}:=t^{\gamma}\,\big(\min_{0<\left\lVert\vec{q}\,\right\rVert_{n}\leq\,t\,\,\,\,\vec{p}\,\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left\lVert\Theta\vec{q}-\vec{p}\,\right\rVert_{m}\big).

One can easily show that if
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{\gamma}(\Theta,t)=c
for some

then for any
\delta,\varepsilon
with

we have
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{\delta}(\Theta,t)=0

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{\varepsilon}(\Theta,t)=\infty
. So by Dirichlet’s theorem we have that if
\gamma<n/m
then
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{\gamma}(\Theta,t)=0

\Theta\in M_{m,n}
.

Now for
\Theta\in M_{m,n}

(w.r.t.
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,n}

\boldsymbol{\hat{\omega}(\Theta)}=\hat{\omega}(\Theta)_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,n}}:=\sup\big\{\,\gamma>0\,|\,\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\,\chi_{\gamma}(\Theta,t)<\infty\,\big\}.

By Dirichlet’s theorem, clearly we have
\hat{\omega}(\Theta)\geq n/m
for every
\Theta\in M_{m,n}
, and by (
3
) we have
\hat{\omega}(\Theta)=n/m
for Lebesgue almost every
\Theta\in M_{m,n}
(see e.g
[
KMW21
]
for further discussion and results).

More generally, let
t>0
and let
\psi(t)
be a positive continuous decreasing function.

For
\Theta\in M_{m,n}
fixed define

\boldsymbol{\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t)}=\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t)_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,n}}:=\min_{(\vec{q},\vec{p}\,)\in\,\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}}\,\max\,\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}t^{-1/n}\,\left\lVert\vec{q}\,\right\rVert_{n}\\
\psi(t)^{-1/m}\,\left\lVert\Theta\vec{q}-\vec{p}\,\right\rVert_{m}\end{array}

(4)

and for
\gamma>0
define
\boldsymbol{\psi_{\gamma}(t)}:=t^{-\gamma}
.

That is

\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}(t)=\min_{(\vec{q},\vec{p}\,)\in\,\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}}\,\max\,\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}t^{-1/n}\,\left\lVert\vec{q}\,\right\rVert_{n}\\
t^{\,\gamma/m}\,\left\lVert\Theta\vec{q}-\vec{p}\,\right\rVert_{m}\end{array}.

For
\Theta
fixed, clearly
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}(t)
and
\chi_{\gamma}(\Theta,t)
are closely related functions; note however that
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}(t)
is a continuous function of

, but

is not. In section 6 we give some elementary statements connecting these two functions. In particular, we prove Lemma

which states that for

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\,\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}(t)\,^{1+\gamma n/m}=\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\,\chi_{\gamma n/m}(\Theta,t).

(5)

Now for
\psi(t)
positive continuous and decreasing, we define
the
\psi
-Dirichlet spectrum

of

(w.r.t.
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,n}
) to be

\boldsymbol{\mathbb{D}_{m,n}(\psi)}:=\big\{\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\,\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t)\,\,|\,\,\Theta\in M_{m,n}\big\}.

In particular for
\gamma>0
we have that

\mathbb{D}_{m,n}(\psi_{\gamma m/n})=\big\{\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\,\chi_{\gamma}(\Theta,t)\,\,|\,\,\Theta\in M_{m,n}\big\}.

In this paper we also prove the following:

Theorem 3
.

Let
m
and
n
such that
\max\,(m,n)>1
, let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m}

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{n}

One-parameter diagonalizable flows on
\mathcal{X}_{d}

Let
\mathcal{X}_{d}
,

\Lambda_{\Theta}
,
\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta}
and

as in section 1.1. Let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{d}
be an arbitrary norm on

For convenience, denote
\boldsymbol{\vec{m}_{1}}:=(1/m\,,\,...\,,\,1/m)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

\boldsymbol{\vec{n}_{1}}:=(1/n\,,\,...\,,\,1/n)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

For a lattice
\Lambda
(not necessarily unimodular) define

successive minimum

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{d}

\boldsymbol{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)}=\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)(\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{d}):=\inf\{\,\left\lVert\vec{u}\right\rVert_{d}\,|\,\vec{0}\neq\vec{u}\in\Lambda\}=\min\{\,\left\lVert\vec{u}\right\rVert_{d}\,|\,\vec{0}\neq\vec{u}\in\Lambda\}

where the last equality holds by discreteness. Most of the time we’ll denote the function
\lambda_{1}(\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{d})
only as
\lambda_{1}

If
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{d}=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,n}
for two arbitrary norms on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and if
\vec{\alpha}=\vec{m}_{1}
and
\vec{\beta}=\vec{n}_{1}
, then for any
\Theta\in M_{m,n}
and any

a simple calculation shows that

\lambda_{1}(g_{t,\vec{m}_{1},\vec{n}_{1}\,}\Lambda_{\Theta})=\lambda_{\Theta,t^{-1}}(t)=\min_{(\vec{q},\vec{p}\,)\in\,\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}}\,\max\,\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}t^{-1/n}\,\left\lVert\vec{q}\,\right\rVert_{n}\\
t^{1/m}\,\left\lVert\Theta\vec{q}-\vec{p}\,\right\rVert_{m}\end{array}

(6)

for

as in formula (

). So by Lemma

\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\,\lambda_{1}(g_{t,\vec{m}_{1},\vec{n}_{1}\,}\Lambda_{\Theta})\,^{1+n/m}=\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\,\chi(\Theta,t)_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,n}}.

(7)

So with a little more work, one can show that in the specific case of
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{d}=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,n}
, for any
\Theta\in M_{m,n}
and any

\lambda_{1}(g_{t,\vec{m}_{1},\vec{n}_{1}\,}\Lambda_{\Theta})\,^{1+n/m}\leq\Delta_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,n}},

for

Furthermore, one can show that in fact for any

we still have that

\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)\leq\Delta_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,n}}^{m/(m+n)}

and that

Notice that for general norm and weights

Nevertheless, we still have that there exists a minimal constant

such that for any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{X}_{d}
we have that

\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)\leq r_{\,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{d}},

(8)

and in case
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{d}=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,n}
we have that

Now for
\varepsilon>0

\boldsymbol{K_{\varepsilon}}=K_{\varepsilon}(\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{d}):=\{\Lambda\in\mathcal{X}_{d}\,|\,\varepsilon\leq\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)\,\}
. As before, we’ll denote this set only as

, keeping in mind that this is also a norm-dependent set. By Mahler’s compactness criterion and by (

.

With this notation, for
\Lambda\in\mathcal{X}_{d}
we have that

\begin{split}\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)&=\inf\,\,\{\varepsilon>0\,\,|\,\,\Lambda\notin K_{\varepsilon}\,\},\end{split}

(9)

and in particular for

\begin{split}\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{1}(g_{t,\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta}\,}\,\Lambda_{\Theta})=\inf\,\,\{\,\varepsilon>0\,\,|\,\,g_{t,\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta}\,}\,\Lambda_{\Theta}\notin K_{\varepsilon}\,for\,\,all\,\,t\,\,large\,\,enough\},\end{split}

with the convention that

So in other words, Theorem 1 tells us that for every

More generally, given

4
A topological theorem

Given a topological space
\Xi
and
(X_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
a sequence of subsets of
\Xi
, and given
\Omega
an open set in
\Xi
, we say
\Omega
is connected via

if there exists

such that
\Omega\cap X_{j}\neq\O
and such that for any

and any

a neighborhood of

such that
\Omega\,\cap\,X_{i}
is connected,
\Omega\cap X_{i}\cap U_{y}\neq\O
and
\Omega\cap X_{i}\cap X_{j}\neq\O
.

We say
the space

(X_{n})
if for any

and every
U_{x}
a neighborhood of

\Omega_{x}
an open neighborhood of

\Omega_{x}
is connected via
(X_{n})
and satisfies
\overline{\Omega_{x}}\subset U_{x}
.

Notice that in particular if

then
\Xi
is locally compact, and that

\cup_{n}X_{n}
is dense in
\Xi

We now prove the following:

with the one point compactification topology.

Let

be a sequence of subsets of
\Xi
such that the following hold:

•

The space
\Xi
is locally connected via
(X_{n})
.

•

and every

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}f(x,t)=0

, every

.

Assume that there exists

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\psi(t)=0
,
\Theta\in M_{m,n}
fixed, and let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{n}
be arbitrary norms on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By discreteness of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and since
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}
is continuous and
\psi
is decreasing to zero, there exists a sequence
(t_{k},\vec{q}_{k},\vec{p}_{k})\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}
with
1=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}...
such that if
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]

\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t)=\max\,\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}t^{-1/n}\,\left\lVert\vec{q}_{k}\right\rVert_{n}\\
\psi(t)^{-1/m}\,\left\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k}\,\right\rVert_{m}.\end{array}

(16)

In case there exists
(\vec{Q},\vec{P}\,)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}
such that
\Theta\vec{Q}=\vec{P}
then the sequence
(t_{k},\vec{q}_{k},\vec{p}_{k})
is finite, and for all

\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t)=t^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{Q}\lVert_{n}
. Otherwise, the sequence
(t_{k},\vec{q}_{k},\vec{p}_{k})
is infinite, and we have that
t_{k}\longrightarrow\infty
.

In both of these cases, we say
\bm{(t_{k},\vec{q}_{k},\vec{p}_{k})}

realizes

\bm{\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}}
.

Let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{n}

and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\psi(t)
be a positive

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\psi(t)=0
, and let
\Theta\in M_{m,n}
. Let
(t_{k},\vec{q}_{k},\vec{p}_{k})\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}
which realizes
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}
as in (
16
).

Then there exists a sequence of times

with
z_{k}\in(t_{k},t_{k+1})
such that for all
k

\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t)

,

•

•

.

6.1
Proofs

Proof of Lemma 3.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We can assume that the segment in which
(\vec{q}_{k},\vec{p}_{k})
realizes
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}
is of maximum of length. I.e. if
t\notin[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
then

\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t)<\max\,\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}t^{-1/n}\,\left\lVert\vec{q}_{k}\right\rVert_{n}\\
\psi(t)^{-1/m}\,\left\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k}\,\right\rVert_{m}\end{array}.

Let
S,T
with
t_{k}<S<t_{k+1}<T<t_{k+2}
. Then we have:

\displaystyle\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(S)=\max\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}S^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k}\lVert_{n}\\
\psi(S)^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k}\,\lVert_{m}\end{array}

\displaystyle<\,\,\,\max\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}S^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\\
\psi(S)^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k+1}-\vec{p}_{k+1}\,\lVert_{m}\end{array}

\displaystyle\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t_{k+1})=\max\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}t_{k+1}^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k}\lVert_{n}\\
\psi(t_{k+1})^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k}\,\lVert_{m}\end{array}

\displaystyle=\,\,\,\max\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}t_{k+1}^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\\
\psi(t_{k+1})^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k+1}-\vec{p}_{k+1}\,\lVert_{m}\end{array}

\displaystyle\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(T)=\max\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}T^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\\
\psi(T)^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k+1}-\vec{p}_{k+1}\,\lVert_{m}\end{array}

\displaystyle<\,\,\,\max\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}T^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k}\lVert_{n}\\
\psi(T)^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k}\,\lVert_{m}\end{array}.

As we assume that
\psi
is decreasing to zero, we get Lemma 3. ∎

Proof of Lemma 4.
By Lemma
3
we have for all
k

\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t_{k+1})=t_{k+1}^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\,>\,\psi(t_{k+1})^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k+1}-\vec{p}_{k+1}\,\lVert_{m}

and so
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}
is decreasing in some right neighbourhood of
t_{k+1}
.

As
\psi
is decreasing to zero, the function
h(t):=\max\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}t^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\\
\psi(t)^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k+1}-\vec{p}_{k+1}\,\lVert_{m}\end{array}

is the maximum between two positive functions – the first is decreasing to zero, the second increases to infinity – hence there exists a minimum point
z\in(t_{k+1},\infty)
such that
h(t)
is decreasing in
(0,z]
and increases to infinity in
[z,\infty)
.

So we are only left with showing that
z<t_{k+2}
. Indeed, assume by contradiction
z\geq t_{k+2}
. Then we have

\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t_{k+2})=t_{k+2}^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\,\geq\,\psi(t_{k+2})^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k+1}-\vec{p}_{k+1}\,\lVert_{m}.

(17)

So as
(t_{k},\vec{q}_{k},\vec{p}_{k})
realizes
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}
, we must have that

t_{k+2}^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\,=\max\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}t_{k+2}^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+2}\lVert_{n}\\
\psi(t_{k+2})^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k+2}-\vec{p}_{k+2}\,\lVert_{m}.\end{array}

By Lemma
3

,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}<\lVert\vec{q}_{k+2}\lVert_{n}
so we must have

t_{k+2}^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\,=\psi(t_{k+2})^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k+2}-\vec{p}_{k+2}\,\lVert_{m},

hence by (
17
)

\psi(t_{k+2})^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k+2}-\vec{p}_{k+2}\,\lVert_{m}\,\geq\,\psi(t_{k+2})^{-1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k+1}-\vec{p}_{k+1}\,\lVert_{m},

which cannot hold by Lemma
3
. ∎

Proof of Lemma 1.
Notice that as we assume that
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\,\lambda_{\Theta,t^{-1}}(\Theta,t)>0
we have that the sequence which realizes
\lambda_{\Theta,t^{-1}}
is infinite, hence also the sequence which realizes
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}
. Now assume by contradiction that
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t)<\infty
,  and let
(t_{k},\vec{q}_{k},\vec{p}_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}
which realizes
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}
.

By Lemma
4
,
(t_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is the sequence of local maxima of
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}
and for all
k\geq 0
we have that

\begin{split}\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t_{k+1})=\lVert\psi(t_{k+1})^{-1/m}\,(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}=\lVert t_{k+1}^{-1/n}\,\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}.\end{split}

(18)

So by (
18
) we have that

\begin{split}\infty>\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t)^{m+n}&=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\Theta,\psi}(t_{k+1})^{m+n}\\
&=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,(t_{k+1}\,\psi(t_{k+1}))^{-1}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}^{n}\,\,\lVert(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}^{m}.\end{split}

As we assume
\psi(t)=o(t^{-1})
we must have

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}^{n}\,\,\lVert(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}^{m}\,=0.

(19)

Now let
(s_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be the sequence which satisfies for all
k\geq 0
that

\lVert s_{k+1}^{-1/n}\,\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}=\lVert s_{k+1}^{1/m}\,(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}.

By Lemma
3
we have
s_{1}<s_{2}<...<s_{k}<...
and
s_{k}\longrightarrow\infty
.

Now define a function
g:[s_{1},\infty)\longrightarrow(0,\infty)
such that for
t\in[s_{k},s_{k+1})
we set

g(t):=\max\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}t^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k}\lVert_{n}\\
t^{1/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k}\,\lVert_{m}\end{array}

Notice that there exists a sequence of times
(z_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
with
z_{k}\in(s_{k},s_{k+1})
such that for all
k\geq 1
we have that
g
is decreasing in
[s_{k},z_{k}]
, increasing in
[z_{k},s_{k+1}]
,
and we have that

•

t\in[s_{k},z_{k}]\Longrightarrow g(t)=\lVert t^{-1/n}\,\vec{q}_{k}\lVert_{n}

•

t\in[z_{k},s_{k+1}]\Longrightarrow g(t)=\lVert t^{1/m}\,(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}
.

Indeed, assume by contradiction that
g
is increasing in some right neighborhood of
s_{k+1}
for some
k
. So we must have that
\lVert s_{k+1}^{-1/n}\,\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\leq\lVert s_{k+1}^{1/m}\,(\Theta\vec{q}_{k+1}-\vec{p}_{k+1})\lVert_{m}
, which cannot hold since
\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k}\lVert_{m}>\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k+1}-\vec{p}_{k+1}\lVert_{m}
and the way we chose the sequence
(s_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
.

Similarly, assume by contradiction that
g
is decreasing in some left neighborhood of
s_{k+1}
for some
k
. So we must have that
\lVert s_{k+1}^{1/m}\,(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}\leq\lVert s_{k+1}^{-1/n}\,\vec{q}_{k}\lVert_{n}
, which cannot hold since
\lVert\vec{q}_{k}\lVert_{n}<\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}
and the way we chose the sequence
(s_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
.

So
(s_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is the sequence of local maxima of
g(t)
, and for all
t\geq s_{1}
we have
\lambda_{\Theta,t{-1}}\leq g(t)
.

In particular,

\begin{split}\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\Theta,t^{-1}}(t)^{m+n}&\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}g(t)^{m+n}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}g(s_{k+1})^{m+n}\\
&=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\,\big(\lVert s_{k+1}^{-1/n}\,\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\big)^{n}\,\big(\lVert s_{k+1}^{1/m}\,(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}\big)^{m}\\
&=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\,\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}^{n}\,\,\lVert(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}^{m}\,=0\end{split}

where the last equality follows from (
19
).

So we get in total
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\Theta,t^{-1}}(t)=0
, contradicting our main assumption on
\Theta
.

This finishes the proof of Lemma 1. ∎

Proof of Lemma 2.
In case
\Theta\vec{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
for some
\vec{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}
we have that both quantities are equal to zero (in this case,
\Theta\in X_{j}
for some
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
(X_{j})
as in the proof of Theorem 2 in section 5, where we explain why
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\,\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}(t)=0
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\Theta\in X_{j}
). So assume
\Theta\vec{q}\notin\mathbb{Z}^{m}
for all non-zero
\vec{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Assume that
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}(t)^{1+\gamma n/m}=\,L
.

Let
(t_{k},\vec{q}_{k},\vec{p}_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}
which realizes
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}
as in (
16
). So if
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
we have that

\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}(t)=\max\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}t^{-1/n}\,\left\lVert\vec{q}_{k}\right\rVert_{n}\\
t^{\,\gamma/m}\,\left\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k}\,\right\rVert_{m}\end{array}.

Again by Lemmas
3
and
4
we have that
(t_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is the sequence of local maxima of
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}
and for all
k\geq 0
we have that

\begin{split}\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}(t_{k+1})=\lVert t_{k+1}^{\,\,\gamma/m}\,(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}=\lVert t_{k+1}^{-1/n}\,\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}.\end{split}

So in particular we have that

\begin{split}t_{k+1}=\big(\,\lVert\,\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\,\,\lVert\,(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}^{-1}\big)^{nm/(\gamma n+m)},\end{split}

and we have that

\begin{split}\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}(t)^{2}&=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}(t_{k+1})^{2}\\
&=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,t_{k+1}^{{}^{\,\gamma/m-1/n}}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\,\,\lVert(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}\\
&=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,t_{k+1}^{{}^{\,(\gamma n-m)/nm}}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\,\,\lVert(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}\\
&=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\big(\,\lVert\,\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\,\lVert(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}^{-1}\big)^{(\gamma n-m)/(\gamma n+m)}\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}\,\,\lVert(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}\\
&=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}^{\,2\gamma n/(m+\gamma n)}\,\lVert(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}^{\,2m/(m+\gamma n)}\end{split}

(20)

and as we assume
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}(t)^{1+\gamma n/m}=\,L
we have in total that

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}^{\gamma n/m}\,\lVert(\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}_{k})\lVert_{m}=L.

Now define a function
f:[\left\lVert\vec{q}_{0}\right\rVert,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
by

f(t):=t^{\gamma n/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}\,\lVert_{m}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\

for
t\in[\,\left\lVert\vec{q}_{k}\right\rVert,\left\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\right\rVert\,)
.

So in particular we have

\begin{split}\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{\gamma n/m}(t)\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}f(t)=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\vec{q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}^{\gamma n/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{q}_{k}-\vec{p}\,\lVert_{m}=L.\end{split}

Now assume by contradiction that
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{\gamma n/m}(t)<L
,  and let
(\vec{Q}_{k},\vec{P}_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{m}
which realizes
\chi_{\gamma n/m}(\Theta,t)
. I.e. if
t\in[\lVert\vec{Q}_{k}\lVert_{n},\lVert\vec{Q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n})
then we have that

\chi_{\gamma n/m}(\Theta,t)=t^{\gamma n/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{Q}_{k}-\vec{P}_{k}\lVert_{m},

and by our assumption we have that

\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\chi_{\gamma n/m}(\Theta,t)=\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\vec{Q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}^{\gamma n/m}\,\,\lVert\Theta\vec{Q}_{k}-\vec{P}_{k}\lVert_{m}<L.

(21)

Now repeat the process as in the proof of Lemma
1
.

Let
(s_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be the sequence which satisfies that for all
k\geq 0
we have

\lVert s_{k+1}^{-1/n}\,\vec{Q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}=\lVert s_{k+1}^{\gamma/m}\,(\Theta\vec{Q}_{k}-\vec{P}_{k})\lVert_{m}.

So as we have that
\Theta\vec{q}\notin\mathbb{Z}^{m}
for all non-zero
\vec{q}
, we have that
s_{1}<s_{2}<...<s_{k}<...
and that
s_{k}\longrightarrow\infty
.

Now define a function
g:[s_{1},\infty)\longrightarrow(0,\infty)
such that for
t\in[s_{k},s_{k+1})
we set

g(t):=\max\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}t^{-1/n}\,\lVert\vec{Q}_{k}\lVert_{n}\\
t^{\gamma/m}\,\lVert\Theta\vec{Q}_{k}-\vec{P}_{k}\,\lVert_{m}\end{array}

By the same arguing as in Lemma
1
,
(s_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is the sequence of local maxima of
g(t)
, and for all
t\geq s_{1}
we have that
\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}\leq g(t)
.

In particular, by the same calculations as in (
20
) we have that

\begin{split}L^{2m/(m+\gamma n)}&=\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\Theta,\psi_{\gamma}}(t)^{2}\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}g(t)^{2}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}g(s_{k+1})^{2}\\
&=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\vec{Q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}^{(2\gamma n)/(m+\gamma n)}\,\lVert(\Theta\vec{Q}_{k}-\vec{P}_{k})\lVert_{m}^{(2m)/(m+\gamma n)}.\end{split}

So in total we have

L^{m}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\lVert\vec{Q}_{k+1}\lVert_{n}^{\gamma n}\,\lVert(\Theta\vec{Q}_{k}-\vec{P}_{k})\lVert_{m}^{m}

contradicting formula (
21
).

This finishes the proof of Lemma 2. ∎

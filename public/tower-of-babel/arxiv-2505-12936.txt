Abstract
. This paper explores the existence of solutions to a class of nonlinear elliptic equations involving a mixed local-nonlocal operator of the form − Δ 𝔹 N + ( − Δ 𝔹 N ) s -\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}+(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{s} , with 0 < s < 1 0<s<1 , set in the hyperbolic space. By employing variational methods, we address both subcritical and critical nonlinearities, establishing the existence of weak solutions under appropriate conditions. We also compare the best Sobolev constants for the mixed and local operators on 𝔹 N \mathbb{B}^{N} with the Euclidean Sobolev constant. 2020 MSC: 35B09, 35B38, 35J20, 35J61, 35R01

Introduction

In this work, we study the existence of solutions to a class of nonlinear elliptic problems involving mixed-order local and nonlocal operator on the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{B}^{N}
. Specifically, we consider the equation

(
P_{\lambda,s}
)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u+(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{s}u-\lambda u=|u|^{p-1}u,&u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N}),\end{cases}

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator and
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{s}
is the fractional Laplacian of order
0<s<1
, both defined on the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{B}^{N}
. The parameter
\lambda<\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}
, the bottom of the
L^{2}
-spectrum of
-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}
, and the nonlinearity exponent satisfies
1<p<2^{*}-1=\frac{N+2}{N-2}
for
N\geq 3
. The natural variational setting for this problem is the Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
(refer to Section
2
).

In addition to this subcritical setting, we also consider a problem involving critical nonlinearity. Specifically, we study a perturbed critical exponent problem of the form

(
\tilde{P}_{\lambda,s}
)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u+(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{s}u-\lambda u=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u+|u|^{p-1}u,&u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N}),\end{cases}

where the term
|u|^{p-1}u
acts as a lower-order perturbation of the critical power nonlinearity
|u|^{2^{*}-2}u
. Our interest lies in analyzing the existence of solutions in the presence of the geometric and analytic complexities introduced by nonlocal structure in the hyperbolic space.

Semilinear elliptic equations of the type involving local operators such as the Laplacian have been extensively studied in the Euclidean setting. Foundational results concerning the existence, regularity, and qualitative properties of solutions to such problems trace back to classical works such as those by Brezis and Nirenberg, and have been developed further in various contexts (see, e.g.,
[
16
,
54
,
40
]
). Parallel to these developments, there has been a growing interest in nonlocal problems governed by the fractional Laplacian, which naturally arises in models related to reaction-diffusion problems, quantum mechanics, image processing, and mathematical finance. Such equations are governed by integro-differential operators that reflect long-range interactions and lack the strong locality of classical Laplacian-type operators, leading to new analytical challenges and rich mathematical phenomena (see
[
46
,
52
,
49
,
25
,
27
,
53
,
19
]
).

More recently, a growing body of research has focused on elliptic equations that combine local and nonlocal effects-so-called mixed-order or mixed-local-nonlocal problems, in relation to the possible existence of positive solutions for critical problems and in connection with possible optimizers of suitable Sobolev inequalities. For example, in
[
12
]
, the authors investigate the Brezis–Nirenberg-type problem for a mixed local–nonlocal operator of the form

-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u-\lambda u=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,\ u=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a smooth bounded domain,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is a parameter, and
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
is the critical Sobolev exponent. Their study includes a detailed investigation of the associated Sobolev inequality, showing that the optimal constant is never attained. Furthermore, they establish existence and nonexistence results for the subcritical perturbation of the problem. (cf.
[
44
,
15
,
13
,
11
,
33
]
).

There has also been a substantial shift toward studying elliptic equations on non-Euclidean spaces, particularly due to their applicability in modeling various physical and geometric phenomena. A central motivation in this direction is to understand how the curvature of the underlying space, especially the negative curvature of hyperbolic geometry, affects the existence, uniqueness, and qualitative behavior of solutions. Seminal contributions in this area include the work of Sandeep and Mancini
[
45
,
22
]
, who investigated semilinear equations involving the Laplace–Beltrami operator on the hyperbolic space.
In
[
45
]
, Sandeep and Mancini proved the existence and uniqueness (up to isometries) of finite-energy positive solutions to the homogeneous elliptic equation

(1.1)

-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u\,-\,\lambda u\,=\,u^{p},\quad u\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}\right),

where
\lambda\leq\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4},

1<p\leq\frac{N+2}{N-2}
if
N\geq 3;

1<p<\infty
if
N=2.
They established that, in the subcritical case and for
p>1
, equation (
1.1
) admits a positive solution if and only if
\lambda<\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}
, and that such solutions are unique modulo hyperbolic isometries except possibly when
N=2
and
\lambda>\frac{2(p+1)}{(p+3)^{2}}
.

Following their work, subsequent studies have deepened the analysis of (
1.1
) in various directions. For instance,
[
10
,
31
,
32
]
addressed the existence of sign-changing solutions, compactness properties, and non-degeneracy, while
[
6
,
14
]
explored infinite-energy solutions and their asymptotic profiles. Building upon this framework, the authors of this article, in collaboration with Ganguly, Bhakta, and Sahoo, have analyzed non-radial perturbations of (
1.1
) and contributed to the understanding of Palais-Smale decomposition, multiplicity, and asymptotic behavior of solutions (see
[
28
,
30
,
7
,
29
,
8
]
).
Moreover, Sandeep and Mancini showed that equation (
1.1
) arises as the Euler–Lagrange equation of the Hardy–Sobolev–Maz’ya-type inequality in the hyperbolic space, which has led to extensive work on related inequalities and higher-order conformally covariant operators, including Paneitz-type operators studied in the hyperbolic setting (see
[
42
,
43
,
35

A natural extension of the above discussion involves extending the analysis to nonlocal problems in the hyperbolic space. The fractional Laplacian
\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{s}
, for
0<s<1
, on the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{B}^{N}
can be defined using the standard functional calculus. However, in
[
4
]
, the authors showed that
\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{s}
can be interpreted as the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator associated with a degenerate elliptic extension problem, analogous to the Caffarelli–Silvestre extension for the Euclidean setting
[
20
]
, and its generalization to manifolds
[
23
]
. In addition, they provided an explicit integral representation of the fractional Laplacian in the hyperbolic space as a principal value (P.V.) singular integral, expressed as a convolution with a well-behaved kernel
\mathcal{K}_{s}
(up to a normalizing constant):

\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{s}u(x)=\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(u(x)-u(\xi))\;\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,\xi))\;\mathrm{d}\xi,

where the kernel
\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,\xi))
is comparable to
\frac{1}{d(x,\xi)^{N+2s}}
as
d(x,\xi)\to 0
, and exhibits exponential decay as
d(x,\xi)\to\infty
(see
[
4
, Theorems 2.4 and 2.5]
; also see Section
2
). Notably, as
d(x,\xi)\to 0
, the structure of this kernel is reminiscent of the Euclidean singular kernel
\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}
that appears in the classical definition of the fractional Laplacian.

Further refinements appear in
[
38
]
, where the authors, focusing on the case
N=3
, determined the explicit value of the normalizing constant, which proves essential in certain applications. This representation was later extended to the fractional
p
-Laplacian in the hyperbolic space in
[
39
, Theorem 1.2]
, where the authors incorporated the normalizing constant into the generalized formulation. A broader perspective on fractional Laplacians on manifolds is given in
[
21
]
, where several equivalent definitions are established and shown to coincide (up to multiplicative constants) on closed Riemannian manifolds (i.e., compact without boundary). These approaches often rely on the Fourier transform on hyperbolic space, which serves as a key analytical tool in
[
4
,
38
]
. However, in nonlinear settings
[
39
]
where the Fourier transform is unavailable, alternative techniques such as the heat kernel associated with the Laplace operator on
\mathbb{B}^{N}
are employed.

These foundational results provide a rigorous framework for analyzing semilinear problems involving the fractional Laplacian on hyperbolic space. In particular, when the nonlinearity is derived from a double-well potential, one can establish the existence and uniqueness of layer solutions, along with certain symmetry properties, as shown in
[
34
]
. Additionally, in
[
36
]
, the authors addressed fractional
p
-Laplacian equations with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on compact Riemannian manifolds.

Motivated by the above developments, we turn our attention to nonlinear elliptic problems involving mixed-order local and nonlocal operators in the hyperbolic space. Our broader objective is to study the Brezis–Nirenberg problem involving a mixed local-nonlocal operator in the hyperbolic space. However, we observe a notable gap in the literature: while significant progress has been made for such problems in the Euclidean setting, the study of elliptic equations involving the fractional Laplacian in hyperbolic space remains largely unexplored. Main Novelty and Strategy of the Proofs.
Our analysis is grounded in the variational approach within the natural functional space
H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})

H^{s}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
(see Lemma
2.1

[

H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\hookrightarrow H^{s}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
in Lemma

The noncompactness issues inherent in the hyperbolic setting are managed through carefully crafted variational techniques.
To overcome the loss of compactness in the subcritical regime, we restrict our
attention to radially symmetric functions and work within the subspace

H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, where compactness is recovered (see Lemma

The key tool for reducing general functions to radial ones is Schwarz symmetrization,
which preserves or improves the relevant functional inequalities. For problem
(

~
P

λ
,

failure of the Palais-Smale condition for the associated energy functional
J

this is precisely where the condition (
1.2

into play. The threshold condition (
1.2

technical artifact but the exact analytic manifestation of the loss of compactness in
the critical regime, playing here the role that the strict sub-threshold estimate
plays in the classical Brezis-Nirenberg analysis
[

and in its fractional

[

A compactness condition of the type (

found in the literature on critically perturbed problems, such

[

severe than in the Euclidean setting. On

the critical Sobolev quotient is invariant under the dilations

\mathbb{B}^{N}

groups coincide and the Möbius isometries translate mass rather than concentrate it.

The threshold condition (

Comparison of the best Sobolev constants.
We further remark that the threshold in (
1.2
) involves the
mixed best Sobolev constant
S_{\lambda,s}
of (

Sobolev constant

(defined in (

-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s}
the best constant equals

and is never attained, since under the

[

On the hyperbolic space the situation is more delicate, and its resolution is one of the
results of this paper (Proposition
2.1

(1.4)

S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}:=\inf_{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\bigl(|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u|^{2}-\lambda u^{2}\bigr)\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}}{\|u\|_{2^{\ast}}^{2}}

when

\frac{N(N-2)}{4}<\lambda<\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}
(refer to
[

The strict comparison

When

[

Remark 1.2
.

We emphasize that Proposition
2.1
locates
S_{\lambda,s}
strictly above

S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
but leaves open its position relative to

intend to pursue these questions in our future

Remark 1.3

(Non-attainment)
.

Whenever
S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}=S
, the mixed constant satisfies
S_{\lambda,s}=S
by the chain (
1.5
), and is not achieved. Suppose that it were
attained by some

, we have

forcing

nonzero function in

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will introduce some of the notations and definitions used in this

related to the Sobolev space on the hyperbolic space.

We will denote by

the disc model of the hyperbolic space, i.e., the Euclidean unit ball

\displaystyle{\rm d}s^{2}=\left(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\right)^{2}\,{\rm d}x^{2},

\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=\big(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\big)^{N}{\rm d}x,
where
{\rm d}x
denotes the Lebesgue
measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}
denote gradient
vector field and Laplace-Beltrami operator, respectively, which take the form

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=\left(\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}\nabla,\quad\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=\left(\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}\Delta+(N-2)\left(\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{2}\right)x\cdot\nabla,

where
\nabla,\Delta
are the standard Euclidean gradient vector field and Laplace operator, respectively, and ’
\cdot
’ denotes the

\mathbb{R}^{N}.

2.1.
Möbius Transformations and Convolution

[
41
]

The Möbius transformations
T_{a}
for each
a\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
are defined as follows:

T_{a}(x)=\frac{|x-a|^{2}a-\left(1-|a|^{2}\right)(x-a)}{1-2x\cdot a+|x|^{2}|a|^{2}}

where
x\cdot a=x_{1}a_{1}+x_{2}a_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}a_{n}
represents the scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The measure on
\mathbb{B}^{N}
is known to be invariant under Möbius transformations.

2.2.
Hyperbolic Distance on
\mathbb{B}^{N}

The distance between
x
and
y
in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
can be defined as follows utilizing the Möbius transformations:

\rho(x,y)=\rho\left(T_{x}(y)\right)=\rho\left(T_{y}(x)\right)=\log\frac{1+\left|T_{y}(x)\right|}{1-\left|T_{y}(x)\right|}

where
\rho(x)=d(x,0)=\log\frac{1+|x|}{1-|x|}
is the geodesic distance from the origin.

As a result, a subset of
\mathbb{B}^{N}
is a hyperbolic sphere in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
if and only if it is a Euclidean sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and contained in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
, possibly
with a different centre and different radius, which can be explicitly computed from the formula of
d(x,y)

[
48
]
. Geodesic balls in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
of radius
r
centred at
x\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
will be denoted by

B(x,r):=\{\,y\in\mathbb{B}^{N}:d(x,y)<r\,\},

while Euclidean balls in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
of radius
r
centred at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
will be denoted by

B_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(x,r):=\{\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x-y|<r\,\}.

This section introduces the mathematical tools and definitions necessary for our analysis.

2.3.
Helgason Fourier Transform on the Hyperbolic Space

We give a brief overview of the Helgason Fourier transform, which serves as a foundation for defining the fractional Laplacian in the hyperbolic space. For comprehensive treatments and further references, see
[
4
,
41
]
.

Analogous to the Euclidean context, the Fourier transform can be defined as follows,

\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f(x)h_{\beta,\theta}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}},

for
\beta\in\mathbb{R},\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, where
h_{\beta,\theta}
are the generalized eigenfunctions of the Laplace Beltrami operator that satisfy

\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}h_{\beta,\theta}=-\left(\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\right)h_{\beta,\theta}.

Furthermore, given
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, the following inversion formula is valid:

f(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\bar{h}_{\beta,\theta}(x)\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)\frac{\mathrm{d}\beta\mathrm{d}\theta}{|c(\beta)|^{2}},

where
c(\beta)
represents the Harish-Chandra coefficient:

\frac{1}{|c(\beta)|^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)\right|^{2}}{|\Gamma(N-1)|^{2}}\frac{\left\lvert\,\Gamma\left(i\beta+\left.\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)\right|^{2}\right.\right.}{|\Gamma(i\beta)|^{2}}.

Moreover, the following Plancherel formula holds

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|f(x)|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\beta\mathrm{d}\theta}{|c(\beta)|^{2}}.

It is straightforward to verify through integration by parts for compactly supported functions, and thus for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
that

\displaystyle\widehat{\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f}(\beta,\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f(x)h_{\beta,\theta}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f(x)\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}h_{\beta,\theta}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)

\displaystyle=-\left(\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\right)\hat{f}(\beta,\theta).

Given the theory discussed above, the fractional Laplacian on the hyperbolic space can be defined as
{\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)}^{s}f
, which is the operator satisfying

(2.2)

(-\widehat{\left.\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{s}}f=\left(\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\right)^{s}\hat{f},\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

For
N\geq 2
and
s\in(0,1)
, the fractional Laplacian admits the following pointwise representation:

(2.3)

\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{s}u(x)=\text{ P.V. }\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(u(x)-u(\xi))\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,\xi))\mathrm{d}\xi

where
\rho:=d(x,\xi)
denotes the hyperbolic distance between the points
x
and
\xi
, and the kernel
\mathcal{K}_{s}(\rho)
is given explicitly as follows (see
[
39
, Theorem 1.2]
,
[
4
]
):

when
N\geq 3
is odd

\mathcal{K}_{s}(\rho)=C(N,s)\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\left(\rho^{-\frac{1+2s}{2}}K_{\frac{1+2s}{2}}\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\rho\right)\right),

and when
N\geq 2
is even

\mathcal{K}_{s}(\rho)=C(N,s)\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\left(r^{-\frac{1+2s}{2}}K_{\frac{1+2s}{2}}\left(\frac{N-1}{2}r\right)\right)\mathrm{d}r,

where
K_{\nu}
denotes the modified Bessel function of the second kind, and the constant
C(N,s)
is given by

C(N,s)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\;2^{2s}\;\Gamma\left(\frac{N+2s}{2}\right)}{2\Gamma(\frac{3}{2})\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}|\Gamma(-s)|}\frac{1}{2^{\frac{N-2+2s}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{N+2s}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{\frac{1+2s}{2}}.

2.4.
A sharp Poincaré-Sobolev inequality

(see
[
45
]
)

Sobolev Space:
We will denote by
{H^{1}}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
the Sobolev space on the disc
model of the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{B}^{N}
, equipped with norm
\|u\|=\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where

For

S_{\lambda,p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u|^{p+1}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+1}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left[|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u|^{2}-\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}u^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}},

for every

If

A crucial point to note is that the bottom of the spectrum of

on

is

\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}=\inf_{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}}{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}}.

Remark 2.1
.

As a result of (

then

For

Note that

S_{\lambda,p}\left(\int|u|^{p+1}\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+1}}\leq\|u\|^{2}_{\lambda},\quad p\in\left(1,\frac{N+2}{N-2}\right]\quad\text{for}\quad u\in\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{N}).

Additionally, throughout this article,

2.5.
Fractional Sobolev Spaces

There exist multiple definitions of Sobolev spaces on the hyperbolic space, depending on the underlying analytical framework. In this work, we shall employ the following definition for the fractional Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
with
0<s<1
:

H^{s}(\mathbb{B}^{N})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\mid\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}+\left[f\right]_{s}^{2}<\infty\right\}.

where the seminorm
[f]_{s}
is defined by

[f]_{s}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The full norm is then defined as

\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|f(x)|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)+[f]_{s}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This space serves as an intermediary Banach space between
L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
and
H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, endowed with the natural norm defined above. In what follows, we establish the continuous embedding stated in (
2.7
). As a key step towards this, we first prove an important auxiliary result concerning the behavior of the kernel
\mathcal{K}_{s}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0<s<1
. Then the kernel
\mathcal{K}_{s}
, defined in (
2.3
), is positive and strictly decreasing with respect to the geodesic distance
\rho
.

Proof.

The positivity of the kernel
\mathcal{K}_{s}
follows from
[
39
, Corollary 5.2]
. For notational convenience, define

\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}(\rho):=\rho^{-\nu}K_{\nu}(a\rho),

where
K_{\nu}
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind, with parameters
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
and
a>0
.

Case 1:
N\geq 3
odd.
In this case, the kernel admits the explicit formula

\mathcal{K}_{s}(\rho)=C(N,s)\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}K_{\frac{1+2s}{2}}\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\rho\right),

for a constant
C(N,s)>0
defined in (
2.3
). Differentiating this expression, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\rho}\mathcal{K}_{s}(\rho)

\displaystyle=C(N,s)\frac{d}{d\rho}\left(\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+2s}{2},\frac{N-1}{2}}(\rho)\right)

\displaystyle=C(N,s)(-\sinh\rho)\frac{-1}{\sinh\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left[-\frac{1}{\sinh\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\right]^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+2s}{2},\frac{N-1}{2}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=C(N,s)(-\sinh\rho)\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{\frac{N+1}{2}}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+2s}{2},\frac{N-1}{2}}(\rho)<0,

where the inequality in the final step follows from
[
39
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Case 2:
N\geq 2
even.
In this case, the kernel is represented by the integral

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{s}(\rho)

\displaystyle=C(N,s)\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\left(r^{-\frac{1+2s}{2}}K_{\frac{1+2s}{2}}\left(\frac{N-1}{2}r\right)\right)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=C(N,s)\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+2s}{2},\frac{N-1}{2}}(r)\mathrm{d}r

Now define, for
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

F_{m}(r):=\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}(r),

where
\nu>1/2
and
a\geq 1/2
. With this notation, the kernel becomes

\mathcal{K}_{s}(\rho)=\frac{C(N,s)}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}F_{\frac{N}{2}}(r)\,dr,

for
\nu=\frac{1+2s}{2}
and
a=\frac{N-1}{2}
.
According to
[
39
, Lemma 3.1]
,
F_{m}
is integrable on
(\rho,\infty)
and satisfies the recursive identity

\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)\int_{\rho}^{\infty}F_{m}(r)\,dr=\int_{\rho}^{\infty}F_{m+1}(r)\,dr.

Using this identity, we compute

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\rho}\mathcal{K}_{s}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{C(N,s)}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{d}{d\rho}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}F_{\frac{N}{2}}(r)\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{C(N,s)}{\sqrt{\pi}}(-\sinh\rho)\int_{\rho}^{\infty}F_{\frac{N}{2}+1}(r)\,dr<0.

The inequality again follows from
[
39
, Theorem 5.1]
, which ensures that the integrand
F_{\frac{N}{2}+1}(r)
is strictly positive. This confirms that
\mathcal{K}_{s}
is strictly decreasing in
\rho
.
∎

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
0<s<1
. Then for all

,

\left[f\right]_{s}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}.

In particular, keeping in mind (
2.5
), we obtain the following continuous embedding

(2.7)

H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\hookrightarrow H^{s}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\;\;\;\text{for }0<s<1.

Proof.

The kernel
\mathcal{K}_{s}
is positive, and the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{K}_{s}
, as described in
[

[
4
]

(2.8)

\mathcal{K}_{s}(\rho)\lesssim\rho^{-n-2s}\;\;\text{for}\;\;\rho<l,

(2.9)

\mathcal{K}_{s}(\rho)\lesssim\rho^{-1-s}e^{-(n-1)\rho}\;\;\text{ for }\rho>m.

Now consider

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{B(y,l)}}_{I_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{B(y,m)^{\complement}}}_{I_{2}}+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{B(y,m)\setminus B(y,l)}}_{I_{3}}.

First, consider the integral
I_{1}
, where for each pair
x,y\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
, we denote by
\gamma_{x,y}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
the minimizing geodesic joining
x
to

. Then, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{B(y,l)}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{B(y,l)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f(\gamma_{x,y}(t))|^{2}}{(d(x,y))^{N+2s-2}}dt\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq\;

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{B(y,l)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f(\gamma_{x,y}(t))|^{2}}{(d(x,y))^{N+2s-2}}dt\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

[
50

51

24

\Phi_{x,t}:y\mapsto\gamma_{x,y}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{l}r^{-2s+1}dr+O\left(\int_{0}^{l}r^{-2s+3}dr\right)=\frac{l^{-2s+2}}{-2s+2}+O(l^{-2s+4})\text{ for $s\in(0,1)$}.

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{B(y,m)^{\complement}}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(f(y))^{2}\int_{B(y,m)^{\complement}}\frac{1}{d(x,y)^{1+s}e^{(N-1)d(x,y)}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(f(x))^{2}\int_{B(x,m)^{\complement}}\frac{1}{d(x,y)^{1+s}e^{(N-1)d(x,y)}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}\int_{B(x,m)^{\complement}}\frac{1}{d(x,y)^{1+s}e^{(N-1)d(x,y)}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle=C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}\int_{B(0,m)^{\complement}}\frac{1}{d(z,0)^{1+s}e^{(N-1)d(z,0)}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(z)

\displaystyle=C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int_{m}^{\infty}\frac{(\sinh r)^{N-1}}{r^{1+s}e^{(N-1)r}}\mathrm{~d}r\mathrm{~d}\sigma.

Now, we use the approximation
\sinh r\thicksim e^{r}\;\text{for large }r

m
large enough

\displaystyle\int_{m}^{\infty}\frac{(\sinh r)^{N-1}}{r^{1+s}e^{(N-1)r}}\mathrm{~d}r\thicksim\int_{m}^{\infty}r^{-1-s}dr=\frac{m^{-s}}{s}\quad\text{for }s>0.

Thus we get

I_{2}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}.

Finally, we estimate
I_{3}
as follows

\displaystyle I_{3}:=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{B(y,m)\setminus B(y,l)}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}\int_{B(0,m)\setminus B(0,l)}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(z,0))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(z).

For any
z\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
such that
d(z,0)>l
, we have

d(z,0)>l>\tilde{l}=d(q,0)
for some
\tilde{l}>0,q\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
. Thus using Lemma
2.1
, we get

\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(z,0))\leq\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(q,0))\;\;\forall z\in\mathbb{B}^{N}\text{ such that }d(z,0)>l>d(q,0).

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(q,0))C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}\int_{B(0,m)\setminus B(0,l)}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(z)\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}.

Therefore combinig all the above estimates of
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)\lesssim\left(\|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}\right).

Hence the lemma follows.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Moreover, if we define the fractional Laplacian using the Fourier transform (
2.2
), the above embedding naturally follows from the Plancherel formula (
2.1
) in a straightforward way as follows.

The fractional Sobolev space can be defined as

\widehat{H}^{s}(\mathbb{B}^{N})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\mid\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}+\|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{s/2}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}<\infty\right\},

where
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{s/2}
is understood in the sense of (
2.2
).
Then for
N\geq 3
,
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{s/2}f|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|(\widehat{-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{s/2}f}(\beta,\theta)|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\beta\mathrm{d}\theta}{|c(\beta)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\left(\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\right)^{s}|\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\beta\mathrm{d}\theta}{|c(\beta)|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\left(\beta^{2}+\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\right)|\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\beta\mathrm{d}\theta}{|c(\beta)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\hat{f}(\beta,\theta)\widehat{(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})\bar{f}}(\beta,\theta)\frac{\mathrm{d}\beta\mathrm{d}\theta}{|c(\beta)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f(x)(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\bar{f}(x))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}f|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x).

Having established the continuous embedding, the associated best constant

S_{\lambda,s}
of (
1.3
) is well defined and positive.
Recall the sharp Sobolev inequality on

(

,

]

for every

where
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the completion of

The above infimum is attained by the Aubin-Talenti family

,

It is natural to ask how

compares with the sharp Euclidean Sobolev constant

.

Proposition 2.1

(Comparison of the mixed, local, and Euclidean best constants)
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
s\in(0,1)
, and
\lambda<\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}
. Then

(i)

S_{\lambda,s}\leq S.

(ii)

If

\lambda\leq\dfrac{N(N-2)}{4}
, then

S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}=S_{\lambda,s}=S,

where
S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is defined in (
1.4
).

(iii)

If
N\geq 4
and

\dfrac{N(N-2)}{4}<\lambda<\dfrac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}
, then

S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}<S_{\lambda,s}.

Proof.

We prove the three parts in turn. Part (i) is obtained by testing the mixed
quotient with a concentrating Aubin-Talenti bubble; part (ii) follows by combining (i)
with a conformal identity for the local energy; and part (iii) rests on the
Palais-Smale decomposition result of
[
10
]
.

Proof of Part (i):
S_{\lambda,s}\leq S
.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
be radial with
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
,
\varphi\equiv 1
on

\{|x|\leq r/2\}
and
\supp\varphi\subset K:=\{|x|\leq r\}
for some
0<r<1
. Fix

r_{1}\in(r,1)
and set
K_{1}:=\{|x|\leq r_{1}\}
, so that
K\subset\subset K_{1}\subset\subset\mathbb{B}^{N}
.
For
\varepsilon>0
set

(2.12)

w_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}U(x/\varepsilon),\qquad v_{\varepsilon}=\varphi\,w_{\varepsilon},\qquad u_{\varepsilon}=\Bigl(\tfrac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\Bigr)^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}v_{\varepsilon}.

Step 1: Estimate of the nonlocal term.
Throughout,

\Theta(x):=\bigl(\tfrac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\bigr)^{-(N-2)/2}
for the conformal factor, so that

u_{\varepsilon}=\Theta\,v_{\varepsilon}
and
\supp u_{\varepsilon}\subset K
. On the compact set
K
,
\Theta
is smooth and satisfies

(2.13)

C(N,r_{1})\leq\Theta(x)\leq\Theta_{\max},\qquad|\Theta(x)-\Theta(y)|\leq L_{\Theta}|x-y|\quad\text{for }x,y\in K_{1},

with
C(N,r_{1})>0
, and
L_{\Theta}=L_{\Theta}(N,r_{1}):=\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}(K_{1})}<\infty
.

Since
u_{\varepsilon}=\Theta\,v_{\varepsilon}
is smooth with
\supp u_{\varepsilon}\subset K\subset\subset\mathbb{B}^{N}
,
therefore
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
by

[
45
, Lemma 2.3]
. In particular,
u_{\varepsilon}
is an admissible test function for
S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
and
S_{\lambda,s}
.

Split

[u_{\varepsilon}]_{s}^{2}=\underbrace{\iint_{K_{1}\times K_{1}}}_{\mathrm{(I)}}+\underbrace{2\iint_{K_{1}\times K_{1}^{c}}}_{\mathrm{(II)}}+\underbrace{\iint_{K_{1}^{c}\times K_{1}^{c}}}_{\mathrm{(III)\equiv 0}}\bigl(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\bigr)^{2}\,\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\,\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y).

We begin by estimating
\mathrm{(II)}
. Since
u_{\varepsilon}
vanishes outside
K

\mathrm{(II)}=2\int_{K}u_{\varepsilon}(x)^{2}\Bigl(\int_{K_{1}^{c}}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)\Bigr)\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x).

We now bound the inner integral uniformly in
x\in\supp u_{\varepsilon}
. Using

d(0,z)=\log\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}=2\operatorname{artanh}|z|
,

K=\{|x|\leq r\}=B(0,R),\qquad K_{1}=\{|x|\leq r_{1}\}=B(0,R_{1}),

where
R=2\operatorname{artanh}(r),\ \ R_{1}=2\operatorname{artanh}(r_{1})
.
Hence, for
x\in\supp u_{\varepsilon}\subset K
and
y\in K_{1}^{c}
, the triangle inequality gives

d(x,y)\geq d(0,y)-d(0,x)>R_{1}-R=2\bigl(\operatorname{artanh}(r_{1})-\operatorname{artanh}(r)\bigr)=:d_{0}>0.

Proceeding as in estimates
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
in the proof of Theorem
2.7
,

\int_{K_{1}^{c}}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)\leq\int_{\{y\,:\,d(x,y)\geq d_{0}\}}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)\leq C,

where
C=C(N,s,r,r_{1})
is independent of
x
. Therefore, since
\Theta
is bounded on

K
and
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
,

(2.14)

\mathrm{(II)}\leq C\int_{K}v_{\varepsilon}(x)^{2}\,dx\leq C(N,s,r,r_{1})\,\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}.

In Region
\mathrm{(I)}
, both
x,y\in K_{1}
. Using (
2.13
), we write

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)

\displaystyle=\Theta(x)\bigl(v_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(y)\bigr)+v_{\varepsilon}(y)\bigl(\Theta(x)-\Theta(y)\bigr).

\displaystyle\implies\bigl(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(N,r_{1})\left[\bigl(v_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(y)\bigr)^{2}+\,v_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,|x-y|^{2}\right].

Since
\supp v_{\varepsilon}\subset K
, and bounding each volume factor by
\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\leq C(N,r_{1})\,dx
on
K_{1}
,

(2.15)

\mathrm{(I)}\leq C\!\iint_{K_{1}\times K_{1}}\!\!\bigl(v_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(y)\bigr)^{2}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,dx\,dy+C\!\iint_{K_{1}\times K}\!\!v_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}|x-y|^{2}\,\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,dx\,dy.

On
K_{1}
the geodesic distance is comparable to the Euclidean one:

C^{-1}|x-y|\leq d(x,y)\leq C|x-y|
with
C=C(r_{1})
. Indeed, in the ball model,

\cosh d(x,y)=1+t,\qquad t:=\frac{2|x-y|^{2}}{(1-|x|^{2})(1-|y|^{2})},

and from the integral representation

\operatorname{arccosh}(1+t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d\tau}{\sqrt{\tau^{2}+2\tau}}
, together with the
elementary inequalities
\sqrt{2\tau}\leq\sqrt{\tau^{2}+2\tau}\leq\sqrt{2\tau}\,\sqrt{1+\tfrac{t}{2}}

valid for
\tau\in[0,t]
, we obtain

(2.16)

\frac{\sqrt{2t}}{\sqrt{1+t/2}}\;\leq\;\operatorname{arccosh}(1+t)\;\leq\;\sqrt{2t}.

For
x,y\in K_{1}=\{|x|\leq r_{1}\}
we have
(1-|x|^{2})(1-|y|^{2})\in\bigl[(1-r_{1}^{2})^{2},\,1\bigr]

and
|x-y|\leq 2r_{1}
, hence

(2.17)

2|x-y|^{2}\;\leq\;t\;\leq\;\frac{2|x-y|^{2}}{(1-r_{1}^{2})^{2}},\qquad 1+\frac{t}{2}\;\leq\;1+\frac{4r_{1}^{2}}{(1-r_{1}^{2})^{2}}=\Bigl(\frac{1+r_{1}^{2}}{1-r_{1}^{2}}\Bigr)^{2}.

Substituting the upper bound for
t
into the right-hand inequality of (
2.16
)
gives

d(x,y)\leq\sqrt{2t}\leq\frac{2}{1-r_{1}^{2}}\,|x-y|,

while the lower bound for
t
and the last estimate in (
2.17
) give, via the
left-hand inequality of (
2.16
),

d(x,y)\geq\frac{\sqrt{2t}}{\sqrt{1+t/2}}\geq\frac{2|x-y|}{\bigl(1+r_{1}^{2}\bigr)/(1-r_{1}^{2})}=\frac{2(1-r_{1}^{2})}{1+r_{1}^{2}}\,|x-y|.

Consequently,

(2.18)

\frac{2(1-r_{1}^{2})}{1+r_{1}^{2}}\,|x-y|\;\leq\;d(x,y)\;\leq\;\frac{2}{1-r_{1}^{2}}\,|x-y|,\qquad x,y\in K_{1}.

Returning to the first integral in (
2.15
), we split, we split
K_{1}\times K_{1}
at
d(x,y)=l
. On the near
part
\{d(x,y)<l\}
, by (
2.8
) and (
2.18
),

\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\leq C\,d(x,y)^{-(N+2s)}\leq C(N,s,r_{1})\,|x-y|^{-(N+2s)},

while on the far part
\{d(x,y)\geq l\}
, and both points lie in
K_{1}
, so

d(x,y)\leq d(x,0)+d(0,y)\leq 2R_{1}
with
R_{1}=2\operatorname{artanh}(r_{1})
. Hence

ranges in the compact interval

and

\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\leq C
. Thus the first
integral in the RHS of (

) satisfies

\iint_{K_{1}\times K_{1}}(v_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(y))^{2}\,\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,dx\,dy\leq C(N,s,r_{1})\left[[v_{\varepsilon}]_{s,\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{2}+\,\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\right],

where for measurable
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
,

[u]_{s,\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{2}:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy
denotes the
Gagliardo seminorm.

For the second integral in (
2.15
), we proceed similarly. Fix
y\in K
and split the inner

x
-integral at
d(x,y)=l
. On the near part, by (
2.18
),

|x-y|^{2}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\leq C\,|x-y|^{2-(N+2s)}
, and since

|x-y|\leq C(r_{1})\,l

\int_{\{d(x,y)<l\}}|x-y|^{2}\,\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,dx\leq C(N,s,r_{1})\int_{|x-y|\leq Cl}|x-y|^{2-(N+2s)}\,dx<\infty,

since

. On the far part
\{d(x,y)\geq l\}

|x-y|^{2}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\leq C(N,s,r_{1})
, and the inner
x
-integral is bounded,
uniformly in
y
. Therefore,

\iint_{K_{1}\times K}v_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}|x-y|^{2}\,\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,dx\,dy\leq C(N,s,r_{1})\,\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}.

Combining the two estimates gives

(2.19)

\mathrm{(I)}\;\leq\;C(N,s,r_{1})\left[[v_{\varepsilon}]_{s,\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{2}+\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\right].

Step 1a: the Gagliardo energy.

We now estimate the first term in (

with

(a) Near
y
(

Definition 3.1

I(g(1))<0
.

Moreover, for any

, we have
I(g(1))<0
, which leads to the estimate

\frac{1}{2}\|g(1)\|_{\lambda}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}[g(1)]_{s}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|g(1)|^{p+1}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}<0.

Thus, we deduce that

I^{\prime}(g(1))(g(1))<\left(\frac{2}{p+1}-1\right)\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|g(1)|^{p+1}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}<0.

From this, we further obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle 0>I^{\prime}(g(1))(g(1))

\displaystyle=\|g(1)\|_{\lambda}^{2}+[g(1)]_{s}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|g(1)|^{p+1}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\displaystyle\geq\|g(1)\|_{\lambda}^{2}-\|g(1)\|_{\lambda}^{p+1}S_{\lambda,p}^{-\frac{p+1}{2}}.

Setting
\tilde{r}=\left(\frac{1}{4}S_{\lambda,p}^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
, we conclude that
\|g(1)\|_{\lambda}>\tilde{r}
. Since
g(0)=0
, there exists some
\tilde{t}_{g}\in(0,1)
such that
\|g(\tilde{t}_{g})\|_{\lambda}=\tilde{r}
.

Applying (
3.7
), we obtain

\min_{\|u\|_{\lambda}=\tilde{r}}I^{\prime}(u)(u)\geq\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{1}{4}S_{\lambda,p}^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}>0,\quad\text{for all }u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N}).

In particular,
I^{\prime}(g(\tilde{t}_{g}))(g(\tilde{t}_{g}))>0
. By combining this observation with (
3.6
), we establish our claim, which immediately yields

. This completes the proof of
(2)
.

Proof of

(3)
: Suppose that there exists a minimizer
u\in\Lambda
of
I
, subject to
\Lambda
, which is not radially symmetric up to a translation. Now, let
u^{*}
be the symmetric rearrangement of

. Then, from
[
5

, and Lemma
2.1
, one has a strict inequality

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{s}u^{*}|^{2}\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}<\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{s}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}.

As in the proof of
(1)
, there exists
t_{0}
such that
t_{0}u^{*}\in\Lambda
.
Now consider

\displaystyle I(t_{0}u^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{2}\|u^{*}\|_{\lambda}^{2}+\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{2}[u^{*}]_{s}^{2}-\frac{t_{0}^{p+1}}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u^{*}|^{p+1}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\displaystyle<\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{2}\|u\|_{\lambda}^{2}+\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{2}[u]_{s}^{2}-\frac{t_{0}^{p+1}}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u|^{p+1}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

This contradicts the definition of

.∎

4.
A critical exponent problem

The aim of this section is to establish the existence of non-trivial solutions to the problem (

~
P

λ
,
s

). To this end, we will again adapt the variational techniques to the non-local setting. Specifically, we investigate the critical points of the associated energy functional
J:H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by

(4.1)

\displaystyle J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}{\mathcal{K}_{s}}(d(x,y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u|^{p+1}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}.

Remark 4.1
.

Observe that, by the principle of symmetric criticality
[
47
]
, it is sufficient to prove the existence of a critical point of
J\text{ in }H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
to establish the existence of a solution to (

~
P

λ
,
s

).

The equation (

~
P

λ
,
s

) is the Euler–Lagrange equation corresponding to the energy functional
J
defined in (
4.1
). The functional
J
is Fréchet differentiable on the radial Sobolev space
H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, and for every
\varphi\in H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, its derivative is given by

\displaystyle\langle J^{\prime}(u),\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\varphi\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u\varphi\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))\,\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\varphi\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}-\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u|^{p-1}u\varphi\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}.

Thus, by Remark
4.1
, the critical points of
J
correspond to weak solutions of problem (

~
P

λ
,
s

). To identify such critical points, we make use of the Mountain Pass Theorem. However, the classical Mountain Pass Theorem cannot be directly applied in this context due to the lack of compactness in the embedding
H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
. This lack of compactness implies that the functional
J
does not satisfy the Palais–Smale condition globally. Nevertheless, compactness can still be recovered within a suitable range of energy levels, specifically those below a threshold determined by the best Sobolev constant
S_{\lambda,s}
associated with the mixed local–nonlocal operator defined in (
1.3
).

To circumvent the limitations imposed by the lack of compactness, we employ a generalized version of the Mountain Pass Theorem that does not require the Palais–Smale condition to hold globally (see
[
1
]
,
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
). This approach hinges on verifying that the functional
J
possesses an appropriate geometric structure-specifically, the so-called mountain pass geometry on the radial Sobolev space
H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
.

4.1.
Existence of a Solution in the Critical Case with a Lower Order Perturbation

We begin by establishing that the functional
J
indeed exhibits the mountain pass geometry required to apply the variant of the Mountain Pass Theorem stated in
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume
\lambda<\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}
and
1<p<2^{*}-1
. Then there exist positive constants
\rho>0
and
\beta>0
such that for every
u\in H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
satisfying
\|u\|_{\lambda}=\rho
, it holds that
J(u)\geq\beta
.

Proof.

Let
u\in H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
. Using (
2.6
), we estimate the functional
J
as follows:

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|u\|^{2}_{\lambda}+[u]_{s}^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u(x)|^{2^{*}}\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u(x)|^{p+1}\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\lambda}^{2}-\frac{C_{1}}{2^{*}}\|u\|_{\lambda}^{2^{*}}-\frac{C_{2}}{p+1}\|u\|_{\lambda}^{p+1}

\displaystyle\geq C\|u\|_{\lambda}^{2}\left(1-C_{3}\|u\|_{\lambda}^{2^{*}-2}-C_{4}\|u\|_{\lambda}^{p-1}\right).

Now, let
u\in H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
be such that
\|u\|_{\lambda}=\rho>0
. Since
2^{*}>p+1>2
, by choosing
\rho
sufficiently small, we ensure that

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\\
\|u\|_{\lambda}=\rho\end{subarray}}J(u)\geq C\rho^{2}\left(1-C_{3}\rho^{2^{*}-2}-C_{4}\rho^{p-1}\right)=:\beta>0,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\lambda<\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}
and
1<p<2^{*}-1
, and suppose that the condition (
1.2
) is satisfied. Then, there exists
e\in H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
such that
e\geq 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
,
\|e\|_{\lambda}>\rho
, and
J(e)<\beta
, where
\rho
and
\beta
are the constants from Proposition
4.1
.

Moreover, such a function

can be explicitly constructed as

(4.2)

e=\zeta_{0}u_{0},

where
u_{0}
is given as in (
1.2
) and
\zeta_{0}>0
is chosen sufficiently large.

Proof.

Let us fix any nontrivial function
u\in H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
satisfying
\|u\|_{\lambda}=1
and
u\geq 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
.

Now, for any
\zeta>0
, evaluate:

\displaystyle J(\zeta u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta^{2}}{2}\left(\|u\|_{\lambda}^{2}+[u]_{s}^{2}\right)-\frac{\zeta^{2^{*}}}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u(x)|^{2^{*}}\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)-\frac{\zeta^{p+1}}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u(x)|^{p+1}\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x).

Due to the super-quadratic growth of the nonlinearities (since
2^{*}>2
and
p+1>2
), we conclude that
J(\zeta u)\to-\infty
.

Consequently, we can choose
\zeta>0
sufficiently large so that the corresponding function

satisfies both
\|e\|_{\lambda}>\rho
and

. In particular, under assumption (
1.2
), we can take
u=u_{0}/\|u_{0}\|_{\lambda}
(with

), which yields the desired result for

with a suitable choice of
\zeta_{0}>0
large enough.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Thanks to Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
, we know that the functional
J
satisfies the geometric conditions required to apply the variant of the Mountain Pass Theorem formulated in
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
.

In addition, it is immediate to observe that

, and by Proposition
4.1
,
0<\beta
.

We define the mountain pass level associated to

as

(4.3)

where

denotes the class of continuous paths

\mathcal{P}=\left\{P\in C([0,1],H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})):P(0)=0,\,P(1)=e\right\},

with

constructed in Proposition
4.2
.

To establish Theorem
1.2
, we proceed through several steps.

Step 1:

We first show that the level

defined in (
4.3
) satisfies

\beta\leq m<\frac{1}{N}S_{\lambda,s}^{N/2},

where
\beta
is the constant from Proposition
4.1
, and

is as defined in (

).

Proof of Step 1:

Let us begin by noting that, for any
P\in\mathcal{P}
, the map

is continuous on
[0,1]
. Moreover, we have
\|P(0)\|_{\lambda}=\|0\|_{\lambda}=0<\rho
and

, where

is the constant introduced in Proposition

.

By continuity, there exists some
\bar{t}\in(0,1)
such that

. Consequently, we deduce

\sup_{v\in P([0,1])}J(v)\geq J(P(\bar{t}))\geq\inf_{\|v\|_{\lambda}=\rho}J(v),

which immediately implies that

, with

.

Next, recall that
e=\zeta_{0}u_{0}
according to (
4.2
), and therefore the path defined by

for

belongs to

. Using assumption (

), we then obtain

hence

This proves the desired bounds on

.

By applying
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
, we conclude that there exists a sequence

satisfying

and

(4.5)

as

.

Step 2:

The sequence

is bounded in

Proof of Step 2:

From (

Combining these two estimates, we obtain

On the other hand, a direct computation shows that

(4.7)

\displaystyle J(u_{j})-\frac{1}{p+1}\langle J^{\prime}(u_{j}),u_{j}\rangle\hskip-1.9919pt

\displaystyle=\hskip-2.84544pt\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\left(\|u_{j}\|_{\lambda}^{2}\hskip-1.9919pt+[u_{j}]_{s}^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{p+1}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\right)\hskip-5.69046pt\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{2^{*}}\hskip-2.84544pt\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}\|u_{j}\|_{\lambda}^{2},

where the inequality holds since

. Thus, by comparing (
4.6
) and (

is bounded, thereby proving Step 2.

Step 3.

Problem
(

~
P

λ
,
s

)
admits a solution

u_{\infty}\in H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
.

Proof of Step 3.
From the previous step, we know that the sequence
\{u_{j}\}
is bounded in
H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
. By reflexivity, there exists
u_{\infty}\in H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
such that, up to a subsequence,

u_{j}\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}\quad\text{weakly in}\quad H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N}).

That is, for any

,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u_{j}\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\phi\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u_{j}\phi\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u_{\infty}\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\phi\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u_{\infty}\phi\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

as

.

Moreover, by the embedding (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)){\mathcal{K}_{s}}(d(x,y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(u_{\infty}(x)-u_{\infty}(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)){\mathcal{K}_{s}}(d(x,y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

for any

Since

is bounded in

, it follows from the Sobolev embedding (

is bounded in

as

Furthermore, using Lemma

u_{j}\to u_{\infty}\quad\text{strongly in}\quad L^{\nu}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\quad\text{for any}\quad\nu\in(2,2^{*}),

and

as

Now, for any

, we have by definition

\displaystyle\langle J^{\prime}(u_{j}),\varphi\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u_{j}\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\varphi\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u_{j}\varphi\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

Keeping in mind (

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u_{\infty}\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\varphi\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u_{\infty}\varphi\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(u_{\infty}(x)-u_{\infty}(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

Step 4.

throughout
\mathbb{B}^{N}
.

\{u_{j}\}
is bounded in
H_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
. Then, employing (

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leftarrow\langle\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{j}),u_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u_{j}|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|^{2}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)-\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{p+1}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}},

which, in light of (

\|u_{j}\|_{\lambda}^{2}+[u_{j}]_{s}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\to 0

as

.

Since Step 2 ensures that
\|u_{j}\|_{\lambda}
remains bounded in
\mathbb{R}
, it follows, by (

), that

is also bounded. Thus, possibly after passing to a subsequence, we can assume

\displaystyle\|u_{j}\|_{\lambda}^{2}+[u_{j}]_{s}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u_{j}|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|^{2}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)

Moreover, from (

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u_{j}|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{2}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|^{2}\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-\left.\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{p+1}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right]

where we used again that the subcritical term vanishes by (

. In addition, by (

\|u_{j}\|_{\lambda}^{2}+[u_{j}]_{s}^{2}\geq S_{\lambda,s}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{B}^{N})}^{2},

contradicting Step 1.

Thus, we conclude that

in

, completing Step 4.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 5.1

(
Weak Maximum Principle
)
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
be a weak solution satisfying
Lu\geq 0
in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
in the weak sense, where the operator
L
is defined by

Lu:=-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u+(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}})^{s}u-\lambda u.

Then
u\geq 0
in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
.

Proof.

We test the inequality
Lu\geq 0
with
u^{-}
, the negative part of
u
. This yields:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u^{-}|^{2}\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(u(x)-u(y))(u^{-}(x)-u^{-}(y))\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y)+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u^{-}|^{2}\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}.

Splitting
u=u^{+}-u^{-}
and rearranging, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq-\|u^{-}\|_{\lambda}^{2}-[u^{-}]^{2}_{s}-\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\left(u^{+}(x)u^{-}(y)+u^{-}(x)u^{+}(y)\right)\mathcal{K}_{s}(d(x,y))\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(y).

Since each term on the right-hand side is non-positive, the inequality implies that all must vanish. In particular,
\|u^{-}\|_{\lambda}=0
and
[u^{-}]_{s}=0
, which forces
u^{-}\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
. Hence,
u\geq 0
in
\mathbb{B}^{N}
, completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{j}v\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\longrightarrow\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{\infty}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{\infty}v\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\quad\text{for all }v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N}),

where the sequence
\{u_{j}\}
is as defined in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof.

(5.1)

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{j}v\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}=\int_{B(0,r)}|u_{j}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{j}v\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{N}\setminus B(0,r)}|u_{j}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{j}v\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}},

where the radius
r>0
will be chosen later.
First, we analyze the convergence on the ball
B(0,r)
using Vitali’s Convergence Theorem, noting that
u_{j}\to u_{\infty}
a.e. For any
\varepsilon>0
, we choose
\Omega\subset B(0,r)
such that

\left(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2^{*}}\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{(MS_{\lambda,2^{*}-1}^{-1/2})^{2^{*}-1}},

where
M
is a constant such that
\|u_{j}\|_{\lambda}<M
and
S_{\lambda,2^{*}-1}
is the best constant in the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality as given in (
2.4
). Since
|v|^{2^{*}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, the choice of
\Omega
is valid. By Hölder’s inequality, we then have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}|u_{j}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{j}v\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right|\leq\int_{\Omega}|u_{j}|^{2^{*}-1}|v|\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{j}|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-1}{2^{*}}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(S_{\lambda,2^{*}-1}^{-1/2}\|u_{j}\|_{\lambda}\right)^{2^{*}-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon,

which shows that
|u_{j}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{j}v
is uniformly integrable on
B(0,r)
. By Vitali’s Convergence Theorem, we can pass the limit
j\to\infty
in the first integral of (
5.1
).

Now, we focus on the second term in (
5.1
). We define
v_{j}=u_{j}-u_{\infty}
, which satisfies
v_{j}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\left||v_{j}+u_{\infty}|^{2^{*}-2}(v_{j}+u_{\infty})-|u_{\infty}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{\infty}\right|\leq\varepsilon|v_{j}|^{2^{*}-1}+C_{\varepsilon}|u_{\infty}|^{2^{*}-1}.

Thus, we can estimate the difference between the integrals on
B(0,r)^{c}
as follows

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B(0,r)^{c}}\left\{|u_{j}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{j}v-|u_{\infty}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{\infty}v\right\}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{B(0,r)^{c}}|v_{j}|^{2^{*}-1}|v|\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{B(0,r)^{c}}|u_{\infty}|^{2^{*}-1}|v|\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\left(\int_{B(0,r)^{c}}|v_{j}|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-1}{2^{*}}}\left(\int_{B(0,r)^{c}}|v|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C_{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{B(0,r)^{c}}|u_{\infty}|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-1}{2^{*}}}\left(\int_{B(0,r)^{c}}|v|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|v_{j}\|_{\lambda}^{2^{*}-1}\left(\int_{B(0,r)^{c}}|v|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}+C_{\varepsilon}\|u_{\infty}\|_{\lambda}^{2^{*}-1}\left(\int_{B(0,r)^{c}}|v|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}.

Since the sequence
\{\|v_{j}\|_{\lambda}\}
is uniformly bounded and
|v|^{2^{*}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we can choose
r
sufficiently large such that

\left|\int_{B(0,r)^{c}}\left\{|u_{j}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{j}v-|u_{\infty}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{\infty}v\right\}\,\mathrm{~d}V_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right|<\varepsilon.

This concludes the proof.∎

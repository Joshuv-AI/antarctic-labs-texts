Hasse-Schmidt derivations and locally trivial deformations in positive characteristic
Takuya Miyamoto

Abstract
In this paper, we first study the lifting problem of Hasse-Schmidt derivations and then apply the results to locally trivial deformations of algebraic schemes, both in positive characteristic setting. In particular, we show that any locally trivial deformation of an affine scheme is trivial even in positive characteristic.



1
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we fix an algebraically closed field
k
of positive characteristic
p
. We assume that any
k
-algebra is unital and commutative.

In this paper, we study the following two lifting problems in positive characteristic: Suppose

0\to k\cdot t\to B\to A\to 0

is a small extension of artinian local rings of finite type over
k
. First, we study when a deformation automorphism of the trivial deformation
R\otimes_{k}A
lifts to
R\otimes_{k}B
, where
R
is a
k
-algebra of finite type. We apply the theory of Hasse-Schmidt derivations introduced in
[
8
]
to this problem. Second, let
X_{0}
be an algebraic scheme and
X
be a locally trivial deformation of
X_{0}
over
A
(a formal deformation that is Zariski-locally isomorphic to the trivial deformation). We study the obstruction structure of
X
along
B\to A
. In other words, we use cohomological classes to give a characterization when
X
lifts to a locally trivial deformation over
B
. (We note that if
X_{0}
is smooth, then any formal deformation is locally trivial. Thus, the notion of locally trivial deformations coincides with usual deformations.)

Let us note that the theory of locally trivial deformation in positive characteristic is not well-established compared with characteristic
0
case. For example, in characteristic
0
, the locally trivial deformation functor
\operatorname{Def}_{X_{0}}^{\prime}
satisfies Schlessinger’s conditions (see
[
5
, Theorem 2.11]
,
[
1
, Remark 2.7]
). However, it was not known until recently that, in positive characteristic, there exist examples where
\operatorname{Def}_{X_{0}}^{\prime}
does not satisfy Schlessinger’s
(H_{1})
(see
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
).

The aim of this paper is to show that, even in positive characteristic, their behavior is tame to some extent and that there is room for further research in this area. For example, the following is one of our main results.

Theorem 1.1

(= Theorem
5.1
)
.

Any locally trivial deformation of an affine algebraic scheme
X_{0}
over
k
is trivial.

Note that the same result is an immediate consequence of
[
5
, Theorem 2.11]
in characteristic
0
or if
X_{0}
is smooth. In spite of its fundamental importance, Theorem
1.1
has been unknown until now, and this paper is the first one which, among other results, confirms this important theorem.

In characteristic
0
or if
X_{0}
is smooth, the proof is as follows: Because
H^{1}(\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}))=0
, we can apply Schlessinger’s theorem to obtain a smooth morphism of functors

\operatorname{Hom}(R,-)\to\operatorname{Def}_{X_{0}}^{\prime},

where
R
is a nonzero quotient of
k[\![t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]\!]
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{R}/\mathfrak{m}_{R}^{2}=0
. We have
\mathfrak{m}_{R}=0
by Nakayama’s lemma and
R=k
, so that the functor
\operatorname{Hom}(R,-)
is trivial. We see that for every finite artinian local
k
-algebra
A
,
\operatorname{Def}_{X_{0}}^{\prime}(A)
consists only of one element, i.e. the trivial deformation, and this completes the proof.

Note that this argument does not work in characteristic
p
because
\operatorname{Def}_{X_{0}}^{\prime}
may not satisfy
(H_{1})
(cf.
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
), although it follows from the theorem.

In Section 2, we study Hasse-Schmidt derivations
\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)
, where
R
is a
k
-algebra essentially of finite type. This is equivalent to the notion of deformation automorphisms of
R\otimes_{k}(k[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1})
. See
[
8
]
,
[
2
, Chapter 1]
and
[
3
, Section 27]
for basic facts of Hasse-Schmidt derivations. The problem of extending a Hasse-Schmidt derivation corresponds to extending a deformation automorphisms along
k[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1}\to k[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m}
. To describe this problem, we introduce the
m
-th
obstruction module

\operatorname{Ob}^{m}_{R}
whose definition is very natural (Definition
2.11
,
2.13
). Its definition uses the first cotangent module
T^{1}_{R/k}
(see
[
6
, Definition 1.1.6]
for example). The problem is that, by definition, it is merely an abelian group; it is not evident whether it is an
R
-module or even constitutes a sheaf over
\operatorname{Spec}R
. Only for
m=p^{i}
, we know that it has a natural module structure. To deal with this problem, we closely follow the methods of Hernández
[
7
, Lemma 2.10]
to show that, for general
m
, we have
\operatorname{Ob}^{m}_{R}=\operatorname{Ob}^{p^{i}}_{R}
where
i
is the largest integer with
p^{i}|m
. In addition, we utilize the methods of Macarro
[
2
, Proposition 1.3.5]
to show that
\operatorname{Ob}^{p^{i}}_{R}
is compatible with localization. Thus, we can conclude that
\operatorname{Ob}^{p^{i}}_{R}
constitutes a coherent sheaf (Proposition
2.14
, Theorem
2.17
). Also, this shows that Hasse-Schmidt derivations are useful not only in commutative algebra, but also in algebraic geometry. In addition, we study the relation between
m
-integrable derivations
[
2
, Definition 2.1.1]
that have also been shown to constitute coherent sheaves by Maccaro
[
2
, Corollary 2.3.7]
. In this section, we essentially use the fact that
k
contains
n
-th roots of unity. Note that the definition of the
m
-th obstruction modules
\operatorname{Ob}^{m}_{R}
does not use the characteristic
p
assumption. However, since any Hasse-Schmidt derivation extends in characteristic
0
, we see that
\operatorname{Ob}^{m}_{R}
is interesting only in positive characteristics.

In Section 3, we generalize the results of Section 2 to arbitrary small extensions. To make the notation consistent with that of Section 2, we denote the group of deformation automorphisms of
R\otimes_{k}A
by
\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R)
. For an arbitrary small extension
\Phi:0\to k\cdot t\to B\to A\to 0
, we can define the obstruction module
\operatorname{Ob}^{\Phi}_{R}
in the same way. Again, the problem is that it is merely an abelian group. To overcome this issue, we prove the following key result:

Theorem 1.2

(= Theorem
3.23
)
.

For any
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R)
, we have a decomposition

D=D^{1}\langle y_{1}\rangle\circ\dots\circ D^{m}\langle y_{m}\rangle

where
y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\in\mathfrak{m}_{A}
and
D^{i}\in\operatorname{HS}^{r(A,y_{i})}(R)
. (The symbols
D^{i}\langle y_{i}\rangle
,
r(A,y_{i})
will be defined in Definition
3.13
,
3.14
).

From this theorem, we can reduce the problem to the case of usual Hasse-Schmidt derivations. As a consequence, we deduce
\operatorname{Ob}^{\Phi}_{R}=\operatorname{Ob}^{p^{i}}_{R}
where
i
is an integer depending on
\Phi
. The proof of the above theorem is the most technical part of this paper. Its proof begins with defining artinian rings denoted by
A(e,a)
, where
e=(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})
is a sequence of positive integers and
a
a sequence in
k
. It is very close to the ring
A(e):=k[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]/(X_{1}^{e_{1}},\dots,X_{n}^{e_{n}})
. We first prove the theorem in the case
A=A(e,a)
using geometric interpretation and various results in Section 2. Then, we reduce the general cases to
A(e,a)
and this completes the proof. In this section, we use the perfectness of
k
(we take
p^{i}
-th roots).

In Section 4 and Section 5, we apply the previous results to locally trivial deformations of an algebraic scheme
X_{0}
along a small extension
\Phi:0\to k\cdot t\to B\to A\to 0
. To utilize previous results on Hasse-Schmidt derivations, we first work with a fixed affine open cover of
X_{0}
. Then, we show that the arguments are independent of the choice of the cover. We decompose the problem of existence of a locally trivial lift into two parts: First, we determine whether lifts of gluing morphisms exist. Second, we determine whether we can take the lifts in such a way that they satisfy the cocycle conditions. The first one is described in terms of
H^{1}(\operatorname{Ob}^{\Phi}_{X_{0}})
(Theorem
4.10
). The second is in terms of
H^{2}(\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}))
and
H^{0}(\operatorname{Ob}^{\Phi}_{X_{0}})
(Theorem
4.13
). We also examine the fiber of a locally trivial deformation
X\in\operatorname{Def}^{\prime}_{X_{0}}(A)
along
\alpha:\operatorname{Def}^{\prime}_{X_{0}}(B)\to\operatorname{Def}^{\prime}_{X_{0}}(A)
(Proposition
4.19
). The first application of this study is Theorem
1.1
. Determining the complete structure of a fiber is difficult, even in characteristic
0
or even if
X_{0}
is smooth. However, at least we have the following:

Theorem 1.3

(= Theorem
5.5
)
.

We have an isomorphism of sets

\alpha^{-1}(X)/H^{1}(\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}))\simeq V(X)/L(X).

The symbols
V(X),L(X)
will be defined in terms of
\operatorname{Ob}^{\Phi}_{X_{0}}
in Definition
4.17
,
4.18
.

On the left hand side, we take the quotient of the group action by
H^{1}(\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}))
; see
[
5
, Remark 2.15]
. Note that the above isomorphic sets are either empty or
\{\operatorname{pt}\}
when
\operatorname{Def}^{\prime}_{X_{0}}
satisfies Schlessinger’s
(H_{1})
(see the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 2.11]
). Thus, the sets represent the degree of non-prorepresentability of
\operatorname{Def}^{\prime}_{X_{0}}
in a sense. At the end of Section 5, we apply the above theorem and the theory of
m
-integrable derivations to give a necessary condition for Schlessinger’s
(H_{1})
in the case
H^{2}(\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}))=0.

Hasse-Schmidt derivations

We first recall basic facts of Hasse-Schmidt derivations (cf.
[
8
]
,
[

,
[
3

) and results in
[

[
7
]
.

Definition 2.1

(=
[
2
, Definition 1.2.1-1.2.6]
)
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra. Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. Then, a
Hasse-Schmidt derivation
of length
m
is a sequence
D=(D_{i})_{i=0}^{m}
of
k
-linear endomorphisms
R\to R
such that
D_{0}=\operatorname{id}
and that

D_{i}(xy)=\sum_{j+k=i}D_{j}(x)D_{k}(y)

for every
x,y\in R,i\geq 1.
Let
E=(E_{i})_{i=0}^{m}
be another Hasse-Schmidt derivation. We define the
composition
of
D
and
E
by

D\circ E=(F_{i})_{i=0}^{m}\mathrm{\ \ where\ }\ F_{i}(x)=\sum_{j+k=i}D_{j}(E_{k}(x)).

The symbol
\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)(=\operatorname{HS}^{m}_{k}(R)=\operatorname{HS}(R,m))
denotes the set of all the Hasse-Schmidt derivations of length
m.
It is a group whose multiplication is given by
\circ
. Let
x\in R
. We define

x\bullet:\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)

by

x\bullet(D_{i})_{i=0}^{m}=(x^{i}D_{i})_{i=0}^{m}.

For
n\geq 1,
we define

[n]:\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}^{mn}(R)

by

D[n]=(E_{i})_{i=0}^{mn}\mathrm{\ \ where\ }\ E_{jn}=D_{j}\mathrm{\ for\ }0\leq j\leq m\mathrm{\ and\ }E_{i}=0\mathrm{\ otherwise.}

A Hasse-Schmidt derivation
D=(D_{i})_{i=0}^{m}
defines a natural
k
-algebra homomorhism

R\to R[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1},\ x\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{m}D_{i}(x)\varepsilon^{i}.

Let
1\leq l\leq m
be another integer. We define the
truncation map

\tau:\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}^{l}(R)

by

(D_{i})_{i=0}^{m}\mapsto(D_{i})_{i=0}^{l}.

It is a group homomorphism and commutes with
x\bullet
for
x\in R
.
Note that the set
\operatorname{HS}^{1}(R)
can be identified with the
R
-module
\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R,R)
under

D=(\operatorname{id},D_{1})\mapsto D_{1}.

Definition 2.2

(=
[
2
, Definition 2.1.1]
)
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra and let
1\leq m<\infty
. We define the module of

m
-integrable derivations
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(R)
as the image of

\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)\overset{\tau}{\to}\operatorname{HS}^{1}(R)\simeq\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R,R).

It is a sub-
R
-module of
\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R,R)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1\leq n\leq m<\infty
be integers and
T
be a formally smooth
k
-algebra. Then, any Hasse-Schmidt derivation
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{n}(T)
lifts to
E\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(T)
.

Proof.

This corresponds to the existence of the dotted arrow in the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\varepsilon^{n+1})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{n+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

where the vertical arrow is induced from
D
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer,
T
be a formally smooth
k
-algebra,
I\subset T
be an ideal and
R:=T/I
. Then, any
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)
lifts to
E\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(T)
.

Proof.

Let
S=T[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1}
and let
J\subset S
be
the ideal generated by
x\varepsilon,x\in I
. The
k
-linear map

f:T\to S/J,\ x\mapsto x+\sum_{i=1}^{m}D_{i}(\overline{x})\varepsilon^{i}

is a
k
-algebra homomorphism, where
\overline{x}
is the image of
x
in
R
. Because
T
is formally smooth and because
J
is nilpotent,
f
lifts to

\widetilde{f}:T\to S=T[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1}

and
\widetilde{f}
induces
E\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(T)
that is a lift of
D
.
∎

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra,
m\geq 1
an integer and
S\subset R
a multiplicatively closed subset. Then, we have a canonical map

\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}^{m}(S^{-1}R)

and it induces

\alpha:S^{-1}\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(R)\to\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(S^{-1}R).

Now, let us prove a theorem by Macarro that states that
m
-integrable derivations are compatible with localizations.

Theorem 2.5

(=
[
2
, Corollary 2.3.5]
)
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra essentially of finite type over
k
. Let
m\geq 1
an integer. Let
S\subset R
a multiplicatively closed subset. Then, we have a canonical isomorphism

\alpha:S^{-1}\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(R)\overset{\sim}{\to}\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(S^{-1}R).

Proof.

As
S^{-1}\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R)\to\operatorname{Der}_{k}(S^{-1}R)
is an isomorphism,
\alpha
is injective. For surjectivity, let us take
D=(D_{i})_{i=0}^{m}\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(S^{-1}R)
. Let
T
be a localization of
k[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
and
I\subset T
an ideal such that
T/I=R.
Let
S^{\prime}\subset T
be the preimage of
S
. There exists
D^{\prime}=(D_{i}^{\prime})_{i=0}^{m}\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(S^{\prime-1}T)
such that

D^{\prime}\operatorname{mod}S^{\prime-1}I=D.

As
m
and
n
are finite, there exists
s_{1}\in S^{\prime}
such that
s_{1}\bullet D^{\prime}
is induced from
\widetilde{D}\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(T)
. Again, as
m
and
n
are finite and
I
is finitely generated, there exists
s_{2}\in S^{\prime}
such that
s_{2}\bullet\widetilde{D}=\widetilde{E}
satisfies
\widetilde{E}_{i}(I)\subset I
for every
0\leq i\leq m
. Then,
E=\widetilde{E}\operatorname{mod}I
is an element of
\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)
and
(s_{1}s_{2})^{-1}(E_{1})=D_{1}
. This shows the surjectivity of
\alpha
.
∎

The following definition is inspired by
[
7
, Lemma 2.10]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra. Let
m\geq 1
and
n\geq 2
integers where
p
does not divide
n
. Let us take a primitive
n
-th root of unity
\zeta_{n}\in k
. We define a map

\chi_{n}:\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)

by

\chi_{n}(D)=(\frac{\zeta_{n}^{n-1}}{\sqrt[n]{n}}\bullet D)\circ(\frac{\zeta_{n}^{n-2}}{\sqrt[n]{n}}\bullet D)\circ\dots\circ(\frac{\zeta_{n}^{0}}{\sqrt[n]{n}}\bullet D).

Although the map
\chi_{n}
depends on the choice of
\zeta_{n}
and
\sqrt[n]{n}
, any such choice will work in the following arguments. Note that
\chi_{n}
commutes with truncation maps.

Lemma 2.7
.

In the setting of Definition
2.6
, let us assume
n\nmid m
. Let
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)
be such that, for every
1\leq l<m
satisfying
n\nmid l
, we have
D_{l}=0
. Then, for
E=\chi_{n}(D),
we have
E_{m}=0
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle E_{m}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\zeta_{n}^{n-1}}{\sqrt[n]{n}})^{m}\cdot D_{m}+(\frac{\zeta_{n}^{n-2}}{\sqrt[n]{n}})^{m}\cdot D_{m}+\dots+(\frac{\zeta_{n}^{0}}{\sqrt[n]{n}})^{m}\cdot D_{m}

\displaystyle=(\zeta_{n}^{m\cdot(n-1)}+\zeta_{n}^{m\cdot(n-2)}+\dots+\zeta_{n}^{m\cdot 0})\cdot\frac{1}{n^{\frac{m}{n}}}D_{m}

\displaystyle=0.

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

In the setting of Definition
2.6
, let
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)
and
E=\chi_{n}(D)
. Then, for every
i\geq 1
such that
p^{i}n\leq m
, we have

E_{p^{i}n}=D_{p^{i}n}+\mathrm{(noncommutative\ polynomial\ in\ }D_{1},\dots,D_{p^{i}n-1}).

Proof.

The coefficient of
D_{p^{i}n}
in
E_{p^{i}n}
is

\displaystyle(\frac{\zeta_{n}^{n-1}}{\sqrt[n]{n}})^{p^{i}n}+(\frac{\zeta_{n}^{n-2}}{\sqrt[n]{n}})^{p^{i}n}+\dots+(\frac{\zeta_{n}^{0}}{\sqrt[n]{n}})^{p^{i}n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\zeta_{n}^{{p^{i}n}\cdot(n-1)}+\zeta_{n}^{{p^{i}n}\cdot(n-2)}+\dots+\zeta_{n}^{{p^{i}n}\cdot 0})\cdot\frac{1}{n^{\frac{{p^{i}n}}{n}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((\zeta_{n}^{{n}\cdot(n-1)}+\zeta_{n}^{{n}\cdot(n-2)}+\dots+\zeta_{n}^{{n}\cdot 0})\cdot\frac{1}{n})^{p^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1^{p^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.

∎

Lemma 2.9
.

In the setting of Definition
2.6
, let us assume
m=p^{i}n,i\geq 0,n\geq 2
. Then, for any
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)
, there exists
D^{[n]}\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)
such that
D^{[n]}_{l}=0
for every

satisfying

, and that

D^{[n]}_{m}=D_{m}+\mathrm{(noncommutative\ polynomial\ in\ }D_{1},\dots,D_{m-1}).

Proof.

Let us apply
\chi_{n}
to

iteratively sufficiently many times. Let
D^{[n]}\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)
denote the resulting element. Then, Lemma

and

imply that

satisfies the desired properties. ∎

Now, we can give an easier proof of a theorem by Hernández under the assumption that

Theorem 2.10

(=
[
7
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra that is essentially of finite type. For every
m\geq 2,
the inclusion
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(R)\subset\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m-1}(R)
is proper only if
m
is of the form
m=p^{i}
for
i\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let us assume
m=p^{i}n,i\geq 0,n\geq 2
where
p\nmid n
. It suffices to show that
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(R)=\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m-1}(R)
. Let us take
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{m-1}(R)
. We would like to show that
D_{1}\in\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(R)
. Let
T
be a localization of a polynomial algebra over
k
and
I\subset T
an ideal such that
T/I=R.
Then,
D
lifts to
E^{\prime}\in\operatorname{HS}^{m-1}(T)
and

lifts to
E\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(T)
. Let us apply Lemma
2.9
to

and let
E^{[n]}
denote the resulting element. We have
E^{[n]}_{l}=0
for every

satisfying

, in particular
E^{[n]}_{1}=0
, and we have

E^{[n]}_{m}=E_{m}+\mathrm{(noncommutative\ polynomial\ in\ }E_{1},\dots,E_{m-1}).

Let us define

. We have

and for every

,

is a noncommutative polynomial in

(the term

.
∎

Proposition 2.12
.

In the situation of Definition
2.11
, the map
\operatorname{ob}_{m}
is a group homomorphism that does not depend on the choice of
T,I,E
. A Hasse-Schmidt derivation
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{m-1}(R)
extends to an element in
\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)
if and only if
\operatorname{ob}_{m}(D)=0
. For every
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{m-1}(R)
and
x\in R
, we have

\operatorname{ob}_{m}(x\bullet D)=x^{m}\cdot\operatorname{ob}_{m}(D).

Proof.

First, let us show that
\operatorname{ob}_{m}
is a group homomorphism. Let
D^{1},D^{2}\in\operatorname{HS}^{m-1}(R)
be Hasse-Schmidt derivations and let
E^{1},E^{2}\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(T)
be their lifts. Let
J\subset T[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1}
be the ideal generated by
\varepsilon\cdot I
. Note that

(T[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1})/J=T\oplus\varepsilon R\oplus\dots\oplus\varepsilon^{m}R.

Let

\psi_{1},\psi_{2}:T[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1}\rightrightarrows T[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1}

be the induced morphisms from
E^{1},E^{2}
. Note that
\psi_{1}(I),\psi_{2}(I)\subset(I,\varepsilon^{m})
because they are lifts of
R[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m}\rightrightarrows R[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m}
. Thus,
\psi_{1}(J),\psi_{2}(J)\subset J
and there are induced morphisms

\overline{\psi}_{1},\overline{\psi}_{2}:(T[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1})/J\rightrightarrows(T[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1})/J

from
E^{1},E^{2}
. For
a\in I,
we have equalities in
(T[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1})/J
:

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{m}\cdot f_{E^{1}\circ E^{2}}(a+I^{2})

\displaystyle=(\overline{\psi}_{1}\circ\overline{\psi}_{2})(a)-a

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi}_{1}(a+\varepsilon^{m}\cdot f_{E^{2}}(a+I^{2}))-a

\displaystyle=a+\varepsilon^{m}\cdot f_{E^{1}}(a+I^{2})+\varepsilon^{m}\cdot f_{E^{2}}(a+I^{2})-a

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{m}\cdot(f_{E^{1}}+f_{E^{2}})(a+I^{2}).

This implies
f_{E^{1}\circ E^{2}}=f_{E^{1}}+f_{E^{2}}
and
\operatorname{ob}_{m}(D^{1}\circ D^{2})=\operatorname{ob}_{m}(D^{1})+\operatorname{ob}_{m}(D^{2})
, so
\operatorname{ob}_{m}
is a group homomorphism.
Now, let us show that the element
\operatorname{ob}_{m}(D)
does not depend on the choice of
T,I
nor
E
. First, fix
T
and
I
and let
F\in\operatorname{HS}^{m}(T)
be another lift of
D
. Then,
E
and
F
induce
k
-algebra homomorphisms

\varphi_{E},\varphi_{F}:T\rightrightarrows R[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1}

such that
\varphi_{E}=\varphi_{F}
modulo
\varepsilon^{m}.
Hence,
\varphi_{E}-\varphi_{F}
is a
k
-derivation from
T
to
\varepsilon^{m}\cdot R[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{m+1}
. This means that
f_{E}=f_{F}
in
T^{1}_{R/k}.
A similar argument also shows that

extends to an element of
\operatorname{HS}^{m}(R)
if and only if
\operatorname{ob}_{m}(D)=0
. Now, let
T^{\prime},I^{\prime}
be another choice of
T,I
. Then, there exists
\psi:T^{\prime}\to T
that induces
T^{\prime}/I^{\prime}\simeq T/I
. Note that
\varphi_{E}\circ\psi
is induced from some

(by an argument similar to that of Lemma
2.4
.) Note that
\psi
induces
\widetilde{\psi}:I^{\prime}/I^{\prime 2}\to I/I^{2}
. We have to show that
f_{E}
and
f_{E}\circ\widetilde{\psi}
induce the same element in
T^{1}_{R/k}
, and this follows from the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(I/I^{2},R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T^{1}_{R/k}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(I^{\prime}/I^{\prime 2},R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T^{1}_{R/k}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\psi}^{\sharp}}

Finally, the equality
\operatorname{ob}_{m}(x\bullet D)=x^{m}\cdot\operatorname{ob}_{m}(D)
follows from the construction.
∎

3
Obstructions along small extensions

Definition 3.1
.

The symbol
\mathcal{A}
denotes the category whose objects are local artinian
k
-algebras
A=(A,\mathfrak{m}_{A})
with
A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}\simeq k
and whose morphisms are local
k
-algebra homomorphisms.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
A
be an object in
\mathcal{A}
and
R
be a
k
-algebra. Then, we define
\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R)(=\operatorname{HS}^{A}_{k}(R)=\operatorname{HS}(R,A))
to be the group consisting of
A
-algebra automorphisms
A\otimes_{k}R\to A\otimes_{k}R
inducing
\operatorname{id}:A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}\otimes_{k}R\to A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}\otimes_{k}R
. (Note that this is not a common notation.)

Remark 3.2.1
.

Let
x\in R
and
R_{x}
be the localization of
R
by
x
. If
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R)
, then the difference of
1\otimes x
and
D(1\otimes x)
is nilpotent. Thus,
D
induces

A\otimes R_{x}\simeq(A\otimes R)_{1\otimes x}\to(A\otimes R)_{D(1\otimes x)}\simeq A\otimes R_{x}.

This implies that we have a natural group homomorphism
\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R_{x})
. More generally, if
\operatorname{Spec}R^{\prime}
is an affine open subscheme of
\operatorname{Spec}R
, then there induced a natural group homomorphism
\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R^{\prime}).

Definition 3.3
.

Let
A
be an object in
\mathcal{A}
and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}
be a
k
-linear basis of
\mathfrak{m}_{A}.
Let
R
be a
k
-algebra and
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R)
. Then,
\{D_{\lambda_{i}}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
denotes the family of
k
-linear endomorphisms
R\to R
such that

D(1\otimes r)=1\otimes r+\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\otimes D_{\lambda_{i}}(r),

which is uniquely determined as
A\otimes_{k}R
is a free
R
-module.

Remark 3.3.1
.

For
A=k[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{n+1},
we can canonically identify
\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R)
with
\operatorname{HS}^{n}(R)
by taking
\lambda_{1}=\varepsilon,\lambda_{2}=\varepsilon^{2},\dots
as a basis of
\mathfrak{m}_{A}.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
f:A^{\prime}\to A
be a morphism in
\mathcal{A}
. By

f_{*}:\operatorname{HS}^{A^{\prime}}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R),

we denote the natural group homomorphism defined by

f_{*}(D):=\operatorname{id}_{A}\otimes_{A^{\prime}}D:A\otimes_{k}R\to A\otimes_{k}R.

Here, we note that
A\otimes_{k}R\simeq A\otimes_{A^{\prime}}(A^{\prime}\otimes_{k}R)
.

Remark 3.4.1
.

The elements
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R)
correspond bijectively to
k
-algebra morphisms
D|_{R}:R\to A\otimes_{k}R
such that the composition

R\overset{D|_{R}}{\to}A\otimes_{k}R\overset{}{\to}(A/\mathfrak{m}_{A})\otimes_{k}R\simeq R

is the identity. Here,
D|_{R}
is given as the composition

R\simeq k\otimes_{k}R\overset{\iota\otimes\operatorname{id}}{\to}A\otimes_{k}R\overset{D}{\to}A\otimes_{k}R,

where
\iota:k\to A
is the
k
-structure morphism.

Remark 3.4.2
.

If
f:A^{\prime}\hookrightarrow A
is an inclusion in
\mathcal{A}
and
D\in\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R)
is such that the image of
D|_{R}
is contained in
A^{\prime}\otimes_{k}R
, then

D^{\prime}=\operatorname{id}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes_{k}(D|_{R}):A^{\prime}\otimes_{k}R\to A^{\prime}\otimes_{k}R

satisfies
f_{*}(D^{\prime})=D
by construction.

Over general artinian rings, we do not have an analogue of the action
x\bullet
on
\operatorname{HS}^{n}(R)
. We could define
x\bullet D
as a map of sets, but it is not always a ring homomorphism.

A

(in
\mathcal{A}

Proposition 3.9

We show that

D^{1},D^{2}\in\operatorname{HS}^{A}(R)
be Hasse-Schmidt derivations and let

be their lifts. Let

be the ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes_{k}I
. We have

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
X_{0}
is an affine algebraic
k
-scheme. Then, for any object
A
in
\mathcal{A}
, any locally trivial deformation of

over

is trivial.

Proof.

By induction on the length of

. The theorem is true for
A=k
. To proceed with the induction, suppose that we have a small extension

\Phi:0\to k\cdot t\to B\to A\to 0

and assume that the theorem is true for

. It suffices to show that, if
X\in\operatorname{Def}^{\prime}_{X_{0}}(A)
is the trivial deformation, then its fiber along
\operatorname{Def}^{\prime}_{X_{0}}(B)\to\operatorname{Def}^{\prime}_{X_{0}}(A)
consists only of the trivial deformation. Suppose that

is in the fiber. For a sufficiently fine affine open cover
\mathfrak{U}=\{U_{\alpha}\}
of

, there exists a couple

considered over
\mathfrak{U}
that satisfies the cocycle conditions and that

(see Definition
4.6
).

is also a lift of the trivial couple

. As

is trivial and affine, we have

and the trivial lift lie in the same orbit of the action by
\check{H}^{1}(\mathfrak{U},\operatorname{Der}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}))=0
. This implies that

is also trivial.

Tate-valued Characteristic Classes II: Applications
Shachar Carmeli, Kiran Luecke

Abstract
We present a construction that manufactures 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} orientations of Tate fixed-point objects together with useful formulas for these maps, and then give a number of applications. For example, we produce a formula for the Frobenius homomorphisms of Thom spectra such as MU \mathrm{MU} as well as certain lifts of Frobenius. We prove a rigidity property of MU \mathrm{MU} as a cyclotomic base . We construct a general obstruction theory for 𝔼 n \mathbb{E}_{n} complex orientations and establish various non-existence results for p p -typical 𝔼 n \mathbb{E}_{n} orientations for low values of p p and n n . We end with some miscellaneous further applications.

Introduction

1.1
Background and Main Results

The
complex bordism
spectrum
\mathrm{MU}
has played a central role in stable homotopy theory ever since the pioneering work of Adams, Novikov, and Quillen, (
[
Ada74
]
,
[
Nov67
]
,
[
Qui71
]
) . Moreover,
\mathrm{MU}
is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring spectrum (
[
Lew78
]
)—a fact which was implicitly used as early as
[
Qui71
]
—and recent perspectives have made it increasingly clear that this
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
structure is also of central significance. Recall that a complex orientation of a homotopy ring spectrum
R
is a homotopy ring map
\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R
. Thus, a fundamental question in understanding the
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
structure of
\mathrm{MU}
is: if
R
is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring, when does it admit a complex orientation by an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
map? This question is notoriously difficult but has seen much progress: for example, Ando, Hopkins, and Rezk (
[
AHR10
]
) establish a correspondence between
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
orientations of
R
(for suitable
R
) and certain sequences in
\pi_{*}R
. Hopkins and Lawson (
[
HL18
]
) constructed an obstruction theory for lifting a homotopy complex orientation to an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
one. Hahn and Yuan (
[
HY20
]
) showed that the
periodic
bordism spectrum
\mathrm{MUP}
admits at least two different
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-structures, and initiate a program to understand various aspects of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
orientation theory. More recently, Balderrama (
[
Bal23
]
) establishes the existence of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
orientations of Morava
E
-theories at heights
\leq 2
, and Burklund, Schlank, and Yuan (
[
BSY24
]
), for algebraically closed Morava
E
-theories of arbitrary height.

The
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
algebra structure of
\mathrm{MU}
arises from its presentation as the
Thom spectrum
of an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
map, namely the
j
-homomorphism
\mathrm{bu}\rightarrow\mathrm{pic}(\mathbb{S})
. We will exploit the
\mathrm{ku}
-module structure of
\mathrm{bu}
to manufacture
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
orientations, at the cost of passing to a Tate fixed-point object, generalizing our construction from
[
CL25
]
to complex representations of general compact Lie groups. More precisely, we give a construction (
2.4
) that takes as input a suitable representation
\mathscr{V}
of a compact Lie group
G
and an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring map
\omega:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R
, and produces an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring map

\#_{\mathscr{V}}(\omega):\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G},

where
(-)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
is an appropriate Tate fixed-points functor. Crucially, our construction comes with a usable formula (
2.8
) for the underlying homotopy ring map. With it we are able to transport simple linear algebraic facts about the representation
\mathscr{V}
into concrete formulas controlling the
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
algebra structure of
R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
.

A central example of an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring map valued in Tate fixed-points (and one which has received much recent attention) is the
Frobenius homomorphism
, defined by Nikolaus and Scholze (
[
NS18
, IV.1]
). This is a map
\mathrm{Fr}:R\rightarrow R^{tC_{p}}
which is natural in the
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring
R
and encodes its cyclic power operations (cf.
[
NS18
, IV.1.21]
). Our first theorem is that, in the complex oriented context, the Frobenius is a special case of our sharp construction.

Theorem A
.

(
2.9
)
Let
\rho
be the complex regular representation of
C_{p}
. Let
\mathrm{id}
be the identity map of
\mathrm{MU}
. Then the map

\#_{\rho}(\mathrm{id}):\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow\mathrm{MU}^{tC_{p}}

gotten by
2.4
is homotopic to the Frobenius as an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring map.

Combined with the formulas we get for sharp maps (
2.8
) we immediately get the following corollary.

Corollary 1.1
.

(
2.11
)
Let
\mathbf{x}\in(\mathrm{MU}^{tC_{p}})^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
be the coordinate corresponding to the unit map
\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow\mathrm{MU}^{tC_{p}}
. Then the coordinate corresponding to the Frobenius is given by

\mathrm{Fr}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{\mathbf{x}+_{\mathbf{F}}[k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{t})}{[k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{t})}.

Thus, one can think of
A
as an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
lift of this formula.

Remark 1.2
.

We make no claim to originality regarding the formula: it is surely known to experts. As mentioned above, it is equivalent to the knowledge of the cyclic power operations in
\mathrm{MU}
which goes back to
[
Qui71
]
. However, to our knowledge this formula has not appeared in the literature, and we give a self contained proof that does not rely on manifold geometry or transversality arguments (as used by Quillen).

Next, we would like to demonstrate the usefulness of this with some applications. First, as another corollary of
A
we get certain lifts of the Frobenius map of
\mathrm{MU}
, wich recovers an unpublished result of Hahn-Devalapurkar-Raksit-Yuan

Corollary 1.3
.

(

The Frobenius
\mathrm{Fr}:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow\mathrm{MU}^{tC_{p}}
factors, as an

(\mathrm{MU}^{t\mathbb{T}})_{(p)}\rightarrow\mathrm{MU}^{tC_{p}}

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}

cyclotomic bases
(cf.

); such rings
A
are of great interest because the relative
\mathrm{THH}

is one which commutes with these lifts of Frobenius. Using our formulas, we prove the following rigidity result, indicating that
\mathrm{MU}
admits rather few automorphisms as a cyclotomic base.

Theorem B
.

(
3.9
)
The only homotopy ring endomorphism of
\mathrm{MU}
which can be lifted to a map of cyclotomic bases is the identity map.

Next, we use our formulas to build an obstruction theory for
\mathbb{E}_{n}
complex orientations of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
rings (cf.
4.8
). When applied to
p

[
JN10
]
, but enjoys a few differences (cf.
4.17
). We use this obstruction theory to prove some explicit results, including the following. Recall that the Quillen idempotent is the map
\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow\mathrm{MU}_{(p)}
which represents the universal
p

Theorem C
.

(
4.12
,
4.15
)
If
R
is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring spectrum with nonzero
T(1)
-localization at
p=2
then
R
admits no complex orientation which is both 2-typical and representable by an
\mathbb{E}_{5}
map. Moreover, the Quillen idempotent at
p=3
is not representable by an
\mathbb{E}_{5}
-map.

The best previously known bounds on the commutativity of the Quillen idempotent are due to Lawson and Senger, who showed it is not
\mathbb{E}_{7}
at
p=2
and not
\mathbb{E}_{9}
at
p=3
, respectively (
[
Law18

[

).

A “sharp” construction and the Frobenius of
\mathrm{MU}

In this section we produce a formula for the complex orientation of
\mathrm{MU}^{tC_{p}}
induced by the Frobenius map
\mathrm{Fr}:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow\mathrm{MU}^{tC_{p}}
(
2.11
). Our approach is to present a more general construction for producing
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
maps from Thom spectra to Tate fixed-point objects, together with usable formulas for these maps (
2.4
). It is a generalization of the “sharp” construction of
[
CL25
, 5.11]
, which was itself inspired by (and named after) Ando-French-Ganter’s construction in
[
AFG08
]
.

2.1
The sharp construction

Let
G
be a compact Lie group and let
\mathscr{V}=\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{triv}}\oplus\overline{\mathscr{V}}
be a complex virtual representation of
G
such that
\overline{\mathscr{V}}
contains no trivial summands. Write
\mathcal{F}
for the family of subgroups
H
of
G
for which
\overline{\mathscr{V}}
does contain a trivial summand when restricted to
H
. For
\mathscr{C}\in\calg(\cat^{\mathrm{perf}})
, write
\mathscr{C}[\mathcal{F}]
for the thick tensor ideal generated by induced objects
X\otimes G/H
, with
X\in\mathscr{C}
,
H\in\mathcal{F}
, and
G
acting by translation on the
G/H
factor. Write

\mathrm{Nm}_{\mathcal{F}}:\mathscr{C}[\mathcal{F}]\rightarrow\mathscr{C}^{BG}

for the inclusion, and
T_{\mathcal{F}}
for the categorical Tate construction (Verdier quotient)

\mathbf{t}_{\mathcal{F}}:\mathscr{C}^{BG}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}:=\mathscr{C}^{BG}/\mathrm{Nm}_{\mathcal{F}}

which on unit objects recovers the classical Tate construction
(-)^{t_{\mathbf{F}}G}
(cf.
[
GM95b
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

For an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring
R
, we write

\mathrm{pic}^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}(R):=\mathrm{pic}(\Perf(R)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G})

Note that this is not the same as
\mathrm{pic}(R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G})
—indeed, we introduce these categories because, unlike
\Perf(R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G})
, the
\infty
-category
\Perf(R)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
receives the Tate fixed-point construction as a
strong symmetric monoidal
functor. However, to connect to complex orientation theory, we need to define “
j
-homomorphisms” for them. Let
\pi:BG\rightarrow\text{pt}
be the projection, and
\pi^{*}:\Perf(R)\rightarrow\Perf(R)^{BG}
the induced “trivial action” map.

Definition 2.2
.

Define the
j
-homomorphism of

\mathrm{pic}(R)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
to be the composite

\mathrm{bu}\xrightarrow{j_{R}}\mathrm{pic}(R)\xrightarrow{\pi^{*}}\mathrm{pic}(R)^{BG}\xrightarrow{T_{\mathcal{F}}}\mathrm{pic}(R)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}.

On objects, this sends
V\mapstochar\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{V}\otimes R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
, where
R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
refers to the unit of
\Perf(R)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
.

R

Lemma 2.3
.

The composite in Display (
1
), which sends
V\mapstochar\rightarrow(\mathbb{S}^{\mathscr{V}\otimes V}\otimes R)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
, is canonically homotopic to the
j
-homomorphism of
\mathrm{pic}(R)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
(cf.
2.2
).

Proof.

Because of the assumptions on
\overline{\mathscr{V}}
and
\mathcal{F}
, the representation sphere
\mathbb{S}^{\overline{\mathscr{V}}}\in\mathrm{Sp}^{BG}
built out of
G
-cells of the form
\mathbb{S}^{k}\times G/H
with
H\in\mathcal{F}
, and the same holds for
\mathbb{S}^{\overline{\mathscr{V}}\otimes V}
for a finite dimensional vector space
V
. Thus it satisfies

(\mathbb{S}^{\overline{\mathscr{V}}\otimes V})^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}=0,

so that the inclusion
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{triv}}\otimes V}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{\mathscr{V}\otimes V}
becomes an isomorphism after applying
(-)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
. Thus, the induced natural transformation

S^{V}\otimes R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}=(\mathbb{S}^{\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{triv}}\otimes V}\otimes R)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}\rightarrow(\mathbb{S}^{\mathscr{V}\otimes V}\otimes R)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}

is an isomorphism.
∎

Construction 2.4
.

Let
\omega:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R
be an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring map,
G
a compact Lie group and
\mathscr{V}
and
\mathcal{F}
as above. We will construct an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring map

\#_{\mathscr{V}}(\omega):\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}.

First we claim that it suffices to produce a nullhomotopy of Display (
1
). Indeed, note that the unit object of
\mathrm{pic}(R)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
is
R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
. Thus by
2.3
, the space of nullhomotopies of Display (
1
) is canonically isomorphic to the space of nullhomotopies of the
j
-homomorphism of
\mathrm{pic}(R)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
. By connectedness of
\mathrm{bu}
and the fact that the basepoint of
\mathrm{pic}(R)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
is
R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
, this is isomorphic to the space of nullhomotopies of the classical
j
-homomorphism
j_{R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}}:\mathrm{bu}\rightarrow\mathrm{pic}(R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G})
, i.e.
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring maps
\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
.

Finally, to produce our desired nullhomotopy: with a slight abuse of notation, let
\omega
also denote the nullhomotopy of
j_{R}:\mathrm{bu}\rightarrow\mathrm{pic}(R)
corresponding to the orientation
\omega
of
R
. Then the map
\#_{\mathscr{V}}(\omega)
corresponds to the nullhomotopy of Display (
1
) given by

\mathbf{t}_{\mathcal{F}}\circ\omega^{BG}\circ(\mathscr{V}\otimes(-)).

Remark 2.5
.

For comparison, in
[
CL25
, Section 5.1]
we focused on the special case of
G=\mathbb{T}
,
\mathscr{V}=1-L
for the standard representation
L
of
\mathbb{T}
, and
\mathcal{F}=\{1\}
. This has the added benefit that one can replace the domain of the first map of Display (
1
) with
\mathrm{ku}
(becaue
1-L
has virtual dimension 0), thereby producing
\mathrm{MUP}
-orientations out of
\mathrm{MU}
-orientations.

Remark 2.6
.

There are some obvious generalizations and variations. First, one can replace
\mathrm{bu}
by any
\mathrm{ku}
-module over
\mathrm{bu}
(for example, a highly connected cover
\mathrm{bu}\langle n\rangle
) and
\mathrm{MU}
by the corresponding Thom spectrum (for example,
\mathrm{MU}\langle n\rangle
). In an orthogonal direction, one can also replace
\mathrm{bu}
with

2.2
The Euler-Tate map

In this subsection we derive a formula for the “Hirzebruch series” of a map gotten via
2.4
.

Let
R
be a complex-orientable commutative ring spectrum. Then
\calg(\mathrm{MU},R)
is a torsor for
\mathrm{Map}(\mathrm{bu},\mathfrak{gl}_{1}R)
. Thus for every pair of
f,g\in\calg(\mathrm{MU},R)
there is a unique element
\frac{f}{g}\in\mathrm{Map}(\mathrm{bu},\mathfrak{gl}_{1}R)
such that

f=g\cdot\frac{f}{g}\in\calg(\mathrm{MU},R).

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\omega:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R,G,\mathscr{V}=\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{triv}}\oplus\overline{\mathscr{V}}
, and
\mathcal{F}
be such that
2.4
applies. Let
u:R\rightarrow R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
be the unit map, and
u_{*}\omega:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
the composite orientation. The
Euler-Tate map
of
\#_{\mathscr{V}}(\omega)
is defined to be

c^{\mathfrak{et}}_{\mathscr{V},\omega}:=\frac{\#_{\mathscr{V}}(\omega)}{u_{*}\omega}\in\mathrm{Map}(\mathrm{bu},\mathfrak{gl}_{1}R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}).

Thus the Euler-Tate map is what was classically referred to as a “stable exponential characteristic class” with values in
R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
. A priori it is only defined on rank 0 complex vector bundles. We extend to vector bundles
V\rightarrow X
of positive rank
n
by the formula

c^{\mathfrak{et}}_{\mathscr{V},\omega}(V):=c^{\mathfrak{et}}_{\mathscr{V},\omega}(V-n).

For a complex oriented ring
\omega:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow R
write
e_{\omega}(V)\in R^{n}(\mathbf{x})
for the euler class of
V
in the orientation
\omega
. The next proposition gives a formula for the Euler-Tate map of
\#_{\mathscr{V}}
as a ratio of euler classes.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
V\rightarrow X
be a rank
n
vector bundle over a finite complex
X
. Let
\overline{\mathscr{V}}
also denote the induced vector bundle over
BG
. Then

c^{\mathfrak{et}}_{\mathscr{V},\omega}(V)=\frac{e_{u_{*}\omega}(V\otimes\overline{\mathscr{V}})}{e_{u_{*}\omega}(\overline{\mathscr{V}})^{n}}\in\pi_{0}\mathrm{Map}(X,\mathfrak{gl}_{1}R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}).

Proof.

The characteristic class
c^{\mathfrak{et}}_{\mathscr{V},\omega}
is defined as the ratio of two complex orientations of
R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
. Thus
c^{\mathfrak{et}}_{\mathscr{V},\omega}(V)
is the ratio of the two Thom classes, or more precisely, the composite of one Thom isomorphism with the inverse of the other. Since
X
is finite we have
(R^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G})^{X}=(R^{X})^{t_{\mathcal{F}}G}
. Write
e_{\omega}(\mathcal{F})
for the collection of euler classes of
G
-representations
U
such that
U^{K}=\{0\}
for all subgroups
K
not in
\mathcal{F}
. By the Greenlees-May inversion formula (
[
GM95a
, 3.20]
)
the denominator Thom isomorphism
u_{*}\omega
is gotten from the
\omega
-Thom isomorphism for
V\rightarrow X\times BG
after inverting
e_{\omega}(\mathcal{F})
:

R^{*}(X\times BG)[e_{\omega}(\mathcal{F})^{-1}]\simeq R^{*+n}(\mathbb{S}^{V}\times BG)[e_{\omega}(\mathcal{F})^{-1}].

Similarly, the numerator isomorphism
\#_{\mathscr{V}}
is given composing the
e_{\omega}(\mathcal{F})
-inverted
\omega
-Thom isomorphism

R^{*}(X\times BG)[e_{\omega}(\mathcal{F})^{-1}]\simeq R^{*+n}(\mathbb{S}^{V\otimes\mathscr{V}})[e_{\omega}(\mathcal{F})^{-1}]

with the restriction along the zero section
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{triv}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{\mathscr{V}}

R^{*+n}(\mathbb{S}^{V}\times BG)[e_{\omega}(\mathcal{F})^{-1}]

Thus the relevant composite of these two Thom isomorphisms is precisely the
u_{*}\omega
-euler class of
V\otimes\overline{\mathscr{V}}
. The denominator of
e_{u_{*}\omega}(\overline{\mathscr{V}})^{n}
is acquired since one must first shift
V
to be rank zero by subtracting a rank
n
trivial bundle.
∎

2.3
The complex oriented Frobenius

In this section we illustrate how
2.4
and
2.8
can be used to obtain a formula for the Frobenius homomorphism of
\mathrm{MU}
.

Recall that for any
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring
R
and prime
p
Nikolaus and Scholze (cf.
[
NS18
, IV.1]
) define a
Frobenius homomorphism

\mathrm{Fr}:R\rightarrow R^{tC_{p}}

where
(-)^{tC_{p}}
is taken with respect to the trivial action. The Frobenius is natural in
R
, and thus encodes quite a lot of the
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring structure of
R
—in particular, it encodes much of the data of the power operations of
R
(cf.
[
NS18
, IV.1.21]
). It is thus very favorable to have formulas for the Frobenius homomorphism. As it turns out, the Frobenius is a special case of the
\#
construction.

Let
\rho
be the regular representation of
C_{p}
. We may apply
2.4
with
G=C_{p}
,
\mathscr{V}=\rho
, and
\mathcal{F}=\{1\}
(so that
(-)^{t_{\mathcal{F}}C_{p}}=(-)^{tC_{p}}
) to obtain an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring map

\#_{\rho}:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow\mathrm{MU}^{tC_{p}}.

Theorem 2.9
.

\#_{\rho}
and
\mathrm{Fr}
are homotopic as
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
ring maps.

Proof.

Consider the following diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{bu}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{bu}^{BC_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{pic}(\mathbb{S})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{pic}(\mathbb{S})^{BC_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{pic}(\mathrm{MU})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{pic}(\mathrm{MU})^{BC_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{pic}(\mathrm{MU})^{tC_{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes\rho=(-)^{\oplus p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\otimes p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)_{\mathrm{MU}}^{\otimes p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}

First note that both columns are canonically null, via nullhomotopies we will call
\omega_{\mathrm{id}}
(left column) and
\omega_{\mathrm{id}}^{BC_{p}}
(right column). By functoriality the rectangle commutes up to canonical homotopy. That is, the two outer paths of the rectangle
\mathrm{bu}\rightarrow\mathrm{pic}(\mathrm{MU})^{BC_{p}}
are canonically homotopic
and
the nullhomotopies of each path (given by the relevant pre/post compositions of
\omega_{\mathrm{id}}
and
\omega_{\mathrm{id}}^{BC_{p}}
) are homotopic.

By
[
Car23
, Corollary 2.14]
on unit objects the bottom horizontal composite is homotopic to the Frobenius

to the nullhomotopy of the “L”-shaped path from top left to bottom right given by the canonical nullhomotopy of the left column. By what we have concluded about the rectangle, this is homotopic to the nullhomotopy of “Z”-shaped composite from top left to bottom right given by the canonical nullhomotopy of the right column, which is the orientation corresponding to
\#_{\rho}
by definition.
∎

\rho_{k,\ell,\mathrm{MU}}^{*}(\mathbf{x})=[k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{x})+_{\mathbf{F}}[\ell]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{y}).

\rho_{k,\ell,A}^{*}(x)=[k]_{F}(x)+_{F}[\ell]_{F}(y).

Since
\phi
is a morphism of spectra, it satisfies
\phi\circ\rho_{k,\ell,\mathrm{MU}}^{*}=\rho_{k,\ell,A}^{*}\circ\phi
. Hence we get

\phi([k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{x})+_{\mathbf{F}}[\ell]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{y}))=\phi\circ\rho_{k,\ell,\mathrm{MU}}^{*}(\mathbf{x})=\rho_{k,\ell,A}^{*}f(x)=f(\rho_{k,\ell,A}^{*}(x))=f([k]_{F}(x)+_{F}[\ell]_{\mathbf{F}}(y)).

∎

If

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbf{x}
denotes the universal coordinate on

We must show that

for the power series expressing

\mathbf{x}

\psi_{p}\circ\phi(\mathbf{x})-\phi^{t\mathbb{T}}\circ\psi_{p}(\mathbf{x})=0.

Now

\psi_{p}(f)\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{\mathbf{x}+_{\mathbf{F}}[k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{t})}{[k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{t})}\right)-f(\mathbf{x})\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{f(\mathbf{x}+_{\mathbf{F}}[k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{t}))}{f([k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{t}))}=0.

\displaystyle\mathrm{MU}_{*}=

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{Z}[b_{1},b_{2},...]

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x})=

\displaystyle\ \mathbf{x}+f_{1}\mathbf{x}^{2}+f_{2}\mathbf{x}^{3}+...

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{x}):=

\displaystyle\ \psi_{2}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}+g_{1}(\mathbf{t})\mathbf{x}^{2}+g_{2}(\mathbf{t})\mathbf{x}^{3}+...

4

\mathbb{E}_{n}
orientations

In this section we us the formula of
2.11
to construct a general obstruction theory for
\mathbb{E}_{n}
complex orientations of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
rings. When applied to

]

4.17

Recall that the logarithm of the universal formal group law

is the power series

\log_{u}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}+m_{1}\frac{\mathbf{x}^{2}}{2}+m_{2}\frac{\mathbf{x}^{3}}{3}+m_{3}\frac{\mathbf{x}^{4}}{4}+m_{4}\frac{\mathbf{x}^{5}}{5}+...

[

classified by a ring map
\gamma:\mathrm{MU}_{*}\rightarrow R_{*}

to the coefficients of

is called

Notation 4.1
.

Write
\rho
for the regular representation of

An immediate consequence of the above discussion is the following:

Corollary 4.5
.

In the settings of

The first step in making this computationally useful is the following.

Proposition 4.6
.

In the setting of

, identify
\mathfrak{jn}^{\phi}_{d}
and
\mathfrak{o}^{\phi}
with series

\mathfrak{jn}^{\phi}_{d}(t)\in A_{*}[\![t]\!]/([p]_{F}(t))[-2dp]

(p,t^{\lfloor\frac{(n-1)(p-1)}{2}\rfloor+1})

Proof.

We must investigate the restriction map

Using the orientation

to trivialize

A^{*}(BC_{p}\times\mathbb{CP}^{\infty})\rightarrow A^{*}(\mathrm{Conf}(p,\mathbb{R}^{n})_{hC_{p}}\times\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}).

To finish the proof, it suffices to show that this map detects the mod
p

for

By
[

the restriction map on mod

\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}(BC_{p}\times\mathbb{CP}^{\infty})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}(\mathrm{Conf}(p,\mathbb{R}^{n})_{hC_{p}}\times\mathbb{CP}^{\infty})

for

p
residue of these elements. Since

-cohomology also detects the mod
p

∎

Now that we have a computable vanishing condition on the series

\mathrm{Fr}^{\mathrm{MU}}(\mathbf{x})

-typical and
\mathbb{E}_{2p+3}
.

Remark 4.14
.

We obtain as an immediate corollary that the Quillen idempotent at

is not
\mathbb{E}_{5}
(and not
E_{2p+3}
at
p=3,...,13
). The best previously known bound is due to Senger, who showed it is not
\mathbb{E}_{7}
(and matches his bound of
\mathbb{E}_{2p+3}
at

) (
[

Now we showcase that the obstruction theory using

can do a bit better.

Theorem 4.15
.

The Quillen idempotent at
p=3
is not
\mathbb{E}_{5}
.

Proof.

Consider the (3-localized) Todd orientation
\mathrm{Td}:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow\mathrm{ku}_{(3)}
. This is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
orientation (cf. e.g.
[
Joa04
]
,
[
AHR10
, Theorem 10.3]
). Thus, it suffice to show that composite
\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow\mathrm{MU}_{(3)}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Td}}\mathrm{ku}_{(3)}
classifying the
3
-typification of the Todd orientation is not
\mathbb{E}_{5}
. Call this composite
\phi
.

We have
(\mathrm{ku}_{(3)})_{*}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{(3)}[\beta]
with
|\beta|=2
, and the formal group law associated to the Todd orientation is given by

x+_{\mathrm{Td}}y=x+y-\beta xy,

the 3-series is given by

[3]_{\mathrm{Td}}(t)=3t-3\beta t^{2}+\beta^{2}t^{3},

and the logarithm is

\mathrm{log}_{\mathrm{Td}}(x)=x+\beta\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\beta^{2}\frac{x^{3}}{3}+\beta^{3}\frac{x^{4}}{4}+\beta^{4}\frac{x^{5}}{5}+...

The logarithm of the 3-typification is

\log_{\phi}(x)=x+\beta\frac{x^{3}}{3}+\beta^{3}\frac{x^{9}}{9}+...

Written in terms of the Todd coordinate,
f(x)=\phi(\mathbf{x})
becomes

f(x):=\exp_{\phi}\circ\log_{\mathrm{Td}}(x)=x+\frac{\beta}{2}x^{2}-\frac{\beta^{3}}{4}x^{4}-\frac{\beta^{4}}{20}x^{5}+....

Finally, we have
\mathrm{Fr}(\beta^{k})=3^{k}\beta^{k}
by
[
NS18
, IV.1.12]
. Using these facts, we can explicitly expand the expression in
4.8
. If
\phi
is an
\mathbb{E}_{5}
map, then upon long division of that expression by
\langle 3\rangle_{\mathrm{Td}}(t)
, the remainder must be
O(t^{5})
. With computer assistance (
Section

6
) we apply this to the coefficient of
x^{9}
. After expanding, the coefficient of
x^{9}
is

-\frac{1215\beta^{3}}{8t^{3}}+\frac{15957\beta^{4}}{32t^{2}}+\frac{1041093\beta^{5}}{64t}-\frac{15676713\beta^{6}}{448}+...

...+\frac{8826507\beta^{7}t}{2240}+\frac{304379169\beta^{8}t^{2}}{17920}-\frac{334416123\beta^{9}t^{3}}{35840}-\frac{37570767\beta^{10}t^{4}}{6400}

and find that after long division by
3-3\beta t+\beta^{2}t^{2}
the remainder modulo 3 is
\beta^{10}t^{4}
.
∎

Remark 4.16
.

Recall that
\mathrm{KU}^{tC_{p}}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})[\beta^{\pm 1}]
, under which the class
t
maps to
\zeta_{p}-1
. Accordingly, the above computation can be interpreted as a computation in the power series ring
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})[\![x]\!]
, where the condition of being
0
mod
t^{4}
translates to the
p
-adic valuation of the resulting expression being
\geq 2
.

Remark 4.17
.

The obstruction theory with
\mathfrak{o}^{\phi}
has the following benefits over the one using
\mathfrak{jn}^{\phi}
and the Johnson-Noel calculations: first, it does not require
\phi
to be
p
-typical. Second, because of the formula

P_{\infty}^{\mathrm{MU}}(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}[k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{t})\cdot\mathrm{Fr}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\mathbf{x}+_{\mathbf{F}}[k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{t}),

which pushes forward nicely under arbitrary complex orientations, we are able to compose the Quillen idempotent with a map
\mathrm{MU}_{(p)}\rightarrow\mathrm{ku}_{(p)}
and thus make all our computations in the ground ring
(\mathrm{ku}_{(p)})_{*}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\beta]
. In contrast, there is no such formula for
P_{\infty}^{\mathrm{MU}}[\mathbb{CP}^{d}]
, and so Johnson-Noel are left with a problem of much higher computational complexity since the ground ring is
(\mathrm{MU}_{(p)})_{*}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[b_{1},b_{2},...]
.
We expect that one can find obstructions in a larger range of primes because of this. On the other hand, in the range where Johnson-Noel do find obstructions, their formulas are much stronger than a mere obstruction to the Quillen idempotent, as they occur in a universal ring.

We end this section by outlining a streamlined procedure to calculate Johnson-Noel’s elements
P_{\infty}^{\mathrm{MU}}([\mathbb{CP}^{d}])
using the Frobenius formula of
2.11
.

Consider the logarithm of the formal group law corresponding to the orientation
\mathrm{Fr}:\mathrm{MU}\rightarrow\mathrm{MU}^{tC_{p}}
. On the one hand, as mentioned at the beginning of this section, the logarithm pushes forward under ring maps:

\log_{\mathrm{Fr}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{Fr}_{*}(\log_{u}(\mathbf{x})):=\mathbf{x}+\mathrm{Fr}(m_{1})\frac{\mathbf{x}^{2}}{2}+\mathrm{Fr}(m_{2})\frac{\mathbf{x}^{3}}{3}+\mathrm{Fr}(m_{3})\frac{\mathbf{x}^{4}}{4}+\mathrm{Fr}(m_{4})\frac{\mathbf{x}^{5}}{5}+...

On the other hand, we know that the power series

f_{\mathrm{Fr}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{\mathbf{x}+_{\mathbf{F}}[k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{t})}{[k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{t})}

is the coordinate transformation relating the Frobenius orientation and the unit orientation, so

\log_{\mathrm{Fr}}(\mathbf{x})=\log_{u}\circ f_{\mathrm{Fr}}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}).

Since
m_{i}=[\mathbb{CP}^{i}]
, the elements
P_{\infty}^{\mathrm{MU}}([\mathbb{CP}^{d}])=\left(\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}[k]_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{t})\right)^{d}\cdot\mathrm{Fr}([\mathbb{CP}^{d}])
can be extracted from the coefficients of this composition of power series.

5.1

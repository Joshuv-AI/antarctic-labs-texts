Counting elliptic curves with prescribed entanglements
Zachary Couvillon, Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. We establish asymptotic lower bounds for the number of elliptic curves over ℚ \mathbb{Q} with prescribed entanglement of division fields, ordered by naive height. Such elliptic curves are obtained as 1 1 -parameter families arising from certain genus 0 0 modular curves. We apply techniques from the geometry of numbers and sieve methods to prove that the number of elliptic curves with unexplained entanglements ℚ ⁡ ( E ⁡ [ 2 ] ) ∩ ℚ ⁡ ( E ⁡ [ 3 ] ) ≠ ℚ \mathbb{Q}(E[2])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[3])\neq\mathbb{Q} and ℚ ⁡ ( E ⁡ [ 2 ] ) ∩ ℚ ⁡ ( E ⁡ [ 5 ] ) ≠ ℚ \mathbb{Q}(E[2])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[5])\neq\mathbb{Q} and naive height ≤ X \leq X , grows as ≫ X 1 / 9 \gg X^{1/9} and ≫ X 1 / 12 \gg X^{1/12} , respectively.

1.
Introduction

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with torsion subgroup
E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}
of the Mordell–Weil group. Mazur famously showed that there are precisely 15 possible torsion subgroups for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
, namely

(1.1)

\begin{split}\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}&\quad\text{for }1\leq N\leq 10\text{ or }N=12,\\
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}&\quad\text{for }N=2,4,6,8.\end{split}

Each of these groups arises infinitely often, as they correspond to the cases where the associated modular curves have genus
0
. A natural question is how frequently these groups occur when elliptic curves are ordered by their
naive height
. Every elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
admits a unique Weierstrass equation of the form

y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B,

where
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\gcd(A^{3},B^{2})
is not divisible by any twelfth power. The naive height of
E
is defined by

\operatorname{ht}(E)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max(4|A|^{3},27|B|^{2}).

For any group
G
appearing in (
1.1
), Harron and Snowden
[
5
]
showed that

\#\{E/\mathbb{Q}\mid\operatorname{ht}(E)\leq X,\,E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}\simeq G\}\gg X^{1/d(G)},

where
d(G)>0
is explicitly given in Table 1 of
loc. cit.
Similar results were obtained by Cullinan, Kenny, and Voight
[
2
]
. The case
G=0
follows from work of Duke
[
4
]
, who showed that almost all elliptic curves have no
exceptional primes
.

Let
\rho_{E,n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(E[n])\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})

denote the Galois representation on the
n
-torsion of
E
. The elliptic curve
E
admits a cyclic
n
-isogeny precisely when (for a suitable choice of basis for
E[n]
) the image of
\rho_{E,n}
consists of upper triangular matrices. Asymptotics for the number of elliptic curves for prescribed
n
-cyclic isogeny have been studied using the parametrization from the modular curve
Y_{0}(n)
, which has genus
0
for
1\leq n\leq 10
and
n=12,13,16,18,25
. The study of the asymptotic for

\#\{E_{/\mathbb{Q}}\mid\rho_{E,n}\text{ is reducible and }H(E)\leq X\}

as
X\rightarrow\infty
has garnered significant interest.
For
n=3
, an exact asymptotic formula is established by Pizzo–Pomerance–Voight
[
9
]
, and for
n=4
by Pomerance–Schaefer
[
10
]
. For
n=7,10,13,
and
25
, Boggess–Sankar
[
1
]
obtain asymptotic lower bounds, while for
n=7
, Molnar–Voight
[
8
]
obtain the precise asymptotic.

The action of
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
on the Tate module
T(E)=\varprojlim_{n}E[n]
induces an adelic Galois representation

\widehat{\rho}_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(T(E))\cong\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}).

Understanding the image of
\widehat{\rho}_{E}
is a central problem in what is known as Mazur’s Program B, following
[
7
]
which seeks to classify all Galois images for elliptic curves. As part of this program, it is natural to study
entanglements
of division fields.

Let
a
and
b
be positive integers with
d=\gcd(a,b)
. It is easy to see that

\mathbb{Q}(E[a])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[b])\supseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[d]).

An
(a,b)
-entanglement occurs when this containment is strict, i.e.,

K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}(E[a])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[b])\neq\mathbb{Q}(E[d]).

An entanglement is said to be
unexplained
if it cannot be explained by the Weil pairing on
E
, for further details, see Definition
2.3
. Such entanglements are mysterious and their properties are of significant interest. The
type
of the entanglement is the isomorphism class of
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}(E[d]))
. Given an unexplained entanglement
\mathfrak{T}
, specified by a tuple
(a,b)
and a group
G
, let
N_{\mathfrak{T}}(X)
denote the number of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with
\operatorname{ht}(E)\leq X
exhibiting
\mathfrak{T}
as an entanglement (i.e.
E
has an
(a,b)
-entanglement of type
G
). Below is the main result of this article.

Theorem A

(Theorem
4.4
and
4.7
)
.

With respect to the notation above, the following assertions hold:

(1)

N_{\left((2,3),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)}(X)\gg X^{1/9}
;

(2)

N_{\left((2,5),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)}(X)\gg X^{1/12}.

We now provide a brief yet comprehensive overview of the method of proof. Building on the results of
[
3
]
, we exploit the existence of explicit families of elliptic curves of the form
y^{2}=x^{3}+f(t)x+g(t)
with prescribed unexplained entanglement. Let
\mathcal{F}_{1}
denote the family of elliptic curves for which:

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-3t^{2}-36)c(t),

\displaystyle g(t)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(2t^{5}+72t^{3}+864t)c(t),

\displaystyle c(t)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\gcd(f(t),g(t))=t^{4}+36t^{2}+432,

and let
\mathcal{F}_{2}
denote the family defined by:

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-3t^{4}-30t^{2}-15)c(t),

\displaystyle g(t)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=2(t^{4}+4t^{2}-1)(t^{4}+22t^{2}+125)c(t),

\displaystyle c(t)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\gcd(f(t),g(t))=t^{4}+22t^{2}+125.

Setting
\mathfrak{T}_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=((2,3),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathfrak{T}_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=((2,5),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
, the family
\mathcal{F}_{i}
consists of elliptic curves exhibiting the entanglement
\mathfrak{T}_{i}
. Let
A(u,v)
,
B(u,v)
, and
C(u,v)
be nonconstant homogeneous polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[u,v]
.

Assumption 1.1
.

We assume that:

(i)

The degrees of
A
and
B
are related by the equality
3\deg A=2\deg B
. We set
d\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=3\deg A=2\deg B
.

(ii)

The polynomials
A(u,v)
and
B(u,v)
have no common real roots in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
.

(iii)

The discriminant is non-vanishing, i.e.,
4A^{3}+27B^{2}\neq 0
.

(iv)

The greatest common divisor of
A(u,v)
and
B(u,v)
, denoted by
C(u,v)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\gcd(A,B)
, is a non-constant, squarefree polynomial that is not divisible by
u
or
v
. Moreover, its degree satisfies
r\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\deg(C)\leq 4
.

In our applications, the polynomials
A
,
B
, and
C
will be the homogenizations of
f
,
g
, and
c
, respectively. Consider the family of elliptic curves with minimal Weierstrass model given by
\mathcal{E}_{a,b}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=E_{A(a,b),B(a,b)}
. Defining

\mathcal{C}(X)=\left\{\mathcal{E}_{a,b}\mid\mathcal{E}_{a,b}\text{ is minimal and }\operatorname{ht}(\mathcal{E}_{a,b})\leq X\right\},

we establish the following general result.

Theorem B

(Theorem
3.11
)
.

With respect to the notation above, we have

\#\mathcal{C}(X)\sim\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R})X^{2/d},

where
\mathcal{R}
is the bounded region defined by

\mathcal{R}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid v>0,4|A(u,v)|^{3}\leq 1\;\text{and }\;27|B(u,v)|^{2}\leq 1\},

and
\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\in(0,1]
is an explicit constant satisfying

\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{(r+\ell)}{\ell^{8}}\right)

for all but finitely many primes.

The product
\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}
is non-vanishing via a straightforward argument, see Remark
4.5
. The proof of this result combines analytic techniques, sieve methods, and an application of Davenport’s lemma from the geometry of numbers. The overall approach differs significantly from those in previous works, although it bears some resemblance to the methods of Harron–Snowden and Molnar–Voight. The key distinction between our technique and that of Harron and Snowden lies in the fact that, in our setting, the polynomials
f(t)
and
g(t)
share a non-trivial common factor. In contrast, the method of Molnar and Voight applies only to a more restricted situation and does not immediately extend to the generality of Theorem B. Moreover, the geometry of numbers framework we adopt is considerably more flexible, as it could naturally adapt to settings involving several variables and higher-dimensional parameter spaces, whereas analytic techniques relying on iterated sums and sieve-theoretic estimates would soon become much more complicated.

Theorem
A
is then deduced by applying Theorem
B
to the families of elliptic curves
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
. However, our approach does not yield a precise asymptotic formula, as the families
\mathcal{F}_{i}
do not encompass all elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with entanglement type
\mathfrak{T}_{i}
. This incompleteness arises from the fact that the parametrization in terms of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
excludes certain subfamilies that arise from quadratic twists. Thus, we are not counting all
\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphism classes of elliptic curves with
j
-invariants given in
[
3
]
.

A crucial aspect of our method is the assumption that the polynomial
c(t)
has no real roots. This condition is essential in the application of Davenport’s lemma, as it guarantees that the region
\mathcal{R}
appearing in the geometry-of-numbers argument remains bounded. When
c(t)
admits real roots, the region becomes unbounded, rendering the application of Davenport’s lemma ineffective. Consequently, this restriction prevents us from extending our strategy to certain other entanglement families, such as those arising in the parametrization of Morrow and Daniels, where the geometry of the corresponding regions is more complicated.

Nevertheless, the results of this article offer new insights into the arithmetic statistics of elliptic curves, and we expect that they will stimulate further investigation into related families and their distribution. In particular, it would be interesting to determine whether a modification of our sieve-theoretic framework could be adapted to cover the missing families, potentially leading to a more comprehensive asymptotic count for entangled elliptic curves.

2.
Galois images and entanglements

Throughout this article, we shall fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
and let
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
denote the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
. Let
E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Given
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, let
\rho_{E,n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
be the Galois representation associated to
E[n]
. Denote by
G_{E,n}
the image of
\rho_{E,n}
and
\mathbb{Q}(E[n])\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\rho_{E,n}}
. The field
\mathbb{Q}(E[n])
is called the
n
-division field of
E
. The map
\rho_{E,n}
gives us a natural identification of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[n])/\mathbb{Q})
with the Galois image
G_{E,n}
. Let
\chi_{n}
be the mod-
n
cyclotomic character. As a consequence of the Weil–pairing,
\det\rho_{E,n}=\chi_{n}
and therefore,
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{n})
is contained in
\mathbb{Q}(E[n])
. If
E
is a non-CM elliptic curve then Serre’s open image theorem
[
11
]
asserts that the index
d_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{image}\widehat{\rho}_{E}]
is finite. Serre moreover showed that
d_{E}
is always divisible by
2
and that, in fact, the image of
\widehat{\rho}_{E}
is contained in a prescribed index-two subgroup
H_{E}
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. Elliptic curves for which
d_{E}=2
are called Serre curves. Building upon previous work of Duke
[
4
]
, Jones
[
6
]
showed that when ordered by
height
, most elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
are Serre curves. Serre curves have the property that for every prime
\ell
, the Galois representation
\rho_{E,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
is surjective.

The image of
\widehat{\rho}_{E}
could be strictly smaller than
H_{E}
in various ways. The first arises from the existence of an
exceptional
prime, i.e., a prime
\ell
for which the Galois representation
\rho_{E,\ell}
is not surjective. For non-exceptional primes
\ell
, the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell])/\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
. A conjecture of Serre
[
12
, p. 399]
and Zywina
[
13
]
predicts that if
E
is a non-CM elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, then there are no exceptional primes
\ell>37
. In fact, Zywina provides a more refined conjecture
[
14
, Conjecture 1.1]
describing precisely when
\rho_{E,\ell}
is surjective, along with a practical algorithm for computing the image. Another obstruction to surjectivity arises from the presence of an entanglement, as defined in the introduction.

These entanglements are further subdivided into two categories: explained and unexplained. Let us illustrate the difference through an example. Let
\Delta_{E}
denote the minimal discriminant of
E
, and assume that
\Delta_{E}
is not a square. It is straightforward to verify that
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta_{E}})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
. Now, choose
m>2
such that
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta_{E}})
is contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{m})
. Since
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{m})
is itself contained in
\mathbb{Q}(E[m])
, it follows that

\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta_{E}})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[2])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[m]).

If the minimal such
m
is odd, we obtain a
(2,m)
-entanglement of type
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. When
m
is even, one instead finds either a
(4,m/4)
-entanglement of type
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, arising from

\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{-\Delta_{E}}\right)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[4])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[m/4]),

or an
(8,m/8)
-entanglement of type
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, arising from

\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{\pm 2\Delta_{E}}\right)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[8])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[m/8]).

These are referred to as
explained entanglements
, following Daniels and Morrow
[
3
]
. Let
E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}-x^{2}-1033x-12438
with Cremona label
100a3
. Then
[
3
, Example 1.3 on p. 829]
shows that
\mathbb{Q}(E[3])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[2])=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
. In this case, there is an entanglement
((2,3),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
which cannot be explained by the Weil pairing or the Kronecker–Weber theorem. Such entanglements are more interesting and called
unexplained entanglements
. We give a formal definition later in this section.

Definition 2.1
.

A subgroup
G
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
is said to be
admissible
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

G
is a proper subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
, i.e.,
G\neq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
,

(b)

The determinant map induces an equality
\operatorname{det}(G)=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
,

(c)

G
contains an element with trace
0
and determinant
-1
that fixes a point in
(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{2}
of exact order
N
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
the mod-
N
Galois representation is not surjective. Then
\rho_{E,N}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is an admissible subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
, see
[
13
, Proposition 2.2]
for further details. Let
G
be a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
for some
n\geq 2
whose determinant map is surjective. Consider two divisors
a
and
b
of
n
with
a<b
, and define
c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)
and
d\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{gcd}(a,b)
. Let
\pi_{c}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z})
be the canonical reduction map, and set
G_{c}=\pi_{c}(G)
. We then introduce the following reduction maps and associated normal subgroups of

:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\pi_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z}),&N_{a}=\ker(\pi_{a})\cap G_{c},\\
\pi_{b}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/b\mathbb{Z}),&N_{b}=\ker(\pi_{b})\cap G_{c},\\
\pi_{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}),&N_{d}=\ker(\pi_{d})\cap G_{c}.\end{array}

Definition 2.2
.

With respect to notation above, we say that
G
represents an
(a,b)
-entanglement if

\left\langle N_{a},N_{b}\right\rangle\subsetneq N_{d}.
The type of the entanglement is the isomorphism type of the group
N_{d}/\left\langle N_{a},N_{b}\right\rangle
.

Consider the set

\mathcal{T}_{G}=\{((a,b),H)\mid G\text{ represents an }(a,b)\text{-entanglement of type }H\}

and declare that

\left(\left(a_{1},b_{1}\right),H_{1}\right)\leqslant\left(\left(a_{2},b_{2}\right),H_{2}\right)

if one of the following conditions hold.

(a)

The groups
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are isomorphic, and either
a_{2}
divides
a_{1}
and
b_{2}
divides
b_{1}
, or
b_{2}
divides
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
divides
b_{1}
.

(b)

The group
H_{1}
is isomorphic to a quotient of
H_{2}
, and either
a_{1}
divides
a_{2}
and
b_{1}
divides
b_{2}
, or
b_{1}
divides
a_{2}
and
a_{1}
divides
b_{2}
.

One says that
G
represents a
primitive

(a,b)
-entanglement of type
H
if
((a,b),H)
is the unique maximal element of
\mathcal{T}_{G}
and
n=\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)
.

Definition 2.3
.

A group
G
exhibits an explained
(a,b)
-entanglement of type
T
if it satisfies the conditions for a primitive
(a,b)
-entanglement of type
T

\left[(\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{det}\left(N_{a,b}(G)\right)\right]=\left[G_{c}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}N_{a,b}(G)\right]

holds. Equivalently, this occurs when the kernel

of

is contained in

.

In contrast,

-entanglement of type

(a,b)
-entanglement of type

but the above equality fails.

Theorem 2.4
.

[

There exist precisely nine pairs

, where

3.
Density results

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve defined by a Weierstrass equation in
short form
:

E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B,

where
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}
. The
height
of its Weierstrass model is defined by
\operatorname{H}(A,B)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max\left\{|4A^{3}|,|27B^{2}|\right\}
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
minimality defect

\operatorname{md}(A,B)
is the largest
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
d^{4}\mid A
and
d^{6}\mid B
, and the
height
of
E
is

\operatorname{ht}(E)=\frac{H(A,B)}{\operatorname{md}(A,B)^{12}}.

Each elliptic curve has a unique
globally minimal model
, i.e., a model for which
\operatorname{md}(A,B)=1
.

Choose non-constant homogeneous polynomials
A(u,v),B(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}[u,v]
satisfying the conditions of Assumption
1.1
. Given a homogeneous polynomial
F(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}[u,v]
, we write it in the form

F(u,v)=\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{t}(u-\alpha_{i}v)^{n_{i}}

as a product of linear factors, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
. By convention, we interpret
(u-\infty v)
as
v
.

According to condition (ii) above,
C(u,v)
has no roots in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Assume therefore upon replacing
C(u,v)
with
-C(u,v)
if necessary that
C(u,v)\geq 0
for all
(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We write
A(u,v)=C(u,v)A_{0}(u,v)
and
B(u,v)=C(u,v)B_{0}(u,v)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the set of all pairs of integers
(a,b)
with
b>0
. For
(a,b)\in\mathcal{F}
, define

H(a,b)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H(A(a,b),B(a,b))\quad\text{and}\quad m(a,b)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{md}(A(a,b),B(a,b)).

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
denote the subset consisting of pairs
(a,b)
satisfying:

(1)

\gcd(a,b)=1
,

(2)

4A(a,b)^{3}+27B(a,b)^{2}\neq 0
,

(3)

m(a,b)=1
.

For each
(a,b)\in\mathcal{C}
, define the elliptic curve

\mathcal{E}_{a,b}=E_{A(a,b),B(a,b)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+A(a,b)x+B(a,b).

Note that

H(a,b)=H(\mathcal{E}_{a,b})\quad\text{and}\quad m(a,b)=\operatorname{md}(\mathcal{E}_{a,b})=1.

Since
m(a,b)=1
, the curve
\mathcal{E}_{a,b}
is globally minimal.

Given a subset
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and a positive real number
X
, define

\mathcal{S}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(a,b)\in\mathcal{S}\mid H(a,b)\leq X\}.

In particular, the set
\mathcal{C}(X)
is identified with the set of globally minimal elliptic curves of the form
\mathcal{E}_{a,b}=E_{A(a,b),B(a,b)}
of height at most
X
, namely

\mathcal{C}(X)=\{\mathcal{E}_{a,b}\mid\mathcal{E}_{a,b}\text{ is minimal and }H(\mathcal{E}_{a,b})\leq X\}.

Let
\mathcal{R}(X)
denote the region
\mathcal{R}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid v>0,H(u,v)\leq X\}
,
and set
\mathcal{R}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{R}(1)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

With respect to notation above, the set
\mathcal{R}(X)
is compact and
\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R}(X))=X^{2/d}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathcal{R}(X)
is closed; we show that
\mathcal{R}(X)
is bounded. We write
A(u,v)=c_{A}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{A}}(u-\alpha v)^{n_{\alpha}}
and
B(u,v)=c_{B}\prod_{\alpha\in R_{B}}(u-\alpha v)^{m_{\alpha}}
, where
R_{A}
(resp.
R_{B}
) are the roots of
A
(resp.
B
). Assume without loss of generality that
X\geq 27
. For
(u,v)\in\mathcal{R}(X)
one has that
|A(u,v)|\leq X_{1}
and
|B(u,v)|\leq X_{2}
where
X_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(X/4)^{1/3}
and
X_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(X/27)^{1/2}
. Note that
X_{1}\geq 1
and
X_{2}\geq 1
. Given
z=(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
set
|z|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{max}\{|u|,|v|\}
and suppose by way of contradiction that
\mathcal{R}(X)
is unbounded. Then, there is an infinite sequence
z_{n}=(u_{n},v_{n})\in\mathcal{R}(X)
such that
|z_{n}|\rightarrow\infty
. Since
|A(u_{n},v_{n})|\leq X_{1}
and
X_{1}\geq 1
, it follows that there is a root
\alpha
of
A(u,v)
such that
|u_{n}-\alpha v_{n}|\leq X_{1}
.

First, consider the case when for infinitely many values of
n
,
|v_{n}|\geq|u_{n}|
. Upon passing to a subsequence, assume without loss of generality that for all
n
,
|v_{n}|\geq|u_{n}|
. In particular,
|v_{n}|\rightarrow\infty
. Assume that
\alpha\neq\infty
, then we have that

|u_{n}/v_{n}-\alpha|\leq X_{1}/|v_{n}|.

Therefore, for the root
\alpha
of
A(u,v)
, we have that
|u_{n}/v_{n}-\alpha|\rightarrow 0
. In particular,
\alpha
must be a real number. The same reasoning shows that there is a real root
\beta
of
B(u,v)
such that
|u_{n}/v_{n}-\beta|\rightarrow 0
. Being the limit of the same sequence, we get that
\alpha=\beta
. Since it is assumed that
A
and
B
have no common real root, this gives a contradiction. If
\alpha=\infty
, we find that
|v_{n}|\leq X_{1}
, which is a contradiction since
|v_{n}|\rightarrow\infty
.

Consider now the case when all but finitely many values of
n
,
|u_{n}|\geq|v_{n}|
. Assume without loss of generality that
|u_{n}|\geq|v_{n}|
for all
n
. The symmetric argument with variables
u
and
v
interchanged gives a contradiction. Therefore, we have shown that
\mathcal{R}(X)
is compact.

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{R}(X)=X^{1/d}\cdot\mathcal{R}
. Thus,
\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R}(X))=X^{2/d}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R})
. This completes the proof.
∎

We recall the Principle of Lipschitz, which is also known as Davenport’s lemma.

Lemma 3.3

(Principle of Lipschitz/ Davenport’s lemma)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded region with a rectifiable boundary
\partial\mathcal{R}
. Then, the number of integer lattice points contained in
\mathcal{R}
satisfies

\#(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2})=\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R})+O(\operatorname{len}(\partial\mathcal{R})),

where the implicit constant depends only on the similarity class of
\mathcal{R}
and is independent of its size, orientation, or location in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
together imply that

(3.1)

\#\mathcal{F}(X)\sim X^{2/d}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R}).

Theorem 3.4
.

For all
(a,b)\in\mathcal{F}
, we have

(3.2)

c_{1}C(a,b)^{d/r}\leq H(a,b)\leq c_{2}C(a,b)^{d/r},

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are positive constants.

Proof.

The function
f(a,b)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{H(a,b)}{C(a,b)^{d/r}}
is real-valued. Since
C(a,b)\geq 0
, and
f(a,b)
is homogeneous of degree
0
, there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

c_{1}\leq f(a,b)\leq c_{2}.

for all
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The desired result follows immediately.
∎

Our goal is to pass from an asymptotic for
\#\mathcal{F}(X)
to an asymptotic for
\#\mathcal{C}(X)
. The idea here is that
\mathcal{C}(X)
is cut out by congruence conditions at all prime numbers. We use a sieve to obtain the estimate. The main difficulty here is to obtain an upper bound on the tail estimate of the sieve.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
N
be a (positive) natural number, then a congruence condition modulo
N
is a subset
\mathcal{B}_{N}
of
\mathcal{F}
such that there exists
s_{N}\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{2}
for which
\mathcal{B}_{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi_{N}^{-1}(s_{N})
where
\pi_{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{2}
is the mod-
N
reduction map.

Given a congruence condition, we set
\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{B}_{N})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\#s_{N}}{N^{2}}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{N}\subset\mathcal{F}
be a congruence condition modulo
N
, then,

\#\mathcal{B}_{N}(X)\sim\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{B}_{N})\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R})X^{2/d}.

Proof.

For each
a\in s_{N}
, pick
\tilde{a}\in\mathcal{B}_{N}
such that
a=\tilde{a}\pmod{N}
. Let
\Lambda
consist of all pairs
(a,b)\in\mathcal{F}
such that
(a,b)\equiv(0,0)\pmod{N}
. We write
\mathcal{B}_{N}
as a union of translates

\mathcal{B}_{N}=\bigsqcup_{a\in s_{N}}(\Lambda+\tilde{a});

and thus we note that

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{B}_{N}(X)=&\sum_{a\in s_{N}}\#\left((\Lambda+\tilde{a})\cap\mathcal{R}(X)\right),\\
=&\sum_{a\in s_{N}}\#\left(\Lambda\cap(\mathcal{R}-\tilde{a})(X)\right),\\
=&\sum_{a\in s_{N}}\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\left(\frac{1}{N}\cdot(\mathcal{R}-\tilde{a})\right)(X)\right).\end{split}

Thus by Lemma
3.3
,

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{B}_{N}(X)\sim&\sum_{a\in s_{N}}\operatorname{Area}\left(\left(\frac{1}{N}\cdot(\mathcal{R}-\tilde{a})\right)(X)\right),\\
=&\frac{\#s_{N}}{N^{2}}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R}(X)),\\
=&\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{B}_{N})X^{2/d}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R}).\end{split}

∎

A congruence condition at a prime number
\ell
is a set of the form
\mathcal{B}_{\ell^{k}}
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be the set of tuples
(a,b)\in\mathcal{F}
such that

•

\operatorname{gcd}(a,b)=1
,

•

\operatorname{md}(a,b)=1
.

Then we may write
\mathcal{D}=\bigcap_{\ell}\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
,
where
\ell
ranges over all prime numbers and
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
consists of all pairs
(a,b)\in\mathcal{F}
such that:

•

\ell\nmid\operatorname{gcd}(a,b)
,

•

\ell^{4}\nmid A(a,b)
or
\ell^{6}\nmid B(a,b)
.

Clearly
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
is determined by a congruence modulo
\ell^{6}
. The set of residue classes determining
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
is

s_{\ell^{6}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(a,b)\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{6})\mid\ell\nmid a\text{ or }\ell\nmid b\text{, and }\ell^{4}\nmid A(a,b)\text{ or }\ell^{6}\nmid B(a,b)\}.

Note that here
\ell^{6}|B(a,b)
is equivalent to
B(a,b)=0
since the elements
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{6}
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
and consider the associated set of residue classes
s_{\ell^{6}}^{\prime}
consisting of
(a,b)
such that at least one of the following is satisfied:

•

\ell|a
and
\ell|b
,

•

\ell^{4}|A(a,b)
and
\ell^{6}|B(a,b)
.

We may as well write
s_{\ell^{6}}^{\prime}=a_{\ell}\cup b_{\ell}
where
a_{\ell}
consists of pairs for which
\ell|a
and
\ell|b
and
b_{\ell}
consists of pairs for which
(a,b)\notin a_{\ell}
and
\ell^{4}|A(a,b)
and
\ell^{6}|B(a,b)
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\Sigma
be the finite set of primes dividing
\operatorname{Res}(A_{0}(t,1),B_{0}(t,1))
,
\operatorname{Res}(A_{0}(1,t),B_{0}(1,t))
,
\operatorname{Disc}(C(t,1))
, or
\operatorname{Disc}(C(1,t))
.

Suppose that
\ell\notin\Sigma
, then if
\ell^{4}|A(a,b)
and
\ell^{6}|B(a,b)
, it follows that
\ell^{4}|C(a,b)
. There are at most
r+\ell
solutions
(a,b)\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{2}
to
C(a,b)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
. Since
\ell\nmid\operatorname{Disc}(C)
, it follows that each of these solutions lift to exactly one solution
(a,b)\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{4})^{2}
such that
\ell^{4}|C(a,b)
. This in turn implies that the number of solutions in
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{6})^{2}
lifting a solution in
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{2}
is
\ell^{4}
. Thus, in all,
\#b_{\ell}\leq(r+\ell)\ell^{4}
and
\#a_{\ell}=\ell^{10}
. Thus,

\frac{\#s_{\ell^{6}}^{\prime}}{\ell^{12}}\leq\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{(r+\ell)}{\ell^{8}}.

Set

\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{D}_{\ell})\geq\left(1-\frac{\#s_{\ell^{6}}^{\prime}}{\ell^{12}}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{(r+\ell)}{\ell^{8}}\right).

It is easy to see that the product
\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}
converges and since the sum of logarithms

-\sum_{\ell}\log\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{r+\ell}{\ell^{8}}\right)

converges, it follows that this product is non-zero. We show in this section that

\#\mathcal{D}(X)\sim\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R})X^{2/d}.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
(a,b)\in\mathcal{F}(X)
then
|a|,|b|\ll X^{1/d}
.

Proof.

Let
C>0
be such that the square
[-C,C]^{2}
contains
\mathcal{R}
. This implies that

\mathcal{R}(X)=X^{1/d}\cdot\mathcal{R}\subseteq[-CX^{1/d},CX^{1/d}]^{2},

from which the result follows.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
z>0
be a real number, then with respect to notation above, we have that:

(3.3)

\bigcup_{\ell>z}\#\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{\prime}(X)\leq C\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}X^{2/d}+o(X^{2/d})

where
C>0
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

Since
z
can be chosen to be large and
\Sigma
is finite, we assume without loss of generality that
\ell\leq z
for all primes
\ell\in\Sigma
. Let’s write
\mathcal{G}_{z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcup_{\ell>z}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}^{\prime}
. Notice that
\mathcal{G}_{z}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{2}\cup\mathcal{S}_{3}
, where:

•

the set
\mathcal{S}_{1}
consists of the pairs such that
\ell|a
and
\ell|b
for some prime
\ell>z
.

•

The set
\mathcal{S}_{2}
consists of
(a,b)
such that
C(a,b)\neq 0
,
\ell\nmid a,b
and
C(a,b)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{4}}
for some prime
\ell>z
.

•

The set
\mathcal{S}_{3}
consists of
(a,b)
such that
C(a,b)=0
.

First we bound
\#\mathcal{S}_{1}(X)
. Since
H(a,b)\leq X
, Lemma
3.9
implies that
|a|,|b|\ll X^{1/d}
. The number of choices of
(a,b)
such that
\ell
divides
a
and
b
for some
\ell>z
can be bounded above as follows:

\#\mathcal{S}_{1}(X)\ll\sum_{\ell>z}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{X^{1/d}}{\ell}\right\rfloor\right)^{2}\ll\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{X^{2/d}}{\ell^{2}}.

Next, let’s bound
\#\mathcal{S}_{2}(X)
. By Theorem
3.4
,
C(a,b)\ll X^{r/d}
and thus, if
\ell>z
is as above for which
\ell^{4}|C(a,b)
and
C(a,b)\neq 0
, then
\ell\ll X^{r/4d}
. For every
a
, there are at most
r
residue classes of
b
mod
\ell
which satisfy
C(a,b)=0\mod{\ell}
. Since
\ell\notin\Sigma
, it follows that each residue class
b
lifts to a unique solution modulo
\ell^{4}
by Hensel’s lemma. The number of
b
in each residue class mod
\ell^{4}
is
\ll\frac{X^{1/d}}{\ell^{4}}+O(1)
. Thus, we find that

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{S}_{2}(X)\ll&\sum_{z<\ell\ll X^{r/4d}}X^{1/d}\left(\frac{X^{1/d}}{\ell^{4}}+O(1)\right)\\
\leq&\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{1}{\ell^{4}}X^{2/d}+O(\pi(X^{r/4d})X^{1/d})\\
=&\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{1}{\ell^{4}}X^{2/d}+o(X^{2/d}).\end{split}

Here we have used the assumption that
r\leq 4
and replaced
X^{r/4d}
with
X^{1/d}
. Finally, in order to bound
\#\mathcal{S}_{3}(X)
we see that each value of
a
determines
b
up to
O(1)
choices, and thus,

(3.4)

\#\mathcal{S}_{3}(X)=O(X^{1/d})=o(X^{2/d}).

Combining these estimates, we have proven (
3.3
).

∎

Theorem 3.11
.

With respect to notation above,

\#\mathcal{C}(X)\sim\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R})X^{2/d}.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{C}(X)
consists of all pairs
(a,b)\in\mathcal{D}(X)
for which
4A(a,b)^{3}+27B(a,b)^{2}\neq 0
. From (
3.4
), we find that
\#\mathcal{C}(X)=\#\mathcal{D}(X)+O(X^{1/d})
. Hence it suffices to show that

\#\mathcal{D}(X)\sim\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R})X^{2/d}.

We prove our result by showing that

(3.5)

\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{D}(X)}{X^{2/d}}\leq\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R}).

and

(3.6)

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{D}(X)}{X^{2/d}}\geq\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R}).

Let
z
be a positive real number and
\mathcal{D}^{z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcap_{\ell\leq z}\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
. Since it is defined by conditions at finitely many primes,
\mathcal{D}^{z}
is defined by a congruence condition for a large natural number
N
. Thus, we have that
\mathcal{D}^{z}(X)\sim\prod_{\ell\leq z}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R})X^{2/d}
. Since
\mathcal{D}(X)
is contained in
\mathcal{D}^{z}(X)
, we find that

\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{D}(X)}{X^{2/d}}\leq\prod_{\ell\leq z}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R}),

and letting
z\rightarrow\infty
, one has that

\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{D}(X)}{X^{2/d}}\leq\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R}).

Since

\mathcal{D}^{z}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{D}(X)\cup\left(\bigcup_{\ell>z}\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{\prime}(X)\right),

where we recall that
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{\prime}
is the complement of
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
. Then, (
3.3
) implies that there is an absolute constant
C>0
for which

\begin{split}\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{D}(X)}{X^{2/d}}\geq&\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{D}^{z}(X)}{X^{2/d}}-C\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}},\\
=&\prod_{\ell\leq z}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R})-C\sum_{\ell>z}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}.\end{split}

Letting
z\rightarrow\infty
, we find that

(3.7)

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{D}(X)}{X^{2/d}}\geq\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R}).

Therefore, the limit
\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{D}(X)}{X^{2/d}}
exists and equals
\prod_{\ell}\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R})
, proving our result.
∎

4.
Counting elliptic curves with prescribed entanglements

In this section, we count elliptic curves with prescribed entanglement. The Assumption
1.1
holds for the unexplained entanglements
((2,3),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
and
((2,5),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
, and it is for these types that our results apply.

4.1.
(2,3)
entanglements of Type
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

In this subsection, we let
\mathcal{F}_{1}
be the family of elliptic curves
y^{2}=x^{3}+f(t)x+g(t)
, where

\begin{split}f(t)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-3t^{2}-36)c(t),\\
g(t)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(2t^{5}+72t^{3}+864t)c(t),\\
c(t)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=t^{4}+36t^{2}+432,\\
\end{split}

where
t\in\mathbb{Q}
. Such elliptic curves have
j
-invariant given by
j(t)=(t^{2}+12)^{3}
and have a
(2,3)
-entanglement of Type
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
(cf.
[
3
, p. 852]
).

Setting
f_{0}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-3t^{2}-36)
and
g_{0}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(2t^{5}+72t^{3}+864t)
, we have that
c(t)=\operatorname{gcd}(f(t),g(t))
and
f_{0}
and
g_{0}
are coprime.

Consider the homogenized equations, given by:

\begin{split}A(a,b)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-3a^{2}-36b^{2})C(a,b)=A_{0}(a,b)C(a,b),\\
B(a,b)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(2a^{5}+72a^{3}b^{2}+864ab^{4})C(a,b)=B_{0}(a,b)C(a,b),\\
C(a,b)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=a^{4}+36a^{2}b^{2}+432b^{4}.\\
\end{split}

Note that
B_{0}(a,b)=2aC(a,b)
, so
B(a,b)=2aC(a,b)^{2}
. We find that

d=3\operatorname{deg}A=2\operatorname{deg}B=18\text{, and }r=\operatorname{deg}C=4.

Since
r\leq 4
and
C(u,v)
has no real roots, the conditions of Assumption
1.1
are satisfied.

We note that

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Res}(A_{0}(t,1),B_{0}(t,1))=-2^{12}\times 3^{10},\\
&\operatorname{Res}(A_{0}(1,t),B_{0}(1,t))=2^{10}\times 3^{8},\\
&\operatorname{Disc}(C(t,1))=2^{16}\times 3^{9},\\
&\operatorname{Disc}(C(1,t))=2^{20}\times 3^{12}\end{split}

and therefore
\Sigma=\{2,3\}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
\ell\notin\Sigma
,
\ell^{4}|A(a,b)
and
\ell^{6}|B(a,b)
if and only if
\ell^{4}|C(a,b)
.

Proof.

Note that
C(a,b)|A(a,b)
and
C(a,b)^{2}|B(a,b)
and therefore, if
\ell^{4}|C(a,b)
then
\ell^{4}|A(a,b)
and
\ell^{6}|B(a,b)
. On the other hand, since
\ell\nmid\operatorname{Res}(A_{0},B_{0})
we find that
\ell^{4}|C(a,b)
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
C(a,b)=a^{4}+36a^{2}b^{2}+432b^{4}
and
\Sigma=\{2,3\}
. For every prime
\ell\notin\Sigma
one has

\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\;=\;1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{r_{\ell}(\ell-1)}{\ell^{5}},

where
r_{\ell}
is the number of solutions of the congruence

t^{4}+36t^{2}+432\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}.

For the exceptional primes we have the exact values

\mathfrak{d}_{2}\;=\;\frac{1}{2},\qquad\mathfrak{d}_{3}\;=\;\frac{2}{3}.

Proof.

First we prove the result for
\ell\neq 2,3
. Then
\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}=\frac{|s_{\ell^{4}}|}{\ell^{8}}
, where
s_{\ell^{4}}
is the subset of
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{4}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
consisting of all pairs
(a,b)
with
C(a,b)\neq 0\mod\ell^{4}.
We will examine
s_{\ell^{4}}
by counting the number of solutions to
C(a,b)=0
in
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{4}\mathbb{Z})^{2}.
First, suppose that
\ell
divides both
a
and
b
. Then we immediately have
C(a,b)=0\mod\ell^{4}
. Since the kernel of
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{4}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
has size
\ell^{3}
, we have a total of
\ell^{6}
residue classes mod
\ell^{4}
of ordered pairs where this occurs. Now suppose that
\ell\nmid a
or
\ell\nmid b
. If
\ell|b
, then
\ell|a^{4}
. Thus it is clear that
\ell\nmid b
. Let
t\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=a/b
and consider
p(t)=t^{4}+36t^{2}+432
. Let
t_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
be one such solution. Since
p
has no repeated root modulo
\ell
, it follows by Hensel’s lemma that
t_{0}
lifts to a unique solution in
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{4}\mathbb{Z}
. Thus there are
r_{\ell}|(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{4}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}|=r_{\ell}\ell^{3}(\ell-1)
solutions where
r_{\ell}
is the number of solutions to
p(t)=0
for
t\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
. Thus we see that
\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}=1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{r_{\ell}(\ell-1)}{\ell^{5}}
.

Next, we prove our results for
\ell=2,3
. We set

u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\min\{v_{\ell}(a),v_{\ell}(b)\},\qquad a=\ell^{u}a^{\prime},\ b=\ell^{u}b^{\prime},

with at least one of
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
coprime to
2
. Recall that

A(a,b)=A_{0}(a,b)\,C(a,b),\qquad B(a,b)=B_{0}(a,b)\,C(a,b),

with

A_{0}(a,b)=-3(a^{2}+12b^{2}),\qquad B_{0}(a,b)=2a\,C(a,b),

so that
B(a,b)=2a\,C(a,b)^{2}
. For a fixed prime
\ell
set

\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=v_{\ell}\big(A_{0}(a,b)\big),\quad\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=v_{\ell}(a),\quad\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=v_{\ell}\big(C(a,b)\big),\quad\delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=v_{\ell}(2).

Then
v_{\ell}(A)=\alpha+\gamma
,
v_{\ell}(B)=\delta+\beta+2\gamma
and the simultaneous non-minimality condition at
\ell
is

\alpha+\gamma\geq 4,\qquad\delta+\beta+2\gamma\geq 6.

Set
u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\min\{v_{\ell}(a),v_{\ell}(b)\}
and write
a=\ell^{u}a^{\prime},\qquad b=\ell^{u}b^{\prime}
,
so at least one of
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
is not divisible by
\ell
. A straightforward computation gives

\gamma=v_{\ell}\big(C(a,b)\big)=4u+v_{\ell}\big(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\big),

\alpha=v_{\ell}\big(A_{0}(a,b)\big)=2u+v_{\ell}\big(a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2}\big),\qquad\beta=u+v_{\ell}(a^{\prime}).

Substituting these expressions into
(\ast)
yields the equivalent inequalities

2u+v_{\ell}(a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2})+4u+v_{\ell}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 4,

\delta+u+v_{\ell}(a^{\prime})+2(4u+v_{\ell}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})))\geq 6,

which simplify to

6u+v_{\ell}(a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2})+v_{\ell}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 4,

9u+\delta+v_{\ell}(a^{\prime})+2v_{\ell}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 6.

We now analyze separately the two ranges
u\geq 1
and
u=0
.

(A) The case
u\geq 1
.
If
u\geq 1
then
6u\geq 6
, so (I) already holds; moreover
9u\geq 9
and hence (II) holds as well. Thus every pair with
u\geq 1
(equivalently
\ell\mid a
and
\ell\mid b
) satisfies
(\ast)
, and therefore every residue class coming from
(a,b)\equiv(0,0)\pmod{\ell}
is excluded. Counting modulo
\ell^{6}
this produces the full
\ell^{6}
-block of excluded classes coming from common divisibility by
\ell
.

(B) The case
u=0
.
Here at least one of
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
is an
\ell
-unit; we must show that
(\ast)
forces
v_{\ell}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 4
(i.e.
\ell^{4}\mid C(a,b)
), except for a few residue classes which will be handled by direct enumeration. Since
u=0
the inequalities (I) and (II) reduce to

v_{\ell}(a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2})+v_{\ell}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 4,

\delta+v_{\ell}(a^{\prime})+2v_{\ell}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 6.

We now treat the two exceptional primes separately.

The case
\ell=2
.:

Here
\delta=v_{2}(2)=1
. Because
u=0
we have
a^{\prime}=a,\ b^{\prime}=b
and at least one of
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
is odd.

(1):

If both
a^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}
are odd then

a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2}\equiv 1+12\cdot 1\equiv 13\equiv 1\pmod{2},

so
v_{2}(a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2})=0
. Also

C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=a^{\prime 4}+36a^{\prime 2}b^{\prime 2}+432b^{\prime 4}\equiv a^{\prime 4}\equiv 1\pmod{2},

so
v_{2}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))=0
. Thus (I
0
) and (II
0
) are false: the left sides are
\leq 0
and
1+0+0<6
respectively. Therefore no such pair with both coordinates odd can be excluded.

(2):

Suppose
a^{\prime}
is odd and
b^{\prime}
is even (so
v_{2}(b^{\prime})\geq 1
). Write
b^{\prime}=2^{t}b_{1}
with
t\geq 1
and
b_{1}
odd. Then

v_{2}(a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2})=v_{2}(a^{\prime 2}+12\cdot 2^{2t}b_{1}^{2})=0

(since the first term is odd and the second is divisible by
4
). Thus (I
0
) forces
v_{2}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 4
. But if
v_{2}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\leq 3
then (II
0
) reads
1+0+2v_{2}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\leq 1+2\cdot 3=7
, which is not enough to break the earlier inequality — so the only way both (I
0
) and (II
0
) can hold is that
v_{2}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 4
. In particular any excluded pair in this subcase must satisfy
2^{4}\mid C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
.

(3):

Suppose
a^{\prime}
is even and
b^{\prime}
is odd (so
v_{2}(a^{\prime})\geq 1
,
v_{2}(b^{\prime})=0
). Write
a^{\prime}=2^{t}a_{1}
with
t\geq 1
and
a_{1}
odd. Then

v_{2}(a^{\prime})=t\geq 1,\qquad v_{2}(a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2})=v_{2}(2^{2t}a_{1}^{2}+12b^{\prime 2})=0

unless
t=1
and a special cancellation occurs; but checking
t=1
gives
a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2}\equiv 4+a_{1}^{2}\not\equiv 0\pmod{2}
since
a_{1}^{2}\equiv 1
. Hence
v_{2}(a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2})=0
. Then (I
0
) again forces
v_{2}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 4
. Moreover (II
0
) becomes
1+t+2v_{2}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 6
, so with
t\geq 1
this also forces
v_{2}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 2
; combining both inequalities implies in fact
v_{2}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 4
. Thus in this subcase too any excluded pair must satisfy
2^{4}\mid C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
.

Therefore for
\ell=2
and
u=0
the only possible excluded residue classes are those for which
2^{4}\mid C(a,b)
. (There are no other sporadic possibilities beyond those: one checks the finitely many borderline residue classes modulo
2^{6}
and finds they are already included among the
2^{4}\mid C
lifts or in the
\ell|a,\ell|b
block.)

The case
\ell=3
:

Now
\delta=v_{3}(2)=0
. Again
u=0
means
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
are the original residues with at least one of them a
3
-unit.

(1):

If both
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
are
3
-units modulo
3
(i.e.
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
), then

a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2}\equiv a^{\prime 2}+0\equiv a^{\prime 2}\not\equiv 0\pmod{3},

so
v_{3}(a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2})=0
. Also

C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\equiv a^{\prime 4}\pmod{3}

(since
36\equiv 0,432\equiv 0\pmod{3}
), and
a^{\prime 4}\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
. Thus
v_{3}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))=0
, and neither (I
0
) nor (II
0
) can hold. Hence no such pair is excluded.

(2):

If one of
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
is divisible by
3
and the other is not, say
a^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{3}
and
b^{\prime}\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
, then
v_{3}(a^{\prime})\geq 1
and
v_{3}(b^{\prime})=0
. Compute

a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2}\equiv 12b^{\prime 2}\equiv 0\pmod{3},

so
v_{3}(a^{\prime 2}+12b^{\prime 2})\geq 1
. Also

C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=a^{\prime 4}+36a^{\prime 2}b^{\prime 2}+432b^{\prime 4}\equiv 432b^{\prime 4}\equiv 0\pmod{3},

and one checks modulo higher powers that it is possible for
v_{3}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 4
precisely when
a^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{3}
and
b^{\prime}
is a
3
-unit lifting a root
t
of
t^{4}+36t^{2}+432
modulo
3
(and then by Hensel such
t
lifts to higher powers when the root is simple). Moreover (II
0
) becomes

0+v_{3}(a^{\prime})+2v_{3}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 6,

so with
v_{3}(a^{\prime})\geq 1
this forces
v_{3}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 3
, and combining with (I
0
) yields in fact
v_{3}(C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 4
. Thus excluded pairs in this subcase arise only when
3^{4}\mid C(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\ell
be an odd prime with
\ell\nmid 6
, and define

p(t)=t^{4}+36t^{2}+432,\qquad r_{\ell}=\#\{t\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}p(t)=0\}.

Set
\Delta=-432
, and denote by
\left(\tfrac{\cdot}{\ell}\right)
the Legendre symbol modulo
\ell
. Then

r_{\ell}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\left(\tfrac{\Delta}{\ell}\right)=-1,\\[6.0pt]
2+\left(\tfrac{u_{1}}{\ell}\right)+\left(\tfrac{u_{2}}{\ell}\right),&\text{if }\left(\tfrac{\Delta}{\ell}\right)=1,\end{cases}

where
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
are the roots of
u^{2}+36u+432=0
(so
u_{1}+u_{2}=-36
and
u_{1}u_{2}=432
). In particular,

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{\Delta}{\ell}\right)=-1\ \Longrightarrow\ r_{\ell}=0,

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{\Delta}{\ell}\right)=1,\ \left(\tfrac{432}{\ell}\right)=-1\ \Longrightarrow\ r_{\ell}=2,

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{\Delta}{\ell}\right)=1,\ \left(\tfrac{432}{\ell}\right)=1\ \Longrightarrow\ r_{\ell}\in\{0,4\}.

Equivalently, if
\sqrt{\Delta}\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
exists, then

r_{\ell}=2+\left(\frac{\frac{-36+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2}}{\ell}\right)+\left(\frac{\frac{-36-\sqrt{\Delta}}{2}}{\ell}\right),

and this expression is independent of the choice of sign of
\sqrt{\Delta}
.

Proof.

Writing
u=t^{2}
, we have

p(t)=(t^{2}-u_{1})(t^{2}-u_{2}),

where
u_{1},u_{2}
are the roots of
u^{2}+36u+432=0
. If
\left(\tfrac{\Delta}{\ell}\right)=-1
, then
u_{1},u_{2}\notin\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
and
p(t)
has no root in
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
, giving
r_{\ell}=0
. If
\left(\tfrac{\Delta}{\ell}\right)=1
, then
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
, and each equation
t^{2}=u_{i}
contributes
1+\left(\tfrac{u_{i}}{\ell}\right)
solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
. Hence

r_{\ell}=(1+\tfrac{u_{1}}{\ell})+(1+\tfrac{u_{2}}{\ell})=2+\left(\tfrac{u_{1}}{\ell}\right)+\left(\tfrac{u_{2}}{\ell}\right),

which yields the stated cases using
u_{1}u_{2}=432
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The constant

\mathfrak{d}_{2}\mathfrak{d}_{3}\prod_{\ell\neq 2,3}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{r_{\ell}(\ell-1)}{\ell^{5}}\right)

in (
4.1
) is nonzero since the product does not diverge to
0
. Equivalently, the sum of logarithms

\begin{split}&-\sum_{\ell\neq 2,3}\log\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{r_{\ell}(\ell-1)}{\ell^{5}}\right)\\
=&\sum_{\ell\neq 2,3}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{r_{\ell}(\ell-1)}{\ell^{5}}\right)^{n}\\
=&\sum_{\ell\neq 2,3}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n\ell^{2n}}\left(1+\frac{r_{\ell}(\ell-1)}{\ell^{3}}\right)^{n}\\
\end{split}

converges since
r_{\ell}
is bounded (in fact,
r_{\ell}\leq 4
).

4.2.
(2,5) entanglements of Type
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

These entanglements come in two families, as described in
[
3
]
. We consider one of these families, with
j
-map given by
j(t)=\frac{\left(t^{4}+10t^{2}+5\right)^{3}}{t^{2}}
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{2}
consist of elliptic curves
E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+f(t)x+g(t)

\begin{split}f(t)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-3t^{4}-30t^{2}-15)c(t),\\
g(t)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=2(t^{4}+4t^{2}-1)(t^{4}+22t^{2}+125)c(t),\\

\end{split}

According to the first table on
[
3
, p. 853]
, each of these elliptic curves has a
(2,5)
-entanglement of Type
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. We remark that there is yet another family of elliptic curves with the same entanglement type, which we have not considered here. We have that
A_{0}(a,b)=(-3a^{4}-30a^{2}b^{2}-15b^{4})

,

Lemma 4.6
.

For every prime
\ell\notin\Sigma
one has

\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}\;=\;1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{r_{\ell}(\ell-1)}{\ell^{5}},

where
r_{\ell}
is the number of solutions of the congruence

t^{4}+22t^{2}+125\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}.

Proof.

As in Lemma
4.2
, we first treat the case
\ell\notin\Sigma
.
Then

\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}=\frac{|s_{\ell^{4}}|}{\ell^{8}},

where
s_{\ell^{4}}
is the subset of
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{4}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
consisting of pairs
(a,b)
such that
C(a,b)\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{4}}
. The complement consists of all pairs satisfying
C(a,b)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{4}}
. We count these.

If
\ell\mid a,b
then clearly
\ell^{4}\mid C(a,b)
, giving
\ell^{6}
solutions.
If
\ell\nmid a
or
\ell\nmid b
, then after scaling we may assume
\ell\nmid b
and set
t=a/b
. Writing

p(t)=t^{4}+22t^{2}+125,

we note that
p^{\prime}(t)=4t^{3}+44t
, so the roots of
p(t)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
are simple for
\ell\notin\Sigma
.
Hence, each such root
t_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
lifts uniquely to a root modulo
\ell^{4}
by Hensel’s lemma. Thus there are exactly

r_{\ell}\,\ell^{3}(\ell-1)
such pairs with
\ell\nmid b
. We find that

\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}=1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{r_{\ell}(\ell-1)}{\ell^{5}}.

Improved 2-Approximate Shortest Paths for close vertex pairs
Manoj Gupta

Abstract
An influential result by Dor, Halperin, and Zwick (FOCS 1996, SICOMP 2000) implies an algorithm that can compute approximate shortest paths for all vertex pairs in O ~ ​ ( n 2 + O ⁡ ( 1 k ) ) \widetilde{O}(n^{2+O\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)}) time 1 1 1 O ~ ​ ( ) \widetilde{O}() notation hides poly log ⁡ n \log n factors , ensuring that the output distance is at most twice the actual shortest path, provided the pairs are at least k k apart, where k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . We present the first improvement on this result in over 25 years. Our algorithm achieves roughly same O ~ ​ ( n 2 + 1 k ) \widetilde{O}(n^{2+\frac{1}{k}}) runtime but applies to vertex pairs merely O ⁡ ( log ⁡ k ) O(\log k) apart, where log ⁡ k ≥ 1 \log k\geq 1 . When k = log ⁡ n k=\log n , the running time of our algorithm is O ~ ​ ( n 2 ) \widetilde{O}(n^{2}) and it works for all pairs at least O ⁡ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) O(\log\log n) apart. Our algorithm is combinatorial, randomized, and returns correct results for all pairs with a high probability.

Introduction

Let
G
be an undirected, unweighted graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges. Let
st
denote the shortest path between
s
and
t
and
|st|
denote its length. Computing all-pairs shortest paths (APSP) is a fundamental problem in graph algorithm. For unweighted graphs, combinatorial methods achieve a time complexity of
O(mn)
, while fast matrix multiplication (
FMM
) solves APSP in
\widetilde{O}(n^{\omega})
time
[
Sei95
]
, where
\omega
denotes the matrix multiplication exponent
[
AW24
,
LG14
,
Sto10
,
Wil12
,
DWZ23
,
WXXZ24
]
.

However, these algorithms have super-quadratic runtimes in dense graphs. To achieve near-quadratic performance, we must allow approximate solutions. Let
est(s,t)
be the approximate shortest path length computed by an algorithm for each pair
(s,t)
. An algorithm is said to be an
(\alpha,\beta)
-approximate APSP, or to provide an
(\alpha,\beta)
-approximation of the length of a path, if for every pair
(s,t)
, the following holds:

|st|\leq est(s,t)\leq\alpha|st|+\beta\hskip 71.13188pt\text{(where $\alpha\geq 1$ and $\beta\geq 0$)}

If
\beta=0
, we omit it from the notation and call the algorithm
\alpha
-approximate APSP. When
\alpha=1
, we refer to it as a
+\beta
-approximate APSP. The former provides a multiplicative approximation, while the latter gives an additive approximation. We first discuss prior results on additive approximation.

Aingworth, Chekuri, Indyk and Motwani
[
ACIM99
]
designed an
\widetilde{O}(n^{2.5})
algorithm for
+2
-approximate APSP. Dor, Halperin and Zwick
[
DHZ00
]
improved the above time to
\widetilde{O}(\min\{n^{3/2}m^{1/2},n^{7/3}\})
. Deng, Kirkpatrick, Rong, Williams and Zhong
[
DKR+22
]
used
FMM
to improve this running time to
\widetilde{O}(n^{2.26})
(using an improved result in
[
Dür23
]
).

For a
+k
-approximate APSP, Dor, Halperin, and Zwick
[
DHZ00
]
designed a combinatorial algorithm with a running time of
\widetilde{O}(\min\{n^{2-\frac{2}{k+2}}m^{\frac{2}{k+2}},n^{2+\frac{2}{3k-2}}\})
. Saha and Ye
[
SY24
]
recently improved this bound using
FMM
. These results serve as fundamental building blocks for multiplicative approximation.

In this paper, we primarily focus on 2-approximate APSP algorithm. A 2-approximate APSP algorithm can be constructed using a
+k
-approximate APSP algorithm based on the following observation:

Observation 1.1
.

A
+k
-approximate APSP algorithm provides
2
-approximate distances for all vertex pairs with a distance of at least
k
.

Using this observation, the above algorithms produce 2-approximate answers for vertex pairs sufficiently far apart.
Thus, +2-approximate algorithm of
[
ACIM99
]
implies a 2-approximate APSP algorithm with a running time
\widetilde{O}(n^{2.5})
. In particular, look at the running time of Dor, Halperin and Zwick
[
DHZ00
]
. If
k=O(\log n)
, their algorithm’s running time is
\widetilde{O}(n^{2})
. This result implies that we can compute a 2-approximate APSP for vertex pairs at least
O(\log n)
apart. One of the most important open questions in this field is
designing a 2-approximate APSP algorithm that runs in
\widetilde{O}(n^{2})
time and handles all vertex pairs
.

Unfortunately, thus far, only
(2,\beta)
-approximate APSP algorithms have been achieved in
\widetilde{O}(n^{2})
time, where
\beta
is a small constant. Indeed, several algorithms get
(2,1)
-approximate APSP in
\widetilde{O}(n^{2})
time
[
BK07
,
PR10
,
Som16
,
Knu17
]

[

[
BK06
]
achieved
\widetilde{O}(m\sqrt{n}+n^{2})
time. Both perform well on sparse graphs, but
[
BK06
]
is more efficient than
[
CZ01
]
. In fact, Baswana and Kavitha’s algorithm serves as a building block for many future algorithms.
Until recently, these remained the best-known results for 2-approximate APSP. However, recent advancements have improved upon them.

still have a maximum running time of

. In this paper, we make significant progress on this problem, establishing the following result:

Theorem 1.2
.

There is a randomized combinatorial algorithm that, with high probability, finds
2
-approximate shortest paths for all vertex pairs at least
O(\log k)
apart and runs in
\widetilde{O}(n^{2+\frac{1}{k}})
time, where
\log k\geq 1
is an integer.

Thus, we can compute a
2
-approximation significantly faster than obtaining a
+k
-approximation for vertex pairs at least
k
apart. Specifically, when
k=\log n
, the running time of our algorithm is
\widetilde{O}(n^{2})
and it applies to all pairs at least
O(\log\log n)
apart. In fact, most of our paper focuses on this special case – our starting point for this problem. Once we resolve this case, we extend our algorithm to any
k
. Our result is randomized and relies on a simple combinatorial technique that, to our knowledge, has not been exploited for this problem. We hope this technique will prove useful in related problems within this field.

2.

Approximate Distance Oracle

Given a graph
G
, we can preprocess it to construct a data structure that supports approximate distance queries between any two vertices. To this end, we design a query algorithm that returns an estimate upon request. We extend the definition of
(\alpha,\beta)
-approximate APSP to an
(\alpha,\beta)
-approximate distance oracle, where distances are computed only when queried rather than for all vertex pairs. Consequently, the preprocessing time can be subquadratic when
m<n^{2}
.

In their seminal paper, Thorup and Zwick
[
TZ01
]
designed a
(2k-1)
-approximate distance oracle with a preprocessing time of
O(kmn^{1/k})
, oracle size
\widetilde{O}(kn^{1+1/k})
, and query time
O(k)
. This result was later improved in
[
BK06
,
WN12
,
WN13
,
Che14
,
Che15
]
. Akav and Roditty
[
AR20
]
developed a
(2+\epsilon,5)
-approximate distance oracle with a preprocessing time of
\widetilde{O}(m+n^{2-\Omega(\epsilon)})
, where
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
is a constant. More recently, Chechik and Zhang
[
CZ22
]
designed a
(2,3)
-approximate distance oracle of size
\widetilde{O}(n^{5/3})
with a preprocessing time of
\widetilde{O}(m+n^{1.987})
. They also constructed a
(2+\epsilon,c(\epsilon))
-approximate distance oracle of size
\tilde{O}(n^{5/3})
in
\tilde{O}(m+n^{5/3+\epsilon})
time, where

depends exponentially on
1/\epsilon
.

Definition 2.2
.

(Nested Vertex Sampling)
Let

The following lemma follows directly from the definition and is proved in the appendix.

Lemma 2.3

Pr[v\in A_{i}]=\frac{1}{2^{2^{i}}}
for

\widetilde{O}\left(\frac{n}{2^{2^{i}}}\right)

(Pivots and Balls)

By definition,

. The following lemma is immediate using the above definitions and we prove it in the appendix.

Lemma 2.5

(Size of ball)
With high probability, for all

\widetilde{O}(2^{2^{i}})
.

We can use

ball_{i}(s)

Lemma 2.6

With high probability, each vertex

Lemma 2.7

Overview

In majority of the paper, we aim to achieve an
+O(\log\log n)
-approximate APSP for some specific vertex pairs. In the overview, we present an algorithm that achieves an
+O(\log\log n)
approximation of the length of
st
path. Unfortunately, the algorithm does not work in general — it relies on a specific assumption (in addition to
2.8
), which we describe next.

Assumption 3.1
.

Consider an
st
path. For each
i
(where
0\leq i\leq\log\log n-2
),
ball_{i+1}(u_{i})
contains
u_{i+1}
and
ball_{i+1}(v_{i})
contains
v_{i+1}
.

By definition,
u_{i+1}\in A_{i+1}
, and
ball_{i+1}(u_{i})
cannot contain a vertex from
A_{i+1}
. Thus, this assumption is clearly incorrect. In fact,
u_{i}
may be far from
u_{i+1}
along the
st

ball_{i+1}(u_{i})
may not even contain a vertex close to
u_{i+1}
. However, let us examine what this assumption leads to. Using this assumption and

, we prove the following lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
0\leq i\leq\log\log n-1

est(u_{i},v_{i})\leq|a_{i}b_{i}|+4(\log\log n-i)

The above lemma implies an additive
O(\log\log n)
-approximation of the

i=0
in the lemma gives
est(u_{0},v_{0})\leq|a_{0}b_{0}|+4\log\log n
. Since
a_{0}=u_{0}=s
and
b_{0}=v_{0}=t
, we obtain
est(s,t)\leq|st|+4\log\log n
. This yields an
+O(\log\log n)
approximate APSP and a 2-approximation for the
st

|st|\geq 4\log\log n

We now prove the above lemma using induction on
i

\log\log n-1

u_{i+1}
and

est(u_{i+1},v_{i+1})\leq|a_{i+1}b_{i+1}|+4(\log\log n-(i+1))

and

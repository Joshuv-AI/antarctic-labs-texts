Orthogonal Oscillator Representations, Laplace Equations


Abstract
Associated varieties are geometric objects appearing in infinite-dimensional representations of semisimple Lie algebras (groups). By applying Fourier transformations to the natural orthogonal oscillator representations of special linear Lie algebras, Luo and the second author (2013) obtained a big family of infinite-dimensional irreducible representations of the algebras on certain spaces of homogeneous solutions of the Laplace equation. In this paper, we prove that the associated varieties of these irreducible representations are the intersections of explicitly given determinantal varieties. This provides an explicit connection among representation theory, partial differential equations and algebraic geometry. Keywords : special linear Lie algebra; orthogonal oscillator representation; associated variety; irreducible module; filtrations; determinantal variety.

Introduction

In 1971, Bernstein
[
11
]
defined a variety associated with a filtrated module of the algebra of differential operators, in order to construct the fundamental solution of a linear partial differential equation with constant coefficients. A primitive ideal of the universal enveloping
algebra of a semisimple Lie algebra is the kernel of an irreducible representation of the Lie algebra. Borho and Jantzen
[
13

(1977) found a translation principle among primitive ideals and used it to prove the Dixmier orbit map is well defined for
sl(n)
. Vogan
[
33

(1978) found particularly refined techniques
in the description of the leading coefficient in the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial of a Harish-Chandra module for a semisimple Lie algebra and its maximal compact subalgebra, where the filtration of the module is naturally induced from the canonical filtration of the universal enveloping
algebra of the Lie algebra. Moreover, he
[
34
]
(1980) derived a new order relation in primitive ideals and used it to prove a Joseph’s conjecture on primitive ideals and multiplicities in the composition series of principal series representations.

Borho and Brylinsky
[
12

(1982) proved that the kernel of the representation of the universal enveloping
algebra of a semisimple Lie algebra by global differential operators on a complete homogeneous space of the corresponding Lie group is the annihilator of a generalized Verma module, and the associated variety (the term firstly appeared) of the ideal is irreducible. Joseph
[
22

(1983) gave an extensive review on the classification of primitive ideals in the universal enveloping
algebra of a semisimple Lie algebra. He
[
23

[

[
6

(e.g., cf.

Determinantal varieties are natural important geometric objects in algebraic geometry. Barshay
[
9
]
(1973) used them to prove the coordinate ring of the Veronese is arithmetically normal and Cohen-Macaulay. Catanese
[
14

(1981) used symmetric determinantal varieties to prove Babbage’s conjecture. de Concini, Eisenbud and Procesi
[

(1980) found an important connection of Young diagrams with determinantal varieties. Griffiths
[
18

(1983) found that determinantal varieties play important roles in the infinitesimal variations of Hodge structure. Enright and Hunziker

[

(2016) derived a homological projective duality for determinantal varieties.
Wong
[
37

[

By applying Fourier transformations to the natural orthogonal oscillator representations of special linear Lie algebras, Luo and the second author
[
26

(2013) obtained a big family of infinite-dimensional irreducible representations of the algebras on certain spaces of homogeneous solutions of the Laplace equation. Bai
[
2

(2015) found the Gel’fand-Kirillov dimensions of these representations. In this paper, we prove that the associated varieties of these irreducible representations are intersections of explicitly given determinantal varieties. This provides an explicit connection among representation theory, partial differential equations and algebraic geometry. It also partially meets Bernstein’s original concern in
[
11

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with characteristic 0, and let
n>1
be an integer. Denote by
\mathscr{A}=\mathbb{F}[x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{n}]
the polynomial algebra in
2n
variables. Let
E_{r,s}
be the square matrix with 1 as its
(r,s)
-entry and 0
as the others. The special linear Lie algebra

sl(n)=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(\mathbb{F}E_{i,j}+\mathbb{F}E_{j,i})+\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{F}(E_{r,r}-E_{r+1,r+1})

(1.1)

with the commutator as its Lie bracket. For any
two integers
p\leq q
, we denote
\overline{p,q}=\{p,p+1,\cdots,q\}
. The natural orthogonal oscillator representation
\pi
of
sl(n)
on
\mathscr{A}
is given as follows:

\pi(E_{i,j})=x_{i}\partial_{x_{j}}-y_{j}\partial_{y_{i}}\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;i,j\in\overline{1,n}.

(1.2)

With respect to the above representation, the Laplace operator

\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{y_{i}}.

(1.3)

Denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of nonnegative integers. For
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\mathscr{A}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
the subspace of homogeneous polynomials with the degree
\ell_{1}
in
\{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}\}
and the degree
\ell_{2}
in
\{y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{n}\}
. Then
\mathscr{A}=\bigoplus_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{A}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
is an
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-graded algebra.

Set the subspace of homogeneous harmonic polynomials as follows:

{\mathscr{H}}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}=\{f\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}\mid\Delta(f)=0\}.

(1.4)

Then
{\mathscr{H}}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
forms an irreducible
sl(n)
-module with highest weight
\ell_{1}\lambda_{1}+\ell_{2}\lambda_{n-1}
(e.g., cf.
[
38
]
), where
\lambda_{i}
denotes the
i
th fundamental weight of
sl(n)
.

Denote by
\mathscr{F}_{z}
the Fourier transformation on
z
. Fix
n_{1},n_{2}\in\overline{1,n}
with
n_{1}\leq n_{2}
. Letting (
1.2
) act on the space

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}=\mathscr{F}_{x_{1}}\cdots\mathscr{F}_{x_{n_{1}}}\mathscr{F}_{x_{n_{2}+1}}\cdots\mathscr{F}_{x_{n}}(\mathscr{A})

(1.5)

of distributions, one can obtain a big family of infinite-dimensional irreducible representations of
sl(n)
on certain spaces of homogeneous solutions of the Laplace equation. However, this picture is not convenient to be dealt with. Instead, we consider another equivalent representation as follows.
In the representation formulas given in (
1.2
), applying the Fourier transformations on operators:

\partial_{x_{r}}\mapsto-x_{r},\;x_{r}\mapsto\partial_{x_{r}},\;\partial_{y_{s}}\mapsto-y_{s},\;y_{s}\mapsto\partial_{y_{s}}\qquad\mbox{for}\;r\in\overline{1,n_{1}},\;s\in\overline{n_{2}+1,n},

(1.6)

we obtain a new representation
\tilde{\pi}
on
\mathscr{A}
. The representations
(\pi,\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
and
(\tilde{\pi},\mathscr{A})
are equivalent (or isomorphic).
Under the changes in (
1.6
), the Laplace operator becomes

\tilde{\Delta}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}x_{i}\partial_{y_{i}}-\sum_{r=n_{1}+1}^{n_{2}}\partial_{x_{r}}\partial_{y_{r}}+\sum_{s=n_{2}+1}^{n}y_{s}\partial_{x_{s}}.

(1.7)

Take the degree of
\{x_{1},...,x_{n_{1}},y_{n_{2}+1},...,y_{n}\}
as
-1
and that of
\{x_{n_{1}+1},...,x_{n},y_{1}...,y_{n_{2}}\}

as 1. Denote by
\mathbb{Z}
the ring of integers. For
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by
\mathscr{A}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle}
the subspace of homogeneous polynomials with the degree
\ell_{1}
in
\{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}\}
and the degree
\ell_{2}
in
\{y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{n}\}
.
Then
\mathscr{A}=\bigoplus_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathscr{A}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle}
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded algebra. Set

{\mathscr{H}}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle}=\{f\in{\mathscr{A}}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle}\mid\tilde{\Delta}(f)=0\}.

(1.8)

The following is a result from
[
26
]
:

Proposition 1

For

\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, the necessary and sufficient condition for
{\mathscr{H}}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle}
to be an infinite-dimensional irreducible
sl(n)
-module is as follows:

(1) When
n_{1}+1<n_{2}
, (a)
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq n_{1}-n_{2}+1
; (b)
n_{2}=n
,
\ell_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell_{2}=0
; (c)
n_{2}=n
,
\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell_{1}\geq n_{1}-n+2
;

(2) If
n_{1}+1=n_{2}
,
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq 0
or
n_{2}=n
and

0\leq\ell_{2}\leq\ell_{1}
;

(3) In the case
n_{1}=n_{2}
,
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq 0
and:(a)

\ell_{2}\leq 0
,
n_{1}<n-1
and
n\geq 3
;
(b)
\ell_{1}\leq 0
,
1<n_{1}<n
and
n\geq 3
; (c)
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\leq 0
,
n_{1}=1
and
n=2
.

The above irreducible modules are infinite-dimensional highest weight modules with distinct highest weights. For instance, if
n_{1}+1<n_{2}<n

and
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
with
m_{1}+m_{2}\geq n_{2}-n_{1}-1
,
{\mathscr{H}}_{\langle-m_{1},-m_{2}\rangle}
has a highest-weight vector

x_{n_{1}}^{m_{1}}y_{n_{2}+1}^{m_{2}}
of weight

m_{1}\lambda_{n_{1}-1}-(m_{1}+1)\lambda_{n_{1}}-(m_{2}+1)\lambda_{n_{2}}+m_{2}(1-\delta_{n_{2},n-1})\lambda_{n_{2}+1}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra and let
U(\mathfrak{g})
be the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Denote

U_{0}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbb{F}
and for
0<m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
U_{m}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbb{F}+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{r}
. Then
\{U_{k}(\mathfrak{g})\mid k\in\mathbb{N}\}
forms a filtration of
U(\mathfrak{g})
. Suppose that
M
is a finitely generated
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module and
M_{0}
is a finite-dimensional subspace of
M
satisfying

(U(\mathfrak{g}))(M_{0})=M.

(1.9)

Setting
M_{k}=(U_{k}(\mathfrak{g}))(M_{0})
, we get a filtration
\{M_{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
M
.

The associated graded module is given by

\overline{M}=\mbox{gr}(M;M_{0})=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{M_{k}}\quad\text{with}\quad\overline{M}_{k}=M_{k}/M_{k-1},

(1.10)

and it is a finitely generated graded
S(\mathfrak{g})
-module, since graded algebra
\mbox{gr}(U(\mathfrak{g}))
is canonically isomorphic to the symmetric tensor algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})
by the PBW theorem. Here we identify
S(\mathfrak{g})
with
\mathbb{F}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
, the polynomial ring over
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, in the canonical way through the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
.

The annihilator of
\overline{M}
in
S(\mathfrak{g})
is defined as

\operatorname{Ann}_{S(\mathfrak{g})}(\overline{M})=\{\xi\in S(\mathfrak{g})\mid\xi(v)=0,\;\forall\;v\in\overline{M}\},

(1.11)

which is a graded ideal of
S(\mathfrak{g})
. It defines the associated variety in the dual space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
:

{\mathscr{V}}(M)=\{f\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\mid u(f)=0,\;\forall\;u\in\operatorname{Ann}_{S(\mathfrak{g})}(\overline{M})\}.

(1.12)

The variety does not depend on the choice of the subspace
M_{0}
satisfying (
1.9
).

Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be subsets of
\overline{1,n}
.
Denote the determinantal ideal
I_{t}(S_{1},S_{2})
to be the ideal generated by all the
t
-minors in the matrix
(z_{i,j})_{i\in S_{1},j\in S_{2}}
, and
\mathscr{V}_{t}(S_{1},S_{2})=\mathscr{V}(I_{t})
is called a
determinantal variety
.
For convenience, we denote
I_{t}(S_{1},S_{2})=0
and
\mathscr{V}_{t}(S_{1},S_{2})=\mathbb{A}^{|S_{1}||S_{2}|}
, when
t>|S_{1}|
or
t>|S_{2}|
.

Recall
n_{1},n_{2}\in\overline{1,n}
with
n_{1}\leq n_{2}
. Set

J_{1}=\overline{1,n_{1}},\quad J_{2}=\overline{n_{1}+1,n_{2}},\quad J_{3}=\overline{n_{2}+1,n}.

(1.13)

The following is our main theorem in this paper:

Main Theorem

If
n_{1}\leq n_{2}
,
\ell_{1}\leq 0
or
\ell_{2}\leq 0
,

\mathscr{V}({\mathscr{H}}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle})\cong\mathscr{V}_{3}(J_{2}\cup J_{3},J_{1}\cup J_{2})\cap\mathscr{V}_{2}(J_{2},J_{1})\cap\mathscr{V}_{2}(J_{3},J_{2})\cap\mathscr{V}_{1}(J_{2},J_{2}).

(1.14)

If
n_{1}<n_{2}=n
and
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}>0
,

\mathscr{V}({\mathscr{H}}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle})\cong\mathscr{V}_{2}(J_{2},J_{1}).

(1.15)

The theorem covers all the irreducible
sl(n)
-modules
{\mathscr{H}}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle}
listed in Proposition 1.
In particular, when
n_{1}=n_{2}
and
|J_{1}|,|J_{3}|\geq 3
,

\mathscr{V}({\mathscr{H}}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle})\cong\mathscr{V}_{3}(J_{3},J_{1})

(1.16)

is exactly a determinantal variety. When
n_{2}=n
, the projective version of the associated variety

\mathscr{V}({\mathscr{H}}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle})\cong\mathscr{V}_{2}(J_{2},J_{1})

(1.17)

is the well-known Serge variety in algebraic geometry.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we explicitly describe the filtration of
{\mathscr{H}}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle}
with respect to the representation
\tilde{\pi}
and a properly selected finite-dimensional subspace

(cf. (

In Section 3, we study certain linear independence on certain subsets in the spanning subsets of filtration subspaces by introducing the notion of “3-chain” , which plays a key role in next section. Finally in Section 4, we present a proof of the main theorem by using certain “order” on the products of quadratic alternating polynomials.

Explicit Description of the Filtration for
{\mathscr{H}}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle}

In this section, we select a special subspace
M_{0}
of
M={\mathscr{H}}_{\langle\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle}

on

In the rest of this paper, we only consider the representation
\tilde{\pi}
of

obtained under the Fourier transformations (

\tilde{\pi}(E_{i,j})=E_{i,j}^{x}-E_{j,i}^{y}\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;i,j\in\overline{1,n}

and

Denote

2.
Identifying
\mathrm{G}
-structures with minimal left ideals for Clifford algebras

In this section, we provide an overview of what it means to identify a special geometric structure with a minimal left ideal of a Clifford algebra (see
[
12
]
for a more in-depth introduction).
We begin by defining a
\mathrm{G}
-structure for a general oriented Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of dimension
n
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a closed Lie subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
. A
\mathrm{G}
-structure
on
M
is a reduction of the structure group
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
of the frame bundle
\mathcal{F}(M)
to
\mathrm{G}
. That is, a
\mathrm{G}
-structure is a principal sub-bundle
\mathcal{P}(M)
of
\mathcal{F}(M)
with fibre
\mathrm{G}
. If
\mathrm{G}
is one of the groups that appear in the Berger classification theorem (see below), then
\mathrm{G}
is called a
special geometric structure
.

An important question pertaining to Riemannian manifolds is, which Lie subgroups of
\rm{GL(n,{\mathbb{R}})}
are also holonomy groups of the Levi-Civita connection, and what conditions need to be imposed on the Riemannian manifold for this to be the case? Berger’s theorem provides an answer to this question for any
n
-dimensional, simply connected manifold with an irreducible, non-symmetric Riemannian metric (see
[
1
,
3
,
6
,
11
]
for more on the concept of Riemannian holonomy).

Theorem 2.2

(Berger’s Theorem)
.

Suppose that
M
is a simply connected manifold of dimension
n
, and that
g
is an irreducible, non-symmetric Riemannian metric on
M
. Then exactly one of the following seven cases holds.

(1)

\mathrm{Hol}(g)=\mathrm{SO}(n)
.

(2)

n=2m
with
m\geq 2
, and
\mathrm{Hol}(g)=\rm{U(m)}
in
\mathrm{SO(2m)}
.

(3)

n=2m
with
m\geq 2
, and
\mathrm{Hol}(g)=\mathrm{SU}(m)
in
\mathrm{SO}(2m)
.

(4)

n=4m
with
m\geq 2
, and
\mathrm{Hol}(g)=\mathrm{Sp}(m)
in
\mathrm{SO}(4m)
.

(5)

n=4m
with
m\geq 2
, and
\mathrm{Hol}(g)=\mathrm{Sp}(m)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
in
\mathrm{SO}(4m)
.

(6)

n=7
, and
\mathrm{Hol}(g)=\mathrm{G_{2}}
in
\mathrm{SO(7)}
.

(7)

n=8
, and
\mathrm{Hol}(g)=\mathrm{Spin(7)}
in
\mathrm{SO(8)}
.
∎

Now via the general holonomy principle, we can identify each holonomy group on Berger’s list with parallel tensor fields on the Riemannian manifold. That is, for each holonomy group in Berger’s classification list, we can find
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}\in\Gamma(\bigotimes T^{*}M\otimes\bigotimes TM)
such that
\nabla^{g}\sigma_{1}=\cdots=\nabla^{g}\sigma_{k}=0
. It is well known that the local descriptions of these parallel tensor fields can be identified with model tensors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and that the holonomy groups in Berger’s list can be expressed as the subgroups of
\textrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
that stabilize these model tensors (see
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
11
]
). Since every oriented Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
comes with a Riemannian metric
g
and a volume form
dV_{g}
that uniquely defines its orientation, and since in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the subgroup of
\textrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
that stabilizes these two tensors is
\textrm{SO}(n)
, it is immediate that the holonomy group
\textrm{Hol}(g)
is strictly smaller than
\rm{SO(n)}
if and only if there exist additional nontrivial parallel tensor fields on
M^{n}
with respect to the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
. Hence, for our purposes, the holonomy groups in Berger’s list can be thought of as stabilizer subgroups of
\mathrm{SO}(n)
that stabilize additional tensors added to the standard inner product and volume form in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

We now change our focus to Clifford algebras. (For elementary properties of Clifford algebras, see
[
4
,
7
,
8
]
). Let
V
be a finite-dimensional
{\mathbb{R}}
vector space with quadratic form
q
, and let
V^{\otimes}=\bigoplus_{k}V^{\otimes k}
be its tensor algebra. We define the
Clifford algebra
of the pair
(V,q)
as the quotient of the tensor algebra by the two-sided ideal
I_{q}=\langle v\otimes v+q(x)1_{V^{\otimes}}\rangle
; that is,
C_{q}(V)=V^{\otimes}/I_{q}
. Choosing a basis for
(V,q)
, say
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
, we get the following canonical basis for the Clifford algebra generated by the following
2^{n}
monomials:

\{1_{V}\}\cup\{e_{i_{1}}\cdots e_{i_{k}}:i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k},\ k=1,\ldots,n\}.

Any quadratic space over
{\mathbb{R}}
is isomorphic to a quadratic space of the form
{\mathbb{R}}^{p,q}
with signature
(p,q)
, where
p
is the number of positive definite generators and
q
is the number of negative definite generators.

1

It should be clear from context when the symbol
q
means the quadratic form, when it means the second part of the signature
(p,q)
, and when it means the quantization map.
The generators
e_{1},\ldots,e_{p}
are the positive definite generators, meaning that
q(e_{i})=1
for
1\leq i\leq p
; and the generators
e_{p+1},\ldots,e_{p+q}
are the negative definite generators, meaning that
q(e_{j})=-1
for
p+1\leq j\leq p+q
. For the quadratic spaces
{\mathbb{R}}^{p,q}
, we denote the associated Clifford algebras by
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
.

It is well known that Clifford algebras are isomorphic to matrix algebras over the division algebras
{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}},
and
\mathbb{H}
. This classification is given in the following theorem (see
[
10
]
).

Theorem 2.3
.

The Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
, where
p+q=n
, has the following minimal representations over
{\mathbb{R}}
,
{\mathbb{C}}
, and
\mathbb{H}
:

i)

{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}\cong{\mathbb{R}}(2^{\frac{n}{2}})
if
q-p=0,6\mod 8
.

ii)

{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}\cong{\mathbb{C}}({2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}})
if
q-p=1,5\mod 8
.

iii)

{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}\cong\mathbb{H}({2^{\frac{n-2}{2}}})
if
q-p=2,4\mod 8
.

iv)

{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}\cong\mathbb{H}({2^{\frac{n-3}{2}}})\oplus\mathbb{H}({2^{\frac{n-3}{2}}})
if
q-p=3\mod 8
.

v)

{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}\cong{\mathbb{R}}({2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}})\oplus{\mathbb{R}}({2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}})
if
q-p=7\mod 8
.

It should be noted that some of the structure of
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
does not carry over to the matrix algebra with which it is associated. An alternative way to generate spinor modules, which generate our representations of these real Clifford algebras, is to classify our Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
in terms of their minimal left ideals, generated by primitive idempotents (see
[
9
]
for the theory behind these constructions). We begin by defining what a minimal left ideal is in the context of a Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
.

Definition 2.4
.

For the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
, any
minimal left ideal
is of the form
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f
where
f
is a primitive idempotent: that is, an
f\in{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
such that
f^{2}=f
and
f
cannot be written as the sum of two orthogonal idempotents. The minimal left ideals of the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
are called
spinor spaces
, and the elements of the minimal left ideals are called
algebraic spinors
. In
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f
, we define
f
as the
identity unit spinor
that is identified with
1\in{\mathbb{R}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
.

It is immediate that
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f
is a left
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
module, where module multiplication is given by
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f
via
(\phi,\psi f)\mapsto(\phi\psi)f
for all
\phi\in{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
and
\psi f\in{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f.
The following theorem gives us the construction and classification of minimal left ideals in
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
(see
[
9
]
).

Theorem 2.5
.

A minimal left ideal of
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
is of type
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f
where
f=\dfrac{1+e_{t_{1}}}{2}\cdots\dfrac{1+e_{t_{k}}}{2}
is a primitive idempotent in
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
and
e_{t_{1}},\ldots,e_{t_{k}}
is a set of commuting elements of the canonical basis such that
e_{t_{i}}^{2}=1
for all
i=1,\ldots,k=q-r_{q-p}
; moreover, the generators form a multiplicative group of order
2^{q-r_{q-p}}
. The numbers
r_{q-p}
are called the Radon-Hurwitz numbers, given by the recurrence formula
r_{0}=0,\ r_{1}=1,\ r_{2}=2,\ r_{3}=2,\ r_{j}=3
where
4\leq j\leq 7
,
r_{i+8}=r_{i}+4
for
i\geq 0
,
r_{-1}=-1
, and
r_{-i}=1-i+r_{i-2}
for
i\geq 2
.
∎

The minimal left ideals constructed in Theorem
2.5
are not only left
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
modules, but they also take the structure of right
f\,{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f
modules, where this right module structure gives the minimal left ideal
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f
an
{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}
, or
\mathbb{H}
vector space structure (see
[
8
]
). In order to relate
\mathrm{G}
-structures with minimal left ideals of Clifford algebras, we establish a vector space isomorphism between the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
and the exterior algebra of
{\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}
, denoted
\bigwedge^{\bullet}{\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}
. This isomorphism is achieved via the assignment of the
quantization map

q:\bigwedge^{\bullet}{\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
, where
e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{k}}\mapsto e_{i_{1}}\cdots e_{i_{k}}
. The inverse of this isomorphism is called the
symbol map
, which we denote by
\sigma:{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}\rightarrow\bigwedge^{\bullet}{\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}
, given by
\sigma(e_{i_{1}}\cdots e_{i_{k}})=e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{k}}
.

These maps have natural extensions onto the spaces of exterior forms on
{\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}
. We denote these extensions by
q^{*}:\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{p+q})^{*}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
, where
q^{*}(e^{i_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{k}})=e_{i_{1}}\cdots e_{i_{k}}
; and
\sigma^{*}:{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}\rightarrow\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{p+q})^{*}
, where
\sigma^{*}(e_{i_{1}}\cdots e_{i_{k}})=e^{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{k}}
.
Here
e^{1},\ldots,e^{n}
is the dual basis to the canonical basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
.

We now define what it means for a minimal left ideal to be recoverable from a
\mathrm{G}
-structure.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k}\in\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{p+q})^{*}
define a
\mathrm{G}
-structure. We say that
the
\mathrm{G}
-structure induces a minimal left ideal

{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f
in the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
if the primitive idempotent
f
that defines that minimal left ideal can be recovered from an algebraic expression
\psi(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k})
in the exterior algebra
\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{p+q})^{*}
via the isomorphism induced from the quantization map; that is,
q^{*}(\psi(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k}))=f
.

For the converse, we make the following definition.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
f
be a primitive idempotent that defines a minimal left ideal
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f
, and let
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k}\in\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{p+q})^{*}
define a
\mathrm{G}
-structure. We say that
the
\mathrm{G}
-structure is recoverable from
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f
if
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k}
can be written as algebraic expressions of the graded components of the primitive idempotent in the Clifford algebra; that is,
\phi_{1}=\sigma^{*}(\psi_{1}(f)),\ldots,\phi_{k}=\sigma^{*}(\psi_{k}(f))
for algebraic expressions
\psi_{1}(f),\ldots,\psi_{k}(f)
of
f
in the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
.

In this paper, unlike in
[
12
]
, we do not assume that the dimension of the underlying real space of the Clifford algebra (where we assign a primitive idempotent) and the dimension of the underlying real space of our exterior algebra (where we identify the
\mathrm{G}
-structure) are equal. Hence, we now introduce the concept of an embedded induced minimal left ideal in order to identify a
\mathrm{G}
-structure with a minimal left ideal in the present case.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k}\in\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{*}
define a
\mathrm{G}
-structure. We say that
the
\mathrm{G}
-structure induces an embedded minimal left ideal

{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}f
in the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
if the primitive idempotent
f
can be recovered from an algebraic expression
\psi(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k})
in
\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{*}
via the embedding of the extended quantization map
q^{*}:\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{*}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
, where
\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{*}
is isomorphic to its image in
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
for
p+q>n
.

We can also extend this concept, where the dimension of the Clifford algebra is larger than the dimension of the exterior algebra, to
\mathrm{G}
-structures recovered by minimal left ideals; but instead of an embedding, this is done by restricting the symbol map to a Clifford subalgebra.

Definition 2.9
.

We say that a
\mathrm{G}
-structure can be
recovered by projection
if there exists an element
\tilde{f}
in a subalgebra
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
(where
p+q>n
) such that
A
is isomorphic to a Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{r,s}
(where
r+s=n
), where the differential forms that makes up the
\mathrm{G}
-structure,
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k}\in\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{*}
, are the images of algebraic expressions
\psi_{1}(\tilde{f}),\ldots,\psi_{k}(\tilde{f})\in A
via the extension of the symbol map restricted to the subalgebra
A
; that is,
\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}:A\xrightarrow{\cong}\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{*}
, where
\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}(\psi_{1}(\tilde{f}))=\phi_{1},\ldots,\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}(\psi_{k}(\tilde{f}))=\phi_{k}
.

We now link the primitive idempotent and the
\mathrm{G}
-structures recovered by projection.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
A
be a Clifford subalgebra of
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
such that
A\cong{\mathbb{R}}_{r,s}
where
r+s=n
. Consider a
\mathrm{G}
-structure recovered by projection from
\tilde{f}\in A
. We say that
\tilde{f}
is a
factor of the primitive idempotent
that recovers a
\mathrm{G}
-structure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
if the differential forms that makes up the
\mathrm{G}
-structure,
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k}\in\wedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{*}
, are given by
\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}(\psi_{1}(\tilde{f}))=\phi_{1},\ldots,\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}(\psi_{k}(\tilde{f}))=\phi_{k}
, and the primitive idempotent
h
that defines the minimal left ideal
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}h
can be expressed as the product of
\tilde{f}
and an additional element
e
in
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
, that is,
h=\tilde{f}e.

In the next section, we show how we can identify
\textrm{U}(n)
-structures with their induced minimal left ideals, or their induced embedded minimal left ideals, for Clifford algebras of various signatures.

3.
\textrm{U}(n)
-structures and their induced minimal left ideals

The matrix group
\textrm{U}(n)
, known as the
unitary group
, is composed of
n\times n
complex matrices
U
such that
U^{*}U=I_{n}
, where
U^{*}
is the Hermitian conjugate of
U
. We can also view
\textrm{U}(n)
as a subgroup of
\textrm{SO}(2n)
. In order to view
\textrm{U}(n)
as a stabilizer subgroup of
\textrm{SO}(2n)
, we must first introduce a
\textrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-structure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
, that is, an
almost complex structure

J_{0}\in\textrm{End}({\mathbb{R}}^{2m})
such that
J_{0}^{2}=-\textrm{id}
.
After a choice of basis (ours will be the canonical one), we can view
({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},J_{0})
as a complex vector space, where via this identification we reduce the structure group
\textrm{GL}(2n,{\mathbb{R}})
to
\textrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}).
As stated in Section 2,
\textrm{SO}(2n)
is the stabilizer of the model tensors
g_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}e^{i}\otimes e^{i}
, and the volume form
dV_{g_{0}}=e^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{2n}.
Now if on
({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},J_{0})
the complex structure is
g_{0}
-orthogonal, we can define a skew-symmetric form
\omega_{0}(\cdot\,,\cdot):=g_{0}(J\cdot\,,\cdot)\in\bigwedge^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})^{*}
. This is known as the
standard Kahler form
. With the aid of the Kahler form on the vector space
({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},J_{0})
, we are able to define a Hermitian metric (a positive definite Hermitian inner product), via
h_{0}(\cdot\,,\cdot)=g_{0}(\cdot\,,\cdot)-i\omega_{0}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
whose stabilizer is the unitary group
\textrm{U}(n)
. Moreover, from the description of the Hermitian metric
h_{0}
as a sum of the real part
g_{0}
and the imaginary part
\omega_{0}
, we can view
\mathrm{U}(n)
as
\mathrm{U}(n)=\mathrm{O}(2n)\cap\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
where
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
is the subgroup of
\textrm{SO}(2n)
that stabilizes the symplectic form
\omega_{0}
. Now since, for the triple
(g_{0},J_{0},\omega_{0})
, it is possible to generate one of the tensors from the other two, via the identification
\omega_{0}(\cdot,\cdot)=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
we can identify with a
\textrm{U}(n)
-structure with stabilizer subgroup of the tensors
J_{0}
and
\omega_{0}
, that is,
\textrm{U}(n)=\textrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})\cap\textrm{Sp}(2n,{\mathbb{R}})
.
Hence, from now on, a
\textrm{U}(n)
-structure is identified with the parallel tensors
J_{0},\omega_{0},
and
g_{0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
, and it is viewed as the stabilizer of any two of the three tensor fields.

For our exploration in identifying
\mathrm{U}(n)
-structures with minimal left ideals of Clifford algebras, we fix a complex structure
J_{0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
such that on the standard basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{2n}
, we have
J_{0}(e_{k})=e_{k+n}
and
J_{0}(e_{k+n})=-e_{k}
for all
k=1,2,\ldots,n.
This complex structure allows us to identify
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
with
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
via
w_{k}=e_{k}+ie_{n+k}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,n.

Lemma 3.1
.

For the complex structure
J_{0}
such that
J_{0}(e_{k})=e_{k+n}
and
J_{0}(e_{k+n})=-e_{k}
for all
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, the standard Kahler form can be locally defined in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
by the formula

\omega_{0}=e^{1}\wedge e^{n+1}+\cdots+e^{n}\wedge e^{2n}
.

Proof.

For the complex structure given by
J_{0}
such that
J_{0}(e_{k})=e_{k+n}
, and
J_{0}(e_{k+n})=-e_{k}
for all
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, and the standard inner product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
given by
g_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}e^{i}\otimes e^{i}
, we define the standard Kahler form via
\omega_{0}(\cdot\,,\cdot)=g_{0}(J_{0}\cdot\,,\cdot).
For any two vectors
v,w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
, we have
\omega_{0}(v,w)=g_{0}(J_{0}(v),w).
Then we obtain

\displaystyle\omega_{0}(v,w)

\displaystyle=g_{0}(J_{0}(v),w)=g_{0}(J_{0}\big(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2n}v_{\alpha}\big),\sum_{\beta=1}^{2n}w_{\beta})

\displaystyle=g_{0}\big(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}v^{\alpha}e_{\alpha+n}-v^{\alpha+n}e_{\alpha},\sum_{\beta=1}^{2n}w^{\beta}e_{\beta}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\big\{g_{0}(v^{\alpha}e_{\alpha+n},w^{\alpha+n}e_{\alpha+n})-g_{0}(v^{\alpha+n}e_{\alpha},w^{\alpha}e_{\alpha})\big\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}(v^{\alpha}w^{\alpha+n}-v^{\alpha+n}w^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}e^{\alpha}\otimes e^{\alpha+n}-e^{\alpha+n}\otimes e^{\alpha}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}e^{\alpha}\wedge e^{\alpha+n}(v,w).

Therefore the local description of this symplectic form associated with
J_{0}
is
\omega_{0}=e^{1}\wedge e^{1+n}+\cdots+e^{n}\wedge e^{2n}
. It follows that
d\omega_{0}=0
; hence the symplectic form
\omega_{0}
is Kahler.
∎

Now if we take the wedge product of the Kahler form with itself, we obtain
\omega_{0}\wedge\omega_{0}=(e^{1}\wedge e^{1+n}+\cdots+e^{n}\wedge e^{2n})\wedge(e^{1}\wedge e^{1+n}+\cdots+e^{n}\wedge e^{2n})=\sum_{i\neq j}(e^{i}\wedge e^{i+n})\wedge(e^{j}\wedge e^{j+n})
. Since
(e^{i}\wedge e^{i+n})\wedge(e^{j}\wedge e^{j+n})=(e^{j}\wedge e^{j+n})\wedge(e^{i}\wedge e^{i+n})
for
i\neq j
, we get can rewrite the sum as

\omega_{0}\wedge\omega_{0}=2\sum_{i<j}^{n}e^{i}\wedge e^{i+n}\wedge e^{j}\wedge e^{j+n}.

Modifying the notation to write
\omega_{0}^{2}:=\omega_{0}\wedge\omega_{0}
, we have

\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{2}}{2}=\sum_{i<j}^{n}e^{i}\wedge e^{i+n}\wedge e^{j}\wedge e^{j+n}.

Extending this analysis to products of the form
e^{i_{1}}\wedge e^{i_{1}+n}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{m}}\wedge e^{i_{m}+n}
, where
1\leq i_{k}\leq n
for
k=1,2,\ldots,m
, we make use of the following two properties.

(1)

If, for any
k,j
such that
1\leq k,j\leq m\leq n
and
k\neq j
, we have
i_{k}=i_{j}
, then the entire product is zero; that is,
e^{i_{1}}\wedge e^{i_{1}+n}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{m}}\wedge e^{i_{m}+n}=0.

(2)

Since all products of the form
e^{i_{j}}\wedge e^{i_{j}+n}
commute, we can rearrange the product
e^{i_{1}}\wedge e^{i_{1}+n}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{m}}\wedge e^{i_{m}+n}
in
m!
different ways.

Now for an
m
product,
m\leq n
, of Kahler forms, we obtain

\omega_{0}^{m}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\leq n}e^{i_{1}}\wedge e^{i_{1}+n}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{m}}\wedge e^{i_{m}+n}.

By property
(1)
, it is immediate that only nonzero wedge products are associated to the indices where
i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}
are all distinct from one another. Moreover, from
(2)
, we can rearrange all of the nonzero sums in the same sequential order, namely one where
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n.
From this we can immediately see that

\omega_{0}^{m}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n}m!e^{i_{1}}\wedge e^{i_{1}+n}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{m}}\wedge e^{i_{m}+n},

or, equivalently,

\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{m}}{m!}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n}e^{i_{1}}\wedge e^{i_{1}+n}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{m}}\wedge e^{i_{m}+n}.

With
\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{m}}{m!}
well-defined for
0\leq m\leq n
, where for the
m=0
case we define
\omega_{0}^{0}=1
, we can now define an all one polynomial in the Kahler form
\omega_{0}
, which we state in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

For the standard Hermitian form in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
locally defined in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
by the formula
\omega_{0}=e^{1}\wedge e^{1+n}+\cdots+e^{n}\wedge e^{2n}
, we define the Kahler polynomial associated with
\omega_{0}
as the differential form

P(\omega_{0})=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{m}}{m!}.

The Kahler polynomial
P(\omega_{0})
is, in fact, an all one polynomial, as the leading coefficients of all of the terms are
1
. ∎

The expanded form of the Kahler polynomial is given by

P(\omega_{0})=1+\sum_{m=1}^{n}\,\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n}e^{i_{1}}\wedge e^{i_{1}+n}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{m}}\wedge e^{i_{m}+n}.

In order for us to relate the
\textrm{U}(n)
-structures to minimal left ideals for certain Clifford algebras of signature
(p,q)
, we modify the definition of the Kahler polynomial slightly.

Definition 3.3
.

For the Kahler polynomial
P(\omega_{0})
associated with the Kahler form
\omega_{0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
, we define the
rational Kahler polynomial
by
P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0})=\dfrac{1}{2^{n}}P(\omega_{0}).

The rational Kahler polynomial
P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0})
provides the necessary algebraic expression in order for our
\textrm{U}(n)
-structure to induce a minimal left ideal.

Theorem 3.4
.

The
U(p)
-structure
(J_{0}\,\omega_{0},g_{0})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
, where the complex structure is given by
J_{0}(e_{k})=e_{k+p}
and
J_{0}(e_{k+p})=-e_{k}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,p
, and where the Kahler form is locally defined by the formula

\omega_{0}=e^{1}\wedge e^{1+p}+\cdots+e^{p}\wedge e^{2p}
, induces a minimal left ideal on the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
, and induces an embedded minimal left ideal for the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
for
p\geq 1
. The defining primitive idempotent in terms of the
\textrm{U}(p)
-structure is given by
f_{\textrm{U}(p)}=q^{*}(P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0}))
.

Proof.

We begin with a
\textrm{U}(p)
-structure
(J_{0},\omega_{0})
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
where the complex structure is given by
J_{0}(e_{k})=e_{k+p}
and
J_{0}(e_{k+p})=-e_{k}
, for
k=1,2,\ldots,p
. The Kahler form
\omega_{0}=e^{1}\wedge e^{1+p}+\cdots+e^{p}\wedge e^{2p}
then defines the rational Kahler polynomial
P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0})\in\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{2p})^{*}
.
Shifting our attention to Clifford algebras of the form
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
,
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
, and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
where
p\geq 1
, we introduce the index notation
\sigma_{j}=(j,p+j)
for
j=1,2,\ldots,p
, where we have, for any
1\leq i<j\leq p
the commutation property
e_{\sigma_{i}}e_{\sigma_{j}}=e_{\sigma_{j}}e_{\sigma_{i}}
.
From this, we define the following element for
m\leq p
:

f_{m}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq p}e_{\sigma_{i_{1}}}\cdots e_{\sigma_{i_{k}}}.

When
m=p
, we denote the element
\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}f_{p}
by
f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
.

Now the rational Kahler polynomial given by
\omega_{0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
is explicitly given by the formula

P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0})=\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}\left(1+\sum_{m=1}^{p}\,\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq p}e^{i_{1}}\wedge e^{i_{1}+p}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{m}}\wedge e^{i_{m}+p}\right).

Applying the extended quantization map,
q^{*}:\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{2p})^{*}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
, to the rational Kahler polynomial, we obtain

\displaystyle q^{*}(P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0}))

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}\left(1+\sum_{m=1}^{p}\,\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq p}q^{*}(e^{i_{1}}\wedge e^{i_{1}+p}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{m}}\wedge e^{i_{m}+p})\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}\left(1+\sum_{m=1}^{p}\,\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq p}e_{i_{1}}e_{i_{1}+p}\cdots e_{i_{m}}e_{i_{m}+p}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}\left(1+\sum_{m=1}^{p}\,\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq p}e_{\sigma_{1}}\cdots e_{\sigma_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}f_{p}=:f_{\textrm{U}(p)}.

Moreover, if we use the extended quantization map
q^{*}
to embed the algebra
\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{2p})^{*}
into its isomorphic image in
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
, we again have the result
q^{*}(P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0}))=f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
. Thus we can conclude that
q^{*}(P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0}))=f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
in the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p},{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1},
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
.
To show that these are minimal left ideals, we begin with the fact that we can decompose
f_{p}
in the following manner:

f_{p}=(f_{p-1}+f_{p-1}e_{\sigma_{p}}),

Where the expanded form is

f_{p}=\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq p-1}e_{\sigma_{i_{1}}}\cdots e_{\sigma_{i_{k}}}\right)+\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq p-1}e_{\sigma_{i_{1}}}\cdots e_{\sigma_{i_{k}}}\right)e_{p}.

Thus, we can decompose the image
q^{*}(P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0}))=f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
as
q^{*}(P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0}))=\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}(f_{p-1}+f_{p-1}e_{\sigma_{p}}).

Now if we split off the
e_{p-1}
term in
f_{p-1}
in an identical manner, we obtain
f_{p-1}=f_{p-2}+f_{p-2}e_{\sigma_{p-1}}
, from which we obtain
f_{\textrm{U}(p)}=\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}f_{p-1}(1+e_{\sigma_{p}})=\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}f_{p-2}(1+e_{\sigma_{p-1}})(1+e_{\sigma_{p}}).

Continuing with this process until we get the full factorization for
q^{*}(P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0}))=f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
gives us the following:

f_{\textrm{U}(p)}=\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}\prod_{i=1}^{p}(1+e_{\sigma_{i}})=\prod_{i=1}^{p}\big(\dfrac{1+e_{\sigma_{i}}}{2}\big).

As we established above, all of the
e_{\sigma_{i}}
for
i=1,2,3,\ldots,p
are commuting involutions in the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p},{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1},
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
; and from Theorem
2.5
, we can see that for these Clifford algebras the number of commuting involutions needed to generate a primitive idempotent is
p
.
We provide the calculation of this fact in Table
1
.

k=p-r_{2}=p+2-2=p

Thus it follows that
f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
is a primitive idempotent for
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p},{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1},
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
. Hence the
\textrm{U}(p)
-structure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
induces a minimal left ideal for the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
, and induces an embedded minimal left ideal for the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p},{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1},
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}.

∎

Since
\textrm{U}(n)
contains
\textrm{SU}(n)
as a subgroup, we have that
SU(n)
, when viewed as a stabilizer subgroup of
\textrm{SO}(2n)
, will be the stabilizer of the same tensor fields as
\textrm{U}(n)
with one additional structure. That is, it is also the stabilizer of the complex volume form
\Omega=dz^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz^{n}
, where the complex coordinates in our construction are given by
dz^{j}=e^{j}+ie^{p+j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n.

Now since
U(p)
-structures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
induce minimal left ideals (resp. embedded minimal left ideals) on the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
(resp.
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
) with the aid of the rational Kahler polynomial, it is immediate that
\textrm{SU}(p)
-structures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
also induce minimal left ideals (resp. embedded minimal left ideals) on Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
(resp.
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
) with the aid of the same rational Kahler polynomial. Thus, when using an
\textrm{SU}(p)
-structure to generate minimal left ideals, we make no immediate use of the complex volume form. We therefore have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let the complex structure
J_{0}
and the Kahler form
\omega_{0}
define a
U(p)
-structure that induces a minimal left ideal (resp. an embedded minimal left ideal) for the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
(resp.
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
) as in Theorem
3.4
. Then any
SU(p)
-structure where the complex structure and Kahler form coincide with the
U(p)
-structure also induces a minimal left ideal (resp. embedded minimal left ideal) for the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
(resp.
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
). Moreover, the primitive idempotent that defines the minimal left ideal is given by
f_{\textrm{SU}(p)}=q^{*}(P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0})).

∎

As is well known (see
[
8
]
), the minimal left ideals we described,
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,s}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
for
s=p,p+1,p+2
, are themselves right
\mathfrak{D}=f_{\textrm{U}(p)}{\mathbb{R}}_{p,s}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
-modules. The minimal left ideals
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}f_{\textrm{U}(p)},{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}f_{\textrm{U}(p)},
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
can be seen as vector spaces of real dimension
2^{p},2^{p+1},
and
2^{p+2}
respectively, where the minimal left ideal representations can be given by the left multiplication endomorphism:

\displaystyle L:{\mathbb{R}}_{p,s}\rightarrow\textrm{End}_{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathbb{R}}_{p,s}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}).

Given the matrix algebra isomorphism for each Clifford algebra, it is immediate that
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
is of real type,
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
is of complex type, and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
is of quaternionic type. Moreover, it is well known (see
[
8
]
) that
\mathfrak{O}
for these Clifford algebras takes the isomorphisms shown in Table
2
.

-modules can be thought of as right

and
\mathbb{H}

{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}

{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
will yield the real representation
\textrm{End}_{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}f_{\textrm{U}(p)})\cong{\mathbb{R}}(2^{p}).
The Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
, on the other hand, is of complex type. Moreover, since the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
is an odd Clifford algebra, the pseudoscalar
\Gamma=e_{1}\cdots e_{2p+1}
is negative definite and central for this signature (see
[
2
]
for more information on the pseudoscalar). That is, we can use
\Gamma
as our complex structure that gives our minimal left ideal a complex vector space structure, where
(a+bi)hf_{\textrm{U}(p)}=ahf_{\textrm{U}(p)}+\Gamma hf_{\textrm{U}(p)}.
Hence, with the aid of
\Gamma
, we can define a complex basis on the minimal left ideal
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
, where left multiplication by
\Gamma
will coincide with right multiplication by
f_{\textrm{U}(p)}\Gamma f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
. This is immediate since for any
hf_{\textrm{U}(p)}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
, we have

\displaystyle(hf_{\textrm{U}(p)})(f_{\textrm{U}(p)}\Gamma f_{\textrm{U}(p)})

\displaystyle=h(f_{\textrm{U}(p)})^{2}\Gamma f_{\textrm{U}(p)}

\displaystyle=hf_{\textrm{U}(p)}\Gamma f_{\textrm{U}(p)}

\displaystyle=h\Gamma(f_{\textrm{U}(p)})^{2}

\displaystyle=h\Gamma f_{\textrm{U}(p)}

\displaystyle=\Gamma hf_{\textrm{U}(p)}.

It is immediate that
\Gamma
, which will define our complex structure via the left multiplication endomorphism, will also define the right
\mathfrak{O}
-structure on

{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
; and with this choice of complex structure, we obtain matrix representations
\textrm{End}_{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}f_{\textrm{U}(p)})\cong{\mathbb{C}}(2^{p}).

Lastly, the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
is of quaternionic type, and we have a convenient choice for the quaternionic structure on the minimal left ideal
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
. Notice that
e_{2p+1},e_{2p+2},
and
e_{2p+1}\cdot e_{2p+2}
define a quaternion structure on
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
in the sense that the left action by
a+bi+cj+dk\in\mathbb{H}
can be identified with a left action by
a+be_{2p+1}+ce_{2p+2}+de_{2p+1}\cdot e_{2p+2}
. All of these elements commute with the primitive idempotent
f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
, and hence are the elements in
\mathfrak{O}
that define the isomorphism
\mathfrak{O}\cong\mathbb{H}
. This choice of quaternionic structure, giving us an
\mathbb{H}
basis on
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
that also allows us to define a right
\mathfrak{O}
module structure on
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
, will provide us with the isomorphism
\textrm{End}_{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}f_{\textrm{U}(p)})\cong\mathbb{H}(2^{p}).

We conclude this section with three examples that relate to
\textrm{U}(3)
-structures.

3.1.
\textrm{U}(3)
-structure and the induced minimal left ideal in the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{3,3}

We begin with a
U(3)
-structure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{6}
given by a Kahler form in
{\mathbb{R}}^{6}
that is described locally by the equation
\omega_{0}=e^{14}+e^{25}+e^{36}
, and a complex structure
J_{0}\in\textrm{End}({\mathbb{R}}^{6})
completely determined by the Kahler form
\omega_{0}
and the standard inner product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{6}
via the relation
\omega_{0}(\cdot\,,\cdot)=\langle J_{0}(\cdot),\cdot\rangle.

The rational Kahler polynomial is then given by

\displaystyle P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}(1+\omega_{0}+\frac{\omega_{0}\wedge\omega_{0}}{2}+\frac{\omega_{0}^{3}}{6})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}(1+e^{14}+e^{25}+e^{36}+e^{1425}+e^{1436}+e^{2536}+e^{142536})\in\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{6})^{*}.

From the extension of the quantization map
q^{*}:\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{6})^{*}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{3,3}
, we send the rational Kahler polynomial into the expression
q^{*}(P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0}))=\dfrac{1}{8}(e_{14}+e_{25}+e_{36}+e_{1425}+e_{1436}+e_{2536}+e_{142536})
, which decomposes as the product to
(\frac{1+e_{14}}{2})(\frac{1+e_{25}}{2})(\frac{1+e_{36}}{2})
in the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{3,3}
. By Theorem
2.5
, it is immediate that this is a primitive idempotent, which we denote
f_{\textrm{U}(3)}
, that gives us the minimal left ideal
{\mathbb{R}}_{3,3}f_{\textrm{U}(3)}
.

This minimal left ideal is an
8
-dimensional real vector space given by the following basis elements:

(1)

f_{\textrm{U}(3)}=\frac{1}{8}(1+e_{14}+e_{25}+e_{36}-e_{1245}-e_{1346}-e_{2356}-e_{123456})

(2)

g_{0}=(e^{1})^{2}+\cdots+(e^{6})^{2}
have an induced minimal left ideal
{\mathbb{R}}_{3,3}f_{\textrm{U}(3)}
, where the primitive idempotent is given by
q^{*}(P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0}))=f_{\textrm{U}(3)}
.
One can then view the complex structure and the quaternionic structures in the minimal left ideals

{\mathbb{R}}_{3,4}f_{\textrm{U}(3)}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{3,5}f_{\textrm{U}(3)}
as direct sums of copies of the minimal left ideal
{\mathbb{R}}_{3,3}f_{\textrm{U}(3)}
, showing that the induced embedded minimal left ideals for the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{3,4}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{3,5}
are all induced and well understood from the same
\textrm{U}(3)
-structure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{6}.

4.
\textrm{U}(n)
-structures recovered from Clifford algebras of certain signatures

In this section, we start with any Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
and ask, for which signatures can we recover a
\textrm{U}(m)
-structure, and for which signatures can we recover a
\textrm{U}(m)
-structure by projection? For the
\textrm{U}(m)
-structures recoverable by projection, we then ask how the image of the primitive idempotent looks in the larger exterior algebra
\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{p+q})^{*}
. We begin with an immediate corollary to Theorem
3.4
, in which we identify the recovery of a
\textrm{U}(p)
-structure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
, directly or from projection, from Clifford algebras of signatures
(p,p)
,
(p,p+1),
and
(p,p+2)
.

Corollary 4.1
.

For real Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
, we can always recover a
\textrm{U}(p)
-structure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
from minimal left ideals
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}f
; and for the real Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
, we can recover a
\textrm{U}(p)
-structure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
by projection, where the rational Kahler polynomial on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
is given by
\sigma^{*}(f)=P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0})
, and the Kahler form is given by the expression
\sigma^{*}(\langle 2^{m}f\rangle_{2})=\omega_{0}
.

Proof.

For the real Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
,
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1},
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
such that
p\geq 1
, the primitive idempotents
f
that generate the minimal left ideals are generated by
p
commuting involutions. From Theorem
3.4
, we have that the minimal left ideal for each of these Clifford algebras is given by
f_{\textrm{U}(p)}:=q^{*}(P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0}))=\prod_{i=1}^{p}\big(\dfrac{1+e_{i}e_{i+p}}{2}\big)
, where
\omega_{0}
is the Kahler form on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
induced by the standard Euclidean inner product and the complex structure given by
J(e_{k})=e_{k+p}
and
J(e_{k+p})=-e_{p}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,p
. Hence if we define
f_{p}:=2^{p}f_{\textrm{U}(p)}:=2^{p}q^{*}(P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\omega_{0}))
, then the image of
f_{p}
under the symbol map
\sigma^{*}:{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}\rightarrow\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{2p})^{*}
is the Kahler polynomial
P(\omega_{0})
; that is,
P(\omega_{0})=\sigma^{*}(f_{p})
. From this, if we restrict
f_{p}
to its degree
2
components, we recover the Kahler form in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
; that is,
\sigma^{*}(\langle f_{p}\rangle_{2})=\omega_{0}
. Thus, for the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
, we are able to recover a
\textrm{U}(p)
-structure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
. For the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
, we define the subalgebra generated by the standard basis vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
as
A={\mathbb{R}}\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{2p}\rangle
. It then follows that

A\cong{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
. The image of this algebra under the extended symbol map is isomorphic to
\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{2p})^{*}
. Hence, for the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
, we have
\textrm{U}(p)
-structures recovered by projection on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
via
\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}(\langle f_{p}\rangle_{2})=\tilde{\omega}_{0}
, where we define
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
as the Kahler form in the underlying vector space that generates
\sigma^{*}(A)
isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
. The Kahler form
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
is isomorphic to
\omega_{0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
when the underlying vector spaces are identified. Since
f_{p}
is an algebraic expression of
f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
in the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
, we have that from minimal left ideals on the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
, we are able to recover by projection a
\textrm{U}(p)
-structure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

In the proof of Corollary
4.1
, we do not make any mention of recovering the almost complex structure
J_{0}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
needed to have a
\textrm{U}(p)
-structure. The reason is that
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
has the standard Euclidean inner product, and since we can recover the Kahler form
\omega_{0}
from
f_{\textrm{U}(p)}
, it then follows that we can easily recover
J_{0}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
. This is because in a
\textrm{U}(p)
-structure, any two parallel tensors define the third. In fact, given that
\sigma^{*}(\langle f\rangle_{2})=\omega_{0}=e^{1}\wedge e^{p+1}+\cdots+e^{p}\wedge e^{2p}
, it is clear that the recovered complex structure
J_{0}
is defined by
J(e_{k})=e_{k+m}
and
J(e_{k+m})=-e_{m}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,p
.

We now turn our attention to Clifford algebras of signature
(p,q)
in general, for
p\neq q
and
p,q\neq 0
. In the general case, we do not recover a
\textrm{U}(m)
-structure projectively from the primitive idempotent that defines the minimal left ideal, as was the case for
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
,
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+1}
, and
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+2}
. Instead, the
U(p)
-structure is projectively recovered from a factor of the primitive idempotent that defines a minimal left ideal for Clifford algebras of signature
(p,q)
.

Proposition 4.3
.

For real Clifford algebras of signature
(p,q)
such that
p\neq q
,
p,q\neq 0
, and
q\not\in\{p+1,p+2\}
, there exists a factor
\tilde{f}
of a primitive idempotent
h
that recovers by projection a
\textrm{U}(m)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2m}
, where
m=\textrm{min}\{p,q\}
. Moreover, the image of the primitive idempotent
h=\tilde{f}e
in
\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{p+q})^{*}
via the extended symbol map splits as the product
\sigma^{*}(h)=P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\tilde{\omega_{0}})\wedge\sigma^{*}(e)
, where
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
is a
2
-form in
{\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}
isomorphic to the Kahler form in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2m}
that defines the
\textrm{U}(m)
-structure projectively recovered in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2m}
.

Proof.

For Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,q}
such that
p\neq q
,
p,q\neq 0
, and
q\not\in\{p+1,p+2\}
, we break down our analysis into two cases.

(1)

If
p>q
, then we can write
p=q+l
for some
l>0
. Thus, for the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{q+l,q}
, we can index
q
generators of degree
2
of the canonical basis as
e_{\sigma_{j}}=e_{j}e_{{}_{q+j}}
for
j=1,\ldots,q
. We then define
\tilde{f}=(1+e_{\sigma_{1}})\cdots(1+e_{\sigma_{q}})
, where
e_{\sigma_{j}}^{2}=1
and each
e_{\sigma_{j}}
commutes with all other elements
e_{\sigma_{k}}
where
j\neq k
. The expanded form
\tilde{f}
is given by

\tilde{f}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{q}\,\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}\leq q}e_{\sigma_{j_{1}}}\cdots e_{\sigma_{j_{k}}}.

In the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{q+l,q}
, we denote the subalgebra generated by the basis vectors
e_{1},\ldots,e_{q},e_{q+1+l},\ldots,e_{2q+l}
as
A={\mathbb{R}}\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{q},e_{q+1+l},\ldots,e_{2q+l}\rangle
. It is immediate that this Clifford algebra
A
is isomorphic to the real Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{q,q}
.
Restricting the symbol map to the subalgebra
A
, we have at the level of images that
\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}(A)\cong\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{2q})^{*}
. Utilizing this isomorphism, we now have
\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}(\tilde{f})=P(\omega_{0})
, where
\sigma\big|_{A}^{*}(\langle\tilde{f}\rangle_{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}(e_{\sigma_{j}})=\sum_{j=1}^{q}e^{j}\wedge e^{q+j}:=\omega_{0}\in\wedge^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2q})^{*}
. Hence
\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}(\tilde{f})
gives us our Kahler form
\omega_{0}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2q}
, and thus we have a
\textrm{U}(q)
-structure recovered by the projection of the element
\tilde{f}\in A
. For the minimal left ideal associated with the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{q+l,q}
, the primitive idempotent that defines it,
h
, is the product of
k
commuting involutions (see Theorem
2.5
). Considering that in the construction of
\tilde{f}
we already have
q
commuting positive definite generators, we may partition
k
as
k=q+m
for some
m\geq 0
. Thus, to generate a primitive idempotent
h
in
{\mathbb{R}}_{q+l,q}
, we need an additional
m
more commuting positive definite generators chosen from the subalgebra
{\mathbb{R}}\langle e_{q+1},\ldots,e_{q+l}\rangle\cong{\mathbb{R}}_{l,0}
; we denote these generators by
e_{\alpha_{1}},\dots,e_{\alpha_{m}}
. Hence our primitive idempotent will be of the form
h=(\dfrac{1}{2^{q}}\tilde{f})(e)=\dfrac{1}{2^{q}}(\tilde{f}e)
, where
e=\dfrac{1}{2^{m}}(1+e_{\alpha_{1}})\cdots(1+e_{\alpha_{m}}).
By construction, this product is a sum of elementary tensors. Therefore, the symbol map preserves the Clifford product that makes up
h
. That is,
\sigma^{*}(\dfrac{1}{2^{q}}\tilde{f}e)=\sigma^{*}(\dfrac{1}{2^{q}}\tilde{f})\wedge\sigma^{*}(e)=P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\tilde{\omega_{0}})\wedge\sigma^{*}(e)
, where
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
is the image of the
2
-form in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2q+l}
generated by
\tilde{f}\in A
isomorphic to the Kahler
2
-form
\omega_{0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2q}
that defines our
\textrm{U}(q)
-structure recovered by projection, and
\sigma^{*}(e)
is an exterior form in the subalgebra
\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{l})^{*}\subset\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{2q+l})^{*}
, where we view
{\mathbb{R}}^{l}={\mathbb{R}}\{e_{q+1},\ldots,e_{q+l}\}
, and we view
{\mathbb{R}}^{2q+l}={\mathbb{R}}^{2q}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
.

(2)

If
p<q
, then we can write
q=p+l
for some
l>0
. Then on the Clifford algebras
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+l}
, we can index as we did above:
e_{\sigma_{j}}=e_{j}e_{{}_{p+j}}
for
j=1,\ldots,p
, where
e_{\sigma_{j}}^{2}=1
for
j=1,\ldots,p
, and the
e_{\sigma_{j}}
are commuting elements in
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+l}
. When we expand the product, we obtain

\tilde{f}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{p}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{k}\leq p}e_{\sigma_{j_{1}}}\cdots e_{\sigma_{j_{k}}}.

In the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+l}
, we have that the subalgebra
A={\mathbb{R}}\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{2p}\rangle
is isomorphic to the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p}
.
Again, when we restrict the symbol map to the subalgebra
A
, we have at the level of images that
\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}(A)\cong\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{2p})^{*}
. Using this isomorphism, we now have
\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}(\tilde{f})=P(\omega_{0})
, where
\sigma\big|_{A}^{*}(\langle\tilde{f}\rangle_{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sigma^{*}\big|_{A}(e_{\sigma_{j}})=\sum_{j=1}^{p}e^{j}\wedge e^{p+j}:=\omega_{0}\in\wedge^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2p})^{*}
. Hence we have a
\textrm{U}(p)
-structure
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
recovered by the projection of our constructed element
\tilde{f}\in A
.
Since in the construction of
\tilde{f}
we already have
p
commuting positive definite generators, we may partition
k
as
k=p+m
for some
m\geq 0
(where
m=0
when we have signature
(p,p+1)
or
(p,p+2)
).
Since we have
p
commuting involutions used for the construction of the element
\tilde{f}
, where
\sigma^{*}(\tilde{f})=P(\omega_{0})
, the primitive idempotent that generates the minimal left ideal can be generated using these
p
commuting involutions and an additional
m
commuting involutions chosen from the subalgebra
{\mathbb{R}}\langle e_{2p+1},\ldots,e_{2p+l}\rangle\cong{\mathbb{R}}_{0,l}
, which again we denote
e_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,e_{\alpha_{m}}
.
Our primitive idempotent will be of the form
h=(\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}\tilde{f})(e)=\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}(\tilde{f}e)
, where
e=\dfrac{1}{2^{m}}(1+e_{\alpha_{1}})\cdots(1+e_{\alpha_{m}}).

Given that this product is the sum of monomials of elementary tensors (basis elements in the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{R}}_{p,p+l}
) that do not overlap in indices, the symbol map preserves the product. Thus, we have
\sigma^{*}(\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}\tilde{f}e)=\sigma^{*}(\dfrac{1}{2^{p}}\tilde{f})\wedge\sigma^{*}(e)=P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\tilde{\omega_{0}})\wedge\sigma^{*}(e)
, where
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
is the image of the
2
-form in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p+l}
generated by
\tilde{f}\in A
isomorphic to the Kahler
2
-form
\omega_{0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p}
that defines our
\textrm{U}(q)
-structure recovered by projection, and
\sigma^{*}(e)
is an exterior form in the subalgebra
\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{l})^{*}\subset\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{2p+l})^{*}
, where we view
{\mathbb{R}}^{l}={\mathbb{R}}\{e_{2p+1},\ldots,e_{2p+l}\}
, and we view
{\mathbb{R}}^{2p+l}={\mathbb{R}}^{2p}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
.

Hence we have shown that for any signature
(p,q)
where
p\neq q
,
p,q\neq 0
, and
q\not\in\{p+1,p+2\}
, we can find a factor of a minimal left ideal that recovers a
\textrm{U}(m)
-structure through projection. Moreover, the image of the primitive idempotent
h
in the exterior algebra
\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{p+q})^{*}
is the product of the rational Kahler polynomial
P^{\mathbb{Q}}(\tilde{\omega}_{0})
, where
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
is a
2
-form in
{\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}
isomorphic to the Kahler form in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2m}
, and an exterior form
\sigma^{*}(e)
in the exterior subalgebra
\bigwedge^{\bullet}({\mathbb{R}}^{l})^{*}
.

∎

5.
Future research

Having established a relationship between
\textrm{U}(n)
-structures and their associated minimal left ideals, we next plan to focus on the following questions:

(1)

How can we extend this identification between
\mathrm{U}(n)
-structures and minimal left ideals to spinor bundles on Kahler manifolds?

(2)

Can we establish an identification between the quaternionic holonomy groups and minimal left ideals on Clifford algebras?

(3)

Is there a way we can associate minimal left ideals with the holonomy groups of pseudo-Riemannian manifolds?

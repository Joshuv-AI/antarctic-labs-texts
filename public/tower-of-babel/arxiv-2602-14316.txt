Observability and Semiclassical Control for Schrödinger Equations on Non-compact Hyperbolic Surfaces
Xin Fu, Yulin Gong, Yunlei Wang

Abstract
. We study the observability of the Schrödinger equation on X X , a non-compact covering space of a compact hyperbolic surface M M . Using a generalized Bloch theory, functions on X X are identified as sections of flat Hilbert bundles over M M . We develop a semiclassical analysis framework for such bundles and generalize the result of semiclassical control estimates in [Dyatlov and Jin, Acta Math., 220 (2018), pp. 297-339] to all flat Hilbert bundles over M M , with uniform constants with respect to the choice of bundle. Furthermore, when the Riemannian cover X → M X\to M is a normal cover with a virtually Abelian deck transformation group Γ \Gamma , we combine the uniform semiclassical control estimates on flat Hilbert bundles with the generalized Bloch theory to derive observability from any Γ \Gamma -periodic open subsets of X X . We also discuss applications of the uniform semiclassical control estimates in spectral geometry.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we study observability for the Schrödinger equation on a non-compact connected Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
. The corresponding Cauchy problem is

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\Delta_{g}u=0&&\text{in }(0,\infty)\times X,\\
&u|_{t=0}=u_{0}&&\mathrm{on}\ X,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Delta_{g}
denotes the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
(X,g)
, locally given by

\Delta_{g}f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{G}}\partial_{i}\big(\sqrt{G}g^{ij}\partial_{j}f\big),\qquad G=\mathrm{det}(g_{ij}),

and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(X)
. The notion of observability is given as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
T>0
and
S
be a measurable subset of
X
with positive measure. Problem (
1.1
) is said to be
observable from the set
S
in time
T>0
if there exists a constant
C=C(X,S,T)>0
such that, for any
u_{0}\in L^{2}(X)
, the mild solution
u(t,x)
of problem (
1.1
) satisfies

(1.2)

\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{S}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dxdt.

By the Hilbert uniqueness method
[
40
,
41
]
, observability is equivalent to exact controllability. More precisely, problem (
1.1
) is observable from the set
S
in time
T>0
if and only if, for any
u_{0},u_{1}\in L^{2}(X)
, there exists a control
f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times S)
such that the following problem is solvable:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\Delta_{g}u=f&&\text{in }(0,\infty)\times X,\\
&(u|_{t=0},u|_{t=T})=(u_{0},u_{1})&&\mathrm{on}\ X.\end{aligned}\right.

Observability has been extensively studied over the past decades, especially on compact Riemannian manifolds with and without boundary. We shall present a brief review in Section
1.3
. In the compact setting, if
S
satisfies the Geometric Control Condition (GCC), first introduced by
[
6
]
, that is, every (generalized) geodesic meets
S
within a fixed time
T_{0}>0
, then the observability inequality (
1.2
) holds for any
T>0
by Lebeau’s approach
[
38
]
. However, while GCC is necessary and sufficient for observability of wave equations, it is only a sufficient condition for observability of Schrödinger equations on standard compact domains, such as disks, tori and compact hyperbolic surfaces.

By contrast, very little is known for non-compact domains in which GCC fails, since the usual compactness arguments break down. In this case one must develop new techniques to overcome the lack of compactness. The main goal of this work is to establish observability for Schrödinger equations on certain kinds of non-compact Riemannian manifolds from observation sets that do not satisfy GCC.

1.1.
Problem setting

Throughout this article, we assume that
(M,g)
is a compact connected Riemannian manifold, and
\pi:X\rightarrow M
is a Riemannian covering map with the set of right cosets

\Gamma=\pi_{*}(\pi_{1}(X))\backslash\pi_{1}(M).

See (
2.12
) for the explicit meaning of
\Gamma
. We note that if
\pi
is normal, then
\Gamma
is isomorphic to the deck transformation group of
\pi
. We assume that
\Omega\subset M
is a non-empty open set and
S=\pi^{-1}(\Omega)
. In other words,
S
is a
\Gamma
-periodic set with the fundamental domain
\Omega
.

The following question arises naturally:

Question 1.2
.

If problem (
1.1
) on the base space
M
is observable from
\Omega
in time
T>0
, does it follow that the corresponding problem on
X
is observable from
S=\pi^{-1}(\Omega)
in time
T
, and how does the control constant
C
in (
1.2
) depend on
\Gamma
?

For the Euclidean case
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and
M=\mathbb{T}^{d}
, this question has been answered affirmatively in
[
60
,
57
,
36
]
. In this work, we treat the case where
M
is a compact hyperbolic surface and
\Gamma
may be non-commutative.

1.2.
Main results

Under suitable assumptions on
\pi
and
\Gamma
, we will give an affirmative answer to question
1.2
(see Theorem
1.6
). Before giving the precise statement of the main result, we need some technical preparations.

To study the observability problem on the covering space
X
, we employ a generalized Bloch theory that reduces this problem to an observability problem on flat Hilbert bundles over the base space
M
. In Section
2.2
, we extend the non-commutative Bloch transform (see
[
50
, (5.1)]
) to an arbitrary Riemannian covering map
\pi:X\to M
and construct a unitary isometry
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}:L^{2}(X)\rightarrow L^{2}(M;F^{\rho_{\pi}})
, where
F^{\rho_{\pi}}
denotes a flat Hilbert bundle over
M
. The non-commutative Bloch transform
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}
satisfies

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\circ\Delta_{g}=\Delta^{\rho_{\pi}}\circ\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}},

where
\Delta^{\rho_{\pi}}
denotes the twisted Bochner–Laplace operator; see Section
2.1
.

The discussion above leads us to study observability for Schrödinger equations on flat Hilbert bundles over
M
. For this problem, we use the microlocal approach initiated in
[
53
,
54
,
6
,
13
]
. The argument proceeds in two steps:

1.

obtaining a semiclassical (high-frequency) control estimate;

2.

removing the remaining low-frequency term through a compactness argument.

In the scalar case on compact hyperbolic surfaces, the semiclassical control estimate was obtained by Dyatlov and Jin
[
18
]
. In this work, we develop a semiclassical calculus on flat Hilbert bundles over general compact Riemannian manifolds and extend the results of
[
18
]
to the setting of flat Hilbert bundles over compact hyperbolic surfaces, which are of independent interest.

1.2.1.
Semiclassical control estimates on flat Hilbert bundles

Let
\pi_{1}(M)
be the fundamental group of
M
. We denote

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{m}:=\{(\mathcal{H},\rho):

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\mathrm{\ is\ a\ Hilbert\ space\ with}\ \mathrm{dim}\,\mathcal{H}\leq m,

\displaystyle\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})\mathrm{\ is\ a\ unitary\ representation}\},

and

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}:=\{(\mathcal{H},\rho):

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\mathrm{\ is\ a\ separable\ Hilbert\ space},

\displaystyle\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})\mathrm{\ is\ a\ unitary\ representation}\}.

For each
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}
, the associated flat Hilbert bundle
F^{\rho}
is defined in (
2.2
) (by the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, any flat Hilbert bundle over
M
has the form of
F^{\rho}
for some
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}
).

For each
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}
and
0<h\leq 1
, we introduce, in Section
4.2
, the quantization procedure

\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(a)\in\Psi^{\mathrm{comp},sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho}):L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\to L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})

for
a=a(x,\xi;h)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
. Here the superscript
sc
indicates that the symbol
a
is scalar-valued. The key observation is that the operator-norms of
\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(a)
are
uniformly
bounded in
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}
and
0<h\leq 1
; see Proposition
4.5
. Conversely, for any
A\in\Psi^{\mathrm{comp},sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
, we define the principal symbol
\sigma^{\rho}_{h}(A)
in Definition
4.9
such that
A-\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(\sigma^{\rho}_{h}(A))\in h\Psi^{\mathrm{comp},sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
.

To define the cutoff Schrödinger propagator
\exp\left(-itP/h\right)
in (
4.54
), as defined in
[
18
, (2.13)]
, we need to show that the functional calculus of the semiclassical twisted Laplacian
-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}
remains in
\Psi^{\mathrm{comp},sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
:

Proposition 1.1
.

For any
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

(1.5)

f(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})\in\Psi^{\mathrm{comp},sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\quad\mathrm{with}\ \sigma_{h}^{\rho}(f(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}))=f(|\xi|^{2}_{g}).

Moreover,
\mathrm{WF}_{h}(f(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}))\subset\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M:|\xi|_{g}^{2}\in\mathrm{supp}\,f\}
.

Following the proof of the semiclassical control estimate in
[
18
, Theorem 2]
, we extend the Fourier integral operators to the setting of flat Hilbert bundles and introduce an anisotropic quantization associated with a Lagrangian foliation
L
on
T^{*}M
. More precisely, for any anisotropic symbol
a\in S^{\mathrm{comp}}_{L,\mu}
, we define

\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho,L}(a):L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\mapsto L^{2}(M;F^{\rho});

see Sections
4.4
and
4.5
. Notably, the operator-norms of
\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho,L}(a)
are also uniformly bounded in
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}
and
0<h\leq 1
. Moreover, we show that the long-time propagation of symbol
a\in C_{0}^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
belongs to the anisotropic symbol class
S^{\mathrm{comp}}_{L,\mu}
, which yields a uniform long-time Egorov’s Theorem
4.20
.

Notice that the key feature of the quantization procedures introduced in Section
4
is the uniformity of the constants appearing in the relevant inequalities with respect to
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}
. As a consequence, we extend the semiclassical control estimates in
[
18
]
to uniform semiclassical control estimates on flat Hilbert bundles. The following theorem is a direct corollary of Theorem
5.1
.

Throughout this work, we use
C(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n})
to denote a constant depending only on the parameters
\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}
, whose value may change from line to line.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a compact hyperbolic surface and
a\in C_{0}^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
such that
a|_{S^{*}M}\not\equiv 0
. There exist constants
C=C(M,a)>0
and
h_{0}=h_{0}(M,a)>0
such that for any
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}
, any
0<h<h_{0}
and any
u\in H^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
,

(1.6)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C\left(\left\|\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(a)u\right\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}+\frac{|\log h|}{h}\left\|\left(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}-I\right)u\right\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\right).

A direct corollary of Theorem
1.2
is the
uniform
lower bound of eigensections. For any
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}
, let
u_{\lambda,\rho}
be a normalized eigensection of
-\Delta^{\rho}:L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\to L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
with respect to eigenvalue
\lambda
,
i.e.
,

(1.7)

\Delta^{\rho}u_{\lambda,\rho}+\lambda u_{\lambda,\rho}=0,\qquad\|u_{\lambda,\rho}\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}=1.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
M
be a compact hyperbolic surface and
\Omega\subset M
be a nonempty open subset. There exist constants
c(M,\Omega)>0
and
\lambda_{0}(M,\Omega)>0
such that for any
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}
, any
\lambda>\lambda_{0}(M,\Omega)
and any eigensection
u_{\lambda,\rho}
satisfying (
1.7
),

\|u_{\lambda,\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;F^{\rho})}\geq c(M,\Omega).

Remark 1.3
.

A particular case is when
\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional. In this case, the spectrum of the Laplacian
\Delta^{\rho}:L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\to L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
is discrete and consists of only eigenvalues. Corollary
1.3
together with the unique continuation principle yield: for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and any
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}_{m}
, any eigensection
u_{\rho}
of
\Delta^{\rho}
satisfies

\|u_{\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;F^{\rho})}\geq c(M,\Omega,m)\|u_{\rho}\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

We now apply Theorem
1.2
to the flat Hilbert bundle
F^{\rho_{\pi}}
associated with the Riemannian covering map
\pi:X\to M
. By combining Theorem
1.2
with the non-commutative Bloch transform
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}
, we obtain a uniform semiclassical control estimate on any Riemannian cover
X
of
M
:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
M
be a compact hyperbolic surface and
\Omega\subset M
be a nonempty open subset. There exist constants
C=C(M,\Omega)>0
and
h_{0}=h_{0}(M,\Omega)>0
such that for any
0<h<h_{0}
and any
u\in H^{2}(X)
,

\|u\|_{L^{2}(X)}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}\left(\pi^{-1}(\Omega)\right)}+\frac{|\log h|}{h}\left\|\left(-h^{2}\Delta_{g}-I\right)u\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}\right).

Proofs of Corollary
1.3
, Theorem
1.4
and other applications of Theorem
1.2
in quantum chaos are given in Section
5.4
.

1.2.2.
Observability inequality

At this point, we have obtained the semiclassical control estimate for any Riemannian covering map
\pi:X\rightarrow M
. To obtain the observability inequality, the second (final) step is to remove the remaining low-frequency term through a compactness argument.

However, when
X
is non-compact, the compactness argument in the usual proof of observability fails because
\Delta_{g}
is not a Fredholm operator on
L^{2}(X)
. Additionally,
\Delta^{\rho}
is not a Fredholm operator on
L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
for any infinite-dimensional unitary representation
\rho
.

To address the difficulties arising from non-compactness, we apply the generalized Bloch transform

(1.8)

\mathcal{B}:L^{2}(X)\to\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}^{\oplus}L^{2}(M;\mathrm{End}(F^{\rho}))\,d\mu(\rho),

which decomposes functions on
X
into a family of sections of flat Hilbert bundles over
M
associated with irreducible representations. The generalized Bloch transform can be viewed as the composition of the fiber-wise Fourier transform and the non-commutative Bloch transform; see Definition
2.3
.

We impose the following assumption:

Assumption H
.

The Riemannian covering map
\pi:X\rightarrow M
is normal, and the deck transformation group
\Gamma
is a type I group.

We assume Assumption
H
for three reasons:

(a)

when
\pi
is normal, the set of cosets
\Gamma
becomes a group. This allows us to define the Fourier transform on
\Gamma
and thereby define the generalized Bloch transform;

(b)

when
\Gamma
is a type I group, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}:\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma},d\mu)
is an isometry, where
d\mu
is called the Plancherel measure on the dual group
\widehat{\Gamma}
(see (
2.26
)). Therefore, the generalized Bloch transform (
1.8
) is an isometry;

(c)

when
\Gamma
is a type I group, the supremum of the dimensions of the irreducible unitary representations of
\Gamma
is finite, denoted by
d_{\Gamma}
(see
[
32
, Theorem 4]
).

We refer to Section
2.4
for the definition and properties of type I groups. Note that every Abelian group is type I. We also give a non-commutative example of type I groups in Section
2.5
.

Assuming Assumption
H
, our strategy for proving the observability inequality is as follows. Applying the generalized Bloch transform and using the isometry property, we reduce the observability for the observable set
S
to the uniform observability for the observable set
\Omega
on a family of finite-dimensional flat Hilbert bundles. Then, applying Theorem
1.2
and the usual compactness argument to remove the low-frequency error term.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that Assumption
H
holds. Let
T>0
,
M
be a compact hyperbolic surface and
\Omega\subset M
be a nonempty open subset. For any
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}_{m}
, there exists a constant
K=K(M,\Omega,T,m)>0
such that for any
u\in L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
,

(1.9)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}^{2}\leq K\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;F^{\rho})}\,dt.

By taking
\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{d_{\Gamma}})
and
m=(d_{\Gamma})^{2}
in Theorem
1.5
and utilizing the unitary property of the generalized Bloch transform (Theorem
2.5
), we obtain the desired observability inequality with a control constant
C=C(M,\Omega,T,d_{\Gamma})>0
, which depends only on
M,\Omega,T
and the maximum dimension of the irreducible unitary representations of
\Gamma
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1.5
, we can find a constant
C=C(M,\Omega,T,d_{\Gamma})>0
such that for any
u\in L^{2}(X)
,

\|u\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta_{g}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\pi^{-1}(\Omega))}\,dt.

Remark 1.4
.

Some remarks are in order.

(a)

Consider the
\Gamma
-periodic Schrödinger operator
H=-\Delta+P_{1}+P_{0}
, where
H
is self-adjoint on
L^{2}(X)
and
P_{i}
are differential operators of order
i
on
X
. By Theorem
5.1
, Theorem
1.6
remains valid for
H
.

(b)

In the spirit of the work of Dyatlov, Jin, and Nonnenmacher
[
17
]
, we expect that all results above still hold if
M
is a negatively curved surface. The key point is that one can select uniform constants in the semiclassical calculus for scalar symbols on the flat Hilbert bundle; see Section
4
.

(c)

With the restriction of type I groups, some interesting covering transformation groups (
e.g.
, the Fuchsian subgroup) are not covered. There are two main difficulties in extending our results to non type I groups:

–

the mapping properties of the generalized Bloch transform for non type I groups are hard to study; we refer to
[
50
,
30
]
for some recent progress on Bloch analysis for non type I groups;

–

when the dual group
\widehat{\Gamma}
contains infinite-dimensional irreducible representations, it is unclear how to apply the usual compactness argument to remove the low-frequency error term from the semiclassical control estimate.

By Theorem
1.4
, we obtain a uniform semiclassical control estimate for all Riemannian covering spaces of a compact hyperbolic surface. However, the observability inequality in Theorem
1.6
holds only for the type I group covering. Therefore, it is reasonable to propose the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.7
.

Let
X
be a
\Gamma
-covering of a compact hyperbolic surface
M
,
S\subset X
be a nonempty,
\Gamma
-periodic, open subset. Then, for any
T>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(X,S,T)>0
such that the observability inequality (
1.2
) holds. Furthermore, denoted by
\Omega=\pi(S)
, then the constant
C
in (
1.2
) can be chosen such that it depends only on
M,\Omega
and
T
.

1.3.
Comments on history

In this section, we review observability results for Schrödinger type equations. We begin with the compact setting and then discuss recent developments on non-compact domains.

The observability problem on compact Riemannian manifolds has been extensively studied over the past decades; we refer to the surveys
[
35
,
43
]
for comprehensive overviews. As mentioned earlier, Lebeau
[
38
]
showed that the Geometric Control Condition (GCC) is sufficient to guarantee observability of the Schrödinger equation at any time
T>0
. The GCC was originally introduced for wave equations by Rauch and Taylor
[
54
]
, and by Bardos, Lebeau and Rauch
[
6
]
. However, GCC is in general not a necessary condition for observability of Schrödinger type equations.

A notable example is provided by the
d
-dimensional rational flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. For the Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, observability from any nonempty open subset of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, which does not necessarily satisfy GCC, was established by Haraux and Jaffard
[
24
,
27
]
for
d=2
, and by Komornik
[
34
]
for
d\geq 2
. These results were later extended in several directions by: adding a
C^{\infty}
potential by Burq and Zworski
[
11
]
, adding a broad class of potentials including continuous ones by Anantharaman and Macià
[
4
]
, adding a
L^{2}
potential by Bourgain, Burq and Zworski
[
8
]
for
d=2
, extending to rough observable sets by Burq and Zworski
[
12
]
for
d=2
. For those interested in the observability of magnetic Schrödinger equations, we refer to the recent work of Le Balc’h, Niu, and Sun
[
37
]
, which established an almost-sharp Magnetic Geometric Control Condition on the two-dimensional flat torus. One may also see
[
1
,
44
,
56
]
for other developments.

Another important geometric setting is that of compact hyperbolic surfaces. In this case, Jin
[
28
]
proved observability of the Schrödinger equation from any nonempty open subset at any time
T>0
, relying on the deep result of the semiclassical control estimate established by Dyatlov and Jin
[
18
]
. This was subsequently generalized to Anosov surfaces by Dyatlov, Jin, and Nonnenmacher
[
17
]
. For the disk, Anantharaman, Léautaud and Macià
[
2
,
3
]
characterized open subsets from which observability holds.

Recently, observability of Schrödinger equations on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
has attracted considerable interest. In the absence of spatial compactness, the standard techniques break down and new challenges appear. In dimension one, Huang, Wang and Wang
[
26
]
and Martin, Pravda-Starov
[
49
]
independently proved that the free Schrödinger equation is observable in some time
T>0
from a subset
S\subset\mathbb{R}
if and only if
S
is
thick
, meaning that

\exists\gamma,L>0,\ \text{ s.t. }\forall x\in\mathbb{R},\ |S\cap(x+[0,L])|\geq\gamma.

Here
|E|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
E\subset\mathbb{R}
. In
[
26
]
, the authors further considered Schrödinger equations with confining potential
V=|x|^{2m},m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, and showed that observability holds from
S
in some time when
m=1
(respectively, in any time when
m\geq 2
) if and only if
S
is
weakly thick
, that is,

\liminf_{x\to+\infty}\frac{|S\cap[-x,x]|}{|[-x,x]|}>0.

A generalization of this condition in higher dimensions, together with its necessity, was also given in
[
49
]
. These results rely heavily on harmonic analysis tools. In
[
52
]
, Prouff considered a large class of subquadratic confining potentials and derived a sufficient and almost necessary characterization of observable sets from which observability holds. His method relies on the establishment of a uniform Egorov’s theorem in semiclassical analysis. In
[
55
]
, Su, Sun and Yuan proved quantitative observability for one-dimensional Schrödinger equations with
V\in L^{\infty}
from thick sets. The key idea in
[
55
]
is to establish a spectral inequality at low frequency and a resolvent estimate at high frequency, and then merge them in a proper way.

There is another route to observability on non-compact domains that exploits symmetry. Given a Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
and a symmetry group
\Gamma
, one may study observability from
\Gamma
-periodic sets. The central idea is to use the symmetry to reduce the problem to a family of observability estimates on compact spaces. This philosophy originates from physics, where it appears in the study of Schrödinger equations with periodic potentials and is known as Floquet–Bloch theory
[
21
,
7
]
. Using Bloch theory on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Täufer
[
57
]
applied Ingham-type inequalities to establish observability for the free Schrödinger equation from any nonempty periodic open set. Although not stated explicitly, this result can also be deduced from the proof of Proposition 4 in
[
60
]
with the help of Bloch theory. To our best knowledge, Wunsch
[
60
]
was the first to apply Bloch theory to obtain polynomial energy decay for wave equations with periodic damping. In dimension two, Le Balc’h and Martin
[
36
]
extended these results to periodic measurable sets with positive measure, relying on semiclassical defect measures and adaptations of strategies developed in
[
11
,
8
,
12
]
. Similar strategy was used by Niu and Zhao
[
51
]
to prove observability in the semi-periodic setting. These works naturally raise the question of whether such techniques can be extended to other symmetric spaces than
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Kocábová and Šťovíček
[
32
]
generalized Bloch theory to type I groups. More recently, hyperbolic lattices have attracted growing interest in physics, motivated by advances in engineered photonic structures and circuit-based realizations; see
[
33
,
39
]
. In parallel, Maciejko and Rayan
[
45
,
46
]
developed a mathematical theory of hyperbolic bands. Based on Magee’s work
[
47
,
48

, Nagy and Rayan
[

introduced the hyperbolic Bloch transform and proved its injectivity and asymptotic unitary property. However, the problem regarding the

Non-abelian Bloch theory naturally leads to the study of twisted Bochner–Laplace operators on high-dimensional unitary flat bundles, including bundles with separable Hilbert fibers. Independently, geometric quantization has motivated the study of spectral theory on unitary flat bundles. Two independent groups, Ma, Ma

.

1.4.
Notations

We collect some notations and conventions used throughout this work:

•

We use
C(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n})
to denote a constant depending only on the parameters
\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}
, whose value may change from line to line.

•

Given a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, we denote by
\pi_{M}:\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
the Riemannian universal covering map, and by
\pi_{1}(M)
the fundamental group of
M
.

•

Unless otherwise specified, we write
A=\mathcal{O}(f(h))_{X}
for some
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, where
X
is a normed space, if there is a constant
C
independent of
(\mathcal{H},\rho,h)
such that
\|A\|_{X}\leq Cf(h)
.

•

We use the
Einstein summation convention
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we develop a generalized Bloch theory. In Section
3
, we review the geodesic flow and the anisotropic symbol classes on compact hyperbolic surfaces. In Section
4
, we introduce the quantization of scalar symbols and Fourier integral operators on flat Hilbert bundles. Section
5
is devoted to proving the uniform semiclassical control estimate on flat Hilbert bundles. Finally, we prove the observability inequality on non-compact hyperbolic surfaces with type I symmetry groups in Section
6
.

2.
A generalized Bloch theory

In this section, we develop a generalized Bloch theory. While much of the material can be found in the existing literature (see
[
50
,
32
]
), we present an almost self-contained treatment for the reader’s convenience, including all necessary details.

In this section, we always assume that
\mathcal{H}
is a separable Hilbert space with a countable orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
(M,g)
is a compact connected Riemannian manifold.

2.1.
Laplacian on flat Hilbert bundles

A
flat Hilbert bundle
is a Hilbert bundle with a flat metric-compatible connection. According to the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence (see
[
31
, Section 1.2-1.4]
for the finite-dimensional case), any unitary representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
corresponds to a flat Hilbert bundle
F^{\rho}
over
M
, which is constructed as follows. Let

(2.1)

F^{\rho}:=\widetilde{M}\times_{\rho}\mathcal{H}

denote the quotient of
\widetilde{M}\times\mathcal{H}
by the action of
\pi_{1}(M)
given by

(2.2)

\gamma:(\widetilde{x},v)\in\widetilde{M}\times\mathcal{H}\mapsto(\gamma\widetilde{x}\,,\,\rho(\gamma)v)\in\widetilde{M}\times\mathcal{H},\qquad\gamma\in\pi_{1}(M).

Here we identify
\pi_{1}(M)
as the deck transformation group of
\pi_{M}:\widetilde{M}\to M
.

We now check that
F^{\rho}
defined in (
2.1
) admits a flat Hilbert bundle structure over
M
. The bundle projection is defined as

(2.3)

\pi_{\rho}:F^{\rho}\rightarrow M,\qquad[\widetilde{x},v]\mapsto\pi_{M}(\widetilde{x}).

For any
x\in M
, choose a neighborhood
U\subset M
of
x
and a local lift
\iota_{U}:M\rightarrow\widetilde{M}
such that
\pi_{M}\circ\iota_{U}=\mathrm{Id}_{U}
. The local trivialization is defined as

(2.4)

\Phi_{U}:\pi_{\rho}^{-1}(U)\rightarrow U\times\mathcal{H},\qquad[\iota_{U}(y),v]\mapsto(y,v).

It is easy to see that the transition function between two local trivializations is constant (in fact,
\rho(\gamma)
for some
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(M)
). The fiberwise inner product
h_{x}^{\rho}
at
x\in M
is defined as:

(2.5)

h_{x}^{\rho}\big([\iota_{U}(x),v],[\iota_{U}(x),w]\big):=\langle v,w\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

Note that the inner product (
2.5
) is well-defined because the representation
\rho
is unitary. Therefore,
F^{\rho}
defined in (
2.1
) is a Hilbert bundle. We now describe the corresponding flat connection and twisted Laplacian from both global and local perspectives.

For the theory of differential and integration of Hilbert-space-valued functions on manifolds, we refer to
[
25
,
29
]
. Let
C^{\infty}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
be the space of smooth
\mathcal{H}
-valued functions. The space of smooth sections of
F^{\rho}
, denoted by
C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho})
, is identified with the space of
\rho
-equivariant
\mathcal{H}
-valued functions:

C^{\infty}_{\rho}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H}):=\{f\in C^{\infty}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H}):f(\gamma x)=\rho(\gamma)f(x),\ \forall\gamma\in\pi_{1}(M)\}.

In the same manner, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
\mathcal{H}
-valued
k
-form space
\Omega^{k}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
, the
\rho
-equivariant
\mathcal{H}
-valued
k
-form space
\Omega^{k}_{\rho}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
, and the
F^{\rho}
-valued
k
-form space
\Omega^{k}(M;F^{\rho})
. When
\rho=\rho_{\mathrm{triv}}
is the trivial representation, the corresponding function and
k
-form spaces
C^{\infty}_{\rho}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
and
\Omega^{k}_{\rho}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
are denoted by
C^{\infty}_{\Gamma}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
and
\Omega^{k}_{\Gamma}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
, respectively.

For any

u=u^{i}\otimes e_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\widetilde{M})\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{H}\cong C^{\infty}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H}),

where
\widehat{\otimes}
denotes the completed tensor product, we define the operator
d\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}
by:

(2.6)

(d\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}})u:=(du^{i})\otimes e_{i}\in\Omega^{1}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H}).

Since
d
is invariant under the
\pi_{1}(M)
-action, (
2.6
) induces a map from
C^{\infty}_{\rho}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
to
\Omega^{1}_{\rho}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
. Consequently, we obtain the flat connection
\nabla^{\rho}:C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho})\to\Omega^{1}(M;F^{\rho})
. The twisted Bochner–Laplace operator (short for twisted Laplacian)
\Delta^{\rho}
is then defined as:

(2.7)

\Delta^{\rho}:=\mathrm{tr}(\nabla^{\rho}\circ\nabla^{\rho}):C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho})\to C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho}),

which coincides with
\Delta_{g}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}
on
C_{\rho}^{\infty}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
.

The above provides a global description of the flat connection
\nabla^{\rho}
and the twisted Laplacian
\Delta^{\rho}
. In the following, we characterize the flat connection and the twisted Laplacian locally.

Let
\{e_{i}(x)\equiv e_{i}:M\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\}_{i\in I}
be the orthonormal frame field of
M\times\mathcal{H}
. For any local trivialization
(\Phi_{U},U)
of
F^{\rho}
,
\{\Phi_{U}^{-1}(e_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is an orthonormal frame field of
\pi_{\rho}^{-1}(U)
. The flat connection
\nabla^{\rho}
is defined so that
\Phi_{U}^{-1}(e_{i})
is parallel for every
i\in I
,
i.e.
,

(2.8)

\nabla^{\rho}\Phi_{U}^{-1}(e_{i})=0,\qquad\forall i\in I.

Then,
\nabla^{\rho}
is compatible with the inner product
h^{\rho}
: for any vector fields
u=\Phi_{U}^{-1}(e_{i})u^{i},\,v=\Phi_{U}^{-1}(e_{i})v^{i}
and
w=\Phi_{U}^{-1}(e_{i})w^{i}
on
U
, we have

\displaystyle u(h^{\rho}(v,w))

\displaystyle=u^{j}\partial_{j}(\delta_{ik}v^{i}w^{k})

\displaystyle=\delta_{ik}(\partial_{j}v^{i}u^{j})w^{k}+\delta_{ik}v^{i}(\partial_{j}w^{k}u^{j})

\displaystyle=h^{\rho}(\Phi_{U}^{-1}(e_{i})\partial_{j}v^{i}u^{j},\Phi_{U}^{-1}(e_{k})w^{k})+h^{\rho}(\Phi_{U}^{-1}(e_{i})v^{i},\Phi_{U}^{-1}(e_{k})\partial_{j}w^{k}u^{j})

\displaystyle=h^{\rho}(\nabla^{\rho}_{u}v,w)+h^{\rho}(v,\nabla^{\rho}_{u}w).

Therefore,
F^{\rho}
is a Hilbert bundle over
M
with a flat connection
\nabla^{\rho}
such that
\nabla^{\rho}
is
h^{\rho}
-compatible.

From the construction above, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\pi:F\rightarrow M
be a flat Hilbert bundle with fiber isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}
. Then, there exists a finite collection
\{(\Phi_{\alpha},U_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
such that:

•

\{(U_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is an atlas of
M
.

•

For each
\alpha\in\Lambda
,
\Phi_{\alpha}:\pi^{-1}(U_{\alpha})\rightarrow U_{\alpha}\times\mathcal{H}
is a local trivialization of
F
, and
\{\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}(e_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is a local parallel orthonormal frame of
F
.

We refer to the triple
(\Phi_{\alpha},U_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})
as a parallel orthonormal local trivialization chart of
F
.

Using the local parallel orthonormal frame of
F^{\rho}
, the twisted Laplacian
\Delta^{\rho}
can be diagonalized as follows: for any smooth section
u\in C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho})
, we can locally express
u
as
u=u^{j}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}(e_{j})
, then

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\rho}u

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\,(\nabla^{\rho}\circ\nabla^{\rho})u=\sum_{i}\nabla^{\rho}\circ\nabla^{\rho}(v_{\alpha,i},v_{\alpha,i})u

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\left(\nabla^{\rho}_{v_{\alpha,i}}\circ\nabla^{\rho}_{v_{\alpha,i}}-\nabla^{\rho}_{\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}_{v_{\alpha,i}}v_{\alpha,i}}\right)\big(u^{j}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}(e_{j})\big)

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{g}u^{j})\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}(e_{j}),

where
\{v_{\alpha,i}\}_{i\in I}
is a local orthonormal frame of
TM
and
\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}
denotes the Levi-Civita connection on
TM
.

We review the deformation of representations. To simplify, we only consider the finite-dimensional unitary representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathrm{U}(n)
. Since
\pi_{1}(M)
is generated by
2g
generators

a_{1},\cdots,a_{g},b_{1},\cdots,b_{g}

under the relation

[a_{1},b_{1}]\cdots[a_{g},b_{g}]=1,

the representation variety
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(M),\mathrm{U}(n))
can be identified as a compact algebraic variety of
\mathrm{U}(n)^{2g}
. Thus, the topology of the moduli space of representations
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(M),\mathrm{U}(n))/\mathrm{U}(n)
is naturally inherited from
\mathrm{U}(n)
. If
[\rho_{i}]\to[\rho]
in
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(M),\mathrm{U}(n))/\mathrm{U}(n)
as
i\rightarrow\infty
, then we can choose representative elements
\rho_{i}
and
\rho
of
[\rho_{i}]
and
[\rho]
, respectively, such that

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{i}(a_{j})=\rho(a_{j})\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{i}(b_{j})=\rho(b_{j})\qquad\mathrm{in}\ \mathrm{U}(n)

for any
j=1,\cdots,g
. For more on the moduli space of unitary representations of the surface group, we refer to Atiyah and Bott
[
5
]
and Goldman
[
23
]
. In the remainder of this subsection, we assume that the representation
\rho
is finite-dimensional.

Fix a reference point
x_{0}\in\widetilde{M}
. There exists a smooth map
\mathfrak{U}_{\rho}:\widetilde{M}\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
, defined by parallel transport on the flat bundle
(F^{\rho},\nabla^{\rho})
from
x_{0}
, such that

(2.10)

\mathfrak{U}_{\rho}(\gamma x)=\rho(\gamma)\mathfrak{U}_{\rho}(x),\qquad\forall\gamma\in\pi_{1}(M),\,x\in\widetilde{M}.

More precisely,
\mathfrak{U}_{\rho}
is the local solution to the following initial value problem:

\displaystyle d\mathfrak{U}_{\rho}+\pi_{M}^{*}(A_{\rho})\mathfrak{U}_{\rho}=0,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}_{\rho}(x_{0})=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Here,
A_{\rho}
denotes the connection 1-form for the flat connection
\nabla^{\rho}
. In the case where
M
is a compact hyperbolic surface, the construction of
\mathfrak{U}_{\rho}
is referred to
[
50
]
.

We define the Laplacian
\Delta_{\rho}
on the
\Gamma
-equivalent space
L^{2}_{\Gamma}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
:

(2.11)

\Delta_{\rho}f(x):=\mathfrak{U}_{\rho}^{-1}(x)(\Delta_{g}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}})(\mathfrak{U}_{\rho}f)(x),\qquad\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{\Gamma}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H}),\,x\in\widetilde{M}.

Since
\mathfrak{U}_{\rho}
gives an isometry between
L^{2}_{\Gamma}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
and
L^{2}_{\rho}(\widetilde{M};\mathcal{H})
, the twisted Laplacian
\Delta^{\rho}
is unitarily equivalent to the Laplacian
\Delta_{\rho}
defined as (
2.11
), which is considered as an operator on
L^{2}(M;\mathcal{H})
. Moreover, the following continuity holds:

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume that
\rho_{i}\to\rho
in
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(M),\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}))
. For any
\varepsilon>0
,
(z-\Delta_{\rho_{i}})^{-1}
converges to
(z-\Delta_{\rho})^{-1}
uniformly in
|\mathrm{Im}\,z|\geq\varepsilon
:

\lim_{\rho_{i}\rightarrow\rho}\sup_{|\mathrm{Im}\,z|\geq\varepsilon}\left\|(z-\Delta_{\rho_{i}})^{-1}-(z-\Delta_{\rho})^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}(M;\mathcal{H})\rightarrow L^{2}(M;\mathcal{H})}=0.

Proof.

The proof is straightforward by using local coordinates and patching argument. We refer to
[
36
, Proposition 3.2]
for the Euclidean space case.
∎

2.2.
Non-commutative Bloch transform

In this section, we assume that
\pi:X\to M
is an arbitrary Riemannian covering map. By the universal property of the universal cover, we have
\pi_{M}=\pi\circ\pi_{X}
. We write

M=\Gamma_{M}\backslash\widetilde{M}\quad\text{and}\quad X=\Gamma_{X}\backslash\widetilde{M},

where
\Gamma_{X}\leqslant\Gamma_{M}\leqslant\mathrm{Iso}(\widetilde{M})
are discrete subgroups of the isometry group of
\widetilde{M}
. We fix base points
x_{0}\in M
,
x_{0}^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}(x_{0})
, and
\widetilde{x}_{0}\in\pi_{X}^{-1}(x_{0}^{\prime})
. Then,
\Gamma_{M}\cong\pi_{1}(M,x_{0})
and
\Gamma_{X}\cong\pi_{1}(X,x_{0}^{\prime})
. Let

(2.12)

V:=\Gamma_{X}\backslash\Gamma_{M}=\left\{\left[\gamma\right]:=\Gamma_{X}\cdot\gamma\ :\ \gamma\in\Gamma_{M}\right\}

be the set of right cosets of
\Gamma_{X}
in
\Gamma_{M}
. Since
\Gamma_{M}
is a countable set,
V
is also countable. Moreover,
\pi
is normal if and only if
V
forms the deck transformation group of
\pi
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(V)
denote the Hilbert space of square-summable functions on
V
. Let
\rho_{\pi}:\pi_{1}(M)\cong\Gamma_{M}\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
be the quasiregular representation on
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(V)
, defined by

\rho_{\pi}(\eta)(\delta_{[\gamma]}):=\delta_{[\gamma\eta^{-1}]},\qquad\forall\,\eta\in\Gamma_{M},\,[\gamma]\in V,

where
\delta_{[\gamma]}\in\ell^{2}(V)
is the Kronecker delta function supported at
[\gamma]\in V
. Let
F^{\rho_{\pi}}
denote the flat Hilbert bundle over
M
associated with the representation
\rho_{\pi}
, constructed as in Section
2.1
.

We now introduced the non-commutative Bloch transform. For
\psi\in L^{2}(X)
, we can view it as a
\Gamma_{X}
-invariant function on
\widetilde{M}
, that is,
\psi(\gamma x)=\psi(\gamma^{\prime}x)
whenever
[\gamma]=[\gamma^{\prime}]\in V
.

Definition 2.1
.

For any
\psi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, the non-commutative Bloch transform
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi
is defined as a function on
\widetilde{M}\times\mathcal{H}
:

(2.13)

(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(x):=\sum_{[\gamma]\in V}\psi(\gamma x)\delta_{[\gamma]},\qquad\forall\,\psi\in L^{2}(X),\,x\in\widetilde{M}.

For any
\eta\in\Gamma_{M}
and
x\in\widetilde{M}
, we have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(\eta x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\gamma]\in V}\psi(\gamma\eta x)\rho_{\pi}(\eta)(\delta_{[\gamma\eta]})=\rho_{\pi}(\eta)\left(\sum_{[\gamma]\in V}\psi(\gamma\eta x)\delta_{[\gamma\eta]})\right)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\pi}(\eta)\big((\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(x)\big).

Therefore,
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi
is
\Gamma_{M}
-equivariant and can be identified as a
F^{\rho_{\pi}}
-valued section over
M
. With this identification, we have

(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(x)=\sum_{[\gamma]\in V}\psi(\gamma x)\Phi^{-1}_{U}(\delta_{[\gamma]})

locally. By (
2.9
), we get

(2.14)

(\Delta^{\rho_{\pi}}\circ\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(x)=\sum_{[\gamma]\in V}\Delta_{g}\psi(\gamma x)\Phi^{-1}_{U}(\delta_{[\gamma]})=(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\circ\Delta_{g}\psi)(x).

The following proposition lists some properties of the non-commutative Bloch transform:

Proposition 2.3
.

For any
a\in C(M)
,
\pi^{*}a=a\circ\pi\in C(X)
. Let
M_{a}
and
M_{\pi^{*}a}
be the multiplication operators on
L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
and
L^{2}(X)
, respectively. We have

(\Delta^{\rho_{\pi}}\circ\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(x)=(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\circ\Delta_{g}\psi)(x),

(M_{a}\circ\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(x)=(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\circ M_{\pi^{*}a}\psi)(x),

and
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}:L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(M;F^{\rho_{\pi}})
is an isometry,
i.e.
,

(2.15)

\|\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho_{\pi}})}^{2}=\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}.

2.3.
Generalized Bloch transform

Although the non-commutative Bloch transform maps functions on the covering space
X
to sections of the vector bundle
F^{\rho_{\pi}}
over the base manifold
M
, the fiber of
F^{\rho_{\pi}}
(isomorphic to
\ell^{2}(V)
) may be infinite-dimensional, posing challenges for further analysis.
To address this issue, we compose the fiber-wise Fourier transform with the non-commutative Bloch transform to obtain sections of finite-dimensional Hermitian bundles over the representation space. This results in the so-called generalized Bloch transform, which decomposes the original function into a direct integral of sections of finite-dimensional Hermitian bundles.

To define the generalized Bloch transform, we assume that the covering map
\pi:X\rightarrow M
is normal. It follows that the set
V
defined in (
2.12
) is isomorphic to the deck transformation group of
\pi
, which is denoted by
\Gamma
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, let
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{irr}}(\Gamma,\mathrm{U}(n))
be the space of all
n
-dimensional irreducible unitary representations of
\Gamma
. Let

(2.16)

\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\Gamma}:=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{irr}}(\Gamma,\mathrm{U}(n))/\mathrm{U}(n)

be the space of all
n
-dimensional irreducible unitary representations of
\Gamma
up to unitary equivalence, and

\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}:=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\Gamma}.

Let
(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}})
be the Hilbert space consisting of all endomorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
equipped with the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}
, that is,

(2.17)

\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}:=\mathrm{tr}\,(AB^{*}).

For all
(\rho,A)\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{irr}}(\Gamma,\mathrm{U}(n))\times\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
U\in\mathrm{U}(n)
, we define

(2.18)

U\cdot(\rho,A):=(U\rho U^{*},UAU^{*}),

which gives a free, linear action of projective unitary group
\mathrm{PU}(n)
on

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{irr}}(\Gamma,\mathrm{U}(n))\times\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Thus,

(2.19)

\mathscr{O}^{n}:=\big(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{irr}}(\Gamma,\mathrm{U}(n))\times\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\big)\big/\mathrm{PU}(n)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\Gamma}

defines a Hermitian bundle over
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\Gamma}
. Let
\Gamma(\mathscr{O}^{n})
be the space of sections of
\mathscr{O}^{n}
. Let
\mathscr{O}
be the Hilbert-sheaf of
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
induced by the vector bundles
\mathscr{O}^{n}
, and
\Gamma(\mathscr{O})=\bigoplus\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\Gamma(\mathscr{O}^{n})
. Finally, let

C_{0}(\Gamma):=\bigl\{f:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}\,:\,|\Gamma\setminus f^{-1}(0)|<\infty\bigr\}

be the space of compactly supported, complex-valued functions on
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.2
.

The Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}:C_{0}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathscr{O})
is defined by

(2.20)

(\mathcal{F}f)([\rho]):=\left[\rho,\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma^{-1})\rho(\gamma)\right],\qquad\forall f\in C_{0}(\Gamma).

Similarly to the construction of
\mathscr{O}^{n}
, we define a Hilbert bundle
\mathcal{V}^{n}
over
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\Gamma}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. Since the covering map
\pi:X\rightarrow M
is normal,
N=\pi_{*}(\pi_{1}(X))
is a normal subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M)
, and
\Gamma
is isomorphic to
\pi_{1}(M)/N
. This gives an embedding map
\iota:\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{irr}}(\Gamma,\mathrm{U}(n))\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{irr}}(\pi_{1}(M),\mathrm{U}(n))
, which is defined as

(2.21)

\iota(\rho)(\gamma):=\rho(\gamma N),\quad\forall\gamma\in\pi_{1}(M).

For any
[\rho]\in\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
, let
F^{\rho}
denote the flat Hermitian bundle
\widetilde{M}\times_{\rho}\mathbb{C}^{n}
constructed in Section
2.1
(here, we use the embedding (
2.21
) to view
\rho
as a representation of
\pi_{1}(M)
). Any
[\rho]\in\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
induces a natural group homomorphism
\widetilde{\rho}:\Gamma\to\mathrm{U}(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
by

\widetilde{\rho}(\gamma):A\mapsto\rho(\gamma)\circ A,\qquad\forall A\in\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Therefore, we can define the flat Hermitian bundle
\mathrm{End}(F^{\rho}):=\widetilde{M}\times_{\widetilde{\rho}}\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
as in Section
2.1
.
For any

(\rho,\psi)\in\bigsqcup_{\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{irr}}(\Gamma,\mathrm{U}(n))}L^{2}(M;\mathrm{End}(F^{\rho})),

and
U\in\mathrm{U}(n)
, we define

(2.22)

U\cdot(\rho,\psi):=(U\rho U^{*},U\psi U^{*}),

which gives a free, linear action of
\mathrm{PU}(n)
on

\bigsqcup\limits_{\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{irr}}(\Gamma,\mathrm{U}(n))}L^{2}(M;\mathrm{End}(F^{\rho}))
. Thus,

(2.23)

\mathcal{V}^{n}:=\left(\bigsqcup_{\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{irr}}(\Gamma,\mathrm{U}(n))}L^{2}(M;\mathrm{End}(F^{\rho}))\right)\bigg/\mathrm{PU}(n)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\Gamma}

defines a Hilbert bundle over
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\Gamma}
. Let
\Gamma(\mathcal{V}^{n})
be the space of sections of
\mathcal{V}^{n}
. Finally, let
\mathcal{V}
be the Hilbert-sheaf of
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
induced by the vector bundles
\mathcal{V}^{n}
, and
\Gamma(\mathcal{V})=\bigoplus\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\Gamma(\mathcal{V}^{n})
.

Definition 2.3
.

For any
x\in X
and
[\rho]\in\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
, the generalized Bloch transform
\mathcal{B}:C_{0}^{\infty}(X)\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{V})
associated with
(x,[\rho])

is defined via

(2.24)

(\mathcal{B}\psi)([\rho])(x):=\left[\rho,\mathcal{F}\Big((\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(x)\Big)\right]=\left[\rho,\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\psi(\gamma^{-1}x)\rho(\gamma)\right],

where the second equality follows from (
2.2
) by taking
f(\gamma)=\psi(\gamma x)
.

Example 2.4
.

We review the classical Bloch transform on Euclidean space. Let
X=\widetilde{M}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and
M=\mathbb{T}^{d}
be the unit torus. The group action of
\Gamma
acting on
X
is given by
n\cdot x=x+n
. Since
\Gamma
is commutative, any irreducible unitary representation of
\Gamma
is one dimensional and can be identified by a parameter
\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
:

\rho_{\theta}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad n\mapsto e^{2\pi in\cdot\theta}.

The non-commutative Bloch transform is

(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\psi(n\cdot x)\delta_{n}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\psi(x+n)\delta_{n}.

The Fourier transform is

(\mathcal{F}f)(\theta)=(\mathcal{F}f)(\rho_{\theta})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(n^{-1})\rho_{\theta}(n)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(n)e^{-2\pi in\cdot\theta},

where
n^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
n
, viewed as a group element of
\Gamma
. Then, the classical Bloch transform is recovered:

(2.25)

(\mathcal{B}\psi)(x,\theta)=\mathcal{F}\big((\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(x)\big)(\rho_{\theta})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\psi(x+n)e^{-2\pi in\cdot\theta}.

It is clear that, for any
\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
m\in\Gamma
,

(\mathcal{B}\psi)(x+m,\theta)=e^{2\pi im\cdot\theta}(\mathcal{B}\psi)(x,\theta)=\rho_{\theta}(m)(\mathcal{B}\psi)(x,\theta).

Therefore,
(\mathcal{B}\psi)(\cdot,\theta)
is a section of the flat Hermitian bundle
\mathrm{End}(F^{\rho_{\theta}})
over
\mathbb{T}^{d}
for each
\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

2.4.
Mapping properties of the generalized Bloch transform for type I groups

In this section, we further assume that
\Gamma
is a type I group. Under this assumption, the mapping properties of the generalized Bloch transform are well understood. Before presenting the main results, we introduce some additional concepts.

Let
G
be a topological group and
\pi
be a unitary representation of
G
. We say that
\pi
is a
factor representation
of
G
if the Von Neumann algebra generated by the family
\pi(G)
has a trivial center
\mathbb{C}I
. Moreover, if
\pi
is a direct sum of irreducible representations,
\pi
is a factor representation if and only if all its irreducible subrepresentations are unitarily equivalent.

There are many equivalent definitions of type I groups. We adopt the following one
[
22
, Page 229]
:

Definition 2.5
.

Let
G
be a topological group.
G
is a type I group if every factor representation of
G
is a direct sum of many copies of the same irreducible unitary representation.

The celebrated Thoma’s theorem characterizes type I countable discrete groups.

Theorem 2.4

(Thoma’s theorem,
[
58
,
59
]
)
.

A countable discrete group
\Gamma
is type I if and only if
\Gamma
is a virtually Abelian group,
i.e.
, there is an Abelian normal subgroup of
\Gamma
with finite index. In this case, the supremum of the dimensions of the irreducible unitary representations of
\Gamma
is finite, denoted by
d_{\Gamma}
.

As a consequence,
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}=\widehat{\Gamma}
is the dual space of
\Gamma
. There exists a measure
\mu
on
\widehat{\Gamma}
such that the Plancherel formula holds
[
22
]
:

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma)\overline{g(\gamma)}=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\big\langle\mathcal{F}f([\rho]),\mathcal{F}g([\rho])\big\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}\,d\mu([\rho]),\qquad\forall f,g\in C_{0}(\Gamma).

Using this formula, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
in Definition
2.2
extends to an isometry
\mathcal{F}:\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\rightarrow L^{2}(\widehat{\Gamma},\mu)
,
i.e.
,

(2.26)

\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\|\mathcal{F}f([\rho])\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\,d\mu([\rho]),\qquad\forall f\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma).

Finally, we denote

L^{2}(\mathcal{V})=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}^{\oplus}L^{2}(M;\mathrm{End}(F^{\rho}))d\mu([\rho]).

Theorem 2.5
.

The generalized Bloch transform in Definition
2.3
can be extended to the map

\mathcal{B}:L^{2}(X)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathcal{V})

with the unitary property:

(2.27)

\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\int_{M}\big\|(\mathcal{B}\psi)([\rho])(x)\big\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\,dxd\mu([\rho]),\qquad\forall\psi\in L^{2}(X).

Proof.

Let
\psi\in L^{2}(X)
. We have

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho_{\pi}})}^{2}=\int_{M}\|(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(x)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\Gamma)}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\left\|\mathcal{F}\Big((\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(x)\Big)([\rho])\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\,d\mu([\rho])dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\int_{M}\left\|\mathcal{F}\Big((\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}\psi)(x)\Big)([\rho])\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\,dxd\mu([\rho])

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\int_{M}\big\|(\mathcal{B}\psi)([\rho])(x)\big\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\,dxd\mu([\rho]),

where we used the unitary property (
2.15
) of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{NC}}
for the first equality, the unitary property (
2.26
) of
\mathcal{F}
for the second line, Fubini theorem for the third line, and the definition of
\mathcal{B}
for the last line. The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Dynamics of geodesic flow and anisotropic symbol classes

In this section, we review the geodesic flow and the anisotropic symbol classes on compact hyperbolic surfaces; see
[
19
,
18
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented compact hyperbolic surface and
T^{*}M\setminus\{0\}
consist of elements of the cotangent bundle
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M
such that
\xi\neq 0
. Denote by
S^{*}M=\left\{|\xi|_{g}=1\right\}
the cosphere bundle, where
|\xi|_{g}:=\langle\xi,\xi\rangle_{g}^{1/2}
. Define the symbol
p\in C^{\infty}\left(T^{*}M\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{R}\right)
by
p(x,\xi)=|\xi|_{g}
. The Hamiltonian flow of
p
is the homogeneous geodesic flow given by

\varphi_{t}:=\exp\left(tH_{p}\right):T^{*}M\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow T^{*}M\setminus\{0\}.

We use an explicit frame on
T^{*}M\setminus\{0\}
consisting of four vector fields

(3.1)

H_{p},\,U_{+},\,U_{-},\,D\in C^{\infty}\big(T^{*}M\setminus\{0\};T(T^{*}M\setminus\{0\})\big).

Here
H_{p}
is the generator of
\varphi_{t}
and
D=\xi\cdot\partial_{\xi}
is the generator of dilation. The vector fields
U_{\pm}
are defined on
S^{*}M
as stable (
U_{+}
) and unstable (
U_{-}
) horocyclic vector fields and extended homogeneously to
T^{*}M\setminus\{0\}
. They satisfy the commutation relations

(3.2)

\left[H_{p},U_{\pm}\right]=\pm U_{\pm},\quad\left[U_{\pm},D\right]=\left[H_{p},D\right]=0.

Thus on each level set of
p
, the flow
\varphi_{t}
has a flow/stable/unstable decomposition, with
U_{+}
spanning the stable space and
U_{-}
spanning the unstable space. We use the following notation for the weak stable/unstable spaces:

(3.3)

L_{s}:=\operatorname{span}\left(H_{p},U_{+}\right),\ L_{u}:=\operatorname{span}\left(H_{p},U_{-}\right)\subset T(T^{*}M\backslash\{0\}).

Then,
L_{s}
and
L_{u}
are Lagrangian foliations; see
[
19
, Definition 3.1]
. Here we give a brief introduction of Lagrangian foliations.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a manifold,
U\subset T^{*}M
be an open set, and

L_{(x,\xi)}\subset T_{(x,\xi)}(T^{*}M),\qquad(x,\xi)\in U,

a family of subspaces depending smoothly on
(x,\xi)
. We say that
L
is a
Lagrangian foliation
on
U
if

•

L_{(x,\xi)}
is integrable in the sense that if
X,Y
are two vector fields on
U
lying in
L
at each point (we denote this by
X,Y\in C^{\infty}(U;L)
), then the Lie bracket
[X,Y]
lies in
C^{\infty}(U;L)
as well;

•

L_{(x,\xi)}
is a Lagrangian subspace of
T_{(x,\xi)}\left(T^{*}M\right)
for each
(x,\xi)\in U
.

A basic example of Lagrangian foliation is the vertical foliation on
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let

T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}=\{(y_{1},\cdots,y_{d},\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{d})\},

and define the vertical foliation
L_{0}
by

L_{0}=\operatorname{span}\big(\partial_{\eta_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{\eta_{d}}\big).

By
[
19
, Lemma 3.6]
, every Lagrangian foliation is locally symplectomorphic to
L_{0}
:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
U\subset T^{*}M
be open and
L
be a Lagrangian foliation on
U
. For any
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in U
, there exists an open neighborhood
U_{0}
of
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
and a symplectomorphism
\kappa:U_{0}\rightarrow V_{0}
, such that
U_{0}\subset U
,
V_{0}\subset T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

(\kappa_{*}L)_{(x,\xi)}=d\kappa_{(x,\xi)}(L_{(x,\xi)})=\left(L_{0}\right)_{\kappa(x,\xi)},\qquad\forall(x,\xi)\in U_{0}.

The triple
(\kappa,U_{0},V_{0})
is called a Lagrangian chart.

In Dyatlov, Zahl
[
19
]
and Dyatlov, Jin
[
18
]
, the authors introduced the anisotropic symbol classes associated with Lagrangian foliations. We review the definition below, following
[
18
, (2.16) & Appendix A.1]
:

Definition 3.2
.

For two parameters
0\leq\mu<1
,
0\leq\mu^{\prime}\leq\frac{\mu}{2}
,
\mu+\mu^{\prime}<1
, an
h
-dependent symbol
a=a(x,\xi;h)
lies in the class
S_{L,\mu,\mu^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{comp}}(U)
if

•

a(x,\xi;h)
is smooth in
(x,\xi)\in U
, defined for
0<h\leq 1
, and supported in an
h
-independent compact subset of
U
;

•

a
satisfies the derivative bounds

\sup_{x,\xi}\left|Y_{1}\cdots Y_{m}Z_{1}\cdots Z_{k}a(x,\xi;h)\right|\leq Ch^{-\mu k-\mu^{\prime}m},\quad 0<h\leq 1

for all vector fields
Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m},Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{k}
on
U
such that
Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m}
are tangent to
L
. Here the constant
C
depends on
Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m},Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{k}
but does not depend on
h
.

The class
S_{L,\mu}^{\text{comp}}(U)
is defined as

(3.4)

S_{L,\mu}^{\text{comp}}(U)=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}S_{L,\mu+\varepsilon,\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{comp}}(U).

The following lemma (see
[
18
, Lemma A.1]
) is useful later:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
C
be an arbitrary fixed constant and assume that
a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}\in S_{L,\mu,\mu^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{comp}}(U)
,
1\leq N\leq C|\log h|
are such that
\sup\left|a_{j}\right|\leq 1
and each
S_{L,\mu,\mu^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{comp}}(U)
seminorm of

is bounded uniformly in

. Then for all small
\varepsilon>0
the product
a_{1}\cdots a_{N}
lies in
S_{L,\mu+\varepsilon,\mu^{\prime}+\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{comp}}(U)
.

Now we return to the case where

is a compact hyperbolic surface. If

is an

-independent symbol, then it follows from the commutation relations (

) that

(3.5)

H_{p}^{k}U_{+}^{\ell}U_{-}^{m}D^{n}(a\circ\varphi_{t})=e^{(m-\ell)t}(H_{p}^{k}U_{+}^{\ell}U_{-}^{m}D^{n}a)\circ\varphi_{t}.

Therefore, for
0\leq\mu<1
, we have

(3.6)

a\circ\varphi_{t}\in S_{L_{s},\mu}^{\mathrm{comp}}(T^{*}M\setminus\{0\})\text{ uniformly in }t,\qquad 0\leq t\leq\mu|\log h|.

Similarly,

(3.7)

a\circ\varphi_{-t}\in S_{L_{u},\mu}^{\mathrm{comp}}(T^{*}M\setminus\{0\})\text{ uniformly in }t,\qquad 0\leq t\leq\mu|\log h|.

.

4.

For any

Our key observation is Proposition

For any open subset

If

(4.3)

All the operators in Section

4.1.
Semiclassical Sobolev spaces on flat Hilbert bundles

We start by generalizing the notion of
cutoff chart
, which is widely used in the quantization of symbols on manifolds (
c.f.

[
20
, Definition E.9]
), to the setting of flat Hilbert bundles.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
(\Phi,U,\varphi,\chi)
is a
cutoff normal chart
of a flat Hilbert bundle

if
(\Phi,U,\varphi)
is a parallel orthonormal local trivialization chart in the sense of Proposition
2.1

\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(U)

We can view the local trivialization
\Phi:\pi^{-1}(U)\subset F\rightarrow U\times\mathcal{H}

\Phi:C^{\infty}(U;F)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(U;\mathcal{H})

(\Phi f)(x)=\Phi(f(x)),\qquad\forall x\in U.

We define the operators

(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\Phi\chi:C^{\infty}(U;F)\rightarrow C_{0}^{\infty}(\varphi(U);\mathcal{H}),\qquad(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\Phi\chi f(x)=\chi(\varphi^{-1}(x))\Phi\big(f(\varphi^{-1}(x))\big),

and

\chi\Phi^{-1}\varphi^{*}:C^{\infty}(\varphi(U);\mathcal{H})\rightarrow C_{0}^{\infty}(U;F),\qquad\chi\Phi^{-1}\varphi^{*}g(x)=\chi(x)\Phi^{-1}\big(g(\varphi(x))\big).

These two operators can be naturally extended to

(4.4)

(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\Phi\chi:C^{\infty}(M;F)\to C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathcal{H}),\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\chi\Phi^{-1}\varphi^{*}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathcal{H})\to C^{\infty}(M;F),

respectively.

Let
U_{\alpha}
and
U_{\beta}
be two overlapping open subsets of

, with two cutoff normal charts
(\Phi_{\alpha},U_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha})
and
(\Phi_{\beta},U_{\beta},\varphi_{\beta},\chi_{\beta})
. Since
F
is flat, the transition map
\Phi_{\alpha\beta}(x)=(\Phi_{\alpha}\circ\Phi_{\beta}^{-1})(x,\cdot)\in\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
is a constant unitary operator on
\mathcal{H}
:

\Phi_{\alpha\beta}(x)=\Phi_{\alpha\beta}\in\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}),\qquad\forall x\in U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}.

The composition of the operators in (
4.4
) can be written as:

(4.5)

\big[(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}\big]\circ\big[\chi_{\beta}\Phi_{\beta}^{-1}\varphi_{\beta}^{*}\big]=\varphi_{\beta\alpha}^{*}\chi_{\alpha\beta}\otimes\Phi_{\alpha\beta}=\chi_{\beta\alpha}\varphi_{\beta\alpha}^{*}\otimes\Phi_{\alpha\beta}.

where
\varphi_{\beta\alpha}=\varphi_{\beta}\circ\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1}
denotes the transition function, and
\chi_{\alpha\beta}:=(\chi_{\alpha}\chi_{\beta})\circ\varphi_{\beta}^{-1}
denotes the multiplication operator:

(\chi_{\alpha\beta}f)(x)=\chi_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\beta}^{-1}(x))\chi_{\beta}(\varphi_{\beta}^{-1}(x))f(x),\qquad\forall f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We now introduce semiclassical Sobolev spaces on the flat Hilbert bundle
F\rightarrow M
. First, for any Radon measure
d\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<h\leq 1
, and
u=u^{i}e_{i}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathcal{H})
, we define the semiclassical Sobolev norm of
u
as:

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{H},d\mu)}:=\sum_{i\in I}\|u^{i}\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},d\mu)}=\sum_{i\in I}\|\mathrm{Op}_{h}(\langle\xi\rangle^{s})u_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},d\mu)},

where
\langle\xi\rangle=\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}
denotes the Japanese bracket and
\mathrm{Op}_{h}
denotes the standard semiclassical quantization on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(4.6)

(\mathrm{Op}_{h}(a))u(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi h)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi;h)u(y)e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi/h}\,dyd\xi.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
d\mu
be a Radon measure on
M
and
\mathcal{U}=\left\{\left(\Phi_{\alpha},U_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha}\right)\right\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
be a finite cover of cutoff normal charts of
F
such that
\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\chi_{\alpha}^{2}=1
. For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<h\leq 1
, and
u\in C^{\infty}(M;F)
, we define the semiclassical Sobolev norm of
u
as:

(4.7)

\|u\|_{H_{h}^{s}(M,F,\mathcal{U},d\mu)}^{2}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\left\|\big((\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}\big)u\right\|_{H_{h}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{H},(\varphi_{\alpha})_{*}d\mu)}^{2},

where
(\varphi_{\alpha})_{*}d\mu
denotes the pushforward of the measure
d\mu
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Remark 4.3
.

The semiclassical Sobolev norm coincides with the
L^{2}
norm when
s=0
:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H_{h}^{s=0}(M,F,\mathcal{U},d\mu)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\chi_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1}(x))|^{2}\big\|\Phi_{\alpha}\big(u(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1}(x))\big)\big\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\,(\varphi_{\alpha})_{*}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\int_{U_{\alpha}}|\chi_{\alpha}(y)|^{2}\big\|\Phi_{\alpha}\big(u(y)\big)\big\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\|u(x)\|^{2}_{F_{x}}\,d\mu(x),

where we used
\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\chi_{\alpha}^{2}=1
in the last equality.

Let
F=F^{\rho}
be the flat Hilbert bundle associated with the representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
(see Section
2.1
). At first glance, the semiclassical Sobolev norm defined above appears to depend on the choices of the cover
\mathcal{U}
and the measure
d\mu
. We will show, however, that the semiclassical Sobolev norms corresponding to different choices of
(\mathcal{U},d\mu)
are equivalent. More importantly, this equivalence holds uniformly with respect to the semiclassical parameter
h
, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and the representation
\rho
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<h\leq 1
, and any two pairs of
(\mathcal{U},d\mu)
and
(\mathcal{U}^{\prime},d\mu^{\prime})
satisfying the assumptions of Definition
4.2
. There is a constant
C>0
, depending only on
d,s,\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U}^{\prime},d\mu,d\mu^{\prime}
and in particular independent of
(h,\mathcal{H},\rho)
, such that for every
u\in C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho})
one has

\frac{1}{C}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho},\mathcal{U}^{\prime},d\mu^{\prime})}\leq\|u\|_{H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho},\mathcal{U},d\mu)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho},\mathcal{U}^{\prime},d\mu^{\prime})}.

Proof.

Denote
\mathcal{U}=\{(\Phi_{\alpha},U_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
and
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}=\{(\Phi_{\beta}^{\prime},U_{\beta}^{\prime},\varphi_{\beta}^{\prime},\chi_{\beta}^{\prime})\}_{\beta\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
. Let
u\in C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho})
. Since
\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\chi_{\alpha}^{2}=1
and

\big[\chi_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}\big]\circ\big[(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}\big]u=\chi_{\alpha}^{2}u,\qquad\forall\alpha\in\Lambda,

we get

(4.8)

\displaystyle((\varphi_{\beta}^{\prime})^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\beta}^{\prime}\chi_{\beta}^{\prime}u

\displaystyle=((\varphi_{\beta}^{\prime})^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\beta}^{\prime}\chi_{\beta}^{\prime}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\chi_{\alpha}^{2}u

\displaystyle=((\varphi_{\beta}^{\prime})^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\beta}^{\prime}\chi_{\beta}^{\prime}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\big[\chi_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}\big]\circ\big[(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}\big]u

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}G_{\beta\alpha}\circ\big[(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}\big]u,

where
G_{\beta\alpha}
is the composition operator appear in (
4.5
):

G_{\beta\alpha}:=\big[((\varphi_{\beta}^{\prime})^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\beta}^{\prime}\chi_{\beta}^{\prime}\big]\circ\big[\chi_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}\big]:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathcal{H})\rightarrow C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathcal{H}).

Denote
\varphi_{\alpha\beta}=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ(\varphi_{\beta}^{\prime})^{-1}
,
\chi_{\beta\alpha}=(\chi_{\beta}^{\prime}\chi_{\alpha})\circ(\varphi_{\beta}^{\prime})^{-1}
and
\Phi_{\beta\alpha}=\Phi_{\beta}^{\prime}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{\alpha}\in\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
. By (
4.5
), we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|G_{\beta\alpha}\|_{H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{H},(\varphi_{\alpha})_{*}d\mu)\to H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{H},(\varphi^{\prime}_{\beta})_{*}d\mu^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi_{\alpha\beta}^{*}\chi_{\beta\alpha}\otimes\Phi_{\beta\alpha}\|_{H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{H},(\varphi_{\alpha})_{*}d\mu)\to H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{H},(\varphi^{\prime}_{\beta})_{*}d\mu^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi_{\alpha\beta}^{*}\chi_{\beta\alpha}\|_{H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},(\varphi_{\alpha})_{*}d\mu)\to H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},(\varphi^{\prime}_{\beta})_{*}d\mu^{\prime})},

which is a constant depending only on
d,s,\alpha,\beta,d\mu,d\mu^{\prime}
(the independence with respect to
h
is nontrivial, see
[
20
, Page 561]
). It follows from (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho},\mathcal{U}^{\prime},d\mu^{\prime})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|((\varphi_{\beta}^{\prime})^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\beta}^{\prime}\chi_{\beta}^{\prime}u\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{H},(\varphi^{\prime}_{\beta})_{*}d\mu^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\|G_{\beta\alpha}((\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}u)\|_{H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{H},(\varphi^{\prime}_{\beta})_{*}d\mu^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\|(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}u\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{H},(\varphi_{\alpha})_{*}d\mu)}

\displaystyle=C\|u\|_{H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho},\mathcal{U},d\mu)}^{2},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
d,s,\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U}^{\prime},d\mu,d\mu^{\prime}
. The same argument yields the other direction. The proof is complete.
∎

In view of Proposition
4.1
, we can safely omit
\mathcal{U}
and
d\mu
from the notation and simply write
\|\cdot\|_{H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})}
for the semiclassical Sobolev norm. The semiclassical Sobolev space
H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
is defined as the completion of
C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho})
with respect to this norm. For
h=1
, we suppress the subscript
h
.

Proposition 4.2
.

We have the following properties for the semiclassical Sobolev spaces:

(a)

Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
. For different values of
h\in(0,1]
, the spaces
H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
are the same sets with different norms. These norms are mutually equivalent, but the equivalence is not uniform with respect to
h
.

(b)

For any
s>t
and
0<h\leq 1
, one has the continuous embedding
H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\subset H^{t}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
. If
\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional, the natural inclusion
\iota:H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow H^{t}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
is compact. In contrast, when
\mathcal{H}
is infinite-dimensional,
\iota
is not compact.

(c)

For any cutoff normal chart
(\Phi,U,\varphi,\chi)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
(\mathcal{H},\rho,h)
such that the operators in (
4.4
) are continuous:

(4.10)

\|(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\Phi\chi\|_{H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\to H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{H})}+\|\chi\Phi^{-1}\varphi^{*}\|_{H^{s}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{H})\to H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C.

Proof.

The proof in the setting of flat Hilbert bundles follows the same lines as in the manifold case and is entirely routine, so we omit the details. For the corresponding argument for Sobolev spaces on manifolds, we refer to
[
20
, Appendix E]
.

We give an example to show that the natural inclusion
\iota:H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow H^{t}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
is not compact when
s>t
and
\mathcal{H}
is infinite-dimensional. Let
(\Phi,U,\varphi,\chi)
be a cutoff normal chart of
F^{\rho}
,
g\in H^{s}_{h}(M)
with
\mathrm{supp}\,g\subset U
and
u_{i}=g\otimes\Phi^{-1}(e_{i})
for every
i\in I
. Then

\|u_{i}\|_{H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})}=\|g\|_{H^{s}_{h}(M)},\qquad\forall i\in I,

and

\|u_{i}-u_{j}\|^{2}_{H^{t}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})}=2\|g\|_{H^{t}_{h}(M)},\qquad\forall i,j\in I.

Therefore,
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is bounded in
H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
but has no convergent subsequence in
H^{t}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
. The inclusion
\iota:H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow H^{t}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
is not compact when
\mathcal{H}
is infinite-dimensional.
∎

4.2.
Quantization of scalar symbols on flat Hilbert bundles

In the general theory of pseudodifferential operators on vector bundles (
c.f.
the semiclassical analysis on flat bundles and on principal bundles with finite-dimensional fibers in
[
42
,
14
]
), symbols are smooth sections of
\pi_{0}^{*}\mathrm{End}(F)
, where
\pi_{0}^{*}\mathrm{End}(F)
denotes the pullback of the endomorphism bundle
\mathrm{End}(F)
over
T^{*}M
via the natural projection
\pi_{0}:T^{*}M\to M
.

In this work, however, we restrict our attention to scalar symbols of the form
a(x,\xi;h)\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}
, which are canonically identified as
a\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
. We start by showing how pseudodifferential operators transform under a change of coordinates in Euclidean space.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set,
\varphi:U\rightarrow\varphi(U)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a diffeomorphism, and
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(U)
. For any
k\in\mathbb{R}
and any
a\in S^{k}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists
b\in S^{k}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\chi\varphi^{*}\mathrm{Op}_{h}(a)(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\chi=\mathrm{Op}_{h}(b).

Here
S^{k}\left(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
is defined as in (
4.1
), with
M
replaced by
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, the symbol
b
admits an asymptotic expansion
b\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}h^{j}L_{j}(a\circ\widetilde{\varphi})
, in the sense that,

b-\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}h^{j}L_{j}(a\circ\widetilde{\varphi})\in h^{N}S^{k-N}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}),\qquad\forall N\in\mathbb{N}.

Here
\widetilde{\varphi}=(\varphi,(d\varphi)^{-t}):T^{*}U\to T^{*}\varphi(U)
denotes the natural lift of
\varphi
to the cotangent bundle,
L_{0}=\chi^{2}
, and, for
j\geq 1
,
L_{j}
is an
h
-independent differential operator of order
2j
on
T^{*}U
with coefficients compactly supported in
x
.

Proof.

See
[
20
, Proposition E.10]
.
∎

To give a fine description of the pseudodifferential operators with scalar symbols on flat Hilbert bundles, we introduce the so-called
locally fiberwise diagonal operators
. For brevity of notation, we write
A=A(\theta_{1};\cdots;\theta_{n})
to indicate that
A
denotes a family of operators indexed by the parameters
\theta_{1};\cdots;\theta_{n}
.

Definition 4.4
.

We say that a
(\mathcal{H},\rho,h)
-family of operators

A=A(\mathcal{H};\rho;h):C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho})\to C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho}),\qquad\forall(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C},\,0<h\leq 1,

is
locally fiberwise diagonal
if, for any
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}
,
0<h\leq 1
, and any two cutoff normal charts
(\Phi_{i},U_{i},\varphi_{i},\chi_{i})
,
i=1,2
, of
F^{\rho}
, with the transition map
\Phi_{12}:=\Phi_{1}\circ\Phi_{2}^{-1}
, there exists an operator
A_{\varphi_{12},\chi_{12}}(h):C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, independent of
(\mathcal{H},\rho)
, such that

(4.11)

(\varphi^{-1}_{1})^{*}\Phi_{1}\chi_{1}A(\mathcal{H};\rho;h)\chi_{2}\Phi^{-1}_{2}\varphi^{*}_{2}=A_{\varphi_{12},\chi_{12}}(h)\otimes\Phi_{12}.

The residual class of semiclassical pseudodifferential operators
A(h)\in h^{\infty}\Psi^{-\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as in
[
20
, Definition E.11]
. We now introduce the corresponding notion of
locally fiberwise diagonal residual class
in the setting of flat Hilbert bundles.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
A=A(\mathcal{H};\rho;h)
be a family of locally fiberwise diagonal operators. We say that
A\in h^{\infty}\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
or
A=\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})_{\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})}
, if

(4.12)

\|A(\mathcal{H};\rho;h)\|_{H^{-N}(M;F^{\rho})\to H^{N}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq Ch^{N},\qquad\forall(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C},\,0<h\leq 1,N\in\mathbb{N}.

where
C=C(N)>0
is a constant depending only on
N
.

Remark 4.6
.

It is easy to show that
A\in h^{\infty}\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
if and only if
A_{\varphi_{12},\chi_{12}}=\mathrm{Op}_{h}(a_{\varphi_{12},\chi_{12}})
for some
a_{\varphi_{12},\chi_{12}}\in h^{\infty}S^{-\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d})
in (
4.11
).

We are ready to define pseudodifferential operators with scalar symbols on flat Hilbert bundles. These operators are given by finite sums of local pseudodifferential operators defined in cutoff normal charts, modulo the locally fiberwise diagonal residual class.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{R}
. We say that a
(\mathcal{H},\rho,h)
-family of operators

A=A(\mathcal{H};\rho;h):C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho})\to C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho}),\qquad\forall(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C},\,0<h\leq 1,

belongs to the class
\Psi_{h}^{k,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
, if

(4.13)

A=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\chi_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}(\mathrm{Op}_{h}(a_{\alpha})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}})(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}+\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})_{\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Here
\{(\Phi_{\alpha},U_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is a finite family of cutoff normal charts of
F^{\rho}
and
a_{\alpha}\in S^{k}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We denote by

\Psi^{\infty,sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho}):=\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\Psi^{k,sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})

the set of semiclassical pseudodifferential operators with scalar symbols on flat Hilbert bundle
F^{\rho}
.

Analogous to
[
20
, Proposition E.13]
, we have the following characterization for
\Psi^{k,sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
:

Proposition 4.4
.

A family of operators
A=A(\mathcal{H};\rho;h):C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M;F^{\rho})
belongs to the class
\Psi_{h}^{k,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

•

For any cutoff normal chart
(\Phi,U,\varphi,\chi)
, there exists
a_{\varphi,\chi}\in S^{k}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d})
, independent of
(\mathcal{H},\rho)
, such that

(4.14)

(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\Phi\chi A(\mathcal{H};\rho;h)\chi\Phi^{-1}\varphi^{*}=\mathrm{Op}_{h}(a_{\varphi,\chi})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}.

•

For each
\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that
\operatorname{supp}\psi\cap\operatorname{supp}\psi^{\prime}=\varnothing
, we have

(4.15)

\psi A\psi^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})_{\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
Assume that
A\in\Psi_{h}^{k,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
. For any cutoff normal chart
(\Phi,U,\varphi,\chi)
, by (
4.13
), we have

\displaystyle(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\Phi\chi A(\mathcal{H};\rho;h)\chi\Phi^{-1}\varphi^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\Phi\chi\chi_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}(\mathrm{Op}_{h}(a_{\alpha})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}})(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}\chi\Phi^{-1}\varphi^{*}

\displaystyle+(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\Phi\chi R\chi\Phi^{-1}\varphi^{*},

where
\{(\Phi_{\alpha},U_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is a finite family of cutoff normal charts,
a_{\alpha}\in S^{k}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
R\in h^{\infty}\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
. It follows that
(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\Phi\chi R\chi\Phi^{-1}\varphi^{*}=\mathrm{Op}_{h}(r)\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}
for some
r\in h^{\infty}S^{-\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see Remark
4.6
). By (
4.5
), we have

(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\Phi\chi\chi_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}=\chi_{0\alpha}\varphi^{*}_{0\alpha}\otimes\Phi_{0\alpha},\quad(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}\chi\Phi^{-1}\varphi^{*}=(\varphi_{0\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\chi_{0\alpha}\otimes\Phi_{0\alpha}^{-1},

where
\Phi_{0\alpha}=\Phi\circ\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}
,
\varphi_{0\alpha}=\varphi\circ\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1}
, and
\chi_{0\alpha}=(\chi\chi_{\alpha})\circ\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1}
. Therefore,

(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\Phi\chi A(\mathcal{H};\rho;h)\chi\Phi^{-1}\varphi^{*}=\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\chi_{0\alpha}\varphi^{*}_{0\alpha}\mathrm{Op}_{h}(a_{\alpha})(\varphi_{0\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\chi_{0\alpha}+\mathrm{Op}_{h}(r)\right)\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}.

We get (
4.14
) from Proposition
4.3
.

For each
\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that
\operatorname{supp}\psi\cap\operatorname{supp}\psi^{\prime}=\varnothing
. Let
\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(U_{\alpha})
such that
\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}=1
near
\mathrm{supp}\,\chi_{\alpha}
. Then,

(4.16)

\psi\chi_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}=\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}\psi_{\alpha}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}\psi^{\prime}=\psi^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha},

where
\psi_{\alpha}=(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}(\psi\chi_{\alpha})
and
\psi^{\prime}_{\alpha}=(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}(\chi_{\alpha}\psi^{\prime})
have disjoint supports. Substituting (
4.16
) into (
4.13
), we obtain

(4.17)

\psi A(\mathcal{H};\rho;h)\psi^{\prime}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}\big((\psi_{\alpha}\mathrm{Op}_{h}(a_{\alpha})\psi^{\prime}_{\alpha})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}\big)(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}+\psi R\psi^{\prime},

where
R\in h^{\infty}\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
. Since
\psi_{\alpha}\mathrm{Op}_{h}(a_{\alpha})\psi^{\prime}_{\alpha}\in h^{\infty}\Psi^{-\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see
[
20
, Proposition E.13]
), using (
4.10
), we conclude that
\psi A\psi^{\prime}
satisfies (
4.12
). To prove (
4.15
), it remains to show that
\psi A\psi^{\prime}
is locally fiberwise diagonal. For any two cutoff normal charts
(\Phi_{i},U_{i},\varphi_{i},\chi_{i})
,
i=1,2
, of
F^{\rho}
, by (
4.17
), we have

(4.18)

\displaystyle(\varphi^{-1}_{1})^{*}\Phi_{1}\chi_{1}\psi A(\mathcal{H};\rho;h)\psi^{\prime}\chi_{2}\Phi^{-1}_{2}\varphi^{*}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\Big((\varphi^{-1}_{1})^{*}\Phi_{1}\chi_{1}\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}\Big)\Big((\psi_{\alpha}\mathrm{Op}_{h}(a_{\alpha})\psi^{\prime}_{\alpha})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}\Big)\Big((\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}\chi_{2}\Phi^{-1}_{2}\varphi^{*}_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+(\varphi^{-1}_{1})^{*}\Phi_{1}\chi_{1}\psi R\psi^{\prime}\chi_{2}\Phi^{-1}_{2}\varphi^{*}_{2}.

Let
\widetilde{\chi}_{i}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(U_{i})
such that
\widetilde{\chi}_{i}=1
near
\mathrm{supp}\,\chi_{i}
,
i=1,2
. In the spirit of (
4.5
) and (
4.16
), we have

(4.19)

\displaystyle(\varphi^{-1}_{1})^{*}\Phi_{1}\chi_{1}\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}\varphi_{\alpha}^{*}=\chi_{1\alpha}\varphi_{1\alpha}^{*}\otimes\Phi_{1\alpha},

\displaystyle(\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{\alpha}\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}\chi_{2}\Phi^{-1}_{2}\varphi^{*}_{2}=(\varphi_{2\alpha}^{-1})^{*}\chi_{2\alpha}\otimes\Phi_{\alpha 2},

Definition 4.13
.

\mathrm{WF}_{h}(A-B)\cap U=\varnothing

From the microlocal partition of unity

Proposition 4.8
.

Assume that

Definition 4.15

For any

and

The elliptic set is important because it characterizes the microlocal invertibility of

near elliptic points; we refer to
[
20
, Proposition E.32]
. The proof there is readily adapted to our setting:

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
k,\ell\in\mathbb{R}
,
A\in\Psi_{h}^{k,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
, and

(4.28)

For any

i.e.

\operatorname{ell}_{h}(P)=\overline{T}^{*}M

(4.29)

|\sigma_{h}^{\rho}(P)(x,\xi)|>c\langle\xi\rangle^{k},\qquad\forall(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M,

for some

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{R}
and
P\in\Psi_{h}^{k,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
. By Proposition
4.5
, for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

P(\mathcal{H};\rho;h):H^{s+k}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})

is bounded uniformly in
(\mathcal{H},\rho,h)
. If
P
is elliptic, then there exists
h_{0}\in(0,1]
, independent of
(\mathcal{H},\rho)
, such that for any
h\in(0,h_{0})
,

P(\mathcal{H};\rho;h)
is invertible and the inverse

P^{-1}\in\Psi_{h}^{-k,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
.

Proof.

Taking
A=I=\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(1)
in Proposition
4.9
, there exists
Q,\,Q^{\prime}\in\Psi^{-k,sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
and
R,\,R^{\prime}\in h^{\infty}\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})

I=PQ+R=Q^{\prime}P+R^{\prime}.

Since
R\in h\Psi^{0}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
, by Proposition
4.5
, there exists
M_{s}>0
, independent of
(\mathcal{H},\rho,h)
, such that

(4.30)

\|R(\mathcal{H};\rho;h)\|_{H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq M_{s}h.

Taking
h_{0}=(2M_{s})^{-1}
. Then, for any
h\in(0,h_{0})
,
P(\mathcal{H};\rho;h):H^{s+k}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
is invertible, and the inverse
P^{-1}=Q(I-R)^{-1}:H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow H^{s+k}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
is bounded uniformly in
(\mathcal{H},\rho,h)
.

We now show that
P^{-1}\in\Psi_{h}^{-k,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
. We first check that
P^{-1}-Q
is locally fiberwise diagonal. Since
R\in h^{\infty}\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
and
Q\in\Psi^{-k,sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
, by Proposition
4.6
.(c), we have
QR^{n}\in h^{\infty}\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
for any

. In particular,
QR^{n}
is locally fiberwise diagonal. For any two cutoff normal charts
(\Phi_{i},U_{i},\varphi_{i},\chi_{i})
,
i=1,2
, there exists an operator
A_{\varphi_{12},\chi_{12},n}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, independent of

, such that

(\varphi_{1}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{1}\chi_{1}QR^{n}\chi_{2}\Phi_{2}^{-1}\varphi_{2}^{*}=A_{\varphi_{12},\chi_{12},n}\otimes\Phi_{12},\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

By (
4.10

h\in(0,h_{0})
and

\|A_{\varphi_{12},\chi_{12},n}\|_{H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\to H^{s+k}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C\|QR^{n}\|_{H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\to H^{s+k}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C/2^{n}.

Thus, we have

\widetilde{A}_{\varphi_{12},\chi_{12}}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{\varphi_{12},\chi_{12},n}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho}),H^{s+k}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})),

and

(4.31)

(\varphi_{1}^{-1})^{*}\Phi_{1}\chi_{1}(P^{-1}-Q)\chi_{2}\Phi_{2}^{-1}\varphi_{2}^{*}=\widetilde{A}_{\varphi_{12},\chi_{12}}\otimes\Phi_{12}.

Therefore,
P^{-1}-Q
is locally fiberwise diagonal.

We now check (
4.15
) for
P^{-1}
. Let
\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that
\operatorname{supp}\psi\cap\operatorname{supp}\psi^{\prime}=\varnothing

\psi Q\psi^{\prime}\in h^{\infty}\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
and

is locally fiberwise diagonal,

\psi P^{-1}\psi^{\prime}=\psi Q\psi^{\prime}+\psi(P^{-1}-Q)\psi^{\prime}

is also locally fiberwise diagonal. On the other hand, since
R\in h^{\infty}\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})
and

is bounded uniformly in

, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on

\displaystyle\|\psi P^{-1}\psi^{\prime}\|_{H^{-N}(M;F^{\rho})\to H^{N}(M;F^{\rho})}

\displaystyle=\|\psi Q\psi^{\prime}-\psi P^{-1}R\psi^{\prime}\|_{H^{-N}(M;F^{\rho})\to H^{N}(M;F^{\rho})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{N}

for any

. Therefore,
\psi P^{-1}\psi^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})_{\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})}

4.15

P^{-1}
.

We now check (
4.14
) for
P^{-1}
. For any cutoff normal chart
(\Phi,U,\varphi,\chi)
, by (
4.31

A_{\varphi,\chi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{s}_{h}(M;F^{\rho}),H^{s+k}_{h}(M;F^{\rho}))
, independent of
(\mathcal{H},\rho)
, such that

(4.32)

and all

,
1\leq k\leq N+N^{\prime}
. Here
\mathrm{ad}_{A}B:=[A,B]
,

.

Let

near
\mathrm{supp}\,(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\chi
. For any linear function

We take

with
\chi^{\prime}\equiv 1
near

\displaystyle(\operatorname{ad}_{\ell(x,hD_{x})}A_{\varphi,\chi})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ad}_{\ell(x,hD_{x})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}}\big((\varphi^{-1})^{*}\Phi\chi P^{-1}\chi\Phi^{-1}\varphi^{*}\big)

where we used (
4.32
) for the first equality. By iterating (

)

\displaystyle\big(\operatorname{ad}_{x_{i_{1}}}\cdots\operatorname{ad}_{x_{i_{N}}}\operatorname{ad}_{hD_{x_{i_{N+1}}}}\cdots\operatorname{ad}_{hD_{x_{i_{N+N^{\prime}}}}}A_{\varphi,\chi}\big)\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}

&L_{hD_{x_{i_{k}}},\chi_{k}}&&k=N+1,\cdots,N+N^{\prime},\end{aligned}\right.

\chi_{k}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\varphi(U))
be such that
\chi_{k}\equiv 1
near
\mathrm{supp}\,\chi_{k+1}
,
\chi_{N+N^{\prime}}\equiv 1
near
\mathrm{supp}\,(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\chi
, and
\chi^{\prime}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(U)

near

By (

) and (

), we have

\mathrm{ad}_{L_{k}}P\in\left\{\begin{aligned} &h\Psi^{k-1,sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho}),&&k=1,\cdots,N,\\
&h\Psi^{k,sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho}),&&k=N+1,\cdots,N+N^{\prime}.\end{aligned}\right.

For any operators

\mathrm{ad}_{L}P^{-1}=P^{-1}(\mathrm{ad}_{L}P)P^{-1},\quad\mathrm{ad}_{L}(AB)=(\mathrm{ad}_{L}A)B+A(\mathrm{ad}_{L}B).

, (

Then, (

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We begin with an outline of the argument. Recall that the semiclassical Laplacian
-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}
is diagonalized under parallel orthonormal local trivializations; see (
2.9
). We take a finite cover of cutoff normal charts
\mathcal{U}=\{\Phi_{\alpha},U_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
with
\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\chi_{\alpha}\equiv 1
on

. Let
\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}=\chi_{\alpha}\circ\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1}
and
\psi_{\alpha}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(U_{\alpha})
such that
\psi_{\alpha}\equiv 1
near
\mathrm{supp}\,\chi_{\alpha}
. Then, by (

), it is easy to check that

It follows that

(4.40)

-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}\in\Psi^{2,sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{h}^{\rho}(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})(x,\xi)=|\xi|^{2}_{x}.

Recall the Helffer-Sjöstrand formula (see
[
16
, Theorem 8.1]
): for any
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and any self-adjoint operator
P
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we have

(4.41)

f(P)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f}(z)(P-z)^{-1}\,L(dz),

where
L(dz)
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and
\widetilde{f}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
is an almost analytic continuation of

such that

•

\widetilde{f}\big|_{\mathbb{R}}=f
;

•

\mathrm{supp}\,\widetilde{f}\subset\{z\in\mathbb{C}\ |\ \mathrm{Re}\,z\in\mathrm{supp}\,f,\ |\mathrm{Im}\,z|\leq 1\}
;

•

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C=C(N)>0
such that
|\overline{\partial}_{z}\widetilde{f}(z)|\leq C|\operatorname{Im}z|^{N}
.

The integral in (

) is convergent in the sense of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
-valued Riemann integration.

In the Helffer–Sjöstrand formula (
4.41
), we may take
P=-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}
. For
\mathrm{Im}\,z\neq 0
,
h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}+z\in\Psi^{2}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
is elliptic, and hence

(h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}+z)^{-1}\in\Psi^{-2}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})

by Proposition
4.10
. However, in order
to apply the Helffer–Sjöstrand formula and conclude that
f(P)
is a pseudodifferential operator,
it is necessary to obtain uniform control on the growth of
(P-z)^{-1}
and its commutator
\mathrm{ad}_{L}(P-z)^{-1}
as
\mathrm{Im}\,z\rightarrow 0
. More precisely, we require that these operators grow at most polynomially in
|\mathrm{Im}\,z|^{-1}
. Then, repeating the argument of Proposition
4.10
yields that
f(P)
is a pseudodifferential operator.

In what follows, we establish a generalization of Theorem
1.1
based on the idea mentioned above. Our proof follows
[
61
, Theorem 14.9]
.

Theorem 4.11
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let

be a self-adjoint operator on

P+i
is elliptic in
\Psi^{k,sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})

For simplicity of notations, below we write
H^{s}_{h}
for

Step 1. We show that

4.43

4.43

\displaystyle\|(P-z)^{-1}u\|_{H^{n+k+1}_{h}}

4.4.

\displaystyle{\left[P,\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho,L}(b)\right]}

\displaystyle=-ih\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}\,\mathrm{Op}_{h}\big(\{p\circ\kappa^{-1},b\circ\kappa^{-1}\}\big)\mathcal{B}+\mathcal{O}\big(h^{2-\mu-\mu^{\prime}}\big)_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Since

the second line of (

This completes the proof of (
4.70

Now, we put

,

. By (

(4.74)

\displaystyle ih\partial_{t}\left(e^{-itP/h}\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho,L}\left(a_{t}\right)e^{itP/h}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-itP/h}\left(ih\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho,L}\left(\partial_{t}a_{t}\right)+\left[P,\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho,L}\left(a_{t}\right)\right]\right)e^{itP/h}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\big(h^{2-\mu-\mu^{\prime}}\big)_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})},\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

Integrating this from

to

∎

We now restrict ourselves to the case when

is a compact hyperbolic surface,

,

with

as in (

). The following Egorov’s theorem is a long-time version of Proposition

for times up to

.

Theorem 4.20
.

Assume that

is

-independent. Then we have, uniformly in

\displaystyle A(t)=\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho,L_{s}}(a\circ\varphi_{t})+\mathcal{O}(h^{1-\mu}|\log h|)_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})},

\displaystyle A(-t)=\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho,L_{u}}(a\circ\varphi_{-t})+\mathcal{O}(h^{1-\mu}|\log h|)_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Proof.

We prove first line of (

5.
Uniform semiclassical control on flat Hilbert bundles

In this section, we prove the uniform semiclassical control estimate:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
M
be a compact hyperbolic surface and
a\in C_{0}^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
such that
a|_{S^{*}M}\not\equiv 0
. There exist constants
C=C(M,a)>0
and
h_{0}=h_{0}(M,a)>0
such that for any
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}
, any
0<h<h_{0}
, any self-adjoint
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}(h)\in\Psi^{2,sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
with
\sigma_{h}^{\rho}(\mathcal{P})=|\xi|_{g}^{2}
and any
u\in H^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
,

(5.1)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C\left(\|\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(a)u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}+\frac{|\log h|}{h}\left\|(\mathcal{P}-I\right)u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\right).

Our proof follows the argument of Dyatlov and Jin
[
18
, Theorem 2]
. We emphasize that their proof involves only the
L^{2}
-norm estimate in semiclassical microlocal analysis. In section
4
, we establish the semiclassical calculus for scalar symbols with operator-norm bounds uniform over all flat
\mathcal{H}
-bundles. Here we only list the main steps and highlight that the constants are independent of
(\mathcal{H},\rho)
. The strategy of the proof is as follows.

We decompose the phase space
T^{*}M
into several distinct regions:

•

Outside of
S^{*}M
, elliptic estimates imply that
u
can be controlled by
(\mathcal{P}-1)u
.

•

On
S^{*}M
, we further divide into two parts: the region controlled by
a
, and the region not controlled by
a
:

–

the part controlled by
a
is precisely the union of all geodesic flow orbits passing through
\{a\neq 0\}
. In this region, by propagation estimates and Egorov’s theorem,
u
can be controlled by
\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(a)u
and
(\mathcal{P}-1)u
;

–

the uncontrolled part is given by

K:=S^{*}M\setminus\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t}(\{a\neq 0\}).

On
K
, which is “fractal” in both stable and unstable directions, we apply the fractal uncertainty principle, see
[
18
]
, to obtain control.

5.1.
Partitions and key estimates

We take
U^{\prime}=T^{*}M\setminus\{0\}
and
U=\left\{1/4<|\xi|_{g}<4\right\}
in this section. Fix conical open sets

\mathcal{U}_{1},\,\mathcal{U}_{2}\subset T^{*}M\setminus\{0\},\quad\mathcal{U}_{1},\,\mathcal{U}_{2}\neq\varnothing,\quad\overline{\mathcal{U}_{1}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{U}_{2}}=\varnothing,\quad\overline{\mathcal{U}_{2}}\cap S^{*}M\subset\{a\not=0\}.

We start with a rough microlocal partition of unity
I=A_{0}+A_{1}+A_{2}
constructed as follows.

•

Let
\psi_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
satisfy

(5.2)

\operatorname{supp}\psi_{0}\cap[1/4,4]=\varnothing,\quad\operatorname{supp}\left(1-\psi_{0}\right)\subset(1/16,16).

Then,

(5.3)

A_{0}:=\psi_{0}(\mathcal{P})\in\Psi^{0,sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})

is microlocalized in the region away from
S^{*}M
. Since
1-\psi_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, by Theorem
4.11
,
I-A_{0}\in\Psi^{\mathrm{comp},sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho})
and there exists
b\in C_{0}^{\infty}(U)
such that

(5.4)

I-A_{0}=\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(b)+\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})_{\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})},

where
b\equiv 1-\psi_{0}(|\xi|^{2}_{g})
mod
hS^{-1}(T^{*}M)
.

•

Let

(5.5)

\Omega_{1}:=(U\setminus\overline{\mathcal{U}_{1}})\cap\big(\{a\not=0\}\cup(T^{*}M\setminus S^{*}M)\big).

Roughly speaking,
\Omega_{1}
represents the region “controlled” by
a
. Let
\Omega_{2}:=U\setminus\overline{\mathcal{U}_{2}}
. It is clear that
\mathrm{WF}_{h}(I-A_{0})\subset U\subset\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
. For
i=1,2
, choosing
\chi_{i}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{i};[0,1])
such that
\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}\equiv 1
near
\mathrm{supp}\,b
, we define

(5.6)

\displaystyle A_{1}:=\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(\chi_{1}b)\in\Psi^{\mathrm{comp},sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho}),

\displaystyle A_{2}:=I-A_{0}-A_{1}=\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(\chi_{2}b)+\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})_{\Psi^{-\infty,sc}(M;F^{\rho})}.

In summary, we have constructed a microlocal partition of unity
I=A_{0}+A_{1}+A_{2}
such that:

(5.7)

\displaystyle A_{0}=\psi_{0}(\mathcal{P})\in\Psi^{0,sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho}),\qquad A_{1},\,A_{2}\in\Psi^{\mathrm{comp},sc}_{h}(M;F^{\rho});

\displaystyle a_{i}:=\sigma^{\rho}_{h}(A_{i})=\chi_{i}\big(1-\psi_{0}(|\xi|^{2}_{g})\big)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{i};[0,1]),

\displaystyle\mathrm{WF}_{h}(A_{i})\subset\Omega_{i},\qquad\ \ i=1,2.

We next dynamically refine the partition
A_{j}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, define the set of words of length

,

\mathcal{W}(n):=\{1,2\}^{n}=\big\{\mathbf{w}=w_{0}\cdots w_{n-1}:w_{0},\cdots,w_{n-1}\in\{1,2\}\big\}.

For each word
\mathbf{w}=w_{0}\cdots w_{n-1}\in\mathcal{W}(n)
, we define the operator and the symbol by

(5.8)

A_{\mathbf{w}}=A_{w_{n-1}}(n-1)A_{w_{n-2}}(n-2)\cdots A_{w_{1}}(1)A_{w_{0}}(0),\qquad a_{\mathbf{w}}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(a_{w_{j}}\circ\varphi_{j}\right).

For a subset
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{W}(n)
, we define the operator
A_{\mathcal{E}}
and the symbol
a_{\mathcal{E}}
by

A_{\mathcal{E}}:=\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{E}}A_{\mathbf{w}},\qquad a_{\mathcal{E}}:=\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{E}}a_{\mathbf{w}}.

Since
A_{1}+A_{2}=I-A_{0}=I-\psi_{0}(\mathcal{P})
and

(see (

) and (

) for the definitions of

and

) are both functions of

, they commute with each other. Consequently,

commutes with

. It follows that

A_{\mathcal{W}(n)}=\left(A_{1}+A_{2}\right)^{n}.

This operator is equal to the identity microlocally near
S^{*}M
, implying the following lemma (see
[
18
, Lemma 3.1]
):

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
u\in H^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
,

(5.9)

\|u-(A_{1}+A_{2})^{n}u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C\|(\mathcal{P}-I)u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})},

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
u,n,\mathcal{P}
and
(\mathcal{H},\rho,h)
.

Take
\mu\in(0,1)
very close to

, to be chosen later (in Proposition
5.5
), and put

By Proposition
4.12
and
4.18
, we have:

Lemma 5.3
.

For each

(5.10)

a_{\mathbf{w}}\in S_{L_{s},\mu/4}^{\mathrm{comp}}(U),\qquad A_{\mathbf{w}}=\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho,L_{s}}(a_{\mathbf{w}})+\mathcal{O}(h^{3/4})_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})};

if instead

, then

(5.11)

a_{\mathbf{w}}\in S_{L_{s},\mu}^{\mathrm{comp}}(U),\qquad A_{\mathbf{w}}=\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho,L_{s}}(a_{\mathbf{w}})+\mathcal{O}(h^{1-\mu-})_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Here the constant in

\mathbf{w}
and
(\mathcal{H},\rho,h)
.

Now, define the density function

(5.12)

F:\mathcal{W}\left(N_{0}\right)\rightarrow[0,1],\qquad F(w_{0}\cdots w_{N_{0}-1})=\frac{\#\big\{j\in\{0,\cdots,N_{0}-1\}:w_{j}=1\big\}}{N_{0}}.

Fix small

to be chosen later (in (

Writing words in

(5.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(2N_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{X}\sqcup\mathcal{Y},

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}

By
[

(5.14)

Now we state two key estimates. The first one estimates the mass of an approximate eigenfunction on the controlled region
\mathcal{Y}

[

:

Proposition 5.4

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
and any

\|A_{\mathcal{Y}}u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq\frac{C}{\alpha}\|\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(a)u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}+\frac{C|\log h|}{\alpha h}\|(\mathcal{P}-I)u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}+Ch^{1/8}\|u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Here the constant
C>0
is independent of
u,\alpha,\mathcal{P}
and
(\mathcal{H},\rho,h)
.

The second estimate, based on a fractal uncertainty principle, gives a norm-bound on the operator corresponding to every single word of length
2N_{1}\approx 2\mu|\log h|
; see
[
18
, Proposition 3.5]
:

Proposition 5.5
.

There exist
\beta>0
,
\mu\in(0,1)
depending only on

and
\mathcal{U}_{2}
such that

\sup_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}(2N_{1})}\|A_{\mathbf{w}}\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq Ch^{\beta}.

Propositions
5.4
and
5.5
will be proved in the following two sections. Assuming that these results hold at this moment, we can prove Theorem

:

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Take
\beta,\mu
from Proposition
5.5

\beta<1/8
. Since
A_{\mathcal{X}}+A_{\mathcal{Y}}=A_{\mathcal{W}(2N_{1})}=(A_{1}+A_{2})^{2N_{1}}

\|u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq\|A_{\mathcal{X}}u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}+\|A_{\mathcal{Y}}u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}+\big\|u-(A_{1}+A_{2})^{2N_{1}}u\big\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Combining (
5.14
) with Proposition
5.5
and using the triangle inequality, we have

\|A_{\mathcal{X}}u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}=\mathcal{O}\big(h^{\beta-4\sqrt{\alpha}}\big)\|u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Combining this with Proposition
5.4
and Lemma

we obtain

(5.15)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\alpha}\|\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(a)u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}+\frac{C|\log h|}{\alpha h}\|(\mathcal{P}-I)u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}+Ch^{\beta-4\sqrt{\alpha}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

The proof is complete by choosing

and taking

∎

5.2.
The controlled region: propagation estimates

In this section, we prove Proposition
5.4
. Recall that

\mathrm{supp}\,a_{1}\cap S^{*}M\subset\{a\not=0\},

there exist
b,q\in C_{0}^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
such that
a_{1}=ab+q(p^{2}-1)
. Since
\sigma_{h}^{\rho}(A_{1})=a_{1}
and
\sigma_{h}^{\rho}(\mathcal{P}-I)=p^{2}-1

A_{1}=\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(b)\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(a)+\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(q)(\mathcal{P}-I)+\mathcal{O}(h)_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

By Proposition
4.5
, we have the elliptic estimate: there is a constant
C>0
independent of
u,\mathcal{P}
and
(\mathcal{H},\rho,h)
such that, for any
u\in H^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
,

(5.17)

\|A_{1}u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C\|\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho}(a)u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}+C\|(\mathcal{P}-I)u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}+Ch\|u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Following
[
18
, Lemma 4.2]
, we have the uniform version of the propagation estimate:

Lemma 5.6
.

Assume that
A=A(\mathcal{H};\rho;h):L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})\rightarrow L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
is uniformly bounded: there is a universal constant

Then, for any

and

(5.18)

\|A(t)u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq\|Au\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}+\frac{C\Lambda|t|}{h}\|(\mathcal{P}-I)u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Here the constant

is independent of

and
(\mathcal{H},\rho,h)
.

More generally, one can consider operators obtained by assigning a coefficient to each word. For a function

It follows from
[

and Proposition

that

Lemma 5.7
.

Assume that

. Then,

The
S_{L_{s},1/2,1/4}^{\mathrm{comp}}
seminorms of

and the constant in

are independent of

.

Proof.

We first notice that

a_{c}\in S_{L_{s},1/2,1/4}^{\mathrm{comp}}(U)

\displaystyle A_{c}

∎

By the same argument in

.

Since
0\leq\alpha\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{Z}}\leq F\leq 1

For any

.

Proof of Proposition
5.4
.
Recall the partition (

. Let

By Proposition

5.4.
Applications

In this section, we present several applications of Theorem

First, we derive a uniform semiclassical control estimate on Riemannian covering spaces,

5.4.1.
Semiclassical control on covering spaces

By Theorem
1.2
, we have the following corollary.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
M
be a compact hyperbolic surface and

. There exist constants

Proof.

For any

Let
b(x,\xi)=a(x)\psi_{0}(|\xi|_{x}^{2})(|\xi|_{x}^{2}-1)^{-1}\in S^{-2}(T^{*}M)

Combining Theorem
1.2
with the two estimates (

∎

5.4.2.
Control of eigensections on flat Hilbert bundles

Corollary
1.3
provides a uniform lower bound for the

-norm of eigensections on any open subset
\Omega\subset M
. Next, we investigate how Theorem
1.2
can be applied in spectral theory and quantum chaos. Dyatlov and Jin
[

established that any semiclassical defect measure has full support on the cosphere bundle

. Motivated by this, we will define the semiclassical defect measure associated with eigensections and demonstrate a uniform delocalization result for any Laplacian corresponding to a unitary representation.

We define the microlocal lift of the

-normalized eigensection

as the Wigner measure

(5.31)

W_{j,\rho}:C_{0}^{\infty}(T^{*}M)\to\mathbb{R},\quad W_{j,\rho}(a):=\langle\mathrm{Op}^{\rho}_{h_{j,\rho}}(a)u_{j,\rho},u_{j,\rho}\rangle_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})},\quad h_{j,\rho}=\lambda_{j,\rho}^{-1/2}.

A semiclassical defect measure
\mu
is a weak-
*
limit of the sequence
W_{j,\rho}
as

. Using the existence of the elliptic parametrix (
4.28
), Egorov’s Theorem
4.12
, and the sharp Gårding inequality Proposition
4.7
, one concludes that

is supported on

, is invariant under the geodesic flow

, and is a probability measure. Furthermore, Theorem
1.2
provides a uniformly fully supported property for

Theorem 5.11
.

For any open subset
\Omega\subset S^{*}M
, there exists a constant

independent of

such that for any semiclassical defect measure
\mu
given by

as
\lambda_{j,\rho}\to\infty
, we have

. Thus,

.

Proof.

For any open subset
\Omega\subset S^{*}M
, we take

such that

and

. Let

and

be as in (

implies that

However, if

∎

5.4.3.
Application in mixed quantization

The Berezin-Toeplitz quantization provides another quantization procedure on the positive line bundle
L
with Chern connection
\nabla^{L}
over a compact complex manifold
N
. It implies that

is a Kähler manifold.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}
, let

be the
p

, and let

be the space of holomorphic sections of

on

, which is a finite-dimensional space. Let

P_{p}:L^{2}(N,L^{p})\rightarrow H^{(0,0)}(N,L^{p})

be the associated orthogonal projection. The Berezin-Toeplitz quantization of

is given by

T_{\mathcal{H},p}=P_{p}\mathcal{H}P_{p}\in\operatorname{End}\left(L^{2}(N,L^{p})\right).

Recently, two independent teams, Ma, Ma
[
42
]
and Cekić, Lefeuvre
[

Let
\mathrm{U}
be the group of isomorphisms on

\nabla^{L}

\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{U}

M
. Let

be the Laplacian acting on
C^{\infty}(M;F_{p})
. Let the eigenvalues of
\Delta^{F_{p}}
be
\lambda_{j,p}=\lambda_{j,\rho^{\otimes p}}
and the associated eigensections be
u_{j,p}=u_{j,\rho^{\otimes p}}
.

Let
q:\mathcal{N}=\widetilde{M}\times_{\rho}N\to M
be the fiber bundle with fiber
N
. The pullback bundle
q^{*}(T^{*}M)
is referred to as the total phase space. For any
\mathcal{A}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(q^{*}(T^{*}M))
, the mixed quantization
\mathrm{Op}_{h}(\mathcal{A})
is given by

\mathrm{Op}_{h}^{\rho^{\otimes p}}(T_{\mathcal{A},p}):L^{2}(M;F_{p})\to L^{2}(M;F_{p});

see
[
42

14
]
. Furthermore, if the range of

[

By

(\widetilde{\varphi}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
-invariant, we have

k\nu(\mathcal{U})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\nu\big(\widetilde{\varphi}_{t_{i}}(\mathcal{U})\big)\geq\nu\left(q^{-1}\big(\big[\widetilde{U}_{k}\big]\big)\right)>0,

from which we conclude the proof.
∎

6.
Proof of observability

In this section, we derive the uniform observability of Schrödinger equations on flat Hilbert bundles from Theorem
5.10
. Our method closely follows that of the standard Schrödinger setting in
[
28
]
, with an addition of uniformity in the resulting bounds.

For convenience, we recall the definitions of the semiclassical Fourier transform and its adjoint on
\mathbb{R}
:

\mathcal{F}_{h}\varphi(\tau):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-it\tau/h}\varphi(t)\,dt,\quad\mathcal{F}_{h}^{*}\varphi(\tau):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\tau/h}\varphi(t)\,dt

for any
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. They can be extended to any
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and satisfy the Parseval’s identity

(6.1)

\|\mathcal{F}_{h}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|\mathcal{F}_{h}^{*}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=(2\pi h)^{1/2}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a Hilbert space. If
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{V})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{V})
, the tensor extension
\mathcal{F}_{h}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{V}}
gives the semiclassical Fourier transform on
\mathcal{V}
-valued functions. Let
\mathcal{V}:=L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
, we define the operators

\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}:=\mathcal{F}_{h}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{V}},\qquad\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}:=\mathcal{F}_{h}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{V}}.

In this section, we always assume that
M
is a compact connected hyperbolic surface.

6.1.
Semiclassical observability

First, we establish a semiclassical version of observability.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((1/2,2))
,
0\not\equiv\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
and
\Omega\subset M
be an nonempty open subset. There exist constants
C=C(M,\Omega,\chi,\psi)>0
,
h_{0}=h_{0}(M,\Omega,\chi,\psi)>0
such that, for any
(\mathcal{H},\rho)\in\mathcal{C}
, any
0<h<h_{0}
, and any
u\in L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
,

(6.2)

\|\chi\bigl(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}\bigr)u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\psi(t)e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}\chi\bigl(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}\bigr)u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;F^{\rho})}\,dt.

Proof.

For any single pair
(\mathcal{H},\rho)
, one can obtain the above result directly from
[
10
, Theorem 4]
. Here we follow the proof in
[
28
, Proposition 2.1]
to give a complete proof and keep the control constant uniform in
(\mathcal{H},\rho)
.

Define
v(t)=e^{ith\Delta^{\rho}}\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}
and
w(t)=\psi(ht)v(t)
. It is clear that
v
solves the semiclassical Schrödinger equation

(ih\partial_{t}+h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})v=0.

This gives

(ih\partial_{t}+h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})w=ih^{2}\psi^{\prime}(ht)v(t).

Then we take the adjoint semiclassical Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}
in
t
which gives

(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}-\tau)\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}w(\tau)=-ih^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}\bigl(\psi^{\prime}(ht)v(t)\bigr)(\tau).

By a simple rescaling of Theorem
5.10
, we have, uniformly in
\tau\in[1/2,2]
, for any
0<h<h_{0}
and any
u\in H^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
,

(6.3)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;F^{\rho})}+\frac{|\log h|}{h}\left\|\left(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}-\tau\mathrm{Id}\right)u\right\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\right).

For any
\tau\in[1/2,2]
, applying (
6.3
) to
u=\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}w(\tau)
and
\mathrm{supp}\,\eta\subset\Omega
, we obtain

(6.4)

\|\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}w(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C\|\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}w(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;F^{\rho})}+Ch|\log h|\|\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}\bigl(\psi^{\prime}(ht)v(t)\bigr)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

For
\tau\not\in[1/2,2]
, by definition, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}w(\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-it(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}-\tau)/h}\psi(ht)\chi(-h^{2}\Delta)u_{0}\,dt.

We rewrite it as

(6.5)

\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}w(\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}+\tau)^{-N}(hD_{t})^{N}e^{-it(-h^{2}\Delta-\tau)/h}\psi(ht)\chi(-h^{2}\Delta)u_{0}\,dt.

Recall that
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((1/2,2))
and therefore we have

(6.6)

\|(h^{2}\Delta^{\rho}+\tau)^{-N}\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C_{N}\langle\tau\rangle^{-N}\|\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})},

where
C_{N}
is a positive constant depending on
N
but not on the choice of
(\mathcal{H},\rho)
. Substituting (
6.6
) into (
6.5
), we obtain

(6.7)

\|\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}w(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}=\mathcal{O}((h\langle\tau\rangle^{-1})^{\infty})\|\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Combining (
6.4
) with (
6.7
), we obtain

(6.8)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}w(\tau)\|^{2}_{(\mathbb{R}_{\tau},L^{2}(M;F^{\rho}))}\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}w(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\tau},L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho}))}

\displaystyle\,+Ch^{2}|\log h|^{2}\|\mathcal{F}_{\rho,h}^{*}\bigl(\psi^{\prime}(ht)v(t)\bigr)(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\tau},L^{2}(M;F^{\rho}))}

\displaystyle\,+\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})\|\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

By the Parseval identity (
6.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|w\|^{2}_{(\mathbb{R}_{t},L^{2}(M;F^{\rho}))}\leq

\displaystyle C\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{t},L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho}))}

\displaystyle+Ch^{2}|\log h|^{2}\|\psi^{\prime}(ht)v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{t},L^{2}(M;F^{\rho}))}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})\|\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

By the definition of
v
and
w
, we have

\displaystyle\|w\|^{2}_{(\mathbb{R}_{t},L^{2}(M;F^{\rho}))}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(ht)^{2}\|e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(ht)^{2}\,dt\right)\|\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{-1}\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})},

\displaystyle\|w\|^{2}_{(\mathbb{R}_{t},L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho}))}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(ht)^{2}\|\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho})}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\psi(t)e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt,

and

\displaystyle\|\psi^{\prime}(ht)v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{t},L^{2}(M;F^{\rho}))}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi^{\prime}(ht)|^{2}\|e^{ith\Delta^{\rho}}\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{-1}\|\psi^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|\chi(-h^{2}\Delta^{\rho})u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

As long as
h
is small enough and
\psi\not\equiv 0
, the last two terms on the right-hand side of (
6.8
) can be absorbed into the left-hand side and the proof is completed.
∎

6.2.
Observability with an error term

We prove Theorem
1.5
with an error term:

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
u\in L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
,
\Omega
is a non-empty open subset of
M
,
T>0
, then there exists a constant
C_{T}=C(M,\Omega,T)>0
such that

(6.9)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C_{T}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho})}\,dt+\|u\|^{2}_{H^{-4}(M;F^{\rho})}\right).

Proof.

Again this follows directly from
[
10
, Theorem 7]
. We present the argument in our concrete setting, which is essentially the same as the one given in
[
28
, Proposition 2.2]
. We use a dyadic decomposition

1=\varphi_{0}(r)^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}(r)^{2},

where

\varphi_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}\bigl((-2,2);[0,1]\bigr),\quad\varphi_{k}(r)=\varphi(2^{-k}|r|),\quad\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\bigl((1/2,2);[0,1]\bigr).

Then we have

(6.10)

\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\varphi_{k}(-\Delta^{\rho})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}.

We choose an integer
K
large enough so that
2^{-K}<h_{0}^{2}
. Then for any integer
k\geq K
, by (
6.2
), we obtain

(6.11)

\|\varphi_{k}(-\Delta^{\rho})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\psi(t)e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}\varphi_{k}(-\Delta^{\rho})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho})}\,dt

uniformly in
k
for a chosen nonvanishing function
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,T);[0,1])
. Taking (
6.11
) into (
6.10
), we obtain

(6.12)

\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\|\varphi_{k}(-\Delta^{\rho})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}+\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\psi(t)e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}\varphi_{k}(-\Delta^{\rho})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho})}\,dt.

Since

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{-4}(M;F^{\rho})}\sim\|(-\Delta^{\rho}+I)^{-2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-4k}\|\varphi_{k}(-\Delta^{\rho})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})},

The first sum in the right-hand side of (
6.12
) can be bounded from above by
H^{-4}
norm of
u
, that is,

(6.13)

\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\|\varphi_{k}(-\Delta^{\rho})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{H^{-4}(M;F^{\rho})}.

To estimate the second sum in the right-hand side of (
6.12
), we use the Schrödinger equation
(D_{t}-\Delta^{\rho})e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}=0

to change the frequency localization in space
\varphi_{k}(-\Delta^{\rho})
to frequency localization in time
\varphi_{k}(D_{t})
. Precisely speaking, we have

e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}\varphi_{k}(-\Delta^{\rho})u=\varphi_{k}(-\Delta^{\rho})e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u=\varphi_{k}(-D_{t})e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u=\varphi_{k}(D_{t})e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u,

where we used the fact that all
\varphi_{k}
are even. We introduce another cutoff function
\widetilde{\psi}\in C_{0}^{\infty}\bigl((0,T);[0,1]\bigr)
such that
\widetilde{\psi}=1
on a neighborhood of
\mathrm{supp}\,\psi
. Then we have

\psi(t)\varphi_{k}(D_{t})=\psi(t)\varphi_{k}(D_{t})\widetilde{\psi}(t)+R_{k}(t,D_{t})

where
R_{k}(t,D_{t})=\psi(t)[\widetilde{\psi}(t),\varphi(2^{-k}D_{t})]
with its symbol
R_{k}(t,\tau)
satisfying

(6.14)

\partial^{\alpha}R_{k}(t,\tau)=\mathcal{O}\bigl(2^{-kN}\langle t\rangle^{-N}\langle\tau\rangle^{-N}\bigr),\quad\forall N.

Then we have

(6.15)

\|\psi(t)e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}\varphi_{k}(-\Delta^{\rho})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho})}\leq\|\varphi_{k}(D_{t})\widetilde{\psi}(t)e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho})}+\|R_{k}(t,D_{t})e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho})}.

Taking (
6.13
) and all (
6.15
) into (
6.12
), we obtain

(6.16)

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\varphi_{k}(D_{t})\widetilde{\psi}(t)e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho})}\,dt

\displaystyle+C\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|R_{k}(t,D_{t})e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt+C\|u\|^{2}_{H^{-4}(M;F^{\rho})}.

The first sum is bounded by

(6.17)

\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\varphi_{k}(D_{t})\widetilde{\psi}(t)e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho})}\,dt\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\widetilde{\psi}(t)e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho})}\,dt.

To estimate the second sum, we write

\displaystyle R_{k}(t,D_{t})e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u

\displaystyle=R_{k}(t,D_{t})(-D_{t}+1)^{2}e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}(-\Delta^{\rho}+I)^{2}u

\displaystyle=\widetilde{R}_{k}(t,D_{t})\langle t\rangle^{-2}e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}(-\Delta^{\rho}+I)^{-2}u

where the symbol of
\widetilde{R}_{k}(t,D_{t})=R_{k}(t,D_{t})(-D_{t}+1)^{2}\langle t\rangle^{2}
also satisfies (
6.14
) and thus
\widetilde{R}_{k}(t,D_{t})=\mathcal{O}(2^{-k}):L^{2}(R,F^{\rho})\to L^{2}(R,F^{\rho})
. Therefore the second sum of (
6.16
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|R_{k}(t,D_{t})e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}2^{-2k}\|\langle t\rangle^{-2}e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}(-\Delta^{\rho}+I)^{-2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times M;F^{\rho})}

(6.18)

\displaystyle\leq C\|(-\Delta^{\rho}+I)^{-2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}=C\|u\|^{2}_{H^{-4}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Taking (
6.17
) and (
6.18
) into (
6.16
) finishes the proof of (
6.9
).
∎

If we apply
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
, we obtain the observability with error term for any covering space of
M
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Assume that
\Omega\subset M
is a non-empty open subset and
T>0
, then there exist constants
C>0
and
h_{0}>0
depending only on
\Omega
,
M
and
T
, independent of
\Gamma
, such that for all
h\in\left(0,h_{0}\right)
, all
u\in L^{2}(X)
we have the estimate

\|u\|_{L^{2}(X)}\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{it\Delta_{g}}u\|_{L^{2}(\pi^{-1}(\Omega))}dt+\|u\|_{H^{-4}(X)}\right).

However, when
\mathcal{H}
is infinite-dimensional,
\Delta^{\rho}
is not a Fredholm operator on
L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
. We cannot apply the uniqueness-compactness argument to remove the remainder term. Finally, we assume that
\Gamma
is type I group. Then, the dimension of the irreducible representation of
\Gamma
is finite,
i.e.
,
d_{\Gamma}<\infty
. By the generalized Bloch Theorem, we can fix a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{d_{\Gamma}})
.

6.3.
Removing the error term

The last step is to remove the error term and therefore complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
. We use the uniqueness-compactness argument of Bardos, Lebeau and Rauch
[
6
]
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Fix
\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{d_{\Gamma}})
, for any unitary representation
\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
, any non-empty open subset
\Omega\subset M
, and any
T>0
, there exists a
C=C(M,\Omega,T,d_{\Gamma})
such that for any
u\in L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
,

(6.19)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt.

For any unitary representation
\rho
and any
T>0
, we define a linear subspace

N_{T}^{\rho}=\{u\in L^{2}(M;F^{\rho}):e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u\equiv 0\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Omega\}.

Lemma 6.5
.

For any unitary representation
\rho
and any
T>0
, we have
N^{\rho}_{T}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Given any fixed
\rho
and
T>0
, let
\varepsilon>0
and
u\in N_{T}^{\rho}
. We have

v_{\epsilon}=\frac{e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}u-u}{\varepsilon}\in N_{T-\delta}^{\rho},\quad\forall\varepsilon\leq\delta.

Since
u\in L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
,
v_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{-4}(M;F^{\rho})
for
\varepsilon<T/2
,

\|v_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{-4}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C,\quad\forall\varepsilon\leq\delta,

where
C>0
is a positive constant. Then (
6.9
) gives

\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})}^{2}\leq C_{T/2}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{-4}(M;F^{\rho})}\leq C_{T/2}C,\quad\forall\varepsilon\leq\delta.

By definition of
e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}
, we obtain
v_{\varepsilon}\to i\Delta^{\rho}u
in
L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
for
\varepsilon\to 0
and hence
u\in H^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
. This gives
\Delta^{\rho}u\in N_{T-\delta}^{\rho}
for any
\delta>0
and hence
\Delta^{\rho}u\in N_{T}^{\rho}
. Therefore,
N_{T}^{\rho}
is stable under
\Delta^{\rho}
. Again by Proposition
6.2
, the
H^{-4}(M;F^{\rho})
norm is equivalent to the
L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
norm on
N_{T}^{\rho}
, so the unit ball in
N_{T}^{\rho}
is compact and thus
N_{T}^{\rho}
is of finite dimension. If it is not
\{0\}
, then it must contain some eigenfunction
\varphi
, but this would imply that
\varphi\equiv 0
on
\Omega
, which violates Theorem
5.10
. Hence we finish the proof.
∎

We notice that this step is the only place we need the Fredholm property of
\Delta^{\rho}
, because we need the compactness of the embedding
H^{-4}\hookrightarrow L^{2}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

To remove the error term, we proceed by contradiction. Suppose that there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
in
L^{2}(M;F^{\rho_{n}})
such that

(6.20)

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(M;F^{\rho_{n}})}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{it\Delta^{\rho_{n}}}u_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;F^{\rho_{n}})}\,dt\leq\frac{1}{n}.

Then there exists a subsequence
\{u_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
and a sequence of maps
\{\mathfrak{U}_{\rho_{n_{k}}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
defined in Section
2.1
, such that

\mathfrak{U}_{\rho_{n_{k}}}u_{n_{k}}\mathfrak{U}_{\rho_{n_{k}}}^{-1}\rightharpoonup\mathfrak{U}_{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\mathfrak{U}_{\rho_{0}}^{-1}\ \ \ \ \text{ weakly in }L^{2}(M;\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{d_{\Gamma}}))

for some
u_{0}\in L^{2}(M;F^{\rho})
and some finite dimensional unitary representation
\rho_{0}
with the
\rho_{0}
-equivalent map
\mathfrak{U}_{\rho_{0}}
. Thus
\mathfrak{U}_{\rho_{n_{k}}}u_{n_{k}}\mathfrak{U}_{\rho_{n_{k}}}^{-1}\to\mathfrak{U}_{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\mathfrak{U}_{\rho_{0}}^{-1}
strongly in
H^{-4}(M;\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{d_{\Gamma}}))
. On the one hand, applying (
6.9
) and (
6.20
), we obtain

(6.21)

1\leq C\frac{1}{n_{k}}+C\|u_{n_{k}}\|^{2}_{H^{-4}(M;F^{\rho})}.

Taking
k\to\infty
, we obtain

(6.22)

\|(1-\Delta_{\rho})^{2}\mathfrak{U}_{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\mathfrak{U}_{\rho_{0}}^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(M;\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{d_{\Gamma}})}\geq C^{-1/2}>0.

On the other hand, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{it\Delta_{g}}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;F^{\rho})}\,dt=0.

Then
u_{0}\in N_{T}^{\rho}
and thus
u_{0}\equiv 0
, which contradicts to (
6.22
) finishing the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
∎

Now we complete the proof of Theorem
1.6
:

Proof.

According to the generalized Bloch Theorem,

(6.23)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\|\mathcal{B}u([\rho],x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M,F^{\rho})}d\mu([\rho])

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}}\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{it\Delta^{\rho}}\mathcal{B}u([\rho],x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,F^{\rho})}dtd\mu([\rho])

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{it\Delta_{g}}u(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\pi^{-1}(\Omega))}dt.

The proof is complete.
∎

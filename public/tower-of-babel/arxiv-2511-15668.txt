Nonlinear scalar field equations with a critical Hardy potential
Bartosz Bieganowski, Daniel Strzelecki

Abstract
. We study the existence of solutions for the nonlinear scalar field equation − Δ ​ u − ( N − 2 ) 2 4 ​ | x | 2 ​ u = g ⁡ ( u ) , in ​ ℝ N ∖ { 0 } , -\Delta u-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4|x|^{2}}u=g(u),\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}, where the potential − ( N − 2 ) 2 4 ​ | x | 2 -\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4|x|^{2}} is the critical Hardy potential and N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 . Introduction

We study the existence, multiplicity, and qualitative properties of solutions for the nonlinear scalar field equation

(1.1)

-\Delta u-\mu\frac{u}{|x|^{2}}=g(u),\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

where
N\geq 3
. This paper focuses specifically on the
critical case

\mu=\mu_{c}=\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
, where
\mu_{c}
is the best constant in the classical Hardy inequality. The inverse-square potential
\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}
, commonly referred to as the Hardy potential, is of significant importance in quantum mechanics, modeling diverse phenomena such as the interaction of a charge with a dipole or certain molecular potentials
[
1
,
2
,
5
]
. The nonlinearity
g
is assumed to satisfy the general Berestycki-Lions conditions, (G1)–(G5) below, which do not include the celebrated Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition.

This problem presents a combination of two significant analytical challenges: the
criticality of the potential
and the
generality of the nonlinearity
.

The foundational case, where
\mu=0
, was famously studied by Berestycki and Lions in their seminal work
[
3
]
. They established the existence of a ground state solution in the standard Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
under these general conditions. The inclusion of the
critical
Hardy potential
\mu_{c}/|x|^{2}
, however, fundamentally alters the problem’s functional analytic structure. The associated quadratic form
\xi(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u|^{2}-\mu_{c}|x|^{-2}u^{2})\,dx
is merely positive semi-definite, not coercive, on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. This loss of coercivity necessitates moving the analysis to a larger, Sobolev-type space,
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which is the completion of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the problem’s natural norm. The properties of this space, which is essential for our analysis, build on foundational work by authors such as Frank
[
9
]
, Suzuki
[
25
]
, Mukherjee, Nam, and Nguyen
[
20
]
, Trachanas and Zographopoulos
[
27
,
28
]
.

Our focus on the
critical
case
\mu=\mu_{c}
should be contrasted with the
subcritical
case
\mu<\mu_{c}
. For subcritical potentials, the quadratic form remains coercive (or can be made so with an equivalent norm), allowing for a more standard application of variational methods within
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
or related weighted spaces. The subcritical case was of interest to many authors (see e.g.
[
12
,
17
,
18
,
31
]
and references therein). For instance, Li, Li, and Tang
[
17
]
studied the subcritical problem under Berestycki-Lions-type conditions, establishing the existence of a ground state solution
within
the
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
space. We find that for the critical potential, any nonnegative solution is genuinely singular and
does not
belong to
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. This highlights a fundamental difference in the qualitative nature of solutions as the potential transitions from subcritical to critical. Other related works, such as those by Deng, Jin, and Peng
[
6
]
, Felli, Pistoia
[
8
]
, Smets
[
23
]
, and Terracini
[
26
]
, have investigated problems combining the Hardy potential with the
critical Sobolev exponent

2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
, which introduces a different set of compactness challenges related to the nonlinearity itself.

The second major challenge, the absence of the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition, means that standard mountain pass arguments do not guarantee the boundedness of Palais-Smale sequences. For the classical problem (
\mu=0
), this difficulty is often overcome by seeking solutions on the Pohožaev constraint. A systematic approach to find such solutions was developed by Jeanjean
[
15
]
, and later refined by Jeanjean, Tanaka
[
16
]
and Hirata, Ikoma, Tanaka
[
13
]
. They introduced an
augmented functional
technique to construct a bounded Pohožaev-Palais-Smale sequence at the mountain pass level. We adopt this strategy to construct a bounded Pohožaev-Palais-Smale sequence for our singular problem.

The mere boundedness of a (PPS) sequence is not sufficient; one must additionally establish its convergence. The primary tool for this in translation-invariant problems is the profile decomposition method, developed by Gérard
[
10
]
and Nawa
[
21
]
. This method fails in our setting because the
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
space, due to the potential centered at the origin, is
not
translation-invariant. A central technical contribution of this paper is the development of a
new
profile decomposition specifically adapted to bounded (PPS) sequences in this non-translation-invariant space. This decomposition is crucial as it shows that any potential
bubbles
(weak limits of translated sequences) must be solutions to the limiting problem
without
the potential, i.e., in the classical
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
space. By showing that our mountain pass level is strictly less than the ground state energy of this limiting problem (an energy comparison adapting
[
16
]
), we prove that no bubbles can form, forcing the strong convergence of the sequence to our solution.

Finally, we extend our analysis to the existence of non-radial solutions. This is a classical and challenging question, famously posed by Berestycki and Lions
[
3
]
for the
\mu=0
case. This question was recently answered for the classical problem by Mederski
[
19
]
, who found non-radial solutions by minimizing the functional on a subspace of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-functions orthogonal to the space of radial functions. We successfully adapt this symmetry-based minimization argument of
[
19
]
from the standard
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
space to our non-translation-invariant, singular
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
setting, thereby establishing the existence of non-radial solutions for the critical Hardy potential problem under general nonlinearities.

In the critical case, (
1.1
) rewrites as

(1.2)

-\Delta u-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4|x|^{2}}u=g(u),\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

We consider the following general conditions

(G1)

g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous;

(G2)

-\infty<\liminf_{s\to 0}\frac{g(s)}{s}\leq\limsup_{s\to 0}\frac{g(s)}{s}=-\ell<0
;

(G3)

\lim_{|s|\to\infty}\frac{g(s)}{|s|^{p-1}}=0
and
\lim_{|s|\to\infty}g(s)s\geq 0
for some
p\in\left(2,2^{*}\right)
;

(G4)

there is
\zeta_{0}>0
such that
G(\zeta_{0})>0
, where
G(s):=\int_{0}^{s}g(\tau)\,d\tau>0
;

(G5)

there are
\nu>2
and
\gamma_{0}<0
such that

G(s)-\frac{1}{\nu}g(s)s\leq\gamma_{0}s^{2}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Assumptions (G1)–(G4) are standard, in the spirit of
[
3
]
, although we do not require oddness of
g
. The condition (G5) is new and is required to verify the boundedness of (PPS) sequences. It can be treated as a variant of the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition, which in its original form

(AR)

\mbox{there is }\nu>2\mbox{ such that }0\leq G(s)\leq\frac{1}{\nu}g(s)s,\quad s\in\mathbb{R}

is not necessarily satisfied under (G1)–(G5).

We consider the so-called
positive-mass case
(
\ell>0
). Therefore, it is convenient to define
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

(1.3)

f(s):=\ell s+g(s),\quad s\in\mathbb{R}

and then
G
can be rewritten as

G(s)=-\frac{\ell}{2}s^{2}+F(s),\quad F(s):=\int_{0}^{s}f(\tau)\,d\tau.

Then conditions (G1)–(G4) imply the following

(F1)

f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous;

(F2)

-\infty<\liminf_{s\to 0}\frac{f(s)}{s}\leq\limsup_{s\to 0}\frac{f(s)}{s}=0<\ell
;

(F3)

\lim_{|s|\to\infty}\frac{f(s)}{|s|^{p-1}}=0
and
\lim_{|s|\to\infty}f(s)s\geq 0
for some
p\in\left(2,2^{*}\right)
;

(F4)

there is
\zeta_{0}>0
such that
F(\zeta_{0})>\frac{\ell}{2}\zeta_{0}^{2}
;

(F5)

there are
\nu>2
and
\gamma_{0}<\ell\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\nu}\right)
such that

F(s)-\frac{1}{\nu}f(s)s\leq\gamma_{0}s^{2}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Observe that
f
also doesn’t need to satisfy the (
AR
) condition. In fact, (
AR
) implies (F5). Below we present a few examples of nonlinearities satisfying (F1)–(F5).

Example 1.1
.

In what follows, we fix
p\in(2,2^{*})
.

(a)

It is standard to check that
f(s)=|s|^{q-2}s
satisfies (F1)–(F4) with
2<q<p
in (F3). To see (F5) note that

F(s)-\frac{1}{q}f(s)s=\frac{1}{q}|u|^{q}-\frac{1}{q}|u|^{q}=0.

(b)

Consider
f(s)=\ln(e+|s|)|s|^{q-2}s
, where
2<q<p
. It is clear that (F1)–(F3) are satisfied. Since
f
is bounded from below by the power nonlinearity, (F4) is obvious. To get (F5) observe that, for
\nu=q

F(s)-\frac{1}{q}f(s)s\leq\ln(e+|s|)\left(\int_{0}^{s}|s|^{q-2}s\,ds-\frac{1}{q}|s|^{q}\right)=0.

(c)

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
2<q<p
,
0<\alpha<q-2
. Consider
f(s)=\frac{|s|^{q-2}s}{1+\varepsilon|s|^{\alpha}}
. Clearly (F1)–(F3) are satisfied. To see (F4), we compute for sufficiently large
s>1
,

F(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{|\tau|^{q-2}\tau}{1+\varepsilon|\tau|^{\alpha}}\,d\tau\geq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon s^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{s}\tau^{q-1}\,d\tau=\frac{s^{q}}{q(1+\varepsilon s^{\alpha})}>\frac{s^{q-\alpha}}{q(1+\varepsilon)}=\frac{s^{q-\alpha-2}}{q(1+\varepsilon)}s^{2}>\frac{\ell}{2}s^{2}.

To get (F5) we note that
s\mapsto H(s):=F(s)-\frac{1}{\nu}f(s)s
is even, so it is enough to verify (F5) for
s>0
. For such
s
we compute

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{s^{q-1}}{1+\varepsilon s^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{qs^{q-1}(1+\varepsilon s^{\alpha})-\alpha\varepsilon s^{q+\alpha-1}}{(1+\varepsilon s^{\alpha})^{2}}=\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{s^{q-1}}{1+\varepsilon s^{\alpha}}\left(\nu-q+\frac{\alpha\varepsilon s^{\alpha}}{1+\varepsilon s^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{s^{q-1}}{1+\varepsilon s^{\alpha}}\left(\nu-q+\alpha\right)=0

for
\nu=q-\alpha>2
. Taking into account that
H(0)=0
, we obtain

F(s)-\frac{1}{q-\alpha}f(s)s\leq H(0)=0.

(d)

Consider
f(s)=(2+\arctan(s))|s|^{q-2}s
with
2<q<p
. Since
s\mapsto 2+\arctan s
is increasing and bounded away from zero, it satisfies
(F1)-(F5)
in the same way as in (b).

(e)

Consider
2<r<q<p
and

f(s)=|s|^{q-2}s-|s|^{r-2}s.

Clearly (F1)–(F4) are satisfied. To see (F5) note that

F(s)-\frac{1}{q}f(s)s=\frac{1}{q}|s|^{q}-\frac{1}{r}|s|^{r}-\frac{1}{q}|s|^{q}+\frac{1}{q}|s|^{r}=\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}\right)|s|^{r}<0

for
s\neq 0
. Note that
f
doesn’t satisfy (
AR
).

(f)

Let
2<q<p
and

f(s)=|s|^{q-2}s+e^{-1/|s|}\frac{|s|^{q-2}s}{1+|s|^{q-2}}.

It is easy to check that (F1)–(F4) hold. To show (F5), as in (c) we have that
s\mapsto H(s):=F(s)-\frac{1}{q}f(s)s
is even. Hence, let
s>0
. Note that the power part
|s|^{q-2}s
of
f
does not give any contribution in
H
, so

H(s)=\int_{0}^{s}e^{-1/\tau}\frac{\tau^{q-1}}{1+\tau^{q-2}}\,d\tau-\frac{1}{q}e^{-1/s}\frac{s^{q}}{1+s^{q-2}}.

Then

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=e^{-1/s}\frac{s^{q-1}}{1+s^{q-2}}-\frac{1}{q}e^{-1/s}\frac{2s^{2q+1}+s^{2q}+qs^{q+3}+x^{q+2}}{(s^{q}+s^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-1/s}\left((q-2)s^{2q+1}-s^{2q}-s^{q+2}\right)}{q(s^{q}+s^{2})^{2}}\leq\frac{(q-2)s^{2q+1}}{q(s^{q}+s^{2})^{2}}\leq\frac{q-2}{q}s.

Therefore

H(s)\leq\frac{q-2}{2q}s^{2}=:\gamma_{0}s^{2}.

Hence the assumptions are satisfied with

\ell>\gamma_{0}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{-1}=1.

Note that this example does not satisfy (
AR
). Indeed, for
H_{\nu}(s):=F(s)-\frac{1}{\nu}f(s)
we have

H_{\nu}^{\prime}(s)=\frac{e^{-1/s}\left(((\nu-2)s-1)s^{2q}+s^{q+2}(\nu s-qs-1)\right)}{\nu(s^{q}+s^{2})^{2}}.

Hence, for
\nu>2
there is
\lim_{s\to\infty}H_{\nu}^{\prime}(s)=\infty
, so
\lim_{s\to\infty}H_{\nu}(s)=\infty
and (
AR
) is not satisfied for any
\nu>2
.

The natural energy functional associated with (
1.2
) is given by

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx+\frac{\ell}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)\,dx.

Clearly,
J
is well defined on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, however the quadratic form

u\mapsto\|u\|^{2}:=\xi(u)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,dx,

where

\xi(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx,

generates a norm in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which is not complete (and, in particular, is not equivalent to the standard one). Therefore, to work in a Banach space, we need to slightly enlarge the energy space. More precisely, we define

X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\mbox{completion of }H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\mbox{ with respect to the norm }\|\cdot\|.

For properties of
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and the variational setting (including the appropriate definition of
\xi
and
\|\cdot\|
), see Sections
2
and
3
, respectively. Under the foregoing assumptions,
J
is a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
functional on
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and its critical points are weak solutions to (
1.2
).

It is natural to consider the Pohožaev constraint associated with (
1.2
), namely

{\mathcal{M}}:=\left\{u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}\ :\ \xi(u)=2^{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,dx\right\}.

In the classical setting it is known that every weak solution of a scalar field equation belongs to the Pohožaev constraint. Here, due to the presence of the critical Hardy potential, it is unclear whether all nontrivial critical points of
J
belong to
{\mathcal{M}}
. In our analysis we will find a solution that minimizes
J
on
{\mathcal{M}}
, although it is vague whether it is a ground state solution.

We are ready to state our first result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that (G1)–(G5) are satisfied.

(a)

There exists a nontrivial solution
u_{0}\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of (
1.2
) that satisfies the Pohožaev constraint
{\mathcal{M}}
.

(b)

u_{0}
is a minimizer of the energy functional
J
on
{\mathcal{M}}
.

(c)

Every solution
u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of (
1.2
) belongs to
C^{1,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

(d)

If, in addition,
g
is Hölder continuous, every solution
u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of (
1.2
) is of the class
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
.

(e)

If, in addition,
g
is odd, there exists a nontrivial, nonnegative, radial and radially nonincreasing solution in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
of (
1.2
), which is a minimizer of
J
on
{\mathcal{M}}
.

(f)

Every nonnegative solution of (
1.2
) in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
does not belong to
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

We would like to point out that if
g
is odd, even if we restrict the setting to radially symmetric functions, it is unclear whether a direct minimization on
{\mathcal{M}}
can be applied. Specifically, it is unclear whether a minimizing sequence on
{\mathcal{M}}
(that is, sequence
(u_{n})\subset{\mathcal{M}}
such that
J(u_{n})\to\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}}J
) is bounded.

In this paper, we present a profile decomposition approach, which is based on the concentration-compactness principle in the spirit of Lions. The decomposition of minimizing sequences, in the spirit of
[
21
,
10
]
is a challenging problem, as the space
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is not translation-invariant, and even if the sequence has a weak limit after finding proper translations, it should be a solution to the limiting problem without the singular potential, for which the proper energy space is
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, not
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Next, we investigate the existence of non-radial solutions. We follow the ideas of
[
19
]
. Suppose that
N\geq 4
and write
\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2M}
, where
2\leq M\leq\frac{N}{2}
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}
we will write
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
, where
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
and
x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-2M}
. We introduce an action
\tau:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
by

\tau(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):=(x_{2},x_{1},x_{3})

and the subspace

(1.4)

X_{\tau}:=\left\{u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ :\ u(x)=-u(\tau x)\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\right\}.

Then, if
u\in X_{\tau}
is radially symmetric, then
u\equiv 0
and therefore
X_{\tau}
does not contain nontrivial, radial functions.

Let
\mathcal{O}:=\mathcal{O}(M)\times\mathcal{O}(M)\times\mathrm{id}_{N-2M}\subset\mathcal{O}(N)
. We introduce the subspace
X^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of
\mathcal{O}
-invariant functions.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that (G1)–(G5) are satisfied and that
g
is odd. Then

\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}\cap X_{\tau}\cap X^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}J\geq 2\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}}J

and there exists a nontrivial solution
v_{0}\in{\mathcal{M}}\cap X_{\tau}\cap X^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of (
1.2
), which minimizes
J
on
{\mathcal{M}}\cap X_{\tau}\cap X^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In particular,
v_{0}
is not radial.

The paper is organized as follows. Sections
2

2.
Functional setting

Recall the Hardy inequality

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\leq\frac{4}{(N-2)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,\quad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

In particular, it implies that

\|u\|^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}+u^{2}\,dx,\quad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

In what follows we introduce the notation

(2.5)

\xi(u,v):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\left(\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}\nabla u\nabla v\,dx-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}\frac{uv}{|x|^{2}}\,dx-\frac{N-2}{2}\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{|x|=\varepsilon}uv\,dS\right)

and

\xi(u):=\xi(u,u)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\left(\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx-\frac{N-2}{2}\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{|x|=\varepsilon}u^{2}\,dS\right).

Such a characterization plays a role for functions
u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Otherwise, namely if
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it is more convenient to use the formula (
2.2
) instead of (
2.4
).

Observe that
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is not translation invariant, and that the maximal translation invariant subspace is
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Indeed, if
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
u(\cdot-y)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover we have the following fact.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
,
u(\cdot-y)\not\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Note that, thanks to e.g. the characterization of the norm (
2.4
) from
[
27
,
28
]
, we know that
\nabla u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\varepsilon))
for every
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, since
u\not\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\nabla u\not\in L^{2}(B(0,\varepsilon))
for every
\varepsilon>0
. Fix
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
and choose
\varepsilon<\frac{|y|}{2}
. Then
\nabla u(\cdot-y)\not\in L^{2}(B(y,\varepsilon))
, but
B(y,\varepsilon)\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\varepsilon)
, so
\nabla u(\cdot-y)\not\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\varepsilon))
, meaning that
\nabla u(\cdot-y)\not\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
∎

This fact makes the argument based on the Lions’ concentration-compactness lemma and translations significantly more challenging than in the standard case. We overcome this difficulty by the use of continuous embeddings into
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which is translation-invariant, and by making use of the regularity theory to find that limits of translated Pohožaev-Palais-Smale sequences are indeed in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and even in
H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. For this purpose we show that such a limit is a solution to an elliptic problem without the singular potential in the distributional sense. Then we make use of Weyl’s lemma for the Poisson problem to see that such a solution lies in
H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then, to show even more - the limit point belongs globally to
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
- we make use of a variant of the Caccioppoli inequality.

3.
Variational setting

Define
J:X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\xi(u)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,dx.

Then, in view of (G1)–(G3) and embeddings (
2.3
),
J
is of
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
class and

J^{\prime}(u)(v)=\xi(u,v)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(u)v\,dx.

Observe that on
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we can introduce an equivalent norm

\|u\|_{\ell}^{2}:=\xi(u)+\ell\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,dx

and the scalar product

\langle u,v\rangle_{\ell}:=\xi(u,v)+\ell\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uv\,dx.

Then
J
and its derivative can be rewritten as

J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\ell}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)\,dx,\quad J^{\prime}(u)(v)=\langle u,v\rangle_{\ell}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u)v\,dx,\quad u,v\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Note that if
u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a critical point of
J
, then
u
is a weak solution to (
1.2
) on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Indeed, take any test function
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
and consider the equality
J^{\prime}(u)(\varphi)=0
. Then

\xi(u,\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(u)\varphi\,dx.

Recall (
2.5
) and, since
\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,\varepsilon)
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, we see that

\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,dx,\quad\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}\frac{u\varphi}{|x|^{2}}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u\varphi}{|x|^{2}}\,dx,\quad\int_{|x|=\varepsilon}u\varphi\,dS=0.

Hence

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,dx-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u\varphi}{|x|^{2}}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(u)\varphi\,dx

and
u
is a weak solution to (
1.2
) on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
.

We are going to show that
J
has the mountain pass geometry.

Lemma 3.1
.

There is
r>0
such that
\inf_{\|u\|_{\ell}=r}J(u)>0
.

Proof.

Note that, from (G1)–(G3) there is
C>0
such that
G(u)\leq-\frac{\ell}{4}|u|^{2}+C|u|^{p}
. Hence

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\xi(u)+\frac{\ell}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\,dx-C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\xi(u)+\frac{1}{4}\|u\|_{\ell}^{2}-C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\,dx\geq\frac{1}{4}\|u\|_{\ell}^{2}-C\|u\|_{\ell}^{p}.

Since
p>2
, we get
\inf_{\|u\|_{\ell}=r}J(u)>0
for sufficiently small
r>0
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

There is
v\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
J(v)<0
and
\|v\|_{\ell}>r
, where
r
is given in Lemma
3.1
.

Proof.

Following the idea from
[
3
]
, for any
R>0
, we define

w_{R}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\zeta_{0}&\quad|x|<R\\
\zeta_{0}(R+1-|x|)&\quad R\leq|x|\leq R+1\\
0&\quad|x|>R+1\end{array}\right.,

where
\zeta_{0}
is defined in (G4).
It is clear that
w_{R}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for every
R>0
. Moreover
w_{R}(x)\geq 0
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Recall that

|B(0,R)|=c_{N}R^{N}

for some
c_{N}>0
. Hence
|B(0,R+1)\setminus B(0,R)|=c_{N}\left((R+1)^{N}-R^{N}\right)\lesssim R^{N-1}
. Hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(w_{R})\,dx

\displaystyle\geq G(\zeta_{0})c_{N}R^{N}-\sup_{\zeta\in[0,\zeta_{0}]}G(\zeta)\alpha_{N}R^{N-1},\quad\mbox{for some }\alpha_{N}>0,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w_{R}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{N-1},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{R}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\gtrsim R^{N}.

Then

J(w_{R})\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w_{R}|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(w_{R})\,dx\leq C_{1}R^{N-1}-C_{2}R^{N}+C_{3}R^{N-1}

with
C_{2}>0
. Moreover

\|w_{R}\|_{\ell}^{2}\geq\ell\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{R}|^{2}\,dx\gtrsim R^{N}.

Thus
J(w_{R})<0
and
\|w_{R}\|_{\ell}>r
for
R>0
large enough.
∎

Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
imply that
J
has the mountain pass geometry. We define the mountain pass level

(3.1)

c:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}J(\gamma(t)),

where the family of paths
\Gamma
is given by

(3.2)

\Gamma:=\left\{\gamma\in{\mathcal{C}}([0,1];X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\ :\ \gamma(0)=0,\ J(\gamma(1))<0\right\}.

The following fact is a well-known consequence of the deformation lemma and we include it here for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 3.3
.

If there is
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
t^{*}\in(0,1)
with

c=J(\gamma(t^{*}))>J(\gamma(t))\quad\mbox{for }t\neq t^{*},

then
J^{\prime}(\gamma(t^{*}))=0
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
J^{\prime}(\gamma(t^{*}))\neq 0
. Since
J
is of
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
class, we may choose
\delta>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\inf\{\|J^{\prime}(v)\|\ :\ \|v-\gamma(t^{*})\|\leq\delta\}>\frac{8\varepsilon}{\delta}.

Then, by the deformation lemma
[
30
, Lemma 2.3]
, we may find a flow
\eta\in{\mathcal{C}}([0,1]\times X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N});X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
such that
\eta(1,\gamma(\cdot))\in\Gamma
and
J(\eta(1,\gamma(t^{*})))\leq c-\varepsilon<c
. Moreover, for every
t\neq t^{*}
,
J(\eta(1,\gamma(t)))\leq J(\gamma(t))<c
. Hence, for every
t\in[0,1]
we have
J(\eta(1,\gamma(t)))<c
. Thus
\sup_{t\in[0,1]}J(\eta(1,\gamma(t)))<c
and we obtain a contradiction.
∎

We define

P(u)=\xi(u)-2^{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,dx.

Remark 3.4
.

Note that, because of the presence of singular terms in
\xi(u)
, it is not known if all critical points
u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of
J
satisfy the condition
P(u)=0
.

To find a Pohožaev-Palais-Smale sequence, we will use the technique of the augmented functional introduced by Jeanjean
[
15
]
(see also
[
13
,
16
]
). Then, thanks to the density of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we can choose such a sequence in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which will later simplify notations and computations, but it is not necessary for the argument to work.

Lemma 3.5
.

There is
(u_{n})\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

J(u_{n})\to c,\quad J^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0,\quad P(u_{n})\to 0.

Proof.

Set
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\times X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
given by

\Phi(t,u)(x):=u(e^{-t}x).

Then

J(\Phi(t,u))=\frac{e^{(N-2)t}}{2}\xi(u)-e^{Nt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,dx.

It is clear that
J\circ\Phi
is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
.
Then we consider the following family of paths

\widetilde{\Gamma}:=\left\{\widetilde{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{C}}([0,1];\mathbb{R}\times X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\ :\ \widetilde{\gamma}(0)=(0,0),\,(J\circ\Phi)(\widetilde{\gamma}(1))<0\right\}.

Note that
\Gamma=\{\Phi\circ\widetilde{\gamma}\ :\ \widetilde{\gamma}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}\}
. Hence

c=\inf_{\widetilde{\gamma}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}(J\circ\Phi)(\widetilde{\gamma}(t)).

For every
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{c}{2}\right)
we may choose a path
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\sup_{t\in[0,1]}(J\circ\Phi)(0,\gamma(t))\leq c+\varepsilon
. Hence, by Ekeland’s variational principle
[
30
, Theorem 2.8]
, there exists
(t,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\displaystyle c-2\varepsilon\leq(J\circ\Phi)(t,u)\leq c+2\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\inf_{s\in[0,1]}\left(|t|^{2}+\|u-\gamma(s)\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq 2\sqrt{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\left\|(J\circ\Phi)^{\prime}(t,u)\right\|\leq 2\sqrt{\varepsilon}

and we can construct a sequence
(t_{n},v_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}\times X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

t_{n}\to 0,\quad(J\circ\Phi)(t_{n},v_{n})\to c,\quad(J\circ\Phi)^{\prime}(t_{n},v_{n})\to 0.

Set
\widetilde{u}_{n}:=\Phi(t_{n},v_{n})
. Then, for every
(h,w)\in\mathbb{R}\times X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

(J\circ\Phi)^{\prime}(t_{n},v_{n})(h,w)=J^{\prime}(\widetilde{u}_{n})(\Phi(t_{n},w))+\frac{N-2}{2}P(\widetilde{u}_{n})h.

It is clear that
J(\widetilde{u}_{n})=J(\Phi(t_{n},v_{n}))\to c
. Taking
w=0
and
h=1
we obtain that

o(1)=(J\circ\Phi)^{\prime}(t_{n},v_{n})(1,w)=P(\widetilde{u}_{n})

so that
P(\widetilde{u}_{n})\to 0
. It is also classical to check that
J^{\prime}(\widetilde{u}_{n})\to 0
. Since
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a dense subset of
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, for each
n
we can find
u_{n}
such that

\|\widetilde{u}_{n}-u_{n}\|\leq\frac{1}{n}

Then, from the continuity of
J:X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
J^{\prime}:X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow(X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{-1}
,
P:X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

J(u_{n})\to c,\quad J^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0,\quad P(u_{n})\to 0.

∎

4.
Profile decomposition of Pohožaev-Palais-Smale sequences

In this section, we study the profile decomposition of bounded Pohožaev-Palais-Smale sequences. We begin with several lemmas that will be useful in proving the forthcoming decomposition result.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
(y_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfy
|y_{n}|\to\infty
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle u,w(\cdot-y_{n})\rangle_{\ell}=0.

Proof.

Recall that
w(\cdot-y_{n})\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Indeed, fix
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and note that

\displaystyle\quad\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla w(\cdot-y_{n})\nabla\varphi+w(\cdot-y_{n})\varphi\,dx\right|=\left|\int_{-y_{n}+\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi}\nabla w\nabla\varphi((\cdot+y_{n}))+w\varphi(\cdot+y_{n})\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\left(\int_{-y_{n}+\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi}|\nabla w|^{2}+w^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}}_{\to 0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\varphi^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\to 0.

Now, take any
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and let
\varphi_{k}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the sequence such that
\varphi_{k}\to v
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then

\displaystyle\quad\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla w(\cdot-y_{n})\nabla v+w(\cdot-y_{n})v\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla w(\cdot-y_{n})\nabla(v-\varphi_{k})+w(\cdot-y_{n})(v-\varphi_{k})\,dx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla w(\cdot-y_{n})\nabla\varphi_{k}+w(\cdot-y_{n})\varphi_{k}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|v-\varphi_{k}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+o_{n}(1).

Now, taking
k\to\infty
we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla w(\cdot-y_{n})\nabla v+w(\cdot-y_{n})v\,dx\to 0.

Thus, indeed
w(\cdot-y_{n})\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then, since the embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is continuous, thanks to
[
4
, Theorem 3.10]
, we get
w(\cdot-y_{n})\rightharpoonup 0
in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence
\langle u,w(\cdot-y_{n})\rangle_{\ell}\to 0
and the proof is completed.
∎

We will apply the following lemma several times.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

If
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
(y_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfy
|y_{n}|\to\infty
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(\cdot-y_{n})|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx=0.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
(y_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfy
|y_{n}|\to\infty
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u(\cdot-y_{n})\|=\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Moreover, as a simple consequence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u(\cdot-y_{n})\|_{\ell}^{2}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+\ell\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot-y_{n})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u(\cdot-y_{n})|^{2}\,dx-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(\cdot-y_{n})|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(\cdot-y_{n})|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(\cdot-y_{n})|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx.

Moreover, from Lemma
4.2
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(\cdot-y_{n})|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\to 0

and the proof is completed.
∎

From Lions’ concentration-compactness principle, we easily obtain the following corollary, which can be viewed as a variant of the concentration–compactness principle in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(u_{n})\subset X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a bounded sequence with

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B(z,r)}|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx=0

for some
r>0
. Then
u_{n}\to 0
in
L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
t\in(2,2^{*})
.

Proof.

Fix
t\in(2,2^{*})
. Then there is
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
t<\frac{2N}{N-2(1-\varepsilon)}
. Since
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset H^{1-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we know that
u_{n}\to 0
in
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
2<s<\frac{2N}{N-2(1-\varepsilon)}
. Therefore,
u_{n}\to 0
in
L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
∎

In the profile decomposition presented below, we construct weak solutions to the
limiting problem

-\Delta u=g(u).

Since we work in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, while the natural energy space for this equation is
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we first need a few regularity results.

We recall the following fact, which is a consequence of Weyl’s lemma.

Theorem 4.5

(
[
7
, Lemma 3]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set and
h\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
. Suppose that
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfies

-\Delta u=h\quad\mbox{in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega).

Then
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.

Applying this theorem for
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we can get the following result in the setting that we are interested in.

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose that
g
satisfies (G1)–(G3). Suppose that
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
s\in(0,1)
is chosen so that
p<\frac{2N}{N-2s}
, satisfies

-\Delta u=g(u)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Then
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u
is a weak solution to
-\Delta u=g(u)
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

Step 1.

The distributional solution
u
belongs to
H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Let
u
be as in the statement. Since

|g(u)|\lesssim|u|+|u|^{p-1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})+L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

by Theorem
4.5
we know that
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Step 2.

The solution
u
lies globally in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Now, we will show that
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. First, we will show the following variant of the Caccioppoli inequality (cf.
[
11
, Chapter 4]
,
[
14
]
)

\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\lesssim\int_{B(0,2R)}|g(u)u|\,dx+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}u^{2}\,dx.

Take
R>0
and
\eta_{R}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\eta_{R}\equiv 1\mbox{ on }B(0,R),\quad\eta_{R}\equiv 0\mbox{ outside of }B(0,2R)

and

0\leq\eta_{R}\leq 1,\quad|\nabla\eta_{R}|\leq\frac{c}{R}

for some
c\geq 1
. Then
\eta_{R}^{2}u\in H^{1}_{0}(B(0,2R))
. Take a sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,2R))
such that
\varphi_{n}\to\eta_{R}^{2}u
in
H^{1}_{0}(B(0,2R))
. Testing the equation with
\varphi_{n}
we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\nabla\varphi_{n}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(u)\varphi_{n}\,dx.

Since
\varphi_{n}\to\eta_{R}^{2}u
in the strong topology of
H^{1}_{0}(B(0,2R))
, passing to the limit, we easily get that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\nabla(\eta_{R}^{2}u)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(u)\eta_{R}^{2}u\,dx.

It can be rewritten as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta_{R}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(u)\eta_{R}^{2}u\,dx.

Now, using Young’s inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\left|2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta_{R}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\eta_{R}|\nabla u|)(2|\nabla\eta_{R}||u|)\,dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\eta_{R}|^{2}|u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{2c^{2}}{R^{2}}\int_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}u^{2}\,dx.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|g(u)u|\eta_{R}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{2c^{2}}{R^{2}}\int_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}u^{2}\,dx

or equivalently

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{B(0,2R)}|g(u)u|\eta_{R}^{2}\,dx+\frac{2c^{2}}{R^{2}}\int_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}u^{2}\,dx.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\lesssim\int_{B(0,2R)}|g(u)u|\eta_{R}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}u^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(0,2R)}|g(u)u|\,dx+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}u^{2}\,dx.

Hence, we obtained the following variant of the Caccioppoli inequality

(4.1)

\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\lesssim\int_{B(0,2R)}|g(u)u|\,dx+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}u^{2}\,dx.

Recall that

g(u)\lesssim|u|+|u|^{p-1}.

Thus, from the Caccioppoli inequality (
4.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B(0,2R)}|g(u)u|\,dx+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}u^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}

Now, taking

.
∎

We introduce the energy and Pohožaev functionals for the limiting equation. We define
{J_{\infty}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}}
and
P_{\infty}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle J_{\infty}(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\ell}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)\,dx,

\displaystyle P_{\infty}(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{2^{*}\ell}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,dx-2^{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)\,dx.

Remark 4.7

It is well known that every critical point

of

satisfies
P_{\infty}(w)=0
.

Put

&-2\left(\langle u_{n},w^{2}(\cdot-y_{n}^{2})\rangle_{\ell}+\langle u_{0},w^{2}(\cdot-y_{n}^{2})\rangle_{\ell}\right)-2\langle w^{1}(\cdot-y_{n}^{1}),w^{2}(\cdot-y_{n}^{2})\rangle_{\ell}\\

\begin{split}\|v_{n}^{3}\|^{2}_{\ell}&=\|v_{n}^{2}\|^{2}_{\ell}-\left(\|\nabla w^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+\ell\|w^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\right)+o(1)\\

Finally we can write

Step 4.
We can repeat the above procedure to construct, for some

•

,

.

Note that, from (F1)–(F3), for every

such that

\displaystyle+\ell\|w^{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(w^{j})w^{j}\,dx\leq\varepsilon\|w^{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|w^{j}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}

5.
Existence of a solution

We will start by showing that the Pohožaev-Palais-Smale sequences are bounded.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
c>0
and
(u_{n})\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a sequence such that
J(u_{n})\to c
,
J^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
and
P(u_{n})\to 0
. Then
(u_{n})
is bounded in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

Since
J(u_{n})\to c
, and using
P(u_{n})\to 0
, we get

\frac{1}{2}\xi(u_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u_{n})\,dx+c+o(1)=\frac{1}{2^{*}}\xi(u_{n})+c+o(1)

and therefore

\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\right)\xi(u_{n})=c+o(1).

In particular,
(\xi(u_{n}))
is bounded. Now, what remains is to show that
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
is bounded. For this purpose, using (G5), we consider

J(u_{n})-\frac{1}{\nu}J^{\prime}(u_{n})(u_{n})=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\nu}\right)\xi(u_{n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(G(u_{n})-\frac{1}{\nu}g(u_{n})u_{n}\right)\,dx\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\nu}\right)\xi(u_{n})-\gamma_{0}\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence

(5.1)

\displaystyle-\gamma_{0}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leq J(u_{n})-\frac{1}{\nu}J^{\prime}(u_{n})(u_{n})-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\nu}\right)\xi(u_{n})\leq C-\frac{1}{\nu}J^{\prime}(u_{n})(u_{n})

for some
C>0
. Since
J^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
, we get that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\nu}J^{\prime}(u_{n})(u_{n})\right|\leq\delta_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{\ell}=\delta_{n}\left(\xi(u_{n})+\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{n}\left(C+\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{n}\left(C^{1/2}+\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right).

for some
C>0
and
\delta_{n}\to 0
. Finally, combining it with (
5.1
) we find that

-\gamma_{0}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leq C+C\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

and
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
is bounded, since
\gamma_{0}<0
.
∎

6.1.

Recall the Pohožaev constraint

Proof of Theorem
1.2
(b).

Since

, clearly

. Hence it is enough to show that

. For that purpose, take any

and consider the rescaled function
U(\theta):=u(\cdot/\theta)
for

, where we set

The last inequality holds, because there is no

, and clearly
\frac{d}{d\theta}\Psi(\theta)>0
for
\theta\in(0,1)

\frac{d}{d\theta}\Psi(\theta)<0

.

Hence, the following properties are satisfied by

\gamma:=U(\vartheta\cdot)

belongs to

, where

is given by (

6.3.
There is a radial solution, which is a minimizer on
{\mathcal{M}}

For a nonnegative, measurable function
u
, let

denote its radially decreasing rearrangement (also known as the Schwarz symmetrization). The well-known Pólya-Szegő inequality states that if
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u^{*}|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx.

Moreover, the following properties hold

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u^{*}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u^{*})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,dx.

Observe that these inequalities imply that

\xi(u^{*})\leq\xi(u),\quad J(u^{*})\leq J(u)

(Pólya-Szegő inequality in

)
.

For any

,
u\geq 0
, there holds

Take any

,

and take a sequence

,

such that

in

. Consider

Since

in

is bounded in

we see that the following concentration-compactness principle holds in the subspace
H^{s}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of
{\mathcal{O}}
-invariant functions in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Corollary 7.1
.

Suppose that
(u_{n})\subset H^{s}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is bounded and for all
r>0

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N-2M}}\int_{B((0,0,z),r)}|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx=0.

Then
u_{n}\to 0
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any
p\in\left(2,\frac{2N}{N-2s}\right)
.

Hence, with the same reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
4.4
, we obtain the following concentration-compactness principle in
X^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Corollary 7.2
.

Suppose that
(u_{n})\subset X^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is bounded and for all
r>0

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N-2M}}\int_{B((0,0,z),r)}|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx=0.

Then
u_{n}\to 0
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any
p\in\left(2,2^{*}\right)
.

Now, we can repeat the proof of Theorem
4.8
, using Corollary
7.2
instead of Lemma
4.4
, and obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 7.3
.

Suppose that
(u_{n})\subset X_{\tau}\cap H^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a bounded in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, Pohožaev-Palais-Smale sequence for
J
at level
c>0
. Then, passing to a subsequence, there are an integer
k\geq 0
,
u_{0}\in X_{\tau}\cap X^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
u_{0}\geq 0
, and sequences
(y_{n}^{j})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-2M}
,
w^{j}\in X_{\tau}\cap H^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that

(a)

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
,
J^{\prime}(u_{0})=0
and
P(u_{0})=0
;

(b)

|y_{n}^{j}|\to\infty
and
|y_{n}^{j}-y_{n}^{j^{\prime}}|\to\infty
for
j\neq j^{\prime}
;

(c)

w^{j}\neq 0
and
w^{j}
is a weak solution to

-\Delta w_{j}=g(w_{j})\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}

and is a critical point of
J_{\infty}
for each
1\leq j\leq k
;

(d)

u_{n}-u_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}w^{j}(\cdot-(0,0,y_{n}^{j}))\to 0
in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
;

(e)

\|u_{n}\|^{2}_{\ell}\to\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{\ell}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\|\nabla w^{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+\ell\|w^{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\right)
;

(f)

J(u_{n})\to J(u_{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}J_{\infty}(w^{j})
.

The slight difference in the proof is that, we know
w^{j}
are weak solutions to the limiting problem in
X_{\tau}\cap H^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, so a critical point of
J_{\infty}|_{X_{\tau}\cap H^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}}
. However, from Palais’ principle of symmetric criticality, it is a critical point of the free functional
J_{\infty}
. Similarly we know that
u_{0}
is a critical point of
J
.

In the same way as in Lemma
3.1
, there is
\rho>0
such that

\inf_{\|u\|_{\ell}=\rho}J(u)>0.

To show that
J
has a mountain pass geometry, in Lemma
3.2
it is shown how to, for any
R>0
, construct a radial function
w_{R}\in H^{1}(B(0,R+1))\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(w_{R})\,dx\geq C_{1}R^{N}-C_{2}R^{N-1},\quad C_{1},C_{2}>0,

and

\|w_{R}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\gtrsim R^{N}.

Let
R>1
. Now, as in
[
19
, Remark 4.2]
, we choose an odd and smooth function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[-1,1]
such that
\varphi(x)=1
for
x\geq 1
, and
\varphi(x)=-1
for
x\leq-1
. Then, as shown in
[
19
, Remark 4.2]
, the function (with a small abuse of notation
w_{R}(x)=w_{R}(|x|)
)

\widetilde{w}_{R}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):=w_{R}(\sqrt{|x_{1}|^{2}+|x_{2}|^{2}+|x_{3}|^{2}})\varphi(|x_{1}|-|x_{2}|)

belongs to
X_{\tau}\cap H^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(\widetilde{w}_{R})\,dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(w_{R})\,dx-C_{3}\sum_{i=N-M}^{N-1}R^{i}\geq C_{1}R^{N}-C_{2}\sum_{i=N-M}^{N-1}R^{i}.

We compute

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\widetilde{w}_{R}|^{2}\,dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{R}|^{2}|\varphi(|x_{1}|-|x_{2}|)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R+1}\int_{0}^{R+1}\int_{0}^{R+1}|w_{R}(r)|^{2}|\varphi(r_{1}-r_{2})|^{2}r_{1}^{M-1}r_{2}^{M-1}r_{3}^{N-2M-1}\,dr_{1}\,dr_{2}\,dr_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{R+1}\int_{0}^{R+1}\int_{r_{2}}^{R+1}|w_{R}(r)|^{2}|\varphi(r_{1}-r_{2})|^{2}r_{1}^{M-1}r_{2}^{M-1}r_{3}^{N-2M-1}\,dr_{1}\,dr_{2}\,dr_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{R}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{R+1}\int_{0}^{R+1}\int_{r_{2}}^{r_{2}+1}\underbrace{|w_{R}(r)|^{2}(1-|\varphi(r_{1}-r_{2})|^{2})}_{\leq\zeta_{0}^{2}}r_{1}^{M-1}r_{2}^{M-1}r_{3}^{N-2M-1}\,dr_{1}\,dr_{2}\,dr_{3}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{R}|^{2}\,dx-2\zeta_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{R+1}\int_{0}^{R+1}\int_{r_{2}}^{r_{2}+1}r_{1}^{M-1}r_{2}^{M-1}r_{3}^{N-2M-1}\,dr_{1}\,dr_{2}\,dr_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{R}|^{2}\,dx-\sum_{i=N-M}^{N-1}\beta_{i}R^{i}

for some
\beta_{i}>0
. Hence, for sufficiently large
R>1
we have

J(\widetilde{w}_{R})<0,\quad\|\widetilde{w}_{R}\|_{\ell}^{2}>\rho.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

From the above reasoning,
J
has the mountain pass geometry, and repeating the same arguments as in Lemma
3.5
we find a Pohožaev-Palais-Smale sequence for
J
on the mountain pass level. Then, using Theorem
7.3
repeating the proof of Theorem
1.2
(a), we find a nontrivial critical point of
J|_{X_{\tau}\cap X^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, which - thanks to Palais’ principle of symmetric criticality - is also a critical point of
J
. Moreover the solution belongs to
{\mathcal{M}}
, and - as in Theorem
1.2
(b) - it is a minimizer of
J
on
X_{\tau}\cap H^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap{\mathcal{M}}
.

It remains to show

\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}\cap X_{\tau}\cap X^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}J\geq 2\inf_{\mathcal{M}}J.

Let
u\in{\mathcal{M}}\cap X_{\tau}\cap X^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the minimizer found above. Define two open sets

\Omega_{1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\ :\ |x_{1}|>|x_{2}|\},\quad\Omega_{2}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\ :\ |x_{1}|<|x_{2}|\}.

Define
u_{1}:=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{1}}u
,
u_{2}:=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{2}}u
. Then
u_{1},u_{2}\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, since
u=0
on
\{|x_{1}|=|x_{2}|\}
, and
\xi(u)=2\xi(u_{1})=2\xi(u_{2})
. We also have
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,dx=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u_{1})\,dx
. Hence
u_{1}\in{\mathcal{M}}
,
J(u_{1})=J(u_{2})
and since
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
have disjoint supports, we get

J(u)=J(u_{1})+J(u_{2})=2J(u_{1})\geq 2\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}}J.

∎

The p p - θ \theta relation in mating of trees
Morris Ang Xin Sun Pu Yu

Abstract
In the mating-of-trees approach to Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE) and Liouville quantum gravity (LQG), it is natural to consider two pairs of correlated Brownian motions coupled together. This arises in the scaling limit of bipolar-orientation-decorated planar maps (Gwynne-Holden-Sun, 2016) and in the related skew Brownian permuton studied by Borga et al. There are two parameters that can be used to index the coupling between the two pairs of Brownian motions, denoted as p p and θ \theta in the literature: p p describes the Brownian motions, whereas θ \theta describes the SLE curves on LQG surfaces. In this paper, we derive an exact relation between the two parameters and demonstrate its application to computing statistics of the skew Brownian permuton. Our derivation relies on the synergy between mating-of-trees and Liouville conformal field theory (LCFT), where the boundary three-point function in LCFT provide the exact solvable inputs.



1
Introduction

Liouville quantum gravity (LQG) coupled with Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE) is a central topic in random conformal geometry. The mating-of-trees approach
[
DMS21
,
GHS23
]
provides a powerful framework to study this coupling and its relation to decorated random planar maps. One important class of models are the so-called bipolar-orientation decorated maps introduced in
[
KMSW19
]
and further studied in
[
GHS16
]
. A closely related model called Schynder-wood-decorated triangulations was then studied in
[
LSW24
]
. Compared with previously studied planar map models, a novel feature of these models is the presence of multiple pairs of random trees instead of a single pair. They gained much interest in recent years due to their connection to the scaling limit of a natural class of random permutations, namely the Baxter permutation and its relatives
[
BM22
,
Bor23
,
BHSY23
]
.

In this paper, we solve a basic question in this line of research, which is the exact relation between the
p
and
\theta
parameters as introduced in
[
GHS16
]
. Here
\theta
is the angle of an imaginary geometry flow line relative to a space-filling SLE curve, and
p
is the proportion of time that the space-filling SLE curve is to the left of the flow line. Each parameter can be used to index the coupling between two pairs of trees and they have a 1-to-1 correspondence. We show
(in Theorem
1.1
) that they are related by:

\frac{p}{1-p}=\frac{\sin((\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{8})(\pi-2\theta))}{\sin((\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{8})(\pi+2\theta))}

(1.1)

where
\gamma\in(0,2)
is the LQG parameter. See Section
1.1
for the precise definitions of
p
and
\theta
, and see Figure
1
for the setup. Previously the relation (
1.1
) was only known when
\theta=0
and
p=\frac{1}{2}
by symmetry, and when
\theta=\frac{\pi}{6}
and
p=\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{2}}
and
\gamma=1
by the relation to Schnyder-wood-decorated triangulation
[
LSW24
]
. There is another natural quantity relating the local time of a Brownian motion to the LQG length measure, which we determine in Theorem
1.2
.

Besides its intrinsic interest, the exact relation (
1.1
) between
p
and
\theta
has important implications in the study of the aforementioned discrete models. The key to the mating-of-trees framework is to encode the LQG/SLE coupling via Brownian motions. This reduces scaling limit questions to the convergence of random walk to Brownian motion. The parameter
p
describes by
the Brownian motions, hence can be used to parametrize the limiting object. For example, the family of skew Brownian permutons
[
Bor23
]
, as a generalization of the Baxter permuton (i.e. the scaling limit of uniformly random Baxter permutations), are parametrized by
p
. On the other hand, the
\theta
parameter is more natural in the original LQG/SLE setting. Hence knowing the relation (
1.1
) allows one to analyze the continuum object via LQG/SLE tools beyond mating-of-trees. In particular, the relation (
1.1
) yields the explicit involution rate and density function for the skew Brownian permuton (Theorem
1.4
). Previously, this was only known for the Baxter permuton
[
BHSY23
]
and the few
cases for which (
1.1
) is known. See Section
1.2
for details.

Our derivation of (
1.1
) goes
beyond the mating-of-trees framework. It relies on the exact input coming from the integrability of Liouville conformal field theory (LCFT), which describes the variants of Gaussian free field naturally appeared in the mating-of-trees framework.
In particular, we use the boundary three-point structure constant for LCFT studied in
[
ARSZ23
]
.
We give an overview of our proof in Section
1.3
.
The earlier derivation of another basic mating-of-trees quantity, the variance of the Brownian motion
[
ARS25
]
, also relied on the synergy with LCFT.
See, e.g.,
[
AHS24
,
NQSZ23
,
ASYZ25
,
LSYZ24
]
for other exact results in random conformal geometry obtained by combining the mating-of-trees framework and LCFT.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
1.1
we state our main result. In Section
1.2
, we explain applications of our results to the skew Brownian permuton. The proof strategy is explained in Section
1.3
. In Section
2
we provide preliminaries on SLE and LQG. In Section
3
, we prove a conformal welding result, based on which we derive the exact formula (
1.1
) in Section
4
.

1.1
Main Result

We briefly recall the mating-of-trees framework in
[
DMS21
]
. Let
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
\kappa=\gamma^{2}
. Let
(\mathbb{C},h,0,\infty)
be a
\gamma
-quantum cone,
\widehat{h}
a whole plane Gaussian free field (GFF) independent from
h
, and
\eta^{\prime}
the north-going space-filling counterflowline of
\widehat{h}
from
\infty
to
\infty
(so
\eta^{\prime}
is a whole-plane
\mathrm{SLE}_{16/\kappa}
curve). See Sections
2.1
and
2.4
for the precise definitions of these objects. We parameterize
\eta^{\prime}
such that
\eta^{\prime}(0)=0
and for
-\infty<s<t<\infty
,
t-s
is the
\gamma
-LQG area of
\eta^{\prime}([s,t])
induced by
h
. Then one can define a process
(L_{t},R_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
tracing the change of the LQG-lengths of the left and right boundaries of
\eta^{\prime}((-\infty,t])
. The process
(L_{t},R_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
evolves as a correlated 2D Brownian motion, and determines the pair
(h,\eta^{\prime})
up to an affine transform.

An illustration of the mating-of-trees setup. Here
p_{\gamma}(\theta)
is the same as the probability where
\eta^{\prime}(t)
is on the left of the flow line
\eta_{\theta}
, and the local time of
|X|
at 0 is
2c_{\gamma}(\theta)
times the quantum length of
\eta^{\prime}([0,t])\cap\eta_{\theta}
.

Now let
\theta\in[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
and let
\eta_{\theta}
be the
\theta
-angle flow line of
\widehat{h}
started from 0 as defined through imaginary geometry
[
MS17
]
. See Figure
1
for the setup. Using the scale invariance of the quantum cone
[
DMS21
, Proposition 4.13]
, the probability that
\eta^{\prime}(t)
lies on the left hand side of
\eta_{\theta}
does not depend on
t
. We denote this probability by
p_{\gamma}(\theta)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
\theta\in[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
, and let
p_{\gamma}(\theta)
be the constant defined above. Then

\frac{p_{\gamma}(\theta)}{1-p_{\gamma}(\theta)}=\frac{\sin((\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{8})(\pi-2\theta))}{\sin((\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{8})(\pi+2\theta))}.

(1.2)

As shown in
[
GHS16
, Proposition 3.2]
, one can define a continuous process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, which evolves as
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
whenever
\eta^{\prime}
is on the right hand side of
\eta_{\theta}
, and evolves as
(R_{t})_{t\geq 0}
whenever
\eta^{\prime}
is on the left hand side of
\eta_{\theta}
. This process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has the law of skew Brownian motion with parameter
p=p_{\gamma}(\theta)
. Moreover, as shown in
[
GHS16
, Proposition 3.3]
, there exists a constant
c=c_{\gamma}(\theta)
such that the local time of
|X|
at 0 is equal to
2c_{\gamma}(\theta)
times the quantum length of
\eta^{\prime}([0,t])\cap\eta_{\theta}
. See Section
4.1
for a concrete description of the setup.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
\theta\in[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
, and let
c_{\gamma}(\theta)
be the constant introduced above. Then

c_{\gamma}(\theta)=\frac{\cos((1-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4})\theta)}{\sin(\frac{\pi\gamma^{2}}{8})}.

(1.3)

It was shown in
[
GHS16
, Corollary 5.6]

that
c_{\gamma}(0)=2
for
\gamma=\sqrt{4/3}
. In Section
4
, we derive (
1.3
) for
(\theta,\gamma)\neq(0,\sqrt{4/3})
.

1.2
Applications to skew Brownian permuton and related models

A permuton is a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
[0,1]^{2}
whose marginals are both uniform, i.e., for any
0\leq a<b\leq 1
we have
\mu([a,b]\times[0,1])=\mu([0,1]\times[a,b])=b-a
. Any permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,2,...,n\}
can be viewed as a permuton by setting
\mu
as
n
times the Lebesgue measure on
\{[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}]\times[\frac{\sigma(i)-1}{n},\frac{\sigma(i)}{n}]:i=1,...,n\}
.

Permuton limits have been investigated for various models of random permutations. For many models, the permuton limits are deterministic, for instance, Erdös-Szekeres permutations
[
Rom06
]
, Mallows permutations
[
Sta09
]
, random sorting networks
[
Dau22
]
, almost square permutations
[
BS20
,
BDS21
]
, and permutations sorted with the
runsort
algorithm
[
ADK22
]
. For random permutations which have a scaling limit, the limiting permutons appear to be random in many cases. In
[
Bor23
]
, Borga introduced a two-parameter family of permutons, called the
skew Brownian permuton
, which covers many of the known examples.

The skew Brownian permuton
\mu_{\rho,q}
is indexed by parameters
\rho\in(-1,1]
and
q\in[0,1]
, and
\mu_{-1/2,1/2}
coincides with the Baxter permuton
[
BM22
]
. We now recall the construction of the skew Brownian permuton for
\rho\in(-1,1)
and
q\in[0,1]
. For
\rho\in(-1,1)
, let
(W_{\rho}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}=(X_{\rho}(t),Y_{\rho}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
be
a
two-dimensional Brownian motion of correlation

\rho
. This is a continuous two-dimensional Gaussian process such that the components
X_{\rho}
and
Y_{\rho}
are standard one-dimensional Brownian motions, and
\mathrm{Cov}(X_{\rho}(t),Y_{\rho}(s))=\rho\cdot\min\{t,s\}
. Let
(E_{\rho}(t))_{t\in[0,1]}
be a
two-dimensional Brownian loop of correlation

\rho
. Namely, it is a two-dimensional Brownian motion of correlation
\rho
conditioned to stay in the non-negative quadrant
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
and to end at the origin at time 1, i.e.
E_{\rho}(1)=(0,0)
.
For
q\in[0,1]
, consider the solutions of the following family of stochastic differential equations (SDEs) indexed by
u\in[0,1]
and driven by
E_{\rho}=(X_{\rho},Y_{\rho})
:

\begin{cases}dZ_{\rho,q}^{(u)}(t)=\mathds{1}_{\{Z_{\rho,q}^{(u)}(t)\geq 0\}}dY_{\rho}(t)-\mathds{1}_{\{Z_{\rho,q}^{(u)}(t)<0\}}dX_{\rho}(t)+(2q-1)\cdot dL^{Z_{\rho,q}^{(u)}}(t),&t\in(u,1),\\
Z_{\rho,q}^{(u)}(t)=0,&t\in[0,u],\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
L^{Z_{\rho,q}^{(u)}}(t)
is the symmetric local-time process at zero of
Z_{\rho,q}^{(u)}
, i.e.,

L^{Z^{(u)}_{\rho,q}}(t)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\mathds{1}_{\left\{Z^{(u)}_{\rho,q}(s)\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\right\}}ds.

More precisely, we say
\left\{Z^{(u)}_{\rho,q}\right\}_{u\in[0,1]}
is a solution to (
1.4
) if, for a.e.
\omega
in the probability space, for a.e.
u\in[0,1]
the process
Z^{(u)}_{\rho,q}(\omega)
is a solution to (
1.4
).
The solutions to the SDEs (
1.4
) exist and are unique thanks to
[
Bor23
, Theorem 1.7]
; for further details see the discussion below
[
Bor23
, Theorem 1.7]
.
The collection of stochastic processes
\left\{Z^{(u)}_{\rho,q}\right\}_{u\in[0,1]}
is called the
continuous coalescent-walk process
driven by
(E_{\rho},q)
. Let

\varphi_{Z_{\rho,q}}(t)=\mathrm{Leb}\left(\big\{x\in[0,t)\,|\,Z_{\rho,q}^{(x)}(t)<0\big\}\cup\big\{x\in[t,1]\,|\,Z_{\rho,q}^{(t)}(x)\geq 0\big\}\right),\quad t\in[0,1].

Definition 1.3
.

Fix
\rho\in(-1,1)
and
q\in[0,1]
. The
skew Brownian permuton
of parameters
\rho,q
, denoted
\mu_{\rho,q}
, is the push-forward of the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
via the mapping
(\mathbb{I},\varphi_{Z_{\rho,q}})
. That is,

\mu_{\rho,q}(\cdot)=(\mathbb{I},\varphi_{Z_{\rho,q}})_{*}\mathrm{Leb}(\cdot)=\mathrm{Leb}\left(\{t\in[0,1]\,|\,(t,\varphi_{Z_{\rho,q}}(t))\in\cdot\,\}\right).

An SLE/LQG description for the skew Brownian permuton
\mu_{\rho,q}
is given in
[
Bor23
, Theorem 1.17]
. Let
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
\theta\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})
be chosen such that
\rho=-\cos(\frac{\pi\gamma^{2}}{4})
and
q=p_{\gamma}(\theta)
where
p_{\gamma}(\theta)
is the function in Theorem
1.1
. Consider a unit area
\gamma
-LQG sphere
(\mathbb{C},h,0,\infty)
and an independent whole plane GFF
\widehat{h}
, and let
\eta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}_{\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}}
be space-filling counterflowlines of
\widehat{h}
of angle 0 and
\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}
, both parameterized by the
\gamma
-LQG area induced by
h
. Then, roughly speaking,
\mu_{\rho,q}
describes the order of the points
\{\eta^{\prime}(t):t\in[0,1]\}
that are hit by
\eta^{\prime}_{\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}}
.

For a permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,...,n\}
, we define its inversion rate by

\widetilde{\mathrm{occ}}(21,\sigma)=\frac{\#\{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq n:\sigma(i_{2})>\sigma(i_{1})\}}{\tbinom{n}{2}}.

For a permuton
\mu
, its inversion rate is defined by

\widetilde{\mathrm{occ}}(21,\mu)=2\iint_{[0,1]^{2}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{x_{1}<x_{2};\,y_{1}>y_{2}\right\}}\mu(dx_{1}dy_{1})\mu(dx_{2}dy_{2}).

(1.5)

It is shown in
[
BBF+20
, Theorem 2.5]
that if
(\sigma_{n})_{n}
is a sequence of random permutations converging in distribution in the permuton sense to a limiting random permuton
\mu
, i.e.
\mu_{\sigma_{n}}\xrightarrow{d}\mu
, then

\widetilde{\mathrm{occ}}(21,\sigma_{n})
converges in distribution to
\widetilde{\mathrm{occ}}(21,\mu)
. In
[
BHSY23
, Proposition 1.14]
, we expressed the expected inversion rate
\widetilde{\mathrm{occ}}(21,\mu_{\rho,q})
of the skew Brownian permuton in terms of the angle
\theta
based on the SLE/LQG description of the skew Brownian permuton. Combined with Theorem
1.1
, we have

Theorem 1.4
.

For all
(\rho,q)\in(-1,1)\times[0,1]
, let
\theta\in[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
be related to
q
by the relation
q=p_{\gamma}(\theta)
given in Theorem
1.1
with
\gamma\in(0,2)
such that
\rho=-\cos(\pi\gamma^{2}/4)
. Then

\mathbb{E}[(\widetilde{\mathrm{occ}}(21,\mu_{\rho,q}))]=\frac{\pi-2\theta}{2\pi}.

(1.6)

Finally, in
[
BHSY23
, Theorem 3.8]
, the intensity measure
\mathbb{E}[\mu_{\rho,q}]
is expressed in terms of the quantum area of quantum disks with fixed boundary lengths, where the weights of the quantum disks are determined linearly according to the angle
\theta
. On the other hand, in
[
ARSZ23
]
, with Remy and Zhu the first two named authors computed the Laplace transform of the density function for the quantum area of quantum disks (see also (
2.18
)). Therefore the intensity measure
\mathbb{E}[\mu_{\rho,q}]
can in principle be solved by further applying the
q=p_{\gamma}(\theta)
relation as in Theorem
1.1
, but the formula is rather involved in general.

1.3
Proof strategy

As pioneered by Sheffield
[
She16
]
, a cornerstone of random planar geometry is the
conformal welding
of random surfaces, where the interface under the conformal welding of two independent LQG surfaces is an SLE curve. Similar type of results were also proved in
[
DMS21
,
AHS23
,
ASY25
,
AHSY25
,
AG23
]
.

Liouville conformal field theory (LCFT) is a 2D quantum field theory rigorously developed in
[
DKRV16
]
and subsequent works. It has been demonstrated that many natural LQG surfaces can be described by LCFT
[
AHS17
,
Cer21
,
AHS24
,
ASY25
]
. LCFT is in the framework of Belavin, Polyakov, and Zamolodchikov’s conformal field theory
[
BPZ84
]
, has been extensively explored in the physics literature
[
DO94
,
ZZ96
,
PT02
]
and mathematically in
[
KRV20
,
RZ22
,
ARS25
,
GKRV20
,
GKRV21
,
ARSZ23
]
, and enjoys rich and deep exact solvability. Alongside the conformal welding of LQG surfaces mentioned earlier, with various coauthors we derived several exact formulae regarding SLE and their loop variants called conformal loop ensembles
[
AHS24
,
ARS25
,
ACSW21
,
ASYZ25
,
LSYZ24
]
.

Our proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are another application of the theory of imaginary geometry, the conformal welding of random surfaces together with integrability of LCFT.
In Theorem
3.1
, we show that by drawing an independent
\mathrm{SLE}_{\kappa}(W-2;-W,W-2)
curve on top of a quantum wedge of weight
W
, the surface that is cut off from the wedge is a
quantum triangle
, a finite area LQG surface with three marked boundary points we introduced in
[
ASY25
]
. Now by the conformal welding of quantum wedges
[
DMS21
, Theorem 1.2]
, the surfaces
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{L}}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{R}}
formed by the parts of
\eta^{\prime}([0,\infty))
that lies on the left (resp. right) hand side of
\eta_{\theta}
are quantum wedges of weights
W^{\mathrm{L}}
and
W^{\mathrm{R}}
, where
W^{\mathrm{L}}
and
W^{\mathrm{R}}
is explicit in terms of
\theta
. We then sample a point
z
on the flow line
\eta_{\theta}
, let
\sigma_{1}
be the time when
\eta^{\prime}
hits
z
, and consider the left and right boundaries
\eta^{\mathrm{L}}
and
\eta^{\mathrm{R}}
of
\eta^{\prime}((-\infty,\sigma_{1}))
. The theory of imaginary geometry
[
MS16
, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.4]
yields that
\eta^{\mathrm{L}}
and
\eta^{\mathrm{R}}
are
\mathrm{SLE}_{\kappa}(W^{\mathrm{L}}-2;-W^{\mathrm{L}},W^{\mathrm{L}}-2)
and
\mathrm{SLE}_{\kappa}(W^{\mathrm{R}}-2;-W^{\mathrm{R}},W^{\mathrm{R}}-2)
curves in
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{L}}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{R}}
, respectively. Therefore the surfaces
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{L}}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{R}}
formed by the parts of
\eta^{\prime}((0,\sigma_{1}))
that are on the left or right hand side of
\eta_{\theta}
are quantum triangles. On the other hand, using the contour decomposition described in
[
GHS16
, Proposition 3.2]
(see also Proposition
4.1
), we can express the constants
p_{\gamma}(\theta)
and
c_{\gamma}(\theta)
in terms of quantum area of the quantum triangles
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{L}}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{R}}
. Combined with the results on the boundary three-point function for LCFT from
[
ARSZ23
]
as listed in Section
2.3
, we calculate in Section
4.3
the quantum areas of
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{L}}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{R}}
, which eventually leads to the formulae (
1.2
) and (
1.3
).
We point out two new features of our proofs compared to previous work involving SLE and LCFT. One is having quantum wedges and quantum triangles in the same picture; the other is the crucial usage of the boundary three-point function of LCFT with positive bulk cosmological constant as derived in
[
ARSZ23
]
.

2
Preliminaries

In Section
2.1
, we start with the definition of the Gaussian free field (GFF) and review the definition of quantum surfaces. In Section
2.2
, we review the basic settings of Liouville conformal field theory and quantum triangles. In Section
2.3
, we review the LCFT description of quantum disks in
[
AHS24
, Section 2]
and provide the counterpart for quantum wedges. In Section
2.4
, we recall the basics of SLE and imaginary geometry flow lines.

In this paper we work with non-probability measures and extend the terminology of ordinary probability to this setting. For a finite or
\sigma
-finite measure space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},M)
, we say
X
is a random variable if
X
is an
\mathcal{F}
-measurable function with its
law
defined via the push-forward measure
M_{X}=X_{*}M
. In this case, we say
X
is
sampled
from
M_{X}
and write
M_{X}[f]
for
\int f(x)M_{X}(dx)
.
Weighting
the law of
X
by
f(X)
corresponds to working with the measure
d\tilde{M}_{X}
with Radon-Nikodym derivative
\frac{d\tilde{M}_{X}}{dM_{X}}=f
, and
conditioning
on some event
E\in\mathcal{F}
(with
0<M[E]<\infty
) refers to the probability measure
\frac{M[E\cap\cdot]}{M[E]}
over the space
(E,\mathcal{F}_{E})
with
\mathcal{F}_{E}=\{A\cap E:A\in\mathcal{F}\}
. If
M
is finite, we write
|M|=M(\Omega)
and
M^{\#}=\frac{M}{|M|}
for its normalization.

2.1
The Gaussian free field and quantum surfaces

Fix a domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}
. We consider the
Neumann GFF
(or
free boundary GFF
)
h_{D}
on
D
by starting with the Hilbert space
H(D)
, the closure of
C^{\infty}(D)
(the space of smooth functions with finite Dirichlet energy modulo a global constant) with respect to the inner product
(f,g)_{\nabla}=\int_{D}(\nabla f\cdot\nabla g)dxdy
. Then set

h_{D}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\xi_{n}f_{n}

(2.1)

where
(\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a collection of i.i.d. standard Gaussians and
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is an orthonormal basis of
H(D)
. One can show that the sum (
2.1
) a.s. converges to a random distribution independent of the choice of the basis
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. We emphasize that
H(D)
and (
2.1
) are defined modulo an additive (random) global constant, which could be fixed in various ways. For
D=\mathcal{S}
, the horizontal strip
\mathbb{R}\times(0,\pi)
, we fix the constant by requiring every function in
H(\mathcal{S})
has mean value zero on
\{0\}\times[0,\pi]
, and we denote the corresponding laws of
h_{D}
by
P_{\mathcal{S}}
. See
[
DMS21
, Section 4.1.4]
for more details.

For
h_{\mathcal{S}}
, the covariance kernel
G_{D}(z,w):=\mathbb{E}[h_{D}(z)h_{D}(w)]
is given by

\begin{split}G_{\mathcal{S}}(z,w)=-\log|e^{z}-e^{w}|-\log|e^{z}-e^{\bar{w}}|+2\max\{\text{Re}\,z,0\}+2\max\{\text{Re}\,w,0\}.\end{split}

(2.2)

We will also need the radial-lateral decomposition of
h_{\mathcal{S}}
. Consider the subspace
H_{1}(\mathcal{S})\subset H(\mathcal{S})
(resp.
H_{2}(\mathcal{S})\subset H(\mathcal{S})
) of functions which are constant (resp. have mean zero) on
[t,t+i\pi]:=\{t\}\times(0,\pi)
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then we have the orthogonal decomposition
H(\mathcal{S})=H_{1}(\mathcal{S})\oplus H_{2}(\mathcal{S})
, yielding

h_{\mathcal{S}}=h_{\mathcal{S}}^{1}+h_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}

(2.3)

where
h_{\mathcal{S}}^{1}
and
h_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
are independent.
Moreover, writing
h_{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(t)
for the common value of
h_{\mathcal{S}}^{1}
on
[t,t+i\pi]
, the process
\{h_{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
agrees in law with
\{B_{2t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
where
\{B_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is the standard two-sided Brownian motion.
See
[
DMS21
, Section 4.1.6]
for more details.

Now we turn to Liouville quantum gravity and quantum surfaces. Throughout this paper, we fix the LQG coupling constant
\gamma\in(0,2)
and set

Q=\frac{2}{\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{2},\qquad\qquad\kappa=\gamma^{2}.

For two tuples
(D,h,z_{1},...,z_{m})
and
(\tilde{D},\tilde{h},\tilde{z}_{1},...,\tilde{z}_{m})
, where
D
and
\tilde{D}
are domains on
\mathbb{C}
with
(z_{1},...,z_{m})
and
(\tilde{z}_{1},...,\tilde{z}_{m})
being
m
marked points on the bulk and the boundary of
D
and
\tilde{D}
, and
h
(resp.
\tilde{h}
) a distribution on
D
(resp.
\tilde{D}
), we say

(D,h,z_{1},...,z_{m})\sim_{\gamma}(\tilde{D},\tilde{h},\tilde{z}_{1},...,\tilde{z}_{m})

(2.4)

if one can find a conformal mapping
\psi:D\to\tilde{D}
such that
\psi(z_{j})=\tilde{z}_{j}
for each
j
and
\tilde{h}=\psi\bullet_{\gamma}h
where

\psi\bullet_{\gamma}h:=h\circ\psi^{-1}+Q\log|(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}|
.
We call an equivalence class
(D,h,z_{1},...,z_{m})/{\sim_{\gamma}}
a
\gamma
-
quantum surface
. We define
curve-decorated
quantum surfaces by considering tuples
(D,h,z_{1},\dots,z_{m},\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n})
where the
\eta_{i}
are curves in
\overline{D}
, and in the definition of
\sim_{\gamma}
we further require that
\psi\circ\eta_{i}
agrees with
\tilde{\eta}_{i}
up to monotone reparametrization.

For a
\gamma
-quantum surface
(D,h,z_{1},...,z_{m})
, its
quantum area measure

\mu_{h}
is defined by the weak limit
\mu_{h}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}e^{\gamma h_{\varepsilon}(z)}d^{2}z
, where
d^{2}z
is the Lebesgue area and
h_{\varepsilon}(z)
is the circle average of
h
over
\partial B(z,\varepsilon)
. When
D=\mathcal{S}
, we can also define the
quantum boundary length measure

\nu_{h}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}h_{\varepsilon}(x)}dx
where
h_{\varepsilon}(x)
is the average of
h
over the semicircle
\{x+\varepsilon e^{i\theta}:\theta\in(0,\pi)\}
. It has been shown in
[
DS11
,
SW16
,
Ber17
]
that all these weak limits are well-defined for the GFF and its variants we are considering in this paper. Moreover, the quantum area and boundary length measures are intrinsic to the quantum surface: if
\psi
is a conformal automorphism of
\mathcal{S}
and
\tilde{h}=\psi\bullet_{\gamma}h
, then
\psi_{*}\mu_{h}=\mu_{\tilde{h}}
and
\psi_{*}\nu_{h}=\nu_{\tilde{h}}
. As such, the definition of
\nu_{h}
can be extended to general simply connected domains
D\subset\mathbb{C}
using
\bullet_{\gamma}
.

We now recall the notions of quantum disk and quantum wedge introduced in
[

. Let
\beta=\gamma+\frac{2-W}{\gamma}<Q
.

2.2
Liouville conformal field theory and quantum triangles

In this section, we review the fundamentals of Liouville CFT, and the definitions of quantum triangles.

We begin with the LCFT on the strip. Recall that
P_{\mathcal{S}}
is the law of the Neumann GFF on
\mathcal{S}
normalized to have mean zero on
\{0\}\times[0,\pi]
. For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we shall use the shorthand

\Delta_{\beta}:=\frac{\beta}{2}(Q-\frac{\beta}{2}).

(2.8)

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(h,\mathbf{c})
be sampled from

is a

on
\mathcal{S}
and let
\textup{LF}_{\mathcal{S}}
be its law.

Definition 2.7

(Liouville field with insertions)
.

Let
(h,\mathbf{c})

\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\in\mathbb{R}

Now we recall the notion of quantum triangle as in

]

W_{1},W_{2},W_{3}>0

Definition 2.8

(Thick quantum triangles)

Fix

\textup{QT}(W_{1},W_{2},W_{3})

(\mathcal{S},\phi,+\infty,-\infty,0)/{\sim_{\gamma}}

Definition 2.9

(Thin quantum triangles)
.

Fix
W_{1},W_{2},W_{3}\in(0,\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})\cup(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2},\infty)

\textup{QT}(W_{1},W_{2},W_{3})

\tilde{W}_{i}=W_{i}

\tilde{W}_{i}=\gamma^{2}-W_{i}

\tilde{a}_{i}

from

at point

be the concatenated quantum surface with three marked points

, where
a_{i}=\tilde{a}_{i}
if

, and
a_{i}
is the second marked point of

We briefly recall the boundary length law of the three-pointed Liouville fields. We start with the
disintegration
of quantum surfaces with respect to boundary length. The quantum wedges have infinite boundary lengths. For quantum disks, one has the following disintegration

{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W;\ell)\ d\ell

where each
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W;\ell)
is a measure supported on the space of two-pointed quantum surfaces with right boundary arc having quantum length
\ell
. Similarly, for quantum triangles, we have the identity

\mathrm{QT}(W_{1},W_{2},W_{3})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{QT}(W_{1},W_{2},W_{3};\ell)\ d\ell

where each
\mathrm{QT}(W_{1},W_{2},W_{3};\ell)
is a measure supported on three-pointed quantum surfaces such that the boundary arc between the weight
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
points has quantum length
\ell

[
AHS23
, Section 2.6]
for more background on disintegration.

To describe the boundary length laws, we will need several special functions. The functions

\log\Gamma_{b}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t}\bigg(\frac{e^{-zt}-e^{-\frac{(b^{2}+1)t}{2b}}}{(1-e^{-bt})(1-e^{-\frac{1}{b}t})}-\frac{(b^{2}+1-2bz)^{2}}{8b^{2}}e^{-t}+\frac{2bz-b^{2}-1}{2bt}\bigg)dt.

\frac{\Gamma_{b}(z)}{\Gamma_{b}(z+b)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Gamma(bz)b^{-bz+\frac{1}{2}},\ \ \frac{\Gamma_{b}(z)}{\Gamma_{b}(z+b^{-1})}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Gamma(b^{-1}z)b^{\frac{z}{b}-\frac{1}{2}}.

Lemma 2.13

H^{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})}_{(0,0,0)}=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}\left(\frac{y}{\mu_{\hat{h}}(\mathcal{S})}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}(\frac{1}{2}\sum\beta_{i}-Q)}\frac{1}{\gamma y}\,dy\right]=\mathbb{E}[\mu_{\hat{h}}(\mathcal{S})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}(Q-\frac{1}{2}\sum\beta_{i})}]\cdot\frac{1}{\gamma}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}(\frac{1}{2}\sum\beta_{i}-Q)\right).

We have
\lim_{s\downarrow 0}s\Gamma(s)=\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\Gamma(s+1)=1
, so it suffices to show
\mathbb{E}[\mu_{\hat{h}}(\mathcal{S})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}(Q-\frac{1}{2}\sum\beta_{i})}]\to 1
.

Let
\beta_{*}=2Q-\beta_{2}-\beta_{3}
and fix
\beta^{*}\in(Q-\beta_{2}-\beta_{3},Q)
, and consider
\beta_{1}\in(\beta_{*},\beta^{*})
.
Let

be defined in the same way as
\hat{h}
but with
\beta_{1}
replaced by
\beta_{*}=2Q-\beta_{2}-\beta_{3}
, and similarly define

. We have
\mu_{h_{*}}(\mathcal{S})<\mu_{\hat{h}}(\mathcal{S})<\mu_{h^{*}}(\mathcal{S})
and
\frac{1}{\gamma}(Q-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\beta_{i})<0
, so

\mathbb{E}[\mu_{h^{*}}(\mathcal{S})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}(Q-\frac{1}{2}\sum\beta_{i})}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\mu_{\hat{h}}(\mathcal{S})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}(Q-\frac{1}{2}\sum\beta_{i})}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\mu_{h_{*}}(\mathcal{S})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}(Q-\frac{1}{2}\sum\beta_{i})}]
. Since
\mathbb{E}[\mu_{h_{*}}(\mathcal{S})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}(Q-\frac{1}{2}\sum\beta_{i})}+1]<\infty

[

, Corollary 6.11]
and
\lim_{\beta_{1}\downarrow\beta_{*}}\frac{1}{\gamma}(Q-\frac{1}{2}\sum\beta_{i})=0
, the dominated convergence theorem implies the upper and lower bounds both converge to 1, hence
\lim_{\beta_{1}\downarrow\beta_{*}}\mathbb{E}[\mu_{\hat{h}}(\mathcal{S})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}(Q-\frac{1}{2}\sum\beta_{i})}]=1
as desired.
∎

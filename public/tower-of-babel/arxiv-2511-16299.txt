Emulation Capacity between Idempotent Channels
Idris Delsol, Omar Fawzi, Li Gao, Mizanur Rahaman

Abstract
We study the optimal rates of emulation (also called interconversion) between quantum channels. When the source and the target channels are idempotent, we give a single-letter expression for the zero-error emulation capacity in terms of structural properties of the range of the two channels. This expression shows that channel emulation is not reversible for general idempotent channels. Furthermore, we establish a strong converse rate that matches with the zero-error emulation capacity when the source or the target channel is either an identity or a completely dephasing channel.



1
Introduction

Given target and source quantum channels
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
(respectively), a central problem in quantum information theory is to understand the optimal interconversion rates
k/n
such that one can use
n
copies of
\mathcal{G}
, combined with an encoder and a decoder channel, to emulate the action of
k
copies of
\mathcal{F}
. In the special case where
\mathcal{F}
is a perfect (noiseless) channel, this corresponds to the problem of channel coding for
\mathcal{G}
, which is central in Shannon theory
[
14
]
. Symmetrically, in the special case where
\mathcal{G}
is the identity channel, this corresponds to the problem of channel simulation for
\mathcal{F}
which is also well-studied within both classical and quantum Shannon theory
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
.

In this article, we study the interconversion problem for more general channels. We start by considering the zero-error regime.
We say that a channel
\mathcal{G}
can emulate another channel
\mathcal{F}
with rate
r
, if there exists an encoding and a decoding channel
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{D}
(respectively) as well as a pair of integers
(k,n)
such that

\mathcal{F}^{\otimes k}=\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}\mathcal{E},

and
r\leq k/n
. Such a rate
r
is called
achievable
. The supremum of achievable rates is called the zero-error emulation capacity of
\mathcal{F}
by
\mathcal{G}
and is denoted
C(\mathcal{G}\mapsto\mathcal{F})
. We note that, unlike most works in the literature on reverse Shannon theory
[
2
]
, the model we consider here does not allow shared randomness or entanglement between the encoder and decoder. The first contribution of this work is a single-letter expression of
C(\mathcal{G}\mapsto\mathcal{F})
when both
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are assumed to be idempotent, i.e. they satisfy
\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}\circ\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}
. Note that for an arbitrary channel
\Phi
, the sequential composition
\Phi^{n}=\Phi\circ\cdots\circ\Phi
has a subsequence that converges as
n\to\infty
to an idempotent channel
[
19
]
. The capacities of sequential composition of channels have been studied in
[
16
,
8
,
15
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}\circ\mathcal{G}
be two idempotent channels with
\lambda(\mathcal{F})\neq(1)
1
1

1

If
\lambda(\mathcal{F})=(1)
, then
\mathcal{F}
is basically a replacer channel (see Proposition
1
) and it is simple to see that we can emulate it with any other idempotent channel
\mathcal{G}
with an arbitrarily large rate.
, then

C(\mathcal{G}\mapsto\mathcal{F})=\inf_{p\in[1,+\infty]}\frac{\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{p})}{\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{p})}.

(1)

In particular, when
\mathcal{F}
or
\mathcal{G}
is either the identity channel or the completely dephasing channel, it suffices to restrict the infimum to
p\in\{1,+\infty\}
:

C(\mathcal{G}\mapsto\mathcal{F})=\min_{p\in\{1,+\infty\}}\frac{\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{p})}{\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{p})}.

(2)

In the right-hand side of eq. (
1
), the two terms
\lambda(\mathcal{F})
and
\lambda(\mathcal{G})
are integer vectors which contain information on decomposition properties of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
. They are called the
shape vector
of their respective channel and their precise definition will be given in Section
3
, Proposition
1
. For now, it suffices to say that the definition of shape vector is specific to idempotent channel and that given an idempotent channel
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
,
\lambda(\mathcal{F})
can be computed in polynomial time in the dimension of
\mathcal{H}
(see
[
8
]
for the algorithm). Shape vectors were first introduced by Kuperberg in
[
12
]
to study the problem of emulating
quantum memories
one with another. Also, for
u\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
p\in[1,+\infty]
,
\|u\|_{p}
denotes the
\ell_{p}
-norm of
u
.

A remarkable aspect about this result is that it is a single-letter formula, i.e. the expression does not involve a limit. This comes from the fact that the norm of the shape vector is multiplicative under tensor product. Another implication is that the emulation capacity of idempotent channels is additive under tensor products of source channels, i.e.
C(\mathcal{G}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{G}_{2}\mapsto\mathcal{F})=C(\mathcal{G}_{1}\mapsto\mathcal{F})+C(\mathcal{G}_{2}\mapsto\mathcal{F})
.

One interesting consequence of this formula is that emulating
\mathcal{F}
with
\mathcal{G}
is in general not reversible. In fact, as shown in Example
1
, there exists idempotent channels for which
C(\mathcal{G}\mapsto\mathcal{F})C(\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{G})^{-1}<1
. This follows from the fact that the infimum in the formulas for
C(\mathcal{G}\mapsto\mathcal{F})
and
C(\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{G})
can be reached for different values of
p
.

Next, we consider the setting beyond the zero-error regime, where the emulation does not need to be exact: the rate
r
is achievable with error
\delta\in[0,1]
if there exists channels
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{D}
and
k,n
with
r\leq k/n
such that

\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{F}^{\otimes k}-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond}\leq\delta,

where
\|\cdot\|_{\diamond}
is the diamond norm (the completely bounded norm between trace classes).
We believe that for idempotent channels, the capacity (with vanishing error) should be the same as the zero-error case. Here, we only proved a weaker statement: the capacity cannot be larger than the same formula except that we only take the minimum over
p\in\{1,+\infty\}
. More specifically, we establish a strong converse rate for the emulation capacity.

Theorem 2

(Strong converse rate)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}\circ\mathcal{G}
be two idempotent channels with
\lambda(\mathcal{F})\neq(1)
. Let
(k_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(n_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}
be two integer sequences with
\lim_{\nu\to\infty}n_{\nu}=+\infty
such that there exists
\eps>0
satisfying:

\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\frac{k_{\nu}}{n_{\nu}}\geq\min_{p\in\{1,+\infty\}}\frac{\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{p})}{\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{p})}+\eps.

(3)

Then, for all sequences of encoding and decoding channels
(\mathcal{E}_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(\mathcal{D}_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}
,

\frac{1}{2}\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\|\mathcal{F}^{\otimes k_{\nu}}-\mathcal{D}_{\nu}\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n_{\nu}}\mathcal{E}_{\nu}\|_{\diamond}=1.

(4)

Note that for any two channels
\Phi
and
\Psi
, we have that
\|\Phi-\Psi\|_{\diamond}\leq 2
. Therefore, Theorem
2
states that if one tries to emulate
\mathcal{F}
with
\mathcal{G}
with a rate greater than
\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{p})/\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{p})
for
p\in\{1,+\infty\}
, the error in the limit of large block-length is maximal.

If
\mathcal{F}
or
\mathcal{G}
are identity channels or dephasing channels, then the achievability result of Theorem
1
and the strong converse rate in Theorem
2
match. In particular, for the case of
\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{Id}
, we obtain an expression for the quantum capacity of arbitrary idempotent channels together with a strong converse. In the case of self-adjoint idempotent channels (also known as tracial conditional expectation), this was proved in
[
9
]
(see also
[
13
]
).

We conjecture that Theorem
2
can be improved to take an infimum over all
p\in[1,+\infty]
, which would imply that the zero-error achievability in Theorem
1
is tight for all idempotent channels
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{G}
even if vanishing error is allowed.

The techniques used in our proofs stem from the theory of operator algebras. Nevertheless, familiarity with this theory is not required
to understand the present article. We will introduce in Section
2
the basic theory of operator algebras that we use throughout our work.

This article is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce our notations and basic preliminaries on quantum information and operator algebra theories. In Section
3
, we discuss the decomposition properties of idempotent channels. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1
. We prove Theorem
2
in Section
5
. Finally, we apply the theory of approximate
C^{*}
-algebras recently developed by Kitaev
[
10
]
to strengthen the converse of Theorem
1
in the appendix.

2
Preliminaries and notations

All Hilbert spaces in this work are assumed to be finite-dimensional. Given a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we write
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
the set of linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
. This set is endowed with the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product, denoted
\langle x,y\rangle:=\tr(x^{*}y)
for
x,y\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, with
x^{*}
the conjugate transpose of
x
and
\tr(\cdot)
the trace on
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. Note that
x^{*}
is the adjoint of
x
for the Hermitian product of
\mathcal{H}
. We denote
\mathds{1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Given
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, we call the
(right) support
of
x
, denoted
\supp(x)\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, the orthogonal complement of its (right) kernel. We write
x^{0}
the orthogonal projector on the support of
x
, parallel to its kernel. Furthermore, for
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, we write
\|x\|_{1}
for its Schatten
1
-norm and
\|x\|
its Schatten
\infty
-norm, which coincide with its operator norm as a linear operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Recall that the set of Hermitian operators in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is a partially ordered set: for
x=x^{*},y=y^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, we write
x\leq y
whenever
y-x
is positive semidefinite.

A state is represented by a positive semidefinite operator
\rho\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
with trace one, which is called a
density operator
. We will take
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
to be the set of density operators belonging to
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. A measurement on a physical system
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is represented by a Positive Operator Valued Measure, POVM for short, which is a set of positive operators
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{K}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, called
effects
, which sum to the identity, i.e.
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mu_{k}=\mathds{1}
.

A quantum channel
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
is a completely positive trace preserving linear map. Each linear map
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
admits an adjoint
\mathcal{F}^{*}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
for the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product such that for all
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
,
y\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
,
\langle\mathcal{F}(x),y\rangle=\langle x,\mathcal{F}^{*}(y)\rangle
. When
\mathcal{F}
is a channel, its adjoint
\mathcal{F}^{*}
is a unital completely positive map. In Section
5
, part of the discussion will be focused on the identity channel which we write
\mathrm{Id}_{d}:\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{C}}^{d})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{C}}^{d})
and on the completely dephasing channel
\Delta_{d}:\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{C}}^{d})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{C}}^{d})
, which maps every
x\in\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{C}}^{d})
onto its diagonal
\operatorname{Diag}(x_{11},\dots,x_{dd})
(w.r.t. the standard orthonormal basis). This channel is a ‘classical identity’ as it preserves perfectly the classical information while mapping its input state on a completely decorrelated state, hence a classical one.

We use, in Theorem
2
, the diamond norm as a figure of merit for the quality of the emulation. We recall that for
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
, the diamond norm is defined as

\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\diamond}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\|\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{n}\|_{1\to 1},

with
\|\cdot\|_{1\to 1}
the ‘one-to-one’ norm, i.e. the map norm when the input and output spaces are endowed with the Schatten 1-norm, denoted
\|\cdot\|_{1}
. That is, for all linear maps
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
,

\|\mathcal{F}\|_{1\to 1}=\sup_{\|x\|_{1}\leq 1}\|\mathcal{F}(x)\|_{1}.

Then, we recall basic properties of the
\ell_{p}
-norms. For
u\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
, the
\ell_{p}
-norm of
u
is defined as

\|u\|_{p}=\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|u_{k}|^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(5)

and
\|u\|_{\infty}=\sup_{k}|u_{k}|
. In the discussions of the next sections, we will frequently make use of the fact that the
\ell_{p}
-norm are
multiplicative
under the tensor product of vectors. Given
u\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, the tensor product
u\otimes v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{nm}
is the vector whose coordinates are the products of the coordinates of
u
and
v
, i.e.
(u_{k}v_{l})_{k,l}
for
k\in[n]
,
l\in[m]
. Then, for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
,
\|u\otimes v\|_{p}=\|u\|_{p}\|v\|_{p}
.

Finally, we end this section by introducing the notions of finite-dimensional
*
-algebras and their morphisms.

In this article, we actually consider only
*
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. Such a
*
-subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
which is stable under the multiplication and the
*
operation of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, i.e. taking the transpose conjugate.
That is, for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
,
xy\in\mathcal{A}
and for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
,
x^{*}\in\mathcal{A}
. Furthermore, all
*
-algebras in this work are assumed to be
unital
, that is there is an identity
\mathds{1}\in\mathcal{A}
satisfying
\mathds{1}^{*}=\mathds{1}
and, for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
,
\mathds{1}x=x\mathds{1}=x
. Such an identity operator is necessarily unique in each
*
-algebra.

Given two
*
-algebras
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, a
*
-homomorphism
\iota:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is a linear map that preserves the product and the
*
operation in
\mathcal{A}
. That is, for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
,
\iota(xy)=\iota(x)\iota(y)
and
\iota(x^{*})=\iota(x)^{*}
. In many texts,
*
-homomorphisms between unital
*
-algebras are assumed to be unital as well, that is they are assumed to map the identity of
\mathcal{A}
on the identity of
\mathcal{B}
. Nevertheless, in this article, we will work with more general
subunital

*
-homomorphisms. Such a map
\iota:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
maps the identity of
\mathcal{A}
on
\iota(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{A}})
, which satisfies
\iota(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{A}})\leq\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{B}}
. Note that
*
-homomorphisms are completely positive
[
6
]
.

3
Decomposition properties of idempotent channels

In this section, we discuss the decomposition properties of the range of idempotent channels, which are summarized in the following propositions.
The range of a map
f:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega^{\prime}
, denoted
\operatorname{Rg}(f)
, is the set of all possible images over its domain
\Omega
, i.e.
\operatorname{Rg}(f)=\{f(x)~|~x\in\Omega\}
. The range of an idempotent channel coincides with its set of fixed points and the structure of this space is well understood, see for example the lecture notes
[
19
]
. The following proposition summarizes the properties that we use.

Proposition 1

(Structure of the range of idempotent channels, see
[
19
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be an idempotent channel, i.e. satisfying
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{F}
. We can write
\mathcal{H}
as the direct sum

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1},

(6)

with
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\supp(\mathcal{F}(\mathds{1}))
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\ker(\mathcal{F}(\mathds{1}))
, and
\mathcal{H}_{0}
can further be decomposed as the orthogonal direct sum

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{H}_{k,1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k,2},

(7)

so that there is a set of density operators
\{\rho_{k}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{k,2})~|~k\in[K]\}
, such that

\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F})=\Bigl(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})\otimes\rho_{k}\Bigr)\oplus 0.

(8)

Let
e=\mathcal{F}(\mathds{1})^{0}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be the orthogonal projector on
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, and
V:\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be the isometry embedding
\mathcal{H}_{0}
into
\mathcal{H}
such that
VV^{*}=e
and
V^{*}V=\mathds{1}_{0}
the identity operator in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
. The
reduced quantum channel
associated to
\mathcal{F}
, denoted
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
is then defined as

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}:x\mapsto V^{*}\mathcal{F}(VxV^{*})V,

(9)

which is idempotent and whose range can be written as

\operatorname{Rg}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}})=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})\otimes\rho_{k}.

(10)

Furthermore, we can express
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
and its adjoint
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
in the following way:

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tr_{k,2}(P_{k}xP_{k})\otimes\rho_{k},

(11)

and

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{*}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tr_{k,2}(P_{k}xP_{k}(\mathds{1}_{d_{k}}\otimes\rho_{k}))\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}},

(12)

where, for all
k\in[K]
,
P_{k}
is the orthogonal projector onto
\mathcal{H}_{k,1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k,2}
,
d_{k}=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})\geq 1
,
m_{k}=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{k,2})
and
\tr_{k,2}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k,2})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})
is the partial trace over
\mathcal{H}_{k,2}
.

Therefore, the range of
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
is a unital
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
and the following decomposition holds:

\operatorname{Rg}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{*})=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}.

(13)

We call the
shape
of
\mathcal{F}
the
K
-dimensional vector whose coordinates are the dimensions
(d_{k})_{k\in[K]}
sorted in non-increasing order, which we write
\lambda(\mathcal{F})
.

Note that
\mathcal{F}
and its reduced channel
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
both have the same shape vector, i.e.
\lambda(\mathcal{F})=\lambda(\widehat{\mathcal{F}})
.

As an example of a shape vector, consider the qubit channel
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
defined as

\mathcal{F}:x\mapsto\tr(x)|0\rangle\!\langle 0|=\begin{pmatrix}\tr(x)&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},

where the matrix expression is written in the basis
(\ket{0},\ket{1})
. We have

\mathcal{F}(\mathds{1})=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then, in the notations of eq. (
7
), we get
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\supp(\mathcal{F}(\mathds{1}))={\mathbb{C}}\ket{0}
,
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\ker(\mathcal{F}(\mathds{1}))={\mathbb{C}}\ket{1}
, we can write
\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F})={\mathbb{C}}\otimes|0\rangle\!\langle 0|\oplus 0
and thus
\lambda(\mathcal{F})=(1)
. To construct the reduced version of
\mathcal{F}
we can take the isometry
V=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}:\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
, where the expression of
V
is written in the basis
(\ket{0},\ket{1})
. Note that we clearly have

VV^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{F}(\mathds{1})^{0}.

Then,
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}:\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{C}})
is defined by the equation

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}(x)=V^{*}\mathcal{F}(VxV^{*})V=x,

so that
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is the identity on
\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Furthermore, we remark that we have
\lambda(\mathcal{F})=(1)
if and only if its reduced channel is a replacer channel, i.e.
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}:x\mapsto\tr(x)\rho
with
\rho
a fixed state. This fact follows easily from eq. (
11
) taking
K=1
.

Before we present the proof of Proposition
1
, we need to state the two following lemmas which will be used in the proof.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\Phi:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be a positive linear map
2
2

2

Here ’positive’ means that
x\geq 0
implies
\Phi(x)\geq 0
.
, let
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\supp(\Phi(\mathds{1}))
and let
V:\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be an isometry embedding
\mathcal{H}_{0}
into
\mathcal{H}
so that
VV^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is the orthogonal projection on
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Then for all
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
,

VV^{*}\Phi(x)VV^{*}=\Phi(x).

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be positive semidefinite, then

0\leq x\leq\|x\|\mathds{1},

so that by positivity of
\Phi
, we have

0\leq\Phi(x)\leq\|x\|\Phi(\mathds{1}).

Hence,
\Phi(x)
is supported on the support of
\Phi(\mathds{1})
which is, by definition,
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Furthermore, as
x
and
\mathds{1}
are positive semidefinite,
\Phi(x)
and
\Phi(\mathds{1})
are positive semidefinite as well so that their respective range (as linear maps on
\mathcal{H}
) coincides with their support. Hence, we have

VV^{*}\Phi(x)VV^{*}=\Phi(x).

Now, if
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is arbitrary, we can use the polarisation identity (see eq. (1.45) in
[
19
]

x=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=0}^{3}i^{k}(x+i^{k}\mathds{1})^{*}(x+i^{k}\mathds{1}),

The following example shows that the emulation capacity is not

Example 1

be two idempotent channels equal to their reduced channels, i.e.
\mathcal{F}=\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathcal{G}=\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
, and let
\mathcal{D}
,
\mathcal{E}
be two channels such that

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}.

Taking the adjoint of this equation, we get

\mathcal{F}^{*}=\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}=\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*},

where the right-hand side follows from the idempotence of
\mathcal{G}^{*}
.
Let
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})
, applying Lemma
8
to the channel
\Phi=\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}
, we get that

\mathcal{F}^{*}(x)=\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}(e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{G}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}})=\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x),

with
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}
defined in the statement of Proposition
7
.
Let
x\in\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
. By the assumption that
\mathcal{F}=\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
,
\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
is a
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})
, thus
x^{*}x\in\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
, and as
\mathcal{F}^{*}
is idempotent,
xx^{*}
is a fixed point of
\mathcal{F}^{*}
, so that we have:

\displaystyle x^{*}x

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{*}(x^{*}x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*}x)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\geq}\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*})\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x))

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\geq}\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*}))\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x))

\displaystyle=x^{*}x,

where we used the Kadison-Schwarz inequality to get both
(a)
and
(b)
. Since the left-hand side and the right-hand side of this chain of inequalities coincide, we conclude that
(a)
and
(b)
are in fact equalities. Therefore,

\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*}x)-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*})\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x))=0,

where, by the Kadison-Schwarz inequality,

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*}x)-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*})\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\geq 0.

Therefore, by the second statement in Lemma
8
applied to the channel
\Phi=\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}
, we obtain

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*}x)-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*})\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)=0.

We can swap the roles of
x
and
x^{*}
in
(a)
and
(b)
to find that

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(xx^{*})-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*})=0.

Therefore,
x
is in the multiplicative domain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
. Thus,
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
restricted to
\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
is an injective unital
*
-homomorphism into the
*
-algebra
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
, which is a
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})
and whose identity operator is
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
. It is injective as, for all
x\in\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
,

x=\mathcal{F}^{*}(x)=\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x),

hence
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)=0
implies
x=0
. It is unital as it maps the identity of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})
to
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
which is the identity operator of the
*
-algebra
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
. Indeed, as both
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{D}
are channels,
\mathcal{G}^{*}
and
\mathcal{D}^{*}
are unital so that
\mathcal{G}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})=e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
.

Now, to show that
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
is a
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})
which satisfies for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
,

{\|\lambda(e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}})\|_{p}\leq\|\lambda(\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*}))\|_{p}}
, it suffices to remark that, by Proposition
1
, we can decompose the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}}
as

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{H}_{k,1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k,2},

and the
*
-algebra
\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})
as

\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}},

with
m_{k}=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{k,2})
for all
k\in[K]
. Then
\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})\in\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G})
can itself be decomposed as

\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}x_{k}\otimes\rho_{k},

with
\{x_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})~|~k\in[K]\}
and
\{\rho_{k}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{k,2})~|~k\in[K]\}
. Thus,

e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})^{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}x_{k}^{0}\otimes\rho_{k}^{0},

and

e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K}x_{k}^{0}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})x_{k}^{0}\otimes\rho_{k}^{0}.

Hence,
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
is a
*
-subalgebra for all
k\in[K]
, the
k
-th coordinate of the shape vector of
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
satisfies

\lambda(e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}})_{k}\leq\lambda(\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*}))_{k},

which allows us to conclude.
∎

We can now prove the converse of Theorem
1
.

Converse of Theorem
1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})
and
\mathcal{G}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})
be two idempotent channels, let
k,n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that there are two channels
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{D}
satisfying

\mathcal{F}^{\otimes k}=\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}\mathcal{E}.

By Proposition
3
, then, if we denote
\widehat{\mathcal{F}^{\otimes k}}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}
the reduced channels associated to
\mathcal{F}^{\otimes k}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}
, this condition is equivalent to

\widehat{\mathcal{F}^{\otimes n}}=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime},

for some channels
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
.
Thus, by Proposition
7
, there is an injective unital
*
-homomorphism from
\operatorname{Rg}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}^{\otimes k}}^{*})
into the
*
-algebra
e_{\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\operatorname{Rg}(\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}^{*})e_{\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
, which is itself a
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})
. Then, by Theorem
7
and the second statement of Proposition
7
, we have for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
:

\|\lambda(\widehat{\mathcal{F}^{\otimes k}})\|_{p}=\|\lambda(\operatorname{Rg}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}^{\otimes k}}^{*}))\|_{p}\leq\|\lambda(e_{\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\operatorname{Rg}(\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}^{*})e_{\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}})\|_{p}\leq\|\lambda(\operatorname{Rg}(\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}^{*}))\|_{p}=\|\lambda(\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}^{*})\|.

By the multiplicativity property of the shape vectors and the
\ell_{p}
-norms with the tensor product (Proposition
5
), we get for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
:

\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{p}^{k}=\|\lambda(\widehat{\mathcal{F}})\|_{p}^{k}\leq\|\lambda(\widehat{\mathcal{G}})\|_{p}^{k}=\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|^{n}_{p}.

Thus,

\frac{k}{n}\leq\inf_{p\in[1,+\infty]}\frac{\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{p})}{\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{p})},

which ends the proof.
∎

Finally, we give a slightly stronger version of the converse of Theorem
1
when allowing for dimension dependent errors in the Appendix. This stronger result is an application of approximate
C^{*}
-algebras, which were recently introduced by Kitaev in
[
10
]
.

5
Emulation of idempotent channels with errors

In Theorem
1
, the converse states that it is impossible to emulate an idempotent channel
\mathcal{F}
by another idempotent channel
\mathcal{G}
at a rate higher than the infimum in eq. (
1
) without error. In this section, we prove Theorem
2
which shows that we can not achieve better rates than

\inf_{p\in\{1,+\infty\}}\frac{\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{p})}{\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{p})},

even if one allows errors. This strong converse rate is probably not tight in general, as the emulation capacity is expressed in Theorem
1
as an infimum over all
p\in[1,+\infty]
, we therefore conjecture that our strong converse rate can be improved to the infimum over all
p\in[1,+\infty]
.

Theorem
2
is actually a corollary of the following theorem, which gives a lower bound on the minimal error achievable for the one-shot emulation of
\mathcal{F}
by
\mathcal{G}
. Note that this theorem can be seen as a generalisation of eq. (18) in
[
11
]
about emulation of identity channels. In fact, even in the special case of identity channels, we strengthen the bound of
[
11
]
by a factor of
2
.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
be two idempotent channels, then

\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}}\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond}\geq 1-\min_{p\in\{1,+\infty\}}\frac{\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{p}}{\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{p}}.

(36)

To prove this theorem, we use the Holevo-Helstrom bound applied to the problem of channel discrimination.

Lemma 10

(see e.g. Theorem 3.52 in
[
18
]
)
.

Let
\Phi_{1},~\Phi_{2}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
be two quantum channels. For any choice of auxiliary system
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime})
of dimension
d=\dim(\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime})
, for any choice of positive semidefinite operator
\mu\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime})
satisfying
\mu\leq\mathds{1}
and of density operator
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime})
,

\frac{1}{2}\|\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}\|_{\diamond}\geq\tr(\mu\Phi_{1}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{d}(\sigma))-\tr(\mu\Phi_{2}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{d}(\sigma)).

(37)

We can now prove Theorem
9
.

Proof of Theorem
9
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})
and
\mathcal{G}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})
be two idempotent channels. If we denote
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
their respective reduced channels, we showed as Proposition
3
that

\inf_{\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}}\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond}=\inf_{\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}}\|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}-\mathcal{D}\widehat{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond}.

Therefore, to simplify our notations, we will suppose without loss of generality that
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are equal to their reduced version, i.e.
\mathcal{F}=\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathcal{G}=\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
.
We prove separately the two following equations:

\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}}\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond}\geq 1-\frac{\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{1}}{\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{1}},

(38)

and

\frac{1}{2}\inf_{\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}}\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond}\geq 1-\frac{\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{\infty}}{\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{\infty}},

(39)

which taken together amount to the statement of Theorem
9
.

To prove each equation, we use Lemma
10
on the channels
\Phi_{1}=\mathcal{F}
and
\Phi_{2}=\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}
and we find an Ansatz
(\sigma,\mu)
in eq. (
37
) such that on the one hand

\tr(\mu\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{d}(\sigma))=1,

and, on the other hand

\tr(\mu\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{d}(\sigma))\leq\frac{\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{p}}{\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{p}},

with
p=1
for one Ansatz and
p=+\infty
for the other.

We begin with the case
p=1
. As we saw in Proposition
1
, we can decompose
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}
as an orthogonal direct sum

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{H}_{k,1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k,2},

so that

\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F})=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})\otimes\rho_{k},

with, for all
k\in[K]
,
\rho_{k}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{k,2})
. To simplify our notations, we write
d_{k}=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})
and
m_{k}=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{k,2})
for all
k\in[K]
, so that
\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}
. Now, for each
k\in[K]
, let
\{\ket{\nu}_{k}~|~\nu\in[d_{k}]\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{k,1}
. We define our Ansatz
\sigma
as being, basically, the sum of the maximally correlated states on the
\mathcal{H}_{k,1}
spaces for
k\in[K]
:

\sigma=\frac{1}{\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\nu=1}^{d_{k}}|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}}{m_{k}}\otimes|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}}{m_{k}},

(40)

where
|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}=|\nu\rangle_{k}\!\langle\nu|_{k}
for notational simplicity. Then, we take

\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\nu=1}^{d_{k}}|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}\otimes|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}.

(41)

We now have:

\displaystyle\tr(\mu\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathrm{Id}(\sigma))

\displaystyle=\tr(\mathcal{F}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{Id}(\mu)\sigma)

\displaystyle=\tr(\mu\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{1}}\tr(\bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\nu=1}^{d_{k}}|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}\otimes|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}\bigr)\bigl(\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\sum_{\nu^{\prime}=1}^{d_{k^{\prime}}}|\nu^{\prime}\rangle\!\langle\nu^{\prime}|_{k^{\prime}}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k^{\prime}}}}{m_{k^{\prime}}}\otimes|\nu^{\prime}\rangle\!\langle\nu^{\prime}|_{k^{\prime}}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k^{\prime}}}}{m_{k^{\prime}}}\bigr))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{1}}\tr(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\nu=1}^{d_{k}}|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}

\displaystyle=1,

where, for the second equality, we used the fact that as
|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})
,
|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}\in\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
for all
k\in[K]
so that
\mathcal{F}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{Id}(\mu)=\mu
. For the upper bound on
\tr(\mu\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathrm{Id}(\sigma))
, we have

\displaystyle\tr(\mu\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathrm{Id}(\sigma))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{1}}\tr(\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{aligned} k&\in[K]\\
\nu&\in[d_{k}]\end{aligned}}|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}\otimes|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{aligned} k^{\prime}&\in[K]\\
\nu^{\prime}&\in[d_{k^{\prime}}]\end{aligned}}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\bigl(|\nu^{\prime}\rangle\!\langle\nu^{\prime}|_{k^{\prime}}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k^{\prime}}}}{m_{k^{\prime}}}\bigr)\otimes|\nu^{\prime}\rangle\!\langle\nu^{\prime}|_{k^{\prime}}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k^{\prime}}}}{m_{k^{\prime}}}\Bigr)),

so that

\tr(\mu\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathrm{Id}(\sigma))=\frac{1}{\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{1}}\tr(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\nu=1}^{d_{k}}(|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}})(\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}(|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}}{m_{k}}))).

(42)

From Proposition
1
, we can decompose
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\mathcal{G}
as

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{k,1}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{k,2},

and

\mathcal{G}:x\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{K^{\prime}}\tr_{k,2}(P^{\prime}_{k}xP^{\prime}_{k})\otimes\rho^{\prime}_{k},

with
P^{\prime}_{k}
the orthogonal projector on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{k,1}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{k,2}
,
\tr_{k,2}(\cdot)
the partial trace over
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{k,2}
and
\rho^{\prime}_{k}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{k,2})
for all
k\in[K^{\prime}]
. We can therefore factorise
\mathcal{G}
into two channels, one being the sum of the partial traces and the other being the sum of tensor products with the states
\rho^{\prime}_{k}
for
k\in[K^{\prime}]
. We write
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{k,1}
and we define
\mathcal{G}_{1}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}})
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}:x

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{K^{\prime}}\tr_{k,2}(P^{\prime}_{k}xP^{\prime}_{k}),

(43)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}:x

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{K^{\prime}}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{k}x\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{k}\otimes\rho^{\prime}_{k},

(44)

where
\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}})
is the orthogonal projector on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{k,1}
for all
k\in[K^{\prime}]
. Now,
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
are channels and we can write
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{2}\circ\mathcal{G}_{1}
. Furthermore, let
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}}
be the identity operator in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}})
, we then have, similarly as in the proof of eq. (18) in
[
11
]
,

\displaystyle\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K^{\prime}}\dim(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{k,1})

\displaystyle=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}})

\displaystyle=\tr(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}})

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\tr(\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\nu=1}^{d_{k}}\tr(\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}))

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\geq}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\nu=1}^{d_{k}}\tr(\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}})\mathcal{G}_{1}\mathcal{E}(|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}}{m_{k}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\nu=1}^{d_{k}}\tr(|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}_{2}\mathcal{G}_{1}\mathcal{E}(|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}}{m_{k}}))

\displaystyle=\tr(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\nu=1}^{d_{k}}(|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}})(\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}(|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}}{m_{k}}))),

where
(a)
follows from the fact that
\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}_{2}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})
is a channel, so that
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
is unital. Then
(b)
follows from the fact that for all
k\in[K]
and
\nu\in[d_{k}]
,
|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}}{m_{k}}
is a state so that
\tr(\mathcal{G}_{1}\mathcal{E}_{1}(|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}}{m_{k}}))=1
, hence
0\leq\mathcal{G}_{1}\mathcal{E}_{1}(|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\frac{\mathds{1}_{m_{k}}}{m_{k}})\leq\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{1}}
, and that as
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
is completely positive,
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(|\nu\rangle\!\langle\nu|_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m_{k}})\geq 0
. We then simply use that if
A\geq 0
and
B\leq\mathds{1}
,
\tr(A)\geq\tr(AB)
.
When injecting the last equation into eq. (
42
), we find

\tr(\mu\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}(\sigma))\leq\frac{\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{1}}{\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{1}}.

We now move on to the proof of eq. (
39
), i.e. the case
p=+\infty
. To deal with the case where the blocks of the range of
\mathcal{F}^{*}
have multiplicities, we factorise
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{2}\circ\mathcal{F}_{1}
as we did for
\mathcal{G}
in eq. (
43
), so that we have the decompositions

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{H}_{k,1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k,2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:x

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tr_{k,2}(P_{k}xP_{k})\otimes\rho_{k},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}:x

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tr_{k,2}(P_{k}xP_{k}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}:x

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{K}\widetilde{P}_{k}x\widetilde{P}_{k}\otimes\rho_{k},

where, for
k\in[K]
,
\rho_{k}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{k,2})
,
P_{k}
is the orthogonal projector on
\mathcal{H}_{k,1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k,2}
and

\mathcal{G}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})
be two idempotent channels equal to their reduced channels, i.e.
\mathcal{F}=\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathcal{G}=\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
, such that there exists two channels
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{D}
satisfying

\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond}\leq\frac{\delta_{\max}\lambda_{\min,1}}{6d},

(59)

with
d=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})
,
\delta_{\max}
is the constant of Theorem
12
and
\lambda_{\min,1}
is the minimal non-zero eigenvalue of
\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})
.
We write
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})^{0}
the orthogonal projector on the support
\supp(\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}))
and we define

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}:x\mapsto e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{G}^{*}(x)e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}.

Then,
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
is a
\delta_{\max}
-inclusion from the
*
-algebra
\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
into the
*
-algebra
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
.
By the Error Reduction Theorem
12
, there is therefore an injective unital
*
-homomorphism embedding
\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
into
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
,
thus, for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
,

\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{p}\leq\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{p}.

We divide the proof that the map
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
restricted to
\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
satisfies all the properties of a
\delta_{\max}
-inclusion into
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
in multiple lemmas, each of which being devoted to prove one of these properties.

We begin with the one related to the almost preservation of the Schatten
\infty
-norm. We use the
completely bounded operator norm
in its proof, which is defined for all linear maps
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
as

\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathrm{cb}}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\|\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{n}\|_{\infty\rightarrow\infty},

with
\|\cdot\|_{\infty\rightarrow\infty}
the ‘infinity-to-infinity’ norm, i.e. the map norm of linear maps from
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
to
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
when the input and output spaces are endowed with the Schatten
\infty
-norms. That is:

\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\infty\to\infty}=\sup_{\|x\|\leq 1}\|\mathcal{F}(x)\|.

Note that for any linear map
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
, we have

\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\diamond}=\|\mathcal{F}^{*}\|_{\mathrm{cb}}.

Lemma 15

(Almost norm preservation)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{G}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
,
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{D}
be defined as in the statement of Proposition
8
and let

\delta\geq\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond}=\|\mathcal{F}^{*}-\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}\|_{\mathrm{cb}}.

Then, for all
x\in\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
,

(1-\delta)\|x\|\leq\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\|\leq\|x\|.

Note that this lemma implies that as long as
\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond}\leq\delta<1
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
is injective as every element of its kernel
x\in\ker(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*})
satisfies
(1-\delta)\|x\|\leq\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\|=0
, hence
\|x\|=0
so that
x=0
.

Proof.

The right-hand side inequality follows from the fact that
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})
is a subunital completely positive map (when the identity considered in the output space of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
is the identity of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})
). For the left-hand side inequality, recall that, by Lemma
8
, for all
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})
,

\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)=\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x).

Then, we have for all
x\in\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
:

\displaystyle\|x\|-\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\|

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\|x\|-\|\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\|

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\|x-\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\|

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{=}\|\mathcal{F}^{*}(x)-\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{F}^{*}-\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}\|_{\mathrm{cb}}\|x\|

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|x\|,

where
(a)
follows from the fact that
\|\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\|\leq\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\|
,
since
\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}
is a unital completely positive map. Then,
(b)
follows from the triangular inequality. Finally,
(c)
follows from the fact that
\mathcal{F}^{*}(x)=x
for all
x\in\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
.
The last inequality can be rewritten as

(1-\delta)\|x\|\leq\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\|.

∎

We then move on to the proof that
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}:\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})\rightarrow e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
is unital, where we recall that the unit of the
*
-algebra
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
is
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
, as, for every element
x=e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}x^{\prime}e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\in e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
,
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}x=xe_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}=x
. Also note that for a general
\delta
-inclusion, one only requires almost unitality.

Lemma 16

(Exact unitality)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{G}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
,
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{D}
be defined as in Proposition
8
. Then:

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})=e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{D}
are assumed to be channels, their adjoints are unital maps on
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})
, so that

\mathcal{G}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{G}}.

Therefore

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})=e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{G}^{*}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}))e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}=e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{G}}e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}=e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}.

∎

Finally, to prove the almost preservation of the multiplication on
\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
by
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
, we will need the following additional lemma.

Lemma 17
.

Let
\Phi:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
be a channel, write
d=\dim(\mathcal{H})
,
e=\Phi(\mathds{1})^{0}
and
\lambda_{\min,1}
the minimal non-zero eigenvalue of
\Phi(\mathds{1})
. Then, for all positive semidefinite
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
,

\frac{\lambda_{\min,1}}{d}\|exe\|\leq\|\Phi^{*}(x)\|.

(60)

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
be positive semidefinite, then

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{*}(x)\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{d}\tr(\Phi^{*}(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\tr(x\Phi(\mathds{1}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{\min,1}}{d}\tr(exe)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{\min,1}}{d}\|exe\|.

∎

Note that this lemma implies the second statement of Lemma
8
. We can now move on to the proof of the almost preservation of the multiplication, which uses our Theorem
13
on the approximate multiplicative domain of maps satisfying the Kadison-Schwarz inequality.

Lemma 18

(Almost preservation of the multiplication)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{G}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
,
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{D}
be defined as in the statement of Proposition
8
, let

\delta\geq\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond},

let
\lambda_{\min,1}
be the minimal non-zero eigenvalue of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{E}^{*}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})
,
d=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})
and let
x,y\in\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
. We then have:

\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(xy)-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(y)\|\leq\frac{6\delta d}{\lambda_{\min,1}}\|x\|\|y\|.

Proof.

We prove that, for all
x\in\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*}x)-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*})\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\delta d}{\lambda_{\min,1}}\|x\|^{2},

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(xx^{*})-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\delta d}{\lambda_{\min,1}}\|x\|^{2},

and then use Theorem
13
to conclude.
First, to simplify our notations, we let
\Phi^{*}=\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}=\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
. We have:

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{*}(x^{*}x)-\Phi^{*}(x^{*})\Phi^{*}(x)\|

\displaystyle=\|\Phi^{*}(x^{*}x)-\Phi^{*}(x^{*})\Phi^{*}(x)+x^{*}x-x^{*}x\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi^{*}(x^{*}x)-x^{*}x\|+\|x^{*}x-\Phi^{*}(x^{*})\Phi^{*}(x)+x^{*}\Phi^{*}(x)-x^{*}\Phi^{*}(x)\|

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\delta\|x\|^{2}+\|x\|\|x-\Phi^{*}(x)\|+\|x^{*}-\Phi^{*}(x^{*})\|\|\Phi^{*}(x)\|

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}3\delta\|x\|^{2},

where
(a)
follows from the fact that as
x\in\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
, we have

\|\Phi(x^{*}x)-x^{*}x\|=\|\Phi^{*}(x^{*}x)-\mathcal{F}^{*}(x^{*}x)\|\leq\|\Phi^{*}-\mathcal{F}^{*}\|_{\mathrm{cb}}\|x^{*}x\|\leq\delta\|x\|^{2}.

By the same argument, we also have that
\|x-\Phi^{*}(x)\|\leq\delta\|x\|
and
\|x^{*}-\Phi^{*}(x^{*})\|\leq\delta\|x\|
, hence
(b)
follows.

Then, applying the Kadison-Schwarz inequality, we get:

\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*}x)\geq\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*})\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x))\geq\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*})\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x).

Therefore:

\|\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{G}^{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*}x)-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*})\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x))\|\leq\|\Phi^{*}(x^{*}x)-\Phi^{*}(x^{*})\Phi^{*}(x)\|\leq 3\delta\|x\|^{2}.

Thus, by Lemma
17
, we obtain

\|\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*}x)-\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x^{*})\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}(x)\|\leq\frac{3d\delta}{\lambda_{\min,1}}\|x\|^{2}.

We can apply the exact same line of reasoning to prove the same inequality but with the roles of
x
and
x^{*}
swapped.
∎

Gathering all these lemmas, the proof of Proposition
8
is then as follows.

Proof of Proposition
8
.

Let
\delta\geq\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond}
. Since we assumed that
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are equal to their reduced channels, i.e.
\mathcal{F}=\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathcal{G}=\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
,
\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
is a
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})
and
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
is a
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}})
.
By Lemmas
15
,
16
and
18
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
is a
\max\{\delta,\frac{6d\delta}{\lambda_{\min,1}}\}
-inclusion (we trivially have that
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
is Hermitian preserving). However, as

d=\tr(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})=\tr(\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}))\geq\lambda_{\min,1},

\frac{d}{\lambda_{\min,1}}\geq 1
so that
\delta\leq\frac{6d}{\lambda_{\min,1}}\delta
. Finally,
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\mathcal{D}^{*}
is a
\frac{6d\delta}{\lambda_{\min,1}}
-inclusion of
\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
into
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
.

Then, if we suppose that

\frac{6d\delta}{\lambda_{\min,1}}\leq\delta_{\max},

i.e.

\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}\|_{\diamond}\leq\delta\leq\frac{\delta_{\max}\lambda_{\min,1}}{6d},

by the Error Reduction Theorem (Theorem
12
), there is an injective
*
-homomorphism from
\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{F}^{*})
into
e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}
, thus by Kuperberg’s theorem (Theorem
7
) and the second part of Proposition
7
, for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
,

\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{p}\leq\|\lambda(e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Rg}(\mathcal{G}^{*})e_{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{E}})\|_{p}\leq\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{p}.

∎

We can finally show Theorem
11
.

Proof of Theorem
11
.

Basically, we simply apply Proposition
8
to
{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{\otimes k}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\otimes k})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\otimes k})}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes n}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes n})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes n})
, with
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
the reduced channels respectively associated to
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
. Just note that the dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\otimes k}
is
d^{k}
, with
d=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}})
. Thus, by Proposition
8
, if the hypotheses of Theorem
11
are met, for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
, we have:

\|\lambda(\mathcal{F}^{\otimes k})\|_{p}=\|\lambda(\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{\otimes k})\|_{p}\leq\|\lambda(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes n})\|_{p}=\|\lambda(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})\|_{p}.

This implies, by the compatibility of the
\ell_{p}
-norms of the shape vector with the tensor product, that

\frac{k}{n}\leq\inf_{p\in[1,+\infty]}\frac{\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{G})\|_{p})}{\log(\|\lambda(\mathcal{F})\|_{p})}=C(\mathcal{G}\mapsto\mathcal{F}),

where the right-hand side equality is the first statement in Theorem
1
.

∎

Emergent order spectrum for transitive homeomorphisms
F. Ciavattini, M. Farotti, C. Lucamarini

Abstract
The Emergent Order Spectrum Ω ⁡ ( x , y ) \Omega(x,y) is a topological invariant of dynamical systems providing order-types induced by the limit order of order-compatible nested ε n \varepsilon_{n} -chains (with ε n → 0 \varepsilon_{n}\to 0 ) from x x to y y . In this paper, we investigate how rich these spectra can be under natural dynamical hypotheses. For a transitive homeomorphism f f of a compact metric space X X without isolated points and of cardinality 𝔠 \mathfrak{c} , we show that the global spectrum Ω f ​ ( X 2 ) \Omega_{f}(X^{2}) is universal at the countable scattered level: every countable scattered order-type together with the order-type of the rationals appears in Ω f ​ ( X 2 ) \Omega_{f}(X^{2}) . More precisely, there exists a comeagre subset ℳ ⊆ X 2 \mathcal{M}\subseteq X^{2} such that, for every ( x , y ) ∈ ℳ (x,y)\in\mathcal{M} , the individual spectrum Ω f ​ ( x , y ) \Omega_{f}(x,y) already realizes all countably infinite scattered order-types; moreover, the order-type of the rationals belongs to Ω f ​ ( x , y ) \Omega_{f}(x,y) for every pair ( x , y ) ∈ X 2 (x,y)\in X^{2} . Keywords: Topological Dynamics; Chain Recurrence; Scattered Linear Orderings; Conjugacy Invariants. MSC2020: 37B20; 37B35; 06A05.

Introduction

The structure of attractors and the decomposition of a topological dynamical system in gradient-like and chain-transitive subsets are best understood in terms of
\varepsilon
–chains (or pseudo-orbits). The
chain relation

\mathcal{C}
, indeed, is the basis for both Conley’s decomposition theory
[
1
]
and Akin’s structural approach to attractors (see, e.g.,
[
2
, Theorem 2.68]
).
Topological transitivity, on its part (by which here we mean the existence of a point with a dense orbit), is a dynamical property characterizing many important systems or subsystems, and has been studied deeply in the classical continuous case (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
) and also for discontinuous maps (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
).

In this work, we investigate the chain relation in the case of transitive maps.
Specifically, we explore some structural properties of the invariant chain-related backbone of transitive homeomorphisms of compact spaces.

Throughout the paper, by
compact dynamical system
(or simply dynamical system) we mean a pair
(X,f)
where
X
is a compact, metrizable space and
f
is a self-map of
X
.
For
x\in X
, we denote by
\mathcal{O}^{+}(x)
and
\mathcal{O}^{-}(x)
, respectively, the
forward
and
backward orbit
of
x
under
f
, that is

\mathcal{O}^{+}(x)=\{f^{k}(x):k\in\mathbb{N}\}\quad,\quad\mathcal{O}^{-}(x)=\{f^{k}(x):k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{N}_{0}\},

where by
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}
we denote, respectively, the sets of positive and non-negative integers.
The two-sided orbit of
x
, or more simply the
orbit
of
x
, is the set

\mathcal{O}(x)=\mathcal{O}^{+}(x)\cup\{x\}\cup\mathcal{O}^{-}(x).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(X,f)
be a dynamical system with
f
a homeomorphism. We say that
f
is
transitive
if there exists a point
x\in X
whose orbit is dense in
X
, that is,
\overline{\mathcal{O}(x)}=X
.

Remark 1.2
.

Notice that if
(X,f)
is a dynamical system with
f
a transitive homeomorphism and
X
has no isolated points, then the set

\mathcal{I}=\{x\in X\,|\,\overline{\mathcal{O}^{-}(x)}=\overline{\mathcal{O}^{+}(x)}=X\}

of points whose forward and backward orbits are both dense in
X
is comeagre in
X
(see, for instance,
[
11
, pag. 70]
).

Let us recall the notions of
\varepsilon
-chain and chain relation.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
(X,f)
be a dynamical system. Given two points
x,y\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
, an
\varepsilon
-chain
from
x
to

is an indexed, finite sequence of points of
X
, that is a map

C:\{0,1,\dots,m\}\longrightarrow X,

with

, such that, setting

, we have:

i)

x_{0}=x
and
x_{m}=y
,

ii)

d(f(x_{i}),x_{i+1})<\varepsilon
, for every
i=0,1,\dots,m-1
.

By
\widehat{C}
, we denote the support of the chain
C
, that is,

\widehat{C}=C(\{0,1,\dots,m\})=\{x_{0},\dots,x_{m}\}.

With an abuse of notation, we may indicate a chain
C
by writing simply

C:x_{0},\,x_{1},\dots,\,x_{m}.

We say that
x,y\in X
are in
chain recurrence relation
(or simply in chain relation), and we write
x\,\mathcal{C}\,y
, if and only if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an
\varepsilon
-chain from
x
to
y
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
(X,f)
be a dynamical system.
Assuming that, for
x,y\in X
, we have
x\,\mathcal{C}\,y
, we say that the sequence
\{C_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\varepsilon_{n}
-chains from
x
to
y
is a
complete sequence of chains
if
\varepsilon_{n}
converges to 0 monotonically.

In
[
12
]
, it was shown that on compact systems, the chain relation already encodes a much finer canonical structure.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
(X,f)
be a dynamical system. For
x,y\in X
, we say that
x\,\mathcal{C}_{\subseteq}\,y
if and only if, for a sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive real numbers converging to 0 monotonically, there is a complete sequence
\{C_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\varepsilon_{n}
-chains from
x
to
y
such that
\widehat{C_{n}}\subseteq\widehat{C_{n+1}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We will call
\{C_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a
sequence of nested chains for the sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

Definition 1.6
.

For
x,y\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
let
C:x_{0},\,x_{1},\dots,\,x_{m}
be an
\varepsilon
-chain from
x=x_{0}
to
y=x_{m}
. We say that

contains a

if one of the following conditions holds:

•

for some
i,j\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}
such that

, we have
x_{i}=x_{j}
;

•

for some
i\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}
, we have
x_{i}=x_{0}
or
x_{i}=x_{m}
.

We say that
C
is

if it does not contain any cyclic sub-chain.

It is easily seen that any
\varepsilon
-chain

with a cyclic sub-chain can be modified (by suitably eliminating some points) to get an
\varepsilon
-chain
C^{\prime}
with no cyclic sub-chain and such that
\widehat{C^{\prime}}\subseteq\widehat{C}
.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
(X,f)
be a dynamical system.
Assume that two points
x,y\in X
are such that
x\,\mathcal{C}_{\subseteq}\,y
. We say that
x\,\mathcal{C}_{\preceq}\,y
if the sequence of nested chains
\{C_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
can be chosen so as to make all its chains without cyclic sub-chains.

In
[
12
, Theorems 3.6 and 5.2]
, it has been proved that the three relations
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\subseteq}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\preceq}
coincide in compact dynamical systems with continuous maps.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
(X,f)
be a compact dynamical system with
f
continuous. For
x,y\in X
the following are equivalent:

(1)

x\,\mathcal{C}\,y
, i.e. for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists an
\varepsilon
-chain from
x
to
y
;

(2)

x\,\mathcal{C}_{\subseteq}\,y
, i.e. there exists a vanishing sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
and a family of
\varepsilon_{n}
–chains
C_{n}
from
x
to
y
with
nested supports

\widehat{C_{n}}\subseteq\widehat{C_{n+1}}
;

(3)

x\,\mathcal{C}_{\preceq}\,y
, i.e. one can choose the nested family in (2) to be
acyclic
.

Consequently,

\mathcal{C}\;=\;\mathcal{C}_{\subseteq}\;=\;\mathcal{C}_{\preceq}.

Remark 1.9
.

The properties in (2)-(3), as shown in
[
12
]
(see Remark 2.13, Remark 2.16 and Lemma 2.18), are independent both of the metric and of the particular choice of the vanishing sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
(A,\leq_{A})
and
(B,\leq_{B})
be two posets.
An
order-isomorphism
between
A
and
B
is a bijection
f:A\to B
such that for every
x,y\in A
,
x\leq_{A}y
if and only if
f(x)\leq_{B}f(y)
.
We write
(A,\leq_{A})\cong(B,\leq_{B})
if such a map exists.
This defines an equivalence relation on the class of all posets.

The
order-type
of a poset
(A,\leq_{A})
is the equivalence class

\operatorname{otp}(A,\leq_{A})\;=\;\{(B,\leq_{B})\mid(B,\leq_{B})\cong(A,\leq_{A})\}.

Remark 1.11
.

Of course, the collection
\operatorname{otp}(A,\leq_{A})
is in general not a set but a proper class in ZF.
One common way to avoid this size issue is to work inside a
universe of sets
(for example, the cumulative hierarchy
V
in von Neumann’s construction (see, for instance,
[
13
, p. 63]
)), and to take order-types with respect to the posets contained in that universe.

We denote by:

i)

K
the finite linear order on
K
elements;

ii)

\omega
the first infinite ordinal;

iii)

\omega^{*}
the reverse order-type of
\omega
, that is, the order-type of non-positive integers under the usual relation
\leq
;

iv)

\zeta=\omega^{*}+\omega
the order-type of the integer numbers
\mathbb{Z}
;

v)

\eta
the countable dense order-type without extrema;

vi)

\omega_{1}
the first uncountable ordinal.

Recall the concept of
very discrete rank
(or
Hausdorff rank
) of a linear order (see
[
14
, Def. 5.20]
).

Definition 1.12
.

We define inductively for each countable ordinal
\alpha<\omega_{1}
a class
VD_{\alpha}
as follows

•

0,1\in VD_{0}
.

•

Given a linear order
I
of order-type
\omega
,
\omega^{*}
,
\zeta
or
K
, and for each
i\in I
a linear order
L_{i}\in\cup\{VD_{\beta}\mid\beta<\alpha\}
, then
\sum\{L_{i}\mid i\in I\}\in VD_{\alpha}
.

The set
VD=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}VD_{\alpha}
is called the class of (countable)
very discrete linear orders
.
Thus, automatically, given a linear order
L\in VD
, there would be a smallest ordinal
\alpha
, called the
VD
-rank of
L
and denoted
\operatorname{rank}_{VD}(L)
, such that
L\in VD_{\alpha}
.

For example, we find
\operatorname{rank}_{VD}(\omega)=1
,
\operatorname{rank}_{VD}(\omega^{n})=n
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
), and
\operatorname{rank}_{VD}(\omega^{\omega})=\omega
.

Definition 1.13
.

Let
(X,f)
be a dynamical system and take
x,y\in X
with
x\,\mathcal{C}_{\preceq}\,y
. An
order-compatible nested sequence
from
x
to
y
is a sequence
\{C_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of acyclic
\varepsilon_{n}
-chains (
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
) with nested supports
\widehat{C_{n}}\subseteq\widehat{C_{n+1}}
such that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for every
z,w\in\widehat{C_{n}}\setminus\{x,y\}
, if
z
appears no later than
w
in
C_{n}
, namely

\min\{i\in\text{dom}(C_{n})\mid C_{n}(i)=z\}\leq\min\{j\in\text{dom}(C_{n})\mid C_{n}(j)=w\},

then
z
appears no later than
w
in
C_{n+1}
, too.

In
[
12
]
it has been proven that, for any pair
(x,y)\in X^{2}
, the
Emergent Order Spectrum

\Omega_{f}(x,y)
can be introduced:

Definition 1.14

(Emergent Order Spectrum)
.

Let
\{C_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an order-compatible nested sequence from
x
to
y
and set

C=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\widehat{C_{n}}\setminus\{x,y\}.

Define a linear order
\leq_{\infty}
on
C
by

\displaystyle z\leq_{\infty}w\;\Longleftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle z=w\ \text{ or $z$ appears no later than $w$ in $C_{n}$}\ \text{for every $n\in\mathbb{N}$}

\displaystyle\text{such that $z,w\in\widehat{C_{n}}$}.

The
Emergent Order Spectrum
(EOS) of the pair
(x,y)\in X^{2}
is

\displaystyle\Omega_{f}(x,y)=\bigl\{\operatorname{otp}\bigl(C,\leq_{\infty}\bigr)\mid\{C_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ is an order-compatible nested sequence}\ \text{from $x$ to $y$}\bigr\}.

When there is no ambiguity, we write
\Omega(x,y)
for
\Omega_{f}(x,y)
.

The EOS is a topological invariant (see
[
12
, Theorem 6.8]
):

Theorem 1.15
.

Let
(X,f)
and
(Y,g)
be topologically conjugate via a homeomorphism
h:X\to Y
, and set
H=h\times h:X^{2}\to Y^{2}
. Then

\Omega_{f}=\Omega_{g}\circ H\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Omega_{g}=\Omega_{f}\circ H^{-1}.

Equivalently, for all
(x,y)\in X^{2}
,

\Omega_{f}(x,y)=\Omega_{g}\bigl(h(x),h(y)\bigr).

For our aim, we recall two results from
[
12
]
, namely
Theorems 6.9 and 6.13.

Theorem 1.16
.

Let
x,y\in X
.
Then, there is
a finite ordinal
K
such that

K\in\Omega(x,y)
if and only if
y\in\mathcal{O}^{+}(x)
.

Theorem 1.17
.

If
(X,f)
is a transitive dynamical system with
\operatorname{card}(X)=\infty
, then, for every
x,y\in X
, we have
\eta\in\Omega(x,y)
.

In this paper, we investigate how large the EOS can be under natural dynamical hypotheses.
Our main result, Theorem
2.8
, shows that for a transitive homeomorphism on a compact metric space without isolated points of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
, the global spectrum
\Omega(X^{2})
contains every countable scattered order-type together with the countable dense order, and for a comeagre set of pairs
(x,y)
the individual spectrum
\Omega(x,y)
already realizes all countably infinite scattered types.
Thus, chain recurrence, when combined with topological transitivity, encodes the entire Hausdorff hierarchy of countable scattered orders inside a single conjugacy invariant.
The proof combines set-theoretic
\alpha
-structures on orbit classes with Hausdorff’s analysis of scattered linear orders, and uses them to organize families of nested
\varepsilon_{n}
-chains according to their
VD
-rank and to assemble them via sums indexed by
K
,
\omega
,
\omega^{*}
, or
\zeta
.

We emphasize that our arguments are self-contained with respect to the EOS:
nowhere do we assume a priori an existence theory for the Emergent Order Spectrum in the transitive setting.
Instead, the proof of our main result is constructive, and, in the process of realizing the desired order-types, it simultaneously yields the existence of a sequence of order-compatible nested
\varepsilon_{n}
-chains for transitive homeomorphisms.
In this sense, Theorem
2.8
can be viewed as an independent existence result for the EOS under our hypotheses.

Results

Let us recall that a
scattered
linear order is a linear order that does not contain a subset order-isomorphic to the rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
.
A classical representation theorem by Hausdorff provides a general expression of linearly ordered sets in terms of scattered sets (see
[
14
, Theorem 4.9]
):

Theorem 2.1

(Hausdorff)
.

Any linear order
L
is a dense sum of scattered linear orders; that is, there is a dense linear order
L_{*}
and a map
h
from
L_{*}
to scattered linear orders such that

L=\sum\{h(i)\mid i\in L_{*}\}.

In turn, scattered sets admit a complete classification, also due to Hausdorff (see
[
14
, Theorem 5.24]
):

Theorem 2.2

(Hausdorff)
.

The set of countable scattered linear orders is the smallest set of orders that contains singleton orders and is closed under concatenation indexed by K,
\omega
, or
\omega^{*}
.

Let us introduce the concept of
\alpha
-structure and prove a set-theoretic technical result (Lemma
2.6
) that we will use in the proof of Theorem
2.8
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
M
be a set of cardinality at least
\aleph_{1}
.
For
\alpha\geq 2
a countable ordinal, we define recursively on
\alpha
an
\alpha
-
structure

A_{\alpha}
in

(a family of families nested to depth
\alpha
) and its support
\operatorname{supp}(A_{\alpha})
as:

•

\alpha=2
:
A_{2}
is a countably infinite subset of

and
\operatorname{supp}(A_{2})=A_{2}
.

•

\alpha=\beta+1
:
A_{\alpha}=\{B_{i}\mid i<\omega\}
, where each
B_{i}
is a
\beta
-structure such that the
\operatorname{supp}(B_{i})
are pairwise disjoint and we set

\operatorname{supp}(A_{\alpha})=\bigcup_{i<\omega}\operatorname{supp}(B_{i}).

•

\alpha

\{2\leq\gamma_{i}<\alpha\mid i<\omega\}
be an increasing sequence cofinal in
\alpha
.
We define
A_{\alpha}
as
A_{\alpha}=\{B_{i}\mid i<\omega\}
, where each
B_{i}
is a
\gamma_{i}
-structure such that the
\operatorname{supp}(B_{i})
are pairwise disjoint and we set

\operatorname{supp}(A_{\alpha})=\bigcup_{i<\omega}\operatorname{supp}(B_{i}).

Definition 2.4
.

Given an
\alpha
-structure
A
and a finite set

, we define the
\alpha
-structure
A\,\dot{-}\,F
inductively over
\alpha
.

•

If
\alpha=2
, then

•

Remark 2.5
.

Given an
\alpha
-structure

\alpha

.

Let
M
be a set with
|M|\geq\aleph_{1}
. There exists a family

\{A_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
of structures, where each

is an
\alpha
-structure in

Proof.

Fix a subset

be a partition of

in
\aleph_{1}
sets of cardinality
\aleph_{1}
.
We construct the family
\{A_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\omega_{1}\}

is an
\alpha
-structure with support contained in

:

•

\alpha=2
: Set

to be a countably infinite subset of

. Its support is

.

Let now

Let us treat the case

∎

We are now ready to prove our main result.
The proof proceeds in two main stages.
First, for a generic pair
(x,y)\in X^{2}
with disjoint two–sided orbits, transitivity and the density of forward and backward orbits allow us to construct order-compatible nested families of
\varepsilon_{n}
-chains that realize the basic building blocks of countably infinite scattered orders, namely order-types of the form
\omega+k
,
h+\omega^{*}
, and
h+\zeta+k
.
This is done in Lemma
2.7
.
Then, in the main Theorem, we organize orbit classes into suitable “
\alpha
-structures” indexed by countable ordinals
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, and use these to carry out a transfinite induction on the Hausdorff rank: at stage
\alpha
we assign disjoint orbit classes to the scattered orders of rank
{}_{VD}\leq\alpha
and concatenate the corresponding chains along sums indexed by
K
,
\omega
,
\omega^{*}
or
\zeta
, thereby realizing arbitrary countable scattered order-types in
\Omega(x,y)
for comeagre many pairs
(x,y)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(X,f)
be a compact dynamical system such that
X
has no isolated points,
|X|=\mathfrak{c}
, and
f
is a transitive homeomorphism of
X
. Let
\mathcal{I}=\{x\in X\,|\,\overline{\mathcal{O}^{-}(x)}=\overline{\mathcal{O}^{+}(x)}=X\}
be the set of points with both a dense backward orbit and a dense forward orbit. Set

\Gamma:=\left\{\omega+k,\,h+\omega^{*},\,h+\zeta+k\,|\,k,h\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}.

If
x,y\in\cal I
are such that
\mathcal{O}(x)\cap\mathcal{O}(y)=\varnothing
,
then

\Gamma\subseteq\Omega(x,y).

More precisely, for every
\xi\in\Gamma
, there exists a sequence of order-compatible nested chains
\{C_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that, setting
C=\big(\bigcup_{n}\widehat{C_{n}}\big)\setminus\{x,y\}
, the order-type of
(C,\leq_{\infty})
is
\xi
.

Proof.

Fix a sequence of positive real numbers
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges to zero monotonically.
Let us distinguish three different cases.

1.

Let
\xi=\omega+k
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Since
x\in\mathcal{I}
, it is possible to find a strictly increasing sequence of natural numbers
\{h_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

d(f^{h_{n}+1}(x),f^{-k}(y))<\varepsilon_{n}\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1)

For every
n\in\mathbb{N},
we set

C_{n}:\,x,\,f(x),\dots,\,f^{h_{n}-1}(x),\,f^{h_{n}}(x),\,f^{-k}(y),\,f^{-k+1}(y),\dots,\,f^{-1}(y),\,y.

By (
1
),
C_{n}
is an
\varepsilon_{n}
-chain and, by construction,
\widehat{C_{n}}\subseteq\widehat{C_{n+1}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since

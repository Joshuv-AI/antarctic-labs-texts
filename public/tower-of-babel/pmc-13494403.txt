Homozygosity of germ line JAK2 46/1 haplotype contributes to phenotype and outcome diversity in polycythemia vera
Authors: Paola Guglielmelli, Chiara Maccari, Manjola Balliu, Giuseppe Gaetano Loscocco, Naseema Gangat, Valentina Boldrini, Giulio Capecchi, Leonardo Signori, Alessio Enderti, Giada Rotunno, Chiara Paoli, Sofia Danesi, Cecilia Matteoli, Francesco Mannelli, Tiziano Barbui, Ayalew Tefferi, Alessandro Maria Vannucchi
Published: 2026

TO THE EDITOR:

The JAK2 V617F mutation is the most common driver mutation in myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPN), followed by mutations in CALR, MPL, and JAK2 exon 12.1 Although MPN are typically sporadic, ∼25% of cases are associated with the low-penetrance germ line haplotype JAK2 46/1 (GGCC),2,3 with additional susceptibility loci reported in MECOM, TERT, and MYB.4 The 46/1 haplotype is typically defined by up to 4 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in complete linkage disequilibrium across a 250–kilobase pair region on 9p24.1, encompassing JAK2, INSL4, and INLS6.3,5,6 It accounts for 70% to 80% of JAK2 V617F alleles,5,7, 8, 9, 10 whereas its association with JAK2 wild-type MPN is less consistent.5,9,11 The haplotype has been linked to higher JAK2V617F mutation burden (or variant allele frequency [VAF])12 and its nullizigosity to reduced survival in myelofibrosis (MF).13 This study evaluates the phenotypic correlates and prognostic impact of the 46/1 haplotype in a large cohort of patients with polycythemia vera (PV).

A total of 756 patients (95% Caucasian race; 56.7% male) with JAK2V617F-mutated PV, diagnosed according to International Consensus Classification (ICC) criteria,14 were analyzed. The 46/1 JAK2 haplotype was assessed by allele-specific polymerase chain reaction (rs12343867 TaqMan assay; “C” allele corresponding to 46/1 haplotype8; see supplemental Methods for details). The study was approved by local institutional review board (MYNERVA project number 21267), and all patients provided written informed consent; the study was also conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Clinical and laboratory data, as well as blood and bone marrow samples, were collected within 3 years of diagnosis, and patients were followed until death or last follow-up (1 June 2025). Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS. Continuous variables are reported as median (range), and categorical variables are reported as frequency (percentage). Group comparisons were performed using Mann-Whitney, Kruskal-Wallis, or χ2 test, as appropriate. Cox regression analysis was used to assess risk factors for thrombosis and progression to post-PV MF or blast phase. MF-free survival (MFS), overall survival, and leukemia-free survival were estimated by Kaplan-Meyer method and compared using log-rank test.

Overall, 573 patients (75.8%) carried the 46/1 haplotype allele: 310 (41.0%) were homozygous (CC), and 263 (34.8%) were heterozygous (CT), whereas 183 (24.2%) were nullizygous (TT). Preliminary analyses comparing the 3 genotypes showed that baseline characteristics and outcomes differed significantly between CC and both CT and TT groups, whereas CT and TT were comparable (supplemental Table 1). Notably, the CC+CT group did not differ significantly from TT patients, except for older age, a higher proportion of males, a greater prevalence of pruritus, and higher JAK2 V617F VAF (supplemental Table 2); the latter finding is consistent with reported association of C allele with higher VAF. Given the apparent impact of C allele homozygosity, subsequent analyses compared CC vs CT+TT patients. CC patients were younger (P = .04) and showed higher leukocyte counts, hemoglobin/hematocrit, lactate dehydrogenase levels, and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (all P < .001), along with lower platelet counts (Table 1). They also had higher rates of splenomegaly (38.7% vs 28.9%; P = .008), pruritus (49.3% vs 33.4%; P < .01), and constitutional symptoms (P = .03). Mean JAK2 V617F VAF was 45.1% (±25.2%) overall and was significantly higher in CC vs CT+TT patients (59.8% ± 24.7% vs 34.8% ± 19.9%; P < .001), with VAF ≥50% in 63.8% vs 17.0% (P < .001; Figure 1A). After adjustment for JAK2V617F VAF (as a continuous variable), leukocyte count (P = .04), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (P = .035), hemoglobin (P = .038), and hematocrit (P = .001) remained significantly associated with the CC haplotype.

At diagnosis, 134 patients (17.7) had experienced ≥1 thrombotic event, with no difference across 46/1 genotypes (19.1% in TT+CT vs 14.5% in CC; P = .25); distribution of arterial and venous events was similar. During follow-up, thrombosis occurred in 121 patients (16.0%), including 45 recurrences (Table 1). Venous thrombosis (VT) was more frequent in CC patients (11.6% vs 6.5% in TT+CT; P = .01), with shorter VT-free survival (hazard ratio [HR], 1.5; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.1-2.0; P = .0039; Figure 1B). This association remained significant after adjustment for JAK2V617F VAF (as a continuous variable; P = .040). Among patients with JAK2V617F VAF ≥50%, the CC genotype was associated with a higher risk of incident VT (HR, 3.2; 95% CI, 1.1-9.6; P = .02) and shorter VT-free survival (P = .038).

Progression to post-PV MF occurred in 142 patients (18.7%), with a higher incidence in CC vs TT+CT patients (29.4% vs 11.4%; P < .001). The median MFS was 20.6 months (95% CI, 18.2-21.1) in CC, 24.7 months (95% CI, 18.4-31.0) in TT, and 29.0 months (95% CI, 22.2-6.3) in CT patients (P < .001; Figure 1C). Using TT+CT as reference (MFS, 29.2 months; 95% CI, 23.4-35.0), CC patients had a higher risk of progression (HR, 1.94; 95% CI, 1.3-2.7). In univariate analysis, MF progression was associated with CC genotype (HR, 1.4; 95% CI, 1.2-1.7; P < .001), leukocyte count ≥15 × 109/L (HR, 2.2; 95% CI, 1.3-3.7; P = .002), palpable splenomegaly (HR, 1.7; 95% CI, 1.2-2.5; P = .003), and JAK2V617F VAF ≥50% (HR, 1.7; 95% CI, 1.4-2.1; P < .001). However, the association between CC genotype and progression was not retained in multivariable analysis when accounting for mutation burden, suggesting a predominant role of JAK2 V617F VAF. Accordingly, in patients with VAF ≥50%, the CC genotype was no longer significantly associated with MF progression, whereas splenomegaly and prior VT remained independent predictors.

With a follow-up of 7.6 years (0.3-38.1), 129 patients (17.1%) died, with similar rates across genotypes (18.1% CC, 17.5% TT, and 15.6% CT). Progression to blast phase occurred in 13 patients (1.7%), without significant differences between groups (1.9% CC vs 1.5% TT+CT; P = .70); notably, no cases were observed among nullizygous (TT) patients.

In this large cohort of patients with JAK2 V617F-mutated PV, the 46/1 haplotype, particularly in the homozygous (CC) state, was associated with distinct clinical phenotype, higher JAK2 V617F mutation burden, reinforcing previous reports,15 and an increased risk of incident VT. Notably, the CC genotype remained independently associated with VT even after adjustment for JAK2 V617F VAF. In contrast, its association with progression to post-PV MF appeared largely mediated by mutation burden, suggesting a predominant role of JAK2 V617F VAF in disease evolution.16, 17, 18 These findings are consistent with emerging biological data linking the 46/1 haplotype to disease manifestations and progression, beyond its well-established role in MPN susceptibility.19 In some studies,20,21 the 46/1 homozygous haplotype, in both healthy individuals and JAK2 V617F-mutated patients, was associated with upregulation of programmed death-1 receptor ligand (PD-L1) gene, located proximal to JAK2, through a distinct chromatin conformation compared with nullizygous individuals. Furthermore, PD-L1 expression in CD34+ cells from JAK2 V617F patients correlated with mutation burden22 and JAK2 gene copy number in cases with trisomy 9.23 Elevated PD-L1 levels may promote and sustain an inflammatory microenvironment, potentially driven by increased leukocytes counts and dysfunctional monocytes, which are implicated in vascular complications and fibrosis deposition.24 Inflammation and immune escape mechanisms may facilitate expansion of the JAK2 V617F–mutated clone and promote genomic instability. No significant impact on overall survival or blast transformation was observed. A major limitation of the study is the lack of information on 9pUPD status, which may have hindered unequivocal attribution of the 46/1 haplotype, given that JAK2 V617F is preferentially acquired in cis with this haplotype; further studies are needed to address this issue. An additional limitation is the inability to assess the impact of different treatments on outcome.

Overall, these findings add to the growing evidence that germ line variants, including sex-specific factors,25 contribute to shaping MPN phenotype and clinical course.

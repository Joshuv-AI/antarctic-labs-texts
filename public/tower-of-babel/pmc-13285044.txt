Hybrid metaheuristic optimization of a DeepFusionNet for plant leaf disease diagnosis and recommendation
Authors: Shantilata Palei, Puspanjali Mohapatra, Soubhagya Ranjan Mallick, Princy Diwan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Early diagnosis of plant leaf diseases plays an important role in protecting crop yields and supporting sustainable agriculture. This paper proposes an improved DeepFusionNet model optimized through a hybrid Flower Pollination Algorithm and Butterfly Optimization Algorithm, balancing global exploration with local refinement for faster and more stable convergence. The model combines DenseNet201 and MobileNetV2 by compressing their final convolutional feature maps with 1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times $$\end{document}1 convolutions and fusing them along the channel dimension to form a compact and discriminative representation. This fused representation is then classified using a Random Forest classifier. This framework consistently achieves high accuracy on all eight datasets, with performance ranging between 97.07% and 99.66%. Extensive experiments are performed that include statistical validation, convergence studies, and reliability tests to prove the robustness of the approach. Furthermore, to make it practically useful, the whole system is embedded into a mobile application capable of real-time disease detection and providing actionable recommendations to farmers for the effective treatment and prevention of diseases.

Introduction

Plant leaf diseases remain a severe issue in agriculture, which causes significant reduction of crop yield and quality [1]. Conventionally, farmers rely on a manual system of visual inspection to identify diseases, but this is time-consuming, subjective, and therefore not applicable to large-scale farming. Moreover, the visual pattern of many diseases is very similar, and it is difficult to accurately pinpoint the exact category of a disease from simple observation [2]. In agricultural domains where computer vision applications are becoming more prevalent, the effectiveness of the classification task relies significantly on how efficiently the features are extracted and whether the selected classifier is appropriate for the task [3]. Since each disease has its unique visual appearance, feature extraction accuracy plays an important role in effective classification. Recently, significant advancements in image processing and deep learning (DL) have notably enhanced the performance of disease identification systems. CNNs outperform traditional machine-learning algorithms through automatic learning of discriminative features from leaf images [4]. The performance of the CNN-based models is highly sensitive with respect to their hyperparameters, and poor tuning can decrease the accuracy [5]. Metaheuristic optimization approaches address the aforementioned issue by effectively searching for ideal hyperparameter values, thus improving reliability and computational efficiency in real-world agricultural applications. Also, the integration of recommendation systems and detection models will help farmers get timely and precise illness assessments as well as practical advice based on noticed symptoms [6].

Diagnosis of diseases in plant leaves is still a challenging operation, as even slight delays or wrong diagnosis may lead to severe loss in agricultural yield [7]. Even though machine learning and DL-based approaches have considerably enhanced the automated recognition of illness, their success rate still remains strongly dependent on how accurately the features are retrieved, how efficiently the models are created, and how precisely the hyperparameters are controlled. Poorly optimized models usually demonstrate poor accuracy and generalization across varied situations. Besides, many farmers are still deprived of real-time, data-driven solutions that would facilitate fast and accurate disease assessment. To overcome these limitations, integrating DL with metaheuristic optimization stands as a feasible strategy because it allows for more robust hyperparameter variation and enhances overall model performance. A mobile-based recommendation system can aid farmers in providing real-time disease detection, treatment guidance, and preventive strategies.

Related works

[8] presented a review on the impact of plant leaf diseases and the need for early detection. Even though DNA-based and serological methods assist in diagnosis, they are slow and not very effective in asymptomatic stages. Biosensors, spectroscopy-based remote sensing, and biophotonic sensors represent advanced techniques allowing fast and field-based detection by enhancing disease management. Singh et al.[9] reviewed studies dealing with imaging methods for the early detection of fungal and viral infections. [10] proposed DenseNet201 coupled with an SVM for the categorization of maize diseases. [11] proposed DenseNet201Plus, an improved DL architecture for detecting leaf diseases. [12] proposed MobileNetv2-YOLOv3 to identify tomato leaf spots. Accuracy was enhanced by incorporating the GIoU loss function. The architecture is light and pre-trained using mixup training along with transfer learning, which enhances its flexibility for mobile applications.

In order to improve feature extraction and classification accuracy, Sutaji et al. [13] proposed an ensemble of MobileNetV2 and XceptionNet for illness detection. On the PlantVillage dataset, the model outperformed seven cutting-edge CNNs with high accuracy. With fewer parameters than other ensemble models, it provides a lightweight design appropriate for mobile integration. A plant leaf disease detection model utilizing deep CNN features with SVM and logistic regression ensemble classifiers was proposed by Hassan et al. [14]. Singh et al. [15] developed a multi-disease prediction system using metaheuristics and ML models. Data preprocessing and normalization improved data quality before training classifiers like decision trees, XGBoost, LightGBM, random forest, and neural networks. Models were optimized using grid, random, hyperband, and genetic search, with the random search. Nagaraju et al. [16] used various metaheuristic methods including Bee colony, ACO, PSO, Firefly, and GA, for prediction work. Jain et al. [17] introduced a binary Memetic Salp Swarm Optimization Algorithm (MSSOA) for feature selection on UCI benchmark datasets. Prasanna et al. [18] presented a butterfly algorithm for health disease prediction. Vijaya et al. [19–21] conducted significant research on PSO. Taji et al. [22] proposed a hybrid DL framework for apple and maize disease classification. The approach combines two pre-trained DL models with hybrid preprocessing, ensemble feature engineering (using texture and deep features), and metaheuristic algorithms to optimize feature vectors. The optimized features are classified using ML algorithms. This study enhances food security by improving early disease detection and crop quality.

Kumar et al. [23] presented an open-source, user-friendly web application designed to assist Indian farmers with crop and fertilizer recommendations, plant leaf disease forecasting, and an interactive news feed. It also incorporates interpretability techniques to explain the predictions made by the disease detection model, aiming to improve decision-making, enhance crop production, and promote sustainable agricultural practices. Recent studies show that optimization-guided DL frameworks significantly improve performance when modeling nonlinear, high-dimensional data. In particular, hybrid metaheuristic optimization combined with deep feature fusion has emerged as an effective paradigm for enhancing convergence stability, feature discriminability, and generalization.

Study design

This section describes the dataset description, preprocessing techniques applied to the collected data, and the proposed methodology.

Dataset description

In this study, an automated Apple, Citrus, Corn, Grape, Mango, Potato, Rice, and Tomato leaf disease classification has been implemented. The Apple, Corn, Grape, Potato, Rice, and Tomato leaf datasets were collected from the Kaggle database, while the Citrus and Mango leaf datasets were obtained from the Mendeley database [34–41]. The samples of each class of datasets are shown in Fig. 1.Fig. 1Samples of plant leaf diseases dataset

Samples of plant leaf diseases dataset

Dataset preprocessing entails several steps to get the data ready for analysis. Decoding and converting the gathered images into the JPEG format ensures consistency. Rotating images by augmentation at 30, 60, 90, 120, 180, 220, 250, and 270 degrees to improve dataset variety and model resilience. A consistent size of [224, 224] pixels is used to scale images. Color conversion is used to improve processing, such as when RGB photos are converted to grayscale or other color schemes. These techniques helped balance the sample sizes to 5867 (Apple), 2189 (Citrus), 4184 (Corn), 18060 (Grape), 4000 (Mango), 4072 (Potato), 4026 (Rice), and 11000 (Tomato) leaf images. In the end, the dataset is divided into 20% for testing and validation and 80% for training.

Methodology

In order to categorize leaf diseases in various plants, this research presents a DL-based CNN model called DeepFusionNet. To reduce the trial-and-error process in hyperparameter tuning, this study employed a hybrid optimization method combining FPA and BOA. To improve the balance between exploring and utilizing the solution space, a hybrid FPA-BOA metaheuristic algorithm is suggested. This technique enhances the performance of the DeepFusionNet model.Fig. 2Proposed DeepFusionNet model for plant leaf diseases detect

DeepFusionNet model

The proposed DeepFusionNet is an algorithm that is aimed at the multi-class classification of plant leaf diseases on the basis of feature fusion at the convolution level. It combines the dense space and semantic representations of the DenseNet201 and MobileNetV2 and then carries out RF classification. This hybrid architecture guarantees better learning of features, better discriminative ability as well as better robustness without compromising the computational efficiency. The suggested system makes use of a hybrid FPA-BOA mechanism that effectively balances exploration and exploitation during hyperparameter tuning, in contrast to previous research that rely on traditional CNN models, straightforward ensemble combinations, or individual optimization techniques. Faster convergence and better generalization performance are the outcomes of this combined optimization methodology, which overcomes the typical drawbacks of single metaheuristic techniques in earlier research.

Although single optimization has been applied, as in, e.g., the study by [34] and other works [30] and [44], lightweight networks with minimal or no parameter optimization were typically employed, and, unlike in other networks, sophisticated feature fusion and reliable hyperparameter optimization were typically not part of them. The proposed DeepFusionNet will make sure that spatial and semantic features remain intact in order to have a more informative display through feature fusion at the convolution level between MobileNetV2 and DenseNet201. Conclusion

A hybrid FPA-BOA-driven DeepFusionNet model for accurate plant leaf disease diagnosis across datasets comprising eight plant species is presented in this work. The suggested approach outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods with a high classification accuracy of 97.07%−99.66%. The paper highlights the system’s practical applicability in addition to its excellent performance. By enabling users to undertake on-site illness diagnosis and obtain treatment recommendations along with preventive measures, the model is further implemented in a mobile application that enhances accessibility and successfully supports farmers and agricultural practitioners.

Despite its advantages, the study’s reliance on publicly accessible datasets collected in controlled settings has several drawbacks. As a result, several real-world variations–such as changing lighting and complex backgrounds–are not accurately depicted. Future work will concentrate on expanding to a real-time dataset by adding larger, more varied samples that are taken in the field. Determining the model’s environmental resilience will also need assessing how well it performs in various climatic zones (e.g., plants from hot versus cold zones). Real-time monitoring and decision-making may also be further supported by combining IoT-enabled smart farming infrastructures with Explainable AI approaches.

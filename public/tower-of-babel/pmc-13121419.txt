Effects of graded dietary black soldier fly larvae powder inclusion on semen quality and serum biochemical indices of Luxi game breeder roosters
Authors: Shaojie Cao, Yanmin Guo, Shiwei Li, Jiale Guo, Hongwei Yin, Xianlin Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe present study investigated the effects of graded dietary inclusion of black soldier fly larvae powder (BSFLP) as a partial replacement of soybean meal on semen quality and oxidative status in Luxi game breeding cock. Five levels of BSFLP inclusion diets (isocaloric and isonitrogenous) at 0, 5, 10, 15, or 20% were fed to Luxi game breeding cocks (n = 40) to assess semen quality parameters, antioxidant enzyme activities, and biochemical indicators. The results showed that, compared with the control group, dietary supplementation with 10–20% BSFLP improved sperm motility, viability, and acrosome integrity in Luxi game breeding cocks (p < 0.05). Moreover, sperm linearity, swing, curvilinear motion speed, linear motion speed, and lateral swing amplitude were improved (p < 0.05). However, no statistically significant effects were found for progressive motility, average path velocity (VAP), mean angular displacement of the sperm head trajectory (MAD), or beat cross frequency (BCF) (p > 0.05). Compared with the control group, the activities of CAT, GSH, GR, and SOD in semen were significantly increased (p < 0.05), and the content of MDA in semen was significantly decreased (p < 0.05) when 5–20% BSFLP was added to the diet. Additionally, there were no significant differences in serum ALT, AST, TP, ALB, and ALP among Luxi game breeding cocks supplemented with 10–20% BSFLP (p > 0.05). Polynomial contrast analysis revealed significant linear responses for several reproductive and antioxidant parameters, whereas quadratic effects were not detected. Biochemical indicators were unaffected by the inclusion of BSFLP, suggesting that dietary BSFLP up to 20% did not adversely affect liver-related or metabolic parameters under the conditions of this study. Hence, the present study concludes that the dietary inclusion of 5–20% BSFLP can serve as an effective alternative protein source to enhance semen quality and antioxidant enzyme activity in the Luxi game breeding cock.Keywords: Alternative source of protein, Insect meal, Replacement of soybean, Poultry reproduction

Introduction

Luxi game chicken (fighting breed) is an indigenous poultry breed produced in the southwest of Shandong Province. This is a tall multipurpose breed with strong fighting ability, reared for ornamental, entertainment, and edible purposes. In 2015, it was included in the National Catalogue of Livestock and Poultry Genetic Resources. The Luxi game chicken is renowned for its disease resistance, and consumers favor its meat due to its flavor and nutritional value. However, limited adoption by breeders may be attributed to the breed’s slow growth rate and relatively low reproductive performance. Therefore, improving the reproductive efficiency of Luxi game breeding cocks, along with identifying cost-effective feed ingredients, has become an important focus of current research (Bi et al., 2017; Chen GuanJun et al., 2012).

The primary challenge facing the global poultry industry is the scarcity of protein feed resources. It is well established that soybean and corn meals account for >60% of poultry feed ingredients, providing protein and energy (Ahiwe et al., 2024). Therefore, adopting inexpensive and efficient alternative energy and protein sources in poultry rations may be a sustainable option for the poultry industry (Nassar et al., 2023).. Moreover, feeding is the factor with the most significant environmental impact in the poultry sector, accounting for up to 82% of the total climate change. This factor supports the narrative for adopting environmentally-friendly feeding practices to reduce the impact of climate change associated with poultry production (Andretta et al., 2021). Soybean meal, as a protein source, is one of the most frequently used ingredients in poultry feed formulation (Leinonen et al., 2013). Currently, China is the largest consumer of soybeans worldwide, with an estimated annual import of 100 million tons (Jiang et al., 2025). This heavy reliance on soybean imports raises concerns about the potential vulnerability of its future supply. Furthermore, there are also concerns about the environmental repercussions linked with its production. These concerns encouraged the exploration of alternative protein sources in poultry rations. Edible insects have gained particular attention because they provide a natural source of protein and essential nutrients (Józefiak et al., 2016).

Insects are considered innovative feed sources due to their nutritional, environmental, and economic benefits. The growing demand for animal protein, the efficient use of land and water, and the limitations of non-renewable energy sources highlight the global importance of edible insects (Hancz et al., 2024). The BSFLP is rich in proteins, fatty acids, and vitamins, offers high feed utilization efficiency, and can be easily incorporated into conventional poultry diets without the need for specialized processing or feeding management (Elahi et al., 2022; Sajid et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2020).

The Hermetia illucens, commonly known as the black soldier fly, is a saprophytic insect belonging to the family Stratiomyidae, order Diptera, and genus Hermetia (Tomberlin & van Huis, 2020; Wang & Shelomi, 2017). Due to its short breeding cycle, rapid growth, low cost, and low toxicity, H. illucens has been widely studied by researchers (Alifian et al., 2025; Dafri et al., 2025). The dried, pulverized black soldier fly larvae contain high levels of crude protein (42–48%), fat (31–35%), essential minerals (calcium 4.8–5.1%, phosphorus 0.6–0.63%), and bioactive vitamins such as vitamin B1, niacin, and vitamin E (Xu et al., 2014). The high protein content provides essential amino acids necessary for spermatogenesis and structural protein synthesis in sperm cells (Xue et al., 2022). Lipids contribute to membrane integrity and serve as an important energy source for sperm motility (Bond et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026). Minerals such as calcium and phosphorus play critical roles in sperm activation and the acrosome reaction (Jiwuba et al., 2020). Furthermore, B-complex vitamins function as enzymatic cofactors in mitochondrial energy metabolism, supporting ATP production required for sperm motility (Gonçalves & Portari, 2021), while vitamin E acts as a lipid-soluble antioxidant that protects sperm membranes from oxidative damage (Ogwuegbu et al., 2022). Together, these nutritional components may contribute to improved reproductive performance. As a potential source of high-quality protein, BSFLP has been found to have a good effect on the growth performance, reproductive performance, and immunity of various fish by adding a certain proportion of BSFLP to the diet (Barroso et al., 2014; Dietz & Liebert, 2018; Peng et al., 2022; Peng et al., 2021; Xiao et al., 2018; Xie et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2021). Adding 7%−10% BSFLP to the diet of pigs can significantly increase the daily weight gain of piglets and improve the immunity and growth rate of pigs (Crosbie et al., 2021; Kar et al., 2021; Kawasaki et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2022). Similarly, its dietary supplementation can improve the growth performance in broilers, accelerate slaughter rate, increase the reproductive hormone content, antioxidant capacity of reproductive organs of laying hens, and improve the egg production performance of layers (Al-Qazzaz et al., 2016; Dabbou et al., 2018a; Han et al., 2023; Marono et al., 2017; Mohammed et al., 2017). Given the distinct genetic background, reproductive physiology, and slower metabolic and growth rates of Luxi game cocks compared to commercial broilers or layers, their energy and micronutrient requirements for optimal sperm function may differ substantially. Therefore, investigating the impact of BSFL-derived nutrients on semen quality in this breed is critical for establishing breed-specific nutritional strategies to enhance reproductive performance.

In the current research, the effect of BSFLP on the semen quality of the Luxi game breeding cocks was investigated by adding BSFLP to the diet, which provided a theoretical basis for improving the semen quality of the Luxi game breeding cock and developing new feed.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

The study was approved by the ethical committee of the College of Agriculture and Bioengineering, Heze University, Heze, China, under P#ZR2019MC038. The Luxi breeding cock used in this experiment was selected from Juancheng Hongxiang Animal Husbandry Co., Ltd. (China Luxi game chicken conservation farm).

Experimental animals and grouping

Semen samples were collected from forty healthy Luxi game breeding cocks, 45 weeks of age, with an average body weight of 3.42 ± 0.22 kg, using the abdominal massage technique twice per week. Each collection was treated as a repeated measurement; to avoid pseudoreplication, the mean value of each semen parameter per rooster across all collections was used for statistical analysis. The Luxi game breeding cock was divided into 5 groups, each containing 8 chickens, and kept in cages of 70 × 60 × 75 cm in a control shed poultry farm. All experimental roosters were reared under the same environmental and management conditions and fed an isonitrogenous, isocaloric diet with ad libitum drinking water (Table 1). Previously, the inclusion of BSFL in broiler feed at 5% and 15% showed beneficial effects on growth performance and meat quality (Dabbou et al., 2018b; Schiavone et al., 2017). However, further increases in BSFL levels and its replacement with soybean in poultry rations at 50%, 75%, and 100% had deleterious effects on poultry health and production (Murawska et al., 2021). Therefore, in the present study, BSFL at 0%, 5%, 10%, 15%, and 20% was included. The birds in group 1 were fed 5% BSFLP, group 2 10%, group 3 15%, and group 4 20%. The dietary inclusion levels were designed as a graded partial replacement of soybean meal and bran, rather than as a low-dose supplement. All diets were formulated to be isonitrogenous and isocaloric, ensuring that differences among treatments reflected the nutritional characteristics of BSFLP rather than changes in crude protein or energy supply. The pre-trial period was 28 days for adaptation to the feed, and the feeding trial lasted for 12 weeks. The control group was offered a routine feed with 0% BSFLP. The BSFLP was purchased from the Heze pet market. While commercial sourcing may introduce variability in nutrient composition, microbial safety, and batch consistency, BSFLP from this supplier is widely used in poultry nutrition research and has been reported to contain substantial protein, fat, vitamins, and minerals suitable for experimental diets. Although the proximate composition of the specific batch used was not independently analyzed, previous studies provide a reliable reference for its nutritional profile (Xu et al., 2014). The experimental chickens were offered a standardized routine feed prepared for Luxi game breeding cock. The experimental roosters were trained to collect semen two weeks before the start of semen collection through a systematic training protocol. Briefly, the cocks were acclimatized to human handling and stimulated sexual responsiveness. The training was conducted for two weeks before the initiation of experimental collections. Firstly (days 1–6), the birds were gently handled daily to reduce stress and fear. Each cock was manually restrained for short periods, and a gentle massage of the lower abdominal and cloacal regions was performed to familiarize the birds with the procedure. In the second week (days 7–14), the sexual stimulation was intensified by applying rhythmic dorso-abdominal massage on alternate days. Initially, we found either low semen volume or absence of ejaculate. However, with repeated handling and stimulation, most cocks developed a consistent ejaculation response, and only cocks with a normal response and semen ejaculates were selected for the downstream study. The semen samples were collected after 12 weeks of feeding from each cock in the early morning (thrice a week), before feeding, through dorso-abdominal massage as described previously (Du et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2022). Before semen collection, the feathers around the cloaca of the breeding cock tail were trimmed and disinfected to avoid contamination of semen.

Determination of semen quality and enzyme activity in semen

After semen collection from each cock, the samples were diluted at a ratio of 1:100 (semen: extender) using a modified Ringer’s solution containing sodium chloride (0.68 g), potassium chloride (0.0173 g), calcium chloride (0.0064 g), magnesium sulfate (0.0025 g), and sodium bicarbonate (0.245 g) per 100 mL of distilled water. The higher dilution ratio (1:100) was applied solely for analytical purposes to obtain an optimal sperm concentration for microscopic/CASA evaluation and was not intended for semen preservation or fertility application. The dilution was performed using an isotonic buffered solution to minimize osmotic stress, and the samples were evaluated immediately after dilution to avoid prolonged exposure. for further analysis through an automatic sperm quality analyzer (HS-9000, Chengdu, China, Puhua Technology Co., Ltd) (Aghaei et al., 2010). The analyzer was used to determine the sperm motility, vitality, linearity, swing, forwardness, curve movement speed, linear movement speed, average path speed, average movement angle, side swing amplitude, and whipping frequency. The samples were analyzed with six replications, and mean values were recorded for further analysis.

Determination of sperm viability

Sperm viability was calculated as the percentage of live, active sperm among the total 200 spermatozoa counted per sample. Eosin-nigrosine staining was used to assess sperm viability, as described previously (Mussa et al., 2023). In this method, the live sperm remained unstained and appeared colorless against the dark nigrosine background, while the dead sperm took up the eosin dye and appeared pink or red. This differential staining allows for accurate discrimination between viable and non-viable sperm under a light microscope. Briefly, a 10 µl semen drop was mixed with 20 µl of eosin-nigrosin stain on a glass slide. The slide was air-dried, and >200 sperm were counted under the microscope to determine the proportion of viable sperm.

Determination of acrosome integrity rate

The rate of the acrosomal integrity of the sperm was calculated through Giemsa staining microscopy with slight modifications, as previously described (Zhao et al., 2010; Zhao et al., 2015). A thin smear of semen was prepared on a microscopic slide, air-dried for 5–10 min, and fixed with formalin phosphate fixative for 15 min. After washing and drying, the slides were stained with Giemsa for 90 min, rinsed with tap water, and dried. The sperms were observed under a bright-field microscope (LEIKA DM IRB), and the number of sperms with intact acrosomes was counted.

Determination of antioxidant enzyme activity and MDA in semen

The enzymatic activity was determined as previously described (Zhao et al., 2015). Briefly, the semen was centrifuged at 3000 g for 10 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected. The glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) activity was quantified in U/L using a GSH-Px kit (Nanjing Jiancheng, China) as per the manufacturer's instructions. The glutathione reductase (GR) activity was measured as previously described (Marti et al., 2007). Briefly, a reaction mixture of 0.6 mM oxidized glutathione, sodium phosphate buffer, 0.5 mM EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid), and 85 mM NADPH+H+ was prepared. The activity of the GR enzyme was expressed as U/L. The Catalase (CAT) activity was quantified by the method previously described (Goth, 1991). A 0.3 mL sample was incubated in 1.7 mL 65 mM hydrogen peroxide (substrate) in 50 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.0) at 37.5 °C for 60 s. A 1.0 mL 32.4 mM ammonium molybdate was used to terminate the reaction. The absorbance of the hydrogen peroxide was measured at 405 nm, and the CAT activity was expressed as U/mL. The superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity was quantified as previously described (Vives‐Bauza et al., 2007). The semen sample was diluted 1:5 in phosphate-buffered sodium (0.8 g/dL NaCl, 0.156 g/dL Na2 HPO4—H2O, 0.02 g/dL KCl, and 0.02 g/dL KH2 PO4). The reaction mixture with the samples containing 50 mM sodium-carbonate buffer (pH 10), 0.025 mM nitroblue tetrazolium, 0.1 mM xanthine, 0.1 mM EDTA, and xanthine oxidase was taken in a small cuvette. The unit SOD activity was interpreted as the quantity of enzyme needed for the inhibition of nitroblue tetrazolium; the SOD absorbance was measured at 560 nm through a spectrophotometer and expressed as U/mL. The malondialdehyde (MDA) was measured using a commercial kit (Jiancheng Co. Ltd., China). Briefly, the reaction mixture was incubated at 95 °C-100 °C in boiling water for 50 min, and then the absorbance was measured at 532 nm.

Determination of serum biochemical indicators

At the end of feeding trials and just after semen collection, 5 ml blood samples from each experimental bird were collected from the wing vein using a sterile syringe with a needle. The blood samples were transferred into 4 ml EDTA-free blood collecting tubes for the analysis of serum biochemistry, such as alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), total protein (TP), albumin (ALB), and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) using commercially available kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China). The serum biochemistry was analyzed by the method previously described (Somparn et al., 2024). Briefly, the blood samples were centrifuged at 1500 g for 15 min at 4 °C to separate the serum, and were stored at −20 °C for analysis.

Statistical analysis

Data were compiled in an MS Excel datasheet and analyzed using a completely randomized design in SPSS version 25. BSFLP supplementation level was considered a fixed factor at five levels (0, 5, 10, 15, and 20%), and each rooster was treated as an independent experimental unit. Semen samples were collected twice a week from each rooster, and the average value of each parameter per rooster was used for analysis to avoid pseudoreplication. The statistical model used was as follows:

Yijk = observed value of the dependent variable, μ = overall mean, ai = fixed effects of dietary BSFLP inclusion level (i = 0, 5, 10, 15, and 20%), and Sij = the experimental error.

Each rooster was considered an independent experimental unit, and semen parameters from repeated semen collections were averaged before analysis. Birds were randomly assigned to treatments and exhibited similar body weights at the beginning of the experiment (3.42 ± 0.22 kg); therefore, body weight was not included as a covariate in the statistical model. Before analysis, the data were examined to verify the assumptions of parametric testing. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess the normality of the residuals. The homogeneity of variances was evaluated using Levene’s test. All datasets met the assumptions required for one-way ANOVA. The multiple comparisons among means in significantly different treatment groups were detected through Tukey’s honestly significant difference (HSD). The differences were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05. In addition, orthogonal polynomial contrasts were applied to assess linear and quadratic responses to graded dietary BSFLP inclusion levels at 0, 5, 10, 15, and 20%.

Results and discussion

Breeding cocks play an important role in the breeding of chickens. Nutrition is one of the main factors affecting the breeding ability and fertility of the breeding cocks (Zawisza et al., 2023). Studies have shown that sperm motility and acrosome integrity rate are closely related to the fertilization ability of sperm. These are important indicators for assessing semen quality (Memili et al., 2020). Therefore, the effects of the dietary supplementation of the BSFLP were evaluated, and its different dietary supplementation levels significantly improved the sperm viability, motility, and acrosome integrity rate of the Luxi game breeding cocks (p < 0.05). Polynomial contrast analysis revealed significant linear responses (p < 0.05) for several semen quality and antioxidant parameters, whereas quadratic effects were not significant (p > 0.05). The significant linear responses observed indicate a dose-dependent improvement in reproductive and antioxidant parameters across the evaluated inclusion range.

Effects of adding BSFLP to the diet on sperm motility, viability, and acrosome integrity rate

The dietary inclusion of 15% BSFLP significantly improves the sperm motility, viability, and acrosome integrity rate of the Luxi game breeding cocks compared with the control group (p < 0.05) (Fig. 1, Table 2). However, A dose-dependent improvement was observed in linear motility (LM) as the value increased from 22.09 ± 1.16% in the control group to a maximum of 27.23±1.64% in the 20% supplementation group (p < 0.05). The LM values in all supplemented groups (5%, 10%, 15%, and 20%) were higher than those in the control group, with all supplemented groups showing significant improvements. Total motility was significantly increased in all BSFLP-supplemented groups compared with the control (p < 0.05), showing a linear improvement with increasing inclusion level. The observed linear responses indicate a dose-dependent improvement in motility-related parameters within the evaluated range. Although the 20% group showed the highest numerical values for several traits, these were not statistically different from the 10% and 15% groups. The absence of significant quadratic effects suggests that the optimal inclusion level was not reached within the tested range. Although total motility (MOT) increased significantly (p < 0.05), progressive motility (PMOT) remained unchanged (p > 0.05), indicating that BSFLP primarily enhanced overall sperm activity rather than forward progressive movement. The linear improvements observed in motility and kinematic parameters suggest a dose-dependent response, while the absence of quadratic effects indicates that the inclusion levels remained within physiological tolerance. The velocity parameters also showed marked improvements in response to supplementation. The Curvilinear Velocity (VCL) and Velocity of Straight line (VSL) increased significantly in the 5%, 10%, 15%, and 20% groups compared to the control. Progressively highest VCL and VSL (51.33±1.99 and 34.01±1.32 μm/s) were recorded in the 20% group, compared to the VCL and VSL 47.27±2.70 μm/s and 29.33±1.23 μm/s, respectively, in the control group. The sperm amplitude of lateral head displacement exhibited a similar trend of progressively increasing in all supplemented groups compared to the control. The highest Amplitude of lateral head displacement (ALH) was found in roosters fed with 20% BSFLP (15.60±1.04) as compared with the control group 11.72 ± 1.50. Additionally, polynomial contrast analysis revealed significant linear increases in LM, total motility, VCL, VSL, and ALH with increasing BSFLP inclusion (p < 0.05). No quadratic responses were detected for the evaluated semen traits. Dietary inclusion of BSFLP significantly improved several semen motility parameters in a linear manner (p < 0.05), with inclusion levels of 10%, 15%, and 20% showing higher values compared with the control group. However, no significant differences were observed among the 10%, 15%, and 20% groups. Studies have shown that BSFLP is a nutritious supplement, rich in protein (40%−57%), fat (31%−35%), essential amino acids, minerals, and a variety of vitamins (Shah et al., 2022). The content of vitamin E and vitamin B is relatively high, and the content of calcium (5%−8%) and phosphorus (0.6%−1.5%) is higher than that of fish meal (Makkar et al., 2014; Sogari et al., 2019). It is also rich in chitin, antimicrobial peptides, and other bioactive substances (Elhag et al., 2017; Tran et al., 2022). The ash content is (9.85%), Ca (2.14%), P (1.15%), Mg (0.39%), K (1.35%), Zn (13.10 mg/kg), Cu (11.20 mg/kg), Mn (23.20 mg/kg), and Fe (20.40 mg/kg) 5278.49 kcal/kg of gross energy based on DM (De Marco et al., 2015; Jayanegara et al., 2017). Moreover, the composition of primary essential amino acids includes alanine (Ala), glutamic acid (Glu), leucine (Leu), and valine (Val) (Cullere et al., 2016). Previously, dietary supplementation of crude protein and energy levels significantly (p < 0.05) increased the semen quality and fertility of Domyati ducks (Ghonim et al., 2010; Martínez-Soto et al., 2013). Protein is an important component of sperm and seminal plasma. The lack or deficiency of dietary protein leads to a deficiency of plasma protein and amino acids in the breeder cocks, which negatively affects spermatogenesis or a decrease in sperm motility and consequently decreases the fertility of the breeder cocks (Li et al., 2025). Vitamin E is a natural antioxidant and improves sperm quality through various mechanisms, especially through lipid peroxidation (Rengaraj & Hong, 2015). Several studies have proven the association of impaired male reproductive functions with vitamin E deficiency. Vitamin E has been identified as an anti-sterility vitamin, and its long-term omission or its deficiency in the diet causes abnormal spermatogenesis and affects semen quality, such as sperm motility, sperm viability, and sperm capacity (Bjørneboe et al., 1990; Combs, 1981). Moreover, the BSFLP is rich in lauric acid, palmitic acid, essential amino acids, mineral trace elements, and various bioactive substances (Sharma et al., 2024). The lack of amino acids, vitamins, and trace elements can reduce the number of sperm, increase the number of abnormal sperm, and reduce the motility and vitality of semen (Martínez-Soto et al., 2013). Additionally, the BSFLP contains a high content of B vitamins and trace elements such as zinc and manganese (Li et al., 2025); vitamin B plays a key role in the process of spermatogenesis, which can promote the normal development and maturation of sperm, help to improve the vitality and morphology of sperm, and improve the quality and quantity of sperm (Banihani, 2017). Moreover, Vitamin B plays essential roles as an enzymatic cofactor in cellular metabolism, especially in amino acid, lipid, and carbohydrate pathways to support the production of energy (Depeint et al., 2006; GENÇ, 2024). Several B vitamins, such as B1 (thiamine), B2 (riboflavin), B3 (niacin), and B5 (pantothenic acid), directly regulate oxidative mitochondrial phosphorylation through their roles in key co-enzymes including, TPP (thiamine pyrophosphate), FAD (flavin adenine dinucleotide), coenzyme A, and NAD⁺/NADH (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) (Han et al., 2025). These cofactors facilitate ATP synthesis within mitochondria, which is critical for sustaining sperm motility and flagellar movement. Adequate availability of B vitamins, therefore, supports efficient mitochondrial function and energy metabolism, contributing to improved sperm functional performance (Hrubša et al., 2022). Similarly, zinc has the function of maintaining the stability and permeability of sperm membrane structure. Zinc deficiency can reduce the synthesis and secretion of testosterone, sperm production, sperm count, and motility (Mousavi Esfiokhi et al., 2023). Manganese is a trace element necessary for maintaining normal reproductive function, and its deficiency leads to sperm maturation disorder and, consequently, oligozoospermia or azoospermia (Orzołek et al., 2021). The experimental diets were formulated to be isonitrogenous and supplemented with a premix; therefore, the observed improvements in semen motility cannot be attributed to individual micronutrients alone. Instead, the positive effects of BSFLP likely reflect the combined influence of its high-quality protein, amino acid profile, lipid composition (including lauric acid), and bioactive compounds. The dietary supplementation of 5%−20% BSFLP significantly (p < 0.05) increased the activities of CAT, GSH-px, SOD, and GR while decreasing the activity of MDA in the semen of Luxi game breeding cock, as compared to the control group (Table 3). The semen collected from the Luxi game breeding cock fed with 20% of dietary supplementation of BSFLP, exhibited statistically higher levels (p < 0.05) of the CAT 1.59 U/mL, the GSH-Px 108.83 U·mg⁻¹ protein, SOD 1.51 U/mL, and the GR 4.54 U/L than the values in the control group. The level of MDA 12.74 nmol·mg-1 protein was also reduced numerically in the semen of Luxi game breeding cock fed with 20% dietary supplementation of the BSFLP to the Luxi game breeding cock than the values in the control group. The levels of the SOD, CAT, GR, and GSH-px were higher than those of the control group (p < 0.05). The level of MDA was lower in all treated groups than in the control group. This may be related to the higher content of essential amino acids, minerals, and vitamins in the BSFLP. Furthermore, polynomial contrast analysis revealed significant linear increases in CAT, GSH-Px, SOD, and GR activities (p < 0.05), while MDA concentration showed a significant linear decrease, indicating reduced lipid peroxidation. No significant quadratic responses were detected for antioxidant parameters. Studies have shown that the BSFLP is rich in essential amino acids, minerals, and vitamins, and the content of calcium and phosphorus is higher than that of fish meal and soybean meal (Lu et al., 2020). Some vitamins, such as vitamin E, have antioxidant effects, which can increase the activity of antioxidant enzymes in semen and reduce the content of MDA in semen. Adding BSFLP to the diet of fish can significantly increase the antioxidant enzyme activity and reduce the MDA content in fish muscle (Lu et al., 2020). The linear enhancement of antioxidant enzyme activities alongside the reduction in MDA concentration demonstrates a dose-dependent improvement in oxidative balance with increasing BSFLP inclusion. The absence of quadratic effects suggests that antioxidant capacity continued to improve across the evaluated inclusion range without evidence of oxidative overload or diminishing returns. This experiment proved that the addition of BSFLP to the diet can increase the activity of antioxidant enzymes in semen and reduce the content of MDA in semen.

The addition of BSFLP to the diet had no significant effect on ALT, AST, TP, ALB, and ALP in the serum of Luxi game breeding cock (p > 0.05) (Table 4). Additionally, polynomial contrast analysis revealed no significant linear or quadratic trends (p > 0.05). The TP and ALB are important indicators for evaluating the health status of the poultry birds. Serum proteins play an important role in maintaining the body's homeostasis. The ALB is the most favorable amino acid source for protein synthesis in the body (Filipovic et al., 2007). The ALT and AST predict liver function, and if there is any abnormality in the body's tissues and cells, the serum ALT and AST activities increase. This study found that there was no significant effect on serum biochemical indexes of the roosters fed with different levels of dietary BSFLP supplementation. Marono and Chen Xiaochun reported that the replacement of soybean meal with black soldier fly larvae meal in the diet had no significant effect on serum biochemical indexes of laying hens (Marono et al., 2017), which was consistent with the results of the present study. The absence of significant changes in serum biochemical indicators suggests that dietary BSFLP inclusion up to 20% did not induce detectable alterations in liver-associated or metabolic markers. However, a comprehensive evaluation of physiological safety would require additional assessments such as histopathological examination and long-term health monitoring.

In the present study, although BSFLP is sometimes described as a feed supplement, however, BSFLP evaluated as a functional protein ingredient partially replacing soybean meal. The maintained dietary crude protein and energy levels confirm that the observed improvements in semen quality and antioxidant status were associated with the protein source and bioactive composition of BSFLP, rather than increased nutrient intake. Moreover, it is admitted that increasing inclusion levels of BSFLP were accompanied by a gradual increase in rice bran content to maintain isonitrogenous and isocaloric diets. Rice bran contains a higher fiber fraction, which may influence nutrient digestibility and intestinal nutrient absorption. However, the total dietary crude protein and metabolizable energy were maintained at constant levels across treatments, and no adverse effects on semen quality were observed. This suggests that the moderate increase in dietary fiber associated with rice bran inclusion did not impair reproductive performance in Luxi game cocks. Moreover, the improved antioxidant status and semen quality observed in BSFLP-fed birds indicate that any potential reduction in nutrient digestibility due to increased fiber was likely offset by the high-quality protein, lipid fraction, and bioactive compounds present in BSFLP. Nevertheless, future studies incorporating direct measurements of nutrient digestibility and fiber fractions are warranted to further clarify the interaction between insect protein inclusion, dietary fiber, and reproductive performance.

Although the current study provides useful preliminary insights into the effects of BSFLP on the semen quality of Luxi game breeding cocks, several methodological limitations need to be acknowledged. First, the sample size was relatively limited, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings and reduce the statistical power to detect subtle treatment effects. Second, semen samples were collected repeatedly from the same birds and averaged prior to analysis; therefore, a repeated-measures statistical model was not applied. Future studies with larger populations and appropriate repeated-measures designs would provide more robust inference regarding temporal changes in semen characteristics. Third, the present study focused exclusively on semen quality parameters, and direct reproductive outcomes such as fertility rate, mating success, and hatchability were not evaluated. Consequently, the potential effects of BSFLP supplementation on overall reproductive performance require further validation through breeding trials.

Conclusions

The present study evaluated the effects of graded dietary inclusion of BSFLP (0–20%) on semen quality parameters in Luxi game breeding cocks. The results showed that inclusion levels of 10–20% were associated with improvements in selected semen quality traits, including sperm motility and related kinematic characteristics. However, as growth performance, feed efficiency, mating success, fertility rate, and hatchability were not assessed, the findings are limited to semen quality outcomes and do not establish overall reproductive performance.

Therefore, while BSFLP appears to be a promising alternative protein source with potential functional properties, further studies are required to evaluate its effects on long-term reproductive outcomes, fertility parameters, and production performance before practical dietary recommendations can be made.

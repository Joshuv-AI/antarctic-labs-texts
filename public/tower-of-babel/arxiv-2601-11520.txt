Abstract
We study joint source–channel coding over Markov channels through the empirical coordination framework. More specifically, we aim at determining the empirical distributions of source and channel symbols that can be induced by a coding scheme. We consider strictly causal encoders that generate channel inputs, without access to the past channel states, henceforth driving the current Markov state evolution. Our main result is the single-letter inner and outer bounds of the set of achievable joint distributions, coordinating all the symbols in the network. To establish the inner bound, we introduce a new notion of typicality, the input-driven Markov typicality, and develop its fundamental properties. Contrary to the classical block-Markov coding schemes that rely on blockwise independence for discrete memoryless channels, our analysis directly exploits the Markov channel structure and improves beyond the independence-based arguments.



I
Introduction

Markov chains, as canonical models of stochastic recursion, are often represented as being driven by exogenous independent randomness
[
1
]
. This perspective casts the evolution as a state update rule acted on by fresh external randomness at each step, unifying formulations across random dynamical systems and stochastic algorithms. When combined with structural conditions such as monotonicity or contractivity, it leads to tractable analyses of stationarity and convergence
[
2
,
3
]
. From a control perspective, such exogenous inputs correspond to the classical open-loop operation
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

In information theory literature, Markov channels, sometimes known as finite state channels (FSC), have been studied extensively through the lens of channel capacity and control mechanisms
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. When feedback is available, the encoder observes the channel state and can adapt its inputs accordingly, leading to a rich body of results on FSCs with feedback
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
. These capacity characterizations, however, are complicated and typically involve multi-letter expressions. Recently, under the assumption of unichain and ergodicity, a single-letter capacity representation was obtained for Markov channels with feedback
[
18
]
.

In this work, we study the Markov evolution structure from a joint source–channel coding (JSCC) perspective
[
19
,
20
]
.
Specifically, the source sequence is generated independently at each time, and the channel input is generated by strictly causal encoders based solely on the past source sequence, and does not adapt to the latent channel state history. The channel input, together with the previous state, updates the current channel state. The decoder operates noncausally, observing the entire state sequence to produce its action sequence.

Empirical coordination coding framework, introduced in
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
, characterizes cooperative behavior among agents by requiring the empirical distribution of symbol sequences to converge to a prescribed target distribution. In this framework, encoders and decoders select their actions based on local observations not only to ensure reliable communication, but also to induce a desired joint behavior. Coordination results for JSCC with (strictly) causal decision-makers over
dmc
(
dmc
) were established in
[
24
,
25
]
. Extensions to point-to-point settings with channel state or feedback were developed in
[
26
,
27
]
, and recently have been further applied to distributed decision-making
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
etc..

To the best of our knowledge, this work is the first to study JSCC over Markov channels through the lens of coordination coding. Under our Markov channel model assumption, we derive single-letter inner and outer bounds that characterize the set of achievable joint distributions that can be arbitrarily well approximated by suitable coding schemes. To establish the inner bound, we introduce a new notion of typicality, the input-driven Markov typicality, and develop its fundamental properties, such as asymptotic equipartition property (AEP) and a packing lemma, etc.. In contrast to the standard random-coding arguments for
dmc
that rely on block independence in the block-Markov coding constructions, such as in
[
27
,
31
]
, this notion of typicality enables us to exploit the Markov channel structure directly, allowing the analysis to go beyond independence-based arguments while still recovering the results. The outer bound shares the same information constraint expression as the inner bound, but is defined over a generally larger set of distributions.

III
Main Results

Our main result provides necessary and sufficient conditions for a distribution that is achievable.

Theorem 1

(Main Result)
.

A joint probability distribution
\mathcal{P}_{U,X,Y^{\prime},Y,V}
is achievable, if there exists an auxiliary random variable
W
, satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{U}\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{P}_{W|U,X}\pi_{Y^{\prime}}\mathcal{W}_{Y|X,Y^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}_{V|Y,X,W}

(9)

with marginal
\mathcal{P}_{U,X,Y^{\prime},Y,V}
such that we have

\displaystyle I(X;Y|Y^{\prime})-I(U;W|X)\geq 0,

(10)

where
\pi_{Y^{\prime}}
is the unique equilibrium distribution (
8
). Conversely,
\mathcal{P}_{U,X,Y^{\prime},Y,V}
is achievable only if there exists an auxiliary random variable
W
satisfying (
10
) and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{U}\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{P}_{Y^{\prime}|X}\mathcal{W}_{Y|X,Y^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}_{W|U,X,Y,Y^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}_{V|Y,X,W}

(11)

with marginal
\mathcal{P}_{U,X,Y^{\prime},Y,V}
.

The first part of Theorem
1
is an inner bound of the achievable distribution region, whereas the second part is the outer bound.

When the Markov channel reduces to a
dmc
(e.g. take
Y^{\prime}=\varnothing
), our inner bound specializes to the coordination coding result for joint source–channel coding with strictly causal encoders established in
[
24

The auxiliary random variable

plays a role analogous to that in coordination coding for

[

]

10

Remark 1

(Source-Channel Separation)

In the case when the random variables of the source
(U,V)
are independent of those of the channel

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{U,X,Y^{\prime},Y,V}=\mathcal{P}_{U,V}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{X,Y^{\prime},Y},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq I(X;Y|Y^{\prime})-I(U;W|X)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(a)}}}{{\leq}}I(X;Y|Y^{\prime})-I(U;V|X)

\displaystyle=I(X;Y|Y^{\prime})-I(U;V)

where the inequality (a) comes from the Markov chain
V-\!\!\!\!\minuso\!\!\!\!-(X,Y,W)-\!\!\!\!\minuso\!\!\!\!-(U,Y^{\prime})

Appendix A
Input-driven Markov Typicality

In this section, we introduce the input-driven Markov typicality for two sequences
(X^{n},Y^{n})

[
35
,
36
,
34

i.i.d. generated, and
Y^{n}
a Markov chain with starting state

\displaystyle(X^{n},Y^{n})\sim\mathcal{P}_{X}^{\otimes n}\cdot\prod_{t=1}^{n}\mathcal{W}(Y_{t}|X_{t},Y_{t-1}).

(12)

Now, for simplicity, we denote the target product distribution by
\mathcal{Q}_{Y^{\prime}XY}=\pi_{Y^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}_{X}\mathcal{W}_{Y|X,Y^{\prime}}

Definition 3

(Input-driven Markov Typicality)
.

For sequences
(x^{n},y^{n})
, define the empirical distribution of joint symbols over adjacent indices of triplets:

\displaystyle Q^{n}_{Y^{\prime}XY}(i,x,j)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{(y_{t-1},x_{t},y_{t})=(i,x,j)\},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad x\in\mathcal{X},(i,j)\in\mathcal{Y}^{2}.

We define the set of input-driven Markov typicality sequences by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{(n)}(\mathcal{Q}_{Y^{\prime}XY})=\{(x^{n},y^{n}):\lVert Q_{Y^{\prime}XY}^{n}-\mathcal{Q}_{Y^{\prime}XY}\lVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon\}.

For a fixed

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{(n)}(\mathcal{Q}_{Y^{\prime}XY}|x^{n})

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{(n)}(\mathcal{Q}_{Y^{\prime}XY}|x^{n})=\{

\displaystyle y^{n}\in\mathcal{Y}^{n}:

\displaystyle\lVert Q_{Y^{\prime}XY}^{n}-\pi_{Y^{\prime}}Q_{X}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{Y|X,Y^{\prime}}\lVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon\}.

where

\displaystyle Q_{X}^{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{x_{t}=x\}=\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{Y}}Q^{n}_{Y^{\prime}XY}(i,x,j),\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

Theorem 2

(Ergodicity)

Let

\displaystyle\forall\delta>0,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}((X^{n},Y^{n})\in\mathcal{T}_{\delta}^{(n)}(\mathcal{Q}_{Y^{\prime}XY}))=1

This theorem shows that no matter how the initial symbol of is generated, the Markov type of

We denote

Theorem 3

(AEP and Cardinality Bound)

For any
\delta>0

\forall\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}

1.

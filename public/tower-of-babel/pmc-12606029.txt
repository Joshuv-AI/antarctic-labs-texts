Electrohydraulic Lithotripsy for Hepatolithiasis With Novel VedVision Cholangioscope and New Biodegradable Stent (UNITY‐B) for Intrahepatic Biliary Stricture: A Case Report With Video
Authors: Scalvini Davide, Mazza Stefano, Ciccioli Carlo, Cappellini Alessandro, Alfieri Daniele, Bina Niccolò, Mauro Aurelio, Radaelli Franco, Bardone Marco, Anderloni Andrea
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTCholangioscopy has improved the management of complex biliary diseases; however, digital single‐operator cholangioscopy (DSOC) still faces key limitations, especially in treating intrahepatic disease like hepatolithiasis and strictures, which often require surgery with significant mortality risk. This video case report illustrates the successful endoscopic treatment of an elderly woman with multiple left intrahepatic duct stones and a benign stricture. A next‐generation 11‐Fr high‐definition cholangioscope (VedVision) was employed, offering enhanced imaging, improved maneuverability, and a dual‐channel system enabling simultaneous guidewire and lithotripsy probe use. Electrohydraulic lithotripsy achieved complete stone clearance. A balloon‐expandable biodegradable stent (UNITY‐B), composed of magnesium alloy with polymer coating, was placed across the stricture to reshape it. Subsequent cholangioscopy confirmed correct positioning. This approach highlights a promising, non‐surgical strategy for managing benign intrahepatic biliary disease. The biodegradable stent eliminates the need for removal, reducing the need for further interventions. However, long‐term data and larger studies are necessary to confirm its safety and efficacy.Keywords: biliary stricture, biodegradable stent, cholangioscopy, electrohydraulic lithotripsy, hepatolithiasis

Introduction

Cholangioscopy is changing the management of difficult biliary stones and enhancing diagnostic accuracy for indeterminate biliary strictures [1]. However, current digital single‐operator cholangioscopy (DSOC) still has several limitations, including suboptimal image resolution, limited maneuverability, the size of the working channel, and low diagnostic accuracy of biopsy samples [2]. Additionally, this technique still has ceilings in the treatment of intrahepatic diseases, such as stones and strictures, which frequently necessitate surgical intervention. Intrahepatic biliary stones, also referred to as hepatolithiasis, are more frequent in East populations than in Western ones, accounting for less than 2% of the population with recognized risk factors like female sex and age > 50 years [3]. Moreover, intrahepatic strictures may not only cause hepatolithiasis but can also result from it [3]. Patients with hepatolithiasis need their removal for relief of the pain, prevention of cholangitis, and the progression of the disease in cholangiocarcinoma [4].

In a large Chinese series of hepatolithiasis in the era before DSOC, 63% of patients were treated with surgery [5] and in another cohort of 718 consecutive patients, surgical morbidities were up to 38.5% with an in‐hospital mortality rate of 0.4% [6].

Furthermore, encouraging results have been reported with a new biodegradable pancreatic stent, primarily designed to prevent post‐endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) pancreatitis [7].

These considerations drive the search for new technological solutions aimed at improving patient outcomes. This video case report highlights the use of a next‐generation high‐definition cholangioscope and an innovative biodegradable stent to address these challenges.

Case Report

The case involves a 75‐year‐old woman with a history of biliary stones, cholangitis, and an ASA score of three, considered at high risk for surgery. This patient underwent magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) for recurrent abdominal pain that revealed multiple stones in dilated left intrahepatic ducts (IHDs), the largest measuring 8 mm and a doubt of IHD stricture in the left lobe [Figure 1A]. Previous ERCP in another center was unsuccessful; thus, we decided to perform a cholangioscopy in order to do a cholangioscopy‐assisted lithotripsy and to evaluate the suspect biliary stricture [Video S1]. The one we used is a novel high‐definition 11‐Fr (3.7 mm) cholangioscope (VedVision, Jiangsu Vedkang Medical Science and Technology Co. Ltd., Changzhou, China), with several features introduced to try to overpass the abovementioned limitations: enhanced visualization (160 000 pixels CMOS sensor), bright LED illumination with automatic or manual adjustment, a field of view of 120°, a 2.0 mm working channel, and a separate 1.2 mm channel for a 0.025 hydrophilic guidewire or irrigation [8].

(A) Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography revealing dilated left intrahepatic ducts, stones and a doubt of stricture. (B) Cholangiogram confirming MRCP evidence. (C) Cholangioscopy evaluation of intrahepatic benign stricture. (D) Through‐the‐stent cholangioscopy visualization.

After guidewire‐assisted biliary cannulation of a previously treated papilla, cholangiography confirmed the dilation of left IHDs containing multiple filling defects suggestive of hepatolithiasis [Figure 1B]. The cholangioscope was advanced over the wire, with a combination of direct cholangioscope catheter pushing, endoscope, and precise tip control, navigating through the IHDs until that a benign‐appearing biliary stricture was identified in the left hepatic branches [Figure 1C]. The stricture was dilated with an 8 mm balloon that allowed passage through the narrowing, revealing multiple stones upstream. They were effectively fragmented using a cholangioscope‐assisted electrohydraulic lithotripsy probe. Standard extraction, with balloon and basket, was employed to clear the ducts, and complete clearance was confirmed via cholangioscopy.

Considering the presence of a biliary stricture, a new balloon‐expandable biodegradable stent, called UNITY‐B (Q3 Medical Group, Dublin, Ireland), was assessed [9]. This is a biodegradable stent, composed of a magnesium alloy with a polymer coating on the surface that offers flexibility and adaptability, with a diameter of 10 × 57 mm in length and a time for degradation ranging between 1 and 3 months. The stent was deployed under fluoroscopic guidance across the stricture to support and reshape the duct. A subsequent cholangioscopy, including a through‐the‐stent inspection, was performed to confirm accurate positioning within the intrahepatic ducts [Figure 1D].

No adverse events or new onset of acute cholangitis occurred in the following 3 months, and a MRCP revealed a proper degradation of the stent and the absence of a new stricture.

For completed technical specifications of VedVision cholangioscope and UNITY‐B stent, please refer to the specific references [8, 9].

Discussion

This video case report highlights the successful endoscopic treatment of a dual pathology that often requires surgery with a considerable mortality. This novel cholangioscope provided significant advantages in maneuverability, allowing a smoother navigation in the IHDs. Additionally, the dual‐channel design facilitated the simultaneous use of a guidewire and a lithotripsy probe, enhancing procedural efficiency. The decision to refrain from obtaining biopsies was guided by the patient's clinical history of biliary lithiasis, cholangitis, the operators' expertise in the management of biliary strictures/cholangioscopy‐based diagnostic assessment and, primarily, by cholangioscopic findings demonstrating complete absence of malignant features such as tortuous vessels, irregular nodulations, mucosal friability, or ulcerations [10].

The use of this brand‐new biodegradable stent may offer a tailored, non‐surgical solution for patients with benign strictures (e.g., inflammatory, fibrotic, or post‐cholecystectomy). The biodegradable nature of the stent may eliminate the necessity of performing a new ERCP for stent removal or replacement, reducing the need for further invasive exams and radiation exposure. Its particular shape allows for significantly decreasing the risk of re‐obstruction and may reduce biofilm buildup within the stent commonly present in plastic stents.

This is the first evidence of the use of this particular biodegradable stent in intrahepatic ducts. Extended follow‐up and strong supporting literature are needed to establish the real evidence of the efficacy of this stent.

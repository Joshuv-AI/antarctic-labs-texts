P16/Ki67 dual staining versus cytology for identifying high-grade cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN2+/CIN3+) in triage management of HR-HPV-positive women: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Hang Zhou, Yu Dai, Hua Zhang, Yuan Yang, Ruoji Pi, Lingling Zhu, Wen Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe co-expression of P16 and Ki67 suggests the presence of high-grade cervical intraepithelial neoplasia, which makes P16/Ki67 dual staining a promising marker in cervical cancer screening. This study provides a systemic review of the established evidence on the performance of P16/Ki67 versus cytology in detecting cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN)2+/CIN3+ among high-risk human papillomavirus (HR-HPV)-positive women and HR-HPV-positive individuals with cytology-negative results for triage management.MethodsStudies published from database inception to April 26, 2024, were systematically searched. A total of 26 studies were eventually included in the study, comprising 26,424 women, with 14,625 classified as P16/Ki67-positive and 11,799 as P16/Ki67-negative.ResultsIn the triage management of HR-HPV-positive women, P16/Ki67 achieved sensitivities of 85% for CIN2+ and 88% for CIN3+, with specificities of 63% and 57%, respectively. In comparison, cytology showed lower sensitivities of 76% for CIN2+ and 79% for CIN3+, with lower specificities of 57% and 54%. In triage management of human papillomavirus (HPV)-positive but cytology-negative women, 18.48% of the HPV-positive patients, initially missed by cytology, were correctly retrieved by the addition of P16/Ki67. A negative P16/Ki67 test result reduced the risk of underlying CIN3+ to 4% for HR-HPV-positive individuals and 1% for the HR-HPV-positive individuals with cytology-negative results, prompting a recommendation for follow-up at 1-year intervals.ConclusionP16/Ki67 exhibits diagnostic superiority over liquid-based cytology in detecting CIN2+/CIN3+, among HR-HPV-positive women. Furthermore, its ability to identify patients missed by cytology highlights its value as a complementary tool for triage of HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative population. By effectively reducing unnecessary colposcopy referrals, P16/Ki67 conserves healthcare resources and improves outcomes.Trial RegistrationPROSPERO Identifier: CRD42024567623Keywords: p16(INK4A), Ki-67 Antigen, Meta-Analysis, Human Papillomavirus, Cervical Cancer, Triage

INTRODUCTION

Cervical cancer ranks fourth in terms of incidence and mortality among all cancers in women and is one of the malignant tumors possessing severe threats to women’s health [1]. Its disease burden is growing, with a notable shift towards a younger age group [1]. The latest global cancer statistics for 2022 indicate that there are approximately 661,021 new cases and 348,189 deaths of cervical cancer per year [1].

Screening is an effective secondary preventive measure for cervical cancer. Cervical cancer has an easily recognizable precancerous lesion, cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN), which typically takes 10–20 years to develop into cancer, providing favorable conditions for screening [2]. The commonly used screening methods for CIN include cytology and human papillomavirus (HPV) testing. Cytology, while straightforward, cost-effective, and economically advantageous, is constrained by its relatively low sensitivity and high false negative rate, limiting its standalone utilization in cervical cancer screening [3]. Persistent high-risk human papillomavirus (HR-HPV) infection is the leading cause of cervical carcinogenesis [4]. The implementation of HR-HPV testing can further improve the effectiveness of cervical cancer screening programs [5].

In recent years, HR-HPV testing has become a common strategy for cervical cancer screening [6]. However, most HR-HPV-positive women naturally combat the infection without developing CIN or cervical cancer, and the testing lacks precision in distinguishing non-progressive infections from those with the potential to progress to cervical cancer [7,8]. Therefore, without stratifying out the HR-HPV-positive individuals without the potential to progress to cervical cancer, the colposcopy referral rate would be excessively high, leading to wasting healthcare resources [9]. Also, the triage management of cytology or repeat HPV testing in HR-HPV-positive women was not satisfactory [10,11]. Based on current cervical cancer screening strategies, cytology stands as the most prevalent triage tool for women with HR-HPV infection, but its relatively low sensitivity may result in the misdiagnosis of potential CIN2+/CIN3+ patients during follow-ups [12]. More efficient markers are also needed to identify women with potentially high-grade CIN in the HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative population.

In recent research, CDKN2A (P16) and MKI67 (Ki67) have emerged as promising biomarkers for cervical neoplasia detection. P16, a tumor suppressor gene, is involved in regulating the cell cycle, and its expression levels correlate with the severity of cervical neoplasia [13,14]. Meanwhile, abnormal Ki67 protein expression indicates increased cell proliferation and is positively associated with cervical lesion severity [15]. These morphology-independent biomarkers, P16 and Ki67, have demonstrated effectiveness in triaging cytology results [16]. Their concurrent expression within a single cervical epithelial cell signifies cell-cycle dysregulation. It suggests the presence of CIN2+/CIN3+ [17], thereby offering promising applications in the triage management of both HR-HPV-positive women and those who are HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative.

In 2020, the Food and Drug Administration approved the P16/Ki67 DS CINtec PLUS Cytology (Roche Diagnostics, Indianapolis, IN, USA) for the triage of HPV-positive individuals in screening programs, regardless of whether the primary screening is HPV testing alone or co-testing using HPV testing plus cytology [18]. Recently, the Enduring Consensus Cervical Cancer Screening and Management Guidelines [19] recommended that P16/Ki67 is acceptable for the triage of individuals with positive HPV results from primary HPV testing (with or without genotyping) or individuals with HPV-positive testing results and negative for intraepithelial lesion or malignancy (NILM), atypical squamous cells of undetermined significance (ASC-US), or low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion cytology results in the co-testing scenarios. The guideline recommendations are largely based on evidence from several large international clinical studies, but there is a lack of systematic reviews that comprehensively summarize the evidences. To further validate and support the guideline recommendations, a systematic review and meta-analysis is warranted.

In this study, we summarize the existing evidences on the performance of P16/Ki67 versus cytology in detecting CIN2+/CIN3+ among HPV-positive women in triage management. Additionally, we conduct a meta-analysis examining the efficacy of P16/Ki67 in the triage of HPV-positive but cytology-negative population. The results are presented in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis guidelines (Table S1) [20]. We hope the findings would provide valuable insights for the implementation of triage methods in cervical cancer screening cohorts.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Clinical question

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the accuracy of the P16/Ki67, as compared to cytology, for identifying underlying CIN2+/CIN3+ in women who either tested positive for HR-HPV or were HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative.

Ethical and informed consent was deemed unnecessary for this study as it only involved publicly accessible data and publications.

2. Index test and reference standard

Through immunocytochemistry, co-expression of CDKN2A (P16) and MKI67 (Ki67) within the squamous epithelium cells of the cervix can be visualized [21]. A positive result is characterized by the simultaneous presence of brown staining alongside red staining within a single cell. A slide was considered positive if at least one cell exhibits both staining patterns [17].

Liquid-based cytology samples were collected from the study subjects. Experienced cytologists assessed the samples using the Bethesda system. Cytological diagnosis was conducted with blinded experts. Samples were considered positive if classified as ASC-US+, while NILM indicated negative results.

Subsequent to colposcopy and/or biopsy, a rigorous histological assessment of cervical precancer was conducted by a panel of independent and blinded pathologists, serving as the reference standard.

3. Outcome measures

Outcome measures were pre-established prior to conducting the literature search. The primary outcome focused on assessing the sensitivity and specificity of P16/Ki67, compared to cytology for detecting CIN2+/CIN3+. The secondary outcome included likelihood ratio-positive and -negative, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, area under the curve (AUC), diagnostic odds ratio (DOR), and Youden index. Fagan nomogram plots were also included in the secondary outcomes.

4. Data sources and searches

We have officially registered our meta-analysis with PROSPERO under the registration number CRD42024567623.

The literature search was conducted in the recognized medical databases, including PubMed, Web of Science, the Cochrane Library, and Embase, to retrieve relevant studies published from database inception to April 26, 2024. Each database was systematically queried with a carefully constructed search string encompassing key index terms, including “Cyclin-Dependent Kinase Inhibitor p16,” “Ki-67 Antigen,” “HPV,” “cytology,” and “Cervical Intraepithelial Neoplasia.” The search strategy was language-agnostic and utilized specialized tools such as Medical Subject Headings (MeSH Terms) and Boolean operators (AND/OR) to improve both the precision and comprehensiveness of the search results, the details of which are shown in Table S2.

5. Inclusion and exclusion criteria of literature

Inclusion criteria: 1) include patients with CIN and/or cervical cancer diagnosed by histological samples; 2) include HPV-positive women and/or HPV-positive but cytology-negative women; 3) can extract several crucial raw measurement data, including true-positive value, false-positive value, false-negative value, and true-negative value, for screening CIN and cervical cancer using the P16/Ki67 dual staining and cytology testing; 4) literature in all languages were acceptable; and 5) comparative analyses between P16/Ki67 and cytology in triage management of HR-HPV-positive women were established.

Exclusion criteria: 1) conference reports, reviews, comments and abstracts; 2) without describing concrete diagnostic criteria or without histological or biopsy results; and 3) with incomplete or unavailable data.

6. Study selection

The literature search results were managed and evaluated using EndNote (version 21.0; Clarivate, Philadelphia, PA, USA). Two researchers (ZH and DY) independently applied unified inclusion and exclusion criteria in the review process. Any discrepancies were resolved through group discussions to achieve consensus. The entire literature screening process was documented for traceability. The initial screening phase involved a rapid review of literature titles, followed by a meticulous examination of abstracts and full texts to confirm eligibility for inclusion. A flow diagram, employed to visually represent the study selection process, was created utilizing an online tool developed by Haddaway et al. [22].

7. Data extraction and quality assessment

Two researchers (ZH and DY) utilized a self-designed information extraction form to independently extract data from the selected literature. The extracted information included key details such as the research title, first author, study location, publishing journal, age range of participants, source of study population (hospital-based or screening-based), study design (prospective or retrospective), and relevant data in primary and secondary outcomes. Any discrepancies encountered during this process were thoroughly discussed.

The methodological quality of each study was evaluated by a checklist comprising 13 QUADAS-2 criteria [23]. These criteria were categorized into 4 groups pertaining to: 1) patient selection, 2) index test, 3) reference standard, and 4) flow/timing of tests to assess the potential for bias based on each group. Two other researchers (ZH and ZLL) independently assessed and verified the potential bias in the screened literature and crosschecked them. Discrepancies were solved through discussion or consulting a third researcher (DY). The quality assessment was conducted using RevMan (version 5.3; Cochrane Collaboration, Oxford, UK).

8. Data analysis

The meta-analysis synthesized the findings of all literature included and visually presented the results through a forest plot. Potential publication bias was assessed using funnel plots and Deeks’ funnel plot asymmetry tests. Summary receiver operating characteristic (SROC) curves with prediction and confidence contours were generated for pooled tests.

Q-statistics were employed to assess the statistical heterogeneity across the studies. A p-value of <0.1 for the Q-statistics or an I2 value exceeding 50% signified a statistically significant heterogeneity. In case of significant heterogeneity, the data were analyzed using the random-effects model, whereas the fixed-effects model was applied otherwise [24]. When the level of heterogeneity was high, meta-regression and subgroup analysis were utilized to explore potential sources of heterogeneity if the number of included studies exceeded 10. The threshold effect was assessed by calculating the spearman correlation coefficient between sensitivity and 1-specificity [25]. A significantly positive correlation indicates the presence of a threshold effect [26].

The clinical applicability of the triage tests was illustrated using Fagan Nomogram plots [27]. Pretest risks were calculated based on the prevalence of CIN3+ in HPV-positive women, which was determined from the meta-analysis results for the included literature. Risk levels (high, medium, and low) were categorized based on the thresholds of CIN3+ immediate risk at 4% and 0.55%, in accordance with the 2019 American Society for Colposcopy and Cervical Pathology (ASCCP) for colposcopy referral [28]. These risk categories guide clinical decision-making for patient management, ranging from immediate referral for colposcopy, if necessary, biopsy taking and/or rapid treatment for high-risk individuals, further surveillance for medium-risk individuals, and routine screening for low-risk individuals.

The meta-analysis part was conducted using Stata (version 17; StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX, USA). When an analysis could not be executed using Stata, it was conducted using the online web version of Meta-DiSc [29]. Other statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.3.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A significance level of p<0.05 was used to determine statistical significance, except for the Q-statistics. All p-values were 2-tailed, and the confidence interval (CI) was set at a bilateral coverage rate of 95%.

RESULTS

1. Literature search results

In this systematic review, we screened 2,050 studies sourced from various recognized medical databases, as outlined in the ‘Data Sources and Searches.’ Initially, a refined set of 672 studies was included. Through careful assessments of titles and abstracts, 351 studies were excluded. Additionally, one unavailable manuscript was also excluded. Subsequently, a thorough assessment of the full texts of the remaining 320 articles was undertaken to determine their eligibility. Within this process, 294 studies were excluded due to various reasons. Ultimately, a total of 26 studies was rigorously selected for inclusion in this study. A clear flow chart depicting this selection process is presented in Fig. 1.

HPV, human papillomavirus.

We have summarized the research characteristics in Table S3. Among them, 22 studies (22/26, 84.6%) focus on the screening population, 3 studies (3/26, 11.5%) investigate the outpatient population, and 1 study includes both the screening and outpatient population (1/26, 3.8%). 14 studies (14/26, 53.8%) are Prospective, and 12 studies (12/26, 46.2%) are retrospective. Most of the studies were conducted in China (7/26, 26.9%), followed by America (5/26, 19.2%), the Netherlands (3/26, 11.5%). Additionally, 42.3% (11/26) of the included studies exclude subjects younger than 30 years. The characteristics of diagnostic tests utilized in these included studies are elaborated in Table S4. Notably, the Roche CINtec® PLUS Kit was utilized for P16/Ki67 immunostaining in most studies (20/26, 76.9%). Regarding HPV tests, Cobas 4800 (13/26, 50.0%) was most frequently utilized, followed by HC2 (5/26, 19.2%). Cytology sampling methods predominantly used were ThinPrep (16/26, 61.5%), with SurePath employed in 6 studies (6/26, 23.1%). Across most of the studies, the gold standard and criteria for P16/Ki67 positivity were consistent.

For the triage of HR-HPV-positive women, 24 studies presented head-to-head analyses that directly compared P16/Ki67 and cytology, explicitly focusing on the outcome of CIN2+. Seventeen studies with similar design focused on the outcome of CIN3+. Regarding the triage of HR-HPV-positive women with cytology-negative results, 8 studies provided accurate data on P16/Ki67 for CIN2+ outcomes and 4 studies for CIN3+ outcomes. 5 studies provided accurate data on both P16/Ki67 and cytology from the same population, focusing on the CIN2+ outcome. Overall, 27,198 women from 26 studies were included in the analysis with 12,252 being P16/Ki67-positive and 14,946 being P16/Ki67-negative.

2. Assessment of study quality

Fig. S1 depicts the results of the bias evaluation for the studies included in this review. Generally, 3 studies exhibited high patient selection bias, while 5 studies displayed high flow and timing bias. All included studies demonstrated low index test bias. Only 1 study had an unclear risk for the reference standard, with the remainder exhibiting a low risk of bias. The risk of bias may generally be avoided by using the same gold standard, P16/Ki67 positivity criteria, and implementing blinding methods for pathologists. None of the studies raised applicability concerns. In summary, the included studies exhibited a satisfactory methodological quality. More details about the risk of bias and applicability concerns were summarized in Fig. S2.

3. Diagnostic performance

Most of the meta-analyses in this study exhibited statistically significant heterogeneity (p<0.001, I2>50%; Figs. S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6), with the exception of the specificity of P16/Ki67 for the CIN3+ outcome in triaging HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative women (p=0.480, I2=0%; Fig. S5B). As the sensitivity of P16/Ki67 in the CIN3+ subgroup had statistically significant heterogeneity, the random-effects model was used to perform all meta-analyses using the Midas package. Funnel plots indicated minimal publication bias in most meta-analyses (p>0.050; Figs. S7 and S8), except for the assessment of P16/Ki67 for CIN2+ outcome in triaging HR-HPV positive women (p=0.010; Fig. S7A). To explore the sources of study heterogeneity and the pooled accuracy of subgroups, we conducted meta-regression and subgroup analyses (where the number of studies >10). The results of these analyses are presented in Table 1 and Figs. S9 and S10. The threshold effect results for each meta-analysis are summarized in Table S5, showing that only cytology for CIN2+ (r=0.625, p=0.001) and P16/Ki67 for the CIN3+ (r=0.615, p=0.009) in triaging HR-HPV-positive women exhibited threshold effects. Regarding the meta-analysis of the sensitivity and specificity of P16/Ki67 to triage HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative women for CIN2+ in head-to-head comparative studies, it failed to be conducted using Stata and thus was conducted the online web version of Meta-DiSc. Secondary outcomes for each meta-analysis are shown in Table S6.

Triage of HR-HPV-positive women

The sensitivity of triage HR-HPV-positive women using P16/Ki67 was 85% (95% CI=82%–88%) for identifying CIN2+ and 88% (95% CI=84%–91%) for CIN3+. Its specificity for in ruling out CIN2+ and CIN3+ was 63% (95% CI=58%–68%) and 57% (95% CI=51%–63%), respectively. In contrast, cytology demonstrated lower sensitivity, achieving 76% (95% CI=67%–83%) for CIN2+ and 79% (95% CI=68%–86%) for CIN3+. Likewise, its specificity was also lower than P16/Ki67, giving 57% (95% CI=46%–68%) for CIN2+ and 54% (95% CI=44%–63%) for CIN3+. All these results are summarized in Table 2.

The overall diagnostic accuracy of cytology to detect CIN2+ was superior to that of CIN3+ (SROC curves AUC: 0.73 vs. 0.70; Fig. S11B and D), whereas P16/Ki67 DS demonstrated a similar result (SROC curves AUC: 0.82 vs. 0.80; Fig. S11A and C). Also, the overall diagnostic performance of P16/Ki67 in detecting CIN2+ and CIN3+ was superior to cytology. These findings suggest that the P16/Ki67 outperformed liquid-based cytology in CIN detection and suggest its potential as a triage tool for cervical cancer screening, particularly for identifying CIN2+ lesions.

Meta-regression and subgroup analyses revealed that population resource, study design and age range were statistically significant factors influencing the sensitivity of P16/Ki67 for detecting CIN2+ and CIN3+ in triaging HR-HPV-positive women (Figs. S9A and S10A). Specifically, for detecting CIN2+ using the P16/Ki67, the sensitivity was superior in outpatient populations compared to the screening population (0.89 vs. 0.84, p<0.001). Additionally, studies with retrospective design showed higher sensitivity than those with Prospective design for detecting CIN2+ using P16/Ki67 (0.87 vs. 0.83, p<0.001). The sensitivity of studies included subjects younger than 30 years was significantly higher for CIN2+ detection using P16/Ki67 (0.86 vs. 0.83, p<0.001), with similar results also observed for CIN3+ detection. Regarding the specificity of P16/Ki67 for the triage of HR-HPV-positive women for detecting CIN2+, both the study design method and the age range were found to be statistically significant factors (Fig. S9A). More details are presented in Table 1.

Triage of HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative women

In head-to-head studies, cytology demonstrated a slightly higher sensitivity than P16/Ki67 for CIN2+ detection. However, the specificity of cytology was lower than that of P16/Ki67. These detailed results are demonstrated in Table 3. Notably, cytology was first used to triage HR-HPV-positive patients. Those with negative cytology results were then triaged using P16/Ki67. Thus, the sensitivity of P16/Ki67 highlighted an extra benefit for patients whom cytology failed to identify.

The P16/Ki67 revealed a superior diagnostic accuracy for detecting CIN2+ compared to CIN3+ (SROC curve AUC: 0.78 vs. 0.73; Fig. S12A and B). However, the DOR (6 for CIN2+ vs. 19 for CIN3+) and the Youden index (0.41 for CIN2+ vs. 0.59 for CIN3+) were lower for CIN2+ (Table S6). Therefore, P16/Ki67 exhibited comparable diagnostic performance for both CIN2+ and CIN3+ outcomes in the triage of HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative women.

In head-to-head studies, the underdiagnosis rate/false negative rate (1-sensitivity) was calculated within the same cohort. When considering CIN2+ as the outcome, cytology in HR-HPV-positive women exhibited a sensitivity of 0.72 (95% CI=0.67–0.77), giving a cytology underdiagnosis rate of 1−0.72=0.28, indicating that 28% of patients were missed by cytology. Meanwhile, among HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative women, the sensitivity of P16/Ki67 was 0.66 (95% CI=0.58–0.74). The sensitivity of 0.66 for P16/Ki67 suggested that 28%×66%=18.48% of the HPV-positive patients, initially missed by cytology, were correctly retrieved by P16/Ki67.

4. Fagan nomogram and triage management

In order to better illustrate the clinical applicability of our findings, the Fagan Nomogram was used to determine pretest probabilities and posttest probabilities after applying P16/Ki67 and cytology in the triage of HR-HPV population (17 studies) and HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative population (4 studies) for the CIN3+ outcome, shown in Fig. 2.

(B) Fagan Nomogram of cytology in triage of HR-HPV population for the outcomes CIN3+. (C) Fagan Nomogram of P16/Ki67 in triage of HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative population for the outcomes CIN3+.

CIN3+, cervical intraepithelial neoplasia of grade 3 or worse; HR-HPV, high-risk human papillomavirus; LR−, likelihood ratio-negative; LR+, likelihood ratio-positive; PTP, post-test probability.

The Fagan Nomograms in Fig. 2A and B showed that for an HR-HPV-positive woman, the underlying risk of CIN3+ is 17%, exceeding the 4% threshold and requires referral for colposcopy. However, universal referral of HR-HPV-positive women would lead to an unduly high rate of colposcopy referrals and an overburdening of healthcare resources. In contrast, a negative P16/Ki67 test reduces this risk to 4%, aligning with the threshold. It is recommended for follow-up at 1-year intervals and the reasons have been represented under the discussion section. In HR-HPV-positive women, even if the cytology test is negative, the threshold is exceeded (7%>4%) and colposcopy is required, so cytology for triage of HR-HPV-positive women is less effective (Fig. 2B).

For the triage of HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative population for the outcomes CIN3+, a positive P16/Ki67 test exceeds the threshold of 4% with 13 percentage points, prompting colposcopy referrals, whereas a negative P16/Ki67 test leads to a posttest risk of 1% (Fig. 2C), which lies in the middle risk region, where one is recommended for further surveillance.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we carefully evaluated the diagnostic performance of P16/Ki67 DS in the triage management of HR-HPV-positive and HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative women by summarizing the current research evidences. Comparisons of the diagnostic performance between P16/Ki67 and cytology were also conducted. To our knowledge, this is the first meta-analysis study to explore the diagnostic value of P16/Ki67 in the population mentioned above. We found that P16/Ki67 is an effective method for identifying CIN2+ and CIN3+ lesions in triage management of HR-HPV-positive women.

According to the meta-regression and subgroup analyses, the P16/Ki67 test is more suitable for use in the outpatient population than in the screening population because of its significantly higher sensitivity. P16/Ki67 exhibits higher sensitivity within the outpatient population, potentially due to its ability to identify individuals with underlying significant illnesses that may exhibit advanced pathologic stages, which could increase the likelihood of positive P16/Ki67 results. However, it is important to note that outpatient-population-based surveys, while potentially representing opportunistic screenings, may be prone to selection bias [7].

Furthermore, in retrospective studies, P16/Ki67 exhibited superior sensitivity and specificity for CIN2+ compared to Prospective studies. Generally, Prospective studies offer a stronger level of evidential support [30]. The diagnostic performance of P16/Ki67 in retrospective studies may be overestimated due to recall bias, suggesting that its performance in real-world settings could be inferior to retrospective studies. Therefore, the more comprehensive assessment of diagnostic performance P16/Ki67 could benefit from higher-quality study designs, such as Prospective studies and real-world studies.

Additionally, previous research has highlighted the influence of age on the diagnostic effectiveness of P16/Ki67 in HR-HPV-positive populations [31]. The P16/Ki67 CINtec PLUS Cytology test is approved for use in specific age groups: it serves as a triage test alongside the Cobas HPV test in primary screening for women aged 25 to 65. Furthermore, it can be used for women aged 30 to 65 who present with HPV positivity but have normal Pap test results [32]. This study found that the sensitivity of P16/Ki67 for CIN2+ detection triaging HR-HPV-positive women is higher in populations including subjects younger than 30 years. However, the specificity is higher in population excluded subjects under 30 years. These results could be attributed to accelerated cell growth and regeneration in young women [33,34], which led to more positive P16/Ki67 results and, thus, enhanced sensitivity. Meanwhile, physiological characteristics in young women may also contribute to increased non-cancerous cell proliferation [33], potentially causing more false positive cases and thus reducing the specificity. Another explanation for the reduced specificity could potentially be relevant to the fact that younger women may be more likely to be exposed to transient HPV infections, which can be naturally cleared by the immune system within 1–2 years without causing harm [35].

In this article, while the meta-analysis demonstrates P16/Ki67’s diagnostic superiority over cytology in HR-HPV-positive women, its global applicability is constrained by geographical disparities. The findings provide a strong foundation for regions with similar healthcare systems and resources to China (7/26, 26.9%), America (5/26, 19.2%) and European countries (12/26, 46.2%), but caution is needed when extrapolating to low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). China, America and European countries have well-established organized screening programs, whereas many LMICs lack systematic screening, affecting the generalizability of triage tools (P16/Ki67). Future research must address regional disparities to ensure equitable implementation of evidence-based triage strategies worldwide, while LMICs currently lack sufficient study results.

In our analysis utilizing Fagan nomograms for risk calculations, due to limitations in the available data of HPV16/18 and other high-risk types, we focused solely on evaluating the risks associated with negative and positive results for P16/Ki67 and cytology within the HR-HPV population with average HR-HPV subtype risk. Our calculated risk estimates are higher than those reported in the current clinical study [19], potentially due to heterogeneity that may not be fully accounted for through statistical adjustments. Additionally, this discrepancy may arise from increased statistical power and effect size in meta-analyses compared to individual studies: even if a risk is not significant in a single study, aggregating data from multiple studies can reveal higher overall risk estimates.

In HR-HPV-positive women, a negative P16/Ki67 test reduces CIN3+ immediate risk to 4%. We believe the risk lies in the middle region, further surveillance (1-year return) is advocated for individuals testing HPV-positive and P16/Ki67-negative for the following 2 reasons: 1) The 2019 ASCCP guidelines were validated using data from the Kaiser Permanente of Northern California (KPNC), Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)’s National Breast and Cervical Cancer Early Detection Program across specific screening categories (well-screened, rarely/never/unknown screened), and the Addressing THE Need for Advanced HPV Diagnostics (ATHENA) trial. In these studies, the immediate CIN3+ risks used to determine colposcopy threshold exceeded 4% (KPNC: 4.3%, CDC well-screened: 4.1%, CDC rarely/never/unknown screened: 6.2%, and ATHENA trial: 8.1%) [36]. 2) considering that the colposcopy referral threshold was a consensus decision specific to America, it needs to be adjusted for low-resource international settings or countries with more conservative screening practices [28,36]. So in the current study, based on the global practice variations, the management of 1-year return is accepted for the CIN3+ immediate risk 4%.

This study analyzed 26 original research articles focusing on cases of positivity for 14 HR-HPV (including HPV16/18) types. 13 studies utilized the cobas 4800 platform, which is capable of genotyping HPV16/18 and distinguishing them from 12 other HR-HPV types. However, most studies compared P16/Ki67 and liquid-based cytology for triaging women positive for any of the 14 HR-HPV types rather than directly referring HPV16/18-positive individuals to colposcopy. Notably, one study demonstrated that P16/Ki67 triage across all 14 HR-HPV types obviates the need to differentiate HPV16/18, offering similarly effective risk stratification with genotype-specific classification [37]. Future research could perform a meta-analysis on the performance of P16/Ki67 triage for the remaining 12 HR-HPV types within the existing framework of direct colposcopy referral for HPV16/18 positivity, which is constrained by limited primary data currently.

These meta-analyses exhibited several heterogeneities, and the potential contributors are as follows: 1) population resources, study design, and age range; 2) different countries, economic disparities, different cervical cancer prevention and control strategies, as well as different screening program implementation statutes; 3) HPV subtypes; 4) the confirmation of threshold effects indicates potential discrepancies in criteria adopted by various regions, hospitals, and laboratories [25]; 5) the diagnostic level of histopathology and cytopathology; and 6) differences in reagent manufacturers. As diagnostic tests, heterogeneities are difficult to avoid due to the objective factors mentioned above. However, in this study, head-to-head studies were selected for the comparisons of P16/Ki67 versus cytology, so these heterogeneities had less impact on the comparative results. Secondly, these heterogeneities did not undermine the rigor of study design, and the results of the included studies were real and reliable, so the results of the meta-analysis were convincing.

Furthermore, the findings of this study are consistent with the recommendations of the Enduring Consensus Cervical Cancer Screening and Management Guidelines [19]. The recommendations were analyzed using data from several established studies [37,38,39,40], and thus, this meta-analysis synthesized them and other studies to obtain more comprehensive and integrated evidence and further supported the recommendations by pooled accuracy in this study. Therefore, we hope to utilize the findings of this study to aid in implementing the recommendations, that can expand the proportion and number of people who benefit, to accelerate the elimination of cervical cancer.

This study has some strengths and limitations. The strengths are as follows: 1) this is the first systemic review and meta-analysis study to explore the diagnostic value of P16/Ki67 in the triage management of HR-HPV-positive women and HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative women, providing strong evidence for the utilization of P16/Ki67 in the triage management of this population; 2) comparative analyses for P16/Ki67 and cytology are head-to-head comparisons in the same population, thereby enhancing the evidential strength of the relevant findings; 3) the study performed meta-regression and subgroup analyses to explore the performance in the different subgroups, thereby enhancing the applicability of the findings depending on the different scenarios; 4) this study used the underdiagnosis rate and Fagan Nomograms to represent that P16/Ki67 could optimize cervical cancer triaging, providing more robust evidence for its generalization. The limitations are as follows: 1) heterogeneities could be high in this study while also revealing the complexities of cervical cancer screening and triaging in different scenarios. These heterogeneities have been roughly discussed above; 2) one meta-analysis may be at risk of publication bias. To present the comparison results entirely, we kept the meta-analysis. Due to the large sample size and the inclusion of head-to-head studies, we believe publication bias had less impact on the results; 3) Original studies validating P16/Ki67 may be susceptible to researcher bias. However, rigorous quality evaluation, bias-risk assessment, comprehensive literature review, and systematic steps to mitigate publication bias were implemented to reduce potential biases. Though researcher bias might impact individual study reliability, the consistent results from 26 studies provide robust evidence of the clinical value of P16/Ki67.

In conclusion, this study has demonstrated that P16/Ki67 outperforms liquid-based cytology in detecting CIN2+/CIN3+ in triage management, making it a viable triage tool for cervical cancer screening, particularly for enhanced detection of CIN2+ in triaging HR-HPV-positive women. Furthermore, P16/Ki67 demonstrates a satisfactory ability to identify CIN2+/CIN3+ patients missed in the co-testing strategy, suggesting its potential to be utilized as a complementary tool for triaging HR-HPV-positive women with cytology-negative results. The P16/Ki67 also effectively reduces unnecessary colposcopy referrals and conserves healthcare resources within the HR-HPV-positive and the HR-HPV-positive but cytology-negative populations with a CIN3+ outcome. Therefore, expedited adoption of P16/Ki67 in the triage management of these populations could accelerate the elimination of cervical cancer.

Product–Congruence Games: A Unified Impartial-Game Framework for RSA ( ϕ ​ – ​ MuM \phi\text{--}\mathrm{MuM} ) and AES ( poly – ​ MuM \textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM} )
Satyam Tyagi

Abstract
RSA exponent reduction and AES S-box inversion share a hidden commonality: both are governed by the same impartial combinatorial principle, which we call a Product–Congruence Game ( PCG \mathrm{PCG} ). A PCG \mathrm{PCG} tracks play via the modular (or finite-field) product of heap values, providing a single invariant that unifies the algebraic cores of these two ubiquitous symmetric and asymmetric cryptosystems. We instantiate this framework with two companion games. First, ϕ ​ – ​ MuM \phi\text{--}\mathrm{MuM} , where a left-associated “multi-secret” RSA exponent chain compresses via our Compression Theorem into the game of Multiplicative Modular Nim, PCG ⁡ ( k , { 1 } ) \mathrm{PCG}(k,\{1\}) , with k = ord N ⁡ ( g ) k=\operatorname{ord}_{N}(g) . The losing predicate of this game can then be factorized using the Chinese Remainder Theorem, mirroring RSA’s implementation. Second, poly – ​ MuM \textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM} , our model for finite-field inversion in systems like AES. For poly – ​ MuM \textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM} , we prove the single-hole property holds within its Threshold Region, which in turn implies that the Sprague-Grundy ( SG \operatorname{SG} ) values are multiplicative for disjunctive sums in that region. Beyond these concrete instances, we establish four structural theorems for general Product–Congruence Games PCG ⁡ ( m , R ) \mathrm{PCG}(m,R) : (i) single-heap repair above the modulus, (ii) ultimate period m m per coordinate, (iii) exact and asymptotic losing densities, and (iv) confinement of optimal play to a finite Indeterminacy Region . An operation-alignment collapse principle finally explains why some variants degenerate to a single aggregate while MuM, ϕ ​ – ​ MuM \phi\text{--}\mathrm{MuM} and poly – ​ MuM \textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM} retain non-trivial structure. All ingredients (orders, CRT, finite fields) are classical; the novelty lies in this unified aggregation-compression viewpoint that embeds RSA and AES inside one impartial-game framework, together with the structural and collapse theorems for the resulting games.

From exponent chain to product congruence (Compression Theorem).

Fix an RSA modulus

2
The Exponent Chain Game and its Compression

This section isolates the RSA lens (the “exponent-chain” game) and shows it

compresses
exactly to MuM at modulus
k=\operatorname{ord}_{N}(g)
.

2.1
Game definition

Fix an RSA modulus
N\geq 2
and
g\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
of order

k=\operatorname{ord}_{N}(g)
(so
k\mid\lambda(N)
). The exponent-chain
(
“
\phi\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
”
) game is:

Positions

\mathbf{h}=(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with each
h_{i}\geq 1
.

Evaluation

E(\mathbf{h})=(((g^{h_{1}})^{h_{2}})^{h_{3}}\cdots)^{h_{n}}\pmod{N}.

Losing predicate

\mathbf{h}
is losing iff
E(\mathbf{h})\equiv g\pmod{N}
.

Moves

Pick an index
i
with
h_{i}>1
and replace
h_{i}
by some

h_{i}^{\prime}<h_{i}
.
Once
h_{i}\geq k
we restrict the decrement to

1\leq h_{i}-h_{i}^{\prime}\leq k-1
to forbid residue-preserving “null moves.”

Flattening.

Because
(g^{a})^{b}=g^{ab}
we have

E(\mathbf{h})\equiv g^{H(\mathbf{h})}\quad\text{with}\quad H(\mathbf{h}):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}.

2.2
Compression and CRT factorisation

Theorem 2.1

(Compression Theorem)
.

E(\mathbf{h})\equiv g\pmod{N}

iff

H(\mathbf{h})\equiv 1\pmod{k}
.

Proof.

Flattening the exponent chain gives

E(\mathbf{h})\equiv g^{H(\mathbf{h})}\pmod{N}
.
By the definition of multiplicative order
k=\operatorname{ord}_{N}(g)
,

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{h})\equiv g\pmod{N}

\displaystyle\iff g^{H(\mathbf{h})}\equiv g^{1}\pmod{N}

\displaystyle\iff g^{H(\mathbf{h})-1}\equiv 1\pmod{N}

\displaystyle\iff k\mid\bigl(H(\mathbf{h})-1\bigr)

\displaystyle\iff H(\mathbf{h})\equiv 1\pmod{k}.\qed

Corollary 2.2

(CRT factorisation)
.

If
k=\prod_{j=1}^{r}p_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}
, then
\mathbf{h}
is losing iff

H(\mathbf{h})\equiv 1\pmod{p_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}}
for all
j
.

Remark 1

(Strategic isomorphism to MuM)
.

Theorem

2.1
shows the chain game is strategically isomorphic to

\mathrm{PCG}(k,\{1\})
(MuM at modulus
k
): losing means
“
\prod h_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{k}
.” Bounding each decrement by
<k
is

exactly
the MuM move rule and prevents losing
\to
losing null moves.

Example 2.3
.

Let
N=15
and
g=2
. Then
k=\operatorname{ord}_{15}(2)=4
.
A position
(h_{1},h_{2})
is losing iff
h_{1}h_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
; e.g.
(1,1)
,
(3,3)
,
(5,1)
.

The
structure
of the compressed game (single-heap repair, Threshold vs. Indeterminacy, SG behaviour, densities, periodicity) is developed once and for
all in the next section, inside the general PCG framework that also covers the
AES/
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
case.

3
Product–Congruence Games (PCG): the unified framework

We now introduce the game class that simultaneously models

•

classical MuM (
\mathrm{PCG}(m,\{1\})
);

•

the compressed RSA exponent-chain game (“
\phi\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
”);

•

the AES field variant (
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
).

3.1
Definition (ring / field form)

Definition 3.1

(PCG over a finite ring)
.

Let
R
be a finite commutative ring with unit group
R^{\times}
.
Let
(S,<)
be a well-ordered set of heap labels
(often
S\subset\mathbb{N}
), and let

\psi:S\to R^{\times}
be an
injective
(canonical) encoding.
Fix a non-empty losing set
L\subseteq R^{\times}
.

Position

\mathbf{h}=(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})\in S^{n}
.

Invariant

\displaystyle\phi(\mathbf{h}):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\psi(h_{i})\in R^{\times}.

Move

Choose
h_{j}
and replace it by

h_{j}^{\prime}<h_{j}
(so
\psi(h_{j}^{\prime})\neq\psi(h_{j})
).

Losing

\mathbf{h}
is losing iff
\phi(\mathbf{h})\in L
.

We denote the game by
\mathrm{PCG}(R,S,\psi,L)
.

Two cryptographic lenses (our focus).

•

\phi\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
(RSA).

R=\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
with
k=\operatorname{ord}_{N}(g)
;
S=\{1,2,\dots\}
;

\psi(h)=h\bmod k
;
L=\{1\}
.

•

\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
(AES).

R=

\mathbb{F}_{q}
(
q=p^{n}
);

S=\{1,\dots,q-1\}
;
\psi=s
(canonical bijection onto

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
);
L=\{1\}
.

For clarity the structural proofs below use the
numeric
instance

\mathrm{PCG}(m,R)
with
m\geq 2
; the field results are identical with

m
replaced by
q-1
and
S=[1,q-2]
.

3.2
Threshold vs. finite Indeterminacy Region

Definition 3.2

(Regions)
.

For
\mathrm{PCG}(m,R)
put

I_{m}:=[1,m-1]^{n}\quad\text{(finite Indeterminacy Region)},\qquad T_{m}:=\mathbb{N}^{n}\setminus I_{m}\quad\text{(Threshold Region)}.

If some heap
t_{j}\geq m
then
t_{j},t_{j}-1,\dots,t_{j}-(m-1)
cover every
residue mod
m
, which drives the next two lemmas.

Lemma 3.3

(One-move repair in
T_{m}
)
.

Let
t\in T_{m}
with

\Pi(t):=\prod_{i}t_{i}\notin R
.
Then there is a move, acting on a heap
\geq m
, that produces

t^{\prime}
with
\Pi(t^{\prime})\in R
.

Proof.

Pick
t_{j}\geq m
and set

C=\prod_{i\neq j}t_{i}\bmod m\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.
For some
r\in R
choose
d\in[0,m-1]
with

t_{j}-d\equiv C^{-1}r\pmod{m}
; this
d
exists because the residue
window is complete. Replacing
t_{j}
by
t_{j}-d
yields

\Pi(t^{\prime})\equiv r\pmod{m}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Losing-move blocking in
T_{m}
)
.

If
t\in T_{m}
and
\Pi(t)\in R
, then every legal move gives

\Pi(t^{\prime})\notin R
.

Proof.

With
t_{j}\geq m
all residues are reachable; hence no move can keep the
(unique) losing residue in place.
∎

Proposition 3.5

(Outcome classification on
T_{m}
)
.

For
t\in T_{m}
,

t\in\mathcal{P}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\Pi(t)\in R,\qquad t\in\mathcal{N}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\Pi(t)\notin R.

Proof.

Combine Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
.
∎

3.3
Single-hole property and SG multiplicativity on
T_{m}

Definition 3.6

(Single-hole property)
.

A position
G
has SP if the set

\{\operatorname{SG}(G^{\prime}):G^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Opt}(G)\}

omits exactly one value of the SG domain.

Proposition 3.7

(SP in
T_{m}
)
.

Every component game in
T_{m}
has SP.

Proof.

From a maximal heap (
\geq m
) one can reach every smaller residue class,
so the option SG values sweep all but the current one.
∎

Theorem 3.8

(SG multiplicativity on
T_{m}
)
.

For
G_{1},G_{2}\in T_{m}
in
\mathrm{PCG}(m,\{1\})
,

\operatorname{SG}(G_{1}\oplus G_{2})\equiv\operatorname{SG}(G_{1})\,\operatorname{SG}(G_{2})\pmod{m}.

Proof.

Both components satisfy SP; their option sets are complements of single
values
a,b
. Options of
G_{1}\oplus G_{2}
miss exactly
ab\pmod{m}
;

\operatorname{mex}
(with the fixed indexing) returns
ab
.
∎

Remark 2
.

Inside
I_{m}
a heap
<m
does not see all residues; SP can fail and the
multiplicative law need not hold. The field case behaves identically with

T_{q}/I_{q}
.

3.4
Normalisation (aggregation-compression) in
I_{m}

Write
A(t)=\Pi(t)\bmod m
.
Define the analytic normalisation

\mathrm{Norm}(t):=(A(t)),

i.e. compress all heaps to the single canonical heap carrying the product.

Lemma 3.9

(Normalisation preserves the invariant)
.

A(\mathrm{Norm}(t))=A(t)
. Hence
t
is losing iff

\mathrm{Norm}(t)
is losing.

Lemma 3.10

(Normalisation
+
one legal repair)
.

If
t\in I_{m}
and
A(t)\notin R
then from
\mathrm{Norm}(t)

a single legal move reaches
R
.

Proof.

Apply
Lemma

3.3
to the one-heap normalised position.
∎

The field variant is identical with
m
replaced by
q-1
and the
canonical representative map
C
in place of the identity.

3.5
Ultimate periodicity (numeric) and densities

Theorem 3.11

(Ultimate periodicity)
.

Fix
t_{-j}
and view the outcome as a function of one heap
x
.
Then
f_{j}(x)
is ultimately periodic with period
m
:
for all
x\geq m
,
f_{j}(x+m)=f_{j}(x)
.

Proof.

For
x\geq m
the position lies in
T_{m}
; by

Proposition

3.5
its outcome depends only on

\Pi(t_{-j})\,x\bmod m
, hence only on
x\bmod m
.
∎

Proposition 3.12

(Exact / asymptotic losing densities)
.

In finite-group variants (field
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
with
|G|=q-1
; RSA

\phi\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
with
|G|=k
) the losing density is exactly

1/|G|
. For unbounded integer MuM the density tends to
1/m
(or

1/\varphi(m)
if restricted to units).

Proof.

Fix
n-1
coordinates; the last heap is uniquely determined to hit the
losing residue, giving exactly
|G|^{\,n-1}
losing positions among

|G|^{\,n}
. In the unbounded model, residues become asymptotically
independent and uniform.
∎

4
Collapse Boundaries and Operation Alignment

Whenever the
one-heap move set
on a sufficiently large heap can generate

all
target congruence classes of the invariant, the multi-heap game
collapses (outside a finite kernel) to a single-heap problem on the aggregate
invariant. We illustrate this with two toy games, then state a general
operation-alignment principle, and finally explain why MuM/
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
avoid a
total
collapse.

4.1
Additive Sum-Congruence Game

Fix
m\geq 2
. Positions are

\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}^{n}
,
the losing predicate is

\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\equiv s\pmod{m},

and a move replaces any heap by any strictly smaller positive integer. Define

\displaystyle I_{m}^{+}

\displaystyle:=[1,m-1]^{n},

\displaystyle\text{(finite region).}

Theorem 4.1

(Additive collapse)
.

If some
t_{j}\geq m
, then by moving on heap
j
alone one can reach

every
residue class for the total sum modulo
m
in a single move
(in particular the losing residue
s
). Hence every position outside

I_{m}^{+}
is equivalent to its residue-class representative; the multi-heap
structure is trivial (a one-heap game) there.

Proof.

Let
S=\sum_{i}t_{i}
and choose
j
with
t_{j}\geq m
.
For any target residue
r\pmod{m}
select

d\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}
with
S-d\equiv r\pmod{m}
,
set
t_{j}^{\prime}:=t_{j}-d\geq 1
, and observe that the new sum is
S-d\equiv r
.
∎

4.2
Divisor-Move Product Game

Fix
m\geq 2
. Positions are

\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}^{n}
,
the invariant is

P(\mathbf{t})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\pmod{m},

and a move replaces one heap
t_{j}
by any proper divisor
d\mid t_{j}
,

d<t_{j}
. Let
G:=(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
and denote
\bar{x}:=x\bmod m
.

\displaystyle I_{m}^{\times}

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{\mathbf{t}:\forall j,\,t_{j}<m\text{ or }\gcd(t_{j},m)\neq 1\bigr\},

\displaystyle\text{(no heap is both large and a unit).}

Theorem 4.2

(Divisor collapse)
.

Assume there exists
M
such that for every
t\geq M
with

\gcd(t,m)=1
the set

\Bigl\{\overline{d}\,\overline{t}^{-1}\in G:d\mid t,\;d<t\Bigr\}

generates
G
.
Then every position outside
I_{m}^{\times}
is strongly equivalent to the
single-heap game holding the aggregate product
P(\mathbf{t})
:
one heap alone can move
P(\mathbf{t})
to any unit residue (in particular
the losing one), and all further play can be simulated on that aggregate.

Proof.

Pick a heap
t_{j}\geq M
with
\gcd(t_{j},m)=1
.
Replacing
t_{j}
by a divisor
d
multiplies
P(\mathbf{t})
by the unit

\overline{d}\,\overline{t_{j}}^{-1}\in G
.
By hypothesis these units generate
G
, so heap
j
alone can realise any
target residue. Once
P(\mathbf{t})
is set, the remaining coordinates are
strategically irrelevant.
∎

Remark 3
.

The hypothesis is a clean
operation-alignment
condition: the
local move semigroup generated by one large heap acts transitively on the target
group
G
. Without it, collapse need not occur.

4.3
A general operation-alignment principle

Both toy games exemplify the following abstraction.

Let
(M,\circ)
be a cancellative commutative monoid and

\pi:M\to Q
a surjective homomorphism onto a finite monoid
Q
that contains
a losing set
L\subseteq Q
.
A position is
\mathbf{h}=(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})
with
h_{i}\in M
; the invariant is

\phi(\mathbf{h})=\pi(\circ_{i}h_{i})
.

Principle 4.3

(Operation alignment)
.

Suppose there is a finite kernel
B\subset M
such that for every

x\in M\setminus B
the set of one-heap moves from
x
maps (under
\pi
)
to a subset that generates
Q
.
Then every position with at least one heap outside
B
collapses to the
single-heap game on
\phi(\mathbf{h})
: one heap alone can move

\phi(\mathbf{h})
to any target in
Q
(including
L
).

Proof.

Same argument as in
Theorems

4.1
and
4.2
:
transitivity of one heap on
Q
eliminates all strategic relevance of the
other heaps.
∎

4.4
Why MuM (and
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
) avoid total collapse

MuM and
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
lie
off
the operation-alignment axis:

•

The invariant aggregates
multiplicatively
(product in

\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
), but the legal move is an

additive
canonical-index decrement. One large heap therefore
does
not
act transitively on the target multiplicative group.

•

Consequently, outside the finite Indeterminacy Region we do

not
collapse to a trivial single-heap game.
Instead we retain strong
local
structure:
(i) single-heap repair in
T_{\bullet}
;
(ii) normalisation (aggregation-compression) inside
I_{\bullet}

followed by one legal repair;
(iii)
multiplicative
SG behaviour for disjunctive sums in

T_{\bullet}
.

5
Unified RSA / AES (
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
) Perspective

We now restate the RSA (exponent-chain /
\phi\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
) and AES (
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
) lenses

inside the same PCG formalism
, give the complete finite-field formulation of

\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
, and then place both in a side-by-side comparison table.

5.1
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
over finite fields (canonical reps)

Let
q=p^{n}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/\langle I(x)\rangle
for a fixed
irreducible polynomial
I(x)
. Write

s:\{1,\dots,q-1\}\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}

for the canonical bijection between integers and standard polynomial representatives,
and let
C:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\to\{1,\dots,q-1\}
be its inverse.

Definition 5.1

(
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
)
.

A position is a vector
\mathbf{h}=(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})
with
h_{i}\in\{1,\dots,q-1\}
.
A move chooses a heap
h_{j}
and replaces it by a strictly smaller
h^{\prime}_{j}<h_{j}

(hence
s(h^{\prime}_{j})\neq s(h_{j})
).
The invariant and losing predicate are

\phi(\mathbf{h}):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}s(h_{i})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times},\qquad\mathbf{h}\text{ is losing }\iff\phi(\mathbf{h})=1.

We write
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}(q)
.

Regions.

Define the field
Threshold Region

T_{q}:=\{\mathbf{h}:\text{some }h_{j}=q-1\}

and the (finite)
Indeterminacy Region

I_{q}:=[1,q-2]^{n}
.

Lemma 5.2

(Single-heap repair)
.

If
\phi(\mathbf{h})\neq 1
and some
h_{j}=q-1
, then the move

h_{j}\longrightarrow C\!\bigl(s(h_{j})\,\phi(\mathbf{h})^{-1}\bigr)\;<\;q-1

is legal and reaches a losing state in one step.

Lemma 5.3

(Normalisation inside
I_{q}
)
.

If
\phi(\mathbf{h})\neq 1
and
\mathbf{h}\in I_{q}
, replace all heaps by the single heap

H_{\mathrm{new}}:=C\!\bigl(\phi(\mathbf{h})\bigr)\in\{1,\dots,q-1\}.

This normalised one-heap position has the same invariant and admits a one-move repair
to a losing state (Lemma
5.2
).

Definition 5.4

(Single-hole property)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be the SG-value domain (with
0
losing).
A position
G
has the
single-hole property
if

\{\operatorname{SG}(G^{\prime}):G^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Opt}(G)\}=\mathcal{V}\setminus\{\operatorname{SG}(G)\}.

Proposition 5.5

(Single-hole in
T_{q}
)
.

Every component game of
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
(q) that lies in
T_{q}
has the single-hole property.

Sketch.

From
q-1
one can move to
every
smaller canonical representative, so the
option SG values sweep all values except the current one.
∎

Theorem 5.6

(SG multiplicativity for
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
in
T_{q}
)
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2}
be positions of
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
(q), each with at least one heap equal to
q-1
.
Then

\operatorname{SG}(G_{1}\oplus G_{2})\;=\;\operatorname{SG}(G_{1})\cdot\operatorname{SG}(G_{2}),

where multiplication is taken in the fixed SG-value group for the game (after any
indexing of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\cup\{0\}
).

Sketch.

Each summand enjoys the single-hole property; thus the option sets of
G_{1}\oplus G_{2}

are the complement of one unique value, and
\operatorname{mex}
returns its product—exactly as in
the numeric MuM proof.
∎

Density.

By the finite-group counting argument (Prop.
3.12
), the losing density among
all
(q-1)^{n}
positions is exactly
1/(q-1)
, independent of
n
.

6
Conclusion and Future Work

Product-Congruence Games provide a single impartial-game lens through which the
algebraic “cores” of RSA and AES can be viewed side-by-side. By compressing a
left-associated RSA exponent chain to
\mathrm{PCG}(k,\{1\})
with
k=\operatorname{ord}_{N}(g)

and modelling AES S-box inversion with its polynomial analogue over
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
,
we have shown that two ciphers usually treated as mathematically disjoint
share a common combinatorial skeleton.

Main contributions

1.

Unified RSA/AES class of games.

RSA exponent reduction and the AES S-box inversion both fit into one

Product-Congruence Game
framework
(
\phi\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
/
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
) driven by a multiplicative invariant.

2.

Compression Theorem (
\mathrm{RSA}\Rightarrow\mathrm{MuM}
).

The left-associated exponent chain compresses to

\mathrm{PCG}(k,\{1\})
with
k=\operatorname{ord}_{N}(g)
; the losing predicate then
CRT-factorises exactly as in RSA practice.

3.

PCG structure: Threshold vs. finite Indeterminacy.

Every instance splits into
T_{\bullet}
and the finite
I_{\bullet}
;
in either numeric or field settings, positions in
T_{\bullet}
are
decided by the invariant alone.

4.

Single-hole
\bm{\Rightarrow}
SG multiplicativity.

Both numeric MuM/
\phi\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
and field
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
have the
single-hole property on
T_{\bullet}
, giving
multiplicative

Sprague-Grundy values under disjunctive sum.

5.

Normalisation (aggregation-compression).

Any position in
I_{\bullet}
normalises to one heap (same invariant)
and then repairs to
\mathcal{P}
in a single legal move; identical
solver for integers and fields.

6.

Collapse boundaries via operation-alignment.

A general principle explains why aligned variants collapse to a
single-heap game, while MuM/
\textit{poly}\text{--}\mathrm{MuM}
avoid total collapse
yet retain strong local structure in
T_{\bullet}
.

7.

Exact / asymptotic losing densities.

In finite-group PCGs the losing density is exactly
1/|G|
;
for unbounded integer MuM it approaches
1/m

(or
1/\varphi(m)
when restricted to units).

Future directions

1.

Discrete-log / Diffie-Hellman PCGs.

Formulate PCGs over DL/DH groups; study Threshold/Indeterminacy and SG
behaviour in that setting.

2.

Elliptic-curve PCGs.

Port the framework to the group
(E(\mathbb{F}_{q}),+)
; investigate whether
SG-multiplicativity or a finite Indeterminacy region persists.

3.

Cryptanalytic angles.

Examine whether the Threshold/Indeterminacy split, normalisation, or
SP give insight into parameter choices or side-channel robustness.

4.

Non-abelian / semigroup extensions.

Replace
R^{\times}
by non-abelian or merely cancellative semigroups
and test how far aggregation-compression and SP extend.

5.

Composite moduli and CRT SG factorisation.

For numeric MuM with composite
m
, characterise how SG values
compose across CRT factors and when mixed Thresholds preserve
multiplicativity.

6.

Rule variants.

Re-analyse under misère play, scoring rules, or in the thermograph
framework.

These results place two cornerstone cryptographic operations under a single
impartial-game umbrella and open several promising paths for exporting the
technique to further algebraic settings.

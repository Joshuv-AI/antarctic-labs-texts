A surface with representable CH 0 \operatorname{CH_{0}} -group but no universal zero-cycle
Theodosis Alexandrou

Abstract
. We introduce a new obstruction to the existence of a universal 0 0 -cycle on a smooth projective complex variety. As an application, we construct a smooth projective complex surface whose Chow group of 0 0 -cycles is representable but which does not admit a universal 0 0 -cycle. This provides a two-dimensional analogue of Voisin’s recent threefold counterexample to a question of Colliot-Thélène. As a further consequence, we exhibit the first example of a smooth projective threefold of Kodaira dimension zero carrying a non-torsion Hodge class of degree 4 4 that is not algebraic. The construction relies on the geometry of bielliptic surfaces of type 2 2 .

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective complex variety of dimension
d=\dim X
. Given a base-point
x_{0}\in X(\mathbf{C}),
the universal morphism

\al_{X}\colon X\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\textnormal{Alb}(X)

to the Albanese variety of
X
(see
[
20
, Theorem 1.4.4]
) induces the so-called Abel–Jacobi map for
0
-cycles on
X

(1.1)

\alpha_{X}\colon\operatorname{CH_{0}}(X)_{\hom}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\textnormal{Alb}(X).

The group homomorphism
\alpha_{X}
no longer depends on the choice of the base-point and it is known to be surjective and regular. The latter asserts that, for any smooth projective complex variety
Z
equipped with a base-point
z_{0}\in Z(\mathbf{C})
, and for any codimension-
d
cycle
[\Gamma]\in\textnormal{CH}^{d}(Z\times X)
, the assignment

(1.2)

\psi_{(Z,[\Gamma])}\colon Z\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\textnormal{Alb}(X),\quad z\longmapsto\alpha_{X}\circ[\Gamma]_{\ast}(z-z_{0})

defines a morphism of algebraic varieties; see
[
20
, Definition 1.6.1]
. In fact, the Abel–Jacobi map
\alpha_{X}
also enjoys the universal property of being initial among all regular homomorphisms from
\operatorname{CH_{0}}(X)_{\hom}
to abelian varieties; see
[
20
, Example 1.8(b)]
.

Since the homomorphism
\alpha_{X}
in (
1.1
) is regular and surjective, there exists a codimension-
d
cycle
[\Gamma]\in\textnormal{CH}^{d}(\textnormal{Alb}(X)\times X)
and a positive integer
n
such that the morphism
\psi_{(\textnormal{Alb}(X),[\Gamma])}
in (
1.2
), with respect to the chosen base-point
0_{\textnormal{Alb}(X)}\in\textnormal{Alb}(X)(\mathbf{C})
, is equal to
n\cdot\Id_{\textnormal{Alb}(X)}
; see
[
20
, Corollary 1.6.3]
.

The present work is concerned with a strengthening of this statement, in the sense of the following property of the variety
X
, first introduced and studied by Voisin; see
[
27
,
28
]
.

Definition 1.1

(
[
29
]
, Definition 1.1)
.

We say that
X
admits a
universal
0
-cycle
if there exists a codimension-
d
cycle
[\Gamma]\in\textnormal{CH}^{d}(\textnormal{Alb}(X)\times X)
such that the morphism
\psi_{(\textnormal{Alb}(X),[\Gamma])}
in (
1.2
), with respect to the base-point
0_{\textnormal{Alb}(X)}\in\textnormal{Alb}(X)(\mathbf{C})
, is the identity map
\Id_{\textnormal{Alb}(X)}
on the Albanese variety of
X
.

If
X
is a curve, then the Poincaré divisor on
\text{Jac}(X)\times X
induces a universal
0
-cycle on
X
. In contrast, Voisin showed in
[
28
]
that for each
d\geq 2
, there exists a smooth projective complex
d
-fold
X
admitting no universal
0
-cycle; see
[
28
, Corollary 0.14]
.

In
[
10
]
, Colliot–Thélène considered whether the existence of a universal
0
-cycle persists under additional geometric assumptions on a smooth projective complex variety
X
. In particular, he asked whether this property holds for varieties with representable
\operatorname{CH_{0}}
-group, that is, for which the homomorphism
\alpha_{X}
in (
1.1
) is an isomorphism; see
[
19
,
22
]
. Building on earlier ideas of Benoist–Ottem
[
5
]
, Voisin subsequently constructed in
[
29
]
a smooth projective complex threefold
X
with representable
\operatorname{CH_{0}}
-group but admitting no universal
0
-cycle.

This leads to the following question, raised by Totaro after Voisin’s talk at the workshop
Hodge Theory and Algebraic Cycles
(Clay Mathematics Institute, September 2025).

Question 1.2
.

Does there exist a smooth projective complex surface
S
whose Chow group of
0
-cycles
\textnormal{CH}_{0}(S)
is representable, but which admits no universal
0
-cycle?

The main result of the present paper answers Question
1.2
in the affirmative.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
E
be a smooth complex elliptic curve with
\End(E)=\mathbb{Z}
. There exists a smooth projective complex surface
S
with
\textnormal{Alb}(S)\cong E
for which
\operatorname{CH_{0}}(S)
is representable, while
S
admits no universal
0
-cycle.

Recall that ruled surfaces have representable Chow groups of
0
-cycles and do admit a universal
0
-cycle; this follows directly from the corresponding result for curves.

More generally, let
X
be a non-uniruled smooth projective surface with geometric genus
p_{g}(X)=0
and irregularity
q(X)\neq 0
. By work of Beauville, the structure of such surfaces is well understood; see
[
4
, Chapter VI]
. Specifically,
X
is birational to a quotient

\bigl(C\times D\bigr)/\Gi,

where
C
and
D
are smooth projective curves and
\Gi
is a finite group acting faithfully on both curves, without fixed points on
C\times D
. Moreover, at least one of the curves is elliptic. Up to exchanging
C
and
D
, one has

C/\Gi\cong E,\qquad D/\Gi\cong\mathbf{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}},

where
E
is an elliptic curve. In this situation, the induced fibration

(1.3)

\phi_{X}\colon X\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E

coincides with the Albanese morphism of
X
.

It was shown in
[
29
]
that if the index of the fibration
\phi_{X}
in (
1.3
), defined as the greatest common divisor of the degrees
\deg(C/E)
, where
C\subset X
ranges over irreducible curves dominating
E
, is equal to one, then
X
admits a universal
0
-cycle. In particular, this condition is satisfied when the group
\Gi
is cyclic; see
[
29
, Proposition 4.4]
.

Our example in Theorem
1.3
arises from a very general bielliptic surface
S
of type 2 (see
[
3
,
25
]
) whose Albanese variety
\textnormal{Alb}(S)
is isomorphic to the prescribed elliptic curve
E
. In this situation, the Albanese fibration
\phi_{S}\colon S\rightarrow\textnormal{Alb}(S)\cong E
has index 2. As observed in
[
29
,
\lx@sectionsign 2.2
]
, this condition alone does not suffice to imply that
S
admits no universal
0
-cycle. The proof of Theorem
1.3
therefore rests on the following geometric result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\neq 2,
and let
E
be an elliptic curve over
k
with
\End(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Set
\Delta:=\Spec k[[t]],
and fix an algebraic closure
K
of its function field. Then there exists a regular, flat, projective scheme
\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\Delta,
together with a morphism

\phi_{\mathcal{S}}\colon\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E\times\Delta

such that the following properties hold:

(1)

The geometric generic fibre
S_{K}
is a bielliptic surface of type
2
, and the induced morphism
\phi_{S_{K}}\colon S_{K}\rightarrow E\times_{k}K
coincides with the Albanese fibration of
S_{K}
.

(2)

The special fibre
S_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}
is a reduced simple normal crossings divisor on
\mathcal{S}
whose dual graph is a chain; that is, each intersection
R_{i}\cap R_{i+1}
is a smooth irreducible curve, and
R_{i}\cap R_{j}=\varnothing
whenever
j\notin\{i-1,i,i+1\}
.

(3)

For each
i,
let
\al_{i}:\textnormal{Alb}(R_{i})\rightarrow E
denote the homomorphism of Albanese varieties induced by the restriction of
\phi_{\mathcal{S}}
to
R_{i}
. Then the sum morphism

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\al_{i}\colon\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\textnormal{Alb}(R_{i})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E

does not admit a section.

As we shall explain, the non-existence of a section in (
3
) provides the key obstruction to the existence of a universal
0
-cycle on the geometric generic fibre of the family
\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\Delta
; see Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
.

We conclude the introduction with the following application of Theorem
1.3
to the integral Hodge conjecture.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
E
be a smooth elliptic curve over
\mathbf{C}
with
\End(E)=\mathbb{Z}
. Then there exists a bielliptic surface
S
of type 2 whose Albanese variety
\textnormal{Alb}(S)
is isomorphic to
E,
and for which the integral Hodge conjecture for
1
-cycles on
E\times S
fails. In particular, there exists a non-torsion integral Hodge class in
H^{4}(E\times S,\mathbb{Z})
that is not algebraic.

Corollary
1.5
adds a further example to the list of threefolds with torsion canonical bundle for which the integral Hodge conjecture fails for
1
-cycles. This should be compared with the counterexamples of Benoist–Ottem
[
5
]
, where the obstruction arises from a non-algebraic torsion cohomology class in degree 4. In contrast, our construction relies on a different degeneration strategy: rather than degenerating the elliptic curve
E,
we degenerate the surface
S
. This idea is inspired by recent work of the author
[
2
,
1
]
and by work of Schreieder
[
23
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In
\lx@sectionsign\ref{sec:preliminaries}
we review the necessary background material. Section
3
introduces an obstruction to the existence of a universal
0
-cycle on a smooth projective variety
X
with representable Chow group of
0
-cycles. In
\lx@sectionsign\ref{sec:degen_bielliptic}
we prove Theorem
1.4
. Finally,
\lx@sectionsign\ref{sec:final}
is devoted to the proofs of our main results, including Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.5
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Let
n
be a positive integer and
A
an abelian group. We denote by

A[n]:=\{a\in A\mid na=0\}

the
n
-torsion subgroup of
A
. By a slight abuse of notation, for a homomorphism of abelian groups

\varphi\colon A\rightarrow B
we write

B/A:=\coker(\varphi).

For convenience, we also set

A/n:=A\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/n.

Let
k
be a field. An
algebraic
k
-scheme
is a separated scheme of finite type over
k
, and a
variety
over
k
is an integral algebraic
k
-scheme. If
k\subset K
is a field extension and
X
is a
k
-scheme, we denote by

X_{K}:=X\times_{\Spec k}\Spec K

the base change of
X
to
K
.

Let
R
be a discrete valuation ring with algebraically closed residue field
k
, and fix an algebraic
closure
K
of its fraction field. For a flat
R
-scheme

\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\Spec R,
we denote by

X_{0}:=\mathcal{X}\times_{R}k\quad\text{and}\quad X_{K}:=\mathcal{X}\times_{R}K

the special fibre and the geometric generic fibre, respectively. We say that

\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\Spec R
is
strictly semistable
if
\mathcal{X}
is regular,
X_{K}
is smooth,
and
X_{0}
is a reduced simple normal crossings divisor on
\mathcal{X}
; that is, the irreducible
components of
X_{0}
are smooth, and the scheme-theoretic intersection of any
r
distinct components
of
X_{0}
is either empty or smooth and equidimensional of codimension
r
in
\mathcal{X}
.

The
Chow group
of codimension-
i
cycles on a variety
X
modulo rational equivalence is denoted by
\textnormal{CH}^{i}(X)
.

2.2.
Cohomology

Let
X
be a scheme, let
\ell
be a prime invertible on
X,
and fix a positive integer
\nu
. Denote by
\mu_{\ell^{\nu}}
the étale sheaf of
\ell^{\nu}
-th roots of unity on
X_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}.
For
n\in\mathbb{Z},
define the Tate twists

\mu^{\otimes n}_{\ell^{\nu}}:=\begin{cases}\mu_{\ell^{\nu}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mu_{\ell^{\nu}}\quad(n\text{ times}),&n\geq 1,\\
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\nu},&n=0,\\
\Hom\bigl(\mu^{\otimes-n}_{\ell^{\nu}},\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\nu}\bigr),&n<0.\end{cases}

For a closed immersion
Z\hookrightarrow X,
the étale cohomology with supports in
Z
is defined by

(2.1)

H^{i}_{Z}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\nu}(n)\bigr):=H^{i}_{Z}\bigl(X_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}},\mu^{\otimes n}_{\ell^{\nu}}\bigr).

When
Z=X,
we simply write

(2.2)

H^{i}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\nu}(n)\bigr):=H^{i}_{X}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\nu}(n)\bigr).

If
X
is an algebraic scheme over an algebraically closed field
k
, we suppress the Tate twist
(n)
from the notation.

The twisted cohomology theory (
2.1
) (for
X
an algebraic
k
-scheme) forms a Poincaré duality theory with supports in the sense of Bloch–Ogus; see
[
7
, Definition (1.3), Example (2.1)]
. In particular, let
X
and
Y
be smooth varieties over a field
k,
with
X
proper of dimension
d=\dim X.
Then any algebraic cycle

[\Gamma]\in\textnormal{CH}^{c}(X\times Y)

induces a correspondence action

(2.3)

[\Gamma]_{\ast}\colon H^{i}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\nu}(n)\bigr)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{i+2c-2d}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}\bigl(Y,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\nu}(n+c-d)\bigr)

given explicitly by

\gamma\longmapsto q_{\ast}\bigl(p^{\ast}\gamma\ \cup\ \text{cl}_{X\times Y}^{c}([\Gamma])\bigr),

where

\text{cl}^{c}_{X\times Y}\colon\textnormal{CH}^{c}(X\times Y)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{2c}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}\bigl(X\times Y,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\nu}(c)\bigr)

is the cycle class map, and

p\colon X\times_{k}Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow X,\qquad q\colon X\times_{k}Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow Y

are the natural projection morphisms.

For a closed immersion
Z\hookrightarrow X,
we define the
\ell
-adic étale cohomology with supports by

(2.4)

H^{i}_{Z}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(n)\bigr):=\varprojlim_{\nu}\ H^{i}_{Z}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\nu}(n)\bigr).

When
Z=X,
we write

(2.5)

H^{i}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(n)\bigr):=H^{i}_{X}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(n)\bigr).

The
\ell
-adic étale cohomology groups defined in (
2.5
) have good formal properties only when the groups
H_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\nu}(n))
are finite. In this case, they agree with Jannsen’s continuous
\ell
-adic étale cohomology
[
12
]
. This finiteness condition holds, for example, when
X
is a variety over an algebraically closed field
k
. By the proper base change theorem, it also holds when
X
is proper over a strictly Henselian local ring
R
(e.g.
R=k[[t]]
); see
[
16
, Corollary VI.2.7]
.

2.3.
Varieties with representable Chow group of
0
-cycles

There are several equivalent characterizations of varieties with representable Chow group of
0
-cycles; we refer to
[
26
, §10.1]
for a detailed account.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero. For a positive integer
n
, denote by
X^{(n)}
the
n
-fold symmetric product of
X
. Its closed points correspond to effective
0
-cycles of degree
n
on
X
. There is a natural map

\rho_{n}\colon X^{(n)}(k)\times X^{(n)}(k)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\operatorname{CH_{0}}(X)_{\hom},\quad([Z_{1}],[Z_{2}])\longmapsto[Z_{1}-Z_{2}].

We say that
\operatorname{CH_{0}}(X)
is
representable
if
\rho_{n}
is surjective for all sufficiently large
n
. This condition is equivalent to the finite-dimensionality of
\operatorname{CH_{0}}(X)
; see
[
26
, Proposition 10.10]
.

By a theorem of Roitman
[
22
, Theorem 4]
,
\operatorname{CH_{0}}(X)
is representable if and only if the Abel–Jacobi map for
0
-cycles

\alpha_{X}\colon\operatorname{CH_{0}}(X)_{\hom}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\textnormal{Alb}(X)

is an isomorphism; see also
[
26
, Theorem 10.11]
. This is further equivalent to the condition that
\operatorname{CH_{0}}(X)
be supported on any smooth complete intersection curve
C\subset X
cut out by ample hypersurfaces; see
[
26
, Proposition 10.12]
. In particular, the Bloch–Srinivas decomposition of the diagonal argument
[
6
]
implies that

H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{i})=0\quad\text{for all }i\geq 2.

We record the following structural consequence.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, and let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
k
satisfying

H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{i})=0\ \text{for all }i\geq 2,\qquad H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})\neq 0.

Then the Albanese morphism
\al_{X}\colon X\rightarrow\textnormal{Alb}(X)
factors as

(2.6)

X\xrightarrow{\ \phi_{X}\ }C\xrightarrow{\ \psi\ }\textnormal{Alb}(X),

where
\phi_{X}
is a surjective morphism with connected fibres onto a smooth projective curve
C
, and
\psi
is finite. Moreover,
\psi
induces an isomorphism

\jac(C)\cong\textnormal{Alb}(X).

Proof.

See
[
29
, Lemma 1.5 and Remark 1.6]
.
∎

An obstruction to the existence of a universal

The following result is the key input for the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field and let
C
be a smooth projective curve over
k
. Set
\Delta:=\Spec k[[t]]
, and fix an algebraic closure
K
of its function field.

Let
p\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\Delta
be a flat, projective morphism of relative dimension
d
with regular total space, and let
\phi_{\mathcal{X}}\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow C\times_{k}\Delta
be a surjective morphism.
Denote by
X_{0}
the special fibre of
p
, by

\phi_{X_{0}}\colon X_{0}\rightarrow C
the induced morphism, and by
X_{K}
the geometric generic fibre of
p
. Write
\phi_{X_{K}}\colon X_{K}\rightarrow C_{K}

for the base change of
\phi_{\mathcal{X}}
to
K
.

We further assume the following:

(1)

X_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{0i}
is a reduced simple normal crossings divisor on
\mathcal{X}
whose dual graph is a chain. Precisely, each intersection
X_{0i}\cap X_{0i+1}
is smooth and irreducible of dimension
d-1,
and
X_{0i}\cap X_{0j}=\varnothing
whenever
j\notin\{i-1,i,i+1\}
.

(2)

There exists a correspondence

[\Gamma]\in\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{K})

inducing a splitting of the pushforward map

(\phi_{X_{K}})_{\ast}\colon\operatorname{CH_{0}}(X_{K})_{\hom}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\jac(C_{K}).

For each
i,
let
\al_{i}\colon\textnormal{Alb}(X_{0i})\rightarrow\jac(C)
be the homomorphism of Albanese varieties induced by the restriction of
\phi_{X_{0}}
to
X_{0i}
. Then the induced sum morphism

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\al_{i}\colon\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\textnormal{Alb}(X_{0i})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\jac(C)

admits a section.

Proof.

By assumption (
2
), there exists a correspondence

[\Gamma]\in\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{K})

such that the induced endomorphism

(\phi_{X_{K}})_{\ast}\circ[\Gamma]_{\ast}\in\End\bigl(\jac(C_{F})\bigr)

is the identity.

We begin with the following reduction step.

Step 1
.

After performing a finite ramified base change
\Delta^{\prime}\rightarrow\Delta
and replacing
{\mathcal{X}}
by a suitable modification of the fiber product
{\mathcal{X}}\times_{\Delta}\Delta^{\prime},

we may assume that the correspondence
[\Gamma]
lifts to a class in

\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}\mathcal{X}).

Proof of Step
1
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the directed system of intermediate fields

k((t))\subset E\subset K

consisting of finite field extensions of
k((t)),
ordered by inclusion. Since Chow groups commute with filtered colimits, we have

\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{K})=\varinjlim_{E\in\mathcal{K}}\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{E}).

Hence there exists a finite field extension
L/k((t))
such that
[\Gamma]
is the image of a class

[\Gamma_{L}]\in\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{L}).

Let
R\subset L
be the integral closure of
k[[t]]
in
L
. Then
R
is a complete discrete valuation ring with residue field
k
and fraction field
L.
In particular, there exists an isomorphism
R\cong k[[\mu]]
under which the inclusion
k[[t]]\hookrightarrow R
is given by

t\longmapsto\mu^{m},

for some
m\geq 1.

Set
\Delta^{\prime}:=\Spec k[[\mu]],
and let
p^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}
be the base change of
p\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\Delta
along the induced morphism
\Delta^{\prime}\rightarrow\Delta
.

In general,
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
need not be regular. As in Hartl’s construction (cf. the proof of
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
), by successively blowing up non-Cartier divisors in the special fibre one obtains a strictly semistable model

\widetilde{p}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}.

Since

X_{0}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}X_{0i}

is a chain, the special fibre of
\widetilde{p},

\widetilde{X}_{0}=X_{01}\cup\bigl(\bigcup_{j=1}^{m-1}R_{1,j}\bigr)\cup X_{02}\cup\bigl(\bigcup_{j=1}^{m-1}R_{2,j}\bigr)\cup\dots\cup X_{0n}

is again a chain. The newly introduced components
R_{i,j}
are
\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}
-bundles over

Y_{i,i+1}:=X_{0i}\cap X_{0i+1},

with projection morphisms
\pi_{i,j}\colon R_{i,j}\rightarrow Y_{i,i+1}
. Moreover, the intersections

X_{0i}\cap R_{i,1},\quad R_{i,m-1}\cap X_{0i+1},\quad R_{i,j}\cap R_{i,j+1}

are all canonically isomorphic to
Y_{i,i+1},
and hence define sections of the corresponding
\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}
-bundles.

The class
[\Gamma_{L}]
is defined on the generic fibre of the family

C\times_{k}\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}.

Taking the closures of the irreducible components of its support yields a class in

\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}\mathcal{\widetilde{X}})

whose restriction to the geometric generic fibre coincides with
[\Gamma]
.

Let

\phi_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow C\times_{k}\Delta^{\prime}

be the morphism induced by
\phi_{\mathcal{X}}.
Write
\textnormal{Alb}(\widetilde{X}_{0})
(resp.
\textnormal{Alb}(X_{0})
) for the direct sum of the Albanese varieties of the irreducible components of
\widetilde{X}_{0}
(resp.
X_{0}
).

It remains to show that if the sum morphism
\textnormal{Alb}(\widetilde{X}_{0})\rightarrow\jac(C)
admits a section, then the same holds for
\textnormal{Alb}(X_{0})\rightarrow\jac(C)
. It suffices to verify that the natural morphism

\textnormal{Alb}(\widetilde{X}_{0})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\jac(C)

factors through
\textnormal{Alb}(X_{0})\rightarrow\jac(C)
. To this end, note that each
R_{i,j}
is a
\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}
-bundle over
Y_{i,i+1}\subset X_{0i}.
Let

\al_{i,j}\colon\textnormal{Alb}(R_{i,j})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\jac(C)

be the homomorphism of Albanese varieties induced by restricting
\phi_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}
to
R_{i,j}
. Since
\al_{i,j}
factors as

\textnormal{Alb}(R_{i,j})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\textnormal{Alb}(X_{0i})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\jac(C)

for each
i,j
, it follows that the natural morphism

\textnormal{Alb}(\widetilde{X}_{0})=\textnormal{Alb}(X_{0})\oplus\bigoplus_{i,j}\textnormal{Alb}(R_{i,j})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\jac(C)

factors through
\textnormal{Alb}(X_{0})\rightarrow\jac(C),
as claimed. This completes the reduction step.
∎

By Step
1
, we may therefore assume that
[\Gamma]
extends to a class

[\Gamma_{\mathcal{X}}]\in\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}\mathcal{X}).

For
1\leq i\leq n,
let

j_{i}\colon X_{0i}\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}

denote the closed immersion, and define

[\Gamma_{i}]:=(\Id\times j_{i})^{\ast}[\Gamma_{\mathcal{X}}]\in\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{0i}).

Let

\alpha_{i}\colon\operatorname{CH_{0}}(X_{0i})_{\hom}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\textnormal{Alb}(X_{0i})

be the Abel–Jacobi homomorphism on
0
-cycles, and set

\beta_{i}:=\alpha_{i}\circ[\Gamma_{i}]_{\ast}\in\Hom\bigl(\jac(C),\textnormal{Alb}(X_{0i})\bigr).

We aim to show that the endomorphism

(3.1)

\Phi\colon\jac(C)\overset{(\beta_{i})_{i}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\textnormal{Alb}(X_{0i})\overset{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\al_{i}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\jac(C)

is the identity.

Corollary 3.2
.

We use the notation and assumptions of Theorem

, with condition (

There exists an algebraically closed field extension

and a correspondence

[\Gamma]\in\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{F})

inducing a splitting of the pushforward map

(\phi_{X_{F}})_{\ast}\colon\operatorname{CH_{0}}(X_{F})_{\hom}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\jac(C_{F}).

Assume moreover that
\End(\jac(C))=\mathbb{Z}
. Then the sum morphism

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\al_{i}\colon\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\textnormal{Alb}(X_{0i})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\jac(C)

admits a section.

Proof.

By assumption (
2
′
), there exists an algebraically closed field extension
F/K
and a correspondence

[\widetilde{\Gamma}]\in\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{F})

such that the composite morphism

(\phi_{X_{F}})_{\ast}\circ[\widetilde{\Gamma}]_{\ast}\in\End\bigl(\jac(C_{F})\bigr)

is the identity. It suffices to show that this implies (
2
) in Theorem
3.1
.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
\Id_{\jac(C_{K})}
is not in the image of the homomorphism

(3.7)

\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{K})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\End\bigl(\jac(C_{K})\bigr),\quad[\Gamma]\longmapsto(\phi_{X_{K}})_{\ast}\circ[\Gamma]_{\ast}.

The pushforward map

(\phi_{X_{K}})_{\ast}\colon\operatorname{CH_{0}}(X_{K})_{\hom}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\jac(C_{K})

is regular and surjective. Consequently, by
[
20
, Corollary 1.6.3]
, the assumption
\End(\jac(C))\cong\mathbb{Z}
implies that the image of (
3.7
) is
m\mathbb{Z}
for some integer
m\geq 2
. In particular, for every

[\Gamma]\in\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{K}),

the induced endomorphism
(\phi_{X_{K}})_{\ast}\circ[\Gamma]_{\ast}
acts trivially on the
m
-torsion subgroup

\jac(C_{K})[m]=\jac(C_{F})[m].

By
[
15
, Théorème 3.11]
, the base-change homomorphism

\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{K})/m\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{F})/m

is an isomorphism. Hence there exists a cycle
[\Gamma]\in\textnormal{CH}^{d}(C\times_{k}X_{K})
whose base change
[\Gamma_{F}]
agrees with
[\widetilde{\Gamma}]
modulo

. It follows that the endomorphism
(\phi_{X_{K}})_{\ast}\circ[\Gamma]_{\ast}
restricts to the identity on
\jac(C_{K})[m]
, contradicting the previous observation.

This contradiction completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

(resp. Corollary

), the assumption that

4.
A degeneration of a Bielliptic surface of type 2

Semistable degenerations of surfaces of Kodaira dimension zero were classified by Kulikov
[
14
]
, Persson–Pinkham
[
21
]
, and Morrison
[
18
]
. In the bielliptic case, among the possible central fibres are cycles of elliptic ruled surfaces (see
[
18
, Theorem 1.8 (1.8.2)]
); chains of elliptic ruled surfaces occur only for types
1
and
2
(see
[
18
, Theorem 1.8 (1.8.3)(c)]
).

The latter are crucial for our purposes. We construct explicitly such a degeneration for a bielliptic surface of type
2
, thereby obtaining a situation to which the obstruction to the existence of a universal
0
-cycle introduced in §
3
applies.

Theorem
1.4
follows from the more precise statement established below.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from
2,
and let
E
be an elliptic curve over
k
. Set
\Delta:=\Spec k[[t]],
and fix an algebraic closure
K
of its function field. Then there exists a regular, flat, projective scheme
\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\Delta
together with a morphism

\phi_{\mathcal{S}}\colon\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E\times\Delta,

such that the following hold:

(1)

The geometric generic fibre
S_{K}
is a bielliptic surface of type 2, and the induced morphism
\phi_{S_{K}}\colon S_{K}\rightarrow E\times_{k}K
coincides with the Albanese fibration of
S_{K}
.

(2)

The special fibre
S_{0}=R_{1}\cup R_{2}
is a reduced simple normal crossing divisor on
\mathcal{S}
with exactly two irreducible components. Each surface
R_{i}
is a minimal ruled surface over an étale double cover
E_{i}\rightarrow E,
and the two double covers are distinct.

The intersection

C:=R_{1}\cap R_{2}

is a smooth, irreducible elliptic curve, embedded in each
R_{i}
as a
2
-fold multisection.

Proof.

Let
p_{\mathcal{F}}\colon\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\Delta
be a minimal regular model of an elliptic curve with geometric generic fibre
F_{K}:=\mathcal{F}\times_{\Delta}K,
and assume that the special fibre
F_{0}
is of Kodaira type
\bf{I}_{4}
. Then

F_{0}=C_{0}\cup C_{1}\cup C_{2}\cup C_{3},

where each
C_{i}\cong\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}
. The components are arranged in a cycle: for
i\in\mathbb{Z}/4,

C_{i}\cap C_{i+1}=\{\text{single node}\},\quad C_{i}\cap C_{j}=\emptyset\ \text{if}\ j\not\equiv i\pm 1\ \text{(mod 4)}.

Each node
C_{i}\cap C_{i+1}
is obtained by identifying the point
\infty\in C_{i}
with the point
0\in C_{i+1}
.

The smooth locus
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{sm}}\rightarrow\Delta
of
p_{\mathcal{F}}
is the Néron model of its generic fibre. Let
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{sm}}[2]
denote its
2
-torsion subgroup scheme; one has

\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{sm}}[2]\cong\mu_{2}\times(\mathbb{Z}/2)_{\Delta}.

This group scheme acts on
\mathcal{F},
and the induced action on the special fibre is as follows: the factor
\mu_{2}
acts on each component
C_{i}
by multiplication by
\pm 1,
fixing the two nodes of
C_{i}
, while the factor
\mathbb{Z}/2
permutes the components by sending
C_{i}
to
C_{i+2}
.

Choose a non-trivial
2
-torsion class

0\neq\epsilon\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{sm}}[2]

whose restriction to the special fibre corresponds to the element
(1,\bar{1})\in\mu_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}/2,
and let
\varphi_{\epsilon}\colon{\mathcal{F}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}
denote translation by
\epsilon
.

The inversion morphism

[-1]\colon F_{K}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow F_{K},\quad x\longmapsto-x

extends canonically to an involution

\iota\colon{\mathcal{F}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}

over
\Delta
. On the special fibre
F_{0},
the automorphism
\iota
induces, for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}/4,
the isomorphism

C_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow C_{-i},\quad(x_{0}:x_{1})\longmapsto(x_{1}:x_{0}).

Fix generators
a,b\in E[2],
and let
\tau_{a},\tau_{b}\colon E\rightarrow E
denote translation by
a
and
b,
respectively. Define

(4.1)

\sigma_{1}:=\tau_{a}\times_{k}\iota,\qquad\sigma_{2}:=\tau_{b}\times_{k}\varphi_{\epsilon}.

The involutions
\sigma_{i}
act freely on
E\times_{k}{\mathcal{F}},
and they commute:
\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{2}\circ\sigma_{1}
.

Set

(4.2)

\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}:=\bigl(E\times_{k}{\mathcal{F}}\bigr)/\sigma_{1}.

The natural projection
\textnormal{pr}_{1}\colon E\times_{k}{\mathcal{F}}\rightarrow E\times_{k}\Delta
descends to a morphism

(4.3)

\phi_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}}\colon\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\bigl(E/a\bigr)\times_{k}\Delta.

The family
\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}\rightarrow\Delta
in (
4.2
) is a strictly semistable degeneration whose geometric generic fibre
\widetilde{S}_{K}
is a bielliptic surface of type 1. The special fibre
\widetilde{S}_{0}
has exactly three irreducible components
\widetilde{R}_{i},
each a minimal elliptic ruled surface. More precisely,

(4.4)

\widetilde{R}_{1}=\bigl(E\times C_{0}\bigr)/\sigma_{1},\quad\widetilde{R}_{2}=\bigl(E\times(C_{1}\sqcup C_{3})\bigr)/\sigma_{1}\cong E\times\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k},\quad\widetilde{R}_{3}=\bigl(E\times C_{2}\bigr)/\sigma_{1}.

The dual graph of
\widetilde{S}_{0}
is a chain with end components
\widetilde{R}_{1}
and
\widetilde{R}_{3}
. The intersections

\widetilde{C}_{1,2}:=\widetilde{R}_{1}\cap\widetilde{R}_{2},\qquad\widetilde{C}_{2,3}:=\widetilde{R}_{2}\cap\widetilde{R}_{3}

are naturally isomorphic to the elliptic curve
E
. Moreover, the induced morphisms

\widetilde{C}_{1,2}\subset\widetilde{R}_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E/a,\qquad\widetilde{C}_{2,3}\subset\widetilde{R}_{3}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E/a

coincide with the canonical double cover

E\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E/a.

In addition, the curves
C_{i,i+1}
embed as disjoint sections of the ruled surface
\widetilde{R}_{2}
.

The involution
\sigma_{2}
in (
4.1
) descends to an automorphism
\widetilde{\sigma}_{2}
of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}
. The quotient

(4.5)

{\mathcal{S}}:=\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}/\widetilde{\sigma}_{2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Delta

is a strictly semistable degeneration of a bielliptic surface of type 2. The involution
\widetilde{\sigma}_{2}
identifies the components
\widetilde{R}_{1}
and
\widetilde{R}_{3}
and leaves
\widetilde{R}_{2}
invariant. Under the isomorphism
\widetilde{R}_{2}\cong E\times\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}
in (
4.4
), its restriction is given by

\sigma\colon\bigl(x,(x_{0}:x_{1})\bigr)\longmapsto\bigl(x+(a+b),(x_{1}:x_{0})\bigr).

It follows that the special fibre
S_{0}
has exactly two irreducible components
R_{i},
each a minimal elliptic ruled surface. More precisely,

(4.6)

R_{1}\cong\widetilde{R}_{1}\cong\widetilde{R}_{3},\qquad R_{2}\cong\bigl(E\times\mathbf{P}^{1}_{k}\bigr)/\sigma.

Their intersection
C:=R_{1}\cap R_{2}
is isomorphic to
E,
and embeds in each ruled surface
R_{i}
as a double cover of its base
E_{i}
. In the notation of (
2
), the corresponding étale double covers
E_{i}\rightarrow E,\ (i=1,2),
are given by

(4.7)

E/a\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E/E[2]\cong E,\qquad E/(a+b)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E/E[2]\cong E,

respectively.

Finally, the morphism
\phi_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}}
defined in (
4.3
) descends, upon passage to the quotient by
\widetilde{\sigma}_{2}
, to a morphism

(4.8)

\phi_{{\mathcal{S}}}\colon{\mathcal{S}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\bigl(E/E[2]\bigr)\times_{k}\Delta\cong E\times_{k}\Delta,

which restricts on the geometric generic fibre to the Albanese fibration of the bielliptic surface
S_{K}
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\rightarrow\Delta
be the degeneration constructed in Theorem
4.1
, together with the induced morphism

\phi_{{\mathcal{S}}}\colon{\mathcal{S}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E\times_{k}\Delta.

Assertions (
1
) and (
2
) of Theorem
1.4
follow directly from (
1
) and (
2
) of Theorem
4.1
. It therefore suffices to verify condition (
3
).

To this end, we show that the sum morphism

(4.9)

\textnormal{Alb}(R_{1})\times_{k}\textnormal{Alb}(R_{2})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E

admits no section under the assumption that
\End(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}
.

As in (
4.7
), the natural morphisms

\textnormal{Alb}(R_{i})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E\qquad(i=1,2)

are given by the étale double covers

E/a\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E/E[2]\cong E,\qquad E/b\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E/E[2]\cong E,

where
a,b\in E[2]
are generators. Consequently, the morphism (
4.9
) can be identified with

E/a\times_{k}E/b\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E/E[2]\cong E,

where the final isomorphism is induced by multiplication by
2
on
E
.

Let
q_{a}\colon E\rightarrow E/a
and
q_{b}\colon E\rightarrow E/b
denote the canonical quotient morphisms. Since
\End(E)=\mathbb{Z},
we have

\Hom\bigl(E,E/a\bigr)=\mathbb{Z}\cdot q_{a},\qquad\Hom\bigl(E,E/b\bigr)=\mathbb{Z}\cdot q_{b}.

It follows that any homomorphism

f\colon E\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E/a\times_{k}E/b

is of the form

for some integers
m_{1},m_{2}
.

For any such

, its composition with the sum morphism (

) is trivial on

Hence the morphism (
4.9
) admits no section. This establishes condition (

), thereby completing the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Main results

5.1.
A bielliptic surface of type 2 admitting no universal
0
-cycle

Theorem
1.3
follows immediately from the result below.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\neq 2
, and let
E
be an elliptic curve over
k
with
\End(E)=\mathbb{Z}
. Set
\Delta:=\Spec k[[t]]
, and fix an algebraic closure
K
of its function field. Let
\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\Delta
be the degeneration constructed in Theorem
1.4
, together with the induced morphism

\phi_{\mathcal{S}}\colon\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E\times_{k}\Delta.

Then for any algebraically closed field extension
F/K
, there exists no correspondence

[\Gamma]\in\textnormal{CH}^{2}(E\times_{k}S_{F})

that induces a splitting of the pushforward map

(\phi_{S_{F}})_{\ast}\colon\textnormal{CH}_{0}(S_{F})_{\hom}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E_{F}.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists an algebraically closed field extension
F/K
and a correspondence

[\Gamma]\in\textnormal{CH}^{2}(E\times_{k}S_{F})

such that the induced endomorphism

(\phi_{S_{F}})_{\ast}\circ[\Gamma]_{\ast}\in\End\bigl(E_{F}\bigr)

is the identity.

S_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}

denote the special fibre of the family
{\mathcal{S}}\rightarrow\Delta,
as in (
2
) of Theorem
1.4
. For each

let
\al_{i}\colon\textnormal{Alb}(R_{i})\rightarrow E
be the morphism of Albanese varieties induced by the restriction of
\phi_{\mathcal{S}}
to

. By Corollary
3.2
, the assumption above implies that the sum morphism

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\al_{i}\colon\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\textnormal{Alb}(R_{i})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E

) of Theorem

. The resulting contradiction completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbf{C}
with
\End(E)=\mathbb{Z}
. Choose a countable algebraically closed subfield

over which

is defined; thus

E\cong E_{0}\times_{k_{0}}\mathbf{C}

for some elliptic curve
E_{0}
over

Let

{\mathcal{S}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Delta:=\Spec k_{0}[[t]]

be the strictly semistable degeneration constructed in Theorem

, together with the induced morphism

\phi_{\mathcal{S}}\colon\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E_{0}\times_{k_{0}}\Delta.

Since

factors through the Laurent series field

By (

) of Theorem

, the surface

5.2.
A counterexample to the integral Hodge conjecture

In this final section, we apply Theorem
1.3
to prove Corollary
1.5
. In particular, we construct a threefold
X
of Kodaira dimension zero for which the integral Hodge conjecture fails for
1
-cycles. In contrast to the counterexamples of Benoist–Ottem
[
5
]
, the non-algebraic Hodge class in degree
4
arising from our construction is non-torsion.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\mathbf{C}
of dimension
d=\dim X
. We begin by recalling how the non-existence of a universal
0
-cycle on
X
is related to the failure of the integral Hodge conjecture. Throughout, all (co)homology groups are taken to be Betti (co)homology, and the subscript "tf" denotes the quotient by torsion.

The following lemma is well-known.

Lemma 5.2
.

There is a natural isomorphism

(5.1)

\Hom\bigl(H_{1}\bigl(\textnormal{Alb}(X),\mathbb{Z}\bigr),\,H_{1}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}\bigr)_{\mathrm{tf}}\bigr)\cong H^{1}\bigl(\textnormal{Alb}(X),\mathbb{Z}\bigr)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}H^{2d-1}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}\bigr)_{\mathrm{tf}}.

Proof.

For any lattice
L
, we write

L^{\vee}:=\Hom(L,\mathbb{Z})

for its dual. By Poincaré duality for the smooth projective variety
X
, there is a natural
isomorphism

H_{1}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}\bigr)_{\mathrm{tf}}\;\cong\;H^{2d-1}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}\bigr)_{\mathrm{tf}}.

The universal coefficient
theorem yields a canonical identification

H^{2d-1}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}\bigr)_{\mathrm{tf}}\;\cong\;H_{2d-1}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}\bigr)_{\mathrm{tf}}^{\vee}.

Combining these, we obtain

\Hom\bigl(H_{1}\bigl(\textnormal{Alb}(X),\mathbb{Z}\bigr),\,H_{1}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}\bigr)_{\mathrm{tf}}\bigr)\;\cong\;\Hom\bigl(H_{1}\bigl(\textnormal{Alb}(X),\mathbb{Z}\bigr),\,H_{2d-1}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}\bigr)_{\mathrm{tf}}^{\vee}\bigr).

By the tensor–Hom adjunction, this group is naturally isomorphic to

\bigl(H_{1}\bigl(\textnormal{Alb}(X),\mathbb{Z}\bigr)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{2d-1}\bigl(X,\mathbb{Z}\bigr)_{\mathrm{tf}}\bigr)^{\vee}.

Since both
H_{1}(\textnormal{Alb}(X),\mathbb{Z})
and
H_{2d-1}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tf}}
are finitely generated
free abelian groups, there is a canonical identification

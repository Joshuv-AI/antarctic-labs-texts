Comparative Evaluation of Centering Ability and Canal Transportation Using Manual and Rotary Instruments – An In Vitro Study
Authors: Tahera Shamim, Priyanka, Mehar Fatima, Amit Kumar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Endodontic success relies heavily on the accurate shaping and cleaning of root canals, especially in curved canals where procedural errors like canal transportation can occur. Instrument design, alloy flexibility, and taper significantly influence canal shaping outcomes.Materials and Methods:A total of 102 extracted mandibular molars with uncalcified, noncarious mesiobuccal canals were selected. Teeth were divided into three groups: Group I (K-file), Group II (NeoEndo S rotary), and Group III (GenEndo rotary). After access cavity preparation and initial glide path formation, root canals were prepared to size 25/02 (manual) and 25/04 (rotary). Cone beam computed tomography scans were performed pre- and postinstrumentation at 2, 4, and 6 mm from the apex to assess canal transportation and centering ability using standard formulas.Results:Group I (K-files) showed minimal mesiobuccal (MB) transportation at 2 mm (0.01 ± 0.22 mm) and the highest centering ratio at 2 mm (0.62 ± 0.85). Group II (NeoEndo S) had the highest transportation at 2 mm (MB: [0.10 ± 0.35 mm], DL: [0.51 ± 1.30 mm]) and the lowest centering ability (0.13 ± 1.75). At 4 mm, NeoEndo S again showed maximum transportation ([0.06 ± 0.35] MB, [0.53 ± 0.93] DL) compared to GenEndo and K-files. At 6 mm, Group III (GenEndo) showed greater MB transportation (0.31 ± 0.54 mm) and the lowest centering ability (0.01 ± 0.83). ANOVA revealed no significant differences for MB and DL transportation (P > 0.05 across groups), but centering ability differed significantly in Group III (P = 0.023), particularly between 2 and 4 mm distances.Conclusion:All tested instruments caused some degree of canal transportation. GenEndo files demonstrated better centering ability and less canal deviation compared to NeoEndo S and K-files, attributed to their design and alloy properties.

INTRODUCTION

Endodontics is the dental specialty concerned with the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of diseases of the dental pulp and periapical tissues. A critical component of successful root canal therapy is the effective chemomechanical preparation of the canal system, which includes shaping, cleaning, disinfection, and, ultimately, obturation with a hermetic seal in three dimensions.[1] The primary aim of canal shaping is to create a continuous tapering funnel from the coronal portion to the apical terminus, maintaining the original canal curvature and anatomy.[2]

According to Schilder’s principles, mechanical objectives of shaping include producing a continuously tapering canal that maintains the original anatomy and facilitates obturation, while biological objectives involve complete removal of pulpal tissue, avoiding extrusion of debris, and preparing the canal for adequate disinfection and obturation.[3] However, due to the complexity and variation in root canal morphology, as highlighted by Miro et al., biomechanical preparation often poses challenges such as procedural errors including ledge formation, canal straightening, perforation, zipping, and apical transportation.[45]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This in vitro study involved 102 extracted human permanent mandibular molars collected. Teeth selected were intact, noncarious, and free from fractures, restorations, or previous endodontic procedures. The mesiobuccal canals were radiographically confirmed to exhibit moderate curvature (10°–20°) using Schneider’s method. Following decoronation to a standardized root length of 18 mm, access cavities were prepared, and the working length was established 1 mm short of the apical foramen using a size 10 K-file. The specimens were randomly divided into three groups (n = 34 each): Group I (manual instrumentation with K-files), Group II (rotary instrumentation with NeoEndo S files), and Group III (rotary instrumentation with GenEndo files).

All canals were negotiated with #10 and #15 K-files to establish a glide path. Pre- and postinstrumentation cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) scans (Planmeca ProMax 3D Mid) were obtained using standardized settings (90 kVp, 10 mA, 200 µm voxel size, 5 cm × 5 cm FOV). Axial slices at 2, 4, and 6 mm from the apex were analyzed. Canal transportation and centering ability were calculated using established formulas, with measurements conducted independently by two blinded endodontists. Statistical analysis was carried out using SPSS version 22.0 (IBM ststistics, USA).

RESULTS

A total of 102 mesiobuccal canals of mandibular molars were assessed across three groups—Group I (K-file), Group II (NeoEndo S), and Group III (GenEndo)—using pre- and postinstrumentation CBCT scans at 2 mm, 4 mm, and 6 mm from the apex. Canal transportation values are presented in Table 1. Group I exhibited the least transportation across all levels, especially at 2 mm (0.01 ± 0.22 mm MB), while NeoEndo S showed the highest deviation, particularly in the disto-lingual direction (0.51 ± 1.30 mm at 2 mm). At 6 mm, GenEndo recorded the highest mesio-buccal transportation (0.31 ± 0.54 mm), indicating a reduced ability to maintain the original canal curvature at deeper levels.

Centering ability results showed K-files again demonstrated superior performance across all levels. DISCUSSION

Root canal preparation, especially in curved canals, often results in procedural errors such as canal transportation due to the instrument’s tendency to straighten within the canal. Preserving the original canal anatomy while achieving effective cleaning and shaping is essential in endodontic therapy. In this study, CBCT provided a nondestructive and accurate three-dimensional assessment of the shaping outcomes, allowing detailed evaluation of canal transportation and centering ability.[67]

At the 2 mm level, the K-file group demonstrated the least canal transportation (0.01 ± 0.22 mm) and the highest centering ratio (0.62 ± 0.85), likely due to the controlled tactile feedback associated with manual instrumentation. In contrast, NeoEndo S files showed the highest deviation and poorest centering, possibly due to their stiffer alloy and aggressive taper, which may limit flexibility in curved canals. At the 4 mm level, although all groups showed increased transportation, NeoEndo S continued to display the most deviation, while K-files and GenEndo maintained better canal centering.

At 6 mm, GenEndo files showed the highest mesio-buccal transportation (0.31 ± 0.54 mm) and the poorest centering ability (0.01 ± 0.83), indicating compromised adaptability at deeper canal levels. Despite these differences, canal transportation did not show statistically significant variation among the groups. However, centering ability at 6 mm was significantly different (P = 0.023), with post hoc analysis revealing a significant difference between NeoEndo S and GenEndo (P = 0.037). These findings highlight the importance of centering ability as a key indicator of instrumentation performance in endodontics.

CONCLUSION

Within the limitations of this in vitro study, manual instrumentation with K-files demonstrated superior centering ability and minimal canal transportation compared to rotary systems. GenEndo files performed better than NeoEndo S in maintaining canal anatomy at greater depths.

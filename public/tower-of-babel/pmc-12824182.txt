Research on the design and simulation of grid-connected system of vertical gravity energy storage
Authors: Yirun Ji, Yi Lu, Jianfeng Hong, Rui Zhou, Junci Cao, Wenqian Yuan, Qing Huai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDue to the characteristics of the body’s movement, the output power of the gravity energy storage system is unstable. When the system is connected to the grid, smooth control of the output power is required. To this end, this paper proposes a control scheme that uses multiple units for joint power generation and complements the output power. The system operation parameters are optimized through an optimization algorithm to achieve power smoothing. The optimized gravity energy storage system can output stable power generation and can be equivalent to a controlled current source in grid-connected systems. Based on this, a corresponding gravity energy storage grid-connected system was designed, and the converter adopted a voltage-oriented vector control strategy for grid connection control. Subsequently, grid-connected system simulation and short-circuit fault simulation experiments were carried out. The simulation results show that the output power of the optimized gravity energy storage system is stable, and the minimum power fluctuation rate can reach 6.7%. The designed grid-connected system has a fast response speed and good steady-state characteristics. It can return to the original value after a sudden change of 0.4 s in the system input, so as to achieve the stable grid connection of the gravity energy storage system.Keywords: Shaft gravity energy storage, Grid-connected power, Power smoothing, Optimization algorithmSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Against the backdrop of increasingly severe global energy shortages and environmental deterioration, renewable energy generation technologies such as solar and wind power have developed rapidly in recent years. However, due to the intermittency and volatility of renewable energy generation, its large-scale grid connection brings many challenges to the operation of the power system, including issues such as power quality and the consumption of new energy1. Energy storage systems can significantly improve energy utilization and facilitate new energy consumption. Because of this, they have become a key research focus in the development of new energy and power systems2–4.

Among various energy storage technologies, pumped storage is the most widely used, but it relies on the water environment and has high requirements for geographical location. Electrochemical energy storage has a high energy conversion efficiency and rapid response capability, but it has safety and thermal management issues and causes certain pollution to the environment. Flywheel energy storage has a long cycle life and low decay rate, but it has certain limitations on materials and is relatively expensive5,6. Gravity energy storage technology has developed on the basis of pumped storage. By changing the energy storage medium from water to solid, it reduces the dependence on site selection. Thanks to its advantages such as large-scale energy storage capacity, environmental friendliness, and flexible site selection, gravity energy storage technology has become a research hotspot in the current energy storage field, demonstrating significant development potential7,8. According to the differences in technical routes, gravity energy storage is mainly divided into piston type, shaft type, frame type and slope type, etc. Among them, the shaft type gravity energy storage technology can be built in abandoned mines and has the characteristics of small land occupation and stable structure. Compared with other forms, it is more feasible in engineering9–11.

Tens of thousands of abandoned mines were left in the UK’s midlands region. To address this, the British company Gravitricity first proposed the vertical shaft gravity energy storage technology. This scheme adopts the construction of gravity energy storage vertical shaft channels in abandoned mines, and uses electric winches to transport heavy objects to achieve energy storage and release. While reducing construction costs, it also solves the problem of mine resource waste12. Based on this, the company conducted a 250 kW vertical shaft gravity energy storage demonstration platform experiment in Edinburgh Lees Port in 2021. A 50-ton load was lifted to a 15-m mine platform and then dropped. The discharge process was completed within 1–2 s. This experiment verified the feasibility of vertical shaft gravity energy storage13. Meanwhile, the University of Oxford theoretically analyzed the vertical shaft gravity energy storage system, established a mathematical model of the system, analyzed and calculated the power generation characteristics and economy of the system, and verified the feasibility of vertical shaft gravity energy storage technology14. The Institute of Electrical Engineering of the Chinese Academy of Sciences has proposed a plan to use a hoist to lift multiple heavy objects. Through PLC, the hoist, track and lifting gear are intelligently controlled to achieve the automated operation of the system. In 2022, the charging and discharging performance tests of a 10KW-level vertical shaft gravity energy storage system were conducted. The system can operate automatically in island mode or grid-connected mode. In the design of grid-connected systems for gravity energy storage, the Chinese Academy of Sciences has proposed a technical solution for the grid-connected hybrid system of gravity energy storage and flywheel energy storage, taking advantage of the high instantaneous power and fast charging and discharging speed of flywheel energy storage to smooth the output power curve of gravity energy storage15. Guizhou University has proposed a voltage-oriented control strategy that conducts control on both the motor side and the grid side respectively, which can simulate and achieve the stable operation of the sloping gravity energy storage system after grid connection16,17.

At present, few studies have focused on the output power characteristics of gravity energy storage itself, and conducted grid connection control based on these characteristics in the field of grid-connected control and system optimization of siloed gravity energy storage. The gravity energy storage grid-connected system designed by the Chinese Academy of Sciences and Guizhou University achieves power smoothing by controlling the speed of the motor15–17. Wang et al. considered the inrush current during grid connection on this basis and achieved better control effects18. However, none of these studies took into account the stepped-changing output power characteristics of gravity energy storage, which increased the difficulty of control, and did not optimize the system operation parameters of gravity energy storage. Elasyed, Botha et al. optimized the system design parameters of gravity energy storage to achieve better energy storage efficiency19,20. Can Lv optimizes the capacity of gravity energy storage to achieve better economic efficiency21. These design optimizations focus more on the energy storage efficiency and economy of the gravity energy storage system itself, while neglecting the important indicator of the power generation capacity of the gravity energy storage system. Based on the above research, this paper innovatively focuses on the output power characteristics of gravity energy storage. By using optimization algorithms to optimize the operating parameters of the gravity energy storage system, the output power of the gravity energy storage system is controlled, and a multi-machine joint approach is adopted to achieve power smoothing, thereby enabling the grid connection of the gravity energy storage system. Achieving power smoothing before grid connection not only reduces the difficulty of motor control but also lessens the impact on the power grid caused by the frequent changes in the output power of gravity energy storage. Subsequently, this paper designs the corresponding gravity energy storage grid-connected system, and then conducts simulation experiments to verify the proposed gravity energy storage grid-connected system, and performs fault simulation.

Vertical shaft gravity energy storage system model

Gravity energy storage working principle

The components of the vertical shaft type gravity energy storage device are shown in Fig. 1, mainly including the vertical shaft channel for lifting heavy objects, the cage and the loaded heavy object blocks, the power electronic converter device, the motor and steel wire rope, and the transmission lifting system composed of the winch and pulley. The heavy block serves as the energy storage medium in the energy storage system. The change in its high and low positions enables the storage and release of energy. The converter plays a crucial role in the entire system. It serves as the channel for power flow between the gravity energy storage device and the power grid. This system transmits electrical energy between the motor and the power grid. During discharge, it sends electricity generated by the descending weight to the grid. During charging, it conveys excess grid electricity to the motor to lift the weight, thereby storing energy. The basic working principle of the vertical shaft gravity energy storage system is as follows: During energy storage, the excess electrical energy from the power grid is transmitted to the gravity energy storage system through a converter. The motor operates in the motor state, pulling the heavy block to rise, converting electrical energy into gravitational potential energy, and achieving energy storage. When releasing energy, the motor operates in the generator state. The descent of a heavy block pulls the motor to rotate, converting gravitational potential energy into electrical energy. This energy is then transmitted to the power grid through a converter, achieving the release of energy.

Converter control strategy

In the grid-connected gravity energy storage system, the grid-side converter serves as the core unit and primarily performs two key functions: first, stabilizing the DC bus voltage, and second, regulating the active and reactive power delivered to the grid25–27. The converter typically adopts a dual-loop control strategy based on coordinate transformation, combined with Space Vector Pulse Width Modulation (SVPWM) technology to achieve efficient energy conversion. SVPWM controls the six sets of switching devices in the three-phase inverter to generate modulation waveforms with specific timing sequences, approximating an ideal sinusoidal current output. Its theoretical foundation lies in the principle of average value equivalence, where combinations of basic voltage vectors are used to achieve the same average value as the target voltage vector within a switching period.

The control strategies for the grid-side converter can be broadly categorized into two types based on the method of coordinate orientation: grid voltage-based control strategies and virtual flux-based control strategies. Among these, the grid voltage-based control strategies include voltage-oriented control and direct power control. This paper adopts the voltage-oriented control strategy based on grid voltage. This strategy relies on monitoring or estimating the grid voltage to regulate the d-axis and q-axis currents on the grid side, thereby maintaining the stability of the DC-side voltage. This enables decoupled control of active and reactive power, allowing independent regulation of active and reactive power. The principle of this control strategy is illustrated in Fig. 528.

In terms of converters, the commonly used converters at present are IGBT and IGCT. IGBTs offer low conduction voltage drop and high blocking voltage capability, making them suitable for medium-power applications such as power conversion and variable-frequency drives. IGCTs, on the other hand, feature low switching losses and superior short-circuit withstand capability, making them ideal for high-power applications like high-voltage direct current (HVDC) transmission and motor drives. Compared to IGCTs, IGBTs have faster switching speeds, capable of reaching nanosecond-level transitions, thus being more suitable for high-frequency applications. However, IGCTs exhibit higher voltage withstand capability, reaching up to several kilovolts, while the withstand voltage of IGBTs typically ranges from a few hundred volts to several kilovolts, relatively lower. Additionally, IGCTs demonstrate excellent short-circuit tolerance, able to withstand high peak currents and large short-circuit energy. In contrast, IGBTs have weaker short-circuit capability and require external protection circuits to prevent overcurrent and overheating. Due to differences in structure and materials, IGCTs offer better thermal stability in high-temperature environments. They can operate at higher temperatures and have relatively lower cooling requirements. IGBTs, however, are prone to thermal runaway under high temperatures and require additional cooling mechanisms to maintain stable operation.

Fault simulation

In grid-connected systems, simulating and analyzing various types of short-circuit faults is critically important. Through such simulations and analyses, the dynamic response of the power system under fault conditions can be predicted, providing a theoretical basis for subsequent fault handling and system design. In the simulation model of the grid-connected gravity energy storage system, a fault module was introduced to simulate single-phase-to-ground short-circuit faults, double-phase-to-ground short-circuit faults, and phase-to-phase short-circuit faults. The faults were applied at the transformer side, connected to the converter and reactor. During the fault simulation experiments, the phase voltages, phase currents, and short-circuit currents at the fault location were measured, and the simulation results were analyzed.

In the simulation model, a single-phase-to-ground fault was set to occur at 2 s, with phase A selected as the faulted phase. The voltage and current waveforms at the fault location obtained from the simulation are shown in Fig. 12.

Conclusion and discussion

Based on the working principle of gravity energy storage, this paper establishes a mathematical model of the power generation output for the gravity energy storage system. To address the instability of single-unit power generation, a grid-connected scheme utilizing multiple units in combination is proposed to achieve complementary power output and ensure stable power delivery. Simulation experiments of the grid-connected system and fault scenarios were conducted. The results indicate:

The optimized gravity energy storage system achieves stable power output. Within the power range of 30–44 MW, the fluctuation rate remains below 9% after optimization, reaching a minimum of 6.7% at 30 MW.

The optimized gravity energy storage system achieves stable power output. Within the power range of 30–44 MW, the fluctuation rate remains below 9% after optimization, reaching a minimum of 6.7% at 30 MW.

The optimized gravity energy storage system can be equivalently represented as a controlled current source in the grid-connected model. However, within a certain range, the greater the output power of the gravity energy storage system, the higher the power fluctuation rate may be, such as 30-36 MW and 38-42 MW, which will affect the stability of grid connection.

The simulation results of the gravity energy storage grid-connected system show that under the designed control links, the grid-connected system has a fast response speed and good steady-state characteristics. After a response time of 1 s, the DC side voltage can reach approximately 8000 V of the control set value, with a steady-state error of about ± 50 V, which is 0.65% of the set value. When there is a sudden change in the system input, the grid-side current stabilizes after 0.4 s, which enables the stable grid connection of the gravity energy storage system. The simulation results of short-circuit faults show that when a fault occurs in the line, the current of the faulty phase increases rapidly, and the voltage of the faulty phase decreases accordingly and then reaches a new steady state.

As a new type of energy storage system, gravity energy storage systems not only can solve the contradiction between power supply and demand, but also are conducive to the economic and safe operation of the power grid. Vertical shafts can be excavated vertically downward, making full use of underground space and reducing the occupation of surface land. They can also be combined with other facilities such as underground parking lots and civil air defense projects for construction, further enhancing the comprehensive utilization efficiency of land. Under the condition of saving construction costs by adopting abandoned mines, the levelized cost per kilowatt-hour can reach 171$/kWh, and the service life is about 50 to 60 years. In addition, the system efficiency can reach 80%, which is higher than that of pumped storage. The fact that it does not pollute the environment is also an advantage that electrochemical energy storage does not have. However, due to the inherent movement characteristics of the vertical shaft type gravity energy storage system, there are problems such as frequent start and stop of multiple motors, stepwise power changes, and poor power quality when responding to continuous power regulation instructions. Therefore, it is necessary to achieve continuous and smooth power control of multiple motors within the gravity energy storage system through the optimization of control strategies. However, when multi-unit coordinated control is adopted, the multi-unit parallel circulation problem during the charging and discharging operation of the gravity energy storage system will be an engineering challenge that needs to be solved in the future.

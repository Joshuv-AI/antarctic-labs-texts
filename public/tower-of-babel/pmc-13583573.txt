Datopotamab deruxtecan versus chemotherapy in previously treated inoperable/metastatic hormone receptor-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative breast cancer: results from the TROPION-Breast01 China cohort
Authors: S. Wang, Q. Zhang, Z. Jiang, Z. Tong, W. Li, J. Wang, Q. Ouyang, T. Yao, Y. Wang, M. Sun, X. Wang, S. Wang, A. Zang, Z. Zhang, W. Chen, X. Wang, H. Li, Y. Sheng, B. Zhang, C. Liu, K. Zhao, M.A. Munegowda, A. Bardia, B. Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn the global phase III TROPION-Breast01 study, datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd) demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in progression-free survival (PFS) by blinded independent central review (BICR) versus investigator’s choice of chemotherapy (ICC) in patients with previously treated, inoperable/metastatic hormone receptor(HR)-positive human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HR+/HER2—) breast cancer. At the final analysis, overall survival (OS) was not statistically significant. We report final results from the prespecified analysis of patients enrolled in mainland China.Patients and methodsPatients with inoperable/metastatic HR+/HER2— breast cancer, who had disease progression on endocrine therapy and for whom endocrine therapy was unsuitable and who had received 1-2 prior lines of chemotherapy in the inoperable/metastatic setting, were randomly assigned 1 : 1 to Dato-DXd (6 mg/kg every 3 weeks) or ICC (eribulin/capecitabine/vinorelbine/gemcitabine). Dual primary endpoints were PFS by BICR and OS.ResultsOverall, 83 patients were enrolled in mainland China (Dato-DXd, n = 44; ICC, n = 39). PFS by BICR numerically favored Dato-DXd versus ICC [hazard ratio (HR) 0.54, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.30-0.96, nominal P = 0.0329; median PFS: 8.1 versus 4.2 months]. OS numerically favored Dato-DXd versus ICC [HR 0.83 (95% CI 0.49-1.43), nominal P = 0.5028]. The rate of grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) was lower with Dato-DXd versus ICC (29.5% versus 58.3%). The most common TRAEs (any grade/grade 3-4) were nausea (47.7%/4.5%) and increased aspartate aminotransferase (40.9%/2.3%) with Dato-DXd, and neutropenia (grouped term, 58.3%/36.1%) and anemia (52.8%/8.3%) with ICC. With Dato-DXd, treatment-related AEs of special interest (grouped terms) oral mucositis/stomatitis and ocular surface events occurred in 43.2% and 47.7% of patients, respectively.ConclusionIn this China cohort, Dato-DXd demonstrated numerically improved efficacy versus ICC and a manageable safety profile, consistent with the global population, supporting Dato-DXd as a new treatment option for Chinese patients with previously treated metastatic HR+/HER2— breast cancer.

Introduction

China represents the largest proportion of breast cancer cases globally, accounting for 18.4% of all cases.1 The incidence and mortality rates of breast cancer in China have been increasing since the 1990s, with around 385 000 new cases and 88 100 deaths in 2021.2,3 In China, just over half of patients diagnosed with breast cancer have hormone receptor-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative HR+/HER2— disease, compared with ∼70% of all breast cancers in Western populations.4, 5, 6 For patients with metastatic HR+/HER2— disease who are resistant to or ineligible for endocrine therapy, international and Chinese guidelines have, until recently, recommended treatment with single-agent chemotherapy.7, 8, 9 However, chemotherapy is associated with limited clinical benefits and substantial toxicity.10,11

Treatment options after endocrine therapy and/or chemotherapy have now expanded with approvals in the United States, European Union, and China for the trophoblast cell surface antigen 2 (TROP2)-directed antibody–drug conjugate (ADC) sacituzumab govitecan (SG) for HER2-negative disease,12, 13, 14, 15, 16 and the HER2-directed ADC trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) for HER2-low and ultralow disease.17, 18, 19, 20, 21 Most recently, datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd), a TROP2-directed ADC, has received approvals for HR+/HER2— breast cancer in several countries globally based on results from the phase III TROPION-Breast01 study.22, 23, 24, 25

In TROPION-Breast01, patients with inoperable or metastatic HR+/HER2— breast cancer, who had disease progression on endocrine therapy and for whom further endocrine therapy was unsuitable, and who had received one or two previous lines of chemotherapy in the inoperable or metastatic setting, received Dato-DXd or investigator’s choice of chemotherapy (ICC).24 In the primary analysis [data cut-off (DCO) 17 July 2023], Dato-DXd demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in the dual primary endpoint of progression-free survival (PFS) by blinded independent central review (BICR) compared with ICC [hazard ratio (HR) 0.63, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.52-0.76, P < 0.0001].24 At the prespecified final analysis (DCO: 24 July 2024), the dual primary endpoint of overall survival (OS) was not statistically significant [HR 1.01 (95% CI 0.83-1.22), P = 0.9445]; however, an imbalance between arms in subsequent ADC treatment may have affected OS results.25 Dato-DXd demonstrated a manageable safety profile in TROPION-Breast0124,25; notably, the rate of grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) in the Dato-DXd arm was less than half that in the ICC arm and TRAEs led to fewer dose reductions and interruptions in patients who received Dato-DXd compared with those who received ICC.24,25 The efficacy and safety findings in the global population in TROPION-Breast01 are complemented by patient-reported outcomes (PROs), which showed a longer time to deterioration in global health status/quality of life (GHS/QoL), pain, and physical functioning with Dato-DXd versus ICC.26

Efficacy and toxicity profiles with cancer therapies can vary among different ethnic groups,27,28 and data on the efficacy and safety of Dato-DXd in Chinese patients with HR+/HER2— breast cancer are limited. Therefore, a prespecified analysis was conducted for TROPION-Breast01 to examine the efficacy and safety of Dato-DXd versus ICC in patients enrolled in mainland China. In this study, we report results from the final analysis.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

Full details of the TROPION-Breast01 study design and methodology have been published previously.24,29 Briefly, the study enrolled adult patients with inoperable or metastatic HR+/HER2— breast cancer [HER2— defined as immunohistochemistry (IHC) 0, 1+, or 2+/in situ hybridization (ISH)-negative, per American Society of Clinical Oncology–College of American Pathologists guidelines].30,31 Patients who had experienced progression on endocrine therapy and for whom further endocrine therapy was unsuitable per investigator assessment were eligible. The original protocol also allowed enrollment of patients who were naïve to endocrine therapy if it was deemed unsuitable. Patients were required to have received one or two prior lines of chemotherapy in the inoperable or metastatic setting.

Eligible patients were randomly assigned 1 : 1 to receive Dato-DXd [6 mg/kg i.v. every 3 weeks (Q3W)] or single-agent ICC (eribulin, 1.4 mg/m2 i.v. on days 1 and 8, Q3W; capecitabine, 1000 or 1250 mg/m2 orally twice daily on days 1 to 14, Q3W; vinorelbine, 25 mg/m2 i.v. on days 1 and 8, Q3W; or gemcitabine, 1000 mg/m2 i.v. on days 1 and 8, Q3W). Random assignment was stratified by number of prior lines of chemotherapy (1 versus 2), geographic region (United States/Canada/Europe versus other geographic regions), and prior use of a CDK4/6 inhibitor (yes versus no). Treatment continued until investigator-defined disease progression according to RECIST version 1.1, unacceptable toxicity, withdrawal of consent, or until another discontinuation criterion was met.

The study protocol was approved by institutional review boards at each participating site. The study was carried out in accordance with the ethical principles set out in the Declaration of Helsinki and consistent with the International Conference on Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines and other applicable regulatory requirements. All patients provided written informed consent.

Endpoints and assessments

The dual primary endpoints were PFS by BICR and OS. Secondary efficacy endpoints included objective response rate (ORR), duration of response (DoR), and disease control rate (DCR) at 12 weeks (all by BICR and investigator assessment). Other secondary endpoints were PFS by investigator assessment, time to first subsequent therapy or death (TFST), time to second subsequent therapy or death (TSST), and time to second progression or death (PFS2).

Secondary PRO endpoints were time to deterioration in GHS/QoL, pain, and physical functioning, measured using the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Core 30-item quality-of-life questionnaire (EORTC QLQ-C30).32 The primary analysis DCO (17 July 2023) represented the final analysis for the dual primary endpoint of PFS by BICR. At the final analysis DCO (24 July 2024), updated efficacy analyses were conducted for OS and secondary endpoints by investigator assessment only.

Safety and tolerability were also assessed; adverse events (AEs) were graded by Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0. Details of the prevention and management (per toxicity management guidelines) of adverse events of special interest (AESIs) for Dato-DXd assessed in TROPION-Breast01 were previously published.24 An independent interstitial lung disease (ILD) adjudication committee reviewed all cases of potential ILD/pneumonitis to confirm the diagnosis and whether the event was related to the study drug. Ophthalmological assessments were mandated for both study arms at screening, every three cycles, as clinically indicated during the study, and at the end of treatment.

Statistical analysis

Statistical methods for the TROPION-Breast01 study were described previously.24 This prespecified analysis included all patients enrolled in mainland China and all statistical analyses in this cohort were exploratory. Efficacy analyses were carried out in the intention-to-treat (ITT) population, which included all randomly assigned patients, and safety was analyzed in the safety analysis set, comprising all patients who received at least one dose of study intervention. PFS and OS were analyzed using a log-rank test with HR and associated 95% CIs estimated using a Cox proportional hazards model. Safety was summarized descriptively.

Details of the analysis of PROs in TROPION-Breast01 have previously been published.33 Scores for the EORTC QLQ-C30 were calculated according to the published scoring manuals.34 Time to deterioration was assessed in the ITT population and was defined as the time from random assignment to the date of the first clinically meaningful change from baseline. Meaningful change thresholds were based on derived thresholds of a 16.6-point change from baseline for GHS/QoL and pain and a 13.3-point change for physical functioning.35 Analysis of time to deterioration used the same methodology as PFS and OS analyses.

Results

Patients

Between 18 October 2021 and 26 December 2022, a total of 732 patients were randomly assigned in the TROPION-Breast01 study. Of these patients, 83 were enrolled in mainland China, of whom 44 were randomly assigned to receive Dato-DXd and 39 to receive ICC [26 (66.7%) eribulin, 6 (15.4%) gemcitabine, 4 (10.3%) capecitabine, 3 (7.7%) vinorelbine]. Three patients who were randomly assigned in the ICC arm did not receive treatment, all of whom were planned to receive eribulin. The data reported in this paper are exclusively from patients recruited in mainland China.

Patient demographics and baseline characteristics in the two treatment groups were generally well balanced, with some numerical differences observed, including prior CDK4/6 inhibitor therapy (received by 77.3% and 61.5% of patients in the Dato-DXd and ICC arms, respectively) (Table 1). Efficacy

At the final analysis for the dual primary endpoint of PFS by BICR, which was conducted at the DCO of 17 July 2023, the median duration of study follow-up in the China cohort was 9.6 months (range 0.0-12.2). In total, 25 of 44 (56.8%) patients who received Dato-DXd and 24 of 39 (61.5%) patients who received ICC had a PFS event as assessed by BICR. There was numerically longer PFS in patients receiving Dato-DXd compared with ICC [HR 0.54 (95% CI 0.30-0.96), nominal P = 0.0329] (Figure 1A). Median PFS was 8.1 months [95% CI 4.4-not calculable (NC)] in the Dato-DXd arm versus 4.2 months (95% CI 1.6-5.5) in the ICC arm. Safety

At this final analysis DCO, the median total treatment duration was numerically longer in the Dato-DXd arm [6.4 months (range 0.7-21.8)] than in the ICC arm [2.8 months (range 0.2-13.3)] in the mainland China cohort. TRAEs occurred in 97.7% and 83.3% of patients in the Dato-DXd (n = 44) and ICC (n = 36) safety populations, respectively. The rate of grade ≥3 TRAEs with Dato-DXd was approximately half that with ICC (29.5% versus 58.3%), and serious TRAEs occurred in 4.5% (n = 2) and 13.9% (n = 5) of patients in the Dato-DXd and ICC arms, respectively. Dose reductions because of TRAEs occurred in 9.1% (n = 4) of patients who received Dato-DXd and in 22.2% (n = 8) of patients who received ICC and TRAEs led to dose interruptions in 15.9% (n = 7) and 25.0% (n = 9) of patients, respectively. One patient (2.3%) in the Dato-DXd arm discontinued treatment due to TRAEs; there were no TRAEs resulting in discontinuation of treatment in the ICC arm. No fatal TRAEs were reported in either arm.

Oral mucositis/stomatitis events occurred in 43.2% of patients (grade 1/grade 2/grade 3: 25.0%/9.1%/9.1%). There were no oral mucositis/stomatitis events resulting in discontinuation of Dato-DXd. The most common oral mucositis/stomatitis events were stomatitis (22.7% of patients) and mouth ulceration (18.2% of patients). Per protocol, patients in both arms underwent ophthalmological assessments throughout the study. Ocular surface events in the Dato-DXd arm were mostly low grade (any grade/grade 1/grade 2: 47.7%/40.9%/4.5%) and led to discontinuation of Dato-DXd in one patient. In the ICC arm, ocular surface events occurred in 13.9% of patients, all of which were grade 1. The most common ocular surface event in both arms was dry eye (Dato-DXd, 36.4% of patients; ICC, 11.1% of patients). One patient (2.3%) in the Dato-DXd arm had an adjudicated drug-related ILD/pneumonitis event (grade 2).

Hematological toxicity was the most notable feature of the safety profile in the ICC arm, with the most frequent TRAEs being neutropenia (grouped term; any grade/grade ≥3: 58.3%/36.1%), leukopenia (grouped term; any grade/grade ≥3: 55.6%/25.0%), and anemia (any grade/grade ≥3: 52.8%/8.3%). Thrombocytopenia (grouped term) occurred in 22.2% (grade 3: 2.8%) of patients. There were no grade 5 hematological events.

Patient-reported outcomes

Compliance rates with EORTC QLQ-C30 in the Dato-DXd and ICC arms, respectively, were 84.1% and 80.6% at baseline and 56.4% and 52.5% overall. The results of time to deterioration in GHS/QoL, pain, and physical functioning were numerically in favor of the Dato-DXd arm versus ICC (Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108538). Median time to deterioration in GHS/QoL with Dato-DXd versus ICC was 4.2 (95% CI 1.4-8.4) versus 1.4 (95% CI 0.8-NC) months [HR 0.64 (95% CI 0.33-1.25)]. For pain, median time to deterioration was 3.5 (95% CI 1.4-NC) versus 2.1 (95% CI 0.8-5.9) months for Dato-DXd versus ICC [HR 0.64 (95% CI 0.31-1.33)]. Median time to deterioration in physical functioning was 8.4 (95% CI 2.1-NC) versus 2.1 (95% CI 0.8-NC) months for Dato-DXd versus ICC [HR 0.62 (95% CI 0.30-1.32)].

Discussion

Results from the TROPION-Breast01 study have led to approvals of Dato-DXd in multiple countries globally for the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic, HR+/HER2— breast cancer who have received prior endocrine-based therapy and chemotherapy for unresectable or metastatic disease.22,23 In the global study population, Dato-DXd demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in the dual primary endpoint of PFS by BICR compared with ICC, with a 37% reduction in risk of progression or death.24 At the prespecified final analysis, OS was not statistically significant.25 In this prespecified analysis of TROPION-Breast01, the efficacy and safety of Dato-DXd in Chinese patients were consistent with the overall global population. The dual primary endpoint of PFS by BICR numerically favored Dato-DXd compared with ICC, with a 46% reduction in risk of progression or death (HR 0.54; nominal P = 0.0329 per BICR). This PFS benefit was maintained over time, with 6-month PFS rates of 55.8% with Dato-DXd and 30.8% with ICC, and 9-month PFS rates of 36.8% and 20.5%, respectively. At the final DCO, OS numerically favored the Dato-DXd arm compared with the ICC arm in the China cohort [HR 0.83 (95% CI 0.49-1.43), nominal P = 0.5028]. Discerning the OS effect of study treatments in the oncology field is increasingly challenging given the growing availability of subsequent therapies,36, 37, 38 and in the global cohort, an imbalance between arms in subsequent ADC treatment may have affected OS results.25 The majority of patients in both arms (Dato-DXd, 84.1%; ICC, 74.4%) in this China cohort received a subsequent therapy after discontinuation of study treatment, which may have affected survival outcomes.

Consistent with the global population, most secondary efficacy endpoints in the China cohort assessed by the investigator were numerically improved with Dato-DXd versus ICC. ORR by investigator assessment in the Dato-DXd arm was approximately three times that in the ICC arm and DCR at 12 weeks was improved with Dato-DXd versus ICC. Given the small number of responders in the ICC arm (n = 4), the numerically longer median DoR with ICC should be interpreted with caution, as it may reflect sampling variability rather than a true difference between treatment groups. In addition, TFST, TSST, and PFS2 were all longer in the Dato-DXd arm, in line with that seen in the global population.

Dato-DXd demonstrated a manageable safety profile in Chinese patients in TROPION-Breast01, consistent with that observed in the global population. Although the median total duration of treatment was longer in the Dato-DXd arm than the ICC arm (6.4 versus 2.8 months), the rate of grade ≥3 TRAEs with Dato-DXd was approximately half that with ICC (29.5% versus 58.3%). The rate of serious TRAEs was also lower with Dato-DXd than ICC (4.5% versus 13.9%). In both the China and global populations in TROPION-Breast01, nausea was the most common TRAE of any grade in the Dato-DXd arm and the most common feature of the safety profile in the ICC arm was hematological toxicity.24 However, unlike the global population, increased AST and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) were among the most common TRAEs in both arms in the China cohort. In particular, increased AST was the second most common TRAE of any grade in the Dato-DXd arm in the China cohort (occurring in 40.9% of patients), whereas it was only reported in 8.6% of patients in the global Dato-DXd arm.24 However, the higher rate of AST- and ALT-increased was observed with both Dato-DXd and ICC in the China cohort and rates of grade ≥3 events were low (≤1 patient in each arm). It is possible that this increase in laboratory value-based AEs could be attributed to differences in local reporting practices by investigators in China versus other geographic regions, as seen in China cohort analyses of other global studies.39, 40, 41 Finally, AESIs for Dato-DXd in this China cohort were typically low grade and manageable per toxicity management guidelines, as previously described.24 Consistent with the global population, the rate of adjudicated drug-related ILD/pneumonitis was low, with only one event (grade 2) reported.

PROs provide meaningful insights into patient experiences of symptoms, functioning, and health-related quality of life.42, 43, 44 As seen in the TROPION-Breast01 global population, time to deterioration in GHS/QoL, pain, and physical functioning (secondary endpoints) was numerically in favor of Dato-DXd compared with ICC in this China cohort, complementing the observed improvement in efficacy and manageable safety profile.

Comparisons between ADCs approved for metastatic HR+/HER2— are limited by differences in study populations, designs, and conduct, and should be interpreted with caution. However, differences in their safety profiles are observed. A subgroup analysis of the DESTINY-Breast04 study was conducted to examine T-DXd versus treatment of physicians’ choice in patients with previously treated metastatic HR+/HER2-low breast cancer who were enrolled in Asia.45 In this cohort, of whom 29.1% of patients were from China, hematological toxicity was a prominent feature of the safety profile of T-DXd, with the most common grade ≥3 TRAEs being neutropenia (16.3%), anemia (12.9%), and leukopenia (11.6%). Additionally, in a phase III study of SG in Asian patients with previously treated metastatic HR+/HER2— breast cancer, of whom 71% were enrolled in China, the most common treatment-emergent AEs of grade ≥3 were neutropenia (69%), leukopenia (42%), and anemia (18%).46 The TROP2-directed ADC, sacituzumab tirumotecan (Sac-TMT), has also shown hematological toxicity in the phase III OptiTROP-Breast02 study of Chinese patients with previously treated metastatic HR+/HER2— breast cancer; the most common TRAEs of grade ≥3 with Sac-TMT were decreased neutrophil count (45%), decreased white blood cell count (31%), and anemia (13%).47 In this TROPION-Breast01 China cohort, the frequency of hematological toxicity with Dato-DXd was higher than that observed in the global population [neutropenia (grouped term), 31.8% versus 10.8%; anemia, 27.3% versus 11.1%; leukopenia (grouped term): 25.0% versus 7.2%]. Differences in reporting practices in China versus other geographic regions, as previously described for other laboratory abnormalities, may also explain the higher frequency of hematological toxicity observed in this study and others. However, the majority of these events in this study were low grade, with only one grade 3-4 neutropenia and one grade 3-4 leukopenia event reported.

It should be noted that this prespecified analysis is limited by the descriptive nature of the results, open-label design, and small sample size. Additionally, although compliance with EORTC QLQ-C30 was generally balanced between arms and baseline questionnaire compliance was consistent with that in the global population,33 overall questionnaire compliance was low in both arms in this China cohort and the sample size was small, particularly at later time points, limiting interpretation of the PRO data. Despite this, the efficacy results and safety profile observed in this Chinese population in TROPION-Breast01 are consistent with those from the wider global population, which included patients enrolled across Asia, Europe, the United States, South America, and Africa.24

Conclusion

The findings from this China cohort, which show numerically improved efficacy and a manageable safety profile consistent with the global population with Dato-DXd versus ICC, support Dato-DXd as a new treatment option for Chinese patients with previously treated metastatic HR-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer.

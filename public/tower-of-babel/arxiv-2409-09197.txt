A relation between the Dirichlet and the Regularity problem for Parabolic equations
Martin Dindoš, Erika Nyström

Abstract
. We study the relationship between the Dirichlet and Regularity problem for parabolic operators of the form L = div ( A ∇ ⋅ ) − ∂ t L=\Div(A\nabla\cdot)-\partial_{t} on cylindrical domains Ω = 𝒪 × ℝ \Omega=\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}} , where the base 𝒪 ⊂ ℝ n \mathcal{O}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} is a uniform domain with n − 1 n-1 -Ahlfors regular boundary (and for one result a Lipschitz domain) in the spatial variables. In the paper we answer the question when the solvability of the L p L^{p} Regularity problem for L L (denoted by ( R L ) p (R_{L})_{p} ) can be deduced from the solvability of the L p ′ L^{p^{\prime}} Dirichlet problem for the adjoint operator L ∗ L^{*} (denoted ( D L ∗ ) p ′ (D_{L}^{*})_{p^{\prime}} ). We show that this holds if for at least one q ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) q\in(1,\infty) the problem ( R L ) q (R_{L})_{q} is solvable. That is, we establish a duality/dichotomy result: Dirichlet solvability implies Regularity solvability in the dual L p L^{p} range, or the Regularity problem is not solvable in any L p L^{p} . Results like these were only known in the elliptic settings (Kenig-Pipher (1993) and Shen (2006)) but are new for parabolic PDEs. Our result is one of the key components needed for the recent advancement of Dindoš, Li and Pipher in understanding solvability of the Regularity problem for operators whose coefficients satisfy certain natural Carleson condition (called also DKP-condition in the elliptic case).



1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the relationship between the parabolic Dirichlet and Regularity problems
on parabolic cylinders
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}

for the operators of the form

(1.1)

L=\Div(A\nabla\cdot)-\partial_{t},

where
A=A(X,t)

is bounded, measurable and uniformly elliptic.

To be precise, for any
1<p<\infty
,
we wish to show that if the
L^{p^{\prime}}
Dirichlet problem is solvable (which we denote by
(D_{L^{*}})_{p^{\prime}}
),
then the
L^{p}
regularity problem
is solvable (denoted by
(R_{L})_{p}
) (see Definitions
2.3
and
2.39
), provided this problem is solvable for at least one
q\in(1,\infty)
.
Note that here
(D_{L^{*}})_{p^{\prime}}
denotes the solvability for the adjoint

L^{*}=\Div(A^{T}\nabla\cdot)+\partial_{t},

which is a backward in time parabolic operator.
Through a change of variables
v(X,t)=u(X,-t)
, and
\tilde{A}(X,t)=A^{T}(X,-t)

we can see that the solvability of
(D_{L^{*}})_{p^{\prime}}
is equivalent to solvability of
(D_{\tilde{L}})_{p^{\prime}}

where

\tilde{L}v\coloneqq\Div(\tilde{A}\nabla v)-\partial_{t}v=0,

is the usual time-forward parabolic PDE.

It is important to establish a result of this type for several reasons.
First, there is the inspiration provided by the elliptic theory: in particular, the recent breakthroughs in solving the elliptic Regularity problem for operators satisfying certain natural Carleson condition (also called the DKP-condition) in
[
16
]
and
[
38
]
. The analogous elliptic duality/dichotomy result has been instrumental in the study of various classes of operators, and the extension of elliptic theory to the parabolic setting follows a natural and historical path.
Second, the parabolic result established here has already played a key role in the study of parabolic Regularity problems.
One of this paper’s co-authors has been involved in efforts to solve the Regularity problem for operators satisfying the DKP-condition, which
was achieved in the recent preprint
[
18
]
. Our main theorem of this paper was a
critical component
of the strategy of
[
18
]
. Further, a recent result
[
44
]
showing that solvability of the parabolic Regularity problem is stable under
large and small Carleson perturbations of coefficients also requires the main result of the present paper.
Finally, our result opens an avenue to establishing solvability of the parabolic Regularity problem for other classes of well studied
and natural parabolic operators once the solvability of the Dirichlet problem is known for data in some
L^{p}
. For example, for an operator in divergence form whose coefficients are independent of
x_{n}
-direction (the direction transversal to the boundary on the domain
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}
), the solvability of the parabolic Dirichlet problem has been
obtained in
[
2
]
. Only in the elliptic case is the Regularity problem for such operators known to be solvable
[
26
]
and it relied on the corresponding elliptic duality/dichotomy result.

The elliptic version of the result we present was fully formulated by Shen in
[
40
]
on bounded Lipschitz domains. It uses substantially an earlier result of Kenig and Pipher
[
31
]
where it was been established that (
(R_{L})_{q}
) implies (
(R_{L})_{p}
)
for all
1<p<q+\varepsilon
for some small
\varepsilon>0
.

The converse, that
(R_{L})_{p}
implies
(D_{L^{*}})_{p^{\prime}}

has already been shown in both the elliptic
[
17
,
31
]
and parabolic
[
14
]
settings,
with the domain in
[
14
]
being a
\Lip(1,1/2)
domain.

Thus, having established the motivation for seeking this result, let us outline the main novelty in our arguments that distinguish it from the earlier elliptic approach.

Let us recall that the main feature that distinguishes parabolic PDEs from the elliptic ones is the presence of the time variable that enjoys different scaling than the remaining spatial variables. This is due to the fact that the second derivative
\nabla^{2}u
behaves like
\partial_{t}u
(as follows from the PDE) and hence the first spatial derivative

\nabla u
scales like a half time-derivative
D^{1/2}_{t}u
.

In particular, the consequence of this fact is then that the parabolic formulation of the Regularity problem requires boundary data with a half-time derivative and full spatial (tangential) gradient (c.f. (
1.2
)).

This simple observation gives rise to multiple problems we needed to overcome. The first (and the most difficult problem) was to build a new parabolic version of the end-point (
p=1
) of parabolic Hardy-Sobolev space, one that respects such a scaling but that can also be (real)-interpolated against the parabolic Sobolev spaces (for
p>1
).
We achieve this objective in section 6. This is by far the longest part of the paper as we had to build the space piece by piece from atoms that respect the desired scaling. The atoms have a free parameter
\beta\in(1,\infty]
, thus potentially obtaining not one
p=1
Hardy-Sobolev space but a whole scale of them indexed by
\beta
. As in the elliptic case we need to establish that for each
\beta
we obtain the same space (where a different choice of
\beta
only changes the norm to an equivalent one). Key for this is the version of the classical “Calderón-Zygmund lemma” (Proposition
6.13
) but now the presence of the half-time derivative
D^{1/2}_{t}
significantly complicates the matter. In this proposition it is unrealistic to expect for the decomposition

f=g+\sum_{i}b_{i}
(where
b_{i}
are multiples of atoms) to have
D^{1/2}_{t}g\in L^{\infty}
(even though we expect and obtain
\nabla g\in L^{\infty}
), as the mapping properties of the half-derivative operator imply that we should (at best) only expect
D^{1/2}_{t}g\in BMO
.

As it turns out this opens up another rabbit hole (that we had to dive into), as proving that a half-derivative - a rather complicated non-local object - belongs to BMO is quite a challenge. We needed to establish a new version of this condition (originally due to Strichartz
[
43
]
and adapted to parabolic case in
[
15
]
) in Theorem
2.20
. Our condition is different from the one established in
[
15
]
and it is more convenient to work with in the light of the new characterization of Trebel-Lizorkin spaces established in
[
46
]
.
Finally, after Proposition
6.13
is established we obtain both equivalence of the newly defined spaces for different parameters
\beta
as well as the desired real interpolation properties using the
K
-method.

Our second important challenge was to adapt our argument to work both on domains that are bounded or unbounded in space (they are always unbounded in time variable). In section 5.2 we present a rather sophisticated localization arguments that show that key challenge are the estimates of non-tangential maximal function near support of the boundary data as “away parts” enjoy better bounds due to exponential decay. The paper
[
18
]
which uses our result exploits this decomposition even further.

The original formulation of the parabolic Regularity problem is due to Fabes and Rivière
[
20
]
who formulated it for
C^{1}
cylinders which was then re-formulated to Lipschitz cylinders by Brown
[
7
,
8
]
. Lipschitz cylinders are domains of the form
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times[0,T]
where
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and time variable runs over a bounded interval
[0,T]
.
Subsequent authors such as Mitrea
[
36
]
, Nystróm
[
39
]
or
Castro-Rodríguez-López-Staubach
[
10
]
have dealt with this issue in a similar way.

What Brown has done was to consider solutions on
\Omega
with initial data
u=0
at
t=0
and naturally extending
u
by zero for all
t<0
. Then a definition of half-derivative using fractional integrals
I_{\sigma}

for functions
f\in C^{\infty}(-\infty,T)
is used to give meaning to
D^{1/2}_{t}f
and
D^{1/2}_{t}u
.

A variant of this approach (which we present here) is to consider the parabolic PDE on on infinite cylinder

\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
. This can be always be achieved by extending the coefficients by setting

A(X,t)=A(X,T)
for
t>T
and
A(X,t)=A(X,0)
for
t<0
. Since the parabolic PDE has a defined direction of time the solution on the infinite cylinder
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
with boundary data
u\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0
for
t<0
will coincide with the solution on the finite cylinder
\mathcal{O}\times[0,T]
with zero initial data.

Having a well-defined half-derivative the authors
[
8
,
10
,
36
,
39
]
then define the parabolic Regularity problem by asking for

(1.2)

\|N[\nabla u]\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+\|N[D^{1/2}_{t}u]\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C(\|\nabla_{T}f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}).

Some authors also ask for control of
\|N[D^{1/2}_{t}H_{t}u]\|_{L^{p}}
, where
H_{t}
is the one-dimensional Hilbert transform in the
t
-variable.

This approach is reasonable, if the domain is not time-varying, i.e. of the form
\mathcal{O}\times(t_{0},t_{1})
but
runs immediately into an obvious issue when this is no longer the case, as any reasonable definition of half-derivative requires
u(X,t)
to be defined for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
or at least on a half-line.

This is the reason we restrict ourselves to domains of the form
\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
here to avoid further complications connected with dealing with this non-local term. In particular, in
[
18
]
, that uses our result, only domains of this type are considered.

Finally, we are ready to state our main result which we split into two theorems as there are slightly different assumptions on the domain in each case.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a uniform domain (i.e. it satisfies interior Corkscrew and Harnack chain conditions) with
n-1
-Ahlfors regular boundary (that is
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(B(x,r)\cap\partial\mathcal{O})\approx r^{n-1}
)
and
L
be a parabolic operator of the form (
1.1
) on the domain
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Suppose that the
L^{p^{\prime}}
Dirichlet problem for the adjoint operator,
(D_{L^{*}})_{p^{\prime}}
, is solvable,
and that the regularity problem
(R_{L})_{q}
is solvable for some
1<q<p
.
Then
(R_{L})_{p}
is also solvable.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
\mathcal{O}
is Lipschitz (either bounded or unbounded) and
L
be a parabolic operator of the form (
1.1
) on the domain
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Suppose that
(R_{L})_{p}
is solvable for some
1<p<\infty
.
Then
(R_{L})_{q}
is solvable for
1<q<p
.

Theorem

1.3
is the parabolic version of the result of
[
40
]
, while
Theorem

1.4
is an analogue of the elliptic result in
[
32
]
.

By combining these two theorems we can obtain the following:

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that
\mathcal{O}
is Lipschitz (either bounded or unbounded) and
L
be a parabolic operator of the form (
1.1
) on the domain
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Suppose that
(D_{L^{*}})_{p^{\prime}}
is solvable for some
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Then either
(R_{L})_{p}
is solvable or
(R_{L})_{q}
is not solvable for any
1<q<\infty
.

In summary, thanks to
Theorem

1.5
and
[
14
]
we have the following two implications

(R_{L})_{p}\implies(D_{L^{*}})_{p^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad(D_{L^{*}})_{p^{\prime}}+(R_{L})_{q}\implies(R_{L})_{p},

for parabolic operators on Lipschitz cylinders.

We have left open some further research directions,
Theorem

1.4
has stronger assumption on the domain
\mathcal{O}
than the other result and thus it might be possible to improve this result further beyond the Lipschitz cylinders. What limits us currently are interpolation results such as
Proposition

6.46
which only works on boundaries that locally look like
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Another natural direction would be to consider time varying domains (restricting initially to domains that locally look like a graph of
\Lip(1,1/2)
functions).

The paper is set up as follows. Section
2

contains definitions needed for the rest of the paper and Section

Theorem

is proven first, motivated by the method from

]
.
The two cases

Theorem

which closely follows its elliptic version as in

2.
Basic definitions

2.1.
Domain

In general our domain will be a cylinder
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}
,
where the base
\mathcal{O}
is a 1-sided non-tangentially accessible domain (also called uniform domain) with Ahlfors regular boundary of dimension
n-1
. As the precise definition does not play any significant role here we omit the details and refer the reader to
[
13
]
which has the same settings as here and contains all definitions in great detail (in particular, the existence of interior Harnack chains and corkscrew balls for any boundary point plus the Ahlfors condition).

We denote points in
\Omega
by
(X,t)
or also
(\xi,\tau)
, where
X,\,\xi\in\mathcal{O}
and
t,\,\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
and points on the boundary by
(P,t)\in\partial\Omega=\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
The space
\Omega
is equipped with the norm
\|(\xi,\tau)\|=\rho
,
where
\rho
is the positive solution to the following equation

(2.1)

\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{4}}=1.

One can easily show that
\|(\xi,\tau)\|\sim|\xi|+|\tau|^{1/2}
and
that this norm scales correctly according to the parabolic nature of the
PDE.

Next we introduce notation for balls, cubes and cylinders

•

B_{r}(X,t):=\{(Y,s)\in\Omega;\|(Y,s)-(X,t)\|\leq r\}
(parabolic ball),

•

B_{r}(X):=\{Y\in\mathcal{O};|Y-X|\leq r\}
(ball in space only),

•

C_{r}(X,t):=B_{r}(X)\times(t-r^{2},t+r^{2})
(parabolic cylinder),

•

Q_{r}(X,t):=\{(Y,s)\in\Omega;|Y_{i}-X_{i}|\leq r,|s-t|\leq r^{2}\}
(parabolic cube),

•

\Delta_{r}(P,t):=\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(P,t)
(boundary ball),

•

T(\Delta_{r}(P,t)):=\Omega\cap B_{r}(P,t)
(Carleson region).

Occasionally, we drop dependence of these sets on the point
(X,t)
and
r
and just write
B,\,C,\,Q
respectively. When doing this, the notation
r(B)
or
r(Q)
will denote the “radius”
r
of these sets. We use the similar convention also for
\Delta
and
T(\Delta)
.
When writing
2B
or
5\Delta
, it means enlarged sets (with same center point but
2\times
or
5\times
the radius).
Next, given
(X,t)\in\Omega
we let
\delta(X,t)=\delta(X)=\dist(X,\mathcal{O})

denotes the parabolic distance to the boundary.

Note that because
\mathcal{O}
is a CAD we have that for
P\in\mathcal{O}
and
\diam\mathcal{O}>r>0

the existence of a corkscrew point
A_{r}(P)
.
Thus for
\Delta=\Delta(P,t;r)\subset\Omega
with
r<\diam\mathcal{O}

we define
V^{+}(\Delta)=(A_{r}(P),t+100r^{2})
and
V^{-}(\Delta)=(A_{r}(P),t-100r^{2})

as the “forward in time” and “backward in time” corkscrew point respectively.

Given an aperture
a>0
, it is customary to define non-tangential approach regions (“cones”) as follows:

\tilde{\Gamma}_{a}(P,t)\coloneqq\{(X,s)\in\Omega:\|(X,s)-(P,t)\|<(1+a)\delta(X)\}.

However such cones lack one desired property, namely that the set (which can be thought of as the reverse cone originating from
(X,t)
)

\{(P,\tau)\in\partial\Omega:\,(X,t)\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{a}(P,\tau)\}

always has the surface area comparable to the
\delta(X,t)^{n+1}
. To fix this issue we do as follows. Let

•

\gamma_{a}(P)\coloneqq\{X\in\mathcal{O}:|X-P|<(1+a)\delta(X)\}
,

•

S_{a}(X)\coloneqq\{P\in\partial\mathcal{O}:X\in\gamma_{a}(P)\}
,

•

\tilde{S}_{a}(X)\coloneqq\bigcup_{P\in S_{a}(X)},\{P^{\prime}\in\partial\mathcal{O}:|P^{\prime}-P|<a\delta(X)\}
,

•

\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(P)\coloneqq\{X\in\Omega:P\in\tilde{S}_{a}(X)\}
,

•

\Gamma_{a}(P,t)\coloneqq\{(X,s)\in\Omega:X\in\tilde{\gamma}_{a}(P),\;|s-t|<a\delta(X)^{2}\}
.

Note that
\Gamma_{a}(P,t)
defined in the last line is our version of the non-tangential approach region and
is equivalent to
\tilde{\Gamma}_{a}(P,t)
since for any
a>0
there exists
a_{1},a_{2}>0
such that

\Gamma_{a_{1}}(P,t)\subset\tilde{\Gamma}_{a}(P,t)\subset{\Gamma}_{a_{2}}(P,t)

and vice versa. However, as the set
\tilde{S}_{a}(X)
can be written as a union of boundary balls of radius
a\delta(X)
it follows that the size of this set is always comparable to
\delta(X,t)^{n+1}
as desired.
For the rest of the paper we will let
a=1
and drop the subscript next to
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a function
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
, the non-tangential maximal function is given by

N_{\sup}[u](P,t)\coloneqq\sup_{\Gamma(P,t)}|u|.

Since
\nabla u
for example, is not defined pointwise it is customary to define a version of non-tangential maximal function using

averages as follows:

N[u](P,t)\coloneqq\sup_{\Gamma(P,t)}\Average^{2}[u],\quad\Average^{2}[u](X,s)\coloneqq\Big(\fint_{C_{\delta(X)/4}(X,s)}|u|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Occasionally, we will also consider truncated non-tangential cones
\Gamma^{r}(P,t):=\Gamma(P,t)\cap B_{r}(P,t)

and corresponding truncated non-tangential maximal function
N^{r}
.

We are now ready to define the Dirichlet problem

Definition 2.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty

We say that the
L^{p}

Dirichlet problem
for
L

(D_{L})_{p}
is solvable
if for each continuous

the solution to

\begin{cases}Lu=0,\quad\text{in }\Omega\\

2.2.
Half time derivative and Parabolic Sobolev Space on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Here we consider an appropriate function space for our boundary data on

\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
.

As a rule of thumb, one derivative in time behaves as two
derivatives in spatial variables. Thus if we impose boundary data having one spatial derivative, the correct order of the time derivative is
1/2
, as we would like to respect the correct parabolic scaling.

This problem has been studied previously in
[
27
,
28
,
39
]
, who
have followed
[
19
]
in defining the homogeneous parabolic Sobolev
space
\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}
in the following way.

Definition 2.4
.

The
homogeneous parabolic Sobolev space

\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, for
1<p<\infty
, is defined to consist of
an equivalence class of functions
f
with distributional derivatives
satisfying
\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty
, where

(2.5)

\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\|\mathbb{D}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

and

(2.6)

(\mathbb{D}f)\,\widehat{\,}\,(\xi,\tau):=\|(\xi,\tau)\|\widehat{f}(\xi,\tau),

with
\|(\xi,\tau)\|
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
defined by (
2.1
).

In addition, following
[
22
]
, we define a parabolic half-order time
derivative by

(2.7)

(\mathbb{D}_{n}f)\,\widehat{\,}\,(\xi,\tau):=\frac{\tau}{\|(\xi,\tau)\|}\widehat{f}(\xi,\tau).

If
0<\alpha\leq 2
, then for
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
the

one-dimensional fractional differentiation operators

D_{\alpha}

are defined by

(2.8)

(D^{\alpha}g)\,\widehat{\,}\,(\tau):=|\tau|^{\alpha}\widehat{g}(\tau).

It is also well known that if
0<\alpha<1
then

(2.9)

D^{\alpha}g(s)=c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{g(s)-g(\tau)}{|s-\tau|^{1+\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

whenever
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
If
h(x,t)\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
then by
D^{\alpha}_{t}h:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
we mean the function
D^{\alpha}h(x,\cdot)
defined a.e. for each
fixed
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
. When
\alpha=1/2
we have

D^{1/2}_{t}D^{1/2}_{t}=-H_{t}\partial_{t},

where
H_{t}
is the Hilbert transform taken in the time variable.

We record the connection between
\mathbb{D}
,
\mathbb{D}_{n}
and
D_{t}^{1/2}
. By

[
14
,
21
,
22
]
we have that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{D}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sim\|\mathbb{D}_{n}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sim\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},

for all
1<p<\infty
, as well as at the
{\rm BMO}
end-point:

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{D}f\|_{{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sim\|\mathbb{D}_{n}f\|_{{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|\nabla f\|_{{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sim\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|\nabla f\|_{{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Here
\nabla
denotes the usual gradient in the
variables
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and
{\rm BMO}
denotes the usual space of bounded mean oscillations (with respect to parabolic balls).

When
p=1
we no longer have (
2.10
) and hence the norm of
\dot{L}^{1}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
might not be equivalent to

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{1}}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}f\|_{L^{1}}
. We therefore define the space

(2.12)

\dot{W}^{p}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\{f\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty\},

with norm
\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}f\|_{L^{p}}
. Modulo constants when
p\in(1,\infty)
we have

\dot{W}^{p}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and the norms are comparable, but when
p=1,\infty
the spaces might not coincide. These spaces will be useful for interpolation. We will also need a weaker end-point space than
\dot{W}^{\infty}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. We denote by
\dot{W}^{\infty,{{\rm BMO}}}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
the space such that

\nabla f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
D^{1/2}_{t}f\in{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

2.3.
Connections to Triebel-Lizorkin and Hajłasz-Sobolev spaces.

Here and in what follows we will use notation
X=(x,t)
to denote points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with components
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let us recall the definition of Triebel-Lizorkin spaces

F^{s}_{p,q}
. The fact that our operator
\mathbb{D}
is defined by (
2.6
) implies that it fits naturally to the settings of these spaces for
q=2
, but we need to account for the parabolic scaling.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
s\in(0,\infty)
and
p\in(0,\infty]
. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\mbox{\rm supp }\hat{\varphi}\subset\{(\xi,\tau):1/2\leq\|(\xi,\tau)\|\leq 3/2\},

and

|\hat{\varphi}(\xi,\tau)|\geq c_{0}>0\mbox{ for }3/5\leq\|(\xi,\tau)\|\leq 5/3.

Let
\varphi_{\rho}(x,t)
denote the usual parabolic dilation by
\rho
, that is

\varphi_{\rho}(x,t)=\rho^{-(n+1)}\varphi(x/\rho,t/\rho^{2}).

The homogeneous parabolic Triebel-Lizorkin space
\dot{F}^{s}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is defined as the collection of all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that
\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{s}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty
where

(2.14)

\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{s}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\left\|\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}4^{ks}|\varphi_{2^{-k}}*f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},

with the usual modification when
p=\infty
.

By the Littlewood-Paley theorem when
M_{k}f=\varphi_{2^{-k}}*f
then for
1<p<\infty

\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\approx\left\|(M_{k}f)_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{0}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Hence

\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\approx\left\|(2^{k}M_{k}f)_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{1}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Note that is is only true when parabolic scaling is applied, in the usual Euclidean metric we have

\dot{L}^{p}_{1/2}({\mathbb{R}})=\dot{F}^{1/2}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\dot{L}^{p}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})=\dot{F}^{1}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})

(see
[
37
]
).

The paper
[
46
]
gave a different characterisation of these spaces using averages. Again we have to account for parabolic scaling, but the argument adapts easily. Let us first define

(2.15)

S_{s,disc}(f)(x,t)=\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}4^{ks}\left(f(x,t)-\fint_{B_{2^{k}}(x,t)}f\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Here
B_{\alpha}(x,t)
is the parabolic ball centred at
(x,t)
with radius
\alpha
. By Theorem 1.1 of
[
46
]

\|S_{s,disc}(f)\|_{L^{p}}\sim\|f\|_{\dot{F}^{s}_{p,2}}
for all
1<p<\infty
. For our further considerations we would prefer continuous version of the square function (
2.15
). It is not hard to see that by averaging over set of balls of radius
2^{\tau k}
,
1\leq\tau\leq 2
we may replace it by

(2.16)

S_{s}(f)(x,t)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha^{-2s-1}\left(f(x,t)-\fint_{B_{\alpha}(x,t)}f\right)^{2}d\alpha\right)^{1/2}.

In particular, for
s=1
we have
\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\approx\|S_{1}(f)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
for
1<p<\infty
.

We develop an interesting connection between this square function and new characterisation of the fact that
\mathbb{D}f\in{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Recall that
[
15
]
(Theorem 2.3) showed the following:

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
f\in{\rm Lip}(1,1/2)
then the following conditions are equivalent:

•

\mathbb{D}f\in{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

•

(2.18)

\sup_{B_{r}}\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int\limits_{B_{r}}\int\limits_{\|(y,s)\|\leq r}\frac{|f(x+y,t+s)-2f(x,t)+f(x-y,t-s)|^{2}}{\|(y,s)\|^{n+3}}\,dy\,ds\,dx\,dt=B_{1}<\infty,

(2.19)

\|\mathbb{D}f\|_{\rm BMO}^{2}\sim B_{1}.

Now in the light of (
2.16
) we need to obtain a new version of the condition (
2.18
) where
the absolute value is moved to the outside of the interior integral. A method how to establish such conditions was developed by Strichartz
[
43
]
and our paper
[
15
]
adapts it to parabolic settings. We claim the following:

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
f\in{\rm Lip}(1,1/2)
then the following conditions are equivalent:

•

\mathbb{D}f\in{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

•

(2.21)

\sup_{B_{r}}\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{0}^{r}\alpha^{-3}\left|f(x,t)-\fint_{B_{\alpha}(x,t)}f\,\right|^{2}\,d\alpha\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x=B_{2}<\infty.

Moreover,
\|\mathbb{D}f\|_{\rm BMO}^{2}\sim B_{2}
and
r
is the diameter of the parabolic ball
B_{r}
. The statement also holds with parabolic cubes
Q_{r}
replacing the parabolic balls
B_{r}
.

Perhaps the two conditions (
2.18
) and (
2.21
) do not immediately look close, but after writing the interior integral in polar coordinates will reveal that we are taking averages over the shells
\|(y,s)\|=const

with the same scaling factor
\|(y,s)\|^{-3}
as in (
2.21
). The fact that the absolute value in (
2.21
) is on the outside of the most interior integral is the innovation
[
46
]
has introduced.

Proof.

Let us recall the method of Strichartz adapted to the parabolic setting in
[
15
]
. Write
F=\mathbb{D}f
where
F
is a tempered distribution.
Let

\varphi=\delta(0,0)-\chi_{\tilde{Q}_{1}(0,0)},

where
Q_{1}(0,0)
is a cube ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We may take a ball here instead of the cube, it will change the Fourier transform somewhat but not the gist of the argument (we prefer not to deal with Bessel’s functions). Then

(2.22)

\begin{split}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi,\tau)&=1-\frac{1-e^{-i\tau}}{i\tau}\prod^{n-1}_{j=1}\frac{1-e^{-i\xi_{j}}}{i\xi_{j}}.\end{split}

with
\widehat{\varphi}(\xi,\tau)\sim|\xi|+|\tau|
for small
\xi
and
\tau
.
We let

\widehat{\psi}=\frac{\widehat{\varphi}(\xi,\tau)}{\|(\xi,\tau)\|},

where
\psi_{\rho}
denotes the usual parabolic dilation
\rho
defined above.
We may rewrite (
2.21
) as

(2.23)

\sup_{Q_{r}}\frac{1}{|Q_{r}|}\int_{Q_{r}}\int_{0}^{r}\left(\psi_{\rho}*F\right)^{2}\frac{\,d\rho}{\rho}\,dx\,dt\sim B_{2}.

The function
\psi
satisfy the following conditions for some
\epsilon_{i}>0

(2.24)

\begin{split}\int\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t&=0,\\
|\psi(x,t)|&\lesssim\|(x,t)\|^{-n-1-\epsilon_{1}}\text{ for }\|(x,t)\|\geq a>0,\\
|\widehat{\psi}(\xi,\tau)|&\lesssim\|(\xi,\tau)\|^{\epsilon_{2}}\text{ for }\|(\xi,\tau)\|\leq 1,\\
|\widehat{\psi}(\xi,\tau)|&\lesssim\|(\xi,\tau)\|^{-\epsilon_{3}}\text{ for }\|(\xi,\tau)\|\geq 1.\end{split}

Therefore if
\mathbb{D}f=F\in{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
then
B_{2}\lesssim\|\mathbb{D}\phi\|_{*}^{2}
by
[
43
, Theorem 2.1]
;
this shows that first condition in
Theorem

2.20
implies the second one.

For the converse we proceed via an analogue of the proof of
[
43
, Theorem 2.6]
.
Consider

\hat{\theta}(\xi,\tau)=\|(\xi,\tau)\|\hat{\zeta}(\xi,\tau),

where
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Let
H^{1}_{00}
be the dense subclass of continuous
H^{1}
functions
g
such that
g
and all its derivatives decay rapidly, see
[
42
, p. 225]
.
Via an analogue of
[
23
, Theorem 3]
,
[
43
, Lemma 2.7]
by assuming (
2.23
) if
g\in H^{1}_{00}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, then

(2.25)

\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\iint\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}}\psi_{\rho}*F(x,t)\theta_{\rho}*g(x,t)\,dx\,dt\frac{\,d\rho}{\rho}\right|\lesssim B^{1/2}_{2}\|g\|_{H^{1}}.

Let

(2.26)

\begin{split}m(\xi,\tau)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{\hat{\psi}\left(-\rho\xi,-\rho^{2}\tau\right)}\|(\xi,\tau)\|\zeta(\rho\|(\xi,\tau)\|)\,d\rho.\end{split}

Such function
m
is homogeneous of degree zero, smooth away from the origin and the associated Fourier multipliers
M
is a Caldorón-Zygmund operator that preserves the class
H^{1}_{00}
and is bounded on
H^{1}
.

The non-degeneracy condition from
[
9
]
is the property that
|m(r\xi,r^{2}\tau)|^{2}
does not vanish identically in
r
for
(\xi,\tau)\neq(0,0)
. Hence as in
[
15
]
we may conclude that

(h,g)=(F,g)
for all
g\in H^{1}_{00}
where
h
is a
{\rm BMO}
function with
\|h\|_{*}^{2}\lesssim B_{2}
.
The identity
\hat{h}=\hat{F}

does not need to hold at the origin wherefore
\hat{h}-\hat{F}
may be supported at the origin and hence
F=h+p
where
p
is a polynomial.
Due to the assumption
\phi\in{\rm Lip}(1,1/2)
, clearly
F
must be a tempered distribution. Hence as in
[
43
]
we may conclude
F=h\in{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. This proves the claim.
∎

Now, we shall make connection between
\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and the Hajłasz-Sobolev space
\dot{M}^{1,p}_{d}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. In fact, we want to consider a more general setting and work on a set
\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}
, where

\partial\mathcal{O}
is
n-1
-Ahlfors regular set with respect to the standard
n-1
-dimensional Hausdoff measure.
In particular this allows us to talk about the Lebesgues spaces
L^{p}(\partial\mathcal{O})
.
Thus, when
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
, we have
\partial\mathcal{O}={\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
and therefore the discussion below applies to the space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}={\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
discussed above.

Definition 2.27
.

Let
d
be a complete metric on a measure space
(\mathcal{X},\mu)
. For
p\geq 1
,
0<s\leq 1
we say that a function
f\in L^{p}_{loc}(\mathcal{X})
belongs to the Hajłasz–Sobolev space
M^{s,p}(\mathcal{X})

if there exists a set
E
of
\mu
-measure zero such that

(2.28)

|f(X)-f(Y)|\leq d(X,Y)^{s}|[g(X)+g(Y)]\qquad\forall X,Y\in\mathcal{X}\setminus E.

The
M^{s,p}_{d}(\mathcal{X})
norm of
f
is given as the infimum of
L^{p}
norms of all such
g
.

In what follows we shall consider the space
M^{1,p}_{d}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}})

defined with respect to the parabolic distance function

(2.29)

d(X,X^{\prime})=d((x,t),(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}))=\|(x-x^{\prime},t-t^{\prime})\|\sim|x-x^{\prime}|+|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/2}.

The underlying measure is the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
. Here we introduce notation that matches the convention used above, i.e.,
X\in\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}
has the spatial coordinate
x
and time coordinate
t
.

Associated with the space
\dot{M}^{1,p}
is the so-called
Sobolev sharp maximal function

f^{\sharp}
defined by

(2.30)

f^{\sharp}(X)=\sup_{B:x\in B}\frac{1}{r(B)}\fint_{B}|f-f_{B}|.

Here
B
denotes a surface ball on the set
\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}
with respect to metric
d
of radius
r(B)
, i.e.,

B=B_{r(B)}(Y)=\{Z\in\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}:d(Y,Z)<r(B)\},

and
f_{B}
denotes the average
f_{B}=\fint_{B}f(Z)\,dZ
.

Let us also introduce
M
which is the usual (non-centred) Hardy-Littlewood parabolic maximal function

(2.31)

M(f)(X)=\sup_{B:X\in B}\fint_{B}|f|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n},

where the supremum is taken over all surface parabolic balls
B
that contain point
X
. Denote by

M_{q}(f)=M(|f|^{q})^{1/q}.

It follows from the usual boundedness of the maximal operator (which is true since
\partial\Omega
is an Ahlfors regular set) that
M
is
L^{p}
bounded for
p>1
and
M_{q}
for
p>q
.

Let us denote by
M^{t}
and
M^{x}
the variants of the maximal operator when the averages are taken only in
t
or
x
direction respectively. That is

M^{x}(f)(x,t)=\sup_{\Delta:x\in\Delta}\fint_{\Delta}|f(y,t)|\,dy,

and
\Delta=\Delta(z)=\{y\in\partial\mathcal{O}:|y-z|<r\}
, with
M^{t}
defined analogously. We also define
M^{x}_{q}
and
M^{t}_{q}
in analogy to
M_{q}
.

As on each fibre for a fixed
t
we have
M^{x}
being bounded on
L^{p}(\partial\mathcal{O})
for
p>1
we see by Fubini that
M^{x}
and
M^{t}
are
L^{p}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}})
bounded for
p>1
and the same is also true for
M^{x}_{q}
and
M^{t}_{q}
for
p>q
.

On metric spaces with doubling measure
\mu
, that is for some constant
C_{s}>1

\mu(B(X,2r))\leq C_{s}\mu(B(X,r)),\qquad\mbox{ for all points $X$ and $r>0$},

we have by
[
33
]
for all
p\geq 1
:

\|f\|_{\dot{M}^{1,p}_{d}}\sim\|f^{\sharp}\|_{L^{p}}

and (
2.28
) holds with
g
replaced by
f^{\sharp}
. In our case for
d
defined by (
2.29
) the volume of surface ball of radius
r
is proportional to
r^{n+1}
and hence the doubling constant
C_{s}
can be taken to be
2^{n+1}
. As in
[
33
]

s=\log_{2}C_{s}
is called the doubling dimension of our space, thus for
\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}
equipped with the metric
d
the doubling dimension is
s=n+1
.

In particular, we have on our space
\dot{M}_{d}^{1,p}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}})
for a.e.
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
:

(2.32)

|f(x,t)-f(y,t)|\leq|x-y|[g(x,t)+g(y,t)]\qquad\mbox{for $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-almost every }x,y\in\mathbb{\partial}\mathcal{O},

and for a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{O}

(2.33)

|f(x,t)-f(x,\tau)|\leq|t-\tau|^{1/2}[g(x,t)+g(x,\tau)]\qquad\mbox{for almost every }t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

For all
p>1
,
by Corollary 1.2 of
[
35
]
when
t
is fixed we have
\dot{L}^{p}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})=\dot{F}^{1}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})=\dot{M}_{|\cdot|}^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})
and so (
2.32
) is equivalent to having
\nabla f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Here
\dot{M}_{|\cdot|}^{1,p}
is defined with respect to the Euclidean distance
|\cdot|
.

When
\mathcal{O}
is a Lipschitz domain, the Triebel-Lizorkin space
\dot{F}^{1}_{p,2}(\partial\mathcal{O})
is still well-defined and we might take it as the definition of the space
\dot{L}^{p}_{1}(\partial\mathcal{O})
.
Thus in this case we still have
\dot{L}^{p}_{1}(\partial\mathcal{O})=\dot{F}^{1}_{p,2}(\partial\mathcal{O})=\dot{M}_{|\cdot|}^{1,p}(\partial\mathcal{O})
as Corollary 1.2 of
[
35
]
applies.

Also since
\dot{L}^{p}_{1/2}({\mathbb{R}})=\dot{F}^{1/2}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}})
, by Corollary 1.2 of
[
35
]

then
\dot{L}^{p}_{1/2}({\mathbb{R}})=\dot{F}^{1/2}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}})\subset\dot{F}^{1/2}_{p,\infty}({\mathbb{R}})=\dot{M}_{|\cdot|}^{1/2,p}({\mathbb{R}})

(here the switch to
1/2
derivative comes due to the fact that (
2.33
) uses the usual Euclidean metric on
{\mathbb{R}}
).

Hence for
f\in\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}})
,
p>1
we have that both (
2.32
) and (
2.33
) hold. This in turn implies that so does

(2.34)

|f(x,t)-f(y,\tau)|\leq d((x,t),(y,\tau))[h(x,t)+h(y,\tau)]

for almost every pairs of points in
\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}
with
h=M^{t}(g)+M^{x}(g)\in L^{p}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}})
.
It follows that

(2.35)

\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}})\subset\dot{M}_{d}^{1,p}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|f\|_{\dot{M}_{d}^{1,p}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}})},\quad p>1.

Consider any bounded nonnegative function supported in
B
and let
\chi(B)=\int_{B}\chi
. Suppose that

\chi(B)\sim|B|
. Consider

\left|f(X)-\frac{1}{{\chi}(B)}\int_{B}f\chi\right|=\left|\frac{1}{{\chi}(B)}\int_{B}(f(X)-f(Z))\chi(Z)dZ\right|

\leq\sup_{Z\in B}d(X,Z)\frac{1}{{\chi}(B)}\int_{B}[f^{\sharp}(X)+f^{\sharp}(Z)]\chi(Z)dZ,

which holds by (
2.28
) and the fact that
f^{\sharp}
can be used in place of
g
. Since
X\in B
we have
\sup_{Z\in B}d(X,Z)\lesssim r(B)
and hence

(2.36)

\frac{1}{r(B)}\left|f(X)-\frac{1}{{\chi}(B)}\int_{B}f\chi\right|\lesssim f^{\sharp}(X)+\fint_{B}f^{\sharp},

for any
X
for which
X\in B
.

2.4.
Parabolic Regularity problem

The previous section has defined the space
\dot{L}_{1,1/2}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}})
in the case when
\mathcal{O}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a bounded or unbounded Lipschitz domain. In particular, our definition implies that when

\mathcal{O}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n}):\,x_{n}>\phi(x^{\prime})\},

for some Lipschitz function
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, then

f\in\dot{L}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)
if

(2.37)

f\circ\pi\in\dot{L}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\qquad\mbox{where}\qquad\pi(x^{\prime},t)=(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}),t).

We would like to extend our definition to the case
\mathcal{O}
is a uniform domain with
n-1
-Ahlfors regular boundary in a way that is consistent with the previous subsection and so that for example (
2.35
) still holds.

Motivated by
[
38
]
with adaptation to our setting,
we may define the spatial gradient on
\partial\mathcal{O}
as above using the
Hajłasz-Sobolev space
. Namely,
For a Borel function
f:\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
we say that a Borel function
g:\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
Hajłasz upper spatial gradient
of
f
if

|f(X,t)-f(Y,t)|\leq|X-Y|(g(X,t)+g(Y,t))\qquad\textrm{for a.e. }(X,t),(Y,t)\in\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}.

We denote the collection of all Hajłasz upper spatial gradients of
f
as
\mathcal{D}_{s}(f)
.
For a measurable function
f
on
\partial\Omega=\partial\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}
we define the Sobolev space
\dot{L}_{1,1/2}^{p}(\partial\Omega)
via the norm

(2.38)

\|f\|_{\dot{L}_{1,1/2}^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\coloneqq\inf\{\|g\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}:\,g\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)\cap\mathcal{D}_{s}(f)\}+\|D_{1/2}^{t}f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

We can also see that
(
2.35
) still holds as it is a consequence of (
2.32
)-(
2.33
) which can be shown for
f
satisfying
(
2.38
). Also, it is easy to see that this defines the same space as previously for
\mathcal{O}
Lipschitz (c.f.
[
38
]
).

Now we are ready to define the Regularity problem.

Definition 2.39
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and the domain
\Omega
be as above.
We say that the
L^{p}
Regularity problem for
L

(R_{L})_{p}
is solvable
if for each continuous
f\in\dot{L}_{1,1/2}^{p}(\partial\Omega)
the solution to

\begin{cases}Lu=0,\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=f,\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

satisfies

(2.40)

\displaystyle\|N[\nabla u]\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{L}_{1,1/2}^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

Remark 2.41
.

One might object that a condition of the type

(2.42)

\displaystyle\|N[D_{1/2}^{t}u]\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{L}_{1,1/2}^{p}(\partial\Omega)},

should be satisfied too (c.f.
[
8
,
10
,
36
,
39
]
).
However, as it turns out, this condition is superfluous.
Indeed by
[
13
]
we have

(2.43)

\displaystyle\|N[D_{1/2}^{t}u]\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|N[\nabla u]\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+\|f\|_{\dot{L}_{1,1/2}^{p}(\partial\Omega)},

and clearly (
2.42
) follows from (
2.43
)
and (
2.40
). This simplifies our task, as we only need to establish bounds for
N[\nabla u]

and the bounds for the half-time derivative will follow.

2.5.
Localization

We shall establish the following useful result that will be needed later.

Proposition 2.44
.

Let
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times{\mathbb{R}}
, where
\mathcal{O}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a uniform domain with
n-1
-Ahlfors regular boundary. Assume that for some
p>1

f\in\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)
(with
\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}
defined by (
2.38
)).
There exists
h\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
with
\|h\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)}
for which the following holds:

Consider any parabolic ball

of radius

, let

and
\|\nabla_{x}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim r^{-1}
.
Then we have the following estimate:

\|(f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)\varphi\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{p}(32\Delta)}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}f\|_{L^{p}(32\Delta)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)}.

Furthermore for all

we have

(2.45)

|D^{1/2}_{t}((f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)\varphi)(x,t)-D^{1/2}_{t}f(x,t)\varphi(x,t)|\lesssim h(x,t).

Proof.

Recall that we know that we have for

(
2.34
) and hence

|f(x,t)-f(y,\tau)|\leq d((x,t),(y,\tau))[f^{\sharp}(x,t)+f^{\sharp}(y,\tau)]

for a.e. pair od points

.
We have to be very careful where we use the condition (

) as this does not characterise the space of functions for which
\|D^{1/2}_{t}f\|_{L^{p}}<\infty
.
We have lost a bit of regularity when we have used in the previous section the inclusion

which is proper.
However, we assume
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}})
which is better than just
D^{1/2}_{t}\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}})
. This is an extra piece of regularity that will make our argument work.
We calculate

. Clearly,

(2.47)

D^{1/2}_{t}((f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)\varphi)(x,t)=c\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(f(x,t)-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)\varphi(x,t)-(f(x,s)-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)\varphi(x,s)}{|t-s|^{3/2}}ds

(2.48)

=\varphi(x,t)D^{1/2}_{t}(f)(x,t)+c\int_{s\in\mathbb{R}\cap\{(x,s)\in 3\Delta\}}\frac{\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(x,s)}{|t-s|^{3/2}}(f(x,s)-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)ds.

The second term needs to be considered further.
We need to think about when the term
\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(x,s)\neq 0
as otherwise there is nothing to do. If the point
(x,t)
is far from support of some enlargement of
\Delta
, say
8\Delta
, whenever
\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(x,s)\neq 0
we will have
|t-s|\approx 2^{i}r^{2}
for some

for all

such that
(x,s)\in 3\Delta
.
We use a crude bound for the numerator

. This allows us to conclude that when
(x,t)\notin 8\Delta
then the second term will be bounded by

\lesssim(2^{-3i/2})r^{-1}M^{t<4r^{2}}(|f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f|)(x,t^{\prime}),\quad\mbox{ for some }i\geq 2.

Here

is an arbitrary point in

and

is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function in
t
-variable only on all balls of size
<4r^{2}
.

This leaves us to consider the case

. It is then possible that

is arbitrary small for

such that

. We use the fact that

is Lipschitz in

\approx r^{-2}
. We also split the integral to sets on which

for

. This gives us for the second term of (

By introducing extra term

into the denominator we again recognise a maximal function in
t
-variable. Thus continuing our calculation we have

\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j/2}r^{-1}M^{t<16r^{2}}(|f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f|)(x,t)\approx r^{-1}M^{t<16r^{2}}(|f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f|)(x,t).

We now estimate this maximal function. Let
(x,t)\in 8\Delta
. Using (
2.36
) for

we get that

(2.51)

r^{-1}\left|f(x,t)-\fint_{\Delta}f\right|\lesssim f^{\sharp}(x,t)+\fint_{8\Delta}f^{\sharp},

and hence averaging over any time-only ball

that contains

we get that

and after taking sup over all such balls of size

We take this as the definition of the function

. Clearly, since

and

bounded for

Combing this with (

) and (

)

\displaystyle|D^{1/2}_{t}((f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)\varphi)(x,t)-D^{1/2}_{t}f(x,t)\varphi(x,t)|\lesssim h(x,t),

After we integrate the inequality above over

Next we estimate the same integral on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8\Delta
. We only need to consider integrating over
(x,t)
for which there is

such that
(x,s)\in 8\Delta
. Let us call such

. Let us call

the set of such points

. It follows that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8\Delta}[D^{1/2}_{t}((f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)\varphi]^{p}\,dx\,dt=\displaystyle\int_{(B\times\mathbb{R})\setminus 8\Delta}[D^{1/2}_{t}((f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)\varphi]^{p}\,dx\,dt.

Recalling (

) and using the estimate in (

) we see that after further splitting the integral above into regions such that the distance of points

to

is approximately

we have that

\hskip-199.16928pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8\Delta}[D^{1/2}_{t}((f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)\varphi]^{p}\,dx\,dt

\lesssim\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\displaystyle\int_{(B\times\{t\in\mathbb{R}:{dist}((x,t),4\Delta)\approx 2^{i}r^{2}\}}(2^{-3ip/2})r^{-p}[M^{t<4r^{2}}(|f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f|)]^{p}(x,s_{0})dx\,dt

\lesssim\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}2^{i(1-3p/2)}\int_{32\Delta}h^{p}\,dx\,dt,

which shows that

of our function belongs to
L^{p}
. For the spatial “gradient” the argument is easier. With

as above we have that

|(f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)\varphi(x,t)-(f-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)\varphi(y,t)|\leq|x-y||f(x,t)-f(y,t)||\varphi(x,t)|

+|(f(y,t)-\textstyle\fint_{\Delta}f)||\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(y,t)|\lesssim|x-y|[f^{\sharp}(x,t)+f^{\sharp}(y,t)+h(y,t)],

where we have used the earlier estimate for

.
This is a good estimate if both

.
If one of the points is not inside

but

and finally if

a trivial estimate

which shows that for

Hence

and furthermore

1.3

5.1.
Unbounded case

For this section we assume that
\diam(\mathcal{O})=\infty
. What this means is that for any boundary point

P\in\partial\mathcal{O}
there exists interior corkscrew balls of arbitrary large size as the domain is unbounded.

5.1.1.
Almost good lambda argument

The bulk of the proof of
Theorem

1.3
is proving the “almost
\lambda
-inequality”
Lemma

5.1
.
Let
p>q
, and set

E(\lambda)\coloneqq\{M[N[\nabla u]^{q}]>\lambda\}\quad\text{and}\quad A=A(\varepsilon)\coloneqq 1/(2\varepsilon)^{q/p}.

Let
g\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
be a Hajłasz upper spatial gradient for
f

and let
h
be like in
Proposition

2.44
.
We have the following inequality:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
1<q<p<\infty
.
Suppose that
(R_{L})_{q}
and
(D_{L^{*}})_{p^{\prime}}
are solvable.
There exists
\varepsilon,\gamma
depending only on
p,q,n,L,\Omega
such that
for all
\lambda>0

(5.2)

\displaystyle|E(A\lambda)|\leq\varepsilon|E(\lambda)|+|\{M[|g|^{q}+|D_{1/2}^{t}f|^{q}+|h|^{q}]>\gamma\lambda\}|,

Proof:

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be a small constant to be determined.
By
[
11
]
there exists a family of “dyadic cubes” such that:

(1)

If
l\geq k
then either
Q_{\beta}^{l}\subset Q_{\alpha}^{k}
or
Q_{\beta}^{l}\cap Q_{\alpha}^{k}=\emptyset
,

(2)

For each
(k,\alpha)
and
l<k
there is a unique
\beta
so that
Q_{\alpha}^{k}\subset Q_{\beta}^{l}
,

(3)

Each
Q_{\alpha}^{k}
contains a ball
\Delta_{\alpha}^{k}
,
with
r(\Delta)=2^{-k}

(4)

There exists a constant
C_{0}>0

such that
2^{-k}\leq\mathrm{diam}(Q_{\alpha}^{k})\leq C_{0}2^{-k}
,

where the diameter is taken in the parabolic metric.
The last listed property implies together with the Ahlfors regularity of the surface measure that
\sigma(Q_{\alpha}^{k})\approx 2^{-k(n+1)}
.

Let
E(\lambda)=\bigcup_{k}Q_{k}

be a Whitney decompotition of
E(\lambda)

into a disjoint collection of maximal such “dyadic subcubes”.
In this place we use unboundedness of our domain,
as on a bounded domain for diam
(Q_{k})>>\mbox{diam}(\mathcal{O})

the cubes become “misshaped” (with long side in
t
much larger that the size in spatial variables).
We claim that we can choose
\varepsilon,\gamma,C_{0}

such that if
Q_{k}
is a cube with the property

(5.3)

\displaystyle Q_{k}\cap\{M[|D_{1/2}^{t}f|^{q}+|g|^{q}+|h|^{q}]\leq\gamma\lambda\}\neq\emptyset,

then

(5.4)

\displaystyle|E(A\lambda)\cap Q_{k}|\leq\varepsilon|Q_{k}|.

The desired estimate (
5.2
) then follows by summation in
k
.
Let us now fix a cube
Q_{k}
and drop the index,
writing
Q=Q_{k},\Delta=\Delta_{k}
and letting
r=r(\Delta)
.
To prove the desired implication, first observe that

(5.5)

\displaystyle M[|{N}[\nabla u]|^{q}](P,\tau)\leq\max\{M^{C_{0}r}[{N}[\nabla u]^{q}](P,\tau),C_{1}\lambda\}

for any
(P,\tau)\in Q
and with
C_{1}=C_{1}(\mathcal{O})
.
This is because
Q
is maximal and so
2C_{0}\Delta\not\subset E(\lambda)
.
Assume that
A\geq C_{1}
.
It follows that

(5.6)

\displaystyle|Q\cap E(A\lambda)|\leq|\{(P,\tau)\in Q:M^{C_{0}r}[{N}[\nabla u]^{q}](P,\tau)>A\lambda\}|.

Next let
\varphi=\varphi_{Q}
be a smooth cut-off function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
such that

\varphi=1\text{ on }C_{2}Q,\quad\varphi=0,\text{ on }\partial\Omega\setminus 2C_{2}Q,\quad|\nabla\varphi|\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-1},

where
C_{2}\geq 160C_{0}
.
Define

f_{Q}\coloneqq f-\alpha_{Q},\quad\alpha_{Q}\coloneqq\fint_{80Q}f

Now let
v=v_{Q}
be the unique weak solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Lv=0,&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
v=\varphi f_{Q},&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

Let
\bar{p}>p
.
In view of (
5.6
), we have

\displaystyle|Q\cap E(A\lambda)|

\displaystyle\leq|\{P\in Q:M^{C_{0}r}[{N}[\nabla(u-v)]^{q}](P,\tau)>\frac{A\lambda}{2^{q}}\}|

\displaystyle\quad+|\{P\in Q:M^{C_{0}r}[{N}[\nabla v]^{q}](P,\tau)>\frac{A\lambda}{2^{q}}\}|

\displaystyle\lesssim(A\lambda)^{-\bar{p}/q}\int_{2C_{0}\Delta}{N}[\nabla(u-v)]^{\bar{p}}+(A\lambda)^{-1}\int_{2C_{0}\Delta}{N}[\nabla v]^{q}\eqqcolon J_{1}+J_{2},

where we have used the weak
(\frac{\bar{p}}{q},\frac{\bar{p}}{q})
and
(1,1)

estimates for the maximal function.

We start with the term
J_{2}
.
Since
(R_{L})_{q}
is solvable, and applying
Proposition

2.44
, we have that for some large
C_{3}>>1
we have that

\displaystyle\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\varphi f_{Q}\|_{\dot{L}_{1,1/2}^{q}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{L}_{1,1/2}^{q}(C_{3}\Delta)}+\|h\|_{L^{q}(C_{3}\Delta)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{C_{3}\Delta}|D_{t}^{1/2}f|^{q}+|g|^{q}+|h|^{q}\lesssim\gamma\lambda|Q|\

and hence

(5.7)

\displaystyle J_{2}\lesssim A^{-1}\gamma|Q|\lesssim\gamma\varepsilon^{q/p}|Q|.

Now we deal with the first term
J_{1}
.
Observe that
(u-\alpha)-v
is a weak solution
whose boundary data
f_{Q}(1-\varphi)
vanishes on
C_{2}\Delta
.
Also note that the solvability of
(D_{L^{*}})_{p^{\prime}}
implies that
(D_{L^{*}})_{\bar{p}^{\prime}}

is solvable for some
\bar{p}>p
due to properties of the
B_{p}
weights.
It then follows by
Theorem

4.1
that

\displaystyle\int_{2C_{0}\Delta}{N}[\nabla(u-v)]^{\bar{p}}

\displaystyle=|2C_{0}\Delta|\fint_{2Q}{N}[\nabla(u-v)]^{\bar{p}}\lesssim|Q|\Big(\fint_{C_{2}\Delta}{N}[\nabla(u-v)]\Big)^{\bar{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|Q|\Bigg[\Big(\fint_{C_{2}\Delta}{N}[\nabla u]^{q}\Big)^{\bar{p}/q}+\Big(\fint_{C_{2}\Delta}{N}[\nabla v]^{q}\Big)^{\bar{p}/q}\Bigg].

Now, we already know that
\left(\fint_{C_{2}\Delta}\tilde{N}[\nabla v]^{q}\right)^{1/q}\lesssim\lambda\gamma

and hence it remains to consider the first term.
Note that since
C_{2}\Delta\not\subset E(\lambda)

there exists a point
(P^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\in C_{2}\Delta
such that
M[N[\nabla u]^{q}](P^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\leq\lambda
.
Let
\Delta^{\prime}
be a ball obtained by translating
\Delta
to be centered at
(P^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
,
then

\fint_{C_{2}\Delta}N[\nabla u]^{q}\lesssim\fint_{2C_{2}\Delta^{\prime}}N[\nabla u]^{q}\leq M[N[\nabla u]^{q}](P^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\leq\lambda.

Thus

(5.8)

\displaystyle J_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{(A\lambda)^{\bar{p}/q}}\lambda^{\bar{p}/q}|Q|=A^{-\bar{p}/q}|Q|=\varepsilon^{\bar{p}/p}|Q|=\varepsilon|Q|\varepsilon^{\bar{p}/p-1}

and combining this with (
5.7
) we get

|Q\cap E(A\lambda)|\lesssim J_{1}+J_{2}\lesssim\varepsilon|Q|(\varepsilon^{\bar{p}/p-1}+\gamma\varepsilon^{-q/p-1}).

Hence, first choosing
\varepsilon
small enough that
\varepsilon^{\bar{p}/p-1}\leq 1/2

and then
\gamma
small enough that
\gamma\varepsilon^{q/p-1}\leq 1/2
we indeed have that

|Q\cap E(A\lambda)|\leq\varepsilon|Q|,

thus completing our the proof.∎

We are now ready to finish the proof of
Theorem

1.3
in the unbounded case, i.e. when
\diam(\mathcal{O})=\infty
.
Like in
[
40
]
we leverage
Lemma

5.1

for a change of variables to see that

\int_{\partial\Omega}{N}[\nabla u]^{p}\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}M[N[\nabla u]^{q}]^{p/q}\sim\lim_{K\to\infty}\int_{0}^{K}|E(\lambda)|\lambda^{p/q-1}d\lambda.

For a fixed
K
the integral on the righthand side is finite as
\lambda^{p/q-1}\leq K^{p/q-1}<\infty
.
Thus for a fixed
K
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{K}|E(\lambda)

\displaystyle|\lambda^{p/q-1}d\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{K/A}|E(A\lambda)|\lambda^{p/q-1}d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{K/A}|E(\lambda)|\lambda^{p/q-1}d\lambda+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{K/A}|\{M[|D_{1/2}^{t}f|^{q}+|g|^{q}+|h|^{q}]>\gamma\lambda\}|\lambda^{p/q-1}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{K/A}|\{M[|D_{1/2}^{t}f|^{q}+|g|^{q}+|h|^{q}]>\gamma\lambda\}|\lambda^{p/q-1}d\lambda,

where in the last step since
K/A\leq K
we have moved the term
\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{K/A}|E(\lambda)|\lambda^{p/q-1}d\lambda
to the lefthand side. We take limit
K\to\infty
. Recall, that

h
is as in
Proposition

2.44

and
g\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
is a Hajłasz upper spatial gradient for
f
.
Thus
\|h\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}

and
\|g\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{L}_{1}^{p}(\partial\Omega)}

therefore we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}{N}[\nabla u]^{p}\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle|\{M[|D_{1/2}^{t}f|^{q}+|g|^{q}+|h|^{q}]>\gamma\lambda\}|\lambda^{p/q-1}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\gamma}\int_{\partial\Omega}M[|D_{1/2}^{t}f|^{q}+|g|^{q}+|h|^{q}]^{p/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\partial\Omega}|D_{1/2}^{t}f|^{p}+|g|^{p}+|h|^{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{L}_{1,1/2}^{p}(\partial\Omega)}^{p},

completing the proof.∎

5.2.
Bounded case

Now suppose that
\diam(\mathcal{O})<\infty
,
by rescaling we may without loss of generality assume that
\diam(\mathcal{O})=1
.
The first problem we run into is that the reverse Holder inequality from

Theorem

4.1
does not work for boundary cubes
\Delta

with
\diam(\Delta)>>1
.
For this reason we can prove an analogue of
Lemma

5.1
but only for local parabolic cubes of a finite size
\sim 1
. Subsequently, we can only obtain local bounds for the
L^{p}
norm of
N[\nabla u]
restricted to boundary parabolic cubes of size
\sim 1
.
To overcome this we will split our domain into subdomains of finite diameter (in space and time).

Denote by
\Omega_{i}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}\times(i,i+1)

and let
\Delta_{i}=\partial_{p}\Omega_{i}=\partial\mathcal{O}\times(i,i+1)
for
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. There exists a nonnegative
C^{1}
function
\psi\in C_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, supp
\psi\subset[-1,2]
such that

\psi_{i}(\cdot)=\psi(\cdot+i),\qquad|\psi^{\prime}|\lesssim 1,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\psi=1,\qquad\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi_{i}\equiv 1.

Clearly,
\|D_{1/2}^{t}\psi\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim 1
, for each
1<r<\infty
.
Set

f_{\Delta_{i}}\coloneqq\fint_{{\Delta}_{i}}f.

Finally, let
\Psi_{k}\coloneqq\sum_{i\geq k}\psi_{i}
and note that
\psi_{i}=\Psi_{i+1}-\Psi_{i}
and

\Psi_{k}\equiv 1
for on
[i+2,\infty)
.

Let
w_{i}
solve the PDE

\begin{cases}Lw_{i}=0,&\text{in }\Omega,\\
w_{i}=(f-f_{\Delta_{i}})\psi_{i}&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

If
w\coloneqq\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}w_{i}
then
v\coloneqq u-w
solves
Lv=0

with boundary data

\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{\Delta_{i}}\psi_{i}=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{\Delta_{i}}(\Psi_{i+1}-\Psi_{i})=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(f_{\Delta_{i-1}}-f_{\Delta_{i}})\Psi_{i}.

Thus we can write
v=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}v_{i}
as where
v_{i}
solves

\begin{cases}Lv_{i}=0,&\text{in }\Omega,\\
v_{i}=(f_{\Delta_{i-1}}-f_{\Delta_{i}})\Psi_{i}&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

If follows that we have decomposed the solution
u
to
Lu=0
with
u\big|_{\partial\Omega}=f
into

u=\sum_{i}v_{i}+\sum_{i}w_{i},

such that
w_{i}\big|_{\partial\Omega}
is supported only on
\partial\mathcal{O}\times(i-1,i+2)
and
v_{i}\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0
for
t<i-1
and
v_{i}\big|_{\partial\Omega}
is constant for
t>i+2
.

Let us also define
\tilde{v}_{i}
, such that

\tilde{v}_{i}
solves

\begin{cases}L\tilde{v}_{i}=0,&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\tilde{v}_{i}=(f_{\Delta_{i-1}}-f_{\Delta_{i}})\Psi_{i}(1-\Psi_{i+20})&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Note that by considering the solution
\tilde{v_{i}}
instead of

v_{i}
, since
v_{i}\big|_{\partial\Omega}(P,\tau)=\tilde{v}_{i}\big|_{\partial\Omega}(P,\tau)
for
\tau\leq i+15

we have
N[\nabla v_{i}](P,\tau)=N[\nabla\tilde{v}_{i}](P,\tau)
for all
\tau\leq i+10
.
However,
\tilde{v_{i}}
is more similar to
w_{i}
as both are now compactly supported and half derivative of

\Psi_{i}(1-\Psi_{i+20})
has faster decay than half derivative of
\Psi_{i}
.

Let us study the properties of
N[\nabla v_{i}]
and
N[\nabla w_{i}]
. Without loss of generality, let

v
be either
v_{i}
or
w_{i}
for some
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. It follows that
v\equiv 0
for
t\leq i-1
. Thus

N[\nabla v](P,\tau)\equiv 0
for all
\tau\leq i-5
.

When
i-5<\tau<i+10
we may study
N[\nabla v](P,\tau)
by considering the
(R_{L})_{p}
boundary value problem. As we have already observed above,
Lemma

5.1
only holds on scales comparable to the diameter of
\mathcal{O}
. Thus by modifying the calculation for the unbounded case we can obtain an estimate on a bounded (in time) portion of
\partial\Omega
, namely that

\int_{\cup_{k=i-5}^{i+9}\Delta_{k}}{N}[\nabla v]^{p}\lesssim\|v\|^{p}_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)}.

This calculation differs only slightly to the one we gave in the unbounded case, and is actually more similar to what Shen did in
[
40
]
as he only considered the case of a bounded domain.
Hence we obtain for
w_{i}
and
v_{i}
(here using
\tilde{v_{i}}
as on this portion of the domain they coincide):

(5.9)

\int_{\cup_{k=i-5}^{i+9}\Delta_{k}}{N}[\nabla w_{i}]^{p}\lesssim\|(f-f_{\Delta_{i}})\psi_{i})\|^{p}_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)},\quad\int_{\cup_{k=i-5}^{i+9}\Delta_{k}}{N}[\nabla v_{i}]^{p}\lesssim|f_{\Delta_{i-1}}-f_{\Delta_{i}}|^{p}.

Here, the second term is already simplified, as we use quietly the fact that
\Psi_{i}(1-\Psi_{i+20})
has bounded half time-derivative on
L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
for all
p>1
.

Finally, for
\tau\geq i+10
we will use the fact that
\nabla v
decays exponentially.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
p\geq 1
and assume that the Dirichlet problem
(D)_{p^{\prime}}^{*}

is solvable. (When
p=1
we assume that
(D)_{q}^{*}
is solvable for some
q>1
).
Let
v
be a solution to
Lv=0
and
suppose that
v=const.
on
\partial\mathcal{O}\times(i+2,\infty)
.
There exists an
\alpha>0
such that for all
j>i+9
we have that

\displaystyle\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{j})}\lesssim e^{-\alpha|i-j|}\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i+3})}.

Hence by (
5.9
) we have for all
j>i+9
:

(5.11)

\int_{\Delta_{j}}{N}[\nabla w_{i}]^{p}\lesssim e^{-\alpha p|i-j|}\|(f-f_{\Delta_{i}})\psi_{i})\|^{p}_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)},\quad\int_{\Delta_{j}}{N}[\nabla v_{i}]^{p}\lesssim e^{-\alpha p|i-j|}|f_{\Delta_{i-1}}-f_{\Delta_{i}}|^{p}.

In order to establish
Lemma

5.10
we start by proving the
L^{2}
decay. Assume first that the coefficients of
A
as smooth.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
u
be such that
{L}u=0
on
\partial\mathcal{O}\times[0,\infty)
and also
Moreover suppose that
u=0
on
\partial\mathcal{O}\times[0,\infty)
.
Then there exists an
\beta>0
such that for
t>0

(5.13)

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim e^{-\beta t}\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}.

Proof.

Set
\eta(t)\coloneqq\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}
.
For
t>0

\displaystyle 2\eta(t)\eta^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}(\eta(t)^{2})=2\int_{\Omega_{t}}u\partial_{t}u=-2\int_{\Omega_{t}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\leq-2\lambda\int_{\Omega_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2},

where
\Omega_{t}=\mathcal{O}\times\{t\}
.
Since
u=0
on
\partial\Omega_{t}
, applying the Sobolev inequality on
\Omega_{t}

we have that

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}\lesssim-\int_{\Omega_{t}}|u|^{2}=-\eta(t)^{2}.

Hence
\eta^{\prime}(t)\lesssim-\eta(t)

and we may apply Gronwall’s inequality to deduce (
5.13
).
∎

Observe that this calculation holds on very general domains
\mathcal{O}
as it only requires the Sobolev inequality. The assumption that
A
is smooth can be removed by approximation as the estimate we are seeking only depends on the ellipticity constant of the operator
A
.

Assume now that
\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i+3})}<\infty
and that
v\big|_{\partial\Omega}\equiv 0
for all times
\geq i+2
.
Clearly, then also

\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{1}(\Delta_{i+3})}\lesssim\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i+3})}<\infty,

and thus by (
4.3
)

\int_{\Omega_{i+3}}|\nabla v|\lesssim\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{1}(\Delta_{i+3})}\lesssim\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i+3})}.

By applying
Lemma

3.6
as we satisfy

v\big|_{\partial\Omega}(P,\tau)=0
for
\tau>i+2
we obtain that for some
\tau\in(i+3,i+4)

\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}\times\{\tau\}}|\nabla v|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i+3})}<\infty,

and finally by Sobolev (given that
v
vanishes on the lateral boundary of
\Omega_{i+3}
):

\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}\times\{\tau\}}|v|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i+3})}<\infty.

Fix now some
j>i+9
. We propagate the
L^{2}
initial data given on
\mathcal{O}\times\{\tau\}
using
Proposition

5.12
to
t\in(j+7,j+12)
. It follows that for all such
t
we have

\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim e^{-\beta(j-i-3)}\|v(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim e^{-\beta|i-j|}\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i+3})}<\infty.

By integrating over the interval
(j+7,j+12)
and then applying
Proposition

3.16

(5.14)

\sup_{\Omega_{j+8}\cup\Omega_{j+9}\cup\Omega_{j+10}}|v|\lesssim e^{-\beta|i-j|}\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i+3})}<\infty.

Finally, we repeat the argument we gave in the proof of
Theorem

4.1
where we estimated

{N}^{r}[\nabla v](P,\tau)
by
{N}_{\sup}^{r}[v/\delta](P,\tau)
and then using the Green’s function eventually we have obtained (for
r=1
as the diameter of the domain is
1
):

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{\Delta_{j+9}}{N}[\nabla v]^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\fint_{\Omega\cap(j+8.5,j+10.5)}|\nabla v|.

The final step is to estimate the above righthand side by the lefthand side of (
5.14
) which can be done thanks to
Proposition

3.4
(boundary Cacciopolli) which finally gives us that

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\Delta_{j+9}}{N}[\nabla v]^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\beta|i-j|}\|{N}[\nabla v]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i+3})}<\infty.

Form this the claim of
Lemma

5.10
follows by applying the above estimate to
the function
v-const.
∎

We are ready to add up the estimates to obtain a bound for the
L^{p}
norm of
u
. Fix
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}

and consider
N[\nabla u]
on
\Delta_{i}
. Since
N
is sub-additive, clearly

N[\nabla u](P,\tau)\leq\sum_{j}\left[N[\nabla v_{j}]+N[\nabla w_{i}]\right](P,\tau),

and therefore

\|N[\nabla u]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i})}\leq\sum_{j}\left[\|N[\nabla v_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i})}+\|N[\nabla w_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i})}\right]

\leq\sum_{j=i-9}^{i+5}\left[\|N[\nabla v_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i})}+\|N[\nabla w_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i})}\right]+\sum_{j<i-9}\left[\|N[\nabla v_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i})}+\|N[\nabla w_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i})}\right]

\hskip-213.39566pt\lesssim\sum_{j=i-9}^{i+5}\left[\|(f-f_{\Delta_{j}})\psi_{j})\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}}+|f_{\Delta_{j-1}}-f_{\Delta_{j}}|\right]

+\sum_{j<i-9}e^{-\alpha|i-j|}\left[\|(f-f_{\Delta_{j}})\psi_{j})\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}}+|f_{\Delta_{j-1}}-f_{\Delta_{j}}|\right],

using (
5.9
) and (
5.11
). We raise both sides to the
p
-th power. It follows that

\hskip-42.67912pt\|N[\nabla u]\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i})}\lesssim\sum_{j=i-9}^{i+5}\left[\|(f-f_{\Delta_{j}})\psi_{j})\|^{p}_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}}+|f_{\Delta_{j-1}}-f_{\Delta_{j}}|^{p}\right]

\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(\sum_{j<i-9}e^{-\alpha|i-j|}\left[\|(f-f_{\Delta_{j}})\psi_{j})\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}}+|f_{\Delta_{j-1}}-f_{\Delta_{j}}|\right]\right)^{p}.

For the last term we use Hölder’s inequality for series. We split the exponential into two terms

e^{-\alpha|i-j|}=e^{-\alpha|i-j|/p^{\prime}}e^{-\alpha|i-j|/p}

to obtain

\left(\sum_{j<i-9}e^{-\alpha|i-j|}\left[\|(f-f_{\Delta_{j}})\psi_{j})\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}}+|f_{\Delta_{j-1}}-f_{\psi_{j}}|\right]\right)^{p}\leq\left(\sum_{j<i-9}\left(e^{-\alpha|i-j|/p^{\prime}}\right)^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{p/p^{\prime}}\times

\qquad\qquad\qquad\sum_{j<i-9}e^{-\alpha|i-j|}\left[\|(f-f_{\Delta_{j}})\psi_{j})\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}}+|f_{\Delta_{j-1}}-f_{\Delta_{j}}|\right]^{p}

\leq C(p,\alpha)\sum_{j<i-9}e^{-\alpha|i-j|}\left[\|(f-f_{\Delta_{j}})\psi_{j})\|^{p}_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}}+|f_{\Delta_{j-1}}-f_{\Delta_{j}}|^{p}\right].

Now we sum over all indices
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since

\|N[\nabla u]\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}=\sum_{i}\|N[\nabla u]\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Delta_{i})}
we obtain after rearranging the summation order:

(5.15)

\|N[\nabla u]\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(1+\sum_{k>9}e^{-\alpha k})\left[\|(f-f_{\Delta_{i}})\psi_{i})\|^{p}_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)}+|f_{\Delta_{i-1}}-f_{\Delta_{i}}|^{p}\right]

\qquad\qquad\lesssim\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[\|(f-f_{\Delta_{i}})\psi_{i})\|^{p}_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)}+\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|f_{\Delta_{i-1}}-f_{\Delta_{i}}|^{p}.

We are nearly done. Recall
Proposition

2.44
which gives estimate for norm of the term

(f-f_{\Delta_{i}})\psi_{i}
. It follows that

(5.16)

\|N[\nabla u]\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|f_{\Delta_{i-1}}-f_{\Delta_{i}}|^{p}+\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+\|h\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)},

where for functions
g\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(f),\,h
we have used the finite overlap of the balls
32\Delta_{i}
which allows up to claim that

\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(32\Delta_{i})}\lesssim\|g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

For both
g
and
h
we have bounds on their
L^{p}
norms by
\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)}
, provided
g
is chosen correctly. Thus (
5.16
) only requires bound on the first term on the righthand side.

We now wish to show

(5.17)

\displaystyle|f_{\Delta_{i-1}}-f_{\Delta_{i}}|\lesssim\|f^{\#}\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\Delta}_{i})},

where
\tilde{\Delta}_{i}
is an enlargement of
\Delta_{i}
and
f^{\#}
is the sharp maximal function of
f
defined by (
2.30
). Recall that we have established that
\|f^{\#}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)}
.

We have by (
2.35
):

\left|f(X,t)\chi_{\Delta_{i-1}}(X,t)-(\fint_{\Delta_{i}}f)\chi_{\Delta_{i-1}}(X,t)\right|\lesssim\left(f^{\sharp}(X,t)+\fint_{B}f^{\sharp}\right)\chi_{\Delta_{i-1}}(X,t),

for
B=\Delta_{i-1}\cup\Delta_{i}
. Integrating over
\partial\Omega
yields

|f_{\Delta_{i-1}}-f_{\Delta_{i}}|\leq C\fint_{B}f^{\sharp}.

Since the volume of
B
is
O(1)
the average can be replaced by
\|f^{\sharp}\|_{L^{1}(\tilde{\Delta_{i}})}
. From this an estimate analogous to (
5.17
) follows. In summary, we have for (
5.16
) that

(5.18)

\|N[\nabla u]\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}(\partial\Omega)},

as desired. Hence the bounded case is also established and
Theorem

1.3
holds.
∎

6.1.
Atomic spaces
\dot{H\hskip-1.70717ptS}^{1,(\beta)}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

We plan to define natural end-point spaces for
p=1
which interpolates well
with the homogeneous parabolic Sobolev spaces
\dot{L}^{p}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
defined above for
p>1
.
It follows that we aim to define spaces that respect parabolic homogeneity. Thus for an “atom”
a
we expect
\nabla a
to satisfy the usual condition defining Hardy-Sobolev space with one derivative. However in the
t
-variable we only expect to have a
1/2
-time derivative. We borrow lots of ideas from
[
3
]
(see Definition 2.16 there).

Definition 6.1
.

Let
1<\beta<\infty
. We say that a function
b:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a homogeneous
(1,1/2,\beta)
-atom associated to a parabolic ball
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of radius
r=r(B)
if

(i)

b
is supported in the ball
B
,

(ii)

\|\nabla b\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq|B|^{-1/\beta^{\prime}}
,

(iii)

\|b\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq r(B)
.

Here
|B|
denotes the usual
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
When
p=\infty
we modify (ii) and replace it by
\|\nabla b\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq|B|^{-1}.

We say that a locally integrable function
f
belongs to the homogeneous parabolic atomic Hardy-Sobolev space

\dot{H\hskip-1.70717ptS}^{1,(\beta)}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
if there exists a family of locally integrable homogeneous
(1,1/2,\beta)
-atoms
(b_{i})_{i}
such that
f=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}b_{i}
, with
\sum_{i}|\lambda_{i}|<\infty
. We equip the space

\dot{H\hskip-1.70717ptS}^{1,(\beta)}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with the norm:

\|f\|_{\dot{H\hskip-1.70717ptS}^{1,(\beta)}_{1,1/2}}=\inf_{(\lambda_{i})_{i}}\sum_{i}|\lambda_{i}|.

The condition (iii) is not strictly necessary as it follows from the conditions (i) and (ii) and Poincaré’s inequality in spatial gradient
\nabla b
. For each fixed
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
let
\Delta_{t}=B\cap\{t=const\}
be the “ball” in spatial variables
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
. Since
b
vanishes on
\partial\Delta_{t}
we have that

\|b\|_{L^{1}(\cdot,t)}=\int_{\Delta_{t}}|b|dx\leq r\int_{\Delta_{t}}|\nabla b|\,dx.

Integrating in
t
we then get

\|b\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq r\int_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{\Delta_{t}}|\nabla b|\,dx\,dt=r\int_{B}|\nabla b|\leq r\|\nabla b\|_{L^{\beta}}|B|^{1/\beta^{\prime}},

by Hölder. From this using (ii) we see that (iii) follows.

In the definition above the condition
(ii)
seems to be “global” as the
L^{\beta}
norm is calculated over whole space. However, clearly supp
\nabla b\subset B
. We also claim that
D^{1/2}_{t}b
is “essentially” localised to a ball
2B=B(X,2r)
and decaying away from the support of
B
. We have the following:

Lemma 6.2
.

For an
(1,1/2,\beta)
-atom
b
supported on
B=B((x,t),r)
we have for
Y=(y,s)
:

(i)

D^{1/2}_{t}b(y,s)=0
if
|x-y|\geq r
.

(ii)

If
|x-y|<r
and
|t-s|>4r^{2}
then

|D^{1/2}_{t}b(y,s)|\leq C|t-s|^{-3/2}\|b\|_{L^{1}(B\cap\{y=const\})}.

Proof.

Using the definition of the half-derivative we have:

|D^{1/2}_{t}b(y,s)|=\left|c_{1}\int_{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{b(y,s)-b(y,\tau)}{|s-\tau|^{3/2}}d\tau\right|\leq c_{1}\int_{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{|b(y,\tau)|}{|s-\tau|^{3/2}}d\tau.

Given that
b
is only supported in
B
, only
\tau\in(t-r^{2},t+r^{2})
contribute to the integral above. Since for such
\tau
:
|s-\tau|\approx|s-t|
we get that

|D^{1/2}_{t}b(y,s)|\lesssim|t-s|^{-3/2}\|b\|_{L^{1}(Q\cap\{y=const\})}.

∎

Our aim is to prove that if
b
is a
(1,1/2,\beta)
-atom then
b\in\dot{W}^{1}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
\dot{W}^{1}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
norm
\lesssim 1
. It then would follow that for every
\beta>1
we have that

(6.3)

\dot{H\hskip-1.70717ptS}^{1,(\beta)}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset\dot{W}^{1}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\|f\|_{\dot{W}_{1,1/2}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H\hskip-1.70717ptS}^{1,(\beta)}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Consider therefore and arbitrary
(1,1/2,\beta)
-atom
b
supported on
B
centred at
(x,t)
. By Hölder we have that

\|\nabla b\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{1}(2B)}=\|\nabla b\|_{L^{1}(2B)}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{1}(2B)}

\leq[\|\nabla b\|_{L^{\beta}(2B)}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{\beta}(2B)}]|2B|^{1/\beta^{\prime}}.

Using the fact that we have a doubling measure and condition (ii) in the definition of the atom the claim

\|\nabla b\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{1}(2B)}\lesssim 1

follows. It remains to estimate
D^{1/2}_{t}b
away from the ball
2B
. For this we use
Lemma

6.2
. By (i) and (ii):

(6.4)

\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 2B)}\leq\int_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,|t-s|>4r^{2}}\int_{|y-x|<r}|D^{t}_{1/2}b(y,s)|dy\,ds

\leq\int_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,|t-s|>4r^{2}}|t-s|^{-3/2}\int_{|y-x|<r}\|b\|_{L^{1}(B\cap\{(y,\cdot)\})}dy\,ds.

The interior integral in
y
will give us
\|b\|_{L^{1}(B)}
thus yielding:

(6.5)

\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 2B)}\leq C\|b\|_{L^{1}(B)}\int_{4r^{2}}^{\infty}\tau^{-3/2}d\tau\approx r^{-1}\|b\|_{L^{1}(B)}\leq 1,

where in the final step we have used property (iii) of the definition of an atom
b
. The calculation of the
{\rm BMO}
norm of
D^{1/2}_{t}b
is analogous for
\beta=\infty
.

Lemma 6.6
.

When
1<\beta\leq\alpha\leq\infty
an
(1,1/2,\alpha)
-atom
b
is a multiple of an
(1,1/2,\beta)
-atom
b

and hence

(6.7)

\dot{H\hskip-1.70717ptS}^{1,(\infty)}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset\dot{H\hskip-1.70717ptS}^{1,(\alpha)}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset\dot{H\hskip-1.70717ptS}^{1,(\beta)}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Moreover, for each
\beta>1
the space
\dot{H\hskip-1.70717ptS}^{1,(\beta)}_{1,1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a Banach space.

Proof.

Only condition (ii) differs for different values of
\beta
. A calculation similar to the one we have performed above implies that

\|\nabla b\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{\beta}(2B)}\leq[\|\nabla b\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{\alpha}(2B)}]|2B|^{1/\beta-1/\alpha}.

This also holds for
\alpha=\infty
replacing the
L^{\infty}
norm by
{\rm BMO}
as
{\rm BMO}\subset L^{\beta}
for
\beta<\infty
.
Assuming that
b
is a
(1,1/2,\alpha)
-atom by (ii) of the definition of an atom an using doubling we get that

\|\nabla b\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{\beta}(2B)}\leq C_{n+1}^{1/\beta-1/\alpha}|B|^{1/\beta-1}=C_{n+1}^{1/\beta-1/\alpha}|B|^{-1/\beta^{\prime}}.

Here
C_{n+1}
is the doubling constant. As before
\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 2B)}
requires a separate treatment. We again use
Lemma

6.2
. By (i) and (ii):

(6.8)

\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|^{\beta}_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 2B)}\leq\int_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,|t-s|>4r^{2}}\int_{|y-x|<r}|D^{t}_{1/2}b(y,s)|^{\beta}dy\,ds

\leq\int_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,|t-s|>4r^{2}}|t-s|^{-3\beta/2}\int_{|y-x|<r}\|b\|^{\beta}_{L^{1}(B\cap\{(y,\cdot)\})}dy\,ds.

By Hölder

\|b\|^{\beta}_{L^{1}(B\cap\{(y,\cdot)\})}\leq\|b\|^{\beta}_{L^{\beta}(B\cap\{(y,\cdot)\})}r^{2(\beta-1)}.

Hence

(6.9)

\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|^{\beta}_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 2B)}\leq C\|b\|^{\beta}_{L^{\beta}(B)}r^{2(\beta-1)}\int_{4r^{2}}^{\infty}\tau^{-3\beta/2}d\tau=C\|b\|^{\beta}_{L^{\beta}(B)}r^{-\beta}.

It follows that

\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 2B)}\leq Cr^{-1}\|b\|_{L^{\beta}(B)}.

As before using the (i) and (ii) we can obtain
\|b\|_{L^{\beta}}\leq Cr\|\nabla b\|_{L^{\beta}(B)}
and hence

\|\nabla b\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}b\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C|B|^{-1/\beta^{\prime}}.

From this it follows that
\frac{b}{C}
is a
(1,1/2,\beta)
-atom. The proof that the defined space is Banach is trivial
(c.f.
[
3
]
for similar proofs).
∎

For

It is an exact interpolation space of exponent

6

Definition 6.10
.

Let
f
be a measurable function on a measure space
(X,\mu)
. The decreasing rearrangement of
f
is the function
f^{*}
defined for every

f^{*}(t)=\inf\left\{\lambda:\,\mu(\left\{x:\,|f(x)|>\lambda\right\})\leq t\right\}.

The maximal decreasing rearrangement of

f
is the function
f^{**}
defined for every
t>0
by

f^{**}(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)ds.

From the properties of
f^{**}
we mention:

1.

(f+g)^{**}\leq f^{**}+g^{**}
.

2.

({M}f)^{*}\sim f^{**}
.

3.

\mu(\left\{x;\,|f(x)|>f^{*}(t)\right\})\leq t
.

4.

\forall 1<p\leq\infty
,
\|f^{**}\|_{p}\sim\|f\|_{p}
.

We exactly know the functional
K
for Lebesgue spaces :

Proposition 6.11
.

Take

K(f,t,L^{p_{0}},L^{p_{1}})\simeq\left(\int_{0}^{t^{\alpha}}\left[f^{*}(s)\right]^{p_{0}}ds\right)^{1/p_{0}}+t\left(\int_{t^{\alpha}}^{\infty}\left[f^{*}(s)\right]^{p_{1}}ds\right)^{1/p_{1}},

where

When the

If

Otherwise, the maximal theorem yields

\displaystyle|\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{-\beta}\big\|(|\nabla f|+|D^{1/2}_{t}f|+|S_{1}(f)|^{\beta}+f^{\sharp})^{\beta}\big\|_{L^{1}}

For

\int_{7B_{i}}(|\nabla f|^{\beta}+|D^{1/2}_{t}f|^{\beta}+|S_{1}(f)|^{\beta}+|f^{\sharp}|^{\beta})d\mu\leq C\alpha^{\beta}|B_{i}|.

Let

\Omega

Using (

Hence by (

=[(D^{1/2}_{t}f)\chi_{i}](x,t)+R_{i}(x,t).

We first integrate over

\|(D^{1/2}_{t}f)\chi_{i}\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\alpha|B_{i}|^{1/\beta}

For the second term using Lipschitzness of

We can recognise that the inside integral is bounded by a truncated maximal function

It follows that the expression is bounded by

\int_{2B_{i}}[M^{t}(f^{\sharp})]^{\beta}\leq\int_{3B_{i}}|f^{\sharp}|^{\beta}.

We have used boundedness of

After putting all terms together we get from (

It remain to consider the away part when

There is nothing to calculate unless there exists

Here
\mathcal{H}^{x}
denotes the line

|R_{i}(x,t)|\leq C\left(\frac{r_{i}}{d((x,t),B_{i})}\right)^{3}\left[\fint_{B_{i}\cap\mathcal{H}^{x}}f^{\sharp}+\fint_{B_{i}}f^{\sharp}\right].

Here

to

.
Hence

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 2B_{i}}|R_{i}|^{\beta}\lesssim|B_{i}|\fint_{B_{i}}|f^{\sharp}|^{\beta}.

Now (

\|b_{i}\|_{\dot{H\hskip-1.70717ptS}^{1,(\beta)}_{1,1/2}}=(\|\nabla b_{i}\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|D^{1/2}_{t}b_{i}\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})})|B_{i}|^{1/\beta^{\prime}}\leq C\alpha|B_{i}|^{1/\beta+1\beta^{\prime}}=C\alpha|B_{i}|.

) is proved.
Let

where in the final step we have used finite overlap of the Whitney balls

.
Thus

Set
\displaystyle g=f-\sum_{i}b_{i}
. Since the sum is locally finite on
\Omega
, as usual,

is defined almost everywhere on

and

. Moreover,

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
It remains to prove (

\displaystyle\nabla g

\displaystyle=\nabla f-\sum_{i}\nabla b_{i}

\displaystyle=\nabla f-(\sum_{i}\chi_{i})\nabla f-\sum_{i}(f-\textstyle\frac{1}{\chi_{i}(B_{i})}\textstyle\int_{B_{i}}f\chi_{i})\nabla\chi_{i}

\displaystyle={\bf 1}_{F}(\nabla f)-\sum_{i}(f-\textstyle\frac{1}{\chi_{i}(B_{i})}\textstyle\int_{B_{i}}f\chi_{i})\nabla\chi_{i}.

From the definition of
F
and the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, we have
{\bf 1}_{F}|\nabla f|\leq\alpha\;\mu-
a.e. We claim that a similar estimate holds for

for all

. For this, note first that

.

Then fix

and fix some

.
Note that
\displaystyle\sum_{i}\chi_{i}(X)=1

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\nabla\chi_{i}(X)=0

h(X)=\sum_{i\in I_{X}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{|7B_{j}|}\int_{7B_{j}}f\right)-\left(\frac{1}{\chi_{i}(B_{i})}\int_{B_{i}}f\chi_{i}\right)\right]\nabla\chi_{i}(X).

We return to the calculation we have done above (

).
With

,

\left|\fint_{7B_{j}}f-\frac{1}{\chi_{i}(B_{i})}\int_{B_{i}}f\chi_{i}\right|\leq Cr_{i}\fint_{7B_{j}}f^{\sharp}\lesssim\alpha r_{j},

\displaystyle|h(X)|\lesssim\sum_{i\in I_{x}}\alpha r_{j}r_{j}^{-1}\leq CN\alpha,

and the bound (
6.15
) for
\nabla g
holds. Next, we look at

. The result for

|g(x,t)-g(y,t)|\leq C\alpha|x-y|.

It follows that we need to prove that

|g(x,t)-g(x,s)|\leq C\alpha|t-s|^{1/2},

since the desired result then follows by triangle inequality
|g(x,t)-g(y,s)|\leq|g(x,t)-g(y,t)|+|g(y,t)-g(y,s)|

g
. If follows from the definition of

g(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),&\quad\mbox{when }x\in F,\\
\sum_{i}(\chi_{i}(B_{i})^{-1}\int_{B_{i}}f\chi_{i})\chi_{i},&\quad\mbox{when }x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

Hence if we simplify it, on the good set

and then smoothly glue them together.

It follows that if

|g(x,t)-g(x,s)|\leq|\partial_{t}g(x,\tau)||t-s|\leq C\alpha|t-s|^{1/2}\frac{|t-s|^{1/2}}{r_{i}}.

Using (

The final case is when

are radii of the balls

and again by (

To prove that

)
for the function

. Let us fix a parabolic ball

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{0}^{r}\gamma^{-3}\left|g(x,t)-\fint_{B_{\gamma}(x,t)}g\,\right|^{2}\,d\gamma\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\sum_{i}\int_{B_{i}\cap B_{r}}\int_{0}^{\min\{r,r_{i}\}}\gamma^{-3}\left|\fint_{|y|\leq\gamma}g(x+y,t)-\fint_{B_{\gamma}(x,t)}g\,\right|^{2}\,d\gamma\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim

\int_{\min\{r,\rho(x,t)\}}^{r}\gamma^{-3}\left|g(y,s)-\fint_{B_{\gamma}(y,s)}f\,\right|^{2}\,d\gamma\leq\alpha^{2}.

\displaystyle\lesssim G(P_{0},2^{j}R,t_{0}+100(2^{j}R)^{2},V^{+}(\Delta_{0}))\lesssim R^{\alpha}(2^{j}R)^{-(n+\alpha)}.

Using this in (
6.50
) implies that

(6.51)

\fint_{\Delta_{j}^{i}}{N}^{2^{j}R}[\nabla u]\lesssim(2^{j}R)^{-1}R^{\alpha}(2^{j}R)^{-(n+\alpha)}\lesssim 2^{-j\alpha}(2^{j}R)^{-n-1}.

Thus

\int_{A_{j}}{N}^{2^{j}R}[\nabla u]\leq\sum_{i}(2^{j}R)^{n+1}\fint_{\Delta_{j}^{i}}{N}^{2^{j}R}[\nabla u]\lesssim 2^{-j\alpha}.

So far we have an estimate for the truncated non-tangential maximal function at the height
2^{j}R
for
A_{j}
.
Consider any point
(X,t)\in\Gamma(S,\tau)
for
(S,\tau)\in A_{j}
and
\delta(X,t)\geq 2^{j}R
.
By Caccioppolli and interior Harnack

\Big(\fint_{C_{\delta(X,t)/4}(X,t)}|\nabla u|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim(2^{j}R)^{-1}\Big(\fint_{C_{\delta(X,t)/2}(X,t)}|u|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\lesssim(2^{j}R)^{-1}u(P_{0},\delta(X,t),t_{0}+100\delta(X,t)^{2}).

This can be further estimated by (
3.12
) and
Lemma

3.13
and taking a supremum over all points with

\delta(X,t)\geq 2^{j}R
again yields a bound by
2^{-j\alpha}(2^{j}R)^{-n-1}
.

Hence by combining these two estimates we obtain that

\int_{A_{j}}{N}[\nabla u]\leq\int_{A_{j}}\tilde{N}^{2^{j}R}[\nabla u]+|A_{j}|(2^{-j\alpha}(2^{j}R)^{-n-1}\lesssim 2^{-j\alpha}.

Summing over all
i\geq 3
yields that

\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus 2\Delta_{0}}{N}[\nabla u]=\sum_{j\geq 3}\int_{A_{j}}{N}[\nabla u]\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 3}2^{-j\alpha}\lesssim_{\alpha}1.

In the proof above we have used
Theorem

4.1
which implies that the argument given above is fully correct only if the domain
\mathcal{O}
is unbounded. If
\mathcal{O}
is bounded we can only consider sets
A_{j}
up to the size of comparable to the diameter of
\mathcal{O}
, that is for
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{j}R\lesssim\mbox{diam}(\mathcal{O})
. Say
d=\mbox{diam}(\mathcal{O})
and let
\Delta_{10d}=\Delta_{10d}(P_{0},t_{0})\subset\partial\Omega
. Then the calculation above yields the estimate

\int_{\Delta_{10d}\setminus 2\Delta_{0}}{N}[\nabla u]\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 3}^{[\log_{2}(R^{-1}d)]+4}2^{-j\alpha}\lesssim_{\alpha}1.

The set of boundary points with distance more than
10d
to
(P_{0},t_{0})
requires a separate argument.
However, for such points
Lemma

5.10
does apply implying an exponential decay in the
L^{1}
norm of
{N}[\nabla u]
and thus obvious summability. Therefore the claim as stated also holds in the case
\mathcal{O}
is bounded. ∎

3.4
